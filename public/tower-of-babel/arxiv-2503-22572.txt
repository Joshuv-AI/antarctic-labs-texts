Higher-order p p -form asymptotic symmetries in D = p + 2 D=p+2
Matteo Romoli, Federico Manzoni

Abstract
We investigate higher-order asymptotic symmetries for a p p -form gauge field in ( p + 2 ) (p+2) -dimensional Minkowski spacetime, where Hodge duality with a scalar holds. Employing symplectic renormalization, we identify N + 1 N+1 asymptotic charges, with each charge being parametrised by a function of the angular variables. By means of the Hodge decomposition, these charges share the same formal structure independently from p p and are manifestly dual to a scalar charge. We work in Lorenz gauge, therefore the gauge parameters require a radial expansion involving logarithmic (subleading) terms to ensure nontrivial angular dependence at leading order. At the same time we assume a power expansion for the field strength, allowing logarithms in the gauge field expansions within pure gauge sectors. We compute the charge algebra, which turn out to be abelian up to possible central extension due to mixed electric/magnetic charge sector and/or unexploited ambiguities during symplectic renormalization.

2.3
Covariant phase space formalism

The most important tool in order to understand whether an asymptotic symmetry has a physical interpretation is the asymptotic charge. We introduce this notion employing the covariant phase space formalism, whose main idea is to combine the differential calculus in both spacetime and field space to give rise to the variational bicomplex and jet bundle.

Let

(22)

2.4
Symplectic renormalization

The roots of symplectic renormalization can be traced back to the introduction of the
covariant symplectic structure on the space of classical solutions
Zuckerman (1986)
.
Soon after, the role of boundary terms was clarified and was showed that the presymplectic potential is defined only up some ambiguities
Lee and Wald (1990)
;
Iyer and Wald (1994)
.
The presymplectic potential admits two types of ambiguities

\theta\sim_{\text{eq}}\theta+\delta\Xi+d\Upsilon.

(34)

The first does not change
\omega
, since
\delta\circ\delta=0
, and corresponds to the addition of a
boundary term to the Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}\sim_{\text{eq}}\mathcal{L}+d\Xi
. The second modifies
\omega\sim_{\text{eq}}\omega+d\delta\Upsilon
but
does not affect
d\theta
, since
d\circ d=0
.
Symplectic renormalization refers to the procedure of modifying the
presymplectic potential by exploiting the ambiguities in its definition so
that the resulting symplectic form is well defined. Concretely, one starts from a local
presymplectic current
\omega
derived from a Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}
,
and observes that the integral (
24
)
may diverge or fail to be independent of the choice of Cauchy surface.
The key insight is that the ambiguities (
34
) can be exploited to cure these problems.
By choosing ambiguities appropriately, often guided by boundary conditions or
asymptotic symmetries, one obtains a renormalized symplectic form which is finite and well-defined on the physical phase space.

Methodologically, symplectic renormalization closely parallels
holographic renormalization
Henningson and Skenderis (1998)
;
de Haro et al. (2001)
;
Skenderis (2002)
. One introduces a regulator, computes the divergent contributions to the
symplectic form and cancels them by local counterterms intrinsic to the
boundary. The resulting renormalized symplectic structure has several
desirable properties: it yields finite generators for large
gauge transformations and diffeomorphisms, provides well-defined
Poisson brackets for asymptotic charges, and ensures integrability of
the associated conserved quantities.

The ansatz on the field strength expansion implies the following behaviour of the

3.2
Asymptotic symmetries and charges

Considering a constant time surface
\Sigma_{t}
, we can define a charge
Q_{t}
as

Q_{t}:=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}dud\Omega_{D-2}\;{\cal J}^{t}

(54)

with
{\cal J}
being the Noether current

{\cal J}^{t}=\frac{1}{(p-1)!}\partial_{\mu}\left(\sqrt{|g|}H^{t\mu\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{p-1}}\epsilon_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{p-1}}\right)\,.

(55)

Neglecting a total
(D-2)
-sphere divergence, we can write
Q_{t}
respect to the Bondi components, namely

Q_{t}:=\frac{1}{(p-1)!}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}dud\Omega_{D-2}\;(\partial_{u}-\partial_{r})\left(r^{D-2}H^{uri_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}\epsilon_{i_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}\right)\,.

(56)

The asymptotic charge is thus defined taking the limit
t\to\infty
at fixed
u
, so that
\Sigma_{t}
approaches
{\cal I}^{+}
, this is equivalent to the limit
r\rightarrow\infty
. However, for higher-order asymptotic symmetries there are divergences which make the limit ill-defined. Symplectic renormalization is employed to cancel these divergent terms and perform the limit. The asymptotic charge is thus defined as

Q_{B}:=\lim_{t\to\infty}Q_{t,\text{ren}}\,,

(57)

where we denoted with
Q_{t,\text{ren}}
the symplectic-renormalized version (details in
section 4
) of the charge (
56
) and the limit is taken at constant
u
.
Given the Hodge decomposition of the gauge parameter (
49

\begin{split}\epsilon_{i_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}=&D_{[i_{1}}\epsilon^{\prime\prime}_{i_{2}\dots i_{p-1}]}+\varepsilon_{i_{1}\dots i_{p}}D^{i_{p}}\epsilon^{\prime}\,,\end{split}

(58)

and of the field strength component

H_{uri_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}=D_{[i_{1}}H^{\prime\prime}_{i_{2}\dots i_{p-1}]ur}+\varepsilon_{i_{1}\dots i_{p}}D^{i_{p}}H^{\prime}_{ur}\,,

Q_{t}=-\int_{\Sigma_{t}}dud\Omega_{D-2}\;(\partial_{u}-\partial_{r})\left(r^{D-2}\epsilon^{\prime}\Delta H_{ur}^{\prime}\right)\,,

(60)

where we used

D^{i_{1}}H_{uri_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}=0\,,

(61)

which originates from the Bianchi identity
\nabla_{[i_{1}}H_{uri_{2}\dots i_{p}]}=0

Therefore, the only relevant component is

\epsilon^{\prime}=\sum_{n=-N-(\frac{p}{2})}^{\infty}\frac{\epsilon^{\prime(n)}}{r^{n}}+\sum_{n=\frac{2-p}{2}}^{\infty}\hat{\epsilon}^{\prime(n)}\frac{\ln{(r)}}{r^{n}}\,.

(63)

If we do not consider the logarithmic series, namely

\epsilon^{\prime(-n-p/2)}(u,\{x^{i}\})=\sum_{m=0}^{N-n}u^{m}\epsilon^{\prime(-n-p/2,m)}(\{x^{i}\})\,,

4
Symplectic renormalization of the charge

The study of
{\cal O}(r^{N})
asymptotic symmetries and related charges naturally leads to divergences that are of two types: those arising from
positive powers of
t
and those resulting from the
du
integration. To address these issues, we employ a symplectic renormalization procedure, as proposed in
Freidel et al. (2019)
.

As we discussed in the preliminaries, the presymplectic potential admits two types of ambiguities that allow us to define an
equivalence relation

\theta^{\mu}\sim_{\text{eq}}\theta^{\mu}+\partial_{\nu}\Upsilon^{\mu\nu}+\delta\Xi^{\mu}\,,

(108)

with
\Upsilon^{\mu\nu}
an antisymmetric rank-two tensor. Our main goal is to use these ambiguities to cancel the
divergences in the asymptotic charges while leaving the rest unchanged.

In
(u,t,\{x^{i}\})
coordinates we have

\partial_{t}\theta^{t}\approx\delta\mathcal{L}-\partial_{u}\theta^{u}-\partial_{i}\theta^{i}\,,

(109)

where “
\approx
” denotes an on-shell relation. We can show that in the limit
t\rightarrow+\infty
we have that
\theta^{t}
admits, as already observed in
Romoli (2024)
for the
p=2
case in
D=4
, a general structure. Hence we have the following general structure for
\theta^{t}

\theta^{t}=\theta^{t}_{(0)}(u,t,\{x^{i}\})+\sum_{n=1}^{N}t^{n}\theta^{t}_{(n)}(u,\{x^{i}\})\,,

(110)

with
\theta^{t}_{(0)}(u,t,\{x^{i}\})
such that
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\theta^{t}_{(0)}(u,t,\{x^{i}\})=\theta^{t}_{(0)}(u,\{x^{i}\})
.
Indeed, the presymplectic potential is given by

\displaystyle\theta^{t}

\displaystyle=\theta^{u}+\theta^{r}=r^{D-2}\frac{|\gamma|}{p!}(H^{u\mu_{1}...\mu_{p}}+H^{r\mu_{1}...\mu_{p}})\delta{\cal B}_{\mu_{1}...\mu_{p}}

(111)

\displaystyle=\frac{|\gamma|}{r^{p}}\left[r^{2}{\prod_{k=1}^{p-1}\gamma^{i_{k}j_{k}}}H_{uri_{1}...i_{p-1}}(\delta{\cal B}_{uj_{1}...j_{p-1}}-\delta{\cal B}_{rj_{1}...j_{p-1}})-{\prod_{k=1}^{p}\gamma^{i_{k}j_{k}}}H_{ui_{1}...i_{p}}\delta{\cal B}_{j_{1}...j_{p}}\right]\,,

where
|\gamma|:=\text{det}(\gamma_{ij})
. The field strength components admit a power expansion with falloffs (
38
)

H_{uri_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}={\cal O}\left(r^{\tfrac{p-4}{2}}\right),\qquad H_{ri_{1}\dots i_{p}}={\cal O}\left(r^{\tfrac{p-2}{2}}\right),\qquad H_{ui_{1}\dots i_{p}}={\cal O}\left(r^{\tfrac{p}{2}}\right)\,.

(112)

Moreover, the variation of the
p
-form field is

\delta{\cal B}_{\mu_{1}...\mu_{p}}=\delta B_{\mu_{1}...\mu_{p}}-\partial_{[\mu_{1}}\delta b_{\mu_{2}...\mu_{p}]}

(113)

with

\begin{split}&B_{i_{1}\dots i_{p}}={\cal O}\left(r^{\tfrac{p}{2}}\right)\,,\;\;\;\qquad\qquad B_{ri_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}={\cal O}\left(r^{\tfrac{p-4}{2}}\right)\,,\\
&B_{ui_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}={\cal O}\left(r^{\tfrac{p-2}{2}}\right)\,,\qquad B_{uri_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}={\cal O}\left(r^{\tfrac{p-6}{2}}\right)\,,\end{split}

(114)

with a power expansion, and
b
terms admitting a polyhomogenoues expansion of the form (
47
). Inserting them into (
111
) we have three kind of contributions. The first one is given, schematically, by

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{r^{p}}H_{ui_{1}...i_{p}}\delta B_{j_{1}...j_{p}}={\cal O}(r^{0})\,,\\
&\frac{1}{r^{p-2}}H_{uri_{1}...i_{p-1}}\delta B_{uj_{1}...j_{p-1}}={\cal O}(r^{-1})\,,\\
&\frac{1}{r^{p-2}}H_{uri_{1}...i_{p-1}}\delta B_{rj_{1}...j_{p-1}}={\cal O}(r^{-2})\,.\end{split}

(115)

The second kind of terms are, schematically

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{r^{p}}H_{ui_{1}...i_{p}}\partial_{[j_{1}}\delta b_{j_{2}...j_{p}]}={\cal O}(r^{N})\,,\\
&\frac{1}{r^{p-2}}H_{uri_{1}...i_{p-1}}\partial_{[u}b_{j_{1}...j_{p-1}]}={\cal O}(r^{N-1})\,,\\
&\frac{1}{r^{p-2}}H_{uri_{1}...i_{p-1}}\partial_{[r}b_{j_{1}...j_{p-1}]}={\cal O}(r^{N-1})\,.\end{split}

(116)

The last kind of terms are those containing logarithms which are, schematically

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{r^{p}}H_{ui_{1}...i_{p}}\partial_{[j_{1}}\delta\hat{b}_{j_{2}...j_{p}]}={\cal O}(\tfrac{\ln(r)}{r})\,,\\
&\frac{1}{r^{p-2}}H_{uri_{1}...i_{p-1}}\partial_{[u}\hat{b}_{j_{1}...j_{p-1}]}={\cal O}(\tfrac{\ln(r)}{r})\,,\\
&\frac{1}{r^{p-2}}H_{uri_{1}...i_{p-1}}\partial_{[r}\hat{b}_{j_{1}...j_{p-1}]}={\cal O}(\tfrac{\ln(r)}{r^{2}})\,.\end{split}

(117)

Hence, logarithmic terms are vanishing in the
t\rightarrow+\infty
limit and can be collected into
\theta^{t}_{(0)}(u,t,\{x^{i}\})
together with the
{\cal O}(1)
terms and other possible vanishing terms. In this way we have

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\theta^{t}_{(0)}(u,t,\{x^{i}\})=\theta^{t}_{(0)}(u,\{x^{i}\})\,.

(118)

The term
\theta^{t}_{(0)}(u,\{x^{i}\})
receives contributions from the first of (
115
) and from (
116
). In the case
N=0
, to which we refer as “ordinary” asymptotic symmetries, we have simply

\theta^{t}_{(0)}(u,\{x^{i}\}):=|\gamma|{\prod_{k=1}^{p}\gamma^{i_{k}j_{k}}}\partial_{u}B^{(-\tfrac{p}{2})}_{i_{1}...i_{p}}\delta{\cal B}^{(-\tfrac{p}{2})}_{j_{1}...j_{p}}\qquad\text{ for }N=0.

(119)

The equation (
109
), implies that
\theta_{(n)}^{t}(u,\{x^{i}\})
with
n>0
is an ambiguity that is given by

\theta_{(n)}^{t}(u,\{x^{i}\})=\frac{1}{n}\left(\delta\mathcal{L}_{(n-1)}-\partial_{u}\theta^{u}_{(n-1)}-\partial_{i}\theta^{i}_{(n-1)}\right)\,.

(120)

From the above equation, we can identify

\displaystyle\Upsilon^{tu}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{t^{n}}{n}\theta_{(n-1)}^{u}+\Upsilon^{tu}_{(0)}\,,

(121)

\displaystyle\Upsilon^{ti}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{t^{n}}{n}\theta_{(n-1)}^{i}+\Upsilon^{ti}_{(0)}\,,

\displaystyle\Xi^{t}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}-\frac{t^{n}}{n}\mathcal{L}_{(n-1)}+\Xi^{t}_{(0)}\,,

where
\Upsilon^{tu}_{(0)},\Upsilon^{ti}_{(0)},\Xi^{t}_{(0)}
are free functions with a well-defined behaviour in the
t\rightarrow+\infty
limit. Therefore we have

\theta^{t}\sim_{\text{eq}}\theta^{t}+\partial_{u}\Upsilon^{tu}_{(0)}+\partial_{i}\Upsilon^{ti}_{(0)}+\delta\Xi^{t}_{(0)}\,,

(122)

which means that only the
t^{0}
finite order contributes to the charge in the
t\rightarrow+\infty
limit.

The next issue is to address the
u
-divergences that arise from the
du
integration. These divergences are cured using
\Upsilon^{t\mu}_{(0)}
. As first step we can show that
\theta^{t}_{(0)}
admits the general structure

\theta^{t}_{(0)}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\theta_{(0,n)}^{t}u^{n-2}+\Theta_{0}^{t}(u,\{x^{i}\})\,,

(123)

where
\Theta_{0}^{t}(u,\{x^{i}\})
is the only
u
-integrable part of
\theta^{t}_{(0)}
. It is enough to show the statement for the
N=0
case since if
N>0
we are just adding positive powers of
u
to the field expansions. From (
119
) we have

\theta^{t}_{(0)}(u,\{x^{i}\}):=|\gamma|{\prod_{k=1}^{p}\gamma^{i_{k}j_{k}}}\partial_{u}B^{(-\tfrac{p}{2})}_{i_{1}...i_{p}}\delta B^{(-\tfrac{p}{2})}_{j_{1}...j_{p}},

(124)

while from (
46
) we have

\displaystyle B^{(-\tfrac{p}{2})}_{j_{1}...j_{p}}=B^{(-\tfrac{p}{2},0)}_{j_{1}...j_{p}}+{\cal O}(u^{-1})\,,

(125)

\displaystyle\partial_{u}B^{(-\tfrac{p}{2})}_{i_{1}...i_{p}}={\cal O}(u^{-2})\,.

Therefore

\theta^{t}_{(0)}(u,\{x^{i}\})={\cal O}(u^{-2}).

(126)

We now use
\Upsilon^{t\mu}_{(0)}
to cancel the first series of
\theta_{(0)}^{t}
. To do so we can employ, for instance, these expansions

\Upsilon^{tu}_{(0)}=\sum_{n=3}^{N+1}u^{n-2}\Upsilon^{tu}_{(0,n)},\qquad\Upsilon^{ti}_{(0)}=\frac{1}{u}\Upsilon^{ti}_{(0,-1)}

(127)

where

\Upsilon^{tu}_{(0,n)}=-\frac{\theta^{t}_{(0,n+1)}}{n-2},\qquad\partial_{i}\Upsilon^{ti}_{(0,-1)}=-\theta^{t}_{0,1}.

(128)

Here we are assuming that
\Upsilon^{tu}_{(0)}
has a
u
-power expansion, therefore it is impossible to cancel a term of the type
\frac{1}{u}
with a
u
-derivative. To our understanding, this choice is not unique and the renormalization procedure depends on the specific structure of the renormalizing terms. We underline that in showing (
123
) the fundamental assumption is the expansion (
46
) for the free Cauchy data at order
{\cal O}\left(r^{\tfrac{p}{2}}\right)
. This is a power-law Laurent series which does not contain logarithmic terms. Such an expansion can also be considered and would lead to some differences in the renormalization procedure that we can investigate in the future.

In the end, this process leaves us with well-defined quantities, thus showing that it is possible to renormalize the presymplectic potential to make the asymptotic charge finite while accommodating arbitrary powers of the asymptotic parameter. Reminding equation (
33
) we are able to write the asymptotic charge as an integral over
{\cal I}^{+}_{-}
and, specifically, it is given by the
t-
and
u-
independent parts of the Noether two-form component
\kappa^{tu}

Q_{B}=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\,\int_{{\cal I}^{+}_{-}}d\Omega_{D-2}\;\epsilon^{\prime\big(-\tfrac{p}{2}-k,0\big)}\Delta H_{ur}^{\prime\big(\tfrac{4-p}{2}+k,0\big)}\,.

(129)

5
Algebra of charges

In our problem, the only sector of the gauge parameter that enters the charges is the scalar

\epsilon^{\prime}
in the Hodge decomposition on the celestial sphere. The renormalized charges
organize into a tower of
N+1
contributions

\{Q^{(0)},\dots,Q^{(N)}\}
. Their universal form is

Q_{B}\;=\;\sum_{k=0}^{N}Q^{(k)}\,,\qquad Q^{(k)}\;=\;\int_{\mathcal{I}^{+}_{-}}d\Omega_{D-2}\,\epsilon^{\prime\left(-\tfrac{p}{2}-k,\,0\right)}(\{x^{i}\})\;\Delta\,H_{ur}^{\prime\left(\tfrac{4-p}{2}+k,\,0\right)}(\{x^{i}\})\,,

(130)

The expression follows from the covariant definition of the charge together with the
renormalization procedure that removes divergences.

We define the bracket between two charges via the presymplectic form
\Omega
on the
solution space, imposing
i_{\epsilon}\Omega=\delta Q[\epsilon]
. Then, for two
parameters
\epsilon,\eta
, the bracket reads

\{Q[\epsilon],Q[\eta]\}\;:=\;\delta_{\eta}Q[\epsilon]\;=\;\int_{\mathcal{I}^{+}_{-}}d\Omega_{D-2}\,\epsilon^{\prime}\,\Delta\,(\delta_{\eta}H^{\prime}_{ur})\,,

(131)

since
\epsilon^{\prime}
is an independent parameter that does not vary under
\delta_{\eta}

and the measure is invariant. The field strength,
H=dB
is gauge-invariant and so every components; furthermore the component
H_{uri_{1}...i_{p-1}}
admits the Hodge decomposition (
15
) which in coordinates reads as in (
59
). Imposing gauge invariance returns that
H^{\prime}_{ur}
is also gauge invariant while the gauge transformation of the form appearing in the exact part coincide with the exterior derivative of the gauge parameter.
Therefore, using (
130
) we get

0\;=\;\{Q_{B}[\epsilon],Q_{B}[\eta]\}\;=\;\left\{\sum_{k=0}^{N}Q_{B}^{(k)}[\epsilon],\,\sum_{k^{\prime}=0}^{N}Q_{B}^{(k^{\prime})}[\eta]\right\}\;=\;\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=0}^{N}\{Q_{B}^{(k)}[\epsilon],\,Q_{B}^{(k^{\prime})}[\eta]\}\,.

(132)

Therefore, the global identity translates into a hierarchy of conditions
among the different orders
k,k^{\prime}
.
Since we are expanding the charges in a power series we can collect terms by degree

0\;=\;\sum_{k^{\prime\prime}=0}^{2N}\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k+k^{\prime}=k^{\prime\prime}\end{subarray}}\{Q_{B}^{(k)}[\epsilon],\,Q_{B}^{(k^{\prime})}[\eta]\}\,.

(133)

This means that, for each degree
k^{\prime\prime}
, we have the condition

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k+k^{\prime}=k^{\prime\prime}\end{subarray}}\{Q_{B}^{(k)}[\epsilon],\,Q_{B}^{(k^{\prime})}[\eta]\}\;=\;0\,.

(134)

Therefore, asymptotic symmetries do not indefinitely multiply the number of degrees of freedom; rather, they organize themselves into a tower of modes that are interconnected. Moreover, the abelian property is the analogue, for the
O(r^{N})
tower, of the well-known abelian
structure of large gauge symmetries in electrodynamics, now extended to
p
-forms and to
the full hierarchy in
r
. Possible central extensions of the charge algebra can arise due to two mechanisms:

•

magnetic/dual sectors. There always exist two types of charges: the electric ones and the magnetic ones. Considering also the magnetic sector or mixed electric/magnetic sectors, can produce central extensions in the charge algebra. Thus, even if the underlying group is abelian, the set of charges
\{Q_{E}[\epsilon],Q_{M}[\eta]\}
may realize a central extension, as in the classical Kac–Moody cases. The magnetic charges can be constructed following the same reasoning of the electric ones, schematically

\tilde{Q}_{B}\propto\int_{\mathcal{I}^{+}_{-}}d\Omega_{p}\,\tilde{\epsilon}\,\Delta\!\Big[(\star d\phi)_{ri_{1}\cdots i_{p}}\,\gamma^{i_{1}j_{1}}\cdots\gamma^{i_{p}j_{p}}\,\epsilon_{j_{1}\cdots j_{p}}\Big]\,.

(135)

where the magnetic "gauge" parameter is nothing but a harmonic function on the celestial sphere in order to not spoil the chosen fall-offs. In the mixed electric/magnetic sector, the gauge variation is not necessarily zero, as it was instead for the purely electric sector, which implies the presence of a central extension in the case in which the variation does not depend on the fields and a field-dependent extension otherwise;

•

Renormalization ambiguities. The presymplectic formalism admits local ambiguities that are fixed fixed by imposing renormalization conditions.
However, they are not uniquely determined and different choices lead to charge definitions
that can differ by surface terms. If two charges
Q[\epsilon]
and
Q[\eta]
differ by non-trivial surface terms, their bracket may acquire a constant contribution realizing a central extension. Since we have not used the finite term
\Xi^{(0)}
in the renormalization procedure, this could be lead to possible central extension of the charge algebra.

6
Conclusions

In this work, we studied higher-order asymptotic symmetries for
p
-forms in
D=p+2
, which are on-shell duals to a scalar. Indeed, by means of the Hodge decomposition, we were able to reduce the complexity of the problem to a scalar one, expressing the asymptotic charges in terms of a scalar parameter times a
ur
component of a field strength. Surprisingly, up to some overall factors this problem turns out to be
p
-independent. Furthermore, let us remark that, from a dual perspective, this is the first derivation of any scalar asymptotic charges in an odd spacetime dimension and of higher-order scalar asymptotic charges in any spacetime dimension different from
D=4
. In fact, by means of symplectic renormalization, we were able to understand the divergences of the asymptotic charges in terms of ambiguities of the presymplectic potential. Consistently with the other analyses in Lorenz gauge, both
t
- and
u
-divergences were addressed. Along the way, we have shown that the presymplectic potential admits a general structure independent of the specific
p
-form theory. This could also be valid in the case of mixed symmetry tensors and it is a possible direction for the future.

This approach suggests the possibility of discovering an infinite set of asymptotic charges, broadly meaning an infinite number of conserved charges. We stress, however, two points. First, as noted in
Francia and Manzoni (2024)
, in sufficiently high dimension (surely greater than
D=p+2
) it seems to be possible to perform a gauge-for-gauge fixing to cut off the series of the pure gauge terms to start with the order
{\cal O}\left(r^{{(D-2p-2)}/{2}}\right)
. However, the dimension needed to perform this gauge-for-gauge fixing grows proportionally with the (higher) order of the gauge parameter expansion. To derive this result, it is necessary the use of a theorem that characterises the solutions of the harmonic-divgrad system (a system of PDEs needed to discuss gauge-for-gauge fixing) on the sphere
Manzoni (2025b)
. Second, it is crucial to emphasise how we used a specific prescription: to cancel the divergences by means of ambiguities while leaving unaltered the finite parts, which enter in the definition of asymptotic charges. However, it is clear that there are residual ambiguities that might further constraint the number of physical asymptotic charges. In particular, we note that we did not use the residual term
\delta\Xi_{(0)}^{t}
. In this perspective could be possible, appropriately modifying the procedure, to renormalize the divergent asymptotic charges appearing in
Francia and Manzoni (2024)
associated to Coulomb falloffs of the field. Furthermore, we compute the charge algebra which results in an abelian structure generalizing the case of Maxwell fields. The possibility of central extensions lies in the mixed electric/magnetic sector and/or due to the ambiguities of the presymplectic potential.

An enticing perspective regards the study of higher-order asymptotic symmetries in gravity, for which a full
{\cal O}(r^{N})
analysis is still missing. We seek to analyse also theories with higher-spin and mixed-symmetry tensor. Moreover, as
p
-forms are part of the string spectrum and
p
-form gauge theories emerge in the tensionless limit of string theory, the calculation of asymptotic charges and their renomalization may shed new light on the infrared limit of string theory and
D_{p}
-branes.

In addition to this, we would like to explore better the connection with soft theorems, which are not fully understood for
p
-forms. In this sense, the analysis for the duality with a scalar is appealing, since scalar theories represent the simplest models in physics. This connection could also help in understanding the potential role of the residual ambiguities that are left in the symplectic renormalization procedure, whose role is currently under several investigations.

Equations of motion

A.1
Gauge fields

Let us derive the equation of motion for a
p
-form gauge field in a
D=p+2
-dimensional spacetime in Lorenz gauge. We have

\displaystyle\Box{\cal B}_{uri_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}=

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{r}^{2}-2\partial_{u}\partial_{r}+\frac{p-4}{r}(\partial_{u}-\partial_{r})+\frac{\Delta-p}{r^{2}}\right]{\cal B}_{uri_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{r^{3}}D^{i}{\cal B}_{ui\,i_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}\,,

(136a)

\displaystyle\Box{\cal B}_{ui_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}=

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{r}^{2}-2\partial_{u}\partial_{r}+\frac{p-2}{r}(\partial_{u}-\partial_{r})+\frac{\Delta-(p-1)^{2}}{r^{2}}\right]{\cal B}_{ui_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}

\displaystyle\;+\frac{2}{r}\sum_{n=1}^{p-1}D_{i_{n}}{\cal B}_{ui_{1}\dots i_{n-1}\,r\,i_{n+1}\dots i_{p-1}}\,,

(136b)

\displaystyle\Box{\cal B}_{ri_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}=

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{r}^{2}-2\partial_{u}\partial_{r}+\frac{p-2}{r}(\partial_{u}-\partial_{r})+\frac{\Delta-1}{r^{2}}\right]{\cal B}_{ri_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}

\displaystyle\;-\frac{p-2}{r^{2}}{{\cal B}_{ui_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}}-\frac{2}{r^{3}}D^{i}{\cal B}_{ii_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}-\frac{2}{r}\sum_{n=1}^{p-1}D_{i_{n}}{\cal B}_{ri_{1}\dots i_{n-1}\,u\,i_{n+1}\dots i_{p-1}}\,,

(136c)

\displaystyle\Box{\cal B}_{i_{1}\dots i_{p}}=

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{r}^{2}-2\partial_{u}\partial_{r}+\frac{p}{r}(\partial_{u}-\partial_{r})+\frac{\Delta}{r^{2}}\right]{\cal B}_{i_{1}\dots i_{p}}

\displaystyle\;-\frac{2}{r}\sum_{n=1}^{p}D_{i_{n}}({\cal B}_{i_{1}\dots i_{n-1}\,u\,i_{n+1}\dots i_{p}}-{\cal B}_{i_{1}\dots i_{n-1}\,r\,i_{n+1}\dots i_{p}})\,,

(136d)

while the gauge condition
G_{{\mu_{1}}\dots{\mu_{p-1}}}:=\nabla^{\mu}{\cal B}_{\mu\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{p-1}}
reads

\displaystyle G_{uri_{1}\dots i_{p-3}}=\frac{1}{r^{2}}D^{i}B_{urii_{1}\dots i_{p-3}}\,,

(137a)

\displaystyle G_{ui_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}=\left(\partial_{u}-\partial_{r}+\frac{p-4}{r}\right)B_{uri_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}D^{i}B_{u\,i\,i_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}\,,

(137b)

\displaystyle G_{ri_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}=\left(-\partial_{r}+\frac{p-4}{r}\right){\cal B}_{uri_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}D^{i}B_{ri\,i_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}\,,

(137c)

\displaystyle G_{i_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}=\frac{1}{r^{2}}D^{i}{\cal B}_{i\,i_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}-\partial_{u}{\cal B}_{ri_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}-\left(\partial_{r}-\frac{p-2}{r}\right)({\cal B}_{ui_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}-{\cal B}_{ri_{1}\dots i_{p-1}})\,.

(137d)

Order by order, the equations for the physical components
B
of the gauge fields, which admit a power expansion, are

\displaystyle(-2n-p+6)\partial_{u}B_{uri_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}^{(n-1)}=

\displaystyle\big[\Delta+(n-2)(n+p-5)-p\big]B_{uri_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}^{(n-2)}

\displaystyle\;-2D^{i}B_{uii_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}^{(n-3)}\,,

(138a)

\displaystyle(-2n-p+4)\partial_{u}B_{ui_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}^{(n-1)}=

\displaystyle\big[\Delta+(n-2)(n+p-3)-(p-1)^{2}\big]B_{ui_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}^{(n-2)}

\displaystyle\;+2\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}D_{i_{k}}B_{ui_{1}\dots r\dots i_{p-1}}^{(n-1)}\,,

(138b)

\displaystyle(-2n-p+4)\partial_{u}B_{ri_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}^{(n-1)}=

\displaystyle\big[\Delta+(n-2)(n+p-3)-1\big]B_{ri_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}^{(n-2)}-(p-2)B_{ui_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}^{(n-2)}

\displaystyle\;-2D^{i}B_{ii_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}^{(n-3)}+2\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}D_{i_{k}}B_{ui_{1}\dots r\dots i_{p-1}}^{(n-1)}\,,

(138c)

\displaystyle(-2n-p+2)\partial_{u}B_{i_{1}\dots i_{p}}^{(n-1)}=

\displaystyle\big[\Delta+(n-2)(n+p-1)\big]B_{i_{1}\dots i_{p}}^{(n-2)}

\displaystyle-2\sum_{k=1}^{p}D_{i_{k}}(B_{i_{1}\dots i_{k-1}\,u\,i_{k+1}\dots i_{p}}^{(n-1)}-B_{i_{1}\dots i_{k-1}\,r\,i_{k+1}\dots i_{p}}^{(n-1)})\,.

(138d)

The order-by-order gauge condition reads

\displaystyle D^{i}B_{urii_{1}\dots i_{p-3}}^{(n)}=0

(139a)

\displaystyle\partial_{u}B_{uri_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}^{(n)}=-(n+p-5)B_{uri_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}^{(n-1)}+D^{i}B_{ui_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}^{(n-2)}

(139b)

\displaystyle(n+p-4)B_{uri_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}^{(n)}=D^{i}B_{ri_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}^{(n-1)}

(139c)

\displaystyle\partial_{u}B_{ri_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}^{(n)}=D^{i}B_{ii_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}^{(n-2)}+(n+p-3)\big(B_{ui_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}^{(n-1)}-B_{ri_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}^{(n-1)}\big)

(139d)

In the asymptotic charge, only
\epsilon_{i_{1}\dots i_{p}}
is relevant and, specifically, the only contribution comes from the
\epsilon^{\prime}
component in (
58
). Interestingly, that component completely decouples from the rest of the equations, reducing our problem to a scalar one, with the corresponding system being

\displaystyle(-2n-p+4)\partial_{u}\hat{\epsilon}^{\prime\,(n-1)}=\big[\Delta+(n-2)(n+p-3)\big]\hat{\epsilon}^{\prime\,(n-2)}

(140a)

\displaystyle(-2n-p+4)\partial_{u}\epsilon^{\prime\,(n-1)}+2\partial_{u}\hat{\epsilon}^{\prime\,(n-1)}=\big[\Delta+(n-2)(n+p-3)\big]\epsilon^{\prime\,(n-2)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad-(2n+p-3)\hat{\epsilon}^{\prime\,(n-2)}

(140b)

where we used the commutation relation

\Delta D^{i}\varphi=D^{i}(\Delta+D-3)\varphi\,.

(141)

The transversality condition does not contain
\epsilon^{\prime}
and therefore we do not need to discuss it. In determining the relevant field-strength component, we also need this commutation relation

\begin{split}\Delta D^{i}B_{rii_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}&=D^{i}(\Delta-D+2p-1)B_{rii_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}\,.\end{split}

(142)

Appendix B
Solutions of the EOM

B.1
Falloffs

We start with the standard radiation falloffs in
D=p+2

B_{i_{1}\dots i_{p}}={\cal O}\left(r^{\tfrac{p}{2}}\right),\;\;B_{ri_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}\,,\,B_{ui_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}={\cal O}\left(r^{\tfrac{p-2}{2}}\right),\quad B_{uri_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}={\cal O}\left(r^{\tfrac{p-4}{2}}\right)\,.

(143)

To leading order, the wave equation does not bring about any constraint, leaving all the leading terms with arbitrary angular dependence. The gauge condition, implies that

\begin{split}&D^{i}B_{urii_{1}\dots i_{p-3}}^{\big(\tfrac{4-p}{2}\big)}=0\,,\\
&\partial_{u}B_{uri_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}^{\big(\tfrac{2-p}{2}\big)}=0\,,\\
&(p-4)B_{uri_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}^{\big(\tfrac{4-p}{2}\big)}=2D^{i}B_{ri_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}^{\big(\tfrac{2-p}{2}\big)}\,,\\
&\partial_{u}B_{ri_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}^{\big(\tfrac{2-p}{2}\big)}=0\,.\end{split}

(144)

When studying the next-to-leading order equations, we find two constraints. In particular, the equations of motion give

\begin{split}-2\partial_{u}B_{uri_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}^{\big(\tfrac{6-p}{2}\big)}=&\left[\Delta-\frac{p^{2}-2p+8}{4}\right]B_{uri_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}^{\big(\tfrac{4-p}{2}\big)}-2D^{i}B_{uii_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}^{\big(\tfrac{2-p}{2}\big)}\,,\\
-2\partial_{u}B_{ri_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}^{\big(\tfrac{4-p}{2}\big)}=&\left[\Delta-\frac{p^{2}-2p-4}{4}\right]B_{ri_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}^{\big(\tfrac{2-p}{2}\big)}-2D^{i}B_{ii_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}^{\big(-\tfrac{p}{2}\big)}\\
&-(p-2)B_{ui_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}^{\big(\tfrac{2-p}{2}\big)}+2\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}D_{i_{k}}B_{ui_{1}\dots r\dots i_{p-1}}^{\big(\tfrac{4-p}{2}\big)}\,.\end{split}

(145)

From the gauge condition, we read

\begin{split}&\partial_{u}B_{uri_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}^{\big(\tfrac{6-p}{2}\big)}=\frac{4-p}{2}B_{uri_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}^{\big(\tfrac{4-p}{2}\big)}+D^{i}B_{ui_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}^{\big(\tfrac{2-p}{2}\big)}\,,\\
&(n+p-4)B_{uri_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}^{(n)}=D^{i}B_{ri_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}^{(n-1)}\,,\\
&\partial_{u}B_{ri_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}^{\big(\tfrac{4-p}{2}\big)}=D^{i}B_{ii_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}^{\big(-\tfrac{p}{2}\big)}+\frac{p-2}{2}\big(B_{ui_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}^{\big(\tfrac{2-p}{2}\big)}-B_{ri_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}^{\big(\tfrac{2-p}{2}\big)}\big)\,.\end{split}

(146)

From the first equations of each set, we find

\left[\Delta-\frac{p(p-2)}{4}\right]B_{uri_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}^{\big(\tfrac{D-2p+2}{2}\big)}=0\,,

(147)

which implies that the leading order
B_{uri_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}^{\big(\tfrac{4-p}{2}\big)}
does not have an arbitrary angular dependence and, specifically, it is a constant term for
D=4
and there is no solution for
D>4
, since the two-sphere Laplacian is negative semidefinite. Hence, we consistently consider

B_{uri_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}^{\big(\tfrac{4-p}{2}\big)}=0\,.

(148)

Substituting this condition, we find

D^{i}B_{rii_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}^{\big(\tfrac{2-p}{2}\big)}=0\,.

(149)

Performing the same calculation for the second equations of each set, we find

\left[\Delta-\frac{p^{2}+2p-4}{4}\right]B_{ri_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}^{\big(\tfrac{2-p}{2}\big)}=0\,,

(150)

which tells us that

\begin{split}&B_{rii_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}^{\big(\tfrac{2-p}{2}\big)}=0\,.\end{split}

(151)

The correct falloff in Lorenz gauge are thus

\begin{split}&B_{i_{1}\dots i_{p}}={\cal O}\left(r^{\tfrac{p}{2}}\right)\,,\;\,\quad\qquad B_{ri_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}={\cal O}\left(r^{\tfrac{p-4}{2}}\right)\,,\\
&B_{ui_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}={\cal O}\left(r^{\tfrac{p-2}{2}}\right)\,,\qquad B_{uri_{1}\dots i_{p-2}}={\cal O}\left(r^{\tfrac{p-6}{2}}\right)\,.\end{split}

(152)

B.2
Boundary small gauge fixing and field strength

Our aim is to determine the order-by-order expression of the relevant field strength component in terms of the Cauchy data. To achieve it, we perform a further gauge fixing, employing some small residual gauge freedom. The relevant component of the field strength is

H_{uri_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}^{(n)}=\partial_{u}B_{ri_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}^{(n)}+(-1)^{p}(n-1)B_{ui_{1}\dots i_{p-1}}^{(n-1)}+\sum_{n=1}^{p-1}D_{i_{n}}B_{i_{n+1}\dots uri_{1}\dots i_{n-1}}^{(n)}\,.

(153)

Specifically, the quantity that enters in the charge is

H_{ur}^{\prime(n)}=\partial_{u}B_{r}^{\prime(n)}+(-1)^{p}(n-1)B_{u}^{\prime(n-1)}\,,

(154)

and, from the gauge condition, we find

\partial_{u}B_{r}^{\prime(n)}=(-1)^{p-1}B^{(n-2)}+(n+p-3)(B_{u}^{\prime(n-1)}-B_{r}^{\prime(n-1)})\,,

(155)

which gives

H_{ur}^{\prime(n)}=(-1)^{p-1}B^{(n-2)}+(n+p-3)(B_{u}^{\prime(n-1)}-B_{r}^{\prime(n-1)})+(-1)^{p}(n-1)B_{u}^{\prime(n-1)}\,,

(156)

At the same time, from the equations of motion we read

(-2n-p+2)\partial_{u}B_{r}^{\prime(n)}=\big[\Delta+n(n+p-3)\big]B_{r}^{\prime(n-1)}-(p-2)B_{u}^{\prime(n-1)}+(-1)^{p}2B^{(n-2)}\,.

(157)

Combining this with (
155
), we obtain

\begin{split}&\left[\Delta-(n+p-1)(n+p-2)\right]B_{r}^{\prime(n)}+(-1)^{p-1}{(2n+p-2)}B^{(n-1)}\\
&+(n+p-1)(2n+p-2)B_{u}^{\prime(n)}=0\,.\end{split}

(158)

For
n=\tfrac{2-p}{2}
, this equations gives exactly (
151
). For
n>\tfrac{2-p}{2}
, we obtain

\begin{split}B_{u}^{\prime(n)}=&\frac{(-1)^{p}}{n+p-1}B^{(n-1)}-\left[\frac{\Delta}{(n+p-1)(2n+p-2)}-\frac{n+p-2}{2n+p-1}\right]B_{r}^{\prime(n)}\,.\end{split}

(159)

The asymptotic charge is given by

Q_{B}=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\,\int_{{\cal I}^{+}_{-}}d\Omega_{D-2}\;\epsilon^{\prime\big(-\tfrac{p}{2}-k,0\big)}\Delta H_{ur}^{\prime\big(\tfrac{4-p}{2}+k,0\big)}\,.

(160)

In order to express
H_{ur}^{\prime(n,0)}
in terms of the standard Cauchy data
B
, we perform a small gauge fixing at the boundary

B^{\prime(n,0)}_{r}=0\,,

(161)

for all
n\geq\tfrac{4-p}{2}
using the free terms of the subleading gauge parameters
\epsilon^{(n,0)}
with
n>\tfrac{4-p}{2}
and
n\neq 0
. The gauge transformation of
B^{\prime(n,0)}_{r}
depends only on the
\epsilon^{\prime}
part of the Hodge decomposition of the gauge parameter. Indeed by using the Hodge decompositions of
B_{ri_{1}...i_{p-1}}
and
\epsilon_{i_{1}...i_{p-1}}
, by comparing with the terms in
\delta_{\epsilon}B_{ri_{1}...i_{p-1}}
we get

\delta_{\epsilon}B^{\prime(n,0)}_{r}=-(n-1)\epsilon^{\prime(n-1,0)}\,.

(162)

The parameters appearing in (
162
) are subleading with those in the charge since
\frac{2-p}{2}+k>-\frac{p}{2}-k
for every
k\geq 0
. Therefore they do not contribute to the charge (
160
) and are associated to a vanishing charge. The gauge fixing (
161
) can be achieved for every
n
except
n=1
, as observed also in
Romoli (2024)
, which occurs only for even
p
. In practice, we cannot gauge away
B^{\prime(1,0)}_{r}
, which is the natural Cauchy data at that specific order. However, we can gauge away
B^{(0,0)}
using the residual free
\epsilon^{(0,0)}
gauge parameter.

In the end, we find

\begin{split}H_{ur}^{\prime\big(\tfrac{4-p}{2},0\big)}&=(-1)^{p-1}B^{(-{p}/{2},0)}\,,\\
H_{ur}^{\prime(2,0)}\quad\;&=\frac{\Delta}{p}B_{r}^{\prime(1,0)}\,\,\,\,\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \text{if}\ p\in 2\mathbb{N}\,,\\
H_{ur}^{\prime(n,0)}\quad\;&=\frac{(-1)^{p-1}+n-1}{n+p-2}B^{(n-2,0)}\qquad n\neq\tfrac{4-p}{2}\;,\end{split}

(163)

where the second equation holds only for even
p
.

Long time smooth solutions of 2-D quadratic quasilinear wave equations in exterior domains with Neumann boundary conditions
Fei Hou, Huicheng Yin, Meng Yuan [12pt] 1. School of Mathematics, Nanjing University, Nanjing, 210093, China2. School of Mathematical Sciences, Nanjing Normal University, Nanjing, 210023, China3. School of Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence, Aliyun School of Big Data,School of Software, Changzhou University, Changzhou, 213164, China

Abstract
For the 3-D quadratic quasilinear wave equations in exterior domains with Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions, the global existence or the maximal existence time of small data smooth solutions have been established in the past. However, so far it is still open for the corresponding 2-D Neumann boundary value problem. In this paper, we investigate the long time existence of small data solutions to 2-D quadratic quasilinear wave equations with homogeneous Neumann boundary values. Our main ingredients include: establishing some new pointwise spacetime decay estimates for the 2-D initial boundary value problem of the divergence form wave equations, and introducing a series of good unknowns to derive the required energy estimates. The obtained results can be directly applied to the initial boundary value problem of 2-D isentropic and irrotational compressible Euler equations for both the polytropic gases and the Chaplygin gases in exterior domains with impermeable conditions, the 2-D relativistic membrane equations and 2-D membrane equations with homogeneous Neumann boundary values. Keywords. 2-D quadratic quasilinear wave equation, Neumann boundary condition, good unknown, weighted energy estimate, compressible Euler equations, relativistic membrane equation 2020 Mathematical Subject Classification. 35L05, 35L20, 35L70

Introduction

1.1
Main results and remarks

In this paper, we are concerned with the following IBVP (initial boundary value problem) of 2-D quadratic
quasilinear wave equations in exterior domains with homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Box u=Q(\partial u,\partial^{2}u),\hskip 73.97733pt(t,x)\in[0,+\infty)\times\mathcal{K},\\
&\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{n}}u(t,x)=0,\hskip 91.04872pt(t,x)\in[0,+\infty)\times\partial\mathcal{K},\\
&(u,\partial_{t}u)(0,x)=(\varepsilon u_{0},\varepsilon u_{1})(x),\qquad x\in\mathcal{K},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
\partial=(\partial_{0},\partial_{1},\partial_{2})=(\partial_{t},\partial_{x_{1}},\partial_{x_{2}})
,
\displaystyle\Box=\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta
with
\Delta=\partial_{1}^{2}+\partial_{2}^{2}
,
\mathcal{K}=\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2}\setminus\mathcal{O}
,
the obstacle
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2}
is compact and contains the origin, the boundary
\partial\mathcal{K}=\partial\mathcal{O}
is smooth,
\mathbf{n}=(0,n_{1},n_{2})=(0,n_{1}(x),n_{2}(x))
represents the unit outer normal direction of the boundary
[0,+\infty)\times\partial\mathcal{K}
,

(u_{0},u_{1})\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{K})
and
\operatorname{supp}(u_{0},u_{1})\subset\{x\in\mathcal{K}:|x|\leq M_{0}\}
with some fixed constant
M_{0}>1
.
In addition,

\begin{split}Q(\partial u,\partial^{2}u)&=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\mu=0}^{2}Q^{\alpha\beta\mu}\partial^{2}_{\alpha\beta}u\partial_{\mu}u+\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\mu,\nu=0}^{2}Q^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\partial^{2}_{\alpha\beta}u\partial_{\mu}u\partial_{\nu}u,\end{split}

(1.2)

where the constants
Q^{\alpha\beta\mu}=Q^{\beta\alpha\mu}
and
Q^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}=Q^{\beta\alpha\mu\nu}=Q^{\alpha\beta\nu\mu}
.
On the other hand, as in condition (1.8) of
[
38
]
, the following admissible condition on
Q(\partial u,\partial^{2}u)
for Neumann boundary
value problem (
1.1
) is naturally imposed:

\begin{split}&\text{For any $C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})$ smooth functions $w$ and $\psi$ verifying $\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{n}}w=\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{n}}\psi=0$}\\
&\text{on $[0,+\infty)\times\partial\mathcal{K}$, it holds that on $[0,+\infty)\times\partial\mathcal{K}$},\\
&{\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\mu=0}^{2}Q^{\alpha\beta\mu}n_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}w\partial_{\mu}\psi=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\mu,\nu=0}^{2}Q^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}n_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}w\partial_{\mu}\psi\partial_{\nu}\psi\equiv 0.}\end{split}

(1.3)

As usual, in order to find the smooth solutions of problem (
1.1
), the compatibility conditions of
(u_{0},u_{1})

with
\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{n}}u(t,x)=0
for
(t,x)\in[0,+\infty)\times\partial\mathcal{K}
are necessary. That is,
let
J_{k}u=\{\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u:0\leq|\alpha|\leq k\}

and
\partial_{t}^{k}u(0,x)=G_{k}(J_{k}u_{0},J_{k-1}u_{1})
(
0\leq k\leq 2N
), where
G_{k}
depends on
Q(\partial u,\partial^{2}u)
,
J_{k}u_{0}
and
J_{k-1}u_{1}

in terms of the equation and the initial data in problem (
1.1
),
then the compatibility conditions up to
(2N+1)-
order mean that

\begin{split}&\text{$\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{n}}G_{k}$ vanish on $\partial\mathcal{K}$ for all $0\leq k\leq 2N$.}\end{split}

(1.4)

Our main results can be stated as

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that the obstacle
\mathcal{O}
is convex and (
1.2
)-(
1.4
) hold.
Then for any fixed constant
\delta\in(0,1)
, there is a constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that when
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
and

\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2N+1}(\mathcal{K})}+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{2N}(\mathcal{K})}\leq 1\quad\text{with $N\geq 19$},

(1.5)

problem (
1.1
) has a smooth solution
u\in\bigcap\limits_{j=0}^{2N+1}C^{j}([0,\varepsilon^{\delta-2}],H^{2N+1-j}(\mathcal{K}))
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that the obstacle
\mathcal{O}
is convex and (
1.2
)-(
1.4
) hold. Especially,

Q(\partial u,\partial^{2}u)
admits the following form

\begin{split}Q(\partial u,\partial^{2}u)&=2\sum_{\alpha=0}^{2}C^{\alpha}Q_{0}(\partial_{\alpha}u,u)+\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\mu,\nu=0}^{2}Q^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\partial^{2}_{\alpha\beta}u\partial_{\mu}u\partial_{\nu}u,\\
\end{split}

(1.6)

where
C^{\alpha}
,
Q^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}
are constants and
\displaystyle Q_{0}(f,h)=\partial_{t}f\partial_{t}h-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\partial_{i}f\partial_{i}h
.
Meanwhile, the second null condition holds:

\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\mu,\nu=0}^{2}Q^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\xi_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}\xi_{\mu}\xi_{\nu}\equiv 0\quad\text{for any $(\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\{\pm 1\}\times\mathop{\mathbb{S}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits$}.

(1.7)

Then there is a constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that when
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
and

\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2N+1}(\mathcal{K})}+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{2N}(\mathcal{K})}\leq 1\quad\text{with $N\geq 40$},

(1.8)

problem (
1.1
) has a global solution
u\in\bigcap\limits_{j=0}^{2N+1}C^{j}([0,\infty),H^{2N+1-j}(\mathcal{K}))
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle\sum_{|a|\leq N+1}|\partial Z^{a}u|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\langle{x}\rangle^{-1/2}\langle{t-|x|}\rangle^{-1}\ln^{2}(2+t),

(1.9a)

\displaystyle\sum_{|a|\leq N+1}\sum_{i=1}^{2}|\bar{\partial}_{i}Z^{a}u|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\langle{x}\rangle^{-1/2}\langle{t+|x|}\rangle^{0.001-1},

(1.9b)

\displaystyle\sum_{|a|\leq N+1}|Z^{a}u|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\langle{t+|x|}\rangle^{-1/2}\langle{t-|x|}\rangle^{0.001-1/2},

(1.9c)

\displaystyle|\partial\partial_{t}u|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\langle{x}\rangle^{-1/2}\langle{t-|x|}\rangle^{-1},

(1.9d)

where
Z=\{\partial,\Omega\}
with
\Omega=x_{1}\partial_{2}-x_{2}\partial_{1}
,
\langle{x}\rangle=\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}}
,
\bar{\partial}_{i}=\frac{x_{i}}{|x|}\partial_{t}+\partial_{i}
(
i=1,2
)
are the good derivatives (tangent to the outgoing light cone
|x|=t
).
Furthermore, the following time decay estimates of local energy hold

\displaystyle\sum_{|a|\leq 2N-29}\|\partial^{a}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{R})}\leq C_{R}\varepsilon(1+t)^{-1}\ln(2+t),

(1.10a)

\displaystyle\sum_{|a|\leq 2N-30}\|\partial^{a}\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{R})}\leq C_{R}\varepsilon(1+t)^{-1},

(1.10b)

where
R>1
is a fixed constant,
\mathcal{K}_{R}=\mathcal{K}\cap\{x:|x|\leq R\}
and
C_{R}>0
is a constant
depending on
R
.

Remark 1.1
.

For the Cauchy problem of the 2-D quasilinear wave equation in (
1.1
) without the first null condition
(i.e.,
\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\mu=0}^{2}Q^{\alpha\beta\mu}\xi_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}\xi_{\mu}\not\equiv 0
for

(\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\{\pm 1\}\times\mathop{\mathbb{S}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits
), the lifespan
T_{\varepsilon}
(maximal existence time) of the smooth solution fulfills
T_{\varepsilon}\geq C/\varepsilon^{2}
for
some suitable constant
C>0
(see Chapter 6 of
[
14
]
).
Note that the lifespan
T_{\varepsilon}
of the 2-D Cauchy-Neumann problem in Theorem
1.1
satisfies
T_{\varepsilon}\geq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2-}}
,
which is only arbitrarily close to that of the Cauchy problem.
In our forthcoming paper, we intend to show that the lifespan
T_{\varepsilon}
of the 2-D quadratic quasilinear wave equation in exterior domain
with both the Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions is at least
C/\varepsilon^{2}
by constructing delicate approximate solutions and establishing
some more precise energy estimates.

1.2
Applications of main results

1.2.1
Application to 2-D isentropic
and irrotational compressible Euler equations

As the first application of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, we now derive the lifespan
of smooth solutions to 2-D isentropic
and irrotational compressible Euler equations for both the polytropic gases and the Chaplygin
gases in exterior domains with impermeable boundary conditions and small perturbed initial data.

The 2-D compressible isentropic Euler equations are

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+\operatorname{div}(\rho v)=0\hskip 85.35826pt\text{(Conservation of mass)},\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho v)+\operatorname{div}(\rho v\otimes v)+\nabla p=0\hskip 25.6073pt\text{(Conservation of momentum)},\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(1.20)

where
(t,x)=(t,x_{1},x_{2})
,
\nabla=(\partial_{1},\partial_{2})=(\partial_{x_{1}},\partial_{x_{2}})
,
and
v=(v_{1},v_{2}),~\rho,~p
stand for the velocity, density, pressure, respectively.
In addition, the pressure
p=p(\rho)
is a smooth
function of
\rho
for
\rho>0
.
Moreover,
p^{\prime}(\rho)>0
as
\rho>0
.

For the polytropic gases (see
[
7
]
),

p(\rho)=A\rho^{\gamma},

(1.21)

where
A
and
\gamma
(
1<\gamma<3
) are positive constants.

For the Chaplygin gases (see
[
7
]
or
[
11
]
),

p(\rho)=B_{1}-\frac{B_{2}}{\rho},

(1.22)

where
B_{1}>0
and
B_{2}>0
are constants.

If
(\rho,v)\in C^{1}
is a solution of (
1.20
) with
\rho>0
,
then (
1.20
) is equivalent to the following form

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+\operatorname{div}(\rho v)=0,\\
&\partial_{t}v+v\cdot\nabla v+\displaystyle\frac{c^{2}(\rho)}{\rho}\nabla\rho=0,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(1.23)

where the sound speed
c(\rho)=\sqrt{p^{\prime}(\rho)}
.

We impose the perturbed initial boundary data of (
1.23
) in exterior domain
[0,\infty)\times\mathcal{K}
as follows:

(\rho(0,x),v(0,x))=(\bar{\rho}+\varepsilon\rho_{0}(x),\varepsilon v_{0}(x)),\quad x\in\mathcal{K},

(1.24)

and

(0,v)\cdot\mathbf{n}=0,\quad(t,x)\in[0,+\infty)\times\partial\mathcal{K},

(1.25)

where
\bar{\rho}>0
is a constant,
\varepsilon>0
is small such that
\bar{\rho}+\varepsilon\rho_{0}(x)>0
,

\rho_{0}(x),v_{0}(x)=(v_{1,0}(x),v_{2,0}(x))\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathcal{K})
,

\operatorname{supp}\rho_{0}
and
\operatorname{supp}v_{0}
are contained in the ball
B(0,M_{0})
with
M_{0}>1
,

\mathcal{K}=\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2}\setminus\mathcal{O}
with
the bounded obstacle
\mathcal{O}
being convex and smooth,
\mathbf{n}
is the unit outer normal direction of
[0,\infty)\times\partial\mathcal{K}
,
and
(0,v_{0})\cdot\mathbf{n}=0
for
x\in\partial\mathcal{K}
. When
\operatorname{curl}v_{0}(x):=\partial_{1}v_{2,0}-\partial_{2}v_{1,0}\equiv 0
,
as long as
(\rho,v)\in C^{1}
for
0\leq t<T
, then
\operatorname{curl}v\equiv 0

always holds for
0\leq t<T
, where
T>0
is any fixed constant or
T=+\infty
. In this case, one can introduce
the potential function
\phi
such that
v=\nabla\phi
,
then the Bernoulli’s law implies

\partial_{t}\phi+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+h(\rho)=0

(1.26)

with
h^{\prime}(\rho)=\frac{c^{2}(\rho)}{\rho}
and
h(\bar{\rho})=0
.
By the implicit function theorem due to

h^{\prime}(\rho)>0
for
\rho>0
, the density
function
\rho
can be expressed as

\rho=h^{-1}\big(-\partial_{t}\phi-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\big)=:H(\partial\phi),

(1.27)

where
\partial=(\partial_{t},\nabla).
Substituting (
1.27
) into the mass conservation equation in (
1.20
)
yields

\partial_{t}(H(\partial\phi))+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2}\partial_{i}\bigl(H(\partial\phi)\partial_{i}\phi\bigr)=0.

(1.28)

Without loss of generality,
\bar{\rho}=c(\bar{\rho})=1
is assumed, then it holds that
h(\rho)=\frac{\rho^{\gamma-1}-1}{\gamma-1}
and

\rho=H(\partial\phi)=[1-(\gamma-1)(\partial_{t}\phi+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2})]^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}.

(1.29)

It follows from (
1.28
)-(
1.29
) and direct computation that for
(t,x)\in[0,+\infty)\times\mathcal{K}
,

\begin{split}\Box\phi&=-2\sum_{j=1}^{2}\partial_{j}\phi\partial^{2}_{0j}\phi-\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\partial_{i}\phi\partial_{j}\phi\partial^{2}_{ij}\phi-(\gamma-1)(\partial_{t}\phi+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2})\Delta\phi\\
&=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\mu=0}^{2}Q^{\alpha\beta\mu}\partial^{2}_{\alpha\beta}\phi\partial_{\mu}\phi+\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\mu,\nu=0}^{2}Q^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\partial^{2}_{\alpha\beta}\phi\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi,\end{split}

Meanwhile, the boundary condition (
1.25

\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{n}}\phi=0,\quad(t,x)\in[0,+\infty)\times\partial\mathcal{K}.

(1.31)

We next determine the initial data
\phi(0,x)
and
\partial_{t}\phi(0,x)

\begin{split}\phi(0,x)=\varepsilon\bigg[\frac{x_{1}}{r}\int_{M_{0}}^{r}v_{1,0}(\frac{sx_{1}}{r},\frac{sx_{2}}{r})ds+\frac{x_{2}}{r}\int_{M_{0}}^{r}v_{2,0}(\frac{sx_{1}}{r},\frac{sx_{2}}{r})ds\bigg]\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathcal{K}).\end{split}

(1.32)

In addition, it follows from (
1.24
) and (
1.26
) that

\partial_{t}\phi(0,x)=-\varepsilon\rho_{0}(x)+\varepsilon^{2}g(x,\varepsilon),\quad x\in\mathcal{K},

(1.33)

where

is smooth in its arguments and has a compact support in the variable

.

In addition, the admissible condition (
1.3
) holds. In fact, for any smooth functions

w
and
\psi
satisfying
\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{n}}w|_{[0,\infty)\times\partial\mathcal{K}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{n}}\psi|_{[0,\infty)\times\partial\mathcal{K}}=0
,
it follows from (
1.30
) and direct computation that for
(t,x)\in[0,+\infty)\times\partial\mathcal{K}

used in the Cauchy
problem are difficult to be applied for the exterior domain problems since these fields are not tangent to the boundary

(0,+\infty)\times\partial\mathcal{K}
and contain the large factor
t
before the space derivatives
\partial_{i}
(
i=1,2
).
On the other hand, it can be seen from (
1.10a
) and the decay rates of the local energy discussed in Remark
1.4
that the scaling vector field
t\partial_{t}+r\partial_{r}
is also hard to be utilized in the initial boundary value problems
(note that
t\partial_{t}+r\partial_{r}
is not suitable for studying the nonlinear Klein-Gordon equations, one can see Chapter 7 of
[
14
]
).

We now state the difficulties in the proof of Theorem
1.1
without the null condition and illustrate
how to overcome these difficulties.
Note that the crucial estimate in Lemma 3.1 of
[
33
]
heavily relied on the Dirichlet boundary condition and
fails in the Cauchy-Neumann problem (
1.1
), see page 10 of
[
33
]
.
Therefore, the method in
[
33
]
can not be used in the Neumann-wave problem (
1.1
) directly.
Instead, we will apply the approach in our work
[
17
]
.
Motivated by
[
17
]
, we will establish the following pointwise spacetime estimate
by the continuous argument

\sum_{|a|\leq N}|\partial Z^{a}u(t,x)|\leq C\varepsilon\langle{x}\rangle^{-1/2}\langle{t-|x|}\rangle^{-1}\langle{t}\rangle^{\delta/16},

(1.56)

where
\delta>0
is any fixed small constant.
With this pointwise estimate (
1.56
), the required energy estimate can be obtained (see (
4.34
) below).
In this derivation procedure, due to the lack of null conditions for the nonlinearity
Q(\partial u,\partial^{2}u)
as in
[
17
]

and the appearance of large factor
\langle{t}\rangle^{\delta/16}
on the right hand side of (
1.56
) under the analogous
rough bootstrap assumption,
then the decay rate of
Q(\partial u,\partial^{2}u)
in (
1.1
)
is too weak to obtain the required estimate (
1.56
).
To overcome this difficulty, we rewrite the quadratic terms in
Q(\partial u,\partial^{2}u)
of (
1.1
) into such a divergence form

\begin{split}\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\mu=0}^{2}Q^{\alpha\beta\mu}\partial^{2}_{\alpha\beta}u\partial_{\mu}u&=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\mu=0}^{2}[\partial_{\alpha}(Q^{\alpha\beta\mu}\partial_{\beta}u\partial_{\mu}u)+\partial_{\beta}(Q^{\alpha\beta\mu}\partial_{\alpha}u\partial_{\mu}u)-\partial_{\mu}(Q^{\alpha\beta\mu}\partial_{\alpha}u\partial_{\beta}u)].\end{split}

In this case, some better pointwise decay estimates for the initial boundary value problem of the divergence form wave equations can be
achieved (see (
3.11
) for details). Based on this, (
1.56
) can be
derived and further Theorem
1.1
is proved.

We next give some comments on the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
For the nonlinear equation with quadratic nonlinearity in (
1.1
), by the standard energy method with the estimate (
1.9a
),
one can obtain that for the higher order energy
\displaystyle E_{high}(t)=\sum_{|a|\leq N_{0}}\|\partial Z^{a}u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K})}

with
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
being suitably large constant,

\frac{d}{dt}E_{high}(t)\leq C\varepsilon(1+t)^{-1}\ln^{2}(2+t)E_{high}(t).

(1.57)

This, together with the Gronwall’s lemma, leads to

E_{high}(t)\leq E_{high}(0)e^{C\varepsilon\ln^{3}(2+t)}=E_{high}(0)(2+t)^{C\varepsilon\ln^{2}(2+t)}.

(1.58)

Obviously, it is hard to control the energy
E_{high}(t)
by (
1.58
) since it grows very rapidly with the development
of time
t
.
To overcome this difficulty, we intend to find some new good unknowns to eliminate the related quadratic nonlinearities
so that the more precise energy estimates with at least slow time growth can be obtained.
Motivated by the following identity

\Box(fh)=f\Box h+h\Box f+2Q_{0}(f,h),

(1.59)

we now introduce the perturbed wave operator
\displaystyle\Box_{g}=\Box+\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{2}g^{\alpha\beta}\partial^{2}_{\alpha\beta}

with
\displaystyle g^{\alpha\beta}=\sum_{\mu=0}^{2}Q^{\alpha\beta\mu}\partial_{\mu}u+\sum_{\mu,\nu=0}^{2}Q^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}u\partial_{\nu}u
for problem (
1.1
)
and using
\Box_{g}
instead of
\Box
in (
1.59
) to obtain

\Box_{g}(fh)=f\Box_{g}h+h\Box_{g}f+2Q_{0}(f,h)+\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{2}2g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}f\partial_{\beta}h.

(1.60)

From this, one knows that if
f
and
h
are the solutions of the quasilinear wave operator
\Box_{g}
,
then there is no loss of regularities for
\Box_{g}(fh)
when
\Box_{g}f
and
\Box_{g}h
are substituted into the right
hand side of (
1.60
).
By such a careful observation, we start to search for a series of new good unknowns
so that the related
Q_{0}
-type null forms can be eliminated and the regularities of obtained nonlinearities
are not lost correspondingly, meanwhile, the remaining quadratic nonlinearities
and the resulting cubic nonlinearities can admit better pointwise spacetime decay estimates. More concretely speaking,
it follows from the equation in (
1.1
) with (
1.6
)
and direct computation that for
i=1,2\cdots,2N
,

\begin{split}\Box_{g}\partial_{t}^{i}u&=2\sum_{\alpha=0}^{2}C^{\alpha}Q_{0}(\partial_{\alpha}u,\partial_{t}^{i}u)+2\sum_{\alpha=0}^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j+k=i,\\
1\leq j,k\leq i-1\end{subarray}}C^{\alpha}C^{i}_{jk}Q_{0}(\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{t}^{j}u,\partial_{t}^{k}u)\\
&\quad+\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\mu,\nu=0}^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j+k+l=i,\\
j<i\end{subarray}}Q^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}C^{i}_{jkl}\partial^{2}_{\alpha\beta}\partial_{t}^{j}u\partial_{\mu}\partial_{t}^{k}u\partial_{\nu}\partial_{t}^{l}u,\end{split}

(1.61)

where
C^{i}_{jk}
and
C^{i}_{jkl}
are some suitable positive constants.
Introducing the following good unknowns for
i=1,\cdots,2N
(see (
5.10
) below)

V_{i}=\partial_{t}^{i}u-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha=0}^{2}C^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}u\partial_{t}^{i}u-\sum_{\alpha=0}^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j+k=i,\\

(1.62)

where cutoff function
\chi_{[1,2]}(s)\in C^{\infty}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)
with

,

\chi_{[1,2]}(s)=0
for

and
\chi_{[1,2]}(s)=1
for

.
Then by (
1.60
)-(

\begin{split}&\Box_{g}V_{i}-\sum_{\alpha=0}^{2}C^{\alpha}Q_{0}(\partial_{\alpha}u,V_{i})=\text{good terms}.\\
\end{split}

(1.63)

On the other hand, for any multi-index
a\in\mathop{\mathbb{N}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits_{0}^{4}
with

, one has that
for (
1.1

\begin{split}\Box_{g}Z^{a}u&=2\sum_{\alpha=0}^{2}C^{\alpha}Q_{0}(\partial_{\alpha}u,Z^{a}u)+2\sum_{\alpha=0}^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b+c\leq a,\\

Analogously, we also define the good unknowns as follows (see (

By (

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

+\sum_{|a|\leq m-1}\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\langle{s}\rangle^{\eta+\mu}\|\partial^{a}G(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K})}).\end{split}

(2.4)

Proof.

Lemma 2.3
.

For any non-negative constants

,

(2.12)

Proof.

Set

In addition, it follows from direct computation that

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{B}{\mu+1/2}(1+s)^{\mu+1/2}}C(1+s)^{D+\mu-1/2}ds\\
&\leq C(1+t)^{D}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{B}{\mu+1/2}(1+s)^{\mu+1/2}}(1+s)^{\mu-1/2}ds\\
&=\frac{C}{B}(1+t)^{D}(e^{-\frac{B}{\mu+1/2}}-e^{-\frac{B}{\mu+1/2}(1+t)^{\mu+1/2}})\\

\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{B}{\mu+1/2}(1+s)^{\mu+1/2}}A(1+s)^{\mu-1/2}ds\leq\frac{A}{B}(1-e^{-\frac{B}{\mu+1/2}(1+t)^{\mu+1/2}}).

(2.16)

Substituting (
2.15
)-(
2.16
) into (

) and further (

).

&\lesssim\|(w_{0},w_{1})\|_{H^{3}(\mathcal{K})}+\ln(2+t)\sum_{\alpha=0}^{2}\sum_{|a|\leq 2}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\langle{s}\rangle\|\partial^{a}(G^{r},\partial^{\leq 1}G^{\alpha})(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{3})}.\end{split}

(3.12)

Next, we treat
w_{1}^{b}
in the region
|x|\geq R
.
Let
w_{1}^{c}=\chi_{[3,4]}w_{1}^{b}
be a function on
\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2}
.
Then
w_{1}^{c}
solves the Cauchy problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Box w_{1}^{c}=-[\Delta,\chi_{[3,4]}]w_{1}^{b},\\
&(w_{1}^{c},\partial_{t}w_{1}^{c})(0,x)=(\chi_{[3,4]}w_{0},\chi_{[3,4]}w_{1}).\end{aligned}\right.

By
\operatorname{supp}_{x}[\Delta,\chi_{[3,4]}]w_{1}^{b}\subset\{x:|x|\leq 4\}
, (
2.23
) with
H=0
and (
2.25
),
we can arrive at

\begin{split}&\quad\;\langle{x}\rangle^{1/2}\langle{t-|x|}\rangle|\partial w_{1}^{c}|\\
&\lesssim\|(w_{0},w_{1})\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathcal{K})}+\sum_{|a|\leq 6}\sup_{(s,y)\in[0,t]\times\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2}}\langle{s}\rangle|\partial^{a}[\Delta,\chi_{[3,4]}]w_{1}^{b}(s,y)|\\
&\lesssim\|(w_{0},w_{1})\|_{H^{4}(\mathcal{K})}+\sum_{|a|\leq 6}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\langle{s}\rangle\|\partial^{a}[\Delta,\chi_{[3,4]}]w_{1}^{b}(s,\cdot)\|_{H^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2})}\\
&\lesssim\|(w_{0},w_{1})\|_{H^{4}(\mathcal{K})}+\sum_{|a|\leq 9}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\langle{s}\rangle\|\partial^{a}w_{1}^{b}(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{4})}\\
&\lesssim\|(w_{0},w_{1})\|_{H^{9}(\mathcal{K})}+\ln(2+t)\sum_{|a|\leq 8}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\langle{s}\rangle\|\partial^{a}(G^{r},\partial^{\leq 1}G^{\alpha})(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{3})},\end{split}

(3.13)

where the last three inequalities are derived from the Sobolev embedding and (
2.3
) with
m=9
.
Note that
\partial w_{1}^{b}=\partial w_{1}^{c}
holds in the region
|x|\geq 4
, then (
3.12
) with
R>4
and (
3.13
) lead to that for
x\in\mathcal{K}
,

\langle{x}\rangle^{1/2}\langle{t-|x|}\rangle|\partial w_{1}^{b}|\lesssim\|(w_{0},w_{1})\|_{H^{9}(\mathcal{K})}+\ln(2+t)\sum_{|a|\leq 8}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\langle{s}\rangle\|\partial^{a}(G^{r},\partial^{\leq 1}G^{\alpha})(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{3})}.

(3.14)

We now deal with
w_{2}^{b}
.
From (
3.7
), it holds that
\partial w_{2}^{b}=\chi_{[1,2]}(x)\partial w_{2}^{c}+\partial(\chi_{[1,2]}(x))w_{2}^{c}+\partial\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}
.
Due to
\operatorname{supp}_{x}\Box\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}\subset\{x:|x|\leq 2\}
, similarly to (
3.12
)-(
3.14
) for
w_{1}^{b}
,
one then has that for
\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}
,

\begin{split}\langle{t}\rangle\|\partial\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{K}_{R})}&\lesssim\langle{t}\rangle\|\partial\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}\|_{H^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{R+1})}\\
&\lesssim\ln(2+t)\sum_{|a|\leq 2}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\langle{s}\rangle\|\partial^{a}[\Delta,\chi_{[1,2]}]w_{2}^{c}(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{2})}\\
&\lesssim\ln(2+t)\sum_{|a|\leq 3}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\langle{s}\rangle\|\partial^{a}w_{2}^{c}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(|y|\leq 2)}.\end{split}

(3.15)

To estimate
\partial\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}
in the region
\mathcal{K}\cap\{|x|\geq 3\}
, we now set
v_{2}^{c}=\chi_{[2,3]}\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}
.
It follows from
\operatorname{supp}_{x}\Box v_{2}^{c}=\operatorname{supp}_{x}[\Delta,\chi_{[2,3]}]\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}\subset\{x:|x|\leq 3\}
and (
2.25
) that

\begin{split}\langle{x}\rangle^{1/2}\langle{t-|x|}\rangle|\partial v_{2}^{c}|&\lesssim\sum_{|a|\leq 6}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\langle{s}\rangle\|\partial^{a}[\Delta,\chi_{[2,3]}]\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}(s)\|_{H^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2})}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{|a|\leq 9}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\langle{s}\rangle\|\partial^{a}\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{3})}\\
&\lesssim\ln(2+t)\sum_{|a|\leq 9}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\langle{s}\rangle\|\partial^{a}w_{2}^{c}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(|y|\leq 2)}.\end{split}

(3.16)

Note that
\partial\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}=\partial v_{2}^{c}
holds in the region
|x|\geq 3
.
Combining (
3.15
) with (
3.16
) yields

\begin{split}\langle{x}\rangle^{1/2}\langle{t-|x|}\rangle|\partial\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}|\lesssim\ln(2+t)\sum_{|a|\leq 9}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\langle{s}\rangle\|\partial^{a}w_{2}^{c}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(|y|\leq 2)}.\end{split}

(3.17)

We now treat the term
\partial\partial^{a}w_{2}^{c}
with
|a|\leq 8
on the right hand side of (
3.17
).
It can be concluded from (
2.23
) with
\mu=\nu=\eta/2
that

\begin{split}\sum_{|a|\leq 8}\langle{x}\rangle^{1/2}\langle{t-|x|}\rangle|\partial\partial^{a}w_{2}^{c}|\lesssim\sum_{|a|\leq 8}\sum_{\alpha=0}^{2}\sup_{y\in\mathcal{K}}|Z^{a}(G^{r},\partial^{\leq 1}G^{\alpha})(0,y)|\\
+\sum_{|a|\leq 9}\sup_{(s,y)\in[0,t]\times\overline{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{2}}}\langle{y}\rangle^{1/2}\mathcal{W}_{1+\eta,1}(s,y)|Z^{a}(G^{r},\partial^{\leq 1}G^{\alpha})(s,y)|.\end{split}

(3.18)

Collecting (
3.10
), (
3.14
), (
3.17
), (
3.18
) and
\partial w_{2}^{b}=\chi_{[1,2]}(x)\partial w_{2}^{c}+\partial(\chi_{[1,2]}(x))w_{2}^{c}+\partial\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}
yields (
3.11
).
∎

Thirdly, we establish the pointwise spacetime decay estimate of solution itself and
time decay estimate for the inhomogeneous linear wave equation.

Lemma 3.3
.

(Spacetime decay of
w
and time decay of
\|\partial^{\leq 1}\partial_{t}w\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{R})}
)

Let
w(t,x)
be the solution of the IBVP

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Box w=F,\qquad\qquad(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathcal{K},\\
&\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{n}}w=0,\qquad\qquad(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\partial\mathcal{K},\\
&(w,\partial_{t}w)(0,x)=(w_{0},w_{1})(x),\quad x\in\mathcal{K},\end{aligned}\right.

where
(w_{0},w_{1})
has compact support and
\operatorname{supp}_{x}F(t,x)\subset\{x:|x|\leq t+M_{0}\}
.
Then we have that for any
\mu,\nu>0
with
\mu+\nu<1/2
,

\begin{split}&\langle{t+|x|}\rangle^{1/2}\langle{t-|x|}\rangle^{\mu}|w|\\
&\lesssim\|(w_{0},w_{1})\|_{H^{5}(\mathcal{K})}+\sum_{|a|\leq 4}[\sup_{y\in\mathcal{K}}|\partial^{a}F(0,y)|+\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\langle{s}\rangle^{1/2+\mu+\nu}\|\partial^{a}F(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{3})}]\\
&\quad+\sum_{|a|\leq 5}\sup_{(s,y)\in[0,t]\times\overline{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{2}}}\langle{y}\rangle^{1/2}\mathcal{W}_{1+\mu+\nu,1}(s,y)|\partial^{a}F(s,y)|\end{split}

(3.19)

and

\begin{split}&\langle{t}\rangle\|\partial^{\leq 1}\partial_{t}w\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{R})}\\
&\lesssim\|(w_{0},w_{1})\|_{H^{2}(\mathcal{K})}+\sum_{|a|\leq 1}[\sup_{y\in\mathcal{K}}|\partial^{a}F(0,y)|+\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\langle{s}\rangle^{1+\mu}\|\partial^{a}F(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{3})}]\\
&\quad+\sum_{|a|\leq 2}\sup_{(s,y)\in[0,t]\times\overline{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{2}}}\langle{y}\rangle^{1/2}\mathcal{W}_{1+\mu+\nu,1}(s,y)|Z^{a}F(s,y)|.\end{split}

(3.20)

Proof.

Although the proof of (
3.19
) is similar to that for the Dirichlet boundary problem
[
17
]
,
we still give the details for completeness and due to the different boundary condition and slightly different weight.
Let
w_{1}^{b}
and
w_{2}^{b}
be the solutions of

\begin{split}&\Box w_{1}^{b}:=(1-\chi_{[2,3]}(x))F(t,x),\\
&\Box w_{2}^{b}:=\chi_{[2,3]}(x)F(t,x),\\
&\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{n}}w_{1}^{b}|_{[0,\infty)\times\partial\mathcal{K}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{n}}w_{2}^{b}|_{[0,\infty)\times\partial\mathcal{K}}=0,\\
&(w_{1}^{b},\partial_{t}w_{1}^{b})(0,x)=(w_{0},w_{1})(x),\quad(w_{2}^{b},\partial_{t}w_{2}^{b})(0,x)=(0,0),\end{split}

(3.21)

respectively.
Then one can find that
w=w_{1}^{b}+w_{2}^{b}
.
For the estimate of
w_{1}^{b}
in
\mathcal{K}_{R}
, it follows from the fact of
\operatorname{supp}_{x}\Box w_{1}^{b}\subset\{x:|x|\leq 3\}
and (
2.4
) with
m=2
that

\begin{split}&\langle{t}\rangle^{1/2+\mu}\|w_{1}^{b}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{K}_{R})}\lesssim\langle{t}\rangle^{1/2+\mu}\|w_{1}^{b}\|_{H^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{R+1})}\\
&\lesssim\|(w_{0},w_{1})\|_{H^{2}(\mathcal{K})}+\sum_{|a|\leq 1}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\langle{s}\rangle^{1/2+\mu+\nu}\|\partial^{a}[(1-\chi_{[2,3]}(x))F]\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K})}\\
&\lesssim\|(w_{0},w_{1})\|_{H^{2}(\mathcal{K})}+\sum_{|a|\leq 1}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\langle{s}\rangle^{1/2+\mu+\nu}\|\partial^{a}F(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{3})}.\end{split}

(3.22)

We now estimate
w_{1}^{b}
in the region
|x|\geq 4
.
To this end, let
w_{1}^{c}=\chi_{[3,4]}(x)w_{1}^{b}
, then one has

\begin{split}&\Box w_{1}^{c}=-[\Delta,\chi_{[3,4]}]w_{1}^{b},\\
&(w_{1}^{c},\partial_{t}w_{1}^{c})(0,x)=(\chi_{[3,4]}(x)w_{0},\chi_{[3,4]}(x)w_{1}).\end{split}

It can be deduced from
\operatorname{supp}_{x}[\Delta,\chi_{[3,4]}]w_{1}^{b}\subset\{x:|x|\leq 4\}
, (
2.22
) with
H=0
and (
2.24
) that

\begin{split}&\langle{t+|x|}\rangle^{1/2}\langle{t-|x|}\rangle^{\mu}|w_{1}^{c}|\\
&\lesssim\|(w_{0},w_{1})\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{K})}+\sum_{|a|\leq 2}\sup_{(s,y)\in[0,t]\times\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2}}\langle{s}\rangle^{1/2+\mu+\nu/2}|\partial^{a}[\Delta,\chi_{[3,4]}]w_{1}^{b}|\\
&\lesssim\|(w_{0},w_{1})\|_{H^{3}(\mathcal{K})}+\sum_{|a|\leq 2}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\langle{s}\rangle^{1/2+\mu+\nu/2}\|\partial^{a}[\Delta,\chi_{[3,4]}]w_{1}^{b}\|_{H^{2}(\mathcal{K})}\\
&\lesssim\|(w_{0},w_{1})\|_{H^{5}(\mathcal{K})}+\sum_{|a|\leq 4}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\langle{s}\rangle^{1/2+\mu+\nu}\|\partial^{a}F(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{3})},\end{split}

(3.23)

where the last two inequalities are derived from the Sobolev embedding and (
2.4
) with
m=5
.
Notice that
w_{1}^{c}=w_{1}^{b}
holds in the region
|x|\geq 4
.
Then (
3.22
) with
R>4
and (
3.23
) lead to

\langle{t+|x|}\rangle^{1/2}\langle{t-|x|}\rangle^{\mu}|w_{1}^{b}|\lesssim\|(w_{0},w_{1})\|_{H^{5}(\mathcal{K})}+\sum_{|a|\leq 4}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\langle{s}\rangle^{1/2+\mu+\nu}\|\partial^{a}F(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{3})}.

(3.24)

Next, we turn to the estimate of
w_{2}^{b}
.
Denote
w_{2}^{c}
the solution of the Cauchy problem

\Box w_{2}^{c}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\chi_{[2,3]}(x)F(t,x),&&\qquad x\in\mathcal{K}=\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2}\setminus\mathcal{O},\\
&0,&&\qquad x\in\mathcal{O}\end{aligned}\right.

(3.25)

with the initial data
(w_{2}^{c},\partial_{t}w_{2}^{c})(0,x)=(0,0)
.
Set
v_{2}^{b}=\chi_{[1,2]}(x)w_{2}^{c}
on
\mathcal{K}
and then
v_{2}^{b}
is the solution of the IBVP

\begin{split}&\Box v_{2}^{b}=\chi_{[1,2]}\Box w_{2}^{c}-[\Delta,\chi_{[1,2]}]w_{2}^{c}=\chi_{[2,3]}F-[\Delta,\chi_{[1,2]}]w_{2}^{c},\\
&\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{n}}v_{2}^{b}|_{[0,\infty)\times\partial\mathcal{K}}=0,\quad(v_{2}^{b},\partial_{t}v_{2}^{b})(0,x)=(0,0).\end{split}

(3.26)

In addition, let
\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}
be the solution of the IBVP

\Box\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}=[\Delta,\chi_{[1,2]}]w_{2}^{c},\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{n}}\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}|_{[0,\infty)\times\partial\mathcal{K}}=0,\quad(\tilde{v}_{2}^{b},\partial_{t}\tilde{v}_{2}^{b})(0,x)=(0,0).

(3.27)

Thereby, (
3.21
), (
3.26
) and (
3.27
) imply
w_{2}^{b}=\chi_{[1,2]}(x)w_{2}^{c}+\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}
.
Note that by
\operatorname{supp}_{x}\Box\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}\subset\{x:|x|\leq 2\}
, similarly to the estimates (
3.22
) and (
3.23
) for
w_{1}^{b}
,
then we have

\begin{split}\langle{t}\rangle^{1/2+\mu}\|\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{K}_{R})}&\lesssim\langle{t}\rangle^{1/2+\mu}\|\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}\|_{H^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{R+1})}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{|a|\leq 1}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\langle{s}\rangle^{1/2+\mu+\nu/2}\|\partial^{a}[\Delta,\chi_{[1,2]}]w_{2}^{c}]\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K})}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{|a|\leq 2}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\langle{s}\rangle^{1/2+\mu+\nu/2}\|\partial^{a}w_{2}^{c}\|_{L^{\infty}(|y|\leq 2)}.\end{split}

(3.28)

Denote
v_{2}^{c}=\chi_{[2,3]}\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}
, which solves the Cauchy problem
\Box v_{2}^{c}=-[\Delta,\chi_{[2,3]}]\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}
with zero initial data.
In addition,
\operatorname{supp}_{x}\Box v_{2}^{c}\subset\{x:|x|\leq 3\}
and (
2.24
) yield

\begin{split}\langle{t+|x|}\rangle^{1/2}\langle{t-|x|}\rangle^{\mu}|v_{2}^{c}|&\lesssim\sum_{|a|\leq 2}\sup_{(s,y)\in[0,t]\times\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2}}\langle{s}\rangle^{1/2+\mu+\nu/4}|\partial^{a}[\Delta,\chi_{[2,3]}]\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}|\\
&\lesssim\sum_{|a|\leq 5}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\langle{s}\rangle^{1/2+\mu+\nu/4}\|\partial^{a}\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{3})}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{|a|\leq 5}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\langle{s}\rangle^{1/2+\mu+\nu/2}\|\partial^{a}w_{2}^{c}\|_{L^{\infty}(|y|\leq 2)}.\end{split}

(3.29)

By virtue of
v_{2}^{c}=\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}
in the region
|x|\geq 3
, collecting (
3.28
) with
R>3
and (
3.29
) shows

\langle{t+|x|}\rangle^{1/2}\langle{t-|x|}\rangle^{\mu}|\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}|\lesssim\sum_{|a|\leq 5}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\langle{s}\rangle^{1/2+\mu+\nu/2}\|\partial^{a}w_{2}^{c}\|_{L^{\infty}(|y|\leq 2)}.

(3.30)

Note that
w_{2}^{b}=\chi_{[1,2]}(x)w_{2}^{c}+\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}
, it suffices to deal with the estimate of
w_{2}^{c}
, which solves (
3.25
).
Applying (
2.21
) to
\Box\partial^{a}w_{2}^{c}=\partial^{a}(\chi_{[2,3]}(x)F(t,x))
yields

\begin{split}\langle{t+|x|}\rangle^{1/2}\langle{t-|x|}\rangle^{\mu+\nu/2}\sum_{|a|\leq 5}|\partial^{a}w_{2}^{c}|\lesssim\sum_{|a|\leq 4}\sup_{y\in\mathcal{K}}|\partial^{a}F(0,y)|\\
+\sum_{|a|\leq 5}\sup_{(s,y)\in[0,t]\times\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2}}\langle{y}\rangle^{1/2}\mathcal{W}_{1+\mu+\nu,1}(s,y)|\partial^{a}(\chi_{[2,3]}F)(s,y)|,\end{split}

(3.31)

where the term
\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\mathcal{K}}|\partial^{a}F(0,y)|
comes from the initial data of
\partial^{a}w_{2}^{c}
.
Thus, (
3.19
) can be achieved by (
3.24
), (
3.30
) and (
3.31
).

Finally, we start to prove (
3.20
).
Applying (
2.4
) to
w_{1}^{b}
in (
3.21
) with
\eta=1
yields

\langle{t}\rangle\|\partial^{\leq 1}\partial_{t}w_{1}^{b}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{R})}\lesssim\|(w_{0},w_{1})\|_{H^{2}(\mathcal{K})}+\sum_{|a|\leq 1}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\langle{s}\rangle^{1+\mu}\|\partial^{a}F(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{3})}.

(3.32)

Note that
\partial_{t}w_{2}^{b}=\chi_{[1,2]}(x)\partial_{t}w_{2}^{c}+\partial_{t}\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}
.
Set
\phi=\partial_{t}\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}
and then

\Box\phi=[\Delta,\chi_{[1,2]}]\partial_{t}w_{2}^{c},\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{n}}\phi|_{[0,\infty)\times\partial\mathcal{K}}=0,\quad(\phi,\partial_{t}\phi)(0,x)=(0,0).

(3.33)

Since the estimate of
\partial_{t}\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}=\phi
is similar to (
3.32
) in terms of (
3.33
),
we have

\langle{t}\rangle\|\partial^{\leq 1}\partial_{t}\tilde{v}_{2}^{b}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{R})}=\langle{t}\rangle\|\partial^{\leq 1}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{R})}\lesssim\sum_{|a|\leq 1}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\langle{s}\rangle^{1+\mu}\|\partial^{a}\partial_{t}w_{2}^{c}(s,y)\|_{L^{\infty}(|y|\leq 2)}.

(3.34)

By using (
2.23
) with (
3.25
), one can get

\begin{split}\langle{x}\rangle^{1/2}\langle{t-|x|}\rangle^{1+\mu}\sum_{|a|\leq 1}|\partial_{t}\partial^{a}w_{2}^{c}|\lesssim\sum_{|a|\leq 1}\sup_{y\in\mathcal{K}}|\partial^{a}F(0,y)|\\
+\sum_{|a|\leq 2}\sup_{(s,y)\in[0,t]\times\overline{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{2}}}\langle{y}\rangle^{1/2}\mathcal{W}_{1+\mu+\nu,1}(s,y)|Z^{a}F(s,y)|\end{split}

This, together with (
3.32
) and (
3.34
), derives (
3.20
).
∎

Finally, we prove the pointwise spacetime decay estimate of the first order derivatives of solution to the linear wave equation
with a special divergence form source term.

Lemma 3.4
.

(Spacetime decay estimate of
\partial w
)

Let
w(t,x)
be the solution of the IBVP

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Box w=\partial_{t}G^{0},\qquad\qquad(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathcal{K},\\
&\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{n}}w=0,\qquad\qquad\quad(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\partial\mathcal{K},\\
&(w,\partial_{t}w)(0,x)=(w_{0},w_{1})(x),\quad x\in\mathcal{K},\end{aligned}\right.

where
(w_{0},w_{1})
has compact support and
\operatorname{supp}_{x}G^{0}(t,x)\subset\{x:|x|\leq t+M_{0}\}
.
Then it holds that for any
\eta\in(0,1/2)
,

\begin{split}&\langle{x}\rangle^{1/2}\langle{t-|x|}\rangle|\partial w|\lesssim\|(w_{0},w_{1},G^{0}(0))\|_{H^{9}(\mathcal{K})}+\sum_{|a|\leq 9}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\langle{s}\rangle^{1+\eta}\|\partial^{a}G^{0}(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{3})}\\
&\quad+\sum_{|a|\leq 9}\sup_{y\in\mathcal{K}}|\partial^{a}G^{0}(0,y)|+\sum_{|a|\leq 10}\sup_{(s,y)\in[0,t]\times\overline{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{2}}}\langle{y}\rangle^{1/2}\mathcal{W}_{1+\eta,1}(s,y)|Z^{a}G^{0}(s,y)|.\end{split}

(3.35)

Proof.

Although the proof of (
3.35
) is much easier than that of (
3.11
),
we still give the details for reader’s convenience.
Let
w_{1}^{b}
and
w_{2}^{b}
be the solutions of

\begin{split}&\Box w_{1}^{b}:=(1-\chi_{[2,3]}(x))\partial_{t}G^{0},\\
&\Box w_{2}^{b}:=\partial_{t}(\chi_{[2,3]}(x)G^{0}),\\
&\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{n}}w_{1}^{b}|_{[0,\infty)\times\partial\mathcal{K}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{n}}w_{2}^{b}|_{[0,\infty)\times\partial\mathcal{K}}=0,\\
&(w_{1}^{b},\partial_{t}w_{1}^{b})(0,x)=(w_{0},w_{1})(x),\quad(w_{2}^{b},\partial_{t}w_{2}^{b})(0,x)=(0,0),\end{split}

respectively.
Then we have
w=w_{1}^{b}+w_{2}^{b}
.
The estimate of
w_{1}^{b}
in
\mathcal{K}_{R}
can be obtained by (
2.4
) with
m=3
and the fact of
\operatorname{supp}_{x}\Box w_{1}^{b}\subset\{x:|x|\leq 3\}

as follows

\begin{split}&\langle{t}\rangle\|\partial w_{1}^{b}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{K}_{R})}\lesssim\langle{t}\rangle\|\partial w_{1}^{b}\|_{H^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{R+1})}\\
&\lesssim\|(w_{0},w_{1})\|_{H^{3}(\mathcal{K})}+\sum_{|a|\leq 2}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\langle{s}\rangle^{1+\eta}\|\partial^{a}\Box w_{1}^{b}(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K})}\\
&\lesssim\|(w_{0},w_{1})\|_{H^{3}(\mathcal{K})}+\sum_{\alpha=0}^{2}\sum_{|a|\leq 3}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\langle{s}\rangle^{1+\eta}\|\partial^{a}G^{0}(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{3})}.\end{split}

(3.36)

Next we treat

Lemma 5.2
.

(Estimate for the energy

)

Under the assumptions of Theorem

Proof.

Set
\displaystyle E_{j}(t):=\sum_{k=0}^{2N-j}\|\partial\partial_{t}^{k}u(t)\|_{H^{j}(\mathcal{K})}
with

\begin{split}E_{j}(t)&\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{2N-j}[\|\partial\partial_{t}^{k}u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K})}+\sum_{1\leq|a|\leq j}(\|\partial_{t}\partial_{t}^{k}\partial_{x}^{a}u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K})}+\|\partial_{x}\partial_{t}^{k}\partial_{x}^{a}u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K})})]\\

(5.28)

\begin{split}\|\partial_{t}^{k}\partial_{x}^{a}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K})}&\lesssim\|\Delta_{x}\partial_{t}^{k}u\|_{H^{|a|-2}(\mathcal{K})}+\|\partial_{t}^{k}u\|_{H^{|a|-1}(\mathcal{K}_{R+1})}\\

&\quad+\|\partial_{t}^{k}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{R+1})}+\sum_{1\leq|b|\leq|a|-1}\|\partial_{t}^{k}\partial_{x}^{b}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{R+1})},\end{split}

(5.29)

where
\Delta_{x}=\partial_{t}^{2}-\Box
and the equation in (
1.1
) has been used.

5.1
) with
|a|+k\leq 2N+1
, we have

\begin{split}\|\partial_{t}^{k}Q(\partial u,\partial^{2}u)\|_{H^{|a|-2}(\mathcal{K})}&\lesssim\sum_{l\leq k}\varepsilon_{1}(\|\partial_{t}^{l}\partial u\|_{H^{|a|-1}(\mathcal{K})}+\|\partial_{t}^{l+2}u\|_{H^{|a|-2}(\mathcal{K})})\\
&\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}[E_{j}(t)+E_{j-1}(t)].\end{split}

(5.30)

5.28

5.30

(5.31)

Then collecting (
5.3

5.6

5.31

\begin{split}[E_{0}(t)]^{2}&\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}+\varepsilon_{1}^{3}+\varepsilon_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\big\{[E_{0}(s)]^{2}+\varepsilon_{1}^{2}\langle{s}\rangle^{-1}\big\}\frac{ds}{\langle{s}\rangle}\\

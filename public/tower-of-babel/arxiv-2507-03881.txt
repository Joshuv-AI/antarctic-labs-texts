Introduction

We are interested in the Navier-Stokes equations in
Q_{T}

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u-\Delta u+\nabla p=0,\\
&\operatorname{div}u=0,\\
&u_{|_{t=-T}}=a(x),\quad\operatorname{div}a(x)=0.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(1.1)

We assume that the initial data for the Navier-Stokes equations is smooth solenoidal vector fields in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, decaying sufficiently fast as
|x|\rightarrow\infty
. There are many celebrated results on the Navier-Stokes equations. In particular, Leray in the seminal work in 1934
[
1
]
introduced the concept of weak solutions to the Navier-Stokes equations, relaxing the classical smoothness requirements for solutions. Leray proved the global existence of weak solutions and the set of potential singularities must be confined to a sparse region. Afterwards, the regularity of weak solution of Navier-Stokes equations becomes one of the challenging open problems in mathematics. More precisely, whether the solution to (
1.1
) can develop finite time singularities from the regular initial data remains a big question. And it has been called one of the seven most important unsolved problems in mathematics by the Clay Mathematics Institute.

A well-known scaling invariant property to (
1.1
) is that for any given solution
(u,p)
to (
1.1
), the scaled functions

u_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda u(\lambda x,{\lambda}^{2}t),\quad\quad p_{\lambda}(x,t)={\lambda}^{2}p(\lambda x,{\lambda}^{2}t)

are also solutions to (
1.1
) for any
\lambda>0

Leray established the following result: Assume that
a(x)
is a smooth solenoidal vector field on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
decaying sufficiently fast as
|x|\rightarrow\infty
, then there exists a global Leray-Hopf weak solution
u(x,t)
. Moreover, these weak solutions also satisfy a global energy inequality. And the solution is actually a strong solution within a short time interval. Leray also established the blow-up rate of the
L^{p}
norm of
u(x,t)
when
p>3
.

Leray’s results demonstrate that for the Navier-Stokes equations with sufficiently smooth initial data, the global weak solution remains regular for a short time. However, it may develop singularities thereafter. The regularity theory for weak solutions based on the current known estimates for solutions are not enough, since those known a priori estimates for the Navier-Stokes equations are not good enough, in particular, even not scaling invariant. A natural question for Navier-Stokes equations is whether solutions with sufficiently regular initial data remain smooth for all time or develop finite-time singularities.

It is easy to see from the energy estimates, Leray weak solutions are in the following space

\displaystyle u(x,t)\in L^{s}(-T,0;L^{l}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\quad\text{while}\quad\frac{3}{l}+\frac{2}{s}=\frac{3}{2}.

(1.2)

There are many works on the study of regularity of weak solution to the Navier-Stokes equations. We cannot review all those results but list a few related to our studies.
One approach that developed is to focus on establishing regularity criteria. If the Leray-Hopf weak solution lies in a suitable scaling-invariant space, then the weak solution is actually smooth.

Pigmentary Puzzle: A Rare Case of Hyperpigmented Cutaneous Sarcoidosis
Authors: Disha Chakraborty, Shailaja Verma, Debaleena Ghosh, Sudip Das
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

Cutaneous sarcoidosis is observed in approximately 30% of patients and is often the earliest indicator of the disease. We know that sarcoidosis is often referred to as a “great imitator” due to the wide variety of clinical manifestations. Again, several uncommon variants such as verrucous, ichthyosiform psoriasiform, annular, alopecia areata associated, necrobiotic, erythrodermic, sarcoidosis associated with granulomatous tattoo reaction and several others have been reported. We encountered yet another rare variant of hyperpigmented cutaneous sarcoidosis in a young male.[1]

A 13-year-old male presented to our outpatient department with hyperpigmentation over his face, trunk and upper limbs. It was insidious in onset, first appearing over the face, gradually involving the other mentioned sites over 6 months. There was no associated itching, burning or any other discomfort. There was no history of any cutaneous illness. There were no systemic complaints. Cutaneous examination revealed multiple ill to well-defined hyperpigmented macules coalescing to patches over his face and lips, chest, abdomen and back and bilateral upper limbs. Oral mucosa, nail and hair were uninvolved [Figure 1a-c]. We planned for histopathological examination with differentials of idiopathic eruptive macular pigmentation (IEMP), lichen planus pigmentosus (LPP), ashy dermatoses and post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation. We were surprised when the skin biopsy report revealed discrete, compact, naked, non-caseating epithelioid cell granulomas and asteroid bodies. We subsequently performed special staining in which reticulin stain-rich fibres were seen in and around the granulomas further confirming the diagnosis of sarcoidosis [Figure 2a-c]. However, serum calcium and angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) levels were normal. Other laboratory findings like complete blood count, liver, renal function and Mantoux tests were normal. Chest radiography, ultrasonography of the abdomen and ophthalmologic examination did not reveal any significant abnormality. We treated the patient with topical corticosteroids and oral isotretinoin and he is still under our follow-up.

Cutaneous lesions in sarcoidosis can be either sarcoid specific that is typical sarcoid granuloma present or non-specific in which reactive inflammatory changes are predominant. Sarcoid-specific lesions are macules, papules, plaques, subcutaneous nodules, lupus pernio, etc., and non-specific ones can be erythema nodosum, erythema multiforme, prurigo, calcification, etc.[2] Pigmentation in sarcoidosis in the form of pigmented purpuric dermatoses (PPD) has been reported previously.[3] Again, lichenoid sarcoidosis is a very uncommon form of cutaneous sarcoidosis, resembling lichen planus in clinical presentation distinguished by the presence of numerous pinhead-sized yellowish lesions that are densely clustered in round or oval formations.[4] Both are rare variants of cutaneous sarcoidosis with less than 10 cases reported in the literature. In our case, the boy had LPP-like hyperpigmentation with typical features of sarcoidosis on histopathology making this form of presentation yet another rare variant of cutaneous sarcoidosis and justifying it as a ‘great masquerader’. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first such presentation reported so far. Table 1 summarises a few of the rare variants of cutaneous sarcoidosis reported in the literature.

This case highlights the diagnostic challenge of rare types of cutaneous sarcoidosis, which can be easily misinterpreted for another common condition. Misdiagnosis, especially without frequent biopsy, may lead to underreporting. Clinical suspicion for sarcoidosis should be strong, especially in rare instances, and should be kept as a differential in such cases. Accurate diagnosis and treatment of cutaneous sarcoidosis need early detection and biopsy confirmation, improving patient outcomes.

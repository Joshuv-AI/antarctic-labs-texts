Convergence of Markovian Iteration for Z Z -Coupled FBSDEs via Gaussian Smoothing
Zhipeng Huang, Cornelis W. Oosterlee

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the Markovian iteration method for coupled forward-backward stochastic differential equations (FBSDEs) with drift b ⁡ ( t , X t , Y t , Z t ) b(t,X_{t},Y_{t},Z_{t}) and deterministic, time-dependent diffusion. Bender and Zhang (2008) established convergence results for Markovian iteration in the Y Y -coupled setting. Extending these results to equations with Z Z -coupling presents additional challenges, particularly in obtaining uniform control of the spatial Lipschitz constants of the discrete decoupling fields across iterations and time steps. We address this difficulty by representing the discrete Z Z -field through the spatial derivative of the Gaussian-smoothed Y Y -field. This representation, combined with weak differentiation, yields uniform spatial Lipschitz bounds for both discrete decoupling fields without requiring their classical second derivatives. Under suitable contraction conditions, we prove convergence of the Markovian iteration and well-posedness of the discrete scheme. With additional regularity assumptions, we derive an overall error bound accounting for both iteration and time discretization. Numerical experiments compare three practical implementations and demonstrate the effectiveness of the derivative-based and merged approaches.

Forward-backward stochastic differential equations, Markovian iteration, coupled FBSDEs, convergence analysis.

Introduction

In this paper, we study the numerical solution of a system of coupled forward-backward stochastic differential equations (FBSDEs) on a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
equipped with the augmented natural filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}
of a
d_{3}
-dimensional Brownian motion
W\coloneqq\{W_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq T}
,

\left\{\begin{aligned} X_{t}&=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b(s,X_{s},Y_{s},Z_{s})\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s)\mathrm{d}W_{s},\\
Y_{t}&=g(X_{T})+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,X_{s},Y_{s},Z_{s})\mathrm{d}s-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}\mathrm{d}W_{s},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
(X,Y,Z)\coloneqq\{(X_{t},Y_{t},Z_{t})\}_{0\leq t\leq T}
is a triplet of
(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}\times\mathbb{R}^{{d_{2}}\times{d_{3}}})
-valued and adapted stochastic processes. The functions
b:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}\times d_{3}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
,
\sigma:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{3}}
,
f:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}\times d_{3}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
, and
g:\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
are deterministic measurable mappings. The triplet
(X,Y,Z)
is a solution if it satisfies the required measurability and integrability conditions and (
1.1
) holds
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely for all
t\in[0,T]
; see
[
Zha17
,
MY99
]
. Furthermore, the solution
(X,Y,Z)
is linked to a system of parabolic PDEs through the nonlinear Feynman–Kac formula under suitable conditions,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u^{i}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{xx}u^{i}:\bigl(\sigma(t)\sigma(t)^{\top}\bigr)+(\nabla_{x}u^{i})^{\top}b\bigl(t,x,u,(\nabla_{x}u)^{\top}\sigma(t)\bigr)\\
&\qquad+f^{i}\bigl(t,x,u,(\nabla_{x}u)^{\top}\sigma(t)\bigr)=0,\qquad i=1,\ldots,d_{2},\\
&u(T,x)=g(x).\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

with

Y_{t}=u(t,X_{t}),\qquad Z_{t}=(\nabla_{x}u(t,X_{t}))^{\top}\sigma(t)\coloneqq v(t,X_{t}),

(1.3)

and the mappings
u
and
v
are referred to as decoupling fields in the FBSDEs literature.

The study of FBSDEs, or BSDEs in the decoupled case, dates back to the seminal work in
[
Bis73
]
and later
[
PP90
]
, who first investigated general nonlinear BSDEs. Due to their connection with quasi-linear PDEs through the nonlinear Feynman-Kac formula, FBSDEs have broad applications in mathematical finance, physics, and stochastic control. Significant progress has been made in their solution theory following
[
PP90
]
, including the method of contraction mapping
[
Ant93
,
PT99
]
, which establishes well-posedness under standard assumptions when
T
is small; the four-step scheme of
[
MPY94
]
, which removes the restriction on
T
under certain regularity conditions; and the method of continuation
[
HP95
,
PW99
]
, which extends to non-Markovian FBSDEs under a different set of assumptions. For a comprehensive discussion of these methods, see
[
MY99
]
.

Despite the rich theoretical background, numerical methods for FBSDEs remain a highly relevant area of research since these equations are often analytically intractable. Various methods have been developed for the decoupled case, including the Malliavin calculus method
[
BET04
,
BT04
,
CMT10
]
, the quantization method
[
BHJK23
,
PS18
]
, and regression-based approaches
[
LGW06
,
GLW05
]
. For the coupled case, numerical approximation is more challenging due to the intertwined nature of the forward and backward equations. Notable methods include the four-step scheme-based approach
[
MT06
]
, the Markovian iteration scheme
[
BZ08
]
, and Fourier expansion techniques
[
HRO16
,
NO25
]
. More recently, neural network-based algorithms, such as the Deep BSDE method
[
EHJ17
,
HJE18
,
HL20
]
, have gained popularity due to their high accuracy and effectiveness in handling high-dimensional problems. Establishing convergence of the deep BSDE method requires well-posedness and time-discretization error estimates for the underlying discrete FBSDE. The analysis in
[
HL20
]
builds on the results and techniques of
[
BZ08
]
, which treat forward coefficients independent of the
Z
process. Consequently, their convergence analysis is restricted to the
Y
-coupled setting. More recently,
[
RSZ24
]
used the method of continuation under monotonicity conditions to establish well-posedness and stability of fully coupled discrete FBSDEs, including McKean–Vlasov systems, and derive a posteriori error estimates for given approximations. Instead, our work uses different assumptions and proof techniques to establish convergence of the Markovian iteration under suitable contraction conditions, as detailed below.

In this article, we study a Markovian iteration scheme for FBSDEs with fully coupled drift
b(t,X_{t},Y_{t},Z_{t})
and time-dependent diffusion, using a backward step whose driver is implicit in
Y
. The additional
Z
-dependence in the forward equation is not considered in
[
BZ08
]
. The main difficulty is to control the spatial Lipschitz constants of the discrete
Z
-decoupling fields uniformly across time steps and iterations. Our main analytical tool is the Gaussian smoothing operator, also known as Gaussian convolution operator, which represents the discrete
Z
-field through the spatial derivative of the Gaussian-smoothed
Y
-field. Combining its smoothing properties with weak differentiation, the backward recursion, and terminal regularity, we establish an invariant Lipschitz class for both discrete decoupling fields, without requiring classical second derivatives of the iterated fields. These uniform bounds allow us to prove geometric convergence of the Markovian iteration with respect to the iteration index
m
, together with existence and uniqueness of a square-integrable solution to the discrete scheme under suitable contraction conditions. Under additional regularity assumptions, we also derive a time-discretization error estimate.

Our backward scheme evaluates the driver implicitly at
Y_{i}
, whereas
[
BZ08
]
evaluates it at
Y_{i+1}
. This implicit formulation is also used in the reference scheme underlying the convergence analysis of the deep BSDE method in
[
HL20
]
. Although we do not develop a deep BSDE method here, our results provide a theoretical building block for such analyses in the presence of
Z
-dependent forward drifts and deterministic, time-dependent diffusion. The implicit formulation also facilitates the numerical implementation of the Markovian iteration in Section
4
.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section
2
introduces the Markovian iteration scheme considered here, and its Gaussian smoothing representation of the discrete
Z
-field. Section
3
presents the required assumptions and preliminary estimates, including the properties of the Gaussian smoothing operator. It establishes uniform Lipschitz and growth bounds, proves geometric convergence of the Markovian iteration and well-posedness of the discrete FBSDE, and derives time-discretization and overall error estimates. Section
4
presents three numerical implementations and compares their performance through experiments with varying time-step sizes and numbers of Markovian iterations. Finally, Section
5
concludes the paper.

2
The Markovian iteration for FBSDEs with
Z
-coupling

We now introduce the Markovian iteration scheme for the FBSDE (
1.1
). For simplicity, we focus on the one-dimensional setting for the discussion and the convergence analysis.

Let
m
denote the iteration index and
N
the number of time steps. We consider the uniform grid
\pi=\{t_{i}=ih:i=0,1,\ldots,N\}
with step size
h\coloneqq T/N
, and write
\Delta W_{i}\coloneqq W_{t_{i+1}}-W_{t_{i}}
,
\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}[\cdot]\coloneqq\mathbb{E}[\cdot\mid\mathcal{F}_{t_{i}}]
. Following the approach in
[
BZ08
]
, we start from the initialization
u_{i}^{\pi,0}=v_{i}^{\pi,0}=0
for
i=0,\ldots,N-1
,
u_{N}^{\pi,0}=g
, and define, for
m\geq 1
,

\left\{\begin{aligned} X_{0}^{\pi,m}&=x_{0},\\
X_{i+1}^{\pi,m}&=X_{i}^{\pi,m}+h\,b\bigl(t_{i},X_{i}^{\pi,m},u_{i}^{\pi,m-1}(X_{i}^{\pi,m}),v_{i}^{\pi,m-1}(X_{i}^{\pi,m})\bigr)+\sigma(t_{i})\Delta W_{i},\\
Y_{N}^{\pi,m}&=g(X_{N}^{\pi,m}),\qquad u_{N}^{\pi,m}=g,\\
\widehat{Z}_{i}^{\pi,m}&=\frac{1}{h}\,\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}\left[Y_{i+1}^{\pi,m}\Delta W_{i}\right],\\
Y_{i}^{\pi,m}&=\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}\left[Y_{i+1}^{\pi,m}\right]+h\,f\bigl(t_{i},X_{i}^{\pi,m},Y_{i}^{\pi,m},\widehat{Z}_{i}^{\pi,m}\bigr),\\
u_{i}^{\pi,m}(X_{i}^{\pi,m})&=Y_{i}^{\pi,m},\\
v_{i}^{\pi,m}(X_{i}^{\pi,m})&=\widehat{Z}_{i}^{\pi,m}.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.1)

The forward recursion is performed for
i=0,\ldots,N-1
, followed by the backward recursion for
i=N-1,\ldots,0
. Throughout, the discrete decoupling fields
u_{i}^{\pi,m}
are defined for
0\leq i\leq N
, with
u_{N}^{\pi,m}=g
for every
m\geq 0
, whereas
v_{i}^{\pi,m}
is defined only for
0\leq i<N
. The fields
u_{i}^{\pi,m}
and
v_{i}^{\pi,m}
approximate
u(t_{i},\cdot)
and
v(t_{i},\cdot)
, respectively.

The identities in (
2.1
) specify the decoupling fields along the forward process. For the convergence analysis in the later section, we shall define
u_{i}^{\pi,m}
and
v_{i}^{\pi,m}
in a pointwise manner. To this end, fix
i\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
, and restart the scheme at time
t_{i}
from
X_{i}^{\pi,m}=x
, keeping the previous-iteration input fields fixed. We define
u_{i}^{\pi,m}(x)
and
v_{i}^{\pi,m}(x)
as the resulting initial backward values
Y_{i}^{\pi,m}
and
\widehat{Z}_{i}^{\pi,m}
, respectively. These values are deterministic and agree with the identities in (
2.1
) when evaluated along the original forward process. Together with
u_{N}^{\pi,m}=g
, this construction defines the fields pointwise and facilitates the subsequent regularity analysis.

In addition to the
Z
-coupling, our scheme differs from
[
BZ08
]
in that the backward equation in (
2.1
) is implicit in
Y_{i}^{\pi,m}
. At each backward step, once the forward process,
Y_{i+1}^{\pi,m}
, and
\widehat{Z}_{i}^{\pi,m}
are known,
Y_{i}^{\pi,m}
is determined by solving

Y_{i}^{\pi,m}=\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}\left[Y_{i+1}^{\pi,m}\right]+h\,f\left(t_{i},X_{i}^{\pi,m},Y_{i}^{\pi,m},\widehat{Z}_{i}^{\pi,m}\right).

(2.2)

This is the usual implicit Euler step for a decoupled FBSDE. If
f
is globally Lipschitz continuous in
y
, uniformly in
(t,x,z)
, and
h
is sufficiently small that
h\operatorname{Lip}(f(t,x,\cdot,z))<1
for all
(t,x,z)
, the right-hand side defines a contraction in
Y_{i}^{\pi,m}
, and thus the equation admits a unique solution. Here and below, we denote by
\operatorname{Lip}(\phi)
the global Lipschitz constant of a function
\phi
. We impose this condition
h\operatorname{Lip}(f(t,x,\cdot,z))<1
throughout the article without further notice. The advantages of this implicit formulation for numerical implementation will be used in Section
4
.

The contraction-based strategy in
[
BZ08
]
relies on uniform bounds for the spatial Lipschitz constants of the discrete
Y
-decoupling fields. Since successive iterates are evaluated along different forward processes, these bounds allow us to separate the effect of changing the forward state from the difference between the fields, with constants independent of the iteration index. Together with growth estimates and a suitable contraction condition, this yields summable bounds on successive differences, showing that
\{u_{i}^{\pi,m}(x)\}_{m\geq 1}
is a Cauchy sequence for each fixed partition
\pi
, time index
i
, and spatial point
x
. When the forward coefficients do not depend on
Z
as in
[
BZ08
]
, differences between the
Z
-processes can be controlled through backward stability estimates, without requiring uniform spatial Lipschitz bounds for the fields
v_{i}^{\pi,m}
. In the present setting, the additional
Z
-dependence of the forward drift requires us to extend this strategy by establishing such bounds for both
u_{i}^{\pi,m}
and
v_{i}^{\pi,m}
.

Our approach to controlling the spatial Lipschitz constants of
v_{i}^{\pi,m}
exploits the Gaussian structure of the one-step forward transition. To describe this connection, we introduce the Gaussian smoothing operator

(\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi)(a)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[\phi(a+\varsigma\sqrt{h}\,G)\right],\qquad G\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1),

(2.3)

for a fixed parameter
\varsigma\neq 0
and functions
\phi
for which the expectation is finite.

For a fixed time index
i
and state
x\in\mathbb{R}
, the conditional distribution of
X_{i+1}^{\pi,m}
given
X_{i}^{\pi,m}=x
is Gaussian with variance
\sigma(t_{i})^{2}h
, and mean

A_{i}^{\pi,m}(x)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[X_{i+1}^{\pi,m}\mid X_{i}^{\pi,m}=x\right]=x+h\,b\bigl(t_{i},x,u_{i}^{\pi,m-1}(x),v_{i}^{\pi,m-1}(x)\bigr).

(2.4)

Thus, the operator with
\varsigma=\sigma_{i}
, with the convention that
\sigma_{i}\coloneqq\sigma(t_{i})
, describes Gaussian averaging around this conditional mean. If
u_{i+1}^{\pi,m}
is globally Lipschitz continuous, the Gaussian differentiation formula yields

\displaystyle v_{i}^{\pi,m}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h}\mathbb{E}\left[u_{i+1}^{\pi,m}(X_{i+1}^{\pi,m})\Delta W_{i}\,\middle|\,X_{i}^{\pi,m}=x\right]

(2.5)

\displaystyle=\left.\sigma_{i}\,\partial_{a}\mathbb{E}\left[u_{i+1}^{\pi,m}(a+\sigma_{i}\Delta W_{i})\right]\right|_{a=A_{i}^{\pi,m}(x)}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{i}\,\bigl(\partial_{a}\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\sigma_{i}}u_{i+1}^{\pi,m}\bigr)\bigl(A_{i}^{\pi,m}(x)\bigr).

This representation expresses the discrete
Z
-field through the classical derivative of a Gaussian-smoothed
Y
-field, even when the original
Y
-field is not classically differentiable. It provides the starting point for our spatial regularity estimates. This Gaussian smoothing operator acts by convolution with a Gaussian density and is therefore also referred to as a Gaussian convolution operator. Its relevant properties will be stated in Proposition
3.2
.

With additional regularity, for example if
u_{i+1}^{\pi,m}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
with bounded derivative, we may differentiate under the expectation to obtain the classical Gaussian integration-by-parts representation,

v_{i}^{\pi,m}(x)=\sigma_{i}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\partial_{x}u_{i+1}^{\pi,m}(X_{i+1}^{\pi,m})\,\middle|\,X_{i}^{\pi,m}=x\right].

(2.6)

Using this representation to estimate
|v_{i}^{\pi,m}(x)-v_{i}^{\pi,m}(y)|
directly involves controlling differences of
\partial_{x}u_{i+1}^{\pi,m}
at two spatial points. In particular, if both
A_{i}^{\pi,m}
and
\partial_{x}u_{i+1}^{\pi,m}
are globally Lipschitz, a direct estimate gives
\operatorname{Lip}(v_{i}^{\pi,m})\leq|\sigma_{i}|\operatorname{Lip}(A_{i}^{\pi,m})\operatorname{Lip}(\partial_{x}u_{i+1}^{\pi,m})
. With the first two factors uniformly controlled, obtaining a uniform bound through this estimate would require uniform control of
\operatorname{Lip}(\partial_{x}u_{i+1}^{\pi,m})
. This is stronger than the existence and boundedness of the first derivative, and establishing such regularity uniformly in the time-step size, time index, and iteration index is not straightforward. Nevertheless, (
2.6
) remains useful for the numerical implementation in Section
4
.

For the convergence analysis in Section
3
, we instead use the Gaussian smoothing representation (
2.5
) to control the spatial Lipschitz constant of
v_{i}^{\pi,m}
through the second derivative of
\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\sigma_{i}}u_{i+1}^{\pi,m}
. By Gaussian smoothing, this derivative exists classically even when
u_{i+1}^{\pi,m}
is only globally Lipschitz. Using weak differentiation, the additional terminal regularity, together with a transition-operator estimate for bounded measurable functions, we establish
\operatorname{Lip}(v_{i}^{\pi,m})\leq C
uniformly in the time-step size, the time index and the iteration index. Together with the corresponding spatial Lipschitz and growth bounds for the
Y
-fields, these estimates allow us to prove convergence of the Markovian iteration under a suitable contraction condition. The argument follows the same core strategy as
[
BZ08
]
, establishing convergence to a fixed point, but uses different estimates that lead to a comparatively simple contraction condition in the present setting.

3
Convergence analysis

In this section, we analyze the convergence of the scheme using the Gaussian smoothing operator. Section
3.1
presents the assumptions and preliminary estimates, including the properties of this operator. Section
3.2
establishes uniform spatial Lipschitz bounds for the decoupling fields and a linear-growth estimate for the first iteration. Section
3.3
proves geometric convergence of the Markovian iteration under a suitable contraction condition. Finally, Section
3.4
establishes the time-discretization error and combines it with the iteration error to obtain an overall error estimate.

3.1
Preliminaries

We impose the following standard assumptions, together with additional regularity conditions on the diffusion coefficient
\sigma

.

The coefficient functions

\displaystyle\leq b_{x}|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{2}+b_{y}|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{2}+b_{z}|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2},

.

Assumption 3
.

The terminal condition
g\in C^{1,1}(\mathbb{R})
.

\varphi=\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N-1}
and
\xi=\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N-1}

F^{\pi}(\varphi,\xi)=(\Phi,\psi)

), with

replaced by

and the newly generated fields

; only the components with

Proposition 3.2

(Properties of the Gaussian convolution semigroup)
.

Fix
\varsigma\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
. For
h>0
and a function
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
that is either globally Lipschitz or bounded and measurable, define

(\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi)(a)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[\phi\left(a+\varsigma\sqrt{h}\,G\right)\right],\qquad G\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1),

(3.8)

and let
\mathcal{G}_{0}^{\varsigma}
be the identity operator.

The family
\{\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\}_{h\geq 0}
satisfies the semigroup property
\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\mathcal{G}_{s}^{\varsigma}\phi=\mathcal{G}_{h+s}^{\varsigma}\phi
for
h,s\geq 0
. Moreover, the following properties hold for every
h>0
:

(i)

Gaussian smoothing.
If
\phi
is globally Lipschitz or bounded and measurable, then

\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}).

(3.9)

For
\phi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, the operator is independent of the chosen measurable representative and satisfies

\|\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}.

(3.10)

(ii)

Gaussian differentiation formula.
If
\phi
is globally Lipschitz or bounded and measurable, then, for every
a\in\mathbb{R}
,

\partial_{a}(\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi)(a)=\frac{1}{\varsigma\sqrt{h}}\mathbb{E}\left[\phi(a+\varsigma\sqrt{h}\,G)G\right].

(3.11)

Equivalently, if
\Delta W\sim\mathcal{N}(0,h)
, then

\partial_{a}(\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi)(a)=\frac{1}{\varsigma h}\mathbb{E}\left[\phi(a+\varsigma\Delta W)\Delta W\right].

(3.12)

(iii)

Commutation with weak differentiation.
If
\phi
is globally Lipschitz, then it is locally absolutely continuous and differentiable almost everywhere. Its almost-everywhere derivative coincides with its weak derivative, denoted by
\phi^{\prime}
, and satisfies

\phi^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}),\qquad\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\operatorname{Lip}(\phi).

(3.13)

Furthermore,

\partial_{a}(\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi)(a)=(\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi^{\prime})(a),\qquad a\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.14)

Since
\phi^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, properties (
i
) and (
ii
) also apply to
\phi^{\prime}
. In particular,

\partial_{aa}(\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi)(a)=\partial_{a}(\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi^{\prime})(a)=\frac{1}{\varsigma\sqrt{h}}\mathbb{E}\left[\phi^{\prime}(a+\varsigma\sqrt{h}\,G)G\right],\qquad a\in\mathbb{R},

(3.15)

without requiring a classical second derivative of
\phi
.

To accommodate our time-dependent diffusion setting as in (
2.1
), we apply the operator with
\varsigma=\sigma_{i}
and write

\mathcal{G}_{i}\coloneqq\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\sigma_{i}},\qquad 0\leq i<N.

(3.16)

The properties in Proposition
3.2
therefore apply to each
\mathcal{G}_{i}
.

Next, we establish general estimates showing that the transition operator of a discrete-time process with additive Gaussian noise maps bounded measurable functions to Lipschitz continuous functions and propagates the Lipschitz continuity of Lipschitz input functions. These estimates will be used to establish uniform spatial Lipschitz bounds for the discrete
Z
-fields.

Lemma 3.3

(Smoothing for discrete-time processes with additive Gaussian noise)
.

Let
k<\ell
. For each
x\in\mathbb{R}
, consider the discrete-time process
\{X_{r}^{k,x}\}_{r=k}^{\ell}
defined on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{r}\}_{r=k}^{\ell},\mathbb{P})
by

X_{k}^{k,x}=x,\qquad X_{r+1}^{k,x}=X_{r}^{k,x}+hD_{r}(X_{r}^{k,x})+\sigma_{r}\sqrt{h}\,G_{r},\qquad r=k,\ldots,\ell-1,

(3.17)

where
\{G_{r}\}_{r=k}^{\ell-1}
are independent standard normal random variables, and
\mathcal{F}_{r}
is generated by
G_{k},\ldots,G_{r-1}
together with all
\mathbb{P}
-null sets. The drift functions
D_{r}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
are deterministic and satisfy
|D_{r}(x)-D_{r}(y)|\leq K_{D}|x-y|
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
and some constant
K_{D}\geq 0
independent of
r
, and the deterministic diffusion coefficients satisfy
|\sigma_{r}|\geq\underline{\sigma}>0
.

For a measurable function
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, define the transition operator
\mathcal{P}_{k,\ell}
of the process (
3.17
) by

(\mathcal{P}_{k,\ell}\phi)(x)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[\phi(X_{\ell}^{k,x})\right],\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},

(3.18)

whenever the expectation is well defined. Thus,
\mathcal{P}_{k,\ell}\phi
is the deterministic function of the initial state
x
obtained by propagating
\phi
through the discrete process from time
t_{k}
to time
t_{\ell}
.

Then, for every
\phi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, with
\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}
denoting the essential supremum norm,

\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{P}_{k,\ell}\phi)\leq\frac{1+K_{D}(t_{\ell}-t_{k})}{\underline{\sigma}\sqrt{t_{\ell}-t_{k}}}\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}},

(3.19)

Moreover, if
\phi
is globally Lipschitz, then

\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{P}_{k,\ell}\phi)\leq e^{K_{D}(t_{\ell}-t_{k})}\operatorname{Lip}(\phi).

(3.20)

Proof.

Fix
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, and define the number of steps
n\coloneqq\ell-k
and the initial state difference
\delta\coloneqq y-x
.

We first prove (
3.19
). Alongside
X^{k,x}
, we construct a process
Y^{k,y}
starting from
Y_{k}^{k,y}=y
such that

Y_{r}^{k,y}-X_{r}^{k,x}=\frac{\ell-r}{n}\delta,\qquad r=k,\ldots,\ell.

(3.21)

In particular, we have
Y_{\ell}^{k,y}=X_{\ell}^{k,x}
almost surely.

Let us write discrete processes as

\displaystyle X_{r+1}^{k,x}=X_{r}^{k,x}+hD_{r}(X_{r}^{k,x})+\sigma_{r}\sqrt{h}\,G_{r},\qquad Y_{r+1}^{k,y}=Y_{r}^{k,y}+hD_{r}(Y_{r}^{k,y})+\sigma_{r}\sqrt{h}\,(G_{r}+\Gamma_{r}).

(3.22)

To enforce condition (
3.21
), we can choose
\Gamma_{r}
such that

\sigma_{r}\sqrt{h}\,\Gamma_{r}=-\frac{\delta}{n}-h\left[D_{r}(Y_{r}^{k,y})-D_{r}(X_{r}^{k,x})\right].

(3.23)

Then a direct calculation gives

Y_{r+1}^{k,y}-X_{r+1}^{k,x}=Y_{r}^{k,y}-X_{r}^{k,x}-\frac{\delta}{n}=\frac{\ell-r-1}{n}\delta,

(3.24)

and the condition (
3.21
) follows by induction.

With this choice of
\Gamma_{r}
, we use the Lipschitz continuity of
D_{r}
and (
3.21
) and obtain

\displaystyle|\Gamma_{r}|\leq\frac{1}{\underline{\sigma}\sqrt{h}}\left[\frac{|\delta|}{n}+hK_{D}\frac{\ell-r}{n}|\delta|\right]\leq\frac{|\delta|}{\underline{\sigma}\sqrt{h}\,n}\left(1+K_{D}nh\right).

Consequently, since
nh=t_{\ell}-t_{k}
,

\sum_{r=k}^{\ell-1}|\Gamma_{r}|^{2}\leq\frac{|\delta|^{2}}{\underline{\sigma}^{2}(t_{\ell}-t_{k})}\left(1+K_{D}(t_{\ell}-t_{k})\right)^{2}.

(3.25)

Let us define

\Lambda\coloneqq\exp\left(-\sum_{r=k}^{\ell-1}\Gamma_{r}G_{r}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r=k}^{\ell-1}|\Gamma_{r}|^{2}\right).

(3.26)

Since
\Gamma_{r}
is
\mathcal{F}_{r}
-measurable and
G_{r}
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{r}
, the moment-generating function of a standard normal random variable gives

\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\exp\left(-\Gamma_{r}G_{r}-\frac{1}{2}|\Gamma_{r}|^{2}\right)\right|\mathcal{F}_{r}\right]=1.

(3.27)

Together with the deterministic bound (
3.25
), successive conditioning therefore gives
\mathbb{E}[\Lambda]=1
.

Hence,
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}=\Lambda\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}
defines a probability measure. The discrete Gaussian change-of-measure formula implies that, under
\mathbb{Q}
, the shifted increments
G_{r}+\Gamma_{r}
are independent standard normal random variables. Consequently, under
\mathbb{Q}
,
Y^{k,y}
has the same law as the discrete-time process (
3.17
) starting from
y
.

For
\phi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, using the definition of the transition operator, the identity
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}=\Lambda\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}
, and the fact that
Y_{\ell}^{k,y}=X_{\ell}^{k,x}
, we obtain

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{k,\ell}\phi)(y)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\phi(Y_{\ell}^{k,y})\right]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\Lambda\phi(Y_{\ell}^{k,y})\right]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\Lambda\phi(X_{\ell}^{k,x})\right],

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{k,\ell}\phi)(x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\phi(X_{\ell}^{k,x})\right],

Since
X_{\ell}^{k,x}
has a density with respect to Lebesgue measure,
|\phi(X_{\ell}^{k,x})|\leq\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}
,
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left|(\mathcal{P}_{k,\ell}\phi)(y)-(\mathcal{P}_{k,\ell}\phi)(x)\right|=\left|\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[(\Lambda-1)\phi(X_{\ell}^{k,x})\right]\right|\leq\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}|\Lambda-1|.

(3.28)

We next derive a bound for
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}|\Lambda-1|
. Let
H(\mathbb{Q}\mid\mathbb{P})
denote the relative entropy of
\mathbb{Q}
with respect to
\mathbb{P}
, and let
\|\mathbb{Q}-\mathbb{P}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
denote their total variation distance. Since
G_{r}+\Gamma_{r}
is conditionally centered under
\mathbb{Q}
given
\mathcal{F}_{r}
, we obtain

H(\mathbb{Q}\mid\mathbb{P})=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[\log\Lambda]=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\sum_{r=k}^{\ell-1}|\Gamma_{r}|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{|\delta|^{2}}{2\underline{\sigma}^{2}(t_{\ell}-t_{k})}\left(1+K_{D}(t_{\ell}-t_{k})\right)^{2}.

(3.29)

The
L^{1}
representation of total variation distance and Pinsker’s inequality give

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}|\Lambda-1|=2\|\mathbb{Q}-\mathbb{P}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\sqrt{2H(\mathbb{Q}\mid\mathbb{P})}\leq\frac{1+K_{D}(t_{\ell}-t_{k})}{\underline{\sigma}\sqrt{t_{\ell}-t_{k}}}|x-y|.

(3.30)

Combining this estimate with (
3.28
) proves (
3.19
).

Finally, we prove (
3.20
). Let
X^{k,x}
and
X^{k,y}
be two processes satisfying (
3.17
), driven by the same Gaussian increments, but start from different states
x
and
y
, respectively. Then using the Lipschitz continuity of
D_{r}
gives

|X_{r+1}^{k,x}-X_{r+1}^{k,y}|\leq(1+hK_{D})|X_{r}^{k,x}-X_{r}^{k,y}|.

(3.31)

and iterating this estimate gives

|X_{\ell}^{k,x}-X_{\ell}^{k,y}|\leq(1+hK_{D})^{\ell-k}|x-y|\leq e^{K_{D}(t_{\ell}-t_{k})}|x-y|.

(3.32)

Therefore, if
\phi
is globally Lipschitz, we have

\displaystyle\left|(\mathcal{P}_{k,\ell}\phi)(x)-(\mathcal{P}_{k,\ell}\phi)(y)\right|\leq\mathbb{E}\left[|\phi(X_{\ell}^{k,x})-\phi(X_{\ell}^{k,y})|\right]\leq\operatorname{Lip}(\phi)e^{K_{D}(t_{\ell}-t_{k})}|x-y|.

∎

3.2
Lipschitz continuity and linear growth bounds

We first establish an invariant Lipschitz class, in the sense that, if the input discrete fields satisfy prescribed spatial Lipschitz bounds, the output discrete fields obtained after one Markovian iteration satisfy the same bounds.

Let
\bar{L},\bar{V}>0
be candidate bounds for the squared Lipschitz constants of the
Y
- and
Z
-fields, respectively, and define, for later use,

K_{B}\coloneqq\sqrt{b_{x}+b_{y}\bar{L}+b_{z}\bar{V}}.

(3.33)

Recall also that
\theta_{2},\theta_{3}
are the constants introduced in Lemma
3.1
.

Theorem 3.4

(Invariant Lipschitz class)
.

Let Assumptions
1
–
3
hold. Let
h_{0}>0
be chosen such that the implicit backward step is well defined and the conditions of Lemma
3.1
hold for every
0<h\leq h_{0}
.

Suppose that
\bar{L},\bar{V}>0
satisfy the conditions

\left(g_{x}+\theta_{3}T\right)e^{(\theta_{2}+2K_{B})T}\leq\bar{L},

(3.34)

\displaystyle\overline{\sigma}^{2}(1+h_{0}K_{B})^{2}\Bigg[e^{K_{B}T}\operatorname{Lip}(g^{\prime})+\frac{2(1+K_{B}T)\sqrt{T}}{\underline{\sigma}}\left(K_{B}\sqrt{\bar{L}}+\sqrt{f_{x}+f_{y}\bar{L}+f_{z}\bar{V}}\right)\Bigg]^{2}\leq\bar{V}.

(3.35)

Then, for every pair of globally Lipschitz input fields
\varphi=\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N-1}
and
\xi=\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N-1}
satisfying

L(\varphi)\leq\bar{L},\qquad L(\xi)\leq\bar{V},

(3.36)

the output fields
(\Phi,\psi)=F^{\pi}(\varphi,\xi)
satisfy

L(\Phi)\leq\bar{L},\qquad L(\psi)\leq\bar{V}.

(3.37)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq i\leq N-1}\left\|\partial_{aa}\mathcal{G}_{i}\Phi_{i+1}\right\|_{\infty}\leq{}

\displaystyle e^{K_{B}T}\operatorname{Lip}(g^{\prime})

(3.38)

\displaystyle+\frac{2(1+K_{B}T)\sqrt{T}}{\underline{\sigma}}\left(K_{B}\sqrt{\bar{L}}+\sqrt{f_{x}+f_{y}\bar{L}+f_{z}\bar{V}}\right).

Proof.

Let us fix a uniform partition
\pi
with step size
0<h\leq h_{0}
such that Lemma
3.1
holds. Throughout the proof, we suppress the dependence of the fields and processes on
\pi
and omit the iteration index, since only this single application of
F^{\pi}
is considered.

For a state
x
at time
t_{i}
, the one-step forward transition is

X_{i+1}^{i,x}=x+h\,b\left(t_{i},x,\varphi_{i}(x),\xi_{i}(x)\right)+\sigma_{i}\Delta W_{i}=A_{i}(x)+\sigma_{i}\Delta W_{i},\qquad\Delta W_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,h).

(3.39)

where
A_{i}(x)\coloneqq x+h\,b(t_{i},x,\varphi_{i}(x),\xi_{i}(x))
.

We divide the proof into two parts, treating the
Y
-field and the
Z
-field, respectively.

Part 1: Uniform Lipschitz bound for the
Y
-field.

Starting from
\Phi_{N}=g
, we proceed by backward induction. Fix
i\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}
and suppose that
\Phi_{i+1}
is globally Lipschitz. By Assumption
1
, (
3.36
), and the definition of
K_{B}
, we have

\displaystyle|A_{i}(x)-A_{i}(y)|

\displaystyle=\left|x-y+h\left[b\left(t_{i},x,\varphi_{i}(x),\xi_{i}(x)\right)-b\left(t_{i},y,\varphi_{i}(y),\xi_{i}(y)\right)\right]\right|

(3.40)

\displaystyle\leq|x-y|+h\left|b\left(t_{i},x,\varphi_{i}(x),\xi_{i}(x)\right)-b\left(t_{i},y,\varphi_{i}(y),\xi_{i}(y)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq(1+hK_{B})|x-y|.

For
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, let
X_{i+1}^{i,x}
and
X_{i+1}^{i,y}
be the one-step forward transitions in (
3.39
), starting from
X_{i}^{i,x}=x
and
X_{i}^{i,y}=y
, respectively, and driven by the same Brownian increment. Then

|X_{i+1}^{i,x}-X_{i+1}^{i,y}|^{2}=|A_{i}(x)-A_{i}(y)|^{2}\leq(1+hK_{B})^{2}|x-y|^{2}.

(3.41)

Applying Lemma
3.1
and dropping the nonnegative
Z
-term,

\displaystyle|\Phi_{i}(x)-\Phi_{i}(y)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\theta_{2}h)\mathbb{E}\left[|\Phi_{i+1}(X_{i+1}^{i,x})-\Phi_{i+1}(X_{i+1}^{i,y})|^{2}\right]+\theta_{3}h|x-y|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left[(1+\theta_{2}h)(1+hK_{B})^{2}L(\Phi_{i+1})+\theta_{3}h\right]|x-y|^{2}.

Thus,

L(\Phi_{i})\leq(1+\theta_{2}h)(1+hK_{B})^{2}L(\Phi_{i+1})+\theta_{3}h.

(3.42)

Since
\Phi_{N}=g
is globally Lipschitz, this recursion establishes global Lipschitz continuity of every
\Phi_{i}
by backward induction.

Using
L(\Phi_{N})=L(g)\leq g_{x}
and the elementary inequality
1+r\leq e^{r}
for any
r\geq 0
, we have the following by iterating (
3.42
),

L(\Phi)\leq\left(g_{x}+\theta_{3}T\right)e^{(\theta_{2}+2K_{B})T}.

(3.43)

By condition (
3.34
), we prove the first result,

L(\Phi)\leq\bar{L}.

(3.44)

Part 2: Gaussian representation and Lipschitz continuity of the
Z
-field.

By Part 1, each
\Phi_{i}
is globally Lipschitz. Rademacher’s theorem implies that it is differentiable almost everywhere. Moreover, Lipschitz functions on
\mathbb{R}
are locally absolutely continuous, so integration by parts shows that this almost-everywhere derivative coincides with the weak derivative. We denote it by
w_{i}\coloneqq\partial_{x}\Phi_{i}
.

Moreover,

\|w_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\operatorname{Lip}(\Phi_{i})\leq\sqrt{\bar{L}},\qquad i=0,\ldots,N.

(3.45)

Here and below,
L^{\infty}
-norms are essential suprema with respect to Lebesgue measure. The weak derivatives are defined only almost everywhere, but their values on the remaining Lebesgue-null sets may be chosen arbitrarily. Since
\sigma_{i}\neq 0
, the Gaussian random variables used below have densities and hit these sets with probability zero. These choices therefore do not change the corresponding expectations.

Let us recall the definition of the operator
\mathcal{G}_{i}
as in (
3.16
) and its properties stated in Proposition
3.2
. Then we can write one-step backward equation
\Phi_{i}(x)
as follows

\displaystyle\Phi_{i}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\Phi_{i+1}\left(X_{i+1}^{i,x}\right)\right]+hf\left(t_{i},x,\Phi_{i}(x),\psi_{i}(x)\right)

(3.46)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\Phi_{i+1}\left(A_{i}(x)+\sigma_{i}\Delta W_{i}\right)\right]+hf\left(t_{i},x,\Phi_{i}(x),\psi_{i}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{G}_{i}\Phi_{i+1})(A_{i}(x))+hf_{i}(x)

where
f_{i}(x)\coloneqq f\left(t_{i},x,\Phi_{i}(x),\psi_{i}(x)\right)
.

Similarly, and additionally using (
3.12
), the
Z
-field is given by

\displaystyle\psi_{i}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h}\mathbb{E}\left[\Phi_{i+1}\left(X_{i+1}^{i,x}\right)\Delta W_{i}\right]

(3.47)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h}\mathbb{E}\left[\Phi_{i+1}\left(A_{i}(x)+\sigma_{i}\Delta W_{i}\right)\Delta W_{i}\right]

\displaystyle=\sigma_{i}\left(\partial_{a}\mathcal{G}_{i}\Phi_{i+1}\right)(A_{i}(x)),

and by Proposition
3.2
,
\partial_{a}\mathcal{G}_{i}\Phi_{i+1}=\mathcal{G}_{i}w_{i+1}
, and therefore we can further write

\psi_{i}(x)=\sigma_{i}\left(\mathcal{G}_{i}w_{i+1}\right)(A_{i}(x)).

(3.48)

We now prove
L(\psi_{i})\leq\bar{V}
and the smoothed second-derivative estimate by backward induction.

Let
i=N-1
. Since
\Phi_{N}=g
, we have
w_{N}=g^{\prime}
, and therefore

\operatorname{Lip}\left(\mathcal{G}_{N-1}w_{N}\right)=\operatorname{Lip}\left(\mathcal{G}_{N-1}g^{\prime}\right)\leq\operatorname{Lip}(g^{\prime}).

(3.49)

Using (
3.48
) and (
3.40
),

\displaystyle|\psi_{N-1}(x)-\psi_{N-1}(y)|

\displaystyle\leq|\sigma_{N-1}|\,\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{G}_{N-1}g^{\prime})|A_{N-1}(x)-A_{N-1}(y)|

\displaystyle\leq|\sigma_{N-1}|(1+hK_{B})\operatorname{Lip}(g^{\prime})|x-y|.

Consequently, by condition (
3.35
),

L(\psi_{N-1})\leq\overline{\sigma}^{2}(1+h_{0}K_{B})^{2}\operatorname{Lip}(g^{\prime})^{2}\leq\bar{V}.

(3.50)

Moreover, using Proposition
3.2
we can write
\partial_{aa}\mathcal{G}_{N-1}\Phi_{N}=\partial_{a}\mathcal{G}_{N-1}g^{\prime}
, and
\partial_{a}\mathcal{G}_{N-1}g^{\prime}
is smooth. Therefore
\partial_{aa}\mathcal{G}_{N-1}\Phi_{N}
exists classically and we have

\left\|\partial_{aa}\mathcal{G}_{N-1}\Phi_{N}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{G}_{N-1}g^{\prime})\leq\operatorname{Lip}(g^{\prime}).

(3.51)

Thus the required smoothed second-derivative estimate also holds at
i=N-1
.

Now we consider
i\leq N-2
, and suppose that

L(\psi_{j})\leq\bar{V},\qquad j=i+1,\ldots,N-1.

(3.52)

We show that
L(\psi_{i})\leq\bar{V}
at the current index
i
and establish the corresponding smoothed second-derivative estimate.

For
j=i+1,\ldots,N-1
, define
f_{j}(x)\coloneqq f\left(t_{j},x,\Phi_{j}(x),\psi_{j}(x)\right)
. The induction hypothesis and (
3.44
) imply

\displaystyle|f_{j}(x)-f_{j}(y)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq f_{x}|x-y|^{2}+f_{y}|\Phi_{j}(x)-\Phi_{j}(y)|^{2}+f_{z}|\psi_{j}(x)-\psi_{j}(y)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(f_{x}+f_{y}\bar{L}+f_{z}\bar{V}\right)|x-y|^{2}.

Hence
f_{j}
is globally Lipschitz and its weak derivative satisfies

\|f_{j}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\sqrt{f_{x}+f_{y}\bar{L}+f_{z}\bar{V}}.

(3.53)

Likewise, define
b_{j}(x)\coloneqq b\left(t_{j},x,\varphi_{j}(x),\xi_{j}(x)\right)
, and let
b_{j}^{\prime}
denote its almost-everywhere, equivalently weak derivative. Then we have

\|b_{j}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\operatorname{Lip}(b_{j})\leq K_{B},

(3.54)

and since
A_{j}(x)=x+hb_{j}(x)
, we have
A_{j}^{\prime}(x)=1+hb_{j}^{\prime}(x)
for almost every
x
.

Since
\mathcal{G}_{j}\Phi_{j+1}
is smooth, the ordinary chain rule applies at every point where
A_{j}
and
f_{j}
are differentiable. Combining this with (
3.14
) in Proposition
3.2
, we obtain, for almost every
x
,
\partial_{x}\Phi_{j}(x)=w_{j}(x)
, and

\displaystyle w_{j}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\partial_{a}\mathcal{G}_{j}\Phi_{j+1}\right)(A_{j}(x))A_{j}^{\prime}(x)+hf_{j}^{\prime}(x)

(3.55)

\displaystyle=\left(\partial_{a}\mathcal{G}_{j}\Phi_{j+1}\right)(A_{j}(x))\left(1+hb_{j}^{\prime}(x)\right)+hf_{j}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{G}_{j}w_{j+1}\right)(A_{j}(x))\left(1+hb_{j}^{\prime}(x)\right)+hf_{j}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{G}_{j}w_{j+1}\right)(A_{j}(x))+hb_{j}^{\prime}(x)\left(\mathcal{G}_{j}w_{j+1}\right)(A_{j}(x))+hf_{j}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=(Q_{j}w_{j+1})(x)+hR_{j}(x),

where we define the operator
Q_{j}
and the function
R_{j}
by

(Q_{j}\phi)(x)=(\mathcal{G}_{j}\phi)(A_{j}(x)),\qquad R_{j}(x)\coloneqq b_{j}^{\prime}(x)(Q_{j}w_{j+1})(x)+f_{j}^{\prime}(x),

(3.56)

where
R_{j}
is defined for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Iterating (
3.55
) from
i+1
to
N-1
and using the terminal condition
w_{N}=g^{\prime}
gives, almost everywhere,

w_{i+1}=Q_{i+1}\cdots Q_{N-1}g^{\prime}+h\sum_{j=i+1}^{N-1}Q_{i+1}\cdots Q_{j-1}R_{j},

(3.57)

where an empty product is interpreted as the identity, and in particular, for
j=i+1
,
Q_{i+1}\cdots Q_{j-1}=I
.

Applying the operator
\mathcal{G}_{i}
to both sides and defining
\mathcal{R}_{i,j}\coloneqq\mathcal{G}_{i}Q_{i+1}\cdots Q_{j-1}
for
j\geq i+1
, with the same convention for empty products, we obtain

\mathcal{G}_{i}w_{i+1}=\mathcal{R}_{i,N}g^{\prime}+h\sum_{j=i+1}^{N-1}\mathcal{R}_{i,j}R_{j}.

(3.58)

To identify the operator
\mathcal{R}_{i,j}
, let us first recall the definition that

(Q_{r}\phi)(x)=(\mathcal{G}_{r}\phi)(A_{r}(x))=\mathbb{E}\left[\phi\left(x+hb_{r}(x)+\sigma_{r}\sqrt{h}\,G_{r}\right)\right].

(3.59)

where
b_{r}(x)=b\left(t_{r},x,\varphi_{r}(x),\xi_{r}(x)\right)
, and the variables
G_{r}
are independent standard normal random variables. Thus
Q_{r}
is the one-step transition operator of an Euler scheme with drift
b_{r}
, whereas
\mathcal{G}_{i}
is the one-step transition operator with zero drift and diffusion coefficient
\sigma_{i}
.

Accordingly,
\mathcal{R}_{i,j}
can be identified with the transition operator over
[t_{i},t_{j}]
of an auxiliary discrete-time process with additive Gaussian noise whose first step has zero drift and whose subsequent drift functions are given by
b_{r}
, with diffusion coefficients
\sigma_{i},\ldots,\sigma_{j-1}
. These coefficients are deterministic and satisfy
|\sigma_{r}|\geq\underline{\sigma}
. Since
\operatorname{Lip}(b_{r})\leq K_{B}
, all drift functions of this auxiliary chain have Lipschitz constant bounded by
K_{B}
.

To use Lemma
3.3
, we need an estimate for
\|R_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}
. Since
w_{j+1}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, Proposition
3.2
gives
|(\mathcal{G}_{j}w_{j+1})(a)|\leq\|w_{j+1}\|_{L^{\infty}}
for every
a\in\mathbb{R}
. Evaluating at
a=A_{j}(x)
yields

\|Q_{j}w_{j+1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|w_{j+1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\sqrt{\bar{L}}.

(3.60)

Together with (
3.53
) and (
3.54
), this yields

\|R_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq K_{B}\sqrt{\bar{L}}+\sqrt{f_{x}+f_{y}\bar{L}+f_{z}\bar{V}}.

(3.61)

Therefore, using Lemma
3.3
and (
3.61
) yields

\displaystyle\operatorname{Lip}\left(\mathcal{R}_{i,N}g^{\prime}\right)\leq e^{K_{B}T}\operatorname{Lip}(g^{\prime}),\qquad\operatorname{Lip}\left(\mathcal{R}_{i,j}R_{j}\right)\leq\frac{1+K_{B}T}{\underline{\sigma}\sqrt{t_{j}-t_{i}}}\left(K_{B}\sqrt{\bar{L}}+\sqrt{f_{x}+f_{y}\bar{L}+f_{z}\bar{V}}\right).

(3.62)

Consequently, using (
3.58
) we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Lip}\left(\mathcal{G}_{i}w_{i+1}\right)\leq e^{K_{B}T}\operatorname{Lip}(g^{\prime})+\frac{1+K_{B}T}{\underline{\sigma}}\left(K_{B}\sqrt{\bar{L}}+\sqrt{f_{x}+f_{y}\bar{L}+f_{z}\bar{V}}\right)h\sum_{j=i+1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t_{j}-t_{i}}}.

(3.63)

Since

h\sum_{j=i+1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t_{j}-t_{i}}}=\sqrt{h}\sum_{r=1}^{N-i-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\leq 2\sqrt{T},

(3.64)

we obtain

\displaystyle\operatorname{Lip}\left(\mathcal{G}_{i}w_{i+1}\right)\leq e^{K_{B}T}\operatorname{Lip}(g^{\prime})+\frac{2(1+K_{B}T)\sqrt{T}}{\underline{\sigma}}\left(K_{B}\sqrt{\bar{L}}+\sqrt{f_{x}+f_{y}\bar{L}+f_{z}\bar{V}}\right).

(3.65)

Note that by Proposition
3.2
, we can write
\partial_{a}\mathcal{G}_{i}\Phi_{i+1}=\mathcal{G}_{i}w_{i+1}
, and
\mathcal{G}_{i}w_{i+1}
is smooth. Therefore
\partial_{aa}\mathcal{G}_{i}\Phi_{i+1}
exists in the classical sense, and we have

\left\|\partial_{aa}\mathcal{G}_{i}\Phi_{i+1}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\operatorname{Lip}\left(\mathcal{G}_{i}w_{i+1}\right).

(3.66)

Using (
3.65
) yields the estimate (
3.38
) at the current index
i
.

Finally, using (
3.48
), (
3.40
), and (
3.65
),

\displaystyle|\psi_{i}(x)-\psi_{i}(y)|

\displaystyle\leq|\sigma_{i}|\,\operatorname{Lip}\left(\mathcal{G}_{i}w_{i+1}\right)|A_{i}(x)-A_{i}(y)|

\displaystyle\leq|\sigma_{i}|(1+hK_{B})\Bigg[e^{K_{B}T}\operatorname{Lip}(g^{\prime})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{2(1+K_{B}T)\sqrt{T}}{\underline{\sigma}}\left(K_{B}\sqrt{\bar{L}}+\sqrt{f_{x}+f_{y}\bar{L}+f_{z}\bar{V}}\right)\Bigg]|x-y|.

Hence, by condition (
3.35
),

\displaystyle L(\psi_{i})\leq\overline{\sigma}^{2}(1+h_{0}K_{B})^{2}\Bigg[e^{K_{B}T}\operatorname{Lip}(g^{\prime})+\frac{2(1+K_{B}T)\sqrt{T}}{\underline{\sigma}}\left(K_{B}\sqrt{\bar{L}}+\sqrt{f_{x}+f_{y}\bar{L}+f_{z}\bar{V}}\right)\Bigg]^{2}\leq\bar{V}

This completes the induction, and therefore we can conclude that
L(\psi_{i})\leq\bar{V}
,
i=0,\ldots,N-1
, and obtain the desired estimate (
3.38
).

∎

Having established that one Markovian iteration preserves the Lipschitz bounds, uniform bounds across all iterations follow immediately by induction.

Corollary 3.5

(Uniform Lipschitz bounds for the Markovian iteration)
.

Suppose the assumptions of Theorem
3.4
hold. Initialize
u_{i}^{\pi,0}=v_{i}^{\pi,0}=0
for
i=0,\ldots,N-1
, and set
u_{N}^{\pi,0}=g
. Then, for every
m\geq 0
,

L(u^{\pi,m})\leq\bar{L},\qquad L(v^{\pi,m})\leq\bar{V}.

(3.67)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\sup_{m\geq 1}\sup_{0\leq i\leq N-1}\left\|\partial_{aa}\mathcal{G}_{i}u_{i+1}^{\pi,m}\right\|_{\infty}

(3.68)

\displaystyle\leq e^{K_{B}T}\operatorname{Lip}(g^{\prime})+\frac{2(1+K_{B}T)\sqrt{T}}{\underline{\sigma}}\left(K_{B}\sqrt{\bar{L}}+\sqrt{f_{x}+f_{y}\bar{L}+f_{z}\bar{V}}\right).

Proof.

The initial fields belong to the invariant class, since
L(g)\leq g_{x}\leq\bar{L}
. Applying Theorem
3.4
successively proves the Lipschitz bounds by induction on
m
. The smoothed second-derivative bound follows from the same theorem at each iteration
m\geq 1
.
∎

Remark 1

(Uniform boundedness of the discrete
Z
-fields)
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
3.4
, we can write
\psi_{i}(x)=\sigma_{i}(\mathcal{G}_{i}w_{i+1})(A_{i}(x))
, and therefore

|\psi_{i}(x)|^{2}\leq\overline{\sigma}^{2}\|w_{i+1}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\leq\overline{\sigma}^{2}\bar{L}.

(3.69)

Hence the discrete
Z
-fields are uniformly bounded, independently of the partition and the iteration index.

Remark 2

(Feasibility of the invariant conditions)
.

The conditions (
3.34
) and (
3.35
) in Theorem
3.4
are coupled through
K_{B}=\sqrt{b_{x}+b_{y}\bar{L}+b_{z}\bar{V}}
and the term
\sqrt{f_{x}+f_{y}\bar{L}+f_{z}\bar{V}}
. Nevertheless, they define a nonempty parameter regime.

One simple choice is to fix
\bar{L}
and
\bar{V}
such that

\bar{L}>g_{x},\qquad\bar{V}>\overline{\sigma}^{2}\operatorname{Lip}(g^{\prime})^{2}.

(3.70)

Then
K_{B}
is fixed, and the left-hand side of (
3.34
) satisfies

(g_{x}+\theta_{3}T)e^{(\theta_{2}+2K_{B})T}\longrightarrow g_{x}\qquad\text{as }T\downarrow 0.

(3.71)

Similarly, as
T\downarrow 0
and
h_{0}\downarrow 0
, the left-hand side of (
3.35
) converges to
\overline{\sigma}^{2}\operatorname{Lip}(g^{\prime})^{2}
.

Therefore, the strict inequalities in (
3.70
) ensure that both (
3.34
) and (
3.35
) hold for sufficiently small
T
and admissible
h_{0}
. These conditions are sufficient and are not expected to be sharp.

We next establish a linear-growth estimate for the first Markovian iteration, which provides the initial bound needed for the convergence analysis, as explained in Remark
3
. This bound is independent of the partition and is subsequently used to establish convergence and uniform linear growth across all iterations in Theorem
3.7
.

Lemma 3.6

(Linear growth of the first iteration)
.

Suppose that the assumptions of Theorem
3.4
hold. For sufficiently small
h_{0}>0
, there exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
h,i
and
x
, such that, for every
0<h\leq h_{0}
,

|u_{i}^{\pi,1}(x)|^{2}\leq C(1+|x|^{2}),\qquad 0\leq i\leq N,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.72)

Proof.

Throughout this proof, we consider the first iteration,
m=1
, and suppress the superscripts
\pi,1
on the processes, while retaining them on the decoupling fields.

Fix
i_{0}\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Since the input fields vanish at all indices
i<N
, the forward process satisfies,

X_{i_{0}}=x,\qquad X_{i+1}=X_{i}+hb(t_{i},X_{i},0,0)+\sigma_{i}\Delta W_{i},\qquad i=i_{0},\ldots,N-1.

(3.73)

The linear growth of the drift and the boundedness of
\sigma
yield the standard Euler–Maruyama second-moment estimate

\sup_{i_{0}\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{i}|^{2}\right]\leq C(1+|x|^{2}).

(3.74)

For
i=i_{0},\ldots,N-1
, define
\bar{Y}_{i}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}[Y_{i+1}]
. Then we can write

h\widehat{Z}_{i}=\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}\left[Y_{i+1}\Delta W_{i}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}\left[(Y_{i+1}-\bar{Y}_{i})\Delta W_{i}\right].

(3.75)

Applying the conditional Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and using
\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}[|\Delta W_{i}|^{2}]=h
yields

\displaystyle h^{2}|\widehat{Z}_{i}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}\left[|Y_{i+1}-\bar{Y}_{i}|^{2}\right]\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}\left[|\Delta W_{i}|^{2}\right]

(3.76)

\displaystyle=h\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}\left[|Y_{i+1}-\bar{Y}_{i}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=h\left(\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}\left[|Y_{i+1}|^{2}\right]-|\bar{Y}_{i}|^{2}\right).

Rearranging gives

|\bar{Y}_{i}|^{2}+h|\widehat{Z}_{i}|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}\left[|Y_{i+1}|^{2}\right].

(3.77)

Fix
\delta>f_{z}
, independently of
h
. Using the implicit backward equation in (
2.1
), Young’s inequality, and the growth bound on
f
, we obtain

\displaystyle|Y_{i}|^{2}=|\bar{Y}_{i}+hf(t_{i},X_{i},Y_{i},\widehat{Z}_{i})|^{2}\leq(1+\delta h)|\bar{Y}_{i}|^{2}+(\delta^{-1}h+h^{2})\left[f_{0}+f_{x}|X_{i}|^{2}+f_{y}|Y_{i}|^{2}+f_{z}|\widehat{Z}_{i}|^{2}\right].

(3.78)

Using (
3.77
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left[1-f_{y}h(\delta^{-1}+h)\right]|Y_{i}|^{2}+h\left[1+\delta h-f_{z}(\delta^{-1}+h)\right]|\widehat{Z}_{i}|^{2}

(3.79)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\delta h)\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}\left[|Y_{i+1}|^{2}\right]+h(\delta^{-1}+h)(f_{0}+f_{x}|X_{i}|^{2}).

Since
\delta>f_{z}
, the coefficient of
h|\widehat{Z}_{i}|^{2}
is positive,

1+\delta h-f_{z}(\delta^{-1}+h)=1-\frac{f_{z}}{\delta}+(\delta-f_{z})h>0.

(3.80)

Choose
h_{0}>0
sufficiently small that

1-f_{y}h(\delta^{-1}+h)\geq\frac{1}{2},\qquad 0<h\leq h_{0}.

(3.81)

Discarding the nonnegative
Z
-term in (
3.79
) and dividing by
1-f_{y}h(\delta^{-1}+h)
, we obtain

|Y_{i}|^{2}\leq(1+Ch)\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}\left[|Y_{i+1}|^{2}\right]+Ch(1+|X_{i}|^{2}),

(3.82)

where
C>0
is independent of
h
.

Taking expectations in (
3.82
), using (
3.74
) and the terminal growth bound
|Y_{N}|^{2}=|g(X_{N})|^{2}\leq g_{0}+g_{x}|X_{N}|^{2}
, and applying the discrete Gronwall inequality backward yields

\sup_{i_{0}\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|Y_{i}|^{2}\leq C(1+|x|^{2}).

(3.83)

Finally, since
Y_{i_{0}}=u_{i_{0}}^{\pi,1}(x)
and the choice of
(i_{0},x)
was arbitrary, the estimate holds for
i_{0}=0,\ldots,N-1
and every
x\in\mathbb{R}
. At
i_{0}=N
, it follows directly from
u_{N}^{\pi,1}=g
and the growth bound on
g
. This proves (
3.72
).

∎

Remark 3
.

Let
X^{\pi,1,i_{0},x}
denote the first-iteration forward process started from
x
at time
t_{i_{0}}
. By Lemma
3.6
and the forward moment estimate (
3.74
) established in its proof,

\max_{i_{0}\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}\bigl|u_{i}^{\pi,1}(X_{i}^{\pi,1,i_{0},x})\bigr|^{2}\leq C(1+|x|^{2}).

(3.84)

Moreover, Remark
1
gives
|v_{i}^{\pi,1}(y)|^{2}\leq\overline{\sigma}^{2}\bar{L}
for every
i<N
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
(N-i_{0})h\leq T
, it follows that, for every fixed
\eta>0
,

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq i_{0}\leq N}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{1+|x|^{2}}\Bigg[\max_{i_{0}\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}\bigl|u_{i}^{\pi,1}(X_{i}^{\pi,1,i_{0},x})\bigr|^{2}+\eta h\sum_{i=i_{0}}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\bigl|v_{i}^{\pi,1}(X_{i}^{\pi,1,i_{0},x})\bigr|^{2}\Bigg]\leq C,

(3.85)

where
C
may depend on
\eta
but is independent of the partition and the initial pair
(i_{0},x)
.

3.3
Convergence of the Markovian iteration

We now study the convergence of the Markovian iteration, and introduce some necessary notations used in the analysis.

For an initial pair
(i_{0},x)
, let
(X^{\pi,m,i_{0},x},Y^{\pi,m,i_{0},x},\widehat{Z}^{\pi,m,i_{0},x})
denote the processes generated by iteration
m
, restarted at
X_{i_{0}}^{\pi,m,i_{0},x}=x
. For
m\geq 1
, define the successive field differences by

\delta u_{i}^{m}\coloneqq u_{i}^{\pi,m}-u_{i}^{\pi,m-1},\quad 0\leq i\leq N,\qquad\delta v_{i}^{m}\coloneqq v_{i}^{\pi,m}-v_{i}^{\pi,m-1},\quad 0\leq i<N.

(3.86)

and introduce the weighted quantities

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{m}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sup_{0\leq i_{0}\leq N}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{1+|x|^{2}}\max_{i_{0}\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}\bigl|\delta u_{i}^{m}(X_{i}^{\pi,m,i_{0},x})\bigr|^{2},

(3.87)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{m}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sup_{0\leq i_{0}\leq N}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{1+|x|^{2}}h\sum_{i=i_{0}}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\bigl|\delta v_{i}^{m}(X_{i}^{\pi,m,i_{0},x})\bigr|^{2},

(3.88)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{m}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathcal{U}_{m}+\eta\mathcal{V}_{m},\qquad\eta>0.

(3.89)

Empty sums are understood as zero. For each fixed iteration and partition, these quantities are finite, since the fields are globally Lipschitz and the restarted forward processes have second moments with at most quadratic dependence on the starting state. Remark
3
implies
\mathcal{E}_{1}\leq C
, where
C
may depend on
\eta
but is independent of the partition.

For later use, let us define the following nonnegative constants,

\displaystyle B_{X}

\displaystyle\coloneqq b_{x}+2b_{y}\bar{L}+2b_{z}\bar{V},\qquad

\displaystyle C_{X}

\displaystyle\coloneqq T^{-1}+2TB_{X},

(3.90)

\displaystyle\beta_{u}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 4b_{y}T^{2}e^{C_{X}T},

\displaystyle\beta_{v}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 4b_{z}Te^{C_{X}T},

\displaystyle C_{Y}

\displaystyle\coloneqq e^{\theta_{2}T}(g_{x}+\theta_{3}T),

\displaystyle C_{Z}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{g_{x}+\theta_{2}TC_{Y}+\theta_{3}T}{\theta_{1}},

\displaystyle C_{U}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 2(C_{Y}+\bar{L}),

\displaystyle C_{V}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 2(C_{Z}+\bar{V}T),

Here
\theta_{2}
,
\theta_{3}
, and
\theta_{1}
are the constants from Lemma
3.1
. All these constants are independent of
h
and the iteration index
m
, but may depend on the chosen invariant bounds
\bar{L}
and
\bar{V}
.

Theorem 3.7

(Geometric convergence of the Markovian iteration)
.

Suppose that the assumptions of Theorem
3.4
hold. Assume that there exists
\eta>0
such that

\rho_{\eta}:=(C_{U}+\eta C_{V})\max\left\{\beta_{u},\frac{\beta_{v}}{\eta}\right\}<1.

(3.91)

Then

\mathcal{E}_{m+1}\leq\rho_{\eta}\mathcal{E}_{m},\qquad m\geq 1,

(3.92)

and consequently, with the convention
\rho_{\eta}^{0}=1
also when
\rho_{\eta}=0
,

\mathcal{E}_{m}\leq\rho_{\eta}^{m-1}\mathcal{E}_{1},\qquad m\geq 1.

(3.93)

For each fixed partition
\pi
, the fields
(u^{\pi,m},v^{\pi,m})
converge pointwise in the spatial variable, as
m\to\infty
, to a fixed point
(u^{\pi},v^{\pi})
of
F^{\pi}
, satisfying

L(u^{\pi})\leq\bar{L},\qquad L(v^{\pi})\leq\bar{V},\qquad|v_{i}^{\pi}(x)|^{2}\leq\overline{\sigma}^{2}\bar{L}.

(3.94)

Moreover, for some
C
independent of
m
and
h
,

|u_{i}^{\pi,m}(x)|^{2}+|u_{i}^{\pi}(x)|^{2}\leq C(1+|x|^{2}).

(3.95)

For every restarted initial pair
(i_{0},x)
, the coupled discrete FBSDE associated with the limiting decoupling fields
(u^{\pi},v^{\pi})
has a unique square-integrable solution. In particular,
(u^{\pi},v^{\pi})
is the unique fixed point of
F^{\pi}
in the invariant Lipschitz class.

Proof.

Fix a partition
\pi
and an initial pair
(i_{0},x)
. To simplify the notation, we suppress the superscripts
\pi,i_{0},x
on the processes throughout this proof, retaining only the iteration index
m
, while keeping the full notation for the decoupling fields.

For
m\geq 1
, let us write

\Delta X_{i}^{m}:=X_{i}^{m+1}-X_{i}^{m},\quad\Delta Y_{i}^{m}:=Y_{i}^{m+1}-Y_{i}^{m},\quad\Delta\widehat{Z}_{i}^{m}:=\widehat{Z}_{i}^{m+1}-\widehat{Z}_{i}^{m}.

(3.96)

The uniform Lipschitz bounds give

\displaystyle|u_{i}^{\pi,m}(X_{i}^{m+1})-u_{i}^{\pi,m-1}(X_{i}^{m})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\bar{L}|\Delta X_{i}^{m}|^{2}+2|\delta u_{i}^{m}(X_{i}^{m})|^{2},

(3.97)

\displaystyle|v_{i}^{\pi,m}(X_{i}^{m+1})-v_{i}^{\pi,m-1}(X_{i}^{m})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\bar{V}|\Delta X_{i}^{m}|^{2}+2|\delta v_{i}^{m}(X_{i}^{m})|^{2}.

(3.98)

Let
\Delta b_{i}^{m}
denote the difference of the forward drifts in iterations
m+1
and
m
, that is,

\Delta b_{i}^{m}\coloneqq b\bigl(t_{i},X_{i}^{m+1},u_{i}^{\pi,m}(X_{i}^{m+1}),v_{i}^{\pi,m}(X_{i}^{m+1})\bigr)-b\bigl(t_{i},X_{i}^{m},u_{i}^{\pi,m-1}(X_{i}^{m}),v_{i}^{\pi,m-1}(X_{i}^{m})\bigr).

(3.99)

By Assumption
1
,

|\Delta b_{i}^{m}|^{2}\leq B_{X}|\Delta X_{i}^{m}|^{2}+2b_{y}|\delta u_{i}^{m}(X_{i}^{m})|^{2}+2b_{z}|\delta v_{i}^{m}(X_{i}^{m})|^{2}.

(3.100)

Since both iterations use the same deterministic coefficient
\sigma_{i}
at time
t_{i}
, the diffusion increments cancel,

\Delta X_{i+1}^{m}=\Delta X_{i}^{m}+h\Delta b_{i}^{m},\qquad\Delta X_{i_{0}}^{m}=0.

(3.101)

Using Young’s inequality with the form
2ab\leq T^{-1}a^{2}+Tb^{2}
, and
h\leq T
, we obtain

|\Delta X_{i+1}^{m}|^{2}\leq(1+C_{X}h)|\Delta X_{i}^{m}|^{2}+4Tb_{y}h|\delta u_{i}^{m}(X_{i}^{m})|^{2}+4Tb_{z}h|\delta v_{i}^{m}(X_{i}^{m})|^{2}.

(3.102)

Taking expectations and applying discrete Gronwall inequality gives

\displaystyle\max_{i_{0}\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|\Delta X_{i}^{m}|^{2}\leq\beta_{u}\max_{i_{0}\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|\delta u_{i}^{m}(X_{i}^{m})|^{2}+\beta_{v}h\sum_{i=i_{0}}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}|\delta v_{i}^{m}(X_{i}^{m})|^{2}.

(3.103)

For the backward differences, Lemma
3.1
yields

\mathbb{E}|\Delta Y_{i}^{m}|^{2}+\theta_{1}h\mathbb{E}|\Delta\widehat{Z}_{i}^{m}|^{2}\leq(1+\theta_{2}h)\mathbb{E}|\Delta Y_{i+1}^{m}|^{2}+\theta_{3}h\mathbb{E}|\Delta X_{i}^{m}|^{2}.

(3.104)

At the terminal time, Assumption
1
gives

\mathbb{E}|\Delta Y_{N}^{m}|^{2}=\mathbb{E}\left|g(X_{N}^{m+1})-g(X_{N}^{m})\right|^{2}\leq g_{x}\mathbb{E}|\Delta X_{N}^{m}|^{2}.

(3.105)

Dropping the nonnegative
Z
-term in (
3.104
), iterating backward from
N
to
i
gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|\Delta Y_{i}^{m}|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle(1+\theta_{2}h)^{N-i}\mathbb{E}|\Delta Y_{N}^{m}|^{2}+\theta_{3}h\sum_{j=i}^{N-1}(1+\theta_{2}h)^{j-i}\mathbb{E}|\Delta X_{j}^{m}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{\theta_{2}T}g_{x}\max_{i_{0}\leq j\leq N}\mathbb{E}|\Delta X_{j}^{m}|^{2}+\theta_{3}Te^{\theta_{2}T}\max_{i_{0}\leq j\leq N}\mathbb{E}|\Delta X_{j}^{m}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\theta_{2}T}\left(g_{x}+\theta_{3}T\right)\max_{i_{0}\leq j\leq N}\mathbb{E}|\Delta X_{j}^{m}|^{2}.

and therefore we can write

\max_{i_{0}\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|\Delta Y_{i}^{m}|^{2}\leq C_{Y}\max_{i_{0}\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|\Delta X_{i}^{m}|^{2}.

(3.106)

For
Z
-difference, summing (
3.104
) gives

\displaystyle\theta_{1}h\sum_{i=i_{0}}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}|\Delta\widehat{Z}_{i}^{m}|^{2}\leq{}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|\Delta Y_{N}^{m}|^{2}-\mathbb{E}|\Delta Y_{i_{0}}^{m}|^{2}

(3.107)

\displaystyle+\theta_{2}h\sum_{i=i_{0}}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}|\Delta Y_{i+1}^{m}|^{2}+\theta_{3}h\sum_{i=i_{0}}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}|\Delta X_{i}^{m}|^{2}.

Discarding the nonpositive term
-\mathbb{E}|\Delta Y_{i_{0}}^{m}|^{2}
, using (
3.106
), (
3.105
) and taking the maximum of
\mathbb{E}|\Delta X_{i}^{m}|^{2}
yields

h\sum_{i=i_{0}}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}|\Delta\widehat{Z}_{i}^{m}|^{2}\leq C_{Z}\max_{i_{0}\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|\Delta X_{i}^{m}|^{2}.

(3.108)

We now transfer these process estimates to the fields. Using the identities
Y_{i}^{m+1}=u_{i}^{\pi,m+1}(X_{i}^{m+1})
and
Y_{i}^{m}=u_{i}^{\pi,m}(X_{i}^{m})
, the uniform Lipschitz bound, and Young’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left|\delta u_{i}^{m+1}(X_{i}^{m+1})\right|^{2}=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left|\Delta Y_{i}^{m}+u_{i}^{\pi,m}(X_{i}^{m})-u_{i}^{\pi,m}(X_{i}^{m+1})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}|\Delta Y_{i}^{m}|^{2}+2\bar{L}\mathbb{E}|\Delta X_{i}^{m}|^{2}.

Therefore, by (
3.106
), we have

\max_{i_{0}\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}\left|\delta u_{i}^{m+1}(X_{i}^{m+1})\right|^{2}\leq C_{U}\max_{i_{0}\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|\Delta X_{i}^{m}|^{2}.

(3.109)

Similarly for the
Z
process, we have

\delta v_{i}^{m+1}(X_{i}^{m+1})=\Delta\widehat{Z}_{i}^{m}+v_{i}^{\pi,m}(X_{i}^{m})-v_{i}^{\pi,m}(X_{i}^{m+1}).

(3.110)

Using (
3.108
), and
h\sum_{i=i_{0}}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}|\Delta X_{i}^{m}|^{2}\leq T\max_{i_{0}\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|\Delta X_{i}^{m}|^{2}
gives

\displaystyle h\sum_{i=i_{0}}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\left|\delta v_{i}^{m+1}(X_{i}^{m+1})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2h\sum_{i=i_{0}}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}|\Delta\widehat{Z}_{i}^{m}|^{2}+2\bar{V}h\sum_{i=i_{0}}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}|\Delta X_{i}^{m}|^{2}

(3.111)

\displaystyle\leq C_{V}\max_{i_{0}\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|\Delta X_{i}^{m}|^{2},

Combining inequalities (
3.109
) and (
3.111
) with (
3.103
), dividing by
1+|x|^{2}
, and taking suprema over
(i_{0},x)
yields

\mathcal{U}_{m+1}\leq C_{U}(\beta_{u}\mathcal{U}_{m}+\beta_{v}\mathcal{V}_{m}),\qquad\mathcal{V}_{m+1}\leq C_{V}(\beta_{u}\mathcal{U}_{m}+\beta_{v}\mathcal{V}_{m}).

(3.112)

Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{m+1}\leq(C_{U}+\eta C_{V})(\beta_{u}\mathcal{U}_{m}+\beta_{v}\mathcal{V}_{m})\leq(C_{U}+\eta C_{V})\max\{\beta_{u},\beta_{v}/\eta\}\mathcal{E}_{m}=\rho_{\eta}\mathcal{E}_{m}.

(3.113)

This proves (
3.92
), and (
3.93
) follows by iterating the estimate.

Next, we prove the convergence of
u_{i}^{\pi,m}
and
v_{i}^{\pi,m}
. Since the preceding estimates hold for every initial pair
(i_{0},x)
, we may choose
i_{0}=i
. The definitions of
\mathcal{U}_{m}
and
\mathcal{V}_{m}
then yield

|\delta u_{i}^{m}(x)|^{2}\leq(1+|x|^{2})\mathcal{U}_{m}\leq(1+|x|^{2})\rho_{\eta}^{m-1}\mathcal{E}_{1},\quad h|\delta v_{i}^{m}(x)|^{2}\leq(1+|x|^{2})\mathcal{V}_{m}\leq\frac{1+|x|^{2}}{\eta}\rho_{\eta}^{m-1}\mathcal{E}_{1},

(3.114)

which implies

\displaystyle|\delta u_{i}^{m}(x)|\leq(1+|x|^{2})^{1/2}\mathcal{E}_{1}^{1/2}\rho_{\eta}^{(m-1)/2},\qquad|\delta v_{i}^{m}(x)|\leq\left(\frac{1+|x|^{2}}{\eta h}\right)^{1/2}\mathcal{E}_{1}^{1/2}\rho_{\eta}^{(m-1)/2}.

(3.115)

Hence, for any integers
n>m
we can write

\displaystyle|u_{i}^{\pi,n}(x)-u_{i}^{\pi,m}(x)|=\left|\sum_{k=m+1}^{n}\delta u_{i}^{k}(x)\right|\leq\sum_{k=m+1}^{n}|\delta u_{i}^{k}(x)|\leq(1+|x|^{2})^{1/2}\mathcal{E}_{1}^{1/2}\sum_{k=m+1}^{n}\rho_{\eta}^{(k-1)/2},

(3.116)

\displaystyle|v_{i}^{\pi,n}(x)-v_{i}^{\pi,m}(x)|\leq\left(\frac{1+|x|^{2}}{\eta h}\right)^{1/2}\mathcal{E}_{1}^{1/2}\sum_{k=m+1}^{n}\rho_{\eta}^{(k-1)/2}.

Since
\rho_{\eta}<1
, the geometric series
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\rho_{\eta}^{(k-1)/2}
converges, and hence
\sum_{k=m+1}^{n}\rho_{\eta}^{(k-1)/2}\longrightarrow 0
as
m\to\infty
. Therefore,
\{u_{i}^{\pi,m}(x)\}_{m\geq 0}
and
\{v_{i}^{\pi,m}(x)\}_{m\geq 0}
are Cauchy sequences for every fixed admissible
i
and
x
, and we can therefore define

u_{i}^{\pi}(x)\coloneqq\lim_{m\to\infty}u_{i}^{\pi,m}(x),\qquad v_{i}^{\pi}(x)\coloneqq\lim_{m\to\infty}v_{i}^{\pi,m}(x).

(3.117)

The uniform bounds pass to the limit, and hence
L(u^{\pi})\leq\bar{L}
,
L(v^{\pi})\leq\bar{V}
, and
|v_{i}^{\pi}(x)|^{2}\leq\overline{\sigma}^{2}\bar{L}
for every
0\leq i<N
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Moreover, for
0\leq i<N
, summing the first inequality in (
3.115
) and using
u_{i}^{\pi,0}=0
gives

|u_{i}^{\pi,m}(x)|\leq\frac{\mathcal{E}_{1}^{1/2}}{1-\sqrt{\rho_{\eta}}}(1+|x|^{2})^{1/2}.

(3.118)

By Remark
3
,
\mathcal{E}_{1}
is bounded independently of the partition. Taking the limit as
m\to\infty
gives the same growth bound for
u_{i}^{\pi}
. At
i=N
, we have
u_{N}^{\pi,m}=u_{N}^{\pi}=g
, so the terminal growth bound gives the same conclusion. This proves (
3.95
).

Furthermore, (
3.103
), (
3.106
), and (
3.108
), together with (
3.93
), imply that the corresponding forward and backward processes are Cauchy in their natural discrete
L^{2}
-norms. Using the uniform Lipschitz and growth bounds on the fields, the global Lipschitz continuity of the coefficients, and the
L^{2}
-continuity of the conditional projections for fixed
h
, we may pass to the limit in the discrete scheme (
2.1
). Since the initial pair
(i_{0},x)
is arbitrary, the limiting fields satisfy

(u^{\pi},v^{\pi})=F^{\pi}(u^{\pi},v^{\pi})

(3.119)

pointwise, and hence form a fixed point of
F^{\pi}
.

Finally, we prove the uniqueness. Let
(X^{(1)},Y^{(1)},\widehat{Z}^{(1)})
and
(X^{(2)},Y^{(2)},\widehat{Z}^{(2)})
be two square-integrable solutions of the discrete FBSDE with the same initial pair
(i_{0},x)
and driven by the same Brownian increments. We apply the preceding arguments to these two solutions.

Their forward drifts are
b(t_{i},X_{i}^{(\ell)},Y_{i}^{(\ell)},\widehat{Z}_{i}^{(\ell)})
,
\ell=1,2
, and the identical diffusion terms
\sigma_{i}\Delta W_{i}
cancel. The forward estimate therefore gives

\displaystyle\max_{i_{0}\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|X_{i}^{(1)}-X_{i}^{(2)}|^{2}\leq e^{C_{X}^{0}T}\Bigg[2b_{y}T^{2}\max_{i_{0}\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|Y_{i}^{(1)}-Y_{i}^{(2)}|^{2}+2b_{z}Th\sum_{i=i_{0}}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}|\widehat{Z}_{i}^{(1)}-\widehat{Z}_{i}^{(2)}|^{2}\Bigg],

(3.120)

where
C_{X}^{0}\coloneqq T^{-1}+2Tb_{x}
. The backward estimates apply directly to these solutions, yielding

\displaystyle\max_{i_{0}\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|Y_{i}^{(1)}-Y_{i}^{(2)}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{Y}\max_{i_{0}\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|X_{i}^{(1)}-X_{i}^{(2)}|^{2},

(3.121)

\displaystyle h\sum_{i=i_{0}}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}|\widehat{Z}_{i}^{(1)}-\widehat{Z}_{i}^{(2)}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{Z}\max_{i_{0}\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|X_{i}^{(1)}-X_{i}^{(2)}|^{2}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\max_{i_{0}\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|X_{i}^{(1)}-X_{i}^{(2)}|^{2}\leq e^{C_{X}^{0}T}(2b_{y}T^{2}C_{Y}+2b_{z}TC_{Z})\max_{i_{0}\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|X_{i}^{(1)}-X_{i}^{(2)}|^{2}.

(3.122)

Since
C_{X}^{0}\leq C_{X}
,
C_{U}\geq 2C_{Y}
, and
C_{V}\geq 2C_{Z}
, we have

e^{C_{X}^{0}T}(2b_{y}T^{2}C_{Y}+2b_{z}TC_{Z})\leq\frac{1}{4}(C_{U}\beta_{u}+C_{V}\beta_{v})\leq\frac{1}{4}\rho_{\eta}<1.

(3.123)

It follows that
\max_{i_{0}\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|X_{i}^{(1)}-X_{i}^{(2)}|^{2}=0
, and the backward estimates imply equality of the two backward solutions. Since the initial pair
(i_{0},x)
is arbitrary, this also proves uniqueness of the fixed point in the invariant Lipschitz class of Theorem
3.4
.

∎

Remark 4

(Interpretation of the contraction condition)
.

To understand when the contraction condition (
3.91
) holds, recall that

\beta_{u}=4b_{y}T^{2}e^{1+2B_{X}T^{2}},\qquad\beta_{v}=4b_{z}Te^{1+2B_{X}T^{2}}.

(3.124)

With the coefficient bounds and invariant Lipschitz bounds
\bar{L},\bar{V}
fixed, both coefficients vanish as
T\downarrow 0
.

Fix the backward stability constants
\theta_{2},\theta_{3},\theta_{1}
corresponding to a sufficiently small admissible maximal step size
h_{0}
. Then, as
T\downarrow 0
,

\displaystyle C_{U}=2(C_{Y}+\bar{L})\longrightarrow 2(g_{x}+\bar{L}),\quad C_{V}=2(C_{Z}+\bar{V}T)\longrightarrow\frac{2g_{x}}{\theta_{1}}.

(3.125)

Thus
C_{U}
and
C_{V}
remain bounded as
T\downarrow 0
. Since
\beta_{u},\beta_{v}\to 0
, for every fixed
\eta>0
,

\rho_{\eta}=(C_{U}+\eta C_{V})\max\left\{\beta_{u},\frac{\beta_{v}}{\eta}\right\}\longrightarrow 0.

(3.126)

Together with the small-time feasibility of the invariant conditions as discussed in Remark
2
, this yields a nonempty convergence regime.

For a fixed
T
, sufficiently weak drift coupling, as measured by
b_{y}
and
b_{z}
, may also ensure (
3.91
), provided the invariant conditions remain satisfied. Reducing the spatial Lipschitz bound
b_{x}
can also improve this condition. Indeed, with the other constants fixed,
B_{X}
,
\beta_{u}
,
\beta_{v}
, and
\rho_{\eta}
are nondecreasing functions of
b_{x},b_{y}
, and
b_{z}
.

3.4
Convergence of the time discretization

We now consider the time-discretization error. To this end, we require the following additional assumptions on the coefficients and the exact decoupling fields of the continuous FBSDE (
1.1
).

Assumption 4

(Time regularity of the coefficients)
.

The coefficients
b
,
f
, and
\sigma
are uniformly
1/2
-Hölder continuous in time. More precisely, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle|b(t,x,y,z)-b(s,x,y,z)|^{2}+|f(t,x,y,z)-f(s,x,y,z)|^{2}+|\sigma(t)-\sigma(s)|^{2}\leq C|t-s|,

(3.127)

for all
s,t\in[0,T]
and
x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}
.

Assumption 5

(Regularity of the continuous solution)
.

The following properties hold:

(i)

For every initial state
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, the continuous-time FBSDE (
1.1
) has a square-integrable solution
(X,Y,Z)
with common deterministic decoupling fields
u
and
v
, such that
Y_{t}=u(t,X_{t})
and
Z_{t}=v(t,X_{t})

\mathbb{P}
-almost surely for every
t\in[0,T]
.

(ii)

The fields
u,v
are uniformly Lipschitz in the spatial variable over
t\in[0,T]
.

(iii)

The fields
u,v
are
1/2
-Hölder continuous in time with the following spatially weighted bound:

|u(t,x)-u(s,x)|^{2}+|v(t,x)-v(s,x)|^{2}\leq C(1+|x|^{2})|t-s|,

(3.128)

for all
s,t\in[0,T]
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
, where
C
is independent of
s,t
, and
x
.

(iv)

For every admissible uniform partition, the solution satisfies

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq i<N}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}\bigl(|X_{t}-X_{t_{i}}|^{2}+|Y_{t}-Y_{t_{i}}|^{2}\bigr)\right]+\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}|Z_{t}-Z_{t_{i}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})h.

(3.129)

All the constants
C
are independent of the partition and the initial state.

Remark 5
.

We state the properties in Assumption 5 directly to keep the time-discretization analysis focused on the numerical scheme. In particular, this separates the regularity requirements needed for the time-discretization error from the convergence analysis of the Markovian iteration, which is the main focus of this work. Some of these properties can be derived from Assumptions 1–4 on a sufficiently short time interval. In particular, Theorems 8.2.1 and 8.2.3 of
[
Zha17
]
establish existence and uniqueness of a square-integrable solution to the continuous FBSDE, together with spatial Lipschitz continuity of its
Y
-decoupling field. The additional compatibility condition in equation (8.2.1) of that reference is automatically satisfied, since
\sigma
depends only on time in our setting.

Furthermore, under the standing coefficient assumptions, items
(i)
–
(iii)
of Assumption
5
imply item
(iv)
. Indeed, fixing the exact decoupling fields gives the effective forward drift

B(t,x)\coloneqq b(t,x,u(t,x),v(t,x)).

(3.130)

The spatial Lipschitz and time regularity assumptions imply

|B(t,x)-B(t,x^{\prime})|^{2}\leq C|x-x^{\prime}|^{2},\qquad|B(t,x)-B(s,x)|^{2}\leq C(1+|x|^{2})|t-s|.

(3.131)

Moreover, the time regularity estimate at
x=0
shows that
B(t,0)
is bounded uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
. Together with spatial Lipschitz continuity, this yields the uniform linear-growth bound for
B(t,x)
. Since
\sigma
is bounded, the standard moment and increment estimates for the forward SDE therefore give

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|X_{t}|^{2}\right]\leq C(1+|x_{0}|^{2}),\qquad\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[s,t]}|X_{r}-X_{s}|^{2}\right]\leq C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})(t-s),

(3.132)

for all
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
, where
C
is independent of
s,t
, and
x_{0}
.

Using the spatial Lipschitz continuity and time regularity of
u
, we obtain, for
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[s,t]}|Y_{r}-Y_{s}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[s,t]}|u(r,X_{r})-u(r,X_{s})|^{2}\right]+2\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[s,t]}|u(r,X_{s})-u(s,X_{s})|^{2}\right]

(3.133)

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[s,t]}|X_{r}-X_{s}|^{2}\right]+C(1+\mathbb{E}|X_{s}|^{2})(t-s)

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})(t-s).

Similarly, the spatial and time regularity of
v
gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|Z_{t}-Z_{s}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}|v(t,X_{t})-v(t,X_{s})|^{2}+2\mathbb{E}|v(t,X_{s})-v(s,X_{s})|^{2}

(3.134)

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}|X_{t}-X_{s}|^{2}+C(1+\mathbb{E}|X_{s}|^{2})(t-s)

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})(t-s).

Here, the last inequalities use the forward moment and increment estimates established above.

Integrating the estimate for
Z
over each time interval and summing over
i
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}|Z_{t}-Z_{t_{i}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}(t-t_{i})\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{CT}{2}(1+|x_{0}|^{2})h.

(3.135)

Taking
s=t_{i}
and
t=t_{i+1}
in the increment estimates for
X
and
Y
, and then taking the maximum over
i
, yields the remaining term in (
3.129
). Thus, after absorbing the fixed factor
T/2
into the constant, we obtain (
3.129
), which we retain explicitly for convenience.

Next, we shall apply one Markovian iteration to the exact continuous-time decoupling fields and compare its output with the discrete fixed point. To this end, we define the exact grid fields by

u_{i}^{0}(x)\coloneqq u(t_{i},x),\quad 0\leq i\leq N,\qquad v_{i}^{0}(x)\coloneqq v(t_{i},x),\quad 0\leq i<N.

(3.136)

Here
u_{N}^{0}=g
. The pair
(u^{0},v^{0})
denotes the exact grid fields, whereas
(u^{\pi,0},v^{\pi,0})
denotes the initialization of the Markovian iteration, which is zero for
i<N
and satisfies
u_{N}^{\pi,0}=g
.

Let
(\widetilde{u}^{\pi},\widetilde{v}^{\pi})\coloneqq F^{\pi}(u^{0},v^{0})
be the approximation obtained after one iteration with
(u^{0},v^{0})
as input fields. The associated processes satisfy

\left\{\begin{aligned} \widetilde{X}_{0}^{\pi}&=x_{0},\\
\widetilde{X}_{i+1}^{\pi}&=\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi}+hb\bigl(t_{i},\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi},u_{i}^{0}(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi}),v_{i}^{0}(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})\bigr)+\sigma(t_{i})\Delta W_{i},\\
\widetilde{Y}_{N}^{\pi}&=g(\widetilde{X}_{N}^{\pi}),\\
\widetilde{Z}_{i}^{\pi}&=h^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}\left[\widetilde{Y}_{i+1}^{\pi}\Delta W_{i}\right],\\
\widetilde{Y}_{i}^{\pi}&=\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}\left[\widetilde{Y}_{i+1}^{\pi}\right]+hf\bigl(t_{i},\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi},\widetilde{Y}_{i}^{\pi},\widetilde{Z}_{i}^{\pi}\bigr).\end{aligned}\right.

(3.137)

Lemma 3.8

(Consistency of one implicit discrete iteration)
.

Let Assumptions
1
,
2
,
4
, and
5
hold. For sufficiently small
h_{0}>0
, there exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
\pi
and
x_{0}
, such that for every
0<h\leq h_{0}
, the following estimates hold:

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq i<N}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}\bigl(|X_{t}-\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi}|^{2}+|Y_{t}-\widetilde{Y}_{i}^{\pi}|^{2}\bigr)\right]+\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}|Z_{t}-\widetilde{Z}_{i}^{\pi}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})h.

(3.138)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}\bigl|(\widetilde{u}_{i}^{\pi}-u_{i}^{0})(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})\bigr|^{2}+h\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\bigl|(\widetilde{v}_{i}^{\pi}-v_{i}^{0})(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})\bigr|^{2}\leq C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})h.

(3.139)

Proof.

To prove (
3.138
), fix the exact decoupling fields and use the effective drift
B(t,x)=b(t,x,u(t,x),v(t,x))
introduced in Remark
5
. Its uniform spatial Lipschitz continuity and time regularity, together with the moment bound for
X
and the time regularity of
\sigma
, give the standard Euler–Maruyama estimate

\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|X_{t_{i}}-\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi}|^{2}\leq C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})h.

(3.140)

Using
|a+b|^{2}\leq 2|a|^{2}+2|b|^{2}
and the increment bound in (
3.129
), we obtain, for every
0\leq i<N
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}|X_{s}-\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}|X_{s}-X_{t_{i}}|^{2}\right]+2\mathbb{E}|X_{t_{i}}-\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi}|^{2}\leq C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})h.

(3.141)

Taking the maximum over
i
proves the forward estimate required in (
3.138
).

Next, for the backward processes, we follow the proof of Theorem 3.1 in
[
BT04
]
, whose implicit scheme (3.3)–(3.4) coincides with our backward recursion. To facilitate comparison, their discrete values
(X_{t_{i}}^{\pi},Y_{t_{i}}^{\pi},Z_{t_{i}}^{\pi})
correspond to our
(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi},\widetilde{Y}_{i}^{\pi},\widetilde{Z}_{i}^{\pi})
. We denote the corresponding continuous martingale integrand, defined as in their equation (3.5), by
\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\pi}
, and the conditional time average
\overline{Z}_{t_{i}}^{\pi}
in their Lemma 3.2 by
\overline{Z}_{i}
.

We first verify the identities and regularity estimates needed in their proof. The forward estimate above implies
\mathbb{E}|X_{s}-\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi}|^{2}\leq C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})h
for every
s\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}]
, providing the counterpart of their inequality (3.7). Moreover, the definition of
\widetilde{Z}_{i}^{\pi}
and conditional Itô isometry give the identity in their Lemma 3.1.

To verify the regularity estimate in their Lemma 3.2, recall that the conditional time average minimizes the integrated squared error over square-integrable,
\mathcal{F}_{t_{i}}
-measurable random variables. Thus,

\overline{Z}_{i}\coloneqq\frac{1}{h}\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}Z_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}|Z_{s}-\overline{Z}_{i}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}|Z_{s}-Z_{t_{i}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s.

(3.142)

Consequently, (
3.129

\max_{0\leq i<N}\sup_{s\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}\mathbb{E}|Y_{s}-Y_{t_{i}}|^{2}+\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}|Z_{s}-\overline{Z}_{i}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})h.

(3.143)

Thus, the regularity estimate in their Lemma 3.2 holds under our assumptions.

The remaining difference is that our driver is uniformly
1/2
-Hölder continuous in time, whereas their condition (2.3) assumes Lipschitz continuity in time. In their inequality (3.6), this replaces the squared time-step size contribution
h^{2}
by
h
, so its integral over one time step changes from order
h^{3}
to order
h^{2}
. However, their estimates (3.7)–(3.8) already contribute terms of order
h
inside the integral in (3.10), arising from the forward approximation error and the
Y
-increments. After integration, these give the
h^{2}
term in
B_{i}
following (3.12). Thus, our weaker time regularity of the driver
f
preserves the order of the one-step estimate following their (3.13).

Using these estimates, we follow the proof of Theorem 3.1 of
[
BT04
]
to obtain the same type of one-step estimate as the one following their equation (3.13), for sufficiently small

.

Since
g
is globally Lipschitz, the forward error estimate gives

\mathbb{E}|Y_{T}-\widetilde{Y}_{N}^{\pi}|^{2}=\mathbb{E}|g(X_{T})-g(\widetilde{X}_{N}^{\pi})|^{2}\leq C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})h.

(3.144)

Backward iteration and summation of the one-step estimate, following their equation (3.14) and the subsequent argument on page 183, respectively, yield

\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|Y_{t_{i}}-\widetilde{Y}_{i}^{\pi}|^{2}\leq C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})h,\qquad\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}|Z_{s}-\mathcal{Z}_{s}^{\pi}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})h.

(3.145)

The factor
1+|x_{0}|^{2}
follows from the terminal error, the forward error estimate, and the solution regularity bounds used in the argument.

To obtain the estimate for the discrete
Z
-values, we integrate and sum their inequality (3.9), interchanging expectation and integration by Tonelli’s theorem. The second term in that inequality is independent of
s
, so integration over
[t_{i},t_{i+1}]
cancels the factor
h^{-1}
. This gives

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}|Z_{s}-\widetilde{Z}_{i}^{\pi}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\left[\mathbb{E}|Z_{s}-\overline{Z}_{i}|^{2}+\frac{1}{h}\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}|Z_{r}-\mathcal{Z}_{r}^{\pi}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\right]\,\mathrm{d}s

(3.146)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}|Z_{s}-\overline{Z}_{i}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+2\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}|Z_{r}-\mathcal{Z}_{r}^{\pi}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})h,

where the last inequality follows from (
3.143
) and (
3.145
).

Finally, the increment bound (
3.129
) and (
3.145
) give, for every
0\leq i<N
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}|Y_{t}-\widetilde{Y}_{i}^{\pi}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}|Y_{t}-Y_{t_{i}}|^{2}\right]+2\mathbb{E}|Y_{t_{i}}-\widetilde{Y}_{i}^{\pi}|^{2}\leq C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})h.

(3.147)

Taking the maximum over
i
, and combining previous results proves (
3.138
).

It remains to derive (
3.139
). For
0\leq i<N
, using
\widetilde{Y}_{i}^{\pi}=\widetilde{u}_{i}^{\pi}(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})
,
Y_{t_{i}}=u_{i}^{0}(X_{t_{i}})
, and Assumption
5
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{u}_{i}^{\pi}(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})-u_{i}^{0}(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2|\widetilde{Y}_{i}^{\pi}-Y_{t_{i}}|^{2}+2|u_{i}^{0}(X_{t_{i}})-u_{i}^{0}(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})|^{2}\leq 2|\widetilde{Y}_{i}^{\pi}-Y_{t_{i}}|^{2}+C|X_{t_{i}}-\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi}|^{2},

(3.148)

At the terminal time,
\widetilde{u}_{N}^{\pi}=u_{N}^{0}=g
, so the corresponding field difference vanishes. Therefore, (
3.138
) implies

\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\widetilde{u}_{i}^{\pi}(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})-u_{i}^{0}(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})\right|^{2}\right]\leq C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})h.

(3.149)

Similarly, for the
v
-component we have

\displaystyle h\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\left|\widetilde{v}_{i}^{\pi}(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})-v_{i}^{0}(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})\right|^{2}\leq 2h\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\left|\widetilde{Z}_{i}^{\pi}-Z_{t_{i}}\right|^{2}+2h\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\left|v_{i}^{0}(X_{t_{i}})-v_{i}^{0}(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})\right|^{2}.

(3.150)

We estimate the two terms on the right-hand side separately. For the first term, using (
3.138
) and (
3.129
) gives

\displaystyle h\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\left|\widetilde{Z}_{i}^{\pi}-Z_{t_{i}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}|Z_{t}-\widetilde{Z}_{i}^{\pi}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]+2\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}|Z_{t}-Z_{t_{i}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})h,

and for the second term, the spatial Lipschitz continuity of
v
and (
3.138
) give

\displaystyle h\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\left|v_{i}^{0}(X_{t_{i}})-v_{i}^{0}(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})\right|^{2}\leq Ch\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}|X_{t_{i}}-\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi}|^{2}\leq C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})h,

Combining these results gives the desired estimate (
3.139
).

∎

Theorem 3.9

(Time-discretization error)
.

Suppose that the assumptions of Theorem
3.7
and Lemma
3.8
hold. Let
(X^{\pi},Y^{\pi},\widehat{Z}^{\pi})
be the discrete fixed-point solution. There exists a constant
C>0
, independent of the partition and
x_{0}
, such that

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq i<N}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}\bigl(|X_{t}-X_{i}^{\pi}|^{2}+|Y_{t}-Y_{i}^{\pi}|^{2}\bigr)\right]+\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}|Z_{t}-\widehat{Z}_{i}^{\pi}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})h.

(3.151)

Proof.

Let
(\widetilde{u}^{\pi},\widetilde{v}^{\pi})=F^{\pi}(u^{0},v^{0})
be the approximation obtained after one iteration with the exact grid fields
(u^{0},v^{0})
as inputs, and denote the corresponding processes by
(\widetilde{X}^{\pi},\widetilde{Y}^{\pi},\widetilde{Z}^{\pi})
. We compare this approximation with the discrete fixed-point solution, using the same initial state and Brownian increments.

Define

\Delta X_{i}\coloneqq X_{i}^{\pi}-\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi},\qquad\Delta Y_{i}\coloneqq Y_{i}^{\pi}-\widetilde{Y}_{i}^{\pi},\qquad\Delta\widehat{Z}_{i}\coloneqq\widehat{Z}_{i}^{\pi}-\widetilde{Z}_{i}^{\pi}.

(3.152)

Since both forward processes start from
x_{0}
, we have
\Delta X_{0}=0
.

By Theorem
3.7
, the fixed-point fields satisfy
L(u^{\pi})\leq\bar{L}
and
L(v^{\pi})\leq\bar{V}
. Consequently, we can show that

\displaystyle\left|u_{i}^{\pi}(X_{i}^{\pi})-u_{i}^{0}(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})\right|^{2}\leq 2\left|u_{i}^{\pi}(X_{i}^{\pi})-u_{i}^{\pi}(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})\right|^{2}+2\left|(u_{i}^{\pi}-u_{i}^{0})(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})\right|^{2}\leq 2\bar{L}|\Delta X_{i}|^{2}+2\left|(u_{i}^{\pi}-u_{i}^{0})(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})\right|^{2},

(3.153)

\displaystyle\left|v_{i}^{\pi}(X_{i}^{\pi})-v_{i}^{0}(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})\right|^{2}\leq 2\bar{V}|\Delta X_{i}|^{2}+2\left|(v_{i}^{\pi}-v_{i}^{0})(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})\right|^{2}.

Since both discrete forward equations use the same coefficient
\sigma_{i}
and the same Brownian increment, their diffusion terms cancel when the equations are subtracted. Applying the same forward difference estimate as in the proof of Theorem
3.7
therefore gives

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|\Delta X_{i}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{u}\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}\left|(u_{i}^{\pi}-u_{i}^{0})(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})\right|^{2}+\beta_{v}h\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\left|(v_{i}^{\pi}-v_{i}^{0})(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})\right|^{2}.

(3.154)

The backward difference estimates apply to these two solutions as well, yielding

\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|\Delta Y_{i}|^{2}\leq C_{Y}\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|\Delta X_{i}|^{2},\qquad h\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}|\Delta\widehat{Z}_{i}|^{2}\leq C_{Z}\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|\Delta X_{i}|^{2}.

(3.155)

We now estimate the two field-difference terms on the right-hand side of (
3.154
). Since
Y_{i}^{\pi}=u_{i}^{\pi}(X_{i}^{\pi})
and
\widetilde{Y}_{i}^{\pi}=\widetilde{u}_{i}^{\pi}(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})
, we have

\displaystyle(u_{i}^{\pi}-u_{i}^{0})(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})=u_{i}^{\pi}(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})-u_{i}^{\pi}(X_{i}^{\pi})+\Delta Y_{i}+(\widetilde{u}_{i}^{\pi}-u_{i}^{0})(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi}).

(3.156)

For any
\varepsilon>0
, Young’s inequality and the Lipschitz bound on
u_{i}^{\pi}
give

\displaystyle\left|(u_{i}^{\pi}-u_{i}^{0})(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2(1+\varepsilon)\left[\bar{L}|\Delta X_{i}|^{2}+|\Delta Y_{i}|^{2}\right]+\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\left|(\widetilde{u}_{i}^{\pi}-u_{i}^{0})(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})\right|^{2}.

(3.157)

Taking expectations and maxima, and using (
3.155
), we obtain

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}\left|(u_{i}^{\pi}-u_{i}^{0})(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})\right|^{2}\leq 2(1+\varepsilon)(C_{Y}+\bar{L})\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|\Delta X_{i}|^{2}+\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}\left|(\widetilde{u}_{i}^{\pi}-u_{i}^{0})(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})\right|^{2}.

(3.158)

Similarly, using
\widehat{Z}_{i}^{\pi}=v_{i}^{\pi}(X_{i}^{\pi})
and
\widetilde{Z}_{i}^{\pi}=\widetilde{v}_{i}^{\pi}(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|(v_{i}^{\pi}-v_{i}^{0})(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})\right|^{2}\leq 2(1+\varepsilon)\left[\bar{V}|\Delta X_{i}|^{2}+|\Delta\widehat{Z}_{i}|^{2}\right]+\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\left|(\widetilde{v}_{i}^{\pi}-v_{i}^{0})(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})\right|^{2}.

(3.159)

Taking expectations, multiplying by
h
, and summing over
i=0,\ldots,N-1
, we can derive

\displaystyle h\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\left|(v_{i}^{\pi}-v_{i}^{0})(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})\right|^{2}\leq 2(1+\varepsilon)(C_{Z}+\bar{V}T)\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|\Delta X_{i}|^{2}+\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)h\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\left|(\widetilde{v}_{i}^{\pi}-v_{i}^{0})(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})\right|^{2}.

(3.160)

Substituting (
3.158
) and (
3.160
) into (
3.154
) gives

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|\Delta X_{i}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2(1+\varepsilon)\left[\beta_{u}(C_{Y}+\bar{L})+\beta_{v}(C_{Z}+\bar{V}T)\right]\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|\Delta X_{i}|^{2}

(3.161)

\displaystyle+\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\beta_{u}\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}\left|(\widetilde{u}_{i}^{\pi}-u_{i}^{0})(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\beta_{v}h\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\left|(\widetilde{v}_{i}^{\pi}-v_{i}^{0})(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})\right|^{2}.

By the definitions of
C_{U}
,
C_{V}
, and
\rho_{\eta}
, the coefficient satisfies

2\beta_{u}(C_{Y}+\bar{L})+2\beta_{v}(C_{Z}+\bar{V}T)=C_{U}\beta_{u}+C_{V}\beta_{v}\leq(C_{U}+\eta C_{V})\max\left\{\beta_{u},\frac{\beta_{v}}{\eta}\right\}=\rho_{\eta}.

(3.162)

Since
\rho_{\eta}<1
, we may choose
\varepsilon>0
, independently of the partition, such that
(1+\varepsilon)\rho_{\eta}<1
. Moving the corresponding term in (
3.161
) to the left-hand side yields

\displaystyle\left[1-(1+\varepsilon)\rho_{\eta}\right]\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|\Delta X_{i}|^{2}\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\Bigg[\beta_{u}\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}\left|(\widetilde{u}_{i}^{\pi}-u_{i}^{0})(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})\right|^{2}+\beta_{v}h\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\left|(\widetilde{v}_{i}^{\pi}-v_{i}^{0})(\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi})\right|^{2}\Bigg].

(3.163)

By the consistency estimate (
3.139
) in Lemma
3.8
, the expression in square brackets on the right-hand side is bounded by
C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})h
. Since
\varepsilon
,
\rho_{\eta}
,
\beta_{u}
, and
\beta_{v}
are independent of the partition, we conclude that

\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|X_{i}^{\pi}-\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi}|^{2}\leq C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})h.

(3.164)

Combining this estimate with (
3.155
) gives

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|X_{i}^{\pi}-\widetilde{X}_{i}^{\pi}|^{2}+\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|Y_{i}^{\pi}-\widetilde{Y}_{i}^{\pi}|^{2}+h\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}|\widehat{Z}_{i}^{\pi}-\widetilde{Z}_{i}^{\pi}|^{2}\leq C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})h.

(3.165)

Finally, split the errors through
(\widetilde{X}^{\pi},\widetilde{Y}^{\pi},\widetilde{Z}^{\pi})
and apply
|a+b|^{2}\leq 2|a|^{2}+2|b|^{2}
. The differences between the discrete approximations are constant in
t
on each interval
[t_{i},t_{i+1}]
. Combining the process consistency estimate (
3.138
) with (
3.165
) therefore proves (
3.151
).

∎

Corollary 3.10

(Total discretization and iteration error)
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
3.9
, let
(X^{\pi,M},Y^{\pi,M},\widehat{Z}^{\pi,M})
be the approximation after
M\geq 1
iterations. There exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
h
,
M
, and
x_{0}
, such that

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq i<N}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}\bigl(|X_{t}-X_{i}^{\pi,M}|^{2}+|Y_{t}-Y_{i}^{\pi,M}|^{2}\bigr)\right]

(3.166)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}|Z_{t}-\widehat{Z}_{i}^{\pi,M}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})\bigl(h+\rho_{\eta}^{M-1}\bigr).

Proof.

For the initial pair
(0,x_{0})
, the forward and backward difference estimates established in the proof of Theorem
3.7
give, for every
m\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|X_{i}^{\pi,m+1}-X_{i}^{\pi,m}|^{2}+\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|Y_{i}^{\pi,m+1}-Y_{i}^{\pi,m}|^{2}+h\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}|\widehat{Z}_{i}^{\pi,m+1}-\widehat{Z}_{i}^{\pi,m}|^{2}

(3.167)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})\mathcal{E}_{m}\leq C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})\rho_{\eta}^{m-1},

where we have used the partition-independent bound on
\mathcal{E}_{1}
.

We first consider the
X
-component. Fix
M\geq 1
and let
K>M
. Using the telescoping identity, Minkowski’s inequality, and (
3.167
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|X_{i}^{\pi,K}-X_{i}^{\pi,M}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}\left|\sum_{m=M}^{K-1}\left(X_{i}^{\pi,m+1}-X_{i}^{\pi,m}\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(3.168)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=M}^{K-1}\left(\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|X_{i}^{\pi,m+1}-X_{i}^{\pi,m}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})^{1/2}\sum_{m=M}^{K-1}\rho_{\eta}^{(m-1)/2}.

Letting
K\to\infty
and using the convergence of
X^{\pi,K}
to
X^{\pi}
in this discrete
L^{2}
-norm, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|X_{i}^{\pi}-X_{i}^{\pi,M}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})^{1/2}\sum_{m=M}^{\infty}\rho_{\eta}^{(m-1)/2}=C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})^{1/2}\frac{\rho_{\eta}^{(M-1)/2}}{1-\sqrt{\rho_{\eta}}}.

Squaring both sides yields

\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathbb{E}|X_{i}^{\pi,M}-X_{i}^{\pi}|^{2}\leq C(1+|x_{0}|^{2})\rho_{\eta}^{M-1},

(3.169)

where the factor
(1-\sqrt{\rho_{\eta}})^{-2}
has been absorbed into
C
.

The same argument applies to the
Y
-component and to the
Z
-component, and for the latter we use the discrete
L^{2}
-norm
\bigl(h\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}|\widehat{Z}_{i}^{\pi,K}-\widehat{Z}_{i}^{\pi,M}|^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}
. Combining these estimates with Theorem
3.9
and using the inequality
|a+b|^{2}\leq 2|a|^{2}+2|b|^{2}
, we obtain (
3.166
).

4
Numerical experiments

We investigate the numerical performance and convergence behavior of the Markovian iteration scheme. We first describe three numerical implementations and discuss how Monte Carlo sampling error may affect their performance, motivating our preference for the derivative-based and merged implementations. We then introduce the experimental setup and present the numerical examples.

4.1
Three numerical implementations

We consider three implementations, referred to as
direct
,
derivative-based
, and
merged
. They share the same initialization and forward recursion in the scheme (
2.1
) but differ in how the backward step is implemented. Under suitable conditions, they yield the same discrete processes at the population level, meaning that expectations are evaluated exactly and minimization is performed over all square-integrable functions of the current state.

Direct implementation.
The direct implementation uses Monte Carlo regression to approximate the conditional expectations in (
2.1
). In particular, the
Z
-field is approximated by regressing the target
h^{-1}Y_{i+1}^{\pi,m}\Delta W_{i}
onto functions of
X_{i}^{\pi,m}
, according to

\widehat{Z}_{i}^{\pi,m}=\frac{1}{h}\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}\left[Y_{i+1}^{\pi,m}\Delta W_{i}\right].

(4.1)

Once the
Z
-field has been approximated, the
Y
-field is obtained by regression using the implicit backward equation, with the same parametrization of
Y_{i}^{\pi,m}
used both inside and outside the driver.

Derivative-based implementation.
The derivative-based implementation also approximates the
Z
-field by Monte Carlo regression, but uses the Gaussian integration-by-parts representation (
2.6
),

\widehat{Z}_{i}^{\pi,m}=\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}\left[\sigma_{i}\,\partial_{x}u_{i+1}^{\pi,m}\bigl(X_{i+1}^{\pi,m}\bigr)\right].

(4.2)

This classical representation holds under additional spatial regularity, for example if
u_{i+1}^{\pi,m}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
with bounded derivative. The regression target is then
\sigma_{i}\,\partial_{x}u_{i+1}^{\pi,m}(X_{i+1}^{\pi,m})
, which replaces the Brownian-weighted target in (
4.1
). Whenever the integration-by-parts identity applies, the two targets have the same conditional expectation. Since the
Y
-field is obtained using the same implicit regression formulation, the derivative-based and direct implementations are equivalent at the population level.

Merged implementation.
Even when the required regularity holds, an accurate approximation of
u_{i+1}^{\pi,m}
does not necessarily yield an accurate approximation of its derivative. We therefore also consider a merged implementation, which avoids differentiating the approximated
Y
-field and approximates both backward fields through a joint regression. Its population formulation is

\displaystyle(y_{i}^{\star},z_{i}^{\star})

\displaystyle=\underset{y_{i},z_{i}\in L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{t_{i}})}{\arg\min}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|Y_{i+1}^{\pi,m}-\left(y_{i}-hf(t_{i},X_{i}^{\pi,m},y_{i},z_{i})+z_{i}\Delta W_{i}\right)\right|^{2}\right].

(4.3)

Under the standing step-size condition, the unique minimizer satisfies
y_{i}^{\star}=Y_{i}^{\pi,m}
and
z_{i}^{\star}=\widehat{Z}_{i}^{\pi,m}
, where
Y_{i}^{\pi,m}
and
\widehat{Z}_{i}^{\pi,m}
are the values obtained from the direct implementation. For a justification and application of this equivalence, see for example,
[
GLW05
,
HPW20
]
. We remark that this equivalence only holds when the driver
f
depends on
Y_{i}^{\pi,m}
in the backward scheme, instead of
Y_{i+1}^{\pi,m}
. In practice,
y_{i}
and
z_{i}
are parametrized as functions of
X_{i}^{\pi,m}
, and their coefficients are optimized jointly.

Thus, whenever the derivative representation is valid, the three implementations yield the same backward values at the population level. Since they also share the same initialization and forward recursion, they produce the same discrete processes
(X_{i}^{\pi,m},Y_{i}^{\pi,m},\widehat{Z}_{i}^{\pi,m})
at every Markovian iteration.

Monte Carlo sampling error.
Despite their equivalence at the population level, the three implementations may differ numerically because of restricted parametrizations, finite samples, and optimization errors. At each Markovian iteration, the input fields are fixed and the backward recursion uses an implicit Euler scheme. Numerical difficulties associated with implementing this backward step therefore also arise within the iteration. To illustrate Monte Carlo sampling error as one possible source of these differences, we compare the direct and derivative-based estimators of
v_{i}^{\pi,m}(x)
.

Fix a partition
\pi
, a time index
i
, an iteration
m
, and a starting state
x
, and hold field
u_{i+1}^{\pi,m}
fixed. We treat this field and, where required, its derivative as known functions, so that only the sampling error in approximating the conditional expectations is considered. Let
N_{\mathrm{mc}}
denote the Monte Carlo sample size, and let
X_{i+1}^{\pi,m,(j)}
,
j=1,\ldots,N_{\mathrm{mc}}
, be independent one-step transitions from
x
, driven by independent increments
\Delta W_{i}^{(j)}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,h)
. Setting
Y_{i+1}^{\pi,m,(j)}=u_{i+1}^{\pi,m}(X_{i+1}^{\pi,m,(j)})
, we obtain the following Monte Carlo estimators:

\displaystyle\widehat{Z}_{i,N_{\mathrm{mc}}}^{\mathrm{dir}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{mc}}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\mathrm{mc}}}\frac{Y_{i+1}^{\pi,m,(j)}\Delta W_{i}^{(j)}}{h},\qquad\widehat{Z}_{i,N_{\mathrm{mc}}}^{\mathrm{der}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{i}}{N_{\mathrm{mc}}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\mathrm{mc}}}\partial_{x}u_{i+1}^{\pi,m}\left(X_{i+1}^{\pi,m,(j)}\right).

(4.4)

For the direct estimator, we have

\mathbb{E}\left|\widehat{Z}_{i,N_{\mathrm{mc}}}^{\mathrm{dir}}-v_{i}^{\pi,m}(x)\right|^{2}=\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{mc}}}\operatorname{Var}\left(\frac{Y_{i+1}^{\pi,m,(1)}\Delta W_{i}^{(1)}}{h}\right)=\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{mc}}h}\right).

(4.5)

The variance identity follows from independence, while the order estimate follows from the Gaussian moments and the uniform Lipschitz and growth bounds established earlier under our standing assumptions. For related discussions of variance amplification and statistical error estimates in more general settings, see
[
AA13
,
LdRS15
,
GL08
]
. The order
(N_{\mathrm{mc}}h)^{-1}
is attained when
u_{i+1}^{\pi,m}
is a nonzero constant. Thus, for a fixed sample size, the sampling error may increase as the time step decreases. In the full numerical scheme, these errors may also propagate across time steps and successive Markovian iterations.

For the derivative-based estimator, whenever the classical integration-by-parts representation applies, the uniform Lipschitz bound gives
|\partial_{x}u_{i+1}^{\pi,m}(y)|^{2}\leq\bar{L}
for every
y\in\mathbb{R}
. Independence and the boundedness of
\sigma
therefore yield

\mathbb{E}\left|\widehat{Z}_{i,N_{\mathrm{mc}}}^{\mathrm{der}}-v_{i}^{\pi,m}(x)\right|^{2}=\frac{\sigma_{i}^{2}}{N_{\mathrm{mc}}}\operatorname{Var}\left(\partial_{x}u_{i+1}^{\pi,m}\left(X_{i+1}^{\pi,m,(1)}\right)\right)\leq\frac{\overline{\sigma}^{2}\bar{L}}{N_{\mathrm{mc}}}.

(4.6)

Thus, the mean-square sampling error is
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{mc}}^{-1})
, uniformly in the time-step size, time index, iteration index, and starting state
x
. This estimate avoids the explicit
h^{-1}
amplification associated with the direct estimator, although practical performance also depends on the accuracy of the derivative approximation.

The merged implementation avoids a separate regression with the Brownian-weighted target and requires no derivative approximation. In particular, avoiding this target is a feature shared by the derivative-based implementation, which may help explain their comparable numerical performance shown in the subsequent subsections. For statistical error analyses of methods based on joint regression objectives of the form (
4.3
), under different parametrizations and assumptions, see
[
GLW05
,
OXW25
]
.

This local comparison of Monte Carlo sampling errors offers a possible explanation for the numerical behavior observed in subsequent subsections. A complete analysis accounting for parametrization, optimization, and error propagation across time steps and iterations is beyond the scope of this work.

4.2
Experimental setup

We now describe the parametrization, reference solution, and error measures used in the numerical experiments. For all three implementations, we follow
[
BZ08
]
in parametrizing both decoupling fields using quadratic polynomial basis functions. For simplicity, we retain the notation
u_{i}^{\pi,m}
and
v_{i}^{\pi,m}
for their numerical approximations throughout the numerical experiments. More precisely, for
i=0,\ldots,N-1
, on the computational domain,

u_{i}^{\pi,m}(x)=\alpha_{0}^{i,m}+\sum_{j=1}^{d_{1}}\alpha_{j}^{i,m}x_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{d_{1}}\alpha_{d_{1}+j}^{i,m}x_{j}^{2}+\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq d_{1}}\alpha_{j,k}^{i,m}x_{j}x_{k},

(4.7)

where
x_{j}
denotes the
j
-th component of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
. The coefficients
\alpha_{0}^{i,m}
,
\alpha_{j}^{i,m}
,
\alpha_{d_{1}+j}^{i,m}
, and
\alpha_{j,k}^{i,m}
are determined by solving the corresponding regression problem. The same class of basis functions is used for
v_{i}^{\pi,m}
, with separate coefficients for each component, collectively denoted by
\beta^{i,m}
. The terminal field is prescribed by
u_{N}^{\pi,m}=g
.

The inputs to the basis functions are clipped to a bounded computational domain, and the derivative-based implementation uses the derivatives of the resulting clipped functions. Although our convergence analysis is restricted to one dimension for simplicity, the numerical implementations extend to multidimensional processes by applying the parametrization componentwise to the backward processes
Y
and
Z
. The resulting regression problems are solved using SciPy’s implementation of the BFGS algorithm in Python.

For each test problem with known continuous decoupling fields, we construct a reference solution by first decoupling the FBSDE and then applying the Euler–Maruyama method on a fine uniform grid with
N^{\prime}=2\times 10^{4}
time steps. The reference values of
Y
and
Z
are obtained by evaluating these fields along the reference forward process. We choose
N
so that
N^{\prime}/N
is an integer and construct each coarse Brownian increment by summing the corresponding fine increments, ensuring that the coarse and reference approximations use the same Brownian paths. The fine-grid reference solution is sampled at the coarse-grid time points and serves as a surrogate for the continuous-time solution.

Let
N_{\mathrm{mc}}
denote the number of Monte Carlo sample paths, and let the superscript
(j)
identify the
j
-th sample path. We report the following error measures after
M
Markovian iterations, so that
m=M
below.

\displaystyle\mathrm{Err}(X)

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\leq i\leq N}\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{mc}}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\mathrm{mc}}}|X_{i}^{\pi,m,(j)}-X_{i}^{(j)}|^{2},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Err}(Y)

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\leq i\leq N}\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{mc}}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\mathrm{mc}}}|Y_{i}^{\pi,m,(j)}-Y_{i}^{(j)}|^{2},

(4.8)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Err}(Z)

\displaystyle=\frac{T}{NN_{\mathrm{mc}}}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\mathrm{mc}}}|\widehat{Z}_{i}^{\pi,m,(j)}-Z_{i}^{(j)}|^{2},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Total\ Err}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Err}(X)+\mathrm{Err}(Y)+\mathrm{Err}(Z).

Here
X_{i}^{(j)}
,
Y_{i}^{(j)}
, and
Z_{i}^{(j)}
denote the corresponding reference values at time
t_{i}
, and
|\cdot|
denotes the Euclidean norm for vectors and the Frobenius norm for matrices. Since
h=T/N
,
\mathrm{Err}(Z)
approximates the time-integrated squared
L^{2}
error used in the theoretical analysis through Monte Carlo sampling and left-endpoint quadrature. Throughout the experiments, we choose
N_{\mathrm{mc}}=15000
unless otherwise specified.

Example 1: A one-dimensional FBSDE

5
Conclusion

We have established convergence of the Markovian iteration for FBSDEs with a
Z
-dependent forward drift and deterministic, time-dependent diffusion. The main ingredient is a Gaussian smoothing representation of the discrete
Z
-field, which yields uniform spatial Lipschitz bounds for the discrete decoupling fields without requiring classical second derivatives. Under suitable contraction conditions, this leads to geometric convergence of the Markovian iteration and well-posedness of the discrete FBSDE. Combined with the time-discretization analysis, we obtain an overall error bound accounting for both the iteration and discretization errors.

The numerical experiments support the theoretical convergence results and illustrate the practical performance of the derivative-based and merged implementations. Extending the analysis to multidimensional systems and more general, in particular state-dependent, diffusion coefficients is a natural direction for future work.

Proofs of auxiliary results

A.1
Implicit backward stability

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Let us denote
\Delta\overline{Y}_{i}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}\left[\Delta Y_{i+1}\right]
. Since
\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}[\Delta W_{i}]=0
, the definition of
\widehat{Z}_{i}^{(\ell)}
gives

\Delta\widehat{Z}_{i}=\frac{1}{h}\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}\left[\Delta Y_{i+1}\Delta W_{i}\right]=\frac{1}{h}\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}\left[(\Delta Y_{i+1}-\Delta\overline{Y}_{i})\Delta W_{i}\right].

(A.1)

By the conditional Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and
\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}[|\Delta W_{i}|^{2}]=h
,

\displaystyle h^{2}|\Delta\widehat{Z}_{i}|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}\left[|\Delta Y_{i+1}-\Delta\overline{Y}_{i}|^{2}\right]\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}\left[|\Delta W_{i}|^{2}\right]=h\left(\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}\left[|\Delta Y_{i+1}|^{2}\right]-|\Delta\overline{Y}_{i}|^{2}\right).

(A.2)

Hence,

|\Delta\overline{Y}_{i}|^{2}+h|\Delta\widehat{Z}_{i}|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}\left[|\Delta Y_{i+1}|^{2}\right].

(A.3)

Subtracting the two implicit equations in (
3.6
) gives

\Delta\overline{Y}_{i}=\Delta Y_{i}-h\Delta f_{i},

(A.4)

where
\Delta f_{i}\coloneqq f(t_{i},X_{i}^{(1)},Y_{i}^{(1)},\widehat{Z}_{i}^{(1)})-f(t_{i},X_{i}^{(2)},Y_{i}^{(2)},\widehat{Z}_{i}^{(2)})
.

Substituting into (
A.3
) and rearranging, we obtain

\displaystyle|\Delta Y_{i}|^{2}+h|\Delta\widehat{Z}_{i}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}\left[|\Delta Y_{i+1}|^{2}\right]+2h\Delta Y_{i}\Delta f_{i}-h^{2}|\Delta f_{i}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}\left[|\Delta Y_{i+1}|^{2}\right]+2h\Delta Y_{i}\Delta f_{i}.

(A.5)

To estimate the product
\Delta Y_{i}\Delta f_{i}
, we decompose the driver difference as

\displaystyle\Delta f_{i}={}

\displaystyle f(t_{i},X_{i}^{(1)},Y_{i}^{(1)},\widehat{Z}_{i}^{(1)})-f(t_{i},X_{i}^{(1)},Y_{i}^{(2)},\widehat{Z}_{i}^{(1)})

(A.6)

\displaystyle+f(t_{i},X_{i}^{(1)},Y_{i}^{(2)},\widehat{Z}_{i}^{(1)})-f(t_{i},X_{i}^{(2)},Y_{i}^{(2)},\widehat{Z}_{i}^{(1)})

\displaystyle+f(t_{i},X_{i}^{(2)},Y_{i}^{(2)},\widehat{Z}_{i}^{(1)})-f(t_{i},X_{i}^{(2)},Y_{i}^{(2)},\widehat{Z}_{i}^{(2)}).

For the difference in the
y
-variable, the monotonicity condition (
3.2
) in Assumption
1
yields

\displaystyle 2\Delta Y_{i}\Big[

\displaystyle f(t_{i},X_{i}^{(1)},Y_{i}^{(1)},\widehat{Z}_{i}^{(1)})-f(t_{i},X_{i}^{(1)},Y_{i}^{(2)},\widehat{Z}_{i}^{(1)})\Big]\leq 2k_{f}|\Delta Y_{i}|^{2}.

(A.7)

For the differences in the
x
- and
z
-variables, the Lipschitz bounds and Young’s inequality with parameters
\lambda_{x}
and
\lambda_{z}
give

\displaystyle 2|\Delta Y_{i}|\bigl|f(t_{i},X_{i}^{(1)},Y_{i}^{(2)},\widehat{Z}_{i}^{(1)})-f(t_{i},X_{i}^{(2)},Y_{i}^{(2)},\widehat{Z}_{i}^{(1)})\bigr|\leq\lambda_{x}|\Delta Y_{i}|^{2}+\frac{f_{x}}{\lambda_{x}}|\Delta X_{i}|^{2},

(A.8)

\displaystyle 2|\Delta Y_{i}|\bigl|f(t_{i},X_{i}^{(2)},Y_{i}^{(2)},\widehat{Z}_{i}^{(1)})-f(t_{i},X_{i}^{(2)},Y_{i}^{(2)},\widehat{Z}_{i}^{(2)})\bigr|\leq\lambda_{z}|\Delta Y_{i}|^{2}+\frac{f_{z}}{\lambda_{z}}|\Delta\widehat{Z}_{i}|^{2}.

Combining these estimates gives

2\Delta Y_{i}\Delta f_{i}\leq\theta_{0}|\Delta Y_{i}|^{2}+\frac{f_{x}}{\lambda_{x}}|\Delta X_{i}|^{2}+\frac{f_{z}}{\lambda_{z}}|\Delta\widehat{Z}_{i}|^{2}.

(A.9)

Substituting (
A.9
) into (
A.5
) yields

(1-\theta_{0}h)|\Delta Y_{i}|^{2}+\left(1-\frac{f_{z}}{\lambda_{z}}\right)h|\Delta\widehat{Z}_{i}|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}\left[|\Delta Y_{i+1}|^{2}\right]+\frac{f_{x}}{\lambda_{x}}h|\Delta X_{i}|^{2}.

(A.10)

Since
1-\theta_{0}^{+}h_{0}>0
, we have
1-\theta_{0}h\geq 1-\theta_{0}^{+}h_{0}>0
for every
0<h\leq h_{0}
. Dividing by
1-\theta_{0}h
gives

\displaystyle|\Delta Y_{i}|^{2}+\frac{1-f_{z}/\lambda_{z}}{1-\theta_{0}h}h|\Delta\widehat{Z}_{i}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{1-\theta_{0}h}\left(\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}\left[|\Delta Y_{i+1}|^{2}\right]+\frac{f_{x}}{\lambda_{x}}h|\Delta X_{i}|^{2}\right).

(A.11)

and we can check that the coefficients satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\theta_{0}h}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\theta_{0}h}{1-\theta_{0}h}\leq 1+\frac{\theta_{0}^{+}h}{1-\theta_{0}^{+}h_{0}}=1+\theta_{2}h,

(A.12)

\displaystyle\frac{f_{x}}{\lambda_{x}(1-\theta_{0}h)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{f_{x}}{\lambda_{x}(1-\theta_{0}^{+}h_{0})}=\theta_{3},

\displaystyle\frac{1-f_{z}/\lambda_{z}}{1-\theta_{0}h}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1-f_{z}/\lambda_{z}}{1+|\theta_{0}|h_{0}}=\theta_{1}>0.

Applying these bounds to (
A.11
) proves (
3.7
).

A.2
Properties of the Gaussian convolution semigroup

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

First,
\mathcal{G}_{0}^{\varsigma}
is the identity operator. If
\phi
is globally Lipschitz, then

|\phi(y)|\leq|\phi(0)|+\operatorname{Lip}(\phi)|y|.

(A.13)

Since Gaussian random variables have finite first absolute moments, this linear-growth bound implies
\mathbb{E}|\phi(a+\varsigma\sqrt{h}\,G)|<\infty
. Thus the expectation defining
\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi
is well defined and finite. The same conclusion holds immediately when
\phi
is bounded and measurable.

Moreover, if
\phi
is globally Lipschitz, then so is
\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi
, since

|(\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi)(a)-(\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi)(b)|\leq\operatorname{Lip}(\phi)|a-b|.

(A.14)

Thus
\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}
maps globally Lipschitz functions into themselves. If
\phi
is bounded and measurable, then
\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi
is measurable and bounded, with
|(\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi)(a)|\leq\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}|\phi(y)|.
Hence, this class of functions is also preserved.

For
h,s>0
, let
G_{1},G_{2}
be independent standard normal random variables. For either class of input functions, absolute integrability and Fubini’s theorem give

(\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\mathcal{G}_{s}^{\varsigma}\phi)(a)=\mathbb{E}\left[\phi\left(a+\varsigma\sqrt{h}\,G_{1}+\varsigma\sqrt{s}\,G_{2}\right)\right].

(A.15)

Since
\sqrt{h}\,G_{1}+\sqrt{s}\,G_{2}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,h+s),
it follows that
(\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\mathcal{G}_{s}^{\varsigma}\phi)(a)=(\mathcal{G}_{h+s}^{\varsigma}\phi)(a).
Together with
\mathcal{G}_{0}^{\varsigma}=I
, this proves the semigroup property.

For the remaining properties, let
p_{h}^{\varsigma}
denote the centered Gaussian density with variance
\varsigma^{2}h
, namely,

p_{h}^{\varsigma}(z)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\varsigma^{2}h}}\exp\left(-\frac{z^{2}}{2\varsigma^{2}h}\right).

(A.16)

By the definition of
\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}
, a change of variables, and the symmetry of
p_{h}^{\varsigma}
,

(\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi)(a)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(y)p_{h}^{\varsigma}(y-a)\,\mathrm{d}y=(\phi*p_{h}^{\varsigma})(a).

(A.17)

Thus
\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi
is the convolution of
\phi
with a Gaussian density. Several of the properties below are standard consequences of this structure; nevertheless, we provide the details for completeness.

For (
i
), first suppose that
\phi
is globally Lipschitz, so that it has at most linear growth. For every integer
m\geq 1
, the derivative
\partial_{a}^{m}p_{h}^{\varsigma}(y-a)
is a polynomial in
y-a
multiplied by a Gaussian density. Hence, locally uniformly in
a
,

|\phi(y)|\left|\partial_{a}^{m}p_{h}^{\varsigma}(y-a)\right|

(A.18)

is dominated by an integrable function of
y
. The same argument applies when
\phi
is bounded and measurable, since then
|\phi(y)|
is bounded by a constant, and no continuity of
\phi
is required.

The dominated convergence theorem therefore justifies differentiation under the integral sign in both cases, yielding

\partial_{a}^{m}(\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi)(a)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(y)\partial_{a}^{m}p_{h}^{\varsigma}(y-a)\,\mathrm{d}y,\qquad m\geq 1.

(A.19)

The same domination, together with the continuity of
\partial_{a}^{m}p_{h}^{\varsigma}(y-a)
in
a
, shows that each derivative is continuous. Therefore,
\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.

For
\phi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, choose a measurable representative. Since the Gaussian law has a density with respect to Lebesgue measure, changing this representative on a Lebesgue-null set does not change the integral in (
A.17
) for any
a\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular, we may choose a bounded measurable representative. The differentiation-under-the-integral argument above then shows that
\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover, for every
a\in\mathbb{R}
,

|(\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi)(a)|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\phi(y)|p_{h}^{\varsigma}(y-a)\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{h}^{\varsigma}(y-a)\,\mathrm{d}y=\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}.

(A.20)

Taking the essential supremum gives
\|\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}.

For (
ii
), the differentiation formula established above applies both to globally Lipschitz functions and to bounded measurable functions. Using

\partial_{a}p_{h}^{\varsigma}(y-a)=\frac{y-a}{\varsigma^{2}h}p_{h}^{\varsigma}(y-a),

(A.21)

we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{a}(\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi)(a)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(y)\partial_{a}p_{h}^{\varsigma}(y-a)\,\mathrm{d}y=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(y)\frac{y-a}{\varsigma^{2}h}p_{h}^{\varsigma}(y-a)\,\mathrm{d}y=\frac{1}{\varsigma\sqrt{h}}\mathbb{E}\left[\phi(a+\varsigma\sqrt{h}\,G)G\right].

The expectation is absolutely integrable in both cases, by boundedness or linear growth of
\phi
and the finite Gaussian moments. This proves (
3.11
). Since
\Delta W=\sqrt{h}\,G\sim\mathcal{N}(0,h)
, it follows immediately that

\partial_{a}(\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi)(a)=\frac{1}{\varsigma h}\mathbb{E}\left[\phi(a+\varsigma\Delta W)\Delta W\right].

(A.22)

Finally, for (
iii
), suppose that
\phi
is globally Lipschitz. Then it is locally absolutely continuous and differentiable almost everywhere. Its almost-everywhere derivative coincides with its weak derivative, denoted by
\phi^{\prime}
, and satisfies

|\phi^{\prime}(y)|\leq\operatorname{Lip}(\phi)\qquad\text{for a.e. }y\in\mathbb{R}.

(A.23)

Hence

\phi^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}),\qquad\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\operatorname{Lip}(\phi),

(A.24)

so that
\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi^{\prime}
is well defined and independent of the chosen measurable representative.

By the absolute continuity of
\phi
, integration by parts on bounded intervals, followed by letting the endpoints tend to infinity, gives

\displaystyle(\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi^{\prime})(a)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{\prime}(y)p_{h}^{\varsigma}(y-a)\,\mathrm{d}y=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(y)\partial_{y}p_{h}^{\varsigma}(y-a)\,\mathrm{d}y.

The boundary term vanishes because
\phi
has at most linear growth, whereas the Gaussian density decays exponentially. Moreover, the integrals above are absolutely convergent.

Since
\partial_{a}p_{h}^{\varsigma}(y-a)=-\partial_{y}p_{h}^{\varsigma}(y-a)
, we obtain

\displaystyle(\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi^{\prime})(a)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(y)\partial_{a}p_{h}^{\varsigma}(y-a)\,\mathrm{d}y=\partial_{a}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(y)p_{h}^{\varsigma}(y-a)\,\mathrm{d}y=\partial_{a}(\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi)(a),

where the second equality follows from the differentiation-under-the-integral argument above. This proves (
3.14
).

Since
\phi^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, applying (
i
) and (
ii
) to a bounded measurable representative of
\phi^{\prime}
gives
\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi^{\prime}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and

\partial_{aa}(\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi)(a)=\partial_{a}(\mathcal{G}_{h}^{\varsigma}\phi^{\prime})(a)=\frac{1}{\varsigma\sqrt{h}}\mathbb{E}\left[\phi^{\prime}(a+\varsigma\sqrt{h}\,G)G\right],\qquad a\in\mathbb{R}.

(A.25)

This proves (
3.15
) without requiring a classical second derivative of
\phi
.

∎

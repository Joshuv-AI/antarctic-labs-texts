AMPLIFY: Estimating Clinical and Humanistic Benefits of Pembrolizumab in Portugal
Authors: Pedro Cardoso, Joana Sousa, João Ferreira, Bernardo Rodrigues
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPembrolizumab has become a cornerstone in the treatment of multiple tumor types. In Portugal, it is currently reimbursed across 25 indications. However, a comprehensive holistic assessment of its cumulative clinical and humanistic impact is lacking.ObjectiveThe aim was to estimate the population-level clinical and humanistic outcomes associated with pembrolizumab use in Portugal from 2017 to 2024, and to project its potential future impact until 2030.MethodsWe developed a Microsoft Excel-based tool to aggregate outputs from validated, Portugal-adapted, cost-effectiveness models across all reimbursed indications for pembrolizumab. Using a real-world number of treated patients, clinical outcomes such as incremental life years (LYs), incremental quality-adjusted life years (QALYs), and avoided deaths were estimated. Forecasts through 2030 were developed using market and epidemiological projections. Alternative scenarios evaluated the additional benefits of accelerated reimbursement decisions and expanded patient access.ResultsBetween 2017 and 2024, among 13,530 treated patients, pembrolizumab was associated with estimated lifetime outcomes of 14,042 incremental QALYs, 17,914 incremental LYs, and 2021 deaths avoided. Scenario analyses showed that with accelerated reimbursement, within 180 days of market approval, and full uptake, cumulative gains could have increased by up to 14,705 QALYs (total 28,746 QALYs) and an additional 1250 deaths could have been avoided (total 3271 avoided deaths). Forecasts project continued substantial benefits for patients treated between 2025 and 2030, with lifetime outcomes estimated at approximately 31,306 QALYs, 38,274 LYs, and 4841 deaths avoided for the currently reimbursed indications.ConclusionPembrolizumab has delivered significant clinical benefits in Portugal. Our results suggest that more timely reimbursement decisions and broader access could have yielded measurable additional health gains. These findings highlight the potential impact of access timelines on health outcomes, while acknowledging that broader assessments of value require consideration of cost-effectiveness, budget impact, and opportunity costs. Pembrolizumab's benefits to the Portuguese health system is expected to grow through 2030.

Key Points for Decision Makers

Pembrolizumab use in Portugal has generated substantial clinical and humanistic benefits across 25 reimbursed indications, yielding an estimated 14,042 quality-adjusted life years (QALYs), 17,914 life years, and 2021 deaths avoided between 2017 and 2024.Scenario analyses show that faster reimbursement decisions and broader patient access could have produced up to 14,705 additional QALYs and 1250 more deaths avoided, highlighting forgone health benefits associated with access delays.Forecasts through 2030 indicate that pembrolizumab’s contribution to population health will continue to grow, adding more than 31,000 QALYs and 4800 deaths avoided in currently reimbursed indications.

Introduction

In Portugal, cancer is a significant public health issue. According to the latest available data from the Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD), in 2022, there were an estimated 66,000 new cancer cases in Portugal. The five most incident cancers across both sexes were colorectal (15%), breast (13%), prostate (11%), lung (9%), and gastric (5%) [1]. Cancer not only presents a high incidence but also imposes a substantial clinical burden, reflected in high mortality and the chronic progression of many tumor types. Estimates from the World Health Organization's Cancer Tomorrow project 74,557 new cancer cases in Portugal by 2030, a 7.2% increase from the 2022 estimate, largely driven by an increase in lung cancer (+ 8.2%), colorectal cancer (+ 9%), pancreatic cancer (+ 10.2%), and bladder cancer (+ 10.9%) [2].

In terms of mortality, cancer was the second leading cause of death in Portugal, representing 23% of all deaths. Lung cancer continues to be the leading cause of cancer-related mortality, followed by colorectal cancer [1]. This substantial mortality burden is expected to persist, and by 2030, cancer is expected to be responsible for 37,124 deaths yearly, with prostate cancer driving this pattern compared to 2022 (+ 14.3%), followed by bladder cancer (+ 13.0%) and non-melanoma skin cancer (+ 12%) [2].

Cancer is a major public-health burden in Portugal, affecting patients, families, and society. It increases healthcare costs, reduces quality of life, and lowers productivity and overall well-being. Several studies have quantified this impact. Lopes et al. estimated a direct impact to healthcare costs of €867 million in 2017 [3]. Adding to this, the OECD Country Cancer Profile estimates that until 2050 cancer will lead to a loss of 200 full-time equivalent workers (FTEs) per 100,000 people in Portugal, higher than average in OECD countries [1].

Taken together, the combined clinical, societal, and personal burden highlights the need for effective therapeutic strategies and timely access to innovation.

Within the Portuguese National Health Service (SNS), cancer management typically involves multidisciplinary approaches including surgery, radiotherapy, systemic therapies, and increasingly precision medicine and immuno-oncology. Access to innovative medicines is regulated by INFARMED through a formal health technology assessment (HTA) process that evaluates clinical benefit, safety, cost-effectiveness, and budget impact before reimbursement decisions are granted.

The process begins with an assessment to determine the drug's therapeutic value and its alignment with public health priorities. Based on this evaluation, the drug may be granted reimbursement at different levels (e.g., full or partial coverage), depending on its therapeutic classification, with higher reimbursement typically provided for essential or life-saving medicines [4].

Timelines for the reimbursement process in Portugal vary based on the complexity of the evaluation. Legally, the assessment process should take up to 180 days after the submission of a complete application, followed by a 30-day period of negotiation [5]. However, this deadline is frequently exceeded, causing significant delays in patients’ access to innovative treatments. If additional information or clarification is required, the timeline may be extended further. A recent report by the European Federation of Pharmaceutical Industries and Associations, estimates the average time from European Medicines Agency (EMA) approval to reimbursement in oncology drugs for Portugal to be 853 days [6]. Once a drug is approved for reimbursement, the decision is published, and the drug becomes available to patients under the specified terms. For innovative or high-cost treatments, negotiations between the pharmaceutical company and INFARMED I.P. may extend the process, ensuring that cost and access considerations are balanced.

Immune checkpoint Inhibitors (ICIs) have revolutionized the management of numerous malignancies. Immune checkpoints, expressed on immune cells, modulate the intensity of immune responses and prevent excessive activation. Several checkpoint molecules have been characterized over recent decades, notably programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1)/programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1), cytotoxic T-lymphocyte associated protein 4 (CTLA-4), lymphocyte-activation gene 3 (LAG-3), and T cell immunoglobulin domain and mucin (TIM-3) [7].

Pembrolizumab, marketed as Keytruda® by MSD, is a humanized monoclonal antibody designed to block the PD‑1 receptor on T cells. Under normal conditions, PD‑1 engages its ligands PD‑L1 and PD‑L2, transmitting inhibitory signals that curb T cell activity. Tumor cells exploit this pathway by overexpressing PD‑L1/PD‑L2, effectively turning off effector T cells and evading immune detection. By targeting PD‑1, pembrolizumab interrupts this “checkpoint” inhibition, reactivating exhausted T cells to recognize and destroy cancer cells [8, 9]. The EMA granted the first approved indication for pembrolizumab in July 2015 [10]. In Portugal, pembrolizumab has been reimbursed for 25 therapeutic indications, beginning with melanoma in 2017, with its use expanding each year since. This progressive expansion has enabled broader access to immunotherapy across early-stage and advanced disease settings within the SNS.

There is limited evidence quantifying the clinical and humanistic benefits of pembrolizumab specifically for the SNS across the indications currently reimbursed. The Health Impact Projection (HIP) model analyzed the clinical and economic impact of PD-1/PD-L1 drugs, but as a whole class and covered only a 3‑year period [11]. A study by Mohamed et al. evaluated the first-line application of pembrolizumab in metastatic non-small-cell lung cancer (mNSCLC), head and neck squamous cell cancer (HNSCC), gastric cancer, and renal cell carcinoma (RCC) using clinical trial data and other published studies across multiple countries, but considered only the clinical benefit measured by the European Society of Medical Oncology – Magnitude of Clinical Benefit Scale (ESMO-MCBS) and American Society of Clinical Oncology – Value Framework (ASCO-VF) [12]. A different study, by Monberg et al., estimated the clinical, humanistic, and economic value of pembrolizumab in the treatment of breast and gynecological cancers, using clinical trial data of the United States and Europe [13]. For the early treatment settings, a study from Belgium [14] highlights a significant benefit for patients and the healthcare system. Other publications, like du Rusquec et al., simply aggregate clinical trial and cohort-based published evidence for each indication, without considering a specific population or time horizon [15].

Despite this evidence, no existing publication provides a comprehensive, Portugal-focused assessment of pembrolizumab across all reimbursed indications.

Ultimately, a single comprehensive model demonstrating the clinical and humanistic benefit of pembrolizumab in Portugal can support healthcare decision-making.

This study aims to estimate the clinical benefits and patient reported outcomes achieved since pembrolizumab's initial reimbursement in 2017, highlighting its tangible benefits to the Portuguese SNS, and projects the potential health outcomes for the next 5 years.

Materials and Methods

The target population in the study combines patients across multiple tumor types and includes 23 pembrolizumab-reimbursed indications in Portugal between 2017 and 2024 (melanoma, non-small cell lung cancer [NSCLC], triple negative breast cancer [TNBC], RCC, head and neck, colorectal, esophageal, urothelial, cervix, Hodgkin lymphoma, biliary and gastric). Two indications reimbursed for pembrolizumab + lenvatinib were excluded because corresponding Portugal-adapted cost-effectiveness models (CEMs) were not available. The developed tool uses data from all the CEMs already adapted to Portugal and submitted to INFARMED I.P., based on the respective clinical trials for each indication, incorporating baseline clinical characteristics of the Portuguese population (age, body mass index, gender distribution, and PD-L1 status), economic variables (health resource utilization and unitary costs), and appropriate comparators (defined by Portuguese clinical experts and clinical guidelines, as per the PICO - Population Intervention Comparator Outcome - defined by INFARMED I.P.). This approach ensures methodological consistency with the models validated and accepted during the national reimbursement process.

Cross‑indication aggregation was performed to estimate the overall clinical impact of pembrolizumab. Each indication was modeled independently using indication‑specific inputs, and aggregation was conducted only at the outcome level, assuming mutually exclusive patient cohorts across indications. Aggregated results therefore represent cumulative lifetime benefits accrued by distinct cohorts initiated during the study period and do not imply additivity or sequential treatment effects at the individual level, thereby minimizing the risk of double counting. Lifetime incremental outcomes from each CEM were attributed to patients at treatment initiation for population-level aggregation. This assumption does not imply immediate realization of benefits, as temporal accrual remains governed by the underlying modeled survival and health-state transitions. It reflects a pragmatic convention used to scale lifetime outcomes to population-level impact.

The CEMs used are an aggregation of partitioned survival models (PSMs) (Fig. 1), with some indications utilizing a cohort-based multistate Markov model (MCM) (Fig. 2). Across all CEMs, a cycle length of 1 week was applied, and the time horizons for the reimbursement process varied between 30 and 40 years according to the indication. The time horizon of each indication‑specific CEM was maintained as submitted to INFARMED, I.P., reflecting the clinically appropriate period over which benefits are expected to accrue for each indication. No time‑horizon harmonization was applied, as the objective was to estimate cumulative impact rather than to enable direct comparison of outcomes across indications. Structural differences across indication‑specific models reflect specificity in disease natural history, treatment sequencing, and available evidence. As models were developed independently and do not share structural assumptions that would consistently inflate or attenuate outcomes, these differences are not expected to bias the direction of aggregated system‑level projections. All relevant data inputs were extracted from each CEM and harmonized for integration into the aggregate tool. In both PSM and MCM models, patients start in a progression- or event-free state. They remain there until one of the events occurs: disease progression/recurrence or death. In PSM, once patients transition into progression state, they typically receive salvage therapy, such as chemotherapy or other agents, which is incorporated into the model’s calculations. In each cycle, patients can either stay in the progression state or transition to death, which is an absorbing state. The MCM uses a more detailed structure, distinguishing between recurrence-free, locoregional recurrence, distant metastasis, and death, making it especially useful in early disease settings. These structures were preserved exactly as implemented in the original reimbursed CEMs, without modification.

A discount rate of 4% was applied to both clinical and economical outcomes in all CEMs, as defined in the economical evaluation guidelines of INFARMED I.P. [16]. This analysis is intended as a policy‑level projection of cumulative clinical impact associated with the reimbursement of pembrolizumab across approved indications, reflecting system‑level consequences of reimbursement decisions. It does not aim to compare effectiveness or value across indications or treatments, as such comparisons require harmonized clinical pathways and CEM assumptions beyond the scope of this analysis. The developed tool is a policy-focused, Microsoft Excel macro-based model that estimates clinical benefits and patient-reported outcomes of pembrolizumab use. In health economic evaluations, a widely used method to estimate the population-level impact of an intervention involves multiplying the outcomes of a CEM, such as incremental QALYs or incremental LYs, by the number of patients expected to receive the treatment [39]. This approach scales individual-level benefits derived from the model to the population level, providing a clear estimate of the total health impact of implementing the intervention. This method assumes that the benefits captured in the CEM are fully realized from the moment a patient begins treatment. The main outputs of each CEM were calculated for the relevant time period and stored in a specific dataset. To calculate the number of avoided deaths, locoregional recurrences, and distant metastases, this tool used the original trace based on the Kaplan-Meier curve with parametric adjustment (specific to each individual CEM). The overall survival traces, progression traces, locoregional recurrence traces, and distant metastasis traces were collected from each individual CEM and structured into a single dataset. The difference between the trace values for pembrolizumab and the selected comparator was calculated for each time point between the year of reimbursement and December 31st, 2024, and organized into a structured dataset. This calculation accounts for the number of patients treated each year and the number of months between the year of reimbursement and December 31st, 2024. For the first year (the year of reimbursement), the number of months was calculated from the reimbursement date (month) to December 31st, 2024. For subsequent years, it was assumed that patients treated in that year began treatment on January 1st. Results from the cohorts of patients who started treatment each year were summed to produce the overall output for the cumulative period. Several forecasting approaches were applied to capture uncertainty. Epidemiological methods reflect disease incidence but do not account for access or adoption dynamics, while purely statistical methods rely on historical trends. The market estimates combined with linear extrapolation were selected as the primary scenario, as they integrate expert-informed expectations and better reflect real-world access conditions. Alternative approaches were retained for consistency checks.

Considering this, the defined scenarios were designed to account for the reimbursement decision timelines by INFARMED I.P. and the number of treated patients. The testing and validation process for this Excel tool was carried out to ensure that it performs as expected, providing accurate and reliable results in various scenarios. The tool underwent comprehensive testing to ensure accuracy and reliability. Each macro, formula, and function was tested individually (unit testing) to verify correct results and proper operation of Visual Basic for Applications (VBA) scripts. Following this, integration testing confirmed that all modules, such as cumulative calculations, forecasts, and scenario comparisons, worked together seamlessly, with particular focus on interactions between user inputs, calculations, and outputs. Boundary testing evaluated extreme values and edge cases, including missing or incomplete data, to detect potential errors or unexpected behavior. Performance testing measured calculation time and memory usage under typical and high-load conditions to identify bottlenecks.

Formal deterministic or probabilistic sensitivity analyses were not performed at the aggregate level. All indication-specific CEMs included in this study were previously validated by INFARMED I.P. and underwent sensitivity analyses addressing uncertainty in clinical inputs, survival extrapolation, utilities, and costs. At the population level, remaining uncertainty is primarily driven by assumptions on patient numbers and future uptake, which were explored through alternative scenarios and forecasting approaches.

Results

Between 2017 and 2024, 13,530 patients were treated with pembrolizumab across all indications. This resulted in an estimated lifetime benefit of 14,042 additional QALYs, 17,914 additional LYs, and 2021 avoided deaths. The use of pembrolizumab was also associated with 1738 progressions avoided and 11,748 LYs free of disease and recurrence. The tumor site with the largest cumulative impact was lung, with 9687 QALYs gained, 12,543 LYs, and 1700 avoided deaths, followed by skin, with 1510 QALYs, 1723 LYs, and 95 avoided deaths, and head and neck, with 382 QALYs and 598 LYs gained and 62 avoided deaths (Table 3), driven by earlier reimbursement. Discussion

This study provides a holistic quantification of the clinical and humanistic value of pembrolizumab for all its reimbursed indications in Portugal from 2017 to 2024. With more than 13,500 patients treated and more than 14,000 QALYs and 17,900 LYs gained, pembrolizumab showed a significant positive impact on Portuguese population health. The modeling also estimates relevant future health gains, with up to 38,000 LYs and over 4800 deaths avoided by 2030 if trends remain unabated and without considering future indications. These findings have important implications for oncology treatment policy in Portugal. These findings exceed the estimates of the HIP model for Portugal [8], which projected that, over 3 years, 7773 patients would be treated with anti-PD-(L)1s, resulting in a gain of 4787 LYs, 6901 progression-free survival years, and 4214 QALYs. This difference is explained by the fact that our model includes five additional indications and considers a longer time horizon (5 vs 3 years).

For early-stage disease, the study by Bento-Abreu et al. estimated that treatment with pembrolizumab in Belgium patients would increase total LYs by 4.4% and QALYs by 4.9% over 10 years [14]. These values are in line with our estimations of a 4.8% increase in LYs and a 5.4% increase in QALYs over a 5-year period.

Of importance in this work is the forgone health benefits of delayed reimbursement and lack of access. The model estimates that quicker access, receiving reimbursement within 6 months of marketing authorization, may have yielded 4901 additional QALYs, 6187 LYs, and 563 additional deaths avoided between the years 2017 and 2024. If all eligible patients had been treated, this would have yielded 8185 additional QALYs and 10,329 additional LYs, and over 500 additional lives would have been saved. The final scenario that aggregates the previous two conditions and the full benefit of pembrolizumab shows that compared to the base scenario, 14,705 QALYs and 18,644 additional LYs would be gained, and 1250 additional deaths would have been avoided. These scenarios demonstrate the importance of faster regulatory and reimbursement processes in Portugal. Identified delays not only disrupt patient access to potentially life-saving care but also create tangible health losses on a population level.

The forecast between 2025 and 2030 also confirms that with the ongoing investment in pembrolizumab, 38,274 lifetime LYs and 31,306 lifetime QALYs will be gained, and 4841 deaths will be avoided. In some indications, such as gastric, TNBC, and cervical cancers, a high clinical benefit is expected, with + 4004, + 3380, and + 913 QALYs until 2030, representing newer indications with significant projected growth. NSCLC will remain the dominant driver of total benefit, but indication diversification implies broader systemic value, significantly increasing the impact in both quality of life and avoided deaths, across multiple tumor sites. This again emphasizes pembrolizumab as a mainstay of immunotherapy strategies in oncology and validates its priority by public healthcare systems.

A few limitations must be outlined. First, while CEMs are validated by robust trial data, real-world effectiveness may differ with variations in adherence, comorbidities, and healthcare infrastructure. This study estimates its impact considering the average patient and an expected distribution of outcomes, which may not apply to all subgroups of patients. Second, the estimated benefits are predicated on extrapolated patient numbers and consistency in clinical practice patterns, which may alter with new entries, biomarker discoveries, or evolving treatment guidelines. This implies that individual-level benefits must be representative of population-level benefits, and the estimates of the number of patients receiving the treatment should be accurate. Despite these limitations, all CEMs were validated and reviewed by INFARMED I.P. during the reimbursement process. Although parameter uncertainty was addressed within each validated indication-specific model, residual uncertainty remains at the aggregate level, particularly related to future eligibility and uptake. Results should therefore be interpreted as policy-level estimates of overall magnitude rather than precise point predictions. The assumption that lifetime incremental outcomes are attributed to patients at treatment initiation may overestimate short‑term cumulative benefits, particularly in early‑stage disease settings where gains accrue later. Finally, the forecasted outcomes are likely underestimated, as only currently reimbursed indications are considered. Forecasting future utilization is inherently uncertain. Error metrics from historical methods were used as internal consistency checks rather than formal validation. Across approaches, directional trends and overall conclusions were consistent. New indications are already being evaluated for reimbursement in Portugal, and others are expected between 2025 and 2030.

Despite these limitations, this study provides a structured framework to estimate population-level health outcomes associated with pembrolizumab use in Portugal. The results may inform discussions on access timelines and treatment uptake, while recognizing that broader policy decisions require integration of clinical, economic, and system-level considerations.

Conclusion

This is the first study to estimate the holistic impact of pembrolizumab usage in Portugal, from early-stage to metastatic disease. The use of pembrolizumab has generated very significant benefits for the SNS, improving both health outcomes and quality of life, by lowering significantly the number of recurrences and deaths. This highlights a significant contribution to population health, and that is expected to continue growing through 2030. The study also estimated a loss of benefit associated with both regulatory and access delays, thus pointing to possible policy changes that allow the maximum benefits of pembrolizumab to be delivered.

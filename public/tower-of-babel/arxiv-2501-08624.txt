A semi-orthogonal decomposition theorem for weighted blowups
Oliver Li

Abstract
. We establish a semi-orthogonal decomposition for the weighted blowup of an algebraic stack along a Koszul-regular weighted centre, generalising the classic result of Orlov. Our approach is based on the work of Bergh-Schnürer.



1.
Introduction

Weighted (stacky) blowups have been getting a lot of attention in recent years, prominently as a tool for improving the efficiency of classical algorithms. For example, they have been used by Abramovich, Temkin and Włodarczyk in
[
1
]
to improve Hironaka’s resolution of singularities in characteristic zero. Examples of weighted blowups include usual blowups and the root stack construction of Cadman
[
8
]
. We refer the reader to
[
19
]
for more background.

A natural question to ask is how a given invariant changes after a weighted blowup. For the integral Chow ring, this has been answered by Arena, Obinna and Abramovich in
[
2
]
. As the first result of this paper, we consider various (quasi-)coherent derived categories. We prove:

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
\mathbf{d}=(d_{0},...,d_{n})
a tuple of positive integers. Let
X
be a quasi-compact algebraic stack and let
\mathcal{I}_{\bullet}
be a Koszul-regular weighted centre on
X
, that smooth-locally is of the form
\sum_{i=0}^{n}(f_{i},d_{i})
. Let
\widetilde{X}
be the blowup of
X
along
\cal{I}_{\bullet}
and let
E
denote the exceptional divisor. Write
Z_{i}=V(\cal{I}_{i})
. We have a commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}

Let
\mathrm{D}
denote one of
\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}
or
\Perf
(i.e. perfect complexes). Write
\Phi_{0}^{\mathrm{D}}
for the functor
L\pi^{*}:\mathrm{D}(X)\rightarrow\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(\widetilde{X})
and for any
r<0
write
\Phi_{r}^{\mathrm{D}}
for the functor

\mathcal{F}\mapsto j_{*}(p^{*}(\mathcal{F})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{E}(r)):\mathrm{D}(Z_{1})\rightarrow\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(\widetilde{X}).

Then there is a semi-orthogonal decomposition with
|\mathbf{d}|:=\sum d_{i}
summands:

\mathrm{D}(\widetilde{X})=\langle\im\Phi^{\mathrm{D}}_{1-|\mathbf{d}|},\dots,\im\Phi^{\mathrm{D}}_{-1},\im\Phi^{\mathrm{D}}_{0}\rangle.

Moreover, if
X
is locally noetherian, then the statement also holds for
\mathrm{D}=\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{Coh}}^{-}
and
\mathrm{D}=\mathrm{D}^{\mathsf{b}}_{\mathsf{Coh}}
.

In the two extreme cases of a weighted blowup, namely root stacks and usual blowups, Theorem
1.1
has been established, most recently by Bergh and Schnürer (
[
4
, Theorem C]
). It is their approach that we will follow. However, their argument uses in two crucial ways (namely in establishing that
L\pi^{*}
is fully faithful and that
\mathrm{D}(X)
is generated by
“just”
the right number of elements) that the blowup of an algebraic stack
X
along a Koszul-regular closed immersion
Z\hookrightarrow X
of constant codimension (locally) embeds into
\mathbb{P}^{\codim(Z/X)-1}_{X}
. Unfortunately, it is not so clear that the analogue of this fact is true in the weighted case, so their argument is not directly applicable.

In this paper, we get around the above-mentioned problem by considering a weighted blowup as the
\Proj
of an
extended
Rees algebra, in the sense of
[
19
]
. The extended Rees algebra has a particularly attractive description (see Example
2.13
) and allows us to work with explicit Koszul complexes.

Example 1.2
.

By
[
19
, Example 5.4.4]
, every regular weighted blowup
\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
factors as a sequence of root stacks, a usual (regular) blowup followed by a sequence of
“de-rootings”
. In other words, there is a morphism
\hat{X}\rightarrow\widetilde{X}
which is a sequence of root stacks such that the composition
\hat{X}\rightarrow X
admits a factorisation as a (potentially different) sequence of root stacks followed by a usual (regular) blowup. Note in particular that the de-rootings are necessary, see for example the explicit example presented in
[
19
, Example 5.4.4]
. Since the derived category of root stacks and usual blowups are classical we have a description of
\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(\hat{X})
. Theorem
1.1
then yields an alternative SOD of
\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(\hat{X})
. See Example
4.6
for an explicit case worked out.

A pleasant byproduct of the work done to obtain Theorem
1.1
is that we will compute the relative dualising sheaf of a (Koszul-regular) weighted blowup. This is our second result:

Theorem 1.3
.

Fix
\mathbf{d}=(d_{0},...,d_{n})
a tuple of positive integers. Let
X
be a concentrated algebraic stack with quasi-finite and separated diagonal. Let
\cal{I}_{\bullet}
be a Koszul-regular weighted centre locally of the form
\sum(f_{i},d_{i})
and let
\pi:\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
be the weighted blowup with exceptional divisor
E
. Then
Rf_{*}
admits a right adjoint
\pi^{!}
given by

\pi^{!}\mathcal{F}=L\pi^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\widetilde{X}}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}((\sum d_{i}-1)E).

This, of course, generalises the well-known result for classical blowups.

1.1.
Related Results

When
X
is a smooth projective complex variety and
\widetilde{X}
is the (usual) blowup of a smooth subvariety, the result for
\mathrm{D}=\mathrm{D}^{\mathsf{b}}_{\mathsf{Coh}}=\Perf
goes back to Orlov
[
18
, Theorem 4.1]
. This was generalised by Elagin to the case where
X
is the quotient of a smooth quasi-projective variety by a linearly reductive group over an arbitrary field in
[
10
, Theorem 10.2]
. And as mentioned, when
X
is an algebraic stack and
\widetilde{X}
is the blowup of a Koszul-regular closed substack (in the sense of
[
20
,
Tag 063J
]
), the result was established by Bergh and Schnürer in
[
4
, Theorem C]
.

The case of root stacks goes back to work of Ishii and Ueda in
[
14
, Theorem 1.6]
, where they established the decomposition for
\mathrm{D}=\mathrm{D}^{\mathsf{b}}_{\mathsf{Coh}}=\Perf
in the case
X
is a smooth Deligne-Mumford stack over a field and
\widetilde{X}
is the root stack associated to a smooth divisor. This was refined to the case where
X
was an arbitrary root stack by Bergh, Lunts and Schnürer as
[
5
, Theorem 4.7]
, and reiterated in the aforementioned work of Bergh and Schnürer
[
4
, Theorem C]
.

When
X
is a noetherian separated scheme over an algebraically closed characteristic zero field and
\widetilde{X}
is a root stack, a periodicity phenomenon in the decomposition of
\mathrm{D}^{\mathsf{b}}_{\mathsf{Coh}}
was observed by Bodzenta and Donovan in
[
7
, Theorem 1.2 and Theorem 1.4]
. The authors use this to construct higher spherical functors. A refinement of this result where
X
is arbitrary was proven by Zhao in
[
21
, Theorem 1.1]
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect necessary definitions and results from the literature.

2.1.
Stacky
\Proj
and Weighted Blowups

Let
S
be an algebraic stack and let
\cal{A}=\bigoplus_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}\cal{A}_{d}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded quasi-coherent sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-algebras (note that we are allowing nonzero components in negative degrees). The grading is equivalent to a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action on
\Spec_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}\cal{A}
over
S
, by declaring
\mathbb{G}_{m}
acts with weight
d
on
\cal{A}_{d}
. Write

\displaystyle\cal{A}_{+}:=\bigoplus_{d>0}\cal{A}_{d},

and

\cal{A}_{-}:=\bigoplus_{d<0}\cal{A}_{d}.

Definition 2.1
.

We define
\Proj_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}(\cal{A})
to be the algebraic stack

\Proj_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}(\cal{A}):=[(\Spec_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}\cal{A}\setminus V(\cal{A}_{+}))/\mathbb{G}_{m}].

Given that we are not requiring
\cal{A}_{-}=0
, the choice to remove only the vanishing of
\cal{A}_{+}
may seem a bit arbitrary. The reason we have opted for this is that we would like to describe blowups as the
\Proj
of an extended Rees algebra, which has negative components.

Just as with usual
\Proj
, one can define the line bundles
\mathcal{O}(r)
corresponding to the shifted modules
\cal{A}(r)
.

We now recall the definition of a weighted blowup. Let
X
be a fixed algebraic stack.

Definition 2.2

(
[
19
, Definition 3.1.1]
)
.

A
Rees algebra
on
X
is a quasi-coherent graded finitely-generated subalgebra
\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{I}_{n}t^{n}
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}[t]
.

Note that the collection
\mathcal{I}_{\bullet}=\{\mathcal{I}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
defines a family of closed substacks
Z_{\bullet}=\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
. In
[
19
]
, this data is called a
weighted closed immersion
, we will use the term
weighted centre
: by a
weighted centre
we will understand to mean the weighted centre of some Rees algebra. Thus weighted centres correspond precisely to Rees algebras.

We will next describe the extension of a Rees algebra.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\cal{I}_{\bullet}
be a weighted centre. The
associated extended Rees algebra
is the graded
\mathcal{O}_{X}[t^{-1}]
-algebra that is the usual Rees algebra
\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{I}_{n}t^{n}
in non-negative degrees, but is
\mathcal{O}_{X}
in all negative degrees. It can be explicitly described as

\cal{A}:=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{I}_{n}t^{n}\oplus\bigoplus_{n<0}\mathcal{O}_{X}t^{n}.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{I}_{\bullet}
be a weighted centre with associated extended Rees algebra
\cal{A}
. The
weighted blowup
of
X
along
\mathcal{I}_{\bullet}
is defined to be
\Bl_{\mathcal{I}_{\bullet}}X:=\Proj_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\cal{A})
.

Example 2.5
.

Suppose
\mathcal{I}_{n}=\mathcal{I}_{1}^{n}
. Then
\Bl_{\mathcal{I}_{\bullet}}
is just the usual blowup of
X
along
V(\mathcal{I}_{1})
, by
[
19
, Remark 3.2.4]
.

Describing the weighted blowup in terms of the extended Rees algebra is advantageous for several reasons: the extended Rees algebra is often easier to describe in practice, and furthermore the exceptional divisor and deformation to the (weighted) normal cone become very easy to define:

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{I}_{\bullet}
be a weighted centre and let
\widetilde{X}
be its blowup. The
exceptional divisor
is the vanishing locus
E:=V(t^{-1})\subseteq\widetilde{X}
. This is an effective Cartier divisor with associated line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(-1)
.

The
weighted normal cone
is defined to be
\Spec_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{I}_{n}/\mathcal{I}_{n+1})
. Note that the exceptional divisor is isomorphic to the
\Proj
of the weighted normal cone. Finally, we define the
deformation to the weighted normal cone
to be
\Spec_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\cal{A}
.

Remark 2.7
.

Unlike with usual blowups, it is not in general true that the following commutative diagram is cartesian:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X,}

where
Z_{1}=V(\cal{I}_{1})
.

Next we discuss the various notions of regularity for a Rees algebra. Let us fix some notation: given
f_{1},...,f_{n}\in\mathcal{O}_{X}
and
d_{1},...,d_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, we write
\sum_{i=1}^{n}(f_{i},d_{i})
for the smallest weighted centre (ordered by degree-wise inclusion) which contains
f_{i}
in degree
d_{i}
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{I}_{\bullet}
be a weighted centre. We say it is
strictly (quasi,
H_{1}
, Koszul etc.)-regular
if
\mathcal{I}_{\bullet}
is of the form
\sum_{i=1}^{n}(f_{i},d_{i})
and
f_{1},...,f_{n}
is a (quasi,
H_{1}
, Koszul etc.)-regular sequence in
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
(see
[
20
,
Tag 062D
]
and
[
20
,
Tag 061M
]
). We say it is
(quasi,
H_{1}
, Koszul etc.)-regular
if smooth-locally at every point on
X
,
\cal{A}
is strictly (quasi,
H_{1}
, Koszul etc.)-regular.

It may not immediately clear why requiring the
f_{i}
to be an [insert adjective]-regular sequence is of utility, but the key is the following result:

Lemma 2.9
.

[
19
, Proposition 5.1.2]

Let
R
be a ring and let
A=\bigoplus I_{n}t^{n}=\sum(f_{i},d_{i})
be a Rees algebra. Then
A
is quasi-regular if and only if each
I_{n}/I_{n+1}
is a free
R/I_{1}
-module.

In particular, the well-known fact that the exceptional divisor of a (usual) blowup is a projective space bundle over the centre if and only if the centre is quasi-regular generalises. To state the generalisation precisely, we need the following terminology:

Definition 2.10

(
[
19
, Definition 2.1.3]
)
.

Let
X
be an algebraic stack. A
twisted weighted vector bundle
on
X
is an algebraic stack
E
smooth and affine over
X
with an action of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
(also over
X
) such that there exists a smooth cover
X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
, an
n\geq 0
and a Cartesian diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\times X^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

where
\mathbb{G}_{m}
acts linearly on
\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\times X^{\prime}
with certain weights
0<d_{0}\leq...\leq d_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
f
is
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant.

Just as vector bundles correspond to locally free sheaves of finite rank, twisted weighted vector bundles correspond to graded quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebras that smooth-locally are isomorphic (as graded algebras) to a polynomial algebra, where the variables have positive weights. The correspondence simply takes such an algebra
\cal{A}
to
\Spec_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\cal{A})
. By abuse of language, we shall also call such algebras twisted weighted vector bundles.

Definition 2.11
.

A
twisted weighted projective stack bundle
is the
\Proj
of a twisted weighted vector bundle.

Proposition 2.12

(
[
19
, Proposition 5.4.1]
)
.

Let
X
be a quasi-compact algebraic stack and let
\cal{A}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{I}_{n}t^{n}
be a Rees algebra. Write
Z_{n}
for the closed substack defined by
\mathcal{I}_{n}
. Then
\cal{A}
is quasi-regular if and only if the weighted normal cone of the blowup of
X
along
\cal{A}
is a twisted weighted vector bundle over
Z_{1}
. In particular, the exceptional divisor will also be a twisted weighted projective stack bundle over
Z_{1}
in this case. Moreover, if
\cal{A}
is strictly quasi-regular then the twisted weighted projective stack bundle over
Z_{1}
is a weighted projective stack (i.e. the
\Proj
of a graded polynomial ring where the variables have positive weight).

Let us conclude with some examples:

Example 2.13
.

Suppose
\mathcal{I}_{\bullet}=(f_{1},d_{1})+...+(f_{n},d_{n})
is a strictly
H_{1}
-regular weighted centre with associated extended Rees algebra
\cal{A}
. Then by
[
19
, Proposition 5.2.2]
, we have an isomorphism

\cal{A}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}[x_{1},...,x_{n},t^{-1}]/(f_{1}-x_{1}t^{-d_{1}},...,f_{n}-x_{n}t^{-d_{n}}).

Example 2.14
.

As a special case of the above example, note that if
X=\mathbb{A}^{n}_{S}=\Spec\mathcal{O}_{S}[x_{1},...,x_{n}]
over some base algebraic stack
S
and
\mathcal{I}_{\bullet}=\sum(x_{i},d_{i})
, then (forgetting the grading), the extended Rees algebra is the polynomial algebra one more variable:
\cal{A}=\mathcal{O}_{S}[u_{1},...,u_{n},t^{-1}]
. In particular, the deformation to the weighted normal cone is also an affine space.

2.2.
Triangulated Categories and Conservative Descent

We begin by recalling the notions of compactness and generation:

Definition 2.15

(
[
15
, Definition 1.7]
)
.

Let
\mathsf{T}
be a triangulated category. An object
\mathcal{F}
is
compact
if
\Hom(\mathcal{F},-)
commutes with coproducts.

Example 2.16
.

Let
X
be a quasi-compact and quasi-separated algebraic stack. If
X
is concentrated then the compact objects in
\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(X)
are exactly the perfect complexes (
[
12
, Lemma 4.4]
).

Definition 2.17

(
[
20
,
Tag 09SI
]
)
.

Let
\mathsf{T}
be a triangulated category and let
\mathcal{F}
be an object. We say
\mathcal{F}
is a
generator
, if
\Hom(\mathcal{F},\E[r])=0
for all
r\in\mathbb{Z}
implies
\E=0
. More generally, a set
\bf{S}
of objects in
\mathsf{T}
is a
generating set
if
\Hom(\mathcal{F},\E[r])=0
for all
\mathcal{F}\in\bf{S}
and all
r\in\mathbb{Z}
implies
\E=0
. We say
\mathsf{T}
is
compactly generated
if there is a generating set consisting of compact objects.

Example 2.18
.

Let
S
be an algebraic stack such that
\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(S)
is compactly generated, and fix
\{\E_{\alpha}\}
a set of compact generators. Let
\pi:B\mathbb{G}_{m,S}\rightarrow S
be the projection map and for each
r\in\mathbb{Z}
let
\chi_{r}
denote the corresponding
\mathbb{G}_{m}
character. Then
\{\pi^{*}\E_{\alpha}\otimes\chi_{r}\}
is a compact generating set for
\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(B\mathbb{G}_{m,S})
. This is easy to see, after noting that
\bigoplus\pi_{*}(\mathcal{F}\otimes\chi_{r})=0
implies
\mathcal{F}=0
, for any
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(B\mathbb{G}_{m,S})
.

Definition 2.19
.

Let
\mathsf{T}
be a triangulated category. Given an object
\mathcal{F}\in\mathsf{T}
, we write
\langle\mathcal{F}\rangle
for the smallest strictly full, saturated (i.e. closed under taking summands) triangulated subcategory of
\mathsf{T}
containing
\mathcal{F}
.

The following simple lemma will turn out useful:

Lemma 2.20

(
[
20
,
Tag 09SK
]
)
.

Given any two objects
\E,\mathcal{F}
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

\Hom(\mathcal{F},\E[r])=0
for all
r\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(ii)

\Hom(\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\E)=0
for all
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\in\langle\mathcal{F}\rangle
.

Let us now recall the notion of a semi-orthogonal decomposition:

Definition 2.21
.

Let
\mathsf{T}
be a triangulated category and
\mathsf{T}_{1},...,\mathsf{T}_{n}
a sequence of full triangulated subcategories. We say the sequence is
semi-orthogonal
if each
\mathsf{T}_{i}
is right-admissible (i.e. the inclusions admit a right adjoint) and for any
i<j
and objects
\mathcal{F}_{i}\in\mathsf{T}_{i}
and
\mathcal{F}_{j}\in\mathsf{T}_{j}
, we have
\Hom(\mathcal{F}_{j},\mathcal{F}_{i})=0
. Suppose now
\mathsf{T}_{1},...,\mathsf{T}_{n}
is semi-orthogonal. We say the sequence is
full
, or that it is a
semi-orthogonal decomposition
if the smallest strictly full triangulated subcategory containing the
\mathsf{T}_{i}
is
\mathsf{T}
. In this case, we write

\mathsf{T}=\langle\mathsf{T}_{1},...,\mathsf{T}_{n}\rangle.

The following result is our key to showing that a semi-orthogonal sequence is full:

Lemma 2.22
.

[
4
, Lemma 6.4]

Let
\mathsf{T}
be a triangulated category with a generator
\mathcal{F}
and let
\mathsf{T}_{1},...,\mathsf{T}_{r}
be a semi-orthogonal sequence. Then the sequence is full if and only if their triangulated hull contains
\mathcal{F}
.

We now recall the theory of conservative descent, as developed in
[
4
]
. This will allow us to reduce to the affine case. Recall that all functors are derived.

Definition 2.23

(
[
4
, Definition 3.3]
)
.

Let
S
be a base algebraic stack and let
X
and
Y
be algebraic stacks over
S
. A
Fourier-Mukai transform from
X
to
Y
over

is a tuple
(K,\cal{P},p,q)
, where we have the commutative diagram of algebraic stacks:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}

and
\cal{P}
is a perfect complex on

, such that the following hold:

(i)

The maps

are perfect (see
[

,

]
- which generalises easily to stacks), proper and concentrated (see
[

).

(ii)

The object

is perfect (where
q^{!}:\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(Y)\rightarrow\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(K)
is the right adjoint to
Rq_{*}
- which exists by
[

(iii)

Given any flat

with Cartesian square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q^{\prime}}

and

(q^{\prime})^{!}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\otimes L(q^{\prime})^{*}(-)\rightarrow(q^{\prime})^{!}(-)

are isomorphisms.

Remark 2.24
.

We should really be using
q^{\times}
instead of
q^{!}
. However, in all situations dealt with in the paper, the two functors coincide.

Given a Fourier-Mukai transform, we have an associated functor
\mathcal{F}\mapsto q_{*}(p^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes\cal{P})
which admits a right adjoint
\mathcal{G}\mapsto p_{*}(q^{!}\mathcal{G}\otimes\cal{P}^{\vee})
. Moreover,
given a flat morphism
S^{\prime}\rightarrow S
, there is an obvious notion of flat base-change.

Theorem 2.25

(
[
4
, Theorem 6.1]
)
.

Let
S
be a base algebraic stack. Let
X_{1},...,X_{n}
and
Y
be algebraic stacks over
S
and let
\Phi_{i}:\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(X_{i})\rightarrow\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(Y)
be (the associated functors of) Fourier-Mukai transforms over
S
. Let
S^{\prime}\rightarrow S
be a faithfully flat map and denote the base-changes by
X_{i}^{\prime},Y^{\prime},\Phi_{i}^{\prime}
etc.

(i)

If
\Phi_{i}^{\prime}
is fully faithful, then so is
\Phi_{i}
.

(ii)

Assuming all the
\Phi_{i}^{\prime}
are fully faithful, if
\im\Phi_{1}^{\prime},...,\im\Phi_{n}^{\prime}
is a semi-orthogonal sequence in
\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(Y^{\prime})
, then so is
\im\Phi_{1},...,\im\Phi_{n}
in
\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(Y)
.

(iii)

If
\im\Phi_{1}^{\prime},...,\im\Phi_{n}^{\prime}
is a semi-orthogonal decomposition of
\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(Y^{\prime})
, then
\im\Phi_{1},...,\im\Phi_{n}
is a semi-orthogonal decomposition of
\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(Y)
.

Remark 2.26
.

Note that our definition differs from
[
4
, Definition 3.3]
slightly, in that
[
4
, Definition 3.3]
further requires
Rp_{*}
and
Rq_{*}
preserve perfect and pseudo-coherent complexes. However, this requirement is used only to show that the induced functor preserves perfect and (locally bounded) pseudo-coherent complexes respectively. The advantage of this requirement is that Theorem
2.25
works with the categories of perfect and (locally bounded) pseudo-coherent complexes in place of
\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}
- however its absence does not affect the corresponding statement for
\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}
itself.

Since it is not so clear at the time of writing that our relevant Fourier-Mukai transforms preserve pseudo-coherent complexes in the non-noetherian setting (the main issue being the absence of a theory of
“approximation by perfect complexes”
), we have opted to remove this requirement, and instead we will manually deduce the corresponding statements for
\Perf
etc.

3.
Some cohomology computations

The goal of this section is to first prove the below result.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
X
be an algebraic stack and let
\pi:\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
be the weighted blowup of a Koszul-regular weighted centre. Then the pullback map
L\pi^{*}:\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(X)\rightarrow\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(\widetilde{X})
is fully faithful.

When
X
is a smooth variety and
\widetilde{X}
is the (usual) blowup along a smooth subvariety, the typical argument (e.g. in
[
13
, III Proposition 3.4]
) proceeds by using the theorem on formal functions to show
R^{i}\pi_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}})=0
for all
i>0
and appealing to the normality of
X
to establish the isomorphism of
\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow R^{0}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}
. When
X
is an arbitrary scheme and
\widetilde{X}
is the blowup of a Koszul-regular closed subscheme (in the sense of
[
20
,
Tag 063J
]
), this result is established in
[
6
, Lemme 3.5]
by a descending induction argument. We will give a different, arguably more intuitive proof.

We begin by recalling the following standard result:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
S
be an algebraic stack. Let
j:\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{S}\setminus\{0\}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{S}
denote the inclusion and
i:Z=0:=S\hookrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{S}
denote the complement. Denote also the coordinates of
\mathbb{A}^{n+1}
by
x_{0},...,x_{n}
. Then

R\Gamma_{Z}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{n+1}})=\frac{1}{x_{0}...x_{n}}\mathcal{O}_{S}[{x_{0}}^{-1},...,x_{n}^{-1}][-n-1].

Moreover, the map
R\Gamma_{Z}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{n+1}})\rightarrow R\Gamma(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{n+1}})
, considered as a map in
\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(S)
, is zero, and consequently

Rj_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{S}\setminus\{0\}})=\mathcal{O}_{S}[x_{0},...,x_{n}]\oplus\frac{1}{x_{0}...x_{n}}\mathcal{O}_{S}[{x_{0}}^{-1},...,x_{n}^{-1}][-n].

Proof.

For
S=\Spec\mathbb{Z}
it is well-known. For the general case, base-change along
S\rightarrow\Spec\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
S
be an algebraic stack and let

f:\mathscr{P}(d_{0},...,d_{n})=\Proj\mathcal{O}_{S}[x_{0},...,x_{n}]\rightarrow S

be the weighted projective stack over
S
with weights
1\leq d_{0}\leq...\leq d_{n}
. Then:

Rf_{*}(\mathcal{O}(r))=\mathcal{O}_{S}[x_{0},...,x_{n}]_{r}\oplus\left(\frac{1}{x_{0}...x_{n}}\mathcal{O}_{S}[{x_{0}}^{-1},...,x_{n}^{-1}]_{r}\right)[-n].

Proof.

This follows immediately from Lemma
3.2
and the fact that
B\mathbb{G}_{m}
is cohomologically affine.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
A
be a ring and let
f_{0},...,f_{n}
be a Koszul-regular sequence in
A
. Then for any tuple
(d_{0},...,d_{n})
of positive integers, the following sequence

f_{0}-x_{0}s^{d_{0}},...,f_{n}-x_{n}s^{d_{n}}

in the polynomial ring
A[s,x_{0},...,x_{n}]
is also Koszul-regular.

Proof.

By
[
20
,
Tag 0669
]
, it follows that
f_{0},...,f_{n},s
is a Koszul-regular sequence in
A[s,x_{0},...,x_{n}]
, and hence
f_{0}-x_{0}s^{d_{0}},...,f_{n}-x_{n}s^{d_{n}},s
is also Koszul-regular, since it generates the same ideal. Let us write

K_{\bullet}:=K_{\bullet}(f_{0}-x_{0}s^{d_{0}},...,f_{n}-x_{n}s^{d_{n}};A[s,x_{0},...,x_{n}])

and

K_{\bullet}^{\prime}:=K_{\bullet}(f_{0}-x_{0}s^{d_{0}},...,f_{n}-x_{n}s^{d_{n}},s;A[s,x_{0},...,x_{n}]).

We have a distinguished triangle in
\mathrm{D}(A[s,x_{0},...,x_{n}])
of Koszul complexes:

K_{\bullet}\xrightarrow{\times s}K_{\bullet}\rightarrow K_{\bullet}^{\prime}\rightarrow K_{\bullet}[1],

and by assumption,
H_{r}(K^{\prime}_{\bullet})=0
for all
r>0
. In particular, multiplication by
s
induces an isomorphism on
H_{r}(K_{\bullet})
for all
r>0
.

Now note that if we invert
s
, then the change-of-variables
x_{i}\mapsto f_{i}-x_{i}s^{d_{i}}
becomes invertible, and hence there is a ring automorphism of
A[s^{\pm 1},x_{0},...,x_{n}]
taking
f_{0}-x_{0}s^{d_{0}},...,f_{n}-x_{n}s^{d_{n}}
to the Koszul-regular sequence
x_{0},...,x_{n}
. Thus for any
r>0
we have

H_{r}(K_{\bullet})\otimes A[s^{\pm 1},x_{0},...,x_{n}]=H_{r}(K_{\bullet}\otimes A[s^{\pm 1},x_{0},...,x_{n}])=0,

and in particular every element of
H_{r}(K_{\bullet})
is annihilated by some power of
s
. But as established, multiplication by
s
is an isomorphism on
H_{r}(K_{\bullet})
, and hence we must have
H_{r}(K_{\bullet})=0
as desired.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
R
be a ring and let
(f_{0},d_{0})+...+(f_{n},d_{n})
be a strictly Koszul-regular weighted centre, with extended Rees algebra
\cal{A}=R[t^{-1},x_{0},...,x_{n}]/(f_{i}-x_{i}t^{-d_{i}})
. Letting
Z=V(x_{0},...,x_{n})\subseteq\Spec\cal{A}
, we have

R\Gamma_{Z}(\mathcal{O}_{\Spec\cal{A}})=\frac{1}{x_{0}...x_{n}}R[t^{-1},x_{0}^{-1},...,x_{n}^{-1}]/(f_{i}-x_{i}t^{-d_{i}})[-n-1].

Proof.

By
[
20
,
Tag 0ALZ
]
, and Lemma
3.2
, we have

R\Gamma_{Z}(\mathcal{O}_{\Spec\cal{A}})=\frac{1}{x_{0}...x_{n}}R[t^{-1},x_{0}^{-1},...,x_{n}^{-1}][-n-1]\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{R[t^{-1},x_{0},...,x_{n}]}R[t^{-1},x_{0},...,x_{n}]/(f_{i}-x_{i}t^{-d_{i}}).

Thus it suffices to show that
f_{0}-x_{0}t^{-d_{0}},...,f_{n}-x_{n}t^{-d_{n}}
is a Koszul-regular sequence for the
R[t^{-1},x_{0},...,x_{n}]
-module
\frac{1}{x_{0}...x_{n}}R[t^{-1},x_{0}^{-1},...,x_{n}^{-1}]
.

Set
M:=\frac{1}{x_{0}...x_{n}}R[t^{-1},x_{0}^{-1},...,x_{n}^{-1}]
and let us consider the Koszul complex

K_{\bullet}=K_{\bullet}(f_{0}-x_{0}t^{-d_{0}},...f_{n}-x_{n}t^{-d_{n}};M).

We define a filtration on
K_{\bullet}
: for
p\in\mathbb{Z}
, we let
F^{p}K_{\bullet}
consist of the subcomplex of
R[t^{-1}]
-modules spanned by the monomials
x_{I}^{a_{I}}
, where
|a_{I}|\geq p
. So
F^{p}K_{\bullet}=0
for all
p\geq-n
. It is clear the filtrations respect the differentials, making
K_{\bullet}
a filtered complex.

Now let us consider the associated spectral sequence, which has
E_{0}
-page
E_{0}^{p,q}=\operatorname{gr}^{p}K_{-p-q}
. By construction, the
\operatorname{gr}^{p}K_{-p-q}
are free
R[t^{-1}]
-modules, and moreover, since the
x_{i}
are killed, each
E_{0}^{p,\bullet}[-p]
is simply the Koszul complex of the
R[t^{-1}]
-Koszul-regular sequence (by Lemma
3.4
)
f_{0}-x_{0}t^{-d_{0}},...,f_{n}-x_{n}t^{-d_{n}}
tensored with the free module spanned by the monomials
x_{I}^{a_{I}}
with
|a_{I}|=p
. Thus
E_{1}^{p,q}=0
unless
q=-p
, and hence the spectral sequence degenerates here. Since our filtration is bounded above and exhaustive the spectral sequence converges, which concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
R
be a ring and let
\cal{A}=(f_{0},d_{0})+...+(f_{n},d_{n})
be a strictly Koszul-regular weighted centre with extension
\cal{A}
. Let
f:\widetilde{X}=\Proj\cal{A}\rightarrow X=\Spec R
denoted the weighted blowup. Then

R\Gamma(\widetilde{X},\mathcal{O}(r))=\cal{A}_{r}\oplus\left(\frac{1}{x_{0}...x_{n}}R[t^{-1},x_{0}^{-1},...,x_{n}^{-1}]/(f_{i}-x_{i}t^{-d_{i}})_{r}\right)[-n].

Proof.

Direct from Lemma
3.5
, Lemma
3.2
and that
B\mathbb{G}_{m}
is cohomologically affine.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

By the projection formula, it suffices to show

. The question is local, so we may assume

∎

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(S)
is compactly generated, and fix

. Here
\mathcal{F}(r)
refers to
\mathcal{F}
, twisted by the line bundle
\mathcal{O}(r)

Proof.

Let us factor
\pi
as below:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\mathbb{G}_{m}\times S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

Letting

and supposing

for each

, by adjunction we have

Thus by Example

. But

is quasi-affine, thus
\mathcal{F}=0
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
R
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded ring and let
x_{0},...,x_{n}
be a Koszul-regular sequence of positively graded homogeneous elements, where
x_{i}
has degree
d_{i}>0
. Let
X=[(\Spec R\setminus V(x_{0},...,x_{n}))/\mathbb{G}_{m}]
. If
R\Hom(\mathcal{O}(d),\mathcal{F})=0
for all
1-\sum d_{i}\leq d\leq 0
, then
\mathcal{F}=0
, In other words, the object
\mathcal{O}(1-\sum d_{i})\oplus...\oplus\mathcal{O}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}
is a generator for
\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(X)
. In particular,
\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(X)
is generated by a single perfect complex.

Proof.

Observe we have the following exact sequence:

(1)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(-\sum d_{i})=\wedge^{n+1}(\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}\mathcal{O}(-d_{i}))\rightarrow...\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}\mathcal{O}(-d_{i})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}\rightarrow 0.

Indeed, this is just the (appropriately graded) Koszul resolution associated to the Koszul-regular sequence
x_{0},...,x_{n}
, whose vanishing locus is removed by assumption, hence the sequence is exact. In particular, this tells us
\mathcal{O}
is quasi-isomorphic to a complex consisting of direct sums of sheaves of the form
\mathcal{O}(-r)
for
-\sum d_{i}\leq-r\leq-1
and so
\mathcal{O}(1)
is quasi-isomorphic to a complex consisting of direct sums of sheaves of the form
\mathcal{O}(-r)
for
1-\sum d_{i}\leq-r\leq 0
(by twisting (
1
)). Applying this inductively, this means every
\mathcal{O}(s)
can be obtained by a finite number of extensions of the
\mathcal{O}(1-\sum d_{i}),...,\mathcal{O}(-1),\mathcal{O}
, or in other words
\mathcal{O}(s)\in\langle\mathcal{O}(1-\sum d_{i})\oplus...\oplus\mathcal{O}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}\rangle
for all

. We win, by Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
2.20
.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
X=\Spec R
be an affine scheme and let

\pi:\mathscr{P}=\Proj R[x_{0},...,x_{n}]\rightarrow X

be the weighted projective stack over
R
with weights
1\leq d_{0}\leq...\leq d_{n}
. Then there is a semiorthogonal decomposition

\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(\mathscr{P})=\langle\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{P}}(1-\sum d_{i})\otimes\pi^{*}\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(X),...,\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{P}}(-1)\otimes\pi^{*}\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(X),\pi^{*}\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(X)\rangle,

where
\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{P}}(r)\otimes\pi^{*}\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(X)
is the full triangulated subcategory consisting of objects of the form
\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{P}}(r)\otimes\pi^{*}(\mathcal{F})
where
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(X)
.

Proof.

First note that, by the projection formula and Lemma
3.3
(ii), the functor
\pi^{*}:\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(\Spec R)\rightarrow\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(\mathscr{P})
(and hence
\pi^{*}(-)\otimes\mathcal{O}(r)
, since twisting is an equivalence.) is fully faithful. Moreover each
\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{P}}(-r)\otimes\pi^{*}\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(X)
is right admissible (i.e. the inclusion has a right adjoint); indeed, the functor

\mathcal{F}\mapsto\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{P}}(-r)\otimes\pi^{*}(R\Gamma(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{P}}(r)))

is the right adjoint.

Now we show that this is indeed a semi-orthogonal decomposition. Indeed, if
1-\sum d_{r}\leq-i<-j\leq 0
it follows

\displaystyle R\Hom(\pi^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}(-j),\pi^{*}\mathcal{G}\otimes\mathcal{O}(-i))

\displaystyle=R\Hom(\pi^{*}\mathcal{F},\pi^{*}\mathcal{G}\otimes\mathcal{O}(-i+j))

\displaystyle=R\Hom(\mathcal{F},R\Gamma(\mathcal{O}(-i+j))\otimes\mathcal{G})

by the projection formula. Since
R\Gamma(\mathcal{O}(-i+j))=0
by Lemma
3.3
, it follows that

R\Hom(\pi^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}(-j),\pi^{*}\mathcal{G}\otimes\mathcal{O}(-i))=0.

In particular the sequence is semi-orthogonal. The fact that it is full now follows directly from Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
2.22
.
∎

Remark 4.4

),
Rf_{*}
admits a right adjoint
f^{!}
. Set
\mathcal{F}=f^{!}(t)
. Then by Yoneda’s lemma, we have a map
\E\rightarrow\mathcal{F}
, which we complete to a triangle

\E\rightarrow\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\cal{G}\rightarrow\E[1].

Applying the cohomological functor
\Hom(\mathcal{L},-)
tells us
\Hom(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{G})=0
for all
\mathcal{L}\in\Omega
, whence
\mathcal{G}=0
.
∎

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
R
be a ring and let
(f_{0},d_{0})+...+(f_{n},d_{n})=:I_{\bullet}
be a strictly Koszul-regular weighted centre, with extended Rees algebra
\cal{A}=R[t^{-1},x_{0},...,x_{n}]/(f_{i}-x_{i}t^{-d_{i}}
. Let
f:\widetilde{X}=\Proj\cal{A}\rightarrow X=\Spec R
be the weighted blowup. Let
f^{!}:\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(X)\rightarrow\mathrm{D}_{\mathsf{qc}}(\widetilde{X})
denote the right adjoint of
Rf_{*}
(which exists by Brown representability and the observation that
\widetilde{X}
is clearly concentrated). Then the trace map induces an isomorphism

f^{!}(-)\xrightarrow{\sim}f^{*}(-)\otimes\mathcal{O}(1-\sum d_{i})

of functors.

Proof.

Lemma
4.1
and Corollary
3.6
in combination with
[
15
, Theorem 5.1]
tell us that
f^{!}
preserves coproducts, and thus by
[
15
, Theorem 5.4]
(it is easy to check the proof applies in this situation) we know
f^{!}
is of the form
f^{!}=f^{*}\otimes\omega
.

The trace map induces a morphism of functors

\eta:R\Hom_{\widetilde{X}}(-,\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(1-\sum d_{i}))\rightarrow R\Hom_{R}(R\Gamma(\widetilde{X},-),R).

By Lemmas
4.1
and
4.8
, it suffices to show that verify that the maps
\eta(\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(r))
are isomorphisms. For
r=1-\sum d_{i}
this follows from Corollary
3.6
, and letting
j:E\hookrightarrow\widetilde{X}
denote the inclusion of the exceptional divisor, using the triangle

\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(r+1)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(r)\rightarrow j_{*}\mathcal{O}_{E}(r)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(r+1)[1]

and the five-lemma we reduce to checking that
\eta(\mathcal{O}_{E}(r))
is an isomorphism for all
r
.

By
[
12
, Theorem 4.14]
,
j^{!}
also preserves coproducts, and we can calculate

j^{!}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}=R\Hom(j_{*}\mathcal{O}_{E},\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}})=[\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(-1)]=\mathcal{O}_{E}(-1)[-1]

(where the final isomorphism should be thought of as an isomorphism of dg-rings). The counit map

j_{*}j^{!}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}=j_{*}\mathcal{O}_{E}(-1)[-1]\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\widetilde{X}}}

is simply the map induced by the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(1)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}\rightarrow j_{*}\mathcal{O}_{E}\rightarrow 0.

Thus we have induced maps

R\Hom_{E}(\mathcal{O}_{E}(r),\mathcal{O}(-\sum d_{i})[-1])\rightarrow R\Hom(R\Gamma(E,\mathcal{O}_{E}(r)),R).

Using Lemma
3.3
and exhaustive checking (noting that
R\Gamma(E,\mathcal{O}_{E}(r))
is a split complex of free
R/f_{0},...,f_{n}
-modules), we may verify they are isomorphisms, which concludes the proof.

∎

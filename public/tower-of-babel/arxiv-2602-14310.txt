Robust Filtering of Lévy-driven Stochastic Models Thanks: This research was supported by the Office of Naval Research (ONR) through grant no. FA9550-24-1-0210.
Sharan Srinivasan, Vijay Gupta, Harsha Honnappa

Abstract
. We study robust nonlinear filtering for stochastic models driven by Lévy processes, where the signal and observation processes are coupled through common Brownian and jump noise. Robustness, defined as the continuous dependence of the filter on the observation path, is essential whenever the observation process deviates from the idealized model, for instance when a path must be reconstructed from discrete-time samples. This question is well understood for continuous semimartingale systems but largely open in the presence of jumps. We construct a version of the filter and establish its continuity in two regimes. For processes with finitely many jumps on compact intervals, we prove continuity in both the rough p p -variation and p p -variation topologies on cadlag path space, without requiring a separability condition on the jump coefficients. For processes with infinitely many jumps, we prove continuity in a modified rough p p -variation topology adapted to cadlag geometric rough paths, under an additional separability assumption. In both cases, our approach relies on Stratonovich and Marcus flow decompositions rather than the Itô-based methods of recent work. The resulting geometric rough-path lifts yield pathwise convergence guarantees and can be constructed directly from discrete observations without knowledge of the underlying probability law.

\displaystyle dX_{t}

Given this setup, the problem of
stochastic filtering
(see, e.g.
[
3
,
9
,
17
]
) seeks to obtain the conditional law of the signal
X_{t}
given the filtration of the observation
\mathcal{F}^{Y}_{t}
up to some time
t
. Given a measurable test function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d_{X}+d_{Y}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},
this is done by computing the conditional expectation (also called
the filter
):

\pi_{t}(f):=\mathbb{E}[f(X_{t},Y_{t})|\mathcal{F}^{Y}_{t}].

Perhaps the most common approach
[
22
,
23
]
is to derive the SDEs (the so-called Kushner-Stratanovich equation) satisfied by the filter. The problem that we are interested in is the so-called
robust filtering problem
, where we would like to show that the filter admits a unique version (which we call
\theta^{f}_{t}(Y_{.})
) which is a continuous map under a suitably defined topology from the space of paths to the reals.

The robustness of the nonlinear stochastic filter arises as a problem in settings where ‘real world’ observation process differs from our hypothesized observation model. For instance, suppose that we observe a data stream only at discrete time points and cannot access the entire observation path. There are now many ways of turning these points into a path; for example by linear interpolation or rectangular interpolation. Nonetheless, we expect the filter predictions computed using these two paths to be close to each other (under some suitable topology).

That the filter obtained through the Kushner-Stratanovich equation is typically not unique has long been known. Clark in
[
7
]
pointed out that it would be natural for the filter
\pi_{t}(f)
to be continuous in the sample paths of the observation process, or that in other words, it makes sense to impose a constraint that there exists a unique version
\theta_{t}^{f}(Y)
of
\pi_{t}(f),
which is a continuous map. Deriving a filter such that the filter is continuous with respect to the observation
Y
in this manner has been termed the
robust filtering problem
. This property is obviously of both theoretical and practical importance. Thus, the robust filtering problem has been well-worked on in the literature dating back at least to the work of Clark
[
7
]
. A significant advance was made by Clark and Crisan in
[
6
]
, which constructed a version
\theta_{t}^{f}(Y)
for a signal-observation model where the two processes are driven by independent Brownian noise processes. The main technical idea is to use the integration by parts formula to prove both the robustness of the filter and the fact that
\theta_{t}^{f}(Y)
is a version of
\pi_{t}(f)
.

A natural generalization to the model used in
[
6
]
is to include common noise (in other words, to consider the case when the signal-observation processes are driven by the same Brownian motion) in the model. However, the inclusion of common noise poses additional technical challenges. In
[
10
]
, Davis assumed that the model is a diffusion on a manifold and that the common noise and observation are one dimensional processes. Under these conditions, he used stochastic flow theory and flow decomposition introduced by Kunita in
[
18
]
to prove the continuity of the filter. Note that Kunita’s results are a bit more general; specifically,
[
18
, Theorem 6.1]
states that if one has the SDE

d\xi_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}(\xi_{t})\circ dM^{i}_{t},

where
M_{t}
is a continuous semi-martingale and
V_{i}
’s are vector fields that form a solvable Lie algebra, then the solution is continuous in sample paths of
M_{t}
. This more general formulation was considered by Elliot and Kohlmann in
[
11
]
.

However, the requirement that the vector fields form a solvable Lie algebra is quite restrictive. Crisan
[
8
]
relaxed this requirement by introducing rough paths to this problem. They lifted the observation process to a (geometric) rough path and then used the same flow idea as Davis to construct rough flows. Using a classic result in rough path theory about the continuity of rough flows in the rough topology, the continuity of the filter follows. This result in rough path theory does not require any conditions on the driving vector field, other than it having at most linear growth and being Lipschitz, which are both natural assumptions for SDEs to have unique strong solutions.

We seek to solve the robust filtering problem in the signal-observation system (
1
) wherein the driving noise includes jump processes. The nonlinear stochastic filtering problem for such noise processes has been solved quite recently by Qiao in
[
22
,
23
]
. Qiao considers a signal-observation model with jumps driven by integer valued random measures, inspired by Lévy processes and the Itô-Lévy decomposition and first introduced in
[
2
]
, where they are called Lévy type SDEs. However, the main result in these works is the derivation of the SDEs that the filter satisfies and robustness of the filter is not discussed.

In this paper, we divide the robust filtering problem into two cases: (i) when there are finitely many jumps on any given time interval, and (ii) when there are infinitely many jumps on any given time interval. In either case, we provide continuity estimates of the version of the filter albeit in different topologies. Specifically, for (i), we show continuity in the rough
p
-variation topology and in the
p
-variation topology on the space of cadlag paths. For (ii), we show continuity in a slight modification of the rough
p
-variation topology (see Section
5
).

The paper closest to ours is the contemporaneous one of Allan et al
[
1
]
, who constructed a version of the filter in
[
22
,
23
]
using (non-geometric) rough paths through the development of new results on discontinuous RDEs and Rough Stochastic Differential Equations (RSDEs). We also consider a similar model in this paper, but use the method of flow decomposition to show robustness. Specifically, we utilize flows

(2)

\begin{split}\phi^{\text{Strat}}(t,x)&:=x+\int_{0}^{t}V(\phi^{\text{Strat}}(s,x))\circ dM_{t},\\
\phi^{\text{Marcus}}(t,x)&:=x+\int_{0}^{t}V(\phi^{\text{Marcus}}(s,x))\diamond dM_{t},\end{split}

(where
M_{t}
is a semi-martingale, and
V
a vector field) generated by Stratonovich and Marcus integrals that naturally satisfy a Wong-Zakai type theorem
[
19
, Theorem 6.5]
. This provides many advantages.

First,
[
1
]
utilizes Itô integrals and hence require the knowledge of the distribution of the constituent random variables since the integral only converges in probability. On the other hand, the use of Stratonovich and Marcus integrals allows us to offer pathwise convergence guarantees. Further, the flows generated by Stratonovich and Marcus integrals are diffeomorphisms on the space we are interested in (see
[
19
, Proposition 4.3]
). Therefore, the methods we have used can be generalized to manifolds easily, which is important to be able to consider constrained systems of practical importance in engineering. On the other hand, Itô integrals are cumbersome to use on manifolds as they do not satisfy the chain rule.

Second, while the model of
[
1
]
covers cases with both finite and infinite jumps, in either of these cases,
[
1
, Assumption 5.12]
is required to split up functions
f(t,X_{t},Y_{t},x)=\sum_{i}h^{i}(t,X_{t},Y_{t})g^{i}(t,x)
to separate the jump sizes from the state-observation process pair. While the Weierstrass theorem guarantees that such functions are dense in the set of all functions of the form
\alpha(t,X_{t},Y_{t},x)
, there are no guarantees that the limit function will lead to a robust version of the filter. Nonetheless, the assumption is key to forming a path from the stochastic integral
\int_{0}^{t}\int_{|x|<1}f(t,X_{t},Y_{t},x)\tilde{N}(dt,dx)
, in order to lift it to a rough path and establish continuity under the rough topology. In contrast, we are able to avoid making this assumption when there are finitely many jumps (see Section
4
for more detail). However, we would like to point out that even in our work, the splitting condition is still required when we consider the infinite jump case for the same reasons as in
[
1
]
.

Finally, note that the Stratonovich and Marcus lifts of semimartingales form geometric rough paths
[
14
]
that satisfy both the shuffle product identity and Chen’s relation, implying that non-linear functionals of the path can be expressed as linear combinations of the signatures of the path
[
5
]
. This potentially offers considerable advantage in numerically computing the filter. The Itô lift, on the other hand, leads to a non-geometric rough path. While there is an analog to Chen’s relation for branched rough paths, the combinatorial structure is rather more complicated than in the geometric setting (see
[
16
, Theorem 4.1]
).

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the notation and present some definitions. In Section
2.3
, we present the robust stochastic filtering problem in a general form. In Section
3
, following Davis
[
10
]
for the case of a continuous semimartingale, we consider a one-dimensional observation model (in our jump-diffusion type setting) and demonstrate how flows can be used to show robustness of the filter. In Section
4
, we revert to the the multidimensional setting and assume that the signal and observation have finitely many jumps in any compact time interval and use continuous rough flows to show robustness. Finally, in Section
5
, we allow the signal and observation processes to have countably infinite jumps on a compact interval (with an additional assumption of separability of the coefficients akin to
[
1
, Assumption 5.12]
).

2.3.
Robustness of the Filter

As observed in the introduction, given the signal-observation system (
1
), the problem of
stochastic filtering
(see, e.g.
[
3
,
9
,
17
]
) is to compute the conditional law of the signal
X_{t}
given the filtration of the observation
\mathcal{F}^{Y}_{t}
up to some time

. To be precise, given a measurable test function

the

\pi_{t}(f)
is defined as the conditional expectation,

\pi_{t}(f):=\mathbb{E}[f(X_{t},Y_{t})|\mathcal{F}^{Y}_{t}].

Under
2.10

2.11
,
2.12
,
2.13

(18)

\pi_{t}(f)=\frac{\mathbb{\tilde{E}}[f(X_{t},Y_{t})\Lambda_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}]}{\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[\Lambda_{t}|\mathcal{F}^{Y}_{t}]}=:\frac{p_{t}(f)}{p_{t}(1)}.

In the following sections, we construct a version of the filter
\pi_{t}(f)
which is continuous with respect to the observation process
Y
under suitable topologies, the latter depending on the whether the common jump noise processes in (

. The contours of our overall strategy for constructing a robust filter are to show that there exists a continuous version of the function

, which we achieve by exploiting a measure change using Girsanov’s theorem. Define a new reference measure
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}\ll\mathbb{P}
using the Radon-Nikodym derivative:

(19)

\left.\frac{d\tilde{\mathbb{P}}}{d\mathbb{P}}\right|_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}:=\Lambda_{T}^{-1},

where the process
\Lambda_{t}:=\exp(I_{t})
, and

I_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}h(s,X_{s},Y_{s})^{T}dW_{s}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|h(s,X_{s},Y_{s})|^{2}ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{U}_{\lambda}}\log(\lambda(s,X_{s-},x))\tilde{N}_{\lambda}(ds,dx)\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{U}_{\lambda}}(1-\lambda(s,X_{s-},x)+\lambda(s,X_{s-},x)\log(\lambda(s,X_{s-},x))\nu_{2}(dx)ds,

for
t\in[0,t]
and the function

is given in (

). Note that in the infinite jump case, when
\lambda
is identically 1, the last two terms in

vanish and the transform is valid.

Under the new measure
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}
, the signal-observation system (

(20)

\displaystyle dX_{t}

\displaystyle=\tilde{b}_{1}(t,X_{t},Y_{t})dt+\sigma_{0}(t,X_{t},Y_{t})\circ dB_{t}+\sigma_{1}(t,X_{t},Y_{t})\circ d\tilde{W}_{t}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{U}_{p}}f_{1}(t,X_{t-},Y_{t-},x)\tilde{N}_{p}(dt,dx)+\int_{\mathbb{U}_{\lambda}}f_{3}(t,X_{t-},Y_{t-},x)\tilde{N}(dt,dx),

\displaystyle dY_{t}

\displaystyle:=\sigma_{2}(t,Y_{t})\circ d\tilde{W}_{t}+\int_{\mathbb{U}_{\lambda}}f_{2}(t,Y_{t-},x)\tilde{N}(dt,dx).

4.
Robust Filtering with Finite Jumps

Coming to our primary contributions, we consider the signal-observation process
(X_{t},Y_{t})
on
\mathbb{R}^{d_{X}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{Y}}
:

\displaystyle dX_{t}

\displaystyle=b_{1}(t,X_{t},Y_{t})dt+\sigma_{0}(t,X_{t},Y_{t})\circ dB_{t}+{\sigma}_{1}(t,X_{t},Y_{t})\circ dW_{t}

(43)

\displaystyle+\int_{|x|<1}f_{1}(t,X_{t-},Y_{t},x)\tilde{N}_{p}(dt,dx)+\int_{|x|<1}f_{3}(t,X_{t},Y_{t},x)\tilde{N}_{\lambda}(dt,dx),

(44)

\displaystyle dY_{t}

\displaystyle={b}_{2}(t,X_{t},Y_{t})dt+{\sigma}_{2}(t,Y_{t})\circ dW_{t}+\int_{|x|<1}f_{2}(t,Y_{t},x)\tilde{N}_{\lambda}(dt,dx).

In this section, the rough-path enhancement is required only for the continuous semimartingale component (the Brownian/observation-driven part), for which the geometric lift is naturally defined via the Stratonovich integral. The jump terms in (42)–(43) are treated in the classical Itô–Lévy sense via integrals against the (compensated) Poisson random measures and, under the standing assumption of finitely many jumps on compact time intervals, do not require a cadlag geometric rough-path lift of the jump driver. We assume that the SDEs have unique strong solutions (see
[
23
]
) and that

are mutually independent. The notation parallels that in the previous section. Since we now consider common jumps and multi-dimensional systems, we can no longer use the stochastic flow approach from the previous section, and instead we will use rough flows to establish the robustness result in considerable generality.

As before, we perform a Girsanov transform to change
N_{\lambda}
to a unit intensity point process and eliminate the drift term in the observation process. To do this define

(45)

\Lambda_{t}^{-1}=\exp\left\{-\int_{0}^{t}h(s,X_{s},Y_{s})\circ dW_{s}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|h(s,X_{s},Y_{s})|^{2}ds\right.\\
\left.-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{|x|<1}\log\lambda(s,X_{s-},x)N_{\lambda}(dt,dx)-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{|x|<1}(\lambda(s,X_{s-},x)-1)ds\nu_{2}(dx)\right\},

where
h(t,X_{t},Y_{t})=\sigma^{-1}_{2}(t,Y_{t})b_{2}(t,X_{t},Y_{t})
.

\mathbb{\tilde{P}}
via

\frac{d\mathbb{\tilde{P}}}{d\mathbb{P}}=\Lambda_{t}^{-1}.

Under
\mathbb{\tilde{P}}

(46)

\tilde{W}_{t}=W_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}h(s,X_{s},Y_{s})ds

(47)

\tilde{N}(dt,dx)=N_{\lambda}(dt,dx)-dt\nu_{2}(dx)

is a compensated Poisson random measure, and the signal-observation processes become

\displaystyle dX_{t}

\displaystyle=b_{3}(t,X_{t},Y_{t})dt+\sigma_{0}(t,X_{t},Y_{t})\circ dB_{t}+\sigma_{1}(t,X_{t},Y_{t})\circ d\tilde{W}_{t}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{|x|<1}f_{1}(t,X_{t-},Y_{t},x)\tilde{N}_{p}(dt,dx)+\int_{|x|<1}f_{3}(t,X_{t},Y_{t},x)\tilde{N}(dt,dx),

\displaystyle dY_{t}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{2}(t,Y_{t})\circ d\tilde{W}_{t}+\int_{|x|<1}f_{2}(t,Y_{t},x)\tilde{N}(dt,dx).

where

b_{3}(t,X_{t},Y_{t})=b_{1}(t,X_{t},Y_{t})-\sigma_{1}(t,X_{t},Y_{t})h(t,X_{t},Y_{t}).

(50)

\pi_{t}(f):=\mathbb{E}[f(X_{t},Y_{t})|\mathcal{Y}_{t}]=\frac{\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[f(X_{t},Y_{t})\Lambda_{t}|\mathcal{Y}_{t}]}{\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[\Lambda_{t}|\mathcal{Y}_{t}]}.

(51)

with respect to the sample paths of the observation process

Remark
.

Note that we do not need an assumption (needed in
[
1
]
and

) that the functions

and

can be split up as the products:

\displaystyle f_{2}(t,y,u)

\displaystyle=m_{2}(t,u)n_{2}(t,y),

\displaystyle f_{3}(t,x,y,u)

\displaystyle=m_{3}(t,u)n_{2}(t,x,y).

5.

Generalized shot noise models can also have countably infinite number of jumps in a finite time interval. Consider a shot noise model of the form

6.
Conclusions and Future Work

We have established the robustness of nonlinear stochastic filters for signal-observation models driven by Lévy-type noise. By exploiting Stratonovich and Marcus formulations and the associated flow decompositions, we constructed versions of the filter that depend continuously on the observation path in two regimes: finite jump activity (Section
4
) and infinite jump activity (Section

p
-variation topologies on cadlag path space, without requiring the separability assumption on the jump coefficients imposed in
[
1
]
. In the infinite-activity case, we proved continuity in a modified rough

p
-variation topology under an analogous separability condition.

A central feature of our approach is that the Stratonovich and Marcus lifts yield geometric rough paths, which brings several structural advantages over the Itô-based framework of
[
1
]
. First, the pathwise nature of the Stratonovich and Marcus integrals provides convergence guarantees that do not depend on knowledge of the underlying probability law, whereas Itô integrals converge only in probability. Second, the resulting flows are diffeomorphisms, making our framework readily extensible to signal-observation models on manifolds, a setting of considerable importance in constrained engineering systems where Itô calculus is awkward to apply. Third, the geometric rough-path signatures satisfy the shuffle product identity and Chen’s relation, opening a path toward efficient numerical computation of the filter via signature methods.

Several directions remain open. The separability condition on the jump coefficients
f_{2}

and
f_{3}
is still required in the infinite-activity regime (Section
5
), and relaxing or removing it is an important challenge. A related question is whether the Girsanov transform can be extended to handle state-dependent compensators in the infinite-activity setting, which would bring the model in Section
5
closer to the generality of Section
4
. On the computational side, exploiting the signature structure of the geometric lift to develop practical filtering algorithms, particularly for high-dimensional or real-time applications, is a natural next step. Finally, extending the robustness results to signal-observation models evolving on Riemannian manifolds, taking full advantage of the diffeomorphic flow structure established here, is a promising direction for future work.

(\sup_{r\leq t}|Y^{1}_{r}(\cdot,\bar{\omega})-Y^{2}_{r}(\cdot,\bar{\omega})|^{2})^{1/2}=\sup_{r\leq t}|Y^{1}_{r}(\cdot,\bar{\omega})-Y^{2}_{r}(\cdot,\bar{\omega})|
. Therefore, it follows that

\displaystyle\left\|Y^{1}_{t}(\cdot,\bar{\omega})-Y^{2}_{t}(\cdot,\bar{\omega})\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\left\|\sup_{r\leq t}|Y^{1}_{r}(\cdot,\bar{\omega})-Y^{2}_{r}(\cdot,\bar{\omega})\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C_{\max}\left\|\sup_{r\leq t}|Y^{1}_{r}(\cdot,\bar{\omega})-Y^{2}_{r}(\cdot,\bar{\omega})\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\left(t+t\int_{U_{1}}\alpha_{1}(x)^{2}\nu_{1}(dx)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad\qquad+C_{f_{2}}\left\|\sup_{r\leq t}|Y^{1}_{s}(\cdot,\bar{\omega})-Y^{2}_{s}(\cdot,\bar{\omega})|\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\qquad\times\sup_{r\leq t}\left(\int_{0}^{r}\int_{U_{1}}\alpha_{2}(x)\tilde{N}_{q}^{2}(ds,dx)(\bar{\omega})+|\tilde{f}_{2}|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{r}\left|\int_{U_{1}}(\tilde{N}_{q}^{1}(ds,dx)(\bar{\omega})-\tilde{N}^{2}_{q}(ds,dx)(\bar{\omega}))\right|\right).

We now want to show that for
\mathbb{\bar{P}}
almost every
\bar{\omega}
,
\int_{0}^{t}\int_{U_{1}}\alpha_{2}(x)\tilde{N}_{q}^{2}(ds,dx)(\bar{\omega})<\infty
, but this is true since we are in the finite jumps setting. Thus, there exists some
M(\bar{\omega})
such that
\int_{0}^{t}\int_{U_{1}}\alpha_{2}(x)\tilde{N}_{q}^{2}(ds,dx)(\bar{\omega})<M(\bar{\omega})<\infty

\bar{\mathbb{P}}
almost every
\bar{\omega}
, and we have

\displaystyle\left\|Y^{1}_{t}(\cdot,\bar{\omega})-Y^{2}_{t}(\cdot,\bar{\omega})\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\sup_{r\leq t}|Y^{1}_{r}(\cdot,\bar{\omega})-Y^{2}_{r}(\cdot,\bar{\omega})\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\max}\left\|\sup_{r\leq t}|Y^{1}_{r}(\cdot,\bar{\omega})-Y^{2}_{r}(\cdot,\bar{\omega})\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\left(t+t\int_{U_{1}}\alpha_{1}(x)^{2}\nu_{1}(dx)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad+C_{f_{2}}\left\|\sup_{r\leq t}|Y^{1}_{s}(\cdot,\bar{\omega})-Y^{2}_{s}(\cdot,\bar{\omega})\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}M(\bar{\omega})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+|\tilde{f}_{2}|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\left|\int_{U_{1}}(\tilde{N}_{q}^{1}(ds,dx)(\bar{\omega})-\tilde{N}^{2}_{q}(ds,dx)(\bar{\omega}))\right|.

Using Gronwall’s lemma, we get

\displaystyle\left\|Y^{1}_{t}(\cdot,\bar{\omega})-Y^{2}_{t}(\cdot,\bar{\omega})\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\sup_{r\leq t}|Y^{1}_{r}(\cdot,\bar{\omega})-Y^{2}_{r}(\cdot,\bar{\omega})\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq|\tilde{f}_{2}|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\left|\int_{U_{1}}(\tilde{N}_{q}^{1}(ds,dx)(\bar{\omega})-\tilde{N}^{2}_{q}(ds,dx)(\bar{\omega}))\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\times\exp\left\{C_{\max}\left(t+t\int_{U_{1}}\alpha_{1}(x)^{2}\nu_{1}(dx))^{1/2}+C_{f_{2}}M(\bar{\omega})\right)\right\}

Define
\tilde{C}(\bar{\omega})=|\tilde{f}_{2}|_{\infty}\exp(C_{\max}(t+t\int_{U_{1}}\alpha_{1}(x)^{2}\nu_{1}(dx))^{1/2}+C_{f_{2}}M(\bar{\omega}))<\infty
, and we have

\displaystyle\left\|Y^{1}_{t}(\cdot,\bar{\omega})-Y^{2}_{t}(\cdot,\bar{\omega})\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\sup_{r\leq t}|Y^{1}_{r}(\cdot,\bar{\omega})-Y^{2}_{r}(\cdot,\bar{\omega})\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}(\bar{\omega})\int_{0}^{t}\left|\int_{U_{1}}(\tilde{N}_{q}^{1}(ds,dx)(\bar{\omega})-\tilde{N}^{2}_{q}(ds,dx)(\bar{\omega}))\right|

Putting this back into equation (
78
), we get the result.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

Construct canonical RDEs

\displaystyle dy^{1}_{t}

\displaystyle=V(y^{1}_{t})\diamond d(\mathbf{x}^{1}_{t},\phi^{1}),

\displaystyle dy^{2}_{t}

\displaystyle=V(y^{2}_{t})\diamond d(\mathbf{x}^{2}_{t},\phi^{2}).

For the continuous RDE solutions
\tilde{y}^{1}_{t}
and
\tilde{y}^{2}_{t}

[
4
]
)

\|\tilde{y}^{1}-\tilde{y}^{2}\|_{p-\text{var}}\lesssim|y^{1}_{0}-y_{0}^{2}|+\rho_{p-\text{var}}(\mathbf{x}^{\phi^{1},\delta}_{1},\mathbf{x}^{\phi^{2},\delta}_{2}).

Now recall the map
\tau
in (
8
) and
\tau_{r}(t)=t(T+r)
/T, and consider the effect of
\tau_{x}=\tau_{r}^{-1}\circ\tau
on the
p

\tau_{r}
and
\tau_{r}^{-1}
are reparameterizations of the path, the
p

Next, for any path

on a Banach space E, the path

is a path in
C^{p-\text{var}}([0,T+r],E)

\hat{x}=x\circ\tau_{r}

\hat{x}\circ\tau
has the property,

\|\hat{x}\circ\tau\|_{p-\text{var};[0,T+r]}\leq\|\hat{x}\|_{p-\text{var};[0,T+r]}=\|x\|_{p-\text{var}[0,T]},

since

\displaystyle\|\hat{x}\|^{p}_{p-\text{var};[0,T+r]}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\mathcal{P}_{[0,T+r]}}\left(\sum_{u,v\in\mathcal{P}_{[0,T+r]}}\|x_{v}-x_{u}\|^{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\mathcal{P}_{[0,t_{1}]}}\left(\sum_{u,v\in\mathcal{P}_{[0,t_{1}]}}\|x_{v}-x_{u}\|^{p}\right)+\sup_{\mathcal{P}_{[t_{1},t_{1}+r_{1}]}}\left(\sum_{u,v\in\mathcal{P}_{[t_{1},t_{1}+r_{1}]}}\|x_{v}-x_{u}\|^{p}\right)+\dots

\displaystyle=\|\hat{x}\circ\tau\|^{p}_{p-\text{var};[0,T+r]}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\|\hat{x}\|^{p}_{p-\text{var};S_{i}},

where
S_{i}

s\in[\tau(t_{i-}),\tau(t_{i}))

Proof of Corollary
5.1.1
.

Consider the canonical rough flows

\displaystyle\Phi^{1}(t,x)

\displaystyle:=x+\int_{0}^{t}g(\Phi^{1}(s,x))\diamond d({\bm{{\eta}}}^{1},\phi^{1}),

\displaystyle\Phi^{2}(t,x)

\displaystyle:=x+\int_{0}^{t}g(\Phi^{2}(s,x))\diamond d({\bm{{\eta}}}^{2},\phi^{2}).

We know from Theorem

Since the flows are of continuous RDEs and are themselves diffeomorphisms, we have the inverse flows:

({\Phi^{i}})^{-1}(t,x):=\Psi^{i}(t,x)=x-\int_{0}^{t}g(x)\partial_{x}\Psi^{i}(s,x)\diamond d({\bm{{\eta}}^{i}},\phi^{i}).

Writing the equations (

) as the composition of flows,

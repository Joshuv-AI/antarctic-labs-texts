The SPAr process: proton captures powering carbon–oxygen shell mergers in massive stars
Lorenzo Roberti, L. Roberti, M. Pignatari

Abstract
Context. Carbon-oxygen (C–O) shell mergers in massive stars play a crucial role in both nucleosynthesis and the final stages of stellar evolution. These convective-reactive events significantly alter the internal structure of the star shortly before core collapse. Aims. We investigate how the enhanced production of light particles (especially protons) during a C–O shell merger, relative to classical oxygen shell burning, affects the energy balance and evolution of the convective shell. Methods. We derive the budget for direct and reverse nucleosynthesis flows across all relevant nuclear reactions from stellar evolution models, and we assess the relative energy produced. Results. We find that proton capture reactions on S 32 , 34 {}^{\makebox[19.87505pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 32,34$}}_{\makebox[19.87505pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{S} , P 31 {}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 31$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{P} , and Ar 38 {}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 38$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ar} (SPAr) dominate the nuclear energy production in typical C–O shell mergers as predicted by 1D stellar models. Their combined energy output is approximately 400 times greater than that of C and O fusion under the same conditions. Conclusions. Our results highlight the critical importance of including these proton-capture reactions in simulations of convective-reactive burning. Excluding their contribution can lead to inaccurate modeling of the dynamics and nucleosynthesis in advanced stellar evolutionary phases. Such results will need to be confirmed by 3D hydrodynamics models.

\gamma
)
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 33$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{P}
), making it a key regulator of the balance between
p

4
Discussion and conclusions

In this paper, we demonstrated that the light particles released during a C–O shell merger (in particular protons), not only impact the nucleosynthesis, but also play a key role in the energy balance of the evolving convective shell. We identified four crucial nuclear reactions that dominate energy generation once the proton abundance becomes sufficiently high:
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 32$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{S}
(
p
,
\gamma
)
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 33$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Cl}
,
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 34$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{S}
(
p
,
\gamma
)
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 35$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Cl}
,
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 31$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{P}
(
p
,
\alpha
)
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 28$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Si}
, and
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 38$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ar}
(
p
,
\gamma
)
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 39$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{K}
, that we referred to as the SPAr process. The nuclear energy released by the SPAr reactions is 400 times greater than that produced by classical O-burning shell conditions. This result is crucial, as neglecting their contribution (often done to simplify and speed up stellar calculations) can lead to inaccurate modeling of the final evolutionary stages of massive stars, potentially altering their core-collapse supernova (CCSN) outcome.

C–O shell mergers are a convective-reactive events, i.e., the ingestion of fresh fuel at the outer boundary of the convective zone promotes further expansion of the shell and additional fuel ingestion. Therefore, simulating this occurrence requires that the equations governing the evolution of the chemical composition to be (1) coupled with the stellar structure equations and mixing processes, and (2) include an extended nuclear network that captures the energy feedback from light particle reactions such as those in the SPAr process. This requirement applies to both 1D and 3D simulations. Based on our analysis on 1D massive star models, applying a boosting factor in the order of
\sim 300-400
to the energy generation rate can, as a first approximation, compensate for the lack of SPAr reactions in models with a reduced network. We would not expect significant modifications to such a correction factors for 3D hydrodynamics simulations of C–O shell mergers, but this should be verified with appropriate hydrodynamics models. Interestingly, boosting factor for energy generation of the order of
\approx 100
have been already in use for some of the hydrodynamics simulations of these environments, to speed up the physical processes leading the simulations
(
Rizzuti et al. 2022
, e.g.,)
.

In this work, we adopt the stellar model from
Roberti et al. 2024b
, using SPAr reaction rates from Reaclib
(
Cyburt et al. 2010
)
and Starlib
(
Sallaska et al. 2013
)
nuclear databases. Specifically, in the case of the

(

,
\gamma
)

was adopted. For the

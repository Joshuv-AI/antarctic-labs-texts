New Crouzeix-Raviart elements of even degree: theoretical aspects, numerical performance, and applications to the Stokes’ equations
A. Bressan, L. Mascotto, M. Mosconi

Abstract
We construct new Crouzeix-Raviart (CR) spaces of even degree p p that are spanned by basis functions mimicking those for the odd degree case. Compared to the standard CR gospel, the present construction allows for the use of nested bases of increasing degree and is particularly suited to design variable order CR methods. We analyze a nonconforming discretization of a two dimensional Poisson problem, which requires a DG-type stabilization; the employed stabilization parameter is considerably smaller than that needed in DG methods. Numerical results are presented, which exhibit the expected convergence rates for the h h -, p p -, and h ​ p hp -versions of the scheme. We further investigate numerically the behaviour of new even degree CR-type discretizations of the Stokes’ equations. AMS subject classification : 65M12; 65M15. Keywords : Crouzeix-Raviart element; hierarchical basis; DG stabilization; h ​ p hp -method; Stokes’ problem.

Introduction

State-of-the-art.

The lowest-order Crouzeix–Raviart (CR) element
was introduced in 1973 in
[
13
]

as an elementwise divergence free discretization
of the Stokes’ equations
for space dimensions
d=2,3
;
see also
[
18
]
.
Fortin and Soulie
[
16
]

constructed explicit piecewise quadratic CR elements;
they showed that the second-order CR global space
is a standard conforming piecewise quadratic space
enriched with elemental nonconforming bubbles.
A CR scheme
with cubic approximation properties
was detailed in
[
13
, Example 5]

by augmenting the space with quartic bubble functions;
Crouzeix and Falk
[
12
]

later introduced an optimal CR cubic element,
which did not require such bubbles.
Cha, Lee, and Lee
[
10
]

constructed quartic CR elements,
where fourth order nonconforming bubbles were employed,
with properties similar to those required by
Fortin and Soulie in the quadratic case.
In the subsequent years, the interest in developing higher (and general)
order CR elements raised a lot;
we refer to
[
24
,
8
,
11
]

for (essentially up to date) developments on this topic.

In the literature,
CR-type nonconforming global spaces
have been defined

•

implicitly
, as spaces of piecewise polynomials
satisfying certain “no-jump” conditions at the interface
between elements;

•

explicitly
, as the span of the union of standard conforming finite element functions
and additional nonconforming bubble functions.

While the former version is particularly useful on the theoretical level,
the latter appears to be very convenient in the implementation of the scheme.

It was clear since the very inception of the CR method
[
13
]
, see also
[
16
]
,
that the
explicit
definition
involves different types of basis functions
depending on the order of the scheme:

•

odd order CR elements are spanned by modal functions, yet
removing the standard hat functions associated with the vertices of the mesh,
and facet nonconforming bubbles;
see Section
1.4.1
below;

•

even degree CR elements are spanned by modal functions
and elemental nonconforming bubbles;
see Section
1.4.2
below.

Apparently, this difference leads to some limitations:

•

in view of the
p
-version of the scheme,
the corresponding basis functions have not a hierarchical structure;

•

designing variable order CR schemes is not as immediate as while
using conforming modal or DG spaces.

Goals of the paper.

In this work, we aim at facing the above issues.
In particular, we design novel even degree CR spaces
based on spanning sets of basis functions similar to those
employed in standard CR elements with odd degree.
This new approach

•

gives a hierarchical structure to the resulting
p
-version CR method;

•

allows for a simple design of variable order CR elements.

A moderate price to pay for these advantages
is that the standard broken
grad-grad bilinear form must be corrected
with DG-type terms in order to retain error estimates of the expected order;
this correction is not needed for the
well-posedness of the method
and, compared to its DG counterpart
[
14
]
,
has a much more moderate effect on the performance of the scheme,
as we shall investigate in practice.

As an interesting per se matter, we shall also check
the behaviour of new even degree CR-type discretizations
of the Stokes’ equations on the numerical level;
this aspect is very much related to the recent
works
[
19
,
20
]
.

of the expansion with respect to that basis.

The next result investigates the relation
between the spaces
V_{n}^{\rm im}
and
V_{n}^{\rm ex}

for odd
p
.

Lemma 1.2
.

The spaces
V_{n}^{\rm im}
and
V_{n}^{\rm ex}
defined
in (
13
) and (
23
) coincide
for odd
p
.

Proof.

Let
N^{T}_{F}
,
N^{I}_{F}
, and
N_{K}
denote the number
of total facets, internal facets, and elements of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, respectively.
We split the proof in two steps.

V_{n}^{\rm ex}
is contained in
V_{n}^{\rm im}
.

The space
V_{n}^{\rm ex}
consists of modal basis functions,
i.e., functions that are
H^{1}
conforming
and therefore contained in
V_{n}^{\rm im}
,
and facet bubble functions as in (
16
).
Such bubble functions
have vanishing jumps over all the facets in
\mathcal{F}_{h}
.

To see this, we fix a generic
F
in
\mathcal{F}_{h}
and
b_{p}^{F}
as in (
16
).
We have that
b_{p}^{F}
is continuous
over the interior of
\omega_{F}
,
whence it has vanishing jumps over
F
.
On the facets
\widetilde{F}
lying on
\partial\omega_{F}
,

b_{p}^{F}{}_{|\widetilde{F}}
is the univariate
Legendre polynomial of order
p

starting from the vertex opposite to
F
;
in particular, its moments with univariate polynomials
of order
p-1
are zero over
\widetilde{F}
.
On the other facets of the mesh,
the bubble is zero by definition, whence it has zero jumps.

V_{n}^{\rm im}
and
V_{n}^{\rm ex}
have the same dimension.

The dimension of
V_{n}^{\rm im}

is the dimension of piecewise polynomials
of maximum order
p
over
\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
minus the total number of “no jump”
conditions over the internal facets:

\dim(V_{n}^{\rm im})=\dim(\mathbb{P}_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))-pN^{I}_{F}=\dim(\mathbb{P}_{p}(K))N_{K}-pN^{I}_{F}.

(18)

On the other hand, the dimension of
V_{n}^{\rm ex}
is given by
the number of total facets
(for the nonconforming bubbles)
plus the number of modal basis functions
excluding those associated with the vertices:

\dim(V_{n}^{\rm ex})=N^{T}_{F}+\dim(\mathbb{P}_{p-2}(F))N^{T}_{F}+\dim(\mathbb{P}_{p-3}(K))N_{K}=pN^{T}_{F}+\dim(\mathbb{P}_{p-3}(K))N_{K}.

(19)

Recall the Euler’s formula

N^{I}_{F}+N^{T}_{F}=3N_{K}.

(20)

Subtracting the identities in (
18
) and (
19
), we deduce

\frac{(p+2)(p+1)-(p-1)(p-2)}{2}N_{K}-p(N^{I}_{F}+N^{T}_{F})\overset{\eqref{euler-1}}{=}0.

∎

Lemma
1.2

entails that two different sets of degrees of
freedom are at disposal.
A set of global degrees of freedom
for Crouzeix-Raviart spaces
is obtained by a coupling of the degrees
of freedom in (
14a
);
alternatively, we pick the coefficients in the expansion
with respect to the basis in (
17
).
The former is used on the theoretical level,
e.g., while deriving interpolation estimates in CR spaces,
see Remark
3
below;
the latter is convenient for the implementation of the method.

1.4.2
Standard Crouzeix-Raviart elements with even degree

Proceeding as in Section
1.4.1
for even
p
,
we are not able to show unisolvence
of the degrees of freedom (
14
):
the proof of Lemma
1.1
fails
since display (
15
) would read

v_{n}(\nu_{1})=v_{n}(\nu_{2})=v_{n}(\nu_{3})=v_{n}(\nu_{1}),

(21)

which does not imply that
v_{n}
is zero
at the vertices of
K
;
furthermore,
using Gauß-Legendre-type degrees of freedom
as those discussed in Remark
1

would lead to a linear dependence condition
as described in
[
16
, pp. 506-507]

for the case
p=2
.

We recall some details here;
the general even degree case presents similar limitations.
Given a triangle
K
,
we denote its unit tangential vector
and the corresponding tangential derivative
by
\bm{\tau}_{K}
and
\partial_{\bm{\tau}_{K}}
.
Let
\{g_{j}^{i}\}_{i=1}^{2}
and
m_{j}

denote the Gauß–Legendre quadrature points
and the midpoints
of the facet
F_{j}
,
j=1,2,3
, respectively.

Given a quadratic polynomial
q_{2}
,

\partial_{\bm{\tau}_{K}}q_{2}{}_{|\partial K}
is piecewise linear:
the integral over the facet
F_{j}

of
\partial_{\bm{\tau}_{K}}q_{2}

is equal to the evaluation at the midpoint
m_{j}
;
in turn, this can be expressed as the difference
of the evaluation at the two Gauß-Legendre nodes,
which are symmetric with respect to
m_{j}
.
Moreover, since
q_{2}
is smooth over
\partial K
,
we have

0=\int_{\partial K}\partial_{\bm{\tau}_{K}}q_{2}\,\mathrm{d}{x}=\sum_{j=1}^{3}{\left|{F_{j}}\right|}\partial_{\bm{\tau}_{K}}q_{2}(m_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{3}{\left|{F_{j}}\right|}\frac{q_{2}(g_{j}^{2})-q_{2}(g_{j}^{1})}{{\left|{F_{j}}\right|}}\sqrt{3}.

Hence,
the following relation holds true:

q_{2}(g_{1}^{2})-q_{2}(g_{1}^{2})+q_{2}(g_{2}^{2})-q_{2}(g_{2}^{1})+q_{2}(g_{3}^{2})-q_{2}(g_{3}^{1})=0.

This is a linear dependence condition involving all the
degrees of freedom.
Moreover, it is possible to check
[
16
]
that the quadratic polynomial

2-3(\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{2}^{2}+\lambda_{3}^{2})

has vanishing degrees freedom but is not zero in
K
.
Consequently, the choice of the degrees of freedom in (
14
)
is not suited to describe even degree CR spaces.

In order to establish a set of unisolvent degrees of freedom,
the even counterpart of the explicit space in (
17
)
comes to the rescue.
With each element
K
in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
we associate the elemental nonconforming bubble function
[
24
]

{b}_{p}^{K}:=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\big(-1+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}L_{p}(1-2\lambda_{i})\big)\qquad\qquad&\text{on }K\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(22)

Following
[
11
,
24
,
9
]
,
under the notation in Section
1.3
, we define

V_{n}^{\rm ex}:=\Span\left(\varphi^{\nu}\quad\forall\nu\subset\mathcal{V}_{h};\quad\{\varphi_{j}^{F}\}_{j=1}^{p-1}\quad\forall F\subset\mathcal{F}_{h};\quad b_{p}^{K}\text{ and }\{\varphi_{\ell}^{K}\}_{\ell=1}^{(p-2)(p-1)/2}\quad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\right).

(23)

As discussed in
[
16
, Proposition 1]
for the case
p=2
,
these functions are linearly dependent:
there exists precisely one elemental nonconforming bubble
that can be written as a linear combination
of the other functions generating the space;
some details are provided in Lemma
1.3
below.

Lemma 1.3
.

The spaces
V_{n}^{\rm im}
and
V_{n}^{\rm ex}
defined
in (
13
) and (
23
) coincide for even
p
.

Proof.

Introduce
\Phi_{n}:=\Span\{b_{p}^{K}\mid K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}
.
Let
N^{T}_{F}
,
N^{I}_{F}
,
N_{V}
, and
N_{K}
denote the number
of total facets, internal facets, vertices, and elements of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, respectively.
We split the proof in two steps.

V_{n}^{\rm ex}
is contained in
V_{n}^{\rm im}
.

The space
V_{n}^{\rm ex}
is defined as
X_{n}+\Phi_{n}
, i.e.,
it consists of modal basis functions,
which are
H^{1}
conforming and therefore contained in
V_{n}^{\rm im}
,
and elemental nonconforming bubbles as in (
22
).
Such bubble functions have vanishing jump moments
with respect to polynomials of order
p-1

over the facets in
\mathcal{F}_{h}
.

To see this,
we fix a generic
F
in
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{I}

and one of the two elements
K
in
\omega_{F}
.
The restriction of
b_{p}^{K}
on
F

is given by the sum of four terms,
see (
22
):
two of them are (scaled) univariate Legendre
polynomials of degree
p
,
which have vanishing moments up to order
p-1
;
the sum of the other two vanishes
since the restriction of the remaining
Legendre polynomial over
F
is equal to
1
.

V_{n}^{\rm im}
and
V_{n}^{\rm ex}
have the same dimension.

As shown in the proof of Lemma
1.1
,
the dimension of
V_{n}^{\rm im}
is given by

\dim(V_{n}^{\rm im})=\dim(\mathbb{P}_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))-pN^{I}_{F}=\dim(\mathbb{P}_{p}(K))N_{K}-pN^{I}_{F}.

As for the dimension of
V_{n}^{\rm ex}
,
we start by investigating the dimensions of
X_{n}

and
\Phi_{n}
, separately.
The former is given by the number of total
modal basis functions:

\dim(X_{n})=\dim(\mathbb{P}_{p-3}(K))N_{K}+\dim(\mathbb{P}_{p-2}(F))N^{T}_{F}+N_{V};

the latter by the number of elements:

\dim(\Phi_{n})=N_{K}.

Based on the Euler’s formulas (
20
) and
N_{K}-N^{T}_{F}+N_{V}=1
,
the dimensions of
V_{n}^{\rm im}
and
X_{n}+\Phi_{n}
satisfy the following relation

\dim(X_{n})+\dim(\Phi_{n})=\dim(V_{n}^{\rm im})+1.

(24)

As elemental bubbles of neighbouring elements
have the same restriction on the interface,
we have

X_{n}\cap\Phi_{n}=\Span\Big\{\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}b_{p}^{K}\Big\},\qquad\qquad\qquad\dim(X_{n}\cap\Phi_{n})=1.

(25)

We deduce

\begin{split}\dim(V_{n}^{\rm im})+1&\overset{\eqref{relation:dim}}{=}\dim(X_{n})+\dim(\Phi_{n})=\dim(X_{n}+\Phi_{n})+\dim(X_{n}\cap\Phi_{n})\overset{\eqref{dim-intersection}}{=}\dim(V_{n}^{\rm ex})+1.\end{split}

∎

Remark 2
.

A consequence of Lemma

As we proved that the implicit and explicit

with respect to the broken seminorm in (

Therefore, method (

]
.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
u
and
u_{n}
be the solutions to (
2
) and (
27
).
Then, we have

{\left|{u-u_{n}}\right|}_{1,h;\Omega}\leq\inf_{v_{n}\in V_{n}^{0}}{\left|{u-v_{n}}\right|}_{1,h;\Omega}+\sup_{v_{n}\in V_{n}^{0}}\frac{|a_{n}(u,v_{n})-(f,v_{n})_{0,\Omega}|}{{\left|{v_{n}}\right|}_{1,h;\Omega}}.

(28)

If
u
belongs to
H^{s}(\Omega)
,

s
larger than
3/2
,
and
p
is smaller than or equal to
s
,
then we have

{\left|{u-u_{n}}\right|}_{1,h;\Omega}\lesssim h^{p}{\left|{u}\right|}_{p+1,\Omega}.

(29)

Proof.

Inequality (
28
) is second Strang’s lemma
[
25
,
6
]
.
As for estimate (
29
), we bound the two terms
on the right-hand side of (
28
) separately.

As for the first one,
the best error in CR spaces
is smaller than the best error in conforming Lagrangian spaces:
the convergence follows from standard
polynomial interpolation theory
[
7
]
;
the
H^{s}(\Omega)
regularity,
s
larger than
3/2
,
suffices to this purpose.

As for the second term, for all
F
in
\mathcal{F}_{h}
,
introduce
\Pi^{0,F}_{p-1}
as the orthogonal projector
with respect to the
L^{2}(F)
inner product
into the space of vector polynomials
\mathbb{P}_{p-1}(F)
.
Denote the elements in
\omega_{F}
by
K_{i}
,
i=1,2
.
An integration by parts, definitions (
6
) and (
7
),
formulation (
1
),
the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,
and the assumption that
u
belongs to
H^{s}(\Omega)
,
s
larger than
3/2
, yield,
for all
q_{p}^{K_{1}}
,
q_{p}^{K_{2}}
,
q_{0}^{K_{1}}
, and
q_{0}^{K_{2}}

in
\mathbb{P}_{p}(K_{1})
,
\mathbb{P}_{p}(K_{2})
,
\mathbb{P}_{0}(K_{1})
, and
\mathbb{P}_{0}(K_{2})
,

\begin{split}a_{n}(u,v_{n})-(f,v_{n})_{0,\Omega}&=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\mathbf{n}_{K}\cdot\nabla u,v_{n})_{0,\partial K}=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}(\mathbf{n}_{F}\cdot\nabla u,\left[\!\left[v_{n}\right]\!\right])_{0,F}\\
&=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\left(\mathbf{n}_{F}\cdot\nabla u-\Pi^{0,F}_{p-1}\mathbf{n}_{F}\cdot\nabla u,\left[\!\left[v_{n}\right]\!\right]-\Pi^{0,F}_{p-1}\left[\!\left[v_{n}\right]\!\right]\right)_{0,F}.\end{split}

(30)

For interior facets, we have

\begin{split}&\left(\mathbf{n}_{F}\cdot\nabla u-\Pi^{0,F}_{p-1}\mathbf{n}_{F}\cdot\nabla u,\left[\!\left[v_{n}\right]\!\right]-\Pi^{0,F}_{p-1}\left[\!\left[v_{n}\right]\!\right]\right)_{0,F}\\
&\leq{\left\|{\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{n}_{K_{1}}\cdot\nabla(u_{|K_{1}}-q_{p}^{K_{1}})-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{n}_{K_{2}}\cdot\nabla(u_{|K_{2}}-q_{p}^{K_{2}})}\right\|}_{0,F}{\left\|{\frac{1}{2}(v_{n}{}_{|K_{1}}-q_{0}^{K_{1}})+\frac{1}{2}(v_{n}{}_{|K_{2}}-q_{0}^{K_{2}})}\right\|}_{0,F}.\end{split}

The multiplicative trace inequality, the Poincaré inequality,
and choosing the vector polynomials above as suitable elementwise
L^{2}

entail that the two terms on the right-hand side of (
30
)
satisfy, for
i=1,2
and positive
\varepsilon
,

\begin{split}{\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{K_{i}}\cdot\nabla(u_{|K_{1}}-q_{p}^{K_{i}})}\right\|}_{0,F}&\lesssim h^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\left\|{\nabla_{h}(u-q_{p}^{K_{i}})}\right\|}_{0,K_{i}}+h^{\varepsilon}{\left|{u-q_{p}^{K_{i}}}\right|}_{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon,K_{i}};\\
{\left\|{v_{n}{}_{|K_{i}}-q_{0}^{K_{i}}}\right\|}_{0,F}&\lesssim h^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\left\|{v_{n}-q_{0}^{K_{i}}}\right\|}_{0,K_{i}}+h^{\frac{1}{2}}{\left|{v_{n}}\right|}_{1,K_{i}}\lesssim h^{\frac{1}{2}}{\left|{v_{n}}\right|}_{1,K_{i}}.\end{split}

Analogous estimates can be proven for boundary facets.
Combining the above displays with (
30
),
and standard polynomial approximation results yield the assertion.
∎

.

For odd degree

Remark 4
.

One of the most striking feature of CR elements
is that the structure of the explicit
basis functions
is very different in the odd and even degree cases;
see (
17
) and (
23
).
On the one hand, in the light of Remark
3
,
this renders the derivation of interpolation estimates
in the
p

than for conforming and DG methods.
On the other hand,
in the assembling process of the matrix stemming from method (

for increasing

it is not feasible to use the same indexing strategy for even and odd degrees
since the types of basis functions under consideration change.
In the odd case, modal vertex functions are not considered
and facet nonconforming bubble functions are used;
in the even case, modal vertex functions are considered
and elemental nonconforming bubble functions are used.
This might be the reason for the lack of literature on
the

- and
hp

)
is one order suboptimal.
In fact, proceeding as in the proof of

,
we rewrite display (

as follows:

\begin{split}a_{n}(u,\widetilde{v}_{n})-(f,\widetilde{v}_{n})&=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\mathbf{n}_{K}\cdot\nabla u,\widetilde{v}_{n})_{0,\partial K}=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}(\mathbf{n}_{F}\cdot\nabla u,\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right])_{0,F}\\
&=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\left(\mathbf{n}_{F}\cdot\nabla u-\Pi^{0,F}_{p-2}(\mathbf{n}_{F}\cdot\nabla u),\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right]-\Pi^{0,F}_{0}\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right]\right)_{0,F},\end{split}

which delivers an order of convergence

\mathcal{O}(h^{p-1})

due to the presence of
\Pi^{0,F}_{p-2}

instead of
\Pi^{0,F}_{p-1}
.

As a matter of notation,
we define the operator
\Pi_{L_{p-1}^{F}}

as zero if

is odd
and as the

onto the Legendre polynomial

if

is even.
In order to recover error estimates of order

,
we modify method (

)
by employing the discrete bilinear form
\widetilde{a}_{n}:[H^{1}(\Omega)+\widetilde{V}_{n}]^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
defined as follows:
for a sufficiently large positive coefficient
\eta
,
see Proposition

below,

\begin{split}\widetilde{a}_{n}(\widetilde{u}_{n},\widetilde{v}_{n})&:=(\nabla_{h}\widetilde{u}_{n},\nabla_{h}\widetilde{v}_{n})_{0,\Omega}-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\left(\Pi_{L_{p-1}^{F}}\left\{\nabla_{h}\widetilde{u}_{n}\right\},\Pi_{L_{p-1}^{F}}\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right]\right)_{0,F}\\
&-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\left(\Pi_{L_{p-1}^{F}}\left[\!\left[\widetilde{u}_{n}\right]\!\right],\Pi_{L_{p-1}^{F}}\left\{\nabla_{h}\widetilde{v}_{n}\right\}\right)_{0,F}+\eta\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}h_{F}^{-1}\left(\Pi_{L_{p-1}^{F}}\left[\!\left[\widetilde{u}_{n}\right]\!\right],\Pi_{L_{p-1}^{F}}\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right]\right)_{0,F}.\end{split}

(36)

Introduce the mesh-dependent norm

\vvvert{\widetilde{v}_{n}}\vvvert_{n}^{2}:={\left|{\widetilde{v}_{n}}\right|}^{2}_{1,h;\Omega}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}h_{F}^{-1}{\left\|{\Pi_{L_{p-1}^{F}}\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right]}\right\|}^{2}_{0,F}.

(37)

We consider and analyze the following method:

\begin{cases}\text{find }\widetilde{u}_{n}\in\widetilde{V}_{n}^{0}\text{ such that}\\
\widetilde{a}_{n}(\widetilde{u}_{n},\widetilde{v}_{n})=(f,\widetilde{v}_{n})_{0,\Omega}\qquad\qquad\forall\widetilde{v}_{n}\in\widetilde{V}_{n}^{0}.\end{cases}

(38)

in

resembles that in standard symmetric interior penalty DG methods

Proof.

The bilinear form in (

)
is coercive with respect to the mesh-dependent norm (

Indeed, given
\widetilde{v}_{n}
in
\widetilde{V}_{n}

definition (

\widetilde{a}_{n}(\widetilde{v}_{n},\widetilde{v}_{n})=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}{\left|{\widetilde{v}_{n}}\right|}^{2}_{1,K}-2\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}(\Pi_{L_{p-1}^{F}}\left\{\nabla\widetilde{v}_{n}\right\},\Pi_{L_{p-1}^{F}}\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right])_{0,F}+\eta\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}h_{F}^{-1}{\left\|{\Pi_{L_{p-1}^{F}}\widetilde{v}_{n}}\right\|}^{2}_{0,F}.

(41)

For a given

in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
the following discrete trace inequality

[

, Lemma 1.46]
holds true:

h_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}{\left\|{\widetilde{v}_{n}}\right\|}_{0,\partial K}\lesssim{\left\|{\widetilde{v}_{n}}\right\|}_{0,K}.

On a fixed

the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,
the property

for

in
\omega_{F}
,
the stability of the

and the discrete trace inequality (

\begin{split}&\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}(\Pi_{L_{p-1}^{F}}\left\{\nabla\widetilde{v}_{n}\right\},\Pi_{L_{p-1}^{F}}\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right])_{0,F}\leq\big(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}h_{F}{\left\|{\Pi_{L_{p-1}^{F}}\left\{\nabla_{h}\widetilde{v}_{n}\right\}{}}\right\|}_{0,F}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}h_{F}^{-1}{\left\|{\Pi_{L_{p-1}^{F}}\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right]}\right\|}^{2}_{0,F}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq\big(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}{\left\|{\nabla_{h}\widetilde{v}_{n}{}_{|K}}\right\|}^{2}_{0,\partial K}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}h_{F}^{-1}{\left\|{\Pi_{L_{p-1}^{F}}\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right]}\right\|}^{2}_{0,F}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim{\left\|{\nabla_{h}\widetilde{v}_{n}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}\big(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}h_{F}^{-1}{\left\|{\Pi_{L_{p-1}^{F}}\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right]}\right\|}^{2}_{0,F}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

Substituting the above inequality in (

\widetilde{a}_{n}(\widetilde{v}_{n},\widetilde{v}_{n})\gtrsim{\left\|{\nabla_{h}\widetilde{v}_{n}}\right\|}^{2}_{0,\Omega}-{\left\|{\nabla_{h}\widetilde{v}_{n}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}\big(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}h_{F}^{-1}{\left\|{\Pi_{L_{p-1}^{F}}\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right]}\right\|}^{2}_{0,F}\big)+\eta\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}h_{F}^{-1}{\left\|{\Pi_{L_{p-1}^{F}}\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right]}\right\|}^{2}_{0,F}.

Further using Young’s inequality,
this yields the coercivity of
\widetilde{a}_{n}(\cdot,\cdot)

with respect to the norm (
37
)
for

sufficiently large.
On the one hand this implies the well-posedness of
method (

).
On the other hand, inequality (

)
follows from the second Strang’s lemma;
see, e.g.,
[

,
6
]
.
The hidden constants therein depends on the discrete coercivity constant, which in turns depend on

and

.

In order to derive the error estimate (

),
we show an upper bound for the two terms
on the right-hand side of (

) separately.
As for the first one, we observe that

\begin{split}&\inf_{\widetilde{v}_{n}\in\widetilde{V}_{n}^{0}}(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}{\left\|{\nabla(u-\widetilde{v}_{n})}\right\|}^{2}_{0,K}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}h_{F}^{-1}{\left\|{\Pi_{L_{p-1}^{F}}(\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right])}\right\|}^{2}_{0,F})\\
&\leq\inf_{\widetilde{v}_{n}\in\widetilde{V}_{n}^{0}}(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}{\left\|{\nabla(u-\widetilde{v}_{n})}\right\|}^{2}_{0,K}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}h_{F}^{-1}{\left\|{\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right]}\right\|}^{2}_{0,F}).\end{split}

The best error in CR spaces is smaller than
the error in
H^{1}
conforming spaces;
in particular the jump terms vanish.
Optimal convergence follows from
polynomial approximation results
[

]
.

As for the second term in (

),

.
Therefore, we focus on the even

On all facets,

\left\{\nabla u\right\}
,
\left[\!\left[u\right]\!\right]
,
and
\Pi_{L_{p-1}^{F}}\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right]

are equal to

,

problem (

and the consistency of the bilinear form (

\begin{split}&\widetilde{a}_{n}(u,\widetilde{v}_{n})-(f,\widetilde{v}_{n})_{0,\Omega}=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\left(\mathbf{n}_{F}\cdot\nabla u,\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right]-\Pi^{0,F}_{p-2}\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right]\right)_{0,F}-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\left(\Pi_{L_{p-1}^{F}}(\mathbf{n}_{F}\cdot\nabla u),\Pi_{L_{p-1}^{F}}\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right]\right)_{0,F}\\
&=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\left(\mathbf{n}_{F}\cdot\nabla u,\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right]-\Pi^{0,F}_{p-1}\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right]\right)_{0,F}=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\left(\mathbf{n}_{F}\cdot\nabla u-\Pi^{0,F}_{p-1}(\mathbf{n}_{F}\cdot\nabla u),\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right]-\Pi^{0,F}_{0}\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right]\right)_{0,F}.\end{split}

The assertion now follows along the same lines
as in the proof of Proposition
1.4

For any mesh

4.1
An “obvious” variable order CR space does not work

We exhibit a counterexample to show why an “obvious”
construction of variable order CR spaces
(and the corresponding method)
cannot deliver a sequence of discrete solutions
converging to the exact one.

Given

in
\mathbb{N}
, we write

\widetilde{p}:=\begin{cases}p&\text{if $p$ is odd}\\
p-1&\text{if $p$ is even}.\end{cases}

With each internal facet
F
,
we associate a double-sided nonconforming bubble

b_{\widetilde{p}_{K_{1}},\widetilde{p}_{K_{2}}}^{F}{}_{|K_{j}}:=\begin{cases}b_{\widetilde{p}_{K_{1}}}^{F}&\text{in }K_{1}\\
b_{\widetilde{p}_{K_{2}}}^{F}&\text{in }K_{2}\\

As in the uniform polynomial degree case,

moreover, as the involved bubbles
are cubic polynomials on that element,
surely all the coefficients of the cubic
modal basis functions cannot vanish.
Bearing this in mind, that linear function,
e.g., on the bottom element,
should be represented by the linear combination
of the linear nonconforming bubbles
(and this alone would be OK)

and
the modal basis functions
where the cubic one has nonzero coefficient.
This is clearly not possible,
whence a cleverer construction must be in order;
see Section
4.2
below.
In a sense, this lack of linear consistency
might be regarded as a locking property
of the “obvious” construction of variable order
CR spaces as detailed above.

The main philosophy that we shall use
in order to fix this issue is as follows:

•

in order to avoid the locking,
we shall always use linear nonconforming bubble functions
(it can be readily checked on the example in Figure
5
that in this case the modal
basis functions on the edges of the left element
are not active);

•

since linear nonconforming bubbles have nonvanishing
linear component jumps, we shall correct the method
similarly to what we did in Section
1.4.2

for all orders, regardless of its parity.

4.3
A variable degree CR method

We define the operator
\Pi^{0,F}_{L^{F}_{1}}

as the

projector onto the scaled Legendre polynomial
of order
1
over
F

and the operator
\widetilde{\Pi}^{0,F}_{L^{F}_{1}}

as the
L^{2}(F)
projector onto the span of

\{L^{F}_{j}\mid j=0,\dots,p_{F}-1,\,j\neq 1\}.

We introduce the discrete bilinear form

\widetilde{a}_{n}:[H^{1}(\Omega)+\widetilde{V}_{n}]^{2}\to\mathbb{R}

defined as follows:
for sufficiently large positive coefficients
\eta_{F}

for all facets
F
,
see Proposition
4.2
below,

\begin{split}\widetilde{a}_{n}(\widetilde{u}_{n},\widetilde{v}_{n})&:=(\nabla_{h}\widetilde{u}_{n},\nabla_{h}\widetilde{v}_{n})_{0,\Omega}-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\left(\Pi^{0,F}_{L^{F}_{1}}\left\{\nabla_{h}\widetilde{u}_{n}\right\},\Pi^{0,F}_{L^{F}_{1}}\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right]\right)_{0,F}\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\left(\Pi^{0,F}_{L^{F}_{1}}\left[\!\left[\widetilde{u}_{n}\right]\!\right],\Pi^{0,F}_{L^{F}_{1}}\left\{\nabla_{h}\widetilde{v}_{n}\right\}\right)_{0,F}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\eta_{F}h_{F}^{-1}\left(\Pi^{0,F}_{L^{F}_{1}}\left[\!\left[\widetilde{u}_{n}\right]\!\right],\Pi^{0,F}_{L^{F}_{1}}\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right]\right)_{0,F}.\end{split}

(49)

Introduce the mesh-dependent norm,
for which Remark
5

still applies,

\vvvert{\widetilde{v}_{n}}\vvvert_{n}^{2}:=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}{\left\|{\nabla\widetilde{v}_{n}}\right\|}^{2}_{0,K}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}h_{F}^{-1}{\left\|{\Pi^{0,F}_{L^{F}_{1}}\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right]}\right\|}^{2}_{0,F}.

We consider and analyze the following method:

\begin{cases}\text{find }\widetilde{u}_{n}\in\widetilde{V}_{n}^{0}\text{ such that}\\
\widetilde{a}_{n}(\widetilde{u}_{n},\widetilde{v}_{n})=(f,\widetilde{v}_{n})_{0,\Omega}\qquad\qquad\forall\widetilde{v}_{n}\in\widetilde{V}_{n}^{0}.\end{cases}

(51)

The following well-posedness and convergence result
for method (

) holds true.

Proposition 4.2
.

Method (
51
) is well-posed.
Let
u
and
\widetilde{u}_{n}
be the solutions to (
2
) and (
51
).
If
\eta_{F}
is sufficiently large
for all
F
in
\mathcal{F}_{h}
,
then we have

\vvvert{u-\widetilde{u}_{n}}\vvvert_{n}\lesssim\inf_{\widetilde{v}_{n}\in\widetilde{V}_{n}^{0}}\vvvert{u-\widetilde{v}_{n}}\vvvert_{n}+\sup_{\widetilde{v}_{n}\in\widetilde{V}_{n}^{0}}\frac{|\widetilde{a}_{n}(u,\widetilde{v}_{n})-(f,\widetilde{v}_{n})_{0,\Omega}|}{\vvvert{\widetilde{v}_{n}}\vvvert_{n}}.

(52)

If
u
belongs to
H^{s}(\Omega)
,

s
larger than
3/2
,
and
p
smaller than or equal to
s
,
we have

\vvvert{u-\widetilde{u}_{n}}\vvvert_{n}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(h^{p_{K}}_{K})^{2}{\left|{u}\right|}_{p_{K+1},K}^{2}.

(53)

Proof.

The bilinear form in (
49
)
is coercive with respect to the mesh-dependent norm (
50
).
Similarly to what we did in the proof
of Proposition
2.3
,
this fact is a consequence of arguments
that are standard in variable order
symmetric interior penalty DG methods;
we refer to
[
14
, Lemma 4.12]
.
On the one hand this implies the well-posedness of
method (
51
)
for sufficiently large
\eta_{F}

for all
F
in
\mathcal{F}_{h}
.

On the other hand, inequality (
52
)
follows from the second Strang’s lemma;
see, e.g.,
[
25
,
6
]
.
The hidden constants therein depends on the discrete coercivity constant, which in turns depend on the collection of
the stabilization parameters
\eta_{F}

and the polynomial degree distribution
\mathbf{p}
.

In order to derive estimate (
53
),
we show an upper bound for the two terms on the right-hand side
of (

) separately.
As for the first one, we observe that

\begin{split}&\inf_{\widetilde{v}_{n}\in\widetilde{V}_{n}^{0}}(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}{\left\|{\nabla(u-\widetilde{v}_{n})}\right\|}^{2}_{0,K}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}h_{F}^{-1}{\left\|{\Pi^{0,F}_{L^{F}_{1}}\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right]}\right\|}^{2}_{0,F})\\
&\lesssim\inf_{\widetilde{v}_{n}\in\widetilde{V}_{n}^{0}}(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}{\left\|{\nabla(u-\widetilde{v}_{n})}\right\|}^{2}_{0,K}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}h_{F}^{-1}{\left\|{\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right]}\right\|}^{2}_{0,F}).\end{split}

The best error in CR spaces is smaller than
the error in
H^{1}
conforming spaces;
in particular the jump terms vanish.
Optimal convergence follows from
polynomial approximation results
[
7
]
.

As for the second term in (

),
we observe that

\left\{\nabla u\right\}
,
\left[\!\left[u\right]\!\right]
,
and
\Pi^{0,F}_{L^{F}_{1}}\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right]

are equal to
\nabla u
,
0
, and
\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right]
,
respectively, on all facets.
These identities, an integration by parts,
problem (
1
),
and the consistency of the bilinear form (
49
) yield

\begin{split}&\widetilde{a}_{n}(u,\widetilde{v}_{n})-(f,\widetilde{v}_{n})_{0,\Omega}=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\left(\mathbf{n}_{F}\cdot\nabla u,\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right]-\widetilde{\Pi}^{0,F}_{L^{F}_{1}}\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right]\right)_{0,F}-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\left(\Pi^{0,F}_{L^{F}_{1}}(\mathbf{n}_{F}\cdot\nabla u),\Pi^{0,F}_{L^{F}_{1}}\left[\!\left[\widetilde{v}_{n}\right]\!\right])_{0,F}\right)\\

The assertion now follows along the same line
as in the proof of Proposition
1.4
.

Crouzeix-Raviart elements were introduced
[

as an inf-sup stable, balanced,
and elementwise divergence free pair
for the discretization of the Stokes’ problem.
Even though the main focus of this work

6
Conclusions

We constructed new Crouzeix-Raviart (CR) elements
and corresponding methods
of even degree
p
for a two dimensional Poisson problem:
such spaces are the span of modal functions
excluding those associated
with the vertices of the given mesh
and facet nonconforming bubble functions
of a suitable odd degree
thus mimicking what happens in the odd degree CR spaces;
the method required a DG-type stabilization
in order to deliver convergence rates of order
p
.

The reason for introducing such spaces was twofold:

•

for increasing polynomial order,
we wanted to construct sequences of spaces
spanned by functions with a “hierarchical” structure
(or even nested bases);

•

we wanted to establish the ground
for a simple design of variable order CR elements.

The heart of the matter in the novel construction
is the observation that adding even degree
nonconforming bubble functions to the modal basis
yields a set of linearly dependent functions.
This is not the case while using odd degree
nonconforming bubbles,
the intrinsic reason being described
in displays (
15
)
and (
21
).
The price to pay is the need of stabilizing
the method in order not to lose convergence.

Variable order CR elements were designed;
employing
hp
-refinements
yielded exponential convergence in terms
of the cubic root of the degrees of freedom
for solutions displaying a singular behaviour
at the corners of the physical domain.
Importantly,

•

for the construction of fixed even degree
p

CR elements, nonconforming bubbles
of odd degree
1
(or
3
,
5
, …) can be employed;

•

for the design of variable order
CR elements,
we use linear nonconforming bubbles so as to retain
the linear consistency of the method;
for this reason, we also had to stabilize the method
on the facets of local odd degree
larger than
1
.

We further investigated whether the novel approach
could fit in an inf-sup stable
discretization of the Stokes’ equations,
which preserves the elementwise
incompressibility constraint
on the discrete level.
This was in fact the case for even polynomial degrees

Comments on the efficacy of paracetamol in the management of hemodynamically significant patent ductus arteriosus in preterm newborns
Authors: Bhagyashree Sharma, Saniya Shah, Suresh Dange, Bhagyashree Dange
Published: 2024

Dear Sir,

We read with interest the article, published in your esteemed journal.[1]

We congratulate the authors for a well planned study.

However, we have a few comments.

Although pharmacological closure of the ductus arteriosus (DA) is associated with decreased severe intraventricular hemorrhage and pulmonary hemorrhage, there is no convincing evidence of long-term benefit from randomized controlled trials.[2] Three drugs (indomethacin, ibuprofen, and paracetamol) have been demonstrated to be effective in placebo-controlled randomized trials. No significant differences in effectiveness have been reported among these medications.[3]

Indomethacin and ibuprofen are currently the only drugs labeled for treatment of the patent DA (PDA).

Paracetamol has comparable efficacy to ibuprofen in ductal closure, but there is limited information on long-term safety.[4] Recently, concern has been raised about paracetamol’s presumed superior safety profile based on reports of neurocognitive impairment after prenatal exposure and of hypotension following intravenous administration.[5]

This concern underscores the need for appropriate pharmacodynamic and follow-up studies examining both the route and dose of paracetamol, as well as the population being studied before we can conclude which is the most effective and safest drug to use when PDA treatment is needed.

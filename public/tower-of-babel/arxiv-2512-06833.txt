. We find upper bounds, sharp in most cases, on the number of real hyperplane sections of real smooth polarized K ​ 3 K3 -surfaces that split into lines. Most bounds coincide with their complex counterparts.

2.
Preliminaries

We adopt the traditional paradigm and mostly treat a
K3
-surface

X\to\mathbb{P}^{d+1}
as its
2d
-polarized Néron–Severi lattice
\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl NS}}\nolimits(X)\ni h
,

h^{2}=2d
. Furthermore, we assume that
\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl NS}}\nolimits(X)
is generated by
h
and the
classes of the lines on
X
, at least over
\mathbb{Q}
. For a concise description of
the algorithms involved, we refer to
[
3
]
(very brief) or

[
11
]
(extended). For the present paper we merely recall
that, in order to single out an equilinear stratum with a given
configuration
\Gamma
of lines, regarded as a (multi-)graph, we need to
consider tha lattice

\mathcal{F}(\Gamma):=(\mathbb{Z}\Gamma+\mathbb{Z}h)/\ker,\quad h^{2}=2d,\quad v^{2}=-2,\ v\cdot h=1\ \text{for $v\in\Gamma$},

and fix

•

a finite index extension
N\supset\mathcal{F}(\Gamma)
admitting a primitive
isometry

N\hookrightarrow\mathbf{L}:=2\mathbf{E}_{8}\oplus 3\mathbf{U}\simeq H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z}),

•

a representative
T\in\operatorname{genus}(N^{\perp\!})
,
known as the
transcendental lattice
of
X
,
of the genus
of
N^{\perp}\subset\mathbf{L}

(by
[
15
]
,
\operatorname{genus}(N^{\perp\!})
is determined by
N
), and

•

a
bijective anti-isometry

(2.1)

\varphi\colon\mathop{\operator@font discr}\nolimits N\to\mathop{\operator@font discr}\nolimits T.

For each configuration
\Gamma
appearing in
[
3
]
all these
data are readily available as they are used as part of the classification of
configurations. Therefore, below we take them as an input and concentrate on
detecting the real structures on
X
or, more precisely, on a representative
of the corresponding equilinear stratum. Note that we do
not
make an
attempt to discuss the connectedness of the real strata: according
to
[
13
,
14
,
5
]
, this may be much more involved than the
complex case.

2.1.
Real configurations

The following statement is
an immediate consequence of the global Torelli theorem for
K3
-surfaces:
given
N,T,\varphi
as in (
2.1
), the real structures

on appropriate representatives
of the respective equilinear stratum
are the involutive elements of the set

(2.2)

\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits_{\pm h}^{-}(N)\times_{\varphi}\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits^{-}(T),

where
\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits^{-}\subset\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits
is the set of autoisometries reversing the

positive sign structure
,
i.e.
, coherent orientation of maximal positive
definite subspaces.
Here, the “appropriate representative” means that an element

g\in\eqref{eq.real}
should be fixed first, whereupon
X
is chosen
very general and such that its period
\omega\in T\otimes{\mathbb{C}}
satisfies

g^{*}(\omega)=\bar{\omega}
.

The finite group
\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits_{\pm h}(N)\supset\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits_{\pm h}^{-}(N)
is easily computed:

\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits_{\pm h}\bigl(\mathcal{F}(\Gamma)\bigr)=\mathop{\operator@font Aut}\nolimits\Gamma\times\{\pm\mathop{\operator@font id}\nolimits\},\qquad\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits_{\pm h}(N)=\mathop{\operator@font stab}\nolimits\mathcal{K},

where
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{F}(\Gamma)
is the kernel of the finite index extension

N\supset\mathcal{F}(\Gamma)
. For
\mathop{\operator@font Aut}\nolimits\Gamma
, we use the
digraph

package in
GAP

[
12
]
. (Needless to say that the bulk of the other
computation is also performed using
GAP

[
12
]
.)

The computation of
\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits(T)\supset\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits^{-}(T)
is also immediate if
\mathop{\operator@font rk}\nolimits N=20

(the so-called
singular
K3
-surfaces
),
i.e.
,
T
is a positive
definite lattice of rank
2
. In general, finding all involutions in

\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits^{-}(T)
may be more involved,
cf.

[
16
]
.
Fortunately, apart from the totally real case in
§2.2

below,
we need this group only when

(2.3)

\text{$T\simeq 2\mathbf{U}(3)$ is a rescaling of the unimodular lattice $2\mathbf{U}$}.

The
involutions

g\in\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits^{-}(T)=\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits^{-}(2\mathbf{U})
are found as in
[
15
]
,
according to their invariant sublattices
g^{+}:=\mathop{\operator@font Ker}\nolimits(g-\mathop{\operator@font id}\nolimits)
, which may be

[2],\ \mathbf{U},\ \mathbf{U}(2),\ [2]\oplus[-2],\ \mathbf{U}\oplus[-2].

(Recall that
g\in\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits^{-}(T)
imposes
\sigma_{+}(g^{+})=1
.)
Since the natural homomorphism

\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits\bigl(2\mathbf{U}(3)\bigr)\to\mathop{\operator@font Aut}\nolimits\bigl(\mathop{\operator@font discr}\nolimits 2\mathbf{U}(3)\bigr)

is surjective,
for (
2.2
) we easily find the three (out of
eight) conjugacy classes of involutions
in
\mathop{\operator@font Aut}\nolimits\bigl(\mathop{\operator@font discr}\nolimits 2\mathbf{U}(3)\bigr)
represented by involutions

g\in\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits^{-}\bigl(2\mathbf{U}(3)\bigr)
.

2.2.
Totally real configurations

For the study of totally real
h
-fragments we can confine ourselves to the
case where
all
lines are real: indeed, we can merely replace
N
with
its primitive sublattice rationally generated by
h
and real lines.

The following criterion (
cf.

[
6
, Lemma 3.8]
) is based on
Nikulin’s classification of real structures on

K3
-surfaces
[
15
]
:

a finite index extension
N\supset\mathcal{F}(\Gamma)
is realized by a real
polarized
K3
-surface with all lines
\ell\in\Gamma
real if and only if
there exists a transcendental lattice
T\in\operatorname{genus}(N^{\perp\!})
such that

(2.4)

T\supset[2]\quad\text{or}\quad T\supset\mathbf{U}(2).

In other words, we need a finite index extension of
N\oplus[2]
or

N\oplus\mathbf{U}(2)
in which
N
is primitive and which admits a primitive
isometry to
\mathbf{L}
.
Using
[
15
]
,
denoting by
r:=\mathop{\operator@font rk}\nolimits T=22-\mathop{\operator@font rk}\nolimits N
the rank of

T
and letting
\mathcal{N}:=\mathop{\operator@font discr}\nolimits N
(and

\mathcal{N}_{p}:=\mathcal{N}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}
for a prime
p
),
this criterion can be restated as follows.

A transcendental lattice
T\supset[2]
exists if and only if

•

for each prime
p>2
, one has either
\ell(\mathcal{N}_{p})\leqslant r-2
or
\ell(\mathcal{N}_{p})=r-1

and, in the latter case,

\det\mathcal{N}_{p}=-2\mathopen{|}\mathcal{N}\mathclose{|}\bmod(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times\!})^{2}
;

•

either
\ell(\mathcal{N}_{2})\leqslant r-2
or
\ell(\mathcal{N}_{2})=r
and, in the letter case,
there is a vector
u\in\mathcal{N}_{2}
of order
2
and square
-\frac{1}{2}\bmod 2\mathbb{Z}

(hence, necessarily an orthogonal direct summand)
such that either

–

u
is not characteristic,
u\cdot v\neq v^{2}\bmod\mathbb{Z}
for some
v\in\mathcal{N}_{2}
of
order
2
, or

–

u
is characteristic and
\det u^{\perp}=\pm 2\mathopen{|}\mathcal{N}\mathclose{|}\bmod(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\times\!})^{2}
.

Assuming that
T\supset[2]
does not exist
, a lattice
T\supset\mathbf{U}(2)

exists if and only if

•

for each prime
p>2
, one has either
\ell(\mathcal{N}_{p})\leqslant r-3
or
\ell(\mathcal{N}_{p})=r-2

and, in the latter case,

\det\mathcal{N}_{p}=-\mathopen{|}\mathcal{N}\mathclose{|}\bmod(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times\!})^{2}
;

•

one has
\mathcal{N}_{2}\simeq\mathcal{U}_{2}\oplus\mathcal{N}_{2}^{\prime}
, where
\mathcal{U}_{2}:=\mathop{\operator@font discr}\nolimits\mathbf{U}(2)
;
in other words, there is a pair
u,v\in\mathcal{N}_{2}
such that

u^{2}=v^{2}=0\bmod 2\mathbb{Z}
and

u\cdot v=\frac{1}{2}\bmod\mathbb{Z}
.

To the Editor:

Desquamative interstitial pneumonia (DIP), recently termed alveolar macrophage pneumonia in the updated European Respiratory Society/American Thoracic Society classification, is a rare smoking-associated interstitial lung disease (ILD) characterised by diffuse intra-alveolar macrophage accumulation and variable interstitial remodelling [1]. Although macrophage accumulation represents the defining histopathologic feature, the immune pathways and epithelial injury programmes associated with DIP remain poorly characterised.

Transcriptomic data from histologically confirmed DIP lungs are extremely limited. A prior analysis of the publicly available dataset GSE32537 identified DIP as transcriptionally distinct among idiopathic interstitial pneumonias, but was constrained by substantial group-size imbalance (four DIP lungs versus 50 control lungs) and lack of demographic matching [2]. In addition, comprehensive single-cell RNA-sequencing (scRNA-seq) lung atlases were not available at the time of that study, limiting interpretation of disease-associated cell states [3, 4].

Therefore, we sought to characterise immune signalling pathways and epithelial cell states associated with DIP by reanalysing matched DIP and control lung transcriptomes and performing targeted histologic validation in independent human lung tissue specimens.

Lung transcriptomic data from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) dataset GSE32537 were analysed. GSE32537 was selected because it includes the largest number of histologically confirmed DIP samples currently available in a publicly accessible lung transcriptomic dataset, enabling exploratory analysis of molecular pathways associated with this rare disease. All DIP samples in the dataset were included (n=4; GSM806215, GSM806271, GSM806272, GSM806341). Four non-diseased control samples from the same dataset (GSM806372, GSM806385, GSM806384, GSM806382) were selected and matched for age, sex, and smoking status to minimise potential confounding [2].

Differential expression analysis was performed using GEO2R with Benjamini–Hochberg correction for multiple testing.

Preranked gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was conducted using curated pathway collections from the Molecular Signatures Database, including lung cell-state signatures derived from published human scRNA-seq atlases (e.g. Travaglini et al. [3]), together with a separately curated gene set representing pre-alveolar type 1 transitional state (PATS)-like epithelial cells [4]. PATS cells were originally described in mouse models of lung injury as intermediate epithelial cells arising during alveolar regeneration between alveolar type 2 (AT2) and alveolar type 1 (AT1) epithelial cells; corresponding PATS-like cells have subsequently been identified in human idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) lungs [4]. Similar transitional alveolar epithelial states termed aberrant basaloid cells, keratin 5 (KRT5)−/keratin 17 (KRT17)+ epithelial cells, keratin 8 (KRT8)+ alveolar differentiation intermediates, and damage-associated transient progenitors, have also been described in mouse and human lung diseases, including IPF, hypersensitivity pneumonitis, acute respiratory distress syndrome, and post-COVID pulmonary fibrosis [3–9].

Immunohistochemistry (CD68, CD20) and immunofluorescence (KRT5, KRT17) were performed on formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded lung tissue specimens from three patients with DIP and three non-diseased controls obtained from our institutions to validate transcriptomic findings at the tissue level.

Differential expression analysis identified 408 genes in DIP compared with matched controls (156 upregulated and 252 downregulated). Among the most upregulated genes were SPP1, CCL24, and IL1RN, whereas ANKRD1 and IL1R2 were among the most downregulated.

Hallmark GSEA demonstrated enrichment of proliferative and metabolic programmes in DIP lungs, including E2F targets, G2M checkpoint, and glycolysis, whereas control lungs showed relative enrichment of tumour necrosis factor (TNF)-α/NF-κB, transforming growth factor (TGF)-β, and Wnt/β-catenin signalling.

Reactome and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes pathway analyses further demonstrated enrichment of adaptive immune pathways in DIP, particularly B-cell receptor (BCR) signalling. Reactome pathway analysis identified enrichment of “Antigen activates BCR leading to generation of second messengers” (normalised enrichment score (NES)=2.37; false discovery rate (FDR) q<0.001) and “CD22-mediated BCR regulation” (NES=2.50; FDR q<0.001) (figure 1a). Gene sets related to cilium organisation, axoneme assembly, microtubule dynamics, and mitosis were also enriched in DIP lungs. Cell-type signature analysis demonstrated enrichment of dendritic cell, macrophage, monocyte, and B-cell transcriptional programmes, whereas signatures corresponding to AT1 and AT2 epithelial cells, endothelial cells, and fibroblasts were relatively depleted.

Based on these transcriptomic findings, histologic evaluation was performed in independent human lung tissue samples. Haematoxylin–eosin staining demonstrated intra-alveolar macrophage accumulation in DIP lungs compared with control lungs (figure 1b). Immunohistochemistry showed CD68-positive macrophages and CD20-positive B-cells in DIP lungs, with sparse staining in control lungs (figure 1c).

Cell-type signature analysis also demonstrated enrichment of a PATS-like epithelial gene signature in DIP lungs (NES=1.66; FDR q<0.001) (figure 1d, top). Leading-edge analysis identified 122 genes contributing to this enrichment signal, including KRT17, MMP7, CDKN2A, TSPAN1, WFDC2, CTSZ, and MUC20. The leading-edge subset also included ANXA4, which encodes annexin A4; circulating autoantibodies against annexin A4 have been reported in DIP [10].

Immunofluorescence demonstrated that KRT5−/KRT17+ epithelial cells were not detected in control lungs but were observed in DIP lungs (figure 1d, bottom).

This integrative transcriptomic and histologic analysis identifies prominent BCR-related immune signalling and transitional alveolar epithelial cell programmes in DIP.

The strong enrichment of CD22-mediated BCR regulation and the presence of CD20+ B-cells in an independent human lung tissue cohort are consistent with involvement of adaptive humoral immune pathways in DIP. Prior histopathologic studies have documented B-lymphocyte infiltration and lymphoid aggregates in DIP lung tissue [11], supporting activation of adaptive immune responses in this disease.

Concurrently, enrichment of PATS-like epithelial gene signatures, together with identification of KRT5−/KRT17+ epithelial cells in DIP lungs, indicates epithelial state remodelling that has not been well characterised in this disease. Transitional alveolar epithelial states similar to the PATS programme have been described across multiple forms of lung injury and fibrosis, where they are thought to represent intermediate epithelial repair states during alveolar regeneration.

The identification of ANXA4 within the leading-edge subset, together with a prior report of annexin A4 autoantibodies in DIP, suggests a potential interface between epithelial cell-state alterations and adaptive immune responses. Recent studies have identified autoantibodies targeting proteins expressed in transitional or aberrant epithelial cells, such as ABLIM1 [12] and vimentin [13], across multiple ILDs, including IPF, interstitial pneumonia with autoimmune features, connective tissue disease-associated ILD, hypersensitivity pneumonitis, and pleuroparenchymal fibroelastosis. In this context, our observation of ANXA4 expression in DIP raises the possibility that epithelial transitional states may contribute to antigenic targets of adaptive immune responses across a spectrum of ILDs, including DIP.

The relative depletion of AT1, AT2, endothelial, and fibroblast transcriptional signatures, together with enrichment of immune and transitional alveolar epithelial programmes, may reflect altered epithelial composition and stromal remodelling in DIP lungs.

In contrast to patterns observed in fibrosing ILDs such as IPF, canonical profibrotic pathways including TGF-β, Wnt/β-catenin, and TNF-α signalling were relatively less enriched in DIP. This pattern may reflect the comparatively limited fibrotic remodelling characteristic of DIP and may indicate predominance of alternative immune–epithelial remodelling programmes.

Although DIP sample size is limited by the rarity of this disease, the consistency of these findings across multiple pathway analyses and their concordance with histologic observations in independent human lung tissue support the robustness of the observed biological signatures.

Together, these findings support BCR signalling and transitional alveolar epithelial states as prominent molecular features of DIP and provide a cell-state-informed framework for understanding its pathobiology. These results suggest coordinated immune and epithelial alterations and support the possibility of immune–epithelial interactions as a basis for future investigation. The present study is hypothesis-generating and warrants validation in larger cohorts and mechanistic studies.

The S S -packing coloring of the infinite diagonal grid with S = ( 1 , 6 , 6 , … ) S=(1,6,6,\ldots)
Teeradej Kittipassorn, Peerawit Suriya

Abstract
For a non-decreasing sequence of positive integers S = ( a 1 , a 2 , … ) S=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots) , the S S -packing chromatic number of a graph G G is the smallest positive integer k k such that the vertices can be colored with k k colors, where the distance between any two distinct vertices of color i i is greater than a i a_{i} . In this paper, we show that the S S -packing chromatic number of the infinite diagonal grid P ∞ ⊠ P ∞ P_{\infty}\boxtimes P_{\infty} with S = ( 1 , 6 , 6 , … ) S=(1,6,6,\ldots) is 40 40 . This confirms a conjecture of the first author and Tiyajamorn.



1
Introduction

Graph coloring is one of the most well-known concepts in graph theory with many applications and open problems. A
proper coloring
of a graph
G
is an assignment of a color to each vertex such that the distance between any two distinct vertices of the same color is greater than
1
. The minimum number of colors is called the
chromatic number
of
G
. A natural question that arises when considering proper coloring is: what is the chromatic number of a given graph
G
?

For a non-decreasing sequence of positive integers
S=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots)
, the
S
-packing coloring
of a graph
G
is an assignment of a color to each vertex such that the distance between any two distinct vertices of color
i
is greater than
a_{i}
. The minimum number of colors is called the
S
-packing chromatic number
, denoted by
\chi_{S}(G)
. Note that a proper coloring is precisely an
S
-packing coloring with
S=(1,1,\ldots)
. In the case
S=(1,2,3,\ldots)
, this coloring is also known as the
packing coloring
, with
packing chromatic number
denoted by
\chi_{\rho}(G)
. This concept was first studied in 2008 by Goddard, S. M. Hedetniemi, S. T. Hedetniemi, Harris and Roll
[
3
]
.

An
S
-packing coloring has been studied in various types of graphs. Several graphs that have been studied is infinite. The
two-way infinite path

P_{\infty}
is a graph such that
V(P_{\infty})=\mathbb{Z}
and
\{u,v\}\in E(P_{\infty})
if
|u-v|=1
. The
infinite square grid

P_{\infty}\Box P_{\infty}
is a graph such that
V(P_{\infty}\Box P_{\infty})=\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
and
\{(x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\}\in E(P_{\infty}\Box P_{\infty})
if either
x=x^{\prime}
and
|y-y^{\prime}|=1
, or
y=y^{\prime}
and
|x-x^{\prime}|=1
. The
infinite diagonal grid

P_{\infty}\boxtimes P_{\infty}
is obtained from
P_{\infty}\Box P_{\infty}
by adding edges
\{(x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\}
if
|x-x^{\prime}|=|y-y^{\prime}|=1
.

In 2012, Goddard and Xu
[
12
]
studied the two-way infinite path
P_{\infty}
for many sequences
S
and obtained exact values and bounds for
\chi_{S}(P_{\infty})
. Goddard and Xu
[
4
]
also studied the infinite square grid
P_{\infty}\Box P_{\infty}
for many sequences in 2014. In the case
S=(1,2,3,\ldots)
, there have been many results on the infinite square grid since 2008
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
, improving the lower and upper bounds for
\chi_{\rho}(P_{\infty}\Box P_{\infty})
. In 2023, Subercaseaux and Heule
[
11
]
finally proved that
\chi_{\rho}(P_{\infty}\Box P_{\infty})=15
by using a SAT solver.

In 2017, Korže and Vesel
[
6
]
studied the case where
S
is of the form
a_{i}=d+\lfloor\tfrac{i-1}{n}\rfloor
, where
d
and
n
are small positive integers, in the infinite diagonal grid
P_{\infty}\boxtimes P_{\infty}
. Later, in 2024, the first author and Tiyajamorn
[
5
]
studied
S
-packing colorings of the infinite diagonal grid with
S=(i,k,k,\ldots)
. One of the incomplete cases is
i=1
with even
k
. They obtained lower and upper bounds for
\chi_{S}(P_{\infty}\boxtimes P_{\infty})
which are

\frac{3k^{2}}{4}+\frac{3k}{2}+3\leq\chi_{S}(P_{\infty}\boxtimes P_{\infty})\leq\left\lceil\frac{3k^{2}+7k+8}{4}\right\rceil.

For
k=2
and
k=4
, from the bounds, the exact values of
\chi_{S}(P_{\infty}\boxtimes P_{\infty})
are
9
and
21
, respectively. For
k=6
, their result implies that
39\leq\chi_{S}(P_{\infty}\boxtimes P_{\infty})\leq 40
. They conjectured that
\chi_{S}(P_{\infty}\boxtimes P_{\infty})=40
.

In this paper, we confirm the conjecture by showing that it is impossible to use only
39
colors for an
S
-packing coloring of
P_{\infty}\boxtimes P_{\infty}
with
S=(1,6,6,\ldots)
.

Theorem 1
.

If
S=(1,6,6,\ldots)
, then
\chi_{S}(P_{\infty}\boxtimes P_{\infty})=40
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we make basic observations. We introduce the main Lemma, Lemma
4
, in Section
3
. In Section
4
, we prove Theorem
1
using Observation
2
and Lemma
4
. Lemma
4
is proved in Section
5
. Finally, in Section
6
, we conclude the paper with some open problems.

We represent the graph

The distance between any two vertices is defined as the minimum number of steps between corresponding squares, where each step moves one square horizontally, vertically, or diagonally, like a king’s move in chess.

6
Concluding remarks

We have proved that
\chi_{S}(P_{\infty}\boxtimes P_{\infty})=40
for
S=(1,6,6,\ldots)
. However, the exact values of
\chi_{S}(P_{\infty}\boxtimes P_{\infty})
for
S=(1,k,k,\ldots)
with even
k\geq 8
are still unknown. The best known bounds of
\chi_{S}(P_{\infty}\boxtimes P_{\infty})
in these cases are

\frac{3k^{2}}{4}+\frac{3k}{2}+3\leq\chi_{S}(P_{\infty}\boxtimes P_{\infty})\leq\left\lceil\frac{3k^{2}+7k+8}{4}\right\rceil,

as shown in
[
5
]
. We conjecture that the exact value of
\chi_{S}(P_{\infty}\boxtimes P_{\infty})
in these cases is equal to the upper bound.

Conjecture 5
.

Let
k\geq 8
be even and
S=(1,k,k,\ldots)
. Then

\chi_{S}(P_{\infty}\boxtimes P_{\infty})=\left\lceil\frac{3k^{2}+7k+8}{4}\right\rceil.

It may be possible to generalize the approach in this paper to
k\geq 8
but one has to carefully choose the order of forbidden patterns of squares of color 1 to consider.

More generally, for sequences of the form
S=(i,k,k,\ldots)
, the problem of determining
\chi_{S}(P_{\infty}\boxtimes P_{\infty})
is open for the case when
i
satisfies
i\leq\lfloor\frac{k-3}{2}\rfloor
and
i+1\nmid k+1
(see
[
5
]
).

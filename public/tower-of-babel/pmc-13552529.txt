Colour Doppler-Guided Superthin Inferior Gluteal Artery Perforator Flap Elevation for Ischial Pressure Ulcer Reconstruction
Authors: Muhammed Said Aydin, Gökay Özler, Şükrü Kasap, Mustafa Nişanci
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryIschial pressure ulcer reconstruction requires durable coverage while preserving local donor options for possible recurrence. The inferior gluteal artery perforator (IGAP) flap is a muscle-sparing option, but superthin elevation requires precise knowledge of the superficial fascial plane and the subcutaneous course of the selected perforator. This technical note describes colour Doppler ultrasonography-guided superficial fascial plane mapping before superthin IGAP flap elevation in a 27-year-old paraplegic patient with a chronic left ischial pressure ulcer who presented in 2025 to a tertiary care university hospital in Muǧla, Turkey. Preoperative high-frequency colour Doppler ultrasonography identified the dominant perforator, measured the relevant fascial intervals and guided a controlled dissection above the superficial fascia. The technique allowed elevation of a thin, pliable flap while preserving deeper gluteal structures and perforator integrity. Colour Doppler-guided fascial mapping may support safer superthin perforator flap elevation in selected pressure ulcer reconstructions.Keywords: Pressure Ulcer, Perforator Flap, Doppler Color Ultrasonography, Ischium, Turkey

1. Introduction

Ischial pressure ulcers remain difficult reconstructive problems because they occur over a high-pressure bony prominence, often recur and may require more than one reconstruction during a patient's lifetime. Muscle and musculocutaneous flaps can provide bulk, but they may sacrifice functional tissue and reduce future donor options. The inferior gluteal artery perforator (IGAP) flap was introduced as a muscle-sparing option for ischial pressure sore reconstruction, and a clinical series supported its durability, design flexibility and low donor-site morbidity in selected patients.1,2,3,4 Anatomical studies of superior and inferior gluteal artery perforators also provide reliable landmarks for perforator flap design in the gluteal region.5

The superthin perforator flap concept refines this approach by elevating the flap above the superficial fascia, producing thin and pliable tissue while preserving deeper structures. The superficial fascial plane has been described as a reliable elevation plane for perforator flaps, and thin-elevation series have shown that this plane can reduce bulk and donor-site morbidity without compromising flap viability when the perforator is preserved.6,7 However, safe superthin elevation requires accurate knowledge of the perforator course within the superficial and deep adipofascial layers. High-resolution ultrasound-based planning has therefore become increasingly relevant for thin and superthin perforator flap surgery.8,9

This technical note describes a superthin modification of the inferior gluteal artery perforator flap based on superficial fascial plane dissection guided by colour Doppler ultrasonography, combined with controlled perforator preservation and 180° propeller rotation for ischial defect reconstruction. The novel aspect of this technical note is the use of high-frequency colour Doppler ultrasonography to integrate perforator mapping with patient-specific measurement of the superficial and deep fascial intervals, thereby defining the intended superthin elevation plane before incision rather than using Doppler solely for perforator localisation. This imaging-to-dissection workflow translates preoperative fascial anatomy directly into the operative plane, allowing controlled superthin IGAP elevation while preserving the perforator and deeper gluteal tissues.

2. Technical note

In 2025, a 27-year-old paraplegic female patient with a history of spinal cord agenesis presented to a tertiary care university hospital in Muǧla, Turkey, with a chronic left ischial pressure ulcer that had persisted despite conservative wound care and pressure off-loading. As durable coverage was required while preserving local muscle and deeper gluteal tissues, a superthin IGAP flap was planned. The operative aim was to use ultrasound-defined fascial landmarks to elevate a thin, preservation-based flap safely.

Preoperative imaging was performed with a handheld high-frequency colour Doppler ultrasonography device (Clarius L20 HD3, Clarius Mobile Health Corp., Vancouver, Canada; 20-MHz linear probe). The scan was used to identify a dominant perforator and follow its trajectory toward the skin. In B-mode imaging, the superficial fascia, deep fascia and intervening adipofascial layer were assessed before incision. The skin-to-superficial fascia distance was 5.6 mm, and the distance between the superficial and deep fascia was 4.8 mm. These measurements provided a layered anatomical map for flap design and defined the deep interval intended for preservation during elevation [Fig. 1].

Preoperative colour Doppler ultrasonography assessment of the perforator and fascial planes. A: High-frequency colour Doppler ultrasonography showing a dominant perforator and visualisation of its trajectory toward the overlying skin. B: Ultrasound-based measurement of tissue planes showing the distance from the skin to the superficial fascia (arrow; D1 [first green dotted-line] = 5.6 mm) and the distance between the superficial and deep fascia (D3 [third green dotted-line] = 4.8 mm). The D3 interval between the superficial and deep fascial layers was used as the deep interval to be preserved during superthin flap elevation.

After standard debridement and preparation of the ulcer bed, flap elevation was started in the superficial fascial plane. Dissection proceeded under direct vision toward the preoperatively mapped perforator. The ultrasound-defined deep interval was preserved, and all structures beneath the superficial fascia were maintained whenever possible. The dominant perforator was protected while the flap was elevated as a thin, pliable fasciocutaneous unit suitable for pressure ulcer coverage [Fig. 2]. The reconstructed defect measured 5 × 4 × 3 cm. A fasciocutaneous superthin IGAP flap measuring 12 × 6 cm was designed according to the defect dimensions and perforator location. A single dominant inferior gluteal artery perforator with a diameter of 0.6 mm was identified. The perforator was dissected for at least 2 cm to ensure adequate mobility and to minimise the risk of kinking or torsion during flap rotation. The flap was then rotated 180° clockwise as a propeller flap to achieve tension-free coverage of the ischial defect. The flap was inset without intentional sacrifice of the underlying structures located deep to the superficial fascia [Fig. 3].

The patient was followed for 12 months postoperatively. No early or late complications, including wound dehiscence, infection, flap necrosis or pressure ulcer recurrence, were observed during the follow-up period. A representative postoperative photograph obtained at 9 months demonstrated complete wound healing, stable soft-tissue coverage and satisfactory contour of the reconstructed ischial region [Fig. 4].

3. Discussion

The key technical point in this article is the use of colour Doppler ultrasonography not only to mark the cutaneous perforator point, but also to map the superficial fascial plane and the adipofascial interval that should be respected during superthin elevation. Conventional handheld Doppler can identify signals, but it does not reliably show the course, depth or fascial relationship of a perforator. By contrast, colour Doppler ultrasonography can show the perforator trajectory and adjacent soft-tissue layers. This advantage has been reported in thoracodorsal artery perforator planning, lower-extremity perforator flap planning, anterolateral thigh flap imaging and broader lower-extremity flap planning studies.10,11,12,13

Superthin flap elevation is particularly dependent on this information. Reports using ultrahigh-frequency ultrasound and colour Doppler planning have shown that preoperative imaging can anticipate superficial microvascular anatomy and help surgeons understand the horizontal course of perforators in the deep adipofascial layer.8,9 Applying the same logic to an IGAP flap for ischial pressure ulcer reconstruction allows the surgeon to identify the perforator and plan the plane of dissection before incision. This may reduce unnecessary deep dissection, protect the vascular pedicle and help preserve future reconstructive options in patients at risk of recurrence.

For ischial pressure ulcers, donor-site conservation is clinically important. Series using IGAP, posterior thigh and reusable perforator-preserving gluteal flaps show that recurrent or multiple pressure ulcers often require staged decision-making and careful preservation of available tissue.2,3,4,14 Modifications of gluteal artery perforator flaps have also been proposed to simplify dissection and maintain reliable coverage in pressure sore reconstruction.15 In the present technique, colour Doppler-defined fascial mapping complements these reconstructive principles by allowing a thinner flap to be raised with intentional preservation of deep gluteal structures.

This report describes a single technical application and cannot establish superiority over standard IGAP flap elevation. The method also depends on operator familiarity with perforator ultrasound and correct interpretation of superficial fascial anatomy. Nevertheless, the required equipment is portable, non-invasive and repeatable, and the information obtained directly informs flap design and dissection strategy.

4. Conclusion

Colour Doppler ultrasonography-guided superficial fascial plane mapping can support controlled superthin IGAP flap elevation for ischial pressure ulcer reconstruction. By defining the perforator course and relevant fascial intervals preoperatively, this technique may help surgeons preserve deep gluteal tissues while maintaining perforator integrity and achieving thin, pliable defect coverage.

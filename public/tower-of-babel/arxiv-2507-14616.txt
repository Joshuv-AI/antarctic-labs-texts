1.
Introduction

For a group
G
with two given compact Gelfand subgroups
H
and
K
and an irreducible representation
\pi
of
G
, let
v_{H}
(resp.
v_{K}
) denote an
H
-invariant (resp.
K
-invariant) vector of unit norm in a given
G
-invariant Hermitian inner product
\langle~,~\rangle_{\pi}
on
\pi
. The correlation coefficient of
\pi
with respect to
H
and
K
is then defined as
[
Gro91
, §8, §9]

c(\pi;H,K)=|\langle v_{H},v_{K}\rangle_{\pi}|^{2}.

Since
\langle~,~\rangle_{\pi}
is unique up to scalar multiplication, and since
v_{H}
and
v_{K}
are unique up to a complex number of absolute value
1
(in a chosen invariant inner product), it follows that
c(\pi;H,K)
is well-defined.

In this paper, we study the correlation coefficient
c(\pi;H,K)
of an irreducible complex representation
\pi
of
G={\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
with respect to a split torus
H
and a non-split torus
K
. This was the theme in
[
AJ22
]
as well, where the embeddings of the two tori were chosen carefully so that the pair
(H,K)
inside
G
is unique up to conjugacy. Consequently, the value of the correlation coefficient
c(\pi;H,K)
is independent of such compatible pairs
(H,K)
. In this situation, the vanishing of the correlation coefficient is closely related to the root number of
\pi
. When
\pi
admits fixed vectors for both
H
and
K
, its root number is
\pm 1
and it is necessary that the root number is
1
for
c(\pi;H,K)\neq 0
. When
q=p
, this condition is also sufficient
[
AJ22
, Corollary 1.2]
. Thus, roughly half the time the correlation vanishes.

The present work is inspired by
[
BHKO24
, §7.1.1]
where the local newform is shown to be a test vector for a local unramified toric period for irreducible supercuspidal representations of
{\rm PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, where the novelty is that the embedding of the unramified torus in
{\rm PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
depends only on the conductor of the representation. This is done for conductor (which is necessarily even)
\geq 4
. Moreover, it is shown that the toric period is an
\ell
-adic unit for a given prime
\ell\neq p
. In the case of conductor two, the problem may be reformulated to that of simultaneous nonvanishing of the correlation coefficient
c(\pi;H,K)
for all irreducible cuspidal representations
\pi
of
{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
for a choice of
K
with
H
being the standard diagonal embedding of the split torus. Motivated by these considerations, it is natural to ask the following question.

Question 1
.

Fix
H
to be the standard diagonal embedding of the split torus in
{\rm PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Is there an embedding
K
of the non-split torus such that the correlation coefficient
c(\pi;H,K)
is nonvanishing for all irreducible representations
\pi
of
{\rm PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
? And such that this is a
p
-adic unit?

With this in mind, we compute the correlation coefficient of
\pi
with respect to
H
and a suitable conjugate of
K
considered in
[
AJ22
]
by a unipotent element as in [BHKO24]. Thus, let
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times 2}
and let

K=K_{\alpha}=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\alpha z\\
z&1\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\alpha\\
1&0\end{array}\right)\mid z\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\right\}.

Let
u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and let

\displaystyle K_{\alpha,u}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&u\\
0&1\end{array}\right)K_{\alpha}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&-u\\
0&1\end{array}\right).

Unlike in
[
AJ22
]
, where the pair
(H,K_{\alpha})
was carefully chosen in order for
c(\pi;H,K_{\alpha})
to be independent of
\alpha
, and in this case roughly half the time the correlation was zero, now we should expect the answer to depend on both
\alpha
and
u
.

We fix an identification of
\mathbb{C}
with
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
and consider the representations as defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
. Let
\mathcal{O}_{m}\subset\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
denote the ring of integers of the unramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
of degree
m
. Let
\chi
be a character of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
that reduces mod
p
to the identity character of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Similarly, let
\psi
be a character of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}
that reduces mod
p
to the identity character of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}
. The unique quadratic character of
{\rm PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is given by
\eta=\chi^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\circ\det
. An irreducible principal series representation, up to isomorphism, of
{\rm PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is of the form
{\rm Ps}(\chi^{r},\chi^{-r})
, where
1\leq r\leq\frac{q-3}{2}
. Irreducible cuspidal representations of
{\rm PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, up to isomorphism, are parametrized by
\psi^{(q-1)(r+1)}
, where
0\leq r\leq\frac{q-3}{2}
. These
(q-2)
representations and the twisted Steinberg representation St
\otimes\eta
(and of course the trivial representation) are precisely the representations that admit both
H
and
K
-invariant vectors. Moreover, for these representations, we have multiplicity one for both the invariant spaces. When
\pi={\rm St}\otimes\eta
, we take
r=(q-1)/2
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\pi=\pi_{r}
be an irreducible representation of
{\rm PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
admitting fixed vectors for both
H
and
K
. Then, we have
c(\pi_{r};H,K_{\alpha,u})\in\mathcal{O}_{m}
and

c(\pi_{r};H,K_{\alpha,u})\equiv[P_{r}(u/\sqrt{\alpha})]^{2}\mod p,

where
P_{r}(X)\in\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{2}][X]
is the
r
-th Legendre polynomial.

Thus, an affirmative answer to Question
1
reduces to the following expectation.

There exists
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times 2}
such that

(x^{2}-c)
is not a factor of any of
P_{r}(x)
mod
p
for
0\leq r\leq(q-1)/2
.

We verify the above expectation when
m\geq 2
and thus obtain the following theorem as a corollary to Theorem
1.1
(see §
5
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
q=p^{m}
with
m\geq 2
. There exists
u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
such that
c(\pi;H,K_{\alpha,u})\in\mathcal{O}_{m}^{\times}
for all the irreducible representations
\pi
of
{\rm PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
admitting fixed vectors for both
H
and
K
.

For the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we closely follow
[
AJ22
, §5]
(see also
[
Vat23
, Theorem 7.2]
). The key non-trivial ingredient in
[
AJ22
]
is the mod
p
reduction of irreducible representations of
{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}

[
Dia07
]
which is the approach as well in
[
Vat23
, Theorem 7.2]
.

Legendre polynomials classically appear in representation theory via the following result due to Vilenkin and Dieudonné
[
Bry92
, Proposition 1.1]
: in the representation

{\rm Sym}^{2r}\mathbb{R}^{2}\otimes\det{{}^{-r}}
of
{\rm PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, the matrix coefficient corresponding to

g=[a,b;c,d]
and
v=x^{r}y^{r}
is given by
P_{r}(ad+bc/ad-bc)
. Clubbing this result with Theorem
1.1
, we can derive new identities for Legendre polynomials (see Equations (
11
) and (
12
)).

We end the introduction by noting that the problem of factorising Legendre polynomials modulo
p
is intimately related to number theory via Hasse invariants of elliptic curves and in turn to class numbers of imaginary quadratic number fields. We refer to
[
BM04
]
and the references therein for further details. In §
6
, we discuss this theme in some detail and derive two identities in Lemma
6.1
.

2.
Recap of the earlier work

In this section we summarize the strategy employed in
[
AJ22
]
. Let
q=p^{m}
and let
r\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
0\leq r\leq q
. Write

r=r_{0}+r_{1}p+\dots+r_{m-1}p^{m-1}

with
0\leq r_{i}\leq p-1
for
0\leq i\leq m-1
. Let
{\rm Sym}^{r_{i}}\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{2}
denote the
r_{i}
-th symmetric power representation of the standard representation of
{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Then

\rho_{r}={\rm Sym}^{r_{0}}\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{2}\otimes{\rm Sym}^{r_{1}}\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{2}\circ{\rm Frob}\otimes\dots\otimes{\rm Sym}^{r_{m-1}}\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{2}\circ{\rm Frob}^{m-1}

{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, where Frob is the Frobenius morphism. Any irreducible mod
p

The Jordan-Hölder constituents of the reduction mod
p
of irreducible representations (over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}

{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
are computed in
[

. As mentioned in the introduction, the

-representations of interest to us are of the form

or

or
{\rm St}\otimes\eta
. The Steinberg representation remains irreducible on reduction mod
p
, whereas in the principal series and cuspidal cases, generically there are
2^{m}
Jordan-Hölder factors in their mod
p
reduction and these are indexed by subsets of

. Among these factors there is precisely one which admits fixed vectors for
H
and
K
and this was isolated in
[
AJ22
, Proposition 4.2 & Proposition 4.4]
which we now state.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\pi_{r}={\rm Ps}(\chi^{r},\chi^{-r})
or
\pi(\psi^{(q-1)(r+1)})
. Write
r=r_{0}+r_{1}p+\dots+r_{m-1}p^{m-1}
with
0\leq r_{i}\leq p-1
for
0\leq i\leq m-1
. Let
a_{0}+a_{1}p+\dots+a_{m-1}p^{m-1}
be the
p
-adic expansion of
2r
(resp.
2r+1
) when
\pi_{r}={\rm Ps}(\chi^{r},\chi^{-r})
(resp.
\pi_{r}=\pi(\psi^{(q-1)(r+1)})
). Let

be defined as

J=\left\{i\mid a_{i}=2r_{i}\mbox{~or~}2r_{i}+1\right\}.

Then the component in the mod
p
reduction of
\pi
corresponding to

is

\left(\bigotimes_{i\in J}\left({\rm Sym}^{2r_{i}}\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{2}\otimes\det{{}^{-r_{i}}}\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{2}\right)\circ{\rm Frob}^{i}\right)\bigotimes\left(\bigotimes_{i\notin J}\left({\rm Sym}^{2p-2-2r_{i}}\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{2}\otimes\det{{}^{r_{i}}}\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{2}\right)\circ{\rm Frob}^{i}\right).

The analysis carried out in
[
AJ22
]
has an inherent symmetry between
r
and
p-1-r
and therefore it suffices to
look at representations of the form

Suppose
q=p
and let

\rho={\rm Sym}^{2r}\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{2}\otimes\det{{}^{-r}}\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{2},

where

\rho
on the basis

\rho\left(\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\

AJ22
]
. Let

H=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&0\\

Let

The suppression of the subscript

3.
Legendre Polynomials

As is well-known, these polynomials can be defined in various ways. For instance, via a generating function

\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-2xt+t^{2}}}=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}P_{r}(x)t^{r},

or via a recursion formula (with
P_{0}(x)=1,P_{1}(x)=x
)

(r+1)P_{r+1}(x)=(2r+1)xP_{r}(x)-rP_{r-1}(x),

or as a solution of the differential equation

(1-x^{2})y^{\prime\prime}-2xy^{\prime}+r(r+1)y=0,

or by applying the Gram-Schmidt process to the standard basis for
\mathbb{R}[x]
with respect to
\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{-1}^{1}f(x)g(x)dx
.
There are many explicit expressions for these polynomials out of which we write down two, which will be of use to us in this paper.
Let

S_{k}=\begin{cases}\{0,2,\dots,k\}&\text{if $k$ is even,}\\
\{1,3,\dots,k\}&\text{if $k$ is odd.}\end{cases}

We have

(3)

P_{r}(x)=\frac{1}{2^{r}}\displaystyle{\sum_{i\in S_{r}}}(-1)^{\frac{r-i}{2}}{r\choose(r-i)/2}{r+i\choose i}x^{i},

and

(4)

P_{r}(x)=\frac{1}{2^{r}}\displaystyle{\sum_{i\in S_{r}}}(-1)^{\frac{r-i}{2}}{r\choose i}{r-i\choose(r-i)/2}(1-x^{2})^{(r-i)/2}(2x)^{i}.

They satisfy the following two congruences (see
[
BM04
, p. 81]
,
[
Bry92
, Corollary 1.3]
). The Ille-Schur congruence states that if
r=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}r_{i}p^{i}
, then

(5)

P_{r}(x)\equiv\prod_{i=0}^{m-1}P_{r_{i}}(x)^{p^{i}}\mod p.

There is also the symmetry around
(p-1)/2
given by

(6)

P_{p-1-r}(x)\equiv P_{r}(x)\mod p.

4.

following the strategy in
[
AJ22
]
recalled in §

for

(see Section

4.1.
The case

Let

\displaystyle Y_{u}\cdot x^{r}y^{r}

\displaystyle=\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u&\alpha-u^{2}\\

\displaystyle=\left[(-\alpha)^{p-1-r}(ux+y)^{r}((\alpha-u^{2})x-uy)^{r}+\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\frac{((1+uz)x+zy)^{r}((\alpha z-u^{2}z)x+(1-uz)y)^{r}}{(1-\alpha z^{2})^{r}}\right].

Equating this expression to

and looking at the coefficient of

\displaystyle t_{r}(u,\alpha)

\displaystyle=(-\alpha)^{p-1-r}u^{r}+\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\frac{z^{r}(1+uz)^{r}}{(1-\alpha z^{2})^{r}}

\displaystyle=(-\alpha)^{p-1-r}u^{r}+\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}(z^{-1}-\alpha z)^{p-1-r}(1+uz)^{r}

\displaystyle=-\displaystyle{\sum_{j\in S_{p-1-r}}}(-1)^{(p-1-r-j)/2}{p-1-r\choose(p-1-r-j)/2}{r\choose j}\alpha^{(p-1-r-j)/2}u^{j}

where, as in §

S_{k}=\begin{cases}\{0,2,\dots,k\}&\text{if $k$ is even,}\\
\{1,3,\dots,k\}&\text{if $k$ is odd.}\end{cases}

Note that when

(cf. (

it follows from (

(7)

t_{r}(u,\alpha)\equiv-(-1)^{p-1-r}2^{p-1-r}\alpha^{(p-1-r)/2}P_{p-1-r}(u/\sqrt{\alpha})\mod p.

Next we compute
s_{r}(u,\alpha)
. We have

X\cdot v_{K_{\alpha,u}}=s_{r}(u,\alpha)\cdot v_{H}.

Note that

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right)\cdot(\alpha x^{2}-y^{2})^{r}=(\alpha ax^{2}-a^{-1}y^{2})^{r},

and therefore

\displaystyle X\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&u\\
0&1\end{array}\right)v_{K}

\displaystyle=-\displaystyle{\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&au\\
0&1\end{array}\right)(\alpha ax^{2}-a^{-1}y^{2})^{r}

\displaystyle=-\displaystyle{\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}}(\alpha ax^{2}-a^{-1}(aux+y)^{2})^{r}

\displaystyle=-\displaystyle{\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}}((\alpha-u^{2})ax^{2}-2uxy-a^{-1}y^{2})^{r}

\displaystyle=s_{r}(u,\alpha)\cdot x^{r}y^{r},

where, noting that
i
and
r
are of the same parity,

s_{r}(u,\alpha)=(-1)^{r}\displaystyle{\sum_{i\in S_{r}}}(-1)^{(r-i)/2}{r\choose i}{r-i\choose(r-i)/2}(\alpha-u^{2})^{(r-i)/2}(2u)^{i}.

In the above computation we started with a negative sign to accommodate
|H|=p-1
in the definition of
X
.
Note that when
u=0
, we have
s_{r}(u,\alpha)=s
(cf. (
1
)). It follows from (
4
) that

(8)

s_{r}(u,\alpha)\equiv(-1)^{r}2^{r}\alpha^{r/2}P_{r}(u/\sqrt{\alpha})\mod p.

As outlined in §
2
, the correlation coefficient is the product
s_{r}(u,\alpha)t_{r}(u,\alpha)
. Thus, combining the identities (
7
) and (
8
), and making use of (
6
), we have proved:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible representation of
{\rm PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
admitting fixed vectors for both
H
and
K_{\alpha,u}
. Thus,
\pi={\rm Ps}(\chi^{r},\chi^{-r})
or
\pi=\pi(\psi^{(p-1)(r+1)})
or
\pi={\rm St}\otimes\eta
in which case we take
r=(p-1)/2
. Then the correlation coefficient
c(\pi;H,K_{\alpha,u})
belongs to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
and

c(\pi;H,K_{\alpha,u})\equiv[P_{r}(u/\sqrt{\alpha})]^{2}\mod p.

Remark 1
.

The symmetry around
(p-1)/2
stated as (
6
) can in fact be seen as follows. If
\pi
is thought of as a representation of
{\rm PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
over
\mathbb{C}
then the complex numbers
s
and
t
determined by the corresponding averaging operators on
\pi
satisfies
\overline{s}=t

[
AJ22
, §2]
. Since complex conjugation, after an identification with
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
, corresponds to the involution

r\mapsto p-1-r
on the weights in reduction mod
p
it follows that one of them is obtained by the other.

Remark 2

(Archimedean analogue)
.

The proof of Proposition
4.1
is purely algebraic and it goes through for finite dimensional representations
\pi_{r}={\rm Sym}^{2r}\mathbb{R}^{2}\otimes\det^{-r}
of
{\rm PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Thus we get, for
H=\mathbb{R}^{\times}
,
K=\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and
u\in\mathbb{R}
,
|\langle v_{H},v_{K_{u}}\rangle|^{2}=P_{r}(\imath u)^{2}
.

4.2.
The case
r_{i}=0
for
i\geq 1

In this subsection,
q=p^{m}
but we assume
r_{i}=0
for
i\geq 1
. Now
u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times 2}
. Denoting
r_{0}
as
r
, everything goes through in §
4.1
with

(9)

t_{r}(u,\alpha)\equiv-(-1)^{q-1-r}2^{q-1-r}\alpha^{(q-1-r)/2}P_{q-1-r}(u/\sqrt{\alpha})\mod p.

instead of (

). The factor
s_{r}(u,\alpha)
has the same expression which we state again:

(10)

s_{r}(u,\alpha)\equiv(-1)^{r}2^{r}\alpha^{r/2}P_{r}(u/\sqrt{\alpha})\mod p.

Thus,

s_{r}(u,\alpha)t_{r}(u,\alpha)=P_{r}(u/\sqrt{\alpha})P_{q-1-r}(u/\sqrt{\alpha}).

Since

, we have
q-1-r=(p-1-r)+\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}(p-1)p^{i}
and therefore by (

P_{q-1-r}(x)\equiv P_{r}(x)\mod p
. Thus, once again,

4.3.
The general case

We follow the strategy in
[
AJ22
, §5.2]
. As mentioned in §
2
, for
q=p^{m}
, we are led to analyse a representation of
{\rm PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
of the form

\rho_{r}=\bigotimes_{i=0}^{m-1}\left({\rm Sym}^{2r_{i}}\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{2}\otimes\det{{}^{-r_{i}}}\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{2}\right)\circ{\rm Frob}^{i}.

In this case, we have

v_{H}=\bigotimes_{i=0}^{m-1}x_{i}^{r_{i}}y_{i}^{r_{i}}

and

v_{K_{\alpha,u}}=\bigotimes_{i=0}^{m-1}(\alpha^{p^{i}}x_{i}^{2}-(u^{p^{i}}x_{i}+y_{i})^{2})^{r_{i}}.

With
X,Y_{u},s_{r}(u,\alpha),t_{r}(u,\alpha)
defined as earlier and by denoting the corresponding scalars for the

and

, we get

\displaystyle s_{r}(u,\alpha)t_{r}(u,\alpha)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=0}^{m-1}s_{i}(u,\alpha)^{p^{i}}t_{i}(u,\alpha)^{p^{i}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=0}^{m-1}[P_{r_{i}}(u/\sqrt{\alpha})]^{2p^{i}}

\displaystyle=[P_{r}(u/\sqrt{\alpha})]^{2}

where the last step is a consequence of the Ille-Schur congruence (cf. (

)). This proves Theorem
1.1
.

5.
Simultaneous nonvanishing

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
in which we have assumed that
m\geq 2
. In order to get simultaneous nonvanishing of the correlation coefficient for all the irreducible representations with fixed vectors for
H
and
K
, we need to exhibit
u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that

P_{r}(u/\sqrt{\alpha})\not\equiv 0\mod p

for all
0\leq r\leq(q-1)/2
. By the Ille-Schur and symmetry congruences (
5
) and (
6
) for Legendre polynomials,
P_{r}(x)\mod p
, for any
r
, is completely determined by
P_{i}(x)\mod p
for
0\leq i\leq(p-1)/2
. Thus, to prove Theorem
1.2
, we only need to note that the product of these
(p-1)/2
polynomials, say,

P(x)=\prod_{i=0}^{(p-1)/2}P_{i}(x)

does not have a factor of the form
(x^{2}-c)
for some
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times 2}
. Indeed, if this is the case then we can take
u=\sqrt{\alpha c}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. As these polynomials have terms only of even degree or only of odd degree, considering
P(x)/x
if necessary, we can treat
P(x)
as a polynomial in
x^{2}
. Note that the degree of
P(t=x^{2})
is
\lfloor(p^{2}-1)/16\rfloor
and
\mathbb{F}_{q}
has
(p^{m}-1)/2
quadratic non-residues and thus we are done if
m\geq 2
.

6.
Legendre Polynomials mod
p

As mentioned in §
1
, the reduction modulo
p
of Legendre polynomials is connected to number theory
[
BM04
]
. More specifically, the reduction mod
p
of the Legendre polynomial in degree
(p-1)/2
, the one corresponding to the correlation coefficient for the Steinberg representation of
{\rm PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
, is related to the Hasse invariant for elliptic curves. It is known that this particular Legendre polynomial has only linear and quadratic factors in its reduction modulo
p
and there are precise formulas of the number of factors of each type which involve the class number of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-p})

[
BM04
, Theorem 1]
.

In this section, we make a few observations about Legendre polynomials which are consequences of Theorem
1.1
.

For the first set of observations, we combine Theorem
1.1
with the result due to Vilenkin and Dieudonné mentioned in the introduction. Applying this result to each element in
K_{\alpha,u}
and taking the sum, on the one side we get the correlation coefficient and on the other side a sum of
(p+1)
many explicit matrix coefficients. This leads to the identity

(11)

P_{r}(x)^{2}\equiv 1+P_{r}(2x^{2}-1)+2\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times 2}}P_{r}\left(\frac{y+1-2x^{2}}{y-1}\right)\mod p,

where the sum ranges over quadratic non-residues in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
.
Expanding
1/(y-1)
as
(y-1)^{p-2}
and taking the sum over quadratic non-residues we get the identity

P_{r}(x)^{2}=\sum_{i\in S_{r}}a_{i}Q_{i}(2x^{2}-1)

where
P_{r}(x)=\sum_{i\in S_{r}}a_{i}x^{i}
and

Q_{i}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{i}(-1)^{j}{i\choose j}{i-j-\frac{1}{2}\choose i}x^{j}.

This is initially obtained modulo
p
but it lifts to characteristic zero. Since,
Q_{i}(x)=x^{i}P_{i}(\frac{x+x^{-1}}{2})
(see
[
Kel59
, p. 1]
), we get:

(12)

P_{r}(x)^{2}=\sum_{i\in S_{r}}a_{i}(\sqrt{2x^{2}-1})^{i}P_{i}\left(\frac{x^{2}}{\sqrt{2x^{2}-1}}\right).

The approach of
[
BM04
]
gives information on reduction modulo
p
also of
P_{r}(x)
for
r=(p-e)/k
for
k=3,4
where
p\equiv e\mod k
. This is because of certain congruences between these Legendre polynomials and the one for
(p-1)/2

[
BM04
, p. 81]
. In degrees other than these, there does not seem to be any pattern in the reduction modulo
p
of
P_{r}(x)
.

As a consequence of the present work, it is easy to derive the following two identities. Probably these are well-known.

Lemma 6.1
.

Modulo p, we have

(1)

\displaystyle{\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}}P_{r}(x)=\left(\frac{-2}{p}\right)(x-1)^{(p-1)/2}
,

(2)

\displaystyle{\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}}P_{r}(x)^{2}=(x^{2}-1)^{(p-1)/2}
.

The main ingredient in proving the second identity is the character relation stated in Lemma
2.2
. For the first identity, we need an appropriately modified version of
[
AJ22
, Proposition 7.1]
which we recall below.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
v_{H}
and
v_{K}
denote respectively the
H
and
K
fixed vectors (normalized) in the principal series representation
\pi={\rm Ps}(\chi,\chi^{-1})
of
{\rm PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times 2}
. Then, we have

\langle v_{H},\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&u\\
0&1\end{array}\right)\cdot v_{K}\rangle_{\pi}=-\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\chi\left(\frac{\alpha-(\lambda+u)^{2}}{\lambda}\right).

Proof of Lemma
6.1
.

Modulo
p
, the left hand side of the identity in Proposition
6.2
is (cf. (
8
))

s_{r}(u,\alpha)=(-1)^{r}2^{r}\alpha^{r/2}P_{r}(u/\sqrt{\alpha}),

if
\chi
mod
p
is the
r
-th power of the identity character of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
. Now,

\displaystyle-\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}\chi\left(\frac{\alpha-(\lambda+u)^{2}}{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}\left(\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}}\chi\left(\frac{\alpha-(\lambda+u)^{2}}{\lambda}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0&\text{$4u+4\alpha+1\notin\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times 2}$,}\\
-(p-1)&\text{$4u+4\alpha+1=0$,}\\
-2(p-1)&\text{$4u+4\alpha+1\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times 2}$.}\end{cases}

Thus,

\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}s_{r}(u,\alpha)=-1+\sum_{r=0}^{p-2}s_{r}(u,\alpha)=(4u+4\alpha+1)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}.

We may treat this as a formal identity and plug in
\alpha=1/4
to get

\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}P_{r}(-2u)=(4u+2)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}.

Put
x=-2u
and the first identity follows.

For the proof of the second identity, we make use of the Gelfand-Graev representation of
{\rm PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. It is well-known that this representation has only two non-zero character values and these are given by
(q^{2}-1)
at the identity and
-1
at the (unique) unipotent conjugacy class. It can be checked that the cardinality of

\left\{(h,k_{\alpha})\mid h\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&u\\
0&1\end{array}\right)k_{\alpha}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&-u\\
0&1\end{array}\right)\mbox{~is unipotent}\right\}

is given by (cf.
[
AJ22
, Remark 17]
)

\begin{cases}q-1&\text{if $p\equiv 1\mod 4$ ~\&~ $\alpha-u^{2}\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times 2}$,}\\
q-3&\text{if $p\equiv 3\mod 4$ ~\&~ $\alpha-u^{2}\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times 2}$,}\\
q-3&\text{if $p\equiv 1\mod 4$ ~\&~ $\alpha-u^{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times 2}$,}\\
q-1&\text{if $p\equiv 3\mod 4$ ~\&~ $\alpha-u^{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times 2}$.}\end{cases}

Thus, the character double sum on the right hand side of the identity in Lemma
2.2
for the Gelfand-Graev representation gives the value

\begin{cases}q/(q+1)&\text{in Cases I and IV,}\\
(q^{2}-q+2)/(q^{2}-1)&\text{in Cases II and III. }\end{cases}

Since the Gelfand-Graev representation contains every irreducible representation other than
1
and
\eta
with multiplicity one, and noting that the Steinberg representation does not have a
K
-fixed vector, it follows again from Lemma
2.2
that

\sum_{\pi}|\langle v_{H},v_{K_{\alpha,u}}\rangle|_{\pi}^{2}=\begin{cases}0&\text{in Cases I and IV,}\\
-2&\text{in Cases II and III,}\end{cases}

modulo
p
. By Theorem
1.1
, the left hand side is

P_{\frac{p-1}{2}}(u/\sqrt{\alpha})^{2}+2\sum_{r=1}^{(p-3)/2}P_{r}(u/\sqrt{\alpha})^{2}

and therefore it follows from (
6
) that

\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}P_{r}(u/\sqrt{\alpha})^{2}=-(u^{2}-\alpha)^{(p-1)/2}.

Taking
x=u/\sqrt{\alpha}
and noting that
\alpha^{\frac{p-1}{2}}=-1
, we get the second identity.
∎

A culturally adapted online Mediterranean diet intervention for metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (TIMA): study protocol for a randomized controlled trial

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe Mediterranean diet (MedDiet) is widely recognized for its beneficial effects on hepatic steatosis. However, optimal strategies to support dietary adherence among patients with metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), particularly in non-Mediterranean regions where the MedDiet is not habitual, remain underexplored.ObjectiveTo evaluate the effectiveness of a culturally adapted MedDiet for MASLD through the implementation of real-time video counseling combined with behavior change techniques (BCTs).MethodsThis is a 12-week, single-center, two-arm, parallel-group randomized controlled trial registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (identifier: NCT06503120). Adults who fulfill the clinical diagnosis of MASLD and have ≥ 5% hepatic fat content on MRI-PDFF will be randomly assigned to receive either an isocaloric culturally adapted MedDiet intervention or standard care. The intervention comprises two components. First, participants will receive four structured real-time video counseling sessions (15–20 min each) at weeks 0, 3, 6, and 9. These sessions will be delivered by a registered dietitian via an online platform, guided by each participant’s 3-day photographic food diary and a Taiwanese version Mediterranean Diet Adherence Screener scores to provide personalized dietary advice. Second, participants will receive weekly text messages underpinned by BCTs to address individual dietary gaps and reinforce adherence. The primary outcome is the relative change in intrahepatic fat content, assessed by MRI-PDFF at week 12. Secondary outcomes include the changes in liver enzymes, lipid profile, liver stiffness, serum metabolites, gut microbiota composition, dietary adherence, quality of life, and anthropometric measures.DiscussionThis is the first randomized controlled trial to evaluate a fully remote, dietitian-led culturally adapted MedDiet intervention using real-time video counseling and text-based BCTs in patients with MASLD. This approach may support scalable, patient-centered dietary strategies for improving liver and metabolic health in this population.Trial registrationClinicalTrials.gov NCT06503120. Introduction

Background and rationale {6a}

Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), formerly known as non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, has emerged as the most prevalent chronic liver condition globally and is closely linked to the increasing burden of obesity, type 2 diabetes, and other cardiometabolic risk factors [1–3]. MASLD encompasses a spectrum of hepatic abnormalities ranging from simple steatosis to steatohepatitis, fibrosis, and cirrhosis and is associated with elevated risks of hepatocellular carcinoma, cardiovascular disease, chronic kidney disease, and overall mortality [4–7]. Early intervention is therefore crucial to prevent the potential adverse outcomes associated with MASLD, including progression to advanced liver disease and increased cardiometabolic risks [7, 8].

Lifestyle modification, particularly dietary intervention, is the cornerstone of MASLD management, as the approved pharmacotherapy for MASLD remains very limited [9, 10]. Among dietary strategies, the Mediterranean diet (MedDiet) has been strongly endorsed in recent international guidelines due to its favorable effects on hepatic fat content, insulin resistance, and cardiovascular risk profiles [11, 12]. The MedDiet emphasizes plant-based foods, whole grains, fish, and healthy fats, notably extra virgin olive oil and nuts, while limiting red meat, added sugars, and processed foods. Prospective cohort studies and randomized controlled trials consistently demonstrate that greater adherence to the MedDiet reduces the risk of MASLD development and progression [13–17]. However, implementing and sustaining dietary changes with the MedDiet can be challenging in real-world practice [18].

Several key factors such as everyday lifestyle constraints, cultural food preferences, and limited ongoing support may hinder long-term adherence, potentially attenuating the therapeutic effects of dietary interventions [9, 13, 19]. Therefore, identifying practical, scalable, and patient-centered strategies to improve implementation and dietary quality in routine care is essential.

Objectives {7}

The objective of the TIMA (Taiwanese Mediterranean dietary intervention for MASLD) study is to evaluate a novel, fully remote, culturally adapted Mediterranean diet program for patients with MASLD. The program incorporates culturally relevant food substitutions, locally available ingredients, and familiar culinary practices to enhance acceptability and adherence. The intervention combines brief, dietitian-led video counseling sessions with weekly behavioral reinforcement via tailored text messages. This study aims to evaluate whether this approach is effective in improving MASLD.

Methods

This protocol is reported in accordance with the SPIRIT 2013 Statement; the completed SPIRIT checklist is provided as Additional file 1.

Trial registration {2a}

ClinicalTrials.gov, NCT06503120. Registered July 9, 2024.

Trial design {8}

This study is a 12-week, single-center, two-arm, parallel-group, superiority randomized controlled trial conducted at Taichung Veterans General Hospital, Taiwan. Eligible adults with MASLD will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to either an online, culturally adapted MedDiet intervention or a standard care control group. The trial incorporates a behavioral nutrition education program delivered via videoconferencing and mobile messaging, with outcomes assessed at baseline and 12 weeks, and additional follow-up assessments at 6 and 12 months to explore longer-term effects.

Study setting {9}

This single-center randomized controlled trial is conducted at Taichung Veterans General Hospital, a tertiary referral center in Taichung, Taiwan. All dietary counseling sessions are delivered remotely via an online videoconferencing platform, while imaging and laboratory assessments are performed on site at the hospital.

Who will take informed consent? {26a}

Written informed consent will be obtained by the investigator before any trial-specific procedures are performed. The consent form will be signed by both the investigator and the participant.

Additional consent provisions for collection and use of participant data and biological specimens {26b}

The main trial consent form covers the collection, analysis, and storage of clinical data, MRI-PDFF images, and blood and stool samples for the purposes described in this protocol. Residual biological specimens will be processed and stored in a secure facility at Taichung Veterans General Hospital for predefined analyses related to MASLD and metabolic or gut microbiota profiles. Any future use of stored specimens or de-identified data for ancillary or unrelated studies will require prior approval by the institutional ethics committee and, where required by local regulations, additional written informed consent from participants. Participants may decline optional future use of their specimens without affecting their participation in the main trial.

Intervention

Intervention description {11a}

Mediterranean diet group

Participants in the intervention group will receive an isocaloric MedDiet plan. Nutrition counseling will be conducted via real-time video sessions using the Google Meet platform and delivered by the same registered dietitian throughout the study to ensure consistency. Each participant will attend four structured online counseling sessions at weeks 0, 3, 6, and 9, each lasting approximately 15–20 min. Criteria for discontinuing or modifying allocated interventions {11b}

Participants may discontinue or temporarily interrupt the allocated intervention at their own request at any time, without affecting their usual clinical care. Reasons for discontinuation or modification will be documented in the case report forms, and participants will be encouraged to attend outcome assessments whenever possible.

Strategies to improve adherence to interventions {11c}

To enhance adherence in patients with MASLD, weekly mobile messages via a mobile messaging platform were used to deliver dietary guidance, which will be underpinned by behavior change techniques (BCTs), such as problem solving, goal setting and social support, as outlined in Table 2 [21, 22]. Message content will be tailored based on each participant’s dietary profile, laboratory abnormalities, family history, and specific health concerns discussed during counseling. Messages will be developed by the research team and delivery will involve the dietitian selecting tailored messages from a structured message bank covering a range of relevant health topics, such as liver health, weight management, and blood glucose control. Standard care group

Explanation for the choice of comparators {6b}

We selected standard care as the comparator because it reflects usual clinical management for MASLD. In routine practice, patients receive lifestyle advice from their hepatologist but no structured, dietitian-led Mediterranean diet program. Using standard care as the control allows us to evaluate the incremental benefit of the culturally adapted online Mediterranean diet intervention over current practice, while remaining ethically acceptable and feasible in a real-world outpatient setting.

Participants in the control group will receive standard care, which constitutes a single 10-min educational session on MASLD and general healthy eating principles, mimicking usual clinical care by physicians. No further structured dietary counseling will be provided during the study period.

Relevant concomitant care permitted or prohibited during the trial {11d}

Use of any pharmacological weight-loss therapy (including, but not limited to, GLP-1 receptor agonists) and bariatric surgery is not permitted during the 12-week intervention period. Participants who require initiation of such pharmacologic or surgical weight-loss treatment for clinical reasons will discontinue the trial intervention and will be documented as major protocol deviations. Changes in antidiabetic, antihypertensive, or lipid-lowering medications that are not primarily intended for weight loss will be recorded at each visit.

Provisions for post-trial care {30}

Given the low-risk nature of this dietary and lifestyle intervention, no specific post-trial clinical care beyond usual practice is planned. After completion of the 12-week study, participants will return to routine follow-up with their treating physicians, as described in the Ethics approval and consent to participate section. In the unlikely event of trial-related harm, participants will be managed according to standard institutional procedures and covered by the hospital’s clinical insurance.

Outcome {12}

Primary outcome

The primary outcome is the relative change in intrahepatic fat content, assessed by MRI-PDFF from baseline to week 12. MRI will be performed using a 3T Siemens VIDA scanner, and hepatic fat will be quantified by averaging three circular regions of interest (2 cm diameter) in the right liver lobe [24, 25].

Sample size {14}

According to prior studies reporting a 25–39% reduction (with a standard deviation of 25–33%) in hepatic fat with a MedDiet over 12 weeks [17, 19, 27], it is assumed that the relative reduction in the intervention group and the standard care group will be approximately 33% and 5%, respectively with a standard deviation of 40%. To detect this difference between the two groups, an evaluable sample size of 35 per group is needed to achieve 80% power at a 5% significance level under equal allocation. Assuming a 10% dropout rate, a total of 78 participants (39 per group) will be enrolled.

Recruitment {15}

Potential participants will be identified from hepatology and metabolic clinics at Taichung Veterans General Hospital using outpatient lists and electronic medical records. Treating hepatologists will briefly introduce the study, and interested patients will be referred to the research team. A trained investigator or research nurse will confirm eligibility, provide written and verbal information, and obtain informed consent before baseline assessments.

Assignment of interventions: allocation

Sequence generation {16a}

Participants will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to the MedDiet intervention or standard care using a computer-generated randomization list with permuted blocks of varying sizes, prepared by an independent biostatistician. No stratification factors will be applied.

Concealment mechanism {16b}

Allocation will be concealed using a secure, password-protected, web-based randomization system managed by the Clinical Trial Center. The system will release the allocation only after eligibility and baseline assessments are completed, and will be accessible only to designated unblinded staff.

Implementation {16c}

Investigators and research nurses will screen and enroll participants and obtain informed consent. After baseline data collection, the study coordinator will randomize participants through the central system. Allocation will be conveyed to the study dietitian for intervention delivery, while outcome assessors and statisticians remain unaware of group assignment.

Assignment of interventions: blinding

Who will be blinded {17a}

Participants and dietitians cannot be blinded due to the nature of the dietary counseling. Radiologists interpreting MRI-PDFF, laboratory personnel, and statisticians analyzing trial data will be blinded to allocation. Analysis datasets will use anonymized group codes until the database is locked and the primary analysis is finalized.

Procedure for unblinding if needed {17b}

Emergency unblinding of blinded staff is not anticipated in this low-risk behavioral trial. In rare situations where unblinding is essential for safety or data integrity, the principal investigator may request allocation information for specific participants from the data manager. Any unblinding will be documented and reported to the Research Ethics Committee.

Data collection and management

Plans for assessment and collection of outcomes {18a}

The primary outcome, relative change in intrahepatic fat, will be assessed by MRI-PDFF at baseline and 12 weeks using a standardized protocol. Liver function tests, metabolic parameters, and other laboratory outcomes will be measured by the central laboratory, and anthropometric and blood pressure measurements will be obtained by trained staff with calibrated equipment. Dietary adherence and lifestyle will be assessed using T-MEDAS, photographic food record, and validated questionnaires according to the study schedule, with data recorded on CRFs by trained research staff.

Plans to promote participant retention and complete follow-up {18b}

Participants will receive reminders via telephone or messaging applications before counseling sessions and the 12-week assessment. Video visit scheduling will be flexible to accommodate participant availability. Participants who discontinue the intervention will be encouraged to attend follow-up visits so that outcome data at 12 weeks can still be collected.

Data management {19}

CRF data will be entered into a secure electronic database hosted by the Clinical Trial Center, using unique study IDs without direct identifiers. Built-in range and logic checks, periodic data queries, and source data verification will be used to ensure data quality. Access to the database will be role-based and password-protected, and de-identified data will be retained in accordance with institutional and regulatory requirements.

Confidentiality {27}

All participants will be assigned a study ID, and this code will be used on CRFs, samples, and analysis datasets. Identifiable information and consent forms will be stored separately in locked cabinets and encrypted files with restricted access. Only de-identified, aggregate data will be reported in publications and presentations, in compliance with institutional policies.

Plans for collection, laboratory evaluation, and storage of biological specimens for genetic or molecular analysis in this trial/future use {33}

Blood and stool samples will be collected at baseline and 12 weeks for routine clinical tests and pre-specified metabolomic and microbiome analyses related to MASLD. Research aliquots will be processed according to standardized protocols and stored at − 80 °C, labeled only with study IDs. Collection, storage, and planned analyses are described in the informed consent form; any future use of remaining samples will require appropriate ethics approval and, where necessary, additional consent.

Statistical methods for primary and secondary outcomes {20a}

The primary outcome is the relative change in intrahepatic fat content measured by MRI-PDFF from baseline to 12 weeks. The intention-to-treat (ITT) population will consist of all randomized participants. The primary analysis population will follow the ITT principle, including all randomized participants analyzed according to their randomized group. Participants who withdraw before the 12-week visit or who do not undergo the 12-week MRI-PDFF assessment will be considered to have stationary intrahepatic fat for the primary analysis of the primary outcome.

In the primary analysis, between-group differences in the relative change in MRI-PDFF from baseline to 12 weeks will be assessed using ANCOVA, with treatment group as the main factor and baseline MRI-PDFF as a covariate. Additional covariates such as age, sex, and presence of type 2 diabetes may be included if they are imbalanced between groups or strongly associated with the outcome. Secondary continuous outcomes will be analyzed using ANCOVA or, when multiple post-baseline time points are available, linear mixed-effects models with appropriate covariance structures. Categorical outcomes will be compared using chi-square tests or Fisher’s exact tests, and logistic regression will be used to estimate adjusted odds ratios with 95% confidence intervals.

A per-protocol (PP) analysis of the primary outcome will be conducted in the subset of participants who attend at least two visits, including the week 12 assessment, and have no major protocol deviations.

Missing data for the primary outcome will be handled using the baseline observation carried forward approach under the ITT principle. The amount and reasons for missing data will be summarized by treatment group. If the proportion of missing primary outcome data is substantial, additional sensitivity analyses using appropriate missing-data methods (e.g., multiple imputation under a missing-at-random assumption) may be performed to assess the impact of missing data on the results. All statistical tests will be two-sided with a significance level of 0.05. All analyses will be performed using R software (version 4.3.1).

Interim analyses {21b}

No interim analyses are planned for this trial, given the relatively short duration and low-risk nature of the intervention.

Methods for additional analyses (e.g., subgroup analyses) {20b}

Prespecified additional analyses will examine whether the intervention effect differs according to short-term treatment response. Participants will be categorized by (i) 12-week relative weight change (< 5% vs ≥ 5% weight loss), (ii) relative change in liver fat (MRI-PDFF reduction < 30% vs ≥ 30%), and (iii) change in Mediterranean diet adherence (T-MEDAS score increase < 5 vs. ≥ 5 points). Interaction terms between group allocation and each subgroup variable will be tested in regression models. All subgroup analyses will be exploratory and interpreted with caution.

Methods in analysis to handle protocol non-adherence and any statistical methods to handle missing data {20c}

The primary analysis will follow the intention-to-treat principle, with participants analyzed in the groups to which they were randomized, regardless of their actual intervention exposure. A per-protocol analysis will be conducted including participants who complete the 12-week assessment and attend at least two visits, without major protocol deviations. For secondary outcomes, the pattern and extent of missing data will be examined; where appropriate, multiple imputation under a missing-at-random assumption will be applied. Complete-case and imputed analyses will be reported in parallel to assess the robustness of the findings.

Plans to give access to the full protocol, participant-level data, and statistical code {31c}

Access to the full protocol, de-identified participant-level data, and statistical code will follow the data-sharing policy described in the Data Availability statement. In brief, materials will be made available from the corresponding author on reasonable request after publication of the main results, subject to approval by the Research Ethics Committee and a data-sharing agreement.

Oversight and monitoring

Composition of the coordinating center and trial steering committee {5d}

The trial will be coordinated by the hepatology research team at Taichung Veterans General Hospital, with support from the institutional Clinical Trial Center. The trial steering committee will consist of the principal investigator, co-investigators, a representative dietitian, and a trial coordinator. The committee will oversee trial conduct, recruitment progress, protocol adherence, and data quality, and will meet at least quarterly or more frequently if needed.

Composition of the data monitoring committee, its role and reporting structure {21a}

Given the low-risk nature of this behavioral dietary intervention and the short follow-up period, an independent data monitoring committee will not be established. Safety oversight will be undertaken by the principal investigator and trial steering committee, who will regularly review recruitment, adherence, and adverse events. Serious adverse events and any safety concerns will be reported to the Research Ethics Committee according to local regulations.

Adverse event reporting and harms {22}

All adverse events reported by participants or observed by study staff during the 12-week trial will be recorded in the case report forms, with information on onset, duration, severity, and relationship to the study intervention. Given the dietary nature of the intervention, anticipated events include mild gastrointestinal symptoms or transient discomfort. Serious adverse events, whether related or unrelated to the intervention, will be reported to the principal investigator within 24 h and to the Research Ethics Committee within the timelines required by institutional policy. Any unexpected serious harms judged related to the intervention will prompt consideration of protocol modification or trial suspension.

Frequency and plans for auditing trial conduct {23}

Independent monitoring or auditing may be performed by the Clinical Trial Center or institutional quality assurance unit in accordance with hospital policy and regulatory requirements. Audits, if conducted, will review informed consent procedures, protocol adherence, data completeness, and source data verification. Audit findings will be communicated to the principal investigator and, if necessary, to the Research Ethics Committee, and corrective actions will be implemented as required.

Plans for communicating important protocol amendments to relevant parties (e.g., trial participants, ethical committees) {25}

Any substantial protocol amendments will be submitted for prior approval to the Research Ethics Committee and updated in the trial registry. Approved amendments will be communicated to investigators, study staff, and, where relevant, participants through revised documents and training. If amendments affect participants’ rights or obligations, updated informed consent will be obtained as required.

Dissemination plans {31a}

The results of this trial will be disseminated through presentations at national and international scientific meetings and publication in peer-reviewed journals, regardless of whether findings are positive, negative, or inconclusive. Authorship will follow standard international guidelines. A plain-language summary of the main results will be made available to participants upon request and may be shared via institutional communication channels.

Discussion

MASLD has emerged as a major global health burden, with limited pharmacological options and lifestyle modification remaining the cornerstone of management [28]. In the recent EASL-EASD-EASO clinical guideline, the MedDiet is explicitly endorsed as the preferred dietary strategy for MASLD due to its well-established metabolic and hepatic benefits [10]. While the MedDiet is now strongly recommended by international guidelines, optimal strategies to promote adherence in real-world clinical settings remain lacking [29]. Furthermore, evidence regarding the application of the MedDiet in non-Mediterranean countries and communities is limited and inconsistent [30]. This trial is the first to evaluate a fully remote, video-based dietary counseling model, augmented by BCTs messages, specifically tailored for patients with MASLD. Such an approach offers a scalable, patient-centered strategy that aligns with global calls for accessible, sustainable, and evidence-based lifestyle interventions.

Emerging evidence indicates that the pathophysiology of MASLD involves a complex interplay of insulin resistance, lipid metabolism dysregulation, and chronic low-grade inflammation, all of which are modifiable through targeted dietary interventions such as the MedDiet [31]. The therapeutic rationale for adapting and adopting the MedDiet in MASLD management is further supported by a growing body of evidence from randomized controlled trials, which consistently demonstrate its beneficial effects on hepatic steatosis, insulin sensitivity, and cardiometabolic health. For example, Ryan et al. demonstrated a 39% reduction in hepatic fat and improved insulin sensitivity following a 6-week MedDiet intervention without significant weight loss [19]. Properzi et al. found both MedDiet and low-fat diet reduced steatosis, but MedDiet provided superior cardiovascular benefits [17]. Gepner et al. reported sustained reductions in hepatic fat and visceral adiposity over 18 months in patients on a MedDiet enriched with polyphenols [15]. Other studies showed significant improvements in liver enzymes, steatosis, and cardiometabolic markers using culturally adapted MedDiet approaches [32, 33].

Despite the growing body of evidence supporting the benefits of the MedDiet for MASLD, implementing educational dietary interventions in real-world clinical settings remains challenging. Traditional face-to-face dietary counseling has several drawbacks, including high time demands, inflexible scheduling, inconsistent content delivery, and insufficient follow-up [34]. These barriers are particularly salient in outpatient settings, where patients may face logistical obstacles such as transportation needs, work obligations, or limited privacy [28]. Moreover, the variability and intensity of in-person counseling can hinder long-term engagement, particularly for individuals requiring sustained lifestyle changes, and are further compounded by the limited availability of trained dietitians and healthcare personnel [35, 36]. To address these issues, our study developed a structured MedDiet education intervention delivered entirely through brief, real-time video counseling sessions. Each 15–20 min session is conducted online and follows a standardized counseling format focused on key dietary domains tailored to the individual’s needs, incorporating BCTs messages to support dietary adherence. While digital interventions have gained traction in other chronic disease populations [37], this is the first study to evaluate a fully remote, online dietary education program specifically for patients with MASLD.

In addition to practical barriers, cultural factors pose significant challenges to the implementation of the MedDiet in non-Mediterranean regions. In Taiwan, for example, high consumption of refined carbohydrates, limited use of olive oil, and preferences for red meat and traditional cooking methods may hinder adherence to MedDiet principles [20]. These challenges highlight the need for culturally adapted MedDiet recommendations that incorporate locally acceptable food substitutions while preserving the core nutritional features of the diet [38]. Our intervention is socio-culturally tailored to the Taiwanese dietary context and focuses on the gradual introduction of MedDiet principles. By incorporating personalized counseling based on each patient’s dietary records, the approach aims to enhance engagement and improve dietary quality without imposing calorie restrictions. Furthermore, by selectively enrolling individuals with a baseline T-MEDAS score of 5 or lower, the study targets those with suboptimal dietary habits as part of a precision lifestyle intervention strategy. This approach is supported by previous studies, including our own, demonstrating that individuals with lower baseline dietary quality responded more favorably to a dietitian-guided program [20, 39].

In conclusion, this study may provide a culturally adapted and clinically practical model for dietary management in patients with MASLD, aligning with global calls for accessible and sustainable lifestyle interventions. It may also serve as a foundation for future research on digital nutrition interventions tailored to metabolic liver disease.

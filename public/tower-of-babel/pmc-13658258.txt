Unusual paraneoplastic thrombocytosis in small bowel neuroendocrine tumor responding to 177Lu-DOTATATE
Authors: Eve Piekarski, Anne-Ségolène Cottereau, Romain Coriat, Lise Willems, Lucas Bouys, Florence Tenenbaum
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractNeuroendocrine tumors (NETs) are often associated with paraneoplastic features including carcinoid syndrome in up to 30% to 40% of cases. However, paraneoplastic thrombocytosis is a rare NET-associated condition. We report the case of a 59-year-old woman referred for significant and unexplained weight loss. Biological evaluation revealed thrombocytosis (900 × 109/L). A thoracoabdominal and pelvic computed tomography (CT) scan revealed multiple liver and bone lesions, suggestive of metastatic disease. Colonoscopic evaluation identified a well-differentiated NET as the primary tumor. Hematologic explorations revealed no underlying hematological condition explaining thrombocytosis. Cold somatostatin analogue treatment was started and resulted in initial stabilization of the disease. However, follow-up CT scan showed liver progression after 2 years concomitant with worsening thrombocytosis. 68Ga-DOTATOC positron emission tomography showed intense uptake of all known tumoral lesions; the patient was then treated with 177Lu-DOTATATE. Under 177Lu-DOTATATE treatment, platelet counts gradually decreased until normalization 8 weeks after the first injection, remaining within the normal range for up to 18 months, in parallel with clinical improvement. In conclusion, we report a rare case of paraneoplastic thrombocytosis associated with a small bowel NET showing rapid biological resolution from the first cycle of 177Lu-DOTATATE.Keywords: thrombocytosis, paraneoplastic syndrome, paraneoplastic thrombocytosis, neuroendocrine tumor, 177Lu-DOTATATE, Lutathera

Introduction

Neuroendocrine tumors (NETs) are often associated with paraneoplastic features, most often secondary to tumoral secretion of various peptides, the most prevalent being carcinoid syndrome in up to 30% to 40% of cases [1-4], but Cushing syndrome, syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion, hypoglycemia, and hypercalcemia can also be observed. On the other hand, paraneoplastic thrombocytosis is a common feature in solid tumors, including non–small cell lung cancer, advanced breast cancer, pancreatic cancer, colorectal cancer, and gastric cancer, with an underlying malignancy detected in up to 35% of thrombocythemia cases, but only reported once with NET corresponding to one case reported to be associated with breast NET [5]. We report an unusual case of paraneoplastic syndrome: paraneoplastic thrombocytosis secondary to small bowel NET.

Case presentation

We report the case of a 59-year-old woman referred for impairment of clinical status (World Health Organization [WHO] Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group [ECOG] performance status of 2) with unexplained significant weight loss (7 kg over the last 6 months), diarrhea, with past medical history of superficial spreading melanoma on the left leg 12 years prior to presentation. Biological evaluation revealed thrombocytosis of 1400 × 109/L (N 150-500 × 109/L). The other biological analysis revealed moderately elevated levels of chromogranin-A (200 μg/L [N < 100 μg/L]), normal levels of gastrin, calcitonin, glucagon, cortisol, normal ionogram and blood glucose, normal hemoglobin and leukocytes counts (glycated hemoglobin, parathyroid hormone, and adrenocorticotropin were not part of the initial work-up). Thoracoabdominal and pelvic computed tomography (CT) scan revealed multiple liver and bone lesions that appeared to be metastatic. Colonoscopic evaluation found an ileal lesion, the biopsy of which revealed a well-differentiated neuroendocrine tumor. Liver biopsy showed grade 1 metastasis of NET (Ki67 < 2%). The patient was therefore treated by cold somatostatin analogue, with an initial disease stabilization and partial improvement of the thrombocytosis with platelet count dropping from 1400 × 109/L to around 900 × 109/L. Antiplatelet treatment was also started to prevent thrombotic complications of thrombocytosis. Nine months after initiation of cold somatostatin analogue, a localized grade 2 pT1a clear cell renal cell carcinoma was detected and surgically removed (partial left nephrectomy), with no effect on thrombocytosis.

After 2 years of cold somatostatin analogue treatment, NET liver metastasis progression was diagnosed on imaging, along with a progressive worsening of the thrombocytosis. The patient was then referred to our nuclear medicine department to evaluate the feasibility of a second-line 177Lu-DOTATATE treatment. Theranostic evaluation (Fig. 1) with 68Ga-DOTATOC positron emission tomography (PET) CT showed intense uptake of the ileal tumor, of the bilobar liver lesions, and of the bone lesions disseminated through the axial and proximal peripheral skeleton, allowing for 177Lu-DOTATATE treatment. At that time, platelet count was about 1000 × 10^9/L.

Diagnostic assessment

In parallel with NET treatment, the patient was evaluated for other potential causes of thrombocytosis. Hematologic explorations revealed no underlying hematological condition explaining thrombocytosis, that is, no inflammatory syndrome or iron deficiency, no myeloproliferative disorder (no JAK2 mutation for polycythemia vera, no BCR ABL fusion, no CALR nor MPL mutation), no Howell-Jolly bodies on blood smear, normal thrombopoietin (TPO) levels, no POEMS (polyneuropathy, organomegaly, endocrinopathy, M protein, and skin changes) syndrome, normal lymphocyte immunophenotyping, and serum protein electrophoresis.

Treatment

177Lu-DOTATATE treatment was started, and through the course of the treatment, platelet counts gradually decreased until normalization at around 500 × 10^9/L 8 weeks after the first injection, remaining in the normal range between 200 and 300 × 10^9/L about 6 weeks after the second injection (Fig. 2), and up to 18 months after the fourth injection and completion of the 177Lu-DOTATATE treatment.

Outcome and follow-up

During and after the 177Lu-DOTATATE course, the patient's clinical status stabilized specifically regarding diarrhea frequency and weight, allowing a return to a WHO ECOG performance status of 0. Biological parameters of NETs such as plasmatic neuron-specific enolase, plasmatic chromogranin A, and urinary 5-hydroxyindoleacetate showed a decrease at first, then stabilized. Imaging evaluation both with CT and 68Ga DOTATOC PET/CT revealed stable disease. After correction of hematotoxicity on hemoglobin level and leucocyte count, normal platelet count persisted, up to the last follow-up (18 month after completion of 177Lu-DOTATATE treatment). Antiplatelet oral treatment was discontinued after the first 177Lu-DOTATATE injection. Cold analogue treatment was pursued in parallel and maintained after completion of the 4 177Lu-DOTATATE injections.

Discussion

This case reports a rare condition, with very few descriptions in the literature of paraneoplastic thrombocytosis associated with NET (only one case of breast NET [5]), and no prior description of paraneoplastic thrombocytosis secondary to a small bowel NET, to the best of our knowledge. Therefore, this case highlights a potential paraneoplastic feature of which physicians managing NETs should be aware. It is also interesting to see how this condition favorably evolved after internal vectorized radiotherapy with a normalization of platelet count observed after only one injection, which is consistent with the usual rapid positive response of carcinoid syndrome to 177Lu-DOTATATE treatment. Radioligand therapy was indicated as second-line treatment, considered standard of care in this clinical setting. Dosing and timing followed guidelines, with standard injected activity of 7400 MBq per injection with a total of 4 injections every 8 weeks. Paraneoplastic thrombocytosis is a rare paraneoplastic syndrome in NETs that has only been described in one case of breast NET [5]. It seems to correlate with chromogranin A positivity in colorectal cancer [6], but is a biological feature often encountered in other solid malignancies (up to one-third of patients). Thrombocytosis is often associated with worse prognosis, driven by known interactions between platelets and cancer cells (loop interactions including interleukin 6 (IL-6) and TPO signalization [7-10]), and potential direct effects of thrombocytosis on the cancer treatment, mainly described in ovarian cancer [8]. In paraneoplastic thrombocytosis, tumors produce IL-6, stimulating liver production of TPO, resulting in increased bone marrow platelet production. Direct TPO secretion by tumor cells has also been described. Increased platelet production can, in the end, participate in cancer promotion through growth stimulation mechanism and avoidance of immune surveillance [7-10].

In conclusion, we present the first case of paraneoplastic thrombocytosis associated with a small bowel NET showing rapid biological resolution from the very first cycle of 177Lu-DOTATATE; this feature is relevant for physicians managing NETs as it is associated with potential thrombotic events and may require preventive therapeutics, and since cancer cells and platelets may have potentially relevant interactions that hinder the prognosis.

Learning points

Although rare, paraneoplastic thrombocytosis should be considered in patients with metastatic NETs once primary hematological conditions are excluded.

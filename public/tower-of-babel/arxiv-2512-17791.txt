Near-Maturity Asymptotics of Critical Prices of American Put Options under Exponential Lévy Models
José E. Figueroa-López, Ruoting Gong

Abstract
. In the present paper, we study the near-maturity ( t → T − t\rightarrow T^{-} ) convergence rate of the optimal early-exercise price b ⁡ ( t ) b(t) of an American put under an exponential Lévy model with a nonzero Brownian component. Two important settings, not previous covered in the literature, are considered. In the case that the optimal exercise price converges to the strike price ( b ⁡ ( T − ) = K b(T^{-})=K ), we contemplate models with negative jumps of unbounded variation (i.e., processes that exhibit high activity of negative jumps or sudden falls in asset prices). In the second case, when the optimal exercise price tend to a value lower than K K , we consider infinite activity jumps (though still of bounded variations), extending existing results for models with finite jump activity (finitely many jumps in any finite interval). In both cases, we show that b ⁡ ( T − ) − b ⁡ ( t ) b(T^{-})-b(t) is of order T − t \sqrt{T-t} with explicit constants proportionality. Furthermore, we also derive the second-order near-maturity expansion of the American put price around the critical price along a certain parabolic branch. KEYWORDS: American options, convergence rate, critical price, exponential Lévy models, near-maturity asymptotics Mathematics Subject Classification (2010) : 60F99 60G40 60G51 91G20 JEL Classification : C6



1.
Introduction

It is generally recognized that the standard Black-Scholes option pricing model is inconsistent with options data, while remaining a widely used model in practice because of its simplicity. Exponential Lévy models provides a tractable extension of the classical Black-Scholes setup by allowing jumps in stock prices and heavy-tailed return distributions, while preserving the independence and stationarity of returns. We refer the readers to the monograph
[
3
]
for further motivations and literature on the use of jump processes in financial modeling. A large number of publications have been devoted to the pricing of European options under various Lévy-based models. In this paper, we revisit the problem of American put option pricing with finite maturity under general exponential Lévy models and, especially, on the study of the near-maturity asymptotics of the critical price (the exercise boundary) in this setting. No closed-form solution are known for American options and, as a result, numerical and approximation methods are employed in practice.

The near-maturity behavior of the critical price of the American put is well understood in the Black-Scholes model. In the pioneer work of Moerbeke
[
9
]
, the near-maturity limit of the critical price for American call options is investigated, which can be easily transferred to American puts. Moreover, the conclusion in
[
9
]
suggests a parabolic behavior for the convergence rate without any restrictions on the model parameters. However, Barles et. al.
[
1
]
show that a parabolic behavior cannot occur in some situations. Indeed, they show that, in the absence of dividends,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow T^{-}}\frac{K-b^{\text{BS}}(t)}{\sigma K\sqrt{-(T-t)\ln(T-t)}}=1,

where
T
is the maturity,
K
is the strike price,
\sigma
is the volatility, and
b^{\text{BS}}(t)
is the critical price at time
t
. This asymptotic behavior remains valid when
0\leq\delta<r
, where
\delta
and
r
denote the respective constant dividend and interest rates, and can be proved by the method of
[
1
]
. Lamberton and Villeneuve
[
8
]
provide the near-maturity behavior of the critical price for the cases
r<\delta
and
r=\delta
. More precisely, they show that the parabolic behavior stated by Moerbeke
[
9
]
holds in the case
r<\delta
. They also prove that, when
r=\delta
, the critical price satisfies the following estimate:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow T^{-}}\frac{K-b^{\text{BS}}(t)}{\sigma K\sqrt{-(T-t)\ln(T-t)}}=\sqrt{2}.

The above results have been generalized to exponential Lévy models under various conditions. Let
\nu
be the Lévy measure of the underlying Lévy process
X
, and let

(1.3)

\displaystyle d:=r-\delta-\int_{0+}^{\infty}\big(e^{z}-1\big)\nu(dz).

Note that in the Black-Scholes model, the quantity

reduces to
d=r-\delta
. Lamberton and Mikou
[
5
]
provide the near-maturity limits of the critical price of the American put for both the cases

and
d<0
(see Theorem
2.5
below). When
X
has finite jump activity (i.e.,
\nu(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\})<\infty
or, equivalently, the process exhibits finitely many jumps on each finite time interval), and a nonzero Brownian component, the convergence rates of the critical price for all three cases
d>0
,
d=0
, and
d<0
have been fully studied by Bouselmi and Lamberton
[
2
]
(see also
[
11
]

) and (
1.2
), respectively, while in the last case, they show that the critical price exhibits an analogous parabolic behavior to the Black-Scholes framework (see Theorem
3.1
below). When

has infinite jump activity, Lamberton and Mikou
[
7

obtain the convergence rates of the critical price in the following scenarios with

(see Theorem

•

is a pure-jump Lévy process with a jump component of finite variation;

•

•

is a pure-jump Lévy process with tempered stable negative small jumps of infinite variation.

In particular, they recover the convergence rate (
1.1
) in the second case above.

In the present paper, we study the near-maturity convergence rate of the critical price of the American put under an exponential Lévy model with a
nonzero
Brownian component, which is arguably more relevant for financial modelling. Firstly, we consider the scenario of
d>0
without imposing any restriction on the jump activity of
X

), thus extending the corresponding results in

to exponential Lévy models with a possibly negative jumps of infinite variation. Our analysis combines a careful decomposition of the jump component of

2.
Setup and Preliminary Results

Throughout this paper, we consider a risky asset with price process
S:=(S_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
, where
\mathbb{R}_{+}:=[0,\infty)
, defined on a complete filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
, where
\mathbb{F}:=(\mathscr{F}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
and

(2.1)

\displaystyle S_{t}=S_{0}\,e^{(r-\delta)t+X_{t}},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Above,
r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
is the interest rate,
\delta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
is the dividend yield, and
X:=(X_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is a Lévy process with Lévy triplet
(b,\sigma^{2},\nu)
.

Assumption 2.1
.

Throughout we will always assume that
X
satisfies at least one of the conditions in each of the following two categories:

(i)

\sigma\neq 0
,
\nu((-\infty,0))>0
, or
\displaystyle{\int_{(0,1]}z\,\nu(dz)=\infty}
;

(ii)

\sigma\neq 0
,
\nu((0,\infty))>0
, or
\displaystyle{\int_{[-1,0)}z\,\nu(dz)=\infty}
.

Remark 2.2
.

By
[
14
, Theorem 21.5]
, Assumption
2.1
implies that the trajectories of
X
are neither almost surely increasing nor almost surely decreasing. This, together with
[
3
, Proposition 9.9]
, ensures that the exponential Lévy model (
2.1
) is arbitrage-free (or equivalently, the existence of martingale measures).

Assumption 2.3
.

To guarantee that
\mathbb{P}
is a martingale measure for the discounted price process
(e^{-(r-\delta)t}S_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
(or equivalently,
(e^{X_{t}})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
), we impose the following two assumptions on
X
:

\displaystyle\text{(i)}\,\,\,\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{z}\,\nu(dz)<\infty;\qquad\text{(ii)}\,\,\,\,b=-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}\big(e^{z}-1-z{\bf 1}_{\{|z|\leq 1\}}\big)\nu(dz),

where
\mathbb{R}_{0}:=\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
.

In view of Assumption
2.3
, we have the Lévy-Itô decomposition of
X
as

\displaystyle X_{t}=\sigma W_{t}+L_{t}:=\sigma W_{t}-\frac{\sigma^{2}t}{2}-t\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}\big(e^{z}-1-z\big)\nu(dz)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}z\,\widetilde{N}(ds,dz),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},

where
W:=(W_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is a standard Brownian motion,
N(ds,dz)
is a Poisson random measure on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{0}
with intensity measure
ds\nu(dz)
, independent of
W
, and
\widetilde{N}(ds,dz):=N(ds,dz)-ds\nu(dz)
is the compensated measure of
N
.

Let
P_{e}(t,s)
and
P(t,s)
be the respective time-
t
risk-neutral prices of the European and American put options on
S
, with strike
K\in(0,\infty)
and maturity
T\in(0,\infty)
, namely,

(2.2)

\displaystyle P_{e}(t,s)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}\Big(e^{-r(T-t)}\big(K-s\,e^{(r-\delta)(T-t)+X_{T-t}}\big)^{+}\Big),\quad(t,s)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}_{+},

(2.3)

\displaystyle P(t,s)

\displaystyle:=\sup_{\tau\in\mathscr{T}_{0,T-t}}\mathbb{E}\Big(e^{-r\tau}\big(K-s\,e^{(r-\delta)\tau+X_{\tau}}\big)^{+}\Big),\quad(t,s)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}_{+},

where
\mathscr{T}_{u,v}
denotes the collection of
\mathbb{F}
-stopping times taking values in
[u,v]
, for any
0\leq u\leq v
. Assumption
2.1
ensures that
P(t,s)\geq P_{e}(t,s)>0
for all
(t,s)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Moreover, we define the European and American critical prices, respectively, as

(2.4)

\displaystyle b_{e}(t)

\displaystyle:=\inf\big\{s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\!:\,P_{e}(t,s)>(K-s)^{+}\big\},\quad t\in[0,T],

\displaystyle b(t)

\displaystyle:=\inf\big\{s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\!:\,P(t,s)>(K-s)^{+}\big\},\quad t\in[0,T].

Clearly,
b(T)=b_{e}(T)=K
. The following proposition summaries some regularity properties of
P
(cf.
[
3
, Section 12.1.3]
,
[
5
, Proposition 3.2]
,
[
7
, Proposition 2.2]
, and
[
11
, Proposition 2.1]
).

Proposition 2.4
.

(a)

For each
t\in[0,T]
,
P(t,\cdot)
is nonincreasing and convex on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and satisfies

\displaystyle\big|P(t,s_{1})-P(t,s_{2})\big|\leq|s_{1}-s_{2}|,\quad s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

(b)

For each
s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
P(\cdot,s)
is continuous and nonincreasing on
[0,T]
.

It follows that (cf.
[
5
, Proposition 4.1]
and
[
11
, Proposition 2.2]
) the American critical price
b
is nondecreasing on
[0,T]
and that

\displaystyle b(t)\in(0,K),\quad P\big(t,b(t)\big)=K-b(t),\quad\text{for any }\,t\in[0,T).

Similar results holds for
b_{e}(t)
and
P_{e}(t,s)
, namely,
b_{e}
is nondecreasing on
[0,T]
and

(2.5)

\displaystyle b_{e}(t)\in(0,K),\quad P_{e}\big(t,b_{e}(t)\big)=K-b_{e}(t),\quad\text{for any }\,t\in[0,T).

Moreover, since
P(t,s)\geq P_{e}(t,s)
for all
(t,s)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we have

(2.6)

\displaystyle 0<b(t)\leq b_{e}(t)<K,\quad\text{for all }\,t\in[0,T).

We are interested in the near-maturity asymptotic behavior of
b(t)
and
P(t,s)
, i.e., when
t\rightarrow T^{-}
. The following result provides the limit of the critical price near maturity (cf.
[
5
, Theorem 4.4]
).

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
d
be defined as in (
1.3
). Then, the following assertions hold:

(a)

If
d\geq 0
, then we have

\displaystyle b(T):=\lim_{t\rightarrow T^{-}}b(t)=K.

(b)

If
d<0
, then we have

\displaystyle b(T):=\lim_{t\rightarrow T^{-}}b(t)=\xi,

where
\xi
is the unique solution in
(0,K)
to the following equation:

(2.7)

\displaystyle rK-\delta\xi-\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}\big(\xi e^{z}-K\big)^{+}\nu(dz)=0.

Remark 2.6
.

When
d\geq 0
, it is intrinsically assumed that

\displaystyle\int_{0+}^{\infty}\big(e^{z}-1\big)\nu(dz)<\infty{\color[rgb]{1,0,0},}

and, thus, this case intrinsically entails that the positive jump part of
X
has finite variation.

The Known Cases of the Convergence Rate of the Critical Price

In this section, we review some known results on the asymptotic behavior of the critical exercise price

3.1.
Finite Jump Activity Case

The following result is a combination of
[
11
, Theorem 4.2]
and
[
2

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that
\sigma>0
and
\nu(\mathbb{R}_{0})<\infty

(a)

When

\displaystyle K-b(t)\sim\sigma K\sqrt{(T-t)\big|\ln(T-t)\big|},\quad t\rightarrow T^{-}.

(b)

When
d=0

\displaystyle K-b(t)\sim\sqrt{2}\,\sigma K\sqrt{(T-t)\big|\ln(T-t)\big|},\quad t\rightarrow T^{-}.

(c)

Suppose that

\xi
be given as in Theorem

by

(3.1)

\displaystyle v_{\lambda,\beta}(y)\!:=\!\!\sup_{\tau\in\mathscr{T}_{0,1}^{W,\overline{N}}}\!\mathbb{E}\bigg(e^{-\lambda\tau}{\bf 1}_{\{\overline{N}_{\tau}=0\}}\!\int_{0}^{\tau}\!f_{\lambda\beta}(y\!+\!W_{s})\,ds+\frac{\beta e^{\lambda\tau}}{2}{\bf 1}_{\{\overline{N}_{\tau}=1\}}\!\Big(L_{\tau}^{W}(-y)\!-\!L_{\overline{T}_{1}}^{W}(-y)\Big)\!\bigg),\,\,

where
\overline{N}:=(\overline{N}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is a Poisson process with intensity
\lambda
which is independent of
W
,
\overline{T}_{1}
is the first jump time of
\overline{N}
,
\mathscr{T}_{0,1}^{W,\overline{N}}

\mathbb{F}^{W,\overline{N}}

(c.1)

If

\displaystyle\xi-b(t)\sim y_{0,0}\sigma\xi\sqrt{T-t},\quad t\rightarrow T^{-}.

(c.2)

If
\nu(\{\ln(K/\xi)\})>0
, then we have

\displaystyle\xi-b(t)\sim y_{\lambda,\beta}\sigma\xi\sqrt{T-t},\quad t\rightarrow T^{-},

with

and

.

3.2.
Finite Variation Case

In this section, we consider in (
2.1
) a Lévy process
X
with a finite variation jump component, i.e.,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\int_{(-1,1)\setminus\{0\}}|z|\,\nu(dz)<\infty.

In this case, the convergence rate of the American critical price is only known when
d>0
.

For a pure-jump (
\sigma=0
) Lévy process

, the following result is due to
[
7

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume
\sigma=0

3.2

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow T^{-}}\frac{1}{T-t}\bigg(\frac{K}{b_{e}(t)}-1\bigg)=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}\big(e^{x}-1\big)^{-}\nu(dx)\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{t\rightarrow T^{-}}\frac{b_{e}(t)-b(t)}{T-t}=0.

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow T^{-}}\frac{1}{T-t}\bigg(\frac{K}{b(t)}-1\bigg)=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}\big(e^{x}-1\big)^{-}\nu(dx).

Next, we consider the case when

has a non-zero Brownian component (i.e.,

\displaystyle K-b^{\text{BS}}(t)\sim\sigma K\sqrt{-(T-t)\ln(T-t)},\quad t\rightarrow T^{-}.

The following result follows from

Assume

\displaystyle 0\leq b^{\emph{BS}}(t)-b(t)\leq C\sqrt{T-t},\quad t\rightarrow T^{-}.

Together with (

\displaystyle K-b(t)\sim\sigma K\sqrt{-(T-t)\ln(T-t)},\quad t\rightarrow T^{-}.

Infinite Variation Case

In this section, we consider in (

4.
New Results on the Convergence Rate of the Critical Price when

In this section, we consider the rate of convergence of

near maturity when
\sigma>0
and

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that
\sigma>0
and

. Then we have

\displaystyle K-b(t)=\sigma K\sqrt{-(T-t)\ln(T-t)}+O\big(\sqrt{T-t}\big),\quad t\rightarrow T^{-}.

The proof of Theorem

[
7

To begin with, recalling that

(4.1)

\displaystyle X_{t}=X_{t}^{W}-t\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}\big(e^{z}-1\big)^{+}\nu(dz)+J_{t}^{+}+J_{t}^{-},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},

where

\displaystyle X_{t}^{W}\!:=\sigma W_{t}\!-\!\frac{\sigma^{2}t}{2},\,\,\,\,J_{t}^{+}\!:=\!\!\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\int_{(0,\infty)}\!\!zN(ds,dz),\,\,\,\,J_{t}^{-}\!:=\!\!\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\int_{(-\infty,0)}\!\!z\widetilde{N}(ds,dz)-t\!\int_{-\infty}^{0}\!\!\big(e^{z}\!-\!1\!-\!z\big)\nu(dz).

Clearly,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big(e^{pJ_{t}^{-}}\big)=\exp\bigg(t\int_{(-\infty,0)}\big(e^{pz}-1-p(e^{z}-1)\big)\nu(dz)\bigg),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

The following lemma characterizes the small-time behavior for a Lévy process with
\sigma>0
and a finite variation positive jump component,
the proof of which is deferred to Appendix

Lemma 4.2

Assume that
\sigma>0

X_{t}/\sqrt{t}\;{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathfrak{D}}}{{\longrightarrow}}}\;\sigma W_{1}

Remark 4.3

\displaystyle K-b_{e}(t)

\displaystyle=P_{e}\big(t,b_{e}(t)\big)=e^{-r\tau}\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(K-b_{e}(t)e^{(r-\delta)\tau+X_{\tau}}\big)^{+}\Big)

\displaystyle=Ke^{-r\tau}-b_{e}(t)e^{-\delta\tau}+e^{-r\tau}\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(b_{e}(t)e^{(r-\delta)\tau+X_{\tau}}-K\big)^{+}\Big),

where
\tau=T-t
, and so

\displaystyle\frac{K}{b_{e}(t)}\big(1-e^{-r\tau}\big)-\big(1-e^{-\delta\tau}\big)=e^{-r\tau}\mathbb{E}\left(\bigg(e^{(r-\delta)\tau+X_{\tau}}-\frac{K}{b_{e}(t)}\bigg)^{+}\right).

Denote by

(4.3)

\displaystyle\zeta(\tau):=\frac{K}{b_{e}(T-\tau)}-1.

By Theorem
2.5
-(a), we deduce that, as
\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}
,

(4.4)

\displaystyle(r-\delta)\tau=e^{-r\tau}\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(e^{(r-\delta)\tau+X_{\tau}}-1-\zeta(\tau)\big)^{+}\Big)+o(\tau)=\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(e^{(r-\delta)\tau+X_{\tau}}-1-\zeta(\tau)\big)^{+}\Big)+o(\tau),\quad\,\,\,

where the second equality above follows from the fact that

(4.5)

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(e^{(r-\delta)\tau+X_{\tau}}-1-\zeta(\tau)\big)^{+}\Big)=0.

The following proposition shows the rate of convergence of
b_{e}(t)
to
K
, as
t\rightarrow T^{-}
, by characterizing the rate of convergence of
\zeta(\tau)
to
0
as
\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Assume that
\sigma>0
and
d>0
. Then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\zeta(\tau)}{\sqrt{-\tau\ln\tau}}=\sigma.

The first step of the proof of Proposition
4.4
is the following lemma, the proof of which is deferred to Appendix
A
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Assume that
\sigma>0
and
d>0
. Then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\zeta(\tau)}{\sqrt{\tau}}=\infty.

The next two lemmas are seeking for a small-time large deviations result for
(X_{\tau}^{W}+J_{\tau}^{-})/\sqrt{\tau}
, the proof of which are again deferred to Appendix
A
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Assume that
\sigma>0
and
d>0
. For any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
r\in(0,\infty)
, let

(4.6)

\displaystyle L=L(\varepsilon;\sigma):=\sigma^{2}+2\int_{[-\varepsilon,0)}z^{2}\nu(dz),\quad\tau_{0}=\tau_{0}(r,\sigma,\varepsilon):=\frac{r^{2}}{\big(\nu((-\infty,-\varepsilon))-\sigma^{2}/2\big)^{2}}.

Then, for any
\tau\in(0,\tau_{0}]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(X_{\tau}^{W}+J_{\tau}^{-}\geq r\sqrt{\tau}\Big)\leq e^{-Lp^{2}/2},

where

(4.7)

\displaystyle p=p(\tau;r,\sigma,\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{L}\bigg[r-\sqrt{\tau}\bigg(\nu((-\infty,-\varepsilon))-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\bigg)\bigg]\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Lemma 4.7
.

Assume that
\sigma>0
and
d>0
. Then for any
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}f(\tau)=\infty
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{f^{2}(\tau)}\ln\mathbb{P}\Big(X_{\tau}^{W}+J_{\tau}^{-}\geq\sqrt{\tau}f(\tau)\Big)=-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}.

Proof of Proposition
4.4
.
In view of (
4.1
) and the independence among
X^{W}
,
J^{+}
, and
J^{-}
, for any
\tau\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, by conditioning on
X^{W}_{\tau}+J^{-}_{\tau}
and noting that,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big(e^{J_{\tau}^{+}}\big)=\exp\bigg(\tau\int_{(0,\infty)}\big(e^{z}-1\big)\,\nu(dz)\bigg),

we first have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(e^{(r-\delta)\tau+X_{\tau}}\!-\!1\!-\!\zeta(\tau)\big)^{+}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}\!\left(\!\left(e^{(r-\delta)\tau+X_{\tau}^{W}+J_{\tau}^{-}}\!\exp\!\bigg(\!\!-\!\tau\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}\!\!\big(e^{z}\!-\!1\big)^{+}\nu(dz)\!\bigg)\mathbb{E}\big(e^{J_{\tau}^{+}}\big)\!-\!1\!-\!\zeta(\tau)\!\right)^{+}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(e^{(r-\delta)\tau+X_{\tau}^{W}+J_{\tau}^{-}}-1-\zeta(\tau)\big)^{+}\Big)\geq\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(X_{\tau}^{W}+J_{\tau}^{-}-\zeta(\tau)\big)^{+}\Big),

where we have used Jensen’s inequality in the first inequality and
e^{z}\geq 1+z
in the last inequality. Together with (
4.4
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\ln\mathbb{E}\left(\bigg(\frac{X_{\tau}^{W}+J_{\tau}^{-}}{\sqrt{\tau}}-\frac{\zeta(\tau)}{\sqrt{\tau}}\bigg)^{+}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\ln\tau+\ln(r-\delta)+o(1),\quad\tau\rightarrow 0^{+},

which, together with Lemma
4.5
, implies that

(4.8)

\displaystyle\liminf_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\tau}{\zeta^{2}(\tau)}\ln\mathbb{E}\left(\bigg(\frac{X_{\tau}^{W}+J_{\tau}^{-}}{\sqrt{\tau}}-\frac{\zeta(\tau)}{\sqrt{\tau}}\bigg)^{+}\right)\leq\liminf_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\tau\ln\tau}{2\zeta^{2}(\tau)}.

Moreover, by Markov’s inequality, for any
\beta\in(1,\infty)
and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(X_{\tau}^{W}+J_{\tau}^{-}\geq\beta\zeta(\tau)\Big)\leq\frac{\sqrt{\tau}}{(\beta-1)\zeta(\tau)}\mathbb{E}\left(\bigg(\frac{X_{\tau}^{W}+J_{\tau}^{-}}{\sqrt{\tau}}-\frac{\zeta(\tau)}{\sqrt{\tau}}\bigg)^{+}\right).

This, together with Lemma
4.7
(with
f(\tau)=\beta\zeta(\tau)/\sqrt{\tau}
) and (
4.8
), implies that

\displaystyle\liminf_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\tau\ln\tau}{2\zeta^{2}(\tau)}\geq\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\tau}{\zeta^{2}(\tau)}\ln\mathbb{P}\Big(X_{\tau}^{W}+J_{\tau}^{-}\geq\beta\zeta(\tau)\Big)+\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\ln\big((\beta-1)\zeta(\tau)/\sqrt{\tau}\big)}{\zeta^{2}(\tau)/\tau}=-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}},

or equivalently,

\displaystyle\liminf_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\zeta(\tau)}{\sqrt{-\tau\ln\tau}}\geq\frac{\sigma}{\beta}.

Since
\beta\in(1,\infty)
is arbitrary, by taking
\beta\rightarrow 1^{+}
above we obtain that

\displaystyle\liminf_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\zeta(\tau)}{\sqrt{-\tau\ln\tau}}\geq\sigma.

In order to derive an upper bound for
\limsup_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}\zeta(\tau)/\sqrt{-\tau\ln\tau}
, we first deduce from (
4.4
) a lower bound for
\mathbb{E}((e^{X_{\tau}^{W}+J_{\tau}^{-}}-1-\zeta(\tau))^{+})
as
\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}
. Indeed, for any
\tau\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(e^{(r-\delta)\tau+X_{\tau}}-1-\zeta(\tau)\big)^{+}\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big(e^{(r-\delta)\tau+X_{\tau}}{\bf 1}_{\{X_{\tau}\geq\ln(1+\zeta(\tau))+(r-\delta)\tau\}}\Big)

(4.10)

\displaystyle\quad-\big(1+\zeta(\tau)\big)\mathbb{P}\big(X_{\tau}\geq\ln(1+\zeta(\tau))+(r-\delta)\tau\big).

Note that Lemmas
4.2
and
4.5
imply that

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathbb{P}\big(X_{\tau}\geq\ln(1+\zeta(\tau))+(r-\delta)\tau\big)=\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathbb{P}\bigg(\frac{X_{\tau}}{\sqrt{\tau}}\geq\frac{\ln(1+\zeta(\tau))}{\sqrt{\tau}}+(r-\delta)\sqrt{\tau}\bigg)=0.

Hence, we obtain from (
4.5
) and (
4.10
) that

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathbb{E}\Big(e^{X_{\tau}}{\bf 1}_{\{X_{\tau}\geq\ln(1+\zeta(\tau))+(r-\delta)\tau\}}\Big)=\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathbb{E}\Big(e^{(r-\delta)\tau+X_{\tau}}{\bf 1}_{\{X_{\tau}\geq\ln(1+\zeta(\tau))+(r-\delta)\tau\}}\Big)=0,

and so

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(e^{(r-\delta)\tau+X_{\tau}}-1-\zeta(\tau)\big)^{+}\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big(e^{X_{\tau}}{\bf 1}_{\{X_{\tau}\geq\ln(1+\zeta(\tau))+(r-\delta)\tau\}}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad-\big(1+\zeta(\tau)\big)\mathbb{P}\big(X_{\tau}\geq\ln(1+\zeta(\tau))+(r-\delta)\tau\big)+o(\tau),\quad\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}.

This, together with (
4.4
), implies that, as
\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}
,

(4.11)

\displaystyle(r-\delta)\tau=\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(e^{X_{\tau}}\!-\!1\!-\!\zeta(\tau)\big){\bf 1}_{\{X_{\tau}\geq\ln(1+\zeta(\tau))+(r-\delta)\tau\}}\Big)+o(\tau)\leq\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(e^{X_{\tau}}\!-\!1\!-\!\zeta(\tau)\big)^{+}\Big)+o(\tau).\qquad

Using (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) as well as the independence among
X^{W}
,
J^{+}
, and
J^{-}
, the expectation on the right-hand side of (
4.11
) can be bounded by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\!\big(e^{X_{\tau}}\!\!-\!1\!-\!\zeta(\tau)\big)^{+}\!\Big)\!

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Big(\!\big(e^{X_{\tau}^{W}\!+J_{\tau}^{+}\!+J_{\tau}^{-}}\!\!-\!1\!-\!\zeta(\tau)\big)^{+}\!\Big)\!\!\leq\!\mathbb{E}\Big(\!\big(e^{J_{\tau}^{+}}\!\!-\!1\big)e^{X_{\tau}^{W}\!+J_{\tau}^{-}}\Big)\!\!+\!\mathbb{E}\Big(\!\big(e^{X_{\tau}^{W}\!+J_{\tau}^{-}}\!\!-\!1\!-\!\zeta(\tau)\big)^{+}\!\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big(e^{J_{\tau}^{+}}-1\big)\mathbb{E}\big(e^{X_{\tau}^{W}}\big)\mathbb{E}\big(e^{J_{\tau}^{-}}\big)+\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(e^{X_{\tau}^{W}+J_{\tau}^{-}}-1-\zeta(\tau)\big)^{+}\Big)

\displaystyle=\exp\bigg(\tau\int_{(0,\infty)}\big(e^{z}-1\big)\nu(dz)\bigg)-1+\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(e^{X_{\tau}^{W}+J_{\tau}^{-}}-1-\zeta(\tau)\big)^{+}\Big)

\displaystyle=\tau\int_{(0,\infty)}\big(e^{z}-1\big)\nu(dz)+\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(e^{X_{\tau}^{W}+J_{\tau}^{-}}-1-\zeta(\tau)\big)^{+}\Big)+o(\tau),\quad\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}.

Together with (
1.3
) and (
4.11
), we deduce that

(4.12)

\displaystyle d\tau\leq\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(e^{X_{\tau}^{W}+J_{\tau}^{-}}-1-\zeta(\tau)\big)^{+}\Big)+o(\tau),\quad\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}.

Next, we will derive an upper bound for
\mathbb{E}((e^{X_{\tau}^{W}+J_{\tau}^{-}}-1-\zeta(\tau))^{+})
as
\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}
. For any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, in view of Lemma
4.5
, we take
\tau\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
small enough so that
\tau\leq\ln^{2}(1+\zeta(\tau))/[\tau(\nu((-\infty,-\varepsilon))-\sigma^{2}/2)^{2}]
. By Lemma
4.6
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\!\big(e^{X_{\tau}^{W}\!+J_{\tau}^{-}}\!\!-\!1\!-\!\zeta(\tau)\big)^{+}\!\Big)\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\int_{\ln(1+\zeta(\tau))}^{\infty}\!\!e^{y}\mathbb{P}\big(X_{\tau}^{W}\!\!+\!J_{\tau}^{-}\!\!\geq\!y\big)dy\!=\!\sqrt{\tau}\!\!\int_{\ln(1+\zeta(\tau))/\sqrt{\tau}}^{\infty}\!\!e^{\sqrt{\tau}z}\mathbb{P}\big(X_{\tau}^{W}\!\!+\!J_{\tau}^{-}\!\!\geq\!\!\sqrt{\tau}z\big)dz

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\tau}\int_{\ln(1+\zeta(\tau))/\sqrt{\tau}}^{\infty}e^{\sqrt{\tau}z}\exp\bigg(\!\!-\!\frac{1}{2L}\bigg[z-\sqrt{\tau}\bigg(\nu((-\infty,-\varepsilon))-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\bigg)\bigg]^{2}\bigg)dz

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{L\tau}\big(1+O(\sqrt{\tau})\big)\int_{[\ln(1+\zeta(\tau))-\tau(L+\nu((-\infty,-\varepsilon))-\sigma^{2}/2)]/\sqrt{L\tau}}^{\infty}e^{-x^{2}/2}\,dx,

where we used change of variable
{x}=\sqrt{L}\omega+\sqrt{\tau}(L+\nu((-\infty,-\varepsilon))-\sigma^{2}/2)
in the last inequality, and
L=L(\varepsilon;\sigma)
is given as in (
4.7
). In view of Lemma
4.5
, the lower limit of the last integral above explodes as
\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}
. Hence, by (
A.2
) we obtain that, as
\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}
,

(4.13)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\!\big(e^{X_{\tau}^{W}\!+J_{\tau}^{-}}\!\!-\!1\!-\!\zeta(\tau)\big)^{+}\!\Big)\!\leq\!\sqrt{2\pi L\tau}\big(1\!+\!O(\sqrt{\tau})\big)\phi\bigg(\frac{\ln\!\big(1\!+\!\zeta(\tau)\big)\!-\!\tau\big(L\!+\!\nu((-\infty,-\varepsilon))\!-\!\sigma^{2}\!/2\big)}{\sqrt{L\tau}}\!\bigg).\qquad

Combining (
4.12
) and (
4.13
), we deduce that

\displaystyle d\sqrt{\tau}+o\big(\sqrt{\tau}\big)\leq\sqrt{2\pi L}\big(1+O(\sqrt{\tau})\big)\phi\bigg(\frac{\ln\big(1+\zeta(\tau)\big)-\tau\big(L+\nu((-\infty,-\varepsilon))-\sigma^{2}/2\big)}{\sqrt{L\tau}}\bigg),\quad\tau\rightarrow 0^{+},

and so

\displaystyle\limsup_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\ln\tau}{2\zeta^{2}(\tau)/\tau}\leq-\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\big[\ln\big(1+\zeta(\tau)\big)-\tau\big(L+\nu((-\infty,-\varepsilon))-\sigma^{2}/2\big)\big]^{2}}{2L\zeta^{2}(\tau)}=-\frac{1}{2L},

or equivalently,

\displaystyle\limsup_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\zeta(\tau)}{\sqrt{-\tau\ln\tau}}\leq\sqrt{L}.

Finally, by taking
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
above and noting from (

\displaystyle\limsup_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\zeta(\tau)}{\sqrt{-\tau\ln\tau}}\leq\sigma,

which completes the proof of the proposition.

4.2.
Step 2: The difference between European and American critical prices

To study the asymptotic behavior of the difference between the European and American critical prices, we first recall some regularity results on the American put price
P
defined as in (
2.3
). It is more convenient to state those results after a logarithmic change of variable. Define

(4.14)

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}(t,x):=P\big(t,e^{x}\big),\quad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R},

Since
x\mapsto e^{x}
is increasing and convex on
\mathbb{R}
, it follows from the analogous properties of
P(t,\cdot)
that
\widetilde{P}(t,\cdot)
is non-increasing and convex on
\mathbb{R}
, for any
t\in[0,T]
. Let
\widetilde{\mathscr{A}}
be the infinitesimal generator of
\widetilde{X}:=(\widetilde{X}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
, where
\widetilde{X}_{t}:=(r-\delta)t+X_{t}
, namely,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathscr{A}}f(x):=\bigg(r-\delta-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\bigg)f^{\prime}(x)+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(x)+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}\big(f(x+z)-f(x)-f^{\prime}(x)\big(e^{z}-1\big)\big)\nu(dz).

The following result (cf.
[
5
, Theorem 3.3]
) shows that the American put price satisfies a variational inequality in the sense of distributions.

Theorem 4.8
.

The distribution
(\partial/\partial t+\widetilde{\mathscr{A}}-r)\widetilde{P}
is a nonpositive measure on
(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}
. Moreover,
(\partial/\partial t+\widetilde{\mathscr{A}}-r)\widetilde{P}=0
on the continuation region
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}:=\{(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}:\widetilde{P}(t,x)>(K-e^{x})^{+}\}
.

The following result provides the estimation of the difference
b_{e}(t)-b(t)
near maturity.

Proposition 4.9
.

Assume
\sigma>0
and
d>0
. Then we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\rightarrow T^{-}}\frac{b_{e}(t)-b(t)}{\sqrt{T-t}}<\infty.

Proof.
Since
\widetilde{P}(\cdot,x)
is non-increasing on
[0,T]
, for each
x\in\mathbb{R}
, it follows from Theorem
4.8
that

(4.15)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathscr{A}}\,\widetilde{P}=r\widetilde{P}-\frac{\partial\widetilde{P}}{\partial t}\geq r\widetilde{P}\geq 0\,\,\,\text{ on }\,\widetilde{\mathcal{C}},

in the sense of distribution, or, equivalently,

\displaystyle\bigg(r-\delta-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\bigg)\frac{\partial\widetilde{P}}{\partial x}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\widetilde{P}}{\partial x^{2}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}\bigg(\widetilde{P}(\cdot\,,\cdot+z)-\widetilde{P}(\cdot\,,\cdot)-\frac{\partial\widetilde{P}}{\partial x}(\cdot\,,\cdot)\big(e^{z}-1\big)\bigg)\nu(dz)\geq 0\,\,\,\text{ on }\,\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}.

Since
\widetilde{P}(t,\cdot)
is convex on
\mathbb{R}
for any
t\in[0,T]
, the right partial derivative of
\widetilde{P}(t,\cdot)
,

(4.16)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{+}\widetilde{P}(t,x)}{\partial x}:=\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\widetilde{P}(t,x+h)-\widetilde{P}(t,x)}{h},

is a well-defined function on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
, and we also have
\partial\widetilde{P}/\partial x=\partial_{+}\widetilde{P}/\partial x
on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
in the sense of distribution. With the notion of
d
defined as in (
1.3
), we deduce that

(4.17)

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\bigg(\frac{\partial^{2}\widetilde{P}}{\partial x^{2}}-\frac{\partial\widetilde{P}}{\partial x}\bigg)+\mathcal{I}\geq-d\frac{\partial_{+}\widetilde{P}}{\partial x}-\int_{(0,\infty)}\sup_{y\in(\ln b(t),\ln K)}\big|\widetilde{P}(\cdot\,,y+z)-\widetilde{P}(\cdot\,,y)\big|\,\nu(dz)\,\,\,\text{ on }\,\widetilde{\mathcal{C}},\qquad

in the sense of distribution, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(t,x):=\int_{(-\infty,0)}\bigg(\widetilde{P}(t,x+z)-\widetilde{P}(t,x)-\frac{\partial_{+}\widetilde{P}}{\partial x}(t,x)\big(e^{z}-1\big)\bigg)\nu(dz),\quad(t,x)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}.

Recall that for each
t\in(0,T)
,
P(t,\cdot)
is non-increasing and Lipschitz on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
(see Proposition
2.4
), and thus, for any
y\in(\ln b(t),\ln K)
and
z\in(0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle 0\leq\widetilde{P}(t,y)-\widetilde{P}(t,y+z)=P\big(t,e^{y}\big)-P\big(t,e^{y+z}\big)\leq e^{y}\big(e^{z}-1\big)\leq K\big(e^{z}-1\big),

and

\displaystyle 0\leq\widetilde{P}(t,y)-\widetilde{P}(t,y+z)=P\big(t,e^{y}\big)-P\big(t,e^{y+z}\big)\leq P\big(t,b(t)\big)=\big(K-b(t)\big)\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{as }\,t\rightarrow T^{-}.

Hence, the dominated convergence theorem ensures that

(4.18)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow T^{-}}\int_{(0,\infty)}\sup_{y\in(\ln b(t),\ln K)}\big|\widetilde{P}(t,y+z)-\widetilde{P}(t,y)\big|\,\nu(dz)=0.

Now for any
t\in(0,T)
and
x\in(\ln b(t),\ln b_{e}(t))
, using (
4.14
), the convexity of
P(t,\cdot)
, and the fact that
(P-P_{e})(t,\cdot)
is non-increasing on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
(cf.
[
7
, Corollary 3.1]
), we have (with
\tau=T-t
)

\displaystyle e^{-x}\frac{\partial_{+}\widetilde{P}}{\partial x}(t,x)=\frac{\partial_{+}P}{\partial s}(t,e^{x})\leq\frac{\partial_{-}P}{\partial s}\big(t,b_{e}(t)\big)\leq\frac{\partial_{-}P_{e}}{\partial s}\big(t,b_{e}(t)\big)=-\mathbb{E}\bigg(e^{-\delta\tau+X_{\tau}}{\bf 1}_{\{b_{e}(t)e^{(r-\delta)\tau+X_{\tau}}\leq K\}}\!\bigg),

where the left-derivatives
\partial_{-}{P}/\partial x
and
\partial_{-}{P}_{e}/\partial x
are defined analogously to (
4.16
), but taking
h\rightarrow 0^{-}
. Using (
4.3
), Lemmas
4.2
and
4.5
, and the martingale property of
(e^{X_{t}})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
, we have

\displaystyle-\!\!\lim_{t\rightarrow T^{-}}\!\!\frac{\partial_{+}P}{\partial s}(t,e^{x})

\displaystyle\geq\!\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}\!\!\mathbb{E}\bigg(e^{-\delta\tau+X_{\tau}}{\bf 1}_{\{b_{e}(t)e^{(r-\delta)\tau+X_{\tau}}\leq K\}}\!\bigg)=\!\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}\!\!\mathbb{E}\bigg(e^{-\delta\tau+X_{\tau}}{\bf 1}_{\{(r-\delta)\tau+X_{\tau}\leq\ln(1+\zeta(\tau))\}}\!\bigg)

(4.19)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathbb{E}\bigg(e^{-\delta\tau+X_{\tau}}{\bf 1}_{\{((r-\delta)\tau+X_{\tau})/\sqrt{\tau}\leq\ln(1+\zeta(\tau))/\sqrt{\tau}\}}\bigg)=\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathbb{E}\big(e^{X_{\tau}}\big)=1.

By combining (
4.18
) and (
4.19
), we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\rightarrow T^{-}}\inf_{x\in(\ln b(t),\ln b_{e}(t))}\bigg(\!-d\,\frac{\partial_{+}\widetilde{P}}{\partial x}(t,x)-\int_{(0,\infty)}\sup_{y\in(\ln b(t),\ln K)}\big|\widetilde{P}(t,y+z)-\widetilde{P}(t,y)\big|\,\nu(dz)\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad=\liminf_{t\rightarrow T^{-}}\inf_{s\in(b(t),b_{e}(t))}\bigg(-ds\frac{\partial_{+}P}{\partial s}(t,s)-\int_{(0,\infty)}\sup_{u\in(b(t),K)}\big|P(t,ue^{z})-P(t,u)\big|\,\nu(dz)\bigg)\geq dK.

Hence, we can choose
\rho\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
t\in(T-\rho,T)
and
x\in(\ln b(t),\ln b_{e}(t))
,

\displaystyle-d\,\frac{\partial_{+}\widetilde{P}}{\partial x}(t,x)-\int_{(0,\infty)}\sup_{y\in(\ln b(t),\ln K)}\big|\widetilde{P}(t,y+z)-\widetilde{P}(t,y)\big|\,\nu(dz)\geq\frac{dK}{2}.

Together with (
4.17
), we obtain that, in the sense of distribution,

(4.20)

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\bigg(\frac{\partial^{2}\widetilde{P}}{\partial x^{2}}-\frac{\partial\widetilde{P}}{\partial x}\bigg)+\mathcal{I}\geq\frac{dK}{2}\,\,\,\text{ on }\,\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\rho},

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\rho}:=\big\{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}:t\in(T-\rho,T),x\in(\ln b(t),\ln b_{e}(t))\big\}.

Next we will derive an upper bound for
\mathcal{I}(t,x)
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}
. To begin with, by (
4.14
) we first have

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(t,x)=\left(\int_{(-\infty,\ln b(t)-x]}+\int_{(\ln b(t)-x,0)}\right)\bigg(P(t,e^{x+z})-P(t,e^{x})-e^{x}\frac{\partial_{+}P}{\partial s}(t,e^{x})\big(e^{z}-1\big)\bigg)\nu(dz),

for any
(t,x)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}
. Note that, for any
z\in(-\infty,\ln b(t)-x]
, we have

\displaystyle P(t,e^{x+z})-P(t,e^{x})-e^{x}\frac{\partial_{+}P}{\partial s}(t,e^{x})\big(e^{z}-1\big)=\big(K-e^{x+z}\big)-P(t,e^{x})-e^{x}\frac{\partial_{+}P}{\partial s}(t,e^{x})\big(e^{z}-1\big)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\big(K-e^{x+z}\big)-\big(K-e^{x}\big)-e^{x}\frac{\partial_{+}P}{\partial s}(t,e^{x})\big(e^{z}-1\big)=e^{x}\bigg(\frac{\partial_{+}P}{\partial s}(t,e^{x})+1\bigg)\big(1-e^{z}\big),

while for any
z\in(\ln b(t)-x,0)
, we have from the convexity of
P(t,\cdot)
that

\displaystyle P(t,e^{x+z})-P(t,e^{x})-e^{x}\frac{\partial_{+}P}{\partial s}(t,e^{x})\big(e^{z}-1\big)\leq e^{x}\big(e^{z}-1\big)\bigg(\frac{\partial_{+}P}{\partial s}\big(t,e^{x+z}\big)-\frac{\partial_{+}P}{\partial s}(t,e^{x})\bigg).

6.
Conclusions

The present work study the convergence rate of the critical price of an American put option near-maturity under an exponential Lévy model with a nonzero Brownian component. We focus on two important scenarios which were not investigated in the literature. Namely, we consider the models with negative jumps of infinite variation when the critical price converges to the strike price and the models with infinite activity jumps when the critical price tends to a value strictly lower than the strike price. In both scenarios, the convergence rate is shown to be of order
O(\sqrt{T-t})
with explicit constants proportionality. As a by product, we obtain a second-order near-maturity expansion of the American put price around the critical price.

\displaystyle\frac{J_{t}^{-}}{\sqrt{t}}\;{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}}\;0,\quad t\rightarrow 0^{+}.

Proof of Lemma

Together with Lemma

-(b), we obtain that

(B.28)

\displaystyle g(t,u)\geq rK-\delta s-\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}\left(P_{e}(t,ue^{z})-P_{e}(t,u)+u\big(e^{z}-1\big)\right)\nu(dz)+o(\sqrt{\theta}),\quad\theta\rightarrow 0^{+}.

In view of (
2.2
) and the martingale property of
(e^{X_{t}})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}=(e^{-(r-\delta)t+\widetilde{X}_{t}})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
,

(B.29)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}\left(P_{e}(t,ue^{z})-P_{e}(t,u)+u\big(e^{z}-1\big)\right)\nu(dz)

(B.30)

\displaystyle\quad=e^{-r\theta}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(K-ue^{z}e^{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}\big)^{+}-\big(K-ue^{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}\big)^{+}+ue^{\delta\theta}\big(e^{z}-1\big)e^{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}\Big)\nu(dz)

(B.31)

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(K-ue^{z}e^{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}\big)^{+}-\big(K-ue^{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}\big)^{+}+u\big(e^{z}-1\big)e^{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}\Big)\nu(dz)+O(\theta),\quad\theta\rightarrow 0^{+},

we thus deduce that, for
u\in[b(t),s]
where
s\in(b(t),b_{e}(t)\wedge b(T))
and
t\in(0,T)
,

(B.32)

\displaystyle g(t,u)\!\geq\!rK\!-\!\delta s\!-\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}\!\!\mathbb{E}\Big(\!\big(K\!-\!ue^{z}e^{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}\big)^{+}\!\!\!-\!\big(K\!-\!ue^{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}\big)^{+}\!\!\!+\!u\big(e^{z}\!-\!1\big)e^{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}\Big)\nu(dz)\!+\!o(\sqrt{\theta}),\,\,\,\,\theta\rightarrow 0^{+}.\qquad

We will estimate the integral term above over various subsets of
\mathbb{R}_{0}
. For this purpose, we set

\displaystyle I(A):=\int_{A}\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(K-ue^{z}e^{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}\big)^{+}-\big(K-ue^{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}\big)^{+}+u\big(e^{z}-1\big)e^{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}\Big)\nu(dz),\quad A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}_{0}).

First, for
A_{1}=(-\infty,0)
, we have

(B.33)

\displaystyle I(A_{1})=\int_{A_{1}}\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(K-ue^{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}\big){\bf 1}_{\{z+\widetilde{X}_{\theta}<\ln(K/u)\leq\widetilde{X}_{\theta}\}}+u\big(e^{z}-1\big)e^{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}{\bf 1}_{\{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}\geq\ln(K/u)-z\}}\Big)\nu(dz)\leq 0.\qquad

Next, for
A_{2}=(0,\ln(K/b(T))/2)
, we have

\displaystyle I(A_{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{A_{2}}\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(ue^{z}e^{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}-K\big){\bf 1}_{\{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}<\ln(K/u)\leq\widetilde{X}_{\theta}+z\}}+u\big(e^{z}-1\big)e^{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}{\bf 1}_{\{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}\geq\ln(K/u)\}}\Big)\nu(dz)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{A_{2}}\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(ue^{z}e^{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}-ue^{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}\big){\bf 1}_{\{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}<\ln(K/u)\leq\widetilde{X}_{\theta}+z\}}+u\big(e^{z}-1\big)e^{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}{\bf 1}_{\{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}\geq\ln(K/u)\}}\Big)\nu(dz)

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{A_{2}}(e^{z}-1)\nu(dz)\cdot 2\mathbb{E}\Big(e^{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}{\bf 1}_{\{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}\geq\ln(K/b(T))/2\}}\Big).

Using an argument similar to those leading to (
5.11
) together with Hölder’s inequality, we deduce that, for some
p\in(1,2)
and
q>2
with
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
,

(B.34)

\displaystyle I(A_{2})\leq 2K\!\int_{A_{2}}\!(e^{z}-1)\nu(dz)\!\cdot\!\left(\mathbb{E}\big(e^{q\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}\big)\right)^{1/q}\!\left(\mathbb{P}\big(\widetilde{X}_{\theta}\geq\ln(K/b(T))/2\big)\right)^{1/p}\!\!=o(\sqrt{\theta}),\,\,\,\,\theta\rightarrow 0^{+}.\qquad\,

Finally, for
A_{3}=[\ln(K/b(T))/2,\ln(K/b(T)))
, by a similar argument leading to (
B.34
) we first have

\displaystyle I(A_{3})

\displaystyle=\int_{A_{3}}\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(ue^{z}e^{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}-K\big){\bf 1}_{\{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}<\ln(K/u)\leq\widetilde{X}_{\theta}+z\}}+u\big(e^{z}-1\big)e^{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}{\bf 1}_{\{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}\geq\ln(K/u)\}}\Big)\nu(dz)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{A_{3}}\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(ue^{z}e^{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}-ue^{z}\big){\bf 1}_{\{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}<\ln(K/u)\leq\widetilde{X}_{\theta}+z\}}\Big)\,\nu(dz)+o(\sqrt{\theta})

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{A_{3}}e^{z}\,\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(e^{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}-1\big){\bf 1}_{\{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}<\ln(K/u)\leq\widetilde{X}_{\theta}+z\}}\Big)\,\nu(dz)+o(\sqrt{\theta}),\quad\theta\rightarrow 0^{+}.

Using the independence between
(W_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
and
(\widetilde{Z}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
, we see that

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(e^{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}-e^{\sigma W_{\theta}}\big){\bf 1}_{\{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}<\ln(K/u)\leq\widetilde{X}_{\theta}+z\}}\Big)\right|\leq\mathbb{E}\big(e^{\sigma W_{\theta}}\big)\mathbb{E}\left(\big|e^{\widetilde{Z}_{\theta}}-1\big|\right)=O(\theta),\quad\theta\rightarrow 0^{+}.

Hence, we deduce that

(B.35)

\displaystyle I(A_{3})

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{A_{3}}e^{z}\,\mathbb{E}\Big(\big|e^{\sigma W_{\theta}}-1\big|{\bf 1}_{\{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}<\ln(K/u)\leq\widetilde{X}_{\theta}+z\}}\Big)\,\nu(dz)+o(\sqrt{\theta})

(B.36)

\displaystyle\leq\sigma K\sqrt{\theta}\int_{A_{3}}e^{z}\,\mathbb{E}\Big(|W_{1}|{\bf 1}_{\{W_{1}\geq(\ln(K/b(T))-z-\widetilde{Z}_{\theta})/\sqrt{\theta}\}}\Big)\,\nu(dz)+o(\sqrt{\theta})

(B.37)

\displaystyle\leq\sigma K\sqrt{\theta}\!\int_{A_{3}}\!\!e^{z}\!\Big(\mathbb{P}\big(W_{1}\!\geq\!(\ln(K/b(T))\!-\!z\!-\!\widetilde{Z}_{\theta})/\sqrt{\theta}\big)\Big)^{1/2}\!\!\nu(dz)\!+\!o(\sqrt{\theta})\!=\!o(\sqrt{\theta}),\,\,\,\theta\rightarrow 0^{+}.\qquad\,

Combining (
B.32
), (
B.33
), (
B.34
), and (
B.37
), we obtain that, as
\theta\rightarrow 0^{+}
,

\displaystyle g(t,u)\geq rK-\delta s-\int_{[\ln(K/b(T)),\infty)}\mathbb{E}\Big(\!\big(K\!-\!ue^{z}e^{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}\big)^{+}\!\!\!-\!\big(K\!-\!ue^{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}\big)^{+}\!\!\!+\!u\big(e^{z}\!-\!1\big)e^{\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}\Big)\nu(dz)+o(\sqrt{\theta}).

Moreover, by (
B.27
), (
B.28
), and (
B.31
) (with
\mathbb{R}_{0}
replaced by
[\ln(K/b(T)),\infty)
), and noting that
P(t,u)>(K-u)
for
u>b(t)
, we have, for any
u\in[b(t),s]
,
s\in(b(t),b_{e}(t)\wedge b(T))
, and
t\in(0,T)
,

(B.38)

\displaystyle g(t,u)

\displaystyle\geq rK-\delta s-\int_{[\ln(K/b(T)),\infty)}\left(P(t,ue^{z})-P(t,u)+u\big(e^{z}-1\big)\right)\nu(dz)+o(\sqrt{\theta})

(B.39)

\displaystyle\geq rK-\delta s-\int_{[\ln(K/b(T)),\infty)}\left(P(t,ue^{z})-\big(K-ue^{z}\big)\right)\nu(dz)+o(\sqrt{\theta}),\quad\theta\rightarrow 0^{+}.

We are left to estimate the integral term above.

In view of the early exercise premium formula (cf.
[
7
, Theorem 3.2]
), we have that

\displaystyle e(\theta,s)=\mathbb{E}\bigg(\int_{0}^{\theta}e^{-ru}\,\Psi\big(t+u,se^{\widetilde{X}_{u}}\big)du\bigg),

where

\displaystyle\Psi(t,s)=\bigg(rK-\delta s-\int_{(0,\infty)}\big(P(t,se^{z})-(K-se^{z})\big)\nu(dz)\bigg){\bf 1}_{\{s<b(t)\}},\quad(t,s)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}.

By Theorem
4.8
, the distribution
(\partial/\partial t+\widetilde{\mathscr{A}}-r)\widetilde{P}
is a nonpositive measure on
(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}
. On the other hand, by
[
7
, Proposition 3.1]
,

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\widetilde{\mathscr{A}}-r\bigg)\widetilde{P}(t,x)=-\Psi(t,e^{x})\quad dt\,dx\text{-a.e.}\,\,\text{on }(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}.

Since
\Psi
is continuous on
[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we deduce that
\Psi
is nonnegative on
[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Noting that the integral term in
\Psi
is nonnegative, we obtain that
0\leq\Psi(t,s)\leq rK
for any
(t,s)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
. It follows that
e(\theta,s)=O(\theta)
as
\theta\rightarrow 0^{+}
, and hence we have

(B.40)

\displaystyle\int_{[\ln(K/b(T)),\infty)}P(t,ue^{z})\,\nu(dz)=\int_{[\ln(K/b(T)),\infty)}P_{e}(t,ue^{z})\,\nu(dz)+o(\sqrt{\theta})

(B.41)

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{[\ln(K/b(T)),\infty)}\mathbb{E}\left(\big(K-s\,e^{z+\widetilde{X}_{\theta}}\big)^{+}\right)\nu(dz)+o(\sqrt{\theta})

(B.42)

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{[\ln(K/b(T)),\infty)}\mathbb{E}\left(\big(K-s\,e^{z+\sigma W_{\theta}}\big)^{+}\right)\nu(dz)+o(\sqrt{\theta})

(B.43)

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{[\ln(K/b(T)),\infty)}\mathbb{E}\left(\big(K-se^{z}(1+\sigma W_{\theta})\big)^{+}\right)\nu(dz)+o(\sqrt{\theta}),\quad\theta\rightarrow 0^{+}.

On the set
(\ln(K/b(T)),\infty)
, we have
b(T)e^{z}>K
, and so

\displaystyle\int_{(\ln(K/b(T)),\infty)}\mathbb{E}\left(\big(K-se^{z}(1+\sigma W_{\theta})\big)^{+}\right)\nu(dz)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{(\ln(K/b(T)),\infty)}\mathbb{E}\left(\big(b(T)e^{z}-se^{z}-se^{z}\sigma\sqrt{\theta}W_{1}\big){\bf 1}_{\{se^{z}\sigma\sqrt{\theta}W_{1}<(K-se^{z})\}}\big)\right)\nu(dz)

\displaystyle\quad=(b(T)-s)\int_{(\ln(K/b(T)),\infty)}e^{z}\,\mathbb{P}\bigg(W_{1}<\frac{Ke^{-z}s^{-1}-1}{\sigma\sqrt{\theta}}\bigg)\nu(dz)

\displaystyle\qquad-\sqrt{\theta}\sigma s\int_{(\ln(K/b(T)),\infty)}e^{z}\,\mathbb{E}\Big(W_{1}{\bf 1}_{\{W_{1}<(Ke^{-z}s^{-1}-1)/(\sigma\sqrt{\theta})\}}\Big)\nu(dz).

Since
s>b(t)
, for all
z>\ln(K/b(T))
we have
Ke^{-z}/s-1<Ke^{-z}/b(t)-1\leq 0
, which implies that

\displaystyle\lim_{\theta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathbb{P}\bigg(W_{1}<\frac{Ke^{-z}s^{-1}-1}{\sigma\sqrt{\theta}}\bigg)=0,\quad\lim_{\theta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathbb{E}\Big(|W_{1}|{\bf 1}_{\{W_{1}<(Ke^{-z}s^{-1}-1)/(\sigma\sqrt{\theta})\}}\Big)=0.

Therefore, by dominated convergence we obtain that, as
\theta\rightarrow 0^{+}
,

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\theta):=\int_{(\ln(K/b(T)),\infty)}e^{z}\,\mathbb{P}\bigg(W_{1}<\frac{Ke^{-z}s^{-1}-1}{\sigma\sqrt{\theta}}\bigg)\nu(dz)

\displaystyle\rightarrow 0,

\displaystyle\sqrt{\theta}\sigma s\int_{(\ln(K/b(T)),\infty)}e^{z}\,\mathbb{E}\Big(W_{1}{\bf 1}_{\{W_{1}<(Ke^{-z}s^{-1}-1)/(\sigma\sqrt{\theta})\}}\Big)\nu(dz)

\displaystyle=o(\sqrt{\theta}).

Consequently, we deduce that, with
\varepsilon(\theta)\rightarrow 0
as
\theta\rightarrow 0^{+}
,

(B.44)

\displaystyle\int_{(\ln(K/b(T)),\infty)}\mathbb{E}\left(\big(K-se^{z}(1+\sigma W_{\theta})\big)^{+}\right)\nu(dz)\leq(b(T)-s)\varepsilon(\theta)+o(\sqrt{\theta}),\quad\theta\rightarrow 0^{+}.

Moreover, on the singleton
\{\ln(K/b(T))\}
, since
s<b(T)
, we have

(B.45)

\displaystyle\int_{\{\ln(K/b(T))\}}\mathbb{E}\left(\big(K-se^{z}(1+\sigma W_{\theta})\big)^{+}\right)\nu(dz)

(B.46)

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\{\ln(K/b(T))\}}\big(K-se^{z}\big)\nu(dz)+\int_{\{\ln(K/b(T))\}}\mathbb{E}\left(\big(se^{z}\sigma W_{\theta}-(K-se^{z})\big)^{+}\right)\nu(dz)

(B.47)

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\{\ln(K/b(T))\}}\big(K-se^{z}\big)\nu(dz)+\frac{sK}{b(T)}\nu(\{\ln(K/b(T))\})\,\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(\sigma W_{\theta}-(s^{-1}b(T)-1)\big)^{+}\Big)

(B.48)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{\{\ln(K/b(T))\}}\big(K-se^{z}\big)\nu(dz)+K\nu(\{\ln(K/b(T))\})\,\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(\sigma W_{\theta}-\ln(b(T)/s)\big)^{+}\Big).

Combining (
B.43
), (
B.44
), and (
B.48
), we obtain that, as
\theta\rightarrow 0^{+}
,

(B.49)

\displaystyle\int_{[\ln(K/b(T)),\infty)}P(t,ue^{z})\,\nu(dz)

\displaystyle\leq(b(T)-s)\varepsilon(\theta)+\int_{\{\ln(K/b(T))\}}\big(K-se^{z}\big)\nu(dz)

(B.50)

\displaystyle\quad+K\nu(\{\ln(K/b(T))\})\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(\sigma W_{\theta}-\ln(b(T)/s)\big)^{+}\Big)+o(\sqrt{\theta}).\quad

Finally, combining (
B.39
) and (
B.50
), and noting that
b(T)
satisfies (
2.7
), we obtain that

\displaystyle g(t,u)

\displaystyle\geq rK\!-\!\delta s\!+\!\!\int_{(\ln(K/b(T)),\infty)}\!\!\!\!\big(K\!-\!se^{z}\big)\nu(dz)\!-\!(b(T)\!-\!s)\varepsilon(\theta)\!-\!K\lambda\mathbb{E}\Big(\!\big(\sigma W_{\theta}\!-\!\ln(b(T)/s)\big)^{+}\!\Big)\!+\!o(\sqrt{\theta})

\displaystyle=(b(T)-s)\bigg(\delta+\int_{(\ln(K/b(T)),\infty)}e^{z}\nu(dz)-\varepsilon(\theta)\bigg)-K\lambda\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(\sigma W_{\theta}-\ln(b(T)/s)\big)^{+}\Big)+o(\sqrt{\theta})

\displaystyle=\big(\bar{\delta}-\varepsilon(\theta)\big)(b(T)-s)-b(T)\bar{\delta}\lambda\beta\,\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(\sigma W_{\theta}-\ln(b(T)/s)\big)^{+}\Big)+o(\sqrt{\theta}),\quad\theta\rightarrow 0^{+},

which, together with (
B.25
), completes the proof of the lemma.
\Box

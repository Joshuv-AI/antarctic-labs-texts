Striking Limb Asymmetry in Linear Morphea
Authors: Clara Ureña-Paniego, Francisco Vilchez-Márquez
Published: 2026

Case Presentation

A 32-year-old female presented with a 15-year history of progressive atrophy of her right upper limb, along with intermittent arthralgias and weakness. Physical examination revealed severe cutaneous sclerosis, hyperpigmentation, and pronounced subcutaneous atrophy, with prominent tendons and superficial veins. The left arm was unaffected, creating a striking asymmetry (Figure 1). Laboratory tests showed a positive ANA (1:160, speckled pattern) and elevated anti- RNP antibodies (94 U/mL), without fulfilling classification criteria for systemic sclerosis or mixed connective tissue disease. Histopathology revealed thickened collagen bundles with perivascular lymphocytic infiltrate and loss of adnexal structures, consistent with linear morphea. The patient was treated with low-dose corticosteroids, methotrexate, and hydroxychloroquine, with limited response. Disease progression led to irreversible atrophy and functional impairment of the limb.

Comparison of the affected and unaffected upper limbs, showing profound unilateral cutaneous and subcutaneous atrophy with subtle hyperpigmentation of the right arm due to linear morphea.

Teaching Point

Linear morphea may present as unilateral limb atrophy with sclerosis and pigmentary changes, potentially mimicking neuromuscular or rheumatological disorders. In this case, the diagnosis became evident at first glance due to the pathognomonic clinical image—a vivid example of how dermatological inspection can provide immediate diagnostic clarity. Prompt recognition is crucial, as early treatment may prevent disfiguring sequelae. This case underscores the diagnostic value of dermatological pattern recognition in complex presentations and the need for early referral when skin changes accompany isolated limb dysfunction [1,2].

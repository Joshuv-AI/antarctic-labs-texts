Subconvexity for GL 2 × GL 2 \rm GL_{2}\times GL_{2} L L -functions in the depth aspect
Tengyou Zhu

Abstract
. Let f f and g g be holomorphic or Maass cusp forms for SL 2 ​ ( ℤ ) \rm SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}) and let χ \chi be a primitive Dirichlet character of prime power conductor q = p n q=p^{n} . For any given ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 , we establish the following subconvexity bound L ( 1 / 2 , f ⊗ g ⊗ χ ) ≪ f , g , ε q 9 / 10 + ε . L(1/2,f\otimes g\otimes\chi)\ll_{f,g,\varepsilon}q^{9/10+\varepsilon}. 1.
Introduction

The analytic behavior of
L
-functions on the critical line encodes essential arithmetic information, and the rate of growth along the critical line is of central importance in analytic number theory.
Let
L(s,f)
be a general
L
-function with an analytic conductor
\mathcal{Q}
.
In many applications of number theory, it is necessary to go beyond the convexity bound to get bounds of the form

(1.1)

L(1/2,f)\ll\mathcal{Q}^{1/4-\delta+\varepsilon}

for some absolute constant
\delta>0
.

The Rankin–Selberg convolution stands as a fundamental construction in the theory of

L
-functions, with its associated
L
-functions playing a pivotal role in modern number theory. These functions have demonstrated remarkable efficacy in resolving central problems across diverse areas, including establishing bounds for the generalized Ramanujan and Selberg conjectures, addressing the quantum unique ergodicity conjecture, and proving the strong multiplicity one theorem, and so on.
Particularly significant progress has been made in studying subconvexity estimates for

{\rm GL}_{2}\times{\rm GL}_{2}
Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions (see
[
1
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
9
]
).
In this paper, we investigate subconvexity bounds for
{\rm GL}_{2}\times{\rm GL}_{2}
Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions on the critical line in the depth aspect.

Let
f
and
g
be holomorphic or Maass cusp forms for
\rm SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

with normalized Fourier coefficients

\lambda_{f}(m)
and
\lambda_{g}(m)
(i.e.,
\lambda_{f}(1)=1
and
\lambda_{g}(1)=1
), respectively.
Let
\chi
be a primitive Dirichlet character of prime power conductor

q=p^{n}
, where
p
is prime and
n
is an integer.
The
L
-function associated with
f
and
g\otimes\chi
is given by

L(s,f\otimes g\otimes\chi)=L\big(2s,\chi^{2}\big)\sum_{m\geqslant 1}\frac{\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{g}(m)\chi(m)}{m^{s}}

for
\text{Re}(s)>1
, which can be analytically extended to
\mathbb{C}
and satisfies
a functional equation relating
s
and
1-s
. The approximate functional equation
and the Phragmén-Lindelöf principle imply that

L(1/2,f\otimes g\otimes\chi)\ll_{f,g,\varepsilon}q^{1+\varepsilon}

for any
\varepsilon>0
, which is the convexity bound in the depth aspect.
The aim of the present paper is to prove the following subconvexity bound
using the delta symbol approach.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p
be an odd prime.
Let
f
and
g
be holomorphic or Maass cusp forms for
\rm SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

and let
\chi
be a primitive Dirichlet character of prime power conductor
q=p^{n}
.
For any given
\varepsilon>0
, we have

L(1/2,f\otimes g\otimes\chi)\ll_{f,g,\varepsilon}q^{9/10+\varepsilon}.

This improves the previous result of Sun
[
18
]
, which states

L(1/2,f\otimes g\otimes\chi)\ll_{f,g,\varepsilon}p^{3/4}q^{15/16+\varepsilon}.

In the
t
-aspect, Huang, Sun and Zhang
[
5
, Corollary 1.5]
have proved that

L(1/2+it,f\otimes g)\ll_{f,g,\varepsilon}(1+|t|)^{9/10+\varepsilon}.

Indeed, in principle the family
L(1/2,f\otimes g\otimes\chi)
where
\chi
runs over
characters modulo
p^{n}
with
n\rightarrow\infty
should behave like
L(1/2+it,f\otimes g)

with
t\rightarrow\infty
. Since, both problems are arithmetic, we end up seeking
square root cancellations. Huang, Sun and Zhang
[
5
]
had to appeal to the
classical second derivative test for exponential integrals to show square root cancellations.
In our case, we achieve the required cancellations
by using the
p
-adic second derivative test
(
[
2
, Theorem 5]
) for exponential sums.

Remark 1.1
.

Since the holomorphic case is usually easier,
to simplify the argument, we prove Theorem
1.1
only for the case of Maass forms.
In fact, the Voronoi formula for Maass forms is
slightly more complicated than that of the holomorphic case.

Remark 1.2
.

Set
\mathcal{Q}=\big(q(1+|t|)\big)^{4}
, the hybrid subconvexity bounds

L(1/2+it,f\otimes g\otimes\chi)\ll_{f,g,\varepsilon}\mathcal{Q}^{1/4-1/40+\varepsilon}

can be proved by combining our approach and that of Huang, Sun and Zhang
[
5
]
,
but this requires a rigorous proof. The exponent
\delta=1/40
in (
1.1
) appears in other contexts as well and it seems to be the limit of the delta symbol approach.

1.1.
A brief history

We will briefly recall the history of the problem but focus only on the in-depth aspects.
Let
\chi
be a primitive Dirichlet character of prime power conductor
q=p^{n}
.
In the
{\rm GL}_{1}
case, by introducing a general new theory of estimation of
short exponential sums involving
p
-adic analytic phases,
Milićević
[
13
]
proved a sub-Weyl subconvexity bound for the central values
L(1/2,\chi)

of Dirichlet
L
-functions of the form

L(1/2,\chi)\ll p^{v}q^{\varpi}(\log q)^{1/2}

with a fixed
v
and
\varpi>\varpi_{0}\approx 0.1645
.
By developing a general result on
p
-adic approximation by rationals
(a
p
-adic counterpart to Farey dissection)
and a
p
-adic version of van der Corput’s method for exponential sums,
Blomer and Milićević
[
2
]

also considered the
{\rm GL}_{2}
case. For
g
being a holomorphic or
Maass newform for
{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, they showed that

L\left(1/2+it,g\otimes\chi\right)\ll_{g,\varepsilon}(1+|t|)^{5/2}p^{7/6}q^{1/3+\varepsilon},

where the implied constant on
p
and
t
is explicit and polynomial.
Using the conductor-lowering trick introduced by Munshi in
[
15
]
,
Munshi and Singh
[
14
]
proved the same result using the approach in
[
15
]
.
Sun and Zhao
[
17
]
extended Munshi and Singh’s results
in
[
14
]
to the
{\rm GL}_{3}
case and proved that for

\pi
being a Hecke-Maass cusp form for
{\rm SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
,

L\left(1/2,\pi\otimes\chi\right)\ll_{\pi,\varepsilon}p^{3/4}q^{3/4-3/40+\varepsilon}

for any
\varepsilon>0
. Sun
[
18
]
also establishes
a subconvexity bound for the
\rm GL_{2}\times GL_{2}
case.
The ideas of treating character sums in the paper
[
17
]
and
[
18
]
can be
applied to prove a
{\rm GL}_{3}\times{\rm GL}_{2}
subconvexity bound of the form

L\left(1/2,\pi\otimes f\otimes\chi\right)\ll_{\pi,f,\varepsilon}q^{3/2-3/20+\varepsilon},

where
\pi
is a Hecke-Maass cusp form for
{\rm SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
,
f
is a holomorphic or Maass cusp form for
{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. This result has been proved by Kumar, Mallesham and Singh
[
10
]
.

1.2.
Principle of the proof

To illustrate the main idea of our approach, we provide
a quick sketch of the proof for Theorem
1.1
.
By the approximate functional equation, our starting point is to estimate

S(N)=\sum_{m\sim N}\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{g}(m)\chi(m),

with
N\sim q^{2}
. Applying the conductor-lowering mechanism
introduced by Munshi
[
16
]
, we have

S(N)=\mathop{\sum\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell,m\sim N\\
\ell\equiv m\bmod p^{r}\end{subarray}}\lambda_{g}(\ell)\lambda_{f}(m)\chi(m)\delta\bigg(\frac{m-\ell}{p^{r}}\bigg),

where
r\geqslant 2
is an integer to be chosen later. After
applying Duke, Friedlander and Iwaniec’s delta method with
c\leqslant C
(see (
3.1
))
and removing the congruence
\ell\equiv m\bmod p^{r}
by exponential sums we get

S(N)\approx\frac{1}{Cp^{r}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\sim C\\
(c,p)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{c}\;\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}\limits_{a\bmod cp^{r}}\mathop{\sum\sum}_{\ell,m\sim N}\lambda_{g}(\ell)\lambda_{f}(m)\chi(m)e\bigg(\frac{a(m-\ell)}{cp^{r}}\bigg),

where here and throughout, the
*
on the sum
\sum_{a\bmod c}
over
a
means that the sum is restricted to
(a,c)=1
, and we take
C=\sqrt{N/p^{r}}
. By using the Ramanujan conjecture on average, trivially we have
S(N)\ll N^{2}
.

We apply the voronoi summation formulas to both
m
and
\ell
sums.
Recall that
\chi
is of modulus
q=p^{n}
.
Then the conductor of the
m
-sum has the size
Cp^{n}
. Applying
\rm GL_{2}
Voronoi summation
to the
m
-sum we get that the dual sum is of size
C^{2}p^{2n}/N\asymp p^{2n-r}
.
The conductor for the
\ell
-sum has the size
cp^{r}
and the dual sum after

\rm GL_{2}
Voronoi summation
is essentially supported on summation of size
C^{2}p^{2r}/N\asymp p^{r}
.
Due to the presence of a Ramanujan sum in the character sum and the assumption of square-root cancellation for the remaining component, we achieve a saving of

\frac{N}{Cp^{n}}\times\frac{N}{Cp^{r}}\times Cp^{r/2}\sim N.

Hence we are at the threshold and need to save a little more.
Generally we arrive at an expression of the form

\sum_{c\sim C}\sum_{m\sim p^{2n-r}}\lambda_{f}(m)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\sim p^{r}\\
\ell\equiv p^{r}\overline{p^{2n-r}}m\bmod c\end{subarray}}\lambda_{g}(\ell)G(m,\ell,c),

where

\displaystyle G(m,\ell,c)=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{u\bmod p^{n}}\chi(u)S\big(u\overline{c},m\overline{c};p^{n}\big)S\big(u\overline{c},\ell\overline{c};p^{r}\big).

Next we apply the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to get rid of the Fourier coefficients.
Then we need to deal with

\sum_{c\sim C}\sum_{m\sim p^{2n-r}}\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\sim p^{r}\\
\ell\equiv p^{r}\overline{p^{2n-r}}m\bmod c\end{subarray}}\lambda_{g}(\ell)G(m,\ell,c)\bigg|^{2}.

After opening the square and applying Poisson summation to the sum over

m
, we obtain two dominant terms: Diagonal term (from
\ell_{1}=\ell_{2}
) with a
saving factor of
p^{r}/C
. Off-diagonal term (from
\ell_{1}\not=\ell_{2}
) with a
saving factor of

\frac{p^{n-2r}}{C}\times\frac{p^{n+r}}{Cp^{r}}=\frac{p^{n-2r}}{Cp^{r/2}}.

Therefore, we are able to save
p^{r}/C\sim p^{3r/2-n}
from the diagonal term
and
p^{2n-r}/(Cp^{r/2})\sim p^{n-r}

from the off-diagonal term. By setting the two savings to be of the same order,
the optimal choice for
r
is given by
r=4n/5
. In total, we have saved

N\times q^{1/10}
. It follows that

L(1/2,f\otimes g\otimes\chi)\ll N^{-1/2}S(N)\ll N^{1/2}q^{-1/10}\sim q^{1-1/10}.

In the following sections we shall provide the proof of the theorem in detail.

2.1.
Notation

We denote by
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
the ring of
p
-adic integers in the field
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
of
p
-adic numbers, and
by
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\setminus p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, the group of its units.
Then
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}\setminus\{0\}=\bigsqcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
; for
x\in p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
,
we write
\text{ord}_{p}x=k
.

We write
e(x)=e^{2\pi ix}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
, and we write
\theta:\mathbb{Q}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
for the standard additive character on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
trivial on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
Specifically, if
x=\sum_{j\geqslant j_{0}}a_{j}p^{j}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, then

\theta(x)=\exp\big(2\pi i\sum_{j<j_{0}}a_{j}p^{j}\big)
.
In particular, on
\mathbb{Z}[1/p]\subseteq\mathbb{Q}_{p}\cap\mathbb{R}
we have
\theta(\cdot)=e(\cdot)
, and
this relation is crucial for moving arithmetic oscillation between
p
-adic and archimedean
places.

If
k\geqslant 0
and a domain
T\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
are
such that
(T+p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p})\subseteq T
, and if
\psi
is
a
p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-periodic function, then by

\sum_{u\bmod p^{k},u\in T}\psi(u),

where the (finite) sum of
\psi(u)
over arbitrary set of representatives
of classes in
T/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

For
x\in\mathbb{Z}
coprime to the modulus (or sometimes for
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
),
the notation
\overline{x}
will always
denote the multiplicative inverse to a modulus which will be obvious from the context.
In particular, if no obvious modulus is specified, then
\bar{x}=x^{-1}

will denote the inverse of
x
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
,
which agrees with the multiplicative inverse of
x
to any prime power
p^{n}
.
While both
\overline{x}
and
x^{-1}
have the same meaning for
for aesthetic reasons we usually give preference to the
former in notations that explicitly depend only on the congruence class of
x
to an obvious
modulus (such as in factors, Kloosterman sums, and so on) and to the latter in expressions
of a more direct
p
-adic nature such as phases in the context of
p
-adic local analysis (even
when the expression happens to be locally constant).

We introduce the following terminology, including an auxiliary class of functions.

Definition 2.1

Let
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}
. We denote by
\mathbf{M}_{p^{\kappa}}
an

, which may

2.2.
Postnikov Formula

We recall the structure of multiplicative characters modulo
q=p^{n}
.
In this section, as everywhere else in the paper,
p
denotes an odd prime;
all statements hold with minor but necessary modifications in the case
p=2
.

Definition 2.2
.

The
p
-adic logarithm,
\log_{p}:1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is the analytic function given as

\log_{p}(1+x):=\sum_{j\geqslant 1}(-1)^{j-1}\frac{x^{j}}{j}.

Access to the above is critical due to the following lemma,
with roots in Postnikov and which we quote from
[
13
, Lemma 13]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\chi
be a primitive character modulo
p^{n}
. Then there exists a
p
-adic unit
\alpha
such that, for every
m\equiv 1\bmod p
,

(2.1)

\chi(m)=\theta\left(\frac{\alpha\log_{p}m}{p^{n}}\right).

In fact, we do not need the full strength of (
2.1
). Form
[
2
, (2.3)]
, we will use the following
corollary, valid for every
\kappa\geqslant 1
and every
u\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
,
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
:

(2.2)

\chi(u+p^{\kappa}t)=\chi(u)\chi(1+p^{\kappa}u^{-1}t)=\chi(u)\theta\left(\frac{\alpha}{p^{n}}\Big(\frac{1}{u}p^{\kappa}t-\frac{1}{2u^{2}}p^{2\kappa}t^{2}\Big)+\mathbf{M}_{p^{3\kappa-n-\iota}}\right),

in the sense of Definition
2.1
. Here we denote
\iota=1
if
p=3
and
\iota=0
otherwise.

2.3.
p
-adic square roots

It will be necessary to handle solutions to quadratic equations over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, which requires the use of
p
-adic square roots. While these square roots naturally arise in
p
-adic towers as
in
[
2
, Section 2.4]
, we keep our exposition elementary and only discuss square roots to a prime
power modulus
p^{n}
. For
p
an odd prime and
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times 2}
, the congruence
u^{2}\equiv x\bmod{p^{n}}
has exactly two solutions modulo every
p^{n}
, which reside within two
p
-adic towers and limit to the solutions of
u^{2}=x
as
n\to\infty
. We denote these solutions
\pm x_{1/2}
. For
(\,\cdot\,)_{1/2}:\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times 2}\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
to be well-defined, a choice of square root for each
y\in(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times 2}
must be made. This set of choices propagates to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times 2}
and represents one of the
2^{(p-1)/2}
branches of the
p
-adic square root. Finally,
x/x_{1/2}
and
(1/x_{1/2})^{2}=1/x
for every
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times 2}
, simply because
x_{1/2}^{2}=x
.

Next we note that, for every
\kappa\geqslant 1
and every
u\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times 2}
,
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
,

\left(u_{1/2}+\frac{1}{2u_{1/2}}p^{\kappa}t-\frac{1}{8u_{1/2}^{3}}p^{2\kappa}t^{2}\right)^{2}\in(u+p^{\kappa}t)+p^{3\kappa}\mathbb{Z}_{p},

so that

(u+p^{\kappa}t)_{1/2}\equiv u_{1/2}+\frac{1}{2u_{1/2}}p^{\kappa}t-\frac{1}{8u_{1/2}^{3}}p^{2\kappa}t^{2}\;\bmod\;p^{3\kappa}.

With the terminology of Definition
2.1
, we claim that actually

(2.3)

(u+p^{\kappa}t)_{1/2}\equiv u_{1/2}+\frac{1}{2u_{1/2}}p^{\kappa}t-\frac{1}{8u_{1/2}^{3}}p^{2\kappa}t^{2}+\mathbf{M}_{p^{3\kappa}}.

For future reference, we note that, for all
u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times 2}
,

(2.4)

\text{ord}_{p}\big((u_{1})_{1/2}-(u_{2})_{1/2}\big)=\text{ord}_{p}(u_{1}-u_{2}).

2.4.
Method of stationary phase

In this section, we collect facts about the so-called
p
-adic method of stationary phase, a powerful tool in the study of complete exponential sums modulo prime powers analogous to the classical method of stationary phase for oscillatory exponential integrals. For more details, we refer to
[
8
, Lemmata 12.2 and 12.3]
for a formulation with phases that are rational functions, or to
[
2
, Lemma 7]
for a general statement.

Let
p>2
be a prime. For
s\in\{0,1\}
and
(A,p)=1
, we define

\epsilon(A,p^{s})=\begin{cases}1,&{s=0,}\\
\big(\frac{A}{p}\big),&{s=1,\;p\equiv 1\bmod 4,}\\
\big(\frac{A}{p}\big)i,&{s=1,\;p\equiv 3\bmod 4.}\end{cases}

Lemma 2.2

(
p
-adic stationary phase)
.

Let
n\geqslant\kappa
, and let
T\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
be such
that
(T+p^{\kappa}\mathbb{Z}_{p})\subseteq T
.

(1)

Suppose that functions
\psi:T/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times},\,\phi_{1}:T\to\mathbb{Q}_{p}
satisfy

(2.5)

\psi(u+p^{\kappa}t)=\psi(u)\theta(\phi_{1}(u)\cdot p^{\kappa}t)

for all
u\in T
and
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Then, we have

\sum_{u\bmod p^{n},u\in T}\psi(u)=p^{n-\kappa}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\bmod p^{\kappa},u\in T\\
\phi_{1}(u)\in p^{-\kappa}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\end{subarray}}\psi(u).

(2)

Suppose that functions
\psi:T/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times},\,\phi_{1},\phi_{2}:T\to\mathbb{Q}_{p}
satisfy

(2.6)

\psi(u+p^{\kappa}t)=\psi(u)\theta\left(\phi_{1}(u)\cdot p^{\kappa}t+\frac{1}{2}\phi_{2}(u)\cdot p^{2\kappa}t^{2}\right)

for all
u\in T
and
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
Assume that
{\rm ord}_{p}\phi_{1}(u)\geqslant-n
and
{\rm ord}_{p}\phi_{2}(u)=\mu

for every
u\in T
, and that
-2n\leqslant\mu\leqslant-2\kappa
.
Then, writing
\mu=-2r-\rho
with
r\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\rho\in\{0,1\}
,

\sum_{u\bmod p^{n},u\in T}\psi(u)=p^{n+\mu/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\bmod p^{r},u\in T\\
\phi_{1}(u)\in p^{-r-\rho}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\end{subarray}}\psi(x)\epsilon\big(\bar{2}(\phi_{2}(u))_{0},p^{\rho}\big)\theta\bigg(-\frac{{\phi_{1}(u)^{2}}}{2\phi_{2}(u)}\bigg).

Proof.

The first claim follows immediately by orthogonality from

\displaystyle\sum_{u\bmod p^{n},u\in T}\psi(u)\;

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\bmod p^{\kappa},u\in T}\sum_{t\bmod p^{n-\kappa}}\psi(u+p^{\kappa}t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\bmod p^{\kappa},u\in T}\psi(u)\sum_{t\bmod p^{n-\kappa}}\theta(\phi_{1}(u)\cdot p^{\kappa}t).

The second claim is immediate if
n=2\kappa
. The odd case is similar, using the classical evaluation of the quadratic Gauss sum; see
[
2
, Lemma 7]
.

Lemma 2.4

(Second derivative test)
.

Let
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
, let
\kappa_{0},\upsilon,\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

and
T\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
be such that
(T+p^{\kappa_{0}}\mathbb{Z}_{p})\in T
, and let

\psi:T\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
,

\phi_{1}:T\to p^{-\upsilon}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,

\phi_{2}:T\to p^{-\lambda}\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
, and

\Omega:\{\kappa\in\mathbb{N}_{0};\kappa\geqslant\kappa_{0}\}\to\mathbb{N}_{0}

be functions such that
|\psi(u)|\leqslant\psi_{0}
,

(2.7)

\psi(u+p^{\kappa}t)=\psi(u)\theta\left(\phi_{1}(u)\cdot p^{\kappa}t+\frac{1}{2}\phi_{2}(u)\cdot p^{2\kappa}t^{2}+\mathbf{M}_{p^{\Omega(\kappa)}}\right)

Proof.

2.5.
Maass cusp forms for
\mathrm{GL}_{2}

We briefly review some basic facts of automorphic

-functions on
\rm\GL_{2}
.
Let
f
be a Hecke-Maass cusp form for
\rm SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

with Laplace eigenvalue
1/4+\mu_{f}^{2}
. Then
f
has a Fourier expansion

f(z)=\sqrt{y}\sum_{m\neq 0}\lambda_{f}(m)K_{i\mu_{f}}(2\pi|m|y)e(mx),

where
K_{i\mu_{f}}
is the modified Bessel function of the third kind.
We need the following average bound

(2.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{m\leqslant N}|\lambda_{f}(m)|^{2}=\mu_{f}N+O\big(N^{3/5}\big).

A ubiquitous tool in the analysis of
L
-functions inside the critical strip
is the approximate functional equation (see
[
8
, §5.2]
).
This equation has various manifestations depending on context and purpose.
For our purposes, the following lemma is convenient,
which follows by applying a dyadic partition of unity to
[
8
, Theorem 5.3]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\chi
be a primitive Dirichlet character modulo
q
. Then we have

L(1/2,f\otimes g\otimes\chi)\ll_{f,g,\varepsilon}q^{\varepsilon}\sup_{N\leqslant q^{2+\varepsilon}}\frac{|S(N)|}{\sqrt{N}}+q^{-A},

where

S(N)=\sum_{m\geqslant 1}\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{g}(m)\chi(m)V\left(\frac{m}{N}\right)

for some smooth function
V
supported in
[1,2]
and satisfying
V^{(j)}(x)\ll_{j}1
.

Now we turn to the Voronoi summation formula for
\rm SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

(see
[
14
, (1.12) and (1.15)]
and
[
9
, Theorem A.4]
).
Define

(2.9)

\displaystyle\gamma_{f}^{\pm}(s)=\frac{1}{2\pi^{1+2s}}\left\{\prod_{\pm}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1+s\pm\mu_{f}}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{-s\pm\mu_{f}}{2}\right)}\mp\prod_{\pm}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{2+s\pm\mu_{f}}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1-s\pm\mu_{f}}{2}\right)}\right\}.

Then we have the following Voronoi summation formula.

Lemma 2.6
.

For
h(x)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,\infty)
, we denote by
\widetilde{h}(s)
the Mellin transform of
h(x)
.
Let
a,\overline{a},c\in\mathbb{Z}
with

,
(a,c)=1

and
a\overline{a}\equiv 1\bmod c
. For

, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{m\geqslant 1}\lambda_{f}(m)e\left(\frac{am}{c}\right)h\left(\frac{m}{N}\right)=\frac{N}{c}\sum_{\pm}\sum_{m\geqslant 1}\lambda_{f}(m)e\left(\mp\frac{\overline{a}m}{c}\right)\Psi_{h}^{\pm}\left(\frac{mN}{c^{2}}\right),

where for
\sigma>-1
,

(2.10)

\displaystyle\Psi_{h}^{\pm}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma)}x^{-s}\gamma_{f}^{\pm}(s)\widetilde{h}(-s)\mathrm{d}s

(2.11)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}h(y)\mathcal{J}_{f}^{\pm}(4\pi\sqrt{xy})\mathrm{d}y,

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{f}^{+}(x)=\frac{-\pi}{\sin(\pi i\mu_{f})}\left(J_{2i\mu_{f}}(x)-J_{-2i\mu_{f}}(x)\right),

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{f}^{-}(x)=4\varepsilon_{f}\cosh(\pi\mu_{f})K_{2i\mu_{f}}(x).

The function
\Psi_{h}^{\pm}(x)
has the following asymptotic expansion when

Lemma 2.7
.

For

\displaystyle\Psi_{h}^{+}(x)=x^{-1/4}\int_{0}^{\infty}h(y)y^{-1/4}\sum_{j=0}^{J}\frac{c_{j}e(2\sqrt{xy})+d_{j}e(-2\sqrt{xy})}{(xy)^{j/2}}\mathrm{d}y+O_{t_{f},J}\left(x^{-J/2-3/4}\right),

for some any fixed integer
J\geqslant 1
and

\displaystyle\Psi_{h}^{-}(x)\ll_{t_{f},A}x^{-A}.

Proof.

See
[
12
, Lemma 3.4]
.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

Notice that the above Lemma are only valid for

. So we also need
the facts which state that, for

,
k\geqslant 0
and
\text{Re}(\nu)=0
. One has see
[

\displaystyle y^{k}J_{\nu}^{(k)}(x)\ll_{k,\nu}\frac{1}{(1+x)^{1/2}},\qquad y^{k}K_{\nu}^{(k)}(x)\ll_{k,\nu}\frac{e^{-x}(1+|\log x|)}{(1+x)^{1/2}}.

3.
The set-up

We recall the set-up and our general assumptions that will be in force for the rest of the paper.
Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
be such that (

)
holds for the character

in Theorem

Now we start to prove Theorem

. Recall that, Lemma
2.5
,
we are considering

with

. We shall establish the following bound.

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume

. Then we have

We take

3.1.
Applying the delta method

Define
\delta:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
with
\delta(0)=1
and
\delta(m)=0
for
m\neq 0
.
To separate oscillations from
\lambda_{g}(m)
and
\lambda_{f}(m)\chi(m)
,
we will use a version of the circle method by Duke, Friedlander and Iwaniec (see
[
8
, Chapter 20]
).
For any
m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
C\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\delta(m=0)=\frac{1}{C}\sum_{1\leqslant c\leqslant C}\;\frac{1}{c}\;\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{a\bmod{c}}e\left(\frac{ma}{c}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(c,\zeta)e\left(\frac{m\zeta}{cC}\right)\mathrm{d}\zeta,

where the
*
on the sum indicates that the sum over
a
is restricted to
(a,c)=1
.
The function
g
has the following properties
(see (20.158) and (20.159) of
[
8
]
)

(3.2)

\displaystyle g(c,\zeta)\ll|\zeta|^{-A},\quad g(c,\zeta)=1+O\left(\frac{C}{c}\left(\frac{c}{C}+|\zeta|\right)^{A}\right)

for any
A>1
and

(3.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial\zeta^{j}}g(c,\zeta)\ll|\zeta|^{-j}\min\left(|\zeta|^{-1},\frac{C}{c}\right)\log C,\quad j\geqslant 1.

In particular the first property in (
3.2
) implies that
the effective range of the integration in
(
3.1
) is
[-C^{\varepsilon},C^{\varepsilon}]
.

In order to use the delta symbol method in (
3.1
), we proceed as
[
11
, (3.7)]

(see
[
18
, Lemma 1]
and
[
19
, Lemma 3.2 ]
for more details)
by writing
\delta(m=0)
in a more analytic form:

\displaystyle\delta(m=0)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{[\log C/\log p]}\frac{1}{C}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant c\leqslant C/p^{k}\\
(c,p)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{cp^{r+k}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{a\bmod cp^{r+k}}e\left(\frac{am}{cp^{r+k}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(p^{k}c,\zeta)e\left(\frac{m\zeta}{cCp^{r+k}}\right)\mathrm{d}\zeta

(3.4)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\lambda=0}^{r}\frac{1}{C}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant c\leqslant C\\
(c,p)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{cp^{r}}\;\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{a\bmod cp^{r-\lambda}}e\left(\frac{am}{cp^{r-\lambda}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(c,\zeta)e\left(\frac{m\zeta}{cCp^{r}}\right)\mathrm{d}\zeta,

where
r\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Instead of using the entire modulus
q
for the conductor-lowering mechanism,
we only use a part
p^{r}
, where
r<n
is optimally chosen later.
This introduces more terms on the diagonal while having less impact on the off-diagonal.

Now we write

S(N)=\sum_{\ell\geqslant 1}\lambda_{g}(\ell)W\left(\frac{\ell}{N}\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\geqslant 1\\
p^{r}|m-\ell\end{subarray}}\lambda_{f}(m)\chi(m)V\left(\frac{m}{N}\right)\delta\left(\frac{m-\ell}{p^{r}}\right),

where
W
is a smooth function supported in
[1/2,5/2]
,
W(x)=1

for
x\in[1,2]
and
W^{(j)}(x)\ll_{j}1
.
Applying (
3.4
) with
C=\sqrt{N/p^{r}},
we have

\displaystyle S(N)=\,

\displaystyle\sum_{m\geqslant 1}\sum_{\ell\geqslant 1}\lambda_{g}(\ell)W\left(\frac{\ell}{N}\right)\lambda_{f}(m)\chi(m)V\left(\frac{m}{N}\right)

\displaystyle\times\bigg\{\sum_{\lambda=0}^{r}\frac{1}{C}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant c\leqslant C\\
(c,p)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{cp^{r}}\;\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{a\bmod cp^{r-\lambda}}e\left(\frac{a(m-\ell)}{cp^{r-\lambda}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(c,\zeta)e\left(\frac{(m-\ell)\zeta}{cCp^{r}}\right)\mathrm{d}\zeta

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{[\log C/\log p]}\frac{1}{C}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant c\leqslant C\\
(c,p)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{cp^{r+k}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{a\bmod cp^{r+k}}e\left(\frac{a(m-\ell)}{cp^{r+k}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(p^{k}c,\zeta)e\left(\frac{(m-\ell)\zeta}{cCp^{r+k}}\right)\mathrm{d}\zeta\bigg\}.

In the above sum, we only consider the first term in the braces with
\lambda=0
, that is,

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}(N):=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{m\geqslant 1}\sum_{\ell\geqslant 1}\lambda_{g}(\ell)W\Big(\frac{\ell}{N}\Big)\lambda_{f}(m)\chi(m)V\Big(\frac{m}{N}\Big)

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{C}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant c\leqslant C\\
(c,p)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{cp^{r}}\;\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{a\bmod{cp^{r}}}e\left(\frac{(m-\ell)a}{cp^{r}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(c,\zeta)e\left(\frac{(m-\ell)\zeta}{cCp^{r}}\right)\mathrm{d}\zeta.

Remark 3.1
.

The other terms are lower-order terms, which can be treated similarly.
The same method applies to the remaining sums and
yields better bounds due to their shorter lengths
(cf.
[
6
, Section 3]
and
[
19
, Section 3]
).

Interchanging the order of integration and summations, we get

(3.5)

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}(N)=\frac{1}{C}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant c\leqslant C\\
(c,p)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{cp^{r}}\;\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{a\bmod cp^{r}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(c,\zeta)S_{g}(N,a,c,\zeta)S_{f}(N,a,c,\zeta)\mathrm{d}\zeta,

where

(3.6)

\displaystyle S_{g}(N,a,c,\zeta):=\sum_{\ell\geqslant 1}\lambda_{g}(\ell)e\left(-\frac{a\ell}{cp^{r}}\right)W\left(\frac{\ell}{N}\right)e\left(-\frac{\ell\zeta}{cCp^{r}}\right),

and

(3.7)

\displaystyle S_{f}(N,a,c,\zeta):=\sum_{m\geqslant 1}\lambda_{f}(m)\chi(m)e\left(\frac{am}{cp^{r}}\right)V\left(\frac{m}{N}\right)e\left(\frac{m\zeta}{cCp^{r}}\right).

\displaystyle S_{g}(N,a,c,\zeta)=\frac{N}{cp^{r}}\sum_{\pm}\sum_{\ell\geqslant 1}\lambda_{g}(\ell)e\left(\pm\frac{\ell\overline{a}}{cp^{r}}\right)\Psi_{h_{1}}^{\pm}\left(\frac{\ell N}{c^{2}p^{2r}}\right),

For

\displaystyle S(0,u;c)=\sum_{d\mid(u,c)}d\mu\left(\frac{c}{d}\right).

4.
Cauchy and Poisson

Applying the Cauchy–Schwartz inequality to

-sum in (
3.16
) and using
the Rankin–Selberg estimate (
2.8
), one sees that

\widetilde{S}_{d}^{\pm,\pm}(N,L,M)\ll\mathcal{L}(L,M)^{1/2},

where

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(L,M)=\sum\limits_{m}U\left(\frac{m}{M}\right)\Bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\sim L\\
\ell\equiv mp^{r}\overline{p^{2n-r}}\bmod d\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda_{g}(\ell)}{\ell^{1/2}}H^{\pm,\pm}(m,\ell,c)G(\mp m,\mp\ell,c)\Bigg|^{2},

with
U
is a smooth function supported in
[1/2,5/2]
,
U(x)=1

for
x\in[1,2]
and
U^{(j)}(x)\ll_{j}1
.
Opening the square and switching the order of summations in (
4.1
), we get

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(L,M)=\sum_{\ell_{1}\sim L}\frac{\lambda_{g}(\ell_{1})}{\ell_{1}^{1/2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{2}\sim L\\
\ell_{2}\equiv\ell_{1}\bmod d\end{subarray}}\frac{\overline{\lambda_{g}(\ell_{2})}}{\ell_{2}^{1/2}}\mathcal{T}(L,M).

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(L,M)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{Z}\\
m\equiv\ell_{1}p^{2n-r}\overline{p^{r}}\bmod d\end{subarray}}U\left(\frac{m}{M}\right)H^{\pm,\pm}(m,\ell_{1},c)\overline{H^{\pm,\pm}(m,\ell_{2},c)}

\displaystyle\times G(\mp m,\mp\ell_{1},c)\overline{G(\mp m,\mp\ell_{2},c)}.

We break the
m
-sum into congruence classes modulo
dp^{n}
and apply
the Poisson summation formula to the sum over
m
.
It is therefore sufficient to consider the following sum

\mathcal{T}(L,M)=\frac{M}{dp^{n}}\sum_{\tilde{m}\in\mathbb{Z}}|\mathcal{K}(\tilde{m})||\mathcal{I}(\tilde{m})|,

where the character sum
\mathcal{K}(\tilde{m}):=\mathcal{K}(\tilde{m};\mp\ell_{1},\mp\ell_{2},c)

is given by

(4.3)

\mathcal{K}(\tilde{m})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\bmod dp^{n}\\
u\equiv\ell_{1}p^{2n-r}\overline{p^{r}}\bmod d\end{subarray}}G(\mp u,\mp\ell_{1},c)\overline{G(\mp u,\mp\ell_{2},c)}e\left(\frac{\tilde{m}u}{dp^{n}}\right),

and the integral
\mathcal{I}(\tilde{m})=\mathcal{I}(\tilde{m};\ell_{1},\ell_{2},c)
is given by

(4.4)

\mathcal{I}(\tilde{m})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}U\left(\xi\right)H^{\pm,\pm}\left(M\xi,\ell_{1},c\right)\overline{H^{\pm,\pm}\left(M\xi,\ell_{2},c\right)}\,e\left(-\frac{\tilde{m}M\xi}{dp^{n}}\right)\mathrm{d}\xi.

We need to analyze the integral
\mathcal{I}(\tilde{m})
and the character sum
\mathcal{K}(\tilde{m})
.
The integral
\mathcal{I}(\tilde{m})
will be computed
by the two dimensional (archimedean) second derivative test;
while the character sum
\mathcal{K}(\tilde{m})
will be
evaluated by an involved non-archimedean stationary phase computation
and the
p
-adic second derivative test.

4.1.
Estimation of the integral

In this subsection, we have the following estimate for
\mathcal{I}(\tilde{m})
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{I}(\tilde{m})
be defined as in (
4.4
). Then,
one has the following estimates.

(1)

If
\tilde{m}\gg\frac{N^{\varepsilon}Cdp^{n}}{cM}
, we have
\mathcal{I}(\tilde{m})\ll N^{-A}
.

(2)

If
\tilde{m}\ll\frac{N^{\varepsilon}Cdp^{n}}{cM}
, we have

\mathcal{I}(\tilde{m})\ll\frac{N^{\varepsilon}c}{C}.

Proof.

By (
3.14
), we have

\xi^{j}\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial\xi^{j}}H^{\pm,\pm}\left(M\xi,\ell,c\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(c,\zeta)\mathfrak{J}_{g}^{\pm}(\ell,c,\zeta)\xi^{j}\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial\xi^{j}}\mathfrak{J}_{f}^{\pm}(M\xi,c,\zeta)\mathrm{d}\zeta,

where by (
3.12
),

\displaystyle\xi^{j}\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial\xi^{j}}\mathfrak{J}_{f}^{\pm}(M\xi,c,\zeta)=\;

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(-i\tau)(-i\tau-1)\cdots(-i\tau-j+1)

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{NM\xi}{c^{2}p^{2r}}\right)^{-i\tau}\gamma_{f}^{\pm}\left(-\frac{1}{2}+i\tau\right)V^{{\dagger}}\left(\frac{N\zeta}{cCp^{r}},\frac{1}{2}-i\tau\right)\mathrm{d}\tau.

By definition,

V^{{\dagger}}\left(\frac{N\zeta}{cCp^{r}},\frac{1}{2}-i\tau\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}V(y_{2})y_{2}^{-1/2}e\left(-\frac{\tau}{2\pi}\log y_{2}+\frac{N\zeta y_{2}}{cCp^{r}}\right)\mathrm{d}y_{2}.

By repeated integration by parts implies that the above integral
is negligible unless
|\tau|\asymp|\xi|N/(cCp^{r}):=X
.
Moreover, by the second derivative test for exponential integrals
in
[
7
, Lemma 5.1.3]
,

(4.5)

V^{{\dagger}}\left(\frac{N\zeta}{cCp^{r}},\frac{1}{2}-i\tau\right)\ll(1+|\tau|)^{-1/2}.

Consequently,

\begin{split}\xi^{j}\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial\xi^{j}}\mathfrak{J}_{f}^{\pm}(M\xi,c,\zeta)=\;&\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega\Big(\frac{|\tau|}{X}\Big)(-i\tau)(-i\tau-1)\cdots(-i\tau-j+1)\\
&\times\left(\frac{NM\xi}{c^{2}p^{2r}}\right)^{-i\tau}\gamma_{f}^{\pm}\left(-\frac{1}{2}+i\tau\right)V^{{\dagger}}\left(\frac{N\zeta}{cCp^{r}},\frac{1}{2}-i\tau\right)\mathrm{d}\tau+O(N^{-A}).\end{split}

where
\omega(x)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,\infty)

satisfies
\omega^{(j)}(x)\ll_{j}1
for any integer
j\geqslant 0
.
Hence

\displaystyle\xi^{j}\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial\xi^{j}}H^{\pm,\pm}\left(M\xi,\ell,c\right)=\;

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(c,\zeta)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega\Big(\frac{|\tau|}{X}\Big)(-i\tau)(-i\tau-1)\cdots(-i\tau-j+1)

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\infty}V(y_{2})y_{2}^{-1/2}e\left(-\frac{\tau}{2\pi}\log y_{2}+\frac{N\zeta y_{2}}{cCp^{r}}\right)\mathrm{d}y_{2}

(4.6)

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{NM\xi}{c^{2}p^{2r}}\right)^{-i\tau}\gamma_{f}^{\pm}\left(-\frac{1}{2}+i\tau\right)\mathfrak{J}_{g}^{\pm}(\ell,c,\zeta)\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{d}\zeta+O(N^{-A}),

where by (
3.9
) for
\ell N/c^{2}p^{2\lambda}\gg N^{\varepsilon}
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{J}_{g}^{\pm}(\ell,c,\zeta)=\frac{(\ell N)^{1/4}}{c^{1/2}p^{r/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}y_{1}^{-1/4}W(y_{1})e\left(\frac{\zeta Ny_{1}}{cCp^{r}}\pm 2\frac{\sqrt{\ell Ny_{1}}}{cp^{r}}\right)\mathrm{d}y_{1},

and by (
3.10
) for
\ell N/c^{2}p^{2r}\ll N^{\varepsilon}
,

(4.7)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{J}_{g}^{\pm}(\ell,c,\zeta)=\frac{\sqrt{\ell N}}{cp^{r}}\int_{0}^{\infty}W(y_{1})e\left(\frac{\zeta Ny_{1}}{cCp^{r}}\right)\mathcal{J}_{g}^{\pm}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{\ell Ny_{1}}}{cp^{r}}\right)\mathrm{d}y_{1}.

It is easily verified that the integral

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(c,\zeta)e\left(\frac{\zeta N(y_{2}-y_{1})}{cCp^{r}}\right)\mathrm{d}\zeta

is negligible unless
|y_{1}-y_{2}|\ll N^{\varepsilon}cCp^{r}/N\ll N^{\varepsilon}c/C
,
where
C=\sqrt{N/p^{r}}
(via (
3.3
)).
Using Stirling’s formula, we express

\gamma_{f}^{\pm}\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}+i\tau\right)=\left(\frac{|\tau|}{e\pi}\right)^{2i\tau}\Upsilon_{\pm}(\tau),\quad\Upsilon_{\pm}^{j}(\tau)\ll|\tau|^{-j},

for any integer
j\geqslant 1
.
Thus for
\ell N/c^{2}p^{2r}\gg N^{\varepsilon}
, applying the substitution

y=y_{2}-y_{1}
, we rewrite the integral (
4.6
) as

\displaystyle\xi^{j}\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial\xi^{j}}H^{\pm,\pm}\left(M\xi,\ell,c\right)=\;

\displaystyle\frac{(\ell N)^{1/4}}{2\pi c^{1/2}p^{r/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(c,\zeta)\int_{|y|\ll N^{\varepsilon}c/C}e\left(\frac{\zeta Ny}{cCp^{r}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{\infty}V_{0}(y_{1},\tau;y)e\left(F(y_{1},\tau;y)\right)\mathrm{d}y_{1}\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}\zeta.

where

\displaystyle V_{0}(y_{1},\tau;y)=

\displaystyle\;\omega\Big(\frac{|\tau|}{X}\Big)y_{1}^{-1/4}(y_{1}+y)^{-1/2}V(y_{1})W(y_{1}+y)

\displaystyle\times(-i\tau)(-i\tau-1)\cdots(-i\tau-j+1)\Upsilon_{\pm}(\tau),

and

F(y_{1},\tau;y)=-\frac{\tau}{2\pi}\log\frac{NM\xi}{c^{2}p^{2r}}+\frac{\tau}{\pi}\log\frac{|\tau|}{e\pi}-\frac{\tau}{2\pi}\log(y_{1}+y)\pm\frac{2\sqrt{\ell Ny_{1}}}{cp^{r}}.

Note that the phase function derivatives satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F(y_{1},\tau;y)}{\partial y_{1}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\tau}{2\pi(y_{1}+y)}\pm\frac{\sqrt{\ell N}}{cp^{r}}y_{1}^{-1/2},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F(y_{1},\tau;y)}{\partial\tau}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\log\frac{NM\xi}{c^{2}p^{2r}}+\frac{1}{\pi}\log\frac{|\tau|}{\pi}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\log(y_{1}+y).

Thus we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}F(y_{1},\tau;y)}{\partial y_{1}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau}{2\pi(y_{1}+y)^{2}}\mp\frac{1}{2}\frac{\sqrt{\ell N}}{cp^{r}}y_{1}^{-3/2}\gg|\tau|,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}F(y_{1},\tau;y)}{\partial\tau^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi\tau}\gg|\tau|^{-1},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}F(y_{1},\tau;y)}{\partial y_{1}\partial\tau}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi(y_{1}+y)}.

This implies that

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{det}F^{\prime\prime}\right|=\left|\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial y_{1}^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\tau^{2}}-\left(\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial y_{1}\partial\tau}\right)^{2}\right|\gg 1.

By applying the two dimensional second derivative test in

[
7
, Lemma 5.1.3]
with

\rho_{1}=|\tau|
,
\rho_{2}=|\tau|^{-1}
,
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{\infty}V_{0}(y_{1},\tau;y)e\left(F(y_{1},\tau;y)\right)\mathrm{d}y_{1}\mathrm{d}\tau\ll X^{j}\ll\left(\frac{|\xi|N}{cCp^{r}}\right)^{j}\ll\left(\frac{C}{c}\right)^{j}

recalling
C=\sqrt{N/p^{r}}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\xi^{j}\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial\xi^{j}}H^{\pm,\pm}\left(M\xi,\ell,c\right)\ll N^{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{C}{c}\right)^{j-1/2}.

This also holds when
\ell N/c^{2}p^{2r}\ll N^{\varepsilon}
.
In fact, by (
4.6
) and (
4.7
), we have

\displaystyle\xi^{j}\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial\xi^{j}}H^{\pm,\pm}\left(M\xi,\ell,c\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\ell N}}{2\pi cp^{r}}\int_{0}^{\infty}V(y_{1})\mathcal{J}_{g}^{\pm}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{\ell Ny_{1}}}{cp^{r}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega\Big(\frac{|\tau|}{X}\Big)(-i\tau)(-i\tau-1)\cdots(-i\tau-j+1)\left(\frac{NM\xi}{c^{2}p^{2r}}\right)^{-i\tau}\gamma_{f}^{\pm}\left(-\frac{1}{2}+i\tau\right)

\displaystyle\times\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(c,\zeta)e\left(\frac{N\zeta}{cCp^{r}}\right)V^{{\dagger}}\left(\frac{N\zeta}{cCp^{r}},\frac{1}{2}-i\tau\right)\mathrm{d}y_{1}\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{d}\zeta+O(N^{-A}).

As before, by considering the
\zeta
-integral and integrating by parts, we find that
the above integral is negligible unless

|y_{1}-y_{2}|\ll N^{\varepsilon}cCp^{r}/N\ll N^{\varepsilon}c/C
.
Furthermore, by Remark
2.1
, we have

\mathcal{J}_{g}^{\pm}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{\ell Ny_{1}}}{cp^{r}}\right)\ll\left(\frac{\ell N}{c^{2}p^{2r}}\right)^{-1/4}.

Combining these estimates with (
4.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\xi^{j}\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial\xi^{j}}H^{\pm,\pm}\left(M\xi,\ell,c\right)\ll\left(\frac{\ell N}{c^{2}p^{2r}}\right)^{1/4}\frac{cN^{\varepsilon}}{C}X^{j+1/2}\ll N^{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{C}{c}\right)^{j-1/2}.

Hence by applying integration by parts repeatedly on the
\xi
-integral in (
4.4
) and evaluating the
resulting
\xi
-integral trivially, we find that

\mathcal{I}(\tilde{m})\ll_{j}N^{\varepsilon}\;\frac{c}{C}\left(\frac{\tilde{m}Mc}{Cdp^{n}}\right)^{-j}

for any integer
j\geqslant 0
. Therefore,
\mathcal{I}(\tilde{m})
is negligible
unless

\tilde{m}\leqslant\frac{N^{\varepsilon}Cdp^{n}}{cM}.

Moreover, by taking
j=0
, one has

\mathcal{I}(\tilde{m})\ll_{j}\frac{N^{\varepsilon}c}{C}.

This completes the proof of the Lemma
4.1
.
∎

4.2.
Evaluation of the character sum

In this subsection we estimate the character sums in (
4.3
).
We write
u=d\overline{d}u_{1}+p^{n}\overline{p^{n}}u_{2}
,
with
u_{1}\bmod p^{n}
and
u_{2}\bmod d
. Then we obtain

\mathcal{K}(\tilde{m})=\sum_{u_{1}\bmod p^{n}}G(u_{1},\mp\ell_{1},c)\overline{G(u_{1},\mp\ell_{2},c)}e\left(\pm\frac{\tilde{m}\overline{d}u_{1}}{p^{n}}\right)\;\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{u_{2}\bmod d\atop u_{2}\equiv\ell_{1}p^{2n-r}\overline{p^{r}}\bmod d}e\left(\frac{\tilde{m}\overline{p^{n}}u_{2}}{d}\right).

We therefore arrive at the expression

\mathcal{K}(\tilde{m})=e\left(\frac{\tilde{m}\ell_{1}p^{n-r}\overline{p^{r}}}{d}\right)\mathcal{C}(\pm\tilde{m},\mp\ell_{1},\mp\ell_{2}),

where

(4.8)

\mathcal{C}(\tilde{m},\ell_{1},\ell_{2})=\sum_{u\bmod p^{n}}G(u,\ell_{1},c)\overline{G(u,\ell_{2},c)}e\left(\frac{\tilde{m}\overline{d}u}{p^{n}}\right).

So to estimate
\mathcal{K}(\tilde{m})
, we only need to evaluate
\mathcal{C}(\tilde{m},\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
. To evaluate such a sum further, we will need to make a few elementary transformations. Again, using the Fourier expansion of
\chi
in terms of additive characters, we have

\displaystyle G(m,\ell,c)=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{u\bmod p^{n}}\;\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{b\bmod p^{n}}\;\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{a\bmod{p^{r}}}\chi(u)e\left(\frac{bu}{p^{n}}+\frac{m\overline{c}\overline{(ap^{n-r}+bc)}}{p^{n}}\right)e\left(-\frac{\overline{c}\ell\overline{a}}{p^{r}}\right).

On changing the variable
ap^{n-r}+bc=h
, we get

\displaystyle G(m,\ell,c)=

\displaystyle\;\frac{1}{q}\sum_{u\bmod p^{n}}\;\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{h\bmod p^{n}}\;\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{a\bmod{p^{r}}}\chi(u)e\left(\frac{u\overline{c}(h-ap^{n-r})}{p^{n}}+\frac{m\overline{c}\overline{h}}{p^{n}}\right)e\left(-\frac{\overline{c}\ell\overline{a}}{p^{r}}\right)

(4.9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\frac{1}{q}\sum_{u\bmod p^{n}}\chi(u)S\big(u\overline{c},m\overline{c};p^{n}\big)S\big(u\overline{c},\ell\overline{c};p^{r}\big).

Remark 4.1
.

It is precisely due to this transformation
that we can directly apply the
p
-adic method of stationary phase
to estimate the character sum
G(m,\ell,c)
,
which is also why our results outperform Sun’s
[
18
]
.
This trick is similar in spirit to that of Lin, Michel,
and Sawin
[
11
, Section 4.2]
, who used high-level
algebraic geometry to estimate the algebraic exponential
sums that arise in their calculations.

Using this transformation, we can get

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\tilde{m},\ell_{1},\ell_{2})=\frac{1}{q^{2}}

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta\bmod p^{n}}\sum_{u_{1}\bmod p^{r}}\chi(u_{1})S\big(u_{1}\overline{c},\beta\overline{c};p^{n}\big)S\big(u_{1}\overline{c},\ell_{1}\overline{c};p^{r}\big)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{u_{2}\bmod p^{r}}\overline{\chi}(u_{2})S\big(u_{2}\overline{c},\beta\overline{c};p^{n}\big)S\big(u_{2}\overline{c},\ell_{2}\overline{c};p^{r}\big)e\left(\frac{\tilde{m}\overline{d}\beta}{p^{n}}\right).

We now provide bounds for the character sums
\mathcal{C}(\tilde{m},\ell_{1},\ell_{2})

for
\tilde{m}\in\mathbb{Z}
with different methods on whether
\tilde{m}=0
or not.

For the case
\tilde{m}=0
, an estimate of
\mathcal{C}(0,\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
can
be obtained in an elementary manner by reducing the problem
to the Ramanujan sum. This yields the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}(\tilde{m},\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
be defined as in (
4.8
).
We have

\mathcal{C}(0,\ell_{1},\ell_{2})=p^{n+r}\delta(\ell_{1}\equiv\ell_{2}\bmod p^{r})-p^{n+r-1}\delta(\ell_{1}\equiv\ell_{2}\bmod p^{r-1}).

Proof.

Let
\tilde{m}=0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(0,\ell_{1},\ell_{2})=\frac{1}{q^{2}}

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta\bmod p^{n}}\sum_{u_{1}\bmod p^{n}}\chi(u_{1})S\big(u_{1}\overline{c},\beta\overline{c};p^{n}\big)S\big(u_{1}\overline{c},\ell_{1}\overline{c};p^{r}\big)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{u_{2}\bmod p^{n}}\overline{\chi}(u_{2})S\big(u_{2}\overline{c},\beta\overline{c};p^{n}\big)S\big(u_{2}\overline{c},\ell_{2}\overline{c};p^{r}\big).

Opening the Kloosterman sums and executing the sum over
\beta
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(0,\ell_{1},\ell_{2})=\frac{1}{q}

\displaystyle\;\sum_{u_{1}\bmod p^{n}}\sum_{u_{2}\bmod p^{n}}\chi(u_{1})\overline{\chi}(u_{2})\;\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\gamma_{1}\bmod p^{r}}e\left(\frac{\gamma_{1}u_{1}\overline{c}+\overline{\gamma_{1}}\ell_{1}\overline{c}}{p^{r}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\;\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\gamma_{2}\bmod p^{r}}e\left(\frac{\gamma_{2}u_{2}\overline{c}+\overline{\gamma_{2}}\ell_{2}\overline{c}}{p^{r}}\right)\;\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\gamma\bmod p^{n}}e\left(\frac{u_{1}\overline{c}-u_{2}\overline{c}}{p^{n}}\gamma\right).

The sum over
\gamma
equals
S(0,u_{1}\overline{c}-u_{2}\overline{c};p^{n})

is the Ramanujan sum, then we have

(4.10)

\displaystyle S(0,u_{1}\overline{c}-u_{2}\overline{c};p^{n})=p^{n}\delta(u_{2}\equiv u_{1}\bmod p^{n})-p^{n-1}\delta(u_{2}\equiv u_{1}\bmod p^{n-1}).

We first consider the contribution from the second
term in (
4.10
) to
\mathcal{C}(0,\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
.
Note that

(4.11)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u_{2}\bmod p^{n}\\
u_{2}\equiv u_{1}\bmod p^{n-1}\end{subarray}}\overline{\chi}(u_{2})e\left(\frac{\gamma_{2}u_{2}\overline{c}}{p^{r}}\right)=e\left(\frac{\gamma_{2}u_{2}\overline{c}}{p^{r}}\right)\overline{\chi}(u_{1})\sum_{\nu\bmod p}\chi(1+\overline{u_{1}}p^{n-1}\nu).

Recall that
\chi
is a primitive character of modulus
p^{n}
.
Thus
\chi(1+tp^{n-1})
is an additive character of modulus
p
.
By Lemma
2.1
, there exists
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
such that

\chi(1+tp^{n-1})=e(\alpha t/p)
. Therefore the
\nu
-sum in (
4.11
) vanishes and
the contribution from the second term in (
4.10
) to
\mathcal{C}(0,\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
is zero.
Plugging the first term in (
4.10
) into
\mathcal{C}(0,\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(0,\ell_{1},\ell_{2})

\displaystyle=\;\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\gamma_{1}\bmod p^{r}}\;\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\gamma_{2}\bmod p^{r}}e\left(\frac{\overline{\gamma_{1}}\ell_{1}\overline{c}+\overline{\gamma_{2}}\ell_{2}\overline{c}}{p^{r}}\right)\sum_{u_{1}\bmod p^{n}}e\left(\frac{(\gamma_{2}+\gamma_{1})\overline{c}u_{1}}{p^{r}}\right)

\displaystyle=p^{n}\;\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\gamma_{1}\bmod p^{r}}e\left(\frac{(\ell_{1}-\ell_{2})\overline{c}\gamma_{1}}{p^{r}}\right)

\displaystyle=p^{n}S(0,\ell_{1}-\ell_{2};p^{r}).

Thus the lemma follows.
∎

We split the proof the case
\tilde{m}\not=0
in two steps, which are treated in
Lemmas
4.3
and
4.4
below.

4.2.1.
p
-adic stationary phase computation

Now we need to analyze the character sum
G(m,\ell,c)
by a non-archimedean stationary phase argument.
The resulting sum is a complete exponential sum, it is exactly evaluated in
the following lemma by using the
p
-adic method of stationary phase, Lemma
2.2
,
and exploiting
p
-adic local information; this is also facilitated by an
explicit evaluation of the Kloosterman sum in Lemma
2.3
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
G(m,\ell,c)
be defined as in (
4.9
).
Then
G(m,\ell,c)=0
unless
mc^{-2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times 2}
and
\ell c^{-2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times 2}
,
in which case
G(m,\ell,c)
can be exactly evaluated as follows. Let

\rho_{1}
resp.
\rho_{2}
be
0
or
1
according as
n
resp.
r
is even or odd.
For
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\{\pm 1\}
, define
\mathcal{Z}^{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}}_{\ell,c}(u)

as for
u\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times 2}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}}_{\ell,c}(u):=\epsilon\big(\sigma_{1}\alpha,p^{\rho_{1}}\big)\epsilon\big(\sigma_{2}\alpha(\overline{u}\ell)_{1/2},p^{\rho_{2}}\big)\overline{\chi}\big((\sigma_{1}u_{1/2}+\sigma_{2}\ell_{1/2}p^{n-r})^{2}\big).

Then we have

\displaystyle G(m,\ell,c)=p^{r/2}\chi(\alpha^{2}c^{2})\theta\left(-\frac{2\alpha}{p^{n}}\right)\epsilon\big(-\overline{2}\alpha,p^{\rho_{1}}\big)\sum_{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\{\pm 1\}}\mathcal{Z}^{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}}_{\ell,c}(m).

Proof.

We can evaluate the Kloosterman sum by Lemma
2.3
. We find
that
S\big(u\overline{c},m\overline{c};p^{n}\big)=0
unless

umc^{-2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times 2}
, and

S\big(u\overline{c},\ell\overline{c};p^{r}\big)=0

unless

Using (

4.2.2.

The character sum

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}(\tilde{m},\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
be defined as in (

).
Then
\mathcal{C}(\tilde{m},\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
vanishes unless

where

Proof.

For
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\{\pm 1\}
and

we define

(4.12)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}^{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}}(u)=\overline{\chi}\big((\sigma_{1}u_{1/2}+\sigma_{2}\ell_{1/2}p^{n-r})^{2}\big)\chi\big((\sigma_{1}u_{1/2}+\sigma_{2}\ell_{1/2}p^{n-r})^{2}\big),

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(\alpha,c)=\chi(\alpha^{2}c^{2})\overline{\chi}(\alpha^{2}c^{2})\epsilon\big(-\overline{2}\alpha,p^{\rho_{1}}\big)\overline{\epsilon}\big(-\overline{2}\alpha,p^{\rho_{1}}\big).

By Lemma
4.3
,
\mathcal{C}(\tilde{m},\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
vanishes
unless

Under the validity of this condition, we derive that

\mathcal{C}(\tilde{m},\ell_{1},\ell_{2})=p^{r}\mathcal{W}(\alpha,c)\sum_{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\{\pm 1\}}\sum_{u\bmod p^{n}}\bm{\epsilon}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}^{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}}(u)\Phi_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}^{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}}(u)e\left(\frac{\tilde{m}u}{dp^{n}}\right),

where

\bm{\epsilon}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}^{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}}(u)=\epsilon\big(\sigma_{1}\alpha,p^{\rho_{1}}\big)\epsilon\big(\sigma_{2}\alpha(\overline{u}\ell_{1})_{1/2},p^{\rho_{2}}\big)\overline{\epsilon}\big(\sigma_{1}\alpha,p^{\rho_{1}}\big)\overline{\epsilon}\big(\sigma_{2}\alpha(\overline{u}\ell_{2})_{1/2},p^{\rho_{2}}\big)

depends only on the class of
u
modulo
p
; in particular,

\bm{\epsilon}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}^{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}}(u+p^{\kappa}t)=\bm{\epsilon}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}^{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}}(u)
for every

\kappa\geqslant 1
and every
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
Let, for brevity,

\eta=\eta(u)=\sigma_{1}u_{1/2}+\sigma_{2}\ell_{1/2}p^{n-r}.

Recalling the expansion (
2.3
), we find that

\eta(u+p^{\kappa}t)=\eta(u)+\frac{\sigma_{1}}{2u_{1/2}}p^{\kappa}t-\frac{\sigma_{1}}{8u_{1/2}^{3}}p^{2\kappa}t^{2}+\mathbf{M}_{p^{3\kappa}}.

Fourther, recalling also (
2.2
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\overline{\chi}(\eta^{2}(u+p^{\kappa}t))

\displaystyle=\overline{\chi}(\eta^{2})\theta\bigg(-\frac{2\alpha}{\eta p^{n}}\bigg(\frac{\sigma_{1}}{2u_{1/2}}p^{\kappa}t-\frac{\sigma_{1}}{8u_{1/2}^{3}}p^{2\kappa}t^{2}\bigg)+\frac{2\alpha}{2\eta^{2}p^{n}}\bigg(\frac{\sigma_{1}}{2u_{1/2}}p^{\kappa}t\bigg)^{2}+\mathbf{M}_{p^{3\kappa-n}}\bigg)

(4.13)

\displaystyle=\overline{\chi}(\eta^{2})\theta\bigg(-\frac{\sigma_{1}\alpha}{\eta p^{n}u_{1/2}}p^{\kappa}t+\frac{\alpha(\sigma_{1}\eta+u_{1/2})}{4\eta^{2}p^{n}u_{1/2}^{3}}p^{2\kappa}t^{2}+\mathbf{M}_{p^{3\kappa-n}}\bigg).

Combining (
4.12
) and (

) , for every
u\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}

\displaystyle\Phi_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}^{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}}(u+p^{\kappa}t)=\Phi_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}^{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}}(u)\theta\bigg(

\displaystyle-\frac{\sigma_{1}\alpha}{\eta_{1}p^{n}u_{1/2}}p^{\kappa}t+\frac{\sigma_{1}\alpha}{\eta_{2}p^{n}u_{1/2}}p^{\kappa}t

\eta_{2}=\eta_{2}(u)=\sigma_{1}u_{1/2}+\sigma_{2}(\ell_{2})_{1/2}p^{n-r}

\displaystyle\Phi_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}^{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}}(u+p^{\kappa}t)=\Phi_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}^{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}}(u)\theta\bigg(

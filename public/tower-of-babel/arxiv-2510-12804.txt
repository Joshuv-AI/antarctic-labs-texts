Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the L p L_{p} dual Minkowski problem for capillary hypersurfaces for p > q p>q , which aims to find a capillary convex body with a prescribed capillary ( p , q ) (p,q) -th dual curvature measure in the Euclidean half-space. We reduce it to a Monge-Ampère type equation with a Robin boundary condition on the unit spherical cap, and prove that there exists a unique smooth solution that solves this problem provided θ ∈ ( 0 , π 2 ) \theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}) .



Keywords
:
L_{p}
dual Minkowski problem, Capillary hypersurfaces, Monge-Ampère equation, Robin boundary condition.

As classical Minkowski problems have progressed, similar questions have naturally emerged in the geometric analysis of capillary hypersurfaces.

Let
\{E_{i}\}_{1}^{n+1}
denote the standard orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
,
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}|x\cdot E_{n+1}>0\}
be the upper Euclidean half-space,
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
be a properly embedded, smooth compact hypersurface with boundary such that

int(\Sigma)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}\qquad and\qquad\partial\Sigma\subset\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}.

We call
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
a
capillary hypersurface
if
\Sigma
intersects
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
at a constant contact angle
\theta\in(0,\pi)
. That means the capillary hypersurface in here is not necessarily a hypersurface of constant mean curvature.

Let
\nu
be the unit outward normal, i.e., the Gauss map of
\Sigma
with respect to the domain
\hat{\Sigma}
, where
\hat{\Sigma}
is a bounded closed region in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
enclosed by the convex capillary hypersurface
\Sigma
and
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
, and denote
\hat{\partial\Sigma}:=\partial\hat{\Sigma}\backslash\Sigma\subset\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
. The contact angle
\theta
is defined by

\cos(\pi-\theta)=\langle\nu,e\rangle,

where
e:=-E_{n+1}
, hence
e
is the unit outward normal of
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
. If
\Sigma
is convex, then the Gauss image
\nu(\Sigma)
of
\Sigma
lies in the spherical cap

\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}|\langle x,E_{n+1}\rangle\geq\cos\theta\right\}.

Instead of the usual Gauss map
\nu
, we give another map

\tilde{\nu}:=T\circ\nu:\Sigma\to C_{\theta},

where
C_{\theta}
is a spherical cap defined by

C_{\theta}:=\left\{\xi\in\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}|~|\xi-\cos\theta\cdot e|=1\right\},

which also a capillary hypersurface,
T:\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}\to C_{\theta}
is a translation in the vertical direction define by
T(z)=z+\cos\theta\cdot e
. The diffeomorphism map
\tilde{\nu}
called the
capillary Gauss map
of
\Sigma
, thus we can reparametrize
\Sigma
using its inverse on
C_{\theta}
(we can refer to
[
36
, Fig. 1]
). Hence, we also view the usual support function
h
of
\Sigma
as a function defined on
C_{\theta}
. Denote
K_{\theta}
as the set of all capillary convex bodies in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
, and
K^{\circ}_{\theta}
as the family of capillary convex bodies with the origin as an interior point of the flat part of their boundary.

Recently, Mei, Wang and Weng
[
36
]
introduce a capillary Minkowski problem, which asks for the existence of a strictly convex capillary hypersurface
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
with a prescribed Gauss-Kronecker curvature on a spherical cap
C_{\theta}
. Subsequently, they
[
37
]
further considered a capillary
L_{p}
-Minkowski problem for
p\geq 1
. Otherwise, Hu, Ivaki and Scheuer
[
17
]
consider the capillary Christoffel-Minkowski problem, Hu and Ivaki
[
15
]
solve the even capillary
L_{p}
-Minkowski problem for the range
-n<p<1
and
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
using an iterative scheme. Wang and Zhu
[
44
]
introduce a more general case, i.e., the capillary Orlicz-Minkowski problem.

We know, in some cases, the above problem can be solved as the application of curvature flow (after obtaining the existence results for the curvature flow). Mei, Wang and Weng
[
38
]
first studied a Gauss curvature type flow for capillary hypersurface, i.e., capillary Gauss curvature flow. Hu, Hu and Ivaki
[
16
]
obtained the long-time existence and asymptotic behavior of a class of anisotropic capillary Gauss curvature flow, they also can obtain the existence of smooth solutions to the capillary even
L_{p}
Minkowski problem in the Euclidean half-space and capillary
L_{p}
Minkowski problem as applications for flow.

In this paper, we want to study the
capillary
L_{p}
dual Minkowski problem
. We denote the capillary
(p,q)
-th dual curvature measure as

dS_{p,q}^{c}:=lh^{1-p}\left(h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2}\right)^{\frac{q-n-1}{2}}\det(\nabla^{2}h+h\sigma)d\sigma,

where
\nabla h
and
\nabla^{2}h
are the gradient and the Hessian of
h
on
C_{\theta}
with respect to the standard spherical metric
\sigma
on
C_{\theta}
respectively, and

l:=\sin^{2}\theta+\cos\theta\langle\xi,e\rangle.

Capillary
L_{p}
dual Minkowski problem.

Given a positive smooth function
f
on
C_{\theta}
, does there exists a capillary convex body
\hat{\Sigma}\in K^{\circ}_{\theta}
such that its capillary
(p,q)
-th dual curvature measure
dS_{p,q}^{c}
equals to
fld\sigma
?

By applying a similar argument to that in
[
36
, Proposition 2.4]
, the capillary
L_{p}
dual Minkowski problem is actually equivalent to solve the following Robin boundary value problem of the Monge-Ampère type equation:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\det\left(\nabla^{2}h+h\sigma\right)=fh^{p-1}\left(h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2}\right)^{\frac{n+1-q}{2}},~~&&in~~C_{\theta}\\
&\nabla_{\mu}h=\cot\theta\cdot h,~~&&on~~\partial C_{\theta}\end{aligned}\right.

where
\mu
is the unit outward normal of
\partial C_{\theta}\subset C_{\theta}
. Here, if

A:=\nabla^{2}h+h\sigma>0~~in~C_{\theta}

is a solution to Eq. (
1.1
), then we called it by the convex solution.

We consider the capillary
L_{p}
dual Minkowski problem for
p>q
, as formulated in Eq. (
1.1
). The case
q=n+1
is the capillary
L_{p}
-Minkowski problem, have been solved in
[
37
]
. The main theorem of this paper extends the previous conclusion to
p>q
and it stated below.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p>q
and
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
. For any positive smooth function
f
defined on
C_{\theta}
, then there exists a unique smooth solution
h
solving Eq. (
1.1
).

As a direct consequence of Theorem
1.1
, the capillary
L_{p}
dual Minkowski problem is solved for
p>q
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we will show some functions and formula which play a crucial role in our paper. In Section 3 and Section 4, several estimates, including
C^{0}
estimate, gradient estimate and curvature estimate, of solutions to the Eq. (
1.1
) will be shown in details. In Section 5, we obtain the uniqueness and use the continuity method to prove Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Perliminaries

In this section, we provide some backgrounds of capillary convex bodies. As the set out in the previous section, we parametrize
\Sigma
by the inverse capillary Gauss map, i.e.,
X:C_{\theta}\to\Sigma
given by

X(\xi)=\tilde{\nu}^{-1}(\xi)=\nu^{-1}\circ T^{-1}(\xi)=\nu^{-1}(\xi-\cos\theta e).

The usual support function
h
of
\Sigma
is defined by

h(X):=\langle X,\nu(X)\rangle.

The support function
h
is equivalently defined by the unique decomposition

X=h(X)\nu(X)+t,\qquad t\in T_{X}\Sigma.

Now, we define the capillary support function
u
by the following unique decomposition

(2.1)

X=u(X)\tilde{\nu}(X)+t^{\prime},\qquad t^{\prime}\in T_{X}\Sigma.

It’s easy to see that

h(X)=u(X)\langle\nu(X),\tilde{\nu}(X)\rangle=u(X)(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle).

Now, let

h(\xi):=\langle X(\xi),\nu(X(\xi))\rangle=\langle X(\xi),T^{-1}(\xi)\rangle=\langle\tilde{\nu}^{-1}(\xi),\xi-\cos\theta e\rangle

and

u(\xi)=\frac{h(\xi)}{|\xi|^{2}-\cos\theta\langle\xi,e\rangle}=\frac{h(\xi)}{\sin^{2}\theta+\cos\theta\langle\xi,e\rangle}=\frac{h(\xi)}{l(\xi)},

due to
|\xi-\cos\theta e|^{2}=1
.

Now we compute the geometric quantities of
X
in terms of
h
and
u
. Let
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be a local orthonormal frame on
C_{\theta}
such that along the
\partial C_{\theta}
,
e_{n}=\mu
is the unit outward normal of
\partial C_{\theta}\subset C_{\theta}
. Together with
T^{-1}(\xi)=\xi-\cos\theta e
, which is the usual normal of
C_{\theta}
at
\xi
, it builds on a local orthonormal frame in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
. Hence by the definition of
h
, for
X(\xi)\in\Sigma
,

X=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle X,e_{i}\rangle e_{i}+\langle X,T^{-1}(\xi)\rangle T^{-1}(\xi)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle X,e_{i}\rangle e_{i}+h(\xi)T^{-1}(\xi).

Since
\nabla
is the standard connection in
C_{\theta}
with respect to the standard spherical metric
\sigma
, it’s easy to see

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle X,e_{i}\rangle e_{i}=\nabla h(\xi).

Hence

X(\xi)=\nabla h(\xi)+h(\xi)T^{-1}(\xi).

By the direct computation

\nabla_{e_{j}}X=(\nabla_{ij}h+h\delta_{ij})e_{i},

where
\delta_{ij}
is the coefficients of the standard metric
\sigma
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Hence, the second fundamental form
A_{ij}
of
\Sigma
is given by

A_{ij}=\langle\nabla_{e_{i}}X,\nabla_{e_{i}}(\xi-\cos\theta e)\rangle=\langle\nabla_{e_{j}}X,e_{i}\rangle=\nabla_{ij}h+h\delta_{ij},

and the induced metric
g_{ij}
of
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
can be derived by Weingarten’s formula

g_{ij}=\langle\nabla_{e_{i}}X,\nabla_{e_{j}}X\rangle=A_{ik}A_{jl}\delta^{kl}.

The Gauss-Kronecker curvature of
\Sigma
at
X
is

K(X(\xi))=\det(g^{ik}A_{kj})=\det(\nabla^{2}h(\xi)+h(\xi)\sigma)^{-1}.

Both functions and formula will play a crucial role in our paper.
For the introduction of
L_{p}
dual curvature measures, we can refer to Section 2.2 in
[
21
]
.
Next, we give the equation of
h
and
u
on
\partial C_{\theta}
, which will be used in subsequent proof process.

Since we choose an orthonormal frame
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
on
C_{\theta}
such that along the
\partial C_{\theta}
,
e_{n}=\mu
. Then

Proposition 2.1
.

The support function
h
and the capillary support funtion
u=l^{-1}h
satisfy the following boundary conditions on
\partial C_{\theta}
:

(2.2)

h_{kn}=0,

which is equivalent to

(2.3)

u_{kn}=-\cot\theta u_{k},

where
k=1,2,\cdots,n-1
.

Proof.

First, taking tangential derivative of the equation
h_{n}=\cot\theta h
along
\partial C_{\theta}
, we have
\nabla_{e_{k}}h_{n}=\cot\theta h_{k}
. It follows that

\begin{split}h_{kn}&=\nabla^{2}h(e_{k},e_{n})=\langle\nabla_{e_{k}}(\nabla h),e_{n}\rangle\\
&=\nabla_{e_{k}}(\langle\nabla h,e_{n}\rangle)-\langle\nabla h,\nabla_{e_{k}}e_{n}\rangle\\
&=\nabla_{e_{k}}h_{n}-\langle\nabla h,\cot\theta e_{k}\rangle\\
&=\cot\theta h_{k}-\cot\theta h_{k}=0,\end{split}

where we have used
\nabla_{e_{k}}e_{n}=\cot\theta e_{k}
. Then the equation (
2.2
) follows. Using the same computation and the fact
u_{n}=0
in (
3.3
), we also have (
2.3
). We complete the proof.

\hfill\Box

3.
A priori estimates

In this section, we give a priori estimates (including
C^{0}
and
C^{1}
estimates) for solutions to Eq. (
1.1
).

Lemma 3.1

(
C^{0}
estimate).

Let
p>q
and
\theta\in(0,\pi)
. Suppose
h
is a positive solution to Eq. (
1.1
). Then there holds

(3.1)

2^{-\frac{n+1-q}{2}}\frac{(1-\cos\theta)^{p-q}}{(\sin\theta)^{2(p-1)}}\min_{C_{\theta}}f^{-1}\leq h^{p-q}\leq\frac{(\sin\theta)^{2(p-q)}}{(1-\cos\theta)^{n+p-q}}\max_{C_{\theta}}f^{-1}

while
n+1-q\geq 0
, and

(3.2)

\frac{(1-\cos\theta)^{p-q}}{(\sin\theta)^{2(n+p-q)}}\min_{C_{\theta}}f^{-1}\leq h^{p-q}\leq 2^{-\frac{n+1-q}{2}}\frac{(\sin\theta)^{2(p-q)}}{(1-\cos\theta)^{p-1}}\max_{C_{\theta}}f^{-1}

while
n+1-q<0
.

Proof.

Consider the capillary support function (see, e.g.,
[
36
, Eq. (1.5)]
)

u:=l^{-1}h.

Since
h
is a solution to Eq.(
1.1
), we know that
u
satisfies

(3.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\det\left(l\nabla^{2}u+\cos\theta\cdot(\nabla u\otimes e^{T}+e^{T}\otimes u)+u\sigma\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad=f(ul)^{p-1}\left((ul)^{2}+|\nabla(ul)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{n+1-q}{2}},~~&&in~C_{\theta}\\
&\nabla_{\mu}u=0,~~&&on~\partial C_{\theta}\end{aligned}\right.

where
e^{T}
is the tangential part of
e
on
C_{\theta}
, and we used that
\nabla_{\mu}l=\cot\theta\cdot l
on
\partial C_{\theta}
.
We divide the proof into two cases: either
n+1-q\geq 0
or
n+1-q<0
.

Case 1:

n+1-q\geq 0
. Suppose
u
attains the maximum value at some point
\xi_{0}\in C_{\theta}
. If
\xi_{0}\in C_{\theta}\backslash\partial C_{\theta}
, then

(3.4)

\nabla u(\xi_{0})=0\qquad and\qquad\nabla^{2}u(\xi_{0})\leq 0.

If
\xi_{0}\in\partial C_{\theta}
, the boundary condition in (
3.3
) implies that (
3.4
) still holds. Substituting (
3.4
) into (
3.3
) at
\xi_{0}
, we obtain

f(ul)^{n+p-q}\leq\det(l\nabla^{2}u+\cos\theta(\nabla u\otimes e^{T}+e^{T}\otimes\nabla u)+u\sigma)\leq u^{n},

which implies

u^{p-q}(\xi_{0})\leq f^{-1}l^{q-n-p}\leq\frac{1}{\min_{C_{\theta}}f\cdot(1-\cos\theta)^{n+p-q}}.

Therefore, for all
\xi\in C_{\theta}
, by
(\sin\theta)^{2}\geq l\geq 1-\cos\theta
, we have

(3.5)

\begin{split}h^{p-q}(\xi)&=(ul)^{p-q}(\xi)\\
&\leq u^{p-q}(\xi_{0})\cdot\left(\max_{\xi\in C_{\theta}}l(\xi)\right)^{p-q}\\
&\leq\frac{(\sin\theta)^{2(p-q)}}{\min_{C_{\theta}}f\cdot(1-\cos\theta)^{n+p-q}}.\end{split}

Similarly, if
u
attains the minimum value at some point
\xi_{1}\in C_{\theta}
, there holds

(3.6)

u^{p-q}(\xi_{1})\geq 2^{-\frac{n+1-q}{2}}f^{-1}l^{1-p}\geq 2^{-\frac{n+1-q}{2}}\frac{1}{\max_{C_{\theta}}f\cdot(\sin\theta)^{2(p-1)}}.

Therefore, for all
\xi\in C_{\theta}
, we have

(3.7)

\begin{split}h^{p-q}(\xi)&=(ul)^{p-q}(\xi)\\
&\geq u^{p-q}(\xi_{1})\left(\min_{\xi\in C_{\theta}}l(\xi)\right)^{p-q}\\
&\geq 2^{-\frac{n+1-q}{2}}\frac{(1-\cos\theta)^{p-q}}{\max_{C_{\theta}}f\cdot(\sin\theta)^{2(p-1)}}.\end{split}

Case 2:

n+1-q<0
. Similarly, suppose
u
attains the maximum value at some point
\xi_{0}\in C_{\theta}
. At
\xi_{0}
, we obtain

2^{\frac{n+1-q}{2}}fl^{p-1}u^{n+p-q}\leq\det(l\nabla^{2}u+\cos\theta(\nabla u\otimes e^{T}+e^{T}\otimes\nabla u)+u\sigma)\leq u^{n},

which implies

u^{p-q}(\xi_{0})\leq 2^{-\frac{n+1-q}{2}}f^{-1}l^{1-p}\leq 2^{-\frac{n+1-q}{2}}\frac{1}{\min_{C_{\theta}}f\cdot(1-\cos\theta)^{p-1}}.

Therefore, for all
\xi\in C_{\theta}
, by
(\sin\theta)^{2}\geq l\geq 1-\cos\theta
, we have

(3.8)

\begin{split}h^{p-q}(\xi)&=(ul)^{p-q}(\xi)\\
&\leq u^{p-q}(\xi_{0})\cdot\left(\max_{\xi\in C_{\theta}}l(\xi)\right)^{p-q}\\
&\leq 2^{-\frac{n+1-q}{2}}\frac{(\sin\theta)^{2(p-q)}}{\min_{C_{\theta}}f\cdot(1-\cos\theta)^{p-1}}.\end{split}

Similarly, then there holds

(3.9)

h^{p-q}(\xi)\geq\frac{(1-\cos\theta)^{p-q}}{\max_{C_{\theta}}f\cdot(\sin\theta)^{2(n+p-q)}}.

This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

Next, we give the
C^{1}
-estimate, which is based solely on convexity and is independent of the specific equation.

Lemma 3.2

(
C^{1}
estimate).

Let
p>q
and
\theta\in(0,\pi)
. Suppose
h
is a positive solution to Eq. (
1.1
), then there holds

(3.10)

\max_{C_{\theta}}|\nabla h|\leq(1+\cot^{2}\theta)^{1/2}\|h\|_{C^{0}(C_{\theta})}.

Proof.

We consider the function

P:=|\nabla h|^{2}+h^{2}.

Suppose that the function
P
attains its maximum value at some point
\xi_{0}\in C_{\theta}
. If
\xi_{0}\in C_{\theta}\backslash\partial C_{\theta}
, we have

0=\nabla_{e_{i}}P=2h_{k}h_{ki}+2hh_{i},\quad for\quad 1\leq i\leq n.

Together with the convexity of
h
, i.e.,
h_{ij}+h\delta_{ij}>0
, it follows
\nabla h(\xi_{0})=0
, so (
3.10
) holds.

If
\xi_{0}\in\partial C_{\theta}
, we choose an orthonormal frame
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
around
\xi_{0}\in\partial C_{\theta}
such that
e_{n}=\mu
. From Proposition
2.1
, we know that

(3.11)

h_{kn}=0\qquad for~~any\quad 1\leq k\leq n-1.

Then we have

0=\nabla_{e_{k}}P=2\sum_{i=1}^{n}h_{i}h_{ik}+2hh_{k},

which implies

(3.12)

h_{k}(\xi_{0})=0.

Together with (
3.12
) and
h_{n}=\cot\theta\cdot h
on
\partial C_{\theta}
, we have

|\nabla h|^{2}(\xi_{0})\leq(|\nabla h|^{2}+h^{2})(\xi_{0})=(h_{n}^{2}+h^{2})(\xi_{0})=(1+\cot^{2}\theta)h^{2}(\xi_{0}).

In conclusion, (
3.10
) holds.

\hfill\Box

Now we give the logarithmic gradient estimate for solutions to Eq. (
1.1
), the choice of test functions is inspired by the ideas developed in
[
34
,
35
,
37
]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
p\geq q>1
and
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
. If
h
is a positive solution to Eq. (
1.1
), then there exists a constant
C
depending on
n
,
p
,
q
,
\min_{C_{\theta}}f
and
\|f\|_{C^{1}(C_{\theta})}
, such that

(3.13)

\max_{C_{\theta}}|\nabla\log h|\leq C.

Furthermore, if
p=q=n+1
, the constant
C
in (
3.13
) is independent of
p
and
q
.

Proof.

Define
v:=\log h
, since
h
is a solution to Eq. (
1.1
), then
v
satisfies

(3.14)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\det\left(\nabla^{2}v+\nabla v\otimes\nabla v+\sigma\right)=fe^{(p-q)v}\left(1+|\nabla v|^{2}\right)^{\frac{n+1-q}{2}}:=\hat{f},~~&&in~C_{\theta}\\
&\nabla_{\mu}v=\cot\theta,~~&&on~\partial C_{\theta}\end{aligned}\right.

Let
N:=(0,\cdots,0,1-\cos\theta)\in C_{\theta}
, denote the function
d:=\frac{1}{2\theta}d^{2}_{N}(\xi)
, where
d_{N}(\xi)
is the geodesic distance function from the point
\xi
to
N
on
C_{\theta}
. We know that the function
d
is well-defined and smooth for all
\xi\in C_{\theta}
. Moreover, from direct computation, on
\partial C_{\theta}
, we have

d=\frac{\theta}{2},\qquad\nabla d=\mu,

and

(3.15)

\nabla^{2}d\geq\min\left\{\frac{1}{\theta},\cot\theta\right\}\sigma=:c_{0}\sigma~~in~~C_{\theta}.

We assume that
|\nabla v|
is large enough, otherwise, the inequality (
3.13
) holds. Consider the text function

\Phi:=\log|\nabla w|^{2}+\varphi(d),

where
w:=v-\cot\theta\cdot d
,
\varphi(d):=e^{sd}
for some constant
s\in\mathbb{R}
to be determined later.

Assume that the function
\Phi
attains its maximum value at some point
\xi_{0}\in C_{\theta}
. We proof it into the following two cases: either
\xi_{0}\in\partial C_{\theta}
or
\xi_{0}\in C_{\theta}\backslash\partial C_{\theta}
.

Case 1.

\xi_{0}\in\partial C_{\theta}
. Let
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be an orthonormal frame around
\xi_{0}
such that
e_{n}=\mu
. Then, at the point
\xi_{0}
, we have

(3.16)

0\leq\nabla_{e_{n}}\Phi(\xi_{0})=\frac{2w_{k}w_{kn}}{|\nabla w|^{2}}+\varphi^{\prime}\nabla_{e_{n}}d.

Notice that

(3.17)

w_{n}=v_{n}-\cot\theta\cdot d_{n}=0,

so
|\nabla w|^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}w_{k}^{2}
. From the Gauss-Weingarten equation of
\partial C_{\theta}\subset C_{\theta}
, we know

(3.18)

w_{kn}=\nabla_{e_{n}}(w_{k})-\nabla w(\nabla_{e_{k}}e_{n})=-\cot\theta\cdot w_{k}.

Inserting (
3.17
) and (
3.18
) into (
3.16
) yields

0\leq\nabla_{e_{n}}\Phi(\xi_{0})=\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}2w_{k}w_{kn}}{|\nabla w|^{2}}+\varphi^{\prime}\nabla_{e_{n}}d=-2\cot\theta+se^{sd(\xi_{0})}<0,

where the last inequality follows if we choose
s\leq c_{1}
with
c_{1}>0
and satisfying

(3.19)

c_{1}e^{\frac{\theta c_{1}}{2}}<2\cot\theta.

This yields a contradiction. Hence
\xi_{0}\in C_{\theta}\backslash\partial C_{\theta}
.

Case 2.

\xi_{0}\in C_{\theta}\backslash\partial C_{\theta}
. Denote

(3.20)

G(B):=\log\det B=\log\hat{f},

where
B:=(B_{ij})=(v_{ij}+v_{i}v_{j}+\delta_{ij})
. Now we choose an orthonormal frame
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
around
\xi_{0}
, such that
B_{ij}
is diagonal at
\xi_{0}
. Hence
G^{ij}:=\frac{\partial G(B)}{\partial B_{ij}}
is also diagonal at
\xi_{0}
. Then, at the point
\xi_{0}
, we have

(3.21)

0=\Phi_{i}=\frac{|\nabla w|_{i}^{2}}{|\nabla w|^{2}}+\varphi^{\prime}d_{i},

and

(3.22)

\begin{split}0&\geq G^{ij}\Phi_{ij}=G^{ij}\left(\frac{|\nabla w|^{2}_{ij}}{|\nabla w|^{2}}-\frac{|\nabla w|^{2}_{i}|\nabla w|^{2}_{j}}{|\nabla w|^{2}}+\varphi^{\prime\prime}d_{i}d_{j}+\varphi^{\prime}d_{ij}\right)\\
&=\frac{2G^{ij}w_{k}w_{kij}+2G^{ij}w_{ki}w_{kj}}{|\nabla w|^{2}}+G^{ij}\left[(\varphi^{\prime\prime}-(\varphi^{\prime})^{2})d_{i}d_{j}+\varphi^{\prime}d_{ij}\right].\end{split}

For the standard metric on spherical cap
C_{\theta}
, the commutator formulae gives

(3.23)

v_{kij}=v_{ijk}+v_{k}\delta_{ij}-v_{j}\delta_{ki}.

Here, we also divide this step into two cases: either
n+1-q\geq 0
or
n+1-q<0
.

Subcase 2.1
.
n+1-q\geq 0
. Taking the first derivatives of Eq. (
3.20
) in the
e_{k}
direction, we have

(3.24)

G^{ij}(v_{ijk}+2v_{ki}v_{j})=(\log\hat{f})_{k}.

Inserting (
3.23
) and (
3.24
) into (
3.22
), we obtain

(3.25)

\begin{split}0&\geq\frac{2G^{ij}w_{k}(v_{kij}-\cot\theta\cdot d_{kij})+2G^{ij}w_{ki}w_{kj}}{|\nabla w|^{2}}+G^{ij}\left[(\varphi^{\prime\prime}-(\varphi^{\prime})^{2})d_{i}d_{j}+\varphi^{\prime}d_{ij}\right]\\
&=\frac{2w_{k}\left[(\log\hat{f})_{k}-2G^{ij}v_{ki}v_{j}+v_{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n}G^{ii}-G^{ij}v_{j}\delta_{ki}-\cot\theta\cdot G^{ij}d_{kij}\right]}{|\nabla w|^{2}}\\
&\qquad+\frac{2G^{ij}w_{ki}w_{kj}}{|\nabla w|^{2}}+G^{ij}\left[(\varphi^{\prime\prime}-(\varphi^{\prime})^{2})d_{i}d_{j}+\varphi^{\prime}d_{ij}\right]\\
&\geq-\frac{C}{|\nabla w|}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}G^{ii}+1\right)+\frac{2G^{ij}w_{ki}w_{kj}-4G^{ij}v_{ki}v_{j}w_{k}}{|\nabla w|^{2}}+2(n+1-q)\frac{w_{k}v_{i}v_{ik}}{(1+|\nabla v|^{2})|\nabla w|^{2}}\\
&\qquad+G^{ij}\left[(\varphi^{\prime\prime}-(\varphi^{\prime})^{2})d_{i}d_{j}+\varphi^{\prime}d_{ij}\right],\end{split}

where the positive constant
C
depends on
p
and
\|f\|_{C^{1}(C_{\theta})}
. In fact, we know that

2(n+1-q)\frac{w_{k}v_{i}v_{ik}}{(1+|\nabla v|^{2})|\nabla w|^{2}}=2(n+1-q)\frac{w_{k}v_{i}(B_{ik}-v_{i}v_{k}-\delta_{ik})}{(1+|\nabla v|^{2})|\nabla w|^{2}},

then, from (
3.21
) we have

2w_{k}(B_{ki}-v_{k}v_{i}-\delta_{ki}-\cot\theta d_{ki})=2w_{k}w_{ki}=-\varphi^{\prime}d_{i}|\nabla w|^{2},

that means for all fixed
1\leq i\leq n
,

(3.26)

2w_{i}B_{ii}=-\varphi^{\prime}d_{i}|\nabla w|^{2}+2v_{i}v_{k}w_{k}+2w_{k}(\delta_{ki}+\cot\theta d_{ki}).

We just consider the case that
w_{i}\neq 0
, so

(3.27)

B_{ii}=\frac{1}{w_{i}}\left[-\varphi^{\prime}d_{i}|\nabla w|^{2}+2w_{k}v_{k}v_{i}+2w_{k}(\delta_{ki}+\cot\theta d_{ki})\right].

Inserting (
3.27
) into (
3.25
), since we assume that
|\nabla v|
is large enough, then we have

(3.28)

\begin{split}0&\geq-\frac{C}{|\nabla w|}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}G^{ii}+1\right)+\frac{2G^{ij}w_{ki}w_{kj}-4G^{ij}v_{ki}v_{j}w_{k}}{|\nabla w|^{2}}\\
&\qquad+G^{ij}\left[(\varphi^{\prime\prime}-(\varphi^{\prime})^{2})d_{i}d_{j}+\varphi^{\prime}d_{ij}\right],\end{split}

where the positive constant
C
depends on
n
,
p
,
q
and
\|\log f\|_{C^{1}(C_{\theta})}
.

In order to obtain the estimate, we denote
I:=2G^{ij}w_{ki}w_{kj}-4G^{ij}v_{ki}v_{j}w_{k}
. From direct computation, we have

\begin{split}I&=-4G^{ij}(B_{ki}-v_{k}v_{i}-\delta_{ki})v_{j}w_{k}\\
&\qquad+2G^{ij}(B_{ki}-v_{k}v_{i}-\delta_{ki}-\cot\theta\cdot d_{ki})\cdot(B_{kj}-v_{k}v_{j}-\delta_{kj}-\cot\theta\cdot d_{kj})\\
&=2G^{ij}B_{ki}B_{kj}-4G^{ij}B_{ki}v_{j}w_{k}-4G^{ij}B_{ki}(v_{k}v_{j}+\delta_{kj}+\cot\theta\cdot d_{kj})\\
&\qquad+2G^{ij}(v_{k}v_{j}+\delta_{kj}+\cot\theta\cdot d_{kj})(v_{k}v_{i}+\delta_{ki}+\cot\theta\cdot d_{ki})\\
&\qquad+4G^{ij}v_{i}v_{j}v_{k}w_{k}+4G^{kj}v_{j}w_{k}\\
&\geq 2\sum_{i=1}^{n}B_{ii}+2|\nabla v|^{2}G^{ij}v_{i}v_{j}+4G^{ij}v_{i}v_{j}v_{k}w_{k}+4G^{ij}v_{i}v_{j}\\
&\qquad-C|\nabla v|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}G^{ii}+1\right)-8|\nabla v|^{2}.\end{split}

By applying the same analysis as in the proof of
[
37
, Lemma 3.5]
, we can obtain

(3.29)

2\sum_{i=1}^{n}B_{ii}+2G^{ij}v_{i}v_{j}|\nabla v|^{2}+4G^{ij}v_{i}v_{j}v_{k}w_{k}-8|\nabla v|^{2}\geq-C|\nabla v|.

Inserting (
3.29
) into (
3.28
), we derive

(3.30)

\begin{split}0&\geq-\frac{C\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}G^{ii}+1\right)}{|\nabla w|}+\frac{4G^{ij}v_{i}v_{j}-C|\nabla v|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}G^{ii}+1\right)}{|\nabla w|^{2}}\\
&\qquad+G^{ij}\left[(\varphi^{\prime\prime}-(\varphi^{\prime})^{2})d_{i}d_{j}+\varphi^{\prime}d_{ij}\right]\\
&\geq-\frac{C\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}G^{ii}+1\right)}{|\nabla w|}+G^{ij}\left[(\varphi^{\prime\prime}-(\varphi^{\prime})^{2})d_{i}d_{j}+\varphi^{\prime}d_{ij}\right].\end{split}

Since

\begin{split}\left(\varphi^{\prime\prime}-(\varphi^{\prime})^{2}\right)G^{ij}d_{i}d_{j}&=\theta^{-2}s^{2}\left(e^{sd}-e^{2sd}\right)d_{N}^{2}G^{ij}(d_{N})_{i}(d_{N})_{j}\\
&\geq-2s^{2}e^{2sd}\sum_{i=1}^{n}G^{ii},\end{split}

together with (
3.15
) and (
3.30
), we have

0\geq\left(c_{0}se^{sd}-2s^{2}e^{2sd}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n}G^{ii}-\frac{C}{|\nabla w|}\cdot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}G^{ii}+1\right).

Choose a small positive constant
c_{2}
such that
s\leq c_{2}
and

(3.31)

\frac{c_{0}}{2}-2c_{2}e^{\frac{\theta c_{2}}{2}}>0,

where
c_{0}
is given by (
3.15
) and is positive since
\theta<\frac{\pi}{2}
. Then we have

(3.32)

0\geq\frac{1}{2}c_{0}se^{sd}\sum_{i=1}^{n}G^{ii}-\frac{C}{|\nabla w|}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}G^{ii}+1\right).

Using the arithmetric-geometric inequality and (
3.1
), we get

(3.33)

\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{n}G^{ii}&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{B_{ii}}\geq n\left(\det B\right)^{-\frac{1}{n}}\\
&=n\left(fe^{(p-q)v}(1+|\nabla v|^{2})^{\frac{n+1-q}{2}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{n}}\\
&\geq\frac{C}{(1+|\nabla v|^{2})^{\frac{n+1-q}{2n}}},\end{split}

where the positive constant
C
depends on
n
,
p
,
q
and
\|f\|_{C^{0}(C_{\theta})}
. Inserting (
3.33
) into (
3.32
), we have

(3.34)

\begin{split}0&\geq\frac{1}{2}c_{0}se^{sd}-\frac{C}{|\nabla w|}\left(1+\frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}G^{ii}}\right)\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}c_{0}se^{sd}-\frac{C}{|\nabla w|}\left(1+(1+|\nabla v|^{2})^{\frac{n+1-q}{2n}}\right).\end{split}

Since
1<q\leq n+1
, we can obtain that
|\nabla w|\leq C
, which in turn implies
\max_{C_{\theta}}|\nabla v|\leq C
.

Subcase 2.2
.
n+1-q<0
. Similar to the above approach, in this case, (
3.32
) form becomes

(3.35)

0\geq\frac{1}{2}c_{0}se^{sd}\sum G^{ii}-\frac{C}{|\nabla w|}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}G^{ii}+1\right)-C.

Inserting (
3.33
) into (
3.35
), we have

(3.36)

\begin{split}0&\geq\frac{1}{2}c_{0}se^{sd}-\frac{C}{|\nabla w|}\left(1+\frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}G^{ii}}\right)-\frac{C}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}G^{ii}}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}c_{0}se^{sd}-\frac{C}{|\nabla w|}\left(1+(1+|\nabla v|^{2})^{\frac{n+1-q}{2n}}\right)-C(1+|\nabla v|^{2})^{\frac{n+1-q}{2n}}.\end{split}

Since
q>n+1
, then
\frac{n+1-q}{2n}<0
, we can obtain that
|\nabla w|\leq C
, then
\max_{C_{\theta}}|\nabla v|\leq C
.
In conclusion, we can choose
s=\min\{c_{1},c_{2}\}
so that conditions (
3.19
) and (
3.31
) are satisfied.
We complete the proof.

\hfill\Box

4.
C^{2}
estimates

Now, we establish the a priori
C^{2}
estimate for the positive solution of Eq. (
1.1
).

Lemma 4.1

(
C^{2}
estimate).

Let
p>q
and
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
. Suppose that
h
is a positive solution to Eq. (
1.1
), then there holds

(4.1)

\max_{C_{\theta}}|\nabla^{2}h|\leq C,

where the positive constant
C
depends on
n
,
p
,
q
,
\min_{C_{\theta}}f
,
\min_{C_{\theta}}h
,
\|f\|_{C^{2}(C_{\theta})}
and
\|h\|_{C^{0}(C_{\theta})}
.

Proof.

We will prove this Lemma in two steps. First, we reduce the global
C^{2}
estimate to the boundary double normal
C^{2}
estimate. Then, we give the boundary double normal estimate.

Step 1.

We want to obtain that

(4.2)

\max_{C_{\theta}}|\nabla^{2}h|\leq C\max_{\partial C_{\theta}}|\nabla^{2}h(\mu,\mu)|+C,

where the positive constant
C
depends on
n
,
p
,
q
,
\min_{C_{\theta}}f
,
\min_{C_{\theta}}h
,
\|f\|_{C^{2}(C_{\theta})}
and
\|h\|_{C^{0}(C_{\theta})}
.
Denote
M:=\max_{\partial C_{\theta}}|\nabla^{2}h(\mu,\mu)|
, we consider the function

P(\xi,\Xi):=\nabla^{2}h(\Xi,\Xi)+L|\nabla h|^{2}(\xi)

for
\xi\in C_{\theta}
and a unit vector
\Xi\in T_{\xi}C_{\theta}
, where
L
is positive constant to be chosen later. Suppose that
P
attains its maximum value at some point
\xi_{0}\in C_{\theta}
and some unit vector
\Xi_{0}\in T_{\xi_{0}}C_{\theta}
. We divide this proof into two cases: either
\xi_{0}\in\partial C_{\theta}
or
\xi_{0}\in C_{\theta}\backslash\partial C_{\theta}
.

Case 1.

\xi_{0}\in C_{\theta}\backslash\partial C_{\theta}
. In this case, we choose an orthonormal frame
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
around
\xi_{0}
, such that
A_{ij}=(h_{ij}+h\delta_{ij})
is diagonal and
\Xi_{0}=e_{1}
. Denote

(4.3)

F(A):=\log\det(A)=\log\left(h^{p-1}(h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2})^{\frac{n+1-q}{2}}f\right)=:\tilde{f},

and

F^{ij}:=\frac{\partial F(A)}{\partial A_{ij}},~~F^{ij,kl}:=\frac{\partial^{2}F(A)}{\partial A_{ij}\partial A_{kl}}.

Then we have

(4.4)

F^{ij}=A^{ij},

(4.5)

F^{ij,kl}=-F^{ij}F^{kl}=-A^{ij}A^{kl},

where
A^{ij}
is the inverse of
A_{ij}
.
Note that

(4.6)

F^{ij}A_{ij}=n.

By taking twice convariant derivatives in the
e_{1}
direction to Eq. (
4.3
), we obtain

(4.7)

\begin{split}F^{ij}A_{ij11}&=-F^{ij,kl}A_{ij1}A_{kl1}+\tilde{f}_{11}\\
&=F^{ij}F^{kl}A_{ij1}A_{kl1}+\tilde{f}_{11}\\
&=(\tilde{f})_{1}^{2}+\tilde{f}_{11}.\end{split}

By the directly computation, we know that

(4.8)

\tilde{f}=(p-1)\log h+\frac{n+1-q}{2}\log(h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2})+\log f,

(4.9)

\tilde{f}_{1}=(p-1)\frac{h_{1}}{h}+(n+1-q)\frac{h_{1}A_{11}}{h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2}}+(\log f)_{1},

(4.10)

\begin{split}(\tilde{f}_{1})^{2}&=(n+1-q)^{2}\frac{h_{1}^{2}A_{11}^{2}}{(h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2})^{2}}\\
&\qquad+\left(2(p-1)(n+1-q)\frac{h_{1}}{h}+2(n+1-q)(\log f)_{1}\right)\frac{h_{1}A_{11}}{h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2}}\\
&\qquad+(p-1)^{2}\frac{h_{1}^{2}}{h^{2}}+2(p-1)(\log f)_{1}\frac{h_{1}}{h}+(\log f)_{1}^{2},\end{split}

and

(4.11)

\begin{split}\tilde{f}_{11}&=(p-1)\frac{h_{11}}{h}-(p-1)\frac{h^{2}_{1}}{h^{2}}+(\log f)_{11}\\
&\qquad+(n+1-q)\frac{h_{1}^{2}+hh_{11}+h_{1k}^{2}+h_{k}h_{k11}}{h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2}}-2(n+1-q)\frac{h_{1}^{2}A_{11}^{2}}{(h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2})^{2}}.\end{split}

Because the text function
P
attains its maximum value in
\xi_{0}\in C_{\theta}
and
\Xi_{0}\in T_{\xi_{0}}C_{\theta}
, we have

h_{11i}=-2Lh_{i}h_{ii},

and

\begin{split}0&\geq P_{ij}=h_{11ij}+2Lh_{kj}h_{ki}+2Lh_{k}h_{kij}\\
&=h_{11ij}+2Lh_{ii}h_{jj}+2Lh_{ijk}h_{k}+2Lh_{k}^{2}\delta_{ij}-2Lh_{i}h_{j}\end{split}

for the standard metric on spherical cap
C_{\theta}
, using the commutator formula
h_{kij}=h_{ijk}+h_{k}\delta_{ij}-h_{j}\delta_{ki}
, then we have

(4.12)

\begin{split}\tilde{f}_{11}&=(p-1)\frac{h_{11}}{h}-(p-1)\frac{h_{1}^{2}}{h^{2}}+(\log f)_{11}\\
&\qquad+(n+1-q)\frac{h_{11}^{2}+hh_{11}-2Lh_{k}^{2}h_{kk}+h_{k}^{2}}{h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2}}-2(n+1-q)\frac{h_{1}^{2}A_{11}^{2}}{(h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2})^{2}}.\end{split}

Inserting (
4.10
) and (
4.12
) into (
4.7
), we get

(4.13)

\begin{split}F^{ij}A_{ij11}&=(n+1-q)^{2}\frac{h_{1}^{2}A_{11}^{2}}{(h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2})^{2}}+(n+1-q)\frac{A_{11}^{2}}{h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2}}-2(n+1-q)\frac{h_{1}^{2}A_{11}^{2}}{(h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2})^{2}}\\
&\qquad+\left(2(p-1)(n+1-q)\frac{h_{1}}{h}+2(n+1-q)(\log f)_{1}\right)\frac{h_{1}A_{11}}{h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2}}\\
&\qquad-(n+1-q)\frac{hA_{11}}{h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2}}+(p-1)\frac{A_{11}}{h}-2L(n+1-q)\frac{h^{2}_{k}}{h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2}}A_{kk}\\
&\qquad+2L(n+1-q)\frac{hh_{k}^{2}}{h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2}}+(n+1-q)\frac{h_{k}^{2}}{h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2}}\\
&\qquad+(p-1)^{2}\frac{h_{1}^{2}}{h^{2}}-(p-1)\frac{h_{1}^{2}}{h^{2}}+2(p-1)(\log f)_{1}\frac{h_{1}}{h}+(\log f)_{1}^{2}+(\log f)_{11}-(p-1).\end{split}

Using the fact

h_{klij}=h_{ijkl}+2h_{kl}\delta_{ij}-2h_{ij}\delta_{kl}+h_{li}\delta_{kj}-h_{kj}\delta_{il},

from which we have

(4.14)

\begin{split}F^{ij}h_{11ij}&=F^{ij}(h_{ij11}+2h_{11}\delta_{ij}-2h_{ij}+h_{1i}\delta_{1j}-h_{1j}\delta_{1i})\\
&=F^{ij}h_{ij11}+2h_{11}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}-2F^{ij}h_{ij}.\end{split}

From the arithmetric-geometric mean inequality, we have

(4.15)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}\geq n(\det A)^{-\frac{1}{n}}.

Combining (
4.6
), (
4.13
), (
4.14
) and (
4.15
), choosing
L:=\frac{|n+1-q|}{2n}N+1
, where
N:=\max_{C_{\theta}}h^{\frac{p-1}{n}}(h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2})^{\frac{1-q-n}{2n}}f^{\frac{1}{n}}
, then we have

(4.16)

\begin{split}0&\geq F^{ij}P_{ij}=F^{ij}h_{11ij}+2LF^{ij}h_{ki}h_{kj}+2LF^{ij}h_{k}h_{kij}\\
&=F^{ij}(A_{ij11}-h_{11}\delta_{ij})+2h_{11}\sum_{i}F^{ii}-2F^{ij}h_{ij}\\
&\qquad+2LF^{ij}h_{ii}h_{jj}+2L\tilde{f}_{k}h_{k}+2Lh_{k}^{2}\sum_{i}F^{ii}-2LF^{ij}h_{i}h_{j}\\
&=F^{ij}A_{ij11}+A_{11}\sum_{i}F^{ii}-2n+h\sum_{i}F^{ii}+2LF^{ii}h_{ii}^{2}+2L\tilde{f}_{k}h_{k}+2Lh_{k}^{2}\sum_{i}F^{ii}-2LF^{ii}h_{i}^{2}\\
&=2LF^{ii}A_{ii}^{2}+\left((n+1-q)^{2}\frac{h_{1}^{2}}{(h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2})^{2}}+(n+1-q)\frac{1}{h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2}}-2(n+1-q)\frac{h_{1}^{2}}{(h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2})^{2}}\right)A_{11}^{2}\\
&\qquad+\left(2(p-1)(n+1-q)\frac{h_{1}}{h}+2(n+1-q)(\log f)_{1}\right)\frac{h_{1}}{h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2}}A_{11}\\
&\qquad-\left((n+1-q)\frac{h}{h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2}}-\frac{p-1}{h}\right)A_{11}+(A_{11}+h+2L(h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2}))\sum_{i}F^{ii}\\
&\qquad+(n+1-q)(h_{i}^{2}+2Lhh_{i}^{2})\frac{1}{h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2}}-4nLh\\
&\qquad+(p-1)^{2}\frac{h_{1}^{2}}{h^{2}}-(p-1)\frac{h_{1}^{2}}{h^{2}}+2(p-1)(\log f)_{1}\frac{h_{1}}{h}+(\log f)_{1}^{2}+(\log f)_{11}-(p-1)\\
&\qquad+2L(p-1)\frac{h_{i}^{2}}{h}+2L(\log f)_{i}h_{i}-2LF^{ii}h_{i}^{2}\\
&\geq 2nL(\det A)^{-\frac{1}{n}}A^{2}_{11}+(n+1-q)\frac{(n+1-q)h_{1}^{2}+h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2}-2h_{1}^{2}}{(h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2})^{2}}A_{11}^{2}-C(A_{11}+1)\\
&\geq 2n(\det A)^{-\frac{1}{n}}A_{11}^{2}-C(A_{11}+1),\end{split}

where the positive constant
C
depends on
n
,
p
,
q
,
\min_{C_{\theta}}f
,
\min_{C_{\theta}}h
,
\|f\|_{C^{2}(C_{\theta})}
and
\|h\|_{C^{0}(C_{\theta})}
. So the coefficient in front of
A^{2}_{11}
is positive, then we also have

(4.17)

A_{11}(\xi_{0})\leq C,

where the positive constant
C
depends on
n
,
p
,
q
,
\min_{C_{\theta}}f
,
\min_{C_{\theta}}h
,
\|f\|_{C^{2}(C_{\theta})}
and
\|h\|_{C^{0}(C_{\theta})}
.

Case 2.

\xi_{0}\in\partial C_{\theta}
. In this case, we divide it into two subcases: either
\Xi_{0}
is tangential or
\Xi_{0}
is non-tangential.

Subcase 2.1
.
\Xi_{0}
is tangential. We choose an orthonormal frame
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
around
\xi_{0}\in\partial C_{\theta}
satisfying
e_{n}=\mu
and
e_{1}=\Xi_{0}
at
\xi_{0}
. Recalling (
2.2
), on
\partial C_{\theta}
, we have

(4.18)

h_{kn}=0,\qquad 1\leq k\leq n-1.

Using (
4.18
),

\begin{split}\nabla_{11n}h&=(\nabla^{3}h)(e_{1},e_{1},e_{n})=\left(\nabla_{e_{1}}(\nabla^{2}h)\right)(e_{1},e_{n})\\
&=\nabla_{e_{1}}\left(\nabla^{2}h(e_{1},e_{n})\right)-\nabla^{2}h\left(\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{1},e_{n}\right)-\nabla^{2}h\left(e_{1},\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{n}\right)\\
&=\cot\theta h_{nn}-\cot\theta h_{11}.\end{split}

In view of the commutator formula
h_{kij}=h_{ijk}+h_{k}\delta_{ij}-h_{j}\delta_{ki}
and
h_{n}=\cot\theta h
on
\partial C_{\theta}
, we have

\nabla_{n11}h=\nabla_{11n}h-h_{n}=\cot\theta(h_{nn}-h_{11}-h),

together with (
4.18
), yields

(4.19)

\begin{split}0&\leq\nabla_{n}P(\xi_{0},\Xi_{0})=\nabla_{n11}h+\nabla_{n}(L|\nabla h|^{2})\\
&=\cot\theta(h_{nn}-h_{11}-h)+2Lh_{n}h_{nn}\leq\cot\theta(h_{nn}-h_{11})+2Lh\cot\theta h_{nn}.\end{split}

Because
\cot\theta>0
, we have

(4.20)

h_{11}(\xi_{0})\leq(1+2Lh)h_{nn}\leq CM.

Subcase 2.2.

\Xi_{0}
is non-tangential. Now we write it as

\Xi_{0}=\alpha\tau+\beta\mu,

where
\beta:=\langle\Xi_{0},\mu\rangle
,
\alpha:=\langle\Xi_{0},\tau\rangle
and
\tau
is a unit tangential vector. It’s clear that
\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}=1
. From (
4.18
), at
\xi_{0}\in\partial C_{\theta}
, we have

\begin{split}\nabla^{2}h(\Xi_{0},\Xi_{0})&=\alpha^{2}h_{\tau\tau}+\beta^{2}h_{\mu\mu}+2\alpha\beta h_{\tau\mu}\\
&=\alpha^{2}h_{\tau\tau}+\beta^{2}h_{\mu\mu}.\end{split}

By the definition of
P(\xi,\Xi)
, we know

\begin{split}P(\xi_{0},\Xi_{0})&=\alpha^{2}P(\xi_{0},\tau)+\beta^{2}P(\xi_{0},\mu)\\
&\leq\alpha^{2}P(\xi_{0},\Xi_{0})+\beta^{2}P(\xi_{0},\mu).\end{split}

It follows

P(\xi_{0},\Xi_{0})\leq P(\xi_{0},\mu).

In view of Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
, we have

(4.21)

\nabla^{2}h(\Xi_{0},\Xi_{0})\leq|h_{\mu\mu}|(\xi_{0})+2L|\nabla h|^{2}\leq M+C.

Finally, combining (
4.17
) and (
4.20
), we finish the proof first step.

Step 2.

Now, we establish the double normal derivative estimate of
h
on the boundary. To begin with, we introduce an important function

\zeta(\xi):=e^{-d_{\partial C_{\theta}}(\xi)}-1,

where
d_{\partial C_{\theta}}(\xi):=dist(\xi,\partial C_{\theta})
is the geodesic distance function to
\partial C_{\theta}
from
\xi\in C_{\theta}
. The function
\zeta(\xi)
has been used in
[
13
, Lemma 3.1]
,
[
36
, Lemma 3.4]
and
[
37
, Lemma 3.7]
. Notice that
\zeta
satisfies

\zeta|_{\partial C_{\theta}}=0,\qquad and\qquad\nabla\zeta|_{\partial C_{\theta}}=\mu.

Denote

F(A):=\log\det(A)=\log\left(h^{p-1}(h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2})^{\frac{n+1-q}{2}}f\right)=:g(x,h,\nabla h).

We consider an auxiliary function

(4.22)

Q(\xi):=\langle\nabla h,\nabla\zeta\rangle-\cot\theta h(\xi),\qquad\xi\in\partial C_{\theta}.

Fix some point
\xi_{0}\in\partial C_{\theta}
, we choose an orthonormal frame
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
around
\xi_{0}
. From the commutator formulae, we have

(4.23)

\begin{split}F^{ij}Q_{ij}&=F^{ij}h_{kij}\zeta_{k}+F^{ij}h_{k}\zeta_{kij}+2F^{ij}h_{ki}\zeta_{kj}-\cot\theta F^{ij}h_{ij}\\
&=F^{ii}(A_{iik}-h_{i}\delta_{ki})\zeta_{k}+2F^{ii}(A_{ii}-h)\zeta_{ii}+F^{ii}h_{k}\zeta_{kii}-\cot\theta F^{ii}h_{ij}\\
&=\zeta_{k}g_{k}(\xi,h,\nabla h)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}h_{i}\zeta_{i}+2\zeta_{ii}-2hF^{ii}\zeta_{ii}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad+F^{ii}h_{k}\zeta_{kii}-\cot\theta F^{ii}h_{ii}\\
&=\zeta_{k}g_{\xi_{k}}+\zeta_{k}g_{h}h_{k}+g_{h_{i}}Q_{i}-g_{h_{i}}h_{k}\zeta_{ki}+g_{h_{i}}\cot\theta h_{i}\\
&\qquad-\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}h_{i}\zeta_{i}+2\zeta_{ii}-2hF^{ii}\zeta_{ii}+F^{ii}h_{k}\zeta_{kii}\\
&\qquad-n\cot\theta+\cot\theta F^{ii}h,\end{split}

so that

|F^{ij}Q_{ij}-g_{h_{i}}Q_{i}|\leq C(1+\mathfrak{T})\leq C\mathfrak{T},

where
\mathfrak{T}:={\rm tr}F^{ij}
, the positive constant
C
depends on
p
,
q
,
\min_{C_{\theta}}f
,
\min_{C_{\theta}}h
,
\|f\|_{C^{2}(C_{\theta})}
and
\|h\|_{C^{0}(C_{\theta})}
.
Consequently, by
[
12
, Corollary 14.5]
, we have

\sup_{\partial C_{\theta}}|\nabla Q|\leq C,

and therefore

(4.24)

\sup_{\partial C_{\theta}}|\nabla^{2}h(\mu,\mu)|\leq C.

Together with the formulae (
4.2
), we obtain

\max_{C_{\theta}}|\nabla^{2}h|\leq C,

where the positive constant
C
depends on
n
,
p
,
q
,
\min_{C_{\theta}}f
,
\min_{C_{\theta}}h
,
\|f\|_{C^{2}(C_{\theta})}
and
\|h\|_{C^{0}(C_{\theta})}
.

\hfill\Box

Based on the above priori estimates, then we have

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
p>q
and
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
. Suppose that
h
is a solution to Eq. (
1.1
), then there holds

(4.25)

\min_{C_{\theta}}h\geq c,

and for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
,

(4.26)

\|h\|_{C^{3,\alpha}(C_{\theta})}\leq C,

where the positive constant
c
,
C
depend only on
n
,
p
,
q
and
f
.

Proof.

Combining Lemma
3.1
, Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
4.1
, we obtain

c\leq\min_{C_{\theta}}h,\qquad and\qquad\|h\|_{C^{2}(C_{\theta})}\leq C.

By the theory of fully nonlinear second-order uniformly elliptic equations and the Schauder estimate we have (
4.26
).

\hfill\Box

5.
Proof of Theorem 1.1

In this section, we use the continuity method to complete the proof of Theorem 1.1. Let

f_{t}:=(1-t)l^{1-p}\left(l^{2}+|\nabla l|^{2}\right)^{\frac{q-n-1}{2}}+tf,\qquad for\quad 0\leq t\leq 1.

Consider the following family of equations

(5.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\det\left(\nabla^{2}h+h\sigma\right)=h^{p-1}\left(h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2}\right)^{\frac{n+1-q}{2}}f_{t},~~&&in~C_{\theta}\\
&\nabla_{\mu}h=\cot\theta h,~~&&on~\partial C_{\theta}\end{aligned}\right.

Define the set

\mathcal{H}:=\{h\in C^{4,\alpha}(C_{\theta})|\nabla_{\mu}h=\cot\theta h~~on~~\partial C_{\theta}\},

and

\mathcal{I}:=\{t\in[0,1]|\rm{Eq.~\eqref{h1}~has~a~positive~solution~in~\mathcal{H}}\}.

For notational simplicity, define

(5.2)

\mathcal{G}(h_{ij},h):=\det(A),

and

(5.3)

\mathcal{J}_{t}(\nabla h,h):=h^{p-1}(h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2})^{\frac{n+1-q}{2}}f_{t}.

We rewrite Eq. (
1.1
) as

(5.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{G}(h_{ij},h)=\mathcal{J}_{1}(\nabla h,h),~~&&in~C_{\theta}\\
&\nabla_{\mu}h=\cot\theta h,~~&&on~\partial C_{\theta}\end{aligned}\right.

In order to prove Theorem
1.1
, we first show the uniqueness part.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
p>q
and
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
, suppose
h^{1}
,
h^{2}\in C^{2,\alpha}(C_{\theta})
are solutions to the Eq. (
5.4
). Then
h^{1}\equiv h^{2}
.

Proof.

We prove this by contradiction. Without loss of generality, we may assume
h^{1}>h^{2}
somewhere on
C_{\theta}
. Hence, there exists a constant
m\geq 1
such that

mh^{2}-h^{1}\geq 0\quad on\quad C_{\theta},\quad and\quad mh^{2}-h^{1}=0\quad\rm{at~some~point~}P\in C_{\theta}.

For
p>q
, by homogeneity of
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{J}_{1}
and
m\geq 1
, we have

\mathcal{G}(h_{ij}^{1},h^{1})=\mathcal{J}_{1}(\nabla h^{1},h^{1}),

and

\mathcal{G}(mh_{ij}^{2},mh^{2})=m^{q-p}\mathcal{J}_{1}(\nabla mh^{2},mh^{2})\leq\mathcal{J}_{1}(\nabla mh^{2},mh^{2}).

Hence

\begin{split}0&\geq\mathcal{G}(mh^{2}_{ij},mh^{2})-\mathcal{G}(h^{1}_{ij},h^{1})+\mathcal{J}_{1}(\nabla h^{1},h^{1})-\mathcal{J}_{1}(\nabla mh^{2},mh^{2})\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\mathcal{G}(\varepsilon mh^{2}_{ij}+(1-\varepsilon)h^{1}_{ij},\varepsilon mh^{2}+(1-\varepsilon)h^{1})}{d\varepsilon}d\varepsilon-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\mathcal{J}_{1}(\varepsilon\nabla mh^{2}+(1-\varepsilon)\nabla h^{1})}{d\varepsilon}d\varepsilon\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial\mathcal{G}(\varepsilon mh^{2}_{ij}+(1-\varepsilon)h^{1}_{ij},\varepsilon mh^{2}+(1-\varepsilon)h^{1})}{\partial h_{ij}}d\varepsilon\left[(mh^{2}-h^{1})_{ij}+(mh^{2}-h^{1})\delta_{ij}\right]\\
&\qquad-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}_{1}(\varepsilon\nabla mh^{2}+(1-\varepsilon)\nabla h^{1})}{\partial h_{i}}d\varepsilon(mh^{2}-h^{1})_{i}\\
&\qquad-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}_{1}(\varepsilon\nabla mh^{2}+(1-\varepsilon)\nabla h^{1})}{\partial h}d\varepsilon(mh^{2}-h^{1}).\end{split}

That is,
mh^{2}-h^{1}
satisfies an elliptic inequality. The strong maximum principle
[
12
, Theorem 3.5]
yields
mh^{2}\equiv h^{1}
on
C_{\theta}
. This implies that
mh^{2}
solves Eq. (
5.4
), i.e.,

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}(mh^{2}_{ij},mh^{2})&=\mathcal{J}_{1}(\nabla mh^{2},mh^{2})\\
&=m^{n+p-q}\mathcal{J}_{1}(\nabla h^{2},h^{2})\\
&=m^{n+p-q}\mathcal{G}(h^{2}_{ij},h^{2}).\end{split}

By the homogeneity of
\mathcal{G}
implies

\mathcal{G}(mh^{2}_{ij},mh^{2})=m^{n}\mathcal{G}(h^{2}_{ij},h^{2}).

Thus, we have
m^{p-q}=1
, which implies that
m=1
.

\hfill\Box

We define
\mathcal{L}_{h}
to be the linearized operator of Eq. (
5.4
) at
h
, that is, for each
\gamma\in C^{2,\alpha}(C_{\theta})
,

(5.5)

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{h}(\gamma)&=\frac{d(\mathcal{G}((h_{\varepsilon}\gamma)_{ij},h_{\varepsilon}\gamma)-\mathcal{J}_{1}(\nabla(h_{\varepsilon}\gamma),h_{\varepsilon}\gamma))}{d\varepsilon}|_{\varepsilon=0}\\
&=\frac{\partial\mathcal{G}}{\partial h_{ij}}((h\gamma)_{ij}+h\gamma\delta_{ij})-\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}_{1}}{\partial h_{i}}(h\gamma)_{i}-\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}_{1}}{\partial h}h\gamma,\end{split}

where
h_{\varepsilon}=he^{\varepsilon\gamma}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
p>q
and
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
. Suppose
\gamma\in\mathcal{H}
and satisfies

\mathcal{L}_{h}(\gamma)=0,

then
\gamma\equiv 0
on
C_{\theta}
.

Proof.

From
\mathcal{L}_{h}(\gamma)=0
and (
5.5
), we have

\begin{split}0=\mathcal{L}_{h}(\gamma)&=\frac{\partial\mathcal{G}}{\partial h_{ij}}((h\gamma)_{ij}+h\gamma\delta_{ij})-\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}_{1}}{\partial h_{i}}(h\gamma)_{i}-\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}_{1}}{\partial h}h\gamma\\
&=\gamma\mathcal{L}_{h}(1)+h\frac{\partial\mathcal{G}}{\partial h_{ij}}\gamma_{ij}+2\frac{\partial\mathcal{G}}{\partial h_{ij}}h_{i}\gamma_{j}-h\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}_{1}}{\partial h_{i}}\gamma_{i}.\end{split}

Note that the matrix
(\frac{\partial\mathcal{G}}{\partial h_{ij}})_{n\times n}
is positive definite. Therefore, at any minimum point of
\gamma
, one has

h\frac{\partial\mathcal{G}}{\partial h_{ij}}\gamma_{ij}+2\frac{\partial\mathcal{G}}{\partial h_{ij}}h_{i}\gamma_{j}-h\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}_{1}}{\partial h_{i}}\gamma_{i}\geq 0,

which implies

\gamma\mathcal{L}_{h}(1)\leq 0.

By homogeneity of the Monge-Ampère equation

\mathcal{G}(h_{ij},h)=\mathcal{J}_{1}(\nabla h,h),

it’s simple to see that if
p>q
,

\mathcal{L}_{h}(1)=(q-p)\mathcal{J}_{1}(h,\nabla h)<0.

That means, the minimum value of
\gamma
must be nonnegative. Similarly, the maximum value of
\gamma
must be nonpositive. Hence
\gamma\equiv 0
.

\hfill\Box

Now, we give the prove of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof.

Clearly
0\in\mathcal{I}
since
h=l\in\mathcal{H}
is a solution to
(
5.1
)
when
t=0
, so the set
\mathcal{I}
is nonempty. We prove that
\mathcal{I}=[0,1]
by showing
\mathcal{I}
is both open and closed. The closeness of
\mathcal{I}
follows from Theorem
4.2
, while the openness of
\mathcal{I}
follows from Lemma
5.2
. Then it follows that
1\in\mathcal{I}
, i.e., we obtain a positive solution to Eq. (
5.4
). This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

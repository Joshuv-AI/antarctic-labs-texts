On the Auslander–Reiten Theory for Extended Hearts of Proper Connective DG Algebras
Nao Mochizuki, Marvin Plogmann

Abstract
. We prove that, for a proper connective dg algebra A A with cohomology concentrated in degrees between 1 − d 1-d and 0 0 , the extended heart 𝒟 fd ( A ) ( − d , 0 ] ⊆ 𝒟 fd ( A ) \mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(A)^{(-d,0]}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(A) is an extriangulated category with almost-split conflations. We also prove a version of the 1st Brauer–Thrall Conjecture in this context.

Primary: 18G80. Secondary: 16E45, 16G70

1.
Introduction

Let
k
be a field and
A
a connective dg algebra over
k
, that is
H^{*}(A)
is concentrated in non-positive cohomological degrees. We denote by
\mathcal{D}(A)
the (unbounded) derived category of (right) dg
A
-modules, and by
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(A)\subseteq\mathcal{D}(A)
the full subcategory of dg
A
-modules
M
whose cohomology
H^{*}(M)
is finite dimensional. In the following, we fix
A
to be
proper
,
i.e.

H^{*}(A)
is finite dimensional. We will use the notation
\Hom_{A}:=\Hom_{\mathcal{D}(A)}
in what follows.

The standard
t
-structure
(\mathcal{D}(A)^{\leq 0},\mathcal{D}(A)^{\geq 0})
on
\mathcal{D}(A)
is given by the full subcategories

\mathcal{D}(A)^{\leq 0}\coloneqq\{M\colon H^{i}(M)=0\text{ for all }i>0\},\quad\mathcal{D}(A)^{\geq 0}\coloneqq\{M\colon H^{i}(M)=0\text{ for all }i<0\};

it restricts to a
t
-structure on
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(A)
whose heart
\mathcal{H}
is the abelian category of finite-dimensional modules over the finite-dimensional algebra
H^{0}(A)
, denoted
\Mod(H^{0}(A))
. For
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we define
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(A)^{\leq n}\coloneqq\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(A)^{\leq 0}[-n]
,
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(A)^{\geq n}\coloneqq\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(A)^{\geq 0}[-n]
and
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(A)^{<n}\coloneqq\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(A)^{\leq n-1}
,
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(A)^{>n}\coloneqq\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(A)^{\geq n+1}
. The main object of study in this article is the
extended heart

{\mathcal{H}^{{d}}_{A}}\coloneqq\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(A)^{\leq 0}\cap\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(A)^{>-d}

for an integer
d\geq 1
. As both
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(A)^{\leq 0}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(A)^{>-d}
are extension closed,
\mathcal{H}^{{d}}_{A}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(A)
is extension closed and hence all of them are extriangulated by
[
NP19
, Rem. 2.18]
. Recall, for an extension closed subcategory
\mathcal{S}
of a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
, the biadditive functor
\mathbb{E}:{\mathcal{S}}^{op}\times\mathcal{S}\to\Mod(k)
is given by
\mathbb{E}(-,?)=\Hom_{\mathcal{T}}(-,?[1])
and the class of
conflations
in
\mathcal{S}
consists of the triangles

L\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}M\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}N{\longrightarrow}L[1]

in
\mathcal{T}
such that
L,M,N\in\mathcal{S}
. In this case
i
is called an
inflation
and
p
is called a
deflation
.

Remark 1.1
.

It follows from the definitions, that

(1)

f\in\mathcal{H}^{{d}}_{A}(X,Y)
is an inflation if and only if
H^{-d+1}(f)
is a monomorphism in
\mathcal{H}
,

(2)

f\in\mathcal{H}^{{d}}_{A}(X,Y)
is a deflation if and only if
H^{0}(f)
is an epimorphism in
\mathcal{H}
.

For a proof see for example
[
Moc25
, Thm. 4.50]
. Moreover, by
[
Moc25
, Thm. 4.44]
the canonical dg enhancement of
\mathcal{H}^{{d}}_{A}
is an abelian
(d,1)
-category in the sense of
[
Moc25
, Def. 4.12]
; we will, however, make no use of this latter fact.

Recall that an object
P
of an extriangulated category
(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
is called
projective
if
\mathbb{E}(P,X)=0
for all
X\in\mathcal{C}
. An object
I
of
\mathcal{C}
is called
injective
if
\mathbb{E}(X,I)=0
for all
X\in\mathcal{C}
. Denote by
{\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{C})}
and
{\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{C})}
, the full subcategories of projective and injective objects in
\mathcal{C}
respectively. We say that
\mathcal{C}
has
enough projectives
, if for all
X\in\mathcal{C}
there exists
P\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{C})
and a deflation
p\colon P\to X
. Dually,
\mathcal{C}
has
enough injectives
, if for all
X\in\mathcal{C}
there exists
I\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{C})
and an inflation
\iota\colon X\to I
. In this case we denote by
\underline{\mathcal{C}}\coloneqq\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{C})
and
\overline{\mathcal{C}}\coloneqq\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{C})
the stable and costable category respectively (see
[
INP24
, Def. 1.21,Rem. 1.22]
).

Since
A
is proper, there exists an integer
d\geq 1
such that
A\in\mathcal{H}^{{d}}_{A}
; and we fix such
d
for the rest of the note. In this case it follows from
[
Bon24
, Thm. 3.15]
, that
\mathcal{H}^{{d}}_{A}
has enough projectives given by
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H}^{{d}}_{A})=\add(A)
. By dualising the arguments in
[
Bon24
]
we will see in
Corollary

2.3
that
\mathcal{H}^{{d}}_{A}
has enough injectives, which are given by
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}^{{d}}_{A})=\add(\mathbb{D}A[d-1])
, where

{\mathbb{D}(-)}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}\Hom_{k}(-,k)\colon\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(A^{op})^{op}\to\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(A)

denotes the
k
-linear duality functor
.

In the case
A\simeq H^{0}(A)=:\Lambda
, the extended heart
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}^{d}
was studied in
[
Gup24
]
and
[
Zho25
]
. In
[
Gup24
, Th. 4.1]
, a bijection between torsion pairs in
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}^{d}
, cotorsion pairs in
\mathrm{K}^{[-d,0]}(\mathrm{proj}(\Lambda))
and
(d+1)
-term silting complexes is proven. In
[
Zho25
, Th. 4.7]
these bijections are extended to include basic
\tau
-tilting pairs in
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}^{d}
. In particular, it is proven in
[
Zho25
, Th. 3.12]
that
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}^{d}
has an Auslander–Reiten–Serre duality and hence almost-split conflations in the sense of
[
INP24
, Def. 2.7]
, which allow to give a description of (part of) the Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}^{d}
. We prove the following generalization of
[
Zho25
, Thm. 3.12]
in the setting of proper connective dg algebras, which was known to be true for
d
-self-injective dg algebras by
[
Jin20
, Thm. 3.1]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
A
be a proper, connective dg algebra and
d>0
such that
A\in\mathcal{H}^{{d}}_{A}
. Then, there exists an
Auslander–Reiten–Serre duality
in the sense of
[
INP24
, Def. 3.4.]
, that is a pair
(\tau,\eta)
of a
k
-linear equivalence
\tau\colon\underline{\mathcal{H}^{{d}}_{A}}\to\overline{\mathcal{H}^{{d}}_{A}}
and a binatural isomorphism

\eta_{X,Y}\colon\underline{\mathcal{H}^{{d}}_{A}}(X,Y)\cong\mathbb{D}\Hom_{\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(A)}(Y,\tau X[1])\quad\text{for any }X,Y\in{\mathcal{H}^{{d}}_{A}}.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
A
and
d
as above. Then, the extended heart
\mathcal{H}^{{d}}_{A}
is an extriangulated category with almost-split conflations.

Proof.

By
[
AMY19
, Prop. 6.12]
,
\mathcal{H}^{{d}}_{A}
is a
k
-linear
\Ext
-finite extriangulated category. Moreover,
\mathcal{D}(A)
is idempotent complete by combining
[
Kel94
, Sec. 4.1]
and
[
Nee01
, Prop. 1.6.8]
. Now
\mathcal{H}^{{d}}_{A}
is closed under direct summands in
\mathcal{D}(A)
and hence
\mathcal{H}^{{d}}_{A}
is Krull-Schmidt by
[
Kra15
, Coro. 4.4]
. Therefore,
\mathcal{H}^{{d}}_{A}
has almost-split conflations by
[
INP24
, Thm. 3.6]
and
Theorem

1.2
.
∎

We prove the following version of the 1st Brauer–Thrall Conjecture in this context by generalising the Harada–Sai Lemma for
\mathcal{H}^{{d}}_{A}
. We refer the reader to
Section

3
for the definitions.

Theorem
3.20
.

Let
A
be
d
-connected and
\mathcal{C}
a bounded connected component of the Auslander–Reiten quiver of
\mathcal{H}^{{d}}_{A}
. Then
\mathcal{C}
is finite and
\mathcal{C}
is the whole of the Auslander–Reiten quiver of
\mathcal{H}^{{d}}_{A}
.

The main part of this note consists in constructing the additive equivalence
\tau
in the statement above. Our construction is a generalisation of the following construction of the Auslander–Reiten translation for a finite dimensional algebra
\Lambda
. In fact, for the case
A\simeq H^{0}(A)
and
d=1
, our construction is exactly the same and, still if
A\simeq H^{0}(A)
but
d\geq 1
, our construction agrees with
[
Zho25
, Def. 3.7]
.

\nu=-\otimes_{\Lambda}\mathbb{D}\Lambda

Then
\tau

Remark 1.4
.

In the case

Proposition 2.1

Every object in

Proof.

Let

in

Dually to

Bon24

, it holds
\mathrm{rk}(f)\leq b-n
.

Proof.

We prove the statement by induction on

. For
n=1
it holds
t(1)=b(d-1)+2
and we want to show
\mathrm{rk}(f)\leq b-1
. If there exists an
i
such that
\dim_{k}H^{*}(M_{i})<b
we can simply take

. Thus we might assume
\dim_{k}H^{*}(M_{i})=b
for all
1\leq i\leq t
. Now
Lemma

3.11
yields the claim.

Assuming we already proved the statement for
n-1
, we want to prove it for
n
. We first observe that by induction hypothesis there exist factorizations

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{M_{1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{M_{t(n-1)}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{M_{2t(n-1)-1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{M_{t(1)t(n-1)-(t(1)-1)}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F_{1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F_{2}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F_{t(1)}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{f_{1}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{f}_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{f_{3}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{3}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{f}_{t(1)-1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{t(1)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{2}h_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{3}h_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{t(1)}h_{t(1)-1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h_{t(1)}}

with
\dim_{k}H^{*}(F_{i})\leq b-(n-1)
and
\tilde{f_{i}}
is the composition of the

in the respective range. If there exists
i
such that
\dim_{k}H^{*}(F_{i})\leq b-n
we can choose
F\coloneqq F_{i}
. Thus we may assume that
\dim_{k}H^{*}(F_{i})=b-(n-1)
. If none of the
g_{i+1}h_{i}
are isomorphisms we can apply
Lemma

3.11
for
b^{\prime}\coloneqq b-(n-1)
to this sequence and obtain
\mathrm{rk}(f)\leq b-n
. Otherwise, there exists
i
such that
g_{i+1}h_{i}
is an isomorphism. Hence
h_{i}
is a split monomorphism and since
M_{i\cdot t(n-1)-(i-1)}
is indecomposable,
h_{i}
is an isomorphism and in particular
\dim_{k}H^{*}(M_{i\cdot t(n-1)-(i-1)})=b-(n-1)
.

Observe that the above argument works analogously if instead of
M_{1},M_{t(n-1)},\ldots
we consider
M_{k},M_{k+t(n-1)},\ldots
for
1\leq k\leq(b-(n-1))(d-1)<t(n-1)
. Therefore, we get for each such

an

for
k^{\prime}\neq k
such that
\dim_{k}H^{*}(M_{i_{k}})=b-(n-1)
. Consider the subsequence of the original sequence which only contains these
M_{i_{k}}
, obtained by composing the respective
f_{j}
between them. This is a sequence of non-isomorphisms as otherwise we would get a split epimorphism and by indecomposability an isomorphism. Thus we can again apply

3.11
to this sequence for

and obtain
\mathrm{rk}(f)\leq b-n
.
∎

Remark 3.13
.

Considering the proof, one could reduce the bound by definining for

4.
Explicit Description of the Auslander–Reiten Translation

In this section, we discuss the explicit calculation of Auslander–Reiten translations
\tau
inside of
\mathcal{H}^{{d}}_{A}
. We first describe the general procedure and illustrate it with an explicit example afterwards. We start with
M\in\mathcal{H}^{{d}}_{A}
.

First Step
: Compute a
d
-term projective resolution of
M
as in the proof of
Lemma

2.7
, that is a sequence of conflations

\Omega^{i}M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota^{i}}}{{\longrightarrow}}P^{i-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p^{i-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Omega^{i-1}M\dashrightarrow\text{ for }1\leq i\leq d+1,

where
\Omega^{0}(M)\coloneqq M
and
P^{i}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H}^{{d}}_{A})
.

Second Step
: Apply the exact functor
\nu_{d}\coloneqq[d-1]\circ\nu_{A}
to obtain

\nu_{d}(\Omega^{i}M)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nu_{d}(\iota^{i})}}{{\longrightarrow}}I^{i-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nu_{d}(p^{i-1})}}{{\longrightarrow}}\nu_{d}(\Omega^{i-1}M)\dashrightarrow\text{ for }1\leq i\leq d+1,

where
I^{i}\coloneqq\nu_{d}(P^{i})\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}^{{d}}_{A})
.

Third Step
: Define
\ker=t^{\leq 0}\circ\Cocone
and inductively

\Sigma^{1}:=\ker(\nu_{d}(\iota^{1})\circ\nu_{d}(p^{1})),\quad\Sigma^{i+1}:=\ker(I^{i+1}\to\Sigma^{i})\text{ for }1\leq i\leq d-1.

We claim that
\Sigma^{d}
computes
\tau M
, that is
\Sigma^{d}\cong\tau M
.

To this end, in the above situation, we define inductively

I^{[0,1]}:=\Cocone(I^{1}\to I^{0}),\quad I^{[0,i]}:=\Cocone(I^{i+1}\to I^{[0,i]})\text{ for }i\geq 1.

Recall from the proof of
Lemma

2.7
, that
P^{[-i,0]}
was defined inductively by

P^{[-1,0]}\coloneqq\cone(P^{-1}\to P^{0}),\quad P^{[-(i+1),0]}\coloneqq\cone(P^{i+1}[i]\to P^{[-i,0]})\text{ for }i\geq 1.

Hence
\nu_{d}(P^{[-i,0]})\cong I^{[0,i]}[-i]
and in particular, by definition of
\tau
,

t^{\leq 0}I^{[0,d]}\cong t^{\leq 0}\left(\nu_{d}(P^{[-d,0]})[-d]\right)\cong t^{\leq 0}\left(\nu_{A}(P^{[-d,0]})[-1]\right)\cong t^{<d}\left(\nu_{A}(P^{[-d,0]})[-d]\right)[d-1]\cong\tau M.

Thus, it remains to show the following proposition, which is a dual version of
Lemma

2.7
.

Proposition 4.1
.

There is an isomorphism
t^{\leq{0}}I^{[0,i]}\cong\Sigma^{i}
for
1\leq i\leq d
.

Proof.

We prove the claim by induction on
i
. For
i=1
, the claim follows by definition. For
i\geq 2
, by induction and the octahedral axiom we have the following diagram of triangles:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I^{i}[1]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I^{i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I^{[0,i-1]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I^{[0,i]}[1]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I^{i}[1]}

6.
Final Remarks

We finish the discussion with the following observation, which highlights again the analogy between
K^{[-1,0]}(\mathrm{proj}(\Lambda))
and
\per(A)^{[-d,0]}
. In this final section we only assume that
A
is proper, connective and
d\geq 1
. As
\per(A)^{[-d,0]}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(A)
is extension-closed, it has the induced structure of an extriangulated category. Recall from
[
Che23a
, Def. 3.3]
that an extriangulated category is called
d
-Auslander
, if it has enough projectives, enough injectives, positive global dimension at most
d+1
and dominant dimension at least
d+1
, see
[
Che23a
, Def. 3.1,3.2]
for the definitions.

Lemma 6.1
.

The extriangulated category
\per(A)^{[-d,0]}
is
(d-1)
-Auslander.

Remark 6.2
.

In this sense,
Theorem

2.5
can be viewed as a special case of a “
(d-1)
-analogue” of
[
GNP23
, Prop. 3.11, Sec. 3.3.3]
.

Proof.

By the same argument as in
Corollary

2.3
, we know that
\per(A)^{[-d,0]}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(A)^{\leq 0}
has enough projectives given by
\add(A)
. We want to argue it has enough injectives as well. We observe that
\add(A[d])\subseteq\mathcal{I}(\per(A)^{[-d,0]})
by
Lemma

2.8
. Moreover, for every
P\in\per(A)^{[-d,0]}
there exists by definition a triangle

P^{\prime}\to P\to P^{-d}[d]\to P^{\prime}[1]

in
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(A)
such that
P^{\prime}[1]\in\per(A)^{[-d,0]}
and
P^{-d}\in\add(A)
. Hence, there exists an inflation
P\to P^{-d}[d]
, which proves there are enough injectives. As in
Corollary

2.3
one proves
\mathcal{I}(\per(A)^{[-d,0]})=\add(A[d])
.

It follows from
Lemma

2.8
that
\per(A)^{[-d,0]}
has positive global dimension at most
d
. It remains to check that the dominant dimension is at least
d
. Let
P\in\mathcal{P}(\per(A)^{[-d,0]})=\add(A)
. Then a minimal injective coresolution is given by the following sequence of conflations

P\rightarrowtail 0\twoheadrightarrow P[1]\dashrightarrow;\;P[1]\rightarrowtail 0\twoheadrightarrow P[2]\dashrightarrow;\;\cdots;\;P[d-1]\rightarrowtail 0\twoheadrightarrow P[d]\dashrightarrow;\;P[d]\rightarrowtail P[d]\twoheadrightarrow 0\dashrightarrow,

where
0
is projective-injective, and
P[d]
is only injective but not projective if
P\neq 0
. It follows that the dominant dimension of
\per(A)^{[-d,0]}
is at least
d
and equal to
d
if
A\not\simeq 0
.
∎

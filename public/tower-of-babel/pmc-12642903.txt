The first complete chloroplast genome and phylogenetic analysis of Rhododendron simsii var. putuoense G. Y. Li & Z. H. Chen (Ericaceae) from East China
Authors: Hong Zhu, Xiaozhen Lv
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe sequenced the first chloroplast (cp) genome of Rhododendron simsii var. putuoense G. Y. Li & Z. H. Chen 2010, an endemic coastal plant species unique to East China, to clarify its phylogenetic relationships. The cp genome is 151,998 bp long with a 35.76% GC content and lacks the typical quadripartite structure. It contains118 genes: 76 mRNAs, 38 tRNAs, and four ribosomal RNAs. Phylogenetic analysis shows that R. simsii var. putuoense is closely related to R. simsii supporting its recent divergence from this species. This study expands genetic resource for the variety and clarifies its position within Rhododendron.Keywords: Rhododendron simsii var. putuoense (Ericaceae), complete chloroplast genome, phylogenetic relationship

Introduction

Rhododendron simsii var. putuoense G. Y. Li & Z. H. Chen 2010, an endemic plant species in China, is exclusively distributed across the coastal regions of Zhoushan Archipelago and adjacent areas in Zhejiang Province (Li et al. 2010). Its unique geographical distribution and ecological niche make it an ideal subject for investigating coastal species conservation and plant adaptive evolution (Zhu et al. 2025).

The chloroplast (cp) genomes have proven to be highly informative for phylogeny, DNA barcoding, genome evolution,and species conservation (Wen et al. 2021; Zhu and Li 2024). To date, no cp data have been reported for R. simsii var. putuoense. To better understand the taxonomic and evolutionary relationships of Rhododendron, we sequenced and assembled the first complete cp genome of R. simsii var. putuoense, and conducted comparative genomics analyses to elucidate its characteristics and phylogenetic position. These findings will provide valuable insights for future studies on the phylogenetic analyses and genetic diversity of Rhododendron species.

Materials and methods

Plant materials

Fresh leaves of R. simsii var. putuoense (Figure 1) were collected from Wuleishan, Dinghai District, Zhoushan City, China (coordinates: 29.43°N, 122.20°E; altitude: 359 m) in September 2024. The sample was identified and collected by the authors, and a specimen was deposited in the Herbarium of Zhejiang Academy of Forestry (HZJAF, contact: Hong Zhu, Email: 1107401987@qq.com) under the voucher number HZ2024-DH-01.

Plant morphological characteristics of R. simsii var. putuoense. (A) whole individual shape; (B) terminal young leaves grow in early spring; (C) early spring flower buds; (D) inflorescence. The photo was taken by Dr. Hong Zhu without any copyright issues. R. simsii var. putuoense is characterized by having rose pink corollas, in contrast to the dark red corollas of its closest relative, R. simsii.

DNA extraction and sequencing, genome assembly, and annotation

Total genomic DNA was extracted using a plant DNA kit (Tiangen Biotech, Beijing, China). The quality and integrity of the extracted DNA were initially evaluated via agarose gel electrophoresis. Subsequently, the DNA was sheared into fragments using ultrasonication. Libraries were constructed and subjected to quality control, after which qualified samples were sequenced using the Illumina Novaseq 6000 platform with paired-end sequencing (PE150). Raw reads were processed for quality filtering using fastp v. 0.23.4 (Chen 2023) and followed by genome assembly with SPAdes v. 3.10.1 (Bankevich et al. 2012). Organelle genome annotation was conducted using GetOrganelle v.1.7.7.1 (Jin et al. 2020). Circular cp genome maps were visualized using online program OGDraw v. 1.2 (Greiner et al. 2019).

Phylogenetic analysis

To investigate the phylogenetic placement of R. simsii var. putuoense in the genus Rhododendron L., cp genome sequences of 24 related taxa were obtained from the GenBank database. Additionally, four outgroup species - Vaccinium bracteatum Thunb. 1784 (LC521967.1), V. oldhamii Miq. 1865 (NC_042713.1), V. macrocarpon Aiton 1789 (JQ248601.1), and Pyrola rotundifolia L. 1753 (KU833271.1) were designated as outgroups for phylogenetic analysis. We aligned these sequences using the program MAFFT v. 7.429 (Katoh et al. 2019) and constructed a phylogenetic tree using the maximum likelihood (ML) algorithm. To do this, we used the software RAxML v. 8.2.12 (Stamatakis 2014) and performed 1000 bootstrap replicates using the (GTR + G) model based on gamma distribution.

Results

Genome sequencing and assembly

After quality control, the obtained Clean Data contained 19,573,801 reads and 5,872,140,300 bases, with Q20 and Q30 being 97.28% and 92.72% respectively. The total length of this cp genome is 151,998 bp (Figure 2), with an average coverage of 830.38× (Figure S1) and GC content of 35.76%. A total of 118 unique genes were annotated, including 38 transfer RNA (tRNA) genes, four ribosomal RNA (rRNA) genes and 76 messenger RNA (mRNA) genes. Among these genes, nine were identified as cis-splicing (ycf3, rps16, rpl16, petB, petD, ndhB, atpF, rpoC and ndhA) (Figure S2), whereas no trans-splicing genes were identified (Figure S3).

The cp genome map of R. simsii var. putuoense. Genes depicted inside the circular map are transcribed in the clockwise direction, while those positioned outside are transcribed counterclockwise. Within the inner ring, light grey and darker grey regions denote AT and GC content, respectively. Functional gene groups are annotated using color-coded boxes for distinction. The cp genome sequence has been deposited in the NCBI database under the accession number PV614722.

Characteristics of cp genomes from R. simsii var. putuoense

The results of gene annotation revealed that the cp genome of R. simsii var. putuoense comprises a total of 118 functional genes. Among these, mRNA genes were the most abundant (76), followed by tRNA genes (38), and rRNA genes were the least numerous (4). Specifically, 14 genes harbored a single intron (including ndhA, ndhB, petB, petD, atpF, rpl16, rps16, rpoC1, trnA-UGC, trnG-UCC, trnI-GAU, trnK-UUU, trnL-UAA, and trnV-UAC), while two genes (rps12 and ycf3) contained two introns each.

Phylogenetic relationships

A phylogenetic tree was constructed based on 24 species within the genus Rhododendron, including five species from subgenus Tsutsusi (Sweet) Pojarkova, one species from subgenus Azaleastrum Planch. ex K. Koch, one species from subgenus Pentanthera (G. Don) Pojarkova, nine species from subgenus Hymenanthes (Blume) K. Koch, and eight species in subgenus Rhododendron K. Koch. Three species from the Vaccinium L. and one species from Pyrola L. were used as an outgroup taxa. Within subgenus Tsutsusi, R. simsii var. putuoense demonstrates the closest phylogenetic relationship with two individuals of R. simsii Planch., forming a strongly supported sister clade (Figure 3).

Discussion and conclusion

In this study, we reported and characterized the first complete cp genome sequence of R. simsii var. putuoense. Our analysis aimed to explore the genomic information of this species, contributing to phylogenetic reconstruction and molecular evidence for Rhododendron species. The high support rates for the phylogenetic clustering of R. simsii var. putuoense (PV614722) with two R. simsii accessions (MW030509.1 and MT239364.1) likely result from their close genetic relationship and recent divergence. The topological structure indicates that R. simsii var. putuoense shares a recent common ancestor with R. simsii, supported by minimal genetic differentiation in their chloroplast genomes. This cpDNA-based conclusion is further corroborated by reduced-representation genomic evidence (Zhu et al. 2025).

Another key finding is that the cp genome of R. simsii var. putuoense lacks inverted repeat (IR) regions, thereby deviating from the typical quadripartite structure of most land plant cp genomes. Notably, this feature aligns with previously reported cp genomes of two congeneric species within subgenus Tsutsusi: R.x pulchrum Sweet 1831 (Shen et al. 2019) and R. farrerae Sweet 1831 (Shen and Huang 2024). Despite limited chloroplast genome data for subgenus Tsutsusi, these shared observations suggest IR region loss may represent a lineage-specific genomic trait within this subgenus. This potential synapomorphy warrants investigation into its taxonomic utility for subgenus delimitation and evolutionary drivers (e.g. common ancestral deletion vs. convergent evolution). Future studies should expand sampling of subgenus Tsutsusi (including additional R. simsii variants and congeners) and employ comparative genomic analyses to reconstruct IR region evolutionary dynamics in Rhododendron.

Collectively, these findings not only advance our understanding of the evolutionary history of Rhododendron but also provide important insights into the genomic dynamics and interspecific relationships within this genus.

Prediction of uterine cavity conception environment using two-dimensional transvaginal ultrasound imaging semantic feature-based machine learning: a case-control study
Authors: Jinwei Zhang, Shuang Liu, Yue Rong, Shangqing Liu, Chunyan Zhong, Peng Tu, Xiaohang Zhang, Suzhen Ran
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIndependently investigating the association between pregnancy outcomes and the uterine cavity conception environment (UCCE) is challenging. Therefore, this study aimed to employ a range of machine learning algorithms to systematically analyze the semantic features derived from two-dimensional transvaginal ultrasound (TVUS) images that characterize the UCCE.MethodsThis case-control study was conducted at the Women and Children’s Hospital of Chongqing Medical University. Preoperative TVUS findings in patients diagnosed with infertility who subsequently achieved clinical pregnancy within 1 year following hysteroscopic intervention alone were classified as abnormal UCCE (case group), whereas TVUS findings in the uterine cavity of women who successfully achieved clinical pregnancy through follicular monitoring during the same cycle were classified as normal UCCE (control group). We randomly divided 442 participants (cases, n = 244; controls, n = 198) into the training (n = 309) and testing (n = 133) sets. A structured description of the ultrasonic semantic features of UCCE was derived by integrating the images and diagnostic reports. To employ logistic regression (LR), the Boruta algorithm, Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator for univariate feature selection, and five machine learning models—LR, random forest (RF), XGBoost, support vector machines (SVM), and LightGBM—were constructed, followed by a comprehensive evaluation of their predictive performance. Additionally, the Shapley additive explanation technology was employed to elucidate the output results from the best-performing model. A nomogram was created for diagnostic prediction model visualization.ResultsEndometrial thickness, echogenicity, polyps, polyp size, continuity and uterine morphology, and septum type were the semantic features with the highest UCCE predictive value. In the testing set, XGBoost and LightGBM showed the highest (0.982) and lowest (0.902) area under the curve (AUC), respectively. XGBoost, SVM, RF, and MLR attained excellent prediction accuracy (0 < Brier score < 0.1) across the training and testing sets. Decision-curve analysis demonstrated that all models provided substantial net clinical benefits. A significant difference was observed in the nomoscore between the normal and abnormal UCCE groups (p < .001).ConclusionsMachine learning models using semantic features derived from two-dimensional TVUS images enabled accurate preliminary screening for natural conception preparation or before embryo implantation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12884-025-08390-6.

Background

Infertility is failure to achieve a successful pregnancy after more than 1 year of unprotected and regular sexual activity. Approximately one in six couples experiences infertility [1]. Optimal embryo quality and a normal uterine cavity conception environment (UCCE) are critical determinants of successful clinical pregnancy [2]. Timely identification of an abnormal UCCE amenable to intervention via minimally invasive hysteroscopic surgery can mitigate the risks associated with unmonitored pregnancy attempts and reduce the wastage of high-quality embryos, thereby significantly improving reproductive efficiency within the population.

Recently, female UCCE assessment has encompassed various complex diagnostic modalities, some of which are invasive or require contrast agent use, such as hysteroscopy, hysterosalpingography, and magnetic resonance imaging. Transvaginal ultrasonography (TVUS) is the predominant diagnostic imaging tool owing to its reliability, non-invasiveness, efficiency, and cost-effectiveness [3, 4]. Research has focused on using ultrasonographic parameters for screening and multivariate analyses. Differences in these parameters across various assisted reproductive technology-associated clinical pregnancy outcomes have been compared to assess endometrial receptivity [5–8]. However, a substantial challenge in these studies is the difficulty of independently investigating the association between pregnancy outcomes and the UCCE. In cases of successful pregnancy, the UCCE is considered normal. Conversely, failed pregnancy cannot be definitively attributed solely to UCCE-related issues. Hysteroscopy—which provides direct uterine cavity visualization—is the gold standard for intrauterine pathology diagnosis and management [9, 10]. Consequently, for women with clinically diagnosed infertility who have subsequently achieved successful pregnancies shortly after hysteroscopic intervention, the pre-intervention uterine cavity condition may be characterized as an abnormal UCCE.

Machine learning, a significant artificial intelligence subfield [11, 12], can construct sample data-derived mathematical models to forecast the likelihood of certain events occurring [13]. On the basis of this, the present study proposed a novel approach: by screening research participants to identify normal UCCE control and abnormal UCCE case groups, and we can perform structured extraction of semantic features from standardized two-dimensional TVUS diagnostic reports for both groups. Subsequently, machine learning algorithms could be employed to conduct a comparative analysis of the characteristics associated with UCCE. Thus, we aimed to develop and validate a robust clinical diagnostic prediction model to assist clinicians in UCCE quality evaluation prior to natural conception or embryo implantation.

Materials and methods

Ethics statements

The Ethics Committee of the Women and Children’s Hospital of Chongqing Medical University approved this retrospective study (approval number: IRB-2024056), and the requirement for informed consent was waived. This study was conducted in accordance with the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. Reporting followed the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology guidelines.

Study design and patients

In this case-control study, which aimed to develop and validate a robust clinical diagnostic prediction model to assist clinicians in UCCE quality evaluation, we conducted a retrospective analysis of women who sought reproductive healthcare at our institute between June 2017 and December 2023. We characterized the preoperative TVUS findings in patients diagnosed with infertility [14] who subsequently achieved clinical pregnancy within 1 year following hysteroscopic intervention alone and classified the patients as having an abnormal UCCE (case group). Women with TVUS findings who successfully achieved clinical pregnancy through follicular monitoring during the same cycle were classified as having a normal UCCE (control group).

Inclusion criteria for cases were (1) fertility requirements and age ≥18 years; (2) clinically diagnosed infertility; and (3) TVUS examination before hysteroscopic intervention (within 5–7 days post-menstruation cessation), with successful clinical pregnancy achieved exclusively within 1-year post-hysteroscopy. Exclusion criteria for cases were (1) TVUS findings inconsistent with hysteroscopic findings and (2) suboptimal ultrasound image quality. Inclusion criteria for controls were (1) fertility requirements and age ≥18 years; (2) ovulation monitoring through continuous TVUS assessment; (3) successful clinical pregnancy within the cycle; and (4) natural conception. Exclusion criteria for controls were (1) uterine surgery history and (2) suboptimal ultrasound image quality. Data on age, conception methods, number of gestations, and delivery modes were collected in both groups. Infertility classifications and hysteroscopic intervention types for cases were recorded.

Ultrasonography

TVUS was performed using ultrasonography equipment (GE E8, GE S6, GE S8, GE E6, GE Healthcare, Chicago, IL, USA; IE33, Q5, Philips Company, Amsterdam, Netherlands; probe frequency, 3–11 MHz). Comprehensive uterine and bilateral adnexal examinations were performed. All sonographers had ≥ 5 years of experience in gynecological ultrasonography and used a standardized ultrasound diagnostic report template, ensuring accurate ultrasound image descriptions.

Structured semantic features of ultrasound imaging

The ultrasound images and diagnostic reports were retrieved from the picture archiving and communication system. A sonographer with 8 years of experience in gynecological ultrasonography (JZ, reader 1) structured the semantic features within the corresponding diagnostic reports and images. The structured ultrasound imaging characteristics of the UCCE were delineated as follows: uterine morphology (normal/irregular/abnormal), endometrial continuity (normal/poor/interrupted), endometrial morphology (uniform/non-uniform), abnormal endometrial echogenicity band (yes/no), endometrial echogenicity (homogeneous/lack of homogeneity/heterogeneous), endometrial compression (yes/no), display of the uterine horns (both/one side/neither), endometrial polyp (none/single/multiple), submucous uterine myomas (none/single/multiple), size of submucous uterine myomas (d1×d2; mm2), intramural uterine myomas/adenomyoma (none/single/multiple), subserosal uterine myomas (none/single/multiple), uterine myometrial echogenicity (homogeneous/lack of homogeneity/heterogeneous), and types of uterine septum (none/fork/uterus arcuatus/partial uterine septum/complete uterine septum). Endometrial thickness was determined by measuring the maximum distance between the two lateral edges of the endometrium in a longitudinal section of the uterus [8, 15]. The size of endometrial polyps (d1×d2; mm2) was transformed into an ordered categorical variable ranging from 1 to 11 for every increment of 10 mm². The above semantic features encompass morphological variations of the uterine cavity observed on TVUS associated with normal states or various uterine pathologies. The semantic features of ultrasound image identification were validated by another sonographer with 12 years of experience in gynecological ultrasonography (SL, reader 2); in case of disagreement, reader 2 re-evaluated the images, and this assessment was considered the final decision.

Screening for predictive features

In the training set, logistic regression (LR) was initially employed for univariate analysis, retaining predictive factors with p <.05. The Boruta algorithm was used to identify features with significant predictive importance, whereas the Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) was applied to discern and select the most relevant predictive features. The optimal value of the LASSO model parameter λ was determined through 10-fold cross-validation. The features selected for predicting UCCE quality were established by integrating the results of the three feature selection methodologies.

Machine learning model construction and evaluation

Using machine learning algorithms available in the R software package, clinical diagnostic prediction models for UCCE quality were constructed. Five machine learning models were selected: LR, random forest (RF), XGBoost, support vector machines (SVM), and LightGBM. Stepwise backward analysis was used to identify independent factors capable of predicting UCCE quality based on Akaike information criterion. Through 1,000 bootstrap re-sampling applications, we assessed the discriminative capability of the model by calculating the area under the curve (AUC), accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, negative predictive value, precision, recall, F1-score, and Brier score for the training and testing sets. Calibration curves were employed to assess the degree of fit between the model predictions and results. Decision-curve analysis (DCA) was used for model clinical utility evaluation across various threshold probabilities. Additionally, the Delong test was used to compare the differences in AUC values among the different models. For the optimal machine learning model, we used the Shapley additive explanation (SHAP) technique [16] to evaluate the contribution of each feature and interpret the optimal model output. Based on the results of feature screening, a nomogram was constructed. The nomogram-derived score (nomoscore) was further calculated and compared between the case and control groups. Finally, the clinical application of the nomogram was demonstrated using samples from the testing set.

Statistical analyses

All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.3.1; R Foundation) with various packages including glm, Boruta, glmnet, forestplot, pROC, rms, rmda, ggplot2, and fastshap. Continuous variables are reported as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range), depending on data normality. Categorical variables are described by frequency (rate). We applied the χ2 or Fisher exact test to compare categorical variables and the Mann–Whitney U-test to compare continuous variables. All statistical analyses were conducted using two-sided tests, with the significance level set at p <.05.

The established research criteria were met by 442 participants who underwent TVUS (cases, n = 244; controls, n = 198). All participants were randomly divided into a training (n = 309) or testing (n = 133) set in a 7:3 ratio. Fig. 1 illustrates the study participant enrollment procedure.

Important features for machine learning model construction

Among the 17 features in the baseline statistical analysis, six were excluded from the feature selection process owing to their insufficient representation in the overall dataset (≤10 instances): abnormal endometrial echogenicity band, endometrial compression, uterine horn display, submucosal uterine myomas and their size, and endometrial morphology. Univariate analysis of the remaining 11 UCCE-associated features showed significant differences among endometrial thickness, echogenicity, polyp, polyp size, and continuity; age; and uterine morphology (p <.05), in decreasing order of significance (Fig. 2). Boruta feature selection showed that the features ranked by their predictive importance (from highest to lowest) were endometrial thickness, echogenicity, continuity, polyps, and polyp size; uterine septum type; and uterine morphology (Fig. 3). LASSO identified the five most significant features based on their non-zero coefficients (from highest to lowest): endometrial echogenicity, continuity, thickness, polyp, and uterine septum type (Fig. 4). Four features were identified by integrating all three methods: endometrial thickness, echogenicity, continuity, and polyps. Additionally, three features were selected on the basis of the convergence of the two methods: endometrial polyp size, uterine septum type, and uterine morphology.

Receiver operating characteristic curve analysis demonstrated commendable predictive performance for all the five machine learning models across the training and testing sets (Fig. 5a-b). XGBoost and LightGBM showed the highest (0.982 [95% confidence interval [CI], 0.962–1.000]) and lowest (0.902 [95% CI, 0.853–0.950]) AUC values in the testing set, respectively. The Delong test indicated that XGBoost did not significantly differ compared to LR, RF, and SVM with regards to AUC values (p =.598, 0.125, and 0.473, respectively), whereas a significant difference was observed when compared with LightGBM (p =.003). Table 2 presents the Brier scores for the five models; specifically, XGBoost, SVM, RF, and LR demonstrated excellent predictive accuracy in the training and testing sets (0 < Brier score < 0.1), whereas LightGBM achieved good predictive accuracy (0.10 < Brier score < 0.25). Additionally, the calibration curves illustrated the consistency of all five machine learning models in predicting UCCE quality (Fig. 5c-d). Furthermore, DCA revealed that across most probability threshold ranges in the training and testing sets, all five models provided substantial net clinical benefits (Fig. 5e-f). The SHAP summary plot presented in Fig. 6 illustrates the top seven XGBoost features ranked by their SHAP values in descending order across all predictions. An increase in endometrial thickness was associated with a more negative impact on prediction outcomes, thereby reducing the likelihood of an abnormal UCCE. Conversely, as indicated by six other features—endometrial echogenicity, continuity, and polyps and their size; uterine septum types; and uterine morphology—the worsening severity of these conditions correlated positively with prediction results, thus increasing the probability of an abnormal UCCE.

Nomogram construction and validation

A nomogram based on LR was constructed to provide a quantitative and convenient tool for predicting the risk of an abnormal UCCE using the variables Endometrial thickness, echogenicity, polyps, polyp size, continuity and uterine morphology, and septum type (Fig. 7). In both the training and testing sets, a significant difference in the nomoscore was observed between the normal and abnormal UCCE groups (p <.001; Fig. 8). This demonstrates that this nomogram can effectively identify patients who may benefit from hysteroscopic surgery to address underlying causes of infertility. Long-term pregnancy outcome follow-up survey

All 442 participants completed the follow-up assessments of pregnancy outcomes (Table 1). The predominant conception mode within the case group was in vitro fertilization (IVF, 60.66% of cases), whereas spontaneous conceptions accounted for 36.48%; conversely, all controls achieved pregnancy spontaneously. The vaginal delivery rate was significantly lower among cases than among controls (p <.001), whereas the cesarean section rate was markedly higher in cases (p <.001). No significant between-group difference was observed in terms of miscarriage rates (p =.328). Additionally, among cases, 2 (0.82%) and 1 (0.41%) women had preterm rupture of membranes and premature vaginal delivery at 33 + 4 weeks, respectively.

Discussion

Various uterine pathologies can adversely affect the normal intrauterine environment, thereby significantly contributing to infertility. Consequently, preemptive identification of an abnormal UCCE amenable to hysteroscopic intervention is clinically relevant for mitigating high-quality embryo loss and enhancing natural conception efficacy. TVUS accurately presents uterine cavity morphological features. Several ultrasonographic features, including endometrial thickness and echogenicity, are independent clinical pregnancy outcome predictors [17, 18]. In this study, we used seven features (endometrial thickness, echogenicity, continuity, polyp, and polyp size; uterine septum types; and uterine morphology) to construct predictive models. These morphological features indicated a significant correlation between the specific abnormal two-dimensional ultrasound UCCE findings and infertility. Targeted hysteroscopic interventions addressing these features can effectively eliminate the underlying infertility causes. Among the excluded features, endometrial morphology (n = 10), abnormal endometrial echogenicity band (n = 8), endometrial compression (n = 2), and uterine horn display (n = 3) were not incorporated into the feature selection because of insufficient sample sizes. Nevertheless, an analysis of the overall sample characteristics revealed that these four features exclusively occurred in cases. Endometrial morphology and abnormal endometrial echogenicity bands exhibited significant differences, indicating their potential clinical predictive value.

This study aimed to use preoperative TVUS findings as a definitive infertility cause in patients who successfully achieved clinical pregnancy within 1 year post-hysteroscopic intervention. Our sample included only one submucosal myoma case, probably because of the low probability of achieving a successful pregnancy within 1 year post-hysteroscopic submucosal myomectomy. Yang et al. reported a median duration to successful pregnancy post-hysteroscopic submucosal myomectomy of 15 months [19]. In the comparative analysis of benign intrauterine lesion treatment by Yong et al., the average durations to successful pregnancy were 13.14 months after hysteroscopic tissue removal system (MyoSure) use and 16.26 months after hysteroscopic electroresection. Among the study cases, submucosal myomas accounted for 21.09% and 25.71% of the two hysteroscopic surgery methods, respectively [20]. Among the cases, 21 women with infertility successfully conceived within 1 year following simple hysteroscopy, which may be attributed to UCCE improvement due to uterine cavity irrigation, uterine distention fluid pressure, and potential endometrial injury during the examination [21–24]. Our five machine learning models demonstrated robust predictive performance, which was attributed to the semantic features derived from two-dimensional ultrasound images and the structured feature classification using binary or ordered multiclass approaches. Although this approach enhanced model generalizability, it compromised the accuracy. Therefore, our model is better suited as a preliminary UCCE screening tool. In the future, we intend to analyze ultrasound endometrial images using radiomics [25] and deep learning [26] to convert the subjective semantic features identified by sonographers into objective high-throughput features, thereby enhancing population screening accuracy.

This study has few limitations. First, compared with cases, controls were selected among patients who underwent routine examination; therefore, the ultrasonographic results were not hysteroscopically verified. Moreover, we used hysteroscopy as the gold standard for diagnosing intracavitary lesions; therefore, case selection required consistent TVUS and hysteroscopy results. However, as hysteroscopy is more accurate than TVUS in intracavitary lesion diagnosis [27], TVUS findings could have been negative in cases of positive hysteroscopy findings [28]. Consequently, our predictive model may be subject to performance overestimation. Thus, a prospective case collection is crucial for model validation. Second, given the retrospective nature of this study, we did not analyze blood flow signal features of the endometrial and subendometrial areas. This decision was based on potential equipment and parameter variations that could influence blood flow signal visualization. Furthermore, the predictive value associated with endometrial and subendometrial blood flow remains unclear [29]. Increasing the sample size and number of participating centers are critical for enhancing prediction accuracy, especially to accrue sufficient cases for feature types that are currently underrepresented in our sample.

Conclusion

This case-control study employed machine learning models that leveraged semantic features derived from two-dimensional TVUS imaging for effective UCCE screening and nomogram construction. This approach offers a novel tool to assist clinicians in UCCE quality evaluation during natural conception preparation or before embryo implantation.

Supervised attention method with co-reference resolution for aspect extraction
Authors: Ganpat Singh Chauhan, Shalini Puri, Ravi Nahta, Neeraj Garg
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAspect-based sentiment analysis (ABSA) of review texts remains a challenging problem due to the contextual interdependence of aspect evaluations across multiple sentences. Most existing approaches perform aspect extraction in isolation, limiting their ability to capture aligned semantics and multi-word (M-W) aspect terms in complex, multi-sentence (M-S) reviews. Furthermore, baseline and recent methods struggle to model long-range contextual dependencies, resulting in incomplete or fragmented aspect identifications. To address these limitations, this paper proposes a supervised hierarchical attention-based model for enhanced aspect term detection. The proposed model incorporates co-referencing resolution (CRR)-based sentence-alignment mechanism before ABSA, which resolves pronouns and referring expressions across sentences, generating semantically aligned inputs for the hierarchical attention network (HAN). This preprocessing step enhances contextual coherence, enabling the word-level and sentence-level attention mechanisms in HAN to more effectively capture aspect-related information across multi-sentence reviews. Furthermore, contextual representations are learned using word vectors enriched with semantic context, which are then processed through HAN using sequence-tagged labeled data. Experiments conducted on the SemEval-16 benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed model consistently outperforms strong baselines and state-of-the-art methods across the Laptop and Restaurant domains. The experimental evaluation shows that the proposed model improves F-Score by + 2.77% and + 3.52% over AspectGCN, and by + 4.16% and + 2.32% over IDGNN + BERT on the laptop and restaurant review datasets, respectively. It highlights that the inclusion of the CRR module yields an additional 3–4% gain over the HAN baseline, strengthening the effectiveness of the context-aware and alignment-driven modeling for aspect term extraction (ATE) from M-S reviews.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-52992-9.Keywords: Aspect-based sentiment analysis, Hierarchical attention, Co-reference resolution, Sustainable learning, Sustainable development, Aspect term extractionSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The rapid growth of e-commerce platforms and online services has led to a substantial increase in user-generated reviews on the internet1,2. These reviews contain valuable opinions about various aspects of the product and services, making them an important source for understanding customer preferences and experiences. Consequently, Aspect-Based Sentiment Analysis (ABSA) has emerged as an important technique for identifying specific aspects mentioned in reviews and analyzing the sentiment associated with them3,4. Consider the review “The food is delicious, but the ambiance is uncomfortable” as shown in Fig. 1. The customer expresses confidence in the quality of the meal because the ASPect CATegory (ASP-CAT) is “FOOD#QUALITY” but disapproves of the ambiance using the “SERVICE#GENERAL” as ASP-CAT. So, the ATE identifies explicit single-word and M-W Aspect Terms (AT) in the sentence that are “food” and “ambiance”. In contrast, ACD maps each extracted and implied aspect to a predefined category, such as “FOOD#QUALITY” and “SERVICE#GENERAL”. Here, categories do not need to appear explicitly in the sentence. They represent semantic groupings defined by the task or domain, such as Restaurant domain reviews5.

Need for sentence alignment

In real-world opinion mining scenarios, user reviews frequently consist of multiple sentences, where ATs are often introduced in one sentence and further described, contrasted, or evaluated in subsequent sentences. Despite notable advancements in ABSA, several challenges remain unresolved, particularly in the context of M-S review and context-dependent reviews. Existing sentence-level and HAN-based approaches primarily process sentences independently or aggregate them sequentially, which can lead to context fragmentation and misalignment of aspect-related information. Moreover, the extraction of M-W and domain-specific aspects remains a persistent challenge due to the insufficient modeling of contextual interactions. Sentence-alignment strategies and their integration with the HAN mechanism have received limited attention in prior research. Furthermore, the role of CRR in supervised ATE has not been systematically investigated. To address these limitations, a sentence-alignment mechanism is introduced using CRR that explicitly captures cross-sentence semantic relationships, enabling more accurate ATE in the M-S reviews.

The sentiment of the most recent review was evaluated by analyzing the association between individual sentences and the sentiment expressed in adjacent sentences. It is crucial to address CRR to gain an insightful analysis in ABSA. The proposed model executes this process in two steps. First, it incorporates CRR for review, which involves aligning sentences to provide context that helps clarify any ambiguity in AET. CRR connects pronouns and refers to the specific entities or aspects mentioned in the text. It identifies M-W aspects like “battery backup”, “customer service”, or “screen resolution”. In this context, CRR ensures that references to various aspects are accurately interpreted. M-W aspects are typically introduced once and subsequently referenced using pronouns or general nouns. So, CRR allows the system to recognize that “it” in the sentence “It lasts all day” refers to “battery life” and improves aspect detection continuity. The proposed model establishes connections among various elements to enhance the user’s understanding of reviews. It is trained on labeled data using Begin-Inside-Outside (BIO) encoding, which improves aspect word prediction13. Its performance is evaluated on a benchmark dataset55 (described in Sect. "Dataset description"), showing that the addition of contextual information reduces uncertainty caused by insufficient data, thereby enhancing the ASP-CAT and AET. Novelty of the proposed work

Unlike existing HAN-based ABSA approaches, which primarily operate at the sentence-level or rely solely on syntactic graph representations, the proposed HAN-based method introduces a sentence-aligned, supervised HAN framework that jointly models words, sentences, and review-level contextual semantics. The novelty of the work lies in the integration of sentence-alignment using CRR as a preprocessing step for AET, which enables consistent semantic mapping of ATs across M-S reviews. This aspect has been largely overlooked in prior studies. Furthermore, the proposed framework systematically incorporates and evaluates CRR within an HAN architecture, demonstrating its impact on supervised ATE. This combination of alignment-driven preprocessing, hierarchical contextual modeling, and co-reference-aware analysis distinguishes the proposed approach from existing attention-based, GCN-based, and hybris methods. From a sustainable perspective, the proposed CRR-based framework promotes model reuse across both domains by effectively leveraging contextual relationships, thereby reducing the need for extensive retraining and the scalability of annotated data.

Related work

ABSA has been extensively studied over the past decade with particular emphasis on ATE and ACD as fundamental subtasks2,15,61. Early approaches primarily relied on rule-based techniques, frequency statistics, and topic modeling to identify ATs from review texts16. The only aspect phrases that could be extracted using these rule-based methods are explicit ones. While ASP-CAT recognition addresses implicit and explicit aspects, the ATE task concentrates on explicit aspects. However, these methods were limited in handling linguistic variability, contextual ambiguity, and M-W aspect expressions. Next, CRR between ATs is not extracted using rule-based approaches17,18. Therefore, rule-based approaches disregard contextual information while identifying aspect categories from the reviews.

Subsequent research introduced using supervised learning techniques and DL models has improved performance19,20,52; however, since a review having numerous phrases must be evaluated, they produce subpar aspect extraction results22. Through the capturing of the review’s deeper significance, the DL-based approaches presented in21 have enhanced ATE performance. A convolutional network introduced to unexplored languages significantly improved the performance of aspect detection. The accuracy of identifying aspect categories has been greatly enhanced by incorporating POS and WE into the network model14,23. This approach utilized a hybrid strategy to extract the most salient contextual features, extending aspect extraction capabilities12. A DL network for ACD is trained using a sentence vector representation approach for word vectors14. Over the past decade, multiple LSTM-based methods27,28 have been proposed for ATE, particularly Laptop and Restaurant samples of the SemEval-16 reviews29–35. LSTM networks are widely used in ABSA as they effectively capture long-range dependencies and contextual information in sequential text data. Recurrent architectures, such as Bi-LSTM and GRU, utilize contextual dependencies among sentences by modeling sequential information57,58. However, LSTMs face limitations, such as challenges in capturing global text context, a lack of parallelization, difficulty handling multiple aspects simultaneously, and sensitivity to input length. Bi-LSTM with attention suffers from the exponential memory delay and discrete attention weighting, which limits its ability to capture long-range and cross-sentence semantic dependencies, leading to over-focusing on high-frequency sentiment cues while missing implicit or low-occurrence aspects27. These approaches leverage sequence labeling and contextual representation to capture local semantic dependencies within sentences. However, the majority of existing models operate at a single-sentence level, implicitly assuming that aspect evaluations are independent across sentences, thereby overlooking inter-sentence contextual dependencies present in real-world reviews.

Recent studies have increasingly adopted transformer-based architectures for ABSA due to their ability to capture long-range contextual dependencies. This section discusses the recent models, specifically published in 2025, based on BERT, RoBERTa, and DeBERTa, which have demonstrated strong performance across multiple ABSA benchmarks. These models utilize transformer encoders to obtain contextual word representations, which enhance aspect detection and sentiment classification61. More recent studies have explored BERT-based2,10,43, hierarchical22,34,61, and attention-based mechanisms32,40 to model sentence-level importance and contextual relevance.

A hybrid ABSA model, SVCCL-GCN50, is a strong recent baseline because it combines BERT + Bi-LSTM to obtain contextual embedding, GCN built on syntactic dependency trees to capture relationships between words, a self-attention based variational encoder to capture global and local semantic information, and a capsule network with contrastive learning to improve feature discrimination between sentiment classes. This architecture enables the model to capture long-distance dependencies and both syntactic and semantic information for better AT extraction73,75. 58 proposed an adaptive contextual memory graph transformer-based contextual embedding with a domain-adaptive knowledge graph. This model utilizes graph transformer layers with memory modules to capture semantic relations between aspects and opinion words, thereby enhancing contextual interaction modeling for ABSA2,45,57. Although these models enhance representation learning, they largely focus on document-level sentiment aggregation rather than explicit aspect alignment across sentences or reviews24–26,51. BERT and its variants introduce contextual embeddings, address these limitations by generating dynamic word representations conditioned on surrounding context, and improve token-level semantics59,60,63. Despite these improvements, transformer-based models primarily focus on linear contextual dependencies and do not explicitly model syntactic and semantic relational structures among words across multiple sentences64. 65,74 proposed an LLM-augmented syntax-aware domain adaptation framework for aspect extraction that integrates contextual knowledge from large language models with dependency-based syntactic features. The model utilizes domain adaptation mechanisms to align representations across domains, thereby enabling improved aspect extraction in cross-domain settings.72 proposes a self-attention-driven transformer-based sentiment classification with speaker intent (TASCI) framework that integrates aspect prediction with intent understanding61,62.

In recent years, to better exploit syntactic and relational information, graph-based neural networks, particularly Graph Convolutional Networks (GCN), have been incorporated into ABSA frameworks. These approaches model sentences as dependency graphs, enabling the capture of long-range syntactic dependencies between words and aspects61,66,67. GCN-based models effectively capture non-local syntactic relations and structural dependencies, leading to improved aspect boundary detection68. The combination of BERT with GCN further enhanced performance by jointly modeling contextual semantics and structural information69. GCNs are robust to sentence complexity and naturally support cross-sentence aspect referencing, enabling improved contextual understanding39. GCN models that leverage BERT along with many weight mechanisms36–38, BERT-based maximal activated weighted memory39, and components focusing on multi-head co-attention40, have significantly benefited ABSA. These methods facilitate the extraction of four aspects using a two-stage neural network41,44,53,54. GCN-based models have demonstrated improvements in ATE by leveraging structural linguistics information; however, they typically rely on sentence-level dependency parsing and remain limited in modeling cross-sentence semantic alignment and review-level contextual dependencies61.

In parallel, unsupervised and hybrid approaches have been proposed to reduce dependency on labeled data by exploiting dependency relations, topic coherence, and graph-based representations. While such methods demonstrate reasonable performance, they face limited generalization and reduced accuracy when handling complete contextual interactions. Additionally, sentence-alignment and preprocessing strategies aimed at eliminating contextual discontinuities are seldom integrated with GCN-based or attention-driven ABSA pipelines61,70,71. Problem statement and contributions

Existing aspect extraction approaches, such as Bi-LSTM with attention and standard HAN, primarily operate at the sentence-level or perform sequential aggregation of sentence representations. As a result, they struggle to accurately extract ATs from M-S reviews, where aspect information is often distributed across multiple sentences and requires alignment of semantically related contexts. The core problem addressed in this work is how to effectively model and align cross-sentence dependencies to improve aspect term detection in M-S reviews. To the best of our knowledge, this is one of the first studies to explicitly integrate sentence-alignment and CRR within a supervised HAN for ATE. Motivated by the gaps identified in prior studies, this work aims to advance ATE in ABSA through the following objectives and contributions.

The proposed approach of aspect terms extraction

This section introduces the proposed HAN model, which extends conventional sentence-level and HAN approaches by explicitly modeling cross-sentence dependencies. Unlike Bi-LSTM with attention, which applies attention over a single sentence sequence, the proposed HAN-based deep network explicitly captures inter-sentence dependencies through sentence-alignment. In contrast to standard HAN, which hierarchically aggregates sentence representations with explicit semantic alignment, our approach introduces a sentence-alignment mechanism. This design enables iterative refinement of aspect representation using aligned cross-sentence context, making the model particularly effective for M-S aspect extraction. The system develops contextual embeddings using BERT to represent the semantics of words across various task domains. Extracting domain-relevant ATs requires an understanding of the overall context of the reviews. Sentence alignment using CRR for ATE and ACD

Although CRR is emphasized in many NLP-related domains, it has not been extensively explored for ATE and ACD in ABSA. For a better understanding, placeholder symbols like Noun Phrase as (NP) or a set of Noun Phrases as (NPs) are defined and replaced. CRR identifies all nouns and NPs related to the same entity within the textual review. It captures the context of the entire review to enable accurate aspect and category detection.

In the proposed framework, co-reference resolution is applied during the preprocessing stage to resolve pronouns and referring expressions across sentences. The pre-trained language model en_core_web_sm-2.3 is used for tokenization, part-of-speech tagging, and dependency parsing. The CRR process is implemented using the neural coreference resolution module available in the spaCy NLP library (version 2.3.5) with the NeuralCoref (version 4.0) extension, which identifies mentions referring to the same entity and establishes mappings between pronouns and their corresponding antecedents based on coreference clusters generated by NeuralCoref. The NeuralCoref module is integrated into the spaCy pipeline using its default configuration for CRR. The proposed framework relies on the default predictions generated by the NeuralCoref module. To ensure consistency with the pre-trained model and avoid introducing additional hyperparameters, no additional filtering or explicit confidence threshold is applied to the resolved mentions. Applying a strict confidence threshold may discard moderately scored yet contextually relevant coreference links, potentially affecting the quality of downstream representation. Every cluster maintains all the mentions referring to the same aspects. In cases where multiple antecedents exist for a pronoun, the NeuralCoref module resolves the ambiguity (no manual disambiguation performed in the proposed model) using its internal scoring mechanism based on contextual and syntactic features. Specifically, the model assigns a compatibility score to each candidate antecedent within the cluster and selects the antecedent with the highest score for resolution. This best-fit selection strategy ensures a single coherent linkage, but may overlook secondary plausible antecedents in complex contexts. The coreference model performed as-is without any fine-tuning on the domain-specific dataset. This step generates semantically aligned sentences that are subsequently provided as input to the HAN for aspect term extraction. However, general-purpose coreference models may not fully capture domain-specific linguistic patterns such as implicit references or context-dependent entity relations, which may affect resolution quality in specialized review datasets. Incorporating domain-adaptive fine-tuning could further improve performance and is considered future work.

Evaluation metrics

To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed ATE framework, the PRE, REC, and F-score metrics are employed for sequence labeling and ATE. PRE measures the proportion of the correctly identified ATs among all predicted ATs. Besides, REC quantifies the proportion of the ground-truth ATs that are correctly detected, which is critical in M-S reviews where aspects may be sparsely distributed. The F-score, defined as the harmonic mean of PRE and REC, provides a balanced measure of overall performance, making it particularly suitable for datasets with class imbalance between aspect and non-aspect tokens. Accuracy is not reported as it is less informative for sequence labeling tasks with highly imbalanced class distributions. It reports micro-averaged PRE, REC, and F-score, following the standard evaluation protocol of SemEval-16. The micro-average F-score is particularly suitable for SemEval-16, as it accounts for class imbalance by weighting each instance equally rather than each class, thereby providing a more reliable measure of overall extraction performance. Moreover, ATE requires exact boundary matching, especially for the M-W aspect expressions; therefore, under the SemEval-16 Task 5 evaluation protocol (explained in Sect. “Dataset Description”), the extracted ATs are considered correct only if their boundary exactly matches the annotated gold standard. These metrics are widely adopted in prior ABSA studies, which compare them with state-of-the-art approaches and assess the improvements introduced by sentence-alignment and hierarchical contextual modeling.

Qualitative error analysis and common failure modes

Despite achieving strong quantitative performance on the SemEval-16 Restaurant and Laptop datasets (explained in Sect. “Dataset description”), the proposed model still exhibits certain limitations. To provide deeper insights beyond aggregate metrics, it presents representative failure cases for both domains.

Cases on Restaurant Domain: Implicit ATs: “The place is always crowded, but I still keep coming back.” For this review, the Gold aspect is “ambience,” but the model failed to predict it. Here, the aspect is implicitly expressed without an explicit noun phrase. Although CRR helps capture contextual relationships across sentences, implicit aspects remain challenging due to the lack of explicit lexical cues. Multiple Aspects with Contrast: “The food was excellent, but the service was painful.” For this review, the Gold aspects are “food” and “service,” but the model only predicted “food” and failed to predict “service” as an aspect term. Here, when multiple aspects occur in a single sentence with contrasting sentiment, the model may prioritize the dominant opinion-bearing aspect, leading to partial extraction.

Cases on Laptop Domain: Cross-Sentence Aspect Reference: “I brought this laptop for work. The battery barely lasts three hours.” For this review, the Gold aspect is “battery,” but the model failed to predict it correctly; it predicts “power” as ATs. Here, although CRR improves inter-sentence modeling, indirect references and paraphrased aspect mentions can still result in coarse-grained or partially incorrect predictions. Aspect Boundary Ambiguity: “The keyboard layout and key travel feel comfortable.” For this review, the Gold aspects are “keyboard layout” and “key travel,” but the model only predicted “keyboard” and failed to predict the correct aspect term. Here, closely related ATs are sometimes merged into a single generalized aspect, especially when they share overlapping syntactic and semantic structures.

These failure cases indicate that while CRR enhances contextual awareness and feature interactions, challenges remain in handling implicit aspects, fine-grained aspect boundaries, and contrastive structures. Addressing these limitations may require incorporating external knowledge or discourse-aware representations in future work. The failure cases discussed here are consistent with the dataset characteristics reported in Table 8, particularly the high frequency of multi-aspect, cross-sentence aspect reference and implicit ATs.

Applications of ABSA

Since the advent of online social platforms, consumer-generated data on the internet has become highly valuable. ABSA has become essential for companies in the digital era, enabling them to observe consumer behavior, understand public sentiment, and evaluate feedback to elevate consumer expectations regarding brands and services6. Over the past 20 years, ABSA has been applied in various fields, including commodity trading forecasting, product reviews, government issues, education, regulatory compliance, and multimedia content platforms such as YouTube.

Public opinions on popular social networking sites provide rich resources for ABSA analysis. In one study33, a vector space model was applied, which required an aspect-polarity word vector to analyze recent news and events for intraday stock forecasting. This method linked intraday returns to specific headlines. Additionally, DL techniques can classify news articles into three categories for aspect extraction: word, category, and sentiment. Correlating business information from public reviews for the extraction of significant elements by establishing elaboration and background dependencies for stock recommendation decisions. Traditionally, news reports have been viewed as the best sources of information. Since the pharmaceutical industry is sensitive to news and media commentary, ABSA can be effectively utilized to understand, identify, and extract the impacts of public sentiment across various media aspects in the industry.

One promising application of ABSA is in stock price forecasting. The primary goal of stock market forecasting is to predict when the value of a stock or the dividend from an investment will rise. Stock market values naturally fluctuate due to numerous factors, including politics, economic conditions, social trends, epidemics, and natural disasters29. Additionally, news stories and business releases directly affect a company’s stock value and the overall trading market. Social media platforms, where users express and discuss their opinions and feelings about financial institutions, generate significant amounts of commercial text related to trading15. ABSA helps forecast prices by assessing public sentiment on specific topics that influence stock values.

Research30 has explored the relationship between public sentiment and market forecasting. For example, some studies31 have combined sentiment analysis from Twitter with financial data to improve predictions about trading markets. Others32 have used crowdsourcing techniques to enhance market forecasts. The impact of social media on stock price predictions has been recognized, noting that social media often has a closer connection to stock market performance than traditional media.

In recent years, YouTube has become one of the most popular platforms for learning about a variety of topics, including investigations, e-learning, the voice industry, education, food, games, and more3,34,35. The platform includes a dedicated section where users can share comments on the videos they watch. The volume of user comments can significantly influence the relevance, quality, and sorting of social media. Unstudied user opinions have attracted the interest of researchers focused on sentiment analysis. By examining multiple aspects of a video’s subject through user comments, ABSA can evaluate its relevance and effectiveness for ranking YouTube videos36.

Governments often introduce new programs to promote economic development, enhance human well-being, and address other concerns with sustainable development and learning. The effectiveness of these public initiatives in enhancing the quality of life for citizens is crucial for gauging their success. ABSA could serve as a valuable tool for evaluating citizen reviews and feedback, helping to gauge public sentiment. Public opinions on various policy areas can inform updates and improvements to governmental policies. Moreover, outcome-based teaching has encouraged academics and educators to enhance the educational environment by evaluating faculty performance, training, and learning through participants’ feedback. Analyzing these inputs and stakeholder feedback can provide insights into various education-related topics, including placements, research, academics, and personality development9. Despite its potential for capturing feedback on diverse topics from user-generated reviews, research indicates that ABSA is underutilized in the context of academic improvement3,37.

Case study: discussion on precision, recall, and F-score

This section explains how the PRE, REC, and F-score values are calculated for the proposed model. Tables 7, 8 and 9 provide insight into the dataset’s statistics. The data indicate that reviews utilizing nouns as aspects tend to rank higher than those that utilize NPs. There is also an imbalance in aspect reviews, aspect categorization, and other classifications. Consequently, PRE, REC, and F-score were selected as performance metrics for the HAN model. The primary goal is to increase the True Positive (TP) value while reducing the False Positive (FP) and False Negative (FN) values. A higher TP indicates that more single-word and M-W aspects are correctly identified; conversely, FP represents incorrect words or phrases identified as aspects, and FN signifies important missing characteristics in the retrieved aspects.

This section focuses solely on performance metrics, as Sect. “The proposed approach of aspect terms extraction” details the specifics and workings of each phase. Initially, the numbers are evaluated: TP (275), FP (165), and FN (132) when NP chunks from the review sentences undergo a new logistic regression method for potential aspect extraction. These values indicate that 275 viable aspects were accurately extracted, but 165 nouns and NPs were incorrectly identified as valid aspects. Additionally, 132 legitimate aspects were not extracted. This means that while invalid aspects were extracted, some valid aspects were overlooked, evidenced by the higher FP and FN values, respectively. Incorporating CRR with LR resulted in improved values: FP (133) and FN (125) while increasing TP (303). Furthermore, using Bi-LSTM with CRR, TP rose to 338 while FP decreased to 85 and FN to 89. Finally, the proposed model improved the TP to 342, reducing FP to 75 and FN to 80. These results highlight the significance of each stage in the proposed hybrid unsupervised approach. Conclusions and future recommendations

The proposed model introduces several novel contributions that distinguish it from existing ABSA research. The proposed model remarkably improved AT detection in ABSA by incorporating CRR for sentence alignment. This step removes partial understanding, establishes proper aspect alignment across multiple sentence reviews, and enhances context-aware aspect detection. The approach successfully represents long-term dependencies among precise features in M-S reviews and detects contextual understanding from those dependencies. Once incorporating word embeddings with linguistic features, contextualized representations, HAN, and CRR, the proposed model constructively detects both local and global contextual cues from M-S reviews. Evaluations conducted on SemEval-16 Restaurant and Laptop datasets show that the proposed approach improved baselines and state-of-the-art methods in terms of PRE, REC, and F-score. Extensive feature-wise and ablation analysis authenticate that the POS features combined with CRR improve extraction efficiency, specifically in REC, and underline the significance of inter-sentence contextual modeling. Qualitative error analysis, backed by dataset statistics, shows that the proposed technique is robust over varying review constructions; however tackles problems while dealing with inbuilt aspects, and multi-sentence reviews.

In terms of computational complexity, the model presents subsidiary processing because of hierarchical attention and sequence modeling layers; however, the training remains feasible on standard-GPU-enabled systems. In spite of optimistic outcomes, the work introduces restrictions, like dependence on annotated datasets and increased computational demands for large-scale corpora. Although the proposed model improves results in aspect detection, the CRR step might generate errors if cross-references are inaccurately linked. Consequently, various future research directions open, comprising domains namely healthcare, finance, education, and e-commerce to evaluate generalizability by integrating multilingual capabilities with pre-trained language models. Integrating transformer architectures will strengthen contextual embedding by employing bidirectional attention mechanisms. The proposed model can be expanded to address the above limitations and enhance accuracy, efficiency, and applicability in various real-world problems. Future research can focus on improving computational efficiency and exploring lightweight architectures. This will extend the model to multilingual datasets, which focus on addressing cross-domain variability, such as training on one domain and testing on another, by enhancing the proposed framework’s ability to generalize across heterogeneous domains with minimal domain-specific adaptation.

Overall, the proposed model presents an effective and scalable solution for aspect extraction in real-world review analysis, providing a strong balance between contextual richness and model interpretability. By improving fine-grained analysis from large-scale user reviews, the proposed approach supports the sustainable development of intelligent decision support systems in domains such as e-commerce and service analytics.

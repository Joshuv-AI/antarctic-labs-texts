Codes with restricted overlaps: expandability, constructions, and bounds
Lidija Stanovnik

Abstract
Consider a q q -ary block code satisfying the property that no l l -letters long codeword’s prefix occurs as a suffix of any codeword for l l inside some interval. We determine a general upper bound on the maximum size of these codes and a tighter bound for codes where overlaps with lengths not exceeding k k are prohibited. We then provide constructions for codes with various restrictions on overlap lengths and use them to determine lower bounds on the maximum sizes. In particular, we construct ( 1 , k ) (1,k) -overlap-free codes where k ≥ n / 2 k\geq n/2 and n n denotes the block size, expand a known construction of ( k , n − 1 ) (k,n-1) -overlap-free codes, and combine the ideas behind both constructions to obtain ( t 1 , t 2 ) (t_{1},t_{2}) -overlap-free codes and codes that are simultaneously ( 1 , k ) (1,k) - and ( n − k , n − 1 ) (n-k,n-1) -overlap-free for some k < n / 2 k<n/2 . In the case when overlaps of lengths between 1 and k k are prohibited, we complete the characterisation of non-expandable codes started by Cai, Wang, and Feng (2023) [ 1 ] .

Introduction

Two not necessarily distinct words

and
v
over an alphabet

-overlap

These codes may be useful in channels where bursts of at least

2n

symbols are read. Some parameter values may even perform better.

So far, only special cases of

-overlap-free codes have been studied. Non-overlapping codes, i.e., the

-overlap-free codes, were established for synchronisation sixty years ago
[
3
]
.
At the beginning of this millennium,

In 2015, the term

,
8

Recently, Blackburn et al., who defined the

-overlap-free codes that have an advantage over the class of

-overlap-free codes and insertion-detection capabilities from

[

Let

Upper bounds

In this section, we determine new upper bounds on the size of
(t_{1},t_{2})
-overlap-free codes.
We first observe that the arguments from
[
1
]
on maximum
(1,k)
-overlap-free codes for

hold also for
(t_{1},t_{2})
-overlap-free codes with
t_{2}<n/2
. In particular, every
(t_{1},t_{2})
-overlap-free code

of length

can be extended into a
(t_{1},t_{2})
-overlap-free code
X=\{x_{1}\cdots x_{t_{2}}\Sigma^{n-2t_{2}}x_{t_{2}+1}\cdots x_{2t_{2}}\mid x_{1}\cdots x_{2t_{2}}\in C\}
of length

, and removing the letters at positions

to

from every word in a
(t_{1},t_{2})
-overlap-free code of length

yields a
(t_{1},t_{2})
-overlap-free code of length

. Hence, the following equality holds.

Proposition 3
.

If

,

\displaystyle S_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(q,n)=q^{n-2t_{2}}S_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(q,2t_{2}).

Now, let us determine an upper bound for the maximum size of the
(t_{1},t_{2})
-overlap-free codes satisfying

. The idea behind its proof comes from the upper bound on the size of non-overlapping codes given in

Proposition 4
.

\displaystyle S_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(q,n)<\frac{q^{n}}{\Bigl\lfloor\frac{2n-t_{1}}{n-t_{2}}\Bigr\rfloor}.

Proof.

Let a code
C
be
(t_{1},t_{2})
-overlap-free. Construct a set
X
consisting of all the words
x_{1}\cdots x_{2n-t_{1}}
from
\Sigma^{2n-t_{1}}
for which there exists a non-negative integer
k
such that

x_{k(n-t_{2})+1}\cdots x_{k(n-t_{2})+n}
is a word in
C
.
We will show that for every word in
X
, there is exactly one such

. Since there are
\Bigl\lfloor\frac{2n-t_{1}}{n-t_{2}}\Bigr\rfloor
possible values of

, this will imply

\displaystyle\lvert X\rvert=\lvert C\rvert\biggl\lfloor\frac{2n-t_{1}}{n-t_{2}}\biggr\rfloor q^{n-t_{1}}.

Assume that for some word
x_{1}\cdots x_{2n-t_{1}}\in X
there exist two (not necessarily distinct) words

and
v
from
C
that start at distinct positions of

. Denote these positions with
k_{1}(n-t_{2})+1
and
k_{2}(n-t_{2})+1
. Without loss of generality, we can assume

. Since the length of

is shorter than
2n
, an overlap exists between

and

. Its length equals
l=n-(k_{2}-k_{1})(n-t_{2})
.
We need to explain that
t_{1}\leq l\leq t_{2}
, contradicting the fact that
C
is
(t_{1},t_{2})
-overlap-free.
If
l
was larger than
t_{2}
, it would also hold that

\displaystyle n-(k_{2}-k_{1})(n-t_{2})

\displaystyle>t_{2}

\displaystyle t_{2}(k_{2}-k_{1}-1)

\displaystyle>n(k_{2}-k_{1}-1)

\displaystyle t_{2}

\displaystyle>n,

which, by definition, is not true. If, on the other hand,

was shorter than

, the length of

should be longer than

, which again cannot hold. To complete the proof, we only need an upper bound on the size of

∎

Providing a tighter bound for
(1,k)
-overlap-free codes for some

is easy. In particular, any
(1,k)
-overlap-free code is also

-overlap-free, so

\displaystyle S_{1}^{k}(q,n)\leq S_{1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(q,n)\leq\frac{q^{n}-q^{\lceil n/2\rceil}}{2\lfloor n/2\rfloor},

Proposition 5
.

If

,

S_{1}^{k}(q,n)\leq\sum_{d\mid n}\frac{\mu(d)}{n}q^{n/d}
.

Proof.

As explained in
[
1
]
, if

is a
(1,k)
-overlap-free code for some

, all words and cyclic shifts of words in

are primitive. More specifically, if a cyclic shift of

had a period

, i.e.,

, there would exist

such that
w_{1}=v_{m}
. Then

\displaystyle w_{1}\cdots w_{d}=v_{m}\cdots v_{d}v_{1}\cdots v_{m-1}=w_{n-d+1}\cdots w_{n},

so

would have a

-overlap with itself.
Since a necessary condition for

,

, and this contradicts the fact that

is
(1,k)
-overlap-free.
We will now explain that all the cyclic shifts are distinct when

.
Suppose that in
C
, there exist words

and
v
, such that the shift of

for
i

\displaystyle u_{i+1}\cdots u_{n}u_{1}\cdots u_{i}=v_{j+1}\cdots v_{n}v_{1}\cdots v_{j}.

Without loss of generality we can assume

. If

, and the equality has to hold. If

, we can distinguish two cases, depending on the value of

-overlap-free.
If

.

.

\displaystyle\lvert C\rvert

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-k-1}\sum_{l=0}^{n-k-1-j}\sum_{\alpha}\lvert L_{1}\rvert^{l+1}\lvert R_{1}\rvert^{n-k-1-j-l+k+j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-k-1}\sum_{l=0}^{n-k-1-j}\sum_{\alpha}(q-x)^{l+1}x^{n-1-l}.

For
j=n-k-1
there is one composition of
n-k-1-j
into zero parts and no compositions into
l>0
parts.
For
j<n-k-1
there are no compositions of
n-k-1-j
into zero parts, and the number of compositions into
l>0
parts equals
\binom{n-k-j-2}{l-1}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\lvert C\rvert

\displaystyle=(q-x)x^{n-1}+\sum_{j=0}^{n-k-2}\sum_{l=1}^{n-k-1-j}\binom{n-k-j-2}{l-1}(q-x)^{l+1}x^{n-1-l},

and after performing additional calculations one obtains the simplified formula for
\lvert C\rvert
.

\displaystyle\lvert C\rvert

\displaystyle=(q-x)x^{n-1}+(q-x)^{2}x^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k-2}x^{j}(q-x+x)^{n-k-2-j}

\displaystyle=(q-x)x^{n-1}+(q-x)^{2}x^{k}q^{n-k-2}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k-2}\left(\frac{x}{q}\right)^{j}

\displaystyle=(q-x)x^{n-1}+(q-x)^{2}x^{k}q^{n-k-2}\cdot\frac{1-\left(\frac{x}{q}\right)^{n-k-1}}{1-\frac{x}{q}}

\displaystyle=(q-x)x^{n-1}+(q-x)x^{k}(q^{n-k-1}-x^{n-k-1})

\displaystyle=(q-x)x^{k}q^{n-k-1}.

We are now left to find the integer value
x\in(0,q)
that produces the largest code
C
. We first analyse
f(x)=(q-x)x^{k}q^{n-k-1}
on the closed interval
\left[0,q\right]
. Since
f
is differentiable,
f(0)=f(q)=0
and
f(x)>0
for
x\in(0,q)
, it follows from Fermat’s theorem that the global maxima of
f
lies at a stationary point in the interior of the interval. Its derivative

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(x)=q^{n-k-1}x^{k-1}(qk-(k+1)x),

has exactly one stationary point on the open interval
(0,q)
, the point
x=\frac{k}{k+1}\cdot q
in which
f
achieves its maximum.
Therefore,
f
restricted to integers attain its maximum either at
\bigl\lfloor\frac{k}{k+1}\cdot q\bigr\rfloor
or
\bigl\lceil\frac{k}{k+1}\cdot q\bigr\rceil
. In the bound, we round the value down since
1\leq\bigl\lfloor\frac{k}{k+1}\cdot q\bigr\rfloor\leq q-1
, while for
q<k+1
,
\bigl\lceil\frac{k}{k+1}\cdot q\bigr\rceil>q-1
giving an invalid partition for our construction.

We are left to cover the case
k<n/2
. We know from Eq. (
4
) that

\displaystyle S_{1}^{k}(q,n)

\displaystyle=q^{n-2k}S_{1}^{k}(q,2k)

\displaystyle\geq q^{n-2k+2k-k-1}\left(q-\biggl\lfloor\frac{k}{k+1}\cdot q\biggr\rfloor\right)\biggl\lfloor\frac{k}{k+1}\cdot q\biggr\rfloor^{k}

\displaystyle=q^{n-k-1}\left(q-\biggl\lfloor\frac{k}{k+1}\cdot q\biggr\rfloor\right)\biggl\lfloor\frac{k}{k+1}\cdot q\biggr\rfloor^{k}.

This shows that the proposition holds for all values of
1\leq k<n
.
∎

We want to remark that for
k=2
the bound in Proposition
9
is the same as the exact value given in Eq. (
5
). Moreover, when
k+1
divides
q
, the lower bound equals
\left(\frac{k}{k+1}\right)^{k}\frac{q^{n}}{k+1}
and we will show that the upper bound on
S_{1}^{k}(q,n)
from Section
3
is tight to within a constant factor.

Proposition 10
.

If
k+1
divides
q

\displaystyle S_{1}^{k}(q,n)\geq\frac{q^{n}}{en},

where
e
is the base of the natural logarithm.

Proof.

By Proposition
9
and since
k+1\leq n
, we obtain

\displaystyle S_{1}^{k}(q,n)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{k}{k+1}\right)^{k}\frac{q^{n}}{k+1}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{k+1}{k}\right)^{-k}\frac{q^{n}}{n}.

From the fact that
\left(\frac{k+1}{k}\right)^{k}=\left(1+\frac{1}{k}\right)^{k}<e
for every positive
k
, we know that
\left(\frac{k+1}{k}\right)^{-k}>e^{-1}
. The bound follows.
∎

Even when
k+1
does not divide
q
, but
q
is large, the lower bound we determined is close to a constant factor of the upper bound, in the sense that

\displaystyle S_{1}^{k}(q,n)\geq\frac{q^{n}}{en}-O(q^{n-1}).

However, for a small value of
q
, the bound we determine can be much lower than the known upper bound. If
q<k+1\leq n
,
\frac{k}{k+1}q>q-1
, and hence the formula on the right-hand side in Proposition
9
achieves a value zero if
\frac{k}{k+1}q
is rounded to
q
, and a non-zero value if we round
\frac{k}{k+1}q
to
q-1
. Thus,

\displaystyle S_{1}^{k}(q,n)

\displaystyle\geq(q-1)^{k}q^{n-k-1}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{q-1}{q}\right)^{k}\frac{q^{n}}{q}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{q-1}{q}\right)^{k}\frac{q^{n}}{n}.

In the case
k=n-1
, we know that the lower bound we determined in Proposition
9
is also the upper bound on the size of a
(1,n-1)
-overlap-free code given in Eq. (
1
). It would be interesting to know whether this result can be generalised, i.e., whether, for all values of
k
, it holds that
S_{1}^{k}(q,n)\leq\left(\frac{k}{k+1}\right)^{k}\frac{q^{n}}{k+1}
. We remark that this conjecture cannot be proven by upper-bounding
S_{1}^{k}(q,n)
by
S_{1}^{k}(q,k+1)q^{n-k-1}
and applying Eq. (
1
). More specifically, for
k\in\{n-2,n-3\}
, we verify in Proposition
11
that
S_{1}^{k}(q,n)\geq S_{1}^{k}(q,k+1)q^{n-k-1}
, and for some values given in Tables
1
and
2
a strict inequality holds. They are written in bold. Still, the computed values are all smaller than
\left(\frac{k}{k+1}\right)^{k}\frac{q^{n}}{k+1}
.

Proposition 11
.

Let
q\geq 2
. For all integers
n\geq 4
,

\displaystyle S_{1}^{n-2}(q,n)

\displaystyle\geq q\cdot S_{1}^{n-2}(q,n-1).

Moreover, for all integers
n\geq 6

\displaystyle S_{1}^{n-3}(q,n)

\displaystyle\geq q^{2}\cdot S_{1}^{n-3}(q,n-2).

Proof.

Let
C
be a maximum non-overlapping code of length
k+1
. Since every maximum non-overlapping code is also maximal,
C
can be obtained from Construction
1
using a collection of pairs of disjoint sets
(L_{i},R_{i})_{i\in\left[k\right]}
.

\displaystyle C=\bigcup_{i\in\left[k\right]}L_{i}R_{k+1-i}.

If
k\geq 2
, the same collection of pairs of disjoint sets can be used to generate a
(1,k)
-overlap-free code
D_{1}
of length
k+2
with Construction
2
,

\displaystyle D_{1}=\bigcup_{i\in\left[k\right]}L_{1}L_{i}R_{k+1-i}\cup\bigcup_{i\in\left[k\right]}L_{i}R_{k+1-i}R_{1}\cup\bigcup_{i\in\left[2,k\right]}L_{i}R_{k+2-i}.

The size of
D_{1}
equals

\displaystyle\lvert D_{1}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert L_{1}\rvert\sum_{i\in\left[k\right]}\lvert L_{i}\rvert\lvert R_{k+1-i}\rvert+\lvert R_{1}\rvert\sum_{i\in\left[k\right]}\lvert L_{i}\rvert\lvert R_{k+1-i}\rvert+\sum_{i\in\left[2,k\right]}\lvert L_{i}\rvert\lvert R_{k+2-i}\rvert

\displaystyle=\left(\lvert L_{1}\rvert+\lvert R_{1}\rvert\right)\lvert C\rvert+\sum_{i\in\left[2,k\right]}\lvert L_{i}\rvert\lvert R_{k+2-i}\rvert

\displaystyle=q\,\lvert C\rvert+\sum_{i\in\left[2,k\right]}\lvert L_{i}\rvert\lvert R_{k+2-i}\rvert

\displaystyle\geq q\,\lvert C\rvert=qS_{1}^{k}(k+1,q).

The size of the maximum
(1,k+2)
-overlap-free code is at least the size of
D_{1}
, proving the first claim of our proposition.

We proceed with the second claim. If
k\geq 3
, the collection of pairs of disjoint sets
(L_{i},R_{i})_{i\in\left[k\right]}
can be used to construct a
(1,k)
-overlap-free code
D_{2}
of length
k+3
with Construction
2
,

\displaystyle D_{2}=

\displaystyle\bigcup_{i\in\left[k\right]}(L_{1}^{2}\cup L_{2})L_{i}R_{k+1-i}\cup\bigcup_{i\in\left[k\right]}L_{i}R_{k+1-i}(R_{1}^{2}\cup R_{2})\cup\bigcup_{i\in\left[k\right]}L_{1}L_{i}R_{k+1-i}R_{1}

\displaystyle\cup L_{1}\bigcup_{i\in\left[2,k\right]}L_{i}R_{k+2-i}\cup\bigcup_{i\in\left[2,k\right]}L_{i}R_{k+2-i}R_{1}\cup\bigcup_{i\in\left[3,k\right]}L_{i}R_{k+3-i}.

We compute its size

\displaystyle\lvert D_{2}\rvert=

\displaystyle(\lvert L_{1}\rvert^{2}+\lvert L_{2}\rvert+\lvert R_{1}\rvert^{2}+\lvert R_{2}\rvert+\lvert L_{1}\rvert\lvert R_{1}\rvert)\lvert C\rvert+

\displaystyle(\lvert L_{1}\rvert+\lvert R_{1}\rvert)\sum_{i\in\left[2,k\right]}\lvert L_{i}\rvert\lvert R_{k+2-i}\rvert+\sum_{i\in\left[3,k\right]}\lvert L_{i}\rvert\lvert R_{k+3-i}\rvert.

Knowing that
\lvert L_{2}\rvert+\lvert R_{2}\rvert=\lvert L_{1}\rvert\lvert R_{1}\rvert
and
\lvert L_{1}\rvert+\lvert R_{1}\rvert=q
, we can rewrite this size as follows

\displaystyle\lvert D_{2}\rvert

\displaystyle=(\lvert L_{1}\rvert+2\lvert L_{1}\rvert\lvert R_{1}\rvert+\lvert R_{1}\rvert)\lvert C\rvert+q\sum_{i\in\left[2,k\right]}\lvert L_{i}\rvert\lvert R_{k+2-i}\rvert+\sum_{i\in\left[3,k\right]}\lvert L_{i}\rvert\lvert R_{k+3-i}\rvert

\displaystyle=q^{2}\lvert C\rvert+q\sum_{i\in\left[2,k\right]}\lvert L_{i}\rvert\lvert R_{k+2-i}\rvert+\sum_{i\in\left[3,k\right]}\lvert L_{i}\rvert\lvert R_{k+3-i}\rvert.

Since
S_{1}^{k}(q,k+3)\geq\lvert D_{2}\rvert\geq q^{2}\lvert C\rvert=q^{2}S_{1}^{k}(q,k+1)
, the second claim is established.
∎

We determine a lower bound on the size of
(t_{1},t_{2})
-overlap-free codes using Proposition
9
and Construction
4
.

Corollary 2
.

\displaystyle S_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(q,n)\geq q^{n-t_{2}-1}\left(q-\biggl\lfloor\frac{t_{2}}{t_{2}+1}\cdot q\biggr\rfloor\right)\biggl\lfloor\frac{t_{2}}{t_{2}+1}\cdot q\biggr\rfloor^{t_{2}},

where

\displaystyle k=\begin{cases}t_{2}&\text{if }t_{1}+t_{2}\leq n,\\
n-t_{1}&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

If
t_{2}\leq(n-t_{1}+1)-1
, i.e.,
t_{1}+t_{2}\leq n
, Construction
4
provides the code
X\Sigma^{t_{1}-1}
with the largest size when
X
is a maximum
(1,t_{2})
-overlap-free code.
Otherwise, it provides the code
X\Sigma^{t_{1}-1}
with the largest size when
X
is a maximum non-overlapping code. Hence, for

\displaystyle k=\begin{cases}t_{2}&\text{if }t_{1}+t_{2}\leq n,\\
n-t_{1}&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

\displaystyle S_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(q,n)

\displaystyle\geq q^{t_{1}-1}S_{1}^{k}(q,n-t_{1}+1).

Now, use Proposition
9

\displaystyle S_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(q,n)

\displaystyle\geq q^{t_{1}-1+n-t_{1}+1-k-1}\left(q-\biggl\lfloor\frac{k}{k+1}\cdot q\biggr\rfloor\right)\biggl\lfloor\frac{k}{k+1}\cdot q\biggr\rfloor^{k}

\displaystyle=q^{n-k-1}\left(q-\biggl\lfloor\frac{k}{k+1}\cdot q\biggr\rfloor\right)\biggl\lfloor\frac{k}{k+1}\cdot q\biggr\rfloor^{k},

to conclude the proof.
∎

When
t_{1}+t_{2}\leq n
and
t_{2}+1
divides
q
,

\displaystyle S_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(q,n)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{t_{2}}{t_{2}+1}\right)^{t_{2}}\frac{q^{n}}{t_{2}+1}

\displaystyle\geq e^{-1}\cdot\frac{q^{n}}{t_{2}+1},

and the upper bound on the size of
(t_{1},t_{2})
-overlap-free codes from Section
3
is tight to up to a factor
\bigl\lfloor\frac{2n-t_{1}}{n-t_{2}}\bigr\rfloor\cdot((t_{2}+1)e)^{-1}
. For large values of
q
, the lower bound is also close to this factor, but the gap may be much greater when
q
is small. In particular, if
q<t_{2}+1
,

\displaystyle S_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(q,n)

\displaystyle\geq q^{n-t_{2}-1}(q-1)^{t_{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{q-1}{q}\right)^{t_{2}}\frac{q^{n}}{q}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{q-1}{q}\right)^{t_{2}}\frac{q^{n}}{t_{2}+1}.

Lastly, we derive a lower bound on the size of a simultaneously
(1,k)
-overlap-free and
(n-k,n-1)
-overlap-free code from Construction
6
. The proof also shows that the maximum number of words in a simultaneously
(1,1)
-overlap-free and
(n-1,n-1)
-overlap-free code is at least
(q-1)^{2}q^{n-3}
.

Proposition 12
.

Let
1<k<n/2
.
The maximum number of words in a simultaneously
(1,k)
-overlap-free and
(n-k,n-1)
-overlap-free code is at least

\displaystyle\biggl\lfloor\frac{qk}{k+2}\biggr\rfloor^{k}\left(q-\biggl\lfloor\frac{qk}{k+2}\biggr\rfloor\right)^{2}q^{n-k-2}.

Proof.

Let
x
be an integer between 0 and
q
.
Take a partition of
\Sigma
so that
L_{1}
has
x
elements and
R_{1}
has
q-x
elements. Use Construction
1
to obtain a non-overlapping code
X
by setting
L_{i}
empty for
i>1
. Note that
R_{i}=L_{1}^{i-1}R_{1}
and
X=L_{1}^{k}R_{1}
.
Now use Construction
6
to get a simultaneously
(1,k)
-overlap-free and
(n-k,n-1)
-overlap-free code

\displaystyle C=\bigcup_{\alpha}X\Sigma^{n-2k-1}R_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots R_{\alpha_{par(\alpha)}}.

Its size equals

\displaystyle\lvert C\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert X\rvert q^{n-2k-1}\sum_{\alpha}\lvert L\rvert^{k-par(\alpha)}\lvert R\rvert^{par(\alpha)}

\displaystyle=x^{k}(q-x)q^{n-2k-1}\sum_{l=1}^{k}\binom{k-1}{l-1}x^{k-l}(q-x)^{l}.

Applying the binomial theorem we obtain

\displaystyle\lvert C\rvert

\displaystyle=x^{k}(q-x)^{2}q^{n-2k-1}(x+q-x)^{k-1}

\displaystyle=x^{k}(q-x)^{2}q^{n-k-2}.

Since
f
is differentiable, we can determine from its derivative

\displaystyle\lvert C\rvert^{\prime}(x)=q^{n-k-2}x^{k}(qk-(k+2)x)(q-x)

that the maximum on
(0,q)
is at
x=\frac{qk}{k+2}
.
Since there are no other local extrema,
f
restricted to integers attains its maximum at one of the roundings of
x
to an integer. We again decide to round the value down because for
k>1
,
1\leq\bigl\lfloor\frac{qk}{k+2}\bigr\rfloor\leq q-1
, while whenever
2q<k+2
,
\bigl\lceil\frac{qk}{k+2}\bigr\rceil>q-1
.
∎

Extending wavelet regularity beyond Gevrey classes
Filip Tomić, Stefan Tutić, Milica Žigić

Abstract
We construct a smooth orthonormal wavelet ψ \psi such that both ψ \psi and its Fourier transform ψ ^ \widehat{\psi} belong to the extended Gevrey class ℰ σ ​ ( ℝ ) \mathcal{E}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}) for σ > 1 \sigma>1 , providing an example that lies beyond all classical Gevrey classes. Our approach uses the idea of invariant cycles to extend the initial Lemarié-Meyer support of the low-pass filter m 0 m_{0} from [ − 2 ​ π 3 , 2 ​ π 3 ] [-\frac{2\pi}{3},\frac{2\pi}{3}] to [ − 4 ​ π 5 , 4 ​ π 5 ] [-\frac{4\pi}{5},\frac{4\pi}{5}] . This extension allows us to control the decay rate of m 0 m_{0} near 2 ​ π 3 \frac{2\pi}{3} , which yields global decay estimates for ψ \psi and ψ ^ \hat{\psi} . In addition, the decay rates are described using special functions involving the Lambert W function, which plays an important role in our construction.



1
Introduction

One of the fundamental concepts in wavelet theory is the notion of a
multiresolution analysis
(MRA in short), introduced by Y. Meyer
[
17
]
and later developed by S. Mallat
[
16
]
. MRA is based on a hierarchical family of closed subspaces of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and involves a
scaling function

\varphi
, which plays a central role in wavelet construction. Under certain technical assumptions,
\hat{\varphi}
can be represented as an infinite product constructed from a
2\pi
-periodic function, known as the low-pass filter. More precisely, if
\varphi
is a scaling function and
m_{0}
the associated low-pass filter, then

\hat{\varphi}(\xi)=\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}m_{0}(2^{-j}\xi),\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{R},

(1)

and the Fourier transform of the corresponding MRA wavelet
\psi
is given by

\hat{\psi}(\xi)=e^{i\xi/2}\,\overline{m_{0}\!\left(\frac{\xi}{2}+\pi\right)}\,\hat{\varphi}\!\left(\frac{\xi}{2}\right),\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

(2)

With this approach, it is therefore sufficient to construct a low-pass filter with the desired regularity properties, which are inherited by
\hat{\varphi}
and
\hat{\psi}
through (
1
) and (
2
). The regularity of
\varphi
and
\psi
is then measured via Paley–Wiener type theorems, which relate the smoothness of a function to the decay of its Fourier transform. For a more detailed exposition of the general theory of wavelets and their applications, we refer to
[
5
,
9
]
.

We will use invariant cycles
\left\{-\frac{2\pi}{3},\frac{2\pi}{3}\right\}
and
\left\{-\frac{2\pi}{5},\frac{2\pi}{5},-\frac{4\pi}{5},\frac{4\pi}{5}\right\}
to extend the support of the low-pass filter
m_{0}
on the interval
[-\pi,\pi)
. For this purpose, let us recall the definition (see
[
10
]
).

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\rho:[-\pi,\pi)\to[-\pi,\pi):\xi\mapsto 2\xi\ (mod\,2\pi
). For
\xi\in[-\pi,\pi)
, if there exists a number
l\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\rho^{l}(\xi)=\xi
, we call the corresponding orbit
\mathcal{O}(\xi)=\{\xi,\rho(\xi),\dots,\rho^{l-1}(\xi)\}
an invariant cycle of length
l
, generated by
\xi
.

The notion of invariant cycles was introduced by A. Cohen to characterize orthonormality conditions for wavelets (see
[
5
]
).
In
[
10
]
, it is shown that for a band-limited MRA wavelet,

\prod_{\xi\in\mathcal{O}(\xi)}m_{0}(\xi)=0
for every invariant cycle
\mathcal{O}(\xi)
.
The authors also constructed a smooth low-pass filter with
m_{0}(\pm 2\pi/3)\neq 0
,
yielding a non-band-limited wavelet regular only up to finite order.
In contrast, Lemarié–Meyer wavelets use smooth low-pass filter that vanish in a neighborhood of
\pm 2\pi/3
, making the corresponding scaling function
\varphi

and wavelet
\psi
band-limited and thus analytic in time.

It is well known that a smooth orthonormal wavelet cannot have exponential decay (see
[
6
]
). Therefore, constructing a smooth wavelet close to the “critical case”—that is, one which is slightly more regular than
C^{\infty}
, or whose decay is slightly slower than exponential—appears to be a challenging task. An example of such a wavelet can be found in
[
7
]
, where the authors employ Gevrey regularity and use the translates of the Gevrey function
e^{-|\xi|^{-\frac{1}{s-1}}}
,
s>1
, to control the decay rate of the low-pass filter near
\frac{2\pi}{3}
.

We apply ideas from the cited work to the function

\displaystyle\begin{split}&f_{\rho,\sigma}(x)=e^{-\rho\,g_{\sigma}\left(1/x\right)},\qquad\rho>0,\;\sigma>1,\\
&\text{where}\\
&g_{\sigma}(x)=\omega_{\sigma}\!\left(\ln\left(1+|x|\right)\right),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\\
&\omega_{\sigma}(x)=x^{\frac{\sigma}{\sigma-1}}\big/W^{\frac{1}{\sigma-1}}(x),\qquad x\geq 0,\;\sigma>1,\qquad\omega_{\sigma}(0)=0,\end{split}

(3)

and
W
denotes the principal branch of the Lambert
W
function. This approach yields a new non-band-limited wavelet whose decay rate is faster than polynomial but slower than subexponential. Moreover, its regularity is captured by the
extended Gevrey classes
.

Extended Gevrey classes of locally smooth functions were introduced in
[
20
]
. They contain the union of all Gevrey classes and therefore describe regularity intermediate between Gevrey and
C^{\infty}
. The growth of the derivatives of their elements is controlled by the sequence
\{M^{\tau,\sigma}_{p}\}_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, where

M^{\tau,\sigma}_{p}=p^{\tau p^{\sigma}},\quad p\in\mathbb{N},\qquad M_{0}^{\tau,\sigma}=1,\qquad\tau>0,\ \sigma>1.

(4)

Since the sequences in (
4
) do not satisfy Komatsu’s condition

\displaystyle(M.2)\quad(\exists\,C>0)\ M_{p+q}\leq C^{p+q+1}M_{p}M_{q},\quad p,q\in\mathbb{N},

for any choice of
\tau>0
and
\sigma>1
, they cannot be treated within the classical framework of ultradifferentiable function theory. Nevertheless, it has recently been shown that extended Gevrey classes form an important example of
weight matrix classes
, where regularity is governed by a family of sequences rather than a fixed one (see
[
11
,
23
]
). Applications of the theory can be found in
[
3
,
12
,
13
,
2
]
, where extended Gevrey classes are often referred as PTT-spaces (Pilipović, Teofanov, Tomić spaces).

The main purpose of this paper is to establish new regularity properties of wavelets that go beyond the classical theory of ultradifferentiable functions. We refine the result of
[
24
]
on band-limited wavelets in extended Gevrey settings by controlling the decay and smoothness of the scaling function in the Fourier domain. At the same time, we obtain a wavelet that is less regular than the one constructed in
[
7
, Theorem 4.4]
; equivalently, its regularity lies closer to
C^{\infty}
(see Lemma
3.6
).

Paper is organized as follows: In Subsection
1.1
we fix the notation and discuss the main properties of the Lambert
W
function. In Section
2
, we introduce the extended Gevrey classes
\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R})
, discuss their defining sequences
M^{\tau,\sigma}_{p}
and corresponding associated functions. In Section
2.1
we prove that functions
f_{\rho,\sigma}
given in (
3
) belong to
\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R})
. Finally, in Section
3
we construct the desired wavelet from the low-pass filter
m_{0}
, by using
f_{\rho,\sigma}
, and prove Theorem
1.2
as our main result:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\sigma>1
. For
\eta>1
set
\displaystyle\sigma_{\eta}=\frac{\sigma+\eta(\sigma-1)}{1+\eta(\sigma-1)}\in(1,\sigma_{0})
where
\sigma_{0}=\max\{2,\sigma\}
. Then there exists an orthonormal wavelet
\psi
such that

\displaystyle\psi\in\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R})\backslash\bigcup_{1<\sigma^{\prime}<\sigma_{\eta}}\mathcal{E}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})\quad{\rm and}\quad\displaystyle\hat{\psi}\in\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R})\backslash\bigcup_{1<\sigma^{\prime}<\sigma}\mathcal{E}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}).

The Appendix contains the proof of Lemma
2.17
and illustrations of the wavelet constructed in Section
3
.

1.1
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper we use the following notation:
\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{C}

denote the sets of natural numbers, non-negative integers, positive real numbers,
real numbers, and complex numbers, respectively. We write
f\asymp g
to denote that the two functions are

asymptotically equivalent
, meaning that
f=O(g)
and
g=O(f)

as
x\to\infty
(here
f=O(g)
,
x\to\infty
, means that

f(x)\leq L\,(g(x)+1)
for some
L\geq 1
and all
x\geq 0
). By
f\prec g
we mean that
f=o(g)
as
x\to\infty
, that is,

f(x)/g(x)\to 0
as
x\to\infty
.

With
\displaystyle{\rm coz}\,f=\{x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}\,|\,f(x_{0})\not=0\}
we denote a
cozero
set (complement of a zero set) of the continuous function
f
. Then the
support
of
f
is
{\rm supp}\,f=\overline{{\rm\operatorname{coz}}\,f}
, where
\overline{X}
denotes the closure of the set
X
. The
interior
of the set
X
is denoted by
{\rm int}\,X
. We write
\mathbb{K}\subset\subset U
when
\mathbb{K}
is an compact subset of an open set
U
.

Let
\nu
be a non-negative, continuous, increasing and even function on
\mathbb{R}
, with
\nu(0)=0
.
Recall (see
[
1
]
),
\nu
is of
Braun-Meise-Taylor
type (BMT in short) if the following conditions hold:

(
\alpha
)
\displaystyle\nu(2x)=O(\nu(x)),\quad x\to\infty,

(
\beta
)
\displaystyle\nu(x)=O(x),\quad x\to\infty,

(
\gamma
)
\displaystyle\ln x=o(\nu(x)),\quad x\to\infty,\;\;

(
\delta
)
\displaystyle\vartheta(x)=\nu(e^{x})\quad
is convex.

Example 1.3
.

Some classical examples of BMT functions are

\displaystyle\nu(x)=|x|^{s},\,0<s\leq 1,\quad\nu(x)=\ln^{s}_{+}|x|,\,s>1,\quad\nu(x)=\frac{|x|}{\ln^{s-1}(e+|x|)},\,s>1,

for
x\in\mathbb{R}
where
\ln_{+}{|x|}=\max\{0,\ln|x|\}
.
Moreover, it was shown in
[
23
]
that
\omega_{\sigma}(\ln_{+}|x|)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\omega_{\sigma}
is given in (
3
), is asymptotically equivalent to a BMT function. Clearly, the same conclusion holds for
g_{\sigma}(x)=\omega_{\sigma}(\ln(1+|x|))
.

We recall some of the basic properties of the
Lambert
W
function
. It is defined as the multivalued inverse of the function
z\mapsto ze^{z}
for
z\in\mathbb{C}
. We will denote its principal branch by
W(z)
,
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,-e^{-1}]
(instead of common
W_{0}(z)
). Lambert
W
function splits the complex plane into infinitely many regions. The boundary curve of its principal branch is given by:

\{(-x\,\operatorname{ctg}x,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,|\,-\pi<x<\pi\}.

Here we list some additional properties of
W
that we will use in the sequel:

(W1)\quad

W(-e^{-1})=-1
,
W(0)=0
,
W(e)=1
and
W(x)
is continuous, increasing and concave on
(-e^{-1},\infty)
.

(W2)\quad

z=W(z)e^{W(z)}
,
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,-e^{-1}]
.

(W3)\quad

\displaystyle\ln x-\ln(\ln x)\leq W(x)\leq\ln x-\frac{1}{2}\ln(\ln x),\quad x\geq e.

(W4)\quad

Derivatives of Lambert
W
function are given by

\displaystyle W^{(n)}(x)=\frac{W^{n}(x)\,p_{n}(W(x))}{x^{n}(1+W(x))^{2n-1}},\quad x>0,\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

where
p_{n}
is polynomial satisfying

p_{n+1}(x)=(1+x)p^{\prime}_{n}(x)-(nx+3n-1)p_{n}(x),\quad p_{1}(x)=1.

Note that
(W1)
implies that
W(x)>0
when
x>0
. From
(W3)
it follows

W(x)\sim\ln x,\quad x\to\infty,

(5)

and hence
W(Cx)\sim W(x),

x\to\infty
, for any
C>0
. Moreover, by
(W1)
and
(W2)
we have

W(x)\sim x,\quad x\to 0,

(6)

and
(W4)
implies that

\displaystyle W^{\prime}(x)=\frac{W(x)}{x(1+W(x))},\quad x>0.

(7)

For more details concerning the Lambert
W
function we refer to
[
4
,
18
]
.

We end this preliminary section with the following lemma that describes the behavior of
e^{W(x)}
.

Lemma 1.4
.

If
W
is the principal branch of Lambert
W
function then

\ln x\prec e^{W(x)}\prec x,\quad x\to\infty.

Proof.

By using L’Hôpital’s rule, (
5
) and (
7
), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{\ln x}{e^{W(x)}}=\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{\frac{1}{x}}{e^{W(x)}\frac{W(x)}{x(1+W(x))}}=\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{1}{e^{W(x)}}\frac{1+W(x)}{W(x)}=0.

For the second part, we use
(W2)
and (
5
) to obtain

\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{e^{W(x)}}{x}=\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{e^{W(x)}}{W(x)e^{W(x)}}=\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{1}{W(x)}=0.\qed

2
Regularity classes

We begin with a Lemma from
[
20
]
that summarizes the main properties of the sequences
M^{\tau,\sigma}_{p}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0},
given in (
4
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\tau>0
,
\sigma>1
,
M_{0}^{\tau,\sigma}=1
,
and
M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma}=p^{\tau p^{\sigma}}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then there exists constant
C>1
such that:

(M.1)

(M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma})^{2}\leq M_{p-1}^{\tau,\sigma}M_{p+1}^{\tau,\sigma},\quad

p\in\mathbb{N},

\widetilde{(M.2)}

M_{p+q}^{\tau,\sigma}\leq C^{p^{\sigma}+q^{\sigma}}M_{p}^{\tau 2^{\sigma-1},\sigma}M_{q}^{\tau 2^{\sigma-1},\sigma},\quad

p,q\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\quad

\widetilde{(M.2)^{\prime}}

M_{p+1}^{\tau,\sigma}\leq C^{p^{\sigma}}M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma},\quad

p\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\quad

(M.3)^{\prime}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{M_{p-1}^{\tau,\sigma}}{M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma}}<\infty.

In addition, if
\sigma_{2}>\sigma_{1}>1
and
\tau_{0}>0
then for every
h,\tau>0
there exists
C>0
such that

h^{p^{\sigma_{1}}}M^{\tau_{0},\sigma_{1}}_{p}\leq CM_{p}^{\tau,\sigma_{2}}.

(8)

Remark 2.2
.

Let us briefly comment the case
\sigma=1
. Note that
\displaystyle M^{\tau,1}_{p}=p^{\tau p}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}
, for
\tau>0
are Gevrey sequences. Then the conditions
\widetilde{(M.2)^{\prime}}
and
\widetilde{(M.2)}
reduce to the classical Komatsu conditions
{(M.2)^{\prime}}
and
{(M.2)}
, respectively. Moreover, the Gevrey sequences satisfy
non-quasianalyticity
condition
{(M.3)^{\prime}}
if and only if
\tau>1
.

Now we can define the extended Gevrey classes
\mathcal{E}_{\tau,\sigma}(\mathbb{R})
for
\tau>0
and
\sigma>1
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\tau>0
and
\sigma>1
and
M^{\tau,\sigma}_{p}=p^{\tau p^{\sigma}}
for
p\in\mathbb{N}
,
M^{\tau,\sigma}_{0}=1
. A smooth function
\phi
belongs to
\mathcal{E}_{\tau,\sigma}(\mathbb{R})
if

(\forall\,\mathbb{K}\subset\subset\mathbb{R})(\exists C>0)\quad\sup_{x\in\mathbb{K}}|\phi^{(p)}(x)|\leq C^{p^{\sigma}+1}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{p},\,\,p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(9)

We denote

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R})=\bigcup_{\tau>0}\mathcal{E}_{\tau,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}).

Remark 2.4
.

In the definition of
\mathcal{E}_{\tau,\sigma}(\mathbb{R})
the space
\mathbb{R}
may be replaced by any open set
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}
. This produces the local classes
\mathcal{E}_{\tau,\sigma}(U)
; their elements satisfy derivative estimates (
9
) on every compact set
\mathbb{K}\subset\subset U
.

Again for
\sigma=1
we obtain some of the well-known classes. For instance,
\mathcal{E}_{1,1}(\mathbb{R})=\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{R})
, and
\mathcal{E}_{\tau,1}(\mathbb{R})=\mathcal{G}_{\tau}(\mathbb{R})
for
\tau>1
; these are the spaces of locally analytic functions and Gevrey functions of order
\tau
, respectively.

Extended Gevrey classes
\mathcal{E}_{\tau,\sigma}
for
\sigma>1
and
\tau>0
were introduced and studied as spaces of locally smooth functions equipped with projective and inductive limit topologies. Since these topologies will not be used in this paper, we omit the details and refer the reader to
[
20
]
. Unlike the classical Gevrey classes, note that in the definition of
\mathcal{E}_{\tau,\sigma}
the geometric factor
C^{p}
is replaced by
C^{p^{\sigma}}
,
\sigma>1
. This modification ensures the stability of the classes under the action of differential operators.

Taking the union with respect to the parameter
\tau
, we obtain the
weight-matrix class

\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}
of ultradifferentiable functions, whose derivatives are controlled by the family of sequences
\{M^{\tau,\sigma}_{p}\}_{\tau>0,\,p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
for
\sigma>1
. These classes can also be treated within the framework of BMT (Braun–Meise–Taylor) theory (see
[
23
,
11
]
).

We summarize several basic properties of the extended Gevrey classes in the following Proposition (see
[
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
]
); the proof relies on the properties of the sequences
M^{\tau,\sigma}_{p}
stated in Lemma
2.5
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\tau>0
and
\sigma>1
.

i)

For
\sigma_{2}>\sigma_{1}>1
we have

\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{R})\subset\bigcup_{\tau>1}{\mathcal{G}}_{\tau}({\mathbb{R}})\subset\bigcap_{\tau>0}{\mathcal{E}}_{\tau,\sigma_{1}}({\mathbb{R}})\subset{\mathcal{E}}_{\tau,\sigma_{1}}({\mathbb{R}})\subset{\mathcal{E}}_{\sigma_{1}}\subset\mathcal{E}_{\sigma_{2}}\subset C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}).

ii)

there exists a compactly supported function
\varphi\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\tau,\sigma}({\mathbb{R}})
.

iii)

{\mathcal{E}}_{\tau,\sigma}({\mathbb{R}})
is closed under the pointwise multiplication.

iv)

{\mathcal{E}}_{\tau,\sigma}({\mathbb{R}})
is closed under finite order derivation.

v)

\mathcal{E}_{\tau,\sigma}({\mathbb{R}})
is closed under superposition. In particular, if
F(x)\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{R})
and
f(x)\in\mathcal{E}_{\tau,\sigma}({\mathbb{R}})
then
F(f(x))\in\mathcal{E}_{\tau,\sigma}({\mathbb{R}})
.

vi)

\mathcal{E}_{\tau,\sigma}({\mathbb{R}})
is invariant under translations and dilatations.

Next we define a function associated to
M^{\tau,\sigma}_{p}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\tau>0
and
\sigma>1
. Then the associated function to the sequence
M^{\tau,\sigma}_{p}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, is given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle T_{\tau,\sigma}(x)=\sup_{p\in\mathbf{N}_{0}}\ln\frac{x^{p}}{M^{\tau,\sigma}_{p}},\quad\;\;x>0.

Remark 2.7
.

Since

\left(M^{\tau,\sigma}_{p}\right)^{1/p}=p^{\tau p^{\sigma-1}}

is clearly bounded below by a positive constant for any
\tau>0
and
\sigma>1
, it follows that
T_{\tau,\sigma}(x)
vanishes for sufficiently small
x>0
(see
[
14
]
). Therefore we may assume
T_{\tau,\sigma}(0)=0
.

It turns out that for any fixed
\tau>0
,
T_{\tau,\sigma}(|x|)
is asymptotically equivalent to the function
\displaystyle g_{\sigma}(x)=\frac{\ln^{\frac{\sigma}{\sigma-1}}(1+x)}{W^{\frac{1}{\sigma-1}}(\ln(1+x))}
which appears in (
3
). In particular, following Proposition holds (see
[
22
,
23
]
).

Proposition 2.8
.

For any
\tau>0
and
\sigma>1
there exists
A_{\sigma},B_{\sigma}>0
and
\widetilde{A}_{\tau,\sigma},\widetilde{B}_{\tau,\sigma}\in\mathbb{R}
such that for
x>0

B_{\sigma}\tau^{-\frac{1}{\sigma-1}}\frac{\ln^{\frac{\sigma}{\sigma-1}}(1+x)}{W^{\frac{1}{\sigma-1}}(\ln(1+x))}+\widetilde{B}_{\tau,\sigma}\leq T_{\tau,\sigma}(x)\leq A_{\sigma}\tau^{-\frac{1}{\sigma-1}}\frac{\ln^{\frac{\sigma}{\sigma-1}}(1+x)}{W^{\frac{1}{\sigma-1}}(\ln(1+x))}+\widetilde{A}_{\tau,\sigma}.

Remark 2.9
.

Let us define

\displaystyle h_{\tau,\sigma}(x):=e^{-T_{\tau,\sigma}(1/x)}=\inf_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{p}x^{p},\quad x>0.

Then Proposition
2.8
implies that for any
\rho>0
and
\sigma>1
there exist constants
A,B,\tau_{1},\tau_{2}>0
such that

Ah_{\tau_{1},\sigma}(x)\leq f_{\rho,\sigma}(x)\leq Bh_{\tau_{2},\sigma}(x),\quad x>0,

(10)

where
f_{\rho,\sigma}(x)=e^{-\rho g_{\sigma}(1/x)}
is given in (
3
).

Note that the right-hand side of (
10
) implies that for any given
\rho>0
there exist
B_{1},\tau>0
such that

\sup_{x>0}\frac{f_{\rho,\sigma}(x)}{x^{p}}\leq B_{1}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{p},\quad p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(11)

The following definition introduces the decay rate studied in this paper.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
\sigma>1
and
\displaystyle g_{\sigma}
be as in (
3
). A continuous function
\phi
belongs to
\displaystyle{{\mathbf{\Gamma}}}_{\sigma}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
if

(\exists\rho>0)\,(\exists C>0)\quad\quad|\phi(\xi)|\leq C\,e^{-\rho g_{\sigma}(\xi)},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

(12)

We can now formulate the Paley–Wiener theorem, which relates the regularity of
\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}
to the decay rate given by
{{\mathbf{\Gamma}}}_{\sigma}
. For further details, we refer to
[

,
25
]
.

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
\sigma>1
and
{{\mathbf{\Gamma}}}_{\sigma}
be as in Definition
2.10
.

a)

Let
\varphi
be a
C^{\infty}
function with compact support. If
\varphi\in\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R})
then
\widehat{\varphi}\in{{\mathbf{\Gamma}}}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R})
.

b)

If the function
\varphi
is such that
\widehat{\varphi}\in{\mathbf{\Gamma}}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R})

then
\varphi\in\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R})
.

Remark 2.12
.

Part
a)
of Theorem
2.11
can be easily extended to smooth functions that are not compactly supported but have sufficient decay at infinity. In particular, if a smooth function
\varphi
satisfies
\widehat{\varphi}\notin\mathbf{\Gamma}_{\sigma}
, then
\varphi\notin\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}
.

We conclude this section with the following technical lemma, which will be used later.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
\sigma>1
and
{{\mathbf{\Gamma}}}_{\sigma}
be as in Definition
2.10
. Then
\phi(\xi)\in\mathbf{\Gamma}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R})
if and only if
(1+|\xi|)^{a}\phi(\xi)\in\mathbf{\Gamma}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R})
for arbitrary
a\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in\mathbf{\Gamma}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R})
and
a\in\mathbb{R}
. Then by (
12
), exist
\rho>0,C>0
such that

\displaystyle(1+|\xi|)^{a}|\phi(\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq(1+|\xi|)^{a}C\exp\left\{-\rho\frac{\ln^{\frac{\sigma}{\sigma-1}}(1+|\xi|)}{W^{\frac{1}{\sigma-1}}(\ln(1+|\xi|))}\right\}

\displaystyle=C(1+|\xi|)^{a}e^{-\rho\,g_{\sigma}(\xi)}

\displaystyle\leq C\max\{1,2^{a}|\xi|^{a}\}e^{-\frac{\rho}{2}\,g_{\sigma}(\xi)}e^{-\frac{\rho}{2}\,g_{\sigma}(\xi)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}e^{-\frac{\rho}{2}\,g_{\sigma}(\xi)},\quad\xi\geq\frac{1}{2},

for
\displaystyle C_{1}:=C\sup_{\xi\neq 0}\{\max\{1,2^{a}|\xi|^{a}\}e^{-\frac{\rho}{2}\,g_{\sigma}(\xi)}\}.
Note that

is finite, because

which follows easily by (

Remark 2.14

In

Enhancing immunotherapy efficacy of hepatocellular carcinoma by inhibiting the key antioxidant factor Nrf2
Authors: Yan Zhu, Maoxuan Liu, Hang Fai Kwok, Liang Chen
Published: 2026

Main text

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) responds poorly to immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), with fewer than 20% of patients deriving clinical benefit. In our recent article, “targeted inhibition of Nrf2 potentiates antitumor immunity and enhances the efficacy of immunotherapy in HCC1” published in the Journal for ImmunoTherapy of Cancer, we show that nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (Nrf2), a master antioxidant transcription factor, acts as a dual suppressor of antitumor immunity in HCC by simultaneously driving programmed death ligand-1 (PD-L1) expression and silencing major histocompatibility complex class I (MHC-I) on tumor cells. Pharmacological inhibition of Nrf2 with brusatol (BRU) reshapes the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment and markedly enhances the efficacy of both anti-PD-1 antibody and chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T cell therapy in preclinical HCC models.

The problem

HCC is one of the most lethal malignancies worldwide, and ICI-based immunotherapy has transformed its clinical management over the past decade.2 Despite this progress, fewer than 20% of patients with HCC respond to ICI therapy, and many who initially benefit subsequently develop acquired resistance.3 Two overlapping mechanisms drive this immune resistance: tumor cells upregulate PD-L1 to suppress T cell activation, while simultaneously downregulating MHC-I to evade T cell recognition.4 Strategies that address only one of these arms—such as anti-PD-1 monotherapy—are insufficient to convert immunologically “cold” HCC tumors into immune-permissive ones.5 A single molecular target that governs both escape mechanisms in concert would represent a compelling and previously unexploited therapeutic opportunity.

The discovery

We identify Nrf2 as precisely such a target. Analysis of The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA-LIHC) patient data revealed that high Nrf2 expression in tumor cells is specifically associated with worse overall survival in patients whose tumors are also enriched in CD8+ T cells—pointing to a functional antagonism between Nrf2 activity and cytotoxic immune responses in HCC. To investigate this relationship mechanistically, we treated immunocompetent C57BL/6J and immune-deficient RAG-1−/− (recombination activating gene 1 knockout) mice bearing Hepa1-6 HCC allografts with BRU, a potent pharmacological Nrf2 inhibitor.6 BRU produced robust tumor regression, achieving a tumor inhibition rate of 46%, in immunocompetent mice but had no measurable antitumor effect in immune-deficient animals. This critical finding established that the therapeutic activity of Nrf2 inhibition depends entirely on an intact adaptive immune system rather than on any direct cytotoxic effect of the drug.

We then delineated two complementary molecular mechanisms underlying this immune-dependent activity. First, Nrf2 directly drives PD-L1 transcription in HCC cells: BRU suppressed surface PD-L1 in a dose-dependent manner, reversed IFN-γ-induced PD-L1 upregulation at both mRNA and protein levels, and reduced PD-L1 promoter activity in dual-luciferase reporter assays. Stable Nrf2-knockdown cell lines recapitulated these findings, and two chemically independent Nrf2 inhibitors—luteolin and ML385—reproduced PD-L1 suppression, effectively excluding off-target artifacts of BRU. Second, and for the first time, we demonstrate that Nrf2 inhibition upregulates MHC-I expression through NF-κB activation: BRU increased phosphorylation of the p65 subunit, which in turn directly occupied the HLA-A and B2M promoters as confirmed by chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP)-qPCR. Genetic knockdown of p65 abolished BRU-induced MHC-I upregulation, confirming the mechanistic dependency on this pathway. Bulk RNA sequencing and single-cell RNA sequencing of tumor-infiltrating immune cells showed that BRU treatment markedly expanded the CD8+ T cell compartment—particularly effector (Ccl5-hi), proliferating, and activated subsets—with concordant increases in granzyme B expression, IFN-γ production, and CD107a degranulation. Building on this dual remodeling of the tumor immune microenvironment, we tested BRU in combination with established immunotherapies. In BRU plus anti-PD-1 combination experiments using the Hepa1-6 allograft model, the combination produced significantly greater tumor suppression than either agent alone, accompanied by increased CD8+ T cell infiltration and elevated granzyme B release, as well as reduced tumor cell surface PD-L1 and increased MHC-I expression compared with anti-PD-1 monotherapy. In BRU plus CAR T experiments using a B-NDG (NOD.CB17-Prkdcscid IL2rgtm1/Bcgen) xenograft model, the combination similarly outperformed CAR T therapy alone in suppressing tumor growth; histopathological analysis of major organs and serum biochemistry across all treatment arms revealed no evidence of hepatotoxicity, nephrotoxicity, or other treatment-related organ injury.

The implications

Our findings position Nrf2 as a transcriptional gatekeeper that simultaneously enforces two core mechanisms of immune escape in HCC. Its activity drives PD-L1-mediated T cell suppression while erasing MHC-I-dependent antigen visibility and pharmacological inhibition reverses both in concert—converting tumors into an immune-permissive state that is more susceptible to T cell-based therapies. This mechanistic duality is conceptually distinct from current PD-1/PD-L1 blockade strategies and provides a preclinical rationale for Nrf2-targeted combination immunotherapy in a disease where the majority of patients fail to benefit from available immunotherapeutic regimens.

Important limitations remain. BRU suppresses global protein synthesis beyond Nrf2 and has demonstrated hepatotoxicity in primary human hepatocytes and in early phase clinical studies, which constrains its direct translation to patients who already carry underlying liver dysfunction.7 Developing more selective and liver-safe Nrf2 inhibitors is therefore a pressing priority. Our findings are also based on murine models; the far greater heterogeneity of the human HCC tumor microenvironment will require rigorous prospective validation. Future studies should extend this combination strategy to other ICI approaches, including anti-CTLA-4, and explore whether Nrf2 inhibition can synergize with NK cell- or macrophage-based immunotherapeutic platforms.

Open problems in K-stability of Fano varieties
Chenyang Xu, Ziquan Zhuang

Abstract
. In this note, we discuss a number of open problems in K-stability theory.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Kähler-Einstein problem

It has been a central question in geometry to find an ‘optimal’ structure on a manifold
X
. In differential geometry, metrics
g
satisfying the vacuum Einstein field equations, i.e.
{\rm Ric}(g)=\lambda g
, provide a candidate, and have been investigated extensively. When
X
is a complex manifold, then it is also natural to consider focus on Kähler metrics
g
. For any Kähler metric
g
, if we consider the associated Kähler form
\omega
, then the Einstein equation becomes

(1)

{\rm Ric}(\omega)=\lambda\omega

and we can assume
\lambda=-1,0
or 1. If we start with a background Kähler metric
\omega_{0}
such that
\lambda\omega_{0}\in c_{1}(X)
, using
\partial\overline{\partial}
-lemma, we can write
{\rm Ric}(\omega_{0})-\lambda\omega_{0}=i\partial\overline{\partial}F
, and
\omega-\omega_{0}=i\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi
. Then the Kähler-Einstein equation (
1
) becomes a complex Monge-Ampere equation

(2)

{\displaystyle(\omega_{0}+i\partial{\bar{\partial}}\varphi)^{n}=e^{F-\lambda\varphi}\omega_{0}^{n}}\,.

Its behaviour is highly sensitive to the sign of the topological constant
\lambda
. It has been proved that when
\lambda=-1
or 1, there is always a solution. However, when
\lambda=1
, the answer is much more delicate. We note in this case,
c_{1}(X)
contains a Kähler class, i.e. the canonical class
-K_{X}
is ample, and we call such
X
Fano manifolds.

To solve
\varphi
, we look at the space of Kähler potentials

\mathcal{H}=\{\varphi\mid\omega_{0}+i\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi>0\}\,,

and it is show in
[
75
]
there exists a K-energy functional
\mathcal{K}\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}
, such that a solution of (
2
) corresponds to a critical point of
\mathcal{K}
. Then in
[
87
]
(also see
[
33
]
), a key speculation is made that to test whether there is a critical point, it should suffice to look at the slope of
\mathcal{K}
along the ray of metrics arising from the pull back of the (normalized) Fubini-Study metic along embeddings
\lambda\cdot X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}:=\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(rK_{X})^{*})
for all one parameter subgroup
\lambda\colon\mathbb{C}^{*}\to{\rm PGL}(N+1)
and
r\gg 0
. Such a condition is coined as
K-stability
. Later, in
[
34
]
, it is realized that the K-stability condition is indeed algebraic and can be generalized to any polarized projective schemes.

1.2.
K-stability of Fano varieties in algebraic geometry

There have been tremendous progress in the study of the Kähler-Einstein problem, from many different perspectives. One remarkable connection happened in the last decade is its deep interaction with higher dimensional geometry. As a result, algebraic K-stability theory of Fano varieties itself has grown to be a major field in algebraic geometry.

Among all achievements of K-stability theory, the most striking one is the construction of projective scheme parametrizing K-polystable Fano varieties with fixed numerical invariants, see Theorem
4
. This construction involves many recipes: different ways to characterize K-stability (e.g. Theorem
2
), progress on minimal model program (e.g.
[
18
]
, Theorem
33
), local KSBA stability of families of higher dimensional varieties (
[
54
]
), and abstract stack theory (
[
6
]
) etc..

Another surprising aspect is that the local-to-global correspondence between Fano varieties and klt singularities can be extended to include stability theory. More precisely, there is a local stability theory, developed based on the notion of normalized volume functions (see
16
). See Theorem
17
. Another deep feature is the boundedness, see Theorem
26
.

There have been several literature surveying the results in K-stability, see e.g.
[
57
,
95
,
98
]
. For readers who want to know the topic in more details, we refer to
[
96
]
. In this note, we would like to discuss a number of open questions, rising from different aspects of the theory. After the construction of K-moduli spaces and the proof of stable degeneration theorem, we hope these questions can lead to future development of K-stability theory, especially by establishing more connections with other fields of higher dimensional geometry.

2.1.
K-stability of explicit Fano varieties

Historically, the question of verifying whether a given Fano admits a Kähler-Einstein metric has been well-studied. The algebraic version, which currently presents the best approach to the original question, is to investigate K-(semi,poly)stability of any given Fano variety (or more generally log Fano pair). This question has drawn a lot of interest partly because it connects to many existing techniques developed in extensive research on Fano varieties. Here we discuss several classes of examples of particular interest to us (also see Problem
23
for other examples).

2.1.1.
Fano hypersurfaces

Problem 6
.

For any
3\leq d\leq n+1
, prove every degree
d
smooth hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
is K-stable.

When

[
41
]

3
,
4
]

n+1-\max\{1,n^{\frac{1}{3}}\}\leq d\leq n+1\,.

It is also easy to see a general degree

whose general points parametrize general

2.2.
Positivity of tangent bundle

It is known that Kähler-Einstein manifolds (for all possible values of
\lambda
in (
1
)) has a stable tangent bundle, because the Kähler-Einstein metric
g_{\rm KE}
on the tangent bundle
T_{X}
will make
(T_{X},g_{\rm KE})
a Hermitian-Einstein metric with respect to the Kähler form
\omega_{\rm KE}
. Then we can apply (the easier direction of) the Donaldson-Uhlenbeck-Yau Theorem which says a vector bundle
E
admits a Hermitian-Einstein metric
g
with respect to the a give Kähler form
\omega
implies
E
is polystable.

A folklore question, which will be an important step to understand stability, is to give a purely algebraic proof of these statements.

Problem 12
.

Let
X
be a Fano variety. Give an algebraic proof of the following:

(1)

If
X
is K-semistable, then the tangent sheaf
T_{X}
is semistable with respect to the polarization
-K_{X}
.

(2)

If
T_{X}
is polystable, then there is a quasi-étale cover
f\colon Y\to X
such that
Y=\prod_{i}X_{i}
and
T_{Y}=f^{*}(T_{X})=\bigoplus_{i}T_{X_{i}}
such that
T_{X_{i}}
is stable with respect to
-K_{Y}
.

A maximal destabilizing sheaf
F\subset T_{X}
will induce a zero morphism

[F,F]\to T_{X}/F\,,

which means it is a integrable foliation. Moreover, for a general complete intersection curve
C
of
H_{i}\in|-mK_{X}|

(m\geq 0)
,
F|_{C}
is ample as it is a stable vector bundle of positive slope by restriction theorem. Therefore, the foliation is indeed algebraic
[
27
,
52
]
, i.e. it is given by a rational map
X\dasharrow Z
.

For a similar statement in the Calabi-Yau case (
\lambda=0
), (1) follows from the fact that the maximal destabilizing subsheaf
F
yields an algebraically integrable foliation by the above argument, and moreover its leaves are rationally connected, a contradiction; nevertheless, the known proof of (2) is of an analytic nature, see
[
48
]
. The canonically polarized case is completely open: as far as we know, there is no purely algebraic proof of (1) even for minimal general type surfaces.

In fact, following
[
86
]
(also see
[
36
]
), we can expect a stronger result by looking at the following construction: the image of the class
c_{1}(X)\in{\rm Pic}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q}^{*}
in
H^{1}(X,\Omega_{X}^{[1]})
determines a canonical extension

0\to\Omega_{X}^{[1]}\to\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}\to 0\,.

Problem 13
.

Let
X
be a Fano variety. If
X
is K-semistable (resp. K-polystable), give an algebraic proof of the following: the canonical extension
\mathcal{E}
is semistable (resp. polystable) with respect to the polarization

2.3.
Gibbs stability

14
.

In
[
15
]
, an invariant
\gamma(X)
is introduced to characterize a notion called
Gibbs stability
, which is conjectured to be identical to
\delta(X)
in the log Fano case.

More precisely, for a Fano variety
X
, let
D_{m}
be the divisor on
X^{N_{m}}
, where
N_{m}=H^{0}(-mK_{X})
, given by the pulling back of the vanishing locus of
{\rm Det}(x_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq N_{m}}
under the embedding
f^{N_{m}}\colon X^{N_{m}}\to(\mathbb{P}^{N_{m}-1})^{N_{m}}
. Then one can define an invariant

(4)

\gamma(X)=\liminf_{m}{\rm lct}(X^{N_{m}},\frac{1}{m}D_{m})\,.

This arises from a conjectural picture of constructing the Kähler-Einstein measure from a probability theoretic viewpoint: The condition
\gamma(X)>1
, i.e.
Gibbs stable
, guarantees the measure

\nu^{(N_{m})}:=i^{N^{2}}({\rm det}(S^{(N_{m})}))^{-\frac{1}{m}}\wedge\overline{({\rm det}(S^{(N_{m})}))^{-\frac{1}{m}}}

on
X^{N_{m}}
is of finite mass for
N_{m}\gg 1
, where
S^{(N_{m})}
is the section in
H^{0}(-mK_{X^{N_{m}}})
corresponding to
D_{m}
. So under this condition, one can define the probability measure on
X^{N_{m}}

\mu^{(N_{m})}=\frac{1}{Z_{N_{m}}}\nu^{(N_{m})},\ \ \mbox{where }Z_{N_{m}}:=\int_{X^{N_{m}}}\nu^{(N_{m})}<+\infty\,,

which yields a random point process on
X
with
N_{k}
particles.

The empirical measure of a random point process is

\delta_{N_{m}}:=\frac{1}{N_{m}}\sum^{N_{m}}_{i=1}\delta_{x_{i}}\,,

which defines a map
\delta_{N_{m}}\colon X^{N_{m}}\to\mathcal{M}_{1}(X)
where
\mathcal{M}_{1}(X)
is the space of normalized measure of
X
.
Then the laws of the empirical measures for the measure
\mu^{(N_{m})}
on
X^{N_{m}}
(conjecturally) satisfies a large deviation principle with speed
N_{m}
and rate functional
F(\mu)
. Here
F(\omega^{n}/V)
is Mabuchi’s K-energy of the Kähler form
\omega
(normalized so that
F
vanishes on the normalized measure
{\rm d}V_{\rm KE}
induced by the Kähler-Einstein metric). Roughly speaking, this is

{\rm Prob}_{X^{N_{m}},\mu^{(N_{m})}}\left(\frac{1}{N_{m}}\sum_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}\in B_{\varepsilon}(\mu)\right)\sim e^{-N_{m}F(\mu)}

as first
N_{m}\to+\infty
and then
\varepsilon\to 0
, where
B_{\varepsilon}(\mu)
denotes a ball of radius
\varepsilon
centered at a given probability measure
\mu
in the space
\mathcal{M}_{1}(X)
of all normalized measures
\mu
on
X
.

As a consequence, the empirical measures of the canonical random point processes (conjecturally) converges in probability to the normalized volume form
{\rm d}V_{\rm KE}
for the Kähler-Einstein metric
\omega_{\rm KE}
(see
[
15
,
9
]
).

A similar picture of this perspective is established for
X
with ample
K_{X}
. For Fano manifolds it is largely conjectural, for instance the following is not known.

Conjecture 15

(Berman)
.

For a Fano variety
X
,
\gamma(X)>1
(resp.
\geq 1
) if and only if
X
is K-stable (resp. K-semistable).

One also expects
\delta(X)=\gamma(X)
when
\delta(X)\leq 1
. One inequality
\delta(X)\geq\gamma(X)
is confirmed in
[
39
]
.

3.
Local K-stability

16
.

The starting point of the local K-stability theory is the definition of the normalized volume of valuations as in
[
64
]
. Let
x\in(X={\rm Spec}(R),\Delta)
be a klt singularity germ of dimension
n
. Let
{\rm Val}_{X,x}
be the space of all valuations whose center is
x\in X
. For any
v\in{\rm Val}_{X,x}
, we can define its log discrepancy
A_{X,\Delta}\colon{\rm Val}_{X,x}\to(0,+\infty]
and volume
{\rm vol}(v)=\frac{n!}{k^{n}}\ell(R/\mathfrak{a}_{k})
for
\mathfrak{a}_{k}=\{f\in R\mid v(f)\geq k\}
. Then the key definition is

\widehat{\rm vol}(v)=\begin{cases}A_{X,\Delta}(v)^{n}\cdot{\rm vol}(v),&\text{if }A_{X,\Delta}(v)<+\infty,\\
+\infty,&\text{if }A_{X,\Delta}(v)=+\infty;\end{cases}

and then we define
volume
of a klt singularity
x\in(X,\Delta)
to be

(5)

\widehat{\rm vol}(x,X,\Delta)=\inf_{v\in{\rm Val}_{X,x}}\widehat{\rm vol}(v)\,.

A deep and surprising phenomenon is that
any
klt singularity admits a canonical K-semistable degeneration, constructed using the minimizer of
\widehat{\rm vol}(v)
.

Theorem 17

(Stable degeneration)
.

For any klt singularity
x\in(X,\Delta)
, we have the following

(1)

There exists a quasi-monomial minimizer
v
of
\widehat{\rm vol}(v)
, and the minimizer is unique up to rescaling.

(2)

The graded ring
{\rm Gr}_{v}R
is finitely generated.

(3)

Let
X_{0}={\rm Spec}({\rm Gr}_{v}R)
,
\Delta_{0}
the divisorial degeneration of
\Delta
,
\xi_{v}
the corresponding Reeb vector, then
(X,\Delta,\xi_{v})
is a K-semistable log Fano cone.

For (1), the existence of a quasi-monomial valuation is proved in
[
26
,
94
]
, and the uniqueness is proved in
[
90
,
21
]
. The most difficult part, namely the finite generation (2) is proved in
[
92
]
(see also
[
30
]
). The K-semistability is addressed in
[
63
,
58
,
62
,
61
]
.

Remark 18
.

Together with
[
60
]
, this produces a two-step canonical degeneration of any klt singularity to a K-polystable Fano cone singularity. Such two-step degeneration process, noticed first in
[
35
]
, also exists in other context in K-stability theory. For instance, using
H
-functional arising from the study of Kähler-Ricci soliton,
[
46
]
and
[
22
]
produces a two-step degeneration of any Fano variety
X
to a K-polystable pair
(Y,\xi)
, where
Y
is a Fano variety with a torus
\mathbb{T}
-action and
\xi\in{\rm Hom}(\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathbb{T})\otimes{\mathbb{R}}
.

3.1.
Symplectic singularities and contact Fano varieties

A surprising application of the general framework of Stable Degeneration Theorem
17

[
76
]
, which relies on analytic results. Here we want to post a more general purely algebraic conjectural picture.

19
Symplectic Singularity
[
13
]
.

A normal singularity
x\in X
is called

if its smooth part
X^{\rm reg}=X\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)
carries a closed non-degenerate
2
-form
\omega
whose pullback to any resolution

extends to a holomorphic
2
-form on
X^{\prime}
. If the symplectic singularity
(X,\omega)
admits a good
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action (i.e.

\mathbb{G}_{m}
-orbit), then there exists a positive integer
w
such that
\lambda\cdot\omega=\lambda^{w}\omega
. We call
x\in X
a
conical
symplectic singularity, and
w

4.3.
Positive characteristics

We could define invariants of a similar flavor in positive characteristics. However, unlike normalized volume, a major difference is that a valuative viewpoint (e.g. in (
5

39
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be an
\mathfrak{m}_{x}
-primary ideal on a strongly
F
-regular singularity
x\in(X={\rm Spec}(R),\Delta)
. One can define an analogue of the log canonical threshold (known as the
F
-pure threshold) as

{\rm fpt}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a})=\sup\{t\geq 0\mid(X,\Delta+t\cdot\mathfrak{a})\mbox{ is strongly $F$-regular}\}\,.

Then one can define the
Frobenius volume
(see
[
57
,
65
]
)

{\rm Fvol}(x,X,\Delta)=\inf_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathrm{mult}(\mathfrak{a})\cdot{\rm fpt}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a})^{n}\,.

and
the
Hilbert-Kunz Frobenius volume

{\rm Fvol_{HN}}(x,X,\Delta)=\inf_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathrm{mult}_{\rm HN}(\mathfrak{a})\cdot{\rm fpt}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a})^{n}\,,

where in the infima run through all
\mathfrak{m}_{x}
-primary ideals and
\mathrm{mult}_{\rm HN}(\mathfrak{a})
is the Hilbert-Kunz multiplicity which is defined as

\mathrm{mult}_{\rm HN}(\mathfrak{a})=\lim_{e\to+\infty}\frac{{\rm length}(R/a^{[p^{e}]})}{p^{e\dim(X)}}\mbox{\ \ and \ \ }a^{[p^{e}]}=(f^{p^{e}}\mid f\in\mathfrak{a})\,.

The comparison with the characteristic 0 definition is via an alternative interpretation of volume by
[
71
]
:

\widehat{\rm vol}(x,X,\Delta)=\inf_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathrm{mult}(\mathfrak{a})\cdot{\rm lct}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a})^{n}\,.

Let
x\in(X,\Delta)
be a klt singularity over characteristic 0. For any ideal
\mathfrak{a}
on
X
, let
x_{p}\in(X_{p},\Delta_{p})
and
\mathfrak{a}_{p}
be its reduction mod
p>0
. We have
[
47
]

{\rm lct}_{x}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a})=\lim_{p\to\infty}{\rm fpt}_{x_{p}}(X_{p},\Delta_{p};\mathfrak{a}_{p})\,.

So it is natural to ask the following

Problem 40

(
[
57
, 6.19]
)
.

Do we always have

(8)

\widehat{\rm vol}(x,X,\Delta)=\lim_{p\to\infty}{\rm Fvol}(x_{p},X_{p},\Delta_{p})\,?

In view of
45

) holds. On the other hand, similar to the conjecture in
[
28
]

has a positive lower bound. We can ask whether there exist constants

which only depends on the

Finite degree formula.

In general, a ‘volume’ like function
V
of a singularity
x\in X
should be considered to measure the volume of the ‘link’ of
x\in X
. Therefore, it should satisfy a
finite degree formula
for finite morphism which is étale outside
x
. We can often expect a stronger version if
f\colon(y\in Y)\to(x\in X)
is a finite morphism which is étale in codimension one, i.e
f^{*}(K_{X})=K_{Y}
.
Then it should satisfy

(10)

\displaystyle V(y,Y)={\rm deg}(f)\cdot V(x,X)\,.

This is established for normalized volume
[
90
]
and
F
-signature
[
28
]
. (We note that this is not proved in full generality for
f\colon(y,Y,\Delta_{Y})\to(x,X,\Delta)
with
f^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta)=K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}
when
f
is not necessarily Galois.)

So if
{\rm Fvol}
and
{\rm Fvol_{HN}}
are the right ‘volume-type’ notions, we should expect the following is true.

Problem 43
.

Let
(y\in Y)\to(x\in X)
be a finite morphism which is étale in codimension one. We have

{\rm Fvol}(y,Y)={\rm deg}(f)\cdot{\rm Fvol}(x,X)\mbox{\ \ and \ \ }{\rm Fvol_{HN}}(y,Y)={\rm deg}(f)\cdot{\rm Fvol_{HN}}(x,X)\,.

One consequence of Theorem
17
is that for any klt singularity
x\in X
any volume
\widehat{\rm vol}(x\in X)
is an algebraic number (see
[
92
]
). So we can ask the following question.

Problem 44

Is
{\rm Fvol}(x,X)
an algebraic number?

We do not have reason to believe the above question has an affirmative answer, but a counterexample will probably shed light on the difference between volumes and
F

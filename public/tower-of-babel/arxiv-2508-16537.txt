Global existence and uniqueness for Hibler’s visco-plastic sea-ice model Thanks: The authors gratefully acknowledge the financial support by DFG project FOR 5528.
Stefan Dingel and Karoline Disser

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove global existence and uniqueness of weak solutions to the momentum equations of Hibler’s visco-plastic model for the dynamics of the arctic sea-ice covers. Although Hibler’s model is standardly used in global climate simulations, there are only few rigorous mathematical results so far that mainly concern local-in-time well-posedness of globally regularized variants. Here, we consider Hibler’s original model with local cut-off for arbitrarily small and large strain rates. Degeneracy and plasticity of the stress tensor hold in this range.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Hibler’s sea-ice model

The arctic sea-ice covers strongly impact global climate. Modelling and simulation of their dynamics is a very challenging topic due to their complex material and thermodynamical behaviour, strong coupling to oceanic and atmospheric conditions and the difficulty of obtaining relevant data,
[
GBC+20
]
,
[
HABea20
]
. For modeling and computational reasons, Hibler’s visco-plastic sea-ice rheology
[
Hib79
]
is used as a standard in global climate models. At the same time, rigorous mathematical analysis of Hibler’s model is still largely missing.

The topic has recently gained increasing attention
[
BDHDH22
,
LTT22
,
BH23
,
BBH24b
]
, but existence of global solutions remains to be an open problem, and results so far concern local well-posedness and regularized versions of Hibler’s model. We also refer to
[
BLTT25
]
, where global existence of the related elasto-visco-plastic (EVP) model was proved, assuming a Kelvin-Voigt regularization.

Here, we prove the first result on global existence of solutions for Hibler’s sea-ice momentum balance, given by

(1.1)

\mathrm{m}(\dot{u}+u\cdot\nabla u)-\mathrm{div}(\sigma)=\mathrm{m}\omega u^{\perp}+\tau_{\mathrm{ocean}}(u)+\tau_{\mathrm{atm}}-\mathrm{m}\mathrm{g}\nabla H+f,

where, up to any time
T>0
,
u\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{2}

denotes the unknown velocity of the sea-ice cover on a bounded Lipschitz
domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and mass
\mathrm{m}>0
,
Coriolis parameter
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
, and gravity
g>0

are given constants, and
\tau_{\mathrm{ocean}},\tau_{\mathrm{atm}},H
and
f
are
forces due to external conditions (details below). Here,
v^{\perp}=(-v_{2},v_{1})^{\top}
denotes the vector perpendicular to
v=(v_{1},v_{2})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
On the boundary
\partial\Omega
, we assume homogenous Dirichlet
boundary conditions.
In Hibler’s model, the visco-plastic stress tensor

\sigma=\sigma(Du)=\sigma(P,Du)=\frac{P}{2}\left[\frac{\lambda\left(\mathrm{Dev}\,Du\right)+\left(\mathrm{div}\,u\right)\mathrm{Id}}{\delta(Du)}-\mathrm{Id}\right]

is derived from the (plastic) tensor

\sigma_{p}(P,Du)=\begin{cases}\frac{P}{2}\left[\frac{\lambda\left(\mathrm{Dev}\,Du\right)+\left(\mathrm{tr}\,Du\right)\mathrm{Id}}{\sqrt{\lambda|\mathrm{Dev}\,Du|^{2}+(\mathrm{tr}\,Du)^{2}}}-\mathrm{Id}\right],&\text{if }Du\neq 0,\\
0,&\text{if }Du=0,\end{cases}

by applying a (local) cut-off to the strain rate in the denominator, see (
1.2
) below.
Here,

Du:=\mathrm{sym}\,\nabla u:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{\top}\right)

denotes the symmetric part of the gradient of
u
, and the constant
\lambda=\frac{2}{\bar{e}^{2}}<1
is derived from the
parameter
\bar{e}\approx 2
in Hibler’s model that describes the ratio
of ellipticity of the yield curve. For the analysis, it is only required
that
\bar{e}>0
. The function
P\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\to[P_{\bullet},\infty)

is a given parameter of local ice strength. In the following, for given
matrices
z,\tilde{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
with coefficients

z_{ij},\tilde{z}_{ij},i,j\in\{1,2\}
, we use the short-cuts

\mathrm{tr}\,z=z_{11}+z_{22},\qquad\mathrm{Dev}\,z=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}z_{11}-z_{22}&z_{12}+z_{21}\\
z_{12}+z_{21}&z_{22}-z_{11}\end{array}\right),

and the Frobenius norm
|z|
induced by the scalar product

z:\tilde{z}:=\sum_{i,j}z_{ij}\tilde{z}_{ij}.

The
visco-plasticity

of the stress tensor
\sigma
is due to the inverse viscosity function

(1.2)

\delta\colon\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}\ni z\mapsto\begin{cases}\delta^{\bullet},&\delta_{p}(z)\geq\delta^{\bullet},\\
\delta_{p}(z):=\sqrt{\lambda|\mathrm{Dev}\,z|^{2}+(\mathrm{tr}\,z)^{2}},&\delta_{\bullet}\leq\delta_{p}(z)\leq\delta^{\bullet},\\
\delta_{\bullet},&\delta_{\bullet}\geq\delta_{p}(z),\end{cases}

with cut-off at an arbitrarily large fixed value
\delta^{\bullet}>0

and at an arbitrarily small fixed value
\delta_{\bullet}>0
. Examples
of explicit values for
\delta_{\bullet},\delta^{\bullet}
are given in
[
Hib79
]
.

Properties of
\sigma
and
\sigma_{p}

The (degenerate) plastic stress tensor
\sigma_{p}
has several distinct
properties that are difficult to deal with in numerical simulation
and analysis:

(1)

degeneracy:
if
\mathrm{Dev}\,z=0\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
, then a
direct calculation shows that
\sigma_{p}(z)=0
, i.e the material
becomes (locally) stress-free. This was intended by Hibler due to
the high resistance of sea ice to compression.

(2)

plasticity:
the equality

(1.3)

\frac{1}{4}|\mathrm{tr}\,(\sigma_{p}(z)+\frac{P}{2}\mathrm{Id})|^{2}+\frac{1}{\lambda}|\mathrm{Dev}\,(\sigma_{p}(z)+\frac{P}{2}\mathrm{Id})|^{2}=\frac{P^{2}}{4},\quad\text{for all }z\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2},

shows that the principle stresses (the eigenvalues of
\sigma_{p}
)
are normed to an elliptic yield curve. This independence of stresses
of the strain rate is desirable in general, particularly for the small
rates
\delta_{p}
that are expected here, but it can also be viewed
as an arbitrarily large viscosity occuring as
\delta_{p}\to 0
. This
is the main reason why Hibler suggests a cut-off in
\delta
. He also suggests to use a value

\delta_{\bullet}
well below what will appear in simulations.

(3)

discontinuity:
clearly,
\sigma_{p}
is discontinuous at
z=0
.

(4)

non-coercivity:
this is a mathematical issue associated to
(
1.3
): it is not true that
|\sigma_{p}(z)|\to\infty

as
z\to\infty
(weak coercivity), so there are no straightforward
a-priori estimates, e.g. on an Galerkin or other type of approximation.

Hibler’s stress tensor
\sigma
, which uses the modified strain rate

\delta
in (
1.2
), has the following properties:

(1)

degeneracy is still present because
\sigma(z)=0
remains true as long
as
\mathrm{Dev}\,z=0
and
\delta_{\bullet}\leq|\mathrm{tr}\,z|\leq\delta^{\bullet}
.

(2)

Equality (
1.3
) also remains true for the typical values

\delta_{\bullet}\leq\delta_{p}(z)\leq\delta^{\bullet}
. Otherwise, principle
stresses remain constrained within the ellipse (
\delta_{p}(z)\leq\delta_{\bullet}
)
or increase linearly in
|z|
(
\delta^{\bullet}\leq\delta_{p}(z)
).

(3)

The cut-off for small
\delta_{p}(z)
turns
\sigma
into a continuous
function. It is however not differentiable at
\delta_{\bullet},\delta^{\bullet}
and thus
often referred to as
non-smooth
.

(4)

Due to the cut-off at large values of
\delta_{p}
, the operator
\mathrm{div}\,\sigma(D\cdot)\colon H_{0}^{1}\to H^{-1}

is coercive in a suitable sense, see Lemma
4
.

To summarize, one can say that Hibler’s stress tensor with local cut-off
respects the two key material properties (
1
) and (
2
) in an arbitrarily
large range of parameters, but helps to solve mathematical issues
associated to the plastic tensor
\sigma_{p}
by improving on (
3
)
and (
4
). This is an advantage with respect to other,
global
regularizations
or modifications used in the literature. For example, the proof of
local well-posedness in
[
BDHDH22
]
and
[
LTT22
]
relies on the
\varepsilon
-regularization
of
\delta_{p}
to

(1.4)

\delta_{p}^{\varepsilon}(z):=\sqrt{\varepsilon+\lambda|\mathrm{Dev}\,z|^{2}+(\mathrm{tr}\,z)^{2}}.

Although
\delta_{p}^{\varepsilon}
gives a better fit to
\delta_{p}
than

\delta
at large
z
, it destroys properties
(
1
) and (
2
) and induces a change of type in turning the momentum
equations into a quasilinear parabolic system,
[
BDHDH22
]
.
In numerical analysis, typically both
\varepsilon
-regularization
and cut-off are used
[
MK21
]
. Our main result here extends to this case, see Subsection
2.5
.
The proof of global existence for the related
EVP model in
[
BLTT25
]
uses the global Kelvin-Voigt
\alpha
-regularization in
a dynamic formulation for
\sigma
. Using
\alpha>0
,
it is shown that it is possible to pass to the limit
\varepsilon\to 0

in the additional
\varepsilon
-regularization. However,

\alpha>0
changes the type of the original elasto-visco-plastic
formulation (which seems to be ill-posed if
\alpha=0
).

Properties (
2
) - (
4
) of
\sigma_{p}
are shared
by the total variation flow associated to the 1-Laplacian
[
ABCM01
]
.
It is not yet well-studied as a system that uses the symmetric gradient
only, or in a degenerate setting, or including additional non-linearities, see
[
BCE25
]
and references therein for recent results.
Hibler’s modification by local cut-off provides a particular form
of approximation that preserves (
1
), (
2
), locally, unlike global approximations by
\varepsilon>0
,
[
FP03
]
,
p>1
,
[
GK19
]
, or added viscosity, used for the related model of Bingham fluids
[
DL76
]
. Investigations of the singular limits
\delta_{\bullet}\to 0,\delta^{\bullet}\to\infty

may be the subject of future work.

External forces

In (
1.1
),

(1.5)

\tau_{\mathrm{ocean}}=\tau_{\mathrm{ocean}}(u)=c_{\mathrm{ocean}}|U_{\mathrm{ocean}}-u|(U_{\mathrm{ocean}}-u)_{\theta}

refers to a typical forcing of the ice sheet due to the given geostrophic
ocean current
U_{\mathrm{ocean}}
, where
c_{\mathrm{ocean}}\geq 0
is a given
constant and
\theta
denotes the water turning angle. Here, for
all
v\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
v_{\theta}=\cos\theta v+\sin\theta v^{\perp}

denotes the vector turned by angle
\theta
.
For the analysis, we assume
0\leq\theta\leq\frac{\pi}{4}
and note that this is a standard assumption
in the literature,
[
HTC+19
]
. For simplicity of notation, we
write

h:=\tau_{\mathrm{atm}}-\mathrm{m}\mathrm{g}\nabla H+f,

where

\tau_{\mathrm{atm}}
denotes forcing due to air flow above the ice
sheet but is typically assumed to be indenpendent of
u
, as is the
force
-\mathrm{m}\mathrm{g}\nabla H
due to varying height
H

of the sea surface, cf.
[
Hib79
]
. The term
f
can be used to collect additional external forces. For typical values of physical parameters in this model, see
[
Hib79
]
or e.g. Table 1 in
[
MDL+21
]
.

Convection term

The term
\mathrm{m}(u\cdot\nabla u)
is typically omitted in simulations and analysis
due to its smallness
[
BLTT25
,
MDL+21
]
. Viewed as a Lipschitzian semilinearity, this term can be included in our analysis by a fixed-point-argument, either locally in time or for small initial data. However, its global estimates would depend on a coherent modelling of the transport of mass (as in compressible
flows), or on incompressibility. In future work, we will investigate the coupled thermodynamic aspects of sea ice dynamics associated to Hibler’s model, with varying coupled ice strength
P
and mass
\mathrm{m}
, and include convection in this context.

1.2.
Main result

Let
V:=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
denote the usual first-order Hilbert
Sobolev space with zero boundary trace. Then

V\overset{c}{\hookrightarrow}H:=L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\simeq H^{*}\overset{c}{\hookrightarrow}V^{*},

with
V^{*}=H^{-1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
the dual space of
V
, forms a
Gelfand triple with compact dense embeddings. The main result is the
following.

Theorem 1
.

Let
T>0
and
u_{0}\in H
be given. Assume that

\displaystyle U_{\mathrm{ocean}}

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;L^{4}),

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle\in L^{2}(0,T;V^{*}),

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega;[P_{\bullet},\infty)),\quad\text{for some }P_{\bullet}>0.

Then there exists a unique weak solution

u\in C([0,T];H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;V),\quad\dot{u}\in L^{2}(0,T;V^{*})

of (
1.1
) with initial value
u(0)=u_{0}
.

The two papers

]

-regularizations, see also

Global existence of solutions for large data was shown recently for the (

]

[

2.
Proof of Theorem
1

2.1.
Hibler’s operator

We are looking for a solution
u
to the weak momentum balance

\langle\mathrm{m}\dot{u}(t),v\rangle+a(t,u(t),v)+g(t,u(t),v)+c(u(t),v)=\langle h(t),v\rangle,

for almost all
t\in(0,T)
and all
v\in V
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

denotes the dual pairing for
V^{*}\times V
,

\displaystyle a(t,u,v)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}\sigma(P(t),Du):Dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\frac{P(t)}{2}\left[\frac{\lambda\mathrm{Dev}\,Du:\mathrm{Dev}\,Dv+\mathrm{div}\,u\,\mathrm{div}\,v}{\delta(Du)}-\mathrm{div}\,v\right]

denotes the form
a\colon[0,T]\times V\times V\to\mathbb{R}
associated to
Hiber’s stress tensor, and

(2.1)

g(t,u,v):=-\int_{\Omega}\tau_{\mathrm{ocean}}(u)\cdot v,\qquad c(u,v):=-\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{m}\omega u^{\perp}\cdot v,

are the forms corresponding to the external forces that depend on

u
.
We consider Hibler’s operator given by

A(t)\colon V\to V^{*},\qquad\langle A(t)u,v\rangle:=a(t,u,v),

in the framework of monotone operators. As a reference, we also define

\displaystyle a_{p}(t,u,v)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}\sigma_{p}(P(t),Du):Dv,

as the form corresponding to the plastic stress tensor
\sigma_{p}

without cut-off and the corresponding operator

A_{p}(t)\colon V\to V^{*},\qquad\langle A_{p}(t)u,v\rangle:=a_{p}(t,u,v).

Lemma 2
.

The operators
A(t)
are monotone
and hemicontinuous, i.e. for all
t\in(0,T)
,

(2.2)

\langle A(t)u-A(t)v,u-v\rangle\geq 0,\qquad\text{for all }u,v\in V,

and for all
u,v,w\in V
, the map

(2.3)

[0,1]\ni h\mapsto a(t,u+hv,w)

is continuous. Furthermore, the operators
A_{p}(t)
are monotone.

Proof.

To shorten the notation, for all
u\in V
, set

D^{\lambda}u:=\lambda\mathrm{Dev}\,Du+(\mathrm{div}\,u)\mathrm{Id}.

Let
u,v\in V
be given. By definition and using Cauchy-Schwarz,

(2.4)

D^{\lambda}u:Du=\delta_{p}^{2}(Du),\qquad\text{and }D^{\lambda}u:Dv\leq\delta_{p}(Du)\delta_{p}(Dv).

By assumption,
P(t)\geq 0
. Using (
2.4
), the calculations are straightforward,

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\langle A(t)u-A(t)v,u-v\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\frac{P(t)}{2}\left(\frac{D^{\lambda}u:Du}{\delta(Du)}-\frac{D^{\lambda}u:Dv}{\delta(Du)}-\frac{D^{\lambda}v:Du}{\delta(Dv)}+\frac{D^{\lambda}v:Dv}{\delta(Dv)}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}\frac{P(t)}{2}\left(\delta_{p}(Du)-\delta_{p}(Dv)\right)\left(f(\delta_{p}(Du))-f(\delta_{p}(Dv))\right),

where
f(\delta_{p}(Du)):=\frac{\delta_{p}(Du)}{\delta(Du)}
is an increasing function in terms of
\delta_{p}(Du)
, so that the monotonicity of
A(t)
directly follows from the monotonicity of
f
. Monotonicity of
A_{p}(t)
follows in the same way, where
f
would be replaced by
f_{p}\equiv 1
.

For the proof of (
2.3
), note that as
u,v,w
are chosen
arbitrarily in the vector space
V
, it is sufficient to check continuity
in one point, for example, in
h=0
. Given
h_{n}\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0
,
consider

(2.5)

\displaystyle|a(t,u+h_{n}v,w)-a(t,u,w)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{P(t)}{2}\left[|D^{\lambda}u:Dw|\left|\frac{1}{\delta(Du+h_{n}Dv)}-\frac{1}{\delta(Du)}\right|+h_{n}\frac{\left|D^{\lambda}v:Dw\right|}{\delta(Du+h_{n}Dv)}\right]

The continuity of
z\mapsto\delta(z)
and the strict positivity,
\delta(z)\geq\delta_{\bullet}>0
,
imply that the integrands converge pointwise a.e. We can use

(2.6)

\lambda|\mathrm{sym}\,z|^{2}=\lambda|\mathrm{Dev}\,z|^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}(\mathrm{tr}\,z)^{2}\leq\lambda|\mathrm{Dev}\,z|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{tr}\,z)^{2}=\delta_{p}^{2}(z)\leq|\mathrm{sym}\,z|^{2},

(here,
\lambda\leq 1
, otherwise, only the constants would be different),
and both Korn’s and Poincaré’s inequalities to obtain equivalence
of norms,

(2.7)

\|u\|_{V}^{2}\lesssim\|Du\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim\int_{\Omega}\delta_{p}^{2}(Du)\lesssim\|Du\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim\|u\|_{V}^{2}.

Thus, convergence to
0
of the integral in (
2.5
) follows
from dominated convergence using (
2.4
) and again the strict
positivity of
\delta
.
∎

Remark 3
.

Clearly,
A(t)
is not strictly monotone. As an example, for every
u\in V
such that
\delta(Du)\in(\delta_{\bullet},\delta^{\bullet})
almost everywhere,
there is an
\varepsilon>0
such that for
v:=(1+\varepsilon)u
, we also have
\delta(Dv)\in(\delta_{\bullet},\delta^{\bullet})
. In this case,
\langle A(t)u-A(t)v,u-v\rangle=0
.

Lemma 4
.

Uniformly in
t\in(0,T]
, the operators
A(t)

are coercive with

(2.8)

\langle A(t)u,u\rangle\geq c_{\bullet}\|u\|_{V}^{2}-C_{\bullet},\qquad\text{for all }u\in V,

and satisfy the growth bound

(2.9)

\|A(t)u\|_{V^{*}}\leq C^{\bullet}+c^{\bullet}\|u\|_{V},\qquad\text{for all }u\in V.

Moreover, the function

(0,T)\ni t\mapsto\langle A(t)u,v\rangle

is measurable for all
u,v\in V
.

Proof.

For all
u,v\in V
, using (
2.4
), (
2.6
) and (
2.7
),
we obtain

a(t,u,u)\geq\frac{1}{2\delta^{\bullet}}\int_{\Omega}P(t)\left(\delta_{p}^{2}(Du)-\delta^{\bullet}|\mathrm{div}\,u|\right)\geq\frac{c(\Omega)P_{\bullet}\lambda}{2\delta^{\bullet}}\|u\|_{V}^{2}-\frac{{\delta^{\bullet}}^{2}\|P(t)\|_{\infty}}{2(1-\lambda)},

and

|a(t,u,v)|\leq C(\Omega,\lambda)\|P(t)\|_{\infty}\left(1+\frac{1}{\delta_{\bullet}}\|u\|_{V}\right)\|v\|_{V}.

The (weak) measurability of
A(\cdot)
is implied by
P\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))
.
∎

2.2.
Coriolis term

Due to

c(u,u-v)-c(v,u-v)=0,\quad c(u,u)=0,

and

c(u,v)\leq\mathrm{m}|\mathrm{\omega}|C(\Omega)\|u\|_{V}\|v\|_{V},

the operators

A(t)+C\colon V\to V^{*},\,\,\langle\left(A(t)+C\right)u,v\rangle=a(t,u,v)+c(u,v),

are also monotone, hemicontinuous, coercive, and satisfy the growth condition (
2.9
).

Remark 5
.

The properties above also hold for non-constant
\mathrm{\omega}\in L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{r}(\Omega))
, with
r>1
.

2.3.
Oceanic forces

It remains to study the time-dependent family of operators

t\mapsto G(t)\colon V\to V^{*},\qquad\langle G(t)u,v\rangle:=-c_{\mathrm{ocean}}\int_{\Omega}|U_{\mathrm{ocean}}(t)-u|(U_{\mathrm{ocean}}(t)-u)_{\theta}\cdot v,

associated to the oceanic currents that force the system.

Lemma 6
.

The family of operators
G(t)\colon V\to V^{*}

is well-defined and measurable w.r.t. time. Furthermore, for every
\varepsilon>0
there are functions
U_{\bullet}\in L^{1}(0,T;[0,\infty))
,
U^{\bullet}\in L^{2}(0,T;[0,\infty))
and a constant
c^{G}>0
, such that the operators
G(t)
are hemicontinous and satisfy

(2.10)

\langle G(t)u,u\rangle\geq-\varepsilon\|u\|_{V}^{2}-U_{\bullet}(t),\qquad\text{for all }u\in V,

as well as the growth bound

(2.11)

\|G(t)u\|_{V^{*}}\leq U^{\bullet}(t)+c^{G}\|u\|_{V}\|u\|_{H},\qquad\text{for all }u\in V.

Proof.

The family
t\mapsto G(t)
is clearly well-defined and (weakly) measurable. Hemicontinuity and
(
2.11
) follow from
the estimate

\int_{\Omega}|U_{\mathrm{ocean}}(t)-u|(U_{\mathrm{ocean}}(t)-u)_{\theta}\cdot v\\
\leq 2(\|U_{\mathrm{ocean}}(t)\|_{H}\|U_{\mathrm{ocean}}(t)\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{H}\|u\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)})\|v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}.

Using that
\cos\theta>0
, the bound in (
2.10
) follows from

\displaystyle\langle G(t)u,u\rangle

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}|U_{\mathrm{ocean}}(t)-u|(U_{\mathrm{ocean}})_{\theta}\cdot u+\int_{\Omega}|U_{\mathrm{ocean}}(t)-u|(\cos\theta)|u|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{4\cos\theta}\int_{\Omega}|U_{\mathrm{ocean}}(t)-u||U_{\mathrm{ocean}}(t)|^{2},

and the integrability assumptions on
U_{\mathrm{ocean}}
.
∎

Lemma 7
.

If
0\leq\theta\leq\frac{\pi}{4}
, then the operators

G(t)
are monotone.

Proof.

Pointwise, we prove the non-negativity of the integrand in

\langle G(t)u-G(t)v,u-v\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\begin{aligned} &\cos\theta\left(|U_{\mathrm{ocean}}(t)-u|^{3}+|U_{\mathrm{ocean}}(t)-u|^{3}\right)\\
&-|U_{\mathrm{ocean}}(t)-u|\left(U_{\mathrm{ocean}}(t)-u\right)_{\theta}\left(U_{\mathrm{ocean}}(t)-v\right)\\
&-|U_{\mathrm{ocean}}(t)-v|\left(U_{\mathrm{ocean}}(t)-v\right)_{\theta}\left(U_{\mathrm{ocean}}(t)-u\right).\end{aligned}

To simplify notation, for every
x\in\Omega
, set
a:=U_{\mathrm{ocean}}(t,x)-u(x)
,

b:=U_{\mathrm{ocean}}(t,x)-v(x)
and
\varphi\in[0,\pi]
as the angle between

a
and
b
. Furthermore, define
\alpha:=|a|=|a_{\theta}|
and
\beta:=|b|=|b_{\theta}|
.
Then

\displaystyle a_{\theta}\cdot b

\displaystyle=\cos\theta\cos\varphi\,\alpha\beta+\sin\theta\sin\varphi\,\alpha\beta,

\displaystyle b_{\theta}\cdot a

\displaystyle=\cos\theta\cos\varphi\,\alpha\beta-\sin\theta\sin\varphi\,\alpha\beta,

and thus, the integrand can be expressed as the polynomial

P(\alpha,\beta):=\cos\theta\left(\alpha^{3}+\beta^{3}-\cos\varphi\alpha\beta(\alpha+\beta)-\tan\theta\sin\varphi\alpha\beta(\alpha-\beta)\right).

We show that
P(\alpha,\beta)\geq 0
for
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
. This is clearly true if
\beta=0
.
Otherwise, we can divide by
\frac{1}{\cos\theta\beta^{3}}
and consider the
rescaled polynomial

p(\gamma):=\gamma^{3}-(\cos\varphi+\tan\theta\sin\varphi)\gamma^{2}+(-\cos\varphi+\tan\theta\sin\varphi)\gamma+1.

The discriminant of
p
, written in terms of
T:=\tan^{2}\theta

and
S:=\cos\varphi
with
\sin\varphi=\sqrt{1-S^{2}}
is

d(S,T):=(1-S^{2})^{2}T^{2}+(-2S^{2}+24S-18)(1-S^{2})T+(S^{4}+8S^{3}+18S^{2}-27).

Due to the condition
0\leq\theta\leq\frac{\pi}{4}
, we only need to
check
0\leq T\leq 1
and
-1\leq S\leq 1
. If
S=1
, then
P(\alpha,\beta)=\cos\theta(\alpha-\beta)^{2}(\alpha+\beta)\geq 0
.
At the
T
-boundaries of the relevant range,
d(S,0)<0
, and

d(S,1)=4S^{4}-16S^{3}+32S^{2}+24S-44<0,

so
d(S,T)<0
for all
0<T<1
, because
d
is quadratic in
T

and the leading coefficient is positive. This means that
p
has
only one real root. Due to
p(0)=1>0
and
p(\lambda)\to\infty

as
\lambda\to\infty
, the real root must be negative. We have shown
that
p>0
, which implies
P>0
and thus, the monotonicity of
G(t)
.
∎

Remark 8
.

Lemmas
6
and
7
remain true for non-constant

\theta\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega;[0,\frac{\pi}{4}])).

2.4.
A-priori estimates and stability

Theorem
1
is obtained by Galerkin approximation and using
monotonicity through Minty’s trick. We refer e.g. to the monograph
of Zeidler
[
Zei90
]

and references therein. Here, we also need the result of Liu
[
Liu11
]
on pseudomonotonicity for specific semilinear terms, in order to deal with the oceanic forcing. If
\tau_{\mathrm{ocean}}=0
, then by Lemmas

2
and
4
, the hypothesis of Theorem
30.A in
[
Zei90
]

are verified and the result follows directly.
More precisely, it is shown that there
exists a unique solution

u\in C([0,T];H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;V),\dot{u}\in L^{2}(0,T;V^{*}),

with initial value
u(0)=u_{0}
of the equation

\dot{u}(t)+(A(t)+C)u(t)=h(t)\in V^{*},

for a.a.
t\in(0,T)
, and
u
satisfies
the corresponding a-priori estimate

\|u\|_{C([0,T];H)}+\frac{c_{\bullet}}{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,T;V)}^{2}\leq C_{\bullet}+\|u_{0}\|_{H}+c(c_{\bullet})\|h\|_{L^{2}(0,T;V^{*})}^{2}.

If
\tau_{\mathrm{ocean}}\neq 0
, then by Lemmas
6
and
7
, the corresponding family of operators

t\mapsto A(t)+C+G(t):V\to V^{*}

satisfies the hypotheses (H1)-(H4) in the paper by Liu,
[
Liu11
]
, thus also proving Theorem

1
. In addition to uniqueness, by Theorem 1.2 in
[
Liu11
]
, well-posedness holds in the sense that

(2.12)

\|u_{1}(t)-u_{2}(t)|_{H}^{2}\leq\|u_{1,0}-u_{2,0}\|^{2}_{H}+\int_{0}^{t}\|h_{1}(s)-h_{2}(s)\|^{2}_{H},

for all solutions
u_{1},u_{2}
corresponding to initial data
u_{1,0},u_{2,0}\in H
and right-hand-sides

(2.13)

h_{1},h_{2}\in L^{2}(0,T;H),

respectively. Note that the requirement (
2.13
) is slightly stronger than the assumption
h\in L^{2}(0,T;V^{*})
in Theorem
1
.

2.5.
Hibler’s operator with
\varepsilon
-regularization

A typical global regularization of Hibler’s operator is given by replacing
\delta_{p}
with
\delta^{\varepsilon}_{p}
defined in (
1.4
). The corresponding operators

A_{p}^{\varepsilon}(t)\colon V\to V^{*},\;\langle A^{\varepsilon}_{p}(t)u,v\rangle:=\int_{\Omega}\frac{P(t)}{2}\left(\frac{D^{\lambda}u:Dv}{\delta_{p}^{\varepsilon}(Du)}-\mathrm{div}\,v\right),

are monotone, hemicontinuous and clearly also satisfy a growth bound of type (
2.9
). The monotonicity follows as for
A(t)
, where in the proof,
f
would be replaced by the monotone function
f_{p}^{\varepsilon}(x):=x/\sqrt{\varepsilon+x^{2}}
. The hemicontinuity also follows analogously due to the strict positivity of
\delta_{p}^{\varepsilon}\geq\sqrt{\varepsilon}>0
.
On the other hand,
A_{p}^{\varepsilon}(t)
is not coercive. This can be changed by introducing a cut-off at large values,

\delta_{p}^{\varepsilon,\bullet}(z):=\max\{\delta_{p}^{\varepsilon}(z),\delta^{\bullet}\},

and is also true in case of a cut-off on both sides, with
\delta_{p}
in (
1.2
) replaced by
\delta^{\varepsilon}_{p}
,

\delta^{\varepsilon}\colon\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}\ni z\mapsto\begin{cases}\delta^{\bullet},&\delta^{\varepsilon}_{p}(z)\geq\delta^{\bullet},\\
\delta^{\varepsilon}_{p}(z),&\delta_{\bullet}\leq\delta^{\varepsilon}_{p}(z)\leq\delta^{\bullet},\\
\delta_{\bullet},&\delta_{\bullet}\geq\delta^{\varepsilon}_{p}(z),\end{cases}

which is standard in numerical simulations
[
MK21
]
. So in both cases, using either
\delta_{p}^{\varepsilon,\bullet}
or
\delta^{\varepsilon}
instead of
\delta
, Theorem
1
also holds.

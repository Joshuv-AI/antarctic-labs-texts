Dynamic changes and heterogeneity in technical efficiency of broiler production under African swine fever shocks: Evidence from production organization and farm size
Authors: Donghao Guo, Xinyue Zhuo, Hua Pu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAfrican swine fever (ASF) has reshaped meat demand by reducing pork supply, driving up pork prices, and triggering substitution in consumption, thereby creating a useful setting for examining changes in technical efficiency in the broiler sector. In recent years, China’s broiler industry has become increasingly contract-based and larger in scale. However, under ASF shock, the effects of substitution demand on technical efficiency across different production arrangements and farm-size categories remain insufficiently understood. Using data from China’s broiler industry for 2017–2024, this study applies a stochastic frontier analysis (SFA) framework to examine the impact of ASF-induced changes in substitution demand on broiler production technical efficiency and its heterogeneity. Three main findings emerge. First, technical efficiency in broiler production exhibited clear stage-specific variation, falling to a period low of 0.913 in 2018, then recovering and peaking at 0.959 in 2021, before declining again thereafter. Second, average technical efficiency was higher for contract growers (0.939) than for non-contract growers (0.924). However, during the adjustment process, efficiency gains among contract growers were smaller, and the subsequent decline was larger, than those among non-contract growers. Third, from 2018 to 2021, efficiency gains across farm-size groups followed an inverted U-shaped pattern, with the largest increase observed among medium-scale operations and the smallest among extra-large-scale operations. From 2021 to 2024, the decline followed a U-shaped pattern, with the smallest decrease among medium-scale operations and the largest among small-scale operations. These results indicate that, under ASF shock, substitution demand does not automatically translate into sustained improvements in broiler production efficiency; rather, its effects are jointly shaped by production organization, farm-size structure, and producers’ adjustment capacity. This study reveals the dynamic patterns and heterogeneous responses of technical efficiency in China’s broiler industry under ASF shock, and provides evidence relevant to optimizing industry organization and strengthening supply resilience.Keywords: African swine fever, Broiler production, Technical efficiency, Contract farming, Farm size

Introduction

African swine fever (ASF) has been a major exogenous shock reshaping China’s meat market in recent years. Following the ASF outbreak in 2018, hog supply contracted sharply, pork prices fluctuated substantially, and risks in the meat market increased. The shock quickly extended beyond internal adjustment within the pig industry and spread through the broader meat supply system via price transmission and consumption substitution (Acosta et al., 2023). In terms of meat output structure, the substitution of poultry meat under the ASF shock showed a clear stage-specific pattern. From 2018 to 2020, the share of poultry meat in total meat output increased from 23.4% to 30.9%, whereas the share of pork declined from 63.4% to 53.8%. Over the same period, the gap between the shares of pork and poultry meat narrowed from 40.0% to 22.9% (Fig. 1; China’s Bureau of Statistics of China). The Fig. 1 shows that the post-ASF decline in the pork share and the rise in the poultry share were not confined to 2018–2021. Although the pork share increased slightly in 2022, it declined again thereafter, whereas the poultry share continued to rise during 2022–2024, indicating the growing importance of poultry in China’s meat supply structure. These changes indicate that poultry meat served as an important alternative source of animal protein during the period when hog supply contraction and pork price increases were most pronounced.

Driven jointly by sharp fluctuations in pork prices and upgrading dietary preferences, consumption of livestock and poultry products shifted further toward broiler meat, which offers stronger cost competitiveness, lower fat content, and a shorter production cycle (Xin et al., 2024). These changes suggest that the economic consequences of ASF went far beyond a contraction in pork supply alone; rather, ASF acted as a systemic shock that was transmitted to alternative protein sectors through price revaluation, consumer substitution, and linkages in input markets (Ward and Inouye, 2019).

From the perspective of the global meat supply structure, poultry meat has assumed an increasingly strategic position. According to FAO data, global poultry meat production reached 120.5 million tonnes in 2017, surpassing pig meat and becoming the world’s most widely produced meat type; it also became the most widely traded meat category interChina’sly. By 2034, the OECD–FAO Agricultural Outlook projects global poultry meat production to increase by 15 million tonnes from the base period, accounting for 62% of the projected increase in total meat output, with China expected to be among the major countries contributing most strongly to global poultry production growth (OECD and FAO, 2025). Against the backdrop of an accelerating restructuring of global animal-protein supply, the broiler industry is no longer merely a substitute sector responding to pork-market shocks. It has also become an important lens through which to examine food-system resilience, the stability of protein supply, and the efficiency of agricultural resource allocation.

With the expansion of the industry, the organizational forms and scale structure of broiler farming in China have continued to adjust (Xin et al., 2025). From 2017 to 2024, the farm-size structure of broiler production showed a gradual shift from small-scale operations toward large- and extra-large farms. The share of small-scale farms declined from 53% to 46%, whereas the shares of large- and extra-large farms increased from 6% and 2% to 12% and 4%, respectively, indicating a continued increase in the degree of scale expansion in broiler production in China (Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs of China). Contract farming, as an important organizational form, is typically arranged by slaughtering and processing firms or leading agribusiness enterprises, which provide chicks, feed, veterinary drugs, vaccines, and technical services in a unified manner. Farmers then organize production according to standardized procedures, and finished broilers are purchased by the firm under pre-agreed terms (Hoang, 2021). This organizational restructuring not only changes production practices, but also reshapes the channels through which market signals are transmitted to the farm level (Mao et al., 2019; Arouna et al., 2021). Because farms of different sizes and production modes differ in input security, access to technical services, financing capacity, and price-response mechanisms, their ability to absorb and adjust to the same external shock may vary substantially. Therefore, evaluating industry development solely on the basis of output growth or market-share expansion is insufficient; it is also necessary to examine, from the perspective of technical efficiency, whether external market opportunities can be translated into actual improvements in farm performance.

Technical efficiency (TE) refers to a producer’s ability to attain the maximum possible output from a given set of inputs under existing technology (Meeusen and van Den Broeck, 1977). As broiler production continues to face constraints such as unstable input quality, insufficient technical services, and high market risk (Hong et al., 2019; Ahimbisibwe et al., 2024), TE not only captures resource-use and production capacity, but also serves as a key indicator for assessing whether external shocks can be translated into improved production performance.

More broadly, major external shocks are an important determinant of technical efficiency in broiler production. Existing studies show that when ASF reduced pork supply, consumer substitution shifted first and most strongly toward chicken, making it the primary substitute for pork (Ma et al., 2021; Xu et al., 2022; Qiao and Ahn, 2022; Gale and Dong, 2023). At the same time, this form of demand substitution induced by major animal disease outbreaks is not unique to ASF. Previous research has shown that although foot-and-mouth disease does not directly affect poultry, its outbreak can still shift demand away from susceptible meats toward poultry and other substitutes through linkages across meat markets (Wilson and Kinsella, 2004). Review evidence likewise suggests that poultry demand tends to be more resilient during epidemic shocks (Knight-Jones and Rushton, 2013), and simulation studies further indicate that, under cross-meat substitution, disease shocks outside the broiler sector may stimulate poultry production through demand reallocation (Paarlberg et al., 2002). Importantly, the effects of ASF are not limited to consumer substitution alone. In addition to altering relative prices across meat products, ASF can also affect broiler production costs through input markets for feed, chicks, and disease-prevention inputs, creating a compound shock in which demand expansion coincides with rising costs (Mason-D’Croz et al., 2020). Under such conditions, substitution demand enters production decisions through price and profit signals. As consumption shifts from pork to chicken, producers adjust chick placement, marketing schedules, and input intensity accordingly (Ullah et al., 2019). These adjustments, however, are not frictionless or instantaneous; rather, they are constrained by adjustment costs associated with restocking, input reallocation, managerial adaptation, and strengthened biosecurity, and ultimately shape technical efficiency in broiler production (Fig. 2). The effect is therefore not unidirectional. On the one hand, substitution demand may raise technical efficiency by improving capacity utilization, encouraging technology adoption, and optimizing input allocation. On the other hand, it may suppress efficiency gains through rising input prices, greater organizational coordination pressure, and resource misallocation. Moreover, farms operating under different production arrangements and at different scales differ substantially in price-signal transmission, equipment conditions, financing capacity, biosecurity standards, and market linkages (Mason-D’Croz et al., 2020; Li et al., 2021), implying that the effects of ASF-induced substitution demand on technical efficiency are likely to be strongly heterogeneous.

Transmission mechanism of technical efficiency change in the broiler industry under ASF shock.

Existing research on ASF has focused primarily on reductions in pork supply, substitution in consumption, and price transmission across meat markets (Wu et al., 2021), while paying relatively limited attention to how changes in substitution demand affect internal production organization and technical efficiency within the poultry sector. Prior studies suggest that demand expansion does not necessarily improve technical efficiency. When organizational coordination is weak, expansion may be accompanied by input misallocation, managerial constraints, and heightened biosecurity risk, thereby undermining efficiency; conversely, agribusiness enterprise may also reduce uncertainty by strengthening contract production, adjusting production scale, and stabilizing supply chains (Otsuka et al., 2016). However, although the existing literature has documented the market substitution effects induced by ASF and has examined the roles of contract farming and scale differences in broiler efficiency, it has yet to integrate external market shocks, production reorganization, and changes in technical efficiency into a unified analytical framework. In particular, evidence remains scarce on how efficiency responses differ systematically across production arrangements and farm-size structures.

Accordingly, the substitution-driven demand shock under the ASF shock should not be understood merely as a quantitative shift in meat consumption across product categories. Rather, it represents a dynamic transmission process jointly shaped by product-market substitution, input-market linkages, firm-level adjustment costs, and differences in production governance. Using multi-period panel data from China’s white-feather broiler farms over 2017–2024, this study applies stochastic frontier analysis (Aigner et al., 1977) to measure technical efficiency in broiler production, with a particular focus on whether, and through which mechanisms, external market shocks are transmitted to the production side. Compared with existing studies that mainly examine the effects of ASF on pork supply, meat prices, and consumption substitution, the marginal contribution of this study lies in integrating substitution-driven demand shocks, production-organization adjustment, and changes in technical efficiency into a unified analytical framework, while further identifying heterogeneous responses across governance structures and farm-size groups exposed to the same external shock. This analysis not only deepens the understanding of efficiency-adjustment mechanisms in China’s broiler industry after ASF, but also provides empirical insights for developing countries seeking to enhance poultry-supply resilience through organized production, appropriately scaled operations, and risk governance under meat-supply volatility, animal-disease shocks, and protein-security pressures.

Materials and methods

Data source

This study uses data from the Broiler Survey and Monitoring Database of the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs of China for 2017–2024. The database covers 6 major broiler-producing provinces—Heilongjiang, Jilin, Liaoning, Hebei, Henan, and Shandong—which together accounted for 82% of China’s broiler marketings in 2024, indicating strong regional representativeness. The database was jointly compiled and monitored by the National Animal Husbandry Service and the China Animal Agriculture Association, and includes contract growers, non-contract growers, and farms of different production scales, thereby providing a suitable basis for heterogeneity analysis. White-feathered broilers account for 72.2% of total chicken meat production in China(Xin et al., 2025); accordingly, the sample in this study consists primarily of white-feathered broiler farms. Unless otherwise specified, the term “broiler” hereafter refers to white-feathered broilers.

Sampling followed a stratified random design. Provincial quotas were allocated according to each province’s relative share of China’s broiler output, with a minimum of 20 sampled farms set for each province to ensure basic regional coverage. Within each province, farms were first stratified by production mode and farm-size category, and farms were then randomly selected within each stratum to minimize potential bias in sample composition. After data cleaning, 1,240 valid observations were obtained. In this study, the production mode of each sampled farm, defined by whether it participated in contract farming, and its farm-size category may change over time in response to market conditions and business strategies. The analysis is based on a balanced panel consisting of 155 broiler farms continuously observed from 2017 to 2024, yielding a total of 1,240 observations (Table 1).

The classification of farm size in this study follows the broiler farm size categories used in the China Animal Husbandry and Veterinary Yearbook, with annual slaughter output as the classification criterion. Specifically, farms with an annual slaughter output of less than 30,000 birds were classified as small-scale farms; those with 30,000 to less than 100,000 birds as medium-scale farms; those with 100,000 to less than 500,000 birds as large farms; and those with 500,000 birds or more as extra-large farms. This classification is consistent with the statistical convention used in the broiler industry and captures the structural transition of broiler production in China from small-scale operations toward medium-, large-, and extra-large-scale production. All sample farms were retained throughout the study period, and the panel contains no missing observations due to farm entry or exit. Therefore, subsequent comparisons of annual technical efficiency are conducted within the same group of farms, avoiding mechanical changes in mean efficiency caused by shifts in sample composition across years.

To improve the reliability of the estimates, multivariate outliers were first identified and removed using the Mahalanobis distance method to reduce the influence of extreme observations on the stochastic frontier analysis (SFA) results (Rousseeuw and van Zomeren, 1990). In addition, following the procedure of Xiao (2024), key economic variables were deflated to enhance comparability across years.

Variable definition and treatment

In the SFA model, output is measured by annual sales revenue at the farm level, which serves as the indicator of economic output and the basis for estimating technical efficiency (Dellnitz and Kleine, 2019).

Input variables include 5 major cost categories: chicks, feed, utilities and fuel, disease prevention and treatment, and labor, all measured by annual farm-level expenditures. According to statistics from the China Animal Agriculture Association, these 5 cost items accounted for 70.9%, 15.0%, 4.5%, 2.5%, and 2.6% of total broiler production costs, respectively, in 2024, summing to 95.5% (Fig. 3). This indicates that the selected input variables capture the major cost components of broiler production well.

Composition of production costs in China’s broiler sector, 2024.

The inefficiency-effects specification follows the analytical framework of Khan et al. (2022) and includes farm size, the ASF-background variable, China’s per capita pork consumption, farm-level broiler mortality rate, and a COVID-19 period indicator. Following related research (Liu et al., 2024), the period 2018–2021 is defined as the phase of severe ASF shock, and an ASF-period dummy is constructed accordingly. This period covers the main phase after the ASF outbreak during which hog supply contracted, pork prices remained high, and meat-consumption substitution continued to spread through the market. Because the definition of the ASF shock period may vary across studies, this study further specifies two alternative windows, 2018–2020 and 2019–2022, in the robustness checks, and uses 2023–2024 as a placebo window to examine whether the main conclusions depend on a particular time-window definition.

Given that the COVID-19 pandemic around 2020 may have affected broiler production through feed-import logistics, labor mobility, transportation, slaughtering and processing activities, and shifts in consumption scenarios, this study further includes a COVID-19 shock variable. Considering that the pandemic’s impacts on agricultural production and meat supply chains were concentrated mainly in this period, 2020 is defined as the COVID-19 shock year. The COVID-19 variable takes the value of 1 for observations in 2020 and 0 otherwise. This variable is used to control for the potential influence of the contemporaneous public health shock on technical efficiency in broiler farming, beyond the ASF shock.

To reduce the influence of price variation on comparability, core input and output variables were deflated using item-specific indices. Specifically, sales revenue, chick costs, and feed costs were deflated using the corresponding price indices published by the Bureau of Animal Husbandry and Veterinary Services of the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs of China. Utility and fuel expenditures were deflated using the Purchasing Price Index for Fuel and Power for Industrial Producers reported in the China Statistical Yearbook, and labor costs were deflated using the average wage index for employees in agriculture, forestry, animal husbandry, and fishery, also reported in the China Statistical Yearbook. Following Yan (2022), disease prevention and treatment expenditures were not deflated.

The production-frontier estimates indicate pronounced nonlinear input–output relationships in white-feather broiler production. Among the first-order terms, the coefficient of energy-related inputs, including water, electricity, coal, and fuel, is 0.792 (p < 0.01), and the coefficient of disease-prevention and veterinary-treatment inputs is 0.440 (p < 0.01); both have significant positive effects on output. This suggests that environmental control, energy use, and disease-prevention inputs are important contributors to broiler output. The first-order coefficient of feed input is −0.347 (p < 0.05), showing a significant negative effect at the sample mean. This implies that the marginal product of feed does not increase in a simple linear manner, but may be jointly affected by input intensity, feed-use efficiency, stocking density, and the combination of other inputs. Because first-order coefficients in a Translog model represent only local elasticities at the sample mean, the subsequent analysis further calculates feed-input elasticity using the full model and conducts regularity checks of the production function to avoid overinterpreting a single first-order coefficient.

The squared and interaction terms further indicate significant nonlinearities and input-combination effects in broiler production. The squared terms of chick input and feed input are significantly positive, whereas the squared term of labor input is significantly negative, suggesting that the marginal effects of major inputs vary with input intensity. Among the interaction terms, the interactions between chick input and energy input, feed input and labor input, labor input and energy input, and energy input and disease-prevention input are significantly positive. By contrast, the interactions between chick input and feed input, chick input and disease-prevention input, feed input and energy input, and labor input and disease-prevention input are significantly negative. These results suggest that output growth in broiler production is not driven simply by the expansion of a single input, but depends more on the structural matching of chicks, feed, labor, energy use, and disease-prevention inputs.

The inefficiency-equation estimates identify the main factors associated with differences in technical efficiency. Because the dependent component in the inefficiency equation is technical inefficiency, a negative coefficient indicates a reduction in inefficiency and thus an improvement in technical efficiency, whereas a positive coefficient indicates higher inefficiency and lower technical efficiency. The coefficient of contract farming is −0.105 (p < 0.05), suggesting that contract farming reduces technical inefficiency after controlling for other factors. The coefficient of Per capita apparent pork consumption is 0.015 (p < 0.01), indicating that changes in pork consumption may affect broiler technical efficiency through meat-market substitution and price-transmission mechanisms. The coefficient of farm-level mortality is 0.298 (p < 0.01), showing that higher mortality significantly increases technical inefficiency and confirming that disease and management risks remain important constraints on technical efficiency. The coefficient of the COVID-19 variable is 0.213 (p < 0.01), suggesting that pandemic-related shocks increased the extent to which farms deviated from the production frontier, possibly through disruptions to feed logistics, labor mobility, slaughtering and processing operations, and consumption settings. The coefficient of the ASF variable is −0.137 (p < 0.01), indicating that, overall, pork supply contraction and increased substitution demand for poultry meat under the ASF shock contributed to improvements in broiler technical efficiency.

To further identify heterogeneous responses across farm types under the ASF shock, interaction terms between ASF and contract farming, as well as between ASF and farm size, are included in the inefficiency equation. The coefficient of ASF × contract farming is 0.154 (p < 0.01), indicating that, compared with non-Contract growers, Contract growers experienced a smaller reduction in inefficiency under the ASF shock; in other words, their marginal room for efficiency improvement was relatively limited.

Regarding scale heterogeneity, extra-large farms are used as the reference group. The coefficients of small-, medium-, and large farms are all significantly positive, at 0.147, 0.106, and 0.107, respectively. This indicates that, during non-ASF periods, farms in these size categories had higher technical inefficiency than extra-large farms, suggesting that extra-large farms still possessed certain advantages in scale management. The interaction terms between ASF and farm size are all negative. Specifically, the coefficients of ASF × small-scale, ASF × medium-scale, and ASF × large farms are −0.264 (p < 0.01), −0.078 (p < 0.10), and −0.102 (p < 0.05), respectively. These results suggest that technical inefficiency declined across farms of different sizes under the ASF shock, indicating a broadly shared improvement in efficiency.

Dynamic changes and heterogeneity in technical efficiency of broiler production

Fig. 4 further illustrates the dynamic changes in technical efficiency across production modes and farm-size groups. To avoid attributing the effects of contemporaneous public health shocks entirely to ASF, a COVID-19 dummy variable is included in the subsequent inefficiency equation. The results show that, under the ASF shock, broiler farms generally followed a pattern of “initial decline, subsequent recovery, and later moderation.” Over time, technical efficiency declined to 0.913 in 2018, rose to a period peak of 0.959 in 2021, and then decreased again thereafter.

Dynamic changes in technical efficiency of broiler production across production arrangements and farm sizes under the ASF shock, 2017–2024.

By production arrangement, average technical efficiency was higher among contract growers (0.939) than among non-contract growers (0.924), indicating a higher overall efficiency level under contract production. In dynamic terms, however, when 2018 is used as the baseline, efficiency gains through 2021 were smaller for contract growers than for non-contract growers across all size categories; when 2021 is used as the baseline, the decline through 2024 was larger for contract growers in every size group. Specifically, during 2018–2021, technical efficiency increased by 0.056 and 0.043, respectively, for small-scale non-contract and contract growers; by 0.060 and 0.058 for medium-scale growers; by 0.045 and 0.039 for large-scale growers; and by 0.042 and 0.026 for extra-large-scale growers. During 2021–2024, technical efficiency declined by 0.040 and 0.066, respectively, for small-scale non-contract and contract growers; by 0.012 and 0.019 for medium-scale growers; by 0.016 and 0.028 for large-scale growers; and by 0.027 and 0.031 for extra-large-scale growers. These results indicate that, in the subsequent adjustment phase, the decline in technical efficiency was generally greater among contract growers than among non-contract growers, suggesting that efficiency gains were more difficult to sustain under contract production.

Across farm-size groups, efficiency gains during 2018–2021 followed an inverted U-shaped pattern for both contract and non-contract growers across the 4 size categories—small, medium, large, and extra-large-scale (Fig. 5). The largest improvement was observed among medium-scale farms (0.060 for contract growers and 0.058 for non-contract growers), whereas the smallest was found among extra-large farms (0.042 for contract growers and 0.026 for non-contract growers). Correspondingly, the decline during 2021–2024 followed a U-shaped pattern (Fig. 6), with the smallest decrease observed among medium-scale farms (0.012 for non-contract growers and 0.019 for contract growers) and the largest among small-scale farms (0.040 for non-contract growers and 0.066 for contract growers). Overall, medium-scale farms exhibited greater stability during the adjustment process, whereas extra-large farms showed more pronounced fluctuations in technical efficiency.

On the one hand, the Translog model is retained as the baseline specification because it captures possible nonlinear relationships and interaction effects among inputs in broiler production. The Cobb–Douglas results show that the direction and significance of contract farming, ASF, and their interactions with farm size remain generally consistent, indicating that the main conclusions on heterogeneous changes in technical efficiency under the ASF shock are not driven solely by the Translog functional form.

Discussion

Dynamic evolution of technical efficiency in broiler production under ASF shock

This study finds that technical efficiency in broiler production did not improve continuously under the ASF shock, but instead followed a staged pattern of “initial decline–subsequent recovery–later decline.” In the early stage, the contraction of pork supply and the rise in pork prices strengthened substitution demand for chicken; however, this shock was not simply a favorable market signal. While broiler farms received stronger demand signals from the product market, they also faced cost pressures arising from linked adjustments in input markets, including feed, chicks, and disease-prevention inputs (Ullah et al., 2019). The production side therefore faced a compound shock characterized by both improved revenue prospects and tighter cost constraints. From the perspective of firm adjustment, producers cannot move costlessly and instantaneously to a new optimal production state when demand rises rapidly. Instead, they are constrained by adjustment costs related to restocking, input reallocation, managerial reorganization, and disease-prevention upgrading, and can only respond to market changes through rapid expansion with frictions (Mitchell et al., 2024). Thus, the decline in technical efficiency in 2018 does not imply that substitution demand had no effect; rather, it more likely reflects short-term efficiency losses caused by non-optimal adjustment in the initial stage of the demand shock.

Subsequently, as market expectations gradually stabilized, the supply system adapted, and producers strengthened their adjustment capacity, technical efficiency began to recover and reached a stage-specific peak in 2021. Thereafter, as pig production capacity recovered, the marginal effect of poultry substitution weakened, and the cost constraints, managerial pressures, and market-structure adjustments generated by earlier expansion became more apparent, leading to a renewed decline in technical efficiency. The descriptive evidence in this study also shows that the share of poultry meat in total meat output peaked around 2020 and then declined as pig production capacity recovered, indicating that the poultry substitution effect induced by ASF was clearly stage-specific rather than continuously intensifying. Therefore, the 2018–2021 period is better understood as a phase in which ASF shock, substitution-demand release, and market recovery adjustment were jointly intertwined.

The alternative ASF-window specifications further indicate that the core findings do not depend entirely on the single 2018–2021 window. Whether the ASF shock period is restricted to 2018–2020, corresponding to the peak period of pork supply contraction, or extended to 2019–2022, corresponding to a broader price-transmission window, the signs of the ASF variable and its interactions with farm-size variables remain generally consistent. This suggests that the changes in broiler technical efficiency and their scale heterogeneity under the ASF shock are reasonably robust. It should be noted that the 2018–2021 period was also affected by COVID-19, poultry disease pressure, and input-market fluctuations. Therefore, this study does not interpret the ASF variable as the net causal effect of a single disease event, but rather as a market-background variable centered on the ASF shock and transmitted to the broiler industry through pork supply contraction and meat substitution demand.

These findings suggest that external market changes may improve resource allocation at certain stages, but they do not necessarily translate into sustained efficiency gains. Existing studies have not reached a consistent conclusion on the relationship between external shocks and technical efficiency. Some studies argue that demand expansion can increase capacity utilization and promote factor reallocation (Dawande et al., 2010), whereas others suggest that external disturbances are often accompanied by input-price volatility and rising managerial pressure, thereby constraining efficiency improvement (Acosta et al., 2023). The findings of this study indicate that these divergent conclusions may correspond to different stages of shock transmission. In this sense, the “initial decline–subsequent recovery–later decline” pattern of technical efficiency under the ASF shock is not an incidental fluctuation, but a dynamic re-equilibration path jointly shaped by product-market substitution, input-market linkages, and firm-level adjustment costs.

Dynamic differences in technical efficiency across production arrangements under ASF shock

This study further shows that contract growers had higher average technical efficiency than non-contract growers, but under ASF shock they experienced smaller efficiency gains and larger subsequent declines.

At the average level, contract farming exhibited higher technical efficiency, indicating that contractual arrangements help improve production performance under normal operating conditions. According to Eccles (1987), contracts and vertical coordination exist because they reduce search costs, bargaining costs, and performance uncertainty in market transactions. In broiler production, the centralized provision of chicks, feed, veterinary drugs, and technical services essentially replaces part of market exchange with intra-organizational coordination. As a result, contract growers are more likely to achieve stable input supply, standardized production, and predictable output under normal conditions, which is consistent with their higher average technical efficiency. This finding is also in line with previous evidence on the efficiency advantages of contract farming (Begum et al., 2012). Similar conclusions have been reported for broiler production in Zimbabwe and Indonesia, where contract growers were also found to outperform non-contract growers in efficiency terms (Zimunya and Dube, 2021; Rokhani et al., 2023).

In dynamic terms, however, contract growers did not exhibit stronger efficiency recovery. This study recognizes that the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020–2021 partially overlapped with the ASF shock period. COVID-19 may have affected broiler production efficiency through disruptions to feed logistics, labor mobility, transportation restrictions, slaughtering and processing activities, and shifts in consumption settings. Therefore, a COVID-19 dummy variable is included in the model as a control. Using 2018 as the base year, efficiency gains across all size groups were smaller for contract growers than for non-contract growers; using 2021 as the base year, efficiency declines through 2024 were generally larger for contract growers. This suggests that although contract farming improves average efficiency, it may also weaken short-run adjustment flexibility because of contractual commitments, input planning, and compliance constraints (Hoang, 2021). From the perspective of price-signal transmission, contract arrangements reduce disease risk and operating uncertainty, but may also dampen growers’ immediate response to changes in market prices and profitability. Thus, the pattern of higher average efficiency but slower dynamic adjustment among contract growers fundamentally reflects the trade-off between stability and flexibility inherent in governance structures (Huang et al., 2018). By contrast, although non-contract growers display lower average efficiency, their greater operational autonomy may provide stronger short-run adaptability when market conditions change rapidly.

Farm expansion and changes in technical efficiency under ASF shock

This study also finds significant heterogeneity in how farms of different sizes responded to market changes under ASF shock. From 2018 to 2021, technical efficiency gains across size groups followed an inverted U-shaped pattern, with the largest increase observed among medium-scale farms and the smallest among extra-large farms. From 2021 to 2024, the decline in technical efficiency showed a U-shaped pattern overall, with the smallest decline among medium-scale farms and the largest declines among small and extra-large farms.

These results indicate that the relationship between scale expansion and technical efficiency is not linear. From the perspective of firm growth and organizational economics, such nonlinearity is not unexpected. Small-scale farms often face constraints in capital, technology, and market access, making it more difficult for them to reallocate inputs and upgrade management in a timely manner, and therefore harder to convert external market opportunities into efficiency gains (Birhanu et al., 2021). By contrast, although extra-large farms possess stronger resource endowments, they are also more prone to managerial diseconomies and delayed responses as organizational layers expand, information chains lengthen, and internal coordination tasks intensify (Houedjofonon et al., 2020). In other words, economies of scale do not expand indefinitely; beyond a certain threshold, organizational complexity itself can become a new constraint on efficiency. Medium-scale farms, by contrast, tend to perform better than small-scale farms in terms of input access, technology adoption, and market connections, while remaining less organizationally complex than extra-large farms. This makes them better able to sustain efficiency gains and relative stability under external shocks (Song and Xu, 2023). In this sense, medium-scale farms appear to be closer to an “optimal scale” range: large enough to benefit from scale economies and specialized division of labor, but not so large as to suffer the pronounced managerial diseconomies associated with extra-large-scale operations. This helps explain their stronger efficiency stability under external shocks.

The literature has not reached a consistent view on the relationship between scale expansion and technical efficiency. Some studies argue that larger scale can improve technical efficiency by spreading fixed costs, facilitating specialized division of labor, and promoting mechanization (Xin et al., 2016). Others emphasize that if managerial capacity and institutional arrangements do not improve in parallel, scale expansion may instead raise coordination costs and generate resource misallocation (Khan et al., 2022). Our results suggest that these views are not contradictory, but rather apply to different scale ranges and market conditions.

Implications for protein security from production-mode optimization under ASF shock

In developing regions such as Southeast Asia and Africa, broiler chicken has become an important source of animal protein for low-income populations because of its relatively low cost, high feed-conversion efficiency, and short production cycle (Singh et al., 2022). However, low technical efficiency, fragmented production organization, and insufficient market coordination remain major constraints on the development of the broiler industry in these regions, potentially weakening its role in reducing malnutrition and strengthening livelihood resilience (Attia et al., 2022). From this perspective, the contribution of the broiler industry to protein security depends not only on capacity expansion, but also on production-organization efficiency, risk-coping capacity, and market-coordination mechanisms.

Using balanced panel data from China’s broiler farms over 2017–2024, this study shows that technical efficiency differs substantially across production modes and farm-size groups under the ASF shock and the associated substitution in meat consumption. It should be emphasized that the results mainly reflect conditional associations under a specific sample, ASF-window definition, and model specification, and should not be interpreted as strict causal effects. Accordingly, the following policy implications are better understood as insights derived from empirical associations and heterogeneity patterns rather than as causal evaluations of any single policy instrument.

First, greater attention should be paid to improving the governance mechanisms of contract farming. The results show a significant negative relationship between contract farming and technical inefficiency, indicating that Contract growers exhibited relatively lower technical inefficiency during the sample period. This suggests that contract farming may provide organizational advantages in production standardization, technical services, input access, and market linkage. However, the ASF interaction results also indicate that the efficiency response of Contract growers under external shocks is not simply equivalent to their average efficiency advantage. Therefore, policies related to contract farming should not focus only on expanding coverage. More attention should be given to contract standardization, risk-sharing arrangements, price-adjustment mechanisms, and the quality of technical services, so as to enhance the adaptability of contractual relationships under market volatility.

Second, the development of medium-scale farms should be emphasized. The results show that technical inefficiency and its changes during the ASF window differ across farm-size groups, suggesting that scale structure is an important factor shaping efficiency performance in broiler production. However, scale expansion does not necessarily lead to efficiency improvement; its effect still depends on management capacity, input-allocation ability, and risk-coping capacity. Industrial policy should therefore avoid a simple pursuit of large-scale expansion and instead focus on the actual capacities of farms of different sizes in production management, disease prevention and control, working-capital turnover, and market access. Support should be directed toward appropriately scaled farms with stable operating foundations and organizational-management capacity.

Third, the organization level and service accessibility of small-scale farms should be improved. Small-scale farms are generally disadvantaged in capital, technology, information access, and market bargaining power, making them more vulnerable to rising costs and market fluctuations under external shocks. The results on scale interactions and production-mode differences suggest that organizational integration may be an important condition affecting the efficiency performance of small-scale farms. Therefore, cooperatives, leading agribusiness firms, socialized service organizations, and regional technical-service platforms can be used to strengthen smallholders’ access to disease-prevention services, production guidance, financial support, and market information, thereby reducing efficiency losses caused by fragmented production.

Fourth, industrial support mechanisms under major shocks should be strengthened. Although ASF directly affected the hog industry, it was associated with changes in broiler production efficiency through shifts in the meat-supply structure, price transmission, and consumption substitution. At the same time, COVID-19, feed-price fluctuations, poultry disease risks, and logistics constraints may also affect broiler production efficiency. Therefore, protein-security support in the broiler industry should not rely solely on short-term capacity expansion. A more systematic mechanism is needed for risk early warning, input supply assurance, disease prevention and control, coordination of slaughtering and processing, and market regulation, so as to strengthen the supply stability of the broiler value chain under major shocks.

Overall, China’s broiler-industry experience under the ASF shock suggests that whether an exogenous demand shock can be transformed into stable protein-supply capacity depends not only on the expansion of market demand itself, but also on the alignment of production organization, scale structure, and risk-governance mechanisms. For developing regions, the role of the broiler industry in protein security lies not only in increasing the supply of animal protein, but also in improving production efficiency and supply resilience under resource constraints through organized production, appropriately scaled operations, and risk-protection systems.

Conclusions

Using balanced panel data from China’s white-feathered broiler farms over 2017–2024, this study applied stochastic frontier analysis to examine the relationship between ASF-induced meat-substitution demand and technical efficiency in broiler production, with particular attention to heterogeneity jointly shaped by production mode and farm-size category. Unlike existing studies that mainly focus on the effects of ASF on pork supply, meat prices, and consumption substitution, this study incorporates ASF-induced substitution demand, production organization, and farm-size structure into a unified analytical framework. In doing so, it provides micro-level evidence on how substitution demand was transmitted to the broiler production side through phased and heterogeneous efficiency responses.

The results show that technical efficiency in broiler production did not increase monotonically under the ASF shock. Instead, it followed a dynamically asymmetric pattern of “short-term decline, phased recovery, and later moderation.” This finding indicates that external demand expansion does not automatically translate into sustained efficiency improvement. Its effect depends on the stage of the shock, the degree of market adaptation, and changes in cost constraints. The impact of ASF on the broiler industry should therefore be understood as a dynamic process of “shock–adjustment–rebalancing,” rather than as a simple demand-expansion-driven improvement in efficiency.

This study further finds that the relationship between contract farming and technical efficiency is conditional. Contract growers had higher average technical efficiency than non-contract growers, suggesting that contractual arrangements may help stabilize input supply, unify production standards, and reduce market uncertainty. However, under the ASF shock, contract growers showed relatively weaker elasticity in efficiency improvement and a more pronounced later-stage decline. These results indicate that contract farming is associated with higher average efficiency, but its stabilizing role may come at the cost of weaker dynamic adaptability under external shocks.

The results on scale heterogeneity indicate that broiler production efficiency does not increase linearly with farm size. During the ASF shock period, efficiency responses differed markedly across farm-size categories. Medium-scale farms showed stronger improvement during the recovery stage and greater stability during the later moderation stage, suggesting that moderate scale may be more conducive to balancing resource-allocation efficiency and operational flexibility. By contrast, small-scale farms may be constrained by limited capital, technology, and market access, whereas extra-large-scale farms may face greater organizational coordination and managerial complexity. Therefore, the efficiency effect of scale expansion varies across scale ranges and should not be simplified as “larger scale means higher efficiency.”

The core contribution of this study is to identify two key features of broiler technical-efficiency responses under the ASF shock. First, technical efficiency exhibits dynamic asymmetry between the recovery and moderation stages, indicating that the efficiency effect of an external demand shock changes with market recovery, cost constraints, and organizational adjustment. Second, production mode and farm-size structure do not affect technical efficiency independently; rather, they jointly shape the response paths of farms to external shocks. In other words, the same market shock can generate different efficiency outcomes depending on the governance structure and scale range of the farm. This finding extends ASF-related research from price changes and meat-substitution effects at the market level to the micro-level mechanisms of production organization and efficiency response.

Overall, China’s broiler-industry experience under the ASF shock suggests that improving protein-supply capacity requires not only expanding production capacity, but also strengthening the ability of different types of farms to convert market opportunities into production efficiency. Future research could further incorporate more detailed data on input prices, contract terms, firm-provided services, and farm management practices to identify the micro-level mechanisms underlying dynamic changes in technical efficiency under different governance structures.

Limitations and future research

This study has several limitations. Due to data availability, the monitoring data distinguish between contract and non-contract growers but do not provide detailed information on specific contract terms, such as input provision, pricing rules, risk-sharing mechanisms, technical-service intensity, quality requirements, or the degree of integrator control. Therefore, the estimated differences between contract and non-contract growers should be interpreted as the overall association between contract participation and technical efficiency, rather than as evidence on the effects of specific contractual arrangements on production efficiency, adjustment flexibility, or risk sharing. In addition, this study uses the farm-level broiler mortality rate to capture within-farm disease-related production losses, but this variable cannot fully reflect the broader poultry disease environment, such as province-year variation in avian influenza, Newcastle disease, infectious bursal disease, mycoplasma infection, or other disease pressures. Although year fixed effects and robustness checks help mitigate these concerns, future research could incorporate more detailed contract-level data, official disease-surveillance records, or veterinary-event data to further identify how contractual arrangements and poultry disease pressure affect technical efficiency in broiler production.

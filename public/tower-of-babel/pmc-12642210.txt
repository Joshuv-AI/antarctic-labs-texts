Correlation between metabolic unhealth and prostate cancer -an inverse probability weighting study
Authors: Mingyue Chen, Yijie Zhou, Jinru Wang, Hao Qiu, Hengqing An, Ning Tao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCurrent research indicates that prostate cancer (PCa) is one of the most common cancers in men, and its occurrence may be linked to metabolic unhealth. Therefore, we conducted a study on the correlation between metabolic unhealth and PCa.ObjectiveTo study the correlation between metabolic unhealth and the incidence of PCa.MethodsA total of 607 patients underwent prostate biopsy in the urology department of a certain tertiary hospital in Urumqi, Xinjiang from May 2018 to September 2022. Age was used as a matching variable inverse probability weighted to reduce the influence of confounding factors. Logistic regression models were used to analyze the association between metabolic unhealthiness and the risk of prostate cancer.Results(1) Logistic regression analysis showed that the risk of prostate cancer in metabolically unhealthy people was 1.684 times higher than that in metabolically healthy people (OR=1.684,95%CI:1.224-2.317,P=0.001); (2) In sensitivity analysis, multifactor Poisson regression showed that metabolically unhealthy people had higher risk of the disease (RR=1.145, 95%CI:1.015-1.292, P =0.028). Subsequently, excluding those with diabetes, sensitivity analysis using inverse probability-weighted logistic regression with age as a matching variable showed that those with unhealthy metabolism had a higher risk of prostate cancer (OR=1.464,95% CI:1.046-2.050, P =0.026).ConclusionMetabolic unhealth, obesity, are risk factors for PCa, which have certain diagnostic and therapeutic value for the risk of PCa. Reducing body weight and managing blood pressure, blood sugar, blood lipids, and high-density lipoprotein through reasonable diet can effectively prevent and control PCa.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12894-025-01969-3.

Introduction

PCa is a malignant tumor of the male urogenital system caused by the malignant proliferation of prostate epithelial cells [1]. Currently, PCa is the second most common male malignant tumor in the world, accounting for about one-third of all male malignant tumors [2, 3]. In 2020, there were 1,414,259 new cases of PCa worldwide, with 375,304 deaths, representing 14.1% and 6.8% of all male cancer incidences and deaths, respectively [4]. Morbidity and mortality from PCa are also on the rise in some Asian countries. In China, situated in Asia, the aging population is becoming a more serious issue. Consequently, the incidence rate of prostate cancer has been rising in recent years, posing an increasing threat to men's health. Due to the geographical, ethnic and economic diversity in Asia, the epidemiology of prostate cancer (PCa) is affected by both modifiable and non-modifiable risk factors. Therefore, metabolism become one of the factors that cannot be ignored [5, 6].

Current studies suggest that the occurrence of PCa may be related to dietary factors, hyperinsulinemia, metabolic syndrome and other metabolic abnormalities. Metabolic syndrome is a state of metabolic abnormality, it can affect the occurrence and progression of prostate cancer through the interaction of insulin resistance, IGF-1, lipid metabolism and inflammatory response [7–11]. Metabolic syndrome characterized by insulin resistance(IR). When patients are in the status of metabolic abnormalities, the levels of insulin-like growth factors in the body increase, inhibiting apoptosis of prostate cells and thereby promoting the occurrence of PCa. IR also induces the production of hyperinsulinaemia [12], stimulating the production of insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1). It also increases the bioavailability of IGF-1 [13–15], activates the p21 ras/media-primer-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathway and the phosphatidylinositol-3 kinase/AKT pathway, and promotes PCa cell proliferation [16, 17]. With the increase of body mass index(BMI), the energy supply of fatty acid metabolism is enhanced, and the body will be in a state of chronic low inflammation throughout the body. Inflammation is a key factor influencing the progression of prostate cancer, inflammatory tumor microenvironment is one of the important mechanisms leading to prostate carcinogenesis. As inflammation progresses, tumor cells recruit additional leukocytes that promote angiogenesis, proliferation, vascular and tissue growth and remodeling, which leads to prostate cancer [18]. It suggests that metabolic abnormalities may be involved in tumor growth, invasion and metastasis.

Metabolic unhealth is an important indicator of metabolic abnormalities, defined by the presence of two or more of four indicators in an individual: elevated blood glucose, elevated triglycerides (TG), decreased HDL-C, and elevated blood pressure [19]. Initially, it was used solely for identifying individuals with insulin sensitivity and obesity, but it was later found to be strongly associated with other diseases as well. Metabolic syndrome is characterized by the presence of any three of the five indicators, which include the aforementioned four indicators and obesity [9]. Compared with metabolic syndrome, metabolic unhealth does not consider BMI and Waist Circumference (WC) in diagnosis. Metabolic unhealth is more sensitive to the identification of non-obese people, and it is more restrictive in defining adverse metabolic conditions [20].

Some studies have indicated that metabolically healthy obesity (MHO) individuals have a reduced cancer risk compared to metabolically unhealthy obesity (MUO) individuals, suggesting that an unhealthy metabolism may play a critical role in cancer development. Besides, there is a lack of detailed information about the burden of PCa in Asia [21]. Thus, the study is based on clinical data from a certain tertiary hospital. This study will comprehensively analyze the correlation between metabolic unhealth and PCa, and to explore whether metabolic unhealth affects the risk of PCa, discuss the risk factors for PCa as well as provide a theoretical basis for clinical diagnosis and treatment.

Subjects

After calculating the sample size, this study collected clinical data from 895 male patients in the Department of Urology at the First Affiliated Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University from May 2018 to September 2022. According to the inclusion and exclusion criteria and exclusion criteria and using listwise deletion. 212 patients with PCa and 395 non-prostate cancer patients were included. Clinical data such as age, height, weight, triglycerides, high-density lipoprotein (HDL), and low-density lipoprotein (LDL) were analyzed. Criteria for determining metabolic unhealthy status

According to the Adult Treatment Panel III criteria, metabolic health was defined as having no more than one of the risk factors: (1) Hypertension (systolic pressure≥130 mmHg or diastolic pressure≥85 mmHg), (2) TG level ≥1.7 mmol/L or 150 mg/dL, (3) HDL-cholesterol level <1.04 mmol/L or 40 mg/dl (men) or 50 mg/dl (women), or (4) Hyperglycemia (fasting blood glucose ≥ 5.6 mmol/L or 100mg/dL). And any two or more risks in any one of the above indicators are metabolical unhealth [19, 22].

The concept is considered as having a lower risk of cardiometabolic diseases than patients with more risk parameters, and metabolic health is useful to stratify the risk of cardiometabolic diseases in obesity [20]. In China, a study have shown that the ATPIII-MS criterion has the highest ability to predict cardiovascular disease and type 2 diabetes mellitus. ATPIII is the best metabolic health criterion for the Chinese population [23].

Research methods

This study was a case-control study that collected clinical data of all admitted patients through the hospital's electronic medical record system, including general information such as age, gender, and medical history. Patients’ laboratory test indicators such as blood glucose and triglycerides were recorded.

Blood Lipid Measurement: Professional medical personnel collected fasting peripheral venous blood from the subjects in the morning (after more than 12 hours) according to strict aseptic operation, and completed the detection of blood lipid indexes (total cholesterol, triacylglycerol, low-density lipoprotein, high-density lipoprotein) by ultra-high-speed differential centrifugation in a unified laboratory.

Blood Pressure Measurement: A calibrated mercury column sphygmomanometer was used to measure blood pressure after the patient rested for ten minutes according to the standard indirect measurement method.

Biopsy: Biopsies were performed by a number of senior urologists using the US BARD biopsy needles using the 12+X puncture method, in which 1–2 needles were punctured as the X-needle for suspicious areas suggested on nuclear magnetic resonance imaging or ultrasound, and the rest of the area was punctured by the systematic 12-needle puncture method [24]. Biopsy tissues were uniformly sent to the Pathology Department of certain tertiary hospital for examination, and the pathological results were diagnosed and recorded by senior pathologists.

Transrectal ultrasound (TRUS) guidance was used for the prostate biopsy procedure in this study, employing an end-fire intracavitary probe. Anesthesia was administered using bupivacaine hydrochloride paste.

Relevant standards and formulas

According to the diagnostic criteria proposed by the Diabetes Branch of the Chinese Medical Association in 2004, overweight and/or obesity were defined as BMI≥25.0kg/m2 [25]. BMI = weight (kg)/height2 (m) [26].

Statistical criteria

Perform comparisons using the t-test or the Mann-Whitney U test and the χ2 test. The standardized mean differences in age were calculated between the case and control groups before and after inverse probability weighting, respectively. The significant decrease in the standardized mean difference(SMD) of age after weighting indicates that the weights are effective in balancing the covariates and the stability of the weights is good. The SMD for age before inverse probability weighted equilibrium is 0.601 and after equilibrium is 0.048.(SMD=0.048<0.1). Employ univariate and multivariate weighted logistic regression to evaluate the association between metabolic unhealthy status and the incidence of PCa. Conduct sensitivity analysis to assess the robustness of the results. and the association intensity was constructed by hazard ratio (OR) and 95% confidence interval (95% CI),and the test level was α=0.05.

Discussion

This study explored the correlation between metabolic unhealth and PCa. The results showed that there was an association between metabolic unhealthy status and prostate cancer. Age was used as a matching variable for inverse probability weighting analysis. A multifactorial logistic regression analysis, effectively correcting for age-related bias and adjusting for confounders such as low-density lipoprotein, potassium, sodium, chloride, and calcium. The result yielded that the risk of prostate cancer in people with metabolically unhealthy status was 68.4% higher than that in people with metabolically healthy status. To further explore the effect modification, this study introduced the additive interaction model. However, there was an additive interaction between the effects of metabolic unhealthy status and BMI on prostate cancer, and high BMI may amplify the carcinogenic effects of metabolic unhealthy status, which necessitated the sensitivity analysis in this study. To test the robustness of the results, this study conducted a sensitivity analysis in two ways: replacement of statistical methods with Poisson regression and adjusting the study population. The findings remain consistent with previous. The results of the sensitivity analyses showed that our results were stable.

Previous studies have shown that metabolic unhealth can increase the incidence of breast cancer through metabolic derangements [9], but there are fewer studies on the relationship between metabolic unhealth and PCa. The literature on prostate cancer has more often examined the effects of physical activity, insulin resistance, hyperglycemia and the metabolic syndrome or a single component on prostate cancer [27, 28]. Although previous literature has elucidated the relationship between metabolic diseases, such as metabolic syndrome, and prostate cancer risk. However, findings also vary by region. Previous studies in Europe have shown that there is a positive correlation between metabolic syndrome and PCa, while studies in North America have found a negative correlation between this group of people and PCa [29]. The metabolic syndrome population is also in a status of metabolic unhealth, inconsistent findings reflect the need to explore the underlying biological mechanisms of metabolic unhealth.

The concept of metabolic unhealth was introduced in 2000 to identify insulin-sensitive people and obese patients. Later, in a cohort study, metabolic unhealth has been proven to be effective in predicting the risk of all-cause mortality and other diseases such as cardiovascular diseases in obese populations, and the understanding of metabolic unhealth has been evolving since its introduction [20, 30]. The research results of Wei Yue et al. have shown that there are differences in the levels of cytokines in different metabolic status, and plasma cytokines in population with metabolic unhealth exhibit higher pro-inflammatory characteristics. Studies have demonstrated a positive association between plasma plasminogen activation inhibitor-1 (PAI-1) levels in population with metabolic unhealth [31]. PAI-1 plays an crucial role in tumor invasion and metastasis, angiogenesis, tumor cell proliferation and apoptosis, and immune evasion. When patients are in in a status of metabolic unhealth, PAI-1 can promote the upregulation of Cyclin D3/CDK4/6, accelerate the progression of the cell cycle from G1 phase to S phase of tumor cells, and promote the proliferation of tumor cells.

Studies by Albanes D et al. have shown that prostate cancer patients exhibit higher fasting blood glucose levels and insulin resistance [32, 33]. While metabolically unhealthy components are strongly associated with insulin resistance [31]. When insulin resistance occurs, the body becomes insensitive to insulin, which in turn leads to elevated blood glucose levels [34]. Studies by Aljada A et al. have proven that elevated blood glucose leads to an increase in the two target genes of NF-kB in the human core, cyclin D1 and cMyc, which play an important role in cell growth and proliferation, thereby influencing the development of PCa [24, 35, 36]. As the elevated blood glucose stimulates the sympathetic nerves, the renal tubules reabsorb sodium ions, which in turn increases the resistance of the peripheral blood vessels, leading to elevated blood pressure [34]. As blood pressure rises, sympathetic nervous system activity increases, and sympathetic nervous system excitation can lead to androgen-mediated growth of PCa cells [7]. Simultaneously, as the body's sensitivity to insulin decreases, free fatty acids in the body are synthesized into various substances in the liver, altering lipase activity, and consequently leading to elevated blood lipids [34]. Elevated blood lipids enhance lipid metabolism in the body, further promoting the production of inflammatory cytokines by adipocytes and forming a tumor microenvironment, thereby affecting the development of PCa [24]. Research by Zha Xiujing et al. showed that when insulin resistance occurs, HDL-C levels are low [37], and the decrease in HDL-C will allow cancer cells to accumulate integrins on the cell surface, enabling cancer cells to bind to other cells through integrins, causing tumor cells to migrate and spread [38].

Given that the initial symptoms of prostate cancer are not obvious, many patients do not exhibit noticeable symptoms in the early stages. Therefore, early screening becomes particularly important [39]. However, the PSA test is not cancer-specific, and its role in early tumor screening and prediction has certain limitations, so it often needs to be used in combination with other indicators [40]. The results of the current study suggest that metabolically unhealthy people have an elevated risk of PCa prevalence, and that metabolic unhealth can serve as a potential indicator of PCa screening. When combined with PSA, metabolic unhealth may improve the efficiency of identifying high-risk groups and provide certain assistance for clinical diagnosis and treatment in practical applications.

This study also has some limitations. First, this study is a single-center case-control study, and the lack of data in the sample selection from patients in different geographic areas leads to limited extrapolation of the study results. In the follow-up, we will strengthen cooperation with more different hospitals in different regions to collect better data and do a multicenter study to further validate this study. Second, although this study attempted to control for the various influencing factors included, the possibility that unmeasured or residual confounding factors could have influenced the results cannot be ruled out. Third, patients with BPH as the control group introduces bias, healthy people will be invited as a third group for control in subsequent studies. Finally, and the patients were not followed up in this study; therefore, we were unable to assess the prognostic information of PCa, which will be validated by further relevant cohort studies.

Conclusion

In summary, metabolic unhealthy status is an important influence on PCa and has some diagnostic and therapeutic value for PCa risk, and metabolic unhealthiness can be evaluated clinically as a factor related to influencing PCa and provide a reference for early screening of PCa.

A Decade of Achievements and Future Directions in Global Antimicrobial Resistance Surveillance System in Korea (Kor-GLASS)

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesTo comprehensively evaluate the 10-year operational outcomes (2016–2025) Global Antimicrobial Resistance Surveillance System in Korea (Kor-GLASS), assess its public health significance for national stewardship and global surveillance, and propose strategies for future development.MethodsThe study described the operational framework of Kor-GLASS, including its strain collection, analysis, and quality control systems, based on surveillance data. It analyzed resistance trends among key bloodstream pathogen isolates collected from 2016 to 2024 and evaluated major achievements, including alignment with the World Health Organization (WHO)’s Global Antimicrobial Resistance Surveillance System (GLASS), integration with the Emerging Antimicrobial Resistance Reporting (EAR) system, and activities as a WHO Collaborating Centre.ResultsKor-GLASS operates on a foundation of standardized, isolate-based surveillance supported by an independent quality management system that complies with WHO GLASS standards. In alignment with the strategic direction of WHO GLASS, the surveillance scope has progressively expanded in terms of catchment areas, target pathogens, specimen types, and antimicrobial panels. From 2016 to 2024, a total of 116,955 clinical isolates were collected and analyzed through the network of collection and analysis centers. This has enabled the continuous generation of nationally representative antimicrobial resistance (AMR) data from general hospitals. The accumulated surveillance data provide fundamental evidence for tracking long-term resistance trends and elucidating the molecular epidemiological characteristics of key pathogens. These outcomes are disseminated through the publication of the “National Antimicrobial Resistance Surveillance Annual Report” and data submissions to WHO GLASS and GLASS-EAR, thereby supporting both national and global AMR surveillance efforts. Furthermore, Kor-GLASS has strengthened international surveillance and One Health collaboration capacities through its designation and redesignation as a WHO Collaborating Centre for AMR Surveillance.ConclusionsOver the past decade, Kor-GLASS has served as the cornerstone of national antimicrobial resistance surveillance, providing evidence to inform policy and supporting global surveillance systems. Moving forward, Kor-GLASS is expected to evolve into a pivotal national AMR surveillance system through the introduction of whole-genome sequencing and stronger integration with national antimicrobial consumption surveillance.Keywords: Drug resistance, microbial; Anti-bacterial agents; Global Antimicrobial Resistance and Use Surveillance System; Public health surveillance

Key messages

① What is known previously?

Global Antimicrobial Resistance Surveillance System in Korea (Kor-GLASS) is the Republic of Korea (ROK)’s national antimicrobial resistance surveillance network, established in accordance with World Health Organization (WHO) Global Antimicrobial Resistance Surveillance System (GLASS) standards. It serves as a core framework for monitoring national antibiotic resistance trends and supporting policy development.

② What new information is presented?

Kor-GLASS produces internationally reliable data through standardized, strain-based surveillance, molecular epidemiological analysis, and quality management systems. It has gained global recognition for its contributions to WHO GLASS-EAR (Emerging Antimicrobial Resistance Reporting) reporting, Candida auris monitoring, and its operations as a WHO Collaborating Centre.

③ What are implications?

Over the past decade, Kor-GLASS has solidified its role as the cornerstone of national antimicrobial resistance surveillance in in the ROK by leveraging accumulated operational expertise and standardized systems. Moving forward, it is expected to evolve into a central hub for integrated national antimicrobial resistance surveillance by enhancing its capabilities and linking with antibiotic usage monitoring programs.

Introduction

Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) is a major public health threat that causes treatment failure of infections, increased mortality, and healthcare costs, requiring continuous and systematic surveillance and management at the national level [1,2]. In response, the World Health Organization (WHO) adopted the “Global Action Plan on AMR” in 2015 and established the Global Antimicrobial Resistance Surveillance System (GLASS) to produce comparable AMR data among countries [3]

According to the “1st National Action Plan on Antimicrobial Resistance (NAP on AMR),” the Republic of Korea (ROK) established and has been operating the Global Antimicrobial Resistance Surveillance System in Korea (Kor-GLASS) since 2016, reflecting the key principles of WHO GLASS: representativeness, specialization, and standardization [4,5]. To strengthen regional representativeness, the Collection Centers were selected by dividing the country into regions, focusing on general hospitals with 500–1,000 beds in each region, and the participating institutions have been expanded in a stepwise manner. In addition, Kor-GLASS continuously produces consistent and reliable resistance information through isolate-based surveillance and independent quality control, and functions as a core national surveillance system responsible for providing evidence for national AMR control policies and linking with international surveillance systems such as WHO GLASS (Figure 1) [6].

This report comprehensively summarizes the operational status and major achievements over the past 10 years (2016–2025) since the launch of Kor-GLASS and aims to present the public health significance and future development directions of the national AMR surveillance system based on the AMR data and resistance trends of major pathogens accumulated through isolate-based surveillance.

Methods

1. Kor-GLASS Operational System

Kor-GLASS is a national AMR surveillance system centered around the National Coordinating Center (NCC) and composed of the National Reference Laboratory (NRL), Collection Centers, Analysis Centers, and the Quality Control Center, and has been operating the project in 3-year cycles since the pilot project (2016). The Collection Centers collect isolates and clinical information, the Analysis Centers perform bacterial identification and antimicrobial susceptibility testing, and the Quality Control Center ensures the accuracy and reliability of the data through quality control. Based on this, national AMR information that meets the standards of the WHO GLASS is being produced (Figure 2).

2. Operation of the National Coordinating Center

The NCC is operated by the Division of Antimicrobial Resistance Control at the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (KDCA) and is responsible for the overall planning and execution of Kor-GLASS. To this end, it establishes surveillance plans and a data management system; selects participating institutions; coordinates cooperation among participating institutions; and collects, verifies, analyzes, and centrally manages surveillance data. In addition, in cooperation with the NRL and the Quality Control Center, it oversees the standardization and quality control of the surveillance system, generates national AMR data to support national policy development, and is responsible for submitting data to WHO GLASS and coordinating international cooperation.

3. Operation of Collection Centers

Collection Centers consist of regional tertiary general hospitals and general hospitals that have full-time laboratory medicine specialists and have obtained Excellent Laboratory Accreditation [5]. Kor-GLASS started in six regions in 2016 and has now expanded to ten regions, collecting 15 target surveillance species isolated from six specimen types along with clinical information. In principle, to exclude duplicate isolates from the same patient, only the first isolate is collected when the same bacterial species is isolated from the same specimen. In addition, the target specimens and species under surveillance are continuously expanded in accordance with WHO GLASS policies and domestic AMR trends (Table 1).

4. Operation of Analysis Centers

Analysis Centers have expanded from four sites during the pilot project to seven sites currently (Table 1) [6], and perform bacterial identification, antimicrobial susceptibility testing (at least ten types of antimicrobial agents per species), and major resistance gene analysis using standardized methods in accordance with the self-established manual for the operation of the national AMR bacteria surveillance system [6]. This manual reflects the susceptibility testing interpretation standards [7,8] of the Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI) and the European Committee on Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing (EUCAST), and applies the latest standards by revising the operating manual when CLSI/EUCAST standards are updated. Additionally, through major resistance gene analysis, it analyzes resistance mechanisms and the distribution and characteristics of high-risk clones.

5. Operation of the Quality Control Center

The Quality Control Center maintains the standardization and reliability of Kor-GLASS data by verifying the test results of the Collection Centers and Analysis Centers through Interlaboratory Proficiency Testing and External Quality Assessment [4,6].

Results

1. Status of Collected Isolates

During the Kor-GLASS project period (2016–2024), a total of 116,955 isolates were collected, including 44,269 isolates from blood, 63,810 from urine, 3,210 from stool, and 5,649 from the lower respiratory tract (2025 data are under validation). Among blood isolates, Escherichia coli was the most common, followed by Klebsiella pneumoniae, Staphylococcus aureus, and Enterococcus faecium. Since surveillance for Candida spp. began in 2020, a total of 2,097 Candida spp. isolates have been collected, and E. coli and K. pneumoniae accounted for most of the isolates from urine (Table 2). Some of the collected isolates have been deposited in the National Culture Collection for Pathogens at the KDCA and are being utilized as research and public health resources.

2. Trends in the Occurrence and Antimicrobial Resistance of Major Bloodstream Infection Pathogens

Analysis of the positivity rates of major bloodstream infection pathogens revealed that E. coli and K. pneumoniae showed a continuous increasing trend, and in particular, the carbapenem resistance rate of K. pneumoniae increased from 2.1% in 2016 to 5.7% in 2024. On the other hand, the carbapenem resistance rate of E. coli remained at a level of <1% (Figure 3). The resistance rates of vancomycin-resistant E. faecium (VRE), methicillin-resistant S. aureus (MRSA), and multidrug-resistant Acinetobacter baumannii (MRAB) showed an overall decreasing trend. On the other hand, the resistance rate of multidrug-resistant Pseudomonas aeruginosa (MRPA) has remained elevated since 2020 (Figure 3). As such, Kor-GLASS has provided evidence to assess the national AMR status by continuously monitoring the occurrence and AMR trends of major bloodstream infection pathogens. However, when interpreting AMR trends, it is necessary to consider that the annual positivity rates and resistance rates may be affected as the number of Collection Centers has gradually increased.

3. Multidrug Resistance and Characterization of Major Antimicrobial-resistant Bacteria

Based on an isolate-based surveillance system, Kor-GLASS continuously analyzes not only AMR rates but also the occurrence status and long-term trends of multidrug-resistant bacteria. In addition, by linking standardized antimicrobial susceptibility testing results with clinical information and analyzing hospital- and community-acquired infection characteristics, resistance genes, and molecular epidemiological characteristics, it contributes to the early detection of the emergence and spread of high-risk antimicrobial-resistant bacteria [9,10].

4. Publication of the Annual Report and National Data Sharing

Based on the Kor-GLASS data, the KDCA has been publishing the “National Antimicrobial Resistant Bacteria Surveillance Annual Report” annually since 2017 and continuously submitting national resistance data for the 12 species under WHO GLASS surveillance (Table 1). The generated data are used to assess the status of domestic AMR and to inform the development of national policies on AMR management, infection prevention and control, and appropriate antimicrobial use.

5. International Reporting of Pandrug-resistant (PDR) Candida auris and Establishment of a National Surveillance System

Based on the GLASS–Emerging Antimicrobial Resistance Reporting (EAR) framework introduced by WHO in 2018, an early detection system for emerging AMR was established through the introduction of surveillance in 2023 and the development of operational procedures in 2025 [11]. Through this system, the first PDR C. auris isolate from blood in the ROK was detected and officially reported to WHO GLASS-EAR. In addition, the occurrence and resistance characteristics of C. auris were continuously monitored to confirm the spread of highly pathogenic clade I [12], and the findings served as supporting evidence for the designation of C. auris infection as a Class 4 notifiable infectious disease and the establishment of a national surveillance system in 2026.

6. Designation as a World Health Organization Collaborating Centre

Based on the operational achievements of Kor-GLASS, the KDCA was designated as a WHO Collaborating Centre for Antimicrobial Resistance Surveillance in 2021, and was further designated in 2025 as the “WHO Collaborating Centre for Antimicrobial Resistance Surveillance and One Health Research (KOR-114),” which includes One Health research. Through this strategy, the ROK has established itself as a core WHO collaborating institution supporting international AMR surveillance and research cooperation.

Conclusion

Kor-GLASS is the only national AMR surveillance system in the ROK developed in accordance with the core principles of WHO GLASS: representativeness, standardization, accuracy, and quality assurance. Over the past 10 years, it has stably operated a standardized surveillance system centered on regional Collection Centers, Analysis Centers, and an independent Quality Control Center, continuously producing high-quality national-scale AMR data, thereby playing a pivotal role in national AMR surveillance in the ROK.

From 2016 to 2024, a total of 116,955 clinical isolates were collected and analyzed to systematically evaluate the AMR status and molecular epidemiological characteristics of major pathogens, and the produced data serve as the foundation for publishing the “National Antimicrobial Resistant Bacteria Surveillance Annual Report” and submitting national data to WHO GLASS. In addition, it is being utilized as key evidence for establishing NAP on AMR, developing infection prevention and control and appropriate antimicrobial use policies, and responding to newly emerging antimicrobial-resistant bacteria. It has also strengthened national early warning and international cooperation systems by establishing an early detection system for newly emerging antimicrobial-resistant bacteria in linkage with WHO GLASS-EAR and reporting the first PDR C. auris case in the ROK to the WHO.

Furthermore, the stable operational achievements and international contributions of Kor-GLASS led to the designation (2021) and redesignation (2025) as a WHO Collaborating Centre for Antimicrobial Resistance Surveillance, and the ROK has established its status as a core collaborating institution supporting the strengthening of AMR surveillance capacity and One Health-based international cooperation in the Asia-Pacific region.

Going forward, Kor-GLASS will evolve into the central platform for Korea’s national surveillance system for antimicrobial-resistant bacteria, building on its 10 years of accumulated operational experience and standardized surveillance system. By implementing whole-genome sequencing at scale [13] and establishing an integrated national AMR surveillance system through close integration with the Korea Antimicrobial Resistance Monitoring System, the Korea Antimicrobial Use Analysis System, and the surveillance system for newly emerging antimicrobial-resistant bacteria, Kor-GLASS will evolve into a key national infrastructure for the early detection of AMR, prediction of its spread, and evidence-based policy formulation. With these efforts, Kor-GLASS is expected to continue strengthening its role as a national surveillance system that not only supports the domestic response to AMR but also leads international surveillance and cooperation.

서 론

항생제 내성(antimicrobial resistance, AMR)은 감염 치료 실패, 사망률 증가 및 의료비 부담을 초래하는 대표적인 공중보건 위협으로 국가 차원의 지속적이고 체계적인 감시와 관리가 요구되고 있다[1,2]. 이에 세계보건기구(World Health Organization, WHO)는 2015년 「항균제 내성 글로벌 행동계획(Global Action Plan on AMR)」을 채택하고, 국가 간 비교 가능한 항균제 내성 자료를 생산하기 위한 국제 항균제 내성 및 사용 감시체계(Global Antimicrobial Resistance Surveillance System, GLASS)를 구축하였다[3].

우리나라는 「제1차 국가 항생제 내성 관리대책」에 따라 2016년 WHO GLASS의 주요 원칙인 대표성(representativeness), 전문성(specialization) 및 표준화(standardization)를 반영한 국가 항균제 내성 감시체계(Global Antimicrobial Resistance Surveillance System in Korea, Kor-GLASS)를 구축하여 운영하고 있다[4,5]. 수집센터는 지역적 대표성을 강화하기 위해 전국을 권역별로 구분하여 각 권역의 500–1,000병상 규모 종합병원을 중심으로 선정하였으며, 참여기관을 단계적으로 확대해 왔다. 또한 균주 기반(isolate-based) 감시와 독립적인 품질관리를 통해 일관되고 신뢰성 있는 내성 정보를 지속적으로 생산하고 있으며, 국가 항생제 내성 관리 정책의 근거자료 제공과 WHO GLASS 등 국제 감시체계와의 연계를 담당하는 핵심 국가 감시체계로서 기능하고 있다(그림 1) [6].

방 법

1. Kor-GLASS 운영체계

Kor-GLASS는 국가조정센터(National Coordinating Center)를 중심으로 국가표준실험실(National Reference Laboratory), 수집센터(Collection Center), 분석센터(Analysis Center), 시험관리센터(Quality Control Center)로 구성된 국가 항균제 내성 감시체계이며, 시범사업(2016년) 이후 3년 단위로 사업을 운영하고 있다. 수집센터는 균주와 임상정보를 수집하고, 분석센터는 균 동정 및 항생제 감수성 시험을 수행하며, 시험관리센터는 품질관리를 통해 자료의 정확성과 신뢰성을 확보하고 있다. 이를 기반으로 WHO GLASS 기준에 부합하는 국가 항생제 내성 정보를 생산하고 있다(그림 2).

2. 국가조정센터 운영

국가조정센터는 질병관리청 항생제내성관리과에서 운영하며, Kor-GLASS의 총괄 기획 및 실행을 담당한다. 이를 위해 감시계획 수립, 데이터 관리 체계 구축, 참여기관의 선정 및 기관 간 협력체계 조정, 감시자료의 수집‧검증‧분석 및 통합 관리를 수행한다. 또한 국가표준실험실 및 시험관리센터와 협력하여 감시체계의 표준화와 품질관리를 운영하고, 국가 항균제 내성 정보를 생산해 국가 정책 수립을 지원하며, WHO GLASS 자료 제출과 국제협력 업무를 담당한다.

3. 수집센터 운영

수집센터는 권역별 상급종합병원 및 종합병원 중 진단검사의학 전문의가 상근하고 우수검사실 신임인증을 획득한 기관으로 구성되어 있다[5]. Kor-GLASS는 2016년 6개 권역에서 시작하여 현재 10개 권역으로 확대되었으며, 6개 검체에서 분리된 15종의 감시 대상 균주와 임상정보를 수집하고 있다. 균주는 동일 환자에서의 중복 분리를 배제하기 위해 검체별 동일 균종이 분리된 경우 최초 분리주만 수집하는 것을 원칙으로 하고 있다. 또한 감시 대상 검체와 균종을 WHO GLASS 정책 및 국내 내성 발생 현황에 따라 지속적으로 확대하고 있다(표 1).

4. 분석센터 운영

분석센터는 시범사업 당시 4개소에서 현재 7개소로 확대되었으며(표 1) [6], 자체 수립한 국내 항균제 내성균 조사 체계 운영 매뉴얼에 따라 표준화된 방법으로 균 동정, 항생제 감수성 시험(균종별 항균제 10종류 이상) 및 주요 내성유전자 분석을 수행한다[6]. 해당 매뉴얼은 Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI) 및 European Committee on Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing (EUCAST)의 감수성 시험 결과 판정 기준[7,8]을 반영하고 있으며, CLSI/EUCAST 기준 변경 시 운영 매뉴얼 개정을 통해 최신 기준을 적용하고 있다. 또한, 주요 내성유전자 분석을 통해 내성 기전 및 고위험 클론(high-risk clone)의 분포와 특성을 분석한다.

5. 시험관리센터 운영

시험관리센터는 검사실 간 숙련도 시험(Interlaboratory Proficiency Testing)과 외부정도관리(External Quality Assessment)를 통해 수집센터와 분석센터의 검사 결과를 검증하여 Kor-GLASS 자료의 표준화와 신뢰성을 유지하고 있다[4,6].

2. 혈액감염 주요 병원균의 발생 및 항생제 내성 추이

혈액감염 주요 병원균의 양성률을 분석한 결과, 대장균과 폐렴막대균은 지속적인 증가 추세를 보였으며, 특히 폐렴막대균의 카바페넴 내성률은 2016년 2.1%에서 2024년 5.7%로 증가하였다. 반면 대장균의 카바페넴 내성률은 1% 미만 수준을 유지하였다(그림 3). 반코마이신 내성 장알균(vancomycin-resistant E. faecium, VRE), 메티실린 내성 황색포도알균(methicillin-resistant S. aureus, MRSA), 다제내성 아시네토박터 바우마니균(multidrug-resistant Acinetobacter baumannii, MRAB)의 내성률은 전반적으로 감소하는 추세를 보였다. 반면 다제내성 녹농균(multidrug-resistant Pseudomonas aeruginosa, MRPA)의 내성률은 2020년 이후 증가한 수준을 유지하였다(그림 3). 이와 같이 Kor-GLASS는 주요 혈액감염 병원체의 발생 및 항생제 내성 추이를 지속적으로 모니터링하여 국가 항생제 내성 현황을 평가하는 근거를 제공해 왔다. 다만, 항생제 내성 추이 해석 시 수집센터 수가 점진적으로 증가함에 따라 연도별 양성률 및 내성률에 영향이 있을 수 있다는 점은 고려할 필요가 있다.

3. 주요 내성균의 다제내성 및 특성 분석

Kor-GLASS는 균주 기반 감시체계를 기반으로 항생제 내성률뿐 아니라 다제내성균(multidrug-resistant)의 발생 현황과 장기 변화 추이를 지속적으로 분석하고 있다. 또한 표준화된 항생제 감수성시험 결과와 임상정보를 연계하여 병원 및 지역사회 감염 특성, 내성유전자 및 분자역학적 특성을 함께 분석함으로써 고위험 내성균의 출현과 확산을 조기에 탐지하는 역할을 수행하고 있다[9,10].

4. 연보 발간 및 국가 데이터 공유

질병관리청은 Kor-GLASS 자료를 기반으로 2017년부터 「국가 항균제 내성균 조사 연보」를 매년 발간하고 있으며, WHO GLASS 감시 대상 12종에 대한 국가 내성 데이터를 지속적으로 제출하고 있다(표 1). 생산된 자료는 국내 항생제 내성 현황을 파악하고 국가 항생제 내성 관리 정책, 감염예방‧관리 및 항생제 적정사용 정책 수립을 위해 활용되고 있다.

5. 범약제내성(pandrug-resistant, PDR) 칸디다 오리스(Candida auris) 국제 보고 및 국가 감시체계 구축

WHO가 2018년에 도입한 GLASS-Emerging Antimicrobial Resistance Reporting (EAR) 체계를 기반으로 2023년 감시 도입 및 2025년 운영 절차 마련을 통해 신규 항생제 내성 조기 탐지 체계를 구축하였다[11]. 해당 체계를 통해 국내 최초의 혈액 유래 PDR 칸디다 오리스를 탐지하여 WHO GLASS-EAR에 공식 보고하였다. 또한 칸디다 오리스의 발생 및 내성 특성을 지속적으로 감시하여 고병원성 clade I형의 확산을 확인하였으며[12], 해당 결과는 2026년 칸디다 오리스 감염증의 제4급 법정감염병 지정 및 국가 감시체계 수립의 근거자료 중 하나로 활용되었다.

6. 세계보건기구 협력센터 지정

Kor-GLASS 운영 성과를 바탕으로 질병관리청은 2021년 WHO 항생제 내성 감시 분야 협력센터(WHO Collaborating Centre)로 지정되었으며, 2025년에는 원헬스(One Health) 연구를 포함하는 「WHO Collaborating Centre for Antimicrobial Resistance Surveillance and One Health Research (KOR-114)」로 확대 지정되었다. 이를 통해 우리나라는 국제 항생제 내성 감시와 연구 협력을 지원하는 WHO 핵심 협력기관으로 자리매김하였다.

결 론

Kor-GLASS는 국제기구(WHO GLASS)의 핵심 원칙인 대표성, 표준화, 정확성(accuracy) 및 품질보증(quality assurance)에 기반하여 구축된 국내 유일의 국가 항균제 내성 감시체계이다. 지난 10년간 권역별 수집센터, 분석센터 및 독립적인 시험관리센터를 중심으로 표준화된 감시체계를 안정적으로 운영하며 전국 단위의 고품질 항균제 내성 자료를 지속적으로 생산하여 우리나라 국가 항균제 내성 감시의 중추적인 역할을 수행하였다.

2016년부터 2024년까지 총 116,955주의 임상 균주를 수집‧분석하여 주요 병원체의 항균제 내성 현황과 분자역학적 특성을 체계적으로 평가하였으며, 생산된 자료는 「국가 항균제 내성균 조사 연보」 발간과 WHO GLASS 국가 데이터 제출의 기반이 되고 있다. 또한 국가 항생제 내성 관리대책 수립, 감염예방관리 및 항생제 적정사용 정책, 신규 출현 내성균 대응을 위한 핵심 근거자료로 활용되고 있으며, WHO GLASS-EAR과 연계한 신규 항균제 내성균 조기 탐지 체계를 구축하고 국내 최초의 PDR 칸디다 오리스를 WHO에 보고함으로써 국가 조기경보 및 국제 공조 체계를 강화하였다.

아울러 WHO 항생제 내성 감시 분야 협력센터 지정(2021년) 및 재지정(2025년)으로 Kor-GLASS의 안정적인 운영과 국제적 기여를 높이게 되었으며, 우리나라는 아시아‧태평양 지역의 항균제 내성 감시 역량 강화와 원헬스 기반 국제협력을 지원하는 핵심 협력기관으로서의 위상을 확립하였다.

향후 Kor-GLASS는 지난 10년간 축적한 운영 경험과 표준화된 감시체계를 기반으로 우리나라 국가 항균제 내성균 감시체계의 중심 플랫폼으로 발전해 나갈 것이다. 전장유전체분석(whole-genome sequencing)을 본격적으로 도입하고[13], 국가 항생제 내성 정보 모니터링(Korea Antimicrobial Resistance Monitoring System), 국가 항생제 사용량 감시체계(Korea Antimicrobial Use Analysis System) 및 신규 출현 내성균 감시체계와의 유기적인 연계를 통해 국가 통합 항균제 내성 감시체계를 구축함으로써 항균제 내성의 조기 탐지와 확산 예측, 근거 기반 정책 수립을 선도하는 핵심 국가 인프라로 도약할 것이다. 이를 바탕으로 국내 항균제 내성 대응은 물론 국제 감시체계와 협력을 선도하는 국가 감시체계로 지속 발전할 것으로 기대된다.

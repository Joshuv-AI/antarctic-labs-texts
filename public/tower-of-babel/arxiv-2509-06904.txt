BIR-Adapter: A Low-Complexity Diffusion Model Adapter for Blind Image Restoration
Cem Eteke, Alexander Griessel, Wolfgang Kellerer, Eckehard Steinbach

Abstract
This paper introduces BIR-Adapter, a low-complexity blind image restoration adapter for diffusion models. The BIR-Adapter enables the utilization of the prior of pre-trained large-scale diffusion models on blind image restoration without training any auxiliary feature extractor. We take advantage of the robustness of pretrained models. We extract features from degraded images via the model itself and extend the self-attention mechanism with these degraded features. We introduce a sampling guidance mechanism to reduce hallucinations. We perform experiments on synthetic and real-world degradations and demonstrate that BIR-Adapter achieves competitive or better performance compared to state-of-the-art methods while having significantly lower complexity. Additionally, its adapter-based design enables integration into other diffusion models, enabling broader applications in image restoration tasks. We showcase this by extending a super-resolution-only model to perform better under additional unknown degradations.

1
Introduction

Blind image restoration is the process of restoring a clean image from a degraded observation without knowing the degradation process. This is a challenging problem in low-level computer vision, as degradations can include, but are not limited to, noise, blur, downsampling, compression artifacts, or even complex cascades of multiple degradations. This variety and the ill-posed nature of the problem require robust models that can generalize across diverse scenarios.

Deep generative models such as Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) enabled photorealistic image synthesis for neural networks
[
9
]
. Researchers leveraged this generative capability to address ill-posed problems, such as style transfer, super-resolution, image inpainting, semantic image synthesis
[
23
,
40
,
6
]
, and blind image restoration
[
37
]
.

Although GAN-based image synthesis was successful, diffusion models have been shown to outperform GANs
[
7
]
. Diffusion models solve a reverse diffusion process that is parameterized by a neural network and gradually remove noise to create an image. The two main factors that contributed to the success of these models are: (i) successful formulation and implementation of the denoising diffusion process on the image space and on the latent space
[
13
,
25
]
, and (ii) billion-parameter scale neural network architectures, made possible by the attention mechanism
[
27
]
. This success extended to their performance on ill-posed problems, including blind image restoration
[
11
]
. In this context, the diffusion model is either trained from scratch for restoration, or a clone of the model is fine-tuned to guide the original backbone model
[
11
]
. The former approach sacrifices the prior learned by a large-scale pre-trained model, while the latter incurs high resource demands, as the clone also contains a large number of parameters.

Modern diffusion models, as mentioned, are a product of large-scale models trained on hyper-scale datasets. Another prominent category of such models is known as foundation models. One example is the vision-language model CLIP
[
24
]
. Cheng
et al
.
showed that the features extracted by CLIP are robust against noise
[
3
]
. Leveraging this, they trained only a decoder for image denoising, resulting in a significantly smaller model without any performance drop.

Inspired by this work, we present
Figure

2
, which illustrates, for a sample image, the similarity of activations of blocks of a diffusion model between a degraded image and its clean counterpart. The figure demonstrates that the diffusion model, depending on the layer, can exhibit varying degrees of robustness to individual or cascaded degradations.

We extend the self-attention mechanism to include these degraded features, which are extracted by the model itself, unlike, for example, from a clone for fine-tuning, significantly reducing the complexity. We further introduce a sampling guidance mechanism that mitigates hallucinations. We name the overall approach BIR-Adapter. We evaluate our approach on both synthetic and real-world degradations. We show that we can significantly reduce the complexity in terms of the number of trained parameters while achieving competitive and, in some cases, better performance. Furthermore, we show that the adapter nature of our model enables plug-and-play into other diffusion models, enabling the combination of blind image restoration with other applications. As a proof of concept, we extend a super-resolution-only model to perform better under unknown degradations.

To summarize, this work introduces BIR-Adapter that (i) enables blind image restoration via training fewer parameters, (ii) extends the diffusion process via guidance, and (iii) enables plug-and-play into other models.

2
Related Work

Image restoration has long been a central topic in low-level computer vision, with early methods relying on handcrafted priors tailored to specific degradations such as downsampling, denoising, and deblurring
[
5
]
. With the advent of deep learning, data-driven models began to surpass classical techniques
[
36
,
8
,
22
]
. These approaches especially excelled in the presence of non-linear or a cascade of degradations
[
35
,
26
]
. Generalization to unknown degradations was further achieved via GAN-based methods such as BSRGAN
[
37
]
and Real-ESRGAN
[
31
]
. They introduced synthetic degradation pipelines that enabled blind restoration under more realistic and complex degradation conditions. Architectural innovations like SwinIR
[
17
]
, which incorporate attention mechanisms, further improved generalization by capturing long-range dependencies.

Diffusion-based generative models, particularly when combined with attention mechanisms at large-scale training, outperformed GAN-based image synthesis
[
7
]
. Latent diffusion models (LDMs) improved the stability and scalability of these techniques
[
25
]
. ControlNet introduced a way to steer these powerful priors by fine-tuning a cloned model and injecting its features into the original diffusion model
[
38
]
. This prior-based control is also applied to image recovery tasks. For instance, PASD introduced pixel-wise cross-attention and a ControlNet-based module for both super-resolution and image stylization
[
33
]
, while StableSR extended LDM’s decoder with feature warping to improve image restoration
[
29
]
. Despite their effectiveness, such models still face challenges when dealing with unknown or compounded degradations. DiffBIR tackled this by incorporating pretrained restoration models like SwinIR into a ControlNet-based pipeline to better generalize across degradation types
[
19
]
. SUPIR approached the problem with a two-stage pipeline—degradation estimation followed by image synthesis—achieving strong performance at the cost of increased training complexity
[
34
]
.

To address the high complexity associated with large ControlNet-based models, Cheng et al. proposed lightweight architectures trained from scratch for super-resolution, introducing DiT-SR
[
4
]
. However, training from scratch hinders generalization to complex degradations. On the other hand, Adapter-based alternatives to full fine-tuning emerged as a low-complexity way to leverage diffusion priors
[
21
]
, and Liang et al. applied this approach to blind image restoration
[
16
]
.

We take the adapter-based approaches one step further and introduce the adapter to the self-attention mechanism, significantly reducing the complexity in terms of trained parameters. We further show our approach’s plug-and-play nature by introducing an improvement on complex degradations for a super-resolution-only method.

3
Background

In this section, we introduce the necessary background that our work relies on. We divided the background into three main parts, in which we discuss the latent diffusion models, sampling, and attention mechanism.

3.1
Latent diffusion models

Diffusion models aim to revert a known
T
-step forward diffusion process defined by the decaying scheduling parameters
\beta_{t}\quad\text{where }t\in\{0,\dots,T\}

[
13
]
. This process starts from a known vector
\mathbf{x_{0}}
and gradually introduces noise until timestep
t
. This defines a Markovian forward process
q
as

q(\mathbf{x}_{t}\mid\mathbf{x}_{t-1})=\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_{t};\sqrt{1-\beta_{t}}\mathbf{x}_{t-1},\beta_{t}\mathbf{I})\text{.}

(1)

We can directly compute the noisy state
\mathbf{x_{t}}
by the reparameterization trick where
\bar{\alpha}_{t}
represents the cumulative scheduling parameter and formulated as
\bar{\alpha}_{t}=\prod_{i=0}^{i=t}\alpha_{i}
where
\alpha_{t}=1-\beta_{t}

q(\mathbf{x}_{t}\mid\mathbf{x}_{0})=\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_{t};\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t}}\mathbf{x}_{0},(1-\bar{\alpha}_{t})\mathbf{I})\text{.}

(2)

A diffusion model aims to reverse this process by modeling the reverse process as a parametric function
p_{\theta}

p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}\mid\mathbf{x}_{t})=\mathcal{N}\big(\mathbf{\mu}_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t},t),\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t},t)\big)

(3)

where
\mathbf{\mu}_{\theta}
and
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\theta}
are controlled by a denoising network
\epsilon_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t},t)
. This model is trained to minimize

\mathbb{E}\big[(\mathbf{\epsilon}-\epsilon_{\theta}(\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t}}\mathbf{x}_{0}+\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t}}\epsilon,t))^{2}\big]

(4)

where the data is generated using the forward model reformulated as
\mathbf{x}_{t}=\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t}}\mathbf{x}_{0}+\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t}}\mathbf{\epsilon}
,
\mathbf{\epsilon}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I})
.

Although diffusion models are shown to outperform GANs, it was challenging to perform a stable synthesis
[
7
]
. Rombach
et al
.
resolved this issue by introducing the Latent Diffusion Models (LDM)
[
25
]
. Instead of supplying a noise-free image
I_{0}
as
\mathbf{x}_{0}
, they perform the diffusion process on a latent space, dictated by an autoencoder with an encoder
\mathbf{x}_{0}=\mathcal{E}(I_{0})
and a decoder
I_{0}=\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{x}_{0})
.

Finally, for controllable diffusion models, the denoising model is extended to include a control signal
\mathbf{y}_{0}
as
\epsilon_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{y}_{0},t)
. This
\mathbf{y}_{0}
could be used such that the LDM is trained together with it or fed through a fine-tuned approach such as ControlNet
[
38
]
. The latter enables the utilization of a hyper-scale LDM as a prior. However, we discussed above that it requires fine-tuning of a large set of parameters, increasing the complexity of the overall model.

3.2
Sampling

Once the LDM is trained, during inference, the goal is to go from noise
\mathbf{x}_{T}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I})
to a clean image
\mathbf{x}_{0}
. Song
et al
.
introduced Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models, which utilize the estimated noise-free latents
\mathbf{x}_{t\rightarrow 0}
to solve the inverse process more efficiently

\mathbf{x}_{t-1}=\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}\mathbf{x}_{t\rightarrow 0}+\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}\epsilon_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{y}_{0},t)\text{.}

(5)

The noise-free latents are estimated by simply inverting the forward model

\mathbf{x}_{t\rightarrow 0}=\bigg(\frac{\mathbf{x}_{t}-\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t}}\epsilon_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{y}_{0},t)}{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t}}}\bigg)\text{.}

(6)

3.3
Attention mechanism

Attention, introduced in the Transformer architecture
[
27
]
, allows models to dynamically weight input features based on their relevance to each other. This is achieved through learned similarity scores between query and key vectors. Let
\mathbf{z}^{k}_{t}
be the output of the layer
k
of
\epsilon_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{y}_{0},t)
. Self-attention is computed as

Attention(Q,K,V)=softmax(\frac{QK^{T}}{\sqrt{d_{k}}})V

(7)

where
Q=\mathbf{z}^{k}_{t}\mathbf{W}^{Q}
,
K=\mathbf{z}^{k}_{t}\mathbf{W}^{K}
, and
V=\mathbf{z}^{k}_{t}\mathbf{W}^{V}
, and
\{\mathbf{W}^{Q},\mathbf{W}^{K},\mathbf{W}^{V}\}
constitute the trainable attention weights named query, key, and value, respectively. Key and value can be replaced with some other modality to form cross-attention. For example, in the case of LDMs, this other modality can be text embeddings of prompts to enable prompt-based image synthesis via
K=\mathbf{y}_{0}\mathbf{W}^{K}
, and
V=\mathbf{y}_{0}\mathbf{W}^{V}
. A typical LDM consists of self-attention and cross-attention layers attached to one another consecutively, as shown in
Fig.

3
. Both attention types finally transform the attention output via
\mathbf{W}^{O}
.

4
Methodology

We are dealing with the problem of blind image restoration where an unknown degradation function
\mathcal{F}
and noise
\eta
were applied to the image such that

\tilde{I}=\mathcal{F}(I_{0})+\eta\text{.}

(8)

The goal is to restore
I_{0}
from
\tilde{I}
. To this extent, we introduce the BIR-Adapter, which can be plugged into the self-attention mechanism of pretrained LDMs to enable blind image restoration. As motivated in
Section

1
we take advantage of the denoising prior of LDMs. This eliminates the necessity of training a large set of parameters with a potentially large set of training data. Since we utilize LDMs, we treat this problem as restoration on the latent space,
i.e
.
obtaining
\mathbf{x}_{0}
from its degraded version
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}=\mathcal{E}(\tilde{I})
.

4.1
BIR-Adapter

Intuitively, the attention mechanism asks the question: ”How should each item (
Q
) be replaced (
V
) based on their relevance (
K
)?”. In a blind image restoration setting, the question is ”How should the degraded parts be cleaned based on their relevance to the clean features?”. In other words, the query consists of the degraded features, while key and value consist of the clean features. However, we do not know the clean features.

Parallel to the diffused latents
\mathbf{x}_{t}
, we pass the degraded latents
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}
through
\epsilon_{\theta}
and end up with, for each layer
k
, the features
\tilde{\mathbf{z}}^{k}
. As we discussed in
Section

3.2
, the reverse diffusion process solved by a denoising model
\epsilon_{\theta}
, denoises,
i.e
.
cleans an image. Inspired by this, we discussed in
Section

1
that we formed the hypothesis that if
\epsilon_{\theta}
is cleaning an image, it should be robust against degradations.
Figure

2
shows, for an example image under various degradations, a proof of concept for this idea. Note that this is not a claim that this always holds true, but a visual motivation for the idea behind our approach. Hence, these degraded features should contain enough information about the original image so we can find similarities between the features
\mathbf{z}^{k}_{t}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{z}}^{k}
. As such, we treat the currently diffused features
\mathbf{z}^{k}_{t}
of a layer
k
at diffusion timestep
t
as the
clean
features to answer the question formulated above. Using these definitions, we formulate an attention mechanism as follows

\displaystyle Attention(Q,K,V)\mathbf{W}^{O}

(9)

\displaystyle+Attention(\tilde{Q},K^{\prime},V^{\prime})\mathbf{W}^{\prime O}\text{,}

where
\tilde{Q}=\tilde{\mathbf{z}}^{k}\mathbf{W}^{Q}
,
K^{\prime}=\mathbf{z}^{k}_{t}\mathbf{W}^{\prime K}
, and
V^{\prime}=\mathbf{z}^{k}_{t}\mathbf{W}^{\prime V}
. This formulation allows us to fine-tune the parameter set
\{\mathbf{W}^{\prime K},\mathbf{W}^{\prime V},\mathbf{W}^{\prime O}\}
of each self-attention layer, initialized via their self-attention counterparts, resulting in lower complexity.
Figure

3
visualizes this architecture inside a simplified LDM. We do not fine-tune the query weights since we want to preserve the features of the degraded image.

4.2
Guided sampling

As the resolution of an image increases and goes beyond the supported resolution of an LDM, a common practice is to tile the latent space with overlaps and execute the diffusion model on the tiles. Finally, the tiles are merged with certain weighting functions
[
29
]
. In our approach, we observed that if a tile only consists of low-frequency information, BIR-Adapter leads to some severe hallucinations. A common practice to avoid this issue is using an initial restoration step, either via an off-the-shelf method
[
19
]
or a custom-trained one
[
34
]
. We assume that we have such an initial restoration, namely
I_{0}^{init}
.

We use this information to guide the diffusion-based sampling discussed in
Section

3.2
. However, we do not apply this guidance to high-frequency regions. To achieve this, we utilize the downsampled and normalized gradients
\mathcal{G}_{\downarrow}
estimated via the Sobel filter
[
19
]
. We compute the corresponding guidance weights as

\mathcal{W}=1-\mathcal{G}_{\downarrow}(I_{0}^{init})\text{.}

(10)

We then use the estimated noise-free latent
\mathbf{x}_{t\rightarrow 0}
found in
Eq.

5
and update it to include the clean low-frequency information that the diffusion process will follow, reducing the likelihood of hallucination

Experiments

5.1
Training

37\times 10^{6}

and
\mathbf{W}^{\prime V}

\mathbf{W}^{\prime O}

7
Conclusion

This paper introduced BIR-Adapter. A low-complexity blind image restoration adapter for diffusion models. We hypothesized that diffusion models should be robust against degradations. This enabled us to extend the self-attention mechanism to include features extracted by the denoising diffusion model itself from the given degraded images. This eliminated the need for any auxiliary model that is trained to extract features from the degraded images. This simplification of feature extraction, combined with adapter-based attention-level manipulations, enabled the utilization of the prior of large-scale diffusion models without increasing the number of parameters significantly. We further mitigated hallucinations with a guidance mechanism. We showed on two datasets under synthetic and real degradations that BIR-Adapter achieves comparable and in some cases better performance than the state of the art, despite being significantly lower in complexity. We discussed the importance of the guidance and the trade-off between reference-based and reference-free metrics. We further displayed the plug-and-play nature of BIR-Adapter by extending a super-resolution-only method to perform better under additional unknown degradations.

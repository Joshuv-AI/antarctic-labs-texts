Langlands parameters for reductive groups over finite fields
Naoki Imai, David A. Vogan, Jr

Abstract
We define Langlands parameters for connected reductive groups over finite fields and formulate the Langlands correspondence for finite fields using these parameters.



1
Introduction

The goal of this paper is try to formulate a parametrization of
representations of a finite Chevalley group
G({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
in terms of the Langlands dual group over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
of
G
. One motivation is to relate
this parametrization for
G({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
to the (still
conjectural!) Langlands parametrization of irreducible representations
of groups over local fields. The reason for using the
dual group over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
is that Langlands’ philosophy suggests that representations of a reductive group
G
on
k=\overline{k}
vector spaces ought to be related to (maps of a Weil group to) a
k
-dual group.
Such a parametrization was given for representations of
GL_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
by Ian Macdonald in
[
Mac80
]
.

For more general Chevalley groups, parametrizations
involving the dual group over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
may be found in

[
DL76
]
,
[
Lus84a
]
, and
[
Lus88
]
. A formulation using the
complex dual group is stated in
[
Lus84b
]
, but even this formulation
is not so well connected to Langlands parameters. The reason is that
the Deligne–Lusztig and Lusztig formulations are stated in terms of a
single
element
of the dual group. In the most fundamental and
simplest example where
G({\mathbb{F}}_{q})={\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
, a
representation is simply a character of the multiplicative group

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
. The dual group over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is

\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
. The special dual group elements that
Deligne and Lusztig consider in
[
DL76
]
and
[
Lus88
]
are elements of
order
q-1
; that is, elements of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
. They are
therefore seeking to parametrize
characters
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
by
elements
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
. Because
the multiplicative group of a finite field is cyclic, such a
parametrization is possible, but it is never natural. The choices
required appear in
[
DL76
, (5.0.1)–(5.0.2)]
: isomorphisms

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{\sim}}({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})_{p^{\prime}}

(1.1)

\displaystyle\text{roots of $1$, order prime to $p$ in $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}^{\times}$}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{\sim}}({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})_{p^{\prime}}.\qquad\qquad

The first of these choices appears also in

[
Lus84a
, (8.4.3)]
. On the right in both isomorphisms is the
additive group of elements of order prime to
p
in
{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
. The isomorphisms exist essentially because all cyclic
groups of the same order are isomorphic; but they cannot be chosen
naturally. The field
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
appears in the second
because the methods of étale cohomology employed by Deligne and
Lusztig produce representations not on complex vector spaces but
rather on vector spaces over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
.

In this paper, we define the Weil–Deligne group for a finite field,
and use these to formulate the Langlands correspondence for the finite
field. Each fiber of this correspondence should be parametrized by
irreducible representations of a finite group attached to a Langlands
parameter, just as for other Langlands correspondences.
In a subsequent paper by the first author, we construct the Langlands correspondence for finite
fields under the good prime assumption, and discuss a conjectural relation between the Langlands correspondence for finite fields and the categorical local Langlands correspondence.

Langlands dual group

The point of root data is that they provide a combinatorial way to
specify a reductive group. Here is a statement.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
k
be a field with separable closure

k^{\mathrm{sep}}
, and let
G
and be a connected reductive algebraic group
defined over
k
. Fix a Borel pair
(T,B)
(not necessarily defined over

). Write

\mathcal{BR}
for the corresponding based root datum
(
[
ABV92
, Definition 2.10]
).

(1)

There is a natural action of the Galois group
\Gamma=\Gal(k^{\mathrm{sep}}/k)
on the based root datum. This action depends
only on the inner class of the
k
-rational form
G
. The action factors
through the Galois group of a finite Galois extension
E/k
.

(2)

Suppose
T^{\prime}\subset B^{\prime}\subset G^{\prime}
is a Borel pair
in another connected reductive algebraic group over
k^{\mathrm{sep}}
, with based root datum
\mathcal{BR}^{\prime}
, and that

\Xi\colon\mathcal{BR}\rightarrow\mathcal{BR}^{\prime}

is an isomorphism of based root data, then

is induced by an

\xi\colon(T\subset B\subset G)\rightarrow(T^{\prime}\subset B^{\prime}\subset G^{\prime})

The isomorphism

is uniquely determined up to pre-composition
with
\Ad(t)
(for some

), or post-composition with

\Ad(t^{\prime})
(for some
t^{\prime}\in T^{\prime}
).

(3)

Suppose that we have pinnings
{\mathcal{P}}
for

and
{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime}
for

. Then
\Xi
is induced by a unique isomorphism of

\xi_{{\mathcal{P}},{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime}}\colon(G,{\mathcal{P}})\rightarrow(G^{\prime},{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime}).

(4)

Suppose
\mathcal{BR}^{\prime\prime}
is any based root datum. Then
there is a Borel pair

, with a pinning

{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime\prime}
, in a
reductive algebraic group

over

\mathcal{BR}^{\prime\prime}
. Because of (3), the pair

(5)

In the setting of (4), suppose in addition that

of a finite Galois extension

. Then there is a
unique definition of
G^{\prime\prime}
over
k
with the following properties:

(a)

The torus
T^{\prime\prime}
is defined over
k
, and the
corresponding action of
\Gamma
on
X^{*}(T^{\prime\prime})
is the

\mathcal{BR}^{\prime\prime}
.

(b)

Each map
\phi_{\alpha^{\prime\prime}}\colon\SL_{2}\to G^{\prime\prime}
in the pinning

is defined over
E
(using the
standard definition of
\SL_{2}
over
E
).

As a consequence of these properties,
B^{\prime\prime}
is also defined
over

, so that

is quasisplit.

Corollary 2.2
.

Suppose
(G,{\mathcal{P}})
is a
connected reductive
algebraic group with a pinning over a separably closed field

, and
\mathcal{BR}
is the corresponding based root
datum. Write

for the group of algebraic

.

Suppose
G
is a reductive algebraic group defined

depends only

k
-rational form

The general shape of a “Langlands conjecture” for group
representations is

irreducible representations of
G(k)
on

K
-vector spaces (up to equivalence) should fall into disjoint

\Pi_{\phi}
indexed by Langlands parameters

(up to

G^{\vee}(K)
).

(2.2)

In this conjecture, a

is a group homomorphism

\phi\colon W_{k}\rightarrow{}^{L}G

(2.3)

subject to requirements including

(1)

\im(\phi)
is semisimple;

(2)

\phi
is compatible with the natural projections to

\Gal(k^{\mathrm{sep}}/k)
.

In this definition of Langlands parameter, the group

is a

for the field

. (Weil groups were defined for local
and global fields in
[

. We have not tried to determine
whether Weil’s motivation in that paper, a good formulation of class

(2.4)

Recall that for a local field

Weil groups of finite fields

Suppose
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is a finite field, and that
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is an algebraic closure. We know that

\Gamma=\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q})=\varprojlim_{m}{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}};

the generator of this group is the
arithmetic Frobenius

\sigma_{q}\colon\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q},\qquad\sigma_{q}(x)=x^{q}.

For an algebraic variety
X
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
let
F\colon X\to X
be the
q
-th power geometric Frobenius morphism.
We will try to
write
\sigma_{q}
for anything related to a Galois
group
action (so that

\sigma_{q}
is invertible, and typically only
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear) and
F

for anything related to an
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-morphism (so often

not
invertible).

The following definition is motivated by
[
Mac80
, §3]
.

Definition 3.1
.

We put

I_{k}=\varprojlim_{m}{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\times}

(3.1)

where the limit is taken over the norm maps

N_{md,m}\colon{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{md}}^{\times}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\times}.

(3.2)

We define the Weil group of
k
by

W_{k}=I_{k}\rtimes\langle\sigma_{q}\rangle

(3.3)

where the conjugation by
\sigma_{q}
acts on
I_{k}
as
q
-th power.

Remark 3.2
.

There is a natural system
\{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{\times}\rtimes\Gal(\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}/\mathbb{F}_{q})\}_{m\geq 0}
of finite quotients of
W_{k}
. These look similar to
W_{\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{C}^{\times}\rtimes\Gal(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R})

Definition 3.3
.

Suppose
k={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is a finite field. Suppose
G
is a reductive algebraic group over

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
,
K
is an algebraically closed field, and
{}^{L}G
is the
L
-group of
G
over
K

(Definition
2.3
). A Weil L-parameter is a group homomorphism

\phi\colon W_{k}\rightarrow{}^{L}G

(3.4)

such that

(1)

the map
\phi
is compatible with projections to
\Gal(\overline{k}/k)
;

(2)

\im(\phi)
is semisimple; and

(3)

\phi|_{I_{k}}
factors to some finite quotient
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\times}
of
I_{k}
.

For a Weil L-parameter
\phi
, write
\phi_{0}
for
\phi|_{I_{k}}
.
The (pointwise) stabilizer of
\phi_{0}
is

Z_{G^{\vee}}(\phi_{0})=\{y\in G^{\vee}\mid\Ad(y)(\phi(w))=\phi(w)\ \ (w\in I_{k})\};

(3.5)

this is a (possibly disconnected) equal rank reductive subgroup of

G^{\vee}
. The Dynkin diagram of
(G^{\vee})^{\phi}
is obtained from
the extended Dynkin diagram of
G^{\vee}
by deleting a nonempty set
of vertices in each simple factor.

We say that two Weil L-parameters
\phi
and
\phi^{\prime}
are equivalent
if the following condition is satisfied: there is
g\in G^{\vee}
such that

\Ad(g)(\phi_{0})=\phi_{0}^{\prime}
, and the images of
\Ad(g)(\phi(\sigma_{q}))
and

\phi^{\prime}(\sigma_{q})
in
{}^{L}G/Z_{G^{\vee}}(\phi^{\prime}_{0})
are same.

We write

\Phi_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(G)

(3.6)

for the set of equivalence classes of Weil L-parameters.

Definition 3.4
.

We say that

\phi_{0}\colon I_{k}\to{}^{L}G

is an inertial L-parameter if it is the restriction of a Weil L-parameter to
I_{k}
.
We say that two inertial L-parameters
\phi_{0}
and
\phi_{0}^{\prime}
are equivalent if they
are conjugate by some

Definition 3.9
.

In the setting of Definition

(\phi,T^{\vee})
such that

(1)

\phi\colon W_{k}\rightarrow{}^{L}G
is a
Weil L-parameter;

(2)

T^{\vee}
is a maximal torus in
G^{\vee}
such that
\phi(I_{k})\subset T^{\vee}
and
\im\phi\subset N_{{}^{L}G}(T^{\vee})

We say that two rigid Weil L-parameters

Proposition 3.10
.

Suppose we are in the setting of Definitions

3.3
and
3.9
.

(1)

Any Weil L-parameter
\phi
is equivalent to the first term of a rigid Weil L-parameter
(\phi^{\prime},T^{\vee})
.

(2)

Assume that two rigid Weil L-parameters
(\phi,T^{\vee})
and
(\phi^{\prime},T^{\vee})

satisfy
\phi|_{I_{k}}=\phi^{\prime}|_{I_{k}}
. Then there is

such that
\phi^{\prime}(\sigma_{q})=\phi(\sigma_{q})g
. They
are equivalent if and only if
g\in N_{Z_{G^{\vee}}(\phi_{0})_{0}}(T^{\vee})
.

For the first, the subgroup

\phi(I_{k})\subset G^{\vee}
is cyclic and
semisimple, and therefore contained in a maximal torus
T^{\vee}

of
G^{\vee}
; then automatically
T^{\vee}\subset Z_{G^{\vee}}(\phi_{0})
. Then

\Ad(\phi(\sigma_{q})^{-1})(T^{\vee})=T_{0}^{\vee}

is another torus of

; so there is an

so that

\Ad(g_{0})(T^{\vee})=T_{0}^{\vee}.

If we define

, then the conclusion is that

\Ad(g)(T^{\vee})=T^{\vee},

so that
g\in N_{{}^{L}G}(T^{\vee})
. Because

,

has the same image

as
\phi(\sigma_{q})
. Similarly,
because
g_{0}
centralizes the image of
\phi_{0}
, we have

\Ad(g)(\phi(w))=\Ad(\phi(\sigma_{q}))(\phi(w))=\phi(w)^{q}

for

.
We define
\phi^{\prime}
by
\phi^{\prime}|_{I_{k}}=\phi|_{I_{k}}
and

\phi^{\prime}(\sigma_{q})=g
.
Then
(\phi^{\prime},T^{\vee})
is a rigid Weil L-parameter, proving
(1).

The second assertion follows easily from the definition.

; so we seek to
enumerate these. Now

\displaystyle N_{{}^{L}G}(T^{\vee}_{0})

\displaystyle=\Gal(\overline{k}/k)\ltimes N_{{}^{\vee}G}(T^{\vee}_{0}),

(3.15)

\displaystyle N_{{}^{L}G}(T^{\vee}_{0})/T^{\vee}_{0}

\displaystyle=\Gal(\overline{k}/k)\ltimes W(G^{\vee},T^{\vee}_{0}).

We write

W=W(G^{\vee},T^{\vee}_{0}).

(3.16)

It is now clear that equivalence classes of rigid Weil L-parameters
are exactly the same thing as
W
-orbits of
pairs

(\phi_{0},x_{0}),\qquad\phi_{0}\colon I_{k}\rightarrow T^{\vee}_{0},\quad x_{0}\in\sigma_{q}\cdot W\subset\Gal(\overline{k}/k)\ltimes W

(3.17)

subject to the requirement that

\Ad(x)(\phi_{0}(w))=\phi_{0}(w^{q}),

(3.18)

\Ad(x^{m})(\phi_{0}(w))=\phi_{0}(w^{q^{m}}).

(3.19)

If
m_{0}
is a positive integer divisible by the order of every element
of
\sigma_{q}W
, then (
3.19
) implies that the image of
\phi_{0}

consists of elements of order dividing
q^{m_{0}}-1
. This means in
particular that
\phi_{0}
must factor to
{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}_{q^{m_{0}}}
. If we
choose a multiplicative generator
\eta
of this group, then
\phi_{0}

is determined by the single element
y_{0}=\phi_{0}(\eta)\in T^{\vee}
,
which is required only to satisfy

x_{0}y_{0}x_{0}^{-1}=y_{0}^{q}.

(3.20)

That is,
for each element
x_{0}\in\sigma_{q}\cdot W
, the set of rigid Langlands
parameters
(\phi_{0},x_{0})
may be identified with the finite subgroup of
elements
y_{0}\in T^{\vee}
(necessarily of order dividing
q^{m}-1
)
satisfying (
3.20
)
.

Therefore the equivalence classes of rigid Weil L-parameters are
partitioned by
W
-conjugacy classes in the coset
\sigma_{q}\cdot W
; and if a
conjugacy class has representative
x_{0}
, then the corresponding set
of parameters may be labelled (not canonically) by the finite group
defined by (
3.20
).

(Deligne–Lusztig

(\phi,T^{\vee})

(1)

4
Weil–Deligne groups and a Langlands correspondence for finite fields

We want to refine the partition of the representations
\Pi(G(k))
in
(
2.2
). We will turn next to a rough outline of the
idea introduced by Deligne for doing that.

The general shape of a Deligne’s modified version of “Langlands conjecture” for group
representations is

irreducible
representations of
G(k)
on
K
-vector spaces (up to
equivalence) should fall into disjoint
packets

\Pi_{\varphi}
indexed by L-parameters

\varphi
of Weil–Deligne type (up to conjugation by
G^{\vee}(K)
).

(4.1)

The conjecture includes an idea about the structure of an
L-packet:

representations
in a packet
\Pi_{\varphi}
should be indexed approximately by some irreducible

G^{\vee}(K)
-equivariant local systems of
K
-vector spaces on
G^{\vee}(K)\cdot\varphi
; that is, by irreducible
K
-representations of the group of
connected components
G^{\vee}(K)^{\varphi}/G^{\vee}(K)^{\varphi}_{0}
.

(4.2)

An even more optimistic version (proven for real groups in
[
ABV92
]
)
is

the category of

G(k)
representations built from a packet
\Pi_{\varphi}
should
be in duality with the category of
G^{\vee}(K)
-equivariant
perverse sheaves on
G^{\vee}(K)\cdot\varphi
.

(4.3)

In this conjecture, an
L-parameter of Weil–Deligne type
is an
algebraic group homomorphism

\varphi\colon\mathit{WD}_{k}\rightarrow{}^{L}G

(4.4)

subject to requirements including

(1)

\im(\varphi|_{W_{k}})
is semisimple;

(2)

\varphi
is compatible with the natural projections to

\Gal(k^{\mathrm{sep}}/k)
.

In this definition of L-parameter of Weil–Deligne type, the group
\mathit{WD}_{k}
is
a
Weil–Deligne group
for the field
k
. We will not state
general requirements for a Weil–Deligne group,
because we do not know how to
formulate them in a way consistent with our fond hope: that there is a
definition of something like an archimedean Weil–Deligne “group” (or
at least of its representations) that incorporates the modified notion
of “Langlands parameter” introduced in
[
ABV92
]
. The same fond
hope asks also that this archimedean definition be made consistent with
Deligne’s non-archimedean definition in
[
Del73
, 8.3.6]
.

Here at any rate is a definition of a Weil–Deligne group for a finite field. Each Weil L-parameter
\phi
will have
several extensions to an L-parameter
\varphi
; we will
try to arrange a corresponding partition of each
L
-packet

\Pi_{\phi}
into several smaller packets.

Definition 4.1
.

The Weil–Deligne group of the finite
field
k
is the locally pro-algebraic group
scheme

\mathit{WD}_{k}=\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}\rtimes W_{k}

(4.5)

over
\mathbb{Z}[1/p]
, where
(\sigma_{q}^{n},w)\in W_{k}
acts on

\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
by the multiplication by
q^{n}
.

Assume that the characteristic of
K
is not
p
.
An L-parameter of Weil–Deligne type is a locally
pro-algebraic group homomorphism

\varphi\colon\mathit{WD}_{k}(K)\rightarrow{}^{L}G

(4.6)

satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

The map
\varphi
is compatible with projections to
\Gal(\overline{k}/k)
.

(2)

\varphi|_{I_{k}}
factors to some finite quotient
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\times}
of
I_{k}
.

We say that the L-parameter
\varphi
is special
if
\varphi|_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}(K)}(1)
is a special unipotent element of

(G^{\vee})^{\varphi(I_{k})}
.
We say that
\varphi
is Frobenius semisimple if

\varphi(\sigma_{q})
is semisimple in
{}^{L}G
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
g=su\in G^{\vee}
be the Jordan decomposition.
Then we have

\pi_{0}\left(Z_{Z_{G^{\vee}}(s)^{\circ}}(u)/Z\left(Z_{G^{\vee}}(s)^{\circ}\right)\right)\cong\pi_{0}\left(Z_{Z_{G^{\vee}}(s)^{\circ}}(u)/Z(G^{\vee})\right).

Proof.

We take a Borel pair
T\subset B
in
G_{k^{\mathrm{sep}}}
. We may assume that
s\in T^{\vee}
. Let
\Delta
be the set of simple root of
G_{k^{\mathrm{sep}}}
with respect to
T\subset B
.
Let
I\subset\Delta
be the subset consisting of elements which are the simple coroots of
Z_{G^{\vee}}(s)^{\circ}
.
Since
\mathbb{Z}\Delta/\mathbb{Z}I
has no torsion, the natural map

(X^{*}(T)/\mathbb{Z}I)_{\mathrm{tor}}\to(X^{*}(T)/\mathbb{Z}\Delta)_{\mathrm{tor}}

is injective.
Then
\pi_{0}(Z(G^{\vee}))\to\pi_{0}(Z(Z_{G^{\vee}}(s)^{\circ}))
is surjective,
since it is identified with

(X^{*}(T)/\mathbb{Z}\Delta)_{\mathrm{tor}}^{\vee}\to(X^{*}(T)/\mathbb{Z}I)_{\mathrm{tor}}^{\vee}.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\pi_{0}(Z_{Z_{G^{\vee}}(s)^{\circ}}(u)/Z(Z_{G^{\vee}}(s)^{\circ}))

\displaystyle\cong\pi_{0}(Z_{Z_{G^{\vee}}(s)^{\circ}}(u))/\pi_{0}(Z(Z_{G^{\vee}}(s)^{\circ}))

\displaystyle\cong\pi_{0}(Z_{Z_{G^{\vee}}(s)^{\circ}}(u))/\pi_{0}(Z(G^{\vee}))

\displaystyle\cong\pi_{0}(Z_{Z_{G^{\vee}}(s)^{\circ}}(u)/Z(G^{\vee})),

where we use the surjectivity of

\pi_{0}(Z(G^{\vee}))\to\pi_{0}(Z(Z_{G^{\vee}}(s)^{\circ}))
at the second equality.
∎

We put

A_{Z_{G^{\vee}}(\varphi(I_{k}))^{\circ}}(\varphi(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}))=\pi_{0}(Z_{Z_{G^{\vee}}(\varphi(I_{k}))^{\circ}}(\varphi(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}))/Z(Z_{G^{\vee}}(\varphi(I_{k}))^{\circ})).

We define
Lusztig’s canonical quotient
\overline{A}_{Z_{G^{\vee}}(\varphi(I_{k}))^{\circ}}(\varphi(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}))

of
A_{Z_{G^{\vee}}(\varphi(I_{k}))^{\circ}}(\varphi(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}))
as in
[
Lus84a
, 13.1]
using the isomorphism given by Lemma
4.2
.
We put
\varphi_{0}=\varphi|_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}\times I_{k}}
and

A(\varphi_{0})=\pi_{0}\left(Z_{G^{\vee}}(\varphi_{0})/Z(G^{\vee})\right).

(4.7)

Further we put

\overline{A}(\varphi_{0})=A(\varphi_{0})/\Ker(A_{Z_{G^{\vee}}(\varphi(I_{k}))^{\circ}}(\varphi(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}))\to\overline{A}_{Z_{G^{\vee}}(\varphi(I_{k}))^{\circ}}(\varphi(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}))).

(4.8)

To obtain an enlargement of
\overline{A}(\varphi_{0})
, we put

\widetilde{Z}(\varphi_{0})=\{(g,\sigma_{q}^{m})\in{}^{L}G\mid\Ad((g,\sigma_{q}^{m}))(\varphi_{0}(x))=\varphi_{0}(\Ad(\sigma_{q}^{m})(x))\ \textrm{for all $x\in\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}\times I_{k}$}\}

\widetilde{A}(\varphi_{0})=\widetilde{Z}(\varphi_{0})/\Ker(Z_{G^{\vee}}(\varphi_{0})\to\overline{A}(\varphi_{0})).

(4.9)

We have

\varphi(\sigma_{q})\in\widetilde{Z}(\varphi_{0})
.
Let
\overline{\varphi(\sigma_{q})}
be the image of

\varphi(\sigma_{q})
under the natural projection

\widetilde{Z}(\varphi_{0})\to\widetilde{A}(\varphi_{0}).

We say that two
L-parameters
\varphi
and
\varphi^{\prime}
of Weil–Deligne type are equivalent
if the following condition is satisfied:
there is
g\in G^{\vee}
such that

\Ad(g)(\varphi_{0})=\varphi_{0}^{\prime}
and

\overline{\varphi(\sigma_{q})}
corresponds to

\overline{\varphi^{\prime}(\sigma_{q})}
under the bijection

\widetilde{A}(\varphi_{0})\cong\widetilde{A}(\varphi^{\prime}_{0})

induced by
\Ad(g)
, where

\varphi_{0}=\varphi|_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}\times I_{k}}
and

\varphi_{0}^{\prime}=\varphi^{\prime}|_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}\times I_{k}}
.
Let
\Phi_{K}(G)
be the equivalence classes of
Frobenius semisimple L-parameters over
K
of
G
.
We write
\Phi_{K}(G)_{\mathrm{sp}}\subset\Phi_{K}(G)
for

We put

A_{\varphi}=Z_{\overline{A}(\varphi_{0})}(\overline{\varphi(\sigma_{q})}).

(4.10)

The following is a formulation of the Langlands correspondence for finite fields.

There is a natural map

Rate of metastability of an iterative algorithm for quadratic optimization
Paulo Firmino

Abstract
In this paper, relying on methods from proof mining, we provide a quantitative analysis of a theorem due to Xu [ 20 ] , stating that an iteration strongly converges to the solution of a well known quadratic optimization problem. Rates of metastability and some rates of asymptotic regularity were obtained. We get quadratic rates of asymptotic regularity for particular sequences.

1
Introduction

Let
H
be a real Hilbert space and
l\in\mathbb{N}
. Xu
[
20
]
considered the well known quadratic optimization problem:

\displaystyle\mathrm{(P)}\quad\min_{x\in F}\frac{1}{2}\langle Ax,x\rangle-\langle x,u\rangle

(1)

where
u\in H
,
A
is a strongly positive self-adjoint bounded linear operator and
F
is the set of common fixed points of
T_{0},\ldots,T_{l-1}
, a family of nonexpansive mappings on
H
.

Xu would proceed with an iterative approach, defining the following iteration:

x_{n+1}=(I-\alpha_{n+1}A)T_{n+1}x_{n}+\alpha_{n+1}u

where
(\alpha_{n})\subset(0,1]
and we define
T_{n}
for
n\geq l
as
T_{n\bmod l}
.

Xu proved the following strong convergence result

Theorem 1
.

[
20
]

Assume the following hold:

1.

\lim_{n}\alpha_{n}=0
and
\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{n}=+\infty
,

2.

\lim_{n}\frac{\alpha_{n}}{\alpha_{n+l}}=1
or
\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}|\alpha_{n}-\alpha_{n+l}|<+\infty
,

3.

F=\Fix(T_{l-1}\cdots T_{1}T_{0})=\Fix(T_{0}T_{l-1}\cdots T_{1})=\ldots=\Fix(T_{l-2}\cdots T_{0}T_{l-1})
.

Then
(x_{n})
converges strongly to the unique solution
x^{*}
of Problem (P).

Our goal is to present a quantitative analysis of Xu’s result and obtain rates of metastability.

This form of quantitative analysis of mathematical results emerged from the research program of proof mining
[
11
,
13
,
14
]
, which consists in analysing mathematical proofs using proof-theoretic tools (namely, functional interpretations) with the objective of obtaining additional computational information.
Theorems regarding convergence of sequences, as this is the case, are a common interest in proof mining. In such cases, the goal is to obtain quantitative information in the form of rates of metastability and rates of asymptotic regularity. A sequence
(x_{n})
is said to be metastable if

\forall k\in\mathbb{N}\forall f\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}\exists n\forall i,j\in[n,f(n)]\left(\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|\leq\frac{1}{k+1}\right)

A metastability rate consists of a bound
\phi(k,f)
on
n
. Metastability is equivalent to the Cauchy property. This equivalence is ineffective. While Cauchy rates (or convergence rates) cannot be extracted in general, metastability rates can, depending on the principles used by the proof.

Körnlein
[
15
,
16
]
had obtained rates of metastability of an iteration due to Yamada
[
21
]
regarding a class of variational problems. We show a different approach, and our interest is to focus on the particular case of (P), analysing Xu’s proof of Theorem
1
. We note that, if
\|A\|<2
, Xu’s iteration is a particular case of Yamada’s iteration.

Xu’s proofs of Theorem
1
involve sequential weak compactness arguments. In this paper we apply the method developed by Ferreira, Leuştean and Pinto
[
7
]
guided by the bounded functional interpretation
[
8
]
for circumventing such arguments and we compute rates of metastability for Xu’s iteration.
We obtain rates of
(\tilde{T}_{n})
-asymptotic regularity, where
\tilde{T}_{i}:=T_{i+l}\cdots T_{i+1}
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
, and common rates of
T_{i}
-asymptotic regularity for all
i
. Quadratic rates of
(\tilde{T}_{n})
-asymptotic regularity for particular sequences were obtained.

Preliminaries and first results

Let
H
be a real Hilbert space and let
A
be a self-adjoint bounded linear operator on
H
. More details on these operators can be found, for example, in
[
1
,
3
]
.

Let us recall that
A
is
strongly positive
if there exists
\gamma>0
such that for all
x\in H
:

\langle Ax,x\rangle\geq\gamma\|x\|^{2}

(2)

The following lemma will be used in the sequel. For the sake of completeness, we prove it here.

Lemma 1
.

Let
A
be strongly positive.
For any
\alpha\in[0,\|A\|^{-1}]
:

\|I-\alpha A\|\leq 1-\alpha\gamma

(3)

Proof.

We clearly have
\|A\|>0
. We have that, given
\alpha\in[0,\|A\|^{-1}]
, for any
x\in H
:

\langle(I-\alpha A)x,x\rangle=\|x\|^{2}-\alpha\langle Ax,x\rangle\geq\|x\|^{2}-\|A\|^{-1}\|A\|\|x\|^{2}=0

Thus

\sup_{\|x\|=1}|\langle(I-\alpha A)x,x\rangle|=\sup_{\|x\|=1}\langle(I-\alpha A)x,x\rangle

As

\sup_{\|x\|=1}|\langle Ax,x\rangle|=\|A\|

we have

\|I-\alpha A\|=\sup_{\|x\|=1}\langle(I-\alpha A)x,x\rangle=\sup_{\|x\|=1}(\|x\|^{2}-\alpha\langle Ax,x\rangle)\leq 1-\alpha\gamma

∎

Furthermore, we shall use the following well known inequality in real Hilbert spaces: for all
x,y\in H
,

\|x+y\|^{2}\leq\|x\|^{2}+2\langle y,x+y\rangle

(4)

In his paper

Proposition 1

As a part of proof mining research, quantitative versions of this lemma were deduced (see

2.2
Xu’s iteration

Let
H
be a Hilbert space. Given
l\in\mathbb{N}
, let
T_{0},\ldots,T_{l-1}
be nonexpansive mappings on
H
. We define
T_{n}
for
n\geq l
as
T_{n\bmod l}
. Define

F:=\bigcap_{i=0}^{l-1}\Fix(T_{i})

(9)

We assume that
F\neq\emptyset
.

Let
u\in H
. Let
A
be a self-adjoint bounded linear operator on
H
satisfying (
2
) for some
0<\gamma\leq 1
and for all
x\in H
.

Xu’s iteration
(x_{n})
is defined by

x_{0}\in H,\quad x_{n+1}=(I-\alpha_{n+1}A)T_{n+1}x_{n}+\alpha_{n+1}u

(10)

where
(\alpha_{n})\subset(0,1]
.

In the sequel we prove some useful properties of Xu’s iteration.

For the rest of the section, we take
p\in F
and assume that

there is
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\alpha_{n+1}\leq\|A\|^{-1}
for every
n\geq N_{0}
,

Lemma 4
.

For every
n\geq N_{0}
:

\displaystyle\|x_{n+1}-p\|

\displaystyle\leq(1-\gamma\alpha_{n+1})\|x_{n}-p\|+\alpha_{n+1}\|u-Ap\|,

(11)

\displaystyle\|x_{n}-p\|

\displaystyle\leq K_{0},

(12)

where
K_{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
is such that:

K_{0}\geq\max\left\{\|x_{N_{0}}-p\|,\frac{\|u-Ap\|}{\gamma}\right\}.

(13)

Proof.

We get that for all
n\geq N_{0}
:

\displaystyle\|x_{n+1}-p\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|(I-\alpha_{n+1}A)T_{n+1}x_{n}+\alpha_{n+1}u-p+\alpha_{n+1}Ap-\alpha_{n+1}Ap\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|(I-\alpha_{n+1}A)(T_{n+1}x_{n}-p)+\alpha_{n+1}(u-Ap)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\gamma\alpha_{n+1})\|T_{n+1}x_{n}-p\|+\alpha_{n+1}\|u-Ap\|\quad\text{by Lemma \ref{I-minus-alpha-A-lemma}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\gamma\alpha_{n+1})\|x_{n}-p\|+\alpha_{n+1}\|u-Ap\|

Thus, (
11
) holds.

We prove (
12
) by induction. The case
n=N_{0}
is obvious. Assume that
\|x_{n}-p\|\leq K_{0}
. It follows from (
11
) that:

\|x_{n+1}-p\|\leq(1-\gamma\alpha_{n+1})K_{0}+\alpha_{n+1}\gamma K_{0}=K_{0}

Thus,
\|x_{n}-p\|\leq K_{0}
for every
n\geq N_{0}
.
∎

Lemma 5
.

Let
b_{A}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
be such that
b_{A}\geq\|A\|
. For every
n\geq N_{0},m\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in H
:

\displaystyle\|u-AT_{n+1}x_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq(b_{A}+1)K_{0},

(14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|x_{n+l+1}-x_{n+1}\|&\leq(1-\alpha_{n+l+1}\gamma)\|x_{n+l}-x_{n}\|\\
&\ \ +(b_{A}+1)K_{0}|\alpha_{n+l+1}-\alpha_{n+1}|,\end{split}

(15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|x_{n+l+m}-x_{n+m}\|&\leq\|x_{n+l}-x_{n}\|\prod_{i=n+1}^{n+m}(1-\alpha_{i+l}\gamma)\\
&\ \ +(b_{A}+1)K_{0}\sum_{i=n+1}^{n+m}|\alpha_{i+l}-\alpha_{i}|,\end{split}

(16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|x_{n+1}-x\|^{2}&\leq(1-\alpha_{n+1}\gamma)\|x_{n}-x\|^{2}+2\|x_{n}-x\|\|T_{n+1}x-x\|\\
&\ \ +\|T_{n+1}x-x\|^{2}+2\alpha_{n+1}\langle u-Ax,x_{n+1}-x\rangle.\end{split}

(17)

Proof.

Let
n\geq N_{0}
and
x\in H
. By (
12
),

\displaystyle\|u-AT_{n+1}x_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|u-Ap\|+\|A\|\|p-T_{n+1}x_{n}\|\leq\|u-Ap\|+\|A\|\|p-x_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\gamma K_{0}+\|A\|K_{0}\leq(b_{A}+1)K_{0}.

\displaystyle\|x_{n+l+1}-x_{n+1}\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|(I-\alpha_{n+l+1}A)T_{n+l+1}x_{n+l}+\alpha_{n+l+1}u-((I-\alpha_{n+1}A)T_{n+1}x_{n}+\alpha_{n+1}u)\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|(I-\alpha_{n+l+1}A)(T_{n+l+1}x_{n+l}-T_{n+1}x_{n})+(\alpha_{n+l+1}-\alpha_{n+1})(u-AT_{n+1}x_{n})\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\alpha_{n+l+1}\gamma)\|T_{n+l+1}x_{n+l}-T_{n+1}x_{n}\|+(b_{A}+1)K_{0}|\alpha_{n+l+1}-\alpha_{n+1}|

by Lemma
1
and (
14
)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\alpha_{n+l+1}\gamma)\|x_{n+l}-x_{n}\|+(b_{A}+1)K_{0}|\alpha_{n+l+1}-\alpha_{n+1}|\quad\text{since $T_{n+l}=T_{n}$}.

By induction, from (
15
) we get (
16
).

\displaystyle\|x_{n+1}-x\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|(I-\alpha_{n+1}A)(T_{n+1}x_{n}-x)+\alpha_{n+1}(u-Ax)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|(I-\alpha_{n+1}A)(T_{n+1}x_{n}-x)\|^{2}+2\alpha_{n+1}\langle u-Ax,x_{n+1}-x\rangle

by
(
4
)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\alpha_{n+1}\gamma)\|T_{n+1}x_{n}-x\|^{2}+2\alpha_{n+1}\langle u-Ax,x_{n+1}-x\rangle

by Lemma
1

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\alpha_{n+1}\gamma)(\|T_{n+1}x_{n}-T_{n+1}x\|+\|T_{n+1}x-x\|)^{2}

\displaystyle+2\alpha_{n+1}\langle u-Ax,x_{n+1}-x\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\alpha_{n+1}\gamma)\|x_{n}-x\|^{2}+2\|x_{n}-x\|\|T_{n+1}x-x\|

\displaystyle+\|T_{n+1}x-x\|^{2}+2\alpha_{n+1}\langle u-Ax,x_{n+1}-x\rangle.

∎

2.3
Quantitative hypotheses

Xu used the following hypotheses on the parameter sequences
(\alpha_{n})
:

(C1)

\displaystyle\lim_{n}\alpha_{n}=0

(C2)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{n}=+\infty

(C3)

\displaystyle\lim_{n}\frac{\alpha_{n}}{\alpha_{n+l}}=1

(C4)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}|\alpha_{n}-\alpha_{n+l}|<+\infty

As we prove quantitative versions of the results in our paper, we use quantitative conditions:

(C1q)

\displaystyle\lim_{n}\alpha_{n}=0\text{ with convergence rate }\sigma_{1}

(C2q)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{n}=+\infty\text{ with divergence rate }\sigma_{2}

(C2q*)

\displaystyle\prod_{n=m}^{+\infty}(1-\gamma\alpha_{n})=0\text{ with rate of convergence }\sigma_{2}^{*}(m,\cdot)\text{ for }m\in\mathbb{N}

(C3q)

\displaystyle\lim_{n}\frac{\alpha_{n}}{\alpha_{n+l}}=1\text{ with convergence rate }\sigma_{3}

(C4q)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}|\alpha_{n}-\alpha_{n+l}|<+\infty\text{ with Cauchy modulus }\sigma_{4}

We make natural monotonicity assumptions on the rates, i.e., that the
\sigma_{i}
and
\sigma_{2}^{*}
are increasing.

Take

∎

.

Assume that (C1q) holds. Let

and

be such that

By Lemma

If (C1q) holds, we may, analogously, use any inequality from Section

3
Connection with Körnlein’s results

In the following, we show that for
\|A\|<2
, Xu’s iteration is a particular case of an iteration due to Yamada. Körnlein
[
15
,
16
]
computed rates of metastability for Yamada’s iteration. Hence, for
\|A\|<2
, these rates are also rates for Xu’s iteration.

We also show that Xu’s iteration is not necessarily a particular case of Yamada’s iteration if
\|A\|\geq 2
, therefore Yamada’s and Körnlein’s results are not applicable in this case.

The quadratic minimization problem (P) associated to the operator
A
is given by (
1
).
As pointed out by Xu, the problem (P) has a unique solution
x^{*}
and, furthermore, (P) has the following variational characterization (
[
20
, Lemma 2.3.]
):

\langle u-Ax^{*},y-x^{*}\rangle\leq 0\quad\text{for all $y\in F$}.

(19)

We consider the variational inequality problem (VIP) consisting in finding
x^{*}\in F
such that,

\forall y\in F\ \langle\mathcal{F}x^{*},y-x^{*}\rangle\geq 0

for some mapping
\mathcal{F}
in
H
.

The following iteration was considered by Yamada
[
21
]
in connection with the VIP:

v_{0}\in H,\quad v_{n+1}=(1-\alpha_{n+1})T_{n+1}v_{n}+\alpha_{n+1}f(T_{n+1}v_{n})

(20)

where
f:H\to H
is a contraction.

Lemma 8
.

If
\|I-A\|<1
, Xu’s iteration is a particular case of Yamada’s iteration (
20
).

Proof.

Let
f(x):=(I-A)x+u
. Then, for all
x,y\in H
,
\|f(x)-f(y)\|=\|(I-A)x+u-(I-A)y-u\|=\|(I-A)(x-y)\|\leq\|I-A\|\|x-y\|
. As
\|I-A\|<1
, we have that
f
is a contraction.
It follows that, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle v_{n+1}

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha_{n+1})T_{n+1}v_{n}+\alpha_{n+1}((I-A)T_{n+1}v_{n}+u)

\displaystyle=T_{n+1}v_{n}-\alpha_{n+1}T_{n+1}v_{n}+\alpha_{n+1}T_{n+1}v_{n}-\alpha_{n+1}AT_{n+1}v_{n}+\alpha_{n+1}u

\displaystyle=T_{n+1}v_{n}-\alpha_{n+1}(AT_{n+1}v_{n}-u)

\displaystyle=(I-\alpha_{n+1}A)T_{n+1}v_{n}+\alpha_{n+1}u

∎

Remark 1
.

We then show that Xu’s iteration is not necessarily a particular case of Yamada’s iteration if
\|A\|\geq 2
.

From the above equalities we get that, if Xu’s iteration is a particular case of Yamada’s iteration, then
f
is such that, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(1-\alpha_{n+1})T_{n+1}v_{n}+\alpha_{n+1}((I-A)T_{n+1}v_{n}+u)=(1-\alpha_{n+1})T_{n+1}v_{n}+\alpha_{n+1}f(T_{n+1}v_{n}),

that is,

f(T_{n+1}v_{n})=(I-A)T_{n+1}v_{n}+u.

This gives

\|f(T_{n+1}v_{n})-f(T_{n+2}v_{n+1})\|\leq\|I-A\|\|T_{n+1}v_{n}-T_{n+2}v_{n+1}\|.

Now assume we can take
v_{0}
such that

\|(I-A)(T_{1}v_{0}-T_{2}v_{1})\|=\|I-A\|\|T_{1}v_{0}-T_{2}v_{1}\|

which leads to the equality for the previous inequality. Thus, if
\|I-A\|\geq 1
,
f
may not be a contraction. This is a contradiction. We conclude that if
\|I-A\|\geq 1
, Xu’s iteration is not necessarily a particular case of Yamada’s iteration. This is the case if
\|A\|\geq 2
, since then
\|I-A\|\geq\|A\|-\|I\|\geq 1
.

We now show how to apply Yamada’s approach to the problem (P), introducing a particular case of Yamada’s iteration, which turns out to be also a particular case of Xu’s iteration.

We first note that (P) is a particular case of the problem

\min_{x\in F}\theta(x)

for some convex function
\theta:H\to\mathbb{R}
twice differentiable on
H
such that there exists
m,M>0
such that, for all
x,v\in H
:

m\|v\|^{2}\leq\langle\theta^{\prime\prime}(x)v,v\rangle\leq M\|v\|^{2}.

This, in turn, is a particular case of the VIP
[
4
]
.
Deutsch and Yamada
[
4
]
had introduced an iteration for this problem (also used by Xu
[
20
]
):

v_{0}\in H,\quad v_{n+1}=T_{n+1}v_{n}-\alpha_{n+1}\mu\theta^{\prime}(T_{n+1}v_{n})

(21)

where
0<\mu<\frac{2}{M}
.

Proposition 5
.

The iteration (
21
) is a particular case of (
20
).

Proof.

We define
\psi(x):=\mu\theta(x)-\frac{1}{2}\|x\|^{2}
. Then
\psi^{\prime}(x)=\mu\theta^{\prime}(x)-x
. It is known
[
4
, Lemma 3.5]
that
\psi^{\prime}
is a contraction for
0<\mu<\frac{2}{M}
. We can thus take
f:=-\psi^{\prime}
in (
20
):

\displaystyle v_{n+1}

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha_{n+1})T_{n+1}v_{n}-\alpha_{n+1}\psi^{\prime}(T_{n+1}v_{n})

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha_{n+1})T_{n+1}v_{n}-\alpha_{n+1}(\mu\theta^{\prime}(T_{n+1}v_{n})-T_{n+1}v_{n})

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha_{n+1})T_{n+1}v_{n}-\alpha_{n+1}\mu\theta^{\prime}(T_{n+1}v_{n})+\alpha_{n+1}T_{n+1}v_{n}

\displaystyle=T_{n+1}v_{n}-\alpha_{n+1}\mu\theta^{\prime}(T_{n+1}v_{n})

∎

We can thus apply Yamada’s approach to the problem (P) by taking
\theta(x):=\frac{1}{2}\langle Ax,x\rangle-\langle x,u\rangle
in (
21
).

Proposition 6
.

Taking
\theta(x):=\frac{1}{2}\langle Ax,x\rangle-\langle x,u\rangle
in (
21
) we obtain:

v_{0}\in H,\quad v_{n+1}=(I-\alpha_{n+1}\mu A)T_{n+1}v_{n}+\alpha_{n+1}\mu u

(22)

with
0<\mu<\frac{2}{\|A\|}
.

Proof.

We have
\theta^{\prime}(x)=Ax-u
. Thus from (
21
) we get

\displaystyle v_{n+1}

\displaystyle=T_{n+1}v_{n}-\alpha_{n+1}\mu(AT_{n+1}v_{n}-u)

\displaystyle=T_{n+1}v_{n}-\alpha_{n+1}\mu AT_{n+1}v_{n}+\alpha_{n+1}\mu u

\displaystyle=(I-\alpha_{n+1}\mu A)T_{n+1}v_{n}+\alpha_{n+1}\mu u

We take
M=\|A\|
since
\theta^{\prime\prime}(x)=A
and, for every

,

\langle Av,v\rangle\leq\|A\|\|v\|^{2}.

∎

Remark 2
.

The iteration (
22
) is identical to Xu’s iteration (
10
) with
\mu A
in place of
A
and
\mu u
in place of
u
. That is, we take

in (

If

for some strongly positive self-adjoint bounded linear operator

and some

We thus have that, if

We also have that, if
\|A\|\geq 2
, Xu’s iteration (

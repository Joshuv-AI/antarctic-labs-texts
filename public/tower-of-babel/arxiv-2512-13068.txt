1
Introduction

This paper is motivated by tractability analysis in quasi-Monte Carlo (QMC) methods, where one encounters sums of the form

S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(m)=\sum_{v\subseteq\mathbb{N}}\gamma_{v}m^{|v|},

with
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,3,\dots\}
and
\boldsymbol{\gamma}=\{\gamma_{v}\mid v\subseteq\mathbb{N}\}
a collection of nonnegative weights. Here, the summation over
v\subseteq\mathbb{N}
runs over all finite subsets of
\mathbb{N}
.

A central problem, arising for instance in the analysis of lattice rules for integration in weighted Korobov spaces
[
3
]
, is to establish under suitable conditions on
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
that

S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(m)<\infty\quad\text{for}\quad m>0.

(1)

Proving (
1
) is key to demonstrating that certain QMC rules achieve error bounds independent of the problem dimension—a property known as strong tractability in information-based complexity
[
12
]
.

Beyond mere convergence, some applications require precise control over the growth of
S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(m)
as
m\to\infty
. In the analysis of base-
b
digital nets, for instance,
m
presents the base-
b
logarithm of the number of points
n=b^{m}
. The goal is often to show
S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(m)
is sub-exponential in
m
:

\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(m)=0,

(2)

or equivalently, that for every
\delta>0
there exists a constant
C_{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\delta}<\infty
such that
S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(m)\leqslant C_{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\delta}b^{\delta m}
.
Establishing (
2
) for specific weights, such as those in
[
14
, equation (13)]
, can imply the strong tractability of classical sequences like those of Niederreiter in weighted Sobolev spaces.

The convergence properties of
S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(m)
are straightforward for product weights
\gamma_{v}=\prod_{j\in v}\gamma_{j}
. In this case, the sum factors into an infinite product:

S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(m)=\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}(1+\gamma_{j}m),

from which it follows that both (
1
) and (
2
) are satisfied if
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{j}<\infty

[
7
, Lemma 3]
.

The analysis becomes more involved for product and order-dependent (POD) weights. These take the general form

\gamma_{v}=\Gamma_{|v|}\prod_{j\in v}\Upsilon_{j},

(3)

parameterized by two nonnegative sequences
\{\Gamma_{\ell}\mid\ell\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\}
and
\{\Upsilon_{j}\mid j\in\mathbb{N}\}
. Here, the factor
\Gamma_{|v|}
introduces a dependence on the order (the size
|v|
) of the subset
v
. POD weights arise naturally in the analysis of QMC methods for partial differential equations with random coefficients
[
6
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
. See
[
8
]
for a comprehensive overview.

For the specific yet important class of POD weights where
\Gamma_{|v|}=(|v|!)^{\sigma}
for
\sigma\geqslant 0
, a common starting point for analysis is the inequality

\sum_{v\subseteq\mathbb{N}}|v|!m^{|v|}\prod_{j\in v}\Upsilon_{j}\leqslant\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\Big(m\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Upsilon_{j}\Big)^{\ell}

(4)

established in
[
10
, Lemma 6.3]
. This bound directly implies the finiteness of
S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(m)
for the case
\sigma=1
, provided
m\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Upsilon_{j}<1
. Handling the general case
\sigma\neq 1
then requires additional steps, such as an application of Jensen’s or Hölder’s inequality, as illustrated in the proof of
[
10
, Theorem 6.4]
.

While useful, inequality (
4
) yields a conservative estimate. It suggests the sum could diverge when
m
exceeds
(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Upsilon_{j})^{-1}
, which severely overestimates its actual growth. In contrast, our Theorem
2
will demonstrate that the sum remains finite for all
m>0
under the sole condition
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Upsilon_{j}<\infty
.

The proof of this stronger convergence result is powered by the following general bounding theorem, which serves as our core analytical engine:

Theorem 1
.

Let
S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(m)=\sum_{v\subseteq\mathbb{N}}\gamma_{v}m^{|v|}
where
\gamma_{v}
are of POD form (
3
) with
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Upsilon_{j}<\infty
. Then

S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(m)\leqslant\Gamma_{0}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\exp(\ell+1)\Gamma_{\ell}m^{\ell}\prod_{J=1}^{\ell}\max\left(\Upsilon_{J},\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=J+1}^{\infty}\Upsilon_{j}\right).

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. The proof of Theorem
1
is presented in Section
2
. Section
3
explores its important corollaries, including precise asymptotic rates for a common class of weights
\gamma_{v}=(|v|!)^{\sigma}\prod_{j\in v}j^{-\rho}
. Finally, in Section
4
, we extend our findings to the broader class of smoothness-driven product and order dependent (SPOD) weights.

2
Proof of Theorem
1

Our proof requires the following technical lemma:

Lemma 1
.

Let
\mathbb{N}_{\ell}=\{v\subseteq\mathbb{N}\mid|v|=\ell\}
for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Given nonnegative
\{\Upsilon_{j}\mid j\in\mathbb{N}\}
satisfying
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Upsilon_{j}<\infty
, we denote

\zeta_{J}=\sum_{j=J}^{\infty}\Upsilon_{j}
.
Then

\sum_{v\in\mathbb{N}_{\ell}}\prod_{j\in v}\Upsilon_{j}\leqslant\prod_{J=1}^{\ell}\left(\Upsilon_{J}+\frac{\zeta_{J+1}}{\ell-J+1}\right)\leqslant\exp(\ell+1)\prod_{J=1}^{\ell}\max(\Upsilon_{J},J^{-1}\zeta_{J+1}).

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be the probability distribution on
\mathbb{N}
defined by the probability mass function
\mathbb{P}({j})=\Upsilon_{j}/\zeta_{1}
for
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Consider
X_{1},\dots,X_{\ell}\overset{i.i.d.}{\sim}\mathbb{P}
. The probability that
X_{1},\dots,X_{\ell}
are all distinct is given by

\sum_{v\in\mathbb{N}_{\ell}}\ell!\prod_{j\in v}\frac{\Upsilon_{j}}{\zeta_{1}}=\frac{\ell!}{\zeta_{1}^{\ell}}\sum_{v\in\mathbb{N}_{\ell}}\prod_{j\in v}\Upsilon_{j}.

(5)

Here, the factor
\ell!
accounts for all possible orderings of the
\ell
distinct indices that form the set
v
.

Next, we let
I_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\boldsymbol{1}(X_{i}=j)
and
\mathcal{I}_{j}
be the
\sigma-
algebra generated by
I_{1},\dots,I_{j}
. The event
X_{1},\dots,X_{\ell}
are all distinct is equivalent to
I_{j}\leqslant 1
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Denoting
A_{j}=\boldsymbol{1}\{I_{j}\leqslant 1\}
, for each
J\in\mathbb{N}
with
J\geqslant 2
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{J}A_{j}\Big|\mathcal{I}_{J-1}\Big)=\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{J-1}A_{j}\Big)\mathbb{E}\Big(A_{J}\Big|\mathcal{I}_{J-1}\Big)=\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{J-1}A_{j}\Big)\Pr\Big(I_{J}\leqslant 1\Big|\mathcal{I}_{J-1}\Big).

Conditioned on
\mathcal{I}_{J-1}
,
I_{J}
follows a binomial distribution with a total number of trials
\ell-\sum_{j=1}^{J-1}I_{j}
and success probability
\Upsilon_{J}/\zeta_{J}
. Because
\sum_{j=1}^{J-1}I_{j}\leqslant J-1
when
I_{j}\leqslant 1
for all
1\leqslant j\leqslant J-1
, we have for
2\leqslant J\leqslant\ell

\displaystyle\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{J-1}A_{j}\Big)\Pr\Big(I_{J}\leqslant 1\Big|\mathcal{I}_{J-1}\Big)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{J-1}A_{j}\Big)\left(\Big(1-\frac{\Upsilon_{J}}{\zeta_{J}}\Big)^{\ell-J+1}+(\ell-J+1)\frac{\Upsilon_{J}}{\zeta_{J}}\Big(1-\frac{\Upsilon_{J}}{\zeta_{J}}\Big)^{\ell-J}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{J-1}A_{j}\Big)\Big(1+(\ell-J)\frac{\Upsilon_{J}}{\zeta_{J}}\Big)\Big(1-\frac{\Upsilon_{J}}{\zeta_{J}}\Big)^{\ell-J}.

Taking expectation on both sides, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{J}A_{j}\Big)\leqslant\mathbb{E}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{J-1}A_{j}\Big)\Big(1+(\ell-J)\frac{\Upsilon_{J}}{\zeta_{J}}\Big)\Big(1-\frac{\Upsilon_{J}}{\zeta_{J}}\Big)^{\ell-J}.

By induction,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}A_{j}\Big)\leqslant\mathbb{E}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}A_{j}\Big)\leqslant

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(A_{1})\prod_{J=2}^{\ell}\Big(1+(\ell-J)\frac{\Upsilon_{J}}{\zeta_{J}}\Big)\Big(1-\frac{\Upsilon_{J}}{\zeta_{J}}\Big)^{\ell-J}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{J=1}^{\ell}\Big(1+(\ell-J)\frac{\Upsilon_{J}}{\zeta_{J}}\Big)\Big(1-\frac{\Upsilon_{J}}{\zeta_{J}}\Big)^{\ell-J},

where the last equality follows because
\mathbb{E}(A_{1})=\Pr(I_{1}\leqslant 1)
and
I_{1}
follows a binomial distribution with a total number of trials
\ell
and success probability
\Upsilon_{1}/\zeta_{1}
. Equating
\mathbb{E}(\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}A_{j})
with (
5
) yields the upper bound

\sum_{v\in\mathbb{N}_{\ell}}\prod_{j\in v}\Upsilon_{j}\leqslant\frac{\zeta_{1}^{\ell}}{\ell!}\prod_{J=1}^{\ell}\Big(1+(\ell-J)\frac{\Upsilon_{J}}{\zeta_{J}}\Big)\Big(1-\frac{\Upsilon_{J}}{\zeta_{J}}\Big)^{\ell-J}.

Furthermore, because
\zeta_{J}=\Upsilon_{J}+\zeta_{J+1}
,

\displaystyle\zeta_{1}^{\ell}\prod_{J=1}^{\ell}\Big(1-\frac{\Upsilon_{J}}{\zeta_{J}}\Big)^{\ell-J}=\zeta_{1}^{\ell}\prod_{J=1}^{\ell}\Big(\frac{\zeta_{J+1}}{\zeta_{J}}\Big)^{\ell-J}=\prod_{J=1}^{\ell}\zeta_{J}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in\mathbb{N}_{\ell}}\prod_{j\in v}\Upsilon_{j}\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\ell!}\prod_{J=1}^{\ell}\Big(\zeta_{J}+(\ell-J)\Upsilon_{J}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{J=1}^{\ell}\left(\frac{\zeta_{J+1}}{\ell-J+1}+\Upsilon_{J}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\prod_{J=1}^{\ell}\max(J^{-1}\zeta_{J+1},\Upsilon_{J})\prod_{J=1}^{\ell}\left(\frac{J}{\ell-J+1}+1\right).

Finally, we use the Stirling’s formula
[
13
]
to conclude

\displaystyle\prod_{J=1}^{\ell}\left(\frac{J}{\ell-J+1}+1\right)=

\displaystyle\prod_{J=1}^{\ell}\frac{\ell+1}{\ell-J+1}=\frac{(\ell+1)^{\ell+1}}{(\ell+1)!}\leqslant\exp(\ell+1).\qed

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Notice that

S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(m)=\Gamma_{0}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\sum_{v\in\mathbb{N}_{\ell}}\gamma_{v}m^{\ell}=\Gamma_{0}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\Gamma_{\ell}m^{\ell}\sum_{v\in\mathbb{N}_{\ell}}\prod_{j\in v}\Upsilon_{j}.

Our conclusion follows directly from Lemma
1
.
∎

3
Tractability analysis of POD weights

In this section, we apply Theorem
1
to derive improved bounds for several common settings in QMC tractability analysis. We first present the following result, which sharpens the key estimate in
[
10
, Lemma 6.3]
.

Theorem 2
.

For nonnegative
\{\Upsilon_{j}\mid j\in\mathbb{N}\}
,

\sum_{v\subseteq\mathbb{N}}|v|!m^{|v|}\prod_{j\in v}\Upsilon_{j}<\infty\text{ for }m>0

(6)

if and only if
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Upsilon_{j}<\infty
.

Proof.

First, we assume
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Upsilon_{j}<\infty
.
A direct application of Theorem
1
with
\Gamma_{\ell}=\ell!
yields

\sum_{v\subseteq\mathbb{N}}|v|!m^{|v|}\prod_{j\in v}\Upsilon_{j}\leqslant\Gamma_{0}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\exp(\ell+1)m^{\ell}\prod_{J=1}^{\ell}\max\left(J\Upsilon_{J},\sum_{j=J+1}^{\infty}\Upsilon_{j}\right).

Since
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Upsilon_{j}<\infty
,

\left(\prod_{J=1}^{\ell}\max\left(J\Upsilon_{J},\sum_{j=J+1}^{\infty}\Upsilon_{j}\right)\right)^{1/\ell}\leqslant\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{J=1}^{\ell}J\Upsilon_{J}+\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{J=1}^{\ell}\sum_{j=J+1}^{\infty}\Upsilon_{j}\to 0

as
\ell\to\infty
. Consequently, (
6
) follows from the root test.

Next, we assume
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Upsilon_{j}=\infty
. We have the lower bound

\sum_{v\subseteq\mathbb{N}}|v|!m^{|v|}\prod_{j\in v}\Upsilon_{j}\geqslant\sum_{v\subseteq\mathbb{N}}m^{|v|}\prod_{j\in v}\Upsilon_{j}=\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}(1+m\Upsilon_{j}),

which diverges given
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Upsilon_{j}=\infty

[
1
, Proposition 5.4]
.
∎

Next, we study the case
\Gamma_{|v|}
is a power of
|v|!
and
\Upsilon_{j}
is proportional
j^{-\rho}
for
\rho>1
. We need the following lemma:

Lemma 2
.

For
\theta>0
,

\lim_{m\to\infty}m^{-1/\theta}\log\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{m^{\ell}}{(\ell!)^{\theta}}\right)=\theta.

Proof.

Let
\ell_{*}=\lfloor m^{1/\theta}\rfloor
. Because

\frac{\left((\ell+1)!\right)^{-\theta}m^{\ell+1}}{(\ell!)^{-\theta}m^{\ell}}=(\ell+1)^{-\theta}m<1

if and only if
\ell\geqslant\ell_{*}
, we see
(\ell!)^{-\theta}m^{\ell}
is maximized at
\ell=\ell_{*}
. Therefore,

\frac{m^{\ell_{*}}}{(\ell_{*}!)^{\theta}}\leqslant\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{m^{\ell}}{(\ell!)^{\theta}}\leqslant 2\ell_{*}\frac{m^{\ell_{*}}}{(\ell_{*}!)^{\theta}}+\sum_{\ell=2\ell_{*}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(\ell!)^{\theta}}m^{\ell}\leqslant\left(2\ell_{*}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}2^{-\theta\ell}\right)\frac{m^{\ell_{*}}}{(\ell_{*}!)^{\theta}},

where we have used
(\ell+1)^{-\theta}m<2^{-\theta}
when
\ell\geqslant 2\ell_{*}
. By the Stirling’s formula,
\log((\ell_{*}!)^{-\theta}m^{\ell_{*}})\sim\theta m^{1/\theta}
. Our conclusion then follows because the lower and upper bounds converge to the same limit.
∎

Theorem 3
.

Given
\rho>1
,
\sigma\geqslant 0
and
C_{\Upsilon}>0
, let
S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(m)=\sum_{v\subseteq\mathbb{N}}\gamma_{v}m^{|v|}
with
\gamma_{\varnothing}=1
and

\gamma_{v}=(|v|!)^{\sigma}\prod_{j\in v}\frac{C_{\Upsilon}}{j^{\rho}}\text{ for }v\neq\varnothing.

Then
S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(m)<\infty
for
m>0
if and only if
\rho>\sigma
, in which case

\liminf_{m\to\infty}m^{-\frac{1}{\rho-\sigma}}\log S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(m)\geqslant(\rho-\sigma)C^{\frac{1}{\rho-\sigma}}_{\Upsilon}

and

\limsup_{m\to\infty}m^{-\frac{1}{\rho-\sigma}}\log S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(m)\leqslant(\rho-\sigma)(C_{\rho}C_{\Upsilon})^{\frac{1}{\rho-\sigma}}

for
C_{\rho}=\exp(1)/\min(\rho-1,1)
.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma_{\ell}=(\ell!)^{\sigma}
and
\Upsilon_{j}=C_{\Upsilon}j^{-\rho}
in Theorem
1
. Because

\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=J+1}^{\infty}\Upsilon_{j}\leqslant\frac{C_{\Upsilon}}{J}\int_{J}^{\infty}j^{-\rho}\,\mathrm{d}j=\frac{C_{\Upsilon}J^{-\rho}}{\rho-1},

we have the upper bound

S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(m)\leqslant 1+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}(\ell!)^{\sigma}\frac{\exp(\ell+1)(C_{\Upsilon}m)^{\ell}}{\min(\rho-1,1)^{\ell}}\prod_{J=1}^{\ell}J^{-\rho}\leqslant\exp(1)\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{(C_{\rho}C_{\Upsilon}m)^{\ell}}{(\ell!)^{\rho-\sigma}}.

Meanwhile, we have the lower bound

S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(m)\geqslant 1+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}(\ell!)^{\sigma}m^{\ell}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\frac{C_{\Upsilon}}{j^{\rho}}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{(C_{\Upsilon}m)^{\ell}}{(\ell!)^{\rho-\sigma}}.

When
\rho\leqslant\sigma
, the lower bound diverges when
m\geqslant C^{-1}_{\Upsilon}
. When
\rho>\sigma
, applying Lemma
2
to both the upper and lower bounds yields our conclusion.
∎

4
Extension to SPOD weights

We conclude by extending our main results to the broader framework of smoothness-driven product and order dependent (SPOD) weights introduced in
[
4
]
. For a fixed smoothness parameter
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote
\{1{:}\alpha\}=\{1,2,\dots,\alpha\}
. Following
[
2
,
5
]
, we define these weights for nonempty
v\subseteq\mathbb{N}
by

\gamma_{v}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\{1{:}\alpha\}^{|v|}}\Gamma_{|\boldsymbol{\nu}|}\prod_{j\in v}\Upsilon_{j,\nu_{j}},

where
|\boldsymbol{\nu}|=\sum_{j\in v}\nu_{j}
for a multi-index
\boldsymbol{\nu}=(\nu_{j})_{j\in v}
, and the parameters are nonnegative sequences
\{\Gamma_{\ell}\mid\ell\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\}
and
\{\Upsilon_{j,k}\mid j\in\mathbb{N},k\in\{1{:}\alpha\}\}
. This general form reduces to the standard POD weights defined in (
3
) when the smoothness parameter is
\alpha=1
.

First, we establish the counterpart of Theorem
2
.

Theorem 4
.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
and nonnegative
\{\Upsilon_{j,k}\mid j\in\mathbb{N},k\in\{1{:}\alpha\}\}
,

\sum_{v\subseteq\mathbb{N}}m^{|v|}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\{1{:}\alpha\}^{|v|}}|\boldsymbol{\nu}|!\prod_{j\in v}\Upsilon_{j,\nu_{j}}<\infty\text{ for }m>0

(7)

if
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\alpha}\Upsilon^{1/k}_{j,k}<\infty
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the statement for
m\geqslant 1
. Let

\Upsilon^{\prime}_{j^{\prime}}=\max_{k\in\{1{:}\alpha\}}\Upsilon^{1/k}_{j,k}\quad\text{for}\quad\alpha(j-1)+1\leqslant j^{\prime}\leqslant\alpha j

and

S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(m)=\sum_{v^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{N}}|v^{\prime}|!m^{|v^{\prime}|}\prod_{j^{\prime}\in v^{\prime}}\Upsilon^{\prime}_{j^{\prime}}.

For each
(v,\boldsymbol{\nu})
pair appearing in (
7
),

|\boldsymbol{\nu}|!m^{|v|}\prod_{j\in v}\Upsilon_{j,\nu_{j}}\leqslant|v^{\prime}|!m^{|v^{\prime}|}\prod_{j^{\prime}\in v^{\prime}}\Upsilon^{\prime}_{j^{\prime}}

(8)

for
v^{\prime}=\{\alpha(j-1)+k\mid j\in v,k\in\{1{:}\nu_{j}\}\}
. Therefore, the left hand side of (
7
) is bounded by
S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(m)
and its finiteness follows from Theorem
2
and

\displaystyle\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{\infty}\Upsilon^{\prime}_{j^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\alpha\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\max_{k\in\{1{:}\alpha\}}\Upsilon^{1/k}_{j,k}\leqslant\alpha\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\alpha}\Upsilon^{1/k}_{j,k}<\infty.\qed

Remark 1
.

It is an open question whether the condition
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\alpha}\Upsilon^{1/k}_{j,k}<\infty
is also necessary. By considering sequences where
\Upsilon_{j,k}=0
for all but a single
k
, the problem can be reduced to the following: For each
k\in\{1{:}\alpha\}
, does

\sum_{v\subseteq\mathbb{N}}(k|v|)!m^{|v|}\prod_{j\in v}\Upsilon_{j,k}=\infty

whenever
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Upsilon^{1/k}_{j,k}=\infty
? Using the bound
(k|v|)!\geqslant(|v|!)^{k}
, Theorem
3
confirms that this divergence holds for sufficiently large
m
in the case
\Upsilon_{j,k}
is proportional to
j^{-\rho}
.

The next theorem generalizes Theorem
3
.

Theorem 5
.

Given
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
,
\rho>1
,
\sigma\geqslant 0
and positive
\{C_{\Upsilon,k}\mid k\in\{1{:}\alpha\}\}
, let
S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(m)=\sum_{v\subseteq\mathbb{N}}\gamma_{v}m^{|v|}
with
\gamma_{\varnothing}=1
and

\gamma_{v}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\{1{:}\alpha\}^{|v|}}(|\boldsymbol{\nu}|!)^{\sigma}\prod_{j\in v}\frac{C_{\Upsilon,\nu_{j}}}{j^{\nu_{j}\rho}}\text{ for }v\neq\varnothing.

Then
S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(m)<\infty
for
m>0
if and only if
\rho>\sigma
, in which case

\liminf_{m\to\infty}m^{-\frac{1}{\rho-\sigma}}\log S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(m)\geqslant(\rho-\sigma)C^{\frac{1}{\rho-\sigma}}_{\Upsilon,1}

(9)

and

\limsup_{m\to\infty}m^{-\frac{1}{\rho-\sigma}}\log S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(m)\leqslant(\rho-\sigma)\left(C_{\alpha,\rho}\max_{k\in\{1{:}\alpha\}}C^{1/k}_{\Upsilon,k}\right)^{\frac{1}{\rho-\sigma}}

(10)

for a finite constant
C_{\alpha,\rho}
depending on
\alpha
and
\rho
.

Proof.

Because

S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(m)\geqslant\sum_{v\subseteq\mathbb{N}}(|v|!)^{\sigma}m^{|v|}\prod_{j\in v}\frac{C_{\Upsilon,1}}{j^{\rho}},

Theorem
3
shows
S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(m)
diverges for sufficiently large
m
if
\rho\leqslant\sigma
and (
9
) holds if
\rho>\sigma
.

Now we assume
\rho>\sigma
and establish an upper bound on
S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(m)
. Let

\Sigma_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}=\left\{(v,\boldsymbol{\nu})\mid v\subseteq\mathbb{N},|v|=\ell,\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\{1{:}\alpha\}^{|v|},|\boldsymbol{\nu}|=\ell^{\prime}\right\}.

Then we can rewrite
S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(m)
as

S_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(m)=1+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}m^{\ell}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=\ell}^{\infty}\left(\ell^{\prime}!\right)^{\sigma}\sum_{(v,\boldsymbol{\nu})\in\Sigma_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}}\prod_{j\in v}\frac{C_{\Upsilon,\nu_{j}}}{j^{\nu_{j}\rho}}.

(11)

Denote
C_{\max}=\max_{k\in\{1{:}\alpha\}}C^{1/k}_{\Upsilon,k}
and
\Upsilon^{\prime}_{j^{\prime}}=C_{\max}(\lceil j^{\prime}/\alpha\rceil)^{-\rho}
.
By an argument similar to (
8
), Lemma
1
implies

\displaystyle\sum_{(v,\boldsymbol{\nu})\in\Sigma_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}}\prod_{j\in v}\frac{C_{\Upsilon,\nu_{j}}}{j^{\nu_{j}\rho}}\leqslant\sum_{v^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}_{\ell^{\prime}}}\prod_{j^{\prime}\in v^{\prime}}\Upsilon^{\prime}_{j^{\prime}}\leqslant\exp(\ell^{\prime}+1)\prod_{J=1}^{\ell^{\prime}}\max\left(\Upsilon^{\prime}_{J},\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j^{\prime}=J+1}^{\infty}\Upsilon^{\prime}_{j^{\prime}}\right).

Because
\Upsilon^{\prime}_{J}\leqslant C_{\max}(J/\alpha)^{-\rho}
and

\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j^{\prime}=J+1}^{\infty}\Upsilon^{\prime}_{j^{\prime}}\leqslant\frac{\alpha C_{\max}}{J}\sum_{j=\lfloor J/\alpha\rfloor+1}j^{-\rho}\leqslant C^{\prime}_{\alpha,\rho}C_{\max}(J/\alpha)^{-\rho}

for a constant
C^{\prime}_{\alpha,\rho}\geqslant 1
depending on
\alpha
and
\rho
,
we can further bound

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=\ell}^{\infty}\left(\ell^{\prime}!\right)^{\sigma}\sum_{(v,\boldsymbol{\nu})\in\Sigma_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}}\prod_{j\in v}\frac{C_{\Upsilon,\nu_{j}}}{j^{\nu_{j}\rho}}\leqslant

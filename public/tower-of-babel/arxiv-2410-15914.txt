1.
Introduction

Generalized Wright Function

The generalized Wright hypergeometric function
{}_{p}\Psi_{q}
[2, 4] is defined by the series

(1.1)

{}_{p}\Psi_{q}(z)=_{p}\Psi_{q}\left[z|\begin{array}[]{c}\left(a_{i},\alpha_{i}\right)_{1,p}\\
\left(b_{j},\beta_{j}\right)_{1,q}\end{array}\right]=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{p}\Gamma(a_{i}+\alpha_{i}k)}{\prod_{j=1}^{q}\Gamma(b_{j}+\beta_{j}k)}\frac{z^{k}}{k!}.

Here
a_{i},b_{j}\in C
and
\alpha_{i},\beta_{j}\in R\left(i=1,2,...,p;j=1,2,...,q\right)
. Conditions for the existence of the generalized Wright in terms of
H-
function were established in [1]. The particular
{}_{p}\Psi_{q}(z)
is an entire function if there holds the condition

(1.2)

\sum_{j=1}^{q}\beta_{j}-\sum_{i=1}^{p}\alpha_{i}>1.

Mittag Leffler Function

The Mittag-Leffler function
E_{\alpha}(z)
[5] is defined by

(1.3)

E_{\alpha}(z)=\sum_{k=o}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{\Gamma(1+\alpha k)}.

For
\alpha=1
, (1.3) arrive at exponential function
e^{x}
. In 1905 Wiman’s [3] arose generalization of the Mittag-Leffler function (1.3)

(1.4)

E_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=\sum_{k=o}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{\Gamma(\alpha k+\beta)}.

Here
E_{\alpha,1}(z)=E_{\alpha}(z)
.
Prabhakar [7] introduced the Mittag-Leffler function
E_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}(z)
defined by

(1.5)

E_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}(z)=\sum_{k=o}^{\infty}\frac{(\gamma)_{k}}{\Gamma(\alpha k+\beta)}\frac{z^{k}}{k!},

where
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in C,\Re(\alpha)>0,\Re(\beta)>0,\Re(\gamma)>0
and
(\gamma)_{n}
is the Pochhamer symbol defined by

(1.6)

(\gamma)_{n}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&(n=0)\\
\gamma(\gamma+1)(\gamma+2)...(\gamma+n-1)&(n\in N)\\
\frac{\Gamma(\gamma+n)}{\Gamma(\gamma)}&(\gamma\in C)\end{array}\right\}

Poisson Distribution

The series

1+m+\frac{m^{2}}{2!}+\frac{m^{3}}{3!}+...=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{m^{r}}{r!}.

Converges, for all values of
m
, to
e^{m}
. Consider the function
f(r)
defined by

f(r)=\frac{m^{r}e^{-m}}{r!},~~~~~~r=0,1,2,...,

(1.7)

=0\vskip 12.0pt plus 4.0pt minus 4.0pt~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~{\text{elsewhere}},

where
m>0
. Since
m>0
, then
f(r)\geq 0
and

\sum_{r}f(r)=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{m^{r}e^{-m}}{r!}=e^{-m}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{m^{r}}{r!}

(1.8)

=e^{-m}e^{m}=1

that is,
f(r)
satisfies the conditions of being a probability density function of a discrete type of random variable. A random variable
X
which has a probability density function in the form of
f(r)
is said to have a Poisson distribution, and any such
f(r)
is called a Poisson probability distribution function [6].

2.
Main Results

Probability Density Function

Since

\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}P(\alpha,\beta,1,m,r)=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{m^{r}}{{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]\Gamma(\alpha r+\beta)}.

\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}P(\alpha,\beta,1,m,r)=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{m^{r}}{\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(1+r)m^{r}}{\Gamma(\alpha r+\beta)r!}\Gamma(\alpha r+\beta)}=1.

Therefore (1.9) and (1.10) is a probability density functions.

Mean:

Method I

\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}rP(\alpha,\beta,m,r)=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{rm^{r}}{{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]\Gamma(\alpha r+\beta)}.

=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(r+1-1)\Gamma(r+1)m^{r}}{{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]\Gamma(\alpha r+\beta)r!}.

=\frac{1}{{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]}\left[\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(r+1)\Gamma(r+1)m^{r}}{\Gamma(\alpha r+\beta)r!}-\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(r+1)m^{r}}{\Gamma(\alpha r+\beta)r!}\right]

=\frac{1}{{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]}\left[\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(r+2)m^{r}}{\Gamma(\alpha r+\beta)r!}-\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(r+1)m^{r}}{\Gamma(\alpha r+\beta)r!}\right]

(2.1)

=\frac{1}{{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]}\left[{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(2,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]-{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]\right]

Method II

\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}rP(\alpha,\beta,m,r)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha rm^{r}}{{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]\Gamma(\alpha r+\beta)}.

=\frac{1}{\alpha}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\alpha r+\beta-1+1-\beta)m^{r}(1)_{r}}{{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]\Gamma(\alpha r+\beta)~~~r!}.

=\frac{1}{\alpha}\frac{1}{{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]}\left[\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{m^{r}(1)_{r}}{\Gamma(\alpha r+\beta-1)~~~r!}+(1-\beta)\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{m^{r}(1)_{r}}{\Gamma(\alpha r+\beta)~~~r!}\right]

(2.2)

=\frac{1}{\alpha}\frac{1}{{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]}\left[E^{1}_{\alpha,\beta-1}(m)+(1-\beta)E^{1}_{\alpha,\beta}(m)\right]

(2.3)

=\frac{1}{\alpha}\frac{1}{{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]}\left[{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta-1,\alpha)\end{array}\right]+(1-\beta){{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]\right]

Note that for
\alpha=\beta=1
above expressions (2.1), (2.2) and (2.3) arrive mean of Poisson distribution [6].

Variance:

Method I

\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}r^{2}P(\alpha,\beta,1,m,r)=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{2}m^{r}}{{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]\Gamma(\alpha r+\beta)}.

=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(r(r-1)+r)(1)_{r}m^{r}}{{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]\Gamma(\alpha r+\beta)~~~r!}.

=\frac{1}{{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]}\left[\sum_{r=2}^{\infty}\frac{r(r-1)\Gamma(r-1)}{\Gamma(r-1)\Gamma(\alpha r+\beta)}+\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{r}{\Gamma(\alpha r+\beta)}\right]\frac{(1)_{r}m^{r}}{r!}

=\frac{1}{{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]}\left[\sum_{r=2}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(r+1)\Gamma(r+1)m^{r}}{\Gamma(r-1)\Gamma(\alpha r+\beta)~~~r!}+\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(r+1-1)\Gamma(r+1)m^{r}}{\Gamma(\alpha r+\beta)~~~r!}\right]

=\frac{1}{{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]}\left[\sum_{r=2}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(r+1)\Gamma(r+1)m^{r}}{\Gamma(r-1)\Gamma(\alpha r+\beta)r!}+\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(r+2)m^{r}}{\Gamma(\alpha r+\beta)r!}-\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(r+1)m^{r}}{\Gamma(\alpha r+\beta)r!}\right]

(2.4)

=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]}\left[{{}_{2}\Psi_{2}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{cc}(1,1)(1,1)\\
(-1,1)(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]+{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(2,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]-{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]\right].

Note that for
\alpha=\beta=1
we arrive variance of Poisson distribution [6].

Method II

\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}r^{2}P(\alpha,\beta,1,m,r)=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{2}m^{r}}{{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]\Gamma(\alpha r+\beta)}.

=\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{2}r^{2}(1)_{r}m^{r}}{{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]\Gamma(\alpha r+\beta)~~~r!}.

=\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left[(\alpha r+\beta-1)(\alpha r+\beta-2)+(\alpha r+\beta-1)(3-2\beta)+(1-\beta)^{2}\right](1)_{r}m^{r}}{\Gamma(\alpha r+\beta)~~~r!}.

=\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]}\left[\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{r}m^{r}}{{\Gamma(\alpha r+\beta-2)~~~r!}}+\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(3-2\beta)(1)_{r}m^{r}}{{\Gamma(\alpha r+\beta-1)~~~r!}}+\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1-\beta)^{2}(1)_{r}m^{r}}{\Gamma(\alpha r+\beta)~~~r!}\right].

(2.5)

=\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]}\left[E^{1}_{\alpha,\beta-2}{m}+(3-2\beta)E^{1}_{\alpha,\beta-1}(m)+(1-\beta)^{2}E^{1}_{\alpha,\beta}\right]

=\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]}{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta-2,\alpha)\end{array}\right]+(3-2\beta){{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta-1,\alpha)\end{array}\right]

(2.6)

\left.+(1-\beta)^{2}{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]\right\}

Note that for
\alpha=\beta=1
, (2.4), (2.5) and (2.6) arrive variance of Poisson distribution [6].

Moment generating Function

Let
X
be a Poisson variate Then MGF of
X
is defined by

(2.10)

M_{x}(t)=E(e^{tx}).

M_{x}(t)=\frac{1}{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{m^{n}\Gamma(1+n)}{\Gamma(\alpha n+\beta)n!}}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{tr}m^{r}}{\Gamma(\alpha r+\beta)}.

=\frac{E_{\alpha,\beta}(e^{t}m)}{E_{\alpha,\beta}^{1}(m)}.

(2.11)

=\frac{E_{\alpha,\beta}(e^{t}m)}{{{}_{1}\Psi_{1}}\left[m|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1)\\
(\beta,\alpha)\end{array}\right]}.

Note that for
\alpha=\beta=1
above expression (2.11) arrive at Moment generating function of Poisson distribution [6].

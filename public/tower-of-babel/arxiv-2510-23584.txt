Newtonian Gravity Can Produce Quantum Entanglement
Feng-Li Lin, Sayid Mondal

Abstract
In this note, we combine the key ingredient for preparing quantum bodies from superposed mass distributions with the post-Newtonian approach to obtain an effective field theory of quantum bodies, thereby addressing the question of whether classical gravity can produce quantum entanglement. In this framework, we find that the effective theory is a two-qubit interaction with a coupling given by the Newtonian nonlocal quadrupole-quadrupole interaction. This non-local coupling produces the entanglement between quantum bodies.



I
Introduction and Summary

A recent paper in Nature
Howl
claims that classical gravity can produce quantum entanglement via the exchange of virtual quantum matter, even though the mediator gravity field is classical. This raises the debate over whether classical gravity can produce or mediate quantum entanglement. Especially in
Marletto2025Classical
;
Oppenheim2025NoClassical
, the authors object to the claim based on a dynamics-independent quantum information scheme, called Gravitationally Induced Entanglement (GIE) protocol
Marletto:2017kzi
;
Bose:2017nin
, which states that local classical mediator systems, such as classical gravity, can create no quantum entanglement.
The seemingly contradictory statements between
Howl
and
Marletto:2017kzi
;
Bose:2017nin
deserve further classification, as will be done in this short note, which basically recapitulates part of earlier work
Lin2024PNEFT
with the emphasis on its relevance to the current issue. We will show that classical gravity, such as Newtonian gravity, induces Coulombic non-local interactions beyond the assumption of
Marletto:2017kzi
;
Bose:2017nin
that couple distantly separated massive quantum states. This non-local interaction will produce quantum entanglement as characterized by the non-zero entanglement entropy of the final state after unitary evolution. Our result is consistent with
Howl
and bypasses the no-go theorem of
Marletto:2017kzi
because the entanglement arises from the induced non-local direct interaction. The scheme we used to derive the results is a direct application of the post-Newtonian approximation to the quantum matter interacting via classical gravity. Thus, the result should be valid if such a scheme holds.

II
Quantum bodies and their interaction via Newtonian gravity

All considerations about detecting the quantumness of gravity are based on a setup originally proposed by Feynman during the 1957 Chapel Hill conference on gravity
CHAP
to prepare a massive object in a quantum superposition of two positions, which we will call a quantum body. Physically, this mass superposition can be realized as distributed N00N states with spatial separation of mode
a
and
b
located respectively at
\vec{x}_{a}
and
\vec{x}_{b}

Dowling:2008pbf
;
Kim:2025hbh
,

\displaystyle|{\rm N00N}\rangle={1\over\sqrt{2}}\big(|N;\vec{x}_{a}\rangle|0;\vec{x}_{b}\rangle+e^{iN\theta}|0;\vec{x}_{a}\rangle|N;\vec{x}_{b}\rangle\big)

(1)

where
N
is either the photon or the atom numbers, depending on the experimental setup. Since all matter gravitates, the N00N states realize the quantum body. The same setup has recently been adopted to study its quantum decoherence by a black hole
Danielson:2021egj
;
Danielson:2022tdw
;
Danielson:2022sga
;
biggs2024comparing
. In particular, in
biggs2024comparing
, it was generalized to model the quantum body with a quadruple moment
Q^{ij}
, allowing it to interact with the fluctuations of the gravity field. The mass quadrupole moment of this quantum body is prepared as depicted in Fig.
1
(a) by superposing two quadrupole mass configurations, such that this quantum uncertainty manifests as a qubit state. We denote the superposed mesoscopic qubit state by

\displaystyle|\pm\rangle={1\over\sqrt{2}}\big(|+Q^{ij}\rangle\pm|-Q^{ij}\rangle\big)

(2)

where
|\pm Q^{ij}\rangle
corresponds to the state of classical configurations with quadrupole moment
\pm Q^{ij}
, as indicated by
\sigma^{3}|\pm Q^{ij}\rangle=\pm|\pm Q^{ij}\rangle
, where
\sigma_{3}
is Pauli
Z
-matrix. On the other hand,
\sigma_{3}|\pm\rangle=|\mp\rangle
reflecting the quantum uncertainty.

Figure 1
:
(a) A quadrupole quantum body is prepared in an equal-weight superposition of two classical quadrupole configurations with opposite signs, which distributed N00N states can physically realize. (b) Two static quantum bodies with quadrupole moments interacting via a mediator gravity field can be effectively described by Newtonian forces.

Given a mass configurations of stress tensor
T_{\mu\nu}
, it will source the metric fluctuation
h_{\mu\nu}
via the interaction Hamiltonian
h_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu}
. For the classical quadrupole moment, it is reduced to

\displaystyle{\cal H}^{c}=-h_{ij}Q^{ij}\;.

(3)

It is natural to generalize it to the quantum body by

\displaystyle{\cal H}^{q}={\cal H}^{c}\sigma^{3}\;,

(4)

so that it gives the correct interaction energy density for the states
|\pm Q^{ij}\rangle
, i.e.,
\langle\pm Q^{ij}|{\cal H}^{q}|\pm Q^{ij}\rangle=\pm{\cal H}^{c}
. For the states
|\pm\rangle
, we have instead
\langle\pm|{\cal H}^{q}|\pm\rangle=0
, which leads to the quantum state reduction problem in the formulation of semi-classical gravity. That is, a quantum measurement may suddenly collapse the quantum body’s qubit state from
|+\rangle
to
|+Q^{ij}\rangle
or
|+Q^{ij}\rangle
, thereby violating energy conservation if gravity is classical and spacetime is smooth
Penrose:1996cv
. In this note, we only consider equal-weight N00N states; however, it is straightforward to consider non-equal-weight cases and to introduce an external control agency, e.g., via electrodynamics, to make the internal qubits more dynamic, e.g., by introducing an energy gap. These generalizations correspond to treating the internal qubits as Unruh-DeWitt detectors in the PN-EFT framework studied in
Lin2024PNEFT
.

It is straightforward to generalize the above to two (or many) quantum bodies with quadruple moments sourcing the metric fluctuation, which can be described by the following interaction Hamiltonian

\displaystyle{\cal H}^{q}_{12}=-h_{ij}\big(Q^{ij}_{1}\sigma^{3}_{1}+Q^{ij}_{2}\sigma^{3}_{2}\big)\;.

(5)

We have adopted it in
Lin2024PNEFT
to study the quantum correlation and entanglement among the quantum bodies with or without the presence of a quantum black hole.

One can study the unitary evolution of the quantum bodies by using (
5
). However, this will also involve the dynamics of the metric fluctuation, and the backreaction should be taken into account. On the other hand, if we are considering the almost static sources, we can solve the (on-shell) tidal electric field
E_{1;ij}
sourced by
Q_{1}^{ij}
from the linear Einstein equation, and then reduce (
5
) to
-{1\over 2}Q_{2}^{ij}E_{1;ij}
. One can iterate this procedure to obtain the post-Newtonian effective field theory (PN-EFT) order by order. By appropriate gauge-fixings, the leading order PN-EFT is just the generalization of the Newtonian interaction between two quadrupole moments,

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{\rm PN,q}^{(0)}={\cal H}^{(0)}_{\rm PN,c}\sigma_{1}^{3}\otimes\sigma_{2}^{3}

(6)

where
{\cal H}_{\rm PN;c}
is the classical part of the above PN-EFT, and takes the form

\displaystyle{\cal H}^{(0)}_{\rm PN,c}=-{G\over r_{12}^{5}}Q^{ij}_{1}\Big(2\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}+35\hat{r}_{i}\hat{r}_{j}\hat{r}_{k}\hat{r}_{l}-20\hat{r}_{i}\delta_{jk}\hat{r}_{l}\Big)Q_{2}^{kl}\;,

(7)

and
\vec{r}_{12}=r_{12}\hat{r}_{12}:=\vec{r}_{1}-\vec{r}_{2}
, with
\vec{r}_{1,2}
the spatial locations of the corresponding quantum bodies.

The above EFT holds only in the low-energy regime:

\displaystyle b\ll r_{12}\ll T

(8)

where
b
is the typical size of the quantum body and
T
is the interaction time. This condition implies that

\displaystyle{\cal H}^{(0)}_{\rm PN,c}\sim{G\delta M_{1}\delta M_{2}\over r_{12}}\Big({b\over r_{12}}\Big)^{4}\ll 1\;.

(9)

where
\delta M_{1,2}
are the overall mass fluctuation responsible for creating the corresponding quadrupole moments. The higher-order terms in PN-EFT are velocity-dependent and will be suppressed in the limit of the weak field and low velocity.

When considering the quantum properties of the internal two-qubit state, we see that (
6
) can be interpreted as the interaction Hamiltonian with a non-local coupling constant under the low-energy condition (
8
). In general, this nonlocal interaction will produce quantum entanglement between two distant quantum bodies. Though the Newtonian PN-EFT (
6
) and (
7
) is derived from the Einstein gravity in the low-energy limit (
8
), its form by itself is Newtonian gravity. Thus, we can claim that Newtonian gravity can produce quantum entanglement.

III
Entanglement produced by Newtonian gravity

Now we will evaluate the entanglement between two qubits produced by the interaction (
6
), which induces a unitary evolution

\displaystyle U_{T}={\cal T}e^{i{\cal H}^{(0)}_{\rm PN,c}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\;w_{T}(t)\;\sigma^{3}_{1}\otimes\sigma^{3}_{2}}=e^{-i\lambda(\vec{r}_{12},T)\;\sigma^{3}_{1}\otimes\sigma^{3}_{2}}\;,

(10)

with

\displaystyle\lambda(\vec{r}_{12},T):=-{\cal H}^{(0)}_{\rm PN,c}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\;w_{T}(t)=-(2\pi)^{1/4}{\cal H}^{(0)}_{\rm PN,c}T\sim{\cal O}(1){G\delta M_{1}\delta M_{2}\over r_{12}}\Big({b\over r_{12}}\Big)^{4}T\;,

(11)

where we have introduced a Gaussian window function
w_{T}(t)={e^{-(t/T)^{2}}\over(\pi/2)^{1/4}}
such that
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|w_{T}(t)|^{2}dt=T
. Thus, the effective interaction time is
T
even though the whole process ranges from the past infinity to the future infinity.

For our purpose, the initial two-qubit state is chosen to be the direct product state of two quantum bodies with the same sign of quadrupole moments,

\displaystyle|{\rm init}\rangle=|+\rangle_{1}\otimes|+\rangle_{2}:=|++\rangle

(12)

which has no entanglement. Since this state is not the eigenstate of
\sigma^{3}_{1}\otimes\sigma^{3}_{2}
, the unitary evolution will produce quantum entanglement, and the resultant final state is

\displaystyle|{\rm final}\rangle=U_{T}|{\rm init}\rangle=\cos\lambda|++\rangle-i\sin\lambda|--\rangle\;.

(13)

Note that the result is for all orders of
\lambda:=\lambda(\vec{r}_{12},T)
due to the simplicity of (
10
). We see that the Schmidt rank generally changes from 1 to 2, indicating that entanglement is produced by Newtonian gravity during the unitary evolution. Or, more quantitatively, by evaluating the entanglement entropy, it gives

\displaystyle S=-\cos^{2}\lambda\;\log[\cos^{2}\lambda]-\sin^{2}\lambda\;\log[\sin^{2}\lambda].

(14)

Given the quantities
\vec{r}_{12}
,
b
,
\delta M_{1,2}
and
T
under the constraint (
8
), one can evaluate the entanglement entropy
S
, which is nonzero unless
\lambda=0,\pi/2,\pi
up to the addition of multiples of
2\pi
. These magic points of vanishing entanglement production in the parameter space deserve further attention.

IV
Discussion

In this note, we show that the PN-EFT approach, which is widely used in the gravitational wave community to study the inspiral phase of binary black holes without invoking quantum gravity, can also be applied to quantum bodies interacting via a classical gravitational field. Based on this scheme, we have arrived at the conclusion that Newtonian gravity can generally produce quantum entanglement. We have some further remarks:

1.

From our point of view, many controversial debates about whether classical gravity can produce entanglement arise from the lack of a unified framework and language. It includes preparing quantum bodies and systematically deriving the final state of two internal qubits.

2.

We have carefully combined all the ingredients and show that a consistent framework for tackling the current issue can be obtained by generalizing the post-Newtonian approach to quantum bodies interacting via classical gravity.

3.

We can consider the effect on the production of quantum entanglement from higher PN interactions, which involve the motion of the quantum bodies.

4.

Even restricted to Newtonian order, our result shows that there are magic points for the effective interaction energy
{\cal H}^{(0)}_{\rm PN,c}T\sim\lambda
, at which the entanglement production suddenly vanishes. It is unclear what the physical implications are.

5.

The Newtonian gravity couples the distant quantum bodies non-locally, so the production of entanglement is not in conflict with the no-go theorem of the GIE protocol, which assumes the local interaction.

6.

The PN-EFT approach also provides a framework to deal with the quantum state reduction in the context of semi-classical gravity by replacing the sudden state reduction with the unitary dynamics based on PN-EFT, in a similar way to resolve the wavefunction collapse by the decoherence process.

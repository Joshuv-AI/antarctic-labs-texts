The best 𝐦 \mathbf{m} -term trigonometric approximations of the classes of periodic functions of one and many variables in the space B q , 1 B_{q,1}
K.V. Pozharska 1,2 , A.S. Romanyuk 1

Abstract
. Exact order estimates are obtained of the best m m -term trigonometric approximations of the Nikol’skii-Besov classes B p , θ r B^{r}_{p,\theta} of periodic functions of one and many variables in the space B q , 1 B_{q,1} . In the univariate case ( d = 1 d=1 ), we get the orders of the respective approximation characteristics on the classes B p , θ r B^{r}_{p,\theta} as well as on the Sobolev classes W p , 𝜶 r W^{r}_{p,{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}} in the space B ∞ , 1 B_{\infty,1} in the case 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞ 1\leq p\leq\infty .

Introduction.

In the paper we investigate the best
m
-term trigonometric approximations of the Nikol’skii-Besov classes
B^{r}_{p,\theta}
of periodic functions of one and many variables in the space
B_{q,1}

L_{q}
-norm.
In the univariate case (
d=1

mentioned approximation characteristics on the classes
B^{r}_{p,\theta}
as well as on the Sobolev classes
W^{r}_{p,\alpha}
in the space
B_{\infty,1}
.

The obtained results, from the one hand side generalize the respective statements for the Lebesque space
L_{q}

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

]
. From the other hand side, they complement the estimates of this quantity on the classes

.
We will comment on this more in remarks to the obtained results.

Let us further define the classes
B^{r}_{p,\theta}
, the spaces
B_{q,1}
and the approximation characteristics.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
, be the
d
-dimensional Euclidean space of points
\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})
, and
L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
,

1\leq p\leq\infty
,
\mathbb{T}^{d}:=\prod^{d}_{j=1}\,[0,\,2\pi)
,
be the space of functions
f(\boldsymbol{x})
that are

2\pi
-periodic in each variable and
their norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{p}

\displaystyle:=\Big((2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|f(\boldsymbol{x})|^{p}\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}\Big)^{1/p},\quad 1\leq p<\infty,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle:=\operatorname{ess\,sup}\limits_{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|f(\boldsymbol{x})|

is finite.

Furthermore, let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\boldsymbol{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
For
f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, we denote

\Delta_{\boldsymbol{h}}f(\boldsymbol{x})=f(\boldsymbol{x}+\boldsymbol{h})-f(\boldsymbol{x})
, and define the difference of order
k
with a step
\boldsymbol{h}
by the following formula

\Delta_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{k}f(\boldsymbol{x})=\Delta_{\boldsymbol{h}}\Delta_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{k-1}f(\boldsymbol{x}),\quad\Delta_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{0}f(\boldsymbol{x})=f(\boldsymbol{x}).

The modulus of smoothness of order
k
for the function
f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
is defined by the formula

\omega_{k}(f,t)_{p}:=\sup_{|\boldsymbol{h}|\leq t}\|\Delta_{\boldsymbol{h}}^{k}f\|_{p},\ \text{where}\ |\boldsymbol{h}|=\sqrt{h_{1}^{2}+\dots+h_{d}^{2}},\ t\in\mathbb{R},\ t>0.

We say that the function
f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
belongs to the space

B^{r}_{p,\theta}
,
1\leq p,\theta\leq\infty
,

r>0
, if

\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}(t^{-r}\omega_{k}(f,t)_{p})^{\theta}\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}\Bigg)^{1/\theta}<\infty,\quad 1\leq\theta<\infty

and

\sup_{t>0}t^{-r}\omega_{k}(f,t)_{p}<\infty,\quad\theta=\infty.

The norm of the space
B^{r}_{p,\theta}
is defined by the formulas

\|f\|_{B^{r}_{p,\theta}}:=\|f\|_{p}+\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}(t^{-r}\omega_{k}(f,t)_{p})^{\theta}\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}\Bigg)^{1/\theta},\quad 1\leq\theta<\infty,

and

\|f\|_{B^{r}_{p,\infty}}:=\|f\|_{p}+\sup_{t>0}t^{-r}\omega_{k}(f,t)_{p},\quad\theta=\infty,

for some
k>r
.

The spaces
H^{r}_{p}\equiv B^{r}_{p,\infty}
and
B^{r}_{p,\theta}
,

1\leq\theta<\infty
, were introduced by S.M. Nikol’skii
[
16
]
and O.V. Besov
[
6
]
, respectively.

The Nikol’skii-Besov class is defined as a unit ball in the space
B^{r}_{p,\theta}
. We will use for it the same notation, i.e., put

B^{r}_{p,\theta}:=\{f\in B^{r}_{p,\theta}\colon\ \|f\|_{B^{r}_{p,\theta}}\leq 1\}.

Note that the classes
B^{r}_{p,\theta}
and
H^{r}_{p}
were studied from the approximation viewpoint in
[
3
,
5
,
8
,
13
,
21
,
23
,
24
,
25
]
, where additional relevant references can be found. In what follows, it will be convenient for us to use the equivalent (up to absolute constants) definition for the norm in the space
B^{r}_{p,\theta}
.

Let
V_{l}(t)
,
l\in\mathbb{N}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
, denotes the de la Vallée-Poussin kernel of the form

V_{l}(t):=l^{-1}\sum\limits^{2l-1}_{k=l}D_{k}(t),

where

D_{k}(t):=\sum_{m=-k}^{k}e^{imt}

is the Dirichlet kernel.

The multidimensional kernel
V_{l}(\boldsymbol{x})
,
l\in\mathbb{N}
,
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, is defined by the formula

V_{l}(\boldsymbol{x}):=\prod_{j=1}^{d}V_{l}(x_{j}).

Let us put

V_{l}(f,\boldsymbol{x}):=(f\ast V_{l})(\boldsymbol{x}),

i.e., define a
convolution of the function
f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
with the multidimensional kernel

V_{l}(\boldsymbol{x})
.

For
f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, we set

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}(f)

\displaystyle:=\sigma_{0}(f,\boldsymbol{x})=V_{l}(f,\boldsymbol{x}),

\displaystyle\sigma_{s}(f)

\displaystyle:=\sigma_{s}(f,\boldsymbol{x})=V_{2^{s}}(f,\boldsymbol{x})-V_{2^{s-1}}(f,\boldsymbol{x}),\ s\in\mathbb{N}.

Then for the function
f\in B^{r}_{p,\theta}
,
1\leq p,\theta\leq\infty
,
r>0
, the following equivalences hold
[
15
]

(1.1)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{r}_{p,\theta}}

\displaystyle\asymp\left(\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\ 2^{sr\theta}\|\sigma_{s}(f)\|_{p}^{\theta}\right)^{1/\theta},\quad 1\leq\theta<\infty,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{r}_{p,\infty}}

\displaystyle\asymp\sup_{s\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}2^{sr}\|\sigma_{s}(f)\|_{p},\quad\theta=\infty,

where
\mathbb{Z}_{+}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Here and below, for positive sequences
a(n)
and
b(n)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we will use the notation
a(n)\asymp b(n)
, that means that there exist the constants
0<C_{1}<C_{2}
such that
C_{1}a(n)\leq b(n)\leq C_{2}a(n)
. If only
b(n)\leq C_{2}a(n)
(or
b(n)\geq C_{1}a(n)
) holds, then we will write
b(n)\ll a(n)
(
b(n\gg a(n))
).

Note, that in the case
1<p<\infty
one can write an equivalent to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) norm definition, using some unions of “blocks” of the Fourier series of the function
f
instead of
\sigma_{s}(f)
.
For
f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
,
1<p<\infty
, we define

f_{(0)}:=f_{(0)}(\boldsymbol{x})=f(0),\ f_{(s)}:=f_{(s)}(\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mu(s)}\widehat{f}(\boldsymbol{k})e^{i(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{x})},\ s\in\mathbb{N},

where

\mu(s):=\{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\colon\ 2^{s-1}\leq\max_{j=1,\dots,d}|k_{j}|<2^{s}\},

(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{x})=k_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+k_{d}x_{d}
and

\widehat{f}(\boldsymbol{k})=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f(\boldsymbol{t})e^{-i(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{t})}{\rm d}\boldsymbol{t}

are the Fourier coefficients of
f
.

Then, if
1<p<\infty
,
1\leq\theta\leq\infty
,
r>0
, for
f\in B^{r}_{p,\theta}
we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{r}_{p,\theta}}

\displaystyle\asymp\left(\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\ 2^{sr\theta}\|f_{(s)}\|_{p}^{\theta}\right)^{1/\theta},\quad 1\leq\theta<\infty,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{r}_{p,\infty}}

\displaystyle\asymp\sup_{s\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}2^{sr}\|f_{(s)}\|_{p},\quad\theta=\infty.

Note, that for the spaces
B^{r}_{p,\theta}
the following embeddings hold

B^{r}_{p,1}\subset B^{r}_{p,\theta_{1}}\subset B^{r}_{p,\theta_{2}}\subset B^{r}_{p,\infty}\equiv H^{r}_{p},\quad 1\leq\theta_{1}\leq\theta_{2}\leq\infty.

Now we define a norm
\|\cdot\|_{B_{q,1}}
in the subspaces
B_{q,1}
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
, of functions
f\in L_{q}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
. Such norm is similar to the decomposition norm of functions from the Besov spaces
B^{r}_{p,\theta}
(see (
1.1
)). So, for trigonometric
polynomials
t
with respect to the multiple trigonometric system
\{e^{i(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{x})}\}_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
, the norm
\|t\|_{B_{q,1}}
is defined by the formula

\|t\|_{B_{q,1}}:=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\|\sigma_{s}(t)\|_{q}.

Analogically
we define the norm
\|f\|_{B_{q,1}}

for any function
f\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, such that the series

\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\|\sigma_{s}(f)\|_{q}
is convergent.

Note, that in the case
1<q<\infty
it holds

\|f\|_{B_{q,1}}\asymp\sum\limits_{s\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\|f_{(s)}\|_{q}.

For
f\in B_{q,1}
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
, the following relations hold:

(1.3)

\|f\|_{q}\ll\|f\|_{B_{q,1}};\qquad\|f\|_{B_{1,1}}\ll\|f\|_{B_{q,1}}\ll\|f\|_{B_{\infty,1}}.

Further we define the approximation characteristics that will be investigated in what follows and a close to it quantity that will appear in remarks.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a normed space with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{X}}
and
\Theta_{m}
be a set of
m
arbitrary
d
-dimensional vectors with integer coordinates. Let us denote by

P(\Theta_{m}):=P(\Theta_{m},\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\Theta_{m}}c_{\boldsymbol{k}}e^{i(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{x})},\ c_{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\mathbb{C},

a trigonometric polynomial with “numbers” of harmonics from the set
\Theta_{m}
. For
f\in\mathcal{X}
, we consider the quantity

e_{m}(f)_{\mathcal{X}}:=\inf\limits_{c_{\boldsymbol{k}}}\inf\limits_{\Theta_{m}}{\left\|f-P(\Theta_{m})\right\|_{\mathcal{X}}},

which is called the best
m
-term
trigonometric approximation of
f
. For a class

F\subset{\mathcal{X}}
, we set

e_{m}(F)_{\mathcal{X}}:=\sup\limits_{f\in F}{e_{m}(f)_{\mathcal{X}}}.

The quantity
e_{m}(f)_{2}:=e_{m}(f)_{L_{2}(\mathbb{T})}
for univariate functions was introduced by S.B. Stechkin
[
32

in order to formulate a criterion of absolute convergence of orthogonal series in the general case of approximations by polynomials with respect to arbitrary orthogonal system in a Hilbert space.

Further the quantities
e_{m}(F)_{\mathcal{X}}
for certain functional classes and spaces
\mathcal{X}=L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
,
d\geq 1
, as well as other normed spaces, were studied by many authors. The detailed overview can be found in the papers

As it was indicated above, when commenting the obtained results, we will refer to the estimates of a close to
e_{m}(F)_{\mathcal{X}}
approximation characteristics.

For
f\in\mathcal{X}
, we denote

S_{\Theta_{m}}(f):=S_{\Theta_{m}}(f,\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\Theta_{m}}{\widehat{f}}(\boldsymbol{k}){e^{i(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{x})}}

and consider the quantity

e_{m}^{\bot}(f)_{\mathcal{X}}:=\inf\limits_{\Theta_{m}}{\|f-S_{\Theta_{m}}(f)\|}_{\mathcal{X}}.

If

The quantity

in the space

. The quantities

we get the following relation

2.
Estimates of the quantities
e_{m}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{B_{q,1}}
(
d\geq 1
)

In this part of the paper we prove (for the case
d\geq 1
) the exact order estimates of the best
m
-term trigonometric approximations of the classes
B^{r}_{p,\theta}
in the space

B_{q,1}
in two cases: a)
1<p\leq 2<q<\infty
; b)
2<p<q<\infty
. When considering the case a), the so-called “small smoothness” effect appeared, that was noted when investigating this quantity
in the
L_{q}
-space on some classes of functions in the papers
[
18
,
31
]
.

In our case, it consists in the following. When the smoothness parameter
r
crosses the limiting case
r=d/p
, a “jump” in the estimate of the quantity
e_{m}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{B_{q,1}}
appears.
In other words, we showed that for
1<p\leq 2<q<\infty
, in the cases
d(1/p-1/q)<r<d/p
and
r>d/p
the quantities
e_{m}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{B_{q,1}}
have different orders.

Before we move to formulating and proving the obtained results, let us recall the known statements that will be used.

Lemma A

[
4
]
.
Let
2<q<\infty
. Then for any trigonometric polynomial

P\left(\Theta_{n};\boldsymbol{x}\right)
and any
m<n
, one can find a trigonometric polynomial
P\left(\Theta_{m};\boldsymbol{x}\right)
such that

\left\|P(\Theta_{n})-P(\Theta_{m})\right\|_{q}\leq C_{3}(q)\sqrt{\frac{n}{m}}\left\|P\left(\Theta_{n}\right)\right\|_{2},

and, moreover,
\Theta_{m}\subset\Theta_{n}.

Theorem A.

Let

t(\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_{|k_{j}|\leq n_{j}}c_{\boldsymbol{k}}e^{i(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{x})},

where
c_{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\mathbb{C}
,
k_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
n_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
,
j=1,\dots d
. Then for
1\leq p<q\leq\infty
the following inequality holds

(2.1)

\|t\|_{q}\leq 2^{d}\prod_{j=1}^{d}n_{j}^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\|t\|_{p}.

The inequality (
2.1
) was obtained by S.M. Nikol’skii
[
16
]
and is referred to as the inequality for different metrics.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
1<p\leq 2<q<\infty
,
1\leq\theta\leq\infty
. Then the following relations hold

(2.2)

e_{m}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{B_{q,1}}\asymp\begin{cases}m^{-\frac{q}{2}(\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q})},&d(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})<r<\frac{d}{p},\\
m^{-\frac{r}{d}+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}},&r>\frac{d}{p}.\end{cases}

Note that in the case
1<p\leq 2<q<p/(p-1)
,
r>d
, the order of the quantity
e_{m}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{B_{q,1}}
was obtained in
[
27
]
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Let us first prove the upper estimates in (
2.2
). In view of the fact, that the respective estimates do not depend on the parameter
\theta
, it is sufficient to consider the case
\theta=\infty
, i.e., to prove the upper estimate of the quantity
e_{m}(H^{r}_{p})_{B_{q,1}}
.

Let
f\in H^{r}_{p}
and a number
l\in\mathbb{N}
is such that satisfies the condition
2^{dl}\leq m<2^{d(l+1)}
, i.e.,
m\asymp 2^{dl}
. We will approximate the function
f
by the polynomials of the form

(2.3)

P(\Theta_{m},\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_{s=0}^{l-1}f_{(s)}(\boldsymbol{x})+\sum_{s=l}^{[\gamma l]}P(\Theta_{m_{s}},\boldsymbol{x}),

where the polynomials
P(\Theta_{m_{s}},\boldsymbol{x})
approximate the “blocks”
f_{(s)}(\boldsymbol{x})
according to Lemma A, and the numbers
m_{s}
,
\gamma
are chosen, depending on the considered case, so that the number of harmonics of the polynomial
P(\Theta_{m},\boldsymbol{x})
is of order
m
. (Here and in what follows,
[a]
denotes the integer part of the number
a
.)

Hence, noting that

f(\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}f_{(s)}(\boldsymbol{x}),

and for the function
f\in H^{r}_{p}
it holds

(2.4)

\|f_{(s)}\|_{p}\ll 2^{-sr},

by (
1.3
) we can write

\displaystyle\|f-P(\Theta_{m})\|_{B_{q,1}}

\displaystyle\ll\Bigg\|\sum_{s=l}^{[\gamma l]}(f_{(s)}-P(\Theta_{m_{s}}))\Bigg\|_{B_{q,1}}+\Bigg\|\sum_{s=[\gamma l]+1}^{\infty}f_{(s)}\Bigg\|_{B_{q,1}}

(2.5)

\displaystyle:=I_{1}+I_{2}.

Let us first estimate the quantity
I_{2}
.

Taking into account the definition of the norm
\|\cdot\|_{B_{q,1}}
, by the inequality of different metrics (
2.1
) and the estimate (
2.4
), we get

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\Bigg\|\sum_{s=[\gamma l]+1}^{\infty}f_{(s)}\Bigg\|_{B_{q,1}}\asymp\sum_{s=[\gamma l]+1}^{\infty}\|f_{(s)}\|_{q}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{s=[\gamma l]+1}^{\infty}2^{ds(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})}\|f_{(s)}\|_{p}\ll\sum_{s=[\gamma l]+1}^{\infty}2^{-s(r-d(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}))}

(2.6)

\displaystyle\asymp 2^{-\gamma ld(\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q})}.

Moving to estimation of the term
I_{1}
, we use Lemma A. Hence, we get

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\Bigg\|\sum_{s=l}^{[\gamma l]}(f_{(s)}-P(\Theta_{m_{s}}))\Bigg\|_{B_{q,1}}\leq\sum_{s=l}^{[\gamma l]}\|f_{(s)}-P(\Theta_{m_{s}})\|_{q}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{s=l}^{[\gamma l]}\left(\frac{2^{sd}}{m_{s}}\right)^{1/2}\|f_{(s)}\|_{2}\ll\sum_{s=l}^{[\gamma l]}\frac{2^{\frac{sd}{2}}2^{sd(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}\|f_{(s)}\|_{p}}{m_{s}^{1/2}}

(2.7)

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{s=l}^{[\gamma l]}\frac{2^{-sd(\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{p})}}{m_{s}^{1/2}}.

To further estimate the quantity

I_{1}
,
let us separately consider two cases.

a) Let
d(1/p-1/q)<r<d/p
. For
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
let us
set

(2.8)

\displaystyle m_{s}

\displaystyle=[2^{dl}2^{s(\frac{d}{p}-r)}2^{-\frac{ql}{2}(\frac{d}{p}-r)}]+1,

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=\frac{q}{2}.

It is easy to verify that for such choice of the numbers
m_{s}
and
\gamma
, the order of harmonics of the polynomial (
2.3
) does not exceed
m
.
We have

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{l-1}|\mu(s)|+\sum_{s=l}^{[\frac{q}{2}l]}m_{s}

\displaystyle\ll 2^{dl}+\left(\frac{q}{2}-1\right)l+2^{dl}2^{-\frac{ql}{2}(\frac{d}{p}-r)}\sum_{s=l}^{[\frac{q}{2}l]}2^{s(\frac{d}{p}-r)}

\displaystyle\ll 2^{dl}+\left(\frac{q}{2}-1\right)l+2^{dl}2^{-\frac{ql}{2}(\frac{d}{p}-r)}2^{\frac{ql}{2}(\frac{d}{p}-r)}\ll 2^{dl}\asymp m.

(Here and in what follows, by
|A|
we denote the number of elements of the finite set
A
.)

Therefore, substituting the values of
m_{s}
from (
2.8
) into
(
2.7
) and making elementary transformations, we obtain the estimate of the quantity
I_{1}
:

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{s=l}^{[\frac{q}{2}l]}2^{-ds(\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{p})}2^{-\frac{dl}{2}}2^{-\frac{s}{2}(\frac{d}{p}-r)}2^{\frac{ql}{4}(\frac{d}{p}-r)}

\displaystyle=2^{-\frac{dl}{2}}2^{\frac{ql}{4}(\frac{d}{p}-r)}\sum_{s=l}^{[\frac{q}{2}l]}2^{-\frac{ds}{2}(\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{p})}\ll 2^{-\frac{dl}{2}}2^{\frac{ql}{4}(\frac{d}{p}-r)}2^{-\frac{dql}{4}(\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{p})}

(2.9)

\displaystyle=2^{-\frac{dl}{2}-\frac{qrl}{2}+\frac{dql}{2p}}=2^{-\frac{dql}{2}(\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q})}\asymp m^{-\frac{q}{2}(\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q})}.

Returning to estimation of the quantity
I_{2}
(see (
2.6
)), we obtain for
\gamma=q/2
that

(2.10)

I_{2}\ll 2^{-\frac{dql}{2}(\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q})}\asymp m^{-\frac{q}{2}(\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q})}.

Combining the estimates (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) with (
2.5
), we get for
f\in H^{r}_{p}
:

e_{m}(f)_{B_{q,1}}\ll m^{-\frac{q}{2}(\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q})},

that concludes the proof of the upper estimate for the quantity

e_{m}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{B_{q,1}}
,
1\leq\theta\leq\infty
, in the case

d(1/p-1/q)<r<d/p
.

b) Let
r>d/p
. Here we set

\displaystyle m_{s}

\displaystyle=[2^{dl(\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{p}+1)}2^{-s(r-\frac{d}{p})}]+1,

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}}\,.

In this case, the number of harmonics in the polynomial (
2.3
) is estimated as follows.

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{l-1}|\mu(s)|+\sum_{s=l}^{[\frac{q}{2}l]}m_{s}

\displaystyle\ll 2^{dl}+(\gamma-1)l+2^{dl(\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{p}+1)}\sum_{s=l}^{[\gamma l]}2^{-s(r-\frac{d}{p})}

\displaystyle\ll 2^{dl}+(\gamma-1)l+2^{dl(\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{p}+1)}2^{-l(r-\frac{d}{p})}

\displaystyle=2^{dl}+2^{dl}+(\gamma-1)l\ll 2^{dl}\asymp m.

Further substituting the values of
m_{s}
into
(
2.7
) and making respective elementary transformations, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{s=l}^{[\gamma l]}2^{-ds(\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{p})}2^{-\frac{dl}{2}(\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{p}+1)}2^{\frac{ds}{2}(\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{p})}

\displaystyle=2^{-\frac{dl}{2}(\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{p}+1)}\sum_{s=l}^{[\gamma l]}2^{-\frac{ds}{2}(\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{p})}\ll 2^{-\frac{dl}{2}(\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{p}+1)}2^{-\frac{dl}{2}(\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{p})}

(2.11)

\displaystyle=2^{-dl(\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{2})}\asymp m^{-\frac{r}{d}+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}.

What concerns the term
I_{2}
from (
2.5
), here for the chosen value of
\gamma
we get

(2.12)

I_{2}\ll 2^{-dl(\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{2})}\asymp m^{-\frac{r}{d}+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}.

Therefore, taking into account the estimates (
2.11
), (
2.12
) and using (
2.5
), we obtain

e_{m}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{B_{q,1}}\ll e_{m}(H^{r}_{p})_{B_{q,1}}\ll m^{-\frac{r}{d}+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}.

The upper estimates in (
2.2
) are proved.

Let us get the respective lower estimates. Here we note that they follow from the known estimates of the quantities

e_{m}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{q}
.

Theorem B.

Let
1<p\leq 2<q<\infty
,
1\leq\theta\leq\infty
. Then for
d\geq 1
the following relations hold

(2.13)

e_{m}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{q}\asymp\begin{cases}m^{-\frac{q}{2}(\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q})},&d(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})<r<\frac{d}{p},\\
m^{-\frac{r}{d}+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}},&r>\frac{d}{p}.\end{cases}

Note that in the case
d(1/p-1/q)<r<d/p
this estimate was obtained in
[
31
]
, and for
r>d/p
in
[
7
]
.

Hence, taking into account that
\|\cdot\|_{B_{q,1}}\gg\|\cdot\|_{q}

and using (
2.13
), we derive to the required lower estimates of the quantity

e_{m}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{B_{q,1}}
.

Theorem
2.1
is proved.
∎

In the following statement, we consider the case
2<p<q<\infty
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
2<p<q<\infty
,
1\leq\theta\leq\infty
,
r>d/2
. Then the following estimate holds

(2.14)

e_{m}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{B_{q,1}}\asymp m^{-\frac{r}{d}}.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

The upper estimate follows from Theorem
2.1
as
p=2
, in view of the embedding

B^{r}_{p,\theta}\subset B^{r}_{2,\theta}
,
p\geq 2
.

The respective lower estimate in (
2.14
) is a corollary from the known statement for the
L_{q}
-space.

Theorem C
[
7
]
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
2<p<q<\infty
,
1\leq\theta\leq\infty
,
r>d/2
. Then the following estimate holds

(2.15)

e_{m}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{q}\asymp m^{-\frac{r}{d}}.

Hence, by the relations (
2.15
) and (
1.3
), we obtain the lower estimate for the quantity
e_{m}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{B_{q,1}}
.

Theorem
2.2
is proved.
∎

Let us comment the results of Theorems
2.1
,
2.2
.

First, let us recall that in the paper
[
27
]
, the authors obtain the estimates for the quantities
e_{m}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{B_{q,1}}
for other relations between the parameters

p
and
q
, namely they proved the following statement.

Corollary A.

Let
d\geq 1
and either
1\leq p\leq q\leq 2
or
1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty
.
Then for
r>d(1/p-1/q)_{+}
,
1\leq\theta\leq\infty
the estimate

(2.16)

e_{m}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{B_{q,1}}\asymp m^{-\frac{r}{d}+(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})_{+}}

holds, where
a_{+}=\max\{a,0\}
.

It is worth mentioning, that the estimates (
2.16
) are realized by approximation of functions
f\in B^{r}_{p,\theta}
by the trigonometric polynomials with the spectrum in cubic regions of the form

t(\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in C^{d}(2^{n})}c_{\boldsymbol{k}}e^{i(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{x})},\ c_{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\mathbb{C},

where
C^{d}(2^{n}):=\{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\colon|k_{j}|<2^{n},j=1,\dots d\}

under the condition
m\asymp 2^{dn}
.

What concerns the relations between the parameters
p
and
q
that were considered in Theorems
2.1
,
2.2
, in these cases the mentioned polynomials do not realize the orders of the respective quantities

e_{m}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{B_{q,1}}
.

It is also worth to compare the obtained results with the estimates for the best orthogonal trigonometric approximations
e^{\bot}_{m}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{B_{q,1}}
.

Let us further formulate the respective statement.

Theorem D

[
27
]
.
Let
d\geq 1
,
1\leq p,q,\theta\leq\infty
,
(p,q)\notin\{(1,1),(\infty,\infty)\}
. Then for
r>d(1/p-1/q)_{+}
it holds

(2.17)

e_{m}^{\bot}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{B_{q,1}}\asymp m^{-\frac{r}{d}+(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})_{+}}.

We note that the relations (
2.17
) are obtained by the approximation of functions
f\in B^{r}_{p,\theta}
by their cubic Fourier sums
S_{n}(f)
of the form

S_{n}(f):=S_{n}(f,\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}f_{(s)}(\boldsymbol{x})

under the condition
m\asymp 2^{dn}
.

Therefore, comparing the results of Theorems
2.1
,
2.2
with the estimate (
2.17
) for the respective values of the parameters
r,p,q
, we see that the quantities
e_{m}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{B_{q,1}}
and
e_{m}^{\bot}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{B_{q,1}}
differ in order.

From the other hand side, comparing the estimates from Theorem D and Corollary A, we see that in both of the cases
1\leq p\leq q\leq 2
and
1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty
,
(p,q)\notin\{(1,1),(\infty,\infty)\}
, it holds

e_{m}^{\bot}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{B_{q,1}}\asymp e_{m}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{B_{q,1}}.

Remark 2.3
.

The question on the order of the quantity
e_{m}^{\bot}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{B_{q,1}}
for the critical smoothness parameter
r=d/p
, that was not considered in Theorem
2.1
, remains open.

3.
Estimate of the quantity

e_{m}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{B_{\infty,1}}
(
d=1
)

As a continuation of investigations made in the previous section, in what follows we consider the univariate case and obtain first an exact order estimate for the quantity
e_{m}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{B_{\infty,1}}
. Afterwards, using this result and the known estimate of the quantity

e_{m}(W^{r}_{p,\alpha})_{\infty}
, we get the order of the best
m
-term trigonometric approximation of the Sobolev classes
W^{r}_{p,\alpha}
in the space
B_{\infty,1}
.

Let us formulate the statement that will be used in further argumentation.

Let
T_{n}(x)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
, is a polynomial of the form

T_{n}:=T_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=-n}^{n}c_{k}e^{ikx},\ c_{k}\in\mathbb{C}.

Lemma B

[
5
]
.
For every
2\leq p<\infty
and
1\leq m\leq 2n
there exists a trigonometric polynomial
T(\Theta_{m})
with at most
m
harmonics, such that
it holds

(3.1)

\left\|T_{n}-T(\Theta_{m})\right\|_{\infty}\leq C_{4}\left(\frac{n}{m}\log\left(\frac{n}{m}+1\right)\right)^{1/p}\left\|T_{n}\right\|_{p},

and the spectrum
\Theta_{m}
is in the segment
[-2n,2n]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
d=1
,
1\leq p,\theta\leq\infty
,
r>\max\{1/p,1/2\}
. Then the following estimate holds

(3.2)

e_{m}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{B_{\infty,1}}\asymp m^{-r+(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})_{+}}.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let us get the upper estimate in (
3.2
) in the case
p=2
and
\theta=\infty
, i.e., for the classes
H^{r}_{2}
. Let us choose
l\in\mathbb{N}
such that
the inequality
2^{l}\leq m<2^{l+1}
holds and for
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
introduce the numbers

(3.3)

\displaystyle m_{s}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}2^{s},&0\leq s\leq l-1,\\
[2^{l(r+\frac{1}{2})}\cdot 2^{-s(r-\frac{1}{2})}]+1,&l\leq s\leq[\gamma l],\\
0,&s\geq[\gamma l]+1,\end{cases}

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=\frac{r}{r-\frac{1}{2}}.

we will approximate a function
f\in H^{r}_{2}
by the polynomial
T(\Theta_{m},x)
of the form

(3.4)

T(\Theta_{m},x)=\sum_{s=0}^{l-1}f_{(s)}(x)+\sum_{s=l}^{[\gamma l]}T(\Theta_{m_{s}},x),

where
T(\Theta_{m_{s}},x)
are the polynomials that approximate the “blocks”
f_{(s)}(x)
of the Fourier series of the function
f(x)
according to Lemma B.

It is easy to verify that in this case, the number of harmonics of the polynomial
T(\Theta_{m},x)
for
r>1/2
is of order
m
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{l-1}2^{s}+\sum_{s=l}^{[\gamma l]}m_{s}

\displaystyle\ll 2^{l}+2^{l(r+\frac{1}{2})}\sum_{s=l}^{[\gamma l]}2^{-s(r-\frac{1}{2})}+(\gamma-1)l

\displaystyle\ll 2^{l}\cdot 2^{l(r+\frac{1}{2})}\cdot 2^{-l(r-\frac{1}{2})}+(\gamma-1)l\ll 2^{l}+(\gamma-1)l\ll 2^{l}\asymp m.

Hence, for
f\in H^{r}_{2}
, by the choice of the polynomial (
3.4
), we can write

\displaystyle\left\|f-T(\Theta_{m})\right\|_{B_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\sum_{s=l}^{[\gamma l]}(f_{(s)}-T(\Theta_{m_{s}}))\right\|_{B_{\infty,1}}+\left\|\sum_{s=[\gamma l]+1}^{\infty}f_{(s)}\right\|_{B_{\infty,1}}

(3.5)

\displaystyle:=I_{3}+I_{4}.

We proceed with estimation of the term
I_{3}
.

Taking into account the definition of
\|\cdot\|_{B_{\infty,1}}
and using a property of the convolution, we get

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\Big\|\sigma_{s}\ast\sum_{s^{\prime}=l}^{[\gamma l]}(f_{(s^{\prime})}-T(\Theta_{m_{s^{\prime}}}))\Big\|_{\infty}\leq\sum_{s=l-1}^{[\gamma l]}\Big\|\sigma_{s}\ast\sum_{s^{\prime}=s}^{s+1}(f_{(s^{\prime})}-T(\Theta_{m_{s^{\prime}}}))\Big\|_{\infty}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s=l-1}^{[\gamma l]}\|\sigma_{s}\|_{1}\cdot\Bigg\|\sum_{s^{\prime}=s}^{s+1}(f_{(s^{\prime})}-T(\Theta_{m_{s^{\prime}}}))\Bigg\|_{\infty},

where
\sigma_{s}=V_{2^{s}}-V_{2^{s-1}}
.

Further, by the fact that
\|V_{2^{s}}\|_{1}\leq C_{5}
,
C_{5}>0
(see, e.g.,
[
34
, Ch. 1,
\lx@sectionsign
1]
), we have

(3.7)

\|\sigma_{s}\|_{1}=\|V_{2^{s}}-V_{2^{s-1}}\|_{1}\leq\|V_{2^{s}}\|_{1}+\|V_{2^{s-1}}\|_{1}\leq C_{6}.

Combining (
3.7
) with (
3.6
) and taking into account the choice (
3.3
) of the numbers
m_{s}
, we get

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{s=l-1}^{[\gamma l]}\Bigg\|\sum_{s^{\prime}=s}^{s+1}(f_{(s^{\prime})}-T(\Theta_{m_{s^{\prime}}}))\Bigg\|_{\infty}\leq\sum_{s=l-1}^{[\gamma l]}\sum_{s^{\prime}=s}^{s+1}\|f_{(s^{\prime})}-T(\Theta_{m_{s^{\prime}}})\|_{\infty}

(3.8)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s=l}^{[\gamma l]}\|f_{(s)}-T(\Theta_{m_{s}})\|_{\infty}+\|f_{([\gamma l]+1)}\|_{\infty}:=I_{5}+I_{6}.

To estimate the term
I_{5}
, we use the relation (
3.1
). In our notation it takes the form

\|f_{(s)}-T(\Theta_{m_{s}})\|_{\infty}\ll\left(\frac{2^{s}}{m_{s}}\log\left(\frac{2^{s}}{m_{s}}+1\right)\right)^{1/2}\|f_{(s)}\|_{2}.

Noting that for
f\in H^{r}_{2}
the estimate
\|f_{(s)}\|_{2}\ll 2^{-sr}
holds and substituting the values of
m_{s}
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{5}

\displaystyle\ll 2^{-\frac{l}{2}(r+\frac{1}{2})}\sum_{s=l}^{[\gamma l]}2^{\frac{s}{2}(r+\frac{1}{2})}\left(\log\left(\frac{2^{s(r+\frac{1}{2})}}{2^{l(r+\frac{1}{2})}}+1\right)\right)^{1/2}2^{-sr}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-\frac{l}{2}(r+\frac{1}{2})}\sum_{s=l}^{[\gamma l]}2^{-\frac{s}{2}(r-\frac{1}{2})}\left(\log\left(\frac{2^{s(r+\frac{1}{2})}}{2^{l(r+\frac{1}{2})}}+1\right)\right)^{1/2}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\ll 2^{-\frac{l}{2}(r+\frac{1}{2})}2^{-\frac{l}{2}(r-\frac{1}{2})}=2^{-lr}\asymp m^{-r}.

Concerning the term
I_{6}
we note that its estimate will be obtained during estimation of the quantity
I_{4}
. Namely, it will be shown that

(3.10)

I_{6}\ll m^{-r}.

Hence, substituting (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) into (
3.8
) we get

(3.11)

I_{3}\ll m^{-r}.

To finish the upper estimation for the quantity
e_{m}(H^{r}_{2})_{B_{\infty,1}}
, it remains to estimate the term
I_{4}
.

In view of the definition of
\|\cdot\|_{B_{q,1}}
, the inequality of different metrics (
2.1
) and the values of
\gamma
, we can write

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle=\Bigg\|\sum_{s=[\gamma l]+1}^{\infty}f_{(s)}\Bigg\|_{B_{\infty,1}}\ll\sum_{s=[\gamma l]+1}^{\infty}\Bigg\|\sigma_{s}\ast\sum_{s^{\prime}=s}^{s+1}f_{(s^{\prime})}\Bigg\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s=[\gamma l]+1}^{\infty}\|\sigma_{s}\|_{1}\Bigg\|\sum_{s^{\prime}=s}^{s+1}f_{(s^{\prime})}\Bigg\|_{\infty}\ll\sum_{s=[\gamma l]+1}^{\infty}\|f_{(s)}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{s=[\gamma l]+1}^{\infty}2^{\frac{s}{2}}\|f_{(s)}\|_{2}\ll\sum_{s=[\gamma l]+1}^{\infty}2^{-s(r-\frac{1}{2})}

(3.12)

\displaystyle\ll 2^{-l\gamma(r-\frac{1}{2})}=2^{-lr}\asymp m^{-r}.

To conclude, we substitute (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) in (
3.5
) and get the upper estimate for the quantity

e_{m}(B^{r}_{2,\theta})_{B_{\infty,1}}
:

(3.13)

e_{m}(B^{r}_{2,\theta})_{B_{\infty,1}}\ll e_{m}(H^{r}_{2})_{B_{\infty,1}}\ll m^{-r}.

From (
3.13
) we can easily get the upper estimate for the quantity

e_{m}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{B_{\infty,1}}
for other values of the parameter
p
.

Let us consider two cases.

a) Let
2<p\leq\infty
. Then, in view of the embedding
B^{r}_{p,\theta}\subset B^{r}_{2,\theta}
, we can write

e_{m}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{B_{\infty,1}}\ll e_{m}(B^{r}_{2,\theta})_{B_{\infty,1}}\asymp m^{-r}.

b) Let
1\leq p<2
. By the inequality of different metrics, for
f\in B^{r}_{p,\theta}
we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{r}_{p,\theta}}

\displaystyle\asymp\left(\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}2^{sr\theta}\|\sigma_{s}(f)\|_{p}^{\theta}\right)^{1/\theta}\gg\left(\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}2^{sr\theta}2^{s(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})\theta}\|\sigma_{s}(f)\|_{2}^{\theta}\right)^{1/\theta}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}2^{s(r-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{2})\theta}\|\sigma_{s}(f)\|_{2}^{\theta}\right)^{1/\theta}\asymp\|f\|_{B^{r-1/p+1/2}_{2,\theta}}\,.

This yields the following embedding

B^{r}_{p,\theta}\subset B^{r-1/p+1/2}_{2,\theta},\ 1\leq p<2,

and hence, by the estimate (
3.13
) with
r-1/p+1/2
in place of
r
, we get

e_{m}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{B_{\infty,1}}\ll e_{m}(B^{r-1/p+1/2}_{2,\theta})_{B_{\infty,1}}\ll m^{-r+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}.

The upper estimate in Theorem
3.1
is proved.

The lower estimate in (
3.2
) is a corollary from the known relation (see
[
20
]
)

(3.14)

e_{m}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{\infty}\asymp m^{-r+(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})_{+}},\ 1\leq p,\theta\leq\infty,\ r>\max\left\{1/p,1/2\right\},

and the inequality
\|\cdot\|_{B_{\infty,1}}\gg\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
.

Theorem
3.1
is proved.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

From the result of Theorem
3.1
and the estimate (
3.14
), the relation

e_{m}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{B_{\infty,1}}\asymp e_{m}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{\infty}\asymp m^{-r+(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})_{+}}.

follows.

From the other hand side, comparing the results of Theorem
3.1
and Theorem D (for
d=1
), we notice that in the considered case the quantities
e_{m}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{B_{\infty,1}}
and

e_{m}^{\bot}(B^{r}_{p,\theta})_{B_{\infty,1}}
differ in order.

We conclude the paper by getting the analogical to Theorem
3.1
statement for the Sobolev classes
W^{r}_{p,\alpha}
. Further we define these classes (see, e.g.,
[
34
, Ch. 1,
\lx@sectionsign
3]
).

Let

F_{r}(x,\alpha)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{-r}\cos(kx-\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}),\ r>0,\ \alpha\in\mathbb{R},

is the Bernoulli kernel.

Then by
W^{r}_{p,\alpha}
we denote a class of functions
f
, such that can be written in the form

f(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\varphi(y)F_{r}(x-y,\alpha){\rm d}y,

where
\varphi\in L_{p}
,
\|\varphi\|_{p}\leq 1
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
d=1
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
r>\max\{1/p,1/2\}
. Then for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
the following estimate holds

(3.15)

e_{m}(W^{r}_{p,\alpha})_{B_{\infty,1}}\asymp m^{-r+(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})_{+}}.

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

The upper estimate follows from Theorem
3.1
as
\theta=\infty
and the embedding
W^{r}_{p,\alpha}\subset H^{r}_{p}
(see, e.g.,
[
34
, Ch. 1,
\lx@sectionsign
3]
). So,

e_{m}(W^{r}_{p,\alpha})_{B_{\infty,1}}\ll e_{m}(H^{r}_{p})_{B_{\infty,1}}\asymp m^{-r+(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})_{+}}.

The respective lower estimate in (
3.15
) follows from the relation (see
[
5
]
)

(3.16)

e_{m}(W^{r}_{p,\alpha})_{\infty}\asymp m^{-\min\{r,r-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{2}\}},\ 1\leq p\leq\infty,\ r>1/p,

and the inequality
\|\cdot\|_{B_{\infty,1}}\gg\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
.

Theorem
3.3
is proved.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Comparing the estimates (
3.15
) and (
3.16
) we conclude that for
r>\max\{1/p,1/2\}
it holds

e_{m}(W^{r}_{p,\alpha})_{B_{\infty,1}}\asymp e_{m}(W^{r}_{p,\alpha})_{\infty}.

From the other hand side, comparing the estimate (
3.15
) with the result of
[
26
, Theorem 2]

e_{m}^{\bot}(W^{r}_{p,\alpha})_{B_{\infty,1}}\asymp m^{-r+\frac{1}{p}},\ 1<p<\infty,\ r>1/p,\ \alpha\in\mathbb{R},

we see that for respective values of the parameters
r
and
p
the quantities
e_{m}(W^{r}_{p,\alpha})_{B_{\infty,1}}
and
e_{m}^{\bot}(W^{r}_{p,\alpha})_{B_{\infty,1}}
differ in order.

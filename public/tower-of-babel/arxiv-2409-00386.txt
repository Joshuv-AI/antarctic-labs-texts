Free boundary value problem for the radial symmetric compressible isentropic Navier-Stokes equations with density-dependent viscosity
Xiangdi Huang, Weili Meng, Anchun Ni* a. Institute of Mathematics, Academy of Mathematics and Systems SciencesChinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing 100080, P.R.China;

Abstract
This paper is devoted to the study of free-boundary-value problem of the compressible Naiver-Stokes system with density-dependent viscosities μ = c ​ o ​ n ​ s ​ t > 0 , λ = ρ β \mu=const>0,\lambda=\rho^{\beta} which was first introduced by Vaigant-Kazhikhov [ 23 ] in 1995. By assuming the endpoint case β = 1 \beta=1 in the radially spherical symmetric setting, we prove the (a priori) expanding rate of the free boundary is algebraic for multi-dimensional flow, and particularly establish the global existence of strong solution of the two-dimensional system for any large initial data. This also improves the previous work of Li-Zhang [ 14 ] where they proved the similar result for β > 1 \beta>1 . The main ingredients of this article is making full use of the geometric advantage of domain as well as the critical space dimension two. Keywords: Compressible Navier-Stokes equations; Free boundary; Strong solutions. Mathematics Subject Classifications (2010): 76W15, 35Q30.

Introduction

We study the free-boundary-value problem of the following multi-dimensional compressible viscous fluids:

\begin{cases}\rho_{t}+\div(\rho\mathbb{u})=0,&\text{ in }\Omega_{t},\\
(\rho\mathbb{u})_{t}+\div(\rho\mathbb{u}\otimes\mathbb{u})=\div\mathbb{S},&\text{ in }\Omega_{t},\end{cases}

(1.1)

with rheological assumption and the stress-free boundary condition:

\mathbb{S}=-P(\rho)\mathbb{I}+2\mu\mathbb{D}\mathbb{u}+\lambda(\rho)\div\mathbb{u}\mathbb{I},

(1.2)

P(\rho)=\rho^{\gamma},\mu=const,\lambda(\rho)=\rho,\gamma>1,

(1.3)

\mathbb{S}\cdot\mathbb{n}=0,\text{ on }\Gamma_{t},

(1.4)

where
\Gamma_{t}
is the free surface of domain
\Omega_{t}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}(d=2,3)
. Here
\rho(\mathbb{x},t),\mathbb{u}(\mathbb{x},t),P(\mathbb{x},t)
stand for the fluid density, velocity and pressure respectively,
\mathbb{S}=\mathbb{S}(\rho,\mathbb{u})
is the total stress tensor and
\mu,\lambda
are the bulk and shear viscosity coefficients which may depend on the density.

The compressible Navier-Stokes equations with density-dependent viscosity
\mu=\mu(\rho),\lambda=\lambda(\rho)
have attracted a lot of attention due to the physical importance and rich phenomena, which can be derived from the fluid-dynamical approximation to the Boltzmann equation. A special case
\mu(\rho)=\rho,\lambda(\rho)=0,\gamma=2
and
d=2
expresses exactly the viscous Saint-Venant system describing the motion for shallow water where
\rho,\mathbb{u}
represent the height and the average velocity, see
[
6
,
16
]
for details. The system considered in this paper follows the model proposed by Vaigant-Kazhikhov
[
23
]
assuming that
\lambda(\rho)=\rho^{\beta}
and
\mu
is a positive constant.

The research on the well-posedness and long-time dynamic behavior of compressible viscous fluids with constant viscosity has significant progress in recent years: see
[

. We briefly recall some results about the strong solution in multi-dimensional cases: Matsumura-Nishida
[

3
D when the initial regular data is close to a equilibrium state, and the case when the initial data with small energy but possible large oscillations and vacuum is proved by Huang-Li-Xin
[
11
]
, the case when density is large is proved by Yu
[
27
]
and Huang-Li-Zhang
[
10

. When the sufficiently small or large assumptions fail, Xin
[

As for the density-dependent viscosity flow

When it comes to the free-boundary-value problem in our setting, many new challenges appear because of the influence of the vacuum state. In our paper, we consider the system in radially spherically symmetric setting:

\rho(\mathbb{x},t)=\rho(r,t),\quad\mathbb{u}(\mathbb{x},t)=u(r,t)\frac{\mathbb{x}}{r},\quad r=|\mathbb{x}|.

(1.5)

And then the stress-free boundary value problem (
1.1
)-(
1.4

\begin{cases}\rho_{t}+(\rho u)_{r}+\frac{d-1}{r}(\rho u)=0,\\
(\rho u)_{t}+(\rho u^{2})_{r}+\frac{d-1}{r}\rho u^{2}+(\rho^{\gamma})_{r}=\left((2\mu+\rho)(u_{r}+\frac{d-1}{r}u)\right)_{r},\end{cases}

(1.6)

with the initial data

\displaystyle\rho(r,0)=\rho_{0}(r),\quad u(r,0)=u_{0}(r),\quad 0\leq r<a_{0},

(1.7)

u(0,t)=0,\quad\left(\rho^{\gamma}-(2\mu+\rho)(u_{r}+\frac{d-1}{r}u)\right)(a(t),t)=0,

(1.8)

where
a(t)
satisfies

a^{\prime}(t)=u(a(t),t),

(1.9)

corresponding to the free surface of
\Omega_{t}
, that is:

\Omega_{t}=\left\{(r,t)|0\leq r\leq a(t),0\leq t\leq T\right\}.

The well-posedness and Lagrangian structure to the spherically symmetric compressible flow with density-dependent viscosity and stress-free boundary has been studied in recent years. For the density-degenerate viscosity
\mu=\rho,\lambda=0

[

[

Our goal is to establish the well-posedness result and analyze the dynamic behavior in the endpoint case
\beta=1
, where the first result can be stated as following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
d=2
or
3
, and
(\rho,\mathbb{u},a)
is a global strong solution of the free-boundary-value problem (
1.6
)-(
1.9
), then the expanding rate of

is algebraic. Indeed, we have

\begin{split}a(t)\geq\begin{cases}C(1+t)^{\frac{1}{d\gamma}},&\gamma>1+\frac{1}{d},\\
C\left(\frac{1+t}{1+\log(1+t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{d\gamma}},&\gamma=1+\frac{1}{d}.\end{cases}\end{split}

(1.10)

Moreover, denote
a_{M}(t)=\sup_{s\in[0,t]}a(s)
, then

a_{M}(t)\geq\begin{cases}C(1+t)^{\frac{1}{d\gamma}},&\gamma>1+\frac{1}{d},\\
C\left(\frac{1+t}{1+\log(1+t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{d\gamma}},&\gamma=1+\frac{1}{d},\\
C(1+t)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}},&\gamma\in(1,1+\frac{1}{d}).\end{cases}

(1.11)

Remark 1.1
.

In
[
14
]
, Li-Zhang proved an analogous result of Theorem
1.1
in 2D case. Following their ideas, the algebraic expanding rate can be also established for 3D flows.

Now we are ready to state our main theorem as follows:

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
\gamma>1,d=2
. Assume that the initial data
(\rho_{0},\mathbb{u}_{0})
satisfy

\rho_{0}\in L^{1}(\Omega_{0})\cap W^{1,q}(\Omega_{0}),\quad\mathbb{u}_{0}\in H^{2}(\Omega_{0}),\quad q>2.

(1.12)

and

\displaystyle\rho_{0}(r)>0\quad\text{for}\quad r\in[0,a_{0}),\quad\rho_{0}(a_{0})\geq 0,\quad\text{and}\int_{\Omega_{0}}\rho_{0}dx=1.

(1.13)

Then for any
T>0
, the free-boundary-value problem (
1.6
)-(
1.9
) has a unique global spherically symmetric strong solution
(\rho,\mathbb{u})
satisfying

\begin{split}\rho\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega_{t})\cap W^{1,q}(\Omega_{t})),\mathbb{u}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega_{t})).\end{split}

Moreover, it holds that

\begin{split}&\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho|\mathbb{u}|^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\rho^{\gamma}\right)d\mathbb{x}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{t}}(2\mu+\rho)|\div\mathbb{u}|^{2}d\mathbb{x}dt\leq C,\\
&\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\Omega_{t}}F^{2}d\mathbb{x}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho|\dot{\mathbb{u}}|^{2}d\mathbb{x}dt\leq C,\\
&\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho|\dot{\mathbb{u}}|^{2}d\mathbb{x}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{t}}(2\mu+\rho)|\div\dot{\mathbb{u}}|^{2}d\mathbb{x}dt\leq C,\\
&\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega_{t})\cap W^{1,p}(\Omega_{t})}+\left\lVert\mathbb{u}\right\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\right)+\int_{0}^{T}\left(\left\lVert\nabla\mathbb{u}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbb{u}_{t}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}\right)dt\leq C.\end{split}

where
F
denotes the effective viscous flux, which is given by

F\eqqcolon(2\mu+\rho)\div\mathbb{u}-P(\rho).

In Kazhikhov’s original paper

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that
\mathbb{u}(\mathbb{x},t)
satisfies the radial setting and the boundary condition
\mathbb{u}(0,t)=0
, then it holds that

\left\lVert\mathbb{u}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}=\left\lVert u(t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,a(t))}\leq\left\lVert\nabla\mathbb{u}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\leq\left\lVert\div\mathbb{u}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}.

(2.1)

Proof.

We observe for any
r\in[0,a(t)]
,

\begin{split}|u(r,t)|^{2}=&2\int_{0}^{r}u\partial_{r}u(s,t)ds\leq 2\int_{0}^{a(t)}\left\lvert\partial_{r}u(s,t)\right\rvert\left\lvert\frac{u(s,t)}{s}\right\rvert sds\\
\leq&\int_{0}^{a(t)}\left(\left\lvert\partial_{r}u\right\rvert^{2}s+\left\lvert\frac{u}{s}\right\rvert^{2}s\right)ds\leq\int_{0}^{a(t)}\left(\partial_{r}u+\frac{u}{s}\right)^{2}sds\\
=&\int_{\Omega_{t}}\left\lvert\div\mathbb{u}(\mathbb{x},t)\right\rvert^{2}d\mathbb{x}=\left\lVert\div\mathbb{u}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}.\end{split}

Here the fourth step we cancel the cross term using the following fact:

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{a(t)}2u\partial_{r}uds=\int_{0}^{a(t)}\partial_{r}(u^{2})ds=u^{2}(a(t),t)\geq 0.\end{split}

Combining together, we obtain the above.

Proof of Theorem

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem

The upper bound of
a(t)
is directly from the energy inequality (
2.3
) in the case

\begin{split}a(t)=&a_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}a^{\prime}(s)ds\leq a_{0}+Ct^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}|a^{\prime}(s)|^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&C\left(1+t^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}u^{2}(a(s),s)ds\right)\leq C(1+t)^{\frac{1}{2}},\end{split}

(3.1)

where in the last step

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t}u^{2}(a(s),s)ds=&\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{a(s)}2uu_{r}drds\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{a(s)}(u_{r}+\frac{u}{r})^{2}rdrds\\
\leq&\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega_{s}}|\div\mathbb{u}|^{2}d\mathbb{x}ds\leq E_{0}.\end{split}

Now we attempt to derive the lower bound for

\begin{split}1\equiv&\int_{0}^{a(t)}\rho r^{d-1}dr=\int_{0}^{a(t)}r^{(d-1)\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}\cdot\rho r^{\frac{d-1}{\gamma}}dr\\

which yields that

(a(t))^{-d(\gamma-1)}\leq C\int_{0}^{a(t)}\rho^{\gamma}r^{d-1}dr.

(3.2)

Therefore, there exists a constant

\displaystyle a(t)\geq C.

(3.3)

) that

\begin{split}((1+t)^{-(2-d(\gamma-1))}H(t))^{\prime}\leq&C(1+t)^{-(2-d(\gamma-1))}((1+t)\frac{d}{dt}\left(a^{d}(t)\right)+\frac{d^{2}}{2})\\
\leq&C\frac{d}{dt}\left((1+t)^{d(\gamma-1)-1}a^{d}(t)\right)+C(1+t)^{-(2-d(\gamma-1))}\\
&-C\left(d(\gamma-1)-1\right)(1+t)^{-(2-d(\gamma-1))}a^{d}(t)\\
\leq&C\frac{d}{dt}\left((1+t)^{d(\gamma-1)-1}a^{d}(t)\right)+C(1+t)^{-(2-d(\gamma-1))}.\end{split}

Then it comes for
\gamma\in(1+\frac{1}{d},1+\frac{2}{d}):

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{a(t)}\rho^{\gamma}r^{d-1}dr\leq Ca^{d}(t)(1+t)^{-1}+C(1+t)^{-1}\leq C(1+t)^{-1}a^{d}(t),\\
\end{split}

(3.9)

and for
\gamma=1+\frac{1}{d}:

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{a(t)}\rho^{\gamma}r^{d-1}dr\leq&Ca^{d}(t)(1+t)^{-1}+C(1+t)^{-1}\log(1+t)\\
\leq&C(1+t)^{-1}(1+\log(1+t))a^{d}(t).\end{split}

(3.10)

Combining (
3.2
), (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), we arrive at (
1.10
) finally.

It remains to check the lower bound of
a_{M}(t)
. For
\gamma\geq 1+\frac{2}{d}
, we deduce from (
3.6
), (
3.8
) and the mass conservation (
2.2
) that

\begin{split}H^{\prime}(t)\leq\frac{d^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{a(t)}(2\mu+\rho)r^{d-1}dr\leq C(1+a^{d}(t)).\end{split}

Integrating the above with respect to time, we get

(1+t)^{2}\int_{0}^{a(t)}\rho^{\gamma}r^{d-1}dr\leq C\left((1+t)+\int_{0}^{t}a^{d}(s)ds\right),

which together with (
3.2
) yields that

\left(a(t)\right)^{-d(\gamma-1)}\leq C(1+t)^{-2}\left((1+t)+\int_{0}^{t}(a(s))^{d}ds\right).

(3.11)

Therefore, we claim (
1.11
) holds by a simple proof of contradiction.

As for
\gamma\in(1,1+\frac{2}{d})
, it can be estimated as

\begin{split}H^{\prime}(t)\leq\frac{2-d(\gamma-1)}{(1+t)}H(t)+C(1+a^{d}(t)).\end{split}

Multiplying it with
(1+t)^{2-d(\gamma-1)}
, we have

\begin{split}(1+t)^{2-d(\gamma-1)}((1+t)^{-(2-d(\gamma-1))}H)^{\prime}\leq&C(a^{d}(t)+1).\end{split}

Consequently, for
\gamma\not=1+\frac{1}{d}
,

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{a(t)}\rho^{\gamma}r^{d-1}dr\leq C(1+t)^{-1}+C(1+t)^{-d(\gamma-1)}\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)^{-(2-d(\gamma-1))}a^{d}(s)ds.\end{split}

(3.12)

For
\gamma=1+\frac{1}{d}
,

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{a(t)}\rho^{\gamma}r^{d-1}dr\leq C(1+t)^{-1}\log(1+t)+C(1+t)^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)^{-1}a^{d}(s)ds.\end{split}

(3.13)

Together with (
3.2
), and proving by contradiction, we finally arrive at (
1.11
).

4
The a priori estimates

In this section, we will derive the main a priori estimates (
4.4
), (
4.6
) and (
4.9
), then obtain the bound (
4.7
) of the density along particle paths, which is crucial to establish the global existence of the strong solutions.

We define the effective viscous flux
F
and auxiliary function
\theta
of the density as follows

\begin{split}F(r,t)=&(2\mu+\rho)\div\mathbb{u}-P,\\
\theta(r,t)=&2\mu\ln\rho+\rho.\end{split}

Then by (
1.6
) it satisfies that

\begin{split}\theta_{t}+u\partial_{r}\theta=-(2\mu+\rho)\div\mathbb{u}=-F-P,\end{split}

(4.1)

and

(\rho u)_{t}+\partial_{r}\left(\rho u^{2}\right)+\frac{\rho u^{2}}{r}=\partial_{r}F.

(4.2)

Integrating (
4.2
) on
[a(t),r]
, we have

\begin{split}F(r,t)=&F(a(t),t)+\int_{a(t)}^{r}(\rho u)_{t}ds+\int_{a(t)}^{r}\partial_{r}(\rho u^{2})ds+\int_{a(t)}^{r}\frac{\rho u^{2}}{s}ds\\
=&\partial_{t}\int_{a(t)}^{r}\rho uds+u\partial_{r}\int_{a(t)}^{r}\rho uds+\int_{a(t)}^{r}\frac{\rho u^{2}}{s}ds.\end{split}

Substitute the above into (
4.1
), we can deduce the following structure

\partial_{t}\left(\theta+\xi\right)+u\partial_{r}\left(\theta+\xi\right)+\int_{a(t)}^{r}\frac{\rho u^{2}}{s}ds+P(\rho)=0,

(4.3)

where
\xi(r,t)=\int_{a(t)}^{r}\rho uds.

Now we first show the higher integrability of the density in the following lemma:

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
\gamma>1
, then there exists a constant
C>0
depends on
\gamma,T
and initial data
(\rho_{0},\mathbb{u}_{0},a_{0})
, such that

\begin{split}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho^{2\gamma+1}\leq C.\end{split}

(4.4)

Proof.

We define
f=(\theta+\xi)_{+}
and test the equation (
4.3
) with
\rho f^{2\gamma-1}
, then it comes

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho f^{2\gamma-1}(\partial_{t}+u\partial_{r})(\theta+\xi)d\mathbb{x}\\
=&\frac{1}{2\gamma}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho\frac{D}{Dt}f^{2\gamma}d\mathbb{x}=\frac{1}{2\gamma}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho f^{2\gamma}d\mathbb{x}\end{split}

using the assumption (
1.9
). And so we have

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2\gamma}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho f^{2\gamma}d\mathbb{x}\leq&\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho f^{2\gamma-1}\left(\int^{a(t)}_{r}\frac{\rho u^{2}}{s}ds\right)d\mathbb{x}\\
\leq&C\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2\gamma+1}(\Omega_{t})}^{\frac{1}{2\gamma}}\left\lVert\rho^{\frac{1}{2\gamma}}f\right\rVert_{L^{2\gamma}(\Omega_{t})}^{2\gamma-1}\left\lVert\int^{a(t)}_{r}\frac{\rho u^{2}}{s}ds\right\rVert_{L^{2\gamma+1}(\Omega_{t})},\end{split}

and here

\begin{split}\left\lVert\int_{a(t)}^{r}\frac{\rho u^{2}}{s}ds\right\rVert_{L^{2\gamma+1}(\Omega_{t})}\leq&\left\lVert\frac{\rho u^{2}}{r}\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{2(2\gamma+1)}{2\gamma+3}}(\Omega_{t})}\leq\left\lVert\rho u\right\rVert_{L^{2\gamma+1}(\Omega_{t})}\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\\
\leq&\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2\gamma+1}(\Omega_{t})}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\leq\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2\gamma+1}(\Omega_{t})}\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}.\end{split}

Hence we have that

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho f^{2\gamma}d\mathbb{x}\leq C\left(1+\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho f^{2\gamma}d\mathbb{x}+\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho^{2\gamma+1}d\mathbb{x}\right)\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}.

On the other hand, for some
C>1
,

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho^{2\gamma+1}d\mathbb{x}\leq&\int_{{\Omega_{t}}\cap\left\{\rho\leq C\right\}}\rho^{2\gamma+1}d\mathbb{x}+\int_{\Omega_{t}\cap\left\{\rho\geq C\right\}}\rho(2\mu\ln\rho+\rho)^{2\gamma}d\mathbb{x}\\
\leq&C\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho d\mathbb{x}+C\int_{\Omega_{t}\cap\left\{\rho\geq C\right\}}\rho(\theta+\xi)_{+}^{2\gamma}d\mathbb{x}+C\int_{\Omega_{t}\cap\left\{\rho\geq C\right\}}\rho|\xi|^{2\gamma}d\mathbb{x}\\
=&C+C\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho f^{2\gamma}d\mathbb{x}+C\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho|\xi|^{2\gamma}d\mathbb{x},\end{split}

(4.5)

and

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho|\xi|^{2\gamma}d\mathbb{x}\leq&C\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{2\gamma+1}{\gamma+1}}(\Omega_{t})}\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert_{L^{2(2\gamma+1)}(\Omega_{t})}^{2\gamma}\leq C\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{2\gamma+1}{\gamma+1}}(\Omega_{t})}\left\lVert\nabla\xi\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{2\gamma+1}{\gamma+1}}(\Omega_{t})}^{2\gamma}\\
\leq&C\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{2\gamma+1}{\gamma+1}}(\Omega_{t})}\left\lVert\rho u\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{2\gamma+1}{\gamma+1}}(\Omega_{t})}^{2\gamma}\leq C\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{2\gamma+1}{\gamma+1}}(\Omega_{t})}\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2\gamma+1}(\Omega_{t})}^{\gamma}\left\lVert\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2\gamma}\\
\leq&C\left(\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega_{t})}+\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2\gamma+1}(\Omega_{t})}\right)\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2\gamma+1}(\Omega_{t})}^{\gamma}\left\lVert\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2\gamma}\\
\leq&C+C\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2\gamma+1}(\Omega_{t})}^{\gamma+1}\leq C_{\epsilon}+\epsilon\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho^{2\gamma+1}dx.\end{split}

so we have that

\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho^{2\gamma+1}d\mathbb{x}\leq C\left(1+\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho f^{2\gamma}d\mathbb{x}\right).

And we conclude:

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho f^{2\gamma}d\mathbb{x}\leq C\left(1+\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho f^{2\gamma}d\mathbb{x}\right)\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}

and so
\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho^{2\gamma+1}d\mathbb{x}\leq C
.
∎

Based on previous lemma, we can show the higher integrability of the velocity as follows.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose
\gamma>1
, then there exists a constant
C>0
depends on
\gamma,T
and initial data
(\rho_{0},\mathbb{u}_{0},a_{0})
, such that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho|\mathbb{u}|^{3}d\mathbb{x}\leq C.

(4.6)

Proof.

Multiplying
\eqref{CNS}_{2}
by
|\mathbf{u}|\mathbf{u}
and integrating by parts over
\Omega_{t}
implies

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{3}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho|\mathbb{u}|^{3}d\mathbb{x}+\int_{\Omega_{t}}(2\mu+\rho)|\mathbf{u}|\div\mathbf{u}|^{2}d\mathbb{x}\\
=&-\int_{\Omega_{t}}(2\mu+\rho)(\div\mathbb{u})\mathbb{u}\cdot\nabla|\mathbb{u}|d\mathbb{x}+\int_{\Omega_{t}}P(\rho)\div(|\mathbb{u}|\mathbb{u})d\mathbb{x}.\end{split}

Here

\begin{split}&-\int_{\Omega_{t}}(2\mu+\rho)(\div\mathbb{u})\mathbb{u}\cdot\nabla|\mathbb{u}|d\mathbb{x}\\
=&-\int_{\Omega_{t}}(2\mu+\rho)\left(\div\mathbb{u}\right)\left(\frac{\mathbb{u}}{|\mathbb{u}|}\cdot\nabla|\mathbb{u}|\right)|\mathbb{u}|d\mathbb{x}=-\int_{\Omega_{t}}(2\mu+\rho)\left(u_{r}+\frac{1}{r}u\right)u_{r}|\mathbb{u}|d\mathbb{x}\\
=&-\int_{\Omega_{t}}(2\mu+\rho)u_{r}^{2}|\mathbb{u}|d\mathbb{x}-\int_{\Omega_{t}}(2\mu+\rho)\left(\frac{1}{r}uu_{r}\right)|\mathbb{u}|d\mathbb{x}\\
\leq&-\int_{\Omega_{t}}(2\mu+\rho)u_{r}^{2}|\mathbb{u}|d\mathbb{x}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{t}}(2\mu+\rho)|\div\mathbb{u}|^{2}|\mathbb{u}|d\mathbb{x}.\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{t}}P(\rho)\div(|\mathbb{u}|\mathbb{u})d\mathbb{x}\leq&C\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho^{\gamma}|\mathbb{u}||\nabla\mathbb{u}|d\mathbb{x}\\
\leq&C\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2\gamma}(\Omega_{t})}^{\gamma}\left\lVert\mathbb{u}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\left\lVert\nabla\mathbb{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\\
\leq&C\left\lVert\nabla\mathbb{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}.\end{split}

Combining together, we have that

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho|\mathbb{u}|^{3}d\mathbb{x}\leq C\left\lVert\nabla\mathbb{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}.

This yields a bound for
\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho|\mathbb{u}|^{3}d\mathbb{x}
.
∎

Having the above lemmas on hand, we come to the most important part of this paper, which gives the lower and upper bound of the density along the particle paths.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(\rho,\mathbb{u},a(t))
be a strong solution of free-boundary value problem (
1.6
)-(
1.9
), then there exist a constant
C>0
depends on
\gamma,(\rho_{0},\mathbb{u}_{0},a_{0})
and time
T
, such that along the particle path
r=r_{x_{0}}(t)
defined by

\begin{cases}\frac{d}{dt}r_{x_{0}}(t)=u(r_{x_{0}}(t),t),\\
r_{x_{0}}(0)=r_{0},x_{0}=1-\int_{r_{0}}^{a_{0}}\rho_{0}(r)rdr,\end{cases}

we have upper bound and lower bound for density:

\rho_{0}(r_{0})e^{-C}\leq\rho(r_{x_{0}}(t),t)\leq\rho_{0}(r_{0})e^{C}.

(4.7)

Proof.

Consider the particle path
r=r_{x_{0}}(t)
and (
4.3
), we have

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}(\theta+\xi)(r_{x_{0}}(t),t)+P(\rho)(r_{x_{0}}(t),t)+\int_{a(t)}^{r_{x_{0}}(t)}\frac{\rho u^{2}}{s}ds=0,\end{split}

which yields that:

\begin{split}&2\mu\log\frac{\rho(r_{x_{0}}(t),t)}{\rho_{0}(r_{0})}+\rho(r_{x_{0}}(t),t)-\rho_{0}(r_{0})+\xi(r_{x_{0}}(t),t)-\xi(r_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}P(\rho)d\tau\\
=&\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int^{a(\tau)}_{r}\frac{\rho u^{2}}{s}ds\right)d\tau\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\rho(\tau)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\tau})}\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\tau})}^{2}d\tau.\end{split}

(4.8)

Next, by using Lemma
4.2
and the bound (
3.1
) of
a(t)
, one obtains

\begin{split}|\xi(r_{x_{0}}(t),t)|\leq\int^{a(t)}_{0}\left\lvert\rho u\right\rvert dr\leq&C\int_{0}^{a(t)}(\rho|u|^{3}r)^{\frac{1}{3}}\cdot\rho^{\frac{2}{3}}\cdot\left(r^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}dr\\
\leq&C\left\lVert\rho(t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}^{\frac{2}{3}}\left(\int_{0}^{a(t)}\rho|u|^{3}rdr\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\left(\int_{0}^{a(t)}r^{-\frac{1}{2}}dr\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\\
\leq&C\left\lVert\rho(t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}^{\frac{2}{3}}.\end{split}

(4.9)

If
\log\frac{\rho(r_{x_{0}}(t),t)}{\rho_{0}(r_{0})}<0
, then

\displaystyle\rho(r_{x_{0}}(t),t)<\rho_{0}(r_{0})\leq\left\lVert\rho_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{0})}.

If
\log\frac{\rho(r_{x_{0}}(t),t)}{\rho_{0}(r_{0})}\geq 0
, by using (
4.8
) we have that

\begin{split}\rho(r_{x_{0}}(t),t)\leq&C+\left\lVert\rho(t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}^{\frac{2}{3}}+C\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\rho(\tau)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\tau})}\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\tau})}^{2}d\tau\\
\leq&C+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\rho(t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}+C\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\rho(\tau)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\tau})}\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\tau})}^{2}d\tau.\end{split}

Therefore, take supreme on the left hand, we get

\begin{split}\left\lVert\rho(t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\leq C+C\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\rho(\tau)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\tau})}\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\tau})}^{2}d\tau.\end{split}

Use the Grönwall inequality, we obtain that

\left\lVert\rho(t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\leq C,
and take supreme about
t\in[0,T]
, we get

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\lVert\rho(t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\leq C

Together with (
4.8
) , it comes that

2\mu\log\frac{\rho(r_{x_{0}}(t),t)}{\rho_{0}(r_{0})}\leq C,

which implies

\rho(r_{x_{0}}(t),t)\leq\rho_{0}(r_{0})e^{C}.

Applying this bound to (
4.8
) again, we can obtain the lower bound

\rho(r_{x_{0}}(t),t)\geq\rho_{0}(r_{0})e^{-C}.

The proof is now finished.
∎

), we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}|I_{2}|&\leq C\int_{0}^{1}\frac{F^{2}}{\sqrt{\rho}}\sqrt{\rho}(ru)_{x}\chi^{2}dx\\
&\leq C\left\lVert F\chi\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\left\lVert F\chi^{\prime}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left\lVert F_{x}\chi\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\left\lVert\frac{F\chi}{\sqrt{\rho}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)}\left\lVert\sqrt{\rho}(ru)_{x}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)}\\
&\leq\dfrac{1}{4}\left\lVert u_{\tau}\chi\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+C\left(1+\left\lVert\frac{F\chi}{\sqrt{\rho}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\right)\left(1+\left\lVert\sqrt{\rho}(ru)_{x}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\right).\end{split}

(5.7)

Next, the Hölder inequality yields that

\displaystyle\begin{split}|I_{3}|&\leq C\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|F|}{\sqrt{\rho}}\sqrt{\rho}(ru)_{x}\chi^{2}dx\leq C\left\lVert\frac{F\chi}{\sqrt{\rho}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)}\left\lVert\sqrt{\rho}(ru)_{x}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)}\\
&\leq C\left\lVert\frac{F\chi}{\sqrt{\rho}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\left\lVert\sqrt{\rho}(ru)_{x}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+C.\end{split}

(5.8)

Finally, note that
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,a(t))}\leq\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}=\left\lVert\sqrt{\rho}(ru)_{x}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)},
we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}|I_{4}|&\leq C\left|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{r}u(ru)_{x}F\chi^{2}dx\right|+C\left|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{r_{x}}{r}u^{2}F\chi^{2}dx\right|\\
&\leq C\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\left\lVert\frac{F\chi}{\sqrt{\rho}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)}\left\lVert\sqrt{\rho}(ru)_{x}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)}+C\left|\int_{0}^{1}u^{2}\frac{1}{\rho r^{2}}F\chi^{2}dx\right|\\
&\leq C\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\left\lVert\frac{F\chi}{\sqrt{\rho}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)}\left\lVert\sqrt{\rho}(ru)_{x}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)}+C\left\lVert\frac{F\chi}{\sqrt{\rho}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}^{2}\left\lVert\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)}\\
&\leq C\left(1+\left\lVert\frac{F\chi}{\sqrt{\rho}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\right)\left(1+\left\lVert\sqrt{\rho}(ru)_{x}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\right),\end{split}

(5.9)

where we used the simple fact that if
x\geq x_{0}
, then there exists a constant
r_{0}>0
such that
r\geq r_{0}
.

Substituting the all above estimates (
5.6
)-(
5.9
) into (
5.5
), we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\int_{0}^{1}u_{\tau}^{2}\chi^{2}dx+\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{d}{d\tau}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{F^{2}}{(2\mu+\rho)\rho}\chi^{2}dx\\
&\leq C\left(1+\left\lVert\frac{F\chi}{\sqrt{(2\mu+\rho)\rho}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\right)\left(1+\left\lVert\sqrt{\rho}(ru)_{x}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\right).\end{split}

Using Gronwall’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq T}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{F^{2}}{(2\mu+\rho)\rho}\chi^{2}dx+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}u_{\tau}^{2}\chi^{2}dxd\tau\leq C.

(5.10)

Next, we deal with the interior estimates in Eulerian coordinates.

Multiplying the momentum equation
\eqref{CNS}_{2}
by
\zeta^{2}\dot{\mathbf{u}}
and integrating the resulting equality over
\Omega_{t}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho|\dot{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}\zeta^{2}dx=\int_{\Omega_{t}}\nabla F\cdot\zeta^{2}\dot{\mathbf{u}}dx=-\int_{\Omega_{t}}F\div\dot{\mathbf{u}}\zeta^{2}dx-\int_{\Omega_{t}}F\nabla(\zeta^{2})\cdot\dot{\mathbf{u}}dx,\end{split}

(5.11)

which we used the stress-free boundary condition (
1.8
).
Note that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\div\dot{\mathbf{u}}&=\frac{D}{Dt}\div\mathbf{u}+|\div\mathbf{u}|^{2}-2\nabla u_{1}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}u_{2}\\
&=\dfrac{D}{Dt}\left(\dfrac{F+P}{2\mu+\rho}\right)+|\div\mathbf{u}|^{2}-2\nabla u_{1}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}u_{2}\end{split}

Then substituting the above equality into (
5.11
), we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho|\dot{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}\zeta^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\frac{F^{2}}{2\mu+\rho}\zeta^{2}dx\\
&=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{t}}F^{2}\div\mathbf{u}\left(\rho\left(\dfrac{1}{2\mu+\rho}\right)^{\prime}-\dfrac{1}{2\mu+\rho}\right)\zeta^{2}dx\\
&\quad-\int_{\Omega_{t}}F\div\mathbf{u}\left(\rho\left(\dfrac{\rho^{\gamma}}{2\mu+\rho}\right)^{\prime}-\dfrac{\rho^{\gamma}}{2\mu+\rho}\right)\zeta^{2}dx\\
&\quad-2\int_{\Omega_{t}}F\nabla u_{1}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}u_{2}\zeta^{2}dx-\int_{\Omega_{t}}F\zeta\nabla\zeta\cdot\dot{\mathbf{u}}dx\\
&\leq C\int_{\Omega_{t}}F^{2}|\div\mathbf{u}|\zeta^{2}dx+C\int_{\Omega_{t}}|F||\div\mathbf{u}|\zeta^{2}dx\\
&\quad+C\left|\int_{\Omega_{t}}F\nabla u_{1}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}u_{2}\zeta^{2}dx\right|+C\left|\int_{\Omega_{t}}F\zeta\nabla\zeta\cdot\dot{\mathbf{u}}dx\right|\\
&:=J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}+J_{4}.\end{split}

(5.12)

For the first term,

\displaystyle\begin{split}|J_{1}|&\leq C\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\left\lVert F\zeta\right\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}\\
&\leq C\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\left\lVert F\zeta\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\left\lVert\nabla(F\zeta)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\sqrt{\rho}\dot{\mathbf{u}}\zeta\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}+C\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}\left\lVert F\zeta\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}\\
&\quad+C\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\left\lVert F\zeta\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\left\lVert F\nabla\zeta\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\sqrt{\rho}\dot{\mathbf{u}}\zeta\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}+C\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}\left\lVert\frac{F\zeta}{\sqrt{2\mu+\rho}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}+C,\end{split}

where we used
\left\lVert F\nabla\zeta\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\leq C
, it is because (
5.10
) and
\text{supp}\{\nabla\zeta\}\subset B_{2\alpha}-B_{\alpha}
.

Next, the Hölder inequality yields that

\displaystyle|J_{2}|\leq C\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\left\lVert\frac{F\zeta}{\sqrt{2\mu+\rho}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}.

Next, the third term can be estimated as

\displaystyle\begin{split}|J_{3}|&\leq\left|\int_{\Omega_{t}}F\nabla(\zeta u_{1})\cdot\nabla^{\perp}(\zeta u_{2})dx\right|+\left|\int_{\Omega_{t}}F(\nabla u_{1}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}u_{2})\zeta^{2}-\nabla(\zeta u_{1})\cdot\nabla^{\perp}(\zeta u_{2}))dx\right|\\
&\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}F\zeta\nabla(\zeta u_{1})\cdot\nabla^{\perp}(\zeta u_{2})dx\right|+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}F(1-\zeta)\nabla(\zeta u_{1})\cdot\nabla^{\perp}(\zeta u_{2})dx\right|\\
&\quad+\left|\int_{\Omega_{t}}F\zeta\nabla\zeta\cdot x\dfrac{u^{2}}{r^{2}}dx\right|.\\
\end{split}

Computing that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}F\zeta\nabla(\zeta u_{1})\cdot\nabla^{\perp}(\zeta u_{2})dx\right|&\leq C\left\lVert F\zeta\right\rVert_{BMO}\left\lVert\nabla(\zeta u_{1})\cdot\nabla^{\perp}(\zeta u_{2})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}\\
&\leq C\left\lVert\nabla(F\zeta)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\left\lVert\nabla(\zeta\mathbf{u})\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}\\
&\leq C(\left\lVert\sqrt{\rho}\dot{\mathbf{u}}\zeta\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+1)(\left\lVert\zeta\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\nabla\zeta\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2})\\
&\leq\dfrac{1}{4}\left\lVert\sqrt{\rho}\dot{\mathbf{u}}\zeta\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}+C\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{4}+C\\
&\leq\dfrac{1}{4}\left\lVert\sqrt{\rho}\dot{\mathbf{u}}\zeta\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}+C\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}\left(\left\lVert\frac{F\zeta}{\sqrt{2\mu+\rho}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}+1\right)+C,\end{split}

which we used
\zeta+\chi\geq 1
and (
5.10
),

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}F(1-\zeta)\nabla(\zeta u_{1})\cdot\nabla^{\perp}(\zeta u_{2})dx\right|\\
&\leq C\int_{B_{2\alpha}-B_{\alpha}}|F|\left|\partial_{r}(\zeta u)\dfrac{\zeta u}{r}\right|dx\\
&\leq C\int_{B_{2\alpha}-B_{\alpha}}|F|\zeta u^{2}dx+C\int_{B_{2\alpha}-B_{\alpha}}|F|\zeta^{2}|u\partial_{r}u|dx\\
&\leq C\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}\left\lVert\frac{F\zeta}{\sqrt{2\mu+\rho}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\\
&\leq C\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}\left(\left\lVert\frac{F\zeta}{\sqrt{2\mu+\rho}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}+1\right),\end{split}

and

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega_{t}}F\zeta\nabla\zeta\cdot x\dfrac{u^{2}}{r^{2}}dx\right|\leq C\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}.

Thus

\displaystyle|J_{3}|\leq\frac{1}{4}\left\lVert\sqrt{\rho}\dot{\mathbf{u}}\zeta\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}+C\left(\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}+1\right)\left(\left\lVert\frac{F\zeta}{\sqrt{2\mu+\rho}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}+1\right).

Then substituting the above equality into (
5.12
), we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\frac{F^{2}}{2\mu+\rho}\zeta^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho|\dot{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}\zeta^{2}dx\\
&\leq C\left(\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}+1\right)\left(\left\lVert\frac{F\zeta}{\sqrt{2\mu+\rho}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}+1\right)+\left\lVert\dot{\mathbf{u}}\nabla\zeta\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}\end{split}

By Gronwall’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\frac{F^{2}}{2\mu+\rho}\zeta^{2}dx+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho|\dot{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}\zeta^{2}dxdt\leq C,

(5.13)

which together with (
5.10
) complete the proof.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

There exists a constant
C
depending on
\gamma,T,\mathbb{x}_{0}
and initial data such that the following estimate holds:

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho|\dot{\mathbb{u}}|^{2}d\mathbb{x}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{t}}(2\mu+\rho)|\div\dot{\mathbb{u}}|^{2}d\mathbb{x}dt\leq C.

(5.14)

Proof.

Differentiating the equation
\eqref{5-1}_{2}
with respect to
\tau
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}u_{\tau\tau}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}u_{\tau}u+(\rho^{\gamma}-(2\mu+\rho)\rho(ru)_{x})_{x\tau}=0

Multiplying the above equation by
ru_{\tau}\chi^{2}
and integrating
x
over
[0,1]
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{d\tau}\int_{0}^{1}u_{\tau}^{2}\chi^{2}dx+\int_{0}^{1}((2\mu+\rho)\rho(ru)_{x}-\rho^{\gamma})_{\tau}\chi^{2}u_{x\tau}rdx\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{r}u_{\tau}^{2}u\chi^{2}dx-\int_{0}^{1}((2\mu+\rho)\rho(ru)_{x}-\rho^{\gamma})_{\tau}(2\chi\chi^{\prime}u_{\tau}r+u_{\tau}\chi^{2}r_{x})dx.\end{split}

(5.15)

Combining the following facts

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}&\leq C\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\leq C\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\leq C\\
\left\lVert\rho_{\tau}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)}&=\left\lVert\rho^{2}(ru)_{x}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq C\\
\left\lVert u_{\tau}\chi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}&\leq C\left\lVert u_{\tau}\chi\right\rVert_{L^{1}(0,1)}+C\left\lVert u_{\tau x}\chi+u_{\tau}\chi^{\prime}\right\rVert_{L^{1}(0,1)}\\
&\leq C\left\lVert u_{\tau}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)}+C\left\lVert\sqrt{\rho}u_{x\tau}\chi\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)}\\
\left\lVert F\chi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}&\leq C\left\lVert F\chi\right\rVert_{L^{1}(0,1)}+C\left\lVert F_{x}\chi+F\chi^{\prime}\right\rVert_{L^{1}(0,1)}\\
&\leq C\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)}+C\left\lVert u_{\tau}\chi\right\rVert_{L^{2}((0,1))},\end{split}

we can obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\frac{d}{d\tau}\int_{0}^{1}(u_{\tau}^{2}+\gamma\rho^{\gamma+1})|(ru)_{x}|^{2})\chi^{2}dx+\int_{0}^{1}(2\mu+\rho)\rho u_{x\tau}^{2}r\chi^{2}dx\\
&\leq C\left(1+\left\lVert u_{\tau}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\right).\end{split}

By Gronwall’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\quad\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq T}\int_{0}^{1}(u_{\tau}^{2}+\gamma\rho^{\gamma+1}|(ru)_{x}|^{2})\chi^{2}dx+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}(2\mu+\rho)\rho u_{x\tau}^{2}r\chi^{2}dxd\tau\leq C.

(5.16)

Next, we deal with the interior estimate in Eulerian coordinates. Operating
\zeta^{2}\dot{\mathbf{u}}_{j}[\partial/\partial_{t}+\div(\mathbf{u}\cdot)]
to both side of
\eqref{CNS}_{2}
and integrating the resulted equations by parts, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho|\dot{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}\zeta^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega_{t}}(2\mu+\rho)|\div\dot{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}\zeta^{2}dx\\
&\leq C+C\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega_{t})}^{4}+C\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}+\frac{\mu}{2}\left\lVert\zeta\div\dot{\mathbf{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}\\
&\leq C+C\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho|\dot{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}\zeta^{2}dx+C\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}+\frac{\mu}{2}\left\lVert\zeta\div\dot{\mathbf{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2},\end{split}

where we used

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega_{t})}^{4}&\leq\left\lVert\nabla(\zeta\mathbf{u})\right\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega_{t})}^{4}+\left\lVert(1-\zeta)\nabla\mathbf{u}-\nabla\zeta\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega_{t})}^{4}\\
&\leq C+\left\lVert\div(\zeta\mathbf{u})\right\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega_{t})}^{4}+\left\lVert(1-\zeta)F\right\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega_{t})}^{4}\\
&\leq C+\left\lVert\zeta\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega_{t})}^{4}\leq C+\left\lVert\zeta F\right\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega_{t})}^{4}\\
&\leq C+\left\lVert\zeta F\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla(\zeta F)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}\\
&\leq C+C\left\lVert\zeta\nabla F\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}\leq C+C\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho|\dot{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}\zeta^{2}dx.\end{split}

Then by Gronwall’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\rho|\dot{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}\zeta^{2}dx+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{t}}(2\mu+\rho)|\div\dot{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}\zeta^{2}dxdt\leq C.

(5.17)

We complete the proof with (
5.16
) and (
5.17
).
∎

In the final proposition, we will show the higher order derivative estimates of the density and velocity, which guarantee the local strong solution can be extended to a global one.

Proposition 5.3
.

For any
p>2
, There exists a constant
C
depending on
\gamma,T,\mathbb{x}_{0}
and initial data such that the following estimate holds:

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega_{t})\cap W^{1,p}(\Omega_{t})}+\left\lVert\mathbb{u}\right\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\right)+\int_{0}^{T}\left(\left\lVert\nabla\mathbb{u}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbb{u}_{t}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}\right)dt\leq C.

(5.18)

Proof.

Operating
\nabla
to the mass equation
\eqref{CNS}_{1}
, multiplying the resulting equation by
q|\nabla\rho|^{p-2}\nabla\rho
, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(|\nabla\rho|^{p})_{t}+\div(|\nabla\rho|^{p}\mathbf{u})+(p-1)|\nabla\rho|^{p}\div\mathbf{u}+p|\nabla\rho|^{p-2}\nabla\rho\cdot(\nabla\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\rho)\\
&\quad+p\rho|\nabla\rho|^{p-2}\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla\div\mathbf{u}=0.\end{split}

(5.19)

Note that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega_{t}}|\nabla\rho|^{p}dx=\int_{\Omega_{t}}\div(|\nabla\rho|^{p}\mathbf{u})dx+\int_{\Omega_{t}}\partial_{t}(|\nabla\rho|^{p})dx.

Integrating the equation (
5.19
) over
\Omega_{t}
we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\dfrac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega_{t})}&\leq C\left(\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega_{t})}\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}+\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega_{t})}\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\right.\\
&\quad+\left.\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega_{t})}+\left\lVert\nabla\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega_{t})}\right)\\
&\leq C\left(1+\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}+\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\right)\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega_{t})}+C\left\lVert\rho\dot{\mathbf{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega_{t})}\\
&\leq C\left(1+\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{\frac{p-2}{2p-2}}\left\lVert\nabla F\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega_{t})}^{\frac{p}{2p-2}}+\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\right)\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega_{t})}+C\left\lVert\rho\dot{\mathbf{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega_{t})}\\
&\leq C\left(1+\left\lVert\dot{\mathbf{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega_{t})}^{\frac{p}{2p-2}}+\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\right)\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega_{t})}+C\left\lVert\rho\dot{\mathbf{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega_{t})}\\
&\leq C\left(1+\left\lVert\div\dot{\mathbf{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\right)\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega_{t})}+C\left\lVert\rho\dot{\mathbf{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega_{t})},\end{split}

which we used Lemma
2.4
to obtain that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\lVert\dot{\mathbf{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega_{t})}&\leq C\left\lVert\dot{\mathbf{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+C\left\lVert\dot{\mathbf{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{\frac{2}{p}}\left\lVert\nabla\dot{\mathbf{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\\
&\leq C(\left\lVert\sqrt{\rho}\dot{\mathbf{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+\left\lVert\nabla\dot{\mathbf{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})})\\
&\leq C(1+\left\lVert\div\dot{\mathbf{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}).\end{split}

Next, by standard
L^{p}
-estimate for elliptic system and Lemma
2.3
, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}&\leq\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{(\zeta\mathbf{u})}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}+\left\lVert(1-\zeta)\nabla\mathbf{u}+\nabla\zeta\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\\
&\leq C(1+\left\lVert\div\dot{\mathbf{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})})\\
&\quad+C\left\lVert\div(\zeta\mathbf{u})\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\log(e+\left\lVert\nabla^{2}(\zeta\mathbf{u})\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega_{t})})+C\left\lVert\nabla(\zeta\mathbf{u})\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\\
&\leq C(1+\left\lVert\div\dot{\mathbf{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})})\\
&\quad+C(1+\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})})\log(e+\left\lVert\nabla\div(\zeta\mathbf{u})\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega_{t})})+C\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\\
&\leq C(1+\left\lVert\div\dot{\mathbf{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})})\\
&\quad+C\left(1+\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{\frac{p-2}{2p-2}}\left\lVert\nabla F\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega_{t})}^{\frac{p}{2p-2}}\right)\log(e+\left\lVert\nabla\div(\zeta\mathbf{u})\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega_{t})})+C\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\\
&\leq C(1+\left\lVert\div\dot{\mathbf{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})})+C(1+\left\lVert\div\dot{\mathbf{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{\frac{p}{2p-2}})\log(e+\left\lVert\nabla F\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega_{t})}+\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega_{t})})\\
&\leq C(1+\left\lVert\div\dot{\mathbf{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})})\log(e+\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega_{t})}),\end{split}

which we used

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\lVert\nabla\zeta\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}&\leq C\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\leq C\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+C\leq C\end{split}

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\lVert(1-\zeta)\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}&=\left\lVert(1-\zeta)\sqrt{(\partial_{r}u)^{2}+\left(\frac{u}{r}\right)^{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\leq C+C\left\lVert(1-\zeta)(\partial_{r}u)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\\
&\leq C+C\left\lVert(1-\zeta)\sqrt{\left(\partial_{r}u+\frac{u}{r}\right)^{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\leq C+C\left\lVert(1-\zeta)F\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\\
&\leq C+C\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{\frac{p-2}{2p-2}}\left\lVert\nabla F\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega_{t})}^{\frac{p}{2p-2}}\leq C+C\left\lVert\div\dot{\mathbf{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}.\end{split}

Thus, by Gronwall’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega_{t})}\leq C.

Finally, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}&\leq\left\lVert\nabla^{2}(\zeta\mathbf{u})\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+\left\lVert(1-\zeta)\nabla^{2}\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+\left\lVert\nabla\zeta\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+C\\
&\leq C\left\lVert\nabla\div(\zeta\mathbf{u})\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+C\left(1+\left\lVert(1-\zeta)\nabla F\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\right.\\
&\left.\quad+\left\lVert(1-\zeta)\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\right)\\
&\leq C+C\left\lVert\zeta\nabla\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+C\left\lVert\nabla(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\zeta)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\\
&\leq C,\end{split}

which we used the fact

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\left\lVert(1-\zeta)\nabla^{2}\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\\
&\leq C\left\lVert(1-\zeta)\partial_{rr}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+C\left\lVert(1-\zeta)\partial_{r}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+C\left\lVert(1-\zeta)u\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\\
&\leq C\left\lVert(1-\zeta)\nabla(\div\mathbf{u})\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+C\left\lVert(1-\zeta)\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+C\left\lVert(1-\zeta)\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+C\\
&\leq C\left(1+\left\lVert(1-\zeta)\nabla F\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+\left\lVert(1-\zeta)\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\right).\end{split}

∎

The remained procedure is standard: we can construct global approximate solutions to the stress-free boundary problem, and then show the convergence to a solution of the original system based on the above a priori estimates.

Here we give the key approximated scheme as follows: The approximation of Lagrangian system is constructed as

\begin{cases}\rho_{\tau}^{(k+1)}+\left(\rho^{(k+1)}\right)^{2}\left(r^{(k)}u^{(k)}\right)_{x}=0,\\
\frac{1}{r^{(k)}}u_{\tau}^{(k+1)}+\left((\rho^{(k)})^{\gamma}-(2\mu+\rho^{(k)})\rho^{(k)}\left(r^{(k)}u^{(k+1)}\right)_{x}\right)_{x}=0,\end{cases}

where
\frac{d}{d\tau}r^{(k)}(x,\tau)=u^{(k)}(x,\tau)
. Then we impose initial data

\left(\rho^{(k+1)},u^{(k+1)}\right)(x,0)=(\rho_{0}^{\epsilon},u_{0}^{\epsilon})=\left(\frac{\rho_{0}+\epsilon}{\int_{0}^{a_{0}}(\rho_{0}+\epsilon)rdr},\frac{\rho_{0}u_{0}}{\rho_{0}+\epsilon}\int_{0}^{a_{0}}(\rho_{0}+\epsilon)rdr\right),

and the boundary condition

u^{(k+1)}(0,\tau)=0,\left((\rho^{(k)}-(2\mu+\rho^{(k)})\rho^{(k)}\left(r^{(k)}u^{(k+1)}\right)_{x}\right)(1,\tau)=0.

Using the contraction mapping theorem and energy estimates, we can prove the global existence of the solution of above approximated system. Since a priori estimates are uniform with respect to
\epsilon
and
k
, we can show the convergence of the approximated solutions. It is direct to check the limit is exactly the unique global classic solution of the original system. The proof of Theorem
1.2
is finished.

The JLMS formula in a large code with approximate error correction
Xi Dong, Donald Marolf, and Pratik Rath

Abstract
Gauge/gravity duality is often described as a quantum error correcting code. However, as seen in the Jafferis-Lewkowycz-Maldacena-Suh (JLMS) formula, exact quantum error correction with complementary recovery (and thus entanglement wedge reconstruction) emerges only in the limit G → 0 G\to 0 . As a result, precise arguments controlling error terms have focused on what we call ‘small’ codes which, as G → 0 G\to 0 , describe only perturbative excitations near a given classical solution. Such settings are quite restrictive and, in particular, they prohibit discussion of any modular flow that would change the classical background. As a result, they forbid consideration of modular flows generated by semiclassical bulk states at order-one modular parameters. In contrast, we present a single ‘large’ code for the bulk theory that can accommodate such flows and, in particular, in the G → 0 G\to 0 limit includes superpositions of states associated with distinct classical backgrounds. This large code is assembled from small codes that each satisfy an approximate Faulkner-Lewkowycz-Maldacena formula. In this extended setting we clarify the meaning of the (approximate) JLMS relation between bulk and boundary modular Hamiltonians and quantify its validity in an appropriate class of states.

In contrast, standard derivations

We refer to such code subspaces as ‘small.’ As in many discussions of JLMS, we assume that we work either with an entire connected component of the CFT spacetime or in a context with a UV cutoff on the CFT so that, at finite bulk Newton’s constant
G

G\rightarrow 0

We resolve this tension below by constructing a ‘large’ code which contains states that can approach arbitrary classical solutions if one chooses to take the limit
G\rightarrow 0

G\rightarrow 0

We stress that there are several different sources of potential errors to be addressed. The most obvious of these arises from the derivation of the JLMS relation (
1

G^{0}

33

35

Furthermore, to be conservative we should also admit the possibility of further (perhaps non-perturbative) errors that might arise if, for example, perhaps due to subtle wormhole effects, AdS/CFT turns out not to be an exactly isometric code with the bulk inner product defined using the usual gravitational path integral. This approach also allows us to use an approximate low-energy version of the bulk path integral in which, for example, perturbative higher derivative corrections might be included only to some finite order.

In addition, as discussed in
41
, certain types of states can provide much more sensitivity to such errors than one might naively expect. We stress in Section
2
that, when combined with any bound on the accuracy of the FLM formula, this places a fundamental limit on our ability to derive a useful JLMS formula in completely general settings. Our goal is thus to describe a particular setting where sensitivity to the above errors is minimized. This setting will necessarily include restricting to an appropriate class of states satisfying a property which we call log-stability, and which requires that no eigenvalue of the bulk state is smaller than a given threshold value.

In deriving our error bounds, it will be convenient to separate the quantum information arguments from the AdS/CFT arguments. We will use this approach to sharpen the discussion of error terms even at the level of our small codes. In particular, our main results will be phrased as showing that, when the errors in certain approximate relations are subject to given bounds (generically denoted

2
AdS/CFT framework for small codes

This section provides a brief discussion of the small codes we envision within the framework of AdS/CFT. In addition, we take this opportunity to quickly review the derivation given in
17
of the JLMS relation (
1

G\rightarrow 0

We also briefly describe our strategy for ameliorating such complications. This will set the stage for a much more technical treatment of error bounds in later sections. In particular, the discussion here will motivate the manner in which various error parameters introduced in later sections are expected to depend parametrically on
G
when our results are used in AdS/CFT.
As noted above, we assume that we work either with an entire connected component of the CFT spacetime or in a context with a UV cutoff on the CFT so that we may treat the associated von Neumann algebras as being of type I and thus admitting well-defined density operators.

As usual, we take the bulk side of the AdS/CFT correspondence to be described by a low-energy effective gravitational theory, which we assume to be valid below some cutoff scale
\Lambda
. We will allow the bulk to be described by a low energy effective action (which may have been truncated to contain only a finite number of higher derivative terms) so that the bulk-to-boundary dictionary need not be exactly isometric.
While the traditional definitions of small codes given in

;
17
proceed by applying quantum perturbations to a given semiclassical reference state, we will instead find it convenient to begin with the construction of small codes outlined in
24
. The differences between these two procedures will be explained below.

For simplicity, let us now assume that the Hilbert space of the bulk low-energy effective theory of gravity is isometrically embedded in the boundary Hilbert space. We can then identify our area-window Hilbert space (

If we can safely neglect the second (and higher) order terms in (

Since this holds for any such
\delta\rho

where

denotes the projection from the full boundary Hilbert space to the specified area-window Hilbert space (

As described in

arise from the fact that it is useful to vary (

We will eventually wish to extend this result to a large code formed by taking the union of many area-window Hilbert spaces. As a result, the large code will include superpositions involving different area-windows that describe well-defined classical backgrounds in the limit
G\rightarrow 0
and, in fact, it will include states that approach many distinct classical backgrounds.
However, before constructing this large code, it remains to address the conditions under which one can in fact neglect terms in (
8
) and (
9
) that are higher than linear order in
\delta\rho
.
The danger is that such higher order terms contain inverse powers of
\rho_{r}
or
\rho_{R}
(collectively called
\rho

\log\rho
in

\rho
is not full rank and when
\delta\rho
has support on the kernel of
\rho

, we present a corresponding example where JLMS fails in this way in a simple random tensor network setting. Since
\rho

Such terms were discussed in e.g.

Furthermore, even when the higher order terms in
\delta\rho
are not strictly infinite, since they scale with inverse powers of
\rho

{\mathcal{O}}(\delta\rho^{2})
corrections to the entropy can be large in comparison with the desired

is taken to be

small. The danger is then that, for such small

7

o(G^{0})
error term rather than by the desired contribution from the bulk modular Hamiltonian. In this case, we may again find large violations of (
12

derivative
of the entropy, results like the Fannes-Audenaert inequality that bound changes in the entropy itself do not appear to provide useful control.

The potential for large corrections becomes particularly acute if we allow our code Hilbert space to contain two semiclassical states that are peaked around distinct classical backgrounds. In such cases, the overlap of two such states is typically exponentially small in
G
. As a result, such a construction could easily lead to eigenvalues of some
\rho_{r}
that are again exponentially small, so that the error terms in (
8

9

\delta\rho
to be exponentially small. But when
\delta\rho
is this small, variations in the FLM formula (
7

o(G^{0})
error term. In this case, the above argument would allow large violations of the JLMS relation (
12

However, the above discussion also shows that the problem here can be ameliorated by simply restricting the derivation of (
12

\rho^{-1}
have bounded effect). This is the essence of the log-stability criterion that we will introduce in Section
3
below, and which will be applied on small code Hilbert spaces. Since, in a Hilbert space of dimension

{\mathcal{O}}(G^{0})
will clearly play an important role in our ability to ensure that all states in a small code satisfy this criterion.

What remains is then to keep track of the errors in detail, taking into account that we wish to work at small but finite
G
, and to then assemble the above JLMS relations on our small code Hilbert spaces into a corresponding relation on the appropriate large code. As noted in the introduction, if there were no errors, and if the small codes satisfy appropriate orthogonality conditions (to be explained in the next paragraph), then one could simply take a direct sum of the small-code JLMS relations to derive the desired result on the large code defined by the corresponding direct sum. Treating corrections perturbatively when there are small eigenvalues will then require additional ‘alignment’ or ‘smoothness’ assumptions as will be explained in Section
4
.

We now discuss the above-mentioned orthogonality conditions required for this direct sum construction. There are in fact two classes of such conditions:
First, to eliminate cross-terms in the density operators
\rho_{R},\rho_{r}
, states in the tensor factors associated with
{\overline{R}},{\overline{r}}
for one small code must be orthogonal to states in the corresponding factor for other small codes. Second, to write the result in a block diagonal form,
states in the tensor factors associated with
R,r
for one small code must be orthogonal to states in the corresponding factor for other small codes. In Section
4
below we will thus make a corresponding ‘approximate subsystem orthogonality’ assumption that encompasses both conditions. As a result, if two small codes labeled by

consists of states with distinct classical backgrounds, our formalism will generally be restricted to cases where the backgrounds on each of the two entanglement wedges

are

\left\|V_{\alpha}^{{\dagger}}K_{\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}}V_{\alpha}-V_{\alpha}^{{\dagger}}V_{\alpha}\left(\frac{A^{\alpha}}{4G}+K_{\rho^{\alpha}_{r}}\right)\right\|_{\infty}\lesssim\epsilon_{\textit{pJLMS}},

(28)

with
\epsilon_{\textit{pJLMS}}

where we have used the convention that

{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}},{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}

Proof.

Using (
14

on
{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}

\left|\operatorname{tr}\left(\sigma^{\alpha}\left[V_{\alpha}^{\dagger}K_{\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}}V_{\alpha}-V_{\alpha}^{{\dagger}}V_{\alpha}\left(\frac{A^{\alpha}}{4G}+K_{\rho^{\alpha}_{r}}\right)\right]\right)\right|\lesssim\epsilon_{\textit{pJLMS}}.

To prove this, consider the variation of the approximate FLM formula (
17
) under
\delta\rho^{\alpha}=\epsilon(\sigma^{\alpha}-\rho^{\alpha})
with some small
\epsilon
to be determined shortly.
On the bulk side, we use Lemma
1

S(\rho^{\alpha}_{r}+\delta\rho^{\alpha}_{r})-S(\rho^{\alpha}_{r})=\operatorname{tr}_{r}(\delta\rho^{\alpha}_{r}K_{\rho^{\alpha}_{r}})+{\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}}\right).

(31)

On the boundary side, we use Lemma
2

(32)

where
\delta\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}

\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}

\delta\rho^{\alpha}
. In deriving (

\delta\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}={\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon)\left(\tilde{\sigma}^{\alpha}_{R}-\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}\right).

(33)

To see this, we explicitly calculate the first-order variation
\delta\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}

\delta\rho^{\alpha}

\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}=W_{\alpha}(\rho^{\alpha})

\delta\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}=\frac{V_{\alpha}\delta\rho^{\alpha}V_{\alpha}^{\dagger}}{\operatorname{Tr}(V_{\alpha}\rho^{\alpha}V_{\alpha}^{\dagger})}-\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(V_{\alpha}\delta\rho^{\alpha}V_{\alpha}^{\dagger})}{\operatorname{Tr}(V_{\alpha}\rho^{\alpha}V_{\alpha}^{\dagger})}.

(34)

Using
\delta\rho^{\alpha}=\epsilon(\sigma^{\alpha}-\rho^{\alpha})

\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}

(35)

where we have used

{\hat{\sigma}}^{\alpha}

Partially tracing over

\Big|\left[S(\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}+\delta\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R})-S(\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R})\right]-\left[S(\rho^{\alpha}_{r}+\delta\rho^{\alpha}_{r})-S(\rho^{\alpha}_{r})\right]\Big|\leq 2\epsilon_{\textit{FLM}}.

\displaystyle\left|\operatorname{Tr}_{R}\left(\sqrt{K_{\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}}}\delta\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}\sqrt{K_{\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}}}\right)-\operatorname{tr}_{r}(\delta\rho^{\alpha}_{r}K_{\rho^{\alpha}_{r}})\right|

\displaystyle\epsilon\left|\operatorname{Tr}_{R}\left[\sqrt{K_{\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}}}\left(\tilde{\sigma}^{\alpha}_{R}-\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}\right)\sqrt{K_{\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}}}\right]\frac{\operatorname{Tr}\hat{\sigma}^{\alpha}}{\operatorname{Tr}\hat{\rho}^{\alpha}}-\operatorname{tr}_{r}\left[\left(\sigma^{\alpha}_{r}-\rho^{\alpha}_{r}\right)K_{\rho^{\alpha}_{r}}\right]\right|

for any state

\left|\operatorname{Tr}_{R}\left[\sqrt{K_{\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}}}\left(\tilde{\sigma}^{\alpha}_{R}-\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}\right)\sqrt{K_{\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}}}\right]-\operatorname{tr}_{r}\left[\left(\sigma^{\alpha}_{r}-\rho^{\alpha}_{r}\right)K_{\rho^{\alpha}_{r}}\right]\right|\lesssim\frac{\epsilon_{\textit{FLM}}}{\epsilon}+\frac{\epsilon}{\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}}+\epsilon_{\textit{iso,small}}|\log\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}|.

\left|\operatorname{Tr}_{R}\left(\sqrt{K_{\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}}}\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}\sqrt{K_{\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}}}\right)-\frac{A^{\alpha}}{4G}-\operatorname{tr}_{r}\left(\rho^{\alpha}_{r}K_{\rho^{\alpha}_{r}}\right)\right|\leq\epsilon_{\textit{FLM}},

we combine this with (

\left|\operatorname{Tr}_{R}\left(\sqrt{K_{\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}}}\tilde{\sigma}^{\alpha}_{R}\sqrt{K_{\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}}}\right)-\frac{A^{\alpha}}{4G}-\operatorname{tr}_{r}\left(\sigma^{\alpha}_{r}K_{\rho^{\alpha}_{r}}\right)\right|\lesssim\frac{\epsilon_{\textit{FLM}}}{\epsilon}+\frac{\epsilon}{\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}}+\epsilon_{\textit{iso,small}}|\log\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}|.

We now write the left-hand side of (
47
) as

\displaystyle\left|\operatorname{Tr}_{R}\left(\sqrt{K_{\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}}}\tilde{\sigma}^{\alpha}_{R}\sqrt{K_{\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}}}\right)-\frac{A^{\alpha}}{4G}-\operatorname{tr}_{r}\left(\sigma^{\alpha}_{r}K_{\rho^{\alpha}_{r}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\operatorname{Tr}\hat{\sigma}^{\alpha}}\left|\operatorname{Tr}\left(\sqrt{K_{\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}}}\hat{\sigma}^{\alpha}\sqrt{K_{\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}}}\right)-\frac{A^{\alpha}}{4G}\operatorname{Tr}\hat{\sigma}^{\alpha}-\operatorname{tr}_{r}\left(\sigma^{\alpha}_{r}K_{\rho^{\alpha}_{r}}\right)\operatorname{Tr}\hat{\sigma}^{\alpha}\right|

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\operatorname{Tr}\hat{\sigma}^{\alpha}}\left|\operatorname{tr}\left[\sigma^{\alpha}V_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\left(K_{\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}}-\frac{A^{\alpha}}{4G}\right)V_{\alpha}\right]-\operatorname{tr}\left(\sigma^{\alpha}K_{\rho^{\alpha}_{r}}\right)\right|+{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon_{\textit{iso,small}}|\log\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}|),

(50)

where on the last line we used (
15
), (

), and (

).
Note that, on the first line,

,

, while on the last line they represent operators on

(in which case they include implicit factors of

\left|\operatorname{tr}\left[\sigma^{\alpha}V_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\left(K_{\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}}-\frac{A^{\alpha}}{4G}\right)V_{\alpha}\right]-\operatorname{tr}\left(\sigma^{\alpha}K_{\rho^{\alpha}_{r}}\right)\right|\lesssim\frac{\epsilon_{\textit{FLM}}}{\epsilon}+\frac{\epsilon}{\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}}+\epsilon_{\textit{iso,small}}|\log\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}|.

(51)

We now insert the desired
V^{\dagger}_{\alpha}V_{\alpha}
in the

\displaystyle\left|\operatorname{tr}\left[\sigma^{\alpha}(V_{\alpha}^{\dagger}V_{\alpha}-1)K_{\rho^{\alpha}_{r}}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\sigma^{\alpha}(V_{\alpha}^{\dagger}V_{\alpha}-1)K_{\rho^{\alpha}_{r}}\right\|_{1}\leq\left\|\sigma^{\alpha}(V_{\alpha}^{\dagger}V_{\alpha}-1)\right\|_{1}\left\|K_{\rho^{\alpha}_{r}}\right\|_{\infty}

\left|\operatorname{tr}\left[\sigma^{\alpha}V_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\left(K_{\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}}-\frac{A^{\alpha}}{4G}\right)V_{\alpha}\right]-\operatorname{tr}\left[\sigma^{\alpha}V_{\alpha}^{\dagger}V_{\alpha}K_{\rho^{\alpha}_{r}}\right]\right|\lesssim\frac{\epsilon_{\textit{FLM}}}{\epsilon}+\frac{\epsilon}{\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}}+\epsilon_{\textit{iso,small}}|\log\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}|,

(54)

or equivalently

\left|\operatorname{tr}\left(\sigma^{\alpha}\left[V_{\alpha}^{\dagger}K_{\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}}V_{\alpha}-V_{\alpha}^{{\dagger}}V_{\alpha}\left(\frac{A^{\alpha}}{4G}+K_{\rho^{\alpha}_{r}}\right)\right]\right)\right|\lesssim\frac{\epsilon_{\textit{FLM}}}{\epsilon}+\frac{\epsilon}{\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}}+\epsilon_{\textit{iso,small}}|\log\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}|.

(55)

We are free to choose
\epsilon
, so to optimize the bound we take

, which yields

\left|\operatorname{tr}\left(\sigma^{\alpha}\left[V_{\alpha}^{\dagger}K_{\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}}V_{\alpha}-V_{\alpha}^{{\dagger}}V_{\alpha}\left(\frac{A^{\alpha}}{4G}+K_{\rho^{\alpha}_{r}}\right)\right]\right)\right|\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon_{\textit{FLM}}}{\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}}}+\epsilon_{\textit{iso,small}}|\log\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}|=\epsilon_{\textit{pJLMS}}.

(56)

This is the promised inequality (
30
), thus proving the approximate JLMS formula (
28
).
∎

In the approximate JLMS formula (
28
), it is important that the
A^{\alpha}/4G
term appear with a factor of
V_{\alpha}^{\dagger}V_{\alpha}
. However, it is not in fact necessary for the
K_{\rho^{\alpha}_{r}}
term to come with this factor. To see this, note that we could have skipped the step of using (
53
) to rewrite (
51
) as (
54
) in the proof, which would prove an alternative – but equally correct – version of the approximate JLMS formula (
28
):

\left\|V_{\alpha}^{{\dagger}}\left(K_{\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}}-\frac{A^{\alpha}}{4G}\right)V_{\alpha}-K_{\rho^{\alpha}_{r}}\right\|_{\infty}\lesssim\epsilon_{\textit{pJLMS}}.

(57)

The standard holographic derivation
29
of the approximate FLM relation (
17
) gives an error that vanishes as
G\rightarrow 0
, though it says little about the rate at which this occurs. Thus we should take
\epsilon_{\textit{FLM}}=o(G^{0})
, using the so-called little
o
notation. We may then usefully apply Theorem
1
,
finding that the JLMS formula holds up to parametrically small errors of order
\sqrt{\epsilon_{\textit{FLM}}/\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}}+\epsilon_{\textit{iso,small}}|\log\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}|\ll 1
,
as long as we restrict to log-stable states with
\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}\gg\epsilon_{\textit{FLM}}
– e.g. for
\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}
an
o(G^{0})
value much greater than
\epsilon_{\textit{FLM}}
– and as long as
\epsilon_{\textit{iso,small}}
is much smaller than
1/|\log\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}|
(a fairly weak condition).

One might hope that the above analysis can be improved in a manner that replaces use of the approximate FLM formula with an appropriate formula in term of a quantum extremal surface (QES). By doing so, one might expect to reduce the error
\epsilon_{\textit{FLM}}
from
o(G^{0})
to
e^{-{\mathcal{O}}(1/G)}
. If this can be done, the lower bound
\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}
on the eigenvalues of
\rho^{\alpha}_{r}
could then be taken to be any number much larger than
e^{-{\mathcal{O}}(1/G)}
. However, we will not pursue this idea here.

3.2
Exponentiated JLMS

We now proceed to our approximate version of the exponentiated JLMS relations. As noted earlier, for the exponentiated relation to hold with general exponents
is
(for real
s
), the entanglement spectrum of the code must be flat. When this holds exactly, it is equivalent to flatness of the Rényi entropies for certain code parts of the state (see e.g. discussion in
16
), and thus to Rényi versions of the FLM formula for general complex replica numbers
n
. We will thus find it convenient to encode approximate flatness of the code entanglement spectrum by simply assuming our code to satisfy approximate versions of Rényi FLM. In particular, this will allow us to derive our approximate exponentiated JLMS relation with exponent
is
(with
s
real) using approximate Rényi FLM for only the particular replica number
n=1+is
.

To this end, we make the following definition.

Definition 5

(Approximate Rényi FLM)
.

For a fixed, generally complex
n
, we say that the approximate Rényi FLM formula holds in the
\alpha
-sector on
R
if an arbitrary state
\rho^{\alpha}
on
{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}
satisfies

\left|S_{n}(\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R})-\left(\frac{A^{\alpha}}{4G}+S_{n}(\rho^{\alpha}_{r})\right)\right|\leq\xi(n)\epsilon_{\textit{FLM}},

(58)

where
A^{\alpha}
is again a c-number, and
S_{n}(\rho):=\frac{1}{1-n}\log\operatorname{Tr}\rho^{n}
is the Rényi entropy of order
n
. Here
\xi(n)
is chosen to satisfy
\xi(1)=1
, so that for
n=1
(
58
) reduces to (
17
).
For later convenience, we define
\xi_{s}:=\xi(1+is)
. Note that, for complex
n
, we generally need to choose a branch cut when defining the
\log
function in
S_{n}
, and we say that the approximate Rényi FLM formula holds if (
58
) can be made true with any choice of branch cuts (which may be different for
S_{n}(\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R})
and
S_{n}(\rho^{\alpha}_{r})
and, when we consider multiple values of
n
, may be chosen to depend on
n
).

Before deriving our exponentiated JLMS, we first establish the following useful lemma.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\rho
,
\sigma
be states in some Hilbert space, not necessarily of finite dimension. Suppose

\operatorname{Tr}\left(\sigma\rho^{-1}\sigma\right)\leq 1/\epsilon_{\textit{tail}},

(59)

where
\operatorname{Tr}
denotes the Hilbert space trace.
Then for any
\epsilon\in[0,1)
and any real
s
, we have

\left|\frac{1}{1+is}\left[\operatorname{Tr}\left(\rho_{\epsilon}^{1+is}\right)-\operatorname{Tr}\left(\rho^{1+is}\right)\right]-\epsilon\operatorname{Tr}\left[\rho^{is}(\sigma-\rho)\right]\right|\leq\frac{|s|\epsilon^{2}}{2(1-\epsilon)\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}},

(60)

where
\rho_{\epsilon}:=\rho+\epsilon(\sigma-\rho)
.

This lemma is the Rényi entropy analogue of Lemma
2
. Since its proof is somewhat technical, we delegate the proof to Appendix
E
.

Our main result for exponentiated JLMS on a small code subspace is then given by the theorem below. The proof is structurally similar to the argument for projected JLMS given in Section
3.1
and is again rather technical. Many readers may thus wish to skip ahead to Section
4
(or even to Section
5
) on a first reading.

2
Approximate exponentiated JLMS in a small code.

Let
s
be a real number. Suppose that the approximate Rényi FLM formula (
58
) for
n=1+is
holds in the
\alpha
-sector on
R
, and that
V_{\alpha}
is an approximate isometry satisfying (
18
).
Then for any log-stable state
\rho^{\alpha}
(as defined in Definition
4
), the exponentiated JLMS formula holds approximately in the sense that we have

\left\|V_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\,e^{-isK_{\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}}}\,V_{\alpha}-e^{-is\left(\frac{A^{\alpha}}{4G}+K_{\rho^{\alpha}_{r}}\right)}\right\|_{\infty}\lesssim\epsilon_{\textit{eJLMS}}^{(s)},

(61)

with
\epsilon_{\textit{eJLMS}}^{(s)}
defined by

\epsilon_{\textit{eJLMS}}^{(s)}:=\sqrt{\xi_{s}\,|s|\frac{\epsilon_{\textit{FLM}}}{\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}}}+\xi_{s}\,|s|\epsilon_{\textit{FLM}}+\epsilon_{\textit{iso,small}}.

(62)

Here
\lesssim
means that we again drop
{\mathcal{O}}(1)
coefficients. Note, however, that we do not require the parameter
s
to be
{\mathcal{O}}(1)
. Instead, we include any terms that might become relevant if
s
is taken to be large in a limit where other parameters become small. In particular, we have kept the
|s|\epsilon_{\textit{FLM}}
term explicit even though
\epsilon_{\textit{FLM}}\ll\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon_{\textit{FLM}}}{\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}}}
.

Proof.

Using (
14
), we only need to show that for any state
\sigma^{\alpha}
on
{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}
, we have

\left|\operatorname{tr}\left(\sigma^{\alpha}\left[V_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\,e^{-isK_{\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}}}\,V_{\alpha}-e^{-is\left(\frac{A^{\alpha}}{4G}+K_{\rho^{\alpha}_{r}}\right)}\right]\right)\right|\lesssim\epsilon_{\textit{eJLMS}}^{(s)}.

(63)

This relation can be rewritten in the form

\left|\operatorname{tr}\left(\sigma^{\alpha}\left[V_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\,\left(\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}\right)^{is}\,V_{\alpha}-e^{-is\frac{A^{\alpha}}{4G}}\left(\rho^{\alpha}_{r}\right)^{is}\right]\right)\right|\lesssim\epsilon_{\textit{eJLMS}}^{(s)}.

(64)

To prove that this is the case, consider the variation of the approximate Rényi FLM formula (
58
) with
n=1+is
, taking
\delta\rho^{\alpha}=\epsilon(\sigma^{\alpha}-\rho^{\alpha})
with a small
\epsilon
to be determined shortly.
We first apply Lemma
3
(and the log-stability of
\rho^{a}
) to both the bulk and the boundary, finding

\left|\frac{1}{1+is}\left[e^{-isS_{1+is}(\rho^{\alpha}_{r}+\delta\rho^{\alpha}_{r})}-e^{-isS_{1+is}(\rho^{\alpha}_{r})}\right]-\operatorname{tr}_{r}\left[(\rho^{\alpha}_{r})^{is}\delta\rho^{\alpha}_{r}\right]\right|\lesssim\frac{|s|\epsilon^{2}}{\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}}

(65)

and

\left|\frac{1}{1+is}\left[e^{-isS_{1+is}(\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}+\delta\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R})}-e^{-isS_{1+is}(\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R})}\right]-\operatorname{Tr}_{R}\left[(\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R})^{is}\delta\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}\right]\right|\lesssim\frac{|s|\epsilon^{2}}{\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}}.

(66)

To proceed, we rewrite the approximate Rényi FLM formula (
58
) with
n=1+is
in the following exponentiated form:

\left|\frac{1}{1+is}\left[e^{-isS_{1+is}(\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R})}-e^{-is\left(\frac{A^{\alpha}}{4G}+S_{1+is}(\rho^{\alpha}_{r})\right)}\right]\right|\leq\frac{|s|}{\sqrt{s^{2}+1}}\,\xi_{s}\epsilon_{\textit{FLM}}.

(67)

To show this, we use

\Big|e^{-isS_{1+is}(\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R})}\Big|=\Big|\operatorname{Tr}_{R}\left[(\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R})^{1+is}\right]\Big|\leq\operatorname{Tr}_{R}\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}=1

(68)

and similarly
\big|e^{-is\big(\frac{A^{\alpha}}{4G}+S_{1+is}(\rho^{\alpha}_{r})\big)}\big|\leq 1
, as well as the fact that any complex numbers
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
satisfying
|c_{1}|,|c_{2}|\leq 1
must obey

\left|c_{1}-c_{2}\right|\leq\left|\log c_{1}-\log c_{2}\right|.

(69)

This holds for any choice of branches for the
\log
functions (which may be different for
\log c_{1}
and
\log c_{2}
). This inequality (
69
) follows directly from the fact that for any
a_{1},a_{2}\leq 0
and any real numbers
b_{1},b_{2}
satisfy

\displaystyle\big|e^{a_{1}+ib_{1}}-e^{a_{2}+ib_{2}}\big|

\displaystyle=\sqrt{e^{2a_{1}}+e^{2a_{2}}-2e^{a_{1}+a_{2}}\cos(b_1-b_2)}

(70)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{e^{2a_{1}}+e^{2a_{2}}-2e^{a_{1}+a_{2}}\left[1-(b_{1}-b_{2})^{2}/2\right]}

(71)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\left(e^{a_{1}}-e^{a_{2}}\right)^{2}+e^{a_{1}+a_{2}}(b_{1}-b_{2})^{2}}

(72)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{(a_{1}-a_{2})^{2}+(b_{1}-b_{2})^{2}}

(73)

\displaystyle=\big|(a_{1}+ib_{1})-(a_{2}+ib_{2})\big|,

(74)

where in passing to the second line we used
\cos x\geq 1-x^{2}/2
for any real
x
, and in passing to the fourth line we used
\big|1-e^{-|a_{1}-a_{2}|}\big|\leq|a_{1}-a_{2}|
and
a_{1},a_{2}\leq 0
.

The inequality (
67
) holds for any
\rho^{\alpha}
on
{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}
, so it also holds for
\rho^{\alpha}+\delta\rho^{\alpha}
. Using it in both ways in (
66
), we thus find

\left|\frac{1}{1+is}\left[e^{-is\left(\frac{A^{\alpha}}{4G}+S_{1+is}(\rho^{\alpha}_{r}+\delta\rho^{\alpha}_{r})\right)}-e^{-is\left(\frac{A^{\alpha}}{4G}+S_{1+is}(\rho^{\alpha}_{r})\right)}\right]-\operatorname{Tr}_{R}\left[(\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R})^{is}\delta\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{R}\right]\right|\\
\lesssim\frac{|s|}{\sqrt{s^{2}+1}}\,\xi_{s}\epsilon_{\textit{FLM}}+\frac{|s|\epsilon^{2}}{\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}}.

(75)

Comparing this inequality with (

4
A large code and its JLMS relations with error bounds

We are now ready to assemble our ‘large’ code from a set of small codes. Indeed, for the moment we simply take an arbitrary collection of orthogonal small code Hilbert spaces
{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}

{\mathcal{H}}_{\textit{large}}=\bigoplus_{\alpha}{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}=\bigoplus_{\alpha}\left({\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}_{r}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}_{{\overline{r}}}\right).

to match those defined in the bulk effective theory, the Hilbert space factors

orthogonal with respect to the inner product of the bulk effective theory (and similarly for
{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}_{\overline{r}}

15

15

If such orthogonality is not exact but only approximate, we can use the results of this section to bound the differences in the modular Hamiltonian and modular flows between
{\mathcal{H}}_{\textit{large}}
and the bulk effective theory. To do so, we need only interpret the relation between
{\mathcal{H}}_{\textit{large}}
and the bulk effective field theory as defining a code and to take

In many cases, this will be straightforward to arrange by e.g. associating each

16

16

A tensor network demonstrating this is in Appendix

As noted in both the introduction and Section

As in our small codes, we will in fact derive two notions of approximate JLMS relations. The first is again a generalization of the projected JLMS formula (

5
Discussion

The goal of our work was to construct a ‘large’ code in which both projected and
exponentiated JLMS relations hold to good accuracy despite the fact that certain entanglement eigenvalues of the relevant states may be exponentially small. We found this to be possible even in contexts in which
two-sided recovery is only approximate, and even where the recovery errors are only power-law small, so that our analysis can apply to AdS/CFT at finite values of the bulk Newton’s constant
G
. However, in order to tame the sort of potentially-large effects emphasized in
41
, we found it important to restrict the bulk side of our code to states which respect cutoffs that remain finite in AdS units even in the limit
G\rightarrow 0
. Our technical arguments were phrased in terms of abstract and somewhat general codes for which AdS/CFT provides examples with additional special properties.

The input to our construction was an appropriate family of ‘small’ codes that map a so-called ‘bulk’ Hilbert space into a so-called ‘boundary’ Hilbert space using an approximate isometry. Each small code was required to satisfy both an approximate FLM relation and approximate Rényi FLM relations. As discussed in Section
2
, in AdS/CFT such codes are naturally constructed by projecting semiclassical states (with perturbative corrections) onto appropriate windows of HRT-area and taking
G
to be small.

We then showed in Section
3
that the desired (projected and exponentiated) approximate JLMS relations hold on these small codes for so-called ‘log-stable’ bulk density operators, by which we mean density operators whose eigenvalues are not too small, and for which an analogous condition holds in the boundary theory. We also argued in Appendix
D
that in AdS/CFT contexts the condition on the boundary density operator in fact follows from the bulk condition. A positive lower bound on the bulk eigenvalues appears to be necessary to avoid the potential issues emphasized in
41
. This condition necessarily limits the dimension of the bulk Hilbert space for each small code and, in AdS/CFT contexts, we took this dimension to approach a constant in the limit
G\rightarrow 0
(i.e., to be of order
G^{0})
. We will comment further on this restriction below.

However, assuming a notion of subsystem orthogonality between the various small codes, Section
4
was then able to sew together a large collection of such small codes into a ‘large’ code in which both projected and exponentiated JLMS again hold to good accuracy for appropriate states. We emphasize that, in principle, this collection can be either countably infinite or of arbitrary finite size. Much smaller entanglement eigenvalues are allowed in the large code, so long as the small parameter is associated with having a small probability for all states within a given small code. However, our results in Sections
4.2
and
4.3
required that the density operator
\sigma
on which the modular flow acts be sufficiently ‘aligned’ with the state
\rho
used to define the modular flow, in the sense that if
\rho
assigns a small probability to a given small code, the probability assigned by
\sigma
is also small. To derive projected JLMS on this large code, we also needed some additional conditions that enhance our notion of log-stability, though these are not needed for exponentiated JLMS.

Alternatively, as shown in Sections
4.4
and
4.5
, one may remove the restriction that
\sigma
be appropriately aligned with
\rho
when the probability
p_{\alpha}
assigned by
\rho
to a small code
\alpha
is a sufficiently ‘smooth’ function of
\alpha
.
Heuristically, our smoothness requirement (Definition
12
in Section
4.4
) concerns smoothness with respect to a notion of distance between small codes that is chosen to reflect the decay of certain subdominant matrix elements. However, the technical definition was formulated without introducing any explicit such distance.

Now, as emphasized in Section
2
, our Rényi FLM assumption for our small codes requires that they have approximately-flat entanglement spectra. In AdS/CFT, such codes are naturally constructed from fixed-area states (and their generalizations in the presence of higher-derivative interactions). In contrast, states in our large code will generally be far from having a flat entanglement spectrum in the sense that the various small codes it contains can have dramatically-different entanglement eigenvalues on the boundary. As discussed in
4
;
16
;
24
, this must be the case in any code large enough to describe standard semiclassical states in AdS/CFT (for which the Rényi entropies of distinct replica numbers
n
require distinct classical backgrounds). It also appears to play a key role in recent constructions of type II
∞
von Neumann algebras in perturbative gravity
58
;
11
. In particular, any type II
∞
von Neumann algebra is the tensor product of a type II
1
algebra with a type I
∞
algebra. This shows that the Hilbert spaces constructed in
58
;
11
can be written as a direct sum of Hilbert spaces
{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}
on which the stated algebra is truncated to a type II
1
algebra. Such
{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}
thus contain states whose entropy is maximal within the given
{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}
, with more general states having entropy that is less by some
finite
amount. Since the algebra has infinite dimension, this would be impossible if every degree of freedom were associated with less-than-maximal entanglement. In this sense, one may say that the entanglement of generic states in
{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}
is ‘almost maximal,’ and thus that each
{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}
is analogous to one of our small codes (where the bulk part of the state can have arbitrary entanglement but the entropy is typically dominated by the underlying code which has a nearly flat entanglement spectrum).
20
20

20

We emphasize that this is only an analogy; these
{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}_{vN}
differ in detail from our small codes
{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}
, in part because our construction has a cutoff in the bulk.
The Hilbert space on which any type II
∞
algebra acts is then analogous to our large code. Indeed, it was argued in
11
that the construction of type II
∞
algebras in cosmology should generally be performed in a direct-sum of fixed-area Hilbert spaces much like what we considered here.
21
21

21

In the de Sitter case
9
, the resulting algebra describing perturbations of empty dS is type II
1
in part because there is no such fixed-area Hilbert space with area larger than the area of the empty dS horizon. (The discrepancy with the more general type II
1
claims of
39
raises questions about whether the assumptions of that work are generally satisfied.)

As noted above, when the FLM error parameter
\epsilon_{\textit{FLM}}
is non-zero, our results are useful only when the dimension of each small code Hilbert space is finite. In particular, in the AdS/CFT context we required the dimension to approach a constant in the limit
G\rightarrow 0.
This then required the use of various cutoffs. It is natural to ask if one can improve these results using von Neumann algebra techniques, and by perhaps emphasizing relative entropies between appropriately-aligned states rather than von Neumann entropies.

We leave this general question as an interesting direction for future investigation. Nevertheless, it is important to ask whether the results stated here in terms of codes with bulk cutoffs are in fact of use in studying general semiclassical bulk states (which may not necessarily respect the cutoffs imposed above). We will return to this question in the forthcoming paper
20
, where we will show that the answer is affirmative in AdS/CFT.

The spectrum in the West Coast model is approximately given by a cutoff thermal spectrum, although there is an uncontrolled region near the edge of the spectrum. Again, as we stated before, one can alternatively choose to consider only log-stable states where the effects of such non-perturbative corrections are small and the JLMS formula holds to good accuracy.

Appendix B
Brief review of Schatten
p
-norms

Recall that the Schatten
p
-norm of an operator
O
is defined to be

\|O\|_{p}\equiv\left(\operatorname{Tr}|O|^{p}\right)^{1/p},\quad|O|\equiv\sqrt{O^{\dagger}O}.

(177)

It satisfies the triangle inequality:

\|O+O^{\prime}\|_{p}\leq\|O\|_{p}+\|O^{\prime}\|_{p},\quad\text{for }1\leq p\leq\infty,

(178)

monotonicity:

\|O\|_{1}\geq\|O\|_{p}\geq\|O\|_{p^{\prime}}\geq\|O\|_{\infty},\quad\text{for }1\leq p\leq p^{\prime}\leq\infty,

(179)

and Hölder’s inequality:

\left\|OO^{\prime}\right\|_{1}\leq\|O\|_{p}\|O^{\prime}\|_{q},\quad\text{for }p,q\in[1,\infty]\text{ such that }\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1.

(180)

In particular, choosing
p=1
and
q=\infty
we find

\|OO^{\prime}\|_{1}\leq\|O\|_{1}\|O^{\prime}\|_{\infty}.

(181)

This leads to a useful inequality

\left|\operatorname{Tr}(OO^{\prime})\right|\leq\operatorname{Tr}|OO^{\prime}|=\|OO^{\prime}\|_{1}\leq\|O\|_{1}\|O^{\prime}\|_{\infty},

(182)

where the first step follows from a basic inequality
\left|\operatorname{Tr}O\right|\leq\operatorname{Tr}\left|O\right|
for any
O
, and the last step follows from Hölder’s inequality (
181
).

We work mostly with the operator norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
and trace norm
\|\cdot\|_{1}
in the main text. For a bounded
Hermitian

O
, one may alternatively find the operator norm using

\|O\|_{\infty}=\sup\big\{\left|\operatorname{Tr}(\rho O)\right|:\rho\text{ is a normalized density operator}\big\}.

(183)

To see this, notice that for any bounded (but not necessarily Hermitian)
O
we have

\left|\operatorname{Tr}(\rho O)\right|\leq\|\rho\|_{1}\|O\|_{\infty}=\|O\|_{\infty}.

(184)

Moreover, when
O
is Hermitian, these inequalities can be saturated by choosing
\rho
to be the projection operator onto an eigenstate of
O
with the largest eigenvalue (or, if the largest does not exist, with an eigenvalue as close to the supremum of eigenvalues).

If we do not already know whether
O
is bounded, we may use the following lemma that generalizes (
183
) and (
14
).

Lemma 7
.

Suppose that
O:\mathcal{D}(O)\subset{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
is a possibly unbounded linear operator whose domain
\mathcal{D}(O)
is a dense subspace of a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
. Further suppose that for any normalized density operator
\rho
on
{\mathcal{H}}
,
\sqrt{\rho}\,O\sqrt{\rho}
is densely defined, bounded on its domain, and in fact trace-class. Then we have
\mathcal{D}(O)={\mathcal{H}}
and

\|O\|_{\infty}\leq 2\sup\big\{\left|\operatorname{Tr}(\sqrt{\rho}\,O\sqrt{\rho})\right|:\rho\text{ is a normalized density operator}\big\}.

(185)

If
O
is self-adjoint, then we also have

\|O\|_{\infty}=\sup\big\{\left|\operatorname{Tr}(\sqrt{\rho}\,O\sqrt{\rho})\right|:\rho\text{ is a normalized density operator}\big\}.

(186)

Proof.

First, we show
\mathcal{D}(O)={\mathcal{H}}
. Suppose that
\mathcal{D}(O)\neq{\mathcal{H}}
; there must be a normalized state
|\psi\rangle\in{\mathcal{H}}
not in
\mathcal{D}(O)
. Let
\rho=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
. Then the domain of
\sqrt{\rho}\,O\sqrt{\rho}
cannot contain any
|\phi\rangle\in{\mathcal{H}}
with
\langle\psi|\phi\rangle\neq 0
. Such a domain cannot be dense in
{\mathcal{H}}
, contradicting the assumption of the theorem. Therefore, we have
\mathcal{D}(O)={\mathcal{H}}
, i.e.,
O
is defined everywhere on
{\mathcal{H}}
.

We now show (
186
). When
O
is self-adjoint, since it is defined everywhere on
{\mathcal{H}}
, it must be bounded (as guaranteed by the Hellinger-Toeplitz theorem). Then for any
\rho
,
\rho O
is trace-class and
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho O)=\operatorname{Tr}(\sqrt{\rho}\,O\sqrt{\rho})
. Our desired (
186
) then follows from (
183
).

Finally, we show (
185
). The statement holds vacuously if the supremum is infinity. If the supremum is finite, then
\{\langle\psi|O|\psi\rangle:|\psi\rangle\in{\mathcal{H}}\text{ is normalized}\}
is bounded. We then find that
O
defines a bounded sesquilinear form on
{\mathcal{H}}
(by using the polarization identity on the sesquilinear form). According to the corollary to the Riesz Lemma in
50

{\mathcal{H}}
which must then coincide with
O
. Thus
O
is bounded. Then for any
\rho
,
\rho O
is trace-class and
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho O)=\operatorname{Tr}(\sqrt{\rho}\,O\sqrt{\rho})
. Our desired (
185

14

Lemma 8

(Approximate isometry of the small code from FLM)
.

If the approximate FLM formula (
17

\alpha

{\overline{R}}
and
{\overline{r}}

must be an approximate isometry in the sense that, after an appropriate rescaling of

{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}

First, we rescale

V_{\alpha}^{{\dagger}}V_{\alpha}-\mathbb{1}_{\alpha}

as
\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}

\rho^{\alpha}_{r}

\rho^{\alpha}

\left|S(\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha})-\left(\frac{A^{\alpha}}{4G}+S(\rho^{\alpha})\right)\right|\leq\epsilon_{\textit{FLM}}.

{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}

\rho^{\alpha}_{1}
,
\rho^{\alpha}_{2}

(190)

(191)

which is designed to correspond to the maximally mixed boundary state on the span of

\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(|\tilde{\psi}^{\alpha}_{+}\rangle\langle|\tilde{\psi}^{\alpha}_{+}|+|\tilde{\psi}^{\alpha}_{-}\rangle\langle|\tilde{\psi}^{\alpha}_{-}|\right).

(192)

(193)

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon_{\textit{FLM}}.

(194)

\left[S(\rho^{\alpha}_{1})-S(\rho^{\alpha}_{2})\right]+\left[S(\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{2})-S(\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{1})\right]\leq 2\epsilon_{\textit{FLM}}.

\displaystyle S(\rho^{\alpha}_{1})-S(\rho^{\alpha}_{2})=S(\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{2})-S(\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\delta}{2}\log(1-\d)+\frac{1+\delta}{2}\log(1+\d)

Log-stability Condition 2 follows from the gravitational path integral

This appendix provides an argument that, in AdS/CFT, Condition 2 of our definition of log-stability (Definition
4

\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}
in Condition 1 is not exponentially small in the limit

\rho^{\alpha}
,
\sigma^{\alpha}
are defined by imposing boundary conditions on the gravitational path integral which can be interpreted as activating sources in the dual CFT, and that such path integrals may be evaluated semiclassically.

In particular, we assume that the bulk path integrals for

\operatorname{Tr}(\tilde{\sigma}^{\alpha})

g_{1}

g_{2}

r
(i.e.,
\operatorname{tr}_{r}(\rho_{r}^{\alpha})

24
24

24

Our language here is adapted to the familiar case where such fluctuations are perturbatively small. But the argument also applies in more general contexts so long as fluctuations of the HRT area

Since these are fixed-area saddles, they need not be smooth and, in particular, the two-dimensional space transverse to the HRT surface will generally have a conical defect. We parameterize the defect in saddle
g_{i}

We also assume that the (potentially complex) saddles

are invariant under a bulk version of the reflection-symmetry that preserves their boundary conditions, and that the fixed-area HRT surface lies on the reflection-symmetric hypersurface in each saddle. Lemma

Define

\frac{d}{dx}e^{A(x)}=\int_{0}^{1}d\theta\,e^{(1-\theta)A}\left(\frac{dA}{dx}\right)e^{\theta A},

\frac{d}{dx}\log A(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\left(A+t\right)^{-1}\left(\frac{dA}{dx}\right)\left(A+t\right)^{-1},

\frac{d}{dx}A(x)^{1+is}=(1+is)\int_{0}^{1}d\theta\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,A^{(1+is)(1-\theta)}\left(A+t\right)^{-1}\left(\frac{dA}{dx}\right)\left(A+t\right)^{-1}A^{(1+is)\theta},

\operatorname{Tr}\left[\rho_{x\epsilon}^{(1+is)(1-\theta)}\left(\rho_{x\epsilon}+t\right)^{-1}\delta\rho\left(\rho_{x\epsilon}+t\right)^{-1}\rho_{x\epsilon}^{(1+is)\theta}\right]=\operatorname{Tr}\left[\rho_{x\epsilon}^{1+is}\left(\rho_{x\epsilon}+t\right)^{-2}\delta\rho\right],

(213)

Moreover, the integral of the above trace over

and

is absolutely convergent, since the magnitude of the trace is bounded from above by
\operatorname{Tr}\left[\rho_{x\epsilon}(\rho_{x\epsilon}+t)^{-2}|\delta\rho|\right]
(whose integral over

\theta

). Therefore, the trace of (

\frac{d}{dx}\operatorname{Tr}\rho_{x\epsilon}^{1+is}=(1+is)\int_{0}^{1}d\theta\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,\operatorname{Tr}\left[\rho_{x\epsilon}^{1+is}\left(\rho_{x\epsilon}+t\right)^{-2}\delta\rho\right]=(1+is)\operatorname{Tr}\left[\rho_{x\epsilon}^{is}\delta\rho\right].

(214)

This useful formula allows us to rewrite
\Delta
as

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}dx\operatorname{Tr}\left[\left(\rho_{x\epsilon}^{is}-\rho^{is}\right)\delta\rho\right]=\int_{0}^{1}dx\int_{0}^{x}dy\frac{d}{dy}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\rho_{y\epsilon}^{is}\delta\rho\right)

(215)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}dx(1-x)\frac{d}{dx}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\rho_{x\epsilon}^{is}\delta\rho\right).

(216)

To bound this, we replace
1+is

is

\delta\rho
to obtain

\frac{d\rho_{x\epsilon}^{is}}{dx}\delta\rho=is\int_{0}^{1}d\theta\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,\rho_{x\epsilon}^{is(1-\theta)}\left(\rho_{x\epsilon}+t\right)^{-1}\delta\rho\,\rho_{x\epsilon}^{is\theta}\left(\rho_{x\epsilon}+t\right)^{-1}\delta\rho.

(217)

For any
t>0
, the integrand on the right hand side has a finite trace norm which satisfies the bound

\displaystyle\left\|\rho_{x\epsilon}^{is(1-\theta)}\left(\rho_{x\epsilon}+t\right)^{-1}\delta\rho\,\rho_{x\epsilon}^{is\theta}\left(\rho_{x\epsilon}+t\right)^{-1}\delta\rho\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\rho_{x\epsilon}^{is(1-\theta)}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\rho_{x\epsilon}^{is\theta}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\left(\rho_{x\epsilon}+t\right)^{-1}\delta\rho\right\|_{2}^{2}

(218)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}\left[\delta\rho\left(\rho_{x\epsilon}+t\right)^{-2}\delta\rho\right].

(219)

The integral of this bound over
t
and
\theta
is finite and therefore bounds the trace of (
217

\displaystyle\left|\frac{d}{dx}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\rho_{x\epsilon}^{is}\delta\rho\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq|s|\int_{0}^{1}d\theta\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,\operatorname{Tr}\left[\delta\rho\left(\rho_{x\epsilon}+t\right)^{-2}\delta\rho\right]=|s|\operatorname{Tr}\left[\delta\rho\,\rho_{x\epsilon}^{-1}\delta\rho\right]

(220)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|s|}{1-x\epsilon}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\delta\rho\,\rho^{-1}\delta\rho\right]=\frac{|s|\epsilon^{2}}{1-x\epsilon}\left(\operatorname{Tr}\left[\sigma\rho^{-1}\sigma\right]-1\right)\leq\frac{|s|\epsilon^{2}}{(1-\epsilon)\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}},

(221)

where in passing to the second line we used

and thus

(where the notation

Integrating (

|\Delta|\leq\int_{0}^{1}dx(1-x)\frac{|s|\epsilon^{2}}{(1-\epsilon)\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}}=\frac{|s|\epsilon^{2}}{2(1-\epsilon)\epsilon_{\textit{tail}}}.

(222)

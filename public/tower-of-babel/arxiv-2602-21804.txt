Quantum Hydrodynamic equation for semiconductor devices in 2-dimensional space and the relaxation time limit
Hao Zheng

Abstract
. This paper extends the author’s previous analysis in [ 9 ] on weak solutions with large norms for the collisional quantum hydrodynamic (QHD) equations in semiconductor modeling to 2-dimensional tori. We first establish the global existence of weak solutions with strictly positive density within the functional framework of GCP solutions introduced in [ 7 ] . A logarithmic Sobolev-type inequality is employed to control oscillations in the mass density. Furthermore, by constructing a combined functional that incorporates both the GCP energy and the physical entropy, we derive the exponential decay of solutions. As a byproduct of our approach, we also prove the global existence of H 2 H^{2} solutions for a nonlinear Schr"odinger-Langevin equation. Finally, for GCP solutions that remain bounded away from vacuum, we justify the time-relaxation limit and provide an explicit convergence rate. Our analysis relies on compactness techniques that does not require the existence or smoothness of solutions to the limiting equations. Moreover, our results impose no well-preparedness conditions on the initial data, thereby accommodating the possible formation of an initial layer.

1.
Introduction and main results

In this paper we focus on weak solutions with large norms of the quantum hydrodynamic (QHD) system for semi-conductor devices on 2-dimensional tori and their time-relaxation limit. The quantum hydrodynamic system is given by the following hydrodynamic equations,

(1.1)

\begin{cases}{\partial}_{t}\rho+\diver J=0\\
{\partial}_{t}J+\diver\left(\frac{J\otimes J}{\rho}\right)+\nabla p(\rho)+\rho\nabla V=\frac{1}{2}\rho\nabla\left(\frac{\triangle\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho}}\right)-\frac{1}{\tau}J\\
-\triangle V=\rho-\mathcal{C}(x),\quad(\rho,J)(0,x)=(\rho_{0},J_{0})(x),\end{cases}

for
(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
. Here
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
denotes the
2
-dimensional torus that, without loss of generality, we assume
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}={\mathbf{R}}^{2}/{\mathbf{Z}}^{2}
. The quantum term can be equivalently written as

(1.2)

\frac{1}{2}\rho\nabla\left(\frac{\triangle\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho}}\right)=\frac{1}{4}\diver(\rho\nabla^{2}\log\rho)=\frac{1}{4}\nabla\triangle\rho-\diver(\nabla\sqrt{\rho}\otimes\nabla\sqrt{\rho}).

where the second expression reads as a quantum stress tensor, and the last identity exhibit the dispersive effect of the quantum term together with the non-linear effect of Fisher information
[
22
]
. The quantum hydrodynamic model (
1.1
) finds application in semiconductor device modeling
[
25
]
. In this context, the function
V
represents a self-consistent electric potential governed by the Poisson equation, while
\mathcal{C}(x)
denotes the density of stationary positive background charges.

Such quantum fluid frameworks are applied to diverse physical systems, particularly where the thermal de Broglie wavelength is on the order of the interatomic distance. Relevant examples include superfluidity in Helium II
[
30
]
, Bose-Einstein condensates
[
40
]
, and quantum plasmas
[
21
]
. Furthermore, the collisional model (
1.1
) with
\tau>0
serves as a toy model for examining interactions between quantum and classical fluids. This relates conceptually to the two-fluid description introduced by Landau and Tisza; see the discussions in
[
30
, Section 17]
and
[
33
, Chapter XVI]
.

Regarding the collisional term’s origin in semiconductor physics, its derivation can proceed via a phenomenological approach, accounting for thermal interactions among charge carriers (see
[
1
,
2
,
19
]
or Section 7 in
[
6
]
). Alternatively, it can be obtained from kinetic models as an approximation for intra-band collision processes
[
11
]
.

The main purpose of this paper is to establish the global well-posedness of weak solutions of (
1.1
) with large norm and strictly positive density
\rho
. Moreover, we will study of the relaxation-time limit of system (
1.1
). By introducing the scaling
[
17
,
38
,
37
]
,

(1.3)

t^{\prime}=\tau t,\quad(\rho_{\tau},J_{\tau})(t^{\prime},x)=\left(\rho,\frac{1}{\tau}J\right)\left(\frac{t^{\prime}}{\tau},x\right),

the system (
1.1
) can be reformulated as

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\partial}_{t^{\prime}}\rho_{\tau}+\diver J_{\tau}=0\\
&\tau^{2}{\partial}_{t^{\prime}}J_{\tau}+\tau^{2}\diver\left(\frac{J_{\tau}\otimes J_{\tau}}{\rho_{\tau}}\right)+\nabla p(\rho_{\tau})+\rho_{\tau}\nabla V_{\tau}=\frac{1}{2}\rho_{\tau}\nabla\left(\frac{\triangle\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}}{\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}}\right)-J_{\tau}\\
&-\triangle V_{\tau}=\rho_{\tau}-\mathcal{C}(x),\quad(\rho_{\tau},J_{\tau})(0,x)=(\rho_{0},J_{\tau,0})(x),\end{aligned}\right.

As
\tau\to 0
, system (
1.4
) formally converges to the following Quantum Drift-diffusion (QDD) equation,

(1.5)

\begin{cases}{\partial}_{t^{\prime}}\bar{\rho}+\diver\left[\frac{1}{2}\bar{\rho}\nabla\left(\frac{\triangle\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}}{\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}}\right)-\nabla p(\bar{\rho})-\bar{\rho}\nabla\bar{V}\right]=0\\
-\triangle\bar{V}=\bar{\rho}-\mathcal{C}(x),\quad\rho_{\tau}(0,x)=\rho_{0}(x),\end{cases}

see for instance
[
25
,
41
]
. The main goal of this paper is rigorously prove this limit in a suitable functional framework that will be explained below.

A natural framework to study system (
1.1
) is the space of finite mass and energy, characterised by the conserved total mass

M(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho(t)dx\equiv M_{0},

and total energy functional

(1.6)

E(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}+\frac{|J|^{2}}{2\rho}+f(\rho)+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla V|^{2}dx.

In the non-collisional case (
\tau=\infty
),
E(t)
is the conserved energy associated to the Hamiltonian QHD system, while in the cases
0<\tau<\infty
, it satisfies the corresponding energy balance law

(1.7)

E(t)+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\frac{|J|^{2}}{\rho}dxds=E(0);

The internal energy
f(\rho)
is related to the pressure
p(\rho)
via the relation

(1.8)

p(\rho)=f^{\prime}(\rho)\rho-f(\rho),

or equivalently

f(\rho)=\rho\int^{\rho}\frac{p(s)}{s^{2}}ds.

In this paper we will restrict ourselves to classical
\gamma-
law equations of state for the pressure with

(1.9)

f(\rho)=\frac{1}{2n}(\rho-M_{0})^{2n},\quad n\in N.

In the Poisson equation in (
1.1
) satisfied by the electric potential
V
, we also assume
\mathcal{C}(x)=M_{0}
, which guarantees the solvability of the Poisson equation.

The existence of finite energy weak solutions is proven by combining the results of
[
4
,
5
]
and
[
7
]
. However, the uniform bounds in the framework of the energy functional only are not sufficient to establish a compactness framework of (
1.4
). Indeed, while the inertial term can be controlled by the scaled energy dissipation, the weak limit of the the quadratic term
(\nabla\sqrt{\rho_{\tau}}\otimes\nabla\sqrt{\rho_{\tau}})
(see (
1.2
) above) may be affected by strong oscillations and concentration phenomena, which cannot be controlled by the sole energy bounds.

Therefore, in order to overcome such difficulty, we apply the compactness framework developed in the authors’ previous work
[
7
]
and introduce the notion of weak solutions with bounded generalised chemical potential (see Definition
7
below), shortened as
GCP solutions
. The chemical potential in multi-dimensional spaces is formally defined as

\mu=-\frac{\triangle\sqrt{\rho}}{2\sqrt{\rho}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{|J|^{2}}{\rho^{2}}+f^{\prime}(\rho)+V,

which can be formally interpreted as the first variation of the total energy functional with respect to the mass density,

\mu=\frac{\delta E}{\delta\rho}.

Indeed, by introducing
v=\frac{J}{\rho}
and noticing that
J=\frac{\delta E}{\delta v}
, equations (
1.1
) can be formally written as the following system (Hamilton system with a damping term) associated to the energy functional

(1.10)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\partial}_{t}\rho=-\diver\frac{\delta E}{\delta v}\\
&{\partial}_{t}v=-\nabla\frac{\delta E}{\delta\rho}-\frac{1}{\tau}v=-\nabla\mu-\frac{1}{\tau}v.\end{aligned}\right.

The objects introduced above allow us to define the GCP functional

(1.11)

I(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\frac{\rho}{2}(\mu^{2}+\sigma^{2})dx,

where

\sigma={\partial}_{t}\log\sqrt{\rho}=-\frac{\diver J}{2\rho}.

The functional
I(t)
characterizes higher order regularity for solutions of (
1.1
). In
[
7
,
8
]
, it is shown that
I(t)
remains uniformly bounded over compact time intervals for non-collisional QHD system. Moreover, a compactness class for weak solutions to QHD is also identified in the framework of
I(t)
.
Following the terminology used in
[
7
,
8
]
, we denote by GCP solutions the family of weak solutions with finite energy (see (
1.6
)) and finite functional
I(t)
(defined in (
1.11
)), see also Definition
7
.
Unlike
[
7
,
8
]
where vacuum regions are allowed,
our analysis requires to consider solutions whose mass density remains uniformly bounded away from vacuum.
Indeed, by using (
1.1
), the time derivative of
I(t)
is formally given by

(1.12)

\frac{d}{dt}I(t)+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho\sigma^{2}dx=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\mu{\partial}_{t}p(\rho)dx+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho\mu{\partial}_{t}Vdx-\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho v^{2}\mu dx.

In particular, the last term in the right hand side of (
1.12
) appears due to the collision term. The chemical potential may experience strong density fluctuations close to vacuum, and possibly generate loss of integrability properties. Therefore in this paper we restrict to strictly positive densities. The rigorous computation of
\frac{d}{dt}I(t)
will be given in Proposition
14
.

The GCP solution framework was successfully applied to (
1.1
) in the one-dimensional case in
[
9
]
, where the strict positivity of the mass density
\rho
is ensured by combining the Poincar’e inequality with the energy balance law (
1.7
). In multi-dimensional settings, however, the energy bound alone proves insufficient to regulate the lower bound of
\rho
. To overcome this difficulty, we introduce a logarithmic
L^{\infty}
embedding inequality (Lemma
17
), which bounds the oscillation of
\rho
by a suitable combination of
E(t)
and
I(t)
. In order to maintain strict positivity of
\rho
, a norm condition (
1.14
) is imposed on the initial energy
E(0)
and the initial GCP functional
I(0)
. We emphasize, nevertheless, that this condition does not exclude initial data with large norms; see Remark
2
.

1.1.
Main result: global well-posedness of GCP solutions and stability

The first result of this paper regards the global well-posedness of GCP weak solutions with strictly positive density. Let us recall that
M_{0}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{0}dx<\infty
is the conserved total mass, and we denote by
E_{0}=E(0)
and
I_{0}=I(t)
to be the total energy and higher order functional at initial time, which are defined by (
1.6
) and (
1.11
) respectively.

Theorem 1

(Global well-posedness of GCP weak solutions)
.

Let us consider a finite energy initial datum
(\rho_{0},J_{0})
satisfying the following conditions.

•

The initial density satisfies
\inf_{x}\rho_{0}\geq\delta
for a
\delta>0
. Let us denote by
v_{0}=J_{0}/\rho_{0}
the initial velocity. We assume that

(1.13)

\int_{\mathbb{T}}v_{0}(y,x_{2})dy=\int_{\mathbb{T}}v_{0}(x_{1},y)dy=0

for almost every
(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
.

•

There exists a generic
\varepsilon>0
, such that the initial energy
E_{0}
and higher order functional
I_{0}
satisfy

(1.14)

e^{E_{0}}\cdot(1+I_{0})\leq\varepsilon\frac{e^{M_{0}-\delta}}{M_{0}-\delta}.

Then, there exists
\tau^{*}>0
, depending on
(\delta,M_{0},E_{0},I_{0})
, such that for
0<\tau\leq\tau^{*}
, the Cauchy problem of the QHD system (
1.1
) has a unique global in time GCP solution
(\rho,J)
, that for any
0\leq t<\infty

•

the density remains strictly positive with lower bound
\inf_{t,x}\rho\geq\delta
;

•

the total energy
E(t)
satisfies the energy balance law, namely for every
t>0
,

(1.15)

E(t)+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|v|^{2}dxds=E_{0};

•

the higher order functional
I(t)
satisfies the bounds

(1.16)

I(t)+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}[({\partial}_{t}\sqrt{\rho})^{2}+\rho|v|^{4}]dxds\leq C(\delta,M_{0},E_{0},I_{0}).

Remark 2
.

The details for the condition (
1.14
) and the constant
\varepsilon>0
are provided in Section
4
through the proof of Theorem
1
, see for example (
4.26
). In particular, for fixed
\delta>0
, the right hand side of (
1.14
) goes to infinity as
M_{0}\to\infty
, which allows Theorem
1
to be applied to initial data with large norm.

Our strategy to prove Theorem
1
is motivated by using the formal equivalence between system (
1.1
) and the following Schrödinger-Langevin type equation

(1.17)

i{\partial}_{t}\psi+\frac{1}{2}\triangle\psi=f^{\prime}(|\psi|^{2})\psi+\frac{1}{\tau}S\psi+V\psi,

see also
[
28
]
.
Here the potential
S
is given through the Madelung transformations
[
36
]
,
\psi=|\psi|e^{iS}
as the phase function associated to
\psi
, and it can be formally defined as
S=\frac{1}{i}\log\left(\frac{\psi}{|\psi|}\right)
. The hydrodynamic system (
1.1
) can be formally recast from (
2.8
) by defining the associated hydrodynamic variables
(\rho,J)=(|\psi|^{2},\IM(\bar{\psi}\nabla\psi))
and by computing the related balance laws, see for instance
[
7
]
.
Equation (
1.17
) was also independently introduced, in the case
f=0
, by Kostin
[
32
]
(see also
[
42
]
and
[
39
]
) to derive a Schrödinger equation which was taking into account the interaction with Brownian particles. The usual difficulty entailed by the definition of the complex logarithm prevents us to study equation (
1.17
) with standard methods.

To prove Theorem
1
, we first show the the well-posedness of the Cauchy problem for Schrödinger-Langevin equation (
1.17
). Then we rigorously establish the equivalence between QHD system (
1.1
) and equation (
1.17
) for solutions with strictly positive density, by the methods of wave function lifting
[
7
,
8
]
(see also
[
43
]
) and polar factorization
[
4
]
developed in the authors’ previous works. As an independent result, we also state the well-posedness of equation (
1.17
) as the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

Let us assume that the initial datum
\psi_{0}\in H^{2}_{x}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
for (
1.17
) satisfies

e^{\|\nabla\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}}\cdot(1+\|\nabla^{2}\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}})\leq\varepsilon\frac{e^{M_{0}-\delta}}{M_{0}-\delta}.

Then there exists
\tau^{*}>0
such that for
0<\tau\leq\tau^{*}
, the Cauchy problem associated to (
2.8
) with initial data
\psi_{0}
has a unique global solution
\psi\in\mathcal{C}([0,\infty);H^{2}_{x}({\mathbb{T}}^{2}))
. Moreover,
\inf_{t,x}|\psi|\geq\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}
and the hydrodynamic functions associated to
\psi
, given by

\rho=|\psi|^{2},\quad J=\IM(\bar{\psi}\nabla\psi)=\rho v,

satisfy the bounds (
1.15
) (with
E_{0}=E(\psi_{0})
) and (
1.16
) on any finite time interval
[0,T]
,
T>0
.

In additionally, by combining the functionals
E(t)
and
I(t)
with the physical entropy

(1.18)

H(\rho)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho\log\left(\frac{\rho}{M_{0}}\right)dx,

we further prove the exponential decay of GCP solutions. Such property was also proved for the following fourth-order parabolic equation
[
18
,
20
,
29
]
in the 1-dimensional case,

(1.19)

{\partial}_{t^{\prime}}\bar{\rho}+\frac{1}{4}\diver\cdot\diver(\bar{\rho}\nabla^{2}\log\bar{\rho})=0,

which can be viewed a reduced QDD equation (
1.5
) without pressure and electric potential. Equation (
1.19
) independently appears also in various places of the mathematical physics literature, which was first derived by Derrida, Lebowitz, Speer, and Spohn in
[
15
,
16
]
, therefore we refer to it as the
DLSS equation
. A similar equation was also obtained in
[
13
,
14
]
as a model for thin films. The next result shows that the exponential decay is true also for the hydrodynamic model.

Theorem 4

(Dissipation for small
\tau
)
.

Let
(\rho,J)
be a GCP solution to the QHD system (
1.1
) provided by Theorem
1
. Then, there exist
C_{0}>0
and
c_{1},c_{2},\tau^{*}>0
, such that for
0<\tau\leq\tau^{*}
the functional

F_{\tau}(t)=H(\rho_{\tau})+E_{\tau}(t)+c_{1}I_{\tau}(t)

satisfies

(1.20)

F(t)\leq F(0)\exp\left(\frac{C_{0}}{\tau}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|v|^{2}dxds-c_{2}\tau t\right).

In particular,
H(\rho_{\tau})
,
E_{\tau}(t)
and

Remark 5
.

The details for the choices of
C_{0}>0
and
c_{1},c_{2},\tau^{*}>0
are given in Proposition

. In particular,
c_{1},c_{2}

(\delta,M_{0},E_{0},I_{0})
, while
C_{0}
is a generic constant.

1.2.
Main result: relaxation-time limit

Let us now consider the relaxation-time limit
\tau\to 0
. Recall the scaling (
1.3
),

(1.21)

t^{\prime}=\tau t,\quad(\rho_{\tau},J_{\tau})(t^{\prime},x)=\left(\rho,\frac{1}{\tau}J\right)\left(\frac{t^{\prime}}{\tau},x\right),

then the QHD system (
1.1
) can be written as it appears in (
1.4
), which formally converges towards the QDD equation (
1.5
) as
\tau\to 0
.

The initial rigorous justification for small, smooth perturbations near a constant state was established in
[
27
]
, with subsequent extension to the bipolar system (modeling electrons and ions) in
[
35
]
. While the relaxation-time limit for finite energy weak solutions is treated in
[
34
]
, that analysis requires the initial data to be well-prepared (as described below) and imposes sufficient smoothness on solutions to the limiting equations (
1.5
). Similar relaxation-time limit studies have also been carried out in the context of classical hydrodynamic equations; see, for instance,
[
23
,
24
,
26
]
.See also
[
3
]
, where the relaxation-time limit was proved for general weak solutions to quantum Navier-Stokes equations.

A critical aspect in the relaxation-time limit is the appearance of an initial layer. The rescaled QHD system (
1.4
) requires two initial components: the density
\rho_{0}
and the momentum density
J_{\tau,0}
. By contrast, the QDD equation (
1.5
) only prescribes the initial density
\rho_{0}
, while its limiting momentum density must satisfy the constitutive relation

(1.22)

\bar{J}=\frac{1}{2}\bar{\rho}\nabla\left(\frac{\triangle\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}}{\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}}\right)-\nabla p(\bar{\rho})-\bar{\rho}\nabla\bar{V},

which can be derived formally by letting
\tau\to 0
in the second equation of (
1.4
).
Initial data for (
1.4
) are said to be well-prepared when
J_{\tau,0}=\bar{J}_{0}
, where
\bar{J}_{0}
is obtained by setting
\bar{\rho}=\rho_{0}
in (
1.22
).
In the present work, we do not assume the initial data to be well-prepared; consequently, an initial layer emerges naturally. As a result, convergence of the momentum density holds only for
{t>0}
.
An additional contribution compared to existing literature is that we show an explicit rate of convergence with respect to the parameter
\tau
.

The proof of Theorem
6
relies on deriving suitable a priori bounds for solutions to the hydrodynamical system, in order to pass to the limit (at least in the distributional sense) all terms in equations (
1.4
). These estimates are obtained by analyzing the physical entropy
H(\rho_{\tau})
defined by (
1.18
), which provides us with the
L^{2}_{t^{\prime}}H^{2}_{x}
bound of
\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}
uniformly with respect to
\tau
. Moreover, we obtain an explicit convergence rate of this limit with respect to
\tau
by considering the relative entropy

\displaystyle H(\rho_{\tau}|\bar{\rho})(t^{\prime})=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}g(\rho_{\tau})-g(\bar{\rho})-g^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})(\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho})dx,

where
\bar{\rho}
is the limiting solution of the QDD equation (
1.5
).

Theorem 6

(Relaxation-time limit)
.

Let
\{(\rho_{\tau},J_{\tau})\}_{\tau>0}
be a sequence of GCP solutions of the rescaled equation (
1.4
), such that

•

\inf_{t\textquoteright,x}\rho_{\tau}\geq\delta
for a uniform constant
\delta>0
,

•

(\rho_{\tau},J_{\tau})
satisfy the uniform bounds

E_{\tau}(t^{\prime})+\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho_{\tau}|v_{\tau}|^{2}dxds\leq E_{0}

and

I_{\tau}(t^{\prime})+\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}[({\partial}_{t^{\prime}}\sqrt{\rho_{\tau}})^{2}+\rho_{\tau}|v_{\tau}|^{4}]dxds\leq C.

Then the relaxation-time limit holds, namely
\{\rho_{\tau}\}
converges to a limiting density
\bar{\rho}
in the form

\|\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t^{\prime}}L^{2}_{x}}+\|\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}(\nabla^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}-\nabla^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})\|_{L^{2}_{t^{\prime},x}}\leq C\tau.

Moreover,
\bar{\rho}
is a weak solution of (
1.5
) in the sense of Definition
8
.

We remark that our result works for general initial data
(\rho_{0},J_{0})
, and we allow the existence of initial layer. On the other hand, a better convergence rate can be obtained by using the relative energy method and in addition by assuming well-prepared initial data, see for instance
[
34
]
.

Our paper is structured as follows. Definitions and preliminaries results are given in Section 2. Section
3
is dedicated to the computation of the time derivatives of the main functionals through the dynamics of (
1.1
) and (
1.17
), and in Section
4
we prove the global well-posedness of GCP solutions (Theorem
1
) and their dissipative property (Theorem
4
). The relation between (
1.17
) and (
1.1
) is rigorously established by the methods of wave function lifting and polar factorization. Finally, the rigorous proof of the relaxation-time limit in the framework of GCP solutions is concluded in Section
5
.

2.
Definitions and preliminaries

In this section we first provide some notation and definition that will be used through out this paper.

Lebesgue and Sobolev norms on
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
are denoted by

||f||_{L_{x}^{p}}\coloneqq\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|f(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

||f||_{W_{x}^{k,p}}\coloneqq\sum_{j=0}^{k}||\partial_{x}^{j}f||_{L_{x}^{p}},

and
H_{x}^{k}\coloneqq H^{k}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
denotes the Sobolev space
W^{k,2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
.
Given a time interval
I\subset[0,\infty)
, the mixed space-time Lebesgue norm of
f:I\to L^{r}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
is
given by

||f||_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}}\coloneqq\left(\int_{I}||f(t)||_{L_{x}^{r}}^{q}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}=\left(\int_{I}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|f(x)|^{r}dx\right)^{\frac{q}{r}}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Similarly the mixed
Sobolev norm
L_{t}^{q}W_{x}^{k,r}
is defined. We use
C_{0}>0
to denote a generic constant that may change from line to line, and
C(A)
indicates dependence on the quantity
A
.

By using identity (
1.2
) and defining the velocity
v=\frac{J}{\rho}
, system (
1.1
) becomes

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\partial}_{t}\rho+\diver(\rho v)=0\\
&{\partial}_{t}v+(v\cdot\nabla)v+\nabla f^{\prime}(\rho)+\nabla V=\frac{1}{2}\nabla\left(\frac{\triangle\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho}}\right)-\frac{1}{\tau}v.\end{aligned}\right.

Let us also recall the total mass and total energy are respectively given by

(2.2)

M(t)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho(t,x)\,dx,

and

(2.3)

E(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\frac{1}{2}\rho|v|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}+f(\rho)+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla V|^{2}dx.

We also recall the definition of GCP solutions, characterized by the (generalized) chemical potential introduced

(2.4)

\mu=-\frac{\triangle\sqrt{\rho}}{2\sqrt{\rho}}+\frac{1}{2}|v|^{2}+f^{\prime}(\rho)+V,\quad\sigma={\partial}_{t}\log\sqrt{\rho}=-\frac{\diver J}{2\rho}.

The
L^{2}
norm of
\mu
and
\sigma
with respect to density measure
\rho dx
is defined as the GCP functional

I(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\frac{1}{2}\rho(\mu^{2}+\sigma)dx.

Let us remark that this definition may apply to a more general class of weak solutions, which contain states with vacuum regions, see

The definition of weak solutions with bounded generalized chemical potential is presented as follows.

Definition 7

(GCP solutions)
.

We say that
(\rho,v)\in
is a GCP solution for the Cauchy problem of QHD system (

) on

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}({\mathbb{T}}^{2}))}+\|\sqrt{\rho}v\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2}))}\leq

\displaystyle C_{0},

\displaystyle\|{\partial}_{t}\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2}))}+\|\sqrt{\rho}\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2}))}\leq

Moreover, if the existence time

, we call

a global solution.

Next, we introduce the physical entropy
H(\rho)
of a density function
\rho\geq 0
on
[0,T)\times{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
is defined by

(2.6)

H(\rho)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho\log\left(\frac{\rho}{M_{0}}\right),

where

is the conserved total mass of

can also be viewed as the average of

For later use in the analysis of relaxation-time limit, we now give the definition and dispersive property of weak solutions to the quantum drift-diffusion equation (
1.5
).

Definition 8
.

We say
\bar{\rho}
is a weak solution to the quantum drift-diffusion equation (
1.5
) with initial data
\bar{\rho}(0)=\bar{\rho}_{0}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
on
[0,T)\times{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
, if
\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}\in L^{2}_{loc}([0,T);H^{1}({\mathbb{T}}^{2}))
satisfies equation (
1.5
) in the sense of distribution.

Moreover,
\bar{\rho}
is called a dissipative solution if we have

(2.7)

H(\bar{\rho})(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|\nabla^{2}\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}|^{2}+|\nabla(\bar{\rho}^{\frac{1}{4}})|^{4}dxdt\leq H(\rho_{0})

for any
0\leq t<T
.

As already mentioned in the Introduction, there is a formal analogy between system (
2.1
) and the Schrödinger-Langevin type equation

(2.8)

i{\partial}_{t}\psi+\frac{1}{2}\triangle\psi=f^{\prime}(|\psi|^{2})\psi+\frac{1}{\tau}S\psi+V\psi,

through the so-called Madelung transformation
[
36
]
. Indeed, this may be immediately seen by expressing the wave function
\psi
in terms of its amplitude and phase, namely

(2.9)

\psi=\sqrt{\rho}e^{iS}.

By plugging (
2.9
) into (
2.8
) and by further separating real and imaginary parts of the resulting equation, a system of evolution equations is obtained for
\rho
and
S
,

(2.10)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\partial}_{t}\rho+\diver(\rho\nabla S)=0\\
&{\partial}_{t}S+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla S|^{2}+f^{\prime}(\rho)+V+\frac{1}{\tau}S=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\triangle\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho}}.\end{aligned}\right.

If we further differentiate the second equation in (
2.10
) with respect
x
, we obtain an Euler-type equation for the velocity field
v=\nabla S
, namely

(2.11)

\textrm{curl }v=0,\;{\partial}_{t}v+\nabla\left(\frac{1}{2}|v|^{2}+f^{\prime}(\rho)+V-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\triangle\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho}}\right)=-\frac{1}{\tau}v.

By recalling the definition of the chemical potential in (
2.4
), we see that (
2.11
) equals the second equation in (
1.10
).
The momentum density equation in the QHD system (
1.1
) is then derived by multiplying (
2.11
) by
\rho
and by using the continuity equation.

Following the intuition above, we say
(\sqrt{\rho},\rho v)
are hydrodynamic functions associated to
\psi\in H^{s}_{x}
,
s\geq 1
, if

(2.12)

(\rho,\rho v)=(|\psi|^{2},\IM(\bar{\psi}\nabla\psi)).

If one further have
\inf|\psi|>0
, then the velocity can be directly written as

(2.13)

v=\IM\left(\frac{\nabla\psi}{\psi}\right).

On the other hand, we can also recover a complex wave function
\psi
from given hydrodynamic function
(\rho,v)
provided
\rho\geq\delta>0
. From (
2.10
), we have

(2.14)

\begin{cases}\nabla S=v,\quad{\partial}_{t}S=-\mu-\frac{1}{\tau}S\\
S(0,0)=S_{*}.\end{cases}

The equation (
2.11
) for the velocity field may be interpreted as the "irrotationality" condition for the gradient equation (
2.14
), namely

(2.15)

\textrm{curl }v=0,\quad{\partial}_{t}v+\nabla(\mu+\frac{1}{\tau}S)=0.

Thus we can formally write the phase function
S
in hydrodynamic functions as

(2.16)

S(t,x)=e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}\left(S_{*}+\int_{l(0,x_{*})}v_{0}(y)\cdot d\vec{l}(y)\right)-\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{s-t}{\tau}}\mu(s,x_{*})ds+\int_{l(x_{*},x)}v_{0}(y)\cdot d\vec{l}(y),

where
x_{*}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
is an arbitrary point and
l(x,y)
is a piecewise smooth curve connecting from point
x
to point
y
. For example, we can choose
l(x,y)
to be the collection of piecewise straight lines parallel to each axis, namely for
x=(x_{1},x_{2})
,
y=(y_{1},y_{2})
, we have

(2.17)

l(x,y)=\{(s,x_{2});s\textrm{ from }x_{1}\textrm{ to }y_{1}\}\cup\{(y_{1},s);s\textrm{ from }x_{2}\textrm{ to }y_{2}\}

Due to the "irrotationality" condition (
2.15
), the choice of point
x_{*}
has no influence on the definition. However, for weak solutions
(\rho,v)
, the chemical potential
\mu
is merely a Lebesgue function or even a distribution. Since (
2.16
) should be independent of the point
x_{*}
over
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
, we can take the average of (
2.16
) with respect to
x_{*}
, which allows us to extend the hydrodynamic definition of the phase function
S
to more general cases.

Definition 9

(Phase function)
.

Let
(\rho,v)
be a solution of (

such that the velocity

and
(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{T}}^{2}

Then we define the phase function

to be the solution of the gradient equation (

\displaystyle S(t,x)=

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}\left(S_{*}+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\int_{l(0,x_{*})}v_{0}(y)\cdot d\vec{l}(y)dx_{*}\right)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{s-t}{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\mu(s,x_{*})dx_{*}ds+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\int_{l(x_{*},x)}v(t,y)\cdot d\vec{l}(y)dx_{*}.

First, we have the following proposition concerning the time derivative of the main functionals

,

and

introduced in Section.

Proposition 14
.

Let
\psi\in L^{\infty}([0,T);H^{2}_{x}({\mathbb{T}}^{2}))
be a solution to the Cauchy problem (
3.1
) such that
\inf_{t,x}|\psi|>0
and let
(\rho,v)
be the hydrodynamic functions associated with
\psi
in the sense of (
2.12
). Then, for any
t\in[0,T^{*})
,
\psi
satisfies the following properties:

(1)

the total mass is conserved

(3.3)

M(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho(t,x)dx\equiv M_{0},

and the total energy, given by (
2.3
), dissipates

(3.4)

E(t)+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho v^{2}dxds=E(0)=E_{0};

(2)

The GCP functional
I(t)
, defined by (
2.5
), is differentiable for almost every

and satisfies

(3.5)

\frac{d}{dt}I(t)+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho\sigma^{2}dx=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\mu{\partial}_{t}p(\rho)dx+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho\mu{\partial}_{t}Vdx-\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|v|^{2}\mu dx,

where
p(\rho)
is the pressure given by (
1.8
).

Proof.

By equation (
3.1
), it is straightforward to check the density
\rho=|\psi|^{2}
satisfies the continuity equation

{\partial}_{t}\rho+\diver J=0,

which directly implies the conservation of the total mass. To prove the energy relation (
3.4
), let us multiply equation (
3.1
) by
-\triangle\bar{\psi}
, take the imaginary part and integrate by parts,

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|\nabla\psi|^{2}dx=-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}[f^{\prime}(\rho)+\tau^{-1}S+V]\IM(\psi\triangle\bar{\psi})dx,

where

\IM(\psi\triangle\bar{\psi})=\diver\IM(\psi\nabla\bar{\psi})=-\diver(\rho v)={\partial}_{t}\rho.

Thus we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\psi|^{2}+f(\rho)dx=

\displaystyle\tau^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}S\diver(\rho v)dx-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}V{\partial}_{t}\rho dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\tau^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|v|^{2}dx+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}V{\partial}_{t}(\triangle V-\mathcal{C}(x))dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\tau^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|v|^{2}dx-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla V|^{2}dx.

In order to prove property (2), we first show for
\psi\in L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}
with
\inf_{t,x}|\psi|>0
, the following identity holds,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho^{-1}[\RE(\bar{\psi}\triangle\psi)-

\displaystyle 2W\rho)^{2}+\IM(\bar{\psi}\triangle\psi)^{2}]dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\IM[\triangle(W\bar{\psi}+\tau^{-1}S\bar{\psi})\triangle\psi]dx

\displaystyle+2\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}[\rho{\partial}_{t}(W^{2})+W^{2}{\partial}_{t}\rho]dx

\displaystyle-2\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}{\partial}_{t}W\RE(\bar{\psi}\triangle\psi)dx-4\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}W\RE({\partial}_{t}\bar{\psi}\triangle\psi)dx

\displaystyle-4\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\diver W\RE(\nabla\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{t}\psi)dx-2\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\triangle W\RE(\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{t}\psi)dx.

where we define the potential
W=f^{\prime}(\rho)+V
. However, to establish this identity rigorously, we need to introduce the standard sequence of mollifiers
\{\chi_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
on
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
and let
g_{\varepsilon}=g\ast\chi_{\varepsilon}
for functions
g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
. We mollify equation (
3.1
), multiply it by
\triangle^{2}\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}
and integrate by parts in the imaginary part, which gives

(3.7)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|\triangle\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}dx=-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\IM[\triangle(W\bar{\psi}+\tau^{-1}S\bar{\psi})_{\varepsilon}\triangle\psi_{\varepsilon}]dx,

where the function
S
is defined by (
2.20
). Since
\psi\in L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}
and
\inf_{t,x}|\psi|>0
, it is easy to check

W\bar{\psi}+\tau^{-1}S\bar{\psi}\in L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}.

Now we expand the integrand in the left hand side of (
3.7
) as

\displaystyle|\triangle\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}=|\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{-2}|\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}\triangle\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}=\rho_{\varepsilon}^{-1}[\RE(\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}\triangle\psi_{\varepsilon})^{2}+\IM(\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}\triangle\psi_{\varepsilon})^{2}],

where we let
\rho_{\varepsilon}=|\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}
, which is different from
\rho\ast\chi_{\varepsilon}
. To match the chemical potential (
3.2
), we write

\displaystyle[\RE(\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}\triangle\psi_{\varepsilon})]^{2}=

\displaystyle[\RE(\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}\triangle\psi_{\varepsilon})-2W_{\varepsilon}\rho_{\varepsilon}]^{2}

\displaystyle-4W_{\varepsilon}^{2}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{2}+4W_{\varepsilon}\rho_{\varepsilon}\RE(\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}\triangle\psi_{\varepsilon}).

thus we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|\triangle\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}dx=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{-1}[\RE(\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}\triangle\psi_{\varepsilon})-2W_{\varepsilon}\rho_{\varepsilon}]^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\IM(\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}\triangle\psi_{\varepsilon})^{2}dx

\displaystyle+2\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}[W_{\varepsilon}\RE(\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}\triangle\psi_{\varepsilon})-W_{\varepsilon}^{2}\rho_{\varepsilon}]dx.

Now we compute the time derivative of the last integral,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}W_{\varepsilon}\RE(\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}\triangle\psi_{\varepsilon})dx=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}{\partial}_{t}W_{\varepsilon}\RE(\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}\triangle\psi_{\varepsilon})dx+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}W_{\varepsilon}\RE({\partial}_{t}\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}\triangle\psi_{\varepsilon})dx

\displaystyle+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}W_{\varepsilon}\RE(\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}\triangle{\partial}_{t}\psi_{\varepsilon})dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}{\partial}_{t}W_{\varepsilon}\RE(\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}\triangle\psi_{\varepsilon})dx+2\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}W_{\varepsilon}\RE({\partial}_{t}\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}\triangle\psi_{\varepsilon})dx

\displaystyle+2\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\diver W_{\varepsilon}\RE(\nabla\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{t}\psi_{\varepsilon})dx+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\triangle W_{\varepsilon}\RE(\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{t}\psi_{\varepsilon})dx,

and

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}W_{\varepsilon}^{2}\rho_{\varepsilon}dx=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}[2{\partial}_{t}(W_{\varepsilon}^{2})\rho_{\varepsilon}+W_{\varepsilon}^{2}{\partial}_{t}\rho_{\varepsilon}]dx.

Since we have
\psi\in L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}
, which implies
{\partial}_{t}\psi\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
, the mollified sequences converge strongly in the same topologies respectively, and the integrals are uniformly bounded with respect to
\varepsilon
. Thus we can pass to the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
, which gives (
3.6
).

In order to reduce (
3.6
) to (
3.5
), it follows (
3.2
) that

\RE(\bar{\psi}\triangle\psi)=-2\rho\mu+2\rho W,\quad\IM(\bar{\psi}\triangle\psi)=2\rho\sigma,

therefore

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho^{-1}[(\RE(\bar{\psi}\triangle\psi)-2\rho W)^{2}+\IM(\bar{\psi}\triangle\psi)^{2}]dx=4\frac{d}{dt}I(t).

We further compute that

\displaystyle-\IM[\triangle(W\bar{\psi}+\tau^{-1}S\bar{\psi})\triangle\psi]=

\displaystyle-[\triangle W+\tau^{-1}\diver v]\IM(\bar{\psi}\triangle\psi)

\displaystyle-2[\nabla W+\tau^{-1}v]\cdot\IM(\nabla\bar{\psi}\triangle\psi),

where we use
\nabla S=v
, and

-2{\partial}_{t}W\RE(\bar{\psi}\triangle\psi)=4{\partial}_{t}W\rho\mu-2\rho{\partial}_{t}(W^{2}).

By equation (
3.1
), we also have

-4W\RE({\partial}_{t}\bar{\psi}\triangle\psi)=4W(W+\tau^{-1}S)\IM(\bar{\psi}\triangle\psi),

-4\nabla\cdot W\RE(\nabla\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{t}\psi)=2\nabla\cdot W\IM(\nabla\bar{\psi}\triangle\psi)-4\nabla W\cdot(W+\tau^{-1}S)\IM(\bar{\psi}\nabla\psi),

and

-2\triangle W\RE(\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{t}\psi)=\triangle W\IM(\bar{\psi}\triangle\psi).

Notice that

\IM(\bar{\psi}\triangle\psi)=\diver\IM(\bar{\psi}\nabla\psi)=\diver(\rho v)=-{\partial}_{t}\rho,

then by summarising the identities above and integrating by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle 4\frac{d}{dt}I(t)=

\displaystyle 4\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho\mu{\partial}_{t}Wdx-\tau^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\diver v\diver(\rho v)dx

\displaystyle+4\tau^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|v|^{2}Wdx-2\tau^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}v\cdot\IM(\nabla\bar{\psi}\triangle\psi)dx.

Last, by using

\displaystyle\IM(\nabla\bar{\psi}\triangle\psi)=

\displaystyle\rho\IM\left(\frac{\nabla\bar{\psi}}{\bar{\psi}}\frac{\triangle\psi}{\psi}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho\left[\IM\left(\frac{\nabla\bar{\psi}}{\bar{\psi}}\right)\RE\left(\frac{\triangle\psi}{\psi}\right)+\RE\left(\frac{\nabla\bar{\psi}}{\bar{\psi}}\right)\IM\left(\frac{\triangle\psi}{\psi}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho[v(2\mu-2W)+\frac{1}{2}\rho^{-2}\nabla\rho\diver(\rho v)],

it follows that

\frac{d}{dt}I(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho\mu{\partial}_{t}Wdx-\tau^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|v|^{2}\mu dx-\frac{1}{4\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\frac{[\diver(\rho v)]^{2}}{\rho}dx,

which proves (
3.5
) by using (
1.8
) and

\frac{[\diver(\rho v)]^{2}}{\rho}=\frac{({\partial}_{t}\rho)^{2}}{\rho}=4\rho\sigma^{2}.

∎

We also need the following computation for the time derivative of the physical entropy

H(\rho)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho\log\left(\frac{\rho}{M_{0}}\right)dx.

Proposition 15
.

Let
(\rho,v)
be a GCP solution of (
2.1
) such that
\inf_{t,x}\rho>0
. Then the time derivative of

is given by

(3.8)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}H(\rho)=

\displaystyle-\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|\nabla^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}dx-4\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}p^{\prime}(\rho)|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle-\tau\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\log\rho{\partial}_{t}\rho dx+4\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho\sigma^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}(\rho v\otimes v):\nabla^{2}\log\rho dx-\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}(\rho-M_{0})^{2}dx.

As a consequence, we have the estimate

(3.9)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle[H(\rho)+\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\log\rho{\partial}_{t}\rho dx]+\frac{\tau}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|\nabla^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 4\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}p^{\prime}(\rho)|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}dx+\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}(\rho-M_{0})^{2}dx\leq 4\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho\sigma^{2}dx+\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|v|^{4}dx.

Proof.

To rigorously establish the computation, we again need to use the standard mollifiers
\{\chi_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
. Let
\rho_{\varepsilon}=\rho\ast\chi_{\varepsilon}
and we consider the entropy
H(\rho_{\varepsilon})
of the mollified density. By mollifying equation (
1.1
) and using (
1.2
) to write the quantum term in a logarithm form, the time derivative of
H(\rho_{\varepsilon})
is computed as

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}H(\rho_{\varepsilon})=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\log\rho_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{t}\rho_{\varepsilon}dx=-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\log\rho_{\varepsilon}\diver(\rho v)_{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\tau}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\log\rho_{\varepsilon}\diver\cdot\diver(\rho\nabla^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho})_{\varepsilon}dx+\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\log\rho_{\varepsilon}\triangle p(\rho)_{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle+\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\log\rho_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{t}\diver(\rho v)_{\varepsilon}dx+\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\log\rho_{\varepsilon}\diver\cdot\diver(\rho v\otimes v)_{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle+\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\log\rho_{\tau,\varepsilon}\diver(\rho\nabla V)_{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}(\rho\nabla^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho})_{\varepsilon}:\nabla^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\varepsilon}dx-\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\nabla p(\rho)_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\log\rho_{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle-\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\log\rho_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{t}^{2}\rho_{\varepsilon}dx+\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}(\rho v\otimes v)_{\varepsilon}:\nabla^{2}\log\rho_{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle-\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\frac{\nabla\rho_{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}\cdot(\rho\nabla V)_{\varepsilon}dx.

where we can further write

-\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\log\rho_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{t}^{2}\rho_{\varepsilon}dx=-\tau\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\log\rho_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{t}\rho_{\varepsilon}dx+\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}{\partial}_{t}\log\rho_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{t}\rho_{\varepsilon}dx.

Since
\psi\in L^{\infty}([0,T^{*});H^{2}_{x}({\mathbb{T}}^{2}))
with
\inf_{t,x}|\psi|>0
and
(\rho,v)
are given by
\psi
via (
2.12
), the integrands in the right hand side of
\frac{d}{ds}H(\rho_{\varepsilon})
are all mollifications of well-defined functions, and the integrals are uniformly bounded with respect to
\varepsilon
. Thus we can pass to the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}H(\rho)=

\displaystyle-\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|\nabla^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}dx-\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\nabla p(\rho)\cdot\nabla\log\rho dx

\displaystyle-\tau\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\log\rho{\partial}_{t}\rho dx+\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}{\partial}_{t}\log\rho{\partial}_{t}\rho dx

\displaystyle+\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}(\rho v\otimes v):\nabla^{2}\log\rho dx-\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla Vdx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|\nabla^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}dx-4\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}p^{\prime}(\rho)|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle-\tau\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\log\rho{\partial}_{t}\rho dx+4\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho\sigma^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}(\rho v\otimes v):\nabla^{2}\log\rho dx+\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho\triangle Vdx.

Last, by using the Poisson equation

-\triangle V=\rho-M_{0}

and
M_{0}=\int_{T^{2}}\rho dx
, we obtain (
3.8
).

For the inequality (
3.9
), we obtain it simply by writting

\displaystyle\tau\left|\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}(\rho v\otimes v):\nabla^{2}\log\rho dx\right|\leq

\displaystyle 2\tau\|\rho^{\frac{1}{4}}v\|_{L^{4}_{x}}^{2}\|\sqrt{\rho}\nabla^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho v^{4}dx+\frac{\tau}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|\nabla^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}dx,

∎

Last, we estabilish the following lemma, which shows that the norms
\|\nabla^{2}\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}
and
\|\nabla\rho^{\frac{1}{4}}\|_{L^{4}_{x}}
are controled by the term
\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|\nabla^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}dx
in the right hand side of (
3.8
).

Lemma 16
.

Let us assume
\rho\in H^{2}_{x}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
and
\rho>0
, then it follows that

(3.10)

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\frac{1}{3}|\triangle\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\frac{2}{3}|\nabla^{2}\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}+\frac{|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}|^{4}}{3\rho}dx\leq\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|\nabla^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}dx

Proof.

Direct computation gives

\sqrt{\rho}\nabla^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}=\sqrt{\rho}\nabla\left(\frac{\nabla\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho}}\right)=\nabla\sqrt{\rho}-\frac{\nabla\sqrt{\rho}\otimes\nabla\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho}},

and

\sqrt{\rho}\triangle\log\sqrt{\rho}=\sqrt{\rho}\diver\left(\frac{\nabla\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho}}\right)=\triangle\sqrt{\rho}-\frac{|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}}{\sqrt{\rho}}.

Thus we have

\displaystyle D_{1}=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|\nabla^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}dx=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|\nabla^{2}\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}+\frac{|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}|^{4}}{\rho}dx-2\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\nabla^{2}\sqrt{\rho}:\frac{\nabla\sqrt{\rho}\otimes\nabla\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{1}+B-I_{1}

and

\displaystyle D_{2}=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho(\triangle\log\sqrt{\rho})^{2}dx=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}(\triangle\sqrt{\rho})^{2}+\frac{|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}|^{4}}{\rho}dx-2\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\triangle\sqrt{\rho}\frac{|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}}{\sqrt{\rho}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{2}+B-I_{2}.

By integrating by parts, it follows that

\displaystyle I_{2}=

\displaystyle-4\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\nabla^{2}\sqrt{\rho}:\frac{\nabla\sqrt{\rho}\otimes\nabla\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho}}dx+2\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\frac{|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}|^{4}}{\rho}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2I_{1}+2B.

Therefore we

2D_{1}+D_{2}=2(A_{1}+B-I_{1})+A_{2}-B+2I_{2}=2A_{1}+A_{2}+B.

Last, by using
D_{2}\leq D_{1}
we obtain (
3.10
).

∎

4.
Global well-posedness of GCP solutions on
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}

The global well-posedness Theorem
1
of GCP solution to system (
1.1
) is proved by analyzing the existence
H^{2}_{x}
solutions to the NLS (
2.8
) and establishing the relationship between (
1.1
) and (
2.8
) by Proposition
13
.

In the 2-dimensional caes, we need the following logarithm
L^{\infty}
-embedding inequality, which was proved in
[
10
]
for the space
{\mathbf{R}}^{2}
. Here we present an improved version of this inequality on
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
.

Lemma 17
.

For any
u\in H^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
with
\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}udx=0
, we have

(4.1)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}({\mathbf{R}}^{2})}\leq C_{0}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{x}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}\left[1+\left|\log\left(\frac{\|\triangle u\|_{L^{2}_{x}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}}{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{x}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}}\right)\right|\right].

Proof.

Let
\{\hat{u}_{j,k}\}_{(j,k)\in{\mathbf{Z}}^{2}}
be the Fourier series of
u
on
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
, then by the assumption

\hat{u}_{0,0}=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}udx=0.

By Plancherel theorem, it follows that

(4.2)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}\leq|\hat{u}_{j,k}|_{l^{1}({\mathbf{Z}}^{2})},\;\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}=\left|\sqrt{j^{2}+k^{2}}\,\hat{u}_{j,k}\right|_{l^{2}({\mathbf{Z}}^{2})}

and

(4.3)

\|\triangle u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}=\left|(j^{2}+k^{2})\hat{u}_{j,k}\right|_{l^{2}({\mathbf{Z}}^{2})},

where
|\cdot|_{l^{p}({\mathbf{Z}}^{2})}
is the
p-
Lebesgue norm for series given by

|w_{j,k}|_{l^{p}({\mathbf{Z}}^{2})}=\sum_{(j,k)\in{\mathbf{Z}}^{2}}|w_{j,k}|^{p}.

For arbitrary
R>0
, by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (
4.2
), (
4.3
), we have

\displaystyle|\hat{u}_{j,k}|_{l^{1}({\mathbf{Z}}^{2})}=

\displaystyle\sum_{|(j,k)|<R,\ (j,k)\neq(0,0)}|\hat{u}_{j,k}|+\sum_{|(j,k)|\geq R}|\hat{u}_{j,k}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{|(j,k)|<R,\ (j,k)\neq(0,0)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{j^{2}+k^{2}}}|\sqrt{j^{2}+k^{2}}\,\hat{u}_{j,k}|

\displaystyle+\sum_{|(j,k)|\geq R}\frac{1}{j^{2}+k^{2}}|(j^{2}+k^{2})\hat{u}_{j,k}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}|\sqrt{j^{2}+k^{2}}\,\hat{u}_{j,k}|_{l^{2}({\mathbf{Z}}^{2})}\left(\sum_{|(j,k)|<R,\ (j,k)\neq(0,0)}\frac{1}{j^{2}+k^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+C_{0}|(j^{2}+k^{2})\hat{u}_{j,k}|_{l^{2}({\mathbf{Z}}^{2})}\left(\sum_{|(j,k)|\geq R}\frac{1}{(j^{2}+k^{2})^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}\log R+C_{0}\|\triangle u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}R^{-1}.

Now consider the function

g(R)=a\log R+bR^{-1},\quad a,b,R>0.

It is straightforward by

g^{\prime}(R)=(aR-b)R^{-2}

to obtain that
g(R)
has a unique minimum at
R_{0}=\frac{b}{a}
. Now we let
a=\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}
and
b=\|\triangle u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}
. If
\|\triangle u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}
, then we choose
R=1
, which implies

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}\leq C_{0}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}.

If
\|\triangle u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}>\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}
, then we can choose
R=\frac{\|\triangle u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}}{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}}>1
and prove (
4.1
).
∎

As the first step to establish the well-posedness result, we will consider the Cauchy problem of NLS (
2.8
),

(4.4)

\begin{cases}i{\partial}_{t}\psi+\frac{1}{2}\triangle\psi=f^{\prime}(|\psi|^{2})\psi+\frac{1}{\tau}S\psi+V\psi\\
-\triangle V=|\psi|^{2}-\mathcal{C}(x),\quad\psi(0)=\psi_{0}\end{cases}

for initial data
\psi_{0}\in H^{2}_{x}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
, where
S
is the phase function of
\psi
given in Definition
9
, and we solve for
\psi
such that
\inf_{t,x}|\psi|\geq\delta>0
. By Proposition
13
of the wave function lifting,
\psi
is associated to the hydrodynamic functions

\rho=|\psi|^{2},\quad v=\IM\left(\frac{\nabla\psi}{\psi}\right)

and the well-posedness result of (
4.4
) is equivalent to the one of the QHD system (
2.1
).

By using the logarithm embedding inequality (
4.1
), we have following estimates on the
L^{\infty}_{x}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
norm of
\psi
and
\rho-M_{0}
, which have essential importance in this section.

Lemma 18
.

Let
\psi\in H^{2}_{x}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
,
\rho=|\psi|^{2}
and
M_{0}=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho dx
, then we have two functions
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
such that

(4.5)

\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq M_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}+g_{1}(\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}_{x}},\|\triangle\psi\|_{L^{2}_{x}})

and

(4.6)

\|\rho-M_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq g_{2}(\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}_{x}},\|\triangle\psi\|_{L^{2}_{x}}),

where

g_{1}(u,w)=C_{0}u(1+|\log(w/u)|)

and

g_{2}(u,w)=C_{0}g_{1}(u,w)\cdot u[1+|\log(M_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}+g_{1}(u,w))|+|\log(w/u)|].

Proof.

Inequality (
4.5
) is a direct consequence of (
4.1
),

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq

\displaystyle\int_{T^{2}}|\psi|dx+C\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\left[1+\left|\log\left(\frac{\|\triangle\psi\|_{L^{2}_{x}}}{\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}_{x}}}\right)\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{T^{2}}|\psi|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\dots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}+g_{1}(\|\nabla\psi_{n}\|_{L^{2}_{x}},\|\nabla\psi\|_{H^{1}_{x}}).

Also by (
4.1
), we have

(4.7)

\|\rho-M_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq C_{0}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}_{x}}(1+|\log(\|\triangle\rho\|_{L^{2}_{x}}/\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}_{x}})|).

We further have

\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq 2\|\psi\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}_{x}},

and

\displaystyle\|\triangle\rho\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|\triangle\psi\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{4}_{x}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|\triangle\psi\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+C_{0}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|\triangle\psi\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

Substituting the estimates of
\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}_{x}}
,
\|\triangle\rho\|_{L^{2}_{x}}
into (
4.7
) and using (
4.5
), we obtain (
4.6
).
∎

Now we can prove the local existence of
H^{2}
solutions to (
4.4
) by a standard Picard iteration scheme.

Proposition 19
.

Let us assume the initial data
\psi_{0}\in H^{2}_{x}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
satisfy
\inf_{x}|\psi_{0}|\geq\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}>0
. There exists a
C_{*}(M_{0},\delta)>0
, where
M_{0}=\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}
, such that if

(4.8)

\|\nabla\psi_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{x}}\leq C_{*}(M_{0},\delta),

then the Cauchy problem (
4.4
) has a unique solution
\psi\in\mathcal{C}([0,T_{*});H^{2}_{x}({\mathbb{T}}^{2}))
local in time, for some
T_{*}>0
, with
\inf_{t,x}|\psi|\geq\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Proof.

For nonlinear Schrödinger equations without the Langevin term
\tau^{-1}S\psi
, the local well-posedness result is well-established in literature, see for example
[
31
]
and references therein. Therefore in this proof we only present the technical details concerning the term
\tau^{-1}S\psi
.

The proof follows a standard Picard iteration argument for solving an approximating sequence
\{\psi_{m}\}
for (
4.4
), by iteratively by solving
\psi_{m}
from the linear Schrödinger equation

(4.9)

i{\partial}_{t}\psi_{m}+\frac{1}{2}\triangle\psi_{m}=f^{\prime}(|\psi_{m-1}|^{2})\psi_{m}+\frac{1}{\tau}S_{m-1}\psi_{m}+V_{m-1}\psi_{m},

with
\psi_{m-1}
,
S_{m-1}
and
V_{m-1}
given in the previous step with
\psi_{m-1}\in\mathcal{C}([0,T_{*});H^{2}_{x}({\mathbb{T}}^{2}))
and
\inf_{t,x}|\psi_{m-1}|\geq\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}
. The Duhamel formula for (
4.9
) is written as

(4.10)

\displaystyle\psi_{m}=e^{\frac{i}{2}t\triangle}\psi_{0}

\displaystyle-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{i}{2}(t-s)\triangle}[f^{\prime}(|\psi_{m-1}|^{2})+V_{m-1}]\psi_{m}(s)ds

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{\tau}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{i}{2}(t-s)\triangle}S_{m-1}\psi_{m}(s)ds,

and the electric potential
V_{m-1}
is provided by the Poisson equation,

-\triangle V_{m-1}=|\psi_{m-1}|^{2}-M_{0}

with
\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}V_{m-1}dx=0
. For the phase function, we first set
S_{0}=0
. For
m\geq 1
, we want to give s suitable definition of
S_{m}
by adapting the idea of Definition
9
for
\psi_{m}
as follows. As before, we define the velocity and chemical potential as

(4.11)

v_{m}=\IM\left(\frac{\nabla\psi_{m}}{\psi_{m}}\right),\quad\mu_{m}=-\frac{1}{2}\RE\left(\frac{\triangle\psi_{m}}{\psi_{m}}\right)+f^{\prime}(|\psi_{m-1}|^{2})+V_{m-1}.

and we will see below this definition is consistent by the positivity of
|\psi_{m}|
. By (
4.9
) the equation of
v_{m}
is given by

(4.12)

\textrm{curl }v_{m}=0,\quad{\partial}_{t}v_{m}=-\nabla\left(\mu_{m}+\frac{1}{\tau}S_{m-1}\right).

Thus following the idea of (
2.14
), we define
S_{m}
to be the solution to the space-time gradient equation

(4.13)

\begin{cases}\nabla S_{m}=v_{m},\quad{\partial}_{t}S_{m}=-\mu_{m}-\frac{1}{\tau}S_{m-1}\\
S_{m}(0,0)=S_{*}\in[0,2\pi).\end{cases}

The "irrotationality" condition (
4.12
) guarantees the solvability of (
4.13
), and following the idea of Definition
9
, the phase function
S_{m}
can be explicitly written as

(4.14)

\displaystyle S_{m}(t,x)=

\displaystyle S_{*}+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\int_{l(0,x_{*})}v_{0}(y)\cdot d\vec{l}(y)dx_{*}

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}(\mu_{m}+\tau^{-1}S_{m-1})(s,x_{*})dx_{*}ds+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\int_{l(x_{*},x)}v_{0}(y)\cdot d\vec{l}(y)dx_{*}.

We claim that the sequence
\{\psi_{m}\}
satisfies the following properties on
[0,T_{*})\times{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
:

(1)

boundedness:
\|\nabla\psi_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}\leq 2\|\nabla\psi_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{x}}
and
\|S_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}}\leq C(\delta,\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{2}_{x}})
;

(2)

positivity:
\inf_{t,x}|\psi_{m}|\geq\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

By our assumption,
\psi_{0}
and
S_{0}
obviously satisfy the properties (1) and (2). In this paper we will only focus on the estimates related to the phase function
S_{m}
and the positivity property (2), and the remaining analysis following the standard argument for non-linear Schrödinger equation.

First, we determine the bound of the initial data
\|\nabla\psi_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}\leq C_{*}(M_{0},\delta)
, such that property (1) implies property (2). Let us define the density
\rho_{m}=|\psi_{m}|^{2}
and the velocity
v_{m}=\IM\left(\frac{\nabla\psi_{m}}{\psi_{m}}\right)
, then from (
4.9
) we obtain the equation of
\rho_{m}
as

{\partial}_{t}\rho_{m}+\diver(\rho_{m}v_{m})=0.

As a consequence, the total mass (and also the average density, since we assume
|{\mathbb{T}}^{2}|=1
)
\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho_{m}dx\equiv M_{0}
is conserved, then by (
4.6
) we have

|\rho_{m}-M_{0}|\leq g_{2}(\|\nabla\psi_{m}\|_{L^{2}_{x}},\|\triangle\psi_{m}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}),

and we can choose
C_{*}(M_{0},\delta)
small such that if
\|\nabla\psi_{m}\|_{H^{1}_{x}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}\leq C_{*}(M_{0},\delta)
holds, we have

|\rho_{m}-M_{0}|\leq g_{2}(\|\nabla\psi_{m}\|_{L^{2}_{x}},\|\triangle\psi_{m}\|_{L^{2}_{x}})\leq M_{0}-\delta,

which also implies

|\psi_{m}|\geq\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For the boundedness of
\|\nabla\psi_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}
, we can directly consider the
H^{2}_{x}
norm of
\psi_{m}
, as we have shown
\|\psi_{m}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\equiv M_{0}
. The last integral in (
4.10
) can be estimated as

\displaystyle\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{i}{2}(t-s)\triangle}S_{m-1}\psi_{m}(s)ds\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}}\leq

\displaystyle T_{*}\|S_{m-1}\psi_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}T_{*}\|S_{m-1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}}\|\psi_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}}.

Thus by the assumption of induction, we have

\frac{1}{\tau}\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{i}{2}(t-s)\triangle}S_{m-1}\psi_{m}(s)ds\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}}\leq C(\delta,\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{2}_{x}})\frac{T_{*}}{\tau}\|\psi_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}},

and we choose
T_{*}>0
small such that
C(\delta,\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{2}_{x}})\frac{T_{*}}{\tau}\leq\frac{1}{4}
. The other terms in (
4.10
) are treated similarly, thus we obtain

\|\psi_{m}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{2}_{x}}\leq\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{2}_{x}}+\frac{1}{2}\|\psi_{m}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{2}_{x}},

namely the first part of property (1).

Last, we prove the second part of property (1), namely
\|S_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}}\leq C(\delta,\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{2}_{x}})
. By
\nabla S_{m}=v_{m}
, we only need to control
\|S_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}
and
\|\nabla v_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}
.
From (
4.14
) we obtain

\displaystyle|S_{m}(t,x)|\leq

\displaystyle S_{*}+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|v_{0}|dx_{*}+\int_{0}^{t}\left|\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\mu_{m}dx_{*}\right|ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\tau^{-1}|S_{m-1}|dx_{*}ds+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|v_{m}|dx_{*}.

Recalling that
v_{m}=\IM(\frac{\nabla\psi_{m}}{\psi_{m}})
, by the bound of
\psi_{m}
and (2), we choose
C(\delta,\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{2}_{x}})
such that

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|v_{m}|dx_{*}\leq 2\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|\nabla\psi_{m}|dx_{*}\leq\frac{1}{4}C(\delta,\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{2}_{x}}).

Same bound holds for
\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|v_{0}|dx_{*}
. By the assumption of induction we have
\|S_{m-1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\leq C(\delta,\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{2}_{x}})
, therefore

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\tau^{-1}|S_{m-1}|dx_{*}ds\leq\frac{1}{4}C(\delta,\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{2}_{x}})

if we choose
T_{*}\leq\frac{\tau}{4}
. Last, by(
2.21
) we can choose
T_{*}>0
small such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left|\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\mu_{m}dx_{*}\right|ds\leq

\displaystyle 2\delta^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|\nabla\psi_{m}|^{2}dx_{*}ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|f^{\prime}(|\psi_{m-1}|^{2})|dx_{*}ds\leq\frac{1}{4}C(\delta,\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{2}_{x}}),

where in the last inequality we use

|f^{\prime}(|\psi_{m-1}|^{2})|\leq C(\|f\|_{C^{2}},\|\psi_{m-1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}})\leq C(\|f\|_{C^{2}},\|\psi_{m-1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}}).

Thus we prove
\|S_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\leq C(\delta,\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{2}_{x}})
. On the other hand, again by the definition of

, we have

\|\nabla v_{m}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L^{2}_{x}}\leq 2\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\triangle\psi_{m}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L^{2}_{x}}+2\delta^{-1}\|\nabla\psi_{m}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L^{4}_{x}}^{2},

where by embedding inequality,

\|\nabla\psi_{m}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L^{4}_{x}}^{2}\leq\|\psi_{m}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{2}_{x}}^{2}\leq 4\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{2}_{x}}^{2}.

Thus we finish the prove of property (1).

Proposition 21
.

Let
\psi\in L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};H^{2}_{x}({\mathbb{T}}^{2}))
be as given in Proposition
14
. Then for any
0\leq t<T^{*}

I(t)
satisfies

(4.16)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle I(t)+\frac{1}{4\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho\sigma^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|v|^{4}dx

\displaystyle C_{0}g_{3}(M_{0},E_{0})\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho\mu^{2}dx+C_{0}\delta^{-1}E_{0}[M_{0}+|f^{\prime}(\delta)|+I(t)]\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|v|^{2}dx,

where

(4.17)

g_{3}(M_{0},E_{0})=M_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}+M_{0}(M_{0}-\delta)^{4n-2}+M_{0}^{\frac{2}{3}}E_{0}^{\frac{1}{3}}.

Proof.

Let us recall (
3.5
) in Proposition
14
, the time derivative of functional

\frac{d}{dt}I(t)+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho\sigma^{2}dx=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\mu{\partial}_{t}p(\rho)dx+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho\mu{\partial}_{t}Vdx-\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|v|^{2}\mu dx,

The right hand side can be estimated in the following way. First we have

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\mu{\partial}_{t}p(\rho)dx\leq 2\|p^{\prime}(\rho)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|\sqrt{\rho}\mu\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|{\partial}_{t}\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

Recalling
p^{\prime}(\rho)=(2n-1)\rho(\rho-M_{0})^{2n-2}
, by definition
(
4.15
)
of
\psi\in X(T^{*})
,
it follows that

\|p^{\prime}(\rho)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq 2(2n-1)M_{0}(M_{0}-\delta)^{2n-2}.

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\mu{\partial}_{t}p(\rho)dx\leq

\displaystyle 2(2n-1)M_{0}(M_{0}-\delta)^{2n-2}\|\sqrt{\rho}\mu\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|{\partial}_{t}\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}M_{0}^{2}(M_{0}-\delta)^{4n-4}\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho\mu^{2}dx+\frac{1}{4\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho\sigma^{2}dx

For the integral
\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho\mu{\partial}_{t}Vdx
, we have

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho\mu{\partial}_{t}Vdx\leq 2\|\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{6}_{x}}\|\sqrt{\rho}\mu\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|{\partial}_{t}V\|_{L^{3}_{x}},

By the Poisson equation of
V
, we have
-\triangle{\partial}_{t}V={\partial}_{t}\rho
, then by embedding inequalities it follow that

\|{\partial}_{t}V\|_{L^{3}_{x}}\leq C_{0}\|{\partial}_{t}\rho\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x}}=2C_{0}\|\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{6}_{x}}\|{\partial}_{t}\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}},

and

\|\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{6}_{x}}\leq C_{0}\|\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{\frac{1}{3}}+C_{0}\|\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq C_{0}(M_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}+M_{0}^{\frac{1}{3}}E_{0}^{\frac{1}{6}})

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho\mu{\partial}_{t}Vdx\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}(M_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}+M_{0}^{\frac{1}{3}}E_{0}^{\frac{1}{6}})\|\sqrt{\rho}\mu\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|\sqrt{\rho}\sigma\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}(M_{0}+M_{0}^{\frac{2}{3}}E_{0}^{\frac{1}{3}})\tau\|\sqrt{\rho}\mu\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho\sigma^{2}dx.

For the last integral in the right hand side of (
3.5
), by recalling

\rho\mu=-\frac{1}{4}\triangle\rho+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\rho|v|^{2}+f^{\prime}(\rho)\rho+\rho V,

we write

\displaystyle-\tau^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|v|^{2}\mu dxds=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|v|^{2}\triangle\rho dx-\frac{1}{2\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|v|^{2}|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|v|^{4}dx-\tau^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}(f^{\prime}(\rho)+V)\rho v^{2}dx.

By integrating by parts, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|v|^{2}\triangle\rho dx=-\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\nabla\sqrt{\rho}\cdot(\sqrt{\rho}\nabla v)\cdot vdx

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}v\cdot\nabla(\sqrt{\rho}v)\cdot\nabla\sqrt{\rho}dx+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|v|^{2}|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}dx

Recall that
v=\nabla S
is irrotational, which implies

\textrm{curl }(\sqrt{\rho}v)=\nabla\sqrt{\rho}\times v,

then by Helmholtz decomposition, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\sqrt{\rho}v)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}\|\diver(\sqrt{\rho}v)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+C_{0}\|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}\times v\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}\|\sqrt{\rho}\sigma\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+C_{0}\||\nabla\sqrt{\rho}||v|\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

Therefore we obtain

\frac{1}{4\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|v|^{2}\triangle\rho dx\leq\frac{1}{8\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho\sigma^{2}dx+\frac{C_{0}}{\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|v|^{2}|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}dx.

Now we need to estimate the integral of
|v|^{2}|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}
. Recall that
\inf_{t,x}\rho\geq\delta
, the by Garliardo-Nirenberg inequality, we have

\displaystyle\delta\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|v|^{2}|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}dx\leq

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{\rho}v\|_{L^{4}_{x}}^{2}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{4}_{x}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}\|\sqrt{\rho}v\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|\nabla(\sqrt{\rho}v)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|\triangle\psi\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle+C_{0}\|\sqrt{\rho}v\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}\|\sqrt{\rho}v\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\left(\|\sqrt{\rho}\sigma\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\||\nabla\sqrt{\rho}||v|\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\right)\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|\triangle\psi\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle+C_{0}\|\sqrt{\rho}v\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2},

which implies

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|v|^{2}|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}dx\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}\delta^{-1}\|\sqrt{\rho}v\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|\sqrt{\rho}\sigma\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|\triangle\psi\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle+C_{0}\delta^{-1}\|\sqrt{\rho}v\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\|\triangle\psi\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+C_{0}\delta^{-1}\|\sqrt{\rho}v\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}.

Again by Proposition
13
we have

\frac{1}{2}\|\triangle\psi\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\leq I(t)+C_{0}E(t),

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|v|^{2}|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}dx\leq\frac{1}{8\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho\sigma^{2}dx+C_{0}\delta^{-1}E_{0}[1+I(t)]\tau^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|v|^{2}dx.

Last, again by the bound

, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}(f^{\prime}(\rho)+V)\rho v^{2}dx\leq

\displaystyle\|f^{\prime}(\rho)+V\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho v^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}(|f^{\prime}(\delta)|+M_{0})\tau^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho v^{2}dx.

Summarizing the inequalities above, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle I(t)+\frac{1}{4\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho\sigma^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|v|^{4}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}g_{3}(M_{0},E_{0})\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho\mu^{2}dx+C_{0}\delta^{-1}E_{0}[M_{0}+|f^{\prime}(\delta)|+I(t)]\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|v|^{2}dx,

which finishes the prove.

As a consequence of Proposition

Proposition 22
.

Let

(4.18)

F(t)\leq F(0)\exp\left(\frac{C_{0}}{\tau}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|v|^{2}dxds-c_{2}\tau t\right).

Proof.

By using (
3.4

(4.21)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}[F(t)+

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{c_{1}}{2\tau}-\frac{2\tau}{\delta}\right)\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|v|^{4}dx+\frac{\tau}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|\nabla^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle+4\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}p^{\prime}(\rho)|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}dx+\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}(\rho-M_{0})^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\tau}{4C_{0}nM_{0}E_{0}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho\mu^{2}dx+c_{1}I(t)\frac{C_{0}}{\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho v^{2}dx,

To prove the exponential decay (

itself by the dissipative terms.

From (

-2\sqrt{\rho}\mu=-\triangle\sqrt{\rho}+\sqrt{\rho}v^{2}+2(f^{\prime}(\rho)+V)\sqrt{\rho},

For the last integral, recall that

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}f^{\prime}(\rho)^{2}\rho dx=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho(\rho-M_{0})^{4n-2}dx\leq 2M_{0}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}(\rho-M_{0})^{4n-2}dx.

On the other hand, by

we have

\displaystyle 4\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}p^{\prime}(\rho)|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}dx=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}(2n-1)(\rho-M_{0})^{2n-2}|\nabla\rho|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2n-1)n^{-2}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|\nabla(\rho_{\tau}-M_{0})^{n}|^{2}dx.

Then by combining it with the bound of the internal energy and using Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}f^{\prime}(\rho)^{2}\rho dx\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}M_{0}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}(\rho-M_{0})^{2n}\right]^{2-\frac{1}{n}-\beta_{n}}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|\nabla(\rho-M_{0})^{n}|^{2}\right]^{\beta_{n}}

where

Combining it with the energy dissipation (

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}f^{\prime}(\rho)^{2}\rho dx\leq C_{0}nM_{0}E_{0}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}p^{\prime}(\rho)|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}dx

Also, by the Poisson equation of

and Poincaré inequality, we have

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho V^{2}dx\leq\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}V^{2}dx\leq C_{0}M_{0}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}(\rho-M_{0})^{2}dx.

Thus by our choice of
c_{1}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4C_{e}nM_{0}E_{0}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho\mu^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4C_{e}nM_{0}E_{0}}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}(\triangle\sqrt{\rho})^{2}dx+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|v|^{4}dx+4\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}f^{\prime}(\rho)^{2}\rho dx\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}(\triangle\sqrt{\rho})^{2}dx+\frac{1}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|v|^{4}dx

\displaystyle+2\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}p^{\prime}(\rho)|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}(\rho-M_{0})^{2}dx.

H(\rho)+E(t)
by the dissipative terms in (

). We recall the following technical lemma.

Lemma 23

(Logarithmic Sobolev inequality
[
18
]
)
.

Let
u\in H^{1}_{x}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
and
\overline{u^{2}}=|{\mathbb{T}}^{2}|^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}u^{2}dx
, then

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}u^{2}\log(u^{2}/\overline{u^{2}})dx\leq C_{0}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx.

By letting

in Lemma

and applying again Poincaré inequality, we obtain

H(\rho)\leq C_{0}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}dx\leq C_{0}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|\nabla^{2}\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}dx.

For the total energy
E(\rho)

also follows the inequality above. On the other hand, the internal energy and the electric potential can be bounded by

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}f(\rho)dx=\frac{1}{2n}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}(\rho-M_{0})^{2n}dx\leq\frac{C_{0}}{n}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|\nabla(\rho-M_{0})^{n}|^{2}dx,

\displaystyle+\frac{\tau}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}(\rho_{\tau}-M_{0})^{2}dx\geq c_{2}\tau[H(\rho)+E(t)].

\frac{c_{1}}{2\tau}-\frac{2\tau}{\delta}-\frac{\tau}{4}\geq 0,

\displaystyle\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\log\rho{\partial}_{t}\rho dx=-\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\log\rho\diver Jdx=2\tau\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\nabla\sqrt{\rho}\cdot(\sqrt{\rho}v)dx\leq 2\tau E(t)

\frac{1}{2}F(t)\leq F(t)+\tau\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\rho{\partial}_{t}\rho dx\leq\frac{3}{2}F(t).

Thus combining (

Proof of Theorem
3
.

By the decay estimate (
4.20
), on the time interval
[0,T^{*}]
of existence, we have

F(t)\leq F(0)\exp\left(\frac{C_{0}}{\tau}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho v^{2}dxds\right).

where the combination functional
F(t)
is given by (
4.19
). The energy dissipation (
3.4
) implies

\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|v|^{2}dxds\leq E_{0},

Then it follows (
2.22
) that

\displaystyle\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}\leq

\displaystyle[I(t)+C_{0}E(t)]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle[c_{1}^{-1}F(t)]^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq c_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}F(0)^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{C_{0}E_{0}}.

Therefore for initial data that
F(0)
is suitably small, we have

\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}\leq C_{*}(M_{0},\delta)

for all

For simplicity of the notation, in the later contents of this paper we still use

I_{\tau}(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\frac{1}{2}\rho_{\tau}(\mu_{\tau}^{2}+\tau^{2}\sigma_{\tau}^{2})dx.

H(\rho_{\tau})=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho_{\tau}\log\left(\frac{\rho_{\tau}}{M_{0}}\right)dx,

5.1.
The time-relaxation limit of the rescaled QHD system (

In this section, we will prove rigorously the time-relaxation limit as

in the framework of GCP solutions with positive density. Moreover, an explicit rate of convergence is given under the uniform boundedness assumption of the functional
I_{\tau}(t)
, and no smallness assumption is required. Let us emphasize again that in this section

We assume
\{(\rho_{\tau},v_{\tau})\}_{\tau\in\mathcal{I}}
to be a sequence of GCP solutions to the rescaled QHD system (

We first prove the following Proposition concerning the weak relaxation-time limit.

The conditions provided above imply

Since our internal energy

For the initial entropy

,

, and a limiting function

Now we turn to the proof of the explicit convergence rate.. As the main technical tool of this part, we use the method of relative entropy. Recall that the physical entropy

H(\rho)(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}g(\rho(t))dx,

\displaystyle H(\rho_{\tau})(t)-H(\bar{\rho})(t)-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}g^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})(\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho})dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}g(\rho_{\tau})-g(\bar{\rho})-g^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})(\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho})dx

with

g^{\prime}(s)=\log\left(\frac{s}{M_{0}}\right)+1.

By Taylor’s formula, we have

g(\rho_{\tau})-g(\bar{\rho})-g^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})(\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho})=\frac{1}{2}g^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{*})(\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho})^{2}

for some
\rho_{*}\in[\min\{\rho_{\tau},\bar{\rho}\},\max\{\rho_{\tau},\bar{\rho}\}]
, and
g^{\prime\prime}(s)=s^{-1}
. Moreover, densities
\rho_{\tau}
and
\bar{\rho}
have a lower bound

To estimate the relative entropy

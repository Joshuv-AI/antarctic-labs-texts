Morse index of min–max stationary integral varifolds
Mitchell Gaudet, Talant Talipov

Abstract
We prove an upper bound for the Morse index of min–max stationary integral varifolds realizing the d d -dimensional p p -width of a closed Riemannian manifold.

The main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

\omega_{p}^{d}(M,g)=\lVert S\rVert(M)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{index}(S)\leq p(n-d),

The reason why the bound is

As a corollary of Theorem

Theorem 1.3

Let
(M^{n},g)

there exist disjoint embedded stationary geodesic nets

\omega_{p}^{1}(M,g)=\sum_{i=1}^{N(p)}\operatorname{length}_{g}(G_{i})\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{N(p)}\mathrm{index}(G_{i})\leq p(n-1).

Outline.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we recall notation and Almgren–Pitts min–max theory. In Section
3.1
, we introduce a notion of
k
-unstable varifold, prove its equivalence with the usual Morse index for stationary geodesic nets, and record some properties. In Section
3.2
, we establish compactness properties for the space of
k
-unstable stationary integral varifolds. In Section
3.3
, we prove the Deformation Theorem and Theorem
1.2
. In Section
4
, we analyze
\omega^{1}_{1}(S^{n},g_{\operatorname{round}})
and prove that the bound must depend on
n
and that it is optimal for
\omega_{1}^{1}
.

We now sketch the proof of Theorem
1.2
. In the minimal hypersurface setting, Morse index bounds
[
34
]
use the Structure theorem
[
1
,
53
,
52
]
to deduce countability of smooth minimal hypersurfaces, which allows one to exclude high-index hypersurfaces inductively, one at a time. In higher codimension the Structure theorem is not available, so we instead use the hierarchical deformation developed in
[
29
,
46
]
, adapted to stationary integral varifolds.

Given a min–max sequence for
\omega^{d}_{p}
, we homotope its image away from the set of
\bigl(p(n-d)+1\bigr)
-unstable stationary integral
d
-varifolds. The main issue is that a homotopy with this property, while also preserving the min–max property, cannot be constructed in a single step. Instead, since the domains are simplicial complexes, we follow the standard procedure: we first define the homotopy on the
0
-cells and then induct on dimension. To construct the homotopy on higher-dimensional cells, we must ensure that the lower-dimensional homotopies are compatible; for this, we use a construction of Marques–Neves
[
34
]
and then follow an associated gradient flow, which pushes the image away from the
\bigl(p(n-d)+1\bigr)
-unstable varifolds.

2.2
Almgren–Pitts min–max theory

By Almgren’s isomorphism theorem
[
5
,
6
,
21
]
, the space of mod-
2

d
-cycles on the
n
-sphere,

\mathcal{Z}_{d}(S^{n};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
, is weakly homotopy equivalent to the Eilenberg–MacLane space
K(\mathbb{Z}_{2},n-d)
.
Let
\overline{\mu}\in H^{\,n-d}\big(\mathcal{Z}_{d}(S^{n};\mathbb{Z}_{2});\mathbb{Z}_{2}\big)
denote the nontrivial cohomology class.
All cup powers of
\overline{\mu}
are nontrivial, and the cohomology ring of
\mathcal{Z}_{d}(S^{n};\mathbb{Z}_{2})

is generated by these cup powers together with their Steenrod squares
[
23
]
.

Choose a smooth map
f\colon M\to S^{n}
that is a diffeomorphism from a small ball
B\subset M

onto
S^{n}\setminus\{p\}
and that sends
M\setminus B
to the point
p
. The induced map on cycle spaces

F\colon\mathcal{Z}_{d}(M;\mathbb{Z}_{2})\to\mathcal{Z}_{d}(S^{n};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
pulls back
\overline{\mu}
to a nontrivial class

\mu:=F^{*}(\overline{\mu})\in H^{\,n-d}\big(\mathcal{Z}_{d}(M;\mathbb{Z}_{2});\mathbb{Z}_{2}\big)
.

Let
X
be a finite simplicial complex. A continuous map
\Phi\colon X\to\mathcal{Z}_{d}(M;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is called a
p
-sweepout

if
\Phi^{*}(\mu^{p})\neq 0\in H^{\,p(n-d)}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
,
\Phi
satisfies the
no-concentration-of-mass property (cf.
[
31
,
33
]
), and
X
is a cubical subcomplex of
I(2p+1,q)
for some
q\geq 1
.
The collection of all such
p
-sweepouts is denoted
\mathcal{P}^{d}_{p}(M,g)
, the
p
-
admissible set
. The
d
-dimensional
p
-width is

\omega_{p}^{d}(M,g):=\inf_{\Phi\in\mathcal{P}^{d}_{p}}\sup_{x\in X}\mathbf{M}\bigl(\Phi(x)\bigr).

Definition 2.1
.

A
min–max sequence
for
\mathcal{P}^{d}_{p}
is a sequence of
p
-sweepouts
\{\Phi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{P}^{d}_{p}

such that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\sup_{x\in X_{i}}\mathbf{M}\bigl(\Phi_{i}(x)\bigr)=\omega_{p}^{d}(M,g).

Its
critical set
is

\mathbf{C}\bigl(\{\Phi_{i}\}\bigr):=\Big\{V\in\mathcal{V}_{d}(M):\|V\|(M)=\omega_{p}^{d}(M,g)\ \text{ and }\ V=\lim_{j}|\Phi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|\Big\},

where the limit is taken in the varifold sense.

We may assume the domains of the min–max sequence are finite simplicial complexes of dimension at most
p(n-d)
(see Lemma 2.25 in
[
48
]
).

Almgren’s theory implies that widths are realized by stationary integral varifolds that are almost minimizing in annuli
[
5
,
6
,
35
]
.

Theorem 2.2

(Min–max theorem for
d
-dimensional widths)
.

For each

there exists
S\in\mathcal{IV}_{d}(M)
that is stationary and almost minimizing in annuli such that

\omega_{p}^{d}(M,g)=\|S\|(M).

For

one has the
stronger regularity: stationary integral
1
-varifolds are stationary geodesic nets
[
5
,
6
,
8
,
40
,
43
,
42
]
. Combining this regularity with Lemma 2.5 of

]
yields the following.

Theorem 2.3

(Min–max theorem for

-dimensional widths)
.

For each

there exist disjoint embedded stationary geodesic nets
G_{i}:\Gamma_{i}\to M
, with
\Gamma_{i}
a good weighted multigraph for each

, such that

\omega_{p}^{1}(M,g)=\sum_{i=1}^{N(p)}\operatorname{length}_{g}(G_{i}).

Definition 2.4
.

The
Almgren–Pitts realization of the d-dimensional

-width

3.1
Unstable variations and index

We introduce a notion of
k
-unstable stationary integral varifolds from
[
34
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
S\in\mathcal{IV}_{d}(M)
be a stationary integral varifold and
\varepsilon\geq 0
. We say that
S
is
k
-unstable in an
\varepsilon
-neighborhood
if there exist
0<c_{0}<1
and a smooth family
\{F_{v}\}_{v\in\overline{B}^{k}}\subset\operatorname{Diff}(M)
with
F_{0}=\operatorname{Id},F_{-v}=F^{-1}_{v}
for all
v\in\overline{B}^{k}
such that, for any
V\in\overline{\textbf{B}}^{\mathbf{F}}_{2\varepsilon}(S)
, the smooth function

A^{V}:\overline{B}^{k}\rightarrow[0,+\infty),\quad A^{V}(v)=\lVert(F_{v})_{\#}V\rVert(M)

satisfies

•

A^{V}
has a unique maximum at
m(V)\in\overline{B}^{k}_{c_{0}/\sqrt{10}}(0)
;

•

-\frac{1}{c_{0}}\operatorname{Id}\leq D^{2}A^{V}(u)\leq-c_{0}\operatorname{Id}
for all
u\in\overline{B}^{k}
.

Here
(F_{v})_{\#}
denotes the push-forward operation. Notice that since
A^{V}
is strictly concave, the maximum is also the unique critical point. Therefore, since
S
is stationary, necessarily
m(S)=0
. If
V_{i}
tends to
V
in the
\mathbf{F}
-topology then
A^{V_{i}}
tends to
A^{V}
in the smooth topology
[
43
, Section 2.3(2)]
.

Notice that if
V_{i}\rightarrow V
then, by the prior remarks,
m(V_{i})\rightarrow m(V)
. Since the ball
\overline{\mathbf{B}}^{\mathbf{F}}_{2\varepsilon}(S)
is metrizable, the map
V\rightarrow m(V)
is continuous on it. Thus if
S
is stationary and
k
-unstable in a
0
-neighborhood then it is
k
-unstable in an
\varepsilon
-neighborhood for some
\varepsilon>0
.

.

Let
S\in\mathcal{IV}_{d}(M)
be a stationary integral varifold. We say that
S
is
k
-unstable
if it is
k
-unstable in an
\varepsilon
-neighborhood for some
\varepsilon>0
.

A^{S}

S
. Note

.

Let
G\in\mathcal{IV}_{1}(M)
be a stationary geodesic
\Gamma
-net with the set of vertices
\mathscr{V}
. We denote
\mathfrak{X}_{k}(G)
the space of continuous vector fields along
G
which are of class
C^{k}
along each edge. Observe that
\mathfrak{X}_{k}(G)
is always well-defined for
k\leq 2
and could be defined for larger values of
k
provided the restrictions of
G
to the edges have enough regularity. By Lemma 2.5 of
[
32
]
,
G
is equal in the varifold sense to a disjoint union of embedded stationary geodesic nets
G_{i}:\Gamma_{i}\to M
, where each
\Gamma_{i}
is a good weighted multigraph, for
1\leq i\leq P
. In what follows, we always regard
G
as equipped with this net structure.

Since the
G_{i}
are disjoint and embedded, every
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{2}(G)
can be identified with its pushforward, which is a section of
TM|_{G(\Gamma)}
. We will use the same notation for
X
and its pushforward.

We define
\mathfrak{C}_{k}(G)\subset\mathfrak{X}_{k}(G)
to be the subspace of vector fields with the property that, at each vertex
v\in\mathscr{V}
, they admit a local
C^{k}
extension to a neighborhood of
v
. The key point is that if
X\in\mathfrak{C}_{\infty}(G)
, then by Lemma 10.12 in
[
26
]
there exists a global smooth vector field
\tilde{X}
on
M
such that

X=\tilde{X}\big|_{G(\Gamma)}.

Let
Q(\cdot,\cdot)
be the bilinear form on
\mathfrak{X}_{2}(G)
given by the second variation of the length functional at
G
(see
[
49
]
for an explicit formula). Define
\operatorname{index}(G)
to be the maximal dimension of a subspace of
\mathfrak{C}_{\infty}(G)
on which
Q
is negative definite. Note that

\operatorname{index}(G)=\sum_{i=1}^{P}\operatorname{index}(G_{i}).

We now show that the two notions of instability agree for stationary geodesic nets.

Proposition 3.3
.

If
G\in\mathcal{IV}_{1}(M)
is a stationary geodesic net, then
G
is
k
-unstable if and only if
\operatorname{index}(G)\geq k
.

Proof.

Since

\operatorname{index}(G)=\sum_{i=1}^{P}\operatorname{index}(G_{i}),

it suffices to prove the statement when
G
is embedded.

Suppose
\operatorname{index}(G)\geq k
. Then there exist vector fields
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}\in\mathfrak{C}_{\infty}(G)
such that

Q(\sum_{i}a_{i}X_{i},\sum_{i}a_{i}X_{i})<0

for any
(a_{i})\neq 0\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. For
\delta>0
and each
i
take the extensions
\tilde{X}_{i}
as above. By construction, these extensions remain linearly independent on an open neighborhood of
G(\Gamma)
, and moreover
Q
is negative definite on
\tilde{X}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{X}_{k}
. Denote by
\phi^{X}_{t}
the flow of
X
and, for some
\varepsilon
to be chosen later, define

F_{v}(x)=F(v,x)=\phi_{\varepsilon}^{\sum_{i}v_{i}\tilde{X}_{i}}(x)

for
v\in\overline{B}^{k}
and
x\in M
. Then
F_{0}=\operatorname{Id},F_{-v}=F^{-1}_{v}
for all
v\in\overline{B}^{k},DA^{G}(0)=0
. Moreover, since

F(tv,x)=\phi_{\varepsilon}^{t\sum_{i}v_{i}\tilde{X}_{i}}(x)=\phi_{t}^{\varepsilon\sum_{i}v_{i}\tilde{X}_{i}}(x),

we have

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}F(tv,x)=\varepsilon\sum_{i}v_{i}\tilde{X}_{i}(x).

Hence
D^{2}A^{G}(0)<0
. By choosing
\varepsilon
sufficiently small, we conclude that
G
is
k
-unstable.

Now suppose
G
is
k
-unstable, and let
\{F_{v}\}_{v\in\overline{B}^{k}}
be the corresponding family of diffeomorphisms. Define
Y_{i}(x)=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mid_{t=0}F(te_{i},x)=\frac{\partial F}{\partial v_{i}}(0,x)
. Then

0>\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\mid_{t=0}\operatorname{length}_{g}\bigl(F_{tv}(G)\bigr)=Q(\sum_{i}v_{i}Y_{i},\sum_{i}v_{i}Y_{i}).

This means
\{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{k}\}
is linearly independent and spans a subspace of vector fields restricted to which
Q
is negative definite. Therefore
\operatorname{index}(G)\geq k
.
∎

For a stationary integral varifold

Lemma 3.6
.

If

\mathbf{F}(S,\tilde{S})<\varepsilon

Lemma 3.7
.

There exists
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,\varepsilon)
, such that for any
\tilde{\varepsilon}\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and

Proof.

Suppose not and there exists
V_{i}\in\mathbf{\overline{B}}^{\mathbf{F}}_{2\varepsilon_{i}}(S),\varepsilon_{i}\rightarrow 0
, and
v_{i}\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}
, such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}(V_{i},S)\leq 2\varepsilon_{i}\text{ and }(F_{v_{i}})_{\#}(V_{i})\in\mathbf{\overline{B}}^{\textbf{F}}_{2{\varepsilon}_{i}}(S).

By the compactness of
\mathbb{S}^{k-1}
, up to a subsequence, we may assume that
v_{i}\rightarrow v\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}
. Since
F_{v}
is smooth, we can take a limit as
i\rightarrow\infty
and obtain

(F_{v})_{\#}(S)=S.

However, by calculating the mass of both sides, we have the following inequality

\displaystyle\|S\|(M)-\|(F_{v})_{\#}S\|(M)

\displaystyle=A^{S}(0)-A^{S}(v)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{dt}A^{S}(tv)dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}A^{S}(sv)dsdt

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{t}D^{2}A^{S}(sv)(v,v)dsdt

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{t}c_{0}dsdt=\frac{c_{0}}{2}>0.

This implies that
(F_{v})_{\#}(S)\neq S
, giving a contradiction.
∎

In the remainder of this paper, for technical reasons, we always take
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{0}
in the definition of
k
-instability, and it is easy to check that the varifold is still
k
-unstable with the new
\varepsilon
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Given any fixed
V\in\mathbf{\overline{B}}^{\textbf{F}}_{2\varepsilon}(S)
, if an integral curve
x:[0,L]\rightarrow\overline{B}^{k}
disjoint from
m(V)
is defined by

\frac{d}{dt}x(t)=-f(t)\nabla A^{V}\bigl(x(t)\bigr),

where
f(t)
is a positive continuous function, then we have

A^{V}\bigl(x(L)\bigr)-A^{V}\bigl(x(0)\bigr)\leq-\frac{c_{0}}{2}|x(L)-x(0)|^{2}.

Proof.

By the fundamental theorem of calculus, we have

\displaystyle A^{V}

\displaystyle\bigl(x(L)\bigr)-A^{V}\bigl(x(0)\bigr)=\int_{0}^{L}\nabla A^{V}\bigl(x(t)\bigr)\cdot\frac{d}{dt}x(t)dt=-\int_{0}^{L}f(t)\Bigl|\nabla A^{V}\bigl(x(t)\bigr)\Bigr|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{0}^{L}f(t)\Bigl|\nabla A^{V}\bigl(x(t)\bigr)\Bigr|\Bigl(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{d}{ds}\Bigl|\nabla A^{V}\bigl(x(s)\bigr)\Bigr|ds\Bigr)dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{L}f(t)\Bigl|\nabla A^{V}\bigl(x(t)\bigr)\Bigr|\Bigl(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\nabla^{2}A^{V}\bigl(x(s)\bigr)\bigl(\nabla A^{V}\bigl(x(s)\bigr),\frac{d}{ds}x(s)\bigr)}{\Bigl|\nabla A^{V}\bigl(x(s)\bigr)\Bigr|}ds\Bigr)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{L}f(t)\Bigl|\nabla A^{V}\bigl(x(t)\bigr)\Bigr|\Bigl(\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\frac{\nabla^{2}A^{V}\bigl(x(s)\bigr)\bigl(\nabla A^{V}\bigl(x(s)\bigr),\nabla A^{V}\bigl(x(s)\bigr)\bigr)}{\Bigl|\nabla A^{V}\bigl(x(s)\bigr)\Bigr|}ds\Bigr)dt

\displaystyle\leq-c_{0}\int_{0}^{L}f(t)\Bigl|\nabla A^{V}\bigl(x(t)\bigr)\Bigr|\Bigl(\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\Bigl|\nabla A^{V}\bigl(x(s)\bigr)\Bigr|ds\Bigr)dt.

Here, we use the fact that
\nabla A^{V}(v)\neq 0
for any
v\neq m(V)
. Indeed, define the path
\gamma:[0,1]\to\overline{B}^{k}
as

\gamma(t)=m(V)+t(v-m(V)).

Consider the function:

g(t)=\langle\nabla A^{V}(\gamma(t)),v-m(V)\rangle.

By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus,

g(1)-g(0)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{dt}g(t)dt.

The derivative is computed using the chain rule:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}g(t)=\left\langle\frac{d}{dt}\left[\nabla A^{V}(\gamma(t))\right],v-m(V)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle D^{2}A^{V}(\gamma(t))\cdot\dot{\gamma}(t),v-m(V)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle D^{2}A^{V}(\gamma(t))(v-m(V)),v-m(V)\rangle

By the
k
-instability condition,

D^{2}A^{V}(u)\leq-c_{0}I\quad\forall u\in\overline{B}^{k},

which implies

\langle D^{2}A^{V}(\gamma(t))w,w\rangle\leq-c_{0}\lVert w\rVert^{2}\quad\forall w\in\mathbb{R}^{k}.

Applying this with
w=v-m(V)
:

\displaystyle g(1)-g(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\langle D^{2}A^{V}(\gamma(t))(v-m(V)),v-m(V)\rangle dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}-c_{0}\lVert v-m(V)\rVert^{2}dt

\displaystyle=-c_{0}\lVert v-m(V)\rVert^{2}.

Note that

\displaystyle g(0)

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla A^{V}(m(V)),v-m(V)\rangle=0\quad\text{(since $\nabla A^{V}(m(V))=0$)}

\displaystyle g(1)

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla A^{V}(v),v-m(V)\rangle

Thus

\langle\nabla A^{V}(v),v-m(V)\rangle\leq-c_{0}\lVert v-m(V)\rVert^{2}<0\quad\text{for}\quad v\neq m(V).

Now consider the function
l
satisfying

l^{\prime}(t)=\Bigl|\frac{d}{dt}x(t)\Bigr|=f(t)\Bigl|\nabla A^{V}(x(t))\Bigr|

with
l(0)=0
. Then

l(L)=\int^{L}_{0}l^{\prime}(t)dt=\int^{L}_{0}\Bigl|\frac{d}{dt}x(t)\Bigr|dt\geq\Bigl|\int^{L}_{0}\frac{d}{dt}x(t)dt\Bigr|=\Bigl|x(L)-x(0)\Bigr|.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle A^{V}\bigl(x(L)\bigr)-A^{V}\bigl(x(0)\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq-c_{0}\int^{L}_{0}l^{\prime}(t)\int_{0}^{t}l^{\prime}(s)dsdt=-c_{0}\int_{0}^{L}l^{\prime}(t)l(t)dt

\displaystyle=-\frac{c_{0}}{2}l(L)^{2}\leq-\frac{c_{0}}{2}\Bigl|x(L)-x(0)\Bigr|^{2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

There exists an increasing function

h_{S}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+},

such that for any
V\in\overline{\mathbf{B}}^{\mathbf{F}}_{2\varepsilon}(S)
, and
v,w\in\overline{B}^{k}
,

|v-w|\geq h_{S}\Bigl(\mathbf{F}\bigl((F_{v})_{\#}(V),(F_{w})_{\#}(V)\bigr)\Bigr)

Proof.

It suffices to show that for every
c>0
, if

\Bigl\{(v,w):\exists V\in\overline{\mathbf{B}}^{\mathbf{F}}_{2\varepsilon}(S),\mathbf{F}\bigl((F_{v})_{\#}(V),(F_{w})_{\#}(V)\bigr)=c\Bigr\}\neq\emptyset

then

\Bigl\{|v-w|:\exists V\in\overline{\mathbf{B}}^{\mathbf{F}}_{2\varepsilon}(S),\mathbf{F}\bigl((F_{v})_{\#}(V),(F_{w})_{\#}(V)\bigr)=c\Bigr\}

has a positive lower bound. We argue by contradiction. Suppose that there exists a sequence
V_{i}\in\overline{\mathbf{B}}^{\mathbf{F}}_{2\varepsilon}(S)
and

with
\mathbf{F}\bigl((F_{v_{i}})_{\#}(V_{i}),(F_{w_{i}})_{\#}(V_{i})\bigr)=c

∎

3.2
Compactness under index bounds

Definition 3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{U}(\omega^{d}_{p})
be the subset of
\mathcal{APR}_{p,d}
consisting of all
\bigl(p(n-d)+1\bigr)
-unstable stationary integral
d
-varifolds, and let
\mathcal{S}(\omega^{d}_{p})
denote its complement in
\mathcal{APR}_{p,d}
, i.e.
\mathcal{S}(\omega^{d}_{p})
is the subset of stationary integral
d
-varifolds with Morse index at most
p(n-d)
.

Remark 3.11
.

If
\mathcal{U}(\omega^{d}_{p})=\emptyset
, the main theorems are trivial, so we may assume
\mathcal{U}(\omega^{d}_{p})\neq\emptyset
.

Lemma 3.12
.

The set
\mathcal{U}(\omega^{d}_{p})
is a countable union of compact sets, i.e.

\mathcal{U}(\omega^{d}_{p})=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{U}_{i}(\omega^{d}_{p}),

where each
\mathcal{U}_{i}(\omega^{d}_{p})
is compact.

Proof.

By the compactness theorem for integral varifolds
[
4
]
,
\mathcal{APR}_{p,d}
in the set of
d
-varifolds is compact in varifold topology (equivalently, in
\mathbf{F}
-topology as
\mathcal{APR}_{p,d}
is uniformly mass bounded). We first show that
\mathcal{S}(\omega^{d}_{p})
is closed in
\mathcal{APR}_{p,d}
, hence compact.

Let
\{S_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{S}(\omega^{d}_{p})
be a sequence converging to
S\in\mathcal{APR}_{p,d}
in the
\mathbf{F}
-topology. We must prove
\operatorname{index}(S)\leq p(n-d)
. Suppose, to the contrary, that
S
is
\bigl(p(n-d)+1\bigr)
-unstable in an
\varepsilon
-neighborhood, with
\varepsilon>0
. Choose
i_{0}
large enough that
\mathbf{F}(S,S_{i})<\varepsilon
for all
i\geq i_{0}
. For such
i
,
S_{i}
is
\bigl(p(n-d)+1\bigr)
-unstable by Lemma
3.6
, contradicting
S_{i}\in\mathcal{S}(\omega^{d}_{p})
. Therefore
S
cannot be
\bigl(p(n-d)+1\bigr)
-unstable, so
\operatorname{index}(S)\leq p(n-d)
and
S\in\mathcal{S}(\omega^{d}_{p})
. This proves
\mathcal{S}(\omega^{d}_{p})
is closed.

If
\mathcal{S}(\omega^{d}_{p})=\emptyset
, then
\mathcal{U}(\omega^{d}_{p})=\mathcal{APR}_{p,d}
is compact and the lemma follows. Otherwise, for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
define

\mathcal{U}_{i}(\omega^{d}_{p}):=\{S\in\mathcal{U}(\omega^{d}_{p}):\ \mathbf{F}\bigl(S,\mathcal{S}(\omega^{d}_{p})\bigr)\geq 1/i\}.

Each
\mathcal{U}_{i}(\omega^{d}_{p})
is closed in the compact set
\mathcal{APR}_{p,d}
, hence compact. Since every
S\in\mathcal{U}(\omega^{d}_{p})
has positive
\mathbf{F}
-distance to the closed set
\mathcal{S}(\omega^{d}_{p})
, we have

\mathcal{U}(\omega^{d}_{p})=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{U}_{i}(\omega^{d}_{p}),

as required.
∎

3.3
Deformation theorem

Fix a sequence of stationary integral varifolds
\{S_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{U}(\omega^{d}_{p})
. By the definition of
\bigl(p(n-d)+1\bigr)
-instability, each
S_{k}
is associated with a quadruple
(\varepsilon_{k},c_{0,k},\{F^{k}_{v}\},m_{k})
.

For a nonempty set
K\subset\mathbb{N}^{+}
, define

\mathbf{B}^{\mathbf{F}}_{K}:=\mathbf{B}^{\mathbf{F}}_{K}\bigl(\{S_{k}\},\{\varepsilon_{k}\}\bigr)=\bigcap_{k\in K}\mathbf{B}^{\mathbf{F}}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(S_{k}),\qquad\bar{k}(K):=\min\{k\in K\}.

For
\lambda\geq 0
, set

\mathbf{B}^{\mathbf{F}}_{\lambda,K}:=\bigcap_{k\in K}\mathbf{B}^{\mathbf{F}}_{(2-2^{-\lambda})\varepsilon_{k}}(S_{k}).

In particular, for any
0\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}
,

\mathbf{B}^{\mathbf{F}}_{K}=\mathbf{B}^{\mathbf{F}}_{0,K}\subset\mathbf{B}^{\mathbf{F}}_{\lambda_{1},K}\subset\mathbf{B}^{\mathbf{F}}_{\lambda_{2},K}\subset\bigcap_{k\in K}\mathbf{B}^{\mathbf{F}}_{2\varepsilon_{k}}(S_{k}).

Let
K\subset\mathbb{N}^{+}
be nonempty and finite,
\lambda\geq 0
, and assume
\mathbf{B}^{\mathbf{F}}_{\lambda,K}\neq\emptyset
. For each

V\in\overline{\mathbf{B}}^{\mathbf{F}}_{\lambda,K}:=\bigcap_{\lambda^{\prime}>\lambda}\mathbf{B}^{\mathbf{F}}_{\lambda^{\prime},K},

define the gradient flow
\{\phi^{V}_{\lambda,K}(\cdot,t)\}_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathrm{Diff}(\overline{B}^{p(n-d)+1})
generated by the vector field

u\longmapsto-\,\mathbf{F}\!\left((F^{\bar{k}(K)}_{u})_{\#}(V),\ (\mathbf{B}^{\mathbf{F}}_{\lambda+1,K})^{c}\right)\,\nabla A^{V}_{\bar{k}(K)}(u),

where
A^{V}_{\bar{k}(K)}(u):=\bigl\|(F^{\bar{k}(K)}_{u})_{\#}(V)\bigr\|(M)
for
u\in\overline{B}^{p(n-d)+1}
.

We now prove two lemmas. The first asserts that the flow decreases mass
uniformly
, provided that we maintain a uniform distance from the set where
\nabla A^{V}_{\bar{k}(K)}=0
.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
\lambda>0
and
K\subset\mathbb{N}^{+}
be finite, and take
\eta>0
sufficiently small. Then there exist constants
c(\lambda,K)>0
and
T>0
such that the following holds: for any
V\in\overline{\mathbf{B}}^{\mathbf{F}}_{\lambda,K}
and any
v\in\overline{B}^{p(n-d)+1}
with
|v-m_{\bar{k}(K)}(V)|\geq\eta
and

(F_{v}^{\bar{k}(K)})_{\#}(V)\in\overline{\mathbf{B}}^{\mathbf{F}}_{\lambda+0.5,K},

we have

A^{V}_{\bar{k}(K)}\bigl(\phi^{V}_{\lambda,K}(v,T)\bigr)<A^{V}_{\bar{k}(K)}\bigl(\phi^{V}_{\lambda,K}(v,0)\bigr)-c(\lambda,K).

Proof.

We may assume
\overline{\mathbf{B}}^{\mathbf{F}}_{\lambda,K}\neq\emptyset
, otherwise the statement is trivial. Note that
\overline{\mathbf{B}}^{\mathbf{F}}_{\lambda,K}
is compact.

For
l\in\mathbb{R}
, set

D_{l}(V):=\bigl\{\,v\in\overline{B}^{p(n-d)+1}:\ (F_{v}^{\bar{k}(K)})_{\#}(V)\in\overline{\mathbf{B}}^{\mathbf{F}}_{\lambda+l,K}\,\bigr\}

and

d(V):=\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{Euc}}\bigl(D_{0.5}(V),\,\partial D_{1}(V)\bigr).

Each
D_{l}(V)
is closed for
l>0
because
v\mapsto(F_{v}^{\bar{k}(K)})_{\#}(V)
is continuous in the varifold topology.

Step 1.
We show
d(V)
has a uniform positive lower bound.

Part 1:
d(V)>0
.

If
w\in\partial D_{1}(V)
, then by continuity either
w\in\partial\overline{B}^{p(n-d)+1}=\mathbb{S}^{p(n-d)}
or
(F_{w}^{\bar{k}(K)})_{\#}(V)\in\partial\overline{\mathbf{B}}^{\mathbf{F}}_{\lambda+1,K}
. The first case is excluded by Lemma
3.7
. In the second case, since
\partial\overline{\mathbf{B}}^{\mathbf{F}}_{\lambda+1,K}\cap\overline{\mathbf{B}}^{\mathbf{F}}_{\lambda+0.5,K}=\emptyset
, we must have
w\notin D_{0.5}(V)
. Thus
d(V)>0
.

Part 2: Uniform positivity.

By compactness there is a uniform lower bound: if not, there would exist
V_{i}\to V
in
\overline{\mathbf{B}}^{\mathbf{F}}_{\lambda+0.5,K}
with
d(V_{i})\to 0
. Compactness yields points
v_{i,0.5}\in D_{0.5}(V_{i})
and
v_{i,1}\in\partial D_{1}(V_{i})
achieving
d(V_{i})=|v_{i,0.5}-v_{i,1}|
. Passing to a subsequence,
v_{i,0.5},v_{i,1}\to v\in\overline{B}^{p(n-d)+1}
. Since
D_{l}(V_{i})
and
\partial D_{l}(V_{i})
are closed for
l>0
, we get
v\in D_{0.5}(V)\cap\partial D_{1}(V)
, hence
d(V)=0
, a contradiction. Therefore
d_{0}:=\inf_{V}d(V)>0
.

Step 2.
Define

P:=\bigl\{(V,v)\in\overline{\mathbf{B}}^{\mathbf{F}}_{\lambda,K}\times\overline{B}^{p(n-d)+1}:\ v\neq m_{\bar{k}(K)}(V),\ v\in D_{0.5}(V)\bigr\}.

For any
(V,v)\in P
, the function
t\mapsto A^{V}_{\bar{k}(K)}\bigl(\phi^{V}_{\lambda,K}(v,t)\bigr)
is strictly decreasing for
t>0
. Set

\delta A_{\lambda,K}(V,v):=\lim_{t\to\infty}\bigl(A^{V}_{\bar{k}(K)}(\phi^{V}_{\lambda,K}(v,0))-A^{V}_{\bar{k}(K)}(\phi^{V}_{\lambda,K}(v,t))\bigr)>0.

We claim there exists
c=c(\lambda,K)>0
such that
\delta A_{\lambda,K}(V,v)\geq 2c
for all
(V,v)\in P
. Indeed, by Lemma
3.8
,

A^{V}_{\bar{k}(K)}(\phi^{V}_{\lambda,K}(v,0))-A^{V}_{\bar{k}(K)}(\phi^{V}_{\lambda,K}(v,t))\ \geq\ \frac{c_{0,\bar{k}(K)}}{2}\,\bigl|\phi^{V}_{\lambda,K}(v,0)-\phi^{V}_{\lambda,K}(v,t)\bigr|^{2}.

Since
\phi^{V}_{\lambda,K}(v,t)
approaches
\partial D_{\mathrm{1}}(V)
as
t\to\infty
, we obtain

\delta A_{\lambda,K}(V,v)\ \geq\ d(V)^{2}\ \geq\ d_{0}^{2}>0.

Step 3.
It remains to find
T=T(\eta,K,\lambda)>0
so that for all
(V,v)\in P
with
|v-m_{\bar{k}(K)}(V)|\geq\eta
,

A^{V}_{\bar{k}(K)}\bigl(\phi^{V}_{\lambda,K}(v,T)\bigr)<A^{V}_{\bar{k}(K)}\bigl(\phi^{V}_{\lambda,K}(v,0)\bigr)-c(\lambda,K).

Arguing by contradiction as in
[
34
, Lemma 4.5]
, assume there exist
(V_{i},v_{i})\in P
with
|v_{i}-m_{\bar{k}(K)}(V_{i})|\geq\eta
such that

A^{V_{i}}_{\bar{k}(K)}\bigl(\phi^{V_{i}}_{\lambda,K}(v_{i},t)\bigr)\ \geq\ A^{V_{i}}_{\bar{k}(K)}\bigl(\phi^{V_{i}}_{\lambda,K}(v_{i},0)\bigr)-c(\lambda,K)\quad\text{for all }t\in[0,i].

By compactness,
V_{i}\to V
and
v_{i}\to v
with
(V,v)\in P
and
|v-m_{\bar{k}(K)}(V)|\geq\eta
. Passing to the limit yields

A^{V}_{\bar{k}(K)}\bigl(\phi^{V}_{\lambda,K}(v,t)\bigr)\ \geq\ A^{V}_{\bar{k}(K)}\bigl(\phi^{V}_{\lambda,K}(v,0)\bigr)-c(\lambda,K)\quad\text{for all }t\geq 0,

which contradicts Step 2. The lemma follows.
∎

Of course, we need a way to perturb “bad”
d
-varifolds away from the critical set
m_{\bar{k}(K)}(V)
. We will use a construction of Marques–Neves to do this.

Notice that, while defining and proving the perturbation, our setting is slightly different from that of
[
34
, Theorem 5.1]
. We shall prove it in sufficient generality to encompass both situations.

The basic topological idea is the following. We are given a map
\theta:X^{l}\rightarrow B^{N+1}
from an
l
-dimensional simplicial complex to a
N+1
-dimensional ball, which we want to uniformly avoid, for some positive integers
l
and
N
. Since the ball is convex, we can define a homotopy between the constant
0
map and any other map into
B^{N+1}
by linear interpolation up to time
t=1
.

In defining the homotopy at
t=1
, we need to construct a map
b:X^{l}\rightarrow B^{N+1}
that maps each face
f
into a small
N+1
-dimensional ball
B^{N+1}(p_{f})
centered at a point
p_{f}
, possibly with the point
\{0\}
removed. As is standard in this context, we construct
b
by induction over the skeleta of increasing dimension. Recall from
[
13
, Theorem 7.1]
that we can extend this function continuously from the boundary of a
(j+1)
-face
e
to
e
if and only if the corresponding obstruction cocycle
\alpha\in C^{j+1}\bigl(e,\pi_{j}(B^{N+1}(p_{e})\setminus\{0\})\bigr)
vanishes.

To guarantee this, notice that
\pi_{j}(B^{N+1}(p_{e})\setminus\{0\})
is either equal to
0
(if
0\not\in B^{N+1}(p_{e})
) or to
\pi_{j}(\mathbb{S}^{N})
otherwise. Therefore, if
j<N
all obstruction cocycles vanish, and so we may extend.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
N>0
be an integer, let
V\in\mathcal{IV}_{d}(M)
be a varifold, and let
\varepsilon>0
. Suppose we are given

1.

l\in\{0,\dots,N\}
and
\delta>0
;

2.

an
l
-dimensional finite simplicial complex
X^{l}
;

3.

a continuous map
\Psi:X^{l}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbf{B}}^{\mathbf{F}}_{2\varepsilon}(V)
;

4.

a continuous map
\phi:\overline{\mathbf{B}}^{\mathbf{F}}_{2\varepsilon}(V)\rightarrow\overline{B}^{N+1}
.

Then there exists a homotopy
\hat{H}:X^{l}\times[0,1]\rightarrow\overline{B}^{N+1}_{\delta}
such that

1.

\hat{H}(x,0)=0
for all
x\in X^{l}
;

2.

\inf_{x\in X^{l}}\bigl|\phi(\Psi(x))-\hat{H}(x,1)\bigr|\geq\eta>0

for some constant
\eta>0
.

Remark 3.15
.

This lemma could actually be stated without referring to varifolds at all, and only in terms of continuous maps from
l
-dimensional simplicial complexes to
B^{i}
with
i>l
(see the discussion prior to the lemma). However, the situation we care about is when
\phi=m_{\bar{k}(K)}
, so it is convenient to phrase it in this way.

Proof.

We start with the same reduction as in
[
34
, Theorem 5.1]
: by repeated barycentric subdivision of the finite simplicial complex and by the uniform continuity of
\phi\circ\Psi
and
\Psi
, we may assume that, for each closed face
e\subseteq X^{l}
, it holds that
\operatorname{diam}_{\mathbf{F}}\bigl(\Psi(e)\bigr)<\alpha
and, if
x,y
lie in the same face of
X^{l}
, then

\bigl|\phi(\Psi(x))-\phi(\Psi(y))\bigr|<\alpha.

Here
\alpha=\min\{\delta 2^{-(N+2)},\,\varepsilon/4\}
.

Now let
(X^{l})^{(i)}
denote the
i
-skeleton. It is clear that, if we define a map
a:X^{l}\rightarrow B^{N+1}_{\delta}
satisfying

\inf_{x\in X^{l}}\bigl|\phi(\Psi(x))-a(x)\bigr|\geq\eta>0,

then we can define the required homotopy by setting
\hat{H}(x,t)=t\,a(x)
.

We also notice that it is enough to define a function
b:X^{l}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N+1}\setminus\{0\}
such that

b(x)\in B^{N+1}_{\delta}\bigl(-\phi(\Psi(x))\bigr),

and then define
a=b+\phi\circ\Psi
. We will define this
b
inductively on the skeleta, as we will do later in the proof of the Deformation theorem.

Clearly, we can define

b:(X^{l})^{(0)}\rightarrow B^{N+1}_{\delta 2^{-N}}\bigl(-\phi(\Psi(x))\bigr)\setminus\{0\}

on the
0
-skeleton.

Suppose we have defined

b:(X^{l})^{(j)}\rightarrow B^{N+1}_{\delta 2^{-N+j}}\bigl(-\phi(\Psi(x))\bigr)\setminus\{0\}

for some
j\in\{0,\dots,N-1\}
; we will construct it on the
(j+1)
-skeleton. Pick a
j
-face
e
of
X^{l}
with center
y_{e}
. Then, by construction,

b:\partial e\rightarrow B^{N+1}_{\delta 2^{-N+j}+\alpha}\bigl(-\phi(\Psi(y_{e}))\bigr)\setminus\{0\}.

Recall that the homotopy group
\pi_{j}(\mathbb{S}^{N})=0
since
j<N
. Therefore, all obstruction cocycles to extending
b
to a map

b:e\rightarrow B^{N+1}_{\delta 2^{-N+j}+\alpha}\bigl(-\phi(\Psi(y_{e}))\bigr)\setminus\{0\}

vanish. Once again, by our subdivision we obtain

b(x)\in B^{N+1}_{\delta 2^{-N+j}+2\alpha}\bigl(-\phi(\Psi(x))\bigr)\setminus\{0\},

but by our choice of parameters we have
\delta 2^{-N+j}+2\alpha<\delta 2^{-N+j+1}
, and so the induction closes.
∎

As an immediate corollary we have the following.

Corollary 3.16
.

Let
\lambda>0
, and let

\sup_{S\in\mathcal{U}_{m}(\omega^{d}_{p})}a_{q+1}(S)\leq\tilde{a}^{m}_{q+1}
.

Proof.

Since

Then

, so

uniformly on

.

Argue by contradiction: suppose there exist

with
\|V_{i}\|(M)<\omega^{d}_{p}-\tilde{\varepsilon}^{m}_{q}

\mathcal{U}_{m}(\omega^{d}_{p})

Claim 4.
If
\Theta(x)\notin\mathbf{B}^{\mathbf{F}}_{\eta(S)/a_{q}(S)}(S)

\|\Theta(x)\|(M)-\omega^{d}_{p}\leq\varepsilon_{q}(S)
, then

Proof.

For

, since

and by the third bullet of

,

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}\bigl(H^{\Theta,\delta}_{\lambda,K}(x,t),S\bigr)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbf{F}\bigl(\Theta(x),S\bigr)-\mathbf{F}\bigl(H^{\Theta,\delta}_{\lambda,K}(x,t),\Theta(x)\bigr)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\eta(S)}{a_{q}(S)}-\delta\ \geq\ \frac{3\,\eta(S)}{4\,a_{q}(S)}\ \geq\ \frac{\eta(S)}{a_{q+1}(S)}.

For
t\in(1/2,1]
, we have
\delta<\varepsilon_{q}(S)
, hence by the third bullet of
Step 2
,

\|H^{\Theta,\delta}_{\lambda,K}(x,1/2)\|(M)\leq\omega^{d}_{p}+2\varepsilon_{q}(S)
. If, toward a contradiction, there exists

, let

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}\!\Big(\bigl((F^{k}_{\cdot})_{\#}(\Theta(x))\bigr)^{-1}\!\bigl(

\displaystyle H^{\Theta,\delta}_{\lambda,K}(x,t_{1})\bigr),\ \bigl((F^{k}_{\cdot})_{\#}(\Theta(x))\bigr)^{-1}\!\bigl(H^{\Theta,\delta}_{\lambda,K}(x,1/2)\bigr)\Big)

4
Optimality of the bound

In this section, we prove that the index bound must depend on

and that it is optimal for
\omega_{1}^{1}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

For any
n\geq 2
,
\omega_{1}^{1}(S^{n},g_{\operatorname{round}})=2\pi
and is realized by closed equatorial geodesics of Morse index
n-1
.

Proof.

By Theorem 1 in
[
44
]
, we have
\omega_{1}^{1}(S^{n},g_{\operatorname{round}})\leq 2\pi
. Suppose, for contradiction, that
\omega_{1}^{1}(S^{n},g_{\operatorname{round}})<2\pi
. Then Theorem
2.3
implies that there exists a stationary geodesic net
G
on
(S^{n},g_{\operatorname{round}})
with
\operatorname{length}_{g}(G)<2\pi
. Proposition 3.6 in
[
2
]
implies that the density of
G
is less than
1
everywhere, which is a contradiction. Hence
\omega_{1}^{1}(S^{n},g_{\operatorname{round}})=2\pi
.

Let
G
be a stationary geodesic net of length
\omega_{1}^{1}(S^{n},g_{\operatorname{round}})
. Since
\omega_{1}^{1}(S^{n},g_{\operatorname{round}})=2\pi<4\pi
, Proposition 3.6 in
[
2
]
implies that the density of
G
is less than
2
everywhere. By Corollary 3.3 in
[
2
]
, only triple junctions can occur in
G
. However, triple junctions are also impossible, since the density at such junctions is not an integer, which contradicts Theorem 4.13 in
[
2
]
. This implies that the only realizations of
\omega_{1}^{1}(S^{n},g_{\operatorname{round}})
are equatorial closed geodesics. Their Morse index is equal to
n-1
(see Example 2.5.7 in
[
25
]
).

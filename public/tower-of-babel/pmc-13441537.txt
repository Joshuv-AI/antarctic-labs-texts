Culture medium modulates nucleolar architecture and disrupts ribosomal RNA levels in preimplantation mouse embryos
Authors: MD Wasim BARI, Jannatul Ferdous JHARNA, Norermi Firzana ALFIAN, Shiori ISHIYAMA, Kazuki MOCHIZUKI, Satoshi KISHIGAMI
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractEmbryonic culture media support preimplantation development in vitro, yet their broader effects on the molecular organization have not been fully characterized. In mammalian embryos, the nucleolus is a prominent and highly dynamic organelle that orchestrates ribosome biogenesis through RNA polymerase I-dependent ribosomal DNA (rDNA) transcription. However, the effect of culture conditions on nucleolar organization and rRNA dynamics during early development remains poorly understood. In this study, we investigated the effects of two compositionally distinct culture media on the nucleolar architecture and rRNA levels in mouse preimplantation embryos cultured in vitro. In vitro fertilized embryos were cultured in either Chatot-Ziomek-Bavister (CZB) or α-minimal essential medium (α-MEM). The nucleolar morphology was assessed by analyzing the localization of the nucleolar protein NOPP140 and rRNA levels were quantified by immunostaining the embryos with the Y10b antibody clone. Quantitative analyses showed that embryos cultured in α-MEM exhibited a significantly higher proportion of single and enlarged nucleoli per blastomere than those cultured in CZB (P < 0.05). However, α-MEM cultured embryos displayed a significant reduction in Y10b signal intensity relative to CZB-cultured embryos (P < 0.05). Although developmental rates were similar between the culture conditions, embryos cultured in α-MEM displayed altered cell lineage allocation compared to those cultured in CZB. Together, these findings indicate that culture medium regulates nucleolar organization and rRNA abundance in mouse preimplantation embryos, with nucleolar plasticity serving as a sensitive molecular indicator of in vitro culture-dependent developmental effects.Keywords: Culture media, In vitro fertilization (IVF), Nucleolus, rRNA

Embryonic cells are highly vulnerable. However, their developmental plasticity allows them to adapt to diverse environmental conditions by altering their metabolic reprogramming [1]. Nutrient availability is a key regulator of embryonic metabolic plasticity and cellular reprogramming, which are distinct from those in somatic cells [2, 3]. During preimplantation development, in utero nutrition influences genome activation, lineage formation, and ribosome biogenesis and is strongly associated with the risk of chronic diseases in adulthood [3, 4]. Formulations of embryo culture media have transitioned from rudimentary to the highly optimized, incorporating diverse energy sources, including proteins and amino acids, to mimic the in vivo milieu [5, 6]. Under in vitro conditions, the culture medium composition plays a decisive role in shaping embryonic growth and viability [7]; therefore optimizing the medium is essential for ensuring normal preimplantation development, enabling successful implantation, and improving pregnancy outcomes.

In mammals, the preimplantation period is characterized by intense cellular remodeling and dynamic epigenetic reprogramming, during which the nucleoli serve as structural platforms. As development progresses, nucleolar maturation represents a key event associated with initiation of ribosomal DNA (rDNA) transcription and ribosome biogenesis [8]. Over the last few decades, considerable interest has focused on understanding the formation and function of the embryonic nucleolus, its associated proteins, and rRNA dynamics in in vitro fertilization (IVF)-derived preimplantation embryos [9, 10]. In mammals, the nucleolar number, size, spatial distribution, and migration dynamics serve as important parameters to assess the embryo developmental competence of the embryo and predict live birth outcomes; however, the molecular mechanisms underlying the formation of distinct nucleolar morphologies remain poorly understood [11, 12]. Although the nucleolus occupies only a small fraction of the oocyte volume, it contains a substantial amount of protein. A single nucleolus is estimated to contain approximately 1.6 ng protein, highlighting its highly concentrated molecular environment that acts as a regulatory hub in the developmental trajectory of the early embryo [13, 14]. Studies on cloned embryos have shown that abnormal nucleolar morphology and disrupted nucleolar protein distribution are associated with developmental failure and defective embryonic genome activation (EGA) [15].

As a membrane-less organelle, the nucleolus exhibits remarkable structural plasticity and can adopt diverse morphological states in response to environmental stress, a phenomenon well documented in multiple somatic cell types [16]. Various stressors markedly alter nucleolar size and volume, inhibiting RNA polymerase I-mediated rRNA synthesis [17]. The nucleolus is a tripartite organelle in which fibrillar centers (FCs), dense fibrillar components (DFCs) and granular components (GCs) coordinate successive steps of ribosome biogenesis via liquid-liquid phase separation (LLPS). Previous studies have demonstrated that nascent rRNA influences nucleolar structure, highlighting the close relationship between rRNA production and nucleolar organization [18]. In porcine embryos, maternally inherited components contribute to nucleolar reorganization and support the onset of rRNA related nucleolar activity during the oocyte-to-embryo transition, although the requirement for the maternal nucleolus appears to be species-specific, as mouse embryos can re-establish nucleoli de novo [19,20,21]. The dynamic regulation of rDNA transcription plays a critical role in mammalian development; and disruption of this process impairs ribosome biogenesis and results in tissue-specific developmental defects particularly craniofacial disorders [22]. Understanding how environmental and nutritional factors shape nucleolar dynamics during the preimplantation period is crucial for improving embryo culture conditions and identifying the early molecular drivers of developmental programming.

During in vitro handling, embryos are subjected to mechanical manipulation and transient fluctuations in temperature and pH, which can activate cellular stress responses that may act independently or synergistically to exacerbate negative effects on embryonic development. Culture conditions, particularly the embryo culture medium, are major determinants of preimplantation development. Embryos exhibit developmental plasticity that enables them to adapt to the environmental conditions, and these adaptations potentially influence the long-term outcomes [23]. Culture medium is directly associated with physical stress, such as osmotic stress, pH, and the metabolic, molecular, and epigenetic states of embryos [24, 25]. However, the mechanism by which culture medium induces nucleolar stress and alters nucleolar organization remains unclear.

In this study, the Chatot-Ziomek-Bavister (CZB) medium was selected as the reference culture medium because of its relatively simple formulation and its ability to support both normal embryonic development and adult physiological outcomes [26]. However, accumulating evidence indicates that embryos exposed into α-minimal essential medium (α-MEM), which has a more complex composition, during the preimplantation period exert long-lasting effects on adult physiology, including metabolic and inflammatory phenotypes, suggesting that culture medium composition perturbs development during the preimplantation stage [27, 28]. In this study, we hypothesized that α-MEM exposure influences early embryonic characteristics by altering the nucleolar organization and rRNA levels in IVF embryos.

Materials and Methods

Animals

Female and male ICR mice (8–12 weeks old) were obtained from the Shizuoka Laboratory Animal Center (SLC Inc., Hamamatsu, Japan). Animals were housed under specific pathogen-free (SPF) conditions at 25°C with 50% relative humidity and a 14 h light/10 h dark cycle. The mice were provided a standard pelleted diet and distilled water ad libitum. All experimental procedures were reviewed and approved by the Animal Experimentation Committee of the University of Yamanashi, Japan, and conducted in compliance with the institutional ethical standards (Approval Number: A6-19).

Reagents

All reagents were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich Chemical Co. (St. Louis, MO, USA), FUJIFILM Wako Pure Chemical Co. (Osaka, Japan), and Nacalai Tesque Inc. (Kyoto, Japan).

Preparation of culture medium

The CZB medium was prepared in our laboratory according to a previously described protocol [29]. After preparation, the medium was dispensed into 5 ml plastic tubes (Assist tube, 60.9921.530S, Sarstedt K.K., Tokyo, Japan) that were tightly capped and stored at 4°C until further experimental use. The embryos were always cultured in fresh CZB and α-MEM medium. α-MEM was purchased from FUJIFILM Wako Pure Chemical Co. (catalog number: 135-15175) and replenished after one month of storage. After opening a new α-MEM bottle, the medium was dispensed into 5 ml plastic tubes under a laminar flow hood. The composition of the culture media is listed in Supplementary Table 1.

IVF

IVF was performed using the method described previously [30]. For IVF, cauda epididymal sperms were collected from male ICR mice and capacitated in human tubal fluid (HTF) medium for 45 min [31]. Following capacitation, sperm counts were quantified and a concentration of 1× 106 sperm/ml was used for IVF. Cumulus-oocyte complexes (COCs) were collected from super ovulated ICR female mice induced by injecting 5 IU pregnant mare serum gonadotropin (PMSG) and 5 IU human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) (ASKA Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., Tokyo, Japan), administered 48 h apart. Fertilization was assessed 6h after the initiation of insemination (sperm-oocyte co-incubation) by morphological evaluation under an inverted microscope (IX73, Olympus, Tokyo, Japan), and confirmed by the presence of two distinct pronuclei and extrusion of the second polar body. The zygotes were temporarily placed in fresh droplets for further experiments after several washes in CZB.

In vitro embryo culture

Following fertilization assessment, the zygotes were washed several times in CZB, transferred to α-MEM or CZB culture medium, and cultured in paraffin oil (Nacalai Tesque; catalog number: 8012-95-1) at 37ºC with 5% CO2. IVF-derived embryos were cultured from the 1-cell stage in CZB and α-MEM media under controlled in vitro conditions and allowed to develop sequentially to the 2-cell, 4-cell, morula, and blastocyst stages. To assess the effect of short-term exposure of α-MEM, embryos were initially cultured in CZB for 24 h and subsequently transferred to α-MEM media after several washes, where they were cultured for an additional 72 h until blastocyst stage. Embryonic development was monitored and embryos were collected at defined time points as follows: 24 h post-insemination (hpi) for the 2-cell stage, 46–48 hpi for the 4-cell stage, 70–72 hpi for the morula stage, and 96 hpi for the blastocyst stage.

Observation of nuclei and nucleolar morphology

To observe the nucleolar morphology, embryos were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA) (Pure Chemical Co., catalog number: 163-20145) in phosphate buffered saline (PBS) for 20 min at room temperature. Following fixation, the embryos were washed in phosphate buffer saline (PBS) containing 1% polyvinyl alcohol (PBS-PVA) and incubated overnight at 4ºC with blocking buffer containing 0.1% Triton X-100 (Nacalai Tesque Inc., catalog number: M2A6272), and 1% bovine serum albumin (BSA) (Merck, Darmstadt, Germany, catalog number: 12657-5GM) in PBS. To check the trophectoderm (TE) and inner cell mass (ICM) of the blastocysts, we used anti-CDX2 mouse monoclonal antibody (BioCare Medical, Pacheco, CA, USA, catalog number: ACR226B) and anti-NANOG rabbit polyclonal antibody (Abcam, Cambridge, UK, catalog number: ab80892) diluted 1:500. Nucleoli were visualized using a mouse monoclonal anti-NOPP140 antibody (1:500; Santa Cruz Bio-Technology, Inc, Santa Cruz, CA, USA, catalog number: sc374033). Embryos were incubated with primary antibody diluted in blocking buffer 3 h at 4ºC. After washing with PBS-PVA, the embryos were incubated with an Alexa Fluor 568-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG secondary antibody (1:500 dilution; Abcam, catalog number: ab175473), for 2 h at room temperature. Finally, the embryos were mounted on glass slides in Vecta-shield (Vector Laboratories Inc., Burlingame, CA, USA) supplemented with 1 µg/ml 4', 6-diamidino-2 phenylindole (DAPI). Images were obtained with a 40 × objective lenses using a confocal laser-scanning microscope (Olympus FV-1200; Evident). The nucleolus was analyzed using ImageJ software based on DAPI and NOPP140 merged images as described in a previous study [32]. Each blastomere was classified as a single nucleolus (SN) if it contained one nucleolus, or multiple nucleoli (MN) if it contained more than one nucleolus. To classify the entire embryo, we first counted the total number of cells based on DAPI-stained nuclei, then determined the number of blastomeres with SN and MN based on NOPP140 positive signal. The percentage of blastomeres with either SN or MN was then calculated, and embryos were categorized based on the majority nucleolar phenotype: if the percentage of SN blastomeres was higher, the embryo was classified as SN type; and if the percentage of MN blastomeres was higher, it was classified as MN embryo.

Quantification of rRNA

To quantify rRNA levels, a mouse monoclonal Y10b antibody was used to label 5.8S rRNA as described previously (1:500 dilution, Santa Cruz Biotechnology Inc, catalog number: sc-33678) [33]. To detect the inner cell mass (ICM) of the blastocysts we used an anti-NANOG rabbit polyclonal antibody (1:500 dilution; Abcam, catalog number: ab80892). Following PBS-PVA washes, the embryos were incubated for 2 h at room temperature with secondary antibodies—Alexa Fluor 568-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG (Abcam, catalog number: ab175473) and Alexa Fluor 488-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG (Abcam, catalog number: ab150077) secondary antibodies diluted 1:500. Embryos were mounted on glass slides using Vecta-shield mounting medium containing DAPI. The Y10b signals were evaluated using an Olympus FV-1200 confocal laser scanning microscope (Olympus FV-1200). After conventional immunostaining, the intensity of Y10b positive signals was measured using fluorescence images acquired at a single focal plane corresponding to center section of each embryo using ImageJ software. To minimize variability, embryos were first scanned along the Z-axis to identify the top and bottom optical sections. A consistent central optical plane corresponding to the maximal and most representative Y10b signal was selected for quantitative analysis. For each embryo, the region of interest (ROI) was manually delineated to encompass the entire embryonic area, including the nucleolar and cytoplasmic Y10b positive signals and the background fluorescence was subtracted from the Y10b negative regions within the same image. The zona pellucida was excluded by cross-referencing bright-field and fluorescence images to clearly distinguish it from the cytoplasm. Y10b fluorescence intensities were quantified and compared across experimental groups, with parallel batch processing to minimize variability associated with immunostaining and image acquisition.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using the JMP Pro software (version 18.0; SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA). Each experiment was independently replicated at least thrice, and the number of samples analyzed per group is indicated in each figure and table. The developmental rate, cell number, nucleolar number, nucleolar size and rRNA intensity levels were compared using the chi-square test and Student’s t-test based on the experimental design. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

Results

Developmental competence of embryos cultured in CZB and α-MEM media

The overall blastocyst development rate was similar in the CZB and α-MEM groups (93% vs. 91%, P > 0.05), indicating that the culture medium composition did not significantly affect the ability of embryos to reach the blastocyst stage after 96 h of individual culture (Table 1). Blastocyst quality was evaluated by immunostaining, using specific cell markers to quantify the different cells types. However, analysis of cell numbers revealed notable differences in blastocyst quality. The total cell number and CDX2 positive TE cell numbers were significantly reduced in embryos cultured in α-MEM compared to those cultured in CZB (P < 0.05), suggesting that cell proliferation was less efficient in α-MEM. However, despite the lower overall cell number, α-MEM-cultured blastocysts contained a higher number of NANOG-positive cells , indicating an increased proportion of cells allocated to the ICM cell numbers (Table 2). However, when the ratio of ICM/TE was compared between CZB and α-MEM embryos, a significantly increased ratio was noted in α-MEM (CZB = 0.15 vs. α-MEM = 0.19; P < 0.05). These results suggest that while α-MEM does not impair blastocyst formation, it may influence lineage allocation, favoring ICM formation at the expense of total cell proliferation.

Nucleolar organization and function at the 2-cell stage

After 24 h of in vitro culture, when the embryos developed to the 2-cell stage, analysis revealed no significant difference of nucleolar number per blastomere between embryos cultured in CZB and α-MEM media (Fig. 1A). Quantification of the number of nucleoli per embryo was quantified at the 2-cell stage also revealed no significant difference between embryos cultured in CZB and α-MEM (Fig. 1B). In contrast, a significant difference was observed in nucleolar size: embryos maintained in α-MEM exhibited substantially larger nucleoli compared with those maintained in CZB (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1C). The increase in nucleolar size under α-MEM conditions highlights a culture medium dependent effect on nucleolar growth rather than nucleolar number. These findings indicate that, although the basic presence of nucleoli is preserved irrespective of the medium, their structural development is highly responsive to the surrounding culture conditions. The Y10b intensity level was similar between embryos cultured in CZB and α-MEM media after 24 h of individual culture (Fig. 1D), indicating no detectable difference in signal intensity between the groups at this early developmental stage (Fig. 1E).

Structural and functional decoupling of the nucleolus at the 4-cell stage

At the 4-cell stage, embryos cultured for 48 h in different media exhibited strikingly divergent nucleolar features (Fig. 2A). The proportion of blastomeres containing SN was significantly higher in α-MEM-cultured embryos (62%) than in CZB-cultured embryos (42%), indicating a medium-dependent effect on nucleolar organization (Fig. 2B). Quantitative analysis of NOPP140 confirmed that the nucleolar size was markedly higher in α-MEM-cultured embryos (Figs. 2C, 2D). α-MEM-cultured embryos displayed significantly (P < 0.05) enlarged nucleoli compared to CZB-cultured embryos. Y10b signal intensity was significantly lower in α-MEM-cultured embryos compared to CZB-cultured embryos (Fig. 2E). Quantitative analysis revealed that embryos cultured in CZB exhibited significantly higher Y10b signals, indicating increased rRNA synthesis and processing (Fig. 2F). These observations suggest that α-MEM culture conditions are associated with nucleolar enlargement at the 4-cell stage, together with reduced Y10b signal intensity, indicating altered nucleolar morphology and rRNA-associated activity.

Morula stage nucleolar plasticity and rRNA level under prolonged culture conditions

At the morula stage, distinct differences in nucleolar morphology and rRNA levels persisted between the embryos cultured in different media (Fig. 3A). Embryos cultured in α-MEM exhibited a significantly (P < 0.05) higher proportion of blastomeres with a SN (78.37%) than those cultured in CZB (59.75%), indicating that the influence of culture conditions on nucleolar structure extends beyond the early cleavage stages (Fig. 3B). Consistent with nucleolar maturation, NOPP140 displayed a more diffuse localization pattern at the morula stage. At the morula stage, the nucleolar volume was not assessed because of the transition from nucleolus precursor bodies (NPBs) to mature nucleoli. Instead, examination of functional activity using Y10b staining revealed significantly reduced rDNA transcriptional activity in α-MEM-cultured embryos (P < 0.05), whereas CZB-cultured morulae retained stronger Y10b signals (Figs. 3C, 3D). These results demonstrate that the structural bias toward SN morphology and reduced transcriptional activity in α-MEM sustained into the morula stage, highlighting a lasting impact of the culture environment on nucleolar adaptation.

Persistence of altered nucleolar architecture and impaired mature rRNA accumulation at the blastocyst stage

Nucleolar organization in the blastocyst stage was characterized by a reticulated nucleolar architecture that was distinct from the compact immature nucleoli observed at earlier developmental stages. In the present study, distinct differences in the nucleolar morphology were observed between the two culture conditions at the blastocyst stage. Nucleolar morphology at the blastocyst stage was evaluated between CZB and α-MEM-cultured embryos using combined DAPI and NOPP140 immunostaining (Fig. 4A). Embryos cultured in CZB for 96 h yielded a total of 1,186 cells from 51 blastocysts, among which 193 exhibited SN morphology. In contrast, embryos cultured in α-MEM yielded 845 cells from 37 blastocysts, with 227 cells displaying SN phenotype. To evaluate the short-term and stage-specific effects of α-MEM exposure, IVF embryos were transferred to α-MEM at the 2-cell stage, a developmental stage at which α-MEM media have been commonly applied in previous embryo culture studies [26]. After 72 h of culture in α-MEM, 32 blastocysts were analyzed, comprising a total of 789 cells, of which 204 exhibited SN morphology (Table 3). To classify the embryos at blastocysts stage based on nucleolar morphology, each blastocyst was defined as SN embryo when ≥ 25% of blastomeres exhibited an SN phenotype, whereas blastocysts with < 25% SN blastomeres were classified as MN embryo. Using this classification, we compared embryos cultured in CZB with those cultured in α-MEM for 96 h and 72 h. The proportions of blastocysts classified as SN embryos was significantly higher in α-MEM-cultured groups, including both the 96 h group (62.5%) and the 72 h group (59.37%) compared with CZB group (31.37%) (P < 0.05) (Fig. 4B). No statistically significant difference was observed between 96 h and 72 h α-MEM groups.

Consistent with the observed differences in nucleolar morphology and functional output, the rRNA levels were also altered, as indicated by significantly lower Y10b signal intensity in blastocysts cultured in α-MEM compared with those cultured in CZB (Fig. 4C). To further evaluate the effect of the exposure duration, IVF embryos cultured in α-MEM for either 96 h or 72 h were analyzed. Both α-MEM exposure groups exhibited significantly lower Y10b signal intensity compared with the CZB group, whereas no significant difference was detected between the 96 h and 72 h α-MEM-cultured embryos (P < 0.05) (Fig. 4D). Together, these findings suggest that the nucleolar morphology and rRNA levels are specifically affected by cultured medium composition.

Discussion

In this study, we demonstrated that the embryo culture medium strongly influences nucleolar morphology and rRNA abundance during preimplantation development. Comparative analysis of CZB and α-MEM revealed that embryos cultured in α-MEM consistently exhibited a higher proportion of SN morphology accompanied by reduced rRNA signals, whereas embryos cultured in CZB more frequently exhibited lower proportion of SN morphology with increased rRNA abundance. These observations indicate that nucleolar architecture is highly sensitive to in vitro culture conditions.

NPBs in mouse oocytes and zygotes are transcriptionally inactive structures surrounded by peri-nucleolar heterochromatin [34]. Following germinal vesicle breakdown, the NPBs disassemble and their components are dispersed into the cytoplasm prior to re-establishment of functional nucleoli through Pol I-dependent rRNA transcription [35]. Under normal developmental conditions, the originally compact NPBs disappear at the morula stage and are replaced by somatic type nucleoli with a characteristic tripartite organization through a process known as reticulation [8, 21, 36]. Studies on bovine and porcine in vivo fertilized embryos have shown that compact NPBs progressively disassemble and are replaced by a reticulated structure at the onset of rRNA transcription during preimplantation development [37, 38]. Therefore, to minimize variability resulting from differences in fertilization timing and early developmental asynchrony, the embryos used in the present study were consistently produced using IVF.

Following 24 h of individual culture in either CZB or α-MEM, 2-cell stage embryos from the two groups showed similar nucleolar numbers and Y10b signal intensity. This observation may reflect the developmental context in which maternal nucleolar components continue to support ribosome biogenesis during 2-cell stage whereas the zygotic genome activation (ZGA) occurs at the late 2-cell stage (36 hpi), suggesting that the Y10b signal may not be sufficiently sensitive to culture conditions during this early stage [39, 40]. The high cytoplasmic viscosity of 2-cell mouse embryos further buffers them against the immediate influence of the external culture medium [41].

However, since our analysis relied on established nucleolar and rRNA markers, such as NOPP140 and Y10b, which primarily reflect nucleolar organization and rRNA associated signals, respectively [33, 36]. Notably, despite similar nucleolar number and rRNA abundance, α-MEM-cultured embryos exhibited larger nucleoli at the 2-cell stage (Fig. 1C). As robust embryonic rDNA transcription is not yet established at this stage, the increased nucleolar size likely reflects an early perturbation of NPB structural organization rather than a change in transcriptional output. Our data suggest that α-MEM culture conditions may influence nucleolar size before detectable changes in rRNA levels at 2-cell stage, potentially reflecting an early perturbation of nucleolar organization induced by medium composition. This interpretation is consistent with reports demonstrating that the nucleolar structure and organization play critical roles during early embryonic development, when structural changes in the nucleolar compartments can occur prior to the full activation of rDNA transcription [42, 43].

At the 4-cell stage, α-MEM-cultured embryos exhibited a higher proportion of SN type blastomeres and a marked increase in nucleolar size, along with significantly reduced Y10b signal intensity that persisted to the blastocyst stage, indicating impaired rDNA transcription across this developmental window [32, 36, 44]. The predominance of SN type blastomeres in α-MEM-cultured embryos at the 4-cell stage, raises the possibility that chromatin organization around the nucleolus may be sensitive to culture medium composition which has been previously associated with rDNA transcription during early embryonic development [10, 43]. The concurrent increase in nucleolar size, rather than reflecting enhanced transcriptional activity, may instead indicate aberrant nucleolar assembly or impaired maturation of the nucleolar compartment. This interpretation is further supported by the significantly reduced Y10b signal intensity, a marker of rRNA levels. Collectively, these findings indicate that α-MEM culture conditions exert a sustained negative influence on rDNA transcription across a critical developmental window.

In the early post-fertilization period, rRNA synthesis relies predominantly on maternally inherited rRNA and nucleolar structures and robust embryonic rDNA transcription requires additional time to be established [45]. As embryonic development proceeded, nucleolar maturation occurred in both CZB- and α-MEM-cultured embryos, accompanied by a more diffuse distribution of NOPP140, indicative of ongoing nucleolar reorganization (Figs. 3A, 4A). However, α-MEM-cultured embryos frequently retained a single and compact nucleolar morphology at later stages. When combined with persistently reduced rRNA levels from the 4-cell stage onward, this phenotype is more consistent with impaired functional nucleolar maturation than with a simple NPB to nucleolus transition delay. However, the current dataset does not permit a definitive mechanistic distinction between these possibilities. Future studies employing time-lapse imaging of nucleolar dynamics combined with orthogonal markers of EGA progression, such as single-embryo transcriptomics and chromatin accessibility profiling, are required to resolve this ambiguity.

Beyond the preimplantation period, a growing body of evidence indicates that culture medium composition during early development can exert lasting consequences on postnatal health, consistent with the developmental origins of the health and disease (DOHaD) framework [4, 46]. Studies in mice have demonstrated that embryos cultured in nutrient-rich and complex media exhibit differences in fetal and placental weight, altered metabolic gene expression and perturbed epigenetic profiles compared to those cultured in simpler formulations [47, 48]. In this regard, it was previously demonstrated that the nucleolus functions as a key regulator of cellular memory, acts as a storage site for early life environmental exposures, and carries the information on molecular changes from the embryonic to later stages [32, 49]. Reduced rDNA copy number and rDNA hypermethylation are strongly associated with obesity and metabolic dysfunction in both humans and mice, supporting the concept that rDNA integrity acts as a form of developmental memory linking early environmental conditions to long-term metabolic outcomes [50, 51]. Consistent with this, the significantly reduced Y10b signal intensity in α-MEM-cultured embryos from the 4-cell stage to blastocyst stage indicates impaired rDNA transcription during a critical developmental window. Notably, α-MEM-derived mice have previously been reported to exhibit altered postnatal phenotypes [27, 52]. The nucleolar patterns observed in the present study, occur during a period when epigenetic marks are actively remodeled and may therefore represent early molecular signatures of the developmental programming previously reported in α-MEM-derived mice [25, 27].

Previous studies have demonstrated that modifications in culture medium composition, including the absence of BSA or the addition of vitamins to the CZB medium disrupts normal cell distribution between the TE and ICM, resulting in altered cell lineage allocation [48, 53]. In the present study, embryos cultured in α-MEM were exposed to a medium lacking BSA supplementation and containing various vitamins (Supplementary Table 1). Based on our current findings, we propose that differences in medium composition, may underlie the altered nucleolar morphology and reduced blastocyst cell numbers reported here, although dissection of the effects of individual component is required to confirm this mechanism.

The present findings are particularly relevant in the context of human-assisted reproductive technology (ART), where the composition of preimplantation embryo culture media has been a subject of ongoing debate [54, 55]. α-MEM is enriched with a broad spectrum of non-essential amino acids and vitamins, giving it a nutrient-rich profile closely resembling that of many advanced ART media commonly used in human IVF laboratories. In contrast, the CZB medium represents a simpler, more defined formulation, conceptually analogous to sequential media systems designed to provide stage-appropriate nutritional support [53]. As mouse embryos serve as the primary preclinical model for evaluating human ART media formulations, our findings that α-MEM alters nucleolar morphology and reduces rRNA abundance suggest that nucleolar organization can function as a molecular-level indicator of embryo quality, complementing conventional morphological assessment during early embryonic development. Systematic evaluation of the nucleolar organization in human embryos cultured in simple versus complex ART media represents a valuable and clinically relevant research direction.

An important limitation of the present study is the absence of direct comparisons with other widely used embryo culture systems, such as potassium simplex optimized medium (KSOM) or in vivo-derived embryos. Such comparisons are critical for determining whether the observed nucleolar alterations represent a medium composition effect of α-MEM or, alternatively, a general response to suboptimal in vitro culture conditions. Without these comparisons, it remains possible that the reported phenotypes reflect non-specific culture stress rather than a unique α-MEM effect [28, 52]. Although we did not directly analyze in vivo-derived embryos in the present study, our independent observations consistently demonstrated that in vivo embryos predominantly exhibited multiple nucleolar structures (unpublished data), closely resembling the nucleolar phenotype observed in embryos cultured in CZB-cultured embryos and clearly distinct from that observed in embryos cultured in α-MEM-cultured embryos. In addition, the causal relationship between nucleolar alterations and reduced developmental competence remains unclear. Although α-MEM-cultured embryos exhibit both altered nucleolar morphology (Fig. 4B) and reduced blastocyst quality (Table 2), these outcomes may represent parallel consequences of an adverse culture environment rather than a direct cause–effect relationship. Thus, the present findings establish a strong association between α-MEM culture and altered nucleolar organization, but causality remains to be established. Nucleolar dysfunction may contribute to reduced developmental competence, or both phenomena may arise as parallel consequences of suboptimal culture conditions [33, 56]. Future studies incorporating in vivo-derived embryos, alternative culture systems, and targeted perturbation of nucleolar function are required to clarify these relationships.

In conclusion, the present study demonstrates that embryos cultured in α-MEM exhibit distinct nucleolar morphological differences from the early embryonic stage to the blastocyst stage, compared to CZB-cultured embryos. These findings are relevant to the broader debate over optimal media complexity in human ART, where compositionally similar formulations are in clinical use and the long-term developmental consequences of preimplantation culture conditions remain an area of active concern. It remains unclear whether nucleolar alterations are drivers of reduced developmental competence or are downstream indicators of compromised embryo quality remains an open and important question. Future studies employing targeted molecular perturbations and systematic evaluation of individual α-MEM components are required to elucidate the mechanistic links between nucleolar architecture and long-term outcomes. Collectively, these findings highlight nucleolar morphology and rRNA biogenesis as sensitive indicators of medium-induced perturbation and propose a new perspective for embryo quality assessment in human ART.

Qualitative analysis and numerical investigations of time-fractional Zika virus model arising in population dynamics
Gaurav Saini, Bappa Ghosh, Sunita Chand

Abstract
Epidemic models play a crucial role in population dynamics, offering valuable insights into disease transmission while aiding in epidemic prediction and control. In this paper, we analyze the mathematical model of the time-fractional Zika virus transmission for human and mosquito populations. The fractional derivative is considered in the Caputo sense of order α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) . \displaystyle\alpha\in(0,1). We begin by conducting a qualitative analysis using the stability theory of differential equations. The existence and uniqueness of the solution are established, and the model’s stability is examined through Hyers-Ulam stability analysis. Furthermore, an efficient difference scheme utilizing the standard L1 technique is developed to simulate the model and analyze the solution’s behavior under key parameters. The resulting nonlinear algebraic system is solved using the Newton-Raphson method. Finally, illustrative examples are presented to validate the theoretical findings. Graphical results indicate that the fractional model provides deeper insights and a better understanding of disease dynamics. These findings aid in controlling the virus through contact precautions and recommended therapies while also helping to predict its future spread.



Keywords:
Zika virus, System of fractional model, Caputo derivative, L1 scheme, Qualitative analysis.

Now, taking the norm and applying the Lipschitz condition, we get

\displaystyle\left\|S_{h}-S_{h}^{\star}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-z)^{\alpha-1}\left\|W_{1}(z,S_{h}(z))-W_{1}(z,S_{h}^{\star}(z))\right\|dz

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\eta_{1}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-z)^{\alpha-1}\left\|S_{h}(z)-S_{h}^{\star}(z)\right\|dz

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\eta_{1}t^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}\left\|S_{h}-S_{h}^{\star}\right\|.

The inequality holds if
\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{1}t^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}<1
, implying
\displaystyle\left\|S_{h}-S_{h}^{\star}\right\|=0
, and hence
\displaystyle S_{h}=S_{h}^{\star}
. Similarly,

\displaystyle\left\|I_{h}-I_{h}^{\star}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-z)^{\alpha-1}\left\|W_{2}(z,I_{h}(z))-W_{2}(z,I_{h}^{\star}(z))\right\|dz

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\eta_{2}t^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}\left\|I_{h}-I_{h}^{\star}\right\|\Rightarrow I_{h}=I_{h}^{\star}.

\displaystyle\left\|S_{m}-S_{m}^{\star}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-z)^{\alpha-1}\left\|W_{3}(z,S_{m}(z))-W_{3}(z,S_{m}^{\star}(z))\right\|dz

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\eta_{3}t^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}\left\|S_{m}-S_{m}^{\star}\right\|\Rightarrow S_{m}=S_{m}^{\star}.

\displaystyle\left\|I_{m}-I_{m}^{\star}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-z)^{\alpha-1}\left\|W_{4}(z,I_{m}(z))-W_{4}(z,I_{m}^{\star}(z))\right\|dz

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\eta_{4}t^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}\left\|I_{m}-I_{m}^{\star}\right\|\Rightarrow I_{m}=I_{m}^{\star}.

Therefore, we conclude that
\displaystyle S_{h}=S_{h}^{\star},\ I_{h}=I_{h}^{\star},\ S_{m}=S_{m}^{\star},\ I_{m}=I_{m}^{\star}
, confirming that the model (
1.2
) has a unique solution.
∎

Conclusion

In this study, we have formulated and analyzed a mathematical model for the Zika virus, evaluating its long-term behavior. We have investigated the existence, uniqueness, and stability properties of the fractional-order Zika virus model. Our numerical scheme demonstrates results, utilizing assumed initial values from existing literature:
\displaystyle S_{h}(0)=800
,
\displaystyle I_{h}(0)=200
,
\displaystyle S_{m}(0)=600
, and
\displaystyle I_{m}(0)=300
.
To analyze the transmission dynamics of Zika infection, we employed the Caputo operator, which is widely used in fractional calculus. By implementing an iterative numerical scheme, we graphically examined the disease dynamics under varying fractional-order values and parameter sensitivities. The findings of this study demonstrate that fractional-order models provide more compatible and biologically feasible outcomes compared to integer-order models. This highlights the importance of incorporating fractional calculus in epidemiological modeling to better capture real-world disease dynamics.

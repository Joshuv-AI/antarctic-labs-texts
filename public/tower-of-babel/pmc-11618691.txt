Supercontinuum generation with extended bandwidth and improved purity in graded-index ring-core fiber
Authors: Qinru Peng, Jian Yang, Yiwen Zhang, Yuanpeng Liu, Yuxi Fang, Wenpu Geng, Zhongqi Pan, Fei Yang, Yang Yue
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this paper, we numerically investigate the impact of shape factor (k) on the mode properties of graded-index ring-core fiber (GIRCF). A 50 mol% Ge-doped GIRCF with shape factor of 2 is devised to generate supercontinuum (SC) carrying orbital angular momentum (OAM) modes, which possesses flat dispersion with a minor variation of < ± 30 ps/nm/km from 1090 to 2590 nm. The generated supercontinuum (SC) in the designed GIRCF carrying OAM1,1 mode covers 2305-nm wavelength range from 750 to 3055 nm at − 40 dB level. The graded refractive index profile (RIP), introduced as a new parameter, offers an additional dimension for the design of ring-core fiber (RCF) supporting OAM modes. The graded RIP contributes to the creation of flat and near-zero dispersion in the RCF structures and reduces the spin–orbit coupling (SOC) effect at the material boundary, leading to broader spectral coverage and higher mode purity in the output spectra.Subject terms: Electronics, photonics and device physics; Optical physics

Introduction

The optical concept known as vortex beam, which carries orbital angular momentum (OAM), has garnered widespread attention in recent years within the scientific and engineering communities1. The wavefront of vortex beams with OAM can be expressed in terms of the phase factor eilθ, where l denotes the topological charge (TC), reflecting the quantity and direction of the helical structure within the beam, and f denotes the azimuthal angle2. This helical phase structure imparts the beam with annular intensity and central phase singularities. Moreover, OAM beams with different TCs form a set of orthogonal modes, enabling efficient mode multiplexing and demultiplexing3,4. Several ring-core fibers (RCF) have been proposed to ensure the stable transmission of OAM beams and restrict the generation of undesired radially higher-order modes5–7. These fibers can match the annular intensity distribution of the OAM beams and effectively separate the quasi-degenerate modes.

In various applications, the distinctive characteristics of OAM beams have brought about innovation and progress in multiple domains, such as optical communication8–12, object detection13–16, optical tweezers17,18, laser material processing19,20, quantum information processing21–23, super-resolution microscopy24–26, and optical sensing27,28. Broadband light sources play a crucial role in these applications. However, directly obtaining a wavelength bandwidth for OAM beams from Gaussian beams is highly limited. Therefore, designing a highly nonlinear annular optical fiber to generate a supercontinuum (SC) spectrum carrying OAM modes is a commendable objective.

Supercontinuum, with a history spanning decades, has found applications in various domains, including optical communications29,30, optical frequency combs31–33, and optical coherence tomography34,35. Demonstrably, the incidence of ultrashort pulses carrying vortex states into nonlinear optical fibers results in SC generation with the same state36. Moreover, pioneering research has revealed that specially designed ring-core fibers can generate a supercontinuum carrying OAM modes spanning several octaves37. In recent researches, the effective extension of the spectral coverage for OAM beams has been achieved by generating SC in highly nonlinear ring-core fibers38–42. However, it is noteworthy that all the fibers employed in these studies are of the step-index ring-core fibers (SIRCF), the significant refractive index contrast between the cladding and ring-core in such fibers can lead to a substantial enhancement of the spin–orbit coupling (SOC) effect in SIRCF, which results in the conversion of OAM carried by partially transmitted beams into spin angular momentum (SAM)43,44. Consequently, the transmitted beams cease to be a purely circularly polarized OAM mode, leading to a decrease in mode purity and an increase in crosstalk between OAM modes45. The design of a graded refractive index distribution can mitigate the aforementioned issue by reducing the abruptness of refractive index at material boundaries46. Furthermore, in the practical fabrication, SIRCF often manifest a graded refractive index profile (RIP) due to the constraints of existing manufacturing technology6. Therefore, it is imperative to explore the impact of graded RIP on fiber mode.

Regarding the fiber materials, silica is chosen as the substrate and germanium as the doping element on account of its outstanding physical characteristics and compatibility with silica, making it one of the most commonly used materials in fiber optics manufacturing. Moreover, the practical feasibility of manufacturing ring-core fibers with Ge-doped silica has been demonstrated47. Simultaneously, this material exhibits low Rayleigh scattering and intrinsic absorption in the near-infrared (IR) spectrum, resulting in remarkably low material loss across a broad spectral range.

In this paper, graded-index ring-core fiber (GIRCF) is numerically calculated with different shape factor (k). The study explores the impact of varying k on the mode properties of GIRCF. Furthermore, detailed investigation and optimization of GIRCF are conducted for SC generation. The generated SC in the designed GIRCF covers 2305-nm wavelength range from 750 to 3055 nm at − 40 dB of power level. The graded RIP, introduced as a new parameter, offers a fresh dimension for the design of RCF supporting OAM modes. This characteristic has the potential to contribute to the creation of near-zero and flat dispersion in RCF structures, resulting in broader spectral coverage and higher mode purity in the generated SC spectra.

Over the past few decades, researchers have extensively studied the fabrication of optical fibers. It is feasible to prepare Ge-doped optical fibers using plasma chemical vapor deposition (PCVD) or modified chemical vapor deposition (MCVD)50,51, and highly Ge-doped optical fiber has been fabricated in practice with the doping concentration of Ge up to 98 mol%52. Meanwhile, RCFs have been successfully pulled47,53. This provides sufficient feasibility assurance for the fabrication of the highly Ge-doped RCF designed in this paper. Compared with traditional step-index optical fiber, the index profile of the ring-core optical fiber is very similar to the intensity distributions of the OAM modes. It thus can stably transmit these unique optical modes. Some studies have shown that when the effective refractive index difference between modes is larger than 10−4, their coupling can be effectively reduced5,54.

In this work, we focus on the mode properties of OAM1,1 in GIRCF, which is composed of , and explore the modulation effects of different RIP on the relevant parameters such as mode dispersion, effective mode field area, and nonlinear coefficient. As a novel regulation dimension, the graded RIP can be leveraged to generate broader and flatter SC spectra with enhanced mode purity, deserving further in-depth exploration.

The impact of the central wavelength (λ0) of the input pulse is illustrated in Fig. 6a. Secant hyperbolic pulses with TFWHM of 40 fs, P0 of 80 kW, centered at 1600 nm, 1800 nm and 2000 nm are used as the pump sources and emitted into the designed GIRCF. It can be clearly observed that the spectrum pumped at 1600 nm fails to extend well into the longer wavelength region. Similarly, the intensity performance of the spectrum pumped at 2000 nm in the short wavelength region is also unsatisfactory. We finally select the input pulse centered at 1800 nm, which achieves over two-octave spectral broadening after propagating 13 mm in the designed GIRCF. It has flatter spectral broadening at both the long and short wavelength regions.

The impact of the P0 of the input pulse on the SC generation is further explored. The 1800-nm 40-fs hyperbolic secant pulses with different P0 are emitted into the 13-mm GIRCF, as depicted in Fig. 6b. When P0 is 60 kW, the spectrum cannot expand into a wider wavelength range due to the low total energy of the input pulse. When P0 is 100 kW, the higher peak power can no longer help the spectrum to expand to longer wavelength range due to the cutoff wavelength for OAM1,1 mode at 3100 nm in the designed GIRCF. Dispersive wave generation in the short wavelength region can be clearly seen at 80 kW and 100 kW. When the total power of the input pulse is too large, more energy in the short wavelength region will be pumped to the dispersion wave, resulting in uneven spectral extent and flatness degradation. Therefore, an input pulse with P0 of 80 kW is selected to reach a balance between the spectral flatness and its broadness.

Finally, the effect of the TFWHM on the input pulse is shown in Fig. 6c. Input pulses with λ0 of 1800 nm, P0 of 80 kW, respectively possessing different TFWHM of 20 fs, 40 fs, 60 fs, are selected as the pump source emitted into the designed GIRCF. When P0 is held constant, the input pulse with larger TFWHM has a higher total power and a narrower frequency spectrum, which corresponds to a larger power spectral density. Therefore, small TFWHM cannot provide enough energy to expand the spectrum to wider wavelength range. Meanwhile, the input pulse with a TFWHM of 60 fs cannot provide greater extension or better flatness to the SC, and the 40-fs input pulse is selected as the pumping source. Some studies proved that it is feasible to generate supercontinuum spectrum in ring-core optical fiber using high-power pulses in experiments59,60. Prabhakar et al. have experimentally demonstrated that supercontinuum spectrum can be generated in optical fiber using pulses with up to 373.8 kw peak power60.

We finally select an 80-kW 40-fs input pulse centered at 1800 nm as the pumping source. Figure 7 depicts the SC broadening process after propagating through fiber lengths of 0, 5, 9, and 13 mm, respectively. Due to the low dispersion and strong nonlinearity of the designed GIRCF, broadband SC generation can be achieved through fiber transmission distances of just a few millimeters. After propagating for about 13 mm, we observe an obvious soliton splitting phenomenon, where the fundamental-order soliton excites a dispersive wave in the short wavelength region. At this point, the broadened spectrum reaches its optimum, and continues to propagate backwards, the energy of the fundamental-order soliton is transferred more to the short-wave dispersive wave, resulting in a decrease in spectral power near the zero-dispersion point.

Spectral broadening with propagation distance in designed GIRCF (k = 2, r0 = 2 µm, Δr = 2 µm).

Figure 8a,b display the temporal and spectral evolutions of the generated SC carrying OAM1,1 mode with propagation distance. In the initial stage, high-frequency and low-frequency components are generated on the falling and rising edges of the pulse on account of self-phase modulation (SPM), leading to the symmetrically broadening around the wavelength of the input pulse. In the region of anomalous dispersion, the spectrum is broadened in the frequency domain and compressed in the time domain due to the high-order soliton effect and stimulated Raman scattering (SRS). Significant soliton fission effects can be observed at around 10 mm of transmission length. Then, in the normal dispersion region, optical wave breaking (OWB) leads to the generation of new spectrum components61. Throughout the entire process, new spectral components are generated continuously due to SPM, self-steepening effect and four-wave mixing (FWM), eventually forming a flat supercontinuum62. The spectrum stops broadening and becomes smoother after 13-mm propagation, which is shown in Fig. 8b. The generated SC covers 2305-nm wavelength range from 750 to 3055 nm at − 40 dB of power level.

(a) Temporal and (b) spectral evolutionary process of the input pulses carrying OAM1,1 mode propagating along the 13-mm designed GIRCF (k = 2, r0 = 2 µm, Δr = 2 µm).

One can see that the fiber structure at k = 2 has the potential to produce wider and flatter SC, as it has flatter and near-zero dispersion and relatively higher nonlinear coefficients over a larger wavelength range. However, it can be found that the supercontinuum generated at k = 1 is the narrowest. While the parabolic profiles represented by k > 2 can compensate for this shortcoming. Conclusions

In summary, we numerically calculate the mode properties of the GIRCF with different shape factor (k). The study explores the impact of varying k on the dispersion, effective mode field area, and nonlinear coefficients of GIRCF. We design a Ge-doped GIRCF supporting OAM modes with optimized parameters (r0 = 2 µm, Δr = 2 µm), which possesses near zero and flat dispersion with a small variation of < ± 30 ps/nm/km from 1090 to 2590 nm. The generated SC in the designed GIRCF carrying OAM1,1 mode covers 2305-nm wavelength range from 750 to 3055 nm at − 40 dB of power level. The graded RIP, introduced as a novel parameter, offers another tuning dimension for the design of RCF supporting OAM modes. The graded RIP has the potential to contribute to the creation of flat and near-zero dispersion in RCF structures, it can also reduce the SOC effect at the material boundary, resulting in broader spectral coverage and higher mode purity in the generated SC spectra.

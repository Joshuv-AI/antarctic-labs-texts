A few sharp estimates of harmonic functions with applications to Steklov eigenfunctions
Xing Wang and Cheng Zhang

Abstract
. On smooth compact manifolds with smooth boundary, we first establish the sharp lower bounds for the restrictions of harmonic functions in terms of their frequency functions, by using a combination of microlocal analysis and frequency function techniques by Almgren [ 1 ] and Garofalo-Lin [ 21 ] . The lower bounds can be saturated by Steklov eigenfunctions on Euclidean balls and a family of symmetric warped product manifolds. Moreover, as in Sogge [ 35 ] and Taylor [ 38 ] , we analyze the interior behavior of harmonic functions by constructing a parametrix for the Poisson integral operator and calculate its composition with the spectral cluster. By using microlocal analysis, we obtain several sharp estimates for the harmonic functions whose traces are quasimodes on the boundary. As applications, we establish the almost-orthogonality, bilinear estimates and transversal restriction estimates for Steklov eigenfunctions, and discuss the numerical approximation of harmonic functions.



1.
Introduction

Let
(\Omega,h)
be a connected smooth compact manifold with smooth boundary
(M,g)
, where
\dim\Omega=n+1\geq 2
and
h|_{M}=g
. The Laplace operators on
\Omega
and
M
are denoted by
\Delta
and
\Delta_{g}
respectively. Let
u
be a harmonic function on
\Omega
with boundary datum
f
, namely

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\Delta u=0\ \ \text{on}\ \Omega,\\
u=f\ \ \ \ \text{on}\ \partial\Omega=M.\end{cases}

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the harmonic extension operator (or Poisson integral operator) on
\Omega
. Then
u=\mathcal{H}f
. Let
P\in OPS^{1}(M)
be classical and self-adjoint with principal symbol
p(x,\xi)=|\xi|_{g(x)}
on the boundary. Two important examples of
P
are
\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}
and the Dirichlet-to-Neumann (DtN) operator
\mathcal{N}
. If
e_{\lambda}
is an eigenfunction of
\mathcal{N}
associated with the eigenvalue
\lambda
, then its harmonic extension
u_{\lambda}=\mathcal{H}e_{\lambda}
is called a Steklov eigenfunction. The Steklov eigenvalue problem has seen a surge
of interest in the past few decades, and there are many open problems in this rapidly expanding area. For the historical background and recent developments, we recommend the survey papers by Girouard-Polterovich
[
22
]
and Colbois-Girouard-Gordon-Sher
[
16
]
.

In this paper, we mainly use microlocal analysis to investigate the interior behavior of
u
with respect to the frequency of
f
. Specifically, we shall use two types of frequencies. The first type is the frequency function as in the seminal works of Almgren
[
1
]
and Garofalo-Lin
[
21
]
. The second type is the spectral frequency described by the associated eigenvalues in its spectral decomposition with respect to
P
. These two types of frequencies coincide when the boundary datum
f
is an eigenfunction of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator on
M
. We shall analyze them by using the microlocal analysis techniques in Sogge
[
35
]
and Taylor
[
38
]
.

1.1.
Geometric quantities

First, we introduce the geometric quantities that will be used to state our results. Let
d_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
,
d_{g}(\cdot,\cdot)
be the Riemannian distance functions on
\Omega
and
M
respectively. Let

(1.2)

\Omega_{t}=\{\omega\in\Omega:d_{h}(\omega,\partial\Omega)\geq t\},\ \ \Omega_{t}^{\mathsf{c}}=\Omega\setminus\Omega_{t},\ \ \Sigma_{t}=\partial\Omega_{t}.

Then
t
is the distance from
\Sigma_{t}
to the boundary.

For each
x\in M
, we have a unique inward geodesic
\gamma_{x}(t)\subset\Omega
normal to
M
such that
\gamma_{x}(0)=x
,
|\nabla\gamma_{x}(t)|=1
. Let
\phi(t,x)=\gamma_{x}(t)
.
Let

(1.3)

\delta=\sup\{\tau>0:\ \phi:[0,\tau]\times M\rightarrow\Omega\text{ is injective}\}.

Then for any
t<\delta
,
\Sigma_{t}
is a smooth hypersurface, and
\gamma_{x}(t)
is normal to
\Sigma_{t}
for any
x\in M
.

By (
1.3
), we get a bijection
\phi:[0,\delta)\times M\rightarrow\Omega_{\delta}^{\mathsf{c}}
, so we can find a local coordinates system near the boundary adapted to the geodesics normal to boundary. Let
\phi^{-1}=(g_{1},g_{2})
with
g_{1}:\Omega_{\delta}^{\mathsf{c}}\rightarrow M
and
g_{2}:\Omega_{\delta}^{\mathsf{c}}\rightarrow[0,\delta)
. We choose a finite covering
M=\cup U_{\nu}
and the corresponding coordinate maps
\kappa_{\nu}:U_{\nu}\to\tilde{U}_{\nu}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let

\tilde{\kappa}_{\nu}:\Omega_{\nu}=\phi([0,\delta)\times U_{\nu})\rightarrow\ \mathbb{R}^{n+1},\ \ p\mapsto(\kappa_{\nu}(g_{2}(p)),g_{1}(p)).

Under this setting, let
x=(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})
be local coordinates on some open set of
M
. Then for
t\in[0,\delta)
and fixed
x
,
(t,x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})
will be a geodesic curve starting from
x
and normal to both
M
and
\Sigma_{t}
, so
\partial_{t}
is the inward normal derivative of
\Sigma_{t}
. Specifically, the metric tensor of
\Omega
can be written as

(1.4)

h=dt^{2}+g_{ij}(t,x)dx_{i}dx_{j}=dt^{2}+g_{t},

where
g_{t}
is the metric on
\Sigma_{t}
,
g_{0}=g
. Then the Laplace operator on
\Omega
can be written as

(1.5)

\Delta=\partial_{t}^{2}+a(t,x)\partial_{t}+\Delta_{g_{t}}

where

(1.6)

a(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\det(g_{ij}(t,x))/\det(g_{ij}(t,x))

and
\Delta_{g_{t}}
is the Laplace operator on
\Sigma_{t}
.

For each
t\in[0,\delta)
and
x\in\Sigma_{t}
, we denote the Weingarten map with respect to the inward normal unit vectors of
\Sigma_{t}
as

(1.7)

\mathcal{W}_{t,x}:T_{x}\Sigma_{t}\rightarrow T_{x}\Sigma_{t},

and
\mathcal{W}_{t,x}^{*}:T^{*}_{x}\Sigma_{t}\rightarrow T^{*}_{x}\Sigma_{t}
is its dual. Let
k_{1}(x)\leq...\leq k_{n}(x)
be the principal curvatures at
x
, and

(1.8)

\tilde{k}_{t}(x)=-\text{Tr}\mathcal{W}_{t,x}+\sup_{\xi\neq 0}\frac{\langle\mathcal{W}^{*}_{t,x}\xi,\xi\rangle_{t}}{\langle\xi,\xi\rangle_{t}}=-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}k_{j}(x),

where the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{t}
on
T_{x}^{*}\Sigma_{t}
is defined as

\langle\xi,\eta\rangle_{t}=\sum_{i,j}g^{ij}(t,x)\xi_{i}\eta_{j}.

Let
|\xi|_{g_{t}(x)}=\sqrt{\langle\xi,\xi\rangle_{t}}
. Then we define
\Theta:[0,\delta)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as

(1.9)

\Theta(t)=\sup_{x\in\Sigma_{t}}\tilde{k}_{t}(x)+\sup_{x\in\Sigma_{t}}\text{Tr}\mathcal{W}_{t,x},

and let

(1.10)

K(t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{\int_{0}^{s}\Theta(\tau)d\tau}ds.

By Taylor’s expansion, we have

K(t)=t+\frac{\Theta(0)}{2}t^{2}+O(t^{3}).

Let

(1.11)

r(t)=\inf_{x\in M,\ \xi\neq 0}\frac{|\xi|_{g_{t}(x)}}{|\xi|_{g(x)}},\ \quad G(t)=\int_{0}^{t}r(s)ds.

We have
r(t)=1+O(t)
and
G(t)=t+O(t^{2})
. We will see that
G(t)=K(t)
when
\Omega
is a Euclidean ball or the warped product manifold in Section 3.

1.2.
Lower bounds

We first prove the lower bounds for the
L^{2}
restrictions of harmonic functions.

Theorem 1
.

Fix any
0<\delta_{0}<\delta
. There is a constant
C>0
dependent on
(\Omega,h)
and
\delta_{0}
, such that for any
0\leq t\leq\delta_{0}
, and any
f\in L^{2}(M)
, we have

(1.12)

\|\mathcal{H}f\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t})}\geq Ce^{-\Lambda K(t)}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)},

where the frequency
\Lambda={\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}}/{\int_{M}u^{2}}
with
u=\mathcal{H}f
, and
K(t)=t+O(t^{2})
is defined in (
1.10
).

The lower bounds are sharp in the sense that they can be saturated by Steklov eigenfunctions on Euclidean balls and a family of symmetric warped product manifolds, see Section 3. When
f\in L^{2}(M)\setminus H^{\frac{1}{2}}(M)
, we have
\Lambda=\infty
and (
1.12
) is trivial in this case. Moreover, a simple consequence is the lower bound for the
L^{p}
norm with
p>2

(1.13)

\|\mathcal{H}f\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma_{t})}\geq Ce^{-\Lambda K(t)}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)}.

It is also sharp, since it can be saturated by Example
2
in Section 3.

As an application, using
\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}=\int_{M}u\partial_{\nu}u
, we get the following exponential lower bound for the Steklov eigenfunctions on smooth manifolds. As before, let
e_{\lambda}
be a DtN eigenfunction and
u_{\lambda}=\mathcal{H}e_{\lambda}
be the Steklov eigenfunction.

Corollary 1
.

Fix any
0<\delta_{0}<\delta
. There is a constant
C>0
dependent on
(\Omega,h)
and
\delta_{0}
, such that for any
0\leq t\leq\delta_{0}
, we have

(1.14)

\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t})}\geq Ce^{-\lambda K(t)}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)}.

For real analytic
\Omega
and
M
, Galkowski-Toth
[
20
]
have proved some lower bounds of this type. Indeed, it follows from
[
20
, Theorem 1]
that for any
\varepsilon>0
,
\lambda>\lambda_{0}(\varepsilon)
and
t\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, there is
c>0
such that

(1.15)

\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t})}\gtrsim e^{-\varepsilon\lambda}e^{-(t+ct^{2})\lambda}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)}.

In comparison, our estimate (
1.14
) is sharp and has no
\varepsilon
-loss. Moreover, we do not need the analyticity assumptions.

1.3.
Upper bounds

From the examples of Steklov eigenfunctions (see Section 3), we naturally expect that any harmonic function should rapidly decay with respect to
\lambda t
, where
t
is the distance to the boundary and
\lambda
is its “lowest spectral frequency”. We shall give a rigorous justification for this observation.

Let
P\in OPS^{1}(M)
be classical and self-adjoint with principal symbol
p(x,\xi)=|\xi|_{g(x)}
. Let
{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\geq\lambda}
be the indicator function of
[\lambda,\infty)
.
We shall prove the following interior decay property when the spectral frequency of the boundary datum
f
is bounded below by
\lambda
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
0\leq\delta_{0}<\delta
,
0<c<1
,
N>0
and
\lambda>0
. Suppose that
f={\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\geq\lambda}(P)f
. Then for
1<p\leq\infty
and
0\leq t\leq\delta_{0}
, we have

(1.16)

\|\mathcal{H}f\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma_{t})}\leq C_{1}e^{-c\lambda G(t)}\|f\|_{L^{p}(M)}+C_{2}\lambda^{-N}\|f\|_{L^{1}(M)},

where
C_{1}=C_{1}(\delta_{0},c,p),C_{2}=C_{2}(\delta_{0},c,N)
, and
G(t)=t+O(t^{2})
is defined in (
1.11
).

The exponential term is essentially sharp, in the sense that
G(t)=K(t)
when
\Omega
is a Euclidean ball or the warped product manifold in Section 3. The remainder term
O(\lambda^{-N})
comes from the smoothing operators that are natural in the theory of pseudo-differential operators on smooth manifolds. The crux of the proof is the parametrix for the Poisson integral operator in Lemma
2
. From the proof, we shall notice that (
1.16
) is valid for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
if the boundary datum
f
has spectral frequency
\approx\lambda
. Moreover, the proof also implies the derivative estimates. Indeed, if
f={\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\geq\lambda}(P)f
then we have for
1<p<\infty

(1.17)

\|\partial_{t,x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{H}f\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma_{t})}\lesssim\lambda^{|\alpha|}e^{-c\lambda G(t)}\|f\|_{L^{p}(M)}+\lambda^{-N}\|f\|_{L^{1}(M)},\ \forall\alpha,\forall N.

Remark 1
.

In comparison to Theorem
1
, we remark that the upper bound of the form with
C,c>0

(1.18)

\|\mathcal{H}f\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t})}\leq Ce^{-c\Lambda t}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)}

cannot hold for general boundary datum
f
, where the frequency
\Lambda={\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}}/{\int_{M}u^{2}}
with
u=\mathcal{H}f
. For example, let
f=1+e_{\lambda}
and
u=\mathcal{H}f=1+u_{\lambda}
, where
e_{\lambda}
is an
L^{2}
normalized DtN eigenfunction. Then both
u
and
u_{\lambda}
have frequencies
\Lambda\approx\lambda
. However, when
\lambda t\gg 1
, we notice that
\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t})}\approx 1
shows no decay while
\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t})}\lesssim(1+\lambda t)^{-N}
decays rapidly. This key observation suggests that the interior decay property of harmonic functions should only rely on the “lowest spectral frequency”, as described in Theorem
2
.

Recall Sogge’s seminal work
[
34
]
on
L^{p}
estimates. If
{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{(\lambda-1,\lambda]}
is the indicator function of
(\lambda-1,\lambda]
, then

(1.19)

\|{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{(\lambda-1,\lambda]}(P)f\|_{L^{p}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{\sigma(p)}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)},\ \ 2\leq p\leq\infty,

where

(1.20)

\sigma(p)=\begin{cases}\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}),\ \ \ 2\leq p<\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}\\
\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n}{p},\ \ \ \ \ \ \frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}\leq p\leq\infty.\end{cases}

See
[
35
, Theorem 5.1.1]
. The
L^{\infty}
bound is due to Hörmander
[
25
]
. The estimates are sharp and they can be saturated by some spherical harmonics on the standard sphere. So we have the following interior decay estimates for Steklov eigenfunctions on smooth manifolds with smooth boundary.

Corollary 2
.

Let
0\leq\delta_{0}<\delta
,
0<c<1
,
N>0
and
\lambda>0
. Then for
2\leq p\leq\infty
and
t\in[0,\delta_{0}]
we have

(1.21)

\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma_{t})}\lesssim(\lambda^{\sigma(p)}e^{-c\lambda G(t)}+\lambda^{-N})\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)},\ \forall N.

This implies

(1.22)

\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma_{t})}\lesssim\lambda^{\sigma(p)}(1+\lambda t)^{-N}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)},\ \forall N.

Here
\sigma(p)
is Sogge’s exponent in (
1.19
).

Our estimates (
1.21
) and (
1.22
) are at least sharp for small
t
, by the sharpness of Sogge’s estimates (
1.19
). We also have the derivative estimates for
2\leq p\leq\infty

(1.23)

\|\partial_{t,x}^{\alpha}u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma_{t})}\lesssim\lambda^{\sigma(p)+|\alpha|}(1+\lambda t)^{-N}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)},\ \forall\alpha,\forall N.

The interior decay properties of Steklov eigenfunctions have been studied by Hislop-Lutzer
[
24
]
, Polterovich-Sher-Toth
[
33
]
, Galkowski-Toth
[
19
]
, Di Cristo-Rondi
[
18
]
, Daudé-Helffer-Nicoleau
[
17
]
, Helffer-Kachmar
[
23
]
, Levitin-Parnovski-Polterovich-Sher
[
31
]
. Now we go over the previous results and compare them with ours.

In 2001, Hislop-Lutzer
[
24
]
proved when the boundary is smooth, Steklov eigenfunctions decay super-polynomially into the interior, namely

(1.24)

\|u_{\lambda}\|_{H^{1}(K)}=O(\lambda^{-N}),\ \forall N,

on any compact subset
K
of the interior of
\Omega
, and they also conjectured that the decay is actually of order
e^{-\lambda t}
if
\Omega
and
M
are real analytic. Under the analyticity assumptions, recently Polterovich-Sher-Toth
[
33
]
(
\dim\Omega=2
) and Galkowski-Toth
[
19
]
(
\dim\Omega\geq 2
) established pointwise bounds with exponential decay. Specifically, Galkowski-Toth
[
19
, Theorem 1]
obtained

(1.25)

\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{t})}\lesssim\lambda^{\sigma(\infty)+\frac{1}{4}}e^{-\lambda(t-ct^{2})}\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)}.

The proof are based on analytic microlocal analysis and thus
use the analyticity hypotheses in extremely strong ways. So one would perhaps believe that the exponential decay property
cannot hold in the smooth case.
For smooth manifolds, Helffer-Kachmar
[
23
, Theorem 1]
proved

(1.26)

\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{t})}\lesssim\lambda^{n-1}(1+\lambda t)^{-N}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)},\ \forall N.

Di Cristo-Rondi
[
18
]
considered the problems of interior decay for very general elliptic equations. The following estimates can be derived from
[
18
, Theorem 3.3]

(1.27)

\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t})}\lesssim(1+\lambda t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)}.

In comparison, our results (
1.21
) and (
1.22
) are better than (
1.25
) when
t\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\log\lambda
, and we do not need the analyticity hypotheses. Moreover, our results are stronger than (
1.26
) and (
1.27
). By the maximum principle, our results imply (
1.24
).

1.4.
Comparable interior and boundary norms

By Theorem
2
, if the spectral frequency is bounded below by
\lambda
, i.e.
f={\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\geq\lambda}(P)f
, then we can integrate in
t
to get the following interior norm estimates for
1<p\leq\infty

(1.28)

\|\mathcal{H}f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(M)}.

Next, we further prove that if the boundary datum
f
has spectral frequency
\approx\lambda
, then the interior norm of its harmonic extension
\mathcal{H}f
is comparable to the norm of
f
on the boundary up to a factor of the frequency. As before, let
P\in OPS^{1}(M)
be classical and self-adjoint with principal symbol
p(x,\xi)=|\xi|_{g(x)}
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\lambda>0
and
\beta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
. Let
f_{\lambda}=\beta(P/\lambda)f
. Then for any
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we have

(1.29)

\|\mathcal{H}f_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\approx\lambda^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|f_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(M)},

i.e.
there is a constant
C=C(\Omega)>0
such that

(1.30)

C^{-1}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|f_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(M)}\leq\|\mathcal{H}f_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\lambda^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|f_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(M)}.

The power gain
\lambda^{-\frac{1}{p}}
is due to the rapid decay property in
\lambda t
, like
(1+\lambda t)^{-N}
. This result is sharp in the sense that it can be saturated by Steklov eigenfunctions on Euclidean balls. As a corollary, we have the following two-sided interior
L^{p}
estimates for Steklov eigenfunctions.

Corollary 3
.

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we have

(1.31)

\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\approx\lambda^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(M)}.

Motivated by the study of nodal sets, the upper bounds in (
1.31
) were first obtained in our joint work with Huang and Sire
[
28
]
for generalized Steklov eigenfunctions with rough potentials.

1.5.
Almost-orthogonality, transversal restrictions and bilinear estimates

We denote the interior and boundary inner products as

\langle u,v\rangle_{\Omega}=\int_{\Omega}u\bar{v}\ \ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \ \langle f,g\rangle_{M}=\int_{M}f\bar{g}.

It is easy to see the gradients of Steklov eigenfunctions are orthogonal on
\Omega
. Indeed,

\langle\nabla u_{\lambda},\nabla u_{\mu}\rangle_{\Omega}=\langle\mathcal{N}e_{\lambda},e_{\mu}\rangle_{M}=0,\ \forall\lambda\neq\mu.

But for general
\Omega
other than Euclidean balls, Steklov eigenfunctions are not orthogonal in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. However, we can show they are almost-orthogonal. Let
\chi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
satisfy

\text{supp }\hat{\chi}\subset(\frac{1}{2},1),\ \ \chi(0)=1.

As before, let
P\in OPS^{1}(M)
be classical and self-adjoint with principal symbol
p(x,\xi)=|\xi|_{g(x)}
.

Theorem 4

(Almost-orthogonality)
.

Let
v_{\lambda}=\mathcal{H}\circ\chi(P-\lambda)f,\ w_{\mu}=\mathcal{H}\circ\chi(P-\mu)g
. We have

(1.32)

|\langle v_{\lambda},w_{\mu}\rangle_{\Omega}|\lesssim(1+|\lambda+\mu|)^{-1}(1+|\lambda-\mu|)^{-N}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)}\|g\|_{L^{2}(M)},\ \forall N.

The crux of the proof is the kernel formula in Lemma
3
. The question on the
L^{2}
-orthogonality of Steklov eigenfunctions has been considered by Auchmuty
[
2
]
, Auchmuty-Rivas
[
9
]
, Cho-Rivas
[
15
]
. Specifically, Cho-Rivas
[
15
]
analyzed the problem on rectangles by the explicit formula for the inner products in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, and provide accompanying numerics. However, a complete description of the orthogonality in
L^{2}(\Omega)
has not been made so far. We address this issue in the following corollary.

Corollary 4
.

For Steklov eigenfunctions
u_{\lambda}
and
u_{\mu}
, we have

(1.33)

|\langle u_{\lambda},u_{\mu}\rangle_{\Omega}|\lesssim(1+\lambda+\mu)^{-1}(1+|\lambda-\mu|)^{-N}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)}\|e_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}(M)},\ \ \forall N.

Thus, by Theorem
3
we have for
\mu\geq\lambda\geq 1

(1.34)

|\langle u_{\lambda},u_{\mu}\rangle_{\Omega}|\lesssim(\lambda/\mu)^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+|\lambda-\mu|)^{-N}\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|u_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\ \ \forall N.

This significantly improves the trivial bounds by Cauchy-Schwarz, and provide a rigorous justification for the almost-orthogonality of Steklov eigenfunctions in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Similarly, we can establish the bilinear estimates and transversal restriction estimates.

Theorem 5

(Bilinear estimates)
.

Let
v_{\lambda}=\mathcal{H}\circ\chi(P-\lambda)f,\ w_{\mu}=\mathcal{H}\circ\chi(P-\mu)g
with
\mu\geq\lambda\geq 1
. Then we have

(1.35)

\|v_{\lambda}w_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}B\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)}\|g\|_{L^{2}(M)},

where

(1.36)

B=\begin{cases}\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ n=2\\
\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\log\lambda},\ \ n=3\\
\lambda^{\frac{n-2}{2}},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ n\geq 4.\end{cases}

Bilinear and multilinear eigenfunction estimates on closed manifolds have been proved by Burq-Gérard-Tzvetkov
[
11
,
12
]
. Our result (
1.35
) shows that the same type of estimates still hold for harmonic functions. In comparison to the results in
[
11
,
12
]
, there is a power gain
\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}
that only depends on the relatively high frequency
\mu
. This is due to the rapid decay property of harmonic functions. Moreover, the bound (
1.35
) is sharp in the sense that it can be saturated by Steklov eigenfunctions on Euclidean balls.

Corollary 5
.

If
B
is defined as in Theorem
6
, then we have the bilinear estimates for Steklov eigenfunctions

(1.37)

\|u_{\lambda}u_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}B\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)}\|e_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}(M)}.

Equivalently, by Theorem
3
we have for
\mu\geq\lambda\geq 1

(1.38)

\|u_{\lambda}u_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}B\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|u_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

The bound

is sharp on on Euclidean balls and only depends on the relatively low frequency

In this section, we introduce some background on pseudo-differential operators. Let

Symbol class
S^{m}

|D_{x}^{\beta}D_{\xi}^{\alpha}p(x,\xi)|\leq C_{\alpha\beta}(1+|\xi|)^{m-|\alpha|},\;\forall\alpha,\beta.

Let

be the set of pseudo-differential operators with symbols in

. Let

|D^{j}_{t}D_{x}^{\beta}D_{\xi}^{\alpha}p(t,x,\xi)|\leq C_{j\alpha\beta}(1+|\xi|)^{m-|\alpha|},\;\ t\in[0,\delta_{0}],\ \forall j,\alpha,\beta.

The associated family of operators are called a smooth family in
OPS^{m}
.

Symbol class

is the set of functions
p(t,x,\xi)
in
C^{\infty}([0,\delta_{0}]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that

Symbol class
\mathcal{P}^{m}_{e}
is the set of functions
p(t,x,\xi)\in C^{\infty}([0,\delta_{0}]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that

(2.3)

p(t,x,\xi)=q(t,x,\xi)e^{-ct\langle\xi\rangle}

with
\langle\xi\rangle=\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}}
for some

and

. See e.g. Taylor
[
38
, Chapter 7]
.

For any

, we denote its harmonic extension on
\Omega
by

that solves the boundary value problem (

).
Then the DtN operator on

, where

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
1

Let

(3.1)

H(t)=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u^{2},\ \ D(t)=-\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u\partial_{t}u,\ \ N(t)=D(t)/H(t).

Let
(t,x)
be the Fermi coordinates described before and suppose
0\leq t\leq\delta_{0}<\delta
. We can identify each
\Sigma_{t}
as
M
with metric tensor
g_{t}
, so we can rewrite
H(t),\ D(t)
as

H(t)=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u^{2}=\int_{M}u(t,x)^{2}d\omega_{t}(x)

D(t)=-\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u\partial_{t}u=-\int_{M}u(t,x)\partial_{t}u(t,x)d\omega_{t}(x)

where
d\omega_{t}(x)=\sqrt{\det(g_{ij}(t,x))}dx
in local coordinates. Then

(3.2)

\displaystyle H^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=2\int_{M}u(t,x)\partial_{t}u(t,x)d\omega_{t}(x)+\int_{M}u^{2}(t,x)\partial_{t}(d\omega_{t}(x)).

For the volume derivative term
\partial_{t}(d\omega_{t}(x))
, As in
[
38
, (C.26) in Section C of Chapter 12]
, under Fermi coordinate, we have

(3.3)

\frac{\partial_{t}\det(g_{ij}(t,x))}{\det(g_{ij}(t,x))}=-2\text{Tr}\mathcal{W}_{t,x}.

Then we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(d\omega_{t}(x))

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}(\sqrt{\det(g_{ij}(t,x))}dx)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial_{t}(\det(g_{ij}(t,x)))}{2\sqrt{\det(g_{ij}(t,x))}}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial_{t}(\det(g_{ij}(t,x)))}{2\det(g_{ij}(t,x))}d\omega_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=-(\text{Tr}\mathcal{W}_{t,x})d\omega_{t}(x).

Thus,

(3.5)

H^{\prime}(t)=2\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u\partial_{t}u-\int_{\Sigma_{t}}(\text{Tr}\mathcal{W}_{t,x})u^{2}=-2D(t)-\int_{\Sigma_{t}}(\text{Tr}\mathcal{W}_{t,x})u^{2}.

Next, we have

(3.6)

D^{\prime}(t)=-\int_{M}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}d\omega_{t}(x)-\int_{M}u\partial_{t}^{2}ud\omega_{t}(x)-\int_{M}u\partial_{t}u\partial_{t}(d\omega_{t}(x)).

By (
1.5
),

0=\Delta u=\partial_{t}^{2}u(t,x)+a(t,x)\partial_{t}u(t,x)+\Delta_{g_{t}}u(t,x)

and we use (
1.6
), (
3.4
) to obtain

(3.7)

\displaystyle\int_{M}u\partial_{t}^{2}ud\omega_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=-\int_{M}u\partial_{t}ua(t,x)d\omega_{t}(x)-\int_{M}u\Delta_{g_{t}}ud\omega_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=-\int_{M}u\partial_{t}u\frac{\partial_{t}(\det(g_{ij}(t,x)))}{2\det(g_{ij}(t,x))}d\omega_{t}(x)-\int_{M}u\Delta_{g_{t}}ud\omega_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=-\int_{M}u\partial_{t}u\partial_{t}(d\omega_{t}(x))-\int_{M}u\Delta_{g_{t}}ud\omega_{t}(x).

Plugging this into (
3.6
), we get

(3.8)

D^{\prime}(t)=-\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}+\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u\Delta_{g_{t}}u.

To handle the second term on the right, we use the fact that
\sqrt{-\Delta_{g_{t}}}
is self-adjoint and Lemma
1
to obtain

(3.9)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u\Delta_{g_{t}}u

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\sqrt{-\Delta_{g_{t}}}u|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|(\mathcal{N}_{t}+\mathcal{B}_{t})u|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\mathcal{N}_{t}u|^{2}-2\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\mathcal{N}_{t}u\mathcal{B}_{t}u-\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\mathcal{B}_{t}u|^{2}.

Since
u
is harmonic on
\Sigma_{t}
and
\mathcal{N}_{t}u=\partial_{t}u
on
\Sigma_{t}
, and
\mathcal{N}_{t}
is self-adjoint, we have

(3.10)

\displaystyle D^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}-2\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\mathcal{N}_{t}u\mathcal{B}_{t}u-\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\mathcal{B}_{t}u|^{2}

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}-2\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u\mathcal{N}_{t}\mathcal{B}_{t}u-\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\mathcal{B}_{t}u|^{2}.

Now we are ready to compute the derivative of
N(t)
. Since
\mathcal{B}_{t}\in OPS^{0}
, it is bounded on
L^{2}
and then
\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\mathcal{B}_{t}u|^{2}/\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|u|^{2}=O(1)
. So by (
3.1
), (
3.5
) and (
3.10
) we have

\displaystyle N^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{D^{\prime}(t)}{H(t)}-\frac{D(t)H^{\prime}(t)}{H(t)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{-2\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}-2\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u\mathcal{N}_{t}\mathcal{B}_{t}u}{\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u^{2}}+\frac{2(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u\partial_{t}u)^{2}}{(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u^{2})^{2}}+\frac{\int_{\Sigma_{t}}(\text{Tr}\mathcal{W}_{t,x})u^{2}}{\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u^{2}}N(t)+O(1)

(3.11)

\displaystyle=\frac{-2\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u^{2}+2(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u\partial_{t}u)^{2}}{(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u^{2})^{2}}-\frac{2\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u\mathcal{N}_{t}\mathcal{B}_{t}u}{\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u^{2}}+\frac{\int_{\Sigma_{t}}(\text{Tr}\mathcal{W}_{t,x})u^{2}}{\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u^{2}}N(t)+O(1)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{2\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u\mathcal{N}_{t}\mathcal{B}_{t}u}{\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u^{2}}+\frac{\int_{\Sigma_{t}}(\text{Tr}\mathcal{W}_{t,x})u^{2}}{\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u^{2}}N(t)+O(1).

The last step follows from Cauchy–Schwartz inequality.

For the rest terms, by Lemma
1
, the principal symbol of
-2\mathcal{N}_{t}\mathcal{B}_{t}\in OPS^{1}
is

-2|\xi|_{g_{t}}\sigma_{t}(x,\xi)\leq|\xi|_{g_{t}}\tilde{k}_{t}(x)\leq|\xi|_{g_{t}}\sup_{x\in\Sigma_{t}}\tilde{k}_{t}(x).

So the symbol of
(\sup_{x\in\Sigma_{t}}\tilde{k}_{t}(x))\mathcal{N}_{t}+2\mathcal{N}_{t}\mathcal{B}_{t}
is the sum of a non-negative principal symbol and a symbol of order 0. Combining with the
L^{2}
boundedness of operators of order 0, we can apply the sharp G
\mathring{\text{a}}
rding inequality
[
26
, Theorem 18.1.14]
to the principal symbol and obtain

-\frac{2\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u\mathcal{N}_{t}\mathcal{B}_{t}u}{\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u^{2}}\leq N(t)\sup_{x\in\Sigma_{t}}\tilde{k}_{t}(x)+O(1).

It is easy to see

(3.12)

\frac{\int_{\Sigma_{t}}(\text{Tr}\mathcal{W}_{t,x})u^{2}}{\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u^{2}}N(t)\leq N(t)\sup_{x\in\Sigma_{t}}\text{Tr}\mathcal{W}_{t,x}.

We finally have

(3.13)

N^{\prime}(t)\leq(\sup_{x\in\Sigma_{t}}\tilde{k}_{t}(x)+\sup_{x\in\Sigma_{t}}\text{Tr}\mathcal{W}_{t,x})N(t)+O(1)=\Theta(t)N(t)+O(1).

So we have,

(3.14)

N(t)\leq e^{\int_{0}^{t}\Theta(y)dy}N(0)+O(1).

By (
3.5
), we have

(\log H(t))^{\prime}=\frac{H^{\prime}(t)}{H(t)}\geq-2N(t)-\sup_{x\in\Sigma_{t}}\text{Tr}\mathcal{W}_{t,x}

again integrating both sides

(3.15)

\displaystyle\log H(t)-\log H(0)

\displaystyle\geq-2\int_{0}^{t}N(s)ds-\int_{0}^{t}\sup_{x\in\Sigma_{s}}\text{Tr}\mathcal{W}_{s,x}ds

\displaystyle\geq-2N(0)\int_{0}^{t}e^{\int_{0}^{t}\Theta(y)dy}dt+O(t)

and this gives us the desired bound

\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t})}\gtrsim e^{-N(0)K(t)}\|u\|_{L^{2}(M)}.

3.2.
Sharpness of Theorem
1

Now we are going to show the exponential lower bound can be saturated by Euclidean balls and a family of symmetric warped product manifolds.

Let
\Omega=[-R,R]\times M_{0}
, where
(M_{0},g)
is a connected closed manifold, and
\Omega
is equipped with the metric
h=\rho(s)^{2}g+ds^{2}
for some positive
\rho\in C^{\infty}([-R,R])
. First, we shall compute some geometric quantities and the Weingarten map for some special cases.

Next, we compute Weingarten maps of manifolds
(\Omega,h)
in the setting as in Proposition
1
without the symmetry condition
\rho(s)=\rho(-s)
. For
0\leq t<R
,

\Sigma_{t}=\{-R+t\}\times M_{0}\cup\{R-t\}\times M_{0}=\Sigma_{t}^{-}\cup\Sigma_{t}^{+}.

We shall only compute it at
\Sigma_{t}^{+}
, under this setting
g_{t}=\rho(R-t)^{2}g
.

As in
[
38
, (4.68)–(4.70) of Appendix C]
, we consider the one-parameter family of submanifolds given by

\phi_{\tau}:\Sigma_{t}^{+}\rightarrow\Omega,\ \phi_{\tau}(R-t,x)=(R-t-\tau,x),\ \forall x\in M_{0}.

Let
\tilde{g}_{\tau}
be the induced metric on the submanifold
\phi_{\tau}(\Sigma_{t}^{+})
and
x_{+}=(R-t,x)
. Then for any tangent vectors
v_{1},\ v_{2}
on
\Sigma_{t}^{+}
at
x_{+}
, we have

\partial_{\tau}\tilde{g}_{\tau}(v_{1},v_{2})|_{\tau=0}=-2\langle\partial_{t},\mathrm{I\!I}_{x_{+}}(v_{1},v_{2})\rangle=-2\langle\mathcal{W}_{t,x_{+}}v_{1},v_{2}\rangle_{t}

where the second fundamental form
\mathrm{I\!I}_{x_{+}}(v_{1},v_{2})=\langle\mathcal{W}_{t,x_{+}}v_{1},v_{2}\rangle_{t}\partial_{t}
, and
\partial_{t}
is the inward unit normal vector on
\Sigma_{t}^{+}
.

Since
\tilde{g}_{\tau}=\rho(R-t-\tau)^{2}g
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\tilde{g}_{\tau}(v_{1},v_{2})|_{\tau=0}

\displaystyle=-2\rho^{\prime}(R-t)\rho(R-t)g(v_{1},v_{2})

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\rho^{\prime}(R-t)}{\rho(R-t)}g_{t}(v_{1},v_{2})

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\rho^{\prime}(R-t)}{\rho(R-t)}\langle v_{1},v_{2}\rangle_{t}.

Thus, for
x\in\Sigma_{t}^{+}

(3.16)

\mathcal{W}_{t,x}=\frac{\rho^{\prime}(R-t)}{\rho(R-t)}I

where
I
is the identity matrix. Similarly for
x\in\Sigma_{t}^{-}
,

\mathcal{W}_{t,x}=-\frac{\rho^{\prime}(-R+t)}{\rho(-R+t)}I.

So the principal curvatures
k_{1}(x),\ \cdots,\ k_{n}(x)
are all equal to
\frac{\rho^{\prime}(R-t)}{\rho(R-t)}
on
\Sigma_{t}^{+}
, and
-\frac{\rho^{\prime}(-R+t)}{\rho(-R+t)}
on
\Sigma_{t}^{-}
, and

\Theta(t)=\max\Big\{\frac{\rho^{\prime}(R-t)}{\rho(R-t)},-\frac{\rho^{\prime}(-R+t)}{\rho(-R+t)}\Big\}.

Specially if
\rho
is symmetric about
0
, then

(3.17)

k_{1}(x)=k_{2}(x)=...=k_{n}(x)=\frac{\rho^{\prime}(R-t)}{\rho(R-t)},\ \ \ \ \tilde{k}_{t}(x)=-\frac{(n-1)\rho^{\prime}(R-t)}{\rho(R-t)},

and

(3.18)

\sigma_{t}(x,\xi)=\frac{(n-1)\rho^{\prime}(R-t)}{2\rho(R-t)},\;\ \ \ \text{Tr}\mathcal{W}_{t,x}=\frac{n\rho^{\prime}(R-t)}{\rho(R-t)},\;\ \ \ \Theta(t)=\frac{\rho^{\prime}(R-t)}{\rho(R-t)}

for all
(x,\xi)\in T^{*}\Sigma_{t}
. Thus,

(3.19)

K(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\rho(R)}{\rho(R-s)}ds.

Example 1
.

Euclidean balls.

Let
\Omega=B(0,R)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
be the ball of radius of
R
centered at the origin. Let

be the induced metric, Laplace operator and DtN operator on
\partial\Omega
(a sphere of radius

). We have

\mathcal{N}=\sqrt{-\Delta+\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4R^{2}}}-\frac{n-1}{2R},

see
[

. Under Fermi coordinates, for

Suppose

\|u_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma_{t})}=\frac{(R-t)^{n/p}}{R^{n/p}}(1-\frac{t}{R})^{R\lambda_{k}}\|e_{k}\|_{L^{p}(M)}\approx e^{-\lambda_{k}R\log(R/(R-t))}\|e_{k}\|_{L^{p}(M)}.

which is exactly the same as the coefficient of

in (

).

.

.

The upper bound in (

) was also obtained in

Proof of Proposition
1
.

By the symmetry of
\rho
, there is a Steklov eigenfunction of the form

u_{\lambda}=b(s)e_{\mu}(x)

associated with the Steklov eigenvalue
\lambda
, where

is symmetric or anti-symmetric about

and

is an

normalized eigenfunction on

.

We just need to handle

\frac{\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma_{t})}}{\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(M)}}=\frac{\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t})}}{\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)}}.

The argument is similar to the proof of Theorem
1
. By (

), we have

(3.22)

N^{\prime}(t)=R_{1}(t)+R_{2}(t)+R_{3}(t)+O(1),

where

R_{1}=\frac{-2\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\partial_{t}u_{\lambda}|^{2}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u_{\lambda}^{2}+2(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u_{\lambda}\partial_{t}u_{\lambda})^{2}}{(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u_{\lambda}^{2})^{2}},

R_{2}=-\frac{2\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u_{\lambda}\mathcal{N}_{t}\mathcal{B}_{t}u_{\lambda}}{\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u_{\lambda}^{2}},\;\ \ \ \ \ R_{3}=\frac{\int_{\Sigma_{t}}(\text{Tr}\mathcal{W}_{t,x})u_{\lambda}^{2}}{\int_{\Sigma_{t}}u_{\lambda}^{2}}N(t).

We notice

, by the symmetry of
b(s)
, and the fact that
u_{\lambda}=b(s)e_{\mu}(x)
.

By (
3.18
), the principal symbol of
\mathcal{B}_{t}
is

\sigma_{t}(x,\xi)=\frac{(n-1)\rho^{\prime}(R-t)}{2\rho(R-t)}=-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{k}_{t}(x),

which is constant for all
x,\xi
on
T^{*}\Sigma_{t}
. So
2\mathcal{N}_{t}\mathcal{B}_{t}+\tilde{k}_{t}\mathcal{N}_{t}\in OPS^{0}
, and we can write

R_{2}=-\frac{(n-1)\rho^{\prime}(R-t)}{\rho(R-t)}N(t)+O(1).

Again by (
3.18
), we have

R_{3}=\frac{n\rho^{\prime}(R-t)}{\rho(R-t)}N(t).

Thus, by (
3.22
) and (
3.18
) we get

\displaystyle N^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho^{\prime}(R-t)}{\rho(R-t)}N(t)+O(1)

\displaystyle=\Theta(t)N(t)+O(1).

Then

(3.23)

N(t)=e^{\int_{0}^{t}\Theta(y)dy}N(0)+O(1).

By (
3.5
), we have

(\log H(t))^{\prime}=\frac{H^{\prime}(t)}{H(t)}=-2N(t)-O(1),

which gives

(3.24)

\displaystyle\log H(t)-\log H(0)

\displaystyle=-2N(0)\int_{0}^{t}e^{\int_{0}^{s}\Theta(y)dy}ds+O(1).

This implies the desired bound

\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t})}^{2}\approx e^{-2N(0)K(t)}\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2}=e^{-2\lambda K(t)}\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2}.

∎

Next, we apply Proposition
1
to construct some examples with various decaying speeds, compared to original conjetured decaying speed
e^{-\lambda t}
by Hislop-Lutzer
[
24
]
. These phenomena have been observed by Galkowski-Toth
[
19
,
20
]
.

Example 2
.

Quasi-cylinder with convex boundary.

Let
M_{0}=\mathbb{R}^{n}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{n})=\mathbb{T}^{n}
be the standard flat torus, and
\Omega=[-1,1]\times M_{0}
with the metric
h=ds^{2}+(1+s^{2})dx^{2}
. Then

\Theta(t)=\frac{\rho^{\prime}(R-t)}{\rho(R-t)}=\frac{2(1-t)}{1+(1-t)^{2}}.

By Proposition
1
, for each Steklov eigenvalue
\lambda
, we can find some
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that

u_{\lambda}=b(s)e^{i\xi\cdot x}
. Then by (
3.21
), we get

\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma_{t})}\approx e^{-\lambda(t+t^{2}+O(t^{3}))}\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(M)},

which decays faster than
e^{-\lambda t}\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(M)}
.

Example 3
.

Quasi-cylinder with concave boundary.

Let
M_{0}=\mathbb{S}^{n}
be the standard sphere and
\Omega=[-\frac{\pi}{3},\frac{\pi}{3}]\times M_{0}
with the metric
h=ds^{2}+(\cos s)dx^{2}
. Then

\Theta(t)=-\tan(\frac{\pi}{3}-s).

By Proposition
1
for each Steklov eigenvalue
\lambda
, we can find a Steklov eigenfunction
u_{\lambda}=b(s)e_{\mu}(x)
such that

\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma_{t})}\approx e^{-\lambda(t-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}t^{2}+O(t^{3}))}\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(M)},

which decays slower than
e^{-\lambda t}\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(M)}
.

4.
Parametrix for the Poisson integral operator

As in Taylor
[
38
, Section 12 of Chapter 7]
, we can construct a parametrix for the Poisson integral operator
u=\mathcal{H}f
near the boundary for the boundary value problem

(4.1)

\begin{cases}\Delta u(t,x)=0,\ \ (t,x)\in\Omega,\\
u(0,x)=f(x),\ \ \ x\in\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

See also Boutet de Monvel
[
10
]
, Lee-Uhlmann
[
30
]
.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be the DtN operator on
M=\partial\Omega
. As in Section 2, the Laplacian
\Delta
on
\Omega
in local coordinates with respect to the boundary has the form

(4.2)

\Delta=(\partial_{t}-A_{1}(t))(\partial_{t}+A(t))+B(t)

where the smooth families
A(t),A_{1}(t)\in OPS^{1}(M)
and
B(t)\in OPS^{-\infty}(M)
. Moreover,
A(0)=\mathcal{N}
modulo a smoothing operator, and
A(t)
has the symbol
a(t,x,\xi)\in S^{1}
with principal symbol
|\xi|_{g_{t}(x)}
. We have

|\partial_{t}^{k}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}a(t,x,\xi)|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta,k}(1+|\xi|)^{1-|\alpha|},\ \ \forall\alpha,\beta,k.

Lemma 2

(Parametrix for the Poisson integral operator)
.

For any
0<\delta_{0}<\delta
and
t\in[0,\delta_{0}]
, under Fermi coordinates, there exist a parametrix
P_{t}
locally given by

(4.3)

P_{t}f(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int e^{ix\cdot\xi}b(t,x,\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)d\xi,\ b\in\mathcal{P}^{0}_{e},

and a smooth family of smoothing operators
R_{t}
such that the Poisson integral operator

(4.4)

\mathcal{H}f(t,x)=P_{t}f(x)+R_{t}f(x).

Moreover, the symbol

b(t,x,\xi)\sim b_{0}(t,x,\xi)+\sum_{j\geq 1}\sum_{2\leq k\leq 2j}b_{0}(t,x,\xi)t^{k}q_{j,k}(t,x,\xi)

with
q_{j,k}(t)
are smooth families in
S^{k-j}
and
b_{0}(t,x,\xi)=e^{-\int_{0}^{t}a(s,x,\xi)ds}\in\mathcal{P}_{e}^{0}
.

The symbol class
\mathcal{P}_{e}^{0}
is defined in Section 2.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{B}_{r}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:|x|<r\}
. Let
\{(U_{j},B_{j},\kappa_{j})\}_{j=1}^{K}
be a collection of open sets on
M
. Each
(U_{j},\kappa_{j})
is a local coordinate chart,
\kappa_{j}(B_{j})=\mathbb{B}_{1}
and
\mathbb{B}_{3}\subset\kappa_{j}(U_{j})
. We may assume that there is a partition of unity
\{\phi^{0}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{K}
adapted to
\{B_{j}\}_{j=1}^{K}
. Let
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
so that
\phi(x)=1
when
|x|<2
and
\phi(x)=0
when
|x|>3
, denote
\phi^{1}_{j}=\kappa_{j}^{-1}(\mathbb{B}_{3})
. For each
j
, we construct a parametrix
P_{j}(t)
in the form of (
4.3
) in
U_{j}
, then

P_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{K}\phi^{1}_{j}P_{j}(t)\phi^{0}_{j}

is a parametrix for the Poisson integral operator
\mathcal{H}
near the boundary.
So we will only work in local coordinates on
M
. As in
[
38
, Section 12 of Chapter 7]
, we only need to construct a parametrix
P_{t}
for the following evolution equation

\partial_{t}u+A(t)u=0,\ u(0)=f,

since
P_{t}
also gives a parametrix for the Poisson integral operator
\mathcal{H}
by (
4.2
).

We build the symbol of
P_{t}
by the series
b(t,x,\xi)\sim\sum_{j\geq 0}b_{j}(t,x,\xi)
, which should satisfy the equation

\partial_{t}b+\sum_{\alpha}\frac{1}{\alpha!}D_{\xi}^{\alpha}a\partial_{x}^{\alpha}b=0,\ \ b(0,x,\xi)=1.

To determine each term of the series, we require that

(4.5)

\partial_{t}b_{0}+ab_{0}=0,\ \ b(0,x,\xi)=1,

and for
j\geq 1
,

(4.6)

\partial_{t}b_{j}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{j}\sum_{|\alpha|=j-\ell}\frac{1}{\alpha!}D_{\xi}^{\alpha}a\partial_{x}^{\alpha}b_{\ell}=0,\ \ b_{j}(0,x,\xi)=0.

Solving (
4.5
) gives

(4.7)

b_{0}(t,x,\xi)=e^{-\int_{0}^{t}a(s,x,\xi)ds}\in\mathcal{P}_{e}^{0},

since
a(s)
is a smooth family in
S^{1}
.
To solve (
4.6
), we let

r_{j}(t,x,\xi)=-\sum_{\ell=0}^{j-1}\sum_{|\alpha|=j-\ell}\frac{1}{\alpha!}D_{\xi}^{\alpha}a\partial_{x}^{\alpha}b_{\ell}.

Then (
4.6
) is equivalent to

\partial_{t}b_{j}+ab_{j}=r_{j},\ \ b_{j}(0,x,\xi)=0

which gives the recursive formula

\displaystyle b_{j}(t,x,\xi)

\displaystyle=b_{0}(t,x,\xi)\int_{0}^{t}r_{j}(s,x,\xi)b_{0}(s,x,\xi)^{-1}ds

\displaystyle=-b_{0}(t,x,\xi)\sum_{\ell=0}^{j-1}\sum_{|\alpha|=j-\ell}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\int_{0}^{t}D_{\xi}^{\alpha}a(s,x,\xi)\partial_{x}^{\alpha}b_{\ell}(s,x,\xi)\cdot b_{0}(s,x,\xi)^{-1}ds.

We claim that for
j\geq 1

(4.8)

b_{j}(t,x,\xi)=b_{0}(t,x,\xi)\sum_{2\leq k\leq 2j}t^{k}q_{j,k}(t,x,\xi),

where
q_{j,k}(t)
are smooth families in
S^{k-j}
. Then we have
b_{j}\in\mathcal{P}_{e}^{-j}
by (
4.7
). So we can build a symbol
b\sim\sum b_{j}\in\mathcal{P}_{e}^{0}
(see e.g.
[
35
, Lemma 3.1.2]
). Now we prove the claim (
4.8
) by induction. For
j=1
,

\displaystyle b_{1}(t,x,\xi)

\displaystyle=-b_{0}(t,x,\xi)\sum_{|\alpha|=1}\int_{0}^{t}D_{\xi}^{\alpha}a(s,x,\xi)\partial_{x}^{\alpha}b_{0}(s,x,\xi)\cdot b_{0}(s,x,\xi)^{-1}ds

\displaystyle=-b_{0}(t,x,\xi)\sum_{|\alpha|=1}\int_{0}^{t}D_{\xi}^{\alpha}a(s,x,\xi)\int_{0}^{s}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}a(\tau,x,\xi)d\tau ds

\displaystyle=b_{0}(t,x,\xi)t^{2}q_{1,2}(t,x,\xi)

where
q_{1,2}(t)
is a smooth family in
S^{1}
. Suppose (
4.8
) holds for
b_{\ell}
with
1\leq\ell<j
. Then for
|\alpha|\geq 1

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{\alpha}b_{0}(t,x,\xi)=b_{0}(t,x,\xi)\sum_{m=1}^{|\alpha|}t^{m}q_{m}(t,x,\xi)

and for
1\leq\ell<j
and
|\alpha|\geq 0

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{\alpha}b_{\ell}(t,x,\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{2\leq k\leq 2\ell}\sum_{|\alpha_{1}|+|\alpha_{2}|=|\alpha|}t^{k}\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}b_{0}(t,x,\xi)\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{2}}q_{\ell,k}(t,x,\xi)

\displaystyle=b_{0}(t,x,\xi)\sum_{2\leq k\leq 2\ell+|\alpha|}t^{k}\tilde{q}_{k-\ell}(t,x,\xi)

where
q_{m}(t),\ \tilde{q}_{m}(t)
are smooth families in
S^{m}
.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle b_{j}(t,x,\xi)

\displaystyle=-b_{0}(t,x,\xi)\sum_{\ell=0}^{j-1}\sum_{|\alpha|=j-\ell}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\int_{0}^{t}D_{\xi}^{\alpha}a(s,x,\xi)\partial_{x}^{\alpha}b_{\ell}(s,x,\xi)\cdot b_{0}(s,x,\xi)^{-1}ds

\displaystyle=-b_{0}(t,x,\xi)\sum_{|\alpha|=j}\sum_{m=1}^{j}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\int_{0}^{t}D_{\xi}^{\alpha}a(s,x,\xi)s^{m}q_{m}(s,x,\xi)ds

\displaystyle\quad\quad-b_{0}(t,x,\xi)\sum_{\ell=1}^{j-1}\sum_{|\alpha|=j-\ell}\sum_{2\leq k\leq j+\ell}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\int_{0}^{t}D_{\xi}^{\alpha}a(s,x,\xi)s^{k}\tilde{q}_{k-\ell}(s,x,\xi)ds

\displaystyle=b_{0}(t,x,\xi)\sum_{2\leq k\leq 2j}t^{k}q_{j,k}(t,x,\xi).

where
q_{j,k}(t)
are smooth families in
S^{k-j}
. So the claim (
4.8
) is proved.
∎

4.1.
Kernel of the composition operator

In the following, let
P\in OPS^{1}(M)
be classical and self-adjoint with principal symbol
p(x,\xi)=|\xi|_{g(x)}
. Let
\chi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
satisfy
\text{supp }\hat{\chi}\subset(\frac{1}{2},1)
and
\chi(0)=1
. By
[
35
, Lemma 5.1.3]
, we can write the kernel of the spectral cluster as

\chi(P-\lambda)(x,y)=\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}e^{i\lambda d_{g}(x,y)}a_{\lambda}(x,y)+r_{\lambda}(x,y)

where
a_{\lambda}(x,y)
is supported in
\{d_{g}(x,y)\approx 1\}
and
|\partial_{x,y}^{\alpha}a_{\lambda}(x,y)|\leq C_{\alpha},\ \forall\alpha.

And the remainder term is smooth and satisfies

|\partial_{x,y}^{\alpha}r_{\lambda}(x,y)|\leq C_{\alpha}\lambda^{-N},\ \ \forall N,\alpha.

Recall
g_{t}
is the metric on
\Sigma_{t}
under Fermi coordinates and
g=g_{0}
. Let

(4.9)

r(t)=\inf_{x\in M,\ \xi\neq 0}\frac{|\xi|_{g_{t}(x)}}{|\xi|_{g(x)}},\ \quad G(t)=\int_{0}^{t}r(s)ds.

We have
r(t)=1+O(t)
and
G(t)=t+O(t^{2})
.
One can check that
G(t)=K(t)
if
\Omega
is a Euclidean ball or the warped product manifold in Proposition
1
. Now we can calculate the kernel of
\mathcal{H}\circ\chi(P-\lambda)
, i.e. the composition of the Poisson integral operator with the spectral cluster, by applying the parametrix in Lemma
2
.

Lemma 3
.

Fix
0<\delta_{0}<\delta
. For any
0\leq t\leq\delta_{0}
, we have

(4.10)

\mathcal{H}(\chi(P-\lambda)f)(t,x)=\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}e^{-c\lambda G(t)}\int e^{i\lambda d_{g}(x,y)}a_{\lambda}(t,x,y)f(y)dz+\int r_{\lambda}(t,x,y)f(y)dy,

where
a_{\lambda}(t,x,y)
is supported in
\{d_{g}(x,y)\approx 1\}
and
|\partial_{t}^{k}\partial_{x,y}^{\alpha}a_{\lambda}(t,x,y)|\leq C_{\alpha,k}\lambda^{k},\ \forall\alpha,k.

And the remainder term has a smooth kernel satisfying

|\partial_{t}^{k}\partial_{x,y}^{\alpha}r_{\lambda}(t,x,y)|\leq C_{\alpha,k}\lambda^{-N},\ \ \forall N,\alpha,k.

The positive constants
c,C_{\alpha,k}
are independent of
\lambda
.

Proof.

For convenience, we choose
x
to be geodesic normal coordinates at
x_{0}
and work in a small neighborhood of
x_{0}
.
Let
\chi_{\lambda}=\chi(P-\lambda)-r_{\lambda}
. By Lemma
2
we can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(\chi(P-\lambda)f)(t,x)

\displaystyle=P_{t}\chi(P-\lambda)f(x)+R_{t}\chi(P-\lambda)f(x)

\displaystyle=P_{t}\chi_{\lambda}f(x)+P_{t}r_{\lambda}f(x)+R_{t}\chi(P-\lambda)f(x).

Here

\displaystyle P_{t}\chi_{\lambda}f(x)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-n}\iint e^{i(x-y)\cdot\xi}b(t,x,\xi)\chi_{\lambda}f(y)dyd\xi

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-n}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\iiint e^{i(x-y)\cdot\xi+i\lambda d_{g}(y,z)}b(t,x,\xi)a_{\lambda}(y,z)f(z)dzdyd\xi.

Since
P_{t}
is a smooth family of operators in
OPS^{0}
, it is smoothing off diagonal, and the contribution of
y
away from
x
is
O(\lambda^{-N})
. Thus, we can assume the integral with respect to
y
in the expression of
P_{t}
is taking near diagonal and
\max\{d_{g}(x,x_{0}),d_{g}(y,x)\}\ll d_{g}(x_{0},z)
.
Since the kernel of
R_{t}
is smooth, we can integrate by parts to see that the kernel of
R_{t}\chi(P-\lambda)
is smooth and
O(\lambda^{-N})
. Similarly, the kernel of
P_{t}r_{\lambda}
is also smooth and
O(\lambda^{-N})
.
Let
\beta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfy

{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{[\frac{1}{2},2]}\leq\beta\leq{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{[\frac{1}{4},4]}.

Let

\displaystyle R_{t}^{\lambda}f(x)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-n}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\iiint e^{i(x-y)\cdot\xi+i\lambda d_{g}(y,z)}b(t,x,\xi)(1-\beta(|\xi|_{g(x)}/\lambda))a_{\lambda}(y,z)f(z)dzdyd\xi.

On the support of
1-\beta(|\xi|_{g(x)}/\lambda)
, and
g_{ij}(x)\approx g_{ij}(x_{0})=\delta_{ij}
,
d_{g}(y,z)\approx|y-z|
, we have

|\partial_{y}((x-y)\cdot\xi+\lambda d_{g}(y,z))|\gtrsim\lambda+|\xi|.

So we integrate by parts in
y
to see the kernel of
R_{t}^{\lambda}
is smooth and
O(\lambda^{-N})
.
Let

\displaystyle P_{t}^{\lambda}f(x)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-n}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}+n}\iiint e^{i\lambda((x-y)\cdot\xi+d_{g}(y,z))}b(t,x,\lambda\xi)\beta(|\xi|_{g(x)})a_{\lambda}(y,z)f(z)dyd\xi dz.

By the support property of
a_{\lambda}(y,z)
, we can integrate by parts in
\xi
to see that the kernel

\displaystyle\Big|\iint e^{i\lambda((x-y)\cdot\xi+d_{g}(y,z))}(1-\eta(x,z))b(t,x,\lambda\xi)\beta(|\xi|_{g(x)})a_{\lambda}(y,z)dyd\xi\Big|\lesssim\lambda^{-N}

if
\eta\in C^{\infty}
equals 1 when
|x-z|\approx 1
and 0 otherwise. Since
a(t,x,\xi)=|\xi|_{g_{t}(x)}\mod S^{0}
, by (
4.9
) on the support of
\beta(|\xi|_{g(x)})
we have

\beta(|\xi|_{g(x)})\geq\lambda r(t)|\xi|_{g(x)}+O(1)\gtrsim\lambda r(t).

Then for some
c>0
we can write

(4.11)

b(t,x,\lambda\xi)=e^{-c\lambda G(t)}\tilde{b}(t,x,\lambda\xi),

where on the support of
\beta(|\xi|_{g(x)})
we have

|\partial_{t}^{k}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\tilde{b}(t,x,\lambda\xi)|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta,k}\lambda^{k},\ \forall\alpha,\beta,k.

So it suffices to handle the operator

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{t}^{\lambda}f(x)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-n}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}+n}\iiint e^{i\lambda((x-y)\cdot\xi+d_{g}(y,z))}\eta(x,z)b(t,x,\lambda\xi)\beta(|\xi|_{g(x)})a_{\lambda}(y,z)f(z)dyd\xi dz

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-n}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}+n}e^{-c\lambda G(t)}\iiint e^{i\lambda((x-y)\cdot\xi+d_{g}(y,z))}\eta(x,z)\tilde{b}(t,x,\lambda\xi)\beta(|\xi|_{g(x)})a_{\lambda}(y,z)f(z)dyd\xi dz.

We shall use stationary phase in
(y,\xi)
. Indeed,

\partial_{y,\xi}((x-y)\cdot\xi+d_{g}(y,z))=0\Rightarrow y=x,\ \xi=\partial_{y}d_{g}(y,z),

and

\partial_{y,\xi}^{2}((x-y)\cdot\xi+d_{g}(y,z))=\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{y}^{2}d_{g}(y,z)&-I\\
-I&0\end{bmatrix}.

Thus,
(y,\xi)=(x,\partial_{y}d_{g}(y,z))
is a non-degenerate critical point of the phase function. By stationary phase
[
35
, Corollary 1.1.8]
, we can write

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{t}^{\lambda}f(x)=\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}e^{-c\lambda G(t)}\int e^{i\lambda d_{g}(x,z)}a_{\lambda}(t,x,z)f(z)dz,

where
a_{\lambda}(t,x,z)
is supported in
\{d_{g}(x,z)\approx 1\}
and
|\partial_{t}^{k}\partial_{x,y}^{\alpha}a_{\lambda}(t,x,y)|\leq C_{\alpha,k}\lambda^{k},\ \forall\alpha,k.
∎

By using an essentially the same argument, we can obtain the kernel of the composition operator
Q\circ\chi(P-\lambda)
for any
Q\in OPS^{m}(M)
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
Q\in OPS^{m}(M)
. We have

(4.12)

(Q\circ\chi(P-\lambda))(x,y)=\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}+m}e^{i\lambda d_{g}(x,y)}\tilde{a}_{\lambda}(x,y)+\tilde{r}_{\lambda}(x,y)

where
\tilde{a}_{\lambda}(x,y)
is supported in
\{d_{g}(x,y)\approx 1\}
and
|\partial_{x,y}^{\alpha}\tilde{a}_{\lambda}(x,y)|\leq C_{\alpha},\ \forall\alpha.

And the remainder term is smooth and satisfies

|\partial_{x,y}^{\alpha}\tilde{r}_{\lambda}(x,y)|\leq C_{\alpha},\ \ \forall\alpha.

The positive constants
C_{\alpha}
are independent of
\lambda
.

This lemma implies that the kernel of
Q\circ\chi(P-\lambda)
has essentially the same form as the kernel of
\chi(P-\lambda)
. So one may use it to obtain natural eigenfunction estimates under the operations in
OPS^{m}
, which extend the seminal works by Sogge
[
35
]
and Burq-Gérard-Tzvetkov
[
13
,
11
,
12
]
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
Q\in OPS^{m}(M)
. For
\lambda\geq 1
, we have

(4.13)

\|Q\circ\chi(P-\lambda)f\|_{L^{p}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{m+\sigma(p)}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)},\ \ 2\leq p\leq\infty,

where

\sigma(p)=\begin{cases}\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}),\ \ \ 2\leq p<\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}\\
\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n}{p},\ \ \ \ \ \ \frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}\leq p\leq\infty.\end{cases}

Proposition 3
.

(Bilinear estimates)
Let
m_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
and
Q_{j}\in OPS^{m_{j}}(M)
for
j=1,2
. If
\mu\geq\lambda\geq 1
, then we have

(4.14)

\|Q_{1}\circ\chi(P-\lambda)f\cdot Q_{2}\circ\chi(P-\mu)g\|_{L^{2}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{m_{1}}\mu^{m_{2}}B\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)}\|g\|_{L^{2}(M)},

where

B=\begin{cases}\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ n=2\\
\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\log\lambda},\ \ n=3\\
\lambda^{\frac{n-2}{2}},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ n\geq 4.\end{cases}

Proposition 4
.

(Restriction estimates)
Let
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
Q\in OPS^{m}(M)
. Let
\Sigma
be a
k
-dimensional submanifold in
M
. For
\lambda\geq 1
, we have

(4.15)

\|Q\circ\chi(P-\lambda)f\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\lambda^{m}A\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)},\ \ 2\leq p\leq\infty,

where

•

If
k\leq n-3
, then
A=\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{k}{p}}
for
2\leq p\leq\infty
.

•

If
k=n-2
, then
A=\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{k}{p}}
for
2<p\leq\infty
, and
A=\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(\log\lambda)^{\frac{1}{2}}
for
p=2
.

•

If
k=n-1
, then
A=\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{k}{p}}
for
\frac{2n}{n-1}\leq p\leq\infty
, and
A=\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{4}-\frac{n-2}{2p}}
for

Let

with

\{|t|<1\}

We decompose

\displaystyle m_{\lambda}(P)

\displaystyle|r_{\lambda}(\tau)|

\displaystyle=|(2\pi)^{-1}\int(1-\hat{\rho}(t))\hat{m}_{\lambda}(t)e^{it\tau}dt|

\displaystyle=|(2\pi)^{-1}\iint(1-\hat{\rho}(t))m_{\lambda}(s)e^{-its}e^{it\tau}dsdt|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int(1+|s-\tau|)^{-N_{1}}(1+|s|)^{\mu_{1}-N}ds

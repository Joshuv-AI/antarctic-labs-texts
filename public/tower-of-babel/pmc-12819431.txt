Laparoscopic radical right hemicolectomy with CME, CVL and division of right gastroepiploic vessels
Authors: Zi Qin Ng, Jih Huei Tan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis case report describes the laparoscopic technique of infrapyloric lymph node dissection as part of a complete mesocolic excision (CME) for right-sided colon cancer. While controversial and not routinely performed, this extended dissection is indicated in the presence of specific risk factors for nodal metastasis, such as elevated CEA (> 17 ng/ml) and obstructive symptoms, as presented in our 88-year-old patient. The article outlines the key surgical steps to safely clear the infrapyloric region, emphasizing ligation of the right gastroepiploic vessels and meticulous dissection to minimize bleeding and chyle leakage.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s10151-025-03270-9.Keywords: Right colon, Cancer, Infrapyloric, Lymph node, 206

Laparoscopic right hemicolectomy with complete mesocolic excision (CME) is increasingly adopted across centres because of improved oncological outcomes [1]. CME includes sharp dissection along the embryological planes to maintain an intact mesocolon, central vascular ligation at the origin and adequate length of bowel resection to harvest the pericolic lymph nodes. However, the field of dissection in CME varies significantly according to a recent systematic review [2]. Only 18% of studies included infrapyloric lymph node dissection as part of the CME [2].

A recent review found that the lymph node metastasis rate to the infrapyloric region or station 206 ranges from 0.7 to 22% [3]. Dissection of this region remains controversial, as it is not considered part of the derivative of colonic embryology [4]. Nevertheless, there is a complex interplay of lymphovascular drainage at the gastrocolic trunk, which has a key connection between the gastroduodenum at the right colon [3, 4]. The previous systematic review identified the risk factors of infrapyloric node involvement in endoscopic obstruction, signet ring adenocarcinoma, CEA level ≥ 17 ng/ml and M1 stage [3]. In the current case, we present an 88-year-old woman who presented with right-sided abdominal pain and difficult passing motion. Her CEA was raised at 18 ng/ml; CT scan showed a hepatic flexure tumour with ileocolic node enlargement but no other distant metastasis. She had laparoscopic extended right hemicolectomy with CME and central vascular ligation (CVL) including infrapyloric nodal dissection (with risk factors such as high CEA and obstructive symptoms). The post-operative recovery was uneventful.

Infrapyloric nodal dissection requires ligation of the right gastroepiploic artery and vein to clear all the fibrofatty tissue located along the vessels. Video 1 demonstrates the en bloc dissection of the infrapyloric lymph node region with the CME.

A combination of SMV dissection with the supracolic dissection helps to reveal the anatomy clearly and decreases the potential risk of bleeding. The key steps of the infrapyloric dissection are: to have the assistant lift the pylorus and dissect the mesocolon until the pancreas is visualized; to dissect above the head of the pancreas capsule; to clip any lymphatics to prevent chyle leakage.

The authors have performed over 20 cases with extended lymphadenectomy in the infrapyloric region and have not encountered complications such as bleeding, delayed gastric emptying, paralytic ileus or chyle leakage.

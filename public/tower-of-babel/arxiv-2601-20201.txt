Kaimei Huang, Yongzhou Wen,
Sherry H.F. Yan

Abstract




Further refinements of Euler-Mahonian statistics for multipermutations

Abstract.

Permutation statistics constitute a classical subject of enumerative combinatorics. In her study of the genus zeta function, Denert discovered a new Mahonian statistic for permutations, which is called the Denert’s statistic (
\mathsf{den}
) by Foata and Zeilberger. As natural extensions of the
r
-descent number (
r\mathsf{des}
) and the
r
-major index (
r\mathsf{maj}
) introduced by Rawlings, Liu introduced the
g
-gap
\ell
-level descent number (
g\mathsf{des}_{\ell}
) and the
g
-gap
\ell
-level major index (
g\mathsf{maj}_{\ell}
) for permutations.
In this paper, we introduce the
g
-gap
\ell
-level Denert’s statistic (
g\mathsf{den}_{\ell}
) and the
g
-gap
\ell
-level excedance number (
g\mathsf{exc}_{\ell}
) for multipermutations, which serve as natural generalizations of the Denert’s statistic (
\mathsf{den}
) and the excedance number (
\mathsf{exc}
) for multipermutations first introduced by Han. By constructing two explicit bijections, we establish the equidistribution of the pairs
(g\mathsf{exc}_{\ell},g\mathsf{den}_{h})
and
(g\mathsf{des}_{\ell},g\mathsf{maj}_{\ell})
over multipermutations for all
1\leq h\leq g+\ell
. Our result provides a new proof of the equidistribution of the pairs (
\mathsf{des}
,
\mathsf{maj}
) and (
\mathsf{exc}
,
\mathsf{den}
) over multipermutations originally derived by Han and enables us to confirm a recent conjecture posed by Huang-Lin-Yan. Furthermore, we demonstrate that for
all
1\leq h\leq g+\ell
, the pair
(g\mathsf{exc}_{\ell},g\mathsf{den}_{h})
is
r
-Euler-Mahonian over multipermutations of
M=\{1^{k},2^{k},\ldots,n^{k}\}
where
r=g+\ell-1
and
k\geq 1
, which extends a recent novel result derived by Liu from permutations to multipermutations.

Keywords
:
g
-gap
\ell
-level descent number,
g
-gap
\ell
-level major index,
g
-gap
\ell
-level Denert’s statistic,
g
-gap
\ell
-level excedance number,
r
-Euler-Mahonian statistic.

1
Introduction

Let
M=\{1^{k_{1}},2^{k_{2}},\ldots,n^{k_{n}}\}
be a multiset where
i^{k_{i}}
denotes
k_{i}
occurrences of
i
. Unless specified otherwise, we always assume that
M=\{1^{k_{1}},2^{k_{2}},\ldots,n^{k_{n}}\}
where
k_{i}\geq 1
and
k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{n}=m
. Let
\mathfrak{S}_{M}
denote the set of all multipermutations on
M
. For a positive integer
n
, let
[n]:=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. When
M=[n]
, we simply write
\mathfrak{S}_{M}
as
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.

Given a multipermutation
w=\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\ldots\alpha_{m}\in\mathfrak{S}_{M}
,
the
descent set
of
w
is defined to be

\mathsf{Des}(w)=\{i\in[m-1]\mid\alpha_{i}>\alpha_{i+1}\},

and
the
descent number
of
w
, denoted by
\mathsf{des}(w)
, is defined to be the cardinality of its descent set.
The
major index
of
w
, denoted by
\mathsf{maj}(w)
, is defined to be

\mathsf{maj}(w)=\sum\limits_{i\in\mathsf{Des}(w)}i.

A pair
(i,j)
is called an
inversion (resp., weakly inversion) pair
of
w
if
i<j
and
\alpha_{i}>\alpha_{j}
(resp.,
\alpha_{i}\geq\alpha_{j}
). Let
\mathsf{Inv}(w)
(resp.,
\mathsf{Imv}(w)
) denote the set of all inversion (resp., weakly inversion) pairs of
w
. The
inversion (resp., weakly inversion) number
of
w
is defined as
\mathsf{inv}(w)=|\mathsf{Inv}(w)|
(resp.,
\mathsf{imv}(w)=|\mathsf{Imv}(w)|
). For example, if
w=4121155325
, then
\mathsf{Des}(w)=\{1,3,7,8\}
,
\mathsf{des}(w)=4
,
\mathsf{maj}(w)=1+3+7+8=19
,
\mathsf{inv}(w)=13
and
\mathsf{imv}(w)=20
. MacMahon’s equidistribution theorem
[
15
]
asserts that

\sum\limits_{w\in\mathfrak{S}_{M}}q^{\mathsf{inv}(w)}=\sum\limits_{w\in\mathfrak{S}_{M}}q^{\mathsf{maj}(w)}.

(1.1)

A statistic is said to be
Mahonian
if it is equidistributed with
\mathsf{inv}
over
\mathfrak{S}_{M}
.
MacMahon type results for other combinatorial objects constitute a classical subject of enumerative combinatorics and have been investigated extensively in the literature;
see
[
2
,
3
,
13
,
14
,
16
,
19
,
20
]
and the references therein.

As a natural generalization of the descent number and the major index,
Liu
[
12
]
introduced the notions of the
g
-gap
\ell
-level descent number
g\mathsf{des}_{\ell}
and the
g
-gap
\ell
-level major index
g\mathsf{maj}_{\ell}
for permutations. Here we extend Liu’s definitions from permutations to multipermutations.
Let
g,\ell\geq 1
. Given a multipermutation
w=\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\ldots\alpha_{m}\in\mathfrak{S}_{M}
, let

g\mathsf{Des}_{\ell}(w)=\{i\in[n-1]\mid\alpha_{i}\geq\alpha_{i+1}+g,\alpha_{i+1}\geq\ell\},

g\mathsf{Inv}_{\ell}(w)=\{(i,j)\in\mathsf{Inv}(w)\mid\alpha_{j}<\ell\,\,\mbox{or}\,\,\alpha_{i}<\alpha_{j}+g\}.

An index
i
is said to be an
g
-gap
\ell
-level descent
of
w
if
i\in g\mathsf{Des}_{\ell}(w)
. The
g
-gap
\ell
-level descent number
of
w
, denoted by
g\mathsf{des}_{\ell}(w)
, is defined to be the number of
g
-gap
\ell
-level descents of
w
.
The
g
-gap
\ell
-level major index
of
w
is defined to be

g\mathsf{maj}_{\ell}(w)=\sum\limits_{i\in g\mathsf{Des}_{\ell}(w)}i+|g\mathsf{Inv}_{\ell}(w)|.

For example, if we let
w=4121155325
, then
2\mathsf{Des}_{3}(w)=\{7\}
,
2\mathsf{des}_{3}(w)=1
and
2\mathsf{Inv}_{3}=\{(1,2),(1,3),(1,4),(1,5),(1,8),(1,9),(3,4),(3,5),(6,9),(7,9),(8,9)\}
. Thus
2\mathsf{maj}_{3}(w)
is given by

\begin{array}[]{ll}2\mathsf{maj}_{3}(w)&=\sum\limits_{i\in 2\mathsf{Des}_{3}(w)}i+|2\mathsf{Inv}_{3}(w)|\\
&=7+11\\
&=18.\end{array}

When
g=\ell=1
, the statistics
g\mathsf{maj}_{\ell}
and
g\mathsf{des}_{\ell}
reduce to the major index
\mathsf{maj}
and the descent number
\mathsf{des}
, respectively.
The
r
-gap
1
-level major index of
w
is also referred as
r
-major index
of
w
, denoted by
r\mathsf{maj}(w)
, and the
r
-gap
1
-level descent number of
w
is called the
r
-descent number
of
w
, denoted by
r\mathsf{des}(\pi)
. These notations were first introduced by Rawlings
[
17
,
18
]
who also established the equidistribution of the statistics
r\mathsf{maj}
and
\mathsf{inv}
over
\mathfrak{S}_{M}
.
The
1
-gap
r
-level major index of
w
is called the
r
-level major index
of
w
, denoted by
\mathsf{maj}_{r}(w)
, and the
1
-gap
r
-level descent of
w
is called the
r
-level descent number
of
w
, denoted by
\mathsf{des}_{r}(w)
in
[
12
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

A pair
(\mathsf{st}_{1},\mathsf{st}_{2})
of permutation statistics is said to be
r
-Euler-Mahonian
over
\mathfrak{S}_{M}
if

\sum\limits_{w\in\mathfrak{S}_{M}}t^{\mathsf{st}_{1}(w)}q^{\mathsf{st}_{2}(w)}=\sum\limits_{w\in\mathfrak{S}_{M}}t^{r\mathsf{des}(w)}q^{r\mathsf{maj}(w)}.

The
1
-Euler-Mahonian statistic is also referred as the
Euler-Mahonian
statistic.
Liu
[
12
]
proved that for all
g,\ell\geq 1
, the pair
(g\mathsf{des}_{\ell},g\mathsf{maj}_{\ell})
is
r
-Euler-Mahonian
over permutations, that is,

\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}t^{r\mathsf{des}(\pi)}q^{r\mathsf{maj}(\pi)}=\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}t^{g\mathsf{des}_{\ell}(\pi)}q^{g\mathsf{maj}_{\ell}(\pi)},

(1.2)

where
r=g+\ell-1
.

In the course of seeking Euler-Mahonian statistics for multipermutations, Han
[
7
]
extended the
Denert’s statistic
, originally introduced by Denert
[
4
]
for permutations in her study of the genus zeta function, to multipermutations.
Suppose that
\bar{w}=x_{1}x_{2}\ldots x_{m}
is the weakly increasing rearrangement of
w
. An index
i
with
1\leq i<m
is called an
excedance place

of
w
if
\alpha_{i}>x_{i}
. Let
\mathsf{Excp}(w)
denote the set of excedance places of
w
and let
\mathsf{exc}(w)=|\mathsf{Excp}(w)|
be the
excedance number
of
w
. For instance, given

\begin{pmatrix}\bar{w}\\
w\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bf 1}1{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bf 1}22{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bf 3}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bf 4}555\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bf 4}1{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bf 2}11{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bf 5}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bf 5}325\end{pmatrix},

(1.3)

we have
\mathsf{Excp}(w)=\{1,3,6,7\}
and
\mathsf{exc}(w)=4
. Define
\mathsf{Nexcp}(w)=[m]\setminus\mathsf{Excp}(w)
as the set of non-excedance places of
w
. For
i\in[m]
, if
i\in\mathsf{Excp}(w)
, then
\alpha_{i}
is called an
excedance letter
of
w
; otherwise,
i\in\mathsf{Nexcp}(w)
and
\alpha_{i}
is a
non-excedance letter
of
w
. Let
\mathsf{Exc}(w)
and
\mathsf{Nexc}(w)
be the subsequences of
w
formed by all the excedance and non-excedance letters of
w
, respectively. Then the
Denert’s statistic
of
w
, denoted by
\mathsf{den}(w)
, is defined as

\mathsf{den}(w)=\sum\limits_{i\in\mathsf{Excp}(w)}i+{\mathsf{imv}}(\mathsf{Exc}(w))+{\mathsf{inv}}(\mathsf{Nexc}(w)).

Continuing with the example
w=4121155325
in (
1.3
), we have

\mathsf{Exc}(w)=4255
and
\mathsf{Nexc}(w)=111325
.
Then
\mathsf{den}(w)
is given by

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathsf{den}(w)&=\sum\limits_{i\in\mathsf{Excp}(w)}i+{\mathsf{imv}}(\mathsf{Exc}(w))+{\mathsf{inv}}(\mathsf{Nexc}(w))\\
&=1+3+6+7+{\mathsf{imv}}(4255)+{\mathsf{inv}}(111325)\\
&=17+2+1\\
&=20.\end{array}

Denert
[
4
]
proposed an intersting conjecture which asserts that

(\mathsf{exc},\mathsf{den})
is Euler-Mahonian on permutations, that is,

\sum\limits_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}t^{\mathsf{exc}(\pi)}q^{\mathsf{den}(\pi)}=\sum\limits_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}t^{\mathsf{des}(\pi)}q^{\mathsf{maj}(\pi)}.

(1.4)

This conjecture was first proved by
Foata and Zeilberger
[
5
]
, and a bijective proof was later provided by Han
[
6
]
. The following multipermutation version of (
1.4
) was attributed to Han
[
7
]
:

\sum\limits_{w\in\mathfrak{S}_{M}}t^{\mathsf{des}(w)}q^{\mathsf{maj}(w)}=\sum\limits_{w\in\mathfrak{S}_{M}}t^{\mathsf{exc}(w)}q^{\mathsf{den}(w)}.

(1.5)

Let
w=\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\ldots\alpha_{m}\in\mathfrak{S}_{M}
be a multipermutation whose weakly increasing rearrangement is
\bar{w}=x_{1}x_{2}\ldots x_{m}
. An index
i\in[m]
is called an
g
-gap
\ell
-level excedance place
and
\alpha_{i}
is called an
g
-gap
\ell
-level excedance letter
if
\alpha_{i}\geq x_{i}+g
and
\alpha_{i}\geq\ell
. We denote by
g\mathsf{Excp}_{\ell}(w)
the set of
g
-gap
\ell
-level excedance places of
w
and by
g\mathsf{Nexcp}_{\ell}(w)
the set of non-
g
-gap-
\ell
-level excedance places of
w
, respectively.
Assume that
g\mathsf{Excp}_{\ell}(w)=\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}\}
with
i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}
and
g\mathsf{Nexcp}_{\ell}(w)=\{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{m-k}\}

with
j_{1}<j_{2}<\cdots<j_{m-k}
. Define

g\mathsf{Exc}_{\ell}(w)=\alpha_{i_{1}}\alpha_{i_{2}}\ldots\alpha_{i_{k}},

and

g\mathsf{Nexc}_{\ell}(w)=\alpha_{j_{1}}\alpha_{j_{2}}\ldots\alpha_{j_{m-k}}.

Definition 1.2
.

The
g
-gap
\ell
-level Denert’s statistic
of
w
, denoted by
g\mathsf{den}_{\ell}(w)
, is defined as

g\mathsf{den}_{\ell}(w)=\sum\limits_{i\in g\mathsf{Excp}_{\ell}(w)}(i+B^{g}_{i}(w))+{\mathsf{imv}}(g\mathsf{Exc}_{\ell}(w))+{\mathsf{inv}}(g\mathsf{Nexc}_{\ell}(w)),

where
B^{g}_{i}(w)=|\{j\mid\alpha_{i}-g<x_{j}<\alpha_{i}\}|
.

For example, if

\begin{pmatrix}\bar{w}\\
w\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bf 1}1122{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bf 3}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bf 4}556\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bf 4}2311{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bf 5}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bf 6}125\end{pmatrix},

(1.6)

then we have
2\mathsf{Excp}_{4}(w)=\{1,6,7\}
,
2\mathsf{Exc}_{4}(w)=456
and
2\mathsf{Nexc}_{4}(w)=2311125
. Hence,
2\mathsf{den}_{4}(w)
is given by

\begin{array}[]{ll}2\mathsf{den}_{4}(w)&=\sum\limits_{i\in 2\mathsf{Excp}_{4}(w)}(i+B^{2}_{i}(w))+{\mathsf{imv}}(2\mathsf{Exc}_{4}(w))+{\mathsf{inv}}(2\mathsf{Nexc}_{4}(w))\\
&=(1+1)+(6+1)+(7+2)+{\mathsf{imv}}(456)+{\mathsf{inv}}(2311125)\\
&=18+0+7\\
&=25.\end{array}

Definition 1.3
.

The
g
-gap
\ell
-level excedance number
of
w
, denoted by
g\mathsf{exc}_{\ell}(w)
, is defined as

g\mathsf{exc}_{\ell}(w)=|\{i\mid\alpha_{i}\geq x_{i}+g,x_{i}\geq\ell\}|.

Continuing with the example
w=4231156125
in (
1.6
), we have
2\mathsf{exc}_{4}(w)=1
.
Clearly,
g\mathsf{den}_{\ell}
and
g\mathsf{exc}_{\ell}
reduces to
\mathsf{den}
and
\mathsf{exc}
respectively when
g=\ell=1
.

Remark 1.4
.

It is easily seen that for any
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, we have

g\mathsf{den}_{\ell}(\pi)=\sum\limits_{i\in g\mathsf{Excp}_{\ell}(\pi)}(i+g-1)+{\mathsf{inv}}(g\mathsf{Exc}_{\ell}(\pi))+{\mathsf{inv}}(g\mathsf{Nexc}_{\ell}(\pi)),

and

g\mathsf{exc}_{\ell}(\pi)=|\{i\mid\pi_{i}\geq i+g,i\geq\ell\}|,

which coincide with the statistics
g\mathsf{den}_{\ell}
and
g\mathsf{exc}_{\ell}
employed in
[
10
,
12
]
.

The
r
-gap
1
-level Denert’s statistic of
w
is called the
r
-gap Denert’s statistic
of
w
, denoted by
r\mathsf{den}(w)
, and the
r
-gap
1
-level excedance number of
w
is called the
r
-gap excedance number
of
w
, denoted by
r\mathsf{exc}(w)
. Similarly, The
1
-gap
r
-level Denert’s statistic of
w
is called the
r
-level Denert’s statistic
of
w
, denoted by
\mathsf{den}_{r}(w)
, and the
1
-gap

-level excedance number of

is called the

of

, denoted by

Motivated by finding refinments of (

), Liu
[

]
proved that

\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}t^{r\mathsf{des}(\pi)}q^{r\mathsf{maj}(\pi)}=\sum_{\pi\mathfrak{S}_{n}}t^{r\mathsf{exc}(\pi)}q^{r\mathsf{den}(\pi)}

and further conjectured that

\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}t^{r\mathsf{des}(\pi)}q^{r\mathsf{maj}(\pi)}=\sum_{\pi\mathfrak{S}_{n}}t^{\mathsf{exc}_{r}(\pi)}q^{\mathsf{den}_{r}(\pi)}.

This conjecture was later confirmed by Huang-Lin-Yan
[
8
]
.
As a further extension of (
1.7
) and (

\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}t^{r\mathsf{des}(\pi)}q^{r\mathsf{maj}(\pi)}=\sum_{\pi\mathfrak{S}_{n}}t^{g\mathsf{exc}_{\ell}(\pi)}q^{g\mathsf{den}_{\ell}(\pi)}=\sum_{\pi\mathfrak{S}_{n}}t^{g\mathsf{exc}_{\ell}(\pi)}q^{g\mathsf{den}_{g+\ell}(\pi)},

where

[

Combining (

A

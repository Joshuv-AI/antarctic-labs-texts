Efficient Weak Galerkin Finite Element Methods for Maxwell Equations on polyhedral Meshes without Convexity Constraints
Chunmei Wang, Shangyou Zhang

Abstract
This paper presents an efficient weak Galerkin (WG) finite element method with reduced stabilizers for solving the time-harmonic Maxwell equations on both convex and non-convex polyhedral meshes. By employing bubble functions as a critical analytical tool, the proposed method enhances efficiency by partially eliminating the stabilizers traditionally used in WG methods. This streamlined WG method demonstrates stability and effectiveness on convex and non-convex polyhedral meshes, representing a significant improvement over existing stabilizer-free WG methods, which are typically limited to convex elements within finite element partitions. The method achieves an optimal error estimate for the exact solution in a discrete H 1 H^{1} norm, and additionally, an optimal L 2 L^{2} error estimate is established for the WG solution. Several numerical experiments are conducted to validate the method’s efficiency and accuracy.

Introduction

In this paper, we aim to develop an efficient weak Galerkin finite element method with reduced stabilizers specifically designed for solving the Maxwell equations without convexity constraints. The Maxwell equations are coupled magnetic and electric equations described by:

(1.1)

\begin{split}\nabla\times{\mathbf{E}}=&-\frac{\partial{\mathbf{B}}}{\partial t},\\
\nabla\times{\mathbf{H}}=&\frac{\partial{\mathbf{D}}}{\partial t}+{\mathbf{j}},\\
\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{D}}=&\rho,\\
\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{B}}=&0,\end{split}

along with the constitutive relations:

{\mathbf{B}}=\mu{\mathbf{H}},\qquad{\mathbf{j}}=\sigma{\mathbf{E}},\qquad{\mathbf{D}}=\epsilon{\mathbf{E}}.

Here
{\mathbf{E}}
represents the electric field intensity,
{\mathbf{H}}
is the magnetic field
intensity,
{\mathbf{D}}
is the displacement current density,
{\mathbf{B}}
denotes the magnetic flux density,
{\mathbf{j}}
signifies the electric current density,
\mu
is the
permeability,
\sigma
is the electric conductivity,
\rho
is the charge density, and
\epsilon
is the permittivity.

For time-harmonic fields, where the time dependence is assumed to follow a harmonic form, i.e.,

exp(i\omega t)
, the Maxwell equations (
1.1
) can be re-expressed for the Fourier transform of the fields by utilizing the constitutive relations as follows

(1.2)

\begin{split}\nabla\times{\mathbf{E}}=&-i\omega\mu{\mathbf{H}},\\
\nabla\times{\mathbf{H}}=&i\omega\epsilon{\mathbf{E}}+\sigma{\mathbf{E}},\\
\nabla\cdot(\epsilon{\mathbf{E}})=&\rho,\\
\nabla\cdot(\mu{\mathbf{H}})=&0.\end{split}

By applying the operator
\nabla\times\mu^{-1}
to the first equation in (
1.2
)
and incorporating the second equation in (
1.2
), we derive

(1.3)

\nabla\times(\mu^{-1}\nabla\times{\mathbf{E}})=(\omega^{2}\epsilon-i\omega\sigma){\mathbf{E}}.

Thus, the electric field intensity
{\mathbf{E}}
can be solved using (
1.3
) and the third equation in (
1.2
), along with appropriate boundary conditions. Subsequently, the magnetic field intensity
{\mathbf{H}}
can be determined using the second and fourth equations in (
1.2
), together with the boundary conditions.

For simplicity, we focus on the equations for the electric field intensity
{\mathbf{E}}
with
\omega=0
in
a heterogeneous medium
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
. A generalized mixed formulation of this model problem
aims to find unknown vector-valued function
{\mathbf{u}}
and scalar-valued
p
that satisfy

(1.4)

\begin{split}\nabla\times(\mu^{-1}\nabla\times{\mathbf{u}})-\epsilon\nabla p=&{\mathbf{f}}_{1},\qquad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
\nabla\cdot(\epsilon{\mathbf{u}})=&g_{1},\qquad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
{\mathbf{u}}\times{\mathbf{n}}=&{\boldsymbol{\phi}},\qquad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega,\\
p=&0,\qquad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega.\end{split}

This paper introduces an efficient weak Galerkin finite element method for solving Maxwell’s equations. Our contributions are summarized as follows:

1. Reduction of Stabilizers:
Compared to traditional stabilizer-dependent WG methods, our method reduces the need for certain stabilizers. Specifically, by utilizing higher-degree polynomials to compute the discrete weak curl, one stabilizer for the discrete weak curl is eliminated, while the stabilizer for the discrete weak gradient is retained. The proposed method maintains the size and global sparsity of the stiffness matrix, thereby reducing programming complexity compared to traditional stabilizer-dependent WG methods.

2. Applicability to Non-Convex polyhedral Meshes:
Unlike existing stabilizer-free WG methods
[
51
,
52
]
, which typically work only on convex elements, our method is applicable to both convex and non-convex elements within polyhedral meshes. This is achieved by employing bubble functions as a critical analytical tool, allowing us to circumvent the restrictive conditions commonly imposed by other stabilizer-free WG methods. This flexibility facilitates broader generalization to various PDEs without the usual implementation challenges. Recently, one of the authors has proposed stabilizer-free WG methods that do not require the convexity assumption, applied to solving the Poisson equation and the biharmonic equation
[
26
,
27
]
. To the best of our knowledge, this paper is the first to propose WG methods with reduced stabilizers for the Maxwell equations.
Theoretical analysis demonstrates that our method achieves an optimal error estimate for WG approximations in a discrete

L^{2}

2
Weak Formulations

In this section, we aim to derive the weak formulation for the Maxwell equations (
1.1
). To this end, we first introduce the following spaces:

H(curl;\Omega)=\{{\mathbf{u}}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{3}:\nabla\times{\mathbf{u}}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{3}\},

H_{0}(curl;\Omega)=\{{\mathbf{u}}\in H(curl;\Omega):{\mathbf{n}}\times{\mathbf{u}}=0\ \text{on}\ \partial\Omega\},

H(div;\Omega)=\{{\mathbf{u}}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{3}:\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{u}}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\},

H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)=\{q\in H^{1}(\Omega):q=0\ \text{on}\ \partial\Omega\},

where
{\mathbf{n}}
denotes the unit outward normal vector to the boundary
\partial\Omega
.

By applying the standard integration by parts, we can derive a weak formulation for the Maxwell model equations (
1.4
). This weak formulation seeks

({\mathbf{u}},p)\in H(curl;\Omega)\times H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that
{\mathbf{u}}\times{\mathbf{n}}={\boldsymbol{\phi}}
on
\partial\Omega
and

(2.1)

\begin{split}(\nu\nabla\times{\mathbf{u}},\nabla\times{\mathbf{v}})-({\mathbf{v}},\nabla p)=&({\mathbf{f}},{\mathbf{v}}),\qquad\forall{\mathbf{v}}\in H_{0}(curl;\Omega),\\
({\mathbf{u}},\nabla q)=&-(g,q),\qquad\forall q\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\end{split}

where

\nu=\frac{1}{\mu\epsilon}
,
{\mathbf{f}}=\frac{{\mathbf{f}}_{1}}{\epsilon}
, and
g=\frac{g_{1}}{\epsilon}
.

3
Weak Differential Operators

The principal differential operators in the weak formulation (
2.1
) for the Maxwell equations (
1.1
) are the gradient operator
\nabla
and the curl operator
\nabla\times
. In this section, we briefly review the discrete weak gradient operator
[
3
,
6
]
and the discrete weak curl operator
[
28
,
45
]
.

Let
T
be a polyhedral domain with boundary
\partial T
. A scalar-valued weak function on
T
refers to
\sigma=\{\sigma_{0},\sigma_{b}\}
where
\sigma_{0}\in L^{2}(T)
and
\sigma_{b}\in L^{2}(\partial T)
. Here
\sigma_{0}
and
\sigma_{b}
represent the values of
\sigma
in the interior and on the boundary of
T
, respectively. Note that
\sigma_{b}
may not necessarily be the trace of
\sigma_{0}
on
\partial T
. Denote by
{\mathcal{W}}(T)
the space of scalar-valued weak functions on
T
, i.e.,

{\mathcal{W}}(T)=\{\sigma=\{\sigma_{0},\sigma_{b}\}:\sigma_{0}\in L^{2}(T),\sigma_{b}\in L^{2}(\partial T)\}.

A vector-valued weak function on
T
refers to
{\mathbf{v}}=\{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\}
where
{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\in[L^{2}(T)]^{3}
and
{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\in[L^{2}(\partial T)]^{3}
. Here,
{\mathbf{v}}_{0}
and
{\mathbf{v}}_{b}
represent the values of
{\mathbf{v}}
in the interior and on the boundary of
T
, respectively. Note that
{\mathbf{v}}_{b}
may not necessarily be the trace of
{\mathbf{v}}_{0}
on
\partial T
. Denote by
V(T)
the space of vector-valued weak functions on
T
, i.e.,

V(T)=\{{\mathbf{v}}=\{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\}:{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\in[L^{2}(T)]^{3},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\in[L^{2}(\partial T)]^{3}\}.

The weak gradient of
\sigma\in{\mathcal{W}}(T)
, denoted by
\nabla_{w}\sigma
, is defined as a linear functional on
[H^{1}(T)]^{3}
such that

(\nabla_{w}\sigma,\boldsymbol{\psi})_{T}=-(\sigma_{0},\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\psi})_{T}+\langle\sigma_{b},\boldsymbol{\psi}\cdot\textbf{n}\rangle_{\partial T},

for all
\boldsymbol{\psi}\in[H^{1}(T)]^{3}
.

The weak curl operator of any
{\mathbf{v}}\in V(T)
, denoted by
\nabla_{w}\times{\mathbf{v}}
, is defined in the dual space of
H(curl;T)
. Its action on
{\mathbf{q}}\in H(curl;T)
is given by

(\nabla_{w}\times{\mathbf{v}},{\mathbf{q}})_{T}=({\mathbf{v}}_{0},\nabla\times{\mathbf{q}})_{T}-\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\times{\mathbf{n}},{\mathbf{q}}\rangle_{\partial T}.

The weak divergence operator of any
{\mathbf{v}}\in V(T)
, denoted by
\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}
, is defined in the dual space of
H^{1}(T)
. Its action on
q\in H^{1}(T)
is given by

(\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}},q)_{T}=-({\mathbf{v}}_{0},\nabla q)_{T}+\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\cdot{\mathbf{n}},q\rangle_{\partial T}.

Denote by
P_{r}(T)
the space of polynomials on
T
with degree no more than
r
.
A discrete version of
\nabla_{w}\sigma
for
\sigma\in{\mathcal{W}}(T)
, denoted by
\nabla_{w,r,T}\sigma
, is defined as the unique polynomial vector in
[P_{r}(T)]^{3}
satisfying

(3.1)

(\nabla_{w,r,T}\sigma,\boldsymbol{\psi})_{T}=-(\sigma_{0},\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\psi})_{T}+\langle\sigma_{b},\boldsymbol{\psi}\cdot\textbf{n}\rangle_{\partial T},

for all
\boldsymbol{\psi}\in[P_{r}(T)]^{3}
. The usual integration by parts gives:

(3.2)

(\nabla_{w,r,T}\sigma,\boldsymbol{\psi})_{T}=(\nabla\sigma_{0},\boldsymbol{\psi})_{T}-\langle\sigma_{0}-\sigma_{b},\boldsymbol{\psi}\cdot\textbf{n}\rangle_{\partial T},

for any
\boldsymbol{\psi}\in[P_{r}(T)]^{3}
, provided that
\sigma_{0}\in H^{1}(T)
.

A discrete version of
\nabla_{w}\times{\mathbf{v}}
for
{\mathbf{v}}\in V(T)
, denoted by
\nabla_{w,r,T}\times{\mathbf{v}}
, is defined as the unique polynomial vector in
[P_{r}(T)]^{3}
satisfying

(3.3)

(\nabla_{w,r,T}\times{\mathbf{v}},{\mathbf{q}})_{T}=({\mathbf{v}}_{0},\nabla\times{\mathbf{q}})_{T}-\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\times{\mathbf{n}},{\mathbf{q}}\rangle_{\partial T},

for any
{\mathbf{q}}\in[P_{r}(T)]^{3}
. The usual integration by parts yields:

(3.4)

\begin{split}(\nabla_{w,r,T}\times{\mathbf{v}},{\mathbf{q}})_{T}=(\nabla\times{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{q}})_{T}-\langle({\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0})\times{\mathbf{n}},{\mathbf{q}}\rangle_{\partial T},\end{split}

for any
{\mathbf{q}}\in[P_{r}(T)]^{3}
, provided that
{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\in H(curl;T)
.

A discrete version of
\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}
for
{\mathbf{v}}\in V(T)
, denoted by
\nabla_{w,r,T}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}
, is defined as the unique polynomial in
P_{r}(T)
satisfying

(3.5)

(\nabla_{w,r,T}\cdot{\mathbf{v}},q)_{T}=-({\mathbf{v}}_{0},\nabla q)_{T}+\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\cdot{\mathbf{n}},q\rangle_{\partial T},

for any
q\in P_{r}(T)
. The usual integration by parts yields:

(3.6)

\begin{split}(\nabla_{w,r,T}\cdot{\mathbf{v}},q)_{T}=(\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{v}}_{0},q)_{T}-\langle({\mathbf{v}}_{0}-{\mathbf{v}}_{b})\cdot{\mathbf{n}},q\rangle_{\partial T},\end{split}

for any
q\in P_{r}(T)
, provided that
{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\in H(div;T)
.

4
Weak Galerkin Algorithm

Let
{\cal T}_{h}
be a finite element partition of the domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
consisting of shape-regular polyhedrons, as defined in
[
46
]
. Denote by
{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
the set of all faces in
{\cal T}_{h}
, and let
{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{0}={\mathcal{E}}_{h}\setminus\partial\Omega

denote the mesh size of each element

h=\max_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}h_{T}
be the mesh size for the partition
{\cal T}_{h}
.

Let

. Denote by

W_{k}(T)
the local discrete space of the scalar-valued weak functions, given by:

(4.1)

\displaystyle W_{k}(T)=\{\{\sigma_{0},\sigma_{b}\}:\sigma_{0}\in P_{k-1}(T),\sigma_{b}\in P_{k}(e),e\subset\partial T\}.

Furthermore, let
{\mathbf{t}}_{1}
and

be two tangential unit vectors on face

.
Denote by

V_{k}(T)
the local discrete space of the vector-valued weak functions, given by:

(4.2)

\displaystyle V_{k}(T)=\{\{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}=v_{1}{\mathbf{t}}_{1}+v_{2}{\mathbf{t}}_{2}\}:{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\in[P_{k}(T)]^{3},v_{1},v_{2}\in P_{k}(e),e\subset\partial T\}.

By patching
W_{k}(T)
over all the elements
T\in{\cal T}_{h}

through a common value
\sigma_{b}
on the interior interface
{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{0}
, we obtain the scalar-valued weak finite element space
W_{h}
:

W_{h}=\big\{\{\sigma_{0},\sigma_{b}\}:\{\sigma_{0},\sigma_{b}\}|_{T}\in W_{k}(T),\forall T\in{\cal T}_{h}\big\}.

The subspace of
W_{h}
with vanishing boundary values on
\partial\Omega
is denoted by
W_{h}^{0}
:

W_{h}^{0}=\{\{\sigma_{0},\sigma_{b}\}\in W_{h}:\sigma_{b}=0\ \text{on}\ \partial\Omega\}.

Similarly, by patching
V_{k}(T)
over all elements
T\in{\cal T}_{h}

through a common value
{\mathbf{v}}_{b}
on the interior interface
{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{0}
, we obtain the vector-valued weak finite element space
V_{h}
:

V_{h}=\big\{\{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\}:\{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\}|_{T}\in V_{k}(T),\forall T\in{\cal T}_{h}\big\}.

The subspace of
V_{h}
with vanishing boundary values on
\partial\Omega
is denoted by

V_{h}^{0}
:

V_{h}^{0}=\big\{\{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\}\in V_{h}:{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\times{\mathbf{n}}=0\ \text{on}\ \partial\Omega\big\}.

Let
r
be an integer. For simplicity of notation and to avoid confusion, for any
\sigma\in W_{h}
and
{\mathbf{v}}\in V_{h}
, denote by
\nabla_{w}\sigma
and
\nabla_{w}\times{\mathbf{v}}
the discrete weak actions
\nabla_{w,k,T}\sigma
and
\nabla_{w,r,T}\times{\mathbf{v}}
computed using (
3.1
) and (
3.3
) on each element
T
, respectively:

(\nabla_{w}\sigma)|_{T}=\nabla_{w,k,T}(\sigma|_{T}),\qquad\sigma\in W_{h},

(\nabla_{w}\times{\mathbf{v}})|_{T}=\nabla_{w,r,T}\times({\mathbf{v}}|_{T}),\qquad{\mathbf{v}}\in V_{h}.

For any
p,q\in W_{h}
and
{\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}}\in V_{h}
, we introduce the
following bilinear forms:

\displaystyle a({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}})=

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}a_{T}({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}}),

\displaystyle b({\mathbf{u}},q)=

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}b_{T}({\mathbf{u}},q),

\displaystyle s(p,q)=

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}s_{T}(p,q),

where

\begin{split}a_{T}({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}})=&(\nu\nabla_{w}\times{\mathbf{u}},\nabla_{w}\times{\mathbf{v}})_{T},\\
b_{T}({\mathbf{u}},q)=&({\mathbf{u}}_{0},\nabla_{w}q)_{T},\\
s_{T}(p,q)=&h_{T}\langle p_{0}-p_{b},q_{0}-q_{b}\rangle_{\partial T}.\end{split}

The following is the simplified weak Galerkin scheme, which eliminates part of the stabilizers, for the Maxwell equations (
1.1
) based on the variational formulation (

):

Weak Galerkin Algorithm 4.1
.

Find
({\mathbf{u}}_{h};p_{h})\in V_{h}\times W_{h}^{0}
, satisfying
{\mathbf{u}}_{b}\times{\mathbf{n}}=Q_{b}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}
on
\partial\Omega
and

(4.3)

\displaystyle a({\mathbf{u}}_{h},{\mathbf{v}})-b({\mathbf{v}},p_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle({\mathbf{f}},{\mathbf{v}}_{0}),\qquad\forall{\mathbf{v}}\in V_{h}^{0},

(4.4)

\displaystyle s(p_{h},q)+b({\mathbf{u}}_{h},q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(g,q_{0}),\qquad\forall q\in W_{h}^{0}.

5
Solution Existence and Uniqueness

Recall that
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
is a shape-regular finite element partition of
the domain
\Omega
. For any
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and
\varphi\in H^{1}(T)
, the following trace inequality holds true
[
46
]
:

(5.1)

\|\varphi\|_{{\partial T}}^{2}\leq C(h_{T}^{-1}\|\varphi\|_{T}^{2}+h_{T}\|\varphi\|_{1,T}^{2}).

Furthermore, if
\varphi
is a polynomial on
T
, the standard inverse inequality yields

(5.2)

\|\varphi\|_{{\partial T}}^{2}\leq Ch_{T}^{-1}\|\varphi\|_{T}^{2}.

For any
p=\{p_{0},p_{b}\}\in W_{h}
, we define
the following energy norm

(5.3)

{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}p{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{W_{h}}=\Big(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}h_{T}\|p_{0}-p_{b}\|_{\partial T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For any
{\mathbf{v}}=\{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\}\in V_{h}
, we define the energy norm

(5.4)

{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{v}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}_{V_{h}}=\Big(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\|\nabla_{w}\times{\mathbf{v}}\|_{T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and the following discrete

norm

(5.5)

\begin{split}\|{\mathbf{v}}\|_{1,h}=&\Big(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\|\nabla\times{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\|_{T}^{2}+h_{T}^{-1}\|{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\times{\mathbf{n}}-{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\times{\mathbf{n}}\|^{2}_{\partial T}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

Lemma 1
.

For
{\mathbf{v}}=\{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\}\in V_{h}
, there exists a constant
C
such that

(5.6)

\|\nabla\times{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\|_{T}\leq C\|\nabla_{w}\times{\mathbf{v}}\|_{T}.

Proof.

Let
T\in{\cal T}_{h}
be a polyhedral element with
N
faces denoted by
e_{1},\cdots,e_{N}
. It is important to emphasis that the polyhedral element
T
can be non-convex. On each face

, we construct a linear equation

such that

as follows:

l_{i}(x)=\frac{1}{h_{T}}\overrightarrow{AX}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}_{i},

where
A=(A_{1},A_{2})
is a given point on the face
e_{i}
,
X=(x_{1},x_{2})
is any point on the face
e_{i}
,
{\mathbf{n}}_{i}
is the normal direction to the face
e_{i}
, and
h_{T}
is the size of the element
T
.

The bubble function of the element
T
can be defined as

\Phi_{B}=l^{2}_{1}(x)l^{2}_{2}(x)\cdots l^{2}_{N}(x)\in P_{2N}(T).

It is straightforward to verify that
\Phi_{B}=0
on the boundary
\partial T
. The function

\Phi_{B}
can be scaled such that
\Phi_{B}(M)=1
where

represents the barycenter of the element

. Additionally, there exists a sub-domain
\hat{T}\subset T
such that
\Phi_{B}\geq\rho_{0}
for some constant
\rho_{0}>0
.

For
{\mathbf{v}}=\{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\}\in V_{h}
, letting
{\mathbf{q}}=\Phi_{B}\nabla\times{\mathbf{v}}_{0}
in (
3.4
) where we can specify
r=2N+k-1
, yields:

(5.7)

\begin{split}&(\nabla_{w}\times{\mathbf{v}},\Phi_{B}\nabla\times{\mathbf{v}}_{0})_{T}\\
=&(\nabla\times{\mathbf{v}}_{0},\Phi_{B}\nabla\times{\mathbf{v}}_{0})_{T}-\langle({\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0})\times{\mathbf{n}},\Phi_{B}\nabla\times{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
=&(\nabla\times{\mathbf{v}}_{0},\Phi_{B}\nabla\times{\mathbf{v}}_{0})_{T},\end{split}

where we used
\Phi_{B}=0
on
\partial T
.

From the domain inverse inequality
[
46
]
, there exists a constant
C
such that

(5.8)

)-(
5.8
), we have:

\begin{split}(\nabla\times{\mathbf{v}}_{0},\nabla\times{\mathbf{v}}_{0})_{T}&\leq C(\nabla_{w}\times{\mathbf{v}},\Phi_{B}\nabla\times{\mathbf{v}}_{0})_{T}\\

&\leq C\|\nabla_{w}\times{\mathbf{v}}\|_{T}\|\nabla\times{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\|_{T},\end{split}

This completes the proof of the lemma.

Lemma 2

For

We first extend

Substituting (

), (

), (

), (

)
into (

) gives (

).

). Let

be the solution of

.

\begin{split}&\quad\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}(\epsilon_{h,0},\epsilon_{h,0})_{T}\\

which, using (

), completes the proof of (

8
L^{2}
Error Estimates

In this section, we derive an
L^{2}
Error Estimate for the WG approximation. To achieve this, we consider an auxiliary problem of finding
({\boldsymbol{\phi}};\xi)
such that

(8.1)

\begin{split}\nabla\times(\nu\nabla\times{\boldsymbol{\phi}})-\nabla\xi=&{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{0},\qquad\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{\phi}}=&0,\qquad\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\times{\mathbf{n}}=&0,\qquad\text{on}\ \partial\Omega,\\
\xi=&0,\qquad\text{on}\ \partial\Omega.\end{split}

where we recall that the error function
{\mathbf{e}}_{h}={\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}_{h}=\{{\mathbf{e}}_{0},{\mathbf{e}}_{b}\}
. We further introduce
{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}={\mathbf{Q}}_{h}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}_{h}=\{{\mathbf{Q}}_{0}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}_{0},{\mathbf{Q}}_{b}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}_{b}\}=\{{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{b}\}
.

Assume that the regularity property for the auxiliary problem (
8.1
) holds, in the sense that
{\boldsymbol{\phi}}
and
\xi
satisfy the following regularity conditions:

(8.2)

\|{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\|_{1+\alpha}+\|\xi\|_{\alpha}\leq C\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{0}\|,

where
0<\alpha\leq 1
.

Theorem 12
.

Assume that the exact solutions of the Maxwell equations (
1.4
) are sufficiently regular such that
({\mathbf{u}},p)\in[H^{k+1}(\Omega)]^{3}\times H^{k}(\Omega)
. Let
({\mathbf{u}}_{h},p_{h})\in V_{h}\times W_{h}^{0}
be the solutions of weak Galerkin scheme (
4.3
)-(
4.4
). Additionally, assume that the regularity assumption (
8.2
) for the dual problem (
8.1
) holds true. Then, the following estimate holds:

\|{\mathbf{e}}_{0}\|\leq Ch^{k+\alpha}(\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{1+k}+\|p\|_{k}).

Proof.

Testing the first equation of (
8.1
) with
{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{0}
yields:

(8.3)

\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{0}\|^{2}=\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}(\nabla\times(\nu\nabla\times{\boldsymbol{\phi}})-\nabla\xi,{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{0})_{T}.

Letting
{\mathbf{u}}={\boldsymbol{\phi}}
,

and

in (

) and (

), and letting

in (

) and
q=Q_{h}\xi
in (
6.7
), and using (
6.2
), the above equation can be formulated as follows

(8.4)

\begin{split}&\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{0}\|^{2}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}(\nu\nabla_{w}\times{\boldsymbol{\phi}},\nabla_{w}\times{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h})_{T}-\ell_{1}({\boldsymbol{\phi}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h})-\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}({\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{0},{\mathbf{Q}}_{0}(\nabla\xi))_{T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}(\nu\nabla_{w}\times{\boldsymbol{\phi}},\nabla_{w}\times({\mathbf{Q}}_{h}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}))_{T}+(\nu\nabla_{w}\times{\boldsymbol{\phi}},\nabla_{w}\times{\mathbf{e}}_{h})_{T}-\ell_{1}({\boldsymbol{\phi}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h})\\
&-\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}({\mathbf{Q}}_{0}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}},\nabla_{w}Q_{h}\xi)_{T}-\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}({\mathbf{e}}_{0},\nabla_{w}Q_{h}\xi)_{T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}(\nu\nabla_{w}\times{\boldsymbol{\phi}},\nabla_{w}\times({\mathbf{Q}}_{h}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}))_{T}\\
&+(\nu\nabla_{w}\times{\mathbf{Q}}_{h}{\boldsymbol{\phi}},\nabla_{w}\times{\mathbf{e}}_{h})_{T}+(\nu\nabla_{w}\times({\boldsymbol{\phi}}-{\mathbf{Q}}_{h}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}),\nabla_{w}\times{\mathbf{e}}_{h})_{T}\\
&-\ell_{1}({\boldsymbol{\phi}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h})-\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}({\mathbf{Q}}_{0}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}},\nabla_{w}Q_{h}\xi)_{T}-\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}({\mathbf{e}}_{0},\nabla_{w}Q_{h}\xi)_{T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}(\nu\nabla_{w}\times{\boldsymbol{\phi}},\nabla_{w}\times({\mathbf{Q}}_{h}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}))_{T}+\ell_{1}({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{Q}}_{h}{\boldsymbol{\phi}})+b({\mathbf{Q}}_{h}{\boldsymbol{\phi}},\epsilon_{h})\\

&-\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}({\mathbf{Q}}_{0}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}},\nabla_{w}Q_{h}\xi)_{T}+s(\epsilon_{h},Q_{h}\xi)-s(Q_{h}p,Q_{h}\xi)-\ell_{2}({\mathbf{u}},Q_{h}\xi)\\
=&\sum_{i=1}^{9}I_{i}.\end{split}

We will estimate

for

individually. For the term

, using (
3.3
) gives

(8.5)

\begin{split}&\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}({\mathbf{Q}}_{0}(\nu\nabla_{w}\times{\boldsymbol{\phi}}),\nabla_{w}\times({\mathbf{Q}}_{h}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}))_{T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}({\mathbf{Q}}_{0}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}},\nabla\times({\mathbf{Q}}_{0}(\nu\nabla_{w}\times{\boldsymbol{\phi}})))_{T}-\langle({\mathbf{Q}}_{b}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}})\times{\mathbf{n}},{\mathbf{Q}}_{0}(\nu\nabla_{w}\times{\boldsymbol{\phi}})\rangle_{\partial T}\\

where we used the properties of the projection operators

Using (

), and (

\begin{split}|I_{1}|=&|\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}(\nu\nabla_{w}\times{\boldsymbol{\phi}}),\nabla_{w}\times({\mathbf{Q}}_{h}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}))_{T}|\\

Regarding to

) with

, (

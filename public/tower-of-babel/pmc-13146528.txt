ABSTRACT NUMBER: ESOC2026A2303 SENESCENCE GENE EXPRESSION IS ASSOCIATED WITH OUTCOME IN PATIENTS WITH ISCHEMIC STROKE
Authors: Maria Guadalupe C Real, Yiran Zhang, Sarina Falcione, Twinkle Joy, Mike Clarke, Sima Abbasi Habashi, Parnian Alavi, Rayna McFeetors, Roobina Boghozian, Glen Jickling
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and aimsCellular senescence increases with age. Senescent cells can contribute to age-related diseases. Senescence accumulation might affect vascular health. We sought to investigate the relationship between senescence gene expression and stroke outcome.MethodsIn 129 patients with acute ischemic stroke and 41 control participants, whole-blood gene expression was measured by microarray. Expression of senescence-associated genes was evaluated using single-sample gene set enrichment analysis using the SenMayo gene set. Stroke outcome was determined by stroke neurologists using the modified Rankin Scale (mRS) at 90-days after stroke onset.ResultsStroke patients were divided into good (90-day mRS<2) and poor (90-day mRS>2) outcome. The stroke outcome groups showed significantly different senescence gene expression enrichment between them (p=0.030) and compared with control participants (Fig. 1A, p=0.020 for good, and p<0.001 for poor outcome). All participants were divided into age tertiles. In the 73-84 years old group, senescence gene expression was found different in patients with good (p=0.010) and poor (p=0.002) stroke outcome compared with controls. In the group >84 years, senescence enrichment in the poor outcome group was different from the good outcome group (Fig. 1B, p=0.041). Patients were classified into low or high senescence using the cohort’s median, outcome was assessed using the full mRS. High senescence was associated with a shift towards worse mRS (p=0.047) even after adjustment for sex, age, stroke severity, and diabetes.ConclusionsSenescence is associated with worse stroke outcomes, potentially affecting vascular health through inflammation. Targeting senescence may offer a novel strategy to improve stroke outcome.Conflict of interestAll authors: nothing to disclose.

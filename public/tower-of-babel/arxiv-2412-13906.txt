Whitney Numbers of Rank-Metric Lattices and Code Enumeration
Giuseppe Cotardo, Alberto Ravagnani, Ferdinando Zullo

Abstract
We investigate the Whitney numbers of the first kind of rank-metric lattices, which are closely linked to the open problem of enumerating rank-metric codes having prescribed parameters. We apply methods from the theory of hyperovals and linear sets to compute these Whitney numbers for infinite families of rank-metric lattices. As an application of our results, we prove asymptotic estimates on the density function of certain rank-metric codes that have been conjectured in previous work.



Introduction

This paper applies
methods from finite geometry to an open order theory problem, solving some new instances. The problem is the computation of the Whitney numbers (of the first kind) of rank-metric lattices.

Rank-metric lattices were introduced in
[
11
]
in connection with the problem of enumerating rank-metric codes with correction capability bounded from below
[
10
,
15
,
18
,
36
]
. They
can be seen as the
q
-analogues of higher-weight Dowling lattices, introduced by Dowling in
[
16
,
17
]
in connection with the
packing problem
for the Hamming metric, which is the central question of classical coding theory
[
16
]
.

Rank-metric lattices are indexed by
four parameters. More precisely,

\mathscr{L}_{i}(n,m;q)
is the subgeometry
of the subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{n}
generated by vectors of rank weight up to
i
; see Section
1
for a precise definition.
In
[
11
]
, the supersolvable rank-metric lattices were completely characterized, and their
Whitney numbers were computed for some sporadic parameter sets.
The Whitney numbers of
\mathscr{L}_{2}(4,4;2)
were determined under the assumption that they are polynomial expressions in the field size
q
. The techniques applied in
[
11
]
do not seem to extend naturally to other rank-metric lattices.

In this paper, we propose a new approach for computing the Whitney numbers of rank-metric lattices, which leverages on techniques from finite geometry. We use the theory of hyperovals and linear sets, as well as the description of rank-metric codes as spaces of linearized polynomials. This allows us to compute the Whitney numbers of rank-metric lattices in several new instances, and to confirm the value conjectured in
[
11
, Section 6]
for the lattice
\mathscr{L}_{2}(4,4;2)
under the polynomial assumption. Using the same methods, we also establish new results on the density function of rank-metric codes, which is another wide open problem in coding theory
[
2
,
7
,
20
,
21
,
19
,
29
]
.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
1
we recall the definition of
rank-metric lattices and outline the motivation for this work. In Section
2
, we introduce the tools we will use throughout the paper: linear sets, hyperovals, rank-metric codes, and linearized polynomials. In Section
3
, we establish the classification of two-dimensional MRD codes in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}}^{4}
and
\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}^{m}
, which we explicitly compute in Section
4
. In Section
5
, we count one-weight rank-metric codes using their multivariate polynomial description. Finally, in Section
6
, we provide a recursive formula for computing the Whitney numbers of the first kind and give a closed formula for the ones of
\mathscr{L}_{2}(n,3;q)
with
n\in\{4,5,6\}
.

1
Motivation and Preliminaries

We start by recalling the definition of a lattice. We refer the reader to
[
40
]
for the necessary background on order theory. We follow the notation of
[
40
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

A
finite lattice

(\mathscr{L},\leq)
is a finite poset where every
s,t\in\mathscr{L}
have a join and a meet.
In this case, join and meet can be seen as commutative and associative operations

\vee,\wedge:\mathscr{L}\times\mathscr{L}\to\mathscr{L}
.
In particular, the
join
(resp., the
meet
) of a non-empty subset
S\subseteq\mathscr{L}
is well-defined as the join (resp., the meet) of its elements and denoted by
\vee S
(resp., by
\wedge S
). Furthermore,
\mathscr{L}
has a minimum and maximum element (
0
and
1
, resp.). Finally, if
\mathscr{L}
is classified, then
rank
of
\mathscr{L}
is
\textnormal{rk}(\mathscr{L})=\rho(1)
, where
\rho
is the rank function of
\mathscr{L}
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\mathscr{L}
be a finite, graded lattice with rank function
\rho
and Möbius function
\mu
. The
characteristic polynomial
of
\mathscr{L}
is the element of
\mathbb{Z}[\lambda]
defined as

\chi(\mathscr{L};\lambda):=\sum_{s\in\mathscr{L}}\mu(s)\lambda^{\textnormal{rk}(\mathscr{L})-\rho(s)}=\sum_{i=0}^{\textnormal{rk}(\mathscr{L})}w_{i}(\mathscr{L})\lambda^{\textnormal{rk}(\mathscr{L})-i},

where

w_{i}(\mathscr{L})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in\mathscr{L}\\
\rho(s)=i\end{subarray}}\mu(s)

is the
i
-
th Whitney number of the first kind
of
\mathscr{L}
. The
i
-
th Whitney number of the first kind
of
\mathscr{L}
, denoted by
W_{i}(\mathscr{L})
, is the number of elements of
\mathscr{L}
of rank
i
.

The family of
rank-metric lattices
was introduced in
[
11
]
, in connection with the problem of enumerating rank-metric codes with prescribed parameters, a current open problem in coding theory
[
2
,
7
,
20
,
21
]
.
We recall their definition after establishing the notation for the rest of the paper.

In the sequel,
q
is a prime power and
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is the finite with
q
elements.
We let
m,n
be positive integers with
m\geq n
.

Definition 1.3
.

The
rank
(
weight
) of a vector
v\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
is the dimension over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-span of its entries.
We denote it by
\textnormal{rk}(v)
. The (
rank
)
distance
between vectors
v,w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{n}
is
d(v,w)=\textnormal{rk}(v-w)
.
A
rank-metric code
is a
k
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear subspace
C\leq\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{n}
with
k\geq 1
. The
minimum distance
of
C
is
d(C):=~\min\{\textnormal{rk}(v):v\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{n}\textup{ with }v\neq 0\}
. We refer to
C
as a
[n,k,d(C)]_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}
-code.

Rank-metric lattices are defined as follows.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
i\in\{1,\ldots n\}
. The
rank-metric lattice
(
RML
in short)
\mathscr{L}_{i}(n,m;q)
associated with the
4
-tuple
(i,n,m,q)
is the geometric lattice whose atoms are the
1
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{n}
generated by the vectors of rank at most
i
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{i}(n,m;q):=\{\left<v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell}\right>_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\colon\ell\geq 1,\,v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell}\in T_{i}(n,m;q)\},

where
T_{i}(n,m;q):=\{v\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{n}:\textnormal{rk}(v)\leq i\}
. The order is given by the inclusion of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{n}
.

This paper focuses on the computation of the Whitney numbers of the
first kind of rank-metric lattices. We follow the notation in
[
11
]
and denote by
w_{j}(i,n,m;q)
be the
j
-th Whitney number of the first kind of the lattice
\mathscr{L}_{i}(n,m;q)
. We apply various techniques, ranging from coding theory, order theory, and finite geometry (hyperovals and linear sets).

2
Tools

In this section we recall some notions and results that will be needed throughout the paper. The section is divided into three subsections.

2.1
Hyperovals and Linear Sets

Let PG
(r,q)
denotes the Desarguesian projective space of dimension
r
of order
q
and let
\textnormal{PG}(V,\mathbb{F}_{q})
denotes the projective space obtained by
V
, for some
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear vector space
V
.
Classical references are
[
23
,
25
,
32
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A set
\mathcal{O}
of
q+1
points in
\mathrm{PG}(2,q)
is called an
oval
if no three of its points are collinear.
Moreover, we say that the ovals
\mathcal{O}_{1}
and
\mathcal{O}_{2}
are
equivalent
if there exists a collineation
\phi
of the plane such that
\phi(\mathcal{O}_{1})=\phi(\mathcal{O}_{2})
.

Observe that conics are a family of ovals. Furthermore, by the Segre’s Theorem we know that when
q
is odd, all the ovals are equivalent to a conic; see
[
38
]
.

When
q
is even, every oval can be uniquely extended to a set of
q+2
points satisfying again the condition that no three of its points are still collinear. These sets are known as
hyperovals
.
We refer to
[
8
,
9
,
14
]
for a list of known examples and classification results.
We will be mainly interested in the case of translation hyperovals.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hyperoval in
\textnormal{PG}(2,q)
, with
q
even. We say that
\mathcal{H}
is a
translation hyperoval
if there exists a secant line
\ell
to
\mathcal{H}
such that the group of elations having axis
\ell
acts transitively on the points of
\mathcal{H}\setminus\ell
.

The set of points of any hyperoval
\mathcal{H}
in
\mathrm{PG}(2,q)
is

\{(x,f(x),1)\colon x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\}\cup\{(1,0,0),(0,1,0)\},

where
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
, up to equivalence.
We will denote such a set of points by
\mathcal{H}_{f}
.
It is not difficult to see that
\mathcal{H}_{f}
is a translation hyperoval if and only if
f
is an additive permutation polynomial and
f(x)/x
is a permutation polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0\}
as well.
Translation hyperovals have been completely classified.

Theorem 2.3
.

(
[
31
, Main Theorem]
and
[
22
]
)
Let
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
with
q=2^{h}
. Then
\mathcal{H}_{f}
is a translation hyperoval if and only if
f(x)=ax^{2^{j}}
for some
j\in\{1,\ldots,h-1\}
with
\gcd(h,j)=1
.

In this paper, we mainly focus on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear sets in the projective line.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
V
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear vector space of dimension 2, let
U
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear subspace of
V
, and let
\Lambda=\textnormal{PG}(V,\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}})=\textnormal{PG}(1,q^{m})
. The set

L_{U}=\{\langle\mathbf{u}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}\,\colon\,\mathbf{u}\in U\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}\}

is called an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
linear set
of
rank

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U)
.

The following is an important concept related to linear sets.

Definition 2.5
.

The
weight
of a point
P=\langle\mathbf{v}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}\in\Lambda
in
L_{U}
is
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U\cap\langle\mathbf{v}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}})
.

The maximum value for
|L_{U}|
can be reached in the case where all the points of
L_{U}
have weight one, therefore if
L_{U}
is any
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear set of rank
k
we have

(2.1)

|L_{U}|\leq\frac{q^{k}-1}{q-1}.

Linear sets attaining the bound in (
2.1
) are called
scattered
and the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear subspace
U
is called a
scattered subspace
.
They were originally introduced in
[
5
]
.
It is difficult, in general, to determine whether or not two linear sets are equivalent. Recall that
L_{U_{1}}
and
L_{U_{2}}
are
equivalent
if there exists
\varphi\in\mathrm{P\Gamma L}_{1}(q^{m})
with
\varphi(L_{U_{1}})=~L_{U_{2}}
.
In
[
12
]
, the authors introduced the following concept for detecting families of linear sets for which this problem is easier to address.

Definition 2.6
.

An
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear set
L_{U}
in
\textnormal{PG}(1,q^{m})=\textnormal{PG}(V,\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}})
is
simple
if for each
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear subspace
W
of
V
,
L_{W}
is
\mathrm{P\Gamma L}_{2}(q^{m})
-equivalent to
L_{U}
if and only if
U
and
W
are in the same orbit of
\mathrm{\Gamma L}_{2}(q^{m})
.
We say that
L_{U}
has
\mathrm{GL}
-class one
if for each
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear subspace
W
of
V
,
L_{W}
is
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(q^{m})
-equivalent to
L_{U}
if and only if
U
and
W
are in the same orbit of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(q^{m})
.

2.2
Polynomial Description of Rank-Metric Codes

We denote by
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}]
the ring of polynomials in the variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}
with coefficient in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We let
\mathrm{GL}_{k}(q)
and
\mathrm{\Gamma L}_{k}(q)
denote the general linear and the general semilinear groups, respectively.

We recall the connection between rank-metric codes and linearized polynomials.
We start describing some properties of linearized polynomials and refer the reader to
[
41
]
for further details.

Definition 2.7
.

A (
linearized
)
q
-polynomial
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
is a polynomial of the form

f:=\sum_{i\geq 0}f_{i}x^{q^{i}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[x].

The largest
i
with
f_{i}\neq 0
is the
q
-degree
of
f
.
The set of
q
-polynomials modulo
x^{q^{m}}-x
is denoted by
\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[x]
.

Note that
\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[x]
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra equipped with the operations of addition and composition of polynomials, and scalar multiplication by elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The elements of
\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[x]
are in one-to-one correspondence with the
q
-polynomials of
q
-degree upper bounded by
m-1
.
Throughout the paper, we abuse notation and denote an element of
\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[x]
as its unique representative of
q
-degree at most
m-1
.

Remark 2.8
.

We have
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra automorphisms

(2.2)

(\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[x],+,\circ)\cong(\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}),+,\circ)\cong(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times m},+,\cdot),

where in the 3-tuples we omitted the scalar multiplication by an element of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
We refer the reader to
[
41
]
and
[
21
]
for further details.

In the following, we consider
\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[x]
endowed with the metric

d_{L}(f,g)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\mathrm{Im}(f-g)),

for any
f,g\in\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[x]
.
It is not hard to check that
(\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[x],d_{L})
and
(\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{m},d)
are isometric, where
d
is the rank distance; see Section
1
.
As a consequence, an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear rank-metric code in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{m}
corresponds to an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear subspace of
\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[x]
.
Therefore, we will sometimes say
“an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear rank-metric code
\mathcal{C}\leq\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[x]
”.

Interpreting rank-metric codes as
spaces of
q
-polynomials will allow us to use results on linear sets and to count certain families of MRD codes.

Notation 2.9
.

If
f=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}f_{i}x^{q^{i}}\in\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[x]
and
\rho\in\textnormal{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is a field automorphism, then we let
f^{\rho}:=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\rho(f_{i})x^{q^{i}}
.
Furthermore, if
\mathcal{C}\leq\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[x]
is a rank-metric code and
\rho\in\textnormal{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, we let
\mathcal{C}^{\rho}:=\{f^{\rho}\mid f\in\mathcal{C}\}
.

We recall two notions of equivalence between rank-metric codes.

Definition 2.10
.

We say that the
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear rank-metric codes
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}\leq\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[x]
are
equivalent

if there exist invertible
q
-polynomials
f,g\in\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[x]
and a field automorphism
\rho\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that

\mathcal{C}_{1}=f\circ\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\rho}\circ g.

The
automorphism group
of
\mathcal{C}
is

\displaystyle\textnormal{Aut}(\mathcal{C})=\{(f_{1},\rho,f_{2})\in\textnormal{GL}_{m}(q)\times\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\times\textnormal{GL}_{m}(q)\mid\mathcal{C}=f_{1}\circ\mathcal{C}^{\rho}\circ f_{2}\},

where
\textnormal{GL}_{m}(q)
denotes the group of invertible linearized polynomials in
\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[x]
.

Remark 2.11
.

Note that for any
\mathcal{C}\leq\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[x]
,
f,g\in\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[x]
and a field automorphism
\rho\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, then
f\circ\mathcal{C}^{\rho}\circ g
may not be an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-subspace of
\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[x]
.

We aim to count the number of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear rank-metric codes in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{n}
and therefore the natural concept of equivalence in this context makes use of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear isometries of
(\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[x],d_{L})
. The following is the second notion of equivalence.

Definition 2.12
.

We say that
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear rank-metric codes
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}\leq\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[x]
are
linearly equivalent

if there exists an invertible
q
-polynomial
g\in\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[x]
such that

\mathcal{C}_{1}=\mathcal{C}_{2}\circ g.

The
linear automorphism group
of
\mathcal{C}
is

\displaystyle\textnormal{Aut}\mathrm{lin}(\mathcal{C})=\{g\in\mathrm{GL}_{m}(q)\mid\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}\circ g\}.

Remark 2.13
.

The linear automorphism group of
\mathcal{C}
is usually called
right idealizer
or
middle nucleus
; see
[
26
,
27
]
.

2.3
Examples of MRD codes

One of the most studied MRD codes are the
Gabidulin codes
. These were first introduced in
[
15
,
18
]
and later generalized in
[
24
]
. They are defined as follows.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
k,m,s
be three positive integers with
k\leq m
and
\gcd(s,m)=1
. Then a
generalized Gabidulin code
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{k,s,m}=\langle x,x^{q^{s}},\ldots,x^{q^{s(k-1)}}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}.

In
[
30
, Theorem 5.4]
(see also
[
37
]
), it has been proved that the number of linearly inequivalent generalized Gabidulin codes in
\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[x]
is
\varphi(m)/2
, where
\varphi
is the Euler’s totient function.
Their automorphism group has been fully determined; see
[
26
,
39
]
.

Theorem 2.15
.

[
39
, Theorem 4]

Let
q=p^{h}
, with
p
a prime and
h
any positive integer.
The automorphism group of
\mathcal{G}_{k,s,m}
is the set of 3-tuples
\smash{(ax^{q^{i}},\rho,bx^{q^{m-i}})}
with
\smash{a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{\times}}
,
0\leq i\leq m-1
, and
\rho\in\textnormal{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. In particular, we have that
|\textnormal{Aut}(\mathcal{G}_{k,s,m})|=hm(q^{m}-1)^{2}
.

We obtain the following by restricting to the linear automorphism group.

Corollary 2.16
.

The linear automorphism group of
\mathcal{G}_{k,s,m}
is

\{ax\colon a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{*}\}.

Successively, Sheekey in
[
39
]
extended the above family of MRD codes (see also
[
28
]
). We recall their definition in the
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear setting.

Definition 2.17
.

Let
k,m,s
be three positive integers with
k\leq m
and
\gcd(s,m)=1
and let
\delta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
with
\mathrm{N}_{q^{m}/q}(\delta)\neq(-1)^{mk}
. Then a
generalized twisted Gabidulin code
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{k,s,m}(\delta)=\langle x^{q^{s}},\ldots,x^{q^{s(k-1)}},x+\delta x^{q^{s(n-1)}}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}.

Also, its automorphism group has been completely determined.

Theorem 2.18
.

(
[
39
, Theorem 7]
and
[
28
, Theorem 4.4]
)
Let
q=p^{h}
, with
p
a prime and
h
any positive integer.
The automorphism group of
\mathcal{T}_{k,s,m}(\delta)
is the set of 3-tuples
\smash{(ax^{q^{i}},\rho,bx^{q^{m-i}})}
with
\smash{a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{\times}}
,
0\leq i\leq m-1
such that

(b^{q^{k}-1})^{\rho q^{i}}\delta^{\rho q^{i}}=\delta,

for some
\rho\in\textnormal{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

As before, we obtain the following by restricting to linear automorphism group.

Corollary 2.19
.

The linear automorphism group of
\mathcal{T}_{k,s,m}(\delta)
is

\{ax\colon a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}\cap\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\}.

3
Classification of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear rank-metric codes of dimension
2

Sheekey in
[
39
]
established the first connection between rank-metric codes and linear sets and he pointed out a relation between
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear rank-metric codes and linear sets in the projective line
\textnormal{PG}(1,q^{m})
.
By
[
39
, Section 5]
, we have the following result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\langle f_{1}(x),f_{2}(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}
be a rank-metric code. Then
\mathcal{C}
is an MRD code if and only if
U_{f_{1},f_{2}}=\{(f_{1}(x),f_{2}(x))\colon x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\}
is a scattered
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear subspace of dimension
m
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{2}
(or equivalently
L_{U_{f_{1},f_{2}}}
is scattered).
Moreover, two MRD codes
\mathcal{C}=\langle f_{1}(x),f_{2}(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\langle g_{1}(x),g_{2}(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}
are equivalent if and only if
U_{f_{1},f_{2}}
and
U_{g_{1},g_{2}}
are
\mathrm{\Gamma L}_{2}(q^{m})
-equivalent.

The previous result can be rephrased as follows in terms of linear equivalence of codes; see for instance
[
1
,
34
]
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\langle f_{1}(x),f_{2}(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\langle g_{1}(x),g_{2}(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}
be MRD codes of dimension 2. We have that
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
are linearly equivalent if and only if
U_{f_{1},f_{2}}
and
U_{g_{1},g_{2}}
are
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(q^{m})
-equivalent.

In
[
13
]
, Csajbók and Zanella refined the classification of scattered
\mathbb{F}_{q}

4
Counting the number of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear MRD codes of dimension
2

In this section, we apply the results derived in the previous sections
to determine the number of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear MRD codes in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{m}
of dimension
2
in the cases
q=2
and any
m
, and

q>2
and
m=4
.

We use the orbit-stabilizer theorem to count the number of certain families of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear MRD codes. In detail, if we determine the number of the equivalence classes of the codes (say
\mathcal{C}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{C}_{t}
is a set of representatives), then the number of codes we are looking for is

(4.1)

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\frac{|\textnormal{GL}_{m}(q)|}{|\mathrm{Autlin}(\mathcal{C}_{i})|}.

As a consequence, we derive some density results for these MRD codes. We define the density function of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear rank-metric codes with parameters
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
as the ratio of the number of codes with these parameters and the number of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{n}
having the same dimension, that is

\delta^{\textnormal{rk}}_{d}(m,n,k;q):=\frac{|\{\mathcal{C}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{n}\mid\dim(\mathcal{C})=k,\,d^{\textnormal{rk}}(\mathcal{C})\geq d\}|}{\begin{bmatrix}{n}\\
{k}\end{bmatrix}_{q^{m}}}.

It is currently an open problem in coding theory to determine the exact proportion of MRD codes
[
2
,
7
,
20
,
21
,
19
,
29
]
.

4.1
Case
q=2

We start by observing that two generalized Gabidulin codes
\mathcal{G}_{k,s,m}
and
\mathcal{G}_{k,t,m}
are equivalent if and only if
s\equiv\pm t\pmod{m}
, by
[
28
]
and this is still true when considering the linear equivalence. Therefore, we have the following.

Proposition 4.1
.

The number of linearly inequivalent generalized Gabidulin codes of a fixed dimension in
\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[x]
is
\frac{\varphi(m)}{2}
.

Therefore, combining (
4.1
), Proposition
4.1
, Theorem
3.4
, and Corollary
2.16
, we obtain the number of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
-linear MRD codes of dimension two in
\mathcal{L}_{m,2}[x]
.

Corollary 4.2
.

The number of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}
-linear MRD codes of dimension two in
\mathcal{L}_{m,2}
is

\frac{\varphi(m)}{2}\cdot\frac{|\mathrm{GL}_{m}(2)|}{(2^{m}-1)}.

The following result provides the density of MRD codes in
\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}^{m}
of dimension
2
and its asymptotic.

Corollary 4.3
.

We have

\delta^{\textnormal{rk}}_{m-1}(m,m,2;2)=\frac{2^{m^{2}}\cdot\varphi(m)\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{j}}\right)(2^{2m}-1)}{(2^{m^{2}}-1)(2^{m^{2}-m}-1)},

and

\delta^{\textnormal{rk}}_{m-1}(m,m,2;2)\in\mathcal{O}\left(m2^{-m^{2}+3m}\right)\textup{ as }m\to+\infty.

4.2
Case

and

In the remainder of this section, we assume

. We derive bounds on the number of the equivalence classes of

-linear MRD codes of dimension two in

Proposition 4.4
.

The number of linearly inequivalent
\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}}
-linear MRD codes of dimension two in

q(q-1)/2
.

Proof.

Let start by noting that the number of linearly inequivalent

-linear MRD codes of dimension two in

is exactly the number of
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(q^{4})
-inequivalent scattered
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear sets of rank
4
in
\textnormal{PG}(1,q^{4})
. This is indeed true because of Corollary
3.2
and as the scattered
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear sets of rank-

-class 1.
Finally,

-linear MRD codes of dimension two in

We can now give the number of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}}
-linear MRD codes of dimension two in
\mathcal{L}_{4,q}[x]
.

Corollary 4.5
.

The number
M(q)
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}}
-linear MRD codes of dimension 2 in
\mathcal{L}_{4,q}[x]
is

\frac{1}{2}q^{7}(q^{3}-1)(q^{2}-1)(q-1)(q^{3}-q^{2}-q-1).

Proof.

By Corollary
2.19
, the linear automorphism group
\mathcal{T}_{2,1,4}(\delta)

\{ax\colon a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}\}

and hence has size

If

Therefore, we have by (

M(q)=\frac{|\mathrm{GL}_{4}(q)|}{q^{4}-1}+\left(\frac{q(q-1)}{2}-1\right)\frac{|\mathrm{GL}_{4}(q)|}{q^{2}-1},

∎

Remark 4.6

Applying this result to

Corollary 4.7

\delta^{\textnormal{rk}}_{3}(4,4,2;q)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{q^{7}(q^{3}-1)(q^{2}-1)(q-1)(q^{3}-q^{2}-q-1)(q^{8}-1)(q^{4}-1)}{(q^{16}-1)(q^{12}-1)}.

5
One-weight codes in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{mk}

In this section we determine the number of one-weight codes within
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{mk}
. We first establish a connection between non-square rank-metric codes and multivariate linearized polynomials. We later use such a relation to compute the desired quantity.

5.1
Non-square rank-metric codes

As proved in
[
4
,
33
]
, any
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear rank-metric code in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{m\ell}
can be described as an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear subspace of multivariate linearized polynomials. We let
\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}]
(or
\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[\underline{x}]
) denote the
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear vector space of linearized polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
in the indeterminates
\underline{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell})
, that is the
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-span in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[\underline{x}]
of

\{x_{i}^{q^{j}}\colon i\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\},j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}.

Moreover, we let
{\mathcal{L}}_{m,q}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}]
(or
{\mathcal{L}}_{m,q}[\underline{x}]
) be the
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear vector space of linearized polynomials
\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[\underline{x}]
modulo the relations
x_{1}^{q^{m}}-x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}^{q^{m}}-x_{\ell}
. It is known that as an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear vector space,
\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[\underline{x}]
is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear isometric to the space of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear maps from
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{\ell}
to
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
and to
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times m\ell}
. Therefore, we define the rank of a
q
-polynomial
f\in{\mathcal{L}}_{m,q}[\underline{x}]
as
\mathrm{rk}(f)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\mathrm{Im}(f))
. In particular,
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear rank-metric codes in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{m\ell}
correspond to
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear subspaces of
{\mathcal{L}}_{m,q}[\underline{x}]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

[
33
, Proposition 4.5]

Let
\mathcal{B}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m\ell})
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{\ell}
(seen as an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear vector space).
The map

\begin{array}[]{cccc}ev_{\mathcal{B}}\colon&{\mathcal{L}}_{m,q}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}]&\rightarrow&\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{m\ell}\\
&f&\mapsto&(f(a_{1}),\ldots,f(a_{\ell m}))\end{array}

is an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear isomorphism which preserves the rank, that is

\mathrm{rk}(f)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\langle f(a_{1}),\ldots,f(a_{\ell m})\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q}})\quad\textup{ for any }\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}].

Moreover, if
\mathcal{C}=\langle f_{1}(\underline{x}),\ldots,f_{k}(\underline{x})\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}
, then a generator matrix for
ev_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{C})
is

G=\begin{pmatrix}f_{1}(a_{1})&f_{1}(a_{2})&\ldots&f_{1}(a_{\ell m})\\
f_{2}(a_{1})&f_{2}(a_{2})&\ldots&f_{2}(a_{\ell m})\\
\vdots&&&\\
f_{k}(a_{1})&f_{k}(a_{2})&\ldots&f_{k}(a_{\ell m})\end{pmatrix}.

Therefore, the map
ev_{\mathcal{B}}
allows us to study rank-metric codes as
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear subspaces of
{\mathcal{L}}_{m,q}[\underline{x}]
. The notion of equivalence of rank-metric codes can be also read in the context of linearized polynomials. Indeed, the linear equivalence of rank-metric codes
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times\ell m}
is given by the natural action of the following group on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times\ell m}

\{(A,B)\colon A\in\mathrm{GL}_{m}(q),B\in\mathrm{GL}_{\ell m}(q)\}.

In terms of linearized polynomials, the elements of
\mathrm{GL}_{\ell m}(q)
can be represented as invertible linear functions in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{\ell}
in itself and hence as elements of
({\mathcal{L}}_{m,q}[\underline{x}])^{\ell}
. It follows that equivalent rank-metric codes can be defined via the natural action of the following group on
{\mathcal{L}}_{m,q}[\underline{x}]

\{(g,f)\colon g\in{\mathcal{L}}_{m,q}[x],f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell})\in({\mathcal{L}}_{m,q}[\underline{x}])^{\ell}\textup{ and }f,g\text{ invertible}\}.

Therefore,
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}\leq\mathcal{L}_{\ell m,q}[\underline{x}]
are
equivalent
(or
linearly equivalent
)
if there exist invertible
q
-polynomials
f\in\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[x]
,
g=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{\ell})^{\ell}\in\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[\underline{x}]
and a field automorphism
\rho\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that

\mathcal{C}_{1}=f\circ\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\rho}\circ g,

(
\mathcal{C}_{1}=f\circ\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\rho}\circ g
, respectively).
The
automorphism group
of
\mathcal{C}
is

\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{C})=\{(g,\rho,f)\in\mathrm{GL}_{m}(q)\times\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\times\mathrm{GL}_{\ell m}(q)\colon g\circ\mathcal{C}^{\rho}\circ f\},

and the
linear automorphism group
of
\mathcal{C}
is

\mathrm{Autlin}(\mathcal{C})=\{f\in\mathrm{GL}_{\ell m}(q)\colon\mathcal{C}\circ f=\mathcal{C}\}.

5.2
Counting one-weight codes

The goal of this section is to establish the number of
[mk,k,m]_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}
-codes in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{mk}
. We recall the following result.

Proposition 5.2
.

(
[
1
, Proposition 3.6]
)
Let
k\geq 2
and let
\mathcal{C}
be a
[km,k]_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}
-code. We have that
\mathcal{C}
is a one-weight code (with weight
m
) if and only if
\mathcal{C}
is linearly equivalent to a code with generator matrix

(I_{k}\mid\alpha I_{k}\mid\cdots\mid\alpha^{m-1}I_{k}),

for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
such that
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}=\mathbb{F}_{q}(\alpha)
.

We start by determining the equivalence classes of one-weight codes.

Proposition 5.3
.

We have only one equivalence class of
[mk,k,m]_{q^{m}/q}
-codes in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{mk}
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
such that
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}=\mathbb{F}_{q}(\alpha)
and consider the codes
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
having as a generator matrix

G=(I_{k}\mid\alpha I_{k}\mid\cdots\mid\alpha^{m-1}I_{k})\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,G=(I_{k}\mid\beta I_{k}\mid\cdots\mid\beta^{m-1}I_{k}),

respectively.
For any
i\in\{1,\ldots,m-1\}
, write

\beta^{i}=a_{0,i}+a_{1,i}\alpha+\ldots+a_{m-1,i}\alpha^{m-1},

with
a_{0},\ldots,a_{m-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Consider the block-matrix

A=\begin{pmatrix}a_{0,0}I_{k}&a_{0,1}I_{k}&\cdots&a_{0,m-1}I_{k}\\
a_{1,0}I_{k}&a_{1,1}I_{k}&\cdots&a_{1,m-1}I_{k}\\
\vdots&&&\\
a_{m-1,0}I_{k}&a_{m-1,1}I_{k}&\cdots&a_{m-1,m-1}I_{k}\\
\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{mk\times mk},

and note that its determinant is equal to the determinant of the matrix
(a_{ij})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times m}
(since it is the Kronecker product of this matrix with the identity matrix), which is invertible since it is the matrix change of basis between the ordered bases
(1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{m-1})
and
(1,\beta,\ldots,\beta^{m-1})
.
Therefore,
A\in\mathrm{GL}_{km}(q)
.
Also,
GA
is equal to

((a_{0,0}+a_{1,0}\alpha+\ldots+a_{m-1,0}\alpha^{m-1})I_{k}\mid(a_{0,1}+a_{1,1}\alpha+\ldots+a_{m-1,1}\alpha^{m-1})I_{k}\mid\cdots\\
\mid(a_{0,m-1}+a_{1,m-1}\alpha+\ldots+a_{m-1,m-1}\alpha^{m-1})I_{k})=G^{\prime},

that is
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
are linearly equivalent.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

The above result can also be proved by using the geometric interpretation of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear rank-metric codes established in
[
34
]
, see also
[
1
]
.

In order to determine the number of
[mk,k,m]_{q^{m}/q}
-codes in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{mk}
, we first determined the related linear automorphism group.
Based on Section
5.1
and on Proposition
5.3
, we may only consider the code

\mathcal{C}=\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{m,q}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}],

since it is an
[mk,k,m]_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}
-code.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
k\geq 2
.
The linear automorphism group of
\mathcal{C}=\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}
is

\mathrm{Autlin}(\mathcal{C})=\{(a_{1,1}x_{1}+\ldots+a_{1,k}x_{k},\ldots,a_{k,1}x_{1}+\ldots+a_{k,k}x_{k})\colon\mathrm{rk}((a_{i,j}))=k\}.

In particular, we get

|\mathrm{Autlin}(\mathcal{C})|=|\mathrm{GL}_{q^{m}}(k)|=(q^{mk}-1)(q^{mk}-q^{m})\cdots(q^{mk}-q^{m(k-1)}).

Proof.

Recall that

\mathrm{Autlin}(\mathcal{C})=\{f\in\mathrm{GL}_{km}(q)\colon\mathcal{C}\circ f=\mathcal{C}\}=\{(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})\in\mathrm{GL}_{km}(q)\colon x_{i}\circ f_{i}\in\mathcal{C},\,\,\forall i\in[k]\}.

Therefore, we have that there exist
a_{i,j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
such that

f_{i}(\underline{x})=a_{i,1}x_{1}+\ldots+a_{i,k}x_{k}

for any
i\in[k]
. Since the map defined by
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})
is invertible if and only if the matrix
\smash{(a_{i,j})}
is invertible, the result follows.
∎

The following result follows by combining the orbit-stabilizer theorem, Proposition
5.3
and Theorem
5.5
.

Corollary 5.6
.

The number of
[mk,k,m]_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}
-codes is

\frac{|\mathrm{GL}_{mk}(q)|}{(q^{mk}-1)(q^{mk}-q^{m})\cdots(q^{mk}-q^{m(k-1)})}.

As a consequence, we can determine the density of MRD codes in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{mk}
of dimension
k
, as well as their asymptotic behaviour.

Corollary 5.7
.

We have

\delta^{\textnormal{rk}}_{m}(m,mk,k;q)=\frac{|\mathrm{GL}_{mk}(q)|}{(q^{mk}-1)(q^{mk}-q^{m})\cdots(q^{mk}-q^{m(k-1)})\begin{bmatrix}{mk}\\
{k}\end{bmatrix}_{q^{m}}},

and

\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\delta^{\textnormal{rk}}_{m}(m,mk,k;q)=1.

6
Explicit results on Whitney numbers

In this section, we establish some numerical results on the Whitney numbers of rank-metric lattices. In some cases we are able to provide closed formulas for them, which is in general a very hard task. We obtain these results as corollaries of the formulas established throughout the paper.

The following result is a consequence of
[
35
, Corollary 3.1]
and Corollary
5.6
.

Corollary 6.1
.

Let
i=2
and
m=3
. For
n\in\{4,5\}
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
we have

\displaystyle w_{j}(2,n,3;q)=\begin{bmatrix}{n}\\
{3}\end{bmatrix}_{q}\begin{bmatrix}{n-1}\\
{j-1}\end{bmatrix}_{q^{m}}(q^{3}-q)(q^{3}-q^{2})(-1)^{j-1}q^{3\binom{j-1}{2}}.

Moreover, for
n=6
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,6\}
we get

\displaystyle w_{j}(2,6,3;q)=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}{6}\\
{3}\end{bmatrix}_{q}\begin{bmatrix}{5}\\
{j-1}\end{bmatrix}_{q^{m}}(q^{3}-q)(q^{3}-q^{2})(-1)^{j-1}q^{\binom{j-1}{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}{4}\\
{j-2}\end{bmatrix}_{q^{m}}(q^{6}-q)(q^{6}-q^{2})(q^{6}-q^{4})(q^{6}-q^{5})(-1)^{j-2}q^{3\binom{j-2}{2}}.

Proof.

For
n\in\{4,5\}
, we have

\displaystyle w_{j}(2,n,3;q)=|\{\left<x\right>\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\colon\textnormal{rk}(x)=3\}|\begin{bmatrix}{n-1}\\
{j-1}\end{bmatrix}_{q^{m}}(-1)^{j-1}q^{3\binom{j-1}{2}}

which implies the first part of the statement. For
n=6
,
[
35
, Corollary 3.1]
implies

w_{j}(2,6,3;q)=|\{\left<x\right>\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\colon\textnormal{rk}(x)=3\}|\begin{bmatrix}{5}\\
{j-1}\end{bmatrix}_{q^{m}}(-1)^{j-1}q^{3\binom{j-1}{2}}\\
+|\{C\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\colon C\textup{ is }[6,2,3]_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}}\textup{-code}\}|\begin{bmatrix}{4}\\
{j-2}\end{bmatrix}_{q^{m}}(-1)^{j-2}q^{3\binom{j-2}{2}}.

The statement now follows from Corollary
5.6
.
∎

The next result is the analogue of
[
35
, Theorem 5.1]
and provides a recursive formula for computing the Whitney numbers of the first kind.

Theorem 6.2
.

If
1\leq ij\leq\min\{n,m\}
then

w_{j}(i,n,m;q)=\sum_{s=1}^{ij}\begin{bmatrix}{n}\\
{s}\end{bmatrix}_{q}\sum_{t=1}^{s}w_{j}(i,t,m;q)\begin{bmatrix}{s}\\
{t}\end{bmatrix}_{q}q^{\binom{s-t}{2}}(-1)^{s-t}.

Proof.

Fix
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and notice that, for all
X\in\mathscr{L}_{i}(n,m;q)
with
\dim(X)=j
, we have
\dim({\textnormal{supp}}(X))\leq ij
. We get

\displaystyle w_{j}(i,n,m;q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\in\mathscr{L}_{i}(n,m;q)\\
\dim(X)=j\end{subarray}}\mu_{n,m,q}^{(i)}(X)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{ij}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\\
\dim(S)=s\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\in\mathscr{L}_{i}(n,m;q)\\
\dim(X)=j\\
{\textnormal{supp}}(X)=S\end{subarray}}\mu_{n,m,q}^{(i)}(X)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{ij}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\\
\dim(S)=s\end{subarray}}f(S)

where, for all
S\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, we define

f(S)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\in\mathscr{L}_{i}(n,m;q)\\
\dim(X)=j\\
{\textnormal{supp}}(X)=S\end{subarray}}\mu_{n,m,q}^{(i)}(X).

For a subspace
S\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
and for all
T\leq S
, define

g(T)=\sum_{T^{\prime}\leq T}f(T^{\prime}).

Since
ij\geq 1
we have
j\geq 1
and
g(\left<0\right>)=0
(sum over an empty index set). Moreover, observe that, for any
X\in\mathscr{L}_{i}(n,m;q)
with
{\textnormal{supp}}(X)=S
there exists a lattice isomorphism that maps the interval
[0,X]
in
\mathscr{L}_{i}(n,m;q)
to
\mathscr{L}_{i}(\dim(S),m;q)
. Therefore, for any
t
-dimensional
T\leq S
, we get

g(T)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\in\mathscr{L}_{i}(n,m;q)\\
\dim(X)=j\\
{\textnormal{supp}}(X)\leq T\end{subarray}}\mu_{n,m,q}^{(i)}(X)=w_{j}(i,t,m;q).

If
s=\dim(S)
, using Möbius inversion, then we get

f(S)=\sum_{T\leq S}g(T)(-1)^{s-\dim(T)}=\sum_{t=1}^{s}w_{j}(i,t,m;q)\begin{bmatrix}{s}\\
{t}\end{bmatrix}_{q}q^{\binom{s-t}{2}}(-1)^{s-t}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle w_{j}(i,n,m;q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{ij}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\\
\dim(S)=s\end{subarray}}f(S)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{ij}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\\
\dim(S)=s\end{subarray}}\sum_{t=1}^{s}w_{j}(i,t,m;q)\begin{bmatrix}{s}\\
{t}\end{bmatrix}_{q}q^{\binom{s-t}{2}}(-1)^{s-t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{ij}\begin{bmatrix}{n}\\
{s}\end{bmatrix}_{q}\sum_{t=1}^{s}w_{j}(i,t,m;q)\begin{bmatrix}{s}\\
{t}\end{bmatrix}_{q}q^{\binom{s-t}{2}}(-1)^{s-t},

which concludes the proof.
∎

One one the main difference with the Hamming-metric is the computation of the quantity
f(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
, which could be explicitly determined in
[
35
, Theorem 5.1]
.

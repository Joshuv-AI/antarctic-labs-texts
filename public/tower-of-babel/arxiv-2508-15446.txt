A smoothed proximal trust-region algorithm for nonconvex optimization problems with L p L^{p} -regularization, p ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) p\in(0,1) Thanks: This work is partially supported by the Office of Naval Research (ONR) under Award NO: N00014-24-1-2147, NSF grant DMS-2408877, the Air Force Office of Scientific Research (AFOSR) under Award NO: FA9550-22-1-0248 and the German Research Foundation DFG under project grant Wa 3626/5-1.
Harbir Antil, Anna Lentz

Abstract
We investigate a trust-region algorithm to solve a nonconvex optimization problem with L p L^{p} -regularization for p ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) p\in(0,1) . The algorithm relies on descent properties of a so-called generalized Cauchy point that can be obtained efficiently by a line search along a suitable proximal path. To handle the nonconvexity and nonsmoothness of the L p L^{p} -pseudonorm, we replace it by a smooth approximation and construct a convex upper bound of that approximation. This enables us to use results of a trust-region method for composite problems with a convex nonsmooth term. We prove convergence properties of the resulting smoothed proximal trust-region algorithm and investigate its performance in some numerical examples. Furthermore, approximate subproblem solvers for the arising trust-region subproblems are considered.



1
Introduction

We consider the optimization problem

\min_{u\in U_{ad}}f(u)+\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\differential x\qquad\text{for }p\in(0,1),\qquad U_{ad}\subseteq U,

(1)

where
U
represents either the space
L^{2}(\Omega)
or
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
for some bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

f:U\to\mathbb{R}
is a Fréchet differentiable function with Lipschitz continuous gradient and
U_{ad}
represents some admissible set.
The nonconvex
L^{p}
-pseudonorm promotes sparsity in solutions, making this problem relevant in various applications.
However, the nonconvexity and nonsmoothness of the objective pose
theoretical and computational challenges.
The aim of this paper is to develop a trust-region algorithm that solves this problem numerically.

Problems of the above form have already been studied thoroughly in the literature, for example in the context of inverse problems
[
14
]
or sparse optimal control. In the latter case, the
L^{p}
-pseudonorm leads to sparse controls, which is for example desirable in actuator placement problems. The article
[
16
]
investigates such optimal control problems, derives necessary optimality conditions and discusses existence of solutions and regularization methods.
Based on this regularization,
[
1
]
develops necessary optimality conditions in the (fractional) Sobolev setting.

Numerically solving such problems can be challenging due to the nonconvexity of the
L^{p}
-term.
Existing approaches include proximal gradient methods
[
23
]
, majorize-minorization methods with a smoothing scheme
[
1
,
16
]
and difference-of-convex methods
[
21
]
.

More general composite problems

\min_{u\in U}f(u)+\phi(u)

consisting of the sum of a (nonconvex) smooth function
f
and a nonconvex and nonsmooth function
\phi
can be solved with proximal gradient methods
[
4
]
, other splitting methods
[
12
]
or alternating minimization algorithms, often under the Kurdyka–Łojasiewicz condition
[
11
,
3
]
.

However, these methods are first order methods and thus possibly slowly converging. Therefore, improvements like an accelerated proximal gradient method were considered in
[
19
]
.
Second-order methods, such as proximal quasi-Newton (see e.g.
[
17
]
) and trust-region algorithms offer faster convergence but traditionally require convexity.

Such a trust-region method for composite problems with the nonsmooth part being convex was introduced in
[
5
]
. This trust-region algorithm uses a so-called generalized Cauchy point to compute a trial step that satisfies a descent condition which is sufficient for convergence of the algorithm, analogous to a Cauchy point in the standard trust-region algorithm. This generalized Cauchy point (GCP) can be computed efficiently using the proximal operator. This fact distinguishes the algorithm from other approaches as for example in
[
2
]
. While that method also applies to nonconvex nonsmooth functions, it requires the computation of a proximal point of
\phi
plus the indicator function of the trust-region, which is more difficult.

However, extending the GCP approach from
[
5
]
to nonconvex problems is non-trivial. The nonconvexity breaks key properties of the proximity operator, making it difficult to ensure descent conditions or global convergence.

In this paper, we aim to generalize the trust-region algorithm from
[
5
]
from the case of a convex function
\phi
to the nonconvex
L^{p}
-pseudonorm.
Due to the nonconvexity we were not able to prove that nonconvex generalized Cauchy points satisfy a descent condition that leads to convergence of the algorithm as in the convex case. At each iteration, we therefore construct a convex upper bound of the
L^{p}
-term with the help of a smooth approximation. This allows us to leverage some of the existing convergence theory from the convex case to the nonconvex
L^{p}
-case, but several new results are required.
Thus, we first smooth and convexify the
L^{p}
-pseudonorm and then compute proximal points of the convex surrogate function. Another approach that we do not pursue further could be to keep working with the
L^{p}
-pseudonorm, but use an approximation of the proximal point instead.

Moreover,
[
5
]
allows for inexact objective function and derivative evaluations. This is relevant when
f
or its gradient can not be evaluated efficiently as for example in PDE-constrained optimization. Therefore, we have also generalized the handling of inexactness from
[
5
]
to our
L^{p}
-setting.

The performance of trust-region algorithms depends significantly on the chosen subproblem solver. In
[
5
]
, a spectral proximal gradient (SPG) method was used. Other subproblem solvers or extensions of the SPG method function were considered in
[
6
,
13
]
. However, those solvers make use of the convexity of the nonsmooth part in the composite problem, so they can not be directly transferred to the
L^{p}
-setting. Therefore, we use some kind of spectral proximal gradient method in our implementation, or a majorize-minimization (MM) algorithm based on the scheme in
[
1
]
. The latter MM scheme does not involve proximal point computations of the
L^{p}
-pseudonorm. This also allows for the application in the
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
-setting, where the proximal point computation can not be reduced to pointwise optimization problems as in the
L^{2}(\Omega)
-case.

The structure of the paper is as follows. Section
2
provides preliminaries by investigating properties of the proximal map in the nonconvex setting, deriving necessary optimality conditions for (
2
) and introducing a smooth approximation of the
L^{p}
-pseudonorm. Based on this, section
3
presents a trust-region algorithm and studies its convergence properties. Section
4
analyzes the use of generalized Cauchy points in the trust-region algorithm to guarantee sufficient decrease of the iterates. The subsequent section
5
examines numerical algorithms to solve the subproblems in each iteration of the trust-region algorithm. Finally, section
6
presents some numerical results.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Notation and standing assumptions

Throughout this paper, we adopt the Hilbert spaces
L^{2}(\Omega)
and
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
as the underlying function spaces. To refer to both of these spaces, we use the notation
U
with norm
\|\cdot\|
and inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)
. If we want to specify which of these two spaces is considered, we use the subscripts
L^{2}(\Omega)
or
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
whenever this is not clear from the setting of the respective section. We often identify the dual
U^{*}
with
U
via the Riesz representation theorem.

Note that we could also work with fractional Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\Omega)
for
s\in(0,1]
as in
[
1
]
, but for simplicity we restrict ourselves to the non-fractional
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
-case in this paper.

The
L^{p}
-pseudonorm for
p\in(0,1)
is denoted by

j(u)\coloneqq\|u\|_{p}^{p}=\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\differential x.

It is called pseudonorm since it is concave and therefore not a norm.

We rewrite problem (
1
) as

\min_{u\in U}f(u)+\phi(u)

(2)

and impose the following assumptions.

Assumption 2.1
.

1.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
d\geq 1
be a bounded domain.

2.

f:U\to\mathbb{R}
is
L
-smooth, i.e. there exists an open set
W\subseteq U
with
\operatorname{dom}\phi\subseteq W
such that
f
is Fréchet differentiable on
W
and its gradient is Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant
L>0
.

3.

Let
\phi:U\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
be defined as

\phi(u)=\beta\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\differential x+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|u\|^{2}+I_{U_{ad}}(u)

(3)

with
\alpha\geq 0,\beta>0
and
p\in(0,1)
. For
U=L^{2}(\Omega)
, the admissible set
U_{ad}
is defined by

U_{ad}\coloneq\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega):a\leq u\leq b\},

(4)

where
a,b\in L^{2}(\Omega)
such that
a<b
.

For
U=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, we only consider the unconstrained case with
U_{ad}=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and assume
\alpha>0
.

4.

The objective function
F\coloneq f+\phi
is bounded below.

For the convergence analysis later on we will impose some assumptions on the curvature of
f
.
We use the same notion of curvature as in
[
5
,
24
]
: For a Fréchet differentiable functional
h:U\to\mathbb{R}
, the curvature of
u\in U
in direction
s\in U
is defined as

\omega(h,u,s)\coloneqq\frac{2}{\|s\|^{2}}(h(u+s)-h(u)-\braket{\nabla h(u),s}).

(5)

As discussed in
[
5
]
, the curvature
\omega(f,u,s)
of
f
satisfying assumption
2.1
is bounded by
L
for
u,u+s\in W
.
Furthermore, for quadratic
h
with self-adjoint Hessian operator
B
it holds

\omega(h,u,s)=\frac{\braket{s,Bs}}{\|s\|^{2}}.

2.2
Nonconvex proximal points

The proximal operator of a function
\tilde{\phi}:U\to\mathbb{R}
is defined for some
r>0
by

\operatorname{prox}_{r\tilde{\phi}}(u)=\arg\min_{v\in U}\frac{1}{2r}\|v-u\|^{2}+\tilde{\phi}(v).

For convex functions, this map is known to be single valued (
[
7
, Chapter 12]
). However, this does not hold in general in the nonconvex case, see Remark
2.12
later on. In this paper, we also use the notation
\operatorname{prox}_{r\tilde{\phi}}(u)
to refer to elements of
\operatorname{prox}_{r\tilde{\phi}}(u)
.

Moreover, several other favorable properties from the convex case do not extend to the nonconvex regime. In view of this, we begin by examining which results from
[
5
]
remain valid when the convexity assumption is dropped.

We start by generalizing
[
5
, Lemma 1.1]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\tilde{\phi}:U\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
be some proper and lower semicontinuous function. For the proximal operator of
\tilde{\phi}
it holds

(\operatorname{prox}_{r\tilde{\phi}}(u)-u,v-\operatorname{prox}_{r\tilde{\phi}}(u))+\frac{1}{2}\|\operatorname{prox}_{r\tilde{\phi}}(u)-v\|^{2}\geq r\tilde{\phi}(\operatorname{prox}_{r\tilde{\phi}}(u))-r\tilde{\phi}(v).

Proof.

From the definition of the proximal operator we obtain

\tilde{\phi}(v)+\frac{1}{2r}\|v-u\|^{2}\geq\tilde{\phi}(\operatorname{prox}_{r\tilde{\phi}}(u))+\frac{1}{2r}\|\operatorname{prox}_{r\tilde{\phi}}(u)-u\|^{2}\qquad\forall v\in U.

Reordering and using
\|v-u\|^{2}=\|\operatorname{prox}_{r\tilde{\phi}}(u)-v\|^{2}+2(\operatorname{prox}_{r\tilde{\phi}}(u)-u,v-\operatorname{prox}_{r\tilde{\phi}}(u))+\|\operatorname{prox}_{r\tilde{\phi}}(u)-u\|^{2}
yields

\tilde{\phi}(v)-\tilde{\phi}(\operatorname{prox}_{r\tilde{\phi}}(u))\geq\frac{1}{2r}\left(\|\operatorname{prox}_{r\tilde{\phi}}(u)-u\|^{2}-\|v-u\|^{2}\right)\\
=\frac{1}{2r}\left[-\|\operatorname{prox}_{r\tilde{\phi}}(u)-v\|^{2}-2(\operatorname{prox}_{r\tilde{\phi}}(u)-u,v-\operatorname{prox}_{r\tilde{\phi}}(u))\right].

(6)

Multiplying by
-r
yields the claim.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\tilde{\phi}:U\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
be some proper and lower semicontinuous function, let
u,d\in U
,
r>0
and define

\displaystyle\Phi(r)

\displaystyle\coloneq\|\operatorname{prox}_{r\tilde{\phi}}(u+rd)-u\|,

\displaystyle\Psi(r)

\displaystyle\coloneq\Phi(r)/r.

1.

Then the mapping
r\mapsto\Phi(r)
is nondecreasing for
r>0
.

2.

For all
r>0
, it holds

(d,\operatorname{prox}_{r\tilde{\phi}}(u+rd)-u)+\tilde{\phi}(u)-\tilde{\phi}(\operatorname{prox}_{r\tilde{\phi}}(u+rd))\geq\frac{1}{2}\Psi(r)\Phi(r).

(7)

Proof.

The proof of
[
8
, Theorem 10.9]
(c.f.
[
5
, Lemma 2]
) can be adapted to the nonconvex case using Lemma
2.2
instead of the corresponding inequality for the convex case. Let
r_{1}>r_{2}
. By setting
r=r_{1}
,
u=u+r_{1}d
and
v=\operatorname{prox}_{r_{2}\tilde{\phi}}(u+r_{2}d)
in Lemma
2.2
we obtain

(\operatorname{prox}_{r_{1}\tilde{\phi}}(u+r_{1}d)-(u+r_{1}d),\operatorname{prox}_{r_{2}\tilde{\phi}}(u+r_{2}d)-\operatorname{prox}_{r_{1}\tilde{\phi}}(u+r_{1}d))\\
+\frac{1}{2}\|\operatorname{prox}_{r_{1}\tilde{\phi}}(u+r_{1}d)-\operatorname{prox}_{r_{2}\tilde{\phi}}(u+r_{2}d)\|^{2}\\
\geq r_{1}\tilde{\phi}(\operatorname{prox}_{r_{1}\tilde{\phi}}(u+r_{1}d))-r_{1}\tilde{\phi}(\operatorname{prox}_{r_{2}\tilde{\phi}}(u+r_{2}d)).

(8)

Let us define

p_{u,d}(r)\coloneq\operatorname{prox}_{r\tilde{\phi}}(u+rd)-u.

Then, this can be written as

(p_{u,d}(r_{1})-r_{1}d,p_{u,d}(r_{2})-p_{u,d}(r_{1}))+\frac{1}{2}\|p_{u,d}(r_{1})-p_{u,d}(r_{2})\|^{2}\\
\geq r_{1}\tilde{\phi}(\operatorname{prox}_{r_{1}\tilde{\phi}}(u+r_{1}d))-r_{1}\tilde{\phi}(\operatorname{prox}_{r_{2}\tilde{\phi}}(u+r_{2}d)).

Interchanging the roles of
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
yields

(p_{u,d}(r_{2})-r_{2}d,p_{u,d}(r_{1})-p_{u,d}(r_{2}))+\frac{1}{2}\|p_{u,d}(r_{2})-p_{u,d}(r_{1})\|^{2}\\
\geq r_{2}\tilde{\phi}(\operatorname{prox}_{r_{2}\tilde{\phi}}(u+r_{2}d))-r_{2}\tilde{\phi}(\operatorname{prox}_{r_{1}\tilde{\phi}}(u+r_{1}d)).

We add these two equations after dividing by
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
, respectively, to obtain

\left(\frac{1}{r_{1}}p_{u,d}(r_{1})-\frac{1}{r_{2}}p_{u,d}(r_{2}),p_{u,d}(r_{2})-p_{u,d}(r_{1})\right)+\left(\frac{1}{2r_{1}}+\frac{1}{2r_{2}}\right)\|p_{u,d}(r_{1})-p_{u,d}(r_{2})\|^{2}\geq 0.

Expanding the two terms and reordering gives

\frac{r_{2}-r_{1}}{2r_{1}r_{2}}\|p_{u,d}(r_{1})\|^{2}+\frac{r_{1}-r_{2}}{2r_{1}r_{2}}\|p_{u,d}(r_{2})\|^{2}\leq 0,

so
\|p_{u,d}(r_{2})\|\leq\|p_{u,d}(r_{1})\|
and the first part is proven.

As done in
[
5
, Lemma 2]
, we set
u=u+rd
and
v=u
in Lemma
2.2
to obtain

(\operatorname{prox}_{r\tilde{\phi}}(u+rd)-(u+rd),u-\operatorname{prox}_{r\tilde{\phi}}(u+rd))+\frac{1}{2}\|\operatorname{prox}_{r\tilde{\phi}}(u+rd)-u\|^{2}\\
\geq r\tilde{\phi}(\operatorname{prox}_{r\tilde{\phi}}(u+rd))-r\tilde{\phi}(u).

Reordering leads to (
7
).
∎

Note that the additional quadratic term in Lemma
2.2
leads to the additional constant
\frac{1}{2}
on the right hand side of inequality (
7
) compared to
[
5
, Lemma 2, part 3]
.

Remark 2.4
.

In the convex case, the function
\Psi
defined in the previous Lemma is nonincreasing. This does not hold in the nonconvex case, as
r\mapsto\operatorname{prox}_{r\tilde{\phi}}(u+rd)
is not continuous anymore as in the convex case by
[
5
, Lemma 3]
. This can be seen for example with
d=0
and
\tilde{\phi}
being the
L^{p}
-pseudonorm around some
r>0
where
\operatorname{prox}_{r\phi}(u)
jumps from zero to some nonzero value as
r
decreases, compare Lemma
2.11
later on.

2.3
Optimality conditions

In this section we investigate optimality conditions for problem (
2
) which will later serve as a basis for the convergence analysis of the trust-region algorithm.

2.3.1
In
U=L^{2}(\Omega)

We start by considering necessary optimality conditions for problem (
2
) using the Fréchet and limiting subdifferential for the case
U=L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Definition 2.5
.

The Fréchet subdifferential of a function
g:U\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
at
\bar{u}
is defined by

\hat{\partial}g(\bar{u})\coloneqq\left\{\eta\in U^{*}|\liminf_{\|h\|\searrow 0}\frac{g(\bar{u}+h)-g(\bar{u})-(\eta,h)}{\|h\|}\geq 0\right\}.

The limiting subdifferential of
g
is given by

\partial g(\bar{u})\coloneqq\left\{\eta\in U^{*}|\exists(u_{k})_{k}\subset U,\exists(\eta_{k})_{k}\subset U^{*}:\right.\\
\left.u_{k}\to\bar{u}\text{ in }U,g(u_{k})\to g(\bar{u}),\eta_{k}\rightharpoonup\eta\text{ in }U^{*},\eta_{k}\in\hat{\partial}g(u_{k})\,\forall k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

In the unconstrained case
U_{ad}=U
and under Assumption
2.1
, part
2
, the sum rule applies to the limiting and Fréchet subdifferential by
[
22
, Theorem 3.36, Proposition 1.107]
. Thus, by Fermat’s rule, a solution
\bar{u}
of the minimization problem (
2
) satisfies the necessary optimality conditions

-\nabla f(\bar{u})\in\hat{\partial}\phi(\bar{u})\qquad\text{and}\qquad-\nabla f(\bar{u})\in\partial\phi(\bar{u}).

(9)

The inclusions (
9
) can be very weak. For example, at
\bar{u}=0
, both the Fréchet and limiting subdifferential of
\phi(u)=|u|^{p}
with
p\in(0,1)
are the whole space
L^{2}(\Omega)
as can be seen from the following lemma from
[
20
]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

[
20
, Theorem 4.6]

The Fréchet and limiting subdifferentials of
j:L^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
,
j(u)\coloneq\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\differential x
for
p\in(0,1)
are given by

\hat{\partial}j(u)=\partial j(u)=\{\eta\in L^{2}(\Omega):\eta=p|u|^{p-2}u\text{ a.e. on }\{u\neq 0\}\}.

Moreover, if
\phi
is the sum of two functions that are not so-called sequentially normally epi-compact functions, then one can not apply the sum rule from
[
22
]
. This is for example relevant if
\phi
is the sum of the
L^{p}
-pseudo norm
\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\differential x
for
p\in(0,1)
and the indicator function corresponding to control constraints.

However, for the special case of box constraints, one can derive a sum rule as already suggested in
[
20
]
.
This fact explains definition (
4
) of the admissible set
U_{ad}
in Assumption
2.1
.

By definition of the convex subdifferential, it holds

\displaystyle\partial I_{U_{ad}}(\bar{u})

\displaystyle=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega):0\geq(v,w-\bar{u})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\forall w\in U_{ad}\}

\displaystyle=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega):v\geq 0\,\text{ a.e. on }\{\bar{u}=b\},v\leq 0\,\text{ a.e. on }\{\bar{u}=a\},v=0\text{ elsewhere}\}.

The Fréchet and limiting subdifferentials coincide with the convex subdifferential for convex functions, see e.g.
[
22
, page 83, 95]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

It holds
\hat{\partial}(j(\bar{u})+I_{U_{ad}}(\bar{u}))=\hat{\partial}j(\bar{u})+\hat{\partial}I_{U_{ad}}(\bar{u})
for all
\bar{u}\in U_{ad}
. I.e.,

\hat{\partial}(j(\bar{u})+I_{U_{ad}}(\bar{u}))=\left\{\eta\in L^{2}(\Omega):\eta\begin{cases}\geq p|\bar{u}|^{p-2}\bar{u}&\text{a.e. on }\{\bar{u}=b\},\\
\leq p|\bar{u}|^{p-2}\bar{u}&\text{a.e. on }\{\bar{u}=a\},\\
=p|\bar{u}|^{p-2}\bar{u}&\text{a.e. elsewhere on }\{\bar{u}\neq 0\}\end{cases}\right\}.

Proof.

The proof is an extension of some proofs in
[
20
]
.

We start with the inclusion "
\subseteq
" following
[
20
, Lemma 4.1]
. Let
\eta\in\hat{\partial}(j+I_{U_{ad}})(\bar{u})
, let
\epsilon>0
and define the set
A_{\epsilon}^{-}\coloneq\{|\bar{u}|>\epsilon\}\cap\{\bar{u}<b-\epsilon\}
. Let
B^{-}\subset A_{\epsilon}^{-}
be a measurable set of positive measure and define
(h_{k})_{k}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
as
h_{k}\coloneq\frac{1}{k}\chi_{B^{-}}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, it holds
\|h_{k}\|\searrow 0
and for
k
large enough one has
\bar{u}+h_{k}\in U_{ad}
.
By definition of the Fréchet subdifferential we obtain

0\leq|B^{-}|^{1/2}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{(j+I_{U_{ad}})(\bar{u}+h_{k})-(j+I_{U_{ad}})(\bar{u})-\int_{\Omega}\eta(x)h_{k}(x)\differential x}{\|h_{k}\|}\\
=|B^{-}|^{1/2}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{j(\bar{u}+h_{k})-j(\bar{u})-\int_{\Omega}\eta(x)h_{k}(x)\differential x}{\|h_{k}\|}\\
=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{B^{-}}\left(k\left(\left|\bar{u}(x)+\frac{1}{k}\right|^{p}-|\bar{u}(x)|^{p}\right)-\eta(x)\right)\differential x=\int_{B^{-}}p|\bar{u}(x)|^{p-2}\bar{u}(x)-\eta(x)\,\differential x

via dominated convergence.
Using the set
A_{\epsilon}^{+}\coloneq\{|\bar{u}|>\epsilon\}\cap\{\bar{u}>a+\epsilon\}
, one can repeat the steps above with
\tilde{h}_{k}=-\frac{1}{k}\chi_{B^{+}(x)}
for subsets
B^{+}\subset A_{\epsilon}^{+}
to obtain a reverse inequality. As the sets
B^{+}
and
B^{-}
were arbitrary, this shows
\eta\leq p|\bar{u}|^{p-2}\bar{u}
a.e. on
A_{\epsilon}^{-}
and
\eta\geq p|\bar{u}|^{p-2}\bar{u}
a.e. on
A_{\epsilon}^{+}
. Using
\{|\bar{u}|\neq 0\}\cap\{\bar{u}<b\}=\cup_{\epsilon>0}A_{\epsilon}^{-}
and
\{|\bar{u}|\neq 0\}\cap\{\bar{u}>a\}=\cup_{\epsilon>0}A_{\epsilon}^{+}
and combining both inequalities on
\{|\bar{u}|\neq 0\}\cap\{a<\bar{u}<b\}
yields the claim.

For the reverse inclusion "
\supseteq
", we proceed similarly as in
[
20
, Theorem 4.4]
. Let
\eta\in L^{2}(\Omega)
with
\eta=p|\bar{u}|^{p-2}\bar{u}\chi_{\{\bar{u}\neq 0\}}+\eta_{1}\chi_{\{\bar{u}=b\}}-\eta_{2}\chi_{\{\bar{u}=a\}}
with functions
0\leq\eta_{1},\eta_{2}
. Let
(h_{k})_{k}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
with
\|h_{k}\|\searrow 0
. We want to show

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{(j+I_{U_{ad}})(\bar{u}+h_{k})-(j+I_{U_{ad}})(\bar{u})-\int_{\Omega}\eta h_{k}\differential x}{\|h_{k}\|}\geq 0.

If
\bar{u}+h_{k}\notin U_{ad}
for all
k
, the statement clearly holds as
\bar{u}\in U_{ad}
, so we can assume w.l.o.g. that
\bar{u}+h_{k}\in I_{U_{ad}}
for
k
large enough. Thus, we have to show

0\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{j(\bar{u}+h_{k})-j(\bar{u})-\int_{\Omega}\eta h_{k}\differential x}{\|h_{k}\|}\\
=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{\int_{\{\bar{u}=0\}}(|h_{k}(x)|^{p}-\eta(x)h_{k}(x))\differential x}{\|h_{k}\|}+\frac{\int_{\{\bar{u}\neq 0\}}(|\bar{u}(x)+h_{k}(x))|^{p}-|\bar{u}(x)|^{p}-\eta(x)h_{k}(x)\differential x}{\|h_{k}\|}\\
\eqqcolon\liminf_{k\to\infty}D_{k}^{1}+D_{k}^{2}.

As
D_{k}^{1}
coincides with the unconstrained case from
[
20
, Theorem 4.4]

3
Trust-region algorithm with smoothing

We aim to solve problem (
2
) with a trust-region algorithm by generalizing the algorithm from
[
5
]
for the convex case.
That algorithm is an inexact proximal trust-region algorithm that solves the composite problem

\min_{u\in U}f(u)+\phi(u)

for smooth
f
and convex
\phi
. It is based on the computation of a generalized Cauchy point (GCP) along a proximal path. This GCP is easy to compute and satisfies a sufficient decrease condition in the convex case. Therefore, we also aim to use this generalized Cauchy path in the nonconvex case. However, the proof of sufficient decrease can not be directly generalized to our
L^{p}
-setting here. To overcome this difficulty and to reuse some of the results from the convex case, we work with a convex upper bound of the objective in the trust-region subproblems using the function
\phi_{k}
from (
12
).

We start by some definitions. Let
h_{k}
be defined as

h_{k}\coloneq\frac{1}{r_{0}}\|\operatorname{prox}_{r_{0}\phi_{k}}(u_{k}-r_{0}\nabla f_{k}(u_{k}))-u_{k}\|

for some
r_{0}>0
, and let

h_{k}(u)\coloneqq\frac{1}{r_{0}}\|\operatorname{prox}_{r_{0}\phi_{k}}(u-r_{0}\nabla f(u))-u\|.

Note that the convex function
\phi_{k}
is used in these definitions, so the proximity operator is single valued.

In each trust-region step, we compute a solution of the trust-region subproblem. We will consider two subproblems defined by the models

m_{k}(u)\coloneq f_{k}(u)+\phi_{k}(u)\qquad\text{and}\qquad m_{k}^{\epsilon}(u)\coloneq f_{k}(u)+\phi_{\epsilon_{k}}(u).

The trust-region subproblems then read

\min_{u\in U}m_{k}^{(\epsilon)}\qquad\text{subject to}\qquad\|u-u_{k}\|\leq\Delta_{k},

where
\Delta_{k}
denotes the trust-region radius.
Here,
f_{k}
is a local model of
f
around
u_{k}
that satisfies the following inexactness conditions from
[
5
]
.

Assumption 3.1
.

The model
f_{k}
is
L_{k}
smooth (on the domain of
\phi
) and the gradient
g_{k}\coloneqq\nabla f_{k}(u_{k})
satisfies

\|g_{k}-\nabla f(u_{k})\|\leq\kappa_{\text{grad}}\min(h_{k},\Delta_{k})\qquad\forall k\in\mathbb{N},

where
\kappa_{\text{grad}}\geq 0
is independent of
k
.

We want to approximate solutions of those subproblems with
m_{k}
or
m_{k}^{\epsilon}
by computing points that satisfy the fraction of Cauchy decrease (FCD) condition

m_{k}^{(\epsilon)}(u_{k})-m_{k}^{(\epsilon)}(u_{k+1})\geq\kappa_{\text{fcd}}h_{k}\min\left(\frac{h_{k}}{1+\omega_{k}},\Delta_{k}\right)

(13)

and that lie inside the trust-region, i.e.

\|u_{k+1}-u_{k}\|\leq\kappa_{\text{rad}}\Delta_{k},

(14)

where the constants
\kappa_{\text{fcd}},\kappa_{\text{rad}}>0
are both independent of
k
.

Remark 3.2
.

Assume the FCD condition (
13
) holds for the convex model
m_{k}
. As
\phi_{k}
majorizes
\phi_{\epsilon_{k}}
, it holds
m_{k}(u_{k+1})\geq m_{k}^{\epsilon}(u_{k+1})
. Substituting this into the FCD condition (
13
) with the convex model
m_{k}
and using
m_{k}(u_{k})=m_{k}^{\epsilon}(u_{k})
then also implies the FCD
condition (
13
) with the nonconvex model
m_{k}^{\epsilon}
.

In trust-region algorithms, the trust-region radius is updated after each iteration according to the ratio of actual and predicted reductions. Thus, for the two models, let

\operatorname{ared}_{k}=f(u_{k})+\phi_{k}(u_{k})-f(u_{k+1})-\phi_{k}(u_{k+1}),\quad\operatorname{ared}_{k}^{\epsilon}=f(u_{k})+\phi_{\epsilon_{k}}(u_{k})-f(u_{k+1})-\phi_{\epsilon_{k}}(u_{k+1}),

\operatorname{pred}_{k}\coloneq m_{k}(u_{k})-m_{k}(u_{k+1}),\quad\operatorname{pred}_{k}^{\epsilon}=m_{k}^{\epsilon}(u_{k})-m_{k}^{\epsilon}(u_{k+1})

and

\tilde{\rho}_{k}\coloneq\frac{\operatorname{ared}_{k}}{\operatorname{pred}_{k}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\tilde{\rho}_{k}^{\epsilon}\coloneq\frac{\operatorname{ared}_{k}^{\epsilon}}{\operatorname{pred}_{k}^{\epsilon}}.

(15)

As mentioned in
[
5
]
, it is not always possible to compute the objective
F
exactly, or here also its smoothed version involving
j_{\epsilon}
. Thus, we introduce the computed reduction
\operatorname{cred}_{k}^{\epsilon}
, i.e. the numerically computed approximation of
\operatorname{ared}_{k}^{(\epsilon)}
. Then we can replace
\operatorname{ared}_{k}^{(\epsilon)}
by the computed reduction
\operatorname{cred}_{k}^{(\epsilon)}
, and we work with the ratios of computed and predicted reductions

\rho_{k}\coloneq\frac{\operatorname{cred}_{k}}{\operatorname{pred}_{k}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\rho_{k}^{\epsilon}\coloneq\frac{\operatorname{cred}_{k}^{\epsilon}}{\operatorname{pred}_{k}^{\epsilon}}

(16)

in the algorithm.

5
Approximate subproblem solutions

Having computed a generalized Cauchy point that satisfies the FCD condition (
13
), we want to improve the step by computing a better solution of the nonconvex subproblem.
To this end, we minimize the nonconvex model
m_{k}^{\epsilon}
with an iterative monotonically decreasing algorithm starting from the iterate
u_{k+1}
computed in the first step with the GCP Algorithm
2
. This nonconvex step improvement leads to an iterate that satisfies the FCD condition (
13
) with the nonconvex model
m_{k}^{\epsilon}
, see also Remark
3.2
.

5.1
Nonconvex SPG

In
[
5
, Appendix C]
, a spectral proximal gradient (SPG) method was used as a subproblem solver to improve upon the trial steps computed with the GCP algorithm. We were trying to employ this algorithm also in our nonconvex setting, but there are two issues:

Firstly, if
\phi
is nonconvex, one can not argue as in the convex case that the line search of this SPG method terminates after finitely many steps. Thus, we iterate over the proximal parameter
r
instead. This results in a proximal gradient method with variable step size and initial Barzilai-Borwein step size.

Second, the SPG method requires the computation of a proximal point of

\phi_{\epsilon}+I_{\Delta_{k}},\quad\text{where}\quad I_{\Delta_{k}}\coloneqq I_{B_{\Delta_{k}}(u_{k})}.

If
\phi_{\epsilon}
was convex, this can be done by finding a zero of

t\mapsto\|\operatorname{prox}_{rt\phi_{\epsilon}}(u_{k}+t(u-u_{k}))-u_{k}\|-\Delta_{k}

(35)

in case
\|\operatorname{prox}_{r\phi_{\epsilon}}(u)-u_{k}\|>\Delta_{k}
.
We were not able to prove that roots of (
35
) are proximal points of
\phi_{\epsilon}+I_{\Delta_{k}}
as in the convex case.
As the function (
35
) is not continuous in our nonconvex setting, it does not even necessarily have a root.
Regardless, since the mapping (
35
) is monotonically increasing by Lemma
2.3
one could compute the largest
t
such that the function is negative and in this way obtain a point
\operatorname{prox}_{rt\phi_{\epsilon}}(u_{k}+t(u-u_{k}))
that is inside the trust-region and might serve as an approximation for the proximal point of
\phi_{\epsilon}+I_{\Delta_{k}}
. Using this point as a new iterate of the SPG algorithm works in the examples in the last section, but we were not able to prove descent properties theoretically.
In
[
5
]
, finding zeros of (
35
) is accomplished using Brent’s method, a root-finding algorithm for continuous functions that combines bisection, quadratic interpolation and the secant method. Due to monotonicity of the function, we can also use Brent’s method in our setting here to compute the largest
t
such that the function (
35
) is negative.

In the SPG subproblem solver and NC-GCP, we have to compute proximal points of
\phi_{\epsilon}
. The resulting minimization problem can not be solved explicitly. We therefore use a gradient method that exploits the structure of the objective function in the proximal point computation:
By symmetry, we assume without loss of generality that the argument of the proximal operator is non-negative. If
\epsilon
is small enough, there is one positive inflection point of the objective when computing
\operatorname{prox}_{r\phi_{\epsilon}}(u)
that is larger than
\epsilon
. This inflection point is given by

u_{0}(r)\coloneq\sqrt[{}^{2-p}]{\frac{rp(1-p)\beta}{1+\alpha r}}.

Thus, we can run the gradient method with starting value larger than this inflection point and compare its solution with the minimum on
[0,\epsilon]
to obtain a proximal point. This minimum on
[0,\epsilon]
is given by

M_{\epsilon}\coloneq\min\left(\epsilon,\frac{u}{1+\alpha r+\beta rp/\epsilon^{2-p}}\right).

We test the algorithm with examples from
[

5

where

denotes the weak solution of a PDE. Furthermore, we choose
U_{ad}\coloneq\{u\in U:-b\leq u\leq b\}
for some
b\in\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
.

In the implementation we work with finite elements and discretize the controls
u
with piecewise constants and the state
y_{u}
with piecewise linear functions. Then we can solve the PDE numerically to evaluate
F
and to compute
\operatorname{cred}_{k}

6.3
Remarks on NC-GCP

When comparing the versions with only Algorithm
2
as subproblem solver, TR-NC-GCP mostly requires fewer iterations than TR-GCP across all examples, although it is slower overall. TR-NC-GCP leads in most cases also to results that are closer to the other numerical solutions.

When comparing the results of TR-SPG with TR-NC-SPG and TR-MM-SPG with TR-NC-MM-SPG, the performance seems to be depending on the example.
However, one can observe that among the unconstrained examples, the nonconvex versions mostly yield better results. Therefore, the fact that Lemma
4.5
and the subsequent Corollary
4.8
only hold in the unconstrained case might also be relevant in practical implementations.

Furthermore, choosing TR-NC-MM-SPG over TR-MM-SPG or TR-NC-GCP over TR-GCP helps for small values of
p
, when TR-MM-SPG does not really lead to sparse solutions. Tables
3
and
6
show that TR-NC-MM-SPG leads to results that are sparser and closer to those obtained with the other algorithms.

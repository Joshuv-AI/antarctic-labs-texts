Tautological classes for ( n , n + 1 ) (n,n+1) torus knots
Eugene Gorsky, Anton Mellit

Abstract
. We construct an explicit isomorphism between the HOMFLY-PT homology of ( n , n + 1 ) (n,n+1) torus knots and the direct sum of hook isotypic components of the space of diagonal coinvariants. As a consequence, we compute the action of tautological classes in HOMFLY-PT homology of ( n , n + 1 ) (n,n+1) torus knots and prove that it extends to an action of the Lie algebra of Hamiltonian vector fields on the plane. We also compute the action of differentials d N d_{N} in Rasmussen spectral sequences from HOMLFY-PT to 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( N ) \mathfrak{gl}(N) homology of ( n , n + 1 ) (n,n+1) torus knots.



1.
Introduction

Consider the polynomial ring
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
with an action of
S_{n}
which permutes the variables
x_{i}
and
y_{i}
simultaneously. The space of diagonal coinvariants
\mathrm{DR}_{n}
is defined as

\mathrm{DR}_{n}=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]/\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]^{S_{n}}_{+}

where
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]^{S_{n}}_{+}
is the ideal generated by symmetric polynomials of positive degree. This space was introduced by Garsia and Haiman in
[
7
]
and has been a subject of very intensive research in algebraic combinatorics
[
14
,
2
]
, algebraic geometry
[
13
,
3
,
16
]
and representation theory
[
8
]
. In particular, the bigraded Hilbert series of its sign component
\mathrm{DR}_{n}^{\mathrm{sgn}}
is known as the
q,t
-Catalan polynomial

c_{n}(q,t)

[
7
]
.

In
[
9
]
, the first author conjectured that the dimensions of triply graded reduced Khovanov-Rozansky homology
\overline{\mathrm{HHH}}
(also known as reduced HOMFLY-PT homology) of
(n,n+1)
torus knots are given by the
q,t
-Catalan polynomials, so we have an isomorphism of bigraded vector spaces

(1.1)

\overline{\mathrm{HHH}}^{0}(T(n,n+1))\simeq\mathrm{DR}_{n}^{\mathrm{sgn}}.

Here and below the superscript
0
refers to zero Hochschild degree (or
a
-degree).
This conjecture was confirmed by Hogancamp in
[
17
]
.
The proof in
[
17
]
was based on an intricate recursion satisfied by the graded dimensions on both sides, and did not give any explicit construction of the isomorphism (
1.1
).
Similarly, one cannot directly access any homology classes in
\overline{\mathrm{HHH}}^{0}(T(n,n+1))
besides a few very special ones. Our first main result addresses these issues.

Observe that there is a natural braid-like cobordism from the link
T(n,n)
to the knot
T(n,n+1)
which adds extra
(n-1)
positive crossings. For such cobordisms, Elias and Krasner
[
6
]
defined a map in HOMFLY homology:

\pi:\mathrm{HHH}(T(n,n))\to\mathrm{HHH}(T(n,n+1)).

Recall that
\mathrm{HHH}(T(n,n))
is naturally a module over
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}]=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

a) We have an isomorphism

\mathrm{HHH}^{0}(T(n,n))/(\mathbf{x})\mathrm{HHH}^{0}(T(n,n))\simeq\mathrm{DR}_{n}^{\mathrm{sgn}}

where
(\mathbf{x})=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\subset\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}]
is the maximal ideal.

b) The cobordism map
\pi:\mathrm{HHH}^{0}(T(n,n))\to\mathrm{HHH}^{0}(T(n,n+1))
is surjective and induces a
grading-preserving isomorphism

(1.2)

\pi^{\prime}:\mathrm{DR}_{n}^{\mathrm{sgn}}\to\overline{\mathrm{HHH}}^{0}(T(n,n+1))

c) More generally, the cobordism map
\pi:\mathrm{HHH}(T(n,n))\to\mathrm{HHH}(T(n,n+1))
is surjective and we have an isomorphism

\mathrm{DR}_{n}^{\mathrm{hook}}\simeq\mathrm{HHH}(T(n,n))/(\mathbf{x})\mathrm{HHH}(T(n,n))\simeq\overline{\mathrm{HHH}}(T(n,n+1))

where
\mathrm{DR}_{n}^{\mathrm{hook}}
is a direct sum of hook isotypic components in
\mathrm{DR}_{n}
, see Section
3.3
.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we use a deformation of
\mathrm{HHH}
known as the
y
-ified triply graded homology
\mathrm{HY}

[
10
]
. For knots
K
such as

the

-ified homology is essentially the same as
\overline{\mathrm{HHH}}
:

(1.3)

\mathrm{HY}(K)=\mathrm{HHH}(K)[y]=\overline{\mathrm{HHH}}(K)[x,y,\theta]

so we do not get any additional information. However, for the
n
-component link
T(n,n)
the

-ified homology (computed in
[
10

-ified version of Theorem

Theorem 1.2
.

a) We have an isomorphism

\mathrm{HY}^{0}(T(n,n))/(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\mathrm{HY}^{0}(T(n,n))\simeq\mathrm{DR}_{n}^{\mathrm{sgn}}

where
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\subset\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]
is the maximal ideal. More generally,

\mathrm{HY}(T(n,n))/(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\mathrm{HY}(T(n,n))\simeq\mathrm{DR}_{n}^{\mathrm{hook}}.

b) There is a map in
y
-ified homology (induced by a
y
-ified version of Elias-Krasner cobordism map)

2.
Background

2.1.
Link homology

We recall some definitions on link homology and refer to
[
10
,
11
]
for more details and grading conventions. Let
R=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. Consider the
R-R
bimodules

B_{i}=\frac{\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},x^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{n}]}{x_{i}+x_{i+1}=x^{\prime}_{i}+x^{\prime}_{i+1},x_{i}x_{i+1}=x^{\prime}_{i}x^{\prime}_{i+1},x_{j}=x^{\prime}_{j}\ (j\neq i,i+1)}.

The category of Soergel bimodules
\mathrm{SBim}_{n}
is defined as the smallest subcategory of
R-R
-bimodules containing
R
and
B_{i}
and closed under direct sums, direct summands, grading shifts and tensor products.

There exist bimodule maps
b_{i}:B_{i}\to R
and
b_{i}^{*}:R\to B_{i}
such that

b_{i}b^{*}_{i}=b^{*}_{i}b_{i}=x_{i}-x^{\prime}_{i+1}.

The
Rouquier complexes
are defined as the cones of these maps:

T_{i}=\left[B_{i}\xrightarrow{b_{i}}R\right],\ T_{i}^{-1}=\left[R\xrightarrow{b_{i}^{*}}B_{i}\right].

Theorem 2.1
.

(
[
22
]
)
The complexes
T_{i},T_{i}^{-1}
satisfy braid relations up to homotopy.

This allows one to define a complex of Soergel bimodules
T(\beta)
for any
n
-strand braid
\beta
. The HOMFLY-PT homology of
\beta
is defined by first applying the Hochschild homology to each term of this complex, and then computing the homology of the result:

\mathrm{HHH}(\beta)=H^{*}(\mathrm{HH}(T(\beta)).

Theorem 2.2
.

(
[
18
,
19
]
)
The homology
\mathrm{HHH}(\beta)
is a topological invariant of the link
L
obtained by the closure of
\beta
.

2.2.
Diagonal harmonics

We recall some results of Haiman with
[
13

as the main reference.

Consider the ring
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
as above, it has an action of
S_{n}
which permutes
x_{i}
and
y_{i}
simultaneously. Let
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]^{S_{n}}_{+}
be the ideal in
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]
generated by homogeneous symmetric polynomials of positive degree.

Definition 2.11
.

The diagonal coinvariant ring is defined by

\mathrm{DR}_{n}=\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]/\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]^{S_{n}}_{+}

Theorem 2.12

(
[
13
]
)
.

We have
\dim\mathrm{DR}_{n}=(n+1)^{n-1}
and
\dim\mathrm{DR}_{n}^{\mathrm{sgn}}=c_{n}
where

c_{n}=\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}

is the
n
th Catalan number.

Let
J
be the ideal in
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]
generated by all antisymmetric polynomials, and let
\mathfrak{m}=(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
be the maximal ideal in
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]
. The following is well known but we include a proof for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 2.13
.

We have
\mathrm{DR}_{n}^{\mathrm{sgn}}\simeq J/\mathfrak{m}J
.

Proof.

Let
L\subset\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]
be the subspace spanned by the products
f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})g(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
where
f
and
g
are homogeneous,
f
has positive degree and symmetric, and
g
is antisymmetric. Let

\mathrm{Alt}=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\sigma,\quad\mathrm{Sym}=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\sigma

be the antisymmetrization and symmetrization operators in
\mathbb{C}[S_{n}]
respectively.

Observe that any polynomial in
L
is antisymmetric, and
L\subset\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]^{S_{n}}_{+}\cap\mathfrak{m}J
. We claim that in fact
L
is the sign isotypic component of both
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]^{S_{n}}_{+}
and
\mathfrak{m}J
.
Indeed, any element in
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]^{S_{n}}_{+}
can be written as
\sum f_{i}h_{i}
where
f_{i}
are symmetric polynomials of positive degree. Then
\mathrm{Alt}(\sum f_{i}h_{i})=\sum f_{i}\mathrm{Alt}(h_{i})\in L
.
Similarly, any polynomial in
\mathfrak{m}J
can be written as

\sum g_{i}h_{i}
where
g_{i}
are antisymmetric polynomials and
h_{i}
are some homogeneous polynomials of positive degree. Then
\mathrm{Alt}(\sum g_{i}h_{i})=\sum g_{i}\mathrm{Sym}(h_{i})\in L
.

Since
J
is generated by antisymmetric polynomials, we have

J/\mathfrak{m}J=\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]^{\mathrm{sgn}}/(\mathfrak{m}J)^{\mathrm{sgn}}=\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]^{\mathrm{sgn}}/L=\mathrm{DR}_{n}^{\mathrm{sgn}}.

∎

Next, we would like to utilize the bilinear pairing on
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]
defined by

\langle f,g\rangle f(\partial_{\mathbf{x}},\partial_{\mathbf{y}})g(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})|_{\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{y}=0}.

It is well-known that this pairing is symmetric, nondegenerate,
S_{n}
-invariant and respects the bigrading.

Definition 2.14
.

The space of diagonal harmonics is defined by

\mathrm{DH}_{n}=\left\{f\in\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]\mid g(\partial_{\mathbf{x}},\partial_{\mathbf{y}})f=0\ \forall g\in\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]^{S_{n}}_{+}.\right\}

It is easy to see that
\mathrm{DH}_{n}
is the graded dual space to
\mathrm{DR}_{n}
with respect to the pairing, and similarly
\mathrm{DH}_{n}^{\mathrm{sgn}}
is graded dual to
\mathrm{DR}_{n}^{\mathrm{sgn}}
.

Next, we define a family of differential operators on
\mathrm{DR}_{n}
and
\mathrm{DH}_{n}
.

Definition 2.15
.

We define

(2.6)

E_{k}^{*}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\partial_{y_{i}}^{k},\ E_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}^{k}\partial_{x_{i}}

and

(2.7)

F_{k}^{*}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}^{k},\ F_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{k}\partial_{y_{i}}

Clearly,
E_{k}
and
E_{k}^{*}
(resp.
F_{k}
and
F_{k}^{*}
) are adjoint with respect to the pairing up to some scalar which we will not need.
Note that
E_{k}^{*}
pairwise commute and commute with
\partial_{y_{i}}
(resp.
F_{k}^{*}
commute with
\partial_{x_{i}}
) for all
i
. It is known that
E_{k}^{*}
and
F_{k}^{*}
preserve the subspace
\mathrm{DH}_{n}
while
E_{k}
and
F_{k}
preserve the ideal
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]^{S_{n}}_{+}
(compare with Lemma
2.20

\mathrm{DR}_{n}
.

Theorem 2.16
.

(
[
13

Let

Corollary 2.19

The operators

2.3.
Hamiltonian vector fields

The algebra
\mathbb{C}[x,y]
of polynomials in two variables has a Poisson bracket

\{f,g\}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\frac{\partial g}{\partial y}-\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\frac{\partial g}{\partial x}

which satisfies

\{f,g\}=-\{g,f\},\{f,gh\}=\{f,g\}h+g\{f,h\}

and the Jacobi identity

\{f,\{g,h\}\}=\{\{f,g\},h\}+\{g,\{f,h\}\}.

Given a polynomial
H\in\mathbb{C}[x,y]
, one can define the corresponding Hamiltonian vector field

v_{H}=\{H,-\}=\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}\partial_{y}-\frac{\partial H}{\partial y}\partial_{x}.

The space of all Hamiltonian vector fields is denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{2}
. The Jacobi identity implies

[v_{H},v_{H^{\prime}}]=v_{\{H,H^{\prime}\}},

so
\mathcal{H}_{2}
is closed under the commutator and forms a Lie algebra. It has a basis
v_{a,b}=v_{x^{a}y^{b}}
(
a+b\geq 1
) and the commutation relations can be written explicitly as

[v_{a,b},v_{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}}]=(ab^{\prime}-a^{\prime}b)v_{a+a^{\prime}-1,b+b^{\prime}-1}.

Here and below we assume
v_{0,0}=0
.
We will consider the following Lie subalgebra:

\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\geq 2}=\langle v_{a,b}\mid a+b\geq 2\rangle\subset\mathcal{H}_{2}.

Also note that

\langle v_{2,0},v_{1,1},v_{0,2}\rangle\simeq\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\subset\mathcal{H}_{2}.

Furthermore,

[v_{a,0},v_{0,b}]=abv_{a-1,b-1},

and the elements
v_{a,0},v_{0,b}
for
a,b\geq 1
already generate
\mathcal{H}_{2}
while
v_{a,0},v_{0,b}
for
a,b\geq 2
generate
\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\geq 2}
.
The Lie algebra
\mathcal{H}_{2}
acts on
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]
by

v_{a,b}\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(ax_{i}^{a-1}y_{i}^{b}\partial_{y_{i}}-bx_{i}^{a}y_{i}^{b-1}\partial_{x_{i}}\right).

In particular, the operators
E_{k},F_{k}
from (
2.6
),(
2.7
) appear as

E_{k}=-\frac{1}{k+1}v_{0,k+1},\ F_{k}=\frac{1}{k+1}v_{k+1,0}

and thus generate an action of
\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\geq 2}
.

Definition 2.22
.

We say that
M
is a module over
\mathbb{C}[x,y]
with a compatible action of
\mathcal{H}_{2}
if for all
m\in M,f\in\mathbb{C}[x,y]
and
v_{H}\in\mathcal{H}_{2}
we have

v_{H}(fm)=v_{H}(f)m+fv_{H}(m).

The following is well known but we include the proof for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 2.23
.

Suppose
M
is a finitely generated graded module over
\mathbb{C}[x,y]
with a compatible action of
\mathcal{H}_{2}
. Then
M=\overline{M}[x,y]
where
\overline{M}
is a finite-dimensional representation of
\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\geq 2}
.

Proof.

Note that
v_{1,0}=\partial_{y}
and
v_{0,1}=-\partial_{x}
, and
[v_{1,0},v_{0,1}]=0
. Define
\overline{M}=\mathrm{Ker}(v_{1,0})\cap\mathrm{Ker}(v_{0,1})
.

We claim that the map
\overline{M}[x,y]\to M
is injective. Indeed, suppose
\sum f_{i}(x,y)m_{i}=0
for some homogeneous
f_{i}\in\mathbb{C}[x,y]
and linearly independent
m_{i}\in\overline{M}
. Without loss of generality
f_{1}
has largest degree among
f_{i}
. Then applying
f_{1}(-v_{0,1},v_{1,0})
we get
\sum\lambda_{i}m_{i}=0
where
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
and
\lambda_{1}\neq 0
, contradiction.

Now let
m\in M
be the element of minimal degree not in
\overline{M}[x,y]
. Let
m_{x}=-v_{0,1}(m)
,
m_{y}=v_{1,0}(m)
, by our assumption these are contained in
\overline{M}[x,y]
. Furthermore,
v_{1,0}(m_{x})=-v_{0,1}(m_{y})
, which implies (since the action of
v_{1,0}
and
v_{0,1}
on
\overline{M}[x,y]
is standard) that
m_{x}=ax^{a-1}y^{b}m_{0}
and
m_{y}=bx^{a}y^{b-1}m_{0}
for some
m_{0}\in\overline{M}
. Then
m-x^{a}y^{b}m_{0}\in\mathrm{Ker}(v_{1,0})\cap\mathrm{Ker}(v_{0,1})
, contradiction.
This completes the proof of the isomorphism
\overline{M}[x,y]
.

Next, we claim that the
\mathbb{C}[x,y]
-submodule
(x,y)\overline{M}\subset\overline{M}[x,y]
is invariant under the action of
\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\geq 2}
. Indeed, for
i+j\geq 1
and
a+b\geq 2
we have

v_{a,b}(x^{i}y^{j}m)=(aj-bi)x^{a+i-1}y^{b+j-1}m+x^{i}y^{j}v_{a,b}(m).

Since
(a+i-1)+(b+j-1)\geq 2+1-2=1
, both terms are contained in
(x,y)\overline{M}
.

Therefore we can define the action of
\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\geq 2}
on
\overline{M}
by writing
\overline{M}=\overline{M}[x,y]/(x,y)\overline{M}
.
∎

Remark 2.24
.

The action of
\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\geq 2}
on
\overline{M}
does not extend to the action of
\mathcal{H}_{2}
. Indeed,
v_{1,0}
acts by zero on
\overline{M}
while
[v_{1,0},v_{a,b}]=bv_{a,b-1}
in
\mathcal{H}_{2}
.
This is also clear from the proof since
v_{1,0}
does not preserve the submodule
(x,y)\overline{M}
.

Let us prove that
\pi^{\prime}
is injective. By Lemma
3.3
we have
\pi^{\prime}(\Delta(\mathbf{y}))=\overline{\pi}_{y}(\delta)\neq 0
.

Now we identify
J/\mathfrak{m}J\simeq\mathrm{DR}_{n}^{\mathrm{sgn}}
and use Lemma
2.21
. Suppose that
f\in J/\mathfrak{m}J
is a nonzero class. Then there exists a polynomial
\psi(E_{1},\ldots,E_{n-1})
such that
\psi(E_{1},\ldots,E_{n-1})f=c\Delta(\mathbf{y})
for
c\neq 0
. Now

\psi(\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n-1})\pi^{\prime}(f)=\pi^{\prime}\left[\psi(E_{1},\ldots,E_{n-1})f\right]=\pi^{\prime}(c\Delta(\mathbf{y}))=c\overline{\pi}_{y}(\delta)\neq 0.

Therefore
\pi^{\prime}(f)\neq 0
as well, and
\pi^{\prime}
is injective. On the other hand, by Theorem
3.10
the total dimensions of
J/\mathfrak{m}J
and
\overline{\mathrm{HHH}}^{0}(\beta_{n,n+1})
agree, hence
\pi^{\prime}
is an isomorphism.
∎

Corollary 3.12
.

The maps
\pi_{y}:\mathrm{HY}^{0}(\mathrm{FT}_{n})\to\mathrm{HY}^{0}(\beta_{n,n+1})
and
\pi:\mathrm{HHH}^{0}(\mathrm{FT}_{n})\to\mathrm{HHH}^{0}(\beta_{n,n+1})
are surjective.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.11
we can write

\mathrm{HY}^{0}(\beta_{n,n+1})\simeq J/(x_{i}-x_{j},y_{i}-y_{j})J,\ \mathrm{HHH}^{0}(\beta_{n,n+1})\simeq J/(x_{i}-x_{j},\mathbf{y})J.

This proves the first statement since
\pi_{y}
can be identified with the projection

J\twoheadrightarrow J/(x_{i}-x_{j},y_{i}-y_{j})J.

For the second statement, we use Theorem
3.1
and identify the map
\pi
with the projection

\mathrm{HHH}^{0}(\mathrm{FT}_{n})=J/(\mathbf{y})J\twoheadrightarrow J/(x_{i}-x_{j},\mathbf{y})J.

∎

3.3.
Hooks and higher
a
-degrees

In this section we generalize Theorem
3.11
to higher
a
-degrees. First, we need some notations. Let
V=\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
be the
(n-1)
-dimensional reflection representation of
S_{n}
, define
V_{i}=\wedge^{i}V,\ 0\leq i\leq n-1
. It is known that
V_{i}
are irreducible representations of
S_{n}
labeled by hook-shaped partitions
(n-i,1^{i})
, and
\wedge^{n-1-i}V=\wedge^{i}V\otimes\mathrm{sgn}
.

Definition 3.13
.

If
W
is a representation of
S_{n}
, we define

W^{\mathrm{hook}}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n-1}\mathrm{Hom}_{S_{n}}(\wedge^{n-1-i}V,W).

By Schur’s lemma we get

W^{\mathrm{hook}}=\left[\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n-1}\wedge^{n-1-i}V\otimes W\right]^{S_{n}}=\left[\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n-1}\wedge^{i}V\otimes W\right]^{\mathrm{sgn}}=\left[\wedge^{\bullet}V\otimes W\right]^{\mathrm{sgn}}.

It will be useful to realize
V
explicitly as the quotient of the
n
-dimensional polynomial representation:

\langle\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n}\rangle=V\oplus\langle\theta_{1}+\ldots+\theta_{n}\rangle,

so that

(3.2)

\wedge(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\wedge^{\bullet}\langle\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n}\rangle=\wedge^{\bullet}V\otimes\wedge^{\bullet}\langle\theta_{1}+\ldots+\theta_{n}\rangle.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
\omega_{N}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{N}\theta_{i}
. Then

\left[\wedge(\boldsymbol{\theta})\otimes\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}]\right]^{S_{n}}=\wedge^{\bullet}\langle\omega_{0},\ldots,\omega_{n-1}\rangle\otimes\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}]^{S_{n}},

\left[\wedge^{\bullet}V\otimes\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}]\right]^{S_{n}}=\wedge^{\bullet}\langle\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n-1}\rangle\otimes\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}]^{S_{n}},

Proof.

We can identify
\wedge(\boldsymbol{\theta})\otimes\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}]
with the space
\Omega(\mathbb{C}^{n})
of polynomial differential forms on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
by writing
\theta_{i}=dx_{i}
. By a result of Solomon
[
23
]
we have

\Omega(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{S_{n}}\simeq\Omega(\mathbb{C}^{n}/S_{n}),

so the
S_{n}
-invariant differential forms are freely generated by power sums
p_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}^{k},\ 1\leq k\leq n
and their differentials

dp_{k}=k\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}^{k-1}dx_{i}=k\omega_{i-1}.

The last statement follows from (
3.2
) since
\omega_{0}=\theta_{1}+\ldots+\theta_{n}
.
∎

Definition 3.15
.

We define the operators
\theta_{i}^{*}
on
\wedge(\boldsymbol{\theta})
by
\theta_{i}^{*}(\theta_{j})=\delta_{ij}
and extending by Leibniz rule.

We define the operators
d^{*}_{N},d_{N}
on
\wedge(\boldsymbol{\theta})\otimes\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}]
as follows:

(3.3)

d_{N}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{i}^{*}x_{i}^{N},\ d_{N}^{*}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}^{N},\ N\geq 0.

For
N>0
we can use (
3.2
) to interpret
d_{N}
and
d_{N}^{*}
as operators on

\wedge^{\bullet}V\otimes\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}]
.

Lemma 3.16
.

a) The space

\mathrm{DH}_{n}^{\mathrm{hook}}\simeq\left[\wedge^{\bullet}V\otimes\mathrm{DH}_{n}\right]^{\mathrm{sgn}}.

is generated by
\Delta(\mathbf{x})
under the action of
\mathbb{C}[F_{1}^{*},\ldots,F_{n-1}^{*}]\otimes\wedge^{\bullet}\langle d_{1}^{*},\ldots,d_{n-1}^{*}\rangle
.

b) For any nonzero homogeneous element

f\in\mathrm{DR}_{n}^{\mathrm{hook}}\simeq\left[\wedge^{\bullet}V\otimes\mathrm{DR}_{n}\right]^{\mathrm{sgn}}

there exists a polynomial
\varphi\in\mathbb{C}[F_{1},\ldots,F_{n-1}]\otimes\wedge^{\bullet}\langle d_{1},\ldots,d_{n-1}\rangle
such that
\varphi(f)
is a nonzero multiple of
\Delta(\mathbf{x})
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.16
the element
\Delta(\mathbf{x})
generates
\wedge^{\bullet}V\otimes\mathrm{DH}_{n}
under the action of

\wedge^{\bullet}(V)\otimes\mathbb{C}[F_{1}^{*},\ldots,F_{n-1}^{*},\partial_{x_{1}},\ldots,\partial_{x_{n}}].

Similarly to Corollary
2.19
, we deduce that
\Delta(\mathbf{x})
generates

\left[\wedge^{\bullet}V\otimes\mathrm{DH}_{n}\right]^{\mathrm{sgn}}

under the action of

\left[\wedge^{\bullet}(V)\otimes\mathbb{C}[F_{1}^{*},\ldots,F_{n-1}^{*},\partial_{x_{1}},\ldots,\partial_{x_{n}}]\right]^{S_{n}}=\mathbb{C}[F_{1}^{*},\ldots,F_{n-1}^{*}]\otimes\left[\wedge^{\bullet}(V)\otimes\mathbb{C}[\partial_{x_{1}},\ldots,\partial_{x_{n}}]\right]^{S_{n}}=

\mathbb{C}[F_{1}^{*},\ldots,F_{n-1}^{*}]\otimes\mathbb{C}[\partial_{x_{1}},\ldots,\partial_{x_{n}}]^{S_{n}}\otimes\wedge^{\bullet}\langle d_{1}^{*},\ldots,d_{n-1}^{*}\rangle.

The last equation follows from Lemma
3.14
. Since any homogeneous polynomial of positive degree in
\mathbb{C}[\partial_{x_{1}},\ldots,\partial_{x_{n}}]^{S_{n}}
annihilates
\Delta(\mathbf{x})
, part (a) follows.

Part (b) is dual to (a) and the proof is similar to Lemma
2.21
.
∎

Definition 3.17
.

Let
\mathcal{J}
be the ideal in
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]\otimes\wedge(\boldsymbol{\theta})
generated by antisymmetric polynomials and let
\overline{\mathcal{J}}=\mathcal{J}/\langle\theta_{1}+\ldots+\theta_{n}\rangle\mathcal{J}\subset\wedge^{\bullet}V\otimes\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]
.

Lemma 3.18
.

We have an isomorphism

\overline{\mathcal{J}}/\mathfrak{m}\overline{\mathcal{J}}\simeq\mathrm{DR}_{n}^{\mathrm{hook}}.

Proof.

We follow the proof of Lemma
2.13
.
Let
\mathcal{L}\subset\wedge^{\bullet}V\otimes\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]
be the subspace spanned by the products
f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})g(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\boldsymbol{\theta})
where
f
and
g
are homogeneous,
f
has positive degree and symmetric, and
g
is antisymmetric.
Let us prove that

\left[\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]^{S_{n}}_{+}\right]^{\mathrm{hook}}\simeq\mathcal{L}.

Indeed, we get

\left[\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]^{S_{n}}_{+}\right]^{\mathrm{hook}}=\left[\wedge^{\bullet}V\otimes\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]^{S_{n}}_{+}\right]^{\mathrm{sgn}}.

The space
\wedge^{\bullet}V\otimes\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]^{S_{n}}_{+}
is spanned by the sums
\sum f_{i}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})h_{i}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\boldsymbol{\theta})
where
f_{i}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
are symmetric polynomials of positive degree, and
h_{i}
are arbitrary. Similarly to the proof of Lemma
2.13
, by antisymmetrizing such a sum we get an element in
\mathcal{L}
.

By
[
10
, Lemma 7.5]
the ideal
\mathcal{J}
(and hence
\overline{\mathcal{J}}
) is in fact generated by antisymmetric polynomials over
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]
. Therefore any element of
\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{J}
can be written as
\sum g_{i}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\boldsymbol{\theta})h_{i}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
where
g_{i}
are antisymmetric and
h_{i}
have positive degree, in particular
\mathcal{J}/\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{J}
is spanned by antisymmetric polynomials. Similarly to the proof of Lemma
2.13
, we get

\mathcal{L}=\left[\mathfrak{m}\overline{\mathcal{J}}\right]^{\mathrm{sgn}}
.
Also, note that

\overline{\mathcal{J}}^{\mathrm{sgn}}=\left[\wedge^{\bullet}V\otimes\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]\right]^{\mathrm{sgn}}\simeq\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]^{\mathrm{hook}}.

Finally, we get an isomorphism

\mathrm{DR}_{n}^{\mathrm{hook}}=\frac{\left[\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]\right]^{\mathrm{hook}}}{\left[\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]^{S_{n}}_{+}\right]^{\mathrm{hook}}}=\frac{\left[\wedge^{\bullet}V\otimes\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]\right]^{\mathrm{sgn}}}{\mathcal{L}}=\left[\overline{\mathcal{J}}/\mathfrak{m}\overline{\mathcal{J}}\right]^{\mathrm{sgn}}=\overline{\mathcal{J}}/\mathfrak{m}\overline{\mathcal{J}}.

∎

Lemma 3.19
.

The action of
\mathcal{F}_{k},\mathcal{E}_{k}
on
\mathrm{HY}(\mathrm{FT}_{n})

agrees with the action of
F_{k},E_{k}
on
\mathcal{J}
under the isomorphism from Theorem
3.1
.

The action of differentials
d_{N}
on
\mathrm{HY}(\mathrm{FT}_{n})
agrees with the action of operators (
3.3
) on
\mathcal{J}
.

Proof.

The proof of the first statement is similar to the proof of Theorem
3.2
.

For the second statement, observe that Rasmussen differentials
d_{N}
are given by (
3.3
) in
\mathrm{HY}(\mathbf{1}_{n})
, and the result follows from Proposition
2.10
.
∎

Now we are ready to prove a generalization of Theorem
3.11
to all
a
-degrees.

Theorem 3.20
.

The map
\overline{\pi}_{y}:\mathrm{HY}(\mathrm{FT}_{n})\to\overline{\mathrm{HHH}}(\beta_{n,n+1})
is surjective. Furthermore, we have an isomorhism

\mathrm{DR}_{n}^{\mathrm{hook}}\simeq\overline{\mathrm{HHH}}(\beta_{n,n+1})

which agrees with the action of tautological classes and differentials
d_{N}
.

Proof.

Recall that

\mathrm{HY}(\beta_{n,n+1})=\overline{\mathrm{HHH}}(\beta_{n,n+1})\otimes\mathbb{C}[x_{1}+\ldots+x_{n},y_{1}+\ldots+y_{n}]\otimes\wedge\langle\theta_{1}+\ldots+\theta_{n}\rangle.

We use the map
\pi_{y}:\mathrm{HY}(\mathrm{FT}_{n})\to\mathrm{HY}(\beta_{n,n+1})
, by Lemma
3.8
and Proposition
2.10
it commutes with the action of tautological classes and differentials.

By Lemma
3.8
the map
\overline{\pi}_{y}:\mathrm{HY}(\mathrm{FT}_{n})\to\overline{\mathrm{HHH}}(\beta_{n,n+1})
factors through
\overline{\mathcal{J}}/\mathfrak{m}\overline{\mathcal{J}}
and we get the following diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{HY}(\mathrm{FT}_{n})\simeq\mathcal{J}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\mathcal{J}}/\mathfrak{m}\overline{\mathcal{J}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\mathrm{HHH}}(\beta_{n,n+1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{DR}_{n}^{\mathrm{hook}}}
.
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\pi}_{y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{\prime}_{y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

The vertical map
\iota
is the isomorphism from Lemma
3.18
.

Let us prove that the composition
\pi^{\prime}_{y}\circ\iota
is injective. Indeed, by Theorem
3.11
we have
\pi^{\prime}_{y}(\iota(\Delta(\mathbf{x})))\neq 0
. Let
f\in\mathrm{DR}_{n}^{\mathrm{hook}}
be a nonzero homogeneous polynomial, then by Lemma
3.16
there exists a polynomial
\varphi\in\mathbb{C}[F_{1},\ldots,F_{n-1}]\otimes\wedge^{\bullet}\langle d_{1},\ldots,d_{n-1}\rangle
such that
\varphi(f)=c\Delta(\mathbf{x})
for
c\neq 0
. Now

\varphi(\pi^{\prime}_{y}(\iota(f)))=\pi^{\prime}_{y}(\iota(\varphi(f)))=c\pi^{\prime}_{y}(\iota(\Delta(\mathbf{x})))\neq 0,

hence
\pi^{\prime}_{y}(\iota(f))\neq 0
.

Finally, by the main result of
[
17
]
we have

\dim\mathrm{DR}_{n}^{\mathrm{hook}}=\dim\overline{\mathrm{HHH}}(\beta_{n,n+1}),

and
\pi^{\prime}_{y}\circ\iota
is an isomorphism.
∎

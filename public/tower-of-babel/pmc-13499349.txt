Post-chikungunya retinitis leading to optic atrophy in a toddler: A rare cause of acute vision loss☆
Authors: Mushtaq Mutashid Muhib, Prapty Amlan Ichchha, Surnar Rahman, Humayun Rashid
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractChikungunya fever is a mosquito-borne viral illness that is increasingly recognized for its atypical and multi-organ involvement. Although self-limiting, it can affect multiple organ systems, including the nervous and ocular systems. Ocular manifestations, particularly retinitis with retinal detachment, are vision-threatening. Recent outbreaks in endemic regions have demonstrated an expanding clinical spectrum, with increasing reports of severe and atypical complications. We report a pediatric case presented with chikungunya infection and concurrent UTI with septicemia. Visual impairment was noted, progressing to bilateral vision loss. Ophthalmic findings showed posterior segment involvement with extensive retinitis with retinal detachment, consistent with post-chikungunya viral retinopathy. Despite treatment with antivirals and corticosteroids, recovery was minimal, resulting in irreversible retinal damage. This case highlights the importance of prompt clinical evaluation and early ophthalmic referral to recognize chikungunya-associated retinitis. Severe bilateral retinitis with early profound visual impairment in pediatric chikungunya is uncommon, emphasizing the need for increased clinical awareness.Keywords: Chikungunya, Viral fever, Blindness, Retinitis, Optic nerve, Optic atrophy

Case illustrated

A previously healthy 2-year−6-month-old boy from Gazipur, Bangladesh, presented with two days of high fever (up to 103 °F), anorexia, vomiting, and severe myalgia/arthralgia. Serology confirmed chikungunya IgM; dengue serology was negative. Investigations showed leukocytosis, neutrophilia, thrombocytopenia, and pyuria, consistent with concurrent chikungunya fever, urinary tract infection, and septicemia. Fever resolved with intravenous paracetamol and meropenem.

On the second day of illness, his parents noticed sudden, profound bilateral visual impairment; he could no longer locate or grab objects. Ophthalmic examination on Day 2 showed no light perception in the right eye and a quiet anterior segment in both eyes, with fundoscopy revealing extensive bilateral retinitis and retinal detachment. He was started on oral valacyclovir, pulsed intravenous methylprednisolone, and tapering oral prednisolone. Vision had not improved by discharge.

At one-month follow-up, the external eye appeared unremarkable (Fig. 1), but fundoscopy showed chorioretinal scarring, pigmentary changes, vascular attenuation, and optic disc fibrosis (Fig. 2), consistent with irreversible post-chikungunya retinopathy. He was diagnosed with post-chikungunya viral retinopathy and continued on antiviral therapy, oral steroids, and light stimulation therapy, with long-term visual rehabilitation planned.

Discussion

Ocular chikungunya usually presents early as conjunctivitis or anterior uveitis from direct viral invasion, while posterior segment disease such as retinitis and optic neuritis tends to appear later and is thought to be immune-mediated [1]. This child's course did not fit either pattern cleanly: profound bilateral vision loss developed within 48 h of fever onset and progressed to retinal detachment, rather than the milder, more common self-limited uveitis [2]. Typhoid-related retinitis and septicemia-related endogenous endophthalmitis were considered and judged unlikely given the timing and negative supporting tests.

Systemic corticosteroids, started early, offer the best chance of visual recovery, but no treatment currently reverses established chorioretinal scarring. Reports of retinitis this severe, this early, in a child this young remain rare [3], [4]. Clinicians managing pediatric chikungunya should keep a low threshold for ophthalmic referral whenever a visual complaint arises, given how quickly and permanently vision can be lost.

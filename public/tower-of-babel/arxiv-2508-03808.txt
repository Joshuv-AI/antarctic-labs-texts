A Dwyer–Rezk classification for polynomial functors in Weiss calculus
David Barnes, Magdalena Kędziorek, Niall Taggart

Abstract
. In Goodwillie calculus, unpublished work of Dwyer and Rezk provides a classification of reduced filtered colimit preserving d d -excisive functors from pointed spaces to spectra as spectrum-valued functors on the category of finite sets of cardinality at most d d and epimorphisms. We prove through different methods the analogous result in Weiss calculus: d d -polynomial functors are equivalent to spectrum-valued functors on the category of finite-dimensional inner product spaces of dimension at most d d and orthogonal epimorphisms. Via similar methods we obtain a new proof of the classification of homogeneous functors.



1.
Introduction

Weiss calculus is a homotopy-theoretic tool designed to study geometric problems arising from differential topology with applications throughout geometry and homotopy theory. For
\mathbb{k}
any of the skew fields
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{C}
or
\mathbb{H}
, the calculus approximates a spectrum-valued functor
F:\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}}\to{{\mathsf{Sp}}}
from the category of finite-dimensional inner product spaces over
\mathbb{k}
and linear isometric embeddings by a tower

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{P_{d}F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{P_{1}F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{P_{0}F}}

of functors in which the functor
P_{d}F
is the best approximation to
F
by a
d
-polynomial functor.

It is reasonable to think of Weiss calculus as an adaptation of Goodwillie calculus where the role of the finite set of
d
elements is replaced by the Euclidean space
\mathbb{k}^{d}
. This ‘geometrization’ of cubical homotopy theory induces subtle complexities within Weiss calculus, and one expects the study of polynomial functors in Weiss calculus to mirror the study of excisive functors from pointed spaces to spectra rather than excisive endofunctors of spectra.

In Goodwillie calculus, there are many known models for the
\infty
-category of
d
-excisive functors between some combination of the categories of (pointed) spaces and spectra. For endofunctors of spectra, Arone–Ching
[
AC15
]
provided a model in terms of truncated coalgebras over a comonad, and Glasman
[
Gla16
]
gives a classification in terms of (spectral) Mackey functors on the category
{\mathsf{Epi}}_{\leq d}
of finite sets of cardinality at most
d
and epimorphisms.
The difference between these classifications lies in the ‘classifying objects’, i.e., the Arone–Ching classification is in terms of the derivative functors while the Glasman classification is in terms of the cross-effects.

For functors from pointed spaces to spectra, Arone–Ching
[
AC15
,
AC16
]
provide a model again in terms of truncated coalgebras over a comonad using the derivative spectra. However, a simpler model can be given using the cross-effect functors: unpublished work of Dwyer–Rezk describes
d
-excisive functors as spectrum-valued functors on the category
{\mathsf{Epi}}_{\leq d}
.
This contrasts with the Mackey functor description of the spectra to spectra case where one has both ‘restrictions’ and ‘transfers’, in the pointed spaces to spectra case one just has ‘restrictions’.
There are several different approaches to models in the style of Dwyer and Rezk, see e.g., work of Arone–Ching
[
AC16
, Proposition 3.15, Theorem 3.82]
, Lurie
[
Lur17
, Theorem 6.1.5.1]
, and Chorny–White
[
CW24
, Corollary 5.1]
, respectively.

In Weiss calculus, work of Malin and the third author
[
MT24
]
used the derivatives as the classifying objects to provide a model for spectrum-valued
d
-polynomial functors in terms of truncated coalgebras over a comonad. In this article we prove the following classification analogous to that of Dwyer–Rezk for
d
-excisive functors.

Theorem A
.

There is an equivalence of
\infty
-categories

\mathsf{Poly}^{\leq d}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})\simeq{\mathsf{Fun}}({\mathsf{OEpi}}_{\leq d},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})

between the
\infty
-category of
d
-polynomial functors and the
\infty
-category of spectrum-valued functors on the category
{\mathsf{OEpi}}_{\leq d}
of finite-dimensional inner product spaces of dimension at most
d
and orthogonal epimorphisms, see
Definition

3.4
.

Given the analogy between Weiss calculus and Goodwillie calculus our classification uses the induction functors of Weiss,
[
Wei95
, Proposition 2.1]
, which we call
Weiss cross-effects
. These are geometrically oriented versions of the cross-effects from Goodwillie calculus coming from the ‘geometrization’ of replacing the set of
d
elements with the Euclidean space
\mathbb{k}^{d}
.
In a somewhat twisted turn of fate, the inherent geometric aspects of Weiss calculus which are famous for making it a challenging pursuit end up greatly simplifying many of our arguments while proving
A
.

Barwick, Dotto, Glasman, Nardin and Shah
[
BDG+16
, Example 10]
conjecture that the stable homotopy theory parametrized by the
\infty
-category
{\mathsf{OEpi}}_{\leq d}
“controls” Weiss calculus
and in particular,
d
-polynomial functors are equivalent to the
\infty
-category of spectral Mackey functors on
{\mathsf{OEpi}}_{\leq d}
.
A
disproves their conjecture:
d
-polynomial functors are not spectral Mackey functors on
{\mathsf{OEpi}}_{\leq d}
, but rather on the free semiadditive
\infty
-category generated by
{\mathsf{OEpi}}_{\leq d}
, or said differently,
d
-polynomial functors have no interesting transfers.

The category
{\mathsf{OEpi}}_{\leq d}
and it’s opposite
\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k},\leq d}
(see
Definition

3.4
) are not equivalent as categories, but we provide a Morita equivalence between them by giving another model for
d
-polynomial functors in terms of left Kan extensions along the inclusion
\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k},\leq d}\hookrightarrow\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}}
.

Theorem B
.

There is an adjoint equivalence

\textstyle{{\mathsf{L}}_{d}:{\mathsf{Fun}}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k},\leq d},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\mathsf{Poly}^{\leq d}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}}):\res_{\leq d}}

between the
\infty
-category of spectrum-valued functors on the
\infty
-category of finite-dimensional inner product spaces of dimension at most
d
and the
\infty
-category of
d
-polynomial functors.

This model is the Weiss calculus version of the well known Morita equivalence between the
\infty
-category of finite pointed sets of cardinality at most
d
and pointed maps, and the
\infty
-category of finite sets of cardinality at most
d
and epimorphisms, see e.g.,
[
Aro25
, Lemma 8.2, Proposition 8.3]
. The Goodwillie version of this story has been known for some time, for instance, a proof that
d
-excisive functors are determined by their restriction to finite pointed sets of cardinality at most
d
was given by Arone and Ching
[
AC16
, §3]
and Lurie
[
Lur17
, Corollary 6.1.5.2]
. The Morita equivalence between these two categories of finite sets dates back to Pirashvili
[
Pir00
]
and various other versions have been proved e.g., by Helmstutler
[
Hel14
]
and Walde
[
Wal22
]
.

The models of
A
and
B
have largely mutually exclusive roles to play. The model of
A
provides a model for polynomial functors in terms of truncated
{\mathsf{OEpi}}
-comodules, Koszul dual to the model of the third author and Malin
[
MT24
, Theorem D]
in terms of truncated ’divided power’
K({\mathsf{OEpi}})
-modules. On the other hand, the model of
B
is expected to be useful for answering convergence questions.

The difference between consecutive polynomial approximations

D_{d}F=\mathsf{fib}(P_{d}F\longrightarrow P_{d-1}F),

is a
d
-homogeneous functor, analogous to monomials of degree
d
from differential calculus. A celebrated theorem of Weiss
[
Wei95
, Theorem 7.3]
provides an identification of the
\infty
-category of
d
-homogeneous functors as the
\infty
-category of Borel
\mathsf{Aut}(d)
-spectra, i.e., as the
\infty
-category of spectra with an action of the automorphism group
\mathsf{Aut}(d)=\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}}(\mathbb{k}^{d},\mathbb{k}^{d})
. The proof technique for
A
may be applied to the
\infty
-category of homogeneous functors to provide a new proof of the classification of
d
-homogeneous spectrum-valued functors.

Theorem C
.

There is an equivalence

\mathsf{Homog}^{d}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})\simeq{{\mathsf{Sp}}}^{\mathsf{Aut}(d)}

between the
\infty
-category of
d
-homogeneous functors and the
\infty
-category of Borel
\mathsf{Aut}(d)
-spectra, realised by sending a Borel
\mathsf{Aut}(d)
-spectrum
\Theta
to the functor
\mathbb{k}^{n}\mapsto(S^{\mathbb{k}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{k}^{n}}\wedge\Theta)_{h\mathsf{Aut}(d)}
.

The methods employed here should provide analogous classification results for polynomial and homogeneous functors in other functor calculi. In particular for Goodwillie calculus of functors from pointed spaces to spectra and for the
{\mathsf{FI}}
-calculus of Arro
[
Arr23
]
. Moreover it is routine to extend to functors taking values in any presentably stable symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category in place of spectra.

2.
Polynomial functors

We assume some familiarity with Weiss calculus, but recap many of the key features throughout. For comprehensive accounts see
[
Wei95
,
Tag22
,
CT24
]
, depending on the reader’s choice of skew field
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{C}
or
\mathbb{H}
, respectively. Denote by
\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}}
the
\infty
-category of finite-dimensional inner product spaces over
\mathbb{k}
and linear isometric embeddings. A concrete model is given by the topological category with space of morphisms
{\mathsf{Vect}}_{\mathbb{k}}(\mathbb{k}^{m},\mathbb{k}^{n})
the Stiefel manifold of
m
-frames in
\mathbb{k}^{n}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A functor
F:\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}}\to{{\mathsf{Sp}}}
is
d
-polynomial
if the canonical map

F(V)\longrightarrow\lim_{0\neq U\subseteq\mathbb{k}^{d+1}}F(V\oplus U)

is an equivalence for each
V\in\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}}
. We define the
\infty
-category
\mathsf{Poly}^{\leq d}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})
of
d
-polynomial functors to be the full sub-
\infty
-category of
{\mathsf{Fun}}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})
spanned by the
d
-polynomial functors.

Given a functor
F:\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}}\to{{\mathsf{Sp}}}
, there is a fibre sequence

(1)

F^{(d+1)}(V)\longrightarrow F(V)\longrightarrow\lim_{0\neq U\subseteq\mathbb{k}^{d+1}}F(V\oplus U)

in which
F^{(d+1)}
is the
(d+1)
-st Weiss cross-effect, see
[
Wei95
, Proposition 5.3 and §2]
. In the stable setting, a functor is
d
-polynomial if and only if the
(d+1)
-st Weiss cross-effect vanishes. We now collect several lemmas
about how cross-effects and polynomial degree interact that will be useful for us later.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
F:\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}}\to{{\mathsf{Sp}}}
is a
d
-polynomial functor for which
F^{(d)}(\mathbb{k}^{0})\simeq 0
, then
F
is
(d-1)
-polynomial.

Proof.

Weiss cross-effects are related by fibre sequences of the form

(2)

F^{(n)}(\mathbb{k}^{m})\longrightarrow F^{(n-1)}(\mathbb{k}^{m})\longrightarrow\Omega^{\mathbb{k}^{n-1}}F^{(n-1)}(\mathbb{k}^{m+1})

see e.g.,
[
Wei95
, Proposition 2.2]
. Since
F
is
d
-polynomial, the
(d+1)
-st Weiss cross-effect vanishes and so by the
n=d+1
case of the fibre sequence (
2
) we have that the map

F^{(d)}(\mathbb{k}^{m})\longrightarrow\Omega^{\mathbb{k}^{d}}F^{(d)}(\mathbb{k}^{m+1})

is an equivalence for each
\mathbb{k}^{m}
. It follows from the assumption that
F^{(d)}(\mathbb{k}^{0})\simeq 0
and stability that the
d
-th Weiss cross-effect of
F
vanishes, and hence by the fibre sequence (
1
) that
F
is
(d-1)
-polynomial.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

If
F:\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}}\to{{\mathsf{Sp}}}
is a
d
-polynomial functor for which
F^{(n)}(\mathbb{k}^{0})\simeq 0
for all
1\leq n\leq d
, then
F
is constant.

Proof.

Applying
2.2

d
-many times we see that
F
is
0
-polynomial. From (
1
) we see that a
0
-polynomial functor is constant.
∎

Weiss
[
Wei95
, Theorem 6.3]
[
Wei98
]
proved (in different language) that the inclusion

{\mathsf{inc}}_{d}:\mathsf{Poly}^{\leq d}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})\hookrightarrow{\mathsf{Fun}}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})

admits a left exact left adjoint, hence there is an adjunction

\textstyle{P_{d}:{\mathsf{Fun}}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\mathsf{Poly}^{\leq d}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}}):{\mathsf{inc}}_{d}}

realising the
\infty
-category of
d
-polynomial functors as a left exact localization of the
\infty
-category of all functors. This localization is compatible (in the sense of
[
Lur17
, Definition 2.2.1.6, Example 2.2.1.7]
) with the Day convolution monoidal structure on
{\mathsf{Fun}}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})
. The proof is a spectral lift of
[
Hen23
, Theorem 4.2.0.1]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

The
d
-polynomial approximation
P_{d}:{\mathsf{Fun}}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})\to\mathsf{Poly}^{\leq d}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})
is a smashing localization on
{\mathsf{Fun}}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})
which is compatible with the Day convolution monoidal structure. In particular, the
\infty
-category of
d
-polynomial functors is a presentably symmetric monoidal stable
\infty
-category with monoidal product given by localized Day convolution.

Proof.

The only part of the proof not contained in
[
Hen23
, Theorem 4.2.0.1]
is the observation that
P_{d}
is smashing. This follows by the construction
[
Wei95
, Theorem 6.3]
of
P_{d}
as a filtered colimit of compact limits, i.e.,
P_{d}
preserves filtered colimits and finite limits, and hence all colimits.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

It is also the case that the
\infty
-category of
d
-polynomial functors is symmetric monoidal with respect to the localized levelwise monoidal product. This can be seen by leveraging results of Hahn and Yuan
[
HY19
]
on the monoidality of the Weiss tower.

The equivalence of
A
uses the Schwede–Shipley
[
SS03
]
identification of stable
\infty
-categories as
\infty
-categories of modules. To apply this to our setting we first need to observe that the
\infty
-category of polynomial functors is compactly generated, and to specify a set of compact generators.

Lemma 2.6
.

The
\infty
-category
\mathsf{Poly}^{\leq d}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})
of
d
-polynomial functors is compactly generated. In particular, the set

G_{d}=\{\mathbb{S}^{n(-)}:\mathbb{k}^{m}\longmapsto\Sigma^{\infty}S^{\mathbb{k}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{k}^{m}}\mid 0\leq n\leq d\}

is a set of compact generators for
\mathsf{Poly}^{\leq d}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})
.

Proof.

We begin by showing that the objects of
G_{d}
are compact objects of
\mathsf{Poly}^{\leq d}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})
. The cofibre sequences of
[
Wei95
, Proposition 1.1, Proposition 1.2]
allow one to inductively construct
\mathbb{S}^{n(-)}
from representable functors and hence
\mathbb{S}^{n(-)}
is compact in
{\mathsf{Fun}}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})
. By
2.4
, it follows that
P_{d}\mathbb{S}^{n(-)}
is compact in
\mathsf{Poly}^{\leq d}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})
. The functor
\mathbb{S}^{n(-)}
is
n
-polynomial (in fact,
n
-homogeneous by
[
Wei95
, Example 5.7]
) and so
\mathbb{S}^{n(-)}\simeq P_{d}\mathbb{S}^{n(-)}
, and hence
\mathbb{S}^{n(-)}
is a compact object of
\mathsf{Poly}^{\leq d}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})
.

By
[
SS03
, Lemma 2.2.1]
to show that a set of compact objects generates, it suffices to show that maps out of this set of compact objects detect trivial objects. In our case, we must show that if

\Hom_{\mathsf{Poly}^{\leq d}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})}(\mathbb{S}^{n(-)},F)\simeq 0

for all
0\leq n\leq d
, then
F\simeq 0
. Since by
[
Wei95
, §2]

\Hom_{\mathsf{Poly}^{\leq d}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})}(\mathbb{S}^{n(-)},F)=\Hom_{{\mathsf{Fun}}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})}(\mathbb{S}^{n(-)},F)=F^{(n)}(\mathbb{k}^{0})

this is equivalent to
F^{(n)}(\mathbb{k}^{0})\simeq 0
for
0\leq n\leq d
implying that
F\simeq 0
. By
2.3
we have that if
F^{(n)}(\mathbb{k}^{0})\simeq 0
for
1\leq n\leq d
then
F
is a constant functor. The additional assumption
F^{(0)}(\mathbb{k}^{0})\simeq 0
completes the proof since
F(\mathbb{k}^{0})=F^{(0)}(\mathbb{k}^{0})
, and so
F
is the constant functor at zero.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

The compact generators of
2.6
are not (weakly) dualizable with respect to neither the Day convolution or pointwise monoidal structures. This is an artifact of the unit for both (localized) monoidal structures being the constant functor at the sphere spectrum. In particular, the
\infty
-category of
d
-polynomial functors is not rigidly-compactly generated.

We will see in
3.5

that the full sub-
\infty
-category spanned by
\{\mathbb{S}^{n(-)}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is equivalent to
{\mathsf{OEpi}}\simeq\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}}^{\mathsf{op}}
. From this we make a conjecture:

Conjecture 2.8
.

The full sub-
\infty
-category of the
\infty
-category
{\mathsf{Fun}}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})
compactly generated by
the set
\{\mathbb{S}^{n(-)}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}

is the
\infty
-category of polynomial functors (i.e., those functors which are
d
-polynomial for some
d
). Moreover, the
\infty
-category of polynomial functors is a strict sub-
\infty
-category of
{\mathsf{Fun}}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})
.

3.
The classification of polynomial functors

The classification of polynomial functors as in
A
will require us to invoke technology in the form of spectrally enriched
\infty
-categories, e.g., the first step in the classification is an
\infty
-categorical enhancement of the classical Schwede–Shipley theorem
[
SS03
]
. We choose to work with Hinich’s
[
Hin20
,
Hin21
]
model for enriched
\infty
-categories. We recall the key details here but a comprehensive account may be found in work of Reutter and Zetto
[
RZ25
]
, although all known models are equivalent
[
Hei23
]
.

We will only define the
\infty
-category of spectrally enriched
\infty
-categories with a specified space of objects, as for us, the space of objects will always be equivalent to a finite subset of the natural numbers. Let
X
be a space. The category of
X
-quivers
{\mathsf{Fun}}(X^{\mathsf{op}}\times X,{{\mathsf{Sp}}})
has a monoidal structure given by

(A\otimes B)(x,z)=\underset{y\in X}{\colim}~A(x,y)\otimes B(y,z)

see e.g.,
[
RZ25
, Observation 2.32]
. Similarly to how enriched (1-)categories may be defined as algebras in the (1-)category of quivers, we have the following definition.

Definition 3.1

(
[
Hin20
, Definition 3.1.1]
)
.

Let
X
be a space. The
\infty
-category of
spectrally enriched
\infty
-categories with space of objects
X
is the
\infty
-category of (associative) algebra objects in the category
{\mathsf{Fun}}(X^{\mathsf{op}}\times X,{{\mathsf{Sp}}})
of
X
-quivers.

Unpacking this definition amounts to the observation that if
X={\mathsf{ob}}(\mathcal{C})
for some spectrally enriched
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
, the hom object

\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(-,-):{\mathsf{ob}}(\mathcal{C})^{\mathsf{op}}\times{\mathsf{ob}}(\mathcal{C})\longrightarrow{{\mathsf{Sp}}},\quad(C,C^{\prime})\longmapsto\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(C,C^{\prime})

is an
X
-quiver and the coherences in composition give us the (associative) algebra structure.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a spectrally enriched
\infty
-category with space of objects
X
. There is an equivalence

{\mathsf{Fun}}(X^{\mathsf{op}}\times X,{{\mathsf{Sp}}})\simeq{\mathsf{End}}_{{\mathsf{Sp}}}^{L}({\mathsf{Fun}}(X^{\mathsf{op}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}}))={\mathsf{Fun}}_{{\mathsf{Sp}}}^{L}({\mathsf{Fun}}(X^{\mathsf{op}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}}),{\mathsf{Fun}}(X^{\mathsf{op}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}}))

between the
\infty
-category of
X
-quivers and the
\infty
-category of
{{\mathsf{Sp}}}
-linear colimit preserving endofunctors of
{\mathsf{Fun}}(X^{\mathsf{op}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})
, see e.g.,
[
RZ25
, Definition 2.30]
. Under this equivalence, the monoidal structure above corresponds to composition of functors
[
RZ25
, Remark 2.31]
. It follows that the category of
X
-quivers acts universally from the left on the category

{\mathsf{Fun}}(X^{\mathsf{op}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})\simeq{\mathsf{Fun}}(X,{{\mathsf{Sp}}}).

As such we may consider the category of left
\mathcal{C}
-modules in
{\mathsf{Fun}}(X,{{\mathsf{Sp}}})
. This leads to a definition of the
\infty
-category of spectrally enriched presheaves, see
[
RZ25
, Observation 2.39]
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a spectrally enriched
\infty
-category with space of objects
X
. The
\infty
-category of spectrally enriched presheaves

on

is the
\infty
-category of left
\mathcal{C}
-modules in
{\mathsf{Fun}}(X,{{\mathsf{Sp}}})
.

We can now begin proving
A
using the
\infty
-categorical enhancement
[
ABS22
, Theorem 4.1]
of the Schwede–Shipley theorem
[
SS03
, Theorem 3.3.3]
. For this, let
\mathcal{G}_{d}
be the full spectrally enriched sub-
\infty
-category of
{\mathsf{Fun}}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})
spanned by the set
G_{d}
of compact generators for
\mathsf{Poly}^{\leq d}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})
from
2.6
.

Lemma 3.3
.

There is an equivalence

\mathsf{Poly}^{\leq d}({\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})\xrightarrow{\ \simeq\ }{\mathsf{PSh}}_{{\mathsf{Sp}}}(\mathcal{G}_{d})

between the
\infty
-category of
d
-polynomial functors and the
\infty
-category of spectral presheaves on

.

Proof.

Combine
2.6
with the
\infty
-categorical Schwede–Shipley Theorem of
[

, Theorem 4.1]
.
∎

The suspension spectrum functor
\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}:\mathcal{S}\to{{\mathsf{Sp}}}
induces a left adjoint functor

\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}:{\mathsf{Cat}}_{\infty}\longrightarrow{\mathsf{Cat}}_{\infty}^{{\mathsf{Sp}}}

relating
\infty
-categories and spectrally enriched

-categories, see e.g.,
[
RZ25
, Construction 2.36]
, such that the space of objects is preserved and on enriched mapping objects

\Hom_{\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)\simeq\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y).

We call the image
\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathcal{C}
, the free spectrally enriched
\infty
-category generated by
\mathcal{C}
. In
3.3
we reduced the proof of

to proving that the spectral
\infty
-category
\mathcal{G}_{d}
is equivalent to the free spectrally enriched
\infty
-category generated by

, defined below.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}}
denote the
\infty
-category of finite-dimensional inner product spaces and linear isometric embeddings. The space of morphisms
\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}}(\mathbb{k}^{m},\mathbb{k}^{n})
is the Stiefel manifold of
m
-frames in
\mathbb{k}^{n}
. We define the
\infty
-category
{\mathsf{OEpi}}
of finite-dimensional inner product spaces and orthogonal epimorphisms to be
\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}}^{\mathsf{op}}
. We will denote by
\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k},\leq d}
and

Lemma 3.5
.

There is an equivalence

\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}{\mathsf{OEpi}}_{\leq d}\longrightarrow(\mathcal{G}_{d})^{\mathsf{op}}

of spectrally enriched
\infty

Proof.

The functor is easier to describe on opposite categories. On opposite categories it is given by

\mathbb{S}^{\ast(-)}:\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k},{\leq d}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{d},\quad\mathbb{k}^{n}\longmapsto\mathbb{S}^{n(-)}

This assignment is compatible with the

. By

(\mathbb{S}^{n(-)})^{(m)}(\mathbb{k}^{0})\simeq(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathsf{Aut}(n)\wedge S^{\mathbb{k}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{k}^{0}})_{h\mathsf{Aut}(n-m)}\simeq\mathsf{Aut}(n)/\mathsf{Aut}(n-m)

and fully faithfulness follows from the homeomorphism
\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}}(\mathbb{k}^{m},\mathbb{k}^{n})\cong\mathsf{Aut}(n)/\mathsf{Aut}(n-m)
and tracing through the computation of
[

, Example 5.7]

∎

(

4.
Homogeneous functors

A functor

is
d
-homogeneous if it is
d
-polynomial and
P_{d-1}F\simeq 0
. Define the
\infty
-category
\mathsf{Homog}^{d}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})
of
d
-homogeneous functors to be the full sub-
\infty
-category of
\mathsf{Poly}^{\leq d}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})
spanned by the
d
-homogeneous functors. The primary example of a
d
-homogeneous functor is the
d
-th layer

D_{d}F=\mathsf{fib}(P_{d}F\longrightarrow P_{d-1}F)

of the Weiss tower. In this section we provide a new proof of the classification of
d
-homogeneous functors as Borel
\mathsf{Aut}(d)
-spectra.

Lemma 4.1
.

The
\infty
-category
\mathsf{Homog}^{d}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})
of
d
-homogeneous functors is compactly generated by the functor
\mathbb{S}^{d(-)}
.

Proof.

We begin by showing that the functor
\mathbb{S}^{d(-)}
is compact in
\mathsf{Homog}^{d}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})
. For this, observe that colimits (and in particular, coproducts) in the
\infty
-category of
d
-homogeneous functors are computed by applying the coreflection functor
D_{d}
to the colimit in the
\infty
-category of
d
-polynomial functors. Since the coreflection functor
D_{d}
is right adjoint to the inclusion, there is an equivalence

\Hom_{\mathsf{Homog}^{d}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})}\left(\mathbb{S}^{d(-)},D_{d}\coprod_{i\in I}F_{i}\right)\simeq\Hom_{\mathsf{Poly}^{\leq d}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})}\left(\mathbb{S}^{d(-)},\coprod_{i\in I}F_{i}\right).

By
2.6
the functor
\mathbb{S}^{d(-)}
is compact in the
\infty
-category of
d
-polynomial functors and so there is a further equivalence,

\Hom_{\mathsf{Poly}^{\leq d}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})}\left(\mathbb{S}^{d(-)},\coprod_{i\in I}F_{i}\right)\simeq\prod_{i\in I}\Hom_{\mathsf{Poly}^{\leq d}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})}(\mathbb{S}^{d(-)},F_{i}),

which completes the proof that
\mathbb{S}^{d(-)}
is compact in
\mathsf{Homog}^{d}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})
upon the observation that for
d
-homogeneous functors
F
and
G
,

\Hom_{\mathsf{Homog}^{d}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})}(F,G)\simeq\Hom_{\mathsf{Poly}^{\leq d}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})}(F,G)

by the fact that the
\infty
-category of
d
-homogeneous functors is a full subcategory of the
\infty
-category of
d
-polynomial functors.

To see that
\mathbb{S}^{d(-)}
generates the
\infty
-category of
d
-homogeneous functors it suffices (see the proof of
2.6
) to show that if

\Hom_{\mathsf{Homog}^{d}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})}(\mathbb{S}^{d(-)},F)\simeq 0,

then

. Since
\mathsf{Homog}^{d}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})
is a full subcategory of
{\mathsf{Fun}}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{k}},{{\mathsf{Sp}}})
, this is equivalent to the statement that if

, then

for

a

-homogeneous functor. By

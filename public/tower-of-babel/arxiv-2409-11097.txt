Basic monodromy operator for quantum superalgebra
A. V. Razumov

Abstract
. We derive the explicit form of the basic monodromy operator for the quantum loop superalgebra U q ​ ( ℒ ⁡ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 | 1 ) ) \mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})) . 1.
Introduction

Quantum algebras, also known as quantum groups, play a significant role in the study of quantum integrable systems. The general notion of a quantum algebra
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
was introduced by Drinfeld and Jimbo
[
1
,
2
]
for the case when
\mathfrak{g}
is a Kac–Moody algebra with a symmetrizable generalized Cartan matrix. In the quantum algebraic approach, all the objects that describe the model and are related to its integrability originate from the universal
R
-matrix of the corresponding quantum algebra. We refer to these objects as integrability objects.

The universal
R
-matrix is an element of the tensor product of two copies of the quantum algebra under consideration. The integrability objects are constructed by selecting representations of the quantum algebra for the factors of that tensor product. When the same representation of the quantum algebra is chosen for both factors, we obtain an
R
-matrix. By using different representations for the factors, a more general integrable object, known as a monodromy operator, is produced. An even broader class of integrable objects can be constructed by applying a homomorphism from the quantum algebra to a certain associative algebra for one of the tensor product factors. For examples, see the papers
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
.

Consider, for example, the case of integrable systems associated with the quantum algebra
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{M}))
, where
\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{M})
denotes the loop algebra of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{M}
. As discovered by Jimbo
[
12
]
, there exists an algebra homomorphism
\epsilon
from the quantum algebra
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{M}))
to the quantum algebra
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{M})
. Using this homomorphism, for any given representation
\pi
of the quantum algebra
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{M})
, we define the representation
\pi\circ\epsilon
of the quantum algebra
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{M}))
. This type of representation is known as evaluation representations.

A monodromy operator, known as the basic monodromy operator, is defined by applying the homomorphism
\epsilon
to the first factor of the universal
R
-matrix and a fixed representation of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{M}))
to the second. This construction allows us to investigate the integrable systems related to all evaluation representations simultaneously.

The explicit derivations of the basic monodromy operators for the quantum algebras
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}))
and
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3}))
are presented in the papers
[
10
]
and
[
13
]
. Two significant results emerge from these derivations: the explicit forms of certain central elements of the quantum algebras
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2})
and
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{3})
, and simple expressions for the generating functions of the images of the root vectors of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}))
and
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3}))
under the Jimbo homomorphism. These generating functions are crucial for deriving the
\ell
-weights of the evaluation representations
[
14
,
15
]
. Along with the
\ell
-weights of the corresponding
q
-oscillator representations
[
16
,
17
]
, they are used to prove the functional relations for the systems under consideration
[
18
,
19
]
.

By appropriately generalizing the defining relations of quantum algebras, one can obtain quantum superalgebras associated with Lie superalgebras
[
20
,
21
]
. Numerous papers have been devoted to investigating the corresponding integrable systems (see, for example, the paper
[
22
]
and the references therein). However, a quantum algebraic derivation of the explicit form of the basic monodromy operator for such systems has yet to be provided. It is worth noting that in the paper
[
23
]
, the basic monodromy operator for the quantum superalgebra
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{M|N}))
was constructed via the Baxterization procedure from the monodromy operators of the quantum algebra
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N})
. In this paper, we present a direct quantum algebraic derivation of the basic monodromy operator for the quantum superalgebra
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))
. As with
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3}))
, we also obtain two additional important results: the explicit forms of certain central elements and simple expressions for the images of the generating functions of the root vectors of the quantum superalgebra
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))
under the Jimbo homomorphism.

Definition of quantum superalgebra
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})

Let
\hbar
be an indeterminate and
q=\exp(\hbar)
. We define the quantum superalgebra
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})
as a unital associative
\mathbb{C}[[\hbar]]
-superalgebra generated by the elements
2
2

2

We use bold letters to denote the generators of quantum algebras and objects related to them.

\bm{K}_{i},\quad i=1,2,3,\qquad\bm{E}_{i},\quad\bm{F}_{i},\quad i=1,2,

which satisfy the corresponding defining relations. The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})
is defined on the generators as follows:

[\bm{K}_{1}]=[\bm{K}_{2}]=[\bm{K}_{3}]=\overline{0},\qquad[\bm{E}_{1}]=[\bm{F}_{1}]={\overline{0}},\qquad[\bm{E}_{2}]=[\bm{F}_{2}]={\overline{1}}.

Before presenting the defining relations of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})
, we introduce the notion of the
q
-supercommutator. The abelian group

Q=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{2}\mathbb{Z}\,\alpha_{i}.

is called the root lattice of
\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1}
. We denote by
Q_{+}
and
Q_{-}
the sublattices

Q_{+}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{2}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\,\alpha_{i},\qquad Q_{-}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{2}\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}\,\alpha_{i}.

Assuming that

\bm{K}_{i}\in\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})_{0},\qquad\bm{E}_{i}\in\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})_{\alpha_{i}},\qquad\bm{F}_{i}\in\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})_{-\alpha_{i}},

we endow
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})
with a
Q
-grading.

For any
a\in\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})_{\alpha}
and
b\in\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})_{\beta}
, we define their
q
-supercommutator by the equation

\displaystyle[\![a,\,b]\!]=ab-(-1)^{[a][b]}q^{-(\alpha|\beta)}ba=ab-(-1)^{[\alpha][\beta]}q^{-(\alpha|\beta)}ba

if
\alpha,\beta\in Q_{+}
, by the equation

\displaystyle[\![a,\,b]\!]=ab-(-1)^{[a][b]}q^{(\alpha|\beta)}ba=ab-(-1)^{[\alpha][\beta]}q^{(\alpha|\beta)}ba

if
\alpha,\beta\in Q_{-}
, and by the equation

\displaystyle[\![a,\,b]\!]=ab-(-1)^{[a][b]}ba=ab-(-1)^{[\alpha][\beta]}ba

if
\alpha\in Q_{+}
and
\beta\in Q_{-}
, or
\alpha\in Q_{-}
and
\beta\in Q_{+}
.

The defining relations of the quantum superalgebra
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})
have the form

\displaystyle[\![\bm{K}_{i},\,\bm{K}_{j}]\!]=0,\qquad i,j=1,2,3,

\displaystyle[\![\bm{K}_{i},\,\bm{E}_{j}]\!]=\alpha_{j}(\mathbb{K}_{i})\bm{E}_{j},\qquad[\![\bm{K}_{i},\,\bm{F}_{j}]\!]=-\alpha_{j}(\mathbb{K}_{i})\bm{F}_{j},\qquad i=1,2,3,\qquad j=1,2,

\displaystyle[\![\bm{E}_{i},\,\bm{F}_{j}]\!]=\delta_{ij}\frac{q^{\bm{H}_{i}}-q^{-\bm{H}_{i}}}{q-q^{-1}},\qquad i,j=1,2,

\displaystyle[\![\bm{E}_{2},\,\bm{E}_{2}]\!]=0,\qquad[\![\bm{F}_{2},\,\bm{F}_{2}]\!]=0,

\displaystyle[\![\bm{E}_{1},\,[\![\bm{E}_{1},\,\bm{E}_{2}]\!]]\!]=0,\qquad[\![\bm{F}_{1},\,[\![\bm{F}_{1},\,\bm{F}_{2}]\!]]\!]=0,

where

\bm{H}_{1}=\bm{K}_{1}-\bm{K}_{2},\qquad\bm{H}_{2}=\bm{K}_{2}+\bm{K}_{3}.

We define the elements

\bm{E}_{13}=[\![\bm{E}_{1},\,\bm{E}_{2}]\!]=\bm{E}_{1}\bm{E}_{2}-q\bm{E}_{2}\bm{E}_{1},\qquad\bm{F}_{13}=[\![\bm{F}_{1},\,\bm{F}_{2}]\!]=\bm{F}_{2}\bm{F}_{1}-q^{-1}\bm{F}_{1}\bm{F}_{2}

and, for consistency, denote

\bm{E}_{12}=\bm{E}_{1},\qquad\bm{E}_{23}=\bm{E}_{2},\qquad\bm{F}_{12}=\bm{F}_{1},\qquad\bm{F}_{23}=\bm{F}_{2}.

We observe that

[\bm{E}_{ij}]=[i]+[j].

The root vectors
\bm{E}_{ij}
and
\bm{F}_{ij}
are linearly independent, and together with the elements
\bm{K}_{i}
, are referred to as the Cartan–Weyl generators of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})
. It appears that the ordered monomials constructed from the Cartan–Weyl generators form a Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt basis of the quantum superalgebra
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})
.

It is important that the quantum loop superalgebra
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})
is a Hopf superalgebra with respect to the comultiplication
\Delta
, the antipode
S
, and the counit
\varepsilon
defined by the relations

\displaystyle\Delta(\bm{K}_{i})=\bm{K}_{i}\otimes 1+1\otimes\bm{K}_{i},

\displaystyle\Delta(\bm{E}_{i})=\bm{E}_{i}\otimes 1+q^{\bm{H}_{i}}\otimes\bm{E}_{i},\qquad\Delta(\bm{F}_{i})=\bm{F}_{i}\otimes q^{-\bm{H}_{i}}+1\otimes\bm{F}_{i},

\displaystyle S(\bm{K}_{i})=-\bm{K}_{i},\qquad S(\bm{E}_{i})=-q^{-\bm{H}_{i}}\bm{E}_{i},\qquad S(\bm{F}_{i})=-\bm{F}_{i}\,q^{\bm{H}_{i}},

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\bm{K}_{i})=0,\qquad\varepsilon(\bm{E}_{i})=0,\qquad\varepsilon(\bm{F}_{i})=0.

2.3.
Modules and representations

We identify a triple
(\lambda_{1},\,\lambda_{2},\,\lambda_{3})
of complex numbers with an element
\lambda\in\mathfrak{k}^{*}
defined by the equation

\lambda(\mathbb{K}_{i})=\lambda_{i},

and vice versa. A
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})
-module
V
is called a highest weight module of highest weight
\lambda\in\mathfrak{k}^{*}
if there exists a weight vector

satisfying the relations

\displaystyle\bm{E}_{i}v^{\lambda}=0,\quad i=1,2,\qquad\bm{K}_{i}v^{\lambda}=\lambda_{i}v^{\lambda},\quad i=1,2,3,

\displaystyle\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})\,v^{\lambda}=V.

Given
\lambda\in\mathfrak{k}^{*}
, let
\widetilde{V}^{\lambda}
denote the corresponding Verma
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})
-module. This is a highest weight module of highest weight
\lambda
. We denote by
\widetilde{\pi}^{\lambda}
the representation of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})
corresponding
to
\widetilde{V}^{\lambda}
. It is clear that
\widetilde{V}^{\lambda}
and

are infinite-dimensional. However, if the difference

is a non-negative integer, there is a maximal submodule such that the respective quotient module is finite-dimensional. We denote this quotient by

and the corresponding representation as

.

In the present paper, we are especially interested in the finite-dimensional representation
\pi^{(1,\,0,\,0)}
of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})
, which is realized on a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space of

, introduced in Section

, as follows:

\displaystyle\pi^{(1,0,0)}(\bm{K}_{1})=\mathbb{K}_{1},\qquad\pi^{(1,0,0)}(\bm{K}_{2})=\mathbb{K}_{2},\qquad\pi^{(1,0,0)}(\bm{K}_{3})=\mathbb{K}_{3},

\displaystyle\pi^{(1,0,0)}(\bm{E}_{12})=\mathbb{M}_{12},\quad\pi^{(1,0,0)}(\bm{E}_{23})=\mathbb{M}_{23},\quad\pi^{(1,0,0)}(\bm{F}_{12})=\mathbb{M}_{21},\quad\pi^{(1,0,0)}(\bm{F}_{23})=\mathbb{M}_{32}.

It is straightforward to show that

\pi^{(1,0,0)}(\bm{E}_{13})=\mathbb{M}_{13},\qquad\pi^{(1,0,0)}(\bm{F}_{13})=\mathbb{M}_{31}.

In what follows, we denote the representation
\pi^{(1,0,0)}

3.5.
Universal
R
-matrix

Let
\sigma
be an automorphism of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))\otimes\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})
defined by the equation

\sigma(a_{1}\otimes a_{2})=(-1)^{[a_{1}][a_{2}]}a_{2}\otimes a_{1},

for all homogeneous
a_{1},a_{2}
. As with any Hopf superalgebra, the quantum superalgebra
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))
possesses another comultiplication
\Delta^{\prime}
, called the opposite comultiplication, defined by the equation

\Delta^{\prime}=\Delta\circ\sigma.

The quantum superalgebra
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))
is quasitriangular. It means that there exists a unique invertible even element
\mathcal{R}\in\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))\otimes\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))
, called the universal
R
-matrix, such that

\Delta^{\prime}(a)=\mathcal{R}\,\Delta(a)\,\mathcal{R}^{-1}

for all
a\in\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))
, and

(\Delta\otimes\id)(\mathcal{R})=\mathcal{R}^{(13)}\mathcal{R}^{(23)},\qquad(\id\otimes\Delta)(\mathcal{R})=\mathcal{R}^{(13)}\mathcal{R}^{(12)}.

Here, if
\mathcal{R}=\sum_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}\otimes b_{\alpha}
, then

\mathcal{R}^{(12)}=\sum_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}\otimes b_{\alpha}\otimes 1,\qquad\mathcal{R}^{(13)}=\sum_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}\otimes 1\otimes b_{\alpha},\qquad\mathcal{R}^{(23)}=\sum_{\alpha}1\otimes a_{\alpha}\otimes b_{\alpha}.

The most significant property of the universal
R
-matrix is the equality

(3.26)

\mathcal{R}^{(12)}\mathcal{R}^{(13)}\mathcal{R}^{(23)}=\mathcal{R}^{(23)}\mathcal{R}^{(13)}\mathcal{R}^{(12)},

known as the Yang–Baxter equation for the universal
R
-matrix.

The expression for the universal
R
-matrix of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))
can be constructed using the procedure proposed by Khoroshkin and Tolstoy
[
24
,
25
,
26
,
32
]
. It has the form

\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}_{\prec\delta}\,\mathcal{R}_{\sim\delta}\,\mathcal{R}_{\succ\delta}\,\mathcal{K},

where
\mathcal{R}_{\prec\delta}
,
\mathcal{R}_{\sim\delta}
,
\mathcal{R}_{\succ\delta}
and
\mathcal{K}
are elements of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))\otimes\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))
described as follows.

The elements
\mathcal{R}_{\prec\delta}
and

are the products over the sets of roots

and

and the quantity

is determined by the equation

[\![\bm{e}_{\gamma},\,\bm{f}_{\gamma}]\!]=a_{\gamma}\frac{q^{\bm{h}_{\gamma}}-q^{-\bm{h}_{\gamma}}}{q-q^{-1}}.

Here for

The order of the factors in

For the quantities

u_{n}=\frac{n}{[n]_{q}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-1\\
-1&-[2]_{q^{n}}\end{array}\right).

Finally, the factor
\mathcal{K}
is expressed as

(3.28)

\mathcal{K}=\exp\big(-\hbar\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}c_{ij}(\bm{h}_{i}\otimes\bm{h}_{j})\big),

where
c_{ij}
are the matrix entries of the matrix

C=A^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-1\\
-1&-2\end{array}\right).

4.
Basic monodromy operator

4.1.
Integrability objects

To address integrability objects, we introduce the concept of a spectral parameter. This is achieved by endowing
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))
with a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading. The procedure follows the method described in the paper
[
11
]
. For a given
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, we define an automorphism
\Gamma_{\zeta}
of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))
by its action on the generators of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))
as

\Gamma_{\zeta}(\bm{h}_{i})=0,\qquad\Gamma_{\zeta}(\bm{e}_{i})=\zeta^{s_{i}}\bm{e}_{i},\qquad\Gamma_{\zeta}(\bm{f}_{i})=\zeta^{-s_{i}}\bm{f}_{i},

where
s_{i}
are arbitrary integers. The automorphisms
\Gamma_{\zeta}
induce the
\mathbb{Z}
-grading on
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))
with the grading subspaces

\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))_{m}=\{a\in\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))\mid\Gamma_{\zeta}(a)=\zeta^{m}a\}.

It is noteworthy that for any
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
the universal
R
-matrix of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))
satisfies the condition

(4.1)

(\Gamma_{\zeta}\otimes\Gamma_{\zeta})(\mathcal{R})=\mathcal{R}.

We use the following notations:

s=s_{0}+s_{1}+s_{2}

and

s_{12}=s_{1},\qquad s_{13}=s_{1}+s_{2},\qquad s_{23}=s_{2}.

In what follows, we assume that
s\neq 0
.

For any homomorphism
\varphi
from
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))
to an associative superalgebra
A
, we define a family of homomorphisms
\varphi_{\zeta}
,
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, from
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))
to
A
by the relation

\varphi_{\zeta}=\varphi\circ\Gamma_{\zeta}.

Let
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
be homomorphisms from
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))
to associative superalgebras
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
. The corresponding integrability object
X_{\varphi_{1}|\varphi_{2}}(\zeta_{1}|\zeta_{2})
is defined by the equation

X_{\varphi_{1}|\varphi_{2}}(\zeta_{1}|\zeta_{2})=\rho_{\varphi_{1}|\varphi_{2}}(\zeta_{1}|\zeta_{2})(\varphi_{\zeta_{1}}\otimes\varphi_{\zeta_{2}})(\mathcal{R}),

where
\zeta_{1}
and
\zeta_{2}
are spectral parameters, and
\rho_{\varphi_{1}|\varphi_{2}}(\zeta_{1}|\zeta_{2})
is a scalar normalization factor. By definition,
X_{\varphi_{1}|\varphi_{2}}(\zeta_{1}|\zeta_{2})
is an element of the superalgebra
A_{1}\otimes A_{2}
.

4.2.
Monodromy operators

In the present paper, we study a special class of integrability objects known as monodromy operators. To construct these operators, we use either an infinite-dimensional representation
\widetilde{\varphi}^{\lambda}
or a finite-dimensional representation
\varphi^{\lambda}
for the first factor of
\mathcal{R}
. These representations were defined in section
3.3
. For the second factor, we use the finite-dimensional representation
\varphi=\varphi^{(1,0,0)}
. Thus, we consider the monodromy operators

\widetilde{M}^{\lambda}(\zeta|\eta)=((\widetilde{\varphi}^{\lambda})_{\zeta}\otimes\varphi_{\eta})(\mathcal{R}),\qquad M^{\lambda}(\zeta|\eta)=((\varphi^{\lambda})_{\zeta}\otimes\varphi_{\eta})(\mathcal{R}).

In fact, it is convenient for applications to define the basic monodromy operator

M(\zeta|\eta)=(\epsilon_{\zeta}\otimes\varphi_{\eta}))(\mathcal{R}).

For any
\zeta
and
\eta
the operator
M(\zeta|\eta)
is an element of the space
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})\otimes\End(V)
. The monodromy operators
\widetilde{M}^{\lambda}(\zeta|\eta)
and
M^{\lambda}(\zeta|\eta)
can be constructed with the help of the relations

\widetilde{M}^{\lambda}(\zeta|\eta)=(\widetilde{\pi}^{\lambda}\otimes\id)(M(\zeta|\eta)),\qquad M^{\lambda}(\zeta|\eta)=(\pi^{\lambda}\otimes\id)(M(\zeta|\eta)).

Using equation (
4.1
), we demonstrate that

(4.2)

M(\zeta\nu|\eta\nu)=M(\zeta|\eta)

for any
\nu\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. We have

M(\zeta|\eta)=M(\zeta\eta^{-1}),

where
M(\zeta)=M(\zeta|1)
.

The monodromy operator
M^{\lambda}(\zeta_{1}|\zeta_{2})
for
\lambda=(1,\,0,\,0)
plays a special role and is referred to as the
R
-operator. We denote it as
R(\zeta_{1}|\zeta_{2})
. It follows from equation (
3.26
) that
R(\zeta_{1}|\zeta_{2})
satisfies the Yang–Baxter equation:

R^{(12)}(\zeta_{1}|\zeta_{2})R^{(13)}(\zeta_{1}|\zeta_{3})R^{(23)}(\zeta_{2}|\zeta_{3})=R^{(23)}(\zeta_{2}|\zeta_{3})R^{(13)}(\zeta_{1}|\zeta_{3})R^{(12)}(\zeta_{1}|\zeta_{2}).

Here, if
\mathcal{R}=\sum_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}\otimes b_{\alpha}
, then

\displaystyle R^{(12)}(\zeta_{1}|\zeta_{2})=\sum_{\alpha}\varphi_{\zeta_{1}}(a_{\alpha})\otimes\varphi_{\zeta_{2}}(b_{\alpha})\otimes\id_{V},\quad R^{(13)}(\zeta_{1}|\zeta_{3})=\sum_{\alpha}\varphi_{\zeta_{1}}(a_{\alpha})\otimes\id_{V}\otimes\varphi_{\zeta_{3}}(b_{\alpha}),

\displaystyle R^{(23)}(\zeta_{2}|\zeta_{3})=\sum_{\alpha}\id_{V}\otimes\varphi_{\zeta_{2}}(a_{\alpha})\otimes\varphi_{\zeta_{3}}(b_{\alpha}).

Applying the mapping
\epsilon_{\zeta}\otimes\varphi_{\zeta_{1}}\otimes\varphi_{\zeta_{2}}
, to both sides of equation (
3.26
), we obtain

(4.3)

M^{(12)}(\zeta|\zeta_{1})M^{(13)}(\zeta|\zeta_{2})R^{(23)}(\zeta_{1}|\zeta_{2})=R^{(23)}(\zeta_{1}|\zeta_{2})M^{(13)}(\zeta|\zeta_{2})M^{(12)}(\zeta|\zeta_{1}).

Here, if
\mathcal{R}=\sum_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}\otimes b_{\alpha}
, then

\displaystyle M^{(12)}(\zeta|\zeta_{1})=\sum_{\alpha}\epsilon_{\zeta}(a_{\alpha})\otimes\varphi_{\zeta_{1}}(b_{\alpha})\otimes\id_{V},\quad M^{(13)}(\zeta|\zeta_{2})=\sum_{\alpha}\epsilon_{\zeta}(a_{\alpha})\otimes\id_{V}\otimes\varphi_{\zeta_{2}}(b_{\alpha}),

\displaystyle R^{(23)}(\zeta_{1}|\zeta_{2})=\sum_{\alpha}\id_{V}\otimes\varphi_{\zeta_{1}}(a_{\alpha})\otimes\varphi_{\zeta_{2}}(b_{\alpha}).

Substituting
\zeta=\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2}
in (
4.3
) and using equation (
4.2
), we arrive at the more familiar equation

R^{(23)}(\zeta_{1}|\zeta_{2})M^{(13)}(\zeta_{1})M^{(12)}(\zeta_{2})=M^{(12)}(\zeta_{2})M^{(13)}(\zeta_{1})R^{(23)}(\zeta_{1}|\zeta_{2}).

4.3.

6.
Conclusions

We derived the explicit expression for the basic monodromy operator for the quantum loop superalgebra
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))
. Two significant additional results emerged from this derivation:
simple expressions for the generating functions of the the images of the root vectors of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))
under the Drinfeld homomorphism and explicit expressions for certain central elements of the quantum superalgebra
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})
. We established the relation between the derived central elements and those obtained using the Drinfeld partial trace method. An advantage of the derived central elements is the simple form of their eigenvalues. The expressions for the generating functions of the root vectors could be used to find the
\ell
-weights of the evaluation representations of the quantum loop superalgebra
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))
.

Superalgebras

Let
V
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space so that

V=V_{\overline{0}}\oplus V_{\overline{1}}.

The elements of the subspaces
V_{\overline{0}}
and
V_{\overline{1}}
are said to be even and odd, respectively. An element belonging to
V_{\overline{0}}
or
V_{\overline{1}}
is said to be homogeneous. The parity of a homogeneous element
v
, denoted as
[v]
, is
\overline{0}
or
\overline{1}
according to whether it is in
V_{\overline{0}}
or
V_{\overline{1}}
.

Throughout the paper, we use the following convenient convention. If
v
is an element of a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space and
[v]
appears in some formula or expression, then
v
is assumed to be homogeneous.
For a finite dimensional
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space
V
, the pair
(\dim V_{\overline{0}},\,\dim V_{\overline{1}})
is called the dimension of
V
. The tensor product of two
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector spaces
V
and
W
is considered as a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space with

(V\otimes W)_{\alpha}=\bigoplus_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}V_{\beta}\otimes W_{\gamma}.

It is clear that

[v\otimes w]=[v]+[w].

An algebra
A
is called a superalgebra if it is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded as a vector space and

A_{\alpha}A_{\beta}\subset A_{\alpha+\beta},\qquad\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

It follows that for any two homogeneous elements
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
, the product
a_{1}a_{2}
is homogeneous and

[a_{1}a_{2}]=[a_{1}]+[a_{2}].

The tensor product of two associative superalgebras
A
and
B
is also an associative superalgebra. Its multiplication is defined by the equation

(a_{1}\otimes b_{1})(a_{2}\otimes b_{2})=(-1)^{[b_{1}][a_{2}]}(a_{1}a_{2})\otimes(b_{1}b_{2})

for any homogeneous elements
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
and
b_{1},b_{2}\in B
. The multiplication in the tensor product of any finite number of associative superalgebras is defined recursively.

Let
V
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space and
M\in\End(V)
. We have

Mv=Mv_{\overline{0}}+Mv_{\overline{1}}=(Mv_{\overline{0}})_{\overline{0}}+(Mv_{\overline{0}})_{\overline{1}}+(Mv_{\overline{1}})_{\overline{0}}+(Mv_{\overline{1}})_{\overline{1}}.

We define

M_{\overline{0}}v=(Mv_{\overline{0}})_{\overline{0}}+(Mv_{\overline{1}})_{\overline{1}},\qquad M_{\overline{1}}v=(Mv_{\overline{0}})_{\overline{1}}+(Mv_{\overline{1}})_{\overline{0}}

so that

M=M_{\overline{0}}+M_{\overline{1}}.

The above decomposition is unique. Therefore, the associative algebra
\End(V)
has a natural structure of a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space. It is clear that

[Mv]=[M]+[v].

Furthermore, it can be shown that for any two homogeneous elements
M,N\in\End(V)
we have

[MN]=[M]+[N].

This implies that
\End(V)
is an associative superalgebra.

A Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a superalgebra with an operation
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
satisfying the conditions

\displaystyle[a,\,b]=(-1)^{[a][b]}[b,\,a],

\displaystyle(-1)^{[a][c]}[a,\,[b,\,c]]+(-1)^{[b][a]}[b,\,[c,\,a]]+(-1)^{[c][b]}[c,\,[a,\,b]]=0.

for all homogeneous elements
a,b,c
in
\mathfrak{g}
. Here and throughout the paper, it is assumed that

(-1)^{\overline{0}}=1,\qquad(-1)^{\overline{1}}=-1.

It is clear that

[\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}},\,\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}]\subset\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}},\qquad[\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}},\,\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}]\subset\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}.

It means that
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
is a Lie algebra, and
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
is a
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
-module.

Given an associative superalgebra
A
, we define the supercommutator on homogeneous elements of
A
by

[a_{1},\,a_{2}]=a_{1}a_{2}-(-1)^{[a_{1}][a_{2}]}a_{2}a_{1}

and then extend the definition by linearity to all elements of
A
. With respect to the supercommutator, the algebra
A
is a Lie superalgebra. In this way, for any
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space
V
, starting with the superalgebra
\End(V)
, we obtain a Lie superalgebra called the general linear superalgebra and denoted by
\mathfrak{gl}(V)
.

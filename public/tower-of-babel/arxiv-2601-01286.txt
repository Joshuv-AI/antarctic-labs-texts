ON THE STABILITY OF DEGENERATE SCHRÖDINGER EQUATION
UNDER BOUNDARY FRACTIONAL DAMPING

Abstract
In this paper we study the well-posedness and stability of degenerate Schrödinger equation with a fractional boundary damping. First, we establish the well-posedness of the degenerate problem ψ t ​ ( x , t ) − ı ​ ( τ ⁡ ( x ) ​ ψ x ​ ( x , t ) ) x = 0 , with ​ x ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \psi_{t}(x,t)-\imath(\tau(x)\psi_{x}(x,t))_{x}=0,\hbox{ with }x\in(0,1) , controlled by Dirichlet-Neumann conditions. 1
Introduction

In this paper, we study the well-posedness and stability of degenerate Schrödinger equation under a fractional
boundary damping represented by the following system:

(1)

\quad\left\{\matrix{\psi_{t}(x,t)-\imath(\tau(x)\psi_{x}(x,t))_{x}=0,\hfill&\hbox{ in }(0,1)\times(0,+\infty),\hfill&\cr\left\{\matrix{\psi(0,t)=0\hfill&\hbox{ if }0\leq m_{\tau}<1\hfill&\cr(\tau(x)\psi_{x})(0,t)=0\hfill&\hbox{ if }1\leq m_{\tau}<2\hfill&\cr}\right.\hfill&\hbox{ in }(0,+\infty),\hfill&\cr(\tau\psi_{x})(1,t)-\imath\rho\partial^{\tilde{\alpha},\wp}\psi(1,t)=0\hfill&\hbox{ in }(0,+\infty),\hfill&\cr\psi(x,0)=\psi_{0}(x)\hfill&\hbox{ on }(0,1),\hfill&\cr}\right.

where
\rho>0
and
\tau\in{\cal C}([0,1])\cap{\cal C}^{1}([0,1])
a function satisfying the following assumptions:

\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\tau(x)>0,\ \forall\ x\in]0,1],\ \tau(0)=0,\\
\\
m_{\tau}=\displaystyle\sup_{x<0\leq 1}\displaystyle\frac{x|\tau^{\prime}(x)|}{\tau(x)}<2,\\
\\
\tau\in{\cal C}^{[m_{\tau}]}([0,1]),\end{array}\right.

where
[.]
stands for the integer part.

\partial^{\tilde{\alpha},\wp}
represents the exponential fractional integral of order
0<\tilde{\alpha}\leq 1

(see
[
6
]
), which is given by

\partial^{\tilde{\alpha},\wp}w({t})=\left\{\matrix{w(t)\hfill&\hbox{ for }\tilde{\alpha}=1,\ \wp\geq 0,\hfill\cr\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\tilde{\alpha})}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{{t}}({t}-s)^{-\tilde{\alpha}}e^{-{\wp}({t}-s)}w(s)\,ds,\hfill&\hbox{ for }0<\tilde{\alpha}<1,\ \wp\geq 0.\hfill\cr}\right.

We analyse the two following cases:

When
m_{\tau}\in[0,1[
, then the problem
is named weakly degenerate at
0
and the usual boundary condition related to
(\ref{P2'})_{1}
is
the Dirichlet boundary condition
\psi(0)=0
.

When
m_{\tau}>1
, then the problem
is named strongly degenerate at
0
and the usual boundary condition related to
(\ref{P2'})_{1}
is
the Neumann boundary condition
(\tau\psi_{x})(0)=0
.

Equation
(\ref{P2'})
has diverse applications in the field of nonlinear
optics, plasma physics, fluid mechanics and quantum gases.
Taking into account such a type of damping is not only of mathematical interests but it is also
related to concrete real-life applications.

G. Fibich
[
10
]
acclaimed that the damping (absorption) term plays an
important effect in the physical model and it is better to not be
ignored. This inspired us to consider
(\ref{P2'})
.

Many authors addressed the Schrödinger equation like Machtyngier and Zuazua
[
17
]
,
where they studied the stabilization of the Schrödinger equation by multiplier techniques.
they obtained an exponential decay under certain geometric conditions in
\Omega
an open bounded domain in
{\bf\hbox{\sc I\hskip-2.0ptR}}^{n}
.

Liu and Wang
[
15
]
applied the backsteping method to study the stabilization problem of an antistable
Schrödinger equation by boundary feedback with only displacement observation.
Firstly, they obtained an exponential stability in
H^{1}(\Omega)

with Newmann boundary damping, then they studied the stability in
L^{2}(\Omega)
where the damping term is
in a neighborhood of board of
\Omega
.

Cui, Liu and Xu
[
7
]
traited the stabilization of Schrödinger equation with
constrained boundary damping. These stability results are similar to those obtained in
[
11
]
and
[
22
]
.

Nicaise and Rebiai
[
19
]
obtained the exponential
stabilization of a Schrödinger equation with boundary or internal difference-type
damping.
Recently, Chen, Xie and Xu
[
5
]
applied the approach comes from
Feng et al.
[
9
]
to the multi-dimensional Schrödinger equation with interior pure delay
control and obtained the exponential stabilization. Moreover, Li, Chen and Xie
[
14
]

used this method to consider the stabilization problem for the Schrödinger equation
with an input time delay, and obtained the exponential stability with arbitrary decay
rate of the closed-loop system.

This paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we give some preliminaries. In section 3,
the well-posedness results of the system
(\ref{P2'})
are given using semigroup theory. In section 4,
we prove exponential and polynomial decay using classical multiplier method. In section 5, we show that the obtained results are optimal.

Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce notations, definitions and
propositions that will be used later. First we introduce some weighted Sobolev spaces:

H^{1}_{\tau}(0,1)=\left\{\psi\in L^{2}(0,1),\psi\hbox{ is locally absolutely continuous in }(0,1],\ \sqrt{\tau(x)}\psi_{x}\in L^{2}(0,1)\right\},

In order to express the boundary conditions of the first component of the solution of

3
Well-posedness

The goal of this section is to prove the well-posedness results of
(\ref{P2'})
using a semigroup approach and the
Lumer-Philips’ Theorem.

We consider system
(P)
in the natural state space

{\cal H}=L^{2}(0,1)\times L^{2}({\bf\hbox{\sc I\hskip-2.0ptR}})

equipped with the inner product

\langle U,\widetilde{U}\rangle_{\cal H}=\int_{0}^{1}\psi(x)\overline{\widetilde{\psi}}(x)dx+\zeta\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\vartheta(\xi)\overline{\widetilde{\vartheta}}(\xi)\,d\xi

for all
U,\widetilde{U}\in{\mathcal{H}}
with
U=(\psi,\vartheta)^{T}
and
\widetilde{U}=(\widetilde{\psi},\widetilde{\vartheta})^{T}
.
Define the system operator of
(P)
as follows:

{\cal A}U=\pmatrix{\imath(\tau(x)\psi_{x})_{x}\cr-(\xi^{2}+\wp)\vartheta+\eta(\xi)\psi(1)\cr}

with domain

D({\mathcal{A}})=\left\{\matrix{(\psi,\vartheta)\in{\cal H}:\psi\in H_{\tau}^{2}(0,1)\cap W_{\tau}^{1}(0,1),\ (\tau(x)\psi_{x})(1)=\imath\zeta\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\eta(\xi)\vartheta(\xi)\,d\xi&\cr-(\xi^{2}+\wp)\vartheta+\eta(\xi)\psi(1)\in L^{2}({\bf\hbox{\sc I\hskip-2.0ptR}}),|\xi|\vartheta\in L^{2}({\bf\hbox{\sc I\hskip-2.0ptR}})&\cr}\right\}.

Then,
(P)
can be written as an evolution equation in
{\mathcal{H}}

(9)

\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}U_{t}={\cal A}U,\\
U(0)=U_{0}.\end{array}\right.

The following theorem gives the existence and uniqueness results of the
solution of the problem (
9
).

Theorem 3.1

Let
U_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
, then there exists a
unique solution
U\in{{\cal C}}^{0}({{\bf\hbox{\sc I\hskip-2.0ptR}}}_{+},{\cal H})
.Moreover, if
U_{0}\in D({\cal A})
, then

U\in{{\cal C}}^{0}({{\bf\hbox{\sc I\hskip-2.0ptR}}}_{+},D({\cal A}))\cap{{\cal C}}^{1}({\bf\hbox{\sc I\hskip-2.0ptR}}_{+},{\cal H})
.

Proof

We use the semigroup approach. In what follows, we prove that
{\cal A}
is dissipative. For any
U\in D({\cal A})

and using (
7
), (
8
) and the fact that

(10)

E(t)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|U\|_{\cal H}^{2},

we have

(11)

\Re\langle{\cal A}U,U\rangle_{\cal H}=-\zeta\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}(\xi^{2}+\wp)|\vartheta(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq 0.

Hence,
{\cal A}
is dissipative. Next, we prove that the operator
\lambda I-{\cal A}
is surjective for
\lambda>0
.
Given
F\in{\cal H}
, we prove that there exists
U\in D({\cal A})
satisfying

(12)

(\lambda I-{\cal A})U=F.

Equation (
12
) is equivalent to

(13)

\left\{\matrix{\lambda\psi-\imath(\tau(x)\psi_{x})_{x}=f_{1},\hfill&\cr\lambda\vartheta+(\xi^{2}+\wp)\vartheta-\psi(1)\eta(\xi)=f_{2}.\hfill&\cr}\right.

By
(\ref{e18})_{2}
we can find
\vartheta
as

(14)

\vartheta(\xi)=\displaystyle\frac{\psi(1)\eta(\xi)+f_{2}(\xi)}{\lambda+\xi^{2}+\wp}.

Problem (
13
) can reformulate as follows

(15)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(\lambda\psi-\imath(\tau(x)\psi_{x})_{x}){\overline{w}}\,dx=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}f_{1}{\overline{w}}\,dx\quad\forall w\in W_{\tau}^{1}(0,1).

Integrating by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\lambda\psi{\overline{w}}\,dx+\rho(\lambda+\wp)^{\tilde{\alpha}-1}\psi(1)\overline{w}(1)+\imath\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\tau(x)\psi_{x}{\overline{w}}_{x}\,dx=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}f_{1}{\overline{w}}\,dx-\zeta\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\eta(\xi)f_{2}(\xi)}{\lambda+\xi^{2}+\wp}\,d\xi\overline{w}(1).

for all

w\in W^{1}_{\tau}(0,1)
.
Multiplying the equation by
(1-i)
, we obtain

(16)

\matrix{(1-i)\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\lambda\psi{\overline{w}}\,dx+(1-i)\rho(\lambda+\wp)^{\tilde{\alpha}-1}\psi(1){\overline{w}}(1)+(1+i)\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\tau(x)\psi_{x}{\overline{w}}_{x}\,dx=(1-i)\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}f_{1}{\overline{w}}\,dx\hfill&\cr-\zeta(1-i)\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\eta(\xi)f_{2}(\xi)}{\lambda+\xi^{2}+\wp}\,d\xi\overline{w}(1)\ \forall w\in W_{\tau}^{1}(0,1).&\cr}

Problem (
16
) is of the form

(17)

{\cal L}(\psi,w)={\cal M}(w),

where
{\cal L}:[W_{\tau}^{1}(0,1)\times W_{\tau}^{1}(0,1)]\longrightarrow{\bf\hbox{\sc I\hskip-7.0ptC}}

is the bilinear form defined by

{\cal L}(\psi,w)=(1-i)\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\lambda\psi{\overline{w}}\,dx+(1-i)\rho(\lambda+\wp)^{\tilde{\alpha}-1}\psi(1){\overline{w}}(1)+(1+i)\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\tau(x)\psi_{x}{\overline{w}}_{x}\,dx,

and

It is easy to verify that
{\cal L}
is continuous and coercive, and
{\cal M}
is continuous.
Consequently, by the Lax-Milgram Lemma, system (
17
) has a unique solution

\psi\in W_{\tau}^{1}(0,1)
.
By the regularity theory for the linear elliptic equations, it follows that
\psi\in H_{\tau}^{2}(0,1)
.
Therefore, the operator
\lambda I-{\cal A}
is surjective for any
\lambda>0
. Consequently, using Hill-Yosida
theorem, the result of Theorem
3.1
follows.

4
Asymptotic stability

In order to state and prove our stability results, we need the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
(
[
2
]
)

Let
{\cal A}
be the generator of a uniformly bounded
C_{0}
.
semigroup
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
on a Hilbert space
{\cal H}
. If:

(i)

{\cal A}
does not have eigenvalues on
i{\bf\hbox{\sc I\hskip-2.0ptR}}
.

(ii)

The intersection of the spectrum
\sigma({\cal A})
with
i{\bf\hbox{\sc I\hskip-2.0ptR}}
is at most a countable set,

then the semigroup
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is asymptotically stable, i.e,
\|S(t)z\|_{{\cal H}}\rightarrow 0

as
t\rightarrow\infty
for any
z\in{\cal H}
.

A study on the knowledge, attitude, and practice of research integrity among medical professionals in Ningxia, China
Authors: Yiran Jin, Jie Wei, Jingyi Zhang, Sang Luo, Liwei Yuan, Xiaojie Zou, Dan Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study utilizes a Knowledge, Attitude, and Practice (KAP) questionnaire to investigate the awareness of medical professionals in the Ningxia region regarding research integrity and their attitudes towards academic misconduct. A comprehensive analysis of the factors contributing to academic misconduct is performed, with the aim of providing insights for the formulation of policies to enhance research integrity.MethodsThis study is a cross-sectional study. It performed between May 2023 and October 2023, and the questionnaires were distributed and collected through Wenjuanxing and WeChat platforms. The research employs a self-designed KAP survey questionnaire distributed online for clinical doctors and nurses in hospital, faculty members in medical higher education institutions, and undergraduates enrolled in medical schools in the Ningxia region.ResultsA total of 1013 valid questionnaires were collected, with a majority of participants (76.2%) having received training in research integrity. The survey results indicate that a significant majority of participants (75.04%) cannot tolerate academic misconduct. More than 70% of respondents believe that the main reasons for academic misconduct are a lack of understanding of academic ethical norms, a deficiency in personal integrity awareness, individual inadequacy in research capabilities, and excessive pressure. When it comes to measures for academic misconduct reduction, over 70% of individuals believe that strengthening research integrity training, improving the assessment methods for research outcomes, and enhancing personal research capabilities can decrease instances of academic misconduct. Most respondents demonstrated a basic understanding of research integrity and maintained a resistant attitude towards academic misconduct. However, there exists an insufficient awareness of certain forms of academic misconduct. The study recommends implementing multifaceted measures to enhance the ethical awareness and attitudes of researchers.ConclusionIn this study, a KAP questionnaire was administered to clinicians, nurses, medical faculty and students to find out their understanding of research integrity and their attitudes towards academic misconduct. Most of the respondents had a negative attitude towards academic misconduct, but their understanding of research integrity could be improved. Furthermore, university teachers’ understanding of research integrity and their attitudes towards academic misconduct were better than other groups, and university students had lower scores in this area, so it is recommended to take various measures to improve their understanding of research integrity.Keywords: Scientific misconduct, Education, Medical, Knowledge, Attitude, Practice

Introduction

Research integrity is a fundamental requirement in scientific research, serving as the cornerstone of scientific development and societal progress. It is the essence of technological innovation and a prerequisite for every researcher’s scientific competence. Adherence to the rules of research integrity is imperative to ensure the credibility, reliability, and sustainability of scientific research, fostering academic exchange and collaboration, and advancing the accumulation and innovation of scientific knowledge [1, 2]. In recent years, the field of medical research in China has yielded abundant achievements, Chinese scholars play an increasingly important role in global scientific research; however, simultaneously, instances of academic misconduct in scientific research have increased prevalently. Such misconduct not only tarnishes the academic ethos but also poses a hindrance to the healthy development of the scientific and technological enterprise, so it is necessary to have a general discussion on the causes and countermeasures for academic misconduct [3]. China has placed high importance on preventing and addressing research misconduct, evidenced by the introduction of several significant policy documents, such as “Opinions on Further Strengthening the Construction of Research Integrity”. These policies aim to standardize, guide, and reinforce the construction of academic integrity.

The development of medical research holds crucial significance for human health and societal progress. Medical science and technology innovation is an important support for the development of modern health care, which is of great significance in improving medical standards and safeguarding people’s health. Academic misconduct, on the other hand, poses a serious threat to the development of the medical field, as it affects the authenticity and reliability of scientific research and hinders scientific progress. Medical academic misconduct refers to the violation of the principles of academic ethics and integrity in medical research and academic activities, including fabrication, falsification or concealment of data, results and methods. Falsification of research results may mislead other researchers and lead them to conduct research on the wrong basis, resulting in a waste of time and resources. Falsified research results, if applied to clinical practice, may harm the health of patients and may lead to incorrect diagnostic and treatment decisions [4]. In the classic case of Andrew Wakefield, his paper claiming that the measles, mumps and rubella vaccine could be linked to childhood autism led many parents to refuse to vaccinate their children, thus increasing the incidence of measles, mumps and rubella during that period [5]. There have also been a number of high-profile cases of academic misconduct in the biomedical field in the past two decades, such as the case of Japanese scientist Haruko Obokata [6] and South Korean scientist Hwang U-seok [7], et al. Therefore, improving the scientific integrity of people in medical-related fields is crucial to ensuring the authenticity and reliability of scientific research, preventing the dissemination of false data and erroneous conclusions, and promoting the healthy development of the scientific field.

Students at higher education institutions are the foundation force for future research activities. Raising their awareness of research integrity and reducing the likelihood of academic misconduct in the future is crucial to the long-term development of academic ethos and scientific research. Although most colleges and universities have formulated corresponding rules and regulations for academic misconduct, practice has proved that the actual effect of these measures is not satisfactory. How to strengthen the prevention and control of undergraduate academic misconduct is a topic worthy of in-depth study.

According to the analyses of Graf C [8], the reasons for the lack of research integrity primarily include: a weak awareness of integrity among researchers, a lack of emphasis and recognition of research integrity, insufficient self-restraint among researchers, a deficiency in self-discipline and self-monitoring abilities, an inadequate evaluation mechanism for research integrity, a lack of effective assessment and supervisory mechanisms for research integrity, leading to delayed detection and handling of violations, and an imperfect system of supervision and rewards and punishments, with insufficient penalties for research integrity violations, making researchers insufficiently fearful of ethical issues. The occurrence of academic misconduct is not attributed to a single factor. Research assessment pressures are also one of the crucial influencing factors for researchers’ academic misconduct behaviors. On the other hand, the academic atmosphere and the behavior of peers have a subtle influence on the academic ethics and research attitudes of researchers. Individual behavior is significantly influenced by the behavior of those around them. These factors to some extent impact the occurrence of academic misconduct, yet it remains unclear which factors are the primary influencers and the extent to which they may affect academic misconduct.

Therefore, this study employs a questionnaire survey method to investigate the levels of research integrity among doctors and nurses in hospitals, university faculty in the medical field, and undergraduate students. The study analyzes the factors influencing their research behavior and delves into the reasons behind the occurrence of academic misconduct. Through this research, we aim to provide valuable reference points for the formulation of policies aimed at elevating research integrity levels.

Methods

Study design

This study is a cross-sectional study. It was performed between May 2023 and October 2023, and the questionnaires were distributed and collected through Wenjuanxing and WeChat platforms. Wenjuanxing (https://www.wjx.cn) is a professional online questionnaire, assessment and voting platform in China. Wenjuanxing is widely used in social science research and has been shown to have a high degree of credibility and data collection accuracy in many studies [9, 10]. Wenjuanxing’s back-office management system monitors and collects response data in real time, ensures data accuracy and completeness, and supports the export of data at any time for further analysis and processing. In addition, the data encryption and anonymous response function provided by the platform guarantees the safety and confidentiality of participants’ personal information, thus enhancing the authenticity and reliability of the research data.

The subjects of this study were clinicians and nurses from hospitals region, teachers from medical colleges and universities, and students from medical schools in Ningxia, China. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of the General Hospital of Ningxia Medical University, and all participants gave informed consent. We promise to keep participants’ personal information strictly confidential and anonymous during the study.

The questionnaire

We developed this questionnaire after reviewing and referring to a large amount of literature. Before the official launch, we conducted a small-scale distribution of the questionnaire (about 100 people) to test the logic and applicability of the questionnaire.

The questionnaire was designed to gain a comprehensive understanding of the perceptions and attitudes of clinicians and nurses in hospitals, as well as teachers and students in medical tertiary institutions, towards academic misconduct, so as to provide a reference and basis for improving academic integrity in the medical field in the future. The questionnaire is divided into two parts: the first part collects basic information about the research participants, including personal background, occupation and other aspects; the second part focuses on academic misconduct, with 38 questions designed from three aspects: knowledge, practice and attitude. The questionnaire was in the form of multiple-choice questions, including multiple-choice and single-choice questions, and in terms of degree evaluation, a five-point Likert scale was used.

To ensure the quality and completeness of the questionnaire, all questions in this questionnaire are mandatory. After the questionnaire collection, the investigators will check the questionnaires and exclude the questionnaires that do not meet the requirements, the specific exclusion criteria are as follows: the participants in the survey are not clinicians, nurses, teachers of medical colleges and universities or students in hospitals in Ningxia; the options in the questionnaire do not conform to the basic logic; the data were incomplete.

Statistical analysis

After the questionnaires have been collected, the data will be imported into Excel and processed using SPSS 27.0 software. This study begins with a descriptive analysis of demographic data. The impact of gender on academic misconduct attitudes and the awareness of knowledge and penalties is analyzed using the t-test. The impact of profession and education level on academic misconduct attitudes and the awareness of knowledge and penalties is analyzed using one-way ANOVA. The impact of research integrity training on the awareness of the paper plagiarism rate is analyzed using the chi-square test. A p-value of less than 0.05 is considered statistically significant.

Discussion

The issue of research integrity is a global challenge, with varying degrees of misconduct in various countries and research fields [11–13]. The problem of research integrity is now particularly prominent in the medical field [14], so in this study, we have selected relevant people in the medical field as our research subjects, including doctors, nurses, medical teachers and medical school students. By investigating and analysing these groups, we hope to reveal the problems that exist in the medical field in terms of research integrity, and to propose corresponding recommendations for improvement, in order to enhance the integrity level and overall quality of medical research.

From the questionnaire on academic misconduct in this survey, it is evident that some individuals in the medical field lack adequate awareness and understanding of academic misconduct and this phenomenon is prevalent among doctors, nurses, medical teachers and medical students. The results of this questionnaire showed that 30% of hospital workers and 70% of undergraduate students had little or no knowledge of research integrity and academic misconduct, while the questionnaire results showed that close to 20% of hospital workers indicated that they had not received training on academic misconduct, and more than 50% of undergraduate students had not received training. This is similar to previous findings from other national regions [15, 16]. This lack of knowledge may be related to the lack of systematic education and training on research integrity.

The average acceptance score for academic misconduct in this survey is 3.53 ± 0.89, which means that the overall attitude of the participants towards academic misconduct falls within the rejection range. Although the majority of the respondents held a rejection attitude towards academic misconduct, some of them still agreed with or were acceptable to certain academic misconduct and showed a greater degree of tolerance. Such an attitude will have a negative impact on the development of medical research studies. In addition, gender had no effect on academic misconduct attitude dimensions and knowledge and punishment knowledge, but education, and occupation had significant differences on these two issues. Higher education teachers(4.15 ± 0.87, 3.19 ± 0.51) have relatively higher knowledge of research integrity and rejection of academic misconduct, and school students(3.25 ± 1.00, 2.16 ± 0.80) have lower scores on both questions.

Prevention and discipline of academic misconduct go hand in hand. However, respondents expressed a more limited understanding of disciplinary measures for academic misconduct. Lack of training and knowledge in this area may lead to ineffective implementation of disciplinary measures, making it difficult to curb academic misconduct. Respondents generally agreed that research integrity training plays an important role in raising researchers’ awareness of integrity and preventing academic misconduct. Therefore, it is necessary to strengthen training and knowledge on disciplinary measures.

Knowledge training is necessary to reduce academic misconduct, but relying solely on training is not enough. The following aspects also need attention. Instead of solely relying on standards to guide researchers, it is also necessary to strengthen their sense of integrity to resolve these issues [17]. Mentors have a significant impact on the research integrity of current students. Inexperienced students may inadvertently make mistakes and omit important details in the early stages of a research project, leading to misconduct. Mentors with knowledge related to research integrity can be effective in helping students avoid academic misconduct during the mentoring and supervision process. Conversely, if a mentor lacks this knowledge, he or she may not be able to detect academic misconduct in a timely manner, which can have a negative impact on both the mentor and the student as well as on the scientific research [18, 19].

The survey results also reveal various reasons behind academic misconduct. Molly [20] suggests that the excessive pressure to publish in the medical field increases the likelihood that researchers will engage in research misconduct. Martinson [21] suggests that researchers are more likely to engage in academic misconduct when unfair practices are prevalent in a research environment. In this study, the top three factors cited by respondents are insufficient understanding of academic ethical norms and a lack of personal integrity awareness, inadequate personal research capabilities coupled with excessive pressure, and the influence of societal utilitarian factors. Effective measures to reduce academic misconduct, according to the respondents, include strengthening research integrity training, improving research outcome evaluation methods, and enhancing individual research capabilities. These findings highlight the importance of targeted interventions to address the specific needs and characteristics of different groups.

In line with the viewpoint of McNutt [22] developed countries also face challenges related to research integrity, sharing certain commonalities with developing countries. Therefore, in fostering research integrity, developed and developing countries can mutually benefit from sharing experiences. However, research integrity is influenced by various factors, including personal and institutional factors, and there are differences in the influencing factors across different countries. Sullivan [23] explored the basic paths for developing countries to foster research integrity and analyzed the patterns of research integrity development. Developing countries need to consider experiences from developed countries and their own research environments to formulate effective strategies for fostering research integrity.

Based on the above findings, we propose several measures to address academic misconduct:

Strengthening Research Integrity Training. The 2009 guidance document issued by the National Institutes of Health (NIH) on teaching responsible conduct of research in academic settings recommends that training in research integrity should begin at the undergraduate level and continue throughout one’s entire career, with a frequency of no less than once every four years. A semester-long course is more effective as it allows for a deeper exploration of various topics, thereby facilitating a broader understanding of the material [24]. The survey results indicate that 24% of respondents have never participated in training related to research integrity, and nearly half lack a comprehensive understanding of this knowledge and the punitive measures following academic misconduct. Furthermore, statistical analysis reveals that individuals who have undergone multiple research integrity training sessions tend to hold more stringent attitudes towards academic misconduct compared to those who have not participated or have had fewer training sessions. However, it is noteworthy that the frequency of training sessions does not enhance respondents’ awareness of plagiarism in their research. Therefore, in order to improve the understanding of scientific research integrity, it is necessary to strengthen the training intensity of scientific research integrity and refine the training content in a targeted way to achieve better training results.

In addition, significant differences in attitudes towards academic misconduct and awareness of research integrity and punitive measures can be found among staff, university faculty, researchers and current students. To address these differences, targeted research integrity training and education based on the characteristics and needs of different groups is recommended. For healthcare professionals, regular training in research integrity can be conducted using a combination of offline and online approaches. The content may include relevant legal regulations, policies, punitive measures for academic misconduct, and analysis of classic academic misconduct case studies. For college students, offering courses on academic ethics education can enable them to systematically learn about research norms, definitions of academic misconduct, and corresponding penalty measures. Through targeted training and education, different groups can improve the understanding and behavior norms of scientific research integrity, so as to further promote the development of scientific research integrity.

Enhancing Individual Research Capabilities: Researchers with strong research capabilities typically possess a solid academic foundation and professional knowledge, enabling them to conduct high-quality research and propose innovative perspectives and findings [11]. When problems are encountered in the course of research, adequate research skills can give them the confidence to use legitimate means to solve research challenges without resorting to unscrupulous means for the sake of results. To enhance the research capability of individuals, hospitals and educational institutions can organize regular training on research methods and skills, including research design and data analysis. Inviting experts and scholars to organize academic lectures and seminars on the latest research advances can broaden participants’ research horizons and enhance their research capabilities. Improving individual research capacity can effectively reduce research pressure and increase confidence, thereby reducing the incidence of academic misconduct. Hospitals and tertiary institutions should enhance the development of research capacity of students and staff and provide the necessary support and training.

Improve the academic governance system: establish a scientific and fair academic evaluation and academic development system, establish a sound management system for scientific research results such as academic theses, and improve the rules for investigating and dealing with behaviours that violate the requirements of scientific research integrity.

The study still has some limitations. Due to the limited number of participants, the findings may not accurately reflect the knowledge and attitudes of members of the medical profession towards academic misconduct. Future studies should expand the scope of the survey and increase the sample size so as to obtain more reliable and valid data. In addition, a variety of survey methods, such as in-depth interviews, can be used to gain a more comprehensive and accurate understanding of academic misconduct. Despite these limitations, this study provides a scientific basis for formulating policies to improve research integrity. On the basis of this study, the issue of academic misconduct can be further explored in depth to promote the development and maintenance of research integrity.

Conclusion

In this study, a KAP questionnaire was administered to clinicians, nurses, medical faculty and students to find out their understanding of research integrity and their attitudes towards academic misconduct. Most of the respondents had a negative attitude towards academic misconduct, but their understanding of research integrity could be improved. Futhermore, university teachers’ understanding of research integrity and their attitudes towards academic misconduct were better than other groups, and university students had lower scores in this area, so it is recommended to take various measures to improve their understanding of research integrity.

High-performance Terfenol-D/PMMA phononic crystal sensor for tunable acoustic wave control and magnetic field detection
Authors: Hussein A. Elsayed, M. A. Abdelkawy, Abinash Panda, Ashour M. Ahmed, Fatma Ramadan, Wail Zoubi, Ahmed Mehaney
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This research exhibits a simple and accurate method for monitoring of the magnetic field intensity using the integration of the acoustic wave device, enhanced by a phononic crystal (PnC) structure. The incorporation of PnCs into the platform significantly enhances the acoustic wave confinement and energy localization, thereby leading to an improvement in the sensor’s sensitivity. Meanwhile, the designed PnC consists of a distinct number of unit cells in which each one is composed of a versatile magneto-strictive material (Terfenol-D) besides poly methyl methacrylate (PMMA), thus, the designed PnC structure is configured as, [Terfenol/PMMA] N. The mainstay of the present study is mainly focused on the inclusion of Terfenol-D in the structural design, whose young’s modulus is highly sensitive to change in the magnetic field intensities, resulting in shifts in the bandgap width in phononic bandgap (PnBG). Therefore, the numerical investigations including the changes in the bandgap behavior and acoustic reflectance properties have been essentially introduced based on the well-known transfer matrix method. Additionally, the tunability of the PnBG due to the changes in the intensity of the applied external magnetic field represents the cornerstone of this study towards the monitoring of the minute variations of this magnetic field. To ensure optimal performance, all the structural and geometrical parameters are meticulously optimized. The simulation upshots revealed that the proposed magnetic field sensor achieves a sensitivity of 5650 Hz/Oe, a figure of merit of 3.248 × 10−3, a detection limit of 914 × 103, a sensor resolution of 5164 × 106, and a signal to noise ratio of 0.142. With this superior performance, the designed sensor shows a great potential for magnetic field sensing applications compared to the existing alternatives.

Introduction

In the last decades, the field of phononic crystals (PnCs) has received a remarkable development, especially in applications related to sound insulation, vibration control, and thermal management. Meanwhile, the first significant research on the PnC structure was published in 20051, where the researchers explored the high-quality hypersonic PnC operating in the GHz range. Notably, this research provides a significant contribution towards the discover of its properties and potential applications as well. Later in 2014, R. V. Craster and S. Guenneau conducted a comprehensive study that not only examined the historical evolution of phononic materials but also studied and analyzed their dynamic behavior2. This work offered numerous valuable insights into the fabrication, development and versatility of PnCs, highlighting their broad range of practical applications. In the last few years, the research on PnCs has advanced across multiple applications in various fields such as acoustic filters, acoustic imaging, acoustic wave guiding, acoustic lenses, noise isolation, communications, and sensors3,4,5,6,7.

PnCs refer to an artificially structures in one, two, or three-dimensional lattices, that are characterized by a periodic modulation in their elastic properties3. This behavior plays a significant role in enabling a remarkable control of the interacting acoustic or elastic waves within the designed PnCs. In PnC structures, phonons interact through scattering and interference with each other, much like electrons scatter in semiconducting crystals8. In fact, the main property in PnCs is the appearance of phononic bandgap (PnBG)9, that can be introduced due to the destructive interference of the propagating waves at the interfaces of the layered structure. In this regard, these PnBG prohibits the propagation of certain range of frequency in the structure, thereby giving a great control over the acoustic waves propagation. Moreover, the characteristics of the PnBG, specifically its width and location are fundamentally governed by the material composition, lattice configuration, and structural periodicity. By engineering these parameters, PnCs can be tailored to exhibit superior acoustic wave confinement, high sensing performances, and high-efficiency frequency-selective filtering. Additionally, the inclusion of a defect layer or cavity could be superior towards the strong localization of some distinct frequencies inside the PnBGs8,9. Here, the emergence of these modes are strongly related to the structural and geometrical parameters of the defect layer including its density, thickness and elastic properties as well9. These attributes render PnCs highly suitable for applications in acoustic insulation, vibration attenuation, waveguiding, filtering systems, and sensing platforms10,11. Over recent decades, PnCs have attracted considerable research attention in the domain of sensing technologies, owing to their design adaptability, operational precision, long-term stability, and enhanced durability12,13.

Among the diverse functionalities of PnCs, their application in magnetic field sensing has gained substantial attention over the past decade. Magnetic field sensors are crucial for the accurate detection and quantification of magnetic fields, thereby facilitating a broad spectrum of technological applications5,14. In particular, bulk acoustic wave (BAW) sensors have emerged as a focal point of research because of their significant sensitivity for monitoring the subtle variations in magnetic field intensity15,16. Notably, a prominent sensing mechanism in BAW-based magnetic sensors involves the modulation of the Young’s modulus in magnetostrictive materials under the influence of the applied magnetic field17. In particular, this field-induced modification in elastic modulus serves as a basis for precise magnetic field detection and measurement18.

In this regard, Terfenol-D an alloy consisting of dysprosium, terbium, and iron is considered as a one of the most remarkable magnetostrictive materials. Due to its excellent magnetostrictive property, it undergoes substantial changes in shape and size, when exposed to an external magnetic field19. Upon applying an external magnetic field across Terfenol-D, its Young’s modulus is altered, that enables a dynamic modulation of acoustic wave interference. Apart from this, Terfenol-D possesses advantageous properties such as flexibility, low weight, and minimal power consumption, making it as a suitable candidate for the improvement of battery-powered and energy-efficient sensor systems20,21. Additionally, the fabrication potential of Terfenol-D has been investigated by M. Sulley and his colleagues, who reported that the optimal sensitivity is achieved when Terfenol-D particles, approximately 250 μm in size, are dispersed within an epoxy resin matrix at a volumetric concentration of 30%22. Due to these unique characteristics, Terfenol-D is extensively valuable in various sensing applications. For example, Samadi et al. introduced a sensor based on a PnC structure to improve the performance of magnetic field sensing23. A.H. Aly et al. investigated the behavior of acoustic waves propagation and localization within PnC design by considering the influence of both electric and magnetic fields. Their results demonstrated that the magnetic field provides a higher impact on the position of the defect mode than the electric field24. Moreover, Zhao et al. incorporated the surface effects to assess the properties of the PnBG based on the analysis of the magneto electro-elastic effects through PnC structure25. Further, Gao et al. investigated the tuning of low frequencies behavior of a magnetic-based PnC incorporating Archimedean spiral beams26. Zhang et al. explored the tunability of the topological edge state through a designed structure of a one dimensional magnetic PnC27. Additionally, Padmanabhan et al. introduced the magnetic deformation besides the anti-plane shear waves in a two dimensional PnC structure for the tunability of the PnBG28.

The current study introduces several innovative aspects in the context of magnetic field sensing. Distinct from traditional acoustic wave sensors, the proposed design integrates a PnC structure, facilitating a precise manipulation of acoustic wave propagation via bandgap engineering, and thereby enhancing the wave confinement. A key advancement lies in the incorporation of the magnetostrictive material Terfenol-D, which imparts magnetic field sensitivity to the system. Furthermore, the structural parameters have been meticulously optimized to achieve maximum sensitivity. This integrated approach presents a promising avenue for the development of miniaturized, high-performance, and frequency-selective magnetic field sensors, which has not been extensively studied the existing literature. Furthermore, the designed structure provides a relative high sensitivity compared to other magnetic field sensors based on PnC structures besides its simplicity towards a smooth and easy fabrication process.

Notably, the exposure of Terfenol-D to an external magnetic field leads to some significant effects into its elastic properties, particularly Young’s modulus, thereby enabling tunability of the PnBG. In contrast, PMMA, known for its low acoustic impedance and elastic modulus, serves as a complementary material. When combined with Terfenol-D, PMMA induces a significant acoustic impedance mismatch, giving rise to PnBG. Experimental setup and fabrication feasibility

The fabrication of the proposed Terfenol-D/PMMA-based PnC structure is feasible using established microfabrication techniques. Terfenol-D can be fabricated via sputtering or pulsed laser deposition (PLD) methods, enabling precise control over film thickness and surface uniformity. Bulk Terfenol-D layers can also be produced using tape casting or bonding pre-sintered sheets, ensuring mechanical stability29. In contrast, PMMA layers, owing to their excellent machinability and low processing temperature, can be deposited through spin-coating or cast-molding techniques30,31. For thicker PMMA slabs, laser cutting, and precision milling can be employed to achieve the desired dimensions. Layer-by-layer assembly of Terfenol-D and PMMA can be facilitated by adhesive bonding, ensuring strong interfacial adhesion without compromising acoustic properties. The inherent flexibility and processability of PMMA allow for compatibility with Terfenol-D substrates, thereby simplifying the fabrication workflow. Furthermore, the low cost and scalability of PMMA processing enhance the feasibility of large-area PnC structures. Acoustic wave of a varying magnetic field intensity can be generated from an acoustic source. Then, an acoustic source can be used to generate acoustic waves, which in turns can interact with the designed PnC magnetic field sensor. Then, the reflected signal can be generated and analyzed by a transducer a personal computer (PC), respectively. Moreover, a 3 dB coupler can be used to couple the incident and reflected signal to the proposed structure. The experiment should be conducted at a controlled room temperature, as variations in temperature could alter the sound velocity in the materials and thereby impact the study’s outcomes. For a clearer understanding regarding the experimental analysis, a complete experimental setup is shown in Fig. 2a. Overall, the proposed structure is practically realizable with existing fabrication technologies, making it suitable for experimental validation of acoustic wave manipulation and magnetic field sensing applications.Fig. 2(a) Experimental setup of the PnC magnetic field sensor (b) structure fabrication steps.

(a) Experimental setup of the PnC magnetic field sensor (b) structure fabrication steps.

For simplicity, the schematic in Fig. 2b depicts the stepwise fabrication procedure for realizing the proposed 1D Terfenol-D/PMMA PnC structure. In the first stage, a thin film of Terfenol-D is deposited onto a cleaned substrate via sputtering or pulsed laser deposition (PLD), ensuring precise thickness control and uniform microstructure. Subsequently, a layer of polymethyl methacrylate (PMMA) is spin-coated onto the metallic film, forming the alternating polymer layer required for phononic periodicity. To define the periodic arrangement, laser interference lithography is employed, enabling the creation of nanoscale stripe patterns with high fidelity across large areas. The patterned PMMA is then developed to remove the exposed (or unexposed, depending on the resist mode) regions, yielding a well-defined sequence of alternating Terfenol-D and PMMA regions. This repeated bilayer stacking process constructs the final [Terfenol-D/PMMA]N multilayer structure, which functions as a 1D PnC stucture. The resultant architecture leverages the significant acoustic impedance mismatch between Terfenol-D and PMMA to generate phononic bandgaps, thereby enabling the manipulation of acoustic wave propagation for potential applications in wave filtering, sensing, and magnetoacoustics devices.

Optimization of structure parameters

In this section, we study the sensitivity of the sensor by optimizing the various geometrical parameters. Nevertheless, the PnC’s sensitivity depends on structural parameters, material characteristics, and external environmental factors, like temperature, humidity and pressure are considered as a constant in the entire simulation process. To maximize the sensitivity, we proceed to optimize the thickness of various layers besides the number of the unit cells of the designed PnC structure as well.

The optimization of the thickness of Terfenol layer

Initially, the thickness of the 1st layer (Terfenol-D) is optimized by changing d1 from 1.5 × 10−4 m to 2.5 × 10−4 m, while maintaining the values of d2 = 2 × 10−4 m, and N = 8. Figure 5 demonstrates the analysis of sensitivity relative to various values of d1. The results confirm that by increasing the thickness of d1, the sensitivity increases up to d1 = 2 × 10−4 m, after which it starts to decrease. The reason for decreasing in sensitivity at greater thickness could be due to that the waves confine more strongly within the increase of the layer’s thickness which in turns reduces the interaction with adjacent layers. Simulation outcomes revealed that a maximum sensitivity of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$4.154\times {10}^{-3}$$\end{document} MHz/Oe is attained at d1 = 2 × 10−4 m, which we select as the optimal thickness value in our sensor design.Fig. 5illustration of the sensitivity relative to different thicknesses of Terfenol layer.

illustration of the sensitivity relative to different thicknesses of Terfenol layer.

Numerical analysis of sensing parameters

Here, we have introduced some performing parameters that could be effectively control the performance of the designed PnC magnetic field sensor. Meanwhile, we have some key parameters such as the sensitivity (S), Figure of Merit (FOM), Detection Limit (DL), Sensor Resolution (SR), and Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) to simulate the overall performance of the designed sensor. Notably, these parameters are critical for estimating the sensor’s effectiveness. In this regard, the sensitivity refers to the ratio of the change in the width of the PnBG (Δw) to the change in the intensity of magnetic field (ΔH), which can be mathematically expressed as42,24\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\text{S}} = \frac{{{\Delta w}}}{{{\Delta H}}}$$\end{document}

Other key sensing parameters such as, the SNR and SR are also computed as they reflect the sensor’s ability to effectively detect a small change in the PnBG width versus the slight variation in the intensity of external magnetic field. DL is the smallest possible intensity of magnetic field which can be identified through a specific analytic technique. While the value of DL decreases, the sensor becomes more effective, allowing it to detect extremely low intensity of magnetic field. where, FWHM refers to the full width at half maximum of the band gap. From Fig. 11a, it is seen that as the value of H rises from 0 to 10 Oe, 20 Oe, 30 Oe, 40 Oe and 50 Oe, the sensitivity and FoM decrease, except at H = 40 Oe where the sensitivity and the FoM increase, and later follow the decrement trend. It is also important to note that the phononic band gap shown in Fig. 12 should be regarded as valid only when the reflectance exceeds 90%, ensuring that the gap is physically significant and not an artifact of weak transmission suppression.

This wide band gap plays a central role in enabling the formation of Fano modes. From a physical perspective, these modes emerge due to the interference between broad (continuum-like) bands and narrow (discrete) bands within the same crystal structure. Such interactions give rise to asymmetric resonance line shapes, characteristic of Fano resonances. When alcohol particularly propanol, which possesses relatively low acoustic properties is introduced into the system, localized evanescent modes are generated. These modes strongly couple with the broad pass band, thereby enhancing the propagation of Fano modes and enriching the overall wave dynamics of the structure.

Conclusions

This study presents a novel approach to magnetic field sensing based on the integration of bulk acoustic wave devices with PnC structures. The proposed PnC configuration consists of a periodically repeated unit cell of {Terfenol/PMMA}N, which substantially enhances the confinement and control of acoustic waves, resulting in improved sensor’s sensitivity and performance. In the proposed structure, the Terfenol-D layer serves as a magneto-sensitive element, where alteration in the external magnetic field induce measurable changes in acoustic impedance. Numerical Simulations based on the transfer matrix method (TMM) revealed a strong interaction between applied magnetic field and the acoustic wave properties, thereby confirming the sensor’s ability to detect subtle changes in magnetic field intensity. The mainstay of this research is to study and examine the shift in the phononic bandgap behavior under varying magnetic field intensities. To achieve maximum sensitivity, various geometrical parameters including the thickness of the individual layers and the period of the structure were optimized. Meanwhile, the optimal sensor performance was achieved at parameters of d1 = 2 × 10−4 m, and d2 = 1 × 10−4 m and N = 4 layers, yielding a sensitivity of 5650 Hz/Oe, a figure of merit of 3.248 × 10−3, a detection limit of 914 × 103, a sensor resolution of 5164 × 106, and a signal to noise ratio of 0.142. Moreover, the structure exhibits a relative simplicity to fabricate, and it offers a significant potential for diverse applications in industrial automation, environmental monitoring, and biomedical diagnostics as well.

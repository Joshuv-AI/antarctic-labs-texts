Development and validation of a prediction model for acute kidney injury following cardiopulmonary bypass surgery
Authors: Jianfeng Zhao, Shi Fu, Mingjie Fu, Nannan Cui, Dabing Huang, Qinghui Fu, Zhitao Li, Shuiqiao Fu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAcute renal impairment following cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) is a serious perioperative complication with sustained occurrence. Proactive risk assessment and preventive strategies are crucial for enhancing surgical outcomes.MethodsThis prospective observational cohort study was conducted at a provincial tertiary hospital in Zhejiang Province, China, and involved 211 patients with heart disease who underwent CPB. Least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression and best-subsets regression (BSR) were used to identify predictive variables for post-CPB acute kidney injury (AKI). A nomogram was constructed based on the selected predictors, and the model performance was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic curves, calibration plots, decision curve analysis (DCA), and internal validation via bootstrap resampling.ResultsAmong the 211 patients, 20.4% (43/211) developed AKI. LASSO and BSR identified body mass index, intrarenal venous flow pattern, ventilation time, and CPB time as significant predictors. These four prognostic factors were integrated into a web-based nomogram. The model yielded an area under the curve of 0.825 (95% confidence interval: 0.748–0.902). DCA demonstrated clinical utility across threshold probabilities ranging from 2 to 98%, confirming robust predictive accuracy.ConclusionsWe developed and validated a nomogram-based model for predicting post-CPB AKI and demonstrated its strong discriminative performance and clinical applicability. This tool may assist clinicians in risk stratification and early intervention.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40001-025-03541-z.

Introduction

Acute kidney injury (AKI) is a common and severe complication following cardiac surgery, with reported incidence ranging from 5 to 42% [1, 2]. Despite recent significant advances in pharmacological therapies, surgical techniques, and medical technologies, the prevalence of AKI continues to rise [3]. Postoperative AKI critically impacts utilization of intensive care units, prolongs hospital stays, increases healthcare costs, and contributes to higher morbidity and mortality rates. Patients who develop AKI during surgery face an eightfold increased risk of progression to chronic kidney disease (CKD) [4]. Even mild AKI is associated with a 50% increased risk of mortality, underscoring the urgency of reliable predictive innovations [5]. Consequently, the early identification and prediction of AKI are pivotal for improving patient outcomes.

Multiple factors influence AKI development during cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) procedures, such as age, comorbidities, operative duration, hemodialysis requirements, and pharmacological agents [6, 7]. Extensive research efforts have identified novel biomarkers for AKI prediction, such as neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin (NGAL), kidney injury molecule-1 (KIM-1), uromodulin (UMOD), N-acetyl-β-D-glucosaminidase (NAG), interleukin-18 (IL-18), cystatin C, myoglobin, and serum follistatin-like 1 (FSTL1) [8–13]. Additionally, the preoperative urinary Dickkopf-3 is an independent predictor of postoperative AKI [14]. Compared to serum creatinine (Scr), these biomarkers can increase in the plasma and urine within hours of renal injury, demonstrating superior specificity and sensitivity. However, owing to limited large-scale clinical validation, their clinical utility remains uncertain, and most biomarkers are still restricted to research settings rather than clinical implementation. Contrastingly, conventional clinical parameters demonstrate greater practicality and accessibility.

In recent years, clinical indicators for predicting AKI have been explored. Traditional predictive models, such as the Cleveland Clinic Score [15], Mehta Score [16], and Simplified Renal Index (SRI) Score [17], are widely used in Europe and the United States to predict cardiac surgery-associated AKI. Nevertheless, these models rely predominantly on clinical data collected over a decade ago and are primarily derived from Caucasian populations, limiting their predictive accuracy in Asian cohorts. In Chinese populations, several AKI prediction models have been developed, including one for hospitalized patients and another focusing on postoperative AKI in patients who underwent CPB with normal preoperative renal function, both demonstrating robust predictive performance [18, 19]. Furthermore, multivariate regression models that integrate clinical characteristics and laboratory indices have been extensively used to enhance risk stratification and guide clinical interventions [20, 21]. Despite these advances, post-CPB AKI remains a complex and serious complication, and existing models show limited clinical utility and predictive accuracy.

With technological advancements and improved diagnostic capabilities, critical care ultrasonography has gained widespread adoption. Building on the established prediction frameworks, we incorporated a venous excess ultrasound grading system. Through the construction of a nomogram to visually stratify AKI risk in patients undergoing CPB, this approach enhances the capacity of clinicians for early risk identification, facilitates tiered management, and optimizes preventive and therapeutic strategies, thereby improving both short- and long-term patient prognoses.

Methods

Patient selection

This was a prospective observational cohort study conducted at the First Affiliated Hospital of Zhejiang University School of Medicine [22]. We collected clinical data from patients who underwent CPB-assisted cardiac surgery and were admitted to the surgical intensive care unit (SICU) between April 1, 2022, and December 30, 2022. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) aged ≥ 18 years and (2) patients undergoing CPB-assisted cardiac procedures. Exclusion criteria comprised the following: (1) suboptimal acoustic windows on ultrasonographic evaluation; (2) clinically significant postoperative complications (including but not limited to post-coronary artery bypass graft occlusion, cardiac tamponade, or hemorrhagic complications requiring surgical re-exploration); (3) baseline Scr > 106 μmol/L; (4) lack of clinical data. The protocol received ethical clearance from the Institutional Review Board (approval No. 2022IT905) in accordance with the ethical principles established by the Declaration of Helsinki, with a formal waiver of informed consent granted for this observational study. We adhered to the guidelines outlined in the Transparent Reporting of a Multivariable Prediction Model for Individual Prognosis or Diagnosis statement for reporting the development and validation of multivariable prediction models [23].

Variable collection

Building on our previous research framework, we incorporated supplementary hemodynamic parameters, including portal pulsatility fraction (PPF), hepatic vein flow (HVF) pattern, intrarenal venous flow (IRVF) pattern, inferior vena cava (IVC) diameter with respiratory variation, venous congestion ultrasound (VExUS) score [20, 24], European System for Cardiac Operative Risk Evaluation II score, and hemoglobin (Hb) decline ratio. Additionally, we systematically collected the following variables: (I) demographic profiles: age, sex, body mass index (BMI), and major comorbidities (hypertension, diabetes mellitus, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease); (II) personal history: smoking status and alcohol consumption patterns; (III) preoperative laboratory indices: Scr and Hb levels; (IV) intraoperative metrics: CPB duration; (V) postoperative parameters: Hb concentrations upon SICU admission.

Renal function was monitored for at least 7 days after surgery, with AKI classified according to the Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) guidelines. All ultrasound evaluations were jointly performed by two physicians who were blinded to the patient details within 3 h of admission to the SICU. Two physicians with varying levels of expertise (physician A: 2 years of general sonography; physician B: 5 years of vascular specialization) conducted all the assessments. Final reporting necessitated a consensus interpretation, and discrepancies were resolved through consultation with a senior sonographer with over 10 years of experience. Standardized protocols included IVC measurement, diameter at 2 cm distal to the hepatic vein confluence during quiet respiration, hepatic/portal venous evaluation, Doppler waveforms obtained between the midaxillary and posterior axillary lines, intrarenal venous assessment, and longitudinal renal views via caudal probe angulation to visualize the interlobar veins. Venous congestion stratification followed the Beaubien–Souligny’s VExUS grading system [24]: Grade 1: normal/mildly abnormal HVF, portal vein flow, and IRVF; Grade 2: severely abnormal flow in ≥ 1 venous system; Grade 3: severely abnormal flow in ≥ 2 venous systems.

Outcome and definition

The primary outcome of the study was the development of AKI within 7 days of CPB surgery. According to the KDIGO [23], AKI is defined as follows: an increase in Scr of 0.3 mg/dL (26.5 μmol/L) within 48 h, Scr increased to 1.5 times the baseline level within 7 days, or urine volume < 0.5 mL/kg/h for 6 h. Scr levels were measured to ascertain AKI presence. Baseline Scr concentration was established as the latest value obtained within 7 days before surgery.

Statistical analysis

The case data and laboratory indicators collected in this study were all routine SICU monitoring indicators, and the data collection quality was strictly controlled by three researchers during the implementation process. All variables included in the analysis had no missing data. We utilized the LASSO regression model to perform variable selection on all 24 candidate variables collected. A penalty parameter (λ) that minimizes the binomial deviance will be selected through tenfold cross-validation, retaining only those variables with non-zero coefficients after shrinkage. All analyses were performed using R Statistical Software (version 4.2.2; http://www.R-project.org, The R Foundation) and the Free Statistics analysis platform (version 2.2; Beijing, China, http://www.clinicalscientits.cn/freestatistics). For all analyses, a two-sided p-value < 0.05 was deemed statistically significant. The creation and validation of the AKI prediction nomogram involved a multistep analytical process and initial screening. LASSO regression was used to identify potential prognostic factors. Model refinement: best-subset regression was applied to enhance the selection of predictors. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve evaluation: the best variable sets for the nomogram were chosen by comparing the area under the curve (AUC). Graphical representation: the selected predictors were incorporated into a multivariate logistic regression model, which was visually depicted in a clinical nomogram. Assessment of model performance: discrimination metrics, including ROC analysis and the concordance index (C-index), were performed. Calibration assessment: the observed outcomes were compared with the predicted probabilities using calibration curves. Internal validation: bootstrap resampling was performed 100 times to compute the adjusted ROC, C-index, and calibration metrics. Additional robustness checks were performed using k-fold cross-validation to evaluate the generalizability of the model.

Results

Between April 1 and December 30, 2022, 271 patients who underwent CPB-assisted cardiac surgery were screened for eligibility. Ultimately, 211 were enrolled in this study. Table 1 presents a comprehensive comparative analysis of the clinical characteristics of 211 patients who underwent CPB stratified by AKI occurrence. The study cohort exhibited a median age of 57.9 ± 12.8 years, with male predominance (60.2%, 127/211). Statistically significant intergroup disparities (p < 0.05) were identified in the following parameters: BMI, central venous pressure (CVP), portal vein flow/HVF/IRVF, preoperative Scr level, mechanical ventilation time, oxygenation index, CPB time, Hb decline percentage, postoperative Hb levels, VExUS grading, and incidence of acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS). Patients with AKI exhibited significantly higher BMI, preoperative New York Heart Association classification scores, and baseline Scr levels; prolonged CPB time; elevated postoperative CVP; advanced PPF, HVF, and IRVF; higher VExUS scores; extended mechanical ventilation requirements; and impaired oxygenation indices. No significant intergroup differences (p > 0.05) were observed in the demographic factors (age and sex) or comorbidities (hypertension, diabetes mellitus, and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease).

LASSO regression analysis identified six predictive variables from an initial pool of 24 candidates: BMI, mechanical ventilation time, CPB time, IRVF, oxygenation index, and ARDS severity (Fig. 2A, 2B). Subsequent best-subset regression analysis confirmed four key predictors: BMI, CPB time, mechanical ventilation time, and IRVF (Fig. 3A). ROC curves were constructed for predictors selected using each method. The four prognostic determinants identified through optimal subset regression analysis were incorporated into the predictive nomogram (Fig. 4A). The traditional nomogram converts regression coefficients into a 0- to 260-point scale, enabling bedside risk quantification. For example, a total score of ≥ 180 corresponds to an AKI occurrence probability of ≥ 80% (supplementary material 1). The web-based nomogram for AKI was https://zhjfeng5238.shinyapps.io/Predictive_model_for_AKI_after_CPB/ (Fig. 4B). The model performance was rigorously evaluated using the AUC, C-index, and calibration metrics. Figure 5A demonstrates that the nomogram achieved an AUC of 0.825 (95% CI: 0.748–0.902) for predicting AKI incidence. Model calibration using the Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test revealed a Brier score of 0.112, with a sensitivity of 0.814, specificity of 0.756, and Youden’s index of 0.570. The model exhibited a C-index of 0.825, which was adjusted to 0.804 after bootstrap resampling validation (100 iterations). To further assess the predictive accuracy, leave-one-out cross-validation was implemented, yielding a refined C-index of 0.825 and a calibration slope of 1.0 (Fig. 5B). Discussion

This study developed and validated a nomogram model that incorporates the IRVF. This model predicts the risk of AKI following CPB surgery. Four independent predictors—BMI, CPB time, mechanical ventilation time, and IRVF—were identified through LASSO regression and best-subset regression analyses. Despite differences between the two methods, they consistently identified core predictive variables. The statistically comparable performance (ΔAUC = 0.04, p = 0.623) demonstrates that the parsimony of the predictive model can be achieved without sacrificing accuracy. The model demonstrated excellent predictive performance (AUC = 0.825) and calibration accuracy (Brier score = 0.112; Eₘₐₓ = 0.036), with DCA confirming significant net clinical benefit within the 2–98% threshold range. This study innovatively integrates the hemodynamic parameter IRVF into the CPB-AKI prediction model. The strong association between IRVF and AKI risk (odds ratio [OR] = 3.68, 95% CI: 1.56–8.7) underscores the critical role of renal venous congestion in postoperative AKI.

The model exhibited predictive efficacy comparable to that of traditional clinical scoring systems. Although the Cleveland score achieved an AUC of approximately 0.81 in European populations [25], its applicability in Asian patients remains limited. A clinical scoring system for post-cardiac surgery in Southeast Asian populations reported AUC = 0.70 (95% CI: 0.68–0.72) [26]. Existing CPB-AKI prediction models for Chinese populations (AUC = 0.77–0.81) predominantly use preoperative static parameters [6, 27]. We innovatively integrated the intraoperative dynamic parameters (CPB time) using postoperative real-time ultrasound metrics (IRVF). This approach extended the predictive window to cover the entire perioperative period. Compared to biomarker-dependent models [28, 29], our approach achieved equivalent precision (AUC > 0.8) using routinely available clinical parameters while enabling immediate bedside application.

Obesity is a well-established risk factor for CKD, but few studies have evaluated BMI as an AKI determinant [30, 31]. This investigation revealed a linear correlation between BMI and AKI risk. Obesity causes hemodynamic changes, such as increased renal plasma flow and glomerular filtration rate, which may lead to hyperfiltration syndrome and renal vulnerability [32]. Additionally, obesity-associated complications, such as hypoventilation, sleep apnea, and pulmonary hypertension, promote sodium retention and peripheral venous congestion [32–34]. Peripheral venous congestion elevates renal venous pressure and reduces urine output [35, 36]. Critically ill obese patients face an increased risk of intra-abdominal pressure, potentially exacerbating venous congestion and arterial hypoperfusion [37]. Cardiac remodeling, including left ventricular hypertrophy and myocardial infiltration, further compromises renal perfusion in this population [38].

IRVF, a key indicator of venous congestion pathophysiology, indicates that elevated interstitial hydrostatic pressure directly compresses the renal tubules, obstructed lymphatic drainage promotes inflammatory mediator accumulation, and medullary hypoxia worsens ischemia–reperfusion injury [39]. Renal venous congestion may alter the intrarenal blood flow distribution through two mechanisms. These include right atrial pressure transmission and interstitial edema-induced renal compression, with the latter being the predominant mechanism [40]. Significantly elevated renal venous pressure impairs peritubular capillary flow and glomerular ultrafiltration gradients [41]. Given that PPF and HVF inadequately reflect renal compressive states, IRVF likely offers a superior predictive value for congestion-induced AKI [42].

In a study of patients with acute aortic dissection, a linear relationship was observed between CPB time and the incidence of AKI. Each additional 10 min of CPB increased the risk of postoperative AKI by 17.1% [43]. The pathogenesis of CPB-associated AKI involves several mechanisms, including hypoperfusion, impaired oxygenation, inflammatory activation, oxidative stress, coagulopathy, hemolysis, and endothelial injury [44]. Potential preventive strategies include precision-adjusted pump flow rates guided by goal-directed perfusion protocols [45] and the application of biocompatible circuit coatings to mitigate inflammation and thrombosis.

Currently, the causal relationship between the mechanical ventilation duration and AKI remains unclear. Prolonged mechanical ventilation has been associated with an increased incidence of AKI [46]. However, other studies have suggested that the occurrence of AKI may lead to an extended duration of mechanical ventilation and a longer ICU stay [47]. Mechanical ventilation induces hemodynamic changes by impairing venous return and reducing cardiac output. Elevated positive end-expiratory pressure further increases the intrathoracic pressure, which is transmitted to the abdominal compartments through the venous system, thereby exacerbating visceral congestion, reducing renal blood flow, and lowering perfusion pressure [48]. The results of this study indicate that mechanical ventilation duration plays a predictive role in AKI.

This study has several strengths: (1) the pioneering incorporation of venous hemodynamic metrics into CPB-AKI prediction models, instead of previous arterial perfusion-focused approaches; (2) robust dual-stage variable selection (LASSO and optimal subset regression), which ensured model parsimony and stability; and (3) strengthened reliability through bootstrap resampling and leave-one-out cross-validation. Limitations include: (1) a single-center design, potentially limiting generalizability despite efforts to mitigate selection bias through strict inclusion criteria; (2) a modest sample size with reliance on secondary data analysis, where unregulated collection quality may introduce error; (3) operator-dependent ultrasound measurements, underscoring the need for standardized protocols; and (4) the absence of external validation: multicenter prospective studies are required to confirm the performance of the model in the future. Future studies should incorporate serial postoperative Doppler assessments to capture dynamic changes and combine emerging biomarkers to enhance sensitivity. Additionally, since the model was derived from Chinese adults, its applicability to other ethnic populations, patients with congenital heart disease, and pediatric or neonatal CPB remains to be confirmed. Validation will require large-scale, population-based prospective cohort studies conducted in collaboration with international pediatric cardiac centers and multicenter research networks.

Conclusion

This study integrated IRVF into the risk-prediction model for AKI following CPB surgery, and internal validation showed good predictive performance. However, multicenter, large-scale prospective cohort studies are needed to improve the model’s generalizability. Consequently, clinicians should apply the model with caution, in combination with individual clinical judgment.

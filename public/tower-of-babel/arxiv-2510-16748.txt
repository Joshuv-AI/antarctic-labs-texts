The Dirichlet eigenvalue problems for some concave elliptic Hessian operators
Jiaogen Zhang

Abstract
In this manuscript, we investigate a priori estimates for the solution to the Dirichlet eigenvalue problem for a broad class of concave elliptic Hessian operators of the form F ⁡ ( D 2 ​ u ) = − Λ ​ u in ​ Ω , u = 0 on ​ ∂ Ω . F(D^{2}u)=-\Lambda u\quad\textrm{in}\,\Omega,\qquad u=0\quad\textrm{on}\,\partial\Omega. These operators encompass the Monge-Ampère operator, the k k -Hessian operators, and the p p -Monge-Ampère operators. We impose a fairly mild constraint on the operator F F , allowing us to demonstrate the existence of the first nonzero eigenvalue and its corresponding Γ \Gamma -admissible eigenfunction on the smooth, strictly Γ \Gamma -convex domain Ω ⊂ ℝ n \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} . Furthermore, we prove that the eigenfunction u 1 u_{1} belongs to C ∞ ​ ( Ω ) ∩ C 1 , 1 ​ ( Ω ¯ ) C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap C^{1,1}(\overline{\Omega}) . As an application, we prove that every invariant Gårding-Dirichlet operator admits a unique first nonzero eigenvalue. Finally, a bifurcation-type theory for these operators is also established.

Introduction

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(for
n\geq 2
) be an open
convex invariant cone
, and let
f:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a
concave elliptic Hessian operator
(as defined in Definitions
2.1
and
2.3
). We define the corresponding set of real symmetric
n\times n
matrices characterizing as

\mathscr{M}(\Gamma)=\{A\in\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\,|\,\lambda(A)\in\Gamma\}

where
\lambda(A)
denotes the eigenvalue vector of
A
. For an arbitrary bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, a smooth function
u
in
\Omega
for which
D^{2}u(x)\in\mathscr{M}(\Gamma)
for all
x\in\Omega
is said to be
\Gamma
-admissible. For notational convenience, we set

F(A)=f(\lambda(A))\,,\qquad A\in\mathscr{M}(\Gamma).

Our main objective is to establish the existence and regularity of a
\Gamma
-admissible eigenpair
(u_{1},\Lambda_{1})

satisfying the following Dirichlet eigenvalue problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\begin{matrix}\begin{aligned} F(D^{2}u)=&\,-\Lambda u\\
u=&\,0\end{aligned}&\begin{aligned} \quad&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
\quad&\mbox{on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\end{matrix}\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
D^{2}u
denotes the Hessian matrix of
u
. A notable characteristic of this equation is its degeneracy near the boundary, as the right-hand side tends to zero.

Fully nonlinear Dirichlet eigenvalue problems form a deep and interdisciplinary area, bridging fully nonlinear PDE theory, spectral geometry, and the calculus of variations. Their complexity stems fundamentally from the nonlinearity of the operator
F
—a phenomenon with no analogue in the classical spectral theory of the Laplacian. Furthermore, the Dirichlet eigenvalue problem for such operators reveals deep geometric content through its connection to the Brunn-Minkowski inequality in convex geometry. For further reading, we refer to
[
4
,
23
,
28
]
, among other works.

The bibliography related to the Dirichlet eigenvalue problem for fully nonlinear second order operators is very wide. A canonical example is the Dirichlet eigenvalue problem for the Monge-Ampère operator. This problem consists in finding a nontrivial pair
(u_{1},\Lambda_{1})\in C(\overline{\Omega})\times\mathbb{R}
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\begin{matrix}\begin{aligned} \det(D^{2}u)=&\,(-\Lambda u)^{n}\\
u=&\,0\end{aligned}&\begin{aligned} \quad&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
\quad&\mbox{on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\end{matrix}\end{array}\right.

A landmark systematic study of this problem was undertaken by Lions
[
22
]
, who established the existence of the first eigenvalue
\Lambda_{1}
, proved the regularity
u_{1}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap C^{1,1}(\overline{\Omega})
for the corresponding eigenfunction, and provided a variational characterization of
\Lambda_{1}
. Subsequent contributions by Tso
[
30
]
, Hong–Huang–Wang
[
17
]
, Le–Savin
[
26
]
, and Le
[
24
,
25
]
established existence, uniqueness, and geometric regularity properties under appropriate convexity constraints.

Wang’s seminal work
[
36
]
extended the eigenvalue framework to the
k
-Hessian operators

\sigma_{k}(D^{2}u)=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\lambda_{i_{2}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}}\,,\qquad\lambda=\lambda(D^{2}u)\,,

unifying Monge-Ampère theory with a broader class of fully nonlinear elliptic operators. Recent progress includes the study of the
p
-Monge-Ampère operators

\mathcal{M}_{p}(D^{2}u)=\prod_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{p}\leq n}(\lambda_{i_{1}}+\lambda_{i_{2}}+\cdots+\lambda_{i_{p}})\,,\qquad\lambda=\lambda(D^{2}u)\,,

with the critical case

resolved in
[
15
,
27
]
, and completion of all integer cases

by the author and Zheng
[
38
]
.

We now describe the several fundamental structures for
f
. Let
\Gamma
be a convex invariant cone with vertex at the origin satisfying

\Gamma_{n}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\lambda_{i}>0,\,i=1,\cdots,n\}\subset\Gamma\subset\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}>0\}=\Gamma_{1}\,.

We shall assume further that
f
satisfies the following condition on
\Gamma
.

Definition 1.1
.

We say an elliptic operator
f:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfies the Condition
(T)
if there exists a uniform constant
\mathscr{T}>0
such that

\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\geq{\mathscr{T}}\,.

(1.2)

Heuristically, Condition
(T)
allows us exploit an established upper bound for
\{\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{n}
to derive a lower bound, thereby providing two-sided control. This type of argument is common in a priori estimates when the maximum principle is applied to the linearized operator. We note that this hypothesis is also employed in the recent study by Guo-Phong
[
14

, which investigates

2.1
Convex invariant cones and concave elliptic Hessian operators

Definition 2.1
.

An open cone
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be invariant if the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

\Gamma+\Gamma_{n}\subset\Gamma
.

(ii)

\Gamma
remains unchanged under permutations of its coordinates.

Additionally, if
\Gamma
is convex, it is referred to as a convex invariant cone.

The following is the concept of a (strictly)
\Gamma
-convex cone that is associated with such cones.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\Omega
be an open bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. We say
\Omega
is strictly
\Gamma
-convex if there exists a large number
R
such that at every point
x\in\partial\Omega

(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n-1},R)\in\Gamma\,,

where
(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n-1})
are the principle curvatures of
\partial\Omega
at
x
.

We now introduce the terminology of concave elliptic Hessian operators.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a convex invariant cone. The map
F:\mathscr{M}(\Gamma)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}

is referred to as a
concave elliptic Hessian operator
if the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

F
is Hessian, i.e. there exists a
C^{2}
smooth function
f:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

F(A)=f(\lambda_{1}(A),\cdots,\lambda_{n}(A)),

where
\lambda_{i}(A)
denotes the
i
-th eigenvalue of
A
.

(ii)

F
is strictly elliptic, i.e.
f_{i}(\lambda):=\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{i}}>0
for every
i
.

(iii)

F
is concave, i.e. the matrix
\big(\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial\lambda_{i}\partial\lambda_{j}}\big)
is positive semi-definite.

(iv)

f(\lambda)>0
for all
\lambda\in\Gamma
and
f(1,\cdots,1)=1
.

(v)

F
is homogeneous of degree one, i.e.
f(t\lambda)=tf(\lambda)
for every
t>0
and for all
\lambda\in\Gamma
.

It is noteworthy that the properties (i)–(v) are common to all examples presented in Remark
1.3
. The following lemma establishes that Condition
(T)
is satisfied by every invariant Gårding-Dirichlet operator.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
f:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a concave elliptic operator. Assume that there exists a constant
c>0
such that

f(\lambda+\tau)-f(\lambda)\geq c(\tau_{1}\cdots\tau_{n})^{\frac{1}{n}},\qquad\text{for all }(\lambda,\tau)\in\Gamma\times\Gamma_{n}.

(2.1)

Then there exists a constant
c^{\prime}>0
such that

\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\lambda)\geq c^{\prime},\qquad\text{for all }\lambda\in\Gamma.

(2.2)

Moreover, the converse also holds.

Proof.

We first show that (
2.1
) implies (
2.2
). By the concavity of
f
together with inequality (
2.1
), we have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\lambda)\tau_{i}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{1}f_{i}(\lambda+s\tau)\tau_{i}ds=f(\lambda+\tau)-f(\lambda)\geq c(\tau_{1}\cdots\tau_{n})^{\frac{1}{n}}\,.

Choosing
\tau_{i}=\frac{1}{f_{i}(\lambda)}
for each
i=1,2,\cdots,n
, we obtain

n\geq c\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\lambda)\Big)^{-\frac{1}{n}}\,.

Rearranging this inequality yields (
2.2
) with
c^{\prime}=(c/n)^{n}
.

Next, we prove that (
2.2
) implies (
2.1
). Using the integral mean value formula, there exists some
s_{0}\in[0,1]
such that

f(\lambda+\tau)-f(\lambda)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{1}f_{i}(\lambda+s\tau)\tau_{i}ds=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\lambda+s_{0}\tau)\tau_{i}\,.

Since each term in the sum is nonnegative (as ensured by the properties of ellipticity and
\tau\in\Gamma_{n}
), it follows that

f(\lambda+\tau)-f(\lambda)\geq f_{i}(\lambda+s_{0}\tau)\tau_{i}\qquad\textrm{ for all }i=1,\cdots,n.

Multiplying these inequalities over
i
, we obtain

\Big(f(\lambda+\tau)-f(\lambda)\Big)^{n}\geq(\tau_{1}\cdots\tau_{n})\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\lambda+s_{0}\tau)\,.

Since
\lambda+s_{0}\tau\in\Gamma
, we may apply (
2.2
) to the product on the right, giving

\Big(f(\lambda+\tau)-f(\lambda)\Big)^{n}\geq c^{\prime}(\tau_{1}\cdots\tau_{n})\,.

Taking the
n
-th root on both sides shows that (
2.1
) holds with
c=(c^{\prime})^{1/n}
.

x_{0}
, we have

0=Q_{i}=\frac{1}{\beta}u_{ii}u_{i}+\varphi^{\prime}u_{i}+ax_{i}

(3.3)

for each
i=1,\cdots,n
as well as

\begin{split}0\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}Q_{ii}=\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{i,k=1}^{n}F^{ii}u_{kii}u_{k}&+\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}u^{2}_{ii}-\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}u_{i}^{2}u^{2}_{ii}\\
&+\varphi^{\prime}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}u_{ii}+\varphi^{\prime\prime}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}u^{2}_{i}+a\mathcal{F}\,.\end{split}

Owing to the monotone increasing property of
\varphi
, then by utilizing (
2.4
)–(
2.5
) along with the inequality (
3.3
), we can infer that

\begin{split}0&\geq\frac{\langle D_{x}\psi,Du\rangle+|Du|^{2}D_{u}\psi}{\beta}+\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}u^{2}_{ii}-\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}u_{i}^{2}u^{2}_{ii}+\varphi^{\prime\prime}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}u^{2}_{i}+\varphi^{\prime}\psi(x,u)+a\mathcal{F}\\
&\geq-\frac{|D_{x}\psi|}{\sqrt{\beta}}-|D_{u}\psi|-\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}(\varphi^{\prime}u_{i}+ax_{i})^{2}+\varphi^{\prime\prime}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}u^{2}_{i}+a\mathcal{F}\\
&\geq-\frac{|D_{x}\psi|}{\sqrt{\beta}}-|D_{u}\psi|+\big(\varphi^{\prime\prime}-2(\varphi^{\prime})^{2}\big)\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}u_{i}^{2}-2a^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}x_{i}^{2}+a\mathcal{F}\\
&\geq-\frac{|D_{x}\psi|}{\sqrt{\beta}}-|D_{u}\psi|+\frac{1}{9(1+2C_{0})^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}u_{i}^{2}+\big(a-2a^{2}(diam\,\Omega)^{2}\big)\mathcal{F}\,,\end{split}

(3.4)

where we have used the inequality
(x+y)^{2}\leq 2x^{2}+2y^{2}
in the third line. Select a constant
a>0
small enough such that
2a(diam\,\Omega)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}
. Thus, we conclude from (
3.4
) that at
x_{0}

\frac{1}{9(1+2C_{0})^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}u_{i}^{2}+\frac{a}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}\leq\frac{|D_{x}\psi|}{\sqrt{\beta}}+|D_{u}\psi|\leq C^{\prime}

(3.5)

for some uniform constant
C^{\prime}
provided that
\beta(x_{0})\gg 1
.
Since each
F^{ii}
is bounded above by
\frac{2C^{\prime}}{a}
, and Condition
(T)
implies the lower bound

F^{ii}\geq\mathscr{T}\big(\frac{a}{2C^{\prime}}\big)^{n-1}\qquad\text{for all }i=1,\cdots,n.

Substituting the lower bound into (
3.5
) yields

\frac{\mathscr{T}}{9(1+2C_{0})^{2}}\big(\frac{a}{2C^{\prime}}\big)^{n-1}|Du|^{2}\leq C^{\prime}.

Consequently, we deduce the required estimate for
|Du|(x_{0})
, thus concluding the proof.
∎

4
Second order estimates

The objective of this section is to establish the second order estimates. To do so, we first reduce the global second order estimate to a corresponding boundary estimate. It then suffices to prove the second order estimates at each boundary point.

4.1
Global second order estimate

In this subsection, we will prove the following global second order estimate:

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose
\Omega
is a bounded and strictly
\Gamma
-convex domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with smooth boundary. Let
u\in C^{4}(\Omega)\cap C^{1,1}(\overline{\Omega})
be a
\Gamma
-admissible solution to the Dirichlet problem (
1.4
). Then there exists a constant
C_{2}
depends on
\Omega,

C_{0}
,
\sup_{\overline{\Omega}\times[-C_{0},0]}\psi
,
\|D_{x}\psi\|_{C^{1}}
and
\|D_{u}\psi\|_{C^{1}}
such that

\sup_{\overline{\Omega}}|D^{2}u|\leq C_{2}\Big(1+\sup_{\partial\Omega}|D^{2}u|\Big)\,.

(4.1)

Proof.

Let
U
denote the matrix
D^{2}u
for simplicity. We consider the auxiliary function

Q=\log\lambda_{1}(U)+\varphi\big(\frac{1}{2}|Du|^{2}\big)+\frac{1}{2}|x|^{2}

on an open subset
\Omega_{+}:=\{x\in\Omega:\lambda_{1}(U)(x)>0\}
, where
\lambda_{1}(U)(x)
is the largest eigenvalue of
U
at
x
, and the function
\varphi(t)
is given by

\varphi(t)=-\frac{1}{2}\log(2K-t),\qquad K=1+\sup_{\overline{\Omega}}|Du|^{2}.

We may assume that
\Omega_{+}
is nonempty; otherwise, we have already done. A direct calculation yields the following:

\varphi^{\prime\prime}=2(\varphi^{\prime})^{2},\qquad\frac{1}{4K}\leq\varphi^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{2K}.

(4.2)

Assume that
Q
achieves its maximum at a point
x_{0}\in\Omega
. We remark that
Q
may not be twice differentiable at
x_{0}
if the eigenspace of
\lambda_{1}(U)
has a dimension greater than
1
. To overcome this difficulty, we will apply a standard perturbation argument.

More precisely, let us define a new matrix
\hat{U}=(\hat{U}_{ij})
by

\hat{U}_{ij}:=u_{ij}-(\delta_{ij}-\delta_{1i}\delta_{1j})

(4.3)

such that

\lambda_{1}(\hat{U})\leq\lambda_{1}(U)\qquad\text{ in }\,\Omega,

with equality holds only at
x_{0}
. We may further order the eigenvalues of
\hat{U}
by
\lambda_{1}(\hat{U})\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}(\hat{U})
and then
\lambda_{1}(\hat{U})>\lambda_{2}(\hat{U})
at the considered point. Hence, the new auxiliary function

\hat{Q}:=\log\lambda_{1}(\hat{U})+\varphi\big(\frac{1}{2}|Du|^{2}\big)+\frac{1}{2}|x|^{2}

is twice differentiable pointwise within
\Omega_{+}
. Moreover, the point
x_{0}
where the maximum of
Q
is attained turns out to be a maximum point for the function
\hat{Q}
. Below it remains to prove that

\lambda_{1}(\hat{U})(x_{0})\leq C_{2}

(4.4)

for a certain positive constant
C_{2}
as in (
4.1
).

Rotating the coordinates
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}
, we may assume that
\hat{U}
is diagonal at
x_{0}
. From the maximum principle, it follows that at
x_{0}
:

0=\hat{Q}_{i}=\frac{\lambda_{1,i}}{\lambda_{1}}+\varphi^{\prime}u_{ii}u_{i}+x_{i}

as well as

\begin{split}0\geq&\quad\frac{\lambda_{1,ii}}{\lambda_{1}}-\Big(\frac{\lambda_{1,i}}{\lambda_{1}}\Big)^{2}+\varphi^{\prime\prime}u^{2}_{ii}u^{2}_{i}+\varphi^{\prime}\Big(u^{2}_{ii}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}u_{jii}u_{j}\Big)+1.\end{split}

The following formulas are well-known in
[
29
]
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\lambda_{1}}{\partial x_{i}}(x_{0})=\frac{\partial u_{11}}{\partial x_{i}}(x_{0})\,;

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\lambda_{1}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}(x_{0})=\frac{\partial u_{11}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}(x_{0})+2\sum_{k=2}^{n}\frac{u_{1ki}u_{1kj}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{k}+1}\,.

We therefore obtain

\frac{u_{11i}}{u_{11}}=-\varphi^{\prime}u_{ii}u_{i}-x_{i}

(4.5)

for each
i=1,\cdots,n
and

\begin{split}0\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}\Big[\frac{u_{11ii}}{u_{11}}&+2\sum_{k=2}^{n}\frac{u^{2}_{1ki}}{u_{11}(u_{11}-u_{kk}+1)}-\frac{u^{2}_{11i}}{u^{2}_{11}}\Big]\\
&+\varphi^{\prime\prime}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}u^{2}_{ii}u^{2}_{i}+\varphi^{\prime}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}\Big[u^{2}_{ii}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}u_{jii}u_{j}\Big]+\mathcal{F}\,.\end{split}

(4.6)

Applying the formula (
2.6
) for
t=1
, we get

\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}u_{11ii}+\sum_{i,j,k,l}F^{ij,kl}u_{1ij}u_{1kl}=\psi_{x_{1}x_{1}}+\psi_{ux_{1}}u_{1}+\psi_{uu}u_{1}^{2}+\psi_{u}u_{11}\geq-Cu_{11}

(4.7)

provided that
u_{11}\geq 1
is large enough. While the second term on the left-hand reads

\begin{split}\sum_{i,j,k,l=1}^{n}F^{ij,kl}u_{1ij}u_{1kl}&=2\sum_{1\leq l<k\leq n}F^{lk,kl}u^{2}_{1kl}+\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}F^{kk,ll}u_{1kk}u_{1ll}\leq 2\sum_{k=2}^{n}F^{1k,k1}u^{2}_{11k}\,.\end{split}

It then follows from (
4.7
) that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}\frac{u_{11ii}}{u_{11}}\geq-2\sum_{k=2}^{n}F^{1k,k1}\frac{u^{2}_{11k}}{u_{11}}-C\,.

(4.8)

Observe that Proposition
3.1
implies that

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}F^{ii}u_{jii}u_{j}=\langle D_{x}\psi,Du\rangle+D_{u}\psi|Du|^{2}\geq-C\,.

(4.9)

Plugging the inequalities (
4.8
) and (
4.9
) into (
4.6
), we therefore obtain

\begin{split}C\geq\sum_{k=2}^{n}\Big[-2F^{1k,k1}\frac{u^{2}_{11k}}{u_{11}}&+2F^{11}\frac{u^{2}_{11k}}{u_{11}(u_{11}-u_{kk}+1)}\Big]-\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}\frac{u^{2}_{11i}}{u^{2}_{11}}\\
&+\varphi^{\prime\prime}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}u^{2}_{ii}u^{2}_{i}+\varphi^{\prime}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}u^{2}_{ii}+\mathcal{F}\,.\end{split}

(4.10)

In what follows, we divide the proof of inequality (
4.4
) into two separate cases.

Case 1: Assume
u_{nn}<-\frac{1}{3}\,u_{11}
.
For the second term in (
4.10
), it follows from (
4.2
), (
4.5
) and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality that

\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}\frac{u^{2}_{11i}}{u^{2}_{11}}&\leq 2(\varphi^{\prime})^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}u^{2}_{ii}u^{2}_{i}+2\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}x_{i}^{2}\,.\end{split}

Inserting it into (
4.10
) and coupling with (
4.2
), we have

\begin{split}C\geq&\,\sum_{k=2}^{n}\Big[-2F^{1k,k1}\frac{u^{2}_{11k}}{u_{11}}+2F^{11}\frac{u^{2}_{11k}}{u_{11}(u_{11}-u_{kk}+1)}\Big]-2\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}x_{i}^{2}\\
&\qquad+\big(\varphi^{\prime\prime}-2(\varphi^{\prime})^{2}\big)\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}u^{2}_{ii}u^{2}_{i}+\varphi^{\prime}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}u^{2}_{ii}+\mathcal{F}\\
\geq&\,\varphi^{\prime}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}u^{2}_{ii}-2(diam\,\Omega)^{2}\mathcal{F}\,.\end{split}

It then follows from the assumption
u_{nn}<-\frac{1}{3}\,u_{11}
that

C\geq\varphi^{\prime}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}u^{2}_{ii}-2(diam\,\Omega)^{2}\mathcal{F}\geq\varphi^{\prime}F^{nn}u^{2}_{nn}-2(diam\,\Omega)^{2}\mathcal{F}\geq\frac{1}{9}\varphi^{\prime}u_{11}^{2}F^{nn}-2(diam\,\Omega)^{2}\mathcal{F}.

(4.11)

Given that
u_{11}\geq\cdots\geq u_{nn}
, we can deduce from a result in Ecker-Huisken
[
10
]
or Spruck
[
29
]
that
F^{11}\leq\cdots\leq F^{nn}
and hence
F^{nn}\geq\frac{1}{n}\mathcal{F}
. Observing that the Condition
(T)
and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality guarantee that
\mathcal{F}\geq n\mathscr{T}^{\frac{1}{n}}
, it follows from (
4.11
) that

C\geq\Big[\frac{1}{9n}\varphi^{\prime}u_{11}^{2}-2(diam\,\Omega)^{2}\Big]\mathcal{F}\geq\Big[\frac{u_{11}^{2}}{36nK}-2(diam\,\Omega)^{2}\Big]n\mathscr{T}^{\frac{1}{n}}\,.

This will enable us to derive the desired upper bound of
u_{11}(x_{0}).

Case 2: Assume
u_{nn}\geq-\frac{1}{3}\,u_{11}
.
Let us introduce an index set

I:=\big\{i\in\{2,\cdots,n\}\,|\,F^{ii}\geq 3F^{11}\big\}\,.

Then for each
i\notin I
, we have
F^{ii}<3F^{11}
. It follows that

\begin{split}\sum_{i\notin I}F^{ii}\frac{u^{2}_{11i}}{u^{2}_{11}}\leq&\,2(\varphi^{\prime})^{2}\sum_{i\notin I}F^{ii}u^{2}_{ii}u^{2}_{i}+2\sum_{i\notin I}F^{ii}x_{i}^{2}\leq\varphi^{\prime\prime}\sum_{i\notin I}F^{ii}u^{2}_{ii}u^{2}_{i}+6n(diam\,\Omega)^{2}F^{11}\,.\end{split}

Inserting it into (
4.10
), we arrive at

\begin{split}C\geq&\sum_{k=2}^{n}\Big[-2F^{1k,k1}\frac{u^{2}_{11k}}{u_{11}}+2F^{11}\frac{u^{2}_{11k}}{u_{11}(u_{11}-u_{kk}+1)}\Big]-\sum_{i\in I}F^{ii}\frac{u^{2}_{11i}}{u^{2}_{11}}\\
&+\varphi^{\prime\prime}\sum_{i\in I}F^{ii}u^{2}_{ii}u^{2}_{i}+\varphi^{\prime}F^{ii}u^{2}_{ii}+\mathcal{F}-6n(diam\,\Omega)^{2}F^{11}\\
\geq&\sum_{i\in I}\Big(-2F^{1i,i1}-\frac{F^{ii}}{u_{11}}\Big)\frac{u^{2}_{11i}}{u_{11}}+\varphi^{\prime}F^{11}u^{2}_{11}+\mathcal{F}-6n(diam\,\Omega)^{2}F^{11}\,.\end{split}

(4.12)

For each
i\in I
, we have
F^{11}\leq\frac{1}{3}F^{ii}
and
u_{ii}\geq u_{nn}\geq-\frac{1}{3}u_{11}
. It follows from (
2.7
) that

-2F^{1i,i1}=2\frac{F^{ii}-F^{11}}{u_{11}-u_{ii}}\geq\frac{F^{ii}}{u_{11}}\qquad\text{for all }\,i\in I\,.

(4.13)

Therefore, we can infer form (
4.2
), (
4.12
), and (
4.13
) that

C\geq\frac{1}{4K}F^{11}u^{2}_{11}+\mathcal{F}-6n(diam\,\Omega)^{2}F^{11}.

(4.14)

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\frac{1}{8K}u_{11}^{2}(x_{0})\geq 6n(diam\,\Omega)^{2}
. Indeed, if this condition fails to hold, our proof is already concluded. Consequently, (
4.14
) simplifies to

C\geq\frac{1}{8K}F^{11}u^{2}_{11}+\mathcal{F}.

Since each
F^{ii}
is bounded from above, according to Condition
(T)
,
F^{11}
is bounded from below and we obtain a uniform upper bound for
u_{11}(x_{0})
and thereby complete the proof of proposition.
∎

4.2
Boundary second order estimate

Proposition 4.2
.

Suppose
\Omega
is a bounded and strictly
\Gamma
-convex domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with smooth boundary. Let
u\in C^{4}(\Omega)\cap C^{1,1}(\overline{\Omega})
be a
\Gamma
-admissible solution to the Dirichlet problem (
1.4
). Then we have

\sup_{\partial\Omega}|D^{2}u|\leq C\,,

where
C
is a constant depends on
p,

\Omega,

C_{0}
,
\sup_{\overline{\Omega}\times[-C_{0},0]}\psi
,
\|D_{x}\psi\|_{C^{1}}
and
\|D_{u}\psi\|_{C^{1}}
.

For each given boundary point
x\in\partial\Omega
, after a translation and a rotation, we may assume that
x=0
is the origin and the boundary
\partial\Omega
can be represented locally in the same manner as (
3.1
). Below it suffices to show

|D^{2}u(0)|\leq C\,.

(4.15)

4.2.1
Double tangential estimates

Differentiating the boundary condition
u(x^{\prime},\rho(x^{\prime}))=0
on
\partial\Omega
twice along the tangential directions, we have

u_{\alpha\beta}(0)=-u_{n}\rho_{\alpha\beta}=-u_{n}\kappa_{\alpha}\delta_{\alpha\beta}\,\qquad\text{for all }\,\alpha,\beta\leq n-1,

(4.16)

where
(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n-1})
is the principle curvatures of
\partial\Omega
at the origin. This implies, using the gradient estimates, that:

\big|u_{\alpha\beta}(0)\big|\leq C\,\qquad\text{for all }\,\alpha,\beta\leq n-1\,.

(4.17)

Notice that the boundedness property of
\kappa_{\alpha}
is the only aspect we utilize here.

4.2.2
Tangential to normal estimates

To establish the estimate

|u_{\alpha n}(0)|\leq C\,\qquad\text{for all }\,\alpha\leq n-1,

(4.18)

it is sufficient to proceed as in the proof of Caffarelli-Nirenberg-Spruck
[
6
, Section 5]
, we include a sketch of proof here for completeness.

Let us define an elliptic operator

L=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}F^{ij}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}.

The following lemma, which is due to B. Guan
[
12
]
, will play a significant role in our proof.

Lemma 4.3
.

There exist some uniform positive constants
t
,
\delta
and
\epsilon
sufficiently small, and an
N
sufficiently large, such that the function

v=(u-\underline{u})+td-\frac{N}{2}d^{2}

satisfies
v\geq 0
in
\overline{\Omega}_{\delta}
and

Lv\leq-\epsilon-\epsilon\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}\qquad\text{ in }\ \Omega_{\delta}\,.

(4.19)

Proof.

First of all, it is easy to see
v=0
on
\partial\Omega\,\cap\,\Omega_{\delta}
. Then, we can shrink
\delta<2t/N
such that
v\geq 0
on
\Omega\,\cap\,\partial\Omega_{\delta}
after
t
and
N
being bounded. Therefore, we infer that
v\geq 0
in
\overline{\Omega}_{\delta}
.

Because
\underline{u}
is strictly
p
-plurisubharmonic, there exists a constant
\epsilon_{0}>0
dependent on
C_{0}
such that

(\underline{u}_{ij}-2\epsilon_{0}\delta_{ij})\in\mathcal{P}_{p}\,.

Notice that

Ld^{2}=2dLd+2\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}F^{ij}d_{i}d_{j}=2dLd+2F^{nn}

since
d_{j}=0
for all
j\leq n-1
, and
d_{n}=1
. It is also easy to verify that

-C_{d}\Big(1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}\Big)\leq Ld\leq C_{d}\Big(1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}\Big)

for a uniform positive constant
C_{d}
.
It follows from (
2.4
) that

\begin{split}Lv&=\sum_{i,j}F^{ij}(u_{ij}-\underline{u}_{ij})-(Nd-t)Ld-NF^{nn}\\
&\leq\sup_{\overline{\Omega}}\psi(x,u)+C_{d}(N\delta+t)+\big(C_{d}(N\delta+t)-2\epsilon_{0}\big)\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}-NF^{nn}\\
&\leq\sup_{\overline{\Omega}}\psi(x,u)+\epsilon_{0}-\Big(\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}+NF^{nn}\Big)-\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}\end{split}

(4.20)

provided that
C_{d}(N\delta+t)\leq 3C_{d}t<\epsilon_{0}
. By the Condition
(T)
and the arithmetic-geometric means inequality, we deduce that

\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}+NF^{nn}\geq n\Big(\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2}\Big)^{1-\frac{1}{n}}N^{\frac{1}{n}}\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}\Big)^{\frac{1}{n}}\geq n\Big(\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2}\Big)^{1-\frac{1}{n}}\mathscr{T}^{\frac{1}{n}}N^{\frac{1}{n}}\,.

We can choose
N
large enough to guarantee that

n\Big(\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2}\Big)^{1-\frac{1}{n}}\mathscr{T}^{\frac{1}{n}}N^{\frac{1}{n}}\geq\frac{3\epsilon_{0}}{2}+\sup_{\overline{\Omega}}\psi(x,u)\,.

By substituting this into (
4.20
) and choosing
\epsilon=\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2}
, we complete the proof of (
4.19
).
∎

With this result we will prove the inequality (
4.18
). Differentiating the equation
F(D^{2}u)=\psi(x,u)
once, we get that for each
k=1,2,\cdots,n
,

-C\Big(1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}\Big)\leq L(u_{k}-\underline{u}_{k})\leq C\Big(1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}\Big)\,.

Consider the auxiliary function given by

Q=\pm(u_{\alpha}-\underline{u}_{\alpha})+Av+B\rho^{2}\,\qquad\text{for all }\alpha<n\,,

where
A
and
B
are constants with
A\gg B\gg 1
, ensuring that
Q\geq 0
on the boundary
\partial\Omega_{\delta}
and
LQ\leq 0
in
\Omega_{\delta}
. According to the maximum principle, it can be deduced that
Q\geq 0
across the entire domain
\Omega_{\delta}
. Given that
Q(x_{0})=0
, the Hopf Lemma
[
9
, Lemma 6.4.2]
implies

Q_{n}(x_{0})\leq 0\,.

This provides us with the desired estimate (
4.18
).

4.2.3
Double normal estimates

Below we will derive the following double normal estimate

|u_{nn}(0)|\leq C\,.

(4.21)

Prior to proving this, let us revisit some valuable concepts from matrix theory: For each
A=(a_{ij})\in\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}(A)
arranged in ascending order, i.e.
\lambda_{1}(A)\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{n}(A)
, we denote the eigenvalues of
\tilde{A}=(a_{\alpha\beta})_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n-1}
by
\lambda^{\prime}_{\alpha}(\tilde{A})
. It follows from Cauchy’s interlace inequality
[
16
]
and
[
6
, Lemma 1.2]
that when
|a_{nn}|\rightarrow\infty

\begin{matrix}\begin{aligned} &\lambda_{\alpha}(A)\leq\lambda^{\prime}_{\alpha}(\tilde{A})\leq\lambda_{\alpha+1}(A)\\[5.69054pt]
&\lambda_{\alpha+1}(A)=\lambda^{\prime}_{\alpha}(\tilde{A})+o(1)\\[5.69054pt]
&a_{nn}\leq\lambda_{n}(A)\leq a_{nn}+O(1)\end{aligned}\end{matrix}

(4.22)

for all
1\leq\alpha\leq n-1
, where the
o(1)
and
O(1)
are uniform terms.

Next, we are proceed to prove (
4.21
). Without loss of generality, we assume that
u_{nn}(0)\gg 1
is sufficiently large; otherwise, there is nothing to prove. We claim that there are two uniform positive constants,
\hat{\delta}
and
R_{0}
, such that for each
R\geq R_{0}
and for each
x\in\partial\Omega
, we have

\big(\lambda^{\prime}(\tilde{U}),R\big)\in\Gamma\quad\textrm{and}\quad f\big(\lambda^{\prime}(\tilde{U}),R\big)\geq\psi(x,u)+\hat{\delta}.

To achieve this, we shall follow an approach proposed by Trudinger
[
32
]
and set

\delta_{R}:=\min_{x\in\partial\Omega}\big[f\big(\lambda^{\prime}(\tilde{U}),R\big)-\psi(x,u)\big]\,,\qquad\delta_{\infty}:=\liminf_{R\rightarrow\infty}\delta_{R}\,.

Subsequently, we are left with showing

\delta_{\infty}\geq\hat{\delta}

(4.23)

for some uniform constant
\hat{\delta}>0.

We can assume that
\delta_{\infty}
is finite; otherwise, our proof is complete. Suppose that
\delta_{\infty}
is attained at a point
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
. If necessary, after performing a rotation, we select a local orthonormal frame
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{n})
such that matrix
\tilde{U}
is diagonal at the point
x_{0}
. Let us denote

\Gamma_{\infty}:=\{\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\,|\,(\lambda^{\prime},R)\in\Gamma\,\textrm{ for some }\,R\}.

We will now proceed to divide the proof of (
4.23
) into two distinct cases.

Case 1:
Assume that
\underset{\lambda_{n}\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}f(\lambda^{\prime},\lambda_{n})=\infty
for each
\lambda^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{\infty}
. Based on (
4.17
) and (
4.18
), it can be deduced that
\lambda^{\prime}(\tilde{U}(x_{0}))
belongs to a compact set
\mathcal{D}
which is a subset of
\Gamma_{\infty}
.
Due to the compactness, there exist two constants,
\delta^{\prime},R^{\prime}>0
, which depend on
\lambda^{\prime}(\tilde{U}(x_{0}))
, such that

f\big(\lambda^{\prime}(\tilde{U}(x_{0})),R\big)\geq\psi(x_{0},u)+2\delta^{\prime}\qquad\text{for all }\,R\geq R^{\prime}.

By continuity, there exists an open cone
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}

with
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\subset\Gamma_{\infty}
such that

f(\lambda^{\prime},R)\geq\psi(x_{0},u)+\delta^{\prime}\qquad\text{for all }\,\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\,\textrm{ and }\,R\geq R^{\prime}.

(4.24)

Applying (
4.22
) to the matrix
D^{2}u(x_{0})
, there exists a sufficiently large constant
R^{\prime\prime}\geq R^{\prime}
such that if
u_{nn}(x_{0})\geq R^{\prime\prime}
, we have

\lambda_{n}(U(x_{0}))\geq u_{nn}(x_{0})\geq R^{\prime\prime}\geq R^{\prime}.

(4.25)

Shrinking
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
if necessary such that

\big(\lambda_{1}(U(x_{0})),\cdots,\lambda_{n-1}(U(x_{0}))\big)\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}.

(4.26)

From (
4.24
), (
4.25
), and (
4.26
), we deduce that

F(D^{2}u)(x_{0})\geq\psi(x_{0},u)+\delta^{\prime},

leading to a contradiction with (
1.4
). Consequently, we establish (
4.23
) by setting
\hat{\delta}=\delta^{\prime}
.

Case 2:
Assume that
\underset{\lambda_{n}\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}f(\lambda^{\prime},\lambda_{n})<\infty
for each
\lambda^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{\infty}
. We define a new function

\tilde{F}(E):=\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}f(\lambda^{\prime}(E),R)

on the set
S:=\{E\in\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})\,|\,\lambda^{\prime}(E)\in\Gamma_{\infty}\}
. Observe that
\tilde{F}
is both concave and finite, given that the operator
F
is concave and continuous. Consequently, there exists a symmetric
(n-1)\times(n-1)
matrix
(\tilde{F}^{\alpha\beta})
such that

\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{n-1}\tilde{F}^{\alpha\beta}(\tilde{U})\big(E_{\alpha\beta}-\tilde{U}_{\alpha\beta}\big)\geq\tilde{F}(E)-\tilde{F}(\tilde{U}),\qquad\text{ for all}\,E\in S.

(4.27)

Analogous to (
4.16
), one can derive

\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{n-1}\big(\tilde{\underline{U}}_{\alpha\beta}-\tilde{U}_{\alpha\beta}\big)=(u-\underline{u})_{x_{n}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{n-1}\kappa_{\alpha}\delta_{\alpha\beta}

at
x_{0}
. Substituting this into (
4.27
) results in

\begin{split}(u-\underline{u})_{x_{n}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n-1}\tilde{F}^{\alpha\alpha}\kappa_{\alpha}\geq&\,\tilde{F}[\tilde{\underline{U}}]-\tilde{F}[\tilde{U}]\\
=&\tilde{F}[\tilde{\underline{U}}]-\psi(x_{0},u)-\delta_{\infty}=\,\tilde{F}[\tilde{\underline{U}}]-\psi(x_{0},\underline{u})-\delta_{\infty}\\
\geq&\,\tilde{F}[\tilde{\underline{U}}]-F[\underline{U}]-\delta_{\infty}\\
\geq&2\epsilon_{0}-\delta_{\infty}\,.\end{split}

(4.28)

Here the second inequality follows from the condition
u=\underline{u}
on
\partial\Omega
. Additionally, the constant
\epsilon_{0}:=\frac{1}{2}\min_{\partial\Omega}(\tilde{F}[\tilde{\underline{U}}]-F[\underline{U}])
is strictly positive due to the ellipticity of
F
.

Without loss of generality, we may assume

(u-\underline{u})_{x_{n}}(x_{0})\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n-1}\tilde{F}^{\alpha\alpha}\kappa_{\alpha}(x_{0})\geq\epsilon_{0}.

(4.29)

If this were not the case, we can deduce from (
4.28
) that
\delta_{\infty}\geq\epsilon_{0}>0
, which would suffice to conclude the proof for this specific scenario. Henceforth, let us denote

\eta(x_{0})=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n-1}\tilde{F}^{\alpha\alpha}\kappa_{\alpha}(x_{0}).

Notice that
(u-\underline{u})_{x_{n}}(x_{0})\geq 0
and hence also strictly positive by virtue of (
4.29
). It follows that

\eta(x_{0})\geq\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2(u-\underline{u})_{x_{n}}(x_{0})}\geq 2\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{0}

(4.30)

for some uniform constant
\epsilon_{1}>0
. We may assume
\eta\geq\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{0}
in a small neighborhood
\Omega_{\epsilon}:=\Omega\cap B_{\epsilon}(x_{0})
when
\epsilon
is sufficiently small.

Let us define a function
\Phi
in
\Omega_{\epsilon}
by

\begin{split}\Phi=&-\eta(u-\underline{u})_{x_{n}}+\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{n-1}\tilde{F}^{\alpha\beta}\big(\tilde{\underline{U}}_{\alpha\beta}-\tilde{U}_{\alpha\beta}(x_{0})\big).\\
\end{split}

A straightforward computation gives

-\eta\,(u-\underline{u})_{x_{n}}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{n-1}\tilde{F}^{\alpha\beta}\big(\tilde{U}_{\alpha\beta}-\tilde{\underline{U}}_{\alpha\beta}\big)\,.

This, together with (
4.27
), yield that

\begin{split}\Phi&=\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{n-1}\tilde{F}^{\alpha\beta}\big(\tilde{U}_{\alpha\beta}-\tilde{U}_{\alpha\beta}(x_{0})\big)\geq\tilde{F}[\tilde{U}]-\tilde{F}[\tilde{U}(x_{0})]=\tilde{F}(\tilde{U})-\psi(x_{0},u)-\delta_{\infty}\,.\\
\end{split}

It follows that
\Phi(x_{0})=0
and
\Phi\geq 0
on
\partial\Omega
. Let us consider a barrier function

\Psi:=\Phi+N_{2}v+N_{1}|x|^{2}\qquad\text{ in }\,\Omega_{\epsilon},

where
N_{1},N_{2}
are certain large positive constants. One can verify that

\underset{x\rightarrow\partial\Omega_{\epsilon}}{\lim\inf}\,\Psi(x)\geq 0,\qquad L\Psi\geq 0\,\text{ in }\,\Omega_{\epsilon}

as long as
N_{2}\gg N_{1}\geq C\epsilon^{2}
. According to Hopf’s lemma, we know that
\Phi_{x_{n}}(x_{0})\geq-C
. This, in conjunction with (
4.30
), provides a uniform upper bound for
u_{x_{n}x_{n}}(x_{0})
.

Now, given that all the eigenvalues of
U(x_{0})
are bounded from above, we can conclude that
\lambda(U)(x_{0})
lies in a compact subset of
\Gamma
. Using the ellipticity of
f
again, we see that

\delta_{\infty}\geq\delta_{R_{0}}=f(\lambda^{\prime}(\tilde{U}(x_{0})),R_{0})-\psi(x_{0},u)>0

when
R_{0}
is sufficiently large. Consequently, we have finished the proof of (
4.23
) if we choose
\hat{\delta}=\min\{\epsilon_{0},\delta_{R_{0}}\}
for a large constant
R_{0}
.

Remark 4.4
.

If
\|\psi\|_{C^{1,1}}
is uniform
bounded, one can apply the Evans-Krylov local argument to conclude the interior
C^{2,\alpha}
estimate. Then, for every subdomain
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
and for every
\alpha\in(0,1)
, there exists a uniform constant
C>0
such that

\|u\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega^{\prime}})}\leq C.

The interior
C^{\infty}
estimate can be also derived using the Schauder theory and a standard iterating argument. We therefore obtain
u\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap C^{1,1}(\overline{\Omega})
, which completes the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

5
The Dirichlet eigenvalue problem

In this section, drawing inspiration from the Lions’ approximation strategy, we will complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
in the next three steps:

Step 1:

We solve a family of Dirichlet problems (
5.1
) that blow up as the parameter approaches a threshold value
\lambda_{1}
.

Step 2:

After a normalization, we apply the previously established estimates and the Arzela-Ascoli theorem to exact a subsequnce that converges to a solution
(\lambda_{1},u_{1})
of (
1.1
).

Step 3:

We demonstrate that the solutions of (
1.1
) are unique, thereby confirming that
(\lambda_{1},u_{1})
is precisely the solution we are seeking.

5.1
Lions’ approximation strategy

Following the procedure of Lions
[
22
]
, we may consider a family of Dirichlet problems as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\begin{matrix}\begin{aligned} F(D^{2}u)=&\,1-\lambda u\\
u=&\,0\end{aligned}&\begin{aligned} \quad&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
\quad&\mbox{on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\end{matrix}\end{array}\right.

(5.1)

where
\lambda
is a parameter. For every
\lambda\geq 0
, we denote the solution of (
5.1
), if it exists, by
u_{\lambda}
and define

I=\big\{\lambda\geq 0:\eqref{family}\textrm{ admits a solution }u_{\lambda}\in C^{1,1}(\overline{\Omega})\big\}\,,\qquad\Lambda_{1}=\sup_{\lambda\in I}\lambda\,.

(5.2)

We begin by noting that in the case of
\lambda=0
, Equation (
5.1
) is non-degenerate. This allows us to adapt the approach of Theorem
1.5
to produce a smooth
\Gamma
-admissible solution
u_{0}
. Therefore, the set
I
is nonempty and
\Lambda_{1}\geq 0
.

Next, we show that
\Lambda_{1}
possesses explicit numerical upper or lower bounds.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\mu_{1}
be the first non-zero eigenvalue of the Laplace operator on
\Omega
. We have

\|u_{0}\|^{-1}_{C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq\Lambda_{1}\leq\frac{\mu_{1}}{n}\,.

(5.3)

Proof.

Assuming that the pair
(\lambda,u_{\lambda})
represents a solution to the Dirichlet problem (
5.1
), it follows from (
2.3
) that

\frac{1}{n}\,\sigma_{1}(D^{2}u_{\lambda})\geq F(D^{2}u_{\lambda})=1-\lambda u_{\lambda}>-\lambda u_{\lambda}\,.

This shows the second inequality of (
5.3
).

We now proceed to verify the first inequality of (
5.3
). For an arbitrary positive constant
\lambda<\|u_{0}\|^{-1}_{C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})}
, we set
C_{\lambda}=(1-\lambda\|u_{0}\|_{C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})})^{-1}
. Then, according to 1-homogeneity of
F
, we infer that

F\big(D^{2}(C_{\lambda}u_{0})\big)=C_{\lambda}\geq 1-\lambda C_{\lambda}u_{0}\geq 1\,.

That is,
C_{\lambda}u_{0}
is a subsolution of the Dirichlet problem (
5.1
). Observe that
u_{0}
is a supersolution of (
5.1
). In view of Theorem
1.5
, the Dirichlet problem (
5.1
) admits a solution
u_{\lambda}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap C^{1,1}(\overline{\Omega})
. This proves that each
\lambda<\|u_{0}\|^{-1}_{C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})}
satisfies the inequality
\lambda\leq\Lambda_{1}
. Thus, we have established the first inequality presented in (
5.3
).
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

For every
\lambda\in[0,\Lambda_{1})
, the Dirichlet problem (
5.1
) admits a
\Gamma
-admissible solution
u_{\lambda}
, i.e.
[0,\Lambda_{1})\subset I
.

Proof.

Since
\lambda<\Lambda_{1}
and we have already established that
u_{0}
is a supersolution of the Dirichlet problem (
5.1
), it is sufficient to construct a subsolution.

From the definition of
\Lambda_{1}
as a supremum, there exists a constant
\tilde{\lambda}
with
\lambda<\tilde{\lambda}<\Lambda_{1}
such that
\tilde{\lambda}\in I
. It follows that
u_{\tilde{\lambda}}
is a desired subsolution of (
5.1
). Indeed, since
u_{\tilde{\lambda}}\leq 0
in
\Omega

F(D^{2}u_{\tilde{\lambda}})=1-\tilde{\lambda}u_{\tilde{\lambda}}\geq 1-\lambda u_{\tilde{\lambda}}\,.

This ends our proof of lemma.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

\|u_{\lambda}\|_{C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})}
tends to infinity as
\lambda\nearrow\Lambda_{1}
.

Proof.

Suppose the contrary, then there exists a sequence of numbers
\mu_{j}\in\Lambda
such that
\mu_{j}\rightarrow\Lambda_{1}
with
\|u_{\mu_{j}}\|_{C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq N<+\infty
for a constant
N
independent of
l
. Thus, we have
-N\leq u_{\mu_{j}}\leq 0
on
\overline{\Omega}
and whence

F(D^{2}u_{0})=1\leq F(D^{2}u_{\mu_{j}})=1-\mu_{j}u_{\mu_{j}}\leq 1+N\Lambda_{1}=F\big((1+N\Lambda_{1})D^{2}u_{0}\big)\,.

Applying the comparison principle, it follows that

(1+N\Lambda_{1})u_{0}\leq u_{\mu_{j}}\leq u_{0}<0\qquad\textrm{ in }\Omega.

From Propositions
3.1
,
4.1
and
4.2
, we deduce that
\|u_{\mu_{j}}\|_{C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C
for a uniform constant
C
. Furthermore, by applying the standard Evans-Krylov theory for non-degenerate uniformly elliptic equations, we also obtain local bounds in
C^{k}(\Omega)
for all
k\geq 1
. Then, by extracting a subsequence if necessary, we may assume
\{u_{\mu_{j}}\}
converges to some function
u_{\mu_{\infty}}\in C^{1,1}(\overline{\Omega})\cap C^{\infty}(\Omega)
as
\mu_{j}\nearrow\lambda_{1}
.

As in the proof of Lemma
5.1
, for every
0<\delta<\|u_{\mu_{\infty}}\|^{-1}_{C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})}
, we define a constant
C_{\delta}=(1-\delta\|u_{\mu_{\infty}}\|_{C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})})^{-1}
to ensure that

F\big(D^{2}(C_{\delta}u_{\mu_{\infty}})\big)=C_{\delta}(1-\Lambda_{1}u_{\mu_{\infty}})\geq 1-(\delta+\Lambda_{1})C_{\delta}u_{\mu_{\infty}},

i.e.
C_{\delta}u_{\mu_{\infty}}
serves as a subsolution of (
5.1
) with
\lambda=\delta+\Lambda_{1}
.

Observe that
u_{0}
remains a supersolution of (
5.1
). Thanks to Theorem
1.5
, then the Dirichlet problem (
5.1
) admits a global
C^{1,1}
solution for every
\Lambda_{1}\leq\lambda<\Lambda_{1}+\delta
, which contradicts our definition of
\Lambda_{1}
. This asserts the lemma.
∎

5.2
Proof of Theorem
1.2

For every constant
0\leq\mu<\Lambda_{1}
, we consider the following normalization for
u_{\mu}
:

v_{\mu}:=\frac{u_{\mu}}{\|u_{\mu}\|_{C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})}}\,.

It can be easily seen that
v_{\mu}
is a solution of the Dirichlet problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\begin{matrix}\begin{aligned} F(D^{2}v_{\mu})=&\,\|u_{\mu}\|^{-1}_{C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})}-\mu v_{\mu}\\
v_{\mu}=&\,0\\
\|v_{\mu}\|_{C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})}=&\,1.\end{aligned}&\begin{aligned} \quad&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
\quad&\mbox{on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\end{matrix}\end{array}\right.

In accordance with Lemma
5.3
, there exists a constant
\nu_{1}
belonging to the interval
(0,\Lambda_{1})
such that for every
\mu
in the range
(\nu_{1},\Lambda_{1})
, the norm
\|u_{\mu}\|_{C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})}
is greater than
1
. Besides, since
-1\leq v_{\mu}<0
in
\Omega
, it follows that

\|u_{\mu}\|^{-1}_{C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})}-\mu v_{\mu}\leq 1+\mu\leq 1+\Lambda_{1}\qquad\text{for all }\,\mu\in(\nu_{1},\Lambda_{1})\,.

Applying the comparison principle, we immediately infer that

(1+\Lambda_{1})u_{0}\leq v_{\mu}\leq 0\qquad\text{for all }\,\mu\in(\nu_{1},\Lambda_{1})

(5.4)

which yields a uniform upper bound on
\|Dv_{\mu}\|_{C^{0}(\partial\Omega)}
independent of
\mu
. Then by Propositions
3.1
,
4.1
and
4.2
, we see that

\|v_{\mu}\|_{C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C,\qquad\text{for all }\,\mu\in(\nu_{1},\Lambda_{1})

for a constant
C
independent of
\mu
.

By virtue of the Arzela-Ascoli theorem, we can extract a subsequence
\{\mu_{j}\}
within the interval
(\nu_{1},\Lambda_{1})
that converges to
\Lambda_{1}
, such that
\{v_{\mu_{j}}\}
converges uniformly to specific function
u_{1}\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
. Furthermore,
u_{1}
is indeed a weak solution to the following Dirichlet problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\begin{matrix}\begin{aligned} F(D^{2}u_{1})=&\,-\Lambda_{1}u_{1}\\
u_{1}=&\,0\\
\|u_{1}\|_{C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})}=&\,1.\end{aligned}&\begin{aligned} \quad&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
\quad&\mbox{on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\end{matrix}\end{array}\right.

(5.5)

Below we will exploit the higher order regularity of
u_{1}
, which can be derived through a barrier argument.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
u_{1}
be a continuous solution of (
5.5
). Then we have
u_{1}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap C^{1,1}(\overline{\Omega})
.

Proof.

Given that
\|u_{1}\|_{C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})}=1
and
u_{1}=0
on
\partial\Omega
,
there exists an interior point
x_{0}\in\Omega
such that
u_{1}(x_{0})=-1
. Observe that
v_{\mu_{j}}\rightarrow u_{1}
implies that

-v_{\mu_{j}}(x_{0})\geq-u_{1}(x_{0})-\frac{1}{3}=\frac{2}{3}\,.

Due to the continuity, there exists a small ball
B_{2r_{0}}=B(x_{0},2r_{0})
centered at
x_{0}
within
\Omega
, for some constant
r_{0}>0
, such that

v_{\mu_{j}}\leq-\frac{1}{3}\qquad\textrm{ on }B_{2r_{0}}

provided
j\in\mathbb{N}
is large enough. Then, it follows from the (
2.3
) that

\Delta v_{\mu_{j}}\geq nF(D^{2}v_{\mu_{j}})\geq-n\mu_{j}v_{\mu_{j}}\geq\frac{n\nu_{1}}{3}\qquad\textrm{ on }B_{2r_{0}}.

Select a smooth function
h:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

0\leq h\leq 1,\qquad|h^{\prime}|+|h^{\prime\prime}|\leq N

for some constant
N
that depends only on
r_{0}
and

h(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&r\leq r_{0}\,;\\[2.84526pt]
0,&r\geq 2r_{0}\,.\end{array}\right.

Let
v
be a smooth solution to the following Dirichlet problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\begin{matrix}\begin{aligned} \Delta v=&\,\frac{n\nu_{1}}{3}h\big(|x-x_{0}|\big)\\
v=&\,0\end{aligned}&\begin{aligned} \quad&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
\quad&\mbox{on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\end{matrix}\end{array}\right.

It is evident that
\sigma_{1}(D^{2}v)\leq\sigma_{1}(D^{2}v_{\mu_{j}})
in
\Omega
and
v=v_{\mu_{j}}
on
\partial\Omega
. In light of the comparison principle and a similar argument as presented in
(
5.4
), it follows that

(1+\Lambda_{1})u_{0}\leq v_{\mu_{j}}\leq v\qquad\textrm{ in }\Omega.

Therefore,
\|u_{\mu_{j}}\|_{C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})}
is uniform bounded provided
j\in\mathbb{N}
is large enough. With the help of Theorem
1.5
, there exists a uniform constant
C
such that

\|v_{\mu_{j}}\|_{C^{1,1}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C\,.

(5.6)

Observe that
v_{\mu_{j}}
is a solution to the following equation

F(D^{2}v_{\mu_{j}})=\|u_{\mu_{j}}\|^{-1}_{C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})}-\mu_{j}v_{\mu_{j}}=:h_{j}\quad\textrm{in }\Omega\,.

Considering the facts that
\mu_{j}\in(\nu_{1},\Lambda_{1})
and (
5.6
), we observe that
\|h_{j}\|_{C^{1,1}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C^{\prime}
for a uniform constant
C^{\prime}
that is independent of
j
. Performing a similar argument as presented in Remark
4.4
, there exists a uniform constant
C^{\prime\prime}
that is independent of
j
such that

\|v_{\mu_{j}}\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\Omega)}\leq C^{\prime\prime}\,.

As a consequence, we conclude that
u_{1}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap C^{1,1}(\overline{\Omega})
and
(\Lambda_{1},u_{1})
is a solution of (
1.1
).
∎

We are now finally in a position to prove the uniqueness of the solution to the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
).

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
(\Lambda_{2},u_{2})\in[0,+\infty)\times(C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap C^{1,1}(\overline{\Omega}))
be another solution of the eigenvalue problem (
1.1
). Then, we have
\Lambda_{2}=\Lambda_{1}
and
u_{2}=cu_{1}
for some constant
c>0
.

Proof.

This proof follows a standard argument, which can be found in
[
38
]
, we include it here for completeness.

Step 1.
\Lambda_{1}
equals to
\Lambda_{2}
.
Let us define the elliptic operator

L_{1}:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}F^{ij}(D^{2}u_{1})\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\,.

Since
L_{1}(u_{1})=F(D^{2}u_{1})=-\Lambda_{1}u_{1}
and
u_{1}
vanishes on
\partial\Omega
, it follows from
[
9
, p. 361]
that

\Lambda_{1}=\mathrm{Re}(\Lambda_{1})\geq\Lambda_{1}(L_{1})\,.

(5.7)

On the other hand, by the characterization of the principle eigenvalue in Berestycki-Nirenberg-Varadhan
[
2
]
, we have

\Lambda_{1}(L_{1})=\sup\{\lambda\geq 0\,:\,\exists\varphi<0,\,L_{1}(\varphi)+\lambda\varphi\geq 0\}\,,

where
\varphi\in C^{1,1}(\overline{\Omega})
and
\varphi=0
on
\partial\Omega
. Owing to the concavity and homogeneity of the operator
F
, we obtain

L_{1}(u_{2})\geq F(D^{2}u_{1}+D^{2}u_{2})-F(D^{2}u_{1})\geq F(D^{2}u_{2})=-\Lambda_{2}u_{2}.

(5.8)

Combining this with inequality (
5.7
), we conclude that
\Lambda_{2}\leq\Lambda_{1}(L_{1})\leq\Lambda_{1}
. A symmetric argument yields
\Lambda_{1}\leq\Lambda_{2}
. Therefore,
\Lambda_{1}=\Lambda_{2}.

Step 2.
u_{2}
is a positive constant multiple of
u_{1}
.
We claim that there exists
t>0
such that

0\leq t(-u_{1})\leq(-u_{2})\,\textrm{ in }\,\overline{\Omega}.

To prove this, first note that since
u_{2}
is subharmonic, Hopf’s Lemma implies that
\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial n}>0
on
\partial\Omega
, where
n
denotes the outer unit normal vector field on
\partial\Omega
. Let
d
be the distance function to
\partial\Omega
. Then there exists a uniform constant
c_{0}>0
such that
d\leq c_{0}(-u_{2})
in
\overline{\Omega}
. On the other hand, let
\rho
be a smooth defining function for
\Omega
such that
(-\rho)\leq c_{1}d
for some constant
c_{1}>0
. Since both
u_{1}
and
\rho
vanish on
\partial\Omega
, we may write
u_{1}=h\rho
for some smooth function
h
defined near
\partial\Omega
. It follows that there exists a constant
c_{2}>0
such that

0\leq(-u_{1})\leq c_{2}(-\rho)\leq c_{1}c_{2}d\leq c_{0}c_{1}c_{2}(-u_{2}).

Thus, the claim holds with
t=(c_{0}c_{1}c_{2})^{-1}
.

The remainder of the proof follows a standard comparative argument as in
[
22
]
. Define

t_{0}=\sup\{t>0\,:\,0\leq t(-u_{1})\leq(-u_{2})\,\textrm{ in }\overline{\Omega}\}.

Denote
w=u_{2}-t_{0}u_{1}
for brevity. It follows from (
5.8
) that
L_{1}(w)\geq-\lambda_{1}w
in
\Omega
. Notice that
w\leq 0
in
\Omega
and
w=0
on
\partial{\Omega}
. There are two cases to consider:

(i)

Assume that
w=0
at some point
x_{0}\in\Omega
. Since
x_{0}
is a maximum point of
w
, the strong maximum principle forces that
w\equiv 0
in
\overline{\Omega}
.

(ii)

If instead
w<0
in
\Omega
, then
L_{1}(w)>0
. Applying Hopf’s Lemma again, there exist a constant
t_{1}>0
such that

0\leq t_{1}(-u_{1})\leq(-w)=t_{0}u_{1}-u_{2},

which implies
(t_{0}+t_{1})(-u_{1})\leq(-u_{2})
in
\overline{\Omega}
. This contradicts the definition of
t_{0}
.

In both cases, we conclude that
u_{2}=t_{0}u_{1}
, which completes the proof.
∎

6
Application to the bifurcation-type theory

The bifurcation-type theory for the Monge–Ampère operator was originally established by Lions
[
22
, Corollary 2]
, in a form analogous to corresponding results for the first eigenvalue of linear elliptic operators. Inspired by this work, we prove in this manuscript that the first eigenvalue of
F
serves as a bifurcation point for the Dirichlet problem (
1.4
).

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\psi(x,s)
be a smooth positive function on
\overline{\Omega}\times(-\infty,0]
such that

\psi_{s}\geq-\Lambda_{0}>-\Lambda_{1},

where
\Lambda_{0}
is a given non-negative constant and
\Lambda_{1}
denotes the first eigenvalue of
F
on
\Omega
. Then the Dirichlet problem (
1.4
) admits a
\Gamma
-admissible solution
u\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
.

Proof.

The proof is based on the approach of
[
5
]
, which was originally formulated for complex Hessian operators.

Step 1. Construction of subsolution.
To establish existence, we first construct a subsolution to (
1.4
). Fix
\lambda\in(\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1})
. By Lemma
5.2
, there exists a
\Gamma
-admissible solution
u_{\lambda}
to (
5.1
). For any constant
N>\|\psi(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{\infty}}>0
, define
u_{N}=Nu_{\lambda}
. Then, using the homogeneity of
F
and the assumption
\psi_{s}\geq-\Lambda_{0}>-\lambda
, we obtain

F(D^{2}u_{N})=N-\lambda u_{N}\geq\|\psi(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{\infty}}-\lambda u_{N}>\psi(x,u_{N})\,,

(6.1)

which shows that
u_{N}
is a subsolution of (
1.4
).

Step 2. Continuity method.
We now prove existence via the continuity method. For a parameter
t\in[0,1]
, consider the family of equations

F(D^{2}u_{t})=t\psi(x,u_{t})+(1-t)F(D^{2}u_{N})\,\quad\textrm{in}\,\Omega,\qquad u_{t}=0\quad\textrm{on}\,\partial\Omega.

(6.2)

Define the set

S=\{t\in[0,1]\,:\,\eqref{family 2}\textrm{ admits a solution }u_{t}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})\}\,.

Note that at
t=0
, the function
u_{N}
is a solution to (
6.2
), so
0\in S
and
S\neq\emptyset
. To apply the continuity method, we shall verify that
S
is open and closed.

Step 3. Openness.
Let

L_{t}=\Delta_{F}-t\psi_{s}(x,u_{t})\qquad\textrm{ where}\quad\Delta_{F}:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}F^{ij}(D^{2}u_{t})\partial_{i}\partial_{j}

denote the linearization of (
6.2
). By concavity,

\Delta_{F}(u_{N}-u_{t})\geq F(D^{2}u_{N})-F(D^{2}u_{t})\,.

Moreover, from (
2.4
) applied to
u=u_{t}
, we get
\Delta_{F}u_{t}=F(D^{2}u_{t})
. This implies

\Delta_{F}u_{N}\geq F(D^{2}u_{N})=N-\lambda u_{N}>-\lambda u_{N}.

It follows that

\lambda_{1}(\Delta_{F})>\lambda\geq t\lambda>-t\psi_{s}\,,

(6.3)

where
\lambda_{1}(\Delta_{F})
is the first eigenvalue of the linear operator
\Delta_{F}
. Therefore, the operator
L_{t}
is invertible, which implies that
S
is open.

Step 4. Closeness.
To prove that
S
is closed, it suffices to establish a uniform
L^{\infty}
estimate for the solutions
u_{t}
. Once this is achieved, the higher-order estimates follow from the arguments in Sections
3
and
4
.

From (
6.1
) and (
6.2
), we obtain

F(D^{2}u_{t})-F(D^{2}u_{N})<t(\psi(x,u_{t})-\psi(x,u_{N})).

(6.4)

Define
u_{r}=ru_{t}+(1-r)u_{N}
for
r\in(0,1)
, and let

\overline{F}^{ij}=\int_{0}^{1}F^{ij}(D^{2}u_{r})dr\,,\quad\overline{\psi}_{s}=\int_{0}^{1}\psi_{s}(x,u_{r})dr\,,\quad\Delta_{\overline{F}}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\overline{F}^{ij}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\,.

Set
w=u_{N}-u_{t}
. Then (
6.4
) simplifies to
\Delta_{\overline{F}}w>t\overline{\psi}_{s}w\,,

which also implies that

\lambda_{1}(\Delta_{\overline{F}})>-t\overline{\psi}_{s}\,.

(6.5)

Let
v_{1}
be the first eigenfunction of
\Delta_{\overline{F}}
, normalized so that
\inf_{\Omega}v_{1}=-1
, and define

\theta_{0}=\inf\{\theta\,:\,w+\theta v_{1}\leq 0\,\textrm{ in }\Omega\}.

We claim that
\theta_{0}\leq 0
. Suppose, for contradiction, that
\theta_{0}>0
. Then from (
6.5
), we derive

\Delta_{\overline{F}}(w+\theta_{0}v_{1})>t\overline{\psi}_{s}w-\lambda_{1}(\Delta_{\overline{F}})\theta_{0}v_{1}>t\overline{\psi}_{s}(w+\theta_{0}v_{1}).

(6.6)

Choose a positive constant
A
such that
A\geq t\overline{\psi}_{s}
. Since
w+\theta_{0}v_{1}\leq 0
by the definition of
\theta_{0}
, it follows that

(\Delta_{\overline{F}}-A)(w+\theta_{0}v_{1})\geq 0.

The strong maximum principle then implies that either
w+\theta_{0}v_{1}\equiv 0
or
w+\theta_{0}v_{1}<0
in
\Omega
. The former case contradicts (
6.6
). For the latter case, by the Hopf lemma for
\Delta_{\overline{F}}
, there exists
\theta_{1}>0
such that
w+\theta_{0}v_{1}\leq\theta_{1}v_{1}
in
\Omega
, which contradicts the definition of
\theta_{0}
. This proves the claim. Therefore, we conclude that
w\leq-\theta_{0}v_{1}\leq 0
, and hence

0\geq u_{t}\geq u_{N}\quad\textrm{ in }\Omega.

Thus,
S
is closed.

Consequently, since
S
is nonempty, open and closed, it follows that
S=[0,1]
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

When
\psi_{s}\geq 0
, Proposition
6.1
partially recovers the classical theorem of Caffarelli–Nirenberg–Spruck
[
6
]
. Our result thus relaxes their structural assumption. However, due to the lack of a variational structure for the general concave elliptic Hessian operator
F
, the question of uniqueness remains an open challenge.

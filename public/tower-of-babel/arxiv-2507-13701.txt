Power quotients of surface groups and mapping class groups
Rémi Coulon, Alessandro Sisto, Henry Wilton

Abstract
Let Γ \Gamma be the fundamental group of a closed, orientable, hyperbolic surface S S . The n n -power quotient , Γ ⁡ ( n ) \Gamma(n) , is the quotient of Γ \Gamma by the n n th powers of simple closed curves. We prove an analogue of the Dehn–Nielsen–Baer theorem for suitable large values of n n : the outer automorphism group of Γ ⁡ ( n ) \Gamma(n) is isomorphic to the quotient of the extended mapping class group of S S by n n th powers of Dehn twists. There is also a corresponding description of the automorphism group as the quotient of the extended mapping class group of the corresponding once-punctured surface, and we relate these groups via a Birman-type exact sequence. Along the way, and as consequences, we prove structural properties of Γ ⁡ ( n ) \Gamma(n) for suitable large values of n n , including: Γ ⁡ ( n ) \Gamma(n) is virtually torsion-free, acylindrically hyperbolic, infinitely presented, with solvable word problem and finite asymptotic dimension.

1.2
Questions

Throughout this subsection, fix a closed surface

There are several natural questions that arise. First of all, because of

Question 1.1

Is

In a different direction,

and gluing in disks. If this complex were aspherical, one could obtain information about homological properties of

\mathchoice{\operatorname{MCG}\left(S\right)}{\operatorname{MCG}(S)}{\operatorname{MCG}(S)}{\operatorname{MCG}(S)}/\operatorname{DT}^{n}(S)

. We then ask:

Question 1.2
.

Is

as above aspherical for all sufficiently large

? Is

a classifying space for proper actions?

Note that, if

is aspherical, then it is not hard to show that

is infinite dimensional. This would in particular show that

is infinitely presented for all sufficiently large

Some of our results hold for all sufficiently large

]
(that

Throughout this article we only consider surfaces with at most one puncture, but the Birman exact sequence holds for surfaces with more punctures as well. We expect that our results can be extended to these cases:

.

In light of

For which integers

residually finite?

1.3
Automorphism groups of free groups

Finally, we discuss the possibility of analogous results in the context of (outer) automorphisms of free groups. Denote by

the free group or rank

\{a_{1},\dots,a_{r}\}
. An element of

. Primitive elements are sometimes seen as the analogues of simple closed curves in the context of free groups, and it is natural to study the quotient

of

and fixing

for

to be the subgroup generated by

\mathchoice{\operatorname{Aut}\left(F_{r}\right)}{\operatorname{Aut}(F_{r})}{\operatorname{Aut}(F_{r})}{\operatorname{Aut}(F_{r})}/\operatorname{TV}^{n}(F_{r})

As in the surface case, these groups have been studied extensively. Bridson–Vogtmann proved that

, with a copy of

in its image

]

]

A strategy similar to the one we used for

Second, more seriously, our proof that

Outline of the paper

The arguments in this paper combine three different toolkits from geometric group theory: geometric small cancellation theory, acylindrical and hierarchical hyperbolicity, and Paulin–Rips–Sela’s theory of limiting actions on

In

In

The real work starts in

Using this sequence of approximations, in

, including

The results of

By induction, every element of

is of the form

for some

and

. Now, direct computation shows that any commutator in

is of the form

[C_{1},C_{2}]=\left[\begin{matrix}1&*\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right]\,.

In particular
[Q_{n},Q_{n}]
is abelian, hence

is metabelian, proving item
(ii)
.

To show that
Q_{n}
is

-periodic, we compute

C^{n}=\left[\begin{matrix}\xi^{kn}&\chi_{n}(\xi^{k})z\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right],

where

is the polynomial defined by

\chi_{n}(X)=1+X+X^{2}+\dots+X^{n-1}.

As
\xi
is an

th root of unity,
\xi^{kn}=1
and

equals zero or

in

(depending on whether
\xi^{k}\neq 1
or
\xi^{k}=1
, respectively).
Therefore, by the definition of

,

, proving
(iii)
.

Finally, we compute the orders of

and
[A,B]
. It is clear by construction that
A
has order
n
. The commutator is

[A,B]=\left[\begin{matrix}1&\xi-1\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right].

The condition

yields

in

.
In other words, there is

such that

in
\mathbf{Z}[\xi]
.
Let

, the degree of the

th cyclotomic polynomial, and note that

implies that

.
Since
\xi
is a primitive

th root of unity,

can be written

where

.
Moreover,
\mathbf{Q}[\xi]
is a degree

\mathbf{Q}
.
Hence the relation

yields

This shows that

is a multiple of

, proving item
(iv)
.
∎

The following lemma puts all simple curves into a standard form.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be the fundamental group of a closed, connected, orientable, hyperbolic surface and let
F_{2}=\langle a,b\rangle
be free of rank 2. For any
\gamma\in G
represented by a simple closed curve, there is a homomorphism
f:\Gamma\to F_{2}
such that:

(i)

f(\gamma)=a
if
\gamma
is non-separating;

(ii)

f(\gamma)=[a,b]
if
\gamma
is separating.

Proof.

Consider the standard presentation

\Gamma=\left\langle a_{1},b_{1},\dots,a_{g},b_{g}\mid[a_{1},b_{1}]\dots[a_{g},b_{g}]\right\rangle

of
\Gamma
. Define
f:\Gamma\to F_{2}
by

\displaystyle f(a_{1})=f(b_{g})=a

\displaystyle f(b_{1})=f(a_{g})=b

and
f(a_{i})=f(b_{i})=1
for
1<i<g
, and note that the relation is satisfied, so this is a homomorphism.

If
\gamma
is non-separating then, by the change-of-coordinates principle, we may assume that
\gamma=a_{1}
by applying an automorphism. Likewise, if
\gamma
is separating, by change of coordinates we may assume that

\gamma=[a_{1},b_{1}]\ldots[a_{i},b_{i}]

for some

. In either case,

is as required.
∎

We denote by
\Gamma^{\prime}=[\Gamma,\Gamma]
and
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}=[\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime}]
the first two groups in the derived series of
\Gamma
. Given

, we write
\Gamma^{n}
for the (normal) subgroup of
\Gamma
generated by the
n
th power of every element. The quotient

M(n)=\Gamma/\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\Gamma^{n}

(1)

is the
maximal
n
-periodic metabelian quotient
of
\Gamma
.
The combination of
Proposition 2.1
and

gives precise information about the images of simple closed curves in

.

Proposition 2.3
.

The image in
M(n)
of any element
\gamma\in\Gamma
representing a simple closed curve has order exactly
n
.

Proof.

Every
n
-periodic metabelian quotient of
\Gamma
factors through
M(n)
, so it suffices to exhibit a homomorphism from
\Gamma
to an
n
-periodic metabelian group sending
\gamma
to an element of order
n
. Let
f
be provided by
Lemma 2.2
and let
\rho:F_{2}\to Q_{n}
send

and
b\mapsto B
. Then
\rho\circ f(\gamma)
has order
n
in every case, which proves the result.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

The image in
\Gamma(n)
of a simple closed curve has order exactly
n
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in\Gamma
be a simple closed curve.
By construction, its order in
\Gamma(n)
divides
n
.
However the projection
\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow M(n)
factors through
\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow\Gamma(n)
.
Moreover, the image of
\gamma
in
M(n)
has order exactly
n
, whence the result.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

Item
(ii)
of
Proposition 2.1
– that

is metabelian – is not needed to prove

. Indeed, any homomorphism

factors through a smallest characteristic quotient, the intersection of the kernels of every surjection
\Gamma\to Q_{n}
, and this quotient would have served equally well to prove

.
∎

Lemma 3.11

Suppose that
\Gamma

The following are equivalent.

(i)

(ii)

No non-trivial elliptic subgroup of

Note that

Assume now that

One proves first that

Indeed, for three such elements,

It follows from our assumption that either

\Gamma_{0}

\gamma_{2}
is loxodromic, and then

is cyclic.
In both cases

Consider now a maximal elliptic subgroup

\Gamma

By commutative transitivity,
\left\langle E,\gamma E\gamma^{-1}\right\rangle

Note that
\left\langle E,\gamma E\gamma^{-1}\right\rangle

Indeed, it follows from

By maximality we deduce that

Remark 4.3
.

This class of group is a variation of the class, also called

Let us highlight here the main differences between the two definitions.
In this article, we study the quotient

for every (sufficiently large) exponent

In the even case, we need to work with a class

In particular such groups could contain infinite dihedral subgroups.
This feature is incompatible with the requirements stated in

All the adjustment we made are designed to allow the existence of infinite dihedral subgroups in

—

Assumption (H2) in

This is no longer a requirement here.
However, since a loxodromic element cannot normalise a non-trivial finite normal subgroup, infinite elementary subgroups are isomorphic to either

or the infinite dihedral group

—

In comparison with Assumption (H1) in

This uniform control of the

—

We do not assume here that the groups

below), since infinite dihedral groups are not CSA.
This assumption is used in
[

]
to ensures that any limit group over the class

is also CSA.
In the present article, we do not conduct an in-depth study of limit groups over

.
On the contrary, for our applications, we know that the limit groups we will deal with are CSA (they are surface groups).
Thus, this hypotheses is no longer required. ∎

Remark 4.9

(About even exponents)
.

Recall that if

The group

The next lemma establishes exactness at

Lemma 5.8
.

The point-pushing map
\Gamma(n)\to\mathchoice{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}\left(S_{*}\right)}{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}(S_{*})}{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}(S_{*})}{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}(S_{*})}/\operatorname{DT}^{n}(S_{*})

Proof.

By the commutativity of the bottom left square in
Figure 1
, the map

Consequently, the point-pushing map is also injective.

We then establish exactness at the middle term of the sequence

Lemma 5.9
.

The image of the point-pushing map

\Gamma(n)\to\mathchoice{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}\left(S_{*}\right)}{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}(S_{*})}{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}(S_{*})}{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}(S_{*})}/\operatorname{DT}^{n}(S_{*})

\mathchoice{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}\left(S_{*}\right)}{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}(S_{*})}{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}(S_{*})}{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}(S_{*})}/\operatorname{DT}^{n}(S_{*})\to\mathchoice{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}\left(S\right)}{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}(S)}{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}(S)}{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}(S)}/\operatorname{DT}^{n}(S)

whenever

has trivial centre.

Proof.

Because both maps descend from the corresponding maps in the Birman exact sequence, and because the composition of the forgetful and point-pushing maps in the Birman exact sequence is trivial, it follows that the image is contained in the kernel.
It remains to show that the kernel of the forgetful map is in the image of the point-pushing map.

Suppose, therefore, that
\phi\in\mathchoice{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}\left(S_{*}\right)}{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}(S_{*})}{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}(S_{*})}{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}(S_{*})}
is such that

is in the kernel of the forgetful map.
Since every Dehn twist on

lifts to a Dehn twist in

, the forgetful map restricts to a surjection
\operatorname{DT}^{n}(S_{*})\to\operatorname{DT}^{n}(S)
, and therefore
\phi

\mathchoice{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}\left(S_{*}\right)}{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}(S_{*})}{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}(S_{*})}{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}(S_{*})}\to\mathchoice{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}\left(S\right)}{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}(S)}{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}(S)}{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}(S)}

is in the image of the point-pushing map from

∎

Finally, we are ready to prove the analogue of the Birman exact sequence.

Proof of
Theorem 5.6

In view of

and

We first recall Bowditch’s definition of an acylindrical action, and Osin’s definition of an acylindrically hyperbolic group.

Definition 6.2
.

Suppose a group
G
acts by isometries on a metric space
X
. The action is called
acylindrical
if, for every
C>0
, there are constants
R,K
so that the number of group elements

satisfying

d(x,gx)\leqslant C\quad\mathrm{and}\quad d(y,gy)\leqslant C

is bounded by
K
, for every
x,y\in X
with
d(x,y)\geqslant R
. A group
G
that admits a non-elementary acylindrical action on a hyperbolic metric space is called
acylindrically hyperbolic
.

For any surface

of finite type, Masur–Minsky proved that the curve complex
\mathcal{C}(S)
is hyperbolic
[
53
]
, while Bowditch proved that the action of the mapping class group on the curve complex
\mathcal{C}(S)
is acylindrical
[
13
]
; in particular, mapping class groups are acylindrically hyperbolic. The reader is referred to
[
56
]
for background material on acylindrically hyperbolic groups.

Let us introduce some notation. For a fixed hyperbolic surface of finite type

, let
X
denote the curve complex
\mathcal{C}(S)
. The results of this section apply to all positive multiples
n
of some large positive integer

. For such an

, we write
\bar{X}
for the quotient space
\operatorname{DT}^{n}(S)\backslash X
and
\psi:X\to\bar{X}
for the quotient map. We denote
\phi:\mathchoice{\operatorname{MCG}\left(S\right)}{\operatorname{MCG}(S)}{\operatorname{MCG}(S)}{\operatorname{MCG}(S)}\to\mathchoice{\operatorname{MCG}\left(S\right)}{\operatorname{MCG}(S)}{\operatorname{MCG}(S)}{\operatorname{MCG}(S)}/\operatorname{DT}^{n}(S)
the quotient homomorphism. Note that
\mathchoice{\operatorname{MCG}\left(S\right)}{\operatorname{MCG}(S)}{\operatorname{MCG}(S)}{\operatorname{MCG}(S)}/\operatorname{DT}^{n}(S)
acts naturally by isometries on
\bar{X}
, making

a
\mathchoice{\operatorname{MCG}\left(S\right)}{\operatorname{MCG}(S)}{\operatorname{MCG}(S)}{\operatorname{MCG}(S)}
-equivariant map.

The main result of
[
24
]
is that, for all large multiples, the power quotient
\mathchoice{\operatorname{MCG}\left(S\right)}{\operatorname{MCG}(S)}{\operatorname{MCG}(S)}{\operatorname{MCG}(S)}/\operatorname{DT}^{n}(S)
is acylindrically hyperbolic. Even better, using the results of the sequel

]
and the theory of hierarchically hyperbolic spaces, we find that the action of the power quotient on the natural quotient of the curve complex is acylindrical.

Theorem 6.3
.

There is a positive integer

such that, for all positive multiples

of

, the quotient space

is hyperbolic, and the natural action of
\mathchoice{\operatorname{MCG}\left(S\right)}{\operatorname{MCG}(S)}{\operatorname{MCG}(S)}{\operatorname{MCG}(S)}/\operatorname{DT}^{n}(S)

\mathchoice{\operatorname{MCG}\left(S\right)}{\operatorname{MCG}(S)}{\operatorname{MCG}(S)}{\operatorname{MCG}(S)}/\operatorname{DT}^{n}(S)

The quotient
\bar{X}
is hyperbolic by
[

. Furthermore,

is the top-level hyperbolic space for a hierarchically hyperbolic groups structure on

Fix a basepoint

\varphi_{k}
is not abelian, since
W_{k}
contains a non-trivial commutator.
For each
k\in\mathbf{N}
,
Theorem 4.7
provides an approximating sequence

(\Gamma_{j,k},X_{j,k},\mathcal{C}_{j,k})_{j\in\mathbf{N}}

for
\Gamma(n_{k})
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
.
Up to pre-composing
\varphi_{k}
with an element of
\mathchoice{\operatorname{Aut}\left(\Gamma\right)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}
, we can assume that, for every
k\in\mathbf{N}
, the morphism
\varphi_{k}
is
\varepsilon
-short among the set

\mathcal{P}_{k}=\mathchoice{\left\{\varphi_{k}\circ\alpha\;:\ \alpha\in\mathchoice{\operatorname{Aut}\left(\Gamma\right)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}\right\}}{\{\varphi_{k}\circ\alpha\;:\ \alpha\in\mathchoice{\operatorname{Aut}\left(\Gamma\right)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}\}}{\{\varphi_{k}\circ\alpha\;:\ \alpha\in\mathchoice{\operatorname{Aut}\left(\Gamma\right)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}\}}{\{\varphi_{k}\circ\alpha\;:\ \alpha\in\mathchoice{\operatorname{Aut}\left(\Gamma\right)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}\}}.

We denote by
\psi_{k}\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma_{j_{k},k}
an optimal approximation of
\varphi_{k}
.

By construction,
\ker\psi_{k}
is contained in
\ker\varphi_{k}
.
It follows from
(i)
that, for every finite subset
W\subset\Gamma\setminus\{1\}
, the intersection
W\cap\ker\psi_{k}
is eventually empty.
(That is, the sequence
\psi_{k}\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma_{j_{k},k}
converges to
{\rm id}\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
in the space of marked groups.)
We next claim that

\lambda_{\infty}(\psi_{k},U)\geqslant k

for every
k\in\mathbf{N}
.
If
j_{k}=0
, the conclusion follows from
(ii)
.
Suppose now that
j_{k}\geqslant 1
.
Observe that the image of
\psi_{k}
is not abelian, since if it were, the image of
\varphi_{k}
would also be abelian.
Recall that
\ell
is the length of the longest relation defining
\Gamma
.
By
Proposition 9.2
, we have

\lambda_{\infty}(\psi_{k},U)\geqslant\frac{\rho(n_{k})}{100\ell},

and the conclusion follows from our choice of
M_{k}
.
Note that our claim implies that
\lambda_{\infty}(\psi_{k},U)
diverges to infinity.

By
Theorem 8.1
, there exists
\kappa>0
, as well as automorphisms
\alpha_{k}\in\mathchoice{\operatorname{Aut}\left(\Gamma\right)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}
, such that

\lambda_{1}^{+}(\psi_{k}\circ\alpha_{k},U)<(1-\kappa)\lambda_{1}^{+}(\psi_{k},U)

for infinitely many
k\in\mathbf{N}
.
Observe that
\psi_{k}\circ\alpha_{k}
is an approximation of
\varphi_{k}\circ\alpha_{k}
.
Since
\varphi_{k}
is
\varepsilon
-short, this approximation is necessarily minimal.
Since
\psi_{k}
is optimal, it satisfies

\lambda_{1}^{+}(\psi_{k},U)\leqslant\lambda_{1}^{+}(\psi_{k}\circ\alpha_{k},U)+\varepsilon\leqslant(1-\kappa)\lambda_{1}^{+}(\psi_{k},U)+\varepsilon\,,

so
\lambda_{1}^{+}(\psi_{k},U)\leqslant\varepsilon/\kappa
. However, recall that
\lambda_{\infty}(\psi_{k},U)
diverges to infinity. By (
17
),
\lambda_{1}^{+}(\psi_{k},U)
also diverges to infinity, which leads to the desired contradiction.
∎

Since there are only finitely many morphisms of a bounded energy, we can quickly upgrade the proposition: after twisting with an automorphism, every morphism either kills an element of a certain finite subset, or lifts to a monomorphism.

Corollary 9.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be the fundamental group of a closed, orientable, hyperbolic surface, and let
U
be a finite generating set for
\Gamma
.
There exist a finite subset
W\subset\Gamma\setminus\{1\}
and an exponent
N\in\mathbf{N}
with the following properties.
For every integer
n\geqslant N
and for every morphism
\varphi\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma(n)
, one of the following holds:

—

there is an automorphism
\alpha\in\mathchoice{\operatorname{Aut}\left(\Gamma\right)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}
such that
W\cap\ker(\varphi\circ\alpha)\neq\emptyset
; or

—

there is a monomorphism
\tilde{\varphi}\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
lifting
\varphi
.

Proof.

Let
X
be a geodesic hyperbolic space endowed with a proper and co-compact action by isometries of
\Gamma
.
We write
W\subset\Gamma\setminus\{1\}
,
\lambda\in\mathbf{R}_{+}
, and
N\in\mathbf{N}
for the objects provided by
Proposition 9.4
.
Note that, up to conjugacy, there are only finitely many morphisms
\psi\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
such that
\lambda_{\infty}(\psi,U)\leqslant\lambda
.
Up to replacing
W
by a larger subset of
\Gamma\setminus\{1\}
, we can assume that for every non-injective such morphism
\psi\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
, we have
W\cap\ker\psi\neq\emptyset
.

Let
n\geqslant N
be an integer.
Let
\varphi\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma(n)
be a morphism.
Assume that
W\cap\ker(\varphi\circ\alpha)
is empty for every
\alpha\in\mathchoice{\operatorname{Aut}\left(\Gamma\right)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}
.
We are going to prove that
\varphi
admits an injective lift.
According to
Proposition 9.4
, there exist
\alpha\in\mathchoice{\operatorname{Aut}\left(\Gamma\right)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}
and a morphism
\psi\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
lifting
\varphi\circ\alpha
such that
\lambda_{\infty}(\psi,U)\leqslant\lambda
.
Note that
W\cap\ker\psi\subset W\cap\ker(\varphi\circ\alpha)
.
It follows from our assumption that
W\cap\ker\psi
is empty.
Thanks to the adjustment we made on the set
W
we can conclude that
\psi
is one-to-one.
Consequently,
\tilde{\varphi}=\psi\circ\alpha^{-1}
is a monomorphism lifting
\varphi
.
∎

We are finally ready to prove our main goal: for large enough
n
, every automorphism of
\Gamma(n)
lifts to
\Gamma
. This now follows quickly from
Corollary 9.5
, combined with the facts that the
\Gamma(n)
converge to
\Gamma
in the space of marked groups and the co-Hopf property of surface groups.

Theorem 9.6
.

Let
\Gamma
be the fundamental group of a closed, orientable, hyperbolic surface.
There is
N\in\mathbf{N}
such that, for every exponent
n\geqslant N
, the morphism
\mathchoice{\operatorname{Aut}\left(\Gamma\right)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}\to\mathchoice{\operatorname{Aut}\left(\Gamma(n)\right)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma(n))}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma(n))}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma(n))}
is onto.

Proof.

For every
n\in\mathbf{N}
, we denote by
K(n)
the kernel of the projection
\pi_{n}\colon\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow\Gamma(n)
.
By
Example 7.2
, we may apply
Corollary 9.5
with
G=\Gamma
.
We write
N\in\mathbf{N}
and
W\subset\Gamma\setminus\{1\}
for the data given by this corollary.
Up to increasing the value of
N
we can assume that for every integer
n\geqslant N
, the intersection
W\cap K(n)
is empty (see
Remark 4.10
).

Let
n\geqslant N
.
Let
\varphi\colon\Gamma(n)\to\Gamma(n)
be an isomorphism.
Recall that
\pi_{n}
induces a map
\mathchoice{\operatorname{Aut}\left(\Gamma\right)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}\to\mathchoice{\operatorname{Aut}\left(\Gamma(n)\right)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma(n))}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma(n))}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma(n))}
that we write
\alpha\mapsto\alpha_{n}
.
In particular,
\varphi\circ\pi_{n}\circ\alpha=\varphi\circ\alpha_{n}\circ\pi_{n}
, for every
\alpha\in\mathchoice{\operatorname{Aut}\left(\Gamma\right)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}
.
As
\varphi
and
\alpha_{n}
are one-to-one, the kernel of
\varphi\circ\alpha_{n}\circ\pi_{n}
coincides with
K(n)
.
Hence
W\cap\ker(\varphi\circ\pi_{n}\circ\alpha)
is empty, for every
\alpha\in\mathchoice{\operatorname{Aut}\left(\Gamma\right)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}
.
Applying
Corollary 9.5
, there exists a monomorphism
\tilde{\varphi}\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
such that
\pi_{n}\circ\tilde{\varphi}=\varphi\circ\pi_{n}
.
Since
\Gamma
is a surface group, it is co-Hopfian.
Thus the monomorphism
\tilde{\varphi}\colon\Gamma\hookrightarrow\Gamma
is actually an automorphism, whence the result.
∎

Combined with
Proposition 6.8
, this completes the proof of
Theorem 1.2
.

Proof of
Theorem 1.2
.

The natural maps
\mathchoice{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}\left(S_{*}\right)}{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}(S_{*})}{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}(S_{*})}{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}(S_{*})}/\operatorname{DT}^{n}(S_{*})\to\mathchoice{\operatorname{Aut}\left(\Gamma(n)\right)}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma(n))}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma(n))}{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma(n))}
and
\mathchoice{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}\left(S\right)}{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}(S)}{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}(S)}{\operatorname{MCG}^{\pm}(S)}/\operatorname{DT}^{n}(S)\to\mathchoice{\operatorname{Out}\left(\Gamma(n)\right)}{\operatorname{Out}(\Gamma(n))}{\operatorname{Out}(\Gamma(n))}{\operatorname{Out}(\Gamma(n))}
are injective by
Proposition 6.8
and surjective by
Theorem 9.6
.
∎

ABSTRACT
IntroductionWorld Health Organization (WHO) in 2010, recommended 0.75 mg/kg single dose primaquine for killing gametocytes after ruling out glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency. However, considering the practical feasibility of G6PD testing, WHO revised the recommendation in 2012 to 0.25 mg/kg single dose primaquine on day-1. This study aims to investigate the efficacy and safety of single-low dose primaquine (0.25 mg/kg) administered on day-1 to intervention arm compared to standard regimen, high-dose primaquine (0.75 mg/kg) administered on day-2 to control arm.Methods and analysisThis is a hospital based, open-label, multi-centric, randomized-controlled trial being conducted at four different sites in India, with a target sample size of 496 i.e., 124 participants per site. Participants above 18 years of age with uncomplicated P. falciparum malaria and normal glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) activity, defined as > 4.0 IU/g Hb using the point-of-care test, weight > 40 kg, haemoglobin > 8 g/dL are treated with ACT and randomized to either control or intervention arm. Efficacy assessment is based on gametocytemia as inferred from examination of stained peripheral blood smears during enrolment and follow-up. In addition, quantitative nucleic acid sequence-based amplification (QT-NASBA) for Pfs25 mRNA, will be used to quantify the gametocytes on day 1, 2/3, 7, and 14. Safety assessment during 14-day follow-up will be based on percentage of participants with more than 25% drop in haemoglobin, haemoglobin drop of 5 g/dl, haemoglobin value less than 8 g/dl, mean of maximum drop in haemoglobin during 28-day follow-up; and frequency of adverse events reported during the follow-up in both the arms. The primary outcome of the study is based on gametocyte density and point prevalence of gametocyte (efficacy) and haemoglobin concentration (safety) from enrolment to day 14 (for efficacy) and day 28 (for safety).Ethics and disseminationStudy has been approved by Institutional Ethics Committee (NIMR/IEC-M/2023/970/v6-Jun/01). The study findings will be disseminated through publications in peer-reviewed journals as well as academic presentations. The findings will support evidence-based recommendations for national malaria control programs in India, potentially advocating for the adoption of the lower dose primaquine regimen.Trial registration numberCTRI/2025/07/091988 (CTRI registration).Trial statusEnrollment started in August, 2025. Introduction

Malaria, a life-threatening disease, is caused by a protozoan parasite of genus Plasmodium and transmitted by an infected female Anopheles mosquito. The four most common type of Plasmodium species that infect humans are P. falciparum, P. vivax, P. malariae, and P. ovale. P. falciparum demonstrates significantly higher morbidity and mortality in contrast to other human malarial parasites [1, 2]. According to the world malaria report there were an estimated 263 million cases and 597,000 death cases in 2023 [3].

The current first line treatment for falciparum malaria includes artemisinin-based combination therapy (ACT) which substantially reduces the transmission at the population level by rapidly clearing asexual parasites and blocking de-novo development of gametocytes. Since, these ACTs lack potential to kill mature gametocytes, and mature gametocyte, if present (in blood) at time of initiating ACT treatment in blood, persist in blood post- treatment resulting in onward transmission to mosquitoes [4–6]. The gametocytes transmission to mosquitoes can occur even at low gametocyte density [7–9]. Only 8-aminoquinolines like primaquine, are effective against mature P. falciparum gametocytes, and is most widely used drug of this class for its gametocytocidal activity [10].

Primaquine is an effective anti-malarial drug that clears the circulating mature P. falciparum gametocytes owing to the oxidative damage caused by primaquine metabolites. This established activity of primaquine is expected to render most malaria patients free of gametocytes, soon after the initiation of treatment with primaquine and ACT [11–13]. Primaquine produces several active and unstable metabolites through oxidase pathway, especially cytochrome P450 2D6 [14]. These reactive metabolites are mainly responsible for gametocytocidal activity and hemolytic toxicity, particularly, acute hemolysis in participants with glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency [15]. World Health Organization (WHO), in 2010, recommended 0.75 mg/kg single dose (45 mg) primaquine for killing gametocytes, in areas where G6PD deficiency risk is low or testing for ruling out G6PD deficiency is available [16]. On the basis of diverse observations, single dose of primaquine (0.75 mg/kg) was found to be well tolerated in Myanmar [17], Colombia [18, 19], Sudan [20], Vietnam [21], India [22], and Thailand [23]. However, a study in Tanzania reported significant decrease in hemoglobin concentration with high dose primaquine regimen (ACT plus 0.75 mg/kg primaquine) for P. falciparum malaria. The study reported mean fall of 25 g/L in G6PD deficient children with G6PD A–variant along with 16 g/L in heterozygotes (G6PD A variant) and 5 g/L in children with wild-type genotype having G6PD B variant [24]. Similar findings from Vanuatu reported seven cases of acute hemoglobinuria with severe anemia in G6PD deficient men [25]. Given the anticipated risk of hemolysis with 45 mg primaquine in G6PD deficient individuals, WHO in 2012 revised the guidelines to 0.25 mg/kg single low- dose primaquine (SLD-PQ) on day-1 without having the requirement for G6PD testing [26]. Additionally, it was mentioned that countries who have already implemented 0.75 mg/kg primaquine will continue with this dose, until further evidence of lower dose of 0.25 mg/kg is generated [27]. Also, in WHO 2023 guideline for malaria, use of primaquine for P. falciparum management was mentioned as “low certainty evidence”.

After 2012, many countries amended the guidelines and recommended the low-dose regimen, as primaquine administered for P. falciparum is for the larger good of the community and does not provide immediate benefit to the patients, thus should not cause any harm to the patients taking primaquine. The recommendations including dose, and day of administration of primaquine varies across the malaria endemic countries [28]. In India, the National programme guidelines recommend 0.75 mg/kg primaquine on day-2. Further, according to the current guidelines, primaquine is dispensed to patients on day-1 for self-administration on day-2, consequently reducing treatment compliance.

There is limited information on the compliance of day-2 primaquine in India, and how day-1 supervised administration will affect gametocytocidal efficacy and safety. In line with WHO recommendations if the National programme has to switch to low dose regimes, it is essential having the evidence of gametocytocidal efficacy with the reduced dose of primaquine, i.e., 0.25 mg/kg. The recommendation of SLD-PQ on day 1 will improve adherence by ensuring initial dose administration under supervision. Moreover, individuals receiving primaquine on day-1 have less chances of gametocytemia on day 7 in comparison to those receiving primaquine on day 2 or 3 [29]. Further, pharmacokinetic action may be enhanced by facilitating gametocyte clearance. Several studies have been done outside India, and with anti-malarial medications it is imperative having regional studies before suggesting or making a change in the recommended dose of primaquine in the guidelines for P. falciparum management.

Given this, the current study was planned primarily to assess the efficacy and safety of single low-dose of primaquine (0.25 mg/kg) administered on day 1 versus standard high-dose (0.75 mg/kg) regimen administered on day 2 among G6PD normal patients. This study will provide evidence on the efficacy and safety of treatment regimen potentially informing updates to the national malaria treatment guidelines. If SLD-PQ demonstrates non-inferiority compared with standard high dose, and ensures good compliance then it may lead to the adoption of new policy for P. falciparum infection control, irrespective of G6PD status. The secondary objective is to study the pharmacokinetics of high-dose primaquine and low-dose primaquine in a subsample.

Methods

This protocol has been framed in accordance with the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) guidelines [30]. The trial documents have been framed in accordance with the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) framework and the study results will be published accordingly [31].

Study design

This is an open-label, multi-centric, randomised-controlled clinical trial in glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) normal patients with uncomplicated P. falciparum malaria comparing a single low-dose primaquine (0.25 mg/kg) to the current standard high-dose primaquine (0.75 mg/kg) treatment for its gametocytocidal efficacy and safety. This trial uses a non-inferiority design for testing efficacy.

Study site

The participants in the trial are being enrolled from four distinct study sites across India viz; Community Health Centre (CHC), Darbha, Bastar district in Chhattisgarh; CHC, Goilkera, West Singhbhum district in Jharkhand; Civil Hospital, Surat, Gujarat; and CHC Chawngte, Lawngtlai district in Mizoram.

Blood sample collection

Venous blood is collected in an ethylene-di-amine tetra acetic acid (EDTA) vial on days: 3 mL on day 1 (in both the arms), and 2 mL on rest of the days, day 2 (only in intervention arm), day 3 (only in control arm), days 7 and 14 (in both the arms). Venous blood is used for testing haemoglobin, G6PD activity, blood smear preparation, and storage in cryovial until further use, for quantification of Pfs25 mRNA. For blood sampling, finger prick is done on day 2 (in control arm), 3 (in intervention arm), 10, 21, and 28 (in both the arms).

Laboratory related procedures

Malaria Rapid Diagnostic Test Kit

Using Standard Q® Malaria P.f/P.v Ag kit, a single step, rapid, immunochromatographic test for the detection of Plasmodium falciparum Histidine-rich Protein-II (HRP-II) and pLDH (Plasmodium lactate dehydrogenase) specific to Plasmodium vivax in human whole blood.

Peripheral blood smear preparation and microscopic examination

For each participant, two blood smears are prepared (both thin and thick smear on one slide). The slides are rapidly stained using 10% Giemsa for 10–15 min.

Giemsa-stained blood smears are examined under microscope by two laboratory technicians for species identification and estimating parasite density—separately for asexual and sexual stages (gametocyte) of the parasite. Parasite density is expressed as the number of asexual parasites per µL of blood, calculation involves the division of number of asexual parasites by the number of white blood cells (counted) and then multiplying by 8000, represented in Eq. 1. A blood smear is classified as negative if it reveals no asexual parasite under 200 high power field (HPF). The reading is recorded as discordant if the difference between the two microscopist is greater than 25%, and in that case third microscopist evaluates the slide.

Hemoglobin assessment

Blood samples are tested for hemoglobin levels using HemoCue, according to manufacturer’s instructions. Hemoglobin assessment is done on day of enrolment and on all follow-up days.

G6PD assessment

Blood samples taken from each study participant is also used for G6PD activity analysis using SD biosensor. The SD biosensor reading > 4.0 IU/g Hb is considered as normal.

Urine pregnancy test

On day 1, female participants of the child-bearing age are tested for pregnancy using urine ᵦ-HCG pregnancy test (as primaquine is contraindicated during pregnancy).

Blood storage for further use

The collected venous blood samples are being stored in TRIzol in duplicates at − 20 °C, and labelled with the participant ID and the time point of collection. These samples will be used to quantify gametocytes using quantitative nucleic acid sequence-based amplification (QT-NASBA) by targeting Pfs25 mRNA [32].

Pharmacokinetics study

Pharmacokinetics study of standard high-dose and single low-dose regimens of primaquine will be conducted in a subset of samples to analyse the 4 different stages of drugs such as absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion, known as ADME. In this context, participants willing to provide blood sample at frequent interval in first 24 h after administration of primaquine will be selected (N = 10 each arm). The total sample size (N = 80), participants will be recruited from four different study sites for pharmacokinetics evaluation. Per site sample size may be small with respect to previously reported studies due to ethical challenge of repeated blood collection at an interval of 4 h from every single participant in the remote tribal settings. Despite this small sample size, the study will provide preliminary insights into pharmacokinetics of low dose and high dose primaquine. Blood samples (2 mL) will be collected at following time points: 0 h, 1 h, 2 h, 4 h, 8 h, 12 h, 18 h and 24 h for pharmacokinetic analysis. The collected blood samples will be centrifuged at 2000 × g for 10 min to obtain plasma which will be stored at − 80 °C until further analysis. In addition, urine sample will be collected at following timepoints: before administration of Primaquine, 0–4, 4–8, 8–12, 12–18, and 18–24 h after administration of primaquine. Following sample preparation, liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (LC–MS) system will be used for analysing primaquine, and its major metabolite 5,6-orthoquinone primaquine (5,6-PQ), a surrogate marker of Hydroxyprimaquine. The following pharmacokinetic parameters will be analysed using plasma and urine samples: maximum concentration (Cmax), time to Cmax, amount of drug excreted, and cumulative amount of drug excreted.

Schedule of events

Day 1–Screening and enrollment

For assessing the eligibility, the venous blood drawn from the study participant is used for preparing blood smears, testing hemoglobin, and G6PD activity. Further, each participant is also assessed on the basis of inclusion and exclusion criteria (mentioned above in study participants section). Also, clinical history, and physical examination is performed for the participant. Moreover, participants in the intervention group receive 15 mg of primaquine on day 1.

Day 2, 3

All participants are reviewed and receive ACT doses in presence of study staff, and those in control arm receives 45 mg primaquine on day 2. Blood sample is collected for hemoglobin assessment, peripheral blood smear preparation, and for storage in cryovial (as per schedule of events). In addition, patients are assessed for clinical symptoms, adverse events (if any). Also, a brief physical examination is conducted.

Day 7, 10, 14, 21, and 28

The study team visits each participant. Again, blood samples are collected for hemoglobin assessment, peripheral blood smear preparation, and for storage in cryovial (Day 7 and 14). In addition, patients are assessed for clinical symptoms, and adverse events including fever, muscle pain, joint pain, fatigue, headache, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal pain, loss of appetite, shortness of breath, dizziness, and dark urine (if any).

All the study related information and results obtained from laboratory procedures are recorded in case record form (CRF).

Interventions

Primaquine is provided in accordance to the treatment arm assigned. In intervention arm, primaquine is provided as 0.25 mg/kg (15 mg) on day 1 itself in presence of study staff. The control arm receives standard recommended regimen, i.e., 0.75 mg/kg (45 mg) primaquine on day 2 in presence of study staff.

In addition to primaquine, ACT as recommended by the National treatment guidelines is provided irrespective of the treatment arm. However, ACT used was different according to the site.

For Mizoram site

Artemether-Lumefantrine (AL) is used as follows:

Day 1 to 3 – Artemether-Lumefantrine (80/480 mg) – two times a day – Morning (supervised) and evening (unsupervised).

For Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, and Jharkhand site

Artesunate Sulphadoxine-Pyrimethamine (AS-SP) is used as follows:

Day 1—Artesunate – 200 mg, SP (1500/75) mg–Once a day (supervised).

Day 2—Artesunate 200 mg–Once a day (supervised).

Day 3—Artesunate 200 mg–Once a day (supervised).

These drugs are administered with food to minimize the gastrointestinal issues. Participants are observed and dose is re-administered in case of vomiting within 60 min. If any participant vomits again after re-administration, he/she will be referred to a physician for a suspected complicated P. falciparum malaria infection and to receive parenteral treatment and will be withdrawn from the study.

Rescue treatment

The study participants who will not respond to treatment will be given rescue treatment. Also, if a patient vomits twice, patients will be withdrawn from the study and will receive parenteral treatment. Patients with signs of severe or complicated malaria will be hospitalised and treated with parenteral therapy and relevant supportive treatment. Patients meeting any criteria of therapeutic failure or reinfection during follow-up, will be treated according to National treatment guidelines.

Criteria for discontinuing or modifying allocated interventions

The participant has the right to withdraw from study at any time. If participant withdraws his/her consent, use antimalarial medications outside study protocol, and is unable to be located for remaining visits, the patient will be excluded/withdrawn. Investigator and study physician judgement will be used to judge whether or not an adverse event (AE) is of sufficient severity to require stopping the participant’s treatment. If the study participant is withdrawn due to adverse event during follow-up, follow-up visits or telephone call will be arranged till the time AE is resolved or participant is stable, whichever is earlier.

Randomization, allocation concealment, and blinding

Randomization and sequence generation

After enrolment (Day 1), participants are randomized to either intervention or control arm, and allocation ratio is 1:1. The randomization sequence was generated by independent statistician not involved with enrolment or participant assignment. To reduce the predictability of pattern, block randomization sequence with unequal block sizes of 8 and 12 was generated. A separate randomization sequence was generated for each site to ensure concealment and reduce bias.

Allocation concealment

This is an open-label trial, as the study participants and study team are aware of the allocation. Thus, to reduce the selection bias, allocation concealment was done centrally using the REDCap software. The randomization sequence generated was fed in the REDCap software for each study site separately. After eligibility assessment, upon entering that the participant is eligible in REDCap, it displays the assigned group for the participant.

For places with limited internet connectivity like Jharkhand and Chhattisgarh sites, sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes (SNOSE) system is employed for the randomization of participants in the trial.

Blinding

Not applicable as the study is an open-label randomized controlled trial.

Outcome measures

The primary efficacy endpoint will be assessed based on point prevalence of gametocyte on day 1, 2, 3, 7, 10, and 14 (for blood smear examinations) and day 1, 2/3, 7, and 14 (for QT-NASBA) for gametocyte density, and also mean time to gametocyte clearance in both the arms

The primary safety endpoint of the study will be based on assessing haemoglobin concentration from day 1 to day 28; and adverse events reported during the follow-up in both the arms.

The secondary endpoint of the study includes pharmacokinetic parameters of primaquine including maximum concentration (Cmax), time to Cmax, amount of drug excreted, and cumulative amount of drug excreted, in a subset of participants receiving single low-dose of primaquine and standard high-dose of primaquine.

Safety reporting and considerations

The site Investigator and site staff are responsible primarily for detecting, documenting and reporting adverse events or serious adverse events occurring during the study duration.

Adverse events (AE)

Definition of AE

It is any untoward medical occurrence in a participant during study, temporally associated with the use of medicinal product, whether or not found related to the medicinal product.

For AE, severity, relationship with the study drug, and outcome is assessed by study physician during the course of the study.

Serious adverse events (SAE)

SAE is any untoward medical occurrence that, at any dose results in death, is life-threatening, requires hospitalisation or prolongation of existing hospitalisation, results in disability/incapacity. In case of any SAE, it has to be reported to site IEC within 24 h, and causality assessment will be done by study team and IEC.

Data safety monitoring board (DSMB)

A Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) has been set up for safeguarding participant interest and monitoring the overall conduct of the study including the recruitment, study progress, adverse events, hospitalisation of participants, blood transfusions and any deaths. DSMB is a committee of 5 to 6 members including clinician, a biostatistician and field experts. DSMB meeting will be held at frequent intervals.

Sample size

For assessing (efficacy) non-inferiority of the single low-dose primaquine arm to the standard group with 80 percent power at the 5 percent significance level, assuming 6.6 days for gametocyte clearance in the standard arm, standard deviation of 6 days, non-inferiority margin of 2 days, 10 percent loss to follow-up [33]. The target sample size is 124 participants per group. As the sample size must be sufficiently powered to demonstrate non-inferiority of single low-dose primaquine for various sites also. Thus, sites from Jharkhand and Chhattisgarh (combined) will recruit 248 participants, 124 in each arm, and sites from Mizoram and Gujarat (combined) will recruit 248 participants, 124 in each arm. Thus, the total sample size is 496 participants over 4 sites, 248 in each arm.

Data analysis

Data collected from participants will be imported to STATA for further analysis. The efficacy analysis will be based on the gametocyte density calculated using blood smear examination and QT-NASBA (targeting pfs25 mRNA) at the enrolment and during follow-up. The point prevalence of gametocyte on day 1, 2, 3, 7, 10, and 14 (for blood smear examinations) and day 1, 2/3, 7, and 14 (for QT-NASBA) will be compared in both the arms. Kaplan Meier curves will be used to assess and compare the gametocyte clearance among the participants who were gametocyte positive at enrolment in both the study arms.

The null hypothesis for the non-inferiority comparison will be tested by calculating the 95% confidence intervals for the difference in mean time to gametocyte clearance between both the groups.. If the upper bound of 95% confidence interval of difference in mean time to gametocyte clearance lies below the non-inferiority margin of 2 days, the low dose intervention will be considered as non-inferior. As per the recommended practice for the non-inferiority trialsls, per-protocol analysis will be done for the primary and secondary outcome measure [34]. However, as recommended in some studies and for minimizing the impact of protocol violations, and missing data we will be using Intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis also [35].

Safety assessment is based on percentage of participants with (a) more than 25% drop in haemoglobin during 14-day follow-up period, (b) haemoglobin drop of 5 g/dl during 14-day follow-up period, (c) haemoglobin value less than 8 g/dl during 14-day follow-up period, (d) mean of maximum drop in haemoglobin during 28-day follow-up; and frequency of adverse events reported during the follow-up in both the arms.

For all the analysis, the participants developing recurrent parasitemia during 28–day follow-up will be censored on the day recurrent parasitemia will be reported.

The secondary outcome of pharmacokinetic study includes maximum drug concentration (Cmax), time to maximum drug concentration (Tmax), and area under the curve (AUC) and will be compared between two treatment groups.

Discussion

The current clinical trial aims to study the gametocytocidal efficacy and safety of single low-dose (15 mg) primaquine given on day 1 versus standard primaquine (45 mg) regimen given on day 2 for falciparum malaria in Indian setting. In low-transmission areas, it is critical targeting mature gametocytes for reducing transmission. In 2010, WHO recommended 0.75 mg/kg single dose primaquine for killing gametocytes after ruling out G6PD deficiency. However, considering the practical unavailability of G6PD testing device at the peripheral health system and risk of hemolysis with primaquine administration in G6PD deficient patients, WHO later revised the recommendation to 0.25 mg/kg single low-dose primaquine on day-1. However, it was mentioned that countries who have already adopted high-dose of primaquine (0.75 mg/kg) may continue to use until evidence is generated for efficacy of lower (0.25 mg/kg) dose of primaquine.

Globally several studies have been conducted to evaluate the effect of primaquine and its different doses for causing gametocyte sterilization in management of falciparum malaria. In Uganda, Eziefula et.al. reported the equivalent efficacy of 0.4 mg/kg primaquine as compared to 0.75 mg/kg primaquine dose [37]. Studies have reported about the safety of single low-dose primaquine among G6PD deficient children in Uganda, Congo, and Cambodia [38, 39]. However, there is paucity of data for India, and knowledge gap exists to support the efficacy and safety of single low-dose primaquine (SLD-PQ) and its adoption in national programme guidelines.

Gametocytaemia is generally assessed by examination of peripheral blood smear. However, it is important to note that its sensitivity is relatively low when the density of circulating gametocyte (sexual parasites) is below 1% [12, 40–42]. Therefore, in support of microscopic observations, a molecular technique quantitative nucleic acid sequence-based amplification (QT-NASBA) will be employed in current study for real-time monitoring of Pfs25 mRNA for quantification of P. falciparum gametocytes. QT-NASBA has a sensitivity of 0.01–0.1 gametocytes/µL of blood whereas standard microscopy has a detection limit of 16 gametocytes/µL of blood [7, 32]. Enhanced sensitivity of QT-NASBA will detect the (sub)microscopic level of gametocytes at the day of enrolment and during follow-up period of 28 days. This was confirmed by previous reports depicting (sub)microscopic gametocytaemia in endemic samples [43–46]. The implementation of molecular technique for evaluating gametocytaemia is a strength of current trial.

This clinical trial will help to generate country-specific evidence in relevance to the efficacy and safety of single low-dose primaquine (0.25 mg/kg). If low-dose primaquine is found non-inferior compared to high-dose primaquine, these findings would support the adoption of single low-dose primaquine (0.25 mg/kg) in national programme, and thus improving overall compliance.

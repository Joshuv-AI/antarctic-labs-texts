1
Introduction

The discovery of a Higgs boson (
\mathrm{H}
) by the ATLAS
[
1
]
and CMS
[
2
]
experiments in 2012
[
3
,
4
]
marks a milestone in understanding the electroweak symmetry breaking.
With a measured mass of
125.38\pm 0.14\,\mathrm{GeV}

[
5
]
, the observed interactions with gauge bosons and third-generation fermions, as well as all measured properties of the Higgs boson, agree with standard model (SM) predictions
[
6
,
7
]
.
Following the first evidence of Higgs boson decays to muons
[
8
]
, an important next step is the observation of the Higgs boson coupling to second-generation quarks.
Searches for Higgs boson decays to a charm quark-antiquark pair (
\mathrm{c\bar{c}}
) provide direct access to the charm Yukawa coupling (
y_{c}
).
Using
138\,\mathrm{fb}^{-1}
of proton-proton collision data at
13\,\mathrm{TeV}
, the ATLAS
[
9
]
and CMS
[
10
]
Collaborations reported observed (expected) 95% confidence level (CL) intervals on
\kappa_{\mathrm{c}}=y_{c}/{y_{c}}^{\text{SM}}
of
|\kappa_{\mathrm{c}}|<4.2
(4.1) and
{1.1<|\kappa_{\mathrm{c}}|<5.5}
(
|\kappa_{\mathrm{c}}|<3.4
), respectively, studying the associated production of a Higgs boson with a
\mathrm{V}
(
\mathrm{W}
or
\mathrm{Z}
) boson.
These proceedings report a new search for
\mathrm{H}{\to}\mathrm{c}\mathrm{\bar{c}}
via associated production of a Higgs boson with a top quark-antiquark pair (
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{H}
), while the
\mathrm{H}{\to}\mathrm{b}\mathrm{\bar{b}}
decay is measured simultaneously. In addition, the SM processes
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{Z}(\mathrm{Z}{\to}\mathrm{c\bar{c}})
and
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{Z}(\mathrm{Z}{\to}\mathrm{b\bar{b}})
are measured to validate the analysis strategy for
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{H}
production.

2
Analysis strategy

The analysis uses proton-proton collision data at
\sqrt{s}=13\,\mathrm{TeV}
, collected by the CMS detector and corresponding to an integrated luminosity of
138\,\mathrm{fb}^{-1}
and is carried out in three channels targeting the fully hadronic (
0L
), semileptonic (
1L
), and dileptonic (
2L
) decays of the
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}
pair.
To identify the jet flavor, the ParticleNet algorithm
[
11
]
is used.
Two discriminants are defined from the ParticleNet outputs:
p_{\mathrm{B}{+}\mathrm{C}}
, which separates heavy- from light-flavor jets, and
p_{\mathrm{B}\textrm{vs}\mathrm{C}}
, which distinguishes
\mathrm{b}
from
\mathrm{c}
jets.
Based on these, 11 mutually exclusive tagging categories are defined as shown in Fig.
1
.
Compared to the previous tagging algorithm used in the CMS Collaboration,
DeepJet

[
12
]
, ParticleNet improves the
\mathrm{b}
vs. light and
\mathrm{c}
vs.
\mathrm{b}
jet rejection by up to a factor of two each at the same signal jet efficiency.

Figure 1:
Distribution of
\mathrm{b}
,
\mathrm{c}
and light-flavor jets in the two-dimensional ParticleNet discriminant plane, together with the tagging categories and the corresponding efficiencies
[
13
]
.

A multiclass event classifier based on ParT
[
14
]
is developed to classify events. The ParT classifier is a transformer based algorithm which combines inputs such basic kinematic properties and tagging information with pairwise features for all object pairs derived using their four-momenta to better learn the event kinematic properties and object correlations.
The classifier is trained to assign likelihood scores across 10 (9) classes in the
0L
(
1L
,
2L
) channel: two
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{H}
classes (
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{H}(\mathrm{H}{\to}\mathrm{c}\mathrm{\bar{c}})
,
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{H}(\mathrm{H}{\to}\mathrm{b}\mathrm{\bar{b}})
), two
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{Z}
classes (
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{Z}(\mathrm{Z}{\to}\mathrm{c\bar{c}})
,
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{Z}(\mathrm{Z}{\to}\mathrm{b\bar{b}})
), five
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}{+}\text{jets}
classes (
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}{+}\mathrm{b}
,
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}{+}{\geq}2\mathrm{b}
,
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}{+}\mathrm{c}
,
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}{+}{\geq}2\mathrm{c}
,
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}{+}\text{light}
), and only in the
0L
channel, one class for QCD multijet.

The output scores of the ParT classifier are used to optimize the event selection and categorization.
In the
0L
channel, a stringent requirement on the QCD multijet score suppresses this background by about four orders of magnitude, making it negligible compared to
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}{+}\text{jets}
.
Similarly, a selection on the
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}{+}\text{light}
discriminant further reduces
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}{+}\text{light}
contamination in all channels.
Only events with a high
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{H}
or
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{Z}
score value are retained for the signal extraction and background estimation.
Events passing these criteria are categorized into four signal regions (SRs), enriched in
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{H}(\mathrm{H}{\to}\mathrm{c}\mathrm{\bar{c}})
,
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{H}(\mathrm{H}{\to}\mathrm{b}\mathrm{\bar{b}})
,
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{Z}(\mathrm{Z}{\to}\mathrm{c\bar{c}})
, and
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{Z}(\mathrm{Z}{\to}\mathrm{b\bar{b}})
, together with five control regions (CRs), to estimate the normalizations of the
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}{+}\text{jets}
background components.

The
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{H}
and
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{Z}
production rates are determined via a binned profile likelihood fit to data.
For each of the four signal processes, the expected yield is scaled by a signal strength modifier
\mu
, defined as
{\left(\sigma\mathcal{B}\right)_{\text{obs}}/\left(\sigma\mathcal{B}\right)_{\text{SM}}}
where
\sigma
is the production cross section and
\mathcal{B}
is the branching fraction of the
\mathrm{H}
or
\mathrm{Z}
decays.
The dominant background,
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}{+}\text{jets}
, is estimated with the normalizations of the
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}{+}\mathrm{c}
,
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}{+}{\geq}2\mathrm{c}
,
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}{+}\mathrm{b}
,
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}{+}{\geq}2\mathrm{b}
, and
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}{+}\text{light}
components floating independently and constrained in dedicated control regions.
For
\mu_{\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{H}(\mathrm{H}{\to}\mathrm{c}\mathrm{\bar{c}})}
, the leading uncertainty is statistical (
{\approx}74\%
), whereas for
\mu_{\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{H}(\mathrm{H}{\to}\mathrm{b}\mathrm{\bar{b}})}
, the largest uncertainties are theoretical in the
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}{+}\text{jets}
model, representing
{\approx}60\%
of the total uncertainty.

3
Results

The analysis is validated by measuring the
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{Z}
signal strengths
\mu_{\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{Z}(\mathrm{Z}{\to}\mathrm{c\bar{c}})}=1.02\,^{+0.79}_{-0.84}
and
\mu_{\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{Z}(\mathrm{Z}{\to}\mathrm{b\bar{b}})}=1.47\,^{+0.45}_{-0.41}
,
which agree with the SM prediction within one (two) standard deviation for the
\mathrm{Z}{\to}\mathrm{c\bar{c}}
(
\mathrm{Z}{\to}\mathrm{b\bar{b}}
) decay mode, and with measurements in the leptonic decay channel
[
15
,
16
]
.

\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{H}(\mathrm{H}{\to}\mathrm{c}\mathrm{\bar{c}})
(or
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{H}(\mathrm{H}{\to}\mathrm{b}\mathrm{\bar{b}})
) and background yields.
The best-fit signal strengths for
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{H}
production are determined to be
\mu_{\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{H}(\mathrm{H}{\to}\mathrm{c}\mathrm{\bar{c}})}=-1.6\pm 4.5
and
\mu_{\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{H}(\mathrm{H}{\to}\mathrm{b}\mathrm{\bar{b}})}=0.91\,^{+0.26}_{-0.22}
, with an observed (expected) significance of 4.4 (4.5) standard deviations for
\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{H}(\mathrm{H}{\to}\mathrm{b}\mathrm{\bar{b}})
.
An upper limit on
\mu_{\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{H}(\mathrm{H}{\to}\mathrm{c}\mathrm{\bar{c}})}
is extracted using the CLs criterion, with an observed (expected) 95% CL upper limit on
\mu_{\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{H}(\mathrm{H}{\to}\mathrm{c}\mathrm{\bar{c}})}

\mathrm{t\bar{t}}\mathrm{H}

\mathrm{V}\mathrm{H}

For
\kappa_{\mathrm{c}}

95\%

|\kappa_{\mathrm{c}}|<3.5
.
This represents the most stringent constraint on
\kappa_{\mathrm{c}}

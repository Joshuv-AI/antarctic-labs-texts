An Arthroscopic Oblique Ripstop Technique in Medial Meniscal Root Repair
Authors: Hafizz Sanitsakul, Wachiraphan Parinyakhup, Tanarat Boonriong, Trisak Kingchan, Piyaonn Musikabhumma, Chaiwat Chuaychoosakoon
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractArthroscopic medial meniscal root repair is essential for restoring native meniscal function in patients with root tears, especially when the articular cartilage remains intact. The pull‐out technique is commonly used; however, suture cut‐through remains a concern when the meniscal tissue is of poor quality. To address this issue, we describe a modified oblique ripstop technique, which incorporates an obliquely oriented horizontal mattress stitch to reinforce the meniscal root and prevent suture cut‐through. This configuration aligns the ripstop suture perpendicular to the cinch stitch, thereby enhancing resistance to pull‐out forces. This technique is simple, reproducible, and may offer biomechanical advantages in compromised meniscal tissue conditions.

Medial meniscal root (MMR) tears have gained increasing attention because of their detrimental effects on knee biomechanics. These lesions disrupt the hoop stress mechanism, leading to abnormal load distribution and joint degeneration. 1 Conservative treatment is often ineffective, making surgical repair the preferred option for restoring function, particularly in active patients or those without advanced osteoarthritis. If left untreated, MMR injury creates a biomechanical environment similar to that of total meniscectomy, significantly increasing the risk of early osteoarthritis. 2 Arthroscopic meniscal root repair is recommended to restore the load‐distributing function of the meniscus, particularly in cases where the articular cartilage is intact or only mildly degenerated. 3 , 4

Among available surgical techniques, the pull‐out method is commonly used because of its simplicity and effectiveness. However, its efficacy is limited when the meniscal tissue quality is compromised, leading to the risk of suture cut‐through. In rotator cuff repair, a ripstop technique was developed to address similar challenges by reinforcing the repair and preventing suture migration. 5 , 6 , 7 Biomechanical studies have shown that this configuration results in a significantly higher load‐to‐failure strength compared with standard single‐row repairs. 8

Inspired by this principle, we propose a modified ripstop approach for meniscal root repair. This technique employs a ripstop suture oriented obliquely in a horizontal mattress configuration and positioned perpendicular to the cinch stitch. This configuration may offer superior resistance to tensile forces and prevent suture cut‐through in cases of meniscal tissue degeneration.

Although effective, the pull‐out technique requires meticulous suture placement and tissue handling to avoid failure. In this context, enhancing the repair strength through biomechanical principles adapted from other orthopedic subspecialties offers an innovative solution. Ripstop stitching, which has been successfully employed in rotator cuff repair, serves as a compelling model for adaptation. The oblique ripstop technique is shown in Video 1.

SURGICAL TECHNIQUE

Before the procedure, magnetic resonance imaging confirms a tear in the MMR without associated cartilage damage (Figure 1). The procedure is performed under general anesthesia with the patient in the supine position and a pneumatic tourniquet applied to the proximal thigh. Standard anteromedial and anterolateral arthroscopic portals are established adjacent to the patellar tendon. Diagnostic arthroscopy confirms an MMR tear without significant chondral damage.

Magnetic resonance imaging of right knee showed the medial meniscal root injury with (A) cleft sign in sagittal view and (B) medial meniscal protrusion in coronal view.

A percutaneous release of the medial collateral ligament is performed to allow adequate exposure of the medial compartment of the knee. The tibial footprint of the MMR is identified and debrided to create a bleeding bone surface conducive to healing. A tibial tunnel is then drilled from the anteromedial tibial cortex to the root footprint using a standard guide.

The repair begins by passing a 2‐0 FiberWire (Arthrex, Naples, FL) through the posterolateral region of the medial meniscus using a self‐retrieving suture passer (Figure 2A). This suture is exchanged for a No. 2 Ethibond (Ethibond, Somerville, NJ). A second suture is passed anteromedial to the first to form a horizontal mattress configuration oriented obliquely across the meniscal root (Figure 2B). This stitch also is exchanged for a No. 2 Ethibond, serving as the ripstop reinforcement.

A third suture, placed posterior to the ripstop configuration, is passed using the same device (Figure 3A) and replaced with a No. 5 FiberWire (Figure 3B). This suture forms a locking cinch stitch in a luggage‐tag configuration. All sutures are shuttled through the tibial tunnel. Tensioning of the cinch stitch reduces the meniscal root onto its footprint, while the oblique ripstop stitch prevents tissue cut‐through during and after fixation (Figure 4). Fixation is completed on the tibial cortex using a cortical button secured with a Nice knot. A summary of the surgical steps and corresponding technical tips is provided in Table 1.

DISCUSSION

MMR tears require anatomical repair to restore hoop stress and protect against early cartilage degeneration. However, the compromised quality of the meniscal tissue at the root often poses a challenge, as it is commonly degenerated and susceptible to suture pull‐out. Traditional pull‐out techniques may be insufficient in these cases because of the unidirectional tensile forces acting along the suture axis.

The ripstop technique, adapted from rotator cuff repair, improves biomechanical stability by distributing tension over a wider footprint and preventing suture migration. 5 , 6 , 7 , 8 In our modified technique, the ripstop stitch is placed obliquely across the root to counteract the force vector exerted by the cinch stitch, which follows the tibial tunnel direction. This configuration provides a perpendicular counterforce, potentially reducing the risk of suture cut‐through.

This technique is relatively straightforward and can be integrated into existing arthroscopic procedures without the need for specialized instruments. This may be particularly beneficial for patients with poor‐quality meniscal tissue. However, further biomechanical validation and clinical studies are necessary to confirm long‐term efficacy. Pearls and Pitfalls

Previous studies have shown that meniscal extrusion and joint space narrowing progress rapidly after root tears. 1 , 9 Biomechanical experiments have indicated that the load distribution after root repair can approximate that of an intact meniscus when anatomical restoration is achieved. 4 , 10 Therefore, secure fixation and precise reduction are essential for optimizing outcomes.

In conclusion, the oblique ripstop technique for MMR repair provides an effective method for reinforcing the repair construct in cases of compromised meniscal tissues. Orienting the ripstop suture perpendicular to the cinch stitch enhances repair stability and reduces the risk of suture cut‐through, offering a practical solution for challenging clinical scenarios. Future research should compare this technique with other augmentation strategies such as suture tape reinforcement, biological scaffolds, or collagen implants. Prospective studies incorporating follow‐up imaging, including second‐look arthroscopy or magnetic resonance imaging, can also validate the healing and functional restoration.

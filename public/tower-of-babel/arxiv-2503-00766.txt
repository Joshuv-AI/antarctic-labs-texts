q q -deformation of random partitions, determinantal structure, and Riemann–Hilbert problem
Taro Kimura

Abstract
. We study q q -deformation of the probability measure on partitions, i.e., q q -deformed random partitions. We in particular consider the q q -Plancherel measure and show a determinantal formula for the correlation function using a q q -deformation of the discrete Bessel kernel. We also investigate Riemann–Hilbert problems associated with the corresponding orthogonal polynomials and obtain q q -Painlevé equations from the q q -difference Lax formalism.



1.
Introduction and summary

A partition of a non-negative integer is a ubiquitous object appearing in various combinatorial aspects of mathematics and physics.
Our main interest is a probability distribution of partitions, i.e., random partitions, and its large scale behavior.
The Plancherel measure is a primary example of the probability measure on the set of partitions through the identification of each partition as the irreducible representation of the symmetric group,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mu_{\text{P}}[\lambda]=\frac{(\dim\lambda)^{2}}{n!}\,,\qquad\lambda\vdash n\,,

where we denote by
\dim\lambda
the dimension of the irreducible representation of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
labeled by
\lambda
.
We are also interested in the Poissonized Plancherel measure, which is a one-parameter extension given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mu_{\text{PP}}[\lambda;\eta]=\mathrm{e}^{-\eta^{2}}\eta^{2|\lambda|}\left(\frac{\dim\lambda}{|\lambda|!}\right)^{2}=\mathrm{e}^{-\eta^{2}}\eta^{2|\lambda|}\prod_{(i,j)\in\lambda}\frac{1}{h(i,j)^{2}}\,,

where
h(i,j)
is the hook length associated with the position
(i,j)
.
In this case, there is no restriction on the size of partitions.
For such a probability measure, we are typically interested in the large scale behavior and its universality.
The result presented by Logan and Shepp
[
LS77
]
, and Vershik and Kerov
[
VK77
]
about the so-called limit shape of random partitions is the pioneering work in this direction.
Moreover, Baik, Deift, and Johansson showed in their seminal work
[
BDJ98
]
that the large scale behavior of random permutations is described by the Tracy–Widom distribution of Gaussian Unitary Ensemble (GUE)
[
TW92
]
, which establishes the universality of random partitions in the large scale limit.
Since then, there have been numerous works on stochastic and probabilistic aspects of random partitions in various contexts.

In this paper, we study
q
-deformation of the Poissonized Plancherel measure on the set of partitions
\mathsf{P}
, that we call the
q
-Plancherel measure.
Let
q,\xi\in[0,1)
.
We define two types of the
q
-Plancherel measure,

(1.3a)

Squared type:

\displaystyle\mu_{q\text{PP}_{S}}[\lambda;\xi,q]=M(\xi;q)^{-1}(\xi^{2}q)^{|\lambda|}q^{2b(\lambda)}\prod_{(i,j)\in\lambda}\frac{1}{(1-q^{h(i,j)})^{2}}\,,

(1.3b)

Mixed type:

\displaystyle\mu_{q\text{PP}_{M}}[\lambda;\xi,q]=\exp\left(\frac{\xi^{2}}{q^{\frac{1}{2}}-q^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\xi^{2|\lambda|}q^{b(\lambda)}\prod_{(i,j)\in\lambda}\frac{1}{h(i,j)(1-q^{h(i,j)})}\,,

for
\lambda\in\mathsf{P}
.
See §
2
for more details.
The squared type was introduced by Fulman to study increasing and decreasing subsequences in non-uniform random permutations
[
Ful01
]
, and the mixed type
was introduced by Kerov to study a
q
-deformation of the so-called hook walk
[
Ker93
]
.

We first point out that both measures are in fact specialization of the multi-variable probability measure on partitions, a.k.a., the Schur measure, introduced by Okounkov
[
Oko99
]
(Proposition
2.4
).
This identification allows us to show that, in contrast to other
q
-deformation of random partitions, e.g., Macdonald measure,
q
-Whittaker measure
[
BC11
]
, the
q
-Plancherel measure of both types are discrete determinantal point processes (Corollary
2.5
): All the correlation functions are given by a determinant of the correlation kernel.
In particular, for the squared type measure, the correlation kernel is given as follows.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
2.6
)
.

The correlation kernel of the squared type
q
-Plancherel measure is given by the
q
-Bessel kernel,

(1.4)

\displaystyle K_{q\text{B}}(r,s)=\xi\frac{J_{r+\frac{1}{2}}J_{s-\frac{1}{2}}-J_{r-\frac{1}{2}}J_{s+\frac{1}{2}}}{q^{\frac{1}{2}(r-s)}-q^{-\frac{1}{2}(r-s)}}\,,\qquad r,s\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}\,,

where
J_{n}=J^{(3)}_{n}(2\xi;q)
is the Hahn–Exton
q
-Bessel function.

This is a natural
q
-deformation of the well-known discrete Bessel kernel introduced by Borodin, Okounkov, and Olshanski
[
BOO99
]
and Johansson
[
Joh99
]
.
We verify that the
q
-Bessel kernel is reduced to the discrete Bessel kernel by putting
\xi=(1-q)\eta
and taking the limit
q\to 1
.
Similar to the discrete Bessel kernel, we can take the scaling limit of the
q
-Bessel kernel to obtain the universal kernels, i.e., the sine kernel and the Airy kernel.
We determine the scaling limit to realize these universal kernels.
From this analysis, we also obtain the limit shape for the
q
-Plancherel measure (Fig.
1
).

We then study the gap probability for the
q
-Plancherel measure.
Since the
q
-Plancherel measure is determinantal, the gap probability is given by a discrete version of the Fredholm determinant.
As shown by Borodin and Okounkov
[
BO99
]
, the discrete Fredholm determinant associated with the specific interval is presented by the Toeplitz determinant.
For the squared type measure, we have the following Toeplitz determinant formula.

Proposition 1.2

(Propositions
3.1
,
3.2
)
.

The gap probability with respect to the squared type
q
-Plancherel measure is given by the Toeplitz determinant,

(1.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{q\text{PP}_{S}}[\ell(\lambda)\leq N]=\frac{1}{M(\xi;q)}\det_{0\leq i,j\leq N-1}I_{-i+j}\,,\quad\mathbb{P}_{q\text{PP}_{S}}[\lambda_{1}\leq N]=\frac{1}{M(\xi;q)}\det_{0\leq i,j\leq N-1}\check{I}_{-i+j}\,,

where
I_{n}=I_{n}^{(1)}(2\xi q^{\frac{1}{2}};q)
and
\check{I}_{n}=q^{\frac{n^{2}}{2}}I_{n}^{(2)}(2\xi;q)
are the modified
q
-Bessel functions, and
M(\xi;q)
is the modified MacMahon function.

We remark that, for the
q
-Plancherel measure, the measure is not invariant under the transposition.
Hence, we have two different expressions for the gap probability with respect to
\lambda_{1}
and
\ell(\lambda)=\lambda_{1}^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{T}$}}
.

In order to derive the Toeplitz determinant formula, we use the unitary matrix integral and the orthogonal polynomials.
The inner product for the orthogonal polynomials appearing in this context is defined on the integral on the unit circle, which shows an analogous structure to the
q
-orthogonal polynomials as we will see later (see, e.g.,
[
Ism05
,
KLS10
]
).
We study the Riemann–Hilbert problem associated with these orthogonal polynomials and also the corresponding linear
q
-difference system based on the Lax formalism:
For a matrix-valued function
\Psi
constructed from the orthogonal polynomials, we have

(1.6)

\displaystyle\Psi_{n+1}(z)=U_{n}(z)\Psi_{n}(z)\,,\qquad\Psi_{n}(qz)=T_{n}(z)\Psi_{n}(z)\,.

We call
(U,T)
the Lax pair associated with the linear
q
-difference system.
Since we have
\Psi_{n+1}(qz)=U_{n}(qz)T_{n}(z)\Psi_{n}(z)=T_{n+1}(z)U_{n}(z)\Psi_{n}(z)
, and
\Psi
is invertible on the complex plane except for a certain set, we have the so-called compatibility condition,

(1.7)

\displaystyle U_{n}(qz)T_{n}(z)=T_{n+1}(z)U_{n}(z)\,,

from which we deduce the following.

Theorem 1.3

(Theorems
4.7
,
4.11
)
.

For the monic orthogonal polynomials associated with the
q
-Plancherel measure,
\{\pi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
and
\{\check{\pi}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
, set
\mathsf{x}_{n}=\xi^{\frac{1}{2}}q^{\frac{n}{2}}\pi_{n}(0)
and
\mathsf{y}_{n}=(-\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}q^{-\frac{n}{2}}\check{\pi}_{n}(0)
.
The following recurrence relations hold,

(1.8a)

\displaystyle\left(\mathsf{x}_{n}\mathsf{x}_{n+1}-1\right)\left(\mathsf{x}_{n-1}\mathsf{x}_{n}-1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(\mathsf{x}_{n}^{2}-\xi)(\mathsf{x}_{n}^{2}-\xi^{-1})}{(1-\xi^{-1}q^{-n}\mathsf{x}_{n}^{2})}\,,

(1.8b)

\displaystyle\left(\mathsf{y}_{n}\mathsf{y}_{n+1}-1\right)\left(\mathsf{y}_{n-1}\mathsf{y}_{n}-1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(\mathsf{y}_{n}^{2}+\xi)(\mathsf{y}_{n}^{2}+\xi^{-1})}{(1+\xi^{-1}q^{-n}\mathsf{y}_{n}^{2})}\,.

These equations are special cases of the
q
-Painlevé V equation (
q
-P
{}_{\text{V}}
)
[
RGH91
]
(see also
[
GR04b
]
).
We have a shifted Toeplitz determinant formula for
\mathsf{x}_{n}
and
\mathsf{y}_{n}
,

(1.9)

\displaystyle\mathsf{x}_{n}=(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})^{n}\xi^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{Z_{n}^{(1)}}{Z_{n}^{(0)}}\,,\qquad\mathsf{y}_{n}=(-q^{-\frac{1}{2}})^{n}(-\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\check{Z}_{n}^{(1)}}{\check{Z}_{n}^{(0)}}\,,

where

(1.10)

\displaystyle Z_{n}^{(k)}=\det_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}I_{-i+j-k}\,,\qquad\check{Z}_{n}^{(k)}=\det_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}\check{I}_{-i+j-k}\,.

We remark that a similar determinant solution using the
q
-Bessel function to the
q
-Painlevé III equation (
q
-P
{}_{\text{III}}
) was obtained in
[
KOS94
]
.
They considered a Casorati type determinant with respect to the
q
-shift of the parameter
\xi
instead of the index
n
as we used.

We verify that the
q
-Painlevé equation above is reduced to the discrete Painlevé II equation (d-P
{}_{\text{II}}
) in the limit
q\to 1
under the parametrization
\xi=(1-q)\eta
as before.
This behavior is consistent with the well-known result for the relation between the ordinary Plancherel measure to the discrete Painlevé equation by Baik
[
Bai01
]
, Borodin
[
Bor01
]
, and Adler and van Moerbeke
[
AvM02
]
.

Notations

We denote by
\mathsf{P}
a set of partitions,
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{\ell}>0)
, where
\ell
is the length of
\lambda
denoted by
\ell(\lambda)
.
We denote by
\mathsf{P}_{N}
the set of partitions whose length is less than
N
,
\mathsf{P}_{N}=\{\lambda\in\mathsf{P}\mid\ell(\lambda)\leq N\}
, i.e.,
\mathsf{P}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathsf{P}_{N}
.
We denote the transposition of
\lambda
by
\lambda^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{T}$}}
.
Then, we have
\ell(\lambda)=\lambda_{1}^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{T}$}}
.
The size of a partition
\lambda
is denoted by
|\lambda|=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}\lambda_{i}
, i.e.,
\lambda\vdash n\iff|\lambda|=n
.
We remark that
\mathsf{P}_{N}\neq\{\lambda\in\mathsf{P}\mid|\lambda|=N\}
.
For a partition
\lambda
, we define

(1.11)

\displaystyle b(\lambda)=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}(i-1)\lambda_{i}\,.

For
(i,j)\in\lambda
, we define a look length,

(1.12)

\displaystyle h(i,j)=\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{T}$}}-i-j+1\,.

2.
q
-Plancherel measure

The Plancherel measure is a probability measure on the set of irreducible unitary representations of a locally compact group
G
.
For the symmetric group
G=\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, irreducible representations are parametrized by partitions
\lambda\vdash n
, which allows us to interpret the corresponding Plancherel measure as a probability measure on the set of partitions,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mu_{\text{P}}[\lambda]=\frac{(\dim\lambda)^{2}}{n!}\,,\qquad\lambda\vdash n\,,

where we denote by
\dim\lambda
the dimension of the irreducible representation characterized by
\lambda
, which agrees with the number of standard Young tableaux of shape
\lambda
.
There is a combinatorial formula to compute the dimension, which is called the hook length formula,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\dim\lambda}{n!}=\prod_{(i,j)\in\lambda}\frac{1}{h(i,j)}\,.

Then, the normalization condition of the Plancherel measure is ensured by a combinatorial identity obtained via the Robinson–Schensted correspondence between permutations and pairs of standard Young tableaux associated with the same shape,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}(\dim\lambda)^{2}=n!\,.

The Poissonized Plancherel measure is a one-parameter deformation of the Plancherel measure obtained via Poissonization,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mu_{\text{PP}}[\lambda]=\mu_{\text{PP}}[\lambda;\eta]=\mathrm{e}^{-\eta^{2}}\eta^{2|\lambda|}\left(\frac{\dim\lambda}{|\lambda|!}\right)^{2}\,,\qquad\lambda\in\mathsf{P}\,.

In this case, there is no restriction on the size of partitions, and hence it is a probability measure on the whole set of partitions
\mathsf{P}
.

Let us introduce two types of
q
-deformation of the (Poissonized-)Plancherel measure.
Set
q,\xi\in[0,1)
in the following.

q,\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
under the condition
|q|,|\xi|<1
.

Squared type

The first
q
-deformation is given as follows,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mu_{q\text{PP}_{S}}[\lambda]=\mu_{q\text{PP}_{S}}[\lambda;\xi,q]=M(\xi;q)^{-1}(\xi^{2}q)^{|\lambda|}q^{2b(\lambda)}\prod_{(i,j)\in\lambda}\frac{1}{(1-q^{h(i,j)})^{2}}\,,

where
M(z;q)
is the modified MacMahon function (
A.1
).
We call this the squared type
q
-Plancherel measure.
This measure was introduced by Fulman to study increasing and decreasing subsequences in non-uniform random permutations
[
Ful01
]
.
This measure has been also studied in the context of supersymmetric gauge theory and topological string theory
[
Nek02
,
NO03
,
ORV03
]
.
In fact, the modified MacMahon function has a two-fold interpretation as the partition function of five-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=1
supersymmetric
\mathrm{U}(1)
gauge theory on
\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
and the topological string partition function of the toric Calabi–Yau 3-fold,
X=\operatorname{Tot}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\mathcal{O}(0)\oplus\mathcal{O}(-2))
.

Mixed type

The second
q
-deformation is given as follows,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mu_{q\text{PP}_{M}}[\lambda]=\mu_{q\text{PP}_{M}}[\lambda;\xi,q]=\exp\left(\frac{\xi^{2}}{q^{\frac{1}{2}}-q^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\xi^{2|\lambda|}q^{b(\lambda)}\prod_{(i,j)\in\lambda}\frac{1}{h(i,j)(1-q^{h(i,j)})}\,.

We call this the mixed type
q
-Plancherel measure.
This measure was introduced by Kerov to study a
q
-deformation of random partitions
[
Ker93
]
.
In fact, this
q
-deformed measure has a natural interpretation from the point of view of the Iwahori–Hecke algebra, which is a
q
-deformation of the symmetric group
[
Str07
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

We have

(2.7)

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 1}\mu_{q\text{PP}_{S}}[\lambda;(1-q)\eta;q]=\lim_{q\to 1}\mu_{q\text{PP}_{M}}[\lambda;(1-q)^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta;q]=\mu_{\text{PP}}[\lambda;\eta]\,.

2.1.
Reduction from Schur measure

The Schur measure is a multi-variable probability measure on partitions introduced by Okounkov
[
Oko99
]
.
We first summarize the Schur measure and show its relation to the
q
-Plancherel measure.

Schur function

Let
s_{\lambda}(X)
be a Schur function of (infinitely many) variables
X=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)
associated with a partition
\lambda
.
We also use the notation,

(2.8)

\displaystyle s_{\lambda}(\{t_{n}\})=s_{\lambda}(X)\quad\text{where}\quad t_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\geq 1}x_{i}^{n}\,.

The Cauchy identities for the Schur functions read

(2.9a)

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\mathsf{P}}s_{\lambda}(X)s_{\lambda}(Y)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i,j\geq 1}(1-x_{i}y_{j})^{-1}=\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}\tr X^{n}\tr Y^{n}\right)\,,

(2.9b)

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\mathsf{P}}s_{\lambda^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{T}$}}}(X)s_{\lambda}(Y)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i,j\geq 1}(1+x_{i}y_{j})=\exp\left(-\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n}\tr X^{n}\tr Y^{n}\right)\,,

where we write
\tr X^{n}=\sum_{i\geq 1}x_{i}^{n}
and
\tr Y^{n}=\sum_{i\geq 1}y_{i}^{n}
.

Set
\mathbb{T}_{N}=\left\{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})\in(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{N}\mid|z_{i}|=1,i=1,\ldots,N\right\}
.
The Schur function of
N
-variable (Schur polynomial) has the following orthonormal property,

(2.10)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N!}\oint_{\mathbb{T}_{N}}s_{\lambda}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})\overline{s_{\lambda^{\prime}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}|z_{i}-z_{j}|^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\differential{z}_{i}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z_{i}}=\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\,.

We remark that
s_{\lambda}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})=0
if
\ell(\lambda)>N
.

Schur measure

Let
x_{i},y_{i}\in[0,1)
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
The Schur measure on the set of partitions is defined using the Schur function,

(2.11)

\displaystyle\mu_{\text{S}}[\lambda]=\mu_{\text{S}}[\lambda;X,Y]=\frac{1}{Z(X,Y)}s_{\lambda}(X)s_{\lambda}(Y)\,,

where the normalization factor is given by

(2.12)

\displaystyle Z(X,Y)=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathsf{P}}s_{\lambda}(X)s_{\lambda}(Y)=\prod_{i,j\geq 1}(1-x_{i}y_{j})^{-1}\,.

The parameters
(x_{i},y_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
can be in general complex: We need a sufficient condition
X=\overline{Y}
so that
\mu_{\text{S}}[\lambda]\geq 0
for any
\lambda
.
We also use the notation,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\mu_{\text{S}}[\lambda;t,\tilde{t}]=\mu_{\text{S}}[\lambda;X,Y]\qquad\text{where}\qquad t_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\geq 1}x_{i}^{n}\,,\quad\tilde{t}_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\geq 1}y_{i}^{n}\,.

Correlation function

Let us consider the correlation function for the Schur measure.
For a partition
\lambda\in\mathsf{P}
, we define the boson-fermion map,

(2.14)

\displaystyle\Xi(\lambda)=\left\{\lambda_{i}-i+\frac{1}{2}\right\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}=:\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}\,.

For a finite subset
\mathsf{Z}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
, we define the correlation function,

(2.15)

\displaystyle\rho(\mathsf{Z})=\mathbb{P}_{\text{S}}[\mathsf{Z}\subset\Xi(\lambda)]\,,

where we denote the probability with respect to the Schur measure by
\mathbb{P}_{\text{S}}[\cdot]
.
When
|\mathsf{Z}|=k
, it is called the
k
-point correlation function.

Theorem 2.2

(Okounkov
[
Oko99
]
)
.

For
z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
, the
k
-point correlation function of the Schur measure is given by a determinant of the form,

(2.16)

\displaystyle\rho(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})=\det_{1\leq i,j\leq k}K(z_{i},z_{j})\,.

The correlation kernel is given by

(2.17)

\displaystyle K(r,s)=\frac{1}{(2\pi\mathrm{i})^{2}}\oint_{|z|>|w|}\frac{\mathbb{J}(z;t,\tilde{t})\mathbb{J}(w;\tilde{t},t)}{z-w}\frac{\differential{z}\differential{w}}{z^{r+\frac{1}{2}}w^{-s+\frac{1}{2}}}\,,

where the integration contour counterclockwise encircles the origin keeping the relation
|z|>|w|
with the auxiliary function,

(2.18)

\displaystyle\mathbb{J}(z;t,\tilde{t})=\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}(t_{n}z^{n}-\tilde{t}_{n}z^{-n})\right)\,.

Therefore, the Schur measure is a discrete determinantal point process.

Proposition 2.3
.

The correlation kernel of the Schur measure has the series expansion,

(2.19)

\displaystyle K(r,s)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}J_{r+k}\tilde{J}_{s+k}\,,

with

(2.20)

\displaystyle J_{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\oint\mathbb{J}(z;t,\tilde{t})\frac{\differential{z}}{z^{n+1}}\,,\qquad\tilde{J}_{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\oint\mathbb{J}(z;\tilde{t},t)\frac{\differential{z}}{z^{n+1}}\,,

where the integration contour counterclockwise encircles the origin.

The following specialization is technically important in the rest of the paper.

Proposition 2.4
.

The
q
-Plancherel measures of both types are obtained from the Schur measure under the specialization,

(2.21a)

\displaystyle\text{Squared type}:\quad

\displaystyle t_{n}=\tilde{t}_{n}=-\frac{\xi^{n}}{n(q^{\frac{n}{2}}-q^{-\frac{n}{2}})}\,,

(2.21b)

\displaystyle\text{Mixed type}:\quad

\displaystyle t_{n}=-\frac{\xi^{n}}{n(q^{\frac{n}{2}}-q^{-\frac{n}{2}})}\,,\quad\tilde{t}_{n}=\xi q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\delta_{n,1}\,.

Proof.

It directly follows from the following identities of the Schur function (e.g.,
[
Mac97
, §I.3]
),

(2.22a)

\displaystyle s_{\lambda}\left(\left\{t_{n}=-\frac{\xi^{n}}{n(q^{\frac{n}{2}}-q^{-\frac{n}{2}})}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=(\xi q^{\frac{1}{2}})^{|\lambda|}q^{b(\lambda)}\prod_{(i,j)\in\lambda}\frac{1}{1-q^{h(i,j)}}\,,

(2.22b)

\displaystyle s_{\lambda}(\{t_{n}=\xi\delta_{n,1}\})

\displaystyle=\xi^{|\lambda|}\prod_{(i,j)\in\lambda}\frac{1}{h(i,j)}\,.

∎

Corollary 2.5
.

The
q
-Plancherel measures are discrete determinantal point processes.

2.2.
q
-Bessel kernel

For the squared type measure, we have the following Christoffel–Darboux type formula for the correlation kernel.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
.
The correlation function of the squared type
q
-Plancherel measure is given by

(2.23)

\displaystyle\rho(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})=\det_{1\leq i,j\leq k}K_{q\text{B}}(z_{i},z_{j})\,,

with the correlation kernel,

(2.24)

\displaystyle K_{q\text{B}}(r,s)=\xi\frac{J_{r+\frac{1}{2}}J_{s-\frac{1}{2}}-J_{r-\frac{1}{2}}J_{s+\frac{1}{2}}}{q^{\frac{1}{2}(r-s)}-q^{-\frac{1}{2}(r-s)}}\,,

where
J_{n}=J^{(3)}_{n}(2\xi;q)
is the Hahn–Exton
q
-Bessel function.
We call this the
q
-Bessel kernel.

Proof.

Under the specialization (
2.21a
), the auxiliary function
\mathbb{J}(z;t,\bar{t})
defined in (
2.18
) is identified with the generating function of the Hahn–Exton
q
-Bessel function
J_{n}=J^{(3)}_{n}(2\xi;q)
(see, e.g.,
[
GR04a
]
),

(2.25)

\displaystyle\mathbb{J}\left(z;t,\tilde{t}\right)=\mathbb{J}\left(z;\tilde{t},t\right)^{-1}=\exp\left(-\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\xi^{n}(z^{n}-z^{-n})}{n(q^{\frac{n}{2}}-q^{-\frac{n}{2}})}\right)=\frac{(\xi q^{\frac{1}{2}}z^{-1};q)_{\infty}}{(\xi q^{\frac{1}{2}}z;q)_{\infty}}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(q^{\frac{1}{2}}z)^{n}J_{n}\,.

Hence, by Proposition
2.3
, the correlation kernel is given by

(2.26)

\displaystyle K(r,s)=q^{\frac{1}{2}(r+s)}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{\prime}}q^{k}J_{r+k}J_{s+k}\,,\qquad r,s\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}\,,

which implies

(2.27)

\displaystyle(q^{r}-q^{s})K(r,s)

\displaystyle=q^{\frac{1}{2}(r+s)}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{\prime}}\left(q^{r+k}J_{r+k}J_{s+k}-q^{s+k}J_{r+k}J_{s+k}\right)\,.

Using the recurrence relation of the Hahn–Exton
q
-Bessel function (
B.8b
),

(2.28)

\displaystyle J_{n+1}=\left(\frac{1-q^{n}}{\xi}+\xi\right)J_{n}-J_{n-1}\,,

we have

\displaystyle(q^{r}-q^{s})K(r,s)

\displaystyle=-\xi q^{\frac{1}{2}(r+s)}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{\prime}}\left(J_{r+k+1}J_{s+k}+J_{r+k-1}J_{s+k}-J_{r+k}J_{s+k+1}-J_{r+k}J_{s+k-1}\right)

(2.29)

\displaystyle=\xi q^{\frac{1}{2}(r+s)}\left(J_{r+\frac{1}{2}}J_{s-\frac{1}{2}}-J_{r-\frac{1}{2}}J_{s+\frac{1}{2}}\right)\,.

from which we obtain the correlation kernel.
∎

q\to 1
limit

Set
\xi=(1-q)\eta
.
In the limit
q\to 1
, the Hahn–Exton
q
-Bessel function is reduced to the ordinary Bessel function,
\lim_{q\to 1}J_{n}^{(3)}((1-q)\eta;q)=J_{n}(\eta)
, which directly implies the reduction of the
q
-Bessel kernel to the discrete Bessel kernel in this limit,

(2.30)

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 1}K_{q\text{B}}(r,s)\Big|_{\xi=(1-q)\eta}=K_{\text{dB}}(r,s)=\eta\frac{J_{r-\frac{1}{2}}(2\eta)J_{s+\frac{1}{2}}(2\eta)-J_{r+\frac{1}{2}}(2\eta)J_{s-\frac{1}{2}}(2\eta)}{r-s}\,.

2.3.
Scaling limit

As in the case of the discrete Bessel kernel, we consider the scaling limit of the
q
-Bessel kernel, which gives rise to the universal correlation kernels.

Bulk scaling limit

We first consider the bulk scaling limit, where the kernel is asymptotic to the sine kernel.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
a=-2\log(1+\xi)
,
b=-2\log(1-\xi)
, and
x\in[a,b]
.
The
q
-Bessel kernel is asymptotic to the sine kernel in the following limit (the bulk scaling limit),

(2.31)

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 1}K_{q\text{B}}\left(-\frac{x}{\log q}+u,-\frac{x}{\log q}+v\right)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\sin\pi\varrho(u-v)}{\pi(u-v)}&(u\neq v)\\
\varrho&(u=v)\end{cases}

where
\varrho=\varrho(x)\in[0,1]
is the bulk one-point function,

(2.32)

\displaystyle\varrho(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\arccos\frac{1}{2}\left(\xi+\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-x}}{\xi}\right)\,,\quad x\in[a,b]\,.

We may apply the same approach as, e.g.,
[
Oko02
]
, to prove this statement.
We here provide a sketch of the proof:
Set
q=\mathrm{e}^{-\epsilon}
with
\epsilon>0
.
From the contour integral formula of the correlation kernel (
2.17
), we have

(2.33)

\displaystyle K_{q\text{B}}\left(r,s\right)=\frac{1}{(2\pi\mathrm{i})^{2}}\oint_{|z|>|w|}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{S(z,r)-S(w,s)}}{z-w}\frac{\differential{z}\differential{w}}{z^{\frac{1}{2}}w^{\frac{1}{2}}}\,,

where we define the action function,

(2.34)

\displaystyle S(z,r)=-\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\xi^{n}(z^{n}-z^{-n})}{n(q^{\frac{n}{2}}-q^{-\frac{n}{2}})}-r\log z=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\xi^{n}(z^{n}-z^{-n})}{n^{2}}-r\log z+O(\epsilon)\,.

Therefore, we may apply the method of steepest descent in the limit
\epsilon\to 0
(
q\to 1
).
Parametrizing
z=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta}
, we have

(2.35)

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}S(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta},r)=2\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\xi^{n}\sin n\theta}{n(q^{\frac{n}{2}}-q^{-\frac{n}{2}})}+r\theta=-\frac{2}{\epsilon}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\xi^{n}\sin n\theta}{n^{2}}+r\theta+O(\epsilon)\,.

We define

(2.36)

\displaystyle\alpha=-2\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\xi^{n}}{q^{\frac{n}{2}}-q^{-\frac{n}{2}}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}\epsilon^{k-1}\,,\qquad\beta=-\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{n^{2}\xi^{n}}{q^{\frac{n}{2}}-q^{-\frac{n}{2}}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\beta_{k}\epsilon^{k-1}\,.

We remark that
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
.
In particular, the leading contributions are given by

(2.37)

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}=2\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\xi^{n}}{n}=-2\log(1 - \xi)\geq 0\,,\qquad\beta_{0}=\sum_{n\geq 1}n\xi^{n}=\frac{\xi}{(1-\xi)^{2}}\geq 0\,.

We compute

(2.38)

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\partialderivative{\theta}S(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta},r)=2\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\xi^{n}\cos n\theta}{q^{\frac{n}{2}}-q^{-\frac{n}{2}}}+r=-\frac{2}{\epsilon}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\xi^{n}}{n}\cos n\theta+r+O(\epsilon)\,,

and hence the stationary equation in the limit
\epsilon\to 0
(
q\to 1
) is given by

\displaystyle\epsilon r

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle O(\epsilon^{2})}}{{=}}2\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\xi^{n}}{n}\cos n\theta=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\xi^{n}}{n}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}n\theta}+\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}n\theta})

(2.39)

\displaystyle=-\log(1 - \xi\ee^{\ii\theta})-\log(1 - \xi\ee^{- \ii\theta})=-\log(1 + \xi^2 - 2 \xi\cos\theta)\,.

Therefore,
\varrho
solves the following equation,

(2.40)

\displaystyle x=2\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\xi^{n}}{n}\cos n\pi\varrho=-\log(1 + \xi^2 - 2 \xi\cos\pi\varrho)\,,

from which we obtain

(2.41)

\displaystyle\cos\pi\varrho=\frac{1}{2}\left(\xi+\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-x}}{\xi}\right)\,.

We remark that such a solution exists only when
\epsilon r\in[a,b]
.
Under this condition, we may apply the method of steepest descent to obtain the sine kernel.
See, e.g.,
[
Oko02
]
, for details.

Edge scaling limit

The Airy kernel is the universal correlation kernel appearing in the so-called edge scaling limit of GUE,

(2.44)

\displaystyle K_{\text{Ai}}(x,y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{Ai}(x+z)\operatorname{Ai}(y+z)\differential{z}=\frac{\operatorname{Ai}(x)\operatorname{Ai}^{\prime}(y)-\operatorname{Ai}^{\prime}(x)\operatorname{Ai}(y)}{x-y}\,,

Proposition 2.8
.

The
q
-Bessel kernel is asymptotic to the Airy kernel in the following limit (the edge scaling limit),

(2.46)

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 1}\left(-\frac{\beta_{0}}{\log q}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}K_{q\text{B}}\left(-\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\log q}+\left(-\frac{\beta_{0}}{\log q}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}x,-\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\log q}+\left(-\frac{\beta_{0}}{\log q}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}y\right)=K_{\text{Ai}}(x,y)\,,

with
\alpha_{0}=-2\log(1 - \xi)

.

\theta=0

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}S\left(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta},\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\epsilon}+\left(\frac{\beta_{0}}{\epsilon}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}x\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle O(\epsilon)}}{{=}}\frac{\beta_{0}}{\epsilon}\frac{\theta^{3}}{3}+\left(\frac{\beta_{0}}{\epsilon}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}x\theta+O(\theta^{5})\,.

Applying the change of variable,

(

3.
Gap probability and orthogonal polynomials

3.1.
Toeplitz determinant formula

Let
I\subset\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
.
The probability to find no element in
I
is called the gap probability, which is one of the most important quantities associated with the measure on partitions.
In particular, for the determinantal case, the gap probability for the interval
I=[N+\frac{1}{2},\infty)
is given by the following Fredholm determinant of the correlation kernel,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\lambda_{1}\leq N]=\det(1 - K)_{\ell^{2}([N+\frac{1}{2},\infty))}\,.

For the Schur measure, Borodin and Okounkov
[
BO99
]
obtained the Toeplitz determinant formula for the gap probabilities.
In this Section, we derive such a Toeplitz determinant formula for the
q
-Plancherel measure.
For the moment, we only focus on the squared type measure.

Proposition 3.1
.

We have the following unitary matrix integral formula for the gap probabilities for the squared type
q
-Plancherel measure,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{q\text{PP}}[\ell(\lambda)\leq N]=\frac{Z_{N}}{Z_{\infty}}\,,\qquad\mathbb{P}_{q\text{PP}}[\lambda_{1}\leq N]=\frac{\check{Z}_{N}}{\check{Z}_{\infty}}\,,

where we define the integral over the unitary group,

(3.3)

\displaystyle Z_{N}=\int_{\mathrm{U}(N)}\det\mathbb{I}(U;\xi)\differential{U}\,,\qquad\check{Z}_{N}=\int_{\mathrm{U}(N)}\det\check{\mathbb{I}}(U;\xi)\differential{U}\,,

with

(3.4a)

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}(z;\xi)

\displaystyle=(\xi q^{\frac{1}{2}}z,\xi q^{\frac{1}{2}}z^{-1};q)_{\infty}^{-1}=\exp\left(-\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\xi^{n}(z^{n}+z^{-n})}{n(q^{\frac{n}{2}}-q^{-\frac{n}{2}})}\right)\,,

(3.4b)

\displaystyle\check{\mathbb{I}}(z;\xi)

\displaystyle=(-\xi q^{\frac{1}{2}}z,-\xi q^{\frac{1}{2}}z^{-1};q)_{\infty}=\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-\xi)^{n}(z^{n}+z^{-n})}{n(q^{\frac{n}{2}}-q^{-\frac{n}{2}})}\right)\,,

and
Z_{\infty}=\check{Z}_{\infty}=M(\xi,q)
.

Proof.

Diagonalizing the unitary matrix, we have

\displaystyle Z_{N}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!}\oint_{\mathbb{T}_{N}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}|z_{i}-z_{j}|^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\xi q^{\frac{1}{2}}z_{i},\xi q^{\frac{1}{2}}z_{i}^{-1};q)_{\infty}^{-1}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\differential{z}_{i}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z_{i}}

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!}\oint_{\mathbb{T}_{N}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}|z_{i}-z_{j}|^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\differential{\mu}(z_{i})

where we denote by
\mathbb{T}_{N}
the
N
-dimensional torus, and we define the measure on the unit circle,
\differential{\mu}(z)=\mathbb{I}(z;\xi)\frac{\differential{z}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z}
.
Similarly, defining
\mathrm{d}\check{\mu}(z)=\check{\mathbb{I}}(z;\xi)\frac{\differential{z}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z}
, we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle\check{Z}_{N}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!}\oint_{\mathbb{T}_{N}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}|z_{i}-z_{j}|^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\differential{\check{\mu}}(z_{i})\,.

Applying the Cauchy identities (
2.9
) and the orthonormal condition of the Schur polynomials (
2.10
), we obtain

(3.7a)

\displaystyle Z_{N}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathsf{P}_{N}}s_{\lambda}\left(\left\{t_{n}=-\frac{\xi^{n}}{n(q^{\frac{n}{2}}-q^{-\frac{n}{2}})}\right\}\right)s_{\lambda}\left(\left\{t_{n}=-\frac{\xi^{n}}{n(q^{\frac{n}{2}}-q^{-\frac{n}{2}})}\right\}\right)=Z_{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{q\text{PP}}[\ell(\lambda)\leq N]\,,

(3.7b)

\displaystyle\check{Z}_{N}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{T}$}}\in\mathsf{P}_{N}}s_{\lambda}\left(\left\{t_{n}=-\frac{\xi^{n}}{n(q^{\frac{n}{2}}-q^{-\frac{n}{2}})}\right\}\right)s_{\lambda}\left(\left\{t_{n}=-\frac{\xi^{n}}{n(q^{\frac{n}{2}}-q^{-\frac{n}{2}})}\right\}\right)=\check{Z}_{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{q\text{PP}}[\ell(\lambda^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{T}$}})\leq N]\,.

Recalling
\ell(\lambda^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{T}$}})=\lambda_{1}
for the latter case, we conclude the formula (
3.2
).
∎

The weight functions
\mathbb{I}(z;\xi)
and
\check{\mathbb{I}}(z;\xi)
take positive real values on the unit circle
z\in\mathbb{T}
for
q,\xi\in[0,1)
.
Parametrizing
z=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta}
, we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta};\xi)=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}(1+\xi^{2}q^{2n+1}-2\xi q^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\cos\theta)^{-1}\,,\quad\check{\mathbb{I}}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta};\xi)=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}(1+\xi^{2}q^{2n+1}+2\xi q^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\cos\theta)\,.

Under this parametrization, we have an alternative expression of the unitary matrix integrals,

(3.9a)

\displaystyle Z_{N}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\left(2\sin\left(\frac{\theta_{i}-\theta_{j}}{2}\right)\right)^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{I}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{i}};\xi)\frac{\differential{\theta}_{i}}{2\pi}\,,

(3.9b)

\displaystyle\check{Z}_{N}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\left(2\sin\left(\frac{\theta_{i}-\theta_{j}}{2}\right)\right)^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\check{\mathbb{I}}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{i}};\xi)\frac{\differential{\theta}_{i}}{2\pi}\,,

Proposition 3.2
.

The unitary matrix integrals are given by the Toeplitz determinants,

(3.10)

\displaystyle Z_{N}=\det_{0\leq i,j\leq N-1}I_{-i+j}\,,\qquad\check{Z}_{N}=\det_{0\leq i,j\leq N-1}\check{I}_{-i+j}\,.

where
I_{n}=I^{(1)}_{n}(2\xi q^{\frac{1}{2}};q)
and
\check{I}_{n}=q^{\frac{n^{2}}{2}}I^{(2)}_{n}(2\xi;q)
.

Proof.

From the generating functions of the modified
q
-Bessel function (
B.7
), we have

(3.11)

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}(z;\xi)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}z^{n}I_{n}\,,\qquad\check{\mathbb{I}}(z;\xi)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}z^{n}\check{I}_{n}\,.

Recalling
\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}|z_{i}-z_{j}|^{2}=\det_{1\leq i,j\leq N}(z_{i}^{j-1})\det_{1\leq i,j\leq N}(z_{i}^{-j+1})
for
z_{1},\ldots,z_{N}\in\mathbb{T}
, we may use Andréief’s formula to evaluate the integral as follows,

(3.12a)

\displaystyle Z_{N}

\displaystyle=\det_{0\leq i,j\leq N-1}\left(\oint_{\mathbb{T}}z^{i-j}\differential{\mu}(z)\right)=\det_{0\leq i,j\leq N-1}I_{-i+j}\,,

(3.12b)

\displaystyle\check{Z}_{N}

\displaystyle=\det_{0\leq i,j\leq N-1}\left(\oint_{\mathbb{T}}z^{i-j}\differential{\check{\mu}}(z)\right)=\det_{0\leq i,j\leq N-1}\check{I}_{-i+j}\,.

∎

Remark 3.3
.

Since
\mathbb{I}(z;\xi)=\mathbb{I}(z^{-1};\xi)
and
\check{\mathbb{I}}(z;\xi)=\check{\mathbb{I}}(z^{-1};\xi)
, we have the symmetry,
I_{n}=I_{-n}
and
\check{I}_{n}=\check{I}_{-n}
.

In the
q
-deformed setup, we have two different Toeplitz determinant formulas for the gap probability for
\lambda_{1}<N
and
\lambda_{1}^{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{T}$}}<N
(see Fig.
1
).
In the case of the ordinary Poissonized Plancherel measure, these two situations are equivalent due to the symmetry of the measure under
\eta\leftrightarrow-\eta
in (
2.4
).
In fact, both
I_{n}=I_{n}^{(1)}(2\xi q^{\frac{1}{2}};q)
and
\check{I}_{n}=q^{\frac{n^{2}}{2}}I_{n}^{(2)}(2\xi;q)
are reduced to the ordinary modified Bessel function
I_{n}(2\eta)
in the limit
q\to 1
under the parametrization
\xi=(1-q)\eta
.

Hypergeometric formula

The hypergeometric form of the modified
q
-Bessel function can be obtained using the residue formula.
Recall that the weight function
\mathbb{I}
has poles at
\xi q^{\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}_{>0}}
in the domain
|z|<1
and
\xi^{-1}q^{\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}_{<0}}
in the domain
|z|>1
.
Hence, the integral on the unit circle is given by the residues of the former poles,

\displaystyle I_{n}=I_{-n}=\oint_{\mathbb{T}}z^{n}d\mu(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\xi^{n}q^{\frac{n}{2}}}{(q,\xi^{2}q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\xi^{2}q;q)_{m}}{(q;q)_{m}}(-1)^{m}q^{m\choose 2}q^{(n+1)m}

(3.13)

\displaystyle=\frac{\xi^{n}q^{\frac{n}{2}}}{(q,\xi^{2}q;q)_{\infty}}{}_{1}\phi_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}\xi^{2}q\\
0\end{matrix};q;q^{n+1}\right)\,,

which agrees with the hypergeometric formula of
I_{n}^{(1)}(2\xi q^{\frac{1}{2}};q)
(
B.5
).

3.2.
Orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle

We define inner products on the unit circle,

(3.14)

\displaystyle\left<f,g\right>_{\mathbb{I}}=\oint_{\mathbb{T}}f(z)\overline{g(z)}\differential{\mu}(z)\,,\qquad\left<f,g\right>_{\check{\mathbb{I}}}=\oint_{\mathbb{T}}f(z)\overline{g(z)}\differential{\check{\mu}}(z)\,.

Under these inner products, we define orthonormal polynomials,

(3.15)

\displaystyle\left<p_{n},p_{m}\right>_{\mathbb{I}}=\delta_{n,m}\,,\qquad\left<\check{p}_{n},\check{p}_{m}\right>_{\mathbb{I}^{\vee}}=\delta_{n,m}\,,

where

(3.16)

\displaystyle p_{n}(z)=\kappa_{n}z^{n}+\cdots\,,\quad\check{p}_{n}(z)=\check{\kappa}_{n}z^{n}+\cdots\,,\quad\kappa_{n},\,\check{\kappa}_{n}>0\,.

We denote the corresponding monic polynomials by

(3.17)

\displaystyle\pi_{n}(z)=\frac{1}{\kappa_{n}}p_{n}(z)\,,\qquad\check{\pi}_{n}(z)=\frac{1}{\check{\kappa}_{n}}\check{p}_{n}(z)\,.

Since the weight functions are symmetric,
\mathbb{I}(z;\xi)=\mathbb{I}(z^{-1};\xi)
and
\check{\mathbb{I}}(z;\xi)=\check{\mathbb{I}}(z^{-1};\xi)
, all the coefficients of these orthogonal polynomials are real, i.e.,
\overline{p_{n}(z)}=p_{n}(z^{-1})
and
\overline{\check{p}_{n}(z)}=\check{p}_{n}(z^{-1})
.

Lemma 3.4
.

The unitary matrix integrals are given by the coefficients of the orthogonal polynomials,

(3.18)

\displaystyle Z_{N}=\prod_{n=0}^{N-1}\kappa_{n}^{-2}\,,\qquad\check{Z}_{N}=\prod_{n=0}^{N-1}\check{\kappa}_{n}^{-2}\,,

and thus

(3.19)

\displaystyle\kappa_{n}^{2}=\frac{Z_{n}}{Z_{n+1}}\,,\qquad\check{\kappa}_{n}^{2}=\frac{\check{Z}_{n}}{\check{Z}_{n+1}}\,,\qquad n\geq 0\,.

Proof.

See, e.g.,
[
Meh04
,
For10
,
EKR15
]
.
∎

The monic polynomials are given by the unitary matrix integral with the characteristic polynomial insertion (Heine’s formula),

(3.20a)

\displaystyle\pi_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{n}}\int_{\mathrm{U}(n)}\det(z - U)\det\mathbb{I}(U;\xi)\differential{U}\,,

(3.20b)

\displaystyle{\check{\pi}}_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\check{Z}_{n}}\int_{\mathrm{U}(n)}\det(z - U)\det\check{\mathbb{I}}(U;\xi)\differential{U}\,.

Let
x_{n}=\pi_{n}(0)
,
y_{n}=\check{\pi}_{n}(0)
.
Then, we have the following determinantal formula,

(3.21a)

\displaystyle x_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{Z_{n}}\int_{\mathrm{U}(n)}\det U\det\mathbb{I}(U;\xi)\differential{U}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{Z_{n}}\det_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}I_{-i+j-1}\,,

(3.21b)

\displaystyle y_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{\check{Z}_{n}}\int_{\mathrm{U}(n)}\det U\det\check{\mathbb{I}}(U;\xi)\differential{U}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{\check{Z}_{n}}\det_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}\check{I}_{-i+j-1}\,.

As mentioned in Remark
3.3
, we may realize
x_{n}
and
y_{n}
from the unitary matrix integral with insertion of
\det U^{-1}
due to the symmetry of the weight function,
\mathbb{I}(z;\xi)=\mathbb{I}(z^{-1};\xi)
.

Lemma 3.5
.

The following relations hold,

(3.22)

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa_{n-1}^{2}}{\kappa_{n}^{2}}=\frac{Z_{n+1}Z_{n-1}}{Z_{n}^{2}}=1-x_{n}^{2}\,,\qquad\frac{{\check{\kappa}}_{n-1}^{2}}{\check{\kappa}_{n}^{2}}=\frac{\check{Z}_{n+1}\check{Z}_{n-1}}{\check{Z}_{n}^{2}}=1-y_{n}^{2}\,.

Proof.

See, e.g.,
[
AvM02
]
.
∎

and

from the Riemann–Hilbert problem associated with the orthogonal polynomial on the unit circle in §

.

3.3.
Relation to
q
-orthogonal polynomials

The Askey–Wilson polynomial
\{p_{n}=p_{n}(\cdot;a,b,c,d;q)\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
is the classical
q
-orthogonal polynomial obeying the following condition (e,g.,
[
Ism05
,
KLS10
]

(3.23)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}p_{n}(x)p_{m}(x)w(x)\frac{\differential{x}}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}=h_{n}\delta_{n,m}\,,

where the weight function is given by

(3.24)

\displaystyle w(x)=w(x;a,b,c,d;q)=\frac{h(x,1)h(x,-1)h(x,q^{\frac{1}{2}})h(x,-q^{\frac{1}{2}})}{h(x,a)h(x,b)h(x,c)h(x,d)}\,,\quad|a|,|b|,|c|,|d|<1\,,

with

(3.25)

\displaystyle h(x,\alpha)=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}(1+\alpha^{2}q^{2n}-2\alpha q^{n}x)\,,

and

(3.26)

\displaystyle h_{n}=\frac{(abcdq^{n-1};q)_{n}(abcdq^{2n};q)_{\infty}}{(q^{n+1},abq^{n},acq^{n},adq^{n},bcq^{n},bdq^{n},cdq^{n};q)_{\infty}}\,.

It has been known that all the classical
q
-orthogonal polynomials are obtained from the reduction of the Askey–Wilson polynomial.
Our orthogonal polynomial have a similar structure to this
q
-orthogonal polynomial as follows.

Due to the symmetry of the weight function
\mathbb{I}(z;\xi)=\mathbb{I}(z^{-1};\xi)
, we may instead consider a modified inner product with respect to the weight function,

(3.27)

\displaystyle\left<f,g\right>=\oint_{\mathbb{T}}f\left(\frac{z+z^{-1}}{2}\right)\overline{g\left(\frac{z+z^{-1}}{2}\right)}\mathbb{I}(z;\xi)\frac{\differential{z}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z}\,.

Applying the change of variable
z=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta}
, we have

\displaystyle\left<f,g\right>

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f\left(\cos\theta\right)\overline{g}\left(\cos\theta\right)\left(\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}(1+\xi^{2}q^{2n+1}-2\xi q^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\cos\theta)\right)^{-1}\frac{\differential{\theta}}{2\pi}

(3.28)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}f\left(x\right)\overline{g}\left(x\right)h(x,\xi q^{\frac{1}{2}})^{-1}\frac{\differential{x}}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}\,,\qquad x=\cos\theta\,.

Compared with the weight function of the Askey–Wilson polynomial, our weight function
h(x;\xi q^{\frac{1}{2}})^{-1}
cannot be obtained from a naive reduction of that of the Askey–Wilson polynomial.
Our weight function is interpreted as a
q
-analogue of the Bessel transform of the orthogonal polynomial measure on the unit circle
[
Gol05
]
.

Applying the residue formula, we may also write the inner product as the basic hypergeometric series,

(3.29)

\displaystyle\left<f,g\right>

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\xi^{2}q;q)_{\infty}(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f\left(z_{n}\right)\bar{g}\left(z_{n}\right)\frac{(\xi^{2}q;q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{n}}(-1)^{n}q^{n\choose 2}q^{n},

where we define

(3.30)

\displaystyle z_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\xi q^{n+\frac{1}{2}}+\xi^{-1}q^{-n-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\,.

This expression is also analogous to the orthogonality condition for the discrete
q
-orthogonal polynomial, e.g.,
q
-Racah polynomial,
q
-Hahn polynomial.
This also implies a
q
-analogue of the Bessel transform for the discrete
q
-orthogonal polynomial.

4.
Riemann–Hilbert problem

In this Section, we study the Riemann–Hilbert problem associated with the orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle defined in §
3.2
.

Dual polynomial

Let
\mathsf{p}
be a polynomial in
z\in\mathbb{T}
of degree
n
.
We define a dual polynomial of
\mathsf{p}
by

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathsf{p}^{*}(z)=\overline{\mathsf{p}}(z^{-1})z^{n}\,.

Cauchy transform

We define the Cauchy transform of
\mathsf{p}
,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathsf{C}[\mathsf{p}](z)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\oint_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\mathsf{p}(w)}{w-z}\differential{w}=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\oint_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\mathsf{p}(w)}{1-z/w}\frac{\differential{w}}{w}\,.

Since this Cauchy transform is defined by the integral on
\mathbb{T}
, it may cause a singularity, which plays an important role in the Riemann–Hilbert problem as discussed below.

4.1.
Weight function
\mathbb{I}

We focus on the weight function
\mathbb{I}
and discuss the other case
\check{\mathbb{I}}
in §
4.2
.
Here is a useful result about the Riemann–Hilbert problem associated with the orthogonal polynomial on the unit circle.

Proposition 4.1

(
[

]
)
.

Let

Under the notation above, the matrix-valued function,

MacMahon function

Let

We define a modified MacMahon function,

(A.1)

\displaystyle M(\xi;q)=\prod_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{(1-\xi q^{n})^{n}}=\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\xi^{n}}{n(q^{\frac{n}{2}}-q^{-\frac{n}{2}})^{2}}\right)\,.

Let

The generating function of

(A.2)

\displaystyle M(1;q)=\prod_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{(1-q^{n})^{n}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p(n)q^{n}\,.

q
-shifted factorial

We define the
q
-shifted factorial,

(A.3)

\displaystyle(x;q)_{n}:=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(1-xq^{k})\,.

The limit
\lim_{n\to\infty}(x;q)_{n}=(x;q)_{\infty}
converges for

We write

(A.4)

\displaystyle(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k};q)_{n}=(x_{1};q)_{n}(x_{2};q)_{n}\cdots(x_{k};q)_{n}\,.

Basic hypergeometric series

We define the basic hypergeometric series,

(A.5)

\displaystyle{}_{r}\phi_{s}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r};b_{1},\ldots,b_{s};q,x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r};q)_{n}}{(q;b_{1},\ldots,b_{s};q)_{n}}\left((-1)^{n}q^{n\choose 2}\right)^{1+s-r}x^{n}\,.

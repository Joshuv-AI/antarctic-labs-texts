Lie-transform derivation of oscillation-center quasilinear Theory
Alain J. Brizard 1,a

Abstract
The derivation of the oscillation-center quasilinear theory in an unmagnetized plasma by Dewar Dewar 1973 is rederived by Lie-transform perturbation method.



1
Introduction

The problem of the quasilinear diffusion of a background Vlasov distribution in the presence of electromagnetic waves is a classic example of wave-particle interactions beyond the linear regime
Stix 1992
. Because the nonlinear Vlasov-Maxwell equations can be represented in terms of a Hamiltonian system, in which energy and momentum are conserved exactly at all orders in wave amplitude, it is important to formulate quasilinear theory in terms of resonant particles, which allow the transfer of energy and momentum to the waves, and non-resonant particles which ensure that total (waves+particles) energy and momentum are conserved. In a classic paper, Kaufman
Kaufman 1972
iteratively solved the Vlasov-Poisson equations for an unmagnetized plasma up to second order, to obtain a resonant first-order Vlasov contribution and a second-order nonresonant Vlasov contribution which yielded exact energy-momentum conservation laws.

In a ground-breaking paper in 1973, Dewar
Dewar 1973
rederived Kaufman’s quasilinear theory
Kaufman 1972
by constructing a Hamiltonian theory of quasilinear theory in an unmagnetized plasma, in which resonant and non-resonant particles contribute to the conservation of energy and momentum in the presence of quasilinear diffusion of resonant particles associated with electrostatic fluctuations. This derivation was based on the canonical transformation from particle phase space to oscillation-center phase space, in which the Hamilton-Jacobi perturbation equation is solved iteratively. The results obtained by Dewar
Dewar 1973
in the first part of the paper are then generalized to the case of a weakly nonuniform plasma, for which the energy-momentum conservation laws are still valid. The interested reader may wish to read the paper written by Krommes
Krommes 2012
on the historical impact of Dewar’s 1973 paper. In addition, the work of Kaufman in plasma wave theory is reviewed by Tracy and Brizard
Tracy and Brizard 2009
, while the mathematical foundations of quasilinear theory in inhomogeneous plasma are presented by Dodin
Dodin 2022
;
Dodin 2024
in two recent review papers. In another recent paper, Brizard and Chan
Brizard and Chan 2022
constructed the Hamiltonian formulation of quasilinear diffusion in a uniform magnetized plasma that is perturbed by electromagnetic waves, and demonstrated that their results were identical to the quasilinear theory of Kennel and Engelmann
Kennel and Engelmann 1966
.

The purpose of the present paper is to rederive Dewar’s oscillation-center quasilinear theory for an unmagnetized plasma in the presence of an electrostatic wave where the canonical transformation from particle phase space to oscillation-center phase space is carried by Lie-transform perturbation method
Kaufman 1978
;
Brizard 2009
. The oscillation-center transformation removes the non-resonant part of the electrostatic wave-particle interactions by constructing the new oscillation-center phase-space coordinates, while the oscillation-center Vlasov equation retains the resonant part of the electrostatic wave-particle interactions. Because of the historical importance of Dewar’s paper
Dewar 1973
, the paper presents a complete derivation of the oscillation-center quasilinear theory as outlined in his paper and, along the way, we also rederive the important energy-momentum conservation laws presented by Kaufman
Kaufman 1972
, where the roles of the resonant and non-resonant Vlasov contributions are explicitly presented.

2.2
Oscillation-center Vlasov theory in the absence of quasilinear diffusion

In the absence of quasilinear diffusion in a uniform unmagnetized plasma (i.e., without wave-particle resonances), the standard oscillation-center theory
Cary and Kaufman 1981
;
Brizard 2009
yields the zeroth-order Vlasov equation
d_{0}F_{0}/dt\equiv 0
, the first-order Vlasov equation
d_{0}F_{1}/dt\equiv 0
, since
H_{1}\equiv 0
, and the second-order Vlasov equation
d_{0}F_{2}/dt=\{K_{2},\;F_{0}\}
, where
K_{2}
denotes the oscillation-center ponderomotive potential.

Since the unperturbed Vlasov background is space- and time-independent, Fourier analysis can be carried out explicitly. First, the perturbation electrostatic potential can be expanded as
\Phi_{1}({\bf x},t)\equiv\overline{\Phi}_{1}\,\exp[i\,\theta({\bf x},t)]+\overline{\Phi}_{1}^{*}\,\exp[-i\,\theta({\bf x},t)]
, where
\theta({\bf x},t)\equiv{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf x}-\omega\,t
, while the first-order scalar field can be expanded as
S_{1}({\bf x},{\bf p},t)\equiv\overline{S}_{1}({\bf p})\,\exp[i\,\theta({\bf x},t)]+\overline{S}_{1}^{*}({\bf p})\,\exp[-i\,\theta({\bf x},t)]
, so that the vanishing of the first-order oscillation-center Hamiltonian
H_{1}\equiv 0

Cary and Kaufman 1981
yields the relation

\overline{S}_{1}({\bf p})\;=\;\frac{i\,e\,\overline{\Phi}_{1}}{(\omega-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})}.

(2.24)

At second order in the perturbation analysis, the second-order oscillation-center Hamiltonian is given by the ponderomotive Hamiltonian
Cary and Kaufman 1977

H_{2}({\bf P})\;\equiv\;-\,\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\left\{S_{1},\frac{}{}e\,\Phi_{1}\right\}\right\rangle\;=\;\frac{i}{2}\,e{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\overline{\Phi}_{1}\,\frac{\partial\overline{S}_{1}^{*}}{\partial\bf P}\;-\;\overline{\Phi}_{1}^{*}\,\frac{\partial\overline{S}_{1}}{\partial\bf P}\right)\;=\;\frac{e^{2}|{\bf k}|^{2}\,|\overline{\Phi}_{1}|^{2}}{m\,(\omega-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})^{2}},

(2.25)

where
\langle\cdots\rangle
denotes an average with respect to the wave phase
\theta
, while the second-order scalar
S_{2}({\bf x},{\bf p},t)
is a solution of the evolution equation

\displaystyle\frac{d_{0}S_{2}}{dt}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\,\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\{S_{1},\frac{}{}e\,\Phi_{1}\right\}\;-\;\left\langle\left\{S_{1},\frac{}{}e\,\Phi_{1}\right\}\right\rangle\right)\;=\;\frac{i}{2}\,e{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(e^{2i\theta}\,\overline{\Phi}_{1}\,\frac{\partial\overline{S}_{1}}{\partial\bf p}\;-\;e^{-2i\theta}\,\overline{\Phi}_{1}^{*}\,\frac{\partial\overline{S}_{1}^{*}}{\partial\bf p}\right)

(2.26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\frac{e^{2}|{\bf k}|^{2}}{2m\,(\omega-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})^{2}}\left(\overline{\Phi}_{1}^{2}\,e^{2i\theta}\;+\;\overline{\Phi}_{1}^{*2}\,e^{-2i\theta}\right).

Here, the purpose of the second-order scalar function
S_{2}
is to remove the second harmonics from the oscillation-center dynamics.

In particle phase space, the first-order particle Vlasov distribution is

f_{1}\;=\;\nabla S_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\bf p}\;=\;\left(\overline{S}_{1}\,e^{i\theta}\;-\;\overline{S}_{1}^{*}\,e^{-i\theta}\right)i\,{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\bf p}\;=\;-e\,\left(\overline{\Phi}_{1}\,e^{i\theta}\;+\;\overline{\Phi}_{1}^{*}\,e^{-i\theta}\right)\frac{{\bf k}}{(\omega-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\bf p},

(2.27)

where
F_{1}=0
in the absence of quasilinear diffusion. When it is inserted into the perturbed Poisson equation

|{\bf k}|^{2}\overline{\Phi}_{1}\;=\;4\pi\,e\int\overline{f}_{1}\,d^{3}p\;=\;-\;4\pi\,e^{2}\,\overline{\Phi}_{1}\,\int\frac{{\bf k}}{(\omega-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\bf p}\;d^{3}p,

(2.28)

\epsilon({\bf k},\omega)\;\equiv 1\;+\;\frac{4\pi\,e^{2}}{|{\bf k}|^{2}}\,\int\frac{{\bf k}}{(\omega-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\bf p}\;d^{3}p\;=\;0.

(2.29)

In what follows, we denote

as one of the roots of the dispersion relation (
3.17

for simplicity of presentation. Otherwise, the amplitudes

are functions of

3
Oscillation-center Vlasov theory in the presence of quasilinear diffusion

In the presence of quasilinear diffusion, the background Vlasov distribution
f_{0}({\bf p},\tau)
acquires a slow time dependence that scales with the second order in the perturbation parameter
\delta
, where
\tau\equiv\delta^{2}t
. Hence,
d_{0}f_{0}/dt\equiv\delta^{2}\,\partial f_{0}/\partial\tau
and the slow time-dependence of
f_{0}
implies that the root
\omega_{0}({\bf k},\delta^{2}t)
of the dispersion relation (
3.17
) also has a slow time dependence. Hence, while the background unmagnetized plasma is still spatially uniform, it is no longer time independent and Fourier analysis can only be carried in space.

3.1
Time-dependent Eikonal Theory

The linear time-dependent Vlasov equation can be solved iteratively by using a multiple-time scale method developed by Bateman and Kruskal
Bateman and Kruskal 1972
. The asymptotic solution proposed by Bateman and Kruskal is presented as follows. First, we consider the partial differential equation

\frac{\partial f(u,\delta\,t)}{\partial t}\;-\;i\,D(\omega,u)\,f(u,\delta\,t)\;=\;g(u,\delta\,t),

(3.1)

where both functions
(f,g)
depend on the coordinate
u
and are weakly dependent on time
(\delta\ll 1)
, while
D\equiv\omega-u
is assumed to be time independent. By introducing the integrating factor
\exp(-\,i\,D\,t)
, we obtain the formal solution

e^{-\,i\,D\,t}\;f(u,\delta\,t)\;=\;\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-i\,D\,t^{\prime}}\;g(u,\tau^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime},

(3.2)

where the “initial” value of the first-order field is
f(u,-\infty)\equiv 0
, and we assume that
\omega
has a small positive imaginary part so that
\exp(-i\,D\,t)\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow-\,\infty
. After performing two integration by parts, this formal solution becomes

e^{-\,i\,D\,t}\;f(u,\delta\,t)\;=\;e^{-\,i\,D\,t}\left(\frac{i\,g}{D}\;+\;\frac{1}{D^{2}}\;\frac{\partial g}{\partial t}\right)\;-\;\frac{1}{D^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-i\,D\,t^{\prime}}\;\frac{\partial^{2}g(u,\delta\,t^{\prime})}{\partial t^{\prime 2}}\,dt^{\prime}\;=\;e^{-\,i\,D\,t}\left(g\;+\;i\,\frac{\partial g}{\partial t}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\omega}\right)\frac{i}{D}\;+\;\cdots,

(3.3)

where we henceforth ignore second-order partial time derivatives on
g(u,\delta\,t)
, and we have expressed
D^{-2}\equiv-\,\partial D^{-1}/\partial\omega
. Hence, the solution of Eq. (
3.1
) is expressed as

f(u,\delta\,t)\;=\;\left(g(u,\delta\,t)\;+\;i\,\frac{\partial g(u,\delta\,t)}{\partial t}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\omega}\right)\frac{i}{\omega-u}.

(3.4)

This general solution, which includes time-dependence up to first order in the perturbation parameter
\delta\ll 1
, will be used prominently when solving the first-order oscillations-center equations for
\overline{S}_{1}({\bf p},\delta\,t)
and
\overline{F}_{1}({\bf p},\delta\,t)
.

3.2
Linear oscillation-center Vlasov equation

Since the time dependence of the background Vlasov distribution is weak, we can proceed with a time-dependent eikonal representation of the perturbation potential

\Phi_{1}({\bf x},t)\;\equiv\;\overline{\Phi}_{1}(\delta t)\,\exp[i\,\Theta({\bf x},t)]+\overline{\Phi}_{1}^{*}(\delta t)\,\exp[-i\,\Theta({\bf x},t)],

(3.5)

where the eikonal phase is defined as
\Theta({\bf x},t)={\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf x}-\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\omega_{0}({\bf k},\delta^{2}t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}

S_{1}({\bf x},{\bf p},t)\;\equiv\;\overline{S}_{1}({\bf p},\delta t)\,\exp[i\,\Theta({\bf x},t)]+\overline{S}_{1}^{*}({\bf p},\delta t)\,\exp[-i\,\Theta({\bf x},t)].

(3.6)

In this case, the first-order oscillation-center Hamiltonian
\overline{H}_{1}\neq 0

\overline{H}_{1}\;\equiv\;e\,\overline{\Phi}_{1}\,\Delta\;+\;i\,e\,\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi}_{1}}{\partial t}\;\frac{\partial\Delta}{\partial\omega_{0}}\;=\;e\,\overline{\Phi}_{1}\;+\;i\,(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})\,\overline{S}_{1}\;-\;\frac{\partial\overline{S}_{1}}{\partial t},

(3.7)

where
\Delta(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})

Dewar 1973

\frac{\partial\overline{S}_{1}}{\partial t}\;-\;i\,(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})\,\overline{S}_{1}\;=\;e\,\overline{\Phi}_{1}\,(1-\Delta)\;+\;i\,e\,\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi}_{1}}{\partial t}\;\frac{\partial(1-\Delta)}{\partial\omega_{0}}.

(3.8)

By using the formal solution (
3.4

\displaystyle\overline{S}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[e\,\overline{\Phi}_{1}\,(1-\Delta)\;+\;i\,e\,\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi}_{1}}{\partial t}\;\frac{\partial(1-\Delta)}{\partial\omega_{0}}\right]\frac{i}{\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}}\;-\;e\,\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi}_{1}}{\partial t}\,(1-\Delta)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\omega_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}}\right)

\displaystyle\left(e\,\overline{\Phi}_{1}\;+\;i\,e\,\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi}_{1}}{\partial t}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\omega_{0}}\right)\frac{i\,(1-\Delta)}{(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})},

(3.10)

which yields the resonant first-order oscillation-center Vlasov distribution

\overline{F}_{1}\;=\;-\,{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\bf p}\left(e\,\overline{\Phi}_{1}\;+\;i\,e\,\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi}_{1}}{\partial t}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\omega_{0}}\right)\frac{\Delta}{(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})},

(3.11)

which is not derived by Dewar
Dewar 1973
.

3.3
Poisson equation in the presence of quasilinear diffusion

We now express the Poisson equation (
2.28
) in particle phase-space as

|{\bf k}|^{2}\,\overline{\Phi}_{1}(\delta\,t)\;=\;4\pi\,e\int\overline{f}_{1}({\bf p},\delta\,t)\;d^{3}p

(3.12)

where the first-order Vlasov distribution is defined as

\overline{f}_{1}\;\equiv\;\overline{F}_{1}\;+\;i\,{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\bf p}\;\overline{S}_{1}\;=\;-\,{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\bf p}\left(e\,\overline{\Phi}_{1}\;+\;i\,e\,\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi}_{1}}{\partial t}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\omega_{0}}\right)\frac{1}{(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})},

(3.13)

where the resonance window function
\Delta
has canceled out. We note that the solution (
3.13
), also given by Eq.
Dewar 1973
(26), exactly matches the solution of the first-order Vlasov equation (
2.13
) given by Eq. (
3.4
).

When Eq. (
3.13
) is inserted into the Poisson equation (
2.28
), we obtain the dispersion relation

\epsilon({\bf k},\omega_{0})\,\overline{\Phi}_{1}\;+\;i\,\frac{\partial\epsilon_{r}({\bf k},\omega_{0})}{\partial\omega_{0}}\;\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi}_{1}}{\partial t}\;=\;0,

(3.14)

where the permitivity
\epsilon({\bf k},\omega_{0})
is defined in Eq. (
3.17
). We now note that the permitivity

\epsilon({\bf k},\omega_{0})\equiv\epsilon_{r}({\bf k},\omega_{0})+i\,\epsilon_{i}({\bf k},\omega_{0})
can be separated into real and imaginary parts, where
|\epsilon_{i}|\ll\epsilon_{r}
. When this decomposition is inserted into the Poisson equation (
3.14
), we obtain the real-valued dispersion relation
\epsilon_{r}({\bf k},\omega_{0})=0
, and the imaginary-valued relation

\epsilon_{i}({\bf k},\omega_{0})\,\overline{\Phi}_{1}\;+\;\frac{\partial\epsilon_{r}({\bf k},\omega_{0})}{\partial\omega_{0}}\;\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi}_{1}}{\partial t}\;=\;0,

which yields the field evolution equation

\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi}_{1}}{\partial t}\;=\;-\,\left(\frac{\partial\epsilon_{r}}{\partial\omega_{0}}\right)^{-1}\epsilon_{i}\,\overline{\Phi}_{1}\;\equiv\;\gamma\,\overline{\Phi}_{1},

(3.15)

where
\gamma
denotes the exponential growth rate of the eikonal perturbation amplitude
\overline{\Phi}_{1}
. In particular, by substituting the Plemelj formula
Stix 1992

\lim_{\nu\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left(\frac{1}{\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}+i\,\nu}\right)\;\equiv\;{\mathcal{P}}\left(\frac{1}{\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}}\right)\;-\;i\,\pi\;\delta(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})

(3.16)

where
{\mathcal{P}}
denotes the principal part, into the permitivity function (
3.17
), we find

\epsilon({\bf k},\omega_{0})\;=\;1\;+\;\frac{4\pi\,e^{2}}{|{\bf k}|^{2}}\int{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\bf p}\left[{\mathcal{P}}\left(\frac{1}{\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}}\right)\;-\;i\,\pi\;\delta(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})\right]d^{3}p,

which immediately yields the dispersion relation

\epsilon_{r}({\bf k},\omega_{0})=1\;+\;\frac{4\pi\,e^{2}}{|{\bf k}|^{2}}\,\int{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\bf p}\left[{\mathcal{P}}\left(\frac{1}{(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})}\right)\right]\;d^{3}p\;=\;0.

(3.17)

while the imaginary part is

\epsilon_{i}({\bf k},\omega_{0})\;=\;-\;\frac{4\pi\,e^{2}}{|{\bf k}|^{2}}\int{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\bf p}\left[\pi\frac{}{}\delta(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})\right]d^{3}p,

(3.18)

so that the growth rate defined in Eq. (
3.15
) becomes

\gamma\;=\;-\,\left(\frac{\partial\epsilon_{r}}{\partial\omega_{0}}\right)^{-1}\epsilon_{i}\;=\;\frac{4\pi\,e^{2}}{|{\bf k}|^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial\epsilon_{r}}{\partial\omega_{0}}\right)^{-1}\int{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\bf p}\left[\pi\frac{}{}\delta(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})\right]d^{3}p,

(3.19)

which is also given by Eq.
Dewar 1973
(32). Hence, the wave field eikonal amplitude
\overline{\Phi}_{1}
grows exponentially as a result of particle energy being transferred from the particles that are resonant with the wave.

4
Quasilinear diffusion in particle phase space

Before continuing with quasilinear theory in oscillation-center phase space, we present quasilinear theory in particle phase space, and rederive the results presented by Kaufman
Kaufman 1972
.

4.1
Second-order particle Vlasov equation

First, we proceed with the derivation of the quasilinear diffusion equation for background Vlasov distribution
f_{0}({\bf p},\tau)
as well as the derivation of the spatially-averaged second-order particle Vlasov distribution
\langle f_{2}\rangle({\bf p},\delta t)
. These two distributions appear in the second-order particle Vlasov equation

\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\tau}\;+\;\frac{\partial\langle f_{2}\rangle}{\partial t}\;=\;e\left\langle\left\{\Phi_{1},\frac{}{}f_{1}\right\}\right\rangle\;=\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf p}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\langle e\,\nabla\Phi_{1}\frac{}{}f_{1}\right\rangle\;\equiv\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf p}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left[e\,i\,{\bf k}\left(\overline{\Phi}_{1}\;\overline{f}_{1}^{*}\;-\frac{}{}\overline{\Phi}_{1}^{*}\;\overline{f}_{1}\right)\right],

(4.1)

where the eikonal amplitude of the first-order particle Vlasov distribution is given by Eq. (
3.13
). Upon substituting Eq. (
3.13
) into Eq. (
4.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\tau}+\frac{\partial\langle f_{2}\rangle}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf p}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left[-\,2\,e^{2}|\overline{\Phi}_{1}|^{2}\;{\bf k}{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\bf p}\;{\rm Im}\left(\frac{1}{\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}}\right)\right]

(4.2)

\displaystyle-\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf p}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left[e^{2}\,{\bf k}{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\bf p}\;\frac{\partial|\overline{\Phi}_{1}|^{2}}{\partial t}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\omega_{0}}{\rm Re}\left(\frac{1}{\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}}\right)\right].

By using the Plemelj formula (
3.16
), we can separate the time evolution of
f_{0}
and
\langle f_{2}\rangle
in terms of the quasilinear diffusion equation for the background Vlasov distribution
f_{0}
:

\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\tau}\;=\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf p}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\mathbb{D}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\bf p}\right),

(4.3)

expressed in terms of the quasilinear diffusion tensor
Kaufman 1972

\mathbb{D}\;\equiv\;e^{2}\,|\overline{\Phi}_{1}|^{2}\;{\bf k}{\bf k}\left[2\pi\frac{}{}\delta(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})\right],

(4.4)

which takes into account only those particles that are resonant with the wave, while the space-averaged second-order particle Vlasov distribution is now expressed as

\langle f_{2}\rangle\;=\;-\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf p}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left[e^{2}|\overline{\Phi}_{1}|^{2}\,{\bf k}{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\bf p}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\omega_{0}}{\mathcal{P}}\left(\frac{1}{\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}}\right)\right],

(4.5)

which takes into account the non-resonant part of the particle Vlasov distribution. This second-order non-resonant particle Vlasov distribution was derived iteratively by Kaufman
Kaufman 1972
and is given in Eq.
Dewar 1973
(42), without derivation, by Dewar.

4.2
Energy-momentum conservation laws

The derivation of the conservation laws of energy and momentum proceeds by properly taking into account the separation between the energy-momentum of the waves and the energy-momentum of the particles.

a
Energy conservation law

The particle energy is simply expressed in terms of the unperturbed kinetic energy of the particles

{\mathcal{E}}_{0}(t)\;=\;\int f_{0}({\bf p},\tau)\;\frac{|{\bf p}|^{2}}{2m}\;d^{3}p.

(4.6)

By using the quasilinear diffusion equation (
4.3
), we obtain the time derivative

\displaystyle\frac{d{\mathcal{E}}_{0}}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\,\int\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\tau}({\bf p},\tau)\;\frac{|{\bf p}|^{2}}{2m}\;d^{3}p\;=\;-\,\delta^{2}\int{\bf v}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{D}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\bf p}\;d^{3}p

(4.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\delta^{2}\,e^{2}\,|\overline{\Phi}_{1}|^{2}\;2\pi\,\omega_{0}\;\int{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\bf p}\;\delta(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})\;d^{3}p,

where the term
{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}
was replaced with
\omega_{0}
through the resonance condition
\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}=0
imposed by the delta function
\delta(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})
.

Next, the total wave energy

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{2}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\left[\frac{|{\bf k}|^{2}}{4\pi}\;|\overline{\Phi}_{1}|^{2}\;+\;\int\frac{|{\bf p}|^{2}}{2m}\;\langle f_{2}\rangle\;d^{3}p\right]

(4.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\;\frac{|{\bf k}|^{2}}{4\pi}\;|\overline{\Phi}_{1}|^{2}\left[1\;+\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\omega_{0}}\left(\frac{4\pi\,e^{2}}{|{\bf k}|^{2}}\int{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\bf p}\;\omega_{0}\,{\mathcal{P}}\left(\frac{1}{\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}}\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\;\frac{|{\bf k}|^{2}}{4\pi}\;|\overline{\Phi}_{1}|^{2}\left[1\;+\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\omega_{0}}\left(\omega_{0}\frac{}{}(\epsilon_{r}-1)\right)\right]\;=\;\delta^{2}\;\frac{|{\bf k}|^{2}}{4\pi}\;|\overline{\Phi}_{1}|^{2}\;\omega_{0}\;\frac{\partial\epsilon_{r}}{\partial\omega_{0}},

where we have used the dispersion relation
\epsilon_{r}({\bf k},\omega_{0})=0
. The time derivative of the wave energy is now expressed as

\frac{d{\mathcal{E}}_{2}}{dt}\;=\;\delta^{2}\;\frac{|{\bf k}|^{2}}{4\pi}\;\left(2\,\gamma\frac{}{}|\overline{\Phi}_{1}|^{2}\right)\;\omega_{0}\;\frac{\partial\epsilon_{r}}{\partial\omega_{0}}\;=\;\delta^{2}\;|{\bf k}|^{2}\;|\overline{\Phi}_{1}|^{2}\;2\pi\,\omega_{0}\;\int{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\bf p}\;\delta(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})\;d^{3}p,

(4.9)

which, when combined with Eq. (
4.7
) yields the energy conservation law
d({\mathcal{E}}_{0}+{\mathcal{E}}_{2})/dt=0
.

b
Momentum conservation law

The particle momentum is simply expressed in terms of the unperturbed momentum of the particles

\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{0}(t)\;=\;\int{\bf p}\;f_{0}({\bf p},\tau)\;d^{3}p.

(4.10)

By using the quasilinear diffusion equation (
4.3
), we obtain the time derivative

\displaystyle\frac{d\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{0}}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\,\int{\bf p}\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\tau}({\bf p},\tau)\;d^{3}p\;=\;-\,\delta^{2}\int\mathbb{D}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\bf p}\;d^{3}p

(4.11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\delta^{2}\,e^{2}\,|\overline{\Phi}_{1}|^{2}\;2\pi\,{\bf k}\;\int{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\bf p}\;\delta(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})\;d^{3}p

Next, the wave momentum is expressed as

\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{2}\;\equiv\;\delta^{2}\int{\bf p}\;\langle f_{2}\rangle\;d^{3}p\;=\;\delta^{2}\;\frac{|{\bf k}|^{2}}{4\pi}\;|\overline{\Phi}_{1}|^{2}\;{\bf k}\;\frac{\partial\epsilon_{r}}{\partial\omega_{0}}\;\equiv\;\frac{\bf k}{\omega_{0}}\;{\mathcal{E}}_{2},

(4.12)

which has a simple relation with the total wave energy (
4.8
). The time derivative of the total wave momentum is now expressed as

\frac{d\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{2}}{dt}\;=\;\delta^{2}\;\frac{|{\bf k}|^{2}}{4\pi}\;\left(2\,\gamma\frac{}{}|\overline{\Phi}_{1}|^{2}\right)\;{\bf k}\;\frac{\partial\epsilon_{r}}{\partial\omega_{0}}\;=\;\delta^{2}\;|{\bf k}|^{2}\;|\overline{\Phi}_{1}|^{2}\;2\pi\,{\bf k}\;\int{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\bf p}\;\delta(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})\;d^{3}p,

(4.13)

which, when combined with Eq. (
4.11
), yields the momentum conservation law
d(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{0}+\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{2})/dt=0
.

5
Quasilinear diffusion in Oscillation-center Phase Space

We now return to our presentation of quasilinear theory in oscillation-center phase space.

5.1
Second-order oscillation-center Vlasov equation

The second-order Vlasov equation in oscillation-center phase space is expressed as

\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\tau}+\frac{d_{0}F_{2}}{dt}\;=\;\{H_{1},\;F_{1}\}\;+\;\{H_{2},\;F_{0}\},

(5.1)

which is unfortunately not discussed by Dewar
Dewar 1973
.

By spatially averaging this equation (equivalent to an eikonal average over
\Theta
), we obtain the averaged second-order oscillation-center Vlasov equation

\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\tau}+\frac{\partial\langle F_{2}\rangle}{\partial t}\;=\;\left\langle\{H_{1},\frac{}{}F_{1}\}\right\rangle\;+\;\{\langle H_{2}\rangle,\;F_{0}\}\;=\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf P}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\langle\nabla H_{1}\frac{}{}F_{1}\right\rangle\;=\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf P}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left[i\,{\bf k}\,\left(\overline{H}_{1}\,\overline{F}_{1}^{*}\;-\frac{}{}\overline{H}_{1}^{*}\,\overline{F}_{1}\right)\right],

(5.2)

where
\langle H_{2}\rangle\equiv K_{2}({\bf P})
is the ponderomotive Hamiltonian (
2.25
) and thus
\{\langle H_{2}\rangle,F_{0}\}\equiv 0
. First, we note that the surviving terms
(\overline{H}_{1},\overline{F}_{1})
on the right side are only associated with resonant wave-particle contributions. In the following analysis, we will use the Plemelj formula (
3.16
) to obtain the resonant and non-resonant identities

\left.\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Delta/(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})&\equiv&-i\,\pi\;\delta(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})\\
&&\\
(1-\Delta)/(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})&\equiv&{\mathcal{P}}\left[(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})^{-1}\right]\end{array}\right\}.

(5.3)

Up to first order in partial-time derivatives, we find

\displaystyle\overline{H}_{1}\,\overline{F}_{1}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[e\,\overline{\Phi}_{1}\,\Delta\;+\;i\,e\,\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi}_{1}}{\partial t}\;\frac{\partial\Delta}{\partial\omega_{0}}\right]\left[-\,{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\bf P}\left(e\,\overline{\Phi}_{1}^{*}\;-\;i\,e\,\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi}_{1}^{*}}{\partial t}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\omega_{0}}\right)\frac{\Delta}{(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})^{*}}\right]

(5.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\bf P}\left[\left(e^{2}\,|\overline{\Phi}_{1}|^{2}\;+\;i\,e^{2}\,\overline{\Phi}_{1}^{*}\,\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi}_{1}}{\partial t}\,\frac{\partial\Delta}{\partial\omega_{0}}\right)\,i\pi\,\delta(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})\;+\;\pi\,e^{2}\,\overline{\Phi}_{1}\,\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi}_{1}^{*}}{\partial t}\,\Delta\,\frac{\partial\delta(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})}{\partial\omega_{0}}\right]

so that

\displaystyle i\,{\bf k}\,\left(\overline{H}_{1}\,\overline{F}_{1}^{*}\;-\frac{}{}\overline{H}_{1}^{*}\,\overline{F}_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf k}\,{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\bf P}\left[e^{2}\,|\overline{\Phi}_{1}|^{2}\;2\pi\,\delta(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})+i\,\pi\,e^{2}\left(\overline{\Phi}_{1}^{*}\,\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi}_{1}}{\partial t}-\overline{\Phi}_{1}\,\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi}_{1}^{*}}{\partial t}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial\omega_{0}}\left(\Delta\frac{}{}\delta(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})\right)\right]

(5.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[e^{2}\,|\overline{\Phi}_{1}|^{2}\frac{}{}2\pi\,\delta(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})\right]{\bf k}\,{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\bf P}\;\equiv\;\mathbb{D}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\bf P},

where the quasilinear diffusion tensor in oscillation-center phase space is identical to Eq.
4.4
, and the partial-time derivative terms

\overline{\Phi}_{1}^{*}\,\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi}_{1}}{\partial t}\;-\;\overline{\Phi}_{1}\,\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi}_{1}^{*}}{\partial t}\;\equiv\;(\gamma-\gamma)\,|\overline{\Phi}_{1}|^{2}\;=\;0

(5.6)

cancel out under the assumption of a real growth rate
\gamma
. Hence, we conclude from the second-order oscillation-center Vlasov equation

\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\tau}+\frac{\partial\langle F_{2}\rangle}{\partial t}\;=\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf P}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\mathbb{D}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\bf P}\right)\;\equiv\;\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\tau}

(5.7)

that the space-averaged second-order oscillation-center Vlasov distribution
\langle F_{2}\rangle
is independent of time.

We now show that
\langle F_{2}\rangle
vanishes, by computing the space-averaged of Eq. (
2.17
), we obtain

\langle f_{2}\rangle\;=\;\langle F_{2}\rangle\;+\;\left\langle\left\{S_{1},\frac{}{}F_{1}\right\}\right\rangle\;+\;\{\langle S_{2}\rangle,\;F_{0}\}\;+\;\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\left\{S_{1},\frac{}{}\{S_{1},\;F_{0}\}\right\}\right\rangle.

(5.8)

First, we note that
\langle S_{2}\rangle
is a function of
{\bf P}
and, therefore, we find
\{\langle S_{2}\rangle,\;F_{0}\}\equiv 0
. Next, the term
\left\langle\left\{S_{1},\frac{}{}F_{1}\right\}\right\rangle
simultaneously involves the contributions of a resonant term
\overline{F}_{1}\sim\Delta
and a non-resonant term
\overline{S}_{1}\sim(1-\Delta)
, which implies that
\left\langle\left\{S_{1},\frac{}{}F_{1}\right\}\right\rangle\equiv 0
must vanish identically.

Hence, we are left with the relation

\langle F_{2}\rangle\;=\;\langle f_{2}\rangle\;-\;\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\left\{S_{1},\frac{}{}\{S_{1},\;f_{0}\}\right\}\right\rangle\;=\;\langle f_{2}\rangle\;-\;\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf p}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\left\langle\nabla S_{1}\,\nabla S_{1}\right\rangle\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\bf p}\right),

(5.9)

which also appears as Eq.
Dewar 1973
(42) in Dewar’s work. First, we find
\left\langle\nabla S_{1}\,\nabla S_{1}\right\rangle\equiv 2\,|\overline{S}_{1}|^{2}\,{\bf k}{\bf k}
, where

\displaystyle|\overline{S}_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\left(e\,\overline{\Phi}_{1}\;+\;i\,e\,\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi}_{1}}{\partial t}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\omega_{0}}\right){\mathcal{P}}\left(\frac{1}{(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})}\right)\right|^{2}

(5.10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{2}\,|\overline{\Phi}_{1}|^{2}\;{\mathcal{P}}\left(\frac{1}{(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})^{2}}\right)\;+\;\frac{i}{2}\,e^{2}\left(\overline{\Phi}_{1}^{*}\,\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi}_{1}}{\partial t}-\overline{\Phi}_{1}\,\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi}_{1}^{*}}{\partial t}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial\omega_{0}}{\mathcal{P}}\left(\frac{1}{(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{2}\,|\overline{\Phi}_{1}|^{2}\;{\mathcal{P}}\left(\frac{1}{(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})^{2}}\right)\;\equiv\;-\;e^{2}\,|\overline{\Phi}_{1}|^{2}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\omega_{0}}{\mathcal{P}}\left(\frac{1}{(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})}\right),

where we have used Eq. (
5.6
). We therefore find

\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf p}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\left\langle\nabla S_{1}\,\nabla S_{1}\right\rangle\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\bf p}\right)\;=\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf p}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left[-\;e^{2}\,|\overline{\Phi}_{1}|^{2}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\omega_{0}}{\mathcal{P}}\left(\frac{1}{(\omega_{0}-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v})}\right)\;{\bf k}{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\bf p}\right]\;\equiv\;\langle f_{2}\rangle,

(5.11)

which is exactly equal to the non-resonant second-order averaged Vlasov distribution
\langle f_{2}\rangle
, defined in Eq. (
4.5
), so that
\langle F_{2}\rangle=0
.

5.2
Energy-momentum conservation laws

The background kinetic energy in oscillation-center phase space is

{\mathcal{E}}_{0}(\delta^{2}t)\;=\;\int\frac{|{\bf P}|^{2}}{2m}\;F_{0}({\bf P},\delta^{2}t)\;d^{3}P

(5.12)

while the second-order oscillation-center total energy is

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\left[\frac{|{\bf k}|^{2}}{4\pi}\;|\overline{\Phi}_{1}|^{2}\;+\;\int\left(\frac{|{\bf P}|^{2}}{2m}\;\langle F_{2}\rangle\;-\;\frac{\bf P}{m}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\langle\nabla S_{1}\;F_{1}\rangle\;+\;\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf P}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\langle\nabla S_{1}\;\frac{\bf P}{m}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla S_{1}\right\rangle\;F_{0}\right)d^{3}P\right]

(5.13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\left[\frac{|{\bf k}|^{2}}{4\pi}\;|\overline{\Phi}_{1}|^{2}\;-\;\int\frac{\bf P}{2m}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\langle\nabla S_{1}\;\nabla S_{1}\right\rangle\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\bf P}\;d^{3}P\right],

where we used
\langle F_{2}\rangle\equiv 0
and
\langle\nabla S_{1}\;F_{1}\rangle\equiv 0
. By substituting Eq. (
5.11
), we see that
{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
is identical to the second-order particle total energy (
4.8
) and, thus, the energy conservation law is also satisfied in oscillation-center phase space.

Likewise, the background momentum density in osccilation-center phase space is

\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{0}(\delta^{2}t)\;=\;\int{\bf P}\;F_{0}({\bf P},\delta^{2}t)\;d^{3}P,

(5.14)

while the second-order oscillation-center momentum density is

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\int\left({\bf P}\;\langle F_{2}\rangle\;-\;\langle\nabla S_{1}\;F_{1}\rangle\;+\;\frac{1}{2}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf P}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\langle\nabla S_{1}\frac{}{}\nabla S_{1}\right\rangle\;F_{0}\right)d^{3}P

(5.15)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\;\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}\int\left\langle\nabla S_{1}\frac{}{}\nabla S_{1}\right\rangle\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\bf P}\;d^{3}P,

where we once again used
\langle F_{2}\rangle\equiv 0
and
\langle\nabla S_{1}\;F_{1}\rangle\equiv 0
. Once again, by substituting Eq. (
5.11
), we see that

\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{2}

6
Summary

By using the Lie-transform perturbation method, we have derived self-consistent quasilinear theories in particle and oscillation-center phase spaces, originally derived by different methods by Kaufman
Kaufman 1972
and Dewar
Dewar 1973
, respectively. Both quasilinear theories preserve the conservation laws of energy and momentum by clearly separating the energy-momentum carried by the resonant particles and the total energy-momentum carried by the waves (which include contributions from the non-resonant particles).

Future work will expand the recent work by Brizard and Chan
Brizard and Chan 2022
, who presented the Hamiltonian formulation of quasilinear diffusion of resonant particles in a uniform magnetized plasma in the presence of electromagnetic-wave fluctuations, by including the non-resonant particle contributions that preserve the conservation laws of energy and momentum. The generalization to the quasilinear diffusion of relativistic particles in a weakly nonuniform magnetized background plasma that is perturbed by electromagnetic waves of arbitrary frequencies, which was considered in previous papers
Brizard and Chan 2001
;
Brizard and Chan 2004
, will also be considered.

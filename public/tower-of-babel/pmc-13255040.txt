Uncovering aroma profile and key odorants of Zisun tea: insights from grades and cultivars
Authors: Liqing Mei, Xiaomei Mao, Wenhan Fan, Siqi Zhang, Haowei Guo, Xinyao Zhong, Shuying Gong, Fangyuan Fan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Zisun tea, produced in Changxing, China, holds a profound historical heritage and has significantly influenced the tea culture in numerous tea-producing and consuming countries worldwide. Despite its high reputation, the flavor profile of Zisun tea remains unclear. This study analyzed aroma profiles and key volatile contributors across four grades and six major cultivars of Zisun tea. Results indicated that lower grades exhibited increased baked leaf aroma and decreased tender and fragrance aroma, while cultivars displayed distinct green or fruity attributes. Nineteen key volatile compounds were linked to grade- and cultivar-specific traits, with terpene and amino acid metabolites being primarily floral alcohols. Grade-cultivar shared compounds including hexanal, phenylethyl alcohol, linalool oxide (pyranoid), geraniol and 2-furanmethanol also aided in discriminating both grades and cultivars. Additionally, indole, cis-jasmone, nonanal, 2-furanmethanol, heptanal, hexanoic acid and benzyl alcohol were found to contribute obviously to the variations of overall aroma profile.

Introduction

Zisun Tea, a renowned green tea produced in Changxing, Zhejiang Province, China, derives its name from the phrase “purple excels, bamboo-shoot-like shape excels” recorded in the Tea Classic. Authored by Lu Yu during the Tang Dynasty, the Tea Classic is the earliest extant monograph on tea science. Zisun tea was designated as a tribute tea as early as the Guangde era of Tang Dynasty (764–765 CE), a prestigious status it maintained for nearly a millennium. During the Tang Dynasty, imperial demand for Zisun Tea rose significantly, reaching a peak tribute volume of 18,400 jin. In 770 CE, the court established the Guzhu Tribute Tea Processing Department, the first dedicated tea processing institution in Chinese history, responsible exclusively for manufacturing tribute tea for the imperial court. The production scale at that period involved “30,000 laborers and over 1,000 artisans”, profoundly influencing the daily life and economic structure of the local region. For centuries that followed, tea cultivation and processing remained a primary occupation for the local population. This development not only significantly stimulated the flourishing of tea culture, but also facilitated the spread of Chinese tea cultivation and consumption practices to tea-producing regions worldwide. In recent decades, the Zisun tea brand has experienced revitalization and growing market recognition, driven largely by its profound historical heritage and consequent rising demand. Today, Zisun tea holds the status of a Geographical Indication Agricultural Product of China, and its processing technique has been recognized as national intangible cultural heritage. As a representative traditional premium tea, Zishun tea is widely valued for its rich cultural legacy, which also reflects strong consumer trust in its superior quality. Nevertheless, scientific research on the quality attributes of Zisun tea remains limited.

The quality of the tea is influenced by geographical origin, tea shoots maturity, cultivars and processing techniques (Li et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2023). According to the product standard for Zisun Tea (GH/T 1391), Zisun tea is a green tea type produced from fresh leaves of suitable medium- and small-leaf tea cultivars grown in Changxing, Zhejiang, China. As production of Zisun tea has expanded, its processing technology has been progressively standardized (Xu et al., 2015). The official release of the normative document “Technical Regulation of Zisun Tea Processing” in 2024 (T/ZJTSS 019) marks the initial establishment of a standardized framework for Zisun tea production, thereby laying a solid foundation for brand development. Zishun tea processing involves a series of interconnected steps, including withering, fixation, cooling and moisture redistribution, rolling, shaping and drying, that collectively contribute to its characteristic fragrance aroma and mellow taste. The main tea cultivars used for Zisun tea production include the indigenous Camellia sinensis cv. Zisun population, along with cv. Longjing 43, Zhongcha 108, Zhenong series and Jiukengzao (Wang et al., 2014). The maturity of fresh tea shoot serves as the primary criterion for product grading, as the chemical compositions varies significantly with developmental stage, thereby shaping the distinct quality and market value of the final tea product (Sun et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2021). Zisun tea products are categorized into four quality grades (Premium Grade, Extra Grade, Grade I and Grade II). The Premium grade is distinguished by the use of the most tender shoots, specifically processed from single buds or newly unfolded one-bud-one-leaf raw materials. The Extra Grade utilizes one-bud-one-leaf to one-bud-two-leaves shoots; Grade I employ one-bud-two-leaves shoots; and Grade II is processed from one-bud-two-leaves to one-bud-three-leaves shoots. Grading system of Zisun tea provides a unified framework for production, processing, marketing, and quality control, enabling producers to adhere to precise quality specifications and ensure consistent and stable product quality.

Tea aroma, as a primary sensory determinant, serves as a key driver of market value and is directly correlated with its commercial worth. To date, more than 700 volatile compounds have been identified in tea (Zhai et al., 2022). Distinct aroma profiles arise from specific volatiles compositions. Previous studies showed that floral aroma in green tea can be attributed to seven key volatile compounds, including linalool, phenylethyl alcohol, geraniol, hexanal, heptanal, benzeneacetaldehyde, and trans-β-ionone (Xie et al., 2023). A systematical comparison of aroma profiles identified key odorants responsible for chestnut-like aroma, including ethylbenzene, heptanal, benzaldehyde, 2-pentylfuran, (E,E)-3,5-octadien-2-one, linalool, (Z)-hex-3-en-1-yl hexanoate and trans-β-ionone (Zhu et al., 2018). Meanwhile, dimethyl sulfide has been confirmed as an important contributor to the cooked corn-like aroma of green teas (Liao et al., 2020). Therefore, the distinctive expression of specialty tea products typically results from the composite interplay of multiple aroma attributes, which are present to varying degrees across different quality grades and cultivars, and ultimately constitute the core identity of the product. The aim of the present study is to identify the key sensory attributes and associated aroma compounds by systematic comparison of samples from major cultivated cultivars and graded reference materials. This work elucidates the aromatic characteristics of Zisun tea and delineates the sensory-chemical relationships underpinning its quality, thereby providing a scientific basis for quality evaluation and quality control in Zisun tea production.

Materials and methods

Tea samples

Zisun tea grading reference samples were produced from four fresh leaves grades according to standard specification of “Technical regulation of Zisun Tea processing” (T/ZJTSS 019), including Premium Grade (ZP), Extra Grade (ZE), Grade I (ZI) and Grade II (ZII). Zisun tea cultivar-samples were prepared using fresh tea leaves of Camellia sinensis cv. Zisun population (QTZ), cv. Jiukengzao (JKZ), cv. Longjing 43 (LJ43), cv. Zhenong 117 (ZN117), cv. Zhenong 139 (ZN139) and cv. Zhongcha 108 (ZC108) with plucking standard of one-bud and one leaf/two-leaves. The manufacturing process of Zisun tea samples were carried out according to the standard specification of “Technical regulation of Zisun Tea processing” (T/ZJTSS 019), including spreading, fixing, shaping and drying. After being plucking, fresh leaves were immediately spread indoors at room temperature for about 10 h. Then tea leaves were fixed in a cylinder type fixation machine at about 300 °C for around 130 s. After cooling to room temperature, the fixed-leaves were processed in a tea straightening machine at temperature of 200 °C for 5 min. The initially shaped tea leaves were further processed by pan-firing in a roasting machine under light pressing at temperature of 170 °C for around 120 s for shaping of Zisun tea. Finally, the shaped leaves were dried using hot air at 90 °C for about 60 min till the moisture content of the leaves decreased to below 5.0%. All samples were stored at −20 °C for further analysis.

Sensory evaluation

Tea aroma for sensory evaluation was prepared according to the Chinese national standards “Methodology for Sensory Evaluation of Tea” (GB/T 23776). Accurately weigh 3.0 g of tea samples and place it into the evaluation cup, followed by injecting 150 mL boiling water and reserved for 4 min, then tea infusion was filtered into the evaluation bowl. The aroma retained in the evaluation cup is used for quantitative description and analysis (QDA) of aroma characteristics. QDA was conducted by a panel of six trained tea experts (three females and three males), who specialized in research of tea sensory for over ten years. Five aroma attributes were adopted to evaluate aroma quality of Zisun tea, including fragrance, tender, baked leaf aroma, green and fruity, based on Chinese national standard (GB/T 22291) and related references. “Fragrance” describes an elegant and rich aroma marked by pronounced complexity and a harmonious integration of multiple pleasant olfactory notes. The term “tender” denotes a pleasant and delicate aroma, akin to steamed baby corn, and is characteristic of tea processed from young tea shoots (Tong et al., 2021). “Baked leaf aroma” refers to the distinctive scent resulting from the roasting of mature tea leaves, specifically the second and third leaves in a one-bud-three-leaf configuration. “Green” indicates an aroma evocative of fresh green plants. “Fruity” denotes a rich and sweet aroma reminiscent of ripe fruits reminiscent of various kinds of fruits. The intensity of each aroma attribute was assessed using a three-point scale from 0 to 2, with 0 indicating “imperceptible”, 1 representing “presence”, and 2 denoting “obviousness”. The final scores for aroma attributes were determined by calculating the mean of sensory panelists' scores.

Ethical statement: Sensory evaluation in this study involved only the sniffing of tea samples by healthy adult volunteers under normal conditions. According to the institutional guidelines, formal ethical approval was not required for this purely descriptive food quality assessment. Nonetheless, all participants were informed of the study purpose and gave verbal consent. Participation was voluntary and anonymous; no personal identifying information was collected. The rights and privacy of all participants were fully respected throughout the study.

Volatile compounds extraction

The volatile compounds of Zisun tea samples were extracted using the modified simultaneous distillation extraction (MSDE) as reported in previous study (Wang et al., 2025). An ethanol-reflux condenser was connected to cylindrical tea container and extraction solvent evaporator on both sides respectively. An amount of 15 g tea sample was placed in tea container. The steam generator was installed at the bottom of the cylindrical tea container. A total of 250 mL water and 30 mL of ethyl ether (containing 1 ppm of butylated hydroxytoluene (BHT) as internal standard) were injected into the steam generator and extraction solvent evaporator respectively, which were kept at temperature of 100 °C and 40 °C respectively enabling continuous evaporation of water and ethyl ether solution. High-temperature steam passed through the tea container to moisten the tea sample and continuously dissolve the volatile compounds in the tea leaves. These volatile compounds are then carried to the extraction chamber and extracted by ether. After passing through the condenser tube, extracting solutions are collected in a flask. The condensed volatiles-ether solution was condensed for 2 h using an ethanol-reflux condenser, and then subjected to dehydration using 5.0 g of anhydrous sodium sulfate overnight, till to a volume of 1.0 mL for subsequent analysis. Each experiment of tea sample was performed in triplicate.

Gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC–MS) detection

The volatiles-ether solution was further detected using a Shimadzu gas chromatograph 2010-plus with a triple quadruple mass spectrometer QP 2020 (Shimadzu). The experimental condition was modified based on previous study (Wang et al., 2025). Gas chromatography analysis was performed using a SH-Rxi-5Sil MS capillary column (30 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm). The injection port temperature was set at 250 °C in splitless mode. High-purity hydrogen (purity >99.999%) was used as the carrier gas at a constant flow rate of 1 mL/min, with an injection volume of 1.0 μL. Mass spectrometric conditions were as follows: The interface temperature was maintained at 230 °C, and the ion source temperature was set to 250 °C. electron ionization (EI) was performed at 70 eV in full-scan mode (mass range: 35–450 amu), with a solvent delay of 3 min. Linear gradient temperature program was: Initial temperature of 50 °C (0–5 min); ramped to 210 °C at 3 °C/min (5–58 min); held at 210 °C for 5 min (58–63 min); increased to 230 °C at 15 °C/min (63–65 min); and finally maintained at 230 °C for 5 min (65–70 min).

Statistical analysis

All analyses were replicated in triplicate. Analysis of variance was performed using SPSS software (v25.0, SPSS Inc.). The deconvolution of volatiles analysis raw data was conducted by LabSolutions GC–MS solution (4.45 SP1, Shimadzu). Volatile compounds that were detected in at least two out of three replicates and had a coefficient of variation (CV) of peak area < 25% were retained. Volatile compounds were preliminarily identified by matching mass spectra with the NIST17 Library (similarity >75%), and further confirmed by comparing retention times, retention indices, and literature data. The relative content of identified volatile compounds was calculated as the ratio of their peak areas to that of the internal standard (butylated hydroxytoluene, BHT) according to previous study (Wang et al., 2025). The software SIMCA 14.1 (Umetrics AB, Umea, Sweden) was utilized for conducting orthogonal Partial Least Squares-Discriminant Analysis (OPLS-DA), principal component analysis (PCA), hierarchical cluster analysis (HCA). Heatmaps were plotted using TBtools (https://github.com/CJ-Che n/TBtools). Correlation network visualized using Cytoscape_v3.10.3.

Results and discussion

Aroma profiles of Zisun tea

The grading reference samples effectively represented the defining sensory profile of Zisun tea across quality grades (Fig. 1A), characterized by three key aroma attributes tender aroma, baked leaf aroma and fragrance. With increasing maturity, the dominant aroma attribute shifted from tender to baked leaf aroma, accompanied by a decline in overall fragrance intensity. The premium grade sample demonstrated the most pronounced tender aroma (average 2.0) and fragrance (average 1.9), followed by the Extra grade sample. The highest intensity of baked leaf aroma (average 1.8) was observed in Grade I sample, which is processed from one bud and two leaves. Consistent with previous studies, tender aroma and fragrance are recognized as hallmark attributes of high-quality green tea (Li et al., 2024; Niu et al., 2024).Fig. 1Aroma profiles of Zisun tea from different grades (A) and different cultivars (B).Fig. 1

Zisun tea samples produced from six predominant cultivars exhibited tender aroma and fragrance intensities ranging from 1.0 to 2.0, while baked leaf aroma scores varied between 0.0 and 1.0, consistent with the use of relatively tender raw materials (Fig. 1B). In addition to these three fundamental attributes, the aromatic profiles also reflected intrinsic cultivar characteristics, expressed as fruity and green notes. Tea samples from cv. Jiukengzao and Zisun population exhibited pronounced fruity notes, with cv. Jiukengzao displaying higher fruity intensity of average score 1.8. In contrast, samples processed from cv. Longjing 43, Zhenong 117, Zhenong 139 and Zhongcha 108 were characterized by distinct green aroma notes.

Profiling of marker compounds in Zisun tea

As shown in Fig. 6A, thirteen shared volatile compounds were consistently detected across both the graded standard samples and different cultivars of Zisun tea, comprising 8 alcohols, 3 heterocyclic compounds，1 aldehyde and 1 ketone. Among these, eight volatile compounds exhibited floral characteristics, predominantly alcohols. Two volatiles imparted roasted aroma notes, including 2-furanmethanol (bready) and 1-(1H-pyrrol-2-yl) ethanone (nutty). Additionally, hexanal contributed fresh-green notes, while α-terpineol displayed fruity/woody characteristics. This finding indicated that these 13 compounds are universally present in Zisun tea and represent its characteristic aroma profile. There were 11 shared volatile compounds were identified as key discriminants for both grade differentiation and cultivar classification in Zisun teas, comprising 6 alcohols, 3 aldehydes, 1 acid and 1 hydrocarbon. Among these, five alcohols exhibited floral characteristics, two aldehydes displayed green notes, while nonanal, hexanoic acid, and 2-furanmethanol contributed green, fatty, and bready attributes, respectively. Further analysis revealed five discriminators for grading and cultivars that consistently served as characteristic volatiles for Zisun teas, including 2-furanmethanol, geraniol, hexanal, phenylethyl alcohol, and linalool oxide (pyranoid). Based on the external standard quantification method, the contents of 2-furanmethanol, geraniol and phenylethyl alcohol gradually increased with decreasing tea grade. The content of hexanal was relatively high in the Premium Grade and Grade II (Table S1). Additionally, among the other eight common markers shared across grades and cultivars, five were key contributors to cultivar differentiation, while the other three were critical for grade discrimination. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that all 19 key volatile compounds are closely associated with the characteristic style of Zisun tea and the expression of cultivar-specific attributes. Among them, ten compounds exhibited floral aromatic characteristics. Association between key volatile markers and aroma attributes in Zisun tea

The 19 key volatile compounds identified as contributors to the aroma attributes of Zisun tea are presented in Fig. 6B. In the chord diagram, the width of each band corresponds to the degree of correlation, with wider bands indicating stronger correlations. Indole, cis-jasmone, nonanal, 2-furanmethanol, heptanal, hexanoic acid and benzyl alcohol were found to contribute obviously to the variations in the overall aroma profile, with the major compounds among them exhibiting odor activity values (OAVs) exceeding 1.0 in the tested cultivar samples. Additionally, compounds such as geraniol, hexanal, nonanal and heptanal showed OAVs exceeding 100 in some samples (Table S2). For different aroma attributes, nonanal and heptanal showed positive correlations with green aroma, whereas cis-jasmone, geraniol, hexanoic acid, and benzyl alcohol exhibited significant negative correlations with this attribute. For fruity aroma, indole, cis-jasmone, trans-linalool oxide (furanoid), geraniol, hexanoic acid, and benzyl alcohol were positively correlated, while pentacosane, nonanal, 2-furanmethanol, and heptanal were negatively correlated. (E)-Nerolidol, linalool oxide (pyranoid), and dehydrolinalool showed positive associations with baked leaf aroma. In contrast, tender aroma was negatively correlated with linalool oxide (pyranoid), indole, cis-jasmone, heptanal, hexanoic acid, and benzyl alcohol. Linalool and α-terpineol were positively associated with fragrance intensity, while heptanal showed a strong negative correlation with this attribute.

Discussion

The maturation stage of tea shoots directly influences metabolic pathways of chemical compositions, thereby shaping their flavor profile (Xu et al., 2021). Previous studies have demonstrated that single bud was enriched with LOX (lipoxygenase)-derived fresh, grassy volatiles, one bud and one leaf showed a pronounced accumulation of floral and fruity compounds, while one bud and two leaves featured Maillard- and phenylalanine-derived volatiles (Liu et al., 2025). Another side, tea cultivars serve as the foundation for product flavor formation and is closely linked to geographical environmental conditions, with different production regions featuring distinct predominant varieties (Li et al., 2024; Ma et al., 2019). This study revealed a grade-dependent evolution in aroma profiles, transitioning from tender and fragrance notes toward baked leaf character with decreasing quality grades. Concurrently, the predominant cultivars exhibited distinct aromatic signatures, characterized by pronounced fruity or green notes. The 19 identified key volatile compounds were grouped into four categories based on metabolic pathways, namely lipid metabolites, amino acid metabolites, terpene metabolites, and Maillard reactions.

Lipids are important precursors for the formation of volatile compounds in green tea by oxidation reactions initiated by free radicals, such as autoxidation, photooxidation, and thermal oxidation, and lipoxygenase-mediated lipid oxidation (Ho et al., 2015). The lipid metabolites hexanal, pentacosane, nonanal, heptanal, hexanoic acid were identified as key contributors for distinguishing both cultivars and grades, while cis-Jasmone was associated solely with grade differentiation. In oolong teas, cis-jasmone were contributors for distinguishing grades (Chen et al., 2023). Heptanal was identified to distinguish different grades of oolong tea, which was negatively correlated with tea grade and quality (Zeng et al., 2023). This study similarly revealed a negative correlation between heptanal and tender/ fragrance aroma. In Tieguanyin tea, hexanal was dominant in stem part, while nonanal exclusively contributed to the floral scent of leaf samples (Ming et al., 2025). Nonanal and heptanal showed positive correlations with green aroma, while cis-jasmone and hexanoic acid were positively correlated with fruity aroma. Previous studies have showed that nonanal and heptanal were highlighted as primary contributors to the aroma profiles of fresh scent green tea (Wang et al., 2024). The formation mechanism of hexanoic acid is mediated by airflow in the lipoxygenase–hydroperoxide lyase (LOX-HPL) pathway during turnover processing of oolong tea (Zhou et al., 2021).

Amino acid metabolites indole, phenylethyl alcohol and benzyl alcohol were identified as key discriminators for grading, while phenylethyl alcohol and benzyl alcohol also contributed significantly to cultivar identification. The floral heterocyclic compound indole was reported to serve as a distinctive marker for distinguishing the grades of oolong tea (Chen et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2024). It is synthesized by the shikimate metabolic pathway (Zeng et al., 2016) and regulated by CsMYC2a and JA signaling (Zhou et al., 2020). Benzyl alcohol is produced by cinnamic acid under the catalysis of phenylalanine with related biological enzymes (Yang et al., 2009), while it is be liberated through glycoside hydrolysis (Guo et al., 2023). Phenylethyl alcohol, an aromatic alcohol generated via the thermal degradation of phenylalanine (Tang et al., 2025), has been reported to be closely related to cultivar suitability (He et al., 2022) and to also serve as distinctive marker for characterizing and distinguishing grades of Shuixian tea (Lin et al., 2024).

Key terpene metabolites mainly consist of alcohol compounds characterized by floral note, including linalool and its oxide series, (E)-nerolidol, geraniol and α-terpineol. Previous studies have indicated that these volatile compounds influence the suitability of oolong tea cultivars and play a major role in the formation of the floral aroma (He et al., 2022). In the present study, terpenes linalool, trans-Linalool oxide (furanoid), linalool oxide (pyranoid), (E)-nerolidol, geraniol and α-terpineol represent common constituents across different grades and cultivars of Zisun teas. Among them except (E)-nerolidol, several other compounds together with dehydrolinalool, serve as key contributors to cultivar differentiation, while (E)-nerolidol was identified as key contributors to grading that aligning with previous studies (Wang et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2018; Zeng et al., 2022). Notably, linalool oxide (pyranoid), geraniol and dehydrolinalool were key discriminators for both cultivar and grade samples. The synthesis of these terpenes requires the same precursor, geranyl pyrophosphate, and there may be competition between the methylerythritol phosphate pathway (MEP) and mevalonic acid pathway (MVA). Monoterpenoids linalool, geraniol and α-terpineol are induced and composed by the MEP pathway, while the sesquiterpenes (E)-nerolidol is induced and composed by the MVA pathway (Picazo-Aragones et al., 2020). Linalool oxides are downstream terpene products of the MEP pathway (Zeng, Watanabe and Yang, 2019, Zeng et al., 2021; Zhou et al., 2017). A positive correlation was observed between linalool content and leaf area across different tea cultivars at the same leaf position, which may be explained by more light absorption and less capable of transformation of linalool to linalool oxides (Zeng et al., 2021). While the biosynthesis of linalool from GPP is catalyzed by gene CsLIS, its final content in different cultivars is likely determined by the balance between its synthesis and its conversion through competitive metabolic pathways (Zeng et al., 2021). In Zisun teas, linalool and α-terpineol correlated positively with fragrance intensity, whereas (E)-nerolidol, linalool oxide (pyranoid), and dehydrolinalool were positively associated with the characteristic baked leaf aroma.

Maillard reactions volatiles benzofuran, 2,3-dihydro- (grease flavor, Tian et al., 2020), 2-furanmethanol (bready) and Ethanone, 1-(1H-pyrrol-2-yl)- (nutty) were consistently identified as common constituents across all grading-samples and cultivars-samples of Zisun tea. These volatile compounds are reported to be formed during the high-temperature process (Dong et al., 2015; Tsai et al., 2021; Yang et al., 2002), which were linked to the expression of baked leaf aroma attribute (Fig. 6B).

Conclusions

In summary, the aroma profiles of Zisun tea were analyzed by quantitative description and analysis (QDA) based on five aroma attributes of fragrance, tender, baked leaf aroma, green and fruity. The decrease in tea grades led to an enhancement of baked leaf aroma intensity, alongside a reduction in the intensities of tender and fragrance attributes. The expressions of green and fruity aroma attributes were primarily influenced by different tea cultivars. Volatile profiling detected 103 compounds in four grading-references samples and 110 compounds in six major cultivars samples, respectively. Multivariate statistical analysis identified 19 key volatile compounds, ten of which exhibited floral aromatic characteristics. Among these key contributors, 13 were common across different tea grades and cultivars, with 11 serving as discriminative markers for both classification levels. Notably, hexanal, phenylethyl alcohol, linalool oxide (pyranoid), geraniol, and 2-furanmethanol were shared across grades and cultivars and served as important contributors to the discrimination of both grade and cultivar. Additionally, indole, cis-jasmone, nonanal, 2-furanmethanol, heptanal, hexanoic acid, and benzyl alcohol were found to contribute substantially to the overall aroma profile.

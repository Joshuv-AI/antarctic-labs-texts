On ortho and disjointly compact operators acting to Fréchet spaces
Svetlana Gorokhova 1 1 Southern Mathematical Institute VSC RAS, Vladikavkaz, Russia

Abstract
We study compactness along orthonormal (disjoint bounded) sequences for operators from Hilbert spaces (Banach lattices) to Fréchet spaces.



Keywords:
Hilbert space, Banach lattice, Fréchet space, orthonormal, disjoint

MSC 2020:
46B42, 47B01, 47B02, 47B07

1
Introduction

Several kinds of compactness along orthonormal sequences were studied recently in
[
4
]
for operators from a Hilbert space to a Banach space.
It was followed by the investigation of compactness along bounded disjoint sequences in a Banach lattice by Emelyanov, Erkurşun-Özcan, and the author in
[
5
]
.
In the present paper, some of results of
[
4
,
5
]
are extended to operators whose codomains are Fréchet spaces.

This paper is organized as follows. Section 2 contains necessary preliminaries.
Section 3 is concerned with an othonormal criteria (Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
) of compactness of operators from a Hilbert space to a Fréchet space.
In section 4, we prove Theorem
4.1
that characterizes operators from a Banach lattice to a Fréchet space that are
compact along disjoint bounded sequences.

Throughout the paper: vector spaces are real; operators are linear;
\mathcal{H}
stands for an infinite dimensional Hilbert space;

X
a Banach space;
B_{X}
the closed unit ball of X;
Y=(Y,\tau)
a locally convex space;
{\cal F}=({\cal F},\tau,p)
a Fréchet space, whose
locally convex topology
\tau
is metrizable (in particular,
\tau
is a Hausdorff topology) by a complete metric
p
;

E
a Banach lattice, and
E_{+}
its positive cone. By a
d-bdd
-sequence in
E
we
understand a disjoint bounded sequence. For further unexplained terminology and notation, we refer to
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
6
]
.

2
Preliminaries

It is well known (see, for example,
[
6
]
) that a bounded linear operator between
Hilbert spaces is compact iff it takes orthonormal sequences to norm-null ones.
This was extended in
[
4
]
as follows.

Proposition 2.1
.

(
[
4
, Theorem 2.3]
) An operator
T
from
\mathcal{H}
to
X
is compact iff
T
maps every orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
into a relatively compact set iff

T
maps every orthonormal sequence of
\mathcal{H}
to norm-null one.

By considering an operator from
[
6
, p.292]
, one can see that it is not possible to replace "maps every orthonormal basis"
by "maps some orthonormal basis" in Proposition
2.1
.
The key ingredient of the proof of Theorem 2.3 in
[
4
]
is the following proposition.

Proposition 2.2
.

(
[
4
, Lemma 1.2]
) An operator
T:\mathcal{H}\to X
is bounded iff
T
is bounded along orthonormal sequences.

In order to extend Proposition
2.1
to operators from Hilbert spaces to Fréchet spaces in Theorem
3.1
,
we shall use the following generalization of Proposition
2.2
, which is interesting itself.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
{\cal T}
be a set of operators from
\mathcal{H}
to
Y
. The following conditions are equivalent.

i)

{\cal T}
is topologically bounded, that is the set
{\cal T}B_{\mathcal{H}}=\bigcup\limits_{T\in{\cal T}}T(B_{\mathcal{H}})
is
\tau
-bounded.

ii)

{\cal T}{\cal E}
is topologically bounded for every orthonormal basis
{\cal E}
of
\mathcal{H}
.

iii)

{\cal T}
is topologically bounded along orthonormal sequences, i.e.,
\{Tx_{n}\}_{T\in{\cal T},n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
\tau
-bounded
for every orthonormal sequence
(x_{n})
in
\mathcal{H}
.

iv)

{\cal T}
is topologically bounded along bounded orthonogonal sequences.

If, additionally,
(Y,\tau)
is metrizable, i.e., there exists a separating family
\{p_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\tau
-continuous seminorms on
Y

(in this case
p(x,y)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{-k}p_{k}(x-y)}{1+p_{k}(x-y)}
is a metric that induces the topology
\tau
), then the above conditions are equivalent to

v)

{\cal T}
is equi-
p
-continuous, that is, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that
\sup\limits_{T\in{\cal T}}p(Tx,Ty)\leq\varepsilon

whenever
\|x-y\|\leq\delta
.

Proof.

The implications
i)\Longrightarrow ii)\Longrightarrow iii)
and equivalence
iii)\Longleftrightarrow iv)
are trivial.

iii)\Longrightarrow i)
: Let
{\cal T}
be topologically bounded along orthonormal sequences, yet

{\cal T}
is not topologically bounded. Then there exists a circled convex
\tau
-neighborhood
U
of zero such that
\lambda{\cal T}B_{\mathcal{H}}\not\subseteq U

for every
\lambda>0
. So, there exist a linearly independent normalized sequence
(x_{n})
in
\mathcal{H}
and a sequence
(T_{n})
in
{\cal T}

such that
T_{n}x_{n}\notin 2^{n}U
for all
n
.
By the Gram–Schmidt orthonormalization, pick an orthonormal sequence
(u_{n})
in
\mathcal{H}
such that
x_{n}\in\text{\rm span}\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
for all
n
.
By the assumption, there exists
\alpha>0
satisfying
\alpha Tu_{n}\in U
for all
T\in{\cal T}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

2^{n}U\not\ni T_{n}x_{n}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(x_{n},u_{k})T_{n}u_{k}\in n\alpha^{-1}U\ \ \ \ (\forall n\in\mathbb{N}),

which is absurd. The obtained contradiction completes the proof of the implication.

Now, assume additionally that
\tau
is induced by the metric
p(x,y)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{-k}p_{k}(x-y)}{1+p_{k}(x-y)}
.

i)\Longrightarrow v)
: Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
{\cal T}B_{\mathcal{H}}
be
\tau
-bounded. So, each semi-norm
p_{k}
is bounded on
{\cal T}B_{\mathcal{H}}
, say

\sup\limits_{T\in{\cal T};\|x\|\leq 1}p_{k}(Tx)\leq M_{k}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
(without lost of generality,
M_{k}\leq M_{k+1}
).
Take
n
such that
\frac{1}{2^{n}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
. Then

\sup\limits_{T\in{\cal T};\|x-y\|\leq\delta}p(Tx,Ty)=\sup\limits_{T\in{\cal T};\|x-y\|\leq\delta}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{-k}p_{k}(T(x-y))}{1+p_{k}(T(x-y))}\leq\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{-k}\delta M_{k}}{1+\delta M_{k}}\leq

\frac{1}{2^{n}}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}2^{-k}\delta M_{k}\leq\frac{1}{2^{n}}+\frac{1}{2}\delta M_{n}\leq\varepsilon

for every
\delta
with
0<\delta M_{n}\leq\varepsilon
. Therefore,
{\cal T}
is equi-
p
-continuous.

v)\Longrightarrow i)
: Let
{\cal T}
be equi-
p
-continuous.
Assume in the contrary
{\cal T}B_{\mathcal{H}}
is not
\tau
-bounded. Then, for some
k_{0}
, the semi-norm
p_{k_{0}}
is not bounded on
{\cal T}B_{\mathcal{H}}
.
So, for each
\delta>0
, there exist
x_{0}\in\delta B_{\mathcal{H}}
and
T\in{\cal T}
such that
\frac{p_{k_{0}}(Tx_{0})}{1+p_{k_{0}}(Tx_{0})}\geq\frac{1}{2}
.
Thus,
\sup\limits_{T\in{\cal T};\|x\|\leq\delta}p(Tx,0)\geq{2^{-k_{0}-1}}
, a contradiction.
∎

The following corollary of Theorem
2.1
generalizes
[
4
, Lemma1.2]
.

Corollary 2.1
.

An operator
T
from a Hilbert space to a locally convex space is topologically bounded iff
T
carries orthonormal basises
onto topologically bounded sets iff
T
is topologically bounded along bounded orthonogonal sequences iff
T
is topologically bounded
along othonormal sequences.

We shall use also the next lemma that follows easily from
[
4
, Lemma 1.1]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Each weakly null sequence
(x_{n})
in
\mathcal{H}

contains a subsequence
(x_{n_{k}})
such that
|(x_{n_{k_{1}}},x_{n_{k_{2}}})|\to 0
as
k_{1}\neq k_{2}
and
k_{1},k_{2}\to\infty
.

3
Compactness along orthonormal sequences

We begin with an extension of
[
4
, Theorem 2.2 i)]
to operators from a Hilbert space to a Fréchet space.
Note that the proof of Theorem 2.2 i) in
[
4
]
contains a gap, which is fixed in the proof of Theorem
3.1
below.
In this and in the next section,
({\cal F},\tau,p)
is a Fréchet space such that
p(x,y)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{-k}p_{k}(x-y)}{1+p_{k}(x-y)}
for
x,y\in{\cal F}
,
where the topology
\tau
is induced by the family
\{p_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of semi-norms on
{\cal F}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
{\cal T}
be a set of operators from
\mathcal{H}
to
({\cal F},\tau,p)
. The following conditions are equivalent.

i)

{\cal T}B_{\mathcal{H}}
is a relatively compact subset of
{\cal F}
.

ii)

{\cal T}{\cal E}
is relatively compact for every orthonormal basis
{\cal E}
of
\mathcal{H}
.

iii)

{\cal T}
is compact along orthonormal sequences, that is the set
\{Tx_{n}:T\in{\cal T},n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is relatively compact
for every orthonormal sequence
(x_{n})
in
\mathcal{H}
.

iv)

{\cal T}
is compact along bounded orthogonal sequences.

Proof.

The implications
i)\Longrightarrow ii)\Longrightarrow iii)
and the equivalence
iii)\Longleftrightarrow iv)
are trivial.

iii)\Longrightarrow i)
: Assume in the contrary
{\cal T}B_{\mathcal{H}}
is not relatively
\tau
-compact.
Then, for some
\alpha>0
, there exist a sequence
(T_{n})
in
{\cal T}
and a sequence
(y_{n})
in

B_{\mathcal{H}}
with
p(T_{n}y_{n},T_{m}y_{m})\geq 3\alpha
for all
m\neq n
.
Since
B_{\mathcal{H}}
is relatively weakly compact, by passing to a subsequence, we may assume
y_{n}\convw y
.
Observe that the set
{\cal T}y
is relatively compact by assumption
iii)
.
So, by passing to a subsequence, we may assume
p(T_{n}y,u)\to 0
for some
u\in{\cal F}
.
By Theorem
2.1
,
{\cal T}
is equi-
p
-continuous. So, for large enough
n
and
m
,
n\neq m
, we have

p(T_{n}(y_{n}-y),T_{m}(y_{m}-y))=p(T_{n}y_{n}-T_{n}y,T_{m}y_{m}-T_{m}y)\geq

p(T_{n}y_{n}-u,T_{m}y_{m}-u)-\alpha=p(T_{n}y_{n},T_{m}y_{m})-\alpha\geq 3\alpha-\alpha=2\alpha.

Without lost of generality, we may assume
p(T_{n}z_{n},T_{m}z_{m})\geq 2\alpha
for all
m\neq n
, where
z_{n}:=y_{n}-y\convw 0
.
Due to Lemma
2.1
, by passing to a subsequence, we may assume
|(z_{n},z_{m})|\to 0
as
n\neq m
and
n,m\to\infty
.
By the weak lower semicontinuity of the norm (cf.
[
1
, Lemma 6.22]
),

2\geq\|z_{n}\|=\|y_{n}-y\|\geq C
for all
n\geq n_{0}
and some
C>0
.
By passing to a further subsequence, we may assume
\|z_{n}\|\to\beta\in[C,2]
.
By the Gram–Schmidt orthogonalization, there exists an orthogonal sequence
(x_{n_{i}})

in
\mathcal{H}
with
\|x_{n_{i}}-z_{n_{i}}\|\to 0
as
i\to\infty
. In particular,
\|x_{n_{i}}\|\to\beta
.
As
{\cal T}
is equi-
p
-continuous, we may assume
\|x_{n_{i}}\|=\beta
for all
i
and
p(T_{n_{i}}x_{n_{i}},T_{n_{j}}x_{n_{j}})\geq\alpha
for all
i\neq j
.

Letting
w_{i}=\frac{x_{n_{i}}}{\beta}
, we have, for
i\neq j
,

p(T_{n_{i}}w_{i},T_{n_{j}}w_{j})=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{-k}\beta^{-1}p_{k}(T_{n_{i}}x_{n_{i}}-T_{n_{j}}x_{n_{j}})}{1+\beta^{-1}p_{k}(T_{n_{i}}x_{n_{i}}-T_{n_{j}}x_{n_{j}})}\geq

\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{-k}\beta^{-1}p_{k}(T_{n_{i}}x_{n_{i}}-T_{n_{j}}x_{n_{j}})}{(\beta+\beta^{-1})+(\beta+\beta^{-1})p_{k}(T_{n_{i}}x_{n_{i}}-T_{n_{j}}x_{n_{j}})}=

\frac{\beta^{-1}}{\beta+\beta^{-1}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{-k}p_{k}(T_{n_{i}}x_{n_{i}}-T_{n_{j}}x_{n_{j}})}{1+p_{k}(T_{n_{i}}x_{n_{i}}-T_{n_{j}}x_{n_{j}})}=\frac{\beta^{-1}}{\beta+\beta^{-1}}p(T_{n_{i}}x_{n_{i}},T_{n_{j}}x_{n_{j}})\geq\frac{\alpha\beta^{-1}}{\beta+\beta^{-1}}>0.

Thus, the set
\{Tw_{i}:T\in{\cal T},i\in\mathbb{N}\}
is not relatively compact, and hence
{\cal T}
is not compact along orthonormal sequences. A contradiction.
∎

Now, we are ready to extend
[
4
, Theorem 2.3]
to operators from Hilbert spaces to Fréchet spaces.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
T:\mathcal{H}\to({\cal F},\tau,p)
. The following are equivalent.

i)

T
is compact in the sense that
T(B_{\mathcal{H}})
is a relatively compact subset of
{\cal F}
.

ii)

T
maps every orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
into a relatively compact subset of
{\cal F}
.

iii)

T
maps every orthonormal sequence of
\mathcal{H}
to a
p
-null sequence.

iv)

T
maps every bounded orthogonal sequence of
\mathcal{H}
to a
p
-null sequence.

Proof.

The equivalence
i)\Longleftrightarrow ii)
is a special case of Theorem
3.1
, and the equivalence
iii)\Longleftrightarrow iv)
is trivial.

ii)\Longrightarrow iii)
: By Theorem
2.1
,
T
is
p
-continuous (and hence
\tau
-continuous).
Let
(u_{n})
be an orthonormal sequence of
\mathcal{H}
.
On the way to a contradiction, suppose
p(Tu_{n},0)\not\to 0
.
By passing to a subsequence, we may suppose
p(Tu_{n},0)\geq M
for all
n
and some
M>0
.
Extending
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
, and involving
ii)
,
we see
\{Tu_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is relatively compact in
{\cal F}
, and hence
(Tu_{n})
has a
p
-convergent subsequence,
say
\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}p(Tu_{n_{j}},y)=0
for some
y\in{\cal F}
.
As
(u_{n_{j}})
is orthonormal,
u_{n_{j}}\convw 0
. Since
T
is
\tau
-continuous,
Tu_{n_{j}}\xrightarrow{\sigma({\cal F},{\cal F}^{\prime})}0
.
It follows from
Tu_{n_{j}}\xrightarrow{\tau}y
that
Tu_{n_{j}}\xrightarrow{\sigma({\cal F},{\cal F}^{\prime})}y
.
Therefore,
y=0
, and hence
\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}p(Tu_{n_{j}},0)=0
, contradicting
p(Tu_{n},0)\geq M>0
for all
n
.

The implication
iii)\Longrightarrow ii)
is trivial.
∎

Note that Proposition
2.1
follows directly from Theorem
3.2
.
It is worth noting that the statements of Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
are also valid for operators
from a Hilbert space to a locally convex space whose topology is metrizable.

4
Compactness along disjoint bounded sequences

In compare with Lemma
2.1
, the case of operators acting from Banach lattices to locally convex spaces is more complicated.
We do not know reasonable (certainly, the domain must be infinite-dimensional) conditions for automatic topological boundedness
of an operator
T:E\to X
in terms of
\tau
-boundedness of
T
along
d-bdd
-sequences in
E
.
Recently, an example of a discontinuous linear functional on
\ell^{p}
which is bounded along
d-bdd
-sequences
was constructed by Storozhuk
[
7
]
. Futhermore, the lattice-version of Theorem
3.1
if failed for compactness along
d-bdd
-sequences,
even for bounded operators from a Banach lattice to a Banach space
[
5
, Example 1]
.

Definition 4.1
.

(cf.,
[
5
, Definition 4]
)
A topologically bounded operator from a Banach lattice to a locally convex space is
d
-compact
whenever it carries
d-bdd
-sequences to relatively compact sets.

Examples like
[
5
, Example 1]
motivate the question: for which
E
and
X
there is a
d
-compact operator

T:E\to X
that is not compact? The compactness along
d-bdd
-sequences is equivalent to several kind of compactness along disjoint sets.

Proposition 4.1
.

(
[
5
, Proposition 2]
) Let
T:E\to X
be bounded. The following are equivalent.

i)

T
is
d
-compact.

ii)

T
compact along
d-bdd
-sequences of
E_{+}
.

iii)

T(D)
is relatively compact for every disjoint normalized subset
D
of
B_{E}
.

iv)

T(D)
is relatively compact for every disjoint normalized subset
D
of
E_{+}
.

We are going to extend Proposition
4.1
for operators from Banach lattices to Fréchet spaces as follows.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
{\cal T}
be a set of topologically bounded operators from
E
to
({\cal F},\tau,p)
. The following conditions are equivalent.

i)

{\cal T}D
is relatively compact in
{\cal F}
for every disjoint bounded subset
D
of
E
.

ii)

{\cal T}D
is relatively compact in
{\cal F}
for every disjoint normalized
D\subseteq E_{+}
.

iii)

For every
d-bdd
-sequence
(x_{n})
in
E
, the set
\{Tx_{n}:T\in{\cal T},n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is relatively compact in
{\cal F}
.

iv)

For every
d-bdd
-sequence
(x_{n})
in
E_{+}
, the set
\{Tx_{n}:T\in{\cal T},n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is relatively compact in
{\cal F}
.

Proof.

Without lost of generality, assume
\dim(E)=\infty
.
Implications
i)\Longrightarrow ii)
and
i)\Longrightarrow iii)\Longrightarrow iv)
are trivial. So, it suffices to prove
ii)\Longrightarrow i)
and
iv)\Longrightarrow ii)
.

ii)\Longrightarrow i)
: Let
D
be a disjoint subset of
E
. Without lost of generality,
D\subseteq B_{E}
. Denote

D_{1}=\{x_{+}:x\in D\ \&\ x_{+}\neq 0\}
and
D_{2}=\{x_{-}:x\in D\ \&\ x_{-}\neq 0\}
.
If one them is empty, say
D_{2}=\emptyset
, then

{\cal T}D\subseteq{\cal T}D_{1}\cup\{0\}\subseteq\text{\rm cch}\bigl(\bigl\{T(\|x\|^{-1}x)\bigl\}_{T\in{\cal T};x\in D_{1}}\bigl),

[formula]

where
\text{\rm cch}(S)
is the circled convex hull of
S
. The set
\bigl\{T(\|x\|^{-1}x)\bigl\}_{T\in{\cal T};x\in D_{1}}
is relatively compact in
{\cal F}
due to
ii)
,
and hence
{\cal T}D
is relatively compact by (1).

If
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are both nonempty then
D\subseteq D_{1}-D_{2}
, and hence

{\cal T}D\subseteq\text{\rm cch}\bigl(\bigl\{T(\|x\|^{-1}x)\bigl\}_{T\in{\cal T};x\in D_{1}}\bigl)+\text{\rm cch}\bigl(\bigl\{T(\|x\|^{-1}x)\bigl\}_{T\in{\cal T};x\in D_{2}}\bigl).

[formula]

It follows from (2) that
{\cal T}D
is relatively compact in
{\cal F}
.

iv)\Longrightarrow ii)
: Let
(y_{n})
be a sequence in
{\cal T}D
, where
D
is a disjoint normalized subset of
E_{+}
.
If
y_{n}=0
for infinitely many
n
then
(y_{n})
has a norm-null subsequence.
If
y_{n}=0
only for finitely many
n
, by removing them, we may suppose all
y_{n}\neq 0
. For each
n
, pick
x_{n}\in D
and
T_{n}\in{\cal T}

with
y_{n}=T_{n}x_{n}
and denote
D_{0}=\bigl\{x_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\bigl\}
.
Then
D_{0}
is a disjoint normalized countable (maybe even finite) subset of
E_{+}
.
By
iv)
,
{\cal T}D_{0}
is relatively compact. Since the sequence

(y_{n})=(T_{n}x_{n})
lies in
{\cal T}D_{0}
, the sequence
(y_{n})
contains a
p
-convergent subsequence, and hence
{\cal T}D
is relatively compact in
{\cal F}
.
∎

An important case of Definition
4.1
occurs (see
[
5
, Subsection 2.3]
) when a locally convex space in the codomain
is a Banach space with its weak topology. The corresponding operators are called
d
-weakly compact accordingly to
[
5
]
.
For a
d
-weakly compact operator that is not weakly compact, we refer to
[
5
, Example 2]
.

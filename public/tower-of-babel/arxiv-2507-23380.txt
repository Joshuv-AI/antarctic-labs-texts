Quantitative homogenisation for differential equations with highly anisotropic partially degenerating coefficients
Shane Cooper and Ilia Kamotski

Abstract
We consider a non-uniformly elliptic second-order differential operator with periodic coefficients that models composite media consisting of highly anisotropic cylindrical fibres periodically distributed in an isotropic background. The degree of anisotropy is related to the period of the coefficients via a ‘critical’ high-contrast scaling. In particular, ellipticity is lost in certain directions as the period, ε \varepsilon , tends to zero. Our primary interest is in the asymptotic behaviour of the resolvent of this operator in the limit of small ε \varepsilon . Two-scale resolvent convergence results were established for such operators in Cherednichenko, Smyshlyaev and Zhikov (Proceedings of The Royal Society of Edinburgh:Seciton A Mathematics. 136 (1), 87–114(2006)). In this work, we provide an asymptotic description of the resolvent and establish operator-type error estimates. Our approach adopts the general scheme of Cooper, Kamotski and Smyshlyaev (preprint available at https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13151). However, we face new challenges such as a directional dependence on the loss of ellipticity in addition to a key ‘spectral gap’ assumption of the above article only holding in a weaker sense. This results in an additional ‘interfacial’ boundary layer analysis in the vicinity of each fibre to arrive at order- ε \varepsilon operator-type error estimates.

Introduction

In this work, we analyse the asymptotic behaviour of the solution
u_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
to the problem

\left.\begin{aligned} &-\mathrm{div}\big(A_{\varepsilon}(\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon})\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)\big)+u_{\varepsilon}(x)=f(x)\ \text{for $f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})$,}\\
&\ A_{\varepsilon}(y)=\chi_{1}(y)I+\chi_{0}(y)\mathrm{diag}\big(\varepsilon^{2},\varepsilon^{2},a(y_{3})\big),\end{aligned}\quad\right\}

(1)

for small (period) parameter
0<\varepsilon<1
. Here,
\square:=\big[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\big)^{3}
is the periodic reference cell of
A_{\varepsilon}
,
\chi_{1}=1-\chi_{0}
and
\chi_{0}
is the
\square
-periodic extension of the
characteristic function of the cylindrical set
F:=B\times\mathbb{R}
where
B
is the disk of radius
r<1/2
centred at the origin. The
1
-periodic coefficient
a
satisfies the usual well-posedness assumptions, i.e. there exists a positive constant
\nu>0
such that

v<a(s)<v^{-1},\qquad\forall s\in[0,1).

Such equations are one possible model for highly anisotropic periodic composite media with partial degeneracies,
VPS
.

It was shown in
ChSmZh
that the solution
u_{\varepsilon}
to (
1
) two-scale converges, as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0

u(x)+v(x,y^{\prime})
the solution of the two-scale homogenised limit problem

\left.\begin{aligned} &-\mathrm{div}A^{\mathrm{h}}\nabla u-a^{\mathrm{h}}\partial_{x_{3}}^{2}\big(|B|u+\langle v\rangle\big)+u+\langle v\rangle=f,&\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
&-\Delta_{y^{\prime}}v-a^{\mathrm{h}}\partial_{x_{3}}^{2}\left(u+v\right)+u+v=f,&\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},y^{\prime}\in B,\\
&\ v(x,y^{\prime})=0,&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},y^{\prime}\in\partial{B},\end{aligned}\quad\right\}

(2)

where
\langle v\rangle(x):=\int_{B}v(x,y^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}
and
A^{\mathrm{h}}
and
a^{\mathrm{h}}
are given constant coefficients (defined below).
Note that the two-scale limit problem is defined on a subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\square)
that is larger than the space on which the original problem is posed. As such, one has convergence in the two-scale topology rather than the standard topology, cf. the seminal works
Ng

In our recent work
CoKaSm
we provide a general methodology to upgrade such two-scale convergence results to operator-type error estimates in the space on which the original problem is posed.
We begin by the standard procedure of quantitative homogenisation for periodic operators in the whole space
to arrive at a family of equivalent problems on the unit cell
\square
. Namely, we sequentially apply, to
u_{\varepsilon}
, the unitary rescaling
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}f(x)=\varepsilon^{3/2}f(\varepsilon x)
, then the unitary Gelfand transform
U:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\rightarrow L^{2}(\square^{*}\times\square)
, for
\square^{*}=[-\pi,\pi)^{3}
the dual cell and
U
being the continuous extension of the mapping

Uf(\theta,y)=(2\pi)^{-3/2}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}f(y+m)e^{-\mathrm{i}\theta\cdot(y+m)}.

Then
u_{\varepsilon,\theta}(y):=\big(U\Gamma_{\varepsilon}u\big)(\theta,y)
solves

-\big(\mathrm{div}+\mathrm{i}\theta\cdot\big)\varepsilon^{-2}A_{\varepsilon}(\nabla+\mathrm{i}\theta)u_{\varepsilon,\theta}+u_{\varepsilon,\theta}=f_{\varepsilon,\theta}\quad y\in\square,\theta\in\square^{*},\quad

where
f_{\varepsilon,\theta}(y):=\big(U\Gamma_{\varepsilon}f\big)(\theta,y)\in L^{2}(\square^{*}\times\square)
. Following
CoKaSm
, we rewrite this problem as: for each
0<\varepsilon<1
and
\theta\in\square^{*}
,

\left.\begin{aligned} &\text{Find $u_{\varepsilon,\theta}\in H:=H^{1}_{per}(\square)$ the solution to}\\
&\varepsilon^{-2}a_{\theta}(u_{\varepsilon,\theta},\tilde{u})+b_{\theta}(u_{\varepsilon,\theta},\tilde{u})=c(f_{\varepsilon,\theta},\tilde{u}),\quad\forall\tilde{u}\in H\end{aligned}\quad\right\},

(3)

for families of non-negative sesquilinear forms

a_{\theta},b_{\theta}:H\times H\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and
c:L^{2}(\square)\times L^{2}(\square)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
given by the quadratic forms

\displaystyle a_{\theta}(u,u)=:a_{\theta}[u]=\int_{\square\backslash F}|(\nabla+\mathrm{i}\theta)u(y)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y+\int_{F}a(y_{3})|

\displaystyle(\partial_{3}+\mathrm{i}\theta_{3})u(y)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y;

(4)

b_{\theta}(u,u)=:b_{\theta}[u]=\int_{F}|(\nabla^{\prime}+\mathrm{i}\theta^{\prime})u|^{2}+\int_{\square}|u|^{2},\quad\nabla^{\prime}=(\partial_{1},\partial_{2}),\ \theta^{\prime}=(\theta_{1},\theta_{2}),

(5)

c[u]=\int_{\square}|u|^{2}.

Notice that,
a_{\theta}+b_{\theta}
are a (uniformly equivalent) family of inner products on

3

The goal now, is to provide leading-order asymptotics for solutions to the family of variational problems (
3
) that are uniform in
\theta
,
\varepsilon
and the right-hand-side
f_{\varepsilon,\theta}
. Then one can invert the transforms

\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
applied above to find uniform estimates for the problem (
1
). In
CoKaSm
, such asymptotics were provided for variational families of the form (
3
) with non-negative forms
a_{\theta}
that are (point-wise) coercive in
H
(after projecting away from the kernel of
a_{\theta}
). Amongst other things, this ensured that first order correction of
u_{\varepsilon,\theta}
lived in
H
which is crucial to the analysis in
CoKaSm
. Another feature of
CoKaSm
is that the coercivity constant degenerates quadratically in
\theta

Here, the form
a_{\theta}
(cf. (
4
)) is clearly not coercive (up to the kernel) in
H^{1}_{per}(\square)
. Indeed, consider

V=\{v\in H^{1}_{per}(\square):\text{$v$ constant in $\square\backslash F$ and $v$ independent of $y_{3}$ in $F$}\}.

(6)

Clearly,
a_{\theta}
is zero on
V
when
\theta_{3}=0
. Now, denote the closed subset
W

H^{1}_{per}(\square)
that is the orthogonal complement of
V

a_{0}+b_{0}
(which is an equivalent

a_{\theta}
is positive on
W

a_{\theta}

4

5
)). However, one has the following weaker coercivity estimate: there exists a positive constant
\gamma
such that

\displaystyle a_{\theta}[w]

\displaystyle\geq\gamma c[w],\quad\forall w\in W,\ \forall\theta\in\square^{*}.

Overcoming this absence of full coercivity is one of the main challenges of this current work as it leads to new analysis not present in
CoKaSm
, in particular, we need to investigate internal boundary-layer effects near each fibre.

Another difficultly is related to the directional dependence of the elliptic degeneracy, which is a feature in various highly anisotropic high-contrast composite media (see for example
Co
), and the failure of the quadratic degeneracy assumption of
CoKaSm
. Loosely speaking, in the present context, this assumption would be satisfied if one could demonstrate that
a_{\theta}[u]
is bounded from below by
|\theta|^{2}c[u]
for all

. This is clearly not the case. However, for (
4
), one has the following ‘directionally-dependent’ quadratic condition: there exists a positive constant
\gamma^{*}
such that

a_{\theta}[u]\geq\gamma^{*}\Big(|\theta_{3}|^{2}c[\chi_{0}u]+|\theta|^{2}c[\chi_{1}u]\Big),\quad\forall u\in H^{1}_{per}(\square),\forall\theta\in\square^{*}.

Such a weaking the quadratic gap assumption of
CoKaSm
present significant challenges. These challenges are too significant to achieve results on the same level of generality as in our previous work
CoKaSm
. However, we were able to succeed for problem (
1
) by exploiting problem-specific features.

2
Results

Our first result concerns uniform asymptotics of (
3
) with operator-norm error estimates.
Upon representing
V
, cf. (
6
), as the direct sum
V=Z_{0}\dot{+}Z_{1}
, where

\displaystyle Z_{0}:=\mathbb{C},\ Z_{1}:=\{v\in H^{1}_{per}(\square)\,:\,\text{$v=0$ in $\square\backslash F$ and $v$ independent of $y_{3}$ in $F$}\},

we introduce the following problem on
V
:

\left.\begin{aligned} &\text{Find $(z_{0},z_{1})\in Z_{0}\times Z_{1}$ the solution to}\\
&\varepsilon^{-2}A^{\mathrm{h}}\theta\cdot\theta\,z_{0}\overline{\tilde{z}_{0}}+a^{\mathrm{h}}\varepsilon^{-2}|\theta_{3}|^{2}\big(z_{0}+z_{1},\tilde{z}_{0}+\tilde{z}_{1}\big)_{L^{2}(B)}\\
&\hskip 58.64937pt+b_{0}(z_{0}+z_{1},\tilde{z}_{0}+\tilde{z}_{1})=c(\mathcal{E}_{\theta}f_{\varepsilon,\theta},\tilde{z}_{0}+\tilde{z}_{1})\quad\forall\tilde{z}_{0}\in Z_{0},\tilde{z}_{1}\in Z_{1}\end{aligned}\quad\right\}.

Here,
\mathcal{E}_{\theta}:L^{2}(\square)\rightarrow L^{2}(\square)
is the unitary operator given by multiplication by the function
\exp(\mathrm{i}\theta^{\prime}\cdot y^{\prime})
,
a^{\mathrm{h}}:=\left(\int_{0}^{1}a^{-1}\right)^{-1}
is the one-dimensional homogenised matrix and
A^{\mathrm{h}}
is the constant, symmetric
3\times 3
perforated-fibre homogenised matrix given, in
ChSmZh
, as

A^{\mathrm{h}}:=|\square\backslash F|e_{3}\otimes e_{3}+\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{2}e_{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}\int_{\square^{\prime}\backslash B}(\partial_{\beta}N_{\alpha}+\delta_{\alpha\beta}),

for
N_{\alpha}:H^{1}_{per}(\square^{\prime}\backslash B)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
\square^{\prime}:=\big[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\big)^{2}
,
\alpha=1,2
, that solve the ‘cell problem’

\left.\begin{aligned} &\int_{\square^{\prime}\backslash B}(\nabla^{\prime}N_{\alpha}+e_{\alpha})\nabla^{\prime}\phi=0,\quad\forall\phi\in H^{1}_{per}(\square^{\prime}\backslash B)\\
&\int_{\square^{\prime}\backslash B}N_{\alpha}=0\end{aligned}\quad\right\}.

Here
e_{k}
,
k=1,2,3
, are the canonical Euclidean basis vectors.

Our first main result is the following uniform estimate:

\|u_{\varepsilon,\theta}-\mathcal{E}_{\theta}^{*}(z_{0}+z_{1})\|_{L^{2}(\square)}\lesssim\varepsilon\|f_{\varepsilon,\theta}\|_{L^{2}(\square)},

which can be rewritten in operator-theoretic language as follows:

Theorem 2.1

There exists a positive constant
C
such that the inequality

\|(\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\theta}+I)^{-1}-\mathcal{E}_{\theta}^{*}(\mathbb{L}_{\theta/\varepsilon}+I)^{-1}P_{0}\mathcal{E}_{\theta}\|_{L^{2}(\square)\rightarrow L^{2}(\square)}\leq C\varepsilon

holds for all
0<\varepsilon<1
and
\theta\in\square^{*}
.

The operators in the above theorem are the following: for each
\varepsilon
and
\theta
,
(\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\theta}+I)
is the unbounded self-adjoint operator in
L^{2}(\square)
generated by problem (
3
); now
denote
\mathcal{H}_{0}
to be the closure of
V
in
L^{2}(\square)
and
P_{0}:L^{2}(\square)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{0}
the orthogonal projection; then, finally, for each
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
(\mathbb{L}_{\xi}+I)
is the unbounded self-adjoint operator in
\mathcal{H}_{0}
generated by the inner product

\mathbb{S}_{\xi}(z_{0}+z_{1},\tilde{z}_{0}+\tilde{z}_{1}):=A^{\mathrm{h}}\xi\cdot\xi z_{0}\overline{\tilde{z}_{0}}+a^{\mathrm{h}}|\xi_{3}|^{2}\big(z_{0}+z_{1},\tilde{z}_{0}+\tilde{z}_{1}\big)_{L^{2}(B)}+b_{0}(z_{0}+z_{1},\tilde{z}_{0}+\tilde{z_{1}}).

Each of the non-negative operators
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\theta}
and
\mathbb{L}_{\xi}
have compact resolvents. Consequently, Theorem
2.1
readily implies uniform approximations for the associated eigenvalues. Indeed, for
\{\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{(k)}(\theta)\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
and
\{\Lambda^{(k)}(\xi)\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
denoting the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\theta}
and
\mathbb{L}_{\xi}
respectively, labelled in increasing order and repeated according to multiplicity, one has the following result:

Theorem 2.2

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a constant
C_{k}>0
such that

\left|\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{(k)}(\theta)-\Lambda^{(k)}\left(\tfrac{\theta}{\varepsilon}\right)\right|\leq C_{k}\varepsilon,\quad\forall\theta\in\square^{*},\,\forall\,0<\varepsilon<1.

Theorem
2.1
enables one to approximate the solution of the original problem (
1
) in terms of the two-scale homogenized limit problem (
2
). To this end, let us define the Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathcal{H}_{0}),

\mathcal{P}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\square)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
the orthogonal projection, and define the dense subspace

\mathcal{V}:=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\dot{+}\mathcal{Z}_{1},\quad\mathcal{Z}_{1}:=\{v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3},Z_{1})\,:\,\partial_{x_{3}}v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\square)\},

of
\mathcal{H}
. Finally, let
\mathcal{L}_{0}+I
be the unbounded self-adjoint operator defined by the closed quadratic form

\displaystyle Q[u+v]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(A^{\mathrm{h}}\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla u}+\ a^{\mathrm{h}}\|\partial_{x_{3}}(u+v)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B)}+b_{0}[u+v]\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x,

with form domain
\mathcal{V}
.

To provide error estimates in
\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
between the resolvents of
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
and
\mathcal{L}_{0}
, one needs to address the fact that the operators are defined on different function spaces. We overcome this issue via a ‘two-scale interpolation’ operator
J_{\varepsilon}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\square)
, introduced in
CoKaSm
(see
Well
for a similar object) given by

J_{\varepsilon}:=\Gamma_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\chi\mathcal{E}_{\theta}^{*}U\Gamma_{\varepsilon}.

Here,
\chi:L^{2}(\square^{*}\times\square)\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\square)
extends functions by zero outside of
\square^{*}
;
\mathcal{F}
is the Fourier transform acting on the first variable (and
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
,
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}^{-1}
are understood to act on the first variable). It was established in
CoKaSm
that
J_{\varepsilon}
is almost-unitary in the following sense:

J_{\varepsilon}^{*}J_{\varepsilon}=I,\quad\text{and}\quad J_{\varepsilon}J^{*}_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow I\ \text{strongly as $\varepsilon\rightarrow 0$.}

Then, the following result holds:

Theorem 2.3

There exists a constant
K>0
such that the inequality

\|(\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}+I)^{-1}-J_{\varepsilon}^{*}(\mathcal{L}_{0}+I)^{-1}\mathcal{P}J_{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq K\varepsilon,

holds for all
0<\varepsilon<1
.

Our results imply comparisons between the spectral properties of
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
and
\mathcal{L}_{0}
. For example, upon noting the fibre decomposition
\big(\mathcal{F}\mathcal{L}_{0}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big)(\xi)=\mathbb{L}_{\xi}
, one sees that Theorem
2.2
provides uniform estimates between the spectral band functions of the operators
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
and
\mathcal{L}_{0}
.

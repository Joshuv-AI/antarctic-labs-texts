Real-world treatment landscape and clinical outcomes in first-line advanced or metastatic gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma in the United States
Authors: Jin Gu, Shivani K Mhatre, Daniel O Koralek, Ravindra Gupta, Jaffer A Ajani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundContemporary real-world evidence in advanced gastric, gastroesophageal junction, or esophageal adenocarcinoma (GC/GEJC/EAC) remains limited. This study evaluated real-world treatment patterns and clinical outcomes in patients with advanced GC/GEJC/EAC treated with first-line therapy in the United States.Patients and methodsTreatment patterns were assessed in adults from the Flatiron database with advanced or metastatic HER2-negative GC/GEJC/EAC who initiated first-line treatment between May 1, 2021, and July 31, 2025 (overall cohort). Clinical outcomes were assessed using the Kaplan–Meier method in a subcohort with ≥6 months of follow‑up.ResultsAmong 2340 eligible patients, the most common first-line regimen was IO+FOLFOX/CAPOX (31.6%). This regimen was used in 47.6% of patients with programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1) combined positive score (CPS) ≥ 1 expression. Starting in 2024, zolbetuximab+FOLFOX/CAPOX was administered more frequently in patients with CPS <1 vs ≥1 (6.1% vs 1.9%) and CPS <5 vs ≥5 (4.6% vs 0.8%) expression. In the subcohort (n = 2117), IO+FOLFOX/CAPOX vs FOLFOX/CAPOX was associated with significantly longer median (months; hazard ratio [95% CI]) real-world overall survival (12.3 vs 11.3; 0.81 [0.69-0.94]), time to next treatment or death (8.0 vs 6.1; 0.64 [0.55-0.75]), and time to treatment discontinuation (6.0 vs 3.0; 0.54 [0.47-0.63]). Differences in outcomes were driven by patients with CPS ≥1 expression, with no differences in patients with CPS <1 expression.ConclusionsIO+FOLFOX/CAPOX was the most frequent first-line regimen in advanced or metastatic GC/GEJC/EAC and showed improved real-world outcomes vs FOLFOX/CAPOX. Despite these gains, overall survival remains poor, underscoring unmet needs and the importance of biomarker-informed treatment selection.Keywords: real-world, advanced or metastatic HER2-negative gastroesophageal, adenocarcinoma, first-line, treatment patterns, clinical outcomes

Following the approval of immuno-oncology-based treatments, combining immunotherapy with standard chemotherapy (FOLFOX or CAPOX) has become the most common first-line treatment for advanced or metastatic HER2-negative gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma, including in patients with PD-L1-positive expression. In the real world, this combination has improved clinical outcomes compared with chemotherapy alone. These findings support the use of immuno-oncology-based treatments in eligible patients, particularly those with PD-L1-positive expression, and underscore the importance of biomarker testing to identify patients likely to benefit from these treatments.

Introduction

Globally, gastroesophageal cancers are among the top 10 leading causes of cancer-related death,1 with 5-year survival rates of 5%-8% in advanced or metastatic disease.2,3 The median age at diagnosis for gastroesophageal cancers is ∼68 years, and approximately 60%-70% of cases are diagnosed at the advanced or metastatic stage.2,3

The therapeutic landscape and clinical guidelines for the first-line treatment of advanced or metastatic gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma have evolved since the approval of immuno-oncology (IO)-based regimens, biologics, and targeted therapies starting in 2021. Due to limited benefit with IO-based regimens among patients with programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1)-negative expression, the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) recently restricted the approval of pembrolizumab-based and nivolumab-based combination therapies to patients with PD-L1 (combined positive score [CPS] expression ≥1); tislelizumab is also approved in patients with PD-L1 ≥ 1 (CPS or tumor area positivity) expression.4–6 Consistent with these regulatory changes, National Comprehensive Cancer Network Clinical Practice Guidelines in Oncology (NCCN Guidelines®) recommend fluoropyrimidine- and platinum-based combination regimens as the backbone of first-line treatment for unresectable locally advanced, recurrent, or metastatic human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma, with the addition of nivolumab, pembrolizumab, or tislelizumab as preferred options for patients with PD-L1 CPS ≥1 expression.7,8 In addition, zolbetuximab in combination with fluoropyrimidine-based chemotherapy and oxaliplatin is a National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN®)-preferred regimen for patients with HER2-negative, claudin 18.2-positive tumors, independent of PD-L1 expression.7,8

Several real-world studies have shown improvements in clinical outcomes for patients with advanced or metastatic gastric, gastroesophageal junction, or esophageal adenocarcinoma (GC/GEJC/EAC) following the approval of IO-based regimens, or when directly comparing IO plus chemotherapy vs chemotherapy alone.9,10 However, critical real-world evidence gaps remain. Current US real-world evidence reflects practice patterns before or without stratification by recent CPS-based label restrictions.11,12 Furthermore, existing real-world studies often do not fully assess CPS and claudin 18.2-testing rates,11,13 limiting the evaluation of biomarker-directed first-line therapy and downstream treatment sequencing. Prior real-world comparisons of IO plus chemotherapy vs chemotherapy alone do not reflect contemporary practice that incorporates PD-L1 and claudin 18.2 status.11 In addition, to our knowledge there are no US real-world studies evaluating treatment patterns or outcomes following the approval of zolbetuximab in patients positive for claudin 18.2 expression, leaving evidence gaps related to the integration of this therapy into first-line pathways and the effects on subsequent therapy lines.

Understanding treatment patterns and clinical outcomes in patients with advanced or metastatic gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma receiving first-line therapy can inform future trial designs and provide insights into real-world outcomes following newly approved treatment approaches. This study evaluated demographics, treatment patterns and sequencing, and clinical outcomes in patients with advanced or metastatic, HER2-negative gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma treated with first-line therapies in the United States. By focusing on the contemporaneous post‑IO era and capturing biomarker‑informed care, we sought to address the evidence gaps in current real-world evidence.

Methods

Data source and patient selection

In this retrospective, observational study, data were collected from the Flatiron Advanced Gastric/Esophageal Cancer Enhanced Datamart longitudinal electronic health record database. The Flatiron database comprises patient-level structured and unstructured data, curated via technology-enabled abstraction, and includes data on patient demographics, diagnosis, treatment, and outcomes. This study, which used certified de-identified data, does not constitute human subjects research under the Common Rule and thus did not require IRB oversight or patient informed consent.

Adult patients with histologically confirmed, advanced or metastatic GC/GEJC/EAC who initiated first-line treatment between May 1, 2021, and July 31, 2025, were included; the index date was the date of first-line treatment initiation. Patients were excluded if they had any primary cancer diagnosis other than GC/GEJC/EAC within 5 years before the index date, had a known HER2-positive tumor or received trastuzumab-containing regimens at any line of therapy, or received clinical trial drugs at any time during the study period. Patients were also excluded if they initiated first-line treatment >360 days after initial diagnosis of advanced or metastatic GC/GEJC/EAC, as their treatment was likely not well captured within the Flatiron network since patients with advanced cancer typically begin treatment soon after diagnosis.

Study variables and outcomes

Baseline characteristics, including age, sex, race, and disease stage at initial diagnosis, were recorded for all patients who met inclusion criteria. Treatment patterns were assessed starting from advanced or metastatic GC/GEJC/EAC diagnosis date until the last activity date, end of data availability, death, or the end of third-line treatment, whichever occurred first. Treatment patterns were assessed in the overall cohort, comprising patients who initiated first-line treatment between May 1, 2021, and July 31, 2025. Within the overall cohort, a subcohort of patients who initiated first-line treatment between May 1, 2021, and January 31, 2025, was evaluated to allow ≥6 months of follow-up for analysis of the following clinical outcomes: real-world overall survival (rwOS), real-world time to next treatment or death (rwTTNTD), and real-world time to treatment discontinuation (rwTTD). rwOS was defined as the time from the index date until death from any cause; patients still alive at the end of the study period or lost to follow-up were censored at the last known date alive. To mitigate potential incompleteness in mortality information, a composite mortality variable that integrated multiple data sources—including administrative death records, publicly available data, and information derived from electronic health records—was used.14 rwTTNTD was defined as the time from the index date until the start of the next line of therapy or death, whichever occurred first. Patients still receiving first-line treatment, alive at the end of the study period, or lost to follow-up were censored at the last known date alive. rwTTD was defined as the time from first-line treatment initiation to discontinuation, with discontinuation defined as having a subsequent systemic therapy after the initial regimen, a gap of >120 days with no systemic therapy after the last administration, or death ≤120 days while receiving the index regimen. Patients without treatment discontinuation of first-line therapy were censored at the end of follow-up.

First-line treatments

First-line treatments administered to patients in this study included the following: IO agents (pembrolizumab, nivolumab, and tislelizumab), chemotherapy (platinum agents [cisplatin, carboplatin, and oxaliplatin]; taxanes [paclitaxel and docetaxel]; fluoropyrimidines [capecitabine and fluorouracil]; and other chemotherapy agents [irinotecan, epirubicin, leucovorin, trifluridine/tipiracil, etoposide, and mitomycin]), and biologics/targeted therapies (ramucirumab, zolbetuximab, larotrectinib, and entrectinib).

Statistical analyses

Baseline demographics and patient characteristics were summarized using descriptive statistics. Medians (interquartile range) were reported for continuous data, and counts and percentages were provided for categorical variables. Clinical outcomes of rwOS, rwTTNTD, and rwTTD were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method and compared between patients who received IO plus either folinic acid, fluorouracil, and oxaliplatin (FOLFOX) or capecitabine and oxaliplatin (CAPOX) vs those who received FOLFOX/CAPOX. The median event times and corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were reported. A multivariate Cox proportional hazards model was used to evaluate the association between first-line treatment and rwOS, rwTTNTD, and rwTTD, adjusting for the following variables (first category listed for each variable is the referent): age categories (<65 and ≥65 years), region (South, Northeast, West, Midwest, other, and missing), treatment setting (community and academic), insurance type at index (commercial, Medicare, Medicaid, other, mixed, and missing), disease stage (stage I-III, stage IV, and unknown), Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (0-1, ≥2, and missing), disease location (EAC and GC/GEJC), and race (White, Asian, Black, other, and missing). Separate models were fitted for patients with PD‑L1 CPS ≥1 and ≥5 expression. Cox proportional hazard ratios (HRs) and the corresponding 95% CIs were estimated. A P value of <.05 was considered statistically significant in all models. The proportional hazards assumption was assessed using Schoenfeld residuals.

Results

Overall cohort

Overall, 17 695 patients with advanced or metastatic GC/GEJC/EC were identified in the Flatiron database during the study period, of which 14 566 patients had confirmed GC/GEJC/EC of adenocarcinoma histology type (GC/GEJC/EAC). Biomarker testing patterns

From 2021 to 2025, the rate of testing for PD-L1 using CPS reported at first-line treatment initiation remained stable at approximately 50%-60% across community and academic settings, with a slight increase starting in 2024 (Figure S1). In contrast, the rate of testing for claudin 18.2 (positive result defined as ≥75% viable tumor cells with moderate-to-intense membranous staining) rose substantially, from 7.1% in 2023 to 32.2% in 2025 (Figure S1).

Treatment patterns

In the overall cohort, the most frequent first-line treatment regimen was IO plus FOLFOX/CAPOX (31.6% [including nivolumab plus FOLFOX/CAPOX, 27.4% and pembrolizumab plus FOLFOX/CAPOX, 4.0%]), followed by FOLFOX/CAPOX (22.7%). Among patients who received first-line IO plus FOLFOX/CAPOX, 33.7% received second-line therapy, most commonly chemotherapy plus targeted therapy. In total, 19.5% of patients with either no CPS testing or unknown CPS status were treated with IO plus FOLFOX/CAPOX.

Treatment sequences in patients in the overall cohort with CPS ≥1 expression are shown in Figure 2. The most frequent first-line treatment regimen in patients with CPS ≥1 expression was IO plus FOLFOX/CAPOX (47.6%), followed by FOLFOX/CAPOX (19.1%). Among patients with CPS ≥1 expression who received first-line IO plus FOLFOX/CAPOX, 33.0% received second-line therapy; among those who received first-line FOLFOX/CAPOX, 46.7% received second-line therapy.

In patients with CPS ≥5 expression, the treatment pathway for the first 3 lines of therapy was similar to that of patients with CPS ≥1 expression (Figure S2). The most frequent first-line treatment regimen in patients with CPS ≥5 expression was IO plus FOLFOX/CAPOX (52.2%; Figure S2).

Starting in 2024, zolbetuximab plus FOLFOX/CAPOX has been used more frequently in patients with CPS <1 vs ≥1 (6.1% vs 1.9%) and CPS <5 vs ≥5 (4.6% vs 0.8%) expression (Figure S3). Zolbetuximab plus FOLFOX/CAPOX use was similar between patients with CPS ≥1 and those with ≥5 expression, although conclusions are limited by small sample sizes.

The median time from diagnosis of advanced/metastatic disease to first-line treatment initiation of IO plus FOLFOX/CAPOX or FOLFOX/CAPOX was similar across PD-L1 subgroups in the overall cohort (1.1-1.4 months); the median duration of first-line treatment ranged from 1.6 to 5.1 months.

Subcohort

Clinical outcomes were investigated in a subcohort of patients (n = 2117) who received first-line treatment between May 1, 2021, and January 31, 2025, to allow for ≥6 months of potential follow-up after the index date. Baseline demographics and clinical characteristics were generally similar between patients treated with first-line IO plus FOLFOX/CAPOX and those treated with FOLFOX/CAPOX, with some exceptions (Table 1). Patients treated with first-line IO plus FOLFOX/CAPOX vs FOLFOX/CAPOX were more likely to have stage IV disease at the initial diagnosis (87.7% vs 75.9% in patients with known disease stage) and less likely to have gastric cancer as the primary disease site (39.3% vs 46.7%). Among patients with known CPS scores, a greater percentage of patients had CPS ≥1 vs <1 expression in both the first-line IO plus FOLFOX/CAPOX (86.1% vs 13.9%) and the FOLFOX/CAPOX (61.7% vs 38.3%) treatment groups. Baseline characteristics stratified by CPS subgroup are shown in Table S1.

Real-world outcomes

Real-world OS was significantly longer in patients receiving first-line IO plus FOLFOX/CAPOX than in those receiving FOLFOX/CAPOX alone (median, 12.3 vs 11.3 months; HR 0.81 [95% CI 0.69-0.94]; P = .0073; Table 2). Similarly, rwTTNTD was significantly prolonged with first-line IO plus FOLFOX/CAPOX vs FOLFOX/CAPOX (median, 8.0 vs 6.1 months; HR 0.64 [95% CI 0.55-0.75]; P < .0001). Real-world TTD was also significantly longer with first-line IO plus FOLFOX/CAPOX vs FOLFOX/CAPOX (median, 6.0 vs 3.0 months; HR 0.54 [95% CI 0.47-0.63]; P < .0001). Significant benefits in rwOS, rwTTD, and rwTTNTD were also observed with first-line IO plus FOLFOX/CAPOX vs FOLFOX/CAPOX in patients with CPS ≥1 and ≥5 expression (Figure 3 and Figure S4). Across these clinical outcomes, there was no significant benefit observed with first-line IO plus FOLFOX/CAPOX vs FOLFOX/CAPOX in patients with CPS <1 expression. Discussion

This study provides valuable insights into real-world treatment patterns, biomarker testing uptake, and clinical outcomes in patients with HER2-negative advanced or metastatic gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma who received first-line treatment after the approval of IO-based regimens in this setting. Baseline demographics and clinical characteristics of the overall cohort were similar to those reported in previous real-world studies of patients with advanced or metastatic GC/GEJC/EAC.15–17 The treatment patterns observed in the overall cohort reflect an increased use of IO starting in 2021 following the approval of nivolumab- and pembrolizumab-based regimens for the treatment of advanced or metastatic gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma, aligning with and expanding on findings from recent real-world studies. Before 2021, the most frequent first-line regimen was fluoropyrimidine- and/or platinum doublet-based combination.15 In contrast, IO-based therapy was the most frequently administered first-line therapy after April 2021.16,17 Among Medicare beneficiaries with metastatic GC/GEJC who initiated first-line, non-HER2-targeted systemic therapy between April 2021 and February 2024, 44.6% were treated with IO-based regimens, most commonly nivolumab plus FOLFOX (28.1%).17 In addition, a retrospective study using data from the ConcertAI Patient360 database reported that 39.1% of patients who initiated treatment between April 2021 and October 2022 received first-line IO-based regimens, most commonly nivolumab plus chemotherapy (29.2%).16

In September 2024, the FDA Oncologic Drugs Advisory Committee (ODAC) voted that the risk-benefit profile of anti–PD-1 antibodies is not favorable for patients with PD-L1 expression <1.18–20 The NCCN Guidelines® include the addition of certain IO regimens to fluoropyrimidine plus platinum-based therapy for the first-line treatment of HER2-negative advanced or metastatic GC/GEJC/EAC in patients with PD-L1 CPS ≥1 expression.7,8 In the current study, IO plus FOLFOX/CAPOX was the most frequent first-line treatment regimen overall and among patients with CPS ≥1 expression, whereas FOLFOX/CAPOX was most used in patients with CPS <1 expression. However, no clear shift in IO uptake after September 2024 was observed in patients with CPS ≥1 expression. This finding reflects established preferential use of IO-based regimens in patients with PD-L1–positive expression before the ODAC vote.

In addition, zolbetuximab plus FOLFOX/CAPOX was administered more often in patients with PD-L1–low (CPS <5) and PD-L1–negative (CPS <1) expression, providing valuable insights into the real-world use of this agent since its approval in October 2024.21 NCCN Guidelines recommend zolbetuximab in combination with fluoropyrimidine-based chemotherapy and oxaliplatin for HER2-negative, claudin 18.2−positive, unresectable locally advanced, recurrent or metastatic gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma, without restrictions based on PD-L1 status.7,8 The guideline approach is consistent with the design of the pivotal zolbetuximab clinical trials in this disease setting, which did not require PD-L1 positivity as an eligibility criterion and, therefore, enrolled patients with either PD-L1-positive or PD-L1-negative expression.22,23 Findings from the current analysis show HCPs support zolbetuximab as an option for patients with CPS <1 expression who may not be candidates for first-line IO-based therapy.

Approximately 49% of patients in the overall cohort had unknown CPS status or were not tested for PD-L1 expression at first-line treatment initiation. In these cases, PD-L1 data may have been unavailable in the Flatiron dataset, or PD-L1 testing may have been deemed unnecessary in patients whose treatment plan had already been selected. Of the patients with untested/unknown PD-L1 expression, 19.5% were treated with IO+FOLFOX/CAPOX. Similarly, in the retrospective ConcertAI Patient360 study, 20.0% of patients without evidence of PD-L1 testing received nivolumab plus chemotherapy, whereas 70.0% received chemotherapy alone.16 Patients with unknown or untested CPS status may have received IO-based regimens for several reasons. For instance, the majority of patients in the current analysis were treated before the FDA restricted the use of IO-based regimens based on PD-L1 expression. In addition, in real-world clinical settings, there may be challenges with obtaining definitive PD-L1 testing results in a timely manner, leading to the initiation of therapy without confirmation of PD-L1 expression level.24

In the overall cohort, 33.7% of patients who received first-line IO+FOLFOX/CAPOX subsequently received second-line therapy, highlighting the importance of optimizing first-line treatment with the most effective available options. The most common second-line regimens were chemotherapy plus targeted therapy (30.7%) and IO plus FOLFOX/CAPOX (18.3%). In a previous analysis, the most common second-line treatment before and after 2021 were ramucirumab in combination with paclitaxel (23.0%) and nivolumab plus FOLFOX (22.3%), respectively.9 These findings suggest a shift toward IO-based regimens in second-line treatment since 2021.

In this analysis, treatment with IO plus FOLFOX/CAPOX was associated with improved clinical outcomes compared with FOLFOX/CAPOX in the subcohort of patients who initiated first-line treatment between May 1, 2021, and January 31, 2025, consistent with findings from pivotal phase 3 IO trials in comparable settings.25,26 In CHECKMATE-649, the median overall survival (OS) was 13.7 months with nivolumab plus chemotherapy vs 11.6 months with chemotherapy alone in the overall population after 3 years of follow-up (HR 0.78 [95% CI 0.70-0.87]).26 In KEYNOTE-859, the median OS was 12.9 months with pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy vs 11.5 months with chemotherapy alone in the intent-to-treat population (HR 0.78 [95% CI 0.70-0.87]; P < .0001).25

CPS-stratified analyses demonstrated no clinically meaningful benefit in rwOS, rwTTNTD, or rwTTD among patients with CPS <1 expression. Clinical benefit was primarily observed in patients with CPS ≥1 expression, with the most pronounced effect in those with CPS ≥5 expression. These results indicate that PD-L1 expression is an important determinant of clinical benefit from IO-based treatment. These findings align with FDA recommendations of IO-based therapies in patients with CPS ≥1, underscoring the use of treatments in patients most likely to benefit. In clinical trials, the magnitude of improvement in OS has been shown to be more pronounced in patients with higher PD-L1 expression,25,27,28 consistent with the current analysis.

Results of previous real-world studies also showed survival improvements in patients with advanced or metastatic GC/GEJC/EAC following the approval of IO-based regimens. In a real-world study of patients who received first-line treatment between April 2021 to December 2022, nivolumab plus chemotherapy demonstrated superior median OS vs chemotherapy alone (12.6 vs 10.0 months; P = .047).10 In another study by Shah et al.,9 the median OS in patients following first-line treatment was 9.5 months vs 12.3 months before and after 2021, respectively. The median OS among Medicare beneficiaries who received first-line systemic therapy from April 2021 to February 2024 was 10.8 months,17 comparable with the median rwOS in patients who received first-line IO plus FOLFOX/CAPOX (12.3 months) or FOLFOX/CAPOX (11.3 months) in the current analysis. However, the current study investigated clinical outcomes in the context of the most recent treatment landscape, including the approval of novel IO therapies (eg, tislelizumab) and targeted therapies (eg, zolbetuximab) that may have impacted outcomes, the testing of new biomarkers (eg, claudin 18.2), and FDA guidance related to PD-L1 expression restrictions for IO-based regimens. In addition, previous real-world studies did not investigate rwTTNTD or rwTTD, which have been found to be meaningful outcomes to assess treatment effectiveness and are often used as proxies for progression-free survival in real-world oncology settings.29,30

Strengths of this study include a large patient cohort and a design that allowed for clinical outcome evaluation in a subcohort with ≥6 months follow-up. This study documents current treatment landscape patterns in advanced or metastatic gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma, incorporating recent first-line treatment approvals and biomarker testing as they entered routine clinical practice in the United States. By providing a timely and comprehensive evaluation of real-world outcomes, this study reflects shifts in treatment patterns with the approval of additional agents. Despite the addition of new IO-based treatment options in recent years, clinical outcomes remain suboptimal; thus, there remains an unmet need for more effective first-line treatment options in this disease setting.

This study had limitations, which are inherent in real-world analyses. Data entry errors at point of care, including information bias or measurement errors, could not be detected or corrected during analysis. The line of therapy was not directly captured, but rather was derived from electronic health records. As the intent behind treatment decisions is often unrecorded in clinical settings, rigid definitions of line of therapy in the real world may result in an inaccurate portrayal of how patients are actually treated. For example, a patient who may appear to start first-line chemotherapy followed by second-line IO plus chemotherapy may in fact have had a planned delayed IO start within the first-line setting while awaiting biomarker testing results. Patient medical history may be incomplete if treatment was received at a nonparticipating center or if biomarker testing was conducted outside the Flatiron network.

Conclusion

This real-world study shows that IO plus FOLFOX/CAPOX became the predominant first-line regimen in the United States following approval of IO-based therapies in 2021, with higher CPS expression associated with greater use of IO-containing treatment. Zolbetuximab plus FOLFOX/CAPOX was administered more often in patients with CPS <1 or <5 vs ≥1 or ≥5 expression. Real-world OS, rwTTNTD, and rwTTD were significantly longer with IO plus FOLFOX/CAPOX vs FOLFOX/CAPOX for all patients in the subcohort as well as those patients with CPS ≥1 and ≥5 within the subcohort, similar to efficacy outcomes observed in clinical trials in this disease setting. Despite these gains, outcomes remain poor in advanced or metastatic gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma, underscoring the need for more effective first-line options and reinforcing the importance of biomarker-guided treatment selection, including PD-L1 and claudin 18.2 testing.

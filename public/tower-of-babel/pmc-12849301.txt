Obacunone alleviated the development of polycystic ovary syndrome via inhibiting STAT3 phosphorylation
Authors: Lu Guan, Haicui Wu, Yuan Li, Yi Yu, Shan Xiang, Fang Lian
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundObacunone (OB) possesses multiple pharmacological properties, including anti-inflammatory, anti-oxidant, anti-hyperglycemic, and anti-tumor activities. However, whether OB can ameliorate polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) remains unclear. This study aimed to investigate the influence and potential mechanism of OB on PCOS.MethodsPCOS mouse and cell models were established using dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA). First, we evaluated the influence of OB on PCOS in mice by using hematoxylin and eosin staining of ovarian tissue, estrous cycle detection and biochemical analysis. Next, the functions of OB on apoptosis, oxidative stress, as well as inflammation in DHEA-treated KGN cells and DHEA-caused PCOS mice were further investigated utilizing western blot, quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction, commercial test kits, flow cytometry and 2’,7’-dichlorodihydrofluorescein diacetate staining. Moreover, the potential mechanism of OB in PCOS was explored using network pharmacology, western blot, immunofluorescence and rescue experiments using Colivelin TFA, a signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) activator.ResultsOB treatment ameliorated ovarian function and corrected reproductive endocrine disorders in PCOS mice. Moreover, OB reduced apoptosis, oxidative stress as well as inflammation in both PCOS mouse and cell models, and inhibited STAT3 phosphorylation. Additionally, Colivelin TFA reversed the effects of OB on apoptosis, oxidative stress, and inflammatory responses in DHEA-treated KGN cells.ConclusionOur findings demonstrated that OB alleviated the symptoms of PCOS via mitigating inflammation, apoptosis, and oxidative stress through the inhibition of STAT3 phosphorylation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13048-025-01934-6.

Introduction

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), a prevalent reproductive endocrine disorder, can affect about 10% of women of child-bearing age [1]. Menstrual irregularities, hirsutism, obesity, androgen excess and infertility are the main clinical manifestations of PCOS, often exerting a negative impact on mental health and interpersonal relationship [2–4]. In addition, PCOS is strongly related to adverse complications including metabolic syndrome, cardiovascular disease, and diabetes [5]. Addressing these complex clinical complications requires an in-depth understanding of the pathogenesis of PCOS and the identification of novel therapeutic targets. To date, the United States Food and Drug Administration has not approved any specific pharmacologic therapy for PCOS [6]. Therefore, greater attention should be directed toward identifying new and effective treatment options.

Emerging evidence has shown that Janus kinase/signal transducer and activator of the transcription (JAK/STAT) signaling plays a crucial role in the pathogenesis of PCOS [7, 8]. Chronic inflammatory responses and oxidative stress are confirmed to be involved in PCOS pathogenesis [9, 10], and the JAK/STAT pathway has been shown to modulate both processes [11, 12]. Thus, the JAK/STAT signaling pathway may be a potential target for PCOS treatment. Many natural products have been used in preclinical trials indicating the ability of repressing signalings involved in inflammation, whose mechanism includes the decrease of signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) phosphorylation [13–15]. Obacunone (OB) is a natural triterpenoid limonoid compound widely found in Rutaceae plants, such as Phellodendron [16]. In recent years, OB is demonstrated to have multifarious pharmacological activities, such as anti-inflammatory, anti-hyperglycemic, anti-oxidant, as well as anti-tumor activities [17–20]. Bai et al. [21] have reported that OB could attenuate liver fibrosis via enhancing anti-oxidant effects. Gao et al. [17] have demonstrated that OB potently decreases pro-inflammatory mediators. However, the potential pharmacological effects of OB in PCOS remain unclear.

Consequently, this research aimed to evaluate the influence of OB on PCOS, and to elucidate the underlying molecular mechanisms. Our findings suggested that OB might ameliorate apoptosis, inflammatory responses and oxidative stress in PCOS through inhibiting STAT3 phosphorylation. These results provide a foundation for further exploration of OB as a potential therapeutic agent for PCOS.

Materials and methods

PCOS mouse models

Prepubertal female BALB/c mice (21-day old) were provided by GemPharmatech (Jiangsu, China), and maintained in an specific pathogen free (SPF) facility under standard conditions (22–24 °C and 65 ± 5% relative humidity) with unlimited access to food and water. The animals were randomly assigned into 5 groups: Control, PCOS, PCOS + OB (25 mg/kg, Yuanye, Shanghai, China), PCOS + OB (50 mg/kg), and PCOS + OB (100 mg/kg). To establish the PCOS model, mice received subcutaneous injections of dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA, 6 mg/100 g body weight, dissolved in sesame oil, Sigma-Aldrich, USA) once daily for 21 consecutive days [22, 23]. Successful model establishment was confirmed by alterations in the estrous cycle, hormonal profiles, and ovarian histology. Mice in Control group were administered sesame oil alone. After three weeks of modeling, OB was administered intragastrically once daily for 28 consecutive days at doses of 25, 50, or 100 mg/kg [24, 25]. The mice in control and PCOS groups received equivalent volumes of normal saline for the same duration. Following four weeks of OB treatment, the ovary tissues of mice were harvested for subsequent analyses.

Vaginal smear

Vaginal smear of each mice was collected daily at 9:00 a.m. from day 15 after the initiation of OB administration and continued for 14 days. Estrous cycle stages were assessed as per the predominant cell types by an optical microscope [26].

Measurement of serum biochemical and hormone levels

After a 12-hour fast, blood samples that collected from tail vein were utilized to examine fasting blood glucose (FBG) using a Glucose Assay kit (Beyotime, Jiangsu, China). The homeostasis model assessment of insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) was calculated as follows: HOMA-IR = [FBG × fasting insulin (FINS)]/22.5. At sacrifice, blood was collected from the abdominal aorta. After centrifuging, the level of FINS and the level of serum sex hormones [follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), luteinizing hormone (LH), testosterone and estradiol (E2)] were detected utilizing mouse enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits, which were provided by Elabscience (Wuhan, China), according to the specifications for each kit, respectively.

Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining

Ovarian tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde solution for 48 h, followed by dehydration and paraffin embedding according to standard protocols. After that, the paraffin blocks were sectioned into 5 μm slices. Sections were deparaffinized and rehydrated, followed by staining with H&E. Finally, ovarian morphology was analyzed under a light microscope. Follicular phases (primary follicles, secondary follicles, and atretic follicles) and the corpora lutea were identified.

Evaluation of oxidative stress

The levels of glutathione (GSH), superoxide dismutase (SOD), malondialdehyde (MDA) and catalase (CAT) in ovarian tissues and in the supernatants from KGN cell culture medium were examined using commercially available assay kits (Solarbio, Beijing, China) following the manufacturer’s protocols.

RNA extraction and quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was extracted from ovarian tissues using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA). Purified RNA samples were quantified utilizing the Nanodrop 2000 Spectrophotometer (Thermo, USA). Next, RNA sample (1 µg) was subjected to reverse transcription using HiScript IV 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Vazyme, Nanjing, China) to generate total cDNA. Using NCBI Primer-BLAST, the mRNA sequences of the target gene and the internal reference gene (GAPDH) were designed. Subsequently, all primers were synthesized by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China). qRT-PCR analysis was performed using SupRealQ Purple Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix (U+) (Vazyme) on an ABI 7900HT Real-Time PCR System. The PCR reaction system included 10 µL of SupRealQ Purple Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix (U+), 0.4 µL of forward primer (10 µM), 0.4 µL of reverse primer (10 µM), 2 µL of cDNA template and 7.2 µL of ddH2O. Each experimental group contains 6 independent biological samples. Three duplicate wells were set for each cDNA sample on the PCR plate. qRT-PCR results got analyzed to obtain the Ct value of the amplified product, and the data were analyzed by the 2-ΔΔCt method. Western blot

To extract total protein, ovarian tissues or KGN cells (human ovarian granulosa cell line) were homogenized employing RIPA buffer (Elabscience, Wuhan, China). Subsequently, protein concentration was determined with a BCA Protein Colorimetric Assay Kit (Elabscience). After that, 50 µg of protein per sample was separated on 12% SDS-polyacrylamide gel, and subsequently electrophoretically transferred onto PVDF membranes. Next, the membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with the following primary antibodies: cleaved caspase-3 (1:500, #9661, Cell Signaling Technology, USA), cleaved caspase-9 (1:1000, MA5-53678, Thermo Fisher, USA), Bad (1:1000, #9239, Cell Signaling Technology), Bcl-xl (1:1000, #2764, Cell Signaling Technology), IL-10 (1:1000, #12163, Cell Signaling Technology), IL-18 (1:1000, #67775, Cell Signaling Technology), TNF-α (1:500, ab183218, Abcam, UK), IL-1β (1:2000, 26048-1-AP, Proteintech, Wuhan, China), Bcl-2 (1:500, ab194583, Abcam), Bax (1:1000, #2772, Cell Signaling Technology), IL-6 (1:2000, 21865-1-AP, Proteintech), p-STAT3 (1:500, ab32143, Abcam) and STAT3 (1:1000, 10253-2-AP, Proteintech). GAPDH (1:2000, 10494-1-AP, Proteintech) served as an endogenous control. After washing, the membrane was incubated with secondary antibody. Finally, the blots were visualized using an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) detection system.

PCOS cell modeling

KGN cells obtained from Cobioer (Nanjing, China) were maintained in DMEM/F12 medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum and 100 units/mL of penicillin and streptomycin under 5% CO2 at 37 °C. KGN cells were treated with 500 nM DHEA for 24 h, followed by removing the medium and replacing medium supplemented with OB (20, 40 and 80 µM) or Colivelin TFA (a STAT3 activator, 0.5 µM, MedChemExpress, USA) for two days [27–29]. Colivelin TFA was prepared in a storage solution with PBS.

CCK-8 assay

Cell viability was evaluated employing cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) assay (Beyotime, Jiangsu, China). KGN cells from each treatment group were inoculated into a 96-well plate and cultured for one day. Next, 10 µL of CCK-8 solution was added to KGN cells and incubated for 120 min. In the end, absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader.

Flow cytometry

Apoptotic cell percentages were determined by flow cytometry using the Annexin V-FITC Apoptosis Detection Kit (Beyotime, Jiangsu, China). After different treatments, KGN cells were resuspended in 100 µL of Annexin binding buffer after centrifugation, followed by the addition of 5 µL Annexin V-FITC and 5 µL propidium (PI) in darkness. Then, flow cytometry was performed to determine cell apoptosis.

Reactive oxygen species (ROS) detection

Total intracellular ROS levels were evaluated using 2’,7’-dichlorodihydrofluorescein diacetate (DCFH-DA) staining (Beyotime, Jiangsu, China). After different treatments, DCFH-DA solution (10 µmol/L) was added to KGN cells. Following the incubation for half an hour, KGN cells were washed three times to remove DCFH-DA that has not entered cells. In the end, images were observed by a fluorescence microscope.

Immunofluorescence

KGN cells were washed with PBS and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min at room temperature, followed by additional PBS washes. Subsequently, KGN cells were blocked with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA) in PBS for 1 h, and then incubated overnight at 4℃ with p-STAT3 antibody (1:300, ab32143, Abcam) diluted in blocking buffer. After three washes with PBS, the cells were incubated with a secondary antibody diluted in blocking buffer for 60 min. Subsequently, nuclei were counterstained with 4’,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI) for 5 min in the dark. Fluorescence images were obtained using a fluorescence microscope.

Statistical analyses

All experimental results are expressed as means ± SD. Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism software (version 8.0; GraphPad Software, USA). A one-way ANOVA with Dunnett’s Multiple Comparison test was used for determining statistical significance. Statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05.

Results

OB treatment ameliorated ovarian function and reproductive endocrine disorder in PCOS mice

The chemical structure of OB was presented in Fig. 1A. To investigate the function of OB, PCOS mouse models were established using DHEA. H&E staining showed that corpus luteum number was significantly decreased while cystic follicles number was increased in PCOS group when compared with those in Control group, which were attenuated after OB administration (Fig. 1B). Considering that the normal estrous cycle is a crucial indicator of healthy ovarian function, vaginal smear test was performed to assess estrous cycle stages in PCOS mice (Fig. 1C). Mice in PCOS group exhibited disordered estrous cycles (Fig. 1D), which were restored after OB treatment (Fig. 1D). In addition, the level of LH and testosterone, and LH/FSH ratio were elevated, whereas E2 level was reduced in PCOS group relative to the Control group (Fig. 2A). Their abnormal levels were attenuated by the treatment of OB (Fig. 2A). No significant differences in FSH levels were observed among the five groups (Fig. 2A). Moreover, the increased of FBG and FINS, as well as HOMA-IR in PCOS mice was significantly attenuated after OB administration (Fig. 2B). All these results indicated that OB treatment could ameliorate ovarian dysfunction and reproductive endocrine disorders in PCOS mice.

OB treatment improved apoptosis, inflammatory responses and oxidative stress in PCOS mice

Cleaved caspase-3, cleaved caspase-9, Bad and Bax expression was upregulated, while Bcl-xl and Bcl-2 level was downregulated in PCOS group in comparison to Control group (Fig. 3A). These abnormalities were reversed following OB treatment (Fig. 3A). Furthermore, the upregulated mRNA level of TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β and IL-18, and the downregulated mRNA level of IL-10 in ovarian tissues of PCOS mice were reversed after the administration of OB (Fig. 3B). Besides, the upregulated level of MDA, and the downregulated level of GSH, SOD and CAT in PCOS mice was also reversed by OB treatment (Fig. 3C).

OB treatment improved apoptosis, oxidative stress and inflammatory responses in DHEA-treated KGN cells

The CCK-8 assay results showed that KGN cell viability was markedly decreased in DHEA group relative to Control group (Fig. 4A). This decrease in cell viability was reversed by OB administration (Fig. 4A). The apoptosis of KGN cells was notably elevated in PCOS group when compared with Control group; however, it was reduced following OB treatment (Fig. 4B). Subsequently, cleaved caspase-3, cleaved caspase-9, Bad and Bax expression was increased, while Bcl-xl and Bcl-2 level was decreased in DHEA group in comparison to Control group; these changes were reversed after OB treatment (Fig. 4C). We next evaluated the effect of OB on oxidative stress in DHEA-treated KGN cells. As Fig. 5A and B manifested, the upregulated level of ROS and MDA, as well as the downregulated level of GSH, SOD and CAT in DHEA-treated KGN cells were restored by OB administration. In addition, the upregulated IL-18, TNF-α, IL-6 and IL-1β level, and the downregulated IL-10 level in DHEA-treated KGN cells were attenuated after the administration of OB (Fig. 5C).

OB treatment inhibited STAT3 phosphorylation in PCOS mouse and cell models

The 133 targets of OB were collected through Super-PRED database. And the 346 PCOS-related targets were collected from the GeneCards database. Subsequently, we used the VENNY 2.1.0 online tool to create the Venn map of drug targets-disease targets, and obtained 12 intersection genes (Fig. 6A). To further predict the correlation between the targets of OB and PCOS, we constructed a PPI network using STRING database, and discovered that eight of them had interaction relationships (Fig. 6B). Next, we performed a molecular docking to assess binding between OB and STAT3 (Fig. 6C). We discovered that OB had a favorable binding affinity for STAT3 (affinity = -9.27 Kcal/mol) (Fig. 6C). Thus, the roles of OB on p-STAT3 and STAT3 expression were further investigated in PCOS mouse and cell models. The results of western blot and immunofluorescence manifested the phosphorylation level of STAT3 was significantly increased in DHEA-treated KGN cells, but was reduced following OB treatment (Fig. 6D and E). Similarly, STAT3 phosphorylation level was significantly elevated in the ovarian tissues of PCOS mice and were markedly reversed after OB administration (Fig. 6F). To sum up, OB treatment could inhibit STAT3 phosphorylation in both the PCOS mouse and cell models.

Colivelin TFA reversed the effects of OB on apoptosis, inflammatory response and oxidative stress in DHEA-treated KGN cells

As shown in Fig. 7A, the phosphorylation level of STAT3 was significantly decreased in DHEA + OB (80 µM) group relative to DHEA group, while its phosphorylation level was increased in DHEA + Colivelin TFA group. Moreover, the phosphorylation level of STAT3 in DHEA + OB (80 µM) + Colivelin TFA group was markedly increased compared with DHEA + OB (80 µM) group, and notably decreased compared with DHEA + Colivelin TFA group (Fig. 7A). The results of flow cytometry manifested that when compared with DHEA group, the apoptosis of KGN cells was notably decreased in DHEA + OB (80 µM) group, but increased in DHEA + Colivelin TFA group (Fig. 7B). The apoptosis in DHEA + OB (80 µM) + Colivelin TFA group was markedly elevated relative to DHEA + OB (80 µM) group, and notably reduced relative to DHEA + Colivelin TFA group (Fig. 7B). Furthermore, when compared with DHEA group, TNF-α, IL-6 and IL-1β expression was notably downregulated in DHEA + OB (80 µM) group, but upregulated in DHEA + Colivelin TFA group (Fig. 7C). Meanwhile, their expression in DHEA + OB (80 µM) + Colivelin TFA group was markedly elevated relative to DHEA + OB (80 µM) group, and notably reduced relative to DHEA + Colivelin TFA group (Fig. 7C). Besides, the results of Fig. 7D and E manifested that when compared with DHEA group, the level of ROS and MDA was notably reduced, and the level of GSH, SOD and CAT was elevated in DHEA + OB (80 µM) group. The level of ROS and MDA was notably increased, and the level of GSH, SOD and CAT was decreased in DHEA + Colivelin TFA group relative to DHEA group (Fig. 7D and E). When compared with DHEA + OB (80 µM) group, the level of ROS and MDA was notably increased, and the level of GSH, SOD and CAT was decreased in DHEA + OB (80 µM) + Colivelin TFA group (Fig. 7D and E). However, when compared with DHEA + Colivelin TFA group, the level of ROS and MDA was notably decreased, and the level of GSH, SOD and CAT was increased in DHEA + OB (80 µM) + Colivelin TFA group (Fig. 7D and E).

Discussion

Recently, the incidence of PCOS has increased, affecting the physical and psychological health of women of child-bearing age and even teenage girls [30]. Although PCOS has been widely studied, its pathogenic mechanisms remain unclear. Traditional Chinese medicine has long been used to treat various metabolic, and inflammatory diseases, including PCOS. OB possesses multiple pharmacological properties, including anti-inflammatory, anti-oxidant, anti-hyperglycemic and anti-tumor activities [17–20]. In this study, we investigated the roles of OB in PCOS mice, and discovered that OB treatment could ameliorate ovarian function and estrous cycle disorders.

The main pathological features of PCOS include hormonal imbalance, insulin resistance (IR) and chronic inflammation [31]. Yong et al. [32] have revealed that hormonal imbalance is a major cause and key factor in PCOS development. Therefore, correcting hormonal imbalance is crucial for PCOS treatment. Our results demonstrated that elevated LH and testosterone, and upregulated LH/FSH ratio, as well as reduced E2 levels in PCOS mice, were significantly improved after OB treatment. Moreover, IR represents a central pathogenic mechanism of PCOS, and prolonged IR can lead to hyperinsulinemia, exacerbating PCOS symptoms [33, 34]. OB administration markedly attenuated the increase in FBG, FINS and HOMA-IR in PCOS mice. As a chronic inflammatory disease, PCOS is characterized by persistently elevated inflammatory markers [35, 36]. Many studies have proved the inhibition of inflammation can effectively alleviate PCOS [37, 38], indicating that relieving inflammatory responses might be necessary for PCOS therapy. Moreover, OB is reported to potently decrease pro-inflammatory mediators [17]. Therefore, we investigated whether OB ameliorated PCOS via inhibiting inflammation. We found that the upregulated level of TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β and IL-18, and the downregulated level of IL-10 in both the ovarian tissues of PCOS mice and DHEA-treated KGN cells was reversed after the administration of OB.

Growing evidence suggests that ovarian granulosa cell apoptosis and dysfunction are strongly associated with PCOS pathophysiology [39, 40]. Therefore, enhancing granulosa cell survival is critical for PCOS treatment. In this study, the decreased viability of DHEA-treated KGN cells was significantly reversed after OB administration. In addition, the increased apoptosis observed in the ovarian tissues of PCOS mice and DHEA-treated KGN cells was improved after OB treatment. Furthermore, previous studies have demonstrated that oxidative stress is one of the major contributors to abnormal granulosa cell function and apoptosis [41–43]. ROS-mediated oxidative stress has been reported to induce apoptosis through death receptor pathway or mitochondrial pathways [44, 45]. Moreover, Moreover, OB is reported to attenuate liver fibrosis via enhancing anti-oxidant effects [21]. Thus, we investigated whether OB ameliorated PCOS via inhibiting oxidative stress. Our results showed that the upregulated level of ROS and MDA, and the downregulated level of GSH, SOD and CAT in PCOS were reversed after the administration of OB, suggesting that OB could repress oxidative stress in PCOS.

We further investigated the underlying mechanisms of OB on PCOS. STAT3, a member of the STAT transcription factor family, is activated through the JAK-mediated phosphorylation pathway [46]. Abnormal activation of the JAK/STAT3 signaling pathway has been implicated in various diseases [47–49]. Recently, evidence suggests that the JAK/STAT pathway also plays a regulatory role in follicular development [50, 51]. A study by Hong et al. has confirmed that catechins could alleviate ovarian dysfunction and insulin resistance in PCOS mice via inhibiting STAT3 phosphorylation [52]. Moreover, JAK/STAT3 signaling is one of main signalings that regulate inflammation and oxidative stress [11, 12]. Interestingly, we discovered that STAT3 was a key binding target of OB via network pharmacology analysis. Therefore, we investigated whether OB inhibited oxidative stress and inflammation through inhibiting STAT3 phosphorylation. Our results indicated that OB treatment could reverse the increased phosphorylation of STAT3 in the ovarian tissues of PCOS mice and DHEA-treated KGN cells. Additionally, treatment with Colivelin TFA, a STAT3 activator, reversed the effects of OB on apoptosis, inflammatory responses, as well as oxidative stress in DHEA-treated KGN cells.

Collectively, our data demonstrated that OB alleviated PCOS symptoms via mitigating inflammation, apoptosis, and oxidative stress through the inhibition of STAT3 phosphorylation. These findings imply that OB holds promise as a potential therapeutic candidate for PCOS. A limitation of this study is that we did not explore the upstream regulators (e.g., JAK kinases) leading to STAT3 phosphorylation, which warrants further investigation.

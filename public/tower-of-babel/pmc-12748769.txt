A creative approach for the protection coordination of microgrids considering parallel events via the pyramidal grouping technique
Authors: Hossein Karimkhan-Zand, Kazem Mazlumi, Hamed Hashemi-Dezaki, Ali Abdali, Juan C Vasquez
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractOver the last few years, numerous research findings have emerged that incorporate the single-event or islanded case when designing models for protection coordination of microgrids via directional overcurrent relays (DOCRs). Nevertheless, the dimensions and complexity of this issue can be significantly increased by the parallel events. This paper introduces an innovative methodology for reducing the scope of parallel events. The pyramidal grouping technique is employed to organize the selected events into separate groups. In brief, the problem’s choice parameters are identified by an innovative mixed algorithm, known as the sequential quadratic, Quasi-Newton, and linear programming method (SQ-QN-LP). It is noteworthy that the SHARIAT feeder, which serves as a real-world grid, and the IEEE 399-bus network have been configured without any signs of miscoordination. In comparison to other comparable methodologies, such as SQ, QN, SQ-LP, QN-LP, and SQ-QN, the actualization of SQ-QN-LP decreases the relay’s time to function by approximately 75% to 22% in the mentioned networks.Keywords: Sequential quadratic programming, Directional overcurrent relays, Pyramidal grouping technique, Optimal protection planning, Parallel eventsSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Background and motivation

DOCRs are required to promptly disconnect the line from operation and locate the problem in the microgrids upon the occurrence of a short circuit or any contingencies. The problem’s fundamental infrastructure is characterized by the accurate functioning of the primary relay, and that is subsequently compensated for by secondary relays at predetermined intervals (referred to by the coordination time interval (CTI)).

Recently, microgrids have replaced conventional radial medium voltage systems, either by integrating distributed generation (DG) or by reconfiguring the network1,2. Conversely, hybrid and heuristic optimization methods have been substantially implemented to effectively address complex concerns while maintaining the constrained character of the issue at hand3–5.

Literature review

Particularly noteworthy procedures were developed in lately investigations for figuring out the ideal choice parameters of DOCR comprised of the pattern search algorithm with linear programming (LP)3, non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm III (NSGA III)-goal attainment algorithm-LP4, imperialistic competition algorithm6, class topper method7, seeker algorithm8, biogeography-based method-LP9, non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm II (NSGA II)10, simulated annealing-whale optimization algorithm11, enhanced differential evolutionary method12, improved firefly algorithm13, improved simulated annealing-LP14, colony optimization algorithm-LP15, grey wolf-moth-flame algorithm16, gradient-based optimizer-LP17, opposition-based training leveraging the partial order class topper method18, improved seagull optimization method19,20, and fuzzy logic-GA21.

Additionally, the choice parameters of DOCR are improved through the use of machine learning techniques during the optimization process. Wu et al.22 have developed an attack plan based on deep reinforcement learning, which is further enhanced by extended short-term memory. Furthermore, deploying the multi-setting groups aspect of DOCRs, as referenced in3,4, and23, provided grouping methods for classifying various system configurations into small subdivisions.

Additionally, Saleh and Mehrizi-Sani have offered ahead-of-the-curve harmonic DOCRs to address the low short circuit current issue caused by inverter-based DGs (IDGs)24. El-Sayed et al. have emphasized a new IDGs device that addresses short circuit currents25.

Conversely, the most significant challenge is the specific establishment of DOCRs, which is impeded by the erratic structure of the power grids. The outage of a line, load, DG, synchronous machine, or any other electrical element may negatively impact the validity of the power infrastructure. It is essential to acknowledge that the conventional method of resolving problems was predicated on a fixed configuration26. This issue results in inadequate relay actions, such as miscoordination, as the CSV and short-circuit current are subject to frequent fluctuations due to the unstable character of the power infrastructure. The investigations have recommended integrating events such as line and DG outages, single events, and the islanded state into the problem to prevent miscoordination of DOCRs27,28. According to an analysis of energy system outages recently released, parallel failures resulted in a significant loss of industrial demand3,29, and30. Consequently, it is conceivable that parallel events occurred.

Research gap

While this work focuses on protection coordination, it is critical to establish its role as a fundamental enabler of overall microgrid reliability and stability. A malfunctioning protection system is a primary cause of cascading outages and system collapse29,30. Therefore, the objective of this paper—to achieve fast and perfectly selective fault isolation under a wide range of contingencies—is directly aimed at preventing such mal-operations. By ensuring that faults are cleared swiftly and only by the intended relays, the proposed methodology establishes a reliable protection backbone. This robust foundation prevents nuisance tripping, contains disturbances, and is an essential prerequisite for maintaining stable and reliable operation of a microgrid, especially when subjected to parallel outages23,26. Moreover, a critical gap remains in the efficient management of N-k (where k > 2) parallel contingency scenarios. The combinatorial explosion of cases makes traditional optimization approaches computationally intractable, leading most studies to ignore them or resort to simplistic and potentially unreliable assumptions.

Problem formulation

The DOCR is presumed to be a multifunctional numerical relay in this paper. Similarly, the issue can be characterized as mixed integer NLP by combining the continuous character of TMSV with the discrete form of CSV. The risk of miscoordination is significantly elevated when the framework includes numerous failures, as opposed to a single event, regardless of the previously mentioned factors. Consequently, the primary aim of the objective function is to reduce miscoordination and expedite the operational time of the DOCRs. Strategy for reduction of parallel events (SRPE)

The solving model is not required to consider these types of contingencies under the conventional condition, as the likelihood of parallel disruptions is exceedingly low. Nevertheless, the Nordic and Baltic grids experienced cascading contingencies as a result of the parallel disruptions of the two buses in 2020, as demonstrated by genuine reports of actual power grid disturbances30. As a result, this investigation assesses the issue by analyzing fixed structures, parallel events, singular events, and islanded states. A novel remedy to the network topology issue, which is the consequence of the swiftly increasing number of cases, is proposed: the contingency reduction technique. This method employs PowerFactory DIgSILENT software to identify only those events that result in the maximum and lowest voltage ranges for each bus in the case study. This approach encompasses the entire range of distribution currents that may occur in any given scenario. PCGA and optimization algorithms

This portion provides a protection strategy that includes the hybrid algorithm SQ-QN-LP, which was chosen through trial and error. Other methodologies implement the aggregation algorithm before the SQ-QN-LP is implemented. Grouping is a mathematical method used to organize data elements that are similar into distinct groups3,4, and23.

The selection of the hybrid SQ-QN-LP algorithm over widely used metaheuristic approaches (e.g., genetic algorithm, particle swarm optimization) was a deliberate choice driven by the specific requirements of the adaptive protection coordination problem. While metaheuristics are powerful for global exploration in complex search spaces, they are inherently stochastic and often require a high computational cost due to the need to evaluate large populations over many generations. This makes them less suitable for applications that require rapid recalibration of settings, such as adaptive protection.

In contrast, the proposed SQ-QN-LP algorithm provides a deterministic, high-precision, and computationally efficient solution. The combination of Sequential Quadratic programming for fast convergence, Quasi-Newton for high-precision refinement, and linear programming for optimal parameter reduction is specifically tailored to exploit the structure of the DOCR coordination problem. This approach guarantees convergence to a local optimum with a very tight tolerance (as will be defined in (Sect. “Results”) and achieves the exceptional computational speed demonstrated in the results (Sect. “Results”), which is critical for real-world, online adaptive protection schemes.

To implement multi-setting groups on DOCR, the analogous choice parameters of DOCR are clustered using PCGA. Multiple settings groups are a feature that allows DOCRs to have a diverse array of setting configurations. In this instance, the maximum number of settings groups is four. Nevertheless, the associated network topology is characterized by the operation of only one of these clusters. The concept of PCGA as an innovative grouping algorithm is derived from the divisive pyramidal grouping algorithm, which organizes all SRPE-OCs into distinct clusters. The PCGA was developed to address specific limitations of conventional clustering techniques, such as K-means3 or fuzzy logic21, in the context of protection coordination. While these methods group configurations based on general system profiles, they cannot guarantee that the resulting clusters will yield a feasible set of relay settings without miscoordination3. In contrast, the PCGA integrates the optimization feasibility check directly into its clustering logic. It employs a specialized similarity metric based on relay operational parameters—Time-to-Trip and fault current ratio —ensuring that grouped configurations have nearly identical impacts on protection operations. Furthermore, its divisive hierarchical structure dynamically determines the optimal number of clusters based on convergence failure, eliminating the need to pre-define this parameter. This approach guarantees that every cluster is both statistically similar and coordination-feasible, a crucial requirement that standard methods cannot meet.

After implementing PCGA for the purpose of grouping the SRPE-OCs, the aforementioned hybrid algorithms are used to optimize the TMSV. In contrast to the other types of proposed algorithms (SQ, QN, SQ-LP, QN-LP, and SQ-QN), the SQ-QN-LP addresses PCPD with attributes such as constrained and unconstrained variables, large and small solution spaces, and a reduced time to operation for DOCRs. The SQ-LP is the primary focus of this protection strategy, as it rapidly addresses the issue during the initial phase. The TMSVs of the SQ-LP will be utilized as an initial point for the QN, a three-step solving procedure known as SQ-QN-LP (as previously mentioned, the CSV is equivalent to the ICSV in the context of LP).

Solution strategy and numerical implementation

The core optimization problem formulated in Sect. 2 is a mixed-integer non-linear programming problem, given the combination of continuous TMSV variables and discrete CSV variables. To enhance computational tractability and ensure robust convergence, the problem is strategically reformulated into a standard NLP problem. This is achieved by pre-determining the optimal CSV for each relay to its lower feasible bound, ICSV​, as derived in Sect. 3.2 (Eq. (12)). This decision is justified because it yields the fastest possible operating time for a given TMSV and fault current, ensuring the solution remains globally optimal in terms of minimizing total operating time.

The resulting NLP, which involves optimizing only the continuous TMSV variables, is solved using the hybrid SQ-QN-LP algorithm. The numerical implementation was carried out in MATLAB R2013a. The fmincon solver was used with the following configuration for the different algorithm phases:

Conclusion

This paper addressed the critical challenge of optimal protection coordination in modern distribution networks and microgrids subjected to parallel outage contingencies. The key findings demonstrate that the proposed integrated methodology successfully resolves the computational complexity of N-k contingency analysis while guaranteeing protection, security, and dependability. The Strategy for SRPE effectively reduced the number of necessary study cases by over 97% for a meshed network, while the novel PCGA dynamically organized these cases into feasible clusters. The hybrid SQ-QN-LP optimization algorithm then determined the optimal relay settings, achieving a significant reduction in total operating time—between 22% and 75% across the tested networks—compared to other benchmark algorithms, while ensuring the complete elimination of miscoordination. The primary contributions of this work are fourfold: (1) the introduction of the SRPE for efficient contingency reduction; (2) the development of the PCGA for feasibility-guaranteed clustering of network configurations; (3) the formulation of a hybrid SQ-QN-LP algorithm that combines computational speed with high precision for parameter optimization; and (4) the establishment of a comprehensive framework that integrates parallel events, single events, islanded operation, and the fixed network state into a single protection coordination model. For future work, this research creates a direct pathway toward fully adaptive protection systems. Immediate directions include integrating the ultra-fast SQ-LP algorithm within an online adaptive protection scheme to enable real-time setting adjustments in response to network changes. Furthermore, validating the method on a real-time digital simulator with hardware-in-the-loop testing is essential to assess performance under dynamic conditions and communication delays. Finally, extending the model to incorporate robust optimization techniques will be crucial in accounting for uncertainties such as measurement errors, fault impedance, and the stochastic nature of renewable generation, thereby enhancing the practical resilience of the protection scheme. This includes:

Dynamic Fault Analysis: Integrating full electromagnetic transient (EMT) simulations to model the transient response of inverter-based resources during faults, which can affect fault current magnitude and timing.

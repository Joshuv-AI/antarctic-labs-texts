A low illumination target detection method based on a dynamic gradient gain allocation strategy
Authors: Zhiqiang Li, Jian Xiang, Jiawen Duan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Current target detection methods perform well under normal lighting conditions; however, they encounter challenges in effectively extracting features, leading to false detections and missed detections in low illumination environments. To address these issues, this study introduces an efficient target detection method for low illumination, named DimNet. This method optimizes the model through enhancements in multi-scale feature fusion, feature extraction, detection head, and loss function. Firstly, efficient multi-scale feature fusion is performed by using a new neck structure in the original model so that it can fully exchange high-level semantic information and low-level spatial information. Secondly, by designing a new feature aggregation module, it can simultaneously fuse channel and spatial information as well as local and global information to improve the representation of the network. Subsequently, to achieve more accurate target recognition, a new detection head is designed by replacing the original convolutional layer and utilizing the reparameterization technique, which enhances recognition performance in complex scenes. Additionally, the size of the improved detection head is reduced by adopting a parameter-sharing approach, thereby balancing detection accuracy with computational efficiency. Finally, to solve the fuzzy boundary problem caused by the target boundary being similar to the surrounding background due to insufficient illumination under low illumination conditions, a new loss function is designed in this paper, which pays more attention to the center of the target and weakly considers the aspect ratio of the target prediction frame, and at the same time, the new loss function employs a dynamic gradient gain assignment strategy to reduce the effect of the low-quality anchor frames and to improve the target localization Accuracy. The experimental results show that DimNet achieves a mAP50 of 75.60% on the ExDark dataset, which is an improvement of 3.77% over the baseline model and 2.25% over the state-of-the-art (SOTA) model. DimNet outperforms the previous and current SOTA methods in terms of detection accuracy and other aspects of performance, which is a clear advantage.

Introduction

As one of the hot tasks in the field of computer vision, target detection has important applications in the fields of autonomous driving1, intelligent surveillance, and industrial and agricultural monitoring. For example, in the field of autonomous driving, it can accurately and efficiently recognize vehicles, pedestrians, traffic signs, and other objects on the road. In the field of surveillance, it can accurately locate and track moving objects within the target range. In the field of agriculture and industry, it can be used in the construction of intelligent agriculture and intelligent factories. In low-light environments, the importance of target detection becomes more and more prominent. Low-light conditions are usually found in scenarios such as nighttime video surveillance, nighttime autopilot, nighttime reconnaissance of UAVs, and industrial production environments with poor lighting conditions. In these situations, the lack of sufficient light results in degraded image quality, which affects target visibility and detection accuracy. However, many critical applications require target detection under these conditions. For example, in nighttime autonomous driving, vehicles need to recognize pedestrians and other obstacles on the road in time to avoid accidents. In smart surveillance, systems need to capture potential security threats promptly in low-light conditions. In agricultural monitoring, crop health status monitoring under low-light conditions is also key to improving productivity. Therefore, the detection of targets in low-light environments can not only enhance the security and reliability of the system but also extend the application scope of these technologies.

Under low illumination conditions, images usually suffer from low contrast, blurriness, obscure boundaries, and noise. These problems seriously affect the accuracy of target detection because existing detection algorithms make it difficult to deal with the relationship between local and global features, which in turn leads to inaccurate target identification and localization. In addition, the boundaries of small targets in low-light environments are even less clear, and the algorithms are less effective in detecting small targets. More importantly, existing target detection algorithms make it difficult to extract effective feature information in low-light environments, and the convolution operation can easily lead to the loss of low-level features, making them blend into the background, which ultimately results in misdetection or omission detection2. Therefore, how to effectively detect targets in low-light environments has become an urgent problem to be solved. To accurately recognize targets in low-light environments, related scholars have also made some attempts and improvements in low-light enhancement and target detection.

Low-light enhancement algorithms are mainly classified into two categories: based on traditional image processing methods and deep learning-based methods. Traditional image processing methods are mainly histogram equalization, Retinex algorithm, filter enhancement (mean filter, median filter), and edge enhancement-based methods (Soble, Laplacian operator). The main deep learning-based methods are Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), which can be used for image enhancement and restoration by learning and extracting features from images, such as DeepISP3 and LLNet4. The other is Generative Adversarial Network (GAN), which learns to generate high-quality images by generating adversarial approaches and can be used to visually enhance low-quality images. Although low-light enhancement algorithms have made significant progress in improving the quality of images under low-light conditions, there are some problems, such as when enhancing low-light images, the algorithms may enhance the level of noise in the image, introduce distortions or artifacts, which may lead to degradation of the image quality or poor visual effect. However, its main problem is the high computational complexity, which requires a large amount of computational resources and time for training and execution, followed by the poor generalization ability of the model, which has limited generalization ability in practical applications.

Deep learning-based target detection algorithms are mainly categorized into two types: two-stage target detection algorithms based on region suggestion and single-stage target detection algorithms. The two-stage target detection algorithms are mainly RCNN as well as Faster-rcnn5. The two-stage target detection algorithm relies on the region-suggested grid, generates a set of object candidate frames through the region-proposed network, and then carries out regression and classification based on the candidate frames. It has good detection accuracy, but its detection speed is slow, and it is not friendly for scenes that need to recognize objects quickly and accurately. The main single-stage target detection algorithms are SSD6, YOLO7–9series, and RetinaNet10,11, which directly predict the class and location of the target through dense points or anchor frames without utilizing region suggestions. Although sacrificing some detection accuracy, they significantly improve the speed of detection compared to two-stage target detection algorithms, which are balanced in all aspects. Although the single-stage target detection algorithms have slightly lower accuracy, they have a great advantage for scenarios where detection speed is required.

Existing target detection frameworks can achieve sufficiently reliable results under good lighting conditions, but sometimes the lighting conditions of some scenes tend to be very harsh, such as indoors and outdoors at night, special environments (tunnels, undersea), and bad weather (fog, haze, cloudy and rainy days). Target detection is greatly affected by imaging conditions and ambient lighting12, once in a low-light environment, the change of lighting will have a great impact on its detection effect, the detector’s performance will drop dramatically, and the performance of the general-purpose target detector will drop dramatically13. To solve this problem, Huang et al.14 tried to introduce a low-light enhancement algorithm into the target detection algorithm, and this preprocessing method will bring additional computational costs and consume more resources, thus affecting the practicality of the algorithm15.

Aiming at the problems of target detection in the low illumination field, this study proposes a target detection model DimNet for low-light conditions. Firstly, this paper proposes a new and efficient feature aggregation module (EFAM), which replaces all the feature aggregation modules in the backbone network in the feature extraction stage, which can consider the channel information and the spatial information at the same time, and further combines the local and global information to improve the representation of the network. Secondly, a new neck structure RepGFPN is used on the Neck, which can extract the global sensory field features to obtain richer contextual features, and then the paths are aggregated to fuse the features of different scales, and finally, the fused features are cascaded with the original features to improve the feature fusion capability of the model. Subsequently, to recognize the target more accurately in complex scenes, a new detection head, Efficient_head, is designed, which achieves a balance between detection accuracy and computation by replacing the original convolutional layer with RepConv to improve the detection ability, and reducing the size of the improved detection head by sharing a parameter between classification and detection. Finally, a new loss function PIoU is designed, which can reduce the influence of fuzzy boundaries and make the model focus more on the center point of the target, and the use of dynamic non-monotonic mechanism can further reduce the influence of low-quality anchor frames, consider a larger number of ordinary anchor frames, and improve the model’s ability to localize the target.

Related work

In the field of low-light target detection, current approaches primarily focus on two aspects. The first involves low-light image enhancement technologies, such as image denoising and enhancement. By designing effective low-light image enhancement algorithms, these techniques enrich image features and make contours more distinct, allowing for easier capture of image details. The second aspect involves developing efficient target detection algorithms that effectively extract and fuse features from low-light images without altering the inherent image characteristics. This improvement enhances the localization ability and recognition accuracy of the target detection network.

The purpose of low-light image enhancement is to recover images with insignificant features as a way to provide higher-quality images. Jiang et al.16 proposed an EnlightenGAN image enhancement algorithm, which effectively improves the visual quality of an image using GAN. However, this method may still introduce additional noise when dealing with low-light scenes, leading to image quality degradation. Ma et al.17 proposed an SCI image enhancement algorithm that utilizes inner domain convolution and self-calibration techniques to improve image quality while effectively handling noise and maintaining details. However, in dynamic or complex scenes, this method may not be able to effectively capture the target details, which may cause dynamic blurring and affect the detection accuracy. Zhu et al.18 proposed a CycleGAN image enhancement algorithm that utilizes GAN to achieve image style conversion, which can convert images between different styles without pairing data and is suitable for art style conversion and image translation tasks. However, in low-light image processing, the lack of paired data support may lead to blurring of target features, which increases artifacts and makes it difficult to identify the target, especially in complex backgrounds. Yang et al.19 proposed the LightingNet method, which achieves image enhancement by learning and fitting the current data through a full-size architecture to provide local high-level features, and utilizing complementary learning sub-networks to provide globally fine-tuned features through learning migrations. However, under extreme low-light conditions, LightingNet may not be able to effectively enhance image details, but may instead generate unwanted noise due to overemphasis on local features. Fu et al.20 proposed an unsupervised method, PairLIE, which achieves image enhancement by using the theory of Retinex applied to the network to align the two radiometric components and then using a self-supervised mechanism to remove inappropriate features from the original image to achieve image detail learning from the paired low-light real columns. Although it can remove inappropriate features from the original image in some cases, the method may not be able to deal with background noise effectively in high-noise environments, leading to difficulties in target recognition. Also, the introduction of the self-supervision mechanism may lead to an increase in processing time, which is particularly critical in scenes that require real-time monitoring. Feng et al.21 fused the DiffLight algorithm for content and detail, which consists of a Denoising Enhancement (DE) branch and a Detail Preservation (DP) branch, and at the same time, introduced Progressive Patch Fusion ( PPF) to address block artifacts, this method ranked high in the CVPR2024 NTIRE low-light enhancement challenge and achieved the SOTA in some datasets22. However, the method may still introduce new artifacts and visual distortions when confronted with extremely low-light scenes, especially when the background is complex or there are dynamic objects, affecting target detectability. Although the above studies can prove that low-light enhancement can achieve good results on low-illumination images, there are existing studies that prove that image enhancement does not necessarily lead to improved detection. In addition, due to the large differences in different scenes and the limited generalization ability of the model, applying low-light enhancement techniques for low-light target detection may bring noise, distortion, and artifacts23, and may also cause delays due to long processing time, which also unacceptable in scenes that require real-time monitoring (e.g., security surveillance or motion capture). Therefore, the use of low-light enhancement techniques for low-light target detection does not meet current practical needs.

Currently, with the development of deep learning technology, some attempts and improvements have been made in the field of target detection. Peng et al.24 proposed a fusion feature enhancement algorithm NLE-YOLO based on yolov5, which designs a new feature extraction module C2fLEFEM, which can suppress high-frequency noise and enhance the basic information. The multi-scale feature extraction module AMC2fLEFEM and the attention sensing field module AMRFB can make the network pay more attention to the target area and improve the feature extraction capability of the network. Sun et al.25 introduced an efficient bidirectional feature pyramid network (FPN)26 for cross-scale feature fusion, which further improves the accuracy of detecting small targets. Xiao et al.27 proposed a specialized FPN and context fusion network based on the RFB-Net model to improve the performance of target detection in low-light scenes. Yang et al.28 proposed a codec network with a multi-scale feature aggregation network, but in the coding stage of the network, the information on the different scale features is not preserved during the successive downsampling process, which leads to the loss of the image features, and affects the recovery of the image high-frequency details. Jiang et al.29 proposed a generalized feature pyramid (GFPN), a new type of channel fusion that includes jump layers and cross-scale connectivity, which is capable of handling different levels and hierarchical levels of feature fusion. Wan et al.30 proposed a lightweight mixed local channel attention module (MLCA), which is capable of fusing channel and spatial information, as well as local and global information, simultaneously to improve the performance of the detection network. Hu et al.31 proposed a PE-YOLO algorithm that uses a pyramid enhancement network and combines it with the YOLOv3 model to achieve excellent detection results in low-illumination scenes. Sasagawa et al.32 proposed a domain adaptive approach that fuses a low-illumination image enhancement model and a target detection model to achieve nighttime target detection. Qiu et al.33 proposed a fogged, weather-adaptive target recognition framework IDOD-YOLOv7.The method combines an image defogging module (AOD), an image enhancement module (SAIP), and a YOLOv7 detection module, which significantly improves target detection in hazy environments. Qin et al.34 proposed DE-YOLO, a joint enhancement detection framework containing DENet and YOLOv3, where DENet is responsible for adaptively enhancing low-light images, and then the enhanced images are input to YOLOv3 for detection.

Although researchers in the field of low-light target detection continue to explore improved methods and try to enhance detection performance under low-light conditions through new algorithms and network architectures, target detection in low-light scenarios still faces multiple challenges. For example, although NLE-YOLO suppresses high-frequency noise and enhances basic information through a new feature extraction module design, it is still difficult to effectively extract key details of the target under extreme low-light conditions. This limits its detection accuracy in complex backgrounds. In addition, its multi-scale feature extraction module and attention mechanism, while helpful in focusing on the target area, still have limitations when dealing with uneven illumination and high-frequency detail recovery. Similarly, PE-YOLO combines the pyramid enhancement network with the YOLOv3 model and achieves certain detection results in low-illumination scenes. However, the algorithm faces problems in practical applications, e.g., in environments with strong shadows or large illumination variations, the ability of pyramid enhancement may not be sufficient to compensate for the lost information in the image, thus affecting the recognition accuracy of small targets. Therefore, despite the progress of existing feature fusion and cross-scale processing techniques, they still fail to completely solve key problems such as high-frequency noise suppression, uneven illumination, and target detail recovery. Future research needs to further improve the feature extraction and information retention mechanisms to cope with complex low illumination environments and improve the stability and accuracy of target detection algorithms in practical applications.

Methodology

Overall network architecture

YOLOv8 is a target detection algorithm developed by Ultralytics and officially released on 10/1/2023. Compared to YOLOv5, YOLOv8 has been significantly improved in several aspects. First, the model introduces the C2f module instead of the original C3 module, which enhances the feature extraction capability and gradient flow. The network architecture of YOLOv8 consists of an input layer, a backbone network, a neck network, and a head network. The input image is first resized to 640 × 640. The backbone network achieves feature extraction through multiple convolutional operations with batch normalization and SiLU activation function. The SPPF module is located at the end of the backbone network, which is combined with multi-scale feature processing to further enhance the feature abstraction capability. The neck network adopts FPN and PAN structures to effectively fuse feature maps at different scales. The head network of YOLOv8 is designed in a decoupled manner to handle regression and classification tasks separately, and this structure improves the performance of the respective tasks. Overall, YOLOv8 performs well in terms of both speed and accuracy and is therefore chosen as the baseline model in this study.

In low illumination environments, images are often difficult to extract and easy to lose features due to problems such as low contrast, blurring, and inconspicuous boundaries, which makes it difficult for the original model to perform well in such scenarios. To solve this problem, an algorithm called DimNet is proposed in this study and the model is optimized from several perspectives. In the Backbone stage, the EFAM module is designed in the paper for better extraction of complex features in low-light images. After EFAM processing, the feature information of the target region in the image becomes richer, while unimportant factors such as background are effectively suppressed. In the Neck stage, we select three feature layers with different scales (P3, P4, and P5) from the Backbone after EFAM processing as inputs for the next stage. Subsequently, the FPN structure in the original model was improved in the article by using the RepGFPN structure. Compared with the original structure, RepGFPN can more fully exchange high-level semantic information and low-level spatial features. Then, its feature fusion module is optimized by combining the reparameterization mechanism and the efficient aggregation layer network to further enhance the feature extraction and fusion capability of the model, so that the features of the P3, P4, and P5 layers can fully interact and fuse with each other, which significantly improves the overall performance of multi-scale target detection. Next, we perform detection on the fused three feature layers and improve the detection head of the original model so that it can extract deeper and richer effective features from the fused complex image features, thus further enhancing the detection capability of the model. Finally, by improving the loss function, the model can evaluate the dynamic quality of the generated anchor frames to reduce the influence of low-quality anchor frames and speed up the convergence of the model.

Overall, the EFAM feature aggregation module significantly improves the effectiveness of feature representation by enhancing the target region features and suppressing the background noise. Compared with the traditional FPN structure, RepGFPN not only introduces the reparameterization mechanism but also further optimizes the fusion ability of multi-scale features through the Efficient Aggregation Layer Network. Finally, the PIoU loss function further accelerates model convergence by reducing the interference of low-quality anchor frames on model training through a dynamic quality assessment mechanism. RepGFPN

In the target detection model, features of different scales capture multiple details and structures of the target respectively, therefore, how to effectively extract and fuse features of different resolutions from the backbone network is directly related to the detection performance of the whole model. The traditional FPN achieves multi-scale feature fusion through top-down paths but is limited by the unidirectional information flow and cannot fully utilize the multi-scale information. PANet35 further optimizes multi-scale feature fusion by adding bottom-up paths, but this significantly increases the amount of computation. BiFPN36 balances the computation efficiency and feature fusion effect by simplifying connections. Efficiency with the effectiveness of feature fusion. However, these methods generally focus on the feature fusion itself, neglecting the deep connection and information exchange between features, thus limiting the overall model performance.

To solve these problems and enhance the overall performance of the model, the model uses a RepGFPN structure that contains jump layers and cross-scale connections. This structure not only fully exchanges high-level semantic information and low-level spatial features but also significantly improves the detection accuracy of the overall model by flexibly controlling the expressive ability of features at different scales. Specifically, by setting different dimensions for features of different scales, RepGFPN makes the detection of small objects more accurate in high resolution, especially in complex scenes such as low illumination. In addition, the feature fusion module of CSPNet is improved by combining the reparameterization mechanism with an efficient aggregation layer network, which further strengthens the feature extraction and fusion capability of the model without increasing the amount of additional computation. It is these designs of RepGFPN that enable DimNet to significantly improve the overall performance of multi-scale target detection while maintaining computational efficiency.

EFAM module

In the feature extraction stage, feature maps at different scales contain different levels of semantic information; while high-level feature maps are rich in semantic content, they have reduced spatial resolution, while lower-level feature maps provide higher spatial resolution but less semantic detail. By integrating feature maps at various scales, DimNet can capture the diversity and complexity of targets more effectively. In target detection tasks, complex environments usually seriously thousand interfere with target recognition. Specifically, factors such as noisy backgrounds, occlusions, and illumination variations may make it difficult for the model to accurately capture target features, thus affecting the recognition performance. Therefore, enhancing the model’s focus on important features, especially in complex environments, is the key to improving target detection accuracy. To address these challenges, DimNet employs the EFAM module, which enhances the model’s focus on critical regions by considering the background information of the feature map. This mechanism helps suppress irrelevant background interference, thus improving the quality of feature representation and detection performance.

Figure 5 shows the overall structure of EFAM. The design goal of EFAM is to enhance the model’s ability to extract target features in complex scenes, especially in low illumination conditions, by fusing the input features with more efficient local and global information. In the EFAM module, the input features are first processed by two different pooling operations: the LAP and the GAP. The LAP operation scans the input feature maps using a sliding window mechanism to capture local spatial relationships to obtain a feature representation with fine resolution. The GAP operation is responsible for obtaining global contextual information by computing global relationships between channels. LAP and GAP produce feature maps with different resolutions that represent spatial information at different scales, respectively. After the feature maps have been processed by LAP and GAP, EFAM uses a convolutional layer to channel compress the features to reduce the computation and extract key features. Subsequently, the local and global feature maps are fused through a series of multiplication and addition operations to enhance attention to locally relevant features while incorporating global contextual information. This process utilizes the core idea of the attention mechanism, i.e., adjusting the weights of local features based on global information, enabling the model to capture important features more accurately. The Bottleneck sub-module of the EFAM module can be stacked in multiple layers to further enhance the feature extraction capability. Each Bottleneck unit carries out a refinement in the process of processing local and global features. Through successive stacking, the EFAM module can gradually focus on the most relevant features while effectively suppressing irrelevant background information. Ultimately, the processed features are output through a convolutional layer, ensuring that the features are consistent with the needs of the downstream network in terms of dimension and resolution.

The design of the EFAM module can significantly improve the feature extraction of target objects when dealing with low illumination, complex backgrounds, or other challenging scenes by balancing local and global features. In particular, through a fine-grained local attention mechanism, EFAM can effectively capture the detailed features of small objects, while global attention ensures the integrity of the overall contextual information. This enables the DimNet model to better understand the target objects in the scene and improve the accuracy and robustness of detection when facing complex environments.

The original detection header contains two branches, one for the regression task and one for the classification task, adopting the idea of Anchor Free. Each branch consists of two 3 \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\times\:$$\end{document} 3 Conv and a 1 \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\times\:$$\end{document} 1 Conv2d layer. Since the model has to deal with more complex visual tasks and scenarios with higher levels of feature abstraction, the model uses the more advantageous RepConv to optimize the original detection head, which has a significant advantage in feature representation capability, flexibility, and generalization capability compared to ordinary Conv. Although the use of RepConv will bring about an improvement in detection accuracy and efficiency, a simple replacement will bring about a significant increase in the number of parameters and computation for the detection head, which is not conducive to model optimization. Therefore, inspired by RetrainNet37, we propose the idea of sharing parameters in the detection head as a way to balance the model complexity and detection accuracy. This is done by replacing the four Conv layers in each detection header with two RepConv layers, changing the double branching before Conv2d to single branching with it, and subsequently performing the classification and regression tasks separately. This idea of adopting shared parameters also brings about an improvement in accuracy with a small increase in the number of parameters and computation. On the original model, the model with Efficient_head increases the computational complexity by 3.60 FLOPs but improves the mAP50 by 1.07%, which effectively improves the accuracy of the model. Therefore, under the premise of slightly increasing the network computation, by flexibly using the reparameterization technique RepConv to optimize the detection head, DimNet can extract deeper and richer image features and retain more spatial information of the image, which significantly improves the detection performance of the model.

Furthermore, our Efficient_head demonstrates strong generalization capabilities. When addressing various tasks, it is possible to substitute RepConv with alternative convolutional modules to accommodate different task requirements. PIOU loss functions

The loss function used in the original model is CIoU38 (Complete-IoU), which takes into account the differences in distance and aspect ratio between the centroids of the bounding box to make the bounding box regression more accurate than the traditional loss function, in addition to the introduction of the penalty terms for distance and shape to further improve the model in dealing with the bounding box shape and position relationship. However, in low illumination scenarios, since the boundaries of the target in the image will become blurred due to illumination, it makes the CIoU unable to accurately capture the true boundaries of the target when calculating the intersection and merger ratio, which in turn affects the accuracy of the loss calculation. For this reason, we design PIoU, which focuses on the distance between the center points of the bounding box to reduce the influence of the blurred boundary. By focusing on the distance between the centroids of the bounding box, the new loss function PloU reduces the influence of fuzzy boundaries on the loss calculation due to insufficient illumination improves the localization accuracy and stability of DimNet under low illumination and complex lighting conditions, and is also able to improve the training convergence speed of the model. Conclusion

In this paper, in order to improve the problem of image feature extraction and recognition under low illumination conditions, this study proposes DimNet, an efficient target detection model. It introduces a novel feature extraction module to improve the feature integration capability, combining spatial, channel, local, and global information. The model also adopts a new feature pyramid structure to flexibly control the representation of features at different scales for efficient multi-scale feature fusion. The detection head section combines the reparameterization technique RepConv and shared parameters to reduce the number of model parameters and improve the detection effect. In addition, in order to cope with the blurring and background interference in low illumination, a new loss function is designed to focus on the center point of the target, which reduces the effect of the low-quality anchor frame. Experiments on the ExDark dataset show that the incoming DimNet model achieves 79.09% and 75.60% on precession and mAP50, respectively, and improves 4.28% and 3.77% compared to the base model YOLOv8, and 4.15% and 2.25% compared to the optimal model YOLOv9, respectively. Multiple comparative experimental data show that DimNet outperforms both previous and current methods in detection accuracy and performance. In the future, we will continue to study the model’s lightweight and generalization capabilities to promote the further development of low-illumination target detection.

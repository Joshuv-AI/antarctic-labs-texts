The Impact of Labor-Delivery-Recovery-Postpartum (LDRP) Care Model on Delivery Outcomes: A Single-Center Retrospective Cohort Study
Authors: Bo Ma, Zelin Yu, Na Mei, Lan Cheng, Hongyan Cui, Xu Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractStudy DesignThis single-center retrospective cohort study compared parturients undergoing delivery under the Labor-Delivery-Recovery-Postpartum (LDRP) model (n=901) with those in traditional delivery rooms (n=2863) to evaluate differences in key obstetric outcomes. The study was conducted at Tianjin Central Maternity and Child Health Hospital (Heping Campus) from July 2023 to August 2024.Participants and DataA total of 3764 eligible parturients (age ≥18 years) were enrolled, with all data systematically extracted from the hospital’s electronic medical record system. Collected variables included demographic, obstetric, neonatal, and delivery-related parameters.Statistical AnalysisGiven the non-randomized group assignment (based on patient preference and financial capacity), 1:1 propensity score matching (PSM) with a 0.02 caliper was employed to balance baseline characteristics, including maternal age, gestational weeks, epidural analgesia use, parity, complications, neonatal sex, and birth weight, resulting in 900 matched pairs per group. Categorical variables are presented as counts/percentages, and continuous variables as medians with 5th–95th percentile ranges. Post-matching, χ2-tests compared outcomes, and binary logistic regression identified independent risk factors.ResultsAfter PSM, the LDRP group demonstrated significantly lower rates of vaginal operative delivery (χ2=19.393, p<0.001) and postpartum hemorrhage (χ2=19.498, p<0.001) compared to the traditional delivery group. No significant difference was observed in intrapartum cesarean delivery rates (χ2=0.147, P=0.701). Multivariate analysis identified delivery in a traditional room (OR=1.990, 95% CI 1.478–2.677), along with neonatal birth weight (OR = 1.146, P = 0.016, 95% CI 1.020–1.218) and maternal age (OR = 1.068, P = 0.001, 95% CI 1.026–1.111), as independent risk factors for postpartum hemorrhage. For intrapartum cesarean delivery, independent risk factors included gestational weeks (OR = 1.913, P<0.001, 95% CI 1.485–2.465), number of deliveries (OR = 0.190, P <0.001, 95% CI 0.092–0.394), the presence of complications (OR = 2.358, P<0.001, 95% CI 1.768–3.144) and the duration of labor (OR = 1.059, P<0.001, 95% CI 1.034–1.085). Delivery location was also an independent factor associated with reduced operative vaginal delivery (OR=0.224, 95% CI 0.111–0.454).ConclusionThe findings suggest that the LDRP delivery model is independently associated with a lower incidence of postpartum hemorrhage and operative vaginal delivery. This indicates that the integrated, continuous-care structure of the LDRP model may contribute positively to maternal delivery outcomes and postpartum recovery experience.Keywords: LDRP, postpartum hemorrhage, intrapartum cesarean deliver, labor duration, vaginal delivery assistance

Introduction

The Labor-Delivery-Recovery-Postpartum (LDRP) model represents a modern, integrated approach to childbirth, where all stages—labor, delivery, recovery, and postpartum care—occur in a single, private room. This design aims to provide a continuous, seamless experience that minimizes maternal stress associated with room transfers and procedural disruptions, while promoting a supportive, family-centered environment.1,2 Driven by advances in medical practice and evolving maternal expectations for personalized care, the LDRP model is increasingly recognized as a key component of contemporary obstetric services, with growing adoption in hospitals and maternity centers globally.3,4

A growing body of evidence indicates that single-room maternity care is consistently associated with high levels of maternal satisfaction and an improved childbirth experience.4 However, the evidence regarding the impact of the LDRP model on critical clinical outcomes—such as postpartum hemorrhage, operative vaginal delivery, and intrapartum cesarean delivery—remains limited and heterogeneous. This evidence gap is particularly notable in diverse healthcare settings, including resource-variable environments, and within the specific context of China’s rapidly evolving maternity services.

In China, alongside national policies promoting natural childbirth and enhanced maternity care, the LDRP model has been introduced in several leading institutions. Tianjin Central Obstetrics and Gynecology Hospital, as a regional pioneer and one of the highest-volume centers implementing this model, provides a valuable setting for such evaluation. Since its implementation in June 2023, the LDRP option has offered an alternative aimed at improving comfort and experience. Nevertheless, robust comparative data on its effectiveness versus the traditional multi-room delivery model in mitigating key maternal risks are still lacking.

Therefore, this study aims to address this gap by utilizing data from this major tertiary center, that can better inform clinical practice and service planning regarding the implementation and value of integrated birth models.

Materials and Methods

General Information

This study collected data from pregnant women who visited and gave birth at Tianjin Central Obstetrics and Gynecology Hospital between July 2023 and August 2024. Patients who met the inclusion and exclusion criteria were included in the study. Group assignment was non-random and based primarily on patient preference and room availability.

Inclusion criteria: (1) Age ≥ 18 years; (2) Prenatal care and delivery at Tianjin Central Obstetrics and Gynecology Hospital (Heping Branch).

Exclusion criteria: (1) Incomplete medical records; (2) Prenatal care not provided at Tianjin Central Obstetrics and Gynecology Hospital; (3) Coexisting abnormalities in other organ systems (eg, liver dysfunction, nephrotic syndrome, heart failure, pulmonary edema, intracranial lesions, etc); (4) History of postpartum hemorrhage.

A total of 3764 patients who met the inclusion and exclusion criteria were grouped based on their delivery location, including the LDRP delivery group (n=901) and the traditional delivery room group (n=2863). Patient grouping was based on the patients’ autonomous choice and individual payment willingness: parturients and their families, after being fully informed (through prenatal education about the differences in environment, services, and costs between the two models), made their choice according to their preferences, expectations for the delivery experience, and financial capacity. Throughout the process, clinicians or researchers did not intervene in the grouping or assign patients based on clinical indications.

Research Methods

Collection of Basic Data

A systematic query of the inpatient electronic medical record system at Tianjin Central Obstetrics and Gynecology Hospital collected basic data from pregnant women at the Heping branch, including prenatal information (maternal age, gestational week, parity, labor duration, delivery location, epidural use, and complications) and postpartum data (newborn birth weight, postpartum hemorrhage, episiotomy, assisted delivery, and delivery mode).

This study analyzed major obstetric complications - including gestational hypertension, gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM), pregnancy-associated hypothyroidism, and antenatal anemia (hemoglobin<110 g/L) - as a composite binary variable (present/absent) to facilitate statistical analysis. In this study, postpartum hemorrhage (PPH) was defined as genital tract bleeding exceeding 500 milliliters within 24 hours after vaginal delivery, or blood loss exceeding 1000 milliliters after cesarean section.

The delivery room and LDRP unit adopt a unified staffing model, each currently staffed with six midwives to form a balanced team structure: two senior midwives (with ≥10 years of midwifery experience) and four junior midwives (with 1–3 years of midwifery experience). In the event of any issues during labor, such as abnormal fetal heart rate monitoring, the clinical physician team will promptly intervene and manage the situation actively and effectively. The team consists of one chief physician, one associate chief physician, one attending physician, and one resident physician. This staffing structure remains consistent, with identical configurations during both day and night shifts. The same staff rotated between units, ensuring comparable expertise and care standards. Standardized personnel allocation ensures that the LDRP delivery model is comparable to traditional delivery rooms in terms of team qualifications. All participating obstetricians and midwives are nationally certified and strictly adhere to standardized clinical procedures.

Statistical analysis compared the LDRP and traditional delivery room groups regarding postpartum hemorrhage, vaginal assisted delivery rates, and intrapartum cesarean delivery. Risk factors for intrapartum cesarean deliver, postpartum hemorrhage, and prolonged labor were also analyzed.

Data Statistical Analysis

All analyses were conducted using SPSS version 27.0. Standard descriptive statistical methods were employed, including determining the absolute and relative frequencies for categorical variables, and calculating the median and the 5th to 95th percentile range for continuous variables. A 1:1 nearest-neighbor matching method was used for propensity score matching (PSM) between the LDRP delivery group and the traditional delivery room group. The matching variables included age, gestational week, whether epidural anesthesia was used, number of pregnancies and deliveries, presence of complications, newborn gender, and birth weight, among other general data. The matching tolerance was set at 0.02. The differences in postpartum hemorrhage, intrapartum cesarean deliver conversion, and assisted delivery rates between the groups were compared. Additionally, binary logistic regression models were used for univariate and multivariate analyses of risk factors. All comparisons were two-tailed, and the significance level (α) was set at 0.05. A p-value below this threshold indicates a statistically significant difference.

Baseline Characteristics Table

A total of 3764 patients were included in this study, divided into two groups based on delivery location: the LDRP delivery group (n=901) and the traditional delivery room group (n=2863). The average age of the entire population was 30.17 (±3.77) years old, the average weight of newborns was 3284.62 (±408.62) g, and the average duration of labor was 9.33 (±5.30) hours.

Most deliveries (59.0%, n=2219) occurred at 37–40 weeks’ gestation, with 89.9% (n=3382) of parturients receiving labor analgesia. Primiparous women made up 78.9% of the population, with 769 in the LDRP group and 2200 in the traditional delivery room group. The overall postpartum hemorrhage rate was 10.4%, 1.4% required vaginal assisted delivery, and 14.9% had a intrapartum cesarean deliver conversion. Detailed data is shown in Table 1 and Table 2.

Group Comparison

In the entire population, 390 cases (10.4%) of postpartum hemorrhage occurred, with 76 (8.43%) in the LDRP group and 314 (11%) in the traditional delivery room group. A total of 52 cases (1.4%) required vaginal assisted delivery, with 12 in the LDRP group and 40 in the traditional group. After PSM matching, there was no statistically significant difference in the rate of intrapartum cesarean deliver between groups (χ2=0.147, P=0.701) (Figure 1a). The assisted vaginal delivery rate (χ2=19.393, p<0.001) and postpartum hemorrhage rate (χ2=19.498, p<0.001) differed significantly between groups (Figure 1b and c).

Analysis of Related Factors

Analysis of Risk Factors for Postpartum Hemorrhage

In this study, an analysis of risk factors for postpartum hemorrhage was conducted by including factors such as age, gestational age, number of pregnancies, choice of epidural anesthesia, and newborn birth weight in the univariate logistic regression analysis. Significant factors identified in the univariate analysis were then included in the multivariate analysis. Multivariate analysis identified neonatal birth weight (OR = 1.146, P = 0.016, 95% CI 1.020–1.218), maternal age (OR=1.068, P=0.001, 95% CI 1.026–1.111), and delivery in the traditional room (OR=1.990, P<0.001, 95% CI 1.478–2.677) as independent risk factors for postpartum hemorrhage.

Discussion

LDRP, an abbreviation for Labor, Delivery, Recovery, and Postpartum, represents a comprehensive, family-centered childbirth approach that integrates labor, delivery, and postpartum recovery into a single, unified process.3 LDRP has transformed traditional maternity care by adopting a woman-centered approach, allowing the entire process—from admission to postpartum recovery—to occur in one location. This “one-stop” model ensures continuous care in a comfortable, supportive environment.5 LDRP rooms are staffed with experienced obstetricians, midwives, pediatric specialists, and other healthcare professionals, offering comprehensive care. They also prioritize maternal mental health by providing family support and psychological counseling, reducing anxiety during labor and the risk of postpartum depression.6 Studies have shown that midwife-led continuity models of care improve the quality of care, enhance maternal engagement and decision-making capacity, reduce interventions while improving perinatal outcomes, thus recommending the implementation of one-to-one continuous care.7

Due to the limited availability of LDRP rooms in China, large-scale studies comparing LDRP and traditional delivery rooms are scarce. This study uses data from a single-center LDRP delivery volume to compare postpartum hemorrhage, assisted delivery, and intrapartum cesarean deliver rates, offering valuable insights for clinical practice.

Intrapartum Cesarean Deliver and Vaginal Delivery Assistance

Intrapartum cesarean delivery is a significant intervention that can affect maternal and neonatal health.8,9 Identifying factors associated with dystocia during the prenatal period is therefore crucial for risk mitigation.10 During vaginal delivery, strict adherence to established indications for operative interventions, such as forceps or vacuum extraction, is essential for managing fetal distress and minimizing trauma.11,12

Previous studies have identified several factors associated with an increased likelihood of intrapartum cesarean delivery, including nulliparity, advanced maternal age (≥35 years), elevated BMI (≥30 kg/m2), excessive gestational weight gain (≥15 kg), fetal macrosomia, labor induction, and premature rupture of membranes.9,10,13–15

Our multivariate logistic regression analysis identified four independent factors associated with conversion from planned vaginal delivery to cesarean section: gestational weeks (OR = 1.913, P < 0.001, 95% CI 1.485–2.465), number of prior deliveries (OR = 0.190, P < 0.001, 95% CI 0.092–0.394), presence of maternal complications (OR = 2.358, P < 0.001, 95% CI 1.768–3.144), and duration of labor (OR = 1.059, P < 0.001, 95% CI 1.034–1.085). Delivery location, LDRP deliver, was independently associated with a lower likelihood of operative vaginal delivery (OR = 0.224, P < 0.001, 95% CI 0.111–0.454).

The LDRP framework facilitates enhanced personalized care through its integrated structure. A core component is the dedicated one-to-one midwifery system, which enables continuous labor support and allows for earlier identification and management of labor abnormalities. This systematic approach includes ongoing intrapartum monitoring, dynamic assessment of uterine activity, and guideline-based adjustment of oxytocin regimens when indicated. The continuous midwifery presence also improves clinician-family communication, provides real-time guidance, and may help alleviate maternal anxiety.

The single-room setting minimizes environmental disruptions, potentially reducing maternal stress and catecholamine release. This physiological effect could support more effective uterine contractility. Furthermore, the private environment facilitates sustained family support, which may further decrease maternal anxiety and pain perception. Together, these factors create conditions conducive to physiological labor and may contribute to the observed reduction in operative interventions.16,17

In summary, the LDRP care model is associated with reduced rates of intrapartum cesarean conversion and operative vaginal delivery. This correlation may be attributed to its integrated, continuous-care structure, which supports physiological labor progression. Further large-scale, multicenter studies with comprehensive biopsychosocial assessments are warranted to confirm these findings and better elucidate the model’s clinical impact.

Postpartum Hemorrhage

Postpartum hemorrhage (PPH) is a severe obstetric complication that poses a significant threat to maternal health.18 The overall PPH incidence in this study was 10.4%, a rate that reflects the increasing prevalence of advanced maternal age and high-risk pregnancies in contemporary obstetric practice. National statistics further indicate notable regional and institutional variations in PPH rates within China, with tertiary public hospitals typically reporting higher rates for vaginal deliveries, likely due to their concentration of complex cases.19

Through propensity score-matched analysis, a statistically significant difference in PPH rates was observed between the traditional delivery and LDRP groups (χ2= 19.498, p < 0.001). Multivariate logistic regression identified three independent factors associated with PPH: neonatal birth weight (OR = 1.146, P = 0.016, 95% CI 1.020–1.218), maternal age (OR = 1.068, P = 0.001, 95% CI 1.026–1.111), and delivery location. Specifically, delivery in a traditional room versus an LDRP setting was associated with nearly twice the risk of PPH (OR = 1.990, P < 0.001, 95% CI 1.478–2.677), suggesting that the care environment itself may be a significant factor influencing PPH risk.

These findings align with and extend existing literature, which documents additional PPH risk factors including a prolonged second stage of labor (≥3 hours), labor induction, and pre-pregnancy BMI ≥30 kg/m2, while identifying the presence of a birth companion and multiparity as protective factors.20 Other studies have linked maternal hematological parameters (eg, hemoglobin, platelet, and fibrinogen levels) and neonatal birth weight to PPH risk.21,22 For intrapartum cesarean deliveries specifically, prolonged labor, lower uterine segment edema, uterine incision extensions, coagulopathy, and fetal macrosomia are recognized contributors.23

The lower incidence of PPH observed in the LDRP group is likely attributable to the integrated structure of this care model. Beyond facilitating personalized care, the LDRP model’s physical design and dedicated staffing protocol are fundamental to its implementation. The private, single-room environment may reduce maternal stress and catecholamine release, while continuous, one-to-one midwifery support enables immediate assessment and early intervention in cases of uterine atony or other potential bleeding triggers.24 Thus, the LDRP model functions as a holistic framework that creates conditions supportive of physiological labor and prompt clinical management.

While the LDRP model was associated with a reduced incidence of PPH in this study, it may not be universally suitable for all parturients. An individualized assessment of each mother’s clinical and psychosocial context remains essential for determining the most appropriate care setting. Future research should investigate the applicability and potential benefits of the LDRP model across diverse maternal populations and explore how specific aspects of LDRP care and room management can be optimized to further mitigate PPH risk.

Labor Duration

A preliminary difference in labor duration was observed between the LDRP and traditional delivery groups prior to propensity score matching (P=0.023), which prompted a supplementary analysis of factors influencing labor progression. Existing literature has established labor duration as an independent factor that can significantly affect maternal and neonatal outcomes.25,26 To optimize childbirth outcomes, it is therefore essential to identify variables associated with prolonged labor. Previous work by Dr. Wang Wenxiang identified gestational age, maternal age, use of uterotonics, parity, fetal weight, fetal position, pelvic dimensions, and prenatal exercise as independent risk factors for prolonged labor.27 Similarly, research by Dr. Ma Dan highlighted fetal macrosomia (≥4.0 kg), premature rupture of membranes, oxytocin administration, a contracted pelvis, and abnormal fetal presentation as key contributors.28

Our findings align with and extend this evidence base. Multivariate analysis confirmed that advanced maternal age (95% CI: 0.89–0.98; P=0.003), a greater number of prior pregnancies (95% CI: 0.78–0.85; P<0.001), and a higher parity (95% CI: 0.72–0.79; P<0.001) were associated with shorter labor duration. Conversely, increased gestational age (95% CI: 1.10–1.21; P<0.001) and the use of epidural analgesia (95% CI: 1.21–1.32; P<0.001) were significantly associated with a longer labor process. These results underscore that obstetric history, maternal age, gestational age, and analgesia choice are important determinants of labor duration.

Within the LDRP model, the continuous, midwife-led monitoring facilitates the early detection of deviations from normal labor progress. This allows for timely, non-pharmacological interventions—such as positional changes or hydration management—that may help correct labor abnormalities and prevent unnecessary prolongation.29

The management of prolonged labor requires a balanced approach. While selective episiotomy retains clinical value for specific indications under continuous monitoring, its application should remain judicious and individualized.

Although our matched analysis did not demonstrate a statistically significant reduction in overall labor duration with the LDRP model (P=0.357), the care environment it provides—characterized by enhanced comfort, privacy, and continuous midwifery support—may promote maternal relaxation and reduce psychological stress. This could potentially foster a more physiological labor pattern. The model’s capacity to indirectly support optimal labor progression, even without shortening total duration, merits further investigation.

Future studies should aim to elucidate the specific mechanisms through which integrated care models like LDRP may influence labor dynamics and to better define their role within comprehensive, evidence-based labor management strategies.

Advantages and Limitations

This study contributes to the field through its analysis of a well-characterized cohort and the application of rigorous propensity score matching. However, limitations should be acknowledged. First, as a single-center study, the generalizability of our findings may be limited. Second, the exclusive reliance on objective clinical metrics, without standardized psychological assessments, constrains the understanding of psychosocial dimensions. Finally, despite propensity score matching, residual confounding from unmeasured variables (eg, socioeconomic status, seasonal variations) remains possible. Future research needs to further validate the efficacy of the LDRP model through prospective cohorts, pragmatic clinical trials, or mixed-method designs, while controlling for a broader range of confounding factors, and to thoroughly investigate the specific mechanisms underlying its benefits. This will provide higher-level evidence for evidence-based decision-making in obstetric nursing practices.

Conclusions

The LDRP model represents a high-end, personalized, and family-centered approach to maternity care. This study suggests that the LDRP model is associated with a lower incidence of postpartum hemorrhage and assisted vaginal delivery, indicating its potential to improve maternal childbirth experience and postpartum recovery.

As one of the larger studies on LDRP delivery, our findings offer relevant insights for clinical practice. Future multicenter studies incorporating patient-reported outcomes, such as satisfaction and psychosocial metrics, are warranted to better inform clinical implementation, optimize obstetric care, and further reduce childbirth-related complications.

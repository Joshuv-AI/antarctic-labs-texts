On a mixed problem for the nonstationary Stokes system in an angle
Jürgen Rossmann

Abstract




1
Introduction

Let
K
be an angle with aperture
\alpha
,
K=\{x=(x_{1},x_{2}):\ 0<r<\infty,\ 0<\varphi<\alpha\}
, where
r
,
\varphi
denote the polar
coordinates of the point
x
. The sides of
K
are the half-lines
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
,
where
\varphi=0
on
\Gamma_{1}
and
\varphi=\alpha
on
\Gamma_{2}
.
The present paper deals with a mixed initial-boundary value problem for the nonstationary Stokes system in
K
,
i. e., we consider the problem

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\mathfrak{u}}}{\partial t}-2\,\mbox{Div}\,\varepsilon({\mathfrak{u}})+\nabla{\mathfrak{p}}={\mathfrak{f}},\quad-\nabla\cdot{\mathfrak{u}}={\mathfrak{g}}\ \mbox{ in }Q_{K}=K\times(0,\infty),

(1)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{u}}(x,t)=0\ \mbox{ for }x\in\Gamma_{1},\ t>0,\ -{\mathfrak{p}}n+2\,\varepsilon_{n}({\mathfrak{u}})={\mathfrak{h}}\ \mbox{ for }x\in\Gamma_{2},\ t>0,

(2)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{u}}(x,0)=0\ \mbox{ for }x\in K.

(3)

Here,
n
is the exterior normal to
\Gamma_{2}
,
\varepsilon({\mathfrak{u}})
denotes the strain tensor with the elements

\varepsilon_{i,j}({\mathfrak{u}})=\frac{1}{2}\,\Big(\frac{\partial{\mathfrak{u}}_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}+\frac{\partial{\mathfrak{u}}_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}\Big),\quad i,j=1,\ldots,N,

\mbox{Div}\,\varepsilon({\mathfrak{u}})
is the vector with the components
\partial_{x_{1}}\varepsilon_{i,1}+\cdots+\partial_{x_{N}}\varepsilon_{i,N}
,
i=1,\ldots,N
, i. e.,
2\,\mbox{Div}\,\varepsilon({\mathfrak{u}})=\Delta{\mathfrak{u}}+\nabla\nabla\cdot{\mathfrak{u}}
, and
\varepsilon_{n}({\mathfrak{u}})=\varepsilon({\mathfrak{u}})\,n
.

The Dirichlet and the Neumann problems for the Stokes system in an angle were already studied in
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
10
]
.
As there, the major part of the present paper deals with the parameter-depending problem

\displaystyle s\,u-\Delta u-\nabla\nabla\cdot u+\nabla p=f,\quad-\nabla\cdot u=g\mbox{ in }K,

(4)

\displaystyle u=0\mbox{ on }\Gamma_{1},\quad-pn+2\,\varepsilon_{n}(u)=h\mbox{ on }\Gamma_{2},

(5)

which arises after the Laplace transformation with respect to the time
t
. We show
that this problem has a unique solution
(u,p)\in(V_{\beta}^{2}(K)\cap V_{\beta}^{0}(K))\times V_{\beta}^{1}(K)
for arbitrary

f\in V_{\beta}^{0}(K)
,
g\in V_{\beta}^{1}(K)\cap(V_{-\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{2}))^{*}
,
h\in V_{\beta}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{2})\cap V_{\beta}^{0}(\Gamma_{2})

if
\alpha<\pi
and
\beta
satisfies the inequalities given in Theorem
4.1
. Furthermore, we obtain the estimate

\displaystyle\|u\|_{V_{\beta}^{2}(K)}+|s|\,\|u\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(K)}+\|p\|_{V_{\beta}^{1}(K)}\leq c\,\Big(\|f\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(K)}+\|g\|_{V_{\beta}^{1}(K)}

\displaystyle\quad+|s|\,\|g\|_{V_{-\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{2}))^{*}}+\|h\|_{V_{\beta}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{2})}+|s|^{1/4}\,\|h\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(\Gamma_{2})}\Big)

(6)

for this solution, where the constant
c
is independent of
f,g,h
and
s
. Here
V_{\beta}^{l}(K)
is the weighted Sobolev space of all functions
(vector functions)
u
on
K
such that
r^{\beta-l+|\alpha|}\partial^{\alpha}u\in L_{2}(K)
for
|\alpha|\leq l
.
Using the properties of the Laplace transform, we prove the
existence and uniqueness of a solution
({\mathfrak{u}},{\mathfrak{p}})
of the problem (
1
)–(
3
), where

{\mathfrak{u}}\in L_{2}(0,\infty;V_{\beta}^{2}(K))
,
\partial_{t}{\mathfrak{u}}\in L_{2}(0,\infty;V_{\beta}^{0}(K))
and

{\mathfrak{p}}\in L_{2}(0,\infty;V_{\beta}^{1}(K))
.

2
The mixed problem for the stationary Stokes system

We consider the problem (
4
), (
5
) in the case
s=0
, i.e.,

\displaystyle-\Delta u-\nabla\nabla\cdot u+\nabla p=f,\quad-\nabla\cdot u=g\mbox{ in }K,

(7)

\displaystyle u=0\mbox{ on }\Gamma_{1},\quad-pn+2\,\varepsilon_{n}(u)=h\mbox{ on }\Gamma_{2},

(8)

Weighted spaces

For nonnegative integer
l
and real
\beta
, we define the weighted Sobolev spaces
V_{\beta}^{l}(K)

as the sets of all functions (or vector functions) with finite norm

\|u\|_{V_{\beta}^{l}(K)}=\Big(\int_{K}r^{2(\beta-l+|\alpha|)}\,\big|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u(x)\big|^{2}\,dx\Big)^{1/2}

(9)

By
V_{\beta}^{l-1/2}(\Gamma_{j})
, we denote the trace space on
\Gamma_{j}
for
V_{\beta}^{l}(K)
,
l\geq 1
, which is equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{V_{\beta}^{l-1/2}(\Gamma_{j})}=\inf\Big\{\|v\|_{V_{\beta}^{l}(K)}:\ v\in V_{\beta}^{l}(K),\ v=u\mbox{ on }\Gamma_{j}\Big\}.

Furthermore,
V_{\beta}^{l}(K,\Gamma_{j})
,
l\geq 1
, is defined as the subspace of all
u\in V_{\beta}^{l}(K)
such that
u=0
on
\Gamma_{j}
.

The operator pencil generated by the mixed problem

Let
\lambda
be a complex number. We consider the operator

\displaystyle{\cal L}(\lambda):\ \Big(\begin{array}[]{c}U\\
P\end{array}\Big)\to\left(\begin{array}[]{c}r^{2-\lambda}\big(-\Delta(r^{\lambda}U(\varphi))-\nabla\nabla\cdot(r^{\lambda}U(\varphi))+\nabla(r^{\lambda-1}P(\varphi)\big)\\
-r^{1-\lambda}\,\nabla\cdot(r^{\lambda}U(\varphi))\\
U(0)\\
-P(\alpha)n+2r^{1-\lambda}\varepsilon_{n}(r^{\lambda}U(\varphi)|_{\varphi}=\alpha\end{array}\right)

which maps the space
W^{2}((0,\alpha))\times W^{1}((0,\alpha))
into
L_{2}((0,\alpha))\times W^{1}((0,\alpha))\times{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
.
As is known (see, e. g.,
[
9
]
) the eigenvalues of the pencil
{\cal L}
are the solutions of the equation

\lambda^{2}\,\sin 2\alpha-\cos^{2}(\lambda\alpha)=0.

(11)

Let
\lambda_{1}(\alpha)
be the smallest positive solution of the equation (
11
). This number is also the solution
with smallest positive real part of the equation (
11
). One can easily prove that
\lambda_{1}(\alpha)>\frac{1}{2}
if
\alpha<\pi
.

Existence of strong and weak solutions of the problem (
7
), (
8
)

Obviously, the operator
(u,p)\to(f,g,h)
of the problem (
7
), (
8
) realizes a linear and continuous mapping

\mbox{from }X_{\beta}=V_{\beta}^{2}(K,\Gamma_{1})\times V_{\beta}^{1}(K)\quad\mbox{into}\quad Y_{\beta}=V_{\beta}^{0}(K)\times V_{\beta}^{1}(K)\times V_{\beta}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{2}).

We denote this operator by
A_{\beta}
. Besides, one can consider weak (variational) solutions of
the problem (
7
), (
8
), i.e, pairs
(u,p)\in V_{\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{1})\times V_{\beta}^{0}(K)
which satisfy
the equations

b_{0}(u,\overline{v})-\int p\,\nabla\cdot\overline{v}\,dx=(F,v)_{K}\ \mbox{for all }v\in V_{-\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{1}),\ \ -\nabla\cdot u=g\mbox{ in }K,

(12)

where

b_{0}(u,v)=2\int_{K}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\varepsilon_{i,j}(u)\,\varepsilon_{i,j}(v)\,dx,

g\in V_{\beta}^{0}(K)
,
F
is a linear and continuous functional on
V_{-\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{1})
and
(\cdot,\cdot)_{K}
denotes the extension of
the sesquilinear form

(F,v)_{K}=\int_{K}F\cdot\overline{v}\,dx

(13)

to pairs
(F,v)\in(V_{-\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{1}))^{*}\times V_{-\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{1})
.
The following theorem can be found, e.g., in
[
8
, Theorem 1.2.5]
).

Theorem 2.1

1.

A_{\beta}
is an isomorphism if there are no solutions of the equation (
11
) on the line
\mbox{Re}\,\lambda=\beta-1
.

2.

If
(u,p)\in X_{\beta}
,
A_{\beta}(u,p)\in Y_{\beta}\cap Y_{\gamma}
and the closed strip between the lines

\mbox{Re}\,\lambda=\beta-1
and
\mbox{Re}\,\lambda=\gamma-1
is free of solutions of the equation (
11
),
then
(u,p)\in X_{\gamma}
.

3.

Suppose that the line
\mbox{Re}\,\lambda=\beta
is free of solutions of the equation (
11
). Then the problem
(
12
) has a unique solution
(u,p)\in V_{\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{1})\times V_{\beta}^{0}(K)
for arbitrary

F\in(V_{-\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{1}))^{*}
and
g\in V_{\beta}^{0}(K)
. If moreover
g\in V_{\beta+1}^{1}(K)
and the functional
F
has
the form

(F,v)_{K}=\int_{K}f\cdot\overline{v}\,dx+\int_{\Gamma_{2}}h\cdot\overline{v}\,dr\ \mbox{ for all }v\in V_{-\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{1}),

where
f\in V_{\beta+1}^{0}(K)
and
h\in V_{\beta+1}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{2})
, then
(u,p)\in X_{\beta+1}
and
A_{\beta+1}(u,p)=(f,g,h)
.

Weak solutions of the parameter-depending problem in the angle

Now we consider the problem (
4
), (
5
) with an arbitrary complex parameter
s
.

3.1
Weighted spaces in an angle

Let
V_{\beta}^{l}(K)
be the weighted space introduced in Section 2. We set
E_{\beta}^{l}(K)=V_{\beta}^{l}(K)\cap V_{\beta}^{0}(K)
.
Note that the spaces
V_{\beta}^{l}(K)
and
E_{\beta}^{l}(K)
can be also defined as the closures of the set

C_{0}^{\infty}(\overline{K}\backslash\{0\})
with respect to the norms (
9
) and

\|u\|_{E_{\beta}^{l}(K)}=\|u\|_{V_{\beta}^{l}(K)}+\|u\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(K)}\,,

respectively. An equivalent norm in
E_{\beta}^{l}(K)
is

\|u\|=\Big(\int_{K}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq l}\big(r^{2\beta}+r^{2(\beta-l+|\alpha|)}\big)\big|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u(x)\big|^{2}\,dx\Big)^{1/2}.

For
l\geq 1
, let
E_{\beta}^{l}(K,\Gamma_{j})=V_{\beta}^{l}(K,\Gamma_{j})\cap V_{\beta}^{0}(K)
, and let
E_{\beta}^{l-1/2}(\Gamma_{j})
be the trace
space for
E_{\beta}^{l}(K)
an
\Gamma_{j}
which is equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{E_{\beta}^{l-1/2}(\Gamma_{j})}=\inf\Big\{\|v\|_{E_{\beta}^{l}(K)}:\ v\in E_{\beta}^{l}(K),\ v=u\mbox{ on }\Gamma_{j}\Big\}.

Note that the norm

\|u\|=\|u\|_{V_{\beta}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{j})}+\|u\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(\Gamma_{j})}\,,\ \mbox{ where }\|u\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(\Gamma_{j})}=\|r^{\beta}u\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma_{j})},

(14)

is equivalent to the
E_{\beta}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{j})
-norm and that
V_{\beta}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{j})\subset V_{\beta-1/2}^{0}(\Gamma_{j})
,
where
V_{\beta}^{0}(\Gamma_{j})
is the set of all functions
u
on
\Gamma_{j}
such that
r^{\beta}u\in L_{2}(\Gamma_{j})

(cf.
[
7
, Lemmas 1.4 and 1.6]
or
[
3
, Lemmas 6.1.2 and 6.5.4]
). Hence,

E_{\beta}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{j})\subset V_{\beta}^{0}(\Gamma_{j})\cap V_{\beta-1/2}^{0}(\Gamma_{j})
.

The dual spaces of
V_{\beta}^{l}(K)
and
V_{\beta}^{l}(K,\Gamma_{j})
are denoted by
(V_{\beta}^{l}(K))^{*}
and

(V_{\beta}^{l}(K,\Gamma_{j}))^{*}
, respectively. Obviously, the sesquilinear form

(u,v)_{K}=\int_{K}u\cdot\bar{v}\,dx

(15)

is continuous on
V_{\beta}^{0}(K)\times V_{-\beta}^{0}(K)
. Hence,
(V_{\beta}^{0}(K))^{*}=V_{-\beta}^{0}(K)
and

V_{l-\beta}^{0}(K)\subset(V_{\beta}^{l}(K))^{*}\subset(V_{\beta}^{l}(K,\Gamma_{j}))^{*}
for
l\geq 1
.

3.2
Solutions of the equation
\mbox{div}\,u=g

Obviously, the operator div continuously maps
V_{\beta}^{1}(K)
into
V_{\beta}^{0}(K)
. If
u\in V_{\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{1})\cap V_{\beta}^{0}(K)
and

v\in V_{-\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{2})
, then

\Big|\int_{K}(\nabla\cdot u)\,v\,dx\Big|=\Big|\int_{K}u\cdot\nabla v\,dx\Big|\leq\|u\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(K)}\ \|v\|_{V_{-\beta}^{1}(K)}

Thus, the operator div realizes a linear and continuous mapping from
E_{\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{1})
into

V_{\beta}^{0}(K)\cap(V_{-\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{2}))^{*}
. We prove that this operator is surjective.

Lemma 3.1

Suppose that
g\in V_{\beta}^{0}(K)\cap(V_{-\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{2}))^{*}
.
Then there exists a vector function
u\in E_{\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{1})
satisfying the equation

-\nabla\cdot u=g
and the estimate

\|u\|_{E_{\beta}^{1}(K)}\leq c\,\Big(\|g\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(K)}+\|g\|_{(V_{-\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{2}))^{*}}\Big)

(16)

with a constant
c
independent of
g
.

Proof.
1. First, we assume that the angle
\alpha
is sufficiently small such that
that
|\beta|
and
|\beta-1|
are less than
\pi/\alpha
and the strip
\beta\leq\mbox{Re}\,\lambda\leq\beta+1
does not contain
solutions of the equation (
11
). Since
g\in V_{\beta}^{0}(K)\cap(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{V}}\!{}_{-\beta}^{1}(K))^{*}
and the
interval
[\beta-1,\beta]
does not contain eigenvalues
j\pi/\alpha
of the operator pencil generated by the Dirichlet problem for the Laplacian,
there exists a unique solution
u_{0}\in V_{\beta}^{2}(G)\cap\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{V}}\!{}_{\beta}^{1}(K)
of the equation
\Delta u_{0}=g

in
K
. Then
U=-\nabla u_{0}
satisfies the equation
-\nabla\cdot U=g
and the estimates

\|U\|_{V_{\beta}^{1}(K)}\leq c\,\|g\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(K)}\,,\quad\|U\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(K)}\leq c\,\|g\|_{(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{V}}{}_{-\beta}^{1}(K))^{*}}\leq c\,\|g\|_{(V_{-\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{2}))^{*}}\,.

However, the vector function
U
does not necessarily satisfy the condition
U=0
on
\Gamma_{1}
. Let
\chi_{1}
be a continuously differentiable function
on the interval
[0,\alpha]
such that
\chi_{1}(0)=1
and
\chi_{1}(\alpha)=0
.
If
r,\varphi
are the polar coordinates of the point
x
, we can consider the function
\chi_{1}
, i.e. the mapping
x\to\chi_{1}(\varphi)
,
as a function on
\overline{K}\backslash\{0\}
. We define
\chi_{2}=1-\chi_{1}
. Then
\chi_{2}U\in E_{\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{1})
and

-\nabla\cdot(\chi_{2}U)=\chi_{2}g+U\cdot\nabla\chi_{1}.

We construct a vector function
w\in V_{\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{1})\cap V_{\beta}^{0}(K)
such that
-\nabla\cdot w=\chi_{1}g-U\cdot\nabla\chi_{1}
.
Since
\chi_{1}=0
on
\Gamma_{2}
and
|\nabla\chi_{1}|\leq c\,r^{-1}
, it follows that
\chi_{1}g\in(V_{-\beta}^{1}(K))^{*}
and

\|\chi_{1}g\|_{(V_{-\beta}^{1}(K))^{*}}\leq c\,\|g\|_{(V_{-\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{2}))^{*}}\,.

Furthermore,

\|U\cdot\nabla\chi_{1}\|_{(V_{-\beta}^{1}(K))^{*}}\leq c\,\|U\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(K)}\ \mbox{ and }\ \|U\cdot\nabla\chi_{1}\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(K)}\leq c\,\|U\|_{V_{\beta}^{1}(K)}\,.

By Theorem
2.1
, there exists a solution of
(w,p)\in V_{\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{1})\times V_{\beta}^{0}(K)
of the problem

b_{0}(w,v)-\int_{K}p\,\nabla\cdot v\,dx=0\ \mbox{ for all }v\in V_{-\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{1}),\quad-\nabla\cdot w=\chi_{1}g-U\cdot\nabla\chi_{1}\ \mbox{ in }K

satisfying the estimate

\|w\|_{V_{\beta}^{1}(K)}+\|p\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(K)}\leq c\,\|\chi_{1}g-U\cdot\nabla\chi_{1}\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(K)}\,.

This implies

\int_{K}w\cdot(-\Delta v-\nabla\nabla\cdot v+\nabla q)\,dx+\int_{K}(q\,\nabla\cdot w-p\nabla\cdot v)\,dx+\int_{\Gamma_{2}}u\cdot(-qn+2\varepsilon_{n}(v))\,dr=0

for all
v\in V_{1-\beta}^{2}(K,\Gamma_{1})
and
q\in V_{1-\beta}^{1}(K)
. Let
\phi
be a vector function in

V_{-\beta}^{0}(K)\cap V_{1-\beta}^{0}(K)
. By Theorem
2.1
, there exists a unique solution

(v,q)
,
v\in V_{-\beta}^{2}(K)\cap V_{1-\beta}^{2}(K)
,
q\in V_{-\beta}^{1}(K)\cap V_{1-\beta}^{1}(K)
, of the system

-\Delta v-\nabla\nabla\cdot v+\nabla q=\phi,\ \nabla\cdot v=0\ \mbox{ in }K,

with the boundary conditions
v=0
on
\Gamma_{1}
,
-qn+2\varepsilon_{n}(v)=0
on
\Gamma_{2}
. Furthermore,

\|v\|_{V_{-\beta}^{2}(K)}+\|q\|_{V_{-\beta}^{1}(K)}\leq c\,\|\phi\|_{V_{-\beta}^{0}(K)}\,.

Then we obtain

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{K}w\cdot\phi\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{K}(\chi_{1}g-U\cdot\nabla\chi_{1})\,q\,dx\Big|\leq\|\chi_{1}g-U\cdot\nabla\chi_{1}\|_{(V_{-\beta}^{1}(K))^{*}}\,\|q\|_{V_{-\beta}^{1}(K))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\,\|\chi_{1}g-U\cdot\nabla\chi_{1}\|_{(V_{-\beta}^{1}(K))^{*}}\,\|\phi\|_{V_{-\beta}^{0}(K))}

for all
\phi\in V_{-\beta}^{0}(K)\cap V_{1-\beta}^{0}(K)
. Consequently,

\|w\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(K)}\leq c\,\|\chi_{1}g-U\cdot\nabla\chi_{1}\|_{(V_{-\beta}^{1}(K))^{*}}\leq c^{\prime}\,\|g\|_{(V_{-\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{2}))^{*}}\,.

Thus
u=\chi_{2}U+w
satisfies the equation
-\nabla\cdot u=g
, the condition
u=0
on
\Gamma_{1}
and the estimate (
16
).
This proves the lemma for small
\alpha
.

2. If
\alpha
is arbitrary less than
2\pi
, then we can argue as in the proof of
[
10
, Lemma 2.6]
and
[
6
, Lemma 3.3]
.
The equation
\nabla\cdot u=-g
can be written as

\partial_{r}\,u_{r}+r^{-1}\,\big(u_{r}+\partial_{\varphi}u_{\varphi}\big)=-g(r,\varphi)\quad\mbox{for }r>0,\ 0<\varphi<\alpha.

(17)

with the polar components
u_{r},u_{\varphi}
. Let
\delta
be a small
positive number and let
v_{r}(r,\varphi)=u_{r}(r,\delta^{-1}\varphi)
,
v_{\varphi}(r,\varphi)=\delta\,u_{\varphi}(r,\delta^{-1}\varphi)
and

h(r,\varphi)=g(r,\delta^{-1}\varphi)
for
r>0
and
0<\varphi<\delta\alpha
. Then (
17
) is equivalent to

\partial_{r}\,v_{r}+r^{-1}\,\big(v_{r}+\partial_{\varphi}v_{\varphi}\big)=-h(r,\varphi)\quad\mbox{for }r>0,\ 0<\varphi<\delta\alpha.

(18)

By the first part of the proof, the equation (
18
) has a solution
(v_{r},v_{\varphi})
satisfying the estimate

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\delta\alpha}r^{2\beta-1}\Big((1+r^{2})\,\big|(v_{r},v_{\varphi})\big|^{2}+\big|\partial_{\varphi}(v_{r},v_{\varphi})\big|^{2}+r^{2}\,\big|\partial_{r}(v_{r},v_{\varphi})\big|^{2}\Big)\,d\varphi\,dr

\displaystyle\leq c\,\Big(\|h\|^{2}_{V_{\beta}^{0}(K^{\prime})}+\|h\|^{2}_{V_{-\beta}^{1}(K^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime}_{N}))^{*}}\Big),

where
K^{\prime}
is the angle
0<\varphi<\delta\alpha
and
\Gamma^{\prime}_{2}
is the half-line
\varphi=\delta\alpha
.
Then
u_{r}
and
u_{\varphi}
satisfy (
17
). Furthermore, the estimate (
16
) holds.
\Box

3.3
Existence of weak solutions

Let
b_{s}
denote the bilinear form

b_{s}(u,v)=b_{0}(u,v)+\int_{K}su\cdot v\,dx=\int_{K}\Big(su\cdot v+2\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\varepsilon_{i,j}(u)\,\varepsilon_{i,j}(v)\Big)\,dx.

Obviously,

|b_{s}(u,v)|\leq c\,\max(1,|s|)\,\|u\|_{E_{\beta}^{1}(K)}\,\|v\|_{E_{-\beta}^{1}(K)}

(19)

for arbitrary
u\in E_{\beta}^{1}(K)
,
v\in E_{-\beta}^{1}(K)
, where
c
is independent of
u,v
and
s
.
Analogously to
[
6
, Lemma 3.1]
, the following lemma holds.

Lemma 3.2

Suppose that
(u,p)\in E_{\gamma}^{2}(K,\Gamma_{1})\times V_{\gamma}^{1}(K)
and
p|_{\Gamma_{2}}\in E_{\gamma}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{2})
, where

\beta\leq\gamma\leq\beta+1
. Then the following assertions are true.

1)
u\in E_{\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{1})
,
p\in V_{\beta}^{0}(K)+V_{\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{2})
and

\|u\|_{E_{\beta}^{1}(K)}+\|p\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(K)+V_{\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{2})}\leq c\,\Big(\|u\|_{E_{\gamma}^{2}(K)}+\|p\|_{V_{\gamma}^{1}(K)}+\|p\|_{E_{\gamma}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{2})}\Big)

with a constant
c
independent of
u
and
p
.

2)
u
and
p
satisfy the equality

b_{s}(u,\bar{v})-\int_{K}p\,\nabla\cdot\bar{v}\,dx=(F,v)_{K}\quad\mbox{for all }v\in E_{-\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{1}),

(20)

where

(F,v)_{K}=\int_{K}\big(su-\Delta u-\nabla\nabla\cdot u+\nabla p\big)\cdot\bar{v}\,dx+\int_{\Gamma_{2}}\big(-pn+2\varepsilon_{n}(u)\big)\cdot\bar{v}\,dr

and

\|F\|_{(E_{-\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{1}))^{*}}\leq c\,\Big(\|u\|_{E_{\gamma}^{2}(K)}+\|p\|_{V_{\gamma}^{1}(K)}+\|p\|_{E_{\gamma}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{2})}\Big)

(21)

with a constant
c
independent of
u
and
p
.

Proof.
1) Obviously,
E_{\gamma}^{2}(K)\subset E_{\beta}^{1}(K)
for
\beta\leq\gamma\leq\beta+1
. Let
\zeta
be a smooth (two times continuously differentiable) function on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
with support in
[0,1]
which is equal to one on the interval
(0,\frac{1}{2})
, and let
\eta=1-\zeta
. The functions
\zeta,\eta
can be considered as smooth functions on
\overline{K}
if one defines
\zeta(x)=\zeta(|x|)
and
\eta(x)=\eta(|x|)
.
If
p\in V_{\gamma}^{1}(K)
and
p|_{\Gamma_{2}}\in E_{\beta}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{2})
, then there exists a function

q\in E_{\gamma}^{1}(K)
such that
q=p
on
\Gamma_{2}
, i.e.,
p-q\in V_{\gamma}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{2})
.
Since
\gamma-1\leq\beta\leq\gamma
, it follows that
\zeta(p-q)\in V_{\beta}^{0}(K)
and
\eta(p-q)\in V_{\beta}^{1}(K,\Gamma_{2})
.
Furthermore
q\in E_{\gamma}^{1}(K)\subset V_{\beta}^{0}(K)
. This means
p=p_{1}+p_{2}
,
where
p_{1}=\zeta p+\eta q=q+\zeta(p-q)\in V_{\beta}^{0}(K)
and

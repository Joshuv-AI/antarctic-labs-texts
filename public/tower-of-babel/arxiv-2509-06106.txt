Fourier Inversion on the Group of Signatures
Frank Filbir, Davide Nobile, Marco Rauscher

Abstract
The main objective of this work is to develop a framework for Fourier analysis on the group of signatures, G N ​ ( ℝ d ) G_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) . Employing Kirillov’s orbit method, we define the Fourier transform on this group via irreducible unitary representations. Our main contribution is the derivation of necessary and sufficient conditions for identifying coadjoint orbits in general position of G N ​ ( ℝ d ) G_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) . This enables the computation of the set of jump-indices of generic orbits, crucial for the Fourier inversion theorem. We also obtain an explicit construction of a polarization for any linear functional in general position, which allows a concrete description of the Fourier transform of functions on G N ​ ( ℝ d ) G_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) . This framework yields an explicit Fourier inversion formula for the group of signatures in arbitrary dimension. Furthermore, we show that the theoretical framework developed here extends naturally to a broader class of graded Lie groups, including the group generated by the truncated tensor algebra T 0 N ​ ( ℝ d ) T_{0}^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) .

Introduction

In many areas of data analysis—ranging from machine learning and hypothesis testing to the approximation of solutions of stochastic differential equations—sequential data arise naturally. Such data can often be represented within spaces of continuous functions of prescribed regularity, commonly referred to as paths. A central challenge in their analysis lies in efficiently capturing the inherent temporal order of the sequence. Rather than working directly with the path itself, it is often advantageous to employ a more compact and expressive representation. The signature map offers precisely such an encoding.

The signature can be formulated for paths with different regularity. For simplicity, we restrict ourselves to continuous paths of bounded variation.

BV([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})\coloneqq\bigg\{x\in C([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})\;\big|\;\sup_{p\in D([0,1])}\sum_{i\in p}|x_{i+1}-x_{i}|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}<\infty\bigg\},

where
D([0,1])
denotes the set of all finite partitions of the interval
[0,1]
. The truncated level
N
signature maps paths
x(\cdot)\in BV([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
to a real tensor space and is defined as follows:

S_{N}(x)_{s,t}\coloneqq\left(1,\int_{s<u<t}dx_{u},\ldots,\int_{s<u_{1}<\ldots<u_{k}<t}dx_{u_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes dx_{u_{k}}\right)\in G_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
G_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset T^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\coloneqq\bigoplus_{k=1}^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes k}
forms a subset in the tensor space. We refer to Section
2.1
for a precise definition of this notation. Moreover, the image under the truncated signature map, denoted by
G_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, forms a Lie group w.r.t. the tensor product. For the non-truncated case, signatures are denoted as
S(x)_{s,t}
and they form an infinite-dimensional Lie group in the completed tensor algebra
T((\mathbb{R}^{d}))
called
G(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

A central result in the theory of signatures is their universality, i.e., every continuous functional on a compact subset of
BV([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
can be uniformly approximated by linear functionals of the signature. Chevyrev and Lyons
Chevyrev and Lyons 2016
established that—under suitable conditions—the law of a random path is uniquely determined by its expected signature. This result was later extended by Chevyrev and Oberhauser
Chevyrev and
Oberhauser 2022
to more general paths using a so-called robust signature. These results naturally induce a distance between probability measures on the path space, called the Signature Maximum Mean Discrepancy. This metric has been employed in various applications, including as a loss function in generative models
Ni et al. 2021
and as a test statistic in nonparametric hypothesis testing
Li et al. 2024
.

In
Chevyrev and Lyons 2016
and
Li et al. 2024
the authors introduce and study an analogue of the classical characteristic function for random variables taking values on the group of signatures. Specifically, for a
G(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-valued random variable
X
, the characteristic function is defined as

\Phi_{X}(M)\coloneq\mathbb{E}[\tilde{M}(X)],

where
M
is a unitary representation of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\tilde{M}
its natural extension to
G(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The key idea in this line of work is the use of representations satisfying a point-separating property, which ensures that the associated characteristic function uniquely determines the law of the distribution.

In practical applications, one typically works with truncated signatures, as handling the full infinite sequence is often numerically infeasible. This approach is justified by the factorial decay of iterated integrals (see, e.g.,
Chevyrev and
Oberhauser 2022
), which ensures that the truncated signature maintains good approximation properties.

Therefore, it is natural to seek a deeper understanding of the truncated group
G_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the approximation behavior of signatures taking values therein. This work aims to investigate the structure of truncated signatures through the lens of harmonic analysis, establishing a foundation for decomposing functions on
G_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into components belonging to subspaces of unitary irreducible representations. In particular, the ultimate goal of this article is to specify the unitary dual
\hat{G}_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, which contains the equivalence classes of all unitary, irreducible representations of
G_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and to develop a meaningful theory of Fourier analysis on the group of truncated signatures.

The concept of Fourier transform can be generalized to functions on a non-commutative, locally compact group
G
using unitary representations. In this setting, the Fourier transform of a function
f\in L^{1}(G)
is defined as a map on the unitary dual
\hat{G}
of
G
, that assigns to each equivalence class
\pi\in\hat{G}
a linear operator defined by

\hat{f}(\pi)=\pi(f)=\int_{G}f(x)\pi(x)dx\;.

Kirillov
Kirillov 1962
showed that when
G
is a connected, simply connected nilpotent Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, its unitary dual
\hat{G}
can be parameterized by linear functionals in
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. This means that every equivalence class of linear functionals
\ell\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
can be identified with an element
\pi\in\hat{G}
denoted as
\pi_{\ell}
, where two linear functionals are equivalent whenever they lie in the same coadjoint orbit. Further, the following Fourier inversion formula holds for any Schwartz function on
G

(
Corwin and
Greenleaf 1990
, Theorem 4.3.9)

f(x)=\int_{U\cap(\mathfrak{g}^{*})_{T}}\sqrt{\operatorname{det}D(\ell)}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\pi_{\ell}(x)^{-1}\pi_{\ell}(f)\right)d\ell\;.

(1)

Here,
\mathfrak{g}^{*}\cong\mathbb{R}^{m}
denotes the dual of the Lie algebra of
G
,
T
is a subset of basis elements of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and
(\mathfrak{g}^{*})_{T}=\mathbb{R}
-span
\{e_{i}\;:\;e_{i}\in T\}
. The measure
d\ell
is the standard Lebesgue measure on
(\mathfrak{g}^{*})_{T}
, and
U
denotes the set of coadjoint orbits in general position. This brings us to the main challenge in applying the theory, which is characterizing the orbits in general position of the group
G
. To the best of our knowledge, this has only been carried out explicitly for a few specific examples; see, for example,
Corwin and
Greenleaf 1990
and
Kirillov 2004
. Moreover, explicitly constructing the representation
\pi_{\ell}
typically requires finding a
polarization
of
\ell\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, which is itself a difficult and often infeasible problem.

The main contribution of our work is Theorem
4.12
, which establishes necessary and sufficient conditions for any coadjoint orbit of
G_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to be in general position. This characterization enables the explicit computation of the set
T
and the matrix
D(l)
in (
1
); see Lemma
4.23
. Additionally, it allows us to construct a polarization explicitly for any linear functional in general position; see Corollary
4.14
. Finally, it enables us to define the Fourier transform as a function on a subspace of
g_{N}^{*}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
instead of on a set of equivalence classes. Although we focus on the group of signatures, in Corollary
4.16
and Remark
4.26
, we show that all our results and proofs remain valid when the group of signatures is replaced by a more general graded Lie group satisfying certain regularity conditions.

We now outline the structure of this paper. Section
2
is dedicated to recalling some basic definitions and results that will be used throughout. Specifically, we recall the construction of the group of signatures and some key results from the theory of nilpotent Lie groups.

In Section
3
, we develop the framework for harmonic analysis on
G_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We begin by constructing invariant measures on the group
G_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and its homogeneous spaces
G_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})/H
. We then introduce the concept of induced representations, which plays a central role in Kirillov theory. Finally, we present Kirillov’s orbits method in our context, which provides a complete description of the unitary dual of
G_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Section
4
is dedicated to the development of Fourier analysis for the group of signatures. We introduce the notion of orbits in general position, which is key to the formulation of the inversion theorem. The central result of this chapter is Theorem
4.12
, which gives a complete characterization of these orbits for the group
G_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for arbitrary
N
and
d
. This characterization enables the computation of the general dimensions and jump indices of the coadjoint orbits of
G_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, as well as the explicit construction of a polarization
\mathfrak{h}
for any
l\in\mathfrak{g}_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{*}
in general position.

Preliminaries

2.1
Signatures

We begin by recalling the construction of the group of signatures. For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, the
N
-step tensor algebra over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is defined as the real algebra

T^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\coloneqq\bigoplus_{k=0}^{N}\;(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes k}\;,

(2)

where
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes k}
denotes the
k
-fold tensor product of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes 0}=\mathbb{R}
. This space becomes an algebra under component-wise addition, and a product defined by the tensor multiplication

p_{k}(g\otimes h)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}(g)\otimes p_{k-i}(h),\ \forall k\in\{1,\dots,N\}

(3)

for all
g,h\in T^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
p_{k}(g)=g^{k}\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes k}
denotes the projection to the
k
-th component.
We also define the subsets

\displaystyle T_{0}^{N}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)=\left\{g\in T^{N}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right):p_{0}(g)=0\right\}\cong\bigoplus_{k=1}^{N}\;(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes k}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle T_{1}^{N}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)=1+T_{0}^{N}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)=\left\{g\in T^{N}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right):p_{0}(g)=1\right\}\;.

and, on these spaces, we define the exponential and logarithmic maps:

\displaystyle\exp:\;

\displaystyle T_{0}^{N}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\rightarrow T_{1}^{N}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right),\quad X\mapsto 1+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{X^{\otimes k}}{k!}

(4)

\displaystyle\log:\;

\displaystyle T_{1}^{N}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\rightarrow T_{0}^{N}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right),\quad(1+X)\mapsto\sum_{k=1}^{N}(-1)^{k+1}\frac{X^{\otimes k}}{k}\;.

(5)

Then,
\exp
is a bijection with inverse given by
\log
(see
(
Lyons et al. 2007
, Lemma 2.21)
. Further, both
\exp
and

are continuously differentiable, as they are essentially polynomial maps in the coordinates of

, and therefore,

is a global diffeomorphism

. The set

becomes a Lie group with group operation given by the tensor product defined in (
3
) restricted to

; see
(
Friz and
Victoir 2010

. Moreover,

becomes a nilpotent Lie algebra, by defining the Lie bracket operation

[\cdot,\cdot]:T_{0}^{N}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\times T_{0}^{N}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\to T_{0}^{N}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right),\;[X,Y]=X\otimes Y-Y\otimes X

(
Friz and
Victoir 2010

Lemma 2.1
.

T_{1}^{N}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)

Definition 2.2

\int_{0<t_{1}<\dots<t_{k}<1}dx_{t_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes dx_{t_{k}}\coloneqq\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}\left(\int_{0<t_{1}<\dots<t_{k}<1}dx_{t_{1}}^{i_{1}}\dots dx_{t_{k}}^{i_{k}}\right)(e_{i_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes e_{i_{k}})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes k}\;.

(6)

Theorem 2.3

Generalities on Nilpotent Lie Groups

\log\left(\exp X\cdot\exp Y\right)=X+Y+\frac{1}{2}[X,Y]+(\textit{linear combination of commutators in }X\text{ and }Y)

Proof.

(

The bijectivity of the exponential map allows us to identify
G_{N}
with
\mathfrak{g}_{N}
and, in particular, to transfer a coordinate system on the algebra to one on the group. Specifically, let
B=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
be a basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{N}
and
x\in G_{N}
. Then, there is a unique
X\in\mathfrak{g}
such that
x=\exp(X)
, and since
B

\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
X=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}X_{i}
. Hence,

x=\exp(X)=\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}X_{i}\right)

so that we can identify
x
with the vector
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This coordinate system is often called

and can be used to construct a Haar measure on
G_{N}
(see section
3.1
). However, it is not the only way to transfer coordinates from
\mathfrak{g}_{N}
to
G_{N}
. First, we need a definition.

Definition 2.6
.

(Strong Malcev basis)
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a nilpotent Lie algebra and

\mathfrak{g}
. We say that

is a strong Malcev basis for
\mathfrak{g}

:

i)

\mathbb{R}
-
\operatorname{span}\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}

\mathfrak{g}
.

ii)

\mathbb{R}
-
\operatorname{span}\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}

\mathfrak{g}
.

A basis that only satisfies condition

is called a weak Malcev basis for
\mathfrak{g}
. Note that by subalgebra we always mean a Lie subalgebra, i.e. a subalgebra closed under Lie bracket operations.

Example 2.7
.

Let

B=\{X_{1}^{N},\ldots,X_{m_{N}}^{N},X_{1}^{N-1},\ldots,X_{m_{N-1}}^{N-1},\ldots,X_{1}^{1},\ldots,X_{m_{1}}^{1}\}\;,

Harmonic Analysis on the Group of Signatures

3.1
Measures on nilpotent Lie groups

Proof.

(

\phi(\alpha_{n},\ldots,\alpha_{k+1})=\exp(\alpha_{n}X_{n})\cdots\exp(\alpha_{k+1}X_{k+1})\cdot H=\gamma(\alpha_{n},\ldots,\alpha_{k+1},0,\ldots,0)\cdot H\;,

where
\gamma
is the function from Proposition
2.8
. Then,
\phi
is a diffeomorphism and if
\lambda
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathfrak{g}_{N}/\mathfrak{h}=\mathbb{R}^{n-k}
then
\phi_{*}(\lambda)
is a
G
-invariant measure on
G_{N}/H
.

Proof.

See
(
Corwin and
Greenleaf 1990
, Theorem 1.2.12)
.

3.2
The inducing construction

Recall that a unitary representation
\pi
of a topological group
G
is a group homomorphism from
G
into the group of unitary operators on some Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
, denoted by
U(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})
, which is continuous with respect to the strong operator topology. In other words,
\pi
is a map
G\to U(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})
such that for all
x,y\in G
,
\pi(xy)=\pi(x)\pi(y)
,
\pi(x^{-1})=\pi(x)^{-1}
and
x\mapsto\pi(x)v
is continuous for all
v\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
.

Two unitary representations
\pi_{1}:G\to U(\mathcal{H}_{\pi_{1}}),\pi_{2}:G\to U(\mathcal{H}_{\pi_{2}})
of
G
are said to be
equivalent
, written
\pi_{1}\cong\pi_{2}
, if there is a unitary operator
U:\mathcal{H}_{\pi_{1}}\to\mathcal{H}_{\pi_{2}}
, such that for all
x\in G

\pi_{2}(x)=U\pi_{1}(x)U^{-1}\;.

Further, a unitary representation
\pi:G\to U(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})
of
G
is called
irreducible
if there is no
\pi
-invariant subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
. That is, there is no subspace
V\subsetneq\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
with
\pi(V)\subseteq V
.

We now introduce the concept of induced representations, i.e. a representation of a group
G
constructed from a representation of a subgroup
H\subseteq G
. For simplicitly, we only present this construction in the situation relevant for our purpose. We refer to
Folland 2015
for a more general and detailed introduction to induced representation.

Let
\ell\in\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{\ell}\subset\mathfrak{g}_{N}
a subalgebra such that
\ell([X,Y])=0
for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{h}_{\ell}
. Then, by the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula (Theorem
2.5
),

\ell\left(\log(\operatorname{exp}X\cdot\operatorname{exp}Y)\right)=\ell(X)+\ell(Y)

for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{h}_{\ell}
. Hence, for
H_{\ell}=\operatorname{exp}(\mathfrak{h}_{\ell})\subset G_{N}
,

\chi_{\ell}:H_{\ell}\to\mathbb{C},\;\chi(\operatorname{exp}X)=e^{i\ell(X)}

is a one-dimensional representation of the subgroup
H_{\ell}
, since

\sigma(\exp X\cdot\exp Y)=e^{i\ell\left(\log(\operatorname{exp}X\cdot\;\operatorname{exp}Y)\right)}=e^{i\ell(\operatorname{exp}X)+i\ell(\operatorname{exp}Y)}=\sigma(\exp X)\sigma(\exp Y)\;.

and the map is clearly continuous. Note that we interpret the complex number
e^{i\ell(X)}
as a unitary operator
\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
,
z\mapsto e^{i\ell(X)}z
.
We now define
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
to be the space containing all functions
f:G_{N}\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\displaystyle i)\;f(\exp X\cdot\exp Y)=e^{i\ell(Y)}f(\exp X)\text{ for all }X\in\mathfrak{g}_{N},Y\in\mathfrak{h}_{\ell}

\displaystyle i

\displaystyle i)\;\int_{G_{N}/H_{\ell}}|f(x)|^{2}d\mu(xH)<\infty

where
\mu=\phi_{*}(\lambda)
is the
G_{N}
-invariant measure from Theorem
3.4
. Note that, by definition, for any
f,g\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
,

f(x)\overline{g(x)}
depends only on the cosets in
G_{N}/H_{\ell}
, since

f(xh)\overline{g(xh)}=e^{i\ell(\log h)}f(x)e^{-i\ell(\log h)}\overline{g(x)}=f(x)\overline{g(x)}

for all
x\in G_{N}
and
h\in H_{\ell}
. Hence, the integral in
ii)
is indeed well defined and, more generally, the map

(f,g):G_{N}/H_{\ell}\to\mathbb{R},\quad(f,g)(xH_{\ell})=f(x)\overline{g(x)}

is a well defined function on
G_{N}/H_{\ell}
. With this observation the space
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
becomes a complete Hilbert space, by defining the following inner product on it
(
Folland 2015
, Chapter 6)

\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{G_{N}/H_{\ell}}(f,g)(xH_{\ell})d\mu(xH_{\ell})=\int_{G_{N}/H_{\ell}}f(x)\overline{g(x)}d\mu(xH_{\ell})\;.

Finally, the induced representation
\operatorname{ind}_{H_{\ell}}^{G_{N}}(\sigma)
of
G_{N}
, induced by the character
\chi_{l}
is the unitary representation defined by

\operatorname{ind}_{H_{\ell}}^{G}(\chi_{l}):G_{N}\to U(\mathcal{F}_{\ell}),x\mapsto L_{x}

where
L_{x}
is the left shift operator defined by
(L_{x}f)(y)=f(x^{-1}y)
. Note that this representation is indeed unitary, since by
G_{N}
invariance of
\mu
we have that

\displaystyle\left\langle L_{y}f,L_{y}g\right\rangle=\int_{G_{N}/H_{\ell}}f(y^{-1}x)\overline{g(y^{-1}x)}d\mu(xH_{\ell})=\int_{G_{N}/H_{\ell}}f(x)\overline{g(x)}d\mu(xH_{\ell})=\left\langle f,g\right\rangle

for all
y\in G_{N}
and
f,g\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
.
The functions in
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
have the following interesting geometric interpretation. Using the function
\gamma
from Proposition
2.8
as a coordinate map, any function in
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
satisfies

f(\gamma(x,h))=f(\gamma(x,0)\gamma(0,h))=e^{i\ell(h)}f(\gamma(x,0))

for all
(x,h)\in\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}_{\ell}\times\mathfrak{h}_{\ell}
. Hence, we see that geometrically,
f
is a complex-valued function, such that its norm only depends on the equivalence classes of
G_{N}/H_{\ell}
or, using
\gamma
, on the elements of
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}_{\ell}
, while the elements in
H_{\ell}=\gamma(\mathfrak{h}_{\ell})=\exp(\mathfrak{h}_{\ell})
only determine the rotation of the function value.

In particular, any function
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
defines a function
\tilde{f}\in L^{2}(G/H_{\ell})
via
\tilde{f}(\gamma(x,0)H_{\ell})\coloneqq f(\gamma(x,0))
and, conversely, every function
\tilde{f}\in L^{2}(G/H_{\ell})
can be extended to a function
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
by defining
f(\gamma(x,h))\coloneqq e^{i\ell(h)}f(\gamma(x,0))
.

The reason for our interest in this type of induced representation is that, by Kirillov’s orbits method, these representations exhaust, up to equivalence, all irreducible unitary representations of
G_{N}
. Hence, they provide a method to parametrize the unitary dual
\hat{G}_{N}
using linear functionals in
\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}
.

3.3
Kirillov theory

We present Kirillov theory in its full generality, as restricting to the special case of the signatures group does not simplify the statements in any way. Kirillov originally established the results presented in this section in
Kirillov 1962
and a more self-contained exposition also appears in his book
Kirillov 2004
. Our exposition mostly follows the one provided by Corwin and Greenleaf in
Corwin and
Greenleaf 1990
. The proofs of the results presented here can be found in any of the mentioned sources.

In the following,
G
is always assumed to be a connected, simply connected, nilpotent Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. We begin with a definition.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
\ell\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Then, we say that a sub-algebra
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}
is subordinate to
\ell
if
\ell([X,Y])=0
for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{h}
.

We say that
\mathfrak{h}
is a maximal subordinate subalgebra for
\ell
, if
\mathfrak{h}
is subordinate to
\ell
and for any other subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
subordinate to
\ell
,
\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}\leq\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{h}
. A maximal subordinate subalgebra is also called a polarization for
\ell
.

Given a functional
\ell\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and a (maximal) subordinate subalgebra
\mathfrak{m}
of
\mathfrak{g}
, we set
M=\operatorname{exp}\mathfrak{m}
and

\chi_{\ell,M}:M\to\mathbb{C},\;\chi_{\ell,M}(\operatorname{exp}X)=e^{i\ell(X)}\;.

Then, we have seen in the previous section that
\chi_{\ell,M}
defines a unitary representation of the subgroup
M\subseteq G
.
The next theorem guarantees the existence of a maximal subordinate subalgebra for any
\ell\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Note that while all polarizations of a functional
\ell
have the same dimension, there might be in general more than one choice of polarization for
\ell
. However, the next theorem also shows that, up to equivalence, the choice of maximal subordinate subalgebra, makes no difference to the induced representation.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\ell\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be a linear functional. Then, there is a maximal subordinate subalgebra
\mathfrak{m}
of
\ell
, and for
M=\exp(\mathfrak{m})
,
\operatorname{ind}_{M}^{G}(\chi_{\ell,M})
is irreducible. Further, if
\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
are two maximal subordinate subalgebras of
\ell
, then
\operatorname{ind}_{M}^{G}(\chi_{\ell,M})\cong\operatorname{ind}_{M^{\prime}}^{G}(\chi_{\ell,M^{\prime}})
.

Remark 3.7
.

Since we are only interested in the equivalence classes of unitary representations, by Theorem
3.6
we can simplify our notation and write
\pi_{\ell}
instead of
\operatorname{ind}_{M}^{G}(\chi_{\ell,M})
for the unitary irreducible representation induced by the functional
\ell\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

The following theorem shows that every irreducible unitary representation of a simply connected nilpotent Lie group is induced from a one-dimensional representation of some subgroup. Moreover, it characterizes the equivalence classes of irreducible unitary representations using the coadjoint action defined in Definition
2.9
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible unitary representation of
G
. Then, there is an
\ell\in g^{*}
such that
\pi_{\ell}\cong\pi
. Further, if
\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, then
\pi_{\ell}\cong\pi_{\ell^{\prime}}
if and only if
\operatorname{Ad}^{*}(G)\ell=\operatorname{Ad}^{*}(G)\ell^{\prime}
, i.e. if and only if
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
belong to the same coadjoint orbit in
g^{*}
.

Remark 3.9
.

Theorem
3.8
can be summarized as follows.
Let
\hat{G}
be the unitary dual of
G
, i.e. the set of equivalence classes of all irreducible unitary representations of
G
. Then,
\hat{G}
is in one-to-one correspondence with the set of coadjoint orbits
O=\{\mathcal{O}_{\ell}\;|\;\ell\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}\}
, where
\mathcal{O}_{\ell}=\operatorname{Ad}^{*}(G)\ell
. Hence, if we define the equivalence relation
\ell\cong\ell^{\prime}:\iff\mathcal{O}_{\ell}=\mathcal{O}_{\ell^{\prime}}
, then by Kirillov’s theory we know that

\hat{G}=\{[\ell]\;|\;\ell\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}\}\;.

Since the computation of coadjoint orbits will play a crucial role in establishing our results, the next two examples are intended to illustrate the concept and serve as simplified versions of the more general computations presented in the next sections.

Example 3.10
.

Let
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}\}

and set

\displaystyle((\operatorname{Ad}^{*}\exp X)\ell)(X_{i})=\ell(\operatorname{Ad}(\exp-X)X_{i})=\ell\left(X_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}g_{j}X_{i,j}\right)=\alpha_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}g_{j}\beta_{i,j}

\displaystyle((\operatorname{Ad}^{*}\exp X)\ell)(X_{i,j})=\ell(\operatorname{Ad}(\exp-X)X_{i,j})=\ell(X_{i,j})=\beta_{i,j}

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\ell}=(\operatorname{Ad}^{*}G)\ell

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{i}(\alpha_{i}+\sum_{j}g_{j}\beta_{i,j})\ell_{i}+\sum_{i,j}\beta_{i,j}\ell_{i,j}\;:\;(g_{1},\ldots,g_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\right\}

From this formula we see that a functional

\displaystyle\tilde{\alpha}_{i}=\alpha_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}g_{j}\beta_{i,j}\quad\forall i=1,\ldots,d

(7)

Note that if we define the matrix

by

,
\tilde{\alpha}=(\tilde{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{\alpha}_{d})
, then (

\displaystyle B\cdot g=\tilde{\alpha}-\alpha\quad\text{for some }g\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\;.

In particular, if the matrix

. Hence, in this case, the orbit of
\ell
is a

and it is simply given by

(\operatorname{Ad}^{*}G)\ell=\sum_{i,j}\beta_{i,j}\ell_{i,j}+\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\alpha_{i}\ell_{i}\;|\;\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}\in\mathbb{R}\right\}=\sum_{i,j}\beta_{i,j}\ell_{i,j}+\mathbb{R}\text{-span }\{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{d}\}\;.

.

We now consider the group

4
A Fourier inversion formula for the group of Signatures

In this section, we present the main contribution of our work. The first part is dedicated to orbits in general position and, specifically, to the proof of Theorem
4.12
. In the second part, we use this result to obtain an explicit description of both the Fourier transform and its inversion on
G_{N}
.

4.1
Orbits in general position

Orbits in general position play a crucial role in the Fourier inversion formula, as they are the only ones that need to be considered to reconstruct a function from its transform. The main result of this section, Theorem
4.12
, provides necessary and sufficient conditions to characterize the general orbits of
G_{N}
. As a consequence, we also obtain an explicit construction of a polarization for any
\ell\in\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}
in general position; see Corollary
4.14
.

Let
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\}
be a strong Malcev basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{N}
. Then, the dual space
\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}
is spanned, as a vector space, by the corresponding dual basis
\{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{m}\}
. For any
\ell\in\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}
the coadjoint orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\ell}=(\operatorname{Ad}^{*}G_{N})\ell
of
\ell
is a closed submanifold of
\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}
, so that, in particular, it has a well defined dimension. For any
j=1,\ldots,m
we set

\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}(j)=\mathbb{R}\text{-span}\{\ell_{j+1},\ldots,\ell_{m}\}

so that
\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}/\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}(j)=\mathbb{R}
-span
\{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{j}\}
. We define the dimension of
\mathcal{O}_{\ell}
in
\mathfrak{g}^{*}/\mathfrak{g}^{*}(j)
as the dimension of
\mathcal{O}_{\ell}/\mathfrak{g}^{*}(j)\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}/\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}(j)
.

Remark 4.1
.

Let
\{X_{1}^{N},\ldots,X_{m_{N}}^{N},X_{1}^{N-1},\ldots,X_{m_{N-1}}^{N-1},\ldots,X_{1}^{1},\ldots,X_{m_{1}}^{1}\}
such that for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
the elements
X_{1}^{k},\ldots,X_{m_{k}}^{k}
are a basis of the
k
-th layer of
\mathfrak{g}_{N}
. Then, for any
k=1,\ldots,N
and
m=1,\ldots,m_{k}
we have

\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}/\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}(k,m)=\mathbb{R}\text{-span}\{l_{1}^{N},\ldots,l_{m}^{k}\}\;.

Definition 4.2
.

(Orbit in general position)
Let
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\}
be a strong Malcev basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{N}
and
\{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{m}\}
the corresponding dual basis. Let
\ell\in\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}
and
\mathcal{O}_{\ell}
its coadjoint orbit.

i)

The orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\ell}
has maximal dimension in
\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}/\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}(j)
if for any other
\ell^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}
we have
\operatorname{dim}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell^{\prime}}/\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}(j))\leq\operatorname{dim}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell}/\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}(j))
.

ii)

The orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\ell}
is in general position or is a generic orbit, if it is an orbit of maximal dimension in
\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}/\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}(j)
for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
. Similarly, we will say that
\ell
is in general position if its orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\ell}
is.

Example 4.3
.

As in Example
3.10
we consider
\mathfrak{g}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\{X_{1,2},X_{1,3},\ldots,X_{d-1,d},X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\alpha_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}g_{j}\beta_{i,j})\ell_{i}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq d}\beta_{i,j}\ell_{i,j}\;:\;(g_{1},\ldots,g_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\right\}\;.

\displaystyle\dim\left(\mathcal{O}_{\ell}/\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}(m)\right)

\displaystyle=\dim\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\alpha_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}g_{j}\beta_{i,j})\ell_{i}+\sum_{i,j}\beta_{i,j}\ell_{i,j}\;:\;(g_{1},\ldots,g_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\right\}

\displaystyle=\dim\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}g_{j}\beta_{i,j}\right)\ell_{i}\;:\;(g_{1},\ldots,g_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\right\}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{rank}B_{\ell}(m)\;,

where
B_{\ell}(m)\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
is defined by
(B_{\ell}(m))_{i,j}=\beta_{i,j}=\ell([X_{i},X_{j}])
. By definition, an orbit is in general position if the dimension of
\mathcal{O}_{\ell}/\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}(m)
is maximal for all
m=1,\ldots,d
. From the previous computation, this condition is equivalent to requiring that the rank of the matrix
B_{\ell}(m)
is maximal for each

. In particular, if there exists an element
\ell
such that

is invertible, then

\dim\left(\mathcal{O}_{\ell}/\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}(m)\right)=m\quad\text{for all}\quad m=1,\ldots,d

and any other
\tilde{\ell}\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
is in general position if and only if
B_{\tilde{\ell}}(d)
is invertible. However, at this point, it is not clear whether such an
\ell
always exists or, more generally, what the maximal attainable rank of the matrix

is. The next proposition addresses this problem for arbitrary dimensions.

The following result is a straightforward consequence of the previous proposition and will be useful in our characterization of the orbits in general position.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
\{X_{1}^{N},\ldots,X_{m_{N}}^{N},\ldots,X_{1}^{1},\ldots,X_{m_{1}}^{1}\}
be a strong Malcev basis for
\mathfrak{g}_{N}
such that for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
the elements
X_{1}^{k},\ldots,X_{m_{k}}^{k}
are a basis of
k
-th layer of
\mathfrak{g}_{N}
. For
k\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
and
m\in\{1,\ldots,m_{N-k}\}
define the matrix
B_{\ell}^{k}(m)\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{k}\times m}
by

(B_{\ell}^{k}(m))_{i,j}=\ell([X_{i}^{k},X_{j}^{N-k}])\quad i\in\{1,\ldots,m_{k}\},\;j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\;.

Then, for any
m\in\{1,\ldots,m_{N-k}\}

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}(k,m)\coloneqq\max_{\ell\in\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}}\left(\operatorname{rank}B_{\ell}^{k}(m)\right)=\min\left\{m_{k},m_{N-k},m\right\}\text{, if }k\neq N/2

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}(N/2,m)\coloneqq\max_{\ell\in\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}}\left(\operatorname{rank}B_{\ell}^{N/2}(m)\right)=\begin{cases}\min\left\{m_{N/2},m\right\}&\text{ if }m_{N/2}\text{ is even}\\
\min\left\{m_{N/2}-1,m\right\}&\text{ if }m_{N/2}\text{ is odd}\\
\end{cases}

and there is an
\ell\in\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}
such that
\operatorname{rank}B_{\ell}^{k}(m)=\dim(k,m)
for all
k,m
.

Further, the following hold for any
k<N/2
:

i)

\operatorname{rank}B_{\ell}^{k}(m)=\dim(k,m)
for all
m=1,\ldots,m_{N-k}
if and only if
\operatorname{rank}B_{\ell}^{k}=\dim(k,m_{k})
, i.e. if and only if
B_{\ell}^{k}
is invertible.

ii)

If
B_{\ell}^{k}
is invertible, then
\operatorname{rank}B_{\ell}^{N-k}(m)=\dim(N-k,m)
for all
m=1,\ldots,m_{k}
.

Proof.

Fix a
k<N/2
. The implication “
\Rightarrow
” in
i)
holds trivially because
m_{k}\leq m_{N-k}
.

Now, assume
\operatorname{rank}B_{\ell}^{k}=\dim(k,m_{k})
. We may assume without loss of generality that
m<m_{k}
, because if
m\geq m_{k}
, then
\operatorname{rank}B_{\ell}^{k}(m)\geq\operatorname{rank}B_{\ell}^{k}(m_{k})=\operatorname{rank}B_{\ell}^{k}=\dim(k,m_{k})=\dim(k,m)
.

For all
m<m_{k}
the matrix
B_{\ell}^{k}(m)\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{k}\times m}
is the submatrix of
B_{\ell}^{k}
containing only the first
m
columns of
B_{\ell}^{k}
. Since, by assumption,
B_{\ell}^{k}
is invertible, these columns are linearly independent so that

\operatorname{rank}B_{\ell}^{k}(m)=m
for all
m\leq m_{k}
. Hence,
\operatorname{rank}B_{\ell}^{k}(m)\geq\min\left\{m_{k},m_{N-k},m\right\}
for all
m\leq m_{k}
, which concludes the proof of
i)
.

Now, consider the matrix
B_{\ell}^{k}(m,m_{N-k})\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m_{N-k}}
containing only the first
m
rows of
B_{\ell}^{k}(m_{N-k})
. Then, it is easy to check that
B_{\ell}^{N-k}(m)=-B_{\ell}^{k}(m,m_{N-k})^{T}
.

Assuming
B_{\ell}^{k}
is invertible, then by part
i)

\operatorname{rank}B_{\ell}^{k}(m_{N-k})=\min\left\{m_{k},m_{N-k}\right\}=m_{k}
, but since
B_{\ell}^{k}(m_{N-k})\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{k}\times m_{N-k}}
this means that the rows of
B_{\ell}^{k}(m_{N-k})
must be linearly independent. Hence, we obtain that
\operatorname{rank}B_{\ell}^{N-k}(m)=\operatorname{rank}B_{\ell}^{k}(m,m_{N-k})=m=\min\left\{m_{k},m_{N-k}\right\}
, which shows
ii)
.

Note that by definition,
B_{\ell}^{k}(m)\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{k}\times m}
for any
k=1,\ldots,N
and
m=1,\ldots,m_{k}
, so that
\operatorname{rank}B_{\ell}^{k}(m)\leq\min\left\{m_{k},m\right\}=\min\left\{m_{k},m_{N-k},m\right\}
. With this observation,
i)
and
ii)
together prove in particular the first part of the corollary for all
k\neq N/2
, since, by Proposition
4.4
, we know that there is an
\ell\in\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}
such that
B_{\ell}^{k}
is invertible for all
k<N/2
. The case
k=N/2
follows easily by using the same arguments as above adapted to
k=N/2
.
∎

for all

4.2
The Fourier inversion formula

Let
\pi_{\ell}:G_{N}\to\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
be a unitary representation of
G_{N}
. Then, for any
f\in L^{1}(G_{N})
we define the continuous linear operator
\pi_{\ell}(f)
on
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
by

\pi_{\ell}(f)=\int_{G_{N}}f(x)\pi_{\ell}(x)\;dx\;.

Note that this operator is in general only weakly defined. That is, for any
u\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
the value of
\pi_{\ell}(f)u
is specified by its inner product with some arbitrary
v\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
, specifically

\langle\pi_{\ell}(f)u,v\rangle=\int_{G_{N}}f(x)\langle\pi_{\ell}(x)u,v\rangle\;dx\;.

One can easily see that
\langle\pi_{\ell}(f)u,v\rangle\leq||f||_{1}||u||||v||
for any
u,v\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
so that
\pi_{\ell}(f)
is in fact bounded with operator norm
||\pi_{\ell}(f)||\leq||f||_{1}
. The idea for generalizing the concept of a Fourier transform to the group of signatures is to consider operators of the form
\pi_{\ell}(f)
for unitary irreducible representations
\pi_{\ell}
of
G_{N}
.

Recall from Remark
3.9
that the unitary dual of
G_{N}
can be written as

\hat{G}_{N}=\left\{[\ell]\;|\;\ell\in\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}\right\}\;,

where
[\ell]
is the equivalence class determined by the relation
\ell\cong\ell^{\prime}:\iff\mathcal{O}_{\ell}=\mathcal{O}_{\ell^{\prime}}
. Using this characterization, we define the Fourier transform as a function on
{G}_{N}
as follows.

Definition 4.18
.

Let
f\in L^{1}(G_{N})
. Then, we define the Fourier transform of
f
as the operator valued mapping

\hat{f}:[\ell]\mapsto\hat{f}(\ell)=\pi_{\ell}(f)=\int_{G}f(x)\pi_{\ell}(x)\;dx\quad\text{for all }[\ell]\in\hat{G}\;.

Remark 4.19
.

Recall that the representation
\pi_{\ell}
was defined in the previous section as the representation induced by the functional
\ell
up to equivalence. Since we know that if
[\ell]=[\ell^{\prime}]
then
\pi_{\ell}\cong\pi_{\ell^{\prime}}
, the map in the previous definition is in fact well defined.

Let
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\}
be a strong Malcev basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{N}
. For
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
we let
d_{j}
be the maximal dimension of orbits in
\mathfrak{g}^{*}/\mathfrak{g}^{*}(j)
. Where
d_{m}
is the maximal dimension of orbits in
\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}
.
Let
S=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{2k}\}
with
j_{1}<\ldots<j_{2k}
be the set of indices
j
such that
d_{j}<d_{j-1}
i.e.
S
is the set of indices
j
at which the maximal dimension of orbits in
\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}/\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}(j)
increases (by one). We define
(\mathfrak{g}^{*})_{S}=\mathbb{R}
-
\operatorname{span}\{\ell_{i}\;:\;i\in S\}
and
(\mathfrak{g}^{*})_{T}=\mathbb{R}
-
\operatorname{span}\{\ell_{i}\;:\;i\in T\}
with
T=\{1,\ldots,m\}\backslash S
. Note that
(\mathfrak{g}^{*})_{S}\times(\mathfrak{g}^{*})_{T}=\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}
.

Remark 4.20
.

The reason for the interest in the set
S
defined above is that it allows a canonical choice of representative for the equivalence classes of functionals in general position in
\hat{G}_{N}=\left\{[\ell]\;|\;\ell\in\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}\right\}
. Specifically, if
\ell\in\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*}
is in general position, its orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\ell}
intersects the subspace
\{0\}\times(\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*})_{T}
in exactly one point
(
Corwin and
Greenleaf 1990
, Theorem 3.1.9)
. Hence, if
U\subseteq\hat{G}_{N}
denotes the set of functionals in general position, we have that

U\cap\hat{G}_{N}\cong U\cap(\mathfrak{g}_{N}^{*})_{T}\;.

Theorem 4.21
.

(General Fourier inversion formula)

Let,
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\}
be an orthonormal strong Malcev basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{N}
and
\{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{m}\}
the corresponding dual basis. Let
U=\left\{(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\;|\;\ell=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\ell_{i}\text{ is in general position}\right\}
be the set of functionals in general position and
S=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{2k}\}
be as above. Further, define
D(\ell)\in\mathbb{R}^{2k\times 2k}
as

(D(\ell))_{i,s}=\ell([X_{j_{i}},X_{j_{s}}])\quad\text{for }i,s\in\{1,\ldots,2k\}\;.

Then, for any Schwartz function

5.1

Definition 5.1
.

We say that a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is graded when it is endowed with a vector space decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\infty}V_{j}\quad\text{ such that }\quad\left[V_{i},V_{j}\right]\subset V_{i+j}

and all but finitely many of the
V_{j}
’s are
\{0\}
. We call the subspace
V_{j}
the
j
-th layer of
\mathfrak{g}
. A Lie group is called graded when it is a connected, simply connected Lie group whose Lie algebra is graded. Further, we say that the group is
n
-step graded if
V_{n}\neq\{0\}
and
V_{k}=0
for all
k>n
. Note that any (
n
-step) graded Lie group is in particular (
n
-step) nilpotent.

Lemma 5.2
.

(Dimension formula)
Let
d,k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, the dimension of

is given by

\operatorname{dim}[\mathbb{R}^{d}]^{k}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{n|k}\mu(n)d^{\frac{k}{n}}\;,

where the sum is taken over all divisors

of

, and

is the Möbius function defined as

\mu(n)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }n=1\\
(-1)^{k}&\text{ if }n\text{ is the product of }k\text{ distinct primes }\\
0&\text{ if }n\text{ is divisible by a square }>1\end{cases}\;.

Proof.

See
Witt 1937
.
∎

Thanks for helping light the uncharted paths ahead!
Authors: Rhonda M Johnson
Published: 2025

“In the midst of darkness, light persists.”

– Mahatma Gandhi

As another year draws to a close amidst apparently growing societal fissures, frequently shrinking resources and the latest alarming trends, optimism and hope may seem to be in short supply. A reflective seasonal pause may be in order. Our daily work was no doubt challenging in this context, yet I am reminded that together we continue to create new understandings and resources, strengthen networks, build community, support health and healing and improve systems of care. Perhaps we have also mourned the passing of beloved colleagues and programs, survived unexpected lessons and disappointments and celebrated some hard-fought successes. We must remember our progress as well as any roadblocks and setbacks, and strive to keep our focus on what we are working toward, not against. Thanks for all you do and have done to advance arctic health and well-being. You are the light that helps move us all forward!

This year was a productive one for the International Journal of Circumpolar Health, with more than 100 diverse and pan-arctic papers submitted for consideration, and more than 50 published. The Special Issue on ‘Arctic Community Perspectives on COVID-19’ was published in early 2025 and is available under the Collections tab on our IJCH website. Another Special Issue focused on ‘Culture, Language and Land in Childbirth Practice’ is well underway and should be published next year. Thanks to all our Guest Editors who make these unique and collaborative collections possible.

The scientific impact and reach of the Journal continue to grow. Our Thompson Impact Factor (IF) has more than doubled since 2015 (from .70 to 1.9 in 2024), with a five year IF of 1.8. This measure reflects the average number of citations per article. The top 20 most cited papers published since 2020 already have between 21-99 citations each, reflecting the current top range of this very positive trend. Our 2024 Cite Score (Scopus) was 2.6 reflecting the average number of citations per article in the Journal over a four-year period. In 2024, for the first time, our Journal ranked in the second quartile (Q2) in all four areas assessed: Epidemiology, Health (Social Sciences), Medicine (Miscellaneous) and Public Health, Environmental and Occupational Health, reflecting both our broad mission and the diversity of topics of interest and value to our readers. We literally couldn’t have done it without you!

Citations are not the sole measure of impact, of course. Our open access status continues to make our journal freely available to all interested. Our Journal averages more than 400,000 downloads/views per year. Most of our articles receive hundreds of views within weeks of publication; quite a few have several thousand downloads. Thanks to all the authors who trust us to be part of the dissemination of their excellent work. If you have not yet submitted a manuscript for consideration in our Journal, we hope you will consider doing so soon.

Thanks, too, to all those who responded to my call last year for more engagement in the Journal. As I noted then, with our gratitude, several of our seasoned editorial team members have been transitioning off the Board, so it is an ideal time to welcome new talent and energy. We are still actively seeking a few new editorial team members.

Have you been an active IJCH reviewer and want to increase your involvement with our Journal? Do you have editorial experience and expertise and seek new opportunities to apply them? Do you have new ideas about how to strengthen and improve the Journal? If you answered yes to one or more of these questions, I hope that you will seriously consider joining our Editorial Board. Editorial Board members are expected to be both active and collegial contributors to the team and will be asked to manage no more than 6 manuscripts a year, seeking appropriate peer reviewers and making initial editorial decisions based on those reviews and their own judgment. If interested in learning more or being considered for one of the vacancies on our Editorial Board, please send a brief email letter of interest to me with either your questions and/or highlighting some relevant aspects from your professional and/or personal background and what you hope to contribute and gain to/from the Editorial role(s). Applications will be considered on a rolling basis.

In the meantime, I remain grateful to our readers, particularly those who work to bring ideas presented within our pages to life in their own communities, to the authors who trust us in the review and handling of their papers, and to our editorial team and many dedicated reviewers who have shared helpful critiques and invested their time and talents to raise the quality and relevance of this Journal. We ‘see’ and appreciate the light in all of you!

Please join me in gratitude to the colleagues below who contribute so much to this continuing scientific and professional exchange.

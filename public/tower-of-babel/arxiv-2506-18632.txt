Ergodicity of the hard-core PCA with a random walk method
Jérôme Casse, Irène Marcovici, Maxence Poutrel

Abstract
The hard-core probabilistic cellular automaton has attracted a renewed interest in the last few years, thanks to its connection with the study of a combinatorial game on percolation configurations. We provide an alternative proof for the ergodicity of this PCA for a neighbourhood of size 2 2 and 3 3 , using the notion of decorrelated islands introduced by Casse in 2023, together with some new ideas. This shortens the previous proofs and provides a more intuitive and unified approach. Keywords : probabilistic cellular automata, ergodicity, percolation, hard-core model.

Ergodicity of PCA.

The ergodicity of PCA has been studied using various methods (coupling, entropy, Fourier analysis, weight functions …) and still raises many questions, see
[
TVS+90
,
MM14a
,
MST19
]
and the references therein. The hard-core PCA has attracted particular interest, since in addition to its connection to percolation games, it has long been known for its connection with statistical physics and with the enumeration of directed animals. For
\epsilon_{1}=0
,
H_{n}
has a Markovian invariant measure and its ergodicity is easier to prove
[
HMM19
]
, but for other values of the parameters, no general method is known.

2
Sketch of the proof

2.1
Decorrelated islands method

Let us consider the evolution of the PCA
\tilde{H}_{n}
, from the initial configuration
\text{{?}}^{\mathbb{Z}}
.

\bullet

With probability
(\epsilon_{0}+\epsilon_{1})^{k}
, the
k
consecutive cells of indices
0,\dots,k-1
go from state
?
to state
0
or
1
, i.e. for all
i\in\{0,\dots,k-1\}
,
X_{i}(1)\in\{0,1\}
. We call such a sequence of consecutive cells a
decorrelated island
, as the states of these cells do not depend on the initial configuration.

\bullet

Our goal is to study the evolution of the size of a decorrelated island, that is a (maximal) set of consecutive cells where the symbol
?
does not appear. With probability
1
, there exists a time
t_{0}\geq 0
when such a decorrelated island is created. For
t\geq t_{0}
, we denote by
i_{t}
and
j_{t}
the positions of the left and right boundary of the decorrelated island, respectively. So, cells between
i_{t}
and
j_{t}
are in state
0
or
1
, while
i_{t}-1
and
j_{t}+1
are in state
?
.

\bullet

If
j_{t}-i_{t}\to_{t\to\infty}\infty
with positive probability, this means that the island grows, so that the symbols
?
progressively disappear, implying that the PCA is ergodic.

To study the behaviour of the left boundary
i_{t}
, we only remember the states of the first
m
cells of the island, given by
f_{t}=(X_{i_{t}},X_{1+i_{t}},\dots,X_{m-1+i_{t}})
, with a well-chosen value of
m
, selected according to the size of the neighborhood
n
. We proceed in a similar way for the right boundary
j_{t}
. In the following, we will set
m=2
when
n=2
, and
m=4
when
n=3
.

This method, applied with
m=1
, gives a sufficient ergodicity criterion for PCA with binary alphabet and two-size neighborhood
[
Casse23
]
. This allows to handle the case of
12
out of
16
deterministic cellular automata with error
\epsilon>0
. By taking
m=2
, it also gives a new proof of the ergodicity of the PCA
\tilde{H}_{2}
(and so of
H_{2}
) when
\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{1}>0
.

2.2
The main improvement of the method

In this article, we improve the method developed in
[
Casse23
]
to give a new and shorter proof of Theorem
1
. The improvement consists in considering one or two time steps at once, according to the value of the states of the boundary, and not just one time step in every situation.

First, observe that in order to prove that
\mathbb{P}\left(j_{t}-i_{t}\to\infty\right)
is positive, since the size of negative steps is bounded (by
1
), it is enough to show that the mean asymptotic drift

D=\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[J_{t}\right]-\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[I_{t}\right]

of
(j_{t}-i_{t})_{t\geq t_{0}}
is positive, where
J_{t}
is the increment of
(j_{t})
, i.e.
J_{t}=j_{t+1}-j_{t}
and
I_{t}
the one of
(i_{t})
. The following remark in fact allows one to restrict ourselves to the study of the right boundary.

Remark 1
.

Let us denote by
R=\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[J_{t}\right]
the mean asymptotic drift of the right boundary. Using the symmetry of the transition rule of
\tilde{H}_{n}
with respect to the left-right symmetry, while taking into account the fact that the neighborhood is not centered, it follows that
D=2R+(n-1)
. Consequently, in the rest of the article, we focus only on the behaviour of the right boundary.

The innovation of the present work is to study the law of
J_{t}
and the one of
J_{t}+J_{t+1}
according to the values of the states of the boundary
f_{t}
. This is summed up in the following lemma.

Lemma 2
.

Consider a homogeneous Markov chain
(j_{t},f_{t})
on
\mathbb{Z}\times F
, where
F
is a finite set, and assume that the increment
J_{t}=j_{t+1}-j_{t}
depends only on
f_{t}
. Precisely, assume that for any
f\in F
, for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, and for any
t\geq 0
,

\mathbb{E}\left[J_{t}|f_{t}=f,j_{t}=k\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[j_{1}|f_{0}=f,j_{0}=0\right].

Then, if the limit
R=\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[J_{t}\right]
exists and is finite, it satisfies:

\displaystyle R\geq\min_{f\in F}\max\left(\mathbb{E}\left[J_{t}|f_{t}=f\right],\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[J_{t}+J_{t+1}|f_{t}=f\right]\right).

(1)

Proof.

First, we split the set
F
into two disjoint parts:

F_{1}=\left\{f\in F:\mathbb{E}\left[J_{0}|f_{0}=f\right]\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[J_{0}+J_{1}|f_{0}=f\right]\right\}\text{ and }F_{2}=F\backslash F_{1}.

The set
F_{1}
represents the states for which the mean increment is larger when we consider a single time step compared to two time steps, and
F_{2}
those for which it is the reverse.

We now define another Markov chain
(\hat{j}_{t},\hat{f}_{t},w_{t})_{t\geq 0}
as follows:

\bullet

(\hat{j}_{0},\hat{f}_{0},w_{0})=(j_{0},f_{0},\mathbb{1}_{f_{0}\in F_{2}})
,

\bullet

if
w_{t}=0
, then
\hat{j}_{t+1}=j_{t+1}
,
\hat{f}_{t+1}=f_{t+1}
and
w_{t+1}=\mathbb{1}_{f_{t+1}\in F_{2}}
,

\bullet

if
w_{t}=1
, then
\hat{j}_{t+1}=j_{t}
,
\hat{f}_{t+1}=f_{t}
and
w_{t+1}=0
.

As a consequence:

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{t}=\hat{j}_{t+1}-\hat{j}_{t}=J_{t}\text{ when }w_{t}=0\text{ and }f_{t}\in F_{1};

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{t}=0\text{ when }w_{t}=1\text{ and }f_{t}\in F_{2};

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{t}=J_{t-1}+J_{t}\text{ when }w_{t}=0\text{ and }f_{t}\in F_{2}.

So, compared to
(j_{t},f_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, the new Markov chain moves one step forward when
\hat{f}_{t}
is in
F_{1}
, while when it is in
F_{2}
, the state does not change at the first time step (when
w_{t}=1
) and then moves of the equivalent of two steps at a time (when
w_{t}=0
). The variable
w_{t}
can thus be thought as a waiting time.

Let us now consider the value
\hat{R}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\hat{J}_{t}\right]
.
We show that
\hat{R}\geq M
, where
M
is the term on the right-hand side of (
1
).
We have

\hat{R}=\sum_{f\in F_{1}}\tau(f,0)\mathbb{E}\left[\hat{J}_{t}|\hat{f}_{t}=f,w_{t}=0\right]+\sum_{f\in F_{2}}\left(\tau(f,1)\times 0+\tau(f,0)\mathbb{E}\left[\hat{J}_{t}|\hat{f}_{t}=f,w_{t}=0\right]\right)

where
\tau
is the invariant measure of the Markov chain
(\hat{f}_{t},w_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, which exists since
F
is finite.

By definition of
(\hat{j}_{t},\hat{f}_{t},w_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, we have for any
f\in F_{1}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\hat{J}_{t}|\hat{f}_{t}=f,w_{t}=0\right]=\max\left(\mathbb{E}\left[J_{t}|f_{t}=f\right],\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[J_{t}+J_{t+1}|f_{t}=f\right]\right)\geq M

and for any
f\in F_{2}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\hat{J}_{t}|\hat{f}_{t}=f,w_{t}=0\right]=2\max\left(\mathbb{E}\left[J_{t}|f_{t}=f\right],\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[J_{t}+J_{t+1}|f_{t}=f\right]\right)\geq 2M.

By construction of the new Markov chain, for any
f\in F_{2}
,
\tau(f,0)=\tau(f,1)
. So we obtain

\hat{R}\geq M\sum_{(f,w)\in(F_{1}\times\{0\})\cup(F_{2}\times\{0,1\})}\tau(f,w)=M
.

To conclude, we only need to prove that
R=\hat{R}
. We recall that the equality
(j_{t},f_{t})=(\hat{j}_{t},\hat{f}_{t})
holds at least once in two, and by the ergodic theorem,

\displaystyle\hat{R}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\hat{J}_{t}\right]=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\hat{j}_{t}-j_{0}\right]}{t}\text{ and }R=\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[J_{t}\right]=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[j_{t}-j_{0}\right]}{t}.

So, we have
\hat{R}=R
. ∎∎

Using Remark
1
together with Lemma
2
, we can then prove Theorem

n\in\{2,3\}
and for every

R\geq\min_{f\in\{0,1\}^{m}}\left(\max\left(\mathbb{E}\left[J_{t}|f_{t}=f\right],\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[J_{t}+J_{t+1}|f_{t}=f\right]\right)\right)>-\frac{n-1}{2}.

(2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{J_{t}}|f_{t}=(*,0,0,0)\right]\geq\min\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{J_{t}}|f_{t}=(0,0,0,0)\right],\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{J_{t}}|f_{t}=(1,0,0,0)\right]\right);

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{J_{t}}|f_{t}=(1,*,0,0)\right]\geq\min\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{J_{t}}|f_{t}=(1,0,0,0)\right],\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{J_{t}}|f_{t}\in S_{1}\right]\right);

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{J_{t}}|f_{t}\in E^{*}\right]\geq\min\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{J_{t}}|f_{t}=(0,0,0,0)\right],\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{J_{t}}|f_{t}=(1,0,0,0)\right],\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{J_{t}}|f_{t}\in S_{1}\right]\right).

For any
(\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{1})\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]^{2}
, we have, see Appendices
6.3
and
6.4
, the two following inequalities:

\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{J_{t}}|f_{t}\in S_{1}\right]\geq I_{0}\text{ and }\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{J_{t}}|f_{t}\in S_{1}\right]\geq\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{J_{t}}|f_{t}=(1,0,0,0)\right].

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{J_{t}}|f_{t}=(*,0,0,0)\right]\geq\min\left(I_{0},\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{J_{t}}|f_{t}=(1,0,0,0)\right]\right);

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{J_{t}}|f_{t}=(1,*,0,0)\right]\geq\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{J_{t}}|f_{t}=(1,0,0,0)\right];

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{J_{t}}|f_{t}\in E^{*}\right]\geq\min\left(I_{0},\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{J_{t}}|f_{t}=(1,0,0,0)\right]\right).

Finally, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{J}_{t}+\tilde{J}_{t+1}|f_{t}=(0,0,0,0)\right]

\displaystyle\geq I_{0}+\mathbb{P}\left(f_{t+1}=(0,0,0,0)|f_{t}=(0,0,0,0)\right)I_{0}

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\left(f_{t+1}\in S_{1}|f_{t}=(0,0,0,0)\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{J}_{t+1}|f_{t+1}\in S_{1}\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\left(f_{t+1}\in\{(1,0,0,0),(1,*,0,0)\}|f_{t}=(0,0,0,0)\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{J}_{t+1}|f_{t+1}=(1,0,0,0)\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\left(f_{t+1}=(*,0,0,0)\cup E^{*}|f_{t}=(0,0,0,0)\right)\min\left(I_{0},\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{J}_{t+1}|f_{t+1}=(1,0,0,0)\right]\right)

(19)

The value of
\min\left(I_{0},\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{J}_{t+1}|f_{t+1}=(1,0,0,0)\right]\right)
can be the left or the right term, depending on the values of
\epsilon_{0}
and
\epsilon_{1}
. Nevertheless, in Appendix
6.5
, we prove that in both cases it is greater than
-2
, and so

\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{J}_{t}+\tilde{J}_{t+1}|f_{t}=(0,0,0,0)\right]>-2.

(20)

Combined with Equations (
12
) and (
18
), it implies Equation (
2
) when
n=3
, and so Theorem
1
for
n=3
.

5
Perspectives

We have proved the desired result for
n=2
and
n=3
, by examining the behaviour of the boundaries of decorrelated islands. The ergodicity of the hard-core PCA for
n\geq 4
is still an open question. Our method could be used to handle the case of larger values of
n
, but the computations are becoming increasingly complex with the size of the neighbourhood, so that the
n=4
case is already difficult to tackle. However, one can ask whether there could be an automated way of handling larger neighbourhoods, and perhaps also other classes of PCA.

Concerning specifically the hard-core PCA, it seems that for fixed
\epsilon_{0}
, the mean increment in 1 (and 2) time step(s) is an increasing function in
\epsilon_{1}
. If this is indeed the case, it could simplify the analysis.

\displaystyle 2\frac{(1-r)^{2}}{r}

\displaystyle=2(1-r)^{2}+2\frac{(1-r)^{3}}{r}\geq 2(\epsilon_{0}+\epsilon_{1})^{2}=2\epsilon_{0}^{2}+4\epsilon_{0}\epsilon_{1}+2\epsilon_{1}^{2}.

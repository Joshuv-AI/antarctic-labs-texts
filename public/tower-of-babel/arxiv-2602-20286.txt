A guide to wall crossing for moduli of varieties
Kristin DeVleming

Abstract
. There have been major developments in the theory of moduli of varieties in the past decade, essentially settling the construction of moduli spaces of log canonically polarized slc pairs (c.f. [ 31 ] ) and moduli spaces of K-polystable log Fano pairs (c.f. [ 46 ] ). Given the construction of these moduli spaces of pairs ( X , D ) (X,D) , it is natural to ask how the moduli spaces vary as the coefficients of D D are perturbed. This phenomenon is known as wall crossing , the theory of which has been developed in several important cases in the past five years. This semi-expository article is an introduction to moduli of varieties and wall crossing, capturing a portion of the theory developed in [ 4 , 47 , 3 , 42 , 2 , 10 ] . It also introduces tools and techniques used in explicit computations and examples, applying them in new examples.



1.
Introduction

In the past decade, there has been a host of developments in the theory of moduli of algebraic varieties. A moduli functor of canonically polarized varieties and pairs admitting a projective coarse moduli space has been settled upon (see
[
31
]
) and K-stability, an algebro-geometric notion originating in differential geometry, has been used to develop a good moduli theory of Fano varieties and log Fano pairs (see
[
46
]
). These different moduli theories can be used to provide compactifications of moduli spaces of smooth varieties of a given type.

However, when compactifying moduli spaces of smooth varieties, there often exist
several
choices of compactifications. For example, if
M_{g}
is the moduli space of smooth genus
g
curves, there exists the celebrated Deligne-Mumford compactification
\overline{M}_{g}
parametrizing stable genus
g
curves. Several alternative compactifications exist: for example, taking the Jacobian of each curve yields a map
M_{g}\to A_{g}
, the moduli space of abelian varieties, and the closure of
M_{g}
inside the Satake compactification of
A_{g}
provides an alternative compactification. In genus
2
, we could view a smooth genus
g
curve as a double cover of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
branched over
6
points, and could instead compactify the
M_{2}
using Geometric Invariant Theory (GIT) for binary sextics. If we add marked points and consider the moduli space of smooth genus
g
curves with
n
marked points
M_{g,n}
, we have even more flexibility: in the Deligne-Mumford compactification
\overline{M}_{g,n}
, we parametrize stable curves with
n
distinct points (that are distinct from any nodes of the curve). We could form alternative compactifications by allowing the markings to come together, e.g. allowing a subset of the
n
points to be equal.

Each of these compactifications produces a moduli space birational to
M_{g}
or
M_{g,n}
. Given these birational varieties, it is natural to ask if there exists a morphism between them or a way to resolve the rational map. In the case of
M_{g,n}
, Hassett proves in
[
25
]
that there are explicit birational morphisms from the moduli space
\overline{M}_{g,n}
to the moduli spaces where more and more points are allowed to collide. The aim of this article is to discuss in general the theory of ‘wall crossing’ in moduli of varieties, which is a study of varying compactifications of moduli spaces and describing the morphisms between them.

Organization

The paper is organized as follows. In §
2
, we provide background on KSBA and K-stable pairs, defining the relevant moduli functors. We also state versions of the KSBA moduli theorem and K-moduli theorem that will be used in this survey. In §
3
, we introduce the theory of wall crossing and outline the proofs of the wall crossing theorems in the introduction. In §
4
, we study moduli spaces of plane quartic curves and the associated wall crossing. The aim of this section is to introduce techniques relevant to explicit computation and classification in general while applying them in this example. In §
5
, we summarize wall crossing for moduli of plane quintics and compute the first KSBA wall crossing. We conclude this section with several results on plane curves of degree
d\geq 6
, proving the results in the introduction. Finally, in §
6
, we offer concluding remarks on generalizations beyond the proportional case and study moduli of curves on the Hirzebruch surface

is klt.

These conditions can be viewed as a sort of ‘asymptotic’ GIT condition (and in some cases, is equivalent to GIT, see Theorem
4.7
). Indeed, a degeneration along a one-parameter subgroup in GIT will give rise to a test configuration, and the Futaki invariant (under suitable circumstances) will be related to the Hilbert-Mumford weight. The key difference, however, is that in GIT, one-parameter subgroups degenerate
X
inside a
fixed
projective space, and in K-stability, the central fiber of the test configuration does not need to embed in the same ambient projective space as
X
. For computational purposes, it is often convenient to have an alternative definition of K-stability, given below.

2.1.4.
K-stability via the
\delta
invariant

Definition 2.11
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a klt log Fano pair of dimension
n
and
E
a prime divisor over
X
. Let
\mu:Y\to X
be any morphism such that
E\subset Y
.

Let
A_{X,\Delta}(E)
be the
log discrepancy
of the divisor
E
, defined as

A_{X,\Delta}(E)=1+a_{X,\Delta}(E),

where
a_{X,\Delta}(E)
is the discrepancy as defined above.

Define
S_{X,\Delta}(E)
to be

S_{X,\Delta}(E)=\frac{1}{(-K_{X}-\Delta)^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{vol}(\mu^{*}(-K_{X}-\Delta)-tE)dt,

where the
volume
of divisor
D
on a normal variety
X
of dimension
n
is

\mathrm{vol}(D)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{h^{0}(X,mD)}{m^{n}/n!}.

This does not depend on choice of
\mu
and
Y
, so we often write

S_{X,\Delta}(E)=\frac{1}{(-K_{X}-\Delta)^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{vol}(-K_{X}-\Delta-tE)dt.

The
\delta
-invariant of
E
is

\delta_{X,\Delta}{E}=\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(E)}{S_{X,\Delta}(E)}.

Remark 2.12
.

In the definition of
S_{X,\Delta}(E)
, we need to compute an improper integral. However,
\mathrm{vol}(\mu^{*}(-K_{X}-\Delta)-tE)>0
if and only if
-\mu^{*}(-K_{X}-\Delta)-tE
is big, and hence the volume is only non-zero if
t\in[0,\tau]
where
\tau
is the
pseudo-effective threshold
, which is finite. Therefore, we could instead write

S_{X,\Delta}(E)=\frac{1}{(-K_{X}-\Delta)^{n}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathrm{vol}(-K_{X}-\Delta-tE)dt.

With this definition, we can relate the K-(semi/poly)stability of
X
to the birational geometry of
X
. The following theorem is known as the
valuative criterion
for K-(semi/poly)stability. For the full strength of the theorem as stated, we need the equivalence of uniform K-stability and K-stability, see
[
37
]
.

Theorem 2.13

(
[
19
,
35
]
)
.

The log Fano pair
(X,\Delta)
is K-semistable (resp. stable) if and only if
\delta_{X,\Delta}(E)\geq 1
(resp.
>1
) for all prime divisors
E
over
X
.

This theorem will prove essential in wall crossing computations.

Definition 2.14

Theorem 2.17

If

Theorem 3.1

Let

Definition 3.2

Definition 4.2

Let

be a klt log pair of dimension

The

\mathrm{vol}_{X,x}(v):=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{O}_{X,x}/\{f\in\mathcal{O}_{X,x}\mid v(f)\geq k\}}{k^{n}/n!}.

The

is a function
A_{(X,D)}:\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}\cup\{+\infty\}
defined in
[

+\infty&\textrm{ if }A_{(X,D)}(v)=+\infty\end{cases}

Property 4.4
.

We list several important properties of the normalized volume.

(1)

Assuming

(2)

[

If

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(x,X)=n^{n}

If

(4)

If

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(x,X)\leq\frac{n^{n}}{|G|}.

Combining this with Theorem

(5)

Combining the previous points, the normalized volume gives an

on the canonical divisor in a K-semistable pair

assuming

Theorem 4.7

(

The idea of the proof comes in two parts: first, one-parameter subgroups in GIT induce test configurations in K-stability, and in this setting the Futaki invariant is proportional to the Hilbert Mumford weight. 4.2.
K-moduli wall crossing for quartic curves

Proposition 4.8

5.2.
KSBA wall crossing toward moduli of surfaces of general type

Consider the KSBA moduli space
P^{\rm KSBA}_{5,\frac{4}{5}}

is ample. Furthermore,

. From the study above, we have a complete understanding of the moduli space compactifying the of pairs

Lemma 5.5
.

Let

be a curve of degree 18 meeting the section of

transversely at one point. If

has at worst

singularities, and has an

singularity if and only if equality holds.

Proof.

First, observe
\mathrm{lct}(C)>\frac{11}{18}
implies

is reduced and
C
can have multiplicity at most 3 at any point. Up to a change of coordinates, assume
C
has a singularity at
[1:0:0]
.

Writing the coordinates of
\mathbb{P}(1,2,9)
as
[x:y:z]
and

as a monomial in

is nonzero. By completing the square, we may assume

is given by an equation of the form

By the transverse assumption, we must have the coefficient of

also nonzero, so there exists some

such that

is given by

Let

be a monomial in

minimal. Because

is singular at

∎

Now, we have a complete description of

5.3.
Using wall crossing to construct degenerations

In this section, we outline some additional applications of wall crossing. For example, in moduli spaces of certain surface pairs, we can use the theory of wall crossing to produce surfaces with many components that admit smoothings.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let

be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein slc surface and

a curve such that

(1)

has an

(2)

(3)

if some component

of

has rational normalization, it either meets

in at least three points or meets
\overline{D\setminus C}
in at least one point (respectively, two points) if

is odd (respectively, even) and passes through

.

Assume

is ample for

. Assume that
[(X,cD)]
is a point of an irreducible component of a KSBA moduli space whose general points
(X^{\prime},cD^{\prime})
are stable for all
c\in[\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{n+1},1]
.

Let

be the surface constructed from

by performing the
(a,b)
weighted blow up of

, where

if

is odd or

if

is even, and gluing

along the exceptional divisor

to the weighted projective space
\mathbb{P}(1,a,b)
along a section of

is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein degeneration of

, and for a divisor

in the linear system corresponding to the specialization of

, the divisor

is ample for

. A general choice of

gives

is a point of the same irreducible component of the KSBA moduli space as
(X^{\prime},cD^{\prime})
for all

. Furthermore, there exists a particular divisor

in this linear system on

with an

singularity at a smooth point

such that

From this, we derive the following Corollary (which proves Theorem
1.4
).

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
X
be
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein slc surface with
k
components and
D\subset X
a curve with
j
components and an

singularity satisfying the conditions in the previous theorem. Then, for any
c\in(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{n+1},1]
and
\ell
such that

, there exists a specialization

of

with

components containing a divisor

with

components such that
D_{Y}
has an
A_{\ell}
singularity and
(Y,cD_{Y})
is a stable pair.

If
c=1
, and
\ell=1
, this produces a stable pair
(Y,D_{Y})
with

components.

For the idea of the proof, suppose
(X,cD)
as above is a stable pair for

, for any larger coefficient of

We begin with a preliminary lemma.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
(X,\Delta+c_{0}D)
be a stable surface pair with
K_{X}+\Delta+cD
ample for all
c\in[c_{0},1]
and assume the following:

(1)

X
is normal and
D
has exactly one singular point
p\in X
and
p\notin\Delta
;

(2)

c_{0}=\mathrm{lct}((X,\Delta),D)
is the log canonical threshold of
D
;

(3)

there exists a birational morphism
\pi:Y\to X
with one exceptional divisor
E
over
p
such that

K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}+c_{0}D_{Y}+E=\pi^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta+cD)

and

(Y,\Delta_{Y}+cD_{Y}+E)
is dlt for all
c\in[c_{0},1]
, where
D_{Y}
is the strict transform of
D
and
\Delta_{Y}
is the strict transform of
\Delta

(4)

D_{Y}
is a union of divisors with positive genus or, if a component has genus

Then,

is ample for all

and
(Y,\Delta_{Y}+cD_{Y}+E)
is a stable pair.

Proof.

Let
\pi:Y\to X
be the birational morphism extracting

, so

K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}+c_{0}D_{Y}+E=\pi^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta+c_{0}D).

Let

. Then,

for

. Because

is exceptional,

, so
(K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}+cD_{Y}+E)\cdot E>0
for any

.

Now, writing
K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}+cD_{Y}+E=K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}+c_{0}D_{Y}+E+(c-c_{0})D_{Y}=\pi^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta+c_{0}D)+(c-c_{0})D_{Y}
and using that

is ample, we see that for any

,

(K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}+cD_{Y}+E)\cdot C=(\pi^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta+c_{0}D)+(c-c_{0})D_{Y})\cdot C=(K_{X}+\Delta+c_{0}D)\cdot\pi(C)+(c-c_{0})D_{Y}\cdot C.

As long as

or

, we conclude
(K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}+cD_{Y}+E)\cdot C>0
. Finally, if

and

, which necessarily implies

, then write

, where

are the other components of

. Then,

and intersecting with

and

(K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}+cD_{Y}+E)\cdot C>0
.

This proves that
K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}+cD_{Y}+E
is ample for any

. The stability of the pair then follows by assumption (3).
∎

Now, we prove Theorem
5.6
.

Proof.

Let

and choose a local parameter

, where

is the strict transform of

in

Proof.

We apply this to moduli of plane curves for

Corollary 5.9
.

In the KSBA moduli space compactifying the locus pairs
(\mathbb{P}^{2},D)
where

and

, there exists a surface with at least

is even and

First, assume
d
is even. We construct a curve of degree

with an
A_{n}
singularity where

. To do this, consider the plane curve
C
given by

, where

f(x,y,z)=(xz^{d/2-1}-y^{d/2})^{2}-x^{d}.

By direct computation, this curve has an isolated singular point at
[0:0:1]
. In the coordinate chart where

, we may write

and consider the change of coordinates given by

(x^{\prime})^{2}-(x^{\prime}+y^{d/2})^{d}=(x^{\prime})^{2}-y^{d^{2}/2}+h.o.t.

(x^{\prime},y)
by
(d^{2}/2,2)
. This defines an
A_{n}
singularity where

. By construction, this curve is the union of two smooth degree

factors as

is odd, we construct a plane curve with an

singularity where

in the same way. This curve

is given by the vanishing of the equation

As above, this has an

singularity at

The curves constructed in these proofs consist of the normalization of

glued to a union of

or

(if

has two components if

is even and one if

is odd. This observation proves the following:

.

For

, the closure of the locus of planar curves of degree

in the moduli space of Deligne-Mumford stable curves

, the idea of wall crossing is applied to study the closure of the locus of

. This perspective merits future exploration and will be studied in upcoming papers.

6.
Wall crossing in the non-proportional case

In the non-proportional case, the K-moduli wall crossing is not well understood. There is a general theory recently developed in
[
39
,
40
]
but few examples have been worked out. One example, relating certain K-moduli to GIT moduli and computing non-proportional wall crossing, can be found in
[
13
]
.

In this final section, we present one additional example of non-proportional K-moduli to indicate the methods and computations involved.

6.1.
K-moduli of hyperelliptic curves on
\mathbb{F}_{1}

Let
\mathbb{F}_{1}
be the first Hirzebruch surface, the blow up of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
at one point. Let
s,f
respectively be the exceptional divisor of the blow up and the strict transform of a line through the point blown up. We recall some standard facts about
\mathbb{F}_{1}
. The curves are the extremal rays on the Mori cone
\overline{NE}(\mathbb{F}_{1})
, and any prime divisor
D
on
\mathbb{F}_{1}
is numerically equivalent to
as+bf
for some
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
. A divisor
D=as+bf
is ample (respectively, nef) if and only if
b>a>0
(respectively,
b\geq a\geq 0
), and it is big if and only if
a,b>0
. The volume of such a divisor
D=as+bf
is given by

\mathrm{vol}(D)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}a(2b-a)&\text{ if }b\geq a\geq 0\\
b^{2}&\text{ if }a>b>0\\
0&\text{ if }b<0\text{ or }a<0.\end{array}\right.

The canonical divisor on
\mathbb{F}_{1}
is given by
2s+3f
.

Let
D\in|2s+4f|
. We discuss the K-moduli spaces compactifying the locus of pairs
(\mathbb{F}_{1},cD)
to prove the following theorem, which proves Theorem
1.7
.

Theorem 6.1
.

The K-moduli space compactifying the locus of pairs
(\mathbb{F}_{1},cD)
, where
D\in|2s+4f|
, is nonempty if and only if
c\in[\frac{1}{10}(4-\sqrt{6}),\frac{1}{2}).
When
c=\frac{1}{10}(4-\sqrt{6})
, the moduli space is a single point. If
D
is smooth and meets the negative section of
\mathbb{F}_{1}
transversely,
(\mathbb{F}_{1},cD)
is K-stable for every
c\in(\frac{1}{10}(4-\sqrt{6}),\frac{1}{2})
.

The first result is that the K-moduli space is empty if
c\ll 1
.

Lemma 6.2
.

For any
D\in|2s+4f|
,
\delta_{\mathbb{F}_{1},cD}(s)\geq 1
if and only if
\mathrm{ord}_{D}(s)=0
and
c\geq c_{0}=\frac{1}{10}(4-\sqrt{6})
. In particular, if
(\mathbb{F}_{1},cD)
is K-semistable, then
c\geq c_{0}
. Note that
c_{0}=\frac{1}{10}(4-\sqrt{6})\approx 0.155
is not rational.

Proof.

We compute
\delta_{\mathbb{F}_{1},cD}(s)=\frac{1-c\mathrm{ord}_{D}(s)}{\frac{(1-c)(7-10c)}{6-9c}}
. If
(\mathbb{F}_{1},cD)
is K-semistable, then
\delta{\mathbb{F}_{1},cD}(s)\geq 1
, and solving for
c
yields
c\geq c_{0}
and
\mathrm{ord}_{D}(s)=0
.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
s_{\infty}\in|s+f|
be a smooth section. Then, for any
D\in|2s+4f|
,
\delta_{\mathbb{F}_{1},cD}(s_{\infty})\geq 1
implies
\mathrm{ord}_{D}(s_{\infty})\leq 2
, and if
\mathrm{ord}_{D}(s_{\infty})=2
, then
c\leq c_{0}
.

Proof.

We compute
\delta_{\mathbb{F}_{1},cD}(s_{\infty})=\frac{1-c\mathrm{ord}_{D}(s_{\infty})}{\frac{(28c^{2}-38c+13)}{12-18c}}
. If
(\mathbb{F}_{1},cD)
is K-semistable, then
\delta_{\mathbb{F}_{1},cD}(s_{\infty})\geq 1
, and solving for
c
yields and
\mathrm{ord}_{D}(s_{\infty})\leq 2
and if
\mathrm{ord}_{D}(s_{\infty})=2
, then
c\leq c_{0}
.
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
f_{1},f_{2}\in|f|
be distinct fibers and
s_{\infty}
a smooth section as above. Then, the pair
(\mathbb{F}_{1},c(2s_{\infty}+f_{1}+f_{2}))
is K-semistable if and only if
c=c_{0}
.

Proof.

The previous lemmas tell us
c=c_{0}
is necessary. To prove it is sufficient, consider the action of
GL_{2}
on
\mathbb{F}_{1}
given on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
by the subgroup

\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b&0\\
c&d&0\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}\right\}\subset\mathrm{PGL}_{3}.

Blowing up the point
[0:0:1]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}
, which is fixed by the action, induces an action of
GL_{2}
on
\mathbb{F}_{1}
. This acts transitively on
|f|
as this is the (strict transform) of the linear system of lines through the point
[0:0:1]
. Therefore, there are no fixed points of the action, and the only divisors fixed by the action are the exceptional divisor of the blow up
s
and the strict transform of
z=0
, which we may assume is our given section
s_{\infty}
. By
[
48
, Theorem 1.2]
, the
\delta
-invariant of this pair can be computed by the infimum of the
\delta
-invariants of invariant divisors. Therefore, from the computation above, for
c>c_{0}
and
c\in\mathbb{Q}
,

\delta(\mathbb{F}_{1},c(2s_{\infty}+f_{1}+f_{2}))=\delta_{\mathbb{F}_{1},cD}(s_{\infty})=\frac{1-2c}{\frac{(28c^{2}-38c+13)}{12-18c}}

and for
c<c_{0}
,
c\in\mathbb{Q}
, we have

\delta(\mathbb{F}_{1},c(2s_{\infty}+f_{1}+f_{2}))=\delta_{\mathbb{F}_{1},cD}(s)=\frac{1}{\frac{(1-c)(7-10c)}{6-9c}}.

By
[
39
]
, for irrational
c
,
\delta(\mathbb{F}_{1},c(2s_{\infty}+f_{1}+f_{2}))=\lim_{c^{\prime}\to c}\delta(\mathbb{F}_{1},c^{\prime}(2s_{\infty}+f_{1}+f_{2}))
where
c^{\prime}
is rational, and the limit of either computation above yields

\delta(\mathbb{F}_{1},c(2s_{\infty}+f_{1}+f_{2}))=1\iff c=c_{0}.

We conclude that
(\mathbb{F}_{1},c_{0}(2s_{\infty}+f_{1}+f_{2}))
is K-semistable if and only if
c=c_{0}
.
∎

Now, we wish to prove that
(\mathbb{F}_{1},c_{0}(2s_{\infty}+f_{1}+f_{2}))
is K-polystable, which we will ultimately use to show that it is the only point of the associated K-moduli space when
c=c_{0}
.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
(X,cD)
be a K-semistable specialization of a K-semistable pair
(\mathbb{F}_{1},cD^{\prime})
for
c<\frac{1}{12}(10-\sqrt{58})\approx 0.2
. Then,
X\cong\mathbb{F}_{1}
.

Proof.

We use the normalized volume to first show that
X
must be smooth. Let
x\in X
be any point. Then, by the properties in
4.4
,

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(X,x)\geq\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}((X,cD),x)\geq\frac{4}{9}(-K_{X}-cD)^{2}=\frac{16}{9}(1-c)(2-3c)

For
c<\frac{1}{12}(10-\sqrt{58})
, we have

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(X,x)\geq\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}((X,cD),x)\geq\frac{4}{9}(-K_{X}-cD)^{2}=\frac{16}{9}(1-c)(2-3c)>2.

By the Gap Conjecture which is known in dimension 2 (see
4.4
), the only point of a surface with
\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(X,x)>2
is a smooth point. Therefore, we conclude
x
is a smooth point. This proves that
X
is smooth.

Because
X
is smooth, we have
K_{X}^{2}=K_{\mathbb{F}^{1}}^{2}=8
. By hypothesis,
-K_{X}-cD
is ample, and we next prove that
-K_{X}
is nef. Suppose
-K_{X}
was not nef, so there exists a curve
C\subset X
such that
-K_{X}\cdot C<0
. Because
-K_{X}-cD
is ample, this implies
-D\cdot C>0
, which implies
C\subset\textrm{Supp}\ D
. Writing

-K_{X}-cD=-(K_{X}+C)+(1-c)C-c(\overline{D\setminus C})
, because
D\cdot C<0
implies
C\cdot C<0
and
c(\overline{D\setminus C})\cdot C>0
, we must have
-(K_{X}+C)\cdot C>0
by ampleness of
-K_{X}-cD
. This implies
C
is a smooth rational curve and
-(K_{X}+C)\cdot C=2
. Because
1-c\geq 0.8
,
(1-c)C<2
if and only if
C^{2}=-1
or
C^{2}=-2
. In either case,
-K_{X}\cdot C\geq 0
, so we have derived a contradiction and therefore
-K_{X}
is nef. Because
-K_{X}=(-K_{X}-cD)+cD
is the sum of an ample and an effective divisor,
-K_{X}
is also big. Therefore,
-K_{X}
is nef and big. Because
(-K_{X})^{2}=8
, by classification of weak Fano surfaces, we conclude
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathbb{F}_{1}
, or
\mathbb{F}_{2}
.

Finally, we show that
X
cannot be
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
or
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. This follows as there exists a smooth big and nef divisor
\Delta
on each (either a section of
\mathcal{O}(1,1)
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
, or a section missing the
-2
curve on
\mathbb{F}_{2}
) with self intersection
2
. By assumption,
X
is smoothable to
\mathbb{F}_{1}
, but the divisor
\Delta
deforms in the family as
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}(-\Delta))=0
, but there exist no smooth curves on
\mathbb{F}_{1}
with self intersection
2
, so this is impossible. Therefore,
X\cong\mathbb{F}_{1}
.
∎

Finally, we study specializations of the divisor on
(\mathbb{F}_{1},c_{0}(2s_{\infty}+f_{1}+f_{2}))
.

Lemma 6.6
.

If
(\mathbb{F}_{1},cD)
is K-semistable for
D\in|2s+4f|
and
c
sufficiently close to
c_{0}
, then
D
must intersect
s
transversely with multiplicity 1.

Proof.

Suppose first that
D
is singular at a point
p\in s
. Let
e
be the exceptional divisor of the blow up of
p
. Then, we compute
\delta_{\mathbb{F}_{1},cD}(e)<1
for any
c
. Next, suppose
D
meets
s
non-transversally at a smooth point. Let
e^{\prime}
be the exceptional divisor of the
(1,2)
weighted blow up of
p
. Then, we compute
\delta_{\mathbb{F}_{1},cD}(e^{\prime})<1
for any
c\in[c_{0},c_{0}+\epsilon)
where
\epsilon\ll 1
.
∎

Corollary 6.7
.

The pair
(\mathbb{F}_{1},c_{0}(2s_{\infty}+f_{1}+f_{2}))
is K-polystable.

Proof.

Suppose
(X,cD)
is any K-semistable specialization of
(\mathbb{F}_{1},c_{0}(2s_{\infty}+f_{1}+f_{2}))
. Then, by Lemma
6.5
,
X=\mathbb{F}_{1}
. Next,
D
must be non-reduced with components that are specializations of
s_{\infty}
and
f_{i}
. This implies
D=2s_{\infty}+f_{1}+f_{2}
, or
2s_{\infty}+2f
, or
D=2s+f_{i}+f_{j}+f_{k}+f_{\ell}
where
f_{i},f_{j},f_{k},f_{\ell}
are not necessarily distinct. By Lemmas
6.2
and
6.6
,
D
cannot contain
s
and cannot contain a multiple fiber, so we must have
D=2s_{\infty}+f_{1}+f_{2}
. Therefore, we have shown that any K-semistable specialization of
(\mathbb{F}_{1},c_{0}(2s_{\infty}+f_{1}+f_{2}))
is itself, so the pair is K-polystable.
∎

Corollary 6.8
.

For
\epsilon\ll 1
, every K-semistable pair
(\mathbb{F}_{1},cD)
for
c\in[c_{0},c_{0}+\epsilon)
is the strict transform of a nodal quartic curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.

Proof.

The blowup of a curve
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
at a point
p
meets the negative section
s\subset\mathbb{F}_{1}
at two distinct points if and only if
C
has a node at
p
. By the previous lemmas, any curve
D\in|2s+4f|
such that
(\mathbb{F}_{1},cD)
is K-semistable meets
s
at two distinct points, so contracting the section
s
realizes
D
as a nodal quartic curve.
∎

Lemma 6.9
.

Any nodal quartic curve
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
admits an isotrivial specialization to the curve
xyz^{2}
. In particular, the strict transform
D
of any nodal quartic satisfies
(\mathbb{F}_{1},c_{0}D)
is K-semistable.

Proof.

Suppose
C
has a node at
p=[0:0:1]
, so
C
can be written as the vanishing locus of an equation

f(x,y,z)=g_{4}(x,y)+g_{3}(x,y)z+xyz^{2}

up to change of coordinates, where
g_{3},g_{4}
are homogeneous polynomials of degrees 3 and 4, respectively. Consider the action of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
given by
\mathrm{diag}(t,t,t^{-1})
, which acts on
f
by

t\cdot f(x,y,z)=t^{4}g_{4}(x,y)+t^{2}g_{3}(x,y)z+xyz^{2}.

This gives a specialization of
C
to the curve defined by
xyz^{2}
.

Taking the induced specialization on strict transforms yields an isotrivial specialization of divisors in
|2s+4f|
meeting
s
at two distinct points to
2s_{\infty}+f_{1}+f_{2}
. Therefore, by openness of K-semistability, all such pairs are K-semistable.
∎

To complete the proof of Theorem
6.1
, we must show that the moduli space is nonempty for any
c>c_{0}
. To do this, we use the Abban-Zhuang method of admissible flags.

Theorem 6.10
.

For any
c\in(c_{0},\frac{1}{2})
and smooth curve
D\in|2s+4f|
that meets
s
transversely, the pair
(\mathbb{F}_{1},cD)
is K-stable.

Proof.

Using the Abban-Zhuang method of admissible flags with the divisor
s
or a generic section of
s_{\infty}
which does not meet
D
tangentially, it is straightforward to compute that
\delta(\mathbb{F}_{1},cD)>1
for all
c\in(c_{0},\frac{1}{2})
.
∎

With calculation using the theory of admissible flags, it is also possible to determine all elements parametrized by the K-moduli space for
c\in[c_{0},\frac{1}{2})
. This is left for forthcoming work.

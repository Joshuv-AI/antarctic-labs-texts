Frames for source recovery from non-uniform dynamical samples
Ruchi, Lalit Kumar Vashisht

Abstract
Motivated by the work of Aldroubi et al., we investigate the stability of the source term of the discrete dynamical system indexing over a non-uniform discrete set arising from spectral pairs in infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert spaces. Extending results due to Aldroubi et al., firstly, we give a necessary and sufficient condition for the recovery of the source term in finitely many iterations. Afterwards, we derive a necessary condition for the stability of the source term in finitely many iterations when it belongs to the closed subspace of an infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert space. Finally, we give a necessary and sufficient condition for the recovery of the source term in infinitely many iterations. AMS Subject Classification (2020): 93B30, 42C15, 42C30. Keywords: Sampling theory; forcing; frames; reconstruction.

The interplay between frame theory and dynamical sampling has emerged as a riveting area of research in functional analysis, offering profound insights into signal processing, data science, and applied mathematics. Frames were pioneered by Duffin and Schaeffer in a fundamental published paper

,

In one of the directions of applications of frames in engineering science, dynamical sampling is a relatively recent framework that addresses the challenge of reconstructing signals from spatially and temporally evolving measurements. The origins of dynamical sampling trace back to the seminal work of Aldroubi et al.
[
2
,
3
]
in 2013, where they investigated conditions under which a function can be reconstructed from coarse samples of its iterates under a linear operator. Recent research has also accentuated applying dynamical sampling to new challenges, such as real-time data streaming, dynamic environments, and non-stationary signals. These advancements have permeated the applicability of dynamical sampling techniques in areas ranging from medical diagnostics and environmental monitoring to robotics and artificial intelligence. Aldroubi et al. studied applications of Bessel sequences, frames, and Riesz bases in sampling theory in a series of papers, see
[
1
,
2
,
4
,
7
]
and many references therein.

On the other hand, Fuglede
[

in the study of commuting self-adjoint partial differential operators introduced the concept of spectral pairs.
By using the theory of spectral pairs in the Lebesgue space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, Gabardo and Nashed
[

]
generalized the notion of multiresolution analysis (MRA). They introduced the concept of non-uniform multiresolution analysis. In non-uniform multiresolution analysis, the corresponding translation set may not be a group but is a spectrum associated with one-dimensional spectral pairs.
It is applicable in input/output models based on shifts of data points, where shifts may not be uniform in nature. Frame conditions for the discrete Gabor system related to spectral pairs can be found in

Preliminaries

Throughout the paper,
\mathbb{Z}

\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{R}

2.1
Discrete Hilbert frames

A countable collection of vectors
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{I}}
in

\mathcal{H}
is called a
discrete frame
(or discrete Hilbert frame) for
\mathcal{H}
if there exist positive real scalars
\alpha_{0}
and
\beta_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}\norm{f}^{2}\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbf{I}}\absolutevalue{\langle f, f_{k} \rangle}^{2}\leq\beta_{0}\norm{f}^{2}\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,f\in\mathcal{H}.

(2.1)

The constants
\alpha_{0}
and
\beta_{0}
are called
lower
and
upper frame bounds
of
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{I}}
, respectively. If
\alpha_{0}=\beta_{0}
, then
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{I}}
is called tight frame and if
\alpha_{0}=\beta_{0}=1
, then it is called the
Parseval frame
for
\mathcal{H}
. If
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{I}}
only satisfies the upper inequality in (
2.1
), then we say
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{I}}
is a
Bessel sequence
in
\mathcal{H}
with
Bessel bound

\beta_{0}
.
Associated with the Bessel sequence
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{I}}
, the bounded linear operator
T:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
given by

\displaystyle T\{c_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{I}}=\sum_{k\in\mathbf{I}}c_{k}f_{k}

is called the
synthesis operator
or the
pre-frame operator
and the adjoint operator of
T
is the map
T^{*}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
given by

\displaystyle T^{*}(f)=\{\langle f,f_{k}\rangle\}_{k\in\mathbf{I}},

and known as the
analysis operator
. The
frame operator
is the map
\Theta=TT^{*}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
given by

\displaystyle\Theta f=\sum_{k\in\mathbf{I}}\langle f,f_{k}\rangle f_{k}.

If
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{I}}
is a frame for
\mathcal{H}
, then the frame operator
\Theta
is bounded, linear and invertible on
\mathcal{H}
.

The following lemma will be used in the sequel.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
22
, p. 221]

Let
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{I}}
be a frame for
\mathcal{H}
and let
f\in\mathcal{H}
. If
f
has a representation
f=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbf{I}}c_{k}f_{k}
for some coefficients
\{c_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{I}}
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbf{I}}{\absolutevalue{c_{k}}}^{2}=\sum_{k\in\mathbf{I}}{\absolutevalue{\langle f,S^{-1}f_{k}\rangle}}^{2}+\sum_{k\in\mathbf{I}}{\absolutevalue{c_{k}-\langle f,S^{-1}f_{k}\rangle}}^{2}

Let
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{I}}
be a frame for
\mathcal{H}
with frame operator
\Theta
. Then, the family
\{\Theta^{-1}f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{I}}
is a frame for
\mathcal{H}
. It is called the
canonical dual
of
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{I}}
. Instinctive search for the freedom of various choices of coefficients instead of the conventional frame coefficients
\{\langle f,S^{-1}f_{k}\rangle\}_{k\in\mathbf{I}}
, led to the theory of dual frames, which is quite important in applied mathematics.
Let
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{I}}
be a frame for
\mathcal{H}
. Then, the sequence
\{g_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{I}}
is called a
dual frame
of
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{I}}
if
\{g_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{I}}
is a frame for
\mathcal{H}
and each
f\in\mathcal{H}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle f=\sum_{k\in\mathbf{I}}\langle f,g_{k}\rangle f_{k}.

(2.2)

That is, every frame
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{I}}
admits at least one dual frame, namely
\{\Theta^{-1}f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{I}}
, the canonical dual frame. A dual
frame of
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{I}}
other than
\{\Theta^{-1}f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{I}}
is known as
alternative dual
. For fundamental properties of dual frames in separable Hilbert spaces, we refer to
[
22
]
.

We use the symbol

to denote the set

,

.

[

Let

.

[

is a spectral pair.

Definition 2.4
.

3
Non-uniform discrete dynamical system

We begin this section with the definition of non-uniform discrete dynamical system. Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
r
be a fixed odd integer co-prime with
N
such that
1\leq r\leq 2N-1
. We recall a notation
\Lambda:=\left\{0,\frac{r}{N}\right\}+2\mathbb{Z}
. Until and unless specified, symbol
[2K]
denotes the set

\displaystyle[2K]:=\Big\{-2K,-2K+\frac{r}{N},\ldots,-2,-2+\frac{r}{N},0,\frac{r}{N},2,\ldots,2K-2,2K-2+\frac{r}{N}\Big\},K\in\mathbb{N};

and
|[2K]|
denotes the cardinality of the set
[2K]
. The set
[2K]
is only used to represent finite number of iterations.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
A
be a bounded linear operator on
\mathcal{H}
,
W
be a closed subspace of
\mathcal{H}
and
w\in W
is the source term or forcing term. A system of the form

\displaystyle x_{\lambda+\frac{r}{N}}=

\displaystyle Ax_{\lambda}+w,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z};

\displaystyle x_{\lambda+2-\frac{r}{N}}=

\displaystyle Ax_{\lambda}+w,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{+}+\frac{r}{N}\cup\left\{\frac{r}{N}\right\};

\displaystyle x_{\lambda-2-\frac{r}{N}}=

\displaystyle Ax_{\lambda}+w,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{-}+\frac{r}{N},

(3.1)

is called a
non-uniform discrete dynamical system
. Vectors
x_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{H}
,
\lambda\in\Lambda
, is the
\lambda
-th state
of the non-uniform discrete dynamical system in (
3.1
). The terms
x_{0}
and
x_{-2}
are called
initial states
.

Time-space sample measurements

\displaystyle D(x_{0},x_{-2},w)=

\displaystyle\left[\langle x_{\lambda},g_{\lambda^{\prime}}\rangle\right]_{\lambda,\,\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda},

(3.2)

where
\Lambda=\left\{0,\frac{r}{N}\right\}+2\mathbb{Z},N\geq 1
is an integer, and
r
be a fixed odd integer coprime with
N
such that
1\leq r\leq 2N-1
, are obtained via inner products
\langle x_{\lambda},g_{\lambda^{\prime}}\rangle
with the vectors of a Bessel system
\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}\subset\mathcal{H}
, referred to as the set of
spatial sampling vectors
. These measurements are organized in the matrix
D(x_{0},x_{-2},w)
, which is known as the
data matrix
. This matrix is also referred to as the data of the system, or alternatively as the set of time-space samples, measurements, or observations.

To define the notion of stable reconstruction, we need to specify the measurement spaces, where the data resides, along with an appropriate norm. This framework enables us to represent the reconstruction operator

\mathscr{R}
as a bounded linear mapping from the data space
\mathcal{B}
to the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. The following spaces will be used in the sequel:

•

The sequence space
\ell^{2}(\Lambda):=\Big\{x=\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset\mathbb{C}:\sum_{\lambda\in\lambda}{\absolutevalue{x_{\lambda}}}^{2}<\infty\Big\}
is a Hilbert space with respect to the inner product defined by

\langle x,y\rangle=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}x_{\lambda}\bar{y_{\lambda}},\,\,x=\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\,y=\{y_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\in\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
.

•

The sequence space
\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda):=\Big\{x=\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset\mathbb{C}:\sup_{\lambda\in\lambda}\absolutevalue{x_{\lambda}}<\infty\Big\}
is a Banach space endowed with the norm
{\norm{x}}_{\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda)}=\sup\limits_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\absolutevalue{x_{\lambda}}
.

•

We write
\mathbb{C}^{[2K]}:=\Big\{x={(x_{\lambda})}_{\lambda\in[2K]}:x_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{C}\Big\}
.

Now, we familiarize the spaces
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\mathbb{C}^{[2K]})
,
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
, and
\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
which are vital for our work.

Definition 3.2
.

The space
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\mathbb{C}^{[2K]})
is the set of all infinite matrices
T=[a_{ij}]_{i\in[2K],j\in\Lambda}
such that each row

r_{i}
of
T
belongs to
\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
. We endow
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\mathbb{C}^{[2K]})
with the norm

\norm{T}_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{[2K]}}=\sum_{i\in[2K]}{\norm{r_{i}}}_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)}
.

The space
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\mathbb{C}^{[2K]})
is a Banach space which is tantamount to the space of bounded linear operators from
\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
to
\mathbb{C}^{[2K]}
, endowed with the operator norm.

Definition 3.3
.

The space
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
is the set of all doubly infinite matrices
T=[a_{ij}]_{i,j\in\Lambda}
such that each row
r_{i}
of
T
belongs to
\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
, and
\sup_{i\in\Lambda}\norm{r_{i}}_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)}
is finite. We endow
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
with the norm
\norm{T}_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda)}=\sup_{i\in\Lambda}{\norm{r_{i}}}_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)}
.

The space
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
is a Banach space which is tantamount to the space of bounded linear operators from
\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
to
\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda)
, endowed with the operator norm. Now, we are ready to define the subspace
\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
.

Definition 3.4
.

The space
\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
is the set of matrices
\{T=[a_{ij}]:i,j\in\Lambda\}\subset\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
such that there exists a
z\in\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
satisfying

\lim_{\absolutevalue{i}\rightarrow\infty}{\norm{r_{i}-z}}_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)}=0
. We endow
\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
with the norm induced by
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
.

We ruminate for the following two cases of non-uniform discrete dynamical system:

i.

In the first case, the data matrix
D(x_{0},x_{-2},w)=\left[\langle x_{\lambda},g_{\lambda^{\prime}}\rangle\right]_{\lambda,\,\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda}

is obtained from
finitely many iterations
|[2K]|
.

ii.

In the second case, the data matrix
D(x_{0},x_{-2},w)=\left[\langle x_{\lambda},g_{\lambda^{\prime}}\rangle\right]_{\lambda,\,\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda}

is obtained from
infinitely many time iterations.

For the first case, all data measurements are carried out in the space
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\mathbb{C}^{[2K]})
. In the second case of infinitely many time iterations, we utilize the space
\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
which is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
.

3.1
Generalized non-uniform discrete dynamical system

In this subsection, we deal with the non-uniform discrete dynamical systems that are the generalized version of (
3.1
). In this general setting, we assume that the states
x_{\lambda},\lambda\in\Lambda
are obtained via the recursive relation

\displaystyle x_{\lambda}=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}(x_{0},x_{\frac{r}{N}},x_{2},x_{2+\frac{r}{N}}\cdots,x_{\lambda-2+\frac{r}{N}},w),\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{+};&\\
\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}(x_{0},x_{\frac{r}{N}},x_{2},x_{2+\frac{r}{N}}\cdots,x_{\lambda-\frac{r}{N}},w),\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{+}+\frac{r}{N}\cup\left\{\frac{r}{N}\right\};&\\
\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}(x_{-2},x_{-2+\frac{r}{N}},x_{-4},x_{-4+\frac{r}{N}}\cdots,x_{\lambda+2+\frac{r}{N}},w),\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{-};&\\
\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}(x_{-2},x_{-2+\frac{r}{N}},x_{-4},x_{-4+\frac{r}{N}}\cdots,x_{\lambda-\frac{r}{N}},w),\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{-}+\frac{r}{N},\end{cases}

(3.3)

with
w
belongings to the closed subspace
W
of
\mathcal{H}
. In particular,
\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}
can be a non-linear functional of its arguments.

To present certain results in the context of setting (
3.3
), we assume that the system satisfies the following properties:

i.

For each
w\in W
, there is a corresponding unique pair of stationary states. More explicitly, given any
w\in W
, there is a pair of initial states
(x_{0}(w),x_{-2}(w))
such that

x_{\lambda}=\frac{(x_{0}+x_{-2})(w)}{2}\,\,\text{for all}\,\lambda\in\Lambda.

ii.

The correspondence between
w
and its unique pair of stationary states
(x_{0}(w),x_{-2}(w))
is bounded. That is, the mapping
S:W\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}
defined by

S(w)=\frac{(x_{0}+x_{-2})(w)}{2}

is a bounded linear operator, and
S
is called as the stationary mapping operator.

iii.

For any source term
w\in W
and any arbitrary initial states
x_{0},x_{-2}\in\mathcal{H}
, we have

\lim_{\absolutevalue{\lambda}\rightarrow\infty}x_{\lambda}=S(w),

where the above limit is in
\norm{.}_{\mathcal{H}}
.

Definition 3.5
.

A non-uniform discrete dynamical system (
3.3
) satisfying the above properties
(i)-(iii)
is denoted by the quadruple
(\mathcal{H},W,\mathscr{F},S)
.

Stable recovery for non-uniform discrete dynamical system

In this subsection, we define the notion of stable recovery for non-uniform discrete dynamical system and presents some important properties of the space

We start with the non-uniform discrete dynamical system of the form (

(

x_{0},x_{-2}\in\mathcal{H}
and measurements
D(x_{0},x_{-2},w)

given by sampling through a Bessel sequence
\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}
in
\mathcal{H}
as in (
3.2
), then

i.

the source term
w\in W\subseteq\mathcal{H}
is said to be stably recovered from the data matrix
D(x_{0},x_{-2},w)
in finitely many time iterations if there exists a bounded linear operator
\mathscr{R}:\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\mathbb{C}^{[2K]})\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}
such that

\mathscr{R}(D(x_{0},x_{-2},w))=w\;\text{for all}\;x_{0},x_{-2}\in\mathcal{H},w\in W
.

ii.

the source term
w\in W\subseteq\mathcal{H}
is said to be stably recovered from the data matrix
D(x_{0},x_{-2},w)
in infinitely many time iterations if there exists a bounded
linear operator
\mathscr{R}:\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}

such that

\mathscr{R}(D(x_{0},x_{-2},w))=w\;\text{for all}\;x_{0},x_{-2}\in\mathcal{H},w\in W
.

The following Lemma is an adaptation of
[
13
, Lemma 4.1]
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
T:\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda)
be a bounded linear operator. Let
[a_{ij}]
be the matrix representation of
T
with respect to the canonical basis
(e_{j})_{j\in\Lambda}
, i.e.,

Te_{j}=\sum_{i\in\Lambda}a_{ij}e_{j},\,j\in\Lambda
.
Then,

\displaystyle\norm{T}_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda)}=\sup_{i\in\Lambda}{\Big(\sum_{j\in\Lambda}{\absolutevalue{a_{ij}}}^{2}\Big)}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Remark 3.7
.

If
\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}\subset\mathcal{H}
is a Bessel sequence, then
\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
(where
s
stands for strong)
consists of all operators
T\in\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
such that the limit

\displaystyle\lim_{\absolutevalue{i}\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j\in\Lambda}a_{ij}g_{j}

(3.4)

exists in the norm of
\mathcal{H}
. Since all the separable Hilbert spaces are unitarily
equivalent, so the definition does not depend on
\mathcal{H}
. Moreover, we write
\lim{T\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}}
for the limit in (
3.4
).

The next result is an adaptation of
[
13
, Lemma 4.4]
. For the sake of completeness, we provide its proof.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
T=[a_{ij}]\in\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
. Then
T\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
if and only if the rows of
T
are norm convergent in
\ell^{2}(\Lambda),i.e.,

there is a vector
z\in\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{\absolutevalue{i}\rightarrow\infty}\norm{r_{i}-z}_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)}=0,

where
r_{i}=(\ldots,a_{i-2},a_{i-2+\frac{r}{N}},a_{i0},a_{i\frac{r}{N}},\ldots)
denotes the
i
-th row of
T
.

Proof.

First, suppose that
T\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
.
Then, by Remark
3.7
,

T\in\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
such that the limit

\lim_{\absolutevalue{i}\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j\in\Lambda}a_{ij}g_{j}

exists in the norm of
\mathcal{H}
for any Bessel sequence

\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}\subset\mathcal{H}

and since all the orthonormal bases are Bessel sequences, so if we take the Bessel sequence which is an orthonormal basis in
\mathcal{H}
, then the series in (
3.4
) is unitarily equivalent to the vector

\displaystyle z=(\ldots,z_{-4},z_{-4+\frac{r}{N}},z_{-2},z_{-2+\frac{r}{N}},z_{0},z_{\frac{r}{N}},z_{2},z_{2+\frac{r}{N}},\ldots)\in\ell^{2}(\Lambda).

Thus, the existence of limit is equivalent to the rows are convergent in
\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
.

For the converse part, let us assume that
T\in\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
and its rows are convergent in
\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
to a vector

z=(\ldots,z_{-4},z_{-4+\frac{r}{N}},z_{-2},z_{-2+\frac{r}{N}},z_{0},z_{\frac{r}{N}},z_{2},z_{2+\frac{r}{N}},\ldots)\in\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
, and fix an arbitrary Bessel sequence
\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}\subset\mathcal{H}
with Bessel bound
\beta>0
.
Then,

\displaystyle h:=\sum_{j\in\Lambda}z_{j}g_{j}

is a well-defined element of
\mathcal{H}
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle\Big\|h-\sum_{j\in\Lambda}a_{ij}g_{j}\Big\|_{\mathcal{H}}=\Big\|\sum_{j\in\Lambda}z_{j}g_{j}-\sum_{j\in\Lambda}a_{ij}g_{j}\big\|_{\mathcal{H}}=\Big\|\sum_{j\in\Lambda}(z_{j}-a_{ij})g_{j}\Big\|_{\mathcal{H}}=\sqrt{\beta}\norm{z-r_{i}}_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)}.

By assumption,

\norm{z-r_{i}}_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)}\rightarrow 0\;\text{as}\;\absolutevalue{i}\rightarrow\infty
.
Hence,

\lim_{\absolutevalue{i}\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j\in\Lambda}a_{ij}g_{j}=h
.
This implies that

T\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
.
∎

The Lemma
3.8
gives the following corollary:

Corollary 3.9
.

\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
.

Proof.

The proof follows along the same lines as presented in
[
13
, Corollary 4.5]
.
∎

Propelled by the work of
[
13
, Theorem 3.1]
, the next result ratifies an important property of the space
\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
, that it is a natural domain of the reconstruction operator
\mathscr{R}
and the operator
\mathscr{R}:\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}
is bounded.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}\subset\mathcal{H}
be a Bessel sequence with optimal Bessel bound
\beta>0
. Then, for each
T=[a_{ij}]\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
, the limit

\displaystyle\lim{T\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}}=\lim_{\absolutevalue{i}\rightarrow\infty}{[a_{ij}]_{i,\,j\in\Lambda}\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}}:=\lim_{\absolutevalue{i}\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j\in\Lambda}a_{ij}g_{j}

exists in
\mathcal{H}
. Moreover, the mapping

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}:\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}\,\,\text{defined as}\,\,T\mapsto\lim{T\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}}

is a well defined bounded operator whose norm is precisely
\sqrt{\beta}
.

Proof.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
be arbitrary and let
r_{i}=(\ldots,a_{i-2},a_{i-2+\frac{r}{N}},a_{i0},a_{i\frac{r}{N}},\ldots)
represents the
i
-th row of
T
. Write

(T\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda})_{i}:=\sum_{j\in\Lambda}a_{ij}g_{j},\,i\in\Lambda
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\norm{(T\{g_{j}\}_{j \in\Lambda})_{i}}_{\mathcal{H}}=\Big\|\sum_{j\in\Lambda}a_{ij}g_{j}\Big\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\sqrt{\beta}\norm{r_{i}}_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)}\leq\sqrt{\beta}\norm{T}_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda)},\,i\in\Lambda.

Letting
\absolutevalue{i}\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\norm{\mathscr{R}(T)}_{\mathcal{H}}=\norm{ \lim{T\{g_{j}\}_{j \in\Lambda}}}_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\sqrt{\beta}\norm{T}_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda)}.

This implies that the operator
\mathscr{R}
is bounded and

\norm{\mathscr{R}}_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))\rightarrow\mathcal{H}}\leq\sqrt{\beta}
.

Now, for the reverse inequality, let
T\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
be such that all its rows are equal to a fixed vector

\displaystyle z=(\ldots,z_{-4},z_{-4+\frac{r}{N}},z_{-2},z_{-2+\frac{r}{N}},z_{0},z_{\frac{r}{N}},z_{2},z_{2+\frac{r}{N}},\ldots)\in\ell^{2}(\Lambda).

Then,

\norm{T}_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda)}=\norm{z}_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{R}(T)=\lim{T\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}}=\sum_{j\in\Lambda}a_{j}g_{j}
.
Hence, the inequality

\displaystyle\norm{\mathscr{R}(T)}_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\norm{\mathscr{R}}_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))\rightarrow\mathcal{H}}\norm{T}_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda)}

converts into

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{j\in\Lambda}a_{j}g_{j}\Big\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\norm{\mathscr{R}}_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))\rightarrow\mathcal{H}}\norm{z}_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)},\,\,z\in\ell^{2}(\Lambda).

But, since
z\in\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
is arbitrary and
\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}
is a Bessel sequence with optimal Bessel bound
\beta>0
.
This implies,

\sqrt{\beta}\leq\norm{\mathscr{R}}_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))\rightarrow\mathcal{H}}
.
This completes the proof.

∎

Next, we illustrate Theorem
3.10
with the following example.

Example 3.11
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
and let
\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}=\{e_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}
be the standard orthonormal basis for
\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
which forms a Bessel sequence with optimal Bessel bound
1
.
Then, for each
T=[a_{ij}]\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
, the limit

\displaystyle\lim{T\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}}=\lim_{\absolutevalue{i}\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j\in\Lambda}a_{ij}e_{j}=\lim_{\absolutevalue{i}\rightarrow\infty}r_{i},

which exist by the definition of
\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
. Thus, the mapping

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}:\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}\,\,\text{defined as}\,\,T\mapsto\lim{T\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}}

is well defined. Now, we compute

\displaystyle\norm{\mathscr{R}(T)}_{\mathcal{H}}=

\displaystyle\norm{ \lim{T\{g_{j}\}_{j \in\Lambda}}}_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\|\lim_{\absolutevalue{i}\rightarrow\infty}r_{i}\Big\|_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\absolutevalue{i}\rightarrow\infty}\norm{r_{i}}_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{i\in\Lambda}\norm{r_{i}}_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\norm{T}_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda)}.

This implies,
\norm{\mathscr{R}}\leq 1
.
Now, for the reverse inequality, let
T\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
be such that all its rows are equal to a fixed vector

\displaystyle z=(\ldots,0,0,0,0,1,0,0,0,\ldots)\in\ell^{2}(\Lambda).

Then,

\norm{\mathscr{R}(T)}_{\mathcal{H}}=\sup\limits_{i\in\Lambda}\norm{r_{i}}_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)}=1=\norm{T}_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda)}
.
This implies that
\norm{\mathscr{R}}=1
.

3.3
Finite Time Iterations

In this subsection, we deal with the case where the reconstruction of the source term is achieved using a finite number of time iterations of the non-uniform discrete dynamical system (
3.1
). In this case, all data measurements are performed by using the space
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\mathbb{C}^{[2K]})
.
In our initial results, the source
w
can be any element of the space
\mathcal{H}
. While this scenario is less practical compared to the more restricted case of closed subspaces, since, in reality, sources are typically confined to specific spatial regions, it offers a mathematically elegant solution to the source recovery problem.

We begin this section by proving the following lemma, which verifies that the data matrix for finite time iterations belongs to the space
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\mathbb{C}^{[2K]})
.

Lemma 3.12
.

For the non-uniform discrete dynamical system (
3.1
) with any initial states
x_{0},x_{-2}\in\mathcal{H}
, and
1\leq|[2K]|<\infty
, the data matrix
D(x_{0},x_{-2},w)=[\langle x_{\lambda},g_{j}\rangle]_{\lambda\in[2K],\,j\in\Lambda}\in\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\mathbb{C}^{[2K]})
.

Proof.

Since
\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}\subset\mathcal{H}
is a Bessel sequence with Bessel bound
\beta>0
. Then,

\displaystyle\norm{D(x_{0},x_{-2},w)}_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{[2K]}}=\sum_{\lambda\in[2K]}\sum_{j\in\Lambda}{\absolutevalue{\langle x_{\lambda},g_{j}\rangle}}^{2}\leq\beta\sum_{\lambda\in[2K]}{\norm{x_\lambda}}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}<\infty.

Hence, the data matrix
D(x_{0},x_{-2},w)=[\langle x_{\lambda},g_{j}\rangle]_{\lambda\in[2K],\,j\in\Lambda}\in\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\mathbb{C}^{[2K]})
.
∎

The next proposition provides conditions for the non-uniform discrete dynamical system (
3.1
), under which the image of data operator becomes a closed subspace of
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\mathbb{C}^{[2K]})
.

Proposition 3.13
.

For the non-uniform discrete dynamical system (
3.1
) with any initial states
x_{0},x_{-2}\in\mathcal{H}
, and
1\leq|[2K]|<\infty
, the image of the data operator
D
defined by

D:\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathscr{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\mathbb{C}^{[2K]})

D(x_{0},x_{-2},w):=[\langle x_{\lambda},g_{j}\rangle]_{\lambda\in[2K],\,j\in\Lambda}

is a closed subspace in
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\mathbb{C}^{[2K]})
if
\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}
is a frame for
\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

Let
\{(x_{0}^{k},x_{-2}^{k},w^{k})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}
such that

\{D(x_{0}^{k},x_{-2}^{k},w^{k})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
T\in\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\mathbb{C}^{|[2N]|})
, i.e.,

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}D(x_{0}^{k},x_{-2}^{k},w^{k})=T
.
By the definition of
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\mathbb{C}^{[2K]})
,
T
is of the form

\displaystyle T=(r_{-2K},r_{-2K+\frac{r}{N}},\cdots,r_{-2},r_{-2+\frac{r}{N}},r_{0},r_{\frac{r}{N}},\cdots,r_{2K-2},r_{2K-2+\frac{r}{N}}),

where,
r_{i}
denotes the rows of
T
, and for each
\lambda\in[2K]
,

\displaystyle r_{\lambda}:=(a_{\lambda-2K},a_{\lambda-2K+\frac{r}{N}},\cdots,a_{\lambda-2},a_{\lambda-2+\frac{r}{N}},a_{\lambda 0},a_{\lambda\frac{r}{N}},\cdots,a_{\lambda 2K-2},a_{\lambda 2K-2+\frac{r}{N}})\in\ell^{2}(\Lambda).

Since
\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}
is a frame for
\mathcal{H}
. So, the image of the analysis operator

R:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\ell^{2}(\Lambda)

R(h)={(\langle h,g_{j}\rangle)}_{j\in\Lambda}

is a closed linear subspace of
\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
. Note that,

\displaystyle r_{0}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}R(x_{0}^{k})\quad\text{and}\quad r_{-2}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}R(x_{-2}^{k}),

where the above limits exist in
\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
-norm. Since the image of
R
is closed, there exist

x_{0}
and
x_{-2}
in
\mathcal{H}
such that

\displaystyle r_{0}=R(x_{0})={(\langle x_{0},g_{j}\rangle)}_{j\in\Lambda}\quad\text{and}\quad r_{-2}=R(x_{-2}):={(\langle x_{-2},g_{j}\rangle)}_{j\in\Lambda}.

(3.5)

Since
\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}
is a frame with optimal lower and upper bounds
\alpha
and
\beta
, respectively.
Thus,

\displaystyle{\norm{x_{0}^{k}-x_{0}}}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\sum_{j\in\Lambda}{\absolutevalue{\langle x_{0}^{k}-x_{0},g_{j}\rangle}}^{2}=\frac{1}{\alpha}{\Big\|R(x_{0}^{k})-\underbrace{R(x_{0})}_{r_{0}}}\Big\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)},

and

\displaystyle{\norm{x_{-2}^{k}-x_{-2}}}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\sum_{j\in\Lambda}{\absolutevalue{\langle x_{-2}^{k}-x_{-2},g_{j}\rangle}}^{2}=\frac{1}{\alpha}{\Big\|R(x_{-2}^{k})-\underbrace{R(x_{-2})}_{r_{-2}}}\Big\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)},

and thus by using (
3.5
), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}{\norm{x_{0}^{k}-x_{0}}}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha}{\norm{R(x_{0}^{k})-{R(x_{0})}}}^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)}=0,

and

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}{\norm{x_{-2}^{k}-x_{-2}}}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha}{\norm{R(x_{-2}^{k})-{R(x_{-2 })}}}^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)}=0.

(3.6)

This implies that
\{x_{0}^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
x_{0}
and
\{x_{-2}^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
x_{-2}
.
Similarly,

\displaystyle r_{\frac{r}{N}}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}R(x_{\frac{r}{N}}^{k})=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}R(Ax_{0}^{k}+w^{k})=R(Ax_{0})+\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}R(w^{k}),

and

\displaystyle r_{-2+\frac{r}{N}}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}R(x_{-2+\frac{r}{N}}^{k})=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}R(Ax_{-2}^{k}+w^{k})=R(Ax_{-2})+\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}R(w^{k}).

In particular,
\{R(w^{k})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges in
\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
, and since the image of
R
is closed, there exists

w\in\mathcal{H}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}{\norm{R(w^{k})-{R(w)}}}^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)}=0.

By using the same argument as in (
3.6
), we have that
\{w^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
w
in
\mathcal{H}
.
Thus,

\displaystyle r_{\frac{r}{N}}=R(Ax_{0}+w)\quad\text{and}\quad r_{-2+\frac{r}{N}}=R(Ax_{-2}+w).

Finally, as the
\lambda
-th state of the non-uniform discrete dynamical system (
3.1
), with arbitrary initial states
x_{0}^{k},x_{-2}^{k}\in\mathcal{H}
and source term

w^{k}\in W
can be expressed as

x_{\lambda}^{k}=\begin{cases}A^{\lambda}x_{0}^{k}+(I+A+A^{2}+\cdots+A^{\lambda-1})w^{k},\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{+};&\\

A^{-\lambda-2}x_{-2}^{k}+(I+A+A^{2}+\cdots+A^{-\lambda-3})w^{k},\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{-};&\\
A^{-\lambda-1+\frac{r}{N}}x_{-2}^{k}+(I+A+A^{2}+\cdots+A^{-\lambda-2+\frac{r}{N}})w^{k},\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{-}+\frac{r}{N},\end{cases}

and similarly, the
\lambda
-th of the non-uniform discrete dynamical system (
3.1
), with arbitrary initial states
x_{0},x_{-2}\in\mathcal{H}
and source term

w\in W
can be expressed as

x_{\lambda}=\begin{cases}A^{\lambda}x_{0}+(I+A+A^{2}+\cdots+A^{\lambda-1})w,\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{+};&\\
A^{\lambda+1-\frac{r}{N}}x_{0}+(I+A+A^{2}+\cdots+A^{\lambda-\frac{r}{N}})w,\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{+}+\frac{r}{N}\cup\left\{\frac{r}{N}\right\};&\\
A^{-\lambda-2}x_{-2}+(I+A+A^{2}+\cdots+A^{-\lambda-3})w,\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{-};&\\
A^{-\lambda-1+\frac{r}{N}}x_{-2}+(I+A+A^{2}+\cdots+A^{-\lambda-2+\frac{r}{N}})w,\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{-}+\frac{r}{N}.\end{cases}

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle r_{\lambda}=

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}R(x_{\lambda}^{k})\;\text{for all}\;\lambda\in\Lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}R(A^{\lambda}x_{0}^{k}+(I+A+A^{2}+\cdots+A^{\lambda-1})w^{k}),\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{+};&\\
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}R(A^{\lambda+1-\frac{r}{N}}x_{0}^{k}+(I+A+A^{2}+\cdots+A^{\lambda-\frac{r}{N}})w^{k}),\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{+}+\frac{r}{N}\cup\left\{\frac{r}{N}\right\};&\\
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}R(A^{-\lambda-2}x_{-2}^{k}+(I+A+A^{2}+\cdots+A^{-\lambda-3})w^{k}),\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{-};&\\
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}R(A^{-\lambda-1+\frac{r}{N}}x_{-2}^{k}+(I+A+A^{2}+\cdots+A^{-\lambda-2+\frac{r}{N}})w^{k}),\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{-}+\frac{r}{N},\end{cases}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}R(A^{\lambda}x_{0}+(I+A+A^{2}+\cdots+A^{\lambda-1})w),\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{+};&\\
R(A^{\lambda+1-\frac{r}{N}}x_{0}+(I+A+A^{2}+\cdots+A^{\lambda-\frac{r}{N}})w),\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{+}+\frac{r}{N}\cup\left\{\frac{r}{N}\right\};&\\
R(A^{-\lambda-2}x_{-2}+(I+A+A^{2}+\cdots+A^{-\lambda-3})w),\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{-};&\\
R(A^{-\lambda-1+\frac{r}{N}}x_{-2}+(I+A+A^{2}+\cdots+A^{-\lambda-2+\frac{r}{N}})w),\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{-}+\frac{r}{N},\end{cases}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R(x_{\lambda}),\,\lambda\in\Lambda.

This implies that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}D(x_{0}^{k},x_{-2}^{k},w^{k})=T=D(x_{0},x_{-2},w)
.
Thus,
T
belongs to the image of the data operator
D
. Hence, the image of
D
is a closed subspace in
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\mathbb{C}^{[2K]})
.
∎

Taking exhortation from
[
13
, Theorem 3.2]
, the following theorem incorporates a necessary and sufficient condition on
\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}\subset\mathcal{H}
for the existence of the reconstruction operator
\mathscr{R}:\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\mathbb{C}^{[2K]})\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}
.

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}\subset\mathcal{H}
be a Bessel sequence with Bessel bound
\beta>0
. Then, for the non-uniform discrete dynamical system (
3.1
), with any arbitrary initial states
x_{0},x_{-2}\in\mathcal{H}
, the source term

w\in W
can be stably recovered from the measurements
D(x_{0},x_{-2},w)=[\langle x_{\lambda},g_{j}\rangle]_{\lambda\in[2K],\,j\in\Lambda}
for some
1\leq|[2K]|<\infty
if and only if
\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}
is a frame for
\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

First, assume that the source term

w\in W
can be stably recovered from the measurements
D(x_{0},x_{-2},w)=[\langle x_{\lambda},g_{j}\rangle]_{\lambda\in[2K],\,j\in\Lambda}
in
|[2K]|
time of iterations. That is, there exists a bounded linear operator

\mathscr{R}:\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\mathbb{C}^{[2K]})\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}
such that

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}(D(x_{0},x_{-2},w))=w\;\text{for all}\;x_{0},x_{-2},w\in\mathcal{H}.

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}(\left[\langle x_{\lambda},g_{j}\rangle\right]_{\lambda,\,j\in\Lambda})=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\Big(\langle x_{-4},g_{j}\rangle-\sum_{i\in\Lambda}\bar{c_{ij}}\langle\sum_{k\in\Lambda}\langle x_{-2+\frac{r}{N}},g_{k}\rangle\tilde{g_{k}},g_{i}\rangle\Big)\tilde{g_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\Big(\langle x_{-4},g_{j}\rangle-\sum_{i\in\Lambda}\bar{c_{ij}}\langle x_{-2+\frac{r}{N}},g_{i}\rangle\Big)\tilde{g_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w,

which completes the proof.
∎

The following example illustrates the Theorem
3.14
.

Example 3.15
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
and consider the non-uniform discrete dynamical system (
3.1
) with
A(e_{j})=\lambda_{j}e_{j}
where
\lambda_{j}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2^{\frac{j}{2}}},&\;\text{if}\;j\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{+}\cup\{0\};\\
{2^{\frac{j}{2}}},&\;\text{if}\;j\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{-};\\
0,&\;\text{if}\,j\in 2\mathbb{Z}+\frac{r}{N}.\end{cases}

Let
\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}=\{e_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}
be the standard orthonormal basis for
\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
, which forms a Parseval frame for
\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
with frame bound
1
.
Let
x_{0}=e_{\frac{r}{N}},x_{-2}=e_{-2}
. Then

\displaystyle Ae_{j}=A^{*}e_{j}=\sum_{i\in\Lambda}c_{ij}e_{i}\;\;\text{where, each}\;\;c_{ij}=\langle A^{*}e_{j},e_{i}\rangle,\,i\in\Lambda.

Case-(i)
When
\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}
, define
\mathscr{R}:\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\mathbb{C}^{[2]})\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}\text{ by}

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}(T)=\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\Big(a_{\frac{r}{N}j}-\sum_{i\in\Lambda}\bar{c_{ij}}\langle\sum_{k\in\Lambda}a_{0k}e_{k},e_{i}\rangle\Big)e_{j},\text{where}\,[2]=\Big\{0,\frac{r}{N}\Big\}.

Case-(ii)
When
\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{+}+\frac{r}{N}\cup\left\{\frac{r}{N}\right\}
, define

\mathscr{R}:\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\mathbb{C}^{[2]})\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}\,\text{by}

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}(T)=\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\Big(a_{2j}-\sum_{i\in\Lambda}\bar{c_{ij}}\langle\sum_{k\in\Lambda}a_{\frac{r}{N}k}e_{k},e_{i}\rangle\Big)e_{j},\text{where}\,[2]=\Big\{\frac{r}{N},2\Big\}.

Case-(iii)
When
\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{-}+\frac{r}{N}
, define

\mathscr{R}:\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\mathbb{C}^{[2]})\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}\quad\text{by}

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}(T)=\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\Big(a_{-4j}-\sum_{i\in\Lambda}\bar{c_{ij}}\langle\sum_{k\in\Lambda}a_{-2+\frac{r}{N}k}e_{k},e_{i}\rangle\Big)e_{j},\text{where}\,[2]=\Big\{-2+\frac{r}{N},-4\Big\}.

Then, in each case
\norm{\mathscr{R}(T)}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}\leq 2{\norm{T}}^{2}_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\mathbb{C}^{[2]})}
, i.e.,
\mathscr{R}
is a bounded linear operator and

\mathscr{R}(D(x_{0},x_{-2},w))=w
. Hence,
w
can be recovered stably in finitely many iterations.

In the next result, we constrain the source term to lie within a closed subspace
W\subset\mathcal{H}
. From a practical perspective, this case is particularly significant, despite being more mathematically intricate. For instance, in applications like environmental monitoring, prior knowledge about the locations of primary pollution sources translates to considering closed subspaces of the ambient space

\mathcal{H}
. However, the mathematical formulation of a solution in this context proves to be more nuanced. Now we give a necessary condition for the stable recovery of source term of the the non-uniform discrete dynamical system (
3.1
) in finitely many iterations.

Theorem 3.16
.

Suppose

1.

\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}
is a Bessel sequence in
\mathcal{H}
with Bessel bound
\beta>0
.

2.

W
is a closed subspace
\mathcal{H}
and
P_{W}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
denotes the orthogonal projection onto
W
.

Then, for the non-uniform discrete dynamical system (
3.1
), with any arbitrary initial states
x_{0},x_{-2}\in\mathcal{H}
and
1\notin\sigma(A)
,
\{P_{W}(I-{A^{*}})^{-1}g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}
is a frame for
W
if the source term

w\in W
can be stably recovered from the measurements
D(x_{0},x_{-2},w)=[\langle x_{\lambda},g_{j}\rangle]_{\lambda\in[2K],\,j\in\Lambda}
for some
1\leq|[2K]|<\infty
.

Proof.

Suppose that the source term

w\in W
can be stably recovered from the measurements
D(x_{0},x_{-2},w)=[\langle x_{\lambda},g_{j}\rangle]_{\lambda\in[2K],\,j\in\Lambda}
in
|[2K]|
time of iterations. That is, there exists a bounded linear operator

\mathscr{R}:\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\mathbb{C}^{[2K]})\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}
such that

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}(D(x_{0},x_{-2},w))=w\;\text{for all}\;x_{0},x_{-2}\in\mathcal{H},w\in W.

Hence, there exists a positive constant
c
such that

\displaystyle\norm{\mathscr{R}(T)}\leq c\sum_{\lambda\in[2K]}\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\absolutevalue{a_{\lambda j}}^{2},\,\,T\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\mathbb{C}^{[2K]}),

(3.18)

and

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}(D(x_{0},x_{-2},w))=w,\,x_{0},x_{-2}\in\mathcal{H},w\in W.

(3.19)

Our aim is to prove that
\{P_{W}(I-{A^{*}})^{-1}g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}
is a frame for
W
. By using (
3.18
) and (
3.19
), we compute

\displaystyle{\norm{w}}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}\leq

\displaystyle c\sum_{\lambda\in[2K]}\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\absolutevalue{\langle x_{\lambda},g_{j}\rangle}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\Big[\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\Big\{\sum_{\lambda\in[2K]\cap 2\mathbb{Z}^{+}}\absolutevalue{\langle x_{\lambda},g_{j}\rangle}^{2}+\sum_{\lambda\in[2K]\cap 2\mathbb{Z}^{+}+\frac{r}{N}\cup\left\{\frac{r}{N}\right\}}\absolutevalue{\langle x_{\lambda},g_{j}\rangle}^{2}\Big.\Big.

\displaystyle\Big.\Big.+\sum_{\lambda\in[2K]\cap 2\mathbb{Z}^{-}}\absolutevalue{\langle x_{\lambda},g_{j}\rangle}^{2}+\sum_{\lambda\in[2K]\cap 2\mathbb{Z}^{-}+\frac{r}{N}}\absolutevalue{\langle x_{\lambda},g_{j}\rangle}^{2}\Big\}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\Big[\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\Big\{\sum_{\lambda\in[2K]\cap 2\mathbb{Z}^{+}}\Big|\langle A^{\lambda}x_{0}+\sum_{i=0}^{\lambda-1}A^{i}w,g_{j}\rangle\Big|^{2}\Big.\Big.

\displaystyle+\sum_{\lambda\in[2K]\cap 2\mathbb{Z}^{+}+\frac{r}{N}\cup\left\{\frac{r}{N}\right\}}\Big|\langle A^{\lambda+1-\frac{r}{N}}x_{0}+\sum_{i=0}^{\lambda-\frac{r}{N}}A^{i}w,g_{j}\rangle\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\lambda\in[2K]\cap 2\mathbb{Z}^{-}}\Big|\langle A^{-\lambda-2}x_{-2}+\sum_{i=0}^{-\lambda-3}A^{i}w,g_{j}\rangle\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\Big.\Big.+\sum_{\lambda\in[2K]\cap 2\mathbb{Z}^{-}+\frac{r}{N}}\Big|\langle A^{-\lambda-1+\frac{r}{N}}x_{-2}+\sum_{i=0}^{-\lambda-2+\frac{r}{N}}A^{i}w,g_{j}\rangle\Big|^{2}\Big\}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\Big[\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\Big\{\sum_{\lambda\in[2K]\cap 2\mathbb{Z}^{+}}\absolutevalue{\langle A^{\lambda}x_{0}+(I-A^{\lambda})(I-A)^{-1}w,g_{j}\rangle}^{2}\Big.\Big.

\displaystyle+\sum_{\lambda\in[2K]\cap 2\mathbb{Z}^{+}+\frac{r}{N}\cup\left\{\frac{r}{N}\right\}}\absolutevalue{\langle A^{\lambda+1-\frac{r}{N}}x_{0}+(I-A^{\lambda+1-\frac{r}{N}})(I-A)^{-1}w,g_{j}\rangle}^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\lambda\in[2K]\cap 2\mathbb{Z}^{-}}\absolutevalue{\langle A^{-\lambda-2}x_{-2}+(I-A^{-\lambda-2})(I-A)^{-1}w,g_{j}\rangle}^{2}

\displaystyle\Big.\Big.+\sum_{\lambda\in[2K]\cap 2\mathbb{Z}^{-}+\frac{r}{N}}\absolutevalue{\langle A^{-\lambda-1+\frac{r}{N}}x_{-2}+(I-A^{-\lambda-1+\frac{r}{N}})(I-A)^{-1}w,g_{j}\rangle}^{2}\Big\}\Big],

where we have used that the non-uniform discrete dynamical system (
3.1
) can be expressed as

x_{\lambda}=\begin{cases}A^{\lambda}x_{0}+(I-A^{\lambda})(I-A)^{-1}w,\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{+};&\\
A^{\lambda+1-\frac{r}{N}}x_{0}+(I-A^{\lambda+1-\frac{r}{N}})(I-A)^{-1}w,\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{+}+\frac{r}{N}\cup\left\{\frac{r}{N}\right\};&\\
A^{-\lambda-2}x_{-2}+(I-A^{-\lambda-2})(I-A)^{-1}w,\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{-};&\\
A^{-\lambda-1+\frac{r}{N}}x_{-2}+(I-A^{-\lambda-1+\frac{r}{N}})(I-A)^{-1}w,\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{-}+\frac{r}{N}.\end{cases}

For any
w\in W
, consider
x_{0}=x_{-2}=(I-A)^{-1}w
and by putting it into the above inequality, we get

\displaystyle\norm{w}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}\leq|[2K]|c\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\absolutevalue{\langle(I-A)^{-1}w,g_{j}\rangle}^{2}\leq c^{\prime}\norm{(I-A)^{-1}w}^{2}\leq c^{\prime\prime}\norm{w}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}},

where
c^{\prime}=|[2K]|c\beta
and
c^{\prime\prime}=|[2K]|c\beta\norm{(I-A)^{-1}}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}}
.
Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\absolutevalue{\langle(I-A)^{-1}w,g_{j}\rangle}^{2}=\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\absolutevalue{\langle w,((I-A)^{-1})^{*}g_{j}\rangle}^{2}=\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\absolutevalue{\langle w,P_{W}(I-A^*)^{-1}g_{j}\rangle}^{2}.

So, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|[2K]|c}\norm{w}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\absolutevalue{\langle w,P_{W}(I-A^*)^{-1}g_{j}\rangle}^{2}\leq c^{\prime\prime}\norm{w}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}},\,w\in W.

Therefore,
\{P_{W}(I-{A^{*}})^{-1}g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}
is a frame for
W
.

∎

x_{-2}
, we necessarily have

\displaystyle\langle x_{-2K},g\rangle=\langle x_{-2K+\frac{r}{N}},g\rangle=\cdots=\langle x_{-2+\frac{r}{N}},g\rangle=\langle x_{0},g\rangle=\langle x_{\frac{r}{N}},g\rangle=\cdots=\langle x_{2K-2+\frac{r}{N}},g\rangle=0.

Hence, it is not possible to stably recover the source term
cw
if we only have the first
|[2K]|
samples of measurements.

3.4
Infinite Time Iterations

In this subsection, we investigate the case in which the data matrix
D(x_{0},x_{-2},w)
entails infinitely many time samples and
W\subsetneq\mathcal{H}
. In this case, all the data measurements are carried out in the space
\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
.

We commence this section by establishing the following lemma which affirms that the data matrix for infinite time iterations belongs to the space
\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
.

Lemma 3.18
.

Let
\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}\subset\mathcal{H}
be a Bessel sequence with Bessel bound
\beta>0
.
Then, for the non-uniform discrete dynamical system (
3.3
)
(\mathcal{H},W,\mathscr{F},S)
, with any initial states
x_{0},x_{-2}\in\mathcal{H}
, the data matrix
D(x_{0},x_{-2},w)=[\langle x_{\lambda},g_{j}\rangle]_{\lambda,\,j\in\Lambda}\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
.

Proof.

By supposition, the states
\{x_{\lambda}\}
of the dynamical system (
3.3
) satisfy

\displaystyle\norm{x_{\lambda}-S(w)}_{\mathcal{H}}\longrightarrow 0\;\text{as}\;\absolutevalue{\lambda}\longrightarrow\infty.

(3.24)

The
\lambda
-th row of
D(x_{0},x_{-2},w)
is

r_{\lambda}=(\langle x_{\lambda},g_{j}\rangle)_{\lambda,\,j\in\Lambda}
.
Let

r=(\langle S(w),g_{j}\rangle)_{\lambda,\,j\in\Lambda}
.
Since

\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}\subset\mathcal{H}
is a Bessel sequence with Bessel bound
\beta>0
.
By using the equation (
3.24
), we have

\displaystyle{\norm{r_{\lambda}-r}}^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)}=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\absolutevalue{\langle x_{\lambda},g_{j}\rangle-\langle S(w),g_{j}\rangle}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\absolutevalue{\langle x_{\lambda}-S(w),g_{j}\rangle}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\beta{\norm{x_{\lambda}-S(w)}}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}

(3.25)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\,0\,\,\text{as}\,\,\absolutevalue{\lambda}\rightarrow\infty.

This implies,

r_{\lambda}\longrightarrow r\,\,\text{as}\,\,\absolutevalue{\lambda}\rightarrow\infty
.
Hence, by using Lemma
3.8
, we have

\displaystyle D(x_{0},x_{-2},w)\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda)).

∎

Motivated by
[
13
, Theorem 3.4]
, the next theorem betokens the characterization for the stable recovery of the source term of the non-uniform discrete dynamical system (
3.3
) from the data measurements
D(x_{0},x_{-2},w)
.

Theorem 3.19
.

Suppose

1.

\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}\subset\mathcal{H}
is a Bessel sequence with Bessel bound
\beta>0
.

2.

W
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{H}
.

Then, for the non-uniform discrete dynamical system (
3.3
)
(\mathcal{H},W,\mathscr{F},S)
with the assumption that
\mathscr{F}
is linear, each source term

w\in W
can be stably recovered from the measurements
D(x_{0},x_{-2},w)=[\langle x_{\lambda},g_{j}\rangle]_{\lambda,\,j\in\Lambda}
if and only if
\{S^{*}g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}
is a frame for
W
.

Proof.

For any
x_{0},x_{-2}\in\mathcal{H}
and any source term
w\in W
,
by using Lemma
3.18
, the data matrix

D(x_{0},x_{-2},w)\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))
. First, assume that each source term

w\in W
can be stably recovered from the measurements
D(x_{0},x_{-2},w)=[\langle x_{\lambda},g_{j}\rangle]_{\lambda,\,j\in\Lambda}
. That is, there exits a bounded linear operator

\mathscr{R}:\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}

such that

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}(D(x_{0},x_{-2},w))=w\;\text{for all}\,x_{0},x_{-2}\in\mathcal{H},w\in W.

Hence,

\displaystyle\norm{w}_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\norm{\mathscr{R}}_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}}\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}{\Big(\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\absolutevalue{\langle x_{\lambda},g_{j}\rangle}^{2}\Big)}^{\frac{1}{2}},\,x_{0},x_{-2}\in\mathcal{H},w\in W.

(3.26)

Since
x_{0},x_{-2}\in\mathcal{H}
are arbitary, so we can take
x_{0}=x_{-2}=S(w)
.
Hence,
x_{\lambda}=S(w)\;\text{for all}\;\lambda\in\Lambda
and the equation (
3.26
) becomes

\displaystyle\norm{w}_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\norm{\mathscr{R}}_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}}{\Big(\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\absolutevalue{\langle S(w),g_{j}\rangle}^{2}\Big)}^{\frac{1}{2}},\;w\in W,

which can be rewritten as

\displaystyle{\norm{\mathscr{R}}}^{-2}_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}}{\norm{w}}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\absolutevalue{\langle w,S^{*}g_{j}\rangle}^{2},\;w\in W.

Finally, since
\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}
is Bessel sequence with Bessel bound
\beta>0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\absolutevalue{\langle w,S^{*}g_{j}\rangle}^{2}=\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\absolutevalue{\langle S(w),g_{j}\rangle}^{2}\leq\beta{\norm{S(w)}}^{2}\leq\beta{\norm{S}}^{2}_{W\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}}{\norm{w}}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}\;\text{for all}\;w\in W.

Hence,

\displaystyle A{\norm{w}}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\absolutevalue{\langle w,S^{*}g_{j}\rangle}^{2}\leq B{\norm{w}}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}\;\text{for all}\;w\in W,

with
A={\norm{\mathscr{R}}}^{-2}_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}}
and
B=\beta{\norm{S}}^{2}_{W\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}}
.
Therefore,
\{S^{*}g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}
is a frame for
W
.

Conversely, let us assume that
\{S^{*}g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}
is a frame for
W
, and
\{\widetilde{g_{j}}\}_{j\in\Lambda}\subset W
represents a dual frame of
\{S^{*}g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}
. Then, by the definition of
D(x_{0},x_{-2},w)
, we have

\displaystyle(D(x_{0},x_{-2},w)\{\widetilde{g_{j}}\}_{j\in\Lambda})_{\lambda}=\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\langle x_{\lambda},g_{j}\rangle\widetilde{g_{j}},\;\lambda\in\Lambda.

Clearly,

(D(x_{0},x_{-2},w)\{\widetilde{g_{j}}\}_{j\in\Lambda})_{\lambda}\in W
. Now, by using the duality of
\{\widetilde{g_{j}}\}_{j\in\Lambda}\subset W
, we have

\displaystyle w=\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\langle w,S^{*}g_{j}\rangle\widetilde{g_{j}}.

Hence, by using the equation (
3.25
), we compute

\displaystyle{\norm{(D\{\widetilde{g_j}\}_{j \in\Lambda})_{\lambda}-w}}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\norm{\sum_{j \in\Lambda} \langle x_{\lambda},g_{j} \rangle\widetilde{g_j}-\sum_{j \in\Lambda} \langle w,S^{*}g_{j} \rangle\widetilde{g_j}}}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\norm{\sum_{j \in\Lambda}\Big(\langle x_{\lambda},g_{j} \rangle- \langle w,S^{*}g_{j} \rangle\Big) \widetilde{g_j}}}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\beta^{\prime}\sum_{j\in\Lambda}{\absolutevalue{\langle x_{\lambda},g_{j} \rangle- \langle w,S^{*}g_{j} \rangle}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta^{\prime}\sum_{j\in\Lambda}{\absolutevalue{\langle x_{\lambda}-S(w),g_{j} \rangle}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\beta^{\prime}\beta{\norm{x_{\lambda}-S(w)}}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}\longrightarrow 0\;\text{as}\;\absolutevalue{\lambda}\rightarrow\infty,

where
\beta^{\prime}
is the upper frame bound of
\{\widetilde{g_{j}}\}_{j\in\Lambda}
. This implies that
\mathscr{R}:\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}\quad\text{defined by}\quad\mathscr{R}(T)=\lim{T\{\widetilde{g_{j}}\}_{j\in\Lambda}}\quad\text{satisfies}\quad\mathscr{R}(D(x_{0},x_{-2},w))=w
.
Therefore, the conclusion follows from the Theorem
3.10
.
∎

We now illustrate Theorem
3.19
with the following example.

Example 3.20
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
and
W=\text{span}\{e_{j}:j\in\Lambda,j\neq 0,-2\}
. Let
\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}=\{e_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}
be the standard orthonormal basis for
\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
which form a Bessel sequence with optimal Bessel bound
1
. Let
A(e_{j})=\begin{cases}e_{j},&\;\text{if}\;j\neq 0,-2;\\
2e_{j},&\;\text{if}\;j\in\Lambda\setminus\{0,-2\}.\end{cases}
Then,
\rho(A)=2
.
Let
x_{0}=x_{-2}=(I-A)w=-w
. Define

S:W\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}
by

S(w)=(I-A)w=-w
. Then, all the conditions of the non-uniform discrete dynamical system (
3.3
) are satisfied.
Also,

\displaystyle S^{*}(g_{j})=\begin{cases}0,&\;\text{if}\;j\neq 0,-2;\\
-e_{j},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Thus,
\{S^{*}g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}
is a frame for
W
. Let
\{\tilde{g_{j}}\}_{j\in\Lambda}=\begin{cases}0,&\;\text{if}\,j\neq 0,-2;\\
-e_{j},&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}
be the canonical dual frame of
\{S^{*}g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}
. Define
\mathscr{R}:\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}
by
\mathscr{R}(T)=\lim{T\{\tilde{g_{j}}\}_{j\in\Lambda}}
.
Then
\mathscr{R}
is a bounded linear operator such that

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}(D)=

\displaystyle\lim{D\{\tilde{g_{j}}\}_{j\in\Lambda}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\absolutevalue{\lambda}\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\langle x_{\lambda},g_{j}\rangle\tilde{g_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\absolutevalue{\lambda}\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j\in\Lambda,j\neq 0,-2}\langle x_{\lambda},e_{j}\rangle(-e_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\Lambda,j\neq 0,-2}\langle\lim_{\absolutevalue{\lambda}\rightarrow\infty}x_{\lambda},e_{j}\rangle(-e_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\Lambda,j\neq 0,-2}\langle S(w),e_{j}\rangle(-e_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\Lambda,j\neq 0,-2}\langle-w,e_{j}\rangle(-e_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\Lambda,j\neq 0,-2}\langle w,e_{j}\rangle e_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w.

Hence,
w
can be recovered stably.

As a fruitage of Theorem
3.19
, we characterize stable recovery for the source term of the non-uniform discrete dynamical system (
3.1
) with the spectral radius
\rho(A)<1
.

Theorem 3.21
.

Suppose

1.

\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}\subset\mathcal{H}
is a Bessel sequence with Bessel bound
\beta>0
.

2.

W
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{H}
.

Then, for the non-uniform discrete dynamical system (
3.1
), with
\rho(A)<1
, each source term
w\in W
can be stably recovered from the measurements
D(x_{0},x_{-2},w)=[\langle x_{\lambda},g_{j}\rangle]_{\lambda,\,j\in\Lambda}
if and only if
\{P_{W}(I-{A^{*}})^{-1}g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}
is a frame for
W
.

Proof.

To prove the result, it is sufficient to prove that the conditions of Theorem
3.19
are satisfied. Notice that the equation (
3.1
) can be written as

x_{\lambda}=\begin{cases}A^{\lambda}x_{0}+(I-A^{\lambda})(I-A)^{-1}w,\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{+};&\\
A^{\lambda+1-\frac{r}{N}}x_{0}+(I-A^{\lambda+1-\frac{r}{N}})(I-A)^{-1}w,\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{+}+\frac{r}{N}\cup\left\{\frac{r}{N}\right\};&\\
A^{-\lambda-2}x_{-2}+(I-A^{-\lambda-2})(I-A)^{-1}w,\,\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{-};&\\
A^{-\lambda-1+\frac{r}{N}}x_{-2}+(I-A^{-\lambda-1+\frac{r}{N}})(I-A)^{-1}w,\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{-}+\frac{r}{N},\end{cases}

which can be further simplified as

x_{\lambda}-(I-A)^{-1}w=\begin{cases}A^{\lambda}\left(x_{0}-(I-A)^{-1}w\right),\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{+};&\\
A^{\lambda+1-\frac{r}{N}}\left(x_{0}-(I-A)^{-1}w\right),\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{+}+\frac{r}{N}\cup\left\{\frac{r}{N}\right\};&\\
A^{-\lambda-2}\left(x_{-2}-(I-A)^{-1}w\right),\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{-};&\\
A^{-\lambda-1+\frac{r}{N}}\left(x_{-2}-(I-A)^{-1}w\right),\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{-}+\frac{r}{N}.\end{cases}

Hence,

\norm{x_{\lambda}-(I-A)^{-1}w}_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\begin{cases}\norm{A^{\lambda}}_{\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}}\norm{x_{0}-(I-A)^{-1}w}_{\mathcal{H}},\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{+};&\\
\norm{A^{\lambda+1-\frac{r}{N}}}_{\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}}\norm{x_{0}-(I-A)^{-1}w}_{\mathcal{H}},\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{+}+\frac{r}{N}\cup\left\{\frac{r}{N}\right\};&\\
\norm{A^{-\lambda-2}}_{\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}}\norm{x_{-2}-(I-A)^{-1}w}_{\mathcal{H}},\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{-};&\\
\norm{A^{-\lambda-1+\frac{r}{N}}}_{\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}}\norm{x_{-2}-(I-A)^{-1}w}_{\mathcal{H}},\,\,\lambda\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{-}+\frac{r}{N}.\end{cases}

By the assumption that
\rho(A)<1
, we have

\displaystyle\norm{A^{\lambda}}_{\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}},\norm{A^{\lambda+1-\frac{r}{N}}}_{\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}}\longrightarrow 0\;\text{as}\;\lambda\rightarrow\infty,\quad\text{and}\quad

\displaystyle\norm{A^{-\lambda-2}}_{\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}},\norm{A^{-\lambda-1+\frac{r}{N}}}_{\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}}\longrightarrow 0\;\text{as}\;\lambda\rightarrow-\infty.

This implies,

\norm{x_{\lambda}-(I-A)^{-1}w}_{\mathcal{H}}\longrightarrow 0\;\text{as}\;\absolutevalue{\lambda}\rightarrow\infty
. This suggests that the pair of the stationary points of the non-uniform discrete dynamical system is uniquely determined by
S(w)=(I-A)^{-1}w
.
In fact, if we take
x_{0}=x_{-2}=(I-A)^{-1}w
, then
x_{\lambda}=\frac{x_{0}+x_{-2}}{2}\;\text{for all}\;\lambda\in\Lambda
.
Also,

\displaystyle\norm{(I-A)w}=\norm{Iw-Aw}\geq\absolutevalue{\norm{w}-\norm{Aw}}\geq\norm{w}.

Thus,
(I-A)^{-1}
exists and bounded. Therefore,
S:=(I-A)^{-1}\restriction_{W}
is bounded and invertible. Also, the adjoint operator of
S
is given by
S^{*}=P_{W}(I-A^{*})^{-1}
. Hence, the conclusion follows from Theorem
3.19
.
∎

The following example validates Theorem
3.21
.

Example 3.22
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
and
W=span\{e_{0},e_{\frac{r}{N}}\}
. Let
\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}=\{e_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}
be the standard orthonormal basis for
\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
which form a Bessel sequence with optimal Bessel bound
1
. Consider the non-uniform discrete dynamical system (
3.1
) with
A(e_{j})=\frac{1}{4}e_{j}\;\text{for all}\;j\in\Lambda
. Then
\rho(A)<1
.
Let
x_{0}=x_{-2}=(I-A)^{-1}w
.
Define
S:W\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}
by

S(w)=(I-A)^{-1}w=\frac{4}{3}w
.
Also,

\displaystyle P_{W}(I-A^{*})^{-1}(g_{j})=\begin{cases}\frac{4}{3}e_{j},&\;\text{if}\;j=0,\frac{r}{N};\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Thus,
\{P_{W}(I-A^{*})^{-1}(g_{j})\}_{j\in\Lambda}=\{\cdots,0,0,\frac{4}{3}e_{0},\frac{4}{3}e_{\frac{r}{N}},0,0,\cdots\}
is a frame for
W
.

Consider the canonical dual
\{\tilde{g_{j}}\}_{j\in\Lambda}=\{\cdots,0,0,\frac{3}{4}e_{0},\frac{3}{4}e_{\frac{r}{N}},0,0,\cdots\}
of
\{P_{W}(I-A^{*})^{-1}(g_{j})\}_{j\in\Lambda}
.
Define
\mathscr{R}:\mathcal{B}^{s}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda))\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}
by
\mathscr{R}(T)=\lim{T\{\tilde{g_{j}}\}_{j\in\Lambda}}
.
Then
\mathscr{R}
is a bounded linear operator such that

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}(D)=

\displaystyle\lim{D\{\tilde{g_{j}}\}_{j\in\Lambda}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\absolutevalue{\lambda}\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\langle x_{\lambda},g_{j}\rangle\tilde{g_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\absolutevalue{\lambda}\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\frac{r}{N}}\langle x_{\lambda},e_{j}\rangle\frac{3}{4}e_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\frac{r}{N}}\langle\lim_{\absolutevalue{\lambda}\rightarrow\infty}x_{\lambda},e_{j}\rangle\frac{3}{4}e_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\frac{r}{N}}\langle S(w),e_{j}\rangle\frac{3}{4}e_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\frac{r}{N}}\langle\frac{4}{3}w,e_{j}\rangle\frac{3}{4}e_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\frac{r}{N}}\langle w,e_{j}\rangle e_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w.

Hence,
w
can be recovered stably.

Standards for 24/7 Emergency Interventional Radiology Care
Authors: Ung Bae Jeon
Published: 2026

Commentary on Mahnken AH, et al. CIRSE standards of practice for the provision of emergency IR care on a 24/7 basis. Cardiovasc Intervent Radiol 2026;49:180-194 (https://doi.org/10.1007/s00270-025-04321-2).

SUMMARY

Interventional radiology (IR) provides effective and minimally invasive treatment for a broad range of emergency conditions, including acute hemorrhage, ischemia, and sepsis (1,2). In their recent CIRSE Standards of Practice document, Mahnken et al. (3) emphasized that the safe and sustainable provision of emergency IR care on a 24/7 basis requires technical expertise and efficient organization and adequate infrastructure.

This document outlines the minimum structural and organizational requirements for emergency IR services. These recommendations address key elements, including staffing, on-call organization, technical equipment, access to imaging facilities, patient preparation, and treatment conditions across various emergency settings (3). Importantly, emergency IR should be regarded as a technique and clinical service system. Sustainable 24/7 care requires sufficient staffing, unrestricted access to CT, and the uninterrupted availability of an IR suite (3).

COMMENT

This paper is important because it shifts the focus of emergency IR from individual procedural capability to service organization and sustainability. Although previous reports from Europe and the United Kingdom have described the organization of emergency IR services relatively well (1,2,3), the current status of emergency IR systems in Korea has not been sufficiently documented.

A previously published special issue on trauma interventional radiology in the Journal of the Korean Society of Radiology highlighted the expanding role of interventional radiologists in trauma centers (4). However, beyond trauma care, the standards for emergency IR staffing, facility requirements, on-call systems, and institutional support in South Korea remain insufficiently discussed. Discussions at the societal level on appropriate reimbursements, institutional incentives for dedicated emergency IR teams, and sustainable workforce models may be needed to develop a Korean emergency IR care model.

The CIRSE standards may serve as a useful framework for improving the organization and sustainability of emergency IR care in Korea.

Quantitative bounds for bounded solutions of 3-D Navier-Stokes equations in the endpoint critical Besov spaces
Ruilin Hu, Phuoc-Tai Nguyen, Quoc-Hung Nguyen, Ping Zhang

Abstract
. Building on Tao’s quantitative regularity theory and triple-logarithmic blow-up estimate in L 3 L^{3} in [ 30 ] , we consider classical solutions ( u , P ) (u,P) of the three-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equations on [ 0 , T ] × ℝ 3 [0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3} . For 3 < p < ∞ 3<p<\infty , under simultaneous uniform control of the two scaling-critical quantities ‖ u ‖ L T ∞ ​ ( B ˙ p , ∞ − 1 + 3 p ) \|u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}(\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}})} and ‖ | D | − 1 + 3 p ​ u ‖ L T ∞ ​ ( L p ) \||D|^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}(L^{p})} , we obtain explicit quantitative estimates for all spatial derivatives of u u . As a consequence, we derive a mixed blow-up criterion coupling a double exponential of the critical Besov norm with the L p L^{p} norm of | D | − 1 + 3 p ​ u |D|^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}u , which forces quantified growth of at least one of these two critical quantities near any finite blow-up time. The low regularity and lack of dyadic summability in the endpoint Besov space are handled through a finite iterative decomposition that successively improves spatial integrability and produces an energy-class remainder, together with refined nonlinear energy estimates. 1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview on the relevant literature

In this paper, we consider the following three-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equations (with the viscosity normalized to be
1
):

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u-\Delta u+u\cdot\nabla u&=-\nabla P,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
\nabla\cdot u&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
u=(u^{1},u^{2},u^{3}):[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
P:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
denote the velocity vector and the scalar pressure function of the viscous fluid, respectively. Here
u\cdot\nabla=\sum_{j=1}^{3}u^{j}\partial_{j}
and
\nabla\cdot u=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\partial_{j}u^{j}
.

The Navier–Stokes equations model the motion of viscous incompressible fluids and are a central subject in the field of mathematical fluid mechanics. The system consists of momentum equations, describing the time evolution of the velocity field
u
under the influence of diffusion, convection, and pressure gradient, along with an incompressibility condition
\nabla\cdot u=0
that enforces volume preservation. This setting in three-dimensional space is known for its mathematical complexity, particularly regarding the existence and regularity of solutions over time.

We shall restrict our attention to
classical
solutions, namely solutions that are smooth and such that all derivatives of
u,P
are in
L_{T}^{\infty}(L^{2})
. We say that
u
blows up at a finite time
T_{*}
if
u
admits no smooth extension to
[0,T_{*}]
.
T_{*}
is referred to as the
maximal time of existence
of
u
.

A conspicuous feature of (
1.1
) lies in its scaling symmetry

u(t,x)\mapsto u_{\lambda}(t,x):=\lambda u(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x),\qquad P(t,x)\mapsto P_{\lambda}(t,x):=\lambda^{2}P(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x),\quad\forall\lambda>0,

(1.2)

which implies in particular that the following spaces

L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{3,q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow\dot{B}_{p,q}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),

(1.3)

with
3<p,q<\infty
, are critical to (
1.1
) in the sense that the norm
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+};X)}
is invariant under the scaling transformation (
1.2
), where
X
is any of the functional spaces listed in (
1.3
).

The research on blowup criteria for problem (
1.1
) in terms of critical norms is a long-standing challenging topic in nonlinear PDEs from both qualitative and quantitative perspectives, initiated by the landmark achievements of Leray
[
20
]
and Prodi–Serrin–Ladyzhenskaya
[
26
,
27
,
19
]
for non-endpoint critical Lebesgue spaces (namely for
L_{t}^{q}(L_{x}^{p})
with
3<p\leq\infty
and
2\leq q<\infty
such that
\frac{2}{q}+\frac{3}{p}=1
). The endpoint case
L_{t}^{\infty}(L_{x}^{3})
remained open for a long time and was settled in a celebrated work by Escauriaza, Seregin and Šverák
[
13
]
. By using a contradiction argument based on a blowup procedure and unique continuation results, they asserted that if a solution
u
of (
1.1
) blows up at a finite time
T_{*}
, then

\limsup_{t\nearrow T_{*}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{3}}=+\infty.

(1.4)

The research topic has recently taken a noticeable leap forward, evidenced by numerous impactful contributions in various contexts and different directions; see, e.g.,
[
1
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
25
,
28
]
. Among many others, we refer to Seregin
[
28
]
, where the superior limit in (
1.4
) was replaced by the limit; to Phuc
[
25
]
for the generalization of (
1.4
) to non-endpoint borderline Lorentz spaces
L^{3,r}
for any
r<\infty
; and to Gallagher, Koch and Planchon
[
17
]
and Albritton
[
1
]
for respectively extending (
1.4
) to the superior limit and limit in non-endpoint critical Besov spaces
\dot{B}_{p,q}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}
with
3<p,q<\infty
. The endpoint case has appeared to be more challenging and was treated by Barker in
[
3
]
, where a qualitative blowup criterion regarding
B_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}
(
3<p<\infty
) was derived under an additional condition imposing the vanishing of
u
at the blowup time
T_{*}
in the limit of a rescaling procedure. We also mention the works
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
for related works concerning one-component regularity criteria and
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
24
]
for recent ill-posedness results for system (
1.1
). Nevertheless, all the cited works provide only qualitative blowup criteria, and the proofs therein, which rely on some form of compactness argument and qualitative ingredients, cannot be employed to extract quantitative information near the blowup time.

Quantitative bounds for critical norms of solutions were first established in a breakthrough paper by Tao
[
30
]
, in which compactness arguments and qualitative results were replaced by quantitative ingredients such as Carleman inequalities and quantitative regularity estimates. Specifically, Tao obtained bounds in terms of a triple exponential function of the critical
L^{3}
-norm. This result implies that the
L^{3}
-norm of
u
must blow up at least at a triple logarithmic rate as the time approaches a finite blowup time, namely

\limsup_{t\nearrow T_{*}}\frac{\|u(t)\|_{L^{3}}}{\bigl(\ln\ln|\ln(T_{*}-t)|\bigr)^{c}}=+\infty,

(1.5)

for some absolute constant
c>0
.

Following Tao’s work, various significant improvements and refinements of (
1.5
) and related results have been developed. These include localized quantitative blowup rates in
L^{3}
in
[
5
,
4
]
, an improvement to a double logarithmic blowup rate for axisymmetric solutions in
[
22
]
, a high-dimensional analog of (
1.5
) in
[
23
]
, and extensions of (
1.5
) to Lorentz spaces in
[
14
,
21
]
.

Despite the recent advancements, the study of quantitative blowup rates involving critical Besov spaces still remains widely under-explored. In this paper, we aim to establish analogous quantitative regularity and blowup criteria for solutions to (
1.1
) in the setting of critical Besov spaces.

1.2.
Main results and strategy of the proofs

In this paper, we consider the Navier–Stokes equations in the framework of critical Besov spaces
\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}
(see the definitions of these spaces in Subsection
2.2
).
Our first main result states as follows:

Theorem 1.1

(Quantitative regularity)
.

Let
3<p<\infty
and
(u,P)
be a classical solution of (
1.1
) in
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}(\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}})}\leq M,

(1.6)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\bigl\||D|^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}u(t)\bigr\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\leq A,

(1.7)

for some
M\geq 2
and
A\geq 2
. Then for any multi-index
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
and any
t\in(0,T]
, there holds

t^{\frac{1+|\alpha|}{2}}\|\nabla^{\alpha}u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim_{|\alpha|}\exp\left(2^{|\alpha|}A\exp\bigl(\exp(M^{c_{p}})\bigr)\right),

(1.8)

where
c_{p}
is a positive constant depending only on
p
.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.1
, we derive the following result, which can be interpreted as a quantitative blowup criterion coupling a double exponential of
\|u(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}}
with
\bigl\||D|^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}u(t)\bigr\|_{L^{p}}
, which implies that at least one of these norms must exhibit a specific logarithmic growth rate as time approaches the blowup time.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(u,P)
be a classical solution of (
1.1
) on
[0,T_{*})
such that
u
blows up at a finite time
T_{*}\in(0,\infty)
. Then for
p\in(3,\infty)
, we have

\limsup_{t\nearrow T_{*}}\left[\frac{\exp\bigl(\exp(\|u(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}}^{c_{p}})\bigr)}{\bigl(|\ln(T_{*}-t)|\bigr)^{b}}\bigl\||D|^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}u(t)\bigr\|_{L^{p}}\right]=+\infty,

(1.9)

where
c_{p}
is a positive constant depending only on
p
and
b
is a positive absolute constant.

In order to prove our main results, we substantially improve the strategy in
[
30
]
(see
[
5
,
23
,
21
]
for comprehensive explanations of Tao’s strategy). Let us recall that the idea in
[
30
]
is to show that if a solution
u
concentrates at a particular time then there will be moderate
L^{3}
concentrations at widely separated spatial scales. Precisely, if
\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{3})}\leq A
for some constant
A\geq 2
and
u
concentrates at
(t_{0},x_{0})
and at frequency
N_{0}
for which

N_{0}^{-1}|P_{N_{0}}u(t_{0},x_{0})|\geq A^{-C_{0}}

(1.10)

where
C_{0}
is a large absolute constant and
P_{N_{0}}
is a Littlewood–Paley projection to frequencies equivalent to
N_{0}
, then
t_{0}N_{0}^{2}\lesssim\exp\exp\exp(A^{c})
. In other words, concentrations cannot occur for high frequencies. This key estimate was obtained in
[
30
]
by using Carleman inequalities in combination with several auxiliary results such as bounded total speed, epochs of regularity, back propagation, iterated back propagation, and annuli of regularity. Subsequently, the triple exponential bounds in
[
30
, Theorem 1.2]
were derived through an adaptation of the local well-posedness and regularity theory for Navier–Stokes as shown in
[
30
, Section 6]
.

A difficulty that we encounter in our analysis while using the aforementioned strategy stems from the low regularity of the Besov spaces
\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}
with
3<p<\infty
. Therefore, unlike
[
30
]
where it is sufficient to use the simple decomposition
u=e^{t\Delta}u_{0}+w
to obtain regularity results of
u
, in the present paper, to tackle this difficulty, we perform a more subtle decomposition via iteration (as in
[
17
]
) as

u=u_{1L}+u_{2L}+\cdots+u_{mL}+v,

(1.11)

with
m
being a large enough integer (precisely,
m=[p]+3
; see Lemma
3.4
), where
u_{1L}:=e^{t\Delta}u_{0}
and
u_{kL}
(
1\leq k\leq m
) is determined iteratively by the terms
u_{iL}
,
1\leq i\leq k-1
(see Subsection
2.3
for more detail). Furthermore, in order to exploit assumption (
1.6
) and stick with critical quantities, at many points in our analysis, we involve appropriate Kato spaces in which the norm of solutions is invariant under the scaling transformation (
1.2
). We note that similar bounds have been obtained in
[
22
,
23
]
.

On one hand, the advantage of (
1.11
) is that it allows us to improve the regularity in critical Kato spaces step by step in the sense that the spatial integrability exponent of
u_{kL}
in the
k
-th step equals
\frac{p}{k}
. Eventually, at the
m
-th step, we deduce that
u_{mL}
belongs to a critical Kato space with spatial exponent
1
. In this procedure, the higher the spatial regularity of
u_{kL}
is, the more singular with respect to time it is at the initial time (see Lemma
3.4
for more detail). On the other hand, due to the decomposition (
1.11
), in the equation satisfied by
v
, in addition to the nonlinear term as in
[
30
]
, there also appear a linear term and a force term both involving all
u_{kL}
,
1\leq k\leq m
(see the system (
2.13
)). This complicates significantly the analysis and requires new ingredients to go through the argument. In particular, we establish several delicate estimates on
u_{kL}
(see Lemma
3.4
) and
v
(see Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
3.6
) which enable us to handle the intricacies induced by the lack of regularity in the proof of the back propagation in Proposition
4.5
and annuli of regularity in Proposition
4.7
.

Main novelties and proof strategy.

Most previous quantitative implementations of the concentration–propagation strategy are formulated under critical Lebesgue or Lorentz-space control. Although qualitative regularity and blowup criteria in critical Besov spaces are available, their compactness-based arguments do not provide the explicit bounds sought here. The endpoint space
\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}
presents a genuinely different difficulty: its norm controls every dyadic block at the critical size, but it provides no summability over the frequency index. Consequently, physical-space estimates that follow directly from an
L^{3}
bound cannot be used without further structure.

The first ingredient is the finite iterative decomposition (
1.11
). At each stage one extracts a component
u_{kL}
satisfying sharp heat-decay estimates in critical Kato spaces, while its spatial integrability improves from
p
to approximately
p/k
. After finitely many steps the last component reaches the integrability level needed for energy and localized kernel estimates. The remainder
v
has enough
L^{2}
-based regularity to recover bounded total speed, epochs of regularity, back propagation, and annuli of regularity. Thus the decomposition converts the non-summable Besov information into a finite collection of quantitatively controlled pieces plus an energy-class remainder.

The second new ingredient concerns the final concentration argument. A positive-kernel approach naturally would require an assumption on
|D|^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}|u|
, which discards the sign and direction of the velocity field. Our hypothesis (
1.7
) is instead imposed directly on the signed vector field
|D|^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}u
; in particular, no corresponding bound with
|u|
is assumed. Near each concentration scale we test this signed quantity against a mean-zero vector field obtained from a localized curl. The mean-zero property produces an additional decay factor. By choosing geometrically separated scales, the associated test functions are almost orthogonal in
L^{p^{\prime}}
, and duality with
|D|^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}u\in L^{p}
bounds the number of possible concentration scales. This yields the key frequency estimate (
5.2
) and hence the quantitative regularity bound (
1.8
).

Let us explain the use of the hypothesis (
1.7
) in more detail. Put
\sigma=1-\frac{3}{p}\in(0,1)
. If one would assume that

|D|^{-\sigma}|u|\in L^{p},

(1.12)

then the Riesz-potential representation

|D|^{-\sigma}|u|(x)=c_{\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|u(y)|}{|x-y|^{3-\sigma}}\,dy

has a positive kernel and a nonnegative integrand. Consequently, every concentration of
u
at a given scale produces a positive contribution to this potential, and contributions coming from suitably separated scales cannot cancel one another. One can therefore sum the resulting lower bounds and continue with the scale-counting argument in the spirit of
[
30
]
. Thus, under (
1.12
), the last step is a rather direct adaptation of Tao’s argument.

This positivity is unavailable under our weaker and more natural signed hypothesis

|D|^{-\sigma}u\in L^{p}.

(1.13)

Indeed, neither a large value of
u
nor a concentration of its vorticity gives a pointwise lower bound for
|D|^{-\sigma}u
, because different spatial regions and different vector components may cancel. We overcome this obstruction by converting each vorticity concentration into a nontrivial signed pairing
\langle u,\Psi_{i}\rangle
, where
\Psi_{i}
is a localized curl and hence has zero mean. By self-adjointness,

\langle u,\Psi_{i}\rangle=\langle|D|^{-\sigma}u,|D|^{\sigma}\Psi_{i}\rangle.

After the critical normalization
\varphi_{i}=r_{i}^{-2}|D|^{\sigma}\Psi_{i}
, the functions
\varphi_{i}
have uniformly bounded
L^{p^{\prime}}
norms. Their mean-zero origin yields improved far-field decay, while the geometric separation of the scales makes the family almost orthogonal in
L^{p^{\prime}}
. Choosing the sign of each
\varphi_{i}
according to its pairing and then using Hölder duality gives

K\varepsilon^{3}\lesssim\left\langle|D|^{-\sigma}u,\sum_{i=1}^{K}\operatorname{sgn}\bigl(\langle|D|^{-\sigma}u,\varphi_{i}\rangle\bigr)\varphi_{i}\right\rangle\lesssim\bigl\||D|^{-\sigma}u\bigr\|_{L^{p}}K^{1/p^{\prime}},

which bounds
K
, the number of concentration scales (see (
5.37
) for more detail). This dual almost-orthogonality argument is precisely what replaces the positive summation available for
|D|^{-\sigma}|u|
.

In this way, the proof uses both components of the assumptions in complementary roles: the critical Besov bound drives the parabolic decomposition and all propagation estimates, while the signed
L^{p}
bound rules out an arbitrarily long cascade of separated concentration scales. Finally, an argument by contradiction combining Theorem
1.1
with the classical Prodi–Serrin–Ladyzhenskaya criterion yields Theorem
1.2
.

(2.2)

(2.3)

Next, we recall Bony’s para-product decomposition. Formally, the product of
f,g\in\mathcal{S}_{h}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
may be decomposed into

fg=T_{f}g+T_{g}f+R(f,g),

(2.4)

where

\displaystyle T_{f}g:=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\dot{S}_{j-1}f\,\dot{\Delta}_{j}g,

\displaystyle R(f,g):=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\,\tilde{\Delta}_{j}g,\qquad\text{with}\quad\tilde{\Delta}_{j}g:=\sum_{j^{\prime}=j-1}^{j+1}\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}g.

The operator
T
is called the homogeneous para-product of
g
by
f
, which represents low–high frequency interactions, while the operator
R
is called the homogeneous remainder of
f
and
g
, which corresponds to the high–high frequency interactions.

As a consequence of Bony’s decomposition, we have the following useful formula:

\dot{\Delta}_{j}(fg)=\dot{\Delta}_{j}\Bigl(\sum_{|j^{\prime}-j|\leq 4}\dot{S}_{j^{\prime}-1}f\,\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}g+\sum_{|j^{\prime}-j|\leq 4}\dot{S}_{j^{\prime}-1}g\,\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}f+\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j-4}\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}f\,\tilde{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}g\Bigr).

(2.5)

Some preliminary estimates

In this section, we establish various estimates of

and

in Besov spaces with time weights. We start with an interpolation inequality.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume
p>3
. Then for any
2<r\leq 3
, we have

\|w\|_{L^{r}}\lesssim\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{\alpha_{1}}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{\alpha_{2}}\|w\|_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}}^{\alpha_{3}},\quad\forall w\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),

(3.1)

where
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\in(0,1)
are given by

\alpha_{1}=\frac{2(4p+r-pr-6)}{pr+2p-4r},\quad\alpha_{2}=\frac{6(p-r)(r-2)}{r(pr+2p-4r)},\quad\alpha_{3}=\frac{3p(r-2)^{2}}{r(pr+2p-4r)},

and
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}=1.

Remark 3.2
.

We note that the condition
2<r\leq 3<p
ensures
\alpha_{i}\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

Let
j_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
to be determined later on. We have

\|w\|_{L^{r}}\lesssim\sum_{j\leq j_{0}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}w\|_{L^{r}}+\sum_{j\geq j_{0}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}w\|_{L^{r}}.

(3.2)

Since
r>2
, we deduce from (
2.6
) that

\sum_{j\leq j_{0}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}w\|_{L^{r}}\lesssim\sum_{j\leq j_{0}}2^{\frac{3(r-2)}{2r}j}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}w\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{\frac{3(r-2)}{2r}j_{0}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.3)

To handle the second term on the right-hand side of (
3.2
), we get, by using Hölder’s inequality, that

\sum_{j\geq j_{0}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}w\|_{L^{r}}\lesssim\sum_{j\geq j_{0}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}w\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2(p-r)}{(p-2)r}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}w\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{p(r-2)}{(p-2)r}}.

(3.4)

From (
2.7
), we deduce

\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}w\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-j}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-j}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}.

Keeping in mind that
pr-4p-r+6<0
due to the assumption
r\leq 3<p
, we thus obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq j_{0}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}w\|_{L^{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\geq j_{0}}\bigl(2^{-j}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}\bigr)^{\frac{2(p-r)}{(p-2)r}}\bigl(2^{(1-\frac{3}{p})j}\|w\|_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}}\bigr)^{\frac{p(r-2)}{(p-2)r}}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\geq j_{0}}2^{\frac{pr-4p-r+6}{(p-2)r}j}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2(p-r)}{(p-2)r}}\|w\|_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}}^{\frac{p(r-2)}{(p-2)r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{\frac{pr-4p-r+6}{(p-2)r}j_{0}}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2(p-r)}{(p-2)r}}\|w\|_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}}^{\frac{p(r-2)}{(p-2)r}}.

By inserting (
3.3
) and (
3.5
) into (
3.2
), we achieve

\|w\|_{L^{r}}\lesssim 2^{\frac{3(r-2)}{2r}j_{0}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}+2^{\frac{pr-4p-r+6}{(p-2)r}j_{0}}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2(p-r)}{(p-2)r}}\|w\|_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}}^{\frac{p(r-2)}{(p-2)r}},\quad\forall j_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}.

By minimizing the right-hand side of the above estimate over
j_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
, we obtain (

\|\text{III}_{j}(v,v)(t)\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\|v\|_{X_{T}}^{1-\kappa}M^{c_{p}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c_{0}(t-s)2^{2j}}ds\lesssim\|v\|_{X_{T}}^{1-\kappa}M^{c_{p}}\min\{t,2^{-2j}\}.

By summarizing the above estimates, we obtain

\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}v_{N}(t)\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\|v\|_{X_{T}}^{1-\kappa}M^{c_{p}}\min\bigl\{t,2^{-2j}\bigr\},

which, together with Definition
2.1
, ensures that

\|v_{N}\|_{X_{T}}\lesssim\|v\|_{X_{T}}^{1-\kappa}M^{c_{p}}.

(3.28)

The estimate of
v_{L}
.
Once again we get, by using decomposition (
3.14
), that

\displaystyle\dot{\Delta}_{j}v_{L}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\bigl(\text{I}_{j}(v,u_{iL})(t)+\text{II}_{j}(v,u_{iL})(t)+\text{III}_{j}(v,u_{iL})(t)\bigr).

Let
r
and
\kappa
be given by (
3.26
). We get, by using Lemma
2.2
, (
3.9
) and (
3.21
), that

\displaystyle\|\dot{S}_{\ell-1}v(s)\otimes\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}u_{iL}(s)\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\dot{S}_{\ell-1}v(s)\|_{L^{r}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}u_{iL}(s)\|_{L^{\frac{r}{r-1}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\dot{S}_{\ell-1}v(s)\|_{L^{r}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}u_{iL}(s)\|_{L^{p}}2^{\left(\frac{3}{r}+\frac{3}{p}-3\right)\ell}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\leq\ell-2}\|\dot{\Delta}_{\ell^{\prime}}v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{1})}^{1-\kappa}\|\dot{\Delta}_{\ell^{\prime}}v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{p})}^{\kappa}\|\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}u_{iL}(s)\|_{L^{p}}2^{\left(\frac{3}{r}+\frac{3}{p}-3\right)\ell}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\leq\ell-2}s^{1-\kappa}\|v\|_{X_{T}}^{1-\kappa}2^{\left(1-\frac{3}{p}\right)\kappa\ell^{\prime}}2^{\left(\frac{3}{r}-2\right)\ell}e^{-c_{i-1}s2^{2\ell}}M^{c_{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-2(1-\kappa)\ell}2^{\left(1-\frac{3}{p}\right)\kappa\ell}2^{\left(\frac{3}{r}-2\right)\ell}M^{c_{p}}\|v\|_{X_{T}}^{1-\kappa}\lesssim M^{c_{p}}\|v\|_{X_{T}}^{1-\kappa}2^{-\ell},

from which and (
3.14
), we deduce that

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|\text{I}_{j}(v,u_{iL})(t)\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim M^{c_{p}}\|v\|_{X_{T}}^{1-\kappa}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c_{0}(t-s)2^{2j}}ds\lesssim M^{c_{p}}\|v\|_{X_{T}}^{1-\kappa}\min\bigl\{t,2^{-2j}\bigr\}.

Since
r
close to
1,

\frac{r}{r-1}>3,
we get, by applying (
3.9
), that for any
0<s<t
,

\displaystyle\|\dot{S}_{\ell-1}u_{iL}(s)\otimes\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}v(s)\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\dot{S}_{\ell-1}u_{iL}(s)\|_{L^{\frac{r}{r-1}}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}v(s)\|_{L^{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim M^{c_{p}}2^{(\frac{3}{r}-2)\ell}\|\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{1})}^{1-\kappa}\|\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{p})}^{\kappa}

\displaystyle\lesssim M^{c_{p}}\|v\|_{X_{T}}^{1-\kappa}2^{-\ell},

which, together with (
3.14
), implies

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|\text{II}_{j}(v,u_{iL})(t)\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim M^{c_{p}}\|v\|_{X_{T}}^{1-\kappa}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)2^{2j}}ds\lesssim M^{c_{p}}\|v\|_{X_{T}}^{1-\kappa}\min\bigl\{t,2^{-2j}\bigr\}.

Finally notice that for any
0<s<t

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\geq j-4}\|\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}u_{iL}(s)\|_{L^{\frac{r}{r-1}}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}v(s)\|_{L^{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\ell\geq j-4}2^{\left(\frac{3}{r}+\frac{3}{p}-3\right)\ell}\|\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}u_{iL}(s)\|_{L^{p}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{1})}^{1-\kappa}\|\dot{\Delta}_{l}v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{p})}^{\kappa}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-j}\|v\|_{X_{T}}^{1-\kappa}M^{c_{p}},

from which we infer that

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|\text{III}_{j}(v,u_{iL})(t)\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim M^{c_{p}}\|v\|_{X_{T}}^{1-\kappa}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c_{0}(t-s)2^{2j}}ds\lesssim M^{c_{p}}\|v\|_{X_{T}}^{1-\kappa}\min\bigl\{t,2^{-2j}\bigr\}.

By summarizing the above estimates, we achieve

\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}v_{L}(t)\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim M^{c_{p}}\|v\|_{X_{T}}^{1-\kappa}\min\bigl\{t,2^{-2j}\bigr\}.

As a result, it comes out

\|v_{L}\|_{X_{T}}\lesssim\|v\|_{X_{T}}^{1-\kappa}M^{c_{p}}.

(3.29)

The estimate of
v_{F}
.
We are going to prove that

\|v_{F}\|_{X_{T}}\lesssim M^{c_{p}}.

(3.30)

Indeed thanks to (
2.14
) and (
3.24
), it suffices to prove that for any
1\leq i\leq m
,

\Bigl\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(u_{mL}\otimes u_{iL})(s)ds\Bigr\|_{X_{T}}\lesssim M^{c_{p}}.

(3.31)

By using decomposition (
3.14
), we have

\dot{\Delta}_{j}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(u_{mL}\otimes u_{iL})(s)ds\right)=\text{I}_{j}(u_{mL},u_{iL})+\text{II}_{j}(u_{mL},u_{iL})+\text{III}_{j}(u_{mL},u_{iL}).

For
r
given by (
3.26
), we get, by applying Lemma
2.2
, (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), that

\displaystyle\|\dot{S}_{\ell-1}u_{mL}(s)\otimes\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}u_{iL}(s)\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\dot{S}_{\ell-1}u_{mL}(s)\|_{L^{r}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}u_{iL}(s)\|_{L^{\frac{r}{r-1}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\leq\ell-2}\|\dot{\Delta}_{\ell^{\prime}}u_{mL}(s)\|_{L^{r}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}u_{iL}(s)\|_{L^{\frac{r}{r-1}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle M^{c_{p}}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\leq\ell-2}s2^{3(1-\frac{1}{r})\ell^{\prime}}2^{(\frac{3}{r}-2)\ell}e^{-c_{i}s2^{2\ell}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle M^{c_{p}}s2^{\ell}e^{-c_{i}s2^{2\ell}}\lesssim M^{c_{p}}2^{-\ell},

from which and (
3.14
), we deduce that

\|\text{I}_{j}(u_{mL},u_{iL})(t)\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim M^{c_{p}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c_{0}(t-s)2^{2j}}ds\lesssim M^{c_{p}}\min\bigl\{t,2^{-2j}\bigr\}.

It follows from (
3.10
) and (
3.21
) that for any
0<s<t
,

\displaystyle\|\dot{S}_{\ell-1}u_{iL}(s)\otimes\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}u_{mL}(s)\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\dot{S}_{\ell-1}u_{iL}(s)\|_{L^{p}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}u_{mL}(s)\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\Bigl(\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\leq\ell-2}\|\dot{\Delta}_{\ell^{\prime}}u_{iL}(s)\|_{L^{p}}\Bigr)\|\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}u_{mL}(s)\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle M^{c_{p}}\Bigl(\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\leq\ell-2}2^{\left(1-\frac{3}{p}\right)\ell^{\prime}}\Bigr)2^{(\frac{3}{p}-2)\ell}\lesssim M^{c_{p}}2^{-\ell},

from which and (
3.14

\|\text{II}_{j}(u_{mL},u_{iL})(t)\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim M^{c_{p}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c_{0}(t-s)2^{2j}}ds\lesssim M^{c_{p}}\min\{t,2^{-2j}\}.

Finally it is easy to observe from (
3.10
) and (
3.21
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\geq j-4}\|\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}u_{mL}(s)\|_{L^{r}}\|\tilde{\Delta}_{\ell}u_{iL}\|_{L^{\frac{r}{r-1}}}\lesssim M^{c_{p}}\sum_{\ell\geq j-4}2^{(1-\frac{3}{r})\ell}2^{(\frac{3}{r}-2)\ell}\lesssim M^{c_{p}}2^{-j},

which implies

\|\text{III}_{j}(u_{mL},u_{iL})(t)\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim M^{c_{p}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c_{0}(t-s)2^{2j}}ds\lesssim M^{c_{p}}\min\bigl\{t,2^{-2j}\bigr\}.

By summarizing the above estimates, we achieve (
3.31
). We complete the proof of (
3.30
).

By substituting (
3.28
), (
3.29
) and (

\|v\|_{X_{T}}\lesssim\|v\|_{X_{T}}^{1-\kappa}M^{c_{p}}+M^{c_{p}},

with

) follows from

4.
Regularity and propagation

In this section, we shall establish necessary ingredients for performing Tao’s strategy, including pointwise derivative estimates, bounded total speed, epochs of regularity, back propagation, iterated back propagation and annuli of regularity. We shall designate
\{c_{j,q}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
to be a nonnegative generic sequence in
\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z})
with
\bigl\|(c_{j,q})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigr\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}\leq 1,
which may be different on each occurrence below. In particular, we shall denote

\{c_{j,1}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
by
\{d_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}.

Lemma 4.1

(Point-wise estimate)
.

Let

u:[t_{0}-T,t_{0}]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a classical solution of (
1.1
) and satisfy (
1.6
). Then for any
t\in[t_{0}-T,t_{0}]
and any
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim 2^{j}M^{c_{p}},\quad\|\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim 2^{2j}M^{c_{p}},

(4.1)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\dot{\Delta}_{j}u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\bigl(2^{3j}+2^{(1+\frac{3}{p})j}(t-(t_{0}-T))^{-1+\frac{3}{2p}}\bigr)M^{c_{p}}.

(4.2)

Proof.

Estimates in (
4.1
) follow directly from Lemma
2.2
and the assumption (
1.6
). Since

\partial_{t}\dot{\Delta}_{j}u=\Delta\dot{\Delta}_{j}u+\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot\dot{\Delta}_{j}(u\otimes u),

in order to prove (
4.2
),
it suffices to handle the term
\nabla\cdot\dot{\Delta}_{j}(u\otimes u)
.
Yet in view of (
2.10
) and (
2.14
), we write

u=v+V_{1},

(4.3)

so that

\dot{\Delta}_{j}(u\otimes u)=\dot{\Delta}_{j}(v\otimes v)+\dot{\Delta}_{j}(V_{1}\otimes v)+\dot{\Delta}_{j}(v\otimes V_{1})+\dot{\Delta}_{j}(V_{1}\otimes V_{1}).

(4.4)

By using Bony’s decomposition (
2.5
), we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}(w\otimes\tilde{w})(t)=\dot{\Delta}_{j}\Bigl(

\displaystyle\sum_{|j^{\prime}-j|\leq 4}(\dot{S}_{j^{\prime}-1}w\otimes\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}\tilde{w})(t)

\displaystyle+\sum_{|j^{\prime}-j|\leq 4}(\dot{S}_{j^{\prime}-1}\tilde{w}\otimes\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}w)(t)+\sum_{\ell\geq j-4}(\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}w\otimes\tilde{\Delta}_{\ell}\tilde{w})(t)\Bigr),

where
w,\tilde{w}\in\{v,V_{1}\}
. Then it follows from Lemma
2.2
, (
3.21
) and (
3.27
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{|j^{\prime}-j|\leq 4}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}(\dot{S}_{j^{\prime}-1}w\otimes\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}\tilde{w})(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|j^{\prime}-j|\leq 4}\Bigl(\sum_{\ell\leq j^{\prime}-2}\|\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}w(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\Bigr)\|\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}\tilde{w}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{2j}\|w(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}}\|\tilde{w}(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}}\lesssim 2^{2j}M^{c_{p}}.

For the remaining terms in Bony’s decomposition of
v\otimes v
,
v\otimes V_{1}
and
V_{1}\otimes v
,
we get, by using (
3.21
) and (
3.22
), that for
w\in\{v,V_{1}\}
,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\sum_{l\geq j-4}\dot{\Delta}_{j}(\dot{\Delta}_{l}v\otimes\tilde{\Delta}_{l}w)(t)\Bigr\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{3j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq j-4\end{subarray}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}v(t)\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}}\|\tilde{\Delta}_{\ell}w(t)\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{3j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq j-4\end{subarray}}2^{\left(\frac{3}{p}-2\right)\ell}\|v(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{1,\infty}^{2}}2^{\left(1-\frac{3}{p}\right)\ell}\|w(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}}\lesssim 2^{2j}M^{c_{p}}.

For the remaining terms in
V_{1}\otimes V_{1}
, we get, by using (
3.9
) and (
3.21
), that

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq j-4\end{subarray}}\dot{\Delta}_{j}(\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}V_{1}\otimes\tilde{\Delta}_{\ell}V_{1})(t)\Bigr\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{\frac{6}{p}j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq j-4\end{subarray}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}V_{1}(t)\|_{L^{p}}\|\tilde{\Delta}_{\ell}V_{1}(t)\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{\frac{6}{p}j}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq j-4\end{subarray}}2^{\left(2-\frac{6}{p}\right)\ell}e^{-c_{k-1}(t-(t_{0}-T))2^{2\ell}}M^{c_{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{\frac{3}{p}j}(t-(t_{0}-T))^{-1+\frac{3}{2p}}M^{c_{p}}.

By summarizing the above estimates and using (
4.4
), we obtain (
4.2
).
This completes the proof of Lemma
4.1
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Let

be the vorticity of the fluid, then

(4.5)

Furthermore, it is obvious to observe from Lemmas

(4.6)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\dot{\Delta}_{j}\omega(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\bigl(2^{4j}+2^{(2+\frac{3}{p})j}(t-(t_{0}-T))^{-1+\frac{3}{2p}}\bigr)M^{c_{p}}.

(4.7)

Next we show that the total speed of

of

(Bounded total speed)

Without loss of generality, we may assume that

[-1,1]\subset[t_{0}-T,t_{0}]
. Restarting the decomposition (
2.10
) at time

, with

,

for

, and

we may apply the estimates of Section

with

We first deduce from (

\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{3}

\|\nabla^{\alpha}V_{1}(t)\|_{L^{r}}\lesssim(t+1)^{-(\frac{|\alpha|}{2}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{2r})}M^{c_{p}},\quad\forall t\in(-1,1],

(4.9)

Indeed in view of (

\begin{split}v(t)=&e^{(t+\frac{1}{2})\Delta}v(-1/2)+v_{N}(-1/2,t)+v_{L}(-1/2,t)+v_{F}(-1/2,t),\end{split}

(4.13)

\displaystyle\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}e^{(t+\frac{1}{2})\Delta}v(-1/2)\|_{L^{1}([0,1];L^{\infty})}

\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}v_{N}(-1/2,t)\|_{L^{1}([-1/2,1];L^{\infty})}\lesssim d_{j}M^{c_{p}}.

(4.17)

The estimate of the linear terms.
Notice that
\dot{\Delta}_{j}=\dot{\Delta}_{j}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}
, hence in view of

we get, by applying Lemma

,

\displaystyle\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}v_{L}(-1/2,t)\|_{L^{1}([-1/2,1];L^{\infty})}

The estimate of the force term.

\displaystyle\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}v_{F}(-1/2,t)\|_{L^{1}([0,1];L^{\infty})}

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\Delta}_{j}(V_{3}\otimes V_{1})\|_{L^{1}([-1/2,1];L^{\infty})}+\|\tilde{\Delta}_{j}(V_{2}\otimes V_{3})\|_{L^{1}([-1/2,1];L^{\infty})}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{\frac{j}{2}}\|V_{3}\|_{L^{2}([-1/2,1];L^{6})}\bigl(\|V_{1}\|_{L^{2}([-1/2,1];L^{\infty})}+\|V_{2}\|_{L^{2}([-1/2,1];L^{\infty})}\bigr)\lesssim 2^{\frac{j}{2}}M^{c_{p}}.

By inserting the above estimates into (
4.19

\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}v_{F}(-1/2,t)\|_{L^{1}([0,1];L^{\infty})}\lesssim\min\bigl\{2^{-\frac{j}{2}},2^{\frac{3j}{2}}\bigr\}M^{c_{p}}\lesssim d_{j}M^{c_{p}}.

(4.20)

In view of (

), we get, by summarizing the estimates (

), (

), (
4.18
) and (

) that

which, together with (
4.9

Proposition 4.4

(Epochs of regularity)

Proof.

By rescaling and time translation, we may assume that

The estimate of
u
.
Let

2.13

[t_{0}-T,t_{0}].

We define the enstrophy-type quantity

Then we get, by taking the gradient of (
2.13
) and then taking

inner product of the resulting equation with

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\mathcal{E}}(t)=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla^{2}v(t)|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Delta v(t)\nabla\cdot(v\otimes v)(t)dx

\displaystyle\|v(t)\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla v(t)\|_{L^{3}}

\displaystyle\|\nabla v(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla v(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla^{2}v(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim{\mathcal{E}}(t)^{\frac{3}{4}}\|\nabla^{2}v(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

2.16

\rho\geq 4\kappa=\exp({M_{6}^{c_{p}}}).

(5.33)

Finally, we partition
\mathbb{R}^{3}
into the
K
pairwise disjoint regions

\mathcal{A}_{i}:=\bigl\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\ \tau_{i}\leq|z|<\tau_{i+1}\bigr\},\qquad\tau_{1}:=0,\quad\tau_{i}:=\frac{a_{i}}{2}\ \ (2\leq i\leq K),\quad\tau_{K+1}:=+\infty.

(5.34)

Lemma 5.7
.

Assume (
5.33
). Then
B(x_{*}^{i},\Lambda_{0}r_{i})\subset\mathcal{A}_{i}
for every

1\leq i\leq K
, and

\sum_{j\neq i}\|\varphi_{j}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{A}_{i})}\leq C_{p}\Lambda_{0}^{-2},\qquad 1\leq i\leq K.

(5.35)

Consequently, for any choice of signs
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{K}\in\{-1,+1\}
, the vector
field
\Phi:=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\theta_{i}\varphi_{i}
satisfies

\|\Phi\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C_{p}K^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}.

(5.36)

Proof.

(i) Localisation.
By (
5.31
) and (
5.33
) we have

|x_{*}^{i}|\leq\kappa a_{i}\leq\frac{\rho a_{i}}{4}=\frac{a_{i+1}}{4}<\tau_{i+1}
and

|x_{*}^{i}|\geq a_{i}>\tau_{i}
, hence
x_{*}^{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
and

\dist(x_{*}^{i},\mathcal{A}_{i}^{c})\geq\min\Bigl\{|x_{*}^{i}|-\frac{a_{i}}{2},\ \frac{a_{i+1}}{2}-|x_{*}^{i}|\Bigr\}\geq\frac{a_{i}}{2}=\Lambda_{0}r_{i},

where for
i=1
(resp.
i=K
) only the second (resp. the first) term occurs. This is the
first assertion.

(ii) The scales
j<i
.
Since
|x_{*}^{j}|\leq\kappa a_{j}=\kappa\rho^{\,j-i}a_{i}\leq\frac{a_{i}}{4}
by (
5.33
), we get

\dist(x_{*}^{j},\mathcal{A}_{i})\geq\frac{a_{i}}{2}-\frac{a_{i}}{4}=\frac{a_{i}}{4}=\frac{\Lambda_{0}}{2}\rho^{\,i-j}r_{j}
, so that (
5.21
) yields

\|\varphi_{j}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{A}_{i})}\leq C_{p}\Lambda_{0}^{-2}\rho^{-2(i-j)}.

(iii) The scales
j>i
.
Here
\mathcal{A}_{i}\subset B\bigl(0,\frac{a_{i+1}}{2}\bigr)

and
\dist(x_{*}^{j},\mathcal{A}_{i})\geq a_{j}-\frac{a_{i+1}}{2}\geq\frac{a_{j}}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\rho^{\,j-i}a_{i}
for
j\geq i+1
. For
j=i+1
we simply use (
5.21
)
with
\Lambda=\Lambda_{0}
. For
j\geq i+2
we use instead the pointwise bound
(
5.20
) together with
|\mathcal{A}_{i}|^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\leq C(\rho a_{i})^{\frac{3}{p^{\prime}}}

and
r_{j}=\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2M_{6}}\rho^{\,j-i}a_{i}
, which gives

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{j}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{A}_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\,r_{j}^{2}\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}\rho^{\,j-i}a_{i}\bigr)^{-4-\sigma}(\rho a_{i})^{\frac{3}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\Lambda_{0}^{-2}\,\rho^{\frac{3}{p^{\prime}}}\,\rho^{-(j-i)(2+\sigma)}\,a_{i}^{\,2-4-\sigma+\frac{3}{p^{\prime}}}\leq C_{p}\Lambda_{0}^{-2}\,\rho^{\,3-2(j-i)},

where we used (
5.24
) again, so that the power of
a_{i}
vanishes, together with

\frac{3}{p^{\prime}}<3
and
2+\sigma>2
. The right-hand side is summable in
j\geq i+2
, with sum
bounded by
C_{p}\Lambda_{0}^{-2}\rho^{-1}
.

Adding the three cases we obtain (
5.35
). Finally, since the regions

\mathcal{A}_{i}
are pairwise disjoint and cover
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and since

|\Phi|\leq\sum_{j}|\varphi_{j}|
pointwise, we deduce from (
5.19
) and
(
5.35
) that

\|\Phi\|^{p^{\prime}}_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\|\Phi\|^{p^{\prime}}_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{A}_{i})}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{K}\Bigl(\|\varphi_{i}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}+\sum_{j\neq i}\|\varphi_{j}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{A}_{i})}\Bigr)^{p^{\prime}}\leq K\bigl(C_{p}+C_{p}\Lambda_{0}^{-2}\bigr)^{p^{\prime}},

which is (
5.36
).
∎

Step 4: conclusion.
Let
g=|D|^{-\sigma}u(0,\cdot)
and let

\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{K}
be as in Step 3. By virtue of (
5.22
) we may define

\theta_{i}:={\rm sgn}\,\langle g,\varphi_{i}\rangle\in\{-1,1\}
and

\Phi:=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\theta_{i}\varphi_{i}
. Then, by (
5.22
), Hölder’s inequality,
(
1.7
) and (
5.36
),

\tfrac{c_{0}}{2}\varepsilon_{0}^{3}K\ \leq\ \sum_{i=1}^{K}|\langle g,\varphi_{i}\rangle|=\langle g,\Phi\rangle\ \leq\ \|g\|_{L^{p}}\|\Phi\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\ \leq\ C_{p}A^{\frac{1}{p}}K^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}},

(5.37)

whence
K^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq 2c_{0}^{-1}C_{p}A^{\frac{1}{p}}\varepsilon_{0}^{-3}
and therefore

K\ \leq\ \bigl(2c_{0}^{-1}C_{p}\bigr)^{p}\,\varepsilon_{0}^{-3p}A\ \leq\ A\exp\bigl(\exp(M_{6}^{c_{p}})\bigr).

(5.38)

Recall that the generic constant
c_{p}
may vary from on appearance to other (we enlarge the constant
c_{p}
to absorb the factor
3p
and the constant coefficient).
Since
K\asymp\ln(2^{2j_{0}}T)/\ln\rho
and

conclude that

\ln\bigl(2^{2j_{0}}T\bigr)\leq M_{6}^{c_{p}}A\exp\bigl(\exp(M_{6}^{c_{p}})\bigr)\leq A\exp\bigl(\exp(M_{6}^{c_{p}})\bigr),

that is,

, which is exactly
(
5.2
). Note that in the above estimate the generic constant
c_{p}
may vary from on appearance to other (again we increase the constant

in the right-hand side to absorb

). This completes the proof of Lemma

.

Remark 5.8
.

The above argument uses neither the positivity of the kernel of
|D|^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}
nor any
pointwise information on
g
; the almost orthogonality involves only the explicit functions

\varphi_{i}
, so that no a priori control of

ingredients make it work: the identity (

, which is automatic since

arises as a curl and which produces the

in (

); and the geometric separation
(

) of the concentration scales.

.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By a time rescaling, we may assume that
T_{*}=1
. Set

X(t):=\|u(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}},\qquad Y(t):=\bigl\||D|^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}u(t)\bigr\|_{L^{p}},\qquad L(t):=|\ln(1-t)|.

Fix a positive absolute constant
b
. We argue by contradiction and assume that
(
1.9
) fails. Since the quantity in
(
1.9
) is nonnegative, there exist

t_{0}\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
and
C_{0}>0
such that

is positive and increasing on

[t_{0},1)
and

\exp\bigl(\exp(X(t)^{c_{p}})\bigr)Y(t)\leq C_{0}L(t)^{b},\qquad t\in[t_{0},1).

By the standard small-data theory, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that
a solution is global whenever, at some positive time,

\|u\|_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}}\leq\varepsilon_{0}
. Moreover,

\|u\|_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}}\lesssim\bigl\||D|^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}u\bigr\|_{L^{p}},

blows up at time

, (

), and
(

), we obtain, for

).

Consequently,

. Since

[

,

Appendix A
The estimates of Kato norms

We start the proof of (
2.15
) by the following Lemma:

Lemma A.1
.

Let
F\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\})
satisfy, for any multi-index
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
,

|\nabla^{\beta}F(\xi)|\lesssim|\xi|^{\sigma-|\beta|}e^{-|\xi|^{2}},\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\},

(A.1)

for some
\sigma>-3
, and let
G(x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(F)(x)
. Then we have

|G(x)|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{3+\sigma}},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

(A.2)

Proof.

By assumption (
A.1
), we see that
F\in L_{\xi}^{1}
, and by the definition of
G
, we have
\|G\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|F\|_{L^{1}}
. Then it suffices to prove that
|G(x)|\lesssim|x|^{-3-\sigma}
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
. In order to do so, for any
\delta>0,

we write

G(x)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(\xi)\chi_{\delta}(\xi)e^{ix\cdot\xi}d\xi+(2\pi)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(\xi)(1-\chi_{\delta}(\xi))e^{ix\cdot\xi}d\xi=:\text{I}+\text{II},

where
\chi_{\delta}
is a smooth cut-off function so that
\chi_{\delta}(\xi)=1
if
|\xi|\leq\delta
,
\chi_{\delta}(\xi)=0
if
|\xi|\geq 2\delta
, and
\|\nabla^{k}\chi_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\delta^{-k}
. Then we deduce from (
A.1
) that

|\text{I}|\lesssim\int_{|\xi|\leq 2\delta}|\xi|^{\sigma}d\xi\lesssim\delta^{3+\sigma}.

To handle
II
, we define the operator
\hat{\nabla}:=\frac{x\cdot\nabla_{\xi}}{|x|^{2}}
. Note that
\hat{\nabla}e^{ix\cdot\xi}=ie^{ix\cdot\xi}
, hence we get, by using integration by parts for
l=[3+\sigma]+1
times, that

\displaystyle|\text{II}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(\xi)(1-\chi_{\delta})(\xi)\hat{\nabla}^{l}e^{ix\cdot\xi}d\xi\right|\lesssim\sum_{l_{1}+l_{2}=l}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\bigl|\hat{\nabla}^{l_{1}}F(\xi)\hat{\nabla}^{l_{2}}(1-\chi_{\delta}(\xi))\bigr|\,d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim|x|^{-l}\Bigl(\sum_{k=0}^{l-1}\delta^{k-l}\int_{\delta\leq|\xi|\leq 2\delta}|\xi|^{\sigma-k}d\xi+\int_{|\xi|\geq\delta}|\xi|^{\sigma-l}d\xi\Bigr)\lesssim|x|^{-l}\delta^{\sigma+3-l}.

As a result, it comes out

|G(x)|\lesssim\delta^{3+\sigma}+|x|^{-l}\delta^{\sigma+3-l}.

Taking
\delta=|x|^{-1}
leads to (
A.2
), and we complete the proof Lemma
A.1
.
∎

p\in[1,\infty]
, we get

\|\nabla^{\alpha}\Gamma(t)\|_{L^{p}}\leq C_{\alpha}t^{-\frac{|\alpha|}{2}-2+\frac{3}{2p}},\quad\forall t>0.

(A.5)

We shall prove (
2.15
) by induction argument. It is easy to observe from (
2.3
) and
(
2.11
) that
(
2.15
) holds for
k=1
.
Inductively, assume that (
2.15
) holds for every
1\leq k\leq\ell
,
where
1\leq\ell\leq m-1
. We shall prove it for
k=\ell+1
.
By the inductive assumption, for every
k\leq\ell
and
|\alpha|\leq N_{0}
, we have

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in(0,T]\\
q\in\bigl[\max\{1,\frac{p}{k}\},\infty\bigr]\end{subarray}}\Bigl(t^{\frac{1}{2}\left(|\alpha|+1-\frac{3}{q}\right)}\|\nabla^{\alpha}u_{kL}(t)\|_{L^{q}}\Bigr)\lesssim\bigl(1+\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}}\bigr)^{2^{k-1}}.

(A.6)

To construct
u_{(\ell+1)L}
, we first consider the case
|\alpha|=0
. By virtue of
(
A.3
) and (
A.5
), for

r=\max\bigl\{1,\frac{p}{\ell+1}\bigr\}
, we infer

\displaystyle\|u_{(\ell+1)L}(t)\|_{L^{r}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|\Gamma(t-\tau)\|_{L^{1}}\Bigl(\|u_{\ell L}\|_{L^{\frac{pr}{p-r}}}\|u_{\ell L}\|_{L^{p}}+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}\|u_{iL}\|_{L^{p}}\|u_{\ell L}\|_{L^{\frac{pr}{p-r}}}\Bigr)(\tau)\,d\tau.

Observing that
\frac{pr}{p-r}\geq\frac{p}{\ell}
due to
r\geq\frac{p}{\ell+1},
we get, by applying (
A.6
), that

\displaystyle\|u_{(\ell+1)L}(t)\|_{L^{r}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\tau^{-1+\frac{3}{2r}}d\tau(1+\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}})^{2^{\ell}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{3}{r})}\bigl(1+\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}}\bigr)^{2^{\ell}}.

Along the same line, for
\varepsilon
being sufficiently small, one has

\displaystyle\|u_{(\ell+1)L}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|\Gamma(t-\tau)\|_{L^{\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}}}\Bigl(\|u_{\ell L}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{\ell L}\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}}+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}\|u_{iL}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{\ell L}\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}}\Bigr)(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon}\tau^{-1+\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon}d\tau\bigl(1+\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}}\bigr)^{2^{\ell}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bigl(1+\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}}\bigr)^{2^{\ell}}.

By interpolating the above two inequalities, we arrive at

\sup_{t\in(0,T]}\Bigl(t^{\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{3}{q}\right)}\|u_{(\ell+1)L}(t)\|_{L^{q}}\Bigr)\lesssim\bigl(1+\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}}\bigr)^{2^{\ell}},\quad\forall q\geq\max\bigl\{1,\frac{p}{\ell+1}\bigr\}.

(A.7)

Next we are going to establish estimates on the derivatives of
u_{(\ell+1)L}
. In view of (
A.3
), for
r=\max\bigl\{1,\frac{p}{\ell+1}\bigr\}
and

|\alpha|\leq N_{0},
we infer

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{\alpha}u_{(\ell+1)L}(t)\|_{L^{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\Gamma(t-\tau)\|_{L^{1}}\Bigl(\|u_{\ell L}\|_{L^{\frac{pr}{p-r}}}\|u_{\ell L}\|_{L^{p}}+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}\|u_{iL}\|_{L^{p}}\|u_{\ell L}\|_{L^{\frac{pr}{p-r}}}\Bigr)(\tau)\,d\tau

\displaystyle+\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\|\Gamma(t-\tau)\|_{L^{1}}\Bigl(\|\nabla^{\beta}u_{\ell L}\|_{L^{\frac{pr}{p-r}}}\|\nabla^{\gamma}u_{\ell L}\|_{L^{p}}+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}\|\nabla^{\beta}u_{iL}\|_{L^{p}}\|\nabla^{\gamma}u_{\ell L}\|_{L^{\frac{pr}{p-r}}}\Bigr)(\tau)d\tau.

In the second term on the right-hand side of the above estimate,
\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
are multi-indices such that
\beta+\gamma=\alpha
. On one hand, since
\frac{pr}{p-r}\geq\frac{p}{\ell}
, we get, by applying (
A.5
) and (
A.6
), that

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{\alpha}u_{(\ell+1)L}(t)\|_{L^{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigl(\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(t-\tau)^{-\frac{1+|\alpha|}{2}}\tau^{-1+\frac{3}{2r}}\,d\tau+\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}(t-\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\tau^{-1+\frac{3}{2r}-\frac{|\alpha|}{2}}d\tau\Bigr)\bigl(1+\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}}\bigr)^{2^{\ell}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{|\alpha|}{2}-\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{3}{r})}\bigl(1+\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}}\bigr)^{2^{\ell}}.

On the other hand, for some
\varepsilon
small enough, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{\alpha}u_{(\ell+1)L}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\Gamma(t-\tau)\|_{L^{\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}}}\Bigl(\|u_{\ell L}\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}}\|u_{\ell L}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}\|u_{iL}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{\ell L}\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}}\Bigr)(\tau)\,d\tau

\displaystyle+\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\|\Gamma(t-\tau)\|_{L^{\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}}}\Bigl(\|\nabla^{\beta}u_{\ell L}\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}}\|\nabla^{\gamma}u_{\ell L}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}\|\nabla^{\beta}u_{iL}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{\gamma}u_{\ell L}\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}}\Bigr)(\tau)\,d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigl(\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(t-\tau)^{-\frac{1+|\alpha|}{2}-\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon}\tau^{-1+\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon}d\tau+\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}(t-\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon}\tau^{-1+\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon-\frac{|\alpha|}{2}}\,d\tau\Bigr)\bigl(1+\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}}\bigr)^{2^{\ell}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{1+|\alpha|}{2}}\bigl(1+\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}}\bigr)^{2^{\ell}}.

By interpolating the above two inequalities, for any
q\geq\max\bigl\{1,\frac{p}{\ell+1}\bigr\},
we achieve

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in(0,T]\\
|\alpha|\leq N_{0}\end{subarray}}\Bigl(t^{\frac{|\alpha|}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{3}{q}\right)}\|\nabla^{\alpha}u_{(\ell+1)L}(t)\|_{L^{q}}\Bigr)\lesssim\bigl(1+\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}}\bigr)^{2^{\ell}}.

(A.8)

By combining(
A.7
) and (
A.8
), we derive (
A.6
) for
k=\ell+1
. By induction, we obtain (
A.6
) for any
1\leq k\leq m
, thus we complete the proof of (
2.15
).
∎

be the heat kernel in

(B.3)

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\mathcal{C}(M_{6}^{-10}R,M_{6}^{10}R)}K(t+3/4,x-y)u_{1L}(-3/4,y)\,dy.

9<\gamma_{1}<10

\displaystyle-\int_{t^{j-1}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Gamma(t-s,x-y)F_{j}(s,y)dyds=:u_{jL}^{1}+u_{jL}^{2},

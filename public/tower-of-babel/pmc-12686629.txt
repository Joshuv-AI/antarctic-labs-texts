Background

Spatio-temporal modeling is a statistical and machine learning approach that integrates time-series and spatial data to capture variations across both temporal and geographical dimensions. A spatio-temporal model is a form of local regression that incorporates geographical information using both panel data and spatial data [2]. Panel data consist of a combination of cross-sectional and time-series observations, while spatial data refer to values influenced by geographical location [3]. Geographical factors may produce spatial effects that vary across regions, resulting in heterogeneous characteristics among observation areas [[4], [5]] in which the value at one location may depend on values observed at neighboring locations. This framework is particularly useful for identifying region-specific patterns and temporal trends that may not be visible in conventional time-series or purely spatial models.

In this study, we employ Geographically and Temporally Weighted Spline Regression (GTWSR), a nonparametric extension of GTWR, to analyze and forecast the prices of eight strategic food commodities rice, shallots, garlic, red chili, cayenne pepper, chicken meat, beef, and chicken eggs across 34 provinces in Indonesia from January 2022 to August 2024. GTWSR is preferred over standard GTWR and panel regression because it flexibly accommodates nonlinear relationships, accounts for spatial heterogeneity, and adapts to temporal dependencies in the data, providing a more robust representation of complex market dynamics.

The dataset consists of secondary data obtained from the Strategic Food Price Information Center (SFPIC) and Statistics Indonesia (BPS). The methodological framework includes data preprocessing (normalization, outlier detection, and treatment of missing values), spatial distance calculation, bandwidth optimization using cross-validation, parameter estimation, statistical hypothesis testing, and short-term price forecasting. Model performance is evaluated using the coefficient of determination (R²), Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), Mean Absolute Error (MAE), and Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE). Assumptions of homoskedasticity and absence of spatial autocorrelation in residuals are tested to ensure model validity. By integrating methodological rigor with practical application, this framework contributes to food security policy by identifying region-specific price dynamics and generating evidence based forecasts to support targeted interventions.

Method details

General methodology description: the influence of spatio-temporal factors on food price dynamics in Indonesia

Indonesia’s geographical diversity, encompassing 34 provinces spread across thousands of islands, results in significant spatial variations in food prices. These spatial disparities, coupled with temporal fluctuations, necessitate a methodological framework capable of simultaneously analyzing both spatial and temporal dimensions.

This study applies Geographically and Temporally Weighted Spline Regression (GTWSR), a nonparametric extension of GTWR, to investigate the dynamics of eight key food commodities rice, shallots, garlic, red chili, cayenne pepper, chicken meat, beef, and chicken eggs over the period January 2022 to August 2024. GTWSR was selected over standard GTWR or panel regression because it can flexibly capture nonlinear relationships, account for spatial heterogeneity, and adapt to temporal dependencies inherent in the data.

The dataset, obtained from the Strategic Food Price Information Center (SFPIC) and Statistics Indonesia (BPS), underwent several preprocessing steps, including handling missing values, normalization, and outlier treatment based on boxplot diagnostics. Extreme observations identified through boxplots were manually reviewed and, where necessary, slightly adjusted to prevent distortion of the overall data pattern while preserving the integrity of the distribution. Missing values were imputed using provincial mean prices since intra-provincial variations were smaller than inter-provincial differences. Temporal interpolation was also tested as an alternative but produced unstable outcomes in provinces with irregular reporting intervals; therefore, provincial mean imputation was considered more robust and interpretable for maintaining consistency across regions. Spatial relationships among provinces were represented through distance-based measures reflecting regional connectivity. Bandwidth selection was optimized via 10-fold cross-validation, where the adaptive tricube kernel achieved the lowest validation error due to its ability to accommodate uneven spatial distributions across provinces. To prevent potential data leakage, folds were stratified temporally, ensuring that later-period data were never used to predict earlier ones. Parameter estimation was performed locally at each province time combination, followed by hypothesis testing to assess the significance of explanatory variables. In the GTWSR model, the parameter θ regulates the balance between spatial and temporal weighting; its calibration was determined empirically by minimizing cross-validation error to achieve optimal spatio-temporal smoothing. Bandwidth optimization was carried out separately for each commodity to account for heterogeneity in price dynamics and spatial dependencies across markets.

Model performance was evaluated using R², RMSE, MAE, and MAPE. Each metric in Table 3 is reported along with its standard deviation across the 10 cross-validation folds to indicate performance variability. The adaptive tricube kernel was also compared against fixed kernels Gaussian and Bisquare, revealing that the adaptive version yielded more stable results and reduced the risk of overfitting in spatially dense regions. Multicollinearity among predictors was examined using the Variance Inflation Factor (VIF), indicating no serious multicollinearity issues.

Experimental design, materials, and methods

This study utilizes secondary data from SFPIC, which provides daily updates on essential food commodity prices in Indonesia, and from BPS. Although SFPIC covers ten commodities, this study focuses on eight due to data completeness and quality considerations. Cooking oil and sugar were excluded due to missing or inconsistent observations across provinces. The analysis covers January 2022 to August 2024 for all 34 provinces.

To ensure policy relevance, daily data were aggregated into monthly averages, since monthly trends better represent macroeconomic food security and inflation management while reducing short-term volatility and noise. The dataset underwent preprocessing consisting of (1) handling missing values through provincial mean imputation, (2) normalization of price series to ensure comparability, and (3) detection and adjustment of outliers using boxplot diagnostics.

Extreme values identified in boxplots were carefully examined and, when necessary, adjusted or removed to maintain realistic price behavior while preserving the data structure. Missing values were imputed with provincial mean prices, providing consistent and interpretable estimates across regions. Temporal interpolation produced unstable results in provinces with irregular reporting intervals, confirming the robustness of provincial mean imputation.

The methodological framework applies GTWSR, a nonparametric extension of GTWR that models nonlinear and heterogeneous spatio-temporal effects. This framework integrates spatial information based on interprovincial connectivity with temporal indices to capture evolving patterns over time. Exploratory data analysis employed bar charts and spatial mapping to identify regional disparities and temporal trends.

Fig. 1, Fig. 2, Fig. 3, Fig. 4, Fig. 5, Fig. 6, Fig. 7, Fig. 8, Fig. 9 provide a comprehensive overview of provincial disparities in the prices of eight strategic food commodities during 2023–2024. Although all 34 provinces are shown, the main insights can be summarized as follows: rice prices remain persistently higher in Kalimantan compared to Java, red chili prices show pronounced volatility in Eastern Indonesia, and beef prices are consistently elevated in provinces with limited livestock production. By presenting all provinces simultaneously, the figures highlight the strong spatial heterogeneity of food price dynamics in Indonesia, while the discussion in the text emphasizes only the most policy-relevant patterns to avoid redundancy.

Method validation

Modeling of national strategic food commodity price index with spatio-temporal method

The analysis proceeds with the implementation of Geographically and Temporally Weighted Spline Regression (GTWSR) [6,18]. an extension of the Geographically Weighted Regression (GWR) framework proposed by Fotheringham, Brunsdon, and Charlton (2002). This stage involves estimating location-varying parameters, testing hypotheses, and evaluating model assumptions including homoscedasticity [9] and residual spatial autocorrelation. Model performance was assessed using multiple accuracy metrics: the coefficient of determination (R²), Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), Mean Absolute Error (MAE), and Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) [7,8].

The general GTWSR model for the i-th province at time t is shown in Eq. (1). Predictor variables are represented using truncated spline basis functions to capture nonlinear patterns in commodity price dynamics. In accordance with spline theory by Wahba (1990) [19], Eubank (1999) [20], and Hardle (1990) [21], truncated splines were constructed using fixed knot locations that follow a quantile-based rule. Internal knots were placed at the 25th, 50th, and 75th percentiles of each predictor’s empirical distribution. The spline construction is implemented per predictor, not shared across predictors, because each covariate exhibits different degrees of smoothness and distributional characteristics. This knot assignment is consistent with the recommendations of Wahba and Eubank, who emphasize variable-specific flexibility in spline smoothing to avoid oversmoothing or undersmoothing across heterogeneous predictors.

Model output description

Based on the factors influencing the Price Index Received by Farmers in 34 provinces of Indonesia, eight distinct groups of spatio-temporal models have been identified. This section focuses on interpreting one specific case—the spatio-temporal model for East Java Province, which belongs to the fourth group. The model, estimated for two time periods (t=1andt=2), is represented by Eq. (4). Using adaptive tricube weighting, the model captures spatial and temporal heterogeneity, allowing coefficient estimates to vary by location and period. The best-fitting specification achieved a coefficient of determination R2 of 91.61 % and a Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) of 1.2186, indicating high explanatory and predictive accuracy. Goodness-of-fit testing confirmed that the spatio-temporal framework significantly outperforms a conventional panel regression model (FGOF=3.3060,p=7.38×10−6).

The spatio-temporal regression model for East Java Province, as shown in Eq. (4), incorporates eight explanatory variables (x1to x8) representing the prices of different strategic food commodities, with coefficients allowed to vary over time. The model indicates that among these variables, the price of red chili (x4) and the price of beef (x7) have the most substantial and statistically significant effects on the Price Index Received by Farmers. The estimated coefficient for red chili is 0.0003, meaning that for every increase of 1 rupiah in red chili prices, the Price Index Received by Farmers in East Java is expected to increase by 0.0003 units. Similarly, the coefficient for beef is 0.0004, indicating that each 1 rupiah increase in beef prices corresponds to a 0.0004 unit increase in the index.

The positive and significant influence of these two commodities highlights their strategic role in shaping farmer welfare in East Java. Fluctuations in red chili and beef prices can directly impact the purchasing power and economic stability of farmers in the region. The relatively small coefficients for the other variables suggest that their price changes exert weaker or insignificant effects on the index within this province, reinforcing the need for a localized policy focus.

From a policy perspective, these findings imply that price stabilization mechanisms targeting red chili and beef could effectively enhance farmer income stability in East Java. Measures such as improving supply chain efficiency, investing in cold storage facilities, and optimizing distribution networks could help reduce price volatility for these commodities. Moreover, integrating this spatio-temporal approach into regular market monitoring could serve as an early warning system for policymakers, enabling timely interventions to safeguard farmer welfare against adverse price shocks.

Method summary

This study presents a spatio-temporal modeling framework to analyze and forecast the Price Index Received by Farmers across 34 provinces in Indonesia, using eight strategic food commodity prices obtained from the Strategic Food Price Information Center (SFPIC). The model employs a Geographically and Temporally Weighted Spline Regression (GTWSR) approach with an adaptive tricube weighting function.

The procedure includes: (1) data preprocessing, (2) spatial distance calculation, (3) bandwidth optimization via cross-validation, (4) parameter estimation, (5) statistical hypothesis testing, and (6) short-term price forecasting. Model performance was evaluated using the coefficient of determination (R²) and Root Mean Square Error (RMSE). The framework provides an effective approach for capturing spatial heterogeneity and temporal trends in price data, and can be applied to other datasets with similar spatio-temporal characteristics.

Limitations

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the analysis relies on monthly data aggregated from daily observations, which may smooth short-term volatility and limit the model’s ability to capture high-frequency price dynamics. Second, the temporal scope of the dataset ends in August 2024, restricting the model’s applicability for longer-term forecasting beyond this period. Third, as the data are obtained from secondary sources (SFPIC and BPS), replication is dependent on the availability and consistency of these datasets. Finally, the model assumes a certain degree of stationarity and homogeneity within the spatio-temporal framework; deviations from these assumptions may affect the robustness of the results. Future studies could address these limitations by incorporating higher-frequency data, extending the temporal coverage, and exploring alternative methodological frameworks.

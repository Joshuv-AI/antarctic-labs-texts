Abstract
In this paper, we will study the issue about the 1- � \Gamma inverse, where � ∈ { † , D , ∗ } \Gamma\in\{{\dagger},\ D,\ *\} , via the M-product. The aim of the current study is threefold. \displaystyle=\mathcal{P}^{-}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{*}

and

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{-}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{*}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{\dagger}\star_{M}\left(\mathcal{P}^{\dagger}\right)^{*}=\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{*}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{\dagger}\star_{M}\left(\mathcal{P}^{\dagger}\right)^{*}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{*}\star_{M}\left(\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{\dagger}\right)^{*}\star_{M}\left(\mathcal{P}^{\dagger}\right)^{*}=\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{*}\star_{M}\left(\mathcal{P}^{\dagger}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{\dagger}\right)^{*}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{*}\star_{M}\left(\mathcal{P}^{\dagger}\right)^{*}=\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\left(\mathcal{P}^{\dagger}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\right)^{*}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{\dagger}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}.

(d)
\Longrightarrow

(a)
If
\mathcal{P}^{-}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{X}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{\dagger}=\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{X}\star_{M}\left(\mathcal{P}^{\dagger}\right)^{*}=\mathcal{P}
, then we can get

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{P}^{-}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{X}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{\dagger}=\mathcal{P}^{-}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{X}\star_{M}\left(\mathcal{P}^{\dagger}\right)^{*}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{*}=\mathcal{P}^{-}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{*}.

∎

Theorem 14
.

Let
\mathcal{P}\in\mdmathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
and

Proof.

(a)
\Longrightarrow

(b)

(b)
\Longrightarrow

(a)
If
\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{-,*}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}
, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{-,*}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{-}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{*}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{*}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}.

(a)
\Longrightarrow

(c)
Since
\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{*}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}
, one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{-,*}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{-,*}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}^{-}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{*}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{-}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{*}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}^{-}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{-}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{*}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}^{-,*}=\mathcal{P}^{-,*}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{-,*}=\mathcal{P}^{-}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{*}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{-}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{*}

Theorem 15
.

Let
\mathcal{P}\in\mdmathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}^{-}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{*}\star_{M}\left(\mathcal{P}^{\dagger}\right)^{*}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{\left(1\right)}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{*}

Theorem 16
.

Let
\mathcal{P}\in\mdmathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
and
\mathcal{B}\in\mdmathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times 1\times n_{3}}

\mathcal{Z}\in\mdmathbb{C}^{n_{2}\times 1\times n_{3}}
,

\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{{\dagger}}\star_{M}\mathcal{B},

(29)

is solvable and
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{P}^{-,{\dagger}}\star_{M}\mathcal{B}+\left(\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{P}^{-}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\right)\star_{M}\mathcal{Z}
are the solutions of (
29
).

Proof.

Clearly,
\mathcal{P}^{-,{\dagger}}\star_{M}\mathcal{B}
satisfies (
29
). Hence, the system (
29
) is consistent. Now,

\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{-,{\dagger}}\star_{M}\mathcal{B}+\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\left(\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{P}^{-}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\right)\star_{M}\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{{\dagger}}\star_{M}\mathcal{B}.

Also, if
\mathcal{X}
is a solution of (
29
), then

\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{P}^{-,{\dagger}}\star_{M}\mathcal{B}+\left(\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{P}^{-}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\right)\star_{M}\mathcal{X}.

So, the general solution of the system (
29
) is given by

\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{P}^{-,{\dagger}}\star_{M}\mathcal{B}+\left(\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{P}^{-}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\right)\star_{M}\mathcal{Z}.

∎

Theorem 17
.

Let
\mathcal{P}\in\mdmathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
and
\mathcal{P}^{-}
be a
\{1\}
-inverse of
\mathcal{P}

Suppose

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}\star_{M}\mathcal{P}^{\dagger}

5
Conclusion

This work presents and studies the 1-
\Gamma
inverse under the M-product. Firstly, the singular value decomposition is used to present an expression for the 1-MP inverse. An equivalent numerical algorithm for computing this inverse is developed. Next, some 1-MP inverse characterization is shown. Secondly, the 1-D inverse of the tensor is further explained. A partial description of the 1-D inverse is provided. Thirdly, the 1-Star inverse under the M-product operation is described. The 1-Star inverse’s numerical computation technique is also constructed, and the associated attributes are provided. Based on these work, the solutions to the 1-MP inverses, 1-D inverses, and 1-Star inverses of the multilinear equations are provided, along with an example program that computes the corresponding 1-
\Gamma
inverse.

Minimal dispersion on the sphere
Alexander E. Litvak, Mathias Sonnleitner, Tomasz Szczepanski *

Abstract
The minimal spherical cap dispersion disp 𝒞 ⁡ ( n , d ) \disp_{\mathcal{C}}(n,d) is the largest number ε ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] \varepsilon\in(0,1] such that, for every n n points on the d d -dimensional Euclidean unit sphere 𝕊 d \mathbb{S}^{d} , there exists a spherical cap with normalized area ε \varepsilon not containing any of these points. We study the behavior of disp 𝒞 ⁡ ( n , d ) \disp_{\mathcal{C}}(n,d) as n n and d d grow to infinity. We develop connections to the problems of sphere covering and approximation of the Euclidean unit ball by inscribed polytopes. Existing and new results are presented in a unified way. Upper bounds on disp 𝒞 ⁡ ( n , d ) \disp_{\mathcal{C}}(n,d) result from choosing the points independently and uniformly at random and possibly adding some well-separated points to close large gaps. Moreover, we study dispersion with respect to intersections of caps. Keywords . dispersion, covering density, covering radius, largest empty cap MSC 2020 . Primary: 52B55, 52C17; Secondary: 52A23, 52C45

Introduction and results

Given a point set
P=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
on the
d
-dimensional Euclidean unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, where
d\geq 1
, define its spherical cap dispersion by

\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(P):=\sup_{C\in\mathcal{C}}\{\sigma(C)\colon C\cap\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}=\emptyset\},

where
\mathcal{C}
denotes the family of spherical caps of
\mathbb{S}^{d}
and
\sigma
is the normalized Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, which
we also call the spherical measure. We define the minimal spherical cap dispersion by

\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(n,d):=\inf_{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}}\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}).

(1.1)

We are interested in the asymptotic behavior of
\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(n,d)
as
n
and
d
grow to infinity.

In the case of the unit cube
[0,1]^{d}
equipped with the Lebesgue measure and the family of axis-parallel boxes,
the minimal dispersion was introduced in the context of uniform distribution theory by Hlawka
[
40
]
and
Niederreiter
[
50
]
. We refer to
[
2
,
15
,
45
,
46
,
62
,
63
]
and references therein for history and recent progress.

In the case of
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, the minimal dispersion has been studied for spherical slices (intersections of half-spheres) by Rote and Tichy
[
56
]
and subsequently by Prochno and Rudolf
[
51
]
for intersections of two spherical caps, that is for the family

\mathcal{L}=\{C_{1}\cap C_{2}\colon C_{1},C_{2}\in\mathcal{C}\},

which we refer to as spherical lenses. The corresponding notion of minimal lens dispersion is then given by

\disp_{\mathcal{L}}(n,d):=\inf_{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}}\,\,\sup_{A\in\mathcal{L}}\,\{\sigma(A)\colon A\cap\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}=\emptyset\}.

(1.2)

The study of spherical dispersion is motivated by a question of Erdős
[
25
, p. 54]

who asked for a lower bound on the minimal spherical cap discrepancy

D(n,d):=\inf_{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}}\sup_{C\in\mathcal{C}}\Big|\frac{|\{i\colon x_{i}\in C\}|}{n}-\sigma(C)\Big|,

where the supremum is taken over the system of spherical caps
\mathcal{C}
(see Blümlinger
[
10
]
for the spherical slice discrepancy).
A lower bound for
D(n,d)
was given by Schmidt
[
58
]
and improved by Beck
[
6
]
, who had provided an almost matching
upper bound in
[
5
]
. It is a well-known open problem to determine the asymptotic behavior of
D(n,d)
as
n\to\infty
for
d\geq 2
.
Clearly,
D(n,d)\geq\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(n,d)
and, as we present below, the behavior of
\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(n,d)
is better understood than
that of
D(n,d)
. At the end of the introduction we will comment on
\disp_{\mathcal{L}}(n,d)
.

Recall that a family
\mathcal{C}
of spherical caps coincides with the family of geodesic balls

B(x,\varphi)=\{y\in\mathbb{S}^{d}\colon\varrho(x,y)\leq\varphi\},\qquad x\in\mathbb{S}^{d},\varphi\in[0,\pi],

where
\varrho(x,y)=\arccos(\langle x,y\rangle)
denotes the geodesic distance.
Note that the dispersion remains unchanged whether we consider open or closed caps,
and we work with the latter. We start with the observation that the minimal spherical
cap dispersion is related to covering the sphere by caps. Define
the minimal spherical covering density of the sphere by

\dens(n,d):=\inf\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma(B(x_{i},\varphi))\colon\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}B(x_{i},\varphi)=\mathbb{S}^{d},x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{d},\varphi\in(0,\pi]\Big\}

and the minimal geodesic covering radius by

\varphi(n):=\inf\Big\{\varphi>0\colon\exists x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}\text{ with }\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}B(x_{i},\varphi)=\mathbb{S}^{d}\Big\}.

These two parameters have been intensively studied, see the books and surveys
[
3
,
11
,
20
,
27
,
29
,
30
,
55
]
.
We summarize the relations between them and dispersion in the following lemma, which will be proved in
Section
2.2
.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
d,n
be positive integers. Then

n\cdot\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(n,d)=\dens(n,d)=n\cdot V(\varphi(n)),

(1.3)

where

V(\varphi)=\sigma(B(x,\varphi))
denotes the normalized volume of a cap with geodesic
radius
\varphi\in[0,\pi]
centered at
x\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
.

In the following, we derive statements about behavior of the spherical cap dispersion.
It is easy to see that
\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(n,d)\geq\frac{1}{n}
(which also follows from
\dens(n,d)\geq 1
and (
1.3
)). When
n\leq d+1
the affine hull of
n
points spans an affine subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
with dimension at most
d
. Moreover, any cap of area at least
\frac{1}{2}
intersects a given pair of antipodal points. Therefore we have

\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(1,d)=1\quad\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\quad\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(2,d)=\cdots=\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(d+1,d)=\frac{1}{2}.

(1.4)

For
d+2\leq n\leq 2d+2
optimizers are expected to be regular structures, see
[
12
, Conjecture 1.3]
and
[
11
, Chapter 6]
.
More precisely, for
n=d+1+k
,
1\leq k\leq d+1
, split
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
into an orthogonal sum of
k
subspaces
E_{i}
of the dimensions

\lceil(d+1)/k\rceil
and
\lfloor(d+1)/k\rfloor
and in each
E_{i}
take a regular simplex inscribed into the Euclidean ball.
Then take convex hull of such simplices. In particular,
for
n=d+2
(so
k=1
) a regular simplex inscribed into
\mathbb{S}^{d}
gives the optimal covering with radius

\varphi(d+2)=\arccos\frac{1}{d+1}
and for
n=2d+2
(so
k=d+1
) the cross-polytope inscribed into
\mathbb{S}^{d}
yields a covering with

n
caps of radius
\arccos\frac{1}{\sqrt{d+1}}
, which is conjectured to be optimal (see also
[
22
, Problem 4]
). We note also
that such arrangements were used to prove sharpness of the lower bound on the distance between convex polytopes with few vertices
and centrally-symmetric bodies
[
31
]
.
It follows from volume bounds given in Section
2.1
(see Remark
2.3
) that

\frac{1}{2}-\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(d+2,d)\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi d}},

(1.5)

where for sequences
(a_{d})_{d}
and
(b_{d})_{d}
of positive numbers we write
a_{d}\sim b_{d}
for
\lim_{d\to\infty}\frac{a_{d}}{b_{d}}=1
. Moreover, if
n=2d+2
, then

\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(2d+2,d)\leq\frac{1+o(1)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-x^{2}/2}\,\mathrm{d}x\qquad\text{as }d\to\infty

(1.6)

(see Lemma
2.4
and Remark
2.5
below).

Remark 1.2
.

For completeness we remark that in the case of
d=1
the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{1}
is a one-dimensional torus and
\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(n,1)=\frac{1}{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, we focus on
d\geq 2
. We refer to
[
2
,
45
,
46
,
57
,
64
]
about dispersion with respect to periodic axis-parallel boxes on a torus of dimension
d\geq 2
.

If
n
is sufficiently large compared to
d
, then local approximation of
\mathbb{S}^{d}
by
\mathbb{R}^{d}
suggests that
\dens(n,d)
is related to the minimal covering density of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by equally sized balls. The latter can be defined by

\vartheta_{d}=\inf_{\mathcal{B}}\lim_{R\to\infty}\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\frac{\vol_{d}(B\cap B^{d}(0,R))}{\vol_{d}(B^{d}(0,R))},

(1.7)

where the infimum is taken over any covering
\mathcal{B}=\{B^{d}(x_{1},1),B^{d}(x_{2},1),\dots\}
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}

by Euclidean balls of unit radius. Indeed, in the next theorem we show that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\dens(n,d)=\vartheta_{d}

(we state all our results in the equivalent notion of dispersion, see Lemma
1.1
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
d\geq 2
. Then the minimal dispersion satisfies

\lim_{n\to\infty}n\cdot\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(n,d)=\vartheta_{d}.

Instead of proving Theorem
1.3
directly, we deduce it from the known asymptotic for the best
approximation of the Euclidean unit ball
B^{d+1}
by inscribed polytopes with
n
vertices in
Hausdorff distance (see e.g.,
[
59
]
).

The minimal covering density of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is known to satisfy
\vartheta_{2}=\frac{2\pi}{3\sqrt{3}}
in the case
d=2
which is attained by the hexagonal lattice (see
[
43
]
) and, for
d\geq 3
,

\frac{d}{e\sqrt{e}}\sim\tau_{d}\leq\vartheta_{d}\leq d\ln d+d\ln\ln d+5d.

(1.8)

The lower bound in (
1.8
) is due to Coxeter-Few-Rogers
[
21
]
and the upper is due to Rogers
[
53
]
,
which was improved by Fejes Tóth
[
28
]
by replacing the summand
5d
with
d
and by Dumer
[
24
]
who obtained the bound
(1/2+o(1))d\ln d
. The constant
\tau_{d}
can be written explicitly in terms of the interior angle of a
d
-dimensional regular simplex.

Remark 1.4
.

The upper bound in (
1.8
) also holds for coverings by other convex shapes, while no nontrivial lower bound for shapes other than the ball is known so far, see Fejes Tóth
[
35
, p. 36]
. We refer to
[
16
]
for recent results developments in this direction.

It follows from Theorem
1.3
and the bounds in (
1.8
) that

d\lesssim\lim_{n\to\infty}n\cdot\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(n,d)\lesssim d\ln d,

(1.9)

where for sequences
(a_{d})_{d}
and
(b_{d})_{d}
of positive numbers we write
a_{d}\lesssim b_{d}
if for some
C>0
and all
d
one has

a_{d}\leq C\,b_{d}
. Note that compared to the minimal dispersion on the cube with respect to axis-parallel boxes (see Bukh and Chao

[
15
]
, who showed
cd
for the lower bound
and
Cd^{2}\ln d
for the upper bound) we have only a logarithmic gap between the lower and upper bounds in the case of the spherical cap dispersion.

Regarding the upper bound in (
1.8
), Erdős and Rogers
[
26
]
have shown that there exists
a covering of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by unit balls such that no point is covered by more than
e(d\ln d+d\ln\ln d+5d)
balls.
In the case of the sphere, Böröczky Jr. and Wintsche
[
12
]
proved that for any
\varphi<\frac{\pi}{2}
there is a covering
of
\mathbb{S}^{d}
by caps of geodesic radius
\varphi
such that no point of
\mathbb{S}^{d}
is covered by more than
400d\ln d
caps.
From this one can derive the following bound.

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
d,n\geq 2
. Then the minimal dispersion satisfies

n\cdot\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(n,d)\leq 400d\ln d.

In fact, in
[
12
]
(Remark 5.1 and the end of its proof, see also
[
48
, Theorem 2.2]
) the authors
proved also that there is a covering of the sphere with the density bounded
by
d\ln d+d\ln\ln d+5d
(note, this is the same
bound as in (
1.8
)).
This leads to the following estimate.

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
d,n\geq 2
. Then the minimal dispersion satisfies

n\cdot\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(n,d)\leq d\ln d+d\ln\ln d+5d.

The bound in Proposition
1.6
can be further improved as follows. Corollary 1.2 in
[
12
]
states that for every
\varphi\in(0,\arccos\frac{1}{\sqrt{d+1}})
there is a covering of
\mathbb{S}^{d}
by at most

C\,\frac{\cos\varphi}{\sin^{d}\varphi}\cdot d^{3/2}\ln(1+d\cos^{2}\varphi)

(1.10)

geodesic balls of radius
\varphi
with density bounded by
c\ln(1+d\cos^{2}\varphi)
, where
C,c>0
are absolute constants.
From this we deduce the following bound which improves Proposition
1.6
if
n
is subexponential in
d
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
d\geq 2
. Then the minimal dispersion satisfies

n\cdot\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(n,d)\leq C\,d\ln\ln\frac{2n}{d}\qquad\text{for }\,\,\,n\geq 2d,

where
C>1
is an absolute constant.

In particular, Theorem
1.7
shows that
\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(cd,d)
can be made arbitrarily small by choosing
c
large enough.
The same is true for the spherical cap discrepancy (see
[
38
, Theorem 3]
which may be adapted to our situation using
Lemma
5.2
below). This is in contrast to the related problem of approximation of the Euclidean ball
B^{d+1}
by inscribed polytopes
with
n
vertices, where
n
needs to grow faster than
d^{d/2}
for the error to converge to zero, which follows from bounds
provided in
[
4
,
18
,
32
]
(which are essentially inequality (
2.7
) below).

Theorem
1.7
and Lemma
1.1
imply that
V(\varphi(n))\leq C\,(d/n)\ln\ln(2n/d)
.
This immediately provides a bound on covering numbers of the sphere.
Recall that a set of points
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathbb{S}^{d}
is called a (geodesic)
\varepsilon
-net if for every
x\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
there is

y\in\mathcal{N}
such that
\varrho(x,y)\leq\varepsilon
. Therefore, adjusting the constant
C
we have the following bound.

Corollary 1.8
.

For every
d\geq 2
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\pi/2)
there exists a geodesic
\varepsilon
-net of
\mathbb{S}^{d}
with cardinality bounded by

d\,\max\Big\{2,\,\frac{C\,\ln\ln(2/V(\varepsilon))}{V(\varepsilon)}\Big\},

where
C>0
is an absolute constant.

This estimate complements standard bounds (see for example Corollary 5.5 in
[
3
]
) and should be compared with bounds
in Examples 6.1 and 6.2 in
[
12
]
.

Moreover, the results of
[
12
]
imply the following lower bound, which unfortunately holds only for
n
superexponential in
d
.
This restriction appears as the proof uses the lower bound in (
1.8
) for
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a
sufficiently good approximation of
B^{d+1}
by a polytope with
n
vertices. We would like to note that a similar
restriction on
n
also appears (although for a different reason)
in the lower bound in
[
15
]
for the minimal dispersion in the case of the cube.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
d\geq 2
.
Then the minimal dispersion satisfies

n\cdot\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(n,d)\geq c_{0}\,d\qquad\text{for }\,\,\,n\geq Cd^{(d+3)/2}\ln d,

where
c_{0},C>0
are absolute constants.

Note that, asymptotically as
n\to\infty
, the constant
c_{0}
in Theorem
1.9
can be chosen as
\frac{1}{e\sqrt{e}}

by Theorem
1.3
.

Several upper bounds, including the ones in Propositions

,
1.6
and Theorem

(all derived from
[

), rely on semi-random constructions of identically and independently distributed (i.i.d.)
random points (with respect to the probability measure

) with well-separated points to fill the gaps left by the random points.
Such a technique goes back at least to Rogers
[

]
, who used it for the upper bound of (

)
and for the spherical covering density in
[

]
. The book
[

, Chapter 5]
contains a presentation
of this technique for the case of covering the sphere by spherical caps. It was also recently used in
[

]
for the minimal dispersion
on the cube. Note that Naszódi

used non-probabilistic tools to reprove the bound from

]
mentioned before

.

If the number of points

is large compared to the dimension

,
then covering of
\mathbb{S}^{d}
relying only on i.i.d. random centers may introduce an additional logarithmic factor in

.
This is because the largest gap between i.i.d. random points is logarithmically larger than the average gap, see
[

,

,

]
for details
(note that in the case

it is an easy fact).
This effect is related to the coupon collector’s problem (see

]
for dispersion on the cube).

Remark 1.10
.

In a recent work, Hoehner and Kur
[
41
]
have shown that covering most of the sphere using i.i.d. random points is
asymptotically optimal in high dimension. More precisely, the maximum proportion of the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}
covered by
n
spherical
caps of volume
\frac{1}{n}
tends to

as

and

tend to infinity.

Such a choice of points was used in recent works on dispersion of the cube
[

,

,

]
and for spherical lens dispersion
[

]
.
Moreover, the above mentioned bounds in

]

]
,

Proposition 1.11
.

Let

. Then

n\cdot\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(n,d)\leq\frac{3(d+2)}{\ln 2}\ln\Big(\frac{2en}{d+2}\Big)\qquad\text{for }\,\,\,n\geq d+2.

Moreover, the bound holds for independent uniformly distributed points on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
.

In fact, it can be deduced from the proof that the bound holds with probability

,

, for i.i.d. random points if the factor

is replaced by a large enough constant
depending only on

. Although the proof of the combinatorial
\varepsilon
-net theorem is not very long
(for the sake of completeness, we present it in Section

\varepsilon
-net argument, which however yields a slightly worse bound in the regime

than Proposition
1.11
.

Proposition 1.12
.

Let
d,n\geq 2
. Then

n\cdot\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(n,d)\leq 12d\ln n.

Moreover, the bound holds for independent uniformly distributed points on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
.

Proposition 1.13
.

Let

We finally would like to note that the bound (

) and Proposition

Theorem 1.14
.

Let

Let

Then

n\cdot\disp_{\mathcal{L}_{k}}(n,d)\leq Cd\,k\ln k\,\ln\Big(\frac{n}{cd\,k\ln k}\Big)\qquad\emph{for}\,\,\,n\geq C\,d\,k\ln k,

where

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Volume bounds

The normalized volume of a spherical cap of geodesic radius
\varphi\in[0,\pi]
can be computed by

V(\varphi)=\frac{\vol_{d-1}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}{\vol_{d}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\int_{0}^{\varphi}\sin^{d-1}t\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{\int_{0}^{\varphi}\sin^{d-1}t\,\mathrm{d}t}{2\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\sin^{d-1}t\,\mathrm{d}t},

(2.1)

where the
d
-dimensional volume of
\mathbb{S}^{d}
is given by

\vol_{d}(\mathbb{S}^{d})=\frac{2\pi^{(d+1)/2}}{\Gamma(\frac{d+1}{2})}.

It is known that the Wallis integral
\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\sin^{d-1}t\,\mathrm{d}t
satisfies

\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2d}}\leq\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\sin^{d-1}t\,\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2(d-1)}}\qquad\text{for}\,\,\,d\geq 2,

(2.2)

(which follows from
[
7
, Lemma 1]
). We need the following volume bounds.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
d\geq 2
.

(i)

If
\varphi\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
, then

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(d+1)}}\sin^{d}\varphi\leq V(\varphi)\leq\frac{1}{2}\sin^{d}\varphi.

(ii)

If
\varphi\leq\arccos\frac{1}{\sqrt{d+1}}
, then

\frac{1}{3\sqrt{2\pi(d+1)}}\cdot\frac{1}{\cos\varphi}\sin^{d}\varphi\leq V(\varphi)\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi d}}\cdot\frac{1}{\cos\varphi}\sin^{d}\varphi.

(iii)

If
\varphi\geq\arccos\frac{1}{\sqrt{d+1}}
, then

\frac{1}{3e\sqrt{2\pi}}\leq V(\varphi)\leq\frac{1}{2}.

(iv)

If
\alpha\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
, then

e^{-\frac{(d-1)\alpha^{2}}{2\cos^{2}\alpha}}\sqrt{\frac{d-1}{2\pi}}\,\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}-V\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}-\alpha\Big)\leq\sqrt{\frac{d}{2\pi}}\,\alpha.

(v)

If
0<\delta\leq w\leq\pi
, then

\frac{V(w)}{V(\delta)}\leq\Big(\frac{w}{\delta}\Big)^{d}.

Proof.

The upper bound in (i) is taken from
[
3
, Proposition 5.1]
. The lower bound in (i) and
bounds in (ii) and (iii) are taken from
[
12
, Corollary 3.2]
.

To prove (iv) write

\frac{1}{2}-V\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}-\alpha\Big)=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}-\alpha}\sin^{d-1}t\,\,\mathrm{d}t}{2\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\sin^{d-1}t\,\,\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{\int_{\frac{\pi}{2}-\alpha}^{\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha}\sin^{d-1}t\,\,\mathrm{d}t}{4\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\sin^{d-1}t\,\,\mathrm{d}t}.

(2.3)

The numerator in (
2.3
) can be written as

\int_{\frac{\pi}{2}-\alpha}^{\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha}\sin^{d-1}t\,\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{-\alpha}^{\alpha}\cos^{d-1}t\,\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{-\alpha}^{\alpha}e^{(d-1)\,f(t)}\,\mathrm{d}t,

where
f(t)=\ln\cos t
on
(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2})

satisfies
f(0)=0
,
f^{\prime}(0)=0
and
f^{\prime\prime}(t)=-\frac{1}{\cos^{2}t}
. Consequently,

-\frac{1}{\cos^{2}\alpha}\frac{t^{2}}{2}\leq f(t)\leq-\frac{t^{2}}{2}\qquad\text{for }t\in(-\alpha,\alpha).

We bound the numerator in (
2.3
) from above by

\int_{-\alpha}^{\alpha}e^{(d-1)\,f(t)}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{-\alpha}^{\alpha}e^{-(d-1)\frac{t^{2}}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq 2\alpha,

and from below by

\int_{-\alpha}^{\alpha}e^{(d-1)\,f(t)}\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\int_{-\alpha}^{\alpha}e^{-\frac{d-1}{\cos^{2}\alpha}\frac{t^{2}}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\geq 2\alpha e^{-\frac{(d-1)\alpha^{2}}{2\cos^{2}\alpha}}.

Combined with (
2.2
) this completes the proof of (iv).

The bound in (v) follows from the Bishop-Gromov volume
comparison theorem (see e.g.
[
19
, Theorem III.4.5]
). Note that
it is essentially proven using elementary tools in
[
12
, Lemma 3.1 (ii)]
for
0<\delta\leq w\leq\pi/2
.
For convenience of the reader we extend the proof to
0<\delta\leq w\leq\pi
.
We will use the following fact.

Claim 2.2
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
0<\delta\leq\pi
. Then for every
1\leq t\leq\frac{\pi}{\delta}
,

(i)

\sin(t\delta)\leq t\sin(\delta),

(ii)

\delta\sin^{d-1}\delta\leq d\int_{0}^{\delta}\sin^{d-1}s\,\mathrm{d}s.

Proof.

Note that the function
\frac{\sin x}{x}
is decreasing on
(0,\pi]
. Since
1\leq t\leq\frac{\pi}{\delta}
,
this implies that

\displaystyle\frac{\sin(t\delta)}{t\sin\delta}=\frac{\sin(t\delta)}{t\delta}\cdot\frac{\delta}{\sin\delta}\leq 1,

which proves (i).

To prove (ii) consider the function
f(\delta)=d\int_{0}^{\delta}\sin^{d-1}s\,\mathrm{d}s-\delta\sin^{d-1}\delta
on
(0,\pi]
.
Then
f(0)=0
and

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(\delta)=d\sin^{d-1}\delta-\sin^{d-1}\delta-(d-1)\delta\sin^{d-2}\delta\cos\delta=(d-1)\sin^{d-2}\delta(\sin\delta-\delta\cos\delta).

Since
\sin\delta-\delta\cos\delta>0
on
(0,\pi]
, we observe that
f^{\prime}(\delta)>0
. This proves that
f
is non-negative
on
(0,\pi]
and thus proves (ii).
∎

We continue to prove (v). Setting
t=w/\delta
we have to show that
V(t\delta)\leq t^{d}V(\delta).

By (
2.1
) it suffices to show that for every
1\leq t\leq\frac{\pi}{\delta}
,

\displaystyle F(t):=t^{d}\int_{0}^{\delta}\sin^{d-1}s\,\mathrm{d}s-\int_{0}^{t\delta}\sin^{d-1}s\,\mathrm{d}s\geq 0.

Using (i) and (ii) of Claim
2.2
we observe that for every
1\leq t\leq\frac{\pi}{\delta}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\int_{0}^{t\delta}\sin^{d-1}s\,\mathrm{d}s\right)

\displaystyle=\delta\sin^{d-1}(t\delta)\leq t^{d-1}\delta\sin^{d-1}\delta

\displaystyle\leq t^{d-1}d\int_{0}^{\delta}\sin^{d-1}s\,\mathrm{d}s=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(t^{d}\int_{0}^{\delta}\sin^{d-1}s\,\mathrm{d}s\right).

This shows that
F^{\prime}\geq 0
and implies the desired result as
F(1)=0
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

The asymptotic relation (
1.5
) for
\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(d+2,d)
is an immediate consequence of Lemma
2.1
(iv) and
\arccos x=\frac{\pi}{2}-x-O(x^{3})
as
x\to 0
.

If
\frac{\pi}{2}-\varphi
is of order
\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}
, then the following lemma gives the asymptotic constant for
V(\varphi)
as
d\to\infty
.

Lemma 2.4
.

For every
\alpha\geq 0
,

V\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{d}}\Big)\sim V\Big(\arccos\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{d+1}}\Big)\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\alpha}^{\infty}e^{-x^{2}/2}\,\mathrm{d}x\qquad\text{as }\,\,d\to\infty.

Proof.

The first “
\sim
” follows from
\arccos x=\frac{\pi}{2}-x-O(x^{3})
as
x\to 0
. For the second “
\sim
” we interpret
V(\arccos\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{d+1}})
as the probability that a uniformly distributed vector on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
is contained in a cap of radius
\arccos\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{d+1}}
which, without loss of generality, is centered at
e_{1}=(1,0,\dots,0)
. Let
g=(g_{1},\dots,g_{d+1})
be a standard Gaussian vector in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. Then
\frac{g}{\|g\|_{2}}
is uniformly distributed on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
and

\displaystyle\sigma\Big(\Big\{y\in\mathbb{S}^{d}\colon\arccos y_{1}\leq\arccos\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{d+1}}\Big\}\Big)=\mathbb{P}\Big[\frac{g_{1}}{\sqrt{S_{d+1}}}\geq\alpha\Big],

where
S_{d+1}:=\frac{1}{d+1}\sum_{i=2}^{d+1}g_{i}^{2}
. By the law of large numbers
S_{d+1}\to 1
in probability and by the continuous mapping theorem the same holds for
\sqrt{S_{d+1}}
. Then Slutsky’s theorem implies that

\mathbb{P}\Big[\frac{g_{1}}{\sqrt{S_{d+1}}}\geq\alpha\Big]\to\mathbb{P}[g_{1}\geq\alpha]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\alpha}^{\infty}e^{-x^{2}/2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

This shows the claimed asymptotic for
V(\arccos\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{d+1}})
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

The asymptotic relation (
1.6
) for
\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(2d+2,d)
directly follows from Lemma
2.4
.

We finally provide a standard estimate on the cardinality of geodesic
\varepsilon
-nets on the sphere.
Recall that a set of points
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathbb{S}^{d}
forms a geodesic
\varepsilon
-net if for every
x\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
there is

y\in\mathcal{N}
such that
\varrho(x,y)\leq\varepsilon
, or in other words, the geodesic covering radius defined in the next subsection satisfies
\varphi(\mathcal{N})\leq\varepsilon
. Note that for every
x,y\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
one has
|x-y|=2\sin(\varrho(x,y)/2)
. The existence of Euclidean or geodesic
\varepsilon
-nets on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}
of cardinality
(C/\varepsilon)^{d}
for some absolute constant
C
is well-known and follows from the volume argument. We provide a proof with explicit constants for completeness.

Lemma 2.6
.

For every
d\geq 1
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\pi)
there is a geodesic
\varepsilon
-net
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathbb{S}^{d}
of cardinality
at most
2(\pi/\varepsilon)^{d}
.

Proof.

For
d=1
this is straightforward, so let
d\geq 2
. If
\varepsilon\geq\frac{\pi}{2}
, then choose
\mathcal{N}=\{x,-x\}
for some
x\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
.

For
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
we use the standard volume argument. Let
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathbb{S}^{d}
be a maximal set with
\varrho(x,y)>\varepsilon
for
x,y\in\mathcal{N}
. Then
\mathcal{N}
is an
\varepsilon
-net. Since the interiors of balls
B(x,\frac{\varepsilon}{2})
,
x\in\mathcal{N}
, have to be pairwise disjoint, we have

1=\sigma(\mathbb{S}^{d})\geq\sum_{x\in\mathcal{N}}\sigma(B(x,\varepsilon/2))=|\mathcal{N}|\,V(\varepsilon/2).

(2.4)

If
d=2
, then
V(\varepsilon/2)=\frac{1}{2}(1-\cos\frac{\varepsilon}{2})\geq\frac{1}{2}(\frac{\varepsilon}{\pi})^{2}
, which proves the claim in this case. If
d\geq 3
, we use Lemma
2.1
(i) and
\sin\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}\varepsilon}{\pi}
,
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
, to obtain that

V(\varepsilon/2)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(d+1)}}\sin^{d}(\varepsilon/2)\geq\sqrt{\frac{2^{d-1}}{\pi(d+1)}}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{\pi}\Big)^{d}\geq\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{\pi}\Big)^{d}.

Combining this with (
2.4
) completes the proof.
∎

2.2
Dispersion and covering (proof of Lemma
1.1
)

Here we prove Lemma
1.1
, which provides relations
between minimal dispersion, density, and covering radius, all of which quantify
the maximum efficiency of placing points on the sphere.
Recall that by definition, given a point set
P=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{S}^{d}
, its spherical cap dispersion is

\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(P)=\sup_{C\in\mathcal{C}}\{\sigma(C)\colon C\cap P=\emptyset\}.

With a slight abuse of notations we denote the geodesic covering radius by

\varphi(P)=\inf\Big\{\varphi>0\colon\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}B(x_{i},\varphi)=\mathbb{S}^{d}\Big\}=\sup\{\varphi>0\colon\exists x\in\mathbb{S}^{d}\text{ with }B(x,\varphi)\cap P=\emptyset\}.

Thus, the geodesic covering radius of
P
equals the geodesic radius of the largest (open)
ball which does not intersect
P
. Consequently,

\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(P)=\sup\big\{V(\varphi)\colon B(x,\varphi)\cap P=\emptyset,x\in\mathbb{S}^{d},\varphi\in(0,\pi)\big\}=V(\varphi(P)).

(2.5)

Taking the infimum over all
n
-point sets
P_{n}\subset\mathbb{S}^{d}
on both sides of this equality and using the continuity of
the function
V(\cdot)
we observe that

\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(n,d)=\inf_{P_{n}\subset\mathbb{S}^{d}}\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(P_{n})=V\big(\inf_{P_{n}\subset\mathbb{S}^{d}}\varphi(P_{n})\big)=V(\varphi(n)),

(2.6)

which proves one part of (
1.3
).

For the relation to minimal density, note that definitions of
\dens(n,d)
and
\varphi(n)

immediately imply that for every
\varphi>\varphi(n)
one has
\dens(n,d)\leq nV(\varphi)
and
for every
\varphi<\varphi(n)
one has
\dens(n,d)\geq nV(\varphi)
. These two facts together with
the continuity of
V(\cdot)
yield
\dens(n,d)=nV(\varphi(n))
, which completes the proof.
∎

2.3
Dispersion and convex body approximation

In this section we present a useful connection between minimal dispersion and best approximation of convex bodies by polytopes. It is well-known that covering
\mathbb{S}^{d}
by geodesic balls of equal radius is related to approximating the
(d+1)
-dimensional Euclidean unit ball
B^{d+1}
by the convex hull
\conv(P)
of the set of centers
P
.
Indeed, it is not difficult to see that the largest ball centered at the origin and contained
in
\conv(P)
has radius
\cos\varphi(P)
, where
\varphi(P)
is the geodesic covering radius of
P
defined above
(see e.g.
[
12
, Lemma 7.1]
). This implies

\delta_{H}(\conv(P),B^{d+1})=1-\cos\varphi(P),

(2.7)

where the Hausdorff distance between convex sets
K,L\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
is given by

\delta_{H}(K,L)=\max\Big\{\sup_{x\in K}\inf_{y\in L}\|x-y\|,\,\,\sup_{x\in L}\inf_{y\in K}\|x-y\|\Big\}.

From (
2.5
) and (
2.7
) we obtain that, for any
n
-point set
P_{n}\subset\mathbb{S}^{d}
,

\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(P_{n})=V(\varphi(P_{n}))=f(\delta_{H}(\conv(P_{n}),B^{d+1})),

with
f(x)=V(\arccos(1-x))
for
x\in(0,\pi)
. Taking the infimum over all
n
-point sets
P_{n}\subset\mathbb{S}^{d}
on both sides and using continuity of
f
we deduce

\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(n,d)=f(\delta_{H}(n,d)),

(2.8)

where
\delta_{H}(n,d)
denotes the error of best approximation of
B^{d+1}
(in the Hausdorff distance) by polytopes with at most
n
vertices on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
.

The relation (
2.8
) allows to transfer bounds for
\delta_{H}(n,d)
to minimal dispersion, and vice versa. This will be used below for the proof of Theorem
1.3
. The survey
[
14
]
contains many references on the behavior of
\delta_{H}(n,d)
. Equivalently, one can study the difference
1-\delta_{H}(n,d)=\cos\varphi(n)
which is the maximal inradius of a polytope with
n
vertices inscribed into
\mathbb{S}^{d}

(see e.g. Lemma 7.1 in
[
12
]
) and thus related to Banach-Mazur distance between the Euclidean ball and the convex hull
n
points
on the sphere (see discussion in Section 8 of
[
12
]
).
In
[
12
, inequality (14)]
lower bounds for
\cos\varphi(n)
are given for
d+1\leq n\leq\sqrt{2}^{d}
.
We will need the following upper bound, which follows from a result on volumes of polytopes with
n\geq 2(d+1)
vertices on the sphere,
proved independently in
[
4
,
18
,
32
]
and a result on the Banach-Mazur distance between the Euclidean ball and
a polytope with
d+2\leq n\leq 2(d+1)
vertices, proved in
[
61
]
. We formulate it in terms of
\varphi(n)

(cf. similar discussion in Section 8 of
[
12
]
). It is known that this bound is sharp.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
n\geq d+2
. Then

\cos\varphi(n)\leq C\sqrt{\frac{\ln(n/d)}{d}},

where
C>0
is an absolute constant.

In the following, we provide the proofs of all Theorems and Propositions in order of appearance with four exceptions. Proposition
1.11
and Theorem
1.14
are proved in Section
5
, while Propositions
1.12

are proved in Section

.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Recall that
\delta_{H}(n,d)
denotes the error of best approximation of
B^{d+1}

by polytopes with at most
n
vertices on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
and that by (
2.8
),

\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(n,d)=V(\arccos(1-\delta_{H}(n,d))).

On the other hand it is well known that

\delta_{H}(n,d)=\frac{1+o(1)}{2}\Big(\frac{\vol_{d}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}{\vol_{d}(B^{d})}\vartheta_{d}\Big)^{2/d}n^{-2/d}\qquad\text{as }n\to\infty,

(3.1)

where
\vartheta_{d}
is the minimal covering density given in (
1.7
) (see e.g. surveys
[
36
, eq. (4) and (5)]
and

[
14
, eq. (7)]
and references therein, note that this result goes back to
[
59

Combining the asymptotics
\arccos(1-x)=\sqrt{2x}+O(x^{3/2})
as
x\to 0
and

V(\varphi)=\frac{d\vol_{d}(B^{d})}{\vol_{d}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\int_{0}^{\varphi}\sin^{d-1}t\,\mathrm{d}t=(1+o(1))\frac{\vol_{d}(B^{d})}{\vol_{d}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\varphi^{d}\qquad\text{as }\varphi\to 0

with (

Remark 3.1
.

Note that in
[
12
, eq. (13)]
a slightly less precise asymptotic for
\delta_{H}(n,d)

than the one in (
3.1
) is derived from the covering bound (
1.10
).

In the next three proofs we will use the following lemma. Recall that
V(\varphi)
denotes the normalized Lebesgue measure of a cap of geodesic radius
\varphi
and, given a cover
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{1},B_{2},...,B_{m}\}
of the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}
the density of this cover is
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sigma(B_{i})
. When the cover
\mathcal{B}
contains only spherical caps of the same radius
\varphi
, we say that
\mathcal{B}
is a
\varphi
-cover and denote its density by
\dens(\varphi,\mathcal{B})
. Clearly,
\dens(\varphi,\mathcal{B})=mV(\varphi)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
\varphi_{0}\in(0,\pi/2]
, and let
D:(0,\varphi_{0}]\to[1,\infty)
be a continuous decreasing function. Assume that for every
\varphi\in(0,\varphi_{0}]
there exists a
\varphi
-cover
\mathcal{B}
of the sphere with density
\dens(\varphi,\mathcal{B})\leq D(\varphi)
.
Then for every
n\geq D(\varphi_{0})/V(\varphi_{0})
one has
n\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(n,d)\leq D(\varphi(n)).

Proof.

Let
n\geq D(\varphi_{0})/V(\varphi_{0})
. Since
1/V
and
D
are continuous decreasing functions with
D\geq 1
and

V(\varphi)\to 0
as
\varphi\to 0
, there exists a

\psi_{n}\in(0,\varphi_{0}]
such that
n=D(\psi_{n})/V(\psi_{n})
. By the definition of
\varphi
-cover and by the
assumptions, there exists
\psi_{n}
-cover
\mathcal{B}
of the sphere by
m
spherical caps of radius
\psi_{n}
with the density

mV(\psi_{n})=\dens(\psi_{n},\mathcal{B})\leq D(\psi_{n})=nV(\psi_{n}).

Thus,
m\leq n
so we also have a cover of the sphere by
n
spherical caps of radius
\psi_{n}
(just by adding caps
if needed). This implies that
\varphi(n)\leq\psi_{n}
. Therefore, using Lemma
1.1
, we obtain

n\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(n,d)=nV(\varphi(n))\leq nV(\psi_{n})=D(\psi_{n})\leq D(\varphi(n)),

which completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition
1.5
.

By
[
12

, for every

,

Proof of Theorem

.

Let

.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.8
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\pi/2)
and let
n
be the minimal cardinality of a geodesic
\varepsilon
-net.
Note that
V(\varepsilon)\leq 1/2
. If
n\leq 2d
we are done, so we assume
m:=n-1\geq 2d
.
By the definition of the minimal covering radius we have

V(\varphi(n))\leq V(\varepsilon)\leq V(\varphi(m))
, therefore Theorem
1.7
and Lemma
1.1

yield

\frac{m}{d}=\frac{m\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(m,d)}{d\,V(\varphi(m))}\leq\frac{C\ln\ln(2m/d)}{V(\varphi(m))}\leq\frac{C\ln\ln(2m/d)}{V(\varepsilon)}.

This implies

\frac{m}{d}\leq C_{1}\frac{\ln\ln(2/V(\varepsilon))}{V(\varepsilon)}

for a large enough absolute constant
C_{1}>0
. Since
n\leq 2m
, the desired bound follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

The following proof is based on
[
12
, Example 6.3]
where the lower bound
by Coxeter-Few-Rogers
[
21
]
(the left hand side of (
1.8
))
is adapted to the sphere. More precisely, in
[
12
, Example 6.3]
it was shown that
for any finite covering
\mathcal{B}
of

by caps of equal geodesic radius

the density satisfies

with an absolute constant

(although not explicitly stated in

]

implies that

n\cdot V(\varphi(n))=n\cdot\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(n,d)=\dens(n,d)\geq c\,d,

(3.2)

whenever

4
Dispersion bounds via geodesic
\varepsilon
-nets

In this section we provide the proofs of Propositions
1.12
and
1.13
. For convenience, we mainly use
|\cdot|
instead of
\sigma(\cdot)
for the normalized spherical volume and the inverse of the minimal spherical dispersion which is given for
\gamma\in(0,1)
by

N_{\mathcal{M}}(\gamma,d):=\min\{n\geq 1\colon\disp_{\mathcal{M}}(n,d)\leq\gamma\},

where
\mathcal{M}
is either
\mathcal{C}
or
\mathcal{L}
, so we use either
N_{\mathcal{C}}
or
N_{\mathcal{L}}

(but in fact it can be any other family of measurable subsets of
\mathbb{S}^{d}
).

Similarly, for each
\gamma\in(0,1)
consider

\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}:=\{A\in\mathcal{M}:|A|=\gamma\},

where
\mathcal{M}
is either
\mathcal{C}
or
\mathcal{L}
, so we use either
\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}
or
\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}
.
As in
[
2
,
45
]
we call a finite family
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}
a
(c_{0}\gamma)
-approximation for
\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}
with
c_{0}\in(0,1)
, if for every
A\in\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}
there exists
B\in\mathcal{N}
such that
B\subset A
and
|B|\geq c_{0}\gamma
. Repeating the proof of

[
45
, Lemma 2.3]
(see also
[
57
, Theorem 1]
) in our setting we obtain the following.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
d\geq 2
and let
\mathcal{M}
be either
\mathcal{C}
or
\mathcal{L}
. Let
c_{0}\in(0,1)
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
. If
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}
is a
(c_{0}\gamma)
-approximation for
\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}
and if
|\mathcal{N}|\geq 3
, then

N_{\mathcal{M}}(\gamma,d)\leq\frac{3\ln|\mathcal{N}|}{c_{0}\gamma}.

Moreover, the result holds for the random choice of points.

It is worth mentioning that recently Arman and Litvak in
[
2
, Lemma 3.3]
proved a variation of the above result,
which again transfers to our setting.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
d\geq 2
and let
\mathcal{M}
be either
\mathcal{C}
or
\mathcal{L}
.
Let
c_{0}\in(0,1)
and
\gamma\in(0,\frac{1}{3c_{0}})
. If
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}
is a
(c_{0}\gamma)
-approximation for
\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}
and if
|\mathcal{N}|\geq\frac{e}{c_{0}\gamma}
, then

N_{\mathcal{M}}(\gamma,d)\leq\frac{\ln(4c_{0}\gamma|\mathcal{N}|)}{c_{0}\gamma}.

We would like to emphasize that the proof of Lemma
4.1
is based on a random choice of points,
while the proof of Lemma
4.2
introduces an additional non-random step.

In order to estimate
N_{\mathcal{M}}(\gamma,d)
from above for some
\gamma\in(0,1)
, one can construct a

(c_{0}\gamma)
-approximation with small cardinality and
c_{0}=\frac{1}{2}
, say. We provide a quick summary
of a construction of such an approximation
\mathcal{N}
for
\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}
. Since every cap

C(v,\delta)
from
\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}
is uniquely determined by its center
v
, we start with an

\varepsilon
-net
\mathcal{N}_{0}
for
\mathbb{S}^{d}
and consider
\mathcal{N}
to be the family of caps centered at
points from
\mathcal{N}_{0}
with the radius
\delta-\varepsilon
. This guarantees that every
cap from
\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}
contains a member of
\mathcal{N}
. Moreover, by choosing
\varepsilon

sufficiently small we ensure that their volumes are comparable.

As a tool, we need the following lemma which will be applied first for the family
\mathcal{C}
and later on for
\mathcal{L}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
\gamma\in(0,1)
and
A=B(v_{1},\delta_{1})\cap B(v_{2},\delta_{2})
with some
v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
.
Let
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\in(0,\pi]
be such that
|A|=\gamma
. Then
\delta=\min\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\}\geq\gamma
.

Proof.

Assuming
\delta\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
and using the bound in Lemma
2.1
(i) we get

\gamma=|A|\leq V(\delta)\leq\frac{\sin^{d}\delta}{2}\leq\frac{\delta^{d}}{2}.

Therefore,

\delta\geq\gamma^{1/d}\geq\gamma.

If
\delta>\frac{\pi}{2}
, then
\delta>1\geq\gamma
, which completes the proof.
∎

To prove Proposition
1.12
we construct a
\frac{\gamma}{2}
-approximation for the family
\mathcal{C}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
d\geq 2
. For every
\gamma\in(0,1)
there exists a
\frac{\gamma}{2}
-approximation

\mathcal{N}
for
\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}
with cardinality

|\mathcal{N}|\leq 2\Big(\frac{3d\pi}{\gamma^{2}}\Big)^{d}.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in(0,1)
and choose
\delta\in(0,\pi)
such that
V(\delta)=\gamma
. Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\delta)
and by Lemma
2.6
choose an
\varepsilon
-net
\mathcal{N}_{0}\subset\mathbb{S}^{d}
of cardinality at most
2(\frac{\pi}{\varepsilon})^{d}
. Then, for every
B(v,\delta)\in\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}
we find
w\in\mathcal{N}_{0}
such that
\varrho(v,w)\leq\varepsilon
and thus
B(w,\delta-\varepsilon)\subset B(v,\delta)
. Let

\mathcal{N}=\{B(w,\delta-\varepsilon)\colon w\in\mathcal{N}_{0}\}.

Then Lemma
2.1
(v) implies that

\frac{|B(v,\delta)|}{|B(w,\delta-\varepsilon)|}\leq\Big(\frac{\delta}{\delta-\varepsilon}\Big)^{d}.

Choosing
\varepsilon=\frac{\gamma\delta}{3d}
, we observe that

\frac{|B(v,\delta)|}{|B(w,\delta-\varepsilon)|}\leq\Big(\frac{1}{1-\frac{\gamma}{3d}}\Big)^{d}=\Big(1+\frac{\gamma}{3d(1-\frac{\gamma}{3d})}\Big)^{d}\leq\Big(1+\frac{\gamma}{2d}\Big)^{d}\leq e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}.

This implies

|B(w,\delta-\varepsilon)|\geq\gamma e^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\geq\frac{\gamma}{2}.

Thus the family
\mathcal{N}
is a
\frac{\gamma}{2}
-approximation for
\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}
of cardinality at most

|\mathcal{N}_{0}|\leq 2\Big(\frac{\pi}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{d}=2\Big(\frac{3d\pi}{\delta\gamma}\Big)^{d}\leq 2\Big(\frac{3d\pi}{\gamma^{2}}\Big)^{d},

where we used that
\delta\geq\gamma
by Lemma
4.3
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
1.12
.

Set
\gamma=\frac{12d}{n}\ln n
. If
\gamma\geq 1
, then
\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(n,d)\leq\gamma
trivially holds
and we are done. Assume
\gamma<1
. Then, using Lemmas
4.1
and
4.4
, we have

N_{\mathcal{C}}(\gamma,d)\leq\frac{6\ln|\mathcal{N}|}{\gamma}\leq\frac{6\ln\Big(2\big(\frac{3\pi d}{\gamma^{2}}\big)^{d}\Big)}{\gamma}\\
\leq\frac{6d\ln\Big(\frac{5\pi d}{\gamma^{2}}\Big)}{\gamma}\leq\frac{n}{2}\cdot\frac{\ln(\frac{n^{2}}{d})}{\ln n}\leq n.

This implies
\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(n,d)\leq\gamma=\frac{12d}{n}\ln n
as required.
∎

Next we construct an approximation for
\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}
.
The construction that will be used is a natural extension of the one we used for caps in Lemma
4.4
, with the only difference
that now one has to control not only centers of caps, but also their radii. First we observe that by Lemma
4.3

caps forming an intersection of volume
\gamma
have radii from the interval
(\gamma,\pi]
. We discretize this interval to approximate radii.
Then we construct a finite family of intersections
\mathcal{N}
using the centers from a given
\varepsilon
-net
and radii from the first step which ensures that every lens of volume
\gamma
contains a member of the family
\mathcal{N}
.
In the final step by choosing
\varepsilon
in an appropriate way we ensure that the volume loss is sufficiently small.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
d\geq 2
.
For every
\gamma\in(0,1)
there exists a
\frac{\gamma}{2}
-approximation
\mathcal{N}
for
\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}
with cardinality

|\mathcal{N}|\leq 9\Big(\frac{12d\pi}{\gamma^{2}}\Big)^{2(d+1)}.

Proof.

Fix
\gamma\in(0,1)
. Let
\varepsilon=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{12d}
.
By Lemma
2.6
there exists an
\varepsilon
-net
\mathcal{N}_{0}\subset\mathbb{S}^{d}
of cardinality at most
2\big(\frac{\pi}{\varepsilon}\big)^{d}
.
By Lemma
4.3
, we have
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\geq\gamma
and we cover the interval
(\gamma,\pi]
by sufficiently small subintervals
in the following way:

(\gamma,\pi]\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\Big(\pi-i\varepsilon,\hskip 5.69046pt\pi-(i-1)\varepsilon\Big],

where
k\in\mathbb{N}
is the smallest integer such that
\pi-k\varepsilon\leq\gamma
, i.e.,

k=\Big\lceil\frac{\pi-\gamma}{\varepsilon}\Big\rceil\leq\frac{\pi}{\varepsilon}+1.

Consider now the family

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}=\{B(w_{1},\pi-i\varepsilon)\cap B(w_{2},\pi-j\varepsilon):w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathcal{N}_{0},i,j=1,2,...,k+1\}.

We show that
\mathcal{N}
is
\frac{\gamma}{2}
-approximation for
\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}
of required cardinality.
We first estimate the cardinality. Since
\varepsilon\leq\frac{\pi}{4}
,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{N}_{0}|\cdot(k+1)\leq 2\left(\frac{\pi}{\varepsilon}\right)^{d}\cdot\left(\frac{\pi}{\varepsilon}+2\right)\leq 3\left(\frac{\pi}{\varepsilon}\right)^{d+1}.

Therefore,

|\mathcal{N}|=|\mathcal{N}_{0}\times\mathcal{N}_{0}|\cdot(k+1)^{2}\leq 9\left(\frac{\pi}{\varepsilon}\right)^{2(d+1)}=9\left(\frac{12\pi d}{\gamma^{2}}\right)^{2(d+1)}.

Next we prove that
\mathcal{N}
is
\frac{\gamma}{2}
-approximation.
Let
A=B(v_{1},\delta_{1})\cap B(v_{2},\delta_{2})\in\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}
.
Then
i=1,2
there exists
\ell_{i}\leq k
such that

\delta_{i}\in\Big(\pi-\ell_{i}\varepsilon,\pi-(\ell_{i}-1)\varepsilon\Big]

and there exist
w_{i}\in\mathcal{N}_{0}
such that

B(w_{i},\pi-(\ell_{i}+1)\varepsilon)\subset B(v_{i},\pi-\ell_{i}\varepsilon)\subset B(v_{i},\delta_{i}).

Since
\delta_{i}\geq\gamma
by Lemma
4.3
, we have

\pi-(\ell_{i}+1)\varepsilon\geq\delta_{i}-2\varepsilon=\delta_{i}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{6d}\geq\delta_{i}\Big(1-\frac{\gamma}{6d}\Big)>0

(4.1)

for
i=1,2
. Note that

B:=B(w_{1},\pi-(\ell_{1}+1)\varepsilon)\cap B(w_{2},\pi-(\ell_{2}+1)\varepsilon)\in\mathcal{N}

and
B\subset A
. Therefore,

\displaystyle|B|\geq|A|-|B(v_{1},\delta_{1})\setminus B(w_{1},\pi-(\ell_{1}+1)\varepsilon)|-|B(v_{2},\delta_{2})\setminus B(w_{2},\pi-(\ell_{2}+1)\varepsilon)|.

(4.2)

Using Lemma
2.1
(v) and (
4.1
) we observe

\displaystyle\frac{|B(v_{i},\delta_{i})|}{|B(w_{i},\pi-(\ell_{i}+1)\varepsilon)|}\leq\Big(\frac{1}{1-\frac{\gamma}{6d}}\Big)^{d}=\Big(1+\frac{\gamma}{d(6-\frac{\gamma}{d})}\Big)^{d}\leq\Big(1+\frac{\gamma}{5d}\Big)^{d}\leq e^{\gamma/5}.

(4.3)

Since

|B(v_{i},\delta_{i})\setminus B(w_{i},\pi-(\ell_{i}+1)\varepsilon)|=|B(v_{i},\delta_{i})|-|B(w_{i},\pi-(\ell_{i}+1)\varepsilon)|,

and since
|B(w_{i},\pi-(\ell_{i}+1)\varepsilon)|\leq 1
, formula (
4.3
) implies

\displaystyle|B(v_{i},\delta_{i})\setminus B(w_{i},\pi-\ell_{i}\varepsilon)|\leq e^{\gamma/5}-1.

Combining the above estimates with (
4.2
) and using
|A|=\gamma
, and
e^{\gamma/5}\leq 1+\frac{\gamma}{4}
for
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
we obtain

\displaystyle|B|\geq\gamma-2(e^{\gamma/5}-1)\geq\frac{\gamma}{2}.

As
B\in\mathcal{N}
and
B\subset A
, this completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition
1.13
.

We proceed as in the proof of Proposition
1.12
. Set

\gamma=\frac{24(d+1)}{n}\ln n
. If
\gamma\geq 1
, then clearly
\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(n,d)\leq 1\leq\gamma
.
Assume
\gamma<1
.
By Lemma

, there exists

\frac{\gamma}{2}
-approximation
\mathcal{N}
for
\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}

.
Applying this with Lemma

N_{\mathcal{L}}(\gamma,d)\leq\frac{6\ln\Big(9\big(\frac{12\pi d}{\gamma^{2}}\big)^{2(d+1)}\Big)}{\gamma}\leq\frac{12(d+1)}{\gamma}\ln\Big(\frac{18\pi d}{\gamma^{2}}\Big)\leq\frac{n}{2}\cdot\frac{\ln(\frac{n^{2}}{d})}{\ln n}\leq n.

(4.4)

This implies the desired bound.
∎

5
Dispersion bounds via
\varepsilon
-traversals

In this section we obtain Proposition
1.11
as a consequence of the combinatorial
\varepsilon
-net theorem.
To phrase it, let
\mathcal{X}
be a nonempty set equipped with a
\sigma
-algebra
\Sigma
. Moreover, let
\mathcal{R}\subset\Sigma

be a universally separable set system of measurable sets, meaning that there exists a countable subsystem
\mathcal{R}_{0}\subset\mathcal{R}

with the following property:
each
R\in\mathcal{R}
can be approximated by a sequence
R_{1},R_{2},\dots
in
\mathcal{R}_{0}
in the sense that

x\in R
if and only if
x\in R_{i}
for all but finitely many indices
i
.
Note that the assumptions are satisfied in the case of
\mathbb{S}^{d}

equipped with its Borel
\sigma
-algebra and for the choice of
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{C}
or
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{L}_{k}
since each cap can be approximated by
caps with rational centers and radii.

A suitable notion of dimension in the general framework was given by Vapnik–-Chervonenkis in
[
65
]
, which is nowadays called the VC-dimension. To introduce it,
first define the shatter function of
\mathcal{R}
by

\Pi_{\mathcal{R}}(k)=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{k}\in\mathcal{X}\\
|P_{k}|=k\end{subarray}}|\{P_{k}\cap R\colon R\in\mathcal{R}\}|,

(5.1)

which returns the maximal number of subsets of a
k
-subset of
\mathcal{X}
which arise as intersections with
R\in\mathcal{R}
. The VC-dimension of
\mathcal{R}
, given by

\vdim(\mathcal{R})=\sup\{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\colon\Pi_{\mathcal{R}}(k)=2^{k}\},

that is, the maximal cardinality of a set such that all its subsets can be realized as intersections with
R\in\mathcal{R}
. The
dispersion
of
P\subset\mathcal{X}
with respect to
\mathcal{R}
and a probability measure
\mu
on
(X,\Sigma)
is

\disp(P,\mathcal{R},\mu)=\sup_{R\in\mathcal{R}\colon R\cap P=\emptyset}\mu(R).

The following probabilistic upper bound on the dispersion is essentially contained in
[
9
, Appendix A]
.
Since other notation, terminology, and concepts are used there, for the reader’s convenience,
we present a proof at the end of this section.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
(X,\Sigma)
. Assume that
d=\vdim(\mathcal{R})
is finite and let
m\geq d
. Suppose that
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}
are distributed independently according to
\mu
. Then for all
\varepsilon>0
one has

\mathbb{P}\Big[\disp(\{X_{1},\dots,X_{m}\},\mathcal{R},\mu)>\varepsilon\Big]\leq 2\Big(\frac{2em}{d}\Big)^{d}2^{-\varepsilon m/2}.

Note that if for a given
m\geq d
we set
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{m}=\frac{3}{\ln 2}\frac{d}{m}\ln(\frac{2em}{d})
, then
the probability in Theorem
5.1
becomes strictly less than 1, which leads to existence of
an
m
-point set on
\mathcal{X}
with dispersion at most
\varepsilon_{m}
. Thus, we only need to have good bounds on
the VC-dimension of the class under consideration. For the class of caps it is given by the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
d\geq 2
.
The class
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{d}
of caps on
\mathbb{S}^{d}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
satisfies
\vdim(\mathcal{C}_{d})=d+2
.

For a short proof of Lemma
5.2
we refer to
[
8
, Prop. 5.12]
(note that there the notation

\mathbb{S}^{n}
is used for
(n-1)
-dimensional sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and that this fact for
d=2
was proved
in
[
1
, Prop. 8]
, where the authors used the definition of VC-dimension which differs by 1
from the standard one).
To obtain an upper bound on the dispersion
in the case of intersections of caps one can use Lemma 3.2.3 in
[
9
, Lemma 3.2.3]
,
which provides an estimate on the VC-dimension (it implies that the class
\mathcal{L}_{k}
, consisting of intersections
of at most
k
spherical caps, has VC-dimension at most
2(d+2)k\log_{2}(3k)
).

Proof of Proposition
1.11
.

Let
X=\mathbb{S}^{d}
(equipped with its Borel
\sigma
-algebra),
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{C}
and
\mu=\sigma
.
Since by Lemma
5.2

\vdim(\mathcal{C}_{d})=d+2
, Theorem
5.1
yields that for every

m\geq d+2
there exists an
m
-point set
P_{m}\subset\mathbb{S}^{d}
with

\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(P_{m})\leq\varepsilon_{m}:=\frac{3}{\ln 2}\,\frac{d+2}{m}\,\ln\Big(\frac{2em}{d+2}\Big).

In particular, this gives
\disp_{\mathcal{C}}(m,d)\leq\varepsilon_{m}
and completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.14
and inequality (
1.11
).

We repeat the proof above (and essentially follow
[
51
]
).
Using that
\vdim(\mathcal{C}_{d})=d+2

.
Therefore Theorem
5.1
implies

\disp_{\mathcal{L}_{k}}(m,d)\leq\frac{6}{\ln 2}\,\frac{(d+2)k\log_{2}(3k)}{m}\,\ln\Big(\frac{em}{(d+2)k\log_{2}(3k)}\Big).

for every

(in the case of
\mathcal{L}
, that is, in the case

).
∎

Remark 5.4
.

The notion of VC-dimension can also be used to bound the size of empirical processes or (spherical cap) discrepancy, see
[
38
,
60
]
.

The remainder of this section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
5.1
, which is essentially taken from
[
9
, Appendix A]
and
[

]
.

A set
P\subset\mathcal{X}
is an
\varepsilon
-traversal
of
\mathcal{R}
if
R\cap P\neq\emptyset
for all

R\in\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}:=\{R\in\mathcal{R}\colon\mu(R)>\varepsilon\}.

This definition is more general than the one of (combinatorial)
\varepsilon
-nets introduced in
[
37
]
.
Theorem
5.1
provides an upper bound on the minimal cardinality of
\varepsilon
-traversals and
thus generalizes the corresponding result for
\varepsilon
-nets in
[
37
]
.

Given
\varepsilon>0
and
m\geq 1
consider the set

Q^{m}_{\varepsilon}=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\in\mathcal{X}^{m}\colon\exists R\in\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}\,\,\mbox{ with }\,\,R\cap\{x_{1},\dots,x_{m}\}=\emptyset\}

of realizations of
\{X_{1},\dots,X_{m}\}
not forming an
\varepsilon
-traversal. Then the probability that
\{X_{1},\dots,X_{m}\}
has dispersion
>\varepsilon
is given by
\mu^{\otimes m}(Q^{m}_{\varepsilon})
, where
\mu^{\otimes m}
denotes the product measure on
X^{m}
. Also define the set

\displaystyle J^{2m}_{\varepsilon}=\Big\{

\displaystyle(\bar{x},\bar{y})=(x_{1},\dots,x_{m},y_{1},\dots,y_{m})\in\mathcal{X}^{2m}\colon

\displaystyle\exists R\in\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}\,\mbox{ such that }\,R\cap\{x_{1},\dots,x_{m}\}=\emptyset\,\mbox{ and }\,|R\cap\{y_{1},\dots,y_{m}\}|\geq\frac{\varepsilon m}{2}\Big\}.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
m\geq 1
be such that
m\varepsilon\geq 2
. Then

\mu^{\otimes 2m}(J^{2m}_{\varepsilon})\leq\mu^{\otimes m}(Q^{m}_{\varepsilon})\leq 2\mu^{\otimes 2m}(J^{2m}_{\varepsilon}).

Proof.

If
\varepsilon\geq 1
, then
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}=\emptyset
and the statement is trivial.
Thus assume
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. The first inequality follows since
J^{2m}_{\varepsilon}
contains a stronger condition
and by Fubini’s theorem.

To prove the second bound, note that by Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle\mu^{\otimes 2m}(J^{2m}_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{X}^{m}}\int_{\mathcal{X}^{m}}\mathbf{1}_{J^{2m}_{\varepsilon}}(\bar{x},\bar{y})\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\otimes m}(\bar{y})\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\otimes m}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle\geq\int_{Q^{m}_{\varepsilon}}\int_{\mathcal{X}^{m}}\mathbf{1}_{J^{2m}_{\varepsilon}}(\bar{x},\bar{y})\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\otimes m}(\bar{y})\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\otimes m}(\bar{x}).

We estimate the inner integral uniformly from below. Fix
\bar{x}\in Q^{m}_{\varepsilon}
.
Then there exists
R\in\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}
such that
R\cap\{x_{1},\dots,x_{m}\}=\emptyset
.
We fix one such
R
and denote it by
R_{\bar{x}}
.
Let
K^{2m}_{\varepsilon}=K^{2m}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})
be the set of
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathcal{X}^{2m}
with
|\{y_{1},\dots,y_{m}\}\cap R_{\bar{x}}|\geq\varepsilon m/2
. Then
K^{2m}_{\varepsilon}\subset J^{2m}_{\varepsilon}
and hence

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{X}^{m}}\mathbf{1}_{J^{2m}_{\varepsilon}}(\bar{x},\bar{y})\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\otimes m}(\bar{y})

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathcal{X}^{m}}\mathbf{1}_{K^{2m}_{\varepsilon}}(\bar{x},\bar{y})\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\otimes m}(\bar{y})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{X}^{m}}\mathbf{1}_{\{|\{y_{1},\dots,y_{m}\}\cap R_{\bar{x}}|\geq\varepsilon m/2\}}(\bar{y})\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\otimes m}(\bar{y}).

The latter integral is identical to the probability that
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\xi_{i}\geq\varepsilon m/2
, where
\xi_{i}=\mathbf{1}_{Y_{i}\in R_{\bar{x}}}
and
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m}

are independently distributed according to
\mu
. Then the random variables
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{m}
are independent Bernoulli random variables distributed with paramater

p=\mu(R_{\bar{x}})>\varepsilon
. We set
p=\varepsilon
which only decreases the above probability.
Since any median
M
of the Binomial distribution with parameters
m,\varepsilon
satisfies
M\geq m\varepsilon-1
,
we observe that for
m\geq 2/\varepsilon
,

\mathbb{P}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{m}\xi_{i}\geq\varepsilon m/2\Big]\geq\frac{1}{2}.

Therefore,

\mu^{\otimes 2m}(J^{2m}_{\varepsilon})\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{Q^{m}_{\varepsilon}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\otimes m}(\bar{x})=\frac{1}{2}\mu(Q^{m}_{\varepsilon}),

which proves the lemma.
∎

We need another lemma. Recall that
\Pi_{\mathcal{R}}
was defined in (
5.1
).

Lemma 5.6
.

For all
\varepsilon>0
and
m\geq 1
,

\mu^{\otimes 2m}(J^{2m}_{\varepsilon})\leq\Pi_{\mathcal{R}}(2m)\,2^{-\varepsilon m/2}.

Proof.

A permutation
\pi
of
\{1,\dots,2m\}
acts on
\bar{z}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{2m})\in\mathcal{X}^{2m}
by
\pi(\bar{z})=(z_{\pi(1)},\dots,z_{\pi(2m)})
and satisfies
\pi\circ\mu^{\otimes 2m}=\mu^{\otimes 2m}
. Averaging over all permutations yields

\displaystyle\mu^{\otimes 2m}(J^{2m}_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{X}^{2m}}\mathbf{1}_{J^{2m}_{\varepsilon}}(\bar{z})\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\otimes 2m}(\bar{z})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{X}^{2m}}\Big(\frac{1}{(2m)!}\sum_{\pi}\mathbf{1}_{J^{2m}_{\varepsilon}}(\pi(\bar{z}))\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\otimes 2m}(\bar{z}),

where the sum is taken over all
(2m)!
such permutations. We provide a uniform upper bound on the integrand.

Fix
\bar{z}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{2m})\in\mathcal{X}^{2m}
and let
Z=\{z_{1},\dots,z_{2m}\}
.
Recall that
\pi(\bar{z})\in J_{\varepsilon}^{2m}
if and only if there is
R\in\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}
such that

R\cap\{z_{\pi(1)},\dots,z_{\pi(m)}\}=\emptyset
and
|R\cap\{z_{\pi(m+1)},\dots,z_{\pi(2m)}\}|\geq\frac{\varepsilon m}{2}
.
In this case we write
\pi\leftrightarrow R
. Without loss of generality, we replace
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}
by the smaller set
system

\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}|_{\bar{z}}=\{R\cap\{z_{1},\dots,z_{2m}\}\colon R\in\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}\}.

Given
R\in\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}|_{\bar{z}}
define the set of permutations
S_{R}=\{\pi\colon\pi\leftrightarrow R\}
.
Then
\pi(\bar{z})\in J_{\varepsilon}^{2m}
if and only if
\pi\in S_{R}
for some (possibly non-unique)
R\in\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}|_{\bar{z}}
. This gives

\frac{1}{(2m)!}\sum_{\pi}\mathbf{1}_{J^{2m}_{\varepsilon}}(\pi(\bar{z}))\leq\frac{1}{(2m)!}\sum_{\pi}\sum_{R\in\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}|_{\bar{z}}}\mathbf{1}_{\pi\in S_{R}}=\sum_{R\in\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}|_{\bar{z}}}\frac{1}{(2m)!}\sum_{\pi}\mathbf{1}_{\pi\in S_{R}}.

Let
R\in\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}|_{\bar{z}}
. If
S_{R}=\emptyset
, omit
R
from the first sum. If
S_{R}\neq\emptyset
, then there exists a subset of distinct indices

I_{R}=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{\ell}\}
such that
\frac{\varepsilon m}{2}\leq\ell\leq m
and
\{z_{1},\dots,z_{2m}\}\cap R=\{z_{i_{1}},\dots,z_{i_{\ell}}\}
and
z_{i}\not\in R
for
i\not\in I_{R}
. Note that for a permutation
\pi
,
\pi\in S_{R}
if and only if
\pi^{-1}(i_{1}),\dots,\pi^{-1}(i_{\ell})\in\{m+1,\dots,2m\}
. The fraction of permutations satisfying this can be computed by noting that there are
\binom{m}{\ell}
ways to map
I_{R}
into
\{m+1,\dots,2m\}
but
\binom{2m}{\ell}
ways to map
I_{R}
into
\{1,\dots,2m\}
. Therefore,

\frac{1}{(2m)!}\sum_{\pi}\mathbf{1}_{\pi\in S_{R}}=\frac{\binom{m}{\ell}}{\binom{2m}{\ell}}=\frac{m(m-1)\cdots(m-\ell+1)}{2m(2m-1)\cdots(2m-\ell+1)}\leq 2^{-\ell}\leq 2^{-\varepsilon m/2}.

We complete the proof by noting that

|R_{\varepsilon}|_{\bar{z}}|\leq|\{R\cap\{z_{1},\dots,z_{2m}\}\colon R\in\mathcal{R}\}|\leq\Pi_{\mathcal{R}}(2m).

∎

These two lemmas yield that for every
\varepsilon>0
and
m\geq 1
with
m\varepsilon\geq 2
,

\mu^{\otimes m}(Q^{m}_{\varepsilon})\leq 2\mu^{\otimes 2m}(J^{2m}_{\varepsilon})\leq 2\Pi_{\mathcal{R}}(2m)2^{-\varepsilon m/2}.

It remains to bound the shatter function in terms of the VC-dimension.
It is done by the Sauer-Shelah lemma, which goes back to independent works by
Vapnik and Chervonenkis, by Sauer, and by Shelah, see the references in
[
47
]
related to
Lemma 5.9 there.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\mathcal{R}\subset\Sigma
be a set system with
\vdim(\mathcal{R})\leq d
. Then for
m\geq 1
,

\Pi_{\mathcal{R}}(m)\leq\Phi_{d}(m):=\sum_{k=0}^{d}\binom{m}{k}.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Let
m\geq d
and
\varepsilon>0
. If
m\varepsilon<2
, then the bound on the probability is trivially satisfied. Thus, let
m\varepsilon\geq 2
. We bound the sum in Lemma
5.7
using the standard bound

\sum_{k=0}^{d}\binom{m}{k}\leq\Big(\frac{me}{d}\Big)^{d},

which follows from

e^{d}\geq\Big(1+\frac{d}{m}\Big)^{m}\geq\sum_{k=0}^{d}\Big(\frac{d}{m}\Big)^{k}\binom{m}{k}\geq\Big(\frac{d}{m}\Big)^{d}\sum_{k=0}^{d}\binom{m}{k}.

Combined with Lemmas
5.5
,
5.6
and
5.7
,
we obtain

\mu^{\otimes m}(Q^{m}_{\varepsilon})\leq 2\Big(\frac{2em}{d}\Big)^{d}2^{-\varepsilon m/2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

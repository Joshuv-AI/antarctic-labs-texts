Abstract
. We discuss a model for phase transitions in which a double-well potential is singularly perturbed by possibly several terms involving different, arbitrarily high orders of derivation. We study by Γ \Gamma -convergence the asymptotic behaviour as ε → 0 \varepsilon\to 0 of the functionals F ε ​ ( u ) := ∫ Ω [ 1 ε ​ W ​ ( u ) + ∑ ℓ = 1 k q ℓ ​ ε 2 ​ ℓ − 1 ​ | ∇ ( ℓ ) u | ℓ 2 ] ​ 𝑑 x , u ∈ H k ​ ( Ω ) , F_{\varepsilon}(u):=\int_{\Omega}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}W(u)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}\varepsilon^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}u|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx,\qquad u\in H^{k}(\Omega), for fixed k > 1 k>1 integer, addressing also the case in which the coefficients q 1 , … , q k − 1 q_{1},...,q_{k-1} are negative and | ⋅ | ℓ |\cdot|_{\ell} is any norm on the space of symmetric ℓ \ell -tensors for each ℓ ∈ { 1 , … , k } \ell\in\{1,...,k\} . The negativity of the coefficients leads to the lack of a priori bounds on the functionals; such issue is overcome by proving a nonlinear interpolation inequality. With this inequality at our disposal, a compactness result is achieved by resorting to the recent paper [ 10 ] . A further difficulty is the presence of general tensor norms which carry anisotropies, making standard slicing arguments not suitable. We prove that the Γ \Gamma -limit is finite only on sharp interfaces and that it equals an anisotropic perimeter, with a surface energy density described by a cell formula. 1.
Introduction

In the recent paper
[
10
]
, it is studied a model for phase transitions in which a double-well potential is perturbed by a singular term involving derivatives of high order. In a one-dimensional setting, this model is described by the family of functionals defined for
\varepsilon>0
as

(1)

E^{1}_{\varepsilon}(u,I):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{I}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}W(u)+\varepsilon^{2k-1}|u^{(k)}|^{2}\Bigr]\,dt&\text{ if }u\in H^{k}(I),\\
+\infty&\text{ if }u\in L^{2}(I)\setminus H^{k}(I),\end{cases}

where
I
is a bounded, open interval,
W
is a non-negative double-well potential, and
k
is a fixed positive integer that can be assumed to be strictly larger than
2
, the cases
k=1
and
k=2
being already studied by Modica and Mortola in
[
21
]
(see also
[
20
]
) and Fonseca and Mantegazza in
[
17
]
, respectively. As customary for phase transition models, the study of the functionals (
1
) is divided in two parts: first, compactness properties are investigated as
\varepsilon
tends to
0
, here it is proved that a family of functions having equi-bounded energy has a sharp interface as a cluster point; then, the asymptotic analysis is performed, in this case, by
\Gamma
-convergence. Under some mild assumptions on the potential
W
, and, in particular, without requiring any growth condition on
W
at infinity, it is proved that (see Theorem
4.1
below for the precise statement) given
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}\subset H^{k}(I)
satisfying
\sup_{\varepsilon}E^{1}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},I)<+\infty
, there exists a function
u\in BV(I;\{-1,1\})
such that, upon extracting a subsequence,
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
in measure as
\varepsilon\to 0
, having set the two wells of
W
at
-1
and
1
. Hence, the
\Gamma
-limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
computed with respect to the convergence in measure is finite only on
BV(I;\{-1,1\})
and it is proved to be equal to
m_{k}\#(S(u)\cap I)
, where
S(u)
denotes the set of the discontinuity points of
u
and
m_{k}
is a strictly positive constant determined by the optimal-profile problem

(2)

m_{k}:=\inf\Bigl\{\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\Bigl[W(v)+|v^{(k)}|^{2}\Bigr]\,dt:v\in H^{k}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}),\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}v(t)=\pm 1\Bigr\}.

Note that such result generalizes the one by Fonseca and Mantegazza for
k=2
to a wider class of double-well potentials.

The precompactness of a family with equi-bounded energy
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
is obtained by adapting an argument originally devised by Solci
[
26
]
for perturbations giving free-discontinuity functionals, which relies on local interpolation inequalities on intervals that allow to estimate the
L^{2}
-norm of an intermediate derivative
u_{\varepsilon}^{(\ell)},\ell\in\{1,...,k-1\}
, in terms of the integral of
W(u_{\varepsilon})
, which is related to the
L^{2}
-norm of
u_{\varepsilon}-1
or
u_{\varepsilon}+1
assuming that
W
is quadratic close to the bottom of the wells, and the
L^{2}
-norm of
u_{\varepsilon}^{(k)}
. In this way, small
transition intervals
on which
u_{\varepsilon}
‘passes from a well to the other’ are detected, with the further condition that, at the endpoints, all the intermediate derivatives are small. These observations lead to considering the minimum problem

\displaystyle m_{k}(\eta,T)

\displaystyle:=\inf\Bigl\{\int_{-T}^{T}\Bigl[W(v)+|v^{(k)}|^{2}\Bigr]\,dt:v\in H^{k}((-T,T)),

\displaystyle\qquad|v(-T)+1|<\eta,|v(T)-1|<\eta,|v^{(\ell)}(\pm T)|<\eta\text{ for all }\ell\in\{1,...,k-1\}\Bigr\}

that, once optimized in
\eta
and
T
, yields (
2
). Since, in turn, (
2
) is proved to be strictly positive, the equi-boundedness of the energy implies that the number of the aforementioned intervals on which a transition occurs is equi-bounded as well, and this property, in broad terms, yields compactness. Having proved that for every
\varepsilon
each transition interval increases the energy
E_{\varepsilon}^{1}
by the fixed amount
m_{k}
, which is independent of
\varepsilon
, the computation of the
\Gamma
-limit follows by standard arguments.

The general
d
-dimensional case deals with the functionals

(3)

E_{\varepsilon}(u,\Omega):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}W(u)+\varepsilon^{2k-1}\|\nabla^{{(k)}}u\|_{k}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx&\text{ if }u\in H^{k}(\Omega),\\
+\infty&\text{ if }u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\setminus H^{k}(\Omega),\end{cases}

where
\Omega
is a bounded, open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with Lipschitz boundary,
W
and
k
are as above, and
\|\cdot\|_{k}
is the operatorial norm of a symmetric
k
-tensor defined as

(4)

\|T\|_{k}:=|\sup\{T(\,\underbrace{\xi,...,\xi}_{k\text{ times}}\,):|\xi|=1\}|.

In
[
10
]
, the study of functionals (
3
) is confined to the computation of the
\Gamma
-limit, and it is remarked that the compactness theorem holds true assuming suitable growth conditions at infinity on
W
. The choice of the operatorial norm makes the asymptotic analysis of such functionals a one-dimensional task since slicing arguments are well suited in this case. Indeed, (
4
) and the slicing properties of Sobolev functions imply that

(5)

\|\nabla^{(k)}v(x)\|_{k}\geq\Bigl|\frac{d^{k}v^{\xi,y}}{dt^{k}}(t)\Bigr|,\qquad\text{ for }\mathcal{L}^{d}\text{-a.e.\ }x\in\Omega,

where
\xi
is a unit vector,
y\in\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:z\cdot\xi=0\}
, and we set
x=y+t\xi
and
v^{\xi,y}(t):=v(y+t\xi)
. Employing the blow-up argument of Fonseca and Müller
[
18
]
together with (
5
) and applying the
\Gamma
-limit result previously obtained in dimension
1
, it is proved that the
\Gamma
-liminf is estimated by

(6)

m_{k}P(\{u=1\};\Omega),

where
P(\{u=1\};\Omega)
is the perimeter of the set
\{u=1\}
in the domain
\Omega
, representing the measure of the interface in higher dimension.
Finally, the construction of a recovery sequence, that by density can be confined to the case of a smooth interface
\partial\{u=1\}\cap\Omega=\partial E\cap\Omega
for some
E
smooth, open set, is obtained by composing the signed distance function from
\partial E
with an (almost) optimal-profile for the one-dimensional problem (
2
) suitably dilated at scale
\varepsilon
. In this way, the energy of such sequence can be computed by slicing in direction of the normal field to
\partial E\cap\Omega
and, since for every
\varepsilon
each slice increases the energy by the fixed amount
m_{k}
, (
6
) is proved to be the
\Gamma
-limit.

In this paper, we study a more general family of functionals in which possibly several singular perturbations, and accordingly several order of derivatives, are involved with no restrictions on the tensor norms. We study the asymptotic behaviour, as the positive parameter
\varepsilon
tends to
0
, of functionals defined as

(7)

F_{\varepsilon}(u,\Omega):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}W(u)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}\varepsilon^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}u|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx&\text{ if }u\in H^{k}(\Omega),\\
+\infty&\text{ if }u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\setminus H^{k}(\Omega),\end{cases}

where
\Omega
is a bounded, open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
C^{1}
boundary,
W
is a non-negative double-well potential,
k
is a fixed positive integer larger than
1
, the coefficients
q_{1},...,q_{k-1}
are real constants, possibly negative,
q_{k}=1
, and
|\cdot|_{\ell}
is a norm on the space of symmetric
\ell
-tensors for every
\ell\in\{1,...,k\}
.
More specifically, we suppose that
W:\mathbb{R}\to[0,+\infty)
is a continuous function satisfying the following assumptions:

(H
1
)

W(s)=0
if and only if
s\in\{-1,1\}
;

(H
2
)

W(s)\geq\min\{(s+1)^{2},(s-1)^{2}\}
for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
;

(H
3
)

W(s)\leq W(t)+1
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
and for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
with
|s|\leq|t|
.

We remark that, with some minor adaptations in the computations, we could also assume more in general that, for some positive constants
\alpha,\beta
, the following conditions hold:

(H
2
’)

W(s)\geq\alpha\min\{(s+1)^{2},(s-1)^{2}\}
for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
;

(H
3
’)

W(s)\leq\beta W(t)+\beta
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
and for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
with
|s|\leq|t|
,

having our main results still holding unchanged.

One of the difficulties in dealing with general tensor norms is the arising of possible anisotropies, which make slicing techniques inadequate. For this reason, we rely on arguments that are not based on a one-dimensional analysis. But the main issue of our study concerns the presence of negative terms in the energy. To overcome this difficulty we combine the approaches of Chermisi, Dal Maso, Fonseca, and Leoni in
[
11
]
, and of Cicalese, Spadaro, and Zeppieri in
[
12
]
, in which, independently, it is treated the case
k=2
: in particular, in the first work, the analysis is performed also in higher dimension (with the operatorial norms), while the latter gives a detailed description of the one dimensional case. Our first step is proving a global interpolation inequality at scale
\varepsilon
that establishes bounds on the square of the
L^{2}
norm of
\nabla^{(\ell)}u
in terms of
\int_{\Omega}W(u)\,dx
and the square of the
L^{2}
norm of
\nabla^{(k)}u
. This inequality also determines lower bounds for the constants
q_{1},...,q_{k-1}
for which our asymptotic analysis can be carried out.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded, open set with
C^{1}
boundary, let
k>1
be an integer, and assume that
(H2)
is satisfied. Then, for every
\ell\in\{1,...,k-1\}
, there exists a positive constant
q^{*}_{\ell}
independent of
\Omega
such that for every
q<q^{*}_{\ell}
there exists
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(q,\Omega)
such that it holds

q\varepsilon^{2\ell}\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla^{(\ell)}u\|_{\ell}^{2}\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}\Bigl[W(u)+\varepsilon^{2k}\|\nabla^{(k)}u\|_{k}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx

for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and
u\in H^{k}(\Omega)
. Moreover, for every
\ell\in\{1,...,k-1\}
, there exists a positive constant
\widetilde{q}^{*}_{\ell}
independent of
\Omega
such that for every
q<\widetilde{q}^{*}_{\ell}
there exists
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(q,\Omega)
such that it holds

q\varepsilon^{2\ell}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{(\ell)}u|_{\ell}^{2}\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}\Bigl[W(u)+\varepsilon^{2k}|\nabla^{(k)}u|_{k}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx

for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and
u\in H^{k}(\Omega)
. In particular,
\widetilde{q}^{*}_{\ell}
depends on
q^{*}_{\ell},|\cdot|_{\ell}
, and
|\cdot|_{k}
.

The above interpolation inequality is proved by, first, adapting the arguments in
[
12
]
, which yield the result in the case
d=1
, and then by applying the slicing method as in
[
11
]
to recover the general case. We remark that assumption (H
2
), despite not being fully satisfactory in describing the model as it prescribes the growth rate at infinity of the double-well potential, is needed to apply classical, global
L^{2}
-interpolation estimates. Indeed, the presence of negative terms in the energy seems to prevent the use of the local arguments applied in
[
10
]
, as it is not evident to us how to estimate the energy when far from the bottom of the wells.

Having at disposal such interpolation estimate allows us to find a priori bounds on the functionals (
7
), provided the coefficients
q_{1},...,q_{k-1}
are not too small, and this is the key ingredient to get the main compactness result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

be a bounded, open set with
C^{1}
boundary, let
\{F_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
be defined as in (
7
) with
k>1
an integer, let
\mathcal{N}:=\{\ell\in\{1,...,k-1\}:q_{\ell}\leq 0\}
, and let
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n}
be a positive sequence converging to
0
. Assume that
(H1)
and
(H2)
are satisfied and that one of the following conditions holds:

(i)

\mathcal{N}=\emptyset
;

(ii)

\mathcal{N}\neq\emptyset
and
q_{\ell}>-\overline{q}_{\ell}
for every
\ell\in\mathcal{N}
for suitable positive constants
\{\overline{q}_{\ell}:\ell\in\mathcal{N}\}
.

If
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset H^{k}(\Omega)
is a sequence such that

\sup_{n}F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n},\Omega)<+\infty,

then there exist a subsequence
\{u_{n_{j}}\}_{j}
and a function
u\in BV(\Omega;\{-1,1\})
such that
u_{n_{j}}\to u
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
j\to+\infty
. In particular, if
\mathcal{N}\neq\emptyset
, letting
\widetilde{q}^{*}_{1},...,\widetilde{q}^{*}_{k-1}
denote the same positive constants appearing in Theorem
1.1
and given
\{\alpha_{\ell}:\ell\in\mathcal{N}\}
such that
\alpha_{\ell}>0
for all
\ell\in\mathcal{N}
and
\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{N}}\alpha_{\ell}=1
, it holds that
\overline{q}_{\ell}=\alpha_{\ell}\widetilde{q}^{*}_{\ell}
for all
\ell\in\mathcal{N}
; hence,
\{\overline{q}_{\ell}:\ell\in\mathcal{N}\}
are independent of
\Omega
.

Clearly, if the coefficients
q_{1},...,q_{k-1}
are non-negative, the compactness result in
[
10
]
suggests there is no need to resort to Theorem
1.1
since, by the equivalence of the tensor norms, compactness of the functionals
\{F_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
can be inferred by compactness of the functionals
\{E_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
defined in (
3
) through a direct comparison. For this reason, we also prove a compactness result for functionals
\{E_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
(Proposition
4.4
). The first step is enhancing the compactness result for functionals (
1
) stated in
[
10
]
taking advantage of assumption (H
2
). Indeed, this assumption implies that
W
has quadratic growth at infinity, and this condition, in turn, allows to improve precompactness in measure into precompactness in
L^{2}
. Then, we recover the general
d
-dimensional case by resorting to a precompactness criterion in
L^{1}
by slicing that has been introduced in
[
2
]
(see Proposition
4.3
below), which in our case is sufficient to obtain precompactness in
L^{2}
.

Afterwards, we compute the
\Gamma
-limit of the family
\{F_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
with respect to the strong convergence in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. In order to state our result, we introduce some notation.
We set
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\xi|=1\}
. For fixed
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and
T>0
, we let the symbol
Q_{T}^{\nu}
denote an open cube of side length
T
, centred at the origin, with two faces orthogonal to
\nu
,

(8)

Q^{\nu}_{T}:=\Bigl\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x\cdot\nu|<\frac{T}{2},\,|x\cdot\nu_{i}|<\frac{T}{2}\text{ for every }i\in\{1,...,d-1\}\Bigr\},

where
\{\nu_{1},...,\nu_{d-1},\nu\}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that we fix arbitrarily.

We fix a function
\overline{u}\in H_{\text{loc}}^{k}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\overline{u}(t)=-1
if
t\leq-1/8
,
\overline{u}(t)=1
if
t\geq 1/8
,
|\overline{u}|\leq 1
, and

0<\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\Bigl[W(\overline{u})+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}|q_{\ell}||\overline{u}^{(\ell)}|^{2}\Bigr]\,dt<+\infty,

then, for fixed
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
we define the function

\overline{u}^{\nu}(x):=\overline{u}(x\cdot\nu)\quad\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

and for every
\varepsilon>0
we further define

\overline{u}^{\nu}_{\varepsilon}(x):=\overline{u}^{\nu}\bigl(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\bigr)=\overline{u}\bigl(\frac{x\cdot\nu}{\varepsilon}\bigr)\quad\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

We introduce a class of admissible functions on unit cubes as

(9)

\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}^{\nu}:=\bigl\{v\in H^{k}(Q_{1}^{\nu}):v=\overline{u}^{\nu}_{\varepsilon}\text{ near }\partial Q_{1}^{\nu}\bigr\},

where by ‘near
\partial Q_{1}^{\nu}
’, we mean ‘in
Q_{1}^{\nu}\setminus Q_{r}^{\nu}
for some
r\in(0,1)
’.

Finally, for every
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
we introduce the function

(10)

g(\nu):=\inf\Big\{\int_{{Q_{1}^{\nu}}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}W(v)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}\varepsilon^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}v|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx:v\in\mathcal{A}^{\nu}_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon\in(0,1)\Big\},

and we state our last main result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

be a bounded, open set with
C^{1}
boundary, let
\{F_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
be defined as in (
7
) with
k>1
an integer, and let
\mathcal{N}:=\{\ell\in\{1,...,k-1\}:q_{\ell}\leq 0\}
. Assume that
(H1)-(H3)
are satisfied and that one of the following conditions holds:

(i)

\mathcal{N}=\emptyset
;

(ii)

\mathcal{N}\neq\emptyset
and
q_{\ell}>-\overline{q}_{\ell}
for every
\ell\in\mathcal{N}
with the same positive constants
\{\overline{q}_{\ell}:\ell\in\mathcal{N}\}
independent of
\Omega
appearing in Theorem
1.2
.

Then the family of functionals
\{F_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}

\Gamma
-converges, with respect to the strong convergence in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, to the functional
F_{0}
given by

F_{0}(u,\Omega):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\partial^{*}\!A\cap\Omega}g(\nu_{A})\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}&\text{ if }u\in BV(\Omega;\{-1,1\}),\,\text{ with }u=2\chi_{A}-1,\\
+\infty&\text{ if }u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\setminus BV(\Omega;\{-1,1\}),\end{cases}

where
A
is a set of finite perimeter in
\Omega
,
\partial^{*}A
is its reduced boundary,
\nu_{A}
is its inner unit normal, and
g
is defined as in (
10
).

In the special case
q_{1}=...=q_{k-1}=0
, this result improves the corresponding one in
[
10
]
as it deals with arbitrary norms on the spaces of tensors, upon assuming stronger hypotheses on the double-well potential.

In order to compute the
\Gamma
-limit, we follow the strategy presented in
[
11
]
. The lower bound is proved by using the blow-up argument and relying on a method due to De Giorgi that allows to vary boundary conditions on converging sequences (Lemma
5.2
). The construction of a recovery sequence is confined to the case of polyhedra, which are seen to be dense in energy for the limit
F_{0}
, being the surface energy density
g
upper semicontinuous (Proposition
2.4
).

As stated below in Remark
2.5
, the density (
10
) can be described by the following asymptotic formula

\displaystyle g(\nu)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\,\inf\Big\{\int_{{Q_{1}^{\nu}}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}W(v)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}\varepsilon^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}v|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx:v\in\mathcal{A}^{\nu}_{\varepsilon}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\lim_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T^{d-1}}\,\inf\Big\{\int_{{Q_{T}^{\nu}}}\Bigl[W(v)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}|\nabla^{(\ell)}v|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx:v\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}^{\nu}_{T}\Big\},

where

\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{T}^{\nu}:=\bigl\{v\in H^{k}(Q_{T}^{\nu}):v=\overline{u}^{\nu}\text{ near }\partial Q_{T}^{\nu}\bigr\},

and the last equality follows by setting
T:=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
and a change of variables.

The formula appearing in (
10
) should be compared with the one in
[
11
]
. In that work such density is a constant because of the absence of anisotropies, and it is given by

\inf\Big\{\int_{{Q}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}W(v)-q\varepsilon\|\nabla v\|_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon^{3}\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{2}\Bigr]\,dx:v\in\mathcal{A},\varepsilon\in(0,1)\Big\},

with
Q=(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})^{d}
, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}:=\bigl\{v\in H^{k}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\,\,

\displaystyle v=-1\text{ near }x\cdot e_{d}=-\frac{1}{2},v=1\text{ near }x\cdot e_{d}=\frac{1}{2},

\displaystyle v(x+e_{i})=v(x)\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},i\in\{1,...,d-1\}\bigr\},

where
\{e_{1},...,e_{d}\}
is the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and by ‘near
x\cdot e_{d}=\pm\frac{1}{2}
’ it is meant ‘in a neighborhood of the set
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:x\cdot e_{d}=\pm\frac{1}{2}\}
’.

In light of this observation, we expect that our density
g
could be characterized as

\inf\Big\{\int_{{Q_{1}^{\nu}}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}W(v)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}\varepsilon^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}v|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx:v\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}^{\nu},\varepsilon\in(0,1)\Big\},

with

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}^{\nu}:=\bigl\{v\in H^{k}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\,\,

\displaystyle v=-1\text{ near }x\cdot\nu=-\frac{1}{2},v=1\text{ near }x\cdot\nu=\frac{1}{2},

\displaystyle v(x+\nu_{i})=v(x)\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},i\in\{1,...,d-1\}\bigr\},

where
\{\nu_{1},...,\nu_{d-1},\nu\}
is the orthonormal basis that we fixed in the definitions (
8
) and (
9
).
To prove this fact, we would need to impose boundary conditions resorting to Lemma
5.2
. In turn, to do this we would need to improve Theorem
1.1
, even just in the case of the operatorial norms, to the case
\Omega
be an open cube. In
[
11
]
, this result is obtained by slicing, which is well suited because, roughly speaking, the operatorial norm of a
1
-tensor, the gradient, coincides with its euclidean norm as a vector of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Because of the higher order of derivatives that our interpolation estimates involve, such argument seems to be difficult to adapt. As an additional consequence of the lack of interpolation estimates on cubes, the proofs of Lemma
5.2
(and its statement) and of the lower bound (Proposition
5.1
) present several differences with respect to their counterparts in
[
11
]
. In particular, we will need to apply our interpolation inequalities on a
C^{1}
subset of the cube
Q^{\nu}_{1}
that coincides with this cube inside the strip
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x\cdot\nu|\leq\frac{1}{4}\}
which is close to the interface on which most of the energy is stored.

We remark that, in the case
k=2
, such asymptotic analysis has already been performed for more general functionals that are bounded from above and from below by positive multiples of the functionals (
7
), allowing, for instance, the dependence of the energy densities on the variable

]
, Baía, Barroso, Chermisi, and Matias studied the case

; while, in the recent paper
[

]
, Donnarumma has addressed the problem of deterministic and stochastic homogenization of such functionals. As a consequence of their results, the validity of Theorem

for generic norms follows. For a treating of phase transitions models encoding homogenization phenomena we also refer to
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
.

In the final section, we further discuss the cell problem in (
10
) in the one-dimensional case, and we also address the case in which all the norms

appearing in (
7
) are ‘compatible with slicing’; that is, satisfying an estimate like (

), and all the coefficients
q_{\ell},\,\ell\in\{1,...,k-1\}
are non-negative. In both cases, the function

in (
10
) is constant, and its value is determined by the one-dimensional optimal-profile problem

m:=\inf\Bigl\{\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\Bigl[W(u)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}|u^{(\ell)}|^{2}\Bigr]\,dt:u\in H^{k}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}),\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}u(t)=\pm 1\Bigr\}.

By a cut-off argument, we are also able to prove, without requiring (H
3
), that the above infimum actually is a minimum, determining the existence of optime-profiles for a wide class of phase-transitions problems on the real line.

We conclude this introductory section by noting that in recent years there has also been a growing interest in non-local counterparts of the models for phase transitions described above. Starting from the foundational work of Savin and Valdinoci
[
24
]
, several works on the subject have been published. We mention here the works
[
14
,
22
,
23
]
and the recent paper by Solci
[
25
]
, in which a non-local counterpart of the results of
[
10
]
is proved. It would be interesting to extend our analysis to that context.

Near the completion of our work, we learnt that, independently, Brazke, Götzmann, and Knüpfer performed the same asymptotic analysis in the one-dimensional case without requiring (H
3
) and with the further assumption that
q_{1}=...=q_{k-2}=0
; that is, taking into account a possibly negative singular perturbation described by the only derivative of order
k-1
(see
[
9
]
) using an argument different from ours for the liminf inequality.

Functions of bounded variation.
Given

The distributional derivative of

\displaystyle\Gamma\text{-}\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}G_{\varepsilon}(u):=\inf\{\liminf_{n\to+\infty}G_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n}):\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n}\text{ vanishing},\,u_{n}\to u\text{ in }L^{2}(\Omega)\};

(12)

\displaystyle\Gamma\text{-}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}G_{\varepsilon}(u):=\inf\{\limsup_{n\to+\infty}G_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n}):\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n}\text{ vanishing},\,u_{n}\to u\text{ in }L^{2}(\Omega)\}.

it holds

(19)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\nabla^{(\ell)}v_{n}(R_{n}x)(e_{i_{1}},...,e_{i_{\ell}})=\nabla^{(\ell)}v(x)(e_{i_{1}},...,e_{i_{\ell}})\,\,\text{ for every }(i_{1},...,i_{\ell})\in\{1,...,d\}^{\ell}.

Let us fix
(i_{1},...,i_{\ell})\in\{1,...,d\}^{\ell}
. For
\mathcal{L}^{d}
-a.e.
x\in Q^{\nu}_{1}
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\nabla^{(\ell)}v_{n}(R_{n}x)(e_{i_{1}},...,e_{i_{\ell}})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\partial^{\ell}v_{n}}{\partial x_{i_{1}}...\partial x_{i_{\ell}}}(R_{n}x)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{d}...\sum_{j_{\ell}=1}^{d}R_{n}^{i_{1},j_{1}}...\ R_{n}^{i_{\ell},j_{\ell}}\frac{\partial^{\ell}v}{\partial x_{j_{1}}...\partial x_{j_{\ell}}}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{d}...\sum_{j_{\ell}=1}^{d}\delta_{i_{1}j_{1}}...\delta_{i_{\ell}j_{\ell}}\frac{\partial^{\ell}v}{\partial x_{j_{1}}...\partial x_{j_{\ell}}}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial^{\ell}v}{\partial x_{i_{1}}...\partial x_{i_{\ell}}}(x)=\nabla^{(\ell)}v(x)(e_{i_{1}},...,e_{i_{\ell}}),

and this equality proves (
19
).

To conclude, we first observe that, since
R_{n}\to\mathbb{I}_{d}
, we may assume that
|R_{n}^{i,j}|\leq 2
for all
i,j\in\{1,...,d\}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
so that

\displaystyle|\nabla^{(\ell)}v_{n}(R_{n}x)|_{\ell}

\displaystyle\leq C\max\Bigl\{\big|\dfrac{\partial^{\ell}v}{\partial x_{i_{1}}...\partial x_{i_{\ell}}}(R_{n}x)\big|:\ (i_{1},....,i_{\ell})\in\{1,...,d\}^{\ell}\Bigr\}

\displaystyle=C\max\Bigl\{\bigl|\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{d}...\sum_{j_{\ell}=1}^{d}R_{n}^{i_{1},j_{1}}...\ R_{n}^{i_{\ell},j_{\ell}}\dfrac{\partial^{\ell}v}{\partial x_{j_{1}}...\partial x_{j_{\ell}}}(x)\bigr|:\ (i_{1},....,i_{\ell})\in\{1,...,d\}^{\ell}\Bigr\}

(20)

\displaystyle\leq C\max\Bigl\{\big|\dfrac{\partial^{\ell}v}{\partial x_{i_{1}}...\partial x_{i_{\ell}}}(x)\big|:\ (i_{1},....,i_{\ell})\in\{1,...,d\}^{\ell}\Bigr\}\leq C|\nabla^{(\ell)}v(x)|_{\ell},

where
C
is a positive constant (that may very from line to line) which only depends on
d
and
\ell
as it arises by the equivalence of the tensor norms. Finally, combining (
19
) with (
20
), we apply the Dominated Convergence Theorem in (
18
), so that by (
17
) we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to+\infty}g(\nu_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{Q_{1}^{\nu}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}W(v(x))+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}\varepsilon^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}v_{n}(R_{n}x)|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{Q_{1}^{\nu}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}W(v(x))+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}\varepsilon^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}v(x)|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx\leq g(\nu)+\eta.

The arbitrariness of
\eta
concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

With the previous proof, we also showed that

g(\nu)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\,\inf\Big\{\int_{{Q_{1}^{\nu}}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}W(v)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}\varepsilon^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}v|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx:v\in\mathcal{A}^{\nu}_{\varepsilon}\Big\}.

This observation will be useful in the last section in order to study the density
g
in the one-dimensional case.

3.
Interpolation

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Furthermore, we obtain a useful result (Corollary
3.7
) that we will use in the proof of Theorem
1.2
. Throughout this section, we keep track of the dependence of the constants on the involved parameters with the only exceptions of
d
, the dimension of the ambient Euclidean space, and
k
, the highest order of derivation.

We start by mentioning a classical interpolation estimate on intervals (see Theorem
7.41
in
[
19
]
). For simplicity of notation, for every interval
I
we let
|I|
denote its length.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be a bounded, open interval and let
k

be a positive integer. Then there exists a positive constant
c
independent of
I
such that

\ \int_{I}|u^{(k-1)}|^{2}\,dt\leq c|I|^{-2(k-1)}\int_{I}u^{2}\,dt+c\int_{I}|u^{(k)}|^{2}\,dt

for every
u\in H^{k}(I)
.

We adapt the argument of Lemma
3.1
in
[
12
]
to bound the square of the
L^{2}
-norm of the derivative of order
k-1
by means of the integral of the double-well potential and the square of the
L^{2}
-norm of the derivative of order
k
. Then, we deduce the same kind of estimate for any derivate of intermediate order
\ell\in\{1,...,k-1\}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be a bounded, open interval, let
k>1

be an integer, and assume that
(H2)
is satisfied. Then there exists a positive constant
q^{\prime}_{k-1}
independent of
I
such that

q\int_{I}|u^{(k-1)}(t)|^{2}\,dt\leq|I|^{-2(k-1)}\int_{I}W(u)\,dt+|I|^{2}\int_{I}|u^{(k)}|^{2}\,dt

for every
q<q^{\prime}_{k-1}
and
u\in H^{k}(I)
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to prove the statement for
I=(0,1)
, the general case being obtained by translating and rescaling.
We set

z:=\int_{0}^{1}u^{(k-1)}\,dt,

and by the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus we infer that

(21)

|u^{(k-1)}(t)-z|\leq\int_{0}^{1}|u^{(k)}|\,dt\qquad\text{for all }t\in(0,1).

As a consequence

\int_{0}^{1}|u^{(k-1)}|^{2}\,dt\leq 2\int_{0}^{1}|u^{(k)}|^{2}\,dt+2z^{2};

therefore, the proof is complete if we prove that

(22)

z^{2}\leq C\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl[W(u)+|u^{(k)}|^{2}\Bigr]\,dt

for some
C>0
. If
z^{2}\leq 4\int_{0}^{1}|u^{(k)}|^{2}\,dt
, then (
22
) is immediately satisfied. Otherwise, by Jensen’s inequality, we have that
|z|>2\int_{0}^{1}|u^{(k)}|\,dt
, which together with (
21
), yields

(23)

|u^{(k-1)}(t)|\geq\frac{|z|}{2}>0\qquad\text{for all }t\in(0,1).

Consider the intervals
(\frac{j}{k},\frac{j+1}{k}),\,j\in\{0,...,k-1\}
. We observe that there exists
j^{*}\in\{0,...,k-1\}
such that
u\neq 0
on
(\frac{j*}{k},\frac{j*+1}{k})
. To see this, assume by contradiction that
u
vanishes on each of these intervals; then,
u
has at least
k
zeros on
I
. By Lagrange’s Theorem, this implies that
u^{\prime}
vanishes at
k-1
points of
I
, and then, iterating this argument, we obtain that
u^{(k-1)}
vanishes at a point of
I
, contradicting (
23
). We suppose
u>0
in
(\frac{j^{*}}{k},\frac{j^{*}+1}{k})
, the case
u<0
being analogous.
Applying Theorem
3.1
to the function
u-1
on the interval
(\frac{j^{*}}{k},\frac{j^{*}+1}{k})
and resorting to (H
2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{j^{*}}{k}}^{\frac{j^{*}+1}{k}}|u^{(k-1)}|^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq ck^{2(k-1)}\int_{\frac{j^{*}}{k}}^{\frac{j^{*}+1}{k}}(u-1)^{2}\,dt+c\int_{\frac{j^{*}}{k}}^{\frac{j^{*}+1}{k}}|u^{(k)}|^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq ck^{2(k-1)}\int_{\frac{j^{*}}{k}}^{\frac{j^{*}+1}{k}}W(u)\,dt+c\int_{\frac{j^{*}}{k}}^{\frac{j^{*}+1}{k}}|u^{(k)}|^{2}\,dt,

therefore, taking into account (
23
),

\displaystyle z^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4k\int_{\frac{j^{*}}{k}}^{\frac{j^{*}+1}{k}}|u^{(k-1)}|^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq 4ck^{2k-1}\int_{\frac{j^{*}}{k}}^{\frac{j^{*}+1}{k}}W(u)\,dt+4ck\int_{\frac{j^{*}}{k}}^{\frac{j^{*}+1}{k}}|u^{(k)}|^{2}\,dt,

which, by the positivity of
W
, proves (
22
) and concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be a bounded, open interval, let
k>1

be an integer, and assume that
(H2)
is satisfied. Then, for every
\ell\in\{1,...,k-1\}
, there exists a positive constant
q^{\prime}_{\ell}
independent of
I
such that

q\int_{I}|u^{(\ell)}|^{2}\,dt\leq|I|^{-2\ell}\int_{I}W(u)\,dt+|I|^{2(k-\ell)}\int_{I}|u^{(k)}|^{2}\,dt

for every
q<q^{\prime}_{\ell}
and
u\in H^{k}(I)
.

Proof.

The thesis follows by applying iteratively Lemma
3.2
.
∎

Now we adapt the interpolation inequality at scale
\varepsilon
, in view of its application to functionals (
7
).

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be a bounded, open interval, let
k>1

be an integer, and assume that
(H2)
is satisfied. Then, for every
\ell\in\{1,...,k-1\}
, there exists a positive constant
q^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}
independent of
I
such that, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,|I|/2)
, it holds

q\varepsilon^{2\ell}\int_{I}|u^{(\ell)}|^{2}\,dt\leq\int_{I}\Bigl[W(u)+\varepsilon^{2k}|u^{(k)}|^{2}\Bigr]\,dt

for every
q<q^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}
and
u\in H^{k}(I)
.

Proof.

We set
v(t):=u(\varepsilon t)
for
t\in\frac{1}{\varepsilon}I
so that

\varepsilon^{2\ell-1}\int_{I}|u^{(\ell)}|^{2}\,dt=\int_{\frac{I}{\varepsilon}}|v^{(\ell)}|^{2}\,dt.

Then, we set
n_{\varepsilon}:=\lfloor\frac{|I|}{\varepsilon}\rfloor
and we subdivide the rescaled interval
\frac{1}{\varepsilon}I
in
n_{\varepsilon}
pairwise disjoint, open intervals
I_{\varepsilon}^{j},\,j\in\{1,...,n_{\varepsilon}\}
each of length
\frac{|I|}{\varepsilon n_{\varepsilon}}
. Then we apply Corollary
3.3
on each subinterval
I_{\varepsilon}^{j}
with
q<q^{\prime}_{\ell}
and we obtain

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2\ell-1}\int_{I}|u^{(\ell)}|^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\varepsilon}}\int_{I_{\varepsilon}^{j}}|v^{(\ell)}|^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{q}\Bigl\{|I_{\varepsilon}^{j}|^{-2\ell}\int_{I_{\varepsilon}^{j}}W(v)\,dt+|I_{\varepsilon}^{j}|^{2(k-\ell)}\int_{I_{\varepsilon}^{j}}|v^{(k)}|^{2}\,dt\Bigr\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{q}\Bigl\{2^{2\ell}\int_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}I}W(v)\,dt+\Bigl(\frac{3}{2}\Bigr)^{2(k-\ell)}\int_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}I}|v^{(k)}|^{2}\,dt\Bigr\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\max\{2^{2\ell},(3/2)^{2(k-\ell)}\}}{q}\int_{I}\Bigl[\frac{W(u)}{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{2k-1}|u^{(k)}|^{2}\Bigr]\,dt,

where the second inequality follows by the fact
|I_{\varepsilon}^{j}|\in(\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2})
if
\varepsilon\in(0,|I|/2)
.

We multiply the above inequality by
\varepsilon
to obtain that

\widetilde{q}\varepsilon^{2\ell}\int_{I}|u^{(\ell)}|^{2}\,dt\leq\int_{I}\Bigl[W(u)+\varepsilon^{2k}|u^{(k)}|^{2}\Bigr]\,dt

for every
\widetilde{q}<q^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,|I|/2)
, where we set

q^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}:=\frac{q^{\prime}_{\ell}}{\max\{2^{2\ell},(3/2)^{2(k-\ell)}\}};

therefore, the proof is complete.
∎

The following immediate corollary proves Theorem
1.1
in dimension
1
.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}
be the union of finitely many bounded, open, disjoint intervals, let
k>1

be an integer, and assume that
(H2)
is satisfied. Then, denoting by
|I_{o}|
the length of the shortest connected component of
\Omega
, for every
\ell\in\{1,...,k-1\}
and for every
\varepsilon\in(0,|I_{o}|/2)
it holds

q\varepsilon^{2\ell}\int_{\Omega}|u^{(\ell)}|^{2}\,dt\leq\int_{\Omega}\Bigl[W(u)+\varepsilon^{2k}|u^{(k)}|^{2}\Bigr]\,dt

for every
q<q^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}
and
u\in H^{k}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

The proof follows by an immediate application of Proposition
3.4
.
∎

Finally, we prove the main result of this section by using the argument presented in the proof of Theorem
1.2
in
[
11
]
. Below, we will make use of notation about slicing that has been introduced in Section
2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

It suffices to prove the part of the statement concerning the operatorial norms, since the other case follows by the equivalence of the norms. We follow the proof of Theorem
1.2
in
[
11
]
. For
r>0,\,\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, and
y\in\Omega^{\xi}
, we define
\Omega^{\xi,y}_{r}
as the finite union of the connected components of the slice
\Omega^{\xi,y}
having length larger than
r
. Then we set
\Omega(\xi,r):=\{y+t\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:y\in\Omega^{\xi},\,t\in\Omega^{\xi,y}_{r}\}
and we observe that
(\Omega(\xi,r))^{\xi,y}=\Omega^{\xi,y}_{r}
.

Applying Corollary
3.5
we obtain

\widetilde{q}\varepsilon^{2\ell}\int_{\Omega^{\xi,y}_{r}}|(u^{\xi,y})^{(\ell)}|^{2}\,dt\leq\int_{\Omega^{\xi,y}_{r}}\Bigl[W(u^{\xi,y})+\varepsilon^{2k}|(u^{\xi,y})^{(k)}|^{2}\Bigr]\,dt

for all
\widetilde{q}<q^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}
and
\varepsilon<r/2
. We integrate the above inequality on
\xi^{\perp}
to get

\widetilde{q}\varepsilon^{2\ell}\int_{\Omega(\xi,r)}|(\nabla^{(\ell)}u)(\xi,...,\xi)|^{2}\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}\Bigl[W(u)+\varepsilon^{2k}\|\nabla^{(k)}u\|_{k}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx,

and setting
A(\xi,r):=\Omega\setminus\Omega(\xi,r)
we obtain

\widetilde{q}\varepsilon^{2\ell}\int_{\Omega}|(\nabla^{(\ell)}u)(\xi,...,\xi)|^{2}\,dx-\widetilde{q}\varepsilon^{2\ell}\int_{A(\xi,r)}\|\nabla^{(\ell)}u\|_{\ell}^{2}\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}\Bigl[W(u)+\varepsilon^{2k}\|\nabla^{(k)}u\|_{k}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx.

Averaging this inequality on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
yields

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2\ell}\frac{\widetilde{q}}{\sigma_{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\int_{\Omega}|(\nabla^{(\ell)}u)(\xi,...,\xi)|^{2}\,dx\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\xi)

\displaystyle-\varepsilon^{2\ell}\frac{\widetilde{q}}{\sigma_{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\int_{A(\xi,r)}\|\nabla^{(\ell)}u\|^{2}\,dx\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\Bigl[W(u)+\varepsilon^{2k}\|\nabla^{(k)}u\|_{k}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx,

where we set
\sigma_{d-1}:=\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
.

By a simple compactness argument, there exists a positive constant
\tau(\ell)
such that

\frac{1}{\sigma_{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|T(\xi,...,\xi)|^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\xi)\geq\tau(\ell)\|T\|^{2}_{\ell}

for every symmetric
\ell
-tensor
T
. Therefore, by Fubini’s Theorem,

(24)

\frac{1}{\sigma_{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\int_{\Omega}|(\nabla^{(\ell)}u)(\xi,...,\xi)|^{2}\,dx\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\xi)\geq\tau(\ell)\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla^{(\ell)}u\|_{\ell}^{2}\,dx.

Again by Fubini’s Theorem, we have

\frac{1}{\sigma_{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\int_{A(\xi,r)}\|\nabla^{(\ell)}u\|_{\ell}^{2}\,dx\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\sigma_{d-1}}\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla^{(\ell)}u\|_{\ell}^{2}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(D(x,r))\,dx,

where
D(x,r):=\{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:x\in A(\xi,r)
}; hence, to conclude the proof, we have to select
r
small enough so that
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(D(x,r))
is small uniformly in
x
. Given
x\in\partial\Omega
, we let
C_{R}(x)
denote the cylinder having height
2R
and radius
R
with axis parallel to the normal
\nu(x)
to
\partial\Omega
at
x
.

Let
q<\tau(\ell)q^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}
, and fix
\widetilde{q}
such that
\frac{q}{\tau(\ell)}<\widetilde{q}<q^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}
. Fix
\eta=\eta(q)>0
such that

(25)

q<\widetilde{q}(\tau(\ell)-\eta);

since
\Omega
has
C^{1}
boundary, there exists
R
such that, for every
x\in\partial\Omega
, it holds
C_{R}(x)\cap\partial\Omega
is the graph of a
C^{1}
function with respect to the base of the cylinder, and moreover,

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:\xi\in T_{\partial\Omega}(y),\,y\in C_{R}(x)\cap\partial\Omega\})\leq\eta\sigma_{d-1}.

Employing the same argument presented in the proof of Theorem
1.2
in
[
11
]
, it holds that,

D(x,r)\subseteq\{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:\xi\in T_{\partial\Omega}(y),\,y\in C_{R}(x)\cap\partial\Omega\}

up to choosing
r<R/2
; as a consequence,
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(D(x,r))\leq\eta
for all
x\in\partial\Omega
if
r<R/2
, and then

(26)

\frac{1}{\sigma_{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\int_{A(\xi,r)}\|\nabla^{(\ell)}u\|_{\ell}^{2}\,dx\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\xi)\leq\eta\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla^{(\ell)}u\|_{\ell}^{2}\,dx,

for all
r<R/2
.

Gathering (
24
) and (
26
), we obtain that

(27)

\widetilde{q}(\tau(\ell)-\eta)\varepsilon^{2\ell}\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla^{(\ell)}u\|_{\ell}^{2}\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}\Bigl[W(u)+\varepsilon^{2k}\|\nabla^{(k)}u\|_{k}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx

for all
\widetilde{q}<q^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}
and
\varepsilon<R/2
, where
R=R(\eta,\Omega)
.

Recalling (
25
), by (
27
) we get

q\varepsilon^{2\ell}\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla^{(\ell)}u\|_{\ell}^{2}\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}\Bigl[W(u)+\varepsilon^{2k}\|\nabla^{(k)}u\|_{k}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx.

The above inequality is valid for all
q<\tau(\ell)q^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}
and
\varepsilon<R(\eta,\Omega)/2
.
Then, the thesis holds with
q^{*}_{\ell}:=\tau(\ell)q^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}
and
\varepsilon_{0}:=R(\eta,\Omega)/2
, which in turn, since
\eta
depends on
q
, only depends on
q
and
\Omega
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

We are not able to prove the interpolation inequality on an open cube. The same slicing arguments employed in
[
11
]
cannot be applied in our case because of the high order of the derivatives that we want to estimate. This complicates our arguments in view of the proof of Theorem
1.3
since, in what follows, it will be important to apply Theorem
1.1
on a class of
C^{1}
sets
\{\Omega_{\rho}:\rho\in(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})\}
for some
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}>0
for which the threshold
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(q,\Omega_{\rho})
can be chosen independently of
\rho\in(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})
. Inspecting the proof of Theorem
1.1
, this amounts to prove that for each properly fixed
q
, given
\eta>0
such that

q<\widetilde{q}(\tau(\ell)-\eta),

there exists
R>0
such that, for every
\rho\in(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})
and for every
x\in\partial\Omega_{\rho}
, it holds that

(28)

C_{R}(x)\cap\partial\Omega_{\rho}\,\,\text{ is the graph of a }C^{1}\text{ function}

and

(29)

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:\xi\in T_{\partial\Omega_{\rho}}(y),\,y\in C_{R}(x)\cap\partial\Omega_{\rho}\})\leq\eta\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}).

We claim that the above conditions are satisfied if we consider
\Omega
a bounded, open set with
C^{2}
boundary, and

\Omega_{\rho}:=\{x\in\Omega:\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)<\rho\}

for
\rho\in(\rho^{*},\rho^{*}+R^{*})
, where
\rho^{*}>0
is fixed and
R^{*}=R^{*}(\rho^{*})>0
. In particular, we fix
\rho^{*}
small enough so that
\{x\in\Omega:\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)=\rho\}
is a
(d-1)
-manifold of class
C^{1}
and there exists a
C^{1}
projection from
\Omega_{\rho}
onto
\partial\Omega
for every
\rho\leq 2\rho^{*}
.

Let
\eta^{\prime}=\eta^{\prime}(\eta,d)
be such that

(30)

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\Bigl(\Bigl\{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:|\xi_{d}|\leq\eta^{\prime}\Bigr\}\Bigr)\leq\eta\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}).

Since
\partial\Omega
is of class
C^{1}
, for every
x\in\partial\Omega
there exist
R>0
and a
C^{1}
function
\varphi
defined on the base of
C_{R}(x)
such that

C_{R}(x)\cap\partial\Omega=C_{R}(x)\cap\text{graph }\varphi.

By a rotation argument, we can further assume that
\nabla\varphi(x)=0
, and upon taking a smaller
R
, that

\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(C_{R}(x))}\leq\eta^{\prime}.

Moreover, since
\partial\Omega
is compact, we can select
R
as above independent of
x\in\partial\Omega
.

Since
\partial\Omega_{\rho^{*}}
is of class
C^{1}
and compact, using the same argument, we find a possibly smaller
R
with the property that for all
x\in\partial\Omega_{\rho^{*}}\cap\Omega
there exists a
C^{1}
function
\varphi_{\rho^{*}}
defined on the base of
C_{R}(x)
such that

C_{R}(x)\cap\partial\Omega_{\rho^{*}}=C_{R}(x)\cap\text{graph }\varphi_{\rho^{*}}\quad\text{ with }\quad\|\nabla\varphi_{\rho^{*}}\|_{L^{\infty}(C_{R}(x))}\leq\eta^{\prime}.

Finally, we assume without loss of generality that
R<\rho^{*}
.

Now, let
x\in\partial\Omega_{\rho^{*}}\cap\Omega
and
0<r<R/4
and note that

\{y\in C_{R-r}(x):\text{dist}(y,\text{ graph }\varphi_{\rho^{*}})=r\}=C_{R-r}(x)\cap\partial\Omega_{\rho^{*}+r}.

Then, letting
x_{r}\in\partial\Omega_{\rho^{*}+r}
denote the unique point such that
|x-x_{r}|=r
, we have that
C_{R-2r}(x_{r})\subset C_{R-r}(x)
, which implies

\{y\in C_{R-2r}(x_{r}):\text{dist}(y,\text{ graph }\varphi_{\rho^{*}})=r\}=C_{R-2r}(x_{r})\cap\partial\Omega_{\rho^{*}+r}.

We also note that there exists a
C^{1}
function
\varphi_{\rho^{*}+r}
such that

\{y\in C_{R-2r}(x_{r}):\text{dist}(y,\text{ graph }\varphi_{\rho^{*}})=r\}=C_{R-2r}(x_{r})\cap\text{ graph }\varphi_{\rho^{*}+r},

and

\|\nabla\varphi_{\rho^{*}+r}\|_{L^{\infty}(C_{R-2r}(x_{r}))}\leq\|\nabla\varphi_{\rho^{*}}\|_{L^{\infty}(C_{R-2r}(x_{r}))}\leq\|\nabla\varphi_{\rho^{*}}\|_{L^{\infty}(C_{R}(x))}.

Recalling that there exists a
C^{1}
projection from
\Omega_{\rho}
onto
\partial\Omega
for every
\rho\in(\rho^{*},\rho^{*}+R/4)
, the above observations prove that for every
\rho\in(\rho^{*},\rho^{*}+R/4)
and for every
x\in\partial\Omega_{\rho}
, it holds that

C_{R-2r}(x)\cap\partial\Omega_{\rho}=C_{R-2r}(x)\cap\text{graph }\varphi_{\rho},

where
\varphi_{\rho}
is a

As

C_{R/2}(x)\cap\partial\Omega_{\rho}=C_{R/2}(x)\cap\text{graph }\varphi_{\rho}

\|\nabla\varphi_{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}(C_{R/2}(x))}\leq\|\nabla\varphi_{\rho^{*}}\|_{L^{\infty}(C_{R/2}(x))}\leq\eta^{\prime}.

At this point, we conclude that our claim is proved with

. Indeed, (
28
)
follows by (

), and we are left with proving that (

) implies (

). To see this, note that for fixed

and

, (

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:\xi\in T_{\partial\Omega_{\rho}}(y),\,y\in C_{R/2}(x)\cap\partial\Omega_{\rho}\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\Bigl\{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:|\xi_{d}|=|\nabla\varphi_{\rho}(y_{1},...,y_{d-1})\cdot(\xi_{1},...,\xi_{d-1})|,\,y\in C_{R/2}(x)\cap\partial\Omega_{\rho}\Bigr\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\Bigl\{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:|\xi_{d}|\leq\eta^{\prime}\Bigr\};

therefore, by (

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:\xi\in T_{\partial\Omega_{\rho}}(y),\,y\in C_{R/2}(x)\cap\partial\Omega_{\rho}\})\leq\eta\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}).

Being the above estimates uniform in

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded, open set with

boundary, let

be an integer, and let

is satisfied. There exist positive constants

independent of

for every

\int_{\Omega}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}W(u)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}\varepsilon^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}u|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx\geq\delta\int_{\Omega}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}W(u)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\varepsilon^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}u|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx

for every

and

. In particular, letting

denote the same positive constants appearing in Theorem

and given

Proof.

By Theorem
1.1
, for every

for every

and

Let
\mathcal{N}:=\{\ell\in\{1,...,k-1\}:q_{\ell}\leq 0\}
and consider

\alpha_{\ell}>0
for all

\displaystyle\alpha_{\ell}\frac{W(u)}{\varepsilon}+q_{\ell}\varepsilon^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}u|_{\ell}+\alpha_{\ell}\varepsilon^{2k-1}|\nabla^{(k)}u|_{k}

\displaystyle=(1-\delta)\Bigl(\alpha_{\ell}\frac{W(u)}{\varepsilon}+\frac{q_{\ell}-\delta}{1-\delta}\varepsilon^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}u|_{\ell}+\alpha_{\ell}\varepsilon^{2k-1}|\nabla^{(k)}u|_{k}\Bigr)

We integrate the above equality and, taking into account (

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Bigl[\alpha_{\ell}\frac{W(u)}{\varepsilon}+q_{\ell}\varepsilon^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}u|_{\ell}+\alpha_{\ell}\varepsilon^{2k-1}|\nabla^{(k)}u|_{k}\Bigr]\,dx

Theorem 4.1
.

By strengthening the assumptions on

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\{E^{1}_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
be defined as in (
1
) on a bounded, open set

, let
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n}
be a positive sequence converging to

, and assume that
(H1)
and
(H2)
are satisfied.
If
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset H^{k}(I)
is a sequence such that
\sup_{n}E^{1}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n},I)<+\infty

\{u_{n_{j}}\}_{j}
and a function
u\in BV(I;\{-1,1\})
such that
u_{n_{j}}\to u
in

\{E^{1}_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}

\Gamma

to the functional

Proof.

It suffices to prove the statement in the case that

be an interval. Let

\sup_{n}E^{1}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n},I)<+\infty

) implies

As for the
\Gamma

, while a recovery sequence for the

-convergence is obtained exactly as in Proposition

in

∎

At this point, we prove an analogous compactness result for functionals (

with

for every

for every

. We say that two sequences

and

are

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume that a sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset L^{1}(\Omega)
is equi-integrable, and that for every
\delta>0
there exist sequences
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
and
\{w_{n}\}_{n}

\delta
-close to
\{u_{n}\}_{n}

is precompact in

for a.e.

is precompact in

.

We employ this result exactly as in
[
17
]
. For the convenience of the reader, we sketch the proof, and for simplicity, we consider only the case

when needed.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\{E_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
be defined as in (
3
), let
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n}
be a positive sequence converging to
0
, and assume that
(H1)
and
(H2)
are satisfied. If
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset H^{k}(\Omega)
is a sequence such that
\sup_{n}E_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n},\Omega)<+\infty
, then there exist a subsequence
\{u_{n_{j}}\}_{j}
and a function

such that,
u_{n_{j}}\to u
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
j\to+\infty
.

Proof.

For simplicity, in the first part of the proof we suppose

and that
\Omega
be the rectangle
I\times J

by essentially disjoint rectangles in order to reduce the proof to our simpler case.
Using the notation previously introduced, we recall that
\{u^{y}_{n}\}_{n}\subset H^{k}(J)
for a.e.

\{u^{z}_{n}\}_{n}\subset H^{k}(I)
for a.e.

. In particular, it holds

\frac{d^{k}u_{n}^{y}}{dz^{k}}(z)=\frac{\partial^{k}u_{n}}{\partial z^{k}}(x),\qquad\frac{d^{k}u_{n}^{z}}{dy^{k}}(y)=\frac{\partial^{k}u_{n}}{\partial y^{k}}(x)

for a.e.

. As a consequence, by the definition of the operatorial norm, we have
\|\nabla^{(k)}u_{n}\|_{k}\geq\max\Bigl\{\Bigl|\displaystyle\frac{d^{k}u_{n}^{y}}{dz^{k}}\Bigr|,\Bigl|\displaystyle\frac{d^{k}u_{n}^{z}}{dy^{k}}\Bigr|\Bigr\}

E_{\varepsilon}(u_{n},\Omega)\geq\int_{I}E^{1}_{\varepsilon}(u_{n}^{y},J)\,dy,\qquad E_{\varepsilon}(u_{n},\Omega)\geq\int_{J}E^{1}_{\varepsilon}(u_{n}^{z},I)\,dz.

Arguing as in the proof of Corollary
4.2
, we find that that
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
and
\{|u_{n}|^{2}\}_{n}
are equi-intergable sequences; therefore, we fix
\delta>0

\mathcal{L}^{2}(A)\leq\delta^{\prime}\mathcal{L}^{1}(J)\implies\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{A}(|u_{n}|+1)\,dx\leq\delta.

Letting

\sup_{n}E_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n},\Omega)

v_{n}^{y}(z):=\begin{cases}u_{n}^{y}(z)&\text{ if }E^{1}_{\varepsilon}(u_{n}^{y},J)\leq S/\delta^{\prime},\\

We also put

and we observe that

\mathcal{L}^{1}(Y_{n})\leq\mathcal{L}^{1}(\{y\in I:E^{1}_{\varepsilon}(u_{n}^{y},J)>S/\delta^{\prime}\})\leq\frac{\delta^{\prime}}{S}\int_{I}E^{1}_{\varepsilon}(u_{n}^{y},J)\,dy\leq\delta^{\prime},

where the last inequality follows by (
38

), implies that

\|u_{n}-v_{n}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\int_{Y_{n}\times J}(|u_{n}|+1)\,dx\leq\delta;

that is,
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
and
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
are
\delta
-close. Moreover, by construction and by (H
1
), we have that for every

is such that
\sup_{n}E^{1}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(v_{n}^{y},J)\leq S/\delta^{\prime}
. Hence, by Corollary
4.2
, this sequence is precompact in

, and thus in

.

A similar argument allows to find a sequence
\{w_{n}\}_{n}
that is
\delta
-close to
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
and such that, for a.e.

, the sequence
\{w_{n}^{z}\}_{n}
is precompact in
L^{1}(I)
. Therefore, by applying Proposition
4.3
we deduce that
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
is precompact in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. Finally, since

is equi-integrable, we deduce the precompactness in

.

To conclude, we prove, without further assumptions on

and
\Omega

converges to

. To see this, using the notation introduced in Section
2
, we prove that for every

, the one-dimensional slice

has bounded variation on the open set

for
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
-a.e.

\int_{\Omega^{e_{m}}}\#(S(u^{e_{m},y})\cap\Omega^{e_{m},y})\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)<+\infty.

\displaystyle+\infty>S

In the following,

We start by proving a lemma which allows to modify boundary conditions on a converging sequence at the cost of an asymptotically negligible amount of energy.

For every

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{n}(\widetilde{Q}_{n})}{r_{n}^{d-1}}=\frac{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n},\widetilde{Q}_{n})}{r_{n}^{d-1}}=\int_{\widetilde{Q}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}W(v_{n})+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}\tau_{n}^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}v_{n}|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]\,dy.

Since
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
satisfies all the hypotheses of Lemma
5.2
, we can apply such Lemma to obtain a sequence
\{w_{n}\}_{n}\subset H^{k}(\widetilde{Q})
which satisfies
(i)
-
(iii)
of said Lemma.

Noting that
\tau_{n}\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
, by (
67
), (
68
) and
(iii)
of Lemma
5.2
, it follows

(69)

f(0)\geq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\widetilde{Q}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}W(w_{n})+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}\tau_{n}^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}w_{n}|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]\,dy.

Now we observe that, by
(ii)
of Lemma
5.2
, putting

w_{n}:=\overline{u}^{e_{d}}_{\tau_{n}}\quad\text{ on }\,\,Q_{1}^{e_{d}}(0)\setminus\widetilde{Q}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\{w_{n}\}_{n}\subset\mathcal{A}^{e_{d}}_{\tau_{n}}
. Moreover, since
|\overline{u}^{e_{d}}(y)|=1
if
y\in\{z:|z\cdot e_{d}|>\frac{1}{8}\}
, it follows that
|\overline{u}_{\tau_{n}}^{e_{d}}(y)|=1
if
y\in\{z:|z\cdot e_{d}|>\frac{\tau_{n}}{8}\}
.

Therefore, by (
64
), we have

f(0)\geq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{Q_{1}^{e_{d}}(0)}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}W(w_{n})+\sum_{i=1}^{k}q_{\ell}\tau_{n}^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}w_{n}|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]\,dy,

and by the definition of
g
in (
10
), we conclude

f(0)\geq g(e_{d}).

Since the above argument holds for
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
-a.e.
x\in\partial^{*}E\cap\Omega
and every
\nu=\nu_{E}(x)
, we obtain

(70)

f(x)\geq g(\nu_{E}(x)).

Finally, by the inequality
\liminf_{n}\mu_{n}(\Omega)\geq\mu(\Omega)
we infer that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n},\Omega)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mu_{n}(\Omega)\geq\mu(\Omega)=\int_{\partial^{*}\!E\cap\Omega}f(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x)+\sigma(\Omega)

and, recalling that
\sigma(\Omega)\geq 0
and exploiting (
70
), this last inequality leads to

\lim_{n\to+\infty}F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n},\Omega)\geq\int_{\partial^{*}\!E\cap\Omega}f(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x)\geq\int_{\partial^{*}\!E\cap\Omega}g(\nu_{E}(x))d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x),

concluding the proof.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

We note that, thanks to (
69
), as a by-product of the above proof we have also showed that

\Gamma\text{-}\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u,\Omega)\geq\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\partial^{*}\!A\cap\Omega}\overline{g}(\nu_{A})\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}&\text{ if }u\in BV(\Omega;\{-1,1\}),\,\text{ with }u=2\chi_{A}-1,\\
+\infty&\text{ if }u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\setminus BV(\Omega;\{-1,1\}),\end{cases}

where

\overline{g}(\nu):=\inf\Big\{\int_{{\widetilde{Q}^{\nu}}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}W(v)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}\varepsilon^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}v|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx:v\in\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{\nu}_{\varepsilon},\,\varepsilon\in(0,1)\Big\}

for every
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and we have set

\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}^{\nu}:=\bigl\{v\in H^{k}(\widetilde{Q}^{\nu}):v=\overline{u}^{\nu}_{\varepsilon}\text{ near }\partial\widetilde{Q}^{\nu}\bigr\}.

Such observation will be useful in order to prove that the
\Gamma
-limit is strictly positive.

6.
Upper bound

In this section we conclude the proof of Theorem
1.3
by proving the limsup inequality. As in Section
5
,
C
denotes a generic positive constant that may change from line to line, but which is independent of the relevant parameters. We now state the main result of the section.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

be a bounded, open set with
C^{1}
boundary. Let
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n}
be a positive sequence converging to
0
and let
u\in BV(\Omega;\{-1,+1\})
. Assume that
(H1)-(H3)
are satisfied. Then there exists a sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset H^{k}(\Omega)
converging to
u
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
n\to+\infty
and such that

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n})\leq F_{0}(u).

Proof.

We subdivide the proof in three parts.

Step 1.

First, we consider the case where
\Omega
has Lipschitz boundary (and not necessarily
C^{1}
) and
u=2\chi_{A}-1
, where

\displaystyle A:=\{x\in\Omega:(x-x_{0})\cdot\nu>0\}

for some
x_{0}\in\Omega
and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
such that

(71)

\nu_{\Omega}(x)\perp\nu\ \text{ for every }x\in\partial\Omega\text{ with }|(x-x_{0})\cdot\nu|\text{ small enough},

recalling that
\nu_{\Omega}(x)
is the inner unit normal to
\Omega
at
x
. For the sake of exposition, we assume
x_{0}=0
and
\nu=e_{d}
, the other cases being analogous, so that for every
x\in\Omega
we have
u(x)=u_{0}^{e_{d}}(x)
, where by definition
u_{0}^{e_{d}}(x)=\text{sgn}(x\cdot e_{d})
, and

F_{0}(u)=g(e_{d})\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\left(\Omega\cap\{x_{d}=0\}\right).

For every
\eta>0
, there exist
0<\varepsilon_{0}<1
and
v\in\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon_{0}}^{e_{d}}
such that
v=\overline{u}^{e_{d}}_{\varepsilon_{0}}
in a neighborhood of
\partial Q^{e_{d}}_{1}
, and

(72)

\int_{Q_{1}^{e_{d}}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{0}}W(v)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}\varepsilon_{0}^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}v|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]dx\leq g(e_{d})+\eta.

By Proposition
2.4
, upon rotating
Q^{e_{d}}_{1}
leaving
e_{d}
fixed, we may assume that
Q^{e_{d}}_{1}=Q:=(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})^{d}
. We then extend
v
to a function in
H^{k}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
which is periodic in the first
d-1
variables; that is, such that

(73)

v(x)=v(x+e_{i}),

for all
i\in\{1,...,d-1\}
. We also set
\Omega_{n}:=\{x\in\Omega:|x_{d}|\leq\tfrac{\varepsilon_{n}}{2\varepsilon_{0}}\}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

We claim that a recovery sequence for
u
is given by the sequence of functions
\{u_{n}\}\subset H^{k}(\Omega)
defined by

(74)

u_{n}(x):=\begin{cases}v\big(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}x}{\varepsilon_{n}}\big)&\text{if }x\in\Omega_{n},\\
u(x)&\text{if }x\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{n}.\end{cases}

To prove the claim, we first show that

(75)

\|u_{n}-u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0\text{ as }n\to+\infty.

To this aim, we set
\Omega^{\prime}:=\big\{x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}:(x^{\prime},0)\in\Omega\big\}
and observe that by (
71
), it holds

\Omega\cap\Omega_{n}=\Omega^{\prime}\times\Bigl[-\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{2\varepsilon_{0}},\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{2\varepsilon_{0}}\Bigr]

for
n
sufficiently large.

Using the change of variables
(x^{\prime},x_{d})\to(x^{\prime},\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{\varepsilon_{0}}x_{d})
, by Fubini’s Theorem and (
74
) we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{n}}|u_{n}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\int_{-\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{2\varepsilon_{0}}}^{\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{2\varepsilon_{0}}}\big|v\Bigl(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}x^{\prime}}{\varepsilon_{n}},\frac{\varepsilon_{0}x_{d}}{\varepsilon_{n}}\Bigr)\big|^{2}dx_{d}dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{\varepsilon_{0}}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\big|v\Bigl(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}x^{\prime}}{\varepsilon_{n}},x_{d}\Bigr)\big|^{2}dx^{\prime}dx_{d}.

We observe that, since
v\in H^{k}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

f(y)=\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|v(y,x_{d})\right|^{2}dx_{d}\quad\text{ for every }y\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}

is integrable on
\Omega^{\prime}
and that, by definition of
v
,
it is periodic. We may then apply the Riemann-Lebesgue Lemma to the function
f
to obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big|v\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}x^{\prime}}{\varepsilon_{n}},x_{d}\Big)\big|^{2}dx_{d}\,dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\int_{(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})^{d-1}}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|v(y^{\prime},x_{d})\right|^{2}dx_{d}\,dy^{\prime}\,dx^{\prime}

(76)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}^{d-1}(\Omega^{\prime})\|v\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2},

which implies

(77)

\int_{\Omega_{n}}|u_{n}|^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{\varepsilon_{0}}\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q)}.

From this inequality, (
74
), and the fact that
u
takes values only in
\{\pm 1\}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|u_{n}-u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq 2\int_{\Omega_{n}}|u_{n}|^{2}\,dx+2\mathcal{L}^{d}(\Omega_{n})\leq C\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{\varepsilon_{0}}+2\mathcal{L}^{d}(\Omega_{n}),

which tends to
0
as
n\to+\infty
. This inequality proves (
75
).

We now show that
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
is a recovery sequence for
u
. To this aim, we first note that for every
x\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{n}
we have

u_{n}(x)=u(x)\in\{-1,1\}\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\nabla^{(\ell)}u_{n}(x)=0\ \text{ for every }\ell\in\{1,...,k\},

while, if
x\in\Omega_{n}
, we have

\nabla^{(\ell)}u_{n}(x)=\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{\varepsilon_{n}}\Big)^{\ell}\nabla^{(\ell)}v\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}x}{\varepsilon_{n}}\Big)\quad\text{for every }\ell\in\{1,...,k\}.

Then, we use (H1), (
74
), Fubini’s Theorem, and a change of variable to obtain

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{n}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}W(u_{n})+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}\varepsilon_{n}^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}u_{n}|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\int_{-\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{2\varepsilon_{0}}}^{\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{2\varepsilon_{0}}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}W\Big(v\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}x^{\prime}}{\varepsilon_{n}},\frac{\varepsilon_{0}x_{d}}{\varepsilon_{n}}\Big)\Big)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}\varepsilon_{n}^{2\ell}\big|\nabla^{(\ell)}v\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}x}{\varepsilon_{n}},\frac{\varepsilon_{0}x_{d}}{\varepsilon_{n}}\Big)\big|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]dx_{d}\,dx^{\prime}

(78)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{0}}W\Big(v\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}x^{\prime}}{\varepsilon_{n}},x_{d}\Big)\Bigl)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}\varepsilon_{0}^{2\ell-1}\big|\nabla^{(\ell)}v\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}x^{\prime}}{\varepsilon_{n}},x_{d}\Big)\big|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]dx_{d}dx^{\prime}

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Since
v
satisfies (
72
), the function
h\colon\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined for
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
by

h(y):=\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{0}}W(v(y^{\prime},x_{d}))+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}\varepsilon_{0}^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}v(y^{\prime},x_{d})|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]dx_{d}

is integrable and, by (
73
), is also periodic. Therefore, we may apply once again the Riemann-Lebesgue Lemma to obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{0}}W\Big(v\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}x^{\prime}}{\varepsilon_{n}},x_{d}\Big)\Big)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}\varepsilon_{0}^{2\ell-1}\big|\nabla^{(\ell)}v\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}x^{\prime}}{\varepsilon_{n}},x_{d}\Big)\big|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]dx_{d}\,dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}^{d-1}(\Omega^{\prime})\int_{Q}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{0}}W(v)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}\varepsilon_{0}^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}v|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]dx.

Combining this equality with (
78
) and (
72
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n})=\mathcal{L}^{d-1}(\Omega^{\prime})\int_{Q}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{0}}W(v)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}\varepsilon_{0}^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}v|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]dx\leq F_{0}(u)+\mathcal{L}^{d-1}(\Omega^{\prime})\eta.

Since
\eta
was arbitrary, a diagonal argument allows to prove the thesis in this special case.

Step 2.

We now assume that
u=2\chi_{A}-1
, with
A=P\cap\Omega
and
P
polyhedral. This means that
\partial P=\bigcup_{i=1}^{M}H_{i}\cup F
, where the sets
(H_{i})_{i=1}^{M}
are pairwise disjoint and relatively open convex polyhedra of dimension
d-1
, while
F
is the union of a finite number of convex polyhedra of dimension
d-2
. In particular, there exists
\nu_{1},...,\nu_{M}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and
x_{1},...,x_{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

H_{i}\subseteq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:(x-x_{i})\cdot\nu_{i}=0\},

and each
\nu_{i}
is the inner unit normal to
\partial P
on
H_{i}
. For simplicity,
we may also assume that
\partial\Omega\cap\partial P
is the union of a finite number of
C^{1}
manifolds of dimension
d-2
.

Let us fix
0<\sigma<1
and let
H_{1}^{\prime},...,H_{M}^{\prime}
relatively open subsets of
H_{1},...,H_{M}
with
(d-2)
-dimensional boundary of class
C^{1}
, such that

\big\{x\in H_{i}\cap\Omega:\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega\cup F)\geq\tfrac{\sigma}{2}\big\}\subseteq H_{i}^{\prime}\subseteq\overline{H_{i}^{\prime}}\subseteq H_{i}.

Fix
0<\eta<\frac{\sigma}{2}
and define for each
i\in\{1,...,M\}

\Omega_{i}:=\{x+t\nu_{i}:x\in H_{i}^{\prime},\,t\in(-\eta,\eta)\}.

We can choose
\eta
so small that the sets
\Omega_{1},...,\Omega_{M}
are pairwise disjoint.
Since each
\Omega_{i},
satisfies (
71
), by the previous step, we obtain sequences
\{u_{n}^{i}\}_{n}\subset H^{k}(\Omega_{i})
such that

(79)

u_{n}^{i}\to u\ \text{in}\ L^{2}(\Omega_{i}),

u_{n}^{i}(x)=u(x)\,\,\text{ for every }x\in\big\{x\in\overline{\Omega}_{i}:\text{dist}(x,H_{i})\geq\tfrac{\varepsilon_{n}}{2\varepsilon^{i}_{0}}\big\},

and

(80)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n}^{i},\Omega_{i})\leq\left(g(\nu_{i})+\eta\right)\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\Omega_{i}\cap H_{i}),

where for every
x\in\Omega_{i}
it holds

u^{i}_{n}(x):=\begin{cases}v^{i}\big(\frac{\varepsilon^{i}_{0}x}{\varepsilon_{n}}\big)&\text{if }|(x-x_{i})\cdot\nu_{i}|\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{2\varepsilon_{0}^{i}},\\
u(x)&\text{if }|(x-x_{i})\cdot\nu_{i}|>\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{2\varepsilon_{0}^{i}},\end{cases}

and, for each
i\in\{1,...,M\}
,
v^{i}
and
\varepsilon_{0}^{i}
are such that

\int_{Q_{1}^{\nu_{i}}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{i}_{0}}W(v)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}(\varepsilon_{0}^{i})^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}v|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]dx\leq g(\nu_{i})+\eta.

In order to present the candidate recovery sequence, we let
(\Psi_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
be a radial smooth convolution kernel, with
\text{supp}\Psi_{\varepsilon}\subset B_{\varepsilon}(0)
and
\int\Psi_{\varepsilon}\,dx=1
for every
\varepsilon>0
.
We extend
u
to the whole
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by setting
u:=2\chi_{P}-1
and introduce smooth functions
\widetilde{u}_{n}
defined for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
by

\widetilde{u}_{n}:=u*\Psi_{\varepsilon_{n}},

where
*
denotes the operation of convolution.
Observe that these functions satisfy the
following conditions

(81)

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}_{n}(x)=u(x)\,\text{ for every }x\in\{x\in\Omega:\text{dist}(x,\partial P)\geq{\varepsilon_{n}}\},

(82)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{u}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 1\,\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\,\||\nabla^{(\ell)}\widetilde{u}_{n}|_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{\ell}}\text{ for every }\ell\in\{1,...,k\}.

For
\tau\in(0,1)
we set

U_{\tau}:=\{x\in\Omega:\text{dist}(x,F\cup(\partial\Omega\cap\partial P))\leq\tau\}

and choose cut-off functions
\phi_{\sigma}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d};[0,1])
such that

(83)

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt\begin{cases}\phi_{\sigma}(x)=1\quad&\text{if }x\in U_{\sigma},\\
\phi_{\sigma}(x)=0\quad&\text{if }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus U_{2\sigma},\\
\||\nabla^{(\ell)}\phi_{\sigma}|_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\sigma^{-\ell}\quad&\text{ for every }\ell\in\{1,...,k\}.\end{cases}

We claim that the sequence of functions
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset H^{k}(\Omega)
defined by

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188ptu_{n}:=\begin{cases}(1-\phi_{\sigma})u_{n}^{i}+\phi_{\sigma}\widetilde{u}_{n}\quad&\text{in }\overline{\Omega}_{i}\text{ for }\ i\in\{1,...M\},\\
\widetilde{u}_{n}\quad&\text{in }\Omega^{\prime}:=\Omega\setminus\cup_{i=1}^{M}\overline{\Omega}_{i}\end{cases}

is a recovery sequence for
u
.

The fact that
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset H^{k}(\Omega)
follows from the same argument used in
[
11
, Theorem 1.3, Substep 2B]
. Also, (
79
)
and the fact that
\widetilde{u}_{n}\to u
in

imply that
u_{n}\to u
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
n\to+\infty
.

We now study the asymptotic behaviour of the energy of
\{u_{n}\}
. First, we show that
F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n},\Omega^{\prime})
is negligible. To this aim, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we set

(84)

\mathcal{R}_{n}:=\Big\{x\in\Omega:\text{dist}(x,\partial P)\leq\max\big\{\varepsilon_{n},\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{2\varepsilon^{1}_{0}},...,\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{2\varepsilon^{M}_{0}}\big\}\Big\}.

We observe that
\Omega^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{R}_{n}\subset U_{\sigma}
for
n
large enough and that
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\partial P\cap U_{\sigma})\leq C\sigma
. Hence, by the definition of
\Omega^{\prime}
, (
81
), and (
82
), we have

(85)

F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n},\Omega^{\prime})=\int_{\Omega^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{R}_{n}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}W(\widetilde{u}_{n})+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}|q_{\ell}|\varepsilon_{n}^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}\widetilde{u}_{n}|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]dx\leq C\sigma.

We now study the energetic contribution of the remaining terms. We fix
i\in\{1,...,M\}
and compute

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n},\Omega_{i})

\displaystyle=F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n}^{i},\Omega_{i}\setminus U_{2\sigma})+F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n},\Omega_{i}\cap(U_{2\sigma}\setminus U_{\sigma}))+F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\widetilde{u}_{n},\Omega_{i}\cap U_{\sigma})

\displaystyle\leq F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n}^{i},\Omega_{i}\setminus U_{2\sigma})+\int_{\Omega_{i}\cap(U_{2\sigma}\setminus U_{\sigma})}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}W(u_{n})+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}|q_{\ell}|\varepsilon_{n}^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}u_{n}|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx

\displaystyle\quad\,\,\,+\int_{\Omega_{i}\cap U_{\sigma}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}W(\widetilde{u}_{n})+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}|q_{\ell}|\varepsilon_{n}^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}\widetilde{u}_{n}|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{K}_{n}^{1}+\mathcal{K}_{n}^{2}+\mathcal{K}_{n}^{3}.

We separately investigate the three terms
\mathcal{K}^{1}_{n},

\mathcal{K}^{2}_{n}
, and
\mathcal{K}^{3}_{n}
, starting from
\mathcal{K}^{1}_{n}
. Recalling that
q_{k}=1
, we observe that

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{n}^{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{i}\setminus U_{2\sigma}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}W(u_{n}^{i})+\varepsilon_{n}^{2k-1}|\nabla^{(k)}u_{n}^{i}|_{k}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx+\int_{\Omega_{i}\setminus U_{2\sigma}}\Bigl[\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}q_{\ell}\varepsilon_{n}^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}{u}_{n}^{i}|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega_{i}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}W(u_{n}^{i})+\varepsilon_{n}^{2k-1}|\nabla^{(k)}u_{n}^{i}|_{k}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx+\int_{\Omega_{i}}\Bigl[\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}q_{\ell}\varepsilon_{n}^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}u_{n}^{i}|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx

\displaystyle\,\quad-\int_{\Omega_{i}\cap U_{2\sigma}}\Bigl[\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}q_{\ell}\varepsilon_{n}^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}{u}_{n}^{i}|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx

(86)

\displaystyle\leq F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n}^{i},\Omega_{i})+\int_{\Omega_{i}\cap U_{2\sigma}}\Bigl[\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}|q_{\ell}|\varepsilon_{n}^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}{u}_{n}^{i}|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx.

By the periodicity of
u_{n}^{i}
with respect to the variables tangential to
H_{i}
, we may argue as in
Step 1
, using Fubini’s Theorem and the Riemann-Lebesgue Lemma to deduce that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega_{i}\cap U_{2\sigma}}\Bigl[\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}|q_{\ell}|\varepsilon_{n}^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}{u}_{n}^{i}|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx\leq C\sigma\int_{Q_{1}^{\nu_{i}}}\Big[\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}v^{i}|_{\ell}^{2}\Big]\,dx\leq C\sigma.

Hence, it follows from (
86
) and (
80
) that

(87)

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{K}_{n}^{1}\leq(g(\nu_{i})+\eta)\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(H_{i}\cap\Omega_{i})+C\sigma.

We now investigate the term
\mathcal{K}^{2}_{n}
. We begin observing that for every
\ell\in\{1,...,k\}
and multi-index
\alpha
of order
|\alpha|=\ell
, we have

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}u_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\leq\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{\beta}\Big(\partial^{\beta}(1-\phi_{\sigma})\partial^{\alpha-\beta}u_{n}^{i}+\partial^{\beta}\phi_{\sigma}\partial^{\alpha-\beta}\widetilde{u}_{n}\Big)

\displaystyle=(1-\phi_{\sigma})\partial^{\alpha}u^{i}_{n}+\phi_{\sigma}\partial^{\alpha}\widetilde{u}_{n}+\sum_{0<\beta\leq\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{\beta}\big(\partial^{\beta}\phi_{\sigma}\,\partial^{\alpha-\beta}(\widetilde{u}_{n}-u_{n}^{i})\big),

which by (
83

Proposition 7.3

Proof.

on each interval

We apply the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus and Hölder’s inequality to obtain

Therefore, there exists

such that, up to subsequences,

converges locally uniformly on compact subsets of

to

for all
\ell\in\{0,...,k-1\}
, with the convention that

, and

weakly in

.

As for the lower semicontinuity of the energy, we first note that, as a consequence of Corollary

, we have

\int_{-\infty}^{-T}\Bigl[W(u_{n})+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}|u_{n}^{(\ell)}|^{2}\Bigr]\,dt+\int_{T}^{+\infty}\Bigl[W(u_{n})+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}|u_{n}^{(\ell)}|^{2}\Bigr]\,dt\geq 0

for every

; moreover, by Fatou’s lemma and the lower semicontinuity of the

(103)

\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\Bigl[W(u_{n})+|u_{n}^{(k)}|^{2}\Bigr]\,dt\geq\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\Bigl[W(u)+|u^{(k)}|^{2}\Bigr]\,dt.

), (

), and (

\displaystyle m=\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\Bigl[W(u_{n})+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}|u_{n}^{(\ell)}|^{2}\Bigr]\,dt

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{-T}^{T}\Bigl[W(u_{n})+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}|u_{n}^{(\ell)}|^{2}\Bigr]\,dt

in (

) by Dominated Convergence Theorem to obtain

m\geq\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\Bigl[W(u)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}|u^{(\ell)}|^{2}\Bigr]\,dt.

If
\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}u(t)=\pm 1
, the proof is completed; while, if
\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}u(t)=\mp 1
, a minimizer is
u(-t)
. For this reason, in order to conclude, we prove that both
\lim_{t\to-\infty}u(t)
and

exist, and that
\{\lim_{t\to-\infty}u(t),\lim_{t\to+\infty}u(t)\}=\{-1,1\}

As a first step we prove the existence of the limit at
+\infty
, the other case being analogous. To see this, it is useful to observe that

\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}u^{\prime}(t)=0.

If

or

which is a contradiction. We are left with considering the case there exist real numbers

\{x_{n}\}_{n},\{y_{n}\}_{n}
such that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}x_{n}=+\infty,\quad\lim_{n\to+\infty}y_{n}=+\infty,\quad\lim_{n\to+\infty}u(x_{n})=a,\quad\lim_{n\to+\infty}u(y_{n})=b.

Without loss of generality, we can suppose

\quad x_{n}<y_{n},\quad u(x_{n})\leq a+\frac{b-a}{8},\quad u(y_{n})\geq b-\frac{b-a}{8}\qquad\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N};

moreover, by the continuity of

, we can also assume that both

and

(a,b)
.

If there exists
\{n_{j}\}_{j}
such that

for every

, then, by Lagrange’s Theorem, there exists

such that

|u^{\prime}(z_{n_{j}})|\geq\frac{|u(y_{n_{j}})-u(x_{n_{j}})|}{2}\geq\frac{3}{8}(b-a),

hence,

). Otherwise, we may assume that

If

large enough. Recalling that the interval

does not contain the points

, the above observation and (H

which is a contradiction, and concludes the proof of the existence of the limits at infinity.
We finally prove that they are different.

By contradiction suppose
\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}u(t)=-1
. By Lemma
3.2
in
[
10
]
and by the convergence

in

, we find points

Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\!\Bigl[W(u_{n_{j}})\!+\!\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}|u_{n_{j}}^{(\ell)}|^{2}\Bigr]\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{t_{j}}\!\Bigl[W(u_{n_{j}})+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}|u_{n_{j}}^{(\ell)}|^{2}\Bigr]\,dt+\int_{t_{j}}^{+\infty}\!\Bigl[W(u_{n_{j}})\!+\!\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}|u_{n_{j}}^{(\ell)}|^{2}\Bigr]dt

\displaystyle v^{(\ell)}(0)=0\text{ for all }\ell\in\{0,...,k-1\},

\displaystyle v^{(\ell)}(1)=u_{\ell}\text{ for all }\ell\in\{0,...,k-1\}\Bigr\},

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle=\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\Bigl[W(u_{n_{j}})+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}|u_{n_{j}}^{(\ell)}|^{2}\Bigr]\,dt

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{t_{j}}\Bigl[W(u_{n_{j}})+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}|u_{n_{j}}^{(\ell)}|^{2}\Bigr]\,dt+m-\widetilde{m}(u_{n_{j}}(t_{j}),...,u^{(k-1)}_{n_{j}}(t_{j}))

which implies

therefore, we deduce that
that

Remark 7.4
.

Restoring the general dimension

, we may deal with the case of special norms on tensors that are ‘compatible with slicing’ upon supposing the non-negativity of the coefficients
q_{1},...,q_{k-1}
. More precisely, we suppose that for every
\ell\in\{1,...,k\}
the norm
|\cdot|_{\ell}
satisfies

(107)

|T|_{\ell}\geq|T(\xi,...,\xi)|\qquad\text{ for every }\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},

and we assume that
q_{\ell}\geq 0
for every
\ell\in\{1,...,k-1\}
. Also in this instance, the density energy
g
defined in (
10
) is the constant
m
given by (
95
).

The argument is similar to the one in the proof of Proposition
6.2
. For fixed
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and
\eta>0
, let
v
and
\varepsilon
be such that

g(\nu)+\eta\geq\int_{{Q_{1}^{\nu}}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}W(v)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}\varepsilon^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}v|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx.

Applying Fubini’s Theorem, (
107
), and the slicing properties of Sobolev functions, we obtain

\displaystyle g(\nu)+\eta

\displaystyle\geq\int_{{Q_{1}^{\nu}}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}W(v)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}\varepsilon^{2\ell-1}|\nabla^{(\ell)}v|_{\ell}^{2}\Bigr]\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{Q_{1}^{\nu}\cap\nu^{\perp}}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}W(v^{\nu,y}(t))+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}\varepsilon^{2\ell-1}|(v^{\nu,y})^{(\ell)}(t)|^{2}\Bigr]\,dt\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)

\displaystyle\geq\inf\Big\{\int_{-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}}^{\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}}\Bigl[W(v)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}q_{\ell}|v^{(\ell)}|^{2}\Bigr]\,dx,\ v\in H^{k}\bigl(\bigl(-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon},\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\bigr)\bigr),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad v(t)=\text{sgn}(t)\text{ if }|t|>M\text{ for some }M\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\bigr)\Big\}

\displaystyle=m\Bigl(\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\Bigr),

where the second inequality also follows by the assumption

for every

. By the arbitrariness of

. The converse inequality is obtained using as a test function for

Current Conservation in the Self-Consistent Josephson Junction
Simon Krekels, Vukan Levajac, Kristof Moors, George Simion, Bart Sorée

Abstract
Conventional treatments of Josephson junctions (JJs) are generally not current-conserving. We introduce a numerical method for the self-consistent treatment of quasi-1D JJs with current conservation, with phase gradient of the order parameter in the leads matching the Josephson current through the weak link. We compare our method to standard methods and calculate the current-phase relationship (CPR) for superconductor–normal metal–superconductor JJs with different gate voltages applied to the normal metal. We show that our approach can weaken or even reverse forward skewedness of the CPR.



The phase-dependent, dissipationless current through a Josephson Junction (JJ) is its key property

;

and the reason for its wide use in superconducting quantum technologies

;

;

;

;

.
Despite being of great practical interest, a theoretical description of the Josephson effect from a microscopic perspective remains very challenging

;

.
A solid understanding of the current-phase relation (CPR) is essential, since it is the precise shape of the CPR that is the source of the nonlinear inductance, which in turn provides anharmonicity to qubits and phase sensitivity to SQUIDs and that makes the JJ so uniquely important
Blais et al. 2021
.
It is well known that these CPRs are often not exactly sinusoidal, but that they may be skewed, and take even more exotic shapes

;
Golubov et al.
;
Levajac et al.
.
Thus, a reliable method to calculate CPRs is necessary, as fabrication and measurement techniques are rapidly advancing

;
Chen et al. 2024
;
Nanda et al. 2017
;
Babich et al. 2023
;
Van Damme et al.
;
Kringhøj et al.
;
Willsch et al.
.
Standard methods for the calculation of current-phase relations include the use of the tunneling formalism between two superconducting reservoirs, or using the Bogoliubov-de Gennes (BdG) formalism with step function potentials
Likharev 1979
;
Beenakker 1992
.

In BdG theory, the superconducting gap
\Delta(\mathbf{r})
must be calculated
self-consistently
for charge to be conserved
de Gennes 1999
;
Sols and Ferrer 1994
;
Sonin
.
This issue was pointed out specifically for JJs by Sols and Ferrer
Sols and Ferrer 1994
, but a resolution to the issue was left open in the scope of BdG formalism, and instead an analysis based on Ginzburg-Landau theory was pursued.
Even recently, there has been debate about how to treat current continuity in the SNS junction
Sonin
;
Thuneberg 2023
;
Sonin 2023
;
Thuneberg 2024
, but no current-conserving solutions were proposed.
Here, we tackle this issue in the BdG framework and present a microscopic treatment of JJs with current conservation.

Previous efforts to describe the Josephson current microscopically have either ignored the self-consistent condition
Kulik 1969
;
Ishii 1970
;
Sonin
, or attempted to calculate
\Delta
without current conservation in mind
Black-Schaffer and Doniach
;
Rodríguez-Mota et al.
, leading to frustration in the self-consistent solving method, and lack of current conservation.
As we will show, this frustration is clearly manifested in the nonzero current source term which vanishes when the system is self-consistent.
The effect of the absence of current conservation is negligible in regimes where the current density in the leads is very small compared to the critical current, such as in a constriction setup, where the current density in the leads can become vanishingly small.
However, for some setups, neglecting current conservation is not justified.
This is particularly true for JJ geometries with large Josephson currents.
Examples include junctions with comparable widths for leads and weak links
Lee et al.
, and semiconductor nanowire-based JJs
Spanton et al. 2017
;
Li et al.
;
Tiira et al.
.

The most striking consequence of the current-conserving treatment of SNS junctions is the appearance of backward-skewed CPRs in some regimes.
Backward-skewed CPRs have been obtained from theory before, notably also in the context of current conservation

;
Kim and Hong 2005
;
Spuntarelli et al. 2007
;
Spuntarelli et al. 2010
;
Golubov et al.
, but the reason for their appearance remains elusive.
Such CPRs have also been experimentally observed in InAs-Al nanowire JJs
Spanton et al. 2017
.
We find marked similarities between the CPRs observed by varying gate voltage in InAS nanowires and those obtained from our numerical calculations.

This observation implies that conventional approaches to JJ modeling invariably violate current conservation, either by ignoring self-consistency altogether or by imposing fixed phases in the leads.

The conventional approach to model an SNS junction in BdG theory is to define an ansatz for the form of
\Delta

\Delta(\mathbf{r})=\begin{cases}\Delta_{0}e^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{L}}&x<-d/2\\
0&-d/2\leq x\leq d/2\\
\Delta_{0}e^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{R}}&x>d/2\end{cases}

(8)

is the length of the junction, as schematically shown in Fig.
1
.
It is then standard practice to either fix the phase in the leads or at the boundary and let
\Delta
relax self-consistently (i.e., re-calculate
\Delta

3

We can confirm our suspicions that current is not conserved in such a system with fixed phases by explicitly calculating the current [Eq. (
6
)] and source term [Eq. (
7
)] after
|\Delta|
has reached self-consistency up to some tolerance, and
\varphi(\mathbf{r})
is kept fixed in the leads.
The result of such a calculation is shown in Fig.
1

Indeed, we see a current being carried within the normal metal, and exponentially decreasing as it enters the bulk.
The source term clearly displays a source and sink around the junction, manifestly not conserving current.

As noted in Ref.
Riedel et al.
, the phase of the self-consistent pairing potential tends to a linear profile, which corresponds to a constant current throughout the bulk.
Since studies of JJ physics often consider varying phase drops over the junction, this tendency to linearity is frustrated, either by keeping the phase fixed in the bulk
Black-Schaffer and Doniach
;
Spuntarelli et al. 2007
;
Spuntarelli et al. 2010
, or by limiting the number of self-consistent iterations, e.g., until
|\Delta|
has reached self-consistency.
In any case, the result is a system in which the phase profile is not entirely self-consistent, and thereby does not conserve current.

Implementing the total phase drop over the system as a boundary condition would eventually yield a self-consistent, current conserving solution to the BdG system, but would severely decrease the size of the phase drop over the weak link, when defined as the difference of the phases immediately left and right of the junction.
Indeed, in absence of a fixed phase in the leads, even the definition of the phase drop becomes a subtle issue.
Failure to recognize this issue can lead to skewed plots of CPRs where the current is plotted against the phase difference between the boundaries, not the phase drop over the junction itself.
From here on, we take the phase drop over the junction to mean the difference in phase immediately right and left of the junction:
\delta\varphi\equiv\varphi(d/2)-\varphi(-d/2)
.

Current-conserving self-consistency scheme
—
The tendency of the phase profile towards linearity is the response of the system to the lack of current conservation.
Recognizing this, we propose a new initial guess for the phase profile and an additional step in the self-consistent iteration.
The suggested ansatz incorporates a constant phase gradient
q
in the leads,

\Delta(\mathbf{r})=\begin{cases}\Delta_{0}e^{-\mathrm{i}\,\delta\varphi/2}e^{\mathrm{i}q(x+d/2)}&x<-d/2\\
0&-d/2\leq x\leq d/2\\
\Delta_{0}e^{\mathrm{i}\,\delta\varphi/2}e^{\mathrm{i}q(x-d/2)}&x>d/2\end{cases}

(9)

where the phase gradient
q
should ensure that the bulk current matches the Josephson current through the weak link.

We can exploit this monotonic dependence of
I_{\mathrm{bulk}}

q
to match the currents in the weak link and in the leads, even without knowledge of the precise relation between the current and
q

By letting
\Delta
relax self-consistently with a linear phase gradient
q
applied at the boundaries, the self-consistent solution tends to a current-conserving solution.

Concretely, we implement two modifications in the BdG self-consistency cycle: additional boundary conditions on
\Delta(\mathbf{r})
with a fixed phase gradient
q
that can be tuned so that the bulk current matches the junction current; and, using the vanishing of the source term and the matching of the currents as halting criteria for the algorithm.
We apply the boundary conditions in a region of
N_{B}
sites at each boundary, with sources and sinks appearing there being considered outside of the system (see Supp. Mat. for details).
This approach ensures that the physical requirement of current conservation is satisfied throughout the system.

The result of this calculation (details in Supp. Mat.), shown in Fig.
1
(solid lines) shows a marked difference with the regular “self-consistent” solution.
The source term is indeed zero throughout the system, and the current is now uniform across the junction.

The CPR is also significantly modified by the current consistency requirement, most notably exhibiting backward skewness rather than the forward skewness typically seen in conventional SNS junctions (Fig.
2
, top panel).
This reversal aligns with predictions from Sols & Ferrer using Ginzburg-Landau theory
Sols and Ferrer 1994
, though it contradicts standard SNS CPR expectations.
The Fourier analysis reveals suppression of higher harmonics in the current-conserving CPR.
The underlying mechanism involves the phase gradient
q
, which boosts all bulk states and proportionally lowers the energy of the lowest bulk excitations, eventually closing the gap at
q\approx\xi^{-1}

de Gennes 1999
, which is when the bulk superconductor reaches its critical current.
This energy shift has profound consequences for the Andreev spectrum.
As Fig.
2
(bottom panel) demonstrates, the Andreev bound states in the current-consistent approach look qualitatively different from those in step-profile or fixed-phase calculations.
The Andreev states mix with the descending bulk states, substantially modifying their energies and current-carrying properties.

For SIS junctions, the currents carried by the junction are much lower.
Therefore,
q
is also significantly smaller than in the SNS junction, and the modification of the CPR becomes negligible.
We can model SIS junctions in the framework presented here by raising a potential
V(\mathbf{r})
inside the normal metal, effectively turning the normal metal into an insulator.

Applying Gate Voltage
—
We can gain some insight into the shape of the current-consistent CPR by calculating the CPR while applying a potential barrier of varying strength inside the normal metal part:
V(x)=-V_{G}
if
|x|<d/2
and zero otherwise.
This is analogous to applying a gate voltage to a nanowire junction.
The chemical potential
\mu
is generally kept constant throughout the system, but by applying a potential within the normal metal effectively modifies the chemical potential to
\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}=\mu+V_{G}
.
This way the number of available conduction electrons can be tuned.
The resulting CPRs are shown in Fig.
3
.
We note that we see the appearance of backward skewed CPRs for a range of
0.5\mu<V_{G}<1\mu
.
For other values of
V_{G}
, the CPR is skewed forward.
Comparing directly with the CPRs obtained from the fixed-phase method (Fig.
3
, middle panel), we see that the shapes of the CPRs are significantly modified.
We note that Fig.
3
reproduces most of the qualitative behavior observed in measurement of InAs nanowire junctions
Spanton et al. 2017
, most notably backward skewedness in a certain regime of
V_{G}
, forward skew otherwise, and a non-monotonic evolution of the maximal current when approaching depletion (
\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}=0
).

Conclusion
—
We propose and evaluate a current conserving self-consistency scheme for the BdG Josephson junction, and compare its results to standard methods.
The examples shown imply that in cases where the current density in the leads is non-negligible, significant modification to the CPR and the Andreev states are expected.
The bulk states now also carry current and descend below the superconducting energy gap as a result, severely affecting the Andreev spectrum.
When current densities are small compared to the critical current, the qualitative change is small, but in e.g. nanowires this is not guaranteed to be the case
Spanton et al. 2017
;
Tiira et al.
.
In some cases the skewedness of the CPR is opposite to what is obtained with more conventional approaches.
The current-conserving method opens up new research avenues regarding the effect of current-carrying bulk states on the Andreev spectrum and their respective contributions to the total Josephson current.
The method also enables the generation of backward-skewed CPRs, and may thus be used to cast light on which factors determine the phase at which the current reaches its maximum.

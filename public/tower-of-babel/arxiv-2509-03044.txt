DCDB: Dynamic Conditional Dual Diffusion Bridge for Ill-posed Multi-Tasks
Chengjie HuangCollege of Computer Science and TechnologyZhejiang University 12521258@zju.edu.cn Jiafeng YanSchool of InformationCentral University of Finance and Economics yan.jiafeng@email.cufe.edu.cn, Jing Li School of InformationCentral University of Finance and Economics lijing2017@cufe.edu.cn, Lu BaiSchool of Artificial IntelligenceBeijing Normal University bailu@bnu.edu.cn

Abstract
Conditional diffusion models have made impressive progress in the field of image processing, but the characteristics of constructing data distribution pathways make it difficult to exploit the intrinsic correlation between tasks in multi-task scenarios, which is even worse in ill-posed tasks with a lack of training data. In addition, traditional static condition control makes it difficult for networks to learn in multi-task scenarios with its dynamically evolving characteristics. To address these challenges, we propose a dynamic conditional double diffusion bridge training paradigm to build a general framework for ill-posed multi-tasks. Firstly, this paradigm decouples the diffusion and condition generation processes, avoiding the dependence of the diffusion model on supervised data in ill-posed tasks. Secondly, generated by the same noise schedule, dynamic conditions are used to gradually adjust their statistical characteristics, naturally embed time-related information, and reduce the difficulty of network learning. We analyze the learning objectives of the network under different conditional forms in the single-step denoising process and compare the changes in its attention weights in the network, demonstrating the superiority of our dynamic conditions. Taking dehazing and visible-infrared fusion as typical ill-posed multi-task scenarios, we achieve the best performance in multiple indicators on public datasets. The code has been publicly released at: https://anonymous.4open.science/r/DCDB-D3C2.

Worse still, some multi-task scenarios further present training ill-posedness. Training datasets lack ground truth (GT) supervision, and task objectives are not unique. A common example is visible-infrared fusion, which cannot construct strictly paired training data through masking operations like image completion tasks. This "truly ill-posed" characteristic of training data without GT poses fundamental obstacles for existing diffusion models (including those attempting multi-task processing) when constructing forward processes.

Furthermore, current conditional diffusion models rely on static conditional inputs. This static conditional input not only results in enormous distance disparities between noisy samples and noise-free conditions in Wasserstein space, but also ignores the dynamic evolutionary characteristics of task features during the denoising process, forcing the network to learn an unnatural "one-step" mapping.

To address the problems of target distribution without explicit ground truth and dynamic condition requirements in multi-tasks faced by traditional conditional diffusion models in ill-posed multi-task scenarios, we propose a dynamic conditional dual diffusion bridge training paradigm, constructing a general framework for ill-posed multi-tasks. Specifically,as shown in Figure
1
, by decoupling the diffusion process from conditional generation logic,this paradigm designs two noise-schedule-shared diffusion bridges: the main diffusion bridge (X-bridge) and the conditional generation bridge (Y-bridge). This design not only cleverly circumvents the dependence on GT for target tasks through conditional generation of the Y-bridge, but also achieves deep coupling of multiple tasks through shared noise schedule and collaborative denoising of the X-bridge.

The Y-bridge progressively adjusts statistical characteristics by generating dynamic conditional signals, adhering to source distribution features in early high-noise reverse steps, and transitioning to target distribution distributions in later low-noise steps. This dynamic evolution mechanism significantly reduces the Wasserstein distance between conditional distributions of adjacent time steps, while naturally embedding time-step-related information, greatly reducing the learning difficulty for the network.

In summary, we make the following core contributions:

•

We propose a dynamic conditional dual diffusion bridge training paradigm that achieves deep coupling of multiple tasks through noise schedule sharing and collaborative denoising, while cleverly circumventing the dependency on GT for ill-posed tasks.

•

We design a concise and efficient dynamic condition generation mechanism that significantly reduces network learning pressure by progressively injecting conditions to reduce the Wasserstein distance between conditional distributions of adjacent time steps. This advantage will be verified through attention weight analysis.

•

We deeply couple multiple tasks within a unified framework, achieving end-to-end collaborative optimization, and demonstrate superiority in the typical ill-posed multi-task scenario of dehazing and visible-infrared fusion.

2.1
Score-based Diffusion Model

The score-based diffusion model is a generative model that transforms data into noise through a diffusion process and generates samples by learning the reverse process. It assumes a data set consists of n-dimensional independent identically distributed (i.i.d.) samples from an unknown distribution
p(x_{0})
. The forward process is described by the SDE:

\mathrm{d}x_{t}=\mathbf{f}(x_{t},t)\mathrm{d}t+g_{t}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{w}_{t},

(1)

where
\mathbf{f}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the drift coefficient,
g_{t}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the scalar diffusion coefficient, and
\mathbf{w}_{t}
denotes the standard Brownian motion. Over time,
p(x_{0})
evolves into
p(x_{t})
approximating a standard Gaussian distribution
p_{\text{prior}}(x)
. The reverse process is given by:

\mathrm{d}x_{t}=\left[\mathbf{f}(x_{t},t)-g_{t}^{2}\nabla_{x_{t}}\log p(x_{t})\right]\mathrm{d}t+g_{t}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{w}_{t}.

(2)

Starting from time
T
,
p(x_{t})
can transform to
p(x_{0})
by following the reverse SDE. The score
\nabla_{x_{t}}\log p(x_{t})
is parameterized as
\mathbf{s}_{\theta}(x_{t},t)

2.2
Generalized Ornstein-Uhlenbeck Bridge (GOUB)

While standard SBDMs
song2020score
focus on unconditional data generation, many real-world tasks (e.g., image restoration, cross-modal fusion) require
conditional
diffusion between two fixed points (e.g., a degraded image
x_{t}
and a clean image
x_{0}
). This is where the GOUB framework becomes critical.

The GOUB extends SBDMs by leveraging the Generalized Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (GOU) process—a mean-reverting SDE—to construct a
diffusion bridge
that connects two fixed states
x_{s}
and
x_{t}
. The GOU process is defined by:

dx_{t}=\theta_{t}(\mu-x_{t})\mathrm{d}t+g_{t}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{w}_{t},

where
\mu
is a given state vector,
\theta_{t}
denotes a scalar drift coefficient and
g_{t}
represents the diffusion coefficient. We required
\theta_{t}
and
g_{t}
to satisfy
2\lambda^{2}=g_{t}^{2}/\theta_{t}^{2}
, where
\lambda^{2}
is a given constant scalar. As time progresses, the process exhibits a tendency to revert to the target mean
\mu
. The drift coefficient
\theta_{t}
controls the speed of reversion, while the diffusion coefficient
g_{t}
determines the volatility of the process. Consequently, the distribution of
x_{t}
gradually converges to a Gaussian distribution centered on
\mu
.

By applying Doob’s h-transform, the GOU process is conditioned on
x_{t}
, resulting in a bridge SDE:

\mathrm{d}x_{t}=\left[\theta_{t}(\mu-x_{t})+g_{t}^{2}\nabla_{x_{t}}\log p(x_{t}|x_{T})\right]\mathrm{d}t+g_{t}d\mathbf{w}_{t}.

This transformation ensures the process starts at
x_{s}
and ends at
x_{t}
with zero terminal variance, making it ideal for tasks requiring precise state transitions (e.g., image restoration from a degraded input).

A key feature of the GOU process is its transition probability, which has a closed-form analytical solution. Specifically, the transition probability from time
s
to time
t
is given by:

p(x_{t}|x_{s})=N\left(\mu+(x_{s}-\boldsymbol{\mu})e^{-\bar{\theta}_{s:t}},\frac{g_{t}^{2}}{2\theta_{t}}\left(1-e^{-2\bar{\theta}_{s:t}}\right)\mathbf{I}\right),

(3)

where
\bar{\theta}_{s:t}=\int_{s}^{t}\theta_{z}\mathrm{d}z
. This transition probability highlights the mean-reverting behavior of the GOU process, as the distribution of
x_{t}
gradually reverts to the mean
\boldsymbol{\mu}
over time.

The Generalized Ornstein-Uhlenbeck Bridge (GOUB) model extends the GOU process by applying Doob’s h-transform. This modification allows the model to bypass the need for artificial noise addition in the reverse process, which can lead to information loss. By specifying a terminal point, GOUB effectively bridges the gap between different states, enhancing the model’s applicability and performance across various multi-task scenarios.

Problem Statement and Modeling

Let us formally define the research problem of this work: achieving the fusion of infrared - visible images under degraded conditions while performing image degradation removal simultaneously. The pair of degraded images is denoted as
\{x_{T},y_{0}\}
, where
x_{T}
represents the degraded visible - light image affected by factors such as haze or low-light, and
y_{0}
is the infrared image of the same scene. Our goal is to generate a high - quality fused image
\hat{x}_{0}
through a function
\Gamma
, i.e.,
\hat{x}_{0}=\Gamma(\{x_{0},y_{0}\})
. Here,
\Gamma
needs to handle two tasks: degradation removal
R
and information fusion
F
, and these two tasks are closely coupled within
\Gamma
rather than being executed independently. We adopt the coupling route (
\Gamma=R\Join F
) instead of simple concatenation, as the latter overlooks the intrinsic connection between the two tasks and leads to limited performance.

In the following, we present a theoretical framework for infrared-visible image fusion based on diffusion bridge models. Our approach involves defining two specialized diffusion bridges to address the specific challenges of multi-modal image fusion under degraded conditions.

3.1
Modality Transformation Bridge (Y-Bridge)

:
The Y-Bridge focuses on the conversion from the degraded visible modality to the infrared modality. Given an infrared image

\mathrm{d}y_{t}=\left(\theta^{y}_{t}+(g^{y}_{t})^{2}\frac{e^{-2\bar{\theta}^{y}_{t:T}}}{(\bar{\sigma}^{y}_{t:T})^{2}}\right)(y_{T}-y_{t})\mathrm{d}t+g^{y}_{t}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{w}_{t}.

(8)

According to diffusion bridge theory, for linear SDEs, given
y_{T}=x_{T}
and

is:

p(y_{t}|y_{0},y_{T})=\mathcal{N}(\bar{\mathbf{m}}^{\prime y}_{t},(\bar{\sigma}_{0:t}^{\prime y})^{2}\mathbf{I}),

(9)

The mean and variance can be calculated as in Lemma

This allows direct computation of the state

at any time

3.2
De-degradation Bridge (X-Bridge)

The X-Bridge focuses on restoring the ideal visible image
x_{t}
from the degraded visible image
x_{T}
and infrared image

Mathematical Justification of the Method.

In previous works, the neural network optimizes
\nabla_{x_{t}}\log p(x_{t}|x_{T},y_{0})
, while the optimization target of the neural network in our work is
\nabla_{x_{t}}\log p(x_{t}|x_{T},y_{t})
. Next, by analyzing their derivatives with respect to time, we can observe the intensity that the neural network needs to learn. From Bayes’s formula, we can obtain

\displaystyle\nabla_{x_{t}}\log p(x_{t}|x_{T},y_{0})=

\displaystyle\nabla_{x_{t}}\left(\log p(y_{0}|x_{t},x_{T})+\log p(x_{t}|x_{T})\right),

(12)

\displaystyle\nabla_{x_{t}}\log p(x_{t}|x_{T},y_{t})=

\displaystyle\nabla_{x_{t}}\left(\log p(y_{t}|x_{t},x_{T})+\log p(x_{t}|x_{T})\right).

(13)

.

Intuitively, inferring information related to
y_{t}
from

and
x_{T}
is simpler than inferring

4.1
Attention Weights Analysis

This section aims to demonstrate that, on the theoretical basis of adjacent denoising time steps, dynamic conditions are easier for the network to learn. It provides an empirical analysis of the attention weights under different network conditions to illustrate the functionality and effectiveness of the dynamic guidance mechanism.

Experimental analysis shows that dynamic guided diffusion helps to embed attention related to time steps into the input conditions during iterative generation in advance. This reduces the learning pressure on the attention modules at the bottom of the U-Net, thereby effectively improving the model’s information interaction capability. The following are specific experimental methods and results.

The control model lacks a dynamic guidance mechanism and uses
y_{0}
as the condition consistently. Except for this, all other settings are the same as the experimental model. It is trained separately and corresponding test values are obtained.

We first defined a new index—the stability coefficient—to reflect the degree of continuous change in time series data over a certain period. It is defined as:

f=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|q_{x_{i+1}}-q_{x_{i}}|

where
x_{i}
represents the value at time
i
and
n
is the number of time steps considered. The lower the value of this index, the more stable the time series.

Based on this index, we designed experiments to calculate the stability coefficients of
q_{x},q_{y},k_{x},k_{y}
, and the overall average value for both the experimental model and the control model. As shown in Figure
4
, our method achieved stable outputs in all three parts and overall, indicating that the dynamic guidance mechanism alleviates the pressure on the attention modules, enabling them to perform better in other aspects.

It is difficult to directly demonstrate the performance of attention. Instead, we use some data to indirectly reflect this performance improvement. On one hand, the final index scores of the model in subsequent ablation experiments can be used for evaluation. On the other hand, we visualized the average values of the corresponding parts of the U-Net bottom attention module at each time step (see Figure

). The visualization clearly shows that the attention matrix of the control model is more blurred. In contrast, our method enables the model to better extract high-frequency fine-grained information, which aligns with the analysis presented earlier.

5
Conclusion

This paper proposes a dynamic conditional double diffusion bridge (DCDB), which addresses the challenges of ill-posed multi-task image processing by decoupling diffusion and conditional generation into two bridges that share noise scheduling, thereby avoiding the dependence on ground truth and achieving deep task coupling.

Theoretical analysis shows that dynamic conditions can shorten the gap between adjacent time step distributions and embed temporal information, making network learning easier than static input based on Bayesian derivatives; Empirical verification through attention weight analysis shows that dynamic guidance can stabilize weight distribution and enhance high-frequency feature extraction, as evidenced by the reduction of stability coefficient and the improvement of heat map visualization; Experimental results on dehazing and visible infrared fusion on the
\mathrm{M^{3}FD}
dataset show that DCDB outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of CC, AG, and EI, while preserving edge details and avoiding distortion. Ablation studies confirm the necessity of dynamic conditions for end-to-end optimization.

Although DCDB has potential in GT-scarce fields such as medical imaging due to its advantage of not requiring deep coupling of GT with multiple tasks, it also faces many limitations, including the inability of the current paradigm to support the collaborative processing of three or more tasks, limitations in other ill-posed scenarios that cannot be solved by conditional control, and reliance on linear Gaussian dynamics for conditional generation, which may not be able to capture more complex task variations.

Appendix C
Appendix of Related Work

To address the problems of target distribution without explicit ground truth and dynamic condition requirements in multi-tasks faced by traditional conditional diffusion models in ill-posed multi-task scenarios, we propose a dynamic conditional dual diffusion bridge training paradigm, constructing a general framework for ill-posed multi-tasks. Specifically, as shown in Fig. 1, by decoupling the diffusion process from conditional generation logic, this paradigm designs two noise-schedule-shared diffusion bridges: the main diffusion bridge (X-bridge) and the conditional generation bridge (Y-bridge). This design not only cleverly circumvents the dependency on GT for target tasks through Y-bridge’s conditional generation, but also achieves deep coupling of multiple tasks through shared noise schedule and collaborative denoising of the X-bridge.

This progressive probabilistic modeling strategy has enabled diffusion models to achieve breakthrough progress in image processing tasks such as image restoration

However, diffusion models’ forward process requires constructing noise injection paths based on target distributions, while the reverse process relies on optimizing corresponding denoising trajectories. This characteristic has led traditional diffusion models to primarily optimize for single tasks. Even when research attempts to extend diffusion models to multi-task scenarios

In computer vision, we categorize ill-posed problems into two types based on their training characteristics: "pseudo-ill-posed" tasks such as image inpainting

This characteristic of training data without GT poses severe challenges for deep learning models (especially diffusion models). Existing methods mainly adopt two strategies: introducing additional quality evaluation metrics
[
45
]

[

. However, these methods either require complex loss function design or rely on unstable adversarial training processes, and often struggle to guarantee the quality and consistency of generated results.

When ill-posedness overlaps with multi-task requirements, problem complexity further increases. For example, in autonomous driving scenarios involving single-image dehazing and infrared-visible fusion, dehazing quality directly affects subsequent fusion effects, while lack of GT supervision makes joint optimization of both tasks difficult. Similarly, medical image analysis tasks such as super-resolution and lesion detection face similar challenges. Existing research often adopts fragmented processing strategies, completing one sub-task first and using its results as input for the next task

C.3
Dynamic Condition Generation

Additionally, traditional conditional diffusion models employ static conditional inputs, a design that faces two main problems when handling complex tasks: the enormous difference between high-noise samples and noise-free conditions in distribution space causes optimization difficulties; and it ignores the dynamic evolutionary characteristics of task features during the denoising process. To alleviate these problems, some research has begun exploring possibilities for dynamically adjusting conditional signals. For instance,
[
48
]
proposed a SABW block to generate dynamic condition, and guide the attention mechanism to generate the differentiated weight. However, these additional network modules, increasing model complexity and training difficulty.

The dynamic conditional dual diffusion bridge training paradigm proposed in this paper formulates the dynamic conditional signals through the Y-bridge’s forward process, not only simplifying the condition generation mechanism but also significantly reducing the network’s learning burden by decreasing the Wasserstein distance between conditional distributions of adjacent time steps. This design simultaneously resolves the GT dependency problem of ill-posed multi-tasks, achieving deep coupling and end-to-end optimization of multiple tasks.

Appendix E
Appendix of Loss Function

The first two MSE terms (
\mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}}
) measure pixel-wise discrepancies:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}}(x_{t-1},x^{\prime}_{t-1})=\mathbb{E}\left[(x_{t-1}-x^{\prime}_{t-1})^{2}\right],\quad\mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}}(y_{t-1},y^{\prime}_{t-1})=\mathbb{E}\left[(y_{t-1}-y^{\prime}_{t-1})^{2}\right].

By minimizing these, the model learns to preserve the overall intensity information (e.g., brightness, thermal signatures) from both input modalities, ensuring the fused image retains the basic visual appearance of the sources.

The gradient loss
\mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}
complements this by prioritizing edge details. Using the Sobel operator
\nabla
to extract gradients, it enforces alignment between the fused image’s edges and the maximum gradients of the inputs:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\nabla x^{\prime}_{t-1}-\max\left(\nabla x_{t-1},\nabla y_{t-1}\right)\right)^{2}\right].

This ensures the fused image inherits the most prominent edges from either input (e.g. object boundaries in visible-light or thermal outlines in infrared), enhancing structural clarity.

Together, the MSE and gradient losses address a key challenge: while MSE terms ensure the fused image aligns with the input intensity distributions (e.g., brightness from visible-light and thermal signatures from infrared), they alone may fail to preserve sharp edges or fine structural details. To compensate, the gradient loss explicitly emphasizes edge information by sharpening structural fidelity, creating a multi-faceted optimization objective. This synergy ensures the final fused image not only matches the input modalities in overall brightness and thermal characteristics but also retains critical edge details, resulting in visually coherent and information-rich results that balance intensity similarity and structural integrity.

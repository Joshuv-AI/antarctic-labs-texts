Insights into Cationic Vacancies in a Prussian Blue Analogues Cathode for Enhanced Reversible Sodium Insertion
Authors: Jiacen Liu, Jianwei Wu, Tianyi Sun, Yongchao Shi, Mathieu Salanne, Sheng Bi, Mingxue Tang, Jiwei Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
As promising cathode materials for sodium-ion batteries (SIBs), Prussian blue analogues (PBAs) have gained significant attention due to their facile synthesis and high theoretical capacity. However, the low practical capacity limits PBAs for battery applications. In this work, we propose a Fe vacancy-type PBA and systematically investigate its electrochemical activities, kinetics, and sodium storage mechanism. We demonstrate that although the capacity is enhanced as the vacancy content increases, an excessive concentration of vacancies leads to a deterioration of the cycling performance. Moreover, we show an enhancement of the Na+ diffusion kinetics due to Fe vacancies through multiscan rate cyclic voltammetry, electrochemical impedance spectroscopy, and molecular simulations. Furthermore, operando X-ray diffraction, ex situ solid-state nuclear magnetic resonance, and Raman reveal the sodium intercalation behavior, confirming the high structural reversibility of vacancy-type PBAs. This study not only proposes a novel vacancy-mediated strategy in PBAs but also highlights the significance of defect engineering in boosting the sodium storage for SIBs.

Introduction

The growing global energy demand and heavy reliance on fossil fuel combustion for electricity generation have led to severe environmental issues, such as global warming and the greenhouse effect. , These challenges have necessitated the exploration of renewable energy sources and sustainable energy technologies to replace traditional fossil fuel utilization. Currently, lithium-ion batteries (LIBs) have been commercialized and widely utilized in portable electronics and electric vehicles. , However, the limited availability and unbalanced global distribution of lithium resource have raised concerns about the large-scale deployment of LIBs. , To address these limitations, sodium-ion batteries (SIBs) emerged as a promising alternative because of the abundant natural reserves of sodium, cost-effectiveness, together with their similarities with LIBs. − Nevertheless, the performance of SIBs heavily relies on the cathode material, which has a prominent impact on the working potential, energy density, and cycling stability. , The design and synthesis of cathode materials allowing for simultaneously exhibiting good electrochemical properties and long cycling life still remain a serious challenge, hindering the practical application process of SIBs.

In recent years, significant researches have been dedicated to the investigation of cathode materials for SIBs, such as layered oxides, polyanionic compounds, and Prussian blue analogues (PBAs). , Layered oxides exhibit the highest capacity performance, but they suffer from a low working voltage and a poor cyclic stability. Polyanionic compounds show great cycling stability, but they are often hindered by a relatively low capacity. Among them, PBAs show high theoretical capacity, high working potential, and unique framework structure that allows for easy regulation of components and properties. Moreover, PBAs can be synthesized via a simple coprecipitation method at room temperature conditions with nontoxic raw materials, which makes PBAs highly suitable for large-scale energy storage production. , The general composition of PBAs is commonly represented as A x M1[M2(CN)6] y ·nH2O, in which A corresponds to alkali-metal ions (e.g., Li, Na, and K) and M corresponds to transition metals. Notably, when M1 and M2 are both Fe element, the Fe-based PBAs could provide a high theoretical specific capacity of 170 mAh g–1. A major focus of previous studies was the enhancement of the cycling stability by reducing the concentration of [Fe­(CN)6]4– anionic vacancies and of crystal water in PBAs. − However, defects existence is hard to be entirely eliminated, and the structure of PBAs will inevitably contain inside defects due to experimental limitations. Instead of merely mitigating defects, another strategy consists in utilizing them. It is however necessary to investigate the associated charging mechanism for efficiently improving the properties of PBAs. This approach not only addresses the limitations of PBAs but also paves a new way for optimizing their performance in SIBs.

Herein, we propose a family of novel defect-type PBA structures by introducing Fe vacancies. We elucidated the relation between vacancy content and electrochemical properties, further exploring the kinetics and sodium storage mechanism for this vacancy-type PBAs. First, the PBAs with different vacancies were synthesized and characterized by X-ray diffraction (XRD), X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), and Raman spectroscopy. Then, through electrochemical performance tests, we observed a positive correlation between vacancy content and discharge capacity. However, excessive vacancies were demonstrated to hamper the structural stability, leading to deteriorate cycling performance. Among the series of materials, PBA□Fe1 with low vacancy content could exhibit both enhanced capacity and more stable cycling process. The dynamic performance of PBA□Fe1 was investigated by multiscan rate cyclic voltammetry (CV) and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS), which shows the enhancement of electrochemical properties. Besides, the improved kinetics was also supported by density functional theory (DFT) calculations and ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) that showed reduced Na+ migration energy barriers in PBA□Fe1. Additionally, the Na+ storage structure evolution of PBA□Fe1 was investigated by operando XRD, ex situ solid-state nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR), and Raman. The results reveal the highly structural reversibility during Na+ insertion/extraction. Finally, the practical application of PBA□Fe1 was evaluated in a full-cell design, showing high energy density and promising electrochemical performance. These results emphasize the superior sodium storage performance and elucidate the charging mechanism of vacancy-type PBAs, providing a novel and effective strategy to optimizing PBAs for next-generation SIBs.

Results and Discussion

Structural Characterizations

PBA and a range of vacancy-containing materials PBA□Fe1, PBA□Fe2, and PBA□Fe3 were synthesized through a coprecipitation method (see the Experimental Section in the Supporting Information for details). The XRD patterns of all samples exhibit the characteristic of a similar cubic symmetry belonging to the Fm–3m space group, confirming a pure phase structure. The specific structure information was obtained through Rietveld refinement, as shown in Figure b. The fitted lattice parameters are a = b = c = 10.29535(3) Å with a cell volume V = 1091.24(6) Å3 for PBA, a = b = c = 10.26641(3) Å and V = 1082.07(5) Å3 for PBA□Fe1, a = b = c = 10.26033(1) Å and V = 1080.15(2) Å3 for PBA□Fe2, and a = b = c = 10.25868(4) Å and V = 1079.63(8) Å3 for PBA□Fe3. Refined atomic positions, atom occupancy rates, and additional parameters are summarized in Tables S1–S4 (Supporting Information). The structure reveals that Fe mainly exists in two spin states of FeLS and FeHS in PBA, which are primarily located at 4a and 4b sites, respectively. , Besides, C and N atoms both occupy the 24e site, connecting with FeLS and FeHS to form FeLSC6 and FeHSN6 octahedrons. , High-spin Fe coordinated by nitrogen atoms is generally more labile than low-spin Fe coordinated by carbon atoms due to the weaker Fe–N bonding environment. Na ions are primarily situated at the 8c site, enclosed by the above-mentioned surrounding octahedrons. The acetic acid that added after the coprecipitation process preferentially reacts with high-spin Fe sites coordinated by nitrogen atoms, leading to partial dissolution of Fe ions and the formation of Fe-site vacancies. Thus, Fe vacancies preferentially be introduced at the high active FeHS site via this reaction that breaks Fe–N bonds, while FeLS could simultaneously be maintained in the structure (Figure a). The elemental composition was quantified using the Inductively Coupled Plasma Optical Emission Spectrometry together with elemental analysis (Table S5). The measured Fe proportion is consistent with the Fe vacancy content determined by Rietveld refinement (Figure S1a). The vacancies content is 0.12, 0.22, and 0.33 for PBA□Fe1, PBA□Fe2, and PBA□Fe3, respectively.

Structural characterization of the of PBA and vacancy-type materials: (a) Schematic showing the location of a vacancy in the lattice, (b) Rietveld refinement of the XRD patterns, and (c) Fe 2p XPS spectrum.

The electronic structure of PBA, PBA□Fe1, PBA□Fe2, and PBA□Fe3 was analyzed through XPS. As shown in Figure c, Fe2+ is characterized by peaks located at 708.4–708.5 eV and 721.3–721.4 eV, while Fe3+ is identified by the peak located at approximately 712.0–712.1 eV and 724.7–724.8 eV, respectively. , And Figure S2 shows the presence of Na, Fe, C, and N elements for all samples. Moreover, the bond structure of all samples was studied through the Fourier-transform infrared (FT-IR) and Raman spectrum. Figure S1c shows the absorption peaks located at around 500, 599.8, and 2060 cm–1 in FT-IR, corresponding to the δ­(Fe–C–N), ν­(Fe–C), and ν­(CN) chemical bonds, respectively. , The characteristic peak of O–H at 1600 eV is due to the residual presence of water. , The Raman spectra is shown in Figure S1d. The peak located at 516 cm–1 arises from the Fe–C stretching vibration of lattice. The peaks between 2000 and 2200 cm–1 are due to the CN bond, while the major peak at 2150 cm–1 exhibits an A1g vibrational mode and the peak at a lower wavenumber of 2100 cm–1 corresponds to a Eg vibrational mode. The different vibrational mode is attributed to the different Fe valence state that coordinates to the cyanide ligands. Thermogravimetric (TG) analysis was performed to quantitatively determine the water content in the samples. As shown in Figure S1b, the weight losses below 300 °C were estimated to be 18%, 9.6%, 10.1%, and 16.5% for PBA, PBA□Fe1, PBA□Fe2, and PBA□Fe3, respectively. Based on these results, the chemical formula is calculated and listed in Table S6. Notably, the water content in PBA□Fe1 and PBA□Fe2 is lower than that of PBA□Fe3. Excessive vacancy generation in PBA□Fe3 leads to an increasing water content due to the formation of more open structural sites. Meanwhile, the reduced water content is considered beneficial for suppressing structural degradation during cycling. Besides, the morphology for the series of samples was demonstrated by the scanning electron microscopy (SEM), which exhibits a cubic morphology for the particles, whose size reaches up to 60 nm, as shown in Figure S3a–d. The presence of cubic particles is more pronounced for PBA□Fe1 than for the other materials. The Na, Fe, N, and C elements are evenly distributed in PBA□Fe1 (Figure S3e), which proves the successful synthesis of PBAs samples.

Electrochemical Properties and Kinetics

The electrochemical properties of PBA and other Fe-vacancies containing PBAs were evaluated in the half-cell, with the testing voltage window of 2–4.2 V (vs Na+/Na). At least three cells were assembled for electrochemical tests, and the reported data represent the average performance. The CV curves (Figure S4) display a pair of major oxidation/reduction peaks at around 2.99/2.58 V corresponding to the redox reaction of FeHS, while the pair of oxidation/reduction peaks at 3.36/3.25 V corresponds to FeLS, respectively, the extra weak peaks are possibly attributed to the different intercalation positions of Na+. Figure a shows the galvanostatic charge–discharge (GCD) curve comparison for the second cycle at the current density of 50 mA g–1. PBA without vacancies could only exhibit a capacity of 105.5 mAh g–1. With the introduction of Fe vacancies, the modified materials display discharge capacities of 135.2, 143.2, and 158.9 mAh g–1 for PBA□Fe1, PBA□Fe2, and PBA□Fe3, respectively (Figure S5). The capacity is significantly enhanced, especially for PBA□Fe3, for which it reaches a value close to a theoretical capacity of 170 mAh g–1. Furthermore, the rate performance was also evaluated (Figure b). PBA□Fe3 has a capacity of 159.8, 117.0, 87.7, 72.9, 63.5, and 52.8 mAh g–1 at current densities of 20, 50, 100, 200, 500, and 1000 mA g–1, respectively. In contrast, the vacancy-free PBA only exhibits reversible capacities of 101.6, 72.4, 53.9, 41.3, 35.7, and 30.4 mAh g–1. However, although PBA□Fe3 exhibits the largest discharging capacity, it suffers from a poor cycling stability (Figure S6). As shown in Figure c, the capacity of PBA□Fe3 is 60.3 mAh g–1 after 300 cycles with a capacity retention of 44.7% only. In comparison, PBA□Fe1 exhibits a higher capacity retention of 75.4% after the same number of cycles. This improved cycling stability can also be attributed to the reduced water content in PBA□Fe1, as excessive water is known to accelerate structural degradation during repeated Na+ insertion/extraction. Besides, it also displays better rate performance, with reversible capacities of 127.9, 119.7, 113.2, 108.3, 100.6, and 93.5 mAh g–1 (Figure b). Accordingly, as shown in Figures d and S7, PBA□Fe1 also reveals the smallest deterioration of Na+ intercalation diffusion rate among these materials after 100 cycles, which is calculated based on GITT (galvanostatic intermittent titration techniques) test.

Electrochemical performance comparison for PBA and vacancy-containing PBAs after one cycle activation: (a) GCD curves at the 2nd cycle, (b) rate performance, (c) cycling performance at 500 mA g–1, and (d) relative Na+ diffusion coefficient according to GITT tests before/after 100 cycles.

Besides, the ex situ XRD and SEM were carried out after 10 and 100 cycles at the same discharge voltage of 2 V to investigate the stability of PBA□Fe1 during cycling. As shown in Figure S8, the characteristic peaks of PBA□Fe1 remain nearly unchanged after prolonged cycling, without additional peaks. And as shown in Figure S9, the electrode maintains its original morphology with well-preserved particle structures even after 100 cycles. No obvious pulverization or severe structural collapse is observed. These results demonstrate that the framework of PBA□Fe1 maintains good structural integrity during the repeated Na+ insertion/extraction processes. Considering its excellent cycling stability and reversibility properties, subsequent studies will focus on PBA□Fe1 for in-depth research.

DFT calculations and AIMD simulations were conducted to investigate the effect of Fe vacancies on sodium migration within the crystal structure. The energy barriers for Na+ diffusion were compared between the pristine PBA and PBA□Fe1 using the climbing image nudged elastic band method, as illustrated in Figure S12. The results show that the Na+ diffusion energy barrier in PBA□Fe1 is lower at 0.71 eV compared to 0.82 eV for the defect-free structure, indicating that Fe vacancies enhance Na+ migration. AIMD results reveal that the mean square displacement of Na+ ions in PBA□Fe1 is about three times greater than in the intact PBA electrode (Figure g). The velocity autocorrelation function (VACF) is defined as eq : 3 Cv(t)=⟨v(0)v(t)⟩⟨v(0)v(0)⟩ where v(0) represents the initial velocity of the molecule and v(t) represents the velocity at the t moment. It shows that Na+ ions in PBA□Fe1 do not display the fast oscillatory pattern (Figure h), suggesting reduced rattling due to structural defects. This is further confirmed by the power spectrum S(ω) of Na+ ions in Figure i, where the high-frequency peak around 800 cm–1 almost disappears, indicating that the Na ions dynamic becomes less coupled to the structural relaxation of the lattice.

Mechanistic Insights

Operando XRD measurement was conducted to analyze the structure evolution during the Na+ intercalation and deintercalation at the second cycle, within the voltage of 2–4.2 V. As shown in Figure a,b, for PBA□Fe1, the 2D contour maps display the diffraction peaks of (200), (220), and (400). All peaks shift to higher angle continuously during the Na+ extraction from 2 to 4.2 V. Then, upon the discharge process, the diffraction peaks of (200), (220), and (400) gradually return to their original positions with Na+ insertion, revealing the high structural reversibility for PBA□Fe1. In addition, the volume change of PBA□Fe1 was evaluated as illustrated in Figure c. The volume variation of PBA□Fe1 (2.84%) is much smaller than that of PBA (4.74%) that we reported previously, which can be attributed to the existence of Fe vacancies.

Solid-state 23Na NMR spectra were collected under the magic angle spinning of 16 kHz and analyzed for PBA□Fe1 electrodes in the pristine state as well as various stages of charging and discharging. Due to the intensive quadrupolar interaction of 23Na, severe broadening and overlapping is observed for the 23Na NMR spectra as shown in Figure d. To analyze these spectra, a constrained deconvolution was performed. In our deconvolution, the chemical shifts and line widths were fixed within narrow ranges, and only the intensities were allowed to vary within physically reasonable limits. This treatment substantially reduces the degrees of freedom in the fitting and helps avoid overparameterization of the overlapping 23Na resonances. Experimentally, at least four overlapping components are required to reproduce the spectral envelope. To further aid the interpretation of these fitted components, DFT calculations were carried out on the Fe-vacancy-containing structure (Figure S13). The results suggest four nonequivalent Na environments, and the relative ordering of their chemical shifts agrees well with the experimental spectrum, as shown in Figure S13b and Table S9.

Based on this constrained deconvolution, the broad experimental spectrum of the pristine electrode can be described by four overlapping components. For the pristine cathode at 2 V, the fitted components centered at 8, −4, −10, and −20 ppm are denoted as Na1, Na2, Na3, and Na4, respectively. Upon charging to 3.1 V, the Na2 component decreases markedly, accompanied by a subtle decrease of the Na3 component, whereas the Na1 and Na4 components remain comparatively unchanged. Upon further charging to 4.2 V, the Na2 component shows a further decrease together with an obvious attenuation of the Na1 component, while the Na3 and Na4 contributions remain nearly unchanged. During the subsequent discharge back to 3.1 V, the Na1 component recovers significantly with a slight increase of the Na2 component, whereas the Na3 and Na4 contributions remain similar to those observed at the corresponding charging state. After discharging back to 2 V, the Na2 component increases markedly, and the overall spectrum becomes highly similar to that of the pristine state. Overall, the 23Na NMR analysis provides insight into the Na extraction/insertion process and suggests a largely reversible evolution of the local Na environments in PBA□Fe1.

Besides, we employed ex situ Raman to verify detailed structural transformation and Na+ content during charging and discharging, as shown in Figure e. And the schematic illustration is displayed in Figure S14. The spectrum was collected at charge/discharge potentials of 2, 3.1, and 4.2 V after the first cycle activating, within the spectral range of 2060–2160 cm–1 due to the stretching vibration of the sensitive –CN– bond. , Initially, it displays triple peaks that locate at 2064, 2106, and 2141 cm–1 at 2 V, corresponding to CN ligands coordinated to the Fe2+ state that indicates the Fe2+–CN–Fe2+ structure and Na-rich situation. , Then, during charging to 3.1 V, two peaks located at lower wavenumber gradually become weakened, with one obviously red-shifted peak remaining at 2146 cm–1. This phenomenon is attributed to the Fe-oxidation and transformation to the Fe3+–CN–Fe2+ bond when extracting Na+. , When charging to 4.2 V, the major peak continues to move to higher wavenumber, reaching 2160 cm–1, which indicates the Fe3+ domination accompanied by a poor Na+ structure. During discharge, the peak totally shifts to the lower wavenumber again and finally splits in three peaks located at 2069, 2109, and 2139 cm–1 when discharging to 2 V. The entire shift of Raman peaks suggests a highly reversible insertion and extraction of Na+ in PBA□Fe1. In addition, the similar valence change of Fe could be observed in the ex situ XPS test, as shown in Figures f and S15. The relative proportion of Fe2+ and Fe3+ at various potentials is fitted according to the peak area. During the charging process, the relative content of Fe3+ keeps increasing, indicating the Na+ extraction and Fe-oxidation. The proportion of Fe3+ reaches the maximum at 4.2 V. Subsequently, the Fe3+ content shrinks, and the Fe2+ content increases until discharging to 2 V, which is attributed to the ions persistent insertion process.

Full-Cell Performance

To demonstrate the practical value of PBA□Fe1, sodium-ion full-cells were constructed with PBA□Fe1 or PBA as the cathode, hard carbon as the anode, and NaClO4 as the electrolyte, as shown in Figure a. In this work, the hard carbon anode was presodiated before full-cell assembly to promote the stable solid electrolyte interphase (SEI) layer formation. The PBA□Fe1//hard carbon full-cell exhibits a discharge capacity about 100 mAh g–1, which apparently exceeds the 65 mAh g–1 of PBA full-cell between 1.5 and 3.8 V (Figure b). At the current density of 100 and 200 mA g–1, the PBA□Fe1 full-cell also displays the capacity of 95.4 and 94.2 mAh g–1 (Figure S16). The full-cell exhibits an initial Coulombic efficiency (ICE) of approximately 90%, which is mainly attributed to irreversible sodium consumption associated with the formation of the SEI. In this work, the hard carbon anode was presodiated before full-cell assembly to promote the stable SEI layer formation, thereby improving the ICE. As shown in Figure S17, the CV curve shows two redox peaks corresponding to the voltage platform in GCD curves for PBA□Fe1 and PBA, respectively. The larger CV curve integral area of the PBA□Fe1 full-cell indicates a better capacity performance. Furthermore, the rate performance of PBA□Fe1 and PBA full-cells was conducted within the potential at 1.5–3.8 V (Figure c). The PBA□Fe1 full-cell shows the reversible capacity of 103.4, 87.7, 72.8, 58.9, 43.6, and 35.2 mAh g–1 at current densities of 20, 50, 100, 200, 500, and 1000 mA g–1, respectively. For comparison, PBA only shows lower capacity of 61.2, 36.1, 26.6, 18.9, 11.6, and 7.2 mAh g–1. As shown in Figure d, PBA□Fe1 shows a better cycling stability and higher Coulombic efficiency of 98.5% than that of PBA after 100 cycles in the full-cell configuration. Besides, the PBA□Fe1 full-cell also exhibits higher specific power and specific energy compared to the PBA full-cell (Figure e). Conclusion

In this work, we synthesized deficient PBAs with different Fe vacancies and characterized the materials via XRD, XPS, FT-IR, Raman, and SEM. Then, we explored the relationship between vacancies and electrochemical performance. We reveal that vacancies enhance specific capacity but lead to deteriorative cycling stability. Among the synthesized materials, PBA□Fe1 with low-vacancy content displays a balanced improvement for both capacity and cycling stability. Further analysis of kinetics in PBA□Fe1 was conducted through multisweep CV, EIS, GITT, DFT calculations, and AIMD simulations, which proved the increased sodium diffusion dynamic within vacancy-type PBAs. Moreover, the structural evolution and sodium intercalation behavior were investigated. The operando XRD indicated a mitigated volume change of PBA□Fe1 upon charging and discharging. Ex situ NMR, Raman, and XPS demonstrate high structural reversibility for PBA□Fe1 during sodium insertion/extraction. Finally, the practical application potential was displayed in the PBA□Fe1 full-cell. These findings not only elucidate the critical role of Fe vacancies in optimizing properties for PBAs but also highlight the defect engineering to develop the electrodes for SIBs.

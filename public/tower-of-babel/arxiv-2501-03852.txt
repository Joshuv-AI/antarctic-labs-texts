On ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -towers of graph coverings arising from a constant voltage assignment
Antonio Lei, Katharina Müller

Abstract
. We investigate properties of ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -towers of graph coverings that arise from a constant voltage assignment. We prove the existence and uniqueness (up to isomorphisms) of such towers. Furthermore, we study the Iwasawa invariants of these towers, and apply our results to towers of isogeny graphs enhanced with level structures, as well as towers arising from volcano graphs.

05C25, 11R23

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

Let
p
be a fixed prime number. We write
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
for the ring of
p
-adic integers. Vallières, Gonet, and McGown–Vallières initiated the study of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-towers of graph coverings (see
[
Val21
,
MV23
,
MV24
,
Gon21
,
Gon22
]
). Let
X
be a finite connected undirected graph. A
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower of graph coverings
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
over
X
is a series of graph coverings
X_{n}/X
, where
X_{n}
is a connected undirected graph such that
X_{n}/X
is a Galois covering whose Galois group is isomorphic to the cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
. We write
\kappa_{n}
for the number of spanning trees of
X_{n}
. Then there exist integers
\mu
,
\lambda
and
\nu
such that

(1.1)

\ord_{p}(\kappa_{n})=\mu p^{n}+\lambda n+\nu

for
n\gg 0
. This is analogous to the classical result of Iwasawa on class numbers of finite sub-extensions inside a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension a number field
[
Iwa73
]
.

A
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower of graph coverings over an undirected
X
can be constructed using a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-valued voltage assignment on a
directed
graph (after choosing an orientation for each edge of
X
). The graph
X_{n}
can be obtained from the derived graph attached to a
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
-valued voltage assignment, which is a priori a directed graph (see Remark
2.7
for a detailed discussion).

In the present article, we study
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-towers of graph coverings over
directed
graphs. We say that a directed graph is connected if its image under the forgetful map (which is the natural map that sends a directed edge to an undirected edge) is connected as an undirected graph. We are interested in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-towers that arise from a voltage assignment on a finite connected directed graph whose image consists of a single element (i.e. a constant voltage assignment).

The primary motivation for studying such
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-towers is that they naturally occur as isogeny graphs enhanced with level structures, as demonstrated in
[
LM25a
]
. We briefly recall the definition of these graphs below.

Let
p
and
r
be two distinct prime numbers.
Let
N
be a natural number coprime to
rp
and let
\ell
be a prime such that
\ell\equiv 1\pmod{Np}
.
We fix a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where
q
is an even power of
r
. We say that two elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
are equivalent if they are isomorphic over
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
. We fix a set of representatives of the equivalence classes of elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, which we denote by
S
.
Given two elliptic curves
E,E^{\prime}\in S
, an isogeny
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E\to E^{\prime}
is a non-constant morphism of varieties that sends the point at infinity of
E
to the point at infinity of
E^{\prime}
. We say that
\phi
is an
\ell
-isogeny if its degree is equal to
\ell
. Since
\ell\nmid q
, this is equivalent to
\ker(\phi)
containing
\ell

E(\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}})
.

Definition 1.1
.

For an integer
n\geq 0
, we define the directed graph
X_{\ell}^{q}(Np^{n})
whose vertices are given by triples
(E,Q_{1},Q_{2})
, where

is a basis of

are given by
\ell
-isogenies, i.e. the set of edges from
(E,Q_{1},Q_{2})
to

is the set of
\ell
-isogenies

such that
\phi(Q_{1})=Q_{1}^{\prime}
and
\phi(Q_{2})=Q_{2}^{\prime}
.

We define the directed graph
Y_{\ell}^{q}(p^{n}N)
as follows.
The set of vertices of
Y_{\ell}^{q}(Np^{n})
is given by tuples
(E,R_{1},R_{2},\zeta)

is a basis of

\zeta

-th root of unity. Note that we can write a point

of order

as

, where

is of order

-torsion. Let

be the following surjection

\displaystyle\Psi_{n}\colon V(X_{\ell}^{q}(Np^{n}))

\displaystyle\to V(Y_{\ell}^{q}(Np^{n})),

\displaystyle(E,Q_{1},Q_{2})

\displaystyle\mapsto(E,R_{1},R_{2},\langle P_{1},P_{2}\rangle),

denotes the Weil pairing.
For every vertex

under

.
The edges of

are given as follows:
for every edge from

to some

.

Let

be the supersingular subgraph of

as the pre-image of

under the natural projection

gives rise to a

-tower over

\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-towers.

1.2.
Main results

We begin with a theorem that gives a sufficient and necessary condition for the existence of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-towers over a graph
X
given by a constant voltage assignment. Furthermore, we show that it is unique up to isomorphisms of graphs. The proof is straightforward and builds on established results from the literature. Given a directed graph
X
, let
\tilde{X}
be the directed graph, where we add a supplementary edge
e^{\iota}
to
X
for each edge
e
of
X
. Given a path
P
in
\tilde{X}
, we define its
weight
to be the number of edges of
P
that belong to
X
minus the number of supplementary edges in
P
(see Definition
3.4
).

Theorem A

(Corollaries
3.6
,
3.7
and
3.12
)
.

Let
X
be a finite connected directed graph. Let
\alpha
be a constant
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-valued voltage assignment on
X
whose image is an element of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
.

(a)

The derived graphs of
\alpha
modulo
p^{n}
give rise to a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower of graph coverings if and only if
\tilde{X}
admits a directed cycle that is of weight coprime to
p
. Furthermore, the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower is independent of
\alpha
, up to isomorphism of graphs.

(b)

If
\tilde{X}
contains a cycle, the derived graphs of
\alpha
modulo
p^{n}
give rise to a disjoint union of isomorphic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-towers over
X
.

In particular, given a finite connected directed graph that contains a cycle, Theorem
A
tells us that there is a
canonical

\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower over
X
, to which we shall refer as the
constant
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower
over
X
. After applying the forgetful map, we can regard this as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower of undirected graphs. The Iwasawa invariants of this tower as given in (
1.1
) will be denoted by
\mu(X)
and
\lambda(X)
. We call them the
\mu
-invariant and the
\lambda
-invariant of
X
.

The following theorem gives sufficient conditions for the (non-)vanishing of the
\mu
-invariant of a constant
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower.

Theorem B

(Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
)
.

Let
X
be a finite connected directed graph that contains a cycle.

(a)

Suppose that
p
divides the in-degree and out-degree of each vertex of
X
, then
\mu(X)>0
.

(b)

Suppose that the total degree of each vertex (i.e. the sum of the in-degree and out-degree) is equal to some constant
k
that is coprime to
p
. If the adjacency matrix of
X
is normal, then
\mu(X)=0
.

Let
X^{\mathrm{ss}}
be the supersingular subgraph of
X_{\ell}^{q}(Np)
given by Definition
1.1
and define
X^{\mathrm{ss}}_{n}
as its pre-image in
X_{\ell}^{q}(Np^{n})
. We recall from
[
LM25a
, Corollary 4.7]
that there exists a minimal integer
m_{0}
such that the number of connected components of
X_{n}^{\mathrm{ss}}
stabilizes. Let
Z_{m_{0}}
be a connected component of
X^{\mathrm{ss}}_{m_{0}}
. For
n\geq m_{0}
, let
Z_{n}
be the pre-image of
Z_{m_{0}}
in
X_{n}
, then
Z_{n}/Z_{m_{0}}
is Galois. Furthermore, the inverse limit
G\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\displaystyle\varprojlim_{n\geq m_{0}}\Gal(Z_{n}/Z_{m_{0}})
is an open subgroup of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.

Let

H_{n}=\left\{x\in\textup{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}N\mathbb{Z})\mid x\equiv I\pmod{Np}\right\}

and
Z^{\prime}_{n}=(Z_{n})^{H_{n}}
. We prove that
(Z^{\prime}_{n})_{n\geq m_{0}}
is a constant
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower over
Z^{\prime}_{m_{0}}
. Furthermore, it can be realized as an intermediate tower of graph coverings of
(Z_{n})_{n\geq m_{0}}
. This allows us to formulate a
\mathfrak{M}_{H}(G)
-conjecture for
(Z_{n})_{n\geq m_{0}}
analogously to the counterpart in non-commutative Iwasawa theory as studied in
[
CFK+05
]
(see Section
4.2
for details). This conjecture is of interest since it implies that the
K
-theoretic formulation of the non-commutative Iwasawa main conjecture is true for the tower
(Z_{n})_{n\geq m_{0}}
(as demonstrated in
[
KM23
]
). In the present article, we prove that the
\mathfrak{M}_{H}(G)
-conjecture holds for
(Z_{n})_{n\geq m_{0}}
:

Corollary C

(Corollaries
4.7
and
5.4
)
.

Assume that
p>2
.
Let
(Z^{\prime}_{n})_{n\geq m_{0}}
be the constant
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower over
Z^{\prime}_{m_{0}}
. Then
\mu(Z^{\prime}_{m_{0}})=0
. Furthermore, the
\mathfrak{M}_{H}(G)
-conjecture holds for the tower
(Z_{n})_{n\geq m_{0}}
. If in addition, the number of spanning trees of
Z^{\prime}_{m_{0}}
and the number of vertices of
Z^{\prime}_{m_{0}}
is not divisible by
p
, then
\lambda(Z^{\prime}_{m_{0}})=1
.

Theorem
B
only proves the vanishing of
\mu(X)
for graphs where the degree of each vertex is equal to the same constant
k
. Under mild additional assumptions, we prove that the
\mu
-invariant vanishes and the
\lambda
-invariant is one for balanced directed graphs (i.e. for each vertex, the in-degree and out-degree are equal).

Theorem D

(Theorem
5.2
)
.

Let
X
be a finite directed and balanced graph. Suppose that
p>2
or
{\tilde{X}}
contains a cycle whose weight is coprime to
p
. Let
k
be the sum of the in-degree (or equivalently out-degree) of all the vertices of
X
. Let
\kappa_{X}
denote the number of spanning trees in the image of
X
under the forgetful map. If
p\not\mid k\kappa_{X}
, then
\mu(X)=0
and
\lambda(X)=1
.

The last assertion of Corollary
C
is proved using Theorem
D
.

Readers familiar with the classical Iwasawa theory of ideal class groups of number fields may find it interesting that the condition
p\not\mid k\kappa_{X}
resembles the definition of a regular prime. Indeed,
p
is said to be regular if it does not divide the class number of
\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p})
. When
p
is a regular prime, the Iwasawa invariants of the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p})
are trivial. The
\lambda
-invariant of a balanced graph
X
such that
\tilde{X}
contains a cycle of weight coprime to
p
is at least one (see Remark
5.3
). Therefore, the analogue of the regularity condition in the context of graph coverings (that is,
p\nmid k\kappa_{X}
) guarantees that the Iwasawa invariants are minimum.

The last family of graphs we study in this article are the so-called volcano graphs, which are neither regular nor balanced. We study their constant
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower coverings and compute the corresponding Iwasawa invariants. These graphs are used to describe the isogeny graphs defined by ordinary elliptic curves, as shown in
[
Koh96
]
.

Theorem E

(Corollaries
6.7
and
6.10
)
.

Let
X
be a finite volcano graph.

(a)

If the crater of
X
is a cycle, then
\mu(X)=0
and
\lambda(X)=1
.

(b)

If the crater of
X
is a single vertex together with two loops, then
\mu(X)=v_{p}(2)
and
\lambda(X)=1
.

The reader is referred to §
6
for a reminder of the definitions of a volcano graph and its crater. We remark that the proof of Theorem
E
relies on an explicit count of spanning trees in a constant
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower, rather than relying on Theorem
D
. Nonetheless, Theorem
D
can be applied to certain isogeny graphs of ordinary elliptic curves, which are closely related to volcano graphs; see Remark
6.14
for a detailed discussion.

2.
Notions in graph theory

We recall notions in graph theory that will be used throughout the article. Given a (directed or undirected) graph
X
(multiple edges and loops are allowed), we write
\mathbb{V}(X)
and
\mathbb{E}(X)
for the set of vertices and edges of
X
, respectively. If
X
is a directed graph, given
v\in\mathbb{V}(X)
, we write
d_{o}(v)
and
d_{i}(v)
for the out-degree and the in-degree of
v
, respectively. In the case of undirected graph, we write
\deg(v)
for the degree of a vertex.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be an undirected graph (finite or infinite) such that the degree of every vertex is finite. For every vertex
v
we define the
principal divisor

P_{v}=\deg(v)v-\sum_{e\in\mathbb{E}_{v}}t(e),

where
\mathbb{E}_{v}
is the set of edges adjacent to
v
and
t(e)
is the second vertex reached by the edge
e
.

We define the
divisor group
of
X
by

\textup{Div}(X)=\left\{\sum_{v\in\mathbb{V}(X)}a_{v}v\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}a_{v}\in\mathbb{Z},a_{v}=0\textup{ for all but finitely many $v$}\right\}.

The group of principal divisors is the subgroup of
\textup{Div}(X)
generated by
P_{v}
as
v
runs through
v\in\mathbb{V}(X)
:

\textup{Pr}(X)=\langle P_{v}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}v\in\mathbb{V}(X)\rangle\subseteq\textup{Div}(X).

The
Picard group
of
X
is defined as the quotient

\textup{Pic}(X)=\textup{Div}(X)/\textup{Pr}(X).

If
X
is a directed graph, we define these objects correspondingly after applying the forgetful map to
X
.

We recall that the number of spanning trees in a finite connected undirected graph is equal to the cardinality of the torsion subgroups of the Picard group.

Definition 2.2
.

If
Y/X
is a covering of directed graphs with the projection map
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Y\to X
, the group of
deck transformations
of
Y/X
, denoted by
\mathrm{Deck}(Y/X)
, is the group of graph automorphisms
\sigma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Y\to Y
such that
\pi\circ\sigma=\pi
.

We say that
Y/X
is
d
-sheeted
if
d
is a positive integer such that each element of
V(X)
has
d
pre-images in
V(Y)
.

Finally, We say that the covering
Y/X
is
Galois
if it is a
d
-sheeted covering and
|\textup{Deck}(Y/X)|=d
.

Galois coverings can be constructed using voltage assignments, which we recall below.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a directed graph and
(G,\cdot)
a group. A
G
-valued
voltage assignment
on
X
is a function
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{E}(X)\rightarrow G
.

Given a
G
-valued voltage assignment on
X
, we define the derived graph
X(G,\alpha)
whose vertices and edges are given by
\mathbb{V}(X)\times G
and
\mathbb{E}(X)\times G
, respectively. If
e\in\mathbb{E}(X)
is an edge of
X
going from
s
to
t
, then the edge
(e,\sigma)\in\mathbb{E}(X(G,\alpha))=\mathbb{E}(X)\times G
goes from
(s,\sigma)
to
(t,\sigma\cdot\alpha(e))
.

The derived graph
X(G,\alpha)
naturally gives rise to a covering of
X
(defined by
Y\rightarrow X
,
(x,\sigma)\mapsto x
).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
X
be a directed graph and
(G,\cdot)
a group. Let
\Phi
be an automorphism on
G
and
\alpha
a
G
-valued voltage assignment on
X
. Then
X(G,\alpha)
and
X(G,\Phi\circ\alpha)
are isomorphic direct graphs.

Proof.

The automorphism
\Phi
defines a natural map
X(G,\alpha)\rightarrow X(G,\Phi\circ\alpha)
given by
(x,\sigma)\mapsto(x,\Phi(\sigma))
. A direct computation shows that this is an isomorphism of graphs.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

Suppose that
\alpha
is a
G
-valued voltage assignment on a finite connected directed graph
X
and the derived graph
X(G,\alpha)
is connected, then
Y/X
is Galois, whose Galois group is isomorphic to

(see
[
Gon22
, Theorem 2.10]
and
[
Gon21

).

Definition 2.6
.

Let
X
be a directed graph. A
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-covering
of
X
, or a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower
over
X
, is a sequence of graph coverings

X=X_{0}\leftarrow X_{1}\leftarrow X_{2}\leftarrow\cdots\leftarrow X_{n}\leftarrow\cdots

such that for each
n\geq 0
, the cover
X_{n}/X
is Galois with Galois group isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
.

Remark 2.7
.

In
[
Gon21
,
Gon22
,
Val21
,
MV23
,
MV24
]
, a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-covering of an undirected graph
X
is constructed as follows. Let
\tilde{X}
be the directed graph such that
\mathbb{V}(\tilde{X})=\mathbb{V}(X)
and we define
\mathbb{E}(\tilde{X})
to be the set of edges obtained from assigning each edge
e
of
X
to two oriented edges
e_{1},e_{2}
, joining the same vertices as
e
, with
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
going in opposite directions. Let
\alpha
be a voltage assignment on
\tilde{X}
such that
\alpha(e_{1})=\alpha(e_{2})^{-1}
for all
e\in\mathbb{E}(X)
. In the derived graph
\tilde{X}(G,\alpha)
, the edges come in pairs, going into opposite directions. We obtain an undirected graph after identifying such two edges with a single undirected edge.

Alternatively, we can obtain a directed graph
X^{\prime}
by assigning each edge of
X
a direction and define a
G
-valued voltage assignment
\alpha^{\prime}
on
X^{\prime}
. We obtain an undirected graph after applying the forgetful to
X^{\prime}(G,\alpha^{\prime})
.

A
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-valued voltage assignment results in
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
-valued assignments for all integer
n\geq 0
. The procedures described above give rise to a sequence of coverings of undirected graphs

X\leftarrow X_{1}\leftarrow X_{2}\leftarrow\cdots X_{n}\leftarrow\cdots.

If each
X_{n}
is connected, this is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower over
X
. We refer the reader to
[
DLRV24
, Corollary 2.16]
for a criterion of the connectedness condition.

We can calculate the
\mu
- and
\lambda
-invariants in (
1.1
) by an explicit power series. Let
\{v_{1},\dots v_{r}\}
be the set of vertices of
X
. Let
\alpha
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-valued assignment on
\tilde{X}
. Define

M_{X,\alpha}(T)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\det(D-A_{\alpha})\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[T]]

where
D
denotes the valency matrix of
X
and
A_{\alpha}
is the
r\times r
matrix whose
(i,j)
-entry is given by
\sum_{e}(1+T)^{\alpha(e)}
, where the sum runs over all edges in
\tilde{X}
that go from
v_{i}
to
v_{j}
. When
T=0
,
A_{\alpha}
becomes the adjacency matrix of
X
and so
M_{X,\alpha}(0)
is the determinant of the Laplacian of
X
, which is zero. In particular,
T
divides
M_{X,\alpha}(T)
. The
p
-adic Weierstrass preparation theorem implies that

M_{X,\alpha}(T)=p^{m}Tg(T)u(T),

for some integer
m\geq 0
, a distinguished polynomial
g(T)
and
u(T)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[T]]^{\times}
. The invariants in (
1.1
) satisfy
\mu=m
and
\lambda=\deg(g)
.

For the rest of the article, we will only consider directed graphs that are finite and connected.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
X
be a finite connected directed graph. Let
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower over
X
. Then
X_{n}
is connected for all
n
.

Proof.

Suppose
X_{n}
contains
r
connected components. It follows from
[
LM25a
, proof of Corollary 2.5]
that the connected components are isomorphic to each other. In particular,
\Gal(X_{n}/X)
admits a subgroup of order
r!
(given by the permutations of the connected components). Since
\Gal(X_{n}/X)\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
, this is only possible if
p=r=2
. Suppose that
p=r=2
. Then
X_{n}\simeq Y_{n}\bigsqcup Y_{n}
, where
Y_{n}
is a connected graph, and
Y_{n}/X
is a graph covering of degree
2^{n-1}
. In particular, the group of Deck transformations of
X_{n}/X
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{Deck}(Y_{n}/X)\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, which is not cyclic whenever
n\geq 2
. Thus,
r=1
. If
X_{n}
is connected for
n\geq 2
, then
X_{1}
is also connected since
X_{n}
is a covering of
X_{1}
.
∎

3.
Derived graphs arising from a constant voltage assignment

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
A
. We begin with the following definitions that will be used throughout the section.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\alpha
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-valued voltage assignment on a graph
X
. Given an integer
n\geq 1
, we write
\alpha_{/n}
for the voltage assignment given by the composition of
\alpha
with the projection map
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let

Lemma 3.3

Let

be a finite connected directed graph. Let

be a constant

-tower over

.

Suppose that

sends all

Definition 3.4

Let

Proposition 3.5
.

Let

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
X
be a finite connected directed graph such that
\tilde{X}
contains a cycle. Let
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a constant
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower over
X
. Suppose that
p
divides the in-degree and out-degree of each vertex of

\mu
-invariant of the tower
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is positive.

Proof.

Consider

M_{X}(T)=\det\left(D-A(1+T)-A^{t}(1+T)^{-1}\right),

as in Definition
3.8
. The sum of the columns of the matrix
D-A(1+T)-A^{t}(1+T)^{-1}
is a column vector whose entries are given by

d_{i}(v)+d_{o}(v)-d_{o}(v)(1+T)-d_{i}(v)(1+T)^{-1},v\in\mathbb{V}(X).

Therefore, if
p|d_{i}(v)
and
p|d_{o}(v)
for all
v\in\mathbb{V}(X)
, the power series
M_{X}(T)
is divisible by
p
. Hence,
\mu(X)>0
by Proposition
3.16
.
∎

Note that if
X
is the
\ell
-isogeny graph of supersingular elliptic curve studied in
[
LM25a
]
, the in-degree and out-degree of each vertex are both equal to

. In particular, if

, we would obtain a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower that has positive
\mu
-invariant. This is a special case of Example 5.7 discussed in
op. cit.

We now prove part (b) of Theorem
B
, which can be considered as a partial converse of part (a).

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
X
be a finite connected directed graph such that
\tilde{X}
contains a cycle. Assume that
d_{i}(v)+d_{o}(v)
takes the same value
k
for all
v\in\mathbb{V}(X)
. Furthermore, assume that
p\nmid k
and that the adjacency matrix
A
is normal. Then
\mu(X)=0
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.16
, it suffices to show that the power series

M_{X}(T)=\det\left(D-A(1+T)-A^{t}(1+T)^{-1}\right)

is not divisible by
p
.
The valency matrix
D
is equal to
kI
. Since
A
is a normal matrix, there exists a unitary matrix
U
such that
U^{*}AU=\mathrm{diag}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r})
for some complex numbers
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}
. Note that since
A
is defined over
\mathbb{Z}
, all the eigenvalues
\alpha_{i}
are algebraic integers. Furthermore, we have
U^{*}A^{t}U=\mathrm{diag}(\overline{\alpha}_{1},\dots,\overline{\alpha}_{r})
, where
\overline{\alpha}_{i}
denotes the complex conjugation of
\alpha_{i}
.

We have

\displaystyle M_{X}(T)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(k-\alpha_{i}(1+T)-\overline{\alpha}_{i}(1+T)^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(k-\alpha_{i}-\overline{\alpha}_{i}+(\overline{\alpha}_{i}-\alpha_{i})T+\overline{\alpha}_{i}(-T^{2}+T^{3}-\dots)\right).

Therefore, it suffices to prove that each power series

k-\alpha_{i}-\overline{\alpha}_{i}+(\overline{\alpha}_{i}-\alpha_{i})T-\overline{\alpha}_{i}(-T^{2}+T^{3}-\dots)

does not belong to
\mathfrak{m}[[T]]
, where
\mathfrak{m}
denotes the maximal ideal of the ring of integers of the field
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r})
.

If
v_{p}(k-\alpha_{i}-\overline{\alpha}_{i})=0
, then our claim is immediately valid. If there exists
i
such that
v_{p}(k-\alpha_{i}-\overline{\alpha}_{i})>0
, then
v_{p}(\alpha_{i}+\overline{\alpha}_{i})=0
since
p\nmid k
. As

\alpha_{i}+\overline{\alpha}_{i}=2\overline{\alpha}_{i}-(\overline{\alpha}_{i}-\alpha_{i}),

we have either
v_{p}(\overline{\alpha_{i}})=0
or
v_{p}(\overline{\alpha}_{i}-\alpha_{i})=0
. Thus, our claim follows.
∎

Remark 4.3

If

is normal.

More generally, as discussed in

p
by Lemma
6.4
, this is equivalent to
j\equiv 0\mod p^{n}a
. Therefore,
\mathbb{V}_{0}(X_{n})
consists of
p^{n_{0}}
copies of a cycle of length
p^{n}a
, as desired.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
E
(a):

Corollary 6.7
.

Let
X
be an abstract
\ell
-volcano whose crater is a cycle. Then
\mu(X)=0
and
\lambda(X)=1
.

Proof.

The proof of Proposition
6.6
tells us that
\hat{X}_{n}
is an abstract
\ell
-volcano whose crater is a cycle of length
p^{n}a
, where
p\nmid a
. The spanning trees for such a graph are obtained from deleting exactly one of the edges in the crater. In particular, the number of spanning trees is exactly
p^{n}a
. Therefore,
\mu(X)=0
and
\lambda(X)=1
.
∎

We introduce the following definition to study abstract volcano graphs in which the crater consists of a single vertex with two self-loops, which were excluded in Proposition
6.6
.

Definition 6.8
.

A finite undirected graph is said to be a
double crater
if its set of vertices is of the form
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{s}\}
for some positive integer
s\geq 2
such that there are exactly two edges between
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
whenever
|i-j|\equiv 1\mod s
and there is no edge between
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
otherwise.

A finite undirected graph
X
is said to be an
abstract augmented
\ell
-volcano
of depth
d\geq 0
if
\displaystyle\mathbb{V}(X)=\bigsqcup_{i=0}^{d}V_{i}
such that there exists a positive integer
\ell
such that:

•

The subgraph induced by
V_{0}
is an abstract double crater;

•

All vertices in
V_{1}\bigsqcup\dots\bigsqcup V_{d-1}
have degree
\ell+1
, whereas those in
V_{0}
have degree
\ell+3
and those in
V_{d}
have degree
1
. If
d=0
all vertices have degree
4
.

•

If
u\in V_{r}
and
v\in V_{k}
are connected by an edge, then
|r-k|\leq 1
;

•

For all
0<r\leq d
, the subgraph induced by
V_{r}
is totally disconnected;

•

For
0<r\leq d
, each vertex in
V_{r}
admits exactly one edge to a vertex in
V_{r-1}
.

As before, the subgraph induced by
V_{0}
is called the
crater
of
X
.
The vertices in
V_{r}
are said to be at
level

r
.

Lemma 6.9
.

Let
X
be a depth-zero abstract
\ell
-volcano whose crater consists of one vertex together with two self-loops. Let
\alpha
be a constant
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-valued voltage assignment on
X
. If the image of
\alpha
lies in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
, then
X(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z},\alpha_{/n})
is a double crater with
p^{n}
vertices.

Proof.

Let
v
be the unique vertex of
X
. Suppose that the image of
\alpha
is
\{1\}
. The vertices of
X(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z},\alpha_{/n})
are of the form
(v,a)
, where
a\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
. Furthermore, there are two edges between
(v,a)
and
(v,a+1)
, whereas there is no edge between
(v,a)
and
(v,b)
when
|a-b|\not\equiv 1\mod p^{n}
. This coincides with the definition of a double crater.
∎

This allows us to prove Theorem
E
(b):

Corollary 6.10
.

Let
X
be an abstract
\ell
-volcano whose crater is a single vertex with two loops. Let
\alpha
be a constant voltage cover on
X
. Then
X_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=X(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z},\alpha_{/n})
is an abstract augmented
\ell
-volcano for all
n\geq 1
. Furthermore,
\lambda(X)=1
and
\mu(X)=v_{p}(2)
.

Proof.

The proof of the first assertion is essentially the same as Proposition
6.6
, except the description of
\mathcal{X}_{n}=X(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z},\beta_{n})
, which is given by Lemma
6.9
.

It remains to determine the Iwasawa invariants. The number of spanning trees of
X_{n}
is equal to the number of spanning trees of
\mathcal{X}_{n}
. As it is a double crater of length

, the number of spanning trees is
2^{p^{n}-1}p^{n}

and

.
∎

Remark 6.11
.

We emphasize that the constant
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-towers studied by Theorem
E
are distinct from the ones constructed in
[
LM24
,
LM25a
]
. Indeed, the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-towers in the aforementioned work consist of the so-called tectonic volcanoes, where the craters are not cycles. Indeed, the in-degree and out-degree of each vertex are 2, not 1.

In
[
Koh96
,
BCP22
]
, the volcanic structure of the isogeny graphs of ordinary elliptic curves is obtained after identifying an isogeny with its dual. More precisely, suppose
E
and
E^{\prime}
give rise to two vertices in the graph
Y_{\ell}^{q}(1)
. If
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E\rightarrow E^{\prime}
is an
\ell
-isogeny, there is a dual isogeny
\hat{\phi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E^{\prime}\rightarrow E
of the same degree. These two isogenies result in two directed edges going in opposite directions in
Y_{\ell}^{q}(1)
. These two edges are identified as one single undirected edge in
[
Koh96
,
BCP22
]
.

We conclude the article with a brief discussion on constant
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-towers that arise from isogeny graphs of ordinary elliptic curves that are, as discussed above, closely related to abstract
\ell
-volcanoes.

Lemma 6.12
.

Let
X
be an abstract
\ell
-volcano of depth
d
. Assume that the crater is a cycle graph of length
k\geq 2
or a single vertex with two loops (in which case we set
k=1
). Then the total degree of
X
is
2k\ell^{d-1}
.
If the crater is a single vertex with a single loop, the total degree of
X
is

(\ell-1)^{-1}(\ell^{d+1}2-(\ell+1)).

If the crater is a single vertex without any edges. Then the total degree is

2(\ell+1)(\ell-1)^{-1}(\ell^{d}-1).

Proof.

For every level
i\geq 2
, we have

|\{\textup{vertices of level $i$}\}|=|\{\textup{vertices of level $i-1$}\}|\cdot\ell.

Each vertex of level
i
for
1\leq i\leq d-1
has in and out degree
(\ell+1)
.
Finally, every vertex of level
d
has degree
1
. If the crater is a cycle graph of length
k\geq 2
or if the crater is a single vertex with two loops (so
k=1
), there are

k
vertices of level zero and
(\ell-1)k
vertices of level
1
.
Thus, the total degree is

\displaystyle\ (\ell+1)(k+k(\ell-1)+k(\ell-1)\ell+\dots+k(\ell-1)\ell^{d-2})+k(\ell-1)\ell^{d-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ (\ell+1)(k+k(\ell-1)(\ell^{d-1}-1)(\ell-1)^{-1})+k(\ell-1)\ell^{d-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ (\ell+1)k\ell^{d-1}+k(\ell-1)\ell^{d-1}=2k\ell^{d-1}.

If the crater is a single vertex with one loop, there is one vertex of level zero and
\ell
vertices of level
1
.
We obtain a total degree of

\displaystyle\ (\ell+1)(1+\ell+\dots\ell^{d-1})+\ell^{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ (\ell+1)(\ell^{d}-1)(\ell-1)^{-1}+\ell^{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ (\ell-1)^{-1}(\ell^{d+1}2-(\ell+1)).

If the crater is a single vertex without edges, there are
(\ell+1)
vertices of level one and we obtain

\displaystyle\ (\ell+1)(1+(\ell+1)+\ell(\ell+1)+\dots(\ell+1)\ell^{d-2})+(\ell+1)\ell^{d-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ (\ell+1)(1+(\ell+1)(\ell^{d-1}-1)(\ell-1)^{-1})+(\ell+1)\ell^{d-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ (\ell+1)(\ell-1)^{-1}((\ell-1)+(\ell+1)(\ell^{d-1}-1)+(\ell-1)\ell^{d-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ 2(\ell+1)(\ell-1)^{-1}(\ell^{d}-1).

∎

Corollary 6.13
.

Let
X
be an abstract
\ell
-volcano. Assume that the crater is a cycle of length
k
or a single vertex with two loops. Let
\tilde{X}
be the directed graph such that
\mathbb{V}(\tilde{X})=\mathbb{V}(X)
and
\mathbb{E}(X)
is defined by assigning an arbitrary orientation to each edge of
X
and adding an additional edge in the opposite direction (except for loops). Assume that
p
is coprime to
2k\ell
. Then
\mu(\tilde{X})=0
and
\lambda(\tilde{X})=1
.

Proof.

Note that the number of spanning trees of
\tilde{X}
is
k2^{k-1}
if
k\geq 2
and
2
if
k=1
. In particular,
\kappa_{X}
is not divisible by
p
under our hypotheses. Furthermore,
\tilde{X}
is balanced by construction. Lemma
6.12
says that the total degree is
2k\ell^{d-1}
, which is also coprime to
p
. Thus, we can apply Theorem
5.2
to deduce that
\mu(\tilde{X})=0
and
\lambda(\tilde{X})=1
.
∎

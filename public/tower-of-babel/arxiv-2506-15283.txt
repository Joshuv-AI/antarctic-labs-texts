Anomalous energy correlations and spectral form factor in the nonergodic phase of the β \beta -ensemble
Basudha Roy, Adway Kumar Das, Anandamohan Ghosh, Ivan M. Khaymovich

Abstract
The β \beta -ensemble is a prototypical model of a single particle system on a one-dimensional disordered lattice with inhomogeneous nearest neighbor hopping. Corresponding nonergodic phase has an anomalous critical energy scale, E c E_{c} : correlations are present above and absent below E c E_{c} as reflected in the number variance. We study the dynamical properties of the β \beta -ensemble where the critical energy controls the characteristic timescales. In particular, the spectral form factor equilibrates at a relaxation time, t R ≡ E c − 1 t_{\mathrm{R}}\equiv E_{c}^{-1} , which is parametrically smaller than the Heisenberg time, t H t_{\mathrm{H}} , given by the inverse of the mean level spacing. Incidentally, the dimensionless relaxation time, τ R ≡ t R / t H ≪ 1 \tau_{\mathrm{R}}\equiv t_{\mathrm{R}}/t_{\mathrm{H}}\ll 1 is equal to the Dyson index, β \beta . We show that the energy correlations are absent within a temporal window t R < t < t H t_{\mathrm{R}}<t<t_{\mathrm{H}} , which we term as the correlation void . This is in contrast to the mechanism of equilibration in a typical many-body system. We analytically explain the qualitative behavior of the number variance and the spectral form factor of the β \beta -ensemble by a spatially local mapping to the Anderson model.

The Fourier transform of the two-level cluster function gives the two-level form factor in the dimensionless time domain,
\tau

\displaystyle b_{2}(\tau)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\Delta\mathcal{E}\>Y_{2}(\Delta\mathcal{E})\cos(2\pi\tau\Egap)

(14)

where the complex integral reduces to a real one due to the symmetry
Y_{2}(\Delta\mathcal{E})=Y_{2}(-\Delta\mathcal{E})

Buijsman et al. 2020
. The two-level form factor controls the long time dynamics of a generic observable
Schiulaz et al. 2019
. For Poisson ensemble,
b_{2}(\tau)=0
at any time while for GOE
Mehta 2004

\displaystyle b_{2}^{\mathrm{GOE}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1-2\tau+\tau\log\mathinner{\left(1+2\tau\right)},&\tau\leq 1,\\
\tau\log\left(\dfrac{2\tau+1}{2\tau-1}\right)-1,&\tau>1.\end{cases}

(15)

In the time evolution of a generic observable, the largest possible timescale is the Heisenberg time, defined as the inverse of the mean level spacing
Schiulaz et al. 2019
;
Kos et al. 2018
. For an unfolded spectrum, the mean level spacing is unity, hence, the dimensionless Heisenberg time,
\tau_{\mathrm{H}}=1
for any discrete energy spectrum. Consequently, we expect the two-level form factor to vanish at a sufficiently long time, i.e.
b_{2}(\tau>\tau_{\mathrm{H}})=0
to account for the absence of the energy correlation. However, the two-level form factor of GOE (Eq. (
15
)) has a quadratic decay at long time,
b_{2}^{\mathrm{GOE}}(\tau\gg 1)\sim\tau^{-2}
. Then, we define a tolerance value as

\displaystyle\epsilon\equiv b_{2}^{\mathrm{GOE}}(\tau_{\mathrm{H}})=\log(3)-1\approx 0.0986

(16)

such that for an arbitrary energy spectrum, whenever the two-level form factor falls below
\epsilon
, we expect the dynamics of a generic observable to equilibrate from a vanishing energy correlation. Then, the dimensionless time where the form factor reaches the tolerance value is the relaxation time
\tau_{\mathrm{R}}
, i.e.
b_{2}(\tau_{\mathrm{R}})=\epsilon
.

In Fig.
4

\gamma
and fixed system size, along with the tolerance value,
\epsilon
from Eq. (
16
) shown via horizontal dashed lines. We identify the intersection of the two-level form factor with the tolerance limit from Eq. (
16
) as
\tau_{\mathrm{R}}
, which are shown via markers.

We observe that the two-level form factor exhibits a power-law decay at long time,
b_{2}(\tau>\tau_{\mathrm{R}})\propto\tau^{-\zeta^{(N)}}
. We extract the finite-size power-law exponents for various values of
\gamma
, as shown in Fig.
4
(b) via solid lines with errorbars for various system sizes. From a linear extrapolation of the values of
\zeta^{(N)}
w.r.t.
N^{-1}
, we extract the intercepts i.e. the thermodynamic exponents,
\zeta\equiv\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}\zeta^{(N)}
and show them in Fig.
4
(b)
as a function of
\gamma
via bold line. It shows that the long-time power-law decay of the two-level form factor slows down as we go towards the localized regime. We also observe that away from
\gamma=1
,
1-b_{2}(\tau)
has a linear decay at times, small compared to the relaxation time. Since, the two-level form factor equilibrates at
\tau=\tau_{\mathrm{R}}
, we can approximate the behavior for
\gamma<1
as

\displaystyle b_{2}(\tau)\sim\begin{cases}1-\dfrac{\tau}{c_{\tau}\tau_{\mathrm{R}}},&\tau\ll\tau_{\mathrm{R}}\\
\tau^{-\zeta},&\tau>\tau_{\mathrm{R}}\end{cases}.

(17)

where
c_{\tau}\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
is a function of
\gamma
but independent of system size. In particular,
c_{\tau}=1/2
and
\zeta=2
for GOE (
\gamma=0
). The linear behavior of
b_{2}(\tau)
for
\tau\ll\tau_{\mathrm{R}}
can be understood as follows: from the spatially local equivalence of the
\beta
-ensemble to the Anderson model, we expect the typical energy correlations to be determined by the largest block,
\Delta_{l_{\mathrm{max}}}
. Corresponding energy spectrum is a superposition of
\mathcal{O}(N^{\gamma})
number of independent spectra following the same statistics as GOE, each coming from an independent sub-block. The resultant two-level cluster function is

\displaystyle Y_{2}^{\mathrm{mix}}(\Delta\mathcal{E})=\sum_{k=1}^{N^{\gamma}}f_{k}^{2}Y_{2}^{(k)}(f_{k}\Delta\mathcal{E})

(18)

where
Y_{2}^{(k)}(\Delta\mathcal{E})=Y_{2}^{\mathrm{GOE}}(\Delta\mathcal{E})
and
f_{k}=N^{-\gamma}
is the fraction of energy levels in a single sub-block. Thus, the two-level form factor becomes

\displaystyle b_{2}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\Delta\mathcal{E}\,N^{-\gamma}Y_{2}\left(\frac{\Delta\mathcal{E}}{N^{\gamma}}\right)\cos(2\pi\tau\Egap)

(19)

\displaystyle=b_{2}^{\mathrm{GOE}}(N^{\gamma}\tau)

\displaystyle\equiv b_{2}^{\mathrm{GOE}}\left(\frac{\tau}{2c_{\tau}\tau_{\mathrm{R}}}\right),

which corresponds to the asympototics given by Eq. (
17
). From the local equivalence to 1D Anderson model the typical block of the
\beta
-ensemble has a localization length
\xi\sim 8.75N^{1-\gamma}
(Eq. (

N
at

\displaystyle\gamma_{*}(N)=\frac{\ln 8.75}{\ln N}.

(20)

For finite system sizes, the block Hamiltonian structure should be clearly visible for
\gamma>\gamma_{*}(N)
and one should observe both
\zeta
and

to be independent of
\gamma
and
N
. We show that the two-level form factor collapses as a function of

and exhibits a universal behavior for

, as shown in the inset of Fig.

III.2
Number variance

The spatially local equivalence of the
\beta
-ensemble to the 1D Anderson model (Fig.
3
) shows a multi-scale local spectra manifesting into an atypical long-range correlation in the global energy spectrum. Corresponding critical energy,
\mathcal{E}_{c}
(Eq. (
12
)) is reflected in the heterogeneous behavior of the measures of long-range energy correlations, e.g. number variance
Bäcker et al. 1995
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Sigma^{2}(\Delta\mathcal{E})&=\left\langle[\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{E}_{0},\Delta\mathcal{E})-\Delta\mathcal{E}]^{2}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{0}}\\
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{E}_{0},\Delta\mathcal{E})&=I\mathinner{\left(\mathcal{E}_{0}+\frac{\Delta\mathcal{E}}{2}\right)}-I\mathinner{\left(\mathcal{E}_{0}-\frac{\Delta\mathcal{E}}{2}\right)}\end{split}

(21)

where
I(\mathcal{E})
is the cumulative distribution of the unfolded energy levels,
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{E}_{0},\Delta\mathcal{E})
is the number of energy levels in the window of span
\Delta\mathcal{E}
and centered at
\mathcal{E}_{0}
. The number variance is related to the two-point energy correlation as
Mehta 2004

\displaystyle\Sigma^{2}(\Delta\mathcal{E})=\Delta\mathcal{E}-2\int_{0}^{\Delta\mathcal{E}}dx(\Delta\mathcal{E}-x)Y_{2}(x).

(22)

For GOE, the energy spectrum is rigid so that any two energy levels are correlated with a logarithmic number variance
Mehta 2004

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\mathrm{GOE}}^{2}(\Delta\mathcal{E})\approx\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\left(\ln(2\pi\Egap)+\gamma_{\mathrm{E}}+1-\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}\right)

(23)

where
\gamma_{\mathrm{E}}
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant.
In the Poisson ensemble, the number of unfolded energy levels are free to fluctuate around their mean position,
\left\langle\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{E}_{0},\Delta\mathcal{E})\right\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{0}}=\Delta\mathcal{E}
and the number variance exhibits a linear behavior,
\Sigma^{2}(\Delta\mathcal{E})=\Delta\mathcal{E}
.

In Fig.
5
(a), we show the ensemble averaged number variance of the
\beta
-ensemble for
N=32768
and various values of
\gamma
along with the analytical expressions of the GOE and Poisson ensemble. For sufficiently small energy interval
\Delta\mathcal{E}
,
\Sigma^{2}(\Delta\mathcal{E})
has a linear behavior as in the Poisson ensemble. Upon increasing the energy interval, the number variance deviates from the Poisson behavior and indicates the presence of long-range correlation. In Fig.
5
(a), we show that the inverse of the relaxation time
\tau_{\mathrm{R}}
obtained from the two-level form factor (Fig.
4
(a)) determines the critical energy,
\mathcal{E}_{c}
, above which the long-range correlation exists. Such a critical energy can be clearly identified from the heterogeneous behavior of the power spectrum of noise
Das and Ghosh 2022b
;
Das et al. 2023
, also useful to identify the Thouless energy scale
Berkovits 2020
;
Corps et al. 2020
.

The system size dependence of the critical energy is shown in Fig.
5
(b) in log-log scale, which indicates a power-law dependence, i.e.
\mathcal{E}_{c}\propto N^{\eta}
where the proportionality constant is
\mathcal{O}(1)
and independent of
\gamma
. The inset of Fig.
5
(b) shows that the power-law exponent,
\eta\approx\gamma
and validates Eq. (
12
), i.e.
\mathcal{E}_{c}\propto N^{\gamma}
. Thus, dimensionless relaxation time has the following behavior

\displaystyle\tau_{\mathrm{R}}\approx\mathcal{E}_{c}^{-1}\propto N^{-\gamma}.

(24)

Above relation is a consequence of the Fourier duality between energy and time, where the absence of energy correlation for
\Delta\mathcal{E}<\mathcal{E}_{c}
translates into the relaxation of dynamics for
\tau>\tau_{\mathrm{R}}
. Eq. (
24
) implies that in the NEE phase of the
\beta
-ensemble, the relaxation time is much smaller than the Heisenberg time and decreases with the system size.

\Delta_{l_{\mathrm{max}}}
. For any energy interval of width
\Delta\mathcal{E}<\mathcal{E}_{c}

\mathcal{O}(1)

\mathcal{O}(\Delta\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{E}_{c})\gg 1
number of correlated energy levels as in the GOE. Such a superposition of uncorrelated spectra has the number variance

(25)

where
\Sigma^{2}_{(k)}(\Delta\mathcal{E})=\Sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{GOE}}(\Delta\mathcal{E})
and
f_{k}=N^{-\gamma}
. Combining the short- and long-range behaviors discussed above, we get the number variance of the
\beta
-ensemble as
Ossipov et al. 2012

\displaystyle\Sigma^{2}(\Delta\mathcal{E})\sim\begin{cases}\Delta\mathcal{E},&\Delta\mathcal{E}\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{c}\\
\dfrac{\mathcal{E}_{c}}{\pi^{2}}\left(\ln\dfrac{2\pi\Delta\mathcal{E}}{\mathcal{E}_{c}}\right),&\Delta\mathcal{E}\gtrsim\mathcal{E}_{c}\end{cases}

(26)

where
\mathcal{E}_{c}\sim N^{\gamma}
is the unfolded critical energy (Eq. (
12

In Fig.
5

26
) via dashed gray curves for different values of
\gamma
and find an excellent agreement.

So, a weak long-range energy correlation persists despite the absence of any short-range correlation in the NEE regime of the
\beta
-ensemble. In contrast, the number variance of a typical many-body system away from the localized phase behaves as in the Rosenzweig-Porter ensemble
Efetov 1983

\displaystyle\Sigma^{2}(\Delta\mathcal{E})\propto\begin{cases}\log\Delta\mathcal{E},&\Delta\mathcal{E}<\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{Th}}\\
\Delta\mathcal{E},&\Delta\mathcal{E}>\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{Th}}\end{cases}

(27)

such that the energy levels are correlated below the Thouless energy scale,
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{Th}}
and uncorrelated otherwise. Comparing Eqs. (
26
) and (

\mathcal{E}_{c}
of the
\beta
-ensemble behaves complimentary to

and present an anomalous long-range energy correlation. Similar anomalous critical energy is also expected in constrained matrix models with correlated entries

.

The SFF can be defined in terms of the energy levels
\left\{E_{n}\right\}
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{K}\mathinner{\left(t\right)}&=\frac{\absolutevalue{\sum\limits_{n = 1}^{N} e^{-iE_n t}}^{2}}{N^{2}}=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{n,m=1}^{N}e^{-i(E_{m}-E_{n})t}.\end{split}

(28)

Then, the ensemble averaged SFF can be expressed as
Mehta 2004
;
Schiulaz et al. 2019
;
Das et al. 2025c

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\langle\mathcal{K}\mathinner{\left(t\right)}\right\rangle&\approx\absolutevalue{\int dE\; e^{-iEt}\mean{\rho(E)}}^{2}+\frac{1}{N}\left(1-b_{2}\mathinner{\left(\frac{t}{t_{\mathrm{H}}}\right)}\right)\end{split}

(29)

\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{d}}(t)
and the last term is the connected SFF,
\mathcal{K}_{c}(t)

is the limiting DOS,
b_{2}(t)
is the two-level form factor. The disconnected part of the SFF determines the initial decay,

N^{-1}

.

The connected part of the SFF reflects the energy correlations and forces the SFF to be below the plateau at an intermediate time window, known as the
ramp
or
correlation hole
. The interplay between the slope and ramp part of the SFF leads to the minimum, which is called the
dip
, observed at

\displaystyle\textrm{dip time}\equiv t_{\mathrm{dip}}.

(30)

Hence, the SFF in a generic system exhibits a slope-dip-ramp-plateau structure
Das et al. 2025b

Das et al. 2025c
;
Kumar et al. 2025

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{GOE}}(t)\right\rangle=\frac{16\mathrm{J}_{1}\mathinner{\left(\frac{Nt}{2}\right)}^{2}}{N^{2}t^{2}}-\frac{1}{N}b_{2}^{\mathrm{GOE}}\mathinner{\left(\frac{t}{t_{\mathrm{H}}^{\mathrm{GOE}}}\right)}+\frac{1}{N},

V
Discussion

In this work, we investigate the dynamical properties of the
\beta
-ensemble, focusing on how anomalous energy correlations manifest in the number variance and the spectral form factor (SFF). In the nonergodic phase of the
\beta
-ensemble, a spatially local equivalence to the 1D Anderson model reveals a multi-scale spectrum and a critical dimensionless energy
\mathcal{E}_{c}\sim N^{\gamma}
, where
N
is the system size and the Dyson index
\beta=N^{-\gamma}
. The number variance,
\Sigma^{2}(\Delta\mathcal{E})
, deviates from the Poisson behavior at
\mathcal{E}_{c}
and we verify its system size scaling. The critical energy
\mathcal{E}_{c}
behaves complimentary to the Thouless energy scale observed in typical many-body systems: weak long-range correlations
(
\Sigma^{2}(\Delta\mathcal{E})\sim\mathcal{E}_{c}\ln(2\pi\Egap/\Ecal_c)
) exist above
\mathcal{E}_{c}
but energy correlations are absent below
\mathcal{E}_{c}
(Fig.
1
). We numerically obtain the two-level form factor as a function of the dimensionless time
\tau
and show that the dimensionless relaxation time
\tau_{\mathrm{R}}
is the inverse of
\mathcal{E}_{c}
.

In a typical non-integrable interacting many-body system, the energy correlations follow Eq. (
27
) away from the many-body localization. As a result, the ensemble averaged SFF exhibits a universal behavior dictated by GOE above the Thouless time
Kos et al. 2018
;
Fritzsch and Kieler 2024
. Beyond the Heisenberg time, the discreteness of the energy spectrum ensures the equilibration of the ensemble averaged SFF such that the relaxation time coincides with
t_{\mathrm{H}}
. This is schematically explained in Fig.
1
(a), where we show the connected SFF (Eq. (
29
)). As the many-body localized phase is approached, the Thouless energy gets smaller such that the Thouless time increases and eventually merges with the Heisenberg time such that the correlation hole vanishes. Such a behavior is observed in various spin-chains
Schiulaz et al. 2019
;
Torres-Herrera and Santos 2014a
, 3D Anderson model
Sierant et al. 2020
, and random matrix models
Hopjan and Vidmar 2023b
.

Contrarily, in the NEE phase of the
\beta
-ensemble, the time evolution of a generic observable equilibrates at the relaxation timescale
t_{\mathrm{R}}
, which is parametrically smaller than
t_{\mathrm{H}}
, as schematically shown in Fig.
1
(b). Following the global scaling of the Hamiltonian as in Eq. (
3
),
t_{\mathrm{H}}\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
irrespective of
\gamma
such that
t_{\mathrm{R}}\simeq\tau_{\mathrm{R}}\sim\beta\ll 1
where
\beta
is the Dyson index.
Below
t_{\mathrm{R}}
, the SFF exhibits a correlation hole reflecting the presence of long-range correlation. We show that by monitoring the gradual depletion of the correlation hole, we can identify the localization transition at
\gamma=1
.
We also look at the 4-point SFF, whose non-equilibrium features and scaling of the characteristic timescales are similar to its two-point counterpart. As we approach the localized regime, the relaxation time shifts away from the Heisenberg time, in stark contrast to the typical many-body systems. The absence of energy correlation below the critical energy
\mathcal{E}_{c}
is reflected in the time window
[t_{\mathrm{R}},t_{\mathrm{H}}]
, which we term the
correlation void
. The anomalous critical energy found in the
\beta
-ensemble is also expected to be present in other random matrix models like the banded matrices
Das et al. 2025c
,
constrained ensembles with correlated entries
Mondal et al. 2017
and physical systems like kicked rotor
Pandey et al. 2017
;
Kumar et al. 2020
, complex atoms
Chirikov 1985
, isolated thick wire
Iida et al. 1990
;
Fyodorov and Mirlin 1993
.

The behavior of the SFF provides experimentally accessible signatures of nonergodicity, offering pathways to probe thermalization in synthetic quantum platforms. As a future direction, we would like to study the finite-temperature SFF in the
\beta
-ensemble, with particular emphasis on the effect of temperature on the timescales and equilibrium behavior of the SFF.

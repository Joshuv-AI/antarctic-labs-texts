The zero blocking numbers of generalized Kneser graphs and generalized Johnson graphs
Hau-Yi Lin, Wu-Hsiung Lin, Gerard Jennhwa Chang

Abstract




Abstract.

This paper extends the results by Afzali, Ghodrati and Maimani for the zero blocking numbers of Kneser graphs and Johnson graphs to generalized Kneser graphs and generalized Johnson graphs.

Keywords.

Zero forcing process, zero blocking set, zero blocking number, (generalized) Kneser graph, (generalized) Johnson graph.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification.

05C69, 05C85, 68R10.

Introduction

In a
zero forcing process
, vertices of a graph are colored
black
or
white
initially.
If there exists a black vertex adjacent to exactly one white vertex,
then the white vertex is
forced
to be black.
A
zero forcing set
is an initial set of black vertices in a zero forcing process that ultimately expands to include all vertices of the graph; otherwise we call it a
failed zero forcing set
and its complement, the initial set of white vertices, a
zero blocking set
.
The
zero forcing number

Z(G)
of a graph
G
is the minimum size of a zero forcing set.
The
failed zero forcing number

F(G)
of
G
is the maximum size of a failed zero forcing set.
The
zero blocking number

B(G)
of
G
is the minimum size of a zero blocking set.
It is clear that
F(G)+B(G)=|V(G)|
for every graph
G
.
Hence, studying
F(G)
and
B(G)
are equivalent.

The zero forcing process was introduced in
[
3
]
to study minimum rank problems in linear algebra, and independently in
[
8
]
to explore quantum systems memory transfers in quantum physics.
The computation of the zero forcing number is NP-hard
[
1
]
.
Considerable effort has been made to find exact values and bounds for this
number for specific classes of graphs, and to investigate a variety of related concepts arising in zero forcing processes.

The concept of failed zero forcing number was first introduced in
[
10
]
.
The computation of the failed zero forcing number is NP-hard
[
15
]
.
Results for exact values and bounds for this number were also established in

[
2
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
9
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
16
]
.
Our main concern is the results for the zero blocking numbers of Kneser graphs
K(n,k)
and Johnson graphs
J(n,k)
given by Afzali, Ghodrati and Maimani
[
2
]
.
In particular, we extend their results to generalized Kneser graphs and generalized Johnson graphs.

All graphs in this paper are finite, undirected, without loops and multiple edges.
In a graph, the
neighborhood
of a vertex
x
is the set
N(x)
of all vertices adjacent to
x
and the
closed neighborhood
of
x
is
N[x]=\{x\}\cup N(x)
.
A vertex
x
is
isolated
if
N(x)=\emptyset
.
Two distinct vertices
x
and
y
are
twins
if either
N(x)=N(y)
or
N[x]=N[y]
.

The following are some basic properties for zero blocking numbers.

Proposition 1
.

If a graph
G
has exactly
r
components
G_{1},G_{2},\ldots,G_{r}
, then
B(G)=\min_{1\leq i\leq r}B(G_{i})
.

Proposition 2
.

For any graph
G
, we have
B(G)\geq 1
, and
B(G)=1
if and only if
G
has an isolated vertex.

Proposition 3
.

(
[
10
]
)

For a graph
G
without isolated vertices,
B(G)=2
if and only if
G
has a pair of twins.

For a positive integer
n
, denote
[n]
the set
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.
For integers
a\leq b
, denote
[a,b]
the set
\{a,a+1,\ldots,b\}
.
The
symmetric difference
of two sets
A
and
B
is the set
A\Delta B=(A\cup B)\setminus(A\cap B)
.
For integers
n\geq k>a\geq 0
,
the
generalized Kneser graph
is the graph
K(n,k,a)

whose vertices are the
k
-subsets of
[n]

and two vertices
A
and
B
are adjacent if
|A\cap B|\leq a
,
and the
generalized Johnson graph
is the graph
J(n,k,a)

whose vertices are the
k
-subsets of
[n]

and
A
and
B
are adjacent if
|A\cap B|=a
.
The graphs
K(n,k,0)
were known as Kneser graphs
K(n,k)
and
J(n,k,k-1)
as Johnson graphs
J(n,k)
.
Results for the zero blocking numbers of Kneser graphs and Johnson graphs were established by Afzali, Ghodrati and Maimani
[
2
]
.

Theorem 4
.

(
[
2
]
)

If
k\geq 2
and
n\geq 2k+1
, then
B(K(n,k))=k+2
.
If
k\geq 1
and
n\geq 2k
, then
B(J(n,k))=k+1
except that
B(J(4,2))=2
.

Notice that
B(K(n,k))=1
or
2
when
k=1
or
k\leq n\leq 2k
, since
K(n,1)=K_{n}
,
K(2k,k)=\frac{1}{2}{2k\choose k}K_{2}
and
K(n,k)={n\choose k}K_{1}
for
k\leq n<2k
.
Also,
B(J(n,k))=1
or
2
when
k=1
or
k=n
, since
J(n,1)=K_{n}
and
J(k,k)=K_{1}
.
The following gives
J(n,k)
when
k+1\leq n<2k
.

Proposition 5
.

If
n\geq 2k-a
, then
B(J(n,k,a))=B(J(n,n-k,n-2k+a))
.
Consequently, if
k+1\leq n<2k
, then
B(J(n,k))=n-k+1
.

Proof.

The first part follows from the fact that
J(n,k,a)
is isomorphic to
J(n,n-k,n-2k+a)

under the mapping
A\mapsto[n]\setminus A
.

For the second part, since
n-k\geq 1
and
n>2(n-k)
, by Theorem
4
,
B(J(n,k))=B(J(n,n-k))=n-k+1
.
∎

This paper extends the results by Afzali, Ghodrati and Maimani for the zero blocking numbers of Kneser graphs and Johnson graphs to generalized Kneser graphs and generalized Johnson graphs.
In particular, it is proved that

B(K(n,k,a))=\lceil\frac{k-a}{a+1}\rceil+2
(when
a\leq k-2
) and
B(J(n,k,a))=\max\{a,k-a\}+2
when
n
is relatively larger than
k
and
a
.

2
Results for generalized Kneser graphs
K(n,k,a)

First, all
K(n,k,a)
with
B(K(n,k,a))
no more than two are characterized.

Proposition 6
.

Suppose integers
n\geq k>a\geq 0
.

(1)

If
n\leq 2k-a-1
, then
B(K(n,k,a))=1
.

(2)

If
a=k-1
, then
B(K(n,k,a))=\min\{n-k+1,2\}
.

(3)

If
n=2k
and
a=0
, then
B(K(n,k,a))=2
.

(4)

If
n\geq 2k-a
and
k-2\geq a
but
(n,a)\neq(2k,0)
, then
B(K(n,k,a))\geq 3
.

Proof.

(1) This follows from
K(n,k,a)={n\choose k}K_{1}
.

(2) This follows from
K(n,k,k-1)=K_{n\choose k}
.

(3) This follows from
K(2k,k,0)=\frac{1}{2}{2k\choose k}K_{2}
.

(4) Since
n\geq 2k-a
, every vertex
A
is adjacent to some vertex
B
with
|A\cap B|=a
. Hence
K(n,k,a)
has no isolated vertices.

Next to see that
K(n,k,a)
has no twins.
Consider two distinct vertices
A
and
B
.
Choose
x\in A\setminus B
and
y\in B\setminus A
.

(i)

Suppose
A\cap B=\emptyset
.
If
n>2k
, then there exists
z\in[n]\setminus(A\cup B)
.
In this case,
C=B\Delta\{y,z\}\notin\{A,B\}
is adjacent to
A
but not to
B
, since
|C\cap A|=0\leq a
and
|C\cap B|=k-1>a
.
Otherwise,
n=2k
and so
a\geq 1
, since
(n,a)\neq(2k,0)
.
Then
C=B\Delta\{x,y\}\notin\{A,B\}
is adjacent to
A
but not to
B
, since
|C\cap A|=1\leq a
and
|C\cap B|=k-1>a
.

(ii)

Suppose
0<|A\cap B|\leq a
.
Since
A\cap B\neq\emptyset
, there exists
w\in A\cap B
.
Then
C=B\Delta\{x,w\}\notin\{A,B\}
is adjacent to
A
but not to
B
, since
|C\cap A|=|(A\cap B)\Delta\{x,w\}|=|A\cap B|\leq a
and
|C\cap B|=k-1>a
.

(iii)

Suppose
a<|A\cap B|\leq k-1
.
Choose
C^{\prime}\subsetneqq A\cap B
with
|C^{\prime}|=a
,
and
C^{\prime\prime}\subseteq[n]\setminus A
with
B\setminus A\subsetneqq C^{\prime\prime}
and
|C^{\prime\prime}|=k-a

by the fact that
|B\setminus A|<k-a
.
Then
C=C^{\prime}\cup C^{\prime\prime}\notin\{A,B\}
is adjacent to
A
but not to
B
, since
|C\cap A|=|C^{\prime}|=a
and
|C\cap B|=|C^{\prime}|+|B\setminus A|\geq a+|\{y\}|=a+1
.

In summary,
K(n,k,a)
has no isolated vertices and has no twins.
These give that
B(K(n,k,a))\geq 3
by Propositions
2
and
3
.
∎

Next we consider
B(K(n,k,a))
for
n\geq 2k-a
and
k-2\geq a
but
(n,a)\neq(2k,0)
.
We are only able to determine
B(K(n,k,a))
for
n
relatively larger than
k
and
a
.

Lemma 7
.

If
m
is a positive integer such that

n\geq mk
and
k+1\leq(a+1)(m-1)
,
then
B(K(n,k,a))\leq m
.

Proof.

Let
{\cal W}=\{W_{1},W_{2},\ldots,W_{m}\}
be a set of
m
disjoint
k
-subsets of
[n]
.
Consider
{\cal W}
as the set of white vertices.
If there exists a black vertex
S

adjacent to exactly one vertex in
{\cal W}
, say
|S\cap W_{1}|\leq a
and

|S\cap W_{i}|\geq a+1
for
2\leq i\leq m
.
Then
k=|S|\geq(a+1)(m-1)
, a contradiction.
Hence,
{\cal W}
is a zero blocking set, which gives
B(K(n,k,a))\leq m
.
∎

Lemma 8
.

If
n\geq 2k-a
and
k-2\geq a
but
(n,a)\neq(2k,0)
,
then
B(K(n,k,a))\geq\lceil\frac{k-a}{a+1}\rceil+2
.

Proof.

Suppose
n=2k-a
.
For every two vertices
A
and
B
, we have
|A\cap B|=|A|+|B|-|A\cup B|\geq k+k-n=a
and so
K(2k-a,k,a)=J(2k-a,k,a)
.
By Proposition
5
and Theorem
4
,

B(K(2k-a,k,a))=B(J(2k-a,k,a))=B(J(2k-a,k-a,0))=B(K(2k-a,k-a))=k-a+2\geq\lceil\frac{k-a}{a+1}\rceil+2
.

Now
n>2k-a
.
Suppose to the contrary that
K(n,k,a)
has
a minimum zero blocking set
\{W_{1},W_{2},\ldots,W_{m}\}
with

m\leq\lceil\frac{k-a}{a+1}\rceil+1
, that is,
k\geq(a+1)(m-1)
.
By Proposition
6
(4),
m\geq 3
.
For
i\geq 2
, let
d_{i}=|W_{i}\setminus W_{1}|
and
d_{0}=\min_{i\geq 2}d_{i}>0
,
and choose
Y_{i}\subseteq W_{i}\setminus W_{1}
with
|Y_{i}|=\min\{a+1,d_{i}\}
.
Also, for
d_{0}\leq d\leq k
, let
X_{d}=\bigcap_{d_{i}\leq d}(W_{i}\cap W_{1})
.
Then
X_{k}\subseteq X_{k-1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq X_{d_{0}}

and

\displaystyle|X_{d}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|W_{1}\setminus\bigcup\nolimits_{d_{1}\leq d}(W_{1}\setminus W_{i})|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|W_{1}|-\sum\nolimits_{d_{i}\leq d}|W_{1}\setminus W_{i}|=|W_{1}|-\sum\nolimits_{d_{i}\leq d}|W_{i}\setminus W_{1}|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle k-d(m-1)\geq(a+1)(m-1)-d(m-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a+1-d)(m-1)\geq a+1-d.

Hence, for each
d_{0}\leq d\leq k
, we may
choose
X^{\prime}_{d}\subseteq X_{d}
with
|X^{\prime}_{d}|=\max\{0,a+1-d\}

such that
X_{k}^{\prime}\subseteq X_{k-1}^{\prime}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq X_{d_{0}}^{\prime}
.
Let
Z=(\bigcup_{i=2}^{m}Y_{i})\cup X^{\prime}_{d_{0}}
.
Then

|Z\cap W_{1}|=|X^{\prime}_{d_{0}}|=\max\{0,a+1-d_{0}\}\leq a.

For
i\geq 2
,
|Y_{i}\cup X^{\prime}_{d_{i}}|=\min\{a+1,d_{i}\}+\max\{0,a+1-d_{i}\}=a+1
and so

|Z\cap W_{i}|\geq|Y_{i}\cup X^{\prime}_{d_{i}}|=a+1.

Also,
|Z|=|\bigcup_{i=2}^{m}(Y_{i}\cup X^{\prime}_{d_{i}})|\leq\sum_{i=2}^{m}|Y_{i}\cup X^{\prime}_{d_{i}}|=(a+1)(m-1)\leq k
.

Since
n>2k-a
,
we can extend
Z
to a
k
-subset
Z^{\prime}\subseteq[n]
with
|Z^{\prime}\cap W_{1}|\leq a
.
Also,
|Z^{\prime}\cap W_{i}|\geq|Z\cap W_{i}|\geq a+1
for
i\geq 2
.
Since
Z^{\prime}
is adjacent to exactly one white vertex
W_{1}
,

Z^{\prime}
must be white, say
Z^{\prime}=W_{2}
.
Similarly,
W_{2}
has a white neighbor, which must be
W_{1}
,
not adjacent to other white vertices.

Notice that
W_{2}=Z^{\prime}\supseteq Z=(\bigcup_{i=2}^{m}Y_{i})\cup X^{\prime}_{d_{0}}
.
If we extend
\bigcup_{i=3}^{m}(Y_{i}\cup X^{\prime}_{d_{i}})
to a
k
-subset
Z^{\prime\prime}

with
|Z^{\prime\prime}\cap W_{1}|\leq a
, then
|Z^{\prime\prime}\cap W_{2}|\geq|Y_{3}\cup X^{\prime}_{d_{3}}|=a+1
and

|Z^{\prime\prime}\cap W_{i}|\geq|Y_{i}\cup X^{\prime}_{d_{i}}|=a+1
for
i\geq 3
.
Hence
Z^{\prime\prime}
is adjacent to exactly one white vertex
W_{1}
and so
Z^{\prime\prime}=W_{2}
.
But
|\bigcup_{i=3}^{m}(Y_{i}\cup X^{\prime}_{d_{i}})|\leq\sum_{i=3}^{m}|Y_{i}\cup X^{\prime}_{d_{i}}|\leq(a+1)(m-2)\leq k-(a+1)<k
.
Since
n>2k-a
, there is more than one way to extend
\bigcup_{i=3}^{m}(Y_{i}\cup X^{\prime}_{d_{i}})
to
Z^{\prime\prime}
, a contradiction.

Hence,
B(K(n,k,a))\geq\lceil\frac{k-a}{a+1}\rceil+2
as desired.
∎

Theorem 9
.

If
n\geq k(\lceil\frac{k-a}{a+1}\rceil+2)
and
k-2\geq a
,
then
B(K(n,k,a))=\lceil\frac{k-a}{a+1}\rceil+2
.

Proof.

The theorem follows from Lemma
7
by taking
m=\lceil\frac{k-a}{a+1}\rceil+2
and Lemma
8
by
noting that
n\geq k(\lceil\frac{k-a}{a+1}\rceil+2)\geq 3k
.
∎

Now the exact value of
B(K(n,k,a))
is determined for sufficient large
n
, but
not for small
n
.
Since
B(K(n,k,0))=k+2
for
n\geq 2k+1
and
k\geq 2
,
the bound of
n
in Theorem
9
is not sharp.
The following is a weaker upper bound of
B(K(n,k,a))
,
but true for arbitrary
n
.

Theorem 10
.

B(K(n,k,a))\leq k-a+2
.

Proof.

The theorem is true for
a=0
or
n=k
.
Now assume that
a\geq 1
and
n\geq k+1
.
Consider the set
{\cal W}=\{W_{1},W_{2},\ldots,W_{k-a+2}\}
,
where
W_{i}=[k+1]\setminus\{i\}
for
1\leq i\leq k-a+2
.
Let
{\cal W}
be the set of all white vertices.
To show that
{\cal W}
is a zero blocking set.
Suppose
S
is a black vertex adjacent to some white vertex
W_{j}
.

If
|S\cap W_{j}|\leq a-1
, then for
j^{\prime}\neq j
,

|S\cap W_{j^{\prime}}|\leq|(S\cap W_{j})\cup\{j\}|\leq(a-1)+1=a
.

If
|S\cap W_{j}|=a
, then there exists
j^{\prime}\in S\cap W_{j}
such that
j^{\prime}\leq k-a+2
, since
|[k-a+3,k+1]|=a-1
.
In this case,
j^{\prime}\neq j
and
S
is adjacent to
W_{j^{\prime}}
, since

|S\cap W_{j^{\prime}}|\leq|((S\cap W_{j})\cup\{j\})\setminus\{j^{\prime}\}|\leq a+1-1=a
.

Hence,
{\cal W}
is a zero blocking set as desired.
The theorem then follows.
∎

Consequently,
B(K(n,k,a))
is monotonically non-increasing in
n
for
n\geq 3k-2a+1
.

Theorem 11
.

If
n\geq 3k-2a+1
, then
B(K(n+1,k,a))\leq B(K(n,k,a))
.

Proof.

If
\{W_{1},W_{2},\ldots,W_{m}\}
is a minimum zero blocking set of
K(n,k,a)

but not a zero blocking set of
K(n+1,k,a)
,
then
K(n+1,k,a)
has a black vertex
S
containing
n+1
that is adjacent to exactly one white vertex, say
W_{1}
.
By Theorem
10
,
m\leq k-a+2
.
For every
S^{\prime}=S\Delta\{x,n+1\}
with
x\in[n]\setminus S
and
|S^{\prime}\cap W_{1}|\leq a
,
we have
|S^{\prime}\cap W_{i}|\geq|S\cap W_{i}|>a
for
i\geq 2
.
Since
n\geq k+(k-1-a)+(k-a+2)\geq|W_{1}|+(|S\setminus\{n+1\}|-a)+m
,
there exists at least
m
choices of
S^{\prime}
,
so that one of them is a black vertex in
K(n,k,a)
that forces
W_{1}
to be black, a contradiction.
Hence,
\{W_{1},W_{2},\ldots,W_{m}\}
is also a zero blocking set of
K(n+1,k,a)
and
so
B(K(n+1,k,a))\leq B(K(n,k,a))
.
∎

3
Results for generalized Johnson graphs
J(n,k,a)

First, all
J(n,k,a)
with
B(J(n,k,a))
no more than two are characterized.

Proposition 12
.

Suppose integers
n\geq k>a\geq 0
.

(1)

If
n\leq 2k-a-1
, then
B(J(n,k,a))=1
.

(2)

If
n\geq 2
and
k=1
, or
n=k+1=a+2
, then
B(J(n,k,a))=2
.

(3)

If
n=2k
and
a=0
, then
B(J(n,k,a))=2
.

(4)

If
n=2k=4a>0
, then
B(J(n,k,a))=2
.

(5)

If
n\geq 2k-a
and
k\geq 2
but
(n,a)\neq(2k,0)
and it is not the case that
n=2k=4a>0

or
n=k+1=a+2
, then
B(J(n,k,a))\geq 3
.

Proof.

(1) This follows from
J(n,k,a)={n\choose k}K_{1}
.

(2) This follows from
J(n,1,0)=J(n,n-1,n-2)=K_{n}
.
Notice that the case of
n=k=1
is included in (1).

(3) This follows from
J(2k,k,0)=\frac{1}{2}{2k\choose k}K_{2}
.

(4) Choose two vertices
A
and
B
with
A\cap B=\emptyset
.
If vertex
C
is adjacent to
A
, then
|C\cap A|=a
and so

|C\cap B|=|C|-|C\cap A|=k-a=a
, implying that
C
is adjacent to
B
.
Similarly, if
C
is adjacent to
B
, then
C
is adjacent to
A
.
Hence
A
and
B
are twins.
This gives
B(J(n,k,a))=2
.

(5) Since
n\geq 2k-a
, every vertex
A
is adjacent to some vertex
B
with
|A\cap B|=a
.
Hence
J(n,k,a)
has no isolated vertices.

Next to see that
J(n,k,a)
has no twins.
Suppose to the contrary that there are twins
A
and
B
.
Choose
x\in A\setminus B
and
y\in B\setminus A
.

(i)

Suppose
A\cap B=\emptyset
.
Choose
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
with
|A^{\prime}|=a

and
B^{\prime}\subseteq B
with
y\in B^{\prime}
and
|B^{\prime}|=k-a
.
Then
C=A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime}
is adjacent to
A
, and so
C=B
or
C
is adjacent to
B
.
If
C=B
, then
a=0
and so
n>2k
, since
(n,a)\neq(2k,0)
.
If
C
is adjacent to
B
, then
k-a=a>0
and so
n>2k
, since it is not the case
n=2k=4a>0
.
Hence there exists
z\in[n]\setminus(A\cup B)
.
Then
C^{\prime}=C\Delta\{y,z\}\notin\{A,B\}
is adjacent to
A
but not to
B
, since

|C^{\prime}\cap A|=|A^{\prime}|=a
,
|C^{\prime}\cap B|=|B\setminus\{y\}|=k-1\geq 1>0=a
when
C=B
,
and
|C^{\prime}\cap B|=|B^{\prime}\setminus\{y\}|=k-a-1=a-1
when
C
is adjacent to
B
.

(ii)

Suppose
0<|A\cap B|\leq a
.
Since
A\cap B\neq\emptyset
, there exists
w\in A\cap B
.
Choose
A^{\prime}
with
((A\cap B)\cup\{x\})\setminus\{w\}\subseteq A^{\prime}\subseteq A\setminus\{w\}

and
|A^{\prime}|=a
, and

B^{\prime}\subseteq B\setminus A
with
|B^{\prime}|=k-a
by the fact that
|B\setminus A|\geq k-a
.
Vertex
C=A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime}\notin\{A,B\}
is adjacent to
A
, since
|C\cap A|=|A^{\prime}|=a
.
Hence
C
is adjacent to
B
, that is
|C\cap B|=a
.
Then
C^{\prime}=C\Delta\{x,w\}\neq A
is adjacent to
A
but not to
B
, since
|C^{\prime}\cap A|=|A^{\prime}\Delta\{x,w\}|=|A^{\prime}|=a
and

|C^{\prime}\cap B|=|(C\cap B)\cup\{w\}|=a+1
.
Hence
C^{\prime}=B
, that is
C=B\Delta\{x,w\}
,
which implies
a=|A\cap B|=|C\cap B|=k-1

and
A\setminus\{x\}=A\cap B=B\setminus\{y\}

with
|A\cup B|=k+1
.
Since it is not the case
n=k+1=a+2
,
there exists
z\in[n]\setminus(A\cup B)
.
Hence
C^{\prime\prime}=C\Delta\{y,z\}\neq\{A,B\}
is adjacent to
A
but not to
B
, since
|C^{\prime\prime}\cap A|=|A^{\prime}|=a
and
|C^{\prime\prime}\cap B|=|B\setminus\{w,y\}|=k-2=a-1
.

(iii)

Suppose
a<|A\cap B|\leq k-1
.
Choose
C^{\prime}\subsetneqq A\cap B
with
|C^{\prime}|=a
,
and
C^{\prime\prime}\subseteq[n]\setminus A
with
B\setminus A\subsetneqq C^{\prime\prime}
and
|C^{\prime\prime}|=k-a
by the fact that
since
|B\setminus A|<k-a
.
Then
C=C^{\prime}\cup C^{\prime\prime}\notin\{A,B\}
is adjacent to
A
but not to
B
, since
|C\cap A|=|C^{\prime}|=a
and
|C\cap B|=|C^{\prime}|+|B\setminus A|\geq a+|\{y\}|=a+1
.

In summary,
J(n,k,a)
has no isolated vertices and has no twins.
These give that
B(J(n,k,a))\geq 3
by Propositions
2
and
3
.
∎

Notice that
J(n,k,0)=K(n,k)
and
J(n,k,k-1)=J(n,k)
.
So we may consider
J(n,k,a)
only for
k-2\geq a\geq 1
.

Theorem 13
.

If
a\geq 1
, then
B(J(n,k,a))\leq\max\{a,k-a\}+2
.

Proof.

The theorem is true for
n=k
, since
B(J(k,k,a))=1
by Proposition
12
(1).
Now assume that
n\geq k+1
.

Let
m=\max\{a,k-a\}+2
.
Consider the set
{\cal W}=\{W_{1},W_{2},\ldots,W_{m}\}
of
k
-subsets of
[n]
,
where
W_{i}=[k+1]\setminus\{i\}
for
1\leq i\leq m
.
Let
{\cal W}
be the set of all white vertices.
We verify that
{\cal W}
is a zero blocking set by showing that
any black vertex
S
adjacent to some
W_{i}
is adjacent to another
W_{i^{\prime}}
with
i^{\prime}\neq i
as follows.
Since
|S\cap W_{i}|=a
and
|[m+1,k+1]|<a<|[m]\setminus\{i\}|
,
there exists
i_{1},i_{2}\in[m]\setminus\{i\}
such that

i_{1}\in S\cap W_{i}
and
i_{2}\in W_{i}\setminus S
.
If
i\in S
, then
|S\cap W_{i_{1}}|=|S\cap(W_{i}\Delta\{i,i_{1}\})|=a+1-1=a
.
If
i\notin S
, then
|S\cap W_{i_{2}}|=|S\cap(W_{i}\Delta\{i,i_{2}\})|=|S\cap W_{i}|=a
.
Hence,
\cal W
is a zero blocking set of
J(n,k,a)
and so

B(J(n,k,a))\leq m
as desired.
∎

By Proposition
5
,

B(J(n,k,a))=B(J(n,n-k,n-2k+a))\leq\max\{k-a,n-2k+a\}+2
for
n\geq 2k-a
.
This upper bound is smaller than
\max\{a,k-a\}+2
for
2k-a\leq n<2k
and
a>k/2
.
Although the exact value of
B(J(n,k,a))
is not known in general, the following shall prove that the upper bound
\max\{a,k-a\}+2
is in fact the value of
B(J(n,k,a))
when
n
is relatively larger than
k
and
a
.

First, two useful lemmas.

Lemma 14
.

In
J(n,k,a)
,
if vertices
A
and
B
have a common neighbor
C
,
then
|A\setminus B|\leq 2(k-a)
.

Proof.

Since
A\setminus B\subseteq(A\setminus C)\cup(C\setminus B)
,
it follows that
|A\setminus B|\leq|A\setminus C|+|C\setminus B|=2(k-a)
.
∎

Lemma 15
.

Suppose
S_{1},S_{2},\ldots,S_{p}
are
p\geq 2
pairwise disjoint sets and
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{p}
are positive integers.
If
|S_{1}|\geq 3
and
|S_{2}|\geq 2
,
and
2\leq t\leq(\sum_{i=1}^{p}|S_{i}|)-p-1
,
then there exists
S\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{p}S_{i}

such that
|S|=t
and
|S\cap S_{i}|\neq a_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq p
.

Proof.

Choose
x_{i}\in S_{i}
for
S_{i}\neq\emptyset
and
y_{1}\in S_{1}\setminus\{x_{1}\}
.
Since
2\leq t\leq(\sum_{i=1}^{p}|S_{i}|)-p-1
,
there exists
S_{0}\subseteq(\bigcup_{i=1}^{p}(S_{i}\setminus\{x_{i}\}))\setminus\{y_{1}\}

such that
|S_{0}|=t
and
S_{0}\cap S_{i}
contains some
z_{i}
for
i=1,2
.
Let
I=\{i:1\leq i\leq p,|S_{0}\cap S_{i}|=a_{i}\}
.
Since
a_{i}>0
, there exists
w_{i}\in S_{0}\cap S_{i}
for
i\in I
.
Suppose
I=\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{r}\}
.

If
r=2s
is even, then
S=(S_{0}\cup\{x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}},\ldots,x_{i_{s}}\})\setminus\{w_{i_{s+1}},w_{i_{s+2}},\ldots,w_{i_{2s}}\}
is as desired.
Now suppose
r=2s+1
is odd with
s\geq 0
.

If
1\notin I
or
2\notin I
, say
i_{0}\in\{1,2\}
but
i_{0}\notin I
.
If
|S_{0}\cap S_{i_{0}}|+1\neq a_{i_{0}}
, then
S=(S_{0}\cup\{x_{i_{0}},x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}},\ldots,x_{i_{s}}\})\setminus\{w_{i_{s+1}},w_{i_{s+2}},\ldots,w_{i_{2s+1}}\}
is as desired.
Otherwise,
|S_{0}\cap S_{i_{0}}|-1\neq a_{i_{0}}
and so
S=(S_{0}\cup\{x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}},\ldots,x_{i_{s+1}}\})

\setminus\{w_{i_{s+2}},w_{i_{s+3}},\ldots,w_{i_{2s+1}},z_{i_{0}}\}
is as desired.

Now suppose
1\in I
and
2\in I
, say
1=i_{1}
, then
r=2s+1\geq 3
and so

S=(S_{0}\cup\{y_{1},x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}},\ldots,x_{i_{s}}\})\setminus\{w_{i_{s+1}},w_{i_{s+2}},\ldots,w_{i_{2s+1}}\}
is as desired.
∎

Now consider the case of
a\geq k/2
.

Theorem 16
.

If
n\geq(a+\frac{5}{2})(k-a+2)-6
and
k-2\geq a\geq k/2
,
then
B(J(n,k,a))=a+2
.

Proof.

By Lemma
13
, it is only necessary to prove that
B(J(n,k,a))\geq a+2
.
For this purpose, let
d=k-a
and so
d\geq 2
.

Suppose to the contrary that
J(n,k,a)
has a minimum zero blocking set
\{W_{1},W_{2},\ldots,W_{m}\}
with
m\leq a+1
.
Choose
x_{i}\in W_{1}\setminus W_{i}
for
2\leq i\leq m

and extend
\{x_{2},x_{3},\ldots,x_{m}\}
to a set
X

with
X\subseteq W_{1}
and
|X|=a
.
Let
I=\{i\geq 2:|W_{i}\setminus W_{1}|\leq 2d\}

and
U=[n]\setminus\bigcup_{i\in\{1\}\cup I}W_{i}
.

If
|U|\geq d
, then choose
Y\subseteq U
with
|Y|=d
.
Then,
|(X\cup Y)\cap W_{1}|=|X|=a
and
x_{i}\in(X\cup Y)\setminus W_{i}
for
2\leq i\leq m
.
Hence,
X\cup Y
is a black vertex adjacent to
W_{1}
.
So,
X\cup Y
must be adjacent to another white vertex
W_{j}
.
By Lemma
14
,
|W_{j}\setminus W_{1}|\leq 2d
and so
j\in I
.
By the definition of
U
and
Y
,
Y\cap W_{j}=\emptyset
,
Hence
a=|(X\cup Y)\cap W_{j}|=|X\cap W_{j}|\leq|X\setminus\{x_{j}\}|<a
, a contradiction.
In conclusion,
|U|\leq d-1
.

For
i\in I
, let
W_{i}^{\prime}=W_{i}\setminus W_{1}
and
D_{i}=W_{i}^{\prime}\setminus\bigcup_{j\neq i,j\in I}W_{j}^{\prime}
.
Notice that
U=[n]\setminus(W_{1}\cup\bigcup_{i\in I}W_{i}^{\prime})
.
It is clear that

\sum_{i\in I}|W_{i}^{\prime}|+\sum_{i\in I}|D_{i}|\geq 2|\bigcup_{i\in I}W_{i}^{\prime}|.

Now,
|\bigcup_{i\in I}W_{i}^{\prime}|=n-|W_{1}|-|U|\geq n-k-d+1
,
|I|\leq m-1\leq a
and
|W_{i}^{\prime}|\leq 2d
for
i\in I
.
In summary,

\sum_{i\in I}|D_{i}|\geq 2|\bigcup_{i\in I}W_{i}^{\prime}|-\sum_{i\in I}|W_{i}^{\prime}|\geq 2(n-k-d+1)-2da\geq a+k
by the assumption that
n\geq(a+\frac{5}{2})(k-a+2)-6
.

There exist
i,j\in I
with
i\neq j
such that
|D_{i}|\geq 3
and
|D_{j}|\geq 2
,
since
\sum_{i\in I}|D_{i}|\geq a+k\geq|I|+\max\{|I|+1,|D_{i}|:i\in I\}
,
which also implies
|I|\geq 2
.
Also,
d\leq a+k-a-1\leq(\sum_{i\in I}|D_{i}|)-|I|-1
.
Now apply Lemma
15

by taking
\{S_{1},S_{2},\ldots,S_{p}\}=\{D_{i}:i\in I\}
,

a_{i}=a-|W_{i}\cap X|
for
i\in I
and
t=d
,
to get
S\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in I}D_{i}
with
|S|=d

and
|S\cap D_{i}|\neq a_{i}
for
i\in I
.
Then
X\cup S
is a black vertex adjacent to
W_{1}
.
For
i\in I
,
|(X\cup S)\cap W_{i}|=|X\cap W_{i}|+|S\cap D_{i}|\neq a
.
For other
i\in[2,m]\setminus I
,
X\cup S
is not adjacent to
W_{i}
by Lemma
14
.
Hence the black vertex
X\cup S
is adjacent to exactly one white vertex
W_{1}
,
a contradiction.
∎

Next consider the case of
a<k/2
.

Theorem 17
.

If
n\geq k(k-a+2)-a
and
k/2>a\geq 1
, then
B(J(n,k,a))=k-a+2
.

Proof.

By Lemma
13
, it is only necessary to prove that
B(J(n,k,a))\geq k-a+2
.
Suppose to the contrary that
J(n,k,a)
has a minimum zero blocking set
\{W_{1},W_{2},\ldots,W_{m}\}
with
m\leq k-a+1
.
Choose
x_{i}\in W_{1}\setminus W_{i}
for
2\leq i\leq m
.
Let
W_{1}^{\prime}=\{x_{2},x_{3},\ldots,x_{m}\}
and
W_{1}^{\prime\prime}=W_{1}\setminus W_{1}^{\prime}
.
Assume that
W_{1}^{\prime}
is chosen so that
r=|W_{1}^{\prime}|
is minimum.
Notice that
r\leq m-1\leq k-a
and so
|W_{1}^{\prime\prime}|\geq a
.

Choose
X_{0}\subseteq W_{1}^{\prime\prime}
with
|X_{0}|=a
.
Let
X=X_{0}
initially and update it at most
m-1
times as follows.
If there exists
2\leq i\leq m
such that
|W_{i}\cap X|=a
, then

|W_{i}\cap(W_{1}\cup X)|\leq|W_{1}\setminus(X\cup\{x_{i}\})|+|W_{i}\cap X|=(k-a-1)+a<|W_{i}|
and so there is some
y_{i}\in W_{i}\setminus(W_{1}\cup X)
.
For such
i
, add
y_{i}
into
X
.
Now
|W_{i}\cap X|>a
.
Repeating this process at most
m-1
times results a set
X
with
a\leq|X|\leq a+m-1\leq k
.
Also
|W_{1}\cap X|=a\neq|W_{i}\cap X|
for
2\leq i\leq m
.
Since
n\geq k(k-a+2)-a\geq km+k-a
, we can add
k-|X|
elements in
[n]\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}W_{i}
into
X
.
Now
|X|=k
with
X\cap W_{1}=X_{0}\subseteq W_{1}^{\prime\prime}
and
X
is adjacent to exactly one white vertex
W_{1}
,
so
X
must be white, say
X=W_{2}
.
Similarly,
W_{2}
has a white neighbor, which must be
W_{1}
,
not adjacent to other white vertices.

Let
I=\{i:2\leq i\leq m,W_{i}\cap W_{1}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\}
.
If
I=\emptyset
, then
r=1<k-a
, else
r\leq|I|
since
\{x_{i}:i\in I\}
has the same property as
W_{1}^{\prime}
and
W_{1}^{\prime}
is chosen so that
r=|W_{1}^{\prime}|
is minimum.
Since
2\notin I
, we have
r\leq|I|\leq m-2<k-a
.
By the arguments in the previous paragraph, each subset
X_{0}\subseteq W_{1}^{\prime\prime}
with
|X_{0}|=a
corresponds to a white vertex
W_{j}
with
2\leq j\leq m
such that
W_{j}\cap W_{1}=X_{0}\subseteq W_{1}^{\prime\prime}
, adjacent to exactly one white vertex
W_{1}
, which must be
W_{2}
.
But there is more than one choice for
X_{0}
, since
r<k-a
, a contradiction.

The theorem then follows.
∎

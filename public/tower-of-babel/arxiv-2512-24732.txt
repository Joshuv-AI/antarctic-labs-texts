Isomorphism between Hopf algebras for multiple zeta values
Li Guo, Hongyu Xiang, Bin Zhang

Abstract
. The classical quasi-shuffle algebra for multiple zeta values have a well-known Hopf algebra structure. Recently, the shuffle algebra for multiple zeta values are also equipped with a Hopf algebra structure. This paper shows that these two Hopf algebras are isomorphic.

1.
Introduction

This paper studies isomorphisms between the shuffle Hopf algebra and quasi-shuffle Hopf algebra encoding multiple zeta values.

1.1.
Shuffle products for MZVs

Multiple zeta values (MZVs) are the values of the multiple zeta series

(1)

\zeta(s_{1},\cdots,s_{k})=\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{k}>0}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{k}^{s_{k}}}

at convergent points with positive integer arguments, that is,
s_{1}>1
,
s_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1},i=2,\cdots,k
. MZVs and their generalizations have been studied extensively from different points of view since 1990s with connections to number theory, algebraic geometry, mathematical physics, quantum groups and knot theory
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
7
,
10
,
13
,

,
17
,

,
24
,

,
31
,

,
33
]
.

A fascinating aspect of MZVs is that the analytically defined values have deep algebraic structures, which have led to rich algebraic relations among MZVs and critical motivic implications, among other applications.
These algebraic structures stem from the two algebraic interpretations of the multiplications of two MZVs, namely the stuffle (or quasi-shuffle) product and shuffle product.

To begin with, there are two intuitive families of relations:

•

stuffle relation, based on the series expression (
1
) of MZVs;

•

shuffle relation, based on the iterated integral expression of MZVs:

(2)

\zeta(s_{1},\cdots s_{k})=\underbrace{\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dt}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{dt}{t}\cdots\int_{0}^{t}\frac{dt}{t}}_{s_{1}-1}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{dt}{1-t}\cdots\underbrace{\int_{0}^{t}\frac{dt}{t}\cdots\int_{0}^{t}\frac{dt}{t}}_{s_{k}-1}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{dt}{1-t}

These relations can be lifted as stuffle multiplication
*
and shuffle multiplication
X
on the vector space

(3)

{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}}=\mathbb{Q}{\bf 1}\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0},\vec{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{k}}\mathbb{Q}[\vec{s}],

which is called the
quasi-shuffle algebra
and the
shuffle algebra
for MZVs.

The subspace

(4)

{\mathcal{H}}^{0}=\mathbb{Q}{\bf 1}\oplus\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0},\vec{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{k},s_{1}>1}\mathbb{Q}[\vec{s}].

is a subalgebra with respect to both multiplications, and MZVs can be viewed as an algebra homomorphism

(5)

\zeta_{\ast},\zeta_{\mbox{\cyrs X}}:{\mathcal{H}}^{0}\to\mathbb{R}

for both multiplications. Therefore

\{\zeta(\vec{s}*\vec{t})-\zeta(\vec{s}{\mbox{\cyrs X}}\vec{t})=0\ |\ \vec{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{k},s_{1}>1,\vec{t}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{\ell},t_{1}>1,k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\}

is a family of
\mathbb{Q}
-linear relations among MZVs, which is called the
double shuffle relation
.
Ihara-Kaneko-Zagier
[
23
]
extended the algebra homomorphisms (
5
) to algebra homomorphisms

\zeta_{*},\zeta_{\mbox{\cyrs X}}:{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}}\to\mathbb{R}[T],

and extended the double shuffle relation to
regularized double shuffle relation
:

\{[1]*[\vec{s}]-[1]{\mbox{\cyrs X}}[\vec{s}],[\vec{s}]*[\vec{t}]-[\vec{s}]{\mbox{\cyrs X}}[\vec{t}]\ |\ \vec{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{k},s_{1}>1,\vec{t}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{\ell},t_{1}>1,k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\}.

Conjecturally, they generate all algebraic relations among MZVs
[
23
]
.

Thus the space
{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}}
carries two commutative associative products
\ast
and
X
. By using Lyndon words technique, it can be proved that both
({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},\ast)
and
({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},{\mbox{\cyrs X}})
are polynomial algebras.

1.2.
Hopf algebra structures on MZV shuffle and quasi-shuffle algebras

Further understanding of MZVs motivates on finding deeper algebraic structures of the MZV quasi-shuffle algebra
({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},*)
and MZV shuffle algebra
({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},{\mbox{\cyrs X}})

Since

is the quasi-shuffle algebra
[

on the alphabet

with the deconcatenation coproduct

, there is a natural Hopf algebra

.
Its action on the MZV shuffle algebra has been used to obtain large classes of algebraic relations of MZVs
[

28
]

Hopf algebras have played a critical role in the study of motivic MZVs
[
6
,
12
,
29
]
.
Such Hopf algebra structures also allow the Connes-Kreimer approach to renormalization in quantum field theory
[
8
]
to be applied to studying the multiple zeta series at divergent arguments
[
9

18
,
27
]
.

Recently, a differential Hopf algebra structure was obtained on the shuffle Hopf algebra
({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},shap)
from MZVs
[
16
]
.
A natural question is, are the Hopf algebras
({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},\ast,\Delta_{\rm dec})
and
({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},\ast,\Delta_{\rm dec})
isomorphic? If so, what can we say about the set of isomorphisms between the Hopf algebras?
These questions will be addressed in this paper.

The paper is structured as following. In Section
2
, we recall the construction of the Hopf algebra structure on the shuffle algebra
({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},{\mbox{\cyrs X}})
, and applied the universal property of the Hopf algebra of quasi-symmetric functions
[
2
]
to obtain Hopf algebra homomorphisms from
({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},\ast,\Delta_{\rm dec})
to
({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},\ast,\Delta_{\rm dec})
that are induced by characters on
({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},\ast,\Delta_{\rm dec})
(Corollary
2.6
).

In Section
3
a well-order is defined on the homogeneous components on
{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}}
which is extended to a pre-order on
{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}}
. This order allows us to give a bijection between the set of Hopf algebra isomorphisms from
({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},\ast,\Delta_{\rm dec})
to
({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},\ast,\Delta_{\rm dec})
and the set of characters on
({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},\ast,\Delta_{\rm dec})
that never vanish (Theorem
3.12

Note that this Hopf algebra isomorphism is different from the one given in
[
20
]

2.
The shuffle Hopf algebra and quasi-shuffle Hopf algebra for MZVs

This section recalls the shuffle Hopf algebra and quasi-shuffle Hopf algebra for MZVs, and then gives a Hopf algebra homomorphism between them.

2.1.
The shuffle Hopf algebra for MZVs

The expression of MZVs in (
2

x_{0}^{s_{1}-1}x_{1}\cdots x_{0}^{s_{k}-1}x_{1},

in the subspace
x_{0}\mathbb{Q}\langle x_{0},x_{1}\rangle x_{1}

in variables
x_{0},x_{1}

\mathbb{Q}\langle x_{0},x_{1}\rangle x_{1}
to denote the linear spanned of monomials ended with
x_{1}
.

The monomials of
\mathbb{Q}\langle x_{0},x_{1}\rangle
can be regarded as the set
W
of words (including the empty word
{\bf 1}
) in letters in
\{x_{0},x_{1}\}
. Then equip
\mathbb{Q}\langle x_{0},x_{1}\rangle=\mathbb{Q}W
with the shuffle multiplication

that is inductively defined by

aw_{1}{\mbox{\cyrs X}}bw_{2}=a(w_{1}{\mbox{\cyrs X}}bw_{2})+b(aw_{1}{\mbox{\cyrs X}}w_{2})

for
w,w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}W
,
a,b\in\{x_{0},x_{1}\}
.

The subspaces
\mathbb{Q}\langle x_{0},x_{1}\rangle x_{1}
and
x_{0}\mathbb{Q}\langle x_{0},x_{1}\rangle x_{1}
are closed under the shuffle multiplication. The subalgebra
\mathbb{Q}\langle x_{0},x_{1}\rangle x_{1}
is called the
shuffle algebra
for MZVs.

Through the linear bijection

\rho:{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}}\to\mathbb{Q}\langle x_{0},x_{1}\rangle x_{1},\ [s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}]\to x_{0}^{s_{1}-1}x_{1}\cdots x_{0}^{s_{k}-1}x_{1},

the shuffle product

is pull back to a multiplication on
{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}}
, which is still denoted by
X

is also called the shuffle algebra for MZVs. Then

Inside the space
{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}}=\mathbb{Q}{\bf 1}\oplus\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0},\vec{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{k}}\mathbb{Q}[\vec{s}]

Then the shuffle product on

.

[

2.2.
Quasi-symmetric functions

The notion of quasisymmetric functions was formally introduced by Gessel
[
11
]
in 1984 with its motivation traced back to the work of Stanley
[
30
]
on
P
-partitions and of MacMahon
[
26
]
on plane partitions.
The importance of quasisymmetric functions became evident, through their Hopf algebra structure as the terminal object in the category of combinatorial Hopf algebras
[
2
]
.

A natural linear basis of the space
\mathrm{QSym}
of quasisymmetric functions is the monomial quasisymmetric functions

(13)

M_{\alpha}:=\sum_{0<i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}}x_{i_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}x_{i_{2}}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{k}}^{\alpha_{k}}

parameterized by compositions
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{k})
of
n
(denoted
\alpha\vDash n
). A composition is simply a vector of positive integers.

The space
\mathrm{QSym}
has a natural graded Hopf algebra structure.
Here the grading is given by

\mathrm{QSym}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{QSym}_{n},

where
\mathrm{QSym}_{n}
is the linear space spanned by all quasisymmetric functions that are homogeneous of degree
n
over
\mathbb{Q}
.

The product in
\mathrm{QSym}
is the usual multiplication of formal power series. For the basis elements of monomial quasisymmetric functions, there is the relation

M_{\alpha}\cdot M_{\beta}=M_{\alpha\ast\beta}

where
\alpha\ast\beta
is the quasi-shuffle product of the compositions
\alpha
and
\beta
with the unit to be the constant function
1=M_{()}
.

The coproduct
\Delta:\mathrm{QSym}\to\mathrm{QSym}\otimes\mathrm{QSym}
is given by deconcatenation of compositions:

\Delta(M_{(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})})=\sum_{i=0}^{k}M_{(a_{1},\dots,a_{i})}\otimes M_{(a_{i+1},\dots,a_{k})}.

with the counit
\epsilon:\mathrm{QSym}\to\mathbb{Q}
being the projection onto the constant term:
\epsilon(M_{\alpha})=1
if
\alpha=\emptyset
, and
0
otherwise.
The resulting bialgebra is graded and connected, thereby possessing a unique antipode
S
, making it a Hopf algebra. The antipode

S:\mathrm{QSym}\to\mathrm{QSym}

can be explicitly given by:

S(M_{\alpha})=(-1)^{k(\alpha)}\sum_{\beta\leq\widetilde{\alpha}}M_{\beta},

where
\widetilde{\alpha}
is the reverse of the composition
\alpha
and
\beta\leq\widetilde{\alpha}
means
\beta
is a refinement of
\widetilde{\alpha}
,
k(\alpha)
is the depth of
\alpha
.

Recall
[
2
]
that a
combinatorial Hopf algebra
over
\mathbb{Q}
is a pair
(H,\zeta)
, where
H
is a graded connected Hopf algebra over
\mathbb{Q}
,
\zeta:H\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}
is an algebra homomorphism, called a character.

For the Hopf algebra
\mathrm{QSym}
, the map

\zeta_{Q}:\mathbb{Q}[x_{1},x_{2},\cdots]\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q},\quad\zeta_{Q}(x_{1})=1,\zeta_{Q}(x_{i})=0,i\neq 1.

defines a character
\zeta_{Q}
.

Proposition 2.4
.

[
2
, Theorem 4.1]

The combinatorial Hopf algebra
(\mathrm{QSym},\zeta_{Q})
is the terminal object in the category of combinatorial Hopf algebras.

In fact,
\mathrm{QSym}
is the terminal object both in the category of combinatorial coalgebras and in the category of combinatorial Hopf algebras.
Explicitly, for a combinatorial coalgebra (resp. Hopf algebra)
(H=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{Q}H_{k},\zeta)
,
there exists a unique morphism of combinatorial coalgebras (resp. Hopf algebras)

\Psi:(H,\zeta)\longrightarrow(\mathrm{QSym},\zeta_{Q}).

Explicitly, for
h\in H_{n}
, we have

\Psi(h)=\sum_{\alpha\models n}\zeta_{\alpha}(h)M_{\alpha}

where, for a composition
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m})
of
n
,
\zeta_{\alpha}
is the composite

(14)

\zeta_{\alpha}:H\xrightarrow{\Delta^{(m-1)}}H^{\otimes m}\xrightarrow{\pi_{\alpha}}H_{\alpha_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes H_{\alpha_{m}}\xrightarrow{\zeta^{\otimes k}}\mathbb{Q},

where

\Delta^{(0)}:=\text{id}_{H},\quad\Delta^{(1)}:=\Delta,

\Delta^{(m)}:=(\Delta\otimes\text{id}^{\otimes(m-1)})\circ\Delta^{(m-1)}=(\Delta^{(m-1)}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Delta,

and
\pi_{\alpha}
is the tensor product of the canonical projections onto the homogeneous components
H_{a_{i}}
.

2.3.
The quasi-shuffle algebra for MZVs

The quasi-shuffle algebra
{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}}
is the quasi-shuffle algebra
[
20
]
on the alphabet
\{y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,\}
under the isomorphism

[s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}]\mapsto y_{s_{1}}\cdots y_{s_{k}}.

The quasi-shuffle product

\ast:{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}}\times{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}}\to{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}}

is defined inductively: for basis elements
{\bf 1}
,
[\vec{s}]
,
[s_{1},\vec{s}\ ^{\prime}]
,
[t_{1},\vec{t}\ ^{\prime}]\in{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}}
,

{\bf 1}\ast[\vec{s}]=[\vec{s}]\ast{\bf 1}=[\vec{s}],

[s_{1},\vec{s}\ ^{\prime}]\ast[t_{1},\vec{t}\ ^{\prime}]=[s_{1},\vec{s}\ ^{\prime}\ast[t_{1},\vec{t}\ ^{\prime}]]+[t_{1},[s_{1},\vec{s}\ ^{\prime}]\ast\vec{t}\ ^{\prime}]+[s_{1}+t_{1},\vec{s}\ ^{\prime}\ast\vec{t}\ ^{\prime}].

Then
({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},\ast,{\bf 1})
is a commutative algebra.
The deconcatenation coproduct, which is a homomorphism for quasi-shuffle product, is given by

\Delta({\bf 1})={\bf 1}\otimes{\bf 1},

\Delta([\vec{s}])=\sum_{[\vec{u},\vec{v}]=[\vec{s}]}[\vec{u}]\otimes[\vec{v}]

for a basis element
[\vec{s}]\in{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}}
, with
\vec{u},\vec{v}\in H_{Z_{\geq 1}}
.
Together with the counit

\displaystyle\epsilon:{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}}\to\mathbb{Q},\quad\epsilon([\vec{s}])=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}&1,&[\vec{s}]={\bf 1},\\
&0,&otherwise.\end{array}\right.

we have the following well-known result.

Proposition 2.5
.

({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},\ast,{\bf 1},\Delta,\epsilon)
is a commutative connected Hopf algebra with grading in (
12
).
In addition, the map

\Phi:\mathrm{QSym}\to{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},M_{\alpha}\mapsto[\vec{\alpha}]

gives
an Hopf algebra isomorphism between
\mathrm{QSym}
and
({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},\ast,{\bf 1},\Delta,\epsilon)
.

Because of this isomorphism, the character
\zeta_{Q}
on
\mathrm{QSym}
gives a character

\zeta_{Q}^{\prime}:{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}}\to\mathbb{Q}

given by

(15)

\zeta_{Q}^{\prime}({\bf 1})=1,\ \zeta_{Q}^{\prime}([s_{1}])=1,\ \zeta_{Q}^{\prime}([s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}])=0(k>1).

Therefore, by
[
2
]
, we have

Corollary 2.6
.

For any character
\chi:({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},{\mbox{\cyrs X}})\to\mathbb{Q}
, there is a unique Hopf algebra homomorphism

\Psi_{\chi}:({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},{\mbox{\cyrs X}},\Delta_{\geq 1})\to({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},\ast,\Delta_{\rm dec})

such that
\zeta^{\prime}_{Q}\circ\Psi_{\chi}=\chi
.

3.
Isomorphisms between Hopf algebras for MZVs

This section studies properties of the Hopf algebra homomorphism

in Corollary
2.6
.

3.1.
An order for tensors

Consider the grading

{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{Q}H_{n}

with
H_{n}
finite. We define an additional order on basis elements of
H_{n}
: for
[s_{1},\cdots,s_{m}]
,
[t_{1},\cdots,t_{\ell}]\in H_{n}
,

[s_{1},\cdots,s_{m}]>[t_{1},\cdots,t_{\ell}],

that is,

[t_{1},\cdots,t_{\ell}]<[s_{1},\cdots,s_{m}],

if and only if there is
1\leq j\leq\min\{m,\ell\}
such that
s_{1}=t_{1},\cdots,s_{j-1}=t_{j-1}
,
s_{j}<t_{j}
. Note that we do not compare elements with different weights.
This defines a well-order on the set of basis elements of
H_{n}
.

Example 3.1
.

(1)

The maximal element in
H_{n}
is
[1_{n}]
, the minimal element in
H_{n}
is
[n]
;

(2)

For basis elements of
H_{4}

Lemma 3.7
.

Proposition 3.10
.

The combinatorial Hopf algebra homomorphism
\Psi_{\chi}
is an isomorphism if and only if for every
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,
\chi([s])\not=0
.

Proof.

We already know that
\Psi_{\chi}|_{\mathbb{Q}H_{n}}
is upper triangular. Now let us find the coefficient
\chi_{[\vec{s}]}
of
[\vec{s}]
in
\Psi_{\chi}([\vec{s}])
.

Let
[\vec{s}]=[s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}]
. If
k=1
, then by definition,

\Delta_{\geq 1}^{(0)}([s_{1}])=[s_{1}],

\chi_{[s_{1}]}([s_{1}])=\chi([s_{1}]).

If
k>1
,
then

\Delta^{(k-1)}_{\geq 1}([\vec{s}])=(\Delta^{(k-2)}_{\geq 1}\otimes\mathrm{id})(\Delta_{\geq 1}([\vec{s}]))

and

\Delta_{\geq 1}([\vec{s}])=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{(\mathrm{id}\text{\textcircled{s}}p_{1}+p_{1}\otimes\mathrm{id})^{s_{1}-1}\cdots(\mathrm{id}\text{\textcircled{s}}p_{k}+p_{k}\otimes\mathrm{id})^{s_{k}-1}}{(s_{1}-1)!\cdots(s_{k}-1)!}([1_{j}]\otimes[1_{k-j}]).

In this sum, consider a term corresponding to
j=0,\cdots,k-2
. Then
\mathrm{dep}([1_{k-j}])\geq 2
. Note that
p_{i}
does not change the depth. So the second tensor factor of the term has depth greater or equal to 2. Then the first tensor factor has depth less or equal to
k-2
. This means that when
\Delta^{(k-2)}_{\geq 1}
is applied to this term, at least one of the resulting
k-1
tensor factors has to be
\bf 1
. This also means that, in their contributions to

\Delta^{(k-1)}_{\geq 1}([\vec{s}])
, the
k
-tensors

has at least one tentor factor
[\vec{u}_{i}]=[\emptyset]={\bf 1}
. Hence it is annihilated by the projection

\pi_{[\vec{s}]}:{\mathcal{H}}^{\otimes k}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}\to\mathbb{Q}H_{s_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{Q}H_{s_{k}}

and hence by
\chi_{[\vec{s}]}
in Eq. (
17
). The same holds for the terms corresponding to
j=k
.
Thus the only contribution to
\chi_{[\vec{s}]}
comes from the term in the sum with
j=k-1
.

Let us call a term a
\pi_{[\vec{s}]}
-zero term
if its image under the operator
\pi_{[\vec{s}]}
is zero. Then from the above argument, for

j=0,\cdots,k-2
or
j=k
, the contributions in
\Delta^{(k-1)}_{\geq 1}([\vec{s}])
from
[1_{j}]\otimes[1_{k-j}]
are the
\pi_{[\vec{s}]}
-zero terms.
So

\displaystyle\Delta^{(k-1)}_{\geq 1}([\vec{s}])=

\displaystyle(\Delta^{(k-2)}_{\geq 1}\otimes\mathrm{id})\Big(\frac{(\mathrm{id}\text{\textcircled{s}}p_{1}+p_{1}\otimes\mathrm{id})^{s_{1}-1}\cdots(\mathrm{id}\text{\textcircled{s}}p_{k}+p_{k}\otimes\mathrm{id})^{s_{k}-1}}{(s_{1}-1)!\cdots(s_{k}-1)!}\Big)([1_{k-1}]\otimes[1]))

\displaystyle+\pi_{[\vec{s}]}\text{-zero terms}.

\pi_{[\vec{s}]}
is the projection to
\mathbb{Q}H_{s_{k}}
, only

in the above expression can increase the last component in
[1_{k-1}]\otimes[1]
to

for some multi-tensor

. Thus the previous expression equals to

\displaystyle(\Delta^{(k-2)}_{\geq 1}\otimes\mathrm{id})\Big(\frac{(\mathrm{id}\text{\textcircled{s}}p_{1}+p_{1}\otimes\mathrm{id})^{s_{1}-1}\cdots(\mathrm{id}\text{\textcircled{s}}p_{k-1}+p_{k-1}\otimes\mathrm{id})^{s_{k-1}-1}}{(s_{1}-1)!\cdots(s_{k-1}-1)!}\Big)([1_{k-1}]\otimes[s_{k}]))

\displaystyle+

The linear map

\chi_{{}_{0}}:({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},{\mbox{\cyrs X}})\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}

[s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}]\mapsto\frac{1}{(s_{1}+\cdots+s_{k})!},\quad\chi_{{}_{0}}({\bf 1})=1

is a character.

Proof.

Let
[\vec{s}]
and
[\vec{t}]
be two basis elements of
{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}}
, with weight
m
and
n
respectively.
The shuffle product
[\vec{s}]{\mbox{\cyrs X}}[\vec{t}]
is the sum of shuffles of
[\vec{s}]
and
[\vec{t}]
, with monic coefficients. Since each shuffle is a permutation of
[\vec{s}]
and
[\vec{t}]
, the weight of a shuffle is the sum
m+n
. Furthermore, there are
\binom{m+n}{m}
shuffles. Thus

\chi_{{}_{0}}([\vec{s}]{\mbox{\cyrs X}}[\vec{t}])=\binom{m+n}{m}\frac{1}{(m+n)!}=\frac{1}{m!}\frac{1}{n!}=\chi_{{}_{0}}([\vec{s}])\chi_{{}_{0}}([\vec{t}]),

as needed.
∎

Corollary 3.14
.

For the character
\chi_{{}_{0}}
in Proposition
3.13
, the homomorphism
\Psi_{\chi_{{}_{0}}}
is a Hopf algebra isomorphism from

({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},{\mbox{\cyrs X}},\Delta_{\geq 1})
to
({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},\ast,\Delta_{\rm dec})
.

We give some values of the Hopf algebra isomorphism
\Psi:=\Psi_{\chi_{{}_{0}}}
.

Example 3.15
.

\displaystyle\Psi([n])

\displaystyle=\chi_{{}_{0}}([n])[n]=\frac{1}{n!}[n],n\geq 1,

\displaystyle\Psi([1,1])

\displaystyle=\chi_{{}_{0}}([1,1])[2]+\chi_{{}_{0}}^{2}([1])[1,1]=\frac{1}{2}[2]+[1,1],

\displaystyle\Psi([2,1])

\displaystyle=\chi_{{}_{0}}([2,1])[3]+\chi_{{}_{0}}([2])\chi_{{}_{0}}([1])[2,1]=\frac{1}{6}[3]+\frac{1}{2}[2,1],

\displaystyle\Psi([1,2])

\displaystyle=\chi_{{}_{0}}([1,2])[3]+\chi_{{}_{0}}([1])\chi_{{}_{0}}([2])[1,2]-\chi_{{}_{0}}([2])\chi_{{}_{0}}([1])[2,1]=\frac{1}{6}[3]+\frac{1}{2}[1,2]-\frac{1}{2}[2,1],

\displaystyle\Psi([1,1,1])

\displaystyle=\chi_{{}_{0}}([1,1,1])[3]+\chi_{{}_{0}}([1])\chi_{{}_{0}}([1,1])[1,2]+\chi_{{}_{0}}([1,1])\chi_{{}_{0}}([1])[2,1]+\chi_{{}_{0}}^{3}([1])[1,1,1]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}[3]+\frac{1}{2}[1,2]+\frac{1}{2}[2,1]+[1,1,1],

\displaystyle\Psi([3,1])

\displaystyle=\chi_{{}_{0}}([3,1])[4]+\chi_{{}_{0}}([3])\chi_{{}_{0}}([1])[3,1]=\frac{1}{4!}[4]+\frac{1}{3!}[3,1],

\displaystyle\Psi([2,2])

\displaystyle=\chi_{{}_{0}}([2,2])[4]+\chi_{{}_{0}}^{2}([2])[2,2]-2\chi_{{}_{0}}([3])\chi_{{}_{0}}([1])[3,1]=\frac{1}{4!}[4]+\frac{1}{4}[2,2]-\frac{1}{3}[3,1].

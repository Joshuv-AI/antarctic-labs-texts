Photoelectrocatalytic self-cross coupling enabling carbon chain elongation
Authors: Yuye Jiao, Zhiqiang Hu, Yurou Song, Biao Yang, Siyu Jiao, Guanghao Chen, Shijie Lu, Jungang Hou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractLong-chain compounds constitute fundamental feedstocks in modern chemical industries. Conventional chain elongation processes, however, remain constrained by their dependency on noble-metal catalysts and harsh operational parameters. To establish green and efficient carbon chain elongation protocols, this study designs a photoelectrocatalytic (PEC) system, enabling radical-mediated self-cross-coupling reactions. The system exhibits PEC performance in upgrading C2 molecules to value-added long-chain C3 products, achieving total selectivity exceeding 50% with a production rate of 159.17 mmol m−2 h−1. Moreover, diverse C2-C6 polyol substrates can be elongated to carbon-chain derivatives, demonstrating universal chain-elongation capability. In situ spectroscopy captures the dynamic evolution of reaction intermediates and verifies the critical role of chlorine radicals in enhancing the generation rates of carbon-centered and hydroxyl radicals. This work not only provides a solar-driven cross-coupling mechanism but also pioneers a green chemical synthesis pathway for sustainable and advanced long-chain chemicals.Subject terms: Photocatalysis, Photocatalysis

Introduction

Long-chain compounds (alcohols, aldehydes, and carboxylic acids) constitute fundamental feedstocks in modern chemical industries, and their production and application hold strategic significance1–3. These compounds serve dual roles as critical components of bio-liquid fuels and essential precursors for synthesizing high-value fine chemicals, such as plasticizers, thickeners, lubricants, and detergents, which play indispensable roles in energy, material, and daily chemical sectors4–7. Conventional production predominantly relies on thermocatalytic processes under high-temperature and high-pressure conditions, resulting in substantial energy consumption and carbon emissions that conflict with green and low-carbon development principles8. To address this obstacle, it is imperative to develop sustainable and green synthesis strategies for long-chain chemicals.

Carbon chain elongation, the critical step for converting simple carbon resources into complex organic molecules, provides broad opportunities for synthesizing value-added chemicals9. However, classical chemical synthesis methods, such as Grignard reactions10, Michael additions11, and aldol condensations12, face inherent limitations despite enabling chain elongation (Fig. 1a). For example, Grignard reactions with aldehydes produce alcohol derivatives with carbon chains elongation13, while Michael additions present chain growth through nucleophilic attacks on unsaturated carbonyl compounds11. These reactions typically require harsh conditions involving elevated temperatures and pressures, generate numerous side products, and often depend on noble metal catalysts, which collectively result in high costs and limited scalability. For example, the formate-derived synthesis of C3-C7 carboxylic acids via aldol condensation exemplifies such challenges, as this pathway is inherently compromised by undesirable dehydration side reactions14. In the conversion of ethanol to long-chain alcohols through Guerbet condensation, an additional hydrogenation step is required to address olefin byproduct formation. Likewise, the production of α-hydroxycarboxylic acids from ethylene glycol and primary alcohols requires high temperature and strongly alkaline conditions, posing environmental and separation concerns15. These limitations originate from the high-temperature-driven mechanisms, which impose stringent catalyst requirements and elevated energy demands, thereby constraining industrial sustainability. This technological landscape highlights the urgency of developing green and efficient carbon chain elongation strategies16,17. Notably, renewable energy-driven photoelectrocatalytic organic synthesis with the benefits from the integrated utilization of both electricity and solar, demonstrates the advanced potential for the green production of valuable chemicals18–21. Motivated by these insights, it is highly desirable but challenging for efforts to explore photoelectrocatalytic synthesis through carbon chain elongation from short-chain mediates.

Herein, we report a carbon chain elongation strategy using a photoelectrochemical self-cross-coupling approach from short-chain mediators (Fig. 1b). The integrated photoanode exhibits exceptional catalytic performance in upgrading C2 molecules to value-added long-chain C3 products, achieving >50% selectivity with a production rate of 159.17 mmol m−2 h−1 and maintaining stable operation over 40 h. Importantly, systematic evaluation across C2-C6 polyol substrates confirm universal chain-elongation capability through substrate-adaptive coupling pathways. In situ Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (in situ FTIR) and electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) analyses elucidate the dynamic generation of glycolaldehyde intermediates and identifies chloride ion-mediated enhancement of carbon-centered radical formation, whereas radical quenching experiments verified hydroxyl radicals (·OH) as the key oxidative species driving glyceraldehyde conversion to glyceraldehyde product. This work provides the PEC experimental validation of self-cross-coupling in C2 molecules to form longer-chain compounds, establishing a radical-mediated mechanism under mild conditions. These findings advance the mechanistic understanding of photoelectrocatalytic carbon chain elongation processes and demonstrate a sustainable pathway for synthesizing long-chain and value-added chemicals from renewable carbon resources.

Results

Electrolyte systems evaluation

To investigate the process of carbon chain elongation, ethylene glycol (EG), a typical C2 molecule, was employed as a model substrate for self-cross-coupling reactions targeting long-chain C3 products, including glycolic acid (GLA), glyceraldehyde (GLD), and dihydroxyacetone (DHA, Fig. 2a). This transformation involves radical chain reactions coupled with selective nucleophilic attacks on reactive intermediates, for which photoelectrocatalysis is a crucial method for mechanistic elucidation. Linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) analysis of BiVO4 (BVO) in various electrolyte systems revealed distinct oxidation peaks between 0.4–0.5 V vs. RHE corresponding to the V3+ → V5+ transition22 (Fig. 2b, Supplementary Fig. 1), wherein rapid hole generation from vanadium oxidation provides the reaction driving force but is counteracted by severe hole accumulation, which critically destabilizes the photoanode and impedes practical photoelectrochemical applications23,24. Chronoamperometric (J-t) evaluations demonstrated rapid photocurrent decay of BVO photoanode within 0.5 h in pure KOH or KCl systems, accompanied by near-complete dissolution from fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) substrates (Supplementary Fig. 2), where KCl introduction marginally enhanced the photocurrent density but exacerbated corrosion, whereas K2SO4-containing or mixed K2SO4-KCl electrolytes significantly improved stability through SO42- adsorption-induced electrostatic repulsion barrier25 (Fig. 2c). This barrier effectively minimizes the direct contact between OH- / Cl- and the electrode surface, thereby suppressing the dissolution of vanadium species25. High-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) analyses revealed that the pristine BVO photoanode exhibited anion-dependent selectivity in the distribution of C1 and C3 products across different electrolyte systems (Fig. 2d, Supplementary Figs. 3-4). In summary, the strong alkaline environment and the mediating effect of Cl- favored the carbon chain elongation reaction but exacerbated the corrosion of the photoanode, whereas effective inhibition of chloride corrosion was achieved after the introduction of SO42- (Fig. 2e). By leveraging the dual functionality of metal oxyhydroxides (MOOH) to enhance hole transfer kinetics, reduce activation energies for C-C bond cleavage, and supply C1 intermediates26, an MOOH-modified BVO photoanode was engineered via facile impregnation, synergistically improving stability and catalytic activity to establish an optimized platform for practical carbon chain elongation reactions (Supplementary Figs. 5-13).

Reaction pathway exploration

Combining the experimental findings from electrolyte optimization and material engineering analyses, systematic investigation of carbon chain elongation pathways requiring refinement reveals two dominant reaction mechanisms (Fig. 3). The primary pathway involves direct C-C bond cleavage of EG followed by oxidation to formic acid (FA), while the secondary route proceeds through sequential oxidation to glycolaldehyde (GLCD) with subsequent transformation to glycolic acid (GA) or glyoxal. To address critical bottlenecks in C1-C2 coupling efficiency, it is necessary to optimize two factors: (1) enhancing direct C-C cleavage efficiency to ensure sufficient C1 intermediate supply; (2) accelerating EG-to-GLCD conversion kinetics while mitigating over-oxidation side reactions. This dual-pathway modulation establishes essential precursor reservoirs for subsequent coupling reactions.

Photoelectrochemical performance

Building upon the preceding mechanistic analysis, the reaction conditions were systematically optimized, and the carbon chain elongation products were qualitatively and quantitatively characterized by liquid chromatograph mass spectrometry (LC-MS) and high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC). LC-MS analyses confirmed the formation of C3 products (GLA, GLD, DHA) under optimized conditions (Supplementary Figs 14, 15). Quantitative HPLC analyses revealed C3 product generation in KCl-supplemented KOH electrolyte when employing CoFeOOH/BVO photoanode (Fig. 4a, Supplementary Fig. 16). In alkaline KCl electrolytes, acidic product yields increased with higher Cl- concentrations, while aldehyde production decreased, indicating Cl- mediation of aldehyde-to-acid oxidation pathways (Fig. 4b, Supplementary Fig. 17). Elevated pH conditions facilitated lactic acid (LA) formation via base-catalyzed Cannizzaro rearrangement of GLD27, whereas low alkaline environments completely suppressed C3 product generation, confirming the necessity of alkaline conditions for carbon chain elongation. The OH- concentration demonstrated a positive correlation with formic acid production efficiency, highlighting the involvement of the OH-group in C-C bond cleavage (Fig. 4c, Supplementary Fig. 18). In addition, systematic optimization of KCl, K2SO4, and EG concentrations in alkaline K2SO4-KCl electrolytes revealed concentration-dependent effects on GLA selectivity and total C3 yield. At low KCl/K2SO4 concentrations or elevated EG levels, excessive EG adsorption occupied the catalytic active sites, whereas reduced ionic strength and sluggish charge transfer kinetics favored aldehyde intermediate desorption over acid formation. Controlled KCl addition enhanced C-C coupling via Cl- mediation, achieving a peak C3 yield at 0.1 M KCl and 0.25 M K2SO4 before side reactions dominated (Fig. 4d-e, Supplementary Figs. 19, 20). When the concentration was low, OH- was more likely to participate in the oxygen evolution reaction (OER) process rather than oxidizing aldehyde intermediates to produce acids. Through evaluating the influence of different alkali metal cations (Li+, Na+, K+ and Cs+) on the reaction pathway and product selectivity, K+ emerged as the most effective choice (Supplementary Fig. 21). To investigate the effect of EG concentration, it was observed that increasing the EG concentration resulted in substantial enhancements in both the total Faradaic efficiency (FE) and C3 yield (Fig. 4f, Supplementary Fig. 22).

Furthermore, the applied potential significantly influenced the EG adsorption-desorption process. At lower potentials, insufficient EG adsorption occurred, resulting in low total FE, whereas at higher potentials, EG was oxidized to GLCD intermediates that tended to desorb directly (Fig. 4g, Supplementary Fig. 23)28. The production of GLD was not detected at the initiation of the reaction. As the reaction time increased, the total C3 yield remained stable, although the glycerol aldehyde production rate exhibited an upward trend (Fig. 4h). Notably, the CoFeOOH/BVO system maintained stable performance for over 40 h in K2SO4-KCl electrolytes (Fig. 4i), confirming its robustness for sustained carbon chain elongation.

After optimizing the electrolyte conditions, the PEC performance of BVO photoanode modified with single- and multi-metal oxyhydroxides (MOOH) was systematically investigated for carbon chain elongation. Initially, the effects of single-metal (Fe, Co, Ni) oxyhydroxides were examined (Fig. 4j, Supplementary Fig. 24), demonstrating that the presence of CoOOH/BVO significantly enhanced the selectivity towards GLA and total C3 products, whereas the presence of FeOOH/BVO and NiOOH/BVO effectively improved the total FE and total C3 yields. To gain deeper insights into the differences between single- and multi-metal oxyhydroxide modifications, a comparative analysis of bimetallic (CoFeOOH/BVO, CoNiOOH/BVO) and trimetallic (CoFeNiOOH/BVO) modifications demonstrated superior performance of CoFeOOH/BVO, achieving total selectivity exceeding 50% with a production rate of 159.17 mmol m−2 h−1 and a GLA FE reaching 24.9%. All MOOH-modified photoanodes demonstrated substantial improvements in both FE and C3 yields relative to the pristine BVO photoanode, confirming the universal effectiveness of oxyhydroxide interfacial engineering for carbon chain elongation reactions. In addition, we evaluated the chain elongation performance using different C2-C6 polyol substrates and demonstrated that the method has a universal chain elongation capability to consistently produce long carbon chain compounds based on LC-MS qualitative analyses (Supplementary Figs. 25–38). The quantitative analyses of higher-carbon-number products present persistent analytical challenges owing to structural complexity and isomeric interference, technical barriers that will be systematically addressed in subsequent investigations.

Mechanism analyses

To elucidate the key intermediates and reaction pathways in carbon chain elongation reaction, in situ Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (in situ FTIR) measurements were conducted during the reaction process (Fig. 5a). In the preliminary stage, a characteristic peak of the ν(C-O) vibration29 of EG was observed at 1050 cm−1. After 30 min, GLA30 formation was evidenced by emerging peaks at 1377 cm-1 (C-H bending), 1438 cm−1 (C-O asymmetric stretching), and 1606 cm−1 (C = O stretching), while the 1666 cm−1 peak indicated DHA31 generation. At 60 min, the characteristic peaks of GLD32 at 1680 cm−1 and 1082 cm−1 were attributed to the C = O stretching vibration and C-O symmetric stretching vibration, respectively. Under different potential conditions (Supplementary Fig. 39), a consumption peak at 1700 cm−1 was observed, which was attributed to the C = O stretching vibration of the key coupling intermediate GLCD33 and the absorption peak at 1037 cm−1 was attributed to the ν(C-O) vibration of secondary alcohols in C3 products34. In conclusion, in situ FTIR results strongly suggest the involvement of GLCD as a key intermediate in the carbon chain elongation process and the generation of the C3 products. To clarify the roles of Cl- and SO42- in carbon chain elongation reaction, EPR experiments were performed using 5,5-dimethyl-1-pyrroline N-oxide (DMPO) as a spin-trapping agent. Under the condition of using KOH, K2SO4 and KCl alone as electrolytes, a characteristic 1:2:2:1 quartet signal was observed, corresponding to the DMPO-·OH adduct (hydroxyl radical adduct)35. Notably, the ·OH signal intensity in the presence of K2SO4 was weaker than that in a pure KOH solution, whereas the addition of KCl significantly enhanced the ·OH signal (Fig. 5b). Subsequently, the introduction of EG to the K2SO4 and KCl systems generated carbon-centered (·CH2OH) radicals, showing stronger signals in KCl (Fig. 5c). Moreover, the signal of ·CH2OH increases with the duration of illumination in the KCl solution and produced a seven-line DMPO-·Cl adduct spectrum (Fig. 5d)36. Following a 3‑hour reaction period, analysis of the K2SO4-KCl mixed electrolyte showed enhanced EPR signals for ·Cl, ·OH, and sulfate radicals (·SO4−)37 (Fig. 5e). To elucidate the mechanistic role of ·OH in carbon chain elongation reaction, radical quenching experiments were performed (Fig. 5f, Supplementary Fig. 40). The near-complete suppression of EG oxidation upon the addition of potassium sulfite (K2SO3) as a hole scavenger confirmed the pivotal role of holes in the PEC process. Tert-butanol (TBA, ·OH scavenger)38,39 and isopropanol (IPA, ·OH/·SO4- scavenger)40,41 comparably reduced GLA FE while increasing GLD yields, confirming the essential role of ·OH in GLD-to-GLA oxidation42. Moreover, within the primary reaction window, C2 intermediates preferentially couple with C1 species to form C3 products with negligible dimerization side reactions, whereas only under prolonged reaction times does a minor extent of C2 dimerization occur due to intermediate concentration imbalances (Supplementary Fig. 41).

Combined with the above experimental results, the regulatory processes of different electrolytes in the photoelectrocatalytic carbon chain elongation system are systematically summarized (Fig. 5g). It is evident that a substantial increase in pH results in a notable elevation of the concentration of OH- in the system. This phenomenon effectively promotes the cleavage of the C-C bonds of C2 molecules, thereby forming ·CH2OH intermediates. The introduction of K2SO4 modifies OH- adsorption dynamics on BVO surface, dualistically regulating reaction pathways by suppressing OER and enhancing solution-phase OH--mediated nucleophilic attacks on EG to accelerate ·CH2OH generation. Moreover, the introduction of Cl- has been demonstrated to cause significant alterations in the product distribution and intermediate transformation pathways. It is evident that an elevated concentration of Cl- results in a significant decline in FE for GLCD, signifying an augmented involvement of C2 intermediates in C-C coupling reactions. Meantime, the decrease in FE of FA and the significant increase in FE of the C3 product are indicative of Cl- promoting the carbon chain growth process, which means that the production of glyoxal is inhibited, thus blocking the isomerization reaction43. Moreover, the Cl-/·Cl cycle functions primarily as a reaction mediator and intermediate activator with negligible chlorination side reactions within the main reaction window (Supplementary Fig. 42). The following reaction pathway for carbon chain elongation is proposed in this study. The C2 molecules are initially oxidized to GLCD on the surface of the photoanode by a double electron transfer process, at the same time, Cl- is oxidized by holes to form Cl2, which is subsequently further converted to ·Cl under light drive44. Subsequently, the α-H of GLCD undergoes a hydrogen atom transfer (HAT) process45 with ·Cl, and the key coupling intermediate formed then undergoes a coupling reaction with ·CH2OH generated by C-C cleavage of C2 molecules, which leads to the formation of GLD (Supplementary Fig. 43). In addition, the ability of GLCD to be reversibly converted to an enolic structure in the alkaline storage environment, making it easier for ·Cl to seize the α-H of GLCD, facilitating the HAT process and thus favoring the C-C coupling reaction. Among the C3 products, GLA is the product of further ·OH mediated oxidation of GLD, and DHA is produced by the isomerization of GLD under alkaline conditions46. Combined with the EPR results, Cl- not only plays a mediating role in promoting the production of GLCD intermediates and preventing their further oxidation to generate glyoxal. Moreover, Cl- attacked the H2O molecules to produce more ·OH47, which could rapidly oxidize GLD to GLA, greatly changing the reaction product distribution and improving the carbon chain elongation product.

Discussion

In summary, we developed a universal and green photoelectrochemical upgrading strategy for the synthesis of value-added long-chain chemicals from short-chain mediators. The optimized PEC system attained a total C3 selectivity of 51.59% with a C3 production rate of 159.17 mmol m−2 h−1 in upgrading C2 molecules. Moreover, diverse C2-C6 polyol substrates can be elongated to carbon-chain derivatives, demonstrating universal chain-elongation capability. Mechanism insights were obtained through in situ characterization techniques, including in situ Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy for capturing dynamic glycolaldehyde intermediate evolution and C3 product formation, and electron paramagnetic resonance spectroscopy for confirming that chloride radicals enhance the generation of carbon-centered radicals and hydroxyl radicals. Radical quenching experiments identified hydroxyl radicals as the primary oxidative species governing glyceraldehyde-to-glyceric acid conversion. These findings not only validate a radical-mediated mechanism for C-C bond cleavage and subsequent coupling of short-chain intermediates into long carbon chain compounds, but also establishes a sustainable solar energy driven pathway for the production of value-added and environmentally benign chemicals.

Methods

Prepared of MOOH/BVO photoanodes

The BVO photoanode was fabricated using a two-step process based on a previously reported method48. BiOI nanosheets were cathodically electrodeposited onto FTO glass at −0.1 V vs. Ag/AgCl and 25 °C for 200 s from a precursor solution containing 0.04 M Bi(NO3)3·5H2O, 0.4 M KI, and 0.23 M p‑benzoquinone (dissolved in ethanol). The resulting BiOI electrode was then converted into porous BVO by drop‑casting a vanadium source (0.2 M VO(acac)2 in DMSO) and annealing at 450 °C for 2 h with a heating rate of 2 °C min−1. Finally, the BVO photoanode was immersed in 1 M NaOH for 1 h to remove excess vanadium species. A simple and efficient impregnation method was employed to fabricate MOOH/BVO photoanodes. Initially, BVO was impregnated in a 10 mM FeCl3·6H2O solution containing 1 M ethylene glycol (EG) for 10 min. Subsequently, the pH of the reaction system was adjusted to 8.0 using NaOH. The treated samples were placed in an oven at 80 °C for 4 h for heat treatment. After the heat treatment, the samples were thoroughly rinsed with deionized water and ethanol to remove residual impurities and unreacted precursors. Finally, the samples were dried at room temperature to obtain the FeOOH/BVO photoanode materials. Different MOOH/BVO photoanodes, including CoOOH/BVO, NiOOH/BVO, NiCoOOH/BVO, CoFeOOH/BVO, and CoFeNiOOH/BVO, were synthesized by simply changing the metal source in the impregnation solution.

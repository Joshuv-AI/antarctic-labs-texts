Comparative analysis of zebrafish, organoid, and organ-on-chip models in breast cancer research
Authors: Reem Alzaharani, Maha Ali Alghadmi, Mitra Amiri
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBreast cancer remains a leading cause of cancer mortality worldwide, underscoring the urgent need for advanced preclinical models that faithfully recapitulate tumor complexity and improve therapeutic prediction. Traditional two-dimensional cell cultures and animal models often fail to capture the heterogeneous and dynamic nature of the disease, limiting their translational relevance. This review addresses a critical gap in the literature by providing a comprehensive and integrated comparison of three contemporary, complementary platforms—patient-derived organoids, zebrafish xenografts, and organ-on-chip systems—within a unified translational framework for breast cancer research. We evaluate each model's capacity to elucidate key signaling pathways, model molecular and genetic alterations, and recapitulate the biology of major breast cancer subtypes. Furthermore, we critically assess their roles in drug screening, personalized treatment prediction, and the study of resistance mechanisms, explicitly distinguishing between early-stage proof-of-concept studies, validated preclinical applications, and emerging clinical predictability. A key contribution of this work is a practical, decision-oriented synthesis, supported by comparative matrices, that guides researchers in model selection based on biological relevance, throughput, cost, and clinical predictability. We also highlight current challenges in reproducibility, scalability, and clinical validation, and discuss how emerging technologies such as CRISPR/Cas9 gene editing, artificial intelligence, and bioprinting are poised to address these limitations. By synthesizing the distinct advantages and applications of each platform, this review aims to accelerate the rational use of these advanced models in mechanistic discovery, drug development, and the advancement of precision oncology for breast cancer.Keywords: Breast cancer, Patient-derived organoids, Zebrafish xenograft, Preclinical model, Organ-on-a-Chip, Drug screening, Precision oncology

Introduction

The global burden of breast cancer and the need for advanced models

Breast cancer remains a significant global health concern, accounting for approximately one in every eight cancer diagnoses and 2.3 million new cases reported annually.1 Despite its prevalence, the clinical translation of novel therapies remains hampered by the inherent limitations of traditional research models. Fundamental differences exist between these systems: traditional two-dimensional (2D) cell culture models fail to accurately mimic the in vivo tumor microenvironment, lacking the 3D architecture, spatial heterogeneity, and mechanical cues necessary for realistic drug testing.2,3 Similarly, while mouse xenografts have historically been the gold standard, they are increasingly criticized for their high costs, low throughput, and significant interspecies differences—particularly regarding immune context and stromal interactions—which often lead to poor clinical predictability.4,5 Furthermore, the prolonged time required for tumor establishment in mice can result in genetic drift, whereas 3D models allow for more rapid and genomic-stable analysis.4,6

Recent advancements in three-dimensional (3D) cancer models, such as patient-derived organoids, organ-on-chip systems, and zebrafish xenografts, offer more physiologically relevant platforms by recapitulating critical aspects of the TME, gene expression, and cellular diversity.7,8 These technologies are poised to accelerate drug discovery and personalize treatment strategies for breast cancer patients (Fig. 1).7,9

Advanced 3D models: distinctive features and translational value

Organoids are small self-organizing 3D structures generated from stem cells or patient specimen-derived spheroids.7 They have revolutionized research by maintaining the histopathology and hormone receptor status of the original tumor over multiple passages.3,8 While they are widely used for preclinical drug response and mechanistic studies—showing high concordance with patient outcomes in several reports—large-scale prospective clinical validation remains a critical requirement for their transition into routine diagnostic use (Fig. 2).10, 11, 12

Zebrafish offer a valuable in vivo system due to their transparent embryos, rapid development, and high-throughput screening capabilities.13,14 Zebrafish xenografts are particularly instrumental for rapid (7–10 day) proof-of-concept studies of tumor invasion, angiogenesis, and early metastatic behavior.15,16 However, their translational value is currently limited by the need for compromised incubation temperatures (33–35 °C), which may alter human cell proliferation compared to the human body (Fig. 3).5,17,18

Organ-on-chip technologies combine engineered tissues with microfluidics to replicate the physiological and mechanical environments of human organs.19 These systems bridge the gap between static in vitro studies and dynamic in vivo mechanisms, offering a mechanistically informative tool for studying tumor–vascular and tumor–stroma interactions.2,20 While they improve physiological relevance by incorporating fluid shear stress and mechanical strain, their clinical predictive validity has not yet been systematically established in large cohorts (Fig. 4).21, 22, 23 The key advantages of these three platforms are summarized in Table 1.

Emerging technologies enhancing cancer models

The utility of 3D models is significantly enhanced by the integration of cutting-edge technologies. CRISPR/Cas9 genome editing facilitates the functional interrogation of key driver genes—such as TP53, PIK3CA, and BRCA1/2—by allowing for the precise induction of breast cancer-relevant alterations in organoids.24,25 Artificial intelligence is increasingly applied to analyze high-dimensional imaging and multi-omic data from these systems.22 Machine-learning algorithms have demonstrated potential for predicting therapeutic sensitivity based on organoid morphology and transcriptomic signatures, thereby accelerating precision oncology pipelines.22,26,27 Furthermore, bioprinting technologies enable the spatial organization of tumor, stromal, and vascular components with high precision, creating intricate structures that more faithfully model the invasive front of tumors28, 29, 30 (see Table 2).

Purpose and scope of this review: novelty and comparative framework

While existing reviews often focus on individual models, a critical gap remains in the integrated comparison of PDOs, zebrafish, and organ-on-chip systems within a unified translational framework. This review directly addresses this gap by systematically assessing their biological relevance, scalability, cost, and clinical predictability. A key novelty of this work is a practical, decision-oriented synthesis, supported by comparative matrices (e.g., Table 3), that guides researchers in model selection for mechanistic discovery and drug development. The Use of Organoids, zebrafish, and organ-on-chip to identify key signaling pathways

Several signaling pathways, including PI3K/AKT/mTOR, Wnt, and Hedgehog, play crucial roles in the initiation, growth, and metastasis of breast cancer. While traditional 2D models provide a basic understanding of these cascades, they fail to capture the dynamic pathway crosstalk influenced by the 3D tumor microenvironment. The following section provides a detailed comparative analysis of these pathways and the specific contributions of advanced preclinical platforms.

PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway

The PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway is a central regulator of survival, growth, and angiogenesis.32,33 Its dysregulation, particularly through PIK3CA mutations or PTEN loss, is a primary driver of resistance to both endocrine and HER2-targeted therapies.34,35 The clinical significance of this pathway is underscored by landmark trials such as BOLERO-2 and TAMRAD, which demonstrated that the addition of the mTOR inhibitor everolimus to hormonal therapy significantly improves progression-free survival in hormone receptor-positive patients refractory to initial treatments.34,36

Advanced models have become indispensable for studying these resistance mechanisms. Patient-derived organoids have reached a stage of validated preclinical application, successfully identifying how PIK3CA mutations drive endocrine resistance and allowing for the testing of combination strategies to overcome this blockade.34,37 Zebrafish xenografts provide a unique high-throughput in vivo advantage, facilitating the rapid screening of novel PI3K and mTOR inhibitors before entering mammalian testing.8,17 Furthermore, organ-on-chip platforms allow researchers to test these inhibitors under physiological shear stress, providing a more accurate representation of drug delivery.2,20

Wnt/β-catenin signaling

The Wnt signaling pathway regulates cell growth and embryonic development. In breast cancer, the upregulation of Caveolin 1 and co-activators like Pygopus homolog 2 activate multidrug resistance genes, contributing to therapeutic evasion.32 While traditional models provide a static view, organoids have revealed Wnt-dependent tumor heterogeneity.6,7 Zebrafish models have been particularly instrumental in providing real-time visualization of Wnt-driven tumor invasion and early metastatic seeding.15,38

Hedgehog signaling

The Hedgehog signaling pathway regulates embryonic development and tissue homeostasis by controlling the nuclear translocation of Gli transcription factors.32 In breast cancer, Hh signaling is implicated in the maintenance of cancer stem cells and the promotion of a pro-metastatic phenotype.32,39 Organoids have been utilized in preclinical studies to test inhibitors targeting components like Patched1 and Smoothened.8,40 Zebrafish xenografts have provided critical in vivo insights into how the Hh pathway maintains tumor-initiating cell survival and drives therapy resistance.15,16

Cytokine signaling and the tumor microenvironment

Beyond intrinsic pathways, extrinsic cytokine signaling (such as oncostatin M and various interleukins) drives progression through STAT3 activation. Organ-on-chip platforms are uniquely suited to study this interface by co-culturing cancer cells with immune cells and stromal fibroblasts, allowing for the precise interrogation of how cytokine gradients within the microenvironment promote invasion and immune evasion.2,41 Integration of these cytokines into PDO cultures remains a challenge, but it represents a critical frontier for creating truly humanized cancer models.40,41

The Use of Organoids, Zebrafish, and Organ-on-Chip to Identify Molecular and Genetic Alterations.

A defining advantage of these advanced models is their ability to recapitulate the complex genetic landscape of the original patient tumor with high fidelity. Unlike 2D cell lines, which often undergo significant genetic drift, patient-derived organoids broadly recapitulate the diversity of the disease, maintaining the DNA copy number variations and sequence changes of the parent tissue even after extended passaging.8 Studies have confirmed that PDO morphologies and gene expression profiles typically match the histopathology and hormone receptor status of the primary tumor.8,42

CRISPR-mediated modeling of carcinogenesis

The integration of CRISPR/Cas9 technology with organoid culture has allowed researchers to move beyond descriptive observation to functional genetic interrogation. By introducing specific knock-outs of tumor suppressor genes—such as TP53, PTEN, RB1, and NF1 (collectively known as the PTRN signature)—into normal human mammary progenitor cells, researchers have successfully modeled the multi-step process of carcinogenesis.43,44 These "engineered" organoids can generate luminal or basal-like tumors upon xenotransplantation, providing a powerful platform to study how specific genetic alterations dictate therapeutic response and subtype identity.43,44 Furthermore, CRISPR-mediated screens are increasingly used to identify functional mediators of therapy resistance.44,45

Genomic predictors of drug sensitivity

Genomic profiling of organoid biobanks has identified key pathogenic mutations that serve as biomarkers for drug response. For instance, PIK3CA mutations are frequently identified in luminal breast cancer organoids (approx. 33%), while TP53 mutations are predominantly found in high-grade TNBC lines (approx. 45%).42 These genetic markers are directly utilized in functional drug screens; for example, the MDM2-TP53 interaction inhibitor Nutlin-3a can be used to distinguish between wild-type and TP53-mutated organoids.46

Subtype-specific modeling in breast cancer

HER2-positive breast cancer: modeling resistance and salvage therapies

HER2-positive breast cancer is characterized by the amplification of the ERBB2 gene. While trastuzumab is a cornerstone of treatment, resistance often emerges through the activation of bypass pathways, such as PTEN loss or PIK3CA oncogenic mutations.45 Organoid and PDX models have been instrumental in identifying salvage strategies; for instance, the pan-HER kinase inhibitor neratinib has shown high efficacy in overcoming trastuzumab resistance in both in vitro organoid screens and in vivo xenografts.47,48 Furthermore, recent studies combining CDK12 inhibition with anti-HER2 drugs have shown promise in re-sensitizing refractory models to treatment, a finding that is now being explored for clinical translation.4

Triple-negative breast cancer: the frontier of precision oncology

TNBC remains the most challenging subtype due to the absence of targeted receptors. Research using organ-on-chip platforms has revealed that TNBC cells exhibit significantly different drug sensitivities in 3D perfused microenvironments compared to standard 2D cultures, highlighting the importance of physical forces in modeling chemotherapy response.2,49 Large-scale prospective studies, such as the TRIPLEX study, are currently investigating the feasibility of using TNBC organoids to predict real-time clinical responses to neoadjuvant chemotherapy and immune checkpoint inhibitors.50,51 While zebrafish "avatars" offer a rapid 10-day readout for metastatic potential in TNBC, these platforms are primarily used for mechanistic discovery.16,51

Functional testing and patient-derived organoids

Organoid technology has reached a stage of validated preclinical application.8 Large-scale biobanks have demonstrated that these models broadly recapitulate the genetic and phenotypic diversity of the disease.8 High-throughput screens in PDOs have shown a high level of concordance with retrospective patient responses, particularly for chemotherapeutic agents like paclitaxel and doxorubicin.8, 38 By directly challenging a patient's own tumor cells with a panel of selected therapies, PDOs act as a functional diagnostic tool that complements genomic profiling.8,54

Zebrafish "zAvatars": real-time clinical decision support

Zebrafish xenografts, or "zAvatars," represent a promising frontier for real-time personalized therapy. The "zAvatar-test" provides a rapid in vivo therapeutic readout within 10 days—a timeline that is clinically actionable.16 This assay facilitates the assessment of critical cancer hallmarks, including angiogenic potential and metastatic dissemination.16,54 While large-scale prospective clinical trials are still ongoing, early data suggests that zebrafish models can accurately forecast chemotherapy responses.10,16,54

Organ-on-chip: high-throughput precision engineering

Organ-on-chip platforms offer a mechanistically informative approach to drug evaluation.31 High-throughput microfluidic systems allow for continuous drug delivery and standardized monitoring of vascularization.31,49 These systems have revealed that breast cancer cells exhibit significantly higher drug resistance when cultured in dynamic 3D microenvironments.49,53

Conclusion and future perspective

The transition toward precision oncology for breast cancer necessitates the integration of high-fidelity models that move beyond the limitations of 2D cultures and interspecies mammalian models. As established in this review, no single platform is currently sufficient to model all aspects of the disease; instead, we propose a complementary workflow. Zebrafish "zAvatars" provide the necessary speed for real-time therapeutic prediction, patient-derived organoids offer a stable biobank for high-throughput genomic and functional screening, and organ-on-chip systems facilitate the precise interrogation of mechanical forces and the metastatic cascade.2,8,16

The future of this field lies in the integration of emerging technologies to address remaining challenges in scalability and standardization. CRISPR/Cas9 will continue to allow for the creation of "humanized" isogenic models to study driver mutations in a patient-specific background.24,25 3D bioprinting will enable the construction of more complex, vascularized tumor microenvironments that better reflect human tissue stiffness and organization.2,28 Finally, the incorporation of artificial intelligence for high-content imaging analysis will provide automated, unbiased readouts of drug efficacy, significantly refining the clinical decision-making process.22,56 By synergizing these platforms, the research community can bridge the translational gap and move closer to truly individualized treatment for every breast cancer patient.

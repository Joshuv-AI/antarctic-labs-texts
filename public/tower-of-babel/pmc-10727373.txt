Temporal Trends in Heart Failure Management and Outcomes: Insights From a Japanese Multicenter Registry of Tertiary Care Centers
Authors: Ryo Nakamaru, Shun Kohsaka, Yasuyuki Shiraishi, Takashi Kohno, Ayumi Goda, Yuji Nagatomo, Mitsunobu Kitamura, Shintaro Nakano, Makoto Takei, Atsushi Mizuno, Tsutomu Yoshikawa
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe management of heart failure (HF) has markedly changed, due to changes in demographics and the emergence of novel pharmacotherapies. However, detailed analyses on the temporal trends in characteristics and outcomes among patients with HF are scarcely available. This study aimed to assess the temporal trends over 11 years in clinical management and outcomes in patients with HF.Methods and ResultsWe analyzed data from a multicenter registry of hospitalized patients with acute HF, including 6877 patients registered from 2011 to 2021. Age‐adjusted mortality was calculated using standardized mortality ratios. During the study period, mean age increased from 75.2 years in 2011 to 2012 to 76.4 years in 2020 to 2021 (P for trend <0.001). The proportion of HF with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF, left ventricular ejection fraction <40%) remained constant (from 43.4% to 42.7%, P for trend=0.38). The median duration of hospital stays (from 15 to 17 days, P for trend<0.001) had increased. As for the implementation of guideline‐directed medical therapy, the use of mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist at discharge increased in patients with HFrEF (from 44.3% to 60.2%, P for trend<0.001). There was also an increase in the use of sodium‐glucose cotransporter‐2 inhibitors following their approval for use. The age‐adjusted 1‐year mortality decreased in patients with HFrEF (from 18.0% to 9.3%, P for trend<0.001) but not in patients with non‐HFrEF (left ventricular ejection fraction ≥40%; from 9.2% to 9.5%, P for trend=0.79).ConclusionsHospitalized patients with HF have been aging over the past decade. Their long‐term outcomes after discharge have improved predominantly because of decreased mortality in patients with HFrEF.Keywords: acute heart failure, left ventricular ejection fraction, mortality, temporal trendSubject Categories: Heart Failure

The global incidence of newly diagnosed heart failure (HF) has been steadily increasing in tandem with the aging of the population. 1 As HF becomes more prevalent among older individuals, 1 , 2 the demographics of the hospitalized patients are shifting. Furthermore, recent advances in the understanding of HF pathophysiology, as well as the emergence of novel pharmacotherapies, have led to changes in clinical practice guideline recommendations. 3 , 4 , 5 Consequently, an update of knowledge regarding temporal trends in patients with HF is imperative to keep pace with the changing landscape of this condition.

Previous time‐trend analyses on patients with HF, mostly based on analyses of administrative data sets, have often lacked important phenotype information such as left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), serum biomarker levels, dispensing of medications, or investigations on longer‐term outcomes. 6 , 7 , 8 , 9 , 10 , 11 , 12 , 13 As a result, the accuracy of trend validation has been limited. Additionally, several studies have primarily focused on outpatient HF cases, 8 , 9 , 12 , 13 , 14 which encompass a heterogeneous range of disease stages. Conversely, analyses based on HF hospitalization offer a more homogeneous patient population with similar disease stages. Furthermore, hospitalization presents a critical opportunity to optimize patient care, including guideline‐directed medical therapy, in contemporary HF management. 3 , 4

We therefore aimed to assess the temporal trends in clinical management and in‐hospital and long‐term outcomes in patients hospitalized for acute HF from 2011 to 2021, using the data from the WET‐HF (West Tokyo Heart Failure) registry. WET‐HF is a prospective multicenter cohort study that includes consecutive hospitalized patients with acute HF during the last decade. To date, the data from this registry have been used in the development of clinical guidelines 5 and advancement of HF research. 2 , 15 , 16 , 17

Methods

The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

Data Sources

The present study represents a post hoc analysis that was part of the WET‐HF, a prospective multicenter cohort registry designed to collect data of consecutive patients (aged ≥20 years) who were admitted for acute HF and required urgent treatment. Patients with acute HF were diagnosed based on the Framingham criteria and physical examinations including laboratory tests and echocardiography 3 , 4 , 18 by cardiologists at each institution. In this study, 98.2% of the participants underwent BNP (brain natriuretic hormone) or NT‐proBNP (N‐terminal pro‐B‐type natriuretic peptide) measurements (Table S1), and echocardiography was performed on all participants at the time of admission.

The WET‐HF registry, was initially launched as a single center database from January 2006 and added institutions since 2011, consisting of 6 tertiary hospitals located in the metropolitan Tokyo area by 2017. Finally, 2 further institutions were incorporated in April 2018 (WET‐HF2 registry) (Figure S1), which featured an update of the collected variables such as clinical frailty scale (CFS), 17 cause of blood transfusion, and following precipitating clinical factors for HF admission: poor adherence to diet or medication, excess physical activity, arrhythmia (according to documented electrocardiogram), infection, myocardial ischemia, anemia, thyroid dysfunction (ie, hyper‐ or hypothyroidism), others, or no specific factors identified. The patient data were entered into an electronic data capture system with a robust data query engine and system validations for data quality. The principal investigators conducted periodic queries to verify the reporting quality at least once a year. Patients who refused to participate in the study or presented with concurrent HF and acute coronary syndrome were excluded from registration. As for the end points, all death‐related events were reviewed by the investigators and classified into the following groups: those requiring adjudication and those with a clearly defined mode of death. Subsequently, central committee members reviewed the abstracted records and adjudicated the modes of death.

Before the launch of this registry, information on the present study's objectives and social significance, as well as an abstract, were provided for clinical trial registration to the University Hospital Medical Information Network of Japan (UMIN000001171). The institutional review boards at each site approved the study protocol, and the research was conducted according to the Declaration of Helsinki. Written or oral informed consent was obtained from each patient before the study.

Study Population

A total of 7641 consecutive patients with HF were registered in the WET‐HF 1 and 2. After excluding patients registered from 2006 and 2010 (N=568 [7.4%]) given a single‐center database during the period or those without LVEF data at baseline (N=196 [2.6%]), we enrolled the remaining participants (N=6877 [90.0%]) in the present study. We then categorized them into 5 groups based on the enrollment (admission) year: group 1 (2011–2012); group 2 (2013–2014); group 3 (2015–2017); group 4 (2018–2019); and group 5 (2020–2021) (Figure 1).

Variables

An LVEF during the index hospitalization was assessed via echocardiography using the modified Simpson's method by board‐certified physicians or physiology technicians. The New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional classification was assessed at the time of admission and discharge by the attending cardiologists. An estimated glomerular filtration rate was calculated using the Modification of Diet in Renal Disease Equation for Japanese Patients, proposed by the Japanese Society of Nephrology. 19

The comorbidities, hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidemia, atrial fibrillation, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, prior stroke, malignant tumor, dementia, and smoking, were defined as having a history of each diagnosis, and the data were collected by reviewing the medical chart. In particular, anemia was defined as hemoglobin level at admission <13 g/dL for men and <12 g/dL for women, and chronic kidney disease was defined as estimated glomerular filtration rate at admission <60 mL/min per 1.73 m2. Furthermore, physical frailty and malnutrition were defined as clinical frailty scale score ≥5 20 and Geriatric Nutritional Risk Index <82 at the time of discharge, 21 respectively. When calculating the number of comorbidities (from 0 to 10), we excluded malignant tumor, dementia, and physical frailty, which were not collected until 2017, from the analysis. We also calculated the Get With The Guidelines‐Heart Failure risk score 22 at admission, which was validated in Japanese patients with HF. 15

Clinical Outcomes

We evaluated the incidence of in‐hospital death and long‐term outcomes such as all‐cause death or HF rehospitalization across the study period. Treating physicians at each participating hospital identified HF rehospitalizations based on their clinical judgment. When analyzing long‐term outcomes, we excluded patients who died during hospitalization or had unavailable follow‐up data (N=857 [12.5%]). We also assessed the proportion of cardiovascular death (CVD) and non‐CVD among all‐cause death. HF death, sudden cardiac death, and other CVDs including acute coronary syndrome, acute aortic syndrome, intracranial hemorrhage, stroke, and pulmonary thromboembolism were considered CVDs. Non‐CVDs were all other causes of death (including unknown cause of death).

Statistical Analysis

We conducted the Cochran–Armitage test for categorical variables and the Jonckheere‐Terpstra test for continuous variables to compare patient characteristics among the five groups.

Considering a significant difference in mean age over the study period, we calculated age‐adjusted incidences of in‐hospital death, 1‐year death, and 1‐year rehospitalization using standardized mortality ratios. 2 We then divided the participants into 7 age categories (<60, 60–65, 65–70, 70–75, 75–80, 80–85, and >85 years at the time of admission) and calculated crude incidences in each age category. When analyzed the incidences of 1‐year death and rehospitalization, we excluded patients with in‐hospital death. After that, the expected number of deaths for each admission year category group (group 1 to 5) was calculated by summing the number of expected deaths achieved by applying the age‐specific expected mortality derived from the whole cohort to the age distribution of each group. We then achieved standardized mortality ratios by dividing the total number of observed deaths by the number of expected deaths for each group and conducted the Cochran–Armitage test to assess the trends in the incidences.

The incidence of all‐cause death was also estimated using the Kaplan–Meier survival function and compared among the 5 groups stratified by LVEF using the log‐rank trend test. Each patient was censored at the occurrence of the outcome of interest or the time of lost follow‐up. A multivariable Cox‐proportional hazard analysis was performed to compare the risk for all‐cause death following discharge among the 5 groups, with patients admitted in 2011 to 2012 serving as a reference group. We assessed the proportional hazards assumption for each of the following variable via calculating the Schoenfeld residuals: age, sex, NYHA functional classification at discharge ≥III, and comorbidities (including atrial fibrillation, hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidemia, anemia, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, current smoking, and malnutrition). We identified that the assumptions were not met for age (as a continuous variable) in the overall population and the non‐HFrEF group, as well as the presence of atrial fibrillation (as a categorical variable) in the HFrEF group (all with P values <0.05). Thus, we recategorized patients as age ≥75 or age <75 years. Furthermore, we conducted stratified Cox regression analyses based on the presence or absence of atrial fibrillation in the analysis for the HFrEF group. We handled the missing values of the variables as follows: the NYHA functional class was imputed ≥class III, and each comorbidity was imputed as none.

Next, we conducted sex‐stratified analyses regarding the temporal trends in in‐hospital and 1‐year mortality rate. Finally, we divided patients into 2 groups based on the LVEF status during admission: HF with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF [LVEF<40%]) and non‐HFrEF (LVEF ≥40%; HF with mildly reduced ejection fraction [HFmrEF] and HF with preserved ejection fraction [HFpEF]). 23 We also assessed the temporal trends in the incidence of death and rehospitalization and the Cox‐proportional hazard analysis in each LVEF group.

Statistical significance was set at P<0.05. All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.2.2; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Results

Patient Characteristics at Admission

In the overall cohort (N=6877), the mean age (SD) was 75.6 (13.3) years, and 2921 (42.5%) had HFrEF (Table S1). The participants were divided into 5 groups based on enrollment year: (1) 2011 to 2012 (N=843 [12.3%]); (2) 2013 to 2014 (N=1285 [18.7%]); (3) 2015 to 2017 (N=1251 [18.2%]); (4) 2018 to 2019 (N=1678 [24.4%]); and (5) 2020 to 2021 (N=1820 [26.4%]) (Figure 1). The temporal trends in patient characteristics are presented in Table 1. The mean age increased significantly over time (from 75.2 [SD, 12.5] years in 2011–2012 to 76.4 [SD, 13.5] years in 2020–2021, P for trend<0.001; Table 1). The mean LVEF and proportion of HFrEF remained unchanged (Figure 2A). The proportion of ischemic heart disease decreased significantly over time (P for trend<0.001). The proportion of chronic kidney disease increased (from 67.0% to 74.0%, P for trend<0.001), whereas the proportion of current smoking markedly decreased (from 44.8% to 10.9%, P for trend<0.001). The proportion of NYHA functional classification at admission ≥III increased significantly over time (from 75.7% to 89.1%, P for trend<0.001), and the Get With The Guidelines risk score also increased.

Inpatient Management

The management of patients with acute HF during hospitalization was demonstrated in Table 2. The use of acute vasodilation therapy, such as nitroglycerin or carperitide, showed a marked decrease over time (from 69.0% to 29.5%, P for trend<0.001). The use of noninvasive positive pressure ventilation remained unchanged until the year 2019 but experienced a decline in the years of 2020 and 2021. There were no significant differences in the use of mechanical circulatory support devices and the prevalence of patients receiving blood transfusion during the study period. Regarding the transfusion, ≈40% of the cases requiring transfusion were attributed to decreases in hemoglobin levels due to invasive therapies, including percutaneous coronary intervention, coronary artery bypass graft, and valve replacement (Table S2). The median duration of hospital stays slightly increased (from 15 to 17 days, P for trend<0.001; Figure 2B). Furthermore, the proportion of the NYHA functional classification ≥III at discharge has decreased significantly over time (from 29.4% to 6.5%, P for trend<0.001; Table 1).

Medications at Discharge

Overall, there was no significant difference in the prescription rate of beta blockers over the study period (P for trend=0.09). The prescription rate of angiotensin‐converting enzyme inhibitors or angiotensin II receptor blockers decreased, whereas the proportional use of mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists increased over time. The prescription rate of loop diuretics increased significantly over time, and the rate of thiazide diuretics decreased.

Among patients with HFrEF, the prescription rates of beta blockers and angiotensin‐converting enzyme inhibitors or angiotensin II receptor blockers remained unchanged (P for trend 0.38 and 0.20, respectively). Conversely, the use of mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists increased significantly over time (from 44.3% in 2011–2012 to 60.2% in 2020–2021, P for trend<0.001; Figure 2C). Furthermore, following the approval for the use of sodium‐glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors, their prescription rate markedly increased.

Clinical Outcomes

In this study, the median follow‐up days was 582 (interquartile range: 267–843 days). The overall crude in‐hospital mortality and 1‐year mortality were 4.3% (N=6877) and 11.2% (N=6020), respectively. The age‐adjusted in‐hospital mortality showed no significant difference over time (from 2.7% in 2011–2012 to 3.4% in 2020–2021, P for trend=0.15; Figure 3A). In contrast, age‐adjusted 1‐year mortality significantly decreased (from 12.7% to 9.2%, P for trend=0.016), and this trend was persistent in the analysis with log‐rank test for trend (P=0.038; Figure S2). Furthermore, in sex‐stratified analyses, these trends were almost robust: (1) age‐adjusted in‐hospital mortality: men, from 2.5% to 3.1%, P for trend=0.45 and women, from 3.0% to 4.4%, P for trend=0.34 (Figure S3); and (2) age‐adjusted 1‐year mortality men, from 13.1% to 9.9%, P for trend=0.033 and women, from 13.0% to 9.1%, P for trend=0.10 (Figure S4).

Overall (A), heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) (B), and non‐HFrEF (C).

When stratified by LVEF status, the decreasing trend in mortality after discharge was persistent in patients with HFrEF (age‐adjusted 1‐year mortality: from 18.0% to 9.3%, P for trend<0.001; Figure 3B; log‐rank test for trend P<0.001; Figure S5) but not those in the non‐HFrEF group (LVEF>40%, from 9.2% to 9.5%, P for trend=0.79; Figure 3C; log‐rank test for trend P=0.30; Figure S6).

A Cox regression analysis revealed that the risk of all‐cause death after discharge was comparable compared with patients enrolled in 2011 to 2012 (Figure 4A). However, even after multivariable adjustment, the risks were significantly lower in patients enrolled in 2018 to 2019 or 2020 to 2021 compared with those enrolled in 2011 to 2012 among patients with HFrEF (Figure 4B) but not among those with non‐HFrEF (Figure 4C). In addition, the proportion of CVD among all‐cause death was consistently higher in patients with HFrEF (Figure S7) compared with those with non‐HFrEF (Figure S8), regardless of the enrollment year.

With respect to HF rehospitalization, the incidence within 1‐year after discharge has unchanged over the study period (age adjusted: from 21.2% to 18.5%, P for trend=0.91; Figure S9). This trend persisted in both patients with HFrEF (P for trend=0.53) and those with non‐HFrEF (P for trend=0.76; Figure S9).

Discussion

This study demonstrated that the temporal trends in clinical management and outcomes among patients hospitalized for HF over an 11‐year period. Our major findings are as follows: first, there has been a progressive aging of patients with HF during the study period; second, age‐adjusted in‐hospital mortality has remained stable; third, there has been an upward trend in the use of guideline‐directed medical therapy, particularly mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists, among patients with HFrEF; and finally, the age‐adjusted 1‐year mortality decreased predominantly in patients with HFrEF but not in patients without HFrEF.

We found a steady increase in the aging population among patients hospitalized for HF. However, contrary to expectations, the proportion of women or patients with HFpEF showed little change over the study period. This may reflect the limited enrollment of the WET‐HF registry in urban tertiary care hospitals, potentially indicating that further aging has occurred in Japan nationwide, including rural areas, considering Japan's status as one of the countries with the most rapidly aging population in the world. Furthermore, even in the most recent data from patients enrolled in 2020 and 2021, over one third of patients exhibited physical frailty, with a projected increasing trend in the future. Although the importance of the interaction between HF and frailty has been emphasized in recent years, 24 , 25 adequate interventions have not yet been identified, and further accumulation of knowledge is warranted. On the other hand, there has been a decreasing trend in the proportion of patients with comorbidities such as hypertension and smoking, suggesting a potential improvement in the management of concurrent diseases. Actually, the proportion of ischemic heart disease or hypertensive heart disease significantly decreased over time, whereas on the other hand, the proportion of amyloidosis significantly increased, suggesting a shift in the disease profile.

Regarding inpatients managements, we found that a significant decrease in the use of vasodilators, which had been commonly employed for patients with acute HF in Japan, consistent with previous trend analyses of HF in Japan. 2 The use of noninvasive positive pressure ventilation for respiratory management has decreased in patients from 2020 to 2021, which may be attributed to the impact of the COVID‐19 pandemic. 26 Furthermore, we found high prevalence of patients receiving transfusion during admission compared with that in Western countries. 10 One potential factor related to this discrepancy could be the inclusion of patients who received transfusions in the analysis. There appears to be no obvious deviation from the threshold of transfusion based on hemoglobin data at the time of transfusion (Table S2). Nevertheless, given the limited availability of data regarding the optimal transfusion strategy for patients with HF, 27 the management of patients with HF incorporating severe anemia is still under discussion. Further investigations are needed to elucidate the clinical issue.

Our study showed a slight increasing trend in length of hospital stay, which differs from previous research from Japan. 2 Considering that the Get With The Guidelines risk score has increased, it is suggested that hospitalized patients have become more severe over time. This could lead to a slight increasing trend in length of hospital stay in this study. Additionally, approximately one‐third of patients underwent right heart catheterizations during the latter half of the study period, which may also be related to the severity of hospitalized patients. A previous report from the United States has shown a resurgence in the use of right heart catheterization after a period of declining rates. 28 The authors described that the findings may indicate increasing use of advanced HF therapies and the preparatory hemodynamic evaluations. Taken together, although we believe that many of the right heart catheterizations performed in our study were warranted for appropriate disease assessment, our findings also highlight the importance of regularly assessing the appropriateness of right heart catheterization use. Nonetheless, the age‐adjusted in‐hospital mortality rate has remained relatively stable. There has been a decrease in the prevalence of NYHA functional class ≥III and the levels of BNP and NT‐proBNP at discharge over time, suggesting an improvement in the quality of comprehensive management for patients with acute HF, particularly in terms of decongestion.

Inconsistent with a previous time‐trend analysis conducted in the United States between 1990 and 2009, 14 we found that 1‐year mortality after discharge in patients with HFrEF showed a temporal decrease. The trend was robust in patients with HFrEF. Even after multivariable adjustment, the risk of all‐cause mortality was lower in patients enrolled in the latter half of the study period. The findings may be attributed to influences of patient characteristics that could not be adjusted or improvements in managements for them in chronic phase. The prescription rate of mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists showed a clear increasing trend among them. Given the expected future increase in prescription rates of angiotensin receptor neprilysin inhibitor and sodium‐glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors, it is expected that optimization of pharmacological therapy is progressing. However, the prescription rates are still suboptimal, indicating the need for further efforts in optimization of guideline‐directed medical therapy. 29 Furthermore, the low rate in the use of implantable cardioverter‐defibrillators (ICDs) over time should be also noted. The following factors may contribute to the low rate of ICD implantation in this cohort. First, it is known that the rate of ICD implantation in patients with HFrEF is lower in Japan compared with Western countries. 30 , 31 Second, our registry data have indicated a decrease in the proportion of patients having a history of HF admission over time. This may suggest a shift toward a higher proportion of de novo HF cases, potentially affecting the rate of ICD implantation at the time of hospitalization. Nevertheless, the suboptimal use of ICDs also remains a challenge in the managements for them. On the other hand, unlike HFrEF, in patients with non‐HFrEF (HF with mildly reduced ejection fraction and HFpEF), there was little change in 1‐year mortality. With the aging population, it is expected that the number of patients with HFpEF will increase, highlighting that the challenges in clinical management for them remain a serious concern. Considering the incidence of HF rehospitalization remained consistently high over time, evidence supporting the use of sodium‐glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors for reducing rehospitalization in these patients is promising. 32 , 33 Furthermore, the needs of individualized and multifactorial interventions have been underscored to address the heterogeneity pathophysiology in HFpEF. 34 , 35 , 36

Study Limitations

Our research had several limitations. First, as mentioned previously, the WET‐HF registry had limited enrollment in urban tertiary care hospitals in Japan, potentially leading to a sample bias. This may affect the generalizability of the findings to other populations, including rural areas. Additionally, participants in the present study were primarily Japanese patients. Thus, the present findings may not also apply to patients in Western countries. Second, as this was an observational study, the clinical management for patients with HF varied according to the attending physicians' discretion, which may have influenced our results. Third, we were unable to conduct detailed analyses on the influences of the conditions under COVID‐19 pandemic, although the incidence of COVID‐19 cases was substantially lower in Japan compared with the United States and Europe. 37 Fourth, we could not assess the prevalence of iron deficiency, an important clinical factor in HF. Consequently, discussions regarding the management of patients with anemia have been limited. Finally, although we observed temporal decreases in 1‐year mortality, potential confounding factors may affect the interpretation of results. However, we confirmed a lower risk of all‐cause death in patients with HFrEF enrolled in the latter half of the study period, even after adjusting for age, sex, and comorbidities.

Conclusions

The present study revealed that hospitalized patients for HF in Japan have been aging over the past decade. Their long‐term outcomes after discharge have improved predominantly because of decreased mortality in patients with HFrEF. The findings from this comprehensive trend analysis using up‐to‐date data would significantly contribute to the current understanding and updating of knowledge regarding clinical management and outcomes in patients with HF, providing valuable insights for optimizing patient care in this population. Further research is needed to optimize care for patients with HF, irrespective of their phenotype.

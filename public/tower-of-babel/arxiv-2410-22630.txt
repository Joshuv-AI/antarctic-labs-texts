Multipartite quantum states over time from two fundamental assumptions
Seok Hyung Lie, James Fullwood

Abstract
The theory of quantum states over time extends the density operator formalism into the temporal domain, providing a unified of treatment of timelike and spacelike separated systems in quantum theory. Although recent results have characterized quantum states over time involving two timelike separated systems, it remains unclear how to consistently extend the notion of quantum states over time to multipartite temporal scenarios, such as those considered in studies of Leggett-Garg inequalities. In this Letter, we show that two simple assumptions uniquely single out the Markovian multipartite extension of bipartite quantum states over time, namely, linearity in the initial state and a quantum analog of conditionability for multipartite probability distributions. As a direct consequence of our result, we establish a canonical correspondence between multipartite QSOTs and Kirkwood-Dirac type quasiprobability distributions, which we show opens up the possibility of experimentally verifying the temporal correlations encoded in QSOTs via the recent experimental technique of simulating quasiprobability known as quantum snapshotting .

Introduction
—
In general relativity, Lorentzian manifolds provide a single mathematical formalism with which to treat both space and time. In quantum theory however, there exists a disparity in the mathematical formalisms one uses to investigate correlations across space on the one hand, and correlations across time on the other. In particular, while correlations across regions of space are encoded by multipartite density operators, temporal correlations arise out of interactions described by quantum channels, yielding multipartite probability distributions which may not in general be described by the density operator formalism. Is such a disparity between the ways in which quantum theory treats space and time merely an artifact of the historical development of quantum theory, or is it a reflection of some fundamental, intrinsic property of nature which forces quantum theory to be incompatible with the notion of spacetime?

Such a question was formally addressed in Ref.
[
21
]
, where the notion of a
quantum state over time
(QSOT) was introduced to give a more balanced treatment of space and time in quantum theory. A QSOT was first proposed as a bipartite hermitian operator of unit trace associated with a two-time process corresponding to a single run of a quantum channel, thus providing a spatiotemporal analog of a bipartite density operator. Contrary to density operators however, QSOTs are not positive in general, as the negative eigenvalues of a QSOT serve as a witness to temporal correlations which have no spatial analog.

In Ref.
[
15
]
, a construction of QSOTs was established in accordance with desiderata put forth in Ref.
[
21
]
, and the operational significance of such QSOTs in terms of two-time correlation functions was recently addressed in Ref.
[
16
]
. For systems of qubits, bipartite QSOTs have been shown to encompass the construction of pseudo-density matrices
[
14
,
28
,
21
]
, which have found applications to a wide variety of topics including quantum channel capacity and the black hole information problem
[
30
,
31
,
22
,
44
,
39
,
50
,
48
]
. The QSOT formalism has also been utilized in Ref.
[
37
]
to formulate a quantum Bayes’ rule, leading to a notion of operational reversibility for open quantum systems
[
38
]
. Recently the QSOT formalism has also been utilized in the study of temporal entanglement
[
32
]
and the quantum transport problem
[
20
]
.

Although bipartite QSOTs have indeed provided a natural extension of the density operator formalism into the temporal domain, there are various issues in regards to unambiguously generalizing QSOTs to a multipartite setting
[
25
]
, leading some to believe that a well-defined multipartite construction of QSOTs is not possible
[
42
]
. In this Letter, we take a cue from the derivation of special relativity, and derive an explicit expression for multi-partite QSOTs based on two simple assumptions. The first assumption is that a QSOT should depend linearly on the initial state of a dynamical process, in accordance not only with the convex structure of quantum state spaces, but also with the statistical behavior of dynamical quantities such as work and heat. The second and final assumption is motivated by the fact that equations of motion should be uniquely determined by initial conditions subject to dynamical laws, which we formulate in terms of a conditionability condition for QSOTs.

While our assumptions are quite simple, we provide further justification for them by showing how the QSOTs derived from our assumptions yield a spatiotemporal generalization of a quantum Markov chain, a notion which was previously only defined in terms of a (spacelike) density operator
[
1
,
10
,
13
,
45
]
. Moreover, as a consequence of our results, we establish a canonical correspondence between QSOTs and multipartite Kirkwood-Dirac distributions associated with POVMs measured over time, which we show admits an operational interpretation in terms of a recently established technique for measuring quantum temporal correlations referred to as
quantum snapshotting

[
46
]
. Finally, we conclude by showing how the QSOTs singled out by our assumptions encode all relevant correlations associated with a Leggett-Garg scenario. Interestingly, we find that QSOTs yield a purely quantum description of Leggett-Garg scenarios without invoking any notion of state collapse.

Preliminaries
—
Given finite-dimensional quantum systems
A_{0},\ldots,A_{n}
, the associated joint system with Hilbert space
\bigotimes_{i=0}^{n}\mathcal{H}_{A_{i}}
will be denoted by
A_{0}\cdots A_{n}
. We will mathematically identify the system
A_{i}
with the algebra of linear operators on
\mathcal{H}_{A_{i}}
, and a completely positive, trace-preserving linear map
\mathcal{E}_{i}:A_{i-1}\to A_{i}
will be referred to as a
quantum channel
. An
n
-tuple
(\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n})
of quantum channels
\mathcal{E}_{i}:A_{i-1}\to A_{i}
will be referred to as an
n
-chain
from
A_{0}
to
A_{n}
, and a density operator
\rho
of the initial system
A_{0}
will be referred to as an
initial state
for the
n
-chain
(\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n})
. The truncations of an
n
-chain
\bm{\mathcal{E}}=(\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{E}_{n}),
from below and above will be denoted by

\displaystyle\underline{\bm{\mathcal{E}}}:=(\mathcal{E}_{2},\mathcal{E}_{3},\dots,\mathcal{E}_{n})\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{\bm{\mathcal{E}}}:=(\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{E}_{n-1})\,.

The following definition yields a quantum analog of the multipartite distribution associated with a sequence
X_{0},\ldots,X_{n}
of classical random variables.

Definition 1

(Quantum state over time)
.

A
\star
-product
(or
spatiotemporal product
) is a binary operation that maps every
n
-chain
\bm{\mathcal{E}}
from
A_{0}
to
A_{n}
with initial state
\rho
to an operator
\bm{\mathcal{E}}\star\rho
on
\mathcal{H}_{A_{0}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{H}_{A_{n}}
such that

\displaystyle\Tr_{A_{0}}[\bm{\mathcal{E}}\star\rho]=\underline{\bm{\mathcal{E}}}\star\mathcal{E}_{1}(\rho)\quad\text{and}\quad\Tr_{A_{n}}[\bm{\mathcal{E}}\star\rho]=\overline{\bm{\mathcal{E}}}\star\rho\,.

In such a case,
\bm{\mathcal{E}}\star\rho
is said to be the
quantum state over time
associated with
n
-chain
\bm{\mathcal{E}}
and the initial state
\rho
.

For
n=1
, it follows that
\overline{\bm{\mathcal{E}}}
and
\underline{\bm{\mathcal{E}}}
are simply the empty chain, so that in such a case we set
\underline{\bm{\mathcal{E}}}\star\mathcal{E}_{1}(\rho)=\mathcal{E}_{1}(\rho)
and
\overline{\bm{\mathcal{E}}}\star\rho=\rho
in Definition
1
. The restriction of a
\star
-product to
n
-chains for a fixed
n
will be referred to as a
\star
-product on
n
-chains
. While a
\star
-product on 1-chains was referred to as a “QSOT function” in Refs.
[
37
,
27
,
36
]
, we use the
\star
-product terminology to be more consistent with the notation
\bm{\mathcal{E}}\star\rho
.

Bipartite QSOTs
—
In their formulation of quantum theory as a causally neutral theory of Bayesian inference
[
25
]
, Leifer and Spekkens introduced the spatiotemporal product on
1
-chains
\star_{\text{LS}}
given by

\mathcal{E}\star_{\text{LS}}\rho=(\sqrt{\rho}_{A}\otimes\mathds{1}_{B})\mathscr{J}[\mathcal{E}](\sqrt{\rho}_{A}\otimes\mathds{1}_{B})\,,

where
\mathscr{J}[\mathcal{E}]=\sum_{i,j}|i\rangle\langle j|_{A}\otimes\mathcal{E}(|j\rangle\langle i|)_{B}
is the
Jamiołkowski matrix
of a quantum channel
\mathcal{E}:A\to B
(which is not to be confused with the Choi matrix, which differs from the Jamiołkowski matrix by a partial transpose). While the LS-product
\star_{\text{LS}}
has appeared extensively in the literature on quantum Bayesian inference
[
7
,
6
,
5
,
4
]
, there are significant drawbacks with the construction. In particular, the LS-product is not linear in
\rho
, and perhaps most importantly, it does not extend in any natural way to multipartite temporal scenarios.

While there are indeed various state-linear
\star
-products on 1-chains which admit natural extensions to multipartite scenarios, such as the left and right products
\star_{\text{L}}
and
\star_{\text{R}}
given by
\mathcal{E}\star_{\text{L}}\rho=(\rho\otimes\mathds{1}_{B})\mathscr{J}[\mathcal{E}]
and
\mathcal{E}\star_{\text{R}}\rho=\mathscr{J}[\mathcal{E}](\rho\otimes\mathds{1}_{B})
, such constructions are based on mathematical simplicity, and are not derived from physical principles. It had then been an open problem to derive a
\star
-product from simple physical assumptions
[
21
]
, and such a derivation was achieved in Refs.
[
27
,
36
]
. Moreover, while the sets of axioms employed in Refs.
[
27
,
36
]
were distinct, the resulting
\star
-product was the same.

The
\star
-product on 1-chains uniquely derived from physical principles was referred to as the
Fullwood-Parzygnat
(FP)-product in Ref.
[
27
]
, which is given by

\mathcal{E}\star_{\text{FP}}\rho=\frac{1}{2}\Big\{\rho_{A}\otimes\mathds{1}_{B},\mathscr{J}[\mathcal{E}]\Big\}

(1)

for all states
\rho
on
A
and quantum channels
\mathcal{E}:A\to B
, where
\{\cdot\,,\cdot\}
denotes the anti-commutator. While such bipartite constructions of QSOTs have proven useful for treating space and time on an equal footing, it turns out that there are a multitude of ways in which one may extend a
\star
-product on
1
-chains to
n
-chains
[
19
,
18
]
. In fact, in the case of the FP-product, we show in Appendix
A
that the number of
\star
-products on
n
-chains which reduce to
\star_{\text{FP}}
on 1-chains grows exponentially with
n
. This is highly undesirable: Imagine how confusing it could have been if there were
2^{n}
different constructions of the density operator for
n
-partite systems. As such, it would be desirable if we could find simple assumptions which single out a unique extension of quantum states over time to the multipartite setting, without imposing one by fiat.

Multipartite extension of QSOTs
—
The main result of this Letter is that two fundamental assumptions imply that
\star
-product is completely determined by its restriction to
1
-chains, thus yielding a unique multi-partite extension of bipartite QSOTs. Our first assumption is simply the requirement that a
\star
-product should be consistent with statistical mixtures: if there is uncertainty about the initial state preparation, this uncertainty should carry over to the overall dynamics to the same degree. Mathematically, this means that
\bm{\mathcal{E}}\star\rho
is convex-linear in
\rho
, i.e., for every
n
-chain
\bm{\mathcal{E}}
, we assume
\bm{\mathcal{E}}\star(\sum_{i}p_{i}\rho^{i})=\sum_{i}p_{i}(\bm{\mathcal{E}}\star\rho^{i})
. This convex-linearity in density operator can be extended to linearity in general operator with the standard procedure
[
47
,
26
]
, and thus will be referred to as
state-linearity
. The second assumption is motivated from the fact that it is customary to describe the evolution of a physical system in terms of initial conditions subject to dynamical laws. In the context of classical stochastic dynamics associated with a with a sequence of random variables
X_{0},\dots,X_{n}
, such a principle manifests itself at the level of the associated joint distribution
P(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})
via the equation

P(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})=P(x_{0})\cdot P(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}|x_{0})\,,

(2)

where
P(x_{0})
is viewed as the initial state of the process. We refer to this property of multipartite probability distributions as
conditionability
, which we now generalize to the setting of QSOTs.

Definition 2

(Conditionability)
.

A
\star
-product is said to be
conditionable
if and only if for every state
\rho
on
A_{0}
, there exists a linear map
\Theta_{\rho}:A_{0}\to A_{0}
such that for every
n
-chain
\bm{\mathcal{E}}
from
A_{0}
to
A_{n}
,

\bm{\mathcal{E}}\star\rho=(\Theta_{\rho}\otimes\text{id}_{A_{1}\cdots A_{n}})(\bm{\mathcal{E}}\star\mathds{1}_{A_{0}})\,.

(3)

The appearance of the map
\Theta_{\rho}
on the RHS of equation (
3
) is an operator analog of being able to factor out the contribution of a prior
P(x_{0})
from a multipartite distribution as in (
2
), while the operator
\bm{\mathcal{E}}\star\mathds{1}_{A_{0}}
is a quantum analog of the conditional distribution
P(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}|x_{0})
.

Before stating our main result we set some notation in place. For a state-linear
\star
-product and an arbitrary quantum channel
\mathcal{E}
,
\mathcal{E}\,\star
can be understood as a linear map
\rho\mapsto\mathcal{E}\,\star\,\rho
. Hence, given an
n
-chain
(\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n})
, after omitting the tensor product with the identity channels,
\mathcal{E}_{k}\star((\mathcal{E}_{1}\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{k-1})\star\rho)
is well-defined for any
2\leq k\leq n
.

Theorem 1

(Unique multi-partite extension of QSOTs)
.

A
\star
-product which is state-linear and conditionable is uniquely determined by its restriction to 1-chains. In particular, for every
n
-chain
\bm{\mathcal{E}}=(\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n})
with initial state
\rho
,

\bm{\mathcal{E}}\star\rho=\mathcal{E}_{n}\star(\mathcal{E}_{n-1}\star(\cdots\star(\mathcal{E}_{1}\star\rho)))\,.

(4)

The proof of Theorem
1
is given in the Supplementary Material
[
26
]
. Due to its structure, the canonical extension of the QSOT characterized in Theorem
1
will be referred to as the
Markovian extension
, the meaning of which will be elaborated upon later. (See FIG.
1
(a)).
In what follows, we explore some further implications of Theorem
1
.

Multipartite KD distributions via QSOTs
—
In quantum theory, incompatible observables cannot be measured simultaneously, as evidenced by the non-existence of well-defined joint probability distributions for their measurement outcomes. In 1933 and 1945 respectively, Kirkwood
[
23
]
and Dirac
[
11
]
independently proposed a way to circumvent this issue by allowing for complex-valued probabilities, which are now commonly referred to as
quasi
probability distributions. This construction, known today as the
Kirkwood-Dirac
(KD) distribution, plays a fundamental role in the study of non-classical features of quantum temporal correlations, especially in the context of quantum thermodynamics
[
29
,
2
]
. In this section, we establish a precise correspondence between
\star
-products and classes of quasiprobability distributions, and then use Theorem
1
to uniquely extend the latter to the multipartite setting.

Let
\{M_{i}\}\subset A
and
\{N_{j}\}\subset B
be POVMs which are to be measured at times
t_{1}
and
t_{2}>t_{1}
. It then follows that for every
\star
-product on 1-chains the elements
Q_{AB}(i,j)\in\mathbb{C}
given by

Q_{AB}(i,j)=\Tr[\mathcal{E}\star\rho\,(M_{i}\otimes N_{j})\Big]

(5)

form a quasiprobability distribution for every channel
\mathcal{E}:A\to B
with initial state
\rho
on
A
. The marginals
Q_{A}(i)=\sum_{j}Q_{AB}(i,j)
and
Q_{B}(j)=\sum_{i}Q_{AB}(i,j)
are probability distributions given by
Q_{A}(i)=\Tr[\rho\hskip 0.56905ptM_{i}]\quad\text{and}\quad Q_{B}(j)=\Tr[\mathcal{E}(\rho)N_{j}]\,
, which are the standard probabilities associated with the Born rule. It turns out that Eq. (
5
) can be used to establish an explicit correspondence between bipartite QSOTs and bipartite quasiprobability distributions, and this correspondence can be easily generalized to the
n
-step setting (see Appendix
B
for more detail). We note that under such a correspondence, the state-linear
\star
-products
\star_{\text{L}}
and
\star_{\text{FP}}
correspond to the Kirkwood-Dirac and Margenau-Hill distributions, respectively.

If a
\star
-product is state-linear, then it follows from the no-go theorem for quasiprobability distributions appearing in Ref.
[
29
]
that the non-positivity of generic
Q_{AB}(i,j)
as given by (
5
) is inevitable. This is a reflection of the fact that the QSOT
\mathcal{E}\star\rho
will generally admit negative eigenvalues, thus the non-positivity of
\mathcal{E}\star\rho
is an indicator of non-classicality for the temporal correlations established by
\mathcal{E}
and
\rho
.

An immediate implication of Theorem
1
is that quasiprobability distributions as given by (
5
) canonically extend to an arbitrary number of POVMs measured over time, provided that the associated
\star
-product is conditionable and state-linear
1
1

1

In particular, we note that Theorem
1
does not provide a unique extension of the Leifer-Spekkens distribution since
\star_{\text{LS}}
is not state-linear
. We also note that while previous works have addressed the issue of extending quasiprobability distributions to multipartite scenarios
[
49
,
3
]
, they were primarily formal and not derived from basic assumptions.

The fact that QSOTs encode quasiprobability distributions implies that the many uses of the Kirkwood-Dirac and Margenau-Hill distributions in quantum chaos, quantum thermodynamics, and quantum metrology
[
2
]
carry over directly to the QSOT. Furthermore, Theorem
1
provides a firm mathematical foundation for studies utilizing certain multipartite KD distributions. For example, in post-selected quantum metrology
[
3
]
, advantages beyond the Heisenberg limit arise only when the tripartite KD distribution associated with the scheme exhibits negativity. Finding necessary conditions for such advantages remains an open problem, and we expect that operator properties of QSOTs (such as their eigenvalues) will provide novel insights in this direction.

Markovianity and quantum snapshotting
—
In classical probability, a sequence of random variables
X_{0},\ldots,X_{n}
is said to form a
Markov chain
if the associated conditional distribution
P(x_{i}|x_{i-1},\ldots,x_{0})
satisfies

P(x_{i}|x_{i-1},\ldots,x_{0})=P(x_{i}|x_{i-1})\qquad 1<i\leq n\,.

(6)

For a quantum system evolving under an
n
-chain
(\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n})
, the associated quasiprobabilities as established in the previous section do not satisfy the Markovian condition (
6
) (see Appendix
C
for an explicit example). Therefore, at the level of quasiprobabilities the Markovianity condition in the classical sense fails, reflecting the fact that a measurement at time
t_{i}
may have causal influence on measurements at times
t>t_{i+1}
.

On the other hand, the formula

(\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2})\star\rho=\mathcal{E}_{2}\star(\mathcal{E}_{1}\star\rho)

as given by Eq. (
4
) of Theorem
1
in the case of 2-chains is more reminiscent of the classical expression
P(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})=P(x_{2}|x_{1})P(x_{1}|x_{0})P(x_{0})
associated with a Markov chain
X_{0}\to X_{1}\to X_{2}
. Moreover, we can see that the 3-time QSOT
(\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2})\star\rho
is obtained from the 2-time QSOT
\mathcal{E}_{1}\star\rho
by the mapping
\mathcal{E}_{2}\,\star
acting on
A_{1}
alone, which is consistent with the definition of a quantum Markov chain of Refs.
[
1
,
10
,
13
,
45
]
for spatial correlations. Such an observation suggests that in the temporal domain, quantum Markovianity is a property naturally formulated at the level of QSOTs rather than at the level of quasiprobabilities, which justifies referring to Eq. (
4
) of Theorem
1
as the Markovian extension of bipartite QSOTs.
As such, an immediate corollary of Theorem
1
is that one can formulate quantum Markov chains directly at the level of the quantum state, covering both spatial and temporal cases, contrary to higher-order constructions such as process matrices
[
33
,
34
]
, process tensors
[
41
,
40
]
, or quantum combs
[
9
]
.

The Markovian nature of multipartite QSOTs is also crucial for the possibility of measuring the expectation values encoded by a QSOT by
quantum snapshotting
, which is a recently proposed technique for experimental verification of quasiprobabilities
[
46
]
. In particular, consider the expectation value
\langle\mathscr{O}_{1},\ldots,\mathscr{O}_{n}\rangle
of observables
\mathscr{O}_{i}
associated with the
i
-th step in an
n
-chain QSOT
(\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{E}_{n})\star\rho
, calculated as
\Tr[(\mathscr{O}_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathscr{O}_{n})(\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{E}_{n})\star\rho]
. As the associated QSOT admits the iterative decomposition as in (
4
), we can express
\langle\mathscr{O}_{0},\dots,\mathscr{O}_{n}\rangle
as

\Tr[\mathscr{O}_{n}(\mathcal{E}_{n}\circ\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{O}_{n-1}}\circ\cdots\circ\mathcal{E}_{1}\circ\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{O}_{0}})(\rho)]\,,

(7)

where
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{O}}=\sum_{i}\gamma_{i}(\mathscr{O})\mathcal{I}_{i}
is a linear map representing the sampling process of the observable
\mathscr{O}
, which can be expressed as a linear combination of physically implementable quantum instruments
\quantity{\mcal{I}_i}
such that
\sum_{i}\mathcal{I}_{i}
is a quantum channel, with complex coefficients
\gamma_{i}(\mathscr{O})
processed classically
[
46
]
(see Appendix
D
for further details).

Notice that, to obtain the expectation value (
7
), one can apply the channels
\mathcal{E}_{i}
and (the physical implementation of) the snapshotting processes
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{O}_{i}}
in an alternating manner, due to the fact that the action of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{O}_{i}}
is localized at the
i
-th time-step in (
7
). This indeed demonstrates the significance of the Markovian extension of QSOTs, as it allows for ‘snapshotting’ in the literal sense: One does not need to retain the memory of all other time-steps to extract information of an observable at time-step
i
. By faithfully capturing Markovianity, such QSOT expansions thus enable feasible experimental verification via quantum snapshotting. (See FIG.
1
.)

A Leggett-Garg scenario
—Let
\star
be the Markovian tripartite extension of
\star_{\text{FP}}
, and consider an experiment in which a qubit in state
\rho
is to be sent through 3 devices
Q_{1}
,
Q_{2}
and
Q_{3}
in sequence, where
Q_{i}
measures the spin observable
\mathscr{O}_{i}=\vec{u}_{i}\cdot\sigma
with
\vec{u}_{1}=(1,0,0)
,
\vec{u}_{2}=(1/2,\sqrt{3}/2,0)
and
\vec{u}_{3}=(-1/2,\sqrt{3}/2,0)
. Denote the projectors onto the
\pm 1
-eigenspaces of
\mathscr{O}_{i}
by
\Pi_{i}^{\pm}
, so that
\mathscr{O}_{i}=\Pi_{i}^{+}-\Pi_{i}^{-}
and
\Pi_{i}^{+}+\Pi_{i}^{-}=\mathds{1}
. We assume that for each run of the experiment, exactly one of the devices
Q_{k}
is deactivated (alternating between
k=1,2,3
), so that each run of the experiment consists of two measurements
\mathscr{O}_{i}
followed by
\mathscr{O}_{j}
with
i<j
. We further assume that the qubit evolves trivially as it travels from device
Q_{i}
to
Q_{i+1}
, so that the QSOT associated with such a three-time scenario is
\textbf{id}^{2}\star\rho
, where
\textbf{id}^{2}:=(\text{id},\text{id})
is the identity 2-chain. Now let
C_{ij}
be the two-time expectation value associated with runs of the experiment where a measurement of
\mathscr{O}_{i}
is followed by a measurement of
\mathscr{O}_{j}
, so that

C_{ij}=P_{ij}(1,1)-P_{ij}(1,-1)-P_{ij}(-1,1)+P_{ij}(1,1)\,,

where
P_{ij}(\pm 1\,,\pm 1)=\Tr[\Pi_{i}^{\pm}\rho\,\Pi_{i}^{\pm}\Pi_{j}^{\pm}]
. If the values of the observables
\mathscr{O}_{1}
,
\mathscr{O}_{2}
and
\mathscr{O}_{3}
are each well-defined for each run of the experiment, regardless of which device
Q_{k}
is deactivated, then such a measurement scenario is consistent with the principle of
macrorealism
, which essentially means that the value of an observable is well-defined irrespective of whether or not it was actually measured. If such a measurement scenario is indeed consistent with the principle of macrorealism, and moreover, if it is possible to measure the values of
\mathscr{O}_{i}
in a non-invasive manner, then it is straightforward to show

C_{12}+C_{23}-C_{13}\leq 1\,,

(8)

which is often referred to as a
Leggett-Garg inequality

[
24
]
. However, in such a case we have
C_{12}=C_{23}=-C_{13}=1/2
(a result which is actually independent of the state
\rho

[
12
,
17
]
), so that
C_{12}+C_{23}-C_{13}=3/2>1
,
thus violating the Leggett-Garg inequality (
8
). It then follows that either the assumption of macrorealism or non-invasive measurability must be invalid (or both), implying that there does not exist a well-defined tripartite probability distribution
P(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
associated with the measurement outcomes of each run of the experiment whose marginals are the distributions
P_{ij}(x_{i},x_{j})
. On the other hand, by direct calculation we find

C_{ij}=\Tr[\textbf{id}^{2}\star\rho\,(\mathscr{O}_{i}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{j}\otimes\mathds{1})\Big]\,,

(9)

thus, the tripartite QSOT
\textbf{id}^{2}\star\rho
encodes the correlations
C_{ij}
for all
i
and
j
, despite the absence of a well-defined tripartite probability distribution associated with each run of the experiment.

Therefore, while such a Leggett-Garg scenario is inconsistent with macrorealism and non-invasive measurability, the QSOT
\textbf{id}^{2}\star\rho
nevertheless is a quantum Markov chain encoding all of the relevant correlations of the experiment, independent of which measurements were actually performed. This demonstrates that such a violation of the Leggett-Garg inequality cannot be attributed to a lack of (quantum) Markovianity. Interestingly, the quasiprobabilities
Q_{ij}(x_{i},x_{j})
associated with the QSOT
\textbf{id}^{2}\star\rho
do not coincide with the probabilities
P_{ij}(x_{i},x_{j})
coming from the state-update rule, yet the expectation values corresponding to the weighted sums of
P_{ij}(x_{i},x_{j})
and
Q_{ij}(x_{i},x_{j})
turn out to be the same. As such, taking (
9
) as a theoretical definition for temporal expectation values yields a spatiotemporal description of Leggett-Garg scenarios without any reference to state collapse.

Discussion
—
In this Letter, we have shown that multipartite QSOTs may be uniquely derived from the assumptions of linear dependence on the initial state and quantum conditionability. Such a result not only lays the foundation for a spatiotemporal extension of the density operator formalism, but also for formulating a relativistic theory of quantum information where space and time are treated on equal footing. In particular, the multipartite QSOTs singled out by our assumptions yield a dynamical notion of quantum Markov chain which naturally extends the definition for spacelike separated systems. Moreover, we have also shown how QSOTs encode quasiprobability distributions associated with measurements of POVMs over time. As quasiprobability distributions have emerged as a key tool for characterizing non-classical features of quantum temporal correlations—particularly in fields such as quantum thermodynamics, quantum chaos and metrology
[
29
]
—we expect that the QSOTs derived in this Letter will also come to play a significant role in these developments. Finally, it would be interesting to see if the assumptions employed in this Letter may also be used to derive QSOTs associated with more exotic temporal scenarios, such as non-Markovian dynamics and indefinite causal structures
[
43
,
35
]
.

Equivalence between QSOT and quasiprobability distributions

In this section, we establish the one-to-one correspondence between multipartite QSOTs and families of quasiprobability distributions. (24)

where
\Theta_{A^{\prime}}^{\ddagger}
is the linear map
\Theta^{\ddagger}
applied to system
A^{\prime}
which is a copy of
A
.

Proof.

Let
\Theta:A\to A
be a linear map, let
\mathscr{J}:\mathbb{Lin}(A,A)\to AA^{\prime}
be the Jamiołkowski isomorphism, let
\gamma:AA^{\prime}\to AA^{\prime}
be the swap isomorphism, let
\mathscr{J}^{\prime}=\gamma\circ\mathscr{J}
, and let
\Theta^{{\ddagger}}=\mathscr{J}^{-1}[\mathscr{J}^{\prime}[\Theta]]
. Then

(\Theta\otimes\text{id}_{A})({\tt SWAP}_{AA^{\prime}})=\mathscr{J}^{\prime}[\Theta]=\mathscr{J}[\Theta^{{\ddagger}}]=(\text{id}_{A}\otimes\Theta^{{\ddagger}})({\tt SWAP}_{AA^{\prime}})\,,

thus (
24
) holds. Moreover,
\Theta^{{\ddagger}}
is the unique map satisfying (
24
), since for any map
\widetilde{\Theta}^{{\ddagger}}
satisfying (
24
) it necessarily follows that
\mathscr{J}[\widetilde{\Theta}^{{\ddagger}}]=\mathscr{J}^{\prime}[\Theta]=\mathscr{J}[\Theta^{{\ddagger}}]
, which implies
\widetilde{\Theta}^{{\ddagger}}=\Theta^{{\ddagger}}
.
∎

Lemma 4
.

Conditionability
\implies
Decomposability
\implies
Broadcasting.

Proof.

Suppose
\star
is conditionable. Then for every
\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{CPTP}(A,B)
and state
\rho\in\mathfrak{S}(A)
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}\star\rho

\displaystyle=(\Theta_{\rho}\otimes\text{id}_{B})(\mathcal{E}\star\mathds{1})

since
\star
is conditionable

\displaystyle=(\Theta_{\rho}\otimes\text{id}_{B})\Big((\text{id}_{A}\otimes\Phi^{(\mathcal{E})})({\tt SWAP})\Big)

by definition of
\Phi^{(\mathcal{E})}

\displaystyle=(\Theta_{\rho}\otimes\Phi^{(\mathcal{E})})({\tt SWAP})\,,

by properties of
\otimes

thus
\star
is decomposable with process rendering map equal to the Jamiołkowski map
\Phi^{(\mathcal{E})}
.

Now suppose
\star
is decomposable, and let
\Theta_{\rho}
and
\Psi^{\mathcal{E}}
be the associated state-rendering and process-rendering maps as defined by (
23
) for every state
\rho
and for every channel
\mathcal{E}
. We then have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\Tr_{A}[\mathcal{E}\star\rho]

by definition of
\star

\displaystyle=\Tr_{A}\Big[\Big(\Theta_{\rho}\otimes\Psi^{\mathcal{E}}\Big)({\tt SWAP})\Big]

since
\star
is decomposable

\displaystyle=\Tr_{A}\Big[\Big(\text{id}_{A}\otimes\Psi^{\mathcal{E}}\Big)\Big(\text{id}_{A}\otimes\Theta_{\rho}^{{\ddagger}}\Big)({\tt SWAP})\Big]

by Lemma
3

\displaystyle=\Tr_{A}\Big[\sum_{i,j}E_{ij}\otimes\Psi^{\mathcal{E}}(\Theta_{\rho}^{{\ddagger}}(E_{ji}))\Big]

since
{\tt SWAP}=\sum_{i,j}E_{ij}\otimes E_{ji}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\Tr[E_{ij}]\Psi^{\mathcal{E}}(\Theta_{\rho}^{{\ddagger}}(E_{ji}))

by properties of partial trace

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\delta_{ij}\Psi^{\mathcal{E}}(\Theta_{\rho}^{{\ddagger}}(E_{ji}))

since
\Tr[E_{ij}]=\delta_{ij}

\displaystyle=\Psi^{\mathcal{E}}(\Theta_{\rho}^{{\ddagger}}(\mathds{1}))\,,

since
\sum_{i}E_{ii}=\mathds{1}

from which it follows that
\mathcal{E}=\Psi^{\mathcal{E}}\circ\Phi
, where
\Phi:A\to A
is the map given by
\Phi(\rho)=\Theta_{\rho}^{{\ddagger}}(\mathds{1})
. We note that the map
\Phi
is linear by item
2
of Lemma
2
. By considering the case
\mathcal{E}=\text{id}
one may conclude that
\Phi
is invertible and that
\Psi^{\text{id}}=\Phi^{-1}
, hence
\Psi^{\mathcal{E}}=\mathcal{E}\circ\Psi^{\text{id}}
. We then have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}\star\rho

\displaystyle=(\Theta_{\rho}\otimes\Psi^{\mathcal{E}})({\tt SWAP})

since
\star
is decomposable

\displaystyle=(\text{id}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{E})\left((\Theta_{\rho}\otimes\Psi^{\text{id}})({\tt SWAP})\right)

since
\Psi^{\mathcal{E}}=\mathcal{E}\circ\Psi^{\text{id}}

\displaystyle=(\text{id}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{E})(\text{id}_{A}\star\rho)\,,

since
\star
is decomposable

thus
\star
is broadcasting, as desired.
∎

Remark 2
.

It turns out that decomposability with
\Theta_{\mathds{1}}
being the identity map is in fact equivalent to conditionability:

Proof.

(\implies)
Suppose
\star
is decomposable, let
\Theta_{\rho}
and
\Psi^{\mathcal{E}}
be the associated state-rendering and process-rendering maps as defined by (
23
) for every state
\rho
and for every channel
\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{CPTP}(A,B)
, and suppose
\Theta_{\mathds{1}}=\text{id}_{A}
. We then have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}\star\rho

\displaystyle=(\Theta_{\rho}\otimes\Psi^{\mathcal{E}})({\tt SWAP})

since
\star
is decomposable

\displaystyle=(\Theta_{\rho}\otimes\text{id}_{B})\Big((\text{id}_{A}\otimes\Psi^{\mathcal{E}})({\tt SWAP})\Big)

by properties of
\otimes

\displaystyle=(\Theta_{\rho}\otimes\text{id}_{B})(\Theta_{\mathds{1}}\otimes\Psi^{\mathcal{E}})({\tt SWAP})

since
\Theta_{\mathds{1}}=\text{id}_{A}

\displaystyle=(\Theta_{\rho}\otimes\text{id}_{B})(\mathcal{E}\star\mathds{1})\,,

since
\star
is decomposable

thus
\star
is conditionable.

(\impliedby)
We have seen from the proof of Lemma
4
that conditionability implies decomposability, and that in such a case the state-rendering map
\Theta_{\rho}
defining conditionability is in fact the state-rendering map defining decomposability. As such, we just have to show that the state-rendering map associated with a conditionable spatiotemporal product is such that
\Theta_{\mathds{1}}=\text{id}_{A}
.

So suppose
\star
is conditionable, and let
\Theta_{\mathds{1}}
be the associated state-rendering map. We then have

\displaystyle(\text{id}_{A}\otimes\Phi^{(\text{id}_{A})})({\tt SWAP})

\displaystyle=\text{id}_{A}\star\mathds{1}

by equation (
15
)

\displaystyle=(\Theta_{\mathds{1}}\otimes\text{id}_{A})(\text{id}\star\mathds{1})

since
\star
is conditionable

\displaystyle=(\Theta_{\mathds{1}}\otimes\text{id}_{A})\Big((\text{id}_{A}\otimes\Phi^{(\text{id}_{A})})({\tt SWAP})\Big)

by equation (
15
)

\displaystyle=(\Theta_{\mathds{1}}\otimes\Phi^{(\text{id}_{A})})({\tt SWAP})\,.

by properties of
\otimes

By item
1
Lemma
2
the map
\Phi^{(\text{id}_{A})}
is invertible, thus
(\Theta_{\mathds{1}}\otimes\text{id}_{A})({\tt SWAP})={\tt SWAP}
. Now define the map
\mathscr{J}^{\prime}=\gamma\circ\mathscr{J}
, where
\gamma:AA^{\prime}\to AA^{\prime}
is the swap isomorphism and
\mathscr{J}:\mathbb{Lin}(A,A)\to AA^{\prime}
is the Jamiołkowski isomorphism. We then have

\mathscr{J}^{\prime}[\Theta_{\mathds{1}}]=(\Theta_{\mathds{1}}\otimes\text{id}_{A})({\tt SWAP})={\tt SWAP}=\mathscr{J}[\text{id}_{A}]\implies\Theta_{\mathds{1}}=\text{id}_{A}\,,

where the final implication follows from the fact that
\mathscr{J}^{\prime}
is an isomorphism.
∎

We now define a mapping associated with a spatiotemporal product which will is fundamental to the notion of iterativity.

Definition 5
.

Given a spatiotemporal product
\star
and a channel
\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{CPTP}(A,B)
, the
bloom
of
\mathcal{E}
with respect to
\star
is the linear map
\mathcal{E}^{\star}:A\to AB
corresponding to the unique linear extension of the map on states given by
\rho\mapsto\mathcal{E}\star\rho
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\star
be a spatiotemporal product which is broadcasting, and for every
n
-chain
(\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n})\in\mathbb{CPTP}(A_{0},\ldots,A_{n})
, let
\Psi^{(\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n})}
be the map given by

\Psi^{(\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n})}=\begin{cases}\mathcal{E}_{1}\hskip 219.08612pt\text{if $n=1$}\,,\\
\mathcal{E}_{2}^{\star}\circ\mathcal{E}_{1}\hskip 198.88492pt\text{if $n=2$}\,,\\
(\text{id}_{A_{1}\cdots A_{n-2}}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{n}^{\star})\circ\cdots\circ(\text{id}_{A_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{3}^{\star})\circ\mathcal{E}_{2}^{\star}\circ\mathcal{E}_{1}\hskip 33.28966pt\text{if $n>2$}\,.\end{cases}

(25)

Then for every
n
-chain
(\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n})\in\mathbb{CPTP}(A_{0},\ldots,A_{n})
and for every state
\rho\in\mathscr{S}(A_{0})

\Big(\text{id}_{A_{0}}\otimes\Psi^{(\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n})}\Big)\Big(\text{id}_{A_{0}}\star\rho\Big)=\Big((\text{id}_{A_{0}\cdots A_{n-2}}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{n}^{\star})\circ\cdots\circ(\text{id}_{A_{0}}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{2}^{\star})\circ\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\star}\Big)(\rho)\

(26)

Proof.

For
n=1

) is just the broadcasting condition, which holds by assumption. For
n=2
we have

\displaystyle\Big(\text{id}_{A_{0}}\otimes\Psi^{(\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2})}\Big)\Big(\text{id}_{A_{0}}\star\rho\Big)

\displaystyle=\Big(\text{id}_{A_{0}}\otimes(\mathcal{E}_{2}^{\star}\circ\mathcal{E}_{1})\Big)\Big(\text{id}_{A_{0}}\star\rho\Big)

by definition of
\Psi^{(\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2})}

\displaystyle=\Big(\Big(\text{id}_{A_{0}}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{2}^{\star}\Big)\circ\Big(\text{id}_{A_{0}}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{1}\Big)\Big)\Big(\text{id}_{A_{0}}\star\rho\Big)

\displaystyle=\Big((\text{id}_{A_{0}}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{2}^{\star})\circ\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\star}\Big)(\rho)\,,

Appendix G
Proof of Main Theorem

Theorem 2
.

Let
\star
be a spatiotemporal product which is state-linear and conditionable. Then for every
n
-chain
(\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n})\in\mathbb{CPTP}(A_{0},\ldots,A_{n})
with
n>0
, and for every state
\rho\in\mathfrak{S}(A_{0})

(\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n})\star\rho=\Big((\text{id}_{A_{0}\cdots A_{n-2}}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{n}^{\star})\circ\cdots\circ(\text{id}_{A_{0}}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{2}^{\star})\circ\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\star}\Big)(\rho)\,.

(27)

Let
n>0
. For every
n
-chain
(\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n})\in\mathbb{CPTP}(A_{1}\ldots,A_{n})
, let
\Phi^{(\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots\mathcal{E}_{n})}:A_{0}\to A_{1}\cdots A_{n}
be the Jamiołkowski map associated with the
n
-chain
(\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n})
and the spatiotemporal product
\star
, which we recall is defined as

\Phi^{(\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n})}=\mathscr{J}^{-1}\Big[(\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n})\star\mathds{1}_{A_{0}}\Big]\,.

Now since
\star
is conditionable and state-linear, the Jamiołkowski map
\Phi^{(\text{id}_{A_{0}})}
is invertible by item
1
of Lemma
2
, thus we can define a map
\chi^{(\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n})}:A_{0}\to A_{1}\cdots A_{n}
as the composition
\chi^{(\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n})}=\Phi^{(\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n})}\circ{\Phi^{(\text{id}_{A_{0}})}}^{-1}

\displaystyle(\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n})\star\mathds{1}_{A_{0}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\text{id}_{A_{0}}\otimes\Phi^{(\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n})}\Big)({\tt SWAP})

\displaystyle=\Big(\text{id}_{A_{0}}\otimes\Big(\Phi^{(\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n})}\circ{\Phi^{(\text{id}_{A_{0}})}}^{-1}\circ\Phi^{(\text{id}_{A_{0}})}\Big)\Big)({\tt SWAP})

\displaystyle=\Big(\text{id}_{A_{0}}\otimes\chi^{(\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n})}\Big)\left(\Big(\text{id}_{A_{0}}\otimes\Phi^{(\text{id}_{A_{0}})}\Big)({\tt SWAP})\right)

(28)

We then have

\displaystyle(\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n})\star\rho

\displaystyle=(\Theta_{\rho}\otimes\text{id}_{A_{1}\cdots A_{n}})((\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n})\star\mathds{1}_{A_{0}})

is conditionable

\displaystyle=\Big(\Theta_{\rho}\otimes\text{id}_{A_{1}\cdots A_{n}}\Big)\Big(\Big(\text{id}_{A_{0}}\otimes\chi^{(\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n})}\Big)\Big(\text{id}_{A_{0}}\star\mathds{1}\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\Big(\text{id}_{A_{0}}\otimes\chi^{(\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n})}\Big)\Big(\text{id}_{A_{0}}\star\rho\Big)\,,

is conditionable

5

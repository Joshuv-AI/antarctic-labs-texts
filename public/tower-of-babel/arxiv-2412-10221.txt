On the product of Weak Asplund locally convex spaces
Jerzy Ka̧kol and Arkady Leiderman

Abstract
. For locally convex spaces, we systematize several known equivalent definitions of Fréchet (Gâteaux) Differentiability Spaces and Asplund (Weak Asplund) Spaces. As an application, we extend the classical Mazur’s theorem as follows: Let E E be a separable Baire locally convex space and let Y Y be the product ∏ α ∈ A E α \prod_{\alpha\in A}E_{\alpha} of any family of separable Fréchet spaces; then the product E × Y E\times Y is Weak Asplund. Also, we prove that the product Y Y of any family of Banach spaces ( E α ) (E_{\alpha}) is an Asplund locally convex space if and only if each E α E_{\alpha} is Asplund. Analogues of both results are valid under the same assumptions, if Y Y is the Σ \Sigma -product of any family ( E α ) (E_{\alpha}) .

1.
Equivalence of definitions

All topological spaces
X
are assumed to be Tychonoff and all topological vector spaces are Hausdorff.
All vector spaces are considered over the field of real numbers
\mathbb{R}
.
The abbreviation lcs means a locally convex space.
A completely metrizable lcs is called a Fréchet lcs.
A topological space
X
is said to be
Baire
space if the intersection of every countable sequence of dense open subsets in
X
is dense.
By a
bounded set
in a topological vector space we understand any set which is absorbed by every
0
-neighbourhood.

There are many senses in which a map between topological linear spaces can be considered differentiable
(see
[
2
]
for details and history), the choice often depends on the applications under consideration.
The authors of notes
[
13
]
,
[
25
]
have suggested and explored several approaches to overcome special difficulties that arise in non-normed cases.

As R. R. Phelps indicated in his thoroughly written book
[
19
]
,
the first infinite-dimensional result about differentiability properties of convex functions was obtained in 1933 by S. Mazur
[
15
]
:
A continuous convex function
f:D\to{\mathbb{R}}
defined on an open convex subset
D
of a separable Banach space
E
, is Gâteaux differentiable on a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
D
.

In 1968, E. Asplund
[
1
]
extended Mazur’s theorem in two ways. Namely, he proved the same statement for a more general class of Banach spaces;
and studied a more restricted class of Banach spaces (now called Asplund spaces) in which a
stronger conclusion of Fréchet differentiability holds.

In the formulations of Definitions
1.1
-
1.4
below
D
denotes a nonempty open convex subset of a lcs
E
.

Definition 1.1
.

A convex function
f:D\to{\mathbb{R}}
is said to be Fréchet differentiable at a point
x_{0}\in D

if there exists a continuous linear functional
f^{\prime}(x_{0})\in E^{\prime}
such that for any bounded subset
B\subset E
and
\epsilon>0
, there is
\delta>0
such that

\sup_{x\in B}\left|\frac{f(x_{0}+tx)-f(x_{0})}{t}-\left\langle f^{\prime}(x_{0}),x\right\rangle\right|<\epsilon

whenever
0<|t|<\delta
.

Definition 1.2
.

A convex function
f:D\to{\mathbb{R}}
is said to be Gâteaux differentiable at a point
x_{0}\in D

if there exists a continuous linear functional
f^{\prime}(x_{0})\in E^{\prime}
such that for any
x\in E
and
\epsilon>0
, there is
\delta>0
such that

\left|\frac{f(x_{0}+tx)-f(x_{0})}{t}-\left\langle f^{\prime}(x_{0}),x\right\rangle\right|<\epsilon

whenever
0<|t|<\delta
.

Similarly to Banach spaces, locally convex spaces can be classified according
to the differentiability properties of the specified class of real valued convex functions. In the paper we follow the abbreviations suggested in
[
20
]
.

Definition 1.3
.

A lcs
E
is called Asplund (Weak Asplund) if every continuous convex function
f:D\to{\mathbb{R}}

is Fréchet (Gâteaux, respectively) differentiable on a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
D
.
Asplund (Weak Asplund) spaces are abbreviated by ASP (WASP, respectively).

Definition 1.4
.

A lcs
E
is called Fréchet (Gâteaux) Differentiability Space if every continuous convex function
f:D\to{\mathbb{R}}

is Fréchet (Gâteaux, respectively) differentiable on a dense subset of
D
.
Fréchet (Gâteaux) differentiable spaces are abbreviated by FDS (GDS, respectively).

The classification of locally spaces according to the dense or generic differentiability
of convex functions continues work of E. Asplund
[
1
]
, D. G. Larman and R. R. Phelps
[
14
]
and I. Namioka and R. R. Phelps
[
18
]
.
It is known that for Banach spaces the points of Fréchet differentiability of any continuous convex function form a
G_{\delta}
set in its domain,
so for Banach spaces ASP and FDS are equivalent.
M. Talagrand
[
22
]
constructed a 1-Lipschitz function on a Banach space
C(K)
whose set of points of Gâteaux differentiability is dense but of the first category.
Moreover, M. Čoban and P. Kenderov
[
6
]
have given examples to show that even when the set
of Gâteaux differentiability points of the
\sup
-norm of a Banach space
C(K)
is dense, it need not contain a
G_{\delta}
set.
In particular, this phenomenon occurs for the
double arrows
compact space
K
.
There is an example of a Banach space that is GDS but not Weak Asplund
[
17
]
.

A systematic research of locally convex spaces that are ASP, WASP, FDS, and GDS was originated in 1990 by B. Sharp
[
20
]
, and then it was developed further in joint papers
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
,

[
12
]
.
Among other results, B. Sharp
[
20
]
obtained a generalization of Mazur’s Theorem.

Theorem 1.5
.

[
20
, Theorem 2.1]

All separable Baire topological linear spaces (not necessarily lcs) are WASP.

Unfortunately, several authors much later rediscovered this result without any proper citation of the original publication
[
20
]
;
the list of such papers includes
[
5
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
26
]
.
Regrettably, it appears that other important works
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
have also not received due attention in the later publications.

Let us emphasize that in Definitions
1.3
and
1.4
one consider a collection of arbitrary continuous convex functions
f:D\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
where
D\subset E
is any nonempty open convex set. A natural question arises whether we can take a subcollection of more specific functions
f
and obtain the same classes of lcs
E
.
The following approach in this direction was developed in
[
11
]
: Consider only
f:E\to{\mathbb{R}}
which are the
Minkowski gauges
of open disks,
equivalently, consider only arbitrary
seminorms
defined on lcs
E
.
For our purposes, the principal results obtained in
[
11
]
can be reformulated as follows.

Theorem 1.6
.

[
11
]
Let
E
be a lcs.

(1)

E
is GDS if and only if every seminorm on
E
is Gâteaux differentiable on a dense subset of
E
.

(2)

E
is FDS if and only if every seminorm on
E
is Fréchet differentiable on a dense subset of
E
.

(3)

Assume that
E
is Baire. Then
E
is WASP if and only if every seminorm on
E\times{\mathbb{R}}
is Gâteaux differentiable on a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
E\times{\mathbb{R}}
.

(4)

Assume that
E
is Baire. Then
E
is ASP if and only if every seminorm on
E\times{\mathbb{R}}
is Fréchet differentiable on a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
E\times{\mathbb{R}}
.

As another natural option, one can take a subcollection of those continuous convex functions
f
which domains coincide with the whole
E
.
Since every seminorm is a continuous convex function, Theorem
1.6
immediately implies

Proposition 1.7
.

Let
E
be a lcs.

(1)

E
is GDS if and only if every continuous convex function
f:E\to{\mathbb{R}}
is Gâteaux differentiable on a dense subset of
E
.

(2)

E
is FDS if and only if every continuous convex function
f:E\to{\mathbb{R}}
is Fréchet differentiable on a dense subset of
E
.

Analogous equivalencies for WASP and ASP provided
E
is a Banach spaces are very well known. However, the published proofs of these equivalencies
are very indirect and use a heavy machinery of the Banach spaces tools (see
[
8
, Theorem 8.26]
,
[
9
, Section I.5]
).
It is not clear whether these arguments can be easily adapted to the case of non-Banach spaces.
Nevertheless, the statement is true for any Baire lcs. It is sufficient to apply another result obtained in
[
11
]
.

We define an
asymptotic seminorm
on a lcs
E
(see
[
11
]
) to be a continuous function
f:E\to{\mathbb{R}}
for which there is a seminorm
g
on
E
satisfying:

(a)

If
x_{\alpha}\rightarrow x
in
E
and
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
then
\frac{1}{\lambda}f(\lambda x_{\alpha})\rightarrow g(x)
;

(b)

For all
x\in E
,
f(x)=f(-x)
and
f(x)\geq g(x)
.

An asymptotic seminorm is convex and every seminorm is an asymptotic seminorm.

Theorem 1.8
.

[
11
]
Let
E
be a Baire lcs.

(1)

E
is WASP if and only if every asymptotic seminorm on
E
is Gâteaux differentiable on a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
E
.

(2)

E
is ASP if and only if every asymptotic seminorm on
E
is Fréchet differentiable on a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
E
.

Theorem
1.8
immediately implies

Proposition 1.9
.

Let
E
be a Baire lcs.

(1)

E
is WASP if and only if every continuous convex function
f:E\to{\mathbb{R}}
is Gâteaux differentiable on a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
E
.

(2)

E
is ASP if and only if every continuous convex function
f:E\to{\mathbb{R}}
is Fréchet differentiable on a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
E
.

2.
Application to products

Theorem
1.6
prompts a question whether the above defined classes of lcs are closed under products.
If
\{E_{i}:i=1,\dots,n\}
is a finite family of Banach Asplund spaces, then so is the product
\prod_{i=1}^{n}E_{i}
.
Does
E
is a Banach WASP imply that
E\times{\mathbb{R}}
also is WASP,
is a long standing open question. For general lcs the answer is not known either.

M. Fabian showed that if
E
is a Banach GDS, then so is the product
E\times{\mathbb{R}}
(see
[
19
, Proposition 6.5]
).
Later this result was extended to the products
E\times Y
, where is
Y
any separable Banach space
[
4
]
.
Note that R. Eyland and B. Sharp
[
11
, 3.2]
proved for any lcs
E
: if
E
is GDS (FDS), then so is
E\times{\mathbb{R}}
, respectively.
A further generalization for the product of lcs was obtained in
[
21
]
: let a lcs
E
be GDS and let
Y
be the product
\prod_{\alpha\in A}E_{\alpha}

of any family of separable Fréchet spaces; then the product
E\times Y
also is GDS.

To the best of our knowledge, the questions about the preservation of WASP and ASP for arbitrary products of locally convex spaces have been not considered in the literature before.
First, we observe how to extend Theorem
1.5
. Note also that the product of two normed Baire spaces need not to be Baire
[
23
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
E
be a separable Baire topological linear space. Then for every separable Fréchet space
Y

the product
E\times Y
is WASP.

Proof.

Clearly,
E\times Y
is separable. Moreover, the product
E\times Y
is Baire.
This claim follows from the remarkable result of W. Moors
[
16
]
:
The product of a Baire space with a metrizable hereditarily Baire space is again a Baire space. Evidently, any completely metrizable space
Y
is
hereditarily Baire, i.e. every closed subspace of
Y
is Baire. Thus,
E\times Y
is WASP by Theorem
1.5
.
∎

Next we prove a general statement which is one of the main results of our paper.
Undoubtedly, our arguments are inspired by the proof of
[
20
, Theorem 5.5]
stating that any lcs with a weak topology is ASP.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
E
be a separable Baire lcs and let
Y
be the product
\prod_{\alpha\in A}E_{\alpha}

of any family of separable Fréchet spaces.
Then the product
E\times Y
is WASP.

Proof.

We shall use two apparently folklore facts.

Fact 1.

Let
f:X\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a convex function defined on a linear space
X
. If
f
is bounded then it is constant.

Proof.
If not, without loss of generality we may assume that
f(0)=0
and there is
x\in X
such that
f(x)>0
. Then for every
\lambda>1
we have

f(x)=f(\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}\cdot 0+\frac{1}{\lambda}\cdot\lambda x)\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}f(\lambda x).

It follows that
f(\lambda x)\geq\lambda f(x)
, which contradicts to the boundedness of
f
.

Fact 2.
(see
[
7
, Proposition 1.3.2]
)

Let
Z
be a Baire topological space and let
{\mathcal{U}}
be an open cover of
Z
. Assume that
\Omega
is a subset of
Z
such that for any
U\in{\mathcal{U}}

the intersection
U\cap\Omega
contains a dense
G_{\delta}
subset in
U
. Then the whole set
\Omega
contains a dense
G_{\delta}
subset in
Z
.

In order to prove Theorem
2.2
we fix a continuous convex function
f:E\times Y\to{\mathbb{R}}
.
We may assume that the set of indices
A
is infinite, otherwise
Y
is a separable Fréchet space, and we apply Proposition
2.1
.
Denote the set of Gâteaux differentiability points of
f
in
Z=E\times Y
by
\Omega
.
According to Remark at the end of paper
[
16
]
, the space
Z
is Baire.
Pick any point
P_{0}\in Z
.
We shall find an open neighborhood
U
of
P_{0}
such that the intersection
U\cap\Omega
contains a dense
G_{\delta}
subset in
U
.

Since
f
is continuous, it is bounded on some basic open convex neighborhood
U\subset Z
of
P_{0}
.
Denote by
U=G\times\prod_{\alpha\in I}G_{\alpha}\times\prod_{\alpha\in A\setminus I}E_{\alpha}
,
where
I\subset A
is finite,
G\subset E
and
G_{\alpha}\subset E_{\alpha}
are open convex neighborhoods.
Also, denote by
\pi
the natural projection of
Y
onto
Y_{I}=\prod_{\alpha\in I}E_{\alpha}
and let
Z_{I}=E\times Y_{I}
.

Now we define a continuous convex function
h:G\times\prod_{\alpha\in I}G_{\alpha}\to{\mathbb{R}}
by the rule:
h(x,\pi(y))=f(x,y)
,
whenever
(x,y)\in U
.
Function
h
is correctly defined because for every fixed
(x,a)\in G\times\prod_{\alpha\in I}G_{\alpha}

a continuous convex function
\varphi(b)=f(x,(a,b)):\prod_{\alpha\in A\setminus I}E_{\alpha}\to{\mathbb{R}}

is constant, by Fact 1.

According to Proposition
2.1
,
E\times Y_{I}
is WASP. Therefore, function
h
has a dense
G_{\delta}
set
V

of Gâteaux differentiability points in its open convex domain, and then function
f
has a dense
G_{\delta}
set
W=V\times\prod_{\alpha\in A\setminus I}E_{\alpha}

of Gâteaux differentiability points in
U
. Indeed, let
z_{0}=(x_{0},y_{0})\in W
.
For any
z=(x,y)\in Z
there is
\lambda>0
such that for all
|t|<\lambda
and each
h^{\prime}\in(Z_{I})^{\prime}
we have

\left|\frac{h((x_{0},\pi(y_{0}))+t(x,\pi(y)))-h(x_{0},\pi(y_{0}))}{t}-\left\langle h^{\prime},(x_{0},\pi(y_{0}))\right\rangle\right|=

=\left|\frac{f((x_{0},y_{0})+t(x,y))-f(x_{0},y_{0})}{t}-\left\langle f^{\prime},(x_{0},y_{0})\right\rangle\right|,

where
f^{\prime}\in Z^{\prime}
is defined by
\left\langle f^{\prime},(x,y)\right\rangle=\left\langle h^{\prime},(x,\pi(y))\right\rangle
.
This means that function
f
is Gâteaux differentiable at
(x_{0},y_{0})
, by Definition
1.2
, because
h
is Gâteaux differentiable at
(x_{0},\pi(y_{0}))
.

Applying Fact 2 we conclude that the whole set
\Omega
contains a dense
G_{\delta}
subset in
Z
, and the proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\{E_{\alpha}:\alpha\in A\}
be any family of Banach spaces.
Then the product
Y=\prod_{\alpha\in A}E_{\alpha}
is an ASP lcs if and only if each
E_{\alpha}
is ASP.

Proof.

First, any product of Banach spaces is a Baire topological space. Second, any finite product of Asplund Banach spaces is Asplund. Then we literally repeat the proof of Theorem
2.3
.
Again, any continuous convex function defined on the product
Z

factorizes thru a finite subproduct
\prod_{\alpha\in I}E_{\alpha}
. The only difference is the following.
At the end, in order to show Fréchet differentiability we need to add a trivial remark that for every bounded set in the product
its image under the projection to a subproduct is bounded.

In the opposite direction, let
f:E_{\beta}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a continuous convex function, where
\beta\in A
is fixed.
Denote the projection of the product
Y
onto
E_{\beta}
by
\pi
. Observe that the projection
\pi
is an open continuous and bound covering mapping,
i.e. for every bounded subset
B
of
E_{\beta}
there is a bounded subset
C
of
Z
such that
B\subset\pi(C)
.
Indeed, it is enough to include to
C
all points which coordinates are zero for all indices
\alpha\neq\beta
and identical to the points from
B
on the place
\beta
.
Take a composition
f\circ\pi
. It is a continuous convex function defined on the Asplund lcs
Z
.
Hence, function
f\circ\pi
has a dense set
\Omega\subset Z
of Fréchet differentiability points. Evidently,
\pi(\Omega)
is dense in
E_{\beta}
.
Notice that since
\pi
is continuous and bound covering mapping,
f
is Fréchet differentiable at
\pi(x)
if and only if
f\circ\pi
is Fréchet differentiable at
x

(see
[
10
]
). So,
f
is Fréchet differentiable at each point of
\pi(\Omega)
. Finally, the Banach space
E_{\beta}
is FDS and we are done since
for Banach spaces ASP and FDS are equivalent.
∎

More information about the behavior of ASP and WASP under linear continuous onto mappings of lcs can be found in
[
10
, Section 1]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
10
, Theorem 1.3]

Let
X
be a Fréchet space,
Y
a regular linear space, and let

T:X\to Y
be continuous, linear, open and onto.

(1)

If
X
is WASP, then so is
Y
.

(2)

Suppose that
T
is also bound covering. If
X
is ASP, then so is
Y
.

Recall that the
\Sigma
-product of topological spaces
\{X_{\alpha}:\alpha\in A\}
, with
a base point
b=(b_{\alpha})\in\prod_{\alpha\in A}X_{\alpha}
,
is the subspace of the product
\prod_{\alpha\in A}X_{\alpha}

consisting of points
x=(x_{\alpha})
with
x_{\alpha}=b_{\alpha}
for all but countably many
\alpha
.
Naturally,
\Sigma
-product is considered with a topology induced from the whole product.
It is easily seen that if
b_{\alpha}
is the zero element of a lcs
E_{\alpha}
, then
the
\Sigma
-product of any family
\{E_{\alpha}:\alpha\in A\}
is a locally convex space.

It has been shown in
[
3
]
that the
\Sigma
-product of any family of completely metrizable spaces is a hereditarily Baire space.
Moreover, using the strategy of the main results of
[
3
]
,
[
16
]

one can show that the product
Z=E\times Y
is Baire, for every Baire space
E
and
\Sigma
-product
Y
of a family of completely metrizable spaces.

Therefore, the following counterparts of Theorems
2.2
and
2.3
are valid, with the same proofs, respectively.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
E
be a separable Baire lcs and let
Y
be the
\Sigma
-product
of any family of separable Fréchet spaces.
Then the product
E\times Y
is WASP.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\{E_{\alpha}:\alpha\in A\}
be any family of Banach spaces.
Then the
\Sigma
-product
Y
of
\{E_{\alpha}:\alpha\in A\}
is an ASP lcs if and only if each
E_{\alpha}
is ASP.

3.
Open questions and remarks

We lack the counterexamples to the very basic questions.

Question 3.1
.

Let
E
be a lcs.
If
E
is Asplund, is then
E\times{\mathbb{R}}
Asplund?

If
E
additionally is assumed to be Baire, Question
3.1
appears as
[
11
, Conjecture 4.0]
.

Question 3.2
.

Let
E
be a lcs.
If
E\times{\mathbb{R}}
is Asplund (Weak Asplund), is then
E
Asplund (Weak Asplund, respectively)?

If
E
additionally is assumed to be Baire, Question
3.2
has been answered positively
[
11
]
.

Question 3.3
.

Let
E
and
F
be lcs.
If
E\times F
is Asplund (Weak Asplund), is then
E
Asplund (Weak Asplund, respectively)?

The answer to Question
3.2
is affirmative, if additionally
E
and
F
are assumed to be Fréchet spaces. It follows from
Theorem
2.4
.

Remark 3.4
.

We should warn the reader that paper
[
24
]
contains several results which proofs are not justified.
Let us provide some details.

One of the claims of
[
24
, Theorem 5.5]
is the following: Let
E
be a Baire lcs, then
E
is ASP if and only if
every seminorm on
E
is Fréchet differentiable on a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
E
.
Such a statement would resolve immediately mentioned above
[
11
, Conjecture 4.0]
.

However, a very short and indirect proof in
[
24
]
is incomplete. The only correctly proved relevant result is Theorem

1.8
: A Baire lcs
E
is ASP if and only if every
asymptotic seminorm
on
E
is Fréchet differentiable on a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
E
.

Furthermore,
[
24
, Theorem 7.1]
claims a very impressive generalization of the Asplund’s theorem, namely:
If the strong dual
E^{*}
of a Baire lcs
E
is separable, then
E
is ASP.

We do not have a counterexample to this statement, but the proof provided in
[
24
]
is incorrect.
First, the authors make an essential use of a problematic
[
24
, Theorem 5.5]
.
Second, let us comment the following argument of
[
24
]
.
Let
E
be a lcs and let
E^{*}
be endowed with the strong dual topology.
Take any continuous seminorm
p
on
E
and denote
E_{0}=\{x\in E:p(x)=0\}
. Consider the quotient space
E_{p}=E/E_{0}
endowed with the following norm
\bar{p}(x+E_{0})=p(x)
.
So, we have a normed space
E_{p}
(not necessarily complete). Clearly, the natural map
Q:E\to E_{p}
is a continuous linear surjection but not necessarily open.
A crucial claim which appears in
[
24
]
is the following: If
E^{*}
is separable, then
(E_{p})^{*}
also is separable. However, this claim is wrong.
Therefore, the proof fails.

Modification of the existing maximum residue levels for acequinocyl in cane fruits and other small fruits and berries
Authors: European Food Safety Authority (EFSA), Giulia Bellisai, Giovanni Bernasconi, Irene Castellan, Monica del Aguila, Lucien Ferreira, Abdulshakur Isah Lawal, Renata Leuschner, Andrea Mioč, Marta Szot, Manuela Tiramani, Alessia Verani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn accordance with Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, the applicant Agro‐Kanesho Kabushiki Kaisha European Branch submitted a request to the competent national authority in Germany to modify the existing maximum residue levels (MRLs) for the active substance acequinocyl in cane fruits and other small fruits and berries. The data submitted in support of the request were found to be sufficient to derive MRL proposals for the commodities under assessment. Adequate analytical methods for enforcement are available to control the residues of acequinocyl in the raw agricultural commodities under consideration at the validated limit of quantification (LOQ) of 0.01 mg/kg. In addition, residue trials were submitted to assess potential carry‐over to honey from the intended uses on cane fruits and other small fruits and berries. Despite being overdosed compared with the intended NEU good agricultural practices (GAPs), the trials indicate that no modification of the current honey MRL (LOQ of 0.05 mg/kg) is required. Based on the risk assessment results, EFSA concluded that the short‐term and long‐term intake of residues resulting from the use of acequinocyl according to the reported agricultural practices is unlikely to present a risk to consumer health. It is noted that, for honey, the calculations relied on overdosed semi‐field (tunnel) trials and are therefore considered conservative.

SUMMARY

In accordance with Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, Agro‐Kanesho Kabushiki Kaisha European Branch submitted an application to the competent national authority in Germany (evaluating Member State, EMS) to modify the existing maximum residue levels (MRLs) for the active substance acequinocyl in cane fruits and other small fruits and berries.

The application, alongside the dossier containing the supporting data in IUCLID format, was submitted through the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) Central Submission System on 29 September 2023. The appointed EMS, Germany, assessed the dossier and declared its admissibility on 3 May 2024. Subsequently, following the implementation of the EFSA's confidentiality decision, the non‐confidential version of the dossier was published by EFSA, and a public consultation launched on the dossier. The consultation aimed to consult stakeholders and the public on the scientific data, studies and other information part of, or supporting, the submitted application, in order to identify whether other relevant scientific data or studies are available. The consultation ran from 24 May 2024 to 14 June 2024. No additional data nor comments were submitted in the framework of the consultation.

At the end of the commenting period, the EMS proceeded drafting the evaluation report in accordance with Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, which was submitted to the European Commission and to EFSA on 19 November 2025. To accommodate for the intended NEU uses of acequinocyl, the EMS proposed to raise the existing MRLs for cane fruits and other small fruits and berries from the limit of quantification (LOQ) of 0.01 to 0.8 mg/kg.

On 28 November 2025, the European Commission sent a mandate to EFSA to assess the application and the evaluation report as required by Article 10 of the MRL regulation.

EFSA identified points which needed further clarification and requested the EMS to address them. The applicant provided the requested information in an updated IUCLID dossier. The additional information was duly considered by the EMS who submitted a revised evaluation report to EFSA on 24 March 2026, which replaced the previously submitted evaluation report.

Based on the conclusions derived by EFSA in the framework of Directive 91/414/EEC, the data evaluated under previous MRL assessments including the review of the existing MRLs for acequinocyl and the additional data provided by the EMS in the framework of this application, the following conclusions are derived.

The metabolism of acequinocyl following foliar and soil application was investigated in crops belonging to the groups of fruit crops. In investigated crops, the metabolic pathway was seen to be similar and proceeds through the hydrolysis of the acetic acid ester to the metabolite acequinocyl‐OH (named as R1 in the peer review), followed by the opening of the naphthalenedione ring leading to the metabolite AKM‐18 and further hydrolysis of the dodecyl aliphatic chain, resulting in the formation of phthalic acid. Based on the conclusion on the peer review of acequinocyl, acequinocyl‐OH and AKM‐18 were considered of a similar toxicity as the parent and unlikely to be genotoxic.

Studies investigating the nature or magnitude of acequinocyl in rotational crops are not required, since the proposed uses of acequinocyl are on semi‐permanent crops. Moreover, acequinocyl exhibits low persistence in soil.

A data gap on studies investigating the effect of processing on the nature of acequinocyl (hydrolysis studies) was identified in the context of the MRL review. Hydrolysis studies submitted in the context of a recent application demonstrated that the active substance degrades to acequinocyl‐OH and AKM‐18 under processing conditions representative of baking, brewing and boiling and sterilisation. In these studies, acequinocyl was radiolabelled only in the phenyl ring. Although the available data are considered sufficient to assess the intended uses on cane fruits and other small fruits and berries, the data gap identified during the MRL review remains partially addressed and should be further evaluated during the confirmatory data process or active substance renewal.

Based on the metabolic pattern identified in metabolism studies and the toxicological relevance of metabolites, the residue definitions for raw agricultural commodities were proposed as ‘acequinocyl’ for enforcement and risk assessment during the EU peer review and the MRL review. EFSA concluded that, for the crops assessed in this application, metabolism of acequinocyl in primary crops has been sufficiently addressed and that the previously derived residue definitions are applicable. These residue definitions are limited to fruits and fruiting vegetables and are also applicable to hops and honey.

For processed commodities, the MRL review proposed tentative residue definitions as ‘acequinocyl and acequinocyl‐OH, expressed as acequinocyl’ for enforcement and risk assessment. This conclusion is still considered valid, and it is recommended to further assess available hydrolysis studies, either in the framework of the confirmatory data for the MRL review or during the upcoming peer review of the renewal assessment of the active substance.

Sufficiently validated analytical methods based on high‐performance liquid chromatography with tandem mass spectrometry (HPLC‐MS/MS) are available to quantify residues in high acid content commodities according to the enforcement residue definition. The methods enable the quantification of residues at or above the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg. Extraction efficiency of the solvent system used in this method was proven by cross‐validation. The extraction efficiency study is also provided for the renewal assessment; it is therefore recommended that all data on extraction efficiency submitted in the framework of this application are further considered and confirmed in the framework of the upcoming peer review for the renewal of acequinocyl.

The available residue trials are sufficient to derive MRL proposals of 0.8 mg/kg for cane fruits and other small fruits and berries, based on the intended uses in northern Europe (NEU). It is noted that the higher MRL of 2 mg/kg was proposed for blueberries, cranberries, currants (black, red and white) and gooseberries (green, red and yellow) in the context of a previous MRL assessment, based on indoor uses.

Specific studies investigating the magnitude of acequinocyl residues in processed commodities are not required, as significant residues are not expected in raw agricultural commodities (RACs) and the individual contribution to the total theoretical maximum daily intake (TMDI) of the crops under assessment is below the trigger value of 10% of the acceptable daily intake (ADI).

Residues of acequinocyl in commodities of animal origin were not assessed since the commodities under consideration in this MRL application are normally not fed to livestock.

As cane fruits and other small fruits and berries are melliferous crops and the use of acequinocyl described in the current application is expected to occur during the flowering period, the applicant submitted residue trials to investigate the potential carry‐over to honey from the uses under assessment. The available residue trials are overdosed compared to the intended NEU GAPs on cane fruits and other small fruits and berries but indicate that a modification of the current MRL in honey, set at the LOQ of 0.05 mg/kg, is not needed.

The toxicological profile of acequinocyl was assessed in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review under Directive 91/414/EEC and the data were sufficient to derive an acceptable daily intake (ADI) of 0.023 mg/kg body weight (bw) per day and an acute reference dose (ARfD) of 0.08 mg/kg bw.

The consumer risk assessment was performed with revision 3.1 of the EFSA Pesticide Residues Intake Model (PRIMo). This exposure calculation includes also commodities for which the Article 12 confirmatory data gaps have so far not been addressed, and therefore, this exposure calculation shall be considered indicative. The short‐term exposure assessment was performed for the crops of interest, and calculations were based on the highest residue (HR) derived from supervised field trials. The highest short‐term exposure accounted for 13.1% of ARfD for blueberries and was lower for the other commodities under assessment. The highest estimated long‐term dietary intake was 9% of the ADI for the NL toddler diet. The highest estimated contribution of residues from the commodities under assessment to the overall long‐term exposure was 0.74% of the ADI for currants (NL toddler), whereas lower contributions were estimated for the other commodities under assessment. Although uncertainties remain due to the data gaps identified in the review of existing MRLs, based on the indicative exposure calculations, EFSA concluded that the intake of residues of acequinocyl resulting from the intended uses is unlikely to pose a risk to consumers' health.

ASSESSMENT

The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) received an application to modify the existing maximum residue levels (MRLs) for acequinocyl in cane fruits and other small fruits and berries. The detailed description of the intended NEU uses of acequinocyl, which are the basis for the current MRL application, is reported in Appendix A.

Acequinocyl is the ISO common name for 3‐dodecyl‐1,4‐dioxo‐1,4‐dihydro‐2‐naphthyl acetate (IUPAC). The chemical structures of the active substance and its main metabolites are reported in Appendix E.

Acequinocyl was evaluated in the framework of Directive 91/414/EEC 1 in conjunction with Regulation (EU) No 188/2011, 2 with the Netherlands designated as rapporteur Member State (RMS) for the representative uses as field and greenhouse foliar applications on ornamentals, apples and pears. The draft assessment report (DAR) prepared by the RMS has been peer reviewed by EFSA (EFSA, 2013). Acequinocyl was approved 3 for use as an acaricide on 1 September 2014. The approval of acequinocyl expires on 15 November 2026, and the renewal of approval process has been initiated.

The European Union (EU) MRLs for acequinocyl are established in Annex II of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005. 4 Since the EU pesticides’ peer review, EFSA has been issued a number of reasoned opinions on the modification of MRLs for acequinocyl, including the review of existing MRLs according to Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 (EFSA, 2020). Following the MRL review, EFSA finalised three opinions on acequinocyl, respectively, in sweet peppers/bell peppers (EFSA, 2022), strawberries (EFSA, 2025a) and various crops (EFSA, 2025b). The proposals from these reasoned opinions have been considered in recent MRL regulations, 5 apart from those from the latest opinion in various crops (EFSA, 2025b). EFSA included the conclusions of this latter opinion in the present assessment. The assessment of Article 12 confirmatory data will be undertaken together with the peer review on the renewal of approval.

In accordance Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 and following the provisions set by the ‘Transparency Regulation’ (EU) 2019/1381, 6 the applicant Agro‐Kanesho Kabushiki Kaisha European Branch submitted on 29 September 2023 an application to the competent national authority in Germany, alongside the dossier containing the supporting data using the IUCLID format. The appointed EMS assessed the dossier and declared its admissibility on 3 May 2024. Subsequently, following the implementation of the EFSA's confidentiality decision, the non‐confidential version of the dossier was published by EFSA, and a public consultation launched on the dossier. The consultation aimed to consult stakeholders and the public on the scientific data, studies and other information part of, or supporting, the submitted application, in order to identify whether other relevant scientific data or studies are available. The consultation run from 24 May 2024 to 14 June 2024. No additional data nor comments were submitted in the framework of the consultation.

At the end of the commenting period, the EMS proceeded drafting the evaluation report in accordance with Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, which was submitted to the European Commission and to EFSA on 19 November 2025. To accommodate for the intended uses of acequinocyl, the EMS proposed to raise the existing MRLs for cane fruits and other small fruits and berries from the limit of quantification (LOQ) of 0.01 mg/kg to 0.8 mg/kg.

On 28 November 2025, the European Commission sent a mandate to EFSA to assess the application and the evaluation report as required by Article 10 of the MRL regulation.

EFSA identified points which needed further clarification and requested the EMS to address them. The applicant provided the requested information in an updated IUCLID dossier. The additional information was duly considered by the EMS, who submitted a revised evaluation report to EFSA on 24 March 2026 (Germany, 2025), which replaced the previously submitted evaluation report.

RESIDUES IN PLANTS

Nature of residues and methods of analysis in plants

Nature of residues in primary crops

The metabolism of acequinocyl was investigated in fruit crops, following both foliar (apples, aubergines and oranges) and soil (aubergines) treatment (Netherlands, 2005), and assessed in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review (EFSA, 2013) and the MRL review (EFSA, 2020). In the studies conducted with apples and aubergines (both foliar and soil treatments), acequinocyl was radiolabelled either in the phenyl ring or the dodecyl chain of the molecule, while in oranges, it was labelled only in the phenyl ring. Results from the different studies were comparable.

In the investigated crops, the metabolic pathway was seen to be similar and proceeded through the hydrolysis of the acetic acid ester to form the metabolite acequinocyl‐OH (referred to as R1 in the peer review), followed by the opening of the naphthalenedione ring leading to the metabolite AKM‐18 and further hydrolysis of the dodecyl aliphatic chain, resulting in the formation of phthalic acid (EFSA, 2013).

Acequinocyl was the major component identified in the total radioactive residues (TRRs) of all the studies, accounting for 28% (0.06 mg eq/kg) to 41% TRR (0.11 mg eq/kg) in apples and oranges 30 days after treatment (DAT) and for 55% TRR (0.01 mg eq/kg) in aubergines, 14 DAT. The other metabolites identified, namely, acequinocyl‐OH, AKM‐18 and phthalic acid, represented less than 0.05 mg eq/kg or 10% TRR. In leaves, at harvest, acequinocyl was also the most prominent component identified, representing up to 58% TRR (1.14 mg eq/kg) (EFSA, 2020).

For the intended uses on cane fruits and on other small fruits and berries, EFSA concludes that the metabolism of acequinocyl is sufficiently elucidated and that no additional investigations are necessary.

Nature of residues in rotational crops

The current application is on semi‐permanent crops; therefore, an assessment of residues in rotational crops is not triggered. In addition, the field DT90 reported in the soil degradation studies evaluated in the framework of the peer review was 21 and 24 h for parent acequinocyl and metabolite acequinocyl‐OH, respectively. For the metabolite AKM‐18, only laboratory data were available, indicating a DT90 of 12 days (EFSA, 2013). Therefore, also considering the low soil persistence of acequinocyl and its main metabolites, studies investigating the nature of acequinocyl residues in rotational crops are not required.

Nature of residues in processed commodities

No standard hydrolysis studies were available during the EU pesticides peer review or MRL review, leading EFSA to identify a data gap (EFSA, 2020). In the absence of such studies, processing trials on citrus fruits were used to estimate the effects of processing on acequinocyl residues, revealing the metabolite acequinocyl‐OH, which was provisionally included in the residue definition for processed commodities.

Acequinocyl has very low water solubility (0.007 mg/L at 25°C), and hydrolysis studies are generally not required for substances with solubility below 0.01 mg/L (OECD, 2007). However, a radiolabelled hydrolysis study was provided in the previous MRL applications (sweet peppers and bell peppers in EFSA, 2022; strawberries in EFSA, 2025a). This study showed that acequinocyl degrades to acequinocyl‐OH and AKM‐18 under hydrolytic conditions, with acequinocyl‐OH exceeding 10% of applied radioactivity (AR) under processing conditions representative of baking, brewing, boiling and sterilisation, and AKM‐18 exceeding 10% of AR under processing conditions representative of sterilisation. Minor degradation products (AKM‐08, AKM‐14, AKM‐15) were found at lower levels (< 5% AR). It is noted that, in the baking/brewing/boiling process, 29% of the applied radioactivity (in the lid extract) was not further analysed, and therefore, the uncertainty regarding the nature of residues remains for this hydrolysis process.

Acequinocyl was radiolabelled only in the phenyl ring. However, no cleavage of the molecule was observed in the study and no other breakdown products would be expected in case a second labelling position in the aliphatic carbon chain (as observed in the metabolism study performed on apples and aubergines) would be applied (EFSA, 2022). The EMS and EFSA conclude that the available data are sufficient to assess the intended uses on cane fruits and other small fruits and berries (see Section 1.2.3). However, considering the limitation identified in the hydrolysis studies (e.g. only one labelling position, not full characterisation of radioactivity identified on the vessel lid under baking/brewing/boiling conditions), the general question on the adequacy of the evidence to fully address the MRL review data gap is still open and should be addressed in the framework of the renewal of approval and the confirmatory data assessment. Furthermore, in view of the future renewal of the approval of acequinocyl, a new assessment of this section will be performed including a re‐assessment of the toxicity of the parent metabolites based on up‐to‐date criteria. Therefore, the conclusions reported in this reasoned opinion might need to be reconsidered and are currently considered provisional.

Analytical methods for enforcement purposes in plant commodities

Analytical methods for the determination of acequinocyl residues in plant commodities were assessed during the EU pesticides peer review, the MRL review and a previous MRL application (EFSA, 2013, 2020, 2025a).

A hyphenated analytical method based on high‐performance liquid chromatography coupled to tandem mass spectrometry (HPLC‐MS/MS) was validated for the determination of parent acequinocyl and metabolite acequinocyl‐OH in high‐acid and high‐water content commodities, with a limit of quantification (LOQ) of 0.01 mg/kg, for each analyte (EFSA, 2013). Additionally, an HPLC‐MS/MS method for parent acequinocyl and metabolite acequinocyl‐OH in high‐acid and dry commodities was made available in the MRL review with the reported LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg. For the determination of parent acequinocyl and metabolite acequinocyl‐OH in hops, a validated HPLC‐MS/MS method is available with an LOQ of 0.1 mg/kg for each analyte (EFSA, 2020).

Additionally, in the framework of the MRL review, the EU Reference Laboratories for Pesticides Residues (EURL) provided validation results for a Quick, Easy, Cheap, Effective, Rugged and Safe (QuEChERS) multiresidue method using liquid chromatography coupled to tandem mass spectrometry (LC‐MS/MS) with an LOQ of 0.1 mg/kg in high‐water, high‐acid and high‐oil content commodities and an LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg for dry commodities for the enforcement of parent acequinocyl in routine analysis. The validation failed at 0.01 mg/kg for high‐water, high‐acid and high‐oil content commodities. According to EURLs, metabolite acequinocyl‐OH can be monitored in routine analysis in high‐water and high‐acid content commodities as well as dry commodities at the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg. According to EURLs, analytical standards for both parent acequinocyl and metabolite acequinocyl‐OH are commercially available (EURL, 2018).

A sufficiently validated QuEChERS method using HPLC‐MS/MS with two mass transitions for enforcement was provided in the framework of a previous application (EFSA, 2025a). It allows quantification of residues in high‐water and high‐acid matrices at the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg and in hops at the LOQ of 0.05 mg/kg for each analyte, respectively. Extraction was performed with acetonitrile which was equivalent to the extraction agent used in the metabolism study, where > 70% of the total TRR was extracted. Extraction efficiency was proven by cross‐validation (EFSA, 2025a). It is noted that the study is also provided in the context of the renewal of the approval assessment. As mentioned in EFSA (2025a), it is therefore recommended that all data on extraction efficiency submitted are further considered and confirmed in the framework of the upcoming peer review for the renewal of acequinocyl.

In conclusion, sufficiently validated analytical methods are available for the determination of acequinocyl residues in high‐acid content commodities (relevant for the crops under consideration) at the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg.

Storage stability of residues in plants

The storage stability of acequinocyl and its metabolite acequinocyl‐OH in plant commodities stored under frozen conditions was investigated in the frameworks of the EU pesticides peer review, the MRL review and a previous MRL application (EFSA, 2013, 2020, 2025a).

For high‐acid content commodities, relevant to the current assessment, storage stability of both parent acequinocyl and its metabolite acequinocyl‐OH was demonstrated for at least 5 months in oranges (EFSA, 2020) and for at least 12 months in strawberries and mandarins stored at −18°C (EFSA, 2025a).

In the present application, the applicant submitted additional storage stability studies performed on tomatoes and fresh hops (high water content matrices) and dried hops (dry matrix) (Germany, 2025). Although these matrices are not relevant to the commodities under assessment, the studies were nevertheless evaluated according to the technical guidelines on storage stability (OECD, 2025). Details of available storage stability studies with acequinocyl are reported in Appendix B.1.1.2.

Proposed residue definitions

Based on the metabolic pattern identified in the metabolism studies, the toxicological relevance of acequinocyl and its metabolites and the capabilities of enforcement analytical methods, the following residue definitions were proposed by the EU pesticides peer review (EFSA, 2013) and confirmed in the MRL review (EFSA, 2020): residue definition for risk assessment (raw agricultural commodity (RAC)): acequinocyl.residue definition for enforcement (RAC): acequinocyl.

residue definition for risk assessment (raw agricultural commodity (RAC)): acequinocyl.

residue definition for risk assessment (raw agricultural commodity (RAC)): acequinocyl.

residue definition for enforcement (RAC): acequinocyl.

residue definition for enforcement (RAC): acequinocyl.

The enforcement and risk assessment residue definitions are limited to fruits and fruiting vegetables and are also applicable to hops (EFSA, 2020).

The residue definition for enforcement set in Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 is identical with the above‐mentioned enforcement residue definition.

For processed commodities, a data gap regarding a study investigating the nature of acequinocyl residues under standard processing conditions was identified during the MRL review (EFSA, 2020). A new hydrolysis study was submitted and was assessed by EFSA under the framework of previous assessments (EFSA, 2022, 2025a) and indicated that acequinocyl is the predominant compound after hydrolysis and partially degrades mainly to acequinocyl‐OH and AKM‐18. The toxicity of the metabolites acequinocyl‐OH and AKM‐18, found in the hydrolysis tests and in primary crops, was assessed during the EU pesticides peer review (EFSA, 2013) and these compounds were characterised as unlikely to be genotoxic. These metabolites were also present in the rat metabolism and considered to be of a similar toxicity as the parent compound (EFSA, 2020). Nonetheless, the available toxicological data will be re‐evaluated in the upcoming peer review of the renewal of the approval assessment. Therefore, despite the deficiencies highlighted regarding the submitted study, the indicative findings do support the tentative residue definition derived in the MRL review (i.e. acequinocyl and acequinocyl‐OH, expressed as acequinocyl). A study with a second radiolabelling position might be useful to fully depict the possible degradation of the side chain during hydrolysis conditions and to confirm the available results. Therefore, the general data gap regarding the nature of residues in processed commodities should still be assessed either in the framework of the confirmatory data for MRL review or in the renewal of the approval of the active substance, and the residue definition derived for processed commodities should still be considered tentative (EFSA, 2022).

Magnitude of residues in plants

Magnitude of residues in primary crops

In support of the intended NEU GAPs on cane fruits and other small fruits and berries, the applicant submitted a total of 10 GAP‐compliant and independent residue trials performed in raspberry (4), currant (5) and blueberry (1). The trials were conducted in Germany (9) and Austria (1), in 2021 and 2022. The trials consisted of one foliar treatment at a nominal application rate of 180 g a.s./ha, performed at BBCH 55–89. Samples were collected 3 days after the treatment, corresponding to the normal commercial preharvest interval (PHI). Half of the trials (5 trials) were conducted as decline trials, with samples collected before application (1 trial), 0 (4 trials), 1, 3 (corresponding to the PHI), 4 and 7 days after the treatment. Decline was observed in all the decline trials.

The samples were analysed for the parent compound (according to the residue definitions for enforcement and risk assessment) and the metabolite acequinocyl‐OH. The samples of these residue trials were stored under conditions for which stability of the residues has been demonstrated. According to the assessment of the EMS, the methods used were sufficiently validated and fit for purpose (Germany, 2025). The analytical methods used for data generation use the same solvent system of the enforcement method, for which extraction efficiency has been demonstrated in a previous assessment (EFSA, 2025a) (see also Section 1.1.4).

The applicant proposed to extrapolate residue data from raspberries to the group of cane fruit and from a combined data set on currants and blueberries to the group of other small fruits and berries. Such an extrapolation is acceptable according to currently applicable EU guidelines (European Commission, 2023b). The available results indicate residue concentrations of acequinocyl ranging between 0.072 and 0.37 mg/kg in raspberries and between 0.051 and 0.42 mg/kg in other small fruits and berries (currants and blueberries). The submitted data are sufficient to derive MRL proposals of 0.8 mg/kg for acequinocyl in cane fruits (based on extrapolation from raspberries) and other small fruits and berries (based on extrapolation from currants and blueberries).

Magnitude of residues in rotational crops

As the intended uses are on semi‐permanent crops, investigation of the magnitude of residues in rotational crops is not triggered. Additionally, based on the very low persistence exhibited by acequinocyl and its metabolites in the field soil degradation studies (DT90 < 100 days), residue levels in rotational crops would not be expected to exceed 0.01 mg/kg (see also Section 1.1.2).

Magnitude of residues in processed commodities

The contribution of cane fruits and other small fruits and berries to the ADI is below 10% when considering the proposed uses (see Section 4) and processing studies would in principle not be required (Regulation (EU) No 544/2011 11 ). However, two processing studies performed with strawberries were submitted within the current application. The same studies were assessed in the framework of a previous assessment, where tentative processing factors were derived (EFSA, 2025a). The available data indicated a decrease of acequinocyl residue concentrations in strawberries processed fractions such as pasteurised juice, canned fruit and pasteurised jam (see Appendix B.1.2.3). Robust PFs could not be derived because of the limited number of trials (2) and because the residue definitions in processed products are still tentative. Therefore, these PFs were not used for consumer risk assessment.

Considering the margin of safety for cane fruits and other small fruits and berries with regard to the consumer dietary exposure to acequinocyl (i.e. total theoretical maximum daily intake (TMDI) below 10% of the ADI), additional data were not deemed necessary for the present assessment.

Proposed MRLs

The available data are considered sufficient to derive MRL proposals of 0.8 mg/kg as well as risk assessment values for acequinocyl in cane fruits (based on extrapolation from raspberries) and other small fruits and berries (based on extrapolation from currants and blueberries). In Section 4, EFSA assessed whether residues on these commodities resulting from the intended uses are likely to pose a consumer health risk. It is noted that, for blueberries, cranberries, currants and gooseberries, a higher MRL (2 mg/kg) was proposed in the context of a previous assessment based on an indoor EU use (EFSA, 2025b); however, this proposal has not yet been implemented in the regulation.

RESIDUES IN LIVESTOCK

Not relevant, as the crops of interest are not used for feed purposes.

RESIDUES IN HONEY

Nature of residues in honey

Honey is produced by bees from sugary secretions of plants (floral nectar mainly) through regurgitation, enzymatic conversion and water evaporation and followed by storage in the bee hives for a certain period.

In the absence of specific metabolism studies with honey bees, studies investigating the nature of residues in primary crops and rotational crops and studies investigating the degradation during pasteurisation should be considered to determine the nature of residues in honey (European Commission, 2018). It is likely that the nature of residues in pollen and nectar collected from primary and rotational crops, as well as in honey (resulting from the residues in floral nectar), is the same as in primary and rotational crops.

It is noted that studies on the nature of residues in rotational crops and on the degradation of the active substance during pasteurisation are not available for the substance under assessment. However, considering that sufficient data investigating the metabolic profile in primary crops are available, no further information is required for the current application according to the technical guidelines (European Commission, 2018).

Analytical methods for enforcement in honey

No method for enforcement of acequinocyl residues in honey was submitted in the context of the current application (Germany, 2025). This is not needed considering that a modification of the existing MRL, which is set at the LOQ of 0.05 mg/kg, is not proposed.

Storage stability of residues in honey

The storage stability of residues of acequinocyl and its metabolite acequinocyl‐OH in honey samples stored under frozen conditions was investigated in the current MRL application according to the technical guidelines on storage stability (OECD, 2025). In addition, the storage stability of residues of acequinocyl was investigated in nectar, pollen, flowers and aqueous spray solutions stored under frozen conditions (Germany, 2025). It was demonstrated that residues of acequinocyl and acequinocyl‐OH were stable in honey for at least 3 months when stored at ≤ −18°C. Similarly, residues of acequinocyl were stable for at least 3 months in nectar and aqueous spray solutions under the same storage conditions whereas stability was not observed in pollen and flowers under the investigated conditions (see Appendix B.1.1.2).

Proposed residue definitions

In the absence of specific metabolism studies on honey, the studies investigating the nature of residues in primary crops, the degradation of the active substance during pasteurisation and, where relevant, the nature of residues in rotational crops are considered to derive the residue definitions for honey. Furthermore, taking into account the information from the overdosed semi‐field trials provided with this application, which showed quantifiable residues of acequinocyl and no detectable residues of acequinocyl‐OH (see Appendix B.3.2), the residue definitions established for primary crops are considered applicable to honey.

Magnitude of residues in honey

Studies investigating the magnitude of residues in honey following the intended uses on cane fruits and other small fruits and berries were submitted in the current application (Germany, 2025). Four semi‐field residue trials were conducted in 2021 in Germany (2 trials) and Spain (2 trials) using Phacelia tanacetifolia as a surrogate melliferous crop. In each trial, acequinocyl was applied once at application rates in the range of 329–337 g a.s/ha at growth stages BBCH 61–63. The trials are overdosed (1.8–1.9N) compared to the intended GAPs on cane fruits and other small fruits and berries (1 × 180 g a.s./ha); however, they show no exceedance of the existing MRL, which is set at the LOQ of 0.05 mg/kg. Samples were analysed for parent acequinocyl and acequinocyl‐OH.

Honey samples were collected at maturity (2 trials) or after artificial drying (to reach water content < 20%; 2 trials). The weight of treated samples ranged between 19 and 190 g. The amount of honey harvested in two trials (19 and 26.4 g) was below the recommended honey amount of at least 100 g honey (European Commission, 2018). However, it is commonly acknowledged that this sample weight is difficult to obtain under semi‐field conditions. This is considered only as a minor deviation not affecting the validity of the trials.

Residues of acequinocyl in honey were in the range between < 0.003 (limit of detection, LOD) and 0.017 mg/kg, whereas residues of acequinocyl‐OH were < 0.003 mg/kg (LOD) in all honey samples. The samples of these residue trials were stored for no longer than 28 days at ≤ −18°C, under conditions for which the integrity of the samples has been demonstrated (see Appendix B.3.1.2). According to the assessment of the EMS, the analytical methods used are sufficiently validated for the quantification of residues of acequinocyl and acequinocyl‐OH and are fit for purpose (Germany, 2025). Information on extraction efficiency of the analytical methods for data generation in honey samples is not available and is not required according to the guidance document on extraction efficiency (European Commission, 2023c).

Proposed MRLs

The residue trials available do not comply with the critical GAP for honey (corresponding to intended GAP for cane fruits and other small fruits and berries as reported in Appendix A) and, consequently, cannot be used to propose an MRL modification. Nonetheless, the data set demonstrates that all residue levels fall below the existing MRL of 0.05 mg/kg (LOQ), with a calculated MRL value of 0.04 mg/kg (see Appendix B.3.2.1).

It should be noted that, currently, MRLs set for honey are not applicable to other apicultural products according to Annex I of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 as amended by Commission Regulation (EU) 2018/62. 12

CONSUMER RISK ASSESSMENT

EFSA performed a dietary risk assessment using revision 3.1 of the EFSA PRIMo (EFSA, 2018, 2019). This exposure assessment model contains food consumption data for different subgroups of the EU population and allows the acute and chronic exposure assessment to be performed in accordance with the internationally agreed methodology for pesticide residues (FAO, 2016).

The toxicological reference values for acequinocyl used in the risk assessment (i.e. acute reference dose (ARfD) 0.08 mg/kg body weight (bw) and acceptable daily intake (ADI) 0.023 mg/kg bw per day) were derived in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review (European Commission, 2014, revised in 2022).

Short‐term (acute) dietary risk assessment

The short‐term exposure assessment was performed in accordance with the internationally agreed methodology and focused on the commodities assessed in this application (FAO, 2016). The calculations were based on the highest residue (HR) derived from supervised field trials on raspberries, currants and blueberries and the semi‐field trials for honey. For honey, the calculations relied on the highest residue (0.017 mg/kg) as derived from overdosed semi‐field trials. EFSA decided to use the HR instead of the existing default LOQ of 0.05 mg/kg, which would be likely to overestimate the actual consumer exposure. The complete list of input values can be found in Appendix D.1.

The short‐term exposure did not exceed the ARfD for any of the commodities assessed in this application. The highest short‐term exposure accounted for 13.1% of ARfD for blueberries and was lower for the other commodities under assessment.

For processed commodities derived from the commodities under assessment (juice and jam), a default processing factor of 1 was considered, assuming the same residue concentration would occur in juice, jam and raw agricultural commodities. Under this assumption, the highest acute exposure for the processed commodities was identified for currant juice (18.2% ARfD) and was lower for elderberry juice and rose hip jam (see Section B.4). It should be noted that these exposure results are indicative only, as a different residue definition for risk assessment was tentatively derived for processed commodities compared with primary crops, in the framework of the MRL review (EFSA, 2020).

Long‐term (chronic) dietary risk assessment

The long‐term exposure assessment was performed taking into account the supervised trials median residue (STMR) values derived for the commodities assessed in this application. For honey, the calculations relied on the STMR (0.007 mg/kg) as derived from overdosed semi‐field trials. EFSA decided to use the STMR instead of the existing default LOQ of 0.05 mg/kg, which would be likely to overestimate the actual consumer exposure. For the remaining commodities covered by the MRL regulation, the existing STMR values derived in the framework of the MRL review and previous MRL applications, as well as from a fast‐track procedure on kaki/Japanese persimmons, were selected as input values (EFSA, 2020, 2022, 2025a, 2025b, European Commission, 2023a, 2023b, 2023c, 13 ). The complete list of input values is presented in Appendix D.1. This exposure calculation includes also commodities for which Article 12 confirmatory data gaps have so far not been addressed, and therefore, this exposure calculation shall be considered indicative.

The highest estimated long‐term dietary intake was 9% of the ADI (NL toddler diet). The highest estimated contribution of residues from the crops of interest to the overall long‐term exposure was 0.74% of the ADI for currants (NL toddler) whereas lower contributions were estimated for the other commodities under assessment (see Section B.4).

Although uncertainties remain due to the data gaps identified in the review of existing MRLs (EFSA, 2020), this indicative exposure calculation did not identify a risk to consumers' health. Based on these calculations, EFSA concluded that the long‐term intake of residues of acequinocyl resulting from the intended uses is unlikely to present a risk to consumer health.

For further details on the exposure calculations, a screenshot of the Report sheet of the PRIMo is presented in Appendix C.

CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS

The data submitted in support of this MRL application were found to be sufficient to derive MRL proposals of 0.8 mg/kg for cane fruits and other small fruits and berries, based on the NEU uses. A higher MRL (2 mg/kg) was proposed (not yet implemented) for blueberries, cranberries, currants (black, red and white) and gooseberries (green, red and yellow) in the context of a previous MRL assessment, based on indoor GAPs (EFSA, 2025b).

For honey, the data set provided (even if overdosed) indicates that no exceedance of the existing MRL, set at the LOQ of 0.05 mg/kg, is expected following the intended NEU uses on cane fruits and other small fruits and berries; therefore, no modification of the current MRL is required.

Although uncertainties remain due to the data gaps identified in the review of existing MRLs (EFSA, 2020), based on the indicative exposure calculations, EFSA concluded that the proposed use of acequinocyl on the commodities under assessment will not result in a consumer exposure exceeding the toxicological reference values and therefore is unlikely to pose a risk to consumers' health.

The MRL recommendations are summarised in Appendix B.5.

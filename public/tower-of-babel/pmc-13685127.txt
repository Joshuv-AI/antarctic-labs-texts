In vitro and in vivo antibacterial activity of pravibismane against Mycobacteroides abscessus
Authors: Amy Leestemaker-Palmer, Bailey Keefe, Luiz E. Bermudez, Brett H. J. Baker
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The increasing prevalence of non-tuberculous mycobacterial lung infection represents a global challenge. Infections caused by Mycobacteroides abscessus commonly occur in individuals with underlying predisposing lung diseases such as cystic fibrosis (CF), non-CF-bronchiectasis, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and tuberculosis (TB). The unmet need in the treatment of M. abscessus pulmonary infection is driven by multidrug-resistance, ability to form biofilms, lengthy treatment, and drug-related side effects. Pravibismane was evaluated for its potential to reduce M. abscessus burden in vitro and in vivo. Pravibismane exhibited significantly lower (16- to 32-fold) minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) compared to amikacin. Treatment of THP-1 M. abscessus-infected macrophages with pravibismane led to a rapid reduction in intracellular M. abscessus while maintaining the viability of the THP-1 macrophages. Pravibismane demonstrated to be well-tolerated and efficacious in a chronic mouse M. abscessus lung infection model in which 28-day treatment with inhaled pravibismane substantially reduced the burden of M. abscessus in murine lung tissues. Overall, pravibismane exhibited promising efficacy by reducing intracellular M. abscessus levels in vitro and in a chronic mouse infection model. These preclinical results warrant investigation of pravibismane’s ability to treat M. abscessus lung infection in relevant patient populations.

Introduction

Patients with chronic lung conditions, such as CF, non-CF bronchiectasis, COPD, and TB have increased risk of developing pulmonary bacterial infections, including infection caused by non-tuberculous mycobacteria (NTM) [1–3]. NTM invades the lungs and causes infection, inflammation, and lung tissue damage, thus posing a serious threat to human health. Pulmonary infection caused by organisms of the Mycobacteroides abscessus species (formerly known as Mycobacterium abscessus) has increasingly become a complication for patients with underlying lung diseases such as CF, bronchiectasis, and emphysema, or those with a weakened immune system [4, 5]. M. abscessus is commonly isolated from environmental sources, and the presence of certain metals in the environment, such as molybdenum, has been associated with infectivity [6, 7]. M. abscessus is also resistant to the action of the majority of antibiotics, and therefore, treatment of the infection can be clinically challenging in individuals with lung pathology [8]. In addition, M. abscessus infection in the lung is associated with the formation of biofilms, which complicates treatment since few antibiotics have activity against M. abscessus biofilms [9, 10].

M. abscessus presents in two morphotypes, characterized as rough or smooth. The rough morphotype seems to be associated with increased virulence and resistance to antibiotics, while the smooth morphotype is efficient in establishing infections; both of which are isolated from infected patients [11]. The smooth form is associated with early colonization by adhering to host tissues, inhibiting phagocytosis, and promoting biofilm formation. The smooth morphotype is able to evade immune surveillance, allowing it to persist and spread in lung tissue before potentially switching to the more aggressive, destructive and antibiotic-resistant rough morphotype. Chronic lung infection with M. abscessus has been shown to be associated with lung function decline [12–14].

Pravibismane is a novel, first in a new class of therapeutics comprised of a compact, covalently bound, bridged structure of bismuth and thiols. A topical formulation of pravibismane is currently being evaluated in clinical studies as a potential therapy for the treatment of diabetic foot ulcer infections (DFI), the leading cause of diabetes-related hospitalizations. In a Phase 1b clinical study conducted to evaluate the safety of topical pravibismane in DFI during a 3-week (3x/week) treatment period, topical pravibismane was safe and well-tolerated at all doses tested and exhibited a trend reducing wound size [15]. In a follow-on Phase 2a clinical study, conducted to evaluate safety and to evaluate a range of efficacy endpoints during a 12 week (3x/week) treatment period, topical pravibismane was safe and well-tolerated at a dose over 3-fold higher than the highest dose in the preceding Phase 1b study. Subjects treated with pravibismane exhibited a trend for achieving a higher level of complete wound closure than those treated with standard of care. In addition, the safety and tolerability of locally administered pravibismane have been evaluated in a Phase 2a study in the treatment of orthopedic device-related infections. Pravibismane was safe and well-tolerated and exhibited a trend for decreased treatment failure rate in treatment of contaminated hardware and bone after fracture fixation compared to a placebo. Pravibismane has been administered to over 340 human subjects to date without drug-related safety or tolerability adverse events. Pharmacokinetic (PK) studies conducted in each of 6 clinical studies conducted to date have demonstrated that pravibismane is not appreciably absorbed into systemic circulation, defusing systemic concerns such as drug-drug interactions and systemic toxicity that are otherwise associated with systemically distributed drugs. Combined, these characteristics strongly underscore pravibismane’s potential as a well-tolerated and promising therapeutic drug.

Pravibismane has demonstrated the ability to collapse membrane potential in E. coli without inducing membrane leakage, and it substantially reduces bacterial concentrations of ATP in E. coli, S. aureus, M. avium, and P. aeruginosa. This bioenergetic disruption (reduction of bacterial ATP availability) has similarly been demonstrated in biofilms of P. aeruginosa and E. coli [16]. Pravibismane has very broad-spectrum activity against both sensitive and multidrug-resistant bacterial pathogens, and has also shown to have potent, broad spectrum anti-biofilm activity at concentrations equal or near the minimum inhibitory concentrations (MIC) of bacteria in their planktonic form (in both Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria) including biofilms produced by M. abscessus [17]. The ability for M. abscessus to form biofilms may be responsible for the persistence of lung infections; biofilms often persist despite immune response mechanisms and treatment with most commercially available antibiotics.

In the current study, the activity of pravibismane was evaluated in vitro and in an in vivo inhalation mouse model system against chronic M. abscessus pulmonary infections. Pravibismane showed robust in vitro activity against clinically relevant smooth and rough strains of M. abscessus, and robust, statistically significant efficacy against chronic in vivo M. abscessus lung infection in a murine model.

Results

Survival of M. abscessus in pravibismane-treated macrophages

The activity of pravibismane against M. abscessus strains was determined by MIC analyses. Pravibismane was evaluated in two physical states, namely solution (1% DMSO-“solution”) and “suspension” (HBSS), and was compared to amikacin, an antibiotic that is used to treat certain NTM infections [17]. To determine the MICs of pravibismane and comparator drug amikacin, three M. abscessus strains were utilized: a standard lab strain M. abscessus strain 19,977 and two CF patient isolates (CF) 00703 and 01715. Amikacin exhibited MICs of 32, 64, and 16 (µg/mL) in M. abscessus strains 19,977, 00703, and 01715 respectively. Notably, the MICs exhibited by pravibismane separately in solution and in suspension were 16–32-fold lower compared to amikacin (Table 1).

Minimal inhibitory concentrations of multiple anti-infectives against M. abscessus strains

To compare the infection capacity of the two CF patient isolates, human THP-1 macrophages were infected with three M. abscessus strains (described in the methods) and intracellular bacteria were evaluated 1 h post-infection. All three M. abscessus strains were selected for use in this study; CF patient isolate 01715 was slightly less effective at infecting THP-1 macrophages but not significantly different than CF strain 00703 or 19,977 (Fig. 1A). To investigate the efficacy of pravibismane to reduce intracellular M. abscessus levels in vitro, M. abscessus infected THP-1 cells were treated with MIC concentrations of either pravibismane formulations once, or repeatedly treated with amikacin every 24 h, then allowing the bacteria to incubate for 72 h. To determine any potential effect of drug combination on the outcome of infection, amikacin was combined with pravibismane solution. Pravibismane treatment resulted in statistically significant reduction in M. abscessus levels compared to the vehicle control. In case of strain 19,977, pravibismane solution alone or in combination with amikacin resulted in lower CFUs compared to vehicle control (Kruskal-Wallis test, p = 0.037 and p = 0.002 respectively, Fig. 1B). Similarly, in CF patient strain 01715 and 00703, CFU levels were lower in THP-1 cells treated with pravibismane solution (p = 0.029 and p = 0.046 respectively) and combination of amikacin and pravibismane solution (p = 0.002 and p = 0.002 respectively, Fig. 1C-D) compared to vehicle control. Overall, pravibismane treatment reduced M. abscessus levels by at least two logs but was most effective against the CF patient isolates, resulting in a 3-log reduction. There was no observable difference in efficacy between the physical formulations (solution or suspension) of pravibismane. Viability of the THP-1 cells was maintained with no substantial THP-1 cell lysis was observed with pravibismane treatment at the concentrations used in this study (based on LDH assay, data not shown). Treatment with amikacin every 24 h was able to reduce intracellular bacterial load compared to no antimicrobial intervention (vehicle), but the reduction was comparatively less than the single, one-time treatment of much lower concentrations of pravibismane. These data suggest that the combination of pravibismane with amikacin results in incrementally better intracellular killing of M. abscessus than either agent alone (p ≤ 0.01). Further indicating that at concentrations of pravibismane (solution or suspension) determined to be efficacious in killing intracellular M. abscessus, human THP-1 macrophage cellular viability is maintained. All other treatment comparisons for M. abscessus strains were not statistically significant.

Since pravibismane reduces ATP levels in bacterial cells, we sought to determine if there were any observable physical changes to the bacteria themselves after exposure to pravibismane. THP-1 macrophages were infected with M. abscessus then subsequently treated with 1 µg/mL pravibismane suspension for 24 h then visualized using transmission electron microscopy (TEM). When macrophages were infected with M. abscessus without anti-infective treatment, typical bacterial phenotypes (consistent with chronic intracellular infections) were observed. Bacteria were encapsulated in autophagosomes and remained completely intact inside the macrophages (Fig. 2A-B). When macrophages were infected with M. abscessus and treated with 1 µg/mL pravibismane suspension, phenotypic differences were observed, as described below. Again, bacteria were encapsulated in autophagosomes (Fig. 2C-D).

Bacteria inside the autophagosomes initially (within 24 h) showed destabilization of the cell wall at the polar regions of M. abscessus (Fig. 2D), which progressed to involve more of the whole bacterial cell wall at 48 h. Also observed were small particles inside the autophagosomes that were not seen without pravibismane treatment. This observation suggests that pravibismane particles are likely penetrating the macrophages and accumulating where the bacteria reside thus increasing the potential for active pravibismane to affect bacteria (Fig. 2D). A macrophage that was neither infected nor treated with pravibismane showed typical cellular structures (Fig. 2E). These data demonstrate the efficacy of pravibismane to disrupt the typical M. abscessus ability to evade host killing mechanisms inside human macrophages.

Inhaled pravibismane treatment of a chronic lung infection murine model

Pulmonary infections with M. abscessus are notoriously difficult to treat due to innate drug resistance mechanisms as well as its ability to establish biofilms in the lung. To determine the efficacy of pravibismane against M. abscessus, SCID/beige mice were infected with M. abscessus 19,977 and the infection was left to establish in the lung for 3 weeks. Subsequently, treatment arms utilizing 0.2 mg/kg/day pravibismane suspension (low), 1 mg/kg/day pravibismane suspension (high), or amikacin (100 mg/kg/day) were inhaled 6 days/week for 28 days.

Mice administered with high pravibismane dose exhibited a statistically significant reduction in pulmonary M. abscessus levels compared to vehicle only treated mice (p = 0.016, Fig. 3A). Inhaled amikacin treatment was not bactericidal for M. abscessus in the lungs, thus showing an advantage with pravibismane treatment for this bacterial lung infection by overcoming either resistance mechanisms or by compromising the M. abscessus capability of establishing biofilms in the lung environment. To measure the extra-pulmonary bacterial burden, M. abscessus load in the spleen of infected mice was compared. There was no change in the spleen bacterial burden in any treatment groups tested (Fig. 3B). All other treatment comparisons were not statistically significant.

The frequency of spontaneous mutation to 4 µg/mL pravibismane was 10− 9, demonstrating an extremely low propensity for the development of bacterial resistance in response to pravibismane treatment, similar to the spontaneous mutation frequency of vancomycin (internal Microbion data), an antibiotic that has demonstrated long-term clinical efficacy (Table 2). In comparison, bacteria grown in ‘7H9 broth only’ showed enormous expansion at all concentrations used based on optical density readings. Previous studies have demonstrated the importance of this observation, although in the case of M. abscessus, there still is not an ideal positive control [17, 18]. This result suggests the potential for long term clinical utility of pravibismane in the treatment of M. abscessus.

Discussion

The results of this study indicate that pravibismane is bactericidal against both the smooth and rough phenotypes of M. abscessus inside cultured macrophages (intracellular infections), in broth, and in a chronic M. abscessus mouse model. Amikacin alone did not show activity against M. abscessus within THP-1 cells, however, pravibismane delivered in combination with amikacin was effective against this intracellular pathogen. Pravibismane’s effect on the M. abscessus bacterial cell wall infecting the THP-1 human macrophage cell line can be observed within 24 h following pravibismane delivery to infected macrophages, with the intracellular bacteria showing loss of cell wall integrity. These data demonstrate that pravibismane can penetrate macrophages and it appears to concentrate inside the eukaryotic cell. Small particles were observed by electron microscopy only inside vacuoles in THP-1 macrophages that had been treated with pravibismane (likely representing pravibismane particles). Due to the known slow solubilization of pravibismane particles, this finding is consistent with the observation that monolayers treated once, one day after infection, maintain a progressive killing activity for 4 days (data not shown).

The experiments in mice with M. abscessus lung infection, and treated with an inhaled pravibismane suspension, demonstrated that the compound exhibits statistically significant activity against the pathogen when delivered at a dose of 1 mg/kg/day, compared to amikacin activity delivered at a dose of 100 mg/kg/day. Amikacin was utilized as a positive control in this study due to lack of an appropriate positive control in cases of M. abscessus including hardships with acquiring liposomal amikacin. Pravibismane’s activity in the airways may have been better than amikacin due to the ability of pravibismane to both inhibit biofilm formation, and to eradicate established biofilms [17].

M. abscessus is known to form biofilms in the airways [9, 10], which has been demonstrated to occur in the model utilized herein [18]. Recent study in the laboratory has also shown that M. abscessus rapidly forms biofilms in the presence of the mucus environment of patients with cystic fibrosis [10]. Amikacin without liposome preparation has little activity in biofilms, which may explain the results observed in our experiments [19].

It is desirable for an anti-infective compound to not trigger resistance-related mechanisms in a bacterial population. The observation that only 1 in 109 organisms express some resistance in a population shows that the infecting inoculum needs to be very high to contain a resistant clone of.

M. abscessus. Clarithromycin, for example, an antibiotic very active against Mycobacterium avium, has an emergence of resistance of 10− 7 to 10− 8 [20, 21]. It is therefore potentially important that the frequency of spontaneous mutation to pravibismane was 10− 9, demonstrating a 10-100-fold lower propensity than clarithromycin for the development of bacterial resistance in response to long-term pravibismane treatment. This result suggests the potential for long-term clinical utility of pravibismane in the treatment of M. abscessus.

Therapy of pulmonary infection by M. abscessus is challenging. Currently, it depends on injectable antibiotics for periods of 1–2 months, if not longer [13, 15]. New drugs with activity against M. abscesssus have been evaluated, some of them taken for the anti-tuberculosis regimens, such as bedaquiline [20]. Aerosolized amikacin has been shown to be effective in many cases of M. avium and more recently in some cases of M. abscessus lung infections [22, 23], and new tetracyclines such as omadacycline have been evaluated in clinics. Despite these advances, active compounds are needed, either to be delivered orally or by aerosol. One of the main concerns with M. abscessus is its ability to form biofilms, which makes it more refractory to therapy. One of the advantages of inhaled pravibismane therapy is the ability to achieve very high concentrations in the airways and deep lung tissue, with minimal to no systemic exposure, an observation already seen with inhaled liposomal amikacin [24, 25]. Moreover, pravibismane exhibits substantially lower MIC and minimum biofilm eradication concentration (MBEC) values compared to amikacin against M. abscessus, suggesting that pravibismane efficiently acts as an anti-infective and anti-biofilm agent against M. abscessus [16, 21].

A limitation of the study is the number of strains tested in vitro and in vivo, although the chosen strains represent the most common susceptibility to compounds used to treatment.

Conclusion

In summary, pravibismane is a new anti-infective with a novel mechanism of action, that can be delivered by aerosol inhalation and has shown very encouraging in vivo efficacy against a human pathogenic strain of M. abscessus with no clinical observations of distress or toxicity in mice treated for 28 days. Combined with the low propensity for the development of resistance against pravibismane, these results support further evaluation of inhaled pravibismane as a potential safe and efficacious therapeutic agent to treat M. abscessus pulmonary infections.

Materials and methods

Bacteria strains and tissue culture

M. abscessus subsp. abscessus strain 19,977 (smooth strain) was obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC). The bacterium was originally isolated from the skin of a patient and more recently has been identified as one of the few dominant clones in airways of patients with pulmonary infection. Other mycobacteria strains utilized included CF patient isolates from National Jewish Hospital (Denver, CO): CF patient strain DNA00703 and CF patient strain DNA01715 (both of rough morphotype). All strains were maintained on 7H10 Middlebrook media plates supplemented with 10% OADC (Oleic acid, Albumin, Dextrose, Catalase). For the assays, bacteria were suspended in Middlebrook 7H9 broth to the desired concentrations, pelleted with centrifugation (2,000 g x 15 min), washed and the final inoculum established in Hanks Balanced Salt Solution (HBSS).

THP-1 human monocytic cells (ATCC) were utilized for in vitro infection assays. THP-1 cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS). THP-1 cells were differentiated with 50 ng/mL of phorbol 12-myristiate-12 acetate (PMA) for 24 h. After removal of PMA, cells were allowed to rest in media for 24 h before use in assays as reported [24].

MIC of pravibismane against M. abscessus isolates

M. abscessus strains were used to determine MICs of pravibismane and amikacin. A range of high to low concentration of anti-infectives was used to determine bacterial susceptibility. M. abscessus strain 19,977, CF patient strain DNA00703, and CF patient strain DNA01715 achieved log phase (4 days) in 7H9 Middlebrook media supplemented with 10% OADC. Amikacin was diluted to 128 µg/mL in 7H9 Middlebrook broth supplemented with 10% OADC then diluted 1:1 until the concentration reached 1 µg/mL. For pravibismane, two formulations of compound were tested: pravibismane was solubilized in DMSO (solution) then diluted; pravibismane suspension was prepared in HBSS (suspension) then diluted. Each type of pravibismane formulation was diluted to 16 µg/mL in 7H9 broth supplemented with OADC then diluted 1:1 until the concentrations reached 0.125 µg/mL. The purity of pravibismane utilized in this study was assessed through Ultra-High Performance Liquid Chromatography, indicating a purity level of 99.1% (data not shown). Control tubes containing no anti-infectives were also included for each anti-infective tested. Inoculums of 109 of each strain of bacteria were measured with optical density (O.D.) at 595 nm to standardize experiments. From these inoculums, 105 bacteria were added to each tube and incubated for 5 days in a shaking incubator at 37 °C and 200 rpm. After incubation, 100 µL from each tube was transferred to a 96-well plate for O.D. reading. Reduction of turbidity (90%) determined the concentration required to reduce OD compared to drug free control. MICs were preformed twice. The assays were repeated three times and the number of bacteria in the inoculum verified by plating for CFU.

THP-1 macrophage survival assays

Differentiated THP-1 macrophages were infected with M. abscessus strains for 1 h with an MOI (multiplicity of infection) of 5, or uninfected [24]. Cells were washed twice, followed by treatment with 200 µg/mL of amikacin for 1 h to remove extracellular bacteria. Cells were washed again and either pravibismane or amikacin was added. After 72 h of anti-infective treatment, supernatant was removed, and cells were lysed with 0.1% triton x-100 in water. Macrophages were disrupted by pipetting approximately 25 times, then serially diluted, and plated for colony-forming unit (CFU) enumeration. A select number of macrophages were lysed after initial infection to quantify the bacterial invasion between three M. abscessus strains [18, 25].

A LDH assay was carried out to determine the existence of toxicity the compounds (data not shown).

Transmission electron microscopy preparation

THP-1 macrophages were differentiated with 50 ng/mL PMA for 24 h, followed by removal of PMA 24 h prior to infection. Differentiated THP-1 macrophages were infected with M. abscessus for 1 h with an MOI of 10. Macrophages were treated with 4 µg/mL pravibismane suspension once, as indicated. The time points for TEM sample preparation were 24 h and 48 h post infection. Cells were not washed prior to TEM sample preparation, to preserve any extracellular bacteria that may have been present. Cells were detached by treatment with 5 mM EDTA for 30 min, quenched with 1X HBSS, and suspended in fixative buffer with 2.5% glutaraldehyde, 1% formaldehyde, and 0.1 M sodium cacodylate for 24 h prior to submission to the electron microscopy facility for processing. Samples were sectioned, dehydrated, and visualized by a FEIT Titan 80–200 TEM/STEM microscope at Oregon State University Electron Microscopy Facility.

M. abscessus strain 19,977 chronic lung infection murine model

The SCID/Beige mouse model was utilized to investigate the impact of pravibismane treatment against chronic M. abscessus infection. The advantage of using SCID/Beige mice is that they develop high levels of exponentially dividing bacterial infection and develop organ histopathology that are typical in human NTM disease [18, 25]. Moreover, immune deficient mice allow for the development of infection allowing for detection of significant differences between untreated and drug treated groups.

The protocol for chronic lung infection mouse model was reviewed and approved by Oregon State University’s Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (protocol number IACUC-2021-0178). Sixty female SCID/Beige mice between 6 and 8 weeks of age were ordered from Charles River Laboratories. Upon arrival to the vivarium, mice were allowed 1 week of acclimation before infection. To establish a chronic lung infection, inoculums of M. abscessus strain 19,977 were prepared from highly virulent frozen stocks with low passage bacteria. Initially, bacteria were allowed to grow for 4 days on 7H10 media agar plates at 37 °C. Once confluent, inoculums were prepared by suspending bacteria at 109 in HBSS as determined by O.D. and further quantified by serial dilution to determine the CFU/mL of suspension.

On the day of infection, mice were anesthetized with isoflurane by the drop jar method. Briefly, mice were scruffed in a standard one-handed grip and the nose of the mouse was held over a tube containing gauze with a small amount of isoflurane. Mice were monitored for breathing and once rapid breaths slowed down, mice were removed from direct anesthesia and 108 of bacterial suspension was deposited on the left nostril. The mouse slowly inhaled the droplet and was held in hand until complete consciousness returned and then placed into its home cage. This inoculation procedure was repeated for every mouse. After infection was initiated, 3 weeks were allowed to pass to establish a chronic lung pathology.

After the M. abscessus infection was established, 12 mice were euthanized the day before start of treatment to determine baseline bacterial load. Mice were euthanized using CO2 in accordance with the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals (NRC 2011). Lungs and spleens were collected, homogenized, and serially diluted for CFU enumeration.

A nose-only inhalation exposure chamber from CH Technologies (Westwood, NJ) was used for inhalation exposure of anti-infectives in this experiment. Mice were split into four inhalation treatment groups: vehicle (buffer containing no pravibismane); amikacin (100 mg/kg/day); pravibismane suspension “low” (0.2 mg/kg/day); and pravibismane suspension “high” (1 mg/kg/day). Ten mice from each treatment group were used for CFU determination. Treatment was conducted 6 days a week over the course of 28 days total. A schematic of treatment timelines is shown in Fig. 4.

Inhalation dosing requires a respirable aerosol to ensure the drug reaches the lungs (as opposed to depositing in the conducting airways, oropharyngeal region, and sinuses). Respirable aerosols are those with < 5 microns mass median aerodynamic diameter (MMAD). The nebulizer used in this study was a commercially available device, the PARI LC STAR® which generates a respirable aerosol of < 4 microns MMAD. To calculate the dose that is deposited in the lungs of a mouse, an equation is used from a well-researched article [26, 27]. For rodents, the lung deposited dose is calculated as 10% of the “presented dose (aka. achieved dose)” because of the efficiency of rodents’ sinuses in filtering out small particles. For the purposes of this research project, doses are shown and discussed as presented doses. To create the correct level of deposited dose of each drug tested, 10 mL of test article was prepared. Amikacin at 20 mg/mL was prepared and stored at 4 °C until use. Pravibismane “high” at a concentration of 8 mg/mL was prepared once a week and stored at room temperature. Pravibismane “low” was diluted from 8 mg/mL to 1.6 mg/mL the day of treatment. Animals were restrained and placed within the rodent restraint tubes on the inhalation apparatus’ plenum. A respiratory aerosol delivery hose connected the nebulizer and the inhalation apparatus. A nebulizer-specific compressor was connected to the nebulizer and set to deliver a minimum of 20 PSIG pressure. The rate of air flow from the compressor to the nebulizer remained at 8.5 L/minute. Once on, the compressor pushed significant air through the entire system and generated a nebulized aerosol fog that each mouse breathed in for approximately 45 min. (Figure 5).

After 28 days, animals were euthanized by CO2 and secondary cardiac puncture was performed to collect whole blood for test article level determination. Mice lungs were split, weighed and snap frozen in liquid nitrogen to determine tissue concentration of pravibismane. Spleens were also deposited in formalin. Lungs and spleens from the mice were homogenized with 1:1 water: Dey-Engley neutralizing broth to deactivate pravibismane. CFUs were determined after serial dilution of organ homogenate [18, 28].

M. abscessus spontaneous mutation frequency associated with resistance to pravibismane

Spontaneous mutation frequency of M. abscessus associated with resistance to pravibismane was determined by using the bactericidal concentration of pravibismane solution (4 µg/mL) and differing concentrations of M. abscessus. This procedure determines the spontaneous mutation frequency in a suspension of bacteria. M. abscessus was grown on 7H10 agar at 37 °C for 3 days. A bacterial suspension of 1011 was prepared in phosphate buffered saline containing 0.05% tween 20. Ten tubes of 7H9 broth containing 4 µg/mL of pravibismane or 7H9 broth alone were prepared and the high bacterial suspension was added to one tube then serially diluted 1:10. After bacterial suspension was added and thoroughly mixed, tubes were incubated at 37 °C in a shaking incubator at 200 rpm for 3 days. Optical density determined the levels of bacterial growth by taking 100 µL of bacterial suspension and placed in a 96-well plate for reading on the Epoch (Biotek) spectrophotometer at 595 nm [18, 29, 30].

Statistical analysis

All data are presented as mean values ± SD of at least 3 biological replicates. For statistical analyses, Kruskal-Wallis test or analysis of variance for multiple comparisons were performed using GraphPad Prism (Boston, MA) software version 8. Differences were considered significant a t p < 0.05.

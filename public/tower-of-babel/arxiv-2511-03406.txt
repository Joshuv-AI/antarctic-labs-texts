Abstract
. In this article, first we give two formulae for the delta invariant of a complex curve singularity that can be embedded as a ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} -Cartier divisor in a normal surface singularity with rational homology sphere link. Next, we consider representable numerical semigroups, they are semigroups associated with normal weighted homogeneous surface singularities with rational homology sphere links (via the degrees of the homogeneous functions). We then prove that such a semigroup can be interpreted as the value semigroup of a generic orbit (as a curve singularity) given by the ℂ ∗ \mathbb{C}^{*} -action on the weighted homogeneous germ. Furthermore, we use the delta invariant formula to derive a combinatorially computable formula for the genus of representable semigroups. Finally, we characterize topologically those representable semigroups which are symmetric.

1.
Introduction

1.1.

Numerical semigroups play a crucial role in various areas of the theory of complex curve and surface singularities. A typical example is when the finite number of gaps in a numerical semigroup provides a combinatorial interpretation of an analytic or topological invariant of the singularity (this number being the genus of the semigroup). One example is the delta invariant of an irreducible curve singularity, which is the genus of the value semigroup associated with the curve.
While the semigroup interpretation of singularity invariants can be very valuable, the efficient computation of the main characteristics of numerical semigroups (like the
Frobenius number, genus, etc.) is generally not a simple task.
In fact, it is generally rather difficult to find closed formulae.

In this context, the second and third authors
[
14
]
have recently proposed a
research program which aims to link the theory of numerical semigroups and surface singularities.
Specifically, we consider a normal, weighted homogeneous surface germ and assume that its link is a rational homology sphere (
\mathbb{Q}HS^{3}
). We associate with this germ a numerical semigroup,
\mathcal{S}
,
formed by the degrees of the homogeneous elements of the coordinate ring of functions, see
4.2
. Since the link is
\mathbb{Q}HS^{3}
, a result of Pinkham
[
25
]
implies that
\mathcal{S}
is a topological invariant, it is computable from the corresponding Seifert structure encoded by the canonical equivariant resolution graph.
We say that a numerical semigroup is
representable

if it can be associated with such a resolution graph in this way.

An important natural questions asks
whether every numerical semigroup can be represented in this way, or if not, what
are the properties which obstruct the representability.
Though this problem is still open, the first and second authors characterized the representable semigroups as quotients of a special class of numerical semigroups, called the flat semigroups, see
[
1
]
. This is in some ways ‘analogue’ to the fact that every numerical semigroup can be written as a quotient of a symmetric semigroup (cf.
[
22
,
27
]
).

The importance of the above-mentioned representability problem lies in the fact that various invariants of the representable numerical semigroups can be calculated or interpreted using methods from singularity theory,
thus providing answers to important questions originally raised in the theory of numerical semigroups.
For example,
[
14
]
gives an affirmative answer to the Frobenius problem by providing a closed formula for the Frobenius number of a representable semigroup.

The present article provides a new step of this program.
We give a formula for the genus of a representable semigroup, which we identified with the delta invariant of the generic orbit curve singularity that appears on the weighted homogeneous surface singularity.

In the sequel we summarize the results of this paper.

1.2.

In terms of results, the article can be divided into two parts.

Section 3, as the first part of the article, is a generalization of the previous work of
[
3
,
4
]
. Namely, in Theorem
3.1
we give a formula (
11
) for the delta invariant of a curve singularity that can be embedded as a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor in a normal surface singularity with
\mathbb{Q}HS^{3}
link. This formula shows how the delta invariant depends on the embedded topology and the analytic type of the ambient surface.

With the additional assumption that the ambient space
(X,o)
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein,
we deduce formula (
12
), which in the second part
will be the key ingredient in the study of representable semigroups.
In order to prove this second formula, we need to establish some new results
valid for
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein normal surface singularities with
\mathbb{Q}HS^{3}
link. A vanishing theorem is provided in Proposition
3.3
and a duality result in Proposition
3.4
, which establishes an intriguing duality property (Corollary
3.5
) for the equivariant geometric genus of such germs.

The second part of the article begins with Section 4.
First, we briefly introduce the reader to the problem of representable semigroups. Then, in Theorem
4.1
, we prove that a representable numerical semigroup can be interpreted as the value semigroup of a generic orbit curve singularity defined by the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action of the corresponding weighted homogeneous surface singularity.
Then the results of the two parts combined provide the
desired formula for the genus of a representable semigroup.

We conclude the article in Section 5, where (based on the just proved genus formula and an earlier
expression of the Frobenius number) we characterize topologically
(using the Seifert invariants of the link of the weightd homogeneous singularity)
those representable semigroups which are symmetric.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Lattices associated with a resolution

We consider a complex normal surface singularity
(X,o)
and one of its good resolutions
\pi:\tilde{X}\to X
with dual resolution graph
\Gamma
whose set of vertices is denoted by
{\mathcal{V}}
. Let
\{E_{v}\}_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}}
be the irreducible components
of the exceptional set
\pi^{-1}(o)
.
We assume that the link
\Sigma
is a
rational homology sphere,
\mathbb{Q}HS^{3}
, i.e.

\Gamma
is a connected tree and all
E_{v}
are rational.

We define the lattice of integral cycles
L
as
H_{2}(\tilde{X},\mathbb{Z})
, endowed with the non-degenerate negative definite intersection form
(,)
. It is generated by the (classes of the) exceptional divisors
E_{v}
,
v\in{\mathcal{V}}
, that is,
L=\oplus_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}}\mathbb{Z}\langle E_{v}\rangle
. Note that in the homology exact sequence of the pair
(\tilde{X},\Sigma)
(where
\Sigma=\partial\tilde{X}
is the link of the singularity) one has

H_{2}(\Sigma,\mathbb{Z})=0
,
H_{1}(\tilde{X},\mathbb{Z})=0
, hence the exact sequence has the form:

(1)

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccc}0&\to&L&\to&H_{2}(\tilde{X},\Sigma,\mathbb{Z})&\to&H_{1}(\Sigma,\mathbb{Z})&\to&0.\\
\end{array}

The dual lattice
L^{\prime}:=\Hom(H_{2}(\tilde{X},\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{Z})
can be identified with
H_{2}(\tilde{X},\Sigma,\mathbb{Z})
by the Lefschetz–Poincaré duality, hence (
1
) implies

L^{\prime}/L\cong H_{1}(\Sigma,\mathbb{Z})
. This finite group will be denoted by
H
for simplicity.

L^{\prime}
embeds into the space of rational cycles
L_{{\mathbb{Q}}}:=L\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
and it can be identified with
\{l^{\prime}\in L_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\,:\,(l^{\prime},L)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\in\mathbb{Z}\}
, where
(\,,\,)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
is the extension of the intersection form to
L_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
. Hence, in the sequel we regard
L^{\prime}
as
\oplus_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}}\mathbb{Z}\langle E^{*}_{v}\rangle
,
the lattice generated by the rational cycles
E^{*}_{v}\in L_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
,

v\in{\mathcal{V}}
, where
(E_{u}^{*},E_{v})_{{\mathbb{Q}}}=-\delta_{u,v}
(Kronecker delta) for any
u,v\in{\mathcal{V}}
.
The inclusion
L\subset L^{\prime}
in the bases
\{E_{v}\}_{v}
and
\{E_{v}^{*}\}_{v}
is given by

-M
, where
M
is the intersection matrix of
L
, that is,

E_{v}=-\sum_{u\in{\mathcal{V}}}(E_{v},E_{u})E_{u}^{*}
. Since
M
is negative definite, the matrix
-M^{-1}

has positive entries, and the
E^{*}_{v}
’s are the columns of
-M^{-1}
.

The dual base elements
E^{*}_{v}
, as rational divisors, have the following
geometrical interpretation as well: consider
\gamma_{v}\subset\tilde{X}
a curvette associated with
E_{v}
, that is, a smooth
irreducible curve germ in
\tilde{X}
intersecting
E_{v}
transversally. Then

\pi^{*}\pi_{*}\gamma_{v}=\gamma_{v}+E_{v}^{*}
, where
\pi^{*}
and
\pi_{*}
are the natural divisorial operators.

Let
K_{\tilde{X}}
be the canonical divisor of the smooth complex analytic surface
\tilde{X}
,
and let
Z_{K}
— the
(anti-)canonical cycle
of
\pi
— be that rational cycle supported on the exceptional locus which has the same numerical properties as
-K_{\tilde{X}}
.
Hence, it can be determined combinatorially by the
linear system of
adjunction formulae

(2)

(Z_{K},E_{v})=(E_{v},E_{v})+2,\textrm{ for all }v\in{\mathcal{V}}.

In particular,
Z_{K}\in L^{\prime}
, and using (
2
) it can be also written as

(3)

Z_{K}-E=\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}}(\delta_{v}-2)E^{*}_{v}.

where
E=\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}}E_{v}
and
\delta_{v}
is the valency of
v
in
\Gamma
.

We say that
(X,o)
, or its topological type
\Sigma
, is
numerically Gorenstein
if
Z_{K}\in L
. Note that this property is independent of the resolution and it is equivalent with the topological triviality of the line bundle
\Omega^{2}_{X\setminus o}
of holomorphic
2
-forms on
X\setminus o
.
(X,o)
is called
Gorenstein
if
\Omega^{2}_{X\setminus o}
is holomorphically trivial.
More generally, we say that
(X,o)
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein
if

(\Omega^{2}_{X\setminus o})^{\otimes r}
is holomorphically trivial
for some
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. If
\Sigma
is a
\mathbb{Q}HS^{3}
then this property is equivalent with
\Omega^{2}_{\tilde{X}}=\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{X}}(K_{\tilde{X}})\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{X}}(-Z_{K})
, where
here
\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{X}}(-Z_{K})
denotes the
‘natural line bundle’ associated with
(-Z_{K})
.

Recall that for any
l^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}
we can consider the unique line bundle
{\mathcal{O}}_{\tilde{X}}(l^{\prime})
,
called the natural line bundle associated with
l^{\prime}
, which has the property that

({\mathcal{O}}_{\tilde{X}}(l^{\prime}))^{\otimes n}={\mathcal{O}}_{\tilde{X}}(nl^{\prime})
for any

n
with
nl^{\prime}\in L
. (In the definition of the natural line bundle the fact that
\Sigma
is a

\mathbb{Q}HS^{3}
is necessary.)

For more regarding this section see eg.
[
20
]
.

2.2.
Minimal
H
–representatives and the generalized Laufer algorithm

L_{\mathbb{Q}}
has a natural partial ordering defined coordinate-wise:
for
l^{\prime}_{1},l^{\prime}_{2}\in L_{\mathbb{Q}}
with
l^{\prime}_{i}=\sum_{v}l^{\prime}_{iv}E_{v}
(
i=\{1,2\}
) one
writes
l^{\prime}_{1}\geq l^{\prime}_{2}
whenever
l^{\prime}_{1v}\geq l^{\prime}_{2v}
for all
v\in{\mathcal{V}}
.
In particular,
l^{\prime}
is an effective rational cycle if
l^{\prime}\geq 0
. We set also
\min\{l^{\prime}_{1},l^{\prime}_{2}\}:=\sum_{v}\min\{l^{\prime}_{1v},l^{\prime}_{2v}\}E_{v}
.

Given an element
l^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}
we denote by
[l^{\prime}]\in H
its class in
H=L^{\prime}/L
.
The lattice
L^{\prime}
admits a partition parametrized by the group
H
, where for any
h\in H
one sets

L^{\prime}_{h}=\{l^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}\mid[l^{\prime}]=h\}\subset L^{\prime}
. In particular,
L^{\prime}_{0}=L
.
Given an
h\in H
one can define the unique minimal representative
r_{h}
of
h
in

L^{\prime}_{h}\cap L^{\prime}_{\geq 0}
. By coordinates,
r_{h}:=\sum_{v}l^{\prime}_{v}E_{v}
if and only if
[r_{h}]=h
and

0\leq l^{\prime}_{v}<1
for avery
v
.

The set
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{Q}}:=\{l^{\prime}\in L_{\mathbb{Q}}\ |\ (l^{\prime},E_{v})\leq 0\ \mbox{for all}\ v\in{\mathcal{V}}\}

is a cone generated over
\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
by
\{E^{*}_{v}\}_{v}
. Then
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}:={\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{Q}}\cap L^{\prime}
is a monoid of anti–nef rational cycles of
L^{\prime}
which is generated over
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
by the cycles
E^{*}_{v}
. It is called the
Lipman cone
.
It also admits a natural equivariant partition
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{h}={\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}\cap L^{\prime}_{h}
indexed by
H
. If
s_{1},s_{2}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{h}
then
\min\{s_{1},s_{2}\}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{h}
. Furthermore, for any
h
there exists a unique
minimal cycle

s_{h}:=\min\{{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{h}\}
, see eg.
[
17
]
.

In the sequel we describe the
generalized Laufer’s algorithm
(
[
17
, Lemma 7.4]
,
[
11
]
) that can be used to calculate
s_{h}
.

For any
l^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}
there exists a unique
minimal element
s(l^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
such that
l^{\prime}\leq s(l^{\prime})
and
[s(l^{\prime})]=[l^{\prime}]\in H
. It can be obtained by the following algorithm. Set
x_{0}:=l^{\prime}
. Then one constructs a computation sequence
\{x_{i}\}_{i}
as follows. If
x_{i}
is already constructed and
x_{i}\not\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
then there exists some
E_{u_{i}}
such that
(x_{i},E_{u_{i}})>0
. In this case take
x_{i+1}:=x_{i}+E_{u_{i}}
(for some choice of
E_{u_{i}}
). Then the procedure after finitely many steps stops, say at
x_{t}
, (ie. when

x_{t}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
) and necessarily
x_{t}=s(l^{\prime})
.

The point is that
r_{h}\leq s_{h}
and
s(r_{h})=s_{h}
, hence the above algorithm produces
s_{h}
from

r_{h}
. However, in general
r_{h}\neq s_{h}
.
This fact does not contradict the minimality of
s_{h}
in
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{h}
since
r_{h}
might not sit in
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{h}
.

Note also that if
l^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}_{\leq 0}
, then
s(l^{\prime})=s_{[l^{\prime}]}
.

2.3.
The Riemann-Roch function

For any fixed
h\in H
we define the (equivariant) Riemann-Roch function as follows

(4)

\chi_{h}:L\to\mathbb{Z},\ \ \ \chi_{h}(l)=(Z_{K}-2s_{h}-l,l)/2.

Note that
Z_{K}-2s_{h}\in L^{\prime}
is a ‘characteristic element’ (cf.
[
20
, 6.3.7]
) in the sense that one satisfies
(Z_{K}-2s_{h}-l,l)\in 2\mathbb{Z}
, hence
\chi_{h}
is well defined. In fact, if we define the (full) Riemann-Roch function by
\chi:L^{\prime}\to\mathbb{Z}
,
\chi(\ell^{\prime}):=(Z_{K}-l^{\prime},l^{\prime})/2
for any
l^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}
then
\chi_{h}(l)=\chi(l+s_{h})-\chi(s_{h})
, hence
\{\chi_{h}\}_{h}
is a conveniently chosen equivariant presentation of
\chi
(restricted to the subsets
L^{\prime}_{h}
). Clearly, if
h=0
then
\chi_{0}(l)=\chi(l)
.
Recall also that by the Riemann-Roch theorem

\chi(l)=h^{0}({\mathcal{O}}_{l})-h^{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{l})

for any effective
l\in L_{>0}
.

3.
The delta invariant of a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier curve

3.1.

Let
(X,o)
be a normal surface singularity with
\mathbb{Q}HS^{3}
link and
(C,o)\subset(X,o)
a reduced curve on it. Assume further that
C
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on
(X,o)
, ie.
nC\subset X
is a Cartier divisor for some
n>0
, or equivalently, the class of
(C,o)
has finite order in the local divisor class group
Cl(X,o)
.

We fix a good embedded resolution
\pi:\tilde{X}\to X
of the pair
C\subset X
. Let
\pi^{*}(C)=\tilde{C}+l^{\prime}_{C}
be the total transform of
C
, where
\tilde{C}
is the strict transform and
l^{\prime}_{C}\in L^{\prime}
is the rational cycle associated with the curve. This means
that
\pi^{*}(C)
is numerically trivial: the divisorial intersection of
\pi^{*}(C)
with
E_{v}
is zero for every
v\in\mathcal{V}
.

The divisor
\tilde{C}
satisfies
c_{1}\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{X}}(-\tilde{C})=l^{\prime}_{C}
in
H^{2}(\tilde{X},\mathbb{Z})\approx L^{\prime}
and,
since
C
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier,
the linear equivalence
n(-\tilde{C})\sim nl^{\prime}_{C}
, where
n
is the order of
l^{\prime}_{C}
in
H
. Furthermore, since the link is a
\mathbb{Q}HS^{3}
,
\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{X}}(-\tilde{C})
is unique with these properties, hence
\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{X}}(-\tilde{C})\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{X}}(l^{\prime}_{C})
, see eg.
[
20
, Prop. 6.1.16]
(where
\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{X}}(l^{\prime}_{C})
is the natural line bundle associated with
l^{\prime}_{C}
).

Now, we regard
(C,o)
as an abstract reduced curve germ with irreducible decomposition
(C,o)=\bigcup_{i\in I}(C_{i},o)
(
I
is finite). Let
\gamma_{i}:(\mathbb{C},0)\to(C,o),t\mapsto\gamma_{i}(t)
be the normalization of the branch
(C_{i},o)
and for any
g\in\mathcal{O}_{(C,o)}
we consider its value
v(g):=(v_{i}(g))_{i\in I}
defined by
v_{i}(g):=\textrm{ord}_{t}(g(\gamma_{i}(t)))
for any
i\in I
. Then the multigerm
(C,o)^{\widetilde{}}:=(\mathbb{C},0)^{|I|}
together with the map
(C,o)^{\widetilde{}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\gamma:=(\gamma_{i})_{i}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(C,o)
serve as the normalization. This defines the delta invariant of
(C,o)
as
\delta(C,o):=\dim\gamma_{*}\mathcal{O}_{(C,0)^{\widetilde{}}}/\mathcal{O}_{C,0}
.

3.2.
The main formulae

In this section we present two formulae for the delta invariant of
(C,o)
assuming that it is embedded as a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor in
(X,o)
. The first one will be a direct consequence of the work of
[
3
]
, while the second one assumes that
(X,o)
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein normal surface singularity.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(X,o)
be a normal surface germ with
\mathbb{Q}HS^{3}
link and
(C,o)\subset(X,o)
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier curve on it and let
h_{C}:=[l^{\prime}_{C}]
be the class in
H
associated with
(C,o)
.

(a) Then the delta invariant of
(C,o)
satisfies the following

(11)

\delta(C,o)=\chi(-l^{\prime}_{C})-\chi(r_{-h_{C}})+p_{g}(X,o)_{-h_{C}}-p_{g}(X,o).

(b) If we further assume that
(X,o)
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein, then we also have

(12)

\begin{split}\delta(C,o)&=\chi_{[Z_{K}]+h_{C}}(Z_{K}+\ell^{\prime}_{C}-s_{[Z_{K}+h_{C}]})+\chi(s_{[Z_{K}+h_{C}]})\\
&-\chi(r_{[Z_{K}+h_{C}]})+p_{g}(X,o)_{[Z_{K}]+h_{C}}-p_{g}(X,o).\end{split}

Proof.

Let us first prove part
(a)
of the theorem. The second part
(b)

will be given after Corollary
3.5
, as a consequence of some preparation.

We know by
[
3
, Thm 1.3.(2)]
that, in general, we have
\delta(C,o)=h^{1}({\tilde{X}},\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(-\widetilde{C}))-p_{g}(X)
. Moreover,
h^{1}({\tilde{X}},\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(-\widetilde{C}))=h^{1}({\tilde{X}},\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(l^{\prime}_{C}))

since
(C,o)\subset(X,o)
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier. Therefore, by
[
3
, Thm 1.3(1)]
one has

(13)

\begin{split}\delta(C,o)=&h^{1}({\tilde{X}},\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(l^{\prime}_{C}))-p_{g}(X,o)\\
=&\chi(-l^{\prime}_{C})-\chi(s_{-h_{C}})+h^{1}({\tilde{X}},\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(-s_{-h_{C}})-p_{g}(X,o),\end{split}

which, together with (
10
), gives (
11
).
∎

Remark 3.2
.

(1) Formula (
11
) shows that the (a priori analytic) delta invariant of
(C,o)
depends on some
embedded topological data
associated with the embedding

(C,o)\subset(X,o)
and on the
(C,o)
-independent analytic data of the
ambient space
(X,o)
given by its equivariant geometric genera.
For example, if two reduced curve germs
(C_{1},o)
and
(C_{2},0)
are embedded in

in such a way that

\delta(C_{1},o)-\chi(-l^{\prime}_{C_{1}})=\delta(C_{2},o)-\chi(-l^{\prime}_{C_{2}}).

In the particular case when
(C,o)
is Cartier we have
h_{C}=0
, and (

) reduces to
\delta(C,o)=\chi(-l^{\prime}_{C})
, a formula which follows from
[
2
]
too, cf.
[

, 3.4]
.

(2) Our motivation behind the second formula (

4
) have shown that the cycle

gives the answer to several key questions.

First, by
[

]
, in the case when
(X,o)
is a weighted homogeneous singularities and
\tilde{C}
is a transversal disc intersecting the central exceptional curve
E_{0}

corresponding to the star shaped graph (of the canonical resolution),
the coefficient of the cycle

along

gives the conductor (or the Frobenius number plus one) of the corresponding representable semigroup (for terminology see section
4
).

This is also true when
(C,o)
is a reduced (not necessarily irreducible) curve on a rational normal surface singularity
(X,o)
,
[
5
]
. In this case, if we fix a good embedded resolution (with

) of the pair

to the support set

is equal to the conductor of the semigroup of
(C,o)
.

(3) Another appearance of the cycle

is in the delta invariant formula from
[
3
,
4
]
in the special case when

is rational. This can be re-obtained from

) as follows. Since

is rational,

p_{g}=h^{1}(\tilde{X},\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{X}})=0
. On the other hand, by (

(p_{g})_{[Z_{K}]+h_{C}}=\chi(r_{[Z_{K}+h_{C}]})-\chi(s_{[Z_{K}+h_{C}]})
since

for
(X,o)
rational (by Lipman’s vanishing theorem
[

]
), cf.
[

. Therefore, (

\delta(C,o)=\chi_{[Z_{K}]+h_{C}}(Z_{K}+\ell^{\prime}_{C}-s_{[Z_{K}+h_{C}]})
(cf.
[
3
, Thm. 1.1]
).

Though in Remark
3.2
(3) the vanishing

holds whenever
(X,o)
is rational, this vanishing does not happen in general. However, in the sequel we will prove that
this vanishing is still valid whenever
(X,o)
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein

Using this vanishing, we will prove for any

a cohomological duality result which connects

with its ‘dual’

(see Proposition

transforms formula (

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume that
(X,o)
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein with
\mathbb{Q}HS^{3}
link. Then for any
h\in H
one has

(14)

h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(-Z_{K}+s_{h}))=0.

Proof.

By the formal function theorem, (
14
) is equivalent to the vanishing

h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell}(-Z_{K}+s_{h}))=0
for every
\ell\in L
and
\ell\gg 0
. In fact, since
(X,o)
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein and the link is a
\mathbb{Q}HS^{3}
one has that
\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{X}}(K_{\tilde{X}}+Z_{K})
is trivial (cf.
[
20
, 6.3.27]
), hence in the duality formulas one can replace
K_{\tilde{X}}
with
-Z_{K}
.
Therefore, Serre duality implies

(15)

h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell}(-Z_{K}+s_{h}))=h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell}(\ell-s_{h})).

On the other hand, by the generalized Grauert–Riemenschneider vanishing theorem
2.2

and Serre duality we have

(16)

h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell}(\ell-r_{h}))=h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell}(-Z_{K}+r_{-h}))=0.

Furthermore, we claim that the map
\tau:H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(-s_{h}))\to H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(-r_{h}))
is injective. Indeed, let us consider
\Delta_{h}:=s_{h}-r_{h}
. Then the exact sequence (cf. (
8
))

0\to\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(-s_{h})\to\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(-r_{h})\to\mathcal{O}_{\Delta_{h}}(-r_{h})\to 0
gives

(17)

0\to H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(-s_{h}))\xrightarrow{\alpha}H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(-r_{h}))\to H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\Delta_{h}}(-r_{h}))\to H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(-s_{h}))\xrightarrow{\tau}\dots.

Since
s(r_{h})=s_{h}
, we consider the computation sequence
\{r_{h}+x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{t}
connecting
r_{h}
with
s_{h}
, where
x_{i}\in L_{\geq 0}
,
x_{0}=0,x_{t}=\Delta_{h}
and
x_{i+1}=x_{i}+E_{v(i)}
for some
E_{v(i)}
such that
(r_{h}+x_{i},E_{v(i)})>0
. Then, by considering for any
i
the long exact sequence

0\to H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{E_{v(i)}}(-r_{h}-x_{i}))\to H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{x_{i+1}}(-r_{h}))\to H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{x_{i}}(-r_{h}))\to\dots

and using that
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{E_{v(i)}}(-r_{h}-x_{i}))=0
by the Chern number inequality from construction of the sequence, one obtains
by induction that
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{x_{i}}(-r_{h}))=0
.
In particular,
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\Delta_{h}}(-r_{h}))=0
, hence in (
17
)

\alpha
is an isomorphism and
\tau
is injective.

Next, consider the following diagram

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccc}0&\to&H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(-s_{h}))&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\gamma}}{{\longrightarrow}}&H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(\ell-s_{h}))&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k}}{{\longrightarrow}}&H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell}(\ell-s_{h}))&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j}}{{\longrightarrow}}&H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(-s_{h}))\\
&&\Big\downarrow\vbox{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle{\alpha}$\hss}}&&\Big\downarrow\vbox{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle{\beta}$\hss}}&&\Big\downarrow\vbox{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle{i}$\hss}}&&\Big\downarrow\vbox{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle{\tau}$\hss}}\\
0&\to&H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(-r_{h}))&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\omega}}{{\longrightarrow}}&H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(\ell-r_{h}))&\to&H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell}(\ell-r_{h}))&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle l}}{{\longrightarrow}}&H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(-r_{h}))\end{array}

By (
16
) we know that
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell}(\ell-r_{h}))=0
, hence
\omega
is an isomorphism. Since
\alpha
is also an isomorphism, one follows that
\beta\circ\gamma
is an isomorphism. Moreover,
\beta
and
\gamma
are injective, hence they are isomorphisms too. This implies that
k=0
and
j
is injective. The last, together with the injectivity of
\tau
,
implies that
\tau\circ j=l\circ i
is also injective. But
l\circ i=0
since
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell}(\ell-r_{h}))=0
, we must therefore have
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell}(\ell-s_{h}))=0
. This, using (
15
), implies (
14
).
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
(X,o)
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein singularity with
\mathbb{Q}HS^{3}
link. Then for any
h\in H
we have the following identity

(18)

h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(-s_{-h}))-\chi(s_{-h})=h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(-s_{[Z_{K}]+h}))-\chi(s_{[Z_{K}]+h}).

Proof.

We consider the cycle
\ell^{\prime}:=Z_{K}-s_{-h}-\ell\in L^{\prime}
with
\ell\in L
. Hence
[\ell^{\prime}]=[Z_{K}]+h
.
Choose the coefficients of
\ell
sufficiently large so that
\ell^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}_{\leq 0}
.
In this case

, cf.
2.2
, and (
9
) implies

(19)

h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(-Z_{K}+s_{-h}+\ell))-\chi(Z_{K}-s_{-h}-\ell)=h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(-s_{[Z_{K}]+h}))-\chi(s_{[Z_{K}]+h}).

Now we consider the exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(-Z_{K}+s_{-h})\to\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(-Z_{K}+s_{-h}+\ell)\to\mathcal{O}_{\ell}(-Z_{K}+s_{-h}+\ell)\to 0

which gives the long exact sequence

(20)

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccc}0&\to&H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(-Z_{K}+s_{-h}))&\to&H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(-Z_{K}+s_{-h}+\ell))&\to&H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell}(-Z_{K}+s_{-h}+\ell))&\to&\\
&\to&H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(-Z_{K}+s_{-h}))&\to&H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(-Z_{K}+s_{-h}+\ell))&\to&H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell}(-Z_{K}+s_{-h}+\ell))&\to&0.\\

Then we get

h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(-Z_{K}+s_{-h}+\ell))=h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell}(-Z_{K}+s_{-h}+\ell))=h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell}(-s_{-h})),

where the first identity is implied by (

\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein singularity with
\mathbb{Q}HS^{3}
link. The equation (

h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell}(-s_{-h}))-\chi(s_{-h}+\ell)=h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(-s_{[Z_{K}]+h}))-\chi(s_{[Z_{K}]+h}).

Finally, from the identities

h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell}(-s_{-h}))=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{\ell}(-s_{-h}))+h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell}(-s_{-h}))

\chi(\mathcal{O}_{\ell}(-s_{-h}))=\chi(\ell)-(\ell,s_{-h})

∎

Proof of Theorem

4.
Generic orbits and representable semigroups of weighted homogeneous germs

4.1.
Preliminaries on representable semigroups

4.1.1.
Notations and some preliminary facts

Let
(X,0)
be a normal weighted homogeneous surface singularity, which is defined as the germ at the origin of a normal affine surface
X
with a good and effective
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action. In particular, its affine coordinate ring is
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
–graded:
R_{X}=\oplus_{\ell\geq 0}R_{X,\ell}
.

We consider the smooth compact curve
E_{0}:=(X\setminus\{0\})/\mathbb{C}^{*}
and by
T
we denote the closure of the graph of the map
X\setminus\{0\}\to E_{0}
in
X\times E_{0}
. Then the first projection
T\to X
is a modification of
(X,0)
, while the second projection
T\to E_{0}
is a Seifert line bundle with zero section
E_{0}
.
T
has at most a finite number of cyclic quotient singularities, which sit at the intersection of
E_{0}
with the singular fibers. After resolving in a minimal way these singularities, we get the
canonical (equivariant) resolution

\pi:\widetilde{X}\to X
.
The exceptional divisor
\pi^{-1}(0)
is a normal crossing divisor and only the central curve
E_{0}
can have a self-intersection number
-1
and positive genus.

Let
\Gamma
be the dual resolution graph of the canonical resolution
\pi
. Then, by

[
24
]

\Gamma
is a ‘star-shaped’ graph with a central vertex
v_{0}
and
d\geq 0
legs connected to it. (A leg is a chain of vertices that corresponds to the resolution of a cyclic quotient singularity of
T
.)

The
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action induces an Seifert
S^{1}
–action on the link
\Sigma
of the singularity, in particular

\Sigma
is a Seifert 3-manifold with a negative definite intersection matrix. This Seifert 3-manifold
is characterized by its normalized Seifert invariants
Sf=(-b_{0},g;(\alpha_{i},\omega_{i})_{i=1}^{d})
, defined as follows. Each leg is determined by a pair of integers
(\alpha_{i},\omega_{i})
,
where
0<\omega_{i}<\alpha_{i}
and
\gcd(\alpha_{i},\omega_{i})=1
. The
i^{\mathrm{th}}
leg has
\nu_{i}
vertices, say
v_{i1},\ldots,v_{i\nu_{i}}
(
v_{i1}
is connected to the central vertex
v_{0}
)
with self-intersection numbers (or Euler decorations)

-b_{i1},\ldots,-b_{i\nu_{i}}
. These can be determined by the Hirzebruch–Jung (negative) continued fraction expansion

\alpha_{i}/\omega_{i}=[b_{i1},\ldots,b_{i\nu_{i}}]=b_{i1}-1/(b_{i2}-1/(\cdots-1/b_{i\nu_{i}})\dots)\ \ \ \ (b_{ij}\geq 2).

All these vertices (except
v_{0}
) have genus–decorations zero. The central vertex
v_{0}
corresponds to the central genus
g
curve
E_{0}
with self-intersection number
-b_{0}
.

In particular,
g=0
if and only if
\Sigma
is a
\mathbb{Q}HS^{3}
, a property which will be assumed in the sequel. In this case, we omit the genus
g
from the notation and simply write
Sf=(-b_{0};(\alpha_{i},\omega_{i})_{i=1}^{d})
.

4.2.
Representable numerical semigroups
[
14
,
1
]

We consider the numerical semigroup
\mathcal{S}_{(X,0)}:=\{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}|R_{X,\ell}\neq 0\}
.
If
\Sigma
is
\mathbb{Q}HS^{3}
then
[
25
]
shows that
\dim(R_{X,\ell})
is
topological, namely it equals

\max\{0,1+N(\ell)\}
, where
N(\ell)

is the quasi-linear function

(24)

N(\ell):=b_{0}\ell-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Big\lceil\frac{\ell\omega_{i}}{\alpha_{i}}\Big\rceil.

Hence, in this case the semigroup
{\mathcal{S}}_{(X,0)}=\{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}\ |\ N(\ell)\geq 0\}
is topological and it can be described by Seifert invariants.
For this reason, we denote it by
{\mathcal{S}}_{\Gamma}
.

We say that a numerical semigroup
{\mathcal{S}}\subset\mathbb{N}
is
representable
if it can be realized as
{\mathcal{S}}_{\Gamma}
for some canonical equivariant resolution graph
\Gamma
as above.

4.3.
The generic orbit curve and its semigroup

Let
(X,0)
be a normal weighted homogeneous surface singularity with
\mathbb{Q}HS^{3}
link. (We fix a good
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action if it is not unique.) As in
4.1.1
consider the canonical equivariant resolution associated with the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action and lift the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
orbits to the resolution space. There are special orbits corresponding to the singular fibers (the strict transform of any of these intersects an
end vertex
E_{i\nu_{i}}
) and a 1-parameter family of generic orbits
(their strict transforms intersect
E_{0}
in the points

E_{0}\setminus\cup_{i}E_{v_{i1}}
). We replace the resolution of the affine
surface by a small convenient neighborhood of the exceptional curve
E
and we denote it by
\widetilde{X}
, and let
\pi:(\widetilde{X},E)\to(X,0)
be the
corresponding resolution map.

Let
\widetilde{C}^{gen}
be the intersection of a lifted generic orbit with
\widetilde{X}
. It is a special cut of the central curve
E_{0}
at a smooth point of
\pi^{-1}(0)
.

In fact, the irreducible reduced germ
(C^{gen},0):=\pi(\widetilde{C}^{gen})
is

\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier in
(X,0)
whose total transform is
\widetilde{C}^{gen}+E^{*}_{0}
, see eg.
[
20
, Ex. 6.2.13]
. This means that the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{{\widetilde{X}}}(-\widetilde{C}^{gen})
is a ‘natural line bundle’ in
\Pic(\widetilde{X})
, ie.

(25)

\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(-\tilde{C}^{gen})\simeq\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(E_{0}^{*}).

In particular, all the generic orbits in
\tilde{X}
define the same line bundle.

Now, we look at the (abstract) curve germ
(C^{gen},0)
and consider the discrete valuation
v:\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{C^{gen}}=\mathbb{C}\{t\}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\cup\infty
defined by the normalization
\bar{C}^{gen}\to C^{gen}
. Then one can consider the value semigroup of
(C^{gen},0)
defined by

{\mathcal{S}}_{C^{gen}}:=\{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\ :\ \mbox{there exists}\ f\in\mathcal{O}_{C^{gen}}\ \mbox{with}\ v(f)=\ell\}.

The next theorem claims that the value semigroup of the generic orbit curve (regarded as an abstract curve)
is the same as the semigroup
{\mathcal{S}}_{\Gamma}
associated with
\Gamma
.

Theorem 4.1
.

The numerical semigroup
{\mathcal{S}}_{C^{gen}}
associated with the germ of the irreducible curve
(C^{gen},0)
coincides with
{\mathcal{S}}_{\Gamma}
.

Proof.

We consider the canonical equivariant resolution
\pi
as a good embedded resolution for
(C^{gen},0)\subset(X,0)
. We also write
p:=\tilde{C}^{gen}\cap E_{0}\in\tilde{X}
.

Recall that
\ell\in{\mathcal{S}}_{C^{gen}}
if and only if there is a function
f\in\mathcal{O}_{C^{gen}}
with
v(f)=\ell
, where
v
is the valuation induced by the normalization of
(C^{gen},0)
. This condition is equivalent to the existence of a function
f\in\mathcal{O}_{(X,0)}
such that
(\textrm{div}(f\circ\pi),\tilde{C}^{gen})_{\tilde{X}}=\ell
, where
\textrm{div}(f\circ\pi):=(f)_{\Gamma}+\tilde{C}_{f}
is the total transform,
(f)_{\Gamma}
is its part supported on
E=\pi^{-1}(0)
and
\tilde{C}_{f}
is the strict transform, and
(\ ,\,)_{\tilde{X}}
denotes the divisorial intersection on
\tilde{X}
.
In particular, if the
E_{0}
coefficient of
(f)_{\Gamma}
is
m_{E_{0}}(f)
, then

\ell=v(f)=m_{E_{0}}(f)+(\tilde{C}_{f},\tilde{C}^{gen})_{p}
, where the last term is the intersection multiplicity at
p\in\tilde{X}
.

On the other hand,
{\mathcal{S}}_{(X,0)}=\{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}|R_{X,\ell}\neq 0\}
has the following reinterpretation. First note that if
f\in R_{X,\ell}
is a homogeneous function, then

m_{E_{0}}(f)
is exactly its homogeneous degree
\ell
. Hence
{\mathcal{S}}_{(X,0)}=\{m_{E_{0}}(f)\ |\ f\ \mbox{is homogeneous}\}
.

The inclusion
{\mathcal{S}}_{(X,0)}\subset{\mathcal{S}}_{C^{gen}}
is rather direct. Let
f_{\ell}\in R_{X,\ell}

be a nonzero element of homogeneous degree
\ell
, and let
\tilde{C}_{f_{\ell}}
be its strict transform.
Then we can choose the generic orbit
\tilde{C}^{gen}
in such a way that
it does not intersect (along
E_{0}
) any of the strict transforms
\tilde{C}_{f_{\ell}}
, hence
(\tilde{C}_{f_{\ell}},\tilde{C}^{gen})_{p}=0
.
Then
v
associated with
\tilde{C}^{gen}
has value
\ell=m_{E_{0}}(f_{\ell})=v(f_{\ell})
.

Next, let us fix
\tilde{C}^{gen}
and
assume that
\ell\in{\mathcal{S}}_{C^{gen}}
. Choose a function
f\in\mathcal{O}_{(X,0)}
such that

\ell=v(f)
. Write
f
as a sum of its homogeneous components
f=\sum_{j\geq 0}f_{j}
, where
each
f_{j}\in R_{X,j}
is nonzero. Then, for any fixed
j
, the strict transform
\tilde{C}_{f_{j}}

of
f_{j}
is a finite union of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-orbits intersected with
\tilde{X}
.
It might contain the fixed orbit
\tilde{C}^{gen}
or not.
Therefore,
v(f_{j})=j+(\tilde{C}_{f_{j}},\tilde{C}^{gen})_{p}
equals
j
if

\tilde{C}_{f_{j}}
does not contain the fixed orbit
\tilde{C}^{gen}
, and it is
\infty
otherwise.
In particular, all the possible finite values
\{v(f_{j})\}_{j}
are distinct, and

\ell=v(f)=\min\{v(f_{j})\ |\ v(f_{j})<\infty\}
. This shows that
\ell=v(f)=v(f_{j})=j

for some
f_{j}\in R_{(X,0)}
, hence
\ell\in{\mathcal{S}}_{(X,0)}
.
∎

4.4.
The genus of a representable semigroup

For a numerical semigroup
{\mathcal{S}}
with

|\mathbb{N}\setminus{\mathcal{S}}|<\infty
, the number of gaps
\mathbb{N}\setminus{\mathcal{S}}

it is called the genus and is denoted by
g({\mathcal{S}})
.

In the previous section, we have proved that every representable semigroup can be viewed as the value semigroup of the generic orbit curve on a weighted homogeneous surface singularity
(X,0)
. Therefore, Theorem
3.1
provides the following formula.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a representable semigroup and we consider one of its representatives
\Gamma
and the associated cycles as in Sect.
2
. Then we have

(26)

g({\mathcal{S}})=\chi_{[Z_{K}]+h_{0}}(Z_{K}+E_{0}^{*}-s_{[Z_{K}]+h_{0}})+\chi(s_{[Z_{K}]+h_{0}})-\chi(r_{[Z_{K}]+h_{0}})+(p_{g})_{[Z_{K}]+h_{0}}-p_{g}.

Remark 4.3
.

We wish to emphasize that in the statement of Corollary
4.2
and formula
(
26
) the specification of the (weighted homogeneous)
analytic structure of
(X,0)
is irrelevant. Indeed,
for any weighted homogeneous surface singularities with
\mathbb{Q}HS^{3}
link, the equivariant geometric genus is topological, it depends only on
\Gamma
(cf.
[
16
,
20
]
).

5.
When is a representable semigroups symmetric?

5.1.

Let us consider the situation and notations of section
4
. Additionally,
However, we will introduce some further invariants of Seifert 3-manifolds.

Let
Sf=(-b_{0};(\alpha_{i},\omega_{i})_{i=1}^{d})
be the Seifert invariants of
(X,0)
and
\Gamma
be the corresponding canonical equivariant resolution graph. The
orbifold Euler number

is defined as
e=-b_{0}+\sum_{i}\omega_{i}/\alpha_{i}
. The negative definiteness of the intersection form is equivalent with
e<0
. We also write
\alpha:=\mathrm{lcm}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d})
. Let
|H|
be the order of
H
, and
let
\mathfrak{o}
be the order of the class of
E^{*}_{0}
in
H
.
Then, by
[
21
]
,

(27)

|H|=\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{d}|e|,\ \ \ \ \mathfrak{o}=\alpha|e|.

Let
\gamma
be the
E_{0}
-coefficient of the rational cycle
Z_{K}-E
. This combinatorial number has several interpretations – eg. exponent of the weighted homogeneous germ, log-discrepancy of
E_{0}
, Goto-Watanabe
a
-invariant, orbifold Euler characteristic, etc. – since it has a central importance with respect to the properties of
(X,0)
. It also equals

\gamma=\frac{1}{|e|}\cdot\Big(d-2-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1}{\alpha_{i}}\Big)\in\mathbb{Q}.

5.2.

In subsection
4.2
we already mentioned that the semigroup
{\mathcal{S}}_{\Gamma}
associated with
(X,0)
and with the central vertex
E_{0}
(or, with the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action)
is topological: it can be determined completely from
\Gamma
.

Let
c
be the conductor of
{\mathcal{S}}_{\Gamma}
, that is,
c
is the smallest integer such that

c+\mathbb{N}\subset{\mathcal{S}}_{\Gamma}
. A numerical semigroup
{\mathcal{S}}\subset\mathbb{N}
is
symmetric

whenever
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
if and only if
c-1-s\not\in{\mathcal{S}}
.

In the sequel, we wish to detect topologically whether
{\mathcal{S}}_{\Gamma}
is symmetric or not.

Recall that by Theorem
4.1
we have
{\mathcal{S}}_{\Gamma}={\mathcal{S}}_{C^{gen}}
, that is,
{\mathcal{S}}_{\Gamma}

is realized as the semigroup of an abstract reduced irreducible curve singularity.
In such a generality, we have the following: For an irreducible curve singularity
(C,o)
with semigroup
{\mathcal{S}}
and delta invariant
\delta
the following facts are equivalent
[
10
,
6
]
:

(28)

\mbox{${\mathcal{S}}$ is symmetric \ $\Leftrightarrow$ \ $(C,o)$ is Gorenstein \ $\Leftrightarrow$ \ $c=2\delta$}.

Having in mind the identity
{\mathcal{S}}_{\Gamma}={\mathcal{S}}_{C^{gen}}
, we can ask if the symmetry of
{\mathcal{S}}_{\Gamma}
can be related with the Gorenstein property of
(X,0)
. The answer in general is no.

Indeed, the Gorenstein property of
(X,0)
does not imply the symmetry of
{\mathcal{S}}_{\Gamma}
:
Example 7.1.3 of
[
14
]

is a Gorenstein singularity with Seifert invariants

(-2;(2,1),(2,1),(3,1),(3,1),(7,1),(7,1),(84,1))

whose semigroup is not symmetric.

On the other hand, a singularity with Seifert invariants
(-2;(3,1),(3,1),(3,1))

has a symmetric semigroup, namely
G(2,3)
, however, it is not even numerically Gorenstein.

5.3.

In the next characterization, we will use the last property of (
28
),
applied for
(C^{gen},0)
.

By
[
14
]
we know that the conductor
c
of
{\mathcal{S}}_{\Gamma}
can be expressed as

(29)

c=(Z_{K}+E_{0}^{*}-s_{[Z_{K}]+h_{0}})_{0}=\gamma+1+\frac{1}{|e|}-\check{s},

where
(\ )_{0}
refers to the
E_{0}
-coefficient of the corresponding cycle, and
\check{s}:=(s_{[Z_{K}]+h_{0}})_{0}
.

Now, using (
29
) and
\chi(E^{*}_{0})=-(E^{*}_{0},E^{*}_{0}-Z_{K})/2=-Z_{K,0}/2+1/(2|e|)
we get

(30)

\begin{split}\chi_{[Z_{K}]+h_{0}}(Z_{K}+E_{0}^{*}-s_{[Z_{K}]+h_{0}})&=\chi(Z_{K}+E^{*}_{0})-\chi(s_{[Z_{K}+h_{0}]})\\
&=\frac{c}{2}+\frac{\check{s}}{2}-\chi(s_{[Z_{K}]+h_{0}}).\end{split}

This rephrases (
26
) as

(31)

g({\mathcal{S}}_{\Gamma})=\frac{c}{2}+\frac{\check{s}}{2}-\chi(r_{[Z_{K}]+h_{0}})+(p_{g})_{[Z_{K}]+h_{0}}-p_{g}.

Now, by (
28
)
{\mathcal{S}}_{\Gamma}
is symmetric if and only if
g({\mathcal{S}}_{\Gamma})=\delta=c/2
,
hence (
31
) reads as follows.

Corollary 5.1
.

A representable semigroup
{\mathcal{S}}_{\Gamma}
is symmetric if and only if
\Gamma
satisfies

(32)

p_{g}-(p_{g})_{[Z_{K}]+h_{0}}=\frac{\check{s}}{2}-\chi(r_{[Z_{K}]+h_{0}}),

or, equivalently

(33)

p_{g}=h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{{\tilde{X}}}(-s_{[Z_{K}]+h}))+\check{s}/2-\chi(s_{[Z_{K}]+h_{0}}).

Example 5.2
.

Assume that the ambient space
(X,0)
is a rational singularity with a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action. Let
\Gamma
be its canonical equivariant resolution graph. In this case, one has
(p_{g})_{[Z_{K}]+h_{0}}=\chi(r_{[Z_{K}]+h_{0}})-\chi(s_{[Z_{K}]+h_{0}})
, hence

(34)

g({\mathcal{S}}_{\Gamma})=\chi_{[Z_{K}]+h_{0}}(Z_{K}+E_{0}^{*}-s_{[Z_{K}]+h_{0}})=\frac{c}{2}+\frac{\check{s}}{2}-\chi(s_{[Z_{K}]+h_{0}}).

In particular,
{\mathcal{S}}_{\Gamma}
is symmetric if and only if

\chi(s_{[Z_{K}]+h_{0}})=\check{s}/2
.
(If
b_{0}\geq d
then we know by
[

that

,

In fact, the symmetry (or
\chi(s_{[Z_{K}]+h_{0}})=\check{s}/2
)
is equivalent to

b_{0}\geq d

In particular, this is the case when we consider any chosen

-action on

In general, from (

which is indeed symmetric, but
[Z_{K}+E_{0}^{*}]\neq 0
in

.

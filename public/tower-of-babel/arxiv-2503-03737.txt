Abstract
. We study the conditions under which the head characters of a solvable group, as defined by I. M. Isaacs, behave well with respect to restriction. We also determine the intersection of the kernels of all head characters of the group. Using G. Navarro’s definition of 𝔉 ′ \mathfrak{F}^{\prime} -characters, we generalize these results for any saturated formation 𝔉 \mathfrak{F} containing the nilpotent groups.



1.
Introduction

If
G
is a solvable group, I. M. Isaacs constructed in 2022
[
10
]
a canonical subset of the complex irreducible characters of
G
associated to the linear characters of a Carter subgroup
C
of
G
. Recall that a Carter subgroup of
G
is a self-normalizing nilpotent subgroup of
G
. He called these the
head characters
of
G
, and proved that the number of those was
|C/C^{\prime}|
, the number of linear characters of
C
.
Long time before, E. C. Dade and D. Gajendragadkar had already constructed in
[
2
,
4
]
another subset of

{\rm Irr}(G)
, this time associated to the system normalizers of the solvable group
G
. This subset was described by Gajendragadkar from the better known
fully factorable characters
.

The head characters, on the other hand, remain mysterious, and
no general properties are known of them. In our first main result, we prove the following key property.

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a solvable group,
C
a Carter subgroup of
G
and
N
normal in
G
.
If
\chi
is a head character of
G
, then the restriction
\chi_{NC}
contains a head character of
NC
.
In particular,
\chi_{N}
contains a
C
-invariant character of
N
.

Theorem A was conjectured by G. Navarro in private communications with I. M. Isaacs, and its proof depends on
new deep properties of the character theory of fully ramified characters. (See Lemma
3.5
.)

In Theorem B, we turn our attention to the kernels of the head characters and prove the following.

Theorem B
.

Let
G
be a solvable group and
C
be a Carter subgroup of
G
. The intersection of the kernels of the head characters of
G
is the largest normal subgroup

N
of
G
such that
N\cap C
is contained in
C^{\prime}
.

Theorem B is the exact analog of an unpublished result of Navarro which we present here with
his permission.

Theorem C

(Navarro)
.

Let
p
be a prime, let
G
be
p
-solvable and let
P
be a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. The intersection of the kernels of the
p^{\prime}
-characters of
G
is the largest normal subgroup
N
of
G
such that
{\bf N}_{N}(P)
is contained in
P^{\prime}
.

What is the relationship between the head characters and the
p^{\prime}
-degree characters of a solvable group? If
\mathfrak{F}
is a saturated formation and
G
is a solvable group, Navarro defined in
[
14
]
a canonical subset
{\rm Irr}_{\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}}(G)
of the irreducible characters of
G
of size
|{\rm Irr}(N_{G}(H)/H^{\prime})|
, where
H
is an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector of
G
.
If
\mathfrak{F}
is the formation of nilpotent groups, then
H
is a Carter subgroup,
and he proved that
{\rm Irr}_{\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}}(G)
are the head characters of
G
; in particular, reproving Isaacs result.
If
\mathfrak{F}
is the formation of
p
-groups, then
H
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup
and this reproved the McKay conjecture for solvable groups.

For solvable groups, Carter subgroups are the
\mathfrak{F}
-projectors associated to the formation of nilpotent groups, in the same way that system normalizers are the nilpotent case of
\mathfrak{F}
-normalizers. The latter are a conjugacy class of subgroups that was used by Dade and Gajendragadkar in a more general way to define their subset of
\text{Irr}(G)
when
\mathfrak{F}
is a saturated formation containing the nilpotent groups.

In fact, our Theorems A and B are also far more general. In the same framework as Dade and Gajendragadkar (that is, if
\mathfrak{F}
is a saturated formation containing the nilpotent groups), we shall prove convenient versions of Theorems A and B for
{\rm Irr}_{\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}}(G)
(see respectively Theorems
6.2
and
7.5
) at the same time
that we drastically simplify Isaacs’ construction (see Theorem
5.4
).

There are still several questions on the head characters and Carter subgroups
that remain open: among them if the character table of
G
determines the set of its head characters.
We are confident that our results might be helpful in solving these and other related questions.

2.
Some properties on projectors

A class of groups
\mathfrak{F}
is called a
formation
if it is closed under quotients and satisfies the following property: whenever
G/N,G/M\in\mathfrak{F}
for some finite group
G
, it follows that
G/(M\cap N)\in\mathfrak{F}
. A formation
\mathfrak{F}
is said to be
saturated
if
G\in\mathfrak{F}
if and only if
G/\Phi(G)\in\mathfrak{F}
, where
\Phi(G)
denotes the Frattini subgroup of
G
. Examples of saturated formations are the classes of finite
p
-groups, finite nilpotent groups, and finite supersolvable groups. As basic references on this topic, see
[
3
]
and
[
15
]
.

The notion of formations was first introduced by Gaschütz in
[
5
]
as a context where both definitions of Carter and Sylow subgroups could be unified under the more general concept of
\mathfrak{F}
-covering subgroups. Gaschütz later proved that, when
\mathfrak{F}
is saturated, these subgroups coincide with
\mathfrak{F}
-projectors
, a preferable term in formation theory.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}
be a saturated formation and
G
a solvable group. There exists exactly one conjugacy class of subgroups
H
of
G
such that, for every normal subgroup
N
of
G
, the subgroup
HN/N
satisfies the following properties:

(a)

HN/N\in\mathfrak{F}
;

(b)

if
HN/N
is a subgroup of the subgroup
V/N
of
G/N
with
V/N\in\mathfrak{F}
, then
HN/N=V/N
.

In other words,
HN/N
is
\mathfrak{F}
-maximal in
G/N
for all normal subgroups
N
of
G
. We say that these subgroups
H
are the
\mathfrak{F}
-projectors of
G
.

As previously mentioned, Carter subgroups can be found through the above definition by choosing the formation to be that of nilpotent groups, which we will note
\mathfrak{N}
from now on. Similarly,
p
-Sylow and
\pi
-Hall subgroups are the
\mathfrak{F}
-projectors for the saturated formations of
p
- and
\pi
-groups respectively.
Listed below are some fundamental properties of
\mathfrak{F}
-projectors which we will need in our proofs.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}
be a saturated formation and
G
be a solvable group. Suppose
H
is an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector of
G
. Then

(a)

if
U
is a subgroup of
G
containing
H
, then
H
is also an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector of
U
.

Moreover, if
N
is a normal subgroup of
G
, then

(b)

HN/N
is an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector of
G/N
;

(c)

if
U/N
is an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector of
G/N
, then
U=H^{g}N
for some
g\in G
;

(d)

{\bf N}_{G}(NH)=N{\bf N}_{G}(H)
.

Finally, if
\mathfrak{N}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}
, then

(e)

if
U
is a subgroup of
G
containing
H
, then
{\bf N}_{G}(U)=U
. In particular,
H
is self-normalizing.

Proof.

Assertions (a) to (c) are proved in Section 9.5 of
[
15
]
. If
g\in{\bf N}_{G}(HN)
, then both
H
and
H^{g}
are
\mathfrak{F}
-projectors of
HN=H^{g}N
. Using (c), there exists
n\in HN
which can be assumed to be in
N
and such that
H^{n}=H^{g}
. Then
gn^{-1}\in{\bf N}_{G}(H)
. Thus
{\bf N}_{G}(HN)\leq N{\bf N}_{G}(H)
. Since the converse is trivial, this proves (d).

Now suppose
\mathfrak{N}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}
and let
H
be an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector of a solvable group
G
. Let
U
be a subgroup of
G
containing
H
. By (a),
H
is also an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector of
{\bf N}_{G}(U)
, and thus by (b) we have that
U/U
is an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector of
{\bf N}_{G}(U)/U
. By definition of projectors, this means that
\{1\}
is
\mathfrak{F}
-maximal in
{\bf N}_{G}(U)/U
. If
U\neq{\bf N}_{G}(U)
, then there exists some non trivial element
x\in{\bf N}_{G}(U)/U
and the subgroup
\langle x\rangle
contradicts the
\mathfrak{F}
-maximality of
\{1\}
.
∎

The
\mathfrak{F}
-residual

G^{\mathfrak{F}}
of
G
is its smallest normal subgroup such that the quotient
G/G^{\mathfrak{F}}
lies in
\mathfrak{F}
.
The following result shows a connection between an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector of a group and its
\mathfrak{F}
-residual, when the latter is abelian.

Theorem 2.3

(Theorem IV.5.18 of
[
3
]
)
.

Let
G
be a finite solvable group and
G^{\mathfrak{F}}
its
\mathfrak{F}
-residual. If
G^{\mathfrak{F}}
is abelian, then
G^{\mathfrak{F}}
is complemented in
G
, and its complements are the
\mathfrak{F}
projectors of
G
.

Note that, if
\mathfrak{F}
is a saturated formation,
K=G^{\mathfrak{F}}
and
L\trianglelefteq\,G
is such that
K/L
is abelian, then by the previous result it is not difficult to prove that
G=KH
and
K\cap LH=L
, where
H
is an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector. It is thus natural to consider this group structure as a hypothesis for some of the theorems in further sections.

We finish this section with a result that can be found as Satz 2.1 of
[
6
]
or Theorem IV.5.4 of
[
3
]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
G
be a finite solvable group and
H
an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector of
G
for a saturated formation
\mathfrak{F}
. If
N,M
are normal subgroups of
G
, then

MN\cap H=(M\cap H)(N\cap H)\,.

3.
Good Extensions

In this section, we introduce results on extensions that, for our purposes, exhibit desirable behavior with respect to certain subgroups and formations. These extensions can be regarded, in some sense, as “
good extensions
”, and they will be of particular interest in Section
6
to address the question of when the restriction of an
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
-character to a subgroup containing an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector of a solvable group
G
contains an
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
-character of that subgroup.

Let us first recall two results on character correspondences.

Lemma 3.1

(Corollary 4.3 of
[
8
]
)
.

Let
N\trianglelefteq G
and
K\subseteq G
with
NK=G
and
N\cap K=M
. Let
\varphi\in{\rm Irr}(M)
be invariant in
K
, and assume
\theta=\varphi^{N}
is irreducible. Then induction defines a bijection
{\rm Irr}(K\mid\varphi)\rightarrow{\rm Irr}(G\mid\theta)
.

Lemma 3.2

(Corollary 4.2 of
[
8
]
)
.

Let
N\trianglelefteq G
and
K\subseteq G
with
NK=G
and
N\cap K=M
. Let
\theta\in{\rm Irr}(N)
be invariant in
G
, and assume
\varphi=\theta_{M}
is irreducible. Then restriction defines a bijection
{\rm Irr}(G\mid\theta)\rightarrow{\rm Irr}(K\mid\varphi)
.

Given an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector
H
of a solvable group
G
, we use the usual notation
{\rm Irr}_{H}(N)
for the
H
-invariant irreducible characters of an
H
-invariant subgroup
N
of
G
. The following theorem, which appears in
[
14
]
and is due to Navarro, is key to our objectives.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}
be a saturated formation with
\mathfrak{N}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}
. Suppose
G
is a finite solvable group. Assume that
K/L
is abelian, where
K
and
L
are normal in
G
. Let
H
be an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector of
G
, and assume that
KH=G
and
K\cap HL=L
. We have the following.

(a)

If
\theta\in{\rm Irr}_{H}(K)
, then there exists a unique
\varphi
in
{\rm Irr}_{H}(L)
under
\theta
.

(b)

If
\varphi\in{\rm Irr}_{H}(L)
, then there exists a unique
\theta
in
{\rm Irr}_{H}(K)
over
\varphi
.

Now fix
\varphi
and
\theta
as before.

(c)

We have that
\theta
extends to
G
if and only if
\varphi
extends to
LH
.

Proof.

This result is Theorem 3.5 of
[
14
]
, taking into account that, since
\mathfrak{N}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}
, the
\mathfrak{F}
-projectors of
G
are self-normalizing by Proposition
2.2
(e).
∎

Let
\mathfrak{F}
be a saturated formation. Suppose that
K
is a normal subgroup of a solvable group
G
such that
G=KH
, where
H
is an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector of
G
. If
L
is a normal subgroup of
H
such that
K/L
is abelian and
K\cap HL=L
, then we say that
(KH,K,L)
satisfies the
Navarro condition
. Whenever
\mathfrak{F}
contains
\mathfrak{N}
and the Navarro condition is satisfied, we can apply Theorem
3.3
.

We are interested in studying whether there is any relationship between the two sets of
H
-invariant character extensions that intervene in the previous theorem.
The result below was generously communicated to us by G. Malle, G. Navarro and P. H. Tiep
[
13
]
. We are thankful to them for allowing us to present it here.

Theorem 3.4

(Malle, Navarro, Tiep)
.

Suppose
G
is a finite solvable group,
K,L\trianglelefteq\,G
such that
K/L
is abelian. Suppose that
H/L
is a self-normalizing nilpotent subgroup of
G/L
, and suppose that
G=KH
and
K\cap H=L
. Let
\theta\in{\rm Irr}_{H}(L)
and let
\varphi\in{\rm Irr}_{H}(K)
lying over
\varphi
. Then the following hold.

(a)

If
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G)
extends
\theta
, then there is an irreducible constituent of
\chi_{H}
extending
\varphi
.

(b)

If
\nu\in{\rm Irr}(H)
extends
\varphi
, then there is an irreducible constituent of
\nu^{G}
extending
\theta
.

In the proof of the following lemma, we will use Isaacs’ notation from
[
7
]
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}
be a saturated formation with
\mathfrak{N}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}
. Suppose that
K
and
L
are normal subgroups of a solvable group
G
such that
K/L
is a chief factor of
G
,
K\cap LH=L
, and
G=KH
, where
H
is an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector of
G
. Moreover, assume that
G
has odd order whenever
\mathfrak{N}\neq\mathfrak{F}
. Let
\varphi\in\mathrm{Irr}(L)
be fully ramified with respect to
K/L
, and let
\theta\in\mathrm{Irr}(K)
be the unique
H
-invariant irreducible character lying over
\varphi
.

(a)

If
\eta
is an irreducible extension of
\varphi
to
LH
, then there exists an extension
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
of
\theta
lying over
\eta
.

(b)

If
\chi
is an irreducible extension of
\theta
to
G
, then there exists an extension
\eta\in\mathrm{Irr}(LH)
of
\varphi
lying under
\chi
.

Proof.

We have that
(G,L,\varphi)
is a character triple. By a character triple isomorphism, we can assume that
L
is a central subgroup of
G
and that
\varphi
is faithful. Additionally, each coset of
L
contains an element
x
such that
\langle x\rangle\cap L=1
by Theorem 8.2 in
[
7
]
.

We also have that
K/L
is a
p
-group. If
|G|
is odd, then
\mathcal{C}=(G,K,H,L,\theta,\varphi)
is a constellation with prime
p
(see Definition 4.5 of
[
7
]
). Let
\psi
be the unique canonical character arising from
\mathcal{C}
that exists by Theorem 5.3 of
[
7
]
. By Corollary 5.9 of
[
7
]
,
\psi
contains as an irreducible constituent the trivial character
1_{H}
. Since for every extension of
\theta
to
G
there is a unique extension
\nu
of
\varphi
to
H
such that
\chi_{H}=\psi\nu
, (a) follows easily.

But the number of extensions of
\theta
to
G
is the same as the number of extensions of
\varphi
to
H
and thus (b) follows, by Corollary 3.6 of
[
7
]
.

Now, we consider the case in which
\mathfrak{F}=\mathfrak{N}
. Then
H
is nilpotent and the result follows by Theorem
3.4
with
H=CL
.
∎

We prove that
H
-invariant extensions to
KH
, as presented in Theorem
3.3
, lie over
H
-invariant extensions to
LH
if
G
is of odd order or
\mathfrak{F}
is the formation of nilpotent groups.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}
be a saturated formation with
\mathfrak{N}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}
.
Suppose that
K
and
L
are normal subgroups of a group
G
, such that
K/L
is an abelian factor of
G
,
K\cap LH=L
, and
G=KH
, where
H
is an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector of
G
. Further assume that
G
has odd order whenever
\mathfrak{N}\neq\mathfrak{F}
. Let
\varphi\in{\rm Irr}_{H}(L)
, and let
\theta\in{\rm Irr}_{H}(K)
be the unique
H
-invariant irreducible character over
\varphi
.

(a)

If
\eta
is an irreducible extension of
\varphi
to
LH
, then there exists an extension
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G)
of
\theta
lying over
\eta
.

(b)

If
\chi
is an irreducible extension of
\theta
, then there exists an extension
\eta\in{\rm Irr}(LH)
of
\varphi
lying under
\chi
.

Proof.

Working by induction on
|K:L|
and
|G:K|
, we can assume that
K/L
is a
G
-chief factor. Otherwise, let
K/M
be a chief factor of
G
, and
\mu\in\text{Irr}_{H}(M)
be the unique
H
-invariant irreducible character under
\theta
and over
\varphi
(this follows from Theorem
3.3
).

By induction, we have that if
\eta
is an irreducible extension of
\varphi
to
LH
, then there exists an irreducible extension
\gamma
of the character
\mu
to
HM
lying over
\eta
.
Then, again by induction, we have that there is an irreducible extension
\chi
of the character
\theta
to
G
lying over
\gamma
. Hence,
\chi
lies over
\eta
and we have proved (a).

Conversely, if
\chi
is an irreducible extension of
\theta
to
G
, then, by induction, there exists an irreducible extension
\gamma
of the character
\mu
to
HM
lying under
\chi
. Then, again by induction, we have that there is an extension
\eta
of the character
\varphi
to
LH
lying under
\eta
. Therefore,
\eta
lies under
\chi
and we have (b).

We may also assume that
\varphi
is
G
-invariant. Otherwise, since the inertia subgroup
I_{G}(\varphi)=LH
is normal in
G
and
K/L
is a
G
-chief factor, we have that
I_{K}(\varphi)=L
. Then, by Lemma
3.1
, induction defines a bijection from
\text{Irr}(LH\mid\varphi)
onto
\text{Irr}(G\mid\theta)
, and (a) and (b) follow.

Now, by the “going down” theorem, we have that either
\theta
is fully ramified with respect to
K/L
or
\theta
is an irreducible extension of
\varphi
to
K
. In the latter case, we have that the restriction defines a bijection
{\rm Irr}(G\mid\theta)\rightarrow{\rm Irr}(K\mid\varphi)
by Lemma
3.2
, and the result follows.

So, we may assume that
\theta
is fully ramified with respect to
K/L
. Therefore the result holds by Lemma
3.5
.
∎

4.
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
-characters

In this section, we present the
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
-characters defined in
[
14
]
for the particular case where
\mathfrak{N}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}
. Recall that saturated formations containing the nilpotent groups share the property of their respective projectors being self-normalizing. Thus, we may begin by noting that in this situation, if
G
is a solvable group and
H
is an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector of
G
, any normal subgroup
N
of
G
such that
NH\trianglelefteq\,G
satisfies in fact that
NH=G
(see Proposition
2.2
(d)).

The construction in
[
14
]
relies on Navarro’s definition of
G^{\mathfrak{F}_{n}}
, the unique minimal normal subgroup of
G
such that
G^{\mathfrak{F}_{n}}H\unlhd G
. It follows from the above paragraph that in our context
G^{\mathfrak{F}_{n}}
coincides with
G^{\mathfrak{F}}
, the
\mathfrak{F}
-residual. This particularly means that
G^{\mathfrak{F}}
is a proper subgroup of
G
.

Using the notation from
[
14
]
, we observe that the tuple
(U_{i},K_{i},L_{i},L_{i}H,U_{i})
satisfies
U_{i}=L_{i}H
for all
i
. We now introduce the notation that will be used throughout this paper.

Let
K_{0}=G^{\mathfrak{F}}
,
L_{0}=K_{0}^{\prime}
,
K_{i}=(L_{i-1}H)^{\mathfrak{F}}
, and
L_{i}=K_{i}^{\prime}
. Note that, since the
\mathfrak{F}
-residual is a proper subgroup, there exists a non-negative integer
m
such that the following chain of subgroups holds:

G>L_{0}H>L_{1}H>\cdots>L_{m-2}H>L_{m-1}H=H\,.

At this point,
K_{m}=(L_{m-1}H)^{\mathfrak{F}}=H^{\mathfrak{F}}=1
.
Thus, we have the following
H
-invariant subnormal series:

G\rhd K_{0}\rhd L_{0}\rhd\cdots\rhd K_{m-1}\rhd L_{m-1}\rhd 1\,.

Note that the subgroups in this chain satisfy
G=K_{0}H
,
L_{i}H=K_{i+1}H
for all
i\geq 0
, and that the Navarro condition is satisfied for
(K_{i}H,K_{i},L_{i})
.

We now proceed to describe the process for computing the characters. To this end, we introduce the following notation: we write
(L,\varphi)\triangleleft(K,\theta)
to indicate that
L
is a normal subgroup of
K
, and the irreducible character
\theta
of
K
lies over the irreducible character
\varphi
of
L
.

We say that
\chi
is an
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
-character
, and we write
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}}(G)
, if there exist characters
\theta_{i}\in{\rm Irr}(K_{i})
and
\varphi_{i}\in{\rm Irr}(L_{i})
, extending to
K_{i}H
and
L_{i}H
, respectively, such that

(1,1)\triangleleft(L_{m-1},\varphi_{m-1})\triangleleft(K_{m-1},\theta_{m-1})\triangleleft\cdots\triangleleft(L_{0},\varphi_{0})\triangleleft(K_{0},\theta_{0})\triangleleft(G,\chi)\,.

By the construction of the
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
-characters, if
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}}(G)
, then the extension
\psi_{i}
of
\varphi_{i}
to
L_{i}H

\theta_{i+1}
to

-character of

is an irreducible character.

Next, we provide information about the number of

-characters of a solvable group in terms of its

Proposition 4.1

Let

That is, the number of

Proof.

This follows from Theorem A of

.

5.
Strong pair series

In this section, we aim to present an alternative characterization of the
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
-characters. This characterization is analogous to the one given by Isaacs in
[
10
]
for the formation
\mathfrak{N}
of nilpotent groups, but it extends to all saturated formations containing
\mathfrak{N}
.

Let
G
be a solvable group and
H
an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector of
G
.
Suppose that
\{S_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq r}
is an
H
-composition series for
G
. This is an
H
-invariant, subnormal series such that
S_{i+1}/S_{i}
is
H
-simple (and thus abelian) for
0\leq i\leq r-1
. The
H
-composition length
r
is an invariant of
G
.

We say that
\{(S_{i},\theta_{i})\}_{0\leq i\leq r}
is an
H
-pair series
for
G
that is associated with the
H
-composition series
\{S_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq r}
, if the characters
\theta_{i}\in{\rm Irr}(S_{i})
are
H
-invariant and
\theta_{i}
lies under
\theta_{i+1}
for
0\leq i<r
. Sometimes, we will write the
H
-pair series as

(1,1)=(S_{0},\theta_{0})\triangleleft(S_{1},\theta_{1})\triangleleft\cdots\triangleleft(S_{r},\theta_{r})

to simplify the understanding of the chain of pairs being used.

We say that an
H
-pair series
\{(S_{i},\theta_{i})\}_{0\leq i\leq r}
for
G
is
strong
if each of the characters
\theta_{i}
extends irreducibly to
S_{i}H
. An irreducible character
\chi
of
G
is said to be
a head character
of
G
if
(G,\chi)
appears as
(S_{r},\theta_{r})
in a strong
H
-pair series.

It is worth noting that these (irreducible) extensions define, in turn, head characters of
S_{i}H
. Indeed, if
\theta_{i}
extends to
\eta_{i}\in{\rm Irr}(S_{i}H)
and
\{T_{j}\}_{0\leq j\leq m}
is such that
T_{0}=S_{i}
,
T_{m}=S_{i}H
and
T_{j+1}/T_{j}
is an
H
-composition factor for all
j
, then one can easily verify that

(S_{0},\theta_{0})\triangleleft\cdots\triangleleft(S_{i},\theta_{i})=(T_{0},\eta_{i_{\mid T_{0}}})\triangleleft\cdots\triangleleft(T_{j},\eta_{i_{\mid T_{j}}})\triangleleft\cdots\triangleleft(T_{m},\eta_{i})=(S_{i}H,\eta_{i})

is a strong
H
-pair series for
S_{i}H
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}
be a saturated formation with
\mathfrak{N}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}
. Let
G
be a solvable group, and let
H
be an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector of
G
. Suppose that
M/U
is an
H
-composition factor of
G
. Let
\alpha
be an irreducible character of
M
lying over the irreducible character
\theta
of
U
. If
\alpha
and
\theta
extend irreducibly to
MH
and
UH
respectively, then
\theta
is the unique
H
-invariant irreducible constituent of
U
lying under
\alpha
. Moreover, if
MH=UH
, then
\alpha_{U}=\theta
.

Proof.

We have that
M\cap UH
is a normal subgroup of
MU
that contains
U
and is contained in
M
. Since
M/U
is an
MH
-chief factor, it follows that
M\cap UH
is either
U
or
M
.

If
M\cap UH=U
, then
(MH,M,U)
satisfies the Navarro condition, and by Theorem
3.3
, we have that
\theta
is the unique
H
-invariant character of
U
lying under
\alpha
.

In the latter case, where
M\cap UH=M
, we have
MH=UH
. Let
\tilde{\theta}
be an extension of
\theta
to
MH
. Then
\tilde{\theta}_{M}
is irreducible. Since
M/U
is abelian, by Gallagher’s theorem, we have
\alpha=\lambda\tilde{\theta}_{M}
for some
\lambda\in\text{Lin}(M/U)
. Therefore,
\alpha_{U}=\theta
, which completes the proof.

∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}
be a saturated formation with
\mathfrak{N}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}
. Let
G
be a solvable group, and let
H
be an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector of
G
. Suppose that
M/V
and
M/U
are two distinct
H
-composition factors, and let
D=U\cap V
. If

(D,\gamma)\triangleleft(U,\theta)\triangleleft(M,\alpha)\,,

with
\gamma
,
\theta
, and
\alpha
extending irreducibly to
DH
,
UH
, and
MH
, respectively, then there exists an
H
-invariant irreducible character
\beta
of
V
, which extends irreducibly to
VH
and satisfies

(D,\gamma)\triangleleft(V,\beta)\triangleleft(M,\alpha)\,.

Proof.

Observe that both
M/U
and
M/V
are
MH
-chief factors; therefore, they are abelian, and
M/D
is abelian as well. Moreover,
M\cap UH
is either
M
or
U
. The same holds with
V
in place of
U
. Since
U/D\cong M/V
, we deduce that
U\cap DH
can be either
U
or
D
. Analogously, the same applies to
V/D
.

Assume first that
MH=VH=DH=UH
. By Lemma
5.1
,
it follows that
\alpha_{U}=\theta
and
\theta_{D}=\gamma
. Therefore,
\alpha
is an extension of
\gamma
. It suffices to consider
\beta=\alpha_{V}
.

Now, suppose that
MH=UH
but
VH<MH
. In this situation, we have that
VH\cap M=V
and that
DH<UH
, which implies
DH\cap U=D
. Since
D=DH\cap U\subseteq VH\cap U\subseteq MH\cap U=U
, it follows that
VH=DH
. Therefore,
M\cap DH=M\cap VH=V
.
Since
\theta
extends irreducibly to
MH=UH
and
\alpha
lies over
\theta
, and because
M/U
is abelian, as a consequence of Gallagher’s theorem, we have that
\alpha_{U}=\theta
.
Note that
(MH,M,V)
satisfies the Navarro condition.
Since
U/D
is a chief factor of
UH=MH
, it follows that either
\gamma
is
U
-invariant and hence
M
-invariant, or
\theta=\gamma^{U}
.

Therefore, there exists a unique irreducible character
\beta
that is
H
-invariant below
\alpha
and extends to
VH
, because
\alpha
does so. In the first case, we have that
\gamma
is the only irreducible constituent of
\alpha_{D}
, so it is clear that
\beta
is a character lying above
\gamma
. Suppose
\gamma
induces irreducibly to
U
. By applying Lemma
3.1
, it follows that
\beta\in{\rm Irr}(V\,|\,\gamma)
, as desired.

Next, suppose that
MH=VH
but
UH<MH
. Similarly to the previous paragraph, by interchanging
U
and
V
, we have
DH=UH
and
U=M\cap UH
. By Lemma
5.1
, it follows that
\theta_{D}=\gamma
. Observe that
(VH,V,D)
satisfies the Navarro condition. Consequently, there exists a unique character
\beta\in{\rm Irr}_{H}(V\,|\,\gamma)
. We want to show that
\beta
is a constituent of
\alpha_{V}
.

If
\theta
is
M
-invariant, we can apply Lemma
3.2
to deduce that
\alpha_{V}\in{\rm Irr}(V\,|\,\gamma)
. Thus,
\beta=\alpha_{V}
. Assume now that
\alpha=\theta^{M}
. In this case,
\beta
is an irreducible constituent of
\gamma^{V}=\theta_{D}^{V}=\theta^{M}_{V}=\alpha_{V}
.

Now we consider the case where
DH<MH=VH=UH
. We have
DH\cap U=D
and
DH\cap V=D
, and we also have
DH=XH
, where
X=M\cap DH
. This is the second case, considering
X
instead of
V
. Hence, there exists
\beta_{0}\in{\rm Irr}(X\,|\,\gamma)
that extends to
XH
and is a constituent of
\alpha_{X}
.

Now, by taking
X
instead of
U
, we have
MH=VH
and
DH=XH
. Then we are in the situation described in the previous case, where we know that there exists
\beta\in\text{Irr}(V\,|\,\gamma)
that extends to
VH
. Moreover, we have
\alpha_{V}=\beta
.

The final case is when
UH<MH
and
VH<MH
. It follows that
UH\cap M=U
and
VH\cap M=V
. Observe that
DH<UH
, as otherwise we would have
U=M\cap UH=M\cap DH\subseteq M\cap VH=V
, which leads to a contradiction. (Similarly,
DH<VH
.) Since both
(MH,M,V)
and
(VH,V,D)
satisfy the Navarro condition, the theorem follows as a direct consequence of applying Theorem
3.3
twice.
∎

The proof of the theorem below follows that of Theorem 5.5 of
[
10
]
, differing only in the final step.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}
be a saturated formation with
\mathfrak{N}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}
. Let
G
be a solvable group, and let
H
be an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector of
G
. Suppose
\{T_{i}\mid 0\leq i\leq r\}
is an arbitrary
H
-composition series for
G
. Let
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G)
be a head character of
G
. Then the series
\{T_{i}\}
is associated with some unique strong
H
-pair series
\{(T_{i},\varphi_{i})\mid 0\leq i\leq r\}
such that
\varphi_{r}=\chi
.

Proof.

Since
\chi
is a head character of
G
, by definition, there exists a strong
H
-pair series

(1,1)=(S_{0},\theta_{0})\triangleleft(S_{1},\theta_{1})\triangleleft\cdots\triangleleft(S_{r},\theta_{r})=(G,\chi)\,.

We want to show the existence of characters
\varphi_{i}\in{\rm Irr}(T_{i})
satisfying that

(1,1)=(T_{0},\varphi_{0})\triangleleft(T_{1},\varphi_{1})\triangleleft\cdots\triangleleft(T_{r},\varphi_{r})=(G,\chi)\,

is a strong
H
-pair series.

Now, note that
S_{r}=G=T_{r}
. Therefore, there exists a unique smallest non-negative integer
m
such that
S_{i}=T_{i}
for all
i
satisfying
m\leq i\leq r
. If
m=0
, then
T_{i}=S_{i}
for all
i
, and we can set
\varphi_{i}=\theta_{i}
, concluding the proof. Thus, we assume
m>0
and proceed by downward induction on
m
.

We have
S_{m}=T_{m}
, but
S_{m-1}\neq T_{m-1}
. Since
S_{0}=1=T_{0}
, it follows that
m-1>0
, and hence
m\geq 2
. Define
M=S_{m}=T_{m}
,
U=S_{m-1}
, and
V=T_{m-1}
. Write
D=U\cap V
. Clearly,
U
and
V
are nontrivial and distinct. Furthermore,
M/U
and
M/V
are
H
-composition factors of
G
, so
U
and
V
are maximal among
H
-invariant normal subgroups of
M
. As
U\neq V
, it follows that
UV=M
. And,
U/D
is
H
-isomorphic to
M/V
, thus
U/D
is an
H
-composition factor of
G
, and similarly,
V/D
is an
H
-composition factor of
G
.

The
H
-composition length of
U=S_{m-1}
is
m-1
, implying that the
H
-composition length of
D
is
m-2
. Thus, we can choose an
H
-composition series

1=D_{0}\triangleleft D_{1}\triangleleft\cdots\triangleleft D_{m-2}=D

for
D
. Appending the subgroups
S_{i}
for
m-1\leq i\leq r
to this series yields a new
H
-composition series for
G
. Denote this series by
\{N_{i}\}
, where
N_{i}=D_{i}
for
0\leq i\leq m-2
, and
N_{i}=S_{i}
for
m-1\leq i\leq r
. In particular,
N_{m-1}=S_{m-1}=U
and
N_{m-2}=D_{m-2}=D
.

Using the inductive hypothesis with the series
\{N_{i}\}
in place of
\{T_{i}\}
and
m-1
in place of
m
, we conclude that
\{N_{i}\}
is associated with a strong
H
-pair series with head character
\chi
. Moreover, for
i\geq m
, we have
T_{i}=S_{i}=N_{i}
. Hence, we may replace
\{S_{i}\}
with
\{N_{i}\}
without loss of generality, and we can assume
N_{i}=S_{i}
for all
i
. In particular,
D=D_{m-2}=N_{m-2}=S_{m-2}
.

Next, construct a new
H
-composition series for
G
by modifying
\{S_{i}\}
, replacing
S_{m-1}=U
with
V
. Note that
S_{m-2}=D\triangleleft V\triangleleft M=S_{m}
. And since
V/D
is
H
-isomorphic to
M/U=S_{m}/S_{m-1}
and
M/V
is
H
-isomorphic to
T_{m}/T_{m-1}
, the series is an
H
-composition series. We denote this new series by
\{X_{i}\}
, where
X_{i}=S_{i}
for
i\neq m-1
and
X_{m-1}=V
.

Finally, by Theorem
5.3
we have that
\{X_{i}\}
is associated with a strong
H
-pair series with head character
\chi
. Assuming this is established, note that
T_{i}=S_{i}=X_{i}
for
i\geq m
and
T_{m-1}=V=X_{m-1}
. Thus, the inductive hypothesis applies to
\{X_{i}\}
in place of
\{S_{i}\}
with
m-1
in place of
m
. Therefore the original series
\{T_{i}\}
is associated with a strong
H
-pair series with head character
\chi
.

The uniqueness of this strong
H
-pair series follows from Lemma
5.1
.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}
be a saturated formation with
\mathfrak{N}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}
. Let
G
be a solvable group, and let
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G)
. Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

\chi
is an
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
-character of
G
;

(b)

\chi
is a head character of
G
.

Proof.

Suppose
\chi
is an
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
-character of
G
. By construction, there exist characters
\theta_{i}\in{\rm Irr}(K_{i})
and
\varphi_{i}\in{\rm Irr}(L_{i})
, extending to
K_{i}H
and
L_{i}H
, respectively, such that

(1,1)\triangleleft(L_{m-1},\varphi_{m-1})\triangleleft(K_{m-1},\theta_{m-1})\triangleleft\cdots\triangleleft(L_{0},\varphi_{0})\triangleleft(K_{0},\theta_{0})\triangleleft(G,\chi)\,.

where

K_{0}=G^{\mathfrak{F}}
,
L_{0}=K_{0}^{\prime}
,
K_{i}=(L_{i-1}H)^{\mathfrak{F}}
, and
L_{i}=K_{i}^{\prime}
.

From this series we can obtain an
H
-subnormal series. Consider
(U,\alpha)\triangleleft(V,\beta)
, where
U/V
is
H
-simple. If
L_{i}\leq U<V\leq K_{i}
, then
(VH,V,U)
satisfies the Navarro condition. By applying Theorem
3.3
to each

-composition factor between

and

, it follows that
\alpha
and
\beta
are

-invariant and extend irreducibly to

and

, respectively.

If

and

, which clearly extend irreducibly to

, respectively.

\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
-characters are constructed in relation to an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector, we are only interested in
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
-characters of subgroups having as an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector the same one we fixed for
G
, that is, subgroups containing
H
.

When considering restriction of
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
-characters
\chi
of
G
to normal subgroups
N
that might not contain
H
, we prove instead that there always exists an
H
-invariant constituent of
\chi_{N}
.

If
G
is of odd order whenever
\mathfrak{N}\neq\mathfrak{F}
, this is a direct consequence of Theorem
6.2
, using the fact that
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
-characters of
NH
restrict irreducibly to
(NH)^{\mathfrak{F}}
and that, by definition,
(NH)^{\mathfrak{F}}
is contained in
N
. Nonetheless, the result remains true without this hypothesis, as we state in Proposition
6.3
below. We point out that Theorem
6.2
does, however, require
G
having odd order if
\mathfrak{N}\neq\mathfrak{F}
. The group SmallGroup(48,28) of order 48 and the saturated formation of supersolvable groups provide a counterexample to removing this hypothesis.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}
be a saturated formation such that
\mathfrak{N}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}
, and let
G
be a solvable group. Suppose
H
is an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector of
G
.
If
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}}(G)
and
N
is a normal subgroup of
G
, then
\chi_{N}
has an
H
-invariant constituent.

Proof.

We can find an
H
-composition series that passes through
G
via
N
. Let us consider

1=S_{0}\triangleleft\cdots\triangleleft S_{m}=N\triangleleft\cdots\triangleleft S_{r}=G\,.

By Theorem
5.3
and Theorem
5.4
, this series is associated with a strong
H
-pair series
\{(S_{i},\theta_{i})\}_{0\leq i\leq r}
such that
\theta_{r}=\chi
. Then
\theta_{m}
is a constituent of
\chi_{N}
which extends to
NH
. In particular, it is
H
-invariant.
∎

7.
Theorem B

In this final section, we prove Theorem B in the more general context of saturated formations containing all nilpotent groups. This is formulated in Theorem
7.5
. We first present some preliminary results.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}
be a saturated formation with
\mathfrak{N}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}
. Let
G
be a solvable group, and let
H
be an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector of
G
. Let
M,T
be
H
-invariant subgroups of
G
such that
T\triangleleft M
and
M/T
is
H
-simple. Suppose
M\cap H\subseteq H^{\prime}
. If
\varphi
is an irreducible
H
-invariant character of
M
with
T
in its kernel that extends to
MH
, then
\varphi
is the trivial character.

Proof.

Since
M/T
is
H
-simple, it is abelian, and thus
M/T(H\cap M)
is also abelian. Notice too that, since
H
is an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector of
G
and in particular of
MH
, we have that
HT(H\cap M)/T(H\cap M)=HT/T(H\cap M)
is an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector of
MH/T(H\cap M)
. Let
\varphi
be an irreducible
H
-invariant character of
M
that extends to
MH
and has
T
in its kernel. By applying Theorem
3.3
(a) to
MH
, we know that there exists a unique
\xi\in{\rm Irr}_{H}(T(H\cap M))
lying under
\varphi
. Moreover,
\xi
extends to some
\eta\in{\rm Irr}(TH)
by Theorem
3.3
(c).

Since
\varphi
has
T
in its kernel,
\xi
also has
T
in its kernel. Notice that, since
M/T
is abelian,
\varphi
is linear, and thus so are
\xi
and
\eta
. Then
\eta
can be seen as a character in
\text{Lin}(HT/T)
with
H^{\prime}T/T
in its kernel. Thus,
H^{\prime}T\subseteq{\rm ker}(\eta)
. But
(H\cap M)T\subseteq H^{\prime}T
since
H\cap M\subseteq H^{\prime}
. Hence,
\xi=\eta_{\mid(H\cap M)T}=1_{(H\cap M)T}
. Since
\varphi
is by Theorem
3.3
(a) the unique
H
-invariant character lying over
\xi
, it must be
1_{M}
.
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}
be a saturated formation with
\mathfrak{N}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}
. Let
G
be a solvable group, and let
H
be an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector of
G
. Suppose that
N
is a normal subgroup of
G
such that
N\cap H\subseteq H^{\prime}
. Then
N\subseteq{\rm ker}(\chi)
for all
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}}(G)
.

Proof.

Let us consider a fixed
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}}(G)
. By Theorem
5.4
, there exists a strong
H
-pair series of the form

(1,1)=(S_{0},\varphi_{0})\trianglelefteq\cdots\trianglelefteq(S_{m},\varphi_{m})=(N,\varphi_{m})\trianglelefteq\cdots\trianglelefteq(S_{r},\varphi_{r})=(G,\chi).

Every
1\leq i\leq m
satisfies that
S_{i}
and
S_{i-1}
are
H
-invariant,
S_{i}/S_{i-1}
is
H
-simple and
S_{i}\cap H\subseteq H^{\prime}
. Moreover,
\varphi_{0}=1_{S_{0}}
. Then, by repeatedly applying Lemma
7.1
, we deduce that
\varphi_{i}=1_{S_{i}}
for every
0\leq i\leq m
. In particular,
\varphi_{m}=1_{N}
. But
N\triangleleft G
and
\varphi_{m}
is a constituent of
\chi_{N}
. Therefore
N\subseteq{\rm ker}(\chi)
.
∎

If
N
is a normal subgroup of
G
, every irreducible character
\tilde{\chi}
of the quotient group
G/N
can be regarded, by inflation, as a character of
G
whose kernel contains
N
. In this case, we denote the corresponding character in
{\rm Irr}(G)
by
\chi
, where
N\subseteq{\rm ker}(\chi)
, and vice versa (Lemma (2.2) of
[
9
]
).

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}
be a saturated formation with
\mathfrak{N}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}
. Suppose that
N
is a normal subgroup of a solvable group
G
. Then

(a)

if
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}}(G)
such that
N\subseteq{\rm ker}(\chi)
, then
\tilde{\chi}\in{\rm Irr}_{\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}}(G/N)
;

(b)

if
\tilde{\chi}\in{\rm Irr}_{\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}}(G/N)
, then
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G)
is an
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
-character.

Proof.

Let
H
be an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector of
G
. First suppose that
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}}(G)
is such that
N\subseteq{\rm ker}(\chi)
.
By Theorem
5.4
, there exists a strong
H
-pair series

(1,1)=(S_{0},\varphi_{0})\trianglelefteq\,(S_{1},\varphi_{1})\trianglelefteq\,\cdots\trianglelefteq\,(S_{m},\varphi_{m})=(N,\varphi_{m})\trianglelefteq\,\cdots\trianglelefteq\,(S_{r},\varphi_{r})=(G,\chi)\,.

Since
S_{m}\subseteq{\rm ker}(\chi)
, and
\varphi_{m}
lies under
\chi
, we have that
\varphi_{m}=1_{N}
is the trivial character. Thus,
\varphi_{i}=1_{S_{i}}
for every
0\leq i\leq m
. Now, let
\bar{G}=G/N
. We have that
\bar{H}=HN/N
is an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector of
\bar{G}
. Adopting the bar convention for the quotients and the tilde convention for their irreducible characters, we can consider the following
\bar{H}
-pair series,

(\bar{1},\tilde{1})=(\bar{S}_{m},\tilde{\varphi}_{m})\trianglelefteq\,\cdots\trianglelefteq\,(\bar{S}_{i},\tilde{\varphi_{i}})\trianglelefteq\,\cdots\trianglelefteq\,(\bar{S}_{r},\tilde{\varphi}_{r})=(\bar{G},\tilde{\chi})

which is a strong
\bar{H}
-pair series as it is not difficult to verify. Hence,
\tilde{\chi}
is an
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
-character of
\bar{G}
.

Now, suppose that
\tilde{\chi}\in{\rm Irr}_{\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}}(G/N)
, then there is a strong
\bar{H}
-pair series

(\bar{1},\tilde{1})=(\bar{S_{0}},\tilde{\varphi_{0}})\triangleleft\cdots\triangleleft(\bar{S_{i}},\tilde{\varphi_{i}})\triangleleft\cdots\triangleleft(\bar{S_{r}},\tilde{\varphi_{r}})=(\bar{G},\tilde{\chi})\,.

Now, let us consider an
H
-composition series of
N

1=R_{0}\triangleleft R_{1}\triangleleft\cdots\triangleleft R_{s}=N\,.

It suffices to consider the following series, viewing the characters of the quotient as characters of the group with
N
in the kernel, as we have mentioned previously:

(1,1)=(R_{0},1)\trianglelefteq\,\cdots\trianglelefteq\,(R_{s},1_{R_{s}})=(N,1_{N})=({S_{0}},{\varphi_{0}})\trianglelefteq\,\cdots\trianglelefteq\,({S_{r}},{\varphi_{r}})=(G,{\chi})\ ,

which again may be verified to be a strong
H
-pair series without difficulty.
∎

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}
be a saturated formation with
\mathfrak{N}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}
. Let
G
be a solvable group
G
. Suppose that
N
is a normal subgroup in
G
contained in
\bigcap_{\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}}(G)}{\rm ker}(\chi)
.
Then

|{\rm Irr}_{\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}}(G)|=|{\rm Irr}_{\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}}(G/N)|\,.

Moreover,
N\cap H\subseteq H^{\prime}
for any
\mathfrak{F}
-projector
H
of
G
.

Proof.

The previous lemma give us an injection
{\rm Irr}_{\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}}(G)\to{\rm Irr}_{\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}}(G/N)
. Hence

|H/H^{\prime}|=|{\rm Irr}_{\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}}(G)|\leq|{\rm Irr}_{\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}}(G/N)|=|H/D|\,,

where
H
is any
\mathfrak{F}
-projector of
G
and
D/(H\cap N)=(H/(H\cap N))^{\prime}
. Then, it follows that
|H^{\prime}|\geq|D|
, but also
H^{\prime}\subseteq D
since
D/(H\cap N)=H^{\prime}(H\cap N)/(H\cap N)
. Then
|H^{\prime}|=|D|
and we have the first part of the lemma. We conclude for the second part by noting that
H\cap N\subseteq D
and
D=H^{\prime}
.
∎

Theorem 7.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}
be a saturated formation with
\mathfrak{N}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}
. Let
G
be a solvable group, and let
H
be an
\mathfrak{F}
-projector of
G
. Then

\bigcap_{\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}}(G)}{\rm ker}(\chi)

is the largest normal subgroup
M
of
G
satisfying
M\cap H\subseteq H^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
M=\bigcap_{\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}}(G)}{\rm ker}(\chi)
. It is clear that
M\cap H\subseteq H^{\prime}
from the previous lemma.

Suppose
N
and
K
are two normal subgroups of
G
such that
N\cap H\subseteq H^{\prime}
and
K\cap H\subseteq H^{\prime}
. It follows that
NK\cap H=(N\cap H)(K\cap H)\subseteq H^{\prime}
, by Lemma
2.4
. Thus, there exists a maximal subgroup
N
satisfying
N\cap H\subseteq H^{\prime}
. The only remaining step is now to prove that if
N
is a normal subgroup of
G
such that
N\cap H\subseteq H^{\prime}
, then
N\subseteq M
. This follows from Lemma
7.2
, completing the proof of the theorem.
∎

The above theorem is the exact analog of an unpublished result by Navarro, which we present here with his permission. Note that, for a prime
p
and a solvable group
G
, the Sylow
p
-subgroups of
G
and the characters of
G
of
p^{\prime}
-degree correspond respectively to the
\mathfrak{F}
-projectors and the
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
-characters of
G
when
\mathfrak{F}
is the formation of
p
-groups. This is a saturated formation not containing
\mathfrak{N}
.

Theorem 7.6

(Navarro)
.

Let
G
be a finite group, let
p
be a prime, and let
P\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
. Then

K=\bigcap_{\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)}{\rm ker}(\chi)

is the largest normal subgroup
K
of
G
satisfying
{\bf N}_{K}(P)\subseteq P^{\prime}
.

Proof.

If
N,M
are normal subgroups of
G
, by Lemma 2.1 of
[
11
]
, we have that

{\bf N}_{NM}(P)={\bf N}_{M}(P){\bf N}_{N}(P)\,.

Therefore there exists a largest normal subgroup
N
of
G
such that
{\bf N}_{N}(P)
is contained in
P^{\prime}
. Since
N\cap P={\bf N}_{N}(P)\subset P^{\prime}
, by Tate’s theorem (Theorem 6.31 of
[
9
]
), it follows that
{\bf O}^{p}(PN)\cap P={\bf O}^{p}(P)=1
. Therefore,
PN
has a normal
p
-complement, and
N
also has one. Let
W
be the normal
p
-complement of
N
. Then, we have
{\bf C}_{W}(P)=1
because
{\bf C}_{W}(P)\subseteq{\bf N}_{N}(P)\cap W\subseteq P^{\prime}\cap W=1
. Let
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
, and let
\nu\in{\rm Irr}(W)
be a
P
-invariant irreducible constituent of the restriction
\chi_{W}
, which exists beacause
\chi
has
p^{\prime}
-degree. By Glauberman’s correspondence (Theorem 2.9 of
[
12
]
), we have that
\nu
is the trivial character of
W
. Hence,
W\subseteq{\rm ker}(\chi)
. So, working in
G/W
we may assume that
N
is a
p
-group contained in
P^{\prime}
. Now, let
\epsilon\in{\rm Irr}(P)
be a linear constituent of
\chi_{P}
. Since
1_{N}
is a constituent of
\epsilon_{N}
, we have that
N\subseteq{\rm ker}(\chi)
.

Let

K=\bigcap_{\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)}{\rm ker}(\chi)\,.

It only remains to prove that
{\bf N}_{K}(P)\subseteq P^{\prime}
.
By Theorem 7.7 of
[
12
]
,
K
has a normal
p
-complement
Y
. If
\delta\in{\rm Irr}(Y)
is
P
-invariant, then there is an extension
\eta
of
\delta
to
YP
(by Corollary 6.28 of
[
9
]
), and therefore
\delta
lies under some irreducible character of
G
of
p^{\prime}
-degree (using that
\eta^{G}
has
p^{\prime}
-degree). Thus
\delta
is the trivial character of
Y
, and hence
{\bf C}_{Y}(P)=1
by Glauberman’s correspondence. Thus
{\bf N}_{K}(P)=P\cap K
. We may assume that
K
is a
p
-group. Suppose that
K
is not contained in
P^{\prime}
. Then some linear
\lambda\in{\rm Irr}(P)
does not contain
K
in its kernel. By inducing up to
G
, we obtain some
\chi\in\text{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
over
\lambda
and therefore a contradiction.
∎

Non-invasive prediction of B-cell lymphoma-2 and Ki-67 expression in primary central nervous system lymphoma using whole-tumor histogram analysis of diffusion weighted imaging, diffusional kurtosis imaging and intravoxel incoherent motion
Authors: Xiaofang Zhou, Feng Wang, Shujie Yu, Feifei Yu, Xiaoye Lin, Dairong Cao, Zhen Xing
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo evaluate and compare the diagnostic performance of whole-tumor histogram analysis using multiple diffusion MRI models for prediction of B-cell lymphoma-2 (BCL-2) and Ki-67 expression in PCNSL.MethodsEighty-one participants who underwent conventional diffusion weighted imaging (DWI), diffusional kurtosis imaging (DKI), and intravoxel incoherent motion (IVIM) examinations between January 2020 and January 2025 were enrolled in this study. Whole-tumor histogram features extracted from apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC), diffusion coefficient (Dk), diffusional kurtosis (K), pure diffusion coefficient (D), pseudo-diffusion coefficient (D*), and perfusion fraction (f) maps were compared between different BCL-2 and Ki-67 expression groups using Mann-Whitney U test. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis and logistic regression analysis were used to evaluate the diagnostic performance of different diffusion models. Internal validation was performed using bootstrap resampling with 1000 repetitions.ResultsA total of 21 and 26 histogram features derived from diffusion maps were identified as effective for distinguishing different BCL-2 and Ki-67 expression statuses in PCNSL (all P < 0.05), respectively. Among the DWI, DKI, IVIM, and combined models, no significant differences were observed in the areas under the receiver operating characteristic curves (AUCs) for predicting BCL-2 expression (AUCs (95%CI): 0.810 (0.683–0.903), 0.830 (0.706–0.917), 0.901 (0.798-1.000), and 0.877 (0.769–0.984), respectively; all corrected P > 0.05, Bonferroni correction) or Ki-67 index (AUCs (95%CI): 0.832 (0.732–0.906), 0.769 (0.662–0.856), 0.792 (0.688–0.874), and 0.824 (0.724-0.900), respectively; all corrected P > 0.05, Bonferroni correction) in PCNSL. Bootstrap internal validation suggested limited model optimism and acceptable calibration.ConclusionsWhole-tumor histogram analysis based on diffusion MRI is an effective noninvasive method for predicting BCL-2 expression and Ki-67 index in PCNSL. No statistically significant difference was detected among the three diffusion models in this preliminary study.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12880-026-02456-3.Keywords: Primary central nervous system lymphoma, B-cell lymphoma-2, Ki-67, Diffusion MRI

Introduction

Primary central nervous system lymphoma (PCNSL) is a highly aggressive and heterogeneous subtype of non-Hodgkin lymphoma confined to the CNS [1, 2], accounting for approximately 5% of all intracranial tumors [3]. Over 90% of PCNSLs are pathologically classified as diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) [4]. Although first-line chemotherapy regimens based on high-dose methotrexate have significantly improved patient outcomes, approximately 40% of individuals still progress to relapsed or refractory disease [5]. The marked genetic and molecular heterogeneity of PCNSL is believed to underlie the variability in treatment response and collectively contributes to the disease aggressiveness [6].

B-cell lymphoma-2 (BCL-2), a tumor suppressor gene, located at chromosome 18q21 [7]. BCL-2 protein overexpression enables tumor cells to evade immune surveillance, promotes aberrant proliferation and malignant transformation, and enhances drug resistance [8, 9]. BCL-2 overexpression serves as a robust and clinically accessible independent predictor of poor prognosis in PCNSL [10]. The Ki-67 labeling index is a nuclear antigen associated with tumor proliferative activity. A high Ki-67 index indicates active tumor cell proliferation and is associated with rapid disease progression and inferior prognosis [11]. Therefore, assessing BCL-2 overexpression and Ki-67 index is crucial for guiding optimal treatment selection and providing prognostic information. Currently, pathological diagnosis based on invasive tissue biopsy remains the gold standard for evaluating BCL-2 and Ki-67 expression. However, biopsy procedures are inherently invasive and carry the risk of sampling error. Consequently, there is an urgent need to develop non-invasive imaging biomarkers capable of comprehensively characterizing tumor heterogeneity.

Diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) quantitatively evaluates Brownian motion of water molecules in biological tissues by single-exponential model, providing a noninvasive method to assess tumor characteristics. Previous studies have shown that conventional analysis of DWI has limited performance in assessing Ki-67 index in PCNSL [12]. Although prior study incorporating histogram features derived from DWI reported improved diagnostic performance, it is limited by small sample sizes [13]. Advanced diffusion-weighted imaging techniques, including diffusion kurtosis imaging (DKI) utilizing the non-Gaussian distribution model [14] and intravoxel incoherent motion (IVIM) based on the biexponential model [15], enable the separation and extraction of information related to both water molecular diffusion and microcirculatory perfusion. These methods provide more accurate characterization of intratumoral water mobility and have proven effective in assessing tissue heterogeneity in various other tumors [16–21]. However, to the best of our knowledge, no studies have systematically applied and compared the efficacy of DWI, IVIM, and DKI techniques in evaluating BCL-2 and Ki-67 expression in PCNSL.

Therefore, the aim of this study is to evaluate and compare the predictive performance of whole-tumor histogram analysis of DWI, IVIM, and DKI in assessing BCL-2 and Ki-67 expression in PCNSL.

Materials and methods

Patients

The study was approved by Institutional Review Board of the First Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University. Individual informed consent was waived due to the retrospective study design. From January 2020 to January 2025, a total of 95 consecutive patients diagnosed with PCNSL were enrolled according to the following inclusion criteria: (1) histopathological confirmation of DLBCL obtained through surgical resection or biopsy; (2) MRI obtained prior to any therapeutic intervention; 3)full diffusion protocol comprising DWI, IVIM and DKI sequences performed; 4) available quantitative data on Ki-67 index; 5) immunocompetent adults (age > 18 years). The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) MRI examinations performed using scanners with magnetic field strengths below 3.0 Tesla (n = 5); (2) incomplete MRI acquisitions or inadequate image quality for analysis (n = 9). The final cohort comprised 81 participants. Ki-67 indices were obtained for all subjects, and BCL-2 protein expression levels were available in 56 of them. MRI protocols

MRI examinations were performed on all patients using a 3.0T system (Siemens Magnetom Prisma or Skyra) equipped with a 20-channel head and neck coil. Standard conventional MRI protocols included axial T2-weighted imaging (T2WI) and pre-/post-contrast T1-weighted imaging (T1WI). Multi–b-value DWI was acquired in the axial plane using a single-shot echo-planar imaging sequence. Imaging parameters included a repetition time of 5000 ms and an echo time of 78 ms, with field of view of 220 × 220 mm²and an acquisition matrix of 150 × 135. A parallel imaging factor of 2 was applied. Slice thickness was set to 5 mm with a 1 mm interslice gap. The scan duration was approximately 8 min and 25 s. A total of 13 b-values (b = 0, 50, 100, 150, 200, 300, 400, 600, 700, 800, 1000, 1400, and 2000 s/mm²) were applied along three orthogonal diffusion directions, with corresponding signal averages of 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 3, 3, 4, and 4, respectively.

Image processing and analysis

For the conventional DWI model, the data fitting was conducted based on the following equation: Sb/S0 = exp(-b×ADC), where Sb and S0 represent the signal intensities at a given b-value and at b = 0 s/mm2, respectively. The ADC map was calculated using two b-values (0 and 1000 s/mm²). In the IVIM model, the following parameters (true diffusion coefficient D, pseudodiffusion coefficient D*, perfusion fraction f) were derived from 11 b-values (0, 50, 100, 150, 200, 300, 400, 600, 700, 800, 1000 s/mm²) using the equation: Sb/S0 = (1- f)× exp (-b × D) + f × exp [-b × (D + D*)]. In the DKI model, diffusion signal intensities from 4 b-values (0, 700, 1400, 2000 s/mm²) were fitted with: Sb/S0 = exp (-b × Dk + b2 × Dk2 × K/6) according to the previously reported DKI fitting scheme in intracranial tumors [19]. Dk is the diffusion coefficient, adjusted ADC without Gaussian bias; and K represents diffusional kurtosis. DWI, IVIM, and DKI analyses were conducted using prototype software (Diffusion Toolbox, Siemens), generating the following quantitative parametric maps: ADC, D, D*, f, Dk, and K maps.

Two neuroradiologists (X.Z. with 7 years and S.Y. with 3 years of neuroimaging experience) who were blinded to BCL-2 and Ki-67 status, manually delineated the volumes of interest (VOIs) through consensus. In cases of discrepancies, the final decision was made by a senior radiologist (D.C., with 32 years of experience in neuroradiology). The VOIs were carefully traced along solid tumor boundaries on ADC and D maps, respectively, with reference to T2WI and contrast-enhanced T1WI to exclude cystic components, necrotic areas, hemorrhagic foci, and vascular structures.The VOIs initially drawn on the D maps were then applied to the corresponding parametric maps derived from IVIM and DKI models, including D*, f, Dk, and K maps. VOI delineation was carried out using the open-source software platform 3D Slicer (version 4.13.0; https://www.slicer.org/). To evaluate potential distortion-related VOI mismatch, a random subset of 10 patients was selected, and the Dice similarity coefficient was calculated between ADC-based VOIs and D-based VOIs. To assess inter-observer reliability, another neuroradiologist (F.Y., 3 years of experience) independently measured the diffusion parameters in 30 randomly selected lesions.

Histogram features of DWI, IVIM, and DKI were extracted using FeAture Explorer, an open-source analysis tool (FAE, version 0.5.5; https://github.com/salan668/FAE) based on the Image Biomarker Standardization Initiative. All maps were first resampled to an in-plane resolution of (0.69, 0.69), and the VOIs were re-segmented using the 3-sigma method. Histogram-based features were extracted from the volume directly. The histogram features extracted using this software included the following: 10th percentile (P10), 90th percentile (P90), energy, entropy, interquartile range (IQR), kurtosis, maximum, mean absolute deviation (MAD), mean, median, minimum, range, robust mean absolute deviation (RMAD), root mean squared (RMS), skewness, uniformity, variance.

Histopathological and immunohistochemical evaluation

BCL-2 and Ki-67 expression was performed on formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded biopsy or surgical specimens following hematoxylin-eosin staining and immunohistochemical analysis, and were evaluated by an experienced neuropathologist (with 9 years of experience). A BCL-2 expression threshold of 50% was used to define positivity: cases with expression ≥ 50% were classified as BCL-2 overexpression /BCL-2 (+), while those with expression < 50% were considered BCL-2 low expression /BCL-2 (−) based on the 2016 World Health Organization classification for double-expressor lymphoma [22]. The Ki-67 index was categorized as low (≤ 70%) or high (> 70%) based on its prognostic significance [11].

Statistical analysis

The Shapiro–Wilk test and Levene’s test were used to assess the normality and homogeneity of variance of continuous variables, respectively. All data were appropriately presented as mean ± standard deviation, median (interquartile range), or number of cases, as applicable. Differences in age and sex between groups were analyzed using the t-test and chi-square test, respectively. The Mann–Whitney U test was employed to compare the differences in histogram parameters extracted from all diffusion imaging sequences between subgroups. Histogram features with statistically significant differences were subjected to binary logistic regression analysis using a backward stepwise selection approach to establish both individual and combined models. Multicollinearity was assessed using the variance inflation factor (VIF), and variables with VIF > 5 were considered collinear. Among collinear variables, the one with stronger diagnostic performance was retained. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to compare the diagnostic performance of different diffusion models. The DeLong test was used to assess differences in the area under the curve (AUC) between models, and Bonferroni correction was applied to minimize type I error due to multiple pairwise comparisons among diffusion models. To assess the robustness of model performance and reduce optimism bias, internal validation was performed using bootstrap resampling with 1000 repetitions. Optimism-corrected AUCs and bootstrap-corrected calibration curves were obtained to assess model performance after correction for optimism. Statistical significance was defined as P value below 0.05. All statistical analyses in this study were conducted using R version 4.5.2, IBM SPSS Statistics version 25.0 and MedCalc version 15.6.

Results

Participant demographics

Eighty-one participants (male: female = 31:50) with a mean age of 61 ± 11 years (range 21–77 years) were included in this study. Based on immunohistochemical results, 17 of 81 patients with PCNSL (21.0%) were classified as low Ki-67 index, while 64 patients (79.0%) exhibited high Ki-67 index. Regarding BCL-2 expression, 11 of 56 patients (19.6%) were classified as BCL-2 (−), whereas 45 patients (80.4%) were classified as BCL-2 (+). There were no statistically significant differences in age or sex distribution among subgroups with different Ki-67 and BCL-2 expression levels. The diagnostic performance of multiple diffusion model for differentiating Low from high Ki-67 index

In the single diffusion models, the logistic regression analysis identified ADCP10, ADCIQR, KRMAD, DP10, and Dentropy as the most informative parameters for distinguishing between low and high Ki-67 groups. In the combined model, ADCMAD and DKP10 emerged as valuable features for differentiating Ki-67 status.

As presented in Table 4 and Fig. 4, ROC analysis demonstrated that all three individual diffusion models and the combined diffusion model, exhibited acceptablediagnostic performance in differentiating between low and high Ki-67 expression levels (AUC = 0.769–0.832). The DWI model achieved the highest AUC (AUC = 0.832). No statistically significant differences in AUC values were observed between the individual diffusion models and the combined model for distinguishing low from high Ki-67 expression in PCNSL (Table S3; all Bonferroni-corrected P > 0.05).

The diagnostic performance of multiple diffusion model for differentiating negative from positive BCL-2 expression

Logistic regression analysis of the individual diffusion models identified ADCmean, DkP10, D*skewness, and fenergy as the most useful features for classifying BCL-2-negative and BCL-2-positive group. In the combined diffusion model, Dmean, D*skewness, fenergy, and DkIQR were recognized as key parameters for effectively stratifying BCL-2 expression status.

Notably, the single IVIM model and combined diffusion model yielded the higher AUC values (0.901 and 0.877, respectively) compared to DWI and DKI model. However, no statistically significant differences in AUC values were observed between the individual diffusion models and the combined model after Bonferroni correction (Table S3; all corrected P > 0.05). Discussion

Non-invasive prediction of BCL-2 and Ki-67 status in PCNSL holds significant clinical value for treatment strategy planning and prognosis evaluation. Our study shows that both individual and combined models based on whole-tumor histogram features from DWI, DKI, and IVIM effectively classify BCL-2 and Ki-67 expression in PCNSL. Notably, no statistically significant difference was detected among the three diffusion models in this preliminary study.

Ki-67, serves as a reliable indicator of lymphoma proliferation activity and malignancy [11]. Previous studies have demonstrated a significant correlation between ADC values and the Ki-67 index across various tumor types [16–20, 23]; however, research focusing on PCNSL remains relatively limited. A study by Schob et al. [13]. reported a strong correlation between ADCP90 and the Ki-67 index in PCNSL; however, the sample size was limited, with only 18 cases included. With a comparatively larger cohort, our results confirmed significantly reduced ADCP10, ADCminimum, ADCmean, and ADCmedian in high versus low Ki-67 groups, aligning with established literature [18–20]. Moreover, we identified elevated ADCentropy, ADCIQR, ADCRMAD, and ADCvariance values in tumors with high Ki-67 expression relative to their low-expression group. Entropy reflects the uncertainty or randomness in the distribution of image gray values, IQR reflects the spread of the central voxel values, RMAD quantify signal dispersion around the mean, while variance represents the range of pixel intensity fluctuations. Higher values of these features indicate a more complex, dispersed gray-level distribution and more irregular image texture. These findings suggest that PCNSL with a high Ki-67 index is associated with altered diffusion-related imaging characteristics. Though Liu and Zhao et al. [24, 25] reported promising results using MRI radiomic features for Ki-67 classification in PCNSL, the biological relevance of many radiomic features remains unclear. In contrast, ADC histogram features are more interpretable, enabling easier and more intuitive clinical application. Furthermore, our study demonstrates that whole-tumor histogram features of ADC provide superior performance compared to conventional ADC analysis in distinguishing Ki-67 expression levels [12]. Therefore, ADC-based histogram analysis may be more suited for clinical application.

Our preliminary results indicate that multiple histogram features derived from DKI and IVIM sequences, can be used to differentiate Ki-67 expression levels in PCNSL. To our knowledge, this is the first study to apply advanced diffusion MRI techniques for stratifying Ki-67 index in PCNSL. Our results demonstrated significantly lower values in DkP10, Dkminimum, Kuniformity, and DP10, whereas KP90, KIQR, Kmaximum, KMAD, Krange, KRMAD, and Kvariance were markedly elevated in the high Ki-67 group compared to the low Ki-67 group. These findings align consistently with previous observations in pediatric tumors [19], parotid gland tumors [20], and sinonasal tumors [18]. The Dk parameter represents the diffusion coefficient corrected for non-Gaussian bias, while the D value reflects pure molecular diffusion without microcirculation effects. Additionally, the K value quantifies the degree to which water molecule diffusion deviates from a Gaussian distribution; higher K values indicate greater tumor complexity or heterogeneity. Therefore, it is unsurprising that diffusion-related parameters correlate significantly with Ki-67 index, as high Ki-67 expression reflects increased tumor cell proliferation, greater heterogeneity, and more restricted water diffusion. Additionally, we found no statistically significant differences in the D* and f maps derived from IVIM between different Ki-67 expression groups. This finding is consistent with previous studies conducted on sinonasal malignancy and parotid gland tumors [18, 20], which may be attributed to the low signal-to-noise ratio and relatively poor reproducibility of these parameters.

BCL-2, a key anti-apoptotic gene, plays a critical role in tumor biology [7, 26]. The imbalance between cell proliferation and apoptosis is fundamental to the growth and dissemination of malignant tumors. To our knowledge, this is the first study to apply multiple diffusion MRI techniques for the noninvasive assessment of BCL-2 protein expression in PCNSL. Our preliminary results indicate that ADC, Dk, and D values were lower, whereas Kmedian and D*skewness values were higher in the BCL-2 (+) group compared to the BCL-2 (-) group. Overexpression of BCL-2 may contribute to tumor cell survival and proliferative activity [8, 9], which may help explain the findings observed in our study. These findings suggest multiparametric diffusion MRI could serve as a noninvasive preoperative biomarker for BCL-2 expression, though further validation through multicenter prospective studies is needed.

In this study, we also compared the diagnostic performance of conventional DWI with that of advanced diffusion modelsin assessing Ki-67 index and BCL-2 status in PCNSL. Although the DWI model for differentiating Ki-67 index and the IVIM model for BCL-2 expression showed the highest AUC, DeLong tests with Bonferroni correction revealed no statistically significant differences among any single or combined models in distinguishing either Ki-67 or BCL-2 status. This may suggest that DWI is more suitable for clinical application, given its shorter acquisition time and superior signal-to-noise ratio compared to advanced diffusion techniques.

Our study has several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this was a single-center retrospective study with a limited sample size and imbalanced outcome distribution, which may have introduced selection bias and limited the generalizability of the findings. Second, feature selection and model building were performed on the same dataset without an independent validation cohort. Although bootstrap internal validation was applied, residual optimism may remain. Therefore, future multicenter studies with larger independent cohorts are warranted to further validate our findings. Third, manual delineation of the VOIs is time-consuming, highlighting the need for future studies to explore deep learning based automatic segmentation methods to improve efficiency. Fourth, the relatively long acquisition time of the diffusion protocol may limit its clinical feasibility. Furthermore, high b-value diffusion imaging is more prone to image distortion, which may affect VOI delineation and parameter estimation. The use of only four b-values for DKI fitting may also have limited the stability of the derived parameters. Future studies with optimized acquisition protocols, more densely sampled b-values, and formal stability analyses are needed to confirm the robustness of these quantitative diffusion parameters. Finally, the diffusion parameters derived from images were not validated through precise point-to-point correlation with histopathological sections. Therefore, prospective radiologic-pathologic correlation studies remain necessary to definitively establish to validate these relationships.

Conclusion

Whole-tumor histogram analysis of diffusion MRI serves as a promising noninvasive approach for assessing BCL-2 and Ki-67 expression in PCNSL. Both individual and combined diffusion models demonstrated potential diagnostic value.

Mortality trends and epidemiological characteristics among people living with HIV in Huangshi, China: a time-series analysis (2004–2024)
Authors: Qingzhi Wang, Haozhen Bai, Li Song, Can Wang, Youyi Xie, Biao Fu, Jumin Xie, Luman Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo analyze the epidemiological characteristics and dynamic trends of people living with HIV (PLWH) in Huangshi between 2004 and 2024, thereby providing a scientific basis for the optimization of regional HIV prevention and control strategies.MethodsSurveillance data of PLWH from Huangshi during the period 2004–2024 were collected. Descriptive epidemiology methods were employed to examine the temporal distribution, demographic features (including age, sex, marital status), transmission routes, clinical manifestations, and mortality patterns. A Cochrane-Armitage trend test was utilized to assess changes in gender composition, while SPSS 27 was leveraged to establish a time-series model based on exponential smoothing techniques to forecast future mortality counts.ResultsThe number of PLWH in Huangshi exhibited a marked increase post-2009, peaking in 2015, followed by a significant decline from 2020 onward. Key structural shifts were observed: (1) among reported cases, the 51–80 age group accounted for the highest proportion (representing the age composition within the infected population, rather than age-specific incidence in the general population). Male cases showed a pronounced upward trend, whereas female cases demonstrated a gradual decrease. (2) Sexual transmission constituted the predominant mode, accounting for 95.02% of cases, with heterosexual transmission highly concentrated among males aged 51–70 years. (3) The primary opportunistic infections were persistent fever and Pneumocystis pneumonia. Post-2013, mortality rates escalated significantly, yet over one-third of deaths were attributed to causes unrelated to HIV, with cardiovascular diseases and malignancies ranking as major contributors, reflecting a shift toward chronic comorbidity management. (4) Time-series modeling predicted a gradual decline in the estimated number of HIV-related deaths for the years 2025–2028.ConclusionHIV transmission in Huangshi has evolved into a stage characterized by sexual transmission as the primary route, with a notable increase in the disease burden among older adults and males. The focus is now transitioning from HIV-related mortality to the management of chronic comorbidities. Future HIV prevention efforts in the city should adopt comprehensive strategies, including targeted interventions and enhanced surveillance for males-particularly older males-strengthened treatment and prevention measures, and integrated approaches to chronic disease management to address the evolving epidemiological landscape.

Introduction

Since its discovery in 1981, the Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) infection and its terminal phase-Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome (AIDS)-have emerged as one of the most significant public health challenges worldwide, particularly in low-and-middle-income countries [1]. At the UN Sustainable Development Summit held on September 25, 2015, the 193 member states unanimously adopted 17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), including Goal 3, which aims to eliminate HIV, tuberculosis, malaria, and neglected tropical diseases by 2030 [2].

The UNAIDS has set an ambitious target for global HIV prevention: by 2025, 95% of all populations and age groups should know their HIV status, 95% of those who know their status should receive antiretroviral treatment (ART), and 95% of those receiving treatment should achieve viral load suppression [3].

According to the latest data released by the World Health Organization (WHO), as of now, HIV has claimed the lives of approximately 44.1 million people across all countries worldwide. By the end of 2024, it is estimated that 40.8 million people will be living with HIV, of which 77% are receiving ART treatment. Additionally, 65% of new HIV infections occur in the African region. In the same year, approximately 630,000 deaths are projected to be linked to HIV-related causes [1]. These statistics highlight the pressing urgency and necessity for global HIV prevention and treatment efforts. Based on these projected trends, it is evident that achieving the ambitious 95-95-95 targets for HIV treatment globally still poses substantial challenges.

According to WHO classification, HIV disproportionately affects specific populations, including men who have sex with men (MSM), people who inject drugs (PWID), prisoners, sex workers, and transgender individuals [4]. In China, epidemiological data consistently identify three primary risk groups: drug users, female sex workers, and MSM [5–7]. Notably, the misuse of opioids exhibits a strong correlation with elevated HIV transmission rates, primarily through needle-sharing practices among PWID, thereby increasing the risk of localized outbreaks and secondary infections [8].

Despite decades of research, HIV infection remains incurable. Early detection serves as a cornerstone for effective control, enabling individuals to modify high-risk behaviors and reducing onward transmission [9]. Diagnostic technologies have advanced significantly, encompassing fourth-generation immunoassays, molecular amplification methods, and point-of-care testing (POCT) platforms [10]. Among these, antibody-based detection and molecular amplification form the diagnostic backbone, while rapid testing has accelerated progress toward global HIV prevention targets [11, 12]. ART has proven effective in suppressing viral replication and improving patient outcomes, significantly reducing HIV-related mortality and transmission. However, challenges persist due to delayed ART initiation in many regions, contributing to ongoing transmission [13].

Social determinants, including poverty, healthcare disparities, stigma, and discrimination, impede comprehensive HIV prevention strategies [14]. In low-and-middle-income countries, annual HIV prevention costs reach approximately $20.2 billion, with substantial reliance on international funding [15]. Moreover, comorbidities among HIV-positive individuals introduce additional complexities to clinical management [16].

In China, HIV has emerged as the most prevalent notifiable infectious disease [17]. While numerous studies have characterized the PLWH epidemic at national and provincial levels in China, systematic analyses spanning two decades at the prefectural city level remain scarce. As the fundamental unit for implementing public health policies in China, a precise characterization of the epidemic profile at the city level is crucial for optimizing local resource allocation and developing tailored prevention and control strategies.

This study utilizes 21 years of reported PLWH data (2004–2024) from Huangshi City to systematically analyze temporal trends, demographic distribution, transmission routes, clinical symptoms, and mortality characteristics. We also employed time-series models to forecast short-term mortality trends for the subsequent four years. The study makes three innovative contributions: (1), it provides long-term longitudinal evidence from a typical prefectural city in central China, addressing the evidence gap between provincial macro-level data and county-level micro-level data. (2), it reveals how national-level epidemic trends manifest as distinct, localized patterns at the city level. (3), it provides direct evidence-based guidance for formulating precise, region-specific prevention strategies and optimizing healthcare resource allocation based on local evidence.

Case information and statistical methods

Case information and data collection

The study population comprised newly reported PLWH in Huangshi City, Hubei Province, China, spanning from January 1, 2004, to December 31, 2024. In this study, PLWH encompassed all newly diagnosed HIV infections (including asymptomatic HIV-infected individuals and patients already at the AIDS stage at initial diagnosis). All case data were sourced from the China Information System for Disease Control and Prevention, which serves as Huangshi City’s statutory reporting system based on passive surveillance.

Data collection included comprehensive demographic and clinical information, such as sex, age, marital status, onset date, diagnosis date, cause of death, transmission route, and the presence of comorbidities or symptoms within the three months prior to diagnosis. Detailed records of symptoms and signs at the time of diagnosis were also documented.

Diagnostic criteria and classification of symptoms

Diagnostic criteria

Diagnosis adhered to guidelines from the Chinese Guidelines for the Prevention and Treatment of AIDS (2005, 2011, 2015, 2018, 2021, and 2024 editions). All diagnostic procedures followed principles of informed consent and confidentiality.

Symptom categories were defined by reference to the HIV/AIDS antiretroviral therapy medical records in the China Disease Prevention and Control Information System and the Chinese Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of HIV/AIDS (2024 edition). Eligibility criteria

Inclusion criteria were: ① Current residence in Huangshi City; ② Case review date between 2004 and 2024; ③ Availability of a complete individual case report form and follow-up records. The reporting system uses unique card numbers and national ID numbers to verify and remove duplicate reports, ensuring that each case in this dataset is included based on its date of initial diagnosis, thereby eliminating interference from duplicate reporting. ④Cause-of-death classification was primarily based on the underlying cause of death recorded on the Medical Certificate of Cause of Death, and was further verified by integrating the patient’s clinical follow-up records, CD4 cell count, viral load, and antiretroviral treatment history. All cause-of-death information underwent final review and classification by specialists (with clinical backgrounds) responsible for AIDS prevention and control at the Huangshi Municipal Center for Disease Control and Prevention.

A total of 2,532 cases of PLWH in Huangshi meeting diagnostic criteria and having complete medical records were included in the study. Among these, 702 cases represented HIV/AIDS-related deaths. Among these death cases, 107 were excluded from the subsequent cause-of-death analysis due to a lack of detailed information regarding the specific cause of death (e.g., registered only as “death” without a specified underlying cause) in the death certificate or follow-up records.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 27.0.1 and R (version 4.5.1). Graphical representations were generated using Origin 2025. Due to the lack of annual population denominator data, this study used the age composition proportion among newly reported cases to describe changes in age structure. This metric directly reflects the evolving demographic characteristics within the infected population.

Definition and calculation of mortality rate

The “mortality rate” reported in this study specifically refers to the annual HIV/AIDS-related case fatality rate. It assesses the proportion of HIV/AIDS-related deaths within the PLWH population that is registered and alive at the beginning of the year, including newly reported cases that year. The calculation formula is: Annual HIV/AIDS-related case fatality rate (%) = (Total number of HIV/AIDS-related deaths reported in a given year) / (Number of alive and registered PLWH at the beginning of the year + Number of newly reported cases entering follow-up that year) × 100%. Denominator data were derived from the “PLWH Follow-up Management” database of the China Information System for Disease Control and Prevention. This involved extracting the number of cases alive on January 1st of each year and combining it with the number of newly reported cases entering follow-up during that year. This indicator reflects the annual change in the death burden within this specific cohort, not a population-based mortality rate.

Time-series modeling for predicting HIV/AIDS mortality

Time-series modeling was applied to predict HIV/AIDS mortality beyond December 31, 2024, using data from January 1, 2004, to the present. The data were analyzed using exponential smoothing to identify trends. A non-seasonal, decelerating trend model was selected to capture the observed pattern of declining mortality rates over the study period. This model provided projections for the next four years based on the established trend parameters.

Results

Basic characteristics of PLWH in Huangshi from 2004 to 2024

Distribution trends

The total number of reported PLWH in Huangshi during the period from 2004 to 2024 is presented in Fig. 1. The data clearly indicate that between 2004 and 2008, the number of cases remained relatively stable, ranging from 23 to 29 cases (Fig. 1A). Starting from 2009, the number of reported cases began to increase steadily, with a particularly sharp rise from 2011 onward. The peak incidence occurred in 2015, reporting 239 cases. Subsequently, the number stabilized at a high level.

Characteristics of PLWH in Huangshi from 2004 to 2024. A Annual and monthly distribution. B Age distribution. C Marital status. D Number and proportion of PLWH among males and females

From 2020 onwards, the number of cases has been decreasing annually, reaching a low of 98 cases in 2023, which is approximately a 32% reduction from the peak in 2015. These fluctuations may be associated with global events, such as the COVID-19 pandemic, or the implementation of effective intervention strategies, however, the specific underlying factors remain unclear due to the absence of contemporaneous testing volume data.

Furthermore, the frequency of reported cases showed significant seasonality, with a higher incidence during May to July. These months accounted for approximately 35% to 40% of the annual total. Specifically, the peak incidence was observed in June, with 14.38 cases per year. Conversely, January and February saw relatively low case numbers, averaging below 7.6 cases per year.

Age distribution

The collected data spanning 21 years revealed that the primary affected population was the middle-aged and elderly groups, aged 51 to 80 years. Within this range, the highest number of cases was observed in the 71–80 age group, accounting for 570 cases (Fig. 1B). The next highest was the 61–70 age group, contributing 542 cases, followed by the 51–60 age group, with 479 cases.

In contrast, the number of cases among younger individuals (1–30 years) was significantly lower. Specifically, the 1–10 year age group had only 15 cases, while the 11–20 year age group reported 52 cases.

During the early years of the study (2004–2012), cases across all age groups were relatively low. A notable increase was observed in the 51–80 age group from 2013 to 2020. Although the numbers declined from 2021 to 2024, the middle-aged and elderly populations continued to represent a substantial proportion of the total cases.

Marital status

From the data spanning 2004 to 2024, male cases consistently represented a higher proportion than female cases across most years. The predominant proportion was observed among married/partnered, with a significant disparity between males and females. Among divorced/widowed individuals, male cases gradually increased, from 2013 to 2019, while female cases experienced a brief rise during 2011–2019. Single individuals constituted a smaller proportion, with males forming the majority and females a minority. Cases with unknown marital status (not specified) accounted for an extremely low overall proportion, with male records limited to 2016–2017, 2020–2021, and 2024, while female records were noted in 2017, 2020, and 2022 (Fig. 1C).

Gender distribution

Over the period from 2004 to 2024, a total of 2,532 PLWH were reported in Huangshi, with 1,908 cases among males (75.36%) and 624 cases among females (24.64%). Analysis revealed that males constituted the majority of cases in 18 out of the 21 years included in the study (Fig. 1D).

The proportion of male cases ranged from a minimum of 44.83% in 2007 to a maximum of 80.38% in 2019, while female cases ranged from a maximum of 55.17% in 2007 to a minimum of 19.62% in 2019.

The Cochrane-Armitage test for trend using the “DescTools” R package indicated a statistically significant linear trend in gender distribution (male: Z = 4.4677, p < 0.001; female: Z = -4.4677, p < 0.001). Over the 21-year period, the proportion of male cases showed an increasing trajectory, while the proportion of female cases demonstrated a declining trend.

From an annual perspective, the male-to-female ratio was relatively balanced during 2004–2008, fluctuating between 44.83% and 65.22% male cases. From 2009 onward, the male proportion steadily increased and stabilized above 75%. The most pronounced growth occurred between 2013 and 2016, reaching a peak in 2019. Conversely, the proportion of female cases decreased progressively from 54.17% in 2004 to 21.62% in 2024, consistently remaining below 25% from 2013 onward (Fig. 1D).

Analysis of transmission routes for PLWH in Huangshi

The transmission routes included in this study encompassed injection drug use (IDU), heterosexual transmission, homosexual transmission, sexual transmission combined with injection drug use, blood/plasma collection, transfusion of blood/blood products, mother-to-child transmission (MTCT), occupational exposure, and other routes (Fig. 2A). Heterosexual transmission and homosexual transmission collectively accounted for 95.02% of all cases, indicating that sexual transmission serves as the predominant mode of HIV transmission in Huangshi, with heterosexual transmission being dominant and homosexual transmission exhibiting a pronounced gender-specific pattern. Notably, there were no confirmed cases related to occupational exposure.

The number of cases by transmission route and gender revealed significant differences between males and females (Fig. 2B). Under heterosexual transmission, there were 1,251 male cases versus 566 female cases. Heterosexual transmission was also the dominant transmission route, which is consistent with the gender disparity observed in the overall epidemic. In the homosexual transmission route, there were 588 male cases and only 1 female case, further highlighting the gender-specific predominance of male cases in sexual transmission. For other transmission routes, such as IDU, the number of male cases was consistently higher than that of female cases, with 2 male cases and 1 female case in IDU, 7 male cases and 4 female cases in sexual transmission combined with IDU, 7 male cases and 1 female case in blood/plasma collection, 9 male cases and 5 female cases in MTCT, and 20 male cases and 12 female cases in other routes. However, the number of cases via transfusion of blood/blood products showed an inverse pattern, with 34 female cases and 24 male cases, indicating a higher proportion of female cases in this route.

Cases related to injection drug use (IDU) totaled 3; each of the age groups 11–20 years, 21–30 years, and 41–50 years accounted for approximately one-third (Fig. 3A).

Heterosexual transmission was the most common route, with the highest case count observed in the 61-70-year age group (519 cases, 28.56%), followed by the 51-60-year group (462 cases, 25.43%) and the 41-50-year group (330 cases, 18.16%) (Fig. 3B). These findings indicate that middle-aged and older individuals constitute the majority of cases transmitted through heterosexual contact. Homosexual sexual transmission primarily affected young adults and middle-aged individuals, with the 21-30-year age group accounting for 32.26% and the 31-40-year group for 19.86%, representing a combined 52.12% of cases (Fig. 3C).

Cases involving transmission through sexual contact combined with injection drug use totaled 11; the 61-70-year age group represented 45.46%, while the 41-50-year and 51-60-year groups each accounted for 27.27% (Fig. 3D). Blood or plasma collection-related cases numbered 8; the 41-50-year age group represented 62.50%, and the 31-40-year group accounted for 25.00% (Fig. 3E). Cases linked to transfusion of blood or blood products totaled 58; the 31-40-year age group (46.55%) and the 41-50-year age group (22.41%) together accounted for nearly 70% (Fig. 3F).

Mother-to-child transmission (MTCT) cases numbered 14; 85.71% occurred in children under the age of 10 years, while 14.29% were in adolescents aged 11–20 years (Fig. 3G). No cases were reported across all age groups related to occupational exposure (Fig. 3H).

Cases attributed to “other” transmission routes totaled 32; these were disproportionately concentrated in the 41-50-year group (25.00%) and the 31-40-year group (21.88%), with a more dispersed distribution across other age groups (Fig. 3I).

This analysis confirms a predominance of sexually transmitted HIV in Huangshi, with distinct age clustering associated with different transmission modes. The number of cases linked to traditional high-risk non-sexual routes like IDU and blood/plasma collection has substantially decreased. Conversely, specific transmission pathways such as transfusion, MTCT, and cases with unknown sources exhibit particular age distribution patterns.

Analysis of clinical symptoms in PLWH in Huangshi

The initial data for this analysis encompassed the clinical symptoms of PLWH in Huangshi during the preceding three months from 2004 to 2024. Using a three-year timeframe as the statistical unit, the case data from 2004 to 2024 were divided into seven consecutive three-year periods. The denominator for analysis was defined as the total number of newly reported HIV cases within each respective period, and the occurrence of specific symptoms—including persistent diarrhea (> 1 month in adults; >2 weeks in children), persistent or intermittent fever lasting one month (> 37.5 °C), and others—was examined. It should be emphasized that the analytical indicator was the number of cases presenting with each specific symptom (expressed as a proportion of total cases within each period), rather than the incidence rate in the general population. Statistical analysis of symptoms within the preceding three months revealed that persistent or intermittent fever lasting one month (> 37.5 °C) accounted for the highest proportion among all documented symptoms. This symptom peaked during the period from 2004 to 2015, but exhibited a gradual downward trend in reported cases from 2016 to 2024 (Fig. 4A).

Pneumocystis pneumonia (PCP) and dermatological manifestations followed as the second and third most common symptoms, respectively, with 61 and 75 cases, first appearing in 2010 and 2007. Tuberculosis, cryptococcal meningitis, disseminated fungal disease, recurrent severe bacterial pneumonia, and other opportunistic infections or tumors constituted a smaller proportion of cases, though their incidences exhibited varying trends. Specifically, symptoms such as oral hairy leukoplakia, oral candidiasis, persistent diarrhea (> 1 month for adults, > 2 weeks for children), and chronic herpes simplex virus infection exhibited an initial increase followed by a decrease and then a rebound, while the remaining symptoms demonstrated a marked upward trend. Lymphocytic interstitial pneumonia (LIP), Mycobacterium avium complex (MAC) infection, and Kaposi sarcoma accounted for a negligible proportion (Fig. 4A).

Additionally, a separate analysis of 14 symptoms, including projectile vomiting and blurred vision, was performed. During 2004–2012, the incidence rates for all symptoms were low with minimal variation. In 2013–2018, the number of cases for most symptoms increased significantly to peak levels, except for projectile vomiting, with substantial variations among symptoms. During 2019–2024, the overall incidence rates showed a downward trend (Fig. 4B).

Analysis of mortality among PLWH

Cause-of-death classification standards and process

To ensure the accuracy of cause-of-death classification, the underlying cause of death for all mortality cases underwent systematic review. The classification process was as follows: ① For in-hospital deaths, the cause of death was initially determined and entered into the system by clinicians following the basic principles of the International Classification of Diseases (ICD-10). ② For deaths outside medical institutions, follow-up managers at community health service centers or township health centers conducted household surveys and verification with family members or witnesses. ③ All reported cause-of-death information was then reviewed and classified by specialists at the Huangshi Municipal Center for Disease Control and Prevention, who integrated the case’s long-term follow-up records, disease progression indicators (e.g., CD4 cell count, viral load), and antiretroviral treatment status. Ambiguous or disputed information was adjudicated through discussion among multiple specialists. The final classification distinguished between HIV/AIDS-related death (underlying cause directly resulting from HIV infection, e.g., opportunistic infections, HIV/AIDS-related cancers) and non-HIV/AIDS-related death (underlying cause not directly associated with HIV, e.g., cardiovascular disease, cancer, accidental injury). To ensure the reliability of the analysis, 107 cases that could not be accurately classified due to insufficient information were excluded.

Mortality analysis and contributing factors of PLWH

The annual HIV/AIDS-related case fatality rate (hereafter referred to as the cohort-based annual mortality) from 2004 to 2024 in Huangshi indicate that the HIV-related mortality rate remained low and stable during 2004–2012, with most years showing rates below 0.7% (Fig. 5A). Starting in 2013, there was a significant rise in mortality, with the rate increasing from 1.00% in 2012 to 3.42% in 2013, further climbing to 3.56% in 2014, a threefold increase. Between 2015 and 2022, mortality continued to rise, peaking in 2022 with 76 deaths and a 10.83% mortality rate, representing the highest case fatality rate within the followed cohort over the study period (Fig. 5A).

Analysis of causes of death shows that over one-third (34.40%) of deaths among PLWH were classified as non-HIV-related deaths, while deaths directly linked to HIV/AIDS constituted approximately 26.38% of cases (Fig. 5B). This highlights the complexity of mortality factors among PLWH. Cardiovascular diseases accounted for approximately 31.79% of HIV-unrelated deaths (109/343), malignancies for 22.16% (76/343), and respiratory conditions for 19.53% (67/343), collectively representing 73.47% of total deaths. External causes, such as suicide or accidental injury, contributed minimally to mortality (Fig. 5C).

Among the HIV-related causes of death, B5: Pneumocystis pneumonia (PCP) was the most common and lethal opportunistic infection, accounting for 53.61% of cases (141 deaths). HIV-related malignancies, particularly lymphomas, and various infections (bacterial, viral, fungal, parasitic) were also significant contributors. Rare complications, including B19: Progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML) and B20: Lymphocytic interstitial pneumonia (LIP), represented a negligible proportion of deaths (Fig. 5D).

Short-term forecasting of HIV-related mortality trends in Huangshi over the next four years

Based on PLWH data from Huangshi City from 2004 to 2024, the annual death counts were extracted, and a time-series model was constructed using SPSS 27.0.1. Following the principle of minimizing information criteria, an automatic algorithm was used to screen and build an exponential smoothing model. Comparison of the BIC values for different models led to the selection of the optimal model with a normalized BIC of 5.180. Model diagnostics show that the Ljung-Box test p-value was > 0.05, indicating the residual series exhibits no significant autocorrelation and can be considered white noise (Fig. 6A), suggesting a good model fit. The model’s goodness-of-fit metrics show an RMSE of 10.724 and a MAPE of 38.824. The forecast results indicate the predicted number of HIV-related deaths in the city for 2025 to 2028 will be 57, 55, 54, and 53, respectively (Fig. 6B&C).

Discussion

This study conducted a systematic analysis of the epidemiological profile of PLWH in Huangshi from 2004 to 2024, revealing significant shifts in the local epidemic trajectory, population distribution, transmission modes, clinical presentations, and disease burden. These findings not only delineate the evolving landscape of the HIV epidemic within Huangshi but also provide crucial scientific evidence to inform the optimization of regional prevention and control strategies.

Our results demonstrate that the number of PLWH in Huangshi entered a phase of rapid increase following 2009–2010, peaking in 2015, and subsequently showed a marked decline starting in 2020. This dynamic is likely attributable to multiple factors. Further surveillance is warranted to determine whether this phenomenon reflects a sustained trend or a transient disruption related to the pandemic.

The substantial rise from 2009 to 2015 may reflect improved surveillance infrastructure and enhanced implementation of the “Four Free and One Care” policy, expanded testing coverage, and heightened awareness and diagnostic capabilities among both the public and healthcare providers, leading to the detection of previously undiagnosed individuals [18, 19].

The decline observed after 2020 should be interpreted with caution, as it coincides temporally with the global COVID-19 pandemic and must be considered in the context of potential pandemic-related influencing factors. Public health interventions, including social distancing, travel restrictions, and limitations on social gatherings, likely reduced opportunities for risky sexual behavior [20]. Concurrently, the reallocation of medical resources towards pandemic response might have temporarily disrupted access to routine HIV testing services, contributing to delayed diagnoses and potentially reduced detection of new cases [21, 22]. This observed decrease aligns with trends noted in numerous international studies regarding the reporting of sexually transmitted diseases during the COVID-19 era [23]. In the absence of direct supporting data, such as concurrent testing service volumes or population testing participation rates, the current discussion regarding the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on HIV surveillance data should be viewed as a preliminary, phenomenon-based inference aimed at generating hypotheses for future investigation, not as a confirmatory conclusion.

Notably, the demographic profile of the HIV epidemic in Huangshi underwent a significant structural shift. Firstly, an aging pattern became apparent, with the 51-80-year-old population becoming the dominant group, particularly peaking in the 71–80 age bracket, consistent with the national trend of an aging HIV epidemic [24, 25]. This phenomenon reflects several complex factors: on one hand, the effectiveness of antiretroviral therapy (ART) has extended the lifespan of infected individuals, increasing the proportion of older adults in the affected population [26]; on the other hand, this age group may experience factors contributing to higher infection risk, such as potentially lower awareness of HIV, fewer perceived immediate harms, or higher engagement in unprotected commercial or non-marital sex. Delays in diagnosis and healthcare-seeking behavior, possibly more pronounced in older individuals, also contribute to higher seroprevalence in this age group, as evidenced by their prominence in heterosexual transmission routes [27].

Secondly, gender disparities widened, with males increasingly representing the majority of cases, contrasting with the national trend in China [28]. This persistent male predominance in Huangshi highlights a transition in the epidemic’s transmission dynamics. It appears to be moving from relatively dispersed heterosexual transmission towards a transmission network increasingly dominated by males, facilitated by men who have sex with men (MSM) and commercial sex work. Over the past decade, MSM have indeed become the fastest-growing group in terms of HIV incidence in China, with national surveillance data indicating their HIV prevalence was already disproportionately high, often exceeding 8% far earlier than other risk groups [29, 30].

A Cochrane-Armitage test for trend confirmed a statistically significant increase in the proportion of male patients over time in Huangshi, suggesting this gender imbalance is not random but follows a discernible linear trajectory. From a behavioral perspective, male risk group exhibit characteristics such as lower condom use rates and higher numbers of sexual partners, making them significant drivers for transmission and contributing to the rise in reported male cases [31, 32]. Conversely, the continued decline in the female proportion might be linked to effective targeted interventions, including sexual health education, screening programs, and risk reduction counseling specifically aimed at women, potentially increasing their awareness and use of preventive measures like condoms. These trends offer insights potentially relevant for male-focused prevention strategies elsewhere. Lastly, a substantial proportion of individuals were categorized as single or divorced/widowed, further suggesting that unstable or non-exclusive sexual partnerships represent an important social determinant for HIV transmission in the region.

Sexually transmitted infections, particularly heterosexual transmission, have become the predominant mode of HIV spread in Huangshi, accounting for the vast majority of cases. MSM transmission also exhibits strong gender clustering, almost exclusively affecting males, contributing significantly to the increase in male cases. This clear transmission pattern provides a critical focal point for targeted intervention by the Huangshi municipal government: prioritizing efforts to curb sexual transmission as the core of HIV prevention activities. The distinct age distributions associated with different transmission routes-high concentration of heterosexual cases in older males versus a more even distribution across ages for MSM and injection-related transmission-underscore the need for immediate and tailored interventions focusing on high-risk populations.

Analysis of clinical symptoms revealed that opportunistic infections, notably persistent or intermittent fever (> 37.5 °C), Pneumocystis pneumonia (PCP), and dermatological manifestations, were primary clinical features among HIV patients in the study period. The observed decline in reporting for some symptoms after 2016 may partly be attributed to improved access to ART and earlier treatment initiation, effectively suppressing viral load and reducing associated complications. The sharp rise in the annual case fatality rate among PLWH after 2013, peaking in 2022, likely reflects the natural progression of the large cohort of individuals diagnosed during the preceding peak years. It should be emphasized that this indicator reflects the death burden among registered PLWH under follow-up in Huangshi, rather than the mortality risk in the general population of the city. The potential contribution of COVID-19, where pandemic-related disruptions might have specifically impacted the care of opportunistic infections (OIs), although potentially less direct, warrants consideration.

Critically, over one-third (34.40%) of deaths were classified as non-HIV-related deaths, primarily due to cardiovascular diseases, malignancies, and respiratory conditions. This high proportion of non-HIV-related mortality marks a significant shift. As ART prolongs survival and transforms HIV into a manageable chronic condition for many, the underlying causes of death in the affected population are increasingly mirroring those of the general population [33]. Furthermore, PCP remains the leading direct cause of HIV-related mortality, emphasizing the persistent importance of opportunistic infection prophylaxis and timely treatment for infected individuals.

In summary, the primary innovative contribution of this study lies in the breadth of its longitudinal description at the local level, the demonstration of localized trend specificity, and the provision of evidence-based guidance for precise regional planning, rather than methodological novelty. The study provides long-term, systematic, city-level evidence from a non-major urban region in China. It offers a valuable empirical foundation for understanding the true profile of HIV/AIDS in China’s vast non-core urban areas, addressing the evidence gap between provincial macro-level data and county-level micro-level data. Second, the study demonstrates the specific manifestations of the epidemic at the local level. This local specificity highlights the need for the adaptation and precision of national prevention and control strategies at the local level. Finally, the study provides direct evidence-based support for regional prevention strategies and healthcare resource allocation.

However, this study has limitations inherent to its design and data source. It relies on passive surveillance data for PLWH in Huangshi, which may introduce challenges such as underreporting, diagnostic delays, and potential incompleteness in case information. Additionally, the specific characteristics of migrant populations within the city’s PLWH were not separately analyzed, even though mobility could be a significant factor influencing local transmission dynamics, particularly given the city’s industrial background. Interpretations of post-2020 trends should be made with caution, as pandemic-related disruptions and potential surveillance artifacts limit the capacity for causal inference; these observations may nevertheless serve as a basis for hypothesis generation. Furthermore, the “aging trend” reported in this study was derived from the age composition proportions of newly reported cases. Due to the absence of annual population denominator data, age standardization was not performed; accordingly, this finding should be interpreted as a descriptive characterization of the age structure within the infected population. Future research should address these limitations and explore the impact of population mobility on the epidemic.

Conclusion

Based on our systematic analysis of the epidemiological profile of HIV in Huangshi from 2004 to 2024, we conclude that the epidemic has entered a new phase characterized by sexual transmission as the primary route, with a significant increase in the disease burden among older adults and males. The focus of the epidemic has shifted from HIV-related mortality to the management of chronic comorbidities. However, these findings are based on descriptive analyses and local data, and are subject to the inherent limitations of a passive surveillance system, including the exclusion of 107 cases with incomplete death cause information. Therefore, these observations should be interpreted as indicative of potential trends rather than definitive evidence of epidemiological transition. Future studies with more comprehensive data—including age-standardized rates and systematic verification of death causes—are warranted to validate these trends. Therefore, future HIV prevention strategies must evolve to prioritize population-centered and health-focused interventions, implementing comprehensive and targeted measures to effectively curb transmission, improve the quality of life for people living with HIV, and ultimately contribute to the goal of ending the AIDS epidemic.

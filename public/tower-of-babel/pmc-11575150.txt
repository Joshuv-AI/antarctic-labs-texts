The macular microvascular changes in children with a history of retinopathy of prematurity treated with intravitreal Bevacizumab injection using optical coherence tomography angiography: a retrospective cohort
Authors: Amir Eftekhari Milani, Mohammad Hosein Ahoor, Fatemeh Etemad, Banafsheh Kharrazi Ghadim, Amin Arasteh
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveOur study aims to evaluate and compare the macular microvascular condition of the intravitreal Bevacizumab (IVB)-treated retinopathy of prematurity (ROP) cases to the preterm cases without ROP and spontaneously regressed ROPs.MethodsIt is a retrospective cohort of 50 premature neonates visited from 2016 to 2017 for ROP screening in Nikookari Eye Hospital and recalled for re-evaluation in 2022. These patients were classified into three groups based on their medical documents: 1. Preterm cases without ROP (PT), 2. Spontaneously regressed ROPs (SR), and 3. IVB-treated ROPs (IVB). All the included cases underwent a thorough ophthalmic exam, cyclo-refraction, Best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) assessment, and macular optical coherence tomography angiography (OCTA). The foveal thickness, foveal avascular zone (FAZ) area, foveal, parafoveal, and perifoveal vascular density (VD) were evaluated as the quantitative variables.ResultsTwenty-five male and 25 female cases with a median age of 5.0 entered the study. Twenty-six eyes were evaluated in the PT group, 50 in the SR group, and 24 in the IVB group. The SR group consisted of 25 zone-2 and 25 zone-3 ROPs. In comparison, the IVB group consisted of 9 zone-1 and 15 zone-2 ROPs (p: < 001). Also, zone-2 cases were more advanced in the IVB group. The IVB-treated eyes demonstrated a significantly higher foveal superficial capillary plexus (SCP) VD than the others (28.5% vs. 23.1 and 22.9, respectively, in PT and SR, p:0.008). However, the parafoveal deep capillary plexus (DCP) VD was lower than the PT cases (48.5% vs. 53.4, p:0.034). The FAZ area was also smaller than the PT cases (0.113 vs.0.219 mm2, p:0.032). The FAZ area was significantly positively correlated with the foveal SCP and DCP VD and negatively correlated with parafoveal DCP VD (CC: -0.770, -0.719, 0.377, respectively, p: < 0.001).ConclusionIn the long term, although the IVB-treated ROP cases experience an excellent visual outcome comparable to those without ROP, they may show some macular microvascular changes. These changes might be related to the effect of IVB or the nature of more advanced stages of ROP.Graphical Abstract Keywords: ROP, IVB, Retinopathy of prematurity, Bevacizumab, Anti-VEGF, OCTA, Vascular density

Background

Retinopathy of prematurity (ROP) is the leading cause of blindness in childhood, with an incidence ranging from 9.3 to 36.6% in the premature infant population in various countries [1]. Low birth weight and gestational age are the main risk factors of ROP; however, other factors, such as maternal disease during the pregnancy, neonatal supplemental oxygen therapy, and neonatal systemic disease, could also induce ROP incidence and its relapse after treatment [2, 3]. The conventional treatment of the severe forms of ROP is pan-retinal photocoagulation (PRP) and intravitreal injection of anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) agents [4].

It has been suggested that the retinal structural vascular abnormalities of advanced cases of ROP persist for years, and some of these findings may be attributed to the treatment they receive [5]. Fluorescein angiographic studies demonstrated the persistence of vascular loops, bulbs, and abnormal foveal vessels even two years after intravitreal Bevacizumab (IVB) treatment in ROP cases [6]. Although the foveal microvascular structure has been demonstrated to be affected by the incidence of ROP and being born preterm, the treatments of ROP may also impact the vascular structure. It has been reported that treated ROP cases have significant peripapillary vasculature differences compared to the spontaneously regressed ROPs [7].

There have been few studies on the microvascular changes of the macula in treated ROP cases compared to spontaneously regressed ones and healthy preterm children. One study declared that the laser and IVB-treated ROP cases also showed a smaller FAZ area and higher foveal superficial vessel density than the control group; however, the control group was not the preterm children [8].

Regarding the lack of enough evidence regarding the microvascular changes in IVB-treated ROP cases, our study aims to evaluate the possible changes and their correlation with visual function.

Methods

Patients

Our study is a retrospective cohort of premature neonates who were visited from 2016 to 2017 for ROP screening at Nikookari Eye Hospital. Regarding the national guideline of ROP screening in Iran, neonates with gestational age (GA) less than 32 weeks and 6 days or birth body weight lower than 1501 g and neonates with higher GA but need for neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) due to systemic instability after birth are screened for ROP. Three groups of these children, including preterm children without ROP (PT), children with Spontaneously regressed ROP (SR), and ROP cases treated with intravitreal Bevacizumab (IVB), were recalled for ophthalmic examination and imaging at ages four to six. The IVB group neonates, after sedation and topical anesthesia, received an intravitreal injection of Bevacizumab (0.25 mg/0.01 mL) with a gauge-30 needle inserted at 0.5 to 1.0 mm distance from limbus supra-in the supratemporal quadrant. Based on the results of previous studies [8] and a mean difference of 9% between the foveal VD of IVB-treated ROPs and PT cases, the standard deviation of 6.5%, alpha of 0.05, and power of 80%, 12 cases were calculated as the required sample size for each group. Due to the higher prevalence of SR cases in our clinic, we doubled the number of SR cases in our study to maximize the power. A total number of 50 cases meeting the inclusion and exclusion criteria were invited to the Retina clinic for the evaluations in 2022. Examinations

All the recalled cases were visited at the retina clinic to evaluate the cyclo-refraction, Best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) (using E-chart, reported in LogMAR scale), and retinal examination. After meeting the inclusion and exclusion criteria, all the patients underwent a macular optical coherence tomography angiography (OCTA).

Cyclo-refraction

The cyclo-refraction was performed using a Cyclopentolate hydrochloride (1%) drop every 5 min three times and waiting 30 min after the last drop. The Topcon KR-800 auto kerato-refractometer measured the refractive error. Each patient underwent the auto-refraction procedure two times, and if there were more than 0.5 D sphere and cylinder differences between the two measurements, the measurement was repeated. The mean measured refraction is reported as spherical equivalence (SE).

OCTA imaging

The AngioVueTM Imaging System (RTVue XR Avanti, Optovue Inc., Fremont, CA, USA) took the macular OCTA. The HD 6.0*6.0 program aided the evaluation of the macular vascular density (VD). The parafovea region was automatically identified as the circular annulus with a width of 2.0 mm, and the central circle with a diameter of 1.0 mm was designated as the fovea. The system automatically assessed the VD of the superficial and deep capillary plexuses. The OCTA photos with a quality score of less than 6/10 or with motion and blinking artifacts were repeated to get the desired outcomes. The imaging's target quantitative variables were the foveal, parafoveal, and perifoveal superficial capillary plexus (SCP) and deep capillary plexus (DCP) VD, FAZ area, and foveal thickness. Two expert retina specialists also assessed the presence of microaneurysms and telangiectasia in the macular OCTA. If there was any disagreement between the two experts regarding the presence of the microvascular abnormality, a third retina specialist evaluated the OCTA.

Statistical analysis

The distribution of the variable was examined using the Shapiro–Wilk test and histograms. The parametric data was analyzed by one-way ANOVA test, and the non-parametric data was analyzed using the Kruskal–Wallis test. The parametric test results are reported as mean and 95% confidence interval (95%CI), and the non-parametric test results are reported as median and interquartile range (IQR). Qualitative data, such as gender, was assessed using the Chi-Square test. The Spearman correlation test and linear regression were used to investigate possible correlations between the variables. The p values less than 0.05 have been considered statistically significant, and adjusted p values (Bonferroni Correction) have been used for multiple comparisons. IBM SPSS Statistics 27.0 software was used to perform the statistical analysis. The post-hoc power analysis was conducted on the results. The figures are created with BioRender.com.

Results

Demographic and clinical characteristics

A total of 100 eyes from 50 patients were involved in our study. Twenty-four eyes (6 males and six females) were ROP cases treated with IVB (group IVB), 50 eyes (12 males and 13 females) were ROP cases with spontaneous regression (group SR), and 26 eyes (7 males and six females) were cases without ROP (group PT). Based on the results of the Chi-square, there was no significant difference between groups according to the sex distribution (p:0.890). The median age of patients was 5.0 (IQR:4.0–6.0), and there was no significant difference between the three groups regarding age (p:0.294). The ROP cases in group IVB consist of 9 (37.5%) zone-1 and 15 (62.5%) zone-2. However, group SR had 25 (50%) zone-2 and 25 (50%) zone-3 cases. The ROP zone distribution significantly differed between the two groups (p: < 0.001). The staging distribution in zone-2 ROPs is significantly different between the two groups (p: < 0.001), and the IVB-treated cases had more advanced stages than the spontaneously regressed cases.

The spherical equivalence of the refractive error did not have any significant difference between the groups, and the median SE was + 0.155 D (IQR: (−0.005) – (+ 0.375)) (p:0.169).

On the other hand, The median BCVA significantly differed between the three groups (p: 0.002). The median BCVA in group IVB 0.045 is significantly weaker than in groups SR 0.000 and PT 0.000 (p:0.041 and 0.002, respectively). The comparison of the zone-2 cases of the IVB and SR also failed to show any significant differences (p:0.472). It suggests that the weaker BCVA in IVB groups might be related to the zone-1 cases. However, the BCVA did not show any significant differences between the various zones of ROP (p:0.052). This statistical insignificance might be related to the limited sample size.

Quantitative characteristics of OCTA

The foveal vascular density was significantly different between the groups in SCP (p:0.008). It was significantly higher in the IVB-treated group compared to the others. The mean SCP VD was 5.66% and 5.45% higher in the IVB groups than groups SR and PT (p:0.009 and 0.032, respectively). The comparison between zone-2 cases of the IVB and SR groups revealed no significant difference (p:0.053). However, this statistical insignificance could be related to the limited sample size of zone-2 cases in the IVB group. A comparison between various zones was conducted to reveal if the difference between the IVB and SR groups is related to the distribution of the various zones, and no significant difference was demonstrated (p:0.150). So, this difference could be merely related to the effect of IVB.

The foveal DCP VD was not significantly different between the groups (p:0.232). Detailed data on the quantitative characteristics of groups in macular OCTA has been provided in Table 2. Besides, the groups had no significant differences in foveal thickness (p:0.184). Figure 1 illustrates the foveal VD in three groups.

On the other hand, the foveal avascular zone differs significantly between the groups (p: 0.038). The IVB-treated ROPs had significantly smaller FAZ areas (0.113 mm2 (IQR:0.060–0.224) than the PT group (0.219 mm2 (IQR:0.141–0.257)) (p:0.032). Concerning the fact that there was no significant difference between the IVB and SR group, the comparison between zone-2 cases was not conducted. The FAZ area also had no significant differences between various zones ROP (p:0.699).

Despite the superficial plexus, the parafoveal vascular density in the deep plexus significantly differed between the three groups (p:0.116 and 0.037, respectively). Regarding the deep plexus, the pairwise comparison revealed a higher median VD in group PT than in group IVB (p: 0.034). The parafoveal DCP VD also had no significant differences between various zones ROP (p:0.212).

The perifoveal vascular density did not differ significantly between the groups, not in SCP or DCP (p:0.144 and 0.179, respectively). These variables also had no significant differences between various zones ROP (p:0.529 and 0.258, respectively, for parafoveal SCP and DCP).

Qualitative characteristics of OCTA

Eighteen eyes (75%) of IVB-treated ROPs showed the presence of microaneurysms and telangiectasia in the macular OCTA. In comparison, only six eyes (12%) of spontaneously regressed ROPs had these features. The difference between the two groups is statistically significant (RR:6.25 (95%CI:2.84–13.07), p: < 0.001). The cases without ROP in group PT had no microvascular abnormality in the OCTA. An intergroup comparison regarding the corresponding involved zone was made to diminish the role of zone-1 and 3 ROPs in this difference. The results showed that the prevalence of these abnormal vascular findings is significantly higher in IVB-treated zone-2 ROPs (73.3% vs. 20%, p: < 0.001). The relative risk of IVB for zone-2 cases was 3.66 (95%CI:1.58–8.50). The subgroup analysis to compare various stages of zone-2 ROPs was impossible due to the limited number of samples in each staging. These significant differences between the IVB and SR groups could not be attributed to the effect of IVB because the IVB-treated cases had significantly advanced stagings in the zone-2 cases. The prevalence of these microvascular abnormalities was compared between various zones of ROP, and the results demonstrated a significant difference between them (p: < 0.001). The prevalence was highest among zone-1 cases (77.8%), followed by zone-2 (40%) and zone-3 cases (4%). These results suggest a possible correlation between the more advanced stages of ROP and macular microvascular abnormalities such as microaneurysms and telangiectasia.

The cases with microvascular abnormalities, such as telangiectasia and microaneurysm, were compared with those without these features in groups IVB and SR regarding the quantitative characteristics of OCTA and BCVA. The only significantly different variable between the two groups was BCVA (p: < 0.001). The BCVA was worse in patients with microvascular abnormalities (0.045 (IQR:0.045–0.096) vs. 0.000 (IQR:0.000–0.045)). This result suggests that weaker BCVA after ROP regression could be partially due to these abnormalities, which happen more in advanced stages regarding our results.

Correlations

The results of the analysis in evaluating the possible correlations between the vascular density, FAZ area, and BCVA solitary showed a significantly strong negative correlation between the foveal SCP and DCP VD and FAZ area (Correlation coefficient (CC): −0.770 and −0.719, respectively, p: < 0.001 for both). The higher the foveal VD was, the smaller the FAZ area was. The FAZ area was significantly negatively correlated with the foveal thickness (CC:−0.649, p: < 0.001). The smaller the FAZ area, the thicker the fovea. Predictably, the foveal SCP and DCP VD had a significant positive correlation with the foveal thickness ( CC: 0.542 and 0.659, respectively, p: < 0.001 for both). The FAZ area was also positively correlated with the parafoveal DCP VD (CC:0.377, p < 0.001). The linear regression analysis was also conducted on the FAZ area and the foveal and parafoveal VD. Similar to the correlation analysis, the linear relationship between the FAZ area and foveal SCP, DCP, and parafoveal DCP was statistically significant (R2: 0.51, 0.45, and 0.17, respectively, p: < 0.001). Discussion

ROP is a biphasic retinal disease, starting with a retinal ischemia due to the downregulation of VEGF and Insulin-Like Growth Factor 1 (IGF-1) and the retinal vascularization arrest ending in overproduction of VEGF from ischemic retina and neovascularization induction. So, anti-VEGF agents and retinal ablation therapy reduce the burden of vitreous VEGF, preventing extreme neovascularizations and abnormal vessel formations [9]. These therapeutic interventions are recommended in type 1 ROPs and aggressive ROP (A-ROP) to prevent more advanced complications of ROP, such as retinal detachments [10].

Anti-VEGF agents have been widely used and approved in treating various retinal diseases such as diabetic retinopathy (DR), Age-Related macular degeneration (AMD), and retinal vein occlusion (RVO) for a long time. It has been suggested that these agents may induce long-term changes in retinal compartments besides the therapeutic targets. The increase in optic disk cup size is one of the long-term side effects related to these agents in DR [11]. On the other hand, a study showed an increase in papillary VD after anti-VEGF therapy in RVO cases [12]. However, Some reports have shown that the macular VD does not change after treatment with anti-VEGFs in DR or AMD [13, 14, 15]. On the other hand, a study demonstrated a decrease in macular VD after treatment with such agents in DR [16].

In recent years, with the increased use of anti-VEGF agents in treating ROP cases, attention has been drawn to studying the short and long-term effects of these medications on the retina in pediatrics. Our retrospective cohort study aimed to evaluate the macular microvascular status in three groups of preterm children: 1. Preterm children without ROP, 2. Preterm children with spontaneously regressed ROP, and 3. ROP cases treated with IVB. Our results show a significantly higher VD in the foveal SCP in the IVB-treated ROPs compared to two other groups (28.56% vs. 22.90 and 23.10% in SR and PT groups, respectively). However, parafoveal DCP had a lower VD in the IVB group compared to the PT group (48.55 vs. 53.45%).

Besides, the FAZ area was significantly smaller in the IVB group than in the preterm cases without ROP (0.113 mm2 vs. 0.219). Furthermore, the higher the foveal vascular density, the smaller the FAZ area.

The prevalence of microvascular abnormalities was significantly higher in the IVB group than in the SR group. However, it was mainly due to the more advanced cases in the IVB group. Based on our findings, microvascular abnormalities were associated with more advanced ROPs and worse BCVA.

A recently published meta-analysis demonstrated a smaller FAZ area in preterm children than in term cases. It declared that preterm cases without ROP had a larger FAZ area than those with ROP. Also, IVB and laser-treated ROPs had smaller FAZ than SR cases, which is coherent with our findings. The foveal SCP VD was significantly higher in IVB-treated ROPs than in preterm cases without ROP, and it was also higher than in SR cases, which is confirmed by our findings [17]. Although this study tried to contribute the smaller FAZ in IVB and laser-treated ROPs to the more advanced forms of ROP and higher levels of VEGF, based on our findings, the FAZ area was smaller in the IVB group compared to the PT group and not compared to the SR cases. Also, the comparison between various ROP zones revealed a significant difference in the FAZ area. Although this meta-analysis is a beneficial source in studying the microvascular status of ROP cases, it should be considered that only two studies out of 11 included studies have evaluated the IVB-treated ROPs, and the total number of IVB-treated cases was 127.

Chen et al. reported a smaller FAZ area and higher foveal VD in anti-VEGF treated ROP cases compared to the normal healthy children and correlated the higher foveal superficial plexus VD to the worse BCVA [18]. Although this study was coherent with our study regarding the FAZ area and foveal VD changes, we failed to show any correlation between the BCVA and these variables. In addition, the BCVA in their study was worse than ours regarding the anti-VEGF treated cases (0.10 vs. 0.045). This difference may be due to the more advanced staging of the cases; all of their cases were stage 3 in zones 1 and 2.

Another study on 25 anti-VEGF-treated ROPs compared to 22 laser-treated ROPs showed a significantly larger FAZ area and higher parafoveal VD (deep and superficial) in the first group. However, the stagings were similar between the two groups. Their results regarding the parafoveal superficial plexus VD and FAZ area were comparable with ours (50.86% and 0.14 mm2, respectively). However, their reported value for the parafoveal deep plexus VD was higher than ours (54.15% vs. 49.5%) [19]. The difference between the laser and anti-VEGF treated cases regarding the FAZ area and parafoveal VD is notable because patients with similar stagings but different treatments showed different FAZ area and parafoveal VD measurements in both of these findings.

We believe two possible hypotheses may justify these differences regarding the FAZ area. The first concerns the role of pigment epithelial-derived factor (PEDF) in FAZ area development and its relationship to PRP and anti-VEGF factors. The literature findings are controversial regarding the role and condition of the PEDF in ROP cases; however, PEDF concentrations are believed to decrease in ROP cases [20]. On the other hand, some studies demonstrated that anti-VEGF agents could increase the PEDF concentration and decrease the VEGF/PEDF ratio to inhibit angiogenesis [21]. Concerning the highly determining role of PEDF concentration and expression in FAZ area development in the embryonic and neonatal period [22], the smaller FAZ area in IVB-treated cases than the preterm cases, without a significant difference with the SR cases may be due to other systemic conditions of the ROP cases, which affected the PEDF and VEGF balance in the eye even before ROP screening. Based on our results, the SR group demonstrated an intermediate amount of FAZ area between the IVB-treated and PT cases. The IVB-treated cases have had more advanced stages of ROP, which shows they have had a more stressful systemic condition, such as hypoxia, low birth weight, and sepsis. Because foveal blood circulation develops earlier than peripheral areas, these cases with smaller FAZ areas may have experienced more predisposing conditions to systemic or ophthalmic ischemia, leading to the deregulation of PEDF expression even in the embryonic period. Nevertheless, more molecular studies on the effect of PRP and anti-VEGF agents on the VEGF/PEDF ratio in ROP cases may reveal a new era in this field.

The second hypothesis concerns the correlation of the parafoveal deep plexus VD and FAZ area. As mentioned, the lower parafoveal deep plexus VD was associated with a smaller FAZ area. This finding may be related to possible ischemia induced by lower parafoveal deep plexus VD, leading to reduced PEDF and increased VEGF in the foveal region, inducing more development and migration of vessels to the center of the macula.

Regarding the higher prevalence of macular microaneurysms and telangiectasia in IVB-treated cases, the IVB could not be recognized as the causative agent, and the presence of these microvascular abnormalities before the treatment should be considered. Also, the more advanced stages in the IVB-treated cases should not be denied. Newer retinal imaging techniques with handheld Swept-Source OCTA would be beneficial in detecting the exact relationship between these findings [23].

Although our study is one of the few studies evaluating and comparing the macular microvascular condition of anti-VEGF-treated ROP cases to the spontaneously regressed ROPs and preterm cases without ROP yet, the lack of variables regarding the systemic condition and inclusion of both eyes of the patients in the study are limitations of our study. Besides, our sample size was mediocre, so more extensive studies with larger sample sizes, including PRP and anti-VEGF-treated cases and comparing them with SR and preterm cases, eliminating all the possible confounding variables' impact, would help strengthen the available evidence in this field and improve the therapeutic guidelines.

Conclusion

We found that ROP cases, whether spontaneously regressed or IVB-treated, experienced an excellent visual function and BCVA comparable to other preterm children without ROP. It seems IVB-treated cases have higher foveal SCP VD compared to those spontaneously regressed. Besides, the IVB-treated cases present lower parafoveal DCP VD and smaller FAZ area than the preterm cases without ROP. Macular microaneurysms and telangiectasia were also more prevalent in advanced ROP cases and correlated with worse BCVA. These findings suggest that although ROP and its treatment with IVB may have an excellent functional outcome, it may have some unknown structural impacts on the macular development.

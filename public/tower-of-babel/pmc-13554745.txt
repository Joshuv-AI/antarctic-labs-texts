Three-Dimensional Right Ventricular Circumferential Strain
Authors: Kevin Ka-Ho Kam
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Corresponding Author

Severe tricuspid regurgitation remains a clinical challenge because operative timing is often delayed until right ventricular dysfunction becomes advanced,1 and traditional metrics may underestimate right ventricular myocardial injury.2 In this context, Wu et al3 provide important prospective evidence that 3-dimensional right ventricular circumferential strain (3D RV-CS) offers meaningful prognostic information in patients undergoing isolated tricuspid valve surgery.

The principal contribution of the study is its demonstration that 3D RV-CS was the strongest discriminator among the tested right ventricular functional indices, outperforming conventional measures such as tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion (TAPSE) and tissue Doppler S velocity, and adding incremental value to established surgical risk scores including EuroSCORE II, Tricuspid Regurgitation Impact Score (TRI-SCORE), and the Society of Thoracic Surgeons models.4, 5, 6 The authors also showed that reduced 3D RV-CS was associated with worse event-free survival, supporting the concept that right ventricular performance in tricuspid disease is not captured fully by longitudinal mechanics alone.

This work is clinically relevant because it shifts attention toward non-longitudinal right ventricular deformation, a physiological component that may better reflect the complex mechanics of the failing right ventricle in severe tricuspid regurgitation.7 The feasibility and reproducibility of the 3D strain measurements further strengthen the case for broader clinical testing, particularly in referral centers with advanced echocardiographic capability.

Several limitations deserve emphasis. First, this was a single-center observational cohort, so the findings require external validation before they can be generalized widely. Second, the cohort was selected from patients already deemed suitable for surgery, which may introduce selection bias and limit applicability to patients with tricuspid regurgitation. Third, strain analysis may be affected by loading conditions and vendor/software dependence, which could influence reproducibility across platforms. Finally, the study focused on preoperative measurements only, so the prognostic role of postoperative reverse remodeling or serial strain changes remains unknown.

Future studies should test whether 3D RV-CS can be integrated into practical risk algorithms that guide the timing of surgery or transcatheter intervention. Multicenter validation, comparison with cardiac magnetic resonance, and longitudinal assessment after intervention will be especially important. It will also be valuable to determine whether strain-guided management improves hard clinical outcomes rather than only risk prediction.

Statement on MRLs for difluoroacetic acid (DFA)
Authors: European Food Safety Authority (EFSA)
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn accordance with Article 43 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, the European Commission requested EFSA to carry out a targeted review of the maximum residue levels (MRLs) for difluoroacetic acid (DFA), following the availability of data on DFA residue levels in commodities for which EU MRLs are based on Codex MRLs. EFSA identified the Codex MRLs implemented into EU legislation that may affect the corresponding MRLs for DFA and perform consumer risk assessment using current residue definitions and the health‐based guidance values, applying the latest version of the pesticide residues intake model (PRIMo) model (revision 3.1). Based on the data available, EFSA derived MRL proposals for DFA in the relevant commodities. The exposure calculations performed by EFSA showed that the proposed MRLs do not result in consumer intake concerns.

BACKGROUND

Difluoroacetic acid (DFA) is a metabolite of the active substance flupyradifurone and is considered together with the parent compound in the assessment of residues in plant and animal commodities.

The peer review of the pesticide risk assessment for flupyradifurone identified both the parent compound and its metabolite DFA as main residues of toxicological relevance in the crops investigated (EFSA, 2015). Within the framework of the EFSA pesticides peer review, residue definitions for enforcement and risk assessment were established for flupyradifurone. For plant commodities, two residue definitions for enforcement were defined, covering flupyradifurone and DFA (expressed as DFA). The residue definition for risk assessment was established as the sum of flupyradifurone and DFA, expressed as flupyradifurone.

Codex maximum residue level (MRL) proposals for flupyradifurone were derived by the Joint FAO/WHO Meeting on Pesticide Residues (JMPR) in 2019 and 2022 and discussed at the 52nd and 54th Sessions of the Codex Committee on Pesticide Residues (CCPR). In these assessments, the JMPR residue definition for enforcement in plant commodities covered only flupyradifurone, and the establishment of a separate residue definition for DFA was considered unnecessary.

As a consequence, no Codex MRLs have been established for DFA, whereas CXLs for flupyradifurone were proposed for several commodities.

In this context, EFSA prepared scientific reports to support the EU position for the 52nd and 54th Sessions of the CCPR and performed chronic and acute consumer exposure calculations for flupyradifurone taking into account the Codex MRL proposals by JMPR. EFSA also noted that information on DFA residue levels was not available in the JMPR reports (EFSA, 2021, 2023). However, data on DFA levels related to the uses assessed by JMPR became available in the detailed JMPR evaluations (FAO and WHO, 2019, 2023). This information can now be used to derive MRL proposals for DFA.

Following the adoption of Codex MRLs, the CXLs for flupyradifurone in blackberries, dewberries, raspberries, hops and pineapples were implemented into EU legislation by Commission Regulations (EU) No 2022/1324 and (EU) 2025/581. In view of this, the European Commission requested EFSA, in accordance with Article 43 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, to carry out a targeted review of the maximum residue levels for the metabolite DFA.

ASSESSMENT

IDENTIFICATION OF CXLS POTENTIALLY AFFECTING DFA MRLS

To address the first term of reference, EFSA considered the Codex maximum residue limits (CXLs) for flupyradifurone implemented in EU legislation by Commission Regulations (EU) 2022/1324 and 2025/581, as specified in the mandate. These CXLs were subsequently assessed to determine their impact on the corresponding MRLs for DFA.

The CXLs concerned had been previously assessed by EFSA in the context of the scientific support provided for the EU position at the 52nd and 54th sessions of the Codex Committee on Pesticide Residues (CCPR), where they were concluded to be safe for consumers.

During these assessments, EFSA noted that the residue definitions established by JMPR and in the EU are not compatible. While the EU residue definition for enforcement in plant commodities includes the metabolite DFA, the JMPR residue definition covers only flupyradifurone. Consequently, although CXLs were proposed for flupyradifurone and subsequently implemented in the EU, corresponding MRLs for DFA were not derived.

EFSA further noted that corresponding MRLs for DFA would need to be derived, provided that adequate residue data are available in the JMPR evaluations.

Based on the CXLs implemented by Regulations (EU) 2022/1324 and 2025/581, the commodities relevant to the present assessment are blackberries, dewberries, raspberries, hops and pineapples.

PROPOSED MRLS FOR DFA

EFSA assessed the supervised residue trials supporting the relevant CXLs for flupyradifurone in blackberries, dewberries, raspberries, pineapples and hops. Although the JMPR reports considered in the previous EFSA assessments (EFSA, 2021, 2023) did not include data on DFA residue levels, this information became available in the corresponding detailed JMPR evaluations (FAO and WHO, 2019, 2023). The available DFA residue data were therefore extracted from these evaluations and used to derive MRL proposals under the present mandate.

For cane berries, JMPR applied the proportionality principle to residue trials conducted at application rates approximately half of the critical GAP application rate. In accordance with this approach, residue values were recalculated using a proportionality factor corresponding to the ratio between the critical GAP application rate and the application rate used in the individual trial.

The DFA residue values reported in the JMPR evaluations were expressed as flupyradifurone equivalents in accordance with the JMPR residue definition for enforcement. To derive MRL proposals for DFA according to the EU residue definition for enforcement, these values were converted to DFA equivalents using a molecular weight conversion factor of 0.33, corresponding to the ratio between the molecular weights of DFA and flupyradifurone.

Consumer risk assessment

According to the terms of reference, EFSA performed a consumer risk assessment using PRIMo version 3.1 (EFSA, 2018, 2019), the currently applicable residue definition and the HBGVs established for flupyradifurone and applicable also to DFA (EFSA, 2015). Acute and chronic dietary exposure assessments were conducted using the input values derived from the JMPR assessment for the commodities under consideration (see Table 1). For all other commodities, the risk assessment values derived in the previous MRL assessments were considered (EFSA, 2026). The detailed PRIMo calculations are presented in Appendix A.

The short‐term exposure did not exceed the acute reference dose (ARfD) for any of the commodities under assessment. The highest acute exposure was identified for blackberries, representing 31% of the ARfD for children and 24% of the ARfD for adults.

The chronic dietary risk assessment performed in the framework of the most recent Article 10 assessment (EFSA, 2026) was considered applicable to the current assessment. As no changes were made to the input values used for the chronic exposure calculations, the conclusions of the Article 10 remain valid. The estimated long‐term dietary intake accounted for a maximum of 55% of the acceptable daily intake (ADI) (NL toddler diet). The highest contribution of residues expected in the commodities assessed was 0.14% of the ADI for strawberries, with lower contributions estimated for all other commodities under assessment. Consequently, the overall chronic dietary exposure to flupyradifurone and DFA is unlikely to pose a consumer health concern.

CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS

EFSA identified blackberries, dewberries, raspberries, pineapples and hops as commodities for which the CXLs implemented by Regulations (EU) 2022/1324 and 2025/581 may affect the corresponding MRLs for DFA. Based on the residue data reported in the detailed JMPR evaluations, EFSA derived MRL proposals for DFA for these commodities (see Table 2). The consumer risk assessment did not indicate any acute or chronic dietary intake concern for any of the commodities assessed. Thus, the estimated dietary exposure to DFA resulting from the proposed MRLs is unlikely to pose a risk to consumer health.

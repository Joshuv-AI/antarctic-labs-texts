Fully viable DHOST bounce with extra scalar
Ok Song An, Jin U Kang, Yong Jin Kim, Ui Ri Mun, and Un Gyong Ri

Abstract
In this paper we construct a class of Degenerate Higher-Order Scalar-Tensor (DHOST) theories with an extra scalar field, which admits viable solutions of bouncing universe satisfying the following requirements: (i) absence of Belinski-Khalatnikov-Lifshitz (BKL) instability, ghost and gradient instability, (ii) absence of superluminality, (iii) generation of nearly scale-invariant curvature perturbations and very small tensor-to-scalar ratio, and (iv) conventional asymptotics in the distant past and future, where gravity sector is described by General Relativity and the DHOST scalar has a canonical form of Lagrangian. We also expect our models to have sufficiently small non-Gaussianities of primordial curvature perturbations to be compatible with observations. As such, this work exemplifies for the first time the fully viable two-field DHOST bouncing cosmology, which is free of instability and superluminality problems as well as compatible with observations.

The inflationary scenario

The most promising alternative that can solve the initial singularity problem is a non-singular bouncing scenario (see

In the non-singular bouncing scenario the Hubble parameter has to grow during the bounce period, which within the framework of General Relativity (GR) is possible only if there exists an exotic matter component that does not respect the Null Energy Condition (NEC). Here the problem is that it is challenging to realize the NEC violation without pathologies such as ghost and gradient instabilities and superluminality

The infamous no-go theorems

One serious issue that has to be addressed in constructing a bounce scenario is the so-called Belinsky-Khalatnikov-Lifshitz (BKL) instability
[
42
]
, that is, the contracting phase is unstable due to the growth of anisotropies. In order to suppress the BKL instability within GR one needs to introduce a matter component with a steep equation of state that drives an ekpyrotic contraction phase (also known as a slow contraction phase)
[
43
,
44
,
45
]
, which turns out to be a robust and rapid smoother even for initial conditions far from being spatially-flat, homogeneous and isotropic
[
46
,
47
,
48
,
49
]
. In a word, the bounce scenario should begin with the ekpyrotic contraction phase in order for the BKL instability to be absent.

The problem is that the ekpyrotic contraction makes all perturbations blue-tilted, which is incompatible with the observational data that the curvature perturbations have nearly scale-invariant power spectrum
[
50
]
. A typical resolution to this issue is to utilize the entropic mechanism, which consists of two step: (i) adding an extra scalar field
\chi
that generates nearly scale-invariant perturbations during the ekpyrotic contraction phase and (ii) converting the entropic perturbations into curvature fluctuations around the bounce phase. It is now manifest that the healthy and phenomenologically viable bouncing scenario with conventional asymptotics should consist of two fields, where the adiabatic field
\phi
is described by the exceptional subclass of DHOST Ia theories.

As for the entropic field
\chi
, we incorporate the entropic mechanism successfully with the exceptional subclass of DHOST Ia theories. Regarding the first step of the entropic mechanism, two classes of the scalar field
\chi
have been studied in the literature: the entropic field
\chi
has a canonical kinetic term and an unstable potential
[
51
,
52
,
53
]
, or the entropic field
\chi
has a non-minimal kinetic coupling to the ekpyrotic scalar field
[
54
,
55
,
56
,
57
]
. In either case, the sound speed of
\chi
is equal to unity. The first class of models has a drawback that the potential has a tachyonic instability and thus the ekpyrotic contraction phase would not last long enough unless a special fine-tuned initial conditions are provided
[
58
]
. On the other hand, the second class of models is stable and does not suffer from fine-tuning of initial conditions. For this reason, we choose the second class for generating a nearly scale-invariant power spectrum.

In the second step of the entropic mechanism the conversion can be done either by a repulsive potential
[
53
,
59
,
60
,
61
,
62
]
or by a higher-order kinetic coupling of
\chi
, where the sound speed of
\chi
becomes less than unity during the conversion process
[
63
,
64
]
. In any case, the conversion is induced by a bending of the background trajectory in the field space. Here, the key requisite is that the conversion process should be efficient and smooth; otherwise the resulting curvature perturbations have a small amplitude and may have very large non-Gaussianities exceeding the current observational bounds, see
[
56
,
61
,
62
]
and references therein.

It is well-known that it is rather non-trivial to obtain an efficient conversion process
[
61
,
62
]
, which becomes more challenging in our case due to the stability constraint. This is because the efficient conversion requires the total bending almost unity (see the argument of
[
56
,
61
]
), which can make the background trajectory get out of the stable region in the field space. In this paper we realize an efficient conversion process in terms of the entropic field with luminal sound speed. It must be noted, though, that all prior works related to conversion process have focused only on efficiency and non-Gaussianity but have not been concerned with the total background trajectory in the field space. In this paper we pay a careful attention to this point, namely we obtain an efficient conversion process maintaining the stability and non-superluminality throughout the whole background trajectory. By construction, our model is expected to have sufficiently small non-Gaussianities of primordial curvature perturbations. We again emphasize that this work exemplifies for the first time the fully viable two-field DHOST bouncing cosmology, which is free of instability and superluminality problems as well as compatible with observations.

2.3
Stabilities and non-superluminality conditions: an exceptional subclass

The main types of instabilities that can potentially emerge at the linearized level of perturbations in the high-momentum regime are ghost and gradient instabilities (see
[
8
]
for details). The presence of a ghost instability implies that the vacuum is quantum mechanically unstable, while the gradient instability leads to the exponential growth of perturbations.

For the tensor modes, the ghost instability appears when
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{T}}<0
and
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}<0
in (
5
), while the gradient instability shows up when
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{T}}>0
,
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}<0
or
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{T}}<0

\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}>0

\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{T}}>0
and
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}>0

c_{T}^{2}\equiv\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}/\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{T}}\leq 1

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{T}}\geq\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}>0.

(11)

\displaystyle P_{Y}>0,\quad\Xi\equiv\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{S}}-YP_{Y}f^{2}>0.

\displaystyle\chi=M_{pl}\bar{\chi},\quad Y=\mu^{4}\bar{Y},\quad P(\chi,Y,\phi)=\mu^{4}\bar{P}(\bar{\chi},\bar{Y},\bar{\phi})=\mu^{4}(\bar{P}_{0}+\bar{Y}P_{1}).

(53)

Let us first make an ansatz for
\bar{P}_{0}(\bar{\chi},\bar{\phi})
as follows:

\displaystyle\bar{P}_{0}(\bar{\chi},\bar{\phi})=-\bar{P}_{02}(\bar{\phi})\bar{\chi}^{2}-\bar{P}_{04}(\bar{\phi})\bar{\chi}^{4}.

(54)

The presence of the quartic term is only for stability of the scalar potential. Of course, it is possible to make the scalar potential bounded from below only in terms of the quadratic term. It, however, turns out to be highly non-trivial in this case to make the conversion from
\delta\chi
into
\tilde{\zeta}
sufficiently efficient. Note that in order for interaction energy
-P_{0}(\bar{\chi},\bar{\phi})
to be bounded from below,
\bar{P}_{04}(\bar{\phi})
should be positive and
-\bar{P}_{02}^{2}(\bar{\phi})/\bar{P}_{04}(\bar{\phi})
should be bounded from below. We also assume that
P_{1}
does not depend on
\chi
and the scalar field
\chi
has canonical kinetic term in the distant future. As we see below, the behavior of
P_{1}
in the distant past is not canonical.

The background equations of motion (
87
), (
88
) and (
89
) become

\displaystyle\left(f_{0}(\bar{t})-\bar{P}_{02}(\bar{t})\bar{\chi}^{2}-\bar{P}_{04}(\bar{t})\bar{\chi}^{4}\right)-\left(f_{1}(\bar{t})+\bar{Y}P_{1}(\bar{t})\right)-3f_{2}(\bar{t})

\displaystyle\hskip 100.00015pt+3\bar{H}\bar{\Theta}+3\bar{H}\dot{g}_{1}-3g_{1}(2\dot{\bar{H}}+3\bar{H}^{2})=0,

(55a)

\displaystyle\left(f_{0}(\bar{t})-\bar{P}_{02}(\bar{t})\bar{\chi}^{2}-\bar{P}_{04}(\bar{t})\bar{\chi}^{4}\right)+\left(f_{1}(\bar{t})+\bar{Y}P_{1}(\bar{t})\right)+f_{2}(\bar{t})+2\dot{\bar{H}}+3\bar{H}^{2}=0,

(55b)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a^{3}}\frac{d}{d\bar{t}}\left(a^{3}\dot{\bar{\chi}}P_{1}(\bar{t})\right)+\bar{P}_{02}(\bar{t})\bar{\chi}+2\bar{P}_{04}(\bar{t})\bar{\chi}^{3}=0.

(55c)

For simplicity, we let
\dot{\chi}
decay exponentially in the asymptotic limit so that
\tilde{\zeta}
and
\delta\chi
decouple both in the distant past and in the distant future. It then follows from (
55c
) that
\bar{P}_{02}(\bar{t})
and
\bar{P}_{04}(\bar{t})
are also suppressed exponentially. This offers two advantages. The first one is that the asymptotic behavior of
f_{0,1,2}(\bar{t})
derived in the previous subsection is not affected through the background equations of motion (
55a
) and (
55b
). The second one is that it is guaranteed that
-\bar{P}_{02}^{2}(\bar{\phi})/\bar{P}_{04}(\bar{\phi})
is bounded from below as long as
\bar{P}_{04}
does not touch zero.

Our goal is to choose
\bar{\chi}(\bar{t})
and the Lagrangian functions
P_{1}
,
\bar{P}_{02}
and
\bar{P}_{04}>0
such that

•

a nearly scale-invariant power spectrum of the extra scalar field
\chi
is generated during the ekpyrotic phase,

•

the perturbations
\delta\chi
are converted into the curvature fluctuations
\tilde{\zeta}
with high efficiency by allowing
\chi
to have non-zero kinetic energy and interact with
\bar{\phi}
,

•

the background equations of motion (
55
) and the constraint (
23
) are satisfied,

•

\dot{\bar{\chi}}(\bar{t})
,
\bar{P}_{02}(\bar{t})
and
\bar{P}_{04}(\bar{t})
decay exponentially with the same exponent as
|\bar{t}|\to+\infty
.

4.2
Scalar spectral index for entropic perturbations

First of all, the nearly scale-invariant perturbations can be generated by taking the form of
P_{1}(\chi,\phi)
as
\phi^{-2(1+b)}
with
b\ll 1

[
54
,
55
,
56
,
62
]
. To be concrete, in the decoupled case the equation for the Fourier mode
\delta\chi_{k}
of the perturbation
\delta\chi
becomes

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a^{3}}\frac{d}{dt}\left(a^{3}P_{1}\frac{d}{dt}\delta\chi_{k}\right)+\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}P_{1}\delta\chi_{k}=0,

(56)

or

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{d\eta^{2}}v_{k}+\left(k^{2}-\frac{1}{z}\frac{d^{2}z}{d\eta^{2}}\right)v_{k}=0,

(57)

where
v_{k}\equiv z\delta\chi_{k}
with
z\equiv a\sqrt{P_{1}}
and
\eta
denotes the conformal time defined by
dt=a\;d\eta
. Note that during the ekpyrotic contraction phase, the conformal time
\eta
is related to
t
by

\displaystyle-\eta\sim(-t)^{\frac{\epsilon-1}{\epsilon}}.

(58)

It follows from
P_{1}\sim(-\bar{t})^{-2(1+b)}
that
z\sim(-\eta)^{-b\epsilon/(\epsilon-1)-1}
. Since in the distant past the mode functions obey the equation for a free field in Minkowski space, we can impose the Bunch-Davies vacuum initial condition for the mode functions, namely

\displaystyle\lim_{k\eta\to-\infty}v_{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2k}}e^{-ik\eta}.

(59)

The solution of (
57
) is then

\displaystyle v_{k}=\frac{\sqrt{-\pi\eta}}{2}H_{\nu}^{(1)}(-k\eta)\quad\text{with }\nu=\frac{3}{2}+\frac{b\epsilon}{\epsilon-1},

(60)

where
H_{\nu}^{(1)}
is a Hankel function of the first kind. In the super-horizon limit, i.e.
|k\eta|\ll 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle v_{k}\sim\frac{(-\eta)^{\frac{1}{2}-\nu}}{k^{\nu}},

(61)

and thus we find the spectral index
n_{s}
related to
b
by

\displaystyle n_{s}=1-\frac{2b\epsilon}{\epsilon-1}.

(62)

Since
\epsilon\gg 1
, for the value of
b
around
0.0158
the spectral tilt becomes
n_{s}\simeq 0.965
, which is in good agreement with the Planck data
[
50
]
. Eventually, once
P_{1}(\bar{\phi})=P_{1}(\bar{t})
behaves in the far past as

\displaystyle P_{1}(\bar{t})\propto\frac{1}{(-\bar{t})^{2(1+b)}}\quad\text{ for }\bar{t}\to-\infty,

(63)

the power spectrum of
\delta\bar{\chi}
is nearly scale-invariant.

4.3
Conversion of entropic perturbations into curvature fluctuations

Note that during the ekpyrotic phase the curvature fluctuation
\tilde{\zeta}
is highly blue-shifted, namely

\displaystyle a\sqrt{\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{S}}}M_{pl}\tilde{\zeta}_{k}=\frac{\sqrt{-\pi\eta}}{2}H_{\nu_{1}}^{(1)}(-k\eta)\quad\text{with}\quad\nu_{1}=\frac{\epsilon-3}{2(\epsilon-1)},

(64)

where
0<\nu_{1}<\frac{1}{2}
, given
\epsilon>3
. It follows that
a\sqrt{\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{S}}}M_{pl}\tilde{\zeta}_{k}\sim\frac{(-\eta)^{\frac{1}{2}-\nu_{1}}}{k^{\nu_{1}}}
for
|k\eta|\ll 1
, and hence the amplitude of
\tilde{\zeta}
is much smaller than the one of
\delta\bar{\chi}
during the ekpyrotic phase. We therefore need to introduce a mechanism to convert
\delta\bar{\chi}
into
\tilde{\zeta}
with high efficiency.

We choose the following form of
P_{1}(\bar{t})
(see Fig.
6
)

\displaystyle P_{1}(\bar{t})=

\displaystyle\;\frac{1}{2}\frac{\sigma\left(-\frac{\bar{t}}{\tau}\right)}{\left(\frac{\bar{t}}{\tau}\right)^{2(1+b)}+1}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma\left(\frac{\bar{t}}{\tau}\right),

(65)

so that the scalar field
\chi
has canonical kinetic energy in the far future.

Plot of
P_{1}
. The parameters are set to be
\tau=10
and
b=0.0158
, respectively.

Now we need to choose
\bar{\chi}
,
\bar{P}_{02}
and
\bar{P}_{04}
such that conversion from the perturbation
\delta\bar{\chi}
into the curvature fluctuation
\tilde{\zeta}
occurs efficiently. We remind that
\bar{P}_{04}
is introduced solely for stability of the potential and
\bar{P}_{02}
is determined by the background equation of motion (
55c
) once
\bar{\chi}
and
\bar{P}_{04}
are specified. It is therefore crucial to choose the form of
\bar{\chi}
appropriately.

Let us first investigate what is the main factor in conversion from entropy to curvature perturbations. It follows from (
7
) that the scalar perturbations obey the following equations:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a^{3}}\frac{d}{d\bar{t}}\left[a^{3}\left(\left(\bar{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{S}}+\frac{\bar{Y}P_{1}}{\bar{\Theta}^{2}}\right)\dot{\tilde{\zeta}}_{k}+\dot{\bar{\chi}}P_{1}\bar{g}\dot{\delta\bar{\chi}}_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\bar{\Psi}_{1}\delta\bar{\chi}_{k}\right)\right]+\hskip 30.00005pt

\displaystyle+\frac{\bar{k}^{2}}{a^{2}}\left(\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\zeta}_{k}+\dot{\bar{\chi}}P_{1}\bar{g}\delta\bar{\chi}_{k}\right)\;

\displaystyle=0,

(66a)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a^{3}}\frac{d}{d\bar{t}}\left[a^{3}\left(P_{1}\dot{\delta\bar{\chi}}_{k}+\dot{\bar{\chi}}P_{1}\bar{g}\dot{\tilde{\zeta}}_{k}\right)\right]+\frac{\bar{k}^{2}}{a^{2}}\left(P_{1}\delta\bar{\chi}_{k}+\dot{\bar{\chi}}P_{1}\bar{g}\tilde{\zeta}_{k}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\bar{\Psi}_{1}\dot{\tilde{\zeta}}_{k}-\bar{\Psi}_{2}\delta\bar{\chi}_{k}\;

\displaystyle=0,

(66b)

where
\bar{k}=\frac{M_{pl}k}{\mu^{2}}
is the (dimensionless) rescaled wavenumber and

\displaystyle\bar{\Psi}_{1}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{2}{\bar{\Theta}^{2}}\left[(\bar{Y}P_{1}+\bar{\Sigma})\dot{\bar{\chi}}P_{1}-\bar{\Theta}(\bar{P}_{02}\bar{\chi}+2\bar{P}_{04}\bar{\chi}^{3})(1+3\Delta)\right],

(66c)

\displaystyle\bar{\Psi}_{2}=

\displaystyle\;-\bar{P}_{02}-6\bar{P}_{04}\bar{\chi}^{2}+\frac{1}{a^{3}}\frac{d}{d\bar{t}}\left(\frac{a^{3}\bar{Y}P_{1}^{2}\left(1-3\Delta\right)}{\bar{\Theta}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\dot{\bar{\chi}}P_{1}}{\bar{\Theta}^{2}}\left[\dot{\bar{\chi}}P_{1}(\bar{Y}P_{1}+\bar{\Sigma})+\bar{\Theta}P_{0\bar{\chi}}(1+3\Delta)\right],

(66d)

\displaystyle\bar{g}=

\displaystyle\;-\frac{1}{\bar{\Theta}}\left(1-3\Delta\right).

(66e)

In the long wavelength limit
\bar{k}\to 0
, which is of observational interest, the equations (
66
) become

\displaystyle\left(\bar{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{S}}+\frac{\bar{Y}P_{1}}{\bar{\Theta}^{2}}\right)\dot{\tilde{\zeta}}_{k}+\dot{\bar{\chi}}P_{1}\bar{g}\dot{\delta\bar{\chi}}_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\bar{\Psi}_{1}\delta\bar{\chi}_{k}=\frac{C_{0}}{a^{3}},

(67a)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a^{3}}\frac{d}{d\bar{t}}\left[a^{3}\left(P_{1}\dot{\delta\bar{\chi}}_{k}+\dot{\bar{\chi}}P_{1}\bar{g}\dot{\tilde{\zeta}}_{k}\right)\right]-\frac{1}{2}\bar{\Psi}_{1}\dot{\tilde{\zeta}}_{k}-\bar{\Psi}_{2}\delta\bar{\chi}_{k}=0,

(67b)

or

\displaystyle 0=\dot{\tilde{\zeta}}_{k}-\frac{1}{\bar{G}_{11}}\left(\frac{C_{0}}{a^{3}}-\bar{G}_{12}\dot{\delta\bar{\chi}}_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\bar{\Psi}_{1}\delta\bar{\chi}_{k}\right),

(68a)

\displaystyle 0=\left(P_{1}-\frac{\bar{G}_{12}^{2}}{\bar{G}_{11}}\right)\ddot{\delta\bar{\chi}}_{k}+\left[3\bar{H}\left(P_{1}-\frac{\bar{G}_{12}^{2}}{\bar{G}_{11}}\right)+\dot{P}_{1}-\frac{d}{d\bar{t}}\left(\frac{\bar{G}_{12}^{2}}{\bar{G}_{11}}\right)\right]\dot{\delta\bar{\chi}}_{k}

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt+\left[\frac{\bar{\Psi}_{1}^{2}}{4\bar{G}_{11}}-3\bar{H}\frac{\bar{G}_{12}}{2\bar{G}_{11}}\bar{\Psi}_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{d\bar{t}}\left(\frac{\bar{G}_{12}\bar{\Psi}_{1}}{\bar{G}_{11}}\right)-\bar{\Psi}_{2}\right]\delta\bar{\chi}_{k}

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt+\frac{C_{0}}{a^{3}}\left[\frac{d}{d\bar{t}}\left(\frac{\bar{G}_{12}}{\bar{G}_{11}}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\bar{\Psi}_{1}}{\bar{G}_{11}}\right],

(68b)

where
C_{0}
is an integration constant, which is determined by the initial condition, and

\displaystyle\bar{G}_{11}=\bar{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{S}}+\frac{\bar{Y}P_{1}}{\bar{\Theta}^{2}},\quad\bar{G}_{12}=\dot{\bar{\chi}}P_{1}\bar{g}.

(69)

It is now evident from (
68a
) that
\bar{\Psi}_{1}/\bar{G}_{11}
is the main factor that controls the efficiency of conversion process. Since
\bar{\Psi}_{1}
depends on
\dot{\bar{\chi}}
, one is tempted to make
|\dot{\bar{\chi}}|
large. This approach has drawbacks, though, due to the constraint (
23
). Therefore, the only possible way to increase
\bar{\Psi}_{1}
is to make
|\bar{P}_{02}\bar{\chi}+2\bar{P}_{04}\bar{\chi}^{3}|=|\frac{d}{d\bar{t}}(a\dot{\bar{\chi}}P_{1})|
large by increasing
\ddot{\bar{\chi}}
instead of
\dot{\bar{\chi}}
. For this reason, we choose the following form of
\bar{\chi}
(see Fig.
7
)

\displaystyle\bar{\chi}=\bar{\chi}_{0}+\lambda\,\text{sech}\left(\frac{\bar{t}-\bar{t}_{c}}{2\tau}\right)\left[e^{-\left(\frac{\bar{t}-\bar{t}_{c}}{4\tau}\right)^{2}}-q\right],

(70)

where the parameter
\lambda
controls the overall rate of change of
\bar{\chi}
and the parameter
\bar{t}_{c}
is related to the moment of conversion from
\delta\chi
into
\tilde{\zeta}
. We set

\bar{\chi}_{0}
is introduced in order for

not to cross zero; otherwise
\bar{P}_{02}
and

become singular at a certain point. The overall shape of the background trajectory around the conversion phase is determined by the function

in (

\bar{P}_{02}
positive in the asymptotic limit. The second role, which is actually played by the parameter

, is necessary since we do not want the tachyonic instability to appear in the asymptotic part of the background trajectory of interest. By adjusting the parameters
\bar{\chi}_{0}
,
\lambda
and

). Here we mean the conversion efficiency by
[

,

\displaystyle|\tilde{\zeta}_{k}(\bar{t}_{conv-end})/\delta\bar{\chi}_{k}(\bar{t}_{conv-begin})|,

(71)

where
\bar{t}_{conv-begin}
and
\bar{t}_{conv-end}
denote the beginning and end moment of the conversion process, respectively. And the conversion is said to be smooth when the comoving Hubble radius
(aH)^{-1}
changes by order of one e-fold during conversion
[
70
,

]

4.4
The amplitude of scalar power spectrum and characteristic energy scale

Now we determine the characteristic energy scale
\mu
which is related to the amplitude of the perturbations
\delta\bar{\chi}
. Since
\delta\bar{\chi}
is almost completely converted into
\tilde{\zeta}
, the power spectrum of
\delta\bar{\chi}
should be

\displaystyle\Delta_{\delta\chi}^{2}(k)\equiv k^{3}\braket{\delta\bar{\chi}_{k}^{2}}=A_{s}\left(\frac{k}{k_{\star}}\right)^{n_{s}-1},

(78)

where
k_{\star}
denotes the pivot scale
0.05\text{Mpc}^{-1}\simeq 10^{-59}M_{pl}
, and
A_{s}
denotes the measured amplitude at
k_{\star}
, namely
[
50
]

\displaystyle A_{s}=(2.105\pm 0.030)\times 10^{-9}.

(79)

After the mode
v_{k}
exits the horizon, its amplitude can be written as

\displaystyle a\sqrt{P_{1}}M_{pl}\delta\bar{\chi}_{k}=v_{k}\simeq\frac{\sqrt{-\pi\eta}}{2}\frac{1}{(-k\eta)^{\nu}}.

(80)

On the other hand, the conformal time
\eta
is related to the rescaled cosmic time
\bar{t}
by

\displaystyle\left(\frac{-\bar{t}}{\tau}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\sim\frac{\epsilon-1}{\epsilon}\frac{\mu^{2}}{M_{pl}}\left(\frac{-\eta}{\tau}\right).

(81)

It then follows that

\displaystyle a\sqrt{P_{1}}\sim\left[\frac{\epsilon-1}{\epsilon}\frac{\mu^{2}}{M_{pl}}\left(\frac{-\eta}{\tau}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}-\nu},

(82)

and thus

\displaystyle\Delta_{\delta\chi}^{2}(k)\sim\frac{\pi}{4}\left(\frac{\epsilon-1}{\epsilon\tau}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\mu}{M_{pl}}\right)^{4}\left(\frac{\epsilon-1}{\epsilon\tau}\frac{\mu^{2}}{M_{pl}k_{\star}}\right)^{1-n_{s}}\left(\frac{k}{k_{\star}}\right)^{n_{s}-1}.

(83)

It follows from (
83
) that for
\mu\sim{\cal O}(10^{-2})M_{pl}
the amplitude of the scalar power spectrum is in good agreement with the Planck data (
79
).

For completeness, we discuss the tensor-to-scalar ratio. Since
\bar{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{T}}=\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathcal{T}}=1
in our scenario, it is evident that the tensor perturbations carry a deep blue spectral index (actually equal to
4-2\nu_{1}
, where
\nu_{1}
is given by (
64
)). It then follows that at the pivot scale the tensor-to-scalar ratio is negligibly small, which is in fact a typical feature of bouncing scenarios that begin with the ekpyrotic contraction phase. Our scenario is therefore compatible with the observational data, regarding the tensor-to-scalar ratio as well.

•

it is free of BKL, ghost and gradient instabilities,

•

it has conventional asymptotics for DHOST sector,

•

it is free of superluminality,

•

it predicts nearly scale-invariant curvature perturbations, generated by an entropic mechanism, compatible with observations,

•

it predicts a sufficiently low tensor-to-scalar ratio.

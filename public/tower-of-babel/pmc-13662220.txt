DecBin: Bayesian-optimized deep clustering for long-read metagenomic binning
Authors: Yi-Heng Zhu, Xinchi Li, Zuo Liu, Jing Liu, Rui Ye, Wei Xue, Gaofei Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAccurate metagenomic binning is essential for reconstructing microbial genomes, thereby facilitating the discovery of novel taxa, the identification of pathogens, and the characterization of antimicrobial resistance genes. Most existing methods are designed for assembled contigs and cannot be directly applied to raw long reads. Although a few dedicated long-read binning methods have emerged, challenges remain in bin-number estimation and representation learning. Here, we propose Deep Embedded Clustering for Long-read Binning (DecBin), an adaptive method for directly binning raw long reads through integrating a variational autoencoder (VAE), Bayesian optimization, and deep embedded clustering within a unified framework. Computational experiments on large-scale simulated and mock-community datasets demonstrate that DecBin outperforms state-of-the-art methods in both long-read binning and metagenome-assembled genome recovery, while also achieving strain-level resolution in a low-complexity natural microbiome. Further analysis shows that the advantage of DecBin is partly attributable to its Davies–Bouldin index-guided Bayesian optimization, which explicitly evaluates candidate cluster numbers using a global cluster-validity criterion, thereby improving bin-number estimation across communities of varying complexity. Meanwhile, joint fine-tuning of the VAE encoder and clustering layer enables clustering feedback to refine the latent space, yielding representations better aligned with genome partitions. These findings highlight the potential of adaptive deep clustering for metagenomic binning directly from raw long reads without prior assembly. The source code of DecBin is freely available at https://github.com/LorMeBioAI/DecBin.

Introduction

Metagenomic binning groups sequence fragments inferred to originate from the same genome into distinct bins, facilitating the reconstruction of microbial draft genomes [1–4]. Accurate binning is critical for improving genome completeness and reducing contamination, thereby supporting the discovery of novel microbial taxa, the identification of pathogenic microorganisms, and the characterization of antimicrobial resistance genes [5–8].

Conventional metagenomic binning is generally performed on contigs assembled from sequencing reads and relies on the assumption that sufficiently long contigs originating from the same genome exhibit similar sequence composition and abundance profiles [9–12]. For example, the Variational Autoencoder for Metagenomic Binning (VAMB) [13] uses a variational autoencoder (VAE) to learn latent representations from the composition and abundance features of contigs, followed by clustering with an iterative medoid algorithm. Adversarial Autoencoders (AAEs) for Metagenomic Binning [14] extends VAMB by employing an AAE to improve feature representation and binning accuracy (ACC). SemiBin [15] leverages deep contrastive learning with Siamese neural networks to incorporate must-link and cannot-link constraints between contigs. BASALT [16] integrates multiple assemblies and uses tetranucleotide frequency and the coverage correlation coefficient to improve binning ACC and the completeness of recovered genomes. LorBin [17] integrates VAE-based feature embedding, two-stage multiscale adaptive clustering, and bin-quality-guided reclustering to improve the recovery of high-quality genomes from imbalanced microbial communities.

Despite these advances in contig-level binning, the growing use of third-generation sequencing has created an increasing demand for methods that can directly bin raw long reads [18–21]. Third-generation sequencing technologies generate reads reaching lengths of up to 1 MB, thereby preserving richer genomic context and making direct binning prior to assembly increasingly feasible [18, 22–25]. However, existing contig-based methods cannot be directly applied to raw long reads because they generally rely heavily on coverage profiles estimated by mapping reads to assembled contigs, whereas reliable coverage profiles are difficult to obtain for individual long reads [26]. Moreover, sequencing errors and large differences in coverage among species can hinder the assembly of long and accurate contigs, particularly for low-abundance species, leading to fragmented assemblies and incomplete genome recovery [27].

Together, these challenges have motivated the development of dedicated methods for directly binning raw long reads prior to assembly. To date, only a limited number of methods have been developed for this purpose, with MetaBCC-LR [28] and LRBinner [26] being representative examples. MetaBCC-LR sequentially applies dimensionality reduction and density-based clustering to k-mer coverage histograms and oligonucleotide composition profiles. LRBinner uses a VAE to jointly encode compositional and abundance-related features, followed by independent clustering of the learned latent representations. Despite their effectiveness, both methods have two limitations. First, the number of output bins emerges from local density- or distance-based clustering rules, without explicitly comparing and optimizing candidate cluster numbers using a global cluster-validity metric. This may lead to inaccurate estimation of the number of genome bins, particularly in complex or imbalanced microbial communities. Second, feature representation and clustering are performed sequentially rather than being jointly optimized. Consequently, clustering feedback cannot be used to adapt the feature space, potentially compromising accurate read-to-genome assignment.

Motivated by these methodological limitations, we developed Deep Embedded Clustering for Long-read Binning (DecBin), an adaptive long-read metagenomic binning method through integrating the VAE, Bayesian optimization, and deep embedded clustering into a unified framework. Specifically, DecBin (i) learns informative latent representations directly from raw long reads using a VAE; (ii) uses Bayesian optimization guided by the Davies–Bouldin index (DBI) [29], a global cluster-validity metric, to adaptively estimate the number of clusters for initializing the deep clustering layer; and (iii) jointly fine-tunes the VAE encoder and deep clustering layer to refine latent representations and cluster assignments. Together, these designs contribute to improved long-read binning ACC and read utilization, thereby facilitating bin-specific assembly and metagenome-assembled genome (MAG) recovery. We demonstrate that DecBin consistently outperforms existing long-read binning approaches on both simulated and experimental mock-community datasets and achieves strain-level resolution in a low-complexity natural microbiome. These advances arise from two main algorithmic contributions: (a) DBI-guided Bayesian optimization for explicit bin-number estimation and (b) clustering-guided refinement of the latent space to better align learned representations with genome partitions.

Materials and methods

Datasets

Three types of datasets were selected to provide complementary evaluations of DecBin under simulated, experimental mock-community, and strain-level resolution settings.

Simulated datasets

Three labeled long-read metagenomic datasets, designated sim10, sim30, and sim70, were generated from reference genomes representing 10, 30, and 70 microbial species, respectively, using PBSIM2, a long-read sequencing simulator [30], in sampling-based mode with profiles derived from PacBio HiFi reads. For each dataset, the count of reads, sequencing depth, species composition and relative abundance, and genomic relatedness among the represented species are summarized in Supplementary Table S1 of the Supporting Information (SI). The known read-to-genome assignments enable direct quantitative evaluation of DecBin’s clustering performance across microbial communities differing in size, abundance structure, and genomic relatedness.

Experimental mock-community dataset

Three experimentally generated PacBio HiFi mock-community datasets, SRR13128012, SRR13128013, and SRR13128014, were obtained from the LRBinner study [26], comprising 21 strains from 17 species, with further composition details summarized in Supplementary Table S2. These datasets contain authentic sequencing noise and uneven species-abundance distributions, enabling evaluation of DecBin’s practical performance on real-world metagenomic data.

Strain-level resolution dataset

The natural whey starter microbiome dataset NWC2 [31] was specifically selected to evaluate DecBin’s ability to achieve strain-level resolution. This dataset mainly contains four bacterial strains from three species (see Supplementary Table S3 for composition details), including two genomically closely related Lactobacillus helveticus (L. helveticus) strains (NWC_2_3 and NWC_2_4), thereby providing a stringent test of intraspecific strain separation.

Overview of the DecBin workflow

We developed DecBin, an adaptive long-read binning method that integrates the VAE [32], Bayesian optimization [33], and deep clustering, with the overall framework illustrated in Fig. 1. The method comprises five key components:

The framework of DecBin; (1) feature representation; (2) VAE pre-training; (3) Bayesian optimization; (4) deep embedded clustering; (5) joint fine-tuning.

(1) Feature representation: For each input metagenomic dataset, the k-mer frequency vector and coverage histogram of each long read were computed and concatenated to form its input feature vector.

(2) VAE pre-training: The resulting feature vectors from all long reads were used to pre-train the VAE in an unsupervised manner, producing a low-dimensional latent representation for each read.

(3) Bayesian optimization: A random 10% subset of the latent representations was used to iteratively evaluate candidate cluster numbers by applying K-means [34] with the DBI [29] as the objective function. The candidate yielding the minimum DBI was selected as the optimal number of clusters (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{upgreek} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document} ${K}^{\ast }$\end{document}).

(4) Deep embedded clustering: A clustering layer was appended to the VAE encoder and initialized using the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{upgreek} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document} ${K}^{\ast }$\end{document} cluster centers obtained by K-means. Student’s t-distribution-based similarity kernel was then used to convert the distances between each latent representation and the cluster centers into soft cluster-assignment probabilities.

(5) Joint fine-tuning: The VAE encoder and clustering layer were jointly fine-tuned to refine the latent representations and cluster assignments, after which each long read was assigned to a genome bin.

After fine-tuning, each read was assigned to the cluster with the highest soft assignment probability.

Evaluation metrics

To evaluate the clustering performance of DecBin, we used three complementary metrics, namely, accuracy (ACC), normalized mutual information (NMI), and adjusted Rand index (ARI), as defined in Zelig et al. [44]. ACC measures the proportion of correctly classified samples after matching the predicted cluster labels to the ground-truth class labels. NMI quantifies the amount of shared information between the clustering results and the true labels. ARI measures the pairwise agreement between the clustering results and the true groupings while correcting for agreement expected by chance.

To assess the quality of the bins obtained by each binning tool, we used CheckM [45] to determine the completeness and contamination of the bins. We defined high-quality bins as those with a completeness of ≥90% and a contamination of ≤5%; medium-quality bins as those with a completeness of ≥50% and a contamination of <10%; and low-quality bins as those with a completeness of <50% and a contamination of <10%.

Implementation details and parameter settings

The hyperparameters for the individual algorithmic modules in DecBin were set as follows. In the feature representation module, the compositional k-mer size was set to k=4 after benchmarking DecBin with k=3, 4, 5, and 6 across three simulated datasets, with the corresponding ACC values reported in Supplementary Table S4 of SI. Overall, k=4 achieved the highest average ACC while maintaining lower computational and storage costs. Values greater than 6 were not further evaluated because of the rapidly increasing computational cost. Feature extraction, including k-mer composition and θ-mer coverage calculation, was performed using 16 CPU threads, whereas model training was performed using a single CPU thread with GPU acceleration.

Overall, DecBin achieved substantially better clustering performance than the two competing methods, with only a moderate increase in runtime and memory usage. Specifically, DecBin used Bayesian optimization to identify clusters that most closely match the true number of genomes and yielded 10, 28, and 58 clusters for sim10, sim30, and sim70, respectively. In comparison, LRBinner identified 9, 17, and 18 clusters for the three datasets, whereas MetaBCC-LR found only 4 clusters across all datasets. DecBin also achieved the highest ACC values of 0.8506, 0.6878, and 0.4898 for sim10, sim30, and sim70, respectively, and surpassed the second-best method, LRBinner, by 4.3%, 9.5%, and 39.1%, respectively.

To further evaluate the recovery of conserved core genomic regions, we used MetaCHIP [47] to identify 43 single-copy marker genes and examined their distribution across the bins recovered by DecBin. For each marker gene, the detection rate was calculated as the proportion of recovered bins in which it was detected. Figure 2c summarizes the distributions of marker-gene detection rates across the three simulated datasets, while the detection rates of individual marker genes are shown in Supplementary Figs S1, S2, and S3. Detection rates ranged from 90.0% to 100% for sim10, 85.7% to 100% for sim30, and 81.0% to 96.6% for sim70. Despite a moderate decline with increasing community complexity, marker-gene detection remained consistently high across the DecBin-recovered bins. These results suggest that DecBin generally preserves conserved core genomic regions across communities of varying complexity.

Given the potential impact of repetitive regions on read assignment, we further assessed DecBin’s robustness by separately evaluating reads identified by RepeatMasker [48] as originating from repetitive genomic regions. DecBin achieved ACC values of 0.8274, 0.7058, and 0.4979 for these reads on sim10, sim30, and sim70, respectively. These results closely matched the corresponding overall ACC values, with differences of no more than 2.32 percentage points, suggesting that DecBin is relatively robust to repetitive genomic regions. Together, these analyses suggest that DecBin exhibits robust performance across conserved and repetitive genomic regions.

Contribution analysis of key DecBin components

To systematically investigate the contributions of DecBin’s key algorithmic components, we conducted component-level analyses on the three simulated datasets. Specifically, we evaluated the effects of (1) Bayesian optimization for cluster-number estimation and (2) joint fine-tuning of the VAE encoder and clustering layer.

(1) Bayesian optimization improves cluster-number estimation

To evaluate the contribution of Bayesian optimization to cluster-number estimation, we replaced it with random search while keeping the upstream components unchanged, including feature representation and VAE pre-training. For both strategies, the same K-means clustering procedure, search range, number of candidate evaluations, and DBI objective were used. As shown in Supplementary Table S6 of the SI, both strategies estimated 10 clusters for the sim10 dataset, matching the expected species number of 10. For sim30 and sim70, random search estimated 23 and 50 clusters, respectively, whereas Bayesian optimization estimated 28 and 58 clusters, which were closer to the expected species numbers of 30 and 70. These results indicate that Bayesian optimization provides more accurate cluster-number estimation than random search, particularly for more complex microbial communities.

(2) Joint fine-tuning enhances binning performance

DecBin uses the deep clustering layer to bin the fine-tuned features. The clustering assignments obtained during initialization were used as the starting point for fine-tuning. We evaluated the effects of the clustering layer on ACC, NMI, and ARI during fine-tuning across three simulated datasets, as illustrated in Fig. 3a. All three metrics improved by at least 1%. The curves of ACC, NMI, and ARI indicated that the clustering layer brought features from long reads within the same cluster closer together while increasing the separation between different clusters.

To demonstrate how joint training of the VAE and clustering layers reshaped the latent feature space, we sampled 1000 long reads from the sim10 dataset and visualized their latent embeddings via t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding [49], as shown in Fig. 3b. As joint training progressed, the boundaries between different clusters became increasingly distinct. Long reads from the same species moved closer together, whereas those from different species became more widely separated.

To further assess the benefits of jointly optimizing feature learning by the VAE encoder and clustering, we conducted ablation experiments on the three simulated datasets using K-means, a simplified Hierarchical Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise (HDBSCAN) procedure from MetaBCC-LR, and iterative medoid clustering from LRBinner. These three algorithms were applied to fixed feature embeddings generated by the pre-trained VAE encoder and did not jointly optimize feature learning and cluster assignments. We compared the ACC, NMI, and ARI values between DecBin and these three methods, as summarized in Fig. 3c. DecBin achieved the best overall performance on sim30 and sim70. On sim30, DecBin achieved 0.6878 (ACC), 0.7517 (NMI), and 0.6319 (ARI), surpassing the best-performing method without joint optimization of feature learning and clustering by at least 5% across all three evaluation metrics. On sim70, DecBin achieved 0.4898 (ACC), 0.6478 (NMI), and 0.4358 (ARI), outperforming the best competing method by at least 2%. HDBSCAN performed best on sim10, which contained the fewest species. However, its performance declined markedly as species diversity increased, suggesting that HDBSCAN is better suited to less complex datasets with fewer species.

Overall, these results demonstrate that jointly optimizing feature learning and clustering improves the quality of the learned embeddings and enhances clustering performance, particularly on datasets with greater species diversity and complexity.

Strain-level resolution in a natural whey starter culture

To evaluate DecBin’s ability to distinguish closely related strains, we analyzed the NWC2 natural whey starter microbiome, a low-complexity community dominated by four bacterial strains representing three species, with its detailed composition summarized in Supplementary Table S3. To quantify their genomic relatedness, we calculated pairwise average nucleotide identity (ANI) among the four reference genomes using FastANI [54], with the resulting ANI matrix shown in Fig. 5a. This analysis showed that the two L. helveticus strains, NWC_2_3 and NWC_2_4, shared an ANI of 98.94%, whereas all other pairwise ANI values were below 77%. Therefore, the high genomic similarity between NWC_2_3 and NWC_2_4 makes distinguishing these two strains particularly challenging and provides a stringent test of strain-level resolution.

We further compared DecBin with two competing binning methods, LRBinner and MetaBCC-LR, on the NWC2 dataset. For each method, reads assigned to individual bins were assembled separately using metaFlye [50], and the resulting contigs were aligned to the four reference genomes using minimap2 [51]. A strain was considered successfully resolved when the contigs from a distinct bin could be assigned to its reference genome. The completeness and contamination of each strain-associated assembly were subsequently assessed using CheckM [45].

Figure 5b summarizes the number of strains successfully resolved by each method, while Fig. 5c presents the CheckM marker-gene profiles of the strain-associated assemblies recovered by DecBin and LRBinner, together with their completeness, contamination, and strain heterogeneity values. Overall, DecBin showed the strongest strain recovery performance, successfully resolving all four strains, compared with three resolved by LRBinner and 0 by MetaBCC-LR. The four strains resolved by DecBin achieved completeness values of 73.91%–96.17%, contamination values of 8.66%–28.55%, and strain heterogeneity values of 13.33%–97.67%. For the three strains resolved by both DecBin and LRBinner, DecBin consistently yielded substantially lower contamination, despite slightly lower completeness. The high strain heterogeneity observed for Lactobacillus delbrueckii (L. delbrueckii) NWC_2_2 and L. helveticus NWC_2_3 indicates that part of the contamination originated from closely related strains. Notably, DecBin was the only method that resolved both highly similar L. helveticus strains, demonstrating its stronger strain-level resolution on the NWC2 dataset.

Discussion and conclusion

In this work, we proposed DecBin, an adaptive method for long-read metagenomic binning that integrates the VAE, Bayesian optimization, and deep embedded clustering into a unified framework. DecBin was systematically benchmarked against state-of-the-art methods on both simulated and experimental mock-community datasets, demonstrating superior binning performance and strain-level resolution, thereby facilitating metagenomic genome reconstruction. This performance advantage can be attributed to the following algorithmic innovations, which partly address the limitations of existing methods.

First, DecBin enables explicit, validity-guided estimation of the cluster number by using Bayesian optimization with the DBI, rather than relying on cluster numbers that emerge indirectly from the local density- or distance-based clustering rules used by existing methods. This explicit optimization strategy improves cluster-number estimation across communities of varying complexity. Second, DecBin jointly fine-tunes the VAE encoder and the deep embedded clustering layer, rather than separating representation learning from clustering as in previous methods, thereby enabling the simultaneous optimization of latent representations and cluster assignments. This joint optimization enhances intra-cluster compactness and inter-cluster separation, yielding representations better aligned with genome partitions. Moreover, the consistent performance improvements shown in Fig. 3c, particularly on the more complex sim30 and sim70 datasets, indicate that clustering-aware representation refinement may be especially beneficial for communities with greater diversity and complexity.

These findings suggest that the two key components of DecBin, namely, (i) DBI-guided Bayesian optimization for cluster-number estimation and (ii) joint optimization of representation learning and clustering, may inform future improvements to existing long-read binning frameworks. Explicit, validity-guided cluster-number estimation could reduce reliance on heuristic local clustering rules, while clustering feedback may yield latent representations better aligned with genome partitions. However, their transferability and effectiveness across different architectures require further evaluation.

Despite these advances, DecBin has several limitations. First, Bayesian optimization and joint fine-tuning introduce additional computational overhead. Second, its current feature representation does not leverage pretrained genomic language models [55], which may further improve feature representation and binning performance. Work in these directions is ongoing.

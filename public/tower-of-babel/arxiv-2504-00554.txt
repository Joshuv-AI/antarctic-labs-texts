Symmetry-based observers for ODE systems
Stefano Battilotti

Abstract
In this paper we introduce an observer design framework for ordinary differential equation (ODE) systems based on various types of existing or even novel one-parameter symmetries (exact, asymptotic and variational) ending up with a certain number of semi-global and global observers, with bounded or unbounded system’s solutions and with infinite- or finite-time convergence. We compare some of these symmetry-based observers with existing observers, recovering for instance the same performances of high-gain semiglobal observers and the finite-time convergence capabilities of sliding mode observers, while obtaining novel global observers where existing techniques are not able to provide any.

A LGT transforms the system map
\Sigma
into
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}
(and also
\Sigma(t)
into
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}(t)
) and any solution
z(t)
of the system
\Sigma(t)=0
into a function
\widetilde{z}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathsf{t}_{\mathfrak{p}})
(in time scale
\mathsf{t}_{\mathfrak{p}}
) which, however, is not always a solution of the system
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathsf{t}_{\mathfrak{p}})=0
. A LGT is a symmetry of the system
\Sigma(t)=0
if
\widetilde{z}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathsf{t}_{\mathfrak{p}})
is a solution of
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathsf{t}_{\mathfrak{p}})=0
.

Definition IV.1

(Symmetries)
. A LGT (resp. GGT)
\Psi_{\mathfrak{p}}
on
{\mathcal{M}}
(with infinitesimal generator
g
) is a
local (resp. global)
C^{\infty}
symmetry (LS, resp. GS)
of the system
\Sigma(t)=0
if either one of the following equivalent conditions holds:

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912pt{\rm(i)}\;\forall{\mathfrak{p}}\in{\mathbb{R}},\forall(t,\mathsf{z})\in{\rm Dom}(\Psi_{\mathfrak{p}}):\Sigma=0\Rightarrow\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}=0,

(15)

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912pt{\rm(ii)}\;\forall(t,\mathsf{z})\in{\mathcal{M}}:\Sigma=0\Rightarrow L_{{\rm pr}^{[1]}{\textbf{g}}}\Sigma=0.\hfill\blacktriangleleft

(16)

Remark IV.1

(A geometric interpretation of conditions (
15
) and (
16
)
). Condition (
16
) is given for an ODE (more generally, for a partial differential equations) system in
[
28
]
and it is a condition based on the invariance of the system map evaluated at the integral curves of
{\rm pr}^{[1]}{\textbf{g}}
, i.e.

\displaystyle\hskip-39.83368pt\frac{\partial}{\partial{\mathfrak{p}}}\mathsf{(F,H)}(\mathsf{t}_{\mathfrak{p}},{\rm pr}^{[1]}\widetilde{x}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathsf{t}_{\mathfrak{p}}),\widetilde{u}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathsf{t}_{\mathfrak{p}}),\widetilde{y}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathsf{t}_{\mathfrak{p}}))\equiv\frac{\partial}{\partial{\mathfrak{p}}}\widetilde{\Sigma}(\mathsf{t}_{\mathfrak{p}})\equiv 0.

(17)

In particular, by setting
{\mathfrak{p}}=0
in (
17
) and using the (prolonged) generator equation we get condition (
16
).

Condition (
15
) is an alternative (equivalent) characterization of a LS and, as it will be seen in Section
VI
, lends itself to interesting and useful generalizations and recalls in a general framework the notion of orbital symmetry of a vector field (or map): see
[
23
]
. The geometric rationale behind condition (
15
) is the following. By the prolongation formula (
4
) the vector field

F(t,\mathsf{x},\mathsf{d})
is mapped by the group of transformations into

\displaystyle\hskip-22.76228pt\Psi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathsf{x}^{(1)}}(t,\mathsf{x},F(t,\mathsf{x},\mathsf{d}))

\displaystyle\hskip-22.76228pt\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-3.28473pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-3.28473pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}(\frac{\partial\Psi^{t}_{\mathfrak{p}}(t)}{\partial t})^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial\Psi^{\mathsf{x}}_{\mathfrak{p}}(t,\mathsf{x})}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial\Psi^{\mathsf{x}}_{\mathfrak{p}}(t,\mathsf{x})}{\partial\mathsf{x}}F(t,\mathsf{x},\mathsf{d})\right)

while the function
H(t,\mathsf{x},\mathsf{d})
is mapped into
\Psi^{\mathsf{y}}_{\mathfrak{p}}(t,H(t,\mathsf{x},\mathsf{d}))
. Condition (
15
) amounts to

\displaystyle\hskip-22.76228pt\Psi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathsf{x}^{(1)}}(t,\mathsf{x},F(t,\mathsf{x},\mathsf{d}))\equiv F(\Psi^{t}_{\mathfrak{p}}(t),\Psi^{\mathsf{x}}_{\mathfrak{p}}(t,\mathsf{x}),\Psi^{\mathsf{d}}_{\mathfrak{p}}(t,\mathsf{x},\mathsf{d})),

\displaystyle\hskip-22.76228pt\Psi^{\mathsf{y}}_{\mathfrak{p}}(t,H(t,\mathsf{x},\mathsf{d}))\equiv H(\Psi^{t}_{\mathfrak{p}}(t),\Psi^{\mathsf{x}}_{\mathfrak{p}}(t,\mathsf{x}),\Psi^{\mathsf{d}}_{\mathfrak{p}}(t,\mathsf{x},\mathsf{d}))

(18)

i.e.
F
(resp.
H
) is mapped into itself or it is
invariant under
\Psi_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Hence, solutions of
\Sigma(t)=0
are mapped by the symmetry into other solutions. From this geometric perspective we recover the well-known notion of
homogeneity
of vector field and maps (see also
[
20
]
). Consider the system map
\Sigma
associated to the pair
(F(t,\mathsf{x},\mathsf{d}),H(t,\mathsf{x},\mathsf{d}))\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-3.28473pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-3.28473pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}(A(\mathsf{x}),C(\mathsf{x}))
, and a GGT of the form
\Psi_{\mathfrak{p}}(t,\mathsf{z})\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-3.28473pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-3.28473pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}(e^{{\mathfrak{p}}a^{t}}t,{\rm diag}_{i}\{e^{{\mathfrak{p}}(a^{\mathsf{x}_{i}}-a^{t})}\}\mathsf{x},\mathsf{d},{\rm diag}_{i}\{e^{{\mathfrak{p}}a^{\mathsf{y}_{i}}}\}\mathsf{y})
, where
a^{\mathsf{x}_{i}}<0
and
a^{\mathsf{y}_{j}},a^{t}\in{\mathbb{R}}
. The map
\Phi_{\mathfrak{p}}({\mathsf{x}})\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-3.28473pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-3.28473pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}{\rm diag}_{i}\{e^{-{\mathfrak{p}}a^{\mathsf{x}_{i}}}\}\mathsf{x}
is known as
one-parameter group of standard (linear) dilations
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
(
[
27
]
). On account of the geometric interpretation given above
\Psi_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a GS of
\Sigma(t)=0
if (and only if) the vector field
A
, resp. the mapping
C
, is
homogeneous with weights
(-a^{\mathsf{x}_{1}},\cdots,-a^{\mathsf{x}_{n}})
and degree
g^{t}
, resp. degrees
(-a^{\mathsf{y}_{1}},\cdots,-a^{\mathsf{y}_{p}})
: in formulas from (
18
) we have

{\rm diag}_{i}\{e^{{\mathfrak{p}}(a^{\mathsf{x}_{i}}-a^{t})}\}A(\mathsf{x})\equiv A({\rm diag}_{i}\{e^{{\mathfrak{p}}a^{\mathsf{x}_{i}}}\}\mathsf{x})
and
{\rm diag}_{i}\{e^{{\mathfrak{p}}a^{\mathsf{y}_{i}}}\}C(\mathsf{x})\equiv C({\rm diag}_{i}\{e^{{\mathfrak{p}}a^{\mathsf{x}_{i}}}\}\mathsf{x})
.
\hfill\blacktriangleleft

In what follows we give some illustrative and motivating examples together with direct procedures for computing symmetries.

Example IV.1

(Computing symmetries from PDE’s)
. A first way of computing a LS of a system
\Sigma(t)=0
is by solving the PDE resulting from (
16
) with unknown infinitesimal generator
g
. Consider the system map
\Sigma
associated to

\displaystyle(F,H)\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-3.28473pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-3.28473pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\left(\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf{x}_{2}\cr\mathsf{x}_{2}^{2}+\mathsf{d}\end{pmatrix},\mathsf{x}_{1}\right),

(19)

\mathsf{x}=(\mathsf{x}_{1},\mathsf{x}_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Assuming an infinitesimal generator
g
of the form
\textbf{g}|_{(t,\mathsf{z})}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-3.28473pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-3.28473pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}g^{t}(t)\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+g^{\mathsf{x}_{1}}(\mathsf{x}_{1})\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathsf{x}_{1}}+g^{\mathsf{x}_{2}}(\mathsf{x})\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathsf{x}_{2}}+g^{\mathsf{d}}(\mathsf{x},\mathsf{d})\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathsf{d}}+g^{\mathsf{y}}(\mathsf{y})\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathsf{y}}
and using the prolongation formula (
5
) for computing
{\rm pr}^{[1]}{\textbf{g}}
in (
14
), the symmetry condition (
16
) is

\displaystyle\hskip-22.76228ptg^{\mathsf{y}}|_{\mathsf{y}=\mathsf{x}_{1}}\equiv g^{\mathsf{x}_{1}},\left(\frac{\partial g^{\mathsf{x}_{1}}}{\partial\mathsf{x}_{1}}-\frac{\partial g^{t}}{\partial t}\right)\mathsf{x}_{2}\equiv g^{\mathsf{x}_{2}},

\displaystyle\hskip-22.76228pt\frac{\partial g^{\mathsf{x}_{2}}}{\partial\mathsf{x}_{1}}\mathsf{x}_{2}+\left(\frac{\partial g^{\mathsf{x}_{2}}}{\partial\mathsf{x}_{2}}-\frac{\partial g^{t}}{\partial t}\right)(\mathsf{x}_{2}^{2}+\mathsf{d})\equiv 2g^{\mathsf{x}_{2}}\mathsf{x}_{2}+g^{\mathsf{d}},

(20)

from which we get a solution for
g
as
\textbf{g}|_{(t,\mathsf{z})}=t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-(1+e^{\mathsf{x}_{1}})\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathsf{x}_{1}}-(1+e^{\mathsf{x}_{1}})\mathsf{x}_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathsf{x}_{2}}-(2+e^{\mathsf{x}_{1}}){\mathsf{d}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\mathsf{d}}}-(1+e^{\mathsf{y}})\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathsf{y}}
.
By solving the group generator equation (
3
) we obtain the local symmetry
\Psi_{\mathfrak{p}}({\mathsf{t}},{\mathsf{z}})=({\mathsf{t}}_{\mathfrak{p}},{\mathsf{x}}_{\mathfrak{p}},{\mathsf{d}}_{\mathfrak{p}},{\mathsf{y}}_{\mathfrak{p}})
where

\displaystyle\hskip-22.76228pt{\mathsf{t}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-3.28473pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-3.28473pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}e^{\mathfrak{p}}t,{\mathsf{x}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-3.28473pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-3.28473pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\left(\ln\frac{e^{-{\mathfrak{p}}+{\mathsf{x}}_{1}}}{1+e^{{\mathsf{x}}_{1}}-e^{-{\mathfrak{p}}+{\mathsf{x}}_{1}}},\frac{e^{-{\mathfrak{p}}}}{1+e^{{\mathsf{x}}_{1}}-e^{-{\mathfrak{p}}+{\mathsf{x}}_{1}}}{\mathsf{x}}_{2}\right),

\displaystyle\hskip-22.76228pt{\mathsf{d}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-3.28473pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-3.28473pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\frac{e^{-2{\mathfrak{p}}}}{1+e^{{\mathsf{x}}_{1}}-e^{-{\mathfrak{p}}+{\mathsf{x}}_{1}}}{\mathsf{d}},{\mathsf{y}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-3.28473pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-3.28473pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\ln\frac{e^{-{\mathfrak{p}}+{\mathsf{y}}}}{1+e^{{\mathsf{y}}}-e^{-{\mathfrak{p}}+{\mathsf{y}}}}.

(21)

Notice that
{\rm Dom}(\Psi_{\mathfrak{p}})={\mathcal{M}}
if
{\mathfrak{p}}>0
and
=\{(t,{\mathsf{z}})\in{\mathcal{M}}:{\mathsf{x}}_{1},{\mathsf{y}}<-\ln(e^{-\mathfrak{p}}-1)\}
if
{\mathfrak{p}}<0
. Moreover, since
\Psi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{t}(t)=e^{\mathfrak{p}}t
,
\Psi_{\mathfrak{p}}
is UU-transforming for
{\mathfrak{p}}>0
. A simpler alternative solution of (
20
) leads to
\textbf{g}|_{(t,\mathsf{z})}=t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\mathsf{x}_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathsf{x}_{2}}-2{\mathsf{d}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\mathsf{d}}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathsf{y}}
from which we get the global symmetry
\Psi_{\mathfrak{p}}({\mathsf{t}},{\mathsf{z}})=({\mathsf{t}}_{\mathfrak{p}},{\mathsf{x}}_{\mathfrak{p}},{\mathsf{d}}_{\mathfrak{p}},{\mathsf{y}}_{\mathfrak{p}})
with

\displaystyle\hskip-34.14322pt{\mathsf{t}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-3.28473pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-3.28473pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}e^{\mathfrak{p}}t,{\mathsf{x}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-3.28473pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-3.28473pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}({\mathsf{x}}_{1},e^{-{\mathfrak{p}}}{\mathsf{x}}_{2}),{\mathsf{d}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-3.28473pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-3.28473pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}e^{-2{\mathfrak{p}}}{\mathsf{d}},{\mathsf{y}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-3.28473pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-3.28473pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}{\mathsf{y}}.

(22)

Example IV.2

(Symmetries with nonlinear time transformations)
. Consider the system map
\Sigma
associated to the pair
(F,H)\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-3.28473pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-3.28473pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}(A\mathsf{x}+B\mathsf{d},C\mathsf{x}+D\mathsf{d})
and assume a candidate infinitesimal generator
g
of the form

\textbf{g}|_{(t,\mathsf{z})}=t^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(G^{\mathsf{x}}(t)\mathsf{x})_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathsf{x}_{j}}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}(G^{\mathsf{d}}(t)\mathsf{d})_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathsf{d}_{j}}+\sum_{j=1}^{p}(G^{\mathsf{y}}(t)\mathsf{y})_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathsf{y}_{j}}
, where
G^{\mathsf{x}}(t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
,
G^{\mathsf{d}}(t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times m}
and
G^{\mathsf{y}}(t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p\times p}

\textbf{g}^{t}|_{t}=t^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}

\Psi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{t}(t)=\frac{t}{1-{\mathfrak{p}}t}

{\rm Dom}(\Psi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{t})=\{t\in{\mathbb{R}}:t\neq\frac{1}{\mathfrak{p}}\}
,
{\rm Dom}^{+}\Psi^{t}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\frac{1}{\mathfrak{p}}>0
for
{\mathfrak{p}}>0
and
{\rm Dom}^{+}\Psi^{t}_{\mathfrak{p}}\searrow 0
for
{\mathfrak{p}}\to+\infty
, hence the LGT with infinitesimal generator
g
is CBU-transforming for
{\mathfrak{p}}>0
and acts locally on the time domain while globally on the space
{\mathcal{Z}}
of points
{\mathsf{z}}
. Using the prolongation formula (
5
) for computing
{\rm pr}^{[1]}{\textbf{g}}
in (
14
), the symmetry condition (
16
) for
\Sigma(t)=0
boils down to

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\frac{\partial G^{\mathsf{x}}}{\partial t}(t)\equiv AG^{\mathsf{x}}(t)-G^{\mathsf{x}}(t)A+2tA,

(23)

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt0\equiv G^{\mathsf{x}}(t)B-B(2t{\textbf{I}}_{m}+G^{\mathsf{d}}(t)),

(24)

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt0\equiv G^{\mathsf{y}}(t)C-CG^{\mathsf{x}}(t),0\equiv G^{\mathsf{y}}(t)D-DG^{\mathsf{d}}(t).

(25)

As well-known, the unique solution of the
differential Sylvester equation
(
23
) is
G^{\mathsf{x}}(t)=e^{At}G^{\mathsf{x}}(0)e^{-At}+At^{2}
,
G^{\mathsf{x}}(0)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
. The remaining (
24
)-(
25
) are (algebraic)
Sylvester equations
.
Once the matrices
G^{\mathsf{x}}(t)
,
G^{\mathsf{d}}(t)
and
G^{\mathsf{y}}(t)
satisfying (
23
)-(
25
) are found, we solve the group generator equation (
3
) and get the LS of
\Sigma(t)=0
in the form

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\Psi_{\mathfrak{p}}(t,\mathsf{z})\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-3.28473pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-3.28473pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}(\frac{t}{1-{\mathfrak{p}}t},\Phi^{\mathsf{x}}({\mathfrak{p}},t)\mathsf{x},\Phi^{\mathsf{d}}({\mathfrak{p}},t)\mathsf{d},\Phi^{\mathsf{y}}({\mathfrak{p}},t)\mathsf{y}),

(26)

where
\Phi^{\mathsf{x}}({\mathfrak{p}},t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
,
\Phi^{\mathsf{d}}({\mathfrak{p}},t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times m}
and
\Phi^{\mathsf{y}}({\mathfrak{p}},t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p\times p}
.
For instance, if

\displaystyle(F,H)\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-3.28473pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-3.28473pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\left(\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf{x}_{2}\cr\mathsf{d}\end{pmatrix},\mathsf{x}_{1}\right),\;\mathsf{x}=(\mathsf{x}_{1},\mathsf{x}_{2}),

(27)

as solution of (
23
)-(
25
) we get

G^{\mathsf{x}}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}t&0\cr 1&-t\end{pmatrix},G^{\mathsf{d}}(t)=-3t,G^{\mathsf{y}}(t)=t.

From these matrices, we compute the matrices
\Phi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathsf{x}}(t,{\mathsf{x}})
,
\Phi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathsf{d}}(t,{\mathsf{u}})
and
\Phi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathsf{y}}(t,\mathsf{y})
in (
26
) resulting in the local symmetry

\displaystyle\hskip-22.76228pt\Psi_{\mathfrak{p}}(t,\mathsf{z})\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-3.28473pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-3.28473pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\left(\frac{t}{1-{\mathfrak{p}}t},\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{1-{\mathfrak{p}}t}&0\cr{\mathfrak{p}}&1-{\mathfrak{p}}t\end{pmatrix}\mathsf{x},(1-{\mathfrak{p}}t)^{3}\mathsf{d},\frac{\mathsf{y}}{1-{\mathfrak{p}}t}\right)

(28)

Example IV.3

(Triangular systems maps)
. Symmetries can be computed as well by exploiting the system’s structure. Consider the
lower triangular
system map
\Sigma
associated to

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866pt(F(t,\mathsf{x},\mathsf{d}),H(t,\mathsf{x},\mathsf{d}))\!=\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}(t,\mathsf{x}_{1})+B_{1}(t,\mathsf{x}_{1})\mathsf{x}_{2}\cr A_{2}(t,\mathsf{x})+B_{2}(t,\mathsf{x})\mathsf{d}\end{pmatrix}\!,\mathsf{x}_{1}\!\right)

(29)

with
\mathsf{x}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-3.28473pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-3.28473pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}(\mathsf{x}_{1},\mathsf{x}_{2})

\mathsf{x}_{1},\mathsf{x}_{2},\mathsf{d}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

\Sigma(t)=0

5

\Sigma(t)=0

\Sigma(t)=0

5

16

{\textbf{g}}_{1}
(on the

(t,{\mathsf{z}})

\Sigma(t)=0

\mathsf{d}_{1}=\mathsf{x}_{2}

\Sigma_{1}(t)=0
must satisfy on account of (

\displaystyle\hskip-22.76228ptg^{\mathsf{y}_{1}}|_{\mathsf{y}_{1}=\mathsf{x}_{1}}\equiv g^{\mathsf{x}_{1}},\;g^{\mathsf{d}_{1}}\equiv B_{1}^{-1}\Big[\left(\frac{\partial g^{\mathsf{x}_{1}}}{\partial\mathsf{x}_{1}}-\frac{\partial g^{t}}{\partial t}\right)(A_{1}+B_{1}\mathsf{d}_{1})

{\textbf{g}}|_{(t,\mathsf{x},\mathsf{d},\mathsf{y})}

\Sigma(t)=0

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt{\textbf{g}}|_{(t,\mathsf{z})}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-3.28473pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-3.28473pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}{\textbf{g}}_{1}|_{(t,\mathsf{x}_{1},\mathsf{x}_{2},\mathsf{y})}+g^{\mathsf{d}}(t,\mathsf{x},\mathsf{d})\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathsf{d}}=g^{t}(t)\frac{\partial}{\partial t}

\Sigma(t)=0
.

\Sigma

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt(F,H)\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-3.28473pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-3.28473pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\left(\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf{x}_{2}\cr\mathsf{x}_{2}\mathsf{x}_{1}^{k}+(1+\mathsf{x}_{1}^{2})\mathsf{d}\end{pmatrix},\mathsf{x}_{1}\right),

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt{\textbf{g}}|_{(t,\mathsf{z})}=kt\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\mathsf{x}_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathsf{x}_{1}}-(1+k)\mathsf{x}_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathsf{x}_{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt+\left(-(1+2k)+\frac{2\mathsf{x}_{1}^{2}}{1+\mathsf{x}_{1}^{2}}\right)\mathsf{d}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathsf{d}}-\mathsf{y}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathsf{y}}

IV-C

Contracting symmetries

The symmetries are one-parameter groups of transformations mapping solutions into other (parameterized) solutions. A useful property of a symmetry, which opens completely new perspectives in control design, is its capability of contracting solutions into other solutions of arbitrarily small magnitude, where the magnitude is parameterized by the group parameter itself. This motivates the following couple of related definitions.

VIII
Conclusions

Different types of symmetries have been introduced for ODE systems with motivating and illustrative examples. Based on these symmetries, we have designed a variety of semiglobal and global observers, in some cases recovering the same performances of semiglobal classical HGOs and in other cases obtaining novel global observers where existing design techniques cannot provide any.

Sentinel serosurvey for Mpox virus infection among key populations in India: Evidence of sporadic past exposure beyond travel-related cases

ABSTRACT
Background and objectivesMpox, declared a public health emergency of international concern by WHO, has demonstrated sporadic transmission globally. While India reported 66 confirmed cases predominantly linked to international travel, the possibility of silent community circulation remains unconfirmed, particularly within high-risk populations. Serological surveys are crucial for detecting past exposure, including asymptomatic infections that may be missed by clinical surveillance. This study aimed to assess the prevalence of anti-MPXV IgG and IgM antibodies and identify socio-demographic and behavioural determinants of seropositivity among key populations in India.MethodsA multicentric, cross-sectional sentinel serosurvey was conducted between September 2023 to March 2024 across seven community-based organisation sites in India. A total of 1,790 participants aged 18-40 y (born after cessation of smallpox vaccination in India) were enrolled. Serological testing was performed using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) for anti-MPXV IgG and IgM antibodies. Plaque reduction neutralisation test (PRNT50) was used to assess functional neutralising antibodies against both MPXV and Vaccinia virus.ResultsAmong 1,790 participants, 14 (0.78%) tested positive for anti-MPXV IgG, with no IgM positivity detected. IgG-positive individuals were predominantly male (57.1%), aged 31-40 y (85.7%), and sexually active (75.9%). Geographic distribution ranged from 0.34% (Delhi NCR) to 2.07% (Kerala). PRNT50 assay revealed functional neutralising antibodies against MPXV in 64.3% (9/14) of IgG-positive samples, with evidence of cross-reactive immunity to Vaccinia virus in 93.9% (13/14).Interpretation and conclusionsThis multicentric sentinel serosurvey identified very low Mpox virus seroprevalence (0.78%) among key populations in India, suggesting limited prior exposure. The absence of IgM antibodies indicates that infections were not recent and provides no evidence of ongoing community transmission during the study period. Detection of neutralising antibodies in a subset of IgG-positive individuals supports prior Orthopoxvirus exposure. These findings establish baseline sero-epidemiological evidence for Mpox in India and underscore the importance of periodic sentinel sero surveillance for early detection and outbreak preparedness.

Mpox, caused by the Mpox virus (MPXV), remains a significant global public health concern. Following the rapid international spread of the virus, the World Health Organization (WHO) declared Mpox a public health emergency of international concern (PHEIC) in July 2022 and reinstated the declaration in August 2024 due to outbreaks of Clade I and Clade II lineages in Africa.1,2

In India, 66 confirmed cases have been reported since the first detection in July 2022,3 with most linked to international travel.4,5 Although genomic sequencing identified the Clade IIb-A.2 lineage, sustained community transmission has not been confirmed.6 However, the possibility of undetected circulation within high-contact networks remains a concern.

Serological studies provide important insights into infectious disease epidemiology by detecting antibodies that persist after acute infection7. Detection of anti-MPXV IgG antibodies indicates prior exposure irrespective of clinical symptoms, whereas IgM antibodies may suggest recent or active infection.8 Since many Mpox infections during recent outbreaks have been mild, atypical, or asymptomatic,9,10 reliance solely on clinical or virological surveillance may underestimate the true burden of infection.

Globally, serosurveys have demonstrated low baseline Orthopoxvirus seroprevalence in non-endemic settings, particularly among individuals born after cessation of routine smallpox vaccination.11 As smallpox vaccination was discontinued in India in 1980,12 a substantial proportion of the current population is likely susceptible to Orthopoxvirus infections.

In India, most available Mpox data are derived from laboratory-confirmed symptomatic cases detected through point-of-entry screening or clinical referrals.13 Evidence regarding prior community exposure remains limited, particularly among key populations such as men who have sex with men (MSM) and transgender persons, who may be more vulnerable because of interconnected social networks, higher prevalence of sexually transmitted infections, and barriers to healthcare access.14,15

Considering the limited sero-epidemiological data on Mpox exposure in India, a multicentric serosurvey was conducted through community-based organisations serving key populations. The study aimed to assess the seropositivity of anti-MPXV IgG and IgM antibodies and describe the socio-demographic and behavioural characteristics associated with seropositivity.

Methods

This multicentre cross-sectional study was jointly undertaken by ICMR-Hq, New Delhi, ICMR-NITVAR, Pune and ICMR-NIV, Pune. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of the ICMR- National Institute of Translational Virology and AIDS Research, Pune and ICMR- National Institute of Virology, Pune and by local institutional review boards at each participating site (CBOs and VRDLs). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrolment. All data were stored securely, and participant confidentiality was strictly maintained. Participants in need of specialised healthcare were referred for further clinical management.

Study design and setting

A multicentric, cross-sectional facility-based sentinel serosurvey was conducted between September 2023 to March 2024 across seven sites to ensure diverse geographical representation across India. This surveillance was carried out through seven established community-based organisations (CBOs) that run sexual health clinics and provide counselling services for key populations such as men who have sex with men and transgender individuals, which were leveraged to facilitate the serosurvey among these key populations.

Study population and eligibility criteria

The study population comprised individuals aged 18 to 40 y who were registered beneficiaries of the participating CBOs across India. This specific age range was selected because it represents the cohort born after the cessation of routine smallpox vaccination in India. To further ensure the selection of individuals unlikely to have received smallpox vaccination, participants were examined for the presence of a smallpox vaccination scar, and individuals bearing such a scar were excluded from the study. This could have led to the introduction of a misclassification bias. As a result, the study population largely represents individuals without prior smallpox vaccination, who are presumed to have limited cross-protective immunity against Orthopoxvirus infections, an important consideration for estimating baseline susceptibility to Mpox.12,16 Participants were recruited using a consecutive sampling approach at clinic and outreach sites.

Sample size determination

Since the baseline seroprevalence of Mpox in the target key population was unknown, a conservative estimate of 50% prevalence was used to ensure the sample size would be sufficiently large. To achieve a 95% confidence interval (CI) and an absolute precision of 2.5%, the calculated sample size was 1,537 individuals. Accounting for an anticipated 10% refusal rate, the total target sample size across all sites was set at 1,690 participants.

Eligible participants were enrolled between October 2023 to February 2024 using both camp-based and clinic-based approaches at seven different locations. All the eligible individuals who provided written informed consent were included, resulting in a final sample size of 1,790 participants. Higher enrolment was achieved due to increased participation across sites in the camp-based approach.

Participant recruitment and data collection

Initially, the staff were trained for identification of signs and symptoms, followed by pictorial flyers detailing the signs, symptoms, and preventive measures for Mpox, which were prominently displayed at the entrance of the participating CBOs and throughout the temporary health camps to disseminate information regarding the disease and the study. All clinic and camp attendees were given a comprehensive briefing about the study’s objectives and procedures. Eligible individuals who expressed willingness to participate were subsequently enrolled following the provision of written informed consent.

Data were systematically collected using a structured, paper-based questionnaire. This instrument was rigorously adapted from the standard World Health Organization (WHO) questionnaire.11,17 The questionnaire was translated into relevant local languages and administered by trained study team members to maximise comprehension and accurate reporting by the participants. The survey was designed to capture extensive data across several critical domains, including sociodemographic characteristics, travel history, clinical history, co-morbidities, detailed sexual behaviour, symptoms suggestive of Mpox, and social history.

Specimen collection and laboratory testing

For each participant, 5 mL of venous blood was collected into serum separator tubes. Site laboratories centrifuged the samples, and serum aliquots were stored at -20°C until they were transported. The cold-chain transport to the central reference laboratory used temperature-monitored insulated carriers.

Detection of IgG antibodies against the Mpox virus

Serological testing for anti-Mpox virus (MPXV) IgG antibodies was performed using a validated in-house enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) developed at the ICMR-National Institute of Virology (NIV), Pune. The assay utilised MPXV antigens, with appropriate positive and negative controls included in each run. The process involved identification of an optimum antigen concentration, serum dilution, and cut-off points from ROC curve analysis. Optical density values exceeding the predefined cut-off were interpreted as seropositive according to established laboratory standards. IgG detection was considered indicative of prior exposure.18

However, due to the conserved antigenic structure among Orthopoxviruses, particularly shared surface proteins and epitopes, there will be serological cross-reactivity to some extent. Antibodies elicited by exposure to related viruses such as Vaccinia virus or other Orthopoxviruses will react with antigens from MPXV, thus overestimating MPXV exposure. This limitation is inherent to binding antibody assays targeting Orthopoxvirus antigens and necessitates cautious interpretation of ELISA-based seroprevalence estimates.

To further assess the presence of functional neutralising antibodies, plaque reduction neutralisation tests (PRNT50) were performed on all ELISA-positive samples against both Mpox virus and Vaccinia virus.

Plaque reduction neutralisation test (PRNT50)

PRNT₅₀ assay was performed using well-defined laboratory strains of Clade II Mpox virus (MPXV) lineage and Vaccinia virus at ICMR-NIV, Pune. Heat inactivated serum was serially diluted to 1:10 with MEM and equal volume of the diluted sera was added to viral stock solutions (containing 50-60 PFU in 0.1 mL) followed by incubation at 37°C for one hour.

Each serum-virus mixture (0.1mL) was inoculated onto confluent monolayers of Vero CCL-81 cells in 24-well plates. After adsorption for 60 min, an overlay medium containing 2% carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC) and 2% fetal calf serum (FCS) prepared in 2× MEM was added, and plates were incubated at 37 °C in 5% CO2 for five days. Following incubation, cell monolayers were stained with 1% amido black to visualise plaques. To ensure assay validity, each run included positive control sera with known neutralising activity and negative control sera from unexposed individual.

An ROC curve analysis was conducted to define the optimal cut-off value of PRNT₅₀ from a validation set that consisted of 20 true positives and 49 true negatives ( Figure). The test showed good discriminatory power based on an area under the ROC curve (AUC) value of 0.96 (P=0.003). The optimal cut-off value was derived by considering two approaches: the Youden Index and optimisation using cost matrix.19 It resulted into the ideal cut-off value would be 1:20 because it provided optimal diagnostic performance with minimal total error rate. At this cut-off point, the test achieved 100% sensitivity and 94% specificity. Additionally, this cut-off is justified in light of existing standards used in the interpretation of orthopoxvirus serology, whereby low titre values (such as 1:10) represent non-specific inhibition or background effects while those of ≥1:20 are usually indicative of biologically relevant neutralisation.

Data management and statistical analysis

Unique participant identifiers were used to anonymise the data, which were then input into the Ona data management system ( https://ona.io ) through web forms and a mobile application. By routinely checking for missing or out-of-range entries and swiftly resolving any inconsistencies, data quality was assured. SPSS version 28 (IBM Corp, New York, USA) and R software (4.4.0) was used to conduct the analysis.

Sociodemographic and behavioural characteristics were summarised using frequencies and percentages. As several contingency tables contained expected cell counts less than 5, associations between IgG seropositivity and sociodemographic variables were assessed using Fisher’s exact test. As multiple sociodemographic variables were evaluated for association with IgG seropositivity, Bonferroni correction was applied to reduce the risk of type I error due to multiple comparisons.

The outcome of interest was rare (seropositivity: 0.78%), resulting in a limited number of events for regression analysis. Consequently, logistic regression models produced unstable odds ratio estimates with wide confidence intervals and unreliable P values; therefore, regression-based inferences are not conducted in this study.

Participants were recruited through community-based organisations providing healthcare and outreach services to these populations.

All enrolled participants underwent serological screening for anti-MPXV IgM and IgG antibodies. Among the study population, 14 participants (0.78%) were found positive for anti-MPXV IgG antibodies, while none tested positive for IgM antibodies, indicating the absence of recent or ongoing infections at the time of data collection. Detection of IgG antibodies does not necessarily indicate protective immunity.

Demographic profile of IgG-positive participants

Sociodemographic characteristics of serosurvey participants and those with IgG positivity are shown in Table I. Overall, the IgG-positive participants were predominantly adults in the 31-40 y age group, residing in urban settings, and engaged in regular employment.

Behavioural profile

Behavioural data indicated that 11 (78.6%) of IgG-positive participants were sexually active within 21 d preceding sample collection. Among IgG-seropositive participants who responded to the question, 70% (7/10) reported having 2-5 sexual partners in the preceding month. None reported recent contact with suspected Mpox cases, international travel, or animal exposure within the prior 21 d.

Geographic distribution

IgG seropositivity was identified in participants from Delhi NCR, Kerala, Telangana, Punjab, and Mizoram ( Table II). No IgG-positive samples were detected among participants from Maharashtra, West Bengal, or Nagaland. Overall, 14 of 1,790 participants tested positive for anti-Mpox IgG antibodies, corresponding to an overall seroprevalence of 0.78%. No IgM-positive samples were identified across study sites.

Five samples showed no detectable neutralisation against Mpox despite being ELISA IgG positive, suggesting possible waning immunity or low-level non-neutralising binding antibodies; whereas 13/14 demonstrated neutralisation against Vaccinia virus with titres ranging from 34.57 to 171.7.

Two samples were positive for Vaccinia neutralisation but negative for Mpox neutralisation, suggesting residual or cross-reactive immunity to other Orthopoxviruses.

Overall, 9 participants (64.3%) while 4 had neutralisation restricted to Vaccinia alone. One participant (7.1%) showed binding IgG seropositivity but lacked neutralising activity against either virus.

These results indicate that while IgG ELISA detected binding antibodies in all cases, PRNT50 identified functional neutralising antibodies in a subset, highlighting the added value of combining serological and neutralisation assays to assess true protective immunity.

Discussion

This multicentric sentinel serosurvey provides baseline evidence of low-level Mpox virus (MPXV) exposure among key populations in India, particularly men who have sex with men (MSM) and transgender persons, accessing community-based health services. The overall seroprevalence of anti-MPXV IgG antibodies was very low, and no participant demonstrated IgM seropositivity, suggesting absence of recent or active infection during the study period. Together, these findings indicate sporadic prior exposure rather than sustained community transmission. The detection of neutralising antibodies in a subset of ELISA IgG-positive individuals further supports prior Orthopoxvirus exposure, including possible asymptomatic or subclinical infections that may not have been identified through routine clinical surveillance. These observations are important in the Indian context, where surveillance has largely relied on traveller screening and symptom-based case detection, potentially missing mild or silent infections.20-23

Serosurveys from endemic regions of Central and West Africa have reported substantially higher Orthopoxvirus seroprevalence because of recurrent zoonotic exposure and ongoing community transmission. In contrast, studies from non-endemic countries, including MSM cohorts in Europe and Asia, have generally shown low baseline seropositivity among populations born after discontinuation of smallpox vaccination.20-25 The findings of the present study are consistent with these observations and suggest that MPXV exposure in India remains limited and sporadic. Although MSM and transgender populations may remain vulnerable because of interconnected social and sexual networks and associated healthcare disparities, the absence of IgM positivity and the very low overall seroprevalence provide no evidence of widespread ongoing community circulation during the study period.21,23,25,26

An important strength of the present study was the combined use of ELISA and plaque reduction neutralisation test (PRNT₅₀) assays to evaluate both binding and functional antibody responses. Orthopoxviruses such as MPXV and Vaccinia virus share conserved antigenic epitopes, resulting in potential serological cross-reactivity. While ELISA-based IgG detection serves as a useful marker of prior exposure, not all binding antibodies confer protective or neutralising immunity. Consistent with global studies, discordance was observed between ELISA and PRNT findings, with some ELISA-positive samples lacking detectable neutralising antibodies against MPXV.27,28 In the present study, several participants demonstrated neutralisation against Vaccinia virus but not MPXV, which may reflect prior exposure to related Orthopoxviruses, cross-reactive immunity, or waning antibody responses. Similar findings have been documented in studies from Africa and Asia evaluating Orthopoxvirus serology.20,24,27

All participants included in this study were born after cessation of routine smallpox vaccination in India in 1980, and individuals with visible smallpox vaccination scars were excluded to minimise vaccine-related seropositivity. Therefore, the antibody responses observed are less likely to represent residual vaccine-induced immunity and may instead reflect natural exposure to MPXV or antigenically related Orthopoxviruses. Nevertheless, interpretation of neutralisation assays requires caution because PRNT₅₀ may not completely distinguish virus-specific neutralisation from cross-neutralising responses among closely related Orthopoxviruses. Consequently, some degree of overestimation of true MPXV exposure cannot be excluded despite the use of confirmatory neutralisation assays. Furthermore, while PRNT reflects functional neutralising activity, it does not necessarily imply sterilising or long-term protective immunity.

The present study also highlights the operational feasibility of integrating Mpox serosurveillance into existing community-based HIV/STI service platforms and laboratory surveillance networks in India. Use of community-based organisations (CBOs) enabled access to key populations that are often underserved and difficult to reach through conventional surveillance mechanisms. Similar decentralised surveillance models implemented in Africa and Southeast Asia have strengthened outbreak preparedness through improved detection, rapid risk communication, and enhanced community engagement.21,29-31 In India, the existing virus research and diagnostic laboratory (VRDL) network could provide an important framework for periodic sentinel serosurveillance and early identification of emerging zoonotic threats among vulnerable populations.

Our findings should be interpreted in light of several limitations. First, the facility-based sampling approach through CBO-run clinics may have introduced selection bias toward individuals already engaged with healthcare and outreach services, thereby limiting generalisability to the broader MSM and transgender populations in India. Second, the cross-sectional design limits temporal inference and prevents determination of the timing of exposure or causal relationships between behavioural risk factors and seropositivity. Third, the limited number of IgG-seropositive participants restricted detailed analytical assessment of behavioural and demographic correlates. Finally, possible cross-reactivity of ELISA assays with other Orthopoxviruses may have resulted in overestimation of true MPXV exposure, although PRNT₅₀ assays were used to partially address this limitation.

Overall, this multicentric serosurvey demonstrates very low-level anti-MPXV seropositivity among key populations in India and suggests sporadic prior exposure without evidence of active community transmission during the study period. These findings provide important baseline epidemiological evidence for future monitoring of Mpox in India. From a public health perspective, the study underscores the need for continued targeted surveillance among high-risk populations, particularly MSM and transgender persons, alongside strengthened risk communication and community engagement strategies. Integration of Mpox surveillance within existing national systems such as the integrated disease surveillance programme (IDSP) and the VRDL network may facilitate early detection and timely outbreak response. Periodic sentinel serosurveys and strengthened laboratory capacity will remain important for monitoring changes in exposure patterns and informing future vaccination and preparedness strategies in the context of evolving global Mpox epidemiology.

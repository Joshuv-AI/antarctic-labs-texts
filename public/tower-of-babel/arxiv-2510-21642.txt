New classes of compact-type spaces
Saak Gabriyelyan, Evgenii Reznichenko

Abstract
Being motivated by the notions of κ \kappa -Fréchet–Urysohn spaces and k ′ k^{\prime} -spaces introduced by Arhangel’skii, the notion of sequential spaces and the study of Ascoli spaces, we introduce three new classes of compact-type spaces. They are defined by the possibility to attain each or some of boundary points x x of an open set U U by a sequence in U U converging to x x or by a relatively compact subset A ⊆ U A\subseteq U such that x ∈ A ¯ x\in\overline{A} . Relationships of the introduced classes with the classical classes (as, for example, the classes of κ \kappa -Fréchet–Urysohn spaces, (sequentially) Ascoli spaces, k ℝ k_{\mathbb{R}} -spaces, s ℝ s_{\mathbb{R}} -spaces etc.) are given. We characterize these new classes of spaces and study them with respect to taking products, subspaces and quotients. In particular, we give new characterizations of κ \kappa -Fréchet–Urysohn spaces and show that each feathered topological group is κ \kappa -Fréchet–Urysohn. We describe locally compact abelian groups which endowed with the Bohr topology belong to one of the aforementioned classes. Numerous examples are given.

Proposition 2.1

Let

be a space such that each compact subset of

is Fréchet–Urysohn (for example,

(i)

X
is open-compact attainable if, and only if, it is
\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohn;

(ii)

is weakly open-compact attainable if, and only if, it is
\kappa
-sequential.

Proof 1

(i) Assume that

is open-compact attainable. To show that

is
\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohn, let

be an open subset of
X
and

. If
x\in U
, then the constant sequence
\{x\}\subseteq U
converges to

. Assume that

. Since

is open-compact attainable, there exists

such that

is compact and

. By assumption

is Fréchet–Urysohn, and hence there is a sequence

in

, we also have

. Thus

is a
\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohn space.

(ii) Assume that
X
is weakly open-compact attainable. Let

be a non-closed open subset of

. Then there are a point

and a compact subset

of

such that

is
\kappa
-sequential.∎

Proposition 2.13

Let

be a strongly zero-dimensional space. If

is weakly open-compact attainable, then

is a sequentially Ascoli space.

Proof 9

Let
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\omega}

is sequentially Ascoli, by Theorem 2.7 of
[
12

is locally finite.

Let

for every

Since

[

Let us show that even the sequence

.

is weakly open-compact attainable, there are a point

and a compact subset

of

such that

. Set

. If

The proof is separated onto the following three claims.

Claim 1. The space

is pseudocompact. Indeed, let us remark first that each open nonempty set

contains an open set of the form

We show that the sequence

\Rightarrow

(i) Let
W\subseteq X

be such that

. Assuming the converse we obtain that

, where

. Therefore,

. Let

(ii)
\Rightarrow

. Then

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii) We show that

Definition 4.2

Let

and

-pseudo open map if an open set

It will be convenient to use the following characterizations of weakly

-pseudo open maps.

Lemma 4.3

Let

and

is a weakly
\kappa
-pseudo open map if, and only if, for every open non-closed set

Consequently, each
\kappa
-pseudo open map is a weakly
\kappa
-pseudo open map.

Proof 23

(\Rightarrow)
Assume that

is a weakly

-pseudo open map, and let

be an open non-closed set. Then the open set

is also not closed. Take

(\Leftarrow)
Let

be open. If

is closed, then clearly

is also closed. Conversely, assume that

is closed and suppose for a contradiction that

The last assertion follows from Lemma

.∎

6
Locally compact groups and locally compact abelian groups with the Bohr topology

We start this section with the following general theorem. Recall that a topological group
G
is called

if it contains a nonempty compact set
K
of countable character in
G
.

Theorem 6.1

Each feathered group
G
is
\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohn.

Proof 32

Let
W\subseteq G
be open and
g\in\overline{W}\setminus W
. We have to prove that there exists a sequence
(g_{n})_{n\in\omega}\subseteq W
converging to
g
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a family of compact subgroups of

of countable character in
G
. Set

\mathcal{B}=\{UH:U\subseteq G\mbox{ is open in }G\mbox{ and }H\in\mathcal{H}\}.

By definition, if
U\in\mathcal{B}
, then there is
H_{U}\in\mathcal{H}
such that

.

Claim 1. The family
\mathcal{B}
satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

if
x\in G
and

, then
xU\in\mathcal{B}
;

(ii)

\mathcal{B}
is a base for

(iii)

if

(iv)

.

Indeed, if
U=UH
for some
H\in\mathcal{H}
, then
xU=(xU)H\in\mathcal{B}
that proves (i).

(ii) is Corollary 4.3.12 of
[
4
]
. Let us give a simple direct proof. By (i), it suffices to verify that the family
\mathcal{B}_{0}=\{U\in\mathcal{B}:e\in U\}
is a base at the identity
e
. Let
S
be a neighborhood of
e
. Then
V^{2}\subseteq S
for some neighborhood
V
of

. Proposition 4.3.11 of
[
4
]
, there exists
H\in\mathcal{H}
such that

. Then
VH\in\mathcal{B}_{0}

VH\subseteq V^{2}\subseteq S
.

To prove (iii), choose

and

. Set

and
(U\cap V)H=U\cap V
.

For every

such that
U_{n}H_{n}=U_{n}

for every

. Therefore,
\big(\bigcup_{n\in\omega}U_{n}\big)H=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}U_{n}
. This proves (iv), and hence Claim 1 is proved.

The next claim means that we can replace

by some

.

Claim 2. There exists
U\in\mathcal{B}
such that
g\in\overline{U}
and
U\subseteq W
.

Indeed, set
\gamma:=\{V\in\mathcal{B}:V\subseteq W\}

is a base of

\bigcup\gamma=W
.
Since
G
is feathered, the

is countable
[
4

. Therefore,
g\in\overline{\bigcup\mu}
for some at most countable

. Let
U=\bigcup\mu
. Then, by (iv) of Claim 1, we have

. Since
g\in\overline{U}
and

, the set
U
is as desired. Claim 2 is proved.

Fix

which satisfies Claim 2. Take

such that

. Let

be a left quotient space.
Since

is a compact subgroup, the quotient mapping

[
4
, Theorem 1.5.7]
and open
[
4

. Set

. As

[

\tau_{b}
induced from the Bohr compactification
bG
of
G
is denoted by
G^{+}
. Numerous topological and algebraic-topological properties of the precompact group
G^{+}
are well-studied, we refer the reader to Chapter 9 of
[
4
]
and references therein. The next result lies in this line of researches.

Theorem 6.3

For a locally compact abelian group
G
, the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

G^{+}
is a
\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohn space;

(ii)

G^{+}
is an open-compact attainable space;

(iii)

G^{+}
is an Ascoli space;

(iv)

G^{+}
is a sequentially Ascoli space;

(v)

G
is a compact space;

(vi)

G^{+}
is a
k^{\prime}
-space.

Proof 33

The implications (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii)
\Rightarrow
(iv) and (v)
\Rightarrow
(vi)
\Rightarrow
(ii) are clear. The implication (iv)
\Rightarrow
(v) is proved in Theorem 3.1 of
[
12
]
, and the implication (v)
\Rightarrow
(i) follows from Corollary
6.2
and the fact that
G^{+}=G
for any compact abelian group.∎

Corollary 6.4

Let
G
be a locally compact abelian group. Then
G^{+}
is a Fréchet–Urysohn space if, and only if,
G
is a compact Fréchet–Urysohn space.

To consider the property of being
\kappa
-sequential or weakly open-compact attainable for locally compact abelian groups with the Bohr topology we use the following assertion.

Theorem 6.5

Let
X
and
Y
be
\kappa
-sequential
(
resp., weakly open-compact attainable
)
spaces. If
X
is connected, then
X\times Y
is a
\kappa
-sequential
(
resp., weakly open-compact attainable
)
space.

Proof 34

Suppose for a contradiction that there is an open non-closed set
W\subseteq X\times Y
such that for every
z\in\overline{W}{\setminus}W
there is no a sequence (resp., a relatively compact in
X\times Y
subset)
A\subseteq W
which converges to
z
(resp.,
z\in\overline{A}
).

We claim that there is an open
U\subseteq Y
such that
W=X\times U
. Indeed, otherwise, there would exist a point
(x^{\prime},y_{\ast})\in W
such that the open nonempty subset
V:=\{x\in X:(x,y_{\ast})\in W\}
of
X
is not equal to
X
. We show that
V
is also closed. To this end, assuming for a contradiction and taking into account that
X
is
\kappa
-sequential (resp., weakly open-compact attainable), we could find a point
x_{\ast}\in\overline{V}{\setminus}V
and a sequence (resp., a relatively compact in
X
subset)
S\subseteq V
such that
x_{\ast}\in\overline{S}
. Then the point
z=(x_{\ast},y_{\ast})
belongs to
\overline{W}{\setminus}W
and the sequence (resp., the relatively compact in
X\times Y
subset)
A:=(S,y_{\ast})\subseteq W
converges to
z
(resp.,
z\in\overline{A}
). But this contradicts our supposition on
W
. Therefore
V
must be also closed. Since
X
is connected, it follows that the clopen nonempty subset
V
of
X
must coincide with
X
. This contradiction shows that
W=X\times U
for some open subset
U
of
Y
.

Since
W
is not closed, we obtain that also
U
is not closed. Since
Y
is
\kappa
-sequential (resp., weakly open-compact attainable), there are a point
y_{0}\in\overline{U}{\setminus}U
and a sequence (resp., a relatively compact in
Y
subset)
S_{0}\subseteq U
such that
y_{0}\in\overline{S_{0}}
. Then for every
x\in X
, the point
z_{0}=(x,y_{0})
belongs to
\overline{W}{\setminus}W
and the sequence (resp., the relatively compact subset)
A_{0}:=(x,S_{0})\subseteq W
converges to
z_{0}
(resp.,
z_{0}\in\overline{A_{0}}
). However this contradicts our assumption on
W
.∎

We finish this section with the following theorem.

Theorem 6.8

For a locally compact abelian group
G
, the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

G^{+}
is a
\kappa
-sequential space;

(ii)

G^{+}
is a weakly open-compact attainable space;

(iii)

G
is topologically isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times H
, where
n\in\omega
and
H
is a compact group.

Proof 35

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) is clear.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii) Assume that
G^{+}
is weakly open-compact attainable. By the structural Theorem 24.30 of
[
20
]
,
G
is topologically isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times H
, where
n\in\omega
and
H
has an open compact subgroup
K
. Denote by
S
the discrete group
G/(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times K)
. By Problem 9.9.N of
[
4
]
, we have
S^{+}=G^{+}/(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times K)^{+}
. Hence, by Corollary
4.8
, the precompact group
S^{+}
is weakly open-compact attainable. By Theorem 9.9.30 of
[
4
]
,
S^{+}
has no infinite compact sets. The last two facts imply that
S
must be finite. Therefore
H
is a compact group.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i) Let
G
be topologically isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times H
, where
n\in\omega
and
H
is a compact group. Then, by Problem 9.9.N of
[
4
]
,
G^{+}=(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{+}\times H
. By Corollary
6.2
,
H
is a
\kappa
-sequential space. It is clear that

is pass-connected, and hence, by Proposition
2.9
,
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{+}
is
\kappa
-sequential. Thus, by Theorem

,
G^{+}
is a
\kappa
-sequential space.∎

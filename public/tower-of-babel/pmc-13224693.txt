The complete chloroplast genome sequence and phylogenetic analysis of Hydrocleys nymphoides (Alismataceae), an aquatic ornamental plant
Authors: Xiuli Lu, Wei Ming
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe complete chloroplast (cp) genome of Hydrocleys nymphoides, an aquatic ornamental plant, was sequenced and assembled in this study. The circular genome was 187,253 bp in length and exhibited a typical quadripartite structure of the large single-copy (LSC, 94,246 bp) and small single-copy (SSC, 483 bp) regions, separated by a pair of inverted repeats (IRs, 46,262 bp). The cp genome contains 116 unique genes, including 80 protein-coding genes, 32 tRNA, and four rRNA genes. The phylogenetic analysis based on whole cp genome demonstrated a close relationship between Hydrocleys and Alisma under current sampling. Our study will offer valuable genetic resources for phylogenetic and evolutionary research.Keywords: Chloroplast genome, Hydrocleys nymphoides, Alismataceae, aquatic plants, phylogenetic analysis

Introduction

Hydrocleys nymphoides (Humb. & Bonpl. ex Willd.) Buchenau 1871, commonly known as water poppy, is a perennial herbaceous aquatic plant. The genus Hydrocleys formerly belonged to the family Limnocharitaceae, which is now incorporated into the family Alismataceae (Chen et al. 2004; Les and Tippery 2013). H. nymphoides is native to Central and South America, and has been introduced worldwide as an ornamental aquatic plant. H. nymphoides is a floating-leaved aquatic plant characterized by long-petioled, circular to broadly ovate leaves (Figure 1). It produces umbel inflorescences with yellow flowers, which contribute to its notable ornamental value. The plant spreads horizontally across the water surface via runners that root at nodes, giving rise to new plants and enabling rapid surface coverage. Due to its growth habit and aesthetic appeal, water poppy is commonly used in ornamental ponds and water gardens (Kodela and Jobson 2018). Current literature has examined the micro-morphology of the reproductive structures in H. nymphoides, such as megasporogenesis and megagametogenesis, and ultrastructural study of pollen and tapetum development (Nicolau et al. 2024, 2025). There are also studies focusing on its medicinal value. For example, Toneto Francisco et al.’s (2025) study proved that the phytochemical-rich extract of H. nymphoides exhibits antimicrobial and antibiofilm activity against multidrug-resistant Staphylococcus. However, there have been few studies on the genetics of H. nymphoides, and the available genetic resources are limited. To date, no complete cp genome of the genus Hydrocleys species has been reported.

In this study, we sequenced and annotated the complete cp genome of H. nymphoide for the first time, and conducted the phylogenetic analysis to infer the placement of genus Hydrocleys in Alismataceae. The results provide valuable genetic resources, which are conducive to further exploration of the species’ conservation, molecular evolution, and taxonomic studies of Hydrocleys.

Materials and methods

Fresh leaves of H. nymphoides were collected from Hangzhou Botanical Garden (120°6.757′E, 30°15.162′N) in Zhejiang Province of China, and dried with silica gel. The voucher specimens (No. QL22103005) were identified by Shengnan Wei and deposited in the Zhejiang University Herbarium (Shengnan Wei, 2023210901077@mails.zstu.edu.cn). Total genomic DNA was extracted from dried materials using DNA Plantzol Reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA) following the manufacturer’s protocol. After evaluating DNA quality and concentration by agarose gel electrophoresis, paired-end sequencing libraries were prepared following the standard Illumina protocols (Illumina, San Diego, CA). The libraries were subsequently sequenced on an Illumina HiSeq2500 platform at the Beijing Genomics Institute (BGI, Shenzhen, China). The raw reads were then used to assemble the cp genomes using NOVOPlasty4.2.1 (Dierckxsens et al. 2017), with the reference genome of Alisma plantago-aquatica (GenBank accession no. NC_044108.1). Subsequently, the quadripartite structure was validated and examined using Bandage (Wick et al. 2015). Annotation was performed using CPGAVAS2 (http://www.herbalgenomics.org/cpgavas2), GeSeq (Tillich et al. 2017), and Geneious Prime 2023.2.1 (Kearse et al. 2012), with the reference genome of A. plantago-aquatica. GB2Sequin (Lehwark and Greiner 2019) and CPGView (Liu et al. 2023) were used to confirm the annotation results and to check the accuracy of introns and trans-spliced genes. Chloroplast (cp) genome map visualization was performed using the online software OGDraw (https://chlorobox.mpimp-golm.mpg.de/OGDraw.html, 3 May 2025). The annotated complete cp genome of H. nymphoides was deposited in GenBank under the accession no. PV633786.

Ten species of Alismataceae with available cp genomes were selected for phylogenetic tree reconstruction analysis. Enhalus acoroides and Thalassia hemprichii were used as outgroup. The sequence alignment was conducted using MAFFT v7.3 (Katoh and Standley 2013). The phylogenetic tree was reconstructed using IQTREE v1.6.7 (Nguyen et al. 2015), with the best-selected GTR + F + R3 model and 1000 bootstrap replicates. Tree visualization was performed in Figtree v1.4.4 (http://tree.bio.ed.ac.uk/software/figtree/).

Results

Chloroplast genome of H. nymphoides was a circular DNA molecule of 187,253 bp in length. The average coverage depth of the genome assembly was 2156.7× (Figure S1). The cp genome had a typical quadripartite structure, consisting of a pair of inverted repeats (IRa and IRb, 46,262 bp) separated by a large single-copy region (LSC, 94,246 bp) and a small single-copy region (SSC, 483 bp) (Figure 2). The overall GC content was 35.1%. The IR regions had a higher GC content (37.1%) than the LSC (33.3%) and SSC (19.3%) regions. The cp genome encoded a set of 116 unique genes, containing 80 protein-coding genes, 32 tRNA genes, and four rRNA genes. Thirty genes, including 18 protein-coding genes, eight tRNA genes, and four rRNA genes, were duplicated in the IR regions. Among all the unique genes identified, 11 genes (rps16, atpF, rpoC1, ycf3, clpP, petB, petD, rpl16, rpl2, ndhB, and ndhA) were cis-splicing genes (Figure S2), and rps12 was a trans-splicing gene with three unique exons (Figure S3).

Structural map of the chloroplast genome of H. nymphoides. Genes inside the circle are transcribed clockwise, and genes outside the circle are transcribed counterclockwise. Genes were color-coded according to their annotated function. The GC content of the plastome was indicated by the dashed gray area.

The reconstructed phylogeny tree based on 13 whole cp genome sequences revealed the position of H. nymphoides within Alismataceae (Figure 3). The phylogenetic tree was classified into two main clades. One clade includes the species of genes Echinodorus, Sagittaria, and Caldesia, and the other clade consists of Hydrocleys and Alisma species. At the present sampling extent, Hydrocleys possesses a closer relationship with Alisma than Echinodorus, Sagittaria, and Caldesia. All of the bootstrap values of the branches were 100, suggesting strong phylogenetic resolution.

Maximum-likelihood tree based on the chloroplast gene sequences of H. nymphoides and 12 other species. The number next to the nodes indicates the bootstrap values. The scale bar in the bottom left corner of the figure represents the phylogenetic distance of 0.03 nucleotide substitutions per site. The following sequences were used: Echinodorus macrophyllus PP503046 (Li et al. 2025), Sagittaria graminea NC_067603, Sagittaria trifolia NC_044119 (Liang et al. 2019), Sagittaria lichuanensis NC_029815 (Luo et al. 2016), Caldesia grandis MH262300 (Mwanzia et al. 2019), Caldesia parnassifolia NC_045926 (Mwanzia et al. 2019), Alisma subcordatum OR773542 (Zheng et al. 2024), Alisma triviale OR773543 (Zheng et al. 2024), Alisma plantago-aquatica NC_044108 (Liang et al. 2019), Alisma orientale OR522698 (Zheng et al. 2024), Enhalus acoroides NC_048519 (Liu et al. 2019), and Thalassia hemprichii NC_043774.

Discussion and conclusions

In this study, we present the complete cp genome sequence of H. nymphoides, reported here for the first time. The genome has a total length of 187,253 bp and displays the typical quadripartite structure, consisting of a LSC region, a SSC region, and two IR regions. This typical quadripartite structure is consistent with that of most angiosperms (Li et al. 2017; Han et al. 2025). Compared with other species in the Alismataceae family, the SSC sequence length of H. nymphoides was drastically shortened, measuring only 483 bp (Li et al. 2023). Compared to the reference sequence of A. plantago-aquatica, all genes from its SSC region are incorporated into the IR region in H. nymphoides, leaving only a portion of intergenic spacer sequences. This phenomenon is also observed in the cps of certain plant species. For instance, in Hoya, the SSC region spans from 2265 bp in H. pandurata to 2306 bp in H. caudata (Odago et al. 2021). In three Asarum species, the entire SSC region was integrated into the IR region and duplicated (Li et al. 2025). These variations are likely attributable to the expansion and contraction of the IR regions (Wei et al. 2021).

Within the monocot order Alismatales, the family Alismataceae is regarded as one of the oldest lineages, playing a significant role in understanding both the systematics and evolutionary history of flowering plants (Soltis et al. 2005; Ross et al. 2016). Based on multiple nuclear and plastid DNA sequences, Chen et al. (2012) conducted a phylogenetic analysis to investigate the generic relationships within Alismataceae. Their findings indicated that the Limnocharitaceae, which includes Hydrocleys, should be merged with the Alismataceae, thereby supporting the recognition of both groups as a single family. Recently, based on analyses of 78 protein-coding genes from each of the cp genomes, Li et al. (2022) reconstructed the phylogenetic tree of Alismataceae. In their study, Hydrocleys was found to be most closely related to Butomopsis, followed by Limnocharis. Within our current sampling range, the phylogenetic analysis based on complete cp genomes revealed a closer relationship between Hydrocleys and Alisma compared to Sagittaria, Caldesia, and Echinodorus. This finding is inconsistent with the results reported by Li et al. (2022), where H. nymphoides clusters with Sagittaria, Echinodorus, and Caldesia species in the same clade other than Alisma. We hypothesize that this phylogenetic discrepancy may stem from the relatively limited sampling scope due to the sparse availability of complete cp genomes. Our study elucidates the cp genome structure of H. nymphoides and provides new genetic resources for future phylogenetic and population genetic research in the Alismataceae family, particularly for the genus Hydrocleys.

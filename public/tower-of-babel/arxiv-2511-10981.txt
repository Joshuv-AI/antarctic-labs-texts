Valence quark distribution of rho meson using light-front quark model
Satyajit Puhan, Shubham Sharma, Narinder Kumar, Harleen Dahiya

Abstract
We investigate the partonic structure of the ρ \rho meson, the lightest spin- 1 1 vector meson, within the light-front quark model (LFQM). To explore the sensitivity to model assumptions, we employ two distinct types of spin wave functions in the LFQM. Using light-front helicity wave functions, we derive explicit expressions for the leading-twist and subleading-twist quark parton distribution functions (PDFs), and evolve the leading-twist PDFs to higher scales with next-to-leading order (NLO) Dokshitzer–Gribov–Lipatov–Altarelli–Parisi (DGLAP) evolution. We have also calculated the Mellin moment from the evolved PDFs using a simple neural network frame and compared with available theoretical predictions. Furthermore, we compute the full set of nine leading-twist transverse-momentum-dependent distributions (TMDs) for the valence quark in the ρ \rho meson, including three tensor TMDs that arise from spin- 1 1 tensor polarization of the hadron. Positivity constraints for the PDFs and TMDs are examined within this framework. Our findings highlight the crucial role of tensor polarization in shaping the three-dimensional partonic structure of vector mesons.

II
Light-Front Quark Model (LFQM)

In the LFQM, the meson Fock state (
|M\rangle
) is described as a bound state of quarks, gluons, and sea quarks as
65
;
48
;
49
;
68

\displaystyle|M\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum|q\bar{q}\rangle\Psi_{q\bar{q}}+\sum|q\bar{q}g\rangle\Psi_{q\bar{q}g}+\sum|q\bar{q}gg\rangle\Psi_{q\bar{q}gg}+|q\bar{q}(q\bar{q})_{sea}\rangle\Psi_{q\bar{q}(q\bar{q})_{sea}}+\cdots\,.

(1)

Representing the meson as a sum over multi-particle Fock states within light-front (LF) quantization, expressed in terms of the momentum and spin of its constituents, we can write

\displaystyle|M(P,\lambda)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n,i,j}\int\prod_{m=1}^{n}\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{m}\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{k}_{\perp m}}{\sqrt{x_{m}}~16\pi^{3}}16\pi^{3}~\delta\Big(1-\sum_{m=1}^{n}x_{m}\Big)\delta^{(2)}\Big(\sum_{m=1}^{n}\mathbf{k}_{\perp m}\Big)~\Psi_{n/N}(x_{m},\mathbf{k}_{\perp m})|n;\mathbf{k}^{+}_{m},\mathbf{k}_{\perp m},i,j\rangle.

Here,
|M(P,\lambda)\rangle
denotes the meson state with momentum
P=(P^{+},P^{-},P_{\perp})
, and
\lambda
is the spin projection of the hadron. The values
\lambda=\pm 1
and
\lambda=0
correspond to the transverse and longitudinal spin projections, respectively. The indices
i
,
j
, and
n
denote the quark helicity, antiquark helicity, and number of flavors, respectively. The four-momentum of the
m^{th}
constituent is
k_{m}=(k_{m}^{+},k_{m}^{-},\mathbf{k}_{\perp m})
, and
x_{m}=k_{m}^{+}/P^{+}
is its longitudinal momentum fraction. Both the longitudinal momentum fractions and the transverse momenta of the constituents satisfy the momentum sum rules

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}\mathbf{k}_{\perp m}=0,\qquad\sum_{m=1}^{n}x_{m}=1.

(2)

As we restrict our analysis to mesons without explicit gluonic components, the hadron Fock state in Eq. (
1
) reduces to
|M\rangle=\sum|q\bar{q}\rangle\,\Psi_{q\bar{q}}
. Neglecting higher Fock-state contributions, the meson state expressed in terms of quark–antiquark helicities becomes

\displaystyle|M(P,\lambda)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{k}_{\perp}}{\sqrt{x(1-x)}\,2(2\pi)^{3}}

(3)

\displaystyle\times\Psi^{\lambda}_{i,j}(x,\mathbf{k}_{\perp}^{2})\,|x,\mathbf{k}_{\perp},i,j\rangle.

Here,
x
and
1-x
are the longitudinal momentum fractions carried by the quark and antiquark, respectively. The total meson wave function
\Psi^{\lambda}_{i,j}(x,\mathbf{k}_{\perp}^{2})
combines spin and momentum-space components, and can be written as

\displaystyle\Psi^{\lambda}_{i,j}(x,\mathbf{k}_{\perp}^{2})=\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(x,\mathbf{k}_{\perp},i,j)\,\phi(x,\mathbf{k}_{\perp}).

(4)

Here,
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(x,\mathbf{k}_{\perp},i,j)
represents the spin wave function, while
\phi(x,\mathbf{k}_{\perp})
denotes the radial wave function. For the radial wave function, we adopt the rotationally invariant Gaussian form
14
;
47
;
29
;
30

\phi(\mathbf{k})=\frac{4\pi^{3/4}}{\beta^{3/2}}\,e^{-\mathbf{k}^{2}/2\beta^{2}},

(5)

where
\mathbf{k}=(k_{z},\mathbf{k}_{\perp})
and
\beta
is a variational parameter obtained from mass-spectroscopic analysis. This Gaussian wave function satisfies the normalization condition

\int\frac{dk_{z}\,d^{2}\mathbf{k}_{\perp}}{2(2\pi)^{3}}\;\big|\phi(\mathbf{k})\big|^{2}=1.

(6)

To ensure rotational invariance, the normalization of
\phi(\mathbf{k})
can also be expressed in terms of
\phi(x,\mathbf{k}_{\perp})
by performing the variable transformation
(k_{z},\mathbf{k}_{\perp})\to(x,\mathbf{k}_{\perp})
as

\int_{0}^{1}\!dx\int\frac{d^{2}\mathbf{k}_{\perp}}{2(2\pi)^{3}}\;\big|\phi(x,\mathbf{k}_{\perp})\big|^{2}=1.

(7)

The function
\phi(x,\mathbf{k}_{\perp})
includes the Jacobian factor,

\phi(x,\mathbf{k}_{\perp})=\sqrt{\frac{\partial k_{z}}{\partial x}}\,\phi(\mathbf{k}),

(8)

with
k_{z}=\tfrac{1}{2}(2x-1)M_{q\bar{q}}
and
\frac{\partial k_{z}}{\partial x}=\tfrac{M_{q\bar{q}}}{4x(1-x)}
for the case of equal quark and antiquark masses. The invariant mass of the bound state is given by

M_{q\bar{q}}=\sqrt{\frac{\mathbf{k}_{\perp}^{2}+m^{2}}{x(1-x)}}.

(9)

In this work, we focus on the
\rho
meson. Within the LFQM, the
\rho
meson is treated under SU(2) isospin symmetry with equal constituent masses
m_{q}=m_{\bar{q}}=m
. We habe obtained
m=0.22~\text{GeV}
and
\beta=0.3659~\text{GeV}
using the variational principle through the solution of the bound state Hamiltonian. The
\rho
meson mass is obtained to be
0.770
GeV through these parameters.

The spin wave function
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(x,\mathbf{k}_{\perp},i,j)
in Eq. (

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(x,\textbf{k}_{\perp},i,j)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{u}(k_{1},i)\Bigg[-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\sqrt{M_{q\bar{q}}^{2}-(m_{q}-m_{\bar{q}})^{2}}}(\gamma^{\mu}-\frac{k_{1}^{\mu}-k_{2}^{\mu}}{M_{q\bar{q}}+m_{q}+m_{\bar{q}}})\epsilon^{\nu}_{\lambda}(P)\Bigg]v(k_{2},j)\,,\ \ \ \ (S-1)

(10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(x,\textbf{k}_{\perp},i,j)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{u}(k_{1},i)\Bigg[\epsilon^{\nu}_{\lambda}(P).\gamma\Bigg]v(k_{2},j)\,.\ \ \ \ (S-2)

\displaystyle{\epsilon}^{\nu}_{\lambda=\pm}(P)

\displaystyle{\epsilon}^{\nu}_{\lambda=0}(P)

\displaystyle\omega_{q\bar{q}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(M_{q\bar{q}}+m_{q}+m_{\bar{q}})\sqrt{x(1-x)[M_{q\bar{q}}^{2}-(m_{q}-m_{\bar{q}})^{2}]}}.

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\mathcal{S}^{*}_{\lambda^{\prime}}(x,\textbf{k}_{\perp},i,j)\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(x,\textbf{k}_{\perp},i,j)=\delta_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}.

III
Parton Distribution Functions

III.1
Leading twist

Quark PDFs play a crucial role in describing hadron structure in terms of the longitudinal momentum fraction (
x
). At leading twist, spin-
1
mesons possess four independent quark PDFs, compared to one for spin-
0
mesons and three for spin-
\tfrac{1}{2}
nucleons. These are the unpolarized distribution
f(x)
, the helicity distribution
h(x)
, the transversity distribution
g(x)
, and the tensor distribution
f_{1LL}(x)
. Among them,
f(x)
,
g(x)
, and
h(x)
are common to spin-
\tfrac{1}{2}
nucleons
61
, while the tensor PDF
f_{1LL}(x)
arises uniquely from the tensor polarization of spin-
1
hadrons
52
. These quark PDFs can be obtained by evaluating the quark–quark correlator for spin-
1
mesons, while taking their polarization states into consideration, as

\displaystyle\Phi_{ij}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{dz^{-}}{2\pi}e^{ik\cdot z}\allowbreak

(19)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\langle M_{\lambda}(P)|\overline{\psi}_{j}(0)\psi_{i}(z)|M_{\lambda}(P)\rangle_{z^{+}=z_{\perp}=0}.

For the spin-
1
case, the quark PDFs can be calculated using the trace with different
\Gamma
matrices as
63
;
16

\displaystyle\langle\Gamma\rangle^{\lambda}_{ij}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}\Big[\Gamma\Phi_{ij}(x)\Big]

(20)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{*\mu}_{\lambda}(P^{*})\langle\Gamma\rangle^{\mu\nu}(x)\epsilon^{\nu}_{\lambda}(P).

The spin-
1
quark PDFs corresponding to different choices of
\Gamma
for the unpolarized, polarized, and tensor-polarized hadron cases are expressed as

\displaystyle\langle\gamma^{+}\rangle^{\lambda}_{ij}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x)+S_{LL}f_{1LL},

(21)

\displaystyle\langle\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5}\rangle^{\lambda}_{ij}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda S_{L}g(x),

(22)

\displaystyle\langle\gamma^{+}\gamma^{i_{1}}\gamma_{5}\rangle^{\lambda}_{ij}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S^{i_{1}}_{\perp}h(x).

(23)

Here,
S_{LL}
denotes the longitudinal tensor polarization of the hadron, written as

\displaystyle S_{LL}=(3\lambda^{2}-2)\Big(\frac{1}{6}-\frac{S_{L}^{2}}{2}\Big),

(24)

with
S=(S_{L},S_{T}^{x},S_{T}^{y})
representing the hadron polarization vector.

For a given polarization direction
S
, the hadron can have three possible spin projections
\lambda=0,\pm 1
.
For longitudinal polarization, where
|S_{T}|=0
and
|S_{L}|=1
, the spin projections are
\lambda=\pm 1,0
along the momentum direction.
For transverse polarization, where
|S_{L}|=0
and
|S_{T}|=1
, the three projections are along the direction perpendicular to the momentum. Here,
i_{1}
denotes the transverse components
x
and
y
. The LF wave function (LFWF) overlap representations of these quark PDFs can be obtained by equating Eqs. (
21
)–(
23
) with Eq. (
20
). The explicit derivation of the LFWF overlap form is provided in Appendix
V
.
The resulting overlap representations of the quark PDFs are given as

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{2}\textbf{k}_{\perp}}{6(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{i,j}\Bigg[|\Psi^{+1}_{i,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})|^{2}+|\Psi^{0}_{i,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})|^{2}+|\Psi^{-1}_{i,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})|^{2}\Bigg],

(25)

\displaystyle g(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{2}\textbf{k}_{\perp}}{4(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{j}\Bigg[|\Psi^{+1}_{\uparrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})|^{2}+|\Psi^{-1}_{\downarrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})|^{2}-|\Psi^{+1}_{\downarrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})|^{2}-|\Psi^{-1}_{\uparrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})|^{2}\Bigg],

(26)

\displaystyle h(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{2}\textbf{k}_{\perp}}{4\sqrt{2}(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{j}\Bigg[\Psi^{+1*}_{\uparrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})\Psi^{0}_{\downarrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})+\Psi^{0*}_{\downarrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})\Psi^{+1}_{\uparrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})+\Psi^{0*}_{\uparrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})\Psi^{-1}_{\downarrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})

(27)

\displaystyle+\Psi^{-1*}_{\downarrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})\Psi^{0}_{\uparrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})\Bigg],

\displaystyle f_{1LL}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{2}\textbf{k}_{\perp}}{2(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{i,j}\Bigg[\frac{-1}{2}\bigg(|\Psi^{+1}_{i,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})|^{2}+|\Psi^{-1}_{i,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})|^{2}\bigg)+|\Psi^{0}_{i,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})|^{2}\Bigg].

(28)

The explicit expressions of these quark PDFs, obtained using the S-1 spin wave function, are given by

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{2}\textbf{k}_{\perp}}{2(2\pi)^{3}}|\phi(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})|^{2},

(29)

\displaystyle g(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{2}\textbf{k}_{\perp}}{4(2\pi)^{3}}\Bigg(\frac{2\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp}}(2x-1)+m^{2}\right)M_{q\bar{q}}+4m\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp}}+m^{2}\right)}{\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp}}+m^{2}\right)(M_{q\bar{q}}+2m)}\Bigg)|\phi(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})|^{2},

(30)

\displaystyle h(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{2}\textbf{k}_{\perp}}{2(2\pi)^{3}}\Bigg(\frac{\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp}}\left(2m(x+1)M_{q\bar{q}}+x(2x-1)M_{q\bar{q}}^{2}+6m^{2}\right)+m^{2}(M_{q\bar{q}}+2m)^{2}+2\textbf{k}_{\perp}^{4}\right)}{\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp}}+m^{2}\right)(M_{q\bar{q}}+2m)^{2}}\Bigg)|\phi(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})|^{2},

(31)

\displaystyle f_{1LL}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

(32)

Similarly, the explicit expressions of the leading-twist quark PDFs using the S-2 type spin wave function are obtained as

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{2}\textbf{k}_{\perp}}{6(2\pi)^{3}}\Bigg(\frac{2\left(\text{A}_{L}^{2}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp}}+m^{2}-\text{M}_{q\bar{q}}^{2}(x-1)x\right)^{2}+2\text{A}_{T}^{2}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp}}(2(x-1)x+1)+m^{2}\right)\right)}{(1-x)x}\Bigg)|\phi(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})|^{2},

(33)

\displaystyle g(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{2}\textbf{k}_{\perp}}{4(2\pi)^{3}}\Bigg(\frac{4\text{A}_{T}^{2}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp}}(2x-1)+m^{2}\right)}{(1-x)x}\Bigg)|\phi(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})|^{2},

(34)

\displaystyle h(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{2}\textbf{k}_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\Bigg(\frac{\text{A}_{L}\text{A}_{T}m\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp}}+m^{2}-\text{M}_{q\bar{q}}^{2}(x-1)x\right)}{(1-x)x}\Bigg)|\phi(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})|^{2},

(35)

\displaystyle f_{1LL}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{2}\textbf{k}_{\perp}}{2(2\pi)^{3}}\Bigg(\frac{-2\text{A}_{T}^{2}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp}}(2(x-1)x+1)+m^{2}\right)+2\text{A}_{L}^{2}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp}}+m^{2}-\text{M}_{q\bar{q}}^{2}(x-1)x\right)^{2}}{(1-x)x}\Bigg)|\phi(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})|^{2}.

(36)

The LFWFs satisfy angular momentum conservation projected along the
z
-axis, i.e.,
J_{z}=s_{q}+s_{\bar{q}}+L_{z}
, where
s_{q}
and
s_{\bar{q}}
denote the spin contributions of the quark and antiquark, respectively, and
L_{z}
is the orbital angular momentum of the
\rho
meson, taking values
L_{z}=0,\pm 1,\pm 2
depending on the specific LFWF. The unpolarized
f(x)
, helicity
g(x)
, and tensor-polarized
f_{1LL}(x)
quark PDFs arise from the diagonal matrix elements of the
6\times 6
helicity amplitude matrix of Eq. (
138
), whereas the transversity
h(x)
corresponds to non-diagonal elements. The PDFs
f(x)
,
g(x)
, and
f_{1LL}(x)
are associated with non-flip hadron and quark polarizations (i.e., zero orbital angular momentum transfer between the initial and final hadron), while
h(x)
involves polarization flips. For the S-1 type spin wave functions, the tensor-polarized PDF
f_{1LL}(x)
vanishes, whereas it is non-zero for the S-2 type.
In Fig.
1
, we plot all leading-twist quark PDFs as functions of the longitudinal momentum fraction
x
carried by the active quark at the model scale
Q^{2}=0.20~\mathrm{GeV}^{2}
. The unpolarized
f(x)
and tensor-polarized
f_{1LL}(x)
PDFs for both spin wave function types are found to satisfy the PDF sum rule

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}dxf(x)=1,\ \ \ \ \int_{0}^{1}dxf_{1LL}(x)=0.

(37)

This implies that the valence quark and antiquark carry
100\%
of the light-front momentum, independent of the hadron polarization. Both
f(x)
and
f_{1LL}(x)
quark PDFs exhibit symmetry under the exchange
x\longleftrightarrow(1-x)
around
x=1/2
. For the
S
-2 type spin wave function, the
g(x)
and
f_{1LL}(x)
PDFs show two nodes.

At the model scale, the quark PDFs computed using the
S
-1 method are strictly positive, whereas in the
S
-2 case,
g(x)
and
f_{1LL}(x)
take both positive and negative values. This kind of mixed distribution has also been reported in the NJL model
63
.
Our
f_{1LL}(x)
and
xf_{1LL}(x)
distributions obtained with the
S
-2 method show qualitative agreement with results from the LFHM
50
, NJL model
63
;
80
, and ILM
59
.
The tensor PDF
f_{1LL}(x)
is related to the structure function
b_{1}(x)
via

\displaystyle b_{1}(x)=\frac{1}{2}f_{1LL}(x),

(38)

which measures the difference in the spin projection of the
\rho
meson, depending solely on the quark spin-average distribution.

In Figs.
2
and
3
, we show the evolution of the quark PDFs obtained from both methods at
Q^{2}=2.4
,
5
, and
20
GeV
2
using NLO DGLAP evolution
62
;
43
;
42
.
With increasing
Q^{2}
, the quark PDFs decrease in magnitude for both methods.
After evolution, all quark PDFs remain positive except
f_{1LL}(x)
.

The average longitudinal momentum fraction, or
x
-moments, of the PDFs are defined as

\displaystyle\langle x^{n}\rangle_{Q^{2}}=\int_{0}^{1}dx\,x^{n}\,\text{PDF}(x,Q^{2}).

(39)

In this work, we have also calculated
\langle x^{n}\rangle
moments up to
n=4
for different PDFs at various scales by fitting the evolved PDFs using a neural network (NN) architecture of
(1,4,1)
, i.e., one input layer, four hidden layers (each with 128 neurons), and one output layer.
We tested several activation functions and chose the Tanh function for the hidden layers, defined as

\displaystyle\sigma(z)=\frac{e^{z}-e^{-z}}{e^{z}+e^{-z}}.

(40)

The resulting
\langle x^{n}\rangle
values are summarized in Table
1
.

At
Q^{2}=2.4
GeV
2
, the mean longitudinal momentum fraction
\langle x\rangle
of the unpolarized PDF
f(x)
is
0.24
for both methods, indicating that the quark and antiquark together carry only
48\%
of the hadron momentum at this scale. This fraction decreases to
40\%
at
Q^{2}=20
GeV
2
. For comparison, the ILM
59
and BSE
75
approaches give
\langle x\rangle=0.201
and
0.316
, respectively, at a similar scale.

The transversity PDF
g(x)
encodes information about the total spin of the
\rho
meson.
The spin sum rule,
\int_{0}^{1}dx\,g(x)
, yields

\int_{0}^{1}dx\,g(x)=\begin{cases}0.59,&\text{S-1},\\
0.41,&\text{S-2}.\end{cases}

This indicates that, at the model scale, valence quarks contribute
59\%
to the
\rho
meson spin in the S-1 method, and
42\%
in the S-2 method.
Consequently, in the S-2 approach, the quark orbital angular momentum (OAM) accounts for
58\%
of the meson spin at the model scale, increasing to
74\%
at
Q^{2}=20
GeV
2
.
A similar trend is observed for the S-1 method at higher scales.
Table
2
compares our spin sum rule results at the model scale and at
Q^{2}=2.4
GeV
2
with other theoretical predictions
75
;
59
;
63
;
80
.

We also find that the valence quark and antiquark contribute
80\%
and
51\%
to the total tensor charge,
\int_{0}^{1}dx\,h(x)
, of the
\rho
meson in the S-1 and S-2 methods at the model scale, respectively.
These contributions decrease to
34\%
and
20\%
at
Q^{2}=20
GeV
2
.
Examining the tensor-polarized PDF
f_{1LL}(x)
, we observe that
\int_{0}^{1}dx\,xf_{1LL}(x)
remains small but finite at high
Q^{2}
; for example, at
Q^{2}=5
GeV
2
, we find
\int_{0}^{1}dx\,xf_{1LL}(x)=0.001
, compared to
0.03
in the NJL model
63
.

Finally, we have studied the positivity constraints for spin-1 meson leading twist PDFs, which can be expressed as

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle\geq 0,

(41)

\displaystyle f(x)-g(x)-\tfrac{1}{3}f_{1LL}(x)

\displaystyle\geq 0,\quad(\text{PS-1})

(42)

\displaystyle 2f(x)+g(x)+\tfrac{1}{3}f_{1LL}(x)

\displaystyle\geq 0,\quad(\text{PS-2})

(43)

\displaystyle\Big(f(x)+\tfrac{2}{3}f_{1LL}(x)\Big)\Big(f(x)+g(x)-\tfrac{1}{3}f_{1LL}(x)\Big)

\displaystyle\geq 2\,|h(x)|^{2},\quad(\text{PS-3})

(44)

\displaystyle|h(x)|\leq f(x)+\frac{1}{6}f_{1LL}(x)\leq\frac{3}{2}f(x).\quad(\text{PS-4})

(45)

For positivity constraints, we have used the notation “PS” throughout this work. The positivity constraints of both S-1 and S-2 type spin wave functions have been plotted in Fig.
4
. We observed that all the positivity constraints are obeyed by both the spin wave functions.

III.2
Sub-leading twist

Similarly, at subleading twist, spin-
1
mesons possess a total of five quark PDFs, in contrast to the three found in spin-
\tfrac{1}{2}
nucleons. The two additional distributions correspond to tensor quark PDFs that are unique
to spin-
1
systems. The twist-3 quark PDFs can be expressed in the form

\displaystyle\langle\textbf{1}\rangle^{\lambda}_{ij}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{M_{\rho}}{P^{+}}(e(x)+S_{LL}e_{LL}(x)),

(46)

\displaystyle\langle\gamma^{i_{1}}\gamma_{5}\rangle^{\lambda}_{ij}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{M_{\rho}}{P^{+}}(S^{i_{1}}_{\perp}g_{T}(x)),

(47)

\displaystyle\langle i\sigma^{+-}\gamma_{5}\rangle^{\lambda}_{ij}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{M_{\rho}}{P^{+}}(\lambda S_{L}h_{L}(x)),

(48)

\displaystyle\langle\gamma^{i_{1}}\rangle^{\lambda}_{ij}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{M_{\rho}}{P^{+}}(S^{i_{1}}_{LT}f_{LT}(x)).

(49)

The overlap representations of these quark PDFs, which incorporate all possible helicity and polarization states of the hadron, are obtained by solving the
6\times 6
matrix presented in the Appendix
V
. The resulting overlap expressions for these quark PDFs are

\displaystyle e(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{2}\textbf{k}_{\perp}}{6(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{i,j}\frac{m}{xM_{\rho}}\Bigg[|\Psi^{+1}_{i,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})|^{2}+|\Psi^{0}_{i,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})|^{2}+|\Psi^{-1}_{i,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})|^{2}\Bigg]=\frac{m}{xM_{\rho}}f(x),

(50)

\displaystyle g_{T}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{2}\textbf{k}_{\perp}}{4\sqrt{2}(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{xM_{\rho}}\sum_{j}\Bigg[\frac{\textbf{k}^{R}_{\perp}}{2}\Bigg(\Psi^{+1*}_{\uparrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})\Psi^{0}_{\uparrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})-\Psi^{+1*}_{\downarrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})\Psi^{0}_{\downarrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})

(51)

\displaystyle+\Psi^{0*}_{\uparrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})\Psi^{-1}_{\uparrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})-\Psi^{0*}_{\downarrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})\Psi^{-1}_{\downarrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})\Bigg)+\frac{\textbf{k}^{L}_{\perp}}{2}\Bigg(\Psi^{0*}_{\uparrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})\Psi^{+1}_{\uparrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})-\Psi^{0*}_{\downarrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})\Psi^{+}_{\downarrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})

\displaystyle+\Psi^{-1*}_{\uparrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})\Psi^{0}_{\uparrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})-\Psi^{-1*}_{\downarrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})\Psi^{0}_{\downarrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})\Bigg)+m\Bigg(\Psi^{+1*}_{\uparrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})\Psi^{0}_{\downarrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})+\Psi^{0*}_{\downarrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})\Psi^{+1}_{\uparrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})

\displaystyle+\Psi^{0*}_{\uparrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})\Psi^{-1}_{\downarrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})+\Psi^{-1*}_{\downarrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})\Psi^{0}_{\uparrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})\Bigg)\Bigg],

\displaystyle h(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{2}\textbf{k}_{\perp}}{4(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{j}\frac{1}{xM_{\rho}}\Bigg[m\Bigg(|\Psi^{+1*}_{\uparrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})|^{2}-|\Psi^{+1*}_{\downarrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})|^{2}+|\Psi^{-1*}_{\downarrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})|^{2}-|\Psi^{-1*}_{\uparrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})|^{2}\Bigg)

(52)

\displaystyle-\textbf{k}^{R}_{\perp}\Bigg(\Psi^{+1*}_{\downarrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})\Psi^{+1}_{\uparrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})-\Psi^{-1*}_{\downarrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})\Psi^{-1}_{\uparrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})\Bigg)

\displaystyle+\textbf{k}^{L}_{\perp}\Bigg(\Psi^{+1*}_{\uparrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})\Psi^{+1}_{\downarrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})-\Psi^{-1*}_{\uparrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})\Psi^{-1}_{\downarrow,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})\Bigg)\Bigg],

\displaystyle e_{LL}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{2}\textbf{k}_{\perp}}{2(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{i,j}\frac{m}{xM_{\rho}}\Bigg[\frac{-1}{2}(|\Psi^{+1}_{i,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})|^{2}+|\Psi^{-1}_{i,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})|^{2})+|\Psi^{0}_{i,j}(x,\textbf{k}^{2}_{\perp})|^{2}\Bigg]=\frac{m}{xM_{\rho}}f_{1LL}(x),

(53)

Conclusion

We have presented a comprehensive analysis of the quark structure of spin-
1
vector mesons within the LFQM framework, in terms of both PDFs and TMDs. The longitudinal degree of freedom of the quarks are studied using the leading twist and subleading twist PDFs. While the transverse structure have been studied using the quark TMDs. The quark PDFs and TMDs have been calculated by considering the trace of spin-1 spin densities. We have presented the PDFs and TMDs through a matrix, which is equal to a LFWFs amplitude matrix. The LFWFs form of quark PDFs and TMDs have been presented along with the explicit forms. We have presented all the quark PDFs and TMDs through two dimensional and three dimensional plots. We have observed that the leading twist quark PDFs and TMDs have similar kind of behavior with available other theoretical models.

The leading twist PDFs have been evolved to higher scales through NLO DGLAP evolutions. These evolved PDFs are plotted through two dimensional pictures. To predict the average longitudinal momentum fraction or Mellin moment, we have used a simple NN frame. The calculated Mellin moment are found to have similar values with other model predictions. The PDF sum rule and positivity constraints are found to be obeyed by our model. The average transverse momenta carried by the quark TMDs also calculated in this model. The positivity constraints on the leading twist TMDs also obeyed by our model. Overall, our model predictions are found to have similar results with other model predictions. We will also looking for the spin asymmetries arises for the case of spin-1 hadrons in our next project.
The upcoming Jefferson Lab, Nuclotron-based Ion Collider fAcility (NICA) and electron ion collider (EIC) will provide more insight about the tensor structure of spin-1 systems.

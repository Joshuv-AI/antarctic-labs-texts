Integrative transcriptomic profiling and machine learning reveal hypoxia-associated molecular signatures for precision diagnosis in thyroid eye disease
Authors: Weijin Qian, Tianyi Zhu, Jin Liu, Yining Wei, Li Yang, Lianfei Fang, Jing Sun, Yinwei Li, Sijie Fang, Huifang Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT

Background

Thyroid eye disease (TED) is a complex autoimmune disorder with an incidence of 2.67–3.3 cases per 100,000 individuals annually in women and 0.54–0.9 cases per 100,000 individuals annually in men [1]. Contrary to the global consensus that mild forms are most common among TED patients [2], a majority of patients (74.2%) diagnosed with TED in China presented with moderate severity based on NOSPECS severity scores, and 16% were classified as severe, indicating potential ethnic differences [3]. These patients often exhibit severe ophthalmic manifestations, such as proptosis, eyelid retraction, inflammation, diplopia, and even vision impairment due to optic nerve compression, significantly affecting patients’ physical and psychological well-being [4]. As the morbidity of TED can be reduced by early diagnosis and appropriate prophylactic measures, as recommended by the Amsterdam Declaration [5], exploring novel diagnostic biomarkers for the early detection of TED and elucidating its underlying mechanisms is essential. Also known as Graves’ ophthalmopathy, TED is closely associated with Graves’ hyperthyroidism (GH). The majority of TED patients present with concomitant GH, and only a small proportion (up to 5%) exhibit euthyroid or hypothyroid [6]. Among patients with GH, up to 40% may develop TED during their course of disease. The close relationship has motivated previous studies to establish several predictive models for TED development in GH patients [7, 8]. However, these models primarily focused on clinical risk factors and baseline characteristics, with limited exploration of gene expression patterns that distinguish TED patients. Another study suggested a ferroptosis-related gene signature for TED prediction, but its reliability was constrained by a limited sample size [9]. Consequently, there remains an urgent need to establish a more robust diagnostic model for TED based on molecular alterations, while further enhancing our understanding of the pathogenic mechanisms of TED.

Hypoxia occurs when the demand for oxygen exceeds its supply, typically resulting from inflammation [10] and aberrant metabolic reprogramming under various pathological conditions [11]. It is an important pathological process and a hallmark feature of several ocular immune diseases. During hypoxia, activation of hypoxia-inducible factor 1 (HIF-1) initiates downstream signaling pathways that regulate critical biological processes, such as glucose metabolism, angiogenesis, oxidative stress, and inflammation [12–14]. Accumulating evidence has demonstrated that the hypoxic environment within the orbit of TED patients promotes the activation of extraocular muscle fibroblasts [15], leading to extraocular muscle enlargement [16], angiogenesis and adipogenesis [17, 18], and extensive infiltration of inflammatory cells [19], all of which adversely influence the progression and severity of TED. Additionally, the crowded muscles, adipose tissue and excessive production of glycosaminoglycan within the confined orbital compartment further aggravate hypoxia, forming a vicious cycle. Although hypoxia has been proven to be a pivotal contributor to TED progression, it remains unclear whether hypoxia and its associated molecules have diagnostic potential for the disease.

In our study, we aimed to construct and validate a diagnostic model for TED based on bulk RNA sequencing data from peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) in our cohort. We discovered that hypoxia was a notably enriched feature in TED and identified three hypoxia-related diagnostic biomarkers using integrated bioinformatic and machine learning approaches. Based on these biomarkers, a TED diagnostic model was established, showing high diagnostic accuracy. Immune cell infiltration and biological functions were further investigated to reveal potential pathogenic mechanisms associated with the identified biomarkers in TED. Furthermore, their cellular distribution and expression levels were validated using single cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq). This work may contribute to optimizing the early diagnosis of TED and promoting timely intervention, which could further control disease progression and potentially prevent irreversible damage in TED patients.

Methods

Data collection and preprocessing

The gene expression profiles and clinical data of 152 TED patients, 20 GH patients and 61 healthy controls (HC) were obtained from previously established in-house cohorts within the framework of the Sample Database Project of Shanghai Ninth People’s Hospital. All samples were originally collected and stored in this biobank as approved by the Institutional Review Board of Shanghai Ninth People’s Hospital (SH9H-2019-T192-2), with informed consent obtained from all participants for sample collection, data use, and scientific research.

The RUVSeq R package was utilized to correct batch effects across the entire count matrix, using ERCC spike-in as references. Additionally, genes expressed in less than 75% of all samples were removed. The schematic diagram of this study is shown in Fig. 1 and was created using Biorender (https://www.biorender.com/).

Schematic diagram of the study design

In this study, eight hypoxia-related gene sets were retrieved from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB, https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb) [20], including BIOCARTA_HIF_PATHWAY, GOBP_HYPOXIA_INDUCIBLE_FACTOR_1ALPHA_SIGNALING_PATHWAY, GOBP_INTRINSIC_APOPTOTIC_SIGNALING_PATHWAY_IN_RESPONSE_TO_HYPOXIA, GOBP_REGULATION_OF_CELLULAR_RESPONSE_TO_HYPOXIA, HALLMARK_HYPOXIA, HARRIS_HYPOXIA, REACTOME_CELLULAR_RESPONSE_TO_HYPOXIA, and REACTOME_REGULATION_OF_GENE_EXPRESSION_BY_HYPOXIA_INDUCIBLE_FACTOR. After removing duplicates, a total of 336 hypoxia-related genes (HRGs) were obtained.

Differential expression analysis

After data preprocessing, the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between TED and HC were identified by DESeq2 R package, with a screening threshold of fold change (FC) greater than 1.1 (|log2(FC)| > log2(1.1) and False Discovery Rate (FDR) adjusted p value less than 0.05 [21]. Volcano plot and heatmap were used for visualization.

Functional enrichment analysis

Biological process (BP), cellular component (CC), and molecular function (MF) categories from the Gene Ontology (GO) [22] and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) [23] databases were employed for functional enrichment analysis of up- and down-regulated DEGs, respectively. The enriched pathways in TED were visualized by dot plots. Additionally, gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was performed using Hallmark and Reactome gene sets from MSigDB, and significantly upregulated gene sets in TED were illustrated by GSEA plots [24].

Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA)

WGCNA was conducted to identify gene modules highly correlated with TED [25]. A normalized gene expression matrix, generated by the median-of-ratios method in DESeq2, and the top 5000 genes with the highest median absolute deviation were utilized as input. One outlier sample in the HC group was removed. With the optimal soft threshold power determined as four, a weighted adjacency matrix was constructed and then transformed into a topological overlap matrix (TOM). Hierarchical clustering was applied to generate a dendrogram of TOM, and genes with similar expression patterns were grouped into the same modules. Each module contained at least 30 genes, and was color-coded for intuitive visualization. Followingly, a heatmap was generated to display the correlation between gene modules and sample groups, while scatter plots were used to show the correlation between gene significance (GS) for TED and module membership (MM) in each module. KEGG enrichment analysis was conducted to explore pathways enriched in each gene module.

Hypoxia-related TED diagnostic genes (HRTDGs) identified via integrative machine learning approaches

DEGs between TED and HC, genes in the module most associated with TED, and HRGs were intersected to identify candidate genes related to TED and hypoxia. To develop a concise and robust gene signature for TED diagnosis, nine independent machine learning algorithms were implemented: random forest (RF), support vector machine (SVM), extreme gradient boosting (XGB), generalized linear model (GLM), gradient boosting machine (GBM), k-nearest neighbors (KNN), neural network (NNET), least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO), and decision tree (DT). 152 TED and 60 HC samples were randomly split into training and testing sets in a 7:3 ratio using the CreateDataPartition function. In the training set, parameters of each model were tuned using five-fold repeated cross-validation by “caret” package. Residual diagnostic analysis was performed by DALEX R package [26]. For each model, the area under curve (AUC) was calculated in the training, testing, and full sample sets. The RF model, which achieved the highest average AUC across these three sets, was considered optimal. The Shapley Additive exPlanation (SHAP) method was used to interpret the RF model by calculating the contribution of each feature to the final prediction results [27]. It is a game theory-based approach that ranks the feature importance and explains the results of the model, enhancing its transparency. Then, genes were subjected to univariate logistic regression analysis. Boruta R package was applied for feature screening by calculating z-score based on the importance of genes and mark each gene to a confirmed, undetermined, or rejected label [28]. Finally, genes with confirmed labels in RF model and statistical significance in the regression analysis were considered critical biomarkers, and were termed Hypoxia-related TED Diagnostic Genes (HRTDGs).

Construction and evaluation of TED diagnostic model

Subsequently, multivariate logistic regression analysis was performed on HRTDGs. An HRTDG score (HRTDGS) was computed based on normalized gene expression levels and regression coefficients derived from multivariate model as follows:

HRTDGS = coef 1 * exp (gene 1) + coef 2 * exp (gene 2) + coef 3 * exp (gene 3).

The HRTDGS was calculated for each TED and HC sample. Clinical variables including age and gender, together with HRTDGS, were included in univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses within the training set to assess whether HRTDGS served as an independent predictive factor for TED diagnosis. The HRTDGS was then considered the diagnostic model for TED and its discrimination, consistency, and clinical utility were evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, calibration curves, and decision curve analysis (DCA) in both training and testing sets. Additionally, 20 TED patients from the testing set and 20 GH samples were combined to create a TED vs. GH cohort for evaluation of the diagnostic performance of HRTDGS in distinguishing TED from GH.

Clinical correlation analysis

The relationship between HRTDGS and clinical features of TED patients was further explored and visualized using boxplots for categorical variables and correlation scatter plots for continuous variables. All TED samples were divided into high and low HRTDGS groups based on the median score, and normalized expression levels of HRTDGs were compared between these two groups.

Immune infiltration analysis

We applied two algorithms to assess immune infiltration differences between high and low HRTDGS groups: single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) [24], and celltype identification by estimating relative subsets of RNA transcripts (CIBERSORT) [29], which were visualized in a heatmap. Correlations between ssGSEA-derived immune cell scores and individual HRTDGs or the HRTDGS were displayed in heatmap or lollipop plot, respectively.

Gene set variation analysis (GSVA)

GSVA is an unsupervised and non-parametric method used to evaluate gene set enrichment based on transcriptomic profiles [30]. In our study, Hallmark, Reactome, KEGG and BP section of GO gene sets were downloaded from MSigDB and scored for each sample by GSVA R package. Differentially enriched gene sets between high and low HRTDGS groups were identified using the limma R package [31], and representative pathways were visualized by bidirectional bar plot.

Single cell RNA sequencing analysis

ScRNA-seq profiles were downloaded from GSE194323, consisting of orbital connective tissues from one TED patient and one HC [32]. During data preprocessing, cells expressing 200-6,000 genes, with fewer than 30,000 UMI, and less than 5% mitochondrial content were retained. Suspected doublets and ambient RNA contamination were removed using DoubletFinder [33] and DecontX packages, respectively [34]. The gene expression matrix was then normalized and the top 2000 highly variable genes were identified by “vst” method, followed by standardization via ScaleData function. Principal components (PCs) were calculated and batch effects were removed by Harmony. Dimensionality reduction and unsupervised clustering were then performed, and cluster features were visualized by t-distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (tSNE) plots.

DEGs across clusters were identified using the FindAllMarkers function, with selection criteria set to expression in more than 25% of cells, absolute log2FC greater than 0.25, and adjusted p-value below 0.05. Canonical cell markers were obtained from the CellMarker database (http://bio-bigdata.hrbmu.edu.cn/CellMarker/) and previous literature for cell type annotation [32, 35]. Marker expression was shown in dot plot, while cell type composition between TED and HC was displayed in bar plot. The expression of HRTDGs across cell types was shown by density plots. Additionally, the AddModuleScore function was used to score the expression levels of HRTDG module at single-cell level. Comparisons across cell types and between TED and HC were conducted using these module scores. The Hallmark hypoxia signaling pathway module score was also computed based on this function. The module scores were visualized by feature plots and violin plots.

Afterwards, cell-cell communication analysis was performed by CellChat [36], a tool for quantitatively simulating intercellular interaction network from scRNA-seq data. By integrating gene expression matrix with a curated ligand-receptor repository, CellChat inferred signaling probabilities among different cell types. Only ligand-receptor pairs with p-values less than 0.05 were considered significant. Circle plots, heatmaps and violin plots showed the strength and distribution of intercellular signals across cell types and groups.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed in R software (version 4.4.0). Between-group comparisons for continuous variables were performed using either Wilcoxon test or t test, depending on the data distribution (normal or non-normal). A two tailed p value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Functional enrichment analyses including KEGG, BP, CC, and MF of GO were performed on upregulated DEGs to explore the underlying biological mechanisms in TED compared to HC. KEGG analysis revealed that the HIF-1 signaling pathway, cellular senescence, EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor resistance, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, T cell receptor signaling pathway, NF-kappa B signaling pathway, and VEGF signaling pathway were enriched in TED (Fig. 2b), suggesting that the PBMCs from TED patients exhibited a signature of hypoxia as well as related characteristics like aging, angiogenesis, and active inflammatory response compared to HC. Consistent with KEGG results, enriched BPs in TED included response to hypoxia, response to decreased oxygen levels, immune response-activating signaling pathway, and positive regulation of lymphocyte activation (Additional file 2: S2b). CC of GO analysis revealed enrichment in focal adhesion, protein kinase complex, endoplasmic reticulum protein-containing complex enrichment (Additional file 2: S2c), while the enriched MF categories consisted of ubiquitin protein ligase binding, intramolecular oxidoreductase activity, cytokine binding, and kinase regulator activity (Additional file 2: S2d).

In addition, we performed GSEA using Hallmark and Reactome gene sets to further explore the overall trends in gene expression variation between TED and HC (Additional file 2: S2e-f). Notably, both the hypoxia signature in Hallmark gene set and the cellular response to hypoxia in Reactome gene set were significantly enriched in TED, highlighting the presence of a hypoxic state in PBMCs of TED patients compared to HC. This finding revealed hypoxia as a promising diagnostic marker for TED. Other enriched pathways included reactive oxygen species pathway, TNFA signaling via NFKB, adipogenesis, apoptosis, glycolysis, cellular senescence, signaling by VEGF, and signaling by interleukins.

Identification of candidate hypoxia-related biomarkers for TED

Since TED is a common ocular complication of GH, it is essential to confirm that the upregulated DEGs represent a gene regulatory pattern specific to TED, rather than being influenced by GH. Hence, WGCNA was employed to identify the most relevant genes to TED, minimizing the impact of GH on gene expression variation. Based on scale independence and mean connectivity in Additional file 3: S3a-b, the soft threshold was set to four. The top 5000 genes with the highest median absolute deviation were clustered into six modules, and the gene dendrogram with color-coded modules is displayed in Additional file 3: S3c. Followingly, the correlation between gene modules and sample groups including TED, HC and GH was examined (Fig. 2c). The green module, containing 117 genes, exhibited the highest correlation with TED (r = 0.45, p = 8e-13) and negative correlations with both HC and GH. This module also displayed the highest gene significance for TED among all modules (Additional file 3: S3d), indicating that genes within the green module were highly specific and strongly associated with TED. Figure 2d illustrates a strong correlation between membership in the green module and gene significance for TED (r = 0.76, p = 2.9e-23). And 94 genes with GS > 0.1 and MM > 0.4 were identified as hub genes within the green module. KEGG analysis revealed significant enrichment in the HIF-1 signaling pathway, EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor resistance, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, and cellular senescence, further supporting a close link between the green module and TED (Additional file 3: S3e). As hypoxia was demonstrated to be a distinct feature of PBMCs in TED patients, a total of 336 hypoxia-related genes (HRGs) were retrieved from MSigDB and intersected with the 2982 DEGs and 94 hub genes from the green module. Ultimately, six genes (EGFR, PIK3CB, CREBBP, IRS2, FLT1, and TP53) were identified as candidate hypoxia-related biomarkers for TED and were further analyzed in subsequent steps (Fig. 2e).

Multiple machine learning algorithms identified key HRGs signature with diagnostic value

To discern the most relevant hypoxia-related biomarkers for diagnosing TED patients, we implemented a comprehensive machine learning pipeline to develop a hypoxia-related TED diagnostic gene signature. Primarily, all TED and HC samples after outlier removal were randomly split into training and testing sets in a 7:3 ratio. Nine independent machine learning algorithms were applied to build classification models in the training set. The baseline characteristics of the training and testing sets are summarized in Table 2, showing no significant difference in the three clinical variables. The ROC curves and corresponding AUC values of nine models across all samples, training and testing sets are displayed in Fig. 3a-c. Interestingly, the optimal model with the highest average AUC across datasets was the RF model, yielding AUC values of 0.962, 0.985, and 0.899 in the all-samples, training, and testing sets, respectively, and an average AUC of 0.949 (Fig. 3d). Residual diagnostics (Additional file 4: S4a) further confirmed that the RF model exhibited the lowest median absolute residual value among all models.

SHAP method was utilized to explain the output of RF model. The global explanation provides an overview of the model’s functionality as well as feature importance. The contribution of features to the model was evaluated using mean SHAP value and presented in a descending order: EGFR, PIK3CB, CREBBP, FLT1, IRS2, and TP53 (Fig. 3e). Besides, the beeswarm plot displayed the strength and direction of each feature influence on model prediction (Additional file 4: S4b). High expression level of EGFR, PIK3CB, CREBBP, IRS2, and TP53 increased the risk of TED. The dependence plot showed that expression levels of EGFR, PIK3CB, CREBBP, and IRS2 were positively correlated with their SHAP value, indicating their higher expression contributed positively to model predictions and was associated with an increased risk of TED (Additional file 4: S4c). In addition to global explanation, local explanation assists in understanding the decision-making process of the model at individual level. The SHAP waterfall plot revealed the contribution of 6 genes to the model prediction for the fourth TED patient (Fig. 3f). An EGFR level of 7.84, PIK3CB level of 9.18, and CREBBP level of 10.7 contributed positively to the final prediction, with SHAP values of + 0.122, + 0.066, and + 0.0581, respectively. TP53 and IRS2 also exerted positive effects, whereas FLT1 showed a negative contribution. The model output ultimately increased from the base value to 0.977, depicting the stepwise formation of prediction for this patient and thereby clarifying the decision-making process.

We adopted RF algorithm and utilized subsequent univariate logistic regression analysis to evaluate the prognostic relevance of the 6 genes. The results indicated that all of the ranked genes were statistically significant (Additional file 4: S4d). Then, we ranked the importance of candidate biomarkers using the Boruta algorithm, which demonstrated that EGFR, PIK3CB, and CREBBP had the highest variable importance (Fig. 3g). Based on their consistent statistical significance and Boruta validation, these three genes were designated as hypoxia-related TED diagnostic genes (HRTDGs). These genes were highly associated with TED and hypoxia, and possessed diagnostic value. Multivariate logistic regression analysis showed that only EGFR (OR: 1.353, 95% CI: 1.115–1.664, p = 0.003) remained significant, suggesting that higher expression level of EGFR was associated with a greater probability of TED (Additional file 4: S4e). Moreover, the three genes showed low inter-gene correlations, indicating negligible multicollinearity and enhancing the interpretability and robustness of the model (Additional file 4: S4f).

Development and validation of HRTDGS as a model for TED diagnosis

Based on the model of the three key genes, we constructed a nomogram for visualization (Additional file 5: S5a). Further, we utilized several tests to evaluate the model performance. ROC curves displayed strong predictive performance in both the training (AUC = 0.785) and testing sets (AUC = 0.905) (Fig. 4a-b). Besides, the HRTDGS model demonstrated high accuracy in distinguishing TED from GH, with an AUC of 0.935, suggesting its excellent discriminative ability (Fig. 4c). While the diagnostic performances of the three genes were respectively demonstrated in Additional file 5: S5b. Calibration curves across all three datasets revealed minimal deviation between predicted probabilities and actual outcomes, supporting the model’s predictive robustness (Fig. 4d-f). Additionally, DCA indicated that the HRTDGS model offered a higher net benefit than either treating all or none, suggesting potential clinical value in guiding intervention strategies (Fig. 4g-i). Followingly, we calculated the HRTDG score (HRTDGS) based on the normalized gene expression and gene-specific coefficients obtained from multivariate logistic regression analysis according to the following formula: HRTDGS = 0.302033 * exp (EGFR) + 0.806343 * exp (PIK3CB) + 1.373169 * exp (CREBBP). Moreover, we further validate the capacity of the model to distinguish TED patients from HC, and therefore only demographic variables (age and gender) available in both groups were included. Univariate logistic regression analysis revealed that HRTDGS was a significant risk factor for TED (OR: 2.718, 95%CI: 1.749–4.225, p < 0.001) (Fig. 4j). It remained statistically significant after being adjusted for clinical covariates including age and gender in multivariate logistic regression (OR: 2.656, 95%CI: 1.735–4.324, p < 0.001), indicating that an increase in HRTDGS was associated with a higher likelihood of TED diagnosis (Fig. 4k). After comprehensive model evaluation, we concluded that the HRTDGS model exhibits excellent performance in predicting the probability of TED diagnosis from both HC and GH comparisons.

High and low HRTDGS groups exhibited diverse immune cells infiltration and biological function

Given that HRTDGS was considered a reliable TED diagnostic model, we further investigated its association with clinical manifestations, immune status, and biological function in TED patients. 152 TED patients were categorized into high and low HRTDGS groups by median HRTDGS. The expression levels of EGFR, PIK3CB, and CREBBP was significantly elevated in the high HRTDGS group (p < 0.001) (Additional file 6: S6a). Followingly, several clinical variables were compared between the two HRTDGS groups and visualized in a heatmap (Fig. 5a). HRTDGS was found to be positively correlated with serum thyrotropin receptor antibody (TRAb) levels (p = 0.039, Fig. 5b), suggesting its potential diagnostic utility, as TRAb elevation is a recognized criterion for TED diagnosis [37]. Additionally, HRTDGS was notably higher in TED patients with abnormal free thyroxine (fT4) level (p = 0.006) (Fig. 5c), indicating a strong relationship between hypoxia-related molecular alterations and thyroid dysfunction in TED. In terms of age, HRTDGS was significantly higher in patients aged 41–60 compared to those aged 18–40 (Additional file 6: S6b). It aligns with previous findings that the peak incidence of TED in China occurs between ages 40 and 50 [3], suggesting a potential link between HRTDGS and disease susceptibility.

Next, we explored the relationship between HRTDGS and immune microenvironment in TED. CIBERSORT and ssGSEA were implemented to estimate the proportions and relative score of various immune cell types. Each cell type was compared between high and low HRTDGS groups and used to generate an immune infiltration heatmap (Fig. 5a). CIBERSORT depicted a significantly increased proportion of naïve B cells in the high HRTDGS group (Additional file 7: S7a-b). Meanwhile, ssGSEA indicated higher enrichment scores for activated CD4 + T cells, CD56dim natural killer cells, central memory CD8 + T cells, natural killer (NK) cells, and T follicular helper cells in the high HRTDGS group (Additional file 7: S7c). These results suggest that the high HRTDGS group exhibited a highly activated immune state, whereas the low HRTDGS group showed a relative increase in memory B cells, indicative of a less active immune profile. Figure 5d illustrates the correlation between EGFR, PIK3CB, CREBBP and immune cells’ scores identified via ssGSEA. Intriguingly, activated CD4 + T cells and NK cells were significantly and positively correlated with all three HRTDGs, highlighting their potential roles in TED pathogenesis. Both cell types also showed strong correlations with the overall HRTDGS (Additional file 7: S7d).

To further elucidate the biological function of HRTDGs and their contribution to disease mechanisms, we performed GSVA to compare pathway activity between high and low HRTDGS groups (Fig. 5e). The high HRTDGS group was enriched in pathways related to ERBB signaling, VEGF signaling, PI3K-Akt activation, hypoxia, TNFA signaling via NFKB, glycolysis, and NK cell mediated cytotoxicity, suggesting that these patients may be involved in inflammation, vascular remodeling, and hypoxia-related processes. In contrast, the low HRTDGS group was more associated with phosphagen metabolism, histone H2A acetylation, and cGMP biosynthesis. These findings were further validated by GSEA, which confirmed enrichment of inflammatory response, hypoxia, oxidative stress, and vasculature development gene sets in the high HRTDGS group (Additional file 8: S8a-d).

ScRNA-seq revealed the cellular distribution of HRTDGs in TED patients and HC

Public scRNA-seq data were downloaded to explore the cellular landscape of TED’s orbital connective tissue and to validate the previously observed association between HRTDGs and immune cells. After quality control, doublets removal, and ambient RNA contamination elimination (Additional file 9: S9a-b), a total of 1499 cells were retained, including 676 from TED and 823 from HC. Ten major cell types were identified, including fibroblast, endothelial cell, myeloid cell, plasma cell, CD4 + T cell, smooth muscle cell (SMC)/pericyte, NK cell, CD8 + T cell, neutrophil and B cell (Fig. 6a). Classical markers for each cell type and their expression profiles are shown in Fig. 6b. By analyzing the cell type ratios within each group, we observed an increased abundance of CD4 + T, NK and CD8 + T cells in TED sample, indicating a prominent role of T/NK cells in the pathophysiology of TED (Fig. 6c, Additional file 9: S9c).

The percentage of cells expressed and average gene expression are displayed by dot size and color. c) Bar plot comparing cell type proportions in TED and HC. (d) Density plots showing expression patterns of PIK3CB, EGFR, and CREBBP across cell types. (e) Feature plot and (f) Violin plot showing HRTDG module score across major cell types. (g) Circle plots showing EGFR signaling interactions among cell types between TED and HC. (h) Violin plots showing the expression of ligands and receptors in EGFR signaling pathway in TED and HC

To decipher the expression patterns of three HRTDGs at single-cell resolution, we applied density plots, which intuitively showed that PIK3CB and CREBBP were predominantly expressed in T/NK cells, whereas EGFR was mainly expressed in fibroblasts (Fig. 6d). Since HRTDGS was identified as a robust diagnostic model, we calculated the average expression of three genes to derive a module score and assessed the distribution across different cell types (Fig. 6e). Fibroblasts showed the highest HRTDG module score, followed by plasma cells and CD4 + T cells (Fig. 6f), emphasizing their essential involvement in the orbit. Notably, NK cells in TED also displayed higher HRTDG module score than those in HC, further supporting the immune infiltration findings and suggesting a close relationship between HRTDGS and circulating CD4 + T and NK cells (Additional file 9: S9d). Similarly, the calculated hypoxia module score was significantly elevated in TED, pointing out the key role of hypoxia and its related gene signatures in the disease microenvironment (Additional file 9: S9e-f).

To further explore cell-cell interactions, we performed intercellular communication analysis using CellChat. The relative strengths of significant outgoing and incoming signaling patterns in various cell types for TED and HC were shown in Additional file 9: S9g-h. Among these, EGFR signaling was more active in TED. In TED, CD4 + T, NK, CD8 + T, and myeloid cells all exhibited signaling interactions with fibroblasts, whereas in HC, only CD8 + T cells were observed to transmit signals to fibroblasts (Fig. 6g). Notably, CD4 + T and NK cells in TED demonstrated higher activity of AREG ligand, which bound to EGFR receptors on fibroblasts (Fig. 6h). Orbital fibroblasts are well-recognized as key pathogenic cell types in TED development [38]. Prior studies have suggested that AREG/EGFR signaling may act upstream of HIF-1α, a transcription factor involved in the differentiation of T helper 9 cells [39, 40]. Collectively, EGFR appears to play a central role in mediating crosstalk between fibroblasts and T cells in the orbits of TED patients, potentially contributing to the formation of a hypoxia microenvironment.

Discussion

Currently, TED diagnosis primarily relies on clinical manifestations, laboratory tests and imaging. However, these methods are limited in early detection, as they are ineffective in identifying potential or early-stage patients without visible ocular symptoms, who would not undergo ophthalmic imaging. Although several serum biomarkers for TED have been reported, including inflammatory proteins [41–43], metabolites [44], and neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio [45], these markers are often non-specific and fail to provide a systematic diagnostic framework, limiting their clinical applicability. To bridge this gap, we adopted a workflow of algorithm screening and clinically interpretable modeling, whereby multiple machine learning models were compared for robust feature selection, and three hypoxia-related genes (HRGs) were subsequently incorporated into a logistic regression–based diagnostic score (HRTDGS). This strategy, increasingly applied in biomarker research [46–48], yielded a novel model that demonstrated strong and independent diagnostic performance and may improve the accuracy of early TED diagnosis.

Hypoxic microenvironment within orbital connective tissues is a key pathogenic driver of TED. It prompts us to investigate whether hypoxia-related molecules in the PBMCs of TED patients can serve as specific diagnostic biomarkers. In this study, hypoxia was found to be a distinct feature in the PBMCs of TED patients compared to HC. The upregulated differential pathways in TED included hypoxia and HIF-1 signaling pathway, as well as a range of hypoxia-associated phenotypes such as angiogenesis, adipogenesis, and inflammatory response. During feature selection, we initially mitigated the influence of GH characteristics on TED-related gene expression changes using WGCNA, a widely adopted approach for identifying hub genes associated with clinical traits in high-dimensional data. We restricted candidate genes to the module most strongly associated with TED, and then refined them to six candidate biomarkers through intersection with DEGs and HRGs. Recently, multiple machine-learning algorithms have been employed to identify novel molecular biomarkers for diagnostic or prognostic assessment in diverse diseases [46, 47, 49], providing valuable insights into disease mechanism. For instance, a study utilized LASSO regression on a dataset comprising lacrimal gland transcriptomic data from 12 TED patients and 14 HCs to identify two ferroptosis-related genes and construct a nomogram [9]. Another study applied four different machine learning algorithms (LASSO, SVM, RF and logistic regression) to the same dataset to identify hypoxia-related genes and build a predictive model [50]. The generalizability and reliability of these studies were limited by the small sample size, with an AUC of only 0.692 in the validation cohort, and the clinical applicability of the model was also constrained by the fact that the model was derived from lacrimal gland rather than a more accessible sample source, like peripheral blood. It underlines the need for more robust approaches with larger sample sizes and improved predictive performance. In the present study, we addressed these limitations by integrating peripheral blood transcriptomic data with nine independent machine learning algorithms, ultimately establishing a hypoxia-related diagnostic model with high discriminative power and predictive consistency. Among nine algorithms tested, we selected the RF model to identify three key HRTDGs, as it achieved the highest AUC values in both the training and testing sets. RF enhances accuracy and robustness by aggregating multiple decision trees, and offers advantages such as high generalization capacity, anti-overfitting, and suitability for large-scale datasets [51]. In terms of clinical practicability, the HRTDGS model can be reproduced using a simple PCR-based assay, making it particularly promising for clinical translation. By leveraging peripheral blood as the sample source, the model offers clear advantages in accessibility and potential for routine clinical implementation, and its high AUC values across training, test, and TED vs. GH cohorts further support its discriminative power. Nevertheless, it is important to acknowledge that hypoxia-related markers in PBMCs might be influenced by other inflammatory or metabolic condition, or even lifestyle factors such as cigarette smoking. Smoking increases oxidative stress and reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, enhancing autoreactive T cell and autoantibody activity and elevating inflammatory mediators, thus increasing autoimmune disease risk [52]. Among patients with thyroid eye disease or other chronic inflammatory diseases, smoking would alter the circulating advanced glycation end products (RAGE) signaling as well as VEGF expression, which are both systemic hypoxia markers [53–55]. Their exact influence on peripheral hypoxic phenotype in TED remains to be fully elucidated. Future studies should incorporate comprehensive clinical information including detailed smoking history to refine feature selection and rigorously control the confounding factors, which would improve the specificity of selected hypoxia markers and the predictive power of HRTDGS model. Further, distinguishing TED from GH patients without ocular involvement would ideally provide the most clinically meaningful model. However, the number of rigorously screened GH cases in our cohort was too limited to support stable model construction. For this reason, we developed the HRTDGS based on TED vs. HC samples to enable earlier identification. Importantly, despite this limitation, the HRTDGS still demonstrated excellent performance in differentiating TED from GH (AUC = 0.935, Fig. 4c), underscoring its clinical applicability.

EGFR, PIK3CB, and CREBBP were identified as HRTDGs and used to construct the HRTDGS diagnostic model. Epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) was the only gene significantly associated with TED in both univariate and multivariate analyses. It is a transmembrane glycoprotein that binds to members of epidermal growth factor family, inducing tyrosine autophosphorylation and subsequent cell proliferation. EGFR has been shown to reduce photosensitivity in patients with systemic lupus erythematosus and promote mucosal repair in colitis [56, 57]. In TED, EGFR has been implicated in activating the proliferation of TED-derived OFs [58]. In TED orbital tissues, increased lncRNA LPAL2 directly bound to miRNA MiR-1287-5p and downregulated its expression, leading to enhanced EGFR signaling and increased expression of cell adhesion factors, which in turn activated OFs. Another study identified EGFR as a downstream target of the transcription factor, SOX9, where overactivation of the SOX9/EGFR signaling axis promoted the proliferation, migration, and transformation of TED-derived OFs [59]. Consistent with previous findings in orbital tissues, our study observed increased EGFR expression in the PBMCs of TED patients, indicating its potential as a sensitive diagnostic biomarker.

Another HRTDG, phosphatidylinositol-4,5-bisphosphate 3-kinase catalytic subunit beta (PIK3CB), was notably upregulated in TED. It encodes a catalytic isoform of PI3K, and is a core component of the PI3K-Akt-mTOR signaling pathway, which plays essential roles in cell growth, survival, motility, and morphology. Xin et al. reported a significant correlation between PIK3CB and TED-related phenotypes at DNA methylation level [60]. Furthermore, TSH has been shown to activate the PI3K-Akt signaling in fibrocytes and OFs from GH patients [61]. This ubiquitously expressed signal is involved in adipogenesis, hyaluronan accumulation, myofibroblast transdifferentiation, and fibrosis within the orbital tissues of TED [62–65]. These findings suggest that PIK3CB may not only be a useful diagnostic marker but also serve as a potential therapeutic target in TED.

CREB binding protein (CREBBP) possesses acetylation activity toward both histone and non-histone proteins, and participates in the stabilization of transcriptional complex during transcription. While CREBBP has been extensively studied in the field of neuroscience, and is indispensable for neuronal growth and synaptic maturation [66, 67]. Raychaudhuri et al. observed that prostaglandin E2 stimulation induced nuclear localization of the CREB/CREBBP/p300 complex and increased IL-6 expression in TED-derived OFs compared to dermal fibroblasts [68]. In our study, CREBBP expression was higher in TED samples than that in HCs. Furthermore, GO functional enrichment analysis identified upregulated functions such as DNA-binding transcription factor binding and transcription coregulator binding in TED, aligning with known biological roles of CREBBP. These results support the potential pathogenic relevance of CREBBP in TED, highlighting its diagnostic value. However, additional studies are needed to further clarify its regulatory mechanisms and role in TED progression.

The immune infiltration analysis revealed that activated CD4 + T cells and NK cells were significantly enriched in high HRTDGS group, which was then verified in scRNA-seq analyses. Since a higher HRTDGS indicated a greater tendency and probability of developing TED, there might be a higher proportion of activated CD4 + T cells and NK cells in the PBMCs of TED patients. Previous studies have reported that CD4 + T cells predominantly infiltrated the orbital tissues of TED patients, mediating inflammation by secreting pro-inflammatory cytokines or by stimulating B cells to produce autoantibodies [38, 69, 70]. This finding was further validated in our scRNA-seq analysis. In the peripheral immune compartment, increased CD4 + T cells infiltration has been observed in the bone marrow of TED mouse models [71], and also in the peripheral blood of TED patients, especially in the Th17 subset [72]. Additionally, a prior study uncovered a novel TED-specific CD4 + T cell population in PBMC, termed CD4 + cytotoxic T cell, characterized by inflammatory and chemotactic signatures [73]. These cells are likely to exhibit enhanced differentiation under hypoxic conditions [74], suggesting a potential pathogenic role in TED. Recent bioinformatic analyses and our scRNA data also identified elevated level of NK cells in the orbits of TED patients compared to HC [75, 76], and a positive correlation between CD4 + T cells and activated NK cells has been reported [77]. Furthermore, our scRNA-seq results demonstrated that PIK3CB and CREBBP were enriched in T/NK cells, while EGFR, which was expressed in fibroblasts, actively interacted with T/NK cell-derived signals. These findings indicate a notable association between three HRTDGs and both CD4 + T and NK cells, suggesting an immunoregulatory role of HRTDGs in TED. However, the underlying molecular mechanisms remain to be explored.

This study has certain limitations. First, despite the use of rigorous analytical methods, hypoxia-related markers in PBMCs are susceptible to various confounding factors, such as systemic inflammation or smoking. Second, the reliable external validation cohort is limited, as there are no publicly accessible transcriptomic datasets generated from peripheral blood of TED. Nonetheless, this study still employed our in-house cohort with the largest sample size of TED to date, providing a solid foundation for the development of hypoxia-related diagnostic model. Future study should focus on prospective cohorts with full-scale clinical information to strengthen the reliability and robustness of our model.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we incorporated hypoxia-related gene signature and developed a robust diagnostic strategy for TED based on integrative bioinformatic analyses and machine learning algorithms. The HRTDGS model represents a promising tool for early and accurate identification and risk stratification of potential TED patients. Furthermore, this study lays the foundation for future research on therapeutic targets, enabling improved therapeutic strategies in TED.

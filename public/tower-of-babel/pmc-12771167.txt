Noble Metal (Pt, Au)-Doped PdS2 Monolayer as a Promising Candidate for O3 Sensing in Air-Insulated Switchgears from First-Principles Investigations
Authors: Gang Wu, Zijun Guo, Si Ying, Tian Yuan, Xida Chen, Jingqi Li, Zhuo Wei, Guotai Yang, Jianliang Guo, Yujia Gong, Hao Cui
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The development of sensitive gas sensors for O3 detection is a promising approach to enable early monitoring of air-insulated switchgears (AIS). This work employing first-principles theory investigates the adsorption behaviors and sensing potential of pristine, Pt-, and Au-PdS2 monolayers in response to O3 molecules. First, the Pt- and Au-doping properties on the pristine PdS2 monolayer are illustrated. Besides, through the analysis of adsorption parameters and electronic properties, the role of noble metal doping in enhancing gas-sensing performance of PdS2-based materials is elucidated. Moreover, the impact of humidity for further promoting the O3 gas-sensing performance of three monolayers is uncovered. The findings highlight the potential of Pt- and Au-PdS2 monolayers as highly promising resistive gas sensors upon O3 detection. It is expected that our work will inspire further experimental research on PdS2-based materials with the aim of exploring their practical applications in gas sensing across a wide range of fields.

Introduction

Air-insulated switchgears (AIS) are critical components in the low- and medium-voltage power systems, playing a vital role in controlling, protecting, and isolating electrical equipment. Utilizing air as the primary insulating medium to separate live components, AIS ensures the safe and reliable transmission of electricity. In cases of insulation defects such as overload or overheating, AIS can swiftly interrupt the current, thereby safeguarding the overall power system. However, prolonged partial discharge (PD) within AIS can lead to the decomposition of air into various gaseous byproducts, with ozone (O3) being the predominant species in the early stages. As the discharge intensifies, other gases, such as nitrogen oxides and oxygen radicals, may also accumulate. Excessive air decomposition can result in AIS breakdown, posing a significant threat to the safe operation of the entire power system. Consequently, O3 has been identified as a characteristic gas and a key indicator of insulation degradation and potential equipment failure, and the detection of O3 is essential for diagnosing PD faults and preventing catastrophic failures at an early stage. , As a result, the development of rapid and sensitive gas sensors for O3 detection has emerged as a critical research area, not only enabling early monitoring of AIS but also ensuring the safety of maintenance personnel in electrical substations.

Among various gas detection methods, gas sensors utilizing nanomaterials have witnessed significant advancements in recent years, particularly with the emergence of two-dimensional (2D) materials. − Transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDs), a novel subgroup within the 2D material family, have garnered considerable attention as promising candidates for gas-sensing applications due to their unique structural and electronic properties. − Notably, 2D TMDs such as MoX2 and WX2 (X = S, Se, and Te) exhibit exceptional chemical reactivity and adsorption capabilities toward various gaseous species. − These characteristics underscore their strong potential for gas-sensing applications in diverse fields. Increasingly, PtS2 and PdS2, which consist of a Pt or Pd atomic layer sandwiched between two S atomic layers, have been identified as “noble TMDs” due to their unique indirect semiconducting properties and relatively smaller band gaps compared to MoS2 and WS2. , Recent studies have extensively explored the electronic properties and gas-sensing potential of pristine and metal-doped PtS2 monolayers, including doping with Rh, Nb, and Pd. − Thus, noble metal doping on nanomaterial surfaces has been widely recognized as an effective strategy to enhance adsorption strength and charge transfer, thereby significantly improving the sensing response of nanosensors toward target gas molecules. However, despite its promising properties, the gas-sensing performance of the PdS2 monolayer remains largely unexplored. To the best of the authors’ knowledge, there are limited reports investigating the interaction of PdS2 with gas species, leaving a significant gap in the understanding of its potential as a gas sensor. This gap warrants further exploration to provide deeper insights into the broader family of TMDs for gas-sensing applications. Moreover, recent experimental advancements have demonstrated the successful synthesis of PdS2 thin films in laboratory settings, , paving the way for its practical application in novel nanodevices. However, before experimental realization, a thorough theoretical investigation into the sensing potential and mechanisms of PdS2-based nanomaterials is essential to give foundational guidance for future experimental work.

Among various noble metals, platinum (Pt) and gold (Au) have been frequently employed as surface dopants due to their exceptional catalytic properties, which facilitate stronger gas–surface interactions and improved adsorption performance. Inspired by these advancements, this study focuses on single-atom doping of Pt or Au onto a PdS2 monolayer (short for Pt-PdS2 and Au-PdS2, respectively) to investigate its sensing potential for O3, with the purpose of developing novel sensing materials for operational status evaluation in AIS. For this end, this work systematically explores and compares the adsorption behavior, electronic properties, and sensing response of pristine, Pt-doped, and Au-doped PdS2 monolayers toward O3 using first-principles calculations. By elucidating the underlying mechanisms of O3 adsorption and charge transfer on these modified surfaces, this study aims to provide a comprehensive understanding of the role of noble metal dopants in improving the sensing capabilities of PdS2-based nanomaterials. The findings of this research not only shed light on the gas-sensing potential of noble-metal-doped PdS2 monolayers but also highlight their broader applicability as novel humidity-resistant gas sensors in various fields. This work contributes to the growing body of knowledge on noble TMD-based materials and paves the way for the development of advanced sensing devices for early fault detection and monitoring in power systems and beyond.

Computational Details

The first-principles simulations in this study, including geometric optimization and electronic structure calculations, were conducted using the DMol3 package. The exchange–correlation interactions were treated using the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) with the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) functional. To account for dispersion corrections, such as van der Waals forces and long-range interactions, the DFT-D2 method proposed by Tkatchenko and Scheffler was employed. A 10 × 10 × 1 k-point grid in the Brillouin zone was utilized for both geometric and electronic simulations to ensure computational accuracy. All geometric configurations were fully optimized with an energy convergence criterion of 10–5 Ha, and a global orbital cutoff radius of 5.0 Å was set to accurately describe the electronic properties of metal atoms.

The PdS2 supercell was constructed as a monolayer consisting of one Pd atomic layer sandwiched between two S atomic layers. A 4 × 4 × 1 supercell was employed, containing 9 Pd atoms and 18 S atoms, with a vacuum region of 20 Å along the z-direction to eliminate interactions between periodic images. Since the S atoms occupy the outermost layer in the PdS2 lattice, the doping configuration in this work is constructed by substituting a S atom with a Pt or Au atom, followed by geometric optimization to achieve stable configurations. This lattice-incorporated approach helped maintain structural integrity while introducing active sites for advantageous surface interaction between the doped atom and gas species. For gas–surface interaction studies, Mulliken population analysis was employed to quantify the charge transfer (Q T) between Au- or Pt-doped PdS2 monolayers and adsorbed gas molecules. In this context, a positive Q T value indicates electron acceptance by the monolayer, while a negative value signifies electron donation from the gas species to the monolayer. This approach provides valuable insights into the electronic interactions governing the adsorption behavior and sensing mechanisms of the modified PdS2 surfaces.

To assess the chemical stability of such two doped monolayers, vibrational analyses are conducted. The results indicate that the vibrational frequencies for the Pt- and Au-PdS2 monolayers range at 95.18 ∼839.58 and 82.11 ∼839.76 cm–1, respectively. The absence of imaginary frequencies in these ranges confirms their good chemical stability.

Besides, the calculated E form in the Pt- and Au-PdS2 systems are −0.41 and 1.17 eV, respectively. These results reveal that the Pt-doping within the PdS2 monolayer is energy-favorable and exothermic; on the other hand, Au-doping is energy-consuming and endothermic. That is, the higher energy barrier makes Au-doping more challenging to achieve compared to that with Pt-doping. However, it is important to note that a positive E form does not mean the impossibility of Au-doping within the PdS2 monolayer; rather, the external energy input would be required for its synthesis, for which the synthesis technologies include chemical vapor deposition and ion implantation. For short, the Pt-PdS2 monolayer exhibits greater stability and feasibility than the Au-doped counterpart. Moreover, this theoretical work aims to evaluate the sensing performances of two monolayers rather than the ease of their synthesis. This provides a clear and critical perspective for future experimental investigations

From the CDD of two monolayers, it is evident that there is an obvious electron depletion around the Pt dopant and a more notable electron depletion around the Au dopant. Such observations are entirely consistent with the results of the Mulliken analysis, which show positively charged values of 0.059 and 0.133 e for the Pt and Au dopant, respectively. These findings reveal coherent electronic behavior exhibited by both Pt and Au dopants within the monolayers, wherein both dopants act as electron donors. It is noteworthy that the electron-donating property of both Pt and Au atoms in the PdS2 monolayer can be attributed to their inherently strong electronegativity compared with the bonded Pd atoms. Furthermore, a larger electronegativity of the Au atom compared with the Pt atom results in a larger amount of electron donation upon the PdS2 monolayer.

To further understand the electronic properties of Au- and Pt-doping on the pristine monolayer, we calculate the band structures (BS) and orbital density of states (DOS) of the pristine and Pt- and Au-PdS2 monolayers, as shown in Figure . The BS of the pristine PdS2 monolayer reveals a band gap of 1.131 eV, with the valence band maximum (VBM) and conduction band minimum (CBM) located at different points. This indicates the indirect semiconducting nature of PdS2, which aligns well with previous studies. Additionally, the orbital DOS herein shows favorable hybridization between the Pd 4d and S 3p orbitals, highlighting the strong binding interaction between the Pd and S atoms in the pristine PdS2 monolayer. Upon the BS of Pt- and Au-PdS2 monolayers, it is found that their band gaps are 1.024 and 0.000 eV, respectively. Specifically, the VBM and CBM in the Pt-PdS2 monolayer are both located at the K point, implying its direct semiconducting property. That is, Pt-doping slightly narrows the band gap and modulates the indirect semiconducting property for the PdS2 monolayer. On the other hand, Au-doping induces a novel state at the CBM crossing the Fermi level, transforming its semiconducting property and making the system metallic. It is necessary to highlight that the metallic property is not an artifact of band gap underestimation by PBE function but is directly caused by the introduction of Au-derived defect states that pin the Fermi level within a continuous band of states, which is a strong indicator of a significant modification of the electronic structure, likely due to the introduction of dopant-induced bands crossing the Fermi level. Such distinct deformations in the BS of two doped monolayers can be attributed to the different electronic configurations and interaction strengths of Pt and Au with the PdS2 monolayer.

The orbital DOS of the Pt-PdS2 monolayer reveals that the Pt 5d states contribute significantly to the conduction band, while the valence band is mainly composed of the Pd 4d and Pt 5d states. These indicate a strong hybridization between Pt 5d and Pd 4d states, mainly at −5.3 ∼0 and 0.8 ∼1.9 eV, which not only reveal the strong binding force of Pt and Pd atoms that facilitates the formation of Pt–Pd bonds but also modifies the band structure that leads to the narrowing of the band gap. In contrast, the orbital DOS for the Au-PdS2 monolayer shows that the state at the Fermi level is composed of Au 5d and Pd 4d orbitals, leading to metallic behavior. Also, the orbital hybridization between the Au and Pt atoms can be found mainly at the VBM, at −5.7 ∼ −1.2 eV. These manifest the favorable binding force on Au–Pt bonds and the tuned electronic property for the Au-PdS2 monolayer compared with the pristine counterpart. These findings provide insights into the electronic properties of Pt- and Au-PdS2 monolayers. Moreover, we would like to mention that the spin up is symmetric with spin down in the DOS diagrams of the pristine, Pt-, and Au-PdS2 monolayers, which reveals the nonmagnetic property for such three systems. In other words, Pt- and Au-doping do not bring about any magnetic property for the PdS2 system.

Initially, we focused on the adsorption behavior of an O3 molecule on the pristine PdS2 monolayer. The O3 molecule is found to be nearly parallel to the PdS2 surface, with no significant bond formation between them. The shortest atomic distance is measured at 2.97 Å, specifically between the S and O atoms. The E ad is calculated to be −0.28 eV, a relatively small value combined with a long S–O distance, suggesting a physisorption interaction. The CDD distribution reveals a thin electron accumulation around the O3 molecule. This observation aligns with the Mulliken population analysis, which indicates that the O3 molecule acts as an electron acceptor with a charge value of −0.128 e. This behavior is consistent with previous studies, where O3 is also found to be an electron acceptor in gas–surface interactions. Collectively, these results highlight the weak interaction between the PdS2 monolayer and the O3 molecule, suggesting that the pristine PdS2 surface is not highly effective for O3 adsorption.

On the Pt-PdS2 monolayer, a distinct improvement in the adsorption performance upon the O3 molecule is observed. It is found that the O3 molecule is vertical to the PdS2 monolayer, and two O atoms are strongly bonded to the Pt dopant with a significant reduction in the adsorption distance to 2.17 Å. The E ad in this system is calculated to be −1.52 eV, which is nearly 6 times larger than that of the PdS2/O3 system, suggesting a strong chemisorption nature. The CDD distribution shows electron transfer from the Pt-PdS2 monolayer to the O3 molecule, as evidenced by electron depletion around the Pt dopant and electron accumulation around the O3 molecule. Also, the electron accumulation and depletion can both be found at the new formed Pt–O bonds, which reveals the electron hybridization and the favorable binding force between the Pt dopant and O atoms. Moreover, the Mulliken analysis further confirms the electron-accepting property of the O3 molecule with a charged value of −0.256 e after adsorption. This significant charge transfer contributes to the strong binding force and stable adsorption configuration of the O3 molecule on the Pt-PdS2 surface. In other words, Pt-doping largely facilitates the electron transfer from the Pt-PdS2 monolayer to the O3 molecule, thus enhancing the adsorption performance.

Turning to the Au-PdS2/O3 system, a similar trend can be observed, albeit with slightly weaker adsorption performance compared to the Pt-PdS2/O3 system. One can see that the O3 molecule is also vertical to the PdS2 surface, and one O atom is bonded with the Au dopant with an adsorption distance of 2.35 Å. It could be presumed that the O3 molecule is chemisorbed onto the Au-PdS2 surface as evidenced by an E ad value of −1.25 eV, which is about 4.5 times compared with the pristine counterpart. The relatively smaller absolute value of E ad, as well as the longer adsorption distance in the Au-PdS2/O3 system, accounts for the weaker adsorption performance than the Pt-PdS2/O3 system. The CDD diagram reveals the electron-releasing property of the Au-PdS2 monolayer, with electron depletion around the Au atom and electron accumulation around the O3 molecule. The Mulliken population analysis gives a charged value of −0.262 e for the adsorbed O3 molecule, further confirming its electron-accepting property similar to those interactions with the pristine and Pt-PdS2 monolayers. Besides, the electron accumulation and depletion found at the Au–O bonds reveal the favorable binding force between the Au dopant and O atoms.

Overall, both Pt- and Au-doping effectively enhance the adsorption performance of the PdS2 monolayer to the O3 molecules, with Pt-doping exhibiting the more favorable effect. Specifically, the surface-doping enhances the electron-accepting property of the O3 molecule, leading to a more favorable interaction. Also, the formation of Pt–O and Au–O bonds plays a crucial role in stabilizing the O3 molecule on doped PdS2, indicating a robust electron hybridization and binding force between the Pt or Au dopant and the O atoms. The results provide valuable insights into the potential of Pt and Au dopants within the structure of the PdS2 surface to facilitate chemisorption upon O3 molecules.

Electronic Properties of Monolayer/O3 Systems

The above analyses reveal that doping with Pt and Au can significantly promote the adsorption performance of the PdS2 monolayer for interacting with the O3 molecule. To gain a deeper understanding of the impact of O3 adsorption on the monolayers, in this section, we investigate the electronic properties of the three monolayer/O3 systems. For this end, the BS of the O3 adsorbed pristine, Pt-doped, and Au-doped PdS2 monolayers, as well as the orbital DOS of the nearest atoms in three systems, are exhibited in Figure .

Electronic properties of gas adsorbed systems. (a1, a2) PdS2/O3 system, (b1, b2) Pt-PdS2/O3 system, and (c1, c2) Au-PdS2/O3 system. The sets in BS and DOS are the same as those in Figure .

Upon O3 adsorption, noticeable changes in BS can be observed, particularly near the Fermi level. For the pristine PdS2 monolayer, the adsorption of O3 led to a somewhat downward shift of the conduction band edge, leading to a strength of the n-type semiconductor characteristics with a band gap of 0.852 eV, decreasing by 0.279 eV from that of the pure PdS2 system. In the meantime, for both Pt- and Au-doped systems, the electronic deformations are more pronounced. In the Pt-PdS2/O3 system, the adsorption of O3 results in a significant narrowing of the band gap by 0.761 eV. That is, the band gap of the Pt-PdS2/O3 system is obtained to be 0.263 eV, and there is a more pronounced downward shift of the conduction band edge, suggesting an enhanced interaction between the Pt-PdS2 and O3 molecule. Conversely, the band gap in the Au-PdS2/O3 system is calculated to be 0.567 eV, much larger than that in the pure Au-PdS2 system of 0.000 eV. Although the changes are less dramatic compared to the Pt-doped system, a noticeably downward shift of valence band edges is still observed. That is, the adsorption of O3 on the Au-PdS2 monolayer deforms its metallic property into a semiconducting property. These alterations in the BS provide evidence for the importance of doping in tuning the electronic properties of PdS2 monolayers for improved interactions with the O3 molecule.

The orbital DOS of the nearest atoms in the three systems further supports these conclusions. In the PdS2/O3 system, the DOS near the Fermi level is mainly contributed by the S 3p and O 2p states, where the VBM is mainly contributed by the S atom and the CBM is mainly contributed by the O atom. These findings not only indicate the orbital hybridization between these states but also verify the charge transfer from the S atom to the O atom. Upon the Pt-PdS2/O3 system, the DOS near the Fermi level exhibits a more prominent orbital hybridization, wherein the state contributions are mainly from the Pt 5d and O 2p states. These align with the enhanced binding force between the Pt dopant and the O atom, and the hybridizations are responsible for the narrowing of the band gap and the pronounced changes in the electronic properties. Notably, the VBM is dominantly influenced by the Pt dopant, whereas the CBM is mainly contributed by the O atom. These not only suggest the Pt-centered hole state and an O-centered electron state but also verify the charge transfer from the Pt-PdS2 monolayer to the O3 molecule. In contrast, for the Au-PdS2/O3 system, while there is also an orbital hybridization between Au 5d and O 2p states near the Fermi level, the contribution is less significant compared to that of the Pt-PdS2/O3 system. The DOS in this region shows a more balanced contribution from both Au 5d and S 3p states, with the VBM being influenced by both Au and S atoms. This indicates a weaker charge transfer and interaction between the Au dopant and the O3 molecule, which is consistent with the smaller band gap change observed in this system.

Overall, the analysis of electronic properties reveals that Pt doping is more effective in tuning the electronic structure of PdS2 monolayer for enhanced interactions with O3 molecules, while Au doping, although less pronounced, still exhibits a noticeable impact. These findings provide important insights into the potential of Pt and Au dopants in improving O3 adsorption performance of PdS2-based materials. Furthermore, it should be added that the spin up in three monolayer/O3 systems is symmetric with the spin down, which indicates the nonmagnetic property of three monolayers for O3 adsorption.

Importantly, the selectivity of these monolayers upon O3 and other characteristic gas species in the AIS is critical and should be analyzed, as well. As reported, the dominant gases in the air decomposed species are O3 and NO2, with quite limited content for other toxic gases such as SO2 and CO. Therefore, the adsorption of NO2 on three monolayers is analyzed to verify their possible selectivity, with the adsorption configurations shown in Figure S2. The calculated adsorption energies for NO2 on the pristine, Pt-, and Au-PdS2 monolayers are −0.17, −0.96, and −0.95 eV, respectively. These values indicate the significantly weaker adsorption performance of three monolayers upon NO2 compared with that of the O3. Besides, there is no bond formation in the three NO2 adsorbed systems, verifying their weak interactions as well. This notable difference in adsorption strength between NO2 and O3 indicates a low probability of NO2 adsorption compared to that of O3, especially on the doped surfaces. The substantially stronger binding of O3, particularly on the Pt- and Au-doped monolayers, provides a clear physical basis for the selective detection of O3 in the presence of NO2 interference, which could be designed with a high level of O3 sensitivity. Therefore, we directly highlight the gas-sensing performance of O3 in the following sections.

Gas Sensing Mechanisms

The aforementioned analysis elucidates that the Pt and Au dopants can substantially promote the electronic deformations within the PdS2 monolayer upon O3 adsorption, thereby demonstrating superior sensing performance compared with the pristine counterpart. By examining the band gap (B g) values obtained for these systems, insights into the variations in their electrical conductivity (σ) can be garnered, taking into account the dependency formulated in eq : 3 σ=λ·e(−Bg/2kT) wherein λ is a constant, T is the temperature, and k is the Boltzmann constant (1.38 × 10–23 J/K). From this equation, one can infer that the reduction in band gaps of the pristine and Pt-doped PdS2 monolayers suggests an enhancement in their electrical conductivity when exposed to O3, while the increase in the band gap of the Au-PdS2 monolayer implies a decrease in its electrical conductivity when exposed to O3. Also, the larger increase in conductivity can be obtained in the Pt-doped system in comparison with the pristine system, attributed to the stronger interaction between the Pt dopant and the O3 molecule and leading to the improved sensing performance. These results pave the way for the further exploration of Pt- and Au-PdS2 monolayers in the development of resistive gas sensors for O3 detection. As a resistance-type gas sensor, the gas-sensing response is formulated by 4 S=|σgas−1−σpure−1|/σpure−1 in which σgas and σpure, respectively, indicate the electrical conductivity of the gas adsorbed monolayer and the pure monolayer. This formula provides a quantitative measure of the sensor’s response to the O3, allowing for the evaluation of its sensitivity and selectivity. Associated with eqs and , the S can be further formulated by 5 S=e|ΔEg|/2kT−1 in which ΔE g is the change in band gap upon gas adsorption.

It is worth noting that the method to calculate the sensing response is a theoretical prediction for a nanomaterial-based gas sensor from the atomic-scale simulation. However, its quantitative value may not perfectly align with experimental measurements due to the idealized nature of the models, which omit complexities such as defects and device integration. Despite this, the primary value of our approach lies in revealing the basic sensing mechanism to establish structure–property relationships, thereby providing essential theoretical guidance for the design of high-performance sensors. In this work, it is calculated using eq that the sensing responses of pristine, Pt-, and Au-PdS2 monolayers upon O3 molecule are 2.28 × 102, 2.72 × 106, and 6.23 × 104, respectively, with an enhanced electrical conductivity for the pristine and Pt-doped PdS2 monolayers, whereas a decrease for the Au-PdS2 monolayer. These results show that the Pt-PdS2 monolayer possesses the most notable sensing response for O3 detection, followed by the Au-PdS2 monolayer, with the pristine PdS2 monolayer exhibiting the least response. Typically, the significantly sensing response in the Pt-PdS2/O3 system provides a theoretical indicator for the Pt-PdS2 monolayer with a low detection limit upon O3, which can be ultimately validated by future experiments. On the other hand, the Au-PdS2 monolayer conducts a relatively smaller response, accounting for a larger detection limit. Moreover, the pristine PdS2 monolayer with a limited sensing response upon the use of an O3 gas is not suggestable for exploration of a typical resistive gas sensor.

Furthermore, it is critical to state that the model to calculate the gas-sensing model serves as a unified theoretical framework for the semiquantitative comparison of the O3 sensing potential across the three materials. The underlying O3 sensing mechanisms for Pt- and Au-PdS2 monolayers are fundamentally distinct and should be interpreted through their electronic properties. For the Pt-PdS2 monolayer, the system exhibits characteristic p-type semiconducting behavior, as determined by the position of the Fermi level (−5.589 eV) closer to the CBM (−5.056 eV) than the VBM­(−6.080 eV). Upon O3 adsorption, a charge transfer from the monolayer to the molecule depletes the minority carrier (electron) in this p-type system, associated with the effectively increased hole density. Therefore, a significant enhancement of electrical conductivity in the Pt-PdS2 monolayer is obtained. In contrast, the Au-PdS2 monolayer with metallic property donates significant electrons after adsorption O3 molecule, causing a drastic depletion of electrons from these critical metallic states. This process disrupts the original conductive pathways, ultimately triggering a metal-to-semiconductor transition and manifesting as a massive increase in the electrical resistance. All of these analyses highlight the crucial role of dopant selection in modulating the sensing properties of PdS2-based 2D materials.

First, the pristine PdS2 monolayer, with its weak physisorption of O2, exhibits an exceptionally short recovery time (on the order of 10–12 to 10–14 s) across the entire temperature range. While this suggests instant recovery, it also implies poor stability and a weak sensing signal at room temperature. In contrast, the doped monolayers show a strong temperature dependence. The Pt-PdS2/O3 system requires a high temperature of 498 K to achieve a rapid recovery time of 0.15 s, making it suitable for applications where thermal annealing can be employed for sensor reset. Notably, the Au-PdS2/O3 system demonstrated a more favorable low-temperature recovery profile. It reaches a swift recovery time of 0.66 s at 398 K, and an even faster 4.40 × 10–4 s at 498 K. This indicates that the Au-PdS2 sensor can be effectively regenerated at a lower operating temperature, which is advantageous for energy-efficient and practical device operation. In conclusion, while pristine PdS2 is unsuitable for stable O3 detection, both doped systems offer viable but distinct recovery pathways: Pt-PdS2 requires higher temperatures for fast recovery, whereas Au-PdS2 achieves rapid recovery at a more practically accessible temperature of 398 K.

Specifically, no new bonds are formed between the H2O molecule and any of the three monolayers. Simultaneously, the adsorption of the O3 molecule remains largely unaffected, with the presence of bonded Pt–O and Au–O interactions still evident in the doped systems. As shown in Table , the pristine, Pt-, and Au-PdS2 monolayers demonstrate stronger adsorption performance for O3 and H2O molecules compared to a single O3 molecule. The calculated c-E ad values are −0.64, −1.83, and −1.61 eV for the pristine, Pt-, and Au-PdS2 systems, respectively. This indicates that the presence of the H2O molecule enhances the adsorption of the O3 molecule on all three monolayers. This trend is consistent with the values of Q T, which reflect the electron-accepting properties of the O3 molecule in its interaction with the monolayers. The Q T values are −0.182, −0.322, and −0.344 e for the pristine, Pt-, and Au-PdS2 systems, respectively. These results suggest that the H2O molecule enhances the electron-accepting capacity of the O3 molecule during these interactions. Conversely, the H2O molecule acts as an electron donor, releasing electrons into the monolayers. The corresponding electron transfer values are 0.029, 0.129, and 0.126 e for the pristine, Pt-, and Au-PdS2 systems, respectively. Collectively, these parameters reveal that the presence of the H2O molecule significantly promotes the adsorption performance of the three monolayers upon the O3 molecule.

Accordingly, the electronic properties of the three monolayers are modulated in the presence of a H2O molecule. BS analysis reveals that the band gaps of these monolayers are further altered in the presence of the H2O molecule, following a similar trend observed after the adsorption of the O3 molecule compared to the isolated monolayer. Specifically, the band gaps of the pristine, Pt- and Au-PdS2 monolayers are calculated to be 0.732, 0.240, and 0.645 eV, respectively, after the coadsorption of H2O and O3 molecules. These results indicate that the presence of the H2O molecule further enhances the sensing response of all three monolayers for the O3 detection. More specifically, in a dry environment, the Pt- and Au-PdS2 monolayers exhibit acceptable sensing responses for O3 detection; in a wet environment, the sensing responses of these monolayers are significantly promoted, leading to a more favorable performance for O3 detection. These findings highlight the crucial role of humidity in enhancing the sensing performance of PdS2-based materials for the O3 detection. Therefore, these results underscore the strong potential of the three PdS2-based materials for practical O3 sensing applications in real-world environments that contain humidity. Future experimental validations are strongly encouraged to further explore the practical applications of these 2D materials for sensing of O3 in real environments.

Conclusions

This paper employs first-principles theory to investigate the adsorption of O3 molecules on pristine, Pt-, and Au-PdS2 monolayers, aiming to analyze the sensing potential of PdS2-based materials and elucidate the role of noble metal doping in enhancing gas-sensing performance. Additionally, the impact of humidity on the gas-sensing performance is explored. The key findings are summarized as follows:(i)The E form for Pt- and Au-doping on pristine PdS2 monolayers are −0.41 and 1.17 eV, respectively, indicating greater stability of the Pt-doped system.(ii)The pristine PdS2 monolayer demonstrates physisorption upon O3, while Pt- and Au-doped monolayers demonstrate chemisorption.(iii)The pristine, Pt-, and Au-PdS2 monolayer exhibits sensing responses of 2.27 × 102, 2.72 × 106, and 6.23 × 104 upon O3 detection, confirming the potential of Pt- and Au-PdS2 monolayers as resistive gas sensors for O3 detection.(iv)The presence of the H2O molecule further enhances the sensing response of all three monolayers for O3 detection.

Spectral Properties of the Zeon Combinatorial Laplacian
G. Stacey Staples

Abstract
Given a finite simple graph G G on m m vertices, the zeon combinatorial Laplacian Λ \Lambda of G G is an m × m m\times m graph having entries in the complex zeon algebra ℂ ​ ℨ \mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z} . It is shown here that if the graph has a unique vertex v v of degree k k , then the Laplacian has a unique zeon eigenvalue λ \lambda whose scalar part is k k . Moreover, the canonical expansion of the nilpotent (dual) part of λ \lambda counts the cycles based at vertex v v in G G . With an appropriate generalization of the zeon combinatorial Laplacian of G G , all cycles in G G are counted by Λ \Lambda . Moreover when a generalized zeon combinatorial Laplacian Λ \Lambda can be viewed as a self-adjoint operator on the ℂ ​ ℨ \mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z} -module of m m -tuples of zeon elements, it can be interpreted as a quantum random variable whose values reveal the cycle structure of the underlying graph. The

Combinatorial properties of zeons have proven useful in problems ranging from enumerating paths and cycles in finite graphs to routing problems in communication networks. Many such enumeration problems are NP complete

Using zeon generators to define the nilpotent adjacency matrix of a finite graph allows one to count paths and cycles by considering powers of the matrix

The current paper is an extension of

Essential terminology and notation is presented in Section

Zeon matrices are viewed as

A\in{\rm Mat}(m,\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z})
is nilpotent if and only if
\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}A
is nilpotent.

Zeon Eigenvectors

A few remarks concerning linear independence of vectors in
{\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}}^{m}
are in order. A subset
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}
of
{\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}}^{m}
is said to be
linearly independent
if and only if for all coefficients
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}
,

\alpha_{1}v_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{k}v_{k}=\mathbf{0}

implies
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=\cdots=\alpha_{k}=0
.

Note that for any nonzero
v\in{\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}}^{m}
, the singleton
\{v\}
can only be linearly independent if it has at least one invertible component. That is,
\{v\}
linearly independent implies
v\notin({\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}}^{\circ})^{m}
; equivalently,
\{v\}
is linearly independent if and only if
\zeta_{[n]}v\neq\mathbf{0}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
A\in{\rm Mat}(m,\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z})
. Let
v_{1},v_{2}\in{\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}}^{m}
be eigenvectors of
A
associated with zeon eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
, respectively, where
\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}\lambda_{1}\neq\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}\lambda_{2}
. Then,
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
are linearly independent.

Proof.

Suppose, to the contrary, that for some nonzero constants
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}
, the following holds:

\mathbf{0}=\alpha_{1}v_{1}+\alpha_{2}v_{2}=(\alpha_{1}v_{1}+\alpha_{2}v_{2})\lambda_{1}.

(2.4)

Further, linearity of
A
implies

\mathbf{0}=A(\alpha_{1}v_{1}+\alpha_{2}v_{2})=\alpha_{1}\lambda_{1}v_{2}+\alpha_{2}\lambda_{2}v_{2}.

(2.5)

Subtracting (
2.5
) from (
2.4
) gives
\alpha_{2}(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})v_{2}=\mathbf{0}
.
Since,
\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}\lambda_{1}\neq\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}\lambda_{2}
, we conclude that
\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}
is invertible. Hence,
\alpha_{2}v_{2}=\mathbf{0}
, which further implies
\alpha_{1}v_{1}=\mathbf{0}
(by (
2.4
)), so that
v_{1},v_{2}\in({\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}}^{\circ})^{m}
, a contradiction.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Note that the only zeon eigenvalue that can be associated with the nullspace of matrix
A\in{\rm Mat}(m,\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z})
is zero. If
\lambda v=0
for nonzero
\lambda
, then
\lambda
is nilpotent and
v\in{{\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}}^{\circ}}^{m}
, violating the requirement that an individual eigenvector be linearly independent.

3
The Zeon Combinatorial Laplacian

For purposes of the discussion at hand, a
graph
on
n
vertices is an ordered pair
G=(V,E)
of sets. Elements of
V=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
are the
vertices
of
G
, and elements of
E\subseteq\{\{v_{i},v_{j}\}:1\leq i<j\leq n\}
are the
edges
of
G
. Two vertices
v_{i},v_{j}\in V
are
adjacent
if there exists an edge
\{v_{i},v_{j}\}\in E
.

A
k
-walk

(v_{0},\ldots,v_{k})
in a graph
G
is a sequence of vertices in
G
with
initial vertex

v_{0}
and
terminal vertex

v_{k}
such that there exists an edge

\{v_{j},v_{j+1}\}\in E
for each
0\leq j\leq k-1
. A
k
-walk contains
k
edges. A
path
is a walk in which no vertex appears more than once; equivalently, a path is a
self-avoiding
walk. A
closed
k
-walk
is a
k
-walk whose initial vertex is also its terminal vertex. A
k
-cycle
is a closed
k
-path with the exception
v_{0}=v_{k}
. A
Hamiltonian cycle
is an
n
-cycle in a graph on
n
vertices; i.e., it contains
V
.

PWICs.

For convenience, we further designate a
path with initial cycle
(PWIC) from
v_{0}
to
v_{t}
(where
v_{0}
and
v_{t}
are distinct vertices of
G
) to be a walk containing exactly one cycle based at the initial vertex
v_{0}
. In particular, PWICs are walks in which no vertex is repeated except for the initial vertex
v_{0}
, which is repeated exactly once.

The significance of PWICs will become evident in the proof of Theorem
3.2
. First, we recall the definition of the nilpotent adjacency matrix associated with a graph.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a simple graph on
n
labeled vertices
V=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
and edges
E
consisting of unordered pairs of vertices. The
nilpotent adjacency matrix of
G
is defined as the
n\times n
matrix
\Psi=(\psi{ij})
having entries in
{\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}}
determined as follows:

\psi_{ij}=\begin{cases}0&\{v_{i},v_{j}\}\notin E,\\
\zeta_{\{j\}}&\{v_{i},v_{j}\}\in E.\end{cases}

Recalling Dirac notation,
\langle i|
will denote the row vector whose entries are all zero, except the
i
th column, which is
1
. Similarly,
|j\rangle
will denote the column vector with
1
in the
j
th row and zeros elsewhere. Further, let us define
\langle\zeta_{\{i\}}|
to denote the row vector whose entries are all zero, except the
i
th column, which is
\zeta_{\{i\}}
. With this notation in mind, the following result is a refinement of one first established in
[
9
]
.

Theorem 3.2

(Nil-Structure Theorem)
.

Let
\Psi
be the nilpotent adjacency matrix of an

n
-vertex graph
G
, and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. For
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
,

\left<\zeta_{\{i\}}|\Psi^{k}|j\right>=\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{I\subseteq V}{|I|=k+1}}\omega_{I}\zeta_{I},

where
\omega_{I}
denotes the number of
k
-paths from
v_{i}
to
v_{j}
on vertices indexed by
I
. Further, when
1\leq i\leq n
,

\left<i|\Psi^{k}|i\right>=\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{I\subseteq V}{|I|=k}}\omega_{I}\zeta_{I},

where
\omega_{I}
denotes the number of
k
-cycles on vertex set
I
based at
v_{i}\in I
.

Proof.

Because the generators of
\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}
square to zero, a straightforward inductive argument shows that the nonzero terms of
\left<i|\Psi^{k}|j\right>
are multivectors corresponding to two types of
k
-walks from
v_{i}
to
v_{j}
: (i.) paths (i.e., walks with no repeated vertices) and (ii.) PWICs in which
v_{i}
is repeated exactly once at some step but are otherwise self-avoiding. For
i\neq j
, multiplication by
\zeta_{\{i\}}
cancels walks of the second type, so that nonzero terms represent paths from
v_{i}
to
v_{j}
.

When
i=j
, walks of the second type are zeroed in the
k^{\rm th}
step when the walk is closed. Hence, terms of
\left<i|\Psi^{k}|i\right>
represent the collection of
k
-cycles based at
v_{i}
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

At times, it will also be convenient to use the notation
\langle v|
or
|v\rangle
to denote the unique row (resp. column) unit vector associated with vertex
v
.

Definition 3.4

(Zeon Laplacian)
.

Let
\Psi
denote the nilpotent adjacency matrix of a simple graph
G
on
m
vertices, and let
\Delta={\rm diag}(d_{1},\ldots,d_{m})
be the diagonal matrix of vertex degrees associated with
G
. The
zeon Laplacian of
G
is defined by

\Lambda=\Delta-\Psi.

(3.1)

It is worthwhile to note that
\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}(\Lambda)=\Delta
, which is invertible if and only if all of its diagonal entries are nonzero; hence, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

The zeon Laplacian of a simple graph
G
is invertible if and only if
G
has no isolated vertices.

Given a multi-index
I
, it is convenient to define

\deg(v_{I})=\prod_{j\in I}\deg(v_{j}).

Row Sums of the Zeon Laplacian

A feature of the ordinary combinatorial Laplacian is that all row sums are zero. Such is not the case for the zeon combinatorial Laplacian. However, vertex degree is directly related to the index of nilpotency of the dual part of the row sum.

Letting
\Lambda=(\lambda_{ij})\in{\rm Mat}(\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z},m)
be the zeon combinatorial Laplacian of a graph
G
on
m
vertices, the
i
th
row sum

r_{i}
of
\Lambda
is

r_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{ij}=\deg(v_{i})-\sum_{\{j:\{v_{j},v_{i}\}\in E\}}\zeta_{\{j\}},

so that
\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}r_{i}=\deg(v_{i})
and
\mathfrak{D}\mspace{1mu}r_{i}=-\sum_{\{j:v_{j}\in\mathcal{N}(v_{i})\}}\zeta_{\{j\}}
. It follows that

(\mathfrak{D}\mspace{1mu}r_{i})^{\deg(v_{i})}=|\mathcal{N}(v_{i})|!\zeta_{I},

(3.2)

where
I=\{\ell:v_{\ell}\in\mathcal{N}(v_{i})\}
; i.e.,
I
is the set of indices of neighbors of
v_{i}
in the graph
G
. Further, the index of nilpotency of
\mathfrak{D}\mspace{1mu}(r_{i})
is
\kappa(\mathfrak{D}\mspace{1mu}(r_{i}))=\deg(v_{i})+1
.

Hence,

{r_{i}}^{-1}=\frac{1}{\deg(v_{i})}\sum_{k=0}^{\deg(v_{i})}\left(\frac{-\mathfrak{D}\mspace{1mu}(r_{i})}{\deg(v_{i})}\right)^{k}

(3.3)

and by applying Taylor series expansion about
\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}r_{i}=\deg(v_{i})
, we obtain

\exp(r_{i})=\sum_{k=0}^{\deg(v_{i})}\frac{\exp(\deg v_{i})}{k!}\mathfrak{D}\mspace{1mu}(r_{i})^{k}.

(3.4)

Laplacian Eigenvalues

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\Lambda
be the zeon combinatorial Laplacian of a simple connected finite graph
G=(V,E)
on
m
vertices, and suppose that for some positive integer
k
,
G
has a unique vertex
v_{0}
of degree
k
. Then
\Lambda
has a spectrally simple eigenvalue
\lambda
whose scalar part is
k
; i.e.,
\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}\lambda=\deg(v_{0})
.

Proof.

First, we note that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}\chi_{\Lambda}(t)=\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}|t\mathbb{I}-\Lambda|

\displaystyle=|t\mathbb{I}-\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}\Lambda|

\displaystyle=\chi_{\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}\Lambda}(t)

\displaystyle=\prod_{v\in V}^{m}(t-\deg(v))

\displaystyle=(t-\deg(v_{0}))\prod_{v\neq v_{0}}(t-\deg(v)).

Hence,
\lambda_{0}=\deg(v_{0})
is a simple zero of
\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}\chi_{\Lambda}(u)
, implying the existence of spectrally simple zero
\lambda=\lambda_{0}+\mathfrak{D}\mspace{1mu}\lambda
of the characteristic polynomial
\chi_{\Lambda}(u)
.
∎

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a simple finite graph on
m
vertices
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}\}
and suppose that for some positive integer
d
, the graph has a unique vertex
v_{k}
of degree
d
. It follows that the zeon combinatorial Laplacian
\Lambda
of
G
has a spectrally simple eigenvalue
\lambda
satisfying

\lambda=d+\sum_{I\subseteq[m]}\omega_{I}\zeta_{I}\prod_{j\in I\setminus\{k\}}(d-\deg(v_{j}))^{-1},

where
(-1)^{|I|}\omega_{I}
is the number of cycles based at
v_{k}
on vertices indexed by
I

Proof.

Begin by relabeling the vertices
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}\}
if necessary so that
v_{m}
is the unique vertex of degree
d
, and let
\Lambda
be the zeon combinatorial Laplacian of
G
after relabeling. Since
v_{m}
is the unique vertex of degree
d
, the first
m-1
main diagonal elements of
\lambda\mathbb{I}-\Lambda
are invertible, while the last main diagonal element is nilpotent. In particular, letting
d_{j}
denote the degree of vertex
v_{j}
and writing
\ell_{j}=\lambda-d_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,m-1
, we have

\lambda\mathbb{I}-\Lambda=\left(\begin{matrix}\ell_{1}&\varepsilon_{12}\zeta_{\{2\}}&\cdots&\cdots&\varepsilon_{1m}\zeta_{\{m\}}\\
\varepsilon_{12}\zeta_{\{1\}}&\ell_{2}&\varepsilon_{13}\zeta_{\{3\}}&\cdots&\varepsilon_{2m}\zeta_{\{m\}}\\
\varepsilon_{13}\zeta_{\{1\}}&\varepsilon_{23}\zeta_{\{2\}}&\ell_{3}&\cdots&\varepsilon_{3m}\zeta_{\{m\}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\varepsilon_{1m}\zeta_{\{1\}}&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\mathfrak{D}\mspace{1mu}\lambda\end{matrix}\right),

(3.5)

where
\varepsilon_{ij}
denotes the function
V\times V\to\{0,1\}
defined by

\varepsilon_{ij}=\begin{cases}1&\text{\rm if }\{v_{i},v_{j}\}\in E,\\
0&\text{\rm otherwise.}\end{cases}

It follows that by simply adding zeon multiples of rows to each other,
\lambda\mathbb{I}-\Lambda
can be placed into upper triangular form

\tau(\lambda\mathbb{I}-\Lambda)=\left(\begin{matrix}\alpha_{1}&*&\cdots&*&\eta_{1}\\
0&\alpha_{2}&\cdots&*&\eta_{2}\\
0&0&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&0&\alpha_{m-1}&\eta_{m-1}\\
0&0&\cdots&0&\eta_{m}\end{matrix}\right),

(3.6)

where
\alpha_{\ell}\in\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}^{\times}
and
\eta_{\ell}\in\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}^{\circ}
for
\ell=1,\ldots,m
. Further, since the only invertible elements of
\lambda\mathbb{I}-\Lambda
lie along the main diagonal, the only nilpotent column of
\lambda\mathbb{I}-\Lambda
is the
m
th column. Since zeros are obtained below each element along the main diagonal by adding a zeon multiple of one row to another, the determinant of the resulting matrix is unchanged.
Hence,

0=|\lambda\mathbb{I}-\Lambda|=|\tau(\lambda\mathbb{I}-\Lambda)|=\eta_{m}\prod_{\ell=1}^{m-1}\alpha_{\ell},

implying
\eta_{m}=0
.

Now set
\tau_{0}=\lambda\mathbb{I}-\Lambda
and let
\tau_{k}
be the matrix obtained after
k
steps of the triangularization process for
k=1,\ldots,m-1
.

Claim 1.

The off-diagonal elements of
\tau_{k}
are given by the following:

(\tau_{k})_{ij}=\begin{cases}0&i>j,j\leq k;\\
\varepsilon_{ij}\zeta_{\{j\}}-\sum_{I\subseteq[k]}\gamma_{I\cup\{j\}}\zeta_{I\cup\{j\}}\prod_{t\in I\cap[k]}{\ell_{t}}^{-1}&i>j>k;\\
\varepsilon_{ij}\zeta_{\{j\}}-\sum_{I\subseteq[k-1]}\gamma_{I\cup\{j\}}\zeta_{I\cup\{j\}}\prod_{t\in I\cap[i-1]}{\ell_{t}}^{-1}&i<j,j>k;\\
\varepsilon_{ij}\zeta_{\{j\}}-\sum_{I\subseteq[i-1]}\gamma_{I\cup\{j\}}\zeta_{I\cup\{j\}}\prod_{t\in I\cap[i-1]}{\ell_{t}}^{-1}&i<j\leq k;\end{cases}

(3.7)

where
(-1)^{|I\cup\{j\}|}\gamma_{I\cup\{j\}}
is the number of paths from
v_{i}
to
v_{j}
on vertices indexed by
I\cup\{j\}
(excluding the initial vertex
v_{i}
; i.e.,
i\notin I\cup\{j\}
).

Proof of the claim is by induction on
k
. When
k=0
, the result holds by definition of
\tau_{0}
. Now suppose (
3.7
) is valid for all off-diagonal entries of
(\tau_{k-1})
.
We now proceed by cases.

Case 1. (
i>j
and
j\leq k
)

Zeros are obtained below
(\tau_{k-1})_{kk}
via Gaussian elimination using the inverse
((\tau_{k-1})_{kk})^{-1}
in the usual way since we assume all diagonal entries (except possibly the last one) are invertible. This gives
(\tau_{k})_{ij}=0
whenever

and

Case 2. (
i>j>k

We consider now those entries below the main diagonal that have not yet been zeroed out. If
v_{i}
is not adjacent to
v_{k}
, then
(\tau_{k-1})_{ik}=0

(\tau_{k})_{ij}=(\tau_{k-1})_{ij}

\displaystyle(\tau_{k})_{ij}

\displaystyle=(\tau_{k-1})_{ij}-\frac{(\tau_{k-1})_{ik}}{\ell_{k}}(\tau_{k-1})_{kj}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{ij}\zeta_{\{j\}}-\sum_{I\subseteq[k-1]}\gamma_{I\cup\{j\}}\zeta_{I\cup\{j\}}\prod_{t\in I\cap[k-1]}{\ell_{t}}^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad-{\ell_{k}}^{-1}(\tau_{k-1})_{ik}(\tau_{k-1})_{kj}

\displaystyle\quad-{\ell_{k}}^{-1}\varepsilon_{ik}\varepsilon_{kj}\zeta_{\{j,k\}}

Corollary 3.9

where
\omega_{I}

on vertices indexed by
I
.
∎

of the nilpotent adjacency matrix.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let

\langle\lambda,\zeta_{I}\rangle=(-1)^{|I|}|I|!\left<\langle v|e^{\Psi}|v\rangle,\zeta_{I}\right>{\varkappa_{v}}^{-1}.

(3.13)

Equivalently,

\left<\langle v|e^{\Psi}|v\rangle,\zeta_{I}\right>=\frac{(-1)^{|I|}\langle\lambda,\zeta_{I}\rangle}{|I|!}\varkappa_{v}.

(3.14)

(3.19)

In particular, keeping in mind that
\zeta_{\{m\}}
annihilates each
\eta_{i}
,

\displaystyle\mu_{m-1}

\displaystyle=-(\alpha_{m-1})^{-1}\eta_{m-1}

\displaystyle=-(d_{m-1}-d)^{-1}\left(\varepsilon_{m-1,m}-\sum_{I\subseteq[m-2]}\gamma_{I\cup\{m\}}\prod_{t\in I\cap[m-2]}{\ell_{t}}^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\subseteq[m-1]}\gamma_{I\cup\{m\}}\zeta_{I\cup\{m\}}\prod_{t\in I\cap[m-1]}{\ell_{t}}^{-1}

where
(-1)^{|I\cup\{m\}|}\gamma_{I\cup\{m\}}
is the number of paths and PWICs from
v_{m-1}
to
v_{m}
on vertices indexed by
I\cup\{m\}
.

Assume now that for some

where

is the number of paths and PWICs from
v_{m-1}
to
v_{m}
on vertices indexed by
I\cup\{m\}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\mu_{m-(k+1)}

\displaystyle=-(d_{m-k-1)}-d)^{-1}

\displaystyle\times\left(\varepsilon_{m-k-1,m}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\sum_{I\subseteq[m-(\ell+1)]}\gamma_{I\cup\{m\}}\prod_{t\in I\cap[m-(\ell+1)]}{\ell_{t}}^{-1}\right)

where

v_{\ell}
to
v_{m}
on vertices indexed by

.

\rho_{m-1}=\left(\begin{matrix}\alpha_{1}&0&0&\cdots&-\alpha_{1}\mu_{1}\\

\vdots&\vdots&0&\alpha_{m-1}&-\alpha_{m-1}\mu_{m-1}\\

Multiplying the nonzeros rows by inverses of the diagonal elements gives the final form seen in (

).

It now follows that for each

\displaystyle=\langle\ell|\lambda\mu\rangle

Here, we have used the fact that

\mu_{\ell}=\frac{1}{d-d_{\ell}}\sum_{\{j:\{v_{j},v_{\ell}\}\in E\}}\zeta_{\{j\}}\mu_{j}.

Moreover,

implies

\displaystyle\lambda=d+\sum_{\{j:\{v_{j},v_{m}\}\in E\}}\zeta_{\{j\}}\mu_{j}.

∎

Observing that for each

, we see that the

4
Generalizations of the Zeon Combinatorial Laplacian

We now turn to generalizations of the zeon combinatorial Laplacian that enumerate all cycles of a finite graph. All eigenvalues of these generalized zeon Laplacians can be obtained from the exponential of the graph’s nilpotent adjacency matrix. Hence the characteristic polynomial itself can be computed this way.

The first approach is to simply replace vertex degrees with a convenient labeling of the graph’s vertices.

Given a finite simple graph
G
on
m
vertices
V=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{m}\}
, let
\mathbf{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\in\mathbb{N}^{m}
be an ordered
m
-tuple of distinct positive integers. Let
\Delta_{\mathbf{f}}
be the
m\times m
diagonal matrix whose main diagonal is
\mathbf{f}
. Letting
\Psi
be the nilpotent adjacency matrix of
G
, the matrix
\Lambda_{\mathbf{f}}=\Delta_{\mathbf{f}}-\Psi\in{\rm Mat}(m,\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z})
is referred to as the
\mathbf{f}
-labeled zeon combinatorial Laplacian
.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a simple finite graph on
m
vertices, and let
\mathbf{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\in\mathbb{N}^{m}
be an ordered
m
-tuple of distinct positive integers. Let
\Lambda_{\mathbf{f}}
be the associated
{\mathbf{f}}
-labeled zeon combinatorial Laplacian of
G
. Then, for each
\ell=1,\ldots,m
,
\Lambda_{\mathbf{f}}
has a spectrally simple eigenvalue
\lambda_{\ell}
satisfying

\lambda_{\ell}=f_{\ell}+\sum_{I\subseteq[m]}\omega_{I}\zeta_{I}\prod_{j\in I\setminus\{\ell\}}(f_{\ell}-f_{j})^{-1},

where
(-1)^{|I|}\omega_{I}
is the number of cycles based at
v_{\ell}
on vertices indexed by
I
.

Given a graph
G
and generalized zeon Laplacian

as described in Corollary
4.1
, each vertex
v
corresponds to a unique eigenvalue
\lambda_{v}
with zeon eigenvector (eigenstate)
\varepsilon_{v}
.

4.1
Symmetric Zeon Laplacians and a Quantum Probabilistic Interpretations of Zeon Matrices

Recalling that self-adjoint linear operators on Hilbert spaces can be interpreted as quantum observables, we seek an analogous interpretation of the zeon combinatorial Laplacian.

Two vectors
v_{1},v_{2}\in{\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}}^{m}
are said to be
orthogonal
if and only if
\langle v_{1}|v_{2}\rangle=0
. Note that any collection of zeon vectors
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}\}
spanning
{\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}}^{m}
can be orthogonalized via Gaussian elimination. Normalizing then yields an orthonormal basis for
{\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}}^{m}
.

Given a normalized element
v\in{\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}}^{m}
, the matrix
vv^{\dagger}
represents orthogonal projection onto
\spn(\{v\})
.

A matrix
A\in{\rm Mat}(m,\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z})
satisfying
A^{\dagger}=A
is clearly self-adjoint with respect to the inner product (
2.1
). Eigenvalues of self-adjoint zeon matrices are elements of the real zeon algebra
\mathfrak{Z}_{n}
; i.e.,
\chi_{A}(\lambda)=0
implies
\lambda=\overline{\lambda}
when
A
is self-adjoint. Further, eigenvectors of self-adjoint zeon matrices associated with distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal. Given distinct eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
associated with eigenvectors
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
, respectively,

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\langle v_{1}|v_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle Av_{1}|v_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle v_{1}|Av_{2}\rangle=\lambda_{2}\langle v_{1}|v_{2}\rangle,

implying
\langle v_{1}|v_{2}\rangle=0
since
\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}\lambda_{1}\neq\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}\lambda_{2}
.

Zeon Spectral Theorem.

Let
A\in{\rm Mat}(m,\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z})
be a self-adjoint zeon matrix with
m
spectrally simple eigenvalues. Let
v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}
denote normalized zeon eigenvectors associated with these eigenvalues and set
\pi_{j}=v_{j}{v_{j}}^{\dagger}
for
j=1,\ldots,m
. Then,

A=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}\pi_{j}.

Given finite simple graph
G
on
m
vertices, let
\Delta_{*}
denote any
m\times m
invertible diagonal matrix with distinct diagonal entries
d_{1},\ldots,d_{m}\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}
, and let
\Psi_{*}=\Psi+\Psi^{\dagger}
, where
\Psi
is the nilpotent adjacency matrix of
G
. Then
\Delta_{*}-\Psi_{*}
is symmetric and diagonalizable and has spectral decomposition
\Delta_{*}-\Psi_{*}=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\pi_{j}
for eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}
and associated eigenspace projections
\pi_{j}
.

Letting
\Lambda_{*}=\Delta_{*}-\Psi_{*}
, it follows immediately that
\Lambda_{*}
is symmetric and therefore self-adjoint with respect to the inner product on
{\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}}^{m}
defined by

\langle x|y\rangle=y^{\dagger}x.

Note that
\Psi\Psi^{\dagger}=\mathbf{0}
. As an immediate consequence,

(\Psi+\Psi^{\dagger})^{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}{\Psi^{\dagger}}^{j}\Psi^{k-j}

for each positive integer
k
. Moreover,
\Psi^{\ell}=\mathbf{0}\Leftrightarrow{\Psi^{\dagger}}^{\ell}=\mathbf{0}
, so that
\kappa(\Psi)=\kappa(\Psi^{\dagger})
.

Observing that
\langle v_{i}|\Psi|v_{j}\rangle=\langle v_{j}|\Psi^{\dagger}|v_{i}\rangle
, the next result follows immediately from Theorem
3.2
.

Lemma 4.2

(Nil-Structure Theorem for
\Psi^{\dagger}
)
.

Let
\Psi
be the nilpotent adjacency matrix of an

m
-vertex graph
G
, and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. For
1\leq i\neq j\leq m
,

\left<v_{i}|{\Psi^{\dagger}}^{k}|\zeta_{\{j\}}\right>=\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{I\subseteq V}{|I|=k+1}}\omega_{I}\zeta_{I},

where
\omega_{I}
denotes the number of
k
-paths from
v_{j}
to
v_{i}
on vertices indexed by
I
. Further, when
1\leq i\leq m
,

\left<v_{i}|{\Psi^{\dagger}}^{k}|v_{i}\right>=\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{I\subseteq V}{|I|=k}}\omega_{I}\zeta_{I},

where
\omega_{I}
denotes the number of
k
-cycles on vertex set
I
based at
v_{i}\in I
.

Having an understanding of both
\Psi
and
\Psi^{\dagger}
, attention is now given to the symmetric nilpotent adjacency matrix
\Psi+\Psi^{\dagger}
.

Theorem 4.3

(Symmetric Nil-Structure Theorem)
.

Let
\Psi
be the nilpotent adjacency matrix of an

m
-vertex graph
G
, and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. For
1\leq i\neq j\leq m
,

\left<\zeta_{\{i\}}|(\Psi+\Psi^{\dagger})^{k}|j\right>=\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{I\subseteq V}{|I|=k+1}}\omega_{I}\zeta_{I},

where
\omega_{I}
denotes the number of
k
-paths from
v_{i}
to
v_{j}
on vertices indexed by
I
. Further, when
1\leq i\leq m
,

\left<i|(\Psi+\Psi^{\dagger})^{k}|i\right>=2\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{I\subseteq V}{|I|=k}}\omega_{I}\zeta_{I},

where
\omega_{I}
denotes the number of
k
-cycles on vertex set
I
based at
v_{i}\in I
.

Proof.

Observing that
\Psi\Psi^{\dagger}=\mathbf{0}
, we see that for any positive integer
k
,

(\Psi+\Psi^{\dagger})^{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}{\Psi^{\dagger}}^{j}\Psi^{k-j}.

By construction, all nonzero entries in the
i
th row of
\Psi^{\dagger}
are
\zeta_{\{i\}}
, so that
\langle\zeta_{\{i\}}|\Psi^{\dagger}=\mathbf{0}
for each
i
. Thus, when
i\neq j
,

\displaystyle\left<\zeta_{\{i\}}|(\Psi+\Psi^{\dagger})^{k}|j\right>

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\left<\zeta_{\{i\}}|{\Psi^{\dagger}}^{\ell}\Psi^{k-\ell}|j\right>

\displaystyle=\left<\zeta_{\{i\}}|\Psi^{k}|j\right>

\displaystyle=\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{I\subseteq V}{|I|=k+1}}\omega_{I}\zeta_{I},

where
\omega_{I}
denotes the number of
k
-paths from
v_{i}
to
v_{j}
on vertices indexed by
I
.

Write
\Psi=(\psi_{ij})
and
\Psi^{\dagger}=({\psi^{\dagger}}_{ij})
, where
{\psi^{\dagger}}_{ij}=\psi_{ji}
. When
1\leq j\leq m-1
, it follows that for each
i=1,\ldots,m
,

\displaystyle\langle i|{\Psi^{\dagger}}^{j}\Psi^{k-j}|i\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}{{\psi^{\dagger}}^{j}}_{i\ell}{\psi^{k-j}}_{\ell i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}{\psi^{j}}_{\ell i}{\psi^{k-j}}_{\ell i}

\displaystyle=0

because
\zeta_{\{i\}}
annihilates both
{\psi^{j}}_{\ell i}
and
{\psi^{k-j}}_{\ell i}
. Hence, the only nonzero terms along the main diagonal of
(\Psi+\Psi^{\dagger})^{k}
occur in the cases
j=0
and
j=k
. Accordingly, we have

\displaystyle\langle i|(\Psi+\Psi^{\dagger})^{k}|i\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\langle i|{\Psi^{\dagger}}^{j}\Psi^{k-j}|i\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle i|\Psi^{k}|i\rangle+\langle i|{\Psi^{\dagger}}^{k}|i\rangle

\displaystyle=2\sum_{|I|=k}\omega_{I}\zeta_{I},

where
\omega_{I}
denotes the number of
k
-cycles based at
v_{i}
on vertices indexed by
I
.
∎

Observing that
\Lambda^{\dagger}=\Delta-\Psi^{\dagger}
, the eigenvectors of
\Lambda^{\dagger}
are characterized in similar fashion to those of
\Lambda
. Assuming
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
\Lambda^{\dagger}
associated with vertex
v_{m}
and following the approach of Theorem
3.12
,
\Lambda^{\dagger}
has an eigenvector of the form
\nu=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{m-1},1)^{\intercal}
where
\nu_{i}\in{\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}_{m}}^{\circ}
for
i=1,\ldots,m-1
. It follows that for each
\ell=1,\ldots,m-1

\langle\nu_{\ell},\zeta_{I}\rangle=\gamma_{I}(d-d_{\ell})^{-1}\prod_{j\in I}(d-d_{j})^{-1},

(4.1)

where
(-1)^{|I|}\gamma_{I}
denotes the number of paths and PWICs
v_{m}\to v_{\ell}
on vertices indexed by
I
. Hence,
\nu_{\ell}=\displaystyle\sum_{I\neq\varnothing}\langle\nu_{\ell},\zeta_{I}\rangle\zeta_{I}
, providing an algebraic representation of all paths and PWICs
v_{m}\to v_{\ell}
in the graph
G
.

Given the zeon combinatorial Laplacian
\Lambda=\Delta-\Psi
of a simple graph
G
, we define the
symmetric zeon combinatorial Laplacian of
G
to be the matrix
\Lambda_{\rm sym}=\Delta-(\Psi+\Psi^{\dagger})
. When
G
has a vertex
v
of unique degree,
\Lambda_{\rm sym}
has a zeon eigenvalue
\lambda_{v}
that can be characterized in terms of cycles based at
v
as follows.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\Lambda=\Delta-\Psi
be the zeon combinatorial Laplacian of a simple graph on
m
vertices, and let
\Lambda_{\rm sym}=\Delta-(\Psi+\Psi^{\dagger})
. If
\lambda
is the zeon eigenvalue of
\Lambda
associated with a vertex of unique degree
d=\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}\lambda
, then
\lambda_{*}=\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}\lambda+2\mathfrak{D}\mspace{1mu}\lambda
is the unique zeon eigenvalue of
\Lambda_{\rm sym}
satisfying
\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}\lambda_{*}=\mathfrak{C}\mspace{1mu}\lambda
.

Proof.

As in the proof of Theorem
3.7
, let us consider the reduced row echelon form of
\lambda_{*}\mathbb{I}-\Lambda_{\rm sym}
, assuming the last vertex,
v_{m}
, is associated with
\lambda_{*}
. Writing
\zeta_{i\oplus j}=\zeta_{\{i\}}+\zeta_{\{j\}}
for ease of notation and setting
\ell_{i}=(d-d_{i})+\mathfrak{D}\mspace{1mu}\lambda_{s}
, we begin with

\lambda_{*}\mathbb{I}-\Lambda_{\rm sym}=\left(\begin{matrix}\ell_{1}&\varepsilon_{12}\zeta_{1\oplus 2}&\cdots&\cdots&\varepsilon_{1m}\zeta_{1\oplus m}\\
\varepsilon_{21}\zeta_{2\oplus 1}&\ell_{2}&\varepsilon_{23}\zeta_{2\oplus 3}&\cdots&\varepsilon_{2m}\zeta_{2\oplus m}\\
\varepsilon_{31}\zeta_{3\oplus 1}&\varepsilon_{32}\zeta_{3\oplus 2}&\ell_{3}&\cdots&\varepsilon_{3m}\zeta_{3\oplus m}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\varepsilon_{m1}\zeta_{m\oplus 1}&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\mathfrak{D}\mspace{1mu}\lambda_{*}\end{matrix}\right).

(4.2)

As before,
\varepsilon_{ij}
is defined by

\varepsilon_{ij}=\begin{cases}1&\text{\rm if }\{v_{i},v_{j}\}\in E,\\
0&\text{\rm otherwise.}\end{cases}

Once again, the only invertible elements lie along the main diagonal, and the matrix can be put in the following form without changing the determinant:

\phi(\lambda_{*}\mathbb{I}-\Lambda_{\rm sym})=\left(\begin{matrix}\alpha_{1}&*&\cdots&*&\eta_{1}\\
0&\alpha_{2}&\cdots&*&\eta_{2}\\
0&0&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&0&\alpha_{m-1}&\eta_{m-1}\\
\varepsilon_{m1}\zeta_{m\oplus 1}&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\mathfrak{D}\mspace{1mu}\lambda_{*}\end{matrix}\right),

(4.3)

where
\alpha_{\ell}\in\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}^{\times}
for
\ell=1,\ldots,m-1
, and
\eta_{\ell}\in\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}^{\circ}
for
\ell=1,\ldots,m
.

In the last step of the triangularization, we obtain

\tau(\lambda_{*}\mathbb{I}-\Lambda_{\rm sym})=\left(\begin{matrix}\alpha_{1}&*&\cdots&*&\eta_{1}\\
0&\alpha_{2}&\cdots&*&\eta_{2}\\
0&0&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&0&\alpha_{m-1}&\eta_{m-1}\\
0&\cdots&\cdots&0&\eta_{m}\end{matrix}\right),

(4.4)

where
\eta_{m}=0
since
0=|\lambda_{*}\mathbb{I}-\Lambda_{\rm sym}|=\eta_{m}\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}\alpha_{j}
.

Notably,

\eta_{m}=\mathfrak{D}\mspace{1mu}\lambda_{*}-\sum_{\{j:\{v_{j},v_{m}\}\in E\}}\zeta_{m\oplus j}\eta_{j},

(4.5)

so that our work will be finished by showing
\sum_{\{j:\{v_{j},v_{m}\}\in E\}}\zeta_{m\oplus j}\eta_{j}=2\mathfrak{D}\mspace{1mu}\lambda
.

Claim.

For
j=1,\ldots,m-1
,

\eta_{j}=\varepsilon_{jm}\zeta_{j\oplus m}-2\sum_{I\subseteq[j-1]}\gamma_{I\cup\{m\}}\zeta_{I\cup\{m\}}\prod_{t\in I\cap[j-1]}{\ell_{t}}^{-1},

(4.6)

where
(-1)^{|I|\cup\{m\}}\gamma_{I\cup\{m\}}
is the number of paths and PWICs from
v_{j}
to
v_{m}
on vertices indexed by
I\cup\{m\}
.

Proof of claim.

Any nonzero off-diagonal entry of
\Lambda_{\rm sym}
is an element of the form

\langle i|\Lambda_{\rm sym}|j\rangle=\zeta_{i\oplus j}.

In light of the symmetric nil-structure theorem, such an entry can be interpreted as the sum of two one-step paths in the graph:
\zeta_{\{i\}}
represents a one-step path from
v_{j}\to v_{i}
, while
\zeta_{\{j\}}
represents a one-step path from
v_{i}\to v_{j}
. Since the graph is undirected, walks
v_{i}\to v_{j}
are in one-to-one correspondence with walks
v_{j}\to v_{i}
. The rest follows
mutatis mutandis
from Cases 3 and 4 of Theorem
3.7
. This concludes the proof of the claim.

Observing that
\zeta_{i\oplus m}\zeta_{m\oplus i}=2\zeta_{\{i,m\}}
and substituting (
4.6
) into (
4.5
), the following now becomes apparent:

\displaystyle\eta_{m}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{D}\mspace{1mu}\lambda_{*}-\sum_{\{j:\{v_{j},v_{m}\}\in E\}}\zeta_{m\oplus j}\eta_{j}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{D}\mspace{1mu}\lambda_{*}-\sum_{\{j:\{v_{j},v_{m}\}\in E\}}\zeta_{m\oplus j}\zeta_{j\oplus m}-2\sum_{I\subseteq[j-1]}\left[\gamma_{I\cup\{m\}}\zeta_{I\cup\{m\}}\prod_{t\in I\cap[j-1]}{\ell_{t}}^{-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{D}\mspace{1mu}\lambda_{*}-2\sum_{\{j:\{v_{j},v_{m}\}\in E\}}\left[\zeta_{\{j,m\}}-\sum_{I\subseteq[j-1]}\gamma_{I\cup\{m\}}\zeta_{I\cup\{m\}}\prod_{t\in I\cap[j-1]}{\ell_{t}}^{-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{D}\mspace{1mu}\lambda_{*}-2\sum_{I\subseteq[m]}\omega_{I}\zeta_{I}\prod_{j\in I\setminus\{m\}}(d-d_{j})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{D}\mspace{1mu}\lambda_{*}-2\mathfrak{D}\mspace{1mu}\lambda.

Since
\eta_{m}=0
, the proof is complete.
∎

The eigenvectors of
\Lambda_{\rm sym}
are characterized below.

Eigenvectors of
\Lambda_{\rm sym}

Let
\lambda_{*}
denote the eigenvalue of the symmetric zeon Laplacian
\Lambda_{\rm sym}
associated with vertex

\xi
be the unit eigenvector satisfying

Claim.

For each

, the following equality holds:

\langle\xi|\zeta_{\{j\}}\rangle=\langle\zeta_{\{m\}}\xi|j\rangle.

Equivalently,

\langle(\zeta_{\{m\}}-\zeta_{\{j\}})\xi|j\rangle=0

for each

. Hence,
\zeta_{\{m\}}-\zeta_{\{j\}}\in{\rm Ann}_{\mathfrak{Z}}\langle\xi|j\rangle
for each

.

Letting

and writing

, we now see that

\displaystyle\lambda_{*}

\displaystyle=d+\langle\Lambda_{\rm sym}\xi|m\rangle

\displaystyle=d+\sum_{\{j:\{v_{j},v_{m}\}\in E\}}(\zeta_{\{m\}}+\zeta_{\{j\}})\xi_{j}

\displaystyle=d+2\zeta_{\{m\}}\sum_{\{j:\{v_{j},v_{m}\}\in E\}}\xi_{j}

\displaystyle=d+2\sum_{\{j:\{v_{j},v_{m}\}\in E\}}\zeta_{\{j\}}\xi_{j}.

Letting
\nu
denote the unit eigenvector of

satisfying

and

\mathfrak{D}\mspace{1mu}\lambda_{*}=2\mathfrak{D}\mspace{1mu}\lambda

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}\mspace{1mu}\lambda_{*}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\{j:\{v_{j},v_{m}\}\in E\}}\zeta_{\{j\}}\nu_{j}.

Proposition 4.5

(Eigenvectors of the Symmetric Zeon Laplacian)
.

Let
\Lambda
denote the zeon combinatorial Laplacian of a graph
G=(V,E)
on
m

\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}\}
, such that

is of unique degree (i.e., label)

be the zeon eigenvalue of
\Lambda
associated with vertex

, and let
\nu\in\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}^{m}
denote the associated unit eigenvector whose

\Lambda_{\rm sym}
has eigenvalue
\lambda_{*}=d+2\mathfrak{D}\mspace{1mu}\lambda
with associated unit eigenvector

satisfying

\zeta_{\{k\}}\xi=\Gamma\nu,

(4.7)

where
\Gamma={\rm diag}(\zeta_{\{1\}},\zeta_{\{2\}},\ldots,\zeta_{\{m\}})

Proof.

Observing that

\Gamma\nu=\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\zeta_{\{1\}}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\ddots&0&0\\
\vdots&\cdots&\zeta_{\{m-1\}}&0\\
0&\cdots&0&\zeta_{\{m\}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\nu_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\nu_{m-1}\\
\nu_{m}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\zeta_{\{1\}}\nu_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\zeta_{\{m-1\}}\nu_{m-1}\\
\zeta_{\{m\}}\nu_{m}\end{pmatrix},

the result is established by showing that
\zeta_{\{k\}}\xi_{\ell}=\zeta_{\{\ell\}}\nu_{\ell}
for each
\ell=1,\ldots,m
. Since
\nu_{k}=\xi_{k}=1
, the result is trivial when
\ell=k
. Assuming
\ell\neq k
, we see that the
\ell
th entry of
\xi
represents sums of PWICs
v_{k}\to v_{\ell}
and
v_{\ell}\to v_{k}
by Theorems
3.12
and
4.4
. Multiplication by
\zeta_{\{k\}}
eliminates both paths and PWICs
v_{\ell}\to v_{k}
and eliminates PWICs

4.1.1
The
\mathbf{q}
-Zeon Combinatorial Laplacian

Finally, we turn to a self-adjoint
\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}
-linear operator whose eigenvalues characterize all cycles in a simple graph.

Definition 4.7

(
\mathbf{q}
-Zeon Combinatorial Laplacian)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a simple graph on
m
vertices
E=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}\}
, and let
\mathbf{q}=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{m})\in{\mathbb{Q}^{+}}^{m}
satisfy
i\neq j\Rightarrow q_{i}\neq q_{j}
. We define the
zeon
\mathbf{q}
-combinatorial Laplacian of
G
to be the matrix
\Lambda_{\mathbf{q}}=\Delta_{\mathbf{q}}-(\Psi+\Psi^{\dagger})
, where
\Delta_{\mathbf{q}}={\rm diag}(\mathbf{q})
.

The matrix
\Lambda_{\mathbf{q}}
can now be considered a quantum observable. The values taken by the observable are the zeon eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}
, each associated with a unique vertex of the graph. The pure states of
\Lambda_{\mathbf{q}}
are the normalized eigenvectors
\xi_{j}\in\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}^{m}
associated with the eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}
.

Letting
\xi_{j}\in\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{Z}^{m}
be a normalized eigenvector associated with the vertex
v_{j}\in V
, the
expectation of
\Lambda_{\mathbf{q}}
in the state
\xi_{j}
is given by

\langle\xi_{j}|\Lambda_{\mathbf{q}}|\xi_{j}\rangle=q_{j}+2\sum_{I\subseteq[m]}\omega_{I}\zeta_{I}\prod_{\ell\in I\setminus\{j\}}(q_{j}-q_{\ell})^{-1},

where
(-1)^{|I|}\omega_{I}
is the number of cycles based at
v_{j}
on vertices indexed by
I
.

As seen in Proposition
4.5
, the eigenvalues and associated eigenvectors of the symmetric zeon combinatorial Laplacian can be derived from the corresponding values associated with the underlying zeon Laplacian,
\Lambda
. These values, in turn, can be obtained by examining powers of the underlying nilpotent adjacency matrix.

5
Concluding Remarks

Beginning with a simple graph
G=(V,E)
on
m
vertices, we define the zeon combinatorial Laplacian
\Lambda=\Delta-\Psi
associated with
G
in terms of the nilpotent adjacency matrix
\Psi
of
G
. In this paper, we have established the following results.

1.

When
G
has a vertex
v
of unique degree
d
, the zeon Laplacian
\Lambda
of
G
has a unique zeon eigenvalue
\lambda
whose scalar part is
d
and whose dual part enumerates the cycles based at
v
.

2.

The eigenvector associated with an eigenvalue of
\Lambda
can be characterized in terms of paths and PWICs terminating at that vertex.

3.

Cycles, paths, and PWICs comprising the eigenvalues and eigenvectors can be computed using powers (or exponentials) of the graph’s nilpotent adjacency matrix.

4.

Eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the symmetric zeon combinatorial Laplacian have been characterized in terms of eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the general zeon combinatorial Laplacian.

5.

The zeon
\mathbf{q}
-combinatorial Laplacian can be viewed as a quantum observable whose values enumerate cycles contained in the associated graph.

5.1
Avenues for Further Exploration

With these results in hand, a number of ideas come to mind for further research. Some are included below.

1.

Graph processes.

By considering sequences of zeon combinatorial Laplacians, limit theorems might be established for sequences of graphs evolving over time.

2.

Quantum algorithms.

Since symmetric zeon Laplacians can be regarded as quantum random variables, one is naturally led to consider their potential applications in developing quantum algorithms for solving graph-theoretical problems.

3.

Graph colorings and/or enumeration problems in colored graphs.

Paths and cycles in colored graphs have been studied in a number of works over the years
[
1
,
2
,
4
,
14
]
. Zeon-algebraic formulations of graph coloring problems were first developed in
[
13
]
. Spectral properties of zeon matrices used in graph coloring problems are natural topics for study.

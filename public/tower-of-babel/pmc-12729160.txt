LONMF: a non-negative matrix factorization model based on graph Laplacian and optimal transmission for paired single-cell multi-omics data integration
Authors: Mengdi Nan, Qing Ren, Yuhan Fu, Xiang Chen, Guanpeng Qi, Liugen Wang, Jie Gao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe rapid development of single-cell sequencing technologies has provided a robust technical support for the efficient resolution of multiple levels of molecular information from a single-cell population. However, the data produced by these technologies often contain a lot of noise and differences in characteristics that make it difficult to integrate and analyze single-cell multi-omics data. In this study, there is a growing demand for methods to integrate single-cell multi-omics data, which is expected to enhance the ability to reveal cellular heterogeneity and provide new biological perspectives for a deeper understanding of cellular phenotypes by jointly analyzing multi-omics data. We propose LONMF, a non-negative matrix factorization algorithm combining graph Laplacian and optimal transmission to enhance clustering performance and interpretability. We apply LONMF to visualize and cluster multi-pair single-cell multi-omics data, including 10X-multi-group, CITE-seq, and TEA-multi-group seq, to facilitate marker characterization and gene ontology enrichment analysis and to provide rich biological insights for downstream analyses. Our comprehensive benchmarking demonstrates that LONMF exhibits comparable performance compared with the current state-of-the-art in cell clustering and outperforms other methods in terms of biological interpretability.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12859-025-06301-2.Keywords: Single-cell multi-omics, Paired data, Non-negative matrix decomposition, Data integration, Optimal transmission, Graph laplacian

Introduction

With the rapid development of single-cell multi-omics technologies, we can simultaneously capture and integrate multiple molecular features of the genome, transcriptome, and proteome at the single-cell level [1–4]. These technologies have significantly improved our ability to resolve cellular states while deepening our understanding of the complexity of biological systems [5–9]. In particular, acquiring paired multi-omics data with multiple molecular signatures from the same cell opens up new possibilities for integrative analyses. These paired multi-omics data have helped us to reveal the dynamics of cellular functions and states and have facilitated in-depth exploration of cellular heterogeneity, gene regulation, and developmental mechanisms [10–13]. Due to the high dimensionality and complexity of single-cell multi-omics data, there is an urgent need to develop analytical methods capable of integrating these paired data to accurately capture the synergistic relationships between molecular levels. Based on this, integration methods developed in recent years can accurately capture associations between multi-omics levels, such as regulatory networks of gene expression or protein functions concerning genomic changes [14, 15], while significantly improving the accuracy of analyses and reducing the noise and bias of traditional methods.

To this end, many advanced methods for integrating unpaired and paired single-cell multi-omics data have been developed [16–21]. For unpaired single-cell multi-omics data integration methods, such as LIGER [16], integral non-negative matrix factorization (iNMF) is employed to integrate data. Due to the non-convex nature of the problem, results may be sensitive to initialization. This method identifies shared cell types across individuals, species, and modalities (e.g., gene expression, epigenetics, spatial transcriptomics) while capturing dataset-specific features, enabling unified analysis of heterogeneous single-cell data. GLUE [17] is a modular integration framework that handles unpaired single-cell multi-omics data and infers cross-omics regulatory relationships. By explicitly modeling regulatory networks, GLUE establishes biologically intuitive connections across different omics feature spaces. eMCI [18] is an interpretable deep neural network-based multimodal integration model that fuses scRNA-seq and spatial transcriptomics data. It excels in cell type classification and deconvolution tasks, identifying key spatial features through attribution analysis and revealing cell type-specific and cell communication spatiotemporal patterns. For paired single-cell multi-omics data integration methods, such as MOFA + [19], which reconstructs a low-dimensional data representation using multi-omics factor analysis and efficient variational inference, while supporting flexible sparsity constraints for joint modeling across multiple sample groups and modalities. Cobolt [20] is a multimodal variational autoencoder (MVAE) based approach to generate modality-independent unified representations for the combined analysis of paired multi-omics data. Furthermore, Mowgli [21] is an approach that effectively integrates paired multi-omics data by combining non-negative matrix decomposition with optimal transmission strategies, thereby enhancing the clustering performance and interpretability of the results. These innovative approaches for paired multi-omics data can more efficiently mine inter-cell shared and complementary information than traditional unpaired data integration methods, thereby greatly advancing the theoretical and practical progress of single-cell multi-omics data integration.

Non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) [22] is commonly used in single-cell multi-omics data integration to reduce high-dimensional data into a low-dimensional space, enabling the extraction of biologically interpretable features. Various extended NMF models have also been developed. For example, JNMF [23] projects heterogeneous data into a unified coordinate system, facilitating the construction of multidimensional modules; sNMF [24] incorporates semi-orthogonal constraints to enhance data representation and improve the interpretability of clustering results; and JSNMF [25] integrates transcriptomic and epigenomic data, using consensus map fusion to address the discrepancy between feature scales and noise levels. However, classical NMF is inherently limited by its linearity assumptions, which limits its ability to model complex nonlinear relationships and higher-order interactions in multi-omics data.

The Laplace matrix is essential for the integration and analysis of single-cell multi-omics data. By constructing inter-cell graph structures, it captures similarities between samples, thereby revealing the data’s local geometric features and global topological relationships. Taking paired multi-omics analysis as an example, this method not only identifies shared features among cells but also preserves the modality specificity of the data. Particularly suited for high-dimensional, high-noise datasets, it constructs low-dimensional embeddings that reflect cellular heterogeneity, significantly enhancing the accuracy and biological interpretability of integrated analyses. In this paper, we present a new non-negative matrix factorization algorithm, LONMF, based on graph Laplacian and optimal transmission. This algorithm is designed for integrative analysis of paired single-cell multi-omics data. The method not only more efficiently captures the similarity between single-cell histological modalities to construct complex relationships between modalities but also reveals the high-dimensional geometric structure of each histology in the original data. The superior clustering performance of the method, as demonstrated by its effectiveness in analyzing datasets such as PBMC 10X, the Liu dataset, and CITE-seq, indicates its superiority over other state-of-the-art methods. The examination of the three modal datasets by LONMF not only accurately distinguishes the inter-specificity of immune cells but also yields profound biological insights into the classification of immune cell subpopulations.

Materials and methods

Data preprocessing

The data preparation stage of this study is conducted using the Python packages Scanpy [26] and Muon [27]. The LONMF preparation of single-cell RNA sequencing gene expression profiles proceeds as follows: initially, quality control, gene filtering, and cell filtering based on bespoke criteria are performed. Subsequently, the gene expression matrix is normalized and log-transformed, and the genes exhibiting the greatest variability are identified. The preparation of scATAC-seq expression profiles mirrors that of scRNA-seq, with the the following steps: initially, quality control of cells and peak and cell filtering are conducted according to bespoke criteria. Subsequently, the peak expression matrix is normalized and log-transformed, and peaks exhibiting high variability are identified. The preparation of scADT-seq expression profiles involves solely normalizing the data without implementing quality control, protein, or cell filtering. Due to the biological and technical nature of the datasets, the first 1500 highly variable genes and 1500 peaks are selected for the Liu dataset, the first 2500 highly variable genes and 5000 peaks are selected for the PBMC 10X(RNA + ATAC) dataset, and for the TEA dataset, 2500 highly variable genes and 15,000 peaks are selected. Similarly, for the OPCITE and PBMC 10X(RNA + ADT) datasets, the first 2500 highly variable genes and 15,000 peaks are assigned.

In a variety of data environments, LONMF performs well in cell embedding and clustering tasks

We apply LONMF to the Liu dataset to evaluate its embedding and clustering performance, while several data transformations are performed for different distribution scenarios of the three groups of cells. To more realistically simulate the distribution scenarios of the three groups of cells, we apply different transformations to a simple dataset consisting of scCAT-seq. The scCAT-seq dataset provides a combined analysis of scRNA and scATAC for the HCT116, HeLa-S3, and K562 cell lines. Unlike simulated data, this approach avoids making assumptions about the data distribution. Generating simulated data according to a Gaussian distribution, for example, will tend to favor methods that approximate single-cell data with a similar distribution.

We design three scenarios to model realistic challenges in multi-omics integration: (i) RNA mixing and mixing of the two scenarios, including two types of cell populations, one of which is mixed in scRNA but completely segregated in scATAC, and the other of which is mixed in scRNA but well segregated in scATAC, or mixed in scATAC but well segregated in scRNA; (ii) rare cell populations, i.e., cell populations that are significantly less numerous than other cell populations; (iii) 82\% sparse, 90\% sparse, and 96\% sparse. Scenario (i) is used to assess the ability of different methods to handle the complementarity of multi-omics data; scenario (ii) is used to test the effectiveness of the methods in recovering rare cell populations; and scenario (iii) is used to test the robustness of the methods to cope with dropout noise while preserving the true pattern of loss in single-cell data. LONMF enhances biological interpretability and identifies key immune cell subpopulations in TEA-seq data through cell-type-specific factors

We benchmark LONMF on the TEA-seq dataset of human PBMCs to evaluate its biointerpretability. The TEA-seq dataset is a single-cell, multi-omics dataset that contains information on gene expression, chromatin accessibility, and tagged proteins and provides an opportunity for method testing to fully account for the complementarity of molecular regulatory hierarchies. We independently apply MOFA + , Mowgli, and LONMF to the three histological integration analyses of the TEA-seq data and cluster the embedded results generated by each of the three methods. Since cellular annotations are not provided in the dataset, we annotate the clustered results with cell types in combination with gene and protein markers (Fig. 5a, b). This process successfully identifies major immune cell types, including CD4T cells, CD8T cells, B cells, natural killer (NK) cells, MAIT T cells, monocytes, and erythroid cells. Notably, the consistency of the cell type annotation results of MOFA + , Mowgli, and LONMF is as high as 98%, which is in high agreement with that of Azimuth’s RNA-based annotation results (see Fig. 5c), suggesting that each of these methods can accurately recover the expected cell types by clustering their embedded data. In addition, we quantify the performance of the LONMF factors using a specificity score based on the Mowgli definition, and the results are shown in bold in Fig. 5d. The analysis shows that LONMF performs well in establishing one-to-one associations between the factors and cell types, and the specificity scores stood out significantly, especially in NK cells, CD8T cells, and CD4T cells. The high selectivity of the LONMF factors efficiently captures the specific biological features of these cell types. Meanwhile, the specificity analysis of LONMF factors is robust to hyperparameter selection (see Supplementary Fig. S5 of Supplementary Materials 1), further validating its reliability in biological interpretability.

Conclusion and discussion

Currently, several technologies have enabled multi-omics analysis of the same set of cells, and with the rapid development of single-cell multi-omics sequencing technologies, the volume of multi-omics data from a single cell continues to increase, further emphasizing the need and urgency to develop data integration methods. Here, we propose LONMF, a non-negative matrix factorization algorithm based on graph Laplacian and optimal transfer, to integrate different sets of cell data.

We thoroughly evaluate the performance of LONMF in cell embedding and clustering tasks by selecting the PBMC 10 × dataset, the CITE-seq dataset, and the scCAT-seq dataset of cancer cell lines for testing, and performing a comprehensive comparative analysis using metrics such as ARI, silhouette coefficient, and purity coefficient. The results show that LONMF outperforms the other four methods (Mowgli, Cobolt, MOFA +, and NMF) in terms of overall performance. This further demonstrates that LONMF is able to effectively preserve the high-dimensional structure of multi-omics datasets while providing significant advantages in the integrated analysis of multi-omics data. In addition, LONMF demonstrates excellent potential in controlled experiments on cancer cell line scCAT-seq datasets, even under challenging conditions.

Second, LONMF shows good interpretability. In the PBMC 10 × dataset (RNA + ATAC), in-depth analysis of Factor 8 and Factor 10 revealed that these two factors are highly correlated with immune cells CD56 bright NK cells and memory B cells, respectively. Further Gene Ontology (GO) Biological Process (BP) enrichment analysis of these two factors shows that they are highly correlated with leukocyte-mediated immunity and immune response-regulated cell surface receptor signaling. The results show that they are closely related to key biological processes such as “leukocyte-mediated immunity” and “immune response-regulated cell surface receptor signaling”. Additionally, we evaluate LONMF’s performance on TEA-seq data, demonstrating its capability to establish one-to-one correlations between factors and cell types. The method successfully captures the unique biological characteristics of NK cells, CD8 T cells, CD4 T cells, and others. Furthermore, LONMF generates biologically meaningful results when applied to paired single-cell RNA sequencing, single-cell ATAC sequencing, and surface protein analysis data. Combined with the clinical significance of CD56, CD16, FceRI, and CD304, among others, the unique characterization of immune cell subpopulations in the immune response is revealed. In particular, the relationship between CD56 and NK cell function, the role of CD16 in antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxic responses, and the contribution of FceRI and CD304 in allergic reactions and antiviral immunity suggest that the combined analysis of these markers contributes to a more comprehensive understanding of the complexity of the immune system. With this approach, key immune cell subpopulations and biomarkers can be accurately identified, providing strong support for the design of personalized protocols for clinical immunotherapy. Meanwhile, the robustness of the hyperparameter is investigated, and the experimental results show that the clustering performance of LONMF remains stable across different instances, a finding that is validated by varying the parameter values. The computational efficiency of LONMF is shown in Supplementary Table S3 of Supplementary Materials 1. Due to its relatively high computational complexity, its operational efficiency is slightly lower compared to other methods. However, LONMF demonstrates excellence in clustering performance and biological interpretability, thereby retaining significant potential value for practical applications and subsequent analyses.

However, all benchmarks focusing on paired multi-omics integration face an important limitation, namely the lack of high-quality cell biology annotations. Although fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) is an effective stand-alone annotation method in some cases, such annotations are still scarce in the available paired multi-omics data. In addition, exploring potential regulatory relationships between different histological features can provide new perspectives and directions for model optimization and in-depth analysis. LONMF can also be extended to integrate larger datasets, potentially enhancing analytical accuracy and broadening its application potential across multiple research fields.

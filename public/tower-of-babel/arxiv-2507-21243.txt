Abstract
In top quark condensation the Brout-Englert-Higgs (BEH) boson is a t ¯ ​ t \overline{t}t bound state. With a UV completion of a single coloron exchange interaction, a recent semiclassical treatment, main ; main2 , gave a novel theory of the BEH boson as an extended object with composite scale M 0 ∼ 6 M_{0}\sim 6 TeV. Presently we obtain the semiclassical theory as an effective action, using the source/Legendre transformation techniques of Jackiw, et al. \!\!, Jackiw ; CJT , and fermion loop effects in the large- N c N_{c} limit by deploying an auxiliary field to implement the sum of leading fermion loop diagrams. The theory remains natural at the loop level, with fine tuning at the level of a few %, and the effective coupling of the 4-fermion interaction, g ¯ 0 2 \overline{g}_{0}^{2} , is significantly enhanced by quantum loops over the fundamental coloron coupling, g 0 2 g_{0}^{2} . Hence a relatively weaker “topcolor” theory can produce critical coupling in the effective BEH bound state theory.

III

We recall the results for the fermion loops that arise in the pointlike NJL model with Yukawa interaction:

\displaystyle\int_{x}\biggl(g_{0}[\overline{\psi}_{iL}(x)\psi_{R}(x)]H^{i}(x)+h.c.\biggr).

IV.1
Physical Consequences of the Improved Yukawa Coupling

The renormalized topcolor coupling in the
0^{+}
binding channel satisfies:

\displaystyle\overline{g}_{0}^{2}=\frac{g_{0}^{2}}{1-\frac{N_{c}g_{0}^{2}}{8\pi^{2}}}

g_{0}^{2}

g_{0}^{2}

and all of the induced couplings,
g_{Y}
and
\lambda
(etc.).
It is
\overline{g}_{0}^{2}
that controls
the formation of the semiclassical bound state. This amplification effect
is not unexpected, since the quantum fermion loops
are essentially NJL model effects, and these are strongly binding.
We see that this can be understood as the
formation of a resonance in the
0^{+}
as
an NJL bound state, enhancing the effective coupling
\overline{g}_{0}^{2}

as
P^{2}\rightarrow 0
.

The bound state is critical (has zero mass
\mu^{2}
) when
\overline{g}_{0}^{2}=\overline{g}_{0c}^{2}

where we determine numerically
main

\displaystyle\frac{\overline{g}_{0c}^{2}N_{c}}{8\pi^{2}}=1.06940.\qquad\text{cf. NJL model:}\;\;\frac{\overline{g}_{0c}^{2}N_{c}}{8\pi^{2}}=1

(87)

This is the criticality condition of the Schrödinger equation with a Yukawa potential
as translated into the SKG equation (note that we’ve often approximated
g_{0c}^{2}\approx 8\pi^{2}/N_{c}

using the NJL critical value in
main
).

Solutions to the SKG equation lead to the eigenvalue

GeV
2

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\overline{g}_{Y}=1=\overline{g}^{2}_{0c}\sqrt{\frac{2N_{c}}{J}}\;\phi(0)=17.236\;\phi(0)\approx 8.7548\sqrt{\frac{|\mu|}{M_{0}}},

IV.2
Physical Consequences of the Improved Quartic Coupling

For the quartic coupling
we obtain above:

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!{\lambda}\approx\frac{\overline{g}_{Y}^{4}N_{c}}{4\pi^{2}}\ln\left(\frac{M_{0}}{\mu}\right).

(91)

The log evolution is just that of a single fermion loop
and matches the result for the pointlike NJL case, with
g_{top}=\overline{g}_{Y}
.
Not surprisingly when we take the pointlike potential limit the loop
result of the bilocal theory confirms a pointlike NJL calculation
(see discussion of Section 4 of ref.(
main2
).

Experimentally, in the SM using the value of
m_{BEH}\approx 125
GeV and
v_{weak}\approx 175
GeV,
we find
\lambda\approx 0.25
.
In the old pointlike NJL top condensation model the quartic coupling
was determined by the RG with “compositeness boundary conditions,”
where we obtained (running-down from the Landau pole at
M_{0}
to
|\mu|
), the result
\lambda\sim 1
.
This is too large and leads to predicted
m_{BEH}\sim 260
GeV. Indeed, the quartic coupling is generally problematic for NJL based theories of a composite BEH boson.

However, in the present bilocal scheme owing to suppression of
\overline{g}_{Y}\propto\phi(0)
, the quartic coupling
is also suppressed
\propto|\phi(0)|^{4}
and is now generated in RG running, from a value
of zero at
M_{0}=6
TeV, down to
|\mu|\sim 88
GeV, using the induced

\overline{g}_{Y}
. Keeping only the
\overline{g}_{Y}^{4}
contribution
we would obtain numerically from eq.(
91
):

\displaystyle\lambda\approx(\overline{g}_{Y}^{4})\frac{N_{c}}{4\pi^{2}}\ln\left(\frac{M_{0}}{|\mu|}\right)\approx 0.321\;\;\;\makebox{(cf., $0.25$ experiment.)};

(92)

This result is significantly better than the old NJL top condensation model.
However, prefactor at one loop level should reflect the full RG running of
\lambda
,
(see, e.g.,
HillTA
), which would yield at leading log approximation:

\displaystyle\lambda\approx(\overline{g}_{Y}^{4}-\overline{g}_{Y}^{2}\lambda-\lambda^{2})\frac{N_{c}}{4\pi^{2}}\ln\left(\frac{M_{0}}{|\mu|}\right)\approx 0.230\;\;\;\makebox{(cf., $0.25$ experiment.)};

(93)

This includes the
\lambda^{2}
term which arises from loops with propagating internal BEH boson.
For the present composite model this not likely to be correct, due to the extended size of the BEH wave-function.
The terms of order
\overline{g}_{Y}^{2}\lambda
are leg-renormalizations, due to the top quark loop and presumably can
be retained. This would imply a result:

\displaystyle\lambda\approx(\overline{g}_{Y}^{4}-\overline{g}_{Y}^{2}\lambda)\frac{N_{c}}{4\pi^{2}}\ln\left(\frac{M_{0}}{\mu}\right)\approx 0.243\;\;\;\makebox{(cf., $0.25$ experiment.)};

(94)

Note that since we only know

M_{0}
to precision of order
\sim 6\;\pm\;1
TeV, we expect the

\lambda
prediction is uncertain by
\sim\pm 0.01
.

IV.3
Summary

We have used the techniques of Jackiw,
et al.

\!\!,

Jackiw
;

to
obtain formally the effective action for the semiclassical third generation fields
\psi_{L},\psi_{R}

in a single coloron exchange interaction field theory of topcolor.
This describes the Brout-Englert-Higgs boson as a composite object with constituents that are
the semiclassical fields composed of

This marks a departure from the old RG improved NJL

Hydrogenic, containing a subset of the low momentum modes in the semiclassical theory.
This gives us the internal wave-function
\phi(r)
. Tuning the theory within
a few percent of the critical coupling yields a hierarchy that defines a low mass
BEH bound state. We have given the central results in the companion paper
main

and addressed the most significant quantum effects presently.
Here we have introduced an auxiliary field,
B_{i}
, which consolidates the main large-

Arrhythmogenicity of monophasic and biphasic PFA waveforms in a porcine model
Authors: Hanife Tugba Kumru, Lars Mattison, Khaldoun G Tarakji, Atul Verma, Daniel C Sigg
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe potential risk of inducing ventricular arrhythmias was explored by employing pulsed field ablation (PFA) through the administration of both monophasic and biphasic waveform deliveries.MethodsPFA was applied to specific locations in the right ventricle (RV, n = 5 sites) in swine (n = 2), utilizing identical settings with consistent amplitude, pulse width, and a number of pulses for both monophasic and biphasic waveforms. PFA deliveries were precisely timed in 10‐ms intervals across the entire T wave.ResultsUsing monophasic waveforms, ventricular fibrillation (VF) was induced 7/7 times when PFA pulses were delivered during the T wave. Biphasic waveforms resulted in no ventricular arrhythmias when PFA was delivered across the entire duration of T wave.ConclusionsOur findings indicate that VF can be consistently induced with monophasic pulse waveforms, but not with biphasic pulse waveforms in a porcine model.Keywords: arrhythmia, electroporation, pulsed field ablation, R‐wave gating

INTRODUCTION/BACKGROUND

Pulsed field ablation (PFA) is a novel energy form for cardiac ablation based on irreversible electroporation (IRE). Permeabilization of cell membranes after application of pulsed electric fields causes cell death. IRE selectively targets cell membranes, preserving the extracellular matrix. This allows for the protection of vital structures like blood vessels and nerves. Previous electroporation literature has highlighted the need for R wave gating to minimize the risk of cardiac arrythmias with monophasic pulse trains. In a study on IRE near the heart, ECG synchronization is proposed as a preventive measure for ventricular arrhythmias during the procedure, aiming to enhance safety. 1

Although current clinical evidence mainly supports PFA's efficacy in atrial fibrillation and the isolation of pulmonary veins, there is a growing interest in extending the application of this energy modality to other cardiac sites, including the ventricles. Little is known about the arrhythmogenic potential of PFA in the ventricles. While biphasic pulse wave deliveries are considered less arrhythmogenic than monophasic pulse waves, 2 no direct comparison between monophasic and biphasic deliveries has been conducted. The goal of the present investigation is to compare the arrhythmogenic potential of an atrial loop catheter (PulseSelect™, Medtronic) delivering either biphasic, bipolar (i.e., commercial waveform) or monophasic, bipolar (i.e., noncommercial waveform) PFA energy to porcine ventricles.

METHODS

Swine (n = 2, 68.1 kg and 65.4 kg) were anesthetized. After gaining vascular access, arterial pressures and ECGs (limb leads) were monitored and recorded. No antiarrhythmic drugs were administered. A PulseSelect™ 25 mm loop catheter (nine (9) 3 mm electrodes with 3.75 mm spacing) was advanced to the right ventricle (RV). PFA energy (1500 V baseline to peak, single pulse trains) was delivered relative to the R wave (Accusync 72, Accusync Medical Research Corporation), in 10 ms increments starting just before the T wave until after the T wave or until ventricular fibrillation (VF) was induced. Following the induction of VF, the animal was resuscitated using external defibrillation delivered via patch electrodes with the catheter left in position.

The delivery sequence consisted of a monophasic pulse train delivery followed by a biphasic pulse train delivery, then another monophasic pulse train delivery at two RV positions (RV free wall); and a monophasic pulse train delivery followed by a biphasic pulse train delivery at three additional RV positions (RV free wall (n = 2), RVOT (n = 1)), all without moving the catheter at a given position. Catheter position and catheter stability were verified under fluoroscopy. Catheter contact was verified via fluoroscopy and initial observation of EGMs. Specifically, monophasic pulses were delivered until either VF occurred or past the T‐wave. Then subsequently biphasic deliveries followed in the same location until either VF occurred or past the T‐wave. In two positions, monophasic deliveries were delivered again. The delay from the R wave was recorded if fibrillation occurred.

DISCUSSION

The objective of this study was to evaluate monophasic and biphasic PFA waveforms for risk of arrhythmogenesis. The tested proprietary pulse waveforms had identical amplitude (1500 V), pulse widths, and number of pulses.

Monophasic pulses delivered during the T‐wave resulted in VF; however, biphasic pulse deliveries did not induce sustained VT or VF.

In comparison to the monopolar technique utilized by Meininger et al., 2 our study employs a bipolar delivery method. Despite this methodological difference as well as the probability of variations in pulse parameters and the delivery location, both studies share the common objective of investigating the arrhythmogenic potential of biphasic pulses during the T‐wave as opposed to monophasic one. Our findings align with Meininger et al.'s demonstrating no sustained changes to ECG waveform or cardiac rhythm. Furthermore, despite variations in delivery time, location, and pulse parameters, our findings remain consistent with Deodhar et al.'s study, 1 which demonstrated the potential for monophasic deliveries to induce VF.

These findings underscore the significance of waveform selection and its influence on the induction of arrhythmic behavior, paving the way for further investigations and potential clinical applications. The positive control of reinducing VF after delivery of biphasic pulses used in the commercial waveform demonstrates that the lack of susceptibility to VF is not due to ablation of the tissue (Table 1 and Figure 1C).

While the findings provide data supporting the safety of a biphasic PFA waveform, this study has several limitations. First, the study was limited by the inclusion of only two healthy porcine subjects. Second, the tests were conducted exclusively in the right ventricle (RV) for PFA delivery locations. To enhance the applicability of the study, future research should explore the effects of PFA in various locations within the cardiac anatomy as well as in diseased cardiac tissues. Further, only bipolar configurations with monophasic and biphasic deliveries using single pulse trains were investigated, a monopolar configuration was not tested. While this approach provides valuable insights, exploring a broader range of pulse wave characteristics and their potential impact on arrhythmogenesis would further aid in our understanding. Despite historical experiments (data not shown) showing VF induction with monophasic but not biphasic waveforms, the findings should not be generalized to all clinical applications. The observed potential safety margin for biphasic waveforms does not ensure complete safety across different settings and patient populations. Further research is essential to comprehensively understand these interactions. No anti‐arrhythmic drugs were given; in a clinical setting, anti‐arrhythmics could affect the propensity to VF. Third, we did not conduct a second run of monophasic pulse wave deliveries in the second animal. We have shown in the first animal (present study) and in previous similar experiments (data not shown) that the effect of monophasic pulse wave deliveries is not mitigated by previous ablation. Also, omitting the second monophasic pulse wave delivery reduces the risk of repeated VF inductions which may result in rapid deterioration of animal health, and repeated defibrillation shocks carry the risk of catheter dislodgement. Fourth, we did not study an inverse delivery order such as biphasic‐monophasic‐biphasic, although we believe that similar results would have been obtained with an inverse delivery sequence. Fifth, this study did not explore the possible pathogenesis of arrhythmia induction. Sixth, this study did not compare the effects of the monophasic and biphasic waveform on muscle stimulation. Lastly, even though the voltage, pulse width, and number of pulses were kept the same for all monophasic and biphasic deliveries, the total energy delivered in the monophasic pulse trains is approximately 50% of the biphasic pulse trains.

CONCLUSIONS

The findings of this study indicate that VF can be consistently induced with monophasic pulse waveforms, but not with biphasic pulse waveforms delivered via a loop catheter in a porcine model.

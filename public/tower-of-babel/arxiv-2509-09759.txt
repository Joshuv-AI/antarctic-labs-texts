Bianchi-I cosmology with scale dependent G G and Λ \Lambda in asymptotically safe gravity Preprint: NITEP 260, NU-QG-11
Chiang-Mei Chen, Akihiro Ishibashi, Rituparna Mandal, Nobuyoshi Ohta

Abstract
We study anisotropic Bianchi-I (BI) cosmology, incorporating quantum gravitational corrections into the Einstein equation through the scale-dependent Newton coupling and cosmological term, as determined by the flow equation of the effective action for gravity. For the cosmological term Λ 0 = 0 \Lambda_{0}=0 , we derive the quantum-corrected power-series solution for a general equation-of-state parameter w w in the range − 1 < w ≤ 1 -1<w\leq 1 in the form of expansions in both inverse cosmic time and the anisotropy parameter. We give a general criterion, valid for any Λ 0 \Lambda_{0} , if the solution becomes isotropic in the late time, which indicates that the universe becomes isotropic for most cases of − 1 < w < 1 -1<w<1 except w = 1 w=1 . By numerical analysis, we show that quantum corrections lead to earlier isotropization compared to the classical case starting from an initially highly anisotropic state. In contrast, for Λ 0 > 0 \Lambda_{0}>0 , we obtain the inverse power-series solution in the exponential of the cosmic time. We find that the universe always becomes isotropic in the late time, in accordance with the cosmic no hair theorem, and the quantum corrections make the isotropization faster. We also briefly summarize the Kasner solution and its generalization with quantum corrections.



I
Introduction

Cosmology provides a natural setting to explore possible signatures of quantum gravity, since it probes
the universe from its earliest stages through its late-time evolution. Among the various approaches,
asymptotic safety (AS), originally proposed in late 1970s
Weinber:1978
;
Weinberg
, attracts considerable
attention as a consistent non-perturbative framework.
Traditional perturbative quantization of gravity fails because the theory
is not renormalizable, while the effective field theory description of gravity, though useful at low energies,
ceases to be predictive near the Planck scale due to the appearance of infinitely many undetermined parameters.
The AS scenario offers a resolution by requiring that the quantum theory of
four-dimensional gravity lie within the ultraviolet critical surface of renormalization group (RG) fixed
points
Reuter:1996cp
;
Souma:1999at
. The non-trivial fixed points control the high-energy
regime, ensuring that dimensionless couplings remain finite.

The study of AS is quite often formulated by using the effective average action,
\Gamma_{k}[g_{\mu\nu}]
,
which encodes the physics of quantum fluctuations above the cutoff scale
k

Percacci:2017
;
Eichhorn:2018yfc
;
Reuter:2019book
. In the limit
k\to 0
,
\Gamma_{k}
approaches the full effective action,
where all quantum fluctuations are integrated out. The evolution of
\Gamma_{k}
is governed by
the functional renormalization group (FRG) equation, also referred to as flow equation
Wetterich:1992yh
:

k\frac{d\Gamma_{k}}{dk}=\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}\left\{\left[\Gamma_{k}^{(2)}+R_{k}\right]^{-1}k\frac{dR_{k}}{dk}\right\}

(1)

where
\Gamma_{k}^{(2)}
denotes the second functional derivative of
\Gamma_{k}
, and
R_{k}
is an infrared regulator.
See also
Morris:1993qb
.
The regulator suppresses fluctuations with
p^{2}\lesssim k^{2}
, decays rapidly for
p^{2}\gtrsim k^{2}
,
and vanishes as
k\to 0
, ensuring a smooth interpolation between microscopic and macroscopic physics.

A non-perturbative solution of the flow equation is obtained by employing “truncation”, in which
the infinite-dimensional space of all possible actions is projected onto a finite-dimensional subspace
relevant to the problem under study. In the so-called Einstein-Hilbert truncation,
the RG flow of general relativity is projected onto a two-dimensional subspace:

\Gamma_{k}=\frac{1}{16\pi G(k)}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[R(g)-2\Lambda(k)\right],

(2)

where
R(g)
is the Ricci scalar of the Riemannian metric
g_{\mu\nu}
,
g=\det(g_{\mu\nu})
,
G(k)

is the scale-dependent Newton coupling, and
\Lambda(k)
is the running cosmological term.
Their flow in
k
is determined by the FRG equations
Reuter:2001ag
;
Bonanno:2001xi
;
Codello:2008vh
.
This truncation to the Einstein and cosmological terms may be justified within the essential RG group
approach which removes inessential couplings by the field redefinition
Baldazzi:2021orb
;
Ohta:2025xxo
.

For applications to late-time cosmology, corresponding to the infrared limit
k\ll 1
, the running
couplings can be expanded in powers of
k^{2}
as

\displaystyle G(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{0}\left[1-\omega G_{0}k^{2}+\omega_{1}G_{0}^{2}k^{4}+\mathcal{O}\left(G_{0}^{3}k^{6}\right)\right],

\displaystyle\Lambda(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}\left[1-\mu G_{0}k^{2}+\mu_{1}G_{0}^{2}k^{4}+\mathcal{O}\left(G_{0}^{3}k^{6}\right)\right]+G_{0}k^{4}\left[\nu+\nu_{1}G_{0}k^{2}+\mathcal{O}(G_{0}^{2}k^{4})\right],

(3)

where the dimensionless parameters
\omega,\omega_{1},\mu,\mu_{1},\nu
and
\nu_{1}
are given in
Chen:2024ebb

in the optimized cutoff, for
\Lambda_{0}=0
, by

\omega=\frac{11}{6\pi},\quad\omega_{1}=\frac{217}{72\pi^{2}},\quad\nu=\frac{1}{8\pi},\quad\nu_{1}=\frac{7}{54\pi^{2}},

(4)

and for
\Lambda_{0}\neq 0
, they are

\displaystyle\omega=\mu=\frac{7}{6\pi},\quad\omega_{1}=\frac{49}{36\pi^{2}}-\frac{5}{24\pi G_{0}\Lambda_{0}},\quad\mu_{1}=\frac{49}{36\pi^{2}},\quad\nu=-\frac{17}{24\pi},\quad\nu_{1}=\frac{119}{144\pi^{2}}.

(5)

These values will be used when required. We note that quantum corrections to the cosmological term start
at order
k^{4}
when
\Lambda_{0}=0
, whereas for
\Lambda_{0}\neq 0
the corrections begin at order
k^{2}
.
Also Eq. (
5
) shows that we cannot take the
\Lambda_{0}\to 0
limit smoothly.
The two cases therefore represent disconnected branches, and quantum-improved cosmological
solutions must be analyzed separately for
\Lambda_{0}=0
and
\Lambda_{0}\neq 0
. Since our interest lies
in an expanding universe, we restrict our attention to
\Lambda_{0}\geq 0
. In our earlier work
Chen:2024ebb
,
we solved the scale dependence of the Newton coupling and the cosmological term directly from the FRG
equations, employing both optimized and exponential regulators, and found that the two choices yield
qualitatively similar behavior.

The Bianchi-I (BI) universe holds particular cosmological importance, as it represents the simplest
anisotropic generalization of the flat Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) metric, which
accurately describes the present universe. However, the adequacy of the isotropic model at late times
does not guarantee its applicability to the early stages of cosmic evolution. In fact, there are theoretical
arguments suggesting that an anisotropic phase may naturally evolve into an isotropic one
Misner:1967uu
.
This motivates us to study the BI universe and its evolution toward isotropy when quantum corrections
are incorporated.

Unlike the FLRW case, where the scale factors are identical in all three spatial directions, the BI universe
is characterized by three independent scale factors. Despite its apparent simplicity, obtaining analytical
solutions of the BI dynamics, even in the classical case with a general equation of state
p=w\rho
,
remains challenging.
For the BI model, two well-known solutions exist: the Kasner solution for the vacuum case
Kasner:1921zz
,
and the Heckmann-Schücking solution for dust
Heckmann:1959
. The latter has been further
generalized to include dust, stiff matter, and a cosmological term
Khalatnikov:2003ph
;
Kamenshchik:2009dt
.
The BI universe also exhibits several notable features. For matter with
p=w\rho
and
w<1
, the initial
anisotropy decays rapidly, and the model quickly approaches isotropy, effectively reducing to an FLRW
universe
Jacobs:1968
. Another important property is that, close to the singularity, the BI universe
asymptotically behaves like a vacuum Kasner solution, even in the presence of matter
Belinskii:1970
.
These characteristics have motivated a wide body of literature exploring BI cosmologies from different
perspectives
Hu:1978zd
;
Cho:1995hz
;
Chen:2000gaa
;
Saha:2001ig
;
Russell:2013oda
.
Also BI cosmological models with time-dependent
G
and
\Lambda
have been previously studied
in the presence of a perfect fluid
Beesham:1994ni
;
Kalligas:1995qh
;
Singh:2007pt
;
Pradhan:2013jg

by just assuming that the
G
and
\Lambda
are functions of cosmic time.
Within the AS approach, the BI universe has also been examined for the
\Lambda_{0}=0
branch under
the standard continuity equation framework
Mandal:2019xlg
.

In this work, we incorporate quantum effects into the Einstein equations by replacing
the classical Newton coupling and cosmological term with their scale-dependent counterparts as prescribed by FRG.
An essential step in this procedure is the identification of the RG scale with a physical quantity of
the system. As discussed in our earlier work
Chen:2024ebb
, the Hubble parameter serves as a suitable
choice for the case
\Lambda_{0}=0
, but fails for
\Lambda_{0}\neq 0
. Accordingly, we have adopted the
“improved” classical Hubble parameter, expressed in terms of the quantum-corrected scale factor,
in the
\Lambda_{0}=0
case, while a different treatment is required when
\Lambda_{0}>0
.
Another ingredient is the modified continuity equation, derived by imposing covariant conservation
of the full left-hand side of the improved Einstein equations, distinct from the ordinary
continuity equation where the energy-momentum tensor of the fluid is conserved
separately
Bonanno:2001xi
;
Bonanno:2001hi
;
Mandal:2019xlg
. This improved approach is now widely employed in
quantum cosmology
Chen:2024ebb
;
Bonanno:2007wg
;
Reuter:2003ca
;
Reuter:2004nv
;
Hindmarsh:2011hx
;
Mandal:2020umo
.

For
\Lambda_{0}=0
, we compute a power-series solution in inverse cosmic time for a general equation of state
parameter
w
. Most importantly we give the general criterion, valid for any
\Lambda_{0}
, if the universe becomes
isotropic in the late time, and show that it becomes isotropic for
-1<w<1
but not for
w=1
.
We also investigate the role of quantum corrections in the isotropization process starting from a highly
anisotropic initial state by numerically integrating the evolution equations. Our results indicate that
the quantum-corrected volume expands more rapidly than in the classical case, leading to earlier isotropization.
For
\Lambda_{0}>0
, we give the inverse power-series solution in the exponential of the cosmic time
and also study the solution numerically.
We find that the isotropy is always achieved in the late time, in accordance with the cosmic no hair theorem,
ensuring that the universe asymptotically approaches a de Sitter state,
and the quantum corrections make the isotropization faster.

This paper is organized as follows. In Sec. II, we present the improved Einstein equations for the BI universe
and outline the method used to determine the expansion rate, the volume element, and the scale factors
for both the classical and quantum-corrected cases within the AS framework.
Sec.
III
focuses on the BI cosmology with
\Lambda_{0}=0
. In Subsec.
III.1
, we discuss
some general classical and quantum solutions, and derive the classical and quantum power-series solutions
in Subsec.
III.2
. Subsec.
III.3
discusses the identification of the RG scale and the choice of
initial conditions for the BI universe. We also provide numerical solutions for the volume element to
compare the classical and quantum-corrected cases. In Sec.
IV
, we give the general criterion,
valid for any value of
\Lambda_{0}
, if the isotropization is achieved in the late time.
By this criterion, we show that the BI universe becomes isotropic for
-1<w<1
, but not for
w=1
with
\Lambda_{0}=0
.
With
\Lambda_{0}>0
, we show that the BI universe always becomes isotropic in the late time.
In Sec.
V
, we extend the analysis of BI universe to the
\Lambda_{0}>0
case,
where we summarize some of the classical exact solutions and the power-series solutions, and present numerical
results for the volume element. These results indicate that the quantum corrections accelerate the isotropization.
Finally, in Sec.
VI
, we conclude with a summary of our main findings.
Appendix
A
describes the Kasner type of solutions for classical case and their quantum extensions.

II
BI Cosmology

II.1
General discussions

In this paper, we consider the homogeneous but anisotropic BI cosmological model with the line element

ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+a_{1}^{2}(t)dx^{2}+a_{2}^{2}(t)dy^{2}+a_{3}^{2}(t)dz^{2},

(6)

where
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
and
a_{3}
represent the scale factor in three different directions which are functions of
cosmic time
t
. For this metric, the Ricci tensors and scalar curvature are given by

R_{00}=-3\dot{H}-\sum_{i=1}^{3}H_{i}^{2},\qquad R_{ii}=a_{i}^{2}\left(\dot{H}_{i}+3HH_{i}\right),\qquad R=6\dot{H}+9H^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}H_{i}^{2},

(7)

where
H_{i}
are directional Hubble parameters and
H
is their average of the three directional Hubble
parameters defined as

H_{i}=\frac{\dot{a}_{i}}{a_{i}},\qquad H=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{i=1}^{3}H_{i}.

(8)

The cosmic matter is assumed to be a perfect fluid, described by the energy-momentum tensor

T^{\mu}{}_{\nu}=\mathrm{diag}(-\rho,p,p,p),

(9)

where
\rho
is the energy density,
p
is the pressure and they are related by the equation of state

p=w\rho.

(10)

Throughout this paper, we consider normal matter with
-1<w\leq 1
.
For the study of cosmology, quantum corrections can be incorporated at the “equation-level” as

R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}Rg_{\mu\nu}=-\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}+8\pi GT_{\mu\nu},

(11)

where the couplings become scale-dependent,
G=G(k)
and
\Lambda=\Lambda(k)
, rather than constant as
in the standard formulation. By identifying the RG scale
k
with a physical scale of the system. such as
the Hubble parameter or the scale factor, both of which depend on cosmic time, then the Einstein equations
can be rewritten in the form

R_{\mu\nu}=\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}+8\pi G\left(T_{\mu\nu}-\tfrac{1}{2}Tg_{\mu\nu}\right).

(12)

For the BI metric, we write these equations in terms of the directional Hubble parameter and the average Hubble parameter

\displaystyle 3\dot{H}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}H_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Lambda-4\pi G(\rho+3p),

(13)

\displaystyle\dot{H}_{i}+3HH_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Lambda+4\pi G(\rho-p).

(14)

To proceed further, we employ the modified continuity equation, where only the full left-hand side of
the Einstein equation is covariantly conserved. Here, the matter source
T_{\mu\nu}
and the left-hand side
of the Einstein equation are not conserved separately. This leads to

D^{\mu}\left(-\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}+8\pi GT_{\mu\nu}\right)=0,

(15)

as a direct consequence of the Bianchi identity,
D^{\mu}\left(R_{\mu\nu}-\tfrac{1}{2}Rg_{\mu\nu}\right)=0
,
in Riemannian geometry.
Using Eq. (
15
), we obtain the generalized energy conservation equation for the BI metric:

\dot{\rho}+3H(\rho+p)=-\frac{8\pi\rho\dot{G}+\dot{\Lambda}}{8\pi G}.

(16)

Summing over three spatial components of the Einstein equation (
14
), we get a new equation
written in terms of averaged Hubble parameter as

\dot{H}+3H^{2}=\Lambda+4\pi G(\rho-p).

(17)

Substituting this equation back into (
14
) replacing the matter part, one can cast
Eq. (
14
) as

\dot{H}_{i}+3HH_{i}=\dot{H}+3H^{2}.

(18)

Now, this equation can be rewritten in a more convenient form

\frac{1}{V}\frac{d}{dt}(VH_{i})=\frac{1}{V}\frac{d}{dt}(VH),

(19)

where the volume element
V
, the multiplication of the scale factors in different directions is defined as

V:=a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad\frac{\dot{V}}{V}=3H.

(20)

It is pretty straightforward to calculate the directional Hubble parameters
H_{i}
from Eq. (
19
)
in terms of the average Hubble parameter and the volume element
V

H_{i}=H+\frac{\kappa_{i}}{V},

(21)

which in turn, leads us to the directional scale factors
a_{i}
in terms of
V
:

a_{i}=a_{i0}V^{1/3}\exp\left(\kappa_{i}\int V^{-1}dt\right),

(22)

where the integration constants
\kappa_{i}
and
a_{i0}
satisfy relations
\sum_{i=1}^{3}\kappa_{i}=0
and

\prod_{i=1}^{3}a_{i0}=1
.
Note that the solutions for each directional Hubble parameter are determined by the integration constants
\kappa_{i}
.
These constants are fixed by the initial values, which are the origin of the anisotropy.

Our next task is to derive the evolution equation for the total volume
V
.
Let us first obtain from (
21
) the sum of the squares of the directional Hubble parameters
in terms of the average Hubble parameter and the volume element:

\sum_{i=1}^{3}H_{i}^{2}=3H^{2}+\frac{\kappa^{2}}{V^{2}},

(23)

where
\kappa^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\kappa_{i}^{2}.

By applying the equation of state (
10
) and substituting the above expression into the Einstein
equations (
13
) and (
17
), we obtain the evolution equations for the volume element
and the energy density in the following form

\displaystyle\frac{\ddot{V}}{V}+\frac{w-1}{2}\frac{\dot{V}^{2}}{V^{2}}-\frac{3(w-1)}{4}\frac{\kappa^{2}}{V^{2}}=\frac{3(w+1)}{2}\Lambda,

(24)

\displaystyle\rho=\frac{1}{8\pi G}\left(\frac{1}{3}\frac{\dot{V}^{2}}{V^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\kappa^{2}}{V^{2}}-\Lambda\right).

(25)

These two equations reduce to isotropic FLRW cosmology for
\kappa=0
and
V=a^{3}
.

We will solve the differential equations (
24
) for the volume
V
.
The matter density
\rho
is then given by (
25
).

II.2
General solutions to (
24
)

To solve Eq. (
24
) for
V
, we define
u:=\dot{V}
, which implies
\ddot{V}=u(du/dV)
.
Substituting these into Eq. (
24
), we rewrite the equation as

\frac{du^{2}}{dV}=\frac{(1-w)(u^{2}-3\kappa^{2}/2)}{V}+3(1+w)\Lambda V,

(26)

which can be solved once
\Lambda
is identified in terms of
V
.

II.2.1
Classical solutions with constant
\Lambda_{0}

The classical solutions for the volume element
V
can be obtained by setting
G=G_{0}
and
\Lambda=\Lambda_{0}
.
We can then rewrite Eq. (
26

\frac{d(u_{\mathrm{cl}}^{2}-3\kappa^{2}/2-3\Lambda_{0}V_{\mathrm{cl}}^{2})}{dV_{\mathrm{cl}}}=\frac{(1-w)(u_{\mathrm{cl}}^{2}-3\kappa^{2}/2-3\Lambda_{0}V_{\mathrm{cl}}^{2})}{V_{\mathrm{cl}}},

(27)

which leads to the solution for the square of the expansion rate of the volume element:

u_{\mathrm{cl}}^{2}=cV_{\mathrm{cl}}^{1-w}+3\kappa^{2}/2+3\Lambda_{0}V_{\mathrm{cl}}^{2},

(28)

where
c
is an integration constant with suitable dimension. Note that the energy density (
25
)
in the classical solutions, upon substituting the above expansion rate of the volume element, is given by

\rho=\frac{cV_{\mathrm{cl}}^{-1-w}}{24\pi G_{0}}.

(29)

The energy density vanishes for
c=0
, giving the classical Kasner universe discussed in Appendix
A
,
regardless of whether the cosmological term is zero or not. Thus, at least at the classical
level, the solution for the volume element in the Kasner universe is always governed by a single integration constant
and this is so because the solution is imposed the condition
\rho=0
.

The solution for the total volume
V
as a function of cosmic time
t
is implicitly given by

t-t_{0}=\int_{{V}_{0}}^{V}\frac{dv}{u_{\mathrm{cl}}(v)}

(30)

where
{V}_{0}
is the initial volume at time
t_{0}
.

II.2.2
Quantum solutions

To study quantum case, we need the identification of the infrared cutoff scale
k^{2}
with some time-dependent quantity.
In the BI universe, the average Hubble parameter, which characterizes the overall expansion rate of the volume,
serves as a plausible choice for this identification. This suggests

\displaystyle k=3\xi H_{\mathrm{cl}}=\xi\frac{\dot{V}_{\mathrm{cl}}}{V_{\mathrm{cl}}},

(31)

which, upon substituting (
28
), gives

\displaystyle k^{2}=\xi^{2}\frac{u_{\mathrm{cl}}^{2}}{V_{\mathrm{cl}}^{2}}=\xi^{2}\left(cV_{\mathrm{cl}}^{-1-w}+\frac{3}{2}\kappa^{2}V_{\mathrm{cl}}^{-2}+3\Lambda_{0}\right),

(32)

where
\xi
is a dimensionless constant of order one.
However this has two undesirable properties.
First of all, this identification makes the Einstein equation (
24
) an “inhomogeneous”
differential equation, where the running coupling
\Lambda(k(V_{\rm cl}))
acts as a source term.
This form of the equation is generally difficult to solve analytically. In fact it is more natural, for both
physical and mathematical considerations, to consider the “improved” identification with
V_{\mathrm{cl}}(t)

replaced by
V(t)
.
This could be regarded as including the back reactions of quantum corrections and also makes
Eq. (
24
) a solvable “homogeneous” differential equation.
Second, we expect that the quantum corrections should vanish for small
k
or large
t
(
V\to\infty
),
but the nonzero cosmological term
\Lambda_{0}
in this identification does not vanish contrary to this expectation.
The second problem is absent for
\Lambda_{0}=0
.
So we propose our identification as

k^{2}=\xi^{2}\left(cV^{-1-w}+\frac{3}{2}\kappa^{2}V^{-2}\right),

(33)

for
\Lambda_{0}=0
.
For
\Lambda_{0}>0
, we discuss the identification later, but it will be in terms of the volume
V
.

Equation (
24
) now takes the form

\frac{\ddot{V}}{V}+\frac{w-1}{2}\frac{\dot{V}^{2}}{V^{2}}-\frac{3(w-1)}{4}\frac{\kappa^{2}}{V^{2}}=\frac{3(w+1)}{2}\Lambda_{0}+\sum_{i=1}\gamma_{i}V^{\lambda_{i}},

(34)

where
\gamma_{i}
and
\lambda_{i}
are constants determined by the
\Lambda(V)
in (
I
)
with the identification (
33
). This equation can be recast as a first-order differential equation
in the following form:

\frac{d\left(u^{2}-3\Lambda_{0}V^{2}-\sum_{i=1}\frac{2\gamma_{i}}{\lambda_{i}+w+1}V^{\lambda_{i}+2}\right)}{dV}=\frac{(1-w)\left(u^{2}-3\kappa^{2}/2-3\Lambda_{0}V^{2}-\sum_{i=1}\frac{2\gamma_{i}}{\lambda_{i}+w+1}V^{\lambda_{i}+2}\right)}{V}.

(35)

Solving this equation for
\lambda_{i}+w+1\neq 0
, the square of the expansion rate of the volume element
can be expressed as

u^{2}=cV^{1-w}+\frac{3}{2}\kappa^{2}+3\Lambda_{0}V^{2}+\sum_{i=1}\frac{2\gamma_{i}}{\lambda_{i}+w+1}V^{\lambda_{i}+2}.

(36)

The summation represent the quantum corrections from the running cosmological term.
The solution for the total volume
V
as a function of cosmic time
t
is again implicitly given by

t-t_{0}=\int_{\mathcal{V}_{0}}^{V}\frac{dv}{u(v)}

(37)

where we have chosen a different initial volume
\mathcal{V}_{0}
at time
t_{0}
for the quantum case.

We shall discuss the explicit solutions for
\Lambda_{0}=0
and
\Lambda_{0}>0
separately.

\displaystyle\left[(c+3\kappa^{2}/2)(t-t_{0})^{2}+2\sqrt{c+3\kappa^{2}/2}\sqrt{\mathcal{V}_{0}^{2}-3G_{0}\tilde{\nu}(c+3\kappa^{2}/2)}(t-t_{0})+\mathcal{V}_{0}^{2}\right]^{1/2}.

On the other hand, in the classical solution, we may impose different initial condition;
V=V_{0}
at

We then have the classical solution

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{cl}}=\left[(c+3\kappa^{2}/2)(t-t_{0})^{2}+2\sqrt{(c+3\kappa^{2}/2)}V_{0}(t-t_{0})+V_{0}^{2}\right]^{1/2}=\sqrt{c+3\kappa^{2}/2}(t-t_{0})+V_{0}.

In this case, each directional scale factor

\displaystyle a_{i}=a_{0i}\left[\sqrt{c+3\kappa^{2}/2}(t-t_{0})+V_{0}\right]^{1/3+\kappa_{i}/\sqrt{c+3\kappa^{2}/2}}.

(43)

Since one of the three

must have a value with the opposite sign from the other two, this solution
asymptotically describes the anisotropically expanding universe, similar to the Kasner solution in
Appendix
A
.
Note that both of these solutions have the time translation invariance and the leading term is precisely the same
since the classical term is dominant asymptotically, as expected.
To study the asymptotic behavior of the solution, let us expand this in
1/t

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{qu}}

\displaystyle\approx\sqrt{c+3\kappa^{2}/2}\ t-\left(\sqrt{c+3\kappa^{2}/2}\,t_{0}-\sqrt{\mathcal{V}_{0}^{2}-3G_{0}\tilde{\nu}(c+3\kappa^{2}/2)}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(t^{-1}\right).

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{cl}}

\displaystyle\approx\sqrt{c+3\kappa^{2}/2}\ t-\left(\sqrt{c+3\kappa^{2}/2}\,t_{0}-V_{0}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(t^{-1}\right).

(44)

The important points are (i) the coefficient of the leading term (
\sim t
) is the same as that of
the classical solution since it does not involve any quantum parameter, but
(ii) the next leading term depends on the quantum parameter

\tilde{\nu}
. Thus the difference
\Delta V:=V_{\mathrm{qu}}-V_{\mathrm{cl}}
is governed by the next leading term:

\Delta V=\sqrt{\mathcal{V}_{0}^{2}-3G_{0}\tilde{\nu}(c+3\kappa^{2}/2)}-V_{0},

(45)

which is a constant.
Note that
\mathcal{V}_{0}^{2}
should be larger than

.
In fact, there are additional higher order corrections in
1/V
with negative sign
in Eq. (
40

\mathcal{V}_{0}^{2}
should be slightly larger than this such that the rhs of Eq. (
40

is non-negative.
We shall perform the precise numerical analysis in what follows.

For
w=0

Similarly to the

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{cl}}=\frac{c}{4}(t-t_{0})^{2}+\sqrt{cV_{0}+3\kappa^{2}/2}(t-t_{0})+{V}_{0}.

In this case, each directional scale factor

The solution with quantum correction is

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{qu}}\approx\frac{c}{4}(t-t_{0})^{2}+\sqrt{c\mathcal{V}_{0}+3\kappa^{2}/2-3c^{2}G_{0}\tilde{\nu}}(t-t_{0})+\mathcal{V}_{0},

Expanding these solutions (

Again the leading term is canceled out from this difference.

On the other hand, the next leading term is affected by the initial condition.

III.2
Power series solutions for
\Lambda_{0}=0
: classical and quantum

It is generally difficult to find exact solutions for arbitrary values of the equation-of-state parameter

w\,(-1<w\leq 1)
. To proceed, we attempt to derive the classical solutions of Eq. (
24
)
for the volume element
V
in the form of a power series in cosmic time
t
.
We assume the general ansatz in the form

V(t)=A_{0}t^{s}\left(1-\frac{A_{1}}{t}-\frac{A_{2}}{t^{2}}+\cdots\right),

(51)

where the coefficients
A_{i}
are constants determined by the Einstein equation.
From Eq. (
24
) with
\Lambda=0
, the characteristic equation becomes

s(s-1)t^{-2}+\frac{w-1}{2}s^{2}t^{-2}-\frac{3(w-1)}{4A_{0}^{2}}\kappa^{2}t^{-2s}=0.

(52)

This yields two possible classes of solutions for the exponent
s
:
(i)
s=1
and the characteristic equation requires
\kappa^{2}=2A_{0}^{2}/3
(Kasner) with arbitrary
w
.
(ii)
s
is greater than unity (
s>1
, valid for
w<1
). In this case, the term
t^{-2s}
is higher order
than
t^{-2}
, and the characteristic equation requires
s=2/(w+1)
.
The power-series solution involves a “two-dimensional” expansion in both of
1/t
and
\kappa^{2}
.

For case (i) the solution can be obtained exactly which is known as Kasner solution discussed
in Appendix
A
.

For the case (ii), the general classical solutions, except
w=1/3
, can be expressed as

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{cl}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{0}(t-t_{0})^{2/(w+1)}\left[1-U-\frac{w(w+1)}{2(5w-3)}U^{2}-\frac{w(w+1)(4w^{2}+5w-3)}{6(5w-3)(7w-5)}U^{3}\right.

(53)

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{w(w+1)(162w^{5}+351w^{4}-269w^{3}-457w^{2}+423w-90)}{24(5w-3)^{2}(7w-5)(9w-7)}U^{4}+\cdots\right],

where we have defined a new variable

U=-\frac{3(1+w)^{2}\kappa^{2}}{8(3w-1)A_{0}^{2}}(t-t_{0})^{2(w-1)/(w+1)}.

(54)

There are three special cases:

(a)

For
w=1/3
,
U
diverges, and this case is a particular isometric solution
of
\kappa^{2}=0
and
V_{\mathrm{cl}}=A_{0}(t-t_{0})^{3/2}
,

(b)

For
w=1

U
becomes constant, and the solution is exact as
V_{\mathrm{cl}}=A_{0}(t-t_{0})
,

(c)

For
w=0
, all coefficients of

for

vanish,
leading again to an exact solution, given by
V_{\mathrm{cl}}=A_{0}(t-t_{0})^{2}-{3\kappa^{2}}/{8A_{0}}

As the governing equation (
24

constants,
A_{0}
and
A_{1}
. All other constants in Eq. (

In the classical solution above, we have redefined
A_{1}
in terms of
t_{0}
as
A_{1}=-2t_{0}/(w+1)
, so that
this constant can be interpreted on the same footing as the cosmic time
t

we could expand the above solution in the power series of both
1/t
and
\kappa^{2}
at late time.
Below, we present the quantum-corrected solution for the volume element using the cutoff identification
in Eq. (
33

The general quantum power series solution of
V(t)

w\neq 1
and
w\neq 1/3

\displaystyle V(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{0}t^{\frac{2}{w+1}}\left\{1-\frac{2}{w+1}\frac{t_{0}}{t}-\frac{w-1}{(w+1)^{2}}\frac{t_{0}^{2}}{t^{2}}-\frac{2w(w-1)}{3(w+1)^{3}}\frac{t_{0}^{3}}{t^{3}}-\frac{w(w-1)(3w+1)}{6(w+1)^{4}}\frac{t_{0}^{4}}{t^{4}}\right.

(55)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{3(w+1)\tilde{\nu}G_{0}c^{2}}{4A_{0}^{2w+2}t_{0}^{2}}\frac{t_{0}^{2}}{t^{2}}+\frac{3w\tilde{\nu}G_{0}c^{2}}{2A_{0}^{2w+2}t_{0}^{2}}\frac{t_{0}^{3}}{t^{3}}+\left.\left[\frac{3w(3w+1)\tilde{\nu}G_{0}c^{2}}{4(w+1)A_{0}^{2w+2}t_{0}^{2}}\!+\!\frac{(w\!+\!1)\tilde{\nu}_{1}G_{0}^{2}c^{3}}{8A_{0}^{3w+3}t_{0}^{4}}\!-\!\frac{9w(w\!+\!1)^{2}\tilde{\nu}^{2}G_{0}^{2}c^{4}}{32A_{0}^{4w+4}t_{0}^{4}}\right]\frac{t_{0}^{4}}{t^{4}}+\mathcal{O}(t^{-5})\right\},

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{3(w+1)^{2}\kappa^{2}}{8(3w-1)A_{0}}t^{\frac{2w}{w+1}}\left\{1-\frac{2w}{w+1}\frac{t_{0}}{t}+\left[\frac{w(w-1)}{(w+1)^{2}}\!-\!\frac{3(w\!+\!1)\tilde{\nu}G_{0}c\left[4(3w\!-\!1)A_{0}^{w+1}\!-\!w(5w\!+\!1)c\right]}{4(w-3)A_{0}^{2w+2}t_{0}^{2}}\right]\frac{t_{0}^{2}}{t^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(t^{-3})\right\},

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\kappa^{4}}{A_{0}^{4}}t^{\frac{2(2w-1)}{(w+1)}}\right)

(1-x)^{\frac{2w}{w+1}}=1-\frac{2w}{w+1}x+\frac{w(w-1)}{(w+1)^{2}}x^{2}+\frac{2w(w-1)}{3(w+1)^{3}}x^{3}+\frac{w(w-1)(w+3)}{6(w+1)^{4}}x^{4}+\frac{w(w-1)(w+2)(w+3)}{15(w+1)^{5}}x^{5}+\cdots.

•

The solution is composed of two “dimensional” series, in powers of

IV
Isotropization of BI models into FLRW universe

The isotropic and homogeneous universe we observe today, described by the FLRW model, may have emerged as
an asymptotic state from an initially highly anisotropic universe. In the anisotropic BI model, we explore
how quantum corrections can influence the process of isotropization.

IV.1
General consideration for
\Lambda_{0}=0

Let us first consider the case
\Lambda_{0}=0
and study whether such an isotropization can occur.
To illustrate the nature of this problem, let us briefly look at the general
behavior of the cosmic shear and the Hubble parameter.

We assume that our BI universe is initially expanding:
H>0
at
t=t_{0}
. At this stage,
by “expanding” we mean that the
3
-volume
V(t)=a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}
is increasing with cosmic time
t
,
but it does not necessarily mean that all scale factors
a_{i}
are increasing with
t
. As a physically
reasonable condition, we impose (a slightly stronger version of) the dominant energy condition, which requires

\rho>|p|\geq 0
for our energy-momentum tensor (
9
) and (
10
), hence
-1<w\leq 1
,
and also the strong energy condition, i.e.,
1+3w>0
.
Let
n^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}=\partial_{t}
be the unit normal vector to
t=\mathrm{const.}

hypersurface
\Sigma_{t}
with the induced metric
g_{ij}
. Then, by using the formula (
21
),
the extrinsic curvature
K_{ij}:=\nabla_{i}n_{j}
of
\Sigma_{t}
is expressed as

K_{ij}=H_{i}g_{ij}=\left(H+\dfrac{\kappa_{i}}{V}\right)g_{ij}\,.

(61)

In our metric ansatz, each fluid element follows the timelike geodesic orthogonal to
\Sigma_{t}
,
and thus the shear
\sigma_{ij}
of the fluid is given by

\sigma_{ij}:=K_{ij}-\dfrac{1}{3}Kg_{ij}=\dfrac{\kappa_{i}}{V}g_{ij}\,,

(62)

where
K:=g^{ij}K_{ij}
. In particular, the shear-squared is expressed as

\sigma_{ij}\sigma^{ij}=\dfrac{\kappa^{2}}{V^{2}}\,.

(63)

By using (
13
) and (
23
), we have for
\Lambda_{0}=0
case,

\dot{H}=-H^{2}-\dfrac{\kappa^{2}}{3V^{2}}-\dfrac{4\pi G}{3}(1+3w)\rho\,.

(64)

By noting that the second term in the right-hand side is the shear-squared (
63
),
this is identified with the Raychaudhuri equation for timelike geodesic congruence with the tangent
n^{a}
.
Then, combining this equation with (
17
), we obtain the constraint equation,

VI
Summary

In this work, we have studied the anisotropic BI universe by incorporating quantum corrections
to the Newton coupling and cosmological term within the framework of asymptotically safe gravity.
Our main focus is on the late-time behavior of the universe, which depends on the low-energy flow of the Newton
coupling and the cosmological term in the effective average action under the Einstein–Hilbert truncation.
In particular, we have employed a modified continuity equation, where only the conservation of the entire
right-hand side of the improved Einstein equations is imposed. The primary aim is to analyze how the anisotropic
universe is influenced by quantum corrections at late times for two distinct branches of FRG trajectories:
one with
\Lambda_{0}=0
and the other with
\Lambda_{0}>0
.

For studying cosmology in these two branches, we have found that the “improved” classical Hubble parameter,
expressed in terms of the quantum scale factor, is a viable cutoff scale choice for the
\Lambda_{0}=0

branch but not for the
\Lambda_{0}>0
branch, consistent with our earlier FLRW analysis
Chen:2024ebb
.
We have derived quantum-improved power series solutions in terms of the cosmic time
t
for a general equation-of-state
parameter
w
and observed that these solutions also contain series expansions in the anisotropy parameter

\kappa^{2}
for both branches.

To quantitatively distinguish between the classical and quantum solutions
for general
w
, we have numerically integrated the differential equations and studied the role of initial conditions.
We have shown that, for the
\Lambda_{0}=0
branch, the coefficients of the leading term and the leading-order

\kappa^{2}
contribution in the power series of
t
depend only on the parameters in the differential equation and
the equation of state, but not on the initial conditions. The next-to-leading-order terms, however, are
affected by the initial conditions, which govern the differences between the quantum and classical solutions
of the volume element. In contrast, for the
\Lambda_{0}>0
branch, the difference between the quantum and
classical solutions exhibits the same leading-order behaviors in
\tau
as the (classical and quantum) volume elements
themselves. This occurs because, unlike in the
\Lambda_{0}=0
case, the leading-order coefficients of
the volume for the quantum case explicitly depends on both the quantum parameter and the initial condition.

We have also examined how the anisotropic Kasner solution is affected by quantum corrections in
Appendix
A
.
In the classical Kasner case, the solution is governed by a single integration constant in order for it to
be a solution for any
w
, and it has the vanishing vacuum energy.
In the quantum-corrected solution, however, the running cosmological term
\Lambda(t)
has generated
an effective energy density, which decays with time and vanishes in the asymptotic future.

Moreover we have given a general criterion, valid for any
\Lambda_{0}
, if the isotropization is achieved
in the late time. For the
\Lambda_{0}=0
branch, we have found that for all matter satisfying
-1<w<1
,
the universe eventually becomes fully isotropic at late times, since the exponent of the leading behavior
of
V(t)
is always greater than unity. In the special case
w=1
, corresponding to stiff matter,
the anisotropic universe may approach the vacuum Kasner solution depending on the behavior of the directional
Hubble parameters.
We have also investigated how quantum effects influence the isotropization of the universe starting from
an initially highly anisotropic state. From the volume element analysis, we have observed that
the quantum-corrected volume grows more rapidly than the classical one,
leading to an earlier isotropization of the BI universe due to quantum effects.

For the
\Lambda_{0}>0
branch, our criterion indicates that the BI universe quickly becomes isotopic.
Indeed we have seen that the cosmic no-hair theorem also holds in the quantum-corrected case:
the BI universe quickly approaches an expanding de Sitter universe in a spatially flat cosmological
chart. In particular, the transition from an anisotropic state to a spatially flat de Sitter universe is
found to occur earlier in the quantum-corrected case than in the classical scenario.

Appendix A
Kasner solutions

A.1
Classical Kasner solutions for
\Lambda_{0}=0
and
\rho=0

Here we examine the well-known classical Kasner solution for the
\Lambda_{0}=0
and
\rho=0
case,
which is the vacuum solution of the BI model.
We find the classical exact solution from Eq. (
24
):

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{cl}}^{\mathrm{K}}(t)=A_{0}(t-t_{0}),\qquad a_{i}(t)=\tilde{a}_{i0}(t-t_{0})^{\kappa_{i}/A_{0}+1/3},

(108)

with

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{3}\kappa_{i}=0,\qquad\kappa^{2}=\frac{2}{3}{A_{0}^{2}},

(109)

where
A_{0}
and
\tilde{a}_{i0}
are dimensionfull integration constants.
This is an exact solution for any value of
w
. The price for this is that there is the additional
constraint given in the last relation in (
109
).
It seems that this constraint is widely considered to be that on the parameter
\kappa^{2}
.
However
\kappa^{2}
is a parameter in the differential equation (
24
) we are solving,
and so it is more natural to consider the constant
A_{0}
in the solution is constrained rather than
\kappa^{2}
.
We should regard the Kasner solution as a special one of Eq. (
24
) with
A_{0}:=\sqrt{3\kappa^{2}/2}

and the differential equation is not constrained.
So there is no constraint on
\kappa_{i}
except for the first relation in (
109
),
and this solution contains only a single integration constant even though it is a solution of the second order
differential equation.

This solution is interesting in that it gives anisotropic solutions for any
w
. This is so
because the solution is given by the power of
t
, consistent with our analysis in Sec.
IV
.
It is also intriguing that this solution evades the general analysis on the leading power law which indicates
that the power of
t
should be in general larger than 1 for
-1<w<1
. This is possible because of
the constraint on
V_{0}
and the power in
t
.

As the Kasner solution corresponds to the vanishing energy density

\displaystyle V(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{2\Lambda_{0}}}\sinh\left(\sqrt{3\Lambda_{0}}\,(t-t_{0})\right),

\displaystyle a_{i}(t)

\displaystyle a_{i0}\left(\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2\Lambda_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}\sinh^{1/3}\left(\sqrt{3\Lambda_{0}}\,(t-t_{0})\right)\left[\tanh\left(\frac{\sqrt{3\Lambda_{0}}}{2}(t-t_{0})\right)\right]^{\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\frac{\kappa_{i}}{\kappa}}.

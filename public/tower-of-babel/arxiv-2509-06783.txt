1.
Introduction

In the paper, we assume that the reader is familiar with standard notation and main results of Nevanlinna Theory (see
[
10
,
27
]
). We use notations
\rho(f)
,
\rho_{1}(f)
,
\mu(f)
and
\lambda(f)
for the order, the hyper-order, the lower order and the exponent of convergence of zeros of a meromorphic function
f
respectively. If
\mu(f)=\rho(f)
, we say that
f
is of regular growth. As usual, the abbreviation CM means “counting multiplicities”, while IM means “ignoring multiplicities”.

The Borel exceptional small function
a(z)
of
f(z)
is defined by

\lambda(f-a)=\limsup\limits_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log^{+}N(r,0;f-a)}{\log r}<\rho(f)

where
\lambda(f-a)
is the exponent of convergence of zeros of
f(z)-a(z)
.

In the paper, the forward difference
\Delta^{n}_{c}f
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
is defined in the standard way by
\Delta^{1}_{c}f(z)=\Delta_{c}f(z)=f(z+c)-f(z)
and
\Delta_{c}^{n}f(z)=\Delta_{c}\left(\Delta_{c}^{n-1}f(z)\right)

n\geq 2
. Moreover
\Delta_{c}^{n}f(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-j}C^{j}_{n}f(z+jc)
, where
C^{j}_{n}
is a combinatorial number.

We introduce the linear difference operator
L_{c}(f)
by

\displaystyle L_{c}(f(z))=\sideset{}{{}^{n}_{j=0}}{\sum}a_{j}(z)f(z+jc)(\not\equiv 0),

(1.1)

where
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}(\not\equiv 0)
are entire functions. If
a_{j}=(-1)^{n-j}C^{j}_{n}
, then
L_{c}(f)=\Delta_{c}^{n}f
. If an equation includes derivatives, shifts or differences of
f
, then the equation is called differential-difference equation.

Meromorphic solutions of complex difference and differential-difference equations and the uniqueness problems of meromorphic functions sharing value (s) with their derivatives, shifts, different types of complex difference and differential-difference operators have become an area of current interest and the study is based on the Nevanlinna value distribution of difference operators established by Halburd and Korhonen
[
9
]
and Chiang and Feng
[
7
]
respectively.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we will find out the precise form of the finite order entire solutions of the differential-difference equation
f^{(k)}(z)=L_{c}(f(z))
, where
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}(\neq 0)\in\mathbb{C}
. In Section 3, we will consider the differential-difference analogue of Brück conjecture and derive a uniqueness result of finite order entire function
f(z)
having a Borel exceptional small function of
f(z)
, when
f^{(k)}(z)
and
L_{c}(f(z))
share a small function of
f(z)
.

2.
The entire solution of the equation
f^{(k)}=L_{c}(f)

The differential-difference (or delay-differential) equation
f^{(1)}(x)=f(x-k)
, where
k>0
is well known and studied in real analysis, some results can
be found in the book
[
1
]
. It seems that we have little understanding on entire solutions of complex differential-difference equation

\displaystyle f^{(1)}(z)=f(z+c),

(2.1)

where
c\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
. The solutions of (
2.1
) exist, for example,
f(z)=e^{z}
is a solution of (
2.1
), where
c=2k\pi i
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
f(z)=\sin z\;\text{or}\;\cos z
are also solutions of (
2.1
) for suitable
c
. Clearly, the equation (
2.1
) has no rational solutions.

In 2014, Liu and Dong
[
21
]
proved that: if
f
is a meromorphic solution of (
2.1
), then
\rho(f)\geq 1
. In the same paper, for the entire solutions of (
2.1
), Liu and Dong
[
21
]
obtained the following result.

Theorem 2.A
.

[
21
, Theorem 3.5]
If
f
is an entire solution of (
2.1
) and
\lambda(f)<\sigma(f)<+\infty
, then
\rho(f)=1
. Furthermore, one of the following three cases holds:

(i)

f(z)=(b_{1}z+b_{0})e^{ez+B}
, where
b_{1}\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
and
c=\frac{1}{e}
;

(ii)

f(z)=b_{0}e^{Az+B}
, where
c=\frac{ln|A|+i(\arg A+2ki\pi)}{A}
and
A\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
;

(iii)

f(z)=g(z)e^{Az+B}
, where
A\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
and
g(z)
is a transcendental entire function such that
g^{(1)}(z)=A[g(z+c)-g(z)]
and
\rho(g)<1
.

In the section, we consider the following differential-difference equation:

\displaystyle f^{(k)}=L_{c}(f),

(2.2)

where
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}(\neq 0)\in\mathbb{C}
.
The solution of Eq. (
2.2
) exist, for example,
f(z)=e^{z}
is a solution of Eq. (
2.2
), where

c=2\pi i
and
a_{0}+a_{1}+\ldots+a_{n}=1
. Obviously, Eq. (
2.2
) has no rational solutions.

Now, we want to improve Theorem 2.A in the following directions:

(i)

We replace
f^{(1)}(z)
by general derivative
f^{(k)}(z)
.

(ii)

We replace
f(z+c)
by more general linear difference operator
L_{c}(f(z))
.

We state our result.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
f
be an entire solution of (
2.2
) such that
\lambda(f)<\rho(f)<+\infty
and let
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}(\neq 0)\in\mathbb{C}
. Then
\rho(f)=1
and one of the following cases holds:

(1)

f(z)=dH(z)e^{c_{1}z}
, where
c_{1},d\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
and
H
is a non-constant polynomial such that

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}_{j=0}^{n}}{\sum}a_{j}e^{jcc_{1}}H(z+jc)=\sideset{}{{}_{j=0}^{k}}{\sum}C^{k}_{j}c_{1}^{k-j}H^{(j)}(z)\;\text{and}\;c_{1}^{k}=\sideset{}{{}_{j=0}^{n}}{\sum}a_{j}e^{jcc_{1}}\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\};

(2.3)

(2)

f(z)=dH(z)e^{c_{1}z}
, where
d\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
and
H
is a transcendental entire function such that
\rho(H)<1
and (
2.3
) holds.

In order to prove Theorem
2.1
, we need the following lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
27
, Theorem 1.51]
Suppose that
f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{n}\;(n\geq 2)
are meromorphic functions and
g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{n}
are entire functions satisfying the following conditions

(i)

\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}e^{g_{j}}=0
;

(ii)

g_{i}-g_{j}
are non-constants for
1\leq i<j\leq n
;

(iii)

T(r,f_{j})=o\left(T(r,e^{g_{h}-g_{k}})\right)

(r\rightarrow\infty,r\not\in E)
for
1\leq j\leq n
,
1\leq h<k\leq n
.

Then
f_{j}\equiv 0
for
j=1,2,\ldots,n
.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
8
, Corollary 2]
Let
f
be transcendental meromorphic function of finite order
\rho(f)
, let
\Gamma=\{(k_{1},j_{1}),(k_{2},j_{2}),\ldots,(k_{m},j_{m})\}
denote a finite set of distinct pairs of integers that satisfy
k_{i}>j_{i}\geq 0
for
i=1,2,\ldots,m
and let
\varepsilon>0
be a given constant. Then, there exists a subset
E\subset(1,\infty)
that has a finite logarithmic measure, such that for all
z
satisfying
|z|\not\in E\cup[0,1]
and for all
(k,j)\in\Gamma
, we have

\displaystyle\left|f^{(k)}(z)/f^{(j)}(z)\right|\leq|z|^{(k-j)(\rho(f)-1+\varepsilon)}.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
2
, Lemma 3.3]
Let
f
be a transcendental meromorphic function such that
\rho(f)<1
. Let
h>0
. Then there exists an
\varepsilon
-set
E
such that

f^{(1)}(z+c)/f(z+c)\rightarrow 0\;\;\text{and}\;\;f(z+c)/f(z)\rightarrow 1\;\text{as}\;z\rightarrow\infty\;in\;\mathbb{C}\backslash E

uniformly in
c
for
|c|\leq h
. Further
E
may be chosen so that for large
z
not in
E
the function
f
has no zeros or poles in
|\zeta-z|\leq h
.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
7
, Corollary 2.6]
Let
f
be a meromorphic function of finite order
\rho
and let
\eta_{1},\;\eta_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\eta_{1}\neq\eta_{2}
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\displaystyle m\left(r,f(z+\eta_{1})/f(z+\eta_{2})\right)=O(r^{\rho-1+\varepsilon}).

Lemma 2.5
.

[
20
, Theorem 2.3]
Let
f
be a transcendental meromorphic solution of finite order
\rho
of a difference equation of the form

U(z,f)P(z,f)=Q(z,f),

where
U(z,f),\;P(z,f)
and
Q(z,f)
are difference polynomials such that the total degree
\deg\left(U(z,f)\right)=n
in
f
and its shifts and
\deg\left(Q(z,f)\right)\leq n
. Moreover, we assume that
U(z,f)
contains just one term of maximal total degree in
f
and its shifts. Then for each
\varepsilon>0
,

m(r,P(z,f))=O(r^{\rho-1+\varepsilon})+S(r,f)

possible outside of an exceptional set of finite logarithmic measure.

Remark 2.1
.

From the proof of Lemma
2.5
, we see that if the coefficients of

U(z,f),P(z,f),\\
Q(z,f)
, say
a_{\lambda}
satisfy
m(r,a_{\lambda})=S(r,f)
, then the same conclusion still holds.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
f
be a finite order transcendental entire function such that
\rho(f)>1
and
\lambda(f-a)<\rho(f)
, where
a
is an entire function with
\rho(a)<\rho(f)
. If
f
is a solution of the equation

\displaystyle f^{(k)}-b=\left(L_{c}(f)-d\right)e^{Q},

(2.4)

where
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
,
c\in\mathbb{C}
,
Q
is a polynomial and
a_{j}
’s are entire functions such that
\rho(a_{j})<\rho(f)-1\;(j=0,\ldots,n)
,
a_{n}\not\equiv 0
and
b,\;d
be small function of
f
, then
\deg(Q)=\rho(f)-1
.

Remark 2.2
.

The conditions “
\rho(f)>1
” and “
\rho(a_{j})<\rho(f)-1
” in Lemma
2.6
are necessary as shown in the following examples.

Example 2.1
.

Let
a_{1}(z)=ze^{-2z+1},\;a_{2}(z)=ze^{-4z-2}
and
Q(z)=-2
. Clearly
f(z)=e^{z^{2}}
is a solution of the equation
f^{(1)}(z)=\left(a_{1}(z)f(z+1)+a_{2}(z)f(z+2)\right)e^{Q(z)}
. Note that
\rho(a_{i})=1=\rho(f)-1
for
i=1,2
, but
\deg(Q)\neq\rho(f)-1
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
a_{1}(z)=z^{2}e^{-3z^{2}+1},\;a_{2}(z)=2z^{2}e^{-3z^{2}-9z-6}
and
Q(z)=-3z-2
. Clearly
f(z)=e^{z^{3}}
is a solution of the equation

f^{(1)}(z)=(a_{1}(z)f(z+1)+a_{2}(z)f(z+2))e^{Q(z)}
. Note that
\rho(a_{j})=2=\rho(f)-1
for
i=1,2
, but
\deg(Q)\neq\rho(f)-1
.

Proof of Lemma
2.6
.

By the given conditions, we have
\rho(a)<\rho(f)
and
\lambda(f-a)<\rho(f)
. Then there exist an entire function
H(\not\equiv 0)
and a polynomial
P
such that

\displaystyle f=a+He^{P},

(2.5)

where
\lambda(H)=\rho(H)<\rho(f-a)
and
\deg(P)=\rho(f-a)
. Since
\rho(a)<\rho(f)
, we have
\rho(f-a)=\rho(f)
. We know that
\mu(f)=\rho(f)=\deg(P)>1
. Since
\rho(a_{j})<\rho(f)-1<\rho(f)=\mu(f)
, by Theorem 1.18
[
27
]
, we get
T(r,a_{j})=S(r,f)
for
j=0,1,\ldots,n
. Similarly
T(r,H)=S(r,f)
. Now from (
2.5
), we have

\displaystyle L_{c}(f(z))=\sideset{}{{}^{n}_{j=0}}{\sum}a_{j}(z)a(z+jc)+H_{n}(z)e^{P(z)},

(2.6)

where

\displaystyle H_{n}(z)=\sideset{}{{}^{n}_{j=0}}{\sum}a_{j}(z)H(z+jc)e^{P(z+jc)-P(z)}.

(2.7)

Also from (
2.5
), we get

\displaystyle f^{(k)}=a^{(k)}+P_{k}(H)e^{P},

(2.8)

where

\displaystyle P_{k}(H)=H^{(k)}+p_{k-1}H^{(k-1)}+\ldots+p_{0}H

(2.9)

and
p_{i}
’s are polynomials such that

\displaystyle\deg(p_{i})=(k-i)\deg(P^{(1)})

(2.10)

for
i=0,1,\ldots,k-1
. Now from (
2.4
)-(
2.6
) and (
2.8
), we have

\displaystyle a^{(k)}-b+P_{k}(H)e^{P}=\Big(\sideset{}{{}^{n}_{j=0}}{\sum}a_{j}(z)a(z+jc)-d+H_{n}e^{P}\Big)e^{Q}.

(2.11)

Let
w_{1}=P_{k}(H)
,
w_{2}=a^{(k)}-b
,
w_{3}=H_{n}
and
w_{4}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{j}(z)a(z+jc)-d
. It is clear that
T(r,w_{j})=S(r,f)
for
j=1,2,3,4
. Then from (
2.11
), we have

\displaystyle w_{1}e^{P}+w_{2}=(w_{3}e^{P}+w_{4})e^{Q}.

(2.12)

Obviously
\deg(Q)\leq\deg(P)
. Let
P(z)=\tilde{a}_{s}z^{s}+\tilde{a}_{s-1}z^{s-1}+\ldots+\tilde{a}_{0}
. Clearly
s\geq 2
.

Now we divide following two cases.

Case 1.
Suppose
\deg(Q)=\deg(P)
. Let
Q(z)=\tilde{b}_{s}z^{s}+\tilde{b}_{s-1}z^{s-1}+\cdots+\tilde{b}_{0}\;(\tilde{b}_{s}\neq 0)
. There are three possibilities:
(1)\;\tilde{a}_{s}=\tilde{b}_{s}
,
(2)\;\tilde{a}_{s}=-\tilde{b}_{s}
and
(3)\;\tilde{a}_{s}\neq\tilde{b}_{s}\;\text{and}\;\tilde{a}_{s}\neq-\tilde{b}_{s}
. Therefore we consider following three sub-cases.

Sub-case 1.1.
Suppose
\tilde{a}_{s}=\tilde{b}_{s}
. Then from (
2.12
), we have

\displaystyle(w_{1}-w_{4}e^{Q-P})e^{P}-w_{3}e^{P+Q}+w_{2}=0.

(2.13)

Clearly
T(r,e^{Q-P})=o(T(r,e^{P}))
and
T(r,e^{Q-P})=o(T(r,e^{Q}))
. Also
T(r,w_{3})=o(T(r,e^{P}))
and
T(r,w_{3})=o(T(r,e^{Q}))
. Consequently

T(r,w_{1}-w_{4}e^{Q-P})=o(T(r,e^{P})),T(r,w_{1}-w_{4}e^{Q-P})=o(T(r,e^{Q})),\ldots,T(r,w_{2})=o(T(r,e^{P+Q})).

Therefore using Lemma
2.1
to (
2.13
), we get
w_{1}-w_{4}e^{Q-P}\equiv 0
,
w_{2}\equiv 0
and
w_{3}\equiv 0
. Now
w_{3}\equiv 0
implies that

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{n}_{j=1}}{\sum}e^{P(z+jc)-P(z)}a_{j}(z)H(z+jc)/H(z)=-a_{0}(z).

(2.14)

Let
R(z)=P(z+c)-P(z)
. By the given condition, we have
\rho(a_{j})<\rho(f)-1
,
j=0,1,\ldots,n
and so
\rho(a_{j})<\rho(e^{R})=\mu(e^{R})
. Clearly
T(r,a_{j})=S(r,e^{R})
,
j=0,1,\ldots,n
. Also by the definition of order, there exists a
R>0
such that
T(r,a_{j})=O(r^{\rho(a_{j})+\varepsilon})
holds for
r\geq R
, where
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary.
Now from Lemma
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle m\left(r,H(z+jc)/H(z)\right)+m\left(r,H(z)/H(z+jc)\right)=O\left(r^{\rho(H)-1+\varepsilon}\right).

(2.15)

We consider following sub-cases.

Sub-case 1.1.1.
Let
n=1
. Then from (
2.14
), we have

\displaystyle e^{R(z)}a_{1}(z)H(z+c)/H(z)=-a_{0}(z).

(2.16)

By the given condition
a_{1}\not=0
. If
a_{0}\equiv 0
, then from (
2.16
) we get
H\equiv 0
, which is impossible. Hence
a_{0}\not\equiv 0
.
Now (
2.16
) shows that
a_{1}(z)H(z+c)/H(z)
is an entire function. Note that

T\left(r,a_{1}(z)H(z+c)/H(z)\right)=m\left(r,a_{1}(z)H(z+c)/H(z)\right)\leq m(r,a_{1})+m\left(r,H(z+c)/H(z)\right)

and so

\displaystyle T\left(r,a_{1}(z)H(z+c)/H(z)\right)=m\left(r,a_{1}(z)H(z+c)/H(z)\right)=O\left(r^{\sigma+\varepsilon}\right),

(2.17)

where
\sigma=\max\{\rho(H)-1,\rho(a_{1})\}
.

Since
\rho(H)<\rho(f)
and
\rho(a_{1})<\rho(f)-1
, we have
\sigma<\rho(f)-1=\rho(e^{R})=\mu(e^{R})
. Now (
2.17
) yields

\rho\big(a_{1}(z)H(z+c)/H(z)\big)=\sigma<\mu(e^{R})
and so

T\big(r,a_{1}(z)H(z+c)/H(z)\big)=S(r,e^{R}).

Then from (
2.16
), we get a contradiction.

Sub-case 1.1.2.
Let
n\geq 2
.

First we suppose that
a_{0}\not\equiv 0
.

Clearly
Q_{1}(z+(j-1)c)\ldots Q_{1}(z+c)=e^{P(z+jc)-P(z+c)}
,
j=2,3,\ldots,n
, where
Q_{1}=e^{R}
. Then (
2.14
) can be written as

\displaystyle U(z,Q_{1})Q_{1}=-a_{0},

(2.18)

where

\displaystyle U(z,Q_{1}(z))=\sum\limits_{1\leq j\leq n}a_{j}(z)Q_{1}(z+(j-1)c)Q_{1}(z+(j-2)c)\cdots Q_{1}(z+c)H(z+jc)/H(z).

Now from (
2.18
), we see
\deg(U(z,Q_{1}))=n-1\geq 1
.
Also from above, we have

\displaystyle m(r,a_{j}(z)H(z+jc)/H(z))=O(r^{\sigma+\varepsilon}),

(2.19)

where
\sigma=\max\{\rho(H)-1,\rho(a_{j})\}
,
j=0,1,\ldots,n
.

Since
\rho(H)<\rho(f)
and
\rho(a_{j})<\rho(f)-1
, it follows that
\sigma<\rho(f)-1=\rho(e^{R})=\mu(e^{R})
. Let us
choose
\varepsilon>0
such that
\sigma+2\varepsilon<\rho(e^{R})=\mu(e^{R})
. By the definition of lower order, there exists
R>0
such that

T(r,e^{R})>r^{\mu(e^{R})-\varepsilon}=r^{\rho(e^{R})-\varepsilon}

holds for
r\geq R
. Then from (
2.19
), we get

\lim\limits_{r\to\infty}\frac{m(r,a_{j}(z)H(z+jc)/H(z))}{T(r,e^{R})}=0

and so

\displaystyle m\left(r,a_{j}(z)H(z+jc)/H(z)\right)=S(r,e^{R(z)})=S(r,Q_{1}(z))

(2.20)

for
j=0,1,\ldots,n
. This shows that if
a_{\lambda}
is a coefficient of
U(z,Q_{1})
, then
m(r,a_{\lambda})=S(r,Q_{1})
. Then in view of Remark
2.1
and using Lemma
2.5
to (
2.18
), we get

m(r,Q_{1})=S(r,Q_{1})
and so

, which is a contradiction.

Next we suppose
a_{0}=0
. For the sake of simplicity we may assume that
a_{1}\not\equiv 0
.
So from (
2.14
) we get

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{n}_{j=2}}{\sum}a_{j}(z)e^{P(z+jc)-P(z+c)}H(z+jc)/H(z)=-a_{1}(z).

Now proceeding in the same way as done above, we get a contradiction.

Sub-case 1.2.
Suppose
\tilde{a}_{s}=-\tilde{b}_{s}
. Then from (
2.12
), we have

\displaystyle w_{1}e^{P}-w_{4}e^{Q}-w_{3}e^{P+Q}+w_{2}=0.

(2.21)

Clearly

,
T(r,w_{1})=o(T(r,e^{P})),\ldots
,

Then using Lemma
2.5

), we get
w_{1}\equiv 0
,
w_{2}-w_{3}e^{P+Q}\equiv 0

w_{4}\equiv 0
.
Since

, which is impossible.

Sub-case 1.3.
Suppose
\tilde{a}_{s}\neq\tilde{b}_{s}
and
\tilde{a}_{s}\neq-\tilde{b}_{s}
. Then from (
2.12
), we have

\displaystyle w_{1}e^{P}-w_{4}e^{Q}-w_{3}e^{P+Q}+w_{2}=0.

3.
Differential-difference analogue of Brück conjecture

We recall the following conjecture proposed by Brück
[
3
]
.

Conjecture 3.A
.

Let
f
be a non-constant entire function such that
\rho_{1}(f)\not\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. If
f
and
f^{(1)}
share one finite value
a
CM, then
f^{(1)}-a=c(f-a),\;\text{where}\;\;c\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
.

Though the conjecture is not settled in its full generality, it gives rise to a long course of research on the uniqueness of entire and meromorphic functions sharing a single value with its derivatives.

Recently, many authors have started to consider the sharing values problems of meromorphic functions with their difference operators or shifts. Some results on difference analogues of Brück conjecture were considered in
[
4
]
-
[
6
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
11
]
-
[
22
]
,
[
23
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
25
]
,
[
26
]
.

Brück
[
3
]
proved that if
f
is a transcendental entire function and
f
and
f^{(1)}
share
0
CM, then
f^{(1)}=Af
. In 2009, Heittokangas et al.
[
11
]
proved that if
f(z)
is a transcendental meromorphic function with
\rho(f)<2
and
f(z)
and
f(z+c)
share
0
CM, then
f(z)=Af(z+c)
. We now consider the differential-difference analogue of Brück conjecture. Note that if
f^{(1)}(z)
and
f(z+c)
share
0
CM, the equation
f^{(1)}(z)=Af(z+c)
is not valid in general. As for example if
f(z)=e^{e^{z}}
and
c=2\pi i
, then
f^{(1)}(z)=e^{z}f(z+c)
. However, for finite order entire functions the differential-difference analogue of Brück conjecture may hold. For example, let
f(z)=e^{z}
and
e^{c}=2
. Then
f^{(1)}(z)
and
f(z+c)
share
0
CM and
f^{(1)}(z)=\frac{1}{2}f(z+c)
. In 2014, Liu and Dong
[
21
]
first considered the differential-difference analogue of Brück conjecture for finite order transcendental entire function. Now we recall their result.

Theorem 3.A
.

[
21
, Theorem 4.3]
Let
f
be a finite order transcendental entire function and
a
be a Picard exceptional value of
f
. If
f^{(1)}(z)
and
f(z+c)
share
b(\in\mathbb{C})
CM, then
f^{(1)}(z)-b=A(f(z+c)-b)
, where
A\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
. Furthermore, if
b\neq 0
, then
A=\frac{b}{b-a}
.

In this section, we will improve Theorem 3.A in the following directions:

(1)

We replace
f^{(1)}(z)
by
f^{(k)}(z)
and

by

.

(2)

We consider
a
as an entire function such that
\lambda(f-a)<\rho(f)
and
\rho(a)<\max\{1,\rho(f)\}
.

(3)

We consider
b
as an entire function such that
\rho(b)<\max\{1,\rho(f)\}
.

We now state our result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f
be a finite order transcendental entire function and let

and

be entire functions such that

(3.35)

for sufficiently large values of
r
. Clearly from (
3.30
), (
3.32
) and (
3.34
), we get

\displaystyle\Big|e^{jsc_{s}cz^{s-1}}e^{P_{s-2,j}(z)}H(z+jc)/H(z)\Big|

(3.36)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\Big(js|c_{s}c|r^{s-1}\cos((s-1)\theta_{0}+\theta_{2}))+r^{\rho(H)-1+\varepsilon}+3|C_{0}|r^{s-2}/2\Big)=\exp(jx+y),

where
x=s|c_{s}c|r^{s-1}\cos((s-1)\theta_{0}+\theta_{2}))
,
y=r^{\rho(H)-1+\varepsilon}+\frac{3|C_{0}|}{2}r^{s-2}
and
j=1,2,\ldots,n
.
Now from (
3.28
), (
3.29
), (
3.31
), (
3.35
) and (
3.36
), we have

\displaystyle\exp(|d_{s-1}|r^{s-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\exp(d_{s-1}z^{s-1})|

(3.37)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\exp(-Q(z))P_{k}(H(z))/H(z)}{\exp(\tilde{P}_{s-2}(z))P_{k}(H(z))/H(z)}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle B\Big|\sideset{}{{}^{n}_{j=1}}{\sum}a_{j}e^{jsc_{s}cz^{s-1}}e^{P_{s-2,j}(z)}H(z+jc)/H(z)+a_{0}\Big|\;|\exp(-\tilde{P}_{s-2}(z))|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle B\Big(\sideset{}{{}^{n}_{j=1}}{\sum}|a_{j}|\exp\left(jx+y\right)+|a_{0}|\Big)\exp\big(3|d_{s-2}|r^{s-2}/2\big).

First we suppose
x\leq 0
, i.e.,
\cos((s-1)\theta_{0}+\theta_{2}))\leq 0
. Let
\cos((s-1)\theta_{0}+\theta_{2}))=-x_{1}
, where
0\leq x_{1}\leq 1
. If
x_{1}=0
, then from (
3.37
), one can easily conclude that

\displaystyle\exp(|d_{s-1}|r^{s-1})<C_{1}\exp\big(r^{\rho(H)-1+\varepsilon}+3(|C_{0}|+|d_{s-2}|)r^{s-2}/2\big),

(3.38)

where
C_{1}>0
. Since
\rho(H)-1+\varepsilon<s-1
, (
3.38
) leads to a contradiction. Next we suppose
0<x_{1}\leq 1
.
In this case, since
\rho(H)-1+\varepsilon<s-1
, we see that

\sideset{}{{}^{n}_{j=1}}{\sum}|a_{j}|\exp\big(-js|c_{s}c|x_{1}r^{s-1}+r^{\rho(H)-1+\varepsilon}+3|C_{0}|r^{s-2}/2\big)\rightarrow 0

as
r\rightarrow\infty
.
Now for
\varepsilon_{1}
in
(0,1)
and
r>r(\varepsilon_{1})
, we get from (
3.37
)

\displaystyle\exp(|d_{s-1}|r^{s-1})<(1+\varepsilon_{1})\exp\big(3|d_{s-2}|r^{s-2}/2\big),

which leads to a contradiction.

Next we suppose
x>0
, i.e.,
\cos((s-1)\theta_{0}+\theta_{2}))>0
. In this case, we have
\exp(jx+y)<\exp(nx+y)
for
j=0,\ldots,n
and so from (
3.37
), we get

\displaystyle\exp(|d_{s-1}|r^{s-1})

(3.39)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle B\big(\sideset{}{{}^{n}_{j=1}}{\sum}|a_{j}|\exp\left(jx+y\right)+|a_{0}|\big)\exp\big(3|d_{s-2}|r^{s-2}/2\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle B\big(\sideset{}{{}^{n}_{j=0}}{\sum}|a_{j}|+1\big)\exp(nx+y)\exp\big(3|d_{s-2}|r^{s-2}/2\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B_{3}\exp\Big(ns|c_{s}c|r^{s-1}\cos((s-1)\theta_{0}+\theta_{2}))+r^{\rho(H)-1+\varepsilon}+3(|C_{0}|+|d_{s-2}|)r^{s-2}/2\Big),

where
B_{3}=B\big(\sum_{j=0}^{n}|a_{j}|+1\big)
. Since
\rho(H)+\varepsilon<s
and
B_{3}=o(r^{s-1})
, from (
3.39
), we get

\displaystyle\exp(|d_{s-1}|r^{s-1})\leq\exp(ns|c_{s}c|\cos((s-1)\theta_{0}+\theta_{2})r^{s-1}+o(r^{s-1})).

(3.40)

By assumption, we have
d_{s-1}\neq nsc_{s}c
and
ns|c_{s}c|\leq|d_{s-1}|
. If
ns|c_{s}c|=|d_{s-1}|
, then
\cos((s-1)\theta_{0}+\theta_{2})\neq 1
and so
\cos((s-1)\theta_{0}+\theta_{2})<1
. Therefore
ns|c_{s}c|\cos((s-1)\theta_{0}+\theta_{2})<ns|c_{s}c|=|d_{s-1}|
. If
ns|c_{s}c|<|d_{s-1}|
, then
ns|c_{s}c|\cos((s-1)\theta_{0}+\theta_{2})\leq ns|c_{s}c|<|d_{s-1}|
. So in either case, we have

ns|c_{s}c|\cos((s-1)\theta_{0}+\theta_{2})<|d_{s-1}|.

Now there exists
\varepsilon_{2}>0
such that
ns|c_{s}c|\cos((s-1)\theta_{0}+\theta_{2})+2\varepsilon_{2}<|d_{s-1}|
and so from (
3.40
), we have

\exp\left(|d_{s-1}|r^{s-1}\right)\leq\exp\left(\left(|d_{s-1}|-2\varepsilon_{2}\right)r^{s-1}\right)
,
which is a contradiction.

Sub-case 2.2.1.2.
Let
ns|c_{s}c|>|d_{s-1}|
. In this case, (
3.28
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle e^{nsc_{s}cz^{s-1}}e^{P_{s-2,n}(z)}

(3.41)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{n-1}_{j=0}}{\sum}\frac{a_{j}}{a_{n}}\frac{H(z+jc)}{H(z+nc)}e^{jsc_{s}cz^{s-1}}e^{P_{s-2,j}(z)}-\frac{1}{a_{n}}\frac{P_{k}(H(z))}{H(z+nc)}e^{d_{s-1}z^{s-1}}e^{\tilde{P}_{s-2}(z)}.

Let
\arg(c_{s}c)=\theta_{1}
and
\arg(d_{s-1})=\theta_{2}
. Take
\theta_{0}
such that
\cos((s-1)\theta_{0}+\theta_{1})=1
. Then using Lemma
3.1
, we see that for any given
\varepsilon\;(0<\varepsilon<s-\rho(H))
, there exists a set
E\subset(1,\infty)
of finite logarithmic measure such that for all
z=re^{i\theta_{0}}
satisfying
|z|=r\not\in[0,1]\cup E
, we have

\displaystyle\exp(-r^{\rho(H)-1+\varepsilon})\leq\big|H(z+jc)/H(z+nc)\big|\leq\exp(r^{\rho(H)-1+\varepsilon})\;(j=0,1,\ldots,n-1).

(3.42)

In this case, we easily get

\displaystyle|\exp(jsc_{s}cz^{s-1})|=\exp(js|c_{s}c|r^{s-1})

(3.43)

j=0,1,\ldots,n
and

\displaystyle|\exp(d_{s-1}z^{s-1})|=|\exp(|d_{s-1}|r^{s-1}\cos((s-1)\theta_{0}+\theta_{2}))|\leq|\exp(|d_{s-1}|r^{s-1})|.

(3.44)

Also we easily obtain

\displaystyle|\exp(\pm P_{s-2,j}(z))|<\exp\big(3|C_{0}|r^{s-2}/2\big)\;\text{and}\;|\exp(\tilde{P}_{s-2}(z))|<\exp\big(3|d_{s-2}|r^{s-2}/2\big)

(3.45)

for sufficiently large values of
r
. Then from (
3.42
), (
3.43
) and (
3.45
), we get

\displaystyle\Big|e^{jsc_{s}cz^{s-1}}e^{P_{s-2,j}(z)}H(z+jc)/H(z+nc)\Big|

(3.46)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\big(js|c_{s}c|r^{s-1}+r^{\rho(H)-1+\varepsilon}+3|C_{0}|r^{s-2}/2\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\big((n-1)s|c_{s}c|r^{s-1}+r^{\rho(H)-1+\varepsilon}+3|C_{0}|r^{s-2}/2\big),

j=1,\ldots,n-1
. Let
\varepsilon_{3}>0
be such that
\rho(H)+\varepsilon_{3}<s
. Using (
2.37
) and (
3.21
), we get

\displaystyle\big|P_{k}(H(z))/H(z)\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{k}_{j=0}}{\sum}|p_{j}(z)|\big|H^{(j)}(z)/H(z)\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{k}_{j=0}}{\sum}3|A_{j}|r^{k(s-1)+j(\rho(H)-s+\varepsilon_{3})/2}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle C_{2}r^{k(s-1)},

where
C_{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{3|A_{j}|}{2}
. For sufficiently large
r
, we get
C_{2}r^{k(s-1)}<\exp(r^{s-\frac{3}{2}})
and so

\displaystyle\big|P_{k}(H(z))/H(z)\big|<\exp(r^{s-\frac{3}{2}}).

(3.47)

Then from (
3.42
), (
3.44
), (
3.45
) and (
3.47
), we get

\displaystyle\Big|e^{d_{s-1}z^{s-1}}e^{\tilde{P}_{s-2}(z)}P_{k}(H(z)/H(z+nc)\Big|

(3.48)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\Big(|d_{s-1}|r^{s-1}+r^{\rho(H)+s-\frac{5}{2}+\varepsilon}+3|d_{s-2}|r^{s-2}/2\Big).

Finally from (
3.41
), (
3.43
), (
3.45
)-(
3.48
), we obtain

\displaystyle\exp(ns|c_{s}c|r^{s-1})

(3.49)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big|\sideset{}{{}^{n-1}_{j=0}}{\sum}\frac{a_{j}}{a_{n}}\frac{H(z+jc)}{H(z+nc)}e^{jsc_{s}cz^{s-1}}e^{P_{s-2,j}(z)}-\frac{1}{a_{n}}\frac{P_{k}(H(z))}{H(z+nc)}e^{d_{s-1}z^{s-1}}e^{\tilde{P}_{s-2}(z)}\Big||e^{P_{s-2,n}(z)}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle B_{4}\exp\Big(x_{2}r^{s-1}+r^{\rho(H)+s-\frac{5}{2}+\varepsilon}+(6|C_{0}|+3|d_{s-2}|)r^{s-2}/2\Big),

where
B_{4}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}|a_{j}|+1
and
x_{2}=\max\{(n-1)s|c_{s}c|,|d_{s-1}|\}
. Since
|d_{s-1}|<ns|c_{s}c|
,
x_{2}<ns|c_{s}c|
. Also
\rho(H)+s-\frac{5}{2}+\varepsilon<s-1
. Consequently from (
3.49
), we get a contradiction.

Sub-case 2.2.2.
Let
\rho(H)\geq 1
. Note that
P_{k}(H)/H=\left(He^{P}\right)^{(k)}/He^{P}
. Let
g=He^{P}
.

First suppose
|g^{(k)}(z)|
is bounded on the ray
\arg z=\theta_{0}
. By considering the formula

\displaystyle g^{(k-1)}(z)=g^{(k-1)}(0)+\int_{0}^{z}g^{(k)}(w)dw,

(3.50)

we obtain that
|g^{(k-1)}(z)|\leq|g^{(k-1)}(0)|+M|z|
for all
z
satisfying
\arg z=\theta_{0}
, where
M=\max\{|g^{(k)}(re^{i\theta_{0}})|\}>0
is some constant.
Again by using the formula

\displaystyle g^{(j-1)}(z)=g^{(j-1)}(0)+\int_{0}^{z}g^{(j)}(w)dw\;\;(1\leq j\leq k),

(3.51)

we easily obtain

\displaystyle|g^{(k-2)}(z)|\leq(M+o(1))|z|^{2}

(3.52)

as
r\to+\infty
for all
z
satisfying
\arg z=\theta_{0}
. Similarly, by using (
3.52
) and the formula (
3.51
), for
k
times, we easily obtain

\displaystyle|g(z)|\leq(M+o(1))|z|^{k}

(3.53)

as
r\to+\infty
for all
z
satisfying
\arg z=\theta_{0}
. Since
|g^{(k)}(z)|
is bounded on the ray
\arg z=\theta_{0}
, there exists an infinite sequence of points
z_{m}=r_{m}e^{i\theta_{0}}
, where
r_{m}\to\infty
such that
|g^{(k)}(z_{m})|>0
. Therefore there exists a positive constant
m
such that

0<m\leq|g^{(k)}(z_{m})|<M
and so from (
3.53
), we get

\displaystyle\big|H(z_{m})/P_{k}(H(z_{m}))\big|=\big|g(z_{m})/g^{(k)}(z_{m})\big|\leq\Big(\frac{M}{m}+o(1)\Big)|z_{m}|^{k}=\Big(\frac{M}{m}+o(1)\Big)r_{m}^{k}\;\text{as}\;r_{m}\to\infty.

Next suppose
|g^{(k)}(z)|
is unbounded on the ray
\arg z=\theta_{0}
. Then by Lemma
3.4
, there exists an infinite sequence of points
z_{m}=r_{m}e^{i\theta_{0}}
, where
r_{m}\to\infty
such that
g^{(k)}(z_{m})\to\infty
and
|g(z_{m})|\leq(1+o(1))|z_{m}|^{k}|g^{(k)}(z_{m})|
as
r_{m}\to\infty
and so

\displaystyle\big|H(z_{m})/P_{k}(H(z_{m}))\big|=\big|g(z_{m})/g^{(k)}(z_{m})\big|\leq(1+o(1))|z_{m}|^{k}=(1+o(1))r_{m}^{k}\;\text{as}\;r_{m}\to\infty.

Thus in either case, we have

\displaystyle\big|H(z_{m})/P_{k}(H(z_{m}))\big|=\big|g(z_{m})/g^{(k)}(z_{m})\big|\leq\Big(\frac{M}{m}+o(1)\Big)|z_{m}|^{k}=\Big(\frac{M}{m}+o(1)\Big)r_{m}^{k}

(3.54)

as
r_{m}\to\infty
.
Note that
r_{m}^{k}<\exp(r_{m}^{\rho(H)-1+\varepsilon})
for sufficiently large
m
. Now from (
3.54
), we get

\displaystyle\big|H(z_{m})/P_{k}(H(z_{m}))\big|<\exp(r_{m}^{\rho(H)-1+\varepsilon})

(3.55)

for sufficiently large values of
m
.

If
ns|c_{s}c|\leq|d_{s-1}|
, then from (
3.28
), (
3.31
), (
3.35
), (
3.36
) and (
3.55
), we have

\displaystyle\exp(|d_{s-1}|r_{m}^{s-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\exp(-Q(z))P_{k}(H(z_{m}))/H(z_{m})}{\exp(\tilde{P}_{s-2}(z_{m}))P_{k}(H(z_{m}))/H(z_{m})}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\sideset{}{{}^{n}_{j=1}}{\sum}a_{j}\frac{H(z_{m}+jc)}{H(z_{m})}e^{jsc_{s}cz_{m}^{s-1}}e^{P_{s-2,j}(z_{m})}+a_{0}\right|\;\left|\frac{H(z_{m})}{P_{k}(H(z_{m}))}\right|\;\left|\exp(-\tilde{P}_{s-2}(z_{m}))\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big(\sideset{}{{}^{n}_{j=1}}{\sum}|a_{j}|\exp\left(jx+y\right)+|a_{0}|\big)\exp\Big(r_{m}^{\rho(H)-1+\varepsilon}+3|d_{s-2}|r_{m}^{s-2}/2\Big).

Now proceeding in the same way as done in Sub-case 2.2.1.1, we get a contradiction. Hence
ns|c_{s}c|>|d_{s-1}|
. In this case also we get a contradiction from Sub-case 2.2.1.2.

This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Open questions

As we know
\lambda(f)\leq\rho(f)
and since in Theorem
2.1
, we have dealt with the case
\lambda(f)<\rho(f)
, it will be interesting to inspect the existence of the solutions of the Eq. (
2.2
) for the case
\lambda(f)=\rho(f)
. In the following example, we point out the fact that when
\lambda(f)=\rho(f)
, the solution of Eq. (
2.2
) may exist.

Example 4.1
.

Let
f(z)=\cos z
,
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
such that
a_{0}+a_{2}+\ldots+a_{n-2}+a_{n}=1
,
a_{1}+a_{3}+\ldots+a_{n-3}+a_{n-1}=0
, where
n
is even,
c=\pi
and
k=4
. Then
\lambda(f)=\rho(f)=1
and
L_{c}(f(z))=\sin z
. Clearly
f(z)
satisfies the Eq.
f^{(k)}(z)=L_{c}(f(z))
.

Again since in Theorem
3.1
, we considered the case
\lambda(f-a)<\rho(f)
, so the conclusions of Theorem
3.1
under the case
\lambda(f-a)<\rho(f)
is an enigma. So, here we place the following questions:

Question 4.1
.

Can one find the precise form of the solutions of Eq. (
2.2
) under the hypothesis
\lambda(f)=\rho(f)
?

Question 4.2
.

Does Theorem
3.1
hold under the hypothesis
\lambda(f-a)=\rho(f)
?

Abstract
. We prove a central limit theorem for linear statistics of a broad class of Pfaffian point processes. As an application, we derive Gaussian limits for scaled linear statistics of step functions in the Pfaffian Sine 4 \mathrm{Sine_{4}} and Sine 1 \mathrm{Sine}_{1} processes.

Primary 60G55; Secondary 30B20, 30H20.

1.
introduction

A determinantal point process is a random collection of points whose probability distribution can be expressed in terms of determinants. Such processes arise naturally in the study of various mathematical objects, including eigenvalues of random matrices, zeros of random analytic functions, and spanning trees on networks. Due to their wide applicability, determinantal point processes have been extensively studied; we refer to [Soshnikov2000] for a comprehensive review.

However, in several important applications—such as eigenvalue distributions in the Gaussian orthogonal ensemble (GOE), Gaussian symplectic ensemble (GSE), and the real Ginibre ensemble, as well as in random combinatorial structures like tilings—the correlation functions are instead described by Pfaffians of antisymmetric matrices. These systems are governed by Pfaffian point processes, a natural counterpart to determinantal processes.

To provide a precise definition of Pfaffian point processes, we first recall some preliminary notation.

Let
X
be a locally compact Polish space, and let

\rm Conf(X)
denote the space of all locally finite configurations on
X
, i.e.,

\displaystyle{\rm Conf}(X)=\big\{\xi=\textstyle\sum\limits_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}\penalty\ |x_{i}\in X\penalty\ {\rm and}\penalty\ \xi(B)<+\infty\penalty\ {\rm for\penalty\ any\penalty\ compact}\penalty\ B\subset X\big\}.

This configuration space is a Polish subspace of
\mathcal{M}(X)
, the space of Radon measures on
X
, equipped with the vague topology. A point process on
X
is a Borel probability measure
\mathbb{P}
on
{\rm Conf}(X)
. For further background, see, e.g.,
[
8
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
.

Now, let
\mu
be a Radon measure on
X
.
A locally integrable function
\rho_{k}:X^{k}\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
is called the
k
-th (
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
) correlation function of the point process
\mathbb{P}
(with respect to the reference measure
\mu
)
if for any
m\in\mathbb{N}_{+},
disjoint measurable compact subsets
A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}
of
X
, and positive integers
k_{1},\cdots,k_{m}
with
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}k_{i}=k
, the following identity holds:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\Big[\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}\#_{A_{i}}(\#_{A_{i}}-1)\cdots(\#_{A_{i}}-k_{i}+1)\Big]=\int_{A_{1}^{k_{1}}\times\cdots\times A_{m}^{k_{m}}}\rho_{k}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k})d\mu(x_{1})\cdots d\mu(x_{k}),

where
\#_{A}(\xi)=\xi(A)
counts the points of
\xi
in
A
. For additional details, we refer to
[
8
,
13
,
16
,
29
]
.

Let
K:X\times X\rightarrow M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
be a matrix-valued kernel of the form

\displaystyle K(x,y)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}K_{1,1}(x,y)&K_{1,2}(x,y)\\
K_{2,1}(x,y)&K_{2,2}(x,y)\end{array}\right),

satisfying the following symmetry conditions:

\displaystyle K_{1,1}(x,y)=K_{2,2}(y,x),\,K_{1,2}(x,y)=-K_{1,2}(y,x),\quad\text{ and }K_{2,1}(x,y)=-K_{2,1}(y,x).

Define the matrix

\displaystyle Z=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
-1&0,\end{array}\right).

and consider the antisymmetric kernel

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}(x,y)=ZK(x,y).

Then,
\mathbb{K}(x,y)^{T}=-\mathbb{K}(y,x)
for any
x,y\in X.

A point process
\mathbb{P}
is called a Pfaffian point process if its
k
-th correlation function
\rho_{k}

(for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{+}
) admits the representation

\displaystyle\rho_{k}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k})=\mathrm{Pf}\big[\mathbb{K}(x_{i},x_{j})\big]_{i,j=1}^{k}.

For further details, see
[

,

,

,

,

,

,

A prominent example of Pfaffian point processes arises as the bulk scaling limits of Pfaffian Gaussian ensembles, known as the Pfaffian sine processes
[
13
]
.
Denote the functions

S(x):=\frac{sin(\pi x)}{\pi x},\,IS(x)=\int_{0}^{x}S(x)dx.

The orthogonal sine process (or Pfaffian
\mathrm{Sine}_{1}
-process), is the
Pfaffian point process on
\mathbb{R}
with the matrix kernel

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}_{\mathrm{Sine}_{1}}(x,y)=ZK_{\mathrm{Sine}_{1}}(x,y),\quad K_{\mathrm{Sine}_{1}}(x,y)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}S(x-y)&S^{\prime}(x-y)-\frac{1}{2}sgn(x-y)\\
IS(x-y)&S(x-y),\end{array}\right)

where
Z=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
-1&0,\end{array}\right)
as before.
Similarly, the symplectic sine process (or Pfaffian
\mathrm{Sine}_{4}
process), is the Pfaffian point process on
\mathbb{R}
with
the matrix kernel

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}_{\mathrm{Sine}_{4}}(x,y)=ZK_{\mathrm{Sine}_{4}}(x,y),\quad K_{\mathrm{Sine}_{4}}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}S(x-y)&S^{\prime}(x-y)\\
IS(x-y)&S(x-y)\end{array}\right).

In the paper we will study the asymptotic behavior of linear statistics

\displaystyle S_{f}(\xi)=\int_{X}fd\xi=\sum\limits_{x\in\xi}f(x),\quad\xi\in{\rm Conf}(X),

for test functions
f
in a scaling limit. For the Pfaffian point process
\mathbb{P}
with the matrix kernel
\mathbb{K}
of the form (
1.1
), the expectation and variance of
S_{f}

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[S_{f}]=\int_{X}f(x)A(x,x)d\mu(x),

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbb{P}}(S_{f})=\int_{X}|f(x)|^{2}A(x,x)d\mu(x)-\int_{X^{2}}f(x)\overline{f(y)}\,\mathrm{det}K(x,y)d\mu(x)d\mu(y).

Central limit theorems (CLTs) for point processes have been established in many cases. For example, Leblé
[
20
]
proved a central limit theorem for the fluctuations of linear statistics in the
\mathrm{Sine}_{\beta}
process, requiring test functions to be of class
C^{4}
with compact support. His approach combined the DLR equations, Laplace transform techniques, and transportation methods. Subsequently, Lambert
[
19
]
eveloped a simpler proof of a CLT for linear statistics of circular
\beta
-ensembles, , which holds at nearly microscopic scales for
C^{3}
functions and consequently yields a central limit theorem for
\mathrm{Sine}_{\beta}
processes. Related results can be found in
[
2
,
32
]
, among other works.

The methods for determinantal point processes differ significantly from those used. The foundation was laid by Costin and Lebowitz
[
7
]
, who established a CLT for particle counts in the determinantal point process on
\mathbb{R}
with the kernel
\sin(\pi(x-y))/\pi(x-y).
Soshnikov
[
27
,
30
]
later extended this work, developing a comprehensive central limit theorem for general determinantal point processes with Hermitian kernels through the moment method. Breuer and Duits
[
4
]
further applied the moment method to obtain CLTs for orthogonal polynomial ensembles and biorthogonal ensembles where the underlying biorthogonal family satisfies a recurrence relation. For Pfaffian point processes, progress has been more limited. To date, the most general result is due to Kargin
[
17
]
, who proved a central limit theorem for Pfaffian point processes with finite-rank kernels.

In this work, we establish a central limit theorem for a broad class of Pfaffian point processes. Our approach introduces a new tool called the finite-rank commutator property (FRCP), which quantifies how closely the kernel admits a tractable symmetry structure. Our main result extends Soshnikov’s framework for determinantal processes to the Pfaffian setting under FRCP. Key examples include the Pfaffian
\mathrm{Sine}_{4}
and
\mathrm{Sine}_{1}
processes, which satisfy this property.

To formalize FRCP, we first recall essential operator constructions. Let

A,B,D,A^{{\dagger}}
denote the integral operator with kernel functions
a(x,y),b(x,y),d(x,y),a(y,x)
. Throughout the paper, when
A
is trace class, we assume
a(x,y)
is sufficiently smooth so that

\int_{X^{k}}a(x_{1},x_{2})a(x_{2},x_{3})\cdots a(x_{k},x_{1})\,d\mu(x_{1})\cdots d\mu(x_{k})=\mathrm{Tr}(A^{k})=\mathrm{Tr}(A^{{{\dagger}}k}),\quad k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}.

For
f,g\in L^{2}(X,\mu)
, we define the rank-1 operator
f\otimes g
by

\displaystyle(f\otimes g)(h)=\langle h,g\rangle_{L^{2}(X,\mu)}f,\quad\mathrm{for}\quad h\in L^{2}(X,\mu).

Then the definition of FRCP is as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a Pfaffian point process with the matrix-valued kernel

\mathbb{K}(x,y)=ZK(x,y),K(x,y)=\lambda\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}a(x,y)&d(x,y)\\
b(x,y)&a(y,x)\end{array}}\right)(\lambda=\frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{or}\,1)

defined on a domain
X
with respect to the measure
\mu
. We say
\mathbb{P}
has the finite-rank commutator property (FRCP) if there exist a constant

N

and functions

f^{(i)},g^{(i)},h^{(i)},e^{(i)}\in L^{2}(X,\mu)(i=1,\cdots,N),

such that

\displaystyle A^{{\dagger}}B-BA=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}f^{(i)}\otimes g^{(i)},

\displaystyle DB-(\alpha A^{2}+\beta A)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}h^{(i)}\otimes e^{(i)},

where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
satisfy
\alpha+\beta=\begin{cases}1,&\mathrm{if}\,\lambda=\frac{1}{2};\\
0,&\mathrm{if}\,\lambda=1,\end{cases}

The tuple
\{N,f^{(i)},g^{(i)},h^{(i)},e^{(i)},\alpha,\beta\}
parametrizes the FRCP for
\mathbb{P}
.

As demonstrated in Section
3
, both the both the Paffian
\mathrm{Sine}_{4}
-process and the Paffian
\mathrm{Sine}_{1}
-process have FRCP.

Building on this framework, we now present our central limit theorem for Pfaffian point processes with FRCP.

Theorem 1.2
.

Fixed an integer
N
. Consider a family
\{\mathbb{P}_{L}\}_{L\geqslant 0}
be a family of Pfaffian point processes having FRCP characterized by the data
\{N,f^{(i)}_{L},g^{(i)}_{L},h^{(i)}_{L},e^{(i)}_{L},\alpha_{L},\beta_{L}\}
with the matrix-valued kernel

\mathbb{K}_{L}(x,y)=ZK_{L}(x,y),K_{L}(x,y)=\lambda_{L}\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}a_{L}(x,y)&d_{L}(x,y)\\
b_{L}(x,y)&a_{L}(y,x)\end{array}}\right)(\lambda_{L}=\frac{1}{2}\,\,\text{or}\,\,1),

defined on the domain
X_{L}
with respect to the measure
\mu_{L}
. Under the following conditions:

(i)

\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbb{P}_{L}}(\#_{X_{L}})\rightarrow+\infty
as
L\rightarrow+\infty;

(ii)

Operators
A_{L},B_{L},D_{L},A^{{\dagger}}_{L}
are bounded with
\sup\limits_{L}(\|A_{L}\|+\|A^{{\dagger}}_{L}\|+|\alpha_{L}|+|\beta_{L}|)<+\infty;

(iii)

A_{L}
is trace-class and satisfies
\|A_{L}-A_{L}^{2}\|_{1}=o\big(\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbb{P}_{L}}(\#_{X_{L}})\big)^{\delta}
for some
\delta>0;

(iv)

for all
i,j=1,\cdots,N,
and
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0},
the inner products

\langle D_{L}A_{L}^{{{\dagger}}\,m}f^{(i)}_{L},{A_{L}}^{n}g^{(j)}_{L}\rangle,\langle D_{L}A_{L}^{{{\dagger}}\,m}f^{(i)}_{L},{A_{L}}^{n}e^{(j)}_{L}\rangle,\langle h^{(i)}_{L},{A_{t}}^{n}g^{(j)}_{L}\rangle,\langle h^{(i)}_{L},{A_{t}}^{n}e^{(j)}_{L}\rangle=o\big(\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbb{P}_{L}}(\#_{X_{L}})\big),

then the normalized counting statistic

\displaystyle\frac{\#_{X_{L}}-\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{L}}[\#_{X_{L}}]}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbb{P}_{L}}(\#_{X_{L}})}}

converges in distribution to
N(0,1)
as
L\rightarrow+\infty
.

\displaystyle K(x,y)^{T}=ZK(y,x)Z^{-1}.

This follows that

\displaystyle W_{n}^{T}

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}_{\mathrm{Sine}_{4}}(x,y)=ZK_{\mathrm{Sine}_{4}}(x,y),\quad K_{\mathrm{Sine}_{4}}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}S(x-y)&S^{\prime}(x-y)\\
IS(x-y)&S(x-y)\end{array}\right),

while

\displaystyle S(x)=\frac{\sin(\pi x)}{\pi x},\quad IS(x)=\int_{0}^{x}S(t)dt.

Clearly the functions
S
,
IS
and
S^{\prime}
are all bounded functions on
\mathbb{R}
. Furthermore,
It is well known that

\displaystyle S(\xi)=\frac{\sin(\pi\xi)}{\pi\xi}=\big(\chi_{[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]}\big)^{\textasciicircum}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i2\pi t\xi}\chi_{[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]}(t)dt.

Therefore, applying Plancherel theorem shows that
S\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
\|S\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=1
.

For
L>0
, let
I_{L}=(-L,L)
and denote by
\#_{L}
the number of the Pfaffian
{\mathrm{Sine}_{4}}
process in
I_{L}.
A straightforward calculation yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{Sine}_{4}}[\#_{L}]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{I_{L}}S(0)dx=L.

Following
[
7
]
, we obtain the asymptotic variance as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{Sine}_{4}}(\#_{L})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{I_{L}}S(0)dx-\int_{I_{L}^{2}}\mathrm{det}K_{{\mathrm{Sine}_{4}}}(x,y)dxdy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{I_{L}}S(0)dx-\frac{1}{4}\int_{I_{L}^{2}}[S^{2}(x-y)-S^{\prime}(x-y)IS(x-y)]dxdy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{I_{L}}S(0)dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{I_{L}^{2}}S^{2}(x-y)dxdy+\frac{1}{4}IS^{2}(2L)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\log L+O(1).

The third equality follows from integration by parts applied to functions
S^{\prime}(x-y)
and
IS(x-y)
.

We next check the FRCP for the Pfaffian
{\mathrm{Sine}_{4}}
process.

Proposition 3.1
.

For any
L>0,
the Pfaffian
{\mathrm{Sine}_{4}}
process on
I_{L}
has the finite rank commutator
property.

Proof.

Let
A_{L}=A_{L}^{{\dagger}},B_{L},D_{L}
be the integral operators on
L^{2}(I_{L})
with the integral kernel

\displaystyle A_{L}(x,y)=A_{L}^{{\dagger}}(x,y)=\chi_{I_{L}}(x)S(x-y)\chi_{I_{L}}(y),

\displaystyle B_{L}(x,y)=\chi_{I_{L}}(x)IS(x-y)\chi_{I_{L}}(y),

\displaystyle D_{L}(x,y)=\chi_{I_{L}}(x)S^{\prime}(x-y)\chi_{I_{L}}(y),

respectively.
It follows from the Fourier transform for
S(x),S^{\prime}(x)
that
0\leqslant A_{L}\leqslant 1,

\|D_{L}\|\leqslant\pi.
However,
\{B_{L}\}_{L>0}
are not uniformly bounded.

From the standard argument for the integral operators, the commutate
A_{L}B_{L}-B_{L}A_{L}
is also a integral operator with
the integral kernel

\displaystyle(A_{L}B_{L}-B_{L}A_{L})(x,y)

\displaystyle=\chi_{I_{L}}(x)\,\chi_{I_{L}}(y)\,\int_{I_{L}}\big(S(x-z)IS(z-y)-IS(x-z)S(z-y)\big)dz

\displaystyle=\big(IS(L-x)IS(L-y)-IS(-L-x)IS(-L-y)\big)\chi_{I_{L}}(x)\chi_{I_{L}}(y).

This implies that the commutate

(3.20)

A_{L}B_{L}-B_{L}A_{L}=IS(L-\cdot)\chi_{I_{L}}(\cdot)\otimes IS(L-\cdot)\chi_{I_{L}}(\cdot)-IS(L+\cdot)\chi_{I_{L}}(\cdot)\otimes IS(L+\cdot)\chi_{I_{L}}(\cdot)\\

is a rank-two operator on
L^{2}(I_{L})
.

Similarly, the integral operator
D_{L}B_{L}-A_{L}^{2}
has the integral kernel

\displaystyle(D_{L}B_{L}-A_{L}^{2})(x,y)

\displaystyle=\chi_{I_{L}}(x)\chi_{I_{L}}(y)\int_{I_{L}}\big(S^{\prime}(x-z)IS(z-y)-S(x-z)S(z-y)\big)dz

\displaystyle=\big(-S(L-x)IS(L-y)+S(-L-x)IS(-L-y)\big)\chi_{I_{L}}(x)\chi_{I_{L}}(y).

It follows that

(3.21)

D_{L}B_{L}-A_{L}^{2}=-S(L-\cdot)\chi_{I_{L}}(\cdot)\otimes IS(L-\cdot)\chi_{I_{L}}(\cdot)-S(L+\cdot)\chi_{I_{L}}(\cdot)\otimes IS(L+\cdot)\chi_{I_{L}}(\cdot)\\

is also a rank-two operator on
L^{2}(I_{L})
, completing the proof of the proposition.
∎

To verify the remaining conditions of Theorem
1.2
, we remark the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Fixed an integer
N
. Let
\{\mathbb{P}_{L}\}_{L\geqslant 0}
be a family of Pfaffian point processes having FRCP with
\{N,f^{(i)}_{L},g^{(i)}_{L},h^{(i)}_{L},e^{(i)}_{L},\alpha_{L},\beta_{L}\}
with the matrix-valued kernel function
\mathbb{K}_{L}(x,y)=ZK_{L}(x,y),

K_{L}(x,y)=\lambda_{L}\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}a_{L}(x,y)&d_{L}(x,y)\\
b_{L}(x,y)&a_{L}(y,x)\end{array}}\right)(\lambda_{L}=\frac{1}{2}\,or\,1),

over the domain
X_{L}
. Suppose that

\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbb{P}_{L}}(\#_{X_{L}})\rightarrow+\infty
as
L\rightarrow+\infty
, and

A_{L},B_{L},D_{L},A^{{\dagger}}_{L}
are bounded operators on
L^{2}(X_{L})
and
M=\sup\limits_{L}(\|A_{L}\|+\|A^{{\dagger}}_{L}\|+\|D_{L}\|)<+\infty.

If there are the decompositions

\displaystyle f_{L}^{(i)}=f_{L,1}^{(i)}+f_{L,2}^{(i)},\quad g_{L}^{(i)}=g_{L,1}^{(i)}+g_{L,2}^{(i)},\quad e_{L}^{(i)}=e_{L,1}^{(i)}+e_{L,2}^{(i)},\,i=1,\cdots,N

in
L^{2}(X_{L})
such that

\displaystyle\{f_{L,2}^{(i)},\penalty\ g_{L,2}^{(i)},\penalty\ e_{L,2}^{(i)},\penalty\ h_{L}^{(i)},\penalty\ A_{L}^{{\dagger}}f_{L,1}^{(i)}-f_{L,1}^{(i)},\penalty\ A_{L}g_{L,1}^{(i)}-g_{L,1}^{(i)},\penalty\ A_{L}e_{L,1}^{(i)}-e_{L,1}^{(i)},\penalty\ D_{L}f_{L,1}^{(i)}:i=1,\cdots,N,L\geqslant 0\}

are uniformly bounded by
M^{\prime}
with respect to
L^{2}
-norm, and

\displaystyle\langle D_{L}f_{L,1}^{(i)},g_{L,1}^{(j)}\rangle,\penalty\ \langle D_{L}f_{L,1}^{(i)},e_{L,1}^{(j)}\rangle,\penalty\ \langle h_{L}^{(i)},g_{L,1}^{(j)}\rangle,\penalty\ \langle h_{L}^{(i)},e_{L,1}^{(j)}\rangle=o\big(\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbb{P}_{L}}(\#_{X_{L}})\big),

then the statement of Theorem
1.2

\mathrm{(iv)}
holds.

Proof.

By the assumption of the Lemma, we have that
for nonnegative integer
m,n

\displaystyle D_{L}A_{L}^{{{\dagger}}\,m}f^{(i)}_{L}

\displaystyle=D_{L}A_{L}^{{{\dagger}}\,m}(f^{(i)}_{L,1}+f^{(i)}_{L,2})

\displaystyle=D_{L}f_{L,1}^{(i)}+D_{L}A_{L}^{{{\dagger}}\,m}f^{(i)}_{L,2}+\sum\limits_{s=0}^{m-1}D_{L}A_{L}^{{{\dagger}}\,s}(A_{L}f^{(i)}_{L,1}-f^{(i)}_{L,1}),

and

A_{L}^{n}g^{(j)}_{L}=g_{L,1}^{(j)}+A_{L}^{n}g^{(j)}_{L,2}+\sum\limits_{s=0}^{n-1}A_{L}^{s}(A_{L}g^{(j)}_{L,1}-g^{(j)}_{L,1}).

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\|D_{L}f_{L,1}^{(i)}\|\leqslant M^{\prime},\quad\big\|D_{L}A_{L}^{{{\dagger}}\,m}f^{(i)}_{L,2}+\sum\limits_{s=0}^{m-1}D_{L}A_{L}^{{{\dagger}}\,s}(A_{L}f^{(i)}_{L,1}-f^{(i)}_{L,1})\big\|\leqslant M^{m+1}M^{\prime}+mM^{s+1}M^{\prime},

and

\big\|A_{L}^{n}g^{(j)}_{L,2}+\sum\limits_{s=0}^{n-1}A_{L}^{s}(A_{L}g^{(j)}_{L,1}-g^{(j)}_{L,1})\big\|\leqslant M^{n}M^{\prime}+nM^{s}M^{\prime}.

This implies that

\displaystyle\langle D_{L}A_{L}^{{{\dagger}}\,m}f^{(i)}_{L},A_{L}^{n}g^{(j)}_{L}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle D_{L}f_{L,1}^{(i)}+[D_{L}A_{L}^{{{\dagger}}\,m}f^{(i)}_{L,2}+\sum\limits_{s=0}^{m-1}D_{L}A_{L}^{{{\dagger}}\,s}(A_{L}f^{(i)}_{L,1}-f^{(i)}_{L,1})],g_{L,1}^{(j)}+[A_{L}^{n}g^{(j)}_{L,2}+\sum\limits_{s=0}^{n-1}A_{L}^{s}(A_{L}g^{(j)}_{L,1}-g^{(j)}_{L,1})]\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle D_{L}f_{L,1}^{(i)},g_{L,1}^{(j)}\rangle+O(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle o\big(\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbb{P}_{L}}(\#_{X_{L}})\big).

The same argument yields analogous asymptotics for the other terms in Theorem
1.2
(iv), completing the proof.
∎

To verify the decomposition required by Lemma
3.2
for the Pfaffian
{\mathrm{Sine}_{4}}
process, the following crucial observation is essential.

Lemma 3.3
.

We have that

\big\|(IS-\frac{1}{2})\chi_{(0,+\infty)}\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant 1.

Proof.

When
0<x\leqslant 1
, it is clear that

IS(x)=\int_{0}^{x}S(u)du\leqslant 1.

When
x>1,
by the fact that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}S(x)dx=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\sin(\pi x)}{\pi x}dx=\frac{1}{2},

we have that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}-IS(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{x}^{+\infty}\frac{sin(\pi u)}{\pi u}du

\displaystyle=\int_{x}^{\lfloor x\rfloor+1}\frac{sin(\pi u)}{\pi u}du+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{+\infty}\int_{\lfloor x\rfloor+k}^{\lfloor x\rfloor+k+1}\frac{sin(\pi u)}{\pi u}du.

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{x}^{\lfloor x\rfloor+1}\big|\frac{sin(\pi u)}{\pi u}\big|du+\int_{\lfloor x\rfloor+1}^{\lfloor x\rfloor+2}\big|\frac{sin(\pi u)}{\pi u}\big|du

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{2}{\pi x},

where the inequality in the third line originates from the treatment of alternating series. This implies that

\displaystyle\big\|(IS-\frac{1}{2})\chi_{(0,+\infty)}\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{0}^{1}+\int_{1}^{+\infty}\Big)\big|IS(x)-\frac{1}{2})\big|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{4}+\int_{1}^{+\infty}\big(\frac{2}{\pi x}\big)^{2}dx\leqslant 1,

which attains Lemma
3.3
.
∎

We begin with a general preparatory result.

For

\displaystyle D_{L}\chi_{I_{L}^{(i)}}B_{t}-(\alpha_{L}AL\chi_{I_{L}^{(i)}}+\beta_{L}A_{L}\chi_{I_{L}^{(i)}}A_{L})=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{M}h_{L}^{(i,j)}\otimes e_{L}^{(i,j)},

where
f^{(i,j)}_{L},g_{L}^{(i,j)},h_{L}^{(i,j)},e_{L}^{(i,j)}\in L^{2}(X_{L})
for
i=1,\cdots,N,

j=1,\cdots,M
, and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
with
\alpha_{L}+\beta_{L}=\begin{cases}1,&\mathrm{if}\,\lambda=\frac{1}{2};\\
0,&\mathrm{if}\,\lambda=1.\end{cases}

\sup\limits_{L}|\alpha_{L}|+|\beta_{L}|<\infty;

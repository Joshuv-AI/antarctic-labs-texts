Phase transition for recurrence of stationary random walks on lamplighter groups
Itai Benjamini, Guy Blachar, Ariel Yadin

Abstract
. We introduce and study a class of random walks on lamplighter groups H ≀ G H\wr G , where H H is a nontrivial finitely generated group and G G is an infinite finitely generated group, called stationary random walks . At each step, the walk switches the lamp at its current position, moves in the base group with a drift towards the identity, and switches the lamp again at the new position. We show that when G G is virtually- ℤ \mathbb{Z} and H H is finite, these walks exhibit a phase transition between recurrence and transience, while when G G is not virtually- ℤ \mathbb{Z} or H H is infinite, they are always transient. In the case G = ℤ G=\mathbb{Z} , we determine the exact critical parameter and provide a quantitative description of this phase transition.



1.
Introduction

Consider the lamplighter group
F\wr\mathbb{Z}
, where
F
is a nontrivial finite group. Elements of this group can be described as pairs
((L(i))_{i\in\mathbb{Z}},x)
, where
(L(i))_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\bigoplus_{\mathbb{Z}}F
and
x\in\mathbb{Z}
. One may think of
(L(i))_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
as a lamp configuration on
\mathbb{Z}
, in which an
F
-valued lamp is placed at each point of
\mathbb{Z}
, but only finitely many are allowed to be nontrivial. The element
x\in\mathbb{Z}
represents the position of a lamplighter, who walks along the
\mathbb{Z}
-axis and can switch the lamps’ values. Such groups play a central role in geometric group theory, particularly in the study of random walks on groups.

This paper studies a family of random walks
R_{n}
on the group
F\wr\mathbb{Z}
, which we refer to as
stationary random walks
. For a fixed parameter
\frac{1}{2}<p<1
, we define
R_{n}
to be a specific switch-walk-switch random walk on the group: at each step, the walk first refreshes the lamp at its current location on the
\mathbb{Z}
-axis (“switch”), then moves along the
\mathbb{Z}
-axis (“walk”), and then refreshes the lamp at its new location (“switch”). We assume the switches are uniform on
F
, while the walk on
\mathbb{Z}
has a constant drift
p
towards the origin.

Formally, the projection of
R_{n}
on
\mathbb{Z}
, denoted
S_{n}
, is given by

\mathbb{P}(S_{n+1}=x+1\mid S_{n}=x)=\begin{cases}p,&x<0\\
\frac{1}{2},&x=0\\
1-p,&x>0\end{cases}

and

\mathbb{P}(S_{n+1}=x-1\mid S_{n}=x)=\begin{cases}1-p,&x<0\\
\frac{1}{2},&x=0\\
p,&x>0.\end{cases}

The corresponding random walk
R_{n}
on the lamplighter group is then defined as follows: Let
(S_{n})_{n}
denote the above random walk on
\mathbb{Z}
, and let
(U_{n},V_{n})_{n}
be i.i.d. random variables uniformly distributed on
F
. Define
R_{n}=((L_{n}(i))_{i\in\mathbb{Z}},S_{n})
, where
L_{n+1}
is obtained from
L_{n}
via

(1)

L_{n+1}(i)=\begin{cases}L_{n}(i)&\textrm{ if }i\not\in\{S_{n},S_{n+1}\},\\
U_{n+1}&\textrm{ if }i=S_{n},\\
V_{n+1}&\textrm{ if }i=S_{n+1}.\end{cases}

We study the asymptotic behaviour of the walk depending on the value of the parameter
p
. In particular, regarding the recurrence or transience of
R_{n}
, we obtain the following phase transition:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
R_{n}
denote the stationary random walk on
F\wr\mathbb{Z}
with parameter
p
. Then
R_{n}
is recurrent if
p\geq\frac{\left|F\right|^{2}}{\left|F\right|^{2}+1}
, and is transient if
p<\frac{\left|F\right|^{2}}{\left|F\right|^{2}+1}
.

We give a quantitative version of the above theorem by considering the local time of the walk. We denote the local time of
R_{n}
at a point
a\in F\wr\mathbb{Z}
until time
n
by

\xi(a,n)=\left|\left\{0\leq k\leq n\,|\,R_{k}=a\right\}\right|.

For two sequences
(\alpha_{n})_{n},(\beta_{n})_{n}
of positive real numbers, we write
\alpha_{n}\lesssim\beta_{n}
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that
\alpha_{n}\leq C\beta_{n}
for all
n
, and we write
\alpha_{n}\simeq\beta_{n}
if
\alpha_{n}\lesssim\beta_{n}
and
\beta_{n}\lesssim\alpha_{n}
. We show:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
R_{n}
denote the stationary random walk on
F\wr\mathbb{Z}
with parameter
p
. Then

(2)

\mathbb{E}[\xi(\mathsf{id},n)]\simeq\begin{cases}1,&p<\frac{\left|F\right|^{2}}{\left|F\right|^{2}+1}\\
\log n,&p=\frac{\left|F\right|^{2}}{\left|F\right|^{2}+1}\\
n^{1-2\alpha},&p>\frac{\left|F\right|^{2}}{\left|F\right|^{2}+1}\end{cases}

where
\alpha=\frac{\log\left|F\right|}{\log\frac{p}{1-p}}
, and
\mathsf{id}
denotes the identity element of
F\wr\mathbb{Z}
. (The implied constants of (
2
) depend on
p
and
\left|F\right|
.)

It would also be interesting to estimate other quantitative aspects of stationary random walks, such as the expected range of
R_{n}
. We expect it to grow polynomially in
n
, with an exponent that depends on
p
and equals
1
when
p<\frac{\left|F\right|^{2}}{\left|F\right|^{2}+1}
. However, we were not able to establish concrete bounds.

We further study whether this phenomenon holds when replacing
\mathbb{Z}
by another finitely generated group
G
, and
F
by an arbitrary finitely generated group
H
. In this setting, we define the stationary random walk on
H\wr G
analogously, where the walk on
G
is the
\lambda
-homesick random walk
. Homesick random walks are a notion of random walks on groups with drift towards the identity (or towards the root of a rooted graph), where the parameter
\lambda\geq 1
controls the drift of the walk –
\lambda=1
corresponds to the simple random walk, and the larger
\lambda
is, the stronger the drift (see precise definition in Section
4
). For
G=\mathbb{Z}
with the standard generating set
S=\left\{\pm 1\right\}
, the
\lambda
-homesick random walk reduces to the biased walk on
\mathbb{Z}
with constant drift
p=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+1}
towards
0
, as above.

We show that the above phase transition extends to the case where the group
G
is virtually-
\mathbb{Z}
, i.e.:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G
be a virtually-
\mathbb{Z}
group, and let
H
be a nontrivial finite group. Then there exists
1<\lambda_{c}<\infty
such that the
\lambda
-stationary random walk on
F\wr G
is transient if
\lambda<\lambda_{c}
, and recurrent if
\lambda>\lambda_{c}
. Moreover, the value of
\lambda_{c}
depends on
H
only through
|H|
.

In fact, the theorem also holds when the switches are made according to some finitely supported, symmetric and non-degenerate random walk, and in this case the value of
\lambda_{c}
does not change. We conjecture that the critical behaviour is like the case
G=\mathbb{Z}
:

Conjecture 1.4
.

If
G
is virtually-
\mathbb{Z}
and
H
is a nontrivial finite group, then the
\lambda
-stationary random walk on
H\wr G
is recurrent also for
\lambda=\lambda_{c}
.

Finally, we show that the phase transition does not occur in any other case:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
G
and
H
be finitely generated groups, where
G
is infinite and
H
is nontrivial. If
G
is not virtually-
\mathbb{Z}
, or if
H
is infinite (and the switches are made according to some finitely supported, symmetric and non-degenerate random walk), then the
\lambda
-stationary random walk on
H\wr G
is transient for every
\lambda\geq 1
.

Remark 1.6
.

The notion of stationary random walks extends naturally to lamplighter graphs
\Gamma^{\prime}\wr\Gamma
. We discuss this in Section
7
.

Remark 1.7
.

Similar phenomena to those described above also hold for the homesick random walks themselves. Lyons
[
5
]
showed that a
\lambda
-homesick random walk
R_{n}
on a Cayley graph
\mathrm{Cay}(G,S)
is transient if
1\leq\lambda<\rho(G,S)\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\left|S^{n}\right|^{1/n}
, and is recurrent if
\lambda>\rho(G,S)
. Furthermore, Lyons–Pemantle–Peres
[
6
]
and Revelle
[
7
]
studied the
\lambda
-homesick random walk on the classical lamplighter group
(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})\wr\mathbb{Z}
, showing that when the walk is transient it has positive speed (even though the speed of the simple random walk on
(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})\wr\mathbb{Z}
is zero).

In contrast, the bias of the stationary random walks on
F\wr G
is not directed towards a specific vertex of the Cayley graph, but depends only on the base group
G
. Moreover, the recurrence–transience phase transition occurs only when
G
is one-dimensional, whereas Lyons’ result applies to any group.

Main ideas and outline of the paper

Consider the
\lambda
-stationary random walk
R_{n}
on
H\wr G
, where
H
is a finite group, and the switch measure is uniform on
H
. The phase transition between transience and recurrence stems from the fact that conditioned on the path of the projection
Z_{n}
of
R_{n}
on
G
, the values of the lamps along the path are independent and uniform on
H
. Conditioning further on
Z_{n}=\mathsf{id}_{G}
, we have

(3)

\mathbb{P}(R_{n}=\mathsf{id}\,|\,Z_{1},...,Z_{n-1},Z_{n}=\mathsf{id}_{G})=\left|H\right|^{-\left|\operatorname{range}(Z_{n})\right|},

where
\operatorname{range}(Z_{n})=\left\{Z_{0},\dots,Z_{n}\right\}
.

In general, when the random walk
Z_{n}
is recurrent, we expect that
\operatorname{range}(Z_{n})
will be close to a ball of radius
C\log n
in the Cayley graph of
G
(where
C
depends on
\lambda
). When
G
is virtually-
\mathbb{Z}
, the size of a ball in its Cayley graph is linear in its radius. The constant
C
decreases as
\lambda
grows, so when
\lambda
is sufficiently close to
1
the probabilities (
3
) will be summable, whereas when
\lambda
is sufficiently large they will not be summable. This gives the claimed phase transition. When
G
is not virtually-
\mathbb{Z}
, the size of balls in the Cayley graph grows at least quadratically in the radius, and thus (
3
) is summable for all
\lambda>1
, so the walk is transient for every value of
\lambda>1
. When
H
is infinite, the probability that a given lamp is
\mathsf{id}_{H}
tends to
0
as
n
tends to
\infty
, and the return probabilities of
R_{n}
are again summable.

When
G=\mathbb{Z}
and
S=\left\{\pm 1\right\}
is the standard generating set, we are able to give precise asymptotic estimates on the range of the projection to
\mathbb{Z}
. This is done in Section
2
, where we prove Theorem
1.1
, and in Section
3
, where we analyze the local time and prove Theorem
1.2
.

In Section
4
we recall the definition of homesick random walks on rooted graphs, and prove two concentration results on their range, showing that they typically stay close to a ball of radius
C\log n
(these bounds are not sharp, but sufficient for our applications). In Section
5
we present the definition of stationary random walks on arbitrary lamplighter groups and establish their basic properties. In Section
6
we combine the results of Section
4
and Section
5
to prove Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.5
. Finally, in Section
7
we discuss the generalization of stationary random walks to lamplighter graphs.

2.
Recurrence and transience over
\mathbb{Z}

In this section, we analyze the stationary random walk
R_{n}
on
F\wr\mathbb{Z}
for a given parameter
\frac{1}{2}<p<1
. As in the Introduction, we write
R_{n}=((L_{n}(i))_{i},S_{n})
, where
(L_{n}(i))_{i}\in\bigoplus_{\mathbb{Z}}F
denotes the lamp configuration of
R_{n}
, and
S_{n}
is a random walk on
\mathbb{Z}
with drift
p
towards the origin.

We denote by
\rho_{0},\rho_{1},\dots
the successive times at which
S_{n}
hits
0
. In other words,
\rho_{0}=0
, and for any
k\geq 1
we write

\rho_{k}=\inf\left\{n>\rho_{k-1}\,|\,S_{n}=0\right\}.

We remark that
\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\dots
are almost surely finite, since the random walk
S_{n}
is recurrent on
\mathbb{Z}
. We call each segment
R_{\rho_{k-1}},R_{\rho_{k-1}+1},\dots,R_{\rho_{k}}
an
excursion
.

Remark 2.1
.

R_{n}
is recurrent if and only if
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id})=\infty
. Indeed, for every
n
with
R_{n}=\mathsf{id}
we must have
S_{n}=0
, showing that the number of visits of the random walk
R_{n}
to
\mathsf{id}
is

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{R_{n}=\mathsf{id}\right\}}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id}\right\}}.

We therefore focus on studying the behaviour of the random walk
R_{n}
at the sequence of times
(\rho_{k})_{k}
.

For any
k\geq 1
, we write

M_{k}^{+}=\max_{0\leq j\leq\rho_{k}}S_{j}

and

M_{k}^{-}=\min_{0\leq j\leq\rho_{k}}S_{j}.

Proposition 2.2
.

For any
k\geq 1
,

\mathbb{P}(R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id}\mid S_{0},S_{1},\dots,S_{\rho_{k}})=\left|F\right|^{-(M_{k}^{+}-M_{k}^{-}+1)}

Proof.

We first recall that
S_{\rho_{k}}=0
, so
R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id}
if and only if
L_{\rho_{k}}(i)=\mathsf{id}_{F}
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. By the definition of
R_{n}
, if
i\notin[M_{k}^{-},M_{k}^{+}]
then
L_{n}(i)=\mathsf{id}_{F}
.

We claim that, conditional on
S_{0},\ldots,S_{\rho_{k}}
, the variables
(L_{\rho_{k}}(i))_{i\in[M_{k}^{-},M_{k}^{+}]}
are independent and uniform on
F
, which immediately implies the proposition. Let us prove this (very intuitive, but subtle) claim.

For each
i\in[M_{k}^{-},M_{k}^{+}]
we let
1\leq n_{i}\leq\rho_{k}
be the last time the random walk
S_{n}
is at
i
, that is,

n_{i}=\max\left\{1\leq t\leq\rho_{k}\,|\,S_{t}=i\right\}.

We note that
0<n_{i}<\rho_{k}
when
i\neq 0
, and by (
1
) we have that
L_{\rho_{k}}(i)=U_{n_{i}+1}
for all
0\neq i\in[M_{k}^{-},M_{k}^{+}]
. The lamp at
i=0
is slightly special: here
n_{0}=\rho_{k}
, and so
L_{\rho_{k}}(0)=V_{\rho_{k}}
.

Therefore
R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id}
if and only if
L_{\rho_{k}}(i)=\mathsf{id}_{F}
for all
i\in[M_{k}^{-},M_{k}^{+}]
, which is in turn is equivalent to

\forall\ i\in[M_{k}^{-},M_{k}^{+}]\setminus\{0\}\ ,\ U_{n_{i}+1}=\mathsf{id}_{F}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad V_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id}_{F}.

Conditioned on
S_{0},\ldots,S_{\rho_{k}}
, this last event has probability
\left|F\right|^{-|[M_{k}^{-},M_{k}^{+}]|}
, proving the proposition.
∎

We next consider the distributions of
M_{k}^{+}
and
M_{k}^{-}
. Since the random walks
(S_{n})_{n}
and
(-S_{n})_{n}
have the same distribution, also
M_{k}^{+}
and
-M_{k}^{-}
have the same distribution. We therefore focus on
M_{k}^{+}
. By definition,
M_{k}^{+}
is the maximum of all positive excursions of
S_{n}
until time
\rho_{k}
. For a single positive excursion, its maximum has the following distribution:

Lemma 2.3
.

For any integer
x\geq 0
,

\mathbb{P}(M_{1}^{+}\leq x\mid S_{1}=1)=1-\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda^{x+1}-1},

where
\lambda=\frac{p}{1-p}
.

Proof.

Fix a non-negative integer
x\geq 0
. We write

\tau_{x+1}=\inf\left\{t\geq 0\,|\,S_{t}=x+1\right\}

and

\tau_{0}^{+}=\inf\left\{t\geq 1\,|\,S_{t}=0\right\}.

Both
\tau_{x+1}
and
\tau_{0}^{+}
are almost surely finite, since the walk
(S_{n})_{n}
is recurrent. We next note that
(S_{n})_{0\leq n\leq\tau_{0}^{+}\wedge\tau_{x+1}}
, conditioned on
S_{1}=1
, can be described using an electrical network, where the edge
(i,i+1)
has resistance
\lambda^{i}
for all
i\geq 0
. The vertices
0
and
x+1
are connected by a series of edges, and thus the effective resistance between them is

\mathcal{R}_{\text{eff}}(0\leftrightarrow x+1)=\sum_{i=0}^{x}\lambda^{i}=\frac{\lambda^{x+1}-1}{\lambda-1},

and thus

\mathbb{P}(M_{1}^{+}\leq x\mid S_{1}=1)=\mathbb{P}(\tau_{0}^{+}<\tau_{x+1})=1-\frac{1}{\mathcal{R}_{\text{eff}}(0\leftrightarrow x+1)}=1-\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda^{x+1}-1}

as required.
∎

Therefore, to study the distributions of
M_{k}^{+}
and
M_{k}^{-}
, we need to know how many excursions are positive and how many are negative. We write, for any
k\geq 1
, the number of positive excursions until time
\rho_{k}
as

N_{k}^{+}=\left|\left\{1\leq j\leq k\,|\,S_{\rho_{j-1}+1}>0\right\}\right|

and the number of negative excursions until time
\rho_{k}
as

N_{k}^{-}=\left|\left\{1\leq j\leq k\,|\,S_{\rho_{j-1}+1}<0\right\}\right|,

so that
N_{k}^{+}+N_{k}^{-}=k
.
We remark for later that (by the strong Markov property) the excursions are independent, so the distribution of
N_{k}^{+}
(as well as
N_{k}^{-}
) is binomial with parameters
k
and
\frac{1}{2}
.

Proposition 2.4
.

For any
k\geq 1
and
0\leq m\leq k
,

\mathbb{P}(R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id}\mid N_{k}^{+}=m)\\
=\frac{(\left|F\right|-1)^{2}}{\left|F\right|}\left(\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}\left|F\right|^{-a}\left(1-\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda^{a}-1}\right)^{m}\right)\left(\sum_{b=1}^{\infty}\left|F\right|^{-b}\left(1-\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda^{b}-1}\right)^{k-m}\right),

where
\lambda=\frac{p}{1-p}
.

Proof.

We first note that, by the strong Markov property, the excursions
((S_{\rho_{k-1}},\ldots,S_{\rho_{k}}))_{k\geq 1}
are independent. Therefore, conditioned on the value of
N_{k}^{+}
, the random variables
M_{k}^{+}
and
M_{k}^{-}
are independent. Thus, by Proposition
2.2
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id}\mid N_{k}^{+}=m]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\left|F\right|^{-M_{k}^{+}+M_{k}^{-}-1}\mid N_{k}^{+}=m]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left|F\right|}\mathbb{E}[\left|F\right|^{-M_{k}^{+}}\mid N_{k}^{+}=m]\cdot\mathbb{E}[\left|F\right|^{M_{k}^{-}}\mid N_{k}^{+}=m].

The measure preserving transformation
(S_{n})_{n}\mapsto(-S_{n})_{n}
shows that the distribution of
M_{k}^{-}
conditioned on
N_{k}^{+}=m
is the same as that of
-M_{k}^{+}
conditioned on
N_{k}^{+}=k-m
. Therefore,

\mathbb{P}[R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id}\mid N_{k}^{+}=m]=\frac{1}{\left|F\right|}\mathbb{E}[\left|F\right|^{-M_{k}^{+}}\mid N_{k}^{+}=m]\cdot\mathbb{E}[\left|F\right|^{-M_{k}^{+}}\mid N_{k}^{+}=k-m].

We next observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\left|F\right|^{-M_{k}^{+}}\mid N_{k}^{+}=m]

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}\left|F\right|^{-a}\mathbb{P}(M_{k}^{+}=a\mid N_{k}^{+}=m)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}(\left|F\right|^{-a}-\left|F\right|^{-a-1})\mathbb{P}(M_{k}^{+}\leq a\mid N_{k}^{+}=m)

\displaystyle=(\left|F\right|-1)\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}\left|F\right|^{-a-1}\big(\mathbb{P}(M_{1}^{+}\leq a\mid S_{1}=1)\big)^{m}

\displaystyle=(\left|F\right|-1)\sum_{a=2}^{\infty}\left|F\right|^{-a}\Big(1-\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda^{a}-1}\Big)^{m}

where in the third line we have used the fact that the excursions are independent, so that

\mathbb{P}(M_{k}^{+}\leq a\mid N_{k}^{+}=m)=\big(\mathbb{P}(M_{1}^{+}\leq a\mid S_{1}=1)\big)^{m},

and we have also used Lemma
2.3
.
∎

We now analyze the factors in the above proposition.

Lemma 2.5
.

Set
\alpha=\frac{\log\left|F\right|}{\log\lambda}
for
\lambda=\frac{p}{1-p}
. Then, for any integer
m
larger than some constant depending on
p,\left|F\right|
, we have

\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}\left|F\right|^{-a}\left(1-\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda^{a}-1}\right)^{m}\simeq\frac{1}{m^{\alpha}},

where the implied constants depend on
p
and
\left|F\right|
.

Proof.

For the upper bound, we note that

\left|F\right|^{-a}\left(1-\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda^{a}-1}\right)^{m}\leq\left|F\right|^{-a}e^{-\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda^{a}-1}m}\leq\left|F\right|^{-a}e^{-\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda^{a}}m}=e^{-a\log\left|F\right|-\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda^{a}}m}.

Let
f(a)=-a\log\left|F\right|-\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda^{a}}m
. The maximum of
f
is achieved when

f^{\prime}(a)=-\log\left|F\right|+\frac{(\lambda-1)m\log\lambda}{\lambda^{a}}=0

i.e., when
a=a_{0}\coloneqq\log_{\lambda}\frac{(\lambda-1)m}{\alpha}
, so that
\lambda^{a_{0}}=\frac{(\lambda-1)m}{\alpha}
. In this case,

e^{f(a_{0})}=\left|F\right|^{-a_{0}}e^{-\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda^{a_{0}}}m}=\frac{1}{(\lambda^{a_{0}})^{\alpha}}e^{-\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda^{a_{0}}}m}=\left(\frac{\alpha}{(\lambda-1)em}\right)^{\alpha}.

Note that if
a\leq a_{0}
then
\lambda^{a}\leq\frac{(\lambda-1)m}{\alpha}
, and thus

f(a)-f(a-1)=-\log\left|F\right|+\frac{(\lambda-1)^{2}}{\lambda^{a}}m\geq-\log\left|F\right|+\alpha(\lambda-1)>0

since
\lambda-1>\log\lambda
. Therefore
e^{f(a-1)}\leq C_{1}e^{f(a)}
for
C_{1}=e^{\alpha(\lambda-1)-\log\left|F\right|}>1
, showing that

\sum_{a\leq a_{0}}e^{f(a)}\leq\frac{C_{1}}{C_{1}-1}e^{f(a_{0})}=\frac{C_{2}}{m^{\alpha}}

for a constant
C_{2}
that depends on
p,\left|F\right|
, while

\sum_{a>a_{0}}e^{f(a)}=\sum_{a>a_{0}}\left|F\right|^{-a}e^{-\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda^{a}}m}\leq\sum_{a>a_{0}}\left|F\right|^{-a}=\frac{\left|F\right|^{-a_{0}}}{\left|F\right|-1}=\frac{1}{\left|F\right|-1}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\lambda-1}\right)^{\alpha}\frac{1}{m^{\alpha}}.

Together we get

\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}\left|F\right|^{-a}\left(1-\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda^{a}-1}\right)^{m}\lesssim\frac{1}{m^{\alpha}}.

For the lower bound, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}\left|F\right|^{-a}\left(1-\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda^{a}-1}\right)^{m}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}\left|F\right|^{-a}\left(1-\frac{1}{\lambda^{a-1}}\right)^{m}

\displaystyle\geq\left|F\right|^{-\left\lceil a_{0}\right\rceil+1}\left(1-\frac{1}{\lambda^{\left\lceil a_{0}\right\rceil}}\right)^{m}

\displaystyle\geq\left|F\right|^{-(a_{0}+2)}\left(1-\frac{1}{\lambda^{a_{0}}}\right)^{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left|F\right|^{2}}\left(\frac{\alpha}{(\lambda-1)m}\right)^{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\alpha\lambda}{(\lambda-1)m}\right)^{m}.

Recall that
\left(1-\frac{x}{m}\right)^{m}\geq e^{-x}\left(1-\frac{x^{2}}{m}\right)
for all
m\geq 1
and
\left|x\right|\leq m
, hence for
m\geq 2\left(\frac{\alpha\lambda}{\lambda-1}\right)^{2}
we have
\left(1-\frac{\alpha\lambda}{(\lambda-1)m}\right)^{m}\geq\frac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{\alpha\lambda}{\lambda-1}}
, showing that

\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}\left|F\right|^{-a}\left(1-\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda^{a}-1}\right)^{m}\geq\frac{1}{2\left|F\right|^{2}}e^{-\frac{\alpha\lambda}{\lambda-1}}\frac{1}{m^{\alpha}},

proving that

\frac{1}{m^{\alpha}}\lesssim\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}\left|F\right|^{-a}\left(1-\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda^{a}-1}\right)^{m}.\qed

We can now conclude:

Corollary 2.6
.

Set
\alpha=\frac{\log\left|F\right|}{\log\lambda}
for
\lambda=\frac{p}{1-p}
. Then, for any integer
k
larger than some constant depending on
p,\left|F\right|
, we have

\mathbb{P}(R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id})\simeq\frac{1}{k^{2\alpha}},

where the implied constants depend on
p,\left|F\right|
.

Proof.

Note first that by Hoeffding’s inequality,

\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{k}{3}\leq N_{k}^{+}\leq\frac{2k}{3}\right)\leq 2e^{-\frac{k}{18}}.

For any
\frac{k}{3}\leq m\leq\frac{2k}{3}
, if
N_{k}^{+}=m
then
\frac{k}{3}\leq N_{k}^{-}=k-m\leq\frac{2k}{3}
. Therefore, when
k
is larger than a constant depending on
p,\left|F\right|
we may apply Lemma
2.5
and deduce

\frac{c}{k^{\alpha}}\leq\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}\left|F\right|^{-a}\left(1-\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda^{a}-1}\right)^{m}\leq\frac{C}{k^{\alpha}}

and

\frac{c}{k^{\alpha}}\leq\sum_{b=1}^{\infty}\left|F\right|^{-b}\left(1-\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda^{b}-1}\right)^{k-m}\leq\frac{C}{k^{\alpha}}

for
c,C>0
constants which depend on
p,\left|F\right|
. Plugging this into Proposition
2.4
, we have

\frac{c^{2}}{k^{2\alpha}}-2e^{-\frac{k}{18}}\leq\mathbb{P}(R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id})\leq\frac{C^{2}}{2k^{2\alpha}}+2e^{-\frac{k}{18}},

which implies the corollary.
∎

We are ready to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By Remark
2.1
, the random walk
(R_{n})_{n}
is recurrent if and only if
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id})=\infty
. Applying Corollary
2.6
, with
\alpha=\frac{\log\left|F\right|}{\log\lambda}
and
\lambda=\frac{p}{1-p}
, this happens if and only if

2\alpha\leq 1\iff\frac{p}{1-p}=\lambda\geq\left|F\right|^{2}\iff p\geq\frac{\left|F\right|^{2}}{\left|F\right|^{2}+1}.\qed

3.
Local time over
\mathbb{Z}

Our next goal is to prove Theorem
1.2
. To do this, we first estimate the local time of
S_{n}
at
0
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
m=\frac{2p}{2p-1}
. Then:

(1)

\mathbb{E}[\rho_{k}]=km
for every
k\geq 1
.

(2)

For any
\varepsilon>0
there exists a constant
c=c(p,\varepsilon)>0
such that

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\rho_{k}-km\right|\geq\varepsilon k\right)\leq 2\exp(-ck)

for all
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

We first note that

(4)

\rho_{k}=\rho_{1}+(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1})+\cdots+(\rho_{k}-\rho_{k-1}),

and by the strong Markov property,
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}-\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{k}-\rho_{k-1}
are independent and identically distributed. Therefore
\mathbb{E}[\rho_{k}]=\mathbb{E}[\rho_{1}]k
.

Next, we note that the distribution of
\rho_{1}
is

\mathbb{P}(\rho_{1}=2t)=C_{t-1}p^{t}(1-p)^{t-1}

for all
t\geq 1
, where
C_{t-1}=\frac{1}{t}\binom{2t-2}{t-1}
is the
(t-1)
-th Catalan number. Its expected value is therefore

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\rho_{1}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}2tC_{t-1}p^{t}(1-p)^{t-1}=2p\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\binom{2t-2}{t-1}(p(1-p))^{t-1}

\displaystyle=2p\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-4p(1-p)}}=\frac{2p}{2p-1}=m,

where the third equality uses
\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\binom{2t-2}{t-1}x^{t}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-4x}}
. With the above, we showed that
\mathbb{E}[\rho_{k}]=\mathbb{E}[\rho_{1}]k=km
, proving the first part of the proposition.

For the second part, we note that the moment generating function of
\rho_{1}
is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[e^{s\rho_{1}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}e^{2st}C_{t-1}p^{t}(1-p)^{t-1}=pe^{2s}\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}C_{t-1}(p(1-p)e^{2s})^{t-1}

\displaystyle=pe^{2s}\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4p(1-p)e^{2s}}}{2p(1-p)e^{2s}}=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4p(1-p)e^{2s}}}{2(1-p)}.

Write
\psi(s)\coloneqq\log\mathbb{E}[e^{s\rho_{1}}]-sm
. The function
\psi
is differentiable continuously twice whenever
s<\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1}{4p(1-p)}\eqqcolon s_{0}
. We also note that
\psi(0)=\psi^{\prime}(0)=0
, and write
c_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\sup_{\left|s\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}s_{0}}\left|\psi^{\prime\prime}(s)\right|
. By Taylor’s expansion, for any
\left|s\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}s_{0}
we have

\psi(s)=\psi(0)+\psi^{\prime}(0)s+\frac{1}{2}\psi^{\prime\prime}(s^{\prime})s^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\psi^{\prime\prime}(s^{\prime})s^{2}\leq c_{0}s^{2}

where
s^{\prime}
is a point which lies between
0
and
s
.

We may now apply Markov’s inequality and (
4
) to deduce that for every
\varepsilon>0
,

\mathbb{P}(\rho_{k}\geq(m+\varepsilon)k)\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}[e^{s\rho_{k}}]}{e^{sk(m+\varepsilon)}}=\frac{\mathbb{E}[e^{s\rho_{1}}]^{k}}{e^{sk(m+\varepsilon)}}=e^{k(\psi(s)-\varepsilon s)}\leq e^{k(c_{0}s^{2}-\varepsilon s)}

and

\mathbb{P}(\rho_{k}\leq(m-\varepsilon)k)\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}[e^{-s\rho_{k}}]}{e^{-sk(m-\varepsilon)}}=\frac{\mathbb{E}[e^{-s\rho_{1}}]^{k}}{e^{-sk(m-\varepsilon)}}=e^{k(\psi(-s)-\varepsilon s)}\leq e^{k(c_{0}s^{2}-\varepsilon s)}.

Taking
s=\min\left\{\frac{\varepsilon}{2c_{0}},\frac{1}{2}s_{0}\right\}
, we get

\mathbb{P}(\left|\rho_{k}-km\right|\geq\varepsilon k)\leq 2e^{-ck}

for some constant
c>0
depending on
p,\varepsilon
, as required.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
m=\frac{2p}{2p-1}
.
Writing
A=\left\{\rho_{\left\lfloor n/2m\right\rfloor}\leq n\leq\rho_{\left\lceil 2n/m\right\rceil}\right\}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\xi(\mathsf{id},n)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\xi(\mathsf{id},n)\mathbbm{1}_{A}]+\mathbb{E}[\xi(\mathsf{id},n)\mathbbm{1}_{A^{c}}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[\xi(\mathsf{id},\rho_{\left\lceil 2n/m\right\rceil})]+n\mathbb{P}(A^{c})

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\xi(\mathsf{id},n)]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}[\xi(\mathsf{id},n)\mathbbm{1}_{A}]\geq\mathbb{E}[\xi(\mathsf{id},\rho_{\left\lfloor n/2m\right\rfloor})\mathbbm{1}_{A}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\xi(\mathsf{id},\rho_{\left\lfloor n/2m\right\rfloor})]-\mathbb{E}[\xi(\mathsf{id},\rho_{\left\lfloor n/2m\right\rfloor})\mathbbm{1}_{A^{c}}]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}[\xi(\mathsf{id},\rho_{\left\lfloor n/2m\right\rfloor})]-\frac{n}{2m}\mathbb{P}(A^{c}).

By Proposition
3.1
, there is a constant
c>0
, depending only on
p
, such that
\mathbb{P}(A^{c})\leq 2e^{-cn}
, and thus

\mathbb{E}[\xi(\mathsf{id},\rho_{\left\lfloor n/2m\right\rfloor})]-\frac{1}{m}ne^{-cn}\leq\mathbb{E}[\xi(\mathsf{id},n)]\leq\mathbb{E}[\xi(\mathsf{id},\rho_{\left\lceil 2n/m\right\rceil})]+2ne^{-cn}.

Finally, Corollary
2.6
shows that for
\alpha=\frac{\log 2}{\log p-\log(1-p)}
,

\mathbb{E}[\xi(\mathsf{id},\rho_{k})]=1+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathbb{P}(R_{\rho_{j}}=\mathsf{id})\simeq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{j^{2\alpha}}\simeq\begin{cases}1,&p<\frac{\left|F\right|^{2}}{\left|F\right|^{2}+1}\\
\log n,&p=\frac{\left|F\right|^{2}}{\left|F\right|^{2}+1}\\
n^{1-2\alpha},&p>\frac{\left|F\right|^{2}}{\left|F\right|^{2}+1}\end{cases}

for both
k\in\left\{\left\lfloor n/2m\right\rfloor,\left\lceil 2n/m\right\rceil\right\}
, and the theorem follows.
∎

4.
Homesick random walks

We turn to study the stationary random walks over an arbitrary finitely generated group
G
. Before we do that, we recall the notion of homesick random walks on graphs. These random walks were first studied on trees (see e.g.
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
), and were later considered on general Cayley graphs
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E)
be a locally finite graph with root
o\in V
, and let
\lambda\geq 1
be a real number. The
\lambda
-homesick random walk

(Z_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
on
\Gamma
is then defined as follows. We first set
Z_{0}=o
. For each
v\in V
, let
v_{1},\dots,v_{j}
denote the neighbors of
v
with
\operatorname{dist}(o,v_{i})=\operatorname{dist}(o,v)-1
for all
1\leq i\leq j
(notice that
j\geq 1
unless
v=o
), and let
v_{1}^{\prime},\dots,v_{k}^{\prime}
denote the other neighbors of
v
. We then define the transition probability from
v\in V
to
w\in V
by

T(v,w)=\begin{cases}\frac{\lambda}{\lambda j+k},&w\in\left\{v_{1},\dots,v_{j}\right\}\\
\frac{1}{\lambda j+k},&w\in\left\{v_{1}^{\prime},\dots,v_{k}^{\prime}\right\}\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

When
\lambda=1
, this is a simple random walk on
\Gamma
, and otherwise has a drift towards the root
o
. Alternatively, the
\lambda
-homesick random walk can be described by an electrical network, where the resistance of an edge
(v,w)\in E
is
\lambda^{\min\left\{\operatorname{dist}(o,v),\operatorname{dist}(o,w)\right\}}
.

We denote by

B_{r}=\left\{v\in V\,|\,\operatorname{dist}(o,v)\leq r\right\}

and

K_{r}=\left\{v\in V\,|\,\operatorname{dist}(o,v)=r\right\}

the ball and sphere of radius
r
around the root
o
, respectively.
Note that
B_{r}
is well defined also for non-integer
r
, in which case
B_{r}=B_{\left\lfloor r\right\rfloor}
.
For a nonempty subset
A\subseteq V
, we write

\partial_{E}A=\left\{(u,v)\in E\,|\,u\in A,\,v\notin A\right\}

for its edge boundary.

We define the stopping times

\tau_{o}^{+}=\inf\left\{n\geq 1\,|\,Z_{n}=o\right\}

and, for any nonempty subset
A\subseteq V
,

\tau_{A}=\inf\left\{n\geq 0\,|\,Z_{n}\in A\right\}.

Both may be infinite if the walk
(Z_{n})
is transient.
For a vertex
v\in V
, we also write
\tau_{v}=\tau_{\left\{v\right\}}
.

We now give two concentration results on the size of
\operatorname{range}(Z_{n})=\left\{Z_{0},\dots,Z_{n}\right\}
. While
Z_{n}
is supported on the whole ball of radius
n
, when the drift is large enough we expect it to remain, with high probability, in a ball of radius
C\log n
(where
C
depends on
\lambda
). This is formulated in the following proposition:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E)
be a locally finite graph with root
o
, let
\lambda\geq 1
, and let
Z_{n}
be the
\lambda
-homesick random walk on
(\Gamma,o)
. Then

\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{i<\tau_{o}^{+}}\operatorname{dist}(o,Z_{i})\geq r\right)\leq\frac{1}{\deg o}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\left|\partial_{E}B_{i}\right|^{-1}\lambda^{i}\right)^{-1}

for every
r\geq 1
.

Proof.

We first recall from the theory of electrical networks that

\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{i<\tau_{o}^{+}}\operatorname{dist}(o,Z_{i})\geq r\right)=\mathbb{P}(\tau_{K_{r}}<\tau_{o}^{+})=\frac{1}{\deg o\;\mathcal{R}_{\text{eff}}(o\leftrightarrow z_{r};\Gamma_{r})}

where
\Gamma_{r}
denotes the graph obtained from
\Gamma
by gluing all vertices of
K_{r}
in
\Gamma
to one vertex
z_{r}
. Let
\Gamma^{\prime}
denote the graph obtained from
\Gamma
by gluing each sphere
K_{d}
in
\Gamma
to a vertex
z_{d}
. Since
\Gamma^{\prime}
is obtained from
\Gamma_{r}
by gluing vertices, we have

\mathcal{R}_{\text{eff}}(o\leftrightarrow z_{r};\Gamma^{\prime})\leq\mathcal{R}_{\text{eff}}(o\leftrightarrow z_{r};\Gamma_{r}),

and so

\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{i<\tau_{o}^{+}}\operatorname{dist}(o,Z_{i})\geq r\right)\leq\frac{1}{\deg o\;\mathcal{R}_{\text{eff}}(o\leftrightarrow z_{r};\Gamma^{\prime})}.

Now
\Gamma^{\prime}
is a one-sided line, where the number of edges from
z_{i}
to
z_{i+1}
is
\left|\partial_{E}B_{i}\right|
, and the resistance of each one is
\lambda^{i}
. Since these are all connected in parallel, we have

\mathcal{R}_{\text{eff}}(z_{i}\leftrightarrow z_{i+1};\Gamma^{\prime})=\left|\partial_{E}B_{i}\right|^{-1}\lambda^{i},

and since
z_{0}=o,z_{1},\dots,z_{r}
are connected in a series we have

\mathcal{R}_{\text{eff}}(o\leftrightarrow z_{r};\Gamma^{\prime})=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\left|\partial_{E}B_{i}\right|^{-1}\lambda^{i}.

We therefore conclude that

\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{i<\tau_{o}^{+}}\operatorname{dist}(o,Z_{i})\geq r\right)\leq\frac{1}{\deg o}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\left|\partial_{E}B_{i}\right|^{-1}\lambda^{i}\right)^{-1},

completing the proof.
∎

We next show that, with high probability,
Z_{n}
covers a positive proportion of a ball of radius
c\log n
. If
Z_{n}
is transient, then its range will be linear in
n
with high probability, so we focus on the case where
Z_{n}
is recurrent. Similarly to the previous sections, we write
\rho_{0},\rho_{1},\dots
for the successive times at which
Z_{n}
hits
o
, i.e.,
\rho_{0}=0
and for every
k\geq 1
we have

\rho_{k}=\inf\left\{n>\rho_{k-1}\,|\,Z_{n}=o\right\}.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E)
be a graph with root
o
, let
\lambda>1
, and let
Z_{n}
be the
\lambda
-homesick random walk on
(\Gamma,o)
. Assume that
Z_{n}
is recurrent. Then, for every
k\geq\left(\frac{5(\lambda-1)}{\deg o}\right)^{1/c}
and
0<c<1
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\operatorname{range}(Z_{\rho_{k}})\right|\leq\frac{N}{4}\right)\leq e^{-\frac{N}{8}}

where

N=\min\left\{\left|B_{\frac{c\log k}{\log\lambda}}\right|,\left\lfloor\frac{\lambda-1}{\deg o}k^{1-c}\right\rfloor\right\}.

Proof.

We first note that for any
v\in V
, there is a path
v_{0}=o,v_{1},\dots,v_{d}=v
for
d=\operatorname{dist}(o,v)
with resistance
\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\lambda^{i}\leq\frac{\lambda^{d}}{\lambda-1}
. Therefore

\mathcal{R}_{\text{eff}}(o\leftrightarrow v)\leq\frac{1}{\lambda-1}\lambda^{\operatorname{dist}(o,v)}

for all
v\in V
, showing that

\mathbb{P}(\tau_{v}<\tau_{o}^{+})=\frac{1}{\deg o\;\mathcal{R}_{\text{eff}}(o\leftrightarrow v)}\geq\frac{\lambda-1}{\deg o}\lambda^{-\operatorname{dist}(o,v)}

for all
v\in V
.

We write
r=\frac{c\log k}{\log\lambda}
,
N=\min\left\{\left|B_{r}\right|,\left\lfloor\frac{\deg o}{\lambda-1}k^{1-c}\right\rfloor\right\}
, and
k^{\prime}=\left\lfloor\frac{k}{N}\right\rfloor
. We take
N
distinct vertices
v_{1},\dots,v_{N}\in B_{r}
, and for each
1\leq j\leq N
we denote by
A_{j}
the event that
Z_{n}
reaches
v_{j}
between times
\rho_{(j-1)k^{\prime}}
and
\rho_{jk^{\prime}}
. The events
A_{1},\dots,A_{N}
are independent, since they depend on mutually disjoint intervals of time. Also, by the above, at each of the
k^{\prime}
excursions between
\rho_{(j-1)k^{\prime}}
and
\rho_{jk^{\prime}}
there is a probability of at least
\frac{\lambda-1}{\deg o}\lambda^{-r}=\frac{\lambda-1}{\deg o}k^{-c}
that
R_{n}
reaches
v_{j}
during that excursion. Therefore

\mathbb{P}(A_{j})\geq 1-\left(1-\frac{\lambda-1}{\deg o}k^{-c}\right)^{k^{\prime}}\geq 1-\frac{5}{4}\left(1-\frac{\lambda-1}{\deg o}k^{-c}\right)^{k/N}\geq 1-\frac{5}{4}e^{-1}>\frac{1}{2}

where the second inequality follows from
k\geq\left(\frac{5(\lambda-1)}{\deg o}\right)^{1/c}
and
k^{\prime}\geq\frac{k}{N}-1
, and the third inequality follows from
N\leq\frac{\deg o}{\lambda-1}k^{1-c}
.

Finally, we note that
\left|\operatorname{range}(Z_{\rho_{k}})\right|\geq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{j}}
, so
\left|\operatorname{range}(Z_{\rho_{k}})\right|
stochastically dominates a binomial random variable with parameters
N
and
\frac{1}{2}
. By Chernoff’s inequality,

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\operatorname{range}(Z_{\rho_{k}})\right|\leq\frac{N}{4}\right)\leq e^{-\frac{N}{8}}

completing the proof.
∎

5.
Stationary random walks: the general case

We are now ready to define the stationary random walks in the general case. Let
G
be a finitely generated group with a finite symmetric generating set
S
, and let
H
be a nontrivial finitely generated group with a finitely supported, symmetric and non-degenerate probability measure
\mu
. For any
\lambda\geq 1
, we define the
\lambda
-stationary random walk
on
H\wr G
as
R_{n}=((L_{n}(g))_{g\in G},Z_{n})
, where
Z_{n}
is the
\lambda
-homesick random walk on the Cayley graph
\mathrm{Cay}(G,S)
, and
L_{n+1}
is obtained from
L_{n}
via

(5)

L_{n+1}(g)=\begin{cases}L_{n}(g)&\textrm{ if }g\not\in\{Z_{n},Z_{n+1}\},\\
L_{n}(g)U_{n+1}&\textrm{ if }g=Z_{n},\\
L_{n}(g)V_{n+1}&\textrm{ if }g=Z_{n+1},\end{cases}

where
(U_{n})_{n},(V_{n})_{n}
are
\mu
-i.i.d. random variables. We note that if
H
is finite and
\mu
is uniform on
H
, then this has the same distribution as the one defined via (
1
).

Since we are interested in studying the recurrence of
R_{n}
, we will often assume that
Z_{n}
is recurrent. In this case we write
\rho_{0}=0,\rho_{1},\dots
for the successive times at which
Z_{n}
hits
\mathsf{id}_{G}
.

Remark 5.1
.

As in the case of
F\wr\mathbb{Z}
, the random walk
R_{n}
is recurrent if and only if
Z_{n}
is recurrent and
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id})=\infty
.

We next rephrase Proposition
2.2
for the general case. To do this, we write the local time of
Z_{n}
at an element
g\in G
until time
n
as

\xi_{G}(g,n)=\left|\left\{0\leq t\leq n\,|\,Z_{t}=g\right\}\right|.

We further write
\mu^{*n}
for the
n
-fold convolution power of
\mu
, i.e.,

\mu^{*n}(h)=\sum_{h_{1}\cdots h_{n}=h}\mu(h_{1})\cdots\mu(h_{n})

for every
h\in H
and
n\geq 1
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Assume that
Z_{n}
is recurrent. Then, for any
k\geq 1
,

\mathbb{P}(R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id}\,|\,Z_{0},\dots,Z_{\rho_{k}})=\prod_{g\in\operatorname{range}(Z_{\rho_{k}})}\mu^{*(2\xi_{G}(g,\rho_{k}))}(\mathsf{id}_{H}).

Proof.

This is essentially the same proof as the proof of Proposition
2.2
. For any
g\in G
we write

\Xi(g,\rho_{k})=\left\{0\leq t<\rho_{k}\,|\,Z_{t}=g\right\}.

By the definition of
R_{n}
, if
g\notin\operatorname{range}(Z_{\rho_{k}})
then
L_{\rho_{k}}(g)=\mathsf{id}_{H}
. If
\mathsf{id}_{G}\neq g\in\operatorname{range}(Z_{\rho_{k}})
, then

L_{\rho_{k}}(g)=\prod_{t\in\Xi(g,\rho_{k})}V_{t}U_{t+1},

whereas for
g=\mathsf{id}_{G}
we have

L_{\rho_{k}}(\mathsf{id}_{G})=U_{1}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}V_{\rho_{i}}U_{\rho_{i}+1}\right)V_{\rho_{k}}.

It follows that, conditional on
Z_{0},\dots,Z_{\rho_{k}}
, the variables
(L_{\rho_{k}}(g))_{g\in\operatorname{range}(Z_{\rho_{k}})}
are independent. Furthermore, the distribution of
L_{\rho_{k}}(g)
is the product of
2\xi_{G}(g,\rho_{k})
independent
\mu
-random variables, and thus
\mathbb{P}(L_{\rho_{k}}(g)=\mathsf{id}_{H})=\mu^{*(2\xi_{G}(g,\rho_{k}))}(\mathsf{id}_{H})
. We therefore have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id}\,|\,Z_{0},\dots,Z_{\rho_{k}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(\forall g\in G:L_{\rho_{k}}(g)=\mathsf{id}_{H})

\displaystyle=\prod_{g\in\operatorname{range}(Z_{\rho_{k}})}\mu^{*(2\xi_{G}(g,\rho_{k}))}(\mathsf{id}_{H}),

completing the proof.
∎

Before studying stationary random walks further, we describe them via an appropriate electrical network.

Observation 5.3
.

The
\lambda
-stationary random walk
R_{n}
on
H\wr G
can also be described using an electrical network, where the underlying graph
\Gamma
of the network is the Cayley graph of
H\wr G
with respect to the switch-walk-switch generators defining
R_{n}
. In details, two elements
a=((L(g))_{g},x)\in H\wr G
and
a^{\prime}=((L^{\prime}(g))_{g},x^{\prime})\in H\wr G
are connected by an edge in
\Gamma
if and only if:

•

x^{\prime}=xs
for some
s\in S
;

•

L(g)=L^{\prime}(g)
for all
g\in G\setminus\left\{x,x^{\prime}\right\}
;

•

and
\mu(L(g)^{-1}L^{\prime}(g))>0
for both
g\in\left\{x,x^{\prime}\right\}
.

The resistance of the edge
e=(a,a^{\prime})
is then achieved by multiplying the resistances of the
\mu
-random walk twice (one time for each switch) and the resistance from
Z_{n}
, i.e.,

r_{e}=\lambda^{\min\{|x|,|x^{\prime}|\}}\mu(L(x)^{-1}L^{\prime}(x))^{-1}\mu(L(x^{\prime})^{-1}L^{\prime}(x^{\prime}))^{-1}.

We apply this viewpoint to prove two claims on the recurrence of the stationary random walks. First, we show that the recurrence of
R_{n}
does not depend on the choice of the measure
\mu
, and in fact only depends on
H
via its order
\left|H\right|
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
G
be an infinite finitely generated group with a finite symmetric generating set
S
, and let
\lambda\geq 1
be a real number. Let
H,H^{\prime}
be two finite groups of the same order, and let
\mu,\mu^{\prime}
be symmetric and non-degenerate probability measures on
H,H^{\prime}
respectively. Write
R_{n},R_{n}^{\prime}
for the
\lambda
-stationary random walks on
H\wr G,H^{\prime}\wr G
with respect to
\mu,\mu^{\prime}
. Then
R_{n}
is recurrent if and only if
R_{n}^{\prime}
is recurrent.

Proof.

By symmetry, it suffices to show that recurrence of
R_{n}
implies recurrence of
R_{n}^{\prime}
. Denote by
\Gamma=(H\wr G,E,(r_{e})_{e})
and
\Gamma^{\prime}=(H^{\prime}\wr G,E^{\prime},(r^{\prime}_{e})_{e})
the electrical networks corresponding to
(R_{n})_{n}
and
(R_{n}^{\prime})_{n}
. For a vertex
a=((L(g))_{g},x)\in H\wr G
, the total conductance of
\Gamma
at
a
satisfies

\alpha^{2}\lambda^{-|x|-1}\leq c_{a}=\sum_{(a,b)\in E}r_{(a,b)}^{-1}\leq\lambda^{-|x|+1},

where
\alpha=\min_{h\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)}\mu(h)
. Similarly, for
a^{\prime}=((L^{\prime}(g))_{g},x)\in H^{\prime}\wr G
we have

\beta^{2}\lambda^{-|x|-1}\leq c^{\prime}_{a^{\prime}}=\sum_{(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in E^{\prime}}(r^{\prime}_{(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})})^{-1}\leq\lambda^{-|x|+1},

where
\beta=\min_{h\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu^{\prime})}\mu^{\prime}(h)
.

Fix a bijection
f\colon H\to H^{\prime}
, which extends to a bijection
\tilde{f}\colon H\wr G\to H^{\prime}\wr G
by applying
f
on each lamp. From the above bounds it follows that

\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\leq\frac{c_{a}}{c^{\prime}_{\tilde{f}(a)}}\leq\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\beta^{2}}

for every
a\in H\wr G
.

Choose an odd integer
k\geq 1
with
\operatorname{supp}(\mu^{*k})=H
(this is possible since
\mu
is symmetric and non-degenerate), and let
\Gamma^{(k)}=(H\wr G,E^{(k)},(r^{(k)}_{e})_{e})
be the network corresponding to
(R_{kn})_{n}
. We claim that there exists a positive number
C>0
, depending only on
|S|,\lambda,\mu,\mu^{\prime}
, such that

(6)

r^{(k)}_{(a_{1},a_{2})}\leq C\,r^{\prime}_{(\tilde{f}(a_{1}),\tilde{f}(a_{2}))}

for all
a_{1},a_{2}\in H\wr G
.

To see this, write
a_{i}=((L_{i}(g))_{g},x_{i})
for
i=1,2
. If
(\tilde{f}(a_{1}),\tilde{f}(a_{2}))\notin E^{\prime}
, then (
6
) is trivial, so we assume
(\tilde{f}(a_{1}),\tilde{f}(a_{2}))\in E^{\prime}
. We note that

\mathbb{P}(R^{\prime}_{1}=\tilde{f}(a_{2})\,|\,R^{\prime}_{0}=\tilde{f}(a_{1}))\leq\mathbb{P}(Z_{1}=x_{2}\,|\,Z_{0}=x_{1})\leq\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+|S|-1},

while

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(R_{k}=a_{2}\,|\,R_{0}=a_{1})

\displaystyle=\mu^{*k}(L_{1}(g_{1})^{-1}L_{2}(g_{1}))\,\mu^{*k}(L_{1}(g_{2})^{-1}L_{2}(g_{2}))\,\mathbb{P}(Z_{k}=x_{2}\,|\,Z_{0}=x_{1})

\displaystyle\geq\gamma^{2}\,\mathbb{P}(Z_{1}=x_{2}\,|\,Z_{0}=x_{1})^{(k+1)/2}\,\mathbb{P}(Z_{1}=x_{2}\,|\,Z_{0}=x_{1})^{(k-1)/2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\gamma^{2}}{(\lambda+|S|-1)^{k}}

for
\gamma=\min_{h\in H}\mu^{*k}(h)>0
. Therefore

\displaystyle r^{(k)}_{(a_{1},a_{2})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{a_{1}}\mathbb{P}(R_{k}=a_{2}\,|\,R_{0}=a_{1})}\leq\frac{(\lambda+|S|-1)^{k}}{\gamma^{2}c_{a_{1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lambda^{2}(\lambda+|S|-1)^{k}}{\alpha^{2}\gamma^{2}c^{\prime}_{\tilde{f}(a_{1})}}\leq\frac{\lambda^{3}(\lambda+|S|-1)^{k-1}}{\alpha^{2}\gamma^{2}c^{\prime}_{\tilde{f}(a_{1})}\,\mathbb{P}(R^{\prime}_{1}=\tilde{f}(a_{2})\,|\,R^{\prime}_{0}=\tilde{f}(a_{1}))}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{3}(\lambda+|S|-1)^{k-1}}{\alpha^{2}\gamma^{2}}\,r^{\prime}_{(\tilde{f}(a_{1}),\tilde{f}(a_{2}))},

establishing (
6
).

By Rayleigh’s monotonicity law,

\mathcal{R}_{\text{eff}}(\mathsf{id}\leftrightarrow\infty;\Gamma^{(k)})\leq C\,\mathcal{R}_{\text{eff}}(\tilde{f}(\mathsf{id})\leftrightarrow\infty;\Gamma^{\prime}).

Since
R_{n}
is recurrent, so is
R_{kn}
, and therefore

\mathcal{R}_{\text{eff}}(\mathsf{id}\leftrightarrow\infty;\Gamma^{(k)})=\infty,

which forces

\mathcal{R}_{\text{eff}}(\tilde{f}(\mathsf{id})\leftrightarrow\infty;\Gamma^{\prime})=\infty.

Thus
R_{n}^{\prime}
is recurrent.
∎

Next, we show that recurrent is a monotone property in
\lambda
:

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group, let
H
be a nontrivial finite group, and let
\lambda>1
be a real number. If the
\lambda
-stationary random walk on
H\wr G
is recurrent, then so is the
\lambda^{\prime}
-stationary random walk for every
\lambda^{\prime}>\lambda
.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
denote the electrical networks representing the
\lambda
- and
\lambda^{\prime}
-stationary random walks on
H\wr G
respectively. These networks have the same underlying graph, and the resistances on the edges satisfy
r_{e}\leq r^{\prime}_{e}
for all
e\in E
. The assumption that the
\lambda
-stationary random walk on
H\wr G
is recurrent implies

\mathcal{R}_{\text{eff}}(\mathsf{id}\leftrightarrow\infty;\Gamma)=\infty.

By Rayleigh’s monotonicity law, it follows that

\mathcal{R}_{\text{eff}}(\mathsf{id}\leftrightarrow\infty;\Gamma)\leq\mathcal{R}_{\text{eff}}(\mathsf{id}\leftrightarrow\infty;\Gamma^{\prime}),

so

\mathcal{R}_{\text{eff}}(\mathsf{id}\leftrightarrow\infty;\Gamma^{\prime})=\infty

as well, which in turn implies that the
\lambda^{\prime}
-stationary random walk on
H\wr G
is recurrent.
∎

6.
Proofs of Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.5

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.5
. Throughout the section, we fix an infinite finitely generated group
G
with a finite symmetric generating set
S
. We also fix a nontrivial and finitely generated group
H
, and a finitely supported, symmetric and non-degenerate probability measure
\mu
on
H
. For a given
\lambda\geq 1
, we write
R_{n}
for the
\lambda
-stationary random walk on
H\wr G
. Throughout this section, we write
B_{r}
for the ball of radius
r
around
\mathsf{id}_{G}
in the Cayley graph of
G
.

We begin with Theorem
1.3
, stating that when
G
is virtually-
\mathbb{Z}
and
H
is finite, there is a phase transition in the recurrence of the stationary random walks.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Assume that
G
is virtually-
\mathbb{Z}
and that
H
is finite. By Proposition
5.4
, we may assume that
\mu
is the uniform measure on
H
. We will prove that there exist
1<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\infty
such that
R_{n}
is transient if
\lambda<\lambda_{1}
, and is recurrent if
\lambda\geq\lambda_{2}
. We note that by the result of Lyons
[
5
]
,
Z_{n}
is recurrent since
G
has linear growth.

We first prove the existence of
\lambda_{1}
. Since
G
is infinite,
\left|B_{r}\right|\geq r
for every
r\geq 1
. Therefore, by Proposition
4.2
with
c=\frac{1}{2}
, for sufficiently large
k
we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\operatorname{range}(Z_{\rho_{k}})\right|\leq\frac{1}{4}N_{k}\right)\leq e^{-\frac{N_{k}}{8}}

for

N_{k}=\min\left\{\left|B_{\frac{\log k}{2\log\lambda}}\right|,\left\lfloor\frac{\lambda-1}{\left|S\right|}\sqrt{k}\right\rfloor\right\}\geq\min\left\{\frac{\log k}{2\log\lambda},\left\lfloor\frac{\lambda-1}{\left|S\right|}\sqrt{k}\right\rfloor\right\}.

When
k
is sufficiently large we have
N_{k}\geq\frac{\log k}{2\log\lambda}
, so Proposition
5.2
implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id}\,|\,\left|\operatorname{range}(Z_{\rho_{k}})\right|\geq\frac{N_{k}}{4}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\operatorname{range}(Z_{\rho_{k}})\right|\leq\frac{N_{k}}{4}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left|H\right|^{-N_{k}/4}+e^{-N_{k}/8}\leq\left|H\right|^{-\log k/8\log\lambda}+e^{-\log k/16\log\lambda}

\displaystyle=k^{-\log\left|H\right|/8\log\lambda}+k^{-1/16\log\lambda}\leq 2k^{-\log\left|H\right|/8\log\lambda}

for sufficiently large
k
. Therefore, if
\lambda<\lambda_{1}\coloneqq\left|H\right|^{1/8}
, then
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id})<\infty
, and thus
R_{n}
is transient for all
\lambda<\lambda_{1}
.

We turn to prove the existence of
\lambda_{2}
. We note that since
G
is virtually-
\mathbb{Z}
, it follows that
G
and
\mathbb{Z}
are quasi-isometric. In particular, there exists
M>0
such that
G
has at most
M
edges of any given distance from
\mathsf{id}_{G}
, i.e.,
\left|\partial_{E}B_{r}\right|\leq M
for all
r\geq 1
. Furthermore, there exists a constant
C>0
such that
\left|B_{r}\right|\leq Cr
for all
r\geq 1
.

We will show that
\operatorname{range}(Z_{\rho_{k}})
is contained in a small ball with high probability. To do this, we first apply Proposition
4.1
and deduce that

\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{i\leq\rho_{1}}\left|Z_{i}\right|\geq r\right)\leq\frac{1}{\left|S\right|}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}M^{-1}\lambda^{i}\right)^{-1}\leq\frac{M}{\left|S\right|\lambda^{r-1}}.

Fix
k\geq 2
. Since the different excursions are independent, it follows that

\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{i\leq\rho_{k}}\left|Z_{i}\right|<r\right)\geq\left(1-\frac{M}{\left|S\right|\lambda^{r-1}}\right)^{k}

for every
r\geq 1
. In particular, taking
r=r_{k}\coloneqq\frac{\log(kM/\left|S\right|)}{\log\lambda}+1
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{i\leq\rho_{k}}\left|Z_{i}\right|<r_{k}\right)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{k}\right)^{k}\geq\frac{1}{4}.

We note that if
\max_{i\leq\rho_{k}}\left|Z_{i}\right|<r_{k}
, then
\operatorname{range}(Z_{\rho_{k}})\subseteq B_{r_{k}-1}
, and thus
\left|\operatorname{range}(Z_{\rho_{k}})\right|\leq\left|B_{r_{k}-1}\right|\leq C(r_{k}-1)
. Therefore

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\operatorname{range}(Z_{\rho_{k}})\right|\leq C(r_{k}-1)\right)\geq\frac{1}{4},

so we apply Proposition
5.2
again and deduce

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id})

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left(R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id}\,|\,\left|\operatorname{range}(Z_{\rho_{k}})\right|\leq C(r_{k}-1)\right)\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\operatorname{range}(Z_{\rho_{k}})\right|\leq C(r_{k}-1)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\left|H\right|^{-C(r_{k}-1)}=\frac{1}{4}\left|H\right|^{-\frac{C\log k}{\log\lambda}+C^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{4}\left|H\right|^{C^{\prime}}k^{-\frac{C\log\left|H\right|}{\log\lambda}}.

It follows that if
\lambda\geq\lambda_{2}\coloneqq\left|H\right|^{C}
then
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id})=\infty
, and thus
R_{n}
is recurrent for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{2}
.

We have proven the existence of
1<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\infty
such that the
\lambda
-stationary random walk
R_{n}
is transient if
\lambda<\lambda_{1}
, and is recurrent if
\lambda\geq\lambda_{2}
, and thus the theorem follows by Proposition
5.5
.
∎

We now turn to prove Theorem
1.5
, i.e., that there is no phase transition if
G
is not virtually-
\mathbb{Z}
or if
H
is infinite. We note that for
\lambda=1
, the
\lambda
-stationary random walk on
H\wr G
is just a simple random walk on this group, which is known to be transient. We therefore focus on the case
\lambda>1
, and prove the theorem under each assumption separately.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
,
G
non-virtually-
\mathbb{Z}
case.

Suppose that
G
is not virtually-
\mathbb{Z}
and
H
is finite. Again by Proposition
5.4
, we may assume that
\mu
is the uniform measure on
H
. By Gromov’s theorem and the Bass–Guivarc’h formula, it follows that the growth of
G
is at least quadratic, so in particular
\left|B_{r}\right|\geq r^{3/2}
for sufficiently large
r
.

Fix
\lambda>1
, and let
R_{n}=((L_{n}(g))_{g},Z_{n})
denote the
\lambda
-stationary random walk on
H\wr G
. If
Z_{n}
is transient, then
R_{n}
is transient as well, so we assume that
Z_{n}
is recurrent.

We apply Proposition
4.2
with
c=\frac{1}{2}
to deduce that for sufficiently large
k
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\operatorname{range}(Z_{\rho_{k}})\right|\leq\frac{N_{k}}{4}\right)\leq e^{-\frac{N_{k}}{8}}

for

N_{k}=\min\left\{\left|B_{\frac{\log k}{2\log\lambda}}\right|,\left\lfloor\frac{\lambda-1}{\left|S\right|}\sqrt{k}\right\rfloor\right\},

so for sufficiently large
k
we have

N_{k}\geq\left(\frac{\log k}{2\log\lambda}\right)^{3/2}\eqqcolon C\log^{3/2}k.

Applying Proposition
5.2
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id}\,|\,\left|\operatorname{range}(Z_{\rho_{k}})\right|\geq\frac{N_{k}}{4}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\operatorname{range}(Z_{\rho_{k}})\right|\leq\frac{N_{k}}{4}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left|H\right|^{-N_{k}/4}+e^{-N_{k}/8}\leq\left|H\right|^{-C\log^{3/2}k/4}+e^{-C\log^{3/2}k/8},

and thus
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id})<\infty
. By Remark
5.1
, it follows that
R_{n}
is transient, and this holds for every
\lambda>1
as required.
∎

We finally prove:

Proof of Theorem
1.5
,
H
infinite case.

We assume now that
H
is infinite, and thus there is a constant
C>0
such that

(7)

\mu^{*2t}(\mathsf{id}_{H})\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}}

for all
t\geq 1
.

For each
g\in S
, we write

N_{g,k}=\left|\left\{0\leq i\leq k-1\,|\,Z_{\rho_{i}+1}=g\right\}\right|.

Since the different excursions are independent, each
N_{g,k}
is a binomial random variable with parameters
k
and
\frac{1}{\left|S\right|}
. Writing
A=\left\{\forall g\in S:N_{g,k}\geq\frac{k}{2\left|S\right|}\right\}
, we may apply Chernoff’s inequality and deduce

\mathbb{P}\left(A^{c}\right)\leq\left|S\right|e^{-\frac{k}{12\left|S\right|}}.

Write
\overline{S}=S\cup\left\{\mathsf{id}_{G}\right\}
. Proposition
5.2
shows that

\mathbb{P}(R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id}\,|\,Z_{0},\dots,Z_{\rho_{k}})\leq\prod_{g\in\overline{S}}\mu^{*(2\xi_{G}(g,\rho_{k}))}(\mathsf{id}_{H}).

We note that
\xi_{G}(s,\rho_{k})\geq N_{s,k}
for every
s\in S
, and that
\xi_{G}(\mathsf{id}_{G},\rho_{k})=k
; therefore, combining with (
7
),

\mathbb{P}(R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id}\,|\,A)\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{2k}}\left(\frac{C}{\sqrt{k/\left|S\right|}}\right)^{\left|\overline{S}\right|-1}=\frac{C^{\prime}}{k^{\left|\overline{S}\right|/2}}.

We can now deduce that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id}\,|\,A)+\mathbb{P}(A^{c})\leq\frac{C^{\prime}}{k^{\left|\overline{S}\right|/2}}+\left|S\right|e^{-\frac{k}{12\left|S\right|}}.

Since
G
is infinite, it follows that
\left|\overline{S}\right|\geq 3
, hence
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id})<\infty
. This shows that
R_{n}
is transient for every
\lambda>1
, completing the proof.
∎

7.
Stationary random walks on lamplighter graphs

The notion of stationary random walks extends naturally to lamplighter graphs
\Gamma^{\prime}\wr\Gamma
. Specifically, assume that
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are bounded-degree graphs, where
\Gamma
is infinite and
\Gamma^{\prime}
is nontrivial. We assume that
\Gamma
is rooted, so the notion of homesick random walks on
\Gamma
is defined with respect to the root
o
of
\Gamma
. Then the ideas of the proofs of Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.5
show:

(1)

If the balls around
o
in
\Gamma
have linear growth and
\Gamma^{\prime}
is finite, then there is a phase transition as in Theorem
1.3
.

(2)

If the balls around
o
in
\Gamma
grow superlinearly, or if
\Gamma^{\prime}
is infinite, then the
\lambda
-stationary random walk is transient for every
\lambda>1
.

In contrast to lamplighter groups, bounded-degree graphs can exhibit much wilder growth behaviours. It is therefore natural to expect that if the growth of balls in
\Gamma
oscillates between linear and superlinear rates, the stationary random walk may display a phase transition, or even remain transient for every value of
\lambda
, depending on the oscillations.

Another contrast between stationary random walks on lamplighter graphs and on lamplighter groups arises in the critical case. While we conjecture that the walk on lamplighter groups is recurrent when the base group is virtually-
\mathbb{Z}
(Conjecture
1.4
), we show that this fails for lamplighter graphs when the base group is not transitive.

Example 7.1
.

Let
\Gamma_{m}=(V_{m},E_{m})
denote the graph obtained from
\mathbb{Z}
by splitting every vertex of distance
2^{i}
(for every
i\geq 1
) from
0
to
m
vertices. Formally, we write

V_{m}=\left(\mathbb{Z}\setminus\left\{\pm 2^{i}\,|\,i\geq 1\right\}\right)\cup\left\{(2^{i},j)\,|\,i\geq 1,\;1\leq j\leq m\right\}.

The edges in
\Gamma_{m}
are given as follows: each
x\in\mathbb{Z}
is adjacent to
x-1,x+1
(unless one of them is split), and each split vertex
(2^{i},j)
is adjacent to
2^{i}-1,2^{i}+1
. Then
\Gamma_{m}
is an infinite bounded-degree graph (with maximal degree
m+1
). We further note that

\left|B_{r}(0)\right|=2r+2(m-1)\left\lfloor\log_{2}r\right\rfloor

for every
r\geq 1
. We denote by
\pi\colon\Gamma_{m}\to\mathbb{Z}
the natural projection of
\Gamma_{m}
to
\mathbb{Z}
, given by
\pi(x)=x
for every non-split
x\in V_{m}
and
\pi(2^{i},j)=2^{i}
.

We consider the lamplighter graph
\Gamma=(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\wr\Gamma_{m}
. Since the growth of
\Gamma_{m}
around
0
is linear, there exists
1<\lambda_{c}<\infty
such that the
\lambda
-stationary random walk is transient for
\lambda<\lambda_{c}
, and recurrent for
\lambda>\lambda_{c}
. To describe it explicitly, let
R_{n}=((L_{n}(v))_{v},Z_{n})
denote the
\lambda
-stationary random walk on
\Gamma
. Then
Z_{n}
is recurrent, and we denote by
(\rho_{k})_{k}
the successive return times to
0
.

Proposition 7.2
.

Assume that
m\geq 3
. Then the
\lambda
-stationary random walk
R_{n}
on
\Gamma
is recurrent for
\lambda>4
, and transient for
\lambda\leq 4
.

Proof.

Calculating effective resistances as in the proof of Proposition
4.1
for
\Gamma_{m}
, we get an equality

\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{0\leq t\leq\rho_{1}}\left|\pi(Z_{t})\right|\geq r\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{s=0}^{r-1}\left|\partial_{E}B_{s}(0)\right|^{-1}\lambda^{s}\right)^{-1}.

We note that

\left|\partial_{E}B_{s}(0)\right|=\begin{cases}2,&s\neq 2^{j}\text{ for }j\geq 1\\
2m,&s=2^{j}\text{ for }j\geq 1,\end{cases}

implying that

\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda^{r}-1}\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{0\leq t\leq\rho_{1}}\left|\pi(Z_{t})\right|\geq r\right)\leq\frac{m(\lambda-1)}{\lambda^{r}-1}.

We now prove the claim. Suppose first that
\lambda>4
. For each
k\geq\lambda+1
, since the different excursions are independent, we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{0\leq t\leq\rho_{k}}\left|\pi(Z_{t})\right|<r\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{0\leq t\leq\rho_{1}}\left|\pi(Z_{t})\right|<r\right)^{k}\geq\left(1-\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda^{r}-1}\right)^{k}.

for every
r\geq 1
. Taking
r_{k}\coloneqq\left\lceil\frac{\log k}{\log\lambda}\right\rceil
, we deduce

\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{0\leq i\leq\rho_{k}}\left|\pi(Z_{i})\right|<r_{k}\right)\geq\left(1-\frac{\lambda-1}{k-1}\right)^{k}\geq\frac{1}{\lambda^{\lambda+1}}

where the last inequality follows from
k\geq\lambda+1
and monotonicity. We note that if
\max_{0\leq t\leq\rho_{k}}\left|\pi(Z_{t})\right|<r_{k}
then
\operatorname{range}(Z_{\rho_{k}})\subseteq B_{r_{k}}(0)
, and thus

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\operatorname{range}(Z_{\rho_{k}})\right|\leq\left|B_{r_{k}}(0)\right|\right)\geq\frac{1}{\lambda^{\lambda+1}}.

It follows (using Proposition
5.2
for graphs) that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\operatorname{range}(Z_{\rho_{k}})\right|\leq\left|B_{r_{k}}(0)\right|\right)\mathbb{P}\left(R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id}\,|\,\left|\operatorname{range}(Z_{\rho_{k}})\right|\leq\left|B_{r_{k}}(0)\right|\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\lambda^{\lambda+1}}\cdot 2^{-\left|B_{r_{k}}(0)\right|}\geq\frac{1}{2\lambda^{\lambda+1}}\cdot\frac{1}{k^{2\log 2/\log\lambda}(\log k)^{2(m-1)}}.

Therefore
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id}\right)=\infty
for
\lambda>4
, showing that
R_{n}
is recurrent in this case.

We now suppose that
\lambda\leq 4
. Fix
k\geq 3
. We set

r_{k}^{\prime}\coloneqq\left\lfloor\frac{\log k-\log\log k+\log((\lambda-1)/4)}{\log\lambda}\right\rfloor,

and define the events

A^{+}_{k}\coloneqq\left\{\max_{0\leq t\leq\rho_{k}}\pi(Z_{t})\geq r_{k}^{\prime}\right\}

and

A^{-}_{k}\coloneqq\left\{\min_{0\leq t\leq\rho_{k}}\pi(Z_{t})\leq-r_{k}^{\prime}\right\}.

At each excursion we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{0\leq t\leq\rho_{1}}\pi(Z_{t})\geq r_{k}^{\prime}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{0\leq t\leq\rho_{1}}\left|\pi(Z_{t})\right|\geq r_{k}^{\prime}\right)\geq\frac{\lambda-1}{2\lambda^{r_{k}^{\prime}}}\geq\frac{2\log k}{k}.

Therefore, since the different excursions are independent,

\mathbb{P}((A^{+}_{k})^{c})=\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{0\leq t\leq\rho_{1}}\pi(Z_{t})<r_{k}^{\prime}\right)^{k}\leq\left(1-\frac{2\log k}{k}\right)^{k}\leq\frac{1}{k^{2}}.

By symmetry,
\mathbb{P}((A^{-}_{k})^{c})\leq\frac{1}{k^{2}}
as well.

Next, we write
r_{k}^{\prime\prime}=\left\lfloor\frac{\log k}{2\log\lambda}\right\rfloor
. For each
1\leq i\leq\log_{2}r_{k}^{\prime\prime}
and
1\leq j\leq m
, we write
B_{i,j,k}^{+}
for the event that
Z_{n}
visits
(2^{i},j)
until time
\rho_{k}
, and
B_{i,j,k}^{-}
for the same event for
(-2^{i},j)
. At a given excursion we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\exists t\leq\rho_{1}:Z_{t}=(2^{i},j)\right)\geq\frac{1}{m}\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{0\leq t\leq\rho_{1}}\pi(Z_{t})\geq r_{k}^{\prime\prime}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2m}\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda^{r_{k}^{\prime\prime}}-1}\geq\frac{\lambda-1}{2mk^{1/2}}.

Therefore

\mathbb{P}((B_{i,j,k}^{+})^{c})\leq\left(1-\frac{\lambda-1}{2mk^{1/2}}\right)^{k}\leq e^{-\frac{\lambda-1}{2m}k^{1/2}},

and by symmetry

\mathbb{P}((B_{i,j,k}^{-})^{c})\leq\left(1-\frac{\lambda-1}{2mk^{1/2}}\right)^{k}\leq e^{-\frac{\lambda-1}{2m}k^{1/2}}.

We note that under the event
\tilde{A}\coloneqq A_{k}^{+}\cap A_{k}^{-}\cap\bigcap_{1\leq i\leq\log_{2}r_{k}^{\prime\prime},1\leq j\leq m}(B_{i,j,k}^{+}\cap B_{i,j,k}^{-})
we have

\left|\operatorname{range}(Z_{\rho_{k}})\right|\geq 2r_{k}^{\prime}+2(m-1)\log_{2}r_{k}^{\prime\prime}\geq\frac{2\log k}{\log\lambda}+(2m-3)\log_{2}\log k.

We apply Proposition
5.2
for graphs again, and deduce

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(R_{\rho_{k}}=\mathsf{id}\,|\,\tilde{A}\right)+\mathbb{P}((A_{k}^{+})^{c})+\mathbb{P}((A_{k}^{-})^{c})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq\log_{2}r_{k}^{\prime\prime}\\
1\leq j\leq m\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}(B_{i,j,k}^{c})

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-(\frac{2\log k}{\log\lambda}+(2m-3)\log_{2}\log k)}+\frac{2}{k^{2}}+m\log_{2}r_{k}^{\prime\prime}e^{-\frac{\lambda-1}{2m}k^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{k^{-\frac{\log 4}{\log\lambda}}(\log k)^{2m-3}}+\frac{2}{k^{2}}+m\log_{2}\log ke^{-\frac{\lambda-1}{2m}k^{1/2}},

which is summable for
m\geq 3
given
\lambda\leq 4
. Hence
R_{n}
is transient for
\lambda\leq 4
, concluding the proof.

∎

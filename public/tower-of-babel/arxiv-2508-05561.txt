Flavour interferometry in Reissner-Nordström background
Jean Alexandre, Emilio Meryn

Abstract
We derive the phase acquired by a neutral scalar particle propagating along Reissner-Nordström geodesics. Considering two flavours propagating on different trajectories which intersect, we plot the interference pattern induced by gravitational lensing from the charged compact object. Although the effect of the charge is subdominant in the metric, it proves to be significant in the phase, and shifts the interference pattern, compared to the Schwarzschild case. This pattern is characterised by two oscillation lengths which, if known, would allow the determination of both eigen masses independently.

I
Introduction

The rich topics of neutrino oscillation and gravitational lensing are mostly studied independently, but considered together lead to the field of neutrino optics
Lambiase et al. 2005
.
The effect of gravity on the phase of a quantum mechanical particle
Stodolsky 1979
started with the original measurement on neutron phases in the Earth gravitational field
Colella et al. 1975
,
and naturally led to the first studies of gravitationally-induced neutrino oscillations in the astrophysical context
Fornengo et al. 1997
;
Ahluwalia and Burgard 1996
.
Works on particle phases in curved spacetimes were then developed and extended to different spins or astrophysical events
Alsing et al. 2001
;
Visinelli 2015
;
Ramezan and Khorrami 2010
;
Sorge and Zilio 2007
;
Yang and Kneller 2017
;
Koutsoumbas and Metaxas 2020
;
Swami et al. 2020
;
Swami et al. 2021
;
Dvornikov 2020
, but also in a context beyond General Relativity
Chakrabarty et al. 2022
;
Chakrabarty et al. 2023
;
Frost 2023
;
Alloqulov et al. 2025
;
Lambiase and Mastrototaro 2021
;
Hammad et al. 2022
;
Wang and Rong 2025
.

The interference pattern induced by flavour oscillations from lensing by a compact object was
studied in
Alexandre and Clough 2018
, based on the original phase calculation in
Fornengo et al. 1997
.
Assuming the compact object at the origin of coordinates,
an interesting feature is the appearance of two oscillation lengths:
(i)
one in the radial direction, depending on the difference of masses squared;

(ii)
the other in the orthoradial direction, depending on the sum of masses squared.
Therefore the observation of such an interference pattern could in principle lead to the values of the eigen masses themselves.

In the present article we extend the study
Alexandre and Clough 2018
to a Reissner-Nordström metric.
The radial propagation in this metric was already calculated in
Huang and Wang 2004
,
and we consider here the more general case of non-radial motion.
For a static, spherical and neutral compact object, the effects of spacetime curvature are at most of order
r_{S}/b
,
where
r_{S}
is the horizon radius and
b
is the impact factor.
The effect of the electric charge, on the other hand, is felt at the order
(r_{S}/b)^{2}
at most, similarly to the effect of the spin in Kerr metric.
As a consequence we naively do not expect a significant difference with the Schwarzschild case in the weak field approximation
b>>r_{S}
.
Nevertheless we find a significant difference in the interference pattern, between the Schwartzschild and the Reissner-Nordström case, because the effects of order
(r_{S}/b)^{2}
accumulate as the particle propagates along a geodesic, and the resulting phase shift is not small.

Sec.
II
introduces the general setting in which we derive the phase of a particle propagating in the Reissner-Nordström background.
In Sec.
III
we evaluate this phase in the ultra-relativistic regime,
and we propose a prescription to avoid artificial divergences and imaginary contributions,
which appear when going to the weak-field approximation. We then plot the resulting interference patterns in Sec.
IV
,
which is significantly shifted, compared to the Schwarzschild case.

II
General setting

We consider a neutral scalar particle scattered by a charged compact object,
and moving along a geodesic with impact parameter
b
measured asymptotically far away, see Fig.
1
.

Figure 1:
The source is located at the point
A
, at the distance
r_{A}
from the compact object,
and the detector is located at the point
B
, at the distance
r_{B}
from the compact object.

b
is the impact parameter and the closest approach distance is denoted by
r_{0}
.

The field corresponding to the particle is assumed to be a plane wave configuration.
The compact object is at the centre of coordinates
(r,\theta,\phi)
, and we focus on an equatorial motion (constant angle
\theta=\pi/2
), for which
the Reissner-Nordström metric is

ds^{2}=A(r)dt^{2}-\frac{dr^{2}}{A(r)}-r^{2}d\phi^{2}~,

(1)

where

A(r)=1-\frac{r_{S}}{r}+\frac{r_{Q}^{2}}{r^{2}}~.

(2)

The study is therefore reduced to a two-dimensional problem, or three-dimensional with cylindrical symmetry.
We consider
2r_{Q}\leq r_{S}
, in which case the two horizons defined by
A(r)=0
are located at

r_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}\left(r_{S}\pm\sqrt{r_{S}^{2}-4r_{Q}^{2}}\right)~.

(3)

The canonical momentum for a particle of mass
m
is

p_{\mu}=mg_{\mu\nu}\frac{dx^{\nu}}{ds}~,

(4)

and it satisfies the mass-shell relation.

m^{2}=g^{\mu\nu}p_{\mu}p_{\nu}=\frac{p_{t}^{2}}{A(r)}-A(r)p_{r}^{2}-\frac{p_{\phi}^{2}}{r^{2}}~.

(5)

The metric does not depend on
t
or
\phi
, such that
E=p_{t}
and
J=p_{\phi}
are conserved quantities along the trajectory of a particle,
and they correspond to the energy and angular momentum measured by an observer in asymptotically flat spacetime.

The phase acquired by a particle moving from
A
to
B
along a geodesic is

\Phi=\int_{A}^{B}p_{\mu}dx^{\mu}=\int_{A}^{B}\left(Edt+p_{r}dr+Jd\phi\right)~,

(6)

and we will approximate the calculation of phase (
6
) for a null geodesic, which corresponds to the ultra-relativistic approximation,
relevant to the propagation of neutrinos. The energy and angular momentum which are constant along a null geodesic are denoted by
E_{0}
and
J_{0}
respectively.
In the ultra-relativistic regime, we have then

E=E_{0}+{\cal O}(m^{2}/E_{0})~~~~\mbox{and}~~~~J_{0}=E_{0}b~.

(7)

It is known that the massless limit of geodesics followed by massive particles gives twice the phase obtained from null geodesics
Lipkin 1995
.
This mismatch is related to the same-momentum approximation in the calculation of the phase, when assuming plane waves for the mass eigenstates
Zhang and Beesham 2003
.
This problem can be solved by assuming wave packets instead, but in the present article we keep the plane wave approximation, while keeping this mismatch in mind.

III
Phase along a geodesic

We follow here the steps given in
Fornengo et al. 1997
to obtain the generic form of the particle phase along a geodesic,
but we consider an alternative evaluation of the integrals, as explained in Sec.
III.3
.

III.1
Radial motion

We consider here a radial motion, for which
d\phi=0
, such that the phase along a null geodesic (denoted by the index 0) is

\Phi=\int_{r_{A}}^{r_{B}}\left(\left.E\frac{dt}{dr}\right|_{0}+p_{r}\right)dr~,

(8)

and the mass-shell relation (
5
) implies

p_{r}=\pm\frac{\sqrt{E^{2}-A(r)m^{2}}}{A(r)}\simeq\pm\left(\frac{E}{A(r)}-\frac{m^{2}}{2E_{0}}\right)~,

(9)

where
\pm
stands for inward or outward propagation. From the metric (
1
), we also have for a radial motion,

\left.\frac{dt}{dr}\right|_{0}=\mp\frac{1}{A(r)}~,

(10)

where the sign is opposite the one for
p_{r}
, such that the phase is

\Phi=\frac{m^{2}}{2E_{0}}|r_{B}-r_{A}|~.

(11)

Note that the latter expression is identical to the one in flat spacetime, but here
|r_{B}-r_{A}|
is not the
proper distance
L
over which particles propagate. The latter is instead

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{r_{A}}^{r_{B}}\frac{dr}{\sqrt{A(r)}}

(12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[r\sqrt{A(r)}+\frac{r_{S}}{4}\ln\left(\frac{r\sqrt{A(r)}+r-r_{S}/2}{r\sqrt{A(r)}-r+r_{S}/2}\right)\right]_{r_{A}}^{r_{B}}~,

and is larger than
|r_{B}-r_{A}|
.

III.2
Non-radial motion

We first consider here a situation where the particle either approaches the compact object or recedes from it,
therefore when the radial coordinate
r
evolves monotonically in time.
The phase (
6
) can be written

\Phi_{A\to B}=\int_{r_{A}}^{r_{B}}\left(E\left.\frac{dt}{dr}\right|_{0}+p_{r}+J\left.\frac{d\phi}{dr}\right|_{0}\right)dr~,

(13)

where

\displaystyle\frac{dt}{dr}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{dt}{ds}\frac{ds}{dr}=-\frac{E}{A^{2}(r)p_{r}}

(14)

\displaystyle\frac{d\phi}{dr}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d\phi}{ds}\frac{ds}{dr}=\frac{J}{r^{2}A(r)p_{r}}~,

(15)

and
p_{r}
is determined by the mass-shell relation (
5
):

\displaystyle A(r)p_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pm E\sqrt{1-\frac{J^{2}A(r)}{r^{2}E^{2}}-\frac{m^{2}}{E^{2}}A(r)}

(16)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\pm E_{0}\sqrt{1-A(r)\frac{b^{2}}{r^{2}}}\mp\frac{m^{2}A(r)}{2E_{0}\sqrt{1-A(r)b^{2}/r^{2}}}~,

where the null geodesic approximation is used. The expression for the phase simplifies as

\displaystyle\Phi_{A\to B}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\pm\int_{r_{A}}^{r_{B}}\left(\frac{E_{0}^{2}}{A^{2}(r)p_{r}}-p_{r}-\frac{b^{2}E_{0}^{2}}{r^{2}A(r)p_{r}}\right)dr

(17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pm\frac{m^{2}}{2E_{0}}\int_{r_{A}}^{r_{B}}\frac{dr}{\sqrt{1-A(r)b^{2}/r^{2}}}~,

where the signs - and + correspond to the particle approaching and receding, respectively.

A comment is in order here: The expansion (
16
) is valid only if the argument of the square root does not vanish.
But it actually does vanish for one specific value of
r
, when the particle reaches the closest approach point
C
along a full geodesic.
Indeed, in this case, the radial coordinate is stationary at
r=r_{0}
corresponding to
p_{r}(r_{0})=0
.
For null geodesics this happens for

1=A(r_{0})\frac{b^{2}}{r_{0}^{2}}~,

(18)

where the integrand appearing in the phase (
17
) diverges, and the expansion (
16
) is not valid.
The corresponding singularity is integrable though, since in the vicinity of
r_{0}
the integrand is
\sim|r-r_{0}|^{-1/2}
.
As a consequence, the expression (
17
) can be used in the integral defining the phase and, taking into account the
\pm
signs for the approaching/receding branches, the phase acquired along a full (null) geodesic is

\displaystyle\Phi_{A\to C\to B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi_{A\to C}+\Phi_{C\to B}

(19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m^{2}}{2E_{0}}\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{A}}\frac{dr}{\sqrt{1-A(r)b^{2}/r^{2}}}

\displaystyle~~~~+\frac{m^{2}}{2E_{0}}\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{B}}\frac{dr}{\sqrt{1-A(r)b^{2}/r^{2}}}~.

We stress that this expression only assumes the ultra-relativistic regime, but at this stage does not involve any other approximation.

III.3
Weak-field approximation

The integrals appearing in the phase (
19
) cannot be evaluated exactly, and from now we assume a large-enough impact parameter,
satisfying
b\gg r_{S}>r_{Q}
. From its definition (
18
), one can then express
r_{0}
in terms of
b

r_{0}=b\left(1-\frac{r_{S}}{2b}-\frac{3r_{S}^{2}}{8b^{2}}+\frac{r_{Q}^{2}}{2b^{2}}+\cdots\right)~,

(20)

where dots represent higher orders in
r_{S}/b
and
r_{Q}/b
. The integrand for the calculation of the phase (
19
) can naively be expanded as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-A(r)b^{2}/r^{2}}}

(21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-b^{2}/r^{2}}}-\frac{b^{2}r_{S}}{2(r^{2}-b^{2})^{3/2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{b^{2}r_{Q}^{2}}{2r(r^{2}-b^{2})^{3/2}}+\frac{3b^{4}r_{S}^{2}}{8r(r^{2}-b^{2})^{5/2}}+\cdots

This expansion artificially introduces divergent integrals at
r=b
though, as well as imaginary parts, since
r_{0}<b
.
To deal with these artifacts, we introduce the following prescription.
We analytically continue the expression (
21
) to negative values of
r_{S}
, which leads to

r_{0}\to r_{1}=b\left(1+\frac{|r_{S}|}{2b}-\frac{3r_{S}^{2}}{8b^{2}}+\frac{r_{Q}^{2}}{2b^{2}}+\cdots\right)~>b~.

(22)

We then evaluate the (convergent) integrals, and go back to positive values of
r_{S}
. The resulting imaginary parts
cancel out between the approaching and receding regimes, for the following reason.
In order to define the square root, one must introduce a branch cut, which can be chosen as the negative imaginary axis.
When the particle approaches the closest point
C
,
z=r^{2}-b^{2}
goes from positive to negative, corresponding to a multiplication by
e^{i\pi}
, such that the square root is multiplied by
e^{i\pi/2}=+i
.
When the particle recedes from the closest point,
z
goes from negative to positive, which corresponds to a multiplication by
e^{-i\pi}
if one wants to avoid the branch cut, such that the square root is multiplied by
e^{-i\pi/2}=-i
.
This approach reproduces the results given in
Fornengo et al. 1997
for the first order in
r_{S}/b
, and can easily be applied to higher orders, as shown below.

III.3.1
Zero-th order in
r_{S}

The integral does not diverge at this order, and we have

\displaystyle\int_{r_{0}}^{r}\frac{du}{\sqrt{1-b^{2}/u^{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{r^{2}-b^{2}}-\sqrt{r_{0}^{2}-b^{2}}

(23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{r^{2}-b^{2}}\pm i\sqrt{|r_{0}^{2}-b^{2}|}~,

such that, taking into account the cancellation of the imaginary parts,

\displaystyle\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{A}}\frac{dr}{\sqrt{1-b^{2}/r^{2}}}+\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{B}}\frac{dr}{\sqrt{1-b^{2}/r^{2}}}

(24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{r_{A}^{2}-b^{2}}+\sqrt{r_{B}^{2}-b^{2}}.

This result is intuitive: the zero-th order in
r_{S}
corresponds to the absence of gravity, where a geodesic is a straight line and
r_{0}=b
.
The distance covered by the particle between
A
and
B
is then simply given by Pythagoras’ theorem.

III.3.2
First order in
r_{S}

The above prescription leads to

\displaystyle-\frac{b^{2}r_{S}}{2}\int_{r_{0}}^{r}\frac{du}{(u^{2}-b^{2})^{3/2}}

(25)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle+\frac{b^{2}|r_{S}|}{2}\int_{r_{1}}^{r}\frac{du}{(u^{2}-b^{2})^{3/2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{|r_{S}|}{2}\left(\frac{r}{\sqrt{r^{2}-b^{2}}}-\frac{r_{1}}{\sqrt{r_{1}^{2}-b^{2}}}\right)~,

and changing back the sign of
r_{S}
gives

\frac{r_{S}}{2}\left(\frac{r}{\sqrt{r^{2}-b^{2}}}\pm\frac{ir_{0}}{\sqrt{|r_{0}^{2}-b^{2}|}}\right)~.

(26)

With the cancellation of the imaginary parts we obtain

\displaystyle-\frac{b^{2}r_{S}}{2}\left(\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{A}}\frac{dr}{(r^{2}-b^{2})^{3/2}}+\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{B}}\frac{dr}{(r^{2}-b^{2})^{3/2}}\right)

(27)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{r_{S}}{2}\left(\frac{r_{A}}{\sqrt{r_{A}^{2}-b^{2}}}+\frac{r_{B}}{\sqrt{r_{B}^{2}-b^{2}}}\right)~,

where
\equiv
means equal in the present prescription.
Note that this result is identical to the one derived in
Fornengo et al. 1997
, since it can also be written

\frac{r_{S}}{2}\left(\frac{b}{\sqrt{r_{A}^{2}-b^{2}}}+\frac{b}{\sqrt{r_{B}^{2}-b^{2}}}+\sqrt{\frac{r_{A}-b}{r_{A}+b}}+\sqrt{\frac{r_{B}-b}{r_{B}+b}}\right)~.

III.3.3
Second order in
r_{Q}

The procedure explained above leads to

\displaystyle\frac{b^{2}r_{Q}^{2}}{2}\int_{r_{0}}^{r}\frac{du}{u(u^{2}-b^{2})^{3/2}}

(28)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle-\frac{r_{Q}^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{r^{2}-b^{2}}}+\frac{1}{b}\arctan\left(\sqrt{r^{2}/b^{2}-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~\pm~~\mbox{imaginary part}~,

such that

\displaystyle\frac{b^{2}r_{Q}^{2}}{2}\left(\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{A}}\frac{du}{u(u^{2}-b^{2})^{3/2}}+\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{B}}\frac{du}{u(u^{2}-b^{2})^{3/2}}\right)

(29)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\frac{r_{Q}^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{r_{A}^{2}-b^{2}}}+\frac{1}{b}\arctan\left(\sqrt{r_{A}^{2}/b^{2}-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~+~(r_{A}\to r_{B})~.

III.3.4
Second order in
r_{S}

We have here

\displaystyle\frac{3b^{4}r_{S}^{2}}{8}\int_{r_{0}}^{r}\frac{du}{u(u^{2}-b^{2})^{5/2}}

(30)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\frac{3r_{S}^{2}}{8}\left(\frac{3r^{2}-4b^{2}}{3(r^{2}-b^{2})^{3/2}}+\frac{1}{b}\arctan\left(\sqrt{r^{2}/b^{2}-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~\pm~~\mbox{imaginary part}~,

such that

\displaystyle\frac{3b^{4}r_{S}^{2}}{8}\left(\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{A}}\frac{du}{u(u^{2}-b^{2})^{5/2}}+\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{B}}\frac{du}{u(u^{2}-b^{2})^{5/2}}\right)

(31)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{3r_{S}^{2}}{8}\left(\frac{3r_{A}^{2}-4b^{2}}{3(r_{A}^{2}-b^{2})^{3/2}}+\frac{1}{b}\arctan\left(\sqrt{r_{A}^{2}/b^{2}-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~+~(r_{A}\to r_{B})~.

Finally, in the ultra-relativistic regime and weak-field approximation, the expression for the phase is

\Phi_{A\to C\to B}=\Psi(r_{A})+\Psi(r_{B})~,

(32)

where

\displaystyle\Psi(r)=\frac{m^{2}}{2E_{0}}\Biggl\{\sqrt{r^{2}-b^{2}}+\frac{r_{S}}{2}\frac{r}{\sqrt{r^{2}-b^{2}}}

(33)

\displaystyle-\frac{r_{Q}^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{r^{2}-b^{2}}}+\frac{1}{b}\arctan\left(\sqrt{r^{2}/b^{2}-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{3r_{S}^{2}}{8}\left(\frac{3r^{2}-4b^{2}}{3(r^{2}-b^{2})^{3/2}}+\frac{1}{b}\arctan\left(\sqrt{r^{2}/b^{2}-1}\right)\right)\Biggr\}~

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~+{\cal O}(r_{S}/b)^{3}

Note that the above expansion in
r_{S}/b
is valid as long as its coefficients are of the same order at most. Comparing the zeroth and first order coefficients,
r_{A}
and
r_{B}
should then satisfy

\frac{r_{A,B}(b/2)}{\sqrt{r_{A,B}^{2}-b^{2}}}\lesssim\sqrt{r_{A,B}^{2}-b^{2}}~~~\to~~~r_{A,B}\gtrsim\frac{5}{4}b~,

(34)

in which case the second order coefficient is also of the same order.
In the numerical analysis which follows, we will therefore assume that both
r_{A}
and
r_{B}
are larger than
5b/4
.

IV
Interference pattern

Having calculated the phase of one particle traveling along a geodesic, we can now study the interference between two coupled flavours on two different geodesics which intersect
(see Fig.
2
).

Figure 2:
Four phases interfere at the intersection point after scattering: two flavours on each trajectory.

IV.1
Oscillation probability

The two different trajectories are part of an incoming plane wave, which is scattered by the compact object.
We therefore consider a source located far away from the compact object (
r_{A}\to\infty
)
in a way which does not affect the phase (
32
), by taking
Alexandre and Clough 2018

\frac{m^{2}}{2E_{0}}\sqrt{r_{A}^{2}-b^{2}}\equiv 2q\pi\to\infty~,

(35)

where
q
is an integer going to infinity. In the expression (
32
), the contribution from
r_{A}
becomes then

\Psi(r_{A})\to 2q\pi+\frac{m^{2}}{4E_{0}}\left(r_{S}-\frac{\pi r_{Q}^{2}}{2b}+\frac{3\pi r_{S}^{2}}{8b}\right)~,

(36)

which corresponds to a non-trivial phase shift, independent of
r_{A}
. In the situation where
r_{B}
also goes to infinity, this phase shift doubles,
since the contributions of
r_{A}
and
r_{B}
are additive.
The phase shift is then independent of the distance between the source and the detector, potentially providing a measurable effect.
As pointed out in
Fornengo et al. 1997
, the net phase shift induced when multiple gravitational sources are passed is then the sum of the phase shifts induced by the individual gravitational sources.

At the intersection point there are then four different phases to consider,
denoted by
\Phi_{i}^{J}
, for two flavours
i
and two trajectories
J
. Discarding the multiple of
2\pi
, we have

\Phi_{i}^{J}=\frac{m_{i}^{2}}{4E_{0}}\left(r_{S}-\frac{\pi r_{Q}^{2}}{2b_{J}}+\frac{3\pi r_{S}^{2}}{8b_{J}}\right)+\Psi_{i}^{J}(r_{B})~,

(37)

where
\Psi_{i}^{J}(r)
is given by eq.(
33
), with
m\to m_{i}
and
b\to b_{J}
.
The flavour-changing oscillation probability was calculated in
Fornengo et al. 1997
and reads

\displaystyle P_{f\to f^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2}(2\Omega)}{8}

(38)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\Big(2+\cos(\Phi_{1}^{1}-\Phi_{1}^{2})+\cos(\Phi_{2}^{1}-\Phi_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle~~~~-\cos(\Phi_{1}^{1}-\Phi_{2}^{1})-\cos(\Phi_{1}^{2}-\Phi_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle~~~~-\cos(\Phi_{1}^{1}-\Phi_{2}^{2})-\cos(\Phi_{1}^{2}-\Phi_{2}^{1})\Big)~,

where
\Omega
is the mixing angle and
f,f^{\prime}
are the two flavours.
The phases (
37
) are proportional to the eigen mass squared, such that the measurement of the probability (
38
)
for different values of
r_{B}
would lead to the values of
m_{1}^{2},m_{2}^{2}
independently, and not only the difference
m_{1}^{2}-m_{2}^{2}
.

IV.2
Expansion for large distances

We pointed out that our results are valid for regions in which
r_{A,B}\gtrsim 5b/4
,
and in principle we don’t need to expand the phase (
33
) in powers of
b/r
.
This expansion is still interesting though, in order to understand better the structure of the probability.
For
r_{A}\rightarrow\infty
and
r\equiv r_{B}
, we have then at the order
b^{2}/r^{2}

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{f\to f^{\prime}}=\sin^{2}(2\Omega)

(39)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\Bigg[\sin^{2}\Bigg(\frac{\Delta m^{2}}{8E_{0}}\Bigg[2r\left(1+\frac{r_{S}}{r}-\frac{r_{Q}^{2}}{2r^{2}}\right)-g(r)\Bigg]\Bigg)

\displaystyle~~~~\times\cos\left(\frac{m_{1}^{2}}{4E_{0}}f(r)\right)\cos\left(\frac{m_{2}^{2}}{4E_{0}}f(r)\right)

\displaystyle~~~~+\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\Delta m^{2}}{8E_{0}}f(r)\right)\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\Sigma m^{2}}{8E_{0}}f(r)\right)\Bigg]~,

where

\displaystyle\Delta b^{2}=b_{1}^{2}-b_{2}^{2}~~,~~\Sigma b^{2}=b_{1}^{2}+b_{2}^{2}

(40)

\displaystyle\Delta m^{2}=m_{1}^{2}-m_{2}^{2}~~,~~\Sigma m^{2}=m_{1}^{2}+m_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle g(r)=\frac{\Sigma b^{2}}{2r}\left(1-\frac{r_{S}}{r}\right)-l_{+}

\displaystyle f(r)=\frac{\Delta b^{2}}{2r}\left(1-\frac{r_{S}}{r}\right)-l_{-}

\displaystyle l_{\pm}=\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\frac{1}{b_{1}}\pm\frac{1}{b_{2}}\right)\left(\frac{3}{4}r_{S}^{2}-r_{Q}^{2}\right)~.

For a detection far from the compact object, the above probability reads

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}\mathcal{P}_{f\to f^{\prime}}=\sin^{2}(2\Omega)

(41)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\Bigg[\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\Delta m^{2}}{8E_{0}}[2r_{S}+l_{+}]\right)\cos\left(\frac{m_{1}^{2}l_{-}}{4E_{0}}\right)\cos\left(\frac{m_{2}^{2}l_{-}}{4E_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~+\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\Delta m^{2}l_{-}}{8E_{0}}\right)\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\Sigma m^{2}l_{-}}{8E_{0}}\right)\Bigg]~,

where
r
is chosen such that
\Delta m^{2}r/(4E_{0})=2q\pi\to\infty
.
As one can see, the effect of the second order terms
r_{S}^{2}
and
r_{Q}^{2}
, accumulated along the geodesic, leads to non-trivial contributions via the lengths
l_{\pm}
.

IV.3
Flavour interferometry

In the weak-field regime
\epsilon\equiv r_{S}/b\ll 1

V
Conclusion

The interference pattern for flavour oscillations, induced by a spherically symmetric gravitational background,
leads to two different oscillation lengths: one relevant to the radial direction, which depends on the difference of masses squared,
and the other relevant to the orthoradial direction, which depends on the sum of the masses squared.
The measurement of this interference pattern is difficult to achieve,
but it is worth keeping in mind that such a setup could lead to the individual values of the masses.
We found that the effect of a charge is not negligible since, compared to the Schwarzschild case,
it significantly shifts the interference pattern.
This feature could lead to a better understanding of astrophysical events,
particularly those emitting neutrinos subjected to gravitational lensing from a compact object.

From a technical point of view, we proposed a systematic way to treat artificial divergences, obtained as a result of a naive expansion of the phase in powers of the horizon radius over the impact parameter. This prescription is based on an analytical continuation, it reproduces known results at the first order in this expansion, and it is easy to apply to higher orders.

We note that the results derived here are also valid for fermions, since spin has no effect on the oscillation mechanism.
Also, in a spherically symmetric background, the phenomenon of spin flip does not occur.
The latter would play a role in the axi-symmetric Kerr background for example, but such a study is beyond the scope of the present work.

ACURATE neo: Reassuring Durability Despite Early Clinical Challenges
Authors: Eric Durand, Christophe Tron, Nicolas Bettinger, Najime Bouhzam, Thibaut Hemery, Helene Eltchaninoff
Published: 2026

Transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) has profoundly transformed the management of severe aortic stenosis over the past decade. Initially developed for patients considered inoperable or at prohibitive surgical risk, the procedure has progressively expanded toward intermediate- and low-risk populations. As this expansion continues, the durability of transcatheter heart valves (THVs) has become one of the most critical questions in contemporary structural heart interventions and younger population. Although early and midterm outcomes are well established, robust long-term durability data remain limited and heterogeneous across valve platforms.

In this issue of Structural Heart, Kim et al.1 report long-term clinical and echocardiographic outcomes following implantation of the self-expanding ACURATE neo THV in a multicenter cohort of 961 patients treated between 2012 and 2018. The population reflects early-generation TAVI practice, consisting of elderly patients with a significant burden of comorbidities (median age 82 years, 61% women). Follow-up was substantial, with a median duration of 5.0 years and a maximum follow-up extending to 11.3 years. Among survivors, the median follow-up reached 6.2 years. For the durability analysis, 758 patients who survived beyond 30 days and had echocardiographic follow-up were included.

Long-term survival in this cohort reflects the advanced age and clinical risk profile of the treated population. Kaplan–Meier estimates of all-cause mortality were 13.6, 43.1, and 67.3% at 1, 5, and 8 years, respectively. Despite this high competing mortality risk, valve durability outcomes appeared reassuring. At 8 years, the cumulative incidence of moderate structural valve deterioration (SVD) was 3.5%, while severe SVD occurred in only 2.1% of patients and bioprosthetic valve failure in 3.2%.1

The study also provides important insights into long-term valve hemodynamics. In the subset of patients with paired echocardiographic follow-up beyond 5 years (n = 243; median follow-up 6.4 years), transvalvular gradients remained low and stable over time. The mean gradients decreased from 45 mmHg at baseline to 9 mmHg early after TAVI and remained low at 6 mmHg at the last follow-up examination. Similarly, the effective orifice area remained preserved over time and measured 1.8 cm2 after implantation and 1.7 cm2 at long-term follow-up.

These findings contribute to the growing body of evidence supporting the durability of transcatheter valves. Observational studies have first reported data regarding structural valve degeneration using standardized definitions. In a multicenter analysis evaluating long-term durability according to the European definition of SVD, we reported a low incidence of transcatheter valve degeneration during extended follow-up, further supporting the favorable durability profile of contemporary THV platforms.2 Randomized trials conducted in lower-risk populations have provided encouraging data regarding long-term valve performance. In the PARTNER 3 trial evaluating balloon-expandable TAVI in low-risk patients, 7-year follow-up demonstrated sustained clinical outcomes and stable valve hemodynamics, providing reassuring signals regarding valve durability in younger and lower-risk populations.3 Similarly, in the Evolut Low Risk trial evaluating a self-expanding supra-annular platform, 6-year follow-up demonstrated comparable outcomes between TAVI and surgery with preserved valve hemodynamics in the transcatheter arm. However, valve reintervention was observed more frequently in patients treated with TAVI, highlighting that longer-term surveillance remains essential as TAVI expands into younger populations.4 However, the interpretation of these reassuring durability findings must be placed in the broader context of the clinical development of the ACURATE platform. Although the valve incorporates several attractive design characteristics—including supra-annular leaflet positioning associated with favorable hemodynamics and potentially easier coronary access—randomized comparisons with contemporary devices yielded mixed results. In the SCOPE I randomized trial comparing ACURATE neo with the balloon-expandable SAPIEN 3 valve, the device failed to demonstrate noninferiority with respect to the composite primary endpoint, largely driven by higher rates of moderate or greater paravalvular regurgitation and acute kidney injury.5 Similarly, the SCOPE II randomized trial comparing ACURATE neo with the self-expanding CoreValve/Evolut platform also failed to demonstrate noninferiority of the ACURATE valve.6 These findings have represented an important limitation to the widespread adoption of the ACURATE neo valve in routine clinical practice.

Against this background, the long-term data reported by Kim et al. are particularly informative. Despite earlier concerns regarding procedural outcomes, the present analysis suggests that structural valve degeneration and clinical valve failure remain relatively uncommon over extended follow-up in an elderly real-world population. The study also provides valuable insights into mechanisms of valve dysfunction. Notably, valvular aortic regurgitation emerged as the predominant mode of structural valve failure, highlighting the importance of long-term surveillance of leaflet function and regurgitation patterns after TAVI.

Nevertheless, several limitations should be acknowledged. As in many durability analyses in TAVI, the observational design and absence of systematic serial echocardiographic follow-up introduce potential biases. The fact that long-term echocardiographic assessment beyond 5 years was only available in a subset of surviving patients raises the possibility of survivor bias and limits the ability to detect progressive hemodynamic deterioration. Furthermore, the advanced age of the study population restricts the extrapolation of these findings to younger patients, in whom valve durability over longer time horizons will be of paramount importance.

Another contextual element is the recent withdrawal of the ACURATE platform from the market following regulatory challenges. Nevertheless, the present findings remain clinically relevant given the large number of patients worldwide who have already undergone implantation of this device. Understanding the long-term durability and mechanisms of valve failure therefore remains essential for guiding the follow-up and management of these patients.

In conclusion, the study by Kim et al. provides valuable long-term evidence supporting the durability of the ACURATE neo valve in an elderly, real-world population. Although earlier randomized trials highlighted limitations of this platform relative to other contemporary devices, the present data suggest that SVD and bioprosthetic valve failure remain uncommon over extended follow-up. Continued long-term surveillance across valve platforms will remain essential as TAVI continues to expand toward younger and lower-risk populations.

Press-Schechter Formalism and The PBH Mass Distributions
Owais Farooq, Romana Zahoor, Balungi Francis

Abstract




Abstract

In this paper, we derive the primordial black hole (PBH) mass function using the Press-Schechter formalism, particularly using the excursion set approach, which is a powerful statistical method for obtaining the mass functions and modeling the formation of PBHs in the early universe by invoking the concepts like random walks. The excursion set formalism provides a framework for calculating the mass function by tracking the evolution of density fluctuations in the primordial universe and their subsequent collapse into PBHs upon crossing critical barriers. By applying this theory, we compute the PBH mass function and explore its dependence on key parameters such as the peak mass, standard deviation, and initial PBH fraction. We also incorporate the effects of observational constraints, including microlensing and Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) measurements, to place limits on the PBH population across different mass ranges. Our results offer important insights into the role of PBHs as potential dark matter candidates and their contribution to the overall matter content in the universe. The derived mass function provides a basis for further exploration of PBH formation, growth, and their possible detection through gravitational waves and other cosmological signatures.

Introduction

Primordial black holes (PBHs) are hypothetical black holes formed in the early universe due to the collapse of high-density fluctuations in a primordial scalar field. Primordial black holes (PBHs) have taken a seat at the forefront of contemporary research in cosmology. Depending on the exact model of inflation leading to necessary large perturbations at scales much smaller than scales probed at the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) surveys, PBHs of masses
\leq 10^{3}M_{\odot}
are formed nearly between the end of inflation and nucleosynthesis. PBHs are nonrelativistic and effectively collisionless, properties which make them viable candidates for dark matter therefore with some recent observations it has been established that besides astrophysical blackholes there does exist certain probability for the existence of primordial Black holes .

To understand the abundance and distribution of PBHs across different mass scales, we use statistical approach such as the Press-Schechter formalism, originally developed to describe the mass function of galaxy clusters. This formalism provides a framework to calculate the number density of collapsed objects (including PBHs) based on the statistical properties of primordial density fluctuations. It relies on the assumption that density fluctuations follow a Gaussian distribution and predicts the number density of PBHs based on the probability of density perturbations exceeding a critical threshold (
\delta_{c}
). The PBH mass function, which describes the distribution of their masses, plays a crucial role in understanding their formation, evolution, and their astrophysical significance. Using the Press-Schechter formalism, the mass function can be derived based on the primordial power spectrum of density perturbations, the threshold for collapse, and the horizon mass at the time of formation.

PBH mass functions can take various forms, including monochromatic, log-normal, power-law, or critical collapse models, depending on the physical conditions during their formation. Observational constraints, such as microlensing, gravitational waves, and Hawking radiation, place limits on the PBH abundance across different mass ranges. PBHs are of significant interest as potential candidates for dark matter, seeds for supermassive black holes, and sources of gravitational waves, offering a unique probe into the physics of the early universe and small-scale features of the primordial power spectrum.

Since we will be particularly using the formalism provided by the Excursion set theory that provides a statistical framework to estimate the primordial black hole (PBH) mass function by analyzing the evolution of density fluctuations in the early universe. In this approach, the density contrast of primordial perturbations, smoothed on different mass scales, undergoes a "random walk" as a function of scale until it crosses a critical threshold,
\delta_{c}
, required for gravitational collapse. The probability of these threshold crossings determines the abundance and mass distribution of PBHs. The mass of a PBH is typically associated with the horizon mass at the time of collapse, which scales with the square of the perturbation scale. By applying the Press-Schechter or extended Press-Schechter formalism within the excursion set framework, one can derive the PBH mass function, which characterizes how PBHs of different masses contribute to the total energy density of the universe. This method provides a powerful tool to connect PBH formation to the underlying power spectrum of primordial density fluctuations and can be used to explore the role of PBHs as dark matter candidates or early-universe probes.

Generalized Press-Schechter Formalism and Mass functions

The generalized Press-Schechter (PS) formalism extends the original Press-Schechter approach by refining the treatment of density fluctuations and their evolution, offering a more accurate prediction of mass functions for collapsed objects such as primordial black holes (PBHs). This formalism incorporates concepts like the excursion set theory and barrier models to provide a more sophisticated understanding of gravitational collapse in the early universe.The Press-Schecter is a Statistical approach to calculate the number density of collapsed structures ( galaxies , clusters , halos) at a given mass
M
and redshift
z

2.1
Excursion Set Theory and Random walks

Where as the standard Press-Schechter formalism provides a useful way to compute the mass functions for various configurations , it is more simplistic model and has got some limitations like ’Cloud in cloud’ problem. Therefore to address the issue it was revised by a more rigorous stochastic framework that we call ’Excursion set Theory’.Excursion set theory provides a statistical method to track how the smoothed density contrast
\delta
evolves with the variance
S(M)
(related to mass M). Instead of a single threshold crossing, this method considers the first-passage distribution of density perturbations.In this formalism density contrast
\delta
performs a stochastic random walk with respect to the variance of perturbations
S(M)
.

The critical threshold
\delta_{c}
acts like a barrier where random walks must cross to collapse into structures i,e ;

\delta_{R}\geq\frac{\delta_{c}}{D(z)}\approx\delta_{c}(z)

Where,

D(z)
is the growth factor describing the evolution of density fluctuations with redshift(z).

\delta_{c}
is the critical threshold.

Since
\delta
is set to perform a markovian random walk as a function of smoothing scale variance
S(M)
. The probability of first crossing the barrier determines the fraction of collapsing mass.the density contrast at a given scale ’R’ is defined as ;

\delta_{R}(M)=\frac{\rho(M)-\bar{\rho}}{\bar{\rho}}

Where;

\rho(M)
is the density within a region of mass M.

\bar{\rho}
is the background density of universe.

Therefore the comoving density contrast smoothing over some scale ’R’ in fourier space is given as ;

\delta_{R}\approx\int d^{3}k\delta(k)W_{R}

Where;

W_{R}
is a sharp-k window function as;

W_{R}(k)=\theta(\frac{1}{R}-k)

is a heavyside step function

Smoothing the density contrast
\delta
over some different scale leads to the variance of density fluctuations corresponding to mass M and is given as ;

S(M)=\int\frac{dk}{k}\Delta^{2}(k)W_{R}(k)^{2}

Where,

\Delta^{2}
is the primordial power spectrum.

W_{R}(k)
is the smoothing function.

Now using the first crossing probability for the stochastic process for some
\delta_{c}
the Press-Schechter mass function is given as;

n(M,z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{\bar{\rho}}{M}\frac{\delta_{c}}{S^{3/2}}\left|\frac{dS}{dM}\right|e^{\frac{-\delta_{c}^{2}}{2S}}

Where;

\bar{\rho}
is the mean matter density of universe.

\delta_{c}
is the critical overdensity.

\frac{dS}{dM}
quantifies how the variance
S(M)
changes as the smoothing scale or mass scale
M
changes.

e^{-\frac{\delta_{c}^{2}}{2S}}
represents the probability of having sufficiently large fluctuations to exceed
\delta_{c}
and collapse.

This gives the number density of halos per unit comoving volume per unit mass range and is known as Press-Schechter mass function. It predicts the abundance and mass distribution of dark matter halos, connects initial density perturbations to large scale structures thereby explains the hierarchical structure formation and hence provides a framework to test and constrain cosmological models.

Primordial Black Hole Mass Functions

Excursion set theory provides a powerful statistical framework to study PBH formation by treating density fluctuations as a random walk in mass variance space. It refines the standard Press-Schechter formalism by addressing key limitations and enabling a more accurate calculation of PBH mass functions. This approach is crucial for understanding the formation and role of PBHs in cosmology and their potential contribution to dark matter. The formation of PBHs can be analyzed by considering the evolution of density contrast. They form when the smoothed density contrast exceeds some critical threshold for some scale
R
as ;

\delta_{R}\geq\frac{\delta_{c}}{D(z)}\approx\delta_{c}(z)

3.1
First-Crossing Probability

Since the density contrast
\delta_{R}
performs a stochastic random walk the probability of first crossing the threshold determines the fraction of mass collapsing into PBHs. Therefore the comoving density contrast smoothed over some scale R in fourier space is ;

\delta_{R}=\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{\iota kR}\delta(k)W_{R}(k)

Where;

\delta(k)
fourier components of density contrast smoothed over some scale R.

W_{R}
is the window function.

Therefore the correlation across different filter scales is given as ;

\left<\delta_{R_{1}}\delta_{R_{2}}\right>=\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{d^{3}k^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{\iota kx}e^{-\iota k^{\prime}x}\left<\delta(k)\delta^{*}(k)\right>\left<W_{R_{1}}W_{R_{2}}^{*}\right>

=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dk}{k}\Delta^{2}(k)W_{R_{1}}W_{R_{2}}^{*}

For a special case
R_{1}=R_{2}=R
;

\left<\delta_{R}^{2}\right>=\sigma^{2}(R)=S(R)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dk}{k}\Delta^{2}(k)\left|W_{R}\right|^{2}

The mass associated with smoothing scale is ;

M=4/3\pi\rho R^{3}

Therefore the variance of density contrast may be molded in the form as ;

S(M)=\sigma^{2}(M)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dk}{k}\Delta^{2}(k)W_{R}^{2}(k)

Since the gaussianity of linear density field is ;

P(\delta,S)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi S}}e^{-\frac{\delta^{2}}{2S}}

The formation of PBHs corresponds to the first-crossing problem,where we determine the probability that
\delta
exceeds
\delta_{c}
.Therefore the probability of first crossing is given as ;

\psi(\delta>\delta_{c},S)=\int_{\delta_{c}}^{\infty}P(\delta,S)dS

=\int_{\delta_{c}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi S}}e^{-\frac{\delta^{2}}{2s}}dS

Now using
erfc=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{x}^{\infty}e^{-t^{2}}dt
and
x=\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{2S}}
we arrive at ;

\psi(\delta>\delta_{c},S)=\frac{1}{2}erfc\left(\frac{\delta_{c}}{\sqrt{2S}}\right)

Now for
\delta
small
erfc\approx e^{-x^{2}}
therefore the probability that the fraction of universe collapses to PBHs is ;

\psi(\delta>\delta_{c},S)\approx e^{-\frac{\delta_{c}^{2}}{2S}}

Mass Fraction of PBHs and The Power Spectrum

The Differential mass fraction of PBHs is given by the differential form of the first-crossing distribution:

\beta(M)=\psi(\delta>\delta_{c})\left|\frac{dS}{dM}\right|

\beta(M)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{\delta_{c}}{\sqrt{S}}e^{-\frac{\delta^{2}}{2S}}\left|\frac{dS}{dM}\right|

where
\beta(M)
represents the fraction of mass contained in PBHs of mass M. The derivative
dS/dM
accounts for how the variance changes with mass.

Since the Primordial power spectrum obeys the power law with
n
corresponding to spectral index is as ;

\Delta^{2}(k)\propto k^{n}

The primordial power spectrum relevant for black holes formed in the early universe also follows a power law but of different form that allows for it to become larger (bluer) at small scales as described by Linde (1994);

\Delta^{2}(k)=\begin{cases}A\left(\frac{k}{k_{0}}\right)^{n_{s}}&\text{for }k<k_{\text{p}},\\
A\epsilon\left(\frac{k}{k_{0}}\right)^{n_{b}}&\text{for }k\geq k_{\text{p}}.\end{cases}

The normalization is set at
k_{0}=0.05Mpc^{-}1,A=2.101\times 10^{-9}Mpc^{3}andn_{s}=0.9649
as determined by planck data.The pivot scale
k_{p}
marks the point where spectral index changes to blue, i,e
n_{b}>1
. A value of
k_{p}=10Mpc^{-}1
is choosen beyond the observable range of linear power in CMB and large structure measurements.The parameters are defined as the power spectrum is continous ;

A\left(\frac{k_{p}}{k_{0}}\right)^{n_{s}}=A\epsilon\left(\frac{k_{p}}{k_{0}}\right)^{n_{b}},\epsilon=\left(\frac{k_{p}}{k_{0}}\right)^{n_{s}-n_{b}}

Using the Mass dependence of variance
S(M)
the power law can be modified as ;

\Delta^{2}(k)\propto M^{-n/3}

Since the variance
S(M)
integrates over
k
with a weighing factor, therefore the dominant contribution comes from the scale
k\propto M^{-1/3}
. The power law may be given as ;

S(M)\propto M^{-\frac{1+n}{3}}

Therefore Differentiating it ;

\frac{dS}{dM}\propto M^{-\frac{4+n}{3}}

This connects the primordial power spectrum to the formation of PBHs through the statistics of density perturbations in early universe cosmology.

4.1
PBH Mass Functions

The primordial black hole (PBH) mass function describes the distribution of PBHs by mass that formed in the early universe due to the collapse of density fluctuations. Understanding the PBH mass function is crucial for determining their potential contributions to dark matter, their impact on cosmological evolution, and their observational signatures through gravitational lensing, radiation emissions, and gravitational waves.The PBH mass function is a crucial theoretical tool for understanding the formation and evolution of PBHs in the early universe. Different formalisms, such as Press-Schechter and excursion set theory, provide frameworks to predict the distribution of PBH masses based on primordial density fluctuations. The initial PBH mass function when accretion just starts, given the mass fraction the number density of PBHs per unit mass is ;

\frac{dn}{dM}\propto\frac{\rho_{DM}}{M}\beta(M)

\frac{dn}{dM}\propto\frac{\rho_{DM}}{M}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{\delta_{c}}{\sqrt{S(M)}}\left|\frac{dS}{dM}\right|e^{-\frac{\delta_{c}^{2}}{2S}}

Now defining the parameter that captures the probability of PBH formation in regions with specific density perturbations as ;

\nu(M)=\frac{\delta_{c}}{\sqrt{S(M)}}

The mass functions reduces to;

\frac{dn}{dM}\propto\frac{\rho_{DM}}{M}\frac{\nu(M)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left|\frac{d\nu}{dM}\right|e^{-\frac{\nu^{2}}{2}}

\frac{dn}{dM}=\frac{2A_{n}f_{PBH}\rho_{DM}}{\sqrt{2\pi M}}\left|\frac{d\nu}{dM}\right|e^{-\frac{\nu^{2}}{2}}

Where;

A_{n}
is normalization related to initial power spectrum.

\rho_{DM}
is the total dark matter density.

f_{PBH}
is the PBH fraction related to dark matter.

This considers the effects of having only a fraction of DM mass in PBHs, represented as
f_{PBH}
. This fraction allows fir calculating the contribution of DM in black holes based on observable phenomena sensitive to PBH presence and can be symbolized in two ways as mass fraction in a halo and contribution to poisson noise as ;

M_{h}^{PBH}=f_{PBH}M_{h}

This gives the fraction of PBH mass in a halo (
M_{h}^{PBH}
) and is used to estimate the number of PBHs forming a halo or distributed across a region of specific V.

Another perspective uses
f_{PBH}
to calculate the poission noise for overdensities.Since overdensities depend on both initial conditions and poisson noise contributions, which are considered independent.In this case the poisson noise due to PBHs is written as ;

\sigma_{p}=f_{PBH}\sigma_{n}

Where
\sigma_{n}
represents poission noise for a specifuc region being analysed.

Including two main constraints to these PBHs enhance the range of applicability as they are microlensing and CMB constraints.The CMB (Cosmic Microwave Background) constraints limit the fraction of dark matter that can consist of PBHs, especially in the mass range from 10 to 10,000 solar masses, by detecting distortions in the CMB power spectrum caused by PBH accretion and gravitational effects. Taking into account the dynamical processes in the late-time evolution of PBHs, for example, accretion and merger the PBH mass function at a specific redshift

may be modified in the form as ;

\frac{dn}{dm}(m,z)=\frac{f_{PBH}(z)\rho_{DM}(z)}{f_{PBH}\rho_{DM}}\frac{dM}{dm}\left(\frac{dn}{dM}\right)

Where
m(M)
is the PBH mass at redshift

The given relation describes how the primordial black hole (PBH) mass function evolves over cosmic time due to astrophysical processes such as accretion, mergers, and interactions with the surrounding environment. It accounts for the changing fraction of dark matter composed of PBHs and the evolving dark matter density with redshift. The inclusion of the mass transformation factor captures how PBH masses shift over time, providing a more accurate representation of their distribution at different cosmic epochs. This relation is crucial for understanding the role of PBHs as a potential dark matter component and for comparing theoretical predictions with observational constraints from sources such as the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) and microlensing surveys.

5
Conclusion

In conclusion, the application of the Press-Schechter formalism to derive the primordial black hole (PBH) mass function provides a comprehensive statistical approach to estimating the abundance of PBHs formed from the early universe’s density fluctuations. This formalism effectively captures the gravitational collapse of primordial density perturbations by considering the non-linear evolution of the density field and the threshold criteria for collapse. The resulting PBH mass function offers valuable insights into the mass distribution of PBHs and their potential contribution to the dark matter density.

By comparing the theoretical mass function with observational constraints, such as those from microlensing surveys (e.g., OGLE, EROS, HSC) and cosmic microwave background (CMB) anisotropies, the allowed parameter space for PBHs can be constrained, refining the understanding of their formation mechanisms and evolution. These results underscore the importance of PBH studies in exploring scenarios beyond the standard
\Lambda CDM
model and assessing their potential role in explaining various astrophysical and cosmological phenomena, including structure formation and gravitational wave observations.

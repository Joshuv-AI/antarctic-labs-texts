A generalization of Dumas irreducibility criterion
Jitender Singh 1,†

Abstract
. Using Newton polygons, a key factorization result for polynomials over discrete valuation domains is proved, which in particular yields new irreducibility criteria including a generalization of the classical irreducibility criterion of Dumas.

1.
Introduction

The classical irreducibility criteria due to Schönemann
[
9
]
, Eisenstein
[
2
]
, Perron
[
8
]
, and Dumas
[
3
]
have become paradigms in the study of testing irreducibility of polynomials having integer coefficients. The irreducibility criterion due to Perron
[
8
]
is based on a condition on the coefficients of a given polynomial forcing its all but one zeros to lie in the interior of the closed unit disk in the complex plane, which immediately implies the irreducibility. On the other hand, each of the irreducibility results due to Schönemann
[
9
]
and Eisenstein
[
2
]
rests on divisibility properties of the coefficients of a polynomial with respect to a prime number. In 1906, Dumas
[
3
]
proved the following irreducibility criterion, which is a far reaching generalization of the irreducibility results of Schönemann
[
9
]
and Eisenstein
[
2
]
.

Theorem
A

(Dumas
[
3
]
)
.

Let
(R,v)
be a discrete valuation domain. Let
f=a_{0}+a_{1}x+\cdots+a_{n}x^{n}\in{R}[x]
be a primitive polynomial such that

(i)

v(a_{n})=0
,

(ii)

\frac{v(a_{0})}{n}<\frac{v(a_{i})}{n-i}
for each
i=1,\ldots,n-1
,

(iii)

\gcd(v(a_{0}),~n)=1
.

Then the polynomial
f
is irreducible in
R[x]
.

The proof of Theorem
A
uses a fundamental result (see Theorem
B
) of Dumas on Newton polygons. Theorem
A
has been extended to polynomials over any valued field in the papers
[
1
,
7
]
.

The condition
(i)
in the statement of Theorem
A
requires that the valuation of the leading coefficient of the underlying polynomial
f
is zero. If instead the valuation of a coefficient other than the leading coefficient of
f
is zero, can one still have any information about factorization of
f?
What can be said about factorization of
f
if the lower bound in the hypothesis
(ii)
of Theorem
A
is allowed to occur at an index
\ell>0?
Of course the hypothesis
(iii)
may require a modification in connection with that in the hypothesis
(ii)
. As we shall see in this paper, the answer to these questions is in the affirmative with appealing modifications of the hypotheses
(i)
-
(iii)
of Theorem
A
.
The purpose of the present article is to devise such conditions yielding new irreducibility results. In view of this, we first prove a factorization result for polynomials over discrete valuation domains, which in particular yields Theorem
A
. Further, our factorization result yields a class of irreducible polynomials having integer coefficients and also having the property that all zeros of such polynomials have the standard archimedean absolute value sufficiently large. In doing so, we use the well known Newton polygon technique. Main results of this paper are stated and discussed in Sec.
2
and their proofs are presented in Sec.
3
.

2.
Main results

The main results of this paper are certainly true for polynomials over more general domains, which require other technical tools. However, in the present article, we will restrict our attention to the polynomials over discrete valuation domains.
In view of this, our first factorization result is the following mild generalization of Theorem
A
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
(R,v)
be a discrete valuation domain. Let
f=a_{0}+a_{1}x+\cdots+a_{n}x^{n}\in{R}[x]
be a primitive polynomial such that there exist indices
j
and
\ell
with
1\leq\ell+1\leq j\leq n
for which the following hold.

(i)

v(a_{j})=0
,

(ii)

\frac{v(a_{\ell})}{j-\ell}<\frac{v(a_{i})}{j-i}
for each
i=0,1,\ldots,j-1
with
i\neq\ell
,

(iii)

\gcd(v(a_{\ell}),~j-\ell)=1
.

Then any factorization
f(x)=f_{1}(x)f_{2}(x)
of
f
in
R[x]
has a factor of degree
\geq j-\ell
. In particular, if
j=n
and
\ell=0
, then the polynomial
f
is irreducible in
R[x]
.

Note that Dumas irreducibility criterion (Theorem
A
) is precisely the case
j=n
and
\ell=0
of Theorem
1
. Further, the case
j=n
of Theorem
1
yields a generalization of the main result proved in
[
6
]
.

Remark
.

The lower bound on the degree of a factor of
f
as mentioned in Theorem
1
is best possible in the sense that there exist polynomials attaining the said bound. For example, given a field
\mathbb{K}
, the degree valuation
v_{\infty}
on the field of fractions
\mathbb{K}(x)
of the polynomial ring
\mathbb{K}[x]
is defined by setting
v_{\infty}(0)=\infty
, and for any
a,b\in\mathbb{K}[x]\setminus\{0\}
,
v_{\infty}(a/b)=\deg b-\deg a
. Then the valuation ring of
v_{\infty}
in
\mathbb{K}(x)
is the localization
(\mathbb{K}[1/x])_{(1/x)}
of
\mathbb{K}[1/x]
by the prime ideal
(1/x)
of
\mathbb{K}[1/x]
. Consequently,
(\mathbb{K}[1/x]_{(1/x)},v_{\infty})
becomes a discrete valuation domain with respect to the valuation
v_{\infty}
with the uniformizing parameter
1/x
for the ring
\mathbb{K}[1/x]
. Now consider the bivariate polynomial

\displaystyle f(1/x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{-\ell}(1+y^{j-\ell})+x^{-j+\ell+1}y^{\ell}+y^{j}+y^{j+\ell}

\displaystyle+(1-x^{-j+\ell+1})y^{2j-\ell}+y^{2j}\in\mathbb{K}[1/x]_{(1/x)}[y],

which satisfies the hypothesis of Theorem
1
for
1\leq\ell<j<2j=n
,
\ell\neq j-\ell
,
a_{0}(x)=x^{\ell}=a_{j-\ell}(x)
,
a_{\ell}(x)=x^{j-\ell-1}\in\mathbb{K}[x]
,
a_{j}=1
, and
a_{i}=0
for
i\not\in\{0,\ell,j-\ell,j,2j-\ell,j+\ell,2j\}
. Here, we have
v_{\infty}(a_{0}(1/x))=\ell
,
v_{\infty}(a_{\ell}(1/x))=j-\ell-1
, and
v_{\infty}(a_{i}(1/x))=\infty
for
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,j-1\}
with
i\not\in\{0,\ell,j-\ell\}
. Consequently, we have
(i)

v_{\infty}(a_{j}(1/x))=0
,
(ii)

\gcd(v_{\infty}(a_{\ell}(1/x)),j-\ell)=\gcd(j-\ell-1,j-\ell)=1
, and

(iii)

\frac{v_{\infty}(a_{\ell}(1/x))}{j-\ell}=1-\frac{1}{j-\ell}<1=\min_{i\neq\ell,i<j}\{\frac{v_{\infty}(a_{i}(1/x))}{j-i}\}
. This in view of Theorem
1
tells us that the polynomial
f
has a factor of degree
\geq(j-\ell)
. A direct computation tells us that
f
factors as

\displaystyle f(1/x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+y^{j-\ell})(x^{-\ell}+x^{-j+\ell+1}y^{\ell}+(1-x^{-j+\ell+1})y^{j}+y^{j+\ell}),

with the factor
1+y^{j-\ell}
of degree
j-\ell
.

As a consequence of Theorem
1
, we may have the following factorization result.

Corollary 2
.

Let
(R,v)
be a discrete valuation domain. Let
f=a_{0}+a_{1}x+\cdots+a_{n}x^{n}\in{R}[x]
be a primitive polynomial such that there exist indices
j
and
\ell
with
1\leq\ell+1\leq j\leq n
for which the following hold.

(i)

v(a_{j})=0
,

(ii)

v(a_{\ell})\leq\frac{{j-\ell}}{j}v(a_{i})
for each
i=0,1,\ldots,j-1
with
i\neq\ell
,

(iii)

\gcd(v(a_{\ell}),~j-\ell)=1
.

Then any factorization
f(x)=f_{1}(x)f_{2}(x)
of
f
in
R[x]
has a factor of degree
\geq j-\ell
. In particular, if
j=n
and
\ell=0
, then the polynomial
f
is irreducible in
R[x]
.

For a prime number
p
, let
v_{p}
denote the
p
-adic valuation of the field of rational numbers with its valuation ring
\mathbb{Z}
. Recall that
v_{p}(0)=\infty
, and for any nonzero integer
a
,
v_{p}(a)
is the largest nonnegative integer such that
p^{v_{p}(a)}
divides
a
.

Now we may have the following irreducibility criterion for a class of polynomials having integer coefficients.

Theorem 3
.

Let
f=a_{0}+a_{1}x+\cdots+a_{n}x^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
be a primitive polynomial such that there exists a prime number
p
and index
j
with
1\leq j\leq n
for which the following hold.

(i)

v_{p}(a_{j})=0
,

(ii)

\frac{v_{p}(a_{0})}{j}<\frac{v_{p}(a_{i})}{j-i}
for each
i=1,\ldots,j-1
,

(iii)

\gcd(v_{p}(a_{0}),~j)=1
.

Let
d=a_{0}/p^{v_{p}(a_{0})}
. If either
j=n
, or each zero
\theta
of
f
in
{\mathbb{C}}
satisfies
|\theta|>d
, then the polynomial
f
is irreducible in
\mathbb{Z}[x]
.

Remarks
.

(1).
Let
f=a_{0}+a_{1}x+\cdots+a_{n}x^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
be such that

\displaystyle|a_{0}|>|a_{1}|d+\cdots+|a_{n-1}|d^{n-1}+|a_{n}|d^{n}>0,

for some positive real number
d
. If
x\in\mathbb{C}
with
|x|\leq d
, then using the hypothesis we have

\displaystyle|f(d)|\geq|a_{0}|-|a_{1}|d-|a_{2}|d^{2}-\cdots-|a_{n}|d^{n}>0.

This shows that each zero
\theta
of
f
must satisfy
|\theta|>d
.

(2).
Let
f=a_{0}+a_{1}x+\cdots+a_{n}x^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
be a polynomial satisfying conditions analogous to that of the well known Eneström-Kakeya’s theorem (see
[
10
]
for a comprehensive review of Eneström-Kakeya’s theorem and its generalizations), that is, the coefficients of
f
satisfy

\displaystyle a_{0}\geq a_{1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n-1}\geq a_{n}\geq 1.

We show that each zero of such a polynomial
f
lies outside the open unit disk in the complex plane. So, assume on the contrary that there exists a zero
\theta\in\mathbb{C}
of
f
with
|\theta|<1
. We observe that

\displaystyle(x-1)f(x)=-a_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(a_{i-1}-a_{i})x^{i}+a_{n}x^{n+1},

which on taking
x=\theta
with
f(\theta)=0
, we arrive at the following:

\displaystyle a_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(a_{i-1}-a_{i})\theta^{i}+a_{n}\theta^{n+1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(a_{i-1}-a_{i})|\theta|^{i}+a_{n}|\theta|^{n+1}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(a_{i-1}-a_{i})+a_{n}=a_{0},

which is absurd. Thus, each zero
\theta\in\mathbb{C}
of
f
satisfies
|\theta|\geq 1
. If such a polynomial
f
has no cyclotomic factor, then each zero
\theta
of
f
satisfies
|\theta|>1
.

(3).
If
f
is a polynomial as in the preceding Remarks (1) or (2), and the coefficients of
f
satisfy the conditions
(i)
-
(iii)
of Theorem
3
, then it follows that such a polynomial
f
is irreducible in
\mathbb{Z}[x]
.

The next two irreducibility criteria may be deduced from Theorem
3
.

Corollary 4
.

Let
f=a_{0}+a_{1}x+\cdots+a_{n}x^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
be a primitive polynomial such that there exists a prime number
p
and index
j
with
1\leq j\leq n
for which the following hold.

(i)

v_{p}(a_{j})=0
,

(ii)

v_{p}(a_{0})\leq v_{p}(a_{i})
for each
i=1,\ldots,j-1
,

(iii)

\gcd(v_{p}(a_{0}),~j)=1
.

Let
d=a_{0}/p^{v_{p}(a_{0})}
. If either
j=n
, or each zero
\theta
of
f
in
{\mathbb{C}}
satisfies
|\theta|>d
, then
f
is irreducible in
\mathbb{Z}[x]
.

We note that Corollary
4
is precisely one of the main results proved in
[
4
]
.
The following irreducibility criterion might be of independent interest.

Corollary 5
.

Let
f=a_{0}+a_{1}x+\cdots+a_{n}x^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
be a primitive polynomial such that
a_{0}=\pm p^{k}d
for a positive integer
d
and a prime number
p\nmid d
. Let there be a positive integer
m
with
(km+1)\leq n
for which

\displaystyle p^{t}\mid a_{(k-t)m+s},~p^{t+1}\nmid a_{((k-t)m+s},

for every
s=1,\ldots,m
, and for every
t=1,\ldots,k
. Let
p
does not divide
a_{km+1}
.
If either
km+1=n
, or each zero
\theta
of
f
in
{\mathbb{C}}
satisfies
|\theta|>d
, then
f
is irreducible in
\mathbb{Z}[x]
.

Example
.

For a prime number
p
and positive integers
k
and
m
, the polynomial

\displaystyle f=p^{k}+px\Bigl(\frac{x^{m}-1}{x-1}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{x^{km}-p^{k}}{x^{m}-p}\Bigr)+x^{km+1}+x^{km+2}\in\mathbb{Z}[x]

satisfies the hypothesis of Corollary
5
for
n=km+2
, since we may express
f
as

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{k}+p^{k}\phi_{m}(x)+p^{k-1}x^{m}\phi_{m}(x)+\cdots+px^{(k-1)m}\phi_{m}(x)+x^{km+1}+x^{km+2},

where we define

\displaystyle\phi_{m}(x)=x+x^{2}+\cdots+x^{m},

so that here,
a_{(k-t)m+s}=p^{t}
for all
t=1,\ldots,k
and
s=1,\ldots,m
. Since the coefficients of
f
are monotonically decreasing positive integers starting from the constant term to the leading term and that
f
has no cyclotomic factor, it follows that all zeros of
f
lie outside the closed unit disk in the complex plane. Thus,
f
must be irreducible.

Finally, we may have the following factorization result.

Theorem 6
.

Let
f=a_{0}+a_{1}x+\cdots+a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
be such that
a_{0}=\pm p_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{k_{r}}
,
r\geq 2
, where
p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}
are
r
distinct primes and
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}
are all positive integers. Suppose that for each
i=1,\ldots,r
, there exists a positive integer
j_{i}\leq n
for which the following hold.

(i)

v_{p_{i}}(a_{j_{i}})=0
,

(ii)

\frac{k_{i}}{j_{i}}<\frac{v_{p_{i}}(a_{t})}{j_{i}-t}
for each
t=1,\ldots,j_{i}-1
,

(iii)

\gcd(k_{i},j_{i})=1
.

If each zero
\theta
of
f
in
\mathbb{C}
satisfies
|\theta|>1
for all
i=1,\ldots,r
, then the polynomial
f
is a product of at most
r
irreducible factors in
\mathbb{Z}[x]
.

Note that factorization results analogous to Corollaries
4
&
5
may be devised as particular cases of Theorem
6
.

Remark
.

Here also, the lower bound mentioned in Theorem
6
is best possible, since the polynomial

\displaystyle f=x^{r}+\sigma_{1}x^{r-1}+\cdots+\sigma_{r-1}x+\sigma_{r}=(x+{p_{1}}^{k_{1}})\cdots(x+{p_{r}}^{k_{r}})\in\mathbb{Z}[x],

where
\sigma_{i}=\sum_{1\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots<t_{i}\leq n}p_{t_{1}}^{k_{t_{1}}}\cdots p_{t_{i}}^{k_{t_{i}}}
for each
i=1,\ldots,r
satisfies the hypothesis of Theorem
6
for
n=r
with
j_{1}=j_{2}=\cdots=j_{r}=1
. Since
\sigma_{i}\leq\sigma_{i+1}
for each
i=1,\ldots,r-1
, the coefficients of
f
are strictly decreasing positive integers starting with the constant term, it follows that
f
has no cyclotomic factor and each zero
\theta\in\mathbb{C}
of
f
satisfies
|\theta|>1
. Thus,
f
is a product of at most
r
irreducible polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}[x]
, where we see that
f
is actually a product of exactly
r
irreducible factors.

3.
Proofs

To prove our main results, we will recall some notations and definitions. In view of this, if
(R,v)
is a discrete valuation domain, then for any polynomial
f=a_{0}+a_{1}x+\cdots+a_{n}x^{n}\in R[x]
of degree
n\geq 1
, the Newton polygon
NP(f)
of
f
with respect to
v
is defined as the lower convex hull of the points of the set

\displaystyle\{(i,v(a_{i}))~|~i=0,1,\ldots,n\}

in the cartesian plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. By definition,
NP(f)
is a polygonal path in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
where each segment of this path is called an edge of
NP(f)
and the point of intersection of two adjacent (non-collinear) edges on
NP(f)
is called a vertex of
NP(f)
. Note that the sum of projections of all edges of
NP(f)
on the
x
-axis in the cartesian plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is equal to
\deg f
.

The following fundamental result of Dumas connects factorization of polynomials with the geometry of Newton polygons, and it will be used in our proofs.

Theorem
B

(Dumas
[
3
]
)
.

Let
(R,v)
be a discrete valuation domain. Let
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
be nonconstant polynomials in
{R}[x]
with
f_{1}(0)f_{2}(0)\neq 0
. If
f(x)=f_{1}(x)f_{2}(x)
, then the edges in the Newton polygon of
f
with respect to
v
may be formed by constructing a polygonal path composed by translates of all the edges that appear
in the Newton polygons of
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
with respect to
v
, using
exactly one translate for each edge, in such a way as to form a
polygonal path with increasing slopes.

The following well known result will also be used in the sequel.

Lemma 7
.

Let
(R,v)
be a discrete valuation domain. Let
f\in R[x]
be a nonconstant polynomial. Corresponding to a segment
PQ
joining the lattice points
P(a_{1},v(a_{1}))
and
Q(a_{2},v(a_{2}))
in the cartesian plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the number of lattice points on the segment
PQ
is equal to

\displaystyle 1+\gcd(|a_{1}-a_{2}|,|v(a_{1})-v(a_{2})|).

Proof of Lemma
7
.

If we let
d=\gcd(|a_{1}-a_{2}|,|v(a_{1})-v(a_{2})|)
, then we may write
the equation of the line joining the given points
P
and
Q
as

\displaystyle y=v(a_{1})+\frac{v(a_{1})-v(a_{2})}{a_{1}-a_{2}}(x-a_{1})=v(a_{1})+\frac{(v(a_{1})-v(a_{2}))}{d}\frac{(x-a_{1})}{(a_{1}-a_{2})/d}.

Consequently,
(x,y)\in(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z})\cap PQ
if and only if
\frac{(x-a_{1})}{(a_{1}-a_{2})/d}\in\mathbb{Z}
, and that
a_{1}\leq x\leq a_{2}
. In view of this, we find that all lattice points
(x,y)
lying on
PQ
are explicitly given by

\displaystyle x=a_{1}+i\frac{(a_{2}-a_{1})}{d},~y=v(a_{1})+i\frac{v(a_{2})-v(a_{1})}{d},~i=0,1,\ldots,d,

which are exactly
1+d
in number.
∎

In view of Lemma
7
, we note that the segment
PQ
of
NP(f)
has no lattice point on it other than the vertex points
P
and
Q
if and only if
\gcd(|a_{1}-a_{2}|,|v(a_{1})-v(a_{2})|)=1
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

By the hypotheses
(i)
and
(ii)
of the theorem,
NP(f)
has an edge of negative slope joining the vertices
P(\ell,v(a_{\ell}))
and
Q(j,0)
. In view of the hypothesis
(iii)
, we have from Lemma
7
that the segment
PQ
has no lattice point on it other than the end points
P
and
Q
. By Theorem
B
, the segment
PQ
on
NP(f)
is a translate of unique edge of Newton polygon of one of
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
. Suppose that
PQ
is a translate of an edge of
NP(f_{1})
. Then
\deg f_{1}
must be at least the length of the horizontal projection of
PQ
, which is
j-\ell
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
2
.

The given polynomial satisfies all the hypothesis of Theorem
1
. To see this, we only need to verify the hypothesis
(ii)
of Theorem
1
for
f
. We proceed as follows. Since
v(a_{\ell})\leq\frac{j-\ell}{j}v(a_{i})
for each
i=0,1,\ldots,j-1
with
i\neq\ell
, we have

\displaystyle v(a_{\ell})\leq\frac{j-\ell}{j}v(a_{i})<\frac{j-\ell}{j}\times\frac{j}{j-i}v(a_{i})=\frac{j-\ell}{j-i}v(a_{i}),~i=0,1,\ldots,j-1,~i\neq\ell,

which is precisely the hypothesis
(ii)
of Theorem
1
. So, any factorization
f(x)=f_{1}(x)f_{2}(x)
of
f
in
R[x]
has a factor of degree
\geq j-\ell
.
∎

Now we may have the following factorization result, which for the case
\ell=0
will be used in proving Theorems
3
and
6
and it might be of independent interest as well.

Lemma 8
.

Let
(R,v)
be a discrete valuation domain. Let
f=a_{0}+a_{1}x+\cdots+a_{n}x^{n}\in{R}[x]
be such that there exist indices
j
and
\ell
with
1\leq\ell+1\leq j\leq n
for which the following hold.

(i)

v(a_{j})=0
,

(ii)

\frac{v(a_{\ell})}{j-\ell}<\frac{v(a_{i})}{j-i}
for each
i=0,\ldots,j-1
with
i\neq\ell
,

(iii)

\gcd(v(a_{\ell}),~j-\ell)=1
,

If
\ell>1
, then each of the following two conditions also holds.

(iv)

\frac{v(a_{0})-v(a_{\ell})}{\ell}>\frac{v(a_{0})-v(a_{i})}{i}
for each
i=1,\ldots,\ell-1
,

(v)

\gcd(v(a_{0})-v(a_{\ell}),\ell)=1
.

Then for any factorization
f(x)=f_{1}(x)f_{2}(x)
of
f
in
R[x]
one of
v(f_{1}(0))
and
v(f_{2}(0))
belongs to the set
\{0,~v(a_{\ell})\}
.

Proof of Lemma
8
.

Since
v
is a discrete valuation on
R
,
v
is nonnegative on
R
. By the given hypotheses
(i)
and
(ii)
of the lemma, we find that in the Newton polygon
NP(f)
of
f
with respect to
v
, there exists an edge joining the vertices
P(\ell,v(a_{\ell}))
and
Q(j,v(a_{j}))=(j,0)
of the negative slope
-v(a_{\ell})/(j-\ell)
. Since
\gcd(v(a_{\ell}),~j-\ell)=1
, it follows from Lemma
7
that there is no lattice point on the edge
PQ
other than its end points
P
and
Q
. Further, we note that
PQ
is the rightmost edge of
NP(f)
having negative slope.
Let

Lemma 9

Let

Optimizers in Sobolev-curl inequalities
Jarosław Mederski, Andrzej Szulkin

Abstract
. We study a Sobolev-type inequality involving the p p -curl operator in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} . We prove the existence of a minimizer which yields a solution to the p p -curl-curl equation in the critical case. The problem is motivated both by nonlinear Maxwell equations and by the occurrence of zero modes in three-dimensional Dirac equations. Moreover, we introduce a new variational approach that allows to treat quasilinear strongly indefinite problems by direct minimization on a Nehari-type constraint. We also consider existence of minimizers under some symmetry assumptions. Finally, our approach offers a new proof of the compactness of minimizing sequences for the Sobolev inequalities in the critical case.

Introduction

Sobolev inequalities and embedding theorems play a crucial role in the theory of partial differential equations as well as in mathematical physics.
We recall the pioneering result by Aubin
[
3
]
and Talenti
[
34
]
.
Suppose that
N\geq 2
,
1<p<N
and let
S_{p}=S_{p}(N)
be the Sobolev constant, i.e. the largest constant such that the inequality

(1.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx\geq S_{p}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p^{*}}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}}}

holds for any
u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
where
p^{*}:=\frac{Np}{N-p}
and
{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
denotes the homogeneous Sobolev space, i.e the completion of
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with respect to the norm
|\nabla\cdot|_{p}
. Here and in the sequel
|\cdot|_{q}
denotes the
L^{q}
-norm for
q\in[1,\infty]
. Equality holds in (
1.1
) if
u
has the form

(1.2)

u(x)=\big(a+b|x|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\big)^{\frac{p-N}{p}}

for some constants
a,b>0
and the formula for
S_{p}
is given in
[
34
]
. Moreover, recently Damascelli et al.
[
11
]
and Sciunzi
[
28
]
showed that (
1.2
) constitute unique positive solutions to the problem

(1.3)

-\mathop{\mathrm{div}}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)=|u|^{p^{*}-2}u\hbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},

up to translations in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and under some additional relation between
a
and
b
.

Throughout the rest of the paper except for Section
5
we assume
N=3
. Motivated
by the classical inequality (
1.1
) and by physical considerations which we shall discuss later, we want to analyse an analogous inequality with
\nabla u
replaced by
\nabla\times u
, the curl of a vector field
u:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The obvious candidate
S
for a corresponding inequality, the largest possible constant such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\times u|^{p}\,dx\geq S\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{p^{*}}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{3-p}{3}}

holds for
u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
, is certainly not suitable because it equals
0
. Indeed,
\nabla\times u
has a nontrivial (in fact an infinite-dimensional) kernel:
\nabla\times(\nabla\varphi)=0
for all
\varphi\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Throughout the paper
\nabla\times u
as well as the divergence of
u
,
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}u
, should be understood in the distributional sense.

In order to properly define a Sobolev-type inequality we first introduce the Banach space

{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3}):=\big\{u\in L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3}):\nabla\times u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})\big\}

which we endow with the norm

\|u\|:=\left(|u|^{2}_{p^{*}}+|\nabla\times u|^{2}_{p}\right)^{1/2}.

It is standard to show that

{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})
is the closure of
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
with respect to
\|\cdot\|
, see Lemma
A.1
.

Denote the kernel of
\nabla\times(\cdot)
in
{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})
by

{\mathcal{W}}:=\big\{w\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3}):\nabla\times w=0\big\},

and let
S_{p,\mathrm{curl}}
be the largest possible constant such that the
Sobolev-curl inequality

(1.4)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\times u|^{p}\,dx\geq S_{p,\mathrm{curl}}\inf_{w\in{\mathcal{W}}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u+w|^{p^{*}}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{3-p}{3}}

holds for any
u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})\setminus\mathcal{W}
. Inequality (
1.4
) is in fact trivially satisfied also for
u\in{\mathcal{W}}
because then both sides are zero.

The inequality (
1.4
) has been introduced for the first time by the authors in
[
26
]
in the case
p=2
, in the context of nonlinear Maxwell equations and the quintic effect of the medium, see also
[
23
,
24
]
and the references therein. One of the main results of
[
26
]
states that
S_{2,\mathrm{curl}}>S_{2}
and equality holds in (
1.4
) for
u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,2}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})\setminus\mathcal{W}
which is a ground state solution to the critical curl-curl problem

(1.5)

\nabla\times(\nabla\times u)=|u|^{4}u.

It has also been observed that
u
cannot be radially symmetric (i.e.
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{O}}(3)
-equivariant) and a question concerning symmetry properties of ground states has been left as an open problem. In
[
17
]
it has been recently proved that there are infinitely many cylindrically symmetric solutions
(u_{n})

of the form

u_{n}(x)=\frac{v_{n}(x)}{\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}}\Bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}-x_{2}\\
x_{1}\\
0\end{smallmatrix}\Bigr),\quad x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus(\{0\}\times\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}),

where
v_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
is a cylindrically symmetric function, i.e. it is invariant with respect to the action of
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{O}}(2)\times\{1\}\subset{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{O}}(3)
. We emphasize the physical relevance of analyzing (
1.5
). Solutions to this problem correspond to time-harmonic fields

E(x,t)=u(x)\cos(\omega t)
which solve the full nonlinear electromagnetic wave equation when combined with Maxwell’s laws and material relations
[
1
,
6
,
32
]
.
Due to the complexity of the exact model, various approximations are commonly used which lead to a (nonlinear) Schrödinger equation. They often involve neglecting the term
\nabla(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}(u))
in the identity
\nabla\times(\nabla\times u)=\nabla(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}(u))-\Delta u
(the so-called scalar approximation), or applying the slowly varying envelope approximation. However, such simplifications can yield
non-physical
results; see
[
2
,
9
]
. The case
p=3/2
, which has a very different physical background, has been considered in
[
14
]
. See the comments below.

Our main aim in this work is to extend the analysis of
[
14
,
26
]
to a general
p
satisfying
1<p<3
and find solutions to the
p
-curl-curl problem
with critical exponent

(1.6)

\nabla\times(|\nabla\times u|^{p-2}\nabla\times u)=|u|^{p^{*}-2}u\quad\hbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Note that in general for divergence-free
u
,
|\nabla\times u|_{p}\neq|\nabla u|_{p}
unless
p=2
which complicates some arguments as we shall see later. In the quasilinear case
p\neq 2
the strongly indefinite structure of
(
1.6
) is more difficult to treat and we have to take into account the nonlinear nature of the
p
-curl-curl operator.

We want to emphasize that the case
p=3/2
has a special and very important significance in the theory of zero modes of the three-dimensional Dirac equations. Namely, in view of the results of Fröhlich, Lieb, Loss and Yau
[
16
,
22
]
, the existence of zero modes is related to the stability of the hydrogen atom, i.e. finiteness of its ground state energy. Recall that
a
zero mode
is a nontrivial solution
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
to the spinor equation

(1.7)

\sigma\cdot(-i\nabla-u)\psi=0

where
\sigma
stands for the vector of Pauli matrices

\sigma_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\;\sigma_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\
i&0\end{pmatrix},\;\sigma_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}

and
u
is a vector potential for the magnetic field
\nabla\times u
. Loss and Yau
[
22
]
showed that if

(1.8)

\displaystyle u(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle 3(1+|x|^{2})^{-2}\big((1-|x|^{2})w+2(w\cdot x)x+2w\times x)\big)

where
w\neq 0
is a constant vector, then (
1.7
) is satisfied for an explicitly given
\psi
and properly chosen
w
, see
[
22
, Section II]
for the details.

Very recently Frank and Loss
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
have considered a necessary condition for the existence of a zero mode which they express in terms of
S_{3/2,\mathrm{curl}}
, and this constant plays a crucial role in their analysis. We would like to underline that in their arguments conformal invariance (which is present only if
p=3/2
) has been used in order to prove a nonlinear variant of the Rellich-Kondrachov theorem and to deal with the lack of compactness of the problem. In
[
14
]
it has also been noted that
u
given by (
1.8
) satisfies the Euler-Lagrange equation associated with
S_{3/2,\mathrm{curl}}
. We shall show in Theorem
1.3
that in fact this
u
satisfies (
1.6
) with
p=3/2
if
|w|=4/3
.

The aim of this work is to analyze the problem in the full range
1<p<3
and to provide new results also in the cases
p=2
and
p=3/2
. We present a new variational approach that allows to treat quasilinear strongly indefinite problems by a direct minimization on a Nehari-type constraint as will be seen in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
We show that
S_{p,\mathrm{curl}}
is attained and that any optimal vector field
u
for
S_{p,\mathrm{curl}}
is (up to rescaling) a ground state solution to (
1.6
).

Let us define the
energy
functional associated with (
1.6
) by setting

(1.9)

J(u):=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\times u|^{p}\,dx-\frac{1}{p^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{p^{*}}\,dx,\quad u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3}),

and introduce a Nehari-type constraint

(1.10)

{\mathcal{N}}:=\Big\{u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})\setminus{\mathcal{W}}:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\times u|^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{p^{*}}\,dx\hbox{ and }\mathop{\mathrm{div}}(|u|^{p^{*}-2}u)=0\Big\}.

We also introduce the following constant
{\mathcal{H}}_{p}
which is largest positive with the property that the inequality

(1.11)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\times v|^{p}\,dx\geq{\mathcal{H}}_{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla v|^{p}\,dx

holds for any

v\in{\mathcal{V}}:=\left\{v\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3}):\mathop{\mathrm{div}}(v)=0\right\}.

Clearly,
{\mathcal{H}}_{2}=1
since
\nabla\times(\nabla\times v)=-\Delta v
as
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}(v)=0
, and hence
|\nabla\times v|_{2}=|\nabla v|_{2}
. It is not a priori clear that
{\mathcal{H}}_{p}>0
. In Corollary
2.2
we shall show that in fact
{\mathcal{H}}_{p}\in(0,2^{p/2}]
.

Our first main result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

(a)
S_{p,\mathrm{curl}}>S_{p}\cdot{\mathcal{H}}_{p}
.

(b) If
(u_{n})\subset{\mathcal{N}}
is a minimizing sequence for
J
, then there are
(s_{n})\subset(0,\infty)
and
(y_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that, passing to a subsequence,

s_{n}^{3/p^{*}}u_{n}(s_{n}\cdot+y_{n})\to u

where
u
is a minimizer for
J
on
{\mathcal{N}}
.

(c)
\inf_{{\mathcal{N}}}J=\frac{1}{3}S_{p,\mathrm{curl}}^{3/p}
is attained,
u
is a ground state solution to (
1.6
) and equality holds in (
1.4
) for this
u
. If
u
satisfies equality in (
1.4
), then there are unique
t>0
and
w\in{\mathcal{W}}
such that
t(u+w)\in{\mathcal{N}}
and
J(t(u+w))=\inf_{{\mathcal{N}}}J
.

We emphasize that the proof of this theorem is effected via a direct minimization argument, without making use of a Palais-Smale sequence related to
(u_{n})
.

Let

(1.12)

{\mathcal{M}}:=\Big\{u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3}):\mathop{\mathrm{div}}(|u|^{p^{*}-2}u)=0\Big\}.

Both
{\mathcal{M}}
and
{\mathcal{N}}
are topological manifolds, see Sections
2
and
3
. As a consequence of Theorem
1.1
(b) we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

If
(u_{n})\subset{\mathcal{M}}
is a minimizing sequence for
S_{p,\mathrm{curl}}
such that
|u_{n}|_{p^{*}}=1
, then there are
(s_{n})\subset(0,\infty)
and
(y_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that, passing to a subsequence,

\big(s_{n}^{3/p^{*}}u_{n}(s_{n}\cdot+y_{n})\big)

is convergent to a minimizer for
S_{p,\mathrm{curl}}
.

Parts (a) and (c) of Theorem
1.1
have been obtained in
[
26
]
for
p=2
whereas Corollary
1.2
constitutes the main result of
[
14
]
for
p=3/2
.

A natural question arises whether ground states have some symmetry properties. It is easy to see that any
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{O}}(3)
-equivariant vector field is the gradient of a radial function (see e.g.
[
5
, Lemma 4]
,
[
10
, Theorem 1.3]
). Therefore any
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{O}}(3)
-equivariant (weak) solution to (
1.6
) is trivial, and in particular a ground state cannot be radially symmetric. Instead we consider the subgroup
{\mathcal{O}}:={\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{O}}(2)\times\{1\}\subset{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{O}}(3)
and let
{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}_{{\mathcal{O}}}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})
be the subspace of
{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})
consisting of
{\mathcal{O}}
-equivariant vector fields. Similarly as above, let
S_{p,\mathrm{curl}}^{{\mathcal{O}}}
be the largest possible constant such that the Sobolev-curl inequality (
1.4
) holds for any
u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}_{{\mathcal{O}}}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})\setminus\mathcal{W}
. We obtain a symmetric variant of Theorem
1.1
; moreover, we gain some additional insights if
p=3/2
.

Theorem 1.3
.

(a)
S_{p,\mathrm{curl}}^{{\mathcal{O}}}\geq S_{p,\mathrm{curl}}
. Moreover,
S_{3/2,\mathrm{curl}}^{{\mathcal{O}}}\leq 4\pi
.

(b) If
(u_{n})\subset{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{O}}:={\mathcal{N}}\cap{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}_{{\mathcal{O}}}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})
is a minimizing sequence for
J
, then there are
(s_{n})\subset(0,\infty)
and
(y_{n})\subset\{0\}\times\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}
such that, passing to a subsequence,

s_{n}^{3/p^{*}}u_{n}(s_{n}\cdot+y_{n})\to u

where
u
is a minimizer for
J
on
{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{O}}
.

(c)
\inf_{{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{O}}}J=\frac{1}{3}\big(S_{p,\mathrm{curl}}^{{\mathcal{O}}}\big)^{3/p}
is attained and
u
is a solution to (
1.6
).

(d)
u
of the form (
1.8
) is a solution to (
1.6
) with
p=3/2
provided that
|w|=4/3
. It is
{\mathcal{O}}
-equivariant if
w=(0,0,\pm 4/3)
.

It is not clear if
u
of the form (
1.8
) with
|w|=4/3
is a ground state solution to (
1.6
) with
p=3/2
, hence we do not know whether a strict inequality holds in the first statement of Theorem
1.3
(a).

As we shall see below, we can find new solutions to (
1.6
) which, even in the case
p=3/2
, are different from
u
of the form (
1.8
).

In physics and mathematics literature devoted to Maxwell’s equations one considers fields of the form

(1.13)

u(x)=\beta(x)\Bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}-x_{2}\\
x_{1}\\
0\end{smallmatrix}\Bigr),\quad x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus(\{0\}\times\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}),

where
\beta(x)=\beta(r,x_{3})
with
r=|(x_{1},x_{2})|
. Note that
for such
u
,
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}(u)=0
and
\nabla\times(\nabla\times u)=-\Delta u
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus(\{0\}\times\{0\}\times\mathbb{R})
, and a natural question arises if
S_{p,\mathrm{curl}}
is attained by
u
as in (
1.13
). In
[
14
]
it has been conjectured that
S_{3/2,\mathrm{curl}}
is attained by
u
of the form (
1.8
).
If this conjecture is true, it would be reasonable to expect that for no
p\in(1,3)
can
u
of the form (
1.13
) realize
S_{p,\mathrm{curl}}
.

In a similar way as in
[
4
, Section 2]
, if
u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}_{{\mathcal{O}}}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})
, then there exist
{\mathcal{O}}
-equivariant
u_{\rho}
,
u_{\tau}
,
u_{\zeta}\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}_{{\mathcal{O}}}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus(\{0\}\times\{0\}\times\mathbb{R})
,

u_{\rho}(x)
(resp.
u_{\tau}(x)
,
u_{\zeta}(x)
) is the projection of
u(x)
onto
\mathrm{span}\{(x_{1},x_{2},0)\}
(resp.
\mathrm{span}\{(-x_{2},x_{1},0)\}
,
\mathrm{span}\{(0,0,1)\}
).
Hence
u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}_{{\mathcal{O}}}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})
is of the form (
1.13
) if and only if
u
is invariant with respect to the action

{\mathcal{T}}(u)={\mathcal{T}}(u_{\rho}+u_{\tau}+u_{\zeta}):=-u_{\rho}+u_{\tau}-u_{\zeta}.

Then

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}_{{\mathcal{O}}}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})^{{\mathcal{T}}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\big\{u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}_{{\mathcal{O}}}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3}):{\mathcal{T}}(u)=u\big\}.

In a similar way we define

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}_{{\mathcal{O}}}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})^{{\mathcal{S}}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\big\{u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}_{{\mathcal{O}}}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3}):{\mathcal{S}}(u):=-{\mathcal{T}}(u)=u\big\}

which consists of vector fields of the form

(1.14)

u(x)=\alpha(x)\Bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\\
0\end{smallmatrix}\Bigr)+\gamma(x)\Bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
0\\
1\end{smallmatrix}\Bigr),\quad x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus(\{0\}\times\{0\}\times\mathbb{R})

where
\alpha(x)=\alpha(r,x_{3})
and
\gamma(x)=\gamma(r,x_{3})
. Again, in a similar way, let
S_{p,\mathrm{curl}}^{{\mathcal{T}}}
(resp.
S_{p,\mathrm{curl}}^{{\mathcal{S}}}
) be the largest possible constant such that the Sobolev-curl inequality (
1.4
) holds for any
u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}_{{\mathcal{O}}}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})^{{\mathcal{T}}}\setminus\mathcal{W}
(resp.
u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}_{{\mathcal{O}}}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})^{{\mathcal{S}}}\setminus\mathcal{W}
). Let
{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{T}}:={\mathcal{N}}\cap\big({\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}_{{\mathcal{O}}}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)^{{\mathcal{T}}}
and
{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{S}}:={\mathcal{N}}\cap\big({\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}_{{\mathcal{O}}}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)^{{\mathcal{S}}}
. There holds:

Theorem 1.4
.

(a)
S_{p,\mathrm{curl}}^{{\mathcal{T}}}\geq S_{p,\mathrm{curl}}^{{\mathcal{O}}}
and
S_{p,\mathrm{curl}}^{{\mathcal{S}}}\geq S_{p,\mathrm{curl}}^{{\mathcal{O}}}
.

(b) If
(u_{n})\subset{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{T}}
(resp.
(u_{n})\subset{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{S}}
) is a minimizing sequence for
J
, then there are
(s_{n})\subset(0,\infty)
and
(y_{n})\subset\{0\}\times\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}
such that, passing to a subsequence,

s_{n}^{3/p^{*}}u_{n}(s_{n}\cdot+y_{n})\to u

where
u
is a minimizer of
J
on
{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{T}}
(resp.
{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{S}}
).

(c)
\inf_{{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{T}}}J=\frac{1}{3}\big(S_{p,\mathrm{curl}}^{{\mathcal{T}}}\big)^{3/p}
and
\inf_{{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{S}}}J=\frac{1}{3}\big(S_{p,\mathrm{curl}}^{{\mathcal{S}}}\big)^{3/p}
are attained and the minimizers are solutions to (
1.6
) of the form (
1.13
) and (
1.14
).

Observe that
u
given by (
1.8
) is not of the form (
1.13
) or (
1.14
), hence in Theorem
1.4
we have found two new different solutions for
p=3/2
.

Finally, in the last section of the paper we show that our approach by minimization also provides a new proof of the following classical result established by Lions
[
21
]
.

Theorem 1.5
.

The Sobolev inequality (
1.1
) has an optimizer

u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Moreover, any minimizing sequence in
{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is, up to translations, dilations and multiplication by constants,
relatively compact in
{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. If
u
is an optimizer, then there is a unique
t>0
such that
u_{0}:=tu
is a weak solution to (
1.3
).

Recall that Lions
[
21
]
dealt with general sequences converging weakly in
{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and developed a concentration-compactness principle. In the final step this principle was applied to minimizing sequences yielding compactness up to translations and dilations.
Very recently Dietze and Nam
[
12
]
found a simplified version of the concentration-compactness principle and applied it in order to get a shorter proof of the above result.

In our approach we do not use the above concentration-compactness arguments. Instead we project a minimizing sequence on a Nehari-type manifold and apply a different argument based on a result by Solimini
[
30
]
which states that
a bounded sequence in
{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
converges to zero in
L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
if and only if every translation and dilation of it converges weakly to zero in
{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. This allows us to give a new, different proof of Theorem
1.5
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we introduce a functional setting and perform a general concentration-compactness analysis for this case. We show that the topological manifold
{\mathcal{M}}

is locally compactly embedded in
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
for
1\leq q<p^{*}
and that if a sequence
(u_{n})
is contained in this manifold and
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
, then
u_{n}\to u
a.e. after passing to a subsequence. This result will play an essential role in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Note that
{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})
is not locally compactly embedded in
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
for any
q
, so the condition
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}(|u|^{p^{*}-2}u)=0
is crucial here.
The proof of the first main result is given in Section
3
.
In Section
4
we find symmetric solutions and prove Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
, and in Section
5
we give a new proof of Theorem
1.5
mentioned above.

Functional setting and preliminaries

Let
{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
denote the completion of
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
with respect to the norm
|\nabla\cdot|_{p}
. In what follows
\lesssim
and
\gtrsim
denote the inequalities up to a multiplicative constant.
The following Helmholtz decomposition holds (see
[
23
,
25
]
for the case
p=2
).

Lemma 2.1
.

\mathcal{V}
and
{\mathcal{W}}
are closed subspaces of
{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})
and

(2.1)

{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})=\mathcal{V}\oplus\mathcal{W}

where

{\mathcal{V}}:=\Big\{v\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle v,\nabla\varphi\rangle\,dx=0\text{ for every $\varphi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{3})$}\Big\}

and

{\mathcal{W}}:=\Big\{w\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle w,\nabla\times\varphi\rangle\,dx=0\text{ for every $\varphi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})$}\Big\}.

Moreover,
{\mathcal{V}}\subset{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
and the norms
|\nabla\cdot|_{p}
and
|\nabla\times\cdot|_{p}
are equivalent in
\mathcal{V}
.

Note that in the sense of distributions
{\mathcal{V}}=\{v\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3}):\mathop{\mathrm{div}}(v)=0\}
and
{\mathcal{W}}=\{w\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3}):\nabla\times w=0\}

Proof.

We follow similar arguments as in
[
25
, Lemma 2.4]
for
p=2
; however, since in general for divergence-free
u
,
|\nabla\times u|_{p}\neq|\nabla u|_{p}
if
p\neq 2
, we have to modify the proof.

Firstly, we easily check that
\mathcal{V}
and
{\mathcal{W}}
are closed subspaces of
{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Now, take any
u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
u_{n}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that
u_{n}\to u
in
{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Let
\varphi_{n}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be the Newtonian potential of
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}(u_{n})
, i.e.
\varphi_{n}
solves
\Delta\varphi_{n}=\mathop{\mathrm{div}}(u_{n})
. Since
u_{n}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
, then by
[
18
, Proposition 1]
,
\nabla\varphi_{n}\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
for every
r\in(1,\infty)
. In particular,
\nabla\varphi_{n}\in L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
Hence

v_{n}:=u_{n}-\nabla\varphi_{n}\in L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3}).

Note also that
\nabla\times v_{n}=\nabla\times u_{n}
and
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}(v_{n})=0
pointwise. Let
v_{n}=(v_{n}^{1},v_{n}^{2},v_{n}^{3})
and let
e_{i}
denote the
i
-th element of the standard basis in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
(so
v_{n}^{i}=\langle v_{n},e_{i}\rangle
). Since

-\Delta v_{n}=\nabla\times\nabla\times v_{n},

employing the vector calculus identity

\mathop{\mathrm{div}}(A\times B)=\langle\nabla\times A,B\rangle-\langle A,\nabla\times B\rangle

with
A=\nabla\times v_{n}
and
B=e_{i}
, we obtain

-\Delta v_{n}^{i}=\langle\nabla\times(\nabla\times v_{n}),e_{i}\rangle=\mathop{\mathrm{div}}((\nabla\times v_{n})\times e_{i})=:\mathop{\mathrm{div}}(f_{n}^{i}),\quad i=1,2,3.

Note that
\nabla\times v_{n}=\nabla\times u_{n}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
and

f_{n}^{i}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Again by
[
18
, Proposition 1]
,

|\nabla v_{n}^{i}|_{p}\leq C_{p}|f_{n}^{i}|_{p}\quad\hbox{for }i=1,2,3

where
C_{p}>0
is a constant.
Hence

(2.2)

|\nabla v_{n}|_{p}\leq D_{p}|\nabla\times v_{n}|_{p}=D_{p}|\nabla\times u_{n}|_{p},

for a constant
D_{p}>0
depending on
C_{p}
.
Similarly we obtain that for
m,n\geq 1
,

|\nabla(v_{n}-v_{m})|_{p}\leq D_{p}|\nabla\times(v_{n}-v_{m})|_{p}=D_{p}|\nabla\times(u_{n}-u_{m})|_{p}\leq D_{p}\|u_{n}-u_{m}\|.

Thus
(v_{n})
is a Cauchy sequence in
{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Let
v:=\lim_{n\to\infty}v_{n}
in
{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle v,\nabla\varphi\rangle\,dx=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle v_{n},\nabla\varphi\rangle\,dx=0

for any
\varphi\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, hence
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}(v)=0
and
v\in{\mathcal{V}}
.
Moreover, as
|\nabla\times v|^{2}\leq 2|\nabla v|^{2}
, we have

(2.3)

|\nabla\times(v_{n}-v)|_{p}\leq 2^{1/2}|\nabla(v_{n}-v)|_{p}\to 0,

so
v_{n}\to v
in
{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})
and

\nabla\varphi_{n}=u_{n}-v_{n}\to u-v
in
{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Since
\nabla\varphi_{n}\in{\mathcal{W}}
and
{\mathcal{W}}
is closed,

u-v\in{\mathcal{W}}
and we get the decomposition

u=v+(u-v)\in{\mathcal{V}}+{\mathcal{W}}.

Now take
v\in{\mathcal{V}}\cap\mathcal{W}
and since
\nabla\times v=0
,
by
[
19
, Lemma 1.1(i)]
,
v=\nabla\xi
for some
\xi\in W^{1,{p^{*}}}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Since
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}(v)=0
,
\xi
is harmonic and so is
v
. Since
v\in L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
, by the mean-value formula we infer that
v=0
, so (
2.1
) holds.

From (
2.2
) with
v
replacing
v_{n}
and (
2.3
) with
v
replacing
v_{n}-v
we see that

(2.4)

2^{-1/2}|\nabla\times v|_{p}\leq|\nabla v|_{p}\leq D_{p}|\nabla\times v|_{p},

i.e. the norms
|\nabla\cdot|_{p}
and
|\nabla\times\cdot|_{p}
are equivalent in
{\mathcal{V}}
.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

{\mathcal{H}}_{p}\in(0,2^{p/2}]
where
{\mathcal{H}}_{p}
is the constant introduced in (
1.11
).

Proof.

According to (
2.4
),
{\mathcal{H}}_{p}^{1/p}\geq D_{p}^{-1}>0
and

|\nabla\times v|_{p}\geq{\mathcal{H}}_{p}^{1/p}|\nabla v|_{p}\geq{\mathcal{H}}_{p}^{1/p}\,2^{-1/2}|\nabla\times v|_{p}.

Hence
{\mathcal{H}}_{p}\in(0,2^{p/2}]
.
∎

In Theorem
2.4
below we formulate a concentration-compactness-type result in
{\mathcal{M}}
.
By convexity, for any
v\in{\mathcal{V}}
we find a unique
w(v)\in{\mathcal{W}}
such that

(2.5)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v+w(v)|^{p^{*}}\,dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v+w|^{p^{*}}\,dx\quad\text{for all }w\in{\mathcal{W}}.

This implies that

(2.6)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle|v+w|^{p^{*}-2}(v+w),\zeta\rangle\,dx=0\quad\text{for all }\zeta\in{\mathcal{W}}\text{ if and only if }w=w(v).

Since in particular we can take
\zeta=\nabla\varphi
with
\varphi\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, equivalently we have

(2.7)

v+w\in{\mathcal{M}}\text{ where }v\in{\mathcal{V}},\ w\in{\mathcal{W}}\quad\text{if and only if}\ w=w(v)

(recall the definition (
1.12
) of
{\mathcal{M}}
).

Lemma 2.3
.

The mapping
v\mapsto w(v)
is continuous from
{\mathcal{V}}
to
{\mathcal{W}}
in the
L^{p^{*}}
-topology, hence also in
{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})
. In particular,
{\mathcal{M}}
is a topological manifold.

Proof.

Suppose
v_{n},v\in{\mathcal{V}}
and
v_{n}\to v
in the
L^{p^{*}}
-topology. By (
2.5
),
(w(v_{n}))
is bounded, hence
w(v_{n})\rightharpoonup w
for some
w\in{\mathcal{W}}
after passing to a subsequence. So

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v_{n}+w(v_{n})|^{p^{*}}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v+w|^{p^{*}}\,dx\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v+w(v)|^{p^{*}}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v_{n}+w(v)|^{p^{*}}\,dx\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v_{n}+w(v_{n})|^{p^{*}}\,dx,

thus
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v_{n}+w(v_{n})|^{p^{*}}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v+w(v)|^{p^{*}}\,dx
and
w(v_{n})\to w(v)
in
{\mathcal{W}}
.
∎

Appendix A
Density lemma

Lemma A.1
.

{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})
is the closure of
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
with respect to
\|\cdot\|
.

Proof.

Let
\chi_{R}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be such that
|\nabla\chi_{R}|\leq 2/R
,
\chi_{R}=1
for
|x|\leq R
and
\chi_{R}=0
for
|x|\geq 2R
. Take
u=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Then
\chi_{R}u\to u
in
L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
as
R\to\infty
. We have

(A.1)

\partial_{i}(\chi_{R}u_{j})-\partial_{j}(\chi_{R}u_{i})=(\partial_{i}\chi_{R})u_{j}-(\partial_{j}\chi_{R})u_{i}+\chi_{R}(\partial_{i}u_{j}-\partial_{j}u_{i}),\quad i\neq j

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\partial_{i}\chi_{R}|^{p}|u_{j}|^{p}\,dx\leq\left(\int_{R\leq|x|\leq 2R}|\partial_{i}\chi_{R}|^{3}\,dx\right)^{p/3}\left(\int_{R\leq|x|\leq 2R}|u_{j}|^{p^{*}}\,dx\right)^{p/p^{*}}.

Since

\int_{R\leq|x|\leq 2R}|\partial_{i}\chi_{R}|^{3}\,dx\leq C

for some
C>0
,

(\partial_{i}\chi_{R})u_{j}\to 0
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. As
\partial_{i}u_{j}-\partial_{j}u_{i}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, it follows that the left-hand side in (
A.1
) tends to
\partial_{i}u_{j}-\partial_{j}u_{i}
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
as
R\to\infty
.
Hence
\chi_{R}u\to u
in
{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})
and functions of compact support are dense in
{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Suppose now
u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})
has compact support. Clearly,
j_{\varepsilon}*u\to u
in
L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
where
j_{\varepsilon}
is the standard mollifier. Since

\partial_{i}(j_{\varepsilon}*u_{j})-\partial_{j}(j_{\varepsilon}*u_{i})=j_{\varepsilon}*(\partial_{i}u_{j}-\partial_{j}u_{i})

and
\partial_{i}u_{j}-\partial_{j}u_{i}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, the right-hand side above tends to
\partial_{i}u_{j}-\partial_{j}u_{i}
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. This completes the proof.
∎

Appendix B

Using (

.

0=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle|v_{n}+w(v_{n})|^{p^{*}-2}(w_{n}-w(v_{n})),w\rangle\,dx\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle|v_{0}+w_{0}|^{p^{*}-2}(v_{0}+w_{0}),w\rangle\,dx

for any
w\in{\mathcal{W}}
, hence taking into account (
2.6
) we get
w_{0}=w(v_{0})
which completes the proof.

\Box

Appendix C
Relation between
v
and
|v|

Suppose
v\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Since
|v(x)|\leq\max\{v_{i}(x):i=1,2,3\}
, it follows from
[
20
, Theorem 6.17 and Corollary 6.18]
that
|v|\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

We show that (
3.4
) holds. For notational convenience we denote the inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
by
\cdot
instead of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. We have

(C.1)

(\partial_{i}|v|)^{2}=\frac{(v\cdot\partial_{i}v)^{2}}{|v|^{2}}\leq|\partial_{i}v|^{2},\qquad i=1,2,3,

where the middle term should be understood as
0
if
v(x)=0
. Summing over
i
gives the result.

Lemma C.1
.

Suppose that
v(x)\neq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
v/|v|\in{\mathcal{D}}_{loc}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Then
\left|\nabla|v|\right|=|\nabla v|
a.e. if and only if there exists a constant vector
e_{0}
such that
v=|v|e_{0}
a.e.

Proof.

If
\left|\nabla|v(x)|\right|=|\nabla v(x)|
, then equality holds in (
C.1
) for this
x
and hence
\partial_{i}v(x)=\lambda_{i}(x)v(x)
,
i=1,2,3
. Write
v(x):=|v(x)|e_{0}(x)
. Then

\lambda_{i}v=\partial_{i}v=(\partial_{i}|v|)e_{0}+|v|\partial_{i}e_{0}=\frac{v\cdot\partial_{i}v}{|v|}\,e_{0}+|v|\partial_{i}e_{0}=\lambda_{i}|v|e_{0}+|v|\partial_{i}e_{0}=\lambda_{i}v+|v|\partial_{i}e_{0}.

So
\nabla e_{0}=0
a.e. and since
e_{0}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{loc}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
, it is a constant vector as claimed.
∎

Duffin–Schaeffer examples, real residue systems, and Bohr-set primes
Stefan M. Hesseling and Felipe A. Ramírez

Abstract
. We prove the following generalization of a well-known result of Duffin and Schaeffer: For any given countable sets Y ⊆ ℝ Y\subseteq\mathbb{R} and Z ⊆ ℝ ∖ span ℚ ⁡ ( { 1 } ∪ Y ) Z\subseteq\mathbb{R}\setminus\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}(\{1\}\cup Y) , there exist functions \psi such that the set of inhomogeneously \psi -approximable numbers has zero measure or full measure, according as the inhomogeneous parameter lies in Y Y or Z Z . The proof uses an analogue of residue systems where the residues can take arbitrary real values, and it also requires information about the distribution of primes lying in Bohr sets. We extend a theorem of Rogers to the more general real residues setting, and we extend Siegel–Walfisz’s prime number theorem for arithmetic progressions to the setting of prime numbers lying in Bohr sets. We also prove that circle rotations equidistribute when sampled along such primes, provided the rotation angle is rationally independent of the Bohr set parameter, generalizing a theorem of Vinogradov. An appendix by Manuel Hauke answers a combinatorial question that is posed in the introduction.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Inhomogeneous Duffin–Schaeffer-type examples

For a real number
y\in\mathbb{R}
and a function
\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
,
we study

W(\psi,y)=\left\{x\in[0,1]:\lVert qx-y\rVert<\psi(q)\textrm{ infinitely often}\right\},

the set of inhomogeneously
\psi
-approximable numbers with
inhomogeneous parameter
y
, where
\lVert\cdot\rVert
denotes distance to

\mathbb{Z}
.

It is a classical theorem of Szüsz
[
14
, 1958]
that
the Lebesgue measure
\mu(W(\psi,y))
is
0
if the series

\sum\psi(q)
converges, and
1
if it diverges and
\psi
is
nonincreasing. This result is usually referred to as the inhomogeneous
Khintchine theorem, since it generalizes Khintchine’s
theorem
[
10
, 1924]
to the
y\neq 0
case. Duffin and
Schaeffer
[
3
, 1941]
showed by example that the
monotonicity condition cannot be omitted from Khintchine’s theorem,
and the second author
[
12
, 2017]
showed that it cannot
be omitted from Szüsz’s inhomogeneous result.

More specifically,
[
12
, Theorem 1]
showed that for any
countable set
Y\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, one can find a function

\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
such that
\sum\psi(q)
diverges, while

\mu(W(\psi,y))=0
for every
y\in Y
. In
addition,
[
12
, Theorem 2]
showed—conditionally—that
if
Z\subseteq\mathbb{R}\setminus\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}(\{1\}\cup Y)
is another countable set,
then one can find
\psi
as before, and with the further property that

\mu(W(\psi,z))=1
for every
z\in Z
. The condition was a
conjectural dynamical statement
[
12
, Conjecture 17]

which could be seen as analogous to Erdős’s famous covering
systems problems
[
6
,
5
]
(to be discussed).

In this paper, we prove the following
unconditional
version
of
[
12
, Theorems 1 and 2]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Duffin–Schaeffer examples with prescribed
behavior)
.

Let
Y,Z\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be countable sets such that

Z\subseteq\mathbb{R}\setminus\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}(\{1\}\cup Y)
. Then there exists

\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
such that

\mu(W(\psi,y))=0\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\mu(W(\psi,z))=1

for every
y\in Y
and
z\in Z
.

The proof is a construction. The functions
\psi
will be supported in
blocks of integers that are products of elements of Bohr sets, that
is, (judiciously chosen) sets of the form

N_{\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{y},{\varepsilon})=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\lVert n\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{v}\rVert<{\varepsilon}\},

where
\mathbf{y},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
and
{\varepsilon}>0
and
\lVert\cdot\rVert
denotes
distance to
\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
. Loosely speaking, the Bohr set condition leads
to small measure for
W(\psi,y)
, and the remaining challenge is to
show large measure for
W(\psi,z)
. This is achieved in part by
addressing the aforementioned obstacle related to covering systems,
leading to ?THM?
below, a generalization of a
theorem of Rogers. In the use of ?THM?
to get lower
measure-bounds, it is convenient if the support of
\psi
consists of
square-free integers. This leads us to prove
?THM?s
and
. The first generalizes
Siegel–Walfisz’s and Dirichlet’s theorems for prime numbers lying in
arithmetic progressions to primes lying in Bohr sets; the second
generalizes a Vinogradov’s theorem
[
17
,
16
,
13
]
on
the equidistribution modulo one of
(pz)_{p\in\mathbb{P}}
, where
\mathbb{P}

denotes the primes, to the equidistribution of
(pz)_{p\in\mathbb{P}\cap N}

where
N
is some Bohr set.

1.2.
Real residue systems

To a pair of
\ell
-tuples
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell}
and

\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
we associate the collection of
arithmetic progressions
\{a_{i}+n_{i}\mathbb{Z}\}_{i=1,\dots,\ell}
and
refer to it as a
residue system
. If

(1)

\mathbb{Z}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\left\lparen a_{i}+n_{i}\mathbb{Z}\right\rparen,

then it is called a
covering system
. Erdős introduced
covering systems in
[
6
, 1950]
, and several questions about
them have attracted much attention over the years. The most well-known
of these questions was the minimum modulus problem—
Do there
exist covering systems with
n_{1}<n_{2}<\dots<n_{\ell}
and
n_{1}

arbitrarily large?
—, but there have been many others. After work
of Filaseta
et al.
where the asymptotic densities of residue
systems were studied
[
7
]
, Hough answered the minimum modulus
question in the negative
[
9
]
. We encourage the reader to
see
[
7
,
9
,
1
]
and the references therein for a
detailed account of the ongoing history of covering systems. One
result that is especially relevant here is due to Rogers, and it
states that

d\left\lparen\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\left\lparen a_{i}+n_{i}\mathbb{Z}\right\rparen\right\rparen\geqslant d\left\lparen\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}n_{i}\mathbb{Z}\right\rparen

for all
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{n}
, where
d
denotes asymptotic
density
[
8
, Page 242]
. In other words, the
asymptotic density of a residue system is minimized when
\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{0}
.

We will make use of an analogue of residue systems where the residues
are allowed to take arbitrary real values. To a pair of
\ell
-tuples

\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell})\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell},

and a real number
{\varepsilon}>0
,
we associate the
real residue system

R({\varepsilon},\mathbf{a},\mathbf{n})=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\left\lparen\left[a_{i}-{\varepsilon},a_{i}+{\varepsilon}\right]+n_{i}\mathbb{Z}\right\rparen.

Notice that the pair
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}\times\mathbb{N}^{\ell}
defines a
covering system if and only if
R(1/2,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{n})=\mathbb{R}
. Notice, also,
that if
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}\times\mathbb{N}^{\ell}
, then

d\left\lparen\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\left\lparen a_{i}+n_{i}\mathbb{Z}\right\rparen\right\rparen=\frac{1}{\lcm(\mathbf{n})}\mu(R(1/2,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{n})\cap\lcm(\mathbf{n})),

where
\lcm(\mathbf{n})=\lcm(n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{\ell})
. Since

R(1/2,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{n})
is periodic, one may replace
\lcm(\mathbf{n})
with any
common multiple of
n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell}
in the above identity.

The proof of ?THM?
relies crucially on the following
extension of Rogers’s result to the setting of real residue systems.

Theorem 1.2

(Rogers’s theorem for real residues)
.

Let
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell}
and
{\varepsilon}=2^{-k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

(2)

\mu\left\lparen R({\varepsilon},\mathbf{a},\mathbf{n})\cap[0,\lcm(\mathbf{n})]\right\rparen\geqslant\mu\left\lparen R({\varepsilon},\mathbf{0},\mathbf{n})\cap[0,\lcm(\mathbf{n})]\right\rparen

for all
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
.

Remark
.

When
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
and
{\varepsilon}=1/2
, ?THM?
is a
restatement of the above mentioned theorem of Rogers.

The functions
\psi
that we construct in the proof
of ?THM?
give rise to a series of real residue
systems, and the measure bound in ?THM?
will redound
to lower bounds on the measures of certain approximation sets
associated to
\psi
and an inhomogeneous parameter
z\in Z
. ?THM?
allows for a comparison to the homogeneous
case
z=0
. Once there, the measure bounds will come from a sieving
argument that is made easier if there is a uniform upper bound on

\gcd(n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})
, where
n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell}
are the moduli
of the associated real residue system and lie in a Bohr set.

This motivates us to construct
\psi
in such a way that the residue
systems that arise have moduli lying in
\mathbb{P}
, the primes. In order to
show that
\psi
, so constructed, has the desired properties
for ?THM?
, it becomes important to understand the
distribution of primes lying in Bohr sets.

1.3.
Distribution of primes lying in Bohr sets

We generalize two classical results about primes lying in arithmentic
progressions to the setting of primes lying in Bohr sets.

The first is a generalization of the prime number
theorem for arithmetic
progressions. For
c\in\mathbb{N}
and

a\in\mathbb{Z}
, denote

\mathbb{P}_{a}=\{p\in\mathbb{P}:p\equiv a\pmod{c}\}.

The prime number theorem for arithmetic progressions states that

\#(\mathbb{P}_{a}\cap[1,X])\sim\frac{X}{\varphi(c)\log X}\qquad(X\to\infty)

if
\gcd(a,c)=1
, where
\varphi
is Euler’s totient function. This
was first proved by Siegel–Walfisz. Previously, Dirichlet had shown
that
\sum_{p\in\mathbb{P}_{a}}1/p
diverges, a result whose corresponding
generalization to Bohr sets we will also need.
(See
[
15
, Chapter II.8]
)

Let
\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
and
{\varepsilon}>0
, and denote

H=\overline{\{n\mathbf{w}\bmod 1:n\in\mathbb{N}\}}\subseteq\mathbb{T}^{\ell}

and its normalized Haar measure
\mu_{H}
. Let
c
be the number of
connected components of
H
and put

H^{\times}=\overline{\{n\mathbf{w}\bmod 1:\gcd(n,c)=1\}}.

Let
\mu^{\times}
denote the renormalized restriction of
\mu_{H}
to

H^{\times}
, that is,

\mu^{\times}=\frac{1}{\mu(H^{\times})}\mathbb{1}_{H^{\times}}\mu_{H}.

We prove the following.

Theorem 1.3

(Prime number theorem for Bohr sets)
.

Let
\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
and
{\varepsilon}>0
. Let
H^{\times}
and

\mu^{\times}
be as above, and let
B(\mathbf{v},{\varepsilon})
the ball of radius

{\varepsilon}>0
centered at
\mathbf{v}
.

a)

If
B(\mathbf{v},{\varepsilon})\cap H^{\times}\neq\emptyset
, then

\#(\mathbb{P}\cap N_{\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{w},{\varepsilon}))=\infty
and, moreover,

\#(\mathbb{P}\cap N_{\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{w},{\varepsilon})\cap[1,X])\sim\mu^{\times}(B(\mathbf{v},{\varepsilon}))\frac{X}{\log X}.

b)

If
B(\mathbf{v},{\varepsilon})\cap H^{\times}=\emptyset
, then
\#(\mathbb{P}\cap N_{\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{w},{\varepsilon}))<\infty
.

Remark
.

When
w=1/c
and
v=aw
, where
(a,c)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{N}
, and

0<{\varepsilon}<1/c
, the above theorem returns the prime number theorem
for arithmetic progressions. In this case,
\mu^{\times}
is the
measure supported uniformly on the points

\{n/c:\gcd(n,c)=1\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, and one has

B(v,{\varepsilon})=\{a/c\}
and
\mu^{\times}(B(v,{\varepsilon}))=1/\varphi(c)
.

The next result concerns equidistribution. A sequence

(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of real numbers is said to be equidistributed modulo
one if for every nonempty open interval
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, one has

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n\leqslant N}\mathbb{1}_{I}(x_{n}\bmod 1)=\mu(I).

The foundational theorem, known as the Kronecker–Weyl theorem, says
that for
z\in\mathbb{R}
, the sequence
(nz)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is equidistributed
modulo one if and only if
z
is irrational.

This descends to the primes. The sequence
(pz)_{p\in\mathbb{P}}
is
equidistributed modulo one if
z
is irrational, a result that is
often attributed to Vinogradov
[
17
]
and can also be found
in work of Rhin
[
13
]
and Vaughan
[
16
]
. It is not hard
to extend this to primes lying in arithmetic progressions: If

\gcd(a,c)=1
and
z
is irrational, then
(pz)_{p\in\mathbb{P}_{a}}
is
equidistributed modulo one. Again, we generalize from primes lying in
arithmetic progressions to primes lying in Bohr sets.

Theorem 1.4

(Equidistribution along Bohr-set primes)
.

Let
\mathbf{y},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
{\varepsilon}>0
, and let
c
be the number of connected components of

\overline{\{n\mathbf{y}\bmod 1:n\in\mathbb{N}\}}.

If

B(\mathbf{v},{\varepsilon})\cap\overline{\{n\mathbf{y}\bmod 1:\gcd(n,c)=1\}}\neq\emptyset,

then for every
z\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}\{1,y_{1},\dots,y_{k}\}
,
the sequence
\left\lparen pz\right\rparen_{p\in\mathbb{P}\cap N_{\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{y},{\varepsilon})}
is
equidistributed modulo one.

Remark
.

When
y=1/c
,
v=ay
, where
\gcd(a,c)=1
, and
0<{\varepsilon}<1/c
,
the above theorem says that
(pz)_{p\in\mathbb{P}_{a}}
is equidistributed
modulo one.

1.4.
Further discussion and questions

Before proceeding to the proofs, we pose some questions that arise
naturally but are not pursued here.

The functions
\psi
that we construct for ?THM?
are
far from monotonic. They are not even monotonic on their support. The
same is true of the classical Duffin–Schaeffer counterexamples
in
[
3
]
and the inhomogeneous versions
in
[
12
]
. The recent paper
[
2
]
constructs
examples that
are
monotonic on their support and in fact can
have values on the support that match any prescribed decreasing
function
f
, although in the inhomogeneous cases they require
restrictions on the inhomogeneous parameters. We ask the following.

Problem 1.5
.

Is ?THM?
true with the added condition that
\psi

is monotonic on its support? Given a non-increasing function

f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
, is ?THM?
true for a function

\psi=\mathbb{1}_{S}f
for some
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}
?

We hasten to point out that the answers cannot simply be yes. By a
result of Yu
[
18
, Theorem 1.3]
, if

\psi(q)\ll\frac{1}{q(\log\log q)^{2}}
and
\sum\psi(q)
diverges,
then
\mu(W(\psi,y))=1
for every “tamely Liouville” number

y
. This immediately implies that if

f(q)=\frac{1}{q(\log\log q)^{2}}
, then ?THM?

must require restrictions on the set
Y\subseteq\mathbb{R}
.

Regarding ?THM?
, part of the proof relies on a trick
where we dilate real residue systems
R({\varepsilon},\mathbf{a},\mathbf{n})
until their
intervals have unit length. For this reason, it is convenient to
restrict to values of
{\varepsilon}>0
that are powers of
1/2
. We ask the
following question.

Problem 1.6
.

Is ?THM?
true with general
{\varepsilon}>0
?

One can also ask a version of the question where
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
. In
this generality, the residue system
R({\varepsilon},\mathbf{a},\mathbf{n})
is not
necessarily periodic, so rather than intersect with a fixed interval
(like
[0,\lcm(\mathbf{n})]
), it would make sense to intersect with an
interval
[-X,X]
and study measure bounds as
X\to\infty
.

Finally, we pose an additive combinatorial question, whose answer
appears in Appendix
A
, contributed by Manuel
Hauke. In Lemma
we show that the Bohr sets arising in
our constructions contain enough primes that the sums of their
reciprocals diverge. The question arose whether this phenomenon holds
more generally.

Problem 1.7

(Answered by ?THM?
)
.

Does every positive-density set
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
have a subset

B\subseteq A
such that
\sup_{m,n\in B}\gcd(m,n)<\infty
and such
that
\sum_{n\in B}\frac{1}{n}
diverges?

After the first posting of this paper, Manuel Hauke wrote to us and
kindly gave a negative answer. ?THM?
shows that the sets
A

for which the answer is “yes” are precisely those that contain

n_{0}\mathcal{P}
for some
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathbb{P}
with

\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}1/p
diverging. ?THM?
uses this
characterization to construct density-one sets for
which ?THM?
does not hold.

2.
Proof of Theorem

For
q\in\mathbb{N}
,
y\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\delta>0
, denote

A_{q}^{y}(\delta)=\{x\in[0,1]:\lVert qx-y\rVert<\delta\}.

When
\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}

A_{q}^{y}(\psi)=A_{q}^{y}(\psi(q))
for brevity.

The proof of ?THM?
reduces to the following
proposition, whose proof appears in Section
5

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\{y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and

z\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}\{1,y_{1},\dots,y_{k}\}
. Let

0<{\varepsilon}<1/2
. Then there exists a square-free integer
P>1
such
that

(3)

\mu\left\lparen\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid P\\
p\textrm{ prime}\end{subarray}}A_{P/p}^{y}\left\lparen\frac{{\varepsilon}}{2p}\right\rparen\right\rparen<2{\varepsilon}\quad\textrm{for each}\quad y\in\{y_{1},\dots,y_{k}\},

and

(4)

\mu\left\lparen\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid P\\

Proof of ?THM?
modulo ?THM?
.

The function
\psi
will be supported in a union of blocks which are

\displaystyle S_{1,1},

\displaystyle S_{2,1},\quad S_{2,2},

\displaystyle S_{3,1},\quad S_{3,2},\quad S_{3,3},

\displaystyle\dots

so that

\supp\psi=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{k}S_{k,\ell}

where each

is a finite set of integers which we now
define.

First, enumerate the sets
Y=\{y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},\dots\}
and

Z=\{z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},\dots\}
. We proceed inductively, moving
through
\{(k,\ell)\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}:1\leqslant\ell\leqslant k\}
according to the lexicographic ordering

\displaystyle(i,j)\prec(k,\ell)

\displaystyle\iff[i<k]\textrm{ or }[i=k\textrm{ and }j<\ell]

\displaystyle\iff\lex(i,j)<\lex(k,\ell)

For each

with

y_{1},\dots,y_{k},z_{\ell},2^{-k}
filling the roles of

3
) and (
4
)

S_{k,\ell}=\left\{\frac{2^{m_{k,\ell}}P_{k,\ell}}{p}:p\mid P_{k,\ell}\textrm{ and }p\textrm{ is prime}\right\},

\lim_{m\to\infty}\mu\left\lparen\bigcup_{q\in S_{i,j}}A_{q}^{z}(\psi)\cap T^{-m}\left\lparen\bigcup_{p\mid P_{k,\ell}}A_{P_{k,\ell}/p}^{z}\left\lparen\frac{2^{-k}}{2p}\right\rparen\right\rparen\right\rparen\\

for all

. In particular, one may take

\psi
fulfills the claims made
in ?THM?
. Let
y\in Y

for some

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\mu\left\lparen\bigcup_{q\in S_{k,\ell}}A_{q}^{y}(\psi)\right\rparen<\infty.

Now by the Borel–Cantelli lemma,

4.
Distribution of primes lying in Bohr sets

A sequence

in a compact topological group

said to be

with respect to a Borel measure

\mu
if for every nonempty open set

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n\leqslant N}\mathbb{1}_{I}(x_{n})=\mu(I).

One of the standard methods for establishing the equidistribution of
sequences is Weyl’s criterion.

Theorem 4.1

(Weyl’s criterion,
[
11
, Corollary 5.2]
)
.

Let
G
be a compact abelian group. A sequence
x_{n}\in G
is
equidistributed with respect to Haar measure
\mu_{G}
if and only if
for every non-trivial periodic character
\chi:G\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, we
have

\sum_{n\leqslant N}\chi(x_{n})=o(N)

as
N\to\infty

4.1.
Equidistribution of toral translations

It is well-known that if the real numbers
1,w_{1},w_{2},\dots w_{\ell}

are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
, then the orbit

\lparen n\mathbf{w}\bmod 1\rparen_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, where
\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},\dots,w_{\ell})
,
equidistributes in the torus
\mathbb{T}^{\ell}
with respect to Lebesgue
measure,
\mu:=\mu_{\mathbb{T}^{\ell}}
. More generally, the orbit of an
arbitrary vector
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
equidistributes in its orbit
closure,

(9)

H:=\overline{\{n\mathbf{w}\bmod 1:n\in\mathbb{N}\}},

which is a subgroup of
\mathbb{T}^{\ell}
. Here, the equidistribution is with
respect to Haar measure,
\mu_{H}
, on
H
. This is known as the
Kronecker–Weyl theorem, and it can be proved by applying Weyl’s
criterion.

Furthermore, the orbit closure
H
is a union of disjoint affine
sub-tori,

(10)

H=H_{0}\sqcup H_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup H_{c-1},

where
H_{a}=a\mathbf{w}+H_{0}
, for

and

is the size
of the torsion subgroup of
\mathbb{T}^{\ell}/H
. The orbit of
\mathbf{w}
cycles
through the tori, with

(11)

n\mathbf{w}\bmod 1\in H_{n\bmod c}\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

It follows, again via Weyl’s criterion, that

\lparen n\mathbf{w}\bmod 1\rparen_{n\equiv 0\pmod{c}}
is equidistributed in

H_{0}
with respect to normalized Haar measure

\mu_{0}:=\mu_{H_{0}}
. Consequently,

\lparen n\mathbf{w}\bmod 1\rparen_{n\equiv a\pmod{c}}
is equidistributed in

H_{a}
with respect to the pushforward measure

\mu_{a}=T_{*}^{a}\mu_{H_{0}},

where

is the mapping
\mathbf{x}\mapsto\mathbf{x}+a\mathbf{w}\pmod{1}
.

along the prime

Proposition 4.3

Let

Proof.

The claim is equivalent to

Monotonicity of Pairs of Operators and Generalized Inertial Proximal Method
Ba Khiet Le, Zakaria Mazgouri, and Michel Théra

Abstract
Monotonicity of pairs of operators is an extension of monotonicity of operators, which plays an important role in solving non-monotone inclusions. One of challenging problems in this new tool is how to design the associated mappings to obtain the monotone pairs. In this paper, we solve this problem and propose a Generalized Inertial Proximal Point Algorithm ( 𝐆𝐈𝐏𝐏𝐀 ) (\mathbf{GIPPA}) using warped resolvents under the monotonicity of pairs. The weak, strong and linear convergence of the algorithm under some mild assumptions are established. We also provide numerical examples illustrating the implementability and effectiveness of the proposed method.



Keywords:

Monotonicity of pairs of operators, Non-monotone inclusions,
Warped resolvents,
Inertial proximal point algorithm.

49J52, 49J53.

Introduction

The notion of
monotonicity of pairs of operators
was introduced in
[
1
]
to address the inclusion problem

0\in F(x),

(1)

where
F:\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
is a (possibly non-monotone) set-valued operator acting on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
The key idea is that, instead of computing the inverse
F^{-1}
, which is often difficult or impossible, one may solve the inclusion (
1
) by constructing an auxiliary mapping
v
such that
v^{-1}
is easy to compute and the pair
(F,v)
is monotone, meaning that
F
and
v
share compatible monotonicity properties.
When such a mapping
v
is available and
F
is single-valued, a forward algorithm
[
1
]
can be employed to solve (
1
). If
F
is possibly set-valued, a Generalized Proximal Point Algorithm
(\mathbf{GPPA})
can be used
[
8
]
. In this setting,
v
need not be invertible; it suffices that the operator
\gamma F+v
be invertible for some
\gamma>0
. Despite these advances, the problem of constructing a suitable mapping
v
for a given operator
F
remains open and challenging in general.
In this paper, our first contribution is to address this fundamental issue. We begin with the quadratic programming setting, where
F=A
is a non-invertible matrix. In this case, we show how to construct a matrix
B
such that the pair
(A,B)
is monotone. This construction can be achieved either through a slight modification of
A
(Theorem
3.1
) or via a general diagonal decomposition (Theorem
3.5
).

When
F=f
is single-valued and nonlinear, its general form naturally motivates viewing
v
as a piecewise linear approximation of
f
in order to obtain local convergence. For instance, if
f
is differentiable and
x_{k}
is a current iterate, one may take
v
as the first-order Taylor approximation of
f
at
x_{k}
, namely

v(x)=f(x_{k})+f^{\prime}(x_{k})(x-x_{k}).

Since the term
f(x_{k})-f^{\prime}(x_{k})x_{k}
is constant, it may be neglected, leading to the simplified choice
v(x)=f^{\prime}(x_{k})x
, with inverse
v^{-1}(x)=(f^{\prime}(x_{k}))^{-1}x
whenever
f^{\prime}(x_{k})
is invertible. In this case, the algorithm proposed in
[
1
]
,

x_{k+1}=x_{k}-hv^{-1}f(x_{k}),\quad x_{0}\in\mathcal{H},\ h>0,

(2)

reduces to

x_{k+1}=x_{k}-h\,(f^{\prime}(x_{k}))^{-1}f(x_{k}),

which is precisely the classical Newton method.

However, if
f^{\prime}(x_{k})
is non-invertible for some
k
, the inverse
(f^{\prime}(x_{k}))^{-1}
is no longer available. To overcome this difficulty, we instead fix
v=f^{\prime}(x^{*})
at a point
x^{*}
where
f^{\prime}(x^{*})
is invertible and for which a solution
\tilde{x}
exists in a neighborhood of
x^{*}
. This leads naturally to a quasi-Newton–type algorithm. Using the framework of monotonicity of pairs of operators, we establish its local linear convergence (Theorem
3.7
).
These results support the viewpoint advocated by R. T. Rockafellar
[
12
,
14
,
15
]
that so-called
hidden monotonicity
or
hidden convexity
can play a significant role in the analysis and design of algorithms for nonconvex optimization problems.

Our second contribution is the introduction of a
Generalized Inertial Proximal Point Algorithm

(\mathbf{GIPPA})
, which extends the Generalized Proximal Point Algorithm
(\mathbf{GPPA})
proposed in
[
8
]
. Motivated by recent advances on inertial methods for monotone bilevel equilibrium problems
[
4
,
5
]
, we develop a generalized inertial proximal scheme for solving (
1
), based on warped resolvents and the framework of monotonicity of pairs of operators.
Inertial proximal algorithms, in which each new iterate depends on the two preceding ones, are widely recognized as discrete analogues of second-order dynamical systems. The incorporation of inertial terms often leads to improved convergence behavior of the generated sequence. From a historical perspective, such methods can be traced back to
[
2
]
, where they were introduced for solving (
1
) in the case where
F
is a
maximally monotone
operator and the solution set
F^{-1}(0)
is assumed to be nonempty.

Here is our proposed inertial scheme.

\mathbf{GIPPA}
(Generalized Inertial Proximal Point Algorithm)

Initialization:
Choose sequences
\{\gamma_{n}\}\subset(0,+\infty)
and
\{\alpha_{n}\}\subset[0,1]
. Take arbitrary
x_{0},x_{1}\in\mathcal{H}
.

Iterative steps:

•

Step 1.
For every
n\geq 1
, given the current iterates
x_{n-1},x_{n}\in\mathcal{H}
, set

y_{n}:=x_{n}+\alpha_{n}(x_{n}-x_{n-1}),

and define

x_{n+1}:=J_{\gamma_{n}F}^{v}(y_{n}),

where
J_{\gamma_{n}F}^{v}:=(\gamma_{n}F+v)^{-1}\circ v
denotes the
warped resolvent
of
\gamma_{n}F
with kernel
v

[
9
]
, and
v
is linear such that the pair
(F,v)
is monotone.

•

Step 2.
If
x_{n+1}=y_{n}
, then stop; in this case,
x_{n+1}
is a solution of (
1
). Otherwise, set
n:=n+1
and return to Step 1.

The warped resolvent introduced in
[
9
]
extends both the classical resolvent and the
D
-resolvent
[
6
]
, and provides a powerful tool for the study of monotone inclusion problems. The monotonicity of pairs of operators guarantees that the warped resolvent is well-defined (see Remark
2.3
) and enables the convergence analysis of algorithms designed to solve non-monotone inclusions
[
8
]
.

The proposed scheme, referred to as the
Generalized Inertial Proximal Point Algorithm

(\mathbf{GIPPA})
, reduces to the Generalized Proximal Point Algorithm
(\mathbf{GPPA})
of
[
8
]
when
\alpha_{n}=0
for all
n\geq 0
. Furthermore, when the kernel operator is chosen as the identity mapping, that is,
v=\operatorname{Id}
, the method simplifies to the classical Proximal Point Algorithm
(\mathbf{PPA})
; see, for example,
[
13
]
. Under mild and natural assumptions, we establish weak, strong, and linear convergence results for the proposed algorithm
(\mathbf{GIPPA})
.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the notation and reviews the necessary background material. Section 3 is devoted to the construction of associated mappings that ensure the monotonicity of pairs of operators. In Section 4, we present a detailed convergence analysis of the algorithm
(\mathbf{GIPPA})
. Section 5 reports two numerical experiments that illustrate the implementability and effectiveness of the proposed method in comparison with
(\mathbf{GPPA})
. Finally, Section 6 concludes the paper with a summary of the main contributions and a discussion of potential directions for future research.

Mathematical Background and Notations

Throughout this work,
\mathcal{H}
denotes a real Hilbert space equipped with the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and the associated norm
\|\cdot\|
. Let
F:\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
be a set-valued operator. The
domain
,
range
,
graph
,
set of zeros
, and
set of fixed points
of
F
are defined, respectively, as:

\displaystyle\operatorname{dom}F

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathcal{H}:F(x)\neq\varnothing\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{ran}F

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{x\in\mathcal{H}}F(x),

\displaystyle\operatorname{gra}F

\displaystyle=\{(x,y):x\in\mathcal{H},y\in F(x)\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{zer}F

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathcal{H}:0\in F(x)\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Fix}F

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathcal{H}:x\in F(x)\}.

The
inverse
of
F
is defined by
F^{-1}(y)=\{x\in\mathcal{H}:y\in F(x)\}
. It is easy to see that
\operatorname{dom}F=\operatorname{ran}F^{-1}
and
\operatorname{ran}F=\operatorname{dom}F^{-1}
.

The set-valued mapping
F
is said to be
monotone
if for all
(x,x^{*}),(y,y^{*})\in\operatorname{gra}F
,

\langle x^{*}-y^{*},x-y\rangle\geq 0,

and
\alpha
-strongly monotone
if
\alpha\in(0,+\infty)
and for all
(x,x^{*}),(y,y^{*})\in\operatorname{gra}F
,

\langle x^{*}-y^{*},x-y\rangle\geq\alpha\|x-y\|^{2}.

Next, we recall the definition of
monotonicity of pairs
introduced in
[
1
]
and used in
[
8
]
, which generalizes classical monotonicity. Given set-valued mappings
F_{1},F_{2}:\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
, we define:

•

The pair
(F_{1},F_{2})
is
monotone
if for all
x,y\in\mathcal{H}
,

\langle F_{1}(x)-F_{1}(y),F_{2}(x)-F_{2}(y)\rangle\geq 0,

i.e., for all
x,y\in\mathcal{H}
,
x_{1}^{*}\in F_{1}(x)
,
y_{1}^{*}\in F_{1}(y)
,
x_{2}^{*}\in F_{2}(x)
,
y_{2}^{*}\in F_{2}(y)
,

\langle x_{1}^{*}-y_{1}^{*},x_{2}^{*}-y_{2}^{*}\rangle\geq 0.

•

The pair
(F_{1},F_{2})
is
\alpha
-strongly monotone
if
\alpha\in(0,+\infty)
and for all
x,y\in\mathcal{H}
,

\langle F_{1}(x)-F_{1}(y),F_{2}(x)-F_{2}(y)\rangle\geq\alpha\|x-y\|^{2},

(3)

i.e., for all
x,y\in\mathcal{H}
,
x_{1}^{*}\in F_{1}(x)
,
y_{1}^{*}\in F_{1}(y)
,
x_{2}^{*}\in F_{2}(x)
,
y_{2}^{*}\in F_{2}(y)
,

\langle x_{1}^{*}-y_{1}^{*},x_{2}^{*}-y_{2}^{*}\rangle\geq\alpha\|x-y\|^{2}.

Remark 2.1
.

(i)

It is straightforward to observe that if
F
is monotone (respectively, strongly monotone), then the pair

(F,\operatorname{Id})
is also monotone (respectively, strongly monotone).

(ii)

The concept of monotonicity for operator pairs naturally arises in problems with block structures, where such formulations often lead to meaningful adaptations, as illustrated in
[
8
, Examples 2.1 and 2.2]
.

Definition 2.2
.

[
9
]

Let
F:\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
and
v:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
with
\operatorname{dom}v=\mathcal{H}
.
The
warped resolvent
of
F
with kernel
v
is defined as

J_{F}^{v}=(F+v)^{-1}\circ v,

provided that
F+v
is injective and
\operatorname{ran}v\subseteq\operatorname{ran}(F+v)
.

Remark 2.3
.

We show that the monotonicity of the pair
(F,v)
, together with the Lipschitz continuity of
v^{-1}
, ensures that the warped resolvent
J_{\gamma F}^{v}
is well-defined for every
\gamma>0
.
Assume that
(F,v)
is monotone and that
v^{-1}
is Lipschitz continuous. Then, for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathcal{H}
,

\bigl\langle\gamma\bigl(F(x_{1})-F(x_{2})\bigr)+v(x_{1})-v(x_{2}),\,v(x_{1})-v(x_{2})\bigr\rangle\geq\|v(x_{1})-v(x_{2})\|^{2}.

Since
v^{-1}
is Lipschitz continuous, there exists
\alpha>0
such that

\|v(x_{1})-v(x_{2})\|\geq\alpha\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|.

As a consequence, the pair
(\gamma F+v,v)
is strongly monotone, which implies that
(\gamma F+v)^{-1}
is single-valued and can be everywhere defined; see
[
1
, Theorem 6]
.

We emphasize that the warped resolvent
J_{\gamma F}^{v}
remains well-defined as long as
(\gamma F+v)^{-1}
exists, even when
v
is not invertible. In this more general setting, it is precisely the monotonicity of the pair
(F,v)
that guarantees the convergence of the associated algorithms.

Strong and weak convergence of sequences are denoted by
\to
and
\rightharpoonup
, respectively. A single-valued mapping
F:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is said to be
weakly continuous
if it is continuous with respect to the weak topology of
\mathcal{H}
; that is, for every sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{H}
such that
x_{n}\rightharpoonup x
, one has
F(x_{n})\rightharpoonup F(x)
.

A set-valued mapping
F:\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
is said to have a
strongly–weakly closed graph
if, for every sequence
(x_{n},y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\operatorname{gra}F
with
x_{n}\rightharpoonup x
and
y_{n}\to y
, it holds that
(x,y)\in\operatorname{gra}F
, that is,
y\in F(x)
. Moreover, we say that
F
has a
closed graph at a point

\bar{x}\in\operatorname{dom}F
if, for every sequence
(x_{n},y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\operatorname{gra}F
satisfying
x_{n}\to\bar{x}
and
y_{n}\to y
, one has
(\bar{x},y)\in\operatorname{gra}F
.

On the other hand, we recall the notion of
R
-continuity
, introduced in
[
7
,
10
]
. Let
F:\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
be a set-valued mapping and let
\bar{x}\in\operatorname{dom}F
.

Definition 2.4
.

The set-valued mapping
F:\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
is said to be
R
-continuous
at
\bar{x}
if there exist
\sigma>0
and a nondecreasing function
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfying

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\rho(r)=\rho(0)=0

such that

F(x)\subset F(\bar{x})+\rho\bigl(\|x-\bar{x}\|\bigr)\mathbb{B},\qquad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{x},\sigma).

Here
\mathbb{B}
denotes the closed unit ball, while
\mathbb{B}(\bar{x},\sigma)
denotes the closed ball with center
\bar{x}
and radius
\sigma>0
.
The function
\rho
is called a
continuity modulus
of
F
at
\bar{x}
, and
\sigma
is referred to as the
radius
.

We note that
R
-continuity is satisfied by a broad class of operators. In particular, if
F
has a closed graph and is locally compact at
\bar{x}
, then
F
is
R
-continuous at
\bar{x}
; see
[
10
, Theorem 3.2]
.

We also require the following technical lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

For all
x,y\in\mathcal{H}
and all
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, the following identity holds:

\|\beta x+(1-\beta)y\|^{2}=\beta\|x\|^{2}+(1-\beta)\|y\|^{2}-\beta(1-\beta)\|x-y\|^{2}.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\{b_{k}\}_{k\geq 0},\{w_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
and
\{\theta_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
be sequences of nonnegative numbers, and there exists a real number
\theta
with
0\leq\theta_{k}\leq\theta\leq 1,
such that for all
k\geq 0
,

b_{k+1}\leq\theta_{k}b_{k}+w_{k}

with

\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}w_{k}<+\infty
, then
\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}b_{k}
exists. Further, if
\theta<1
then
\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}b_{k}<+\infty
.

Since
b_{k}\geq 0
for all
k
and
\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}w_{k}<+\infty,
then the sequence
\{b_{k+1}-\sum_{i=0}^{k}w_{i}\}
is bounded from below.
We also have

b_{k+1}-\sum_{i=0}^{k}w_{i}\leq\theta_{k}b_{k}-\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}w_{i}\leq b_{k}-\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}w_{i},

which implies that the sequence
\{b_{k+1}-\sum_{i=0}^{k}w_{i}\}
is nonincreasing, hence convergent. It follows that
\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}b_{k}
exists.

Observe that

(1-\theta_{k})b_{k}\leq b_{k}-b_{k+1}+w_{k}.

Summing up from
k=0
to
n
, we get

\displaystyle(1-\theta)\sum_{k=0}^{n}b_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n}(b_{k}-b_{k+1})+\sum_{k=0}^{n}w_{k}

\displaystyle=b_{0}-b_{n+1}+\sum_{k=0}^{n}w_{k}

\displaystyle\leq b_{0}+\sum_{k=0}^{n}w_{k}.

Since
\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}w_{k}<+\infty
, if
\theta<1
, we conclude that
\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}b_{k}<+\infty
.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
0\leq\alpha<1
, and let
\{\alpha_{k}\},

\{a_{k}\},

\{\Delta_{k}\}
and
\{w_{k}\}
be sequences of nonnegative numbers such that
\{\alpha_{k}\}
is nondecreasing,
\left\{\alpha_{k}\right\}\subseteq[0,\alpha]
and for all
k\geq 1
,

a_{k+1}\leq(\alpha_{k}+1)a_{k}-\alpha_{k-1}a_{k-1}-\Delta_{k}+w_{k}.

(4)

If
\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}w_{k}<+\infty
, then

i)

a_{k}
is bounded;

ii)

\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\Delta_{k}<+\infty
;

iii)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}a_{k}
exists.

i)

By summing up the inequality (
4
) from
k=1
to
n
, we obtain

a_{n+1}-a_{1}\leq\left(\alpha_{n}a_{n}-\alpha_{0}a_{0}\right)-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Delta_{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}w_{k},

which implies that

a_{n+1}\leq\alpha a_{n}+a_{1}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}w_{k}.

Note that
\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}w_{k}<+\infty
we conclude that

\{a_{n}\}
is bounded by some
C>0
.

ii)
We have

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Delta_{k}\leq a_{1}+\alpha a_{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}w_{k}.

Since
\{a_{n}\}
is bounded and again
\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}w_{k}<+\infty
, then
\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\Delta_{k}<+\infty.

iii)
From (

\displaystyle a_{k+1}-a_{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}a_{k}-\alpha_{k-1}a_{k-1}+w_{k}=(\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{k-1})a_{k}+\alpha_{k-1}(a_{k}-a_{k-1})+w_{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha[a_{k}-a_{k-1}]_{+}+(\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{k-1})C+w_{k}.

Thus

[a_{k+1}-a_{k}]_{+}\leq\alpha[a_{k}-a_{k-1}]_{+}+(\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{k-1})C+w_{k}.

Using the fact that
\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}((\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{k-1})C+w_{k})\leq\alpha C+\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}w_{k}<+\infty
and applying Lemma

\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}[a_{k}-a_{k-1}]_{+}<+\infty.

For any

, we can write

Split each term into its positive and negative parts

Hence,

Now, since
\{a_{n}\}
is nonnegative we imply that

Thus the series
\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}[a_{n}-a_{n-1}]_{+}

∎

We conclude this section by recalling the classical weak convergence result due to Opial.

Lemma 2.8
(Opial’s Lemma
[
11
]
).

Let
S
be a nonempty subset of
\mathcal{H}
, and let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{H}
. Suppose that:

(i)

For every

(ii)

3
Constructing Associated Mappings to Obtain Monotone Pairs

3.1
Global Monotonicity

We first consider a simple yet fundamental setting arising in quadratic programming, where
F=A
is a non-invertible matrix. The central question is how to construct a matrix
B
such that the pair
(A,B)
is monotone, or equivalently, such that the operator
A^{\top}B
is monotone. In addition, it is desirable that
B
be invertible, or at least that
\gamma A+B
be invertible for some
\gamma>0
.

We begin by presenting a construction of
B
based on a slight modification of the matrix
A
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
A
be a non-invertible matrix. Suppose that
B=A+A_{1}
, where
A_{1}
is chosen so that
A_{1}^{\top}A
is monotone. Then the pair
(A,B)
is monotone.

For any
x
, we compute

\langle Ax,Bx\rangle=\langle Ax,(A+A_{1})x\rangle=\|Ax\|^{2}+\langle A_{1}^{\top}Ax,x\rangle\geq\|Ax\|^{2}\geq 0,

where the inequality follows from the monotonicity of
A_{1}^{\top}A
. This proves that the pair
(A,B)
is monotone.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

For example if

A=\begin{bmatrix}1&2&3\\
4&5&6\\
7&8&9\end{bmatrix},\;{\rm we\;can\;choose}\;B_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{bmatrix},B_{2}=A+B_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}2&2&3\\
4&5&6\\
7&8&9\end{bmatrix}.

Then
(A,B_{1})
and
(A,B_{2})
are monotone where
B_{2}
is invertible.

The second approach is based on a diagonal decomposition.

Proposition 3.3
.

Suppose that
A
is a symmetric non-invertible matrix. Then we can decompose

A=ODO^{\top},

where
O
is orthogonal and
D
is diagonal with some zero entries on the main diagonal. Let
D^{\prime}
be the diagonal matrix obtained from
D
by replacing the zero entries with
1
. Let

B=OD^{\prime}O^{\top}.

Then
B
is invertible and
(A,B)
is monotone.

The existence of such a decomposition is classical. Then we have

\displaystyle AB^{\top}

\displaystyle=ODO^{\top}(OD^{\prime}O^{\top})^{\top}

\displaystyle=ODO^{\top}OD^{\prime}O^{\top}

\displaystyle=ODD^{\prime}O^{\top},

which is monotone.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

The choice of
D^{\prime}
can be flexible to ensure the monotonicity of
DD^{\prime}
. We can also easily choose
D^{\prime}
which is non-invertible. In addition, the orthogonal decomposition of
A
can be extended as follows.

Theorem 3.5
.

Suppose that

A=CDE,

where
C,E
are invertible matrices and
D
is diagonal with some zero entries on the main diagonal. Let
D^{\prime}
be the diagonal matrix obtained from
D
by replacing the zero entries with
1
. Let

B=(C^{-1})^{\top}D^{\prime}E.

Then
B
is invertible and
(A,B)
is monotone.

It is obvious that
B
is invertible since
C,D^{\prime},E
are invertible. We have

\displaystyle A^{\top}B

\displaystyle=(CDE)^{\top}(C^{\top})^{-1}D^{\prime}E

\displaystyle=E^{\top}DC^{\top}(C^{\top})^{-1}D^{\prime}E

\displaystyle=E^{\top}DD^{\prime}E,

which is monotone.
∎

3.2
Local Strong Monotonicity and Quasi-Newton’s Algorithm

In this section, we establish local strong monotonicity of pairs of operators as a key ingredient for proving local linear convergence.
Assume that the equation
f(x)=0
admits a solution
\tilde{x}
in a neighborhood of a fixed point
x^{*}
, and that
f
is
C^{1}
in this neighborhood with
f^{\prime}(x^{*})
invertible.
The Taylor expansion of
f

yields

f(x)=f(x^{*})+f^{\prime}(x^{*})(x-x^{*})+o(\|x-x^{*}\|).

We choose
v
as the linear part of

at
x^{*}
, namely

v(x):=f^{\prime}(x^{*})x,

since the constant term
f(x^{*})-f^{\prime}(x^{*})x^{*}
plays no role in monotonicity arguments.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
v(x)=f^{\prime}(x^{*})x
. Then the pair
(f,v)
is locally strongly monotone, i.e., there exist constants
\varepsilon>0
and
\alpha>0
such that for all

\langle f(x)-f(y),\,v(x)-v(y)\rangle\geq\alpha\|x-y\|^{2}.

Fix
x,y\in B(x^{*},\varepsilon)
where
\varepsilon>0
can be chosen later.
By the Mean Value Theorem, there exists
\xi\in[x,y]
such that

f(x)-f(y)=f^{\prime}(\xi)(x-y).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\langle f(x)-f(y),\,v(x)-v(y)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle f^{\prime}(\xi)(x-y),\,f^{\prime}(x^{*})(x-y)\rangle

\displaystyle=\|f^{\prime}(x^{*})(x-y)\|^{2}+\langle(f^{\prime}(\xi)-f^{\prime}(x^{*}))(x-y),\,f^{\prime}(x^{*})(x-y)\rangle.

Since
f^{\prime}(x^{*})
is invertible, there exists
c>0
such that

\|f^{\prime}(x^{*})(x-y)\|\geq c\|x-y\|.

Moreover, by continuity of
f^{\prime}
, for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small,

{\|f^{\prime}(x^{*})\|}\|f^{\prime}(\xi)-f^{\prime}(x^{*})\|\leq\frac{c}{2}.

Combining these estimates yields

\langle f(x)-f(y),\,v(x)-v(y)\rangle\geq\frac{c^{2}}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}.

Setting
\alpha:=c^{2}/2
completes the proof.
∎Note that Newton’s method is a special case of Algorithm (
2
), with

v_{k}(x)=f^{\prime}(x_{k})x.

However, the method may fail to be well-defined if there exists an iterate
x_{k}
such that

is not invertible. To circumvent this difficulty, one may replace

with a fixed invertible operator
f^{\prime}(x^{*})
, where
x^{*}
is a reference point.
Setting

v(x):=f^{\prime}(x^{*})x.

By Theorem
3.6
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that the pair
(f,v)
is strongly monotone on
B(x^{*},\varepsilon)
; that is, there exists
\alpha>0
such that

\langle f(x)-f(y),\,v(x)-v(y)\rangle\geq\alpha\|x-y\|^{2},

(5)

for all
x,y\in B(x^{*},\varepsilon)

Consequently, there exists

such that

Note that

is a neighborhood of
x^{*}
.
We consider the quasi-Newton iteration

x_{k+1}=x_{k}-h\,(f^{\prime}(x^{*}))^{-1}f(x_{k}),\qquad x_{0}\in W,\quad h>0.

(6)

Theorem 3.7
.

Assume that the equation
f(x)=0
admits a solution
\tilde{x}
in

Then

is the unique solution in

converges linearly to

\tilde{x}
in

follows directly from the strong monotonicity of the pair
(f,v)
on
B(x^{*},\varepsilon)
.
Define

Then the iteration (

Clearly,
x_{0}\in W
.
Assume that

.
By (

), we have

\langle y_{k}-\tilde{y},\,f(x_{k})-f(\tilde{x})\rangle\geq\alpha\|x_{k}-\tilde{x}\|^{2}.

Let

denote the Lipschitz constant of

Choosing

\displaystyle\|y_{k+1}-\tilde{y}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|y_{k}-\tilde{y}-h(f(x_{k})-f(\tilde{x}))\|^{2}

Consequently,

4
Generalized Inertial Proximal Point Algorithm (GIPPA) and Convergence Analysis

In this section, we analyze the convergence of algorithm

using warped resolvents under the monotonicity of pairs of operators, to address the non-monotone inclusion (

Assumption 1
: There exists a linear mapping

is monotone,
\gamma_{n}F+v

Assumption 1’

\beta
-strongly monotone and

As a preliminary step toward proving the convergence of the algorithm

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
\operatorname{zer}F\neq\varnothing
and Assumption 1 holds. Let
x^{*}\in\operatorname{zer}F
and set
a_{n}:=\|v(x_{n})-v(x^{*})\|^{2}
. Then, for each
n\geq 1
the following inequality holds:

\begin{array}[]{l}a_{n+1}-a_{n}-{\alpha_{n}}(a_{n}-a_{n-1})\\
\leq(\alpha_{n}-1)\|v(x_{n+1})-v(x_{n})\|^{2}+2\alpha_{n}\|v(x_{n})-v(x_{n-1})\|^{2}.\end{array}

(7)

By
[
8
, Proposition 3.1]

,
x^{*}\in\operatorname{Fix}J_{\gamma_{n}F}^{v}
, i.e.,
(x^{*},x^{*})\in\operatorname{gra}J_{\gamma_{n}F}^{v}
. Since
(y_{n},x_{n+1})\in\operatorname{gra}J_{\gamma_{n}F}^{v}

, which yields

v(y_{n})-v(x_{n+1})\in\gamma_{n}F(x_{n+1}).

(8)

Similarly,
(x^{*},x^{*})\in\operatorname{gra}J_{\gamma_{n}F}^{v}
implies

v(x^{*})-v(x^{*})\in\gamma_{n}F(x^{*}).

Since the monotonicity of
(F,v)
, we have

\langle v(y_{n})-v(x_{n+1})-v(x^{*})+v(x^{*}),v(x_{n+1})-v(x^{*})\rangle\geq 0,

or equivalently,

\displaystyle\|v(x_{n+1})-v(x^{*})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\langle v(y_{n})-v(x^{*}),v(x_{n+1})-v(x^{*})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\|v(y_{n})-v(x^{*})\|^{2}+\|v(x_{n+1})-v(x^{*})\|^{2}-\|v(y_{n})-v(x_{n+1})\|^{2}),

\displaystyle\|v(x_{n+1})-v(x^{*})\|^{2}\leq\|v(y_{n})-v(x^{*})\|^{2}-\|v(y_{n})-v(x_{n+1})\|^{2}.

(9)

On the other hand, since

2.5

\displaystyle\|v(y_{n})-v(x^{*})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|v(x_{n}+\alpha_{n}(x_{n}-x_{n-1}))-v(x^{*})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=(1+\alpha_{n})\|v(x_{n})-v(x^{*})\|^{2}-\alpha_{n}\|v(x_{n-1})-v(x^{*})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha_{n}(1+\alpha_{n})\|v(x_{n})-v(x_{n-1})\|^{2}.

(10)

Also, we have

\begin{array}[]{lll}\|v(x_{n+1})-v(y_{n})\|^{2}&=&\|v(x_{n+1})-v(x_{n})-\alpha_{n}(v(x_{n})-v(x_{n-1}))\|^{2}\\
&=&\|v(x_{n+1})-v(x_{n})\|^{2}+\alpha_{n}^{2}\|v(x_{n})-v(x_{n-1})\|^{2}\\
&&-2\alpha_{n}\langle v(x_{n+1})-v(x_{n}),v(x_{n})-v(x_{n-1})\rangle\\
&\geq&(1-\alpha_{n})\|v(x_{n+1})-v(x_{n})\|^{2}+(\alpha_{n}^{2}-\alpha_{n})\|v(x_{n})-v(x_{n-1})\|^{2}.\end{array}

(11)

Combining (
4

11

9

\begin{array}[]{l}\|v(x_{n+1})-v(x^{*})\|^{2}\\
\leq(1+\alpha_{n})\|v(x_{n})-v(x^{*})\|^{2}-\alpha_{n}\|v(x_{n-1})-v(x^{*})\|^{2}+\alpha_{n}(1+\alpha_{n})\|v(x_{n})-v(x_{n-1})\|^{2}\\

(12)

Finally, by setting

\displaystyle\leq(1+5\alpha_{n})\|v(x_{n})-v(x^{*})\|^{2}+3\alpha_{n}\|v(x_{n-1})-v(x^{*})\|^{2}.

It follows that

\|v(x_{n+1})-v(x^{*})\|^{2}\leq\kappa^{2}(1+5\alpha)\|v(x_{n})-v(x^{*})\|^{2}+3\kappa^{2}\alpha\|v(x_{n-1})-v(x^{*})\|^{2}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\|v(x_{n+1})-v(x^{*})\|^{2}+\tilde{\alpha}\|v(x_{n})-v(x^{*})\|^{2}\leq\rho^{2}\bigl(\|v(x_{n})-v(x^{*})\|^{2}+\tilde{\alpha}\|v(x_{n-1})-v(x^{*})\|^{2}\bigr),

where the parameters
\alpha,\tilde{\alpha}>0
are chosen such that

\rho:=\sqrt{\kappa^{2}(1+5\alpha)+\tilde{\alpha}}<1,\qquad\frac{3\kappa^{2}\alpha}{\kappa^{2}(1+5\alpha)+\tilde{\alpha}}\leq\tilde{\alpha}.

For instance, one may take
\tilde{\alpha}=(1-\kappa^{2})/2
and choose

\alpha>0
sufficiently small.

By induction, we obtain

\displaystyle\|v(x_{n+1})-v(x^{*})\|^{2}+\tilde{\alpha}\|v(x_{n})-v(x^{*})\|^{2}\leq\rho^{2n}\bigl(\|v(x_{1})-v(x^{*})\|^{2}+\tilde{\alpha}\|v(x_{0})-v(x^{*})\|^{2}\bigr),

which implies

\|v(x_{n+1})-v(x^{*})\|\leq\rho^{n}\sqrt{\|v(x_{1})-v(x^{*})\|^{2}+\tilde{\alpha}\|v(x_{0})-v(x^{*})\|^{2}}.

This establishes the claimed linear convergence and completes the proof.

(iv)(b)

If
v
is bijective and linear then
v^{-1}
is
\ell
-Lipschitz continuous for some
\ell>0
. Then

\displaystyle\|x_{n}-x^{*}\|

\displaystyle\leq\ell\|v(x_{n+1})-v(x^{*})\|

\displaystyle\leq\ell\rho^{n}\sqrt{\|v(x_{1})-v(x^{*})\|^{2}+\tilde{\alpha}\|v(x_{0})-v(x^{*})\|^{2}}.

Thus,
(x_{n})
converges linearly to
x^{*}
, which completes the proof.

∎

Remark 4.4
.

The properties stated in
(i)
and
(ii)
are reminiscent of those of the
\mathbf{(DCA)}
(Difference of Convex Algorithm) in nonconvex programming; see
[
16
]
. While DCA is fundamentally based on a
difference
of convex functions, the algorithms proposed here rely instead on a
product
structure, namely the monotonicity of pairs of operators.

Moreover, when
\alpha_{n}=0
, that is, when the
\mathbf{(GIPPA)}
reduces to the
\mathbf{(GPPA)}
, these properties continue to hold without requiring the mapping
v
to be linear.

6
Conclusion and Perspectives

In this paper, we have addressed the question of how to construct the associated mappings so that the resulting pairs are monotone and proposed an inertial proximal-type algorithm based on warped resolvents for solving non-monotone inclusions, leveraging the monotonicity of pairs of operators. Under mild assumptions, we have established weak, strong, and linear convergence of the method, without imposing restrictive conditions on the trajectories. Numerical examples further demonstrate the algorithm’s effectiveness in comparison with the method in
[
8
]
.

Looking ahead, we believe that the framework of monotone operator pairs opens up a promising and largely unexplored research direction. In particular, developing algorithms for problems involving the sum of two operators–where one or both may fail to be monotone–could significantly broaden the scope of resolvent-based techniques. This includes designing new splitting schemes and operator-splitting strategies that strategically exploit pair monotonicity to secure robust convergence guarantees. Continued exploration of these ideas has the potential to reshape algorithmic design for non-monotone problems, extend the reach of monotone operator theory, and stimulate further advances in large-scale optimization and variational analysis.

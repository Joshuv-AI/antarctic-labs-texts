A Minimal Non-Hausdorff Counterexample in Covering–Space Theory
Abhiram Sripat

Abstract




Abstract.

Let
k\geq 2
and consider the
line with
k
inseparable origins

\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}\;=\;\Bigl(\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{R}_{i}\Bigr)\big/\!\sim,\qquad(x,i)\sim(x,j)\ \text{for }x\neq 0,

whose origins
o_{1},\dots,o_{k}
are
T_{1}
-separated yet Hausdorff-inseparable.
This space is a 1-manifold, second-countable, and
simply connected

(
\pi_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp})=0
).

Embed an injective map
\iota:\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\hookrightarrow D\setminus\{z\}

into the interior of a closed disk
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and define

\Gamma=\iota(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}),\qquad Y=\Gamma\cup\{z\},

with
Y
carrying the subspace topology from
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Define the projection

\pi:\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}\longrightarrow Y,\qquad\pi([x]_{i})=\begin{cases}\iota(x),&x\neq 0,\\
z,&x=0.\end{cases}

(1)

Regular locus.

Over
Y\setminus\{z\}
, the restriction

\pi|_{\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}\setminus\pi^{-1}(z)}
is a

1-sheeted
local homeomorphism onto
\Gamma
.

(2)

Singular fibre.

The fibre
\pi^{-1}(z)=\{o_{1},\dots,o_{k}\}
is non-Hausdorff:
every neighbourhood of one origin contains all others. Consequently:

•

path- and homotopy-lifting fail,

•

the monodromy action is
undefined
,

•

the deck group satisfies
\Deck(\pi)\cong\mathfrak{S}_{k}
.

(3)

Algebraic fallout.

Although
\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}
has trivial fundamental group,
none of the usual classification frameworks—
branched covers, étale maps, or semicoverings—
can accommodate
\pi
.

This furnishes a dimension-minimal example showing that the loss of
Hausdorff separation at a
single
fibre suffices to destroy
path-lifting, monodromy, and the deck-group correspondence in
covering-space theory. The construction offers a sharp boundary case
for any future extension of Galois theory to non-Hausdorff spaces.

Keywords:
non-Hausdorff topology, covering space theory, path lifting, monodromy, semicovering, branched cover

1
Introduction

Classical covering–space theory begins with a well-behaved base:
a connected, locally path-connected,
Hausdorff
space
X

that is semilocally simply connected. Under these assumptions,
there is a bijection

\left\{\text{connected covering projections }p:\widetilde{X}\to X\right\}/\!\cong\;\longleftrightarrow\;\left\{\text{conjugacy classes of subgroups of }\pi_{1}(X)\right\},

as in
(
Hatcher 2002
, Chap. 1)
and
(
Spanier 1966
, Chap. 2)
.
This correspondence depends on three interlocking pillars:

•

Unique path and homotopy lifting,

•

Discrete fibres
admitting a properly discontinuous deck action,

•

A well-defined
monodromy representation

\pi_{1}(X)\curvearrowright
fibre.

All three are guaranteed when the total space
\widetilde{X}
is Hausdorff.
But in modern geometry, non-Hausdorff spaces abound:
quotients by non-closed relations, non-separated schemes,
or moduli stacks with glued strata.
Strikingly few examples show
precisely
how and where
Hausdorffness is essential.

Aim.

This paper constructs a minimal, one-dimensional, non-Hausdorff
counterexample that breaks all three pillars simultaneously.
Let
k\geq 2
and consider the topological space

\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}:=\left(\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{R}_{i}\right)\Big/\!\sim,\qquad(x,i)\sim(x,j)\text{ for }x\neq 0,

known classically as the “line with
k
origins”, but here modified
to emphasise
inseparability
of origins (see §
2
).
In our variant, the points
o_{1},\dots,o_{k}
satisfy:

•

each singleton
\{o_{i}\}
is closed, so
\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}
is
T_{1}
,

•

no pair
o_{i},o_{j}
can be separated by disjoint opens: the space is non-Hausdorff,

•

all origins lie in the same neighbourhood filter (inseparable),

•

yet
\pi_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp})=0
(Theorem
2.3
).

Embed an injective map

\iota:\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\hookrightarrow D\setminus\{z\}
into the interior
of a closed disk
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and define

\Gamma:=\iota(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}),\qquad Y:=\Gamma\cup\{z\}\subset D,

endowed with the subspace topology from
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
We study the surjection

\pi:\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}\longrightarrow Y,\qquad\pi([x]_{i})=\begin{cases}\iota(x)&x\neq 0,\\
z&x=0.\end{cases}

•

Regular locus.

Over
Y\setminus\{z\}
, the map
\pi
is a
1-sheeted
local homeomorphism.

•

Singular behaviour.

The fibre
\pi^{-1}(z)=\{o_{1},\dots,o_{k}\}
is non-Hausdorff:
every neighbourhood of one
o_{i}
contains all the others.
As a result:

–

path- and homotopy-lifting both fail at
z
,

–

monodromy is undefined even though
\pi_{1}(Y)=0
,

–

the deck group satisfies
\Deck(\pi)\cong\mathfrak{S}_{k}
.

In short, a single inseparable fibre suffices to destroy every ingredient
of classical covering theory—even though
Y
is just a line with one
accumulation point.

Why “minimal”?

(i)

The total space
\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}
is 1-dimensional and locally Euclidean.

(ii)

The base
Y
is metrisable and sits inside a closed disk.

(iii)

Only one fibre is non-Hausdorff, and it contains finitely many points.

(iv)

Both
\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}
and
Y
are simply connected.

Remark 1.1
.

A 0-dimensional analogue would involve a finite topological space,
but such spaces are never locally Euclidean. The present construction
is therefore minimal in both dimension and complexity.

Relation to existing frameworks.

We show in §
6
that
\pi
lies outside every standard
covering category:

Branched covers

non-discrete singular fibre

Étale spaces

non-separated stalk at
z

Semicoverings

failure of unique homotopy lifting

Thus any future covering theory for non-Hausdorff spaces must either
(1) replace spaces by stacks, (2) replace monodromy by germ groupoids,
or (3) explicitly encode inseparability in the total space.

Organisation.

Section
2
defines and analyses
\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}

(proving
\pi_{1}=0
and inseparability at the origins).
Section
3
defines
\pi
and studies its local behaviour.
Section

\pi
.
Section

\pi

2
The line with
k
inseparable origins

Fix an integer
k\geq 2
. For each
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
let
\mathbb{R}_{i}
denote a
copy of
\mathbb{R}
labelled by
i
with origin
0_{i}
.

2.1
Definition

Set

X:=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{R}_{i},\qquad(x,i)\sim(y,j)\iff\begin{cases}x=y\neq 0,&\text{(glue all non-zero points),}\\
x=y=0\text{ and }i=j.&\text{(distinguish the origins).}\end{cases}

The
line with
k
inseparable origins
is the quotient

\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}:=X/\!\sim,\qquad q\colon X\twoheadrightarrow\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}.

We write
[x]_{i}:=q(x,i)
. For
x\neq 0
, all copies of
x
are identified,
so we often write
[x]
. The
k
distinct points

o_{i}:=[0]_{i}\in\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}

are the
origins
. These points are closed (
T_{1}
), but not Hausdorff-separated;
they are topologically indistinguishable in the sense that every neighbourhood of
one contains all the others (Lemma
A.2
).

Remark 2.1
.

Our notation
\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}
signals that this is
not

the classical model from Munkres
(
Munkres 2000
, Ex. I.3)
, where
different topologies are sometimes used. Here we adopt the inseparable model
used in
Steen and Seebach 1995
, ensuring that all nonzero points share
a common local chart and each origin is closed but inseparable from the others.

2.2
Basic topological properties

Proposition 2.2
.

The space
\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}
satisfies:

(i)

It is a 1-dimensional, locally Euclidean, second-countable,
T_{1}
space.

(ii)

It is
not
Hausdorff: every neighbourhood of one origin contains all the others.

(iii)

It is path-connected and has trivial fundamental group,
\pi_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp})=0
.

(iv)

It is
not
contractible (see
(
Steen and Seebach 1995
, Ex. I.3)
).

Sketch.

(i)–(ii) follow directly from the gluing description:
for
x\neq 0
, any open interval
(x-\varepsilon,x+\varepsilon)

in any copy
\mathbb{R}_{i}
gives a neighbourhood in
\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}
;
at
x=0
, the quotient topology ensures that every open set around
o_{i}

must contain points from every
\mathbb{R}_{j}
, hence includes all origins.

(iii) For path-connectedness, any two points can be joined by a path
entirely inside one branch except possibly at the origin. For
simply-connectedness, see Theorem
2.3
or Appendix
A
.

(iv) Non-contractibility follows from a standard obstruction argument:
see
(
Steen and Seebach 1995
, Ex. I.3)
or Appendix
A
.
∎

Theorem 2.3

(Simply connected but non-Hausdorff)
.

The space
\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}
satisfies
\pi_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp})=0

for all
k\geq 2
.

Proof.

Every loop
\gamma:S^{1}\to\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}
can be homotoped into
a single branch
\mathbb{R}_{i}
by moving within open sets of the form
U_{i}
,
which contain all the origins. Each such branch is simply connected,
and since the entire deformation stays within a contractible chart,
the loop contracts. Details appear in Appendix
A
.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

Several readers and referees have assumed that the nontrivial deck
symmetries of the map
\pi:\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}\to Y
would imply

\pi_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp})\neq 0
. Theorem
2.3

demonstrates this is incorrect: the deck group
\Deck(\pi)\cong\mathfrak{S}_{k}

arises from inseparability, not from fundamental group action.

2.3
A convenient pseudometric

Define

d\bigl([x]_{i},[y]_{j}\bigr):=\begin{cases}|x-y|,&i=j,\\[4.0pt]
|x|+|y|,&i\neq j.\end{cases}

Then
d
is a pseudometric satisfying
d(p,q)=0
if and only if

p=q
or both
p,q
are origins. The open balls
B_{d}(p,\varepsilon)

generate the quotient topology on
\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}
, as shown in
Appendix
A
.

Remark 2.5
.

For
k=2
this recovers the classical “line with two origins” pseudometric

(
Steen and Seebach 1995
, Ex. I.3)
, but the construction extends smoothly to any
k\geq 2
.

The pseudometric formulation will be used in Sections
3

and
4
to demonstrate local homeomorphism away from the singular
fibre and to prove failure of lifting. The triviality of
\pi_{1}
will be
crucial in understanding the collapse of monodromy and deck symmetry in
Sections
4
and
5
.

3
Construction of the projection
\pi

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a closed disk and fix an interior point
z\in D
.
Embed the punctured real line
\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
into the punctured disk via
a continuous injection

\iota:\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\;\hookrightarrow\;D\setminus\{z\},

such that

\lim_{x\to 0^{-}}\iota(x)=z\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}\iota(x)=z.

Define

\Gamma:=\iota\bigl(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\bigr),\qquad Y:=\Gamma\cup\{z\}\subset D,\qquad U:=\Gamma.

Note that
U\cong\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
is disconnected:
each component is an open ray with trivial fundamental group. Fix a basepoint

x_{0}:=\iota(1)\in U,\qquad\widetilde{x}_{0}:=[1]\in\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp},

and work in the component
U_{x_{0}}\subset U
, so
\pi_{1}(U_{x_{0}})=0
.

3.1
Definition of the map

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}
be the line with
k
inseparable origins
(Definition
2.1
). Define

\pi:\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}\longrightarrow Y,\qquad\pi\bigl([x]_{i}\bigr):=\begin{cases}\iota(x),&x\neq 0,\\
z,&x=0.\end{cases}

This is well-defined and continuous, since
\iota\circ\mathrm{pr}_{\mathbb{R}}:X\to Y

is continuous and constant on equivalence classes of
\sim
,
and
\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}=X/\!\sim
.

3.2
Regular locus

Proposition 3.2
.

The map
\pi
is surjective, with singular fibre

\pi^{-1}(z)=\{o_{1},\dots,o_{k}\}
.
Moreover, the restriction

\pi|_{V}:V:=\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}\setminus\pi^{-1}(z)\;\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}\;U

is a homeomorphism; in particular,
\pi
is a 1-sheeted local homeomorphism
over
U=Y\setminus\{z\}
.

Proof.

For
x\neq 0
, all branches
[x]_{i}
map to
\iota(x)\in\Gamma
, and
each origin
o_{i}=[0]_{i}
maps to
z
. Thus
\pi
is surjective with

\pi^{-1}(z)=\{o_{1},\dots,o_{k}\}
. On
V
, the label is redundant:

\pi([x]_{i})=\iota(x)
, and all such preimages collapse under
\sim
,
so
\pi|_{V}
is injective. The inverse map
\iota^{-1}\mapsto[x]
is
continuous, as both
\iota
and the quotient map are embeddings.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Every fibre over
p\in U
has size 1, but the fibre over
z
has size
k
.
Hence
\pi
is not a classical
k
-sheeted covering.

3.3
Failure at the singular point

Proposition 3.4

(Non-covering at
z
)
.

No neighbourhood of
z
in
Y
is evenly covered by
\pi
. Hence

\pi
is not a covering map.

Proof.

Let
W\subset Y
be any open neighbourhood of
z
. Since

\iota(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
accumulates at
z
, the set
W
contains
points
\iota(x)
for arbitrarily small
x\neq 0
. Thus

\pi^{-1}(W)=\{[x]\mid x\neq 0,\ \iota(x)\in W\}\cup\{o_{1},\dots,o_{k}\}.

Because each origin
o_{i}
is topologically inseparable from the others,
any neighbourhood of
o_{i}
must contain all of
\{o_{1},\dots,o_{k}\}
.
Hence
\pi^{-1}(W)
is connected and cannot be written as a disjoint
union of open sets homeomorphic to
W
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

This single inseparable fibre over
z
is what obstructs both path- and
homotopy-lifting in Section
4
.

3.4
Monodromy obstruction

Even within the contractible component
U_{x_{0}}
, monodromy theory requires

unique
path-lifting. Since that fails at
z

(Proposition
4.1
), we obtain:

Corollary 3.6
.

There is no well-defined monodromy action

\pi_{1}(U_{x_{0}})\curvearrowright\pi^{-1}(x_{0})
.

Proof.

Any such action would assign to each loop a permutation of the fibre
over
x_{0}
. But loops passing through
z
may lift to all
k

origins, yielding multiple non-equivalent lifts. Hence no consistent
lift-permutation rule exists.
∎

4
Failure of Classical Covering–Space Properties

Section
3
shows that

\pi:\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}\to Y
is a
1-sheeted
local homeomorphism over

U=\Gamma
but fails to be a covering at the point

z
because the fibre

\pi^{-1}(z)=\{o_{1},\dots,o_{k}\}
is non-Hausdorff.
We now record how this single inseparable fibre annihilates
classical properties: unique path lifting, homotopy lifting, monodromy,
and the expected deck-group correspondence.

Standing notation.

U:=\Gamma=\iota(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}),\qquad V:=\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}\setminus\pi^{-1}(z),\qquad\pi|_{V}:V\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}U\text{ (by Proposition~\ref{prop:pi-regular})}.

4.1
Failure of unique path lifting

Proposition 4.1

(Non-uniqueness of lifts)
.

Fix
x_{0}\in U
with
x_{0}\neq 0
, and let
\widetilde{x}_{0}=[x_{0}]\in\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}
.
There exists a loop

\gamma:[0,1]\to Y,\qquad\gamma(0)=\gamma(1)=x_{0},

that passes through
z
at
t=\tfrac{1}{2}
and admits
k
distinct lifts

\widetilde{\gamma}_{i}:[0,1]\to\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}
with
\widetilde{\gamma}_{i}(0)=\widetilde{x}_{0}
.
Hence
\pi
fails to have the unique path-lifting property.

Proof.

Let
x_{0}>0
and define

\gamma(t)=\begin{cases}\iota\bigl(x_{0}(1-2t)\bigr),&0\leq t<\tfrac{1}{2},\\[4.0pt]
z,&t=\tfrac{1}{2},\\[4.0pt]
\iota\bigl(x_{0}(2t-1)\bigr),&\tfrac{1}{2}<t\leq 1.\end{cases}

Continuity at
t=\tfrac{1}{2}
follows since

\lim_{t\to\tfrac{1}{2}^{\pm}}\iota(x_{0}(1-2t))=z
. The preimage of
z

under any lift must be some
o_{i}
, and after
t=\tfrac{1}{2}
, the lift
must exit via the corresponding branch. Thus each lift is uniquely determined
by its choice of origin at the midpoint, yielding
k
distinct lifts.
∎

Corollary 4.2

(Monodromy undefined)
.

Although
\pi_{1}(U_{x_{0}})=0
, path-lifting is not unique, so no monodromy
action—trivial or otherwise—can be defined on
\pi^{-1}(x_{0})
.

4.2
Failure of homotopy lifting

Proposition 4.3

(Homotopy-lifting obstruction)
.

There exists a homotopy

H:[0,1]^{2}\to Y\quad\text{with}\quad H(s,0)=H(s,1)=x_{0},\quad H\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2},t\bigr)=z,

such that no continuous lift

\widetilde{H}:[0,1]^{2}\to\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}

extending the lift of
H(\cdot,0)
can exist.

Proof.

Let
H
contract the loop
\gamma
from Proposition
4.1

through
z
. Suppose for contradiction that a continuous lift

\widetilde{H}
exists with
\widetilde{H}(s,0)=\widetilde{x}_{0}
. Define

g(s):=\widetilde{H}\left(s,\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\in\{o_{1},\dots,o_{k}\}.

Since the origins are topologically indistinguishable, any such map
g

must be constant. However, lifting the endpoints of
\gamma
yields

g(0)=o_{i}
,
g(1)=o_{j}
for
i\neq j
, a contradiction. Hence no such

\widetilde{H}
exists.
∎

4.3
The deck-transformation group

Definition 4.4
.

A
deck transformation
is a homeomorphism

h:\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}\to\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}
satisfying

\pi\circ h=\pi
.

Proposition 4.5

(Deck group)
.

Every permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}
of the origins defines a
deck transformation
h_{\sigma}
, and

\Deck(\pi)\;\cong\;\mathfrak{S}_{k}.

Proof.

Define
h_{\sigma}([x]_{i}):=[x]_{\sigma(i)}
for
x\neq 0
, and

h_{\sigma}(o_{i}):=o_{\sigma(i)}
. This is continuous and
\pi\circ h_{\sigma}=\pi
.
Injectivity follows from
\sigma\mapsto h_{\sigma}
being faithful.
Surjectivity holds since any deck transformation must fix
[x]
for

x\neq 0
, and can only permute the indistinct origins.
∎

4.4
Summary of failures

Theorem 4.6
.

For every
k\geq 2
, the projection
\pi:\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}\to Y
satisfies:

(a)

Local behaviour:

\pi|_{V}:V\xrightarrow{\cong}U
is a 1-sheeted local homeomorphism.

(b)

Non-covering at
z
:
no neighbourhood of
z
is evenly covered (Proposition
3.4
).

(c)

Path and homotopy lifting fail:
Propositions
4.1
and
4.3
.

(d)

Monodromy absent:
Corollary
4.2
.

(e)

Deck group exotic:

\Deck(\pi)\cong\mathfrak{S}_{k}
, despite
\pi_{1}(U)=0
.

Thus, the classical Galois correspondence between connected covers and
conjugacy classes of subgroups of
\pi_{1}(U)
has no analogue for
\pi
.

5
Algebraic Obstructions to Classification

Section
4
exhibited the
analytic
breakdown of
covering theory for the map

\pi:\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}\to Y=\Gamma\cup\{z\}
.
We now show that no standard algebraic framework—subgroups,
groupoids, or semicoverings—captures this map.

Standing notation.

We write
U:=\Gamma\cong\mathbb{R}
, fix a point
x_{0}\in U
, and let

\widetilde{x}_{0}:=[x_{0}]\in\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}
.

5.1
Fundamental group recap

From Proposition
2.2
(iii),

\pi_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp})=0
, despite
\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}
being non-contractible.
Moreover, the base space also satisfies
\pi_{1}(U)=0
.

5.2
No subgroup realises
\pi

Theorem 5.1
.

The classical subgroup correspondence for covering spaces over

U
cannot produce the map

\pi:(\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp},\widetilde{x}_{0})\to(Y,x_{0})
.

Proof.

Since
\pi
fails to be a covering at
z

(Proposition
3.4
), the correspondence is
a priori

inapplicable. One might still hope to reconstruct
\pi
from its restriction

\pi|_{V}\cong U
together with additional data over the fibre

\{o_{1},\dots,o_{k}\}
. However, since
\pi_{1}(U)=0
, the expected monodromy
and deck group would be trivial. In contrast,
\Deck(\pi)\cong\mathfrak{S}_{k}

(Proposition
4.5
), and no subgroup of a trivial group can account for
this symmetry.
∎

5.3
Failure at the groupoid level

Definition 5.2
.

A functor
F:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
between topological groupoids
is a
covering morphism
if it is bijective on objects and each map

F:\mathcal{G}(x,x)\to\mathcal{H}(Fx,Fx)
is a covering map of
topological spaces.

Proposition 5.3
.

There is no covering morphism

F:\Pi_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp})\to\Pi_{1}(Y)

with object map given by
\pi
.

Proof.

Fix
x_{0}\in U
and its lift
\widetilde{x}_{0}
. Since

\pi_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp})=0
, the vertex group

\Pi_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp})(\widetilde{x}_{0},\widetilde{x}_{0})
is trivial.
If
F
were a covering morphism, the image inside

\Pi_{1}(Y)(x_{0},x_{0})
would have to be discrete. But

\Pi_{1}(Y)(x_{0},x_{0})
contains the non-discrete loop space at
x_{0}

(homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}
), so no such functor can exist.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

We follow
Brown and Higgins 2006
in interpreting
\Pi_{1}
as a

topological
groupoid, with morphism spaces endowed with the natural
quotient topology from loop spaces.

5.4
Obstruction to semicoverings

Definition 5.5

(
Brazas 2011
)
.

A map
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
is a
semicovering
if it is a local
homeomorphism and both paths and homotopies lift
continuously and
uniquely
from every point of
\widetilde{X}
.

Theorem 5.6
.

For every
k\geq 2
, the projection
\pi:\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}\to Y

is
not
a semicovering.

Proof.

Proposition
4.1
exhibits a loop whose lifts are
non-unique, violating the semicovering axioms.
∎

5.5
Synthesis

Corollary 5.7

6
Comparison with Branched, Étale, and Semicovering Maps

Sections

–

over

6.1
Classical branched covers

Definition 6.1
.

Let
X,Y
be connected
Hausdorff
surfaces. A continuous
surjection
f:X\!\to\!Y
is a
branched cover of degree
k

if every

has a neighbourhood
U
with

f^{-1}(U)=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{k}V_{j}
, where each

f|_{V_{j}}:V_{j}\to U
is a finite-sheeted covering, branched only at
isolated points.

Our target
Y
fails the
Hausdorff
hypothesis, so

\pi
is disqualified before any local check. Even embedding
Y
into a
Hausdorff ambient disk (Appendix C) does not salvage this:

Proposition 6.2
.

For every
k\geq 2
, the map
\pi:\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}\to Y
cannot be promoted to a
branched cover of any Hausdorff surface: no neighbourhood of
z

splits into disjoint local sheets.

Proof.

Every neighbourhood of
z
in
Y
contains points of
\Gamma

approaching from both sides. Hence
\pi^{-1}(U)
is connected for any such

U
; a branched cover would require
f^{-1}(U)
to be a disjoint
union of local sheets.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

A genuine branched cover carries local monodromy; for
\pi
, monodromy is

undefined
(Corollary
4.2
), so the obstruction is intrinsic.

6.2
Étale spaces of presheaves

Let
\Sh(Y)
denote the topos of sheaves of sets on
Y
.

Definition 6.4
.

A map
p:E\to Y
is an
étalé space
if it is a local
homeomorphism. It is
separated
iff
E
is Hausdorff—equivalently,
distinct germs over a point are topologically distinguishable.

Proposition 6.5
.

The restriction
\pi|_{V}:V\xrightarrow{\cong}U
is a local homeomorphism, so

\pi
is étale on a dense open. However,
\pi
is
not
the
étalé space of a separated sheaf, because the stalk over
z
is
inseparable.

Sketch.

Consider the presheaf assigning to each open
W\subset Y
the set of locally constant maps
on
W\setminus\{z\}
labelling the branch index in
\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}
.
Two distinct sections labelled
i\neq j
coincide on
W\setminus\{z\}
,
so the separatedness condition fails.
∎

Thus
\pi
is
quasi-étale
(étale off a closed set) but not
Grothendieck-étale.

A map

\pi

Proof.

6.4
Stacks and future frameworks

Current definitions of topological and differentiable stacks require

separated

is non-Hausdorff at the

\pi
cannot be realised as a stacky cover under existing definitions.
A future theory of
non-Hausdorff stacks

points that are topologically inseparable—would be needed to
accommodate projections like
\pi

7
Conclusion and Outlook

7.1
Main findings

For each
k\geq 2
, we constructed a continuous surjection

\pi:\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}\longrightarrow Y:=\Gamma\cup\{z\},

formed by collapsing the embedded image curve
\Gamma\subset D\setminus\{z\}

to the point
z
. Away from
z
, the map is a
1-sheeted
local homeomorphism,
yet possesses a single non-Hausdorff fibre

\pi^{-1}(z)=\{o_{1},\dots,o_{k}\}
.

Sections
3
–
6
establish the following:

1.

\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}
is locally Euclidean, second-countable,
T_{1}
, and has
trivial fundamental group, but is
not
contractible and
fails Hausdorff separation precisely at the origins.

2.

Unique path- and homotopy-lifting both fail for paths meeting

z
; consequently, classical monodromy is undefined.

3.

The deck-transformation group is large:

\Deck(\pi)\cong\mathfrak{S}_{k}
, despite

\pi_{1}(U)=0
.

4.

\pi
fits into none of the classical frameworks—branched covers,
étale maps, or semicoverings—each of which requires
either Hausdorff fibres or unique lifting.

Thus a
single non-separated fibre
suffices to annihilate every
pillar of the classical Galois correspondence, even when the base is as
tame as a punctured real line.

7.2
Why Hausdorffness is indispensable

Classical covering theory rests on three interlocking properties:

•

Unique path and homotopy lifting
, enabling a monodromy action;

•

Discrete fibres
, allowing a faithful deck transformation group;

•

Evenly covered neighbourhoods
at
every
point.

The map
\pi
violates the first two at a single fibre, which in turn
precludes the third. In that precise sense, the example is

minimal
: one notch down in the separation axioms collapses the
entire classification superstructure.

7.3
Towards a non-Hausdorff covering theory

Extending covering-space theory beyond the Hausdorff world may require:

(i)

Encoding inseparable fibres.

A richer object—perhaps a topological stack or groupoid with
“smeared” isotropy—must retain information about indistinguishable
yet distinct lift choices.

(ii)

A softened Galois correspondence.

Instead of a group acting
faithfully
on a discrete fibre,
one may need an action of a
groupoid of germs
, or a
higher-categorical analogue that counts admissible lifts without
enforcing uniqueness.

A promising prototype may be a Deligne–Mumford–type stack with
non-separated inertia, blending stack theory with coarse
(non-separated) geometry.

7.4
Open problems

1.

Finite non-Hausdorff fibres.

Classify all connected, locally trivial surjections over a
punctured surface whose singular fibres are finite but
non-Hausdorff.

2.

Higher-dimensional analogues.

Does an
n
-manifold analogue of the line-with-
k
-origins
exist for
n>1
, locally Euclidean except along a codimension-2
non-Hausdorff stratum?

3.

Stack-theoretic Galois theory.

Develop a correspondence between subgroupoids of a
“topologised”
\pi_{1}
-stack of the base and suitable

stacky covers
whose total spaces may be non-Hausdorff.

4.

Shape-theoretic invariants.

Identify shape or pro-homotopy invariants that detect the failure
of unique lifting in non-Hausdorff covers.

Appendix A
Topological Properties of
\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}

Fix an integer
k\geq 2
and recall:

\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}:=\left(\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{R}_{i}\right)\big/\!\sim,\quad(x,i)\sim(y,j)\iff\begin{cases}x=y\neq 0,\\
x=y=0\text{ and }i=j.\end{cases}

A.1
Local charts and separation axioms

Proposition A.1

(Local Euclideanity)
.

\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}
is a 1-dimensional, second-countable,
T_{1}
space. Every point
admits a neighbourhood homeomorphic to an open interval in
\mathbb{R}
,
so
\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}
is locally Euclidean. However, it is
not

Hausdorff: the
k
origins cannot be separated by disjoint opens.

Proof.

If
p=[x]_{i}
with
x\neq 0
, then
p
lies in
\mathbb{R}_{i}\setminus\{0_{i}\}
,
which is open and homeomorphic to
(x-\varepsilon,x+\varepsilon)
via
[x]_{i}\mapsto x
.
For
p=o_{i}
, let
\varepsilon>0
and define

U_{i}=\{[x]_{i}:|x|<\varepsilon\}\cup\;\bigcup_{j\neq i}\!\!\;\;\{[x]_{j}:0<|x|<\varepsilon\}.

The map sending
[x]_{j}\mapsto x
collapses all origins to 0, yielding
a homeomorphism
U_{i}\cong(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
.
Second-countability follows from the real lines. Points are closed
since singletons are closed in each
\mathbb{R}_{i}
.
No origin can be separated from the others by disjoint opens, proving non-Hausdorffness.
∎

Lemma A.2

(Indistinguishable origins)
.

For every open
N\subset\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}
containing
o_{i}
and every
j
, one has

o_{j}\in N
. Thus all neighbourhood filters of the
o_{i}
coincide.

Proof.

From Proposition
A.1
, a basic open
U_{i}\subset N

contains all non-zero
[x]_{j}
with
0<|x|<\varepsilon
, whose closure
contains each
o_{j}
, hence
o_{j}\in N
.
∎

A.2
Path connectivity and fundamental group

Proposition A.3

(Path-connectedness)
.

\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}
is path-connected.

Proof.

Any two points in the same branch can be joined by a straight path.
If
p
and
q
lie in different branches, join
p
to its origin,
use a small neighbourhood around the origins to move to the origin of the second
branch, then go to
q
. The concatenated path is continuous.
∎

Proposition A.4

(Trivial fundamental group)
.

\pi_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp})=0
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha:S^{1}\to\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}
be a loop. Cover its image with
finitely many local charts as in Proposition
A.1
. By successively
deforming each segment into an interval around an origin, one can contract

\alpha
entirely. Hence
\alpha
is null-homotopic.
∎

A.3
A pseudometric model

Lemma A.5

(Pseudometric generating the topology)
.

Define

d\bigl([x]_{i},[y]_{j}\bigr):=\begin{cases}|x-y|,&i=j,\\
|x|+|y|,&i\neq j.\end{cases}

Then
d
is a pseudometric on
\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}
, and the open balls

B_{d}(p,\varepsilon)
form a basis for its topology. Moreover,

d(p,q)=0
iff
p=q
or
p,q
are both origins.

Proof.

Triangle inequality is clear in both cases. For
[x]_{i}\notin\{o_{1},\dots,o_{k}\}
,
the ball
B_{d}([x]_{i},\varepsilon)
is the usual interval in
\mathbb{R}_{i}
.
For an origin
o_{i}
, the ball
B_{d}(o_{i},\varepsilon)
contains all

[x]_{j}
with
|x|<\varepsilon
and all origins. This matches the basic
opens from Proposition
A.1
.
∎

Consequences for the main text

•

Proposition
A.4
confirms that

\pi_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp})=0
: the space is simply connected,
despite its non-Hausdorff nature.

•

Lemma
A.2
explains all failures of path- and
homotopy-lifting, as continuous maps cannot distinguish among the origins.

Appendix B
Failure of Path and Homotopy Lifting

Fix the projection
\pi:\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}\to Y=\Gamma\cup\{z\}
,
the accumulation point
z\in Y
, and the basepoint

x_{0}=\iota(1)\in U,\quad p_{0}=[1]\in\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}.

On
\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}\setminus\pi^{-1}(z)\cong U
, classical lifting works;
at
z
, it fails.

B.1
Non-uniqueness of path lifts

Lemma B.1
.

Define

\alpha:[0,1]\to Y,\qquad\alpha(t)=\begin{cases}\iota(1-2t),&0\leq t<\tfrac{1}{2},\\[4.0pt]
z,&t=\tfrac{1}{2},\\[4.0pt]
\iota(2t-1),&\tfrac{1}{2}<t\leq 1.\end{cases}

Then
\alpha
is continuous (in the subspace topology from
D
), with

\alpha(0)=\alpha(1)=x_{0}
and
\alpha(\tfrac{1}{2})=z
.
Moreover, for each
o_{i}\in\pi^{-1}(z)
there exists a unique lift

\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}:[0,1]\to\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}
with

\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(0)=p_{0}
and
\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(\tfrac{1}{2})=o_{i}
.
Hence, there are exactly
k
distinct lifts.

Proof.

For
t\neq\tfrac{1}{2}
,
\alpha(t)\in U
, so the lift must be

[x]_{i}
with
x=\iota^{-1}(\alpha(t))
. At
t=\tfrac{1}{2}
, the image is

z
, and
\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(\tfrac{1}{2})=o_{i}
defines a continuous extension
since
\iota(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
accumulates only at
z
.
By Lemma
A.2
, distinct
o_{i}
yield distinct lifts,
yielding
k
total lifts.
∎

Corollary B.2
.

The map
\pi
fails the unique path-lifting property and is therefore not a semicovering.

B.2
Failure of homotopy lifting

Theorem B.3
.

There exists a homotopy

H:[0,1]^{2}\to Y,\qquad H(s,0)=H(s,1)=x_{0},\quad H(\tfrac{1}{2},t)=z,

but no continuous lift
\widetilde{H}:[0,1]^{2}\to\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}
with

\widetilde{H}(s,0)=p_{0}
can exist.

Proof.

Suppose
\widetilde{H}
exists. Then

f(s):=\widetilde{H}(s,1)\in\{o_{1},\dots,o_{k}\}

defines a continuous map into a discrete set of indistinguishable points.
By Lemma
A.2
, such a map must be constant.
However, evaluating at
s=0
and
s=1
yields distinct
o_{i}
, a contradiction.
∎

Summary

Lemmas
B.1
and Theorem
B.3
confirm that

\pi
violates classical path- and homotopy-lifting at the single
non-Hausdorff fibre over
z
, dismantling the usual covering-space
framework.

Appendix C
The Deck–Transformation Group

Let
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
be a covering map.
A
deck transformation
is a homeomorphism

h:\widetilde{X}\to\widetilde{X}
satisfying
p\circ h=p
.
For the projection
\pi:\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}\to Y=\Gamma\cup\{z\}
,
we determine
\Deck(\pi)
.

Lemma C.1
.

The map
\pi
is injective on

V=\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}\setminus\pi^{-1}(z)
,
so any deck transformation must restrict to the identity on
V
.

Proposition C.2

(Deck group)
.

\Deck(\pi)\;\cong\;\mathfrak{S}_{k}.

Proof.

For each permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}
, define

h_{\sigma}\bigl([x]_{i}\bigr):=\begin{cases}[x]_{\sigma(i)},&x\neq 0,\\
o_{\sigma(i)},&x=0.\end{cases}

(i) Well-defined and continuous.

For
x\neq 0
, the class
[x]_{i}
is independent of
i
, so the assignment is consistent.
Each branch
\mathbb{R}_{i}
is mapped homeomorphically onto
\mathbb{R}_{\sigma(i)}
,
and the indistinguishability of the origins (Lemma
A.2
) ensures continuity at
x=0
.

(ii) Commutativity with
\pi
.

For
x\neq 0
, we have

\pi(h_{\sigma}([x]_{i}))=\iota(x)=\pi([x]_{i}),

and for
x=0
, both sides equal
z
. Hence
h_{\sigma}\in\Deck(\pi)
.

(iii) Isomorphism.

The assignment
\sigma\mapsto h_{\sigma}
defines an injective homomorphism

\mathfrak{S}_{k}\hookrightarrow\Deck(\pi)
.
Conversely, any
h\in\Deck(\pi)
must act as the identity on
V

since
\pi
is injective there.
Thus
h
is uniquely determined by its action on the origins,
which must be a permutation, proving surjectivity.
∎

Corollary C.3
.

Although
\pi|_{V}:V\xrightarrow{\cong}U
is a local homeomorphism,
the full map
\pi
is not a regular (Galois) cover in the classical sense
over Hausdorff spaces: the deck group acts trivially on
V
but non-trivially
on the inseparable fibre over
z
.

Remark C.4
.

Classical
k
-sheeted covers of a punctured disk have cyclic deck groups;
here, the non-Hausdorff fibre inflates that symmetry to the full symmetric group

\mathfrak{S}_{k}
.

Appendix D

The main text fixes the codomain

. For readers who prefer a surjective map onto the
entire

D.1
Radially thickened total space

Define the thickened total space:

\mathfrak{L}_{k}^{\sharp}:=\left(\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}\times[0,1]\right)\big/\bigl(([x]_{i},1)\sim([x]_{j},1)\text{ for all }x,i,j\bigr).

Geometrically, each branch of
\mathscr{L}_{k}^{\sharp}
is thickened into a “radial tube,” and the outer ends of all tubes (

D.2
The map
\mathfrak{p}

Let

denote the normalized radial distance from
\Gamma
to
\partial D

\mathfrak{p}\bigl([x]_{i},t\bigr):=(1-t)\,\iota(x)+t\cdot r\bigl(\iota(x)\bigr)\cdot\frac{\iota(x)}{\|\iota(x)\|}.

Lemma D.1
.

The map
\mathfrak{p}:\mathfrak{L}_{k}^{\sharp}\to D
is continuous and surjective.
Its fibre over
z
is
\{o_{1},\dots,o_{k}\}
, retaining its non-Hausdorff structure.

Sketch.

Continuity is straightforward away from
z
. At
t=1
, all tube endpoints map to
\partial D
, leaving the fibre over
z
unchanged. Surjectivity follows from the radial interpolation from
\iota(x)
to the boundary.
∎

D.3
Persistence of the pathologies

Proposition D.2
.

The map
\mathfrak{p}
exhibits exactly the same pathologies as the core construction:

(a)

\mathfrak{p}
restricts to a
1
-sheeted covering on

\mathfrak{L}_{k}^{\sharp}\setminus\mathfrak{p}^{-1}(z)
.

(b)

Path and homotopy lifting fail; monodromy is undefined.

(c)

The deck transformation group is
\Deck(\mathfrak{p})\cong\mathfrak{S}_{k}
, acting by permutation of the radial tubes.

Idea.

One lifts loops and homotopies from Appendix
B
by holding the
t

t>0
, hence corresponds to permuting the tubes, yielding
\mathfrak{S}_{k}
.
∎

Linearity-Inducing Priors for Poisson Parameter Estimation Under L 1 L^{1} Loss Thanks: This work was supported in part by the U.S. National Science Foundation under Grant ECCS-2335876.
Leighton P. Barnes 1 , Alex Dytso 2 , and H. Vincent Poor 3

Abstract
We study prior distributions for Poisson parameter estimation under L 1 L^{1} loss. Specifically, we construct a new family of prior distributions whose optimal Bayesian estimators (the conditional medians) can be any prescribed increasing function that satisfies certain regularity conditions. In the case of affine estimators, this family is distinct from the usual conjugate priors, which are gamma distributions. Our prior distributions are constructed through a limiting process that matches certain moment conditions. These results provide the first explicit description of a family of distributions, beyond the conjugate priors, that satisfy the affine conditional median property; and more broadly for the Poisson noise model they can give any arbitrarily prescribed conditional median.

In Bayesian estimation theory, the optimal estimator under

I-A

Paper Outline and Contribution

Our first contribution is to show that for any increasing function
f
(that satisfies a particular regularity condition) there exists a prior such that

\mathsf{med}(X|Y=y)=f(y),\,y\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(8)

Specializing this result to affine functions
f
, we demonstrate a new family of prior distributions that
do
induce an affine conditional median. They are distinct from the usual conjugate prior/gamma distribution, but may have a qualitatively similar tail as seen in Fig.
2
. They are constructed by a limiting procedure of matching particular moment conditions, and this limiting procedure is guaranteed to have at least a subsequence of distributions that converge in total variation to a measure with the desired properties.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first explicit description of a distribution that is distinct from the usual conjugate prior, but satisfies the affine conditional median property (
6
). In this sense, it could be viewed as a family of
L^{1}
-conjugate
priors, but without necessarily preserving the property that conditioning is closed with respect to the family of distributions.

We proceed as follows: In Section
II
below we state our main existence results, and then Section
III
is devoted to proving these results by constructing our distributions via a limiting process. In Section
IV
we conclude by showing examples and discussing the implications of this construction.

II
Main Results

In this section, we present our main results. We begin by presenting the existence result, followed by a description of a procedure that can be used to construct such a distribution.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that
f:\mathbb{N}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfies the following properties:

•

f
is an increasing function up to a point
c_{0}
and constant afterwards (i.e.,
f(i-1)<f(i),\,i\leq c_{0}
and
f(i+1)=f(c_{0}),i\geq c_{0}
). The point
c_{0}
can be infinity; and

•

there exists
\kappa\geq 1
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{c_{0}}\left(\frac{f\left(\lfloor\frac{i}{\kappa}\rfloor\right)}{f(i)}\right)^{\frac{i}{\kappa}}\mathrm{e}^{f(i)}<\infty.

(9)

Then, there exists a distribution
P_{X}
supported on

\mathcal{W}=\{w_{i}:w_{i}=f(i-1),\,i\in\mathbb{N}\}

(10)

such that for all
y\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

\mathsf{med}(X|Y=y)=f(y).

(11)

The first assumption in Theorem
1
is not very restrictive in view of the following facts:

•

The assumption that
f
is increasing is justified by the fact that for Poisson noise optimal Bayesian estimators are non-decreasing
[
17
]
.

•

If
X\leq A
has bounded support, then
\mathsf{med}(X|Y=y)\leq A,y\in\mathbb{N}
, i.e., the conditional median can increase only up to a point.

Specializing, the above result to linear estimators we have the following result.

Corollary 1
.

Given
0<a<\frac{1}{\mathrm{e}}\approx 0.3678
and
b>0
, let

\mathcal{W}=\left\{w_{i}:w_{i}=a(i-1)+b,\,i\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

(12)

Then, there exists
P_{X}
supported on
\mathcal{W}
such that for all
y\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

\mathsf{med}(X|Y=y)=ay+b.

(13)

Proof:

Setting
f(y)=ay+b
, the sum in (
9
) reduces to

\inf_{\kappa\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{a\lfloor\frac{i}{\kappa}\rfloor+b}{ai+b}\right)^{\frac{i}{\kappa}}\mathrm{e}^{ai+b},

(14)

which converges provided that
a<\frac{1}{\mathrm{e}}
.
∎

Note that unlike for the conditional mean case, the distribution inducing a linear conditional median is not necessarily unique. It is also worth pointing out that the range of
a\in\left(0,\frac{1}{\mathrm{e}}\right)
in Corollary
1
is most likely not exhaustive. The full set of admissible values of
a
is left as an open problem.

III-A

Equivalent Moment Condition

We first produce an equivalent moment condition, which is easier to work with.

Lemma 1
.

Given
f:\mathbb{N}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, suppose there exists a distribution
P_{W}
such that

\mathbb{E}[W^{y}1_{W\leq f(y)}]=\mathbb{E}[W^{y}1_{W>f(y)}],\,y\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(15)

Then,

\mathrm{d}P_{X}(w)=\mathrm{d}P_{W}(w)\mathrm{e}^{w}

(16)

induces

\mathsf{med}(X|Y=y)=f(y),\,y\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(17)

provided that
P_{X}
is a proper distribution.

Proof:

An equivalent condition for the prescribed conditional median is
given by the conditional cdf: for all
y\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

\mathbb{E}\left[1_{X\leq f(Y)}|Y=y\right]=\frac{1}{2}

(18)

which is equivalent to: for all
y\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

\mathbb{E}[1_{X\leq f(Y)}|Y=y]=\mathbb{E}[1_{X>f(Y)}|Y=y].

(19)

Now by defining

\mathrm{d}P_{W}(w)\propto\mathrm{e}^{-w}\mathrm{d}P_{X}(w),

(20)

and using the structure of the
P_{Y|X}
in (
2
) the expression in (
19
) can be re-written as

\int w^{y}1_{w\leq f(y)}\mathrm{d}P_{W}(w)=\int w^{y}1_{w>f(y)}\mathrm{d}P_{W}(w).

(21)

This concludes the proof.
∎

We now proceed to show the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that
f:\mathbb{N}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}_{0}
is increasing up to a point
c_{0}
, such that for every
y\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

\inf_{\kappa\geq 1}\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{i=N+1}^{c_{0}}\left(\frac{f\left(\lfloor\frac{i}{\kappa}\rfloor\right)}{f(i)}\right)^{\frac{i}{\kappa}}(f(i))^{y}=0.

(22)

Then, there exists a discrete probability distribution
P_{W}
supported on the set
\mathcal{W}
defined in (
10
) such that

\mathbb{E}[W^{y}1_{W\leq f(y)}]=\mathbb{E}[W^{y}1_{W>f(y)}],\,y\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(23)

Our approach is to first show a truncated version, where the first
M
moment conditions are satisfied, and then show that we can pass to a subsequence whose limit exists as the truncation point
M
is moved to infinity.

III-B

Proof of the Truncated Version of Theorem
2

We first consider a truncated version of our problem. That is, given
M\geq 1
, we seek to find
P_{W}
such that

\mathbb{E}[W^{y}1_{W\leq f(y)}]=\mathbb{E}[W^{y}1_{W>f(y)}],\,y\in[0:M-1].

(24)

Naturally, we assume that
M\leq c_{0}
.

We start with the following helper lemma.

Lemma 2
.

For every
M\geq 1
and
0<w_{1}<\ldots<w_{M+1}<\infty
, there exists a probability vector
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{M+1})
such that for all
k\in[0:M-1]

\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}p_{i}w_{i}^{k}=\sum_{i=k+2}^{M+1}p_{i}w_{i}^{k}.

(25)

Proof:

We show this by induction. For the base case of
M=1
, we have
p_{1}=p_{2}
,
which results in valid probability vector if
p_{1}=p_{2}=1/2
.

We now make an induction hypothesis that the statement is true for
M
. Let
w_{M+2}>w_{M+1}
. Now by the induction hypothesis for
(w_{1},\ldots,w_{M+1})
there exists a probability vector
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{M+1})
such that for all
k\in[0:M-1]

\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}p_{i}w_{i}^{k}=\sum_{i=k+2}^{M+1}p_{i}w_{i}^{k}.

(26)

Moreover, by the induction hypothesis for
(w_{1},\ldots,w_{M},w_{M+2})
there exists a probability vector
(q_{1},\ldots,q_{M+1})
such that for all
k\in[0:M-1]

\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}q_{i}w_{i}^{k}=\sum_{i=k+2}^{M}q_{i}w_{i}^{k}+q_{M+1}w_{M+2}^{k}.

(27)

We now construct a solution to the case
M+1
for the sequence
(w_{1},\ldots,w_{M},w_{M+1},w_{M+2})
. Fix some
\alpha\in[0,1]
(to be determined later) and let

\displaystyle(m_{1},\ldots,m_{M+2})

\displaystyle=\alpha(p_{1},\ldots,p_{M+1},0)

\displaystyle+(1-\alpha)(q_{1},\ldots,q_{M},0,q_{M+1})

(28)

which obviously is a probability vector. Moreover, by construction, for all
k\in[0:M-1]
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}m_{i}w_{i}^{k}=\sum_{i=k+2}^{M+2}m_{i}w_{i}^{k}.

(29)

To conclude the proof, we need to examine the case of
k=M
and show that

\sum_{i=1}^{M+1}m_{i}w_{i}^{M}=m_{M+2}w_{M+2}^{M},

(30)

which can be re-written as

\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{M+1}p_{i}w_{i}^{M}+(1-\alpha)\sum_{i=1}^{M}q_{i}w_{i}^{M}=(1-\alpha)q_{M+1}w_{M+2}^{M}.

(31)

To show that there exists a choice of
\alpha\in[0,1]
such that (

) is true note that for
\alpha=1
the LHS of (
31
) is larger than the RHS. For
\alpha=0
the LHS is smaller than RHS since

\sum_{i=1}^{M}q_{i}w_{i}^{M}\leq w_{M+2}\sum_{i=1}^{M}q_{i}w_{i}^{M-1}=w_{M+2}q_{M+1}w_{M+2}^{M-1}

where the last equality follows from (

We now show that for every increasing function there exists a
P_{W}
that satisfies (

to be a discrete distribution with the support given by

\mathcal{W}^{M}=\left\{w_{i}:w_{i}=f(i-1),\,i\in[1:M+1]\right\}.

(33)

Theorem 3
.

Suppose that
f:\mathbb{N}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is increasing up to

and

Proof:

With the choice of

\sum_{i=1}^{M+1}p_{i}w_{i}^{y}1_{\{w_{i}\leq f(y)\}}=\sum_{i=1}^{M+1}p_{i}w_{i}^{y}1_{\{w_{i}>f(y)\}},

and which can be further simplified to

\sum_{i=1}^{y+1}p_{i}w_{i}^{y}=\sum_{i=y+2}^{M+1}p_{i}w_{i}^{y},\,y\in[0:M-1].

Now by invoking Lemma

We now produce a concentration bound that will be useful.

Lemma 3
.

Let

be the random variable constructed in Theorem

and

Proof:

\displaystyle P[W\geq w_{i+1}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[1_{\{W\geq f(i)\}}]

(37)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{W}{f(i)}\right)^{\lfloor\frac{i}{\kappa}\rfloor}1_{\{W\geq f(i)\}}\right]

(38)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{W}{f(i)}\right)^{\lfloor\frac{i}{\kappa}\rfloor}1_{\{W\geq f\left(\lfloor\frac{i}{\kappa}\rfloor\right)\}}\right]

(39)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{W}{f(i)}\right)^{\lfloor\frac{i}{\kappa}\rfloor}1_{\{W\leq f\left(\lfloor\frac{i}{\kappa}\rfloor\right)\}}\right]

(40)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{f\left(\lfloor\frac{i}{\kappa}\rfloor\right)}{f(i)}\right)^{\lfloor\frac{i}{\kappa}\rfloor},

(41)

where (

) follows from the assumption that

is non-decreasing; and the equality in (

) follows by using the fact that
P_{W}
satisfies (
24
).
∎

V
Conclusion

This work has addressed the problem of
L^{1}
estimation under Poisson noise, focusing on the Bayesian case. By constructing a novel family of priors, we have demonstrated that any increasing function can serve as an admissible Bayesian estimator, provided certain integrability conditions are met.

In particular, we highlighted a class of priors distinct from the classical gamma distribution, which is known to induce affine conditional means under
L^{2}
loss. These new priors represent a significant departure from the conjugate priors typically used for exponential families and are constructed using a limiting process that guarantees the desired linearity properties.

Future work could explore extensions of this framework to other noise models such as Binomial or Negative Binomial distributions, or to different loss functions.

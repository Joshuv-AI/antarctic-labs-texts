Postbiotic-mediated gut microbiome modulation enhances R-CHOP efficacy in high-risk diffuse large B-cell lymphoma: a case report
Authors: Xiujie Liu, Donghai Cheng, Fangyan Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis case report describes an adult with high-risk DLBCL (IPI score 4) treated with R-CHOP combined with JK5G and JK21 postbiotics for gut microbiome modulation. The patient achieved significant tumor regression while maintaining stable hematological parameters. Microbial analysis demonstrated enrichment of beneficial genera (Veillonella, Bacteroides, Roseburia), potentially linked to postbiotic intervention. Notably, the regimen exhibited excellent tolerability without leukopenia or severe toxicities, suggesting postbiotics may enhance chemotherapy efficacy through microbiome-immune interactions while mitigating adverse effects. These findings warrant further investigation into postbiotics as adjuvant therapy for optimizing DLBCL treatment outcomes.Keywords: Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, R-CHOP chemotherapy, Gut microbiome, Postbiotics, Immunotherapy, Immune modulation

Introduction

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is the most common subtype of non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma, accounting for approximately 30–40% of all cases [1]. Despite advances in treatment, high-risk DLBCL patients—defined by an International Prognostic Index (IPI) score of 3–5—continue to face poor outcomes, with 5-year survival rates as low as 40%[2]. The standard R-CHOP regimen (rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone) remains the cornerstone of DLBCL therapy, yet its efficacy is often limited by treatment resistance and significant toxicity, including myelosuppression and gastrointestinal disturbances [3].

Recent research highlights the gut microbiome’s critical role in modulating cancer therapy outcomes [4]. The gut microbiome, a complex ecosystem of microorganisms, influences immune homeostasis and systemic inflammation, thereby impacting the efficacy of chemotherapy and immunotherapy. Notably, dysbiosis—an imbalance in gut microbial composition—has been associated with reduced treatment response and increased toxicity in cancer patients [5]. Emerging evidence suggests microbiome-targeting interventions like probiotics, prebiotics, and postbiotics may enhance efficacy and reduce adverse events [6].

Postbiotics, bioactive compounds from probiotic metabolism, offer unique advantages, including stability and low infection risk [7]. Preclinical studies have demonstrated that postbiotics can enhance gut barrier function, modulate inflammatory pathways, and potentiate anti-tumor immune responses [8]. However, clinical evidence supporting the use of postbiotics in DLBCL remains limited.

Here, we present a case of an adult with high-risk DLBCL (IPI score 4) who received R-CHOP chemotherapy combined with oral JK5G and JK21 postbiotics. This novel therapeutic strategy aimed to modulate the gut microbiome and enhance immune function, thereby improving treatment outcomes and reducing chemotherapy-related toxicity. Our findings suggest that postbiotic-mediated gut microbiome modulation may represent a promising adjuvant approach for high-risk DLBCL, offering new insights into the gut-immune axis in cancer therapy.

Case presentation

An adult patient presented with night sweats, general fatigue for more than 1 month, and unexplained enlarged inguinal lymph nodes. Physical examination revealed palpable lymph node lesions in the neck and groin region. PET-CT shows multiple enlarged, high-metabolism lymph nodes are present in various locations including the left upper arm, bilateral parotid glands, neck, axillae, right internal mammary region, bilateral hilar and mediastinal areas, bilateral cardiophrenic angles, retroperitoneum, mesentery, pelvic walls (more prominent on the left), left groin, and left lower limb muscle spaces. Additionally, lymphoma infiltration is observed in bones (including bilateral scapulas, clavicles, sternum, multiple ribs, multiple vertebral bodies and annexes, multiple pelvic areas, and right femur), soft tissues around the left lower leg’s medial fibular muscle space and left ankle, left nasopharyngeal wall and posterior roof soft tissues, left tonsil, thyroid, liver segment S4 near the gallbladder fossa, both kidneys, prostate and glans penis, and multiple sites in the small and large intestines. Laboratory tests confirmed the diagnosis of DLBCL by histological analysis of excised lymph node biopsies. Based on age, exercise status, lactate dehydrogenase level, extranidal involvement, and stage, the patient had an IPI score of 4, indicating a poor prognosis.

Given the patient’s high-risk status, a multidisciplinary team decided to implement an adjuvant therapeutic strategy in conjunction with standard R-CHOP chemotherapy. The patient was prescribed oral JK5G and JK21, two novel agents known to modulate gut microbiota and enhance immune function, to be taken concurrently with the chemotherapy for a duration overlapping with the treatment period. This concurrent approach aims to optimize the patient’s overall health status and potentially mitigate chemotherapy-related toxicity.

Treatment and follow-up

Companied with a two-month course of JK5G and JK21, the patient was treated with R-CHOP chemotherapy according to the standard regimen. Throughout the chemotherapy cycle, throughout the entire chemotherapy cycle, rigorous monitoring of the patients’ hematological parameters revealed remarkable stability. There were no clinically significant adverse reactions or toxicities directly attributed to R-CHOP therapy, a notable achievement considering the potential risks associated with such intensive therapy. Specifically, the patients did not experience leukopenia, a common side effect characterized by a decrease in white blood cell count, which can compromise the immune system and increase the risk of infections. Instead, neutrophils, lymphocytes, and other essential immune cell populations remained within normal ranges or swiftly returned to baseline levels, indicating a robust recovery capacity. Mild anemia, if present initially, remained stable without progression, suggesting minimal impact on overall red blood cell function and hemoglobin levels (Table 1). Laboratory analysis of blood biochemistry markers revealed significant and favorable changes. Lactate Dehydrogenase (LDH) and its isoenzymes, along with Hydroxybutyrate Dehydrogenase (HBDH) and Serum Ferritin (SF), which are often elevated in conditions associated with cellular damage or malignancy, decreased markedly and returned to normal ranges, reflecting a positive response to therapy. This reduction underscores the effectiveness of R-CHOP in targeting and reducing the burden of disease. Additionally, Uric Acid (UA) levels also showed a remarkable reduction, indicating improved metabolic homeostasis and potentially reduced risk of complications such as tumor lysis syndrome (Table 1). The assessment of β2-microglobulin, a small protein that can serve as a marker for certain malignancies and may correlate with disease burden, further underscores the success of the chemotherapy regimen. Prior to treatment, the patients’ β2-microglobulin levels were elevated at 4.56 times the upper limit of normal. Following R-CHOP therapy, these levels decreased substantially, dropping to 1.82 times the normal range. This significant reduction is highly indicative of a reduction in the underlying disease activity and tumor load, further validating the effectiveness of the treatment protocol (Table 1).

The microbial community analysis conducted in this study reveals a distinct transformation in the gut microbiome from the pre-chemotherapy state (termed Before) to the post-chemotherapy state influenced by the administration of JK5G and JK21 (termed After). Prior to treatment, the gut microbiome exhibited a diverse array of bacterial species, with Escherichia-Shigella, Fusobacterium, Subdoligranulum, and Klebsiella being particularly prominent. This dominance could be attributed to various factors, including their inherent resilience to the gut environment, dietary influences, and potential interactions with the host’s immune system. However, the administration of chemotherapy in conjunction with JK5G and JK21 significantly altered this bacterial landscape. Specifically, Veillonella, Bacteroides, and Roseburia were found to be greatly enriched after treatment. These three bacteria are recognized as crucial cornerstone bacteria in the intestine, possessing a high capacity for producing propionic acid and butyric acid, which are essential for maintaining gut health. The enrichment of Veillonella, Bacteroides, and Roseburia suggests that JK5G and JK21 played a pivotal role in modulating these changes by fostering the growth of beneficial bacteria and potentially inhibiting the proliferation of opportunistic pathogens. This adjustment led to a more balanced and potentially healthier microbial ecosystem, characterized by an abundance of bacteria that support gut health, immune modulation, and nutrient absorption. Ultimately, this microbial shift mitigated the adverse effects of chemotherapy on the gut microbiome, contributing to a better overall outcome for the patient. While the 16 S rRNA gene sequencing analysis provides valuable insights into the composition and dynamics of the gut microbiome, further in-depth studies are needed to fully elucidate the mechanisms underlying these changes and the specific roles of JK5G and JK21 in modulating the gut microbiome. After the second cycle of R-CHOP chemotherapy, an enhanced CT scan demonstrated a remarkable response to treatment, with the size of multiple enlarged lymph nodes significantly reduced by more than half compared to the pre-treatment baseline PET-CT, achieving partial remission (PR) as per standard criteria (like red circles in Fig. 2). Notably, while R-CHOP alone produced impressive results, there is a hypothesis that incorporating novel agents like JK5G and JK21 into the treatment plan could potentially yield even more pronounced outcomes. These experimental drugs, by targeting distinct molecular pathways, may act synergistically with R-CHOP to disrupt cancer cell survival mechanisms, leading to faster response rates, longer remissions, or even complete remission in some cases. Such a combination therapy could accelerate the patient’s clinical improvement, alleviate symptoms more rapidly, normalize laboratory indicators earlier, and potentially reduce treatment intensity or cycles, ultimately enhancing patient quality of life. However, definitive conclusions on the superiority of this combined approach over R-CHOP alone require further rigorous clinical trials.

Discussion

Our findings suggest that postbiotic-mediated gut microbiome modulation may enhance the efficacy and tolerability of R-CHOP chemotherapy in high-risk DLBCL. The enrichment of Veillonella, Bacteroides, and Roseburia—known for their production of short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs)—likely contributed to improved immune function and reduced chemotherapy toxicity. This aligns with recent studies demonstrating the role of SCFAs in enhancing anti-tumor immunity and mitigating systemic inflammation [9].

Moreover, the stable hematological profile observed in our patient suggests that JK5G and JK21 may protect the bone marrow from chemotherapy-induced damage, potentially through modulation of inflammatory pathways. This is consistent with emerging evidence that postbiotics can enhance gut barrier integrity and reduce bacterial translocation, thereby minimizing systemic inflammatory responses [10].While microbiome analysis offers invaluable insights into health-disease associations, its findings are often constrained by small sample sizes, confounding variables, and technical variability, leading to limited reproducibility and a predominant focus on correlation rather than causation, necessitating standardized protocols, multi-omics integration, and mechanistic validation to enhance scientific rigor.

In the realm of hematologic malignancies, research on postbiotics remains notably scarce, despite the growing recognition of the gut microbiome’s pivotal role in cancer treatment outcomes [11]. While probiotics and prebiotics have been extensively studied in various solid tumors and some hematologic conditions, postbiotics—the bioactive compounds produced by probiotic bacteria—represent a relatively novel and underexplored avenue. Their potential advantages lie in their stability, safety profile, and direct biological activity without the need for live bacterial colonization, which can be particularly relevant in immunocompromised patients undergoing chemotherapy. This scarcity of data in blood cancers underscores the innovative nature of our approach and its potential to open new frontiers in supportive oncology care. In our research, the selection of JK5G and JK21 as specific prebiotic preparations was based on a variety of evidence. JK5G is derived from the metabolic substances of 21 beneficial human lactic acid bacteria strains and 22 inactivated lactic acid bacteria cells, while JK21 is a high-purity metabolic substance of 21 human beneficial lactic acid bacteria strains. Preclinical model studies have shown that they can produce high levels of short-chain fatty acids, especially acetic acid, propionic acid, and butyric acid. These substances are well-known for their anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory effects [12]. For instance, acetic acid is associated with improved energy metabolism and reduced oxidative stress, while propionic acid can regulate the host’s lipid metabolism and exert anti-inflammatory effects [13]. Butyric acid has been proven to enhance the function of regulatory T cells (Tregs) and inhibit pro-inflammatory cytokines, thereby potentially reducing mucosal damage and systemic inflammation caused by chemotherapy [14]. Compared with other prebiotics or probiotics, JK5G and JK21 can provide short-chain fatty acids more specifically and stably, reducing the variability associated with live bacterial colonization and ensuring a more predictable therapeutic effect.

Regarding safety, no serious adverse events were observed during our research process. However, in previous studies, a small number of patients reported mild gastrointestinal symptoms, such as mild abdominal distension and transient diarrhea, especially in the early stage of probiotic treatment. These symptoms are usually self-limiting and do not require discontinuation of the treatment. Monitoring of these events is relatively simple through regular patient interviews and the use of standardized scales to assess stool consistency. Nevertheless, the occurrence of these mild symptoms highlights the importance of close monitoring and patient education in the early stage of probiotic treatment to ensure treatment compliance and manage expectations.

While our case provides promising insights, larger randomized controlled trials are needed to validate these findings and explore the underlying mechanisms. Future research should also investigate the potential of microbiome modulation as a predictive biomarker for treatment outcomes in DLBCL.

Integrating users’ perspectives during the development of the Chronic Pain Recommendation Map: protocol for a qualitative descriptive study
Authors: Ligia Cordovani, Kian Torabiardakani, Lynn Cooper, Stacey A Ritz, Jaris Swidrovich, Ahmad Firas Khalid, Vivian Welch, Gordon Guyatt, Holger Schünemann, Jason W Busse, Andrea J Darzi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionOne in five people experience chronic pain globally, yet management remains suboptimal and discrepant guideline recommendations are common. To increase pain literacy and improve concordance between evidence and practice, we are developing a freely accessible digital Chronic Pain Recommendation Map (e-Chronic Pain RecMap) focusing on three priority areas: opioids, cannabis and spine-related interventional procedures. The aim of this study is to gather participants’ perspectives on the mockups of the RecMap interfaces throughout its development, in terms of design, usability, content relevance and clarity. Feedback will guide iterative refinements of the platform and align the RecMap with user expectations and needs.MethodsWe will use a qualitative descriptive approach to explore participants’ perspectives through online semi-structured interviews. Participants will be purposively sampled to include health researchers, healthcare providers, individuals with living or lived experience of chronic pain and policymakers. Based on information power, we aim to recruit approximately 20 participants, at least 5 participants per target user group, with assessment of sample size adequacy throughout data collection. We will analyse interview transcripts using inductive content analysis and findings will inform iterative refinements to the RecMap’s content and design. To increase methodological rigour, we will apply established qualitative descriptive techniques including detailed description of data analysis, triangulation of investigators, thick data description, dependability, confirmability and reflexivity.Ethics and disseminationThe Hamilton Integrated Research Ethics Board approved this study. We will conduct online interviews and record audio only. After transcription, we will de-identify all collected data and ensure that participants’ names are not linked to any transcripts or study materials. We will share a summary of our final findings with participants and interest-holders via email, and we plan to publish the final manuscript in a peer-reviewed journal.Keywords: Pain management, QUALITATIVE RESEARCH, Protocols & guidelines

Introduction

Chronic pain is a prevalent, costly and complex health issue, affecting 20% of the global population and 7.6 million Canadians.1 2 This figure is projected to reach 9 million by 2030, with combined direct and indirect costs of $C54 billion annually in Canada.2 3 Despite increasing healthcare spending, chronic pain management has remained suboptimal, in part due to poor concordance between evidence and practice.4,8

Clinical practice guidelines (CPGs) play a fundamental role in supporting healthcare decisions and are central to the Knowledge-to-Action framework.9 However, recommendations addressing chronic pain often diverge. For example, in 2022, the US Department of Defense/Veterans Affairs CPG strongly recommended against opioids for chronic pain, whereas the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention guideline stated a trial of opioids should be considered if expected benefits for pain and function are anticipated to outweigh risks to the patient.10 11 Such inconsistencies may stem from differences including evidence considered, methods of analysis, conflict-of-interest management, valuation of patient preferences and the complexity involving vulnerable groups of people that bear disproportionate burdens of chronic pain.12

We are developing a Canadian Institutes of Health Research-funded Chronic Pain Recommendation Map (e-Chronic Pain RecMap) to help identify trustworthy guidelines.13 This product will include tailored interfaces for researchers, healthcare providers, people with lived and living experience and policymakers.13 Recommendation Mapping (RecMap) offers a potential solution to resolving discrepant guidelines by visually arranging CPG recommendations in an interactive matrix. The interface allows users to identify areas of agreement or conflict, inspect certainty ratings, view equity and contextual notes and link directly to full guideline content.9 14 Early evaluations suggest RecMaps reduce cognitive load and facilitate comparison of recommendations across multiple, and often conflicting clinical practice guidelines.9 14

To enhance the RecMap’s practical usefulness and to meet the needs of its target users, incorporating users’ perspectives through co-production and iterative user testing is essential. Evidence from user-centred design research shows that decision-support tools developed with end-users, through cycles of prototyping, usability testing and refinement, can support higher adoption, usability and sustained impact.15 Early engagement of the RecMap’s users may also reveal workflow constraints, accessibility needs and equity considerations. Participatory methods (ie, active participation of interest holders throughout the development process) also help ensure that language, navigation and filtering options accommodate diverse literacy levels and decision contexts.16

Purpose statement and research question

This qualitative study aims to collect participants’ perspectives on the initial mockups for each interface of the e-Chronic Pain RecMap13 that are being produced by Evidence Prime, the platform’s software-development partner.17 Participants’ perspectives will inform the refinement of interfaces prior to their final development and launch. The initial mockups’ drafts will be informed by preliminary discussions with the RecMap’s nine-member steering committee (AJD, LyC, SAR, JS, AFK, VW, GG, HS and JWB), collectively representing expertise in health research methodology (AJD, AFK, VW, GG, HS and JWB), equity, diversity and inclusion (AJD, VW and JS), sex and gender (SAR), an Indigenous perspective (JS), lived and living experience with chronic pain (LyC) and health policy decision-making (AFK).

Our interactive co-production process will start with internal meetings with our international advisory board members, that are detailed in online supplemental file 1, to receive initial informal feedback on layout, navigation and information presentation. We will revise the mockups based on feedback before we start conducting usability one-on-one, semi-structured interviews with members of the public, including health researchers, healthcare providers, people with lived and living experiences and their support members and policymakers. Finally, our steering committee along with Evidence Prime will review the findings from the interviews and agree on final content and visual updates for the RecMap interfaces.

We intend to investigate participants’ perspectives on the design of the interfaces (overall and visual), on the usability of the interfaces (ease of navigation and ability to locate specific information and successfully complete intended actions, such as comparing recommendations), and on the display content (clarity, relevance and completeness).

Our overarching research question for this study is as follows: What are the areas for improvement on the e-Chronic Pain RecMap platform, identified by healthcare providers, health researchers, individuals with living or lived experience and policymakers, that can better align the platform with users’ expectations and needs?

Methodology

Qualitative design

We will use the qualitative description approach described by Sandelowski.18 This approach is less interpretive than other qualitative designs (eg, interpretive description)18; therefore, it seeks to collect straight descriptions of participants’ perspectives, meaning that the findings stay as close as possible to participants’ statements.18 In this study, the realities of participants will be reflected through their perspectives on the e-Chronic Pain RecMap interfaces.

Theoretical framework

Our approach to the development of the RecMap is anchored on the concept of evidence mapping and cataloguing available research, highlighting gaps and presenting results in formats that users can readily navigate.19 Recommendation mapping provides information within a population-by-intervention matrix, revealing areas of divergence, identifying trustworthy recommendations and highlighting knowledge gaps and research priorities.14

The inconsistencies across chronic pain guidelines, specifically regarding opioids, cannabis and spine-related interventional procedures, make such comparative displays essential for clarity and informed decision-making.2 10 11 13 This theoretical lens will be taken into consideration to shape both the RecMap interface and the usability questions we will pose to end-users.

Philosophical foundations

We will adopt a constructivist worldview for our study, which assumes that multiple realities are constructed by individuals as they engage with the world.20 21 This worldview is particularly important when developing online health platforms, as individuals’ experiences with digital health technologies are shaped in part by intersecting factors including digital literacy, internet access and socioeconomic resources. Our ontological position relies on relativism (ie, reality is subjective and varies from person to person), and our epistemological position is based on subjectivism (ie, how individuals understand the phenomenon and the role the researcher plays in the research process). This constructivist lens will be essential as we explore how different users experience and interact with our platform, identifying both facilitators and barriers to engagement across diverse contexts and populations. To support our constructivist axiological position, all researchers participating in the interview process (LiC, KT and AJD) will engage in reflexive journaling to surface preconceptions and analytical decisions. We will present our constructivist rhetoric through a narrative writing style when describing participants’ perspectives and employ terminology consistent with qualitative inquiry.

Setting and participants

Our study will be coordinated by the Michael G DeGroote National Pain Centre at McMaster University (Canada); however, we will recruit participants internationally, as our freely accessible e-Chronic Pain RecMap is intended for global use. Data collection is planned to begin in April 2026 and is expected to be completed by March 2027. Eligible participants are adults (>18 years old), who can speak and read English, able to provide informed consent, and self-report as a health researcher, healthcare provider, person with lived or living experience of chronic pain or policymaker. We will reflect on how the study’s institutional affiliation with the Michael G DeGroote National Pain Centre at McMaster University and our participant eligibility criteria may shape participants’ perspectives. We will explicitly discuss these contextual influences when interpreting our findings.

Recruitment

We will recruit participants through national and international organisations that have agreed to support our efforts (online supplemental file 1). Also, we will send targeted email invitations to individuals affiliated with chronic pain research, healthcare delivery, advocacy networks and policymaking, and post social media advertisements on X (formerly known as Twitter) and LinkedIn. We will make deliberate efforts to recruit participants from low- and middle-income countries through our international collaborating organisations and networks to ensure the consideration of diverse perspectives, including those from the global south. Interested participants will contact our team by email if they wish to participate. In cases where participants suggest additional contacts (ie, snowball sampling), the research assistant will email those referrals directly to check eligibility. We will thank each participant for their time with an electronic gift card. We acknowledge that despite our efforts to recruit internationally, structural barriers including English language requirements, internet access and time zone differences may limit participation from the global south and these limitations will be considered when interpreting our findings.

Sampling

We will use non-probabilistic, purposive sampling, as our aim is to generate information-rich insights to support the development of the e-Chronic Pain RecMap platform, rather than achieve generalisable results.22 We will use purposive sampling to recruit diverse groups of users, including health researchers, healthcare providers, individuals with living or lived experience of chronic pain and policymakers.22 Where feasible, we will seek to include diverse perspectives within each subgroup, including age, gender, geographical location (including participants from the global south) and from groups experiencing inequities. To identify additional participants and broaden our reach, we will rely on snowball sampling.22

In qualitative studies, the sample size has been a topic of discussion among researchers.23,25 We will base our sample size on the concept of information power to guide us on gathering enough rich data to answer our research question.24 This model for assessment of sample size indicates that 20–30 interviews would typically provide sufficient depth. To capture diverse perspectives, we will enrol at least five participants per target user group (ie, health researchers, healthcare providers, individuals living or with lived experience of chronic pain and policymakers) for a minimum total of 20 participants. Throughout data collection we will continuously assess the richness and relevance of the data and whether further interviews are needed.

Data collection

We will collect data by conducting semi-structured, one-on-one interviews via Zoom with eligible participants.26 During each interview, we will share with the participant a dynamic clickable prototype of their specific interface so that they can explore the RecMap in real time and provide immediate feedback. Online supplemental file 2 provides our semi-structured interview guide. The guide will be piloted for feasibility and acceptability with two example end-users who are not part of the study sample.

Before the interview starts, we will obtain written informed consent and record demographic data including age, gender and location (province/territory/state and country), to describe the sample and acknowledge the limitations of our sample. We will also gather information about participants’ experiences and roles related to chronic pain, including relevant clinical conditions, interventions, research areas or policymaking experience, to appropriately characterise our sample and acknowledge potential limitations. We will report these attributes descriptively.

A single investigator (LiC) will conduct every interview and verify the accuracy of Zoom generated transcripts. Investigators AJD and KT will participate as collaborators to answer any technical questions that participants might have related to the RecMap platform, and to provide feedback to LiC on each meeting. We will store de-identified transcripts on secure, password-protected servers and we will remove all names and demographic identifiers to maintain confidentiality. To facilitate the reporting of representative quotes, we will assign anonymised participant codes.

Data analysis

We (LiC, AJD, JWB and KT) will analyse the data (ie, interviews and transcripts) using conventional (inductive) qualitative content analysis, an approach27,29 that is commonly used to analyse text data and organise words into different categories based on their meanings, without preconceived concepts.28 30 We will conduct data entry and analysis concurrently to determine further data collection. We will immerse ourselves in the data, allowing insights that flow from the data. We will disassemble transcripts and notes from the interviews sentence by sentence, with notes and initial codes memoed throughout. Guided by the data, we will develop categories and themes while constantly comparing new information to previous information. Codes will only be counted to better understand patterns for further analysis and interpretation, but not as a presentation of numerical results.31 An experienced single coder (LiC) will lead the analysis and engage the team in reflexive discussion rather than seeking consensus (figure 1).32

Rigour

We will use strategies to enhance rigour using techniques specifically proposed for studies using a qualitative descriptive approach.33,35 To increase credibility, we will describe the data and analysis procedures in detail and use investigator triangulation, a strategy that involves multiple researchers in collecting and/or analysing data.33 In our study, investigator triangulation means that we aim at team collaboration. Instead of trying to reach coding consensus (eg, using coding reliability approaches), one of the investigators (LiC) will share her initial findings with the research group (AJD, KT and JWB) to get their insights. To facilitate transferability, we will provide thick descriptions, data will be described enough to allow readers to make meaning of the participants’ opinions, behaviour and context. This way, readers can judge the extent to which our results can be applied to other settings. Also, in terms of dependability and confirmability, we will document each research step transparently and invite peer examination (from authors) of both process and findings (ie, discussion of the research process and findings with colleagues). As for reflexivity, throughout the study, we will critically identify, reflect on, and examine how our positionality, experiences, beliefs and perceptions of the phenomenon under study influence both our research process and findings. We will use the Standards for Reporting Qualitative Research (SRQR) to guide our qualitative reporting.36

Patient and public involvement

Community engagement is central to this study. Diverse members were involved in the design of the RecMap platform’s first drafts including health research methodologists (AJD, AFK, VW, GG, HS and JWB), experts in equity, diversity and inclusion including sex and gender (AJD, VW, JS and SAR), an Indigenous perspective lead (JS), a person living with chronic pain (LyC) and a health policy decision-making expert (AFK). Patients, healthcare providers, researchers and policymakers that are recruited throughout this study will provide feedback that will be considered when revising the RecMap interfaces. Three engagement leads (two researchers (AJD and VW) and our person with lived experience partner (LyC) who also serves on the RecMap steering committee) will coordinate patient and public involvement (PPI) and liaise with the study team. All have experience supporting participation among individuals with accessibility and communication needs.

Our PPI strategy aims to ensure that our research findings respond to the needs of our population of study. Early PPI consultations informed several protocol elements, including plain language interfaces and the structure of the different interface mock-ups that will be used including the presentation of the guideline information. This partnership will continue throughout data collection, analysis and dissemination to ensure the RecMap remains relevant, inclusive and user-centred.

Ethics and dissemination

The Hamilton Integrated Research Ethics Board (HiREB) approved this study (HiREB #18533). We will take measures to minimise risks and discomfort during data collection. As noted, we will conduct interviews on Zoom, but record audio only. To maximise comfort, participants can choose to keep their camera on or off or opt for an alternative interview arrangement (eg, telephone). The Zoom platform is an externally hosted cloud-based service approved by our research ethics board for use in this study.26 However, as with all data collected on external servers, we recognise a small residual risk of privacy breach remains and we will explain this in our information letter. Also, we will remove all identifiers from transcripts and store de-identified data in a secure McMaster One Drive folder. Participants may withdraw from the study at any point before we include their data in the analysis and remove all the identifiers. In the final manuscript, we will fully de-contextualise quotes to preserve anonymity.

To support dissemination, we will provide participants with a concise summary of the final findings via email, submit a manuscript to a peer-reviewed journal and present results at the 2027 Guidelines International Network Conference. Additionally, to maintain transparency, we will develop an infographic summarising key usability findings and recommended changes that will be posted on the Michael G DeGroote National Pain Centre, on the RecMap platform and shared with our interest-holder organisations.

Magnetic Equivariant Graded Brauer Group
Higinio Serrano, Bernardo Uribe

Abstract
. Given a magnetic finite group, we consider the similarity classes of magnetic equivariant central simple graded algebras over the complex numbers. We call this set the magnetic equivariant graded Brauer group and its structure as an abelian group is explicitly determined. Following Karoubi, we argue that the elements of this graded Brauer group parametrize the twistings of the magnetic equivariant K-theory of a point.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification

(primary) 16K50, 19L50, (secondary) 16S35

Introduction

Magnetic representations of groups are complex representations
where the elements of the group act either complex linearly or complex antilinearly. Fixing the elements of the group that act complex antilinearly, we obtain a magnetic group. This is done with a surjective homomorphism
\phi:G\to\mathbb{Z}/2
which encodes whether the elements act linearly or antilinearly. The study of the representations of magnetic groups was carried by Wigner
in his seminal book
[
28
]
, and the classification on 3 types (
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{C}
or
\mathbb{H}
) of irreducible representations was understood through the structure of the algebra of equivariant endomorphisms of the irreducible representations.

The classification of magnetic equivariant
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded representations was done by Dyson
[
9
]
and a ten-fold classification
was thus obtained. Around the same time, the generalization of the Brauer group of Morita equivalence classes of central simple algebras over a fixed field to the case of central simple graded algebras was carried out by Wall
[
27
]
, and the graded Brauer group of a field was born. The graded Brauer group of the reals was shown to be a cyclic group of order eight, and the one of the complex numbers of order two.

It was clear at the time that the Wedderburn-Artin theorem, on its graded version
[
26
, Thm. 6.2.5]
, was the key ingredient to relate both approaches. The equivariant endomorphisms of an irreducible magnetic graded representation has the structure of a graded division algebra
[
9
]
, while central simple graded algebras
are isomorphic to graded matrix algebras over graded division algebras
by the Wedderburn-Artin theorem.

On the other hand, the endomorphism algebra of a graded magnetic representation
is a magnetic equivariant central simple graded algebra, and as such, it defines an element in the associated graded Brauer group. This Brauer group, which we coined
magnetic equivariant graded graded Brauer group
, is the main study topic in this work.

The equivariant Brauer group of a field was introduced by Fröhlich
[
12
]
and its graded extension was introduced by Riehm
[
23
]
. Here we study central simple graded algebras over
\mathbb{C}
endowed with a magnetic
G
-action of algebra automorphisms; we call these objects magnetic equivariant central simple graded algebras. Two of these algebras are called similar if after tensoring with the endomorphism algebra of degree
0
magnetic representations they become
G
-equivariant isomorphic algebras. The similarity classes of these algebras is a finite group by the tensor product structure and it is our desired magnetic equivariant graded Brauer group.

The main result of this work is Thm.
2.7
where we present an explicit calculation of the abelian group structure of the magnetic equivariant graded Brauer group. This is the following isomorphism:

\displaystyle\mathrm{GrBr}_{(G,\phi)}(\mathbb{C})\ {\cong}\Big(H^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{*}_{\phi})\overset{\bullet}{\times}\mathrm{Hom}(G,\mathbb{Z}/2)\Big)\overset{\bullet}{\times}\mathbb{Z}/2

where the symbols
\overset{\bullet}{\times}
denotes twisted products by explicit 2-cocycles.

We compare our calculation with the previously known calculations of the graded equivariant Brauer groups of
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{C}
, and we highlight some of their similarities and differences. We present explicit calculations of the magnetic equivariant graded Brauer groups noting the
key differences whenever the homomorphism
\phi:G\to\mathbb{Z}/2
does not split.

We finish our work with an application of the magnetic equivariant graded Brauer groups in the classification of twists in magnetic equivariant K-theory
[
25
]
. We follow Karoubi’s approach to study K-theory, and we argue that the magnetic equivariant graded Brauer group parametrizes the twists in the magnetic equivariant K-theory.
We show that the degree shift isomorphism in magnetic equivariant K-theory follows from a property of the magnetic equivariant graded Brauer group, and we further
show that the 4-periodicity in certain magnetic equivariant K-theories follow
from the particular structure of the associated magnetic equivariant graded Brauer groups.

We include in the first section Table
1
where we present a summary of the results in the theory of graded Brauer groups over
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{C}
, in the classification of real division graded algebras, and in the appearance of the ten-fold way in the study of symmetries of periodic Hamiltonians. The explicit algebras defined in the first section are the main characters appearing in the latter sections.

1.
Graded Brauer groups and the ten-fold way

In this section we are going to explore the relation between the graded Brauer groups of the reals and the complex numbers, the set of real graded central division algebras, and the ten-fold classification of non-interacting fermionic Hamiltonians.

The famous Wedderburn-Artin theorem, in its graded version
[
26
, Thm. 6.2.5]
,
implies that any central simple graded algebra over a fixed field is a matrix
algebra over a graded division algebra over the field.
Therefore, the classification of central simple graded algebras can be reduced to the classification of graded division algebras.

Two central simple graded algebras are called similar if they are matrix algebras over the same graded division algebra, and the similarity classes of simple central graded algebras is called the graded Brauer group. It becomes an abelian group because the tensor product of simple central graded algebras is also a central simple graded algebra.

In the case of the reals and the complex numbers the graded Brauer groups are respectively
\mathbb{Z}/8
and
\mathbb{Z}/2
. The real and complex Clifford algebras can be taken as representatives of the similarity classes and the periodicity of the Clifford algebras is the famous Bott periodicity.

The ten possibilities of central simple graded algebras have also appeared in the physics literature in different disguises. Perhaps the first appearance was Dyson classification of symmetries of Quantum systems
[
9
]
, while a more recent one is the famous Altland-Zirnabauer (AZ) classification of free-fermion periodic Hamiltonians
[
3
]
. This classification is also known as the "Ten-fold way" and has been masterfully reinterpreted by Kitaev in terms of the classification of Clifford algebras
[
20
]
.

Here we will summarize the classifications mentioned above and we will present a simple description that allows us to relate the graded Brauer groups over
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{C}
, the graded division algebras over
\mathbb{R}
, and the AZ classification scheme.
The intermediary group that will allow us to relate all these objects is the graded Brauer group of simple central graded complex algebras endowed with a
\mathbb{C}
-antilinear automorphism. The main results of this section are summarized in Table
1
.

\displaystyle C_{A}(B)):=\{a\in A\colon ab=ba\ \mathrm{for}\ \mathrm{all}\ b\in B\}

Recall that two homogeneous elements

The

If

\displaystyle M_{p|q}(k):=\mathrm{End}(k^{p},k^{q}).

(1.4)

\displaystyle A\hat{\otimes}\mathrm{End}(V_{0},V_{1})\cong B\hat{\otimes}\mathrm{End}(W_{0},W_{1})

(1.5)

\displaystyle\mathrm{GrBr}(k):=\mathrm{CSGA}(k)/\sim.

The graded tensor product makes

(1.6)

\displaystyle A^{\mathrm{op}}=\{a^{\mathrm{op}}\colon a\in A\},\ \ a^{\mathrm{op}}b^{\mathrm{op}}:=(-1)^{\partial a\partial b}(ab)^{\mathrm{op}}

A\hat{\otimes}B\cong B\hat{\otimes}A
with
a\otimes b\mapsto(-1)^{\partial a\partial b}b\otimes a
.

The cases of interest for us are when the field is
\mathbb{R}

\mathbb{C}

(1.7)

\displaystyle\mathrm{GrBr}(\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{Z}/8\ \ \ \&\ \ \ \mathrm{GrBr}(\mathbb{C})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2,

(1.8)

\displaystyle C^{p,q}_{\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{R}\langle e_{1},..,e_{p+q}|e_{i}e_{j}=-e_{j}e_{i},e_{j}^{2}=Q(e_{j})\rangle

\mathbb{R}
and satisfy several properties:

\displaystyle C^{p,q}_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{\otimes}C^{r,s}_{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle\cong C^{p+r,q+s}_{\mathbb{R}}\

\displaystyle\ C^{1,1}_{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})

(1.10)

\displaystyle C^{p,q+8}_{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle\cong C^{p+8,q}_{\mathbb{R}}\

\displaystyle\ C^{0,8}_{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{R}^{16},\mathbb{R}^{16}).

(1.11)

\displaystyle\mathrm{GrBr}(\mathbb{R})=\langle[C^{0,1}_{\mathbb{R}}]\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}/8.

For the complex numbers there are only two similarity classes and we have the isomorphisms:

(1.12)

\displaystyle\mathrm{GrBr}(\mathbb{C})=\langle[C^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}]\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}/2.

Here

1.2.
Algebras over
\mathbb{C}
with antilinear automorphism

The field of real numbers can be also understood as the conjugation invariant field of the complex numbers. With this approach we could
see the category of finite dimensional vector spaces over
\mathbb{R}

as equivalent to the category of finite dimensional vector spaces
over
\mathbb{C}
endowed with a
\mathbb{C}
-antilinear involution.

If
\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{R})
denotes the category of finite dimensional
\mathbb{R}
-vector spaces, and
\mathrm{Vect}_{(\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathrm{id})}(\mathbb{C})

denotes the category of finite dimensional
\mathbb{C}

endowed with an action of

[
15

implies that
the functors:

(1.13)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\to\mathrm{Vect}_{(\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathrm{id})}(\mathbb{C})

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vect}_{(\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathrm{id})}(\mathbb{C})

\displaystyle\to\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{R})

(1.14)

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle\mapsto V\underset{\mathbb{R}}{\otimes}\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle\mapsto W^{\mathbb{Z}/2}

provides the equivalence of the categories.

The complex tensor product makes
\mathrm{Vect}_{(\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathrm{id})}(\mathbb{C})

into a tensor category, and an algebra object in this category
is nothing else but an algebra
A
over the complex numbers endowed with a
\mathbb{C}
-antilinear automorphism
\tau:A\overset{\cong}{\to}A
. Two complex algebras
endowed with
\mathbb{C}
-antilinear automorphisms are isomorphic if they
are isomorphic as complex algebras and the isomorphism is equivariant
with respect to the automorphisms.

We call graded algebras over
\mathbb{C}
with antilinear automorphism
GA
{}_{(\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathrm{id})}(\mathbb{C})
, and accordingly, we
may take the central and simple complex algebras with
\mathbb{C}
-antilinear automorphism CSGA
{}_{(\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathrm{id})}(\mathbb{C})
.

Two algebras
(A,\tau_{A})
and
(B,\tau_{B})
in CSGA
{}_{(\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathrm{id})}(\mathbb{C})
are similar
if there is an isomorphism of complex algebras:

(1.15)

\displaystyle A\hat{\otimes}\mathrm{End}(V_{0},V_{1})\cong B\hat{\otimes}\mathrm{End}(W_{0},W_{1})

compatible with the induced
\mathbb{C}
-antilinear automorphisms on each side. Here the automorphism on the endomorphism algebra

\mathrm{End}(V_{0},V_{1})
is given by the adjoint map of the complex conjugation:

(1.16)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ad}_{\mathbb{K}}:\mathrm{End}(V_{0},V_{1})

\displaystyle\to\mathrm{End}(V_{0},V_{1})

(1.17)

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle\mapsto\mathbb{K}\rho\mathbb{K},

which on vectors act in the following form
v\mapsto\mathbb{K}\rho(\mathbb{K}v)
. Here
\mathbb{K}
denotes complex conjugation.

The equivalence classes of similar algebras in CSGA
{}_{(\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathrm{id})}(\mathbb{C})
is the Graded Brauer groups and will be denoted
as:

(1.18)

\displaystyle\mathrm{GrBr}_{(\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathrm{id})}(\mathbb{C}).

The following proposition follows from Speiser’s lemma
[
15
, Lem. 2.3.8]
and is a key result in our generalization of the Graded Brauer group for magnetic groups.

Proposition 1.1
.

There is a canonical isomorphism of groups
\mathrm{GrBr}(\mathbb{R})\cong\mathrm{GrBr}_{(\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathrm{id})}(\mathbb{C})
realized by the maps:

(1.19)

\displaystyle\mathrm{GrBr}(\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\to\mathrm{GrBr}_{(\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathrm{id})}(\mathbb{C})

\displaystyle\mathrm{GrBr}_{(\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathrm{id})}(\mathbb{C})

\displaystyle\to\mathrm{GrBr}(\mathbb{R})

(1.20)

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\mapsto(A\underset{\mathbb{R}}{\otimes}\mathbb{C},\mathrm{Ad}_{\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathbb{K}})

\displaystyle(A,\tau)

\displaystyle\mapsto A^{\tau}.

Here
A^{\tau}
denotes the fixed points of the automorphism and
\mathbb{K}
denotes complex conjugation.

Proof.

The natural map
\lambda:A^{\tau}\underset{\mathbb{R}}{\otimes}\mathbb{C}\to A
,
\lambda(a\otimes\mu)=\mu a
, is a
\mathbb{C}
-linear map
that provides the isomorphism of complex vector spaces; this is
Speiser’s lemma
[
15
, Lem. 2.3.8]

This isomorphism is equivariant with the
\mathbb{C}
-antilinear isomorphisms of the algebras:

(1.21)

\displaystyle\lambda(\mathrm{Ad}_{\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathbb{K}}(a\otimes\mu))=\lambda(\rm{id}\otimes\mathbb{K}(a\otimes\mu)\rm{id}\otimes\mathbb{K})=\lambda(a\otimes\bar{\mu})=\bar{\mu}a=\tau(\mu a)=\tau(\lambda(a\otimes\mu)).

∎

Another tenfold classification structure was shown by Dyson
[
9
]
when studying the symmetries of Hamiltonianis
in nuclear physics, where the threefold classification of complex linear representations was generalized to a tenfold classification whenever
the representations possess both complex linear and complex antilinear
symmetry transformations. A more recent appearance of this tenfold
structure
is the Altland-Zirnbauer (AZ) scheme classification of free-fermion Hamiltonians
[
3

, a classification which was masterfully rewritten
by Kitaev
[
20

in terms of the 2 types of complex Clifford algebras and the 8 types of
real Clifford algebras with the name of "Periodic table for topological insulators and superconductors".

Let us recall the basic ideas in the AZ classification
[
3
]
of non-interacting fermionic Hamiltonians in terms of ten symmetry classes. These are classified in terms of the presence or
absence of certain discrete symmetries on a prescribed Hamiltonian
[
24
, §II]
.

Suppose that this Hamiltonian encodes the electromagnetic properties of
some crystal and take its eigenvectors and its spectrum. Assume that the Fermi level (energy of occupied bands) is set at zero and denote
by
valence bands
the vector space generated by the negative energy eigenvectors, and
conduction bands
the vector space generated by
positive energy eigenvectors.

Assume furthermore that the Hamiltonian is gapped at the Fermi level,
thus modeling an insulating material, and permitting to have a well defined
vector space of valence and conduction bands.

Assign degree 0 to the valence bands and degree 1 to the conduction bands.
This way the vector space spanned by the eigenvectors of the Hamiltonian becomes a graded vector space.

A Hamiltonian preserves
time reversal symmetry
(TRS) if there is a

\mathbb{C}
-antilinear operator
\mathcal{T}
, such that:

(1.47)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}\mathcal{H}\mathcal{T}^{-1}=\mathcal{H},\ \mathrm{with}\ \mathcal{T}=U_{\mathcal{T}}\mathbb{K}\ \mathrm{and}\ \mathcal{T}^{2}=\pm 1

where
U_{\mathcal{T}}
is a unitary operator (
U_{\mathcal{T}}^{-1}=U_{\mathcal{T}}^{\dagger}
). The fact that the operator is
\mathbb{C}
-antilinear induces two non-equivalent possibilities for the operator
\mathcal{T}^{2}
as was shown by Wigner
[
28
]
.
If the Hamiltonian preserves TRS, then the TRS operator
\mathcal{T}

acts on the graded vector space spanned by the eigenvectors of the Hamiltonian as a degree 0 operator.

A Hamiltonian preserves
particle hole symmetry
(PHS) if there is a

\mathbb{C}
-antilinear operator
\mathcal{C}
, such that:

(1.48)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\mathcal{H}\mathcal{C}^{-1}=-\mathcal{H},\ \mathrm{with}\ \mathcal{C}=U_{\mathcal{C}}\mathbb{K}\ \mathrm{and}\ \mathcal{C}^{2}=\pm 1

where
U_{\mathcal{C}}
is a unitary operator (
U_{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}=U_{\mathcal{C}}^{\dagger}
). If the Hamiltonian preserve PHS, then the operator
\mathcal{C}
exchanges occupied states with unoccupied ones, or particles with antiparticles (holes), inverting the charge.
It changes the sign of the energy of an eigenstate of the Hamiltonian, and therefore acts on the graded vector space spanned by the eigenvectors of the Hamiltonian as a degree 1 operator.

If a Hamiltonian preserves both TRS and PHS, then it preserves the composition
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{T}\mathcal{C}
. This is a
\mathbb{C}
-linear transformation, which after rescaling can be made to satisfy
\mathcal{S}^{2}=1
. This symmetry is called
chiral symmetry
and satisfies:

(1.49)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\mathcal{H}\mathcal{S}^{-1}=-\mathcal{H},\ \mathrm{with}\ \mathcal{S}=U_{\mathcal{S}}\ \mathrm{and}\ \mathcal{S}^{2}=1

with
U_{\mathcal{S}}
a unitary operator (
U_{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}=U_{\mathcal{S}}^{\dagger}
). The chiral symmetry operator
\mathcal{S}
becomes a degree 1 operator
exchanging the valence and the conduction bands without inverting the charge. Note that a Hamiltonian
may possess chiral symmetry without possessing TRS or PHS.

The ten different combinations of the three symmetries is known as the AZ
classification scheme and it appears in Table

if a symmetry is present, a

2.
Magnetic Equivariant Graded Brauer group

In the study of the electromagnetic properties
of periodic free-fermion Hamiltonians associated to crystals, the crystal symmetries play a fundamental role. Whenever the crystal has a non-trivial internal magnetization, there might be symmetries of the Hamiltonian which are the composition of either TRS, PHS or chiral symmetry with the geometrical symmetries of the crystal. This gives rise to the concept of magnetic groups, where the elements of the group may act complex linearly or complex antilinearly
[
25
]
.

In this section we are going to study magnetic equivariant simple central complex algebras and we are going to explicitly calculate its similarity classes. This group will be called the
magnetic equivariant graded Brauer group.

Now, the magnetic and equivariant version of the Graded Brauer group incorporates results of magnetic and equivariant projective representations. Therefore, prior to studying the Brauer group we will
review some results in the theory of non abelian cohomology and in the theory of magnetic projective representations. The we will introduce the magnetic equivariant graded Brauer group associated to a magnetic group, and we provide an explicit calculation of this group in terms of cohomology of groups; this is Thm.
2.7
and it is the main result of this section.
Then we will relate this Brauer group with the graded equivariant Brauer groups of real and complex algebras and we calculate some explicit examples.

2.1.
Non-Abelian cohomology

Let us recall the definition of the first cohomology pointed set
H^{1}(G,A)
when
A
is a group which is not necessarily abelian.

Let
G
be a group and
A
other group with a left action of
G
by automorphisms. Denote by:

(2.1)

Z^{1}(G,A)

the set of all maps
f:G\to A
such that:

(2.2)

f(gh)=f(g)\left(g\cdot f(h)\right)

where
f(g)
times
g\cdot f(h)
uses the multiplication in
A
and
g\cdot f(h)
is given by the action of
G
on
A
. Note that
A
acts on
Z^{1}(G,A)
by conjugation:

(2.3)

(a\cdot f)(g):=af(g)(g\cdot(a^{-1}))

where
a\in A
,
f\in Z^{1}(G,A)
and
g\in G
. In fact we have the equalities:

\displaystyle(a\cdot f)(gh)=

\displaystyle af(gh)\left((gh)\cdot(a^{-1})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle af(g)(g\cdot f(h))((gh)\cdot(a^{-1}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle af(g)(g\cdot a)(g\cdot a^{-1})(g\cdot f(h))((gh)\cdot(a^{-1}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\cdot f)(g)\left(g\cdot(a^{-1}f(h)(h\cdot(a^{-1})))\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\cdot f)(g)(g\cdot((a\cdot f)(h))),

thus showing that
(a\cdot f)\in Z^{1}(G,A)
.

Define the first cohomology set:

(2.4)

H^{1}(G,A):=Z^{1}(G,A)/A

as the set of equivalent classes of
Z^{1}(G,A)
modulo the action of
A
. The constant 1-cocycle

g\mapsto 1

is called the base point of
H^{1}(G,A)
. Clearly, if
A
is abelian, then
H^{1}(G,A)
is the usual first cohomology group of
G
with coefficients in
A
. Now, let us recall a fundamental result from
[
15
]
about exact sequences of pointed sets. A sequence of pointed sets:

(2.5)

(A,a)\overset{f}{\longrightarrow}(B,b)\overset{g}{\longrightarrow}(C,c)

is exact at
(B,b)
if
g^{-1}(c)
, the kernel of
g
, is equal to the image of
f
.

From
[
15
, Prop. 2.7.1 & 4.4.1]
we quote:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a group and

(2.6)

1\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow B\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow 1

an exact sequence of groups equipped with
G
-actions and with
G
-equivariant homomorphisms. Then
there is an induced exact sequence of pointed sets:

(2.7)

1\rightarrow A^{G}\rightarrow B^{G}\rightarrow C^{G}\rightarrow H^{1}(G,A)\rightarrow H^{1}(G,B)\rightarrow H^{1}(G,C).

Moreover, if
A
is abelian and it is contained in the center of
B
, then the induced exact sequence of pointed sets extends one term to the right:

(2.8)

1\rightarrow A^{G}\rightarrow B^{G}\rightarrow C^{G}\rightarrow H^{1}(G,A)\rightarrow H^{1}(G,B)\rightarrow H^{1}(G,C)\rightarrow H^{2}(G,A).

Suppose
A
is the direct limit of a
G
-directed set
(A_{k},\psi_{k}:A_{k}\to A_{k+1})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that for each
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
there exist a map:

(2.9)

\Gamma_{ij}:A_{i}\times A_{j}\longrightarrow A_{i+j}

such that:

•

\Gamma_{ij}(g\cdot a,g\cdot b)=g\cdot\Gamma_{ij}(a,b)
for all
g\in G
,
a\in A_{i}
and
b\in A_{j}
.

•

\Gamma_{ij}(aa^{\prime},bb^{\prime})=\Gamma_{ij}(a,b)\cdot\Gamma_{ij}(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
for all
a,a^{\prime}\in A_{i}
and
b,b^{\prime}\in A_{j}
.

•

\Gamma_{ij}
is compatible with
\psi_{i}
and
\psi_{j}
.

In this case we can define a product on
H^{1}(G,A)
as follows.
Define the product on the set of 1-cocycles by the formula:

\displaystyle Z^{1}(G,A_{i})\times Z^{1}(G,A_{j})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow Z^{1}(G,A_{i+j})

(2.10)

\displaystyle(f_{0},f_{1})

\displaystyle\longmapsto f_{0}f_{1}\hskip 28.45274pt(f_{0}f_{1})(a)=\Gamma_{ij}(f_{0}(a),f_{1}(a)),

and it can be shown that this product commutes with the action of
G
, thus inducing a well-defined product in cohomology:

(2.11)

\displaystyle H^{1}(G,A)\times H^{1}(G,A)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow H^{1}(G,A).

2.2.
Magnetic equivariant projective representations.

Recall that the magnetic general linear group
\mathrm{MGL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is the semidirect product:

(2.12)

\displaystyle\mathrm{MGL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}):=\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2

where
\mathbb{Z}/2
acts by conjugation on the matrices of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. Explicitly the product is:

(2.13)

\displaystyle(\Lambda_{0},\tau_{0})(\Lambda_{1},\tau_{1})=(\Lambda_{0}\ {\mathbb{K}}^{\tau_{0}}\Lambda_{1},\tau_{0}\tau_{1}),\ \ \ \ \ {\mathbb{K}}^{\tau_{0}}\Lambda_{1}=\begin{cases}\Lambda_{1}&\mathrm{if}\ \tau_{0}=1\\
\mathbb{K}\Lambda_{1}=\overline{\Lambda_{1}}&\mathrm{if}\ \tau_{0}=-1.\end{cases}

The magnetic projective linear group:

(2.14)

\displaystyle\mathrm{MPGL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}):=\mathrm{PGL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2

fits into the short exact sequence of groups:

(2.15)

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{*}\to\mathrm{MGL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathrm{MPGL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})

where
\mathrm{MPGL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
acts on
\mathbb{C}^{*}

by complex conjugation.

The inclusion of groups:

(2.16)

\displaystyle\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}(\mathbb{C}),\ \ \ A\mapsto\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)

allow us to define the direct limit of groups:

(2.17)

\displaystyle\mathrm{GL}_{\infty}(\mathbb{C})=\lim_{n}\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),

and the groups
\mathrm{PGL}_{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
,
\mathrm{MGL}_{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
and
\mathrm{MPGL}_{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
are defined accordingly, all
of them fitting into the product in a direct limit presented in Eqn. (
2.9
).

A magnetic
(G,\phi)
-representation is nothing else than
a magnetic homomorphism
(G,\phi)\to\mathrm{MGL}_{\infty}(\mathbb{C})

and a projective one is a homomorphism

(G,\phi)\to\mathrm{MPGL}_{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
.
Because of the finite dimensionality property of the magnetic group
(G,\phi)
,
a magnetic
(G,\phi)
-representation factors through a finite dimensional one
(G,\phi)\to\mathrm{MGL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
; the same
applies to magnetic projective representations.

Note that a magnetic representation:

(2.18)

\displaystyle(G,\phi)\to\mathrm{MGL}_{\infty}(\mathbb{C}),\ \ g\mapsto(\Lambda_{g},\phi(g))

can also be represented as a
\phi
-twisted homomorphism from
G
to
\mathrm{GL}_{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
.
The product in the group
g\cdot h=gh
transforms into the equation:

(2.19)

\displaystyle(\Lambda_{g},\phi(g))(\Lambda_{h},\phi(h))=(\Lambda_{(gh)},\phi(gh))

which implies the equation:

(2.20)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{g}\ {\mathbb{K}}^{\phi(g)}{\Lambda_{h}}=\Lambda_{gh}.

Hence a magnetic representation induces an assignment
g\mapsto\Lambda_{g}
such that:

(2.21)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{g}\ {\mathbb{K}}^{\phi(g)}{\Lambda_{h}}\ {\Lambda_{gh}}^{-1}=1,

which is precisely the definition of a
\phi
-twisted homomorphism from
G
to
\mathrm{GL}_{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
.

We see that the condition of
\phi

2.4.
Relation to other equivariant graded Brauer groups

The explicit decomposition of the magnetic equivariant graded Brauer group presented in Thm.
2.7
fits within a series of decomposition results of Brauer groups of different kinds. Here we will survey some of these decompositions.

Using Galois cohomology it can be shown that
the Bauer group of a field
k
can be described in cohomological terms as follows

[
15
, Thm. 4.4.7]
:

(2.131)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Br}(k)\cong H^{2}(\mathrm{Gal}(k_{s}/k),k_{s}^{*})

where
k_{s}
is the separable closure of
k
and the absolute Galois group
\mathrm{Gal}(k_{s}/k)
acts on the coefficients
k_{s}^{*}
.

The equivariant extension of the Brauer group is due to Fröhlich, and in
[
12
, Thm. 4.1]
it is shown the following isomorphism:

(2.132)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Br}_{G}(k)\cong\mathrm{Br}(k)\times H^{2}(G,k^{*})

where
G
acts trivially on
k^{*}
.

The extension to graded algebras was due to Wall and the graded Brauer group was born (also known as Brauer-Wall group). In
[
27
, Thm. 3]
the extension of groups is shown, cf.
[
22
, Thm. 4.4]
:

(2.133)

\displaystyle 0\to\mathrm{Br}(k)\to\mathrm{GrBr}(k)\to\left((k^{*}/k^{*2})\overset{\cdot}{\times}\mathbb{Z}/2\right)\to 0

where the product structure on
(k^{*}/k^{*2})\overset{\cdot}{\times}\mathbb{Z}/2
is given by the formula:

(2.134)

\displaystyle(d,e)(d^{\prime},e^{\prime})=((-1)^{ee^{\prime}}dd^{\prime},e+e^{\prime}),

and the 2-cocycle of the extension has a similar description than the one in Prop.
2.4
.

The equivariant graded Brauer group was introduced by Riehm, and in
[
23
, Thm. 4]
the following isomorphism is shown:

(2.135)

\displaystyle\mathrm{GrBr}_{G}(k)\cong\Big(H^{2}(G,k^{*})\overset{\bullet}{\times}\mathrm{Hom}(G,\mathbb{Z}/2)\Big)\times\mathrm{GrBr}(k)

where the twisted product
\overset{\bullet}{\times}
is defined by the 2-cocycle which is the composition of the cup product with the homomorphism induced by the inclusion of coefficients
\mathbb{Z}/2\to k^{*}
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}(G,\mathbb{Z}/2)\times\mathrm{Hom}(G,\mathbb{Z}/2)

\displaystyle\overset{\cup}{\to}H^{2}(G,\mathbb{Z}/2)\to H^{2}(G,k^{*})

(2.136)

\displaystyle(\psi_{0},\psi_{1})

\displaystyle\mapsto\psi_{0}\cup\psi_{1}\mapsto(-1)^{\psi_{0}\cup\psi_{1}}.

Applying the decomposition formula of Eqn. (
2.135
) to the cases of
k=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}
we obtain the following formulas:

(2.137)

\displaystyle\mathrm{GrBr}_{G}(\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\cong\Big(H^{2}(G,\mathbb{Z}/2)\overset{\bullet}{\times}\mathrm{Hom}(G,\mathbb{Z}/2)\Big)\times\mathbb{Z}/8,

(2.138)

\displaystyle\mathrm{GrBr}_{G}(\mathbb{C})

\displaystyle\cong\Big(H^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{*})\overset{\bullet}{\times}\mathrm{Hom}(G,\mathbb{Z}/2)\Big)\times\mathbb{Z}/2.

Relating these two decompositions of the graded equivariant Brauer groups to the magnetic equivariant graded Brauer groups we get the following homomorphisms.

Take the magnetic group
(G_{0}\times\mathbb{Z}/2,\pi_{2})
and note that the complexification of real algebras induces the homomorphism:

(2.139)

\displaystyle\mathrm{GrBr}_{G_{0}}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathrm{GrBr}_{(G_{0}\times\mathbb{Z}/2,\pi_{2})}(\mathbb{C}).

This homomorphism fails to be an isomorphism for many groups, and the explicit examples will be shown in the next section.

If
G_{0}=\mathrm{ker}(G\overset{\phi}{\to}\mathbb{Z}/2)
is the core of the magnetic group
G
, the restriction of the
G
action to
G_{0}
induces a homomorphism of groups:

(2.140)

\displaystyle\mathrm{GrBr}_{(G,\phi)}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathrm{GrBr}_{G_{0}}(\mathbb{C})

which is surjective whenever the homomorphism:

(2.141)

\displaystyle H^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{*}_{\phi})\to H^{2}(G_{0},\mathbb{C}^{*})

is surjective. The explicit examples will be shown in the next section.

Prior to introducing the examples, let us start by recalling some well known facts on the cohomology of groups.

(2.142)

(2.143)

\displaystyle H^{j}(G,\mathbb{C}_{\phi}^{*})\cong H^{j+1}(G,\mathbb{Z}_{\phi})\ \ \ \mathrm{for}\ \ \ j\geq 1.

From

\displaystyle\cong M^{C}

(2.145)

\displaystyle H^{2k}(C,M)

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{coker}(M_{C}\to M^{C})\ \ \ \mathrm{for}\ \ k\geq 1

(2.146)

\displaystyle H^{2k-1}(C,M)

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{ker}(M_{C}\to M^{C})\ \ \ \mathrm{for}\ \ k\geq 1

where the invariants and the coinvariants are respectively defined as follows:

(2.147)

\displaystyle M^{C}

\displaystyle=\{m\in M|\forall g\in C\ gm=m\}

(2.148)

\displaystyle M_{C}

\displaystyle=M/\langle gm-m|g\in C,m\in M\rangle

and the homomorphism:

(2.149)

\displaystyle M_{C}\to M^{C},\ \ [m]\mapsto\sum_{g\in C}gm

For the group

\mathbb{Z}

Here we have

\displaystyle H^{2k}(\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{2k-1}(\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathbb{Z}_{-})

\displaystyle H^{2k-1}(\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathbb{Z})=H^{2k}(\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathbb{Z}_{-})

To calculate

The second page of the spectral sequence gives us the terms:

(2.153)

\displaystyle E_{2}^{2,0}=

\displaystyle H^{2}(\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathbb{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{id}})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2,

(2.154)

\displaystyle E_{2}^{1,1}=

\displaystyle H^{1}(\mathbb{Z}/2,H^{1}(G_{0},\mathbb{C}^{*}_{\phi})),

and the second differential

is the only differential that might be non-zero. This follows from the fact that the term:

(2.156)

\displaystyle E_{2}^{3,0}\cong H^{3}(\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathbb{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{id}})\cong H^{4}(\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathbb{Z}_{-})=0

.

Consider the magnetic cyclic group

2.6.
Relation to Dyson’s ten-fold classification

In 1962, while studying symmetries of Hamiltonians, physicist F. Dyson argued that the three-fold classification of associative division algebras by Frobenius should be enhanced by a ten-fold classification which incorporates the symmetries of graded vector spaces
[
9
]
.

This ten-fold classification of Dyson is obtained when studying the algebra of endomorphisms of irreducible graded representations of graded groups
[
13
]
. Let us see recall the main constructions.

Let
(G,\phi)
be a magnetic group, and endow it with an extra grading defined by a homomorphism
\psi:G\to\mathbb{Z}/2
. Let
V
be a complex graded magnetic representation of
G
, meaning that
V=V_{0}\oplus V_{1}
is a graded vector space,
the elements of
\psi^{-1}(0)
act by homogeneous endomorphisms of degree
0
while the elements of

\psi^{-1}(1)
act by homogeneous endomorphisms of degree
1
, and the elements of
G_{0}=\phi^{-1}(0)
act complex linearly while the elements of
G\backslash G_{0}
ac complex antilinearly.

The endomorphism algebra
\mathrm{End}(V)
can be endowed with an action of
G
by conjugation:

(2.184)

\displaystyle G\times\mathrm{End}(V)\to\mathrm{End}(V)

(2.185)

\displaystyle(g,\Phi)\mapsto g\Phi g^{-1},

making it a magnetic equivariant central simple graded algebra, and thus defining a similarity class
in
\mathrm{GrBr}_{(G,\phi)}(\mathbb{C})
.

If the representation
V
is irreducible, the algebra of
G
-invariant endomorphisms

\mathrm{End}(V)^{G}
, which is the same as the
G
-equivariant endomorphisms, becomes a graded division algebra over the reals. The explicit classification of the ten possibilities was masterfully done by Dyson
[
9
]
and was later related to the classification of the graded division algebras over the reals
[
13
]
.

If the homomorphism
\psi
is trivial, then the irreducible representation
V
can be taken to be of degree
0
, and the algebra of
G
-invariant endomorphism gives rise to the three-fold classification originally due to Wigner
[
28
]
. If
V|_{G_{0}}
denotes the complex representation restricted to
G_{0}
, then in this case we have:

(2.186)

\displaystyle\mathrm{End}(V)^{G}=\begin{cases}\mathbb{R}&\mathrm{if}\ V|_{G_{0}}\ \mathrm{is\ irreducible},\\
\mathbb{H}&\mathrm{if}\ V|_{G_{0}}\cong W\oplus W\\
\mathbb{C}&\mathrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

where
W
denotes an irreducible complex
G_{0}
-representation.

Note two important facts. First, the algebra
\mathrm{End}(V)
for
V
irreducible of degree
0
, is trivial in the graded Brauer group
\mathrm{GrBr}_{(G,\phi)}(\mathbb{C})
, independently of the magnetic group
G
. Second, for the case
G=\mathbb{Z}/2
we have always that

\mathrm{End}(V)^{\mathbb{Z}/2}\cong\mathbb{R}
.

Therefore we see that there is no way to extract information of the
G
-invariant algebras out of the magnetic equivariant graded Brauer groups, except perhaps for the magnetic group
\mathbb{Z}/2
. Since the algebra
\mathrm{End}(V)

is trivial in
\mathrm{GrBr}_{(G,\phi)}(\mathbb{C})
, then one would expect that its
G
-invariant part would be trivial in
\mathrm{GrBr}(\mathbb{R})
. But as we have shown in Eqn. (
2.186
), this is not possible.

The case of the magnetic group
\mathbb{Z}/2
is special in the sense that taking
\mathbb{Z}/2
-invariants is the inverse map of the complexification isomorphism:

(2.187)

\displaystyle\mathrm{GrBr}(\mathbb{R})\underset{\cong}{\overset{\otimes\mathbb{C}}{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{GrBr}_{(\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathrm{id})}(\mathbb{C})\underset{\cong}{\overset{\wedge(\mathbb{Z}/2)}{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{GrBr}(\mathbb{R}).

For the magnetic group
\mathbb{Z}/2
we can relate its graded equivariant Brauer groups with the set of real graded division algebras, as it is shown in Table
1
. Meanwhile for a general magnetic group
(G,\phi)
, the magnetic equivariant graded Brauer group
\mathrm{GrBr}_{(G,\phi)}(\mathbb{C})
does not relate in the same manner as for the group
\mathbb{Z}/2
to the set of real graded division algebras.

3.
Twistings in magnetic equivariant K-theory

The graded Brauer group appears
as the natural group parametrizing twistings in K-theory. This has been masterfully shown by Donovan and Karoubi in
[
7
]

where they study and analyze twisted K-theory as the appropriate K-theory with local coefficients, building on the theory of Banach categories developed by Karoubi in his thesis
[
17
]
.

The foundations, properties and results of Karoubi’s approach to twisted K-theory provide an appropriate background where graded Brauer groups are the fundamental objects, and the K-theories associated to them recover the well known K-theories built from vector bundles on spaces.
Here we will follow Karoubi’s approach to distinguish the twists on magnetic equivariant K-theory
[
25
]
. The main construction involves the theory of Banach categories which we will follow from Karoubi’s book
[
18
]
, and from the survey on the relation of twisted K-theory and the graded Brauer groups done by the same author
[
19
]
.

Summarizing some of the results by Donovan and Karoubi
[
7
]
and incorporating some of the results of Atiyah and Segal
[
2
]
, we have that the complex and real equivariant graded Brauer groups of a finite
G
-CW-complex

become:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{GrBrU}_{G}(X)

\displaystyle\cong\Big(\mathrm{Tors}(H^{3}_{G}(X,\mathbb{Z}))\overset{\bullet}{\times}H^{1}_{G}(X,\mathbb{Z}/2)\Big){\times}\mathbb{Z}/2,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{GrBrO}_{G}(X)

\displaystyle\cong\Big(H^{2}_{G}(X,\mathbb{Z}/2)\overset{\bullet}{\times}H^{1}_{G}(X,\mathbb{Z}/2)\Big){\times}\mathbb{Z}/8.

Here the
G
-equivariant cohomology groups

stand for the cohomology of the homotopy quotient
X\times_{G}EG
.
These two groups parametrize respectively the twistings in the K-theory
\mathrm{KU}_{G}(X)
of complex
G
-equivariant bundles over
X
and in
the K-theory
\mathrm{KO}_{G}(X)
of real
G
-equivariant bundles over
X
.
In both cases the product structure
\overset{\bullet}{\times}
incorporates the cup product in cohomology as in Cor.
2.5
,
where in the unitary case it is further composed with the integer Bockstein map.

The authors together with M. Xiconténcatl
[
25
]
have introduced the magnetic equivariant K-theory
\mathbf{K}_{(G,\phi)}(X)
as the K-theory group of complex vector bundles over
X

endowed with actions of the magnetic group
(G,\phi)
.
The twistings of this K-theory have been assumed by other authors
[

14

to behave in similar way as the ones of the K-theories
\mathrm{KU}
and
\mathrm{KO}
presented in Eqns. (

) and (

). Here we will argue that
the appropriate graded equivariant Brauer groups for magnetic equivariant K-theory are the magnetic equivariant graded Brauer groups.
In particular we will show that the magnetic equivariant graded Brauer group

parametrizes the twistings of the magnetic equivariant K-theory

One main application of this perspective is the alternative proof of the degree shift isomorphism

presented in Prop.

3.3.
Degree shift isomorphism

Now we can prove the degree shift isomorphism for the total K-theory

Let

(3.37)

\displaystyle\sigma(a,b)=e^{\pi iab}

(3.38)

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}^{*}\underset{\langle(-1,2)\rangle}{\times}\mathbb{Z}/4\to\mathbb{Z}/2,

(3.39)

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}/2\to\mathbb{Z}/4\to\mathbb{Z}/2.

Denote the pullback group

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}/2\to\widehat{G}\to G.

Proposition 3.4

(Degree shift isomorphism)
.

Consider the
\mathbb{Z}/2

\displaystyle M_{2}(\mathbb{C})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2\cong M_{2}(\mathbb{C})_{\sigma}[\mathbb{Z}/2].

\displaystyle M_{2}(\mathbb{C})\rtimes G\cong M_{2}(\mathbb{C})_{\phi^{*}\sigma}[G].

Corollary 3.5

(4-periodicity)
.

Let
(G,\phi)
be a magnetic group such that the cohomology class of
\phi^{*}\sigma
is trivial, ie.
[\phi^{*}\sigma]=1
. Then the magnetic equivariant K-theory
\mathbf{K}_{G}^{*}
is 4-periodic.

Proof.

If
\phi^{*}\sigma=\delta f
for
f:G\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
, then
(\phi^{*}\sigma)(g,h)=f(g)\overline{f(h)}^{\phi(g)}f(gh)^{-1}
and the
assignment:

(3.47)

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[G]

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{C}_{\phi^{*}\sigma}[G]

(3.48)

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle\mapsto f(g)g

is an isomorphism of algebras. Therefore, the algebras

C^{0,4}_{\mathbb{C}}\rtimes G
and
\mathbb{C}[G]

are Morita equivalent and therefore by Prop.
3.4
we obtain the desired 4-periodicity:

(3.49)

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}^{A^{p,q+4}}_{G}\cong\mathbf{K}^{{A^{p,q}}}_{G}.

∎

The 4-periodicity had already appeared in the calculation
of the magnetic equivariant graded Brauer group of the cyclic groups

\mathrm{GrBr}_{(\mathbb{Z}/2n,\mathrm{mod}_{2})}(\mathbb{C})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/4
.

For the particular case of the magnetic group

Local enhancement of the mean-field approximation for bosons
Marius Lemm, Simone Rademacher, Jingxuan Zhang

Abstract
. We study the quantum many-body dynamics of a Bose-Einstein condensate (BEC) on the lattice in the mean-field regime. We derive a local enhancement of the mean-field approximation: At positive distance ρ > 0 \rho>0 from the initial BEC, the mean-field approximation error at time t ≤ ρ / v t\leq\rho/v is bounded as ρ − n \rho^{-n} , for arbitrarily large n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 . This is a consequence of new ballistic propagation bounds on the fluctuations around the condensate. To prove this, we develop a variant of the ASTLO (adiabatic spacetime localization observable) method for the particle non-conserving generator of the fluctuation dynamics around Hartree states.

Introduction

We study the quantum many-body dynamics of a system of bosons on the lattice. We formulate the problem as usual in terms of the algebraic operator formalism of creation and annihilation operators, i.e., we consider the symmetric Fock space

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))=\mathbb{C}\oplus\bigoplus_{N\geq 1}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))^{\otimes_{s}N}

with
d\geq 3
representing the spatial dimenion and
\otimes_{s}N
denotes the
N

N
-body Schrödinger equation

(1.2)

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}\psi_{N,t}=H_{N}\;\psi_{N,t}

with self-adjoint Hamiltonian operator given by

(1.3)

\displaystyle H_{N}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
x\sim y\end{subarray}}(-\Delta_{x,y})\;a_{x}^{*}a_{y}+{\frac{\lambda}{2N}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\end{subarray}}\;a_{x}^{*}a_{x}^{*}a_{x}a_{x}\;.

The main large parameter is

is a (small) order-

\frac{1}{N}

Since one is interested in the regime of large particle number

. Understanding its behavior is an instance of the

and it requires the development of mathematical techniques that are quite different from standard PDE theory, because the dimension is so large.

Mean-field dispersive PDEs of the type (
1.2

\frac{1}{N}
in (

First result: Locally enhanced mean-field approximation

The aforementioned results imply that the dynamical evolution of an initial BEC

Consider an initially factorized state

Due to the interaction term, the factorization is not preserved along the time evolution given by the Schrödinger equation (

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}\varphi_{t}=h_{\varphi_{t}}\varphi_{t}\;\quad\text{with}\quad h_{\varphi_{t}}:=-\Delta+{\lambda|\varphi_{t}|^{2}}.

1.2.
Second result: Propagation bound on fluctuations

The concrete way in which we exploit the general principle of spatial locality is by showing that fluctuations around the Bose-Einstein condensate effectively propagate at bounded speed
v>0
. That is, we derive a “ballistic” propagation bound on the fluctuations (a propagation bound is called “ballistic”, if it is effective for times proportional to distance). This ballistic propagation bound then implies Theorem
1.1
because it says that the fluctuations did not have sufficient time to propagate from
B_{R}^{c}
to
B_{r}
, for times that are short compared to
(R-r)/v
,

The propagation bound concerns the number of excitations, as encoded in a self-adjoint operator
\mathcal{N}^{+}
. The quality of the bound on the number of fluctuations
\mathcal{N}^{+}
directly translates to the quality of the mean-field approximation.

The standard bounds on
\mathcal{N}^{+}
are of global nature. For instance, the influential works
[
RS09
,
LNS15
]
proved

\frac{\langle\psi_{N,t},(\mathcal{N}^{+}+1)\psi_{N,t}\rangle}{\langle\psi_{N,0},(\mathcal{N}^{+}+1)\psi_{N,0}\rangle}\leq Ce^{Ct}

where
\psi_{N,t}
solves (
1.2
). Most importantly, the bound says that the number of excitations is bounded independently of
N
. The term
e^{Ct}
is somewhat undesirable because it rapidly grows in time. At the same time, the exponential growth appears to be very natural because it arises from a Grönwall argument and indeed it is probably optimal in general, i.e., without any further assumptions on the nonlinear effective evolution equation.

One physically natural assumption is that of dispersion (validity of nonlinear Strichartz estimates). This allows to completely remove the growth in time, as shown recently by Dietze-Lee
[
DL23
]
(see also
[
Lee19
]
)

\frac{\langle\psi_{N,t},(\mathcal{N}^{+}+1)\psi_{N,t}\rangle}{\langle\psi_{N,0},(\mathcal{N}^{+}+1)^{2}\psi_{N,0}\rangle}\leq C\;.

Our approach is to consider the
local
fluctuation numbers
\mathcal{N}_{X}^{+}
with
X\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. We show that in the situation of Figure
1
, the fluctuations inside
B_{r}
at time
t
are
\mathcal{O}(\rho^{-\infty})
for
t\leq\frac{\rho}{v}
. More precisely, we derive the enhanced local bound

(1.7)

\frac{\langle\psi_{N,t},(\mathcal{N}^{+}_{B_{r}}+1)\psi_{N,t}\rangle}{\langle\psi_{N,0},(\mathcal{N}^{+}+1)^{2}\psi_{N,0}\rangle}\leq\frac{C}{\rho^{n}},

for
t\leq\frac{\rho}{v}
and arbitrarily large
n\geq 1
. The precise statement is in Theorem
1.2
below. We point out that Theorem
1.2
is actually more general than the situation depicted in Figure
1
, because it does not require the initial state to be a pure condensate.

1.3.
Related literature on many-body propagation bounds

Our idea is to exploit the general principle of spatial locality, which suggests that physical quantities effectively exhibit a maximal speed. In relativistic systems, this maximal speed is the speed of light, but this is neither mathematically valid nor physically meaningful (simply because the speed of light is so large) in a non-relativistic model as the one described by (
1.2
) and (
1.3
). A suitable analog of the speed of light for many-body lattice systems goes by the name of “Lieb-Robinson bounds”
[
LR72
]
, which showed how spatial locality can be used in a many-body context. Note in passing that the standard Lieb-Robinson bounds are concerned with quantum spin systems, which have bounded interactions and therefore do not apply in our setting.

Several recent works have developed propagation bounds (including Lieb-Robinson bounds) for Bose-Hubbard Hamiltonians
[
FLS22b
,
FLS22a
,
KS21
,
KVS24
,
KL24
,
LRSZ23
,
LRZ23
,
SHOE11
,
YL22
]
.
These bounds actually apply to our Hamiltonian (which is a Bose-Hubbard Hamiltonian with the interaction scaled by
N^{-1}
). However, they are not able (nor designed) to give results like ours here. The reason is that
these other approaches are designed to treat all the bosons alike. This has the consequence that they are not able to separate the Bose-Einstein condensate, which is comprised of
\sim N
many bosons, from the fluctuations, which are comprised of
\mathcal{O}(1)
many bosons. Therefore, these bounds would lead to an additional
N
-factor on the right-hand sides of (
1.6
) and (
1.7
), which would no longer be a useful bound. Therefore, the bounds developed in
[
FLS22b
,
FLS22a
,
KS21
,
KVS24
,
KL24
,
LRSZ23
,
LRZ23
,
SHOE11
,
YL22
]
are inconclusive for what we undertake here.
This is why we need to develop new propagation bounds that are specially tailored to tracking the fluctuations.
Propagation bounds for the nonlinear Hartree equation were proved in
[
AFPS23
,
HS21
]
and here we treat for the first time the fluctuations.

As far as we are aware, propagation bounds on fluctuations have not played a large role so far in the dynamical description of bosons in the mean-field regime. A notable exception is a work by Erdös-Schlein
[
ES09
]
which used Lieb-Robinson type ideas between different particle number sectors inspired by
[
NRSS07
]
, but without considering the spatial structure.

Methodologically, our result here builds on the developments on bosonic propagation bounds, but with a new twist. In a nutshell, our key contribution is to demonstrate how the ASTLO (adiabatic spacetime localization observables) method that was originally developed for Bose-Hubbard Hamiltonians (see
[
FLS22b
,
FLS22a
,
LRSZ23
,
LRZ23
]
) can be adapted to the
particle non-conserving
generator of the fluctuation dynamics.

Lastly, we would also like to mention that semiclassical propagation bounds have played an important role in the mean-field description of fermions; see, e.g.,
[
EESY04
,
BPS14
,
LS23
,
FPS23
]
. These bounds are again concerned with exploiting the general principle of spatial locality, but in a physical and mathematical setting that is quite different from ours.

(2.1)

\displaystyle\psi_{N}=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\varphi_{t}^{\otimes(N-j)}\otimes_{s}\xi_{t}^{(j)},\quad\text{where}\quad\xi_{t}^{(j)}\in\ell^{2}_{\perp\varphi_{t}}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})^{\otimes_{s}j}

into products of the condensate wave function
\varphi_{t}
and excitations
\xi_{t}^{(j)}
that are orthogonal to products of the condensates. There is a unitary, first introduced in
[
LNSS15
]
,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{N,t}:\ell^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{Z}^{dN})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{j=0}^{N}\ell^{2}_{\perp\varphi_{t}}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})^{\otimes_{s}j}:=\mathcal{F}_{\perp\varphi_{t}}^{\leq N},\quad\mathcal{U}_{N,t}\psi_{N}=\{\xi_{t}^{(0)},\dots,\xi_{t}^{(N)}\}=:\xi_{N,t}

mapping an
N
-particle wave function
\psi_{N}
to excitations
\xi_{N,t}
. Note that in contrast to the standard bosonic Fock space (
1.1
) that is built over
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
, the truncated Fock space
\mathcal{F}_{\perp\varphi_{t}}^{\leq N}
is built over
\ell^{2}_{\perp\varphi_{t}}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
, the orthogonal complement of
\varphi_{t}
in
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
. Moreover,
\mathcal{F}_{\perp\varphi_{t}}^{\leq N}
contains sequences
\xi_{N,t}
of at most
N
elements.

On the truncated Fock space
\mathcal{F}_{\perp\varphi_{t}}^{\leq N}
we furthermore have for any operator
A
on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\dG(q_{t}Aq_{t})=\dG(A).

Specifically, for the local excitation number operator (c.f. (
1.14
))

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{X}^{+}(t):=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\big(q_{t}\mathds{1}_{X}q_{t}\big)^{(i)},\quad X\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d},

the following identity holds on
\mathcal{F}_{\perp\varphi_{t}}^{\leq N}
for any
X
:

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{X}^{+}(t)=\mathcal{N}_{X}:=\sum_{z\in X}n_{z}\;,\quad\text{with}\quad n_{z}:=a_{z}^{*}a_{z}\;.

2.2.
Fluctuation dynamic
\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)

We are interested in fluctuations around (products of) the condensate that we study through the so-called fluctuation dynamics on
\mathcal{F}_{\perp\varphi_{t}}^{\leq N}
:

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)=\mathcal{U}_{N,t}e^{-iH_{N}(t-s)}\mathcal{U}_{N,s}^{*},

which satisfies

(2.7)

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)=\mathcal{L}_{N}(t)\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s),

with generator

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{N}(t)=\mathcal{U}_{N,t}^{*}H_{N}\mathcal{U}_{N,t}+\big(i\partial_{t}\mathcal{U}_{N,t}^{*}\big)\mathcal{U}_{N,t}\;.

Recall the global number of excitation is described by the operator
\mathcal{N}^{+}(t)
from (
1.13
).
We can explicitly compute the generator
\mathcal{L}_{N}(t)
using that the unitary
\mathcal{U}_{N}(t)
acts on products of creation and annihilation operators for
f,g\in\ell^{2}_{\perp\varphi_{t}}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
as

(2.9)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathcal{U}_{N,t}^{*}a^{*}(\varphi_{t})a(f)\mathcal{U}_{N,t}=&\sqrt{N-\mathcal{N}^{+}(t)}a(f)=:\sqrt{N}b(f),\\
\mathcal{U}_{N,t}^{*}a^{*}(f)a(\varphi_{t})\mathcal{U}_{N,t}=&a^{*}(g)\sqrt{N-\mathcal{N}^{+}(t)}=:\sqrt{N}b^{*}(g),\end{aligned}\right.

and

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{N,t}^{*}a^{*}(\varphi_{t})a(\varphi_{t})\mathcal{U}_{N,t}=N-\mathcal{N}^{+}(t),\quad\mathcal{U}_{N,t}^{*}a^{*}(f)a(g)\mathcal{U}_{N,t}=a^{*}(f)a(g)\;.

In (
2.9
) we introduced modified creation and annihilation operators
b^{*}(f),b(g)
that, in contrast to
a^{*}(f),a(g)
, leave the truncated Fock space
\mathcal{F}_{\perp\varphi_{t}}^{\leq N}
invariant. This properties comes with the price of modified canonical commutation relations (CCR)

(2.11)

\displaystyle[b(f),b^{*}(g)]=\bigg(1-\frac{\mathcal{N}^{+}(t)}{N}\bigg)\langle g,f\rangle-\frac{1}{N}a^{*}(g)a(f),\quad[b^{*}(f),b^{*}(g)]=[b(f),b(g)]=0,

which, compared to the standard CCR

(2.12)

\displaystyle[a(f),a^{*}(g)]=\langle f,g\rangle,

have a correction term of order
\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})
.

2.3.
Basic properties of
\mathcal{W}_{N}(t)

Based on (
2.9
) and (
2.10
), explicit computations show that the generator of the fluctuation dynamics (

) is given by

(2.13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{N}(t):=\mathbb{H}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{R}_{N,t}^{(j)},

where the leading order, quadratic term is given by

(2.14)

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}=\dG\big(h_{\varphi_{t}}+\lambda\widetilde{K}_{1,t}\big)+\frac{\lambda}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\big[\widetilde{K}_{2,t}(x)b_{x}^{*}b_{x}^{*}+\overline{\widetilde{K}}_{2,t}(x)b_{x}b_{x}\big].

Here, with
J:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\rightarrow\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
denoting the anti-linear operator

, we write

(2.15)

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}_{1,s}=

\displaystyle q_{s}K_{1,s}q_{s},\quad\widetilde{K}_{2,s}=(Jq_{s}J)K_{2,s}q_{s},

(2.16)

\displaystyle K_{1,s}(x)=

\displaystyle\varphi_{s}(x)\overline{\varphi}_{s}(x),\quad K_{2,s}(x)=\varphi_{s}(x)\varphi_{s}(x).

The remainder terms are given by

(2.17)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{N,t}^{(1)}=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{2}\dG\big(q_{t}\big[|\varphi_{t}|^{2}\varphi_{t}+\widetilde{K}_{1,t}{-\mu_{t}}\big]q_{t}\big)\frac{1-\mathcal{N}^{+}(t)}{N}+\lambda{\frac{\mathcal{N}^{+}(t)}{\sqrt{N}}b(q_{t}|\varphi_{t}|^{2}\varphi_{t})}+{\rm h.c.},

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{N,t}^{(2)}=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi_{t}(x)a^{*}(q_{t,x})a(q_{t,x})b(q_{t,x})+{\rm h.c.},

(2.18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{N,t}^{(3)}=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{N}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}a^{*}(q_{t,x})a^{*}(q_{t,x})a(q_{t,x})a(q_{t,x})\;.

Here, in (
2.17
), we set

(2.19)

\displaystyle 2\mu_{t}:=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\varphi_{t}(x)|^{2}\;|\varphi_{t}(y)|^{2}.

In the remainder of this paper, we will derive various bounds on (powers of) the number of excitations
\mathcal{N}^{+}(t)
w.r.t. to the solution
\psi_{N,t}
of the Schrödinger equation (

) that is given by

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{N,t},\mathcal{N}^{+}(t)\psi_{N,t}\rangle=\langle e^{-iH_{N}t}\psi_{N,0},\mathcal{N}^{+}(t)e^{-iH_{N}t}\psi_{N,0}\rangle\;.

With the definition of the fluctuation dynamics
\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;0)
in (

) and the observation that

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{N,t}\dG(q_{t}Aq_{t})\mathcal{U}_{N,t}^{*}=\dG(q_{t}Aq_{t})

for any operator
A
on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
from (

\mathcal{U}_{N,t}\mathcal{N}^{+}(t)\mathcal{U}_{N,t}^{*}=\mathcal{N}^{+}(t)

(2.22)

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{N,t},\mathcal{N}^{+}(t)\psi_{N,t}\rangle=\langle\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;0)\mathcal{U}_{N,0}\psi_{N,0},\mathcal{N}^{+}(t)\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;0)\mathcal{U}_{N,0}\psi_{N,0}\rangle.

Lastly, we summarize in the following lemma various estimates to bound the commutators with the generator of the fluctuation dynamics

For

, we use that the flucutation dynamics effectively acts as an asymptotic Bogoliubov dynamics on the observable
\phi_{+}(q_{t}O\varphi_{t})
. For this we define the function

(6.23)

\displaystyle f_{(t,s)}=\mathcal{L}_{(t;s)}q_{t}O\varphi_{t},

where the generator
\mathcal{L}_{(t;s)}
is defined by (
6.12
). The goal is now to show that for the second term of the r.h.s. of (
6.19
) we have

(6.24)

\displaystyle\sqrt{N}\langle\Omega,\mathcal{W}_{N}^{*}(t;0)\phi_{+}(q_{t}O\varphi_{t})\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;0)\Omega\rangle\approx\sqrt{N}\langle\Omega,\phi_{+}(f_{(t,0)})\Omega\rangle,

while the r.h.s. vanishes as
b(h)\Omega=0
.

To be more precise, we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{N}\langle\Omega,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{N}^{*}(t;0)\phi_{+}(q_{t}O\varphi_{t})\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;0)\Omega\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{N}\langle\Omega,\mathcal{W}_{N}^{*}(t;0)\phi_{+}(q_{t}O\varphi_{t})\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;0)\Omega\rangle-\sqrt{N}\langle\Omega,\phi_{+}(f_{(t,0)})\Omega\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\sqrt{N}\int_{0}^{t}ds\;\langle\Omega,\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\bigg(\big[\mathbb{H},\phi_{+}(f_{(t;s)})\big]-\phi_{-}(i\partial_{s}f_{(t;s)})\bigg)\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\rangle

(6.25)

\displaystyle+i\sqrt{N}\int_{0}^{t}ds\;\langle\Omega,\mathcal{W}_{N}^{*}(t;s)\big[\mathcal{R}_{N,t},\phi_{+}(f_{(t,s)})\big]\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\rangle

where we introduced the notation

(6.26)

\displaystyle i\phi_{-}(h)=b(h)-b^{*}(h)\quad\text{for all}\quad h\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\;.

Below we bound the two terms in the r.h.s. of (
6.25
) respectively.

3.1. For the first term of the r.h.s. of (
6.25
), we compute

\displaystyle\big[\mathbb{H},\phi_{+}(f_{(t;s)})\big]-\phi_{-}(i\partial_{s}f_{(t;s)})=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{2}b^{*}\big(\widetilde{K}_{2,s}Jf_{(t;s)}\big)\frac{\mathcal{N}_{+}(t)}{N}-{\rm h.c.}

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda}{2N}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widetilde{K}_{2,s}(x)a^{*}(f_{(t,s)})a^{*}_{x}b_{x}-{\rm h.c.}

(6.27)

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda}{2N}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widetilde{K}_{2,s}(x)b_{x}^{*}a_{x}^{*}a(f_{(t;s)})-{\rm h.c.}

and estimate the terms separately. For the first, we get with
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\widetilde{K}_{2,s}(x)|\leq C
(see (
2.16
)) that

\displaystyle|\langle\Omega,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{*}_{N}(t;s)b^{*}\big(\widetilde{K}_{2,s}Jf_{(t;s)}\big)\frac{\mathcal{N}^{+}(t)}{N}\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{N}\|b^{*}(\widetilde{K}_{2,s}Jf_{(t;s)})(\mathcal{N}+1)^{1/4}\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\|\;\|\mathcal{N}^{3/4}\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\|

(6.28)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{N}\|f_{(t;s)}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\langle\Omega,\mathcal{W}^{*}_{N}(t;s)(\mathcal{N}+1)^{3/2}\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\rangle

and the hermitian conjugate can be estimated similarly. Moreover,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2N}|\langle\Omega,\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widetilde{K}_{2,s}(x)a^{*}(f_{(t;s)})a_{x}^{*}b_{x}\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2N}\|\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widetilde{K}_{2,s}(x)a^{*}_{x}b_{x}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{-1/4}\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\|\;\|a(f_{(t;s)})\mathcal{N}^{1/4}\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\frac{1}{2N}\|\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widetilde{K}_{2,s}(x)\frac{\sqrt{N-\mathcal{N}^{+}(t)+1}}{\sqrt{N}}a_{x}^{*}a_{x}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{-1/4}\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\|\;\|a(f_{(t;s)})\mathcal{N}^{1/4}\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\|

(6.29)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{N}\|f_{(t;s)}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{3/4}\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\|^{2}\;

and the hermitian conjugate as well as the remaining term of the r.h.s. of (
6.27
) can be estimated similarly. Thus we arrive at the following bound for the first term in the r.h.s. of (
6.25
):

\displaystyle|\langle\Omega,

\displaystyle\;\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\bigg(\big[\mathbb{H},\phi_{+}(f_{(t;s)})\big]-\phi_{-}(i\partial_{s}f_{(t;s)})\bigg)\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\rangle|

(6.30)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\frac{C\absolutevalue{\l}}{N}}\|f_{(t;s)}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\langle\Omega,\mathcal{W}^{*}_{N}(t;s)(\mathcal{N}+1)^{3/2}\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\rangle\;.

3.2. For the second term in the r.h.s. of (
6.25
), we recall the splitting
\mathcal{R}_{N,s}=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\mathcal{R}_{N,s}^{(j)}
from (
2.17
)
and bound their contributions separately.

Contribution from
\mathcal{R}_{N,s}^{(1)}
.
We compute

\displaystyle\big[\mathcal{R}_{N,s}^{(1)},\phi_{+}(f_{(t;s)})\big]=

\displaystyle{\frac{\lambda}{2}i\phi_{-}(q_{s}\big[|\varphi_{s}|^{2}+\widetilde{K}_{1,s}-\mu_{s}\big]f_{(t;s)}\big)\frac{1-\mathcal{N}^{+}(t)}{N}}

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda}{N}\dG\big(q_{s}\big[|\varphi_{s}|^{2}+\widetilde{K}_{1,s}-\mu_{s}\big]q_{s}\big)\;i\phi_{-}(f_{(t;s)})

(6.31)

\displaystyle-{\rm h.c.}

where we introduced the notation
i\phi_{-}(h)=b(h)-b^{*}(h)
. Since
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}||\varphi_{s}(x)|^{2}+\widetilde{K}_{1,s}(x)-\mu_{s}|\leq C
(see (
2.15
) and (
2.19
)), we get

\displaystyle|\langle\Omega,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{*}_{N}(t;s)\big[\mathcal{R}_{N,s}^{(1)},\phi_{+}(f_{(t;s)})\big]\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\rangle|

(6.32)

\displaystyle\leq{\frac{C\absolutevalue{\l}}{N}}\|f_{(t;s)}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\langle\Omega,\mathcal{W}^{*}_{N}(t;s)(\mathcal{N}+1)^{3/2}\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\rangle\;.

Contribution from
\mathcal{R}_{N,s}^{(2)}
.
Using that

\displaystyle\big[\mathcal{R}_{N,s}^{(2)},\phi_{+}(f_{(t;s)})\big]=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi_{s}(x)\overline{f}_{(t;s)}(x)b^{*}(q_{s,x})b(q_{s,x})

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi_{s}(x)f_{(t;s)}(x)b(q_{s,x})b(q_{s,x})

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi_{s}(x)f_{(t;s)}(x)a^{*}(q_{s,x})a(q_{s,x})\bigg(1-\frac{\mathcal{N}}{N}\bigg)

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda}{N^{3/2}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi_{s}(x)a^{*}(q_{s,x})a(q_{s,x})a^{*}(f_{(t;s)})b(q_{s,x})

(6.33)

\displaystyle-{\rm h.c.}

and that
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|f_{(t;s)}(x)|^{2}\leq\|f_{(t;s)}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}^{2}
, we get via the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}

\displaystyle|\langle\Omega,\mathcal{W}^{*}_{N}(t;s)\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi_{s}(x)\overline{f}_{(t;s)}(x)b^{*}(q_{s,x})b(q_{s,x})\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\sqrt{N}}\bigg(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\varphi_{s}(x)|^{2}\|b(q_{s,x})\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|f_{(t;s)}(x)|^{2}\|b(q_{s,x})\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

(6.34)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\sqrt{N}}\|f_{(t;s)}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\langle\Omega,\mathcal{W}^{*}_{N}(t;s)(\mathcal{N}+1)\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\rangle\;.

Since
\|\varphi_{s}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq 1
, the second and third term of the r.h.s. of (
6.33
) can be estimated similarly. For the forth term of the r.h.s. of (
6.33
) we find

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{3/2}}|\langle\Omega,\mathcal{W}^{*}_{N}(t;s)\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi_{s}(x)a^{*}(q_{s,x})a(q_{s,x})a^{*}(f_{(t;s)})b(q_{s,x})\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{3/2}}\bigg(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\varphi_{s}(x)|^{2}\|a^{*}(q_{s,x})a(q_{s,x})\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|a^{*}(f_{(t;s)})b(q_{s;x})\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

(6.35)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\sqrt{N}}\|f_{(t;s)}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\langle\Omega,\mathcal{W}^{*}_{N}(t;s)(\mathcal{N}+1)\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\rangle\;.

Thus, we arrive at

\displaystyle|\langle\Omega,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\big[\mathcal{R}_{N,s}^{(2)},\phi_{+}(f_{(t;s)})\big]\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\rangle|

(6.36)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\absolutevalue{\l}}{\sqrt{N}}\|f_{(t;s)}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\langle\Omega,\mathcal{W}^{*}_{N}(t;s)(\mathcal{N}+1)\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\rangle\;.

Contribution from
\mathcal{R}_{N,s}^{(3)}
.

It remains to control

(6.37)

\displaystyle\big[\mathcal{R}_{N,s}^{(3)},\phi_{+}(f_{(t;s)})\big]=\frac{\lambda}{N}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\;\left(\overline{f}_{(t;s)}(x)b_{x}^{*}a^{*}(q_{s,x})a(q_{s,x})-\rm{h.c.}\right).

We estimate this by

\displaystyle\big|\langle\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega,\big[\mathcal{R}_{N,s}^{(3)},\phi_{+}(f_{(t;s)})\big]\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\absolutevalue{\l}}{N}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|f_{(t;s)}(x)|\;\|b_{x}\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\|\;\|a^{*}(q_{s,x})a(q_{s,x})\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\absolutevalue{\l}}{N}\|f_{(t;s)}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\bigg(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|b_{x}\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\|^{2}\|a^{*}(q_{s,x})a(q_{s,x})\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\|^{2}\big)^{1/2}

(6.38)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\absolutevalue{\l}}{N}\|f_{(t;s)}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{1/2}\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\|\;\|(\mathcal{N}+1)\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\|\;.

Since
\mathcal{N}\leq N
on
\mathcal{F}_{\perp\varphi_{t}}^{\leq N}
and
\norm{(\cN+1)^\al\W(t;s)\Om}^{2}=\langle\Omega,\mathcal{W}^{*}_{N}(t;s)(\mathcal{N}+1)^{2\alpha}\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\rangle
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{1/2}\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\|\;\|(\mathcal{N}+1)\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\langle\Omega,\mathcal{W}^{*}_{N}(t;s)(\mathcal{N}+1)\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\rangle^{1/2}\langle\Omega,\mathcal{W}^{*}_{N}(t;s)\frac{\mathcal{N}+1}{N}\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\rangle^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\langle\Omega,\mathcal{W}^{*}_{N}(t;s)(\mathcal{N}+1)\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\rangle.

Plugging this back to (
6.38
) yields

\displaystyle\big|\langle\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega,\big[\mathcal{R}_{N,s}^{(3)},\phi_{+}(f_{(t;s)})\big]\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\rangle\big|

(6.39)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\absolutevalue{\l}}{\sqrt{N}}\|f_{(t;s)}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\langle\Omega,\mathcal{W}^{*}_{N}(t;s)(\mathcal{N}+1)\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\rangle.

Combining (
6.32
), (
6.36
) and (
6.39
), we conclude

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{ \langle\Omega, \mathcal{W}_N^*(t;s) \big[ \mathcal{R}_{N,t}, \phi_+ ( f_{(t,s)} )\big] \mathcal{W}_N (t;s) \Omega\rangle}

(6.40)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C\absolutevalue{\l}}{\sqrt{N}}\|f_{(t;s)}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\langle\Omega,\mathcal{W}^{*}_{N}(t;s)(\mathcal{N}+1)\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\rangle\;.

This bounds the second term in the r.h.s. of (
6.25
).

4. Summarizing (
6.30
) and (
6.40
), we finally arrive at the following bound for (
6.25
):

\displaystyle\big|\langle\Omega,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{N}^{*}(t;0)\phi_{+}(q_{t}O\varphi_{t})\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;0)\Omega\rangle\big|

(6.41)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\absolutevalue{\l}}{\sqrt{N}}\int_{0}^{t}\|f_{(t;s)}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\langle\Omega,\mathcal{W}^{*}_{N}(t;s)(\mathcal{N}+1)\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\rangle\;.

Furthermore, from Lemma
6.2
we get, recalling
\|f_{(t;t)}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}=\|q_{t}O\varphi_{t}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq\|O\|_{\rm op}\|\varphi_{t}\|_{\ell^{2}(B_{r})}
,

\displaystyle\big|\langle\Omega,\mathcal{W}_{N}^{*}(t;0)\phi_{+}(q_{t}O\varphi_{t})\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;0)\Omega\rangle\big|

(6.42)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C\absolutevalue{\l}\|O\|_{\rm op}}{\sqrt{N}}\|\varphi_{t}\|_{\ell^{2}(B_{r})}{\int_{0}^{t}ds\;\exp\bigg( \abs{\l}\int_s^t d\tau\; \| \varphi_\tau\|_{\ell^\infty( \mathbb{Z}^d)}^2 \bigg)}\langle\Omega,\mathcal{W}^{*}_{N}(t;s)(\mathcal{N}+1)\mathcal{W}_{N}(t;s)\Omega\rangle\;.\;

This completes the bound for the second term in the r.h.s. of (
6.19
).

Lastly, combining (
6.22
) and (
6.42
) yields Lemma
6.1
.

Appendix A
Dispersive estimates for discrete Hartree equation

In this section we prove the dispersive estimate needed to control various time integrals of the condensate states
\varphi_{t}
.

We consider discrete Hartree equation on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}),\,d\geq 1
of the form:

(A.1)

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}\varphi=-\Delta\varphi+\lambda\absolutevalue{\varphi}^{2}\varphi.

Here
\absolutevalue{\l}\leq\lambda_{0}
for some
\lambda_{0}>0
to be determined later.
Write
\bigl\langle t\bigr\rangle:=\sqrt{1+t^{2}}
and
\norm{\cdot}_{p}:=\norm{\cdot}_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}
. The key ingredient in proving dispersive estimate for (
A.1
) is the
\ell^{1}\to\ell^{\infty}
estimate for the free propagator obtained in Stefanov–Kevrekidis
[
SK05
, Thm. 3]
: there exists
C_{1}=C_{1}(d)>0
s.th. for all
f\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
,

(A.2)

\displaystyle\norm{e^{it\Lap}f}_{1}\leq C_{1}\bigl\langle t\bigr\rangle^{-d/3}\norm{f}_{\infty}.

By the
\ell^{2}
-mass conservation for (
A.1
) and real interpolation , (
A.2
) implies that for any
2\leq p\leq\infty
and
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
, there exists
C_{2}=C_{2}(d,p)>0
s.th.

(A.3)

\displaystyle\norm{e^{it\Lap}f}_{p}\leq C_{2}\bigl\langle t\bigr\rangle^{-\alpha}\norm{f}_{p^{\prime}},\quad\alpha:=\frac{d}{3}\frac{p-2}{p}.

By a standard fixed-point argument, it follows from (
A.3
) that

Corollary A.1

(
[
KT98
]
,
[
SK05
]
)
.

Let
d\geq 3
. Then for all
2\leq q,\,r\leq\infty
satisfying

(A.4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}+\frac{d}{3r}\leq\frac{d}{6},\quad(q,r,d)\neq(2,\infty,3),

there exist
\lambda_{0}>0
and
C>0
depending only on
d,\,q,\,r
s.th. if
\absolutevalue{\l}\leq\lambda_{0}
in (
A.1
) and
\norm{\varphi_0}_{2}\leq 1
, then there exists a unique global solution to (
A.1
),
\varphi\in C(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
, with initial condition
\varphi_{0}
, satisfying

(A.5)

\displaystyle\norm{\varphi}_{L^{q}_{t}\ell^{r}_{x}}\leq C\norm{\varphi_0}_{2}.

For our purpose, we often need to control the integral
\int_{0}^{t}\norm{\varphi_\tau}_{\infty}\,d\tau
as
t\to\infty
. In dimension
3\leq d\leq 5
, this integral cannot be controlled directly using (
A.5
) and therefore requires more refined estimate.

In the next result, we use (
A.2
) to prove an upper bound on the
\ell^{\infty}
-decay for solutions to (
5.6
) with weak nonlinearity for
d\geq 3
. Related results were obtained in
[
SK05
, Thm. 7 ]
for
d=1,2
. See also
[
HY19
]
.

Theorem A.2
.

Let
d\geq 3
,
\varphi_{0}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
,
and
C_{1}>0
be the constant from (
A.2
). Then there exists
C_{2}=C_{2}(d)>0
s.th. if
\norm{\varphi_0}_{2}\leq 1
,
\absolutevalue{\l}\leq\lambda_{0}
, and either

(A.6)

d\geq 4
,
0<\lambda_{0}\leq\frac{1}{2}(C_{1}C_{2})^{-1}
, and
t>0
,

or

(A.7)

\displaystyle{\text{ $d=3$, $0<\lambda_{0}\leq\frac{1}{4}(C_{1}C_{2})^{-2}$, and $0<t\leq T:=e^{1/\sqrt{\absolutevalue{\l}}}-1$},}

then the unique solution
\varphi_{t}
to (
A.1
) with initial state
\varphi_{0}
obeys

(A.8)

\displaystyle\norm{\varphi_t}_{\infty}\leq 2C_{1}\bigl\langle t\bigr\rangle^{-\alpha}\norm{\varphi_0}_{1},\quad\alpha=\frac{d}{3}.

Proof.

Given a solution
\varphi_{t},\,t\geq 0
to (
A.1
), by the Duhamel principle, we have

(A.9)

\displaystyle\varphi_{t}=e^{it\Delta}\varphi_{0}-i\lambda\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\Delta}\absolutevalue{\varphi_s}^{2}\varphi_{s}\,ds,\quad t\geq 0.

Taking
L^{\infty}
-norm on both sides of (
A.9
) and applying (
A.2
) to the homogeneous part, we obtain

(A.10)

\displaystyle\norm{\varphi_t}_{\infty}\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\bigl\langle t\bigr\rangle^{-\alpha}\norm{\varphi_0}_{1}+\absolutevalue{\l}\int_{0}^{t}\norm{e^{i(t-s)\Lap}\abs{\varphi_s}^2\varphi_s}_{\infty}\,ds,\quad t\geq 0.

For
T>0
, define the quantity

(A.11)

\displaystyle M(T):=\sup_{0\leq s\leq T}\bigl\langle s\bigr\rangle^{\alpha}\norm{{\varphi_s}}_{\infty}.

Our goal is to show that

is a bounded function, globally for
d\geq 4
and up to large
T
for
d=3
.
The remainder proof will be different for
d\geq 4
and
d=3
, since in the latter case the dispersive estimate (
A.2
) is too weak to yields the necessary decay.

The case (
A.6
).
We claim that the inhomogeneous part in (
A.10
) is bounded, for
C_{1}>0
from (
A.2
), some
C_{2}=C_{2}(d)>0
to be specified below, and all

, as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\norm{e^{i(t-s)\Lap}\abs{\varphi_s}^{2}\varphi_s}_{\infty}\,ds\leq\norm{\varphi_0}_{2}^{2}C_{1}C_{2}M(T)\bigl\langle t\bigr\rangle^{-\alpha},\quad t\leq T.

A.12
) holds. Then plugging (
A.12
) back to (
A.10
) yields

\bigl\langle t\bigr\rangle^{\alpha}\norm{\varphi_t}_{\infty}\leq C_{1}\norm{\varphi_0}_{1}+\absolutevalue{\l}\norm{\varphi_0}_{2}^{2}C_{1}C_{2}M(T),\quad t\leq T.

Taking supremum over
t
on the l.h.s., we arrive at

(A.13)

\displaystyle M(T)\leq C_{1}\norm{\varphi_0}_{1}+\absolutevalue{\l}\norm{\varphi_0}_{2}^{2}C_{1}C_{2}M(T).

By condition (
A.6
) and the assumption

A.8

A.6

Thus it remains to prove (
A.12

To this end we apply (
A.2
) to bound the integrand in the l.h.s. of (

\displaystyle\norm{e^{i(t-s)\Lap}\abs{\varphi_s}^2\varphi_s}_{\infty}\leq

\displaystyle\bigl\langle t-s\bigr\rangle^{-\alpha}\norm{\abs{\varphi_s}^2\varphi_s}_{1}

(A.14)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bigl\langle t-s\bigr\rangle^{-\alpha}\norm{\varphi_s}_{2}^{2}\norm{\varphi_s}_{\infty}.

Using the trivial
\ell^{\infty}
-estimate

(A.15)

\displaystyle\norm{\varphi_s}_{\infty}\leq M(T)\bigl\langle s\bigr\rangle^{-\alpha},\quad s\leq T,

the conservation of

-norm, and the fact that

, inequality (

\displaystyle\norm{e^{i(t-s)\Lap}\abs{\varphi_s}^2\varphi_s}_{\infty}\leq\norm{\varphi_0}_{2}^{2}M(T)\bigl\langle t-s\bigr\rangle^{-\alpha}\bigl\langle s\bigr\rangle^{-\alpha},\quad 0\leq s\leq t\leq T.

Inserting (

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\norm{e^{i(t-s)\Lap}\abs{\varphi_s}^{2}\varphi_s}_{\infty}\,ds\leq\norm{\varphi_0}_{2}^{2}M(T)\int_{0}^{t}\bigl\langle t-s\bigr\rangle^{-\alpha}\bigl\langle s\bigr\rangle^{-\alpha}\,ds,\quad 0\leq t\leq T.

It remains to prove decay estimate for the integral in the r.h.s. of (
A.17
). We observe that for

, either

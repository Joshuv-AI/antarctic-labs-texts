Scaling limit of trees with vertices of fixed degrees and heights
Arthur Blanc-Renaudie, Emmanuel Kammerer

Abstract
We consider large uniform random trees where we fix for each vertex its degree and height. We prove, under natural conditions of convergence for the profile, that those trees properly renormalized converge. To this end, we study the paths from random vertices to the root using coalescent processes. As an application, we obtain scaling limits of Bienaymé–Galton–Watson trees in varying environment.

Introduction

1.1
Model

Trees with fixed degree sequence are universal models of random trees and are related to many other objects: Galton–Watson trees, configuration model, bipartite planar maps with fixed face degrees…The aim of this paper is to study
a variant of this model where we fix for each vertex its degree and also its height. More precisely, we are interested in the shortest-path distance on the rooted labelled trees defined below.

Consider a set of vertices
\{(i,j)\}_{i\geq 0,j\geq 1}
. We consider

(i,j)
to have height
i
and degree (number of children)
d_{i,j}^{n}\geq 0
. We use a superscript
n\in\mathbb{N}
as we study the behaviour of the trees as the degrees vary. For convenience, we assume that
on every line
i\geq 0
the sequence
(d^{n}_{i,j})_{j\geq 1}
is non-increasing. Also, to ensure that every vertex
(i,j)
of degree
d^{n}_{i,j}\geq 1
is connected to the root
(0,1)
, we assume that
d^{n}_{0,2}=0
and

\forall i\geq 0,\qquad\#\{j\geq 1;\ d^{n}_{i+1,j}>0\}\leq D^{n}_{i}\coloneqq\sum_{j\geq 1}d^{n}_{i,j}<\infty.

(1)

We construct the plane tree
\mathcal{T}^{n}
height by height:
(0,1)
is the root, and independently and uniformly for every height
i\geq 0
we attach each vertex in
\{(i+1,j)\}_{1\leq j\leq D_{i}^{n}}
to a vertex in
\{(i,j);\ {j\geq 1},d^{n}_{i,j}\geq 1\}
so that every vertex
(i,j)
has exactly
d_{i,j}^{n}
children (see Figure
2
). Note that
\mathcal{T}^{n}
has height
\mathsf{h}^{n}\coloneqq\inf\{i\geq 0;\ d^{n}_{i,1}=0\}
. The tree
\mathcal{T}^{n}
is equipped with the shortest path distance, where each edge has length
1
.

1.2
Main results

We now present our assumptions on the degrees for the scaling limit of
\mathcal{T}^{n}
. Without loss of generality, our assumptions are written so that the random metric space
\mathcal{T}^{n}/n
obtained after dividing the distances by
n

\sum_{i\geq 0}\delta_{i/n}D^{n}_{i}/(\sum_{i\geq 0}D^{n}_{i})

Note that the above assumption is equivalent to requiring a scaling limit for the height of random vertices. It also implies that
\liminf\mathsf{h}^{n}/n\geq 1

Then, to show the convergence of
(\mathcal{T}^{n}/n)
, we look at the
genealogies
of random vertices,
that is their paths to the root
. The rate at which genealogies merge actually only depends on the degrees. We assume informally that this rate converges. We distinguish two cases for such merging: either the paths merge at a vertex with large degree, or they merge among the numerous vertices of small degrees.

Concretely, we use a locally finite measure
\rho
on
(0,1)
to describe the merging rate due to small vertices. We also describe the limit of large degrees, using non negative parameters
\Theta=((\theta_{j}(t))_{j\geq 1})_{t\in(0,1)}
satisfying that:

•

For every height
t\in(0,1)
, the sequence

is non-increasing.

•

For every
t\in(0,1)
, we have

\sum_{j\geq 1}\theta_{j}(t)\leq 1.

•

The set
\{(j,t):\theta_{j}(t)\neq 0\}
is countable. Moreover, for all real numbers
0<x<y<1
:

\sum_{x\leq t\leq y}\sum_{j\geq 1}\theta_{j}(t)^{2}<\infty.

(2)

We assume that as

:

(\mathsf{Lim}_{\rho})

We have the following vague convergence of measures on
((0,1),\mathcal{B}((0,1)))

\sum_{1\leq i<n,j:d_{i,j}>0}\delta_{i/n}\binom{d^{n}_{i,j}}{2}/{\binom{D^{n}_{i}}{2}}\limit_{n\to\infty}\rho+\sum_{j,t:\theta_{j}(t)>0}\theta_{j}(t)^{2}\delta_{t}.

(\mathsf{Lim}_{\Theta})

We have the following vague convergence on

\sum_{1\leq i<n,j:d_{i,j}>0}\delta_{(i/n,d^{n}_{i,j}/D^{n}_{i})}\enskip\mathop{\longrightarrow}_{n\to\infty}\enskip\sum_{t,j:\theta_{j}(t)>0}\delta_{(t,\theta_{j}(t))}.

For every

1.3
Connections with previous works and applications

When one only prescribes the degrees of the vertices but not their heights, one obtains the well studied model of uniform trees with fixed degree sequence, whose scaling limit is studied for example in
[
BM14
]
,
[
BR21
]
and also in
[
Mar18
]
with applications to Bienaymé–Galton–Watson trees and random planar maps. This model is the analogue of the configuration model for random trees. Note that if one conditions on the heights of the vertices in a uniform tree with fixed degree sequence, then one obtains a uniform random tree with fixed degrees and heights. Moreover, the paper
[
AU20
]
gives conditions for the scaling limit of the profile of uniform trees with fixed degree sequence. Checking our assumptions in order to give a new proof of the scaling limit of uniform trees with fixed degree sequence thus seems feasible. In this example and in the next two paragraphs, the associated
\nu
,
\rho
and
\theta
may be random.

Besides, if one considers a Galton–Watson process in varying environment (GWVE) and conditions on the evolution of the total population, i.e. the profile of the tree, and on the number of children of each individual, i.e. the degrees, then the corresponding tree is a uniform random tree with fixed degrees and heights. As a result, our result provides the scaling limit of GWVE trees as soon as the hypothesis are satisfied by the profile and the degrees. The first scaling limit of the profile for GWVE trees was obtained by Kurtz
[
Kur78
]
under a third moment assumption. These results where subsequently extended and general conditions for the convergence of the profile can be found in
[
BS15
]
or in
[
FLL22
]
. See
Section

5
for an application of our results to GWVE trees using the scaling limit of the profile of
[
BS15
]
. Informally, under some assumptions which essentially ensure that the scaling limit of the profile from
[
BS15
]
holds, we obtain scaling limits of GWVE trees.

In the same direction, the scaling limit of the size of the
n
-th generation conditioned on survival up to time
n
was obtained in
[
Ker20
]
for GWVE under mild assumptions. Under a second moment assumption, the genealogy of the individuals at this
n
-th generation in GWVE trees was studied in
[
HPP22
]
in the critical case, and in
[
BFRS22
]
in the near critical case with a GHP scaling limit of the genealogy of the
n
-th generation in this latter work. In
[
CKKM22
]
, for i.i.d. environments, in the critical case and under a second moment assumption, a GHP scaling limit towards the Brownian continuous random tree was obtained. Although we do not wish to pursue in that direction, our results should be useful to study the stable case.

Finally, let us briefly mention that our approach is similar to the one we recently developed with Bellin and Kortchemski in
[
BBRKK23
,
BBRKK24
]
to study (critical) uniform attachment trees with freezing. However, as we no longer work in a Brownian case only, we need to use new techniques and ideas.

Definition of the limit

In this section, we define the limit trees appearing in Theorem
1.2
in a rather implicit way: we first construct, using a growth-coalescent process, a random infinite matrix which describes the distances between i.i.d. random points. We then prove, under the condition

(

Tight
GP

)

, leaf-tightness for this matrix, hence defining the limiting random measured metric space (see Proposition

2.1
Description of the distance matrix between uniform vertices in the limit tree

We go downward in the tree from random vertices to the root, and describe how their genealogies merge. This description gives both their heights and the heights of their most recent common ancestors, and so the distances between those vertices. We start with an informal construction of the genealogies’ coalescent.

Let
k
particles be born at i.i.d. times (heights) of law
\nu
. On each interval
[t,t-\mathrm{d}t]
, two particles merge into a cluster at rate
\rho(\mathrm{d}t)
. Moreover, at every time
t
such that
\theta_{1}(t)>0
, the clusters merge according to the sub-probability measure
(\theta_{j}(t))_{j\geq 1}
as follows: independently, each cluster is associated with some random integer
j
in
\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
with probability
\theta_{j}(t)
when
j\geq 1
and
\theta_{0}(t)\coloneqq 1-\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq 1}\theta_{j^{\prime}}(t)
when
j=0
.
Then, the clusters associated to
0
do not merge whereas for all
j\geq 1
, the clusters associated to
j
merge together into one cluster. Finally, at time zero, all the clusters which have not merged yet coalesce.

Here the birth time of the particle should be seen as the height of random vertices in the tree, and the merging time of two clusters as the height of their most recent common ancestor. And, depending on the degree of those ancestors (
2
or
\infty
), the coalescences occur differently (using respectively
\rho
or
\Theta
).

Formally, let us use the same notation as for coalescent processes where each particle is associated with an integer, where clusters are described by sets of integers and where each coalescence is performed by replacing two clusters by their union. See e.g.
[
Pit06
]
or
[
Ber06
]
for the use of this notation in the classical theory of coalescent and fragmentation processes.
We consider three types of independent random events:

Birth:
For every
r\in\mathbb{N}
, let
H_{r}
be a random variable in
[0,1]
of law
\nu
;

Small merge:
For every
q<r\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\Gamma_{q,r}
be a Poisson point process on
[0,1]
of intensity
\rho(\mathrm{d}t)
;

Large merge:
For every
t
such that
\theta_{1}(t)>0
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\Theta_{t,r}
be a random variable of law given by
\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\Theta_{t,r}=j}}\right)=\theta_{j}(t)
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. We write
S^{\Theta}(t,j)\coloneqq\{r\in\mathbb{N},\ \Theta_{t,r}=j\}
for all
j\geq 1
.

From there we describe a càglàd (left continuous with right limit) jump process
((\mathcal{P}_{r}^{k}(t))_{1\leq r\leq k})_{1\geq t\geq 0}
which is called a growth-coalescent process. The process starts at
\mathcal{P}_{1}^{k}(1)=\mathcal{P}_{2}^{k}(1)=\dots=\mathcal{P}_{k}^{k}(1)=\emptyset
and its jumps occur only at some time
t
satisfying one of the following conditions:

Birth:
If
t=H_{r}
for some
1\leq r\leq k
, then
\mathcal{P}_{r}^{k}(t^{+})=\{\emptyset\}
, and
\mathcal{P}_{r}^{k}(t)=\{r\}
;

Small merge:
If
t\in\Gamma_{q,r}
for some
q<r
, then whenever
\mathcal{P}_{q}^{k}(t^{+})\neq\emptyset
and
\mathcal{P}_{r}^{k}(t^{+})\neq\emptyset
we have
\mathcal{P}_{q}^{k}(t)=\mathcal{P}_{q}^{k}(t^{+})\cup\mathcal{P}_{r}^{k}(t^{+})
and
\mathcal{P}_{r}^{k}(t)=\emptyset
;

Large merge:
If
\theta_{1}(t)>0
, then for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\#(S^{\Theta}(t,j)\cap[k])\geq 2
, we write
m^{k}(t,j)
for the smallest integer
r\in S^{\Theta}(t,j)\cap[k]
such that
\mathcal{P}_{r}^{k}(t^{+})\neq\emptyset
. We have:

\forall r\in(S^{\Theta}(t,j)\cap[k])\setminus\{m^{k}(t,j)\},\quad\mathcal{P}_{r}^{k}(t)=\emptyset,\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{P}_{m^{k}(t,j)}^{k}(t)=\bigcup_{r\in S^{\Theta}(t,j)\cap[k]}\mathcal{P}_{r}^{k}(t^{+}).

There is a final jump at time
0
to reach the configuration
\mathcal{P}_{1}^{k}(0)=\{1,\dots,k\}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{k}(0)=\dots=\mathcal{P}_{k}^{k}(0)=\emptyset
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The process
((\mathcal{P}_{r}^{k}(t))_{1\leq r\leq k})_{0\leq t\leq 1}
is a.s. well defined.

Proof.

To see that such a process is well defined, it suffices to focus on the number of possible jumps.
Births only occur at
\{H_{r}\}_{1\leq r\leq k}
which is a finite subset of
(0,1)
since
\nu
has no atoms at zero or one. Note that for
1\leq r\leq k
and

we must have
\mathcal{P}_{r}^{k}(t)=\emptyset
so that the highest jump occurs at time
\max_{1\leq r\leq k}H_{r}
.

Then, for every
a>0
, we
prove that there is only a finite amount of possible times for small merges and large merges in
[a,\max_{1\leq r\leq k}H_{r}]
, so that the process
((\mathcal{P}_{r}^{k}(t))_{1\leq r\leq k})_{0\leq t\leq 1}
is well defined on
[a,1]
. Since a.s.
\mathcal{P}^{k}_{1}(0)=\{1,\dots,k\}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{k}(0)=\dots=\mathcal{P}_{k}^{k}(0)=\emptyset
, and since
a
is arbitrary this will conclude the proof.

Small merges can only occur when
t\in\Gamma_{q,r}
for some
1\leq q<r\leq k
. And a.s.
\Gamma_{q,r}
is locally finite on
(0,1)
as a Poisson point process of rate
\rho
which is locally finite on
(0,1)
.

For the large merges, note that for a jump to occur at time
t
there must exist
j\in\mathbb{N}
with
\#S^{\Theta}(t,j)\cap[k]\geq 2
. This occurs for every time
t
with probability upperbounded by
k^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\theta_{j}(t)^{2}
. Thus thanks to (
2
), a.s. the number of possible times
t\in[a,\max_{1\leq r\leq k}H_{r}]
where large merges can occur is finite.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

A.s. for every
0\leq t\leq 1
and
1\leq r\leq k<k^{\prime}
we have
\mathcal{P}_{r}^{k^{\prime}}(t)\cap[1,\dots,k]=\mathcal{P}_{r}^{k}(t)
.

Since the number of jumps is locally finite on
(0,1)
the result follows by a simple (but tedious) downward induction on
t
. We omit the details.
∎

The above consistency condition allows us to define for every
r\in\mathbb{N}
,
0\leq t\leq 1
,
\mathcal{P}_{r}(t)\coloneqq\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{P}_{r}^{k}(t)
.

Also, note that below
\min_{1\leq r\leq k}H_{r}
, there are no birth times anymore for the process
((\mathcal{P}_{r}^{k}(t))_{1\leq r\leq k})_{0\leq t\leq 1}
, so that the number of
r\in[k]
such that
\mathcal{P}^{k}_{r}(t)\neq\emptyset
can not increase when
t
decreases below
\min_{1\leq r\leq k}H_{r}
. Therefore, the process
((\mathcal{P}_{r}^{k}(t))_{1\leq r\leq k})_{0\leq t\leq 1}
undergoes only a finite number of jumps in
[0,1]
. The genealogy of the
k
particles is then described by a finite tree
\mathcal{T}_{k}
whose leaves, denoted by
V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}
correspond to the births of each particle and whose internal vertices correspond to the coalescences between clusters. Each vertex
v
of
\mathcal{T}_{k}
is associated with a time
t_{v}
corresponding to the birth time if
v
is a leaf or the coalescence time if
v
is an internal vertex. We equip this tree with the distance induced by the times of birth and coalescence: for every pair
u,v
of vertices of
\mathcal{T}_{k}
, the distance
d(u,v)
between
u
and

is given by

d(u,v)=t_{u}+t_{v}-2c(u,v),

where
c(u,v)
is the first time at which the clusters of
u
and
v
coalesce. Note that if
V_{q},V_{r}
are leaves of
\mathcal{T}_{k}
corresponding to the particles born at times
H_{q},H_{r}
for
1\leq q<r\leq k
, then

d(V_{q},V_{r})=H_{q}+H_{r}-2\max\{t\in[0,1],\ \exists m\in[k],\ q,r\in\mathcal{P}^{k}_{m}(t)\}.

(4)

Since the above equation can also be written

d(V_{q},V_{r})=H_{q}+H_{r}-2\max\{t\in[0,1],\ \exists m\geq 1,\ q,r\in\mathcal{P}_{m}(t)\},

the random variable
d(V_{q},V_{r})
does not depend on
k
.

2.2
Leaf-tightness

While the previous section defines the matrix of distances between random vertices of the limit trees, it is not enough to properly define the limit. This difference is quite subtle and has been extensively studied by Aldous in
[
Ald93
]
. As recalled in Appendix
B
, more precisely by applying Proposition
B.1
to the increasing sequence of the random trees
(\mathcal{T}_{k},d)
for
k\geq 1
defined in the previous subsection, equipped with the uniform probability measure on the leaves
\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}\}
and with the distance
d
defined in (
4
), to properly define the measured real tree
\mathcal{T}(\nu,\rho,\Theta)
as the (increasing) limit when
k\to\infty
of the
\mathcal{T}_{k}
’s when
k\to\infty
, it is enough to prove the following weak leaf tightness criterion:

\forall\delta>0,\qquad\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(d(V_{k+1},\{V_{1},V_{2},\dots,V_{k}\})>{5}\delta}}\right)=0.

Since
H_{k+1}
(the height of
V_{k+1}
) is sampled according to the probability measure
\nu
which satisfies
\nu(\{0\})=0
, we may assume for some
a>0
that
a<H_{k+1}<a+\delta
. Also, since, under

(

Tight
GP

)

,
\nu
has support
[0,1]
, by taking
k
large enough, we may assume that

Let us also state a technical lemma. It may be well known but we could not find it in the literature.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of càdlàg functions from
[0,1]
to
\mathbb{R}
which converges towards a càdlàg function

for the
J_{1}
topology of Skorokhod. Let
(\mathfrak{m}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of finite (positive) measures on
[0,1]
converging weakly towards a measure
\mathfrak{m}
. Assume that
\mathfrak{m}
has no atoms. Then,

\int_{0}^{1}f_{n}(x)\mathfrak{m}_{n}(\mathrm{d}x)\enskip\mathop{\longrightarrow}_{n\to\infty}\enskip\int_{0}^{1}f(x)\mathfrak{m}(\mathrm{d}x).

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{m}_{n}([0,1])
converges towards
\mathfrak{m}([0,1])
, we may assume that
\mathfrak{m}_{n}
and
\mathfrak{m}
are probability measures.
Let
Y_{n}
for
n\geq 1
and
Y
be random variables of respective laws
\mathfrak{m}_{n}
for
n\geq 1
and
\mathfrak{m}
. Since
\mathfrak{m}_{n}
converges weakly towards
\mathfrak{m}
, by Skorokhod’s representation theorem, we may assume that

converges a.s. to
Y
. Moreover, since
\mathfrak{m}
has no atoms, the càdlàg function

is a.s. continuous at

. As a result, by the point (b.5) of Proposition 2.1 in Chapter VI of
[
JS03
]
,

So, by dominated convergence (using the fact that

is bounded since it is càdlàg on

),

\int_{0}^{1}f_{n}(x)\mathfrak{m}_{n}(\mathrm{d}x)=\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[f_{n}(Y_{n})}}\right]\enskip\mathop{\longrightarrow}_{n\to\infty}\enskip\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[f(Y)}}\right]=\int_{0}^{1}f(x)\mathfrak{m}(\mathrm{d}x).

This is the desired result.

Appendix A
Background on the GP, GH and GHP topologies

A.1
The Gromov–Prokhorov (GP) topology

A measured metric space is a triple
(X,d,\mu)
such that
(X,d)
is a Polish space and
\mu
is a Borel probability measure on
X
. Two such spaces
(X,d,\mu)
,
(X^{\prime},d^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
are called GP-isometry-equivalent if and only if there exists an isometry
f:\supp(\mu)\to\supp(\mu^{\prime})
such that if
f_{\star}\mu
is the image of
\mu
by
f
then
f_{\star}\mu=\mu^{\prime}
.
Let
\mathbb{K}_{\text{GP}}
be the set of GP-equivalent classes of measured metric spaces. Given a measured metric space
(X,d,\mu)
, we write
[X,d,\mu]
for the GP-isometry-equivalence class of
(X,d,\mu)
and frequently use the notation
X
for either
(X,d,\mu)
or
[X,d,\mu]
.

We now recall the definition of the Prokhorov distance. Consider a metric space
(X,d)
. For every
A\subset X
and
\varepsilon>0
let
A^{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\{x\in X,d(x,A)<\varepsilon\}
be the open
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of
A
. Then given two (Borel) probability measures
\mu
,
\nu
on
X
, the Prokhorov distance between
\mu
and
\nu
is defined by

d_{P}(\mu,\nu)\coloneqq\inf\{\text{ $\varepsilon>0$: $\mu(A)\leq\nu(A^{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon$ and $\nu(A)\leq\mu(A^{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon$, for all Borel set $A\subset X$}\}.

The Gromov–Prokhorov (for short GP) distance is an extension of the Prokhorov’s distance: For every
[X,d,\mu],[X^{\prime},d^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}]\in\mathbb{K}_{\text{GP}}
the Gromov–Prokhorov distance between
X
and
X^{\prime}
is defined by

d_{\text{GP}}([X,d,\mu],[X^{\prime},d^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}])\coloneqq\inf_{S,\phi,\phi^{\prime}}d_{P}(\phi_{\star}\mu,\phi^{\prime}_{\star}\mu^{\prime}),

where the infimum is taken over all metric spaces
S
and isometric embeddings
\phi:X\to S
,
\phi^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to S
.
d_{\text{GP}}
is indeed a distance on
\mathbb{K}_{\text{GP}}
and
(\mathbb{K}_{\text{GP}},d_{\text{GP}})
is a Polish space (see e.g.
[
ADH13
]
).

We use another convenient characterization of the GP topology using the convergence of distance matrices: For every measured metric space
(X,d^{X},\mu^{X})
let
(x_{i}^{X})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of i.i.d. random variables of common distribution
\mu^{X}
and let
M^{X}\coloneqq(d^{X}(x_{i}^{X},x_{j}^{X}))_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}
. We have the following result from
[
Loe13
]
,

Lemma A.1
.

Let
(X^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{K}_{\text{GP}}^{\mathbb{N}}
and let
X\in\mathbb{K}_{\text{GP}}
then
X^{n}\limit^{\text{GP}}X
as
n\to\infty
if and only if
M^{X^{n}}
converges in distribution toward
M^{X}
for the product topology.

A.2
The Gromov–Hausdorff (GH) topology

Let
\mathbb{K}_{\text{GH}}
be the set of isometry-equivalent classes of compact metric spaces. For every metric space
(X,d)
, we write
[X,d]
for the isometry-equivalence class of
(X,d)
, and frequently write
X
for either
(X,d)
or
[X,d]
.

For every metric space
(X,d)
, the Hausdorff distance between
A,B\subset X
is given by

d_{H}(A,B)\coloneqq\inf\{\varepsilon>0,A\subset B^{\varepsilon},B\subset A^{\varepsilon}\}.

The Gromov–Hausdorff distance between
[X,d]
,
[X^{\prime},d^{\prime}]\in\mathbb{K}_{\text{GH}}
is given by

d_{\text{GH}}([X,d],[X^{\prime},d^{\prime}])\coloneqq\inf_{S,\phi,\phi^{\prime}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(d_{H}(\phi(X),\phi^{\prime}(X^{\prime}))}}\right),

where the infimum is taken over all metric spaces
S
and isometric embeddings
\phi:X\to S
,
\phi^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to S
.
d_{\text{GH}}
is indeed a distance on
\mathbb{K}_{\text{GH}}
and
(\mathbb{K}_{\text{GH}},d_{\text{GH}})
is a Polish space (see e.g.
[
ADH13
]
).

A.3
The Gromov–Hausdorff–Prokhorov (GHP) topology

Two measured metric spaces
(X,d,\mu)
,
(X^{\prime},d^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
are called GHP-isometry-equivalent if and only if there exists an isometry
f:X\to X^{\prime}
such that if
f_{\star}\mu
is the image of
\mu
by
f
then
f_{\star}\mu=\mu^{\prime}
.
Let
\mathbb{K}_{\text{GHP}}\subset\mathbb{K}_{\text{GP}}
be the set of isometry-equivalence classes of compact measured metric spaces.

The Gromov–Hausdorff–Prokhorov distance between
[X,d,\mu]
,
[X^{\prime},d^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}]\in\mathbb{K}_{\text{GHP}}
is given by

d_{\text{GHP}}([X,d,\mu],[X^{\prime},d^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}])\coloneqq\inf_{S,\phi,\phi^{\prime}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(d_{P}(\phi_{\star}\mu,\phi^{\prime}_{\star}\mu^{\prime})+d_{H}(\phi(X),\phi^{\prime}(X^{\prime}))}}\right),

where the infimum is taken over all metric spaces
S
and isometric embeddings
\phi:X\to S
,
\phi^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to S
.
d_{\text{GHP}}
is indeed a distance on
\mathbb{K}_{\text{GHP}}
and
(\mathbb{K}_{\text{GHP}},d_{\text{GHP}})
is a Polish space (see
[
ADH13
]
).

Appendix B
Leaf-tightness criteria

In this section,
\mathbf{X}=((X^{n},d^{n},p^{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
denotes a sequence of random compact measured metric spaces GHP-measurable. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
(x_{i}^{n})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of i.i.d. random variables of common distribution
p^{n}
, then let
M^{n}\coloneqq(d^{n}(x_{i}^{n},x_{j}^{n}))_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}
.

We say that
\mathbf{X}
is weak-leaf-tight if and only if

\forall\delta>0,\lim_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(d^{n}(x^{n}_{k+1},\{x^{n}_{1},x^{n}_{2},\ldots,x^{n}_{k}\})>\delta}}\right)=0.

(51)

We say that
\mathbf{X}
is strong-leaf-tight if and only if

\forall\delta>0,\lim_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(d_{H}(X^{n},\{x^{n}_{1},x^{n}_{2},\ldots,x^{n}_{k}\})>\delta}}\right)=0.

(52)

Those criteria were first introduced by Aldous
[
Ald91
,
Ald93
]
.

Proposition B.1
.

If
(M^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges weakly toward a random matrix
M
,
\mathbf{X}
is weak-leaf-tight, and for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
(X^{n},d^{n})
is a random tree equipped with random edge lengths, then
\mathbf{X}
converges weakly for the GP topology toward a random measured
\mathbb{R}
-tree
(X,d,p)
. Furthermore, if
(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
are i.i.d. random variables of law
p
then
M^{X}\coloneqq(d(x_{i},x_{j}))_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}=^{(d)}M
.

Proof.

The result is directly adapted from Theorem 3 of Aldous
[
Ald93
]
using modern formalism.

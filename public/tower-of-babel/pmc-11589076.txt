Clinical Significance of Prior Ramucirumab Use on the Effectiveness of Nivolumab as the Third-Line Regimen in Gastric Cancer: A Multicenter Retrospective Study
Authors: Yuka Obayashi, Shoichiro Hirata, Yoshiyasu Kono, Makoto Abe, Koji Miyahara, Masahiro Nakagawa, Michihiro Ishida, Yasuhiro Choda, Kenta Hamada, Masaya Iwamuro, Seiji Kawano, Yoshiro Kawahara, Motoyuki Otsuka
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and ObjectiveBecause vascular endothelial growth factor inhibition has been suggested to improve immune cell function in the cancer microenvironment, we examined whether using ramucirumab (RAM) before nivolumab usage is more effective in advanced gastric cancer.MethodsThis was a multicenter retrospective observational study. We analyzed patients who received nivolumab monotherapy as the third-line regimen for unresectable advanced or recurrent gastric cancer between October 2017 and December 2022. They were divided into the RAM (RAM-treated) group and the non-RAM (non-treated) group according to the RAM usage in the second-line regimen. The primary outcome was to compare the overall survival after nivolumab administration in the third-line regimen between the RAM and non-RAM groups.ResultsFifty-two patients were included in the present study: 42 patients in the RAM group and ten patients in the non-RAM group. The median overall survival was significantly longer in the RAM group than in the non-RAM group (8.5 months vs 6.9 months, p < 0.05). In the RAM group, patients without peritoneal metastasis had significantly better median overall survival than those with peritoneal metastasis (23.8 months vs 7.7 months, p = 0.0033). Multivariate Cox-proportional hazards analyses showed that the presence of peritoneal metastasis (hazard ratio, 2.4; 95% confidence interval 1.0–5.7) alone was significantly associated with overall survival in the RAM group.ConclusionsThe use of RAM prior to nivolumab monotherapy may contribute to prolonged survival in patients with gastric cancer, especially those without peritoneal metastasis.

Key Points

Prior ramucirumab therapy could enhance the efficacy of nivolumab monotherapy in the later-line regimen, especially in patients with gastric cancer without peritoneal metastasis.An indication of ramucirumab and the absence of peritoneal metastasis could be significant factors for selecting nivolumab monotherapy in the third-line regimen.

Introduction

Nivolumab (Nivo), an immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI), has been used as the third-line monotherapy regimen in patients with advanced gastric cancer [1]. However, based on the results of two recent phase III trials, Nivo combined with oxaliplatin-based chemotherapy is now widely used as the first-line regimen in patients with human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative gastric cancer [2, 3], and better survival is expected. Nonetheless, whether the use of Nivo in the first-line regimen improves survival in patients with lower combined positive score remains controversial. In such populations, Nivo monotherapy remains an option in the later-line regimen [4–7]. Further, because other types of targeted agents, such as anti-fibroblast growth factor receptor 2 or anti-claudin-18 isoform 2, will be used in the first-line regimen in some cases, the use of Nivo monotherapy in the later-line regimen is expected to increase [8, 9]. Therefore, determining the predictive factors of Nivo monotherapy in the later-line regimen remains crucial.

Anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) agents and ICIs have been reported to have a synergistic effect when used simultaneously or sequentially [10–12]. Currently, ramucirumab (RAM), an anti-VEGF receptor-2 agent, plus paclitaxel, is the standard second-line regimen for patients with advanced gastric cancer [7]. Although a previous study reported a better response to RAM plus taxanes after exposure to ICIs [13], data on the efficacy of Nivo monotherapy after using anti-VEGF agents are scarce. Therefore, the main objective of this study was to examine whether prior use of RAM before Nivo improves survival. Furthermore, we aimed to identify the patient population that benefits from Nivo after RAM administration. We believe that identifying the patient population for whom Nivo treatment should be considered as later rather than as a first-line therapy will clarify the best sequential chemotherapy in gastric cancer.

Methods

Study Design and Participants

We conducted a multicenter retrospective observational study at two institutions (Hiroshima Citizens Hiroshima City Hospital and Okayama University Hospital). Eligible patients were aged 20 years or older with histologically confirmed unresectable advanced or recurrent gastric cancer and were treated with Nivo monotherapy in the third-line regimen between 1 October, 2017 and 31 December, 2022. Those who had a chemotherapy-free interval, prior RAM treatment as the first-line regimen, Nivo monotherapy as later than the third-line regimen, and conversion surgery were excluded.

The study procedures were implemented in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. The study protocol was approved by the ethics committee of each institution (approval number 2307-031), and informed consent was acquired using the opt-out method.

Procedures

We divided the participants into two groups: those who received RAM treatment in the second-line regimen (RAM group) and those who did not (non-RAM group). We retrospectively reviewed the medical records, and the following clinicopathological parameters of all participants were collected: age, sex, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status, metastatic site, main histological type of adenocarcinoma (differentiated or undifferentiated), HER2 status, serum albumin level, neutrophil/lymphocyte ratio, and the prognostic nutritional index.

Outcomes

The primary outcome was to compare overall survival (OS) after Nivo monotherapy in the third-line regimen between the RAM and non-RAM groups. The secondary outcomes were progression-free survival (PFS) after Nivo monotherapy in the third-line regimen, overall response rate (ORR) and disease control rate (DCR) of Nivo monotherapy in the two groups, and exploring favorable populations for better survival in the RAM group. Progression-free survival was assessed by the independent RECIST review committee and defined as the time from the beginning of Nivo monotherapy to the date of confirmed progressive disease as per RECIST guidelines (version 1.1) or death from any cause, whichever occurred first. Overall survival was defined as the time from the beginning of Nivo monotherapy to the date of death from any cause. Patients without disease progression were censored at the latest follow-up. Overall response rate was defined as the proportion of patients with the best overall response of complete or partial response, and DCR was defined as the proportion of patients with complete response, partial response, or stable disease.

Statistical Analyses

Sequential data were expressed as the median and interquartile range. Categorical data were compared using a chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact test. Non-parametric data were compared using Wilcoxon’s signed-rank test. Overall survival and PFS were assessed using the Kaplan–Meier method and compared using the log-rank test. Median values and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were also calculated. The Cox proportional hazards model was adopted to examine the prognostic factors of OS.

All statistical analyses were performed using the JMP Pro software program, version 14.0 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA). Data were considered to be statistically significant when p < 0.05.

Secondary Outcomes

Regarding PFS after Nivo monotherapy as the third-line regimen, no significant differences were observed between the two groups (median PFS: 2.4 months vs 1.9 months, p = 0.68, Fig. 2b).

Table 2 shows the ORR and DCR after Nivo monotherapy as the third-line regimen between the RAM and non-RAM groups. The ORR was relatively higher in the RAM group than in the non-RAM group, although the difference was not statistically significant (26% vs 0, p = 0.54). A similar trend was observed for DCR (57% vs. 25%, p = 0.33). According to the forest plot, sex, lymph node metastasis, peritoneal metastasis (PM), and Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status were significantly associated with OS in the RAM group (Fig. 3). However, only the positivity of PM (hazard ratio: 2.4, 95% CI 1.0–5.7) was significantly associated with OS according to multivariate analyses using significant factors in univariate analyses (Table 3).

Discussion

In the present study, we determined the efficacy of prior RAM treatment before Nivo monotherapy for better survival in patients with unresectable advanced or recurrent gastric cancer. In particular, patients without PM derived more clinical benefit from prior use of RAM, followed by Nivo monotherapy as the third-line regimen.

Our result is consistent with the results of the subpopulation analyses in the ATTRACTION-2 study, which showed reduced risks of death and disease progression in patients with prior RAM treatment [1]. In that study, the ORR and DCR of Nivo was higher in patients with prior RAM treatment than in those without (ORR: 22.2% vs 11.8%, DCR; 55.6% vs 42.2%). In our study, the ORR and DCR in the RAM treatment group were similar to the results of ATTRACION-2 study, which validates the benefit of prior RAM treatment for Nivo effectiveness in a real-world setting. Vascular endothelial growth factor reportedly inhibits T-cell infiltration in the tumor microenvironment [14, 15], whereas anti-VEGF agents, such as RAM, modify such immune-related tumor microenvironments and increase the number of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes, significantly improving the therapeutic effects of ICI therapy [16, 17]. Furthermore, there is a case report where disease control persisted for several months even after the discontinuation of RAM [18]. Although there are no basic data, tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes may remain for a long period after RAM administration. Therefore, we speculate that prior use of RAM before Nivo monotherapy increases TIL levels, which could contribute to the better response to Nivo observed in patients with gastric cancer.

While RAM treatment before Nivo therapy as the third-line regimen in patients without PM showed significantly longer PFS and OS, the effects were relatively minimal in patients with PM. These were consistent with the results of a multicenter retrospective study where patients with PM had a poor prognosis when treated with Nivo as the third-line therapy [19]. In patients with colorectal cancer with PM, TILs are suppressed because of the high intra-tumoral fibrosis [20], which decreases the efficacy of ICIs. As intra-tumoral fibrosis is higher in gastric cancer with PM than in that without PM, as in colorectal cancer [21], we speculate that Nivo therapy is not effective in gastric cancer with PM compared to that without PM.

In our study, the OS after Nivo therapy was significantly longer in the RAM group than in the non-RAM group, while the PFS was not significantly different between the two groups. Previous studies have reported that administering cytotoxic chemotherapy after Nivo therapy may enhance the antitumor effect in gastric cancer [22]. In this study, the percentage of patients who received later-line chemotherapy after Nivo therapy was higher in the RAM group than in the non-RAM group. This may be a reason why only OS, not PFS, improved after Nivo therapy in the RAM group. Furthermore, the better ORR and DCR of Nivo in the RAM group may contribute to a longer OS. We consider that this may be the main contribution of prior RAM treatment to better survival. However, both PFS and OS significantly improved after Nivo therapy in the RAM group, compared with that in the non-RAM group when the patients did not have PM. As described above, PM can induce fibrosis in tumor tissues, preventing TILs and suppressing the efficacy of ICIs [21]. In cases without PM, prior use of RAM before Nivo monotherapy may have additional effects, including increasing TILs, which may induce both longer PFS and OS.

A previous report has examined the impact of prior use of RAM on the efficacy of later ICI therapy [23]. In the report, contrary to our results, the OS with ICI was significantly longer in patients receiving second-line therapy without RAM than in those receiving RAM and a taxane. The discrepancy may be because the patients’ backgrounds, which could affect the response of Nivo, such as the existence of PM, were different from those in our study. Further, although the overall OS was longer in patients receiving second-line therapy without RAM in their study, they also reported that the better response to RAM and a taxane in the second-line chemotherapy was an important and better prognostic marker for OS with the subsequent third-line use of ICI therapy, which is consistent with our results.

The present study had several limitations. First, the sample size was small even though this was a multicenter study. Second, because this was a retrospective study, there are selection biases. The use of RAM was dependent on the condition of the patients, especially with the contraindication of RAM use, such as uncontrolled hypertension, proteinuria, and a history of thromboembolism or cerebrovascular disease. The RAM group had better prognostic factors such as younger age, better performance status, better nutrition status, and less undifferentiated cancers. Furthermore, there may be other unmeasured confounding factors such as the Glasgow Prognostic Score and body mass index that could affect prognosis. Third, because we could not assess the microsatellite instability status of all patients, we could not exclude super-responders of Nivo monotherapy.

Conclusions

In this study, we aimed to clarify prior RAM treatment before Nivo monotherapy prolonged survival and to identify the patient population who benefits from this sequential therapy. Our results showed that RAM treatment as the second-line regimen followed by Nivo monotherapy as the third-line regimen improves survival, especially in patients without PM. These results may provide a rationale for selecting patients who will benefit from later-line Nivo therapy and for determining the appropriate sequence of chemotherapy for advanced gastric cancer. This could influence the clinical practice guidelines and improve patient outcomes. Further investigations, such as prospective studies or randomized controlled trials, are crucial to confirm these findings.

Abstract
An edge-colored graph is called rainbow graph if all the colors on its edges are distinct. Given a positive integer n n and a graph G G , the anti-Ramsey number a ​ r ​ ( n , G ) ar(n,G) is defined to be the minimum number of colors r r such that there exists a rainbow copy of G G in any exactly r r -edge-coloring of K n K_{n} . Wu et al. (Anti-Ramsey numbers for vertex-disjoint triangles, Discrete. Math. , 346 (2022), 113123) determined the anti-Ramsey number a ​ r ​ ( n , k ​ K 3 ) ar(n,kK_{3}) for n ≥ 2 ​ k 2 − k + 2 n\geq 2k^{2}-k+2 . In this paper, we extend this result by improving the lower bound on n n to n ≥ 15 ​ k + 57 n\geq 15k+57 .

Abstract
. In this article, we introduce the notion of n n -cotorsion pairs in extriangulated categories, which extends both the cotorsion pairs established by Nakaoka and Palu and the n n -cotorsion pairs in triangulated categories developed by Chang and Zhou. We further prove that any mutation of an n n -cotorsion pair remains an n n -cotorsion pair. As applications, we provide a geometric characterization of n n -cotorsion pairs in n n -cluster categories of type A ∞ A_{\infty} , and we realize mutations of n n -cotorsion pairs geometrically via rotations of certain configurations of n n -admissible arcs.

1.
Introduction

Extriangulated categories were introduced by Nakaoka and Palu in their foundational work
[
23
]
as a common generalization of exact categories and triangulated categories. This framework encompasses both exact categories, including abelian ones, and extension-closed subcategories of triangulated categories as particular cases. In addition, a number of examples have been constructed that fall outside the realm of either exact or triangulated categories, as shown in
[
23
,
25
,
27
,
15
,
6
]
, thereby demonstrating the flexibility and breadth of the extriangulated setting.

Let
n
be a positive integer. Motivated by structural properties of Gorenstein projective and Gorenstein injective modules over Iwanaga-Gorenstein rings, Huerta, Mendoza, and Pérez
[
14
, Definition 2.2]
formulated the concept of
n
-cotorsion pairs (as well as their left and right variants) in abelian categories. Notably, the case
n=1
recovers the classical notion of complete cotorsion pairs.
Building on this idea, He and Zhou
[
10
]
extended the definition to the framework of extriangulated categories. In particular, when
n=1
, their definition coincides with the cotorsion pairs introduced by Nakaoka and Palu in
[
23
, Definition 4.1]
.
More recently, Chang and Zhou
[
5
]
proposed a notion of
n
-cotorsion pairs in triangulated categories, further generalizing the classical theory. It is important to note that their construction differs from the one in
[
10
]
, even when restricted to triangulated settings, thereby giving rise to distinct notions under the same terminology.
For more details, see
[
5
, Remark 3.4 and Example 3.5]
.

Iyama and Yoshino
[
17
]
introduced the notion of mutation in triangulated categories, and subsequently, Zhou and Zhu
[
26
]
proved that the mutation of a torsion pair remains a torsion pair. In a further development, Zhou and Zhu
[
25
]
formulated the concept of mutation pairs of subcategories in extriangulated categories, extending Iyama-Yoshino’s definition
[
17
, Definition 2.5]
from the triangulated setting.

The concept of
n
-cluster categories originates from the study of cluster algebras and higher-dimensional homological algebra. Cluster categories were initially introduced as a categorical model to realize the combinatorial structures of cluster algebras, which were developed by Fomin and Zelevinsky
[
7
]
. In the classical case where
n=1
, cluster categories provided a triangulated framework to understand tilting theory and the process of mutation among tilting objects.
To generalize these ideas to higher dimensions, Iyama
[
16
]
developed higher Auslander–Reiten theory and introduced the notion of
n
-cluster tilting subcategories. Building upon this, Keller and others
[
20
,
1
,
2
,
18
]
constructed
n
-cluster categories as orbit categories of bounded derived categories, which encode the properties of
n
-cluster tilting theory and provide a natural setting for higher-dimensional analogues of classical tilting.

The study of
n
-cluster categories is significant for several reasons. First, they provide a rich framework in which to explore the mutation and classification of
n
-cluster tilting objects. Second, they serve as a bridge between combinatorial and categorical approaches to higher cluster algebras. Third, they have many nice properties, for instance Hom-finite, Krull–Schmidt, and
(n+1)
-Calabi–Yau, a property that connects them to important topics in geometry and physics, including mirror symmetry and string theory.
Moreover, the theory of
n
-cluster categories has led to the development of new algebraic structures such as
n
-abelian categories
[
8
]
and higher cotorsion pairs
[
14
,
10
,
5
]
, enriching the landscape of modern representation theory. These structures also provide a unifying language that links derived categories, triangulated and extriangulated categories, and higher homological techniques. As such,
n
-cluster categories remain a central and dynamic area of research in higher representation theory.

Classifying cotorsion pairs is an essential step toward understanding the internal structure of categories. In triangulated settings, cotorsion pairs are closely connected to the theories of
t
-structures and co-
t
-structures, which are fundamental tools in the study of derived categories and algebraic geometry. In extriangulated categories, which generalize both exact and triangulated categories, cotorsion pairs offer a natural method for analyzing extension theory in settings that do not conform strictly to either traditional framework.
Furthermore, cotorsion pair classification has deep connections with silting theory, support
\tau
-tilting theory, and the theory of cluster tilting subcategories. These relationships have practical implications for constructing and identifying key categorical objects such as rigid and cluster-tilting objects, as well as for understanding approximation theory.
In the context of higher homological algebra, classifying
n
-cotorsion pairs provides insight into higher extension vanishing phenomena and their relation to
n
-cluster tilting theory. This direction of research continues to expand the understanding of homological dimensions and has become an important theme in modern representation theory and categorical algebra.

In this paper, we propose a weaker version of
n
-cotorsion pairs in extriangulated categories, compared to the definition given by He and Zhou
[
10
]
. Our formulation not only generalizes the cotorsion pairs introduced by Nakaoka and Palu but also encompasses the
n
-cotorsion pairs in triangulated categories developed by Chang and Zhou. We further show that any mutation of an
n
-cotorsion pair yields another
n
-cotorsion pair.
As applications, we provide a geometric characterization of
n
-cotorsion pairs in
n
-cluster categories of type
A_{\infty}
, and offer a geometric interpretation of their mutations via rotations of certain configurations of
n
-admissible arcs.
We note that an
n
-cluster category of type
A_{\infty}
contains infinitely many indecomposable objects. Moreover, its Auslander-Reiten quiver consists of
n
components, making the structure significantly more intricate than in the finite case.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall basic notions of cotorsion pairs and mutation pairs in extriangulated categories. In Section 3, we introduce the definition of
n
-cotorsion pairs, review the construction of subfactor triangulated categories from
[
25
]
, investigate its compatibility with our setting, and establish the main result. In Section 4, we give a geometric characterization of
n
-cotorsion pairs in
n
-cluster categories of type
A_{\infty}
, and a geometric interpretation of their mutations via rotations of certain configurations of
n
-admissible arcs.

\mathcal{C}
is an extriangulated category with enough projective and enough injective objects. An object

is said to be

Dually, an object

2.2.
Cotorsion pairs in extriangulated categories

In this subsection, we recall the notion of cotorsion pairs in extriangulated categories.

Definition 2.4
.

[
23
, Definition 4.1]

Let
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
be full additive subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
, closed under direct summands and isomorphisms. The pair
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
is called a
cotorsion pair
if the following conditions are satisfied:

(a)

\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})=0
;

(b)

The pair
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
is
complete
in the sense of
[
9
]
, that is, for every object
C\in\mathcal{C}
, there exist two
\mathbb{E}
-triangles

V_{C}\to U_{C}\to C\dashrightarrow,\quad C\to V^{C}\to U^{C}\dashrightarrow

with
U_{C},U^{C}\in\mathcal{U}
and
V_{C},V^{C}\in\mathcal{V}
.

Since
\mathcal{C}
has enough projective and injective objects, cotorsion pairs in
\mathcal{C}
admit an equivalent characterization.

Proposition 2.5
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is an extriangulated category with enough projectives and enough injectives,
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
are full additive subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
which are closed under direct summands and isomorphisms. Then
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
is a cotorsion pair if and only if the following conditions are satisfied.

(1)

\mathcal{U}={{}^{\perp_{1}}\mathcal{V}}
.

(2)

\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{U}^{\perp_{1}}
.

(3)

\mathcal{U}
is contravariantly finite and
\mathcal{V}
is covariantly finite in
\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
is a cotorsion pair. Then it is easy to check conditions (1), (2) and (3) are satisfied. Conversely, suppose conditions (1), (2) and (3) are satisfied, then
\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})=0
. Moreover, for any project object
P\in\mathcal{P}
, since
\mathbb{E}(P,\mathcal{C})=0
, we have
\mathbb{E}(P,\mathcal{V})=0
, then
\mathcal{P}\subseteq{{}^{\perp_{1}}\mathcal{V}}=\mathcal{U}
. Similarly, we can prove
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
. By
[
25
, Remark 3.20]
,
\mathcal{U}
is strongly contravariantly finite and
\mathcal{V}
is strongly covariantly finite in
\mathcal{C}
. That means for any object
C\in\mathcal{C}
, there exist two
\mathbb{E}
-triangles

X_{C}\rightarrow U_{C}\overset{f}{\rightarrow}C\dashrightarrow,

where
f
is a right
\mathcal{U}
-approximation of
C
, and

C\overset{g}{\rightarrow}V^{C}\rightarrow Y^{C}\dashrightarrow,

where
g
is a left
\mathcal{V}
-approximation of

\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{U},X_{C})=0
and
\mathbb{E}(Y^{C},V)=0
, so
X_{C}\in\mathcal{U}^{\perp_{1}}=\mathcal{V}
and
Y^{C}\in{{}^{\perp_{1}}\mathcal{V}}=\mathcal{U}
. Thus
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
is a cotorsion pair.

In this section, we fix an integer

3.1.

-cotorsion pairs in extriangulated categories

We define
n
-cotorsion pairs in an extriangulated category, which is a generalization of the cotorsion pairs in the sense of Nakaoka and Palu
[

]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an extriangulated category and
\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}
be two subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
which are closed
under direct summands and isomorphisms. The pair
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
is called an
n
-
cotorsion pair
if the following conditions hold.

(1)

\mathcal{U}=\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{}^{\perp_{k}}}\mathcal{V}
for all
1\leq k\leq n
;

(2)

\mathcal{V}=\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{n}\mathcal{U}^{\perp_{k}}
for all
1\leq k\leq n
;

(3)

\mathcal{U}
is contravariantly finite and
\mathcal{V}
is covariantly finite.

Remark 3.2
.

By Proposition
2.5
, the concept of
1
-cotorsion pair is compatible with the classical definition of a cotorsion pair in the sense of Nakaoka and Palu
[
23
]

is an

-cotorsion pair in
\mathcal{C}
if and only if
\mathcal{U}
is

-cluster tilting.

-cotorsion pair defined in this paper is a weaker version than that in

(2)

To prove the main results of this section, we need the following preparations.

Definition 3.9
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is an extriangulated category. We call a pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
of subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
a
torsion

pair
if

\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})=0\;\;\text{and}\;\;\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{X}\ast\mathcal{Y}.

We have the following Wakamatsu-type lemma.

Lemma 3.10
.

(1)
Let
\cal X
be a contravariantly finite and extension closed subcategory of an extriangulated category
\mathcal{C}
. Then
(\cal X,\cal X^{\bot})
is a torsion pair.

(2)
Let
\cal X

(1) It is obvious that

Assume that

\mathcal{C}
, since

-triangle

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{C\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g}
\textstyle{Y\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\cal X
-approximation

of

. We only have to show

.

Take any morphism

with

.
By
[

, we have a commutative diagram

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\scriptstyle{v}

\textstyle{X^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

(2) This is dual of (1).
∎

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
satisfying
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{X}\subset\mathcal{Z}
. Then

(1)

\mathcal{X}
is a contravariantly finite subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
if and only if
\overline{\mathcal{X}}
is a contravariantly finite subcategory of
\mathfrak{U}
.

(2)

\mathcal{X}
is a covariantly finite subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
if and only if
\overline{\mathcal{X}}
is a covariantly finite subcategory of
\mathfrak{U}
.

(3)

\mathcal{X}
is a functorially finite subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
if and only if
\overline{\mathcal{X}}
is a functorially finite subcategory of
\mathfrak{U}
.

Proof.

We only prove the first assertion, the others can be proved similarly.

Assume that
\mathcal{X}
is a contravariantly finite subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
. Let
Z
be an object in
\mathcal{Z}
. Since
\mathcal{X}
is contravariantly finite, there exists an object
X\in\mathcal{X}
and a morphism
f\colon X\to Z
which serves as a right
\mathcal{X}
-approximation of
Z
in
\mathcal{C}
. It is straightforward to verify that the induced morphism
\overline{f}\colon X\to Z
is a right
\overline{\mathcal{X}}
-approximation of
Z
in the quotient category
\mathfrak{U}
.

Conversely, assume that
\overline{\mathcal{X}}
is a contravariantly finite subcategory of
\mathfrak{U}
. Since
\mathcal{D}
is functorially finite in
\mathcal{C}
, it follows from Lemma
3.11
and its dual that
\mathcal{Z}
is also functorially finite in
\mathcal{C}
. Let
C
be an object in
\mathcal{C}
. Then, as
\mathcal{Z}
is functorially finite, there exists an object
Z\in\mathcal{Z}
along with a morphism
f\colon Z\to C
that is a right
\mathcal{Z}
-approximation of
C
.

By assumption, there exists
X\in\mathcal{X}
and a morphism
\overline{g}\colon X\to Z
in
\mathfrak{U}
such that it is a right
\overline{\mathcal{X}}
-approximation of
Z
. Moreover, since
\mathcal{D}
is functorially finite in
\mathcal{C}
and
Z\in\mathcal{Z}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
, there exists an object
D\in\mathcal{D}
with a morphism
c\colon D\to Z
forming a right
\mathcal{D}
-approximation of
Z
. We claim that the morphism

(f\circ g,\,f\circ c)\colon X\oplus D\to C

is a right
\mathcal{X}
-approximation of
C
in
\mathcal{C}
.

To verify the claim, consider any morphism
h\colon X^{\prime}\to C
with
X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}
. Since
f\colon Z\to C
is a right
\mathcal{Z}
-approximation, there exists
\ell\colon X^{\prime}\to Z
such that
h=f\circ\ell
. As
\overline{g}\colon X\to Z
is a right
\overline{\mathcal{X}}
-approximation in
\mathfrak{U}
, there exists a morphism
\overline{k}\colon X^{\prime}\to X
such that
\overline{\ell}=\overline{g}\circ\overline{k}
and then

\overline{\ell-g\circ k}=\overline{0}.

Hence, the morphism
\ell-g\circ k
factors through some
D_{1}\in\mathcal{D}
; that is, there exist morphisms
a_{1}\colon X^{\prime}\to D_{1}
and
a_{2}\colon D_{1}\to Z
such that
\ell-g\circ k=a_{2}\circ a_{1}
. Since
c\colon D\to Z
is a right
\mathcal{D}
-approximation, there exists
d\colon D_{1}\to D
such that
a_{2}=c\circ d
. Consequently,

\ell=g\circ k+c\circ d\circ a_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad h=f\circ\ell=f\circ(g\circ k+c\circ d\circ a_{1}).

This shows that
h
factors through the morphism
(f\circ g,f\circ c)\colon X\oplus D\to C
via

X^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\binom{k}{d\circ a_{1}}}X\oplus D.

Thus, we conclude that
(f\circ g,f\circ c)
is indeed a right
\mathcal{X}
-approximation of
C
in
\mathcal{C}
, and therefore,
\mathcal{X}
is contravariantly finite.
∎

Thus, one can consider the relationship between cotorsion pairs in
\mathcal{C}
whose cores contain

\mathcal{D}
and cotorsion pairs in the subfactor triangulated category

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
be an
n
-cotorsion pair in
\mathcal{C}
with
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{X})

(\overline{\mathcal{X}},\overline{\mathcal{Y}})
is an

-cotorsion pair in
\mathfrak{U}
with
\mathcal{I}(\overline{\mathcal{X}})=\overline{\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{X})}

is a bijection from the set of
n
-cotorsion pair in
\mathcal{C}
whose cores contain
\mathcal{D}
to the set of
n
-cotorsion pair in
\mathfrak{U}
.

Proof.

Since
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
is an
n
-cotorsion pair in
\mathcal{C}
with
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{X})
, we have
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{X}\subset\mathcal{Z}

\overline{\mathcal{Y}}
are subcategories of

\overline{\mathcal{X}}=\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}{{}^{\bot}}\overline{\mathcal{Y}}\langle i\rangle
for all

(\overline{\mathcal{X}},\overline{\mathcal{Y}})
is an
n
-cotorsion pair in

. In this case, we have
\mathcal{I}(\overline{\mathcal{X}})=\overline{\mathcal{X}}\cap\overline{\mathcal{Y}}=\overline{\mathcal{X}\cap\mathcal{Y}}=\overline{\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{X})}

The bijection from the set of

-cotorsion pair in
\mathcal{C}
whose cores contain

∎

Let

be an

-cotorsion pair in
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{X})
be a functorially finite subcategory of

\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{}^{\perp_{k}}}\mathcal{D}=\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{n}\mathcal{D}^{\perp_{k}}

(\mu^{-1}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{X}),\mu^{-1}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{Y}))\hskip 5.69054pt\mbox{and}\hskip 8.53581pt(\mu_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{X}),\mu_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{Y}))

-cotorsion pairs in

\mathcal{I}(\mu^{-1}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{X}))=\mu^{-1}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{X}))\hskip 5.69054pt\mbox{and}\hskip 8.53581pt\mathcal{I}(\mu_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{X}))=\mu_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{X})).

. So both

and

, our result recovers and extends the classification of classical cotorsion pairs in cluster categories of type
A_{\infty}
previously established by Ng
[
22
]
and Zhou–Zhou
[
26
]
. As an application, we present a geometric realization of the mutation of
n
-cotorsion pairs through rotations of the corresponding configurations of
n
-admissible arcs.

4.1.
Geometric realization of
\mathcal{C}_{n}(A_{\infty})

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field,
n
an integer, and let
\mathcal{T}
be a
k
-linear algebraic triangulated category that is idempotent complete and classically generated by an
(n+1)
-spherical object. This category was first studied in
[
19
]
for the case
n=1
.

For different values of
n
, the category
\mathcal{T}
appears in various well-known forms. When
n=-1
,
\mathcal{T}
is equivalent to
D^{c}(k[X]/(X^{2}))
, the compact derived category of the dual numbers. When
n=0
, it corresponds to
D^{f}(k[![X]!])
, the derived category of complexes with bounded finite-length homology over the formal power series ring. For
n=1
,
\mathcal{T}
is the cluster category of type
A_{\infty}
; see
[
11
]
. When
n>1
, it can be interpreted as the
n
-cluster category of type
A_{\infty}
; see
[
12
]
. On the other hand, for negative values of
n
, the category
\mathcal{T}
is less commonly studied. We now recall the definition of
\mathcal{T}
.

Definition 4.1
.

[
12
, Definition 1.1]

We say that
\mathcal{T}
is a
k
-linear algebraic triangulated category with suspension functor
\Sigma
, which is idempotent complete and classically generated by an
(n+1)
-spherical object

\dim_{k}\mathcal{T}(s,\Sigma^{\ell}s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}1&\text{if\ $\ell=0$ or $\ell=n+1$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

From now on, suppose that

is an integer and

is the

-cluster categories of type

[

acts cyclically

We recall the geometric realization of

based on

Definition 4.2
.

(1)

4.2.
n
-cotorsion pairs in
\mathcal{C}_{n}(A_{\infty})

In this subsection, we give a geometric characterization of
n
-cotorsion pairs in
\mathcal{C}_{n}(A_{\infty})
. The following results are needed.

Lemma 4.7
.

[
12
, Proposition 3.4]

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a subcategory of
\mathcal{C}_{n}(A_{\infty})
and let
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
be the corresponding
set of
n
-admissible arcs. The following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

The subcategory
\mathcal{X}
is contravariantly finite.

(2)

Each right-fountain of
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
is a left-fountain of
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
.

Dually, the following result holds.

Lemma 4.8
.

[
12
, Proposition 3.5]

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a subcategory of
\mathcal{C}_{n}(A_{\infty})
and let
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
be the corresponding
set of
n
-admissible arcs. The following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

The subcategory
\mathcal{X}
is covariantly finite.

(2)

Each left-fountain of
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
is a right-fountain of
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
.

By Lemma
4.6
and Proposition 2.8 in
[
12
]
, we have the following result.

Lemma 4.9
.

Suppose that
u,v
are two
n
-admissible arcs, and
M_{u},M_{v}
are the corresponding indecomposable objects in
\mathcal{C}_{n}(A_{\infty})
. Then
u
does not cross
v
if and only if
\mathop{\rm Ext}\nolimits^{i}_{\mathcal{C}_{n}(A_{\infty})}(M_{u},M_{v})=0
for all
1\leq i\leq n
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a set of
n
-admissible arcs in
\Pi
. We define

\mathop{\rm nc}\nolimits\mathfrak{X}=\{u\in\Pi\text{\;is\;an\;}n\text{-admissible\; arc\;}|\;u\text{\;does\;not\;cross\;any\;}n\text{-admissible\; arc\;in\;}\mathfrak{X}\}.

By Lemma
4.9
,
\mathop{\rm nc}\nolimits\mathfrak{X}
corresponds to the subcategory
\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}E(\mathfrak{X})[-i]^{\perp}=\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}{{}^{\bot}}E(\mathfrak{X})[i]
.

We are ready to give the geometric characterization of
n
-cotorsion pairs in
\mathcal{C}_{n}(A_{\infty})
.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}
be subcategories of
\mathcal{C}_{n}(A_{\infty})
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}},\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}
be the corresponding sets of
n
-admissible arcs. Then the following statements are equivalent.

(1)

(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
is an
n
-cotorsion pair.

(2)

\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}=\mathop{\rm nc}\nolimits\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}=\mathop{\rm nc}\nolimits\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
, each right-fountain of
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
is a left-fountain of
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
, and each left-fountain of
\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}
is a right-fountain of
\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}
.

Proof.

It is easy to check by Definition
3.1
, Lemma
4.7
, Lemma
4.8
, and Lemma
4.9
.
∎

We give a definition of Ptolemy diagram and obtain a necessary condition for an
n
-cotorsion pair.

Definition 4.11
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a set of
n
-admissible arcs in
\Pi
. If for any two crossing
n
-admissible arcs
u=(r,s)
and
v=(t,u)
in
\mathfrak{X}
, those of
(r,t),(r,u),(s,t),(s,u)
, which are
n
-admissible arcs are in
\mathfrak{X}
, then
\mathfrak{X}
is called a
Ptolemy diagram
.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a subcategory of
\mathcal{C}_{n}(A_{\infty})
, and
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
be the corresponding set of
n
-admissible arcs. If
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}=\mathop{\rm nc}\nolimits\mathop{\rm nc}\nolimits\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
, then
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
is a Ptolemy diagram.

Proof.

Suppose that
u=(r,s)
and
v=(t,u)
are two crossing
n
-admissible arcs with
r<t<s<u
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
, and suppose
(r,t)
is an
n
-admissible arc, we need to show
(r,t)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}=\mathop{\rm nc}\nolimits\mathop{\rm nc}\nolimits\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
.

If
(r,t)\not\in\mathop{\rm nc}\nolimits\mathop{\rm nc}\nolimits\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
, then there exists an
n
-admissible arc
w\in\mathop{\rm nc}\nolimits\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
such that
w
crosses
(r,t)
. Observe that every
n
-admissible arc
w\in\mathop{\rm nc}\nolimits\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
crossing
(r,t)
crosses either
u
or
v
, which is a contradiction with the assumption
u,v\in\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
.
∎

By Theorem
4.10
and Lemma
4.12
, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 4.13
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{X}
is a subcategory of
\mathcal{C}_{n}(A_{\infty})
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
is the corresponding set of
n
-admissible arcs. If
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
is an

-cotorsion pair for some subcategory
\mathcal{Y}
, then

(1)

and each right-fountain of
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
is a left-fountain of
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
.

(2)

\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
is a Ptolemy diagram and each right-fountain of
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
is a left-fountain of
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
.

.

Note that when

[

]
and Corollary 5.9 in

F_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}
.

By the assumption above, the subcategory
E(F_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}})
which corresponds to
F_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}
is the core of the
n
-cotorsion pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
, i.e.
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{X})=E(F_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}})
.

Definition 4.17
.

(1)

For any
n
-admissible arc
(t,u)
of
\Pi
, its
rotation
in
\Pi
, denoted by
\rho_{\Pi}(t,u)
, is defined to be
(t-1,u-1)
, i.e.

\rho_{\Pi}(t,u)=(t-1,u-1).

(2)

Let
\mathfrak{D}
be a set of non-crossing
n
-admissible arcs of
\Pi
.

(i)

The
n
-admissible arcs in
\mathfrak{D}
divide
\Pi
into polygons, called
\mathfrak{D}
-
cells
. Thus, any
n
-admissible arc
(t,u)
which neither is in
\mathfrak{D}
nor crosses any
n
-admissible arcs in
\mathfrak{D}
is an
n
-admissible arc of a
\mathfrak{D}
-cell. We call the rotation of
(t,u)
in this
\mathfrak{D}
-cell the
\mathfrak{D}
-
rotation
of
(t,u)
, and denote it by
\rho_{\mathcal{D}}(t,u)
.

(ii)

Let
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
be an
n
-cotorsion pair in
\mathcal{C}_{n}(A_{\infty})
for some
\mathcal{Y}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
be the corresponding set of
n
-admissible arcs with
\mathfrak{D}\subset F_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}
. The
\mathfrak{D}
-
rotation
of
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
is defined as

\rho_{\mathfrak{D}}(\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}):=\{\rho_{\mathfrak{D}}(i,j)|(i,j)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}\backslash\mathfrak{D}\}\cup\mathfrak{D}.

The following result is an easy observation by the Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\mathcal{C}_{n}(A_{\infty})
.

Lemma 4.19
.

Suppose that
x=(r,s)
and
y=(t,u)
are two indecomposable objects in the same component of the Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\mathcal{C}_{n}(A_{\infty})
with
t<r<u<s
. Then there exists a triangle

(t,u)\rightarrow(t,s)\oplus(r,u)\rightarrow(r,s),

where
(x,y)
is taken to be a zero object if
(x,y)
is not an
n
-admissible arc.

Depending on the above observation, we have the following result.

Lemma 4.20
.

Suppose that
x=(r,s)
and
y=(t,u)
are two indecomposable objects in
\mathcal{C}_{n}(A_{\infty})
. Then

\mathop{\rm Ext}\nolimits^{1}(x,y)\neq 0
if and only if
\Sigma^{1}y\in F^{+}(x)\cup F^{-}(Sx)
. In this case, if
\Sigma^{1}y\in F^{-}(Sx)
, then there exists a triangle

(t,u)\rightarrow(t,s)\oplus(r,u)\rightarrow(r,s),

if
\Sigma^{1}y\in F^{+}(x)
, then there exists a triangle

(t,u)\rightarrow(s,u)\oplus(r,t)\rightarrow(r,s).

where
(x,y)
is taken to be a zero object if
(x,y)
is not an
n
-admissible arc.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.6
, we have
\mathop{\rm Ext}\nolimits^{1}(x,y)\neq 0
if and only if
\Sigma^{1}y\in F^{+}(x)\cup F^{-}(Sx)
. Now we show the existence of the triangle. First, suppose
\Sigma^{1}y\in F^{-}(Sx)
. Then
x
and
y
are in the same component of the Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\mathcal{C}_{n}(A_{\infty})
. By the proof of Proposition 2.8 in
[
12
]
, we have
u\equiv s(\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits n)
,
t\leq r-n
and
r+1\leq u\leq s-n
. Thus,
t<r<u<s
and
(t,s)
(resp.
(r,u)
) is either an
n
-admissible arc or an edge. So the triangle

(t,u)\rightarrow(t,s)\oplus(r,u)\rightarrow(r,s)

exists by Lemma
4.19
.

Suppose
\Sigma^{1}y\in F^{+}(x)
. Then
x
is in the “next” component of
y
. Since
\Sigma^{1}y\in F^{+}(x)
, by the proof of Proposition 2.8 in
[
12
]
, we have
u\equiv s+1(\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits n)
,
r+1\leq t\leq s-n
and
s+1\leq u
. Thus,
r<t<s<u
and
(r,t)
(resp.
(s,u)
) is either an
n
-admissible arc or an edge. Observe that
(t,s+1)
is an
n
-admissible arc (since
u\equiv s+1(\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits n)
and
u\equiv t+1(\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits n)
, we have
s\equiv t(\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits n)
), and
(t,s+1)
is in the same component as
(t,u)
. Moreover,
(s,u)
is also in the same component as
(t,u)
, by Lemma
4.19
, there exists a triangle

(t,s+1)\rightarrow(t,u)\rightarrow(s,u)\rightarrow(t-1,s),

where
(t-1,s)=\Sigma^{1}(t,s+1)
is in the same component as
x
. Note that
(r,t)
and
x=(r,s)
are in the same component, so are
(t-1,s)
and
x
. By Lemma
4.19
, there exists a triangle

(t,s+1)\rightarrow(r,t)\rightarrow(r,s)\rightarrow(t-1,s).

Since
(s,u)
and
(r,t)
do not cross,
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits((s,u),\Sigma^{1}(r,t))=0
by Lemma
4.9
. We have a communicative diagram

\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}(t,s+1)&\rightarrow&(t,u)&\rightarrow&(s,u)&\rightarrow&(t-1,s)\\
\parallel&&\downarrow&&\downarrow&&\parallel\\
(t,s+1)&\rightarrow&(r,t)&\rightarrow&(r,s)&\rightarrow&(t-1,s).\end{array}

By Lemma 2.2 in
[
24
]
, we get the triangle

(t,u)\rightarrow(s,u)\oplus(r,t)\rightarrow(r,s).

∎

Theorem 4.21
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
be an
n
-cotorsion pair in
\mathcal{C}_{n}(A_{\infty})
for some
\mathcal{Y}
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
be the corresponding set of
n
-admissible arcs, and
F_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}
be the frame of
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
. Then the followings hold.

(1)

The frame
F_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}
of
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
corresponds to the core of the
n
-cotorsion pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
, i.e.
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{X})=E(F_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}})
, where
E(F_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}})
represents the subcategory of
\mathcal{C}_{n}(A_{\infty})
which corresponds to
F_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}
.

(2)

Let
\mathfrak{D}\subset F_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}
be a set of non-crossing
n
-admissible arcs and
E(\mathfrak{D})
be the corresponding subcategory of
\mathcal{C}_{n}(A_{\infty})
. Then we have that

\mu^{-1}_{E(\mathfrak{D})}(\mathcal{X})=E(\rho_{\mathfrak{D}}(\widetilde{\mathcal{X}})).

Proof.

It is obvious that (1) holds.

In order to prove (2), it is enough to show that for any
n
-admissible arc
(t,u)
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
which is not in
\mathfrak{D}
, if
\rho_{\mathfrak{D}}(t,u)=(r,s)
, then
\mu^{-1}_{E(\mathfrak{D})}(t,u)=(r,s)
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
be the
\mathfrak{D}
-cell containing
(t,u)
. Since
\rho_{\mathfrak{D}}(t,u)=(r,s)
,
(r,s)
is the rotation of
(t,u)
in
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
. That means
(r,s)
is an
n
-admissible arc of
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
, so
(r,s)
does not cross any
n
-admissible arc in
\mathfrak{D}
, we have
(r,s)\in\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}{{}^{\perp_{i}}}E(\mathfrak{D})
.

Since
(r,s)
is an
n
-admissible arc,
(t,u)
crosses
(r,s)
. We have the following two cases.

(1)

Suppose
t<r<u<s
. Then
(r,u)
and
(t,s)
are edges of
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
. Thus,
(r,u)
(resp.
(s,t)
) is a zero object or a non-zero object in
E(\mathfrak{D})
, i.e.
(r,u)\oplus(s,t)\in E(\mathfrak{D})
. By Lemma
4.20
, there exists a triangle

(t,u)\xrightarrow{f}(t,s)\oplus(r,u)\rightarrow(r,s).

Since
(r,s)\in\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}{{}^{\perp_{i}}}E(\mathfrak{D})
, this implies that
f
is a left
E(\mathfrak{D})
-approximation and it is minimal since
(r,s)
is indecomposable. So by definition,
\mu^{-1}_{E(\mathfrak{D})}(t,u)=(r,s)
and we complete the proof of
\mu^{-1}_{E(\mathfrak{D})}(\mathcal{X})=E(\rho_{\mathfrak{D}}(\widetilde{\mathcal{X}})).

(2)

Suppose
r<t<s<u
. Then
(r,t)
and
(s,u)
are edges of
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
. Thus,
(r,t)
(resp.
(s,u)
) is a zero object or a non-zero object in
E(\mathfrak{D})
, i.e.
(r,t)\oplus(s,u)\in E(\mathfrak{D})
. By Lemma
4.20
, there exists a triangle

(t,u)\xrightarrow{f}(r,t)\oplus(s,u)\rightarrow(r,s).

Since
(r,s)\in\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}{{}^{\perp_{i}}}E(\mathfrak{D})
, this implies that
f
is a left
E(\mathfrak{D})
-approximation and it is minimal since
(r,s)
is indecomposable. So by definition,
\mu^{-1}_{E(\mathfrak{D})}(t,u)=(r,s)
and we complete the proof of
\mu^{-1}_{E(\mathfrak{D})}(\mathcal{X})=E(\rho_{\mathfrak{D}}(\widetilde{\mathcal{X}})).

∎

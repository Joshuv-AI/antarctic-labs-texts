Multimodal opioid-free anesthesia containing esketamine versus opioid-based anesthesia: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Ye Fang, Meiling Gao, Xin Wang, Bin Zheng, Meiling He, Xingxing Zeng, Yu Shi, Linan Li, Boyu Zhang, Yang Xu, Xingrui Gong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionThe paradigm shift toward opioid-free anesthesia (OFA) is gaining traction, yet evidence regarding specific viable alternatives remains fragmented. This meta-analysis comprehensively evaluates the efficacy and safety of multimodal OFA regimens containing esketamine compared to traditional opioid-based anesthesia (OBA).MethodsThis study conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis by searching the electronic databases PubMed, The Cochrane Library, Web of Science, and Embase, including randomized controlled trials published from inception to September 20, 2024. The included studies had to feature at least one OFA group (defined as a multimodal regimen containing esketamine), where opioids were not used during the preoperative phase, anesthetic induction, intraoperative maintenance, or before anesthesia recovery.ResultsA total of 14 RCTs (1,703 patients) were analyzed. Regarding the primary outcomes, postoperative pain scores at 24 h (MD = -0.07; 95% CI: -0.27, 0.13; moderate certainty) and 48 h (MD = 0.00; 95% CI: -0.29, 0.30; moderate certainty) showed no statistically significant differences between the groups. However, multimodal OFA regimens containing esketamine demonstrated a reduced risk of chronic postoperative pain based exclusively on data from only two thoracic surgery trials (RR = 0.64; 95% CI: 0.44, 0.92; low certainty). Regarding secondary outcomes, the OFA group showed a significantly lower incidence of intraoperative hypotension (RR = 0.29; 95% CI: 0.17, 0.49; high certainty) and postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV) (RR = 0.47; 95% CI: 0.27, 0.79; high certainty). No statistically significant differences were detected in extubation time, PACU length of stay, rescue analgesia requirements, or the incidence of nightmares or hallucinations. Notably, substantial statistical heterogeneity was observed across several outcomes, including pain scores, PONV, extubation time, and PACU length of stay.ConclusionMultimodal OFA regimens containing esketamine provide comparable postoperative analgesia to opioid-based anesthesia while reducing PONV and intraoperative hypotension. However, these findings represent combined multimodal effects across heterogeneous settings, and potential benefits for chronic pain prevention should be viewed strictly as hypothesis-generating.Trial registrationPROSPERO ID: CRD42024588900Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12871-026-03975-w.

Introduction

While opioids have historically been indispensable for surgical anesthesia via central nervous system receptor binding (μ, δ, and κ), their use carries significant dose-dependent risks. These include respiratory depression, postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV), pruritus, urinary retention, and postoperative delirium. Furthermore, the global opioid abuse crisis has elevated perioperative opioid exposure to a serious public health concern, driving efforts to mitigate these risks and associated economic burdens [1, 2].

Consequently, Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) protocols increasingly advocate minimizing or avoiding perioperative opioids [3–5]. This shift has catalyzed the development of opioid-free anesthesia (OFA), which employs multimodal non-opioid adjuvants—such as dexmedetomidine, clonidine, ketamine, and lidocaine—to achieve analgesia. Evidence indicates that these multimodal combinations can significantly curtail opioid-related adverse effects [6].

Esketamine, the S-enantiomer of ketamine, is an N-Methyl-D-Aspartate (NMDA) receptor antagonist with a stronger receptor affinity, enabling effective analgesia at lower doses. It was specifically developed to reduce adverse effects such as psychosis and addiction [7]. Indeed, systematic reviews in psychiatric settings suggest esketamine possesses a more favorable safety and tolerability profile compared to intravenous racemic ketamine [8, 9]. Despite these potential advantages and its increasing perioperative utilization [10], its specific role and safety in diverse surgical populations remain debated. While previous meta-analyses have extensively evaluated the broad concept of OFA, they have predominantly pooled diverse non-opioid agents into generalized intervention groups. Consequently, the distinct clinical contribution and psychomimetic safety profile of esketamine within these complex regimens remain obscured. Clinical trials evaluating esketamine have yielded inconsistent results regarding its analgesic efficacy compared to traditional regimens. To our knowledge, no systematic review has specifically isolated and synthesized the evidence to address this critical gap.

Therefore, this systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted to evaluate the efficacy and safety of multimodal OFA regimens containing esketamine compared with opioid-based anesthesia (OBA). Beyond the primary assessment of postoperative pain, we undertook subgroup analyses based on surgical types to further define clinical applicability. Furthermore, we evaluated key safety outcomes, including hemodynamic stability and adverse events, to provide a comprehensive assessment of its clinical utility.

Methods

This systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted following the guidelines of the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA). The study was registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO) with the registration number CRD42024588900.

Search strategy

Two independent researchers systematically searched electronic databases, including PubMed, EMBASE, The Cochrane Library, and Web of Science, from their inception to September 20, 2024. The search strategy combined Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) and free-text terms, primarily utilizing “opioid-free anesthesia” and “esketamine.” The search was restricted to peer-reviewed randomized controlled trials (RCTs) without limitations on language, publication date, or sample size. Full search strings and detailed screening strategies for each database are provided in the Supplementary Material (Appendix 1). Additionally, the reference lists of all included studies were manually screened using a backward snowballing approach to identify any further relevant literature. When necessary, the corresponding authors of the original studies were contacted to obtain missing or incomplete data.

Eligibility criteria

The inclusion criteria were defined according to the PICOS framework as follows: Population (P): Adults (aged ≥ 18 years) undergoing surgery under general anesthesia. Intervention (I): Administration of multimodal OFA regimens specifically containing esketamine for anesthetic induction and/or maintenance. Comparison (C): Traditional opioid-based anesthesia (OBA) regimens used for induction and/or maintenance. Outcome (O): Trials reporting the efficacy (e.g., postoperative pain scores) and safety (e.g., adverse events) of OFA as an alternative to OBA. Study Design (S): Randomized controlled trials (RCTs).

The exclusion criteria were: (a) basic research or animal studies; (b) inability to extract valid data; (c) duplicate publications; (d) administration of opioids in the OFA group prior to induction, maintenance, or recovery from anesthesia; (e) non-endotracheal intubation anesthesia; and (f) non-intravenous administration of esketamine.

Study selection

Following the initial database search, duplicate records were removed. Two authors (Meiling He and Xingxing Zeng) independently assessed study eligibility by reviewing titles and abstracts against the predefined criteria. The full texts of potentially eligible studies were subsequently retrieved and reviewed in detail. Any disagreements were resolved through consensus or by consulting a third researcher (Yang Xu). The entire study selection process was documented using a PRISMA flow diagram.

Data extraction

Data were independently extracted by three authors (Yu Shi, Linan Li, and Bin Zheng) from the original texts, figures, and tables, and compiled into an Excel spreadsheet. For data presented exclusively in graphical format (which was required for two included studies), numerical values were extracted using GraphPad Prism software. To ensure reproducibility and accuracy, the three authors independently performed this extraction, demonstrating excellent inter-rater reliability with an intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) of 0.99 (95% CI: 0.993–0.999). The results were cross-verified to minimize observer error. The extracted data included: Baseline characteristics: Author information, publication year, country, patient demographics, sample size, type of surgery, and details of the anesthesia protocols (OFA vs. OBA). Primary outcomes: Postoperative pain scores at 24 and 48 h (expressed as VAS or NRS and converted to a standardized 0–10 scale), and the incidence of postoperative chronic pain. While pain scale conversion is methodologically acceptable for synthesis, the interchangeable use of these differing scales introduces inherent clinical heterogeneity, which was considered in our interpretation. Secondary outcomes: Intraoperative hypotension events (defined as mean arterial pressure < 65 mmHg or a > 20% decrease from baseline), extubation time (min), length of stay in the PACU (min), rescue analgesia events, PONV incidence, and the incidence of nightmares or hallucinations. To address missing variance data, we initially contacted the corresponding authors. If the original data remained unavailable, standard deviations (SDs) were imputed from standard errors (SEs) or confidence intervals (CIs) where possible. For continuous variables reported as medians with ranges or interquartile ranges, means and SDs were estimated using the mathematical methods described by Wan et al. and Shi et al. [11, 12].

Quality assessment

The methodological quality of the included RCTs was independently assessed by three evaluators (Yu Shi, Boyu Zhang, and Yang Xu) using the modified Jadad scale. Scoring was adapted to reflect practical constraints of anesthesia trials where provider blinding is not feasible. Studies achieving a score of ≥ 4 were considered to be of high methodological quality. Furthermore, three researchers (Ye Fang, Xin Wang, and Meiling Gao) independently evaluated the risk of bias for each study across seven domains using the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool (RoB 1.0). For transparency, we acknowledge that RoB 1.0 may not fully capture modern bias domains (e.g., outcome measurement bias) as comprehensively as the updated RoB 2 framework. Finally, the certainty of evidence for each outcome was assessed using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development, and Evaluation (GRADE) approach, classifying the evidence as very low, low, moderate, or high certainty. This GRADE evaluation was conducted independently by three authors (Bin Zheng, Xingrui Gong, and Linan Li).

Statistical analysis

All meta-analyses were performed using Review Manager 5.4 and Stata 16. For dichotomous data, pooled risk ratios (RRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated. For descriptive statistics, continuous variables are presented uniformly as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Given the anticipated clinical and methodological diversity inherent in these multimodal OFA regimens containing esketamine across disparate surgical environments, all analyses were conducted using a random-effects model (Inverse Variance method) a priori, regardless of the observed statistical heterogeneity. This approach conservatively accounts for both within-study and between-study variance. The DerSimonian–Laird estimator was employed to estimate the between-study variance, and CIs were calculated using the standard method based on the normal distribution (without the Hartung-Knapp adjustment). We acknowledge that the confidence intervals may be underestimated due to the absence of the Hartung-Knapp adjustment. Statistical heterogeneity across studies was evaluated using the I2 statistic, with I2 > 50% indicating substantial heterogeneity. Subgroup analyses were performed based on surgical type (laparoscopic vs. other surgeries). Publication bias was assessed visually via funnel plots and statistically using Egger’s test. However, we acknowledge the limited power of Egger’s test when evaluating domains with fewer than 10 studies per outcome. Finally, sensitivity analyses were conducted using the leave-one-out method, sequentially excluding each study to evaluate the stability of the pooled results and identify any single study disproportionately influencing the overall effect size.

Geographically, the studies were predominantly conducted in China (13 trials) [13–25], with one trial conducted in Germany [26]. The participants’ ages ranged from 18 to 80 years. Regarding study populations, three RCTs specifically recruited female patients undergoing gynecological laparoscopic surgeries [13, 14, 26], while the remaining studies included both male and female participants [15–22, 24, 25].

The included trials exhibited significant clinical heterogeneity regarding surgical procedures and anesthesia protocols. Surgical types varied and included gynecological laparoscopic surgeries [13, 14, 26], laparoscopic cholecystectomy [18, 20], laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy [24], thoracoscopic surgeries [17, 19, 23], and other types of surgeries [15, 16, 21, 22, 25]. Significant variation was also noted in the administration of esketamine: 9 studies utilized continuous intraoperative infusion [13, 14, 17, 18, 21, 22, 24–26], while 5 studies employed intermittent bolus injections for anesthesia maintenance [15, 16, 19, 20, 23]. Notably, all OFA protocols were multimodal, incorporating at least two non-opioid medications (e.g., dexmedetomidine, lidocaine) or regional nerve blocks alongside esketamine. One study [23] lacked specific variance data; although attempts were made to contact the author, complete data could not be obtained.

Quality assessment

The methodological quality of the 14 included studies was assessed using the modified Jadad scale (Table 2), which indicated that all clinical trials were of high quality (score ≥ 5). The risk of bias was further evaluated using the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool (RoB 1.0), as presented in Fig. 2. Generally, the randomization process was well-described across all trials. However, specific limitations were noted: three trials did not specify details regarding allocation concealment, and two did not clearly state whether blinding was fully implemented, leading to an assessment of “unclear risk” in these domains. Additionally, one trial reported an “unclear risk” for selective reporting. Consequently, the overall risk of bias was considered low in 7 studies, while the remaining 7 studies presented unclear risks in at least one domain. The GRADE assessment indicated that the certainty of evidence for the primary and secondary outcomes ranged from low to high. Specifically regarding the incidence of PONV, the certainty of evidence was not downgraded for inconsistency despite substantial statistical heterogeneity (I2 = 76%). This decision is explicitly justified by two primary factors: first, the consistency of direction, as all included studies consistently favored the OFA group in terms of risk reduction; and second, the narrow confidence intervals, with the pooled 95% CI remaining relatively narrow and strictly on the side of benefit, far from the line of no effect. Discussion

Summary of main findings

The findings of our meta-analysis, supported by low- to high-certainty evidence according to the GRADE assessment (Table 3), suggest that multimodal OFA regimens containing esketamine provide comparable postoperative analgesia to opioid-based anesthesia (OBA) at 24 and 48 h. Regarding psychiatric adverse events, such as nightmares or hallucinations, no statistically significant difference was detected; however, the confidence interval includes a possible increase or decrease, highlighting the need for further evaluation in future studies. The most distinct advantages of multimodal OFA regimens containing esketamine observed in this meta-analysis were the significant reductions in the risk of postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV) and intraoperative hypotension. Regarding postoperative chronic pain at 3 months, a statistically significant reduction was observed; however, the evidence is of low certainty and limited to thoracic surgery, precluding generalization. While this association is biologically plausible given the NMDA antagonism of esketamine, the current evidence is limited. Consequently, this finding should be viewed as hypothesis-generating evidence only, rather than practice-changing. Overall, the inclusion of esketamine in OFA regimens serves as a valuable component of multimodal analgesia strategies rather than a standalone replacement for opioids, and its specific independent effects remain difficult to isolate due to the concurrent use of other non-opioid adjuvants.

Interpretation of analgesic efficacy and heterogeneity

Our study showed no significant difference in postoperative pain scores at 24 and 48 h between the multimodal OFA regimens containing esketamine and traditional OBA groups. This finding aligns with the comprehensive meta-analysis by Frauenknecht et al. [27], which similarly reported that OFA did not offer superior pain control compared with OBA in general surgical populations. However, this contrasts with findings by Hung et al. [28] in bariatric surgery, where OFA demonstrated superior analgesia, suggesting that the analgesic benefit of OFA may be procedure-specific. Substantial statistical heterogeneity was observed in pain scores (I2 > 75%). Although subgroup analyses based on surgical types were performed, they did not fully resolve this heterogeneity, indicating that clinical variations beyond simple surgical categories are the primary drivers. We posit that the dominant sources of this heterogeneity stem from clinical practice variations rather than solely biological differences (e.g., sex [29]). First, the included studies utilized heterogeneous multimodal analgesia protocols. Many trials incorporated robust regional anesthesia techniques, such as intermediate cervical plexus block (ICPB) in thyroid surgery [25] or transversus abdominis plane (TAP) blocks in abdominal surgery [24]. These powerful analgesic adjuncts likely masked the specific analgesic contribution of esketamine, creating a “ceiling effect” in pain score reductions. Second, the specific co-analgesics utilized in the OFA protocols differed significantly across trials; for instance, some studies combined esketamine with lidocaine [14] or dexmedetomidine [22, 30]. These agents possess intrinsic analgesic and sedative properties that may confound the isolation of esketamine’s independent effects. Third, the dosing regimens of esketamine varied significantly (continuous infusion vs. intermittent bolus), potentially leading to different pharmacokinetic profiles. Fourth, the nature of nociceptive stimuli differed widely across the included studies, ranging from visceral pain (e.g., laparoscopic cholecystectomy [20]) to deep somatic pain (e.g., thoracic surgery [23]). Consequently, despite the appropriate a priori use of a random-effects model, it is crucial that readers do not overinterpret the pooled estimates. These findings must be consistently interpreted as average effects across highly heterogeneous clinical settings rather than precise expectations for any single protocol.

Chronic pain prevention

Chronic pain exerts an enormous personal and economic burden, affecting more than 30% of people worldwide [31]. Our meta-analysis indicated that the incidence of chronic pain was significantly lower with multimodal OFA regimens containing esketamine compared with the OBA group. Although the GRADE assessment indicates this finding is currently supported by low-certainty evidence—given that the data were derived exclusively from two small trials involving thoracoscopic surgery [17, 23]—this result should be viewed strictly as hypothesis-generating rather than practice-changing, precluding broad clinical generalization. Mechanistically, while opioids remain the primary medication for pain control, opioid-induced hyperalgesia (OIH) may contribute to the transition to chronic pain. In contrast, esketamine acts as an N-methyl-D-aspartate (NMDA) receptor antagonist. By inhibiting central sensitization and modulating neuronal excitability in the dorsal root ganglia (DRG) [32, 33], esketamine appears to be beneficial for preventing chronic pain without raising concerns about hyperalgesia.

Safety outcomes: PONV and hemodynamics

Reducing the incidence of PONV significantly improves the postoperative experience and patient satisfaction [34]. Our study confirmed that the multimodal OFA regimens containing esketamine demonstrated a lower incidence of PONV compared with the OBA group. This is consistent with evidence from previous meta-analyses [27, 28, 35, 36]. The mechanism for this benefit is primarily driven by the opioid-sparing effect. Opioids are direct agonists at the chemoreceptor trigger zone and are the leading factor causing PONV [37]. By eliminating intraoperative opioids, the emetic stimulus is markedly reduced. Additionally, our study confirms that the incidence of intraoperative hypotension is lower with these esketamine-containing OFA regimens than in the OBA group, a finding consistent with previous research [38]. Mechanistically, esketamine’s intrinsic sympathomimetic properties help counteract the vasodilatory effects typical of other anesthetic agents. This hemodynamic advantage is particularly valuable for vulnerable populations; for instance, a recent 2025 trial demonstrated that multimodal OFA regimens containing esketamine provided superior intraoperative circulatory stability in elderly patients undergoing hip surgery [39]. Ultimately, this stability may further contribute to an improved quality of recovery.

Psychiatric safety

At present, no addiction-related events have been reported in the included trials. Furthermore, we found no significant increase in the incidence of nightmares or hallucinations between the treatment groups. However, it is important to note that long-term psychiatric or addiction outcomes cannot be adequately assessed based on the current data, primarily due to the limited follow-up durations of the included studies. Future vigilance and longer-term monitoring are therefore warranted.

Implications for clinical practice and research

In terms of clinical practice, the findings of this meta-analysis suggest that multimodal OFA regimens containing esketamine should not yet be viewed as a universal, standalone replacement for traditional opioid-based regimens. Instead, multimodal OFA regimens containing esketamine serve as highly valuable and safe tailored analgesia strategies [40, 41]; however, its specific independent clinical contributions remain difficult to isolate due to the concurrent use of other non-opioid co-analgesics. This approach is particularly advantageous for high-risk patients who are prone to PONV or those requiring strict intraoperative hemodynamic stability [36, 39]. For future research, there is a critical need for large-scale, strictly blinded, multicenter RCTs conducted across diverse international populations and broader surgical specialties. Future study designs must standardize esketamine dosing regimens and background co-analgesic protocols to definitively isolate the specific therapeutic contribution of esketamine. Furthermore, extended follow-up periods are essential to accurately map its impact on chronic postsurgical pain trajectories and validate its long-term psychiatric safety profile [42].

Limitations

Several limitations exist. First, the predominance of trials conducted in China (13 of 14) limits the global external validity of our findings. Regional anesthesia practices (e.g., specific esketamine protocols) and perioperative opioid prescribing norms in China differ markedly from international standards. Consequently, our results must be cautiously extrapolated to Western populations with differing baseline opioid tolerances, or to low-resource settings where components of these complex multimodal OFA regimens may be unavailable. Second, given the substantial heterogeneity (I2 > 75%) across multiple outcomes, our pooled estimates represent averaged effects across diverse clinical contexts rather than precise predictions. Third, insufficient data precluded subgroup analyses on key confounders, such as esketamine dosing regimens and concurrent nerve blocks. Fourth, pooling VAS and NRS scores introduces measurement variability [43–45]. Finally, there are specific methodological constraints. The Cochrane RoB 1 tool may not fully capture modern bias domains. Crucially, using the DerSimonian-Laird estimator without the Hartung-Knapp adjustment—particularly for outcomes with fewer than 10 studies—can underestimate between-study variance. This may yield artificially narrow confidence intervals and reduce overall confidence in these specific pooled estimates. This same limitation of having fewer than 10 studies also constrained the statistical power of Egger’s test for publication bias. Future large-scale, diverse trials are needed to validate these conclusions.

Conclusions

In summary, our meta-analysis demonstrates that multimodal OFA regimens containing esketamine provide comparable postoperative analgesia to traditional opioid-based anesthesia (OBA), while offering distinct advantages in minimizing the risk of PONV and intraoperative hypotension. Importantly, these findings reflect the effectiveness of esketamine-containing multimodal OFA as a comprehensive strategy, rather than the isolated independent effect of esketamine. Regarding the reduction in chronic postoperative pain at 3 months, the current low-certainty evidence is derived exclusively from two trials involving thoracoscopic surgery; therefore, this finding should be viewed strictly as hypothesis-generating rather than practice-changing, precluding broad clinical generalization. Furthermore, regarding psychiatric safety (e.g., nightmares or hallucinations), no statistically significant difference was detected; however, the confidence interval includes a possible increase or decrease, necessitating vigilant monitoring. Ultimately, given the substantial clinical heterogeneity and the multimodal complexity of these regimens, future large-scale trials across diverse surgical settings are imperative to validate these findings and further clarify the specific therapeutic contributions of individual co-analgesics.

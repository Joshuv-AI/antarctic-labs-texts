Fučík spectrum for discrete systems: curves and their tangent lines
Gabriela Holubová, Petr Nečesal

Abstract
In this paper, we study the Fučík spectrum of a square matrix A A and provide necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of Fučík curves emanating from the point ( λ , λ ) (\lambda,\lambda) with λ \lambda being a real eigenvalue of A A . We extend recent results by Maroncelli (2024) and remove his assumptions on symmetry of A A and simplicity of λ \lambda . We show that the number of Fučík curves can significantly exceed the multiplicity of λ \lambda and determine all the possible directions they can emanate in. We also treat the situation when the algebraic multiplicity of λ \lambda is greater than the geometric one and show that in such a case the Fučík curves can loose their smoothness and provide the slopes of their “one-sided tangent lines”. Finally, we offer two possible generalizations: the situation off the diagonal and Fučík spectrum of a general Fredholm operator on the Hilbert space with a lattice structure.

Preliminaries

Let us consider
H=\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, endowed with the standard Euclidean scalar product
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
and Euclidean norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|
.
That is,

u\in H
is an
n
-dimensional vector
u=[u_{1},\dots,u_{n}]^{t}
,
and let us define its positive and negative parts componentwise, i.e., by

u^{\pm}:=[u_{1}^{\pm},\dots,u_{n}^{\pm}]^{t}
with

u_{i}^{+}:=\max\{u_{i},0\},\quad u_{i}^{-}:=\max\{-u_{i},0\},\quad i=1,\dots,n.

Clearly,
u=u^{+}-u^{-}
, and
|u|:=[|u_{1}|,\dots,|u_{n}|]^{t}=u^{+}+u^{-}
.
Further, let us consider a
linear operator

A:H\to H
represented by a real
n
-by-
n
matrix, and denote
A^{\ast}
the corresponding adjoint operator (i.e., the transpose of the matrix
A
).

Definition 1
.

The Fučík spectrum of an operator
A:H\to H
is a set
\Sigma(A)
of all pairs

(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, for which the problem

Au=\alpha u^{+}-\beta u^{-}

(1)

has a nontrivial solution
u\in H
.

Since we study the behavior of the Fučík spectrum
\Sigma(A)
close to the diagonal
\alpha=\beta
, it is more convenient to write (
1
) in the equivalent form

Au=\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}|u|+\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}u.

(2)

From now, let us consider an arbitrary but fixed real eigenvalue of
A
,
\lambda\in\sigma(A)\cap\mathbb{R}
,
and transform
(\alpha,\beta)
into
(\varepsilon,\eta)
by

\alpha:=\varepsilon(\eta+1)+\lambda,\qquad\beta:=\varepsilon(\eta-1)+\lambda,

that is

\varepsilon=\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2},\qquad\varepsilon\eta=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}-\lambda.

Using this transform, the problem (
2
) reads as

(A-\lambda I)u=\varepsilon(|u|+\eta u)

(3)

and instead of
(\alpha,\beta,u)
with
\alpha\neq\beta
, we are looking for a triple

(\varepsilon,\eta,u)
for
\varepsilon\not=0
.
Notice that
\varepsilon
indicates how “close” we are to the diagonal
\alpha=\beta=\lambda
, and
\eta
is related to the “direction” from the point
(\lambda,\lambda)
on the diagonal in
\alpha\beta
plane.
Namely, we have

\eta=\frac{(\alpha-\lambda)+(\beta-\lambda)}{(\alpha-\lambda)-(\beta-\lambda)}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\frac{\eta-1}{\eta+1}=\frac{\beta-\lambda}{\alpha-\lambda}.

(4)

In particular, we study the existence of the Fučík curve described by
(\varepsilon,\eta(\varepsilon),u(\varepsilon))

and the limit value

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\eta(\varepsilon)=:\eta_{0},

which provides us the information about the slope of the Fučík curve emanating from the point
(\lambda,\lambda)
.

To obtain required information, we will use Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction and Implicit Function Theorem. Let us denote

X_{1}:=\Ker(A-\lambda I),\quad Y_{2}:=\Img(A-\lambda I),

and by
X_{2}
,
Y_{1}
their orthogonal complements, i.e.,

H=X_{1}\oplus X_{2}=Y_{1}\oplus Y_{2}.

We recall that

Y_{1}=\Ker(A^{\ast}-\lambda I).

Further, we denote by
P
and
Q
the orthogonal projections onto
X_{1}
and
Y_{1}
, respectively.
For any
u=u(\varepsilon)\in H
, we have

\displaystyle Q(A-\lambda I)u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle(I-Q)(A-\lambda I)u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(A-\lambda I)u.

Hence, the problem (
3
) is equivalent to the couple of equations

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon Q(|u|+\eta u),

(5)

\displaystyle(A-\lambda I)u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon(I-Q)(|u|+\eta u).

(6)

Moreover,

(A-\lambda I)u=(A-\lambda I)(Pu+(I-P)u)=(A-\lambda I)(I-P)u

and the restriction

(A-\lambda I)\big|_{X_{2}}:\ X_{2}\to Y_{2}

is invertible with the inverse
(A-\lambda I)\big|_{X_{2}}^{-1}
defined on
Y_{2}
.
Hence, for any
\varepsilon\not=0
, the problem (
5
)–(
6
) becomes

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q(|u|+\eta u),

(7)

\displaystyle(I-P)u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon(A-\lambda I)\big|_{X_{2}}^{-1}(I-Q)(|u|+\eta u).

(8)

Finally, let us recall the following differentiability notions and properties in Euclidean spaces. Let
F:\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{k_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathbb{R}^{k_{n}}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
By a (partial) derivative of
F
with respect to a vector
x_{i}=[x_{i,1},\dots,x_{i,k_{i}}]^{t}
at
x\in\Omega
we mean a Jacobi (
m\times k_{i}
) matrix

\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}}(x)=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\displaystyle\frac{\partial F_{1}}{\partial x_{i,1}}(x)&\dots&\displaystyle\frac{\partial F_{1}}{\partial x_{i,k_{i}}}(x)\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial F_{m}}{\partial x_{i,1}}(x)&\dots&\displaystyle\frac{\partial F_{m}}{\partial x_{i,k_{i}}}(x)\end{array}\right].

Further, let us introduce the “sign” mapping
\Xi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
the “zero-characteristic” mapping
\Xi^{0}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

by diagonal matrices

\displaystyle\Xi(u)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\diag\left([\sgn(u_{1}),\sgn(u_{2}),\dots,\sgn(u_{n})]\right),

\displaystyle(\Xi^{0}(u))_{ij}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&i=j\mbox{ and }u_{i}=0,\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Notice that
\Xi^{0}(u)=I-\Xi(|u|)
. If
u_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is such that
u_{0,i}\not=0
for all
i=1,\dots,n
, then the mapping

is differentiable at

and the derivative

\frac{\partial f}{\partial u}(u_{0})=\Xi(u_{0}).

Moreover, for any

and

Now, we are ready to formulate our main results.

3
Main results

We will start with a necessary condition, which provides all possible directions the Fučík curves can emanate in from the diagonal point
(\lambda,\lambda)
in
\alpha\beta
-plane.

Theorem 2
.

Let
A:~H\to H
be a linear operator represented by a
n
-by-
n
matrix with a real eigenvalue
\lambda

If there exists a sequence of triples
(\varepsilon_{n},\eta_{n},u_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times H

\varepsilon_{n}\not=0
,
\|u_{n}\|=1
,

, and
(\varepsilon_{n},\eta_{n},u_{n})\to(0,\eta_{0},u_{0})

u_{0}\in\Ker(A-\lambda I)
,
u_{0}\not=0
, and the projection of

is zero, i.e.

\forall v\in\Ker(A^{\ast}-\lambda I):\ \left\langle(|u_{0}|+\eta_{0}u_{0}),v\right\rangle=0.

(10)

Proof.

Since the problem (
3
) is equivalent to (
7
)–(
8
), we get for any

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q(|u_{n}|+\eta_{n}u_{n}),

\displaystyle(I-P)u_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{n}(A-\lambda I)\big|_{X_{2}}^{-1}(I-Q)(|u_{n}|+\eta_{n}u_{n}).

∎

As a consequence, we obtain an analogous assertion concerting a continuous Fučík curve and its slope.

Theorem 3
.

Let
A:~H\to H
be a linear operator represented by a
n
-by-
n

If there exists a continuous Fučík curve

The assertion follows directly from Theorem

If

The nondegeneracy condition (

) in Theorem

includes the requirement
Qu_{0}\not=0
, or, in other words,

and the algebraic multiplicity of

10

7
Generalization

The first possible generalization concerns the situation off the diagonal. Statements analogous to Theorems
2
,
3
,
5
can be formulated replacing
(\lambda,\lambda)
by a Fučík eigenpair
(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})\in\Sigma(A)
. However, due to the lack of linearity, the assumption of simplicity of
(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})
is now essential. Moreover, notice that for
A
non-selfadjoint, the corresponding adjoint problem to (
1
) reads as

A^{\ast}v=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\alpha(I+\Xi(u))+\beta(I-\Xi(u))\right)v,

where
u
is the Fučík eigenfunction with all components nonzero corresponding to the simple Fučík eigenpair
(\alpha,\beta)\in\Sigma(A)
. That is, the adjoint problem is linear with coefficients driven by the nodal properties of the solution of the original (nonlinear) Fučík problem. Due to these technical obstacles we do not formulate and prove the assertions within this paper. The case of
A
selfadjoint is treated in
[
6
]
.

The second possible generalization concerns more general structures than matrices. In particular, let
H
be a Hilbert space over the field of real numbers
\mathbb{R}
, endowed with a scalar product
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
and a norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|
induced by the scalar product.
Moreover, let
H
be ordered by a closed convex cone
K
such that
(H,\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle,K)

The Fučík spectrum of
L

\Sigma(L)

Lu=\alpha u^{+}-\beta u^{-}

\dim\Ker(L-\lambda I)<+\infty,\qquad\Img(L-\lambda I)\mbox{~is~closed},\qquad\codim\Img(L-\lambda I)<+\infty,

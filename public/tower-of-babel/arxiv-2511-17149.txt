Introduction

The study of isolated singularities in semi-linear elliptic equations has been a central focus in mathematical analysis, particularly for understanding the behavior of solutions near the singularity point. As it is well known, the fundamental solutions of the Laplacian operator play a crucial role in many works. A celebrated result due to Brezis and Lions
[
5

-\Delta u+au\geq f\quad\mbox{ in }B_{1}\setminus\{0\}

(1.1)

.

u,\;\Delta u,\;V(|x|)u\in L^{1}_{loc}(B_{1}),

(1.7)

The second goal of this article is concerned with the study of non-negative solutions to the following elliptic equation with convolution term:

-\Delta u+\lambda V(x)u=(K_{\alpha,\beta}*u^{p})u^{q}\quad\text{in }B_{1}\setminus\{0\},

(1.10)

where
0<V\in\mathcal{C}^{0,\nu}(\overline{B}_{1}\setminus\{0\})
,
0<\nu<1
, is not necessarily radial,
\lambda,p,q>0
and

K_{\alpha,\beta}(x)=\lvert x\rvert^{-\alpha}\log^{\beta}\frac{2e}{\lvert x\rvert},\quad\text{where }0\leq\alpha<N,\,\beta\in\mathbbm{R}.

(1.11)

We note that
K_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\in L^{1}(B_{1})
. The quantity
K_{\alpha,\beta}*u^{p}
represents the convolution operation in
B_{1}
given by

(K_{\alpha,\beta}\ast u^{p})(x)=\int_{B_{1}}\frac{\log^{\beta}\frac{2e}{\lvert x-y\rvert}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{\alpha}}u^{p}(y)dy,\quad\text{for all }x\in B_{1}\setminus\{0\}.

We say that a function
u\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(B_{1}\setminus\{0\})
is a solution to (
1.10
) if

u\geq 0\;,\;\;(K_{\alpha,\beta}*u^{p})(x)<\infty\quad\mbox{ for all }x\in B_{1}\setminus\{0\},

(1.12)

and
u
satisfies (
1.10
) pointwise in
B_{1}\setminus\{0\}
. It is easy to see that (
1.12
) implies
u^{p}\in L^{1}(B_{1})
.

If
\beta=0
, then

becomes the standard Riesz potential of order

was studied in the exterior domains of

in
[

I(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{1}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+\lambda V(x)u^{2}\right)-\frac{1}{2p}\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}K_{\alpha,\beta}(x-y)u^{p}(x)u^{p}(y)dxdy.

There are many recent studies of the above and related functional for

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
0\leq\alpha<N
,
\beta\in\mathbbm{R}
,
0\leq\rho\leq\frac{1}{3}

I_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(\rho,x)=\int_{\rho<|y|<1}\frac{\log^{\beta}\frac{2e}{|x-y|}}{|x-y|^{\alpha}|y|^{\gamma}}dy\,,\quad\mbox{ for all }x\in B_{1}\setminus B_{\rho}.

(2.1)

(i)

If

, then
I_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(\rho,x)=\infty
for all

.

(ii)

If
0\leq\gamma<N
, then there exists a constant
C>0
independent of

, we have:

I_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(\rho,x)\geq C\left\{\begin{aligned} &|x|^{N-\alpha-\gamma}\log^{\beta}\frac{2e}{|x|}\quad&\text{if }\alpha+\gamma>N&,\\[5.69046pt]
&\log^{1+\beta}\frac{2e}{|x|}\quad&\text{if }\alpha+\gamma=N&,\,\beta>-1,\\[5.69046pt]
&\log\left(\log\frac{2e}{|x|}\right)\quad&\text{if }\alpha+\gamma=N&,\,\beta=-1,\\[5.69046pt]
&1\quad&\text{if }\alpha+\gamma=N&,\,\beta<-1,\\[5.69046pt]
&1\quad&\text{if }\alpha+\gamma<N&.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.2)

(iii)

If
0\leq\gamma<N
, then

I_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(0,x)\simeq\left\{\begin{aligned} &|x|^{N-\alpha-\gamma}\log^{\beta}\frac{2e}{|x|}\quad&\text{if }\alpha+\gamma>N&,\\[5.69046pt]
&\log^{1+\beta}\frac{2e}{|x|}\quad&\text{if }\alpha+\gamma=N&,\,\beta>-1,\\[5.69046pt]
&\log\left(\log\frac{2e}{|x|}\right)\quad&\text{if }\alpha+\gamma=N&,\,\beta=-1,\\[5.69046pt]
&1\quad&\text{if }\alpha+\gamma=N&,\,\beta<-1,\\[5.69046pt]
&1\quad&\text{if }\alpha+\gamma<N&.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.3)

Proof.

It follows that

Thus,

\displaystyle I_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(\rho,x)

\displaystyle\geq\left(2|x|\right)^{-\alpha}\min\left\{\log^{\beta}\frac{e}{|x|},\log^{\beta}\frac{4e}{|x|}\right\}\int_{|y|<\frac{|x|}{2}}|y|^{-\gamma}dy

\displaystyle=C|x|^{-\alpha}\min\left\{\log^{\beta}\frac{e}{|x|},\log^{\beta}\frac{4e}{|x|}\right\}\int_{0}^{\frac{|x|}{2}}t^{N-\gamma}\frac{dt}{t}=\infty.

It is enough to establish the inequality (
2.2

\displaystyle I_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(\rho,x)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{2|x|<|y|<1}\frac{\min\left\{\log^{\beta}\frac{e}{|y|},\log^{\beta}\frac{4e}{|y|}\right\}}{(2|y|)^{\alpha}|y|^{\gamma}}dy

\displaystyle=c\int_{2|x|}^{1}\min\left\{\log^{\beta}\frac{e}{t},\log^{\beta}\frac{4e}{t}\right\}t^{N-\alpha-\gamma}\frac{dt}{t}.

Let

is the modified Bessel function of the second kind of order

From
[
1
, page 415]
we have the following property of

:

\lim_{z\to 0}\frac{K_{\frac{N-2}{2}}(z)}{|z|^{-\frac{N-2}{2}}}=2^{\frac{N-4}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{N-2}{2}\right),

(3.2)

where
\Gamma
is the standard Gamma function.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
N\geq 3
. Then, for all
\mu>0
we have

\lim_{|x|\to 0}\frac{G_{\mu}(x)}{E(|x|)}=1,

where
E(|x|)
is the fundamental solution of the Laplace operator, see (
1.8
).

Proof.

Since
\sigma_{N}=\frac{2\pi^{N/2}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)}
, from (
1.8
) and (
3.2
) we find

\lim_{|x|\to 0}\frac{G_{\mu}(x)}{E(|x|)}=\frac{N-2}{2^{\frac{N-2}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)}\lim_{|x|\to 0}\frac{K_{\frac{N-2}{2}}(\sqrt{\mu}|x|)}{(\sqrt{\mu}|x|)^{-\frac{N-2}{2}}}=\frac{\frac{N-2}{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{N-2}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)}=1.

7
A new sub and super-solution method in a nonlocal setting

In this section we devise a new sub and super-solution method for the semilinear equation with non-local term:

-\Delta u+V(x)u=(K_{\alpha,\beta}*u^{p})u^{q}\quad\mbox{ in }B_{1}\setminus\{0\}.

(7.1)

We point out that our method is different from that recently obtained in
[
3
]
for the Choquard equation (that is,
q=p-1
and
\beta=0
) in annular or exterior domains.

Proposition 7.1
.

Assume
N\geq 2
,
p,q>0
,
0\leq V\in\mathcal{C}^{0,\nu}(\overline{B}_{1}\setminus\{0\})
and there exists
\widetilde{u},\widetilde{U}\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\overline{B}_{1}\setminus\{0\})\cap L^{p}(B_{1})
such that:

(i)

0<\widetilde{u}\leq\widetilde{U}
in
B_{1}\setminus\{0\}
and
K_{\alpha,\beta}*\widetilde{U}^{p}
is locally bounded in
B_{1}\setminus\{0\}
,

(ii)

for all
k\geq 3
we have

-\Delta\widetilde{u}(x)+V(x)\widetilde{u}(x)\leq\left(\,\int\limits_{\frac{1}{k}<|y|<1}K_{\alpha,\beta}(x-y)\widetilde{u}^{p}(y)dy\right)\widetilde{u}^{q}(x)\quad\mbox{ in }B_{1}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{k}},

(7.2)

(iii)

there holds:

-\Delta\widetilde{U}(x)+V(x)\widetilde{U}(x)\geq\left(\,\int\limits_{0<|y|<1}K_{\alpha,\beta}(x-y)\widetilde{U}^{p}(y)dy\right)\widetilde{U}^{q}(x)\quad\mbox{ in }B_{1}\setminus\{0\}.

Then, there exists a solution

of (
1.16
) such that
\widetilde{u}\leq u\leq\widetilde{U}
in
B_{1}\setminus\{0\}
.

Proof.

We construct a sequence
\{u_{k}\}_{k\geq 3}
as follows:

We let
u_{3}:=\widetilde{u}
and for all

inductively as follows:

\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\Delta u_{k+1}+V(x)u_{k+1}=\left(\,\int\limits_{\frac{1}{k}<|y|<1}K_{\alpha,\beta}(x-y)u_{k}^{p}(y)dy\right)u_{k}^{q}&\quad\mbox{ in }B_{1}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{k}},\\[5.69046pt]

For convenience, we extend

to

Step 1:

Also, from (

[

), it follows that

in

.
Further, we have

\displaystyle-\Delta u_{4}+V(x)u_{4}

\displaystyle=\left(\,\int\limits_{\frac{1}{3}<|y|<1}K_{\alpha,\beta}(x-y)u_{3}^{p}(y)dy\right)u_{3}^{q}\quad\mbox{ in }B_{1}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{3}},

B_{1}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{3}}

in
B_{1}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{3}}

,

Proof of Theorem

(i) We require the following auxiliary result.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let

Then

and there exists a harmonic function

where

(8.3)

Furthermore, we have

\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\overline{u}(r)}{E(r)}=m.

(8.4)

Various versions of the above result are known in the literature (see
[
5
]
,
[
22
, Theorem 1.1.]
or
[
28
, Lemma 1]
) especially in the case
g\geq 0
(so that
u
becomes super-harmonic in
B_{1}\setminus\{0\}
). We provide here a complete detail of its proof and emphasize that

may be any sign-changing function that is locally integrable.

Proof.

From (
8.1
), we have that

-\Delta u=g\quad\text{in }B_{1}\setminus\{0\}.

Taking the spherical average, we obtain:

-(r^{N-1}\overline{u}^{\prime}(r))^{\prime}=r^{N-1}\overline{g}(r)\quad\text{for all }0<r<1.

Let
0<r<\rho<1
. Integrating the above equality over
[r,\rho]
yields

r^{N-1}\overline{u}^{\prime}(r)-\rho^{N-1}\overline{u}^{\prime}(\rho)=\int_{r}^{\rho}s^{N-1}\overline{g}(s)\,ds.

Thus, there exists some

such that

r^{N-1}\overline{u}^{\prime}(r)=c+\frac{1}{\sigma_{N}}\int_{r<|x|<\rho}g(|x|)\,dx\quad\text{for all }0<r<\rho.

Then, by l’Hôpital’s rule, we deduce that there exists

l:=\lim\limits_{r\to 0}\frac{\overline{u}(r)}{E(r)}\geq 0.

In particular, we have

\int_{|x|<\varepsilon}u(x)\,dx=\begin{cases}\displaystyle o\left(\varepsilon\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)&\text{if }N=2,\\[8.5359pt]
\displaystyle o(\varepsilon^{2})&\text{if }N\geq 3.\end{cases}

(8.5)

On the other hand, by Fubini’s theorem, we obtain:

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}}W(x)\Delta\varphi(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}}g(y)\left(\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}}E(|y-x|)\Delta\varphi(x)dx\right)dy

\displaystyle=-\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}}g(y)\varphi(y)dy\quad\text{for all }\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{2}}).

It follows that

-\Delta W=g\quad\text{in }\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(B_{\frac{1}{2}}).

Thus,

-\Delta(u-W)=0\quad\text{in }\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(B_{\frac{1}{2}}\setminus\{0\}),

is a harmonic function in
B_{\frac{1}{2}}\setminus\{0\}
.

By Lemma 1.6.1 in
[
27
]
, we have

\frac{1}{\sigma_{N}r^{N-1}}\int_{|x|=r}E(|x-y|)d\sigma(x)=\begin{cases}E(r)\quad\text{if }|y|<r,\\
E(y)\quad\text{if }|y|>r.\end{cases}

(8.6)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma_{N}r^{N-1}}\int_{|x|=r}\left(\int_{|y|<\frac{1}{2}}E(|y-x|)g(y)dy\right)d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\int_{r<|y|<\frac{1}{2}}g(y)\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{N}r^{N-1}}\int_{|x|=r}E(|y-x|)d\sigma(x)\right)dy

\displaystyle=E(r)\int_{|y|<r}g(y)dy+\int_{r<|y|<\frac{1}{2}}g(y)E(y)dy.

Applying the Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem, we deduce

\int_{|x|<\varepsilon}|u(x)-W(x)|dx=o(\varepsilon)\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0.

From Lemma 1.2 in

for some

where

Consequently, we find

. This establishes (

\frac{\overline{u}(r)}{E(r)}\to m\quad\text{as }r\to 0.

This concludes the proof.

(ii) Our main tool is Proposition

(8.9)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{m\sigma(1-\sigma)}{|x|^{2}}\log^{\sigma-2}\frac{2e}{|x|}

\displaystyle\geq(K_{\alpha,\beta}*\widetilde{U}^{p})\widetilde{U}^{q}(x)\quad\mbox{ in }B_{1}\setminus\{0\}.

This shows that
\widetilde{U}
satisfies (
7.3

\displaystyle-\Delta\widetilde{u}(x)+\lambda V(x)\widetilde{u}(x)

\displaystyle=\lambda V(x)\widetilde{u}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\,\int\limits_{\frac{1}{k}<|y|<1}K_{\alpha,\beta}(x-y)\widetilde{u}^{p}(y)dy\right)\widetilde{u}^{q}(x)\quad\mbox{ in }B_{1}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{k}},

Proof of Theorem
1.6

Assume
N\geq 3
and that
0\leq u\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(B_{1}\setminus\{0\})
is a solution of (
1.10
) with
u(x)\simeq|x|^{2-N}
. Then, by the estimates in Lemma
2.1
(iii) we have:

-\Delta u+\lambda V(x)u\simeq\Psi(x)\quad\text{in }B_{1}\setminus\{0\},

(9.1)

where

\Psi(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &|x|^{-q(N-2)}\quad&\text{if }p<\frac{N-\alpha}{N-2}&,\\[5.69046pt]
&|x|^{-q(N-2)}\quad&\text{if }p=\frac{N-\alpha}{N-2}&\text{ and }\beta<-1,\\[5.69046pt]
&|x|^{-q(N-2)}\log\left(\log\frac{2e}{|x|}\right)\quad&\text{if }p=\frac{N-\alpha}{N-2}&\text{ and }\beta=-1,\\[5.69046pt]
&|x|^{-q(N-2)}\log^{1+\beta}\frac{2e}{|x|}\quad&\text{if }p=\frac{N-\alpha}{N-2}&\text{ and }\beta>-1,\\[5.69046pt]
&|x|^{N-\alpha-(p+q)(N-2)}\log^{\beta}\frac{2e}{|x|}\quad&\text{if }p>\frac{N-\alpha}{N-2}&.\end{aligned}\right.

(9.2)

Next, we divide our proof into four cases.

Case 1
:
p<\frac{N-\alpha}{N-2}
or
p=\frac{N-\alpha}{N-2}
and
\beta<-1
.

Since
V(x)=o\left(|x|^{-(q-1)(N-2)}\right)
as
|x|\to 0
, it follows from (
9.1
) and (
9.2
) that:

-\Delta u\geq C(1-o(1))|x|^{-q(N-2)}\quad\text{in }B_{1}\setminus\{0\}.

(9.3)

Then, there exists
0<\rho<1
small such that

-\Delta u\geq C|x|^{-q(N-2)}\quad\text{in }B_{\rho}\setminus\{0\}.

Thus, by Lemma
6.1
, we conclude that
|x|^{-q(N-2)}\in L^{1}_{loc}(B_{\rho})
, which implies that
q<\frac{N}{N-2}
.

Case 2
:
p=\frac{N-\alpha}{N-2}
and
\beta=-1
.

In this case, we apply a similar argument to that in (
9.3
) to conclude the following:

-\Delta u\geq C|x|^{-q(N-2)}\log\left(\log\frac{2e}{|x|}\right)\quad\text{in }B_{\rho}\setminus\{0\},

where
0<\rho<1
small. Applying Lemma
6.1
, we deduce that
|x|^{-q(N-2)}\log\left(\log\frac{2e}{|x|}\right)\in L^{1}_{loc}(B_{\rho})
, and therefore
q<\frac{N}{N-2}
.

Case 3
:
p=\frac{N-\alpha}{N-2}
and
\beta>-1
.

As above, we apply a similar argument to that in (
9.3
) to conclude:

-\Delta u\geq C|x|^{-q(N-2)}\log^{1+\beta}\frac{2e}{|x|}\quad\text{in }B_{\rho}\setminus\{0\},

where
0<\rho<1
small. Applying Lemma
6.1
again, we deduce that
|x|^{-q(N-2)}\log^{1+\beta}\frac{2e}{|x|}\in L^{1}_{loc}(B_{\rho})
, and therefore
q<\frac{N}{N-2}
.

Case 4
:
p>\frac{N-\alpha}{N-2}
.

We have

V(x)u(x)=o\left(|x|^{N-\alpha-(p+q)(N-2)}\log^{\beta}\frac{2e}{|x|}\right)\quad\text{as }|x|\to 0.

It follows from (
9.1
) that

-\Delta u\geq C|x|^{N-\alpha-(p+q)(N-2)}\log^{\beta}\frac{2e}{|x|}\quad\text{in }B_{\rho}\setminus\{0\},

(9.4)

where
0<\rho<1
small. By Lemma
6.1
, we derive
|x|^{N-\alpha-(p+q)(N-2)}\log^{\beta}\frac{2e}{|x|}\in L^{1}_{loc}(B_{\rho})
and it follows that

\displaystyle\text{either}\quad p+q<\frac{2N-\alpha}{N-2}\quad\text{or}\quad p+q=\frac{2N-\alpha}{N-2}\text{ and }\beta<-1.

Since
p>\frac{N-\alpha}{N-2}
, we also deduce that
q<\frac{N}{N-2}
.

Conversely, assume now that either (
1.17
) or (
1.18
) holds, and let us construct a positive solution

to (
1.10

.
To this aim. we need to provide two suitable functions
\widetilde{u}
and
\widetilde{U}
that fulfill the hypotheses in Proposition
7.1
. We split our analysis into three cases.

Case A:

. If (

(9.5)

If (

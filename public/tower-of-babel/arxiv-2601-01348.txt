1
Introduction

1.1
The Dirichlet Principle

In all this work we will be considering a bounded Jordan curve
\Gamma
and call
\Omega_{i},\Omega_{e}
respectively the interior and exterior connected components of its complement in the sphere. If
f
is a continuous function on
\Gamma
and
F
a continuous extension of
f
to
\overline{\Omega}_{i,e}
which is furthermore assumed to be
C^{1}
in
\Omega_{i,e}
we call
(2-)
energy the quantity

\mathcal{E}_{i,e}(F)=\iint_{\Omega_{i,e}}|\nabla F(z)|^{2}dxdy\leq+\infty.

The Dirichlet principle asserts that among all the extensions of
f
the one with the lowest energy is the harmonic one. Notice that the
(2-)
energy of the harmonic extension of a continuous function may be infinite, in which case every extension has infinite
(2-)
energy. When
\Gamma=\mathbb{T}
, the unit circle, we define
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{D}_{i})
, the Dirichlet space over the unit disk
\Omega_{i}=\mathbb{D}_{i}
as the space of harmonic functions in
\mathbb{D}_{i}
with finite
(2-)
energy. This space coincides with the space of Poisson integrals of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})-
functions such that

\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|n||\hat{f}(n)|^{2}<\infty.

As Douglas observed in
[
14
]
it also coincides with the space of Poisson integrals of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
-functions such that

\iint_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}}\frac{|f(z)-f(\zeta)|^{2}}{|z-\zeta|^{2}}|dz||d\zeta|<\infty.

The map
z\mapsto 1/\bar{z}
is a reflection about the unit circle which is bi-Lipschitz from
\{1/2<|z|<2\}
onto itself. It follows that if
u_{i}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{D}_{i})
is the Poisson integral of
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
then the Poisson extension
u_{e}
of
f
in
\mathbb{D}_{e}=\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{i}
also has a finite energy. We may thus view the Dirichlet space as the subset of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
of functions whose harmonic extension to
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\mathbb{T}
has finite energy.

The Dirichlet space is a special case of homogeneous fractional Sobolev spaces. More precisely, if
0<s<1
, we define
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
as the subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
of functions
f
such that

\|f\|^{2}_{H^{s}}=\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|n|^{2s}|\hat{f}(n)|^{2}<\infty.

It is not difficult to see that the above quantity, the square of the
H^{s}
-semi-norm, is equivalent (in particular, in the case of
s=1/2
it is equal, up to a constant multiplicative factor,) to two other:

1.

The (square of the) Littlewood-Paley semi-norm

\iint_{\mathbb{D}_{i}}|\nabla u_{i}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|)^{1-2s}dxdy,

2.

The (square of the) Douglas norm

\iint_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}}\frac{|f(z)-f(\zeta)|^{2}}{|z-\zeta|^{1+2s}}|dz||d\zeta|.

The mention of Littlewood-Paley comes from the fact that for
s=0
the Littlewood-Paley semi-norm is exactly the
L^{2}
-norm of
\mathsf{g}(f)
, the Littlewood-Paley function associated to
f

[
37
]
.

In terms of Besov spaces,

H^{s}(\mathbb{T})=B_{2,2}^{s}(\mathbb{T}),

and that this space also coincides with
(I-\Delta)^{-s/2}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}))
, namely the space of Bessel potentials of order
s
.

We can further generalize these spaces by replacing
2
with
p>1
and define
B_{p,p}^{s}(\mathbb{T})
as the set of
L^{p}(\mathbb{T})-
functions such that either

1.

(Littlewood-Paley)

\iint_{\mathbb{D}_{i}}|\nabla u_{i}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|)^{(1-s)p-1}dxdy<\infty,

2.

(Douglas)

\iint_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}}\frac{|f(z)-f(\zeta)|^{p}}{|z-\zeta|^{1+ps}}|dz||d\zeta|<\infty.

It has to be noticed that if
p\neq 2
,

B_{p,p}^{s}(\mathbb{T})\neq(I-\Delta)^{-s/2}(L^{p}(\mathbb{T})),

so that, to avoid confusions, we will always use the Besov terminology at least for
p\neq 2
.

The main goal of this paper is to extend these notions to more general Jordan curves. The first restriction we want to impose on the Jordan curve
\Gamma
is that we want it to share with
\mathbb{T}
the reflection property, that is we want the existence of a reflection across
\Gamma
which is bi-Lipschitz in a neighborhood of
\Gamma
. The curves satisfying this property are well-known: they are the quasicircles, i.e., the quasiconformal images of circles
[
1
]
. Geometrically, the Jordan curve
\Gamma
is a quasicircle if and only if there exists
C>1
such that

\forall z_{1},z_{2}\in\Gamma,\quad\min(\mathrm{diam}(\gamma_{1}),\mathrm{diam}(\gamma_{2}))\leq C|z_{1}-z_{2}|

where
\gamma_{j},j=1,2
, are the two subarcs of
\Gamma
with endpoints
z_{1},z_{2}
.

A quasicircle need not be smooth, and the Hausdorff dimension of a quasicircle can take any value in
[1,2)
. As an example, the Von Koch snowflake curve is a quasicircle without any tangent and whose Hausdorff dimension, which coincides with its Minkowski dimension, is
\log 4/\log 3
.

From now on, all the Jordan curves that we consider will be assumed to be quasicircles.

1.2
Statement of the Main Theorems

Let
\Gamma
be a quasicircle and
\Omega_{i,e}
the inner and outer connected components of
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Gamma
as above. Suppose
\Omega
is one of these two components and correspondingly
\mathbb{D}
is one of
\mathbb{D}_{i}
and
\mathbb{D}_{e}
.
We define

\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)=\{u\;\mathrm{harmonic\;in}\;\Omega:\;\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)}^{p}:=\iint_{\Omega}|\nabla u(z)|^{p}d(z,\Gamma)^{(1-s)p-1}dxdy<\infty\}.

We define for functions in these spaces and for some values of
p,\,s
their boundary values on
\Gamma
, which will allow us to identify
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)
with a space of functions defined on
\Gamma
that we denote by
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega\!\to\!\Gamma)
.

In order to define this space we distinguish three cases, namely,
s>1/p,\,s=1/p,\,s<1/p
.

1.

Case
s>1/p
: we will see that in this case
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)\subset\Lambda^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
, the Hölder space with exponent
\alpha=s-1/p
. The boundary value of a function in
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)
is then just its restriction to
\Gamma
.

2.

Case
s=1/p
: we use here the remarkable fact that these spaces are conformally invariant. In particular, if
f\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)
, then
f\circ\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\mathbb{D})
where
\varphi
is a Riemann mapping from
\mathbb{D}
onto
\Omega
, and this isomorphism
T
is almost isometric in the sense that, due to the Koebe distortion theorem,

\forall f\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega),\quad\frac{1}{4}\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega)}\leq\|Tf\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\mathbb{D})}\leq 4\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega)}.

Since this latter space is included in the harmonic Hardy space
h^{p}(\mathbb{D})
we conclude that
f
has a well-defined boundary value in
L^{p}(\omega,\Gamma)
, where
\omega
is the harmonic measure for
\Omega
.

3.

Case
s<1/p
: we proceed here differently. Let
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})|_{\Gamma}=\{f|_{\Gamma}:\;f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})\}
,
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
-functions being
C^{\infty}
-functions with compact support in
\mathbb{C}
. Let
\mathcal{D}_{p}^{s}(\Gamma)
be the set of functions in
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})|_{\Gamma}
such that their harmonic extensions to
\Omega_{i,e}
belongs to
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i,e})
, and we denote the set of such extensions by
\mathcal{D}_{p}^{s}(\Omega_{i,e})
.
We define the space of boundary values
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i,e}\!\to\!\Gamma)
as the abstract closure of this space, i.e., equivalence classes of Cauchy sequences in
\mathcal{D}_{p}^{s}(\Gamma)
, equipped with the
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i,e})
-norm. Notice that this procedure only defines boundary values for the closures of
\mathcal{D}_{p}^{s}(\Gamma)
in
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i,e})
, which may not be the full space. However, the space
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i,e})
in this case is quite problematic, and the closure of
\mathcal{D}_{p}^{s}(\Omega_{i,e})
in
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i,e})
is actually a more robust variant of this space.
.

For all these cases we define

\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)=\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i}\to\Gamma)\cap\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{e}\to\Gamma),

equipped with the natural norm. One of our main tasks will be to examine when these three spaces coincide. The case 2 above is particularly interesting especially since the two harmonic measures may be mutually singular. This drawback has been ruled by
[
36
]
and
[
5
]
by exhibiting a change of variable realizing a "transmission" operator between the spaces on both sides which happens to be an isomorphism, thus showing the equality of the three spaces for all quasicircles.

When
\Gamma
is furthermore assumed to be rectifiable we define, following Douglas
[
14
]
,

B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)=\{f\in L^{p}(\Gamma):\;\|f\|_{B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)}^{p}:=\iint_{\Gamma\times\Gamma}\frac{|f(z)-f(\zeta)|^{p}}{|z-\zeta|^{1+ps}}|dz||d\zeta|<\infty\}.

Here and in what follows,
|dz|
denotes the arc-length measure.
If
\Gamma
is the unit circle the three spaces
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i}\to\Gamma),\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{e}\to\Gamma),B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
coincide (see p.151 in
[
38
]
), but they have no reason to coincide in general. Finding cases of equality is one of our goals. In order to state the first result in this direction, we need some definitions:

A curve
\Gamma
is said to be chord-arc (or
K
-chord-arc) if it is the bi-Lipschitz image of a circle. These curves are geometrically characterized by the fact that they are rectifiable and that

\exists K>1:\quad\forall z_{1},z_{2}\in\Gamma,\,\min(\mathrm{length}(\gamma_{1}),\mathrm{length}(\gamma_{2}))\leq K|z_{1}-z_{2}|,

where
\gamma_{j},\,j=1,2
, are the two subarcs of
\Gamma
with endpoints
z_{1},z_{2}
. Notice that chord-arc curves are in particular rectifiable quasicircles (but the converse is not true).

We will also need the notion of radial-Lipschitz curves. These are the curves given in polar coordinates by the equation
z(\theta)=r(\theta)e^{i\theta}
where
r:\mathbb{R}\to(0,+\infty)
is
2\pi
-periodic and Lipschitz continuous. The radial-Lipschitz curves are in particular chord-arc.

In
[
44
]
and
[
45
]
the present authors have proven

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p>1
.
If
\Gamma
is chord-arc then

\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega_{i}\to\Gamma)=\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega_{e}\to\Gamma)=B_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Gamma).

(1.1)

Conversely, if
\Gamma
is a rectifiable quasicircle such that (
1.1
) holds and
p\geq 2
then
\Gamma
is chord-arc.

The methods we used for the proof of this theorem depend heavily on the special features of the case
s=1/p
, namely, conformal invariance and the existence of a transmission operator.

In this paper our main results in this direction are Theorems
5.5
,
5.7
,
5.8
combined with the claims in appendix
that loosely speaking say that

Theorem 1.2
.

If
\Gamma
is a radial-Lipschitz curve and
p=2
,
s\in(0,1)
then

\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i}\!\to\!\Gamma)=\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{e}\!\to\!\Gamma)=B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma).

(1.2)

This can be generalized to any
p\in(1,\infty)
under a slightly different assumption related to
p
.

We will also prove a result in this regard for general quasicircles. Before stating it we need to introduce some notions:

Astala
[
3
]
has introduced the notion of "Minkowski content"
h(\Gamma)
: the proper definition will be recalled below but let us just mention here that
h(\Gamma)
is the Minkowski dimension if
\Gamma
is a self-similar fractal set (as is the Koch snowflake curve for example).

The Muckenhoupt weights
A_{p}(\mathbb{C}),\,p>1
, are the weights
\omega
such that the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function is bounded on
L^{p}(\omega,\mathbb{C})
. Let
\omega
be such a weight. We define the weighted Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(\omega,\mathbb{C})
as being the space of tempered distributions
f
such that
f
is locally integrable and, in the sense of distributions,
\nabla f\in L^{p}(\omega,\mathbb{C})
.

Theorem 1.3

(see Theorem
3.16
)
.

If
\Gamma
is a quasicircle and

\frac{1}{p}<1-\frac{1}{p}\leq s<\frac{p+K_{*}-h(\Gamma)}{K_{*}p}

where
K_{*}\leq 2
is a geometric quantity depending only on
\Gamma
to be defined in section
3
then

\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i}\to\Gamma)=\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{e}\to\Gamma)=W^{1,p}(\omega,\mathbb{C})|_{\Gamma}

(1.3)

where
\omega(z)=d(z,\Gamma)^{(1-s)p-1}
.

Theorem
1.3
allows us to improve theorem
1.2
for certain values of
p,s
:

Corollary 1.4
.

If
\Gamma
is chord-arc and
p>2,s\in[1-\frac{1}{p},\frac{p+K_{*}-1}{K_{*}p})
then (

) holds.

Another corollary is an "almost-Dirichlet principle" generalizing, in dimension
2
a result of Maz’ya simplified by Mironescu-Russ (
[
29
]
,
[
30
]
). We say that the quasidisk
\Omega
satisfies the almost-Dirichlet principle for
p,s
if any continuous function
f:\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
having a continuous extension
F:\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{C}
which is in
C^{1}(\Omega)
and satisfies

\iint_{\Omega}|\nabla F(z)|^{p}d(z,\Gamma)^{(1-s)p-1}dxdy<\infty

is in
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega\to\Gamma)
, or, in other words, is such that

\iint_{\Omega}|\nabla u(z)|^{p}d(z,\Gamma)^{(1-s)p-1}dxdy<\infty,

where
u
is the harmonic extension of
f
in
\Omega
.

Corollary 1.5
.

Under the assumption of Theorem
1.3

the almost-Dirichlet principle holds for
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)
.

One of the tools for proving Theorem

will be the use of Plemelj-Calderón property which is of independent interest.

1.3
Plemelj-Calderón Problem

It is an old problem, given a bounded Jordan curve
\Gamma
in the plane and a (complex valued) function
f
defined on
\Gamma
, to find two holomorphic functions
G_{i},G_{e}
defined on the interior-connected and the exterior-connected components of
\Gamma
such that

f=G_{i}|_{\Gamma}+G_{e}|_{\Gamma},

the boundary traces being defined in some sense.

This problem was actually raised and solved by Sokhotski in 1873 before being rediscovered by Plemelj as a main ingredient of his attempt to solve Hilbert’s 22th problem in 1908 (
[
34
]
). Assume first that
\Gamma
is smooth and
f
is a
C^{1}
-function on
\Gamma
.
The idea is to use the Cauchy integral and define

F(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\zeta,\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\Gamma.

This defines a function which is holomorphic outside the curve
\Gamma
. Moreover, this function has boundary values on
\Gamma
from inside and outside, and one has Plemelj formula on
\Gamma
:

\displaystyle F_{i}|_{\Gamma}=Tf+1/2f,

\displaystyle F_{e}|_{\Gamma}=Tf-1/2f

where the Cauchy integral

Tf(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\text{p.v.}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\zeta=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{|\zeta-z|>\varepsilon}\frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\zeta.

The problem is then solved with
G_{i}=F_{i}
and
G_{e}=-F_{e}
.

Of course the result remains true for much more general curves and functions. Subsequent generalizations relax the smoothness requirements on the curve
\Gamma
and the function
f
: In order for Plemelj formula to hold true one indeed only needs
\Gamma
to be rectifiable and
f\in L^{1}(\Gamma)
. The modern approach of this problem is then to solve the problem in a given space: Calderón (
[
6
]
)
asked for instance for which rectifiable curves it is true that for any
f\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
Plemelj functions
F_{i}|_{\Gamma}
and
F_{e}|_{\Gamma}
are also in
L^{2}(\Gamma)
? Equivalently, when is the operator
T
bounded on
f\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
? This problem has been solved by Coifman-McIntosh-Meyer (
[
10
]
) for Lipschitz curves (Calderón had previously solved it for curves with small Lipschitz bound (
[
6
]
)) and then the last word was given by David (
[
11
]
) who proved that
T
is bounded on
f\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
(or
L^{p}(\Gamma)
,
1<p<\infty
) if and only if
\Gamma
is Ahlfors-regular, meaning that the length of the part of
\Gamma
lying in a disk of radius
r
is no bigger than
Cr
for some
C
independent of the chosen disk.

Now Plemelj-Calderón problem may be addressed for curves that are not necessarily rectifiable. An example has been given in
[
48
]
where Plemelj-Calderón problem for Hölder classes has been raised and solved in some particular cases. The idea is to replace the Cauchy integral by the identity

f(z)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\bar{\partial}f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\xi d\eta

which is true for any test function
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
, and from which it follows that, in the case of rectifiable boundary,

\displaystyle F_{i}(z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\Omega_{e}}\frac{\bar{\partial}f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\xi d\eta,\qquad z\in\Omega_{i};

\displaystyle F_{e}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\Omega_{i}}\frac{\bar{\partial}f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\xi d\eta,\qquad z\in\Omega_{e}

where
\Omega_{i,e}
stand for the interior and exterior components of
\Gamma
. Later Astala (
[
3
]
) obtained more precise results for the Hölder classes: these will be discussed in section 3 where we will draw the parallel with fractional Sobolev spaces.

In this paper we address the following Plemelj-Calderón problem: given
p>1,\,s\in(0,1)
, can one write a function
f\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
as a sum of two functions
F_{i,e}
that are holomorphic in
\Omega_{i,e}
and whose boundary values belong to
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
?

The main result of the present paper in this direction is Theorems
2.3
,
2.4
,
3.5
and Corollaries
3.8
,
3.17
.
A priori version of these results is as follows.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\Gamma
be a quasicircle ,
p>1,\,s\in(0,1)
. Then a priori Plemelj-Calderón property holds for
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})|_{\Gamma}
, if

(p-1)(h(\Gamma)-1)<sp<p+1-h(\Gamma).

2
Critical Besov Spaces on Quasicircles

Recall that a quasidisk is the image of the unit disk
\mathbb{D}_{i}
by a quasiconformal homeomorphism of the plane, and its boundary curve is called a quasicircle. An homeomorphism
\Phi
of the plane is called quasiconformal if its gradient in the sense of distribution is in
L^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{C})
and if in addition there exists a constant
k<1
such that

\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\bar{z}}=\mu(z)\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial z}

for a function
\mu\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
with
\|\mu\|_{\infty}\leq k
. If we put
K=\frac{1+k}{1-k}
then
K
represents the maximal eccentricity of infinitesimal images of circles by the quasiconformal mapping.

Any quasicircle
\Gamma
admits a quasiconformal reflection across
\Gamma
which is bi-Lipschitz around
\Gamma
. Here, by a quasiconformal reflection we mean an anti-quasiconformal self-homeomorphism
R
of
\overline{\mathbb{C}}
such that
R\circ R=\text{id}
on
\overline{\mathbb{C}}
and
R=\text{id}
when restricted to
\Gamma
. Precisely, let
\Phi
be a quasiconformal homeomorphism of
\overline{\mathbb{C}}
we then have

R(z)=\Phi\circ(1/\overline{\Phi^{-1}(z)})
is a quasiconformal reflection with respect to the quasicircle
\Gamma:=\partial\Phi(\mathbb{D}_{i})
.

Let
\Omega_{i}
and
\Omega_{e}
be domains bounded by a quasicircle
\Gamma
.
If
\varphi_{i}
and
\varphi_{e}
are conformal isomorphisms from
\mathbb{D}_{i}
onto
\Omega_{i}
and from
\mathbb{D}_{e}
onto
\Omega_{e}
, respectively, then they can be extended to a homeomorphism between the closures such that

h:=\varphi_{e}^{-1}\circ\varphi_{i}
is a homeomorphism of
\mathbb{T}
which is quasisymmetric in the sense that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

C^{-1}\leq\frac{|h(e^{i(t+\alpha)})-h(e^{it})|}{|h(e^{it})-h(e^{i(t-\alpha)})|}\leq C

for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
-\pi/2<\alpha\leq\pi/2
. Moreover, every quasisymmetry arises in this way from some quasicircle. For these results on quasiconformal theory, we refer to
[
1
]
for details.

Let
\Gamma
be a Jordan curve and
p>1
. We recall that the spaces
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega_{i,e})
, called the
p
-critical Besov spaces, have the remarkable property of being conformally invariant: if
\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}
are two Jordan domains and
\varphi:\Omega\to\Omega^{\prime}
is a biholomorphism then the map
f\mapsto f\circ\varphi
is a quasi-isometry between
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega^{\prime})
and
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega)
. In order to be as much as possible self-contained, here is a sketch of proof: by the change of variable
\zeta=\varphi(z)
we have

\iint_{\Omega^{\prime}}|\nabla u(\zeta)|^{p}d(\zeta,\Gamma^{\prime})^{p-2}dxdy=\iint_{\Omega}|\nabla u(\varphi(z))|^{p}d(\varphi(z),\Gamma^{\prime})^{p-2}|\varphi^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dxdy.

Now the Koebe distortion theorem
[
1
]
implies that

\forall z\in\Omega,\quad\frac{1}{4}\leq\frac{d(\varphi(z),\Gamma^{\prime})}{d(z,\Gamma)|\varphi^{\prime}(z)|}\leq 4,

(2.1)

and the result follows.

Applying this result for
\Omega^{\prime}=\mathbb{D}
,
\partial\mathbb{D}
being the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
, we see that all the
p
-critical Besov spaces over
\Omega
are quasi-isometric to
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\mathbb{D})
. It is a classical result
[
37
]
that the space
\mathcal{C}_{p}(\mathbb{D})
consisting of functions in
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\mathbb{D})
that have a continuous extension to
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
is dense in
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\mathbb{D})
. This can also be derived from that the set of polynomials is dense in the Bergman space
A^{p}_{p-2}
, the set of analytic functions
g
in
\mathbb{D}
such that

\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|g(z)|^{p}|1-|z|^{2}|^{p-2}dxdy<\infty.

Since a bi-holomorphic homeomorphism between two Jordan domains extends to a homeomorphism between the closures the same property will hold for the analogous spaces
\mathcal{C}_{p}(\Omega)
.

The next step is to prove that
\mathcal{C}_{p}(\Omega_{i})|_{\Gamma}=\mathcal{C}_{p}(\Omega_{e})|_{\Gamma}
for any quasicircle
\Gamma
. We first prove the result for
p=2
.

Let
u_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{2}(\Omega_{i})
and
f=u_{i}|_{\Gamma}
. Recall that there exists a quasiconformal reflection
R
across
\Gamma
.
Put
v(z)=u_{i}(R(z)),z\in\Omega_{e}
. By the bi-Lipschitz property of
R
around
\Gamma
we have that

\iint_{\Omega_{e}}|\nabla v(z)|^{2}dxdy<\infty

(stricly speaking, there is a technical problem at
\infty
, which is settled in the appendix). We invoke then the Dirichlet principle which implies that

\iint_{\Omega_{e}}|\nabla u_{e}(z)|^{2}dxdy\leq\iint_{\Omega_{e}}|\nabla v(z)|^{2}dxdy<+\infty,

where
u_{e}:=P_{e}(f)
stands for the harmonic extension of
f
in
\Omega_{e}
. That is, we have that the transmission operator

\mathcal{C}_{2}(\Omega_{i})\ni u_{i}\mapsto u_{e}\in\mathcal{C}_{2}(\Omega_{e})

is bounded. Actually, it is a bounded isomorphism since the boundedness of the inverse operator can be seen by exchanging the roles of
\Omega_{i}
and
\Omega_{e}
, which proves the result.

If
p>1
and
0<s<1
(except the case of
p=2
and
s=1/2
) we loose the Dirichlet principle and use instead the almost-Dirichlet principle. Recall that the almost-Dirichlet principle holds for
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)
if for any
f
defined on
\Gamma
,
u
denotes its harmonic extension in
\Omega
and
v
is any continuous extension of
f
to
\Omega
which is in
C^{1}(\Omega)
, we have

\iint_{\Omega}|\nabla u(z)|^{p}d(z,\Gamma)^{(1-s)p-1}dxdy\leq C\iint_{\Omega}|\nabla v(z)|^{p}d(z,\Gamma)^{(1-s)p-1}dxdy

for some constant
C
depending only on
\Omega,\,p,\,s
. It was proved in
[
30
]
that the almost-Dirichlet principle holds for
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\mathbb{D}_{i,e})
for all
p>1,\,0<s<1
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a quasicircle and
1<p<\infty
. The almost-Dirichlet principle holds for the space
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega_{i,e})
.

Proof.

The almost-Dirichlet principle in
\mathbb{D}_{i}
applied to
s=1/p
, reads

\iint_{\mathbb{D}_{i}}|\nabla u(z)|^{p}(1-|z|)^{p-2}dxdy\leq C\iint_{\mathbb{D}_{i}}|\nabla v(z)|^{p}(1-|z|)^{p-2}dxdy,

(2.2)

with the same notations as before:
u
is the harmonic function with the boundary value
u|_{\Gamma}=f
and
v
is any
C^{1}
extension of
f
to
\mathbb{D}_{i}
.
Let
\varphi
be a conformal mapping from
\mathbb{D}_{i}
onto
\Omega_{i}
. By the conformal invariance of the space
W^{1,p}(\omega,\mathbb{D}_{i})
where
\omega(z)=(1-|z|)^{p-2}
and using the change of variables, we have

\iint_{\Omega_{i}}|\nabla(u\circ\varphi^{-1})|^{p}d(z,\,\Gamma)^{p-2}dxdy\leq C\iint_{\Omega_{i}}|\nabla(v\circ\varphi^{-1})|^{p}d(z,\,\Gamma)^{p-2}dxdy.

(2.3)

Noting that
u\circ\varphi^{-1}
is harmonic we have proved the almost-Dirichlet principle for the space
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega_{i}).
The assertion for the space
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega_{e})
can be treated similarly.
∎

As a consequence, if
f
is a continuous function on
\Gamma
, its harmonic extension to
\Omega_{i}
is in
\mathcal{C}_{p}(\Omega_{i})
if and only if its harmonic extension to
\Omega_{e}
is in
\mathcal{C}_{p}(\Omega_{e})
.
Then,
C_{p}(\Omega_{i}\!\to\!\Gamma)=C_{p}(\Omega_{e}\!\to\!\Gamma)
so that
we may define
\mathcal{C}_{p}(\Gamma):=\mathcal{C}_{p}(\Omega_{i,e}\!\to\!\Gamma)
.

Let
\varphi_{i,e}
denote the Riemann mappings from
\mathbb{D}_{i,e}
onto
\Omega_{i,e}
as before. By the conformal invariance, we have that
f\circ\varphi_{i,e}\in\mathcal{C}_{p}(\mathbb{T})
and
f\circ\varphi_{e}=f\circ\varphi_{i}\circ h
with
h:=\varphi_{i}^{-1}\circ\varphi_{e}
being a circle homeomorphism called conformal welding. As we have recalled above this homeomorphism is a quasisymmetry: we deduce from this that
V_{h}:g\mapsto g\circ h
is an isomorphism of
\mathcal{C}_{p}(\mathbb{T})
which extends to an isomorphism of
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\mathbb{T})
with
V_{h}^{-1}=V_{h^{-1}}
. Now if one consider any quasisymmetry
h
of the unit circle we know
[
1
]
that
h
is the conformal welding of some quasicircle. We deduce from this geometric approach that
V_{h}
is an isomorphism of
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\mathbb{T})
for any quasisymmetry
h
. Conversely, Nag-Sullivan
[
33
]
for the case
p=2
and Bourdaud
[
5
]
for the general case have proven that if
h
is a circle homeomorphism and if
V_{h}
is an isomorphism of
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\mathbb{T})
then
h
must be a quasisymmetry. Vodop’Yanov
[
42
]
, and later Bourdaud
[
5
]
moreover proved that if
s\neq 1/p
then the circle homeomorphisms
h
such that
V_{h}
operates on
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\mathbb{T})
isomorphically are the bi-Lipschitz ones which form a proper subgroup of the group of quasisymmetries.

2.1
Boundary Values of Functions
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega)

When dealing with a function
u
in
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega_{i})
that is continuous up to the boundary the boundary values are obvious: it is just the restriction to the boundary, and moreover we have just seen that if we take the harmonic extension of this boundary value in
\Omega_{e}
we get a function
v\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega_{e})
with a norm equivalent to the one we started with. Now let us consider a general function
u\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega_{i})
: as we have seen, this function is the limit of a sequence
(u_{n})\in\mathcal{C}_{p}(\Omega_{i})
and we put
f_{n}
to be the boundary value of
u_{n}
. The harmonic extension to
\Omega_{e}
of
f_{n}
is then a Cauchy sequence in
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega_{e})
, thus convergent to some
v\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega_{e})
and we may regard the couple
(u,v)
as an abstract version of the boundary value of
u
, and also
v
.

If we want to be more concrete, we use the Riemann maps
\varphi_{i,e}
:
u\circ\varphi_{i}\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\mathbb{D}_{i})\subset h^{p}(\mathbb{D})
, the classical harmonic Hardy space of the disk, so it has radial boundary values almost everywhere on the circle defining a function
b_{i}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
. Notice that this fact translates in
\Omega_{i}
by saying that
u
has limits along
\omega_{i}
-almost every internal ray, where
\omega_{i}
stands for harmonic measure in
\Omega_{i}
. Notice that this limit
b_{i}
characterizes
u
. We may do the same thing with
v
and define an analogous function
b_{e}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
. When
u\in\mathcal{C}_{p}(\Omega_{i})
we have seen that
b_{e}=V_{h}(b_{i})
where
h
is the conformal welding of
\Gamma
. Unfortunately it does not make sense in
L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
since
h
need not be absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure, a fact which transfers geometrically to the fact that
\omega_{i}
and
\omega_{e}
need not be mutually absolutely continuous. Nevertheless, the relation
b_{e}=V_{h}(b_{i})
remains true if we interpret
V_{h}
on
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\mathbb{T})
as the completion of
V_{h}|_{\mathcal{C}_{p}^{1/p}(\mathbb{T})}
.

In the case of
p=2
, Schippers-Staubach
[
36
]
has given an even more concrete description of the boundary values: we outline their argument. On the one hand, for any
u\in\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{1/2}(\Omega_{i})
we have
u\circ\varphi_{i}\in\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{1/2}(\mathbb{D}_{i})
as before. In this case,
u\circ\varphi_{i}
has radial boundary values everywhere on the circle
\mathbb{T}
except on
a Borel set
F_{1}
of Logarithmic capacity zero,
defining a function
b_{i}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
.
On the other hand, let
h:=\varphi_{i}^{-1}\circ\varphi_{e}
, the conformal welding with respect to the quasicircle
\Gamma
, that is a quasisymmetry (so is
h^{-1}
) as we pointed out above. Since a quasisymmetry
takes a Borel set of Logarithmic capacity zero to a Borel set of Logarithmic capacity zero (see
[
2
]
) we see that
F_{2}:=h^{-1}(F_{1})
is also a Borel set of Logarithmic capacity zero. Then
b_{e}=b_{i}\circ\varphi_{i}^{-1}\circ\varphi_{e}=V_{h}(b_{i})
is well-defined on
\mathbb{T}\setminus F_{2}
and belongs to
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
. It is known that the union
F_{1}\cup F_{2}
is also of Logarithmic capacity zero, and in particular, Lebesgue measure zero since a set of Logarithmic capacity zero has zero Lebesgue measure.
Put
P_{e}(b_{e})
to be the harmonic extension in
\mathbb{D}_{e}
of
b_{e}
, and

v:=P_{e}(b_{e})\circ\varphi_{e}^{-1}
is the harmonic extension in
\Omega_{e}
of
b_{i}\circ\varphi_{i}^{-1}
. We can see that
u
and
v
have the same boundary values along every internal ray on
\Gamma
, except on a set whose harmonic measure is zero with respect to both
\Omega_{i}
and
\Omega_{e}
. Moreover, since the spaces
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega_{i,e})
increase with
p
the same argument also holds for the case of
1<p<2
.

2.2
Plemelj-Calderón Problem for

\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{1/2}(\Gamma)

We can now address Plemelj-Calderón problem for
\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{1/2}(\Gamma)
. The argument is broken into two steps. In the first we consider the function in the space
D(\Gamma)
, the restriction space to
\Gamma
of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
. The second step consists in an approximation process.

For any test function
f
, it holds that

f(z)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\bar{\partial}f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\xi d\eta,\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}.

(2.4)

Indeed,

\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\bar{\partial}f(\zeta)}{z-\zeta}d\xi d\eta=\bar{\partial}f(z)\ast\left(\frac{1}{\pi z}\right)=\bar{\partial}\left(\frac{1}{\pi z}\right)\ast f(z)=\delta_{0}\ast f(z)=f(z)

where
\ast
stands for convolution, and
\delta_{0}
denotes the Dirac measure at
0
.
Define

\widehat{F_{i}}(z)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\Omega_{e}}\frac{\bar{\partial}f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\xi d\eta,\qquad z\in\Omega_{i},

(2.5)

\widehat{F_{e}}(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\Omega_{i}}\frac{\bar{\partial}f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\xi d\eta,\qquad z\in\Omega_{e}.

(2.6)

It is easily seen that
\widehat{F_{i}}
and
\widehat{F_{e}}
are holomorphic functions in
\Omega_{i}
and
\Omega_{e}
, respectively with
\widehat{F_{e}}(z)=O(\frac{1}{|z|})
at
\infty
. Furthermore, it can be shown that
both
\widehat{F_{i}}
and
\widehat{F_{e}}
define continuous functions at each
z\in\mathbb{C}
.
It follows from (
2.4
) that on
\Gamma
,

f=\widehat{F_{i}}-\widehat{F_{e}}.

(2.7)

Here, we have used that the quasicircle
\Gamma
has zero area with respect to the two-dimensional Lebesgue measure. Taking derivative we have that

\widehat{F_{i}}^{\prime}(z)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\Omega_{e}}\frac{\bar{\partial}f(\zeta)}{(\zeta-z)^{2}}d\xi d\eta=B(\bar{\partial}f\chi_{\Omega_{e}})(z).

Here,
\chi_{\Omega_{e}}
is the characteristic function of the domain
\Omega_{e}
and
B
denotes the Beurling operator, i.e., the convolution with
-\frac{1}{\pi}\text{p.v.}\frac{1}{z^{2}}
which is an isometry of
L^{2}(\mathbb{C})
. Then,

\displaystyle\|\widehat{F_{i}}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{1/2}(\Omega_{i})}

\displaystyle=\|\widehat{F_{i}}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i})}=\|B(\bar{\partial}f\chi_{\Omega_{e}})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i})}

(2.8)

\displaystyle\leq\|B(\bar{\partial}f\chi_{\Omega_{e}})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{C})}\leq\|\bar{\partial}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{e})}\leq\|\bar{\partial}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{C})}.

Similarly, it follows from (
2.6
) that

\|\widehat{F_{e}}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{1/2}(\Omega_{e})}\leq\|\bar{\partial}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{C})}.

(2.9)

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
, the integrals in (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) depend only on the boundary values
f
on
\Gamma
, not on the specific extensions belonging to
B_{2,2}^{1/2}(\Omega_{i,e})
of
f
to respectively
\Omega_{i}
and
\Omega_{e}
. In particular,
\widehat{F_{i}}
and
\widehat{F_{e}}
defined in respectively (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) equal to

F_{i}(z)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\Omega_{e}}\frac{\bar{\partial}u_{e}(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\xi d\eta,\qquad z\in\Omega_{i},

(2.10)

F_{e}(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\Omega_{i}}\frac{\bar{\partial}u_{i}(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\xi d\eta,\qquad z\in\Omega_{e}

(2.11)

so that

f=F_{i}-F_{e}

(2.12)

on
\Gamma
.
Here,
u_{i}
and
u_{e}
are harmonic extensions of
f
to respectively
\Omega_{i}
and
\Omega_{e}
.

Proof.

Note that
u_{i}
and
u_{e}
are continuous on
\overline{\Omega}_{i}
and
\overline{\Omega}_{e}
, respectively since the boundary function
f
is continuous on
\Gamma
. By the Dirichlet principle,
u_{i,e}\in\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{1/2}(\Omega_{i,e})
. Using the similar computation we can see that the holomorphic functions
F_{i}\in\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{1/2}(\Omega_{i})
and
F_{e}\in\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{1/2}(\Omega_{e})
.

By the theorem of Gol’dshtein-Latfullin-Vodop’yanov
[
18
]
(see also
[
22
]
)

u_{i}
can be extended to
U_{i}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{C})
, defining
U_{i}=u_{i}\circ R
on
\Omega_{e}
where
R
is a quasiconformal reflection across
\Gamma
. There exists a sequence
(U_{i,n})
of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
converging to
U_{i}
in
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{C})
(see e.g. Ch.11 in
[
26
]
). Denote
U_{i,n}|_{\Gamma}
by
f_{i,n}
. By what proceeds,

may be written as
\widehat{F_{i,n}}-\widehat{F_{e,n}}
on
\Gamma
. Here,

\widehat{F_{i,n}}(z)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\Omega_{e}}\frac{\bar{\partial}U_{i,n}(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\xi d\eta,\qquad z\in\Omega_{i}

and

\widehat{F_{e,n}}(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\Omega_{i}}\frac{\bar{\partial}U_{i,n}(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\xi d\eta,\qquad z\in\Omega_{e}.

Notice that
\widehat{F_{i,n}}
and
\widehat{F_{e,n}}
are
holomorphic in
\Omega_{i},\,\Omega_{e}
respectively, continuous in
\mathbb{C}
, and
F_{e,n}(z)=O(\frac{1}{|z|})
as

.
Similar to the reasoning in (

) we have that

\begin{split}\|\widehat{F_{e,n}}-F_{e}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{1/2}(\Omega_{e})}&=\|(\widehat{F_{e,n}}-F_{e})^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{e})}\leq\|\bar{\partial}U_{i,n}-\bar{\partial}u_{i})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i})}\\
&\leq\|\nabla(U_{i,n}-u_{i})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i})}\leq\|U_{i,n}-U_{i}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{C})}\to 0\end{split}

as

. Combining with the holomorphy of
\widehat{F_{e,n}}
and

in
\Omega_{e}
and
\widehat{F_{e,n}}(z)
,
F_{e}(z)
being

tends to

.
By the Dirichlet principle, we can also see that

\|\nabla(P_{i}(f_{i,n})-u_{i})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i})}\leq\|\nabla(U_{i,n}-u_{i})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i})}\to 0

where

of

.

The sequence

. This proves that on
\Gamma
the function

f=G_{i}-F_{e}.

(2.13)

Let us now consider
u_{e}
the harmonic extension of

in
\Omega_{e}
and
U_{e}
an extension of

belonging to

. If we take a sequence
(U_{e,n})
of

converging to

we can prove, as before, that

f=F_{i}-G_{e}.

(2.14)

By uniqueness of the decomposition, proved in
[

]
mainly based on an identity in
[

]
, we see that these two decomposition (
2.13
),(
2.14
) are same as (
2.7
) so that

\widehat{F_{i}}=F_{i}
,
\widehat{F_{e}}=F_{e}
and

f=F_{i}-F_{e}
on
\Gamma
.
∎

Concerning the above proof, we give two side remarks. Firstly, using a part of the argument in the proof of Lemma
2.2
, we can see that
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})|_{\Gamma}
is a dense subset of
\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{1/2}(\Gamma)
. Precisely, for any
f\in\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{1/2}(\Gamma)
, its harmonic extension

in
\Omega_{i}
can be extended to
U_{i}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{C})
. There exists a sequence
(U_{i,n})
of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
converging to

in
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{C})
. Using the Dirichlet principle, we may conclude the assertion.

Secondly, we explain that if the Jordan curve
\Gamma
is rectifiable then the assertion in Lemma
2.2
can be derived easily from the generalized Green’s formula (see p.150 in
[
28
]
). It says that let
f
have
L^{1}
-derivatives in the domain

and if
\overline{\Omega}\subset G
is a Jordan domain with rectifiable boundary
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
then

\iint_{\Omega}f_{\bar{z}}dxdy=-\frac{i}{2}\int_{\Gamma}fdz.

Let
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
and
\Gamma
be a rectifiable Jordan curve and let
u_{e}
be the harmonic extension of
f
, restricted on
\Gamma
, to
\Omega_{e}
, that is continuous on
\Omega_{e}\cup\Gamma
. By the Dirichlet principle,
u_{e}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega_{e})
.
As we noticed before,
u_{e}
can be extended to
U_{e}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{C})
using the quasiconformal reflection across
\Gamma
. Note that
f-U_{e}
has
L^{2}
-derivatives in
\mathbb{C}
.
By the generalized Green’s formula,

\iint_{\Omega_{e}}\frac{\bar{\partial}(f-u_{e})(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\xi d\eta=-\frac{i}{2}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{(f-u_{e})|_{\Gamma}(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\zeta,\quad z\in\Omega_{i}

which equals to
0
since
(f-u_{e})|_{\Gamma}=0
on
\Gamma
. Thus, we see that

\iint_{\Omega_{e}}\frac{\bar{\partial}f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\xi d\eta=\iint_{\Omega_{e}}\frac{\bar{\partial}u_{e}(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\xi d\eta

which implies that the integral does not depend on the specific extension provided that
\Gamma
is rectifiable.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a quasicircle.
Any function
f\in\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{1/2}(\Gamma)
admits a unique decomposition
f=F_{i}-F_{e}
on
\Gamma
, see (
2.13
), with
F_{i,e}

being holomorphic,
F_{i,e}\in\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{1/2}(\Omega_{i,e})
and

\|F_{i,e}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{1/2}(\Omega_{i,e})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{1/2}(\Gamma)}

for every
f\in\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{1/2}(\Gamma)
where the constant
C
depends only on
\Gamma
.

Proof.

Let now
f
be any function of
\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{1/2}(\Gamma)
. It can be shown that the functions
F_{i}
and
F_{e}
, defined as (
2.10
) and (
2.11
), are still well-defined in this general case, holomorphic in the domains of definition and continuous in
\mathbb{C}
. By the same reasoning as above, we have

\begin{split}&\|F_{i}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{1/2}(\Omega_{i})}\leq\|u_{e}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{1/2}(\Omega_{e})}\simeq\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{1/2}(\Gamma)},\\
&\|F_{e}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{1/2}(\Omega_{e})}\leq\|u_{i}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{1/2}(\Omega_{i})}\simeq\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{1/2}(\Gamma)}.\end{split}

Here and in what follows, the notation "
A\simeq B
" means there exists a constant
C
such that
A/C\leq B\leq CA
.
In the above the implicit constant depends only on
\Gamma
.

Recall that
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})|_{\Gamma}
is a dense subset of
\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{1/2}(\Gamma)
. Using (
2.13
) it is not hard to show
f=F_{i}|_{\Gamma}-F_{e}|_{\Gamma}
by an approximation process. The details are left to the reader.
∎

2.3
Plemelj-Calderón Problem for

\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1-1/p}(\Gamma)

The space
\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{1/2}(\Gamma)
has two remarkable properties: it is conformally invariant and its definition does not involve the distance to the boundary. As a corollary, as we have seen, Plemelj-Calderón property holds for this space for all quasicircles. For
p\neq 2
these properties split: the conformality property remains for the spaces
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Gamma),\,p>1
but in its definition a distance term
d(z,\Gamma)^{p-2}
appears. This case will be discussed in the next section, together with the general case of
s\in(0,1)
with a distance term
d(z,\Gamma)^{(1-s)p-1}
. The other property transfers to
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1-1/p}(\Gamma)
. Notice that
1-1/p=1/p^{\prime}
where
p^{\prime}
is the coefficient conjugate to
p
.

Define

\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{p,p}^{1-1/p}(\Gamma)
as the closure of

C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})|_{\Gamma}\cap\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1-1/p}(\Omega_{i}\to\Gamma)\cap\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1-1/p}(\Omega_{e}\to\Gamma)
in
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1-1/p}(\Gamma)
.
We have the following

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\Gamma
be a quasicircle and
p>2
. Every
f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{p,p}^{1-1/p}(\Gamma)
admits a unique decomposition
f=F_{i}-F_{e}
with
F_{i,e}
being holomorphic,
F_{i,e}\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1-1/p}(\Omega_{i,e})
and

\|F_{i,e}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1-1/p}(\Omega_{i,e})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1-1/p}(\Gamma)}

for every
f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{p,p}^{1-1/p}(\Gamma)
where the constant
C
depends only on
\Gamma
and
p
.

Before we proceed to the proof of this theorem, one must properly define what we mean by
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1-1/p}(\Gamma)
. It happens that functions in
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1-1/p}(\Omega_{i,e})
are Hölder continuous in the closure of
\Omega_{i,e}
with exponent
\alpha=1-2/p
. This may be seen by two different ways. Firstly,
s=1-1/p>1/p
, we will prove later that functions in
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i,e})
are
\alpha
-Hölder continuous in the closure of
\Omega_{i,e}

with
\alpha=s-1/p
. The other way to prove the same thing is to appeal to Jones’s theorem: a function in
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1-1/p}(\Omega_{i,e})
can be extended to a function in
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{C})
even though clearly one cannot extend in this case by using quasiconformal reflection argument, used in the case of
p=2
as above
(see
[
22
]
, also see
[
8
]
). The Morrey’s inequality states that
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{C})\subset\Lambda^{\alpha}(\mathbb{C})
,
p>2
(note that this inclusion relation holds in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
p>n
) for
\alpha=1-2/p
, the space of
\alpha
-Hölder continuous functions in
\mathbb{C}
.
Knowing this fact one may re-define the space
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1-1/p}(\Gamma)
as the space of
\Lambda^{1-2/p}
-functions on
\Gamma
whose harmonic extensions to
\Omega_{i,e}
belong to
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1-1/p}(\Omega_{i,e})
, that is,

\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1-1/p}(\Gamma)=\Lambda^{1-2/p}(\Gamma)\cap\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1-1/p}(\Omega_{i}\to\Gamma)\cap\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1-1/p}(\Omega_{e}\to\Gamma).

Proof.

Thanks to Jones’ theorem the proof is exactly the same as for
p=2
because the Beurling transform is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{C})
for all
p>1
. Notice that the argument of uniqueness used in the case
p=2
goes through here because
L^{p}(\Omega_{i})\subset L^{2}(\Omega_{i})
for
p>2
.
∎

3
Fractional Sobolev Spaces on Quasidisks

3.1
Plemelj-Calderón Property for General
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)

As mentioned in the proceeding section, in order to deal with Plemelj-Calderón property for general
p>1,s\in(0,1)
we need to deal with boundedness of the Beurling transform on
L^{p}(\omega,\,\mathbb{C})
where
\omega
is a weight. By a weight we mean a
nonnegative locally integrable function on
\mathbb{C}
. Since the Beurling transform is a Calderón-Zygmund operator, we know
[
9
]
that this property holds if and only if
\omega
belongs to the so-called class
A_{p}
whose definition we now recall.

Definition 3.1
.

A weight
\omega:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}

is said to satisfy the
A_{1}
condition on
\mathbb{C}
if there exists
C>0
such that for any disk
D
of the plane,

\frac{1}{|D|}\iint_{D}\omega(z)dxdy\leq C\omega(z)

for almost all
z\in D
; while
\omega
is said to satisfy the
A_{\infty}
condition if

\frac{1}{|D|}\iint_{D}\omega(z)dxdy\leq C\exp\left(\frac{1}{|D|}\iint_{D}\log\omega(z)dxdy\right).

For any
p>1
, the
A_{p}
class is the set of weights
\omega:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that there exists
C>1
such that for every disk
D
of the plane,

\frac{1}{|D|}\iint_{D}\omega(z)dxdy\left(\frac{1}{|D|}\iint_{D}w(z)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}dxdy\right)^{p-1}\leq C.

(3.1)

For future use let us notice that
A_{p}
weights on
\mathbb{T}
are defined similarly.
It is known that for all
p>1
,
A_{1}\subset A_{p}\subset A_{\infty}
,
\omega\in A_{p}
is equivalent to
\omega^{-1/(p-1)}\in A_{p^{\prime}}
where
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
, and
A_{p_{1}}\subset A_{p_{2}}
for
1<p_{1}<p_{2}
. By Hölder inequality the left part of the inequality (
3.1
) is always
\geq 1
: one can thus interpret this class as verifying some kind of reverse Hölder inequality. If the weight
\omega
has
A_{p}
then any Calderon-Zygmund operator is bounded on the weighted space
L^{p}(\omega,\mathbb{C})
(see
[
9
]
).

If
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
we recall that

\displaystyle f(z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\bar{\partial}f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\xi d\eta

\displaystyle=F_{i}(z)-F_{e}(z)

where

F_{i}(z)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\Omega_{e}}\frac{\bar{\partial}f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\xi d\eta,\quad F_{e}(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\Omega_{i}}\frac{\bar{\partial}f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\xi d\eta.

Notice
F_{i}^{\prime}(z)=B(\bar{\partial}f\chi_{\Omega_{e}})(z)=B(\bar{\partial}u_{e}\chi_{\Omega_{e}})(z)
,
F_{e}^{\prime}(z)=-B(\bar{\partial}f\chi_{\Omega_{i}})(z)=-B(\bar{\partial}u_{i}\chi_{\Omega_{i}})(z)
where
u_{i,e}
are the harmonic extensions of
f|_{\Gamma}
to
\Omega_{i,e}
, we have that a sufficient condition on
\Gamma
that implies
F_{i,e}\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i,e})
is that the weight
\omega(z)=d(z,\Gamma)^{(1-s)p-1}
satisfies the
A_{p}
condition.
Precisely, under this condition we have

\displaystyle\|F_{i}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i})}\leq C\|u_{e}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{e})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)},

\displaystyle\|F_{e}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{e})}\leq C\|u_{i}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)}

where
C
is an absolute constant.

The dependence of the condition that
d(z,\Gamma)^{(1-s)p-1}
having
A_{p}
on the geometry of the curve has been studied by Astala
[
3
]
. In order to state his results we first need to introduce some notions.

For a compact set
E\subset\mathbb{C}
and
0<\delta\leq 2
let

M_{\delta}(E,t)=\frac{|E+D(0,t)|}{t^{2-\delta}}.

Here,
E+D(0,t)
, by the definition
\{e+z:\;e\in E,\;z\in D(0,t)\}
, is a set of points at a distance less than
t
from
E
which is called Minkowski sausage.
We then define a kind of Minkowski content by

h_{\delta}(E)=\sup\limits_{0<t\leq\mathrm{diam}(E)}{M_{\delta}(E,t)}.

Definition 3.2
.

We say that a Jordan curve
\Gamma
is
\delta
-regular if there exists
C>0
such that for every disk
D(z,R)\subset\mathbb{C}
,

h_{\delta}(\Gamma\cap D(z,R))\leq CR^{\delta}.

It is known that
1
-regularity is equivalent to Ahlfors-regularity.

It has been proved that for any quasicircle
\Gamma
there exists
\delta<2
such that
\Gamma
is
\delta
-regular. We may thus have, for a quasicrcle
\Gamma
,

h(\Gamma)=\inf\{\delta:\Gamma\,\mathrm{is}\,\delta\text{-}\mathrm{regular}\}\in[1,2)

and we may say the degree of regularity of
\Gamma
is
h(\Gamma)
.
For more information on
h(\Gamma)
, and its relation with the Hausdorff dimension
\text{dim}_{H}(\Gamma)
of
\Gamma
, see
[
3
]
.

Definition 3.3
.

A compact subset
E
of the complex plane is said to be porous if there exists
c\in(0,1)
and
r_{0}>1
such that for every
z\in\mathbb{C}
and
0<r\leq r_{0}
, the disk
D(z,r)
contains a disk of radius
cr
which does not intersect
E
.

Theorem 3.4

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\Gamma
be a porous Jordan curve. We have, for any
1<p<\infty
,

d(z,\Gamma)^{\alpha-1}\in A_{p}\Leftrightarrow d(z,\Gamma)^{\alpha-1}\in A_{1}\Leftrightarrow\alpha>h(\Gamma)-1.

(3.2)

Define
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
to be the closure of

C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})|_{\Gamma}\cap\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i}\to\Gamma)\cap\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{e}\to\Gamma)

in
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma).

Notice that every quasicircle is porous (see
[
41
]
).
We may now state the principal theorem of this section:

Figure 1:
Domain formed by points
(h(\Gamma),s)
: case p>2 and case p<2

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a quasicircle and
1<p<\infty,\;0<s<1
. If the point
(h(\Gamma),s)
locates in the shadowed region (see Figure 1); that is,

(h(\Gamma)-1)\frac{p-1}{p}<s<\frac{p+1-h(\Gamma)}{p},

(3.3)

then any
f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
has a decomposition
f=F_{i}-F_{e}
with
F_{i,e}
being holomorphic,
F_{i,e}\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i,e})
and

\|F_{i,e}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i,e})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)}

(3.4)

for every
f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
where the constant
C
depends only on
\Gamma
and
p,\;s
.

Proof.

Following the discussion above, it is clear that (
3.4
) holds if it holds that the weight
d(z,\Gamma)^{(1-s)p-1}\in A_{p}
.
Assume first that
s>\frac{p-1}{p}
so that
(1-s)p<1
. By Theorem
3.4
,
d(z,\Gamma)^{(1-s)p-1}\in A_{p}\Leftrightarrow(1-s)p>h(\Gamma)-1\Leftrightarrow s<\frac{p+1-h(\Gamma)}{p}
.

Assume now
s<\frac{p-1}{p}
. Then,
d(z,\Gamma)^{(1-s)p-1}\in A_{p}\Leftrightarrow d(z,\Gamma)^{\frac{1-(1-s)p}{p-1}}\in A_{p^{\prime}}
. Here,
\frac{1-(1-s)p}{p-1}=\frac{sp}{p-1}-1
with
\frac{sp}{p-1}<1
. By Theorem
3.4
again we have thus that
d(z,\Gamma)^{(1-s)p-1}\in A_{p}\Leftrightarrow s>\frac{p-1}{p}(h-1)
. The decomposition can be proved similar to that of Theorem
2.3
. The case of
s=1-1/p
has been addressed in the last section.
∎

We define
\mathcal{A}
to be the admissible set involved in Theorem
3.5
, that is,

\mathcal{A}=\{(h(\Gamma),p,s)\in[1,2)\times(1,+\infty)\times(0,1):(h(\Gamma)-1)(p-1)<sp<p+1-h(\Gamma)\},

so that if
\Gamma
is a quasicircle and
p>1,\,s\in(0,1)
such that
(h(\Gamma),p,s)\in\mathcal{A}
then Plemelj-Calderón property holds for
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
.
Figure 1 shows slices of
\mathcal{A}
first with
p>1
fixed and Figure 2 with
h(\Gamma)
given.

Figure 2:
Domain formed by points
(p,s)
: case h>3/2 and case h<3/2

We end this part with a comment on the triangular domain above the shadowed region in Figure 1. Let the curve
\Gamma
be a self-similar isotropic fractal which satisfies the open set condition. Its fractal dimension
d(\Gamma)
equals to
h(\Gamma)
in this case (see
[
32
]
). For instance,
the Koch snowflake curve, we mentioned before, is a such quasicircle with
d(\Gamma)=\log 4/\log 3
. Denote by
N(\varepsilon)
the minimal number of disks of radius
\varepsilon
necessary to cover
\Gamma
. Then,

d(\Gamma)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\log N(\varepsilon)}{\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}.

Taking
\varepsilon=2^{-(n+1)}
with sufficiently large
n
one has the difference between the number
N(\varepsilon)
and
2^{(n+1)d(\Gamma)}
approaches
0
as
n
increases to
\infty
.
By dividing the domain
\Omega_{i}
into pieces:

\{z\in\Omega_{i}:\;d(z,\Gamma)\geq 2^{-1}\},\quad\{z\in\Omega_{i}:\;2^{-(j+1)}\leq d(z,\Gamma)<2^{-j}\},\quad j=1,2,3,\cdots

and then computing directly one can see that the integral
\iint_{\Omega_{i}}d(z,\Gamma)^{(1-s)p-1}dxdy
converges if and only if the series
\sum 2^{-n(p-sp+1-h(\Gamma))}
converges, namely,

s<(p+1-h(\Gamma))/p
. Consequently, assuming
s\geq(p+1-h(\Gamma))/p
one can see that in this case even the simplest function
u(z)=z
is not in the space
\mathcal{B}^{s}_{p,p}(\Omega_{i})
.

3.2
Plemelj-Calderón Property for
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Gamma)

Recall that the dense subspace
\mathcal{C}_{p}(\Omega_{i,e})
of
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega_{i,e})
has well-defined boundary values so that
\mathcal{C}_{p}(\Omega_{i}\!\to\!\Gamma)=\mathcal{C}_{p}(\Omega_{e}\!\to\!\Gamma)
, this space of boundary values called
\mathcal{C}_{p}(\Gamma)
. We may then define in an abstract way boundary values
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Gamma)

of functions in
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega_{i,e})
as equivalence classes of Cauchy sequences in
\mathcal{C}_{p}(\Gamma)
.

We may notice that
the dense subspace defined in this way is not very tractable since we do not know the conformal mapping explicitly. The following result gives an explicit dense subspace of
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Gamma)
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\Gamma
be a quasicircle.
If
h(\Gamma)<p<\frac{h(\Gamma)}{h(\Gamma)-1}
, then
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})|_{\Gamma}
is dense in
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Gamma)
.

Remark 3.7
.

Under the assumption of Theorem
3.6
,
when
s<1/p
the only thing that we can say is that
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
contains
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Gamma)
and thus also
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})|_{\Gamma}
.

Proof of Theorem
3.6
.

The assumption on
p
is exactly the condition for
(h(\Gamma),p,\frac{1}{p})\in\mathcal{A}
. If it is satisfied we then have that
\omega(z)=d(z,\Gamma)^{p-2}
is an
A_{p}
weight, in particular, integrable locally in
\mathbb{C}
. Suppose
g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
. Then,

\iint_{\Omega_{i,e}}|\nabla g|^{p}d(z,\Gamma)^{p-2}dxdy\leq\iint_{\mathbb{C}}|\nabla g|^{p}d(z,\Gamma)^{p-2}dxdy<\infty.

By that and the almost-Dirichlet principle (Lemma
2.1
) one may show
g|_{\Gamma}\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Gamma)
.

For a general
f\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Gamma)
, we will use the argument on the boundary values of
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega_{i,e})
in the first paragraph of section 2.1.
Let
u
be the harmonic extension to
\Omega_{i}
of
f
. Noting that
\omega\in A_{p}
we see that
u
has an extension
U\in W^{1,p}(\omega,\;\mathbb{C})
to the whole plane (see
[
8
]
), and there exists a sequence
U_{n}
in
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
such that (see Lemma
3.13
and also
[
8
]
)

\|U_{n}-u\|_{W^{1,p}(\omega,\;\Omega_{i})}\leq\|U_{n}-U\|_{W^{1,p}(\omega,\;\mathbb{C})}\to 0,\qquad n\to\infty.

(3.5)

By the almost-Dirichlet principle (Lemma
2.1
), (
3.5
) implies

\|P_{i}(U_{n}|_{\Gamma})-u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega_{i})}\to 0.

The notations
P_{i}
here and
P_{e}
below denote the harmonic extensions in respectively
\Omega_{i}
and
\Omega_{e}
of functions defined on
\Gamma
. (
3.5
) also implies that the sequence
(U_{n})
is a Cauchy sequence in
W^{1,p}(\omega,\Omega_{e})
, and then, using the almost-Dirichlet principle again, the sequence
(P_{e}(U_{n}|_{\Gamma}))
is also a Cauchy sequence in
W^{1,p}(\omega,\Omega_{e})
, thus convergent to some
v\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega_{e})
.

Consequently,

\|U_{n}|_{\Gamma}-f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Gamma)}^{p}=\|P_{i}(U_{n}|_{\Gamma})-u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega_{i})}^{p}+\|P_{e}(U_{n}|_{\Gamma})-v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega_{e})}^{p}\to 0.

This completes the proof of Theorem
3.6
.
∎

As a corollary of Theorem
3.5
and Theorem
3.6
we immediately have the following.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
\Gamma
be a quasicircle.
If
h(\Gamma)<p<\frac{h(\Gamma)}{h(\Gamma)-1}
then any function
f\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Gamma)
admits the decomposition
f=F_{i}-F_{e}
on
\Gamma

with
F_{i}
and
F_{e}

being holomorphic,
F_{i,e}\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega_{i,e})
and

\|F_{i,e}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega_{i,e})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Gamma)}

for every
f\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Gamma)
where the constant
C
depends on
\Gamma
and
p
.

Remark 3.9
.

We already know that this decomposition is unique if
p=2
. Since the spaces
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\mathbb{D})
increase with
p
we still have uniqueness for
h(\Gamma)<p\leq 2
.

3.3
Boundary Values of Functions in
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)

Let
\Gamma
be a quasicircle and
\Omega
one of its complementary domains

\Omega_{i,e}
as above. The boundary values of the critical case
s=1/p
have been treated in the last section. As announced in the introduction, we will define now what we mean by boundary values of functions in
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega),\,p>1,\,s\in(0,1)
. As we shall see the cases
s<1/p
and
s>1/p
will show to be very different. We start with the case of
s>1/p
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
\Omega
be a (bounded or unbounded) domain bounded by a bounded Jordan curve
\Gamma
. For any
u\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)
with
s\in(1/p,1)

there exists a constant
C>0
such that

|\nabla u(z)|\leq Cd(z,\Gamma)^{\frac{-1-(1-s)p}{p}},\qquad z\in\Omega.

Proof.

Let
z\in\Omega
and
D
is the disk
D(z,d(z,\Gamma)/2)
. By the mean value property of harmonic functions one may write

\nabla u(z)=\frac{1}{|D|}\iint_{D}\nabla u(\zeta)d\xi d\eta.

(Here and in the sequel we write
|D|
for the Lebesgue measure of
D
.)
so that, by Hölder inequality,

\displaystyle|\nabla u(z)|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{\pi d(z,\Gamma)^{2}}\iint_{D}|\nabla u(\zeta)|^{p}d\xi d\eta

\displaystyle\leq Cd(z,\Gamma)^{-1-(1-s)p}\iint_{D}|\nabla u(\zeta)|^{p}d(\zeta,\Gamma)^{(1-s)p-1}d\xi d\eta

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)}^{p}d(z,\Gamma)^{-1-(1-s)p}.

∎

It follows that there exists
C>0
such that
|\nabla u(z)|\leq Cd(z,\Gamma)^{\alpha-1}
with
\alpha=s-1/p
. When
\Gamma
is a quasicircle we may apply the following result due to Gehring-Martio (
[
17
]
).

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
\Omega
be a (bounded or unbounded) domain bounded by a quasicircle
\Gamma
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\forall z\in\Omega,\;|\nabla u(z)|\leq Cd(z,\Gamma)^{\alpha-1}
for some constant
C>0
;

(2)

u\in\Lambda^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
.

Here,
\Lambda^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
stands for the space of Hölder functions of order
\alpha
.

If now
s\in(1/p,1)
then
\alpha=s-1/p\in(0,1)
and Gehring-Martio theorem implies that functions in
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)
are continuous on
\overline{\Omega}
, so that boundary values of functions in
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i,e})
are well-defined and they characterize
u
, as being the unique harmonic extension of this continuous boundary value. We may now define the space
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
for this range
s\in(1/p,1)
as being the space of

(s-1/p)
-Hölder functions
f
on
\Gamma
whose harmonic extension
u_{i,e}
to
\Omega_{i,e}
belongs to
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i,e})
, and the space
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
is assigned the natural norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)}
so that
\|f\|^{p}_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)}=\|u_{i}\|^{p}_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i})}+\|u_{e}\|^{p}_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{e})}
.

3.3.1
Trace Mappings

As we have already seen, in the case of
s>1/p
the spaces
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i,e})
have well-defined boundary values and, with the terminology adopted in the introduction, the trace mapping

\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i,e})\to\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i,e}\to\Gamma)

is an isomorphism. The first purpose of this part is to determine conditions that imply

\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i}\to\Gamma)=\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{e}\to\Gamma).

We already know the result for
s=1/p
and
h(\Gamma)<p<h(\Gamma)/(1-h(\Gamma))
(but in this case the boundary value has a different meaning). Now the space
\Lambda^{s}(\Omega)
introduced above in connection with Gehring-Martio theorem coincides with the Besov space
\mathcal{B}_{\infty,\infty}^{s}(\Omega)
. A theorem of Hinkkanen
[
20
]
(see also
[
48
]
) states that suppose the curve
\Gamma
is a Jordan curve and if
f\in\Lambda^{s}(\Gamma)
with
s<1/2
then the harmonic extension of
f
to
\Omega_{i,e}
is in
\mathcal{B}_{\infty,\infty}^{s}(\Omega)
with norm bounded by that of
f
and the index
1/2
is critical.
Hinkkanen’s result is true for all Jordan curves, and it
has been improved to
s<1/K
in

[
48
]
for quasicircles such that their Riemann mapping has a
K
-quasiconformal extension with
1\leq K\leq 2
. We thus define

K_{*}:=\min{(K,2)}.

We may now state the main result of this part.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
\Gamma,p,s
be such that

h(\Gamma)<p\leq\infty,\;1/p\leq s\;\mathrm{and}\;s<\frac{p+K_{*}-h(\Gamma)}{K_{*}p}

(3.6)

then

\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i}\to\Gamma)=\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{e}\to\Gamma).

(3.7)

Proof.

Let
P_{e}
, as before, be the operator which takes a function on
\Gamma
to its harmonic extension in
\Omega_{e}
. As we have recalled that if

h(\Gamma)<q<\frac{h(\Gamma)}{1-h(\Gamma)},\,\;\;0<\sigma<1/K_{*}

then

\displaystyle P_{e}:\,\mathcal{B}_{q,q}^{1/q}(\Omega_{i}\to\Gamma)

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{B}_{q,q}^{1/q}(\Omega_{e}),

\displaystyle P_{e}:\,\mathcal{B}_{\infty,\infty}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{i}\to\Gamma)

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{B}_{\infty,\infty}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{e}),

are both bounded.
We want to interpolate these results. Using complex interpolation, we have, for
0<\theta<1
,

[\mathcal{B}_{q,q}^{1/q}(\Omega),\mathcal{B}_{\infty,\infty}^{\sigma}(\Omega)]_{\theta}=\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega),

with

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\theta}{q}+\theta\sigma,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\theta}{q}.

We must decide what the couples
(p,s)
are that may be "caught" in this way in view of the constraints: the relations above are equivalent to

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{s-\frac{1}{p}}{\theta},

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle=(1-\theta)p,

and the constraints are

\displaystyle 0<\frac{s-\frac{1}{p}}{\theta}

\displaystyle<1/K_{*},

\displaystyle h(\Gamma)<(1-\theta)p

\displaystyle<\frac{h(\Gamma)}{h(\Gamma)-1}.

That correspond to
\theta
being in the intersection of two intervals

(K_{*}(s-\frac{1}{p}),1),\;(1-\frac{h(\Gamma)}{p(h(\Gamma)-1)},1-\frac{h(\Gamma)}{p}).

We see that in order to have a solution we must have
s>1/p,p>h(\Gamma)
, and
K_{*}(s-\frac{1}{p})<1-\frac{h(\Gamma)}{p}
, which is the condition of the statement of the theorem.
∎

3.3.2
Weighted Sobolev Spaces

Let
\omega
be an
A_{p}
weight defined in
\mathbb{C}
, for some
p>1
. The weighted Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(\omega,\mathbb{C})
is the space of tempered distributions
T
such that
\nabla T
, taken in the sense of distributions, is a function in
L^{p}(\omega,\mathbb{C})
. We will use these spaces for
\omega(z)=d(z,\Gamma)^{(1-s)p-1}\in A_{p}
provided that
(h(\Gamma),p,s)\in\mathcal{A}
.

For the usual technical reasons we need to know that
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
-functions are dense in
W^{1,p}(\omega,\mathbb{C})
.

Lemma 3.13
.

If
\omega\in A_{p}
then the space
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
is dense in
W^{1,p}(\omega,\mathbb{C})
.

Proof.

Let us consider
f\in W^{1,p}(\omega,\mathbb{C})
: without loss of generally we may assume that
f
has compact support. Let then
(\varphi_{\varepsilon})
be a
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
-approximation of identity, so that
f_{\varepsilon}=f*\varphi_{\varepsilon}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
. Now

\|f-f_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{1,p}(\omega,\;\mathbb{C})}^{p}=\iint_{\mathbb{C}}|\nabla f(z)-\nabla f_{\varepsilon}(z)|^{p}\omega(z)dxdy

which converges to
0
by the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem. Indeed
|\nabla f_{\varepsilon}|
is controlled by the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function of
\nabla f
and this maximal function is in
L^{p}(\omega,\mathbb{C})
from the fact that
\omega\in A_{p}
.
∎

In the classical case
\omega\equiv 1
we recall that if
p>2
then
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{C})\subset\Lambda^{\alpha}(\mathbb{C})
with
\alpha=1-2/p
. In the weighted case we do not know this embedding result in general but we have two particular analogues.

Proposition 3.14
.

The following two embedding assertions hold:

•

If
\omega\in A_{1}
then every function in
W^{1,p}(\omega,\mathbb{C})
,
p>2
, is continuous;

•

If
\omega=d(z,\Gamma)^{(1-s)p-1}
is only assumed to be in
A_{q}
for some
q>1
then the same result is true if
p>\frac{6-2h(\Gamma)}{2-h(\Gamma)}
.

Remark 3.15
.

When
\omega(z)=d(z,\Gamma)^{(1-s)p-1}
it is known that for any
q>1
,
\omega\in A_{q}\Leftrightarrow\omega\in A_{1}
if
0<(1-s)p<1
, i.e.,
s>1-1/p
.

Proof of Proposition
3.14
.

Suppose first that
\omega\in A_{1}
. Choose
q
so that
2<q<p
and let
B
be an open ball in
\mathbb{C}
. By Hölder inequality, we get

\int_{B}|\nabla f(x)|^{q}dxdy\leq\left(\int_{B}|\nabla f(x)|^{p}\omega(x)dxdy\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\left(\int_{B}\omega(x)^{-\frac{q}{p-q}}dxdy\right)^{1-\frac{q}{p}}<\infty

because
f\in W^{1,p}(\omega,\mathbb{C})
, and
\omega\in A_{1}\subset A_{p/q}
, which is equivalent to
\omega^{-q/(p-q)}\in A_{p/(p-q)}
, and in particular implies that
\omega^{-q/(p-q)}
is locally integrable.

For the second case let us recall first that if
B
is a ball containing
\Gamma
then

\iint_{B}d(z,\Gamma)^{\beta}dxdy<\infty

if
\beta>h(\Gamma)-2
. By the previous computation it follows that a sufficient condition for the validity of the theorem is

-\frac{2}{p-2}>h(\Gamma)-2

which is equivalent to the condition stated.
∎

We return to Theorem
3.12
. It states that under certain hypothesis on
(h(\Gamma),p,s)
we have

\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i}\to\Gamma)=\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{e}\to\Gamma).

Let us call this space of boundary values
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
.

Theorem 3.16
.

With the following assumption

\frac{1}{p}<1-\frac{1}{p}\leq s<\frac{p+K_{*}-h(\Gamma)}{K_{*}p}

(3.8)

we have that the elements of
W^{1,p}(\omega,\mathbb{C})
are continuous functions in
\mathbb{C}
and that

\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)=W^{1,p}(\omega,\mathbb{C})|_{\Gamma},

(3.9)

where
\omega(z)=d(z,\Gamma)^{(1-s)p-1}
and
W^{1,p}(\omega,\mathbb{C})|_{\Gamma}=\{f|_{\Gamma}:\,f\in W^{1,p}(\omega,\mathbb{C})\}.

Before we start the proof, let us comment on this statement. First of all, the assumption made implies that
\omega\in A_{p}
and that

\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i}\to\Gamma)=\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{e}\to\Gamma)\;(=\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)).

Moreover the inequality
1/p<1-1/p
is equivalent to
p>2
and the inequality
s>1-1/p
further implies, as we have noticed above, that
\omega\in A_{1}
, from which it follows that the elements of
W^{1,p}(\omega,\mathbb{C})
are continuous functions (Proposition
3.14
). Finally, the inequality

1-\frac{1}{p}<\frac{p+K_{*}-h(\Gamma)}{K_{*}p}

is equivalent to
p<\frac{2K_{*}-h(\Gamma)}{K_{*}-1}
.

Proof of Theorem
3.16
.

We first show that
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)\subset W^{1,p}(\omega,\mathbb{C})|\Gamma
. Let
f\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
and let
u_{i}
the harmonic extension of
f
to
\Omega_{i}
. By hypothesis
u_{i}\in W^{1,p}(\omega,\Omega_{i})
and a theorem of
[
8
]
asserts that since
\Gamma
is a quasicircle and
\omega\in A_{p}
,
u_{i}
can be extended to a function of
W^{1,p}(\omega,\mathbb{C})
.

In order to prove that
W^{1,p}(\omega,\mathbb{C})|_{\Gamma}\subset\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
, let us first consider
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
. Recall that

F_{i}(z)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\Omega_{e}}\frac{\bar{\partial}f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\xi d\eta,\quad F_{e}(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\Omega_{i}}\frac{\bar{\partial}f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\xi d\eta

are holomorphic respectively in
\Omega_{i}
and
\Omega_{e}
,
and the fact that
\omega\in A_{p}
implies that
F_{i,e}\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i,e}\to\Gamma)
, which is equal to

\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)

under the assumption of Theorem
3.12
. Using
f|_{\Gamma}=F_{i}-F_{e}
we may see
f|_{\Gamma}\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
.

Let now
f\in W^{1,p}(\omega,\mathbb{C})
. To prove
f|_{\Gamma}\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
we use a standard approximation argument by Lemma
3.13
. There is a sequence
(f_{n})\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})\cap W^{1,p}(\omega,\mathbb{C})
converging to
f
for the
W^{1,p}(\omega,\mathbb{C})
norm. By the preceding discussion we can write
f_{n}|_{\Gamma}=(F_{n})_{i}-(F_{n})_{e}
, with
(F_{n})_{i,e}\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i,e})
and moreover
(F_{n})_{i}
and
(F_{n})_{e}
are converging sequences in
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i,e})
. Since
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i}\to\Gamma)=\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{e}\to\Gamma)
,
f|_{\Gamma}\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
follows.
∎

Corollary 3.17
.

Under the assumption of Theorem
3.16
, the space
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})|_{\Gamma}
is dense in
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
, and thus the conclusions in Theorem
3.5
hold for all
f\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
.

We end this section by a question. Is it true that within the hypothesis of Theorem
3.16
if
f\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
then its extension to the whole plane by the harmonic extension on both sides belongs to
W^{1,p}(\omega,\mathbb{C})
? We do not know the answer to this question in general but only for the cases with improved regularity (bigger
s
):

Proposition 3.18
.

Let
\Gamma
be a quasicircle and let
f
be a function which is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous in
\mathbb{C}
with
\alpha>h(\Gamma)-1
and of class
C^{1}
in
\mathbb{C}\backslash\Gamma
. Then if the function
F(z)=\nabla f(z),\,z\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\Gamma
(thus defined almost everywhere) is in
L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{C})
, we have that, in the sense of distributions,
\nabla f=F
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi
be a
C_{c}^{\infty}
-function in
\mathbb{R}
with support included in
[-1,1]
. As usual we define the approximation of identity
\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\varphi(\frac{x}{\varepsilon})
. From this we can define the approximation of identity in two variables

\psi_{\varepsilon}(z)=\psi_{\varepsilon}(x+iy)=\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y).

We may assume without loss of generality that
f
has compact support, the function
f*\psi_{\varepsilon}
is then a
C^{\infty}
-function with compact support and it converges uniformly in
\mathbb{C}
towards
f
. From this we deduce that

\iint_{\mathbb{C}}\nabla(f*\psi_{\varepsilon})(z)h(z)dxdy=-\iint_{\mathbb{c}}f*\psi_{\varepsilon}(z)\nabla h(z)dxdy

for any
h\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
, and the right-hand side converges to
-\iint_{\mathbb{C}}f(z)\nabla h(z)dxdy
as
\varepsilon
converges to
0
.

We split the left-hand side into two parts:

Let
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
be the set of points at distance less than
2\varepsilon
from
\Gamma
. Then

\iint_{\mathbb{C}\backslash\Gamma_{\varepsilon}}\nabla(f*\psi_{\varepsilon})(z)h(z)dxdy=\iint_{\mathbb{C}\backslash\Gamma_{\varepsilon}}(\nabla F)*\psi_{\varepsilon}(z)h(z)dxdy

which converges as
\varepsilon
goes to
0
towards
\iint_{\mathbb{C}}\nabla F(z)h(z)dxdy
. In order to finish the proof of the proposition, it suffices now to prove that

\iint_{\Gamma_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial(f*\psi_{\varepsilon})(z)}{\partial x}dxdy

converges to
0
, the same fact for
y
being the same. But

\frac{\partial(f*\psi_{\varepsilon})}{\partial x}(z)=f*\frac{\partial(\psi_{\varepsilon})}{\partial x}(z)

which is also equal to

\int_{[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t_{2})\left(\int_{[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]}(f(x-t_{1},y-t_{2})-f(x,y-t_{2}))\varphi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t_{1})dt_{1}\right)dt_{2},

because
\int_{[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]}\varphi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t_{1})dt_{1}=0
. Now
|f(x-t_{1},y-t_{2})-f(x,y-t_{2})|\leq C\varepsilon^{\alpha}
and
|\varphi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t_{1})|\leq C\varepsilon^{-2}
. Since we integrate on an interval of length
2\varepsilon
, the absolute value of the whole integral is bounded by
C\varepsilon^{\alpha-1}
.

Finally,

\iint_{\Gamma_{\varepsilon}}\left|f*\frac{\partial(\psi_{\varepsilon})}{\partial x}(z)\right|dxdy\leq|\Gamma_{\varepsilon}|\varepsilon^{\alpha-1}.

But we know that
|K\Gamma_{\varepsilon}|\varepsilon^{h(\Gamma)-2}\leq C
; we conclude that

|\Gamma_{\varepsilon}|\varepsilon^{\alpha-1}\leq C\varepsilon^{\alpha+1-h(\Gamma)},

and the result follows.
∎

Based on Proposition
3.18
, a sufficient condition for a positive answer to the question is the hypothesis of Theorem
3.16
and
s>\frac{1}{p}+(h(\Gamma)-1)
. To be precise, the function
f\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
, defined to be
u_{i,e}
in
\Omega_{i,e}
, satisfies the assumption of Proposition
3.18
, and thus belongs to
L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{C})
in the sense of distribution, so that
f\in W^{1,p}(\omega,\mathbb{C})
. But for the condition
s>\frac{1}{p}+(h(\Gamma)-1)
to be compatible with (
3.8
) some elementary arithmetic left to the reader leads to the following sufficient conditions:

p>2,\quad\frac{1}{p}+h(\Gamma)-1<s<\frac{p+K_{*}-h(\Gamma)}{pK_{*}}.

Notice that in the case of a chord-arc curve (
h(\Gamma)=1
) there are admissible values of
s
for all
p>2
. In general, for having amissible values of
s
one needs

h(\Gamma)<\frac{K_{*}+1}{K_{*}+\frac{1}{p}}

and for this inequality to hold for any
p>2
and
K_{*}\leq 2
,
h(\Gamma)<\frac{6}{5}
is necessary and sufficient.

3.3.3
Almost-Dirichlet Principle

We start by recalling the classical Dirichlet principle. If
\Omega
is a Jordan domain with
\partial\Omega=\Gamma
and
f
is a continuous function on
\Gamma
having a continuous extension
F:\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{C}
which is in
C^{1}(\Omega)
.
then, if
u
stands for the harmonic extension of
f
to
\Omega
, we have

\iint_{\Omega}|\nabla u(z)|^{2}dxdy\leq\iint_{\Omega}|\nabla F(z)|^{2}dxdy.

Now let
\Gamma
be a quasicircle and
p>1,\,s\in(0,1)
: we will say that
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)
satisfies an almost-Dirichlet principle if there exists
C>0
such that

\iint_{\Omega}|\nabla u(z)|^{p}d(z,\Gamma)^{(1-s)p-1}dxdy\leq C\iint_{\Omega}|\nabla F(z)|^{p}d(z,\Gamma)^{(1-s)p-1}dxdy.

Maz’ya
[
29
]
has proven the almost-Dirichlet principle for the unit disk for all
p>1,s\in(0,1)
and his proof has been simplified by
[
30
]
. Theorem
3.16
shows that if
\Gamma
satisfies the hypothesis of this theorem, then the almost-Dirichlet principle holds.

3.3.4
The Case of
p
–Dirichlet Energy

In this part we highlight the case of
p
-Dirichlet energy corresponding to
\omega\equiv 1
, i.e.,
s=1-1/p
.

Theorem 3.19
.

If
\Gamma
is a quasicircle then for all
p\in\left(2,\frac{2K_{*}-h(\Gamma)}{K_{*}-1}\right)
, there exists a constant
C=C(p,\Gamma,s)
such that for
v\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
,

\iint_{\Omega}|\nabla u(z)|^{p}dxdy\leq C\iint_{\Omega}|\nabla v(z)|^{p}dxdy,

(3.10)

where
u
stands for the harmonic extension of
v|_{\Gamma}
in
\Omega
.

The proof is just by putting
s=1-1/p
in Theorem
3.16
and taking care of the constraints: details are left to the reader. In particular, if
\Gamma
is chord-arc then (
3.10
) holds for
p\in[2,3)
, and we have the following extension if
K<2
:

Corollary 3.20
.

If
\Gamma
is chord-arc with
K<2
then the almost-Dirichlet principle holds for
2\leq p<\frac{2K-1}{K-1}.

Notice that the corollary implies that for any
p>2
the almost-Dirichlet principle is valid if
K
is close enough to
1
.

4
Fractional Sobolev Spaces on Chord-arc Domains

4.1
A Review of Classical Facts about Fractional Sobolev Spaces on Plane Domains.

4.1.1
Fractional Sobolev Spaces on Plane Domains

We have already dealt with the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{C})
,
1<p<\infty
consisting of tempered distributions
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{C})
such that
f
is locally integrable and
\nabla f
, taken in the sense of distributions, is in
L^{p}(\mathbb{C})
. The semi-norm

\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(\mathbb{C})}=\left(\iint_{\mathbb{C}}|\nabla f(z)|^{p}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

equips
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{C})
with a structure of Banach space modulo constants called homogeneous Sobolev space in the literature. The inhomogeneous Sobolev space is given by
\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{C})=W^{1,p}(\mathbb{C})\cap L^{p}(\mathbb{C})
. It is a Banach space equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{C})}=\left(\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(\mathbb{C})}^{p}+\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{C})}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Let
\Omega
be an open set in
\mathbb{C}
.
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and
\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)
should be defined similarly. We point out that if the open set
\Omega
supports classical Poincaré inequality

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}

for every
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
where the constant
C
depends only on
\Omega
and
p
then both spaces are known to coincide. For instance, the bounded chord-arc domain is a such domain. The bounded quasidisk also supports Poincaré inequality since it is an extension domain for Sobolev spaces
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{C})
(see
[
22
,
8
]
) so that one can apply Poincaré inequality in a bigger sufficiently regular domain which, combined with the extension theorem, leads to the conclusion.

For
0<s<1
we define the fractional Besov-Sobolev space
B_{p,p}^{s}(\mathbb{C})
as the space of
L^{p}(\mathbb{C})
-functions
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\int_{\mathbb{C}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{|f(z)-f(\zeta)|^{p}}{|z-\zeta|^{2+sp}}dzd\zeta<\infty.

We remark that suppose
s\geq 1
and
\Omega
is a connected open set in
\mathbb{C}
then any measurable function
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
such that the integral of the above integrand
|f(z)-f(\zeta)|^{p}/|z-\zeta|^{2+sp}
over
\Omega\times\Omega
converges is actually constant.
These spaces may be viewed as intermediate spaces between
W^{0,p}(\mathbb{C})
, i.e.,
L^{p}(\mathbb{C})
and
\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{C})
. More precisely, they may be obtained as real-interpolate spaces between these two spaces:
[L^{p}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{C})]_{s,p}=B^{s}_{p,p}(\mathbb{C})
, see
[
40
]
. We call them Besov-Sobolev spaces because if one uses complex interpolation instead we obtain the spaces
H_{p}^{s}(\mathbb{C})
consisting of Bessel potential of
L^{p}
-functions:

[L^{p}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{C})]_{s}=H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{C})
. and
B_{p,p}^{s}(\mathbb{C})=H_{p}^{s}(\mathbb{C})
only if
p=2
(if
p\neq 2
these two spaces are not even isomorphic).

For further use we extend the definition of Besov-Sobolev spaces to
s\in(1,2)
by saying that
f\in B_{p,p}^{s}(\mathbb{C})
with
s\in(1,2)
if
f\in B_{p,p}^{s-1}(\mathbb{C})
and
\nabla f\in B_{p,p}^{s-1}(\mathbb{C})
. Similarly, it can be further extended to other non-integer orders
s>0
. It is known that the space
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
is dense in
B_{p,p}^{s}(\mathbb{C})
for non-integer orders
s>0
and
1<p<\infty
.

Let us now consider a chord-arc sub-domain of the plane. We recall that a chord-arc domain is the image of a disk by a bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism of the plane. The name comes from the fact that a Jordan domain
\Omega
is chord-arc if and only if
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
is a chord-arc curve.

We define
B_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)
simply as the space of restrictions to
\Omega
of functions in
B_{p,p}^{s}(\mathbb{C})
. Such a function is in particular such that

\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|f(z)-f(\zeta)|^{p}}{|z-\zeta|^{2+sp}}dzd\zeta<\infty

and functions satisfying this last property can be extended to a function in
B_{p,p}^{s}(\mathbb{C})
.

4.1.2
Fractional Sobolev Spaces on Chord-arc Curves

If
\Omega
is a (bounded or unbounded) chord-arc domain with boundary
\Gamma
we may define for
0<s<1
,
p>1
,

B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)=\{f\in L^{p}(\Gamma):\,\iint_{\Gamma\times\Gamma}\frac{|f(z)-f(\zeta)|^{p}}{|z-\zeta|^{1+ps}}|dz||d\zeta|<\infty\}.

We define the trace operator
\gamma
on
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
by
\gamma(u)=u|_{\Gamma}
.

Theorem 4.1

(p.182 in
[
24
]
)
.

Let
s\in(1/p,1+1/p)
. The trace operator
\gamma
can be extended to a bounded linear operator from
B_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)
onto
B_{p,p}^{s-1/p}(\Gamma)
, the order having
1/p
-loss, whose kernel is the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
B_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)
.

4.2
Plemelj-Calderón Problem on Chord-arc Domains

We now come back to Plemelj-Calderón problem. By a deep theorem of David
[
11
]
using the Lipschitz result
[
10
]
, the Cauchy integral operator
T
is bounded on
L^{p}(\Gamma)
(i.e.,
B_{p,p}^{0}(\Gamma)
) for every
p\in(1+\infty)
(see section 1.3). Using it we will show the boundedness of the operator
T
on
B_{p,p}^{1}(\Gamma)
.

Theorem 4.2
.

The operator
T
is bounded on the Sobolev space
B_{p,p}^{1}(\Gamma)
, and moreover, the operator norm on
B_{p,p}^{1}(\Gamma)
equals to that on
L^{p}(\Gamma)
.

Before we proceed to the proof of this theorem, one must properly define what we mean by
B^{1}_{p,p}(\Gamma)
when
\Gamma
is a chord-arc curve (or
K
-chord-arc curve for specifying its chord-arc constant).
Without loss of generality we suppose
\text{length}(\Gamma)=2\pi
.
Let
\varphi:\,\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
be the Riemann map from the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
onto the interior domain
\Omega
of
\Gamma
, which can be extended to a homeomorphism of the closures such that
\varphi
restricted on
\mathbb{T}
is a quasisymmetry (see
[
35
]
).
Let
t\mapsto z(t)
denote the arc-length parametrization of
\Gamma
and
\lambda(e^{it})=z(t)
so that
\varphi=\lambda\circ h
where
h
is an absolutely continuous homeomorphism of
\mathbb{T}
with
|h^{\prime}|=|\varphi^{\prime}|
. It is easy to see
\lambda
is a bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism from
\mathbb{T}
onto
\Gamma
. Indeed, for any
t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,2\pi)
,

K^{-1}|e^{it_{1}}-e^{it_{2}}|\leq|\lambda(e^{it_{1}})-\lambda(e^{it_{2}})|\leq\pi/2|e^{it_{1}}-e^{it_{2}}|.

We say
f\in L^{p}(\Gamma)
belongs to
B_{p,p}^{1}(\Gamma)

if
f\circ\lambda
is an anti-derivative of a function
h\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
. We define the function
f^{\prime}

by
f^{\prime}\circ\lambda\lambda^{\prime}=h
. Then
f^{\prime}\in L^{p}(\Gamma)
. The space
B_{p,p}^{1}(\Gamma)
is endowed with the natural norm
\|f\|_{B_{p,p}^{1}(\Gamma)}=\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}
.

To prepare for the proof we introduce the following definition.
Set
\Gamma_{r}=\tau(|z|=r)
to be "circular curves", where
\tau
is a Riemann map that takes
\mathbb{D}
onto
\Omega
.
The Hardy space
E^{p}(\Omega)
of index
p
on the chord-arc domain
\Omega
consists of holomorphic functions
F
on
\Omega
with a finite norm

\|F\|_{p}=\Bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi}\sup_{r}\int_{\Gamma_{r}}|F(w)|^{p}|dw|\Bigg)^{1/p}.

Notice that
\tau
is a homeomorphism of the closures
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
onto
\Omega\cup\Gamma
, and absolutely continuous on
\mathbb{T}
since
\Gamma
is rectifiable.

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

Let
f\in B_{p,p}^{1}(\Gamma)
.
By David’s theorem we may write

f^{\prime}=G_{i}+G_{e}

(4.1)

on
\Gamma
. Here,
G_{i}
and
G_{e}
belong to the Hardy spaces of index
p
on
\Omega_{i}
and
\Omega_{e}
, respectively, and then
G_{i}
and
G_{e}
have non-tangential boundary limits almost everywhere on
\Gamma
with respect to the arc-length measure, denoted still by
G_{i}
and
G_{e}
, so that
\|G_{i}\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}
and
\|G_{e}\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}
are both controlled from above by
\|f\|_{B_{p,p}^{1}(\Gamma)}
.

Recall that
G_{i}\in E^{p}(\Omega_{i})\subset E^{1}(\Omega_{i})
, so that
G_{i}\circ\tau\tau^{\prime}\in E^{1}(\mathbb{D}_{i})
. Let now
\Phi_{i}
be an anti-derivative of
G_{i}
on
\Omega_{i}
. Then we see
(\Phi_{i}\circ\tau)^{\prime}=G_{i}\circ\tau\tau^{\prime}\in E^{1}(\mathbb{D}_{i})
. By a result of Hardy-Littlewood (see e.g. p.89 in
[
16
]
), we conclude that
\Phi_{i}\circ\tau
is continuous on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{i}
and absolutely continuous on
\mathbb{T}
such that

(\Phi_{i}\circ\tau)^{\prime}(\zeta)=\lim_{r\to 1}(\Phi_{i}\circ\tau)^{\prime}(r\zeta)
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}
, and thus
(\Phi_{i}\circ\tau)^{\prime}(\zeta)=G_{i}\circ\tau(\zeta)\tau^{\prime}(\zeta)
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}
. Using it, let us define
\Phi_{i}^{\prime}=G_{i}
on
\Gamma
, and we can similarly define
\Phi_{e}^{\prime}=G_{e}
on
\Gamma
.
Combined with (
4.1
) that leads to, by adjusting the constants,

f=\Phi_{i}+\Phi_{e}

on
\Gamma

with the norm of
\Phi_{i,e}
in
B^{1}_{p,p}(\Gamma)
controlled from above by
\|f\|_{B_{p,p}^{1}(\Gamma)}
. By Plemelj formula we conclude that the operator
T
is bounded on
B_{p,p}^{1}(\Gamma)
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{B_{p,p}^{1}(\Gamma)}
, and moreover, we have
\|T\|_{B_{p,p}^{1}(\Gamma)\to B_{p,p}^{1}(\Gamma)}=\|T\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)\to L^{p}(\Gamma)}
.
∎

Now we get ready to show the main result of this section: the operator
T
is bounded on
B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
for
0<s<1
.
Because of the isomorphism between
B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
and
B_{p,p}^{s}(\mathbb{T})
, this boils down to proving that the operator with kernel

\text{p.v.}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}(\zeta)}{\lambda(\zeta)-\lambda(\xi)}

is bounded on
B_{p,p}^{s}(\mathbb{T})
. Set
g=f\circ\lambda
.
This operator is more precisely defined by

\widetilde{T}g(\xi)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\text{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{g(\zeta)\lambda^{\prime}(\zeta)}{\lambda(\zeta)-\lambda(\xi)}d\zeta.

Let us introduce Calderón’s interpolation theorem.

Theorem 4.3

(p.38 in
[
40
]
, Ch.6 in
[
4
]
and also
[
7
]
)
.

Let
s_{0},s_{1}\in[0,1]
. If
s_{0}\neq s_{1}
then we have, in terms of complex interpolation theory of Banach spaces,

B_{p,p}^{s}(\mathbb{T})=[B_{p,p}^{s_{0}}(\mathbb{T}),B_{p,p}^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{T})]_{\theta}

where
s=(1-\theta)s_{0}+\theta s_{1}
and the exponent
\theta\in(0,1)
.

The following functorial property of complex interpolation is a basic assertion in interpolation theory.
Let
\{A_{0},A_{1}\}
and
\{B_{0},B_{1}\}
be two interpolation couples of Banach spaces and let
L
be a linear operator mapping from
A_{0}+A_{1}
into
B_{0}+B_{1}
such that its restriction to
A_{j}
is a linear and bounded operator from
A_{j}
into
B_{j}
with norm
M_{j}
, where
j=0,1
. Then the restriction of
L
to

[A_{0},A_{1}]_{\theta}
,
0<\theta<1
is a linear and bounded operator from
[A_{0},A_{1}]_{\theta}
into
[B_{0},B_{1}]_{\theta}
with norm
M_{\theta}
. Further, it is known that the complex interpolation method is an exact interpolation functor of exponent
\theta
(see e.g. p.88 in
[
4
]
) meaning that

M_{\theta}\leq M_{0}^{1-\theta}M_{1}^{\theta}.

(4.2)

It now suffices to invoke Calderón’s interpolation theorem to conclude the following.

Theorem 4.4
.

For
0<s<1
and
1<p<\infty
, the operator
T
is bounded on
B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
.

Proof.

By taking
s_{0}=0
and
s_{1}=1
in Theorem
4.3
, we have that

B_{p,p}^{s}(\mathbb{T})=[L^{p}(\mathbb{T}),B_{p,p}^{1}(\mathbb{T})]_{s}.

(4.3)

By David’s theorem and Theorem
4.2
, we conclude that the operator
\widetilde{T}
is bounded on
B_{p,p}^{s}(\mathbb{T})
with the operator norm
\|\widetilde{T}\|_{B_{p,p}^{s}(\mathbb{T})\to B_{p,p}^{s}(\mathbb{T})}=\|\widetilde{T}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}
, and equivalently, the operator
T
is bounded on
B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
with the operator norm
\|T\|_{B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)\to H^{s}(\Gamma)}=\|T\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)\to L^{p}(\Gamma)}
.
∎

It follows from Plemelj formula that every function
f\in B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
may be written uniquely as
f=\Phi_{i}+\Phi_{e}
with
\|\Phi_{i,e}\|_{B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)}\leq C\|f\|_{B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)}
for some constant
C
,
\Phi_{i,e}
being boundary values of holomorphic functions in
\Omega_{i}
and
\Omega_{e}
, respectively. The uniqueness of decomposition for

just follows from the case of

.

4.2.1
On a Theorem of Murai

Clearly, Theorem
4.4
is also valid for chord-arc curves
\Gamma
passing through
\infty
, and in particular for Lipschitz curves
\Gamma
(i.e., there is a Lipschitz function
A:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
whose graph is
\Gamma
).
A theorem by Murai
[
31
]
states that for a Lipschitz curve
\Gamma
with Lipschitz norm
M
,
\|T\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)\to L^{p}(\Gamma)}
is no more than
C(1+M)^{3/2}
(see also
[
12
]
), where
C
is a universal constant. If we plug this information in the preceding theorem we obtain the same bound for
\|T\|_{B_{p,p}^{1-1/p}(\Gamma)\to B_{p,p}^{1-1/p}(\Gamma)}
. But the norm
\|T\|_{B_{p,p}^{1-1/p}(\Gamma)\to B_{p,p}^{1-1/p}(\Gamma)}
depends only on the
L^{p}(\mathbb{C})
-boundedness of the Beurling transform (see section 2), so that it is actually independent of
M
.
In order to get better estimates of
\|T\|_{B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)\to B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)}
we thus use Calderón’s interpolation theorem between
L^{p}(\Gamma)
and
B_{p,p}^{1-1/p}(\Gamma)
for
0<s<1-1/p
and between
B_{p,p}^{1-1/p}(\Gamma)
and
B_{p,p}^{1}(\Gamma)
for
1-1/p<s<1
. We use more precisely that

\displaystyle B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle=[L^{p}(\Gamma),B_{p,p}^{1-1/p}(\Gamma)]_{\frac{ps}{p-1}},\;\;\;\;\;\,0<s<1-1/p;

\displaystyle B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle=[B_{p,p}^{1-1/p}(\Gamma),B_{p,p}^{1}(\Gamma)]_{ps+1-p},\;\;1-1/p<s<1.

Using (
4.2
),
we get to

Theorem 4.5
.

If
\Gamma
is a Lipschitz curve with Lipschitz constant
M
then we have, for
0<s<1
,

\|T\|_{B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)\to B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)}\leq C(1+M)^{\frac{3}{2}|1-\frac{ps}{p-1}|}

where
C
depends only on
p
.

5
Douglas versus Littlewood-Paley: the Chord-arc Case

5.1
The Conjugate Operator

Let
\Gamma
be a chord-arc curve of length
2\pi
, and
1<p<\infty
,
0<s<1
. We have seen two ways of generalizing the fractional Sobolev spaces from
\mathbb{R}
(or from
\mathbb{T}
) to
\Gamma
. The first one, which can be called the Douglas way is

B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)=\bigg\{f\in L^{p}(\Gamma):\iint_{\Gamma\times\Gamma}\frac{|f(z)-f(\zeta)|^{p}}{|z-\zeta|^{1+ps}}|dz||d\zeta|<\infty\bigg\}.

The second one, via Littlewood-Paley theory, is

\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)=\bigg\{f\in L^{p}(\Gamma):\iint_{\Omega_{i,e}}|\nabla u_{i,e}(z)|^{p}d(z,\Gamma)^{(1-s)p-1}dxdy<\infty\bigg\}.

Here,
u_{i,e}
stands for the harmonic extension of
f
to
\Omega_{i,e}
.
We have seen that Plemelj-Calderón problem is solvable in some sense on both spaces
B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
and
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
so that the natural question arises of whether these spaces coincide.

Assume that
\Omega
is bounded and
\mathbb{D}
is the unit disk.
Let
z_{0}
be a point in
\Omega
. If
u
is harmonic in
\Omega
it is well known that there exists a unique harmonic function
\tilde{u}
in
\Omega
such that
\tilde{u}(z_{0})=0
and
u+i\tilde{u}
is holomorphic in
\Omega
. In the case of
\Omega=\mathbb{D}
,
\tilde{u}
is the harmonic (Poisson) extension of
H(f)
where
H
is the Hilbert transform:

H(f)(e^{i\theta})=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|\theta-\varphi|>\epsilon}\cot\Big(\frac{\theta-\varphi}{2}\Big)f(e^{i\varphi})d\varphi.

By Cauchy-Riemann equations,
|\nabla\tilde{u}|=|\nabla u|
, so that
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)
is conjugate-invariant. We denote the subspace of
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)
consisting of holomorphic functions by
\mathcal{HB}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)
, i.e., the set of
u+i\tilde{u}
with
u,\tilde{u}\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)
.

A necessary condition for
B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)=\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
to hold is thus that
B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
is stable by conjugation. Let
\varphi:\,\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
be the Riemann map with
\varphi(0)=z_{0}
. Recall that
t\mapsto z(t)
denotes the arc-length parametrization of
\Gamma
and
\lambda(e^{it})=z(t)
so that
\varphi=\lambda\circ h
where
h
is an absolutely continuous homeomorphism of
\mathbb{T}
with
|h^{\prime}|=|\varphi^{\prime}|
. Recall that
\lambda
is a bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism from
\mathbb{T}
onto
\Gamma
.
A simple computation shows that if
f\in B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
; that is
g=f\circ\lambda\in B_{p,p}^{s}(\mathbb{T})
, and
u
is its harmonic extension to
\Omega
, then
\tilde{u}
is the harmonic extension of
\tilde{f}=\tilde{g}\circ\lambda^{-1}
. Here,

\tilde{g}=V_{h}^{-1}\circ H\circ V_{h}(g),

where
V_{h}(g)=g\circ h
.

It is known (see
[
9
]
, p.247 in
[
16
]
) that
V_{h}^{-1}HV_{h}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
if and only if
|h^{\prime}|=|\varphi^{\prime}|
belongs to
A_{p}
on
\mathbb{T}
. Based on this comment on the limiting case
s=0
, we now state a theorem about the general case
0\leq s\leq 1
.

Theorem 5.1
.

For
p>1,\,0\leq s\leq 1
, suppose
\Gamma
is such that

•

|\varphi^{\prime}|\in A_{p}
if
1<p\leq 2
;

•

|\varphi^{\prime}|\in A_{p^{\prime}}

(1/p^{\prime}+1/p=1)
if
p>2
.

Then,
B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
is stable by conjugation, and moreover, the conjugate operator
f\mapsto\tilde{f}
on
B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
is bounded.

Before going to the proof, let us comment on the hypothesis of Theorem
5.1
. If
\omega
is an
A_{\infty}
weight let us define
\delta(\omega)=\inf\{q>1:\,\omega\in A_{q}\}
. It is known
[
9
]
that if
\omega\in A_{q}
for some
q>1
then
\delta(\omega)<q
(see p.254 in
[
16
]
). Let us then define
\delta=\delta(|\varphi^{\prime}|)
. Noting that the hypothesis imply that
|\varphi^{\prime}|\in A_{2}
, we see that the hypothesis of the theorem are equivalent to

\delta<q<\frac{\delta}{\delta-1}.

On the other hand, it is known (see
[
21
]
) that
\Gamma
being chord-arc implies
|\varphi^{\prime}|
having

A_{\infty}
but there are examples of chord-arc curves such that
|\varphi^{\prime}|\notin A_{2}
(see
[
23
]
), we thus know the chord-arc condition is not sufficient for Theorem
5.1
to hold.

Proof.

We have just seen that
V_{h}^{-1}HV_{h}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
if and only if
|\varphi^{\prime}|\in A_{p}
for any
1<p<\infty
.
Let now
f\in B_{p,p}^{1}(\Gamma)
so that
g=f\circ\lambda\in B_{p,p}^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, the set of
g\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
such that
g^{\prime}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
.
We have
(g\circ h)^{\prime}=g^{\prime}\circ hh^{\prime}\in L^{p}(|h^{\prime}|^{1-p})
. Recall that a weight
\omega\in A_{p^{\prime}}
if and only if
\omega^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}-1}}\in A_{p}
. By that,
|h^{\prime}|^{1-p}\in A_{p}
is equivalent to
|h^{\prime}|=|\varphi^{\prime}|\in A_{p^{\prime}}
. Then, we have
H((g\circ h)^{\prime})\in L^{p}(|h^{\prime}|^{1-p})
with a norm bounded by the
L^{p}(|h^{\prime}|^{1-p})
-norm of
(g\circ h)^{\prime}

(see
[
9
]
); that implies
(\tilde{g})^{\prime}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
with
\tilde{g}=V_{h}^{-1}HV_{h}(g)
, and thus
\tilde{f}=\tilde{g}\circ\lambda^{-1}\in B_{p,p}^{1}(\Gamma)
such that
\|\tilde{f}\|_{B_{p,p}^{1}(\Gamma)}
bounded by
\|f\|_{B_{p,p}^{1}(\Gamma)}
. Since the weight
A_{p}
increases with
p
and
from the following complex interpolation theorem (see Theorem
4.3
):

[L^{p}(\mathbb{T}),B_{p,p}^{1}(\mathbb{T})]_{s}=B^{s}_{p,p}(\mathbb{T})

(5.1)

the theorem now follows.
∎

Specially, a natural condition on the curve
\Gamma
implying that
|\varphi^{\prime}|\in A_{2}
is the radial-Lipschitz condition. Indeed, if
\Gamma
is a radial-Lipschitz curve it can be shown that
|\varphi^{\prime}|
satisfies the Helson-Szegö condition (see section 5.3 for a specific proof):
\log|\varphi^{\prime}|=u+Hv
with
u\in L^{\infty},\;v\in L^{\infty}
and
\|v\|_{\infty}<\pi/2
, which is equivalent to
|\varphi^{\prime}|\in A_{2}
as follows from the Helson-Szegö theorem (
[
16
]
). We may now state:

Corollary 5.2
.

For
0\leq s\leq 1
, if
\Gamma
is such that
|\varphi^{\prime}|\in A_{2}
then
B_{2,2}^{s}(\Gamma)
is stable by conjugation, and moreover, the conjugate operator
f\mapsto\tilde{f}
on
B_{2,2}^{s}(\Gamma)
is bounded. The conclusion holds in particular for radial-Lipschitz curves.

For
0\leq s\leq 1
,
1<p<\infty
, we denote the subspace of
B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
consisting of functions
f+i\tilde{f}
such that
f,\;\tilde{f}\in B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
by
\text{HB}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
.

5.2
The Operator
V_{s,p}
on
\mathcal{B}^{s}_{p,p}(\Omega)

Recall that
\mathcal{B}^{s}_{p,p}(\Omega)
,
0\leq s<1
and
1<p<\infty
, is the set of harmonic functions
u
on the domain
\Omega
bounded by the Jordan curve
\Gamma
such that its norm

\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}_{p,p}(\Omega)}<\infty
. Suppose
\Gamma
is chord-arc which in particular is rectifiable. It is known that the non-tangential boundary values
f
of
u
exist almost everywhere on
\Gamma
with respect to the arc-length measure and
u
can be recovered from
f
using the harmonic extension. The space of boundary functions of
\mathcal{B}^{s}_{p,p}(\Omega)
is denoted by
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega\!\to\!\Gamma)
so that
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)=\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i}\!\to\!\Gamma)\cap\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{e}\!\to\!\Gamma)
.
We may identify
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)
with
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega\!\to\!\Gamma)
for convenience, and will switch between the two freely. Similarly, for
0\leq s\leq 1
and
1<p<\infty
we also identify
B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
with the set
B_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)
of its harmonic extensions in
\Omega
.

Let
\Omega,\,\Omega^{\prime}
be two Jordan domains containing
0
and
\varphi
a holomorphic diffeomorphism from
\Omega
onto
\Omega^{\prime}
fixing
0
. Let
f\in\mathcal{HB}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega^{\prime})
. Using the change of variable
\zeta=\varphi(z)
we have

\iint_{\Omega^{\prime}}d(\zeta,\Gamma^{\prime})^{(1-s)p-1}|f^{\prime}(\zeta)|^{p}d\xi d\eta=\iint_{\Omega}d(\varphi(z),\Gamma^{\prime})^{(1-s)p-1}|(f\circ\varphi)^{\prime}(z)|^{p}|\varphi^{\prime}(z)|^{2-p}dxdy.

By the Koebe distortion theorem (
2.1
) there exists a universal constant
C>1
such that

\displaystyle C^{-|(1-s)p-1|}\iint_{\Omega}d(z,\Gamma)^{(1-s)p-1}|V_{s,p}(f)^{\prime}(z)|^{p}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega^{\prime}}d(\zeta,\Gamma^{\prime})^{(1-s)p-1}|f^{\prime}(\zeta)|^{p}d\xi d\eta

(5.2)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{|(1-s)p-1|}\iint_{\Omega}d(z,\Gamma)^{(1-s)p-1}|V_{s,p}(f)^{\prime}(z)|^{p}dxdy,

where
V_{s,p}
is the operator defined by

V_{s,p}(f)(z)=\int_{0}^{z}(f\circ\varphi)^{\prime}(u)\varphi^{\prime}(u)^{1/p-s}du.

In other words, the operator
V_{s,p}
is a bounded isomorphism between
\mathcal{HB}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)

and
\mathcal{HB}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega^{\prime})
with the operator norm

\|V_{s,p}\|,\,\|V_{s,p}^{-1}\|\leq C^{|(1-s)-1/p|}.

Notice that for
s=1-1/p
, this operator is nothing but the composition by
\varphi
and that, in this case,
V_{1-1/p,p}
is an isometry.

For convenience of later use, when
p=2
, the operator
V_{s,p}
will be simplified to be
V_{s}
.

5.2.1
The Operator
V_{s}
on
\mathcal{B}^{s}_{2,2}(\Omega)

In order to understand better the operator
V_{s}
let us rewrite it by using an integration by parts:

\displaystyle V_{s}(f)(z)

\displaystyle=f\circ\varphi(z)\varphi^{\prime}(z)^{1/2-s}-(1/2-s)\int_{0}^{z}f\circ\varphi(u)\varphi^{\prime}(u)^{1/2-s}\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}(u)}{\varphi^{\prime}(u)}du

\displaystyle=T_{s}f(z)-(1/2-s)S(T_{s}(f))(z),

where

T_{s}f(z)=f\circ\varphi(z)\varphi^{\prime}(z)^{1/2-s}

and

Sg(z)=\int_{0}^{z}g(u)\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}(u)}{\varphi^{\prime}(u)}du.

Let us now specialize to
s=0
and
(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})=(\mathbb{D},\Omega)
. Recall that the Hardy space
E^{2}(\Omega)
is the space of holomorphic functions
f:\,\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
such that
T_{0}(f)\in E^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, the classical Hardy space of the unit disk. We say that a function
g\in{\rm BMOA}(\mathbb{D})
if
g\in E^{2}(\mathbb{D})
and if in addition the boundary values of
g
on
\mathbb{T}
is of bounded mean oscillation (abbr. BMO) in the sense that

\sup_{I\subset\mathbb{T}}\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}|g(z)-g_{I}||dz|<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all sub-arcs
I
of
\mathbb{T}
and
g_{I}
denotes the integral mean of
g
over
I
. It is known that
\log\varphi^{\prime}\in{\rm BMOA}(\mathbb{D})
if
\varphi(\mathbb{D})
is chord-arc, but not vice versa. By Fefferman-Stein,
\log\varphi^{\prime}\in{\rm BMOA}(\mathbb{D})
if and only if

d\mu=\bigg|\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}(z)}{\varphi^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|^{2}(1-|z|)dxdy

is a Carleson measure in
\mathbb{D}
(see e.g. Ch.VI in
[
16
]
).

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a Jordan domain containing
0
and
\varphi
the Riemann mapping from
\mathbb{D}
onto
\Omega
fixing
0
. The following statements hold:

(1)

If
\log\varphi^{\prime}\in{\rm BMOA}(\mathbb{D})
then
E^{2}(\Omega)\subset\mathcal{HB}_{2,2}^{0}(\Omega)
;

(2)

If
\Omega
is a chord-arc domain then
\mathcal{HB}_{2,2}^{0}(\Omega)\subset E^{2}(\Omega)
.

Moreover, the inclusions are continuous with respect to the norms
\|\cdot\|_{E^{2}(\Omega)}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{0}(\Omega)}
.

Proof.

Suppose
\log\varphi^{\prime}\in{\rm BMOA}(\mathbb{D})
; that is,
\mu
is a Carleson measure in
\mathbb{D}
.
Let
f\in E^{2}(\Omega)
so that
T_{0}f\in E^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Then by Carleson (see e.g. Theorem 3.9 in
[
16
]
)

\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|T_{0}(f)|^{2}d\mu\leq C\|T_{0}f\|_{E^{2}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}

where
C
is a constant depending only on the Carleson norm of
\mu
.
Since
E^{2}(\mathbb{D})=\mathcal{HB}_{2,2}^{0}(\mathbb{D})
we have

\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|(T_{0}f)^{\prime}|^{2}(1-|z|)dxdy<\infty.

Finally,

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|(V_{0}f)^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|)dxdy

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathbb{D}}\Big|(T_{0}f)^{\prime}(z)-\frac{1}{2}T_{0}f(z)\cdot\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}(z)}{\varphi^{\prime}(z)}\Big|^{2}(1-|z|)dxdy

\displaystyle\leq 2\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|(T_{0}f)^{\prime}|^{2}(1-|z|)dxdy+\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|T_{0}(f)|^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle<\infty.

Combined with (
5.2
),
this completes the proof of statement
(1)
.

For the proof of statement
(2)
we will need the definition of the (Littlewood-Paley)
\mathsf{g}
-function of a holomorphic function
f
in
\mathbb{D}
:

\mathsf{g}(f)(e^{i\theta})=\left(\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)|f^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})|^{2}dr\right)^{1/2}.

Suppose now that
f\in\mathcal{HB}_{2,2}^{0}(\Omega)
. Then
V_{0}(f)\in\mathcal{HB}_{2,2}^{0}(\mathbb{D})
, which in turn implies that

\iint_{\mathbb{D}}(1-|u|)|(f\circ\varphi)^{\prime}(u)|^{2}|\varphi^{\prime}(u)|dudv<\infty,

or, in other words, that
\mathsf{g}(f\circ\varphi)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T},|\varphi^{\prime}|d\theta)
. Since
\Gamma
is assumed to be chord-arc, we have that
|\varphi^{\prime}|
has the weight
A_{\infty}
on
\mathbb{T}
.
We can then apply a theorem of Gundy-Wheeden
[
19
]
(see also
[
21
]
) which implies that
\mathsf{g}(f\circ\varphi)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T},|\varphi^{\prime}|d\theta)
if and only if the non-tangential maximal function
\mathsf{n}(f\circ\varphi)
of
f\circ\varphi
is in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T},|\varphi^{\prime}|d\theta)
. On the other hand, for any "circular curves"
\Gamma_{r}
it holds that (see p.233 in
[
21
]
)

\int_{\Gamma_{r}}|f(\zeta)|^{2}d\sigma(\zeta)\leq C\|\mathsf{n}(f\circ\varphi)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T},|\varphi^{\prime}|d\theta)},

which implies
f\in E^{2}(\Omega)
.
∎

Notice that for this theorem we do not need
|\varphi^{\prime}|
having
A_{2}
on
\mathbb{T}
.
This condition is nevertheless necessary for the following corollary to hold by Corollary
5.2
since if it is not attached then the (real) space of real parts of
E^{2}(\Omega\!\to\!\Gamma)
-functions is a proper subspace of the real space
L_{\mathbb{R}}^{2}(\Gamma,d\sigma)

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a chord-arc domain bounded by
\Gamma
. If
|\varphi^{\prime}|\in A_{2}
then
B_{2,2}^{0}(\Gamma)=\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{0}(\Omega\!\to\!\Gamma)
.

5.3
The Equality
\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{s}(\Omega\!\to\!\Gamma)=B_{2,2}^{s}(\Gamma)

The aim of this part is to prove the following theorem that is
one of the main theorems of this paper.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded chord-arc domain bounded by
\Gamma
with
0\in\Omega
,
and
\varphi
its Riemann mapping fixing
0
. If
\Gamma
is such that

\log|\varphi^{\prime}|=b_{1}+Hb_{2},\qquad b_{1},\;Hb_{1},\;b_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})\;\text{and}\;\|b_{2}\|_{\infty}<\frac{\pi}{2},

(5.3)

then, for
0\leq s\leq 1
,
\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{s}(\Omega\!\to\!\Gamma)=B_{2,2}^{s}(\Gamma)
with comparable norms.
The conclusions hold in particular for radial-Lipschitz domains.

Before going further, let us proceed from understanding why the conditions (
5.3
) are assumed. The Helson-Szegö theorem (see Ch.IV in
[
16
]
) says that
\log|\varphi^{\prime}|=b_{1}+Hb_{2}
with
b_{1},b_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
and
\|b_{2}\|_{\infty}<\pi/2
if and only if
|\varphi^{\prime}|\in A_{2}
on
\mathbb{T}
, which is only used to guarantee that
B_{2,2}^{s}(\Gamma)
is stable by conjugation for all
0\leq s\leq 1
(see Corollary
5.2
). For the proof of the corresponding analytic case:
\mathcal{HB}_{2,2}^{s}(\Omega\!\to\!\Gamma)=\text{HB}_{2,2}^{s}(\Gamma)
, this condition can thus be ignored. Note that
\text{Arg}\varphi^{\prime}=H\log|\varphi^{\prime}|=Hb_{1}-b_{2}
. The extra assumption that
Hb_{1}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
is used to derive that
\text{Arg}\varphi^{\prime}
is bounded, geometrically which means that the boundary curve cannot spiral too much. For instance, for the double spiral curve,
\text{Arg}\varphi^{\prime}
keeps increasing and goes to infinity.

Let
r(\theta):\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{(}0,+\infty)
be a
2\pi
-periodic continuous function. Then the curve
\Gamma=\{r(\theta)e^{i\theta}:\;\theta\in[0,2\pi)\}
is called a starlike Jordan curve with respect to
0
and the domain
\Omega
bounded by
\Gamma
is called a starlike domain. Recall that if moreover
r(\theta)
is a Lipschitz function, i.e., there exists a positive constant
M
such that
\|r^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq M
then the curve
\Gamma
is called a radial-Lipschitz curve and
\Omega
is called a radial-Lipschitz domain. Suppose
\varphi
is a Riemann mapping from
\mathbb{D}
onto
\Omega
fixing
0
. It is known that
\Omega
is starlike with respect to
0
if and only if

\varphi^{\prime}(0)\neq 0\;\text{and}\;\text{Arg}\frac{z\varphi^{\prime}(z)}{\varphi(z)}\in\left(-\frac{\pi}{2}\alpha,\frac{\pi}{2}\alpha\right),

(5.4)

where
\alpha=1
. The condition
\varphi^{\prime}(0)\neq 0
implies that
\log(\varphi(z)/z)
is continuous on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
so that
\log|\varphi(z)/z|\in L^{\infty}
, and
\text{Arg}(\varphi(z)/z)\in L^{\infty}
. The condition (
5.4
), combined with this, shows that
\text{Arg}\varphi^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}
. A simple computation shows that
\Omega
is a radial-Lipschitz domain if and only if the condition (
5.4
) holds where the constant
\alpha<1
depends on
M
, from which it follows that
\Omega
is a chord-arc domain (see p.172 in
[
35
]
). Notice that

\displaystyle\log|\varphi^{\prime}(z)|=\log\left|\frac{\varphi(z)}{z}\right|+\log\left|\frac{z\varphi^{\prime}(z)}{\varphi(z)}\right|=\log\left|\frac{\varphi(z)}{z}\right|-H\left(\text{Arg}\frac{z\varphi^{\prime}(z)}{\varphi(z)}\right),

and

H\left(\log\left|\frac{\varphi(z)}{z}\right|\right)=\text{Arg}\frac{\varphi(z)}{z}.

We have that a radial-Lipschitz curve
\Gamma
satisfies the condition (
5.3
). Consequently, the radial-Lipschitz domain has all properties of Theorem
5.5
.

The following abstract interpolation Theorem of Voigt
[
43
]
is the main tool in our proof of Theorem
5.5
:

Theorem 5.6
.

Assume that
(X_{0},X_{1})
,
(Y_{0},Y_{1})
are two interpolation pairs of Banach spaces, and assume that
\check{X}
is a dense subspace of
(X_{0}\cap X_{1},\|\cdot\|_{X_{0}\cap X_{1}})
. Denote the strip
S:=\{z=s+it\in\mathbb{C}:\;0\leq s\leq 1\}
and the interpolation spaces
X_{s}:=[X_{0},X_{1}]_{s}
,
Y_{s}:=[Y_{0},Y_{1}]_{s}
.
Let
(T_{z};\;z\in S)
be a family of linear mappings
T_{z}:\check{X}\to Y_{0}+Y_{1}
with the following properties:

(i)

For all
x\in\check{X}
the function
T_{(\cdot)}x:S\to Y_{0}+Y_{1}
is continuous, bounded on
S
, and analytic in
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{S}}
;

(ii)

for
j=0,1
,
x\in\check{X}
, the function
\mathbb{R}\ni t\mapsto T_{j+it}x\in Y_{j}
is continuous, and

M_{j}:=\sup\{\|T_{j+it}x\|_{Y_{j}};t\in\mathbb{R},x\in\check{X},\|x\|_{X_{j}}\leq 1\}<\infty.

Then, for all
s\in[0,1]
,
T_{s}(\check{X})\subset Y_{s}
,

\|T_{s}x\|_{Y_{s}}\leq M_{0}^{1-s}M_{1}^{s}\|x\|_{X_{s}}\quad for\;all\;x\in\check{X}.

We now start the proof of Theorem
5.5
. Clearly, we only need to show
\mathcal{HB}_{2,2}^{s}(\Omega\!\to\!\Gamma)=\text{HB}_{2,2}^{s}(\Gamma)
with comparable norms.
Recall that
Bergman spaces with standard weights
A^{p}_{\alpha}
are defined as the sets of holomorphic functions
g
on
\mathbb{D}
such that
g\in L^{p}(\mathbb{D},(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}dxdy)
, i.e.,

\|g\|_{A^{p}_{\alpha}}^{p}=\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|g(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}dxdy<\infty

where
p>0
and
\alpha>-1
. Here, the assumption that
\alpha>-1
is essential because the space
L^{p}(\mathbb{D},(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}dxdy)
does not contain any holomorphic function other than
0
when
\alpha\leq-1
. The definition of
A^{p}_{\alpha}
is extended to the case where
\alpha
is any real number; that is consistent with the traditional definition when
\alpha>-1
(see Theorem 13 in
[
47
]
). From the definition it can be seen that if
\alpha<\beta
then the strict inclusion
A^{p}_{\alpha}\subset A^{p}_{\beta}
holds.

For our purpose we in particular mention that the space
A^{2}_{-1}
is the classical Hardy space
E^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, i.e., that is the set of holomorphic functions
g
in
\mathbb{D}
such that

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})dxdy<\infty.

We also need to use the case of
p=2
and
\alpha=1-2s
where
0\leq s<1
, namely,

A^{2}_{1-2s}=\{g\;\text{analytic in}\;\mathbb{D}:\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|g(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{1-2s}dxdy<\infty\}.

This is a closed linear subspace of the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{D},(1-|z|^{2})^{1-2s}dxdy)
. For these spaces, the following interpolation theorem (see Theorem 36 in
[
47
]
) holds true:

[A^{2}_{1},A^{2}_{-1}]_{s}=A^{2}_{1-2s},\qquad 0<s<1

(5.5)

with equivalent norms.

Suppose
\varphi
is a conformal map from
\mathbb{D}
onto
\Omega
fixing
0
. For any
f\in\mathcal{HB}_{2,2}^{s}(\Omega)
,
0\leq s<1
, we have seen from (
5.2
) that

\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|V_{s}(f)^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|)^{1-2s}dxdy\simeq\iint_{\Omega}|f^{\prime}(\zeta)|^{2}d(\zeta,\Gamma)^{1-2s}d\xi d\eta

where the implicit constants depend only on
s
, and

V_{s}(f)^{\prime}(z)=(f\circ\varphi)^{\prime}(z)\varphi^{\prime}(z)^{1/2-s}.

From it we see that

f\in\mathcal{HB}_{2,2}^{s}(\Omega)\;\Leftrightarrow\;V_{s}(f)^{\prime}\in A^{2}_{1-2s}

(5.6)

with comparable norms.
Note that any
g\in A^{2}_{1-2s}
can be written in the form
V_{s}(f)^{\prime}
by picking
f(z)=\int_{0}^{z}g\circ\varphi^{-1}(u)(\varphi^{-1})^{\prime}(u)^{3/2-s}du
.

Motivated by equivalence (
5.6
), we define
\mathcal{HB}_{2,2}^{1}(\Omega)
as the set of analytic functions
f
on
\Omega
satisfying
V_{1}(f)^{\prime}\in A^{2}_{-1}
. It is easy to see that
f
is an anti-derivative of a function in
E^{2}(\Omega)
. To be more precise, recall that
A^{2}_{-1}=E^{2}(\mathbb{D})
we have

\int_{\Gamma}|f^{\prime}(\zeta)|^{2}|d\zeta|=\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f^{\prime}\circ\varphi(z)\varphi^{\prime}(z)^{1/2}|^{2}|dz|=\int_{\mathbb{T}}|V_{1}(f)^{\prime}(z)|^{2}|dz|<\infty.

By this definition,
\mathcal{HB}_{2,2}^{1}(\Omega\!\to\!\Gamma)=\text{HB}_{2,2}^{1}(\Gamma)
, and we also have
\mathcal{HB}_{2,2}^{0}(\Omega\!\to\!\Gamma)=\text{HB}_{2,2}^{0}(\Gamma)
with equivalent norms by Theorem
5.3
. Combining this with (
5.6
), we can see that this boils down to prove

f\in\text{HB}_{2,2}^{s}(\Omega)\;\Leftrightarrow\;V_{s}(f)^{\prime}\in A^{2}_{1-2s}

(5.7)

with comparable norms for
0<s<1
. For this purpose, we will use Theorem
5.6
in the following.

Recall that
(A^{2}_{1},A^{2}_{-1})
is an interpolation pair and
[A^{2}_{1},A^{2}_{-1}]_{s}=A^{2}_{1-2s},\;0<s<1
. Notice that the boundary curve
\Gamma
of
\Omega
is a chord-arc curve which is the image of
\mathbb{T}
under the bi-Lipschitz mapping
\lambda
, we then conclude by (
4.3
) that
(\text{HB}_{2,2}^{0}(\Gamma),\text{HB}_{2,2}^{1}(\Gamma))
is an interpolation pair and
[\text{HB}_{2,2}^{0}(\Gamma),\text{HB}_{2,2}^{1}(\Gamma)]_{s}=\text{HB}_{2,2}^{s}(\Gamma)
.
Note that
\Omega
is a chord-arc domain, in particular a Smirnov domain, that is equivalent to that the Hardy space
E^{2}(\Omega)
(i.e.,
\text{HB}_{2,2}^{0}(\Omega)
) coincides with the
L^{2}(\Gamma)
closure of the set
\check{B}
of polynomials on
\Gamma
(see Theorem 10.6 in
[
15
]
). Since
\text{HB}_{2,2}^{1}(\Gamma)
is the set of functions
f\in E^{2}(\Omega)
such that
f^{\prime}\in E^{2}(\Omega)
, the set
\check{B}
is also a dense subspace of
\text{HB}_{2,2}^{1}(\Gamma)
. By the inclusion relation
\text{HB}_{2,2}^{1}(\Gamma)\subset\text{HB}_{2,2}^{0}(\Gamma)
, we have that
\text{HB}_{2,2}^{0}(\Gamma)\cap\text{HB}_{2,2}^{1}(\Gamma)=\text{HB}_{2,2}^{1}(\Gamma)
.

Define a family of linear mappings
(T_{z};\;z\in S)
on
\check{B}
by

T_{z}(f):=e^{cz^{2}}V_{z}(f)=e^{cz^{2}}f^{\prime}\circ\varphi(\varphi^{\prime})^{\frac{3}{2}-z}.

Here,
c
is a constant which will be determined later. Now we will prove that for each
z\in S
, the linear mapping
T_{z}
sends
\check{B}
to
A^{2}_{1}
, that is also the sum space
A^{2}_{1}+A^{2}_{-1}
, and satisfies the properties (i) and (ii) of Theorem
5.6
.

First, we prove that for each
z=s+it\in S
, each polynomial
f\in\check{B}
, the integral

\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|T_{z}(f)(\zeta)|^{2}(1-|\zeta|)d\xi d\eta

(5.8)

converges.
From simple computation it follows that
|e^{cz^{2}}|^{2}=e^{2c(s^{2}-t^{2})}
,
|(\varphi^{\prime})^{\frac{3}{2}-z}|^{2}=e^{2t\text{Arg}\varphi^{\prime}}|\varphi^{\prime}|^{3-2s}
and
|f^{\prime}\circ\varphi|^{2}<C_{1}
since the diameter of
\Omega
is finite. Here, the constant
C_{1}
depends on
f
and
\Omega
.
Then,

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|T_{z}(f)(\zeta)|^{2}(1-|\zeta|)d\xi d\eta\leq C_{1}e^{2c}\left(e^{-ct^{2}}e^{t\|\text{Arg}\varphi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}}\right)^{2}\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2(1-s)}|\varphi^{\prime}(\zeta)|(1-|\zeta|)d\xi d\eta

Set
A:=\|\text{Arg}\varphi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}
and pick
c\geq A^{2}/4
we then have that
e^{-ct^{2}}e^{tA}\leq e
. By the Koebe distortion theorem (
2.1
),
|\varphi^{\prime}(\zeta)|(1-|\zeta|)
is comparable to
d(\varphi(\zeta),\;\Gamma)
, that is bounded due to the boundedness of the diameter of
\Omega
. By Hölder inequality,

\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2(1-s)}d\xi d\eta\leq\pi^{s}\left(\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}d\xi d\eta\right)^{1-s}<\infty.

We can then conclude that the integral (
5.8
) converges, and moreover it has an upper-bound depending only on
\Omega
and given
f
.
That implies that the linear mapping
T_{z}
sends
\check{B}
to
A^{2}_{1}
, and for any
f\in\check{B}
the function
T_{(\cdot)}f:\;S\to A^{2}_{1}
is bounded. In particular,

\begin{split}\|T_{it}f\|_{A^{2}_{1}}^{2}&=\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|T_{it}(f)(\zeta)|^{2}(1-|\zeta|^{2})d\xi d\eta\\
&\leq e^{2c+3}\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}\circ\varphi|^{2}|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}|\varphi^{\prime}|(1-|\zeta|)d\xi d\eta\\
&\leq 4e^{2c+3}\iint_{\Omega}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}d(z,\Gamma)dxdy,\end{split}

that is comparable to
\|f\|^{2}_{B_{2,2}^{0}(\Omega)}
by Theorem
5.3
. Thus,

M_{0}:=\sup\{\|T_{it}f\|_{A^{2}_{1}};\;t\in\mathbb{R},f\in\check{B},\|f\|_{B_{2,2}^{0}(\Omega)}\leq 1\}<\infty.

Next, for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, any
f\in\check{B}
with
\|f\|_{B_{2,2}^{1}(\Omega)}\leq 1

the norm
\|T_{1+it}(f)\|_{A^{2}_{-1}}
, by Theorem
5.3
, is comparable to
\int_{\mathbb{T}}|T_{1+it}(f)(\zeta)|^{2}|d\zeta|
. Noting

|T_{1+it}(f)(\zeta)|^{2}=|f^{\prime}\circ\varphi(\zeta)|^{2}|\varphi^{\prime}(\zeta)|e^{2c}\left(e^{-ct^{2}}e^{t\text{Arg}\varphi^{\prime}(\zeta)}\right)^{2},\qquad\zeta\in\mathbb{T},

we then have

\int_{\mathbb{T}}|T_{1+it}(f)(\zeta)|^{2}|d\zeta|\leq e^{2(1+c)}\int_{\Gamma}|f^{\prime}(\zeta)|^{2}|d\zeta|\leq e^{2(1+c)}

Thus,

M_{1}:=\sup\{\|T_{1+it}f\|_{A^{2}_{-1}};\;t\in\mathbb{R},f\in\check{B},\|f\|_{B_{2,2}^{1}(\Omega)}\leq 1\}<\infty.

By the continuity of parametrized integral, all functions
S\ni z\mapsto T_{z}f\in A^{2}_{1}
,
\mathbb{R}\ni t\mapsto T_{it}f\in A^{2}_{1}
, and
\mathbb{R}\ni t\mapsto T_{1+it}f\in A^{2}_{-1}
are continuous.

Finally, to prove that for any
f\in\check{B}
,
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{S}}\ni z\mapsto T_{z}f\in A^{2}_{1}
is a holomorphic map, we use a general result about the infinite dimensional holomorphy (see p.206 in
[
25
]
). It says that suppose
X
and
Y
are Banach spaces over the complex numbers and
U
is a domain of
X
then a continuous function
f:U\to Y
is holomorphic if there exists a total subset
A
of the dual
Y^{*}
such that for every
\alpha\in A
the function
\alpha\circ f:U\to\mathbb{C}
is holomorphic. Here, a subset
A
of
Y^{*}
is total if
\alpha(y)=0
for all
\alpha\in A
implies that
y=0
.

It is known that suppose
\alpha>-1
and
0<p<\infty
the inequality

(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{\frac{2+\alpha}{p}}|g(\zeta)|\leq C\|g\|_{A^{p}_{\alpha}}

(5.9)

holds for some constant
C
depending only on
\alpha
and any
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
(see Theorem 2.1 in
[
46
]
, p.73 in
[
47
]
).
For each
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
, define
l_{\zeta}(g)=g(\zeta)
for
g\in A^{2}_{1}
.
By taking
p=2,\alpha=1
in (
5.9
), we see that
\|l_{\zeta}\|\leq C(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{-3/2}
, which implies that
l_{\zeta}\in(A^{2}_{1})^{*}
. Set
A:=\{l_{\zeta};\;\zeta\in\mathbb{D}\}
. Clearly,
A
is a total subset of
(A^{2}_{1})^{*}
. Now for each
l_{\zeta}\in A
, one can see that the function

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{S}}\ni z\mapsto l_{\zeta}\circ T_{z}(f)=e^{cz^{2}}f^{\prime}\circ\varphi(\zeta)(\varphi^{\prime})^{3/2-z}(\zeta)\in\mathbb{C}

is holomorphic.

We can then invoke Theorem
5.6
, and see that for all
s\in(0,1)
,
T_{s}(\check{B})\subset A^{2}_{1-2s}
, and moreover,

\|T_{s}(f)\|_{A^{2}_{1-2s}}\leq M_{0}^{1-s}M_{1}^{s}\|f\|_{B_{2,2}^{s}(\Omega)},\qquad\text{for\; all}\;f\in\check{B}.

(5.10)

Since
\check{B}
is dense in
\text{HB}_{2,2}^{0}(\Gamma)\cap\text{HB}_{2,2}^{1}(\Gamma)
and
\text{HB}_{2,2}^{0}(\Gamma)\cap\text{HB}_{2,2}^{1}(\Gamma)
is dense in
\text{HB}_{2,2}^{s}(\Gamma)
for all
s\in[0,1]
(see
[
43
]
), it follows immediately that
\check{B}
is dense in
\text{HB}_{2,2}^{s}(\Gamma)
. Thus, (
5.10
) holds true for all
f\in\text{HB}_{2,2}^{s}(\Gamma)
. The bounded inverse theorem implies the inverse of the operator
T_{s}
is also bounded. This completes the proof of Theorem
5.5
.

5.4
The Equality
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega\!\to\!\Gamma)=B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)

The aim of this part is to prove the following

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded chord-arc domain bounded by
\Gamma
with
0\in\Omega
and
\varphi
its Riemann mapping fixing
0
. Let
0<s<1
,
1<p<\infty
.
If
\Gamma
is such that
\text{Arg}\,\varphi^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}
and
either

•

when
p\geq 2
,
|\varphi^{\prime}|\in A_{p^{\prime}}

(1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1)
;

•

when
p<2
,
|\varphi^{\prime}|\in A_{p}

then it holds that
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega\!\to\!\Gamma)=B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
with comparable norms.

We recall that if
\delta
stands for the infinimum of the set of
q
such that
|\varphi^{\prime}|\in A_{q}
then the conditions in the theorem are equivalent to

\delta<q<\frac{\delta}{\delta-1}.

In particular,
\delta<2
so that
|\varphi^{\prime}|\in A_{2}
. That implies that
B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
is stable by conjugation by Theorem
5.1
. Together with
\text{Arg}\,\varphi^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}
, that also implies that
\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{s}(\Omega\!\to\!\Gamma)=B_{2,2}^{s}(\Gamma)
,
0\leq s\leq 1
, with comparable norms by Theorem
5.5
. These two results will be used in the proof of Theorem
5.7
.

On the other hand, for the proof of Theorem
5.7
we also need to use the claim:
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega\!\to\!\Gamma)=B_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Gamma)
with comparable norms for
1<p<\infty
provided that the curve
\Gamma
is chord-arc. That was proved in our recent paper
[
45
]
using the conformal invariance of the space
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega)
. We outline its proof here: suppose
f\in B_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Gamma)
and its harmonic extension in
\Omega
is still denoted by
f
then the assertion follows from the following reasoning:

\|f\|_{B_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Gamma)}\simeq\|f\circ\lambda\|_{B_{p,p}^{1/p}(\mathbb{T})}\simeq\|f\circ\varphi\|_{B_{p,p}^{1/p}(\mathbb{T})}\simeq\|f\circ\varphi\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\mathbb{D})}=\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega)},

where the implicit constants depend only on
\Omega
and
p
.
The above steps in order are due to
(1)

\lambda
is a bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism of
\mathbb{T}
onto
\Gamma
;
(2)

\lambda^{-1}\circ\varphi
is a quasisymmetry;
(3)
when
\Gamma=\mathbb{T}
this is well-known as we mentioned before;
(4)
the space
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega)
is conformal invariant.

Recall that
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)
is conjugate invariant. We have seen, under the assumption of Theorem
5.7
, that
B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
is also conjugate invariant, in order to prove Theorem
5.7
we thus only need to prove the holomorphic case:

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded chord-arc domain bounded by
\Gamma
with
0\in\Omega
and
\varphi
its Riemann mapping fixing
0
. Let
0<s<1
,
1<p<\infty
.
If
\Gamma
is such that
\text{Arg}\,\varphi^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}

then it holds that

\mathcal{HB}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega\!\to\!\Gamma)=\text{HB}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)

(5.11)

with comparable norms. The conclusion holds in particular for radial-Lipschitz domains.

Proof.

For any
f\in\mathcal{HB}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)
,
0<s<1
,
1<p<\infty
we have seen from (
5.2
) that

\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|V_{s,p}(f)^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|)^{(1-s)p-1}dxdy\simeq\iint_{\Omega}|f^{\prime}(\zeta)|^{p}d(\zeta,\Gamma)^{(1-s)p-1}d\xi d\eta

where the implicit constant depends only on
s
and
p
, and

V_{s,p}(f)^{\prime}(z)=(f\circ\varphi)^{\prime}(z)\varphi^{\prime}(z)^{1/p-s}.

From it we see that

f\in\mathcal{HB}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)\;\Leftrightarrow\;V_{s,p}(f)^{\prime}\in A^{p}_{(1-s)p-1}

(5.12)

with comparable norms.
Noting that any
g\in A^{p}_{(1-s)p-1}
can be written in the form
V_{s,p}(f)^{\prime}
by choosing
f(z)=\int_{0}^{z}g\circ\varphi^{-1}(u)(\varphi^{-1})^{\prime}(u)^{1+1/p-s}du
, we have that the operator
\mathcal{HB}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)\ni f\mapsto V_{s,p}(f)^{\prime}\in A^{p}_{(1-s)p-1}
is a bounded isomorphism.
This boils down to prove

f\in\text{HB}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)\;\Leftrightarrow\;V_{s,p}(f)^{\prime}\in A^{p}_{(1-s)p-1}

(5.13)

with comparable norms. For this purpose, we will use Theorem
5.6
in the following.

We have proved that for any
1<p<\infty
,

\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Omega\!\to\!\Gamma)=B_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Gamma)
if
\Gamma
is a chord-arc curve; and for any
0<s<1
,

\mathcal{B}_{2,2}^{s}(\Omega\!\to\!\Gamma)=B_{2,2}^{s}(\Gamma)
if moreover
\Gamma
satisfies that
|\varphi^{\prime}|\in A_{2}
and
\text{Arg}\varphi^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}
, thus the equivalence (
5.13
) has already been known to hold for
s=1/p
,
1<p<\infty
, and also holds for
0<s<1
,
p=2
.

Recall that
(A^{p}_{p-2},A^{2}_{1-2s})
is an interpolation pair and for
0\leq\theta\leq 1
,

[A^{p}_{p-2},A^{2}_{1-2s}]_{\theta}=A^{q}_{(1-\alpha)q-1}.

(5.14)

We have also seen that
(\text{HB}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Gamma),\text{HB}_{2,2}^{s}(\Gamma))
is an interpolation pair and

[\text{HB}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Gamma),\text{HB}_{2,2}^{s}(\Gamma)]_{\theta}=\text{HB}_{q,q}^{\alpha}(\Gamma)

(5.15)

provided that
\Gamma
is a chord-arc curve. Here,

\begin{split}\alpha=\alpha(\theta)&:=(1-\theta)/p+\theta s,\\
q=q(\theta)&:=2p/(2(1-\theta)+\theta p).\end{split}

(5.16)

Some elementary arithmetic leads to that for any given
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
q\in(1,\infty)
, there exist
p\in(1,\infty)
,
s\in(0,\,\min(1,5/2-2/p))
and
\theta\in[0,1]
such that (
5.16
) holds.
Recall that in the proof of Theorem
5.5
we have shown that
\check{B}
, the set of polynomials on
\Gamma
, is dense in
\text{HB}_{2,2}^{s}(\Gamma)
. By using the similar argument, we can see that
\check{B}
is also dense in
\text{HB}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Gamma)
and thus
\check{B}
is a dense subspace of the intersection space
\text{HB}_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Gamma)\cap\text{HB}_{2,2}^{s}(\Gamma)
.

Define a family of linear mappings
(\widetilde{T}_{z};\;z=\theta+it\in S)
,
S
standing for a vertical strip with
0\leq\theta\leq 1
as before, on
\check{B}
by

\widetilde{T}_{z}(f):=e^{cz^{2}}V_{\alpha(z),q(z)}(f)^{\prime}=e^{cz^{2}}f^{\prime}\circ\varphi(\varphi^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{q(z)}+1-\alpha(z)}=e^{cz^{2}}f^{\prime}\circ\varphi(\varphi^{\prime})^{(\frac{1}{2}-s)z+1}.

Here, we still choose the constant
c\geq A^{2}/4
as in the proof of Theorem
5.5
where
A:=\|\text{Arg}\varphi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}
, so that
e^{-ct^{2}}e^{tA}\leq e
. We will prove that for each
z=\theta+it\in S
, the linear mapping
\widetilde{T}_{z}
sends
\check{B}
to
A^{p}_{p-2}+A^{2}_{1-2s}
, and the family
(\widetilde{T}_{z})
satisfies the properties (i) and (ii) of Theorem
5.6
.

We first estimate
|\widetilde{T}_{z}(f)(\zeta)|^{p}
for each
z=\theta+it\in S
and each
f\in\check{B}
.
It follows from simple computation that

|e^{cz^{2}}|^{p}=e^{pc(\theta^{2}-t^{2})}\leq e^{pc}e^{-ct^{2}p},

and

|(\varphi^{\prime})^{(1/2-s)z+1}|^{p}=e^{t(s-1/2)p\text{Arg}\varphi^{\prime}}\left(|\varphi^{\prime}|^{1+(1/2-s)\theta}\right)^{p}\leq e^{|t|pA}\left(|\varphi^{\prime}|^{1+(1/2-s)\theta}\right)^{p}.

There exists a constant
C_{1}
such that
|f^{\prime}\circ\varphi|^{p}<C_{1}
since the diameter of the domain
\Omega
with
0
as an interior point is finite. Then,

\displaystyle|\widetilde{T}_{z}(f)(\zeta)|^{p}\leq C_{1}e^{pc}\left(e^{-ct^{2}+|t|A}\right)^{p}\left(|\varphi^{\prime}|^{1+(1/2-s)\theta}\right)^{p}\leq C_{1}e^{pc}e^{p}\left(|\varphi^{\prime}|^{1+(1/2-s)\theta}\right)^{p}.

(5.17)

To show the linear mapping
\widetilde{T}_{z}
sends
\check{B}
to
A^{p}_{p-2}+A^{2}_{1-2s}
,
we split into the following three cases:

Case
1
. when
s\leq 1/2

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|\widetilde{T}_{z}(f)(\zeta)|^{2}(1-|\zeta|)^{1-2s}d\xi d\eta

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}e^{2c}e^{2}\iint_{\mathbb{D}}\left(|\varphi^{\prime}|^{1+(1/2-s)\theta}\right)^{2}(1-|\zeta|)^{1-2s}d\xi d\eta

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}e^{2c}e^{2}\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}|\varphi^{\prime}|^{(1-2s)\theta}(1-|\zeta|)^{(1-2s)\theta}d\xi d\eta

which is bounded by a constant depending only on
s
and
\Omega
.

Case
2
. when
s>1/2
and
p\geq 2

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|\widetilde{T}_{z}(f)(\zeta)|^{p}(1-

\displaystyle|\zeta|)^{p-2}d\xi d\eta\leq C_{1}e^{pc}e^{p}\iint_{\mathbb{D}}\left(|\varphi^{\prime}|^{1+(1/2-s)\theta}\right)^{p}(1-|\zeta|)^{p-2}d\xi d\eta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}e^{pc}e^{p}\iint_{\mathbb{D}}\left((|\varphi^{\prime}|(1-|\zeta|))^{1+(1/2-s)\theta}\right)^{p-2}|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2+(1-2s)\theta}d\xi d\eta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}e^{pc}e^{p}\text{diam}(\Gamma)^{p-2}\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2+(1-2s)\theta}d\xi d\eta

which, using Hölder inequality, is bounded by a constant depending only on
p
and
\Omega
.

Case
3
. When
1/2<s<\min(1,5/2-2/p)
and
1<p<2
, it suffices to show that for all
\theta\in(0,1)
,

\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|\varphi^{\prime}(z)|^{2+p\theta(\frac{1}{2}-s)}d(\varphi(z),\Gamma)^{p-2}dxdy<\infty.

First of all, since
s>1/2
and
p<2
we have that

1<2+p\theta(\frac{1}{2}-s)<2.

By Hölder inequality,

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|\varphi^{\prime}(z)|^{2+p\theta(\frac{1}{2}-s))}d(\varphi(z),\Gamma)^{p-2}dxdy\\
\leq&\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|\varphi^{\prime}(z)|^{2}d(\varphi(z),\Gamma)^{\frac{2(p-2)}{2+p\theta(\frac{1}{2}-s)}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{2+p\theta(\frac{1}{2}-s)}{2}},\end{split}

and the last integral is equal, by the change of variable
\zeta=\varphi(z)
, to

\iint_{\Omega}d(\zeta,\Gamma)^{\frac{2(p-2)}{2+p\theta(\frac{1}{2}-s)}}d\xi d\eta.

The domain being Lipschitz, this integral is finite if and only if

\frac{2(p-2)}{2+p\theta(\frac{1}{2}-s)}>-1,

or, equivalently, if and only if

0<\theta<2\frac{1-\frac{1}{p}}{s-\frac{1}{2}}.

A sufficient condition for the finiteness will thus be that

2\frac{1-\frac{1}{p}}{s-\frac{1}{2}}>1\Leftrightarrow s<\frac{5}{2}-\frac{2}{p}.

Consequently, when
1<p<\infty,\,0<s<\min(1,5/2-2/p)
, the linear ma??pping
\widetilde{T}_{z}
sends
\check{B}
to
A^{p}_{p-2}+A^{2}_{1-2s}
, and for all
f\in\check{B}
, the mapping
\widetilde{T}_{(\cdot)}(f):S\to A^{p}_{p-2}+A^{2}_{1-2s}
is bounded.

Taking
\theta=0
in (
5.17
) we can see

\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|\widetilde{T}_{it}(f)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p-2}dxdy\leq C(p,c)\iint_{\Omega}|f^{\prime}(\zeta)|^{p}d(\zeta,\Gamma)^{p-2}d\xi d\eta

where the constant
C(p,c)
depends only on
p
and
c
.
By that we get

\displaystyle M_{0}

\displaystyle:=\sup\{\|\widetilde{T}_{it}f\|_{A^{p}_{p-2}};\;t\in\mathbb{R},f\in\check{B},\|f\|_{B_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Gamma)}\leq 1\}<\infty.

Taking
\theta=1
in (
5.17
) we can see

\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|\widetilde{T}_{1+it}(f)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{1-2s}dxdy\leq C(c,s)\iint_{\Omega}|f^{\prime}(\zeta)|^{2}d(\zeta,\Gamma)^{1-2s}d\xi d\eta

where
C(s,c)
is a constant depending only on
s
and
c
.
From this it follows that

\displaystyle M_{1}

\displaystyle:=\sup\{\|\widetilde{T}_{1+it}f\|_{A^{2}_{1-2s}};\;t\in\mathbb{R},f\in\check{B},\|f\|_{B_{2,2}^{s}(\Gamma)}\leq 1\}<\infty.

By the continuity of parametrized integral, all functions
S\ni z\mapsto\widetilde{T}_{z}f\in A^{p}_{p-2}+A^{2}_{1-2s}
,
\mathbb{R}\ni t\mapsto\widetilde{T}_{it}f\in A^{p}_{p-2}
, and
\mathbb{R}\ni t\mapsto\widetilde{T}_{1+it}f\in A^{2}_{1-2s}
are continuous. Finally, for any
f\in\check{B}
, the proof of holomorphy of the mapping
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{S}}\ni z\mapsto\widetilde{T}_{z}f\in A^{p}_{p-2}+A^{2}_{1-2s}
is similar to that in the proof of Theorem
5.5
. We omit the details here. This completes the proof of Theorem
5.8
.
∎

5.5
Some Final Remarks

In this part, we let
0<s<1
and
1<p<\infty
, and
suppose
\Gamma
satisfies the assumption of Theorem
5.7
, namely
\Gamma
is a bounded chord-arc curve with
0
in its interior domain
\Omega
, and
\Gamma
is such that
\text{Arg}\,\varphi^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}
and
|\varphi^{\prime}|\in A_{q}
with
q=\min{(p,p^{\prime})}
.
Here,

1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
and

\varphi_{i}^{\prime}
is a Riemann mapping from the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
onto
\Omega
keeping
0
fixed.
Recall that when
p=2
we have shown that the radial-Lipschitz curve satisfies this assumption.

Since
h(\Gamma)=1
one may see that
(h(\Gamma),p,s)
is always in the admissible set
\mathcal{A}
. It follows from Theorem
6.2
and Theorem
5.7
(or Theorem
5.5
for the case of
p=2
) that in this case
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i}\!\to\!\Gamma)=\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{e}\!\to\!\Gamma)
with comparable norms. Recall that we explored earlier that this equality also holds under a different assumption where there are some restrictions on
p
and
s
in Theorem
3.12
.

If
p>2
,
s\geq 1-1/p
we then have that

(i)

\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)=W^{1,p}(\omega,\mathbb{C})|_{\Gamma}
where
\omega(z)=d(z,\Gamma)^{(1-s)p-1}
, i.e., the space
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
is the trace space of
W^{1,p}(\omega,\mathbb{C})
on
\Gamma
;  (by Theorem
3.16
)

(ii)

The space
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})|_{\Gamma}
is dense in
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
, and thus Plemelj-Calderón property holds for any function in
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
.  (by Corollary
3.17
and Theorem
3.5
)

(iii)

for any
f\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
, its extension to
\mathbb{C}
by the harmonic extensions on both connected components
\Omega_{i,e}
of
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Gamma
belongs to
W^{1,p}(\omega,\mathbb{C})
;  (by section 3.3.2)

(iv)

Any function in
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i,e})
satisfies the almost-Dirichlet principle.  (by section 3.2.3)

The above statements are claims: given
p>2
and thus the class of curves
\Gamma
for which claims are true depends on
p
(i,e.,
|\varphi^{\prime}|\in A_{p^{\prime}}
). If we reverse the point of view and start with a curve
\Gamma
satisfying
\text{Arg}\,\varphi^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}
and
|\varphi^{\prime}|\in A_{2}
then the claims are true for
p\in(\delta,\frac{\delta}{\delta-1})
where
\delta
is the infinimum of the set of
p^{\prime}s
such that
|\varphi^{\prime}|\in A_{p}
. By the theory of
A_{\infty}
weights,
\delta<2
.

Appendix

Let
\Gamma
be a Jordan curve with
\infty\notin\Gamma
, and
\Omega_{i,e}
the connected components of
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Gamma
as above.
In all this work we have treated
\Omega_{i}
and
\Omega_{e}
similarly: in doing so we have overlooked the fact that
\Omega_{e}
is unbounded, thus requiring an extra argument. The purpose of this appendix is to fill this gap.

Without loss of generality, suppose that
\Gamma
is included in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
with
0
being an interior point.
The map
z\mapsto 1/z
transforms
\Omega_{e}
into a bounded domain
\Omega^{\prime}_{i}
with boundary
\Gamma^{\prime}=1/\Gamma
;
\Omega_{i}^{\prime}
contains
0
which corresponds to
\infty
in
\Omega_{e}
.
We say
u
is harmonic in the domain
\Omega_{e}
with
\infty
being an interior point if
u(1/z)
is harmonic in the bounded domain
\Omega_{i}^{\prime}
.
Let
p>1
and
s\in(0,1)
as usual and consider
u\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{e})
.
Let
v:\Omega_{i}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{C}
being defined by
v(z)=u(1/z)
. By a change of the variable
z=1/w
we conclude by the Koebe distortion theorem (
2.1
) that

\iint_{\Omega_{e}}|\nabla u(w)|^{p}d(w,\Gamma)^{(1-s)p-1}dudv\simeq\iint_{\Omega_{i}^{\prime}}|\nabla v(z)|^{p}d(z,\Gamma^{\prime})^{(1-s)p-1}|z|^{2(sp-1)}dxdy

(6.1)

where the implicit constant is a universal one.
We have the following

Proposition 6.1
.

If
u\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{e})
then

v\in\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i}^{\prime}).

Proof.

Let
r<1/4
and
R_{1},R_{2}
be such that
\frac{1}{2}+r<R_{1}<R_{2}<1-r
. For any
\zeta
such that
|\zeta|<r
the annulus
A_{\zeta}=\{R_{1}<|z-\zeta|<R_{2}\}
is included in the annulus
\{\frac{1}{2}<|z|<1\}
. By the mean value property for harmonic functions we have, for
|\zeta|<r
,

|\nabla v(\zeta)|=\frac{1}{\pi R_{i}^{2}}\iint_{|z-\zeta|<R_{i}}\nabla v(z)dxdy,\quad i=1,2

so that

|\nabla v(\zeta)|\leq\frac{1}{\pi(R_{2}^{2}-R_{1}^{2})}\iint_{\{1/2<|z|<1\}}|\nabla v(z)|dxdy.

Noting (
6.1
) we can conclude by using Hölder inequality that
|\nabla v|
is uniformly bounded in the disk
|\zeta|<r
by a constant depending only on
\Gamma
multiplied by the norm of
u
in
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{e})
. This estimate implies that

\iint_{|z|<r}|\nabla v(z)|^{p}d(z,\Gamma^{\prime})^{(1-s)p-1}dxdy<\infty.

(6.2)

In order to control the rest of the integral giving the norm of
v
:

\iint_{\Omega^{\prime}_{i}\setminus(|z|<r)}|\nabla v(z)|^{p}d(z,\Gamma^{\prime})^{(1-s)p-1}dxdy,

(6.3)

we first observe that the map
w=1/z
is bi-Lipschitz in the domain
\Omega^{\prime}_{i}\setminus(|z|<r)
and then that there exists a constant
C
depending only on
\Gamma
such that

\frac{1}{C|w|}d(w,\Gamma)\leq d(1/w,\Gamma^{\prime})\leq\frac{C}{|w|}d(w,\Gamma).

The details are left to the reader.

Theorem 6.2
.

If
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i}\!\to\!\Gamma)=B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
, and the identity operator from
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i}\!\to\!\Gamma)
onto
B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
is a bounded isomorphism, then the same assertions hold for the unbounded component
\Omega_{e}
of
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Gamma
, and in particular, the transmission operator from
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i})
onto
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{e})
,

\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i})\to\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{i}\!\to\!\Gamma)\to\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{e}),

is a bounded isomorphism.

Proof.

Let us use the above proposition to prove that
\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega_{e}\!\to\!\Gamma)=B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)

Giant Molecular Toroidal Moment Amenable to Direct Observation in a Fe 10 Dy 10 Ring
AlessandroSoncini, KieranHymas, JonasBraun, Yannik F. Schneider, Simone Calvello, Amer Baniodeh, Yanhua Lan, Wolfgang Wernsdorfer, Marco Affronte, Christopher E. Anson, Annie K. Powell

Abstract
In single molecule toroics (SMTs) atomic spins and orbital currents generate magnetic vortices known as toroidal moments 𝝉 \bm{\tau} , endowed with both magnetic and electric dipole symmetries, which can enable spin control via magnetoelectric effects as well as the development of robust qubits. In the archetypal Dy 3 SMT, 𝝉 \bm{\tau} is challenging to detect and control. Larger molecular rings can offer an enhanced toroidal response amenable to direct observation and manipulation. Here we report SMT properties for the 3 ​ d 3d - 4 ​ f 4f icosanuclear molecular ring Fe 10 Dy 10 , displaying toroidal excitations of unprecedented magnitude and energy dispersion spanning a ∼ \sim 62 billion dimensional toroidal space. We show these properties can be modeled using an ab initio-parameterised transfer matrix approach yielding excellent agreement with experiments. To assess the bulk toroidal polarization attainable in this system, we introduce the molar toroidal susceptibility ξ \xi , a thermodynamic linear response function measuring the SMT finite-temperature toroidal polarization induced by a magnetic field with a small non-vanishing curl. Direct calculation of ξ \xi for Fe 10 Dy 10 reveals a significant finite-temperature ground state toroidal polarization which should be amenable to experimental detection via spatially-focused magnetic field curls, as attainable e.g. using focused femtosecond laser pulses. Our findings could thus pave the way for direct observation and manipulation of molecular toroidal moments.

The interplay between spin-orbit coupling and a low symmetry electrostatic potential, such as that describing chiral matter, can trigger the emergence of unique space-time symmetry breaking physical properties such as chirality-induced spin selectivity (CISS)

The toroidal moment

;
dubovik1990
is one such unique property, which can be visualised either as a poloidal current winding on the surface of a torus, or as a closed loop of magnetic dipoles in a head-to-tail configuration, resulting in a formal absence of magnetic poles (hence the alternative name
anapole
moment).

Despite its intrinsic magnetic origin (odd under time reversal, like all magnetic multipoles), the toroidal moment Eq. (
1

zeldovich1957
;
dubovik1990
;
faglioni2004
;
spaldin2008toroidal

If the molecule is instead achiral, the application of an inhomogeneous magnetic field carrying a non-zero curl
\curl{\bf{B}}

;
spaldin2008toroidal

6

Anapole moments were introduced as early as 1957 in a groundbreaking work by Zeldovich
zeldovich1957
, in order to identify an electromagnetic multipole in matter capable of mediating parity non-conserving (PNC) weak nuclear interactions between electrons and nuclei with non-zero spin. The predicted toroidal (anapole) moment was later measured in Cs atoms in a famous experiment by Wood et al.
wood1997cesium

While also in extended systems realizations of classical toroidal moments have been demonstrated in a number of experimental configurations, ranging from non-collinear magnetic order in low dimensional ferromagnets

\curl{\mathbf{B}\left(\mathbf{r},t\right)}=\mu_{0}\mathbf{J}\left(\mathbf{r},t\right)+\mu_{0}\epsilon_{0}\frac{\partial\mathbf{E}\left(\mathbf{r},t\right)}{\partial t}

(9)

shows that in order to induce a local
\curl{\mathbf{B}}\neq 0
, one way is to have a current density
\mathbf{J}\left(\mathbf{r},t\right)\neq 0
perpendicular to the ring’s plane, which in principle could be achieved via an STM tip
schnack2022toroidalbasics
;
pavlyukh2020toroidal
, although this requires a highly ordered surface sample, and cannot induce a macroscopic toroidal polarization.

Alternatively, for
\mathbf{J}\left(\mathbf{r},t\right)=0
, a bulk toroidal polarization could be achieved via an electric field ramp with sufficiently large
\frac{\partial\mathbf{E}\left(\mathbf{r},t\right)}{\partial t}
, for instance harnessing an asymmetrically shaped laser pulse (e.g. approximating a saw-tooth signal) implementing an inhomogeneous time-distribution of the
\frac{\partial\mathbf{E}\left(\mathbf{r},t\right)}{\partial t}
field, leading to the alternation of short periods of large
\curl{\mathbf{B}}
, to longer intervals of weaker reversed
-\curl{\mathbf{B}}
.

One can make an order-of-magnitude estimate of the radiation-toroidal moment coupling considering a standard 800nm Ti:Sapphire laser with peak power of
P_{\mathrm{max}}\approx 1\mathrm{GW}
, irradiating a sample with
10\mathrm{fs}
pulses (i.e. a pulse energy of
E_{\text{pulse}}\simeq 10\mu
J), assuming a sufficiently large spot diameter of
d_{\text{spot}}\simeq 200\mu
m to avoid sample damage (fluence
=E_{\text{pulse}}/A_{\text{spot}}\simeq 0.03
J/cm
2
) and assuming for simplicity a linear electric field ramp time variation, since the intensity of radiation is related to the peak field
E_{\mathrm{max}}
via
I_{\mathrm{max}}=P_{\mathrm{max}}/A_{\mathrm{spot}}=\frac{1}{2}c\epsilon_{0}E^{2}_{\mathrm{max}}
, an estimate for the peak electric field is
E_{\mathrm{max}}=\sqrt{2P_{\mathrm{max}}/c\epsilon_{0}A_{\mathrm{spot}}}\approx 5\times 10^{9}
V/m. This leads to the following conservative estimate for the magnetic field curl induced within the 10fs pulse interval:

\left|\curl{\mathbf{B}}\right|\approx\mu_{0}\epsilon_{0}\frac{E_{\mathrm{max}}}{\Delta t}\approx 5\times 10^{-4}\mathrm{T}\mathrm{\AA}^{-1}

(10)

It now becomes evident why a large molecular toroidal moment such as that of Fe
10
Dy
10
is amenable to direct detection, as opposed to smaller rings like Dy
3
. In Dy
3
the triangle’s radius is about 2Å, while in Fe
10
Dy
10
the average radius is 10Å. Taking the ionic magnetic moments as
m_{\mathrm{Dy}}=10\mu_{B}
and
m_{\mathrm{Fe}}=5\mu_{B}
, the maximal toroidal moments achievable in the two rings are
\bm{\tau}_{\mathrm{Dy}_{3}}\approx 60\mu_{B}\mathrm{\AA}
and
\bm{\tau}_{\mathrm{Fe}_{10}\mathrm{Dy}_{10}}\approx 1500\mu_{B}\mathrm{\AA}
.
This leads to different coupling strengths for the two rings:

\displaystyle\Delta E_{\pm\bm{\tau}_{\mathrm{Dy}_{3}}}

\displaystyle\approx 2\times\left|\bm{\tau}_{\mathrm{Dy}_{3}}\right|\times\left|\curl{\mathbf{B}}\right|=0.06\,\mathrm{cm}^{-1}

\displaystyle\Delta E_{\pm\bm{\tau}_{\mathrm{Fe}_{10}\mathrm{Dy}_{10}}}

\displaystyle\approx 2\times\left|\bm{\tau}_{\mathrm{Fe}_{10}\mathrm{Dy}_{10}}\right|\times\left|\curl{\mathbf{B}}\right|=1.5\,\mathrm{cm}^{-1}

Thus assuming sufficiently slow toroidal relaxation times, these estimates suggest that for the larger Fe
10
Dy
10
ring, at liquid-He temperature, an electric field ramp like that achievable via a shaped laser pulse under reasonable experimental conditions could result in a measurable bulk toroidal polarization, hence to the first direct observation of molecular toroidal moments.

The general form of the interaction Hamiltonian is reported in Eq. (

The first term in Eq. (

where

The Dy

6.4
Perturbative treatment of Fe-Fe exchange interaction

Since our broken symmetry DFT calculations indicated systematically a smaller value of

with respect to Fe
{}^{\text{III}}
-Dy
{}^{\text{III}}

\displaystyle H_{0}(\bm{\sigma})

\displaystyle=H(\bm{\sigma})-\left(-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{10}J_{C}^{i,i+1}\mathbf{S}^{\text{Fe}}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{S}^{\text{Fe}}_{i+1}\right)

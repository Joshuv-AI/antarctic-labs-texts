Isoparametric Hypersurfaces of ℍ n × ℝ \mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R} and 𝕊 n × ℝ \mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
Ronaldo F. de Lima and Giuseppe Pipoli

Abstract
. We classify the isoparametric hypersurfaces and the homogeneous hypersurfaces of ℍ n × ℝ \mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R} and 𝕊 n × ℝ \mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R} , n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 , by establishing that any such hypersurface has constant angle function and constant principal curvatures. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 53A10 (primary), 53B25, 53C30 (secondary). Key words and phrases: isoparametric hypersurfaces – homogeneous hypersurfaces – constant principal curvatures – product space.



1.
Introduction

The construction and classification of isoparametric hypersurfaces
in general Riemannian manifolds
has become a matter of great interest in submanifold theory since
the early works by Cartan on this subject.
In a series of four remarkable papers
[
3
]
–
[
6
]
,
published in the late 1930’s, he classified the isoparametric
hypersurfaces of the
n
-hyperbolic spaces
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, and brought to light that
the classification of the isoparametric hypersurfaces of the
n
-spheres

\mathbb{S}^{n}
is a rather intricate problem. As a matter of fact,
this classification has been built through many works over the last decades,
and only recently it was announced to be complete (see
[
7
,
9
]
).

The theory of isoparametric hypersurfaces connects with many branches of
physics and mathematics. Recently,
it has been applied to establish existence and classification results
to various extrinsic geometric flows
in Riemannian manifolds, such
as the mean curvature flow, the inverse mean curvature flow, elliptic Weingarten flows, and
higher order mean curvature flows
(see
[
1
,
10
,
11
,
14
,
24
,
30
]
).

By definition, an isoparametric hypersurface has constant mean curvature,
as do any nearby (locally defined) hypersurface which is parallel to it.
Homogeneous hypersurfaces, that is, those which are codimension one orbits
of isometric actions on the ambient space, are well known
examples of isoparametric hypersurfaces.
It turns out that all isoparametric hypersurfaces of Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}

(classified by Segre
[
33
]
),
as well as those of hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}

(classified by Cartan
[
3
]
), are homogeneous.
In
\mathbb{S}^{n},
one has the two types, homogeneous (classified by Hsiang and Lawson
[
25
]
)
and nonhomogeneous (cf.
[
22
,
29
]
).

Besides being isoparametric, homogeneous hypersurfaces have
constant principal curvatures. However, the constancy of the
principal curvatures implies neither being isoparametric nor homogeneous.
For instance, in simply connected space forms,
as proved by Cartan, a hypersurface is isoparametric if and only if it has
constant principal curvatures. On the other hand, as we pointed out, there are
nonhomogeneous isoparametric hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Also,
Rodríguez-Vázquez
[
31
]
showed that, for each
n\geq 3
,
there exists an
n
-dimensional torus which
contains a nonisoparametric hypersurface whose principal curvatures are constant.
In addition, Guimarães, Santos and Santos
[
24
]
applied the theory of
mean curvature flow to obtain
a class of Riemannian manifolds that admit nonisoparametric
hypersurfaces with constant principal curvatures.
It should also be mentioned that, in certain Riemannian manifolds,
such as Damek-Ricci spaces,
there exist isoparametric hypersurfaces
whose principal curvatures are not constant functions (see
[
16
,
23
]
).

In
[
20
]
, Domínguez-Vázquez and Manzano
classified the isoparametric surfaces of all simply connected homogeneous
3-manifolds with 4-dimensional isometry
group. These are the so called
\mathbb{E}(\kappa,\uptau)
spaces with
\kappa-4\uptau^{2}\neq 0
,
which include the products
\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
.
Their result also provides the classification
of the homogeneous surfaces of these spaces, as well as
of the surfaces having constant principal curvatures.
Other results on classification of isoparametric or
constant principal curvature
hypersurfaces of Riemannian manifolds of nonconstant
sectional curvature were
obtained in
[
8
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
28
,
32
]
.

Inspired by Domínguez-Vázquez and Manzano’s work,
we aim to establish here the following classification result for
hypersurfaces of the products
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
, where
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}

stands for the
n(\geq\hskip-2.84544pt2)

constant sectional curvature
\epsilon=\pm 1

the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
for
\epsilon=-1

and the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}

\epsilon=1

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Tensor Curvature of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

Given a field
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R})
, we shall denote by
X^{h}
its component which is
tangent to
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
(called
horizontal
), that is,

X^{h}:=X-\langle X,\partial_{t}\rangle\partial_{t},

where
\partial_{t}
denotes the gradient of the projection
\pi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}}
of

\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
on its second factor
\mathbb{R}.
Note that
\partial_{t}
is a unit parallel field on
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.

When
X=X^{h}
, we say that the field
X
is
horizontal
.

Since the first factor of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
has constant sectional curvature
\epsilon
and its
second factor is one-dimensional, the Riemannian curvature tensor
R_{\epsilon}

of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
is

R_{\epsilon}(X,Y)Z=\epsilon(\langle X^{h},Z^{h}\rangle Y^{h}-\langle Y^{h},Z^{h}\rangle X^{h})\,\,\,\,\,\forall X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}).

2.2.
Hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

Given an oriented hypersurface
\varSigma
of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
(endowed with the
standard product metric),
set
N
for its unit normal field and
A
for its shape operator with respect to

N,
that is

AX=-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{X}N,\,\,\,X\in\mathfrak{X}(\varSigma),

where
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
is the Levi-Civita connection of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R},

and
\mathfrak{X}(\varSigma)
is the tangent bundle of
\varSigma
.
The principal curvatures of
\varSigma,
i.e., the eigenvalues of
the shape operator

A,
will be denoted by
k_{1}\,,\dots,k_{n}
. In this setting,
the non-normalized
mean curvature

H
of
\varSigma

is defined as the sum of its principal curvatures, that is,

H:=k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}.

The
height function

\phi
and the
angle function

\varTheta

of
\varSigma
are defined by

\phi:=\pi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}}|_{\varSigma},\quad\varTheta:=\langle N,\partial_{t}\rangle.

Denoting by
\nabla
the gradient on
C^{\infty}(\varSigma)
and writing

T:=\nabla\phi,
the identity

T=\partial_{t}-\varTheta N

holds everywhere on
\varSigma.
In particular,

\|T\|^{2}=1-\varTheta^{2}.

2.3.
Graphs on parallel hypersurfaces

Let
\mathscr{F}:=\{M_{s}\subset\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\,;\,s\in I\}

be a family of parallel hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n},
where
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is an open interval.
Given a smooth function
\phi
on
I,
let

f\colon M_{s_{0}}\times I\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R},\,\,\,s_{0}\in I,

be the immersion given by

f(p,s):=(\exp_{p}(s\eta_{s_{0}}(p)),\phi(s)),\,\,\,(p,s)\in M_{s_{0}}\times I,

where
\exp
denotes the exponential map of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n},
and
\eta_{s_{0}}
is the unit
normal of
M_{s_{0}}.
The hypersurface
\varSigma=f(M_{s_{0}}\times I)
is a vertical graph
over an open set of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
whose level hypersurfaces
are the parallels
M_{s}
to
M_{s_{0}}.

Definition 1
.

With the above notation, we call
\varSigma
an
(M_{s},\phi)
-
graph
and say that

(1)

\varrho(s):=\frac{\phi^{\prime}(s)}{\sqrt{1+(\phi^{\prime})^{2}(s)}},\,\,\,s\in I,

is the
\varrho
-
function
of
\varSigma
.

Given an
(M_{s},\phi)
-graph
\varSigma
in
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
, it is easily seen that

N=-\varrho(s)\eta_{s}(p)+\varTheta\partial_{t}
is a unit normal to it.
In particular, one has that the equality

(2)

\varrho^{2}+\varTheta^{2}=1

holds everywhere on
\varSigma
.

It was proved in
[
12
]
that,
with this orientation, the principal curvature functions

k_{i}=k_{i}(p,s)
of
\varSigma

at a point
(p,s)\in M_{s_{0}}\times I
are:

k_{i}=-\varrho(s)k_{i}^{s}(p),\,\,\,i\in\{1,\dots,n-1\},\quad\text{and}\quad k_{n}=\varrho^{\prime}(s),

where
k_{i}^{s}(p)
is the
i
-th principal curvature of the parallel
M_{s}
at

\exp_{p}(s\eta_{s_{0}}(p)).

We point out that, from (
1
), one has

\phi(s)=\int_{s_{0}}^{s}\frac{\varrho(u)}{\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}(u)}}du+\phi(s_{0}),\,\,\,s_{0},s\in I.

Hence, an
(M_{s},\phi)
-graph is determined by its
\varrho
-function.

3.
Isoparametric hypersurfaces of constant angle

In this section, we prove Propositions
4

and
5
, which establish
the existence of the parabolic bowl as described in Figure
1
, and
its uniqueness — together with horizontal slices and vertical
cylinders over isoparametric hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
— as a hypersurface
of constant angle and constant principal curvatures in
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
. In the proofs,
the following two lemmas will play a crucial role. They first appeared in
[
36
]
, and
later in
[
15
]
in a more general form. For notational purposes, we
refer to the corresponding results of
[
15
]
.

Lemma 2

(Theorem 7 of
[
15
]
)
.

Let
\{M_{s}\,;\,s\in I\}
be a family of isoparametric
hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
. Consider the first order
differential equation

(3)

y^{\prime}=H^{s}y+H,\,\,\,s\in I,

where
H^{s}
denotes the mean curvature of
M_{s}
and
H
is a constant.
In this setting, if
\varrho\colon I\to(0,1)\subset\mathbb{R}
is a solution to
(
3
),
then the
(M_{s},\phi)
-graph determined by
\varrho
has constant mean curvature
H
.
Conversely, if an
(M_{s},\phi)
-graph
\varSigma
has constant mean curvature
H
,
then
\{M_{s}\,;\,s\in I\}
is isoparametric and the
\varrho
-function
of
\varSigma
is a solution to (
3
).

Lemma 3

(Corollary 4 of
[
15
]
)
.

Let
\varSigma
be a connected hypersurface of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
whose angle function is constant.
Then, one of the following occurs:

•

\varSigma
is an open set of a horizontal slice
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\{t_{0}\}
;

•

\varSigma
is a vertical cylinder over a hypersurface of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
;

•

\varSigma
is locally an
(M_{s},\phi)
-graph with
\phi^{\prime}
a nonzero constant.

Now, we are in a position to state and prove the announced
Propositions
4
and
5
.

Proposition 4
.

Given
H\in(0,n-1)
, there exists an entire
(M_{s},\phi)
-graph

\varSigma_{H}
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
(to be called a
parabolic bowl
) of constant mean
curvature
H,
which is homogeneous and has constant angle. In particular,

\varSigma_{H}
is isoparametric and has constant principal curvatures.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{F}:=\{M_{s}\,;\,s\in\mathbb{R}\}
be a family of parallel horospheres of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Then,
considering the “outward orientation” in each
M_{s}
, for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
and all
p\in M_{s}
, we have that

k_{i}^{s}(p)=-1
for all
i\in{1,\dots,n-1}
. Therefore,
given
H\in(0,n-1)
,
the ODE (
3
) associated to
\mathscr{F}
and
H
is

(4)

y^{\prime}=-(n-1)y+H.

Clearly,
the constant function
\varrho=H/(n-1)<1
is a solution to (
4
). Hence, by
Lemma
2
, the entire
(M_{s},\phi)
-graph
\varSigma_{H}
of
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

which is determined by
\varrho
has constant mean curvature
H
. Moreover,
by (
2
),

\varSigma_{H}
has constant angle function.

Finally, the homogeneity
of
\varSigma_{H}
follows from its invariance by the one-parameter group of
parabolic translations of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
which fix each horosphere
M_{s}
(extended slicewise to
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
),
as well as by the isometries
\Psi_{u}\times\uptau_{u}
,
u\in\mathbb{R},
where

\{\Psi_{u}\,;\,u\in\mathbb{R}\}\subset{\rm Iso}(\mathbb{H}^{n})

is the flow defined by the unit normals of the horospheres
M_{s}
, and

\uptau_{u}
is the vertical translation
(p,t)\mapsto(p,t+\varrho u)
of
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.

∎

When
n=2
, our parabolic bowl

\varSigma_{H}
corresponds to the surface
P_{H,-1,0}

of
[
20
]
, and to the surface

P_{H}
of
[
27
]
. They also appear in
[

]

n=3

in
[

Our chosen nomenclature comes from the extrinsic geometric flows

that, for each

, there exists a parabolic bowl

Proposition 5
.

Let
\varSigma
be a connected hypersurface of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
with constant angle function. Then,

\varSigma
is isoparametric if and only if it has constant principal curvatures. If so,

\varSigma
is an open set of one of the following hypersurfaces:

(i)

a horizontal slice
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\{t_{0}\}
,

(ii)

a vertical cylinder over a complete isoparametric hypersurface of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
,

(iii)

a parabolic bowl if
\epsilon=-1
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3
,
\varSigma
is an open set of a horizontal slice, a vertical cylinder
over a hypersurface of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
, or is locally an
(M_{s},\phi)
-graph such that
\phi^{\prime}
is constant.
If the first occurs, we are done. Assume then that
\varSigma
is a cylinder
\varSigma_{0}\times\mathbb{R}
, where

\varSigma_{0}
is a hypersurface of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
. It is easily seen that

\varSigma
is isoparametric (resp. has constant principal curvatures) if and only if
\varSigma_{0}
is isoparametric
(resp. has constant principal curvatures). However, in
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n},

to be isoparametric and to have constant principal curvatures are equivalent conditions. Besides,
we have from the classification of isoparametric hypersurfaces of space forms that
any isoparametric hypersurface of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
is necessarily an open set of a complete
isoparametric hypersurface.

Let us suppose now that
\varSigma
is locally an
(M_{s},\phi)
-graph of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
,
s\in I
,
such that
\phi^{\prime}
is a nonzero constant. Then, from (
1
),
the
\varrho
-function of
\varSigma
is a nonzero constant as well.
If either
\varSigma
is isoparametric or has
constant principal curvatures, then its mean curvature
H
is constant.
In this case, it follows from Lemma
2
that the hypersurfaces

M_{s}
are isoparametric
and that
\varrho
satisfies

0=\varrho^{\prime}=\varrho H^{s}+H,

which implies that the mean curvature
H^{s}
of
M_{s}

is a constant independent of
s
. Again, by considering the classification of
isoparametric hypersurfaces
of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
, one concludes that

\{M_{s}\,;\,s\in I\}
is necessarily a family of parallel horospheres of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, so that

\varSigma
is an open set of a parabolic bowl of
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
.
∎

4.
The constancy of the angle function

This section will be entirely dedicated to the proof of the following proposition,
which constitutes our main result for establishing Theorem
1
.

Proposition 7
.

The angle function of any connected isoparametric hypersurface of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
is constant.

Proof of Proposition
7
.

Let
\varSigma
be a connected oriented isoparametric hypersurface of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
.
Since its angle function
\varTheta
is continuous, it suffices to prove that

\varTheta
is locally constant on
\varSigma
. We can assume that

\varTheta^{2}\neq 1
, so that the gradient
T
of the height function of
\varSigma

never vanishes. In this setting, define

\Phi^{r}\colon\varSigma\to\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
by

\Phi^{r}(p)=\exp_{p}(rN_{p}),

where
N_{p}
is the unit normal field of
\varSigma

at
p\in\varSigma
and
\exp
stands for
the exponential map of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
. Passing to an open subset of

\varSigma
, we can assume that, for a
small
\delta>0,
and for all
r\in(-\delta,\delta),

the map
\Phi^{r}
is well defined and
\varSigma_{r}:=\Phi^{r}(\varSigma)
is an embedded hypersurface
of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
which lies at distance
|r|
from
\varSigma
.

Given
p\in\varSigma
,
let
\gamma_{p}(r)
be the geodesic of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
such that

\gamma_{p}(0)=p
and
\gamma_{p}^{\prime}(0)=N_{p}
, that is,

\gamma_{p}(r)=\Phi^{r}(p)
. It is easily seen that
the unit normal to
\varSigma^{r}
at
\gamma_{p}(r)
is

N(r):=\gamma_{p}^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle\epsilon R_{\epsilon}(\gamma_{p}^{\prime},\zeta_{j})\gamma_{p}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|T\|^{2}\zeta_{j}-b_{1j}\|T\|\partial_{t}+\varTheta b_{1j}\|T\|N=\|T\|^{2}\zeta_{j}-b_{1j}\|T\|T

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|T\|^{2}(\zeta_{j}-b_{1j}U_{1})=\sum_{i=2}^{n}\|T\|^{2}b_{ij}U_{i}.

This last equality and (

Now, set
(a_{ij})
for the (symmetric) matrix of

Notice that the derivatives of

Given

In particular,

where, in the last equality, we used the fact that

Defining

, it follows from the above that the function

Since

Therefore, for all

where

Now, considering the equalities (

\hline\cr\begin{array}[]{c}-a_{12}s_{\uptau}(r)\\
\vdots\\
-a_{1n}s_{\uptau}(r)\end{array}&\delta_{ij}c_{\uptau}(r)-a_{ij}s_{\uptau}(r)\end{array}\right].

Set

The following lemma will play a fundamental role in our final argument.

Lemma 8
.

Suppose that
n
is odd. Then, for every positive integer
s_{0}
,
there exists
s_{*}\geq s_{0}
such that
d_{s_{*}}\neq 0
.

Proof.

Assume, by contradiction, that

d_{s}=0\quad\forall s\geq s_{0}.

Then, by (
15
),

(17)

D^{(s+1)}(0)=0\quad\forall s\geq s_{0}.

Fix an arbitrary point
p\in\varSigma
. Since the functions

c_{\uptau}
and
s_{\uptau}
are real analytic, so is
D
. Then,
it follows from (
17
) that
D=D(r)
agrees with a polynomial in a
neighborhood of
r=0
.

First, we shall classify the symmetric matrices
A

for which
D
is a polynomial in
r
,
then we will show that none of them can be the second fundamental form
of an isoparametric hypersurface.

Write the matrix in (
16
) in block form as

B(r)=\begin{bmatrix}1-ar&-rv^{T}\\[4.30554pt]
-s_{\uptau}(r)v&c_{\uptau}(r)I_{m}-s_{\uptau}(r)A_{0}\end{bmatrix},

where
m=n-1
and

a=a_{11},\qquad v=(a_{12},\ldots,a_{1n})^{T},\qquad A_{0}=(a_{ij})_{2\leq i,j\leq n}.

Set

M(r)=c_{\uptau}(r)I_{m}-s_{\uptau}(r)A_{0},\qquad\varphi(r)=\det M(r).

The standard determinant formula for block matrices, together with
the matrix determinant lemma, gives

(18)

D(r)=\varphi(r)-r\psi(r)\quad\text{and}\quad\psi(r)=a\varphi(r)+s_{\uptau}(r)v^{T}\operatorname{adj}(M(r))v.

We claim that

(19)

\varphi\equiv 1\quad\text{and}\quad\psi\equiv a.

Indeed, let
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}
be the eigenvalues of the
symmetric matrix
A_{0}
, and let

\widetilde{v}=(\widetilde{v}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{v}_{m})

be the coordinates of
v
with respect to an orthonormal eigenbasis
of
A_{0}
. Then

(20)

\varphi(r)=\prod_{\ell=1}^{m}\bigl(c_{\uptau}(r)-\lambda_{\ell}s_{\uptau}(r)\bigr),

whereas

(21)

s_{\uptau}(r)v^{T}\operatorname{adj}(M(r))v=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\widetilde{v}_{\ell}^{\,2}s_{\uptau}(r)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq\ell\end{subarray}}^{m}\bigl(c_{\uptau}(r)-\lambda_{k}s_{\uptau}(r)\bigr).

Set

\omega=\begin{cases}\sqrt{\uptau},&\uptau>0,\\[5.69054pt]
\sqrt{-\uptau},&\uptau<0.\end{cases}

For
\uptau<0
, we have

(22)

c_{\uptau}(r)=\frac{1}{2}\left(e^{i\omega r}+e^{-i\omega r}\right),\qquad s_{\uptau}(r)=\frac{1}{2i\omega}\left(e^{i\omega r}-e^{-i\omega r}\right),

whereas, for
\uptau>0
,

(23)

c_{\uptau}(r)=\frac{1}{2}\left(e^{\omega r}+e^{-\omega r}\right),\qquad s_{\uptau}(r)=\frac{1}{2\omega}\left(e^{\omega r}-e^{-\omega r}\right).

It follows from (
20
)–(
23
)
that there exist constants
\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}

such that

\varphi(r)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\alpha_{j}e^{\nu_{j}r},\qquad\psi(r)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\beta_{j}e^{\nu_{j}r},

where

\nu_{j}=\begin{cases}(m-2j)\sqrt{\uptau},&\uptau>0,\\[5.69054pt]
i(m-2j)\sqrt{-\uptau},&\uptau<0.\end{cases}

Note that
\nu_{0},\ldots,\nu_{m}
are pairwise distinct. Moreover,
since
m=n-1
is even,

\nu_{j}=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad j=\frac{m}{2}\cdot

Using
D=\varphi-r\psi
, we obtain

0=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j\leq m\\
j\neq m/2\end{subarray}}(\alpha_{j}-r\beta_{j})e^{\nu_{j}r}+\left(\alpha_{m/2}-r\beta_{m/2}-D(r)\right).

Recall the standard uniqueness property of exponential polynomials:
if
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{\ell}
are pairwise distinct and

\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}P_{j}(r)e^{\mu_{j}r}=0

on an open interval, where the
P_{j}
are polynomials, then

P_{j}=0
for every
j
. Since
D
is a polynomial near
r=0
,
this property applied to the preceding equality yields

\alpha_{j}=\beta_{j}=0\quad\forall j\neq\frac{m}{2},\qquad D(r)=\alpha_{m/2}-r\beta_{m/2}.

Thus
D
is affine and

\varphi(r)=\alpha_{m/2},\qquad\psi(r)=\beta_{m/2}.

In particular, both
\varphi
and
\psi
are constant. Since

\varphi(0)=1\qquad\text{and}\qquad\psi(0)=a,

we conclude that (
19
) holds.

Suppose first that
\uptau<0
, and set
z=e^{i\omega r}
. It follows
from (
20
) and (
22
) that

(24)

1=\varphi(r)=2^{-m}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left[\left(1+i\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\omega}\right)z+\left(1-i\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\omega}\right)z^{-1}\right].

However, the coefficient of

z^{m}
in the right-hand side is

2^{-m}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(1+i\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\omega}\right),

which is nonzero because every
\lambda_{j}
is real. Thus the
right-hand side of (
24
) cannot be constant. This contradiction
rules out the case
\uptau<0
.

We must therefore have
\uptau>0
. Set
z=e^{\omega r}
.
From (
20
) and (
23
), we obtain

(25)

1=\varphi(r)=2^{-m}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left[\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\omega}\right)z+\left(1+\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\omega}\right)z^{-1}\right].

The coefficients of
z^{\pm m}
on the right-hand side
of (

) vanish, so

2^{-m}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(1\mp\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\omega}\right)=0.

Hence, after reordering the indices, we may assume that

\lambda_{1}=\omega
and
\lambda_{m}=-\omega
. The corresponding
factors are
2z^{-1}
and
2z
, whose product is
4
. Thus,

1=2^{-(m-2)}\prod_{j=2}^{m-1}\left[\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\omega}\right)z+\left(1+\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\omega}\right)z^{-1}\right].

If
m>2
, comparing the coefficients of

gives

2^{-(m-2)}\prod_{j=2}^{m-1}\left(1\mp\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\omega}\right)=0.

Therefore, again, after reordering the remaining indices, we may assume
that
\lambda_{2}=\omega
and
\lambda_{m-1}=-\omega
.

Since

is even, repeating this argument removes all

factors
in pairs, each corresponding to one eigenvalue
\omega
and one

. Hence the eigenvalues of

are

\omega
and

, each with multiplicity
m/2
.

Now, by (

) and (

s_{\uptau}(r)v^{T}\operatorname{adj}(M(r))v=0.

Differentiating with respect to

, we obtain

Setting

Hence

, and therefore

is a principal direction.
Moreover,

(26)

AU_{1}=aU_{1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\operatorname{spec}\left(A|_{U_{1}^{\perp}}\right)=\{\sqrt{\uptau},-\sqrt{\uptau}\},

with

(27)

\operatorname{mult}_{A|_{U_{1}^{\perp}}}(\sqrt{\uptau})=\operatorname{mult}_{A|_{U_{1}^{\perp}}}(-\sqrt{\uptau})=\frac{m}{2}\cdot

Since the point

was arbitrary, conclusions

) hold at every point of
\varSigma
. The case

\uptau<0

, so that

\varSigma\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\uptau=\|T\|^{2}.

Let

be a connected component of a regular slice of

,

For every

Lemma 18

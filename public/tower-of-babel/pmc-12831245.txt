ProAE: an R package for graphical tools and standardized analysis of patient-reported outcomes and adverse events data
Authors: Blake Langlais, Brie Noble, Briant Fruth, Mia Truman, Gina L Mazza, Brenda Ginos, Carolyn Mead-Harvey, Minji Lee, Claire Yee, Lauren Rogak, Eric Meek, Allison M Deal, John Devin Peipert, Gita Thanarajasingam, Ethan Basch, Amylou C Dueck

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPatient-reported symptomatic adverse events (AE) are increasingly collected in oncology clinical trials to characterize treatment tolerability and inform clinical decision making using the Patient-Reported Outcomes (PRO) version of the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (PRO-CTCAE®). Although there are numerous analysis methods and graphical approaches used for PRO-CTCAE data, the current PRO literature is limited in unified reporting and graphical approaches as well as public-facing analysis tools.ResultsCollaborative efforts from the Standardization Working Group of the National Cancer Institute Cancer Treatment Tolerability Consortium worked to develop the R package, ProAE. Testing and validation of widely used methods were implemented in the R package and deployed to various open-source outlets including the Comprehensive R Archive Network (CRAN).ConclusionProAE is a free and publicly available collection of standardized statistical analysis tools for PRO-CTCAE and other PRO data used in patient care and research. The ProAE package provides oncology researchers with an efficient and modern means to apply the published analysis approaches, including hypothesis testing, descriptive and inferential tables, and longitudinal graphics, without the need for costly software or licensing.Keywords: PRO-CTCAE, Symptomatic adverse event, Tolerability, Toxicity, Statistical programming, Cancer clinical trials

Background

Patient reported outcomes (PRO) are commonly assessed in research to gain the patient’s perspective informing clinical care. The use of PRO data and analysis tools have become widely used. However, there has been limited adoption of the proposed standardized approaches to analyzing and reporting PRO results. This is in part due to the limited analysis software aimed at efficiently reproducing these standardized methods. As such, the research community has developed free and publicly available statistical software packages using the R statistical language to facilitate common methods of PRO analysis. The ProAE R package was designed to analyze PRO data such as the PRO-Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (PRO-CTCAE®), Functional Assessment of Cancer Therapy (FACT) item GP5, and other single-item, adverse event (AE) focused PRO tools. ProAE helps fill the gap in needed PRO analysis tools while still aiming to provide both standard and exploratory methodologies, a variety of data visualizations, and an optimized user experience [1]. This report demonstrates how the ProAE R package can be applied to PRO data to conduct statistical tests and produce standardized publication-ready tables and figures with PRO-CTCAE data as a use case.]. In oncology clinical trials, PRO data are used to evaluate treatment tolerability by tracking treatment-related occurrences of symptomatic adverse events during the trial period. This is accomplished using overall side effect impact tools and the National Cancer Institute’s PRO-CTCAE library of 124 items measuring 78 treatment-related symptomatic AE frequency, severity, and interference attributes for specific symptomatic AEs – the PRO version of the traditional clinician-reported CTCAE [2].

The literature provides guidance on analyzing and reporting these data using standardized approaches to emphasize the importance of communicating results with broad audiences including patients [1, 3–6]. Longitudinal bar charts can be created to visualize the tolerability experience while on trial or at important intervention periods (Fig. 1A. A091105 H/F, Gounder NEJM [7]; Fig. 1B. PROSPECT Pain, Basch JCO [8]), and summarized statistical tables can report common symptom prevalence and severity comparisons between treatment arms (e.g., composite grade tables 3–5 shown by Synder et al. [9]).

Standardized reporting methods (i.e., consistently formatted tables and figures) provide a more efficient way to view differences and similarities of AE symptomatology across trials and can enable clinicians to have a more rapid and accurate understanding of effect sizes and symptomatic adverse event risks needed for patient-facing decision making [10]. Statistical programing languages used for analysis, like R and SAS, often provide vetted packages for specific analyses to aid in timely coding which also guards against coding errors that can occur where similar programming is created and recreated for repeatable tasks. However, there is a deficiency of available packages that provide researchers with these standardized approaches. To address this, a collection of analysis tools designed for PRO-CTCAE and other PRO data was developed within the ProAE R package following guidance from the Standardization Working Group of the National Cancer Institute Cancer Treatment Tolerability Consortium whose role is to identify, develop and communicate standardized approaches to the collection, analysis, graphical representation, and reporting of tolerability data, PROs, including PRO-CTCAE. [U01 CA233046; 11].

Implementation

ProAE uses individual PRO-CTCAE symptom items (frequency, severity, and/or interference scores) to apply pre-analysis data processing, create standardized statistical tables with a variety of summary measures, graphical representations of the symptomatic AE profiles, and other research and exploratory methods [12]. The user provides either PRO-CTCAE numerical or text responses for frequency, severity, and interference attributes as a data frame.

The required variable naming conventions for the package follow a uniform structure where the ProAE package will review the user’s data frame contents for variables that have the prefix ‘PROCTCAE’ followed by the PRO-CTCAE library symptom number and attribute letter, and one of two tags, ‘SCL’ for numerically scaled items or ‘IND’ for indication item, with these pieces separated by underscores. For example, the nausea frequency item would be ‘PROCTCAE_9A_SCL’, where 9A is the symptom number and attribute letter from the library and ‘SCL’ indicates it is a scaled item ranging from low to high. The package documentation further specifies these and other available PRO tool items. Currently, the package’s required naming convention does not accommodate other prescribed conventions (e.g., CDISC, PROMIS) but future updates may make further accommodations.

Prior to analysis or graphical applications, the toxScores function can be used to implement data processing, recode text responses to numerical, apply imputation methods due to data collection skip logic [4], construct composite grades [13], or validate response data assessing for unexpected logical errors (e.g., scores exceeding the expected range, duplicate records within unique patient and time point combinations). PRO-CTCAE data is often collected using text responses rather than numerical responses (e.g., “Not at all” rather than 0, “Very Severe” rather than 4). The ProAE package can convert all text responses to numerical scores for appropriate summary measures and statistical comparisons. The package also offers an imputation method to handle missing data due to skip- or branching-logic seen in some electronic PRO collection tools. For example, some collection tools will not collect (or skip) a severity or interference attribute if the frequency of the symptomatic AE is reported to be ‘Never’. When this function is applied to the user’s data, an R data frame object is returned with the selected functionality.

The toxFigures function can be used to create publication quality, customizable longitudinal bar charts of symptomatic AE profiles (Fig. 1A and 1B). A key element of this figure design is its highly informational and easily interpretable layout. Frequency distributions of PRO-CTCAE scores are shown over the course of the trial and can be stratified by treatment arm. The user can customize these figures extensively using function parameters. For example, annotating bars to include various statistics such as study group size at individual time points or counts/percentages of patients meeting given AE severity thresholds (e.g., count of patients with attribute scores ≥ 3). Summary measures of arm-level AE burden can also be created alone or added alongside the longitudinal bar charts to show statistics such as each patient’s maximum score after treatment initiation, and each patient’s maximum score among only those scores which were worse that the patient’s baseline score (i.e., baseline adjusted maximum score [14, 15]). When this function is applied to the user’s data, an R list object is returned with the available PRO-CTCAE items indexed as elements of the list beside a reference table to assist the user in selecting which item figure to print.

Applying the toxTables function to PRO-CTCAE data will produce structured statistical tables similar to those created for CTCAE data and commonly required to be reported in publications. These tables can show arm-level sample size and frequency distributions for PRO-CTCAE items available in the user’s data (Table 1). Results from statistical comparisons can be added to the tables such as chi squared or Fisher’s exact tests. Risk difference between arms can be created with user-defined alpha-level confidence intervals. When this function is applied to the user’s data, an R data frame object is returned with the available PRO-CTCAE items structured in a typical table format. This data frame can be written to a spreadsheet for further formatting or simply copy-pasted into a document.

For more granularity, the toxSummary function can calculate a variety of standard and exploratory summary measures as an intermediary step for analyses of patient- or arm-level statistics (e.g., power analysis, simulation studies, tertiary graphics). When the function is applied to the user’s data, an R data frame object is returned with the available PRO-CTCAE items summarized to the specified patient- or arm-level statistic. Summary measures for PRO-CTCAE data offered in the ProAE package include the maximum score, maximum score post-baseline, baseline-adjusted maximum [1, 11, 14, 15], incremental area-under-the-curve (AUC) characterizing symptomatic AE worsening from baseline, and the toxicity index [16, 17].

The package also serves as a resource for newly developed methods aimed to summarize patients’ symptomatic experience. For example, the function toxAUC gives the user a set of tools to assess incremental AUC adjusted for baseline symptoms (Fig. 3). The accumulated area of the symptomatic AE profile above baseline can be interpreted as symptom worsening driven by treatment. Arm-level AUCs can be compared statistically using the function parameters. A closed-form expression of this incremental AUC measure can be found in the ProAE reference manual.

Our package also offers simulated data of common symptomatic AE profiles such as acute, cumulative, cyclical, and late toxicity at various effect sizes for power or exploratory analysis [18]. The simulated data are provided with no missing data. If the user’s data has some missingness, the package utilizes all available data required by the summary measure or graphical representation selected. For example, baseline-adjusted methods will require an observed baseline data value from each patient in the user’s data. If a patient is missing required data in this way, they are excluded from the specific calculation and/or visualization being created. Sample size tabulations are available within tables and figures available in the ProAE package to communicate the number of patients represented at each timepoint and within each summary measure.

Results

The following is a brief demonstration of ProAE methods and results using simulated PRO-CTCAE data available within the package. The R code shown was taken from the console. The simulated data in this illustration contain two treatment arms with n = 70 patients in each group and are evaluated for differences in treatment-related nausea; a placebo group with no expected treatment-related nausea, and a drug group with a simulated acute toxicity profile where treatment-related nausea develops within a short timeframe then dissipates.

Reviewing the results produced, Figure 2 shows the nausea profile of the drug and placebo groups in side-by-side bar charts over the course of the trial using the default toxFigures function options. From this figure, distinct acute treatment-related nausea is observed during Cycles 2 and 3 among the drug group, relative to the placebo group. Each of the PRO-CTCAE Nausea attributes are shown together in the same figure, as well as the composite grade allowing for a more complete interpretation of the symptomatic AE item. The toxFigures function has additional features to add sample size annotations, custom labeling, summary measures, and others. Figure 3 demonstrates this by changing the cycle enumeration to show the trial-specific schedule labeling, adding sample size annotations that indicate the number of trial subjects with severe attribute scores (scores or grades ≥ 3) at each timepoint, highlights two standard summary measures (maximum score post-baseline and baseline-adjusted maximum), as well as shows a color-blind-friendly color palette. The summary measures in Figure 3 both suggest the drug group has a higher frequency of post-baseline and baseline-adjusted maximums. To tabulate the summary measures shown, the default options from the toxTables function produced Table 1 showing group sample sizes, baseline-adjusted counts/rates of nausea attribute presence (scores ≥ 1) and severe attribute scores (scores ≥ 3), as well as p values from respective chi square tests. In these simulated data, the drug group experienced higher baseline-adjusted rates of severe scores (nausea frequency, p = 0.002; nausea severity, p < 0.0001) and a higher rate of present nausea frequency (p = 0.0249). However, there was no significant difference in present nausea severity between the drug and placebo groups (p = 0.0670). Lastly, Figure 4 shows that the drug group experienced significantly higher accrued symptomatic toxicity using the nausea composite grade by comparing the iAUC between groups over the course of the trial using a permutation test (p < 0.0001).

Conclusions

The ProAE R package is a collection of tools aimed to facilitate standardized analysis and graphical procedures when using individual patient-reported outcome items that capture symptomatic adverse events, side effect bother, or other tolerability domains. This package provides a common framework to help statisticians and researchers conduct data analysis and efficiently reproduced tables and figures formatted similarly to other published studies. Providing these free and publicly available tools to the community will also aid in the adoption of the standardized methods proposed by the National Cancer Institute Cancer Treatment Tolerability Consortium. As more tools are developed and convergence on standardized approaches is gained by the community, the package can be updated to suit the needs of collaborative efforts. ProAE is publically available and can be applied to structured data to create interpretable output in cancer clinical trials and other areas of clinical PRO research.

A remark on isolated removable singularity of harmonic maps in dimension two
Changyou Wang

Abstract
. For a ball B R ​ ( 0 ) ⊂ ℝ 2 B_{R}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2} , we provide sufficient conditions such that a harmonic map u ∈ C ∞ ​ ( B R ​ ( 0 ) ∖ { 0 } , N ) u\in C^{\infty}(B_{R}(0)\setminus\{0\},N) , with a self-similar bound on its gradient, belongs to C ∞ ​ ( B R ​ ( 0 ) ) C^{\infty}(B_{R}(0)) . Those conditions also guarantee the triviality of such harmonic maps when R = ∞ R=\infty .



1.
Introduction

This short note address a question arising from the author’s recent study with Bang
[
1
]

on the question of rigidity for the steady (simplified) Ericksen-Leslie system in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which seeks to answer that if
(u,d)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\},\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
solves

(1.1)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p=-\nabla\cdot(\nabla d\odot\nabla d),\\
\nabla\cdot u=0,\\
\Delta d+|\nabla d|^{2}d=u\cdot\nabla d,\end{cases}\ \ {\rm{in}}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\},

and satisfies a self-similar bound

(1.2)

|u(x)|\leq\frac{C_{1}(n)}{|x|},\ \ |\nabla d(x)|\leq\frac{C_{2}(n)}{|x|},\ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\},

for some constants
C_{1}(n),C_{2}(n)>0
, then
(u,\nabla d)\equiv(0,0)\ {\rm{in}}\ \mathbb{R}^{n}
?

We obtained in
[
1
]
some partial results towards the question concerning
(
1.1
) and (
1.2
) mentioned above. In particular, among other results
we proved that when
n\geq 3
, there exists
\varepsilon_{n}>0
such that if
C_{1}(n),C_{2}(n)\leq\varepsilon_{n}
then

\nabla d\equiv 0
; while
u\equiv 0
when
n\geq 4
, and is a Landau solution of the steady Navier-Stokes equation
when
n=3
. When
n=2
, we constructed infinitely many nontrivial solutions of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
),
that resemble the so-called Hamel’s solutions of steady Navier-Stokes equation in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

A Liouville theorem on harmonic maps plays an important role in
[
1
]
, that is, for
n\geq 3
if

d\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\},N)
solves the equation of harmonic maps:

(1.3)

\Delta d+A(d)(\nabla d,\nabla d)=0\ \ {\rm{in}}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\},

and there exists an
\varepsilon_{0}(n)>0
such that

(1.4)

|\nabla d(x)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{0}(n)}{|x|},\ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\},

then
d
must be a constant map. Here
N\subset\mathbb{R}^{L}
is a compact smooth Riemann manifold without boundary,
and
A
denotes the second fundamental form of
N
.

A natural question to ask is that whether this Liouville property remains to be true when
n=2
.
More precisely,

Question 1.1
.

Suppose
d\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\},N)
solves (
1.3
) and satisfies (
1.4
) for some
small constant
\varepsilon_{0}(2)
. Then
d
must be constant.

To the knowledge of the author, this question has not been addressed from the literature. In contrast with
n\geq 3
, (
1.4
) alone
does not guarantee
d
has locally finite Dirichlet energy in dimension two,
or, equivalently,
E(d,B_{1}(0))=\int_{B_{1}(0)}|\nabla d|^{2}<\infty
for the unit ball
B_{1}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Thus, one may apply neither
the celebrated theorem by Sacks-Uhlenbeck
[
3
]
on the removability of isolated singularity of harmonic maps in dimension two,
nor the regularity theorem by Hélein
[
2
]
on weakly harmonic maps in dimension two. Observe that

d(x)=\frac{x}{|x|}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
is a harmonic map, satisfying
|\nabla d(x)|=\frac{1}{|x|}
for
x\not=0

and
E(d,B_{1}(0))=\infty
, while
x=0
is a non-removable singular point. This example indicates that
\varepsilon_{0}(2)
in Question 1.1 must be
sufficiently small.

In this note, we will give a partial answer to Question 1.1. More precisely, let
B_{R}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the ball in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

with center
0
and radius
R
, we first prove

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists an

\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that if

u:B_{R}(0)\setminus\{0\}\to N
is a smooth harmonic map, satisfying

(1.5)

|\nabla u(x)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{|x|},\ \ \forall x\in B_{R}(0)\setminus\{0\},

and if, in addition, there exists
r_{i}\to 0
such that

(1.6)

\lim_{i\to\infty}r_{i}\int_{\partial B_{r_{i}}(0)}\big(|\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}|^{2}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\frac{\partial u}{\partial\theta}|^{2}\big)\,d\sigma=0,

then
u\in C^{\infty}(B_{R}(0),N)
.

As a direct consequence of Theorem (
1.2
), we establish

Corollary 1.3
.

There exists an
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that if
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\},N)
is a harmonic map, satisfying

(1.7)

|\nabla u(x)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{|x|},\ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\},

and if, in addition, there exists
r_{i}\to 0
such that

(1.8)

\lim_{i\to\infty}r_{i}\int_{\partial B_{r_{i}}(0)}\big(|\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}|^{2}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\frac{\partial u}{\partial\theta}|^{2}\big)\,d\sigma=0,

then
u
must be a constant map.

2.
Proofs

This section is devoted to the proofs of Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
1.3
. First, we need

Lemma 2.1
.

If
u\in C^{\infty}(B_{R}(0)\setminus\{0\},N)
is a harmonic map, then

(2.1)

\phi(r):=r\int_{\partial B_{r}(0)}\big(|\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}|^{2}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\frac{\partial u}{\partial\theta}|^{2}\big)\,d\sigma

is constant for
r\in(0,R)
.

Proof.

Since
u\in C^{\infty}(B_{R}(0)\setminus\{0\},N)
solves the harmonic map equation (
1.3
),
for any
0<r_{1}<r_{2}<R
, we can multiply (
1.3
) by
x\cdot\nabla u
and integrate the resulting equation
over
B_{r_{2}}(0)\setminus B_{r_{1}}(0)
to obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r_{2}}(0)\setminus B_{r_{1}}(0)}\Delta u\cdot(x\cdot\nabla u)=\int_{B_{r_{2}}(0)\setminus B_{r_{1}}(0)}(u_{j}x_{i}u_{i})_{j}-|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}x_{j}(|\nabla u|^{2})_{j}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial(B_{r_{2}}(0)\setminus B_{r_{1}}(0))}(x\cdot\nabla u)\cdot(\nu\cdot\nabla u)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial(B_{r_{2}}(0)\setminus B_{r_{1}}(0))}|\nabla u|^{2}x\cdot\nu,

where
\nu
denotes the outward unit normal of
\partial(B_{r_{2}}(0)\setminus B_{r_{1}}(0))
. This implies that

\displaystyle r_{2}\int_{\partial B_{r_{2}}(0)}\big(|\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\big)\,d\sigma=r_{1}\int_{\partial B_{r_{1}}(0)}\big(|\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\big)\,d\sigma.

Since
\displaystyle|\nabla u|^{2}=|\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}|^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\frac{\partial u}{\partial\theta}|^{2}
,
it follows that

(2.2)

\displaystyle r_{2}\int_{\partial B_{r_{2}}(0)}\big(|\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}|^{2}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\frac{\partial u}{\partial\theta}|^{2}\big)\,d\sigma=r_{1}\int_{\partial B_{r_{1}}(0)}\big(|\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}|^{2}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\frac{\partial u}{\partial\theta}|^{2}\big)\,d\sigma.

This implies (
2.1
). ∎

Remark 2.2
.

It is easy to check that if
\displaystyle d(x)=\frac{x}{|x|}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
, then
\phi(r)=-2\pi
for all
r>0
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
: From the condition (
1.6
)
and (
2.1
) of Lemma
2.1
, we have that

(2.3)

\int_{\partial B_{r}(0)}|\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}|^{2}\,d\sigma=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\int_{\partial B_{r}(0)}|\frac{\partial u}{\partial\theta}|^{2}\,d\sigma

holds for all
0<r<R
.

We will modify the original argument by Sacks-Uhlenbeck
[
3
]

in showing that
x=0
is a removable singularity for
u
.

First, we will show that

u
has finite Dirichlet energy, i.e.,
u\in H^{1}(B_{R}(0))
.
For this, let
0<r_{*}<R_{*}\leq R
be two given radius. Set
K=\big[\frac{\ln(\frac{R_{*}}{r_{*}})}{\ln 2}\big]\in\mathbb{N}

and define the annuals

\displaystyle A_{m}=B_{2^{m}r_{*}}(0)\setminus B_{2^{m-1}r_{*}}(0),\ \ 1\leq m\leq K.

Denote the radial harmonic function

h_{m}(r):=a_{m}+b_{m}\ln r:A_{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{L}
, where
a_{m}
and
b_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{L}
are chosen
according to the condition

h_{m}(2^{m}r_{*})=\fint_{\partial B_{2^{m}r_{*}}}u\,d\sigma,\ \ h_{m}(2^{m-1}r_{*})=\fint_{\partial B_{2^{m-1}r_{*}}}u\,d\sigma,

where
\displaystyle\fint_{\partial B_{r}(0)}f\,d\sigma=\frac{1}{2\pi r}\int_{\partial B_{r}(0)}f\,d\sigma

denotes the average of
f
over
\partial B_{r}(0)
.

Note that the condition (
1.5
) implies

{\rm{osc}}_{A_{m}}u\leq C\varepsilon_{0},\ \forall 1\leq m\leq K.

Now, by multiplying (
1.3
) by

u-h_{m}
and integrating the resulting equation over
A_{m}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{A_{m}}|\nabla(u-h_{m})|^{2}=\int_{\partial A_{m}}\big(\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}-h_{m}^{\prime}(r)\big)\cdot(u-h_{m})+\int_{A_{m}}A(u)(\nabla u,\nabla u)\cdot(u-h_{m})

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B_{2^{m}r_{*}}(0)}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\cdot(u-h_{m})-\int_{\partial B_{2^{m-1}r_{*}}(0)}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\cdot(u-h_{m})

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{A_{m}}A(u)(\nabla u,\nabla u)\cdot(u-h_{m})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\partial B_{2^{m}r_{*}}(0)}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\cdot(u-h_{m})-\int_{\partial B_{2^{m-1}r_{*}}(0)}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\cdot(u-h_{m})+C\varepsilon_{0}\int_{A_{m}}|\nabla u|^{2}.

Since
h_{m}
depends only on
r
,
we can apply (
2.3
) to obtain that

\int_{A_{m}}|\nabla(u-h_{m})|^{2}\geq\int_{A_{m}}\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\frac{\partial u}{\partial\theta}|^{2}\,d\sigma=\frac{1}{2}\int_{A_{m}}|\nabla u|^{2}.

Hence we have

(2.4)

(\frac{1}{2}-C\varepsilon_{0})\int_{A_{m}}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq\int_{\partial B_{2^{m}r_{*}}(0)}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\cdot(u-h_{m})-\int_{\partial B_{2^{m-1}r_{*}}(0)}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\cdot(u-h_{m})

By summing up (
2.4
) over

1\leq m\leq K
, we obtain that

\displaystyle(\frac{1}{2}-C\varepsilon_{0})\int_{B_{2^{K}r_{*}}(0)\setminus B_{r_{*}}(0)}|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\partial B_{2^{K}r_{*}}(0)}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\cdot(u-h_{K})

(2.5)

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\partial B_{r_{*}}(0)}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\cdot(u-h_{1}).

By Poincarè inequality, (
2.3
) and (
1.5
), the terms in the right hand side of (
2.5
) can
be estimated by

\displaystyle\big|\int_{\partial B_{2^{K}r_{*}}(0)}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\cdot(u-h_{K})\big|

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\int_{\partial B_{2^{K}r_{*}}(0)}|\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}|^{2}\,d\sigma\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\int_{\partial B_{2^{K}r_{*}}(0)}|u-h_{K}|^{2}\,d\sigma\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{K}r_{*}\Big(\int_{\partial B_{2^{K}r_{*}}(0)}|\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}|^{2}\,d\sigma\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{\partial B_{2^{K}r_{*}}(0)}\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\frac{\partial u}{\partial\theta}|^{2}\,d\sigma\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(2.6)

\displaystyle\leq C2^{K}r_{*}\int_{\partial B_{2^{K}r_{*}}(0)}|\nabla u|^{2}\,d\sigma\leq C\varepsilon_{0}^{2},

and, similarly,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\big|\int_{\partial B_{r_{*}}(0)}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\cdot(u-h_{1})\big|\leq Cr_{*}\int_{\partial B_{r_{*}}(0)}|\nabla u|^{2}\,d\sigma\leq C\varepsilon_{0}^{2}.

Substituting the inequalities (
2.6
) and (
2.7
)
into (
2.5
) yields

(2.8)

(\frac{1}{2}-C\varepsilon_{0})\int_{B_{2^{K}r_{*}}(0)\setminus B_{r_{*}}(0)}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq C\varepsilon_{0}^{2}.

Thus, by choosing
\varepsilon_{0}<\frac{1}{4C}
and observing
\frac{R_{*}}{2}\leq 2^{K}r_{*}\leq R_{*}
, we obtain that

(2.9)

\int_{B_{\frac{R_{*}}{2}}(0)\setminus B_{r_{*}}(0)}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq C\varepsilon_{0}^{2}.

Since (
2.9
) holds for any two
0<r_{*}<R_{*}\leq R
, we conclude that

(2.10)

\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}(0)}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq C\varepsilon_{0}^{2}<\infty.

Next, with the help of (
2.10
), we can repeat the above arguments to obtain the Hölder
continuity of
u
near
x=0
. In fact, after labeling
r=2^{K}r_{*}
so that
r_{*}=2^{-K}r
,
(
2.5
), (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) imply that for any
0<r<R
,

(2.11)

\int_{B_{r}(0)\setminus B_{2^{-K}r}(0)}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq Cr\int_{\partial B_{r}(0)}|\nabla u|^{2}\,d\sigma+C2^{-K}r\int_{\partial B_{2^{-K}r}(0)}|\nabla u|^{2}\,d\sigma.

On the other hand, it follows from (
2.10
) that

\lim_{K\to\infty}2^{-K}r\int_{\partial B_{2^{-K}r}(0)}|\nabla u|^{2}\,d\sigma=0.

Hence, after sending
K\to\infty
in (
2.11
), we obtain that for any
0<r<R
,

(2.12)

\int_{B_{r}(0)}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq Cr\int_{\partial B_{r}(0)}|\nabla u|^{2}\,d\sigma.

This implies that there exists an

\alpha\in(0,1)
such that

\int_{B_{r}(0)}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq(\frac{r}{R})^{2\alpha}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}(0)}|\nabla u|^{2},\ \ 0<r\leq\frac{R}{2}.

Hence
u\in C^{\alpha}(B_{\frac{R}{2}}(0))
. By the higher order regularity of harmonic maps,

u\in C^{\infty}(B_{\frac{R}{2}}(0))
(see, for example,
[
3
]
).
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.3
: It follows from Theorem
1.2
and (
2.12
) that

u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, and satisfies

(2.13)

\int_{B_{R}(0)}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq CR\int_{\partial B_{R}(0)}|\nabla u|^{2}\,d\sigma\leq C\varepsilon_{0}^{2},\ \forall R>0.

By choosing sufficiently small
\varepsilon_{0}
in (
2.13
) and applying the
\varepsilon_{0}
-gradient estimate for harmonic maps, we obtain that

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(0))}\leq\frac{C\varepsilon_{0}}{R},\ \forall R>0.

This, after sending
R\to\infty
, yields
u
must be constant.
∎

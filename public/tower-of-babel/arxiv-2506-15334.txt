Pencils of projective hypersurfaces, Griffiths heights and geometric invariant theory. I
Thomas Mordant

Abstract
. We study the Griffiths heights associated to the middle-dimensional cohomology of pencils of projective hypersurfaces, by comparing them to heights defined by means of geometric invariant theory (GIT). Kato and Koshikawa have conjectured a Northcott property for the Kato heights attached to motives over number fields, and investigated its consequences. Bounding these Griffiths heights in terms of the GIT heights would constitute a geometric counterpart, valid over function fields of characteristic zero, of Kato and Koshikawa’s conjecture. Part of our results follows from our earlier works [ Mor22 ] on the computation of these Griffiths heights in the case of pencils with generic singularities, and [ Mor24 ] on semistability criteria for singular projective hypersurfaces, combined with a general formalism of GIT heights over function fields. We also establish estimates between the Griffiths and GIT heights associated to pencils of projective hypersurfaces, which are valid beyond the case of generic singularities. To achieve this, we establish diverse results of independent interest. Notably we extend the computations in [ Mor22 ] to pencils of projective hypersurfaces with semihomogeneous singularities, and we show the lower semicontinuity of the stable Griffiths height attached to polarized variations of Hodge structures over complex algebraic curves.

1.1.

In

[
Fal83

Kato’s height of pure motives, specialized to motives of weight one, essentially coincides with Faltings’ height. Moreover Kato’s definition for general weights is designed to satisfy a property of invariance under isogenies which generalizes the invariance under isogenies of Faltings’ height. This invariance plays a central role in
[

However, while in the geometric case investigated by Zarhin, this invariance property is a direct consequence of the definition of the height, in the number field case considered by Faltings, its proof relies on the validity of the Weil conjecture for abelian varieties over finite fields and global class field theory. Kato’s proof of the invariance under isogenies of the height he attaches to motives over number fields relies on the general validity of the Weil conjecture as established by Deligne, and on the developments of
p
-adic cohomology and comparison theorems.

Kato and Koshikawa conjecture that the heights of pure motives they consider satisfy

[

Unfortunately, at this stage, nothing is known concerning the validity of these Northcott properties, except for motives of weight one, in which case it constitutes one of the most delicate technical points in Faltings’ paper
[
Fal83
]

[

1.4.

Curiously enough, although the geometric situation concerning Griffiths heights of variations of Hodge structures is supposed to be much more approachable than the arithmetic situation concerning Kato heights of motives, for a long time no significant result was apparently known about the former, with the exceptions of the weight one case — in which case the Griffiths height is basically the degree of the Hodge bundle of a pencil of abelian varieties — and of Peters’ positivity result.

With the long-term motivation of getting some better understanding of Kato heights of motives, in
[
Mor22
]
we investigated the Griffiths heights associated to some significant instances of variations of Hodge structures of geometric origin and of arbitrary weight — namely the middle-dimensional cohomology of pencils of projective hypersurfaces.

More precisely, we computed the stable Griffiths height
{\mathrm{ht}}_{GK,stab}\big(\mathbb{H}^{N-1}(H_{\eta}/C_{\eta})\big)
(along with its upper and lower variants) of the relative cohomology
\mathbb{H}^{N-1}(H_{\eta}/C_{\eta})
attached to a pencil of generically smooth hypersurfaces
H
in a projective bundle over a smooth projective complex curve
C

\pi:\mathbb{P}(E):={\rm Proj\,}_{C}S^{\bullet}E^{\vee}\longrightarrow C.

Observe that the fibers of

and have interesting cohomology groups only in middle dimension

Our computation was achieved when the pencil

had “generic singularities” in the following sense: the total space, of dimension

The simplicity of the proportionality relation (

Let us briefly recall the definition of

The invariant

Observe that the isomorphism in (

The comparison (

The first one is the computation in

The second result is a comparison of

, the following equality holds:

(1.7)

{\mathrm{ht}}_{GIT}(H_{\eta})={\mathrm{ht}}_{int}(H/C).

Actually we show in Sections

1.7.

In this paper, we also investigate the relation between

Our computation of

1.8.

These results, based on the computation of

1.9.

Finally we would like to emphasize the exploratory character of this work.

When writing this paper, our primary goal has been to gather some material relevant to the understanding of the Griffiths and Kato heights and their Northcott properties, concerning significant classes of variations of Hodge structures or motives of weight
>1.
According to the general principle claiming that geometry over function fields is simpler than over number fields, and therefore has to be studied first, we focused on the former — that is, on Griffiths heights.

It is rather remarkable that this limited goal leads to various basic questions in complex algebraic and analytic geometry that apparently had not been investigated in the literature — for example the existence of criteria for the semistability of singular projective hypersurfaces of arbitrary dimension and degree, as the ones established in
[
Mor24
]
; or the lower semicontinuity property of the stable Griffiths height established in Section
7
.

Actually, to keep this paper within a limited number of pages, several of these basic questions have been incompletely investigated. This is for instance the case for the construction of heights over function fields using geometric invariant theory and their relations to boundedness properties. A more satisfactory approach to these problems would involve the formalism of good moduli spaces associated to algebraic stacks, as developed by Alper, Halpern-Leistner and Heinloth
[
Alp13
,
AHLH23
]
. We plan to return to this topic in some future work.

The stable Griffiths height of a polarized variation of Hodge structures over a complex algebraic curve

2.1.
The Griffiths line bundle of a variation of Hodge structures and Peters’ construction

2.1.1.

Let
S
be a smooth complex manifold,
{\mathcal{V}}
a vector bundle on
S
and

F^{\bullet}:=F^{0}:=\mathcal{V}\supseteq F^{1}\supseteq\dots\supseteq F^{n}\supseteq F^{n+1}=0

a decreasing filtration by subbundles. As in
[
Gri70
]
, we can attach to
({\mathcal{V}},F^{\bullet})
its
Griffiths line bundle
:

\mathcal{{\mathcal{G}K}}_{S}({\mathcal{V}},F^{\bullet}):=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\det F^{i}\simeq\bigotimes_{r=0}^{n}(\det F^{r}/F^{r+1})^{\otimes r}.

If
C
is a connected smooth projective complex curve, the
Griffiths height

{\mathrm{ht}}_{GK}({\mathcal{V}},F^{\bullet})
is simply the degree of this line bundle.

Using Deligne’s work on extensions of analytic vector bundles with connections admitting only regular singular points, Peters showed how to extend this definition to integral variations of Hodge structures defined on non-empty Zariski open subsets of a connected smooth projective complex curve.

2.1.2.
Peters’ construction: the unipotent case

Let
C
be a connected smooth projective complex curve with generic point
\eta
,
\Delta
a finite subset of
C,
and
{\mathaccent 23{C}}:=C\smallsetminus\Delta
its complement. Let also
\mathbb{V}=(V_{\mathbb{Z}},{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet})
be an integral variation of Hodge structures (VHS) defined on
{\mathaccent 23{C}},
where
V_{\mathbb{Z}}
denotes a local system of finitely generated free
\mathbb{Z}
-modules over
{\mathaccent 23{C}}
, and
{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet}
the Hodge filtration on the associated analytic vector bundle:

{\mathcal{V}}:=V_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{{\mathaccent 23{C}}}}{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathrm{an}}_{{\mathaccent 23{C}}}.

Let
\nabla
be the connection on the vector bundle
{\mathcal{V}}
defined by the local system
V_{\mathbb{Z}}.

First, in the case where the local monodromy
T_{x}
of the connection
\nabla
at every point
x
of
\Delta
is a unipotent endomorphism, the vector bundle with connection
({\mathcal{V}},\nabla)
on
{\mathaccent 23{C}}
admits a canonical Deligne extension
(\overline{{\mathcal{V}}},\overline{\nabla})
to
C
, where:

\overline{\nabla}:\overline{{\mathcal{V}}}\longrightarrow\overline{{\mathcal{V}}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathrm{an}}_{C}}\Omega^{1}_{C}(\Delta)

is a logarithmic connection. This extension is characterized by the property that, for every point
x
in
\Delta,
the residue endomorphism of the logarithmic connection
\overline{\nabla}
at
x
is nilpotent.

According to a result of Griffiths
[
Sch73
, Theorem (4.13), a)]
, the subbundles
({\mathcal{F}}^{p})_{p\geq 0}
of the vector bundle
{\mathcal{V}}
on
{\mathaccent 23{C}}
are algebraic with respect to the algebraic structure defined by the vector bundle
\overline{{\mathcal{V}}}
on
C.
Consequently they admit extensions
(\overline{{\mathcal{F}}}^{p})_{p\geq 0}
as subbundles of
\overline{{\mathcal{V}}}
.

The
\emph{Griffithslinebundle}
of the variation of Hodge structures
\mathbb{V}
is the line bundle on
C
defined by:

\mathcal{GK}_{C}(\mathbb{V}_{\eta}):=\mathcal{GK}_{C}(\overline{{\mathcal{V}}},\overline{{\mathcal{F}}}^{\bullet}),

and the
Griffiths height

{\mathrm{ht}}_{GK}(\mathbb{V}_{\eta})
of
\mathbb{V}
is the degree of this line bundle:

{\mathrm{ht}}_{GK}(\mathbb{V}_{\eta}):=\deg_{C}\mathcal{GK}_{C}(\mathbb{V}_{\eta}).

This line bundle and this height only depend on the restriction of the variation of Hodge structures
to any non-empty Zariski open subset of
C
, or equivalently to any open neighborhood in
C
of the generic point
\eta
. This justifies the notation.

The construction of
\mathcal{GK}_{C}(\mathbb{V}_{\eta})
and of
{\mathrm{ht}}_{GK}(\mathbb{V}_{\eta})
is also compatible with base change in the following sense. If
C^{\prime}
is a connected smooth projective complex curve with generic point
\eta^{\prime},
and if

\sigma:C^{\prime}\rightarrow C

is a finite morphism, then there is a canonical isomorphism of line bundles on
C^{\prime}
:

\mathcal{GK}_{C^{\prime}}\big((\sigma^{*}\mathbb{V})_{\eta^{\prime}}\big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sigma^{*}\mathcal{GK}_{C}(\mathbb{V}_{\eta}),

and consequently the following equality of integers holds:

(2.1)

{\mathrm{ht}}_{GK}\big((\sigma^{*}\mathbb{V})_{\eta^{\prime}}\big)=\deg(\sigma)\,{\mathrm{ht}}_{GK}(\mathbb{V}_{\eta}).

2.1.3.
Peters’ construction: the general case

When the local monodromy is not unipotent, the construction of Deligne extensions of vector bundles with connections depends on the determination of a logarithm (from
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
to
\mathbb{C}
).

There are two natural such choices,

12

12

They are defined by the determinations of the complex logarithm with values in

and have the same meromorphic structure at

over the complex algebraic curve

According to the result of Griffiths cited above, the subbundles
({\mathcal{F}}^{p})_{p\geq 0}
of the vector bundle

on

are again algebraic with respect to this algebraic structure and consequently admit extensions

This leads to the definition of the upper and lower Griffiths line bundles:

\mathcal{GK}_{C,\pm}(\mathbb{V}_{\eta}):=\mathcal{GK}_{C}(\overline{{\mathcal{V}}}_{\pm},\overline{{\mathcal{F}}}^{\bullet}_{\pm}),

and the upper and lower Griffiths heights:

Peters specifically studied the upper variant in

In contrast, it follows from Steenbrink’s theory (see

and the attached extensions

of the subbundles

are canonically isomorphic to the relative logarithmic cohomology of a compactification

of

over

whose singular fibers are divisors with strict normal crossings, equipped with its natural Hodge filtration defined in terms of the relative logarithmic de Rham complex.

2.2.
The stable Griffiths height

In general, the construction of the Deligne extensions, and consequently of the upper and lower Griffiths heights,
is not compatible with base change under finite morphisms of curves. In other words, the equality (
2.1
) does not hold in general with
{\mathrm{ht}}_{GK,+}
or
{\mathrm{ht}}_{GK,-}
instead of
{\mathrm{ht}}_{GK}
when the local monodromy at
\Delta
is not unipotent.

To fix this issue, as in
[
Mor22
]
, one can introduce
the so-called
stable Griffiths height

{\mathrm{ht}}_{GK,stab}(\mathbb{V}_{\eta}),
the definition of which is inspired by the definition of the stable Faltings height of abelian varieties.

To define
{\mathrm{ht}}_{GK,stab}(\mathbb{V}_{\eta}),
recall that,
according to a result of Borel
[
Sch73
, Lemma (4.5)]
, the local monodromy
T_{x}
of the connection
\nabla
at every point
x
of
\Delta
is a quasi-unipotent endomorphism, namely there exists an integer
r_{x}\geq 1
such that the endomorphism
T_{x}^{r_{x}}
is unipotent. Let
C^{\prime}
be a connected smooth projective complex curve and

\sigma:C^{\prime}\rightarrow C

a finite morphism that is ramified at every point
x^{\prime}
of
\Delta^{\prime}:=\sigma^{-1}(\Delta)
with ramification index a multiple of
r_{\sigma(x^{\prime})}.
Such a ramified covering
C^{\prime}
of
C
may be constructed as a cyclic covering of
C
. Moreover any two such ramified covering
C^{\prime}_{1}
and
C^{\prime}_{2}
are dominated by a third one
C^{\prime\prime}
.

According to the ramification assumption, the local monodromy at every point of
\Delta^{\prime}
of the pulled back vector bundle with connection
(\sigma^{*}{\mathcal{V}},\sigma^{*}{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet})
on
\sigma^{-1}({\mathaccent 23{C}})
is unipotent. Consequently it admits a canonical Deligne extension, and as before, we can define the Griffiths line bundle

and the Griffiths height

The

of the variation of Hodge structures

is the rational number defined by:

{\mathrm{ht}}_{GK,stab}(\mathbb{V}_{\eta}):=\deg(\sigma)^{-1}\,{\mathrm{ht}}_{GK}\big((\sigma^{*}\mathbb{V})_{\eta^{\prime}}\big)=\deg(\sigma)^{-1}\,\deg_{C^{\prime}}\mathcal{GK}_{C^{\prime}}\big((\sigma^{*}\mathbb{V})_{\eta^{\prime}}\big).

This rational number indeed does not depend on the choice of the finite covering

By construction, the height

is compatible with base change, in the sense that (

of

Let

6.1.
The GIT quotient

In this section, we review some basic facts concerning the geometric invariant theory associated to the restriction

6.1.1.

The

Let us consider its graded sub-algebra of invariant sections:

(6.1)

\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k},{\mathcal{O}}(\delta))^{SL_{e,k}}
.

Let
\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k,us,\delta}
be the closed subscheme of
\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k}
defined as the vanishing locus of the invariant polynomials
I_{1},\dots,I_{p}
. The support
|\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k,us,\delta}|
is precisely the intersection of the supports of the base loci of the linear systems
\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k},{\mathcal{O}}(i))^{SL_{e,k}}
for
i>0
, and therefore does not depend on the choice of
\delta
. The
k
-points of this support are called the
unstable

k
-points of
\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k}
endowed with this linearized action of
SL_{e,k}
.

The
semistable locus
of
\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k}
endowed with this linearized action of
SL_{e,k}
is defined to be its open subscheme, complement of the closed subscheme
\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k,us,\delta}
:

\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k,ss}:=\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k}\setminus\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k,us,\delta}=\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k}\setminus(I_{1}=\dots=I_{p}=0)\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k}.

It is also the complement of the intersection of the base loci of the linear systems
\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k},{\mathcal{O}}(i))^{SL_{e,k}}
for
i>0.

Consequently the inclusion of graded algebras (
6.1
)
defines a
k
-morphism:

q:\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k,ss}\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k}/\!/SL_{e,k}:={\rm Proj\,}\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k},{\mathcal{O}}(i))^{SL_{e,k}}.

The closed subscheme
\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k,us,\delta}
and the open subscheme
\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k,ss}
are stable under the action of
SL_{e,k}
, and the morphism
q
is
SL_{e,k}
-invariant.

For conciseness’ sake, we shall use the following notation:

M(\rho):=\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k}/\!/SL_{e,k}.

6.1.2.

The morphism
q
admits the following more concrete description.

There is a canonical
k
-isomorphism:

(6.2)

M(\rho):={\rm Proj\,}\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k},{\mathcal{O}}(i))^{SL_{e,k}}\simeq{\rm Proj\,}\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k},{\mathcal{O}}(i\delta))^{SL_{e,k}},

and the canonical surjective morphism of graded
k
-algebras:

S^{\bullet}\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k},{\mathcal{O}}(\delta))^{SL_{e,k}}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k},{\mathcal{O}}(i\delta))^{SL_{e,k}}

induces a closed immersion of
k
-schemes:

(6.3)

{\rm Proj\,}\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k},{\mathcal{O}}(i\delta))^{SL_{e,k}}{\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow}\mathbb{P}\big(\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k},{\mathcal{O}}(\delta))^{SL_{e,k}\vee}\big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{P}^{p-1}_{k},

where the last isomorphism is induced by the basis
(I_{1},\dots,I_{p})
of
\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k},{\mathcal{O}}(\delta))^{SL_{e,k}}
.

Composed with the isomorphism (
6.2
) and the closed immersion (
6.3
), the morphism
q
becomes the morphism defined by the invariant polynomials
I_{1},\dots,I_{p}
:

[I_{1}:\cdots:I_{p}]:\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k,ss}\overset{q}{\longrightarrow}M(\rho)=\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k}/\!/SL_{e,k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm Proj\,}\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k},{\mathcal{O}}(i\delta))^{SL_{e,k}}\,{\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow}\mathbb{P}^{p-1}_{k}

defined by:

[I_{1}:\cdots:I_{p}]([x_{1}:\cdots:x_{v}]):=[I_{1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{v}):\dots:I_{p}(x_{1},\dots,x_{v})].

6.1.3.

Observe that by construction, there is a canonical isomorphism of line bundles over
\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k,ss}
:

q^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{p-1}_{k}}(1)_{|M(\rho)}\simeq[I_{1}:\cdots:I_{p}]^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{p-1}_{k}}(1)\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k,ss}}(\delta).

Consequently, denoting by
L
the
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{p-1}_{k}}(1)_{|M(\rho)}^{\otimes 1/\delta}
on
M(\rho)
, there is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundles over
\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k,ss}
:

q^{*}L\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k,ss}}(1).

The isomorphism class of the
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle

does not depend on the choice of the integer
\delta
or of the invariant polynomials
I_{1},\dots,I_{p}.

6.2.
Homogeneous representations of linear groups, twisting of vector bundles, and geometric invariant theory over a general base

6.2.1.

Recall that, if
S
is a
k
-scheme, the theory of principal bundles (see for instance
[
Bog78
]
) allows one to associate a vector bundle
E^{\rho}
of rank
v
over
S
to any vector bundle
E
of rank
e
over
S
.

If
E
may be trivialized over a Zariski open covering
(U_{i})_{i\in I}
of
S,
and therefore may be defined by some cocycle
(h_{ij})_{(i,j)\in I^{2}},
with

h_{ij}:U_{i}\cap U_{j}\longrightarrow GL_{e,k}

a
k
-morphism, then the vector bundle
E^{\rho}
may be defined by the cocycle
(\rho\circ h_{ij})_{(i,j)\in I^{2}}
with values in
GL_{v,k}
. This construction is compatible with isomorphisms of vector bundles, and with arbitrary base change of the
k
-scheme
S.

6.2.2.

From now on, we assume that
the morphism of
k
-linear groups
\rho
is a homogeneous representation
.
In other words, we assume that there exists
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}
— the
weight
of
\rho
— such that, for every
t
in
k^{\ast}
, the following equality holds in
GL_{v}(k)
:

\rho(t\,{\mathrm{Id}}_{e})=t^{\gamma}\,{\mathrm{Id}}_{v}.

This implies that the subscheme
\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k,ss}
of
\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k}
is stable under the action of
GL_{e,k}
and that the morphism
q
is
GL_{e,k}
-invariant.

It also implies that, for any positive integer
\delta
such that
\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k},{\mathcal{O}}(\delta))^{SL_{e,k}}\neq\{0\},
the following divisibility holds:

e\mid\gamma\delta.

The construction of
\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k,ss}
and of
q
admits the following relative version valid over an arbitrary
k
-scheme.

Proposition 6.1
.

To any
k
-scheme
S
and to any vector bundle
E
of rank
e
over
S
, it is possible to associate, in a unique way, an open subscheme
\mathbb{P}(E^{\rho})_{ss}
of
the
S
-scheme
\mathbb{P}(E^{\rho})
and a

-morphism

such that the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

If

and

, then
\mathbb{P}(E^{\rho})_{ss}=\mathbb{P}^{v-1}_{k,ss}
and
q_{S,E}=q
.

(2)

Let
\sigma:S^{\prime}\rightarrow S
be a morphism of
k
-schemes,

(resp.

) a vector bundle of rank

over
S
(resp. over
S^{\prime}
), and

\varphi:E^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sigma^{\ast}E

. If we denote by

\varphi^{\rho}:E^{\prime\rho}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}(\sigma^{\ast}E)^{\rho}\simeq\sigma^{\ast}(E^{\rho})

and

\mathbb{P}(\varphi^{\rho}):\mathbb{P}(E^{\prime\rho})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{P}(E^{\rho})\times_{S,\sigma}S^{\prime}

the associated isomorphisms of vector bundles and of projective bundles over
S^{\prime}
,
then the following equality of open subschemes of
\mathbb{P}(E^{\rho})\times_{S,\sigma}S^{\prime}
holds:

\mathbb{P}(\varphi^{\rho})(\mathbb{P}(E^{\prime\rho})_{ss})=\mathbb{P}(E^{\rho})_{ss}\times_{S,\sigma}S^{\prime},

and the following diagram is commutative:

\textstyle{\mathbb{P}(E^{\prime\rho})_{ss}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathbb{P}(\varphi^{\rho})}
\scriptstyle{\sim}
\scriptstyle{q_{S^{\prime},E^{\prime}}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{P}(E^{\rho})_{ss}\times_{S,\sigma}S^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbb{P}(E^{\rho})_{ss}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q_{S,E}}
\textstyle{M(\rho)}

where the second horizontal arrow is the first projection.

Moreover, for every
k
-scheme
S
and every vector bundle

of rank

over

, we have an isomorphism of
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundles over
\mathbb{P}(E^{\rho})_{ss}
, where

denotes the structural morphism:

q_{S,E}^{\ast}L\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\big({\mathcal{O}}_{E^{\rho}}(1)\otimes\pi^{\ast}(\det E)^{\otimes\gamma/e}\big)_{|\mathbb{P}(E^{\rho})_{ss}}.

This isomorphism is canonical up to some roots of unity, and equivariant with respect to the natural
GL(E)
-linearizations of its source and its range.

This is a straightforward consequence of the

-invariance of the morphism

and of the definition of the twisted vector bundles

, and we leave the details to the interested reader. Actually, with the notation of

, for every

and

as above, there is a canonical isomorphism of line bundles on

:

(6.4)

q_{S,E}^{\ast}L^{\otimes\delta}:=q_{S,E}^{\ast}{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{p-1}_{k}}(1)_{\mid M(\rho)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}({\mathcal{O}}_{E^{\rho}}(\delta)\otimes\pi^{\ast}(\det E)^{\otimes\gamma\delta/e})_{|\mathbb{P}(E^{\rho})_{ss}}.

This construction already appears, at least implicitly, in the work of Bogomolov

. It is a geometric analogue of the construction of the semistable locus and quotient of a projective bundle over the ring of integers of a number field in

6.3.

In this subsection, we consider a connected smooth projective curve
C
with generic point
\eta
over
k

is

to

Recall that if

As in Subsection

With the above notation, for every point

where

7.
Expression as an integral and lower semicontinuity of the stable Griffiths height

In this section we recall Peters’ expression of the Griffiths height of a polarized variation of Hodge structures with unipotent monodromy as an integral of the Chern form on the Griffiths line bundle defined by the Hodge metric. This expression also computes the stable Griffiths height when the monodromy is not assumed to be unipotent.

Using this result, we will show a lower semicontinuity property for the stable Griffiths height under deformation of the variation of Hodge structures. This property will be used in Subsection

is even, by:

\displaystyle F_{stab}(d,N)

\displaystyle:=\frac{N+1}{24d^{2}}\left[(d-1)^{N}(d^{2}N+2d^{2}-2dN-2)-2(d^{2}-1)\right]

\displaystyle=(N+1)(d-1)/(24d^{2})\left[(d-1)^{N-1}(d^{2}N+2d^{2}-2dN-2)-2(d+1)\right].

For every point
P
in
\Sigma
, the integer
\delta_{P}
is at least 2, and therefore according to Proposition
8.1
the following inequality of rational numbers holds:

12\,w_{N,\delta_{P}}(\delta_{P}-1)^{-N}=g_{N}(\delta_{P})\leq g_{N}(2)=(1/4)[2N+1+3(-1)^{N}].

We can rewrite it as follows:

(8.4)

w_{N,\delta_{P}}\leq\big((\delta_{P}-1)^{N}/48\big)[2N+1+3(-1)^{N}].

Consequently, applying equality (
5.2
), the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle{\mathrm{ht}}_{GK,stab}\big(\mathbb{H}^{N-1}(H_{\eta}/C_{\eta})\big)

\displaystyle=-(N+1)w_{N,d}\;{\mathrm{ht}}_{int}(H/C)+\sum_{P\in\Sigma}w_{N,\delta_{P}}

\displaystyle\leq-(N+1)w_{N,d}\;{\mathrm{ht}}_{int}(H/C)+\sum_{P\in\Sigma}\big((\delta_{P}-1)^{N}/48\big)[2N+1+3(-1)^{N}]

(8.5)

\displaystyle=-(N+1)w_{N,d}\;{\mathrm{ht}}_{int}(H/C)

\displaystyle\quad+(1/48)\,[2N+1+3(-1)^{N}](N+1)(d-1)^{N}{\mathrm{ht}}_{int}(H/C)

\displaystyle=F(d,N)\;{\mathrm{ht}}_{int}(H/C),

where in (

), we have applied equality (
5.1
), and where
F(d,N)
is the rational number defined by:

\displaystyle F(d,N)

\displaystyle:=-(N+1)w_{N,d}+(N+1)(d-1)^{N}/48\,[2N+1+3(-1)^{N}]

\displaystyle=-(N+1)(d-1)/(12d^{2})\,[(Nd+1)(d-1)^{N-1}+(-1)^{N}(d+1)]

\displaystyle\quad+(N+1)(d-1)^{N}/48\,[2N+1+3(-1)^{N}]

\displaystyle=(N+1)(d-1)/(48d^{2})\,[-4(Nd+1)(d-1)^{N-1}-4(-1)^{N}(d+1)

\displaystyle\quad+(d-1)^{N-1}d^{2}(2N+1+3(-1)^{N})]

\displaystyle=(N+1)(d-1)/(48d^{2})\,[(d-1)^{N-1}(2d^{2}N+d^{2}(1+3(-1)^{N})-4dN-4)-4(-1)^{N}(d+1)].

If
N
is odd, this is given by:

\displaystyle F(d,N)

\displaystyle=(N+1)(d-1)/(48d^{2})\,[(d-1)^{N-1}(2d^{2}N-2d^{2}-4dN-4)+4(d+1)]

\displaystyle=(N+1)(d-1)/(24d^{2})\,[(d-1)^{N-1}(d^{2}N-d^{2}-2dN-2)+2(d+1)]

\displaystyle=F_{stab}(d,N),

and if
N
is even, by:

\displaystyle F(d,N)

\displaystyle=(N+1)(d-1)/(48d^{2})\,[(d-1)^{N-1}(2d^{2}N+4d^{2}-4dN-4)-4(d+1)]

\displaystyle=(N+1)(d-1)/(24d^{2})\,[(d-1)^{N-1}(d^{2}N+2d^{2}-2dN-2)-2(d+1)]

\displaystyle=F_{stab}(d,N).

In both cases,
F(d,N)
is precisely

, as wanted.

The assertions in Proposition

concerning the equality cases follow from the above proof and from the observations concerning the decreasing character of

stated after Proposition

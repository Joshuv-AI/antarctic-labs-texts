Visual analysis of college students’ suicide based on Web of Science and CiteSpace software: A bibliometric review from 2002 to 2022
Authors: Xun Huang, Seydaduong Them, Aizhong Liu, Shi Wu Wen, Jing Deng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:College students’ suicide is a major global public health problem. However, no visualization study on college students’ suicide has been reported. This study aimed to explore the hotspots and emerging trends in the field.Methods:Relevant publications between 2002 and 2022 were searched from the Web of Science Core Collection on April 1, 2023. CiteSpace, Microsoft Excel, and Joinpoint software were used to analyze research trends and hotspots in this field.Results:A total of 2262 publications were included in this study. The number of publications related to college students’ suicide has increased rapidly and may increase exponentially in the future. The United States led the way in this field, and the top 10 institutions were all from the United States. However, cooperation between different institutions was weak. The authors with the most publications and the most cited author were Hirsch Jameson K and Beck AT, respectively. Journal of Affective Disorders and Suicide and Life-Threatening Behavior were the most published and cited journals in this field. A keyword cluster analysis revealed research focuses on suicidal ideation and thoughts, suicide prevention, medical students, mental health, perceived burdensomeness, stigma, and mobile health.Conclusion:In conclusion, the study of college student suicide is receiving increasing attention from researchers. Cooperation and communication between countries, institutions, and authors need to be strengthened to accelerate in-depth research in this field. The results of this study can provide guidance and reference for researchers in choosing research directions in this field.

1. Introduction

As a public health issue, suicide is one of the most pressing problems. It is considered the second leading cause of death among young adults, with a death rate of 11.5 per 100,000 people. According to the World Health Organization’s report, around 800,000 people commit suicide each year, and nearly 1 million people die by suicide as a result.[1] College students have a lower suicide rate than the general population. However, suicide is the second leading cause of death among college students.[2] The suicide rate among college students was 7.5 per 100,000 population in 1997.[3] One national survey showed that 10.3% of college students considered suicide, 6.7% planned suicide, and 1.5% attempted suicide at least once.[4] It is reported that the suicide rate among college students in 2022 was higher than that from 2007 to 2021.[5] The increasing number of college student suicides will place a heavy burden on the country, the community, and the family.[6] To prevent the occurrence of college student suicide, there have been a lot of studies on college student suicide.[6–11] However, research hotspots and frontiers of this field remain unclear.

A systematic review of suicide among college students has been conducted.[12] However, it is difficult to systematically analyze the multidimensional state of suicide research and to propose new research directions through traditional reviews. Bibliometrics provides a method for systematically addressing this multidimensional review. As far as bibliometric analysis is concerned, a previous study has been conducted regarding student suicide.[13] This study highlights the need for more research on students’ suicide. More research in this area is needed to increase awareness among students. However, this study includes elementary, secondary, and university students. College students are different from elementary and middle school students. Students in college are in their lives toward adulthood, during which mental health problems are most common and suicide can easily occur.[14] Hence, a quantitative bibliometric analysis is necessary to systematically describe what has been done in college student suicide research and to propose new directions. CiteSpace is an excellent tool for bibliometric and visualization analysis of literature and has been widely used to summarize the hotspots and research trends in various fields of public health.[15–18] It is a Java application for data analysis developed by Chen Chaomei of Drexel University.[19]

Based on related methods of bibliometrics and statistics, the research adopted visual means to analyze the literature characteristics and hotspot evolution in the field of college student suicide. The knowledge base and research process of the literature are displayed visually by studying the annual publication number and trend, distribution of frequently publishing countries, distribution of hot authors, distribution of hot journals, distribution of frequently publishing institutions, used keywords, highly cited literature, and research hotspot evolution, and future development trends are explored based on cluster analysis and timeline views.

To sum up, there have been studies on the literature analysis of college student suicide. However, due to the defects of analysis methods and other reasons, the literature characteristics, research direction, research depth, and research hotspots of college student suicide research are still not comprehensive enough, and more systematic results are needed. Therefore, the purpose of this study is to provide those who are involved in related research with literature assistance and call on research institutions at home and abroad to pay attention to the research in the field. The results of this study will help clarify the evolution law and development direction of college student suicide and provide a reference for subsequent research in this field.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Data sources and search strategies

The Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) is the most frequently used source of scientific information.[20] Specifically, the data were collected from the WoSCC, including the Science Citation Index Expanded, Current Chemical Reactions Expanded, and Index Chemicus. Thus, the data for our study were extracted from the WoSCC.

The search strategy was as follows: TS(Topics) = (college students OR university students OR undergraduate students) AND ((TS = (suicide OR suicidal OR suicidality)) OR (AB = (suicide OR suicidal OR suicidality))). Our search covered the period from January 1, 2002, to December 31, 2022. As shown in Figure 1, we initially obtained 2582 articles using the search terms. Secondly, in order to improve the accuracy and repeatability of the study, and considering the analysis characteristics of CiteSpace software, we excluded 219 unrelated publications (early access; meeting abstracts; editorial material; processing papers; letters; revisions; and data papers). Next, considering the readability of the articles, the 101 non-English publications were filtered out. Finally, 2262 publications (2171 articles and 91 reviews) were analyzed in our study. To avoid bias in the database update, the information from 2262 records was downloaded in the form of a plain text file on April 1, 2023.

Flowchart showing the process of the selection of publications included in this study. TS = Topics.

2.2. Analyzing tools and statistical methods

The selected studies were downloaded and exported to CiteSpace (version 6.2.R2; Drexel University) and Microsoft Excel (version 2016; Miscrosoft Corporation, Redmond) for further analysis. References and full records were included for each study. Microsoft Excel was used to draw the bar chart showing the overall trend of published papers by year. The Joinpoint Regression Program (version 4.9.1.0, National Cancer Institute) was used to analyze the temporal trends in the number of published papers.

By generating a series of visual knowledge maps, CiteSpace explores the research status, research hotspots, research frontiers, and evolution process of a scientific field, to reveal the research direction, research stage, and frontier characteristics of institutions and authors, and finally judge the development trend of this field.[19] Nodes in different maps represent authors, institutions, countries, or keywords. The size of nodes indicates the frequency of occurrence or citation, and the color of nodes indicates the occurrence or citation years. Besides, nodes with purple trims suggest high betweenness centrality, which are often identified as hotspots or turning points in a field.

Microsoft Excel was used to draw the bar chart showing the overall trend of published papers by year. The trend of literature publication can be intuitively understood through the bar chart. The Joinpoint regression model is a statistical method for time-series data analysis that identifies and quantifies Joinpoints in the data to segment the data into different trend segments. In this study, the Joinpoint regression model was used to analyze the trend of the number of published documents, which enables researchers to identify and explain the trend change in the number of published documents more clearly, to provide valuable information for research and practice in related fields. A visualization analysis, including annual publications, countries, institutions, authors, cited authors, journals, cited journals, references, and keywords, was carried out by CiteSpace. CiteSpace parameters were as follows: timespan (2002–2022), time slice (1 year), selection criteria (top 50), pruning (pathfinder and pruning sliced networks), and visualization (cluster view-static and show merged network).

This study does not require ethical approval because no human/patient data were used. The authors declare that the data will not be disclosed to anyone else.

3. Results

3.1. Analysis of annual publication number and trend

A total of 2262 publications were included in the analysis. We summarized the number of publications from 2002 to 2022. The number of published papers each year has risen from 20 in 2002 to 252 in 2022. An exponential fitting model was used to create a time curve of the number of publications to predict the future global publication trend. We can see from Figure 2 that the fitting curve was y = 13.18e0.1531x (R2 = 0.9762), indicating that the number of global publications in the coming years might increase exponentially. Figure 2 shows the detailed information.

The annual number and trend of publications from 2002 to 2022.

As shown in Figure 2, overall, the number of published papers continuously grew. According to the Joinpoint linear regression model, this can be roughly divided into 4 stages (2002–2010; 2011–2016; 2017–2019; 2020–2022) and 3 joinpoints (2011; 2017; 2020). All 3 stages showed an increasing trend, except for the fourth stage (2020–2022), when the number of publications issued was on a downward trend. In the first stage (2002–2010), the annual number of published papers remained flat. Then, in the second stage, the number of published papers increased rapidly. Furthermore, during the third stage, the years after 2017 witnessed a sharp rise in publication numbers. However, there was a decreasing trend in the number of issued papers from 2020 to 2022. This shows that research in the field of college student suicide has been given more and more attention by researchers, but it still lacks emphasis from scholars.

In general, most of the authors are scattered, indicating that few scholars in the international community study college student suicide, scientific research teams lack academic communication, and their research is relatively independent and lacks cooperation.

Based on the top 20 keywords in Table 4, it can be seen that researchers in the field of college student suicide are mainly interested in mental health (depression, anxiety, stress, hopelessness), suicidal ideation, prevalence, suicidal risk, and suicidal behavior among college students. The LLR method was utilized to cluster keywords in the present study. Modularity Q values ranged from 0 to 1, with a value >0.3 indicating an efficient cluster. The S value suggests cluster homogeneity, and an S value > 0.5 indicates a reasonable cluster. The Q value of the keyword cluster was 0.3105 (>0.3), and the S value was 0.5952 (>0.5), suggesting that the cluster was reasonable and effective. All the keywords in this study were classified into 7 clusters (Fig. 5): #0 “suicidal ideation,” #1 “suicide prevention,” #2 “ideation,” #3 “mental health,” #4 “medical health,” #5 “perceived burdensomeness,” and #6 “mHealth (mobile health).” According to Figure 5, keyword clusters such as “suicide ideation,” “suicide prevention,” “ideation,” “mental health,” and “medical health” appeared in 2002 and persisted for 21 years. Keywords clusters “perceived burdensomeness” and “mHealth” appeared in 2005 and 2012, respectively, and persist in 2022.

4.1. Publication number and trend

In this study, we found 20 publications in 2002 versus 252 publications in 2022, corresponding to a 12.6-fold increase. Such a phenomenon is similar to the findings of Astraud, LP et al. Astraud et al’s bibliometric analysis of suicide from 1989 to 2018 found a 6.1-fold increase in the number of publications from 1989 to 2018.[21] According to the Joinpoint regression model, the years 2011 (increase), 2017 (increase), and 2020 (decrease) were 3 significant joinpoints in the trend of annual publication numbers. In order to achieve the global goal of reducing suicide rates by 10% by 2020, as stated in the World Health Organization’s Mental Health Action Plan for 2013 to 2020, more scholars joined suicide research.[22] However, 1 possible explanation for the decrease in 2020 is the emergence of coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19), and many scholars have turned to COVID-19 studies. This suggests that researchers still do not pay enough attention to the relevant research on college student suicide, because it is easily influenced by the external environment.

4.2. Countries and institutions

This study shows that US-related institutions are the leading research institutions in the field of college student suicide. Results of this study showed that the top 10 institutions in the field of college students’ suicide are all from the United States, with the State University System of Florida, the University of Michigan, and the University of Michigan System as the main research institutions (see Table S1, which shows that the top 10 institutions with the largest number of publications). This result is not only related to the level of science in the United States but also to the fact that suicide is the second leading cause of death among college students in the United States.[9] Moreover, our research revealed that the United States, Australia, and England have engaged in significant collaboration with other nations. However, we have also discovered that various institutions lack collaboration. Such findings may be attributed to the unique national conditions of each country. Cooperation between different countries or institutions is necessary if the global public health problem of college students’ suicide is to be prevented.

4.3. Authors and cited authors

Professor Hirsch, Jameson K from the Department of Psychology, East Tennessee State University, and Professor Chang, Edward C from the Department of Psychology, University of Michigan, are the 2 authors with the highest number of publications. Nevertheless, the centrality of these 2 authors was 0.02 and 0, respectively (see Table S2, which demonstrates that Network of cooperation among authors and co-cited authors), indicating insufficient collaboration with other authors. Surprisingly, there was much collaboration between Professor Hirsch, Jameson K, and Chang, Edward C, who jointly conducted studies related to suicide risk among college students. For example, they studied the roles that dispositional optimism and coping strategies play in suicide risk (viz., suicidality) and suicide protection (viz., reasons for a living) in college students,[23] they examined whether the lack of hope agency and hope pathways contributed to further risk for suicide, above and beyond trauma history,[24] they also studied the relationship between negative life events and suicide risk in college students[25,26] and others. Author co-citation analysis is an efficient way to identify the most influential and active authors in a particular scientific research field and explore the distribution of the most cited prominent authors in that field. It is consistent with Cheng et al’s findings that Professor Beck AT is the most cited author in the field of Psychache in Suicidal Population.[15] Beck AT was an American psychiatrist, clinical psychologist, and founder of cognitive behavioral therapy. Cognitive behavioral therapy is arguably the gold standard of the psychotherapy field.[27] In addition, he also developed several instruments that measure the idiosyncratic beliefs associated with major depressive disorders and problems such as depression, suicide, anxiety, substance abuse, anger and hostility, couple problems, and, more recently, schizophrenia.[28] Mental health problems are one of the major global public health issues[29,30] and are becoming increasingly common among college students.[31–33] Moreover, mental disorders are risk factors for suicide,[34,35] and college students are in a high-risk period for the onset of suicidal thoughts and behaviors.[34–36] Therefore, Beck AT deserves to be the most cited and prominent author in the field.

These authors who published more in this field and those who were cited more frequently have more connections, which may lead to certain limitations of other scholars’ research in this field. If we want to increase the interest of more researchers in this field, it is necessary to strengthen the broader and deeper collaboration among authors.

4.4. Journals and cited journals

The papers in this field have been published in 676 journals. The journal Journal of Affective Disorders accounted for the largest number of publications (107, 4.73%), followed by other journals such as Suicide and Life-Threatening Behavior (95, 4.20%), Journal of American College Health (85, 3.76%), and Archives of Suicide Research (79, 3.49%; Table 2). Of interest, the top 2 journals and cited journals are the Journal of Affective Disorders and Suicide and Life-Threatening Behavior, indicating that the Journal of Affective Disorders and Suicide and Life-Threatening Behavior are the authoritative journals in the field of college students’ suicide. Interestingly, this finding is also consistent with Cheng Y et al’s finding that the Journal of Affective Disorders and Suicide and Life-Threatening Behavior were the most cited journals in the field of psychache in suicidal population.[15] The Journal of Affective Disorders publishes papers concerned with affective disorders in the widest sense: depression, mania, mood spectrum, emotions, personality, anxiety, and stress. Epidemiological studies suggest that mental disorders and suicidal thoughts and behaviors are common among university students.[14,37] As a result, studies of college students’ suicide fit well with the scope of the Journal of Affective Disorders. Moreover, the Suicide and Life-Threatening Behavior, founded by the American Association of Suicidology, publishes scientific research on suicidal and other life-threatening behaviors, including research from biological, psychological, and sociological approaches. Thus, Suicide and Life-Threatening Behavior is pertinent for researchers who engage in suicide research. This finding provides a journal reference for the publication of research papers on college students’ suicide in the future.

4.5. References

Among the top 10 most frequently cited references, 4 references discuss college students’ suicide, such as the prevalence of suicide, suicidal thoughts, and behaviors (Table 3). Moreover, these 4 references have been published only in the last few years. Therefore, suicidal thoughts and behaviors might be a good research topic in college students’ suicide. The most cited reference by Mortier et al concluded that college students worldwide report high prevalence rates of suicidal thoughts and behaviors. This indicates that suicidal thoughts and behaviors seem to be common among college students. Future studies should incorporate refusal conversion strategies to obtain adequate response rates and develop more fine-grained measures to assess suicidal ideation.[36,38] It is worth noting that the second-highest-ranked reference is the DSM-5-TR, published by the American Psychiatric Association. The manual is the most commonly used guidebook for diagnosing mental illness in the United States and other countries. DSM is usually used for the initial diagnosis of mental disorders in infancy, childhood, or adolescence. For instance, Carreiras et al developed a new clinical interview for borderline personality disorder (BPD) for adolescents, a new instrument based on a sound clinical interview for BPD in youth.[39] This new instrument is a hybrid clinical interview that evaluates BPD in adolescents, utilizing both categorical and dimensional approaches according to the DSM-5-TR.[40] In addition, specific mental disorders can themselves constitute a risk factor for suicide. Therefore, the DSM-5-TR can be used as a reference compendium for initial guidance on whether a specific disease causes suicide risk.[41] This result may explain why the United States was ranked first with a total of 1188 records, which was far more than that in other countries.

4.6. Research hotspots and trends

Keywords represent the core content of the research. The evolution of keywords reflects the research direction. Clusters contain related keywords for a particular topic. Our study identified high-frequency keywords related to the research topic of “college students’ suicide,” which includes college students, depression, mental health, prevalence, ideation, suicide ideation, adolescents, behavior, risk factors, and risk. It shows that scholars are currently focusing on depression, mental health, suicidal prevalence, suicidal ideation, and suicidal behavior and risk (Table 4). Depression is a prevalent issue among college students, and depressive symptoms have a significant impact on their mental health.[42,43] Studies have shown that the prevalence of depressive symptoms is higher among college students than in the general population.[44] Depression is a common mental health problem among college students. Despite this, the centrality is not particularly high, and adolescents and mental health are the keywords with the highest centrality. This further indicates that college students’ mental health plays a significant role in their risk of suicide.

In this study, the keywords have been categorized into 7 main clusters, each representing a specific research direction. The largest visual cluster was #0 suicidal ideation, followed by #1 suicidal prevention, #2 ideation, #3 mental health, #4 medical students, #5 perceived burdensomeness, and #6 mHealth. Suicidal ideation: According to the definition, suicidal ideation refers to thoughts of self-harm or killing oneself.[45] Altavini et al discovered a 5.9% point prevalence of suicide ideation among Brazilian college students, and the research found that SI was significantly linked with sociodemographic characteristics and mental health determinants.[7] Moreover, a study conducted nationwide, with more than 350,000 students, has indicated a growing trend of suicide ideation among students.[46] Therefore, suicidal ideation is a research hotspot that deserves a lot of attention in the study of college students’ suicide. Mental health: Mental disorders are a common phenomenon among college students.[14] In addition, college students experienced more anxiety, depression, fatigue, and distress than before the pandemic due to the global lockdown and school closures caused by the COVID-19 pandemic.[47] However, according to the 3-step theory of suicide, the first step toward ideation begins with pain. Pain typically (but not necessarily) means psychological or emotional pain.[48] This implies that if psychological disorders can be reduced, suicidal ideation can also be prevented early. Medical students and suicidal prevention: Among medical students, chronic stress and anxiety negatively affected mental health and were associated with suicidal ideation.[49] The sources of stress are commonly found to be heavy course loads, pressures of family and society to be academically flawless, and difficulty accepting lower grades or academic standings from undergraduate years.[50] This may make medical students exhibit a high prevalence of suicidal ideation. In a meta-analysis, the prevalence of suicidal ideation among medical students was estimated to be 11% (95% CI, 9.0%–13.7%).[51] This is significantly higher than the national prevalence of depression and suicidal ideation among United States adults of 4.7% and 4%, respectively.[52] Thus, because of the high prevalence of suicidal ideation among medical students, further research is needed to determine the underlying causes of suicide ideation and identify strategies for preventing suicide ideation in this population. Perceived burdensomeness: Perceived burdensomeness refers to a sense that one is a burden on others. According to interpersonal theory, perceived burdensomeness leads to suicide.[53] The findings of Hovey et al suggest that perceived burdensomeness plays a critical role in the risk for suicide in college students.[54] However, social disconnection has long been studied as a risk factor for suicide, but perceived burdensomeness is a relatively new risk factor in the suicide literature.[55] Therefore, in an effort to curb suicidal behavior in college students, studies related to perceived burdensomeness should receive more attention. mHealth: The mental health problems of college students are pervasive and serious. However, many college students with psychological disorders do not seek help because they face stigma from family, friends, teachers, or other students.[56] This makes many psychological treatments and interventions difficult to implement effectively. mHealth interventions could address some of the existing barriers to mental health care by being scalable, more accessible, private, and personalized. Studies have shown that mHealth interventions have the potential to improve mental health outcomes in young people,[57] who may be more willing to use these interventions.[58] For instance, a systematic review that included randomized controlled trials of mHealth interventions for adolescents and young adults showed that these interventions significantly improved symptoms for multiple outcomes, including depression, anxiety, and stress.[59] Therefore, mHealth technology research in the prevention of suicide in college students may be a promising research direction.

Burst keywords are keywords that are heavily cited in articles over time. Burst keywords are considered to be another reliable indicator of a research hotspot or a new trend that has emerged over time. The evolution of burst keywords over the last 20 years shows the continued progress in research on college students’ suicide. Thoughts and stigma are the burst keywords from 2019 to 2022. One study showed that the prevalence of first suicidal thoughts and behaviors in college ranged from 4.6% to 6.4% and appeared to be higher than that in the general population.[36] During the COVID-19 pandemic, the lack of recreational activities and social independence among college students led to an increase in suicidal thoughts among college students.[60] Therefore, suicidal thoughts among college students might be a hotspot in the future. Stigma toward suicide represents a negative stereotype of suicide. Many college students have a stigma attached to both mental illness and suicide. They often feel shame about seeking counseling, making many preventive measures difficult to implement.[61,62] Therefore, the issue of stigma urgently needs to be addressed to intervene in college students’ suicide. One study found a partial mediating role of suicidal ideation in the association between endorsement of suicide and suicide attempts and the association between stigma toward suicide and suicide attempts.[63] This suggests that in suicide prevention practices of college students, strategies should be developed to reduce or eliminate the negative effects of pro-suicide and stigmatization of suicide on suicidal ideation. In addition, future studies may consider exploring the generalizability of these findings to other populations, such as adolescents. In conclusion, keyword burst analysis can objectively forecast future research hotspots and guide researchers in selecting future research directions.

4.7. Limitations

This study inevitably has some limitations that need to be refined in the future. First, we examined only publications from the Web of Science (WoS) database. The bibliometric analysis relies on literature databases. At present, widely used databases include Scopus, WoS, PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Library. Among them, the WoS database contains large-scale multidisciplinary, high-impact, international, and comprehensive academic journals. Meanwhile, evidence has shown that the WoS database provides a better knowledge map effect when CiteSpace is used for visual analysis.[64] Therefore, it is rational and effective to select the WoS database as our study data source. However, future studies should include all database analyses to the extent possible. Second, non-English publications were not included and only referred to articles and reviews, which may have biased the analysis results based on these factors. Therefore, language limitations may be an issue that needs to be addressed in the future. Third, the results of this study were mainly obtained using CiteSpace software. Therefore, minor errors due to database or software issues may still occur. Updates to CiteSpace software and its combination with other software may help address this limitation. Additionally, this paper is a bibliometric study, and the results of the study are time-sensitive, so more researchers need to update relevant articles in time to get the latest research forefront hotspots.

In summary, our results remain reliable as a visual review of the current field of college students’ suicide based on current bibliometric methods.

5. Conclusion

This study shows that research on college student suicide has advanced rapidly from 2002 to 2022. The United States has the highest number of publications on college students’ suicide. However, there is a lack of extensive cooperation between different institutions. Hirsch, Jameson K, and Beck AT are productive authors in this field. The 2 most influential journals on college students’ suicide are the Journal of Affective Disorders and Suicide and Life-Threatening Behavior. Five areas of research are identified as hotspots for current research, including suicide ideation, suicide prevention, mental health issues, burdensomeness perception, and mHealth. As for potential research directions, suicidal thoughts and stigma are probably the 2 most pertinent topics to investigate. The analysis results are conducive to more promising future research in the field of college students’ suicide. Future research may focus on the correlation between these hotspots and provide a reference for suicide recurrence prevention strategies in college students.

Unobstructed deformations for singular Calabi-Yau varieties
Robert Friedman

Abstract
. Let Y Y be a compact Gorenstein analytic space with only isolated singularities and trivial dualizing sheaf. A recent paper of Imagi studies the deformation theory of Y Y in case the singularities of Y Y are weighted homogeneous and rational and Y Y is Kähler. In this note, assuming that H 1 ​ ( Y , 𝒪 Y ) = 0 H^{1}(Y;\mathcal{O}_{Y})=0 , we generalize Imagi’s results to the case where the singularities of Y Y are Du Bois, with no assumption that they be weighted homogeneous, and where the Kähler assumption is replaced by the hypothesis that there is a resolution of singularities of Y Y satisfying the ∂ ∂ ¯ \partial\bar{\partial} -lemma. As a consequence, if the singularites of Y Y are additionally local complete intersections, then the deformations of Y Y are unobstructed.



1.
Introduction

Let
M
be a compact complex manifold. The nearby deformations of the complex structure on
M
are described by Kuranishi’s theorem: there exists a power series
\Psi
defined in a neighborhood of the origin in
H^{1}(M;T_{M})
and with values in
H^{2}(M;T_{M})
, such that
\Psi(0)=0
and the deformation space of complex structures on
M
, viewed as a germ, is isomorphic to the germ
(\Psi^{-1}(0),0)
. If
\Psi=0
, the deformations of
M
are said to be
unobstructed
. The Bogomolov-Tian-Todorov theorem says that, if
M
is a Calabi-Yau manifold, then the deformations of
M
are unobstructed. A very short proof, using only the property that
K_{M}\cong\mathcal{O}_{M}
and the degeneration of the Hodge-de Rham spectral sequence, can be given by applying the
T^{1}
lifting property of Ran
[
Ran92b
]
, Kawamata
[
Kaw92
]
, and Deligne (unpublished).

There are analogues of
H^{1}(M;T_{M})
and
H^{2}(M;T_{M})
in case the complex manifold is replaced by a compact analytic space
Y
, namely the vector spaces
\mathbb{T}^{1}_{Y}
and
\mathbb{T}^{2}_{Y}
. There is again a Kuranishi map, in this case a power series
\Psi
defined in a neighborhood of the origin in
\mathbb{T}^{1}_{Y}
and with values in
\mathbb{T}^{2}_{Y}
, such that
\Psi(0)=0
and the deformation space of
Y
, viewed as a germ, is isomorphic to the germ
(\Psi^{-1}(0),0)
. If
\Psi=0
, the deformations of
Y
are again said to be
unobstructed
. If
Y
is singular, there can be local obstructions to deforming the singularities as well as global ones. If however the singularities of
Y
are local complete intersection (lci) singularities, then there are no local obstructions to deforming them.

By analogy with the case of Calabi-Yau manifolds, we make the following definition:

Definition 1.1
.

A
singular Calabi-Yau variety

Y
is a (connected) compact Gorenstein singular analytic space with only isolated singularities and trivial dualizing sheaf
\omega_{Y}
, i.e.
\omega_{Y}\cong\mathcal{O}_{Y}
.

If
Y
is a singular Calabi-Yau variety, it is often important to know if the deformations of
Y
are unobstructed. One motivation is to construct smoothings of
Y
. Note however that the property that the deformations of
Y
are unobstructed does not necessarily imply that
Y
is smoothable (and conversely, the existence of a smoothing does not necessarily imply that the deformations of
Y
are unobstructed). To prove smoothing results given that the deformations of
Y
are unobstructed, one typically has to know more about the image of
\mathbb{T}^{1}_{Y}
in
H^{0}(Y;T^{1}_{Y})
, where
T^{1}_{Y}
is a sheaf on
Y
supported on the singular locus which measures the first order change in deformations around the singularities. Some results along these lines may be found in
[
FL22
]
.

A first basic result in the deformation theory of
Y
is the following result of Kawamata
[
Kaw92
]
, Ran
[
Ran92a
]
, and Tian
[
Tia92
]
:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
Y
be a singular Calabi-Yau variety with only ordinary double points such that
\omega_{Y}\cong\mathcal{O}_{Y}
and there exists a resolution
\widehat{Y}\to Y
which is Kähler. Then the deformations of
Y
are unobstructed.

The proofs are based on the
T^{1}
lifting property as well as (for the proof in
[
Ran92a
]
) the related
T^{2}
injective property of
[
Ran95
]
. Here, the hypothesis that there is a Kähler resolution of
Y
could be replaced by: there exists a resolution of
Y
satisfying the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma. Note that Ran stated his result for somewhat more general singularities. However, there are several points in the papers
[
Ran92a
]
and
[
Tia92
]
which need clarification, and the method of
[
Tia92
]
seems to work only in dimension
3
.

In dimension
3
, there is the following result of Namikawa
[
Nam94
]
, which allows more complicated singularities:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
Y
be a singular Calabi-Yau variety of dimension
3
, all of whose singular points are rational lci singularities, such that
H^{1}(Y;\mathcal{O}_{Y})=0
, and such that there exists a resolution
\widehat{Y}\to Y
which is Kähler. Then the deformations of
Y
are unobstructed.

As usual, we can relax the condition that there exist a Kähler resolution to the condition that there exists a resolution satisfying the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma. In
[
FL25
, Theorem 3.7]
, this result has been partially generalized to the case of Du Bois singularities. There are also related results of Gross
[
Gro97
]
, in case
Y
has a crepant resolution, where
Y
has rational but not necessarily lci singularities, which relate the deformation theory of
Y
to the local obstructions to deformations of the isolated singular points.

In higher dimensions, the author and Radu Laza have proved the following generalization of Theorem
1.2

[
FL24b
, Corollary 1.5]
:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
Y
be a singular Calabi-Yau variety of dimension
n
such that all singularities of
Y
are
1
-Du Bois lci singularities and such that there exists a resolution of
Y
satisfying the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma. Then the deformations of
Y
are unobstructed.

Here, we refer to
[
FL24b
, Definition 3.6]
and the references in that paper for the definition of
1
-Du Bois. In particular, lci
1
-Du Bois singularities are automatically rational, by
[
FL24a
]
in the case of isolated lci singularities and by a result of Chen-Dirks-Mustaţă for a general lci singularity
[
CDM24
]
. The proof of Theorem
1.4
does not in fact require that the singularities be isolated as long as there is a Kähler resolution of singularities of
Y
. The key point in the proof is that, if
Y
has
1
-Du Bois lci singularities and under some hypotheses on the resolution, then for any deformation
\mathcal{Y}
of
Y
over
\operatorname{Spec}A
, where
A
is an Artin local
\mathbb{C}
-algebra, the
A
-modules
H^{i}(\mathcal{Y};\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}/\operatorname{Spec}A})
are free and commute with base change in an appropriate sense. This then implies the
T^{1}
lifting property.

Recently, Y. Imagi has posted a paper which proves, among other matters, the following
[
Ima25
, Theorem 1.2]
:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
Y
be a singular Calabi-Yau variety, all of whose singularities are weighted homogeneous lci rational singularities, and such that
Y
is Kähler in a strong sense. Then the deformations of
Y
are unobstructed.

Imagi also deals with the case where the singularities are not necessarily lci along the lines of
[
Gro97
]
.

The hypotheses of Theorem
1.5
are not ideal. The assumption of weighted homogeneous singularities is very restrictive, and the Kähler condition seems hard to verify unless
Y
is actually projective. The method of proof involves choosing model metrics in a neighborhood of the singular points and gluing them in to form a new metric on
Y
. The proof then proceeds via harmonic analysis using the new metric, and finally there is a discussion of deformation theory over Artin local
\mathbb{R}
-algebras, so as to extend the Kähler metric to deformations over such an
\mathbb{R}
-algebra. It seems hard to extend these methods to a more general setting. Nonetheless, there are two insights that can be gleaned from his approach:

(1)

In proving
T^{1}
lifting, or more usefully
T^{2}
injectivity, the
1
-Du Bois hypothesis is too strong. It is not important that

H^{i}(\mathcal{Y};\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}/\operatorname{Spec}A})
is a locally free
A
-module and commutes with base change for
all

i
. In the lci case, the necessary ingredient (for
T^{2}
injectivity) is just that the map

H^{n-2}(\mathcal{Y};\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}/\operatorname{Spec}A})\to H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})

is surjective. In the non lci case, one considers instead the map
H^{n-2}(\mathcal{Y};\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}/\operatorname{Spec}A})\to H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})/\operatorname{Im}H^{n-2}_{Z}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})
. This idea already appears in
[
Nam94
, Proof of Theorem 1]
and
[
Gro97
]
.

(2)

It is useful to look at compactly supported cohomology, which in this case is the relative cohomology
H^{i}(Y,Z)
, where
Z
is the singular locus of
Y
. Given a good resolution
\pi\colon\widehat{Y}\to Y
with exceptional set
E=\pi^{-1}(Z)
,
H^{i}(Y,Z)
is the same as the relative cohomology
H^{i}(\widehat{Y},E)
. In this setting,
H^{i}(\widehat{Y},E)
carries a mixed Hodge structure and we can use these methods to prove
E_{1}
degeneration for an appropriate spectral sequence.

Armed with these extra ideas, we first work under the simplifying assumption of local complete intersection singularities for the sake of clarity, and prove the following in Section
3
:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
Y
be a singular Calabi-Yau variety of dimension
n\geq 3
, such that:

(i)

The
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma holds for some resolution of singularities
\widehat{Y}
of
Y
.

(ii)

The singularities of
Y
are lci and Du Bois (i.e.
0
-Du Bois).

(iii)

H^{1}(Y;\mathcal{O}_{Y})=0
.

Then the deformations of
Y
are unobstructed.

Remark 1.7
.

(i) Steenbrink proved in
[
Ste83
, Proposition 3.7]
that an isolated rational singularity is Du Bois, so Theorem
1.6
covers the case of rational singularities as a special case. Note that a normal Gorenstein singularity is rational
\iff
it is canonical
[
Kol97
, 11.1]
. By a theorem of Ishii
[
Ish85
, proof of Theorem 2.3]
, an isolated Gorenstein singularity of dimension at least
2
is Du Bois
\iff
it is log canonical.

(ii) The assumption
H^{1}(Y;\mathcal{O}_{Y})=0
is typically satisfied if
Y
has isolated singularities (but is not smooth). For the case where
Y
is projective with rational singularities, this follows from a result of Kawamata
[
Kaw85
, Theorem 8.3]
. In this case, an easy argument (which holds in somewhat greater generality) shows that
\mathbb{T}^{0}_{Y}=H^{0}(Y;T^{0}_{Y})=0
, i.e. the deformation functor is prorepresentable.

In Section
4
, we discuss how to modify the results of Section
2
in order for them to cover Imagi’s results in the non lci case and briefly explain some consequences for the deformation theory of
Y
.

Notation and conventions

We work over
\mathbb{C}
with the classical topology.
Y
will always denote a compact Gorenstein analytic variety of dimension
n\geq 3
with isolated singularities, not necessarily satisfying the Calabi-Yau condition,
Z
will denote the singular locus of
Y
(a finite set), and
\pi\colon\widehat{Y}\to Y
will denote a good resolution of
Y
with exceptional set
E
, a divisor with simple normal crossings.

2.
Results from Hodge theory

In this section, we deal with spaces
Y
which do not necessarily satisfy the Calabi-Yau condition. The proofs work more generally under the assumption that
Y
is normal with isolated singularities.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that

(i)

The
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma holds for
\widehat{Y}
.

(ii)

The singularities of
Y
are lci and Du Bois.

(iii)

H^{n-1}(Y;\mathcal{O}_{Y})=0
.

Let
A
be an Artin local
\mathbb{C}
-algebra and let
\mathcal{Y}\to\operatorname{Spec}A
be a deformation of
Y
over
\operatorname{Spec}A
. Then for every quotient
A^{\prime}=A/I
, with
\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}=\mathcal{Y}\times_{\operatorname{Spec}A}A^{\prime}
, the natural homomorphism
H^{n-2}(\mathcal{Y};\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}/\operatorname{Spec}A})\to H^{n-2}(\mathcal{Y}^{\prime};\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}/\operatorname{Spec}A^{\prime}})
is surjective.

Proof.

We have the relative filtered de Rham complex
\underline{\Omega}^{\bullet}_{Y,Z}=R\pi_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E)
, with associated graded terms
\underline{\Omega}^{p}_{Y,Z}=R\pi_{*}\Omega^{p}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E)
. In degree
0
,
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{Y}}(-E)=I_{Z}
, the ideal of the subset
Z
, and the Du Bois assumption says that
R^{i}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{Y}}(-E)=0
for
i>0
. As usual, if
i\colon Z\to Y
is the inclusion, define
i_{!}\mathbb{C}_{Y-Z}
as the kernel of the surjection
\mathbb{C}_{Y}\to i_{*}\mathbb{C}_{Z}
, where
\mathbb{C}_{Y}
and
\mathbb{C}_{Z}
are the constant sheaves on
Y
and
Z
respectively. Set

\Omega^{\bullet}_{Y,Z}=\operatorname{Ker}\{\Omega^{\bullet}_{Y}\to i_{*}\mathbb{C}_{Z}[0]\}=\{I_{Z}\to\Omega^{1}_{Y}\to\Omega^{2}_{Y}\to\cdots\}.

Then there are natural morphisms of complexes

i_{!}\mathbb{C}_{Y-Z}\to\Omega^{\bullet}_{Y,Z}\to R\pi_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E)=\underline{\Omega}^{\bullet}_{Y,Z}

such that the composition
i_{!}\mathbb{C}_{Y-Z}\to\underline{\Omega}^{\bullet}_{Y,Z}
is a quasi-isomorphism.

The hypercohomology spectral sequence with
E_{1}
term

E_{1}^{p,q}=H^{q}(\widehat{Y};\Omega^{p}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E))\implies\mathbb{H}^{p+q}(\widehat{Y};\Omega^{\bullet}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E))\cong H^{p+q}(\widehat{Y},E;\mathbb{C})\cong H^{p+q}(Y,Z;\mathbb{C})

degenerates at
E_{1}
and computes the respective relative cohomology groups. More precisely, let
f\colon\Omega^{\bullet}_{\widehat{Y}}\to j_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{E}/\tau^{\bullet}_{E}
be the natural surjection, where
j\colon E\to\widehat{Y}
is the inclusion,
\Omega^{\bullet}_{E}
is the complex of Kähler differentials on
E
and
\tau^{\bullet}_{E}
is the subcomplex of differentials supported on the singular locus of
E
. Then there is an exact sequence

0\to\Omega^{\bullet}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E)\to\Omega^{\bullet}_{\widehat{Y}}\to j_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{E}/\tau^{\bullet}_{E}\to 0

which gives rise to a filtered quasi-isomorphism

\Omega^{\bullet}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E)[1]\simeq_{\rm{qis}}\operatorname{Cone}^{\bullet}f.

Then the
E_{1}
degeneration and the fact that
\mathbb{H}^{k}(\widehat{Y};\Omega^{\bullet}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E))\cong H^{k}(\widehat{Y},E;\mathbb{C})
are implied by
[
PS08
, Theorem 3.22, Proposition 5.46, and Example 7.25]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

The natural map
\Omega^{1}_{Y}\to\pi_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E)
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that, since the singularities of
Y
are isolated lci of dimension at least
3
, the sheaf
\Omega^{1}_{Y}
is reflexive. (See e.g.
[
Kun86
, Proposition 9.7]
as well as
[
FL24b
, Remark 3.20]
.)
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

(i)

H^{i}(\widehat{Y};\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{Y}}(-E))\cong H^{i}(Y;I_{Z})
, and the homomorphism

H^{i}(\widehat{Y};\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{Y}}(-E))\cong H^{i}(Y;I_{Z})\to H^{i}(Y;\mathcal{O}_{Y})

is surjective for
i=1
and an isomorphism for
i>1
. In particular,
H^{n-1}(\widehat{Y};\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{Y}}(-E))=0
.

(ii)

The map
H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})\to H^{n-2}(\widehat{Y};\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E))
is injective.

(iii)

The differential
d\colon H^{n-2}(Y;\mathcal{O}_{Y})\to H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})
is zero.

Proof.

(i) This follows from the Leray spectral sequence, the Du Bois assumption, and the fact that the map
H^{i}(Y;I_{Z})\to H^{i}(Y;\mathcal{O}_{Y})
is surjective for
i=1
and an isomorphism for
i>1
.

(ii) By a result of Steenbrink
[
Ste97
, Theorem 5]
, if
n\geq 4
, the assumption of lci singularities implies that
R^{n-3}\pi_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E)=0
. From the Leray spectral sequence and Lemma
2.2
, there is an exact sequence

H^{0}(Y;R^{n-3}\pi_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E))\to H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})\to H^{n-2}(\widehat{Y};\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E)).

Thus the map
H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})\to H^{n-2}(\widehat{Y};\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E))
is injective. The same holds if
n=3
because then the map
H^{1}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})=H^{1}(Y;\pi_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E))\to H^{1}(\widehat{Y};\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E))
is automatically injective.

(iii) There is a commutative diagram

\begin{CD}H^{n-2}(\widehat{Y};\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{Y}}(-E))\cong H^{n-2}(Y;I_{Z})@>{}>{}>H^{n-2}(Y;\mathcal{O}_{Y})\\
@V{d}V{}V@V{}V{d}V\\
H^{n-2}(\widehat{Y};\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E))@<{}<{}<H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y}).\end{CD}

By (i), the top horizontal map is an isomorphism if
n\geq 4
and is surjective if
n=3
. The left hand vertical map is
0
. By (ii), the map
H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})\to H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E))
is injective. Thus
d\colon H^{n-2}(Y;\mathcal{O}_{Y})\to H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})
is zero.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y}
be the complex
\{\mathcal{O}_{Y}\to\Omega^{1}_{Y}\}
and let
\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y,Z}
be the complex
\{I_{Z}\to\Omega^{1}_{Y}\}
. Then

(i)

\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y,Z})\cong\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y})\cong H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})
.

(ii)

The homomorphism

i_{!}\mathbb{C}_{Y-Z}\to\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y,Z}
induces a surjection

H^{n-1}(Y,Z;\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y,Z})\cong H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y}).

(iii)

The homomorphism

\mathbb{C}\to\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y}
induces a surjection

H^{n-1}(Y;\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y})\cong H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y}).

Proof.

(i) The isomorphism
\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y,Z})\cong\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y})
follows from the exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y,Z}\to\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y}\to i_{*}\mathbb{C}_{Z}[0]\to 0.

The hypercohomology spectral sequence gives an exact sequence

H^{n-2}(Y;\mathcal{O}_{Y})\xrightarrow{d}H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})\to\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y})\to H^{n-1}(Y;\mathcal{O}_{Y}).

By assumption,
H^{n-1}(Y;\mathcal{O}_{Y})=0
. By Lemma
2.3
(iii),
d\colon H^{n-2}(Y;\mathcal{O}_{Y})\to H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})
is zero. Thus
\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y})\cong H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})
.

(ii) The homomorphism
i_{!}\mathbb{C}_{Y-Z}\to\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y,Z}
defines a homomorphism

H^{n-1}(Y,Z;\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y,Z})=H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y}).

To see that this homomorphism is surjective, let
\mathcal{D}^{\bullet}
be the complex
\{\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{Y}}(-E)\to\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E)\}
.
By the
E_{1}
degeneration described at the beginning of the proof, the homomorphism

H^{i}(Y,Z;\mathbb{C})=\mathbb{H}^{i}(\widehat{Y};\Omega^{p}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E))\to\mathbb{H}^{i}(\widehat{Y};\Omega^{p}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E)/\sigma^{\geq 2})=\mathbb{H}^{i}(\mathcal{D}^{\bullet})

is surjective. Moreover, the hypercohomology spectral sequence for
\mathbb{H}^{i}(\mathcal{D}^{\bullet})
degenerates at
E_{1}
, so that in particular there is an exact sequence

0\to H^{n-2}(\widehat{Y};\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E))\to\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{D}^{\bullet})\to H^{n-1}(\widehat{Y};\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{Y}}(-E))\to 0.

Hence, by Lemma
2.3
(i),
\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{D}^{\bullet})\cong H^{n-2}(\widehat{Y};\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E))
. There is a morphism of complexes
\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y,Z}\to R\pi_{*}\mathcal{D}^{\bullet}
and hence a commutative diagram

\begin{CD}H^{n-1}(Y,Z;\mathbb{C})@>{}>{}>\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y,Z})\cong H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})\\
\Big\|@V{}V{}V\\
H^{n-1}(Y,Z;\mathbb{C})@>{}>{}>\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{D}^{\bullet})\cong H^{n-2}(\widehat{Y};\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E)).\end{CD}

The lower horizontal arrow is surjective. Also, by Lemma
2.3
(ii), the right hand vertical map is injective. Thus
H^{n-1}(Y,Z;\mathbb{C})\to H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})
is surjective.

(iii) There is a commutative diagram

\begin{CD}H^{n-1}(Y,Z;\mathbb{C})@>{}>{}>\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y,Z})\cong H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})\\
@V{\cong}V{}V\Big\|\\
H^{n-1}(Y;\mathbb{C})@>{}>{}>\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y})\cong H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y}).\end{CD}

Thus the result follows from (ii).
∎

Remark 2.5
.

The proof of (ii) above shows that the map

H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})\cong H^{n-2}(Y;\pi_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E))\to H^{n-2}(\widehat{Y};\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E))

is an isomorphism.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
A
be an Artin local
\mathbb{C}
-algebra and let
\mathcal{Y}\to\operatorname{Spec}A
be a deformation of
Y
over
\operatorname{Spec}A
. Then the natural map
H^{n-2}(\mathcal{Y};\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}/\operatorname{Spec}A})\to H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})
is surjective.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{Y}}
be the complex

\{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}\to\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}/\operatorname{Spec}A}\}
. Since
H^{n-1}(Y;\mathcal{O}_{Y})=0
, a standard argument shows that
H^{n-1}(\mathcal{Y};\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}})=0
as well. Arguing as in the proof of Lemma
2.4
(i), there is a surjection

H^{n-2}(\mathcal{Y};\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}/\operatorname{Spec}A})\to\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{Y}}).

From the morphisms of complexes
\mathbb{C}\to A\to\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{Y}}
, there is a commutative diagram

\begin{CD}H^{n-1}(Y;\mathbb{C})@>{}>{}>\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{Y}})\\
@V{=}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
H^{n-1}(Y;\mathbb{C})@>{}>{}>\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y})\cong H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y}).\end{CD}

By Lemma
2.4
(iii), the map
H^{n-1}(Y;\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y})
is surjective. Since it factors through the map
\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{Y}})\to\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y})
, this last homomorphism is surjective as well. Then there is a sequence of surjections

H^{n-2}(\mathcal{Y};\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}/\operatorname{Spec}A})\to\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{Y}})\to\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y})\cong H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y}).

Thus
H^{n-2}(\mathcal{Y};\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}/\operatorname{Spec}A})\to H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})
is surjective.
∎

Finally, the fact that Lemma
2.6
implies Theorem
2.1
is a formal argument
[
Har77
, III 12.10 and 12.5]
(cf. also the first paragraph of the proof of
[
FL24b
, Theorem 4.1]
), namely that for a surjection
A\to A^{\prime}
, the natural map

H^{n-2}(\mathcal{Y};\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}/\operatorname{Spec}A})\otimes_{A}A^{\prime}\to H^{n-2}(\mathcal{Y};\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}/\operatorname{Spec}A}\otimes_{A}A^{\prime})\cong H^{n-2}(\mathcal{Y}^{\prime};\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}/\operatorname{Spec}A^{\prime}})

is surjective. Here, we use the lci assumption to show the flatness of
\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}/\operatorname{Spec}A}
as an
A
-module, which is an easy consequence of the local criterion of flatness (cf.
[
FL24b
, proof of Theorem 2.5]
), but it is enough as noted in the proof of
[
Gro97
, Theorem 2.2]
that it is flat away from a zero-dimensional subspace.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.6

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.6
along the lines of Namikawa
[
Nam94
, Proof of Theorem 1]
and Gross
[
Gro97
, Theorem 2.2]
(cf. also
[
Ima25
, §8]
). First note that, if
Y
is a singular Calabi-Yau variety, then
H^{1}(Y;\mathcal{O}_{Y})=0

\iff

H^{n-1}(Y;\mathcal{O}_{Y})=0
. Thus, if
Y
satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
1.6
, then it satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
2.1
. In addition, if
\mathcal{Y}\to\operatorname{Spec}A
is a deformation of
Y
over the Artin local
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
A
, then the argument of
[
FL24b
, Proof of Corollary 1.5]
shows that, thanks to the Du Bois assumption,
\omega_{\mathcal{Y}/\operatorname{Spec}A}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}
. (There is also another, somewhat easier, proof which uses the hypothesis that
H^{1}(Y;\mathcal{O}_{Y})=0
.)

We begin by describing the
T^{1}
lifting property. Let
D
be the deformation functor
\mathbf{Def}_{Y}
from the category of Artin local
\mathbb{C}
-algebras to sets. Let
A_{k}=\mathbb{C}[t]/(t^{k+1})
and let
B_{k}=A_{k}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}A_{1}
, so that there is a natural morphism
B_{k}\to A_{k}
. Let
\psi_{k}\colon\mathcal{Y}_{k}\to\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}
be a deformation of
Y
over
\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}
and let
\mathcal{Y}_{k-1}=\mathcal{Y}_{k}\times_{\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}}\operatorname{Spec}A_{k-1}
. Define

T^{1}(\mathcal{Y}_{k}/A_{k})=\{\mathcal{Y}\in D(B_{k}):\mathcal{Y}\times_{\operatorname{Spec}B_{k}}\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}\cong\mathcal{Y}_{k}\}.

There is a natural morphism
T^{1}(\mathcal{Y}_{k}/A_{k})\to T^{1}(\mathcal{Y}_{k-1}/A_{k-1})
. According to the
T^{1}
lifting criterion, if this morphism is surjective for every
k
and every choice of
\mathcal{Y}_{k}\in D(A_{k})
, then
D
is unobstructed.

By
[
Kaw92
]
,
T^{1}(\mathcal{Y}_{k}/A_{k})\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}})
. By
[
Gro97
, proof of Theorem 2.2]
, there is a commutative diagram

\begin{CD}T^{1}(\mathcal{Y}_{k}/A_{k})@>{}>{}>T^{1}(\mathcal{Y}_{k-1}/A_{k-1})\\
@V{\cong}V{}V@V{}V{\cong}V\\
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}})@>{}>{}>\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k-1}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k-1}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k-1}}),\end{CD}

where the lower horizontal map comes from applying
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}},-)
to the morphism
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k-1}}
and using the isomorphism
[
Gro97
, Lemma 2.3]

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k-1}})\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k-1}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k-1}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k-1}}).

From the exact sequence of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}}
-modules

0\to\mathcal{O}_{Y}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k-1}}\to 0,

the bottom row of the commutative diagram fits into a long exact sequence

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}})\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k-1}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k-1}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k-1}})\to\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(\Omega^{1}_{Y},\mathcal{O}_{Y})\to\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}}),

where we have again used
[
Gro97
, Lemma 2.3]
to identify
\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}},\mathcal{O}_{Y})
with
\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(\Omega^{1}_{Y},\mathcal{O}_{Y})
. Equivalently, identifying the ideal
t\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}}
with
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k-1}}
as an
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}}
-module, there is an exact sequence of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}}
-modules

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k-1}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}}\to\mathcal{O}_{Y}\to 0.

Tensoring this sequence with
\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}}
gives a commutative diagram which in the lci case has exact rows:

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k-1}}@>{}>{}>\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}}@>{}>{}>\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}@>{}>{}>0\\
@V{}V{}V\Big\|@V{}V{}V\\
0@>{}>{}>\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k-1}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k-1}}@>{}>{}>\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}}@>{}>{}>\Omega^{1}_{Y}@>{}>{}>0.\end{CD}

(In the non lci case, the bottom row is exact away from the finite set
Z
.) There is thus another long exact Ext sequence

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}})\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k-1}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k-1}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}})\to\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(\Omega^{1}_{Y},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}})\to\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}}),

which can be identified with the previous one.

Thus, to verify the
T^{1}
lifting property, we have to show that the map

\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(\Omega^{1}_{Y},\mathcal{O}_{Y})\to\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}})

is injective (“
T^{2}
injectivity”). By Serre duality,
\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(\Omega^{1}_{Y},\mathcal{O}_{Y})
is dual to
H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})
since
\omega_{Y}\cong\mathcal{O}_{Y}
. Applying relative duality to the morphism
\psi_{k}\colon\mathcal{Y}_{k}\to\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}
and using the facts that

R^{i}\psi_{k}{}_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}}=H^{i}(\mathcal{Y}_{k};\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}})
, viewed as an
A_{k}
-module, and that
\omega_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}}
, there is a spectral sequence with
E_{2}
term

E_{2}^{p,q}=\operatorname{Ext}^{p}_{A_{k}}(H^{n-q}(\mathcal{Y}_{k};\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}}),A_{k})\implies\operatorname{Ext}^{p+q}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}}).

Since
A_{k}
is injective as an
A_{k}
-module, the higher
\operatorname{Ext}^{p}_{A_{k}}
terms are
0
, and hence

\operatorname{Hom}_{A_{k}}(H^{n-q}(\mathcal{Y}_{k};\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}}),A_{k})\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{q}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}}).

By Theorem
2.1
(or Lemma
2.6
), the map
H^{n-2}(\mathcal{Y}_{k};\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}})\to H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})
induced by restriction is surjective. Thus the dual map

\operatorname{Hom}_{A_{k}}(H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y}),A_{k})\to\operatorname{Hom}_{A_{k}}(H^{n-2}(\mathcal{Y}_{k};\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}}),A_{k})\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}})

is injective. Moreover,

\operatorname{Hom}_{A_{k}}(H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y}),A_{k})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y}),\mathbb{C})\cong H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})^{\vee}\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(\Omega^{1}_{Y},\mathcal{O}_{Y}),

and the corresponding homomorphism
\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(\Omega^{1}_{Y},\mathcal{O}_{Y})\to\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}})
is injective. Finally, following Gross
[
Gro97
, p. 199]
, we can identify this homomorphism with the homomorphism
\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(\Omega^{1}_{Y},\mathcal{O}_{Y})\to\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}/\operatorname{Spec}A_{k}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}})
described above. This establishes the
T^{1}
lifting property. Hence the deformations of
Y
are unobstructed. ∎

4.
The non lci case

If we want to extend these results to the non lci case, then, following Gross
[
Gro97
, Lemma 2.4]
and
[
Ima25
, §8 and Corollary 13.1]
, we must consider the cokernel of the map
H_{Z}^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})\to H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})
. (In the lci case,
H^{n-2}_{Z}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})
is easily seen to be
0
.) We can assume that
n\geq 4
since the case
n=3
is covered by
[
Gro97
]
. The main point is to show the following generalization of
[
Ima25
, (13.2)]
:

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that
n\geq 4
and that

(i)

The
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma holds for
\widehat{Y}
.

(ii)

The singularities of
Y
are Du Bois (i.e.
0
-Du Bois).

(iii)

H^{n-1}(Y;\mathcal{O}_{Y})=0
.

Let
A
be an Artin local
\mathbb{C}
-algebra and let
\mathcal{Y}\to\operatorname{Spec}A
be a deformation of
Y
over
\operatorname{Spec}A
. Then the natural homomorphism
H^{n-2}(\mathcal{Y};\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}/\operatorname{Spec}A})\to H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})/\operatorname{Im}H_{Z}^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})
is surjective.

Proof.

The lci assumption is used in two places in the proof of Theorem
2.1
: Firstly, it implies that
\Omega^{1}_{Y}
is reflexive and hence that
\pi_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E)=\Omega^{1}_{Y}
. In the general case, we have the following result for
n\geq 4
:

Lemma 4.2
.

For
n\geq 4
, the natural map
H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})\to H^{n-2}(Y;\pi_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E))
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

This follows immediately from the fact that the kernel and cokernel of the morphism
\Omega^{1}_{Y}\to\pi_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E)
are supported on a finite set.
∎

In the second place, for
n\geq 4
, we used the lci assumption to show that
R^{n-3}\pi_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E)=0
. In general, we have the following:

Lemma 4.3
.

For
n\geq 4
, let
R
be the image of
H^{0}(Y;R^{n-3}\pi_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E))
in
H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})
via the composition of the map
H^{0}(Y;R^{n-3}\pi_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E))\to H^{n-2}(Y;\pi_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E))
and the inverse of the isomorphism
H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})\to H^{n-2}(Y;\pi_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E))
of Lemma
4.2
. Then
R
is contained in the image of the map
H_{Z}^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})\to H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an arbitrary sheaf on
\widehat{Y}
.
The Leray spectral sequences in local and ordinary cohomology are compatible, so there is a commutative diagram

\begin{CD}H^{0}_{Z}(Y;R^{i-1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{F})@>{}>{}>H^{i}_{Z}(Y;R^{0}\pi_{*}\mathcal{F})\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
H^{0}(Y;R^{i-1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{F})@>{}>{}>H^{i}(Y;R^{0}\pi_{*}\mathcal{F}).\end{CD}

For
i\geq 2
,
R^{i-1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{F}
is supported on
Z
and hence
H^{0}_{Z}(Y;R^{i-1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{F})\cong H^{0}(Y;R^{i-1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{F})
. Thus, for
i\geq 2
, the image of
H^{0}(Y;R^{i-1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{F})
in
H^{i}(Y;R^{0}\pi_{*}\mathcal{F})
is contained in the image of
H^{i}_{Z}(Y;R^{0}\pi_{*}\mathcal{F})
. Applying this to
\mathcal{F}=\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E)
, since
n\geq 4
, the image of the map

H^{0}(Y;R^{n-3}\pi_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E))\to H^{n-2}(Y;\pi_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E))

is contained in the image of
H^{n-2}_{Z}(Y;\pi_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E))
.
Since the kernel and cokernel of the morphism
\Omega^{1}_{Y}\to\pi_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E)
are supported on a finite set and
n\geq 4
,
H_{Z}^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})\cong H^{n-2}_{Z}(Y;\pi_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E))
. The commutative diagram

\begin{CD}H_{Z}^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})@>{}>{}>H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})\\
@V{\cong}V{}V@V{}V{\cong}V\\
H^{n-2}_{Z}(Y;\pi_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E))@>{}>{}>H^{n-2}(Y;\pi_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E))\end{CD}

then implies that
R
is contained in the image of the map
H_{Z}^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})\to H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})
.
∎

Next, the Hodge theory methods of Section
2
show the following:

Lemma 4.4
.

For
n\geq 4
, let
R
be as in Lemma
4.3
and let
\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y}
be the complex
\{\mathcal{O}_{Y}\to\Omega^{1}_{Y}\}
. Then

(i)

The composed map

\displaystyle H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})/R\cong H^{n-2}(Y;\pi_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E))/\operatorname{Im}H^{0}(Y;R^{n-3}\pi_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E))

\displaystyle\to H^{n-2}(\widehat{Y};\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{Y}}(\log E)(-E))

is injective.

(ii)

The image of the differential
d\colon H^{n-2}(Y;\mathcal{O}_{Y})\to H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})
is contained in
R
, and hence the induced map
H^{n-2}(Y;\mathcal{O}_{Y})\to H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})/R
is zero.

(iii)

The inclusion
\Omega^{1}_{Y}[-1]\to\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y}
induces a surjection
\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y})\to H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})/R
.

(iv)

The homomorphism

\mathbb{C}\to\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y}
induces a surjection
H^{n-1}(Y;\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y})
and hence the composition

H^{n-1}(Y;\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{Y})\to H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})/R

is surjective.

Proof.

The proofs of these statements are minor variations of the proofs of Lemmas
2.3
and
2.4
.
∎

To complete the proof of Theorem
4.1
, define the complex

\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{Y}}=\{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}\to\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}/\operatorname{Spec}A}\}
as in Lemma
2.6
. The proof there shows that the map

H^{n-2}(\mathcal{Y};\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}/\operatorname{Spec}A})\to H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})/R
is surjective. By Lemma
4.3
,
R\subseteq\operatorname{Im}H_{Z}^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})
. Thus there is a well-defined surjection

H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})/R\to H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})/\operatorname{Im}H_{Z}^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y}).

Then the composition
H^{n-2}(\mathcal{Y};\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}/\operatorname{Spec}A})\to H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})/R\to H^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})/\operatorname{Im}H_{Z}^{n-2}(Y;\Omega^{1}_{Y})
is surjective as well.
∎

Finally, we say a few words about the consequences of Theorem
4.1
, following the discussion in
[
Kaw95
]
,
[
Ran95
]
,
[
FM99
, Theorem 2.2]
and in more detail in
[
Gro97
, Theorem 1.9]
. Let
\mathbf{Def}_{Y}
be the deformation functor for
Y
and let
\mathbf{Def}_{Y,Z}
be the local deformation functor for the singular points of
Y
. Thus there is a morphism
\mathbf{Def}_{Y}\to\mathbf{Def}_{Y,Z}
, which at the level of Zariski tangent spaces is the map
\mathbb{T}^{1}_{Y}\to H^{0}(Y;T^{1}_{Y})=\bigoplus_{x\in Z}H^{0}(T^{1}_{Y,x})
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Suppose that
Y
is a singular Calabi-Yau variety of dimension
n\geq 4
such that

(i)

The
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma holds for
\widehat{Y}
.

(ii)

The singularities of
Y
are Du Bois.

(iii)

H^{1}(Y;\mathcal{O}_{Y})=0
.

Then

(i)

If the map
\mathbb{T}^{1}_{Y}\to H^{0}(Y;T^{1}_{Y})
is surjective, then the morphism
\mathbf{Def}_{Y}\to\mathbf{Def}_{Y,Z}
is smooth. In particular, if
(S,0)
is a germ which is a locally semiuniversal deformation of
\mathbf{Def}_{Y,Z}
, then the base of the locally semiuniversal deformation of
Y
is isomorphic to the germ
(H^{1}(Y;T^{0}_{Y})\times S,0)
.

(ii)

In general, if the dimension of the cokernel of the map
\mathbb{T}^{1}_{Y}\to H^{0}(Y;T^{1}_{Y})
is
t
, then there exist
t
functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{t}\in\mathfrak{m}
, where
\mathfrak{m}
is the maximal ideal of
0
in the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{H^{1}(Y;T^{0}_{Y})\times S,0}
, such that the germ of the subspace defined by
f_{1},\dots,f_{t}
has the same underlying reduced subspace as that of the germ of the base of the semiuniversal deformation of
Y
. ∎

These methods have been used in
[
Gro97
, §4]
to give examples of smoothings of singular Calabi-Yau varieties of dimension
3
with isolated rational singularities which are not lci.

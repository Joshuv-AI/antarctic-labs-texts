Recent advances on biogenesis, functions and therapeutic potential of long noncoding RNAs in T cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia
Authors: Parminder Kaur, Pankaj Sharma, Prateek Bhatia, Minu Singh
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
T-cell Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia (T-ALL) is a highly aggressive form of ALL with at least 25% relapse rates. The high relapse rates are often linked to poor prognoses. More detailed studies for novel therapeutic targets for the treatment of T-ALL are required as the genetic and transcriptomic data currently available on T-ALL pathophysiology is insufficient. Long non-coding RNAs are emerging as important players in the regulation of tumour proliferation and metastasis. Studies on various cancers have revealed their potential as biomarkers and therapeutic targets in treatment. This review describes the characterization, biosynthesis, and role of long non-coding RNA in T-ALL and highlights their potential as next generation molecule in development of promising diagnostic, prognostic and/or therapeutic markers.

Introduction

Acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) is the most common malignancy of childhood. B-cell ALL (B-ALL) and T-cell ALL (T-ALL) are the two main sub-types of ALL which are based on the types of immune cells involved. B-ALL is the most common type of ALL, accounting for 85% of the cases while T-ALL comprises the remaining 15%. Though lower in occurrence, patients with T-ALL show a higher relapse rate (~ 25%) as compared to B-ALL [1]. Further, unlike B-ALL, the risk stratification is not possible in T-ALL due to genetic heterogeneity among the patients and therefore all cases are treated upfront as high-risk with intensified therapy regimen. The high-dose multi-agent chemotherapy makes the outcome worse with associated toxicities and long-term side effects. Thus, to decrease the mortality rates, it is important to have an earlier diagnosis and novel treatment drug regimen to counter the relapse rate [2]. The genetic and transcriptomic data currently available on T-ALL is still insufficient and demands alternative approaches such as on the regulatory aspect of the disease pathogenesis and identifying novel targets of treatment, for better management of the patients.

Identification of non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) which form 97% of the human genome has undergone a sudden expansion with the discovery of their role as regulatory elements in the genome [3, 4]. Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs), which are the most abundant (up to 41%) and comprise the most significant class of ncRNAs, are among the most studied ncRNAs in cancers [5]. LncRNA falls under the size category of more than 200 nucleotides without defined open reading frames and generally do not code for proteins. LncRNAs are poorly conserved evolutionary in terms of primary sequences which was the reason they were initially thought of having no functional role [6, 7]. Recent discoveries have shown their role in the regulation of various gene expressions by modulating the epigenetic, transcriptional and post-transcriptional levels.

Initial reports of the regulatory role of lncRNAs were published on the regulation of H19 gene expression in 1990 [6]. Since then, more than 2000 lncRNAs have been reported. However, a large number of lncRNAs are not yet characterized and their biological functions are hotly debated. Though only a few lncRNAs are studied in depth, literature indicates that they play key roles in regulations of different cellular processes. For example, they are shown to be involved in X chromosome inactivation (Xi), genomic imprinting, nuclear compartmentalization, splicing, stem cell pluripotency, cell cycle progression, cellular reprogramming, apoptosis as well as hematopoiesis [8–10]. Changes in the expression of lncRNAs are often linked to the development and progression of various cancers including leukemia [11]. Identifying the functions of specific lncRNAs in hematopoiesis and signaling pathways in the pathophysiology of leukemia can help provide diagnostic, prognostic, and therapeutic strategies for T-ALL disease management. In this review, we have described the most recent findings about lncRNAs in T-ALL pathogenesis including mechanism of action and their role as potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets.

Biogenesis of LncRNAs

The first reports of lncRNAs discovery showed that it displays the properties of mRNA as it undergoes capping at their 5′ ends with 7-methyl guanosine (m7G), polyadenylated at their 3′ ends, and spliced similarly to mRNAs. It does not have conserved ORFs [6]. LncRNA transcription is initiated without the use of protein-coding gene promotors and enhancers and thus have different localization fates [12]. The direction of transcription, strand used for transcription, and position of lncRNAs with respect to coding genes is one of the basis of their characterization as depicted in Fig. 1. These classifications overlap with each other because of complex nature of lncRNAs. Intronic lncRNAs are transcribed from intronic regions while exonic lncRNAs are transcribed only from exonic regions. Intergenic lncRNAs lie between two protein coding genes. Overlapping lncRNAs usually span from one or more introns or exons of protein coding regions. They can be classified as sense if they are coded from strand coding for protein sequence and antisense if coded from an opposite strand. They can be “divergent” if their promoter and the coding transcripts are in close proximity and located in a head-to-head fashion and can be “convergent” if found in the opposite fashion [7, 11]. Most of the lncRNAs are transcribed by polymerase II and undergo processing similar to mRNA. While transcription, lncRNA are more often prematurely cleaved because Polymerase II pauses inefficiently at their promoters [27, 28]. LncRNA that are processed by dysregulated polymerase is usually retained in the nucleus and sometimes degraded by the nuclear exosomal complex while the spliced ones are transported to the cytoplasm by nuclear export factor 1 (NXF1) [29]. Differential splicing leads to the formation of macro lncRNA which originates from unspliced transcripts and retained lncRNAs which contain intronic regions between coding parts [11]. They are also prematurely terminated and cleaved by Pol II-associated endonuclease complex. Many lncRNA escapes this surveillance process and plays their regulatory roles [30]. Further studies to understand their detailed mechanism of biogenesis needs to be conducted in future.

LncRNAs in acute leukemia

Acute leukemia is a type of cancer that involves blood and bone marrow and is characterized by rapid progression of abnormal blast cells. In recent years, studies have shown several lncRNAs that are dysregulated in acute leukemia and plays an important role in its development and progression. Acute myeloid leukemia is the most studied acute leukemia with respect to lncRNAs. CCDC26 was the first lncRNA to be reported in AML and was shown to control the proliferation of leukemic cells by controlling KIT expression [31]. ZNF571-AS1 is also shown to be involved in AML and regulates the KIT and STAT5 by the JAK-STAT signaling pathway [32]. LINC00899 is shown to have the potential to be used as a biomarker and prognostic marker for AML as its expression is often upregulated in AML patients. Its expression is demonstrated to be reduced when patients achieve complete remission, though further signaling pathways associated with it are not known [33]. SNHG5 is also upregulated in AML and is being further studied for its biomarker potential [34]. Ma et al., have shown LINC00265 to be upregulated in AML patients and its association with poor overall survival. The authors have reported that it contributes to AML cell proliferation, migration, and invasion by activating PI3K/AKT signaling [35]. A report by Papaioannou et al. has shown that LncRNA HOXB-AS3 and HOXBLINC are upregulated in NPM1 mutated AML patients and help in the proliferation of leukemic blasts by regulating NPMI-EBP1 interaction. Further in their study they have reported that depletion of HOXB-AS3 shows an anti-leukemic effect suggesting it can be used as target of therapy in AML [36, 37].

Studies show that lncRNAs also play a role in B-ALL development and progression, though such studies are scarce. The first evidence of the same was reported by Fernando et al., showing BALR-2 overexpression in B-ALL patients. It increases leukemic cell proliferation and inhibits apoptosis by regulating the glucocorticoid response pathway. Overexpression of BALR-2 is correlated with poor overall survival and low response to prednisone treatment [38]. TCL6/TCL1B is also shown to be significantly upregulated in ETV6::RUNX1-positive pediatric B-ALL as compared to hyper diploid B-ALL and B-ALL with normal karyotype. Low levels of TCL6 is associated with poor disease-free survival and further studies are underway to test its prognostic potential [39]. LncRNA uc.112 is reported to have a differential expression in T-ALL as compared to B-ALL. Uc.112 is also shown to be differentially expressed among patients with hyper diploidy as compared to other karyotypes [40]. Further, in a study by Bárcenas-López et al. lncRNA LINC00152 and LINC01013 are reported to be differentially expressed in B-ALL patients with early relapse and early mortality suggesting its role as prognostic indicator of early relapse. Their study also demonstrate that LINC00152 regulates the cell-substrate adhesion and peptidyl–tyrosine autophosphorylation related biological processes [41]. In our recent study, we have identified three lncRNAs LINC01221, RP11-472G21.2 and CRNDE being differentially over expressed in T-ALL compared to B-ALL in pediatric population [42]. Thus, multiple reports across different cohorts suggest that expression of various lncRNAs are lineage specific and could be utilized as biomarkers in identifying the subtypes of leukemia following studies with different and larger cohort.

LncRNAs in pathogenesis of T-ALL

T-ALL is a heterogenous disease containing a spectrum of genetic lesions. These genetic lesions include alteration of various signaling pathways, transcription factors, and activation of oncogenes. The genetic alterations along with cellular microenvironments contribute to leukemogenesis, leukemic cell proliferation and therapy resistance. Mutations in NOTCH1 signaling are the most common activating mutation found T-ALL cases (up to 60%) and are responsible for increased leukemic cell growth. MYC, an oncogene is the direct target of NOTCH1 [43]. Chromosomal translocations involving oncogenes and transcription factors are also frequent events in T-ALL. CDKN2A/B (cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor 2A) is one of the most frequent dysregulated genes found in T-ALL. CDKN2A/2B genes deletions are found in > 50% of pediatric T-ALL and are markers of poor prognosis [44]. Among the transcriptional factors, TAL1 alterations is the most frequent and account for 11% of T-ALL cases. Each of these common aberrations is reported to be associated with lncRNA/s. LncRNA associated with deregulation of the TAL1 oncogene

TAL1/SCL (T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia 1/stem cell leukemia) is a class II basic helix-loop-helix family transcription factor that is normally expressed in hematopoietic cells and is downregulated during T-cell development. It binds to the E proteins to form a heterodimer which further binds with GATA1, LM02, and LDBI proteins in erythroid cells. This transcriptional complex is responsible for the regulation of various gene expressions. ETS family proteins and RUNX1 also act in coordination with TAL1 to control gene expressions. These proteins forms autoregulated feedback loop to regulate various target genes and are referred to as “core regulatory circuit”. The TAL1, GATA2, and RUNX1 genes are regulated under super-enhancers in CD34+ human hematopoietic cells where TAL1 acts as the master transcription factor. Alteration in expression of TAL1 leads to leukaemia and is often seen in 40–60% of T-ALL patients. This overexpression results from translocations such as t (1;14) (p32; q11) leading to the fusion of TAL1 gene with the SIL/TAL2 gene accounting for 25–30% of cases or enhancer region mutation. Some chromosomal aberrations result in the juxtaposition of TCR genes to the TAL1 locus and account for 11% cases [52–54].

E proteins also play an important role and B- and T- cell development and show expression pattern like TAL1 in hematopoietic cells. They include E2A/TCF3, HEB/TCF12, and E2-2/TCF4 which form heterodimers or homodimers to regulate gene expression by binding to E-box consensus motifs. E2A is required for TCR expression as it regulates the pre-T-cell receptor alpha, TCR-alpha, RAG1, and RAG2. E proteins play tumour-suppressor role in T-ALL. Overexpression of TAL1 in T-ALL causes sequestering of E protein preventing the formation of E protein dimers. This results in blockage of differentiation of thymocytes at an immature stage because of failure of expression of pre-TCR. Notch1 pathway also causes degradation of E proteins by MAP kinase pathway modification. These indicate that E proteins and TAL1 balance play an important role in tumour suppression and any alteration in their expression causes T-cell leukemogenesis. XLOC_030252 and XLOC_001561

XLOC_030252 is intergenic lncRNA located between MYCN and FAM49A gene locus. It is found in TAL-positive and TLX-positive subgroups of primary ALL. XLOC_001561 is encoded by the RORC gene on chromosome 1 in an antisense direction. This RORC gene plays an important role in normal thymus development. RORC gene is a target of E2A and TAL1 negatively regulates its expression through the inhibition of E-protein dimers. XLOC_030252 is present in hematopoietic stem cells but is downregulated during T-cell development while XLOC_001561 is only present in TAL-1 positive T-ALL cells. It is also negatively regulated by TAL1 and E-proteins. Inhibition of E-protein dimers by TAL-1 causes this negative regulation [15]. XLOC_030252 is bound by TAL1 and their regulatory partner proteins i.e., GATA3, RUNX1 and MYB. In TAL1-positive patients, XLOC_030252 and XLOC_001561 are overexpressed and hence cause leukemogenesis [56].

XLOC_005968 {ARID5B-inducing enhancer associated long noncoding RNA (ARIEL)}

ARID5B is a transcriptional activator that positively regulates the expression of TAL1. It acts as pro-oncogenic factor and help in the proliferation of T-ALL and the radio resistance of cells. ARID5B is expressed in hematopoietic stem cells and is downregulated during the maturation of thymocytes but is activated in T-ALL cells. ARID5B regulates the expression of TAL1 by acting as transcriptional activators and thus functions as pro-oncogenic factor. XLOC_005968 is enhancer overlapping lncRNA which overlaps with 3 exons of protein-coding gene CABCOCO1/C10ORF107 and is expressed specifically in TAL1 positive T-ALL patients. It is expressed at ARID5B-135-kb enhancer locus [15]. ARIEL and ARID5B positively regulate each other. It enhances the chromatin-chromatin interaction between ARID5B enhancer and promoter by recruiting mediator complex to specific genomic elements hence it is termed as ARID5B-inducing enhancer associated long noncoding RNA (ARIEL). It is usually downregulated in double-negative 2 to 4 stages of thymocytes but ARID5B 2135-kb enhancer bound by the TAL1 complex is reported to be increased in T-ALL cells. It affects TAL1 induced transcription by regulating ARID5B. Loss of ARIEL causes decrease mediator binding which results in decreased RNA pol II at both the 2135-kb enhancer and ARID5B promoter regions which further confirms regulatory role of ARID5B. Knockdown of ARIEL in the xenograft model has been shown to decrease disease progression indicating ARIEL plays an important role in T-ALL progression [25, 56].

LncRNA in Notch signalling pathway

Notch signalling pathway play an important role in cell fate determination, cell proliferation, and differentiation. It initiates with the binding of transmembrane NOTCH receptors to ligands expressed on thymic epithelium which directs proteolytic cleavage resulting in activation of Notch intracellular domain (NICD). NICD along with DNA binding proteins forms a transcriptional complex which controls the expression of target genes. It plays an important role in T-lineage specification of lymphoid progenitors. This progression of the T-cell lineage is carried out by interleukin 7 receptor (IL-7R) signalling which is regulated by Notch 1 signalling by binding at the CSL-binding site. Notch1 also regulates the expression of IGF1R in T-ALL cells. Alteration in Notch1 signalling results in T-cell leukaemia and is one of the driver mutations in T-ALL [57–59]. Various lncRNA that are involved in this signalling are explained below.

Leukemia-induced non-coding activator RNA (LUNAR1)

LUNAR1 is the enhancer lncRNA located on chromosome 15 in a 500 kb topologically associating domain which includes IGF1R and PGPEP1L. It shows Notch-dependent expression pattern in T-cell ALL and is localized in nucleus. LUNAR1 is reported to enhance the expression of IGF1R which in turn requires the NOTCH1 signalling to sustain overexpression which results in progression of T-ALL cells [60, 61]. Although, exact mechanism of enhancement is not yet deciphered but it is proposed that it recruits WDR5-containing methyltransferase complexes leading to methylation of histone tails or it stabilizes the RNA Pol III complexes at enhancer elements. High expression levels of LUNAR1, Notch 1 and IGF-1R has shown to result in poor overall survival in T-ALL patients [45, 61].

Lnc-RP11-611D20.2 (NALT: NOTCH1 associated lncRNA in T ALL)

NALT is located 100 bp upstream of the NOTCH1. NALT and NOTCH expressions have been shown to be increased in T-ALL patients by Wang et al. They further show that increased expression of NALT causes an increase in proliferation of leukemic cells which is due to upregulation of NOTCH protein. Additionally they have demonstrated that treatment of tumour in mice xenograft model show a reduction in tumour growth when treated with NALT shRNA and NALT also decreases the side population of stem-like cells by interacting with NOTCH1 [62].

LncRNA related to dysregulation of apoptosis in T-ALL

Dysregulation of apoptosis leads to increase in cell viability and proliferation in leukemia. Apoptosis can act by two pathways: Extrinsic/death receptor pathway and intrinsic/mitochondrial pathways. Interruption of apoptosis can occur at intrinsic or death receptor pathway, extrinsic pathway or due to mitochondrial dysfunction. Extrinsic pathway involves the display of death receptors that belong to TNF receptor family on cell surfaces. Receptors of TNF family have death domain that transmit the death signals. Some of the well-studied ligand receptor interactions are that of FasL/FasR, TNF- α/TNFR1 [63]. The binding between receptors and ligands results in the binding of adaptor proteins which associate with procaspase-8 leading to death effector domain dimerization. Auto-catalysis of procaspase is then initiated with the death-inducing complex resulting in activation of caspase-8 that leads to cell apoptosis [64].

Intrinsic pathway occurs by non-receptor mediated stimulus that acts directly on the targets in the cells. These stimuli induce mitochondrial membrane changes and release of mitochondrial pro-apoptotic proteins such as cytochrome c into the cytoplasm where it forms the complex with the apoptotic protease activating factor 1 (APAF1). This results in activation of procaspase-9 which further activates the caspase-3/6/7. Regulation of these events is carried out by Bcl-2 family proteins by regulating mitochondria membrane permeability. Both intrinsic and extrinsic pathway leads to activation of the execution pathway. Caspase-3, caspase 6 and caspase-7 are executioner caspases while caspase-8, caspase-9, or caspase-10 are initiator caspases [65]. It involves the activation of execution caspases which activate cytoplasmic endonucleases and nuclear proteases. These events ultimately lead to the lysis of cells [66].

T-ALL-R-LncR1

Prostatic apoptosis response protein-4 is coded by the Par-4 gene and is known to have a proapoptotic role. It induces apoptosis by intrinsic and extrinsic pathways. Par-4 is reported to be decreased in various cancers which leads to increased survival of the cells, decreased caspase activity and decreased apoptosis. T-ALL-R-LncR1 is reported to be associated with PAR-4 in T-ALL patients. It is expressed in neoplastic T-cells in T-ALL patients and is not expressed in any other organs. Its full-length cDNA is mapped to 6q24.3 with two exons which has a length of 1468 bp. This sequence contains multiple open reading frames, all of which lack Kozak sequence for translation and do not code for any proteins. T-ALL-R-LncR1 interacts with the Par-4/THAP1 protein complex resulting in the inhibition of apoptosis of leukemic cells. This indicates the role of T-ALL-R-LncR1 in proliferation of tumor cells [14].

Antisense non-coding RNA in the INK4 locus (ANRIL)

ANRIL is located on chromosome 9p21 and is associated with many cancers. Studies by Li et al. showed that expression of ANRIL and transcription factor 4 (TCF4) is increased in T-ALL patients. TCF4 is studied for its abnormal expression in various cancers. TCF4 helps in tumor proliferation by targeting oncogenes like MYC, PRMT 5, CCND 1, CD44, etc. but the detailed mechanism is not known. ANRIL is involved in tumour cell proliferation and decreased apoptotic activity of T-ALL cells by activation of the NF-κB pathway. Upregulation of ANRIL and TCF4 in T-ALL patient results in the downregulation of miR-7-5p. It acts by sponging miR-7-5p which is tumour-suppressive. miR-7-5p bind with the TCF4 indicating ANRIL indirectly influence expression of TCF4. TCF4 up-regulates BIRC5 expression thus increasing the viability of leukemic cells [24].

LINC00511

LINC00511 is shown to be upregulated in various cancers and a similar pattern is seen in T-ALL patients. It acts as competitive endogenous RNA. Knockout studies of LINC00511 showed a decrease in the proliferation of cells indicating it increases the proliferation of leukemic cells. It sponges the miR-195-5p which is also indicated by downregulation in patient samples. miR-195-5p targets the leucine-rich repeat kinase 1 LRRK1 gene which regulates cell proliferation and inhibits apoptosis. LINC00511 sponges the miR-195-5p and upregulates the expression of LRRK1 gene which induces the proliferation of leukemic cells [47].

AWPPH (lncRNA associated with poor prognosis of hepatocellular carcinoma)

AWPPH was first identified in liver and bladder cancer involved in increased cancer progression by interacting with Y-box binding protein 1. AWPPH and Rho-associated protein kinase 2 (ROCK2) are shown to be overexpressed in T-ALL patients and they positively regulate expression of each other. ROCK2 overexpression is an oncogene and is studied in different types of cancer. It regulates the cytokinesis and formation of actin fibres. AWPHH and ROCK2 are overexpressed in T-ALL patients and can be used as biomarkers to differentiate them from healthy controls. AWPHH increases cellular proliferation and acts as oncogene [49]. The detailed mechanism of proliferation and inhibition of AWPPH is still undetermined.

EBLN3P (endogenous bornavirus-like nucleoprotein)

EBLN3P is abnormally expressed in various cancers. It sponges the miR-144-3p and promotes the survival of liver cancer cells. It also binds to the miR-204-5p and promotes the recovery of ganglion neurons. EBLN3P and miR-655-3p expression are shown to be dysregulated in T-ALL samples. Yang and Yang have shown that expression of EBLN3P is upregulated while miR-655-3p is significantly downregulated in T-ALL patients. It negatively regulates the expression of miR-655-3p which is tumour suppressive in nature. The authors further show that MiR-655-3p regulate the cell proliferation by regulating proliferating cell nuclear antigen (PCNA). Knockdown of EBLN3P was shown to decrease the expression of PCNA and MMP-9 expression and enhances caspase-3 activity indicating its role in inhibition of apoptosis [51].

LINC00853

LINC00853 is 669 bp long intergenic non-coding RNA located at chromosome 1p33. LINC00853 was identified by Li et al. which showed differential expression in different ALL subtypes and is downregulated in only T-ALL patients in their study. CC chemokine receptor 9 (CCR9) is member of G-protein coupled receptors expressed on immune cells while CC chemokine ligand 25 (CCL25) is a CC chemokine family molecule. Interaction between CCR9 and CCL25 helps in chemotaxis of monocytes, lymphocytes and T-cell migration in the thymus. Many lncRNAs regulate Chemokine receptors and are linked to many cancers. LINC00853 is one of such lncRNAs which targets CCR9. LINC00853 is shown to negatively regulates the CCR9/CCR25 which promotes tumor metastasis and apoptosis inhibition respectively [46].

CDKN2B-AS1

Chen et al., in their study on pediatric T-ALL have reported lncRNAs CDKN2B-AS1 to be highly expressed in patients as compared to the normal controls. It also shows differential expression in the relapsed patients as compared to the primary patients. Moreover, the authors showed the expression of CDKN2B-AS1 is significantly high in Adriamycin-resistant cell lines. Further, their knockdown studies in Adriamycin resistant cell lines showed that depletion in the level of CDKN2B-AS1 resulted in increased levels of Bax and c-caspase 3 while the levels of Bcl-2 were decreased resulting in increased apoptosis. Depletion of CDKN2B-AS1 also resulted in decreased IC50 of Adriamycin on resistant cell lines indicating its expression plays an important role in chemoresistance. Their in vivo studies on mice model showed that CDKN2B-AS1 depletion results in the suppression of tumor growth when treated with Adriamycin as compared to the control group [50].

lncRNAs involved in response to treatment

Chemoresistance is one of the major challenges faced during the treatment of T-ALL. Patients showing decreased sensitivity to therapy have poor prognosis. The mechanisms underlying the chemoresistance are complex and multifactorial. Various genetic mutations, tumour microenvironment and metabolic processes affect the development of chemoresistance [67]. A number of lncRNAs are shown to be upregulated in chemo-resistant patients and studies are ongoing to use them as therapeutic agents.

ZFAS1

ZFAS1 is associated with increased metastasis in various cancers and is correlated with multi-drug resistance in T-ALL patients. Beta-galactoside alpha-2,6-sialyltransferase 1 (ST6GAL1) is an enzyme involved in addition of sialic acid to terminal groups of glycans and is linked with poor prognosis in T-ALL patients due to drug resistance as it is shown to be expressed only in resistant cell lines. miRNA MiR-150 is reported to decrease the expression of ST6GAL1 and hence acts as tumor suppressor. ZFAS1 targets miR-150 and thus indirectly increases the expression of ST6GAL1 conferring the cells drug-resistance. This interaction mediates the sialyation of EGFR which leads to the activation of P13k/Akt pathway leading to prolonged cell survival and drug-resistance [48].

LncRNA involved in other signaling pathways in T-ALL

NEAT1 and MALAT1

ATP-binding cassette (ABC) transporters overexpression leads to chemotherapy resistance in ALL patients due to ATP-dependent drug efflux. It is regulated by miR-335-3p and miR-335-5p. Dysregulation of these microRNA is often linked to many hematological malignancies. Nuclear paraspeckle assembly transcript 1 (NEAT1) and Metastasis-associated lung adenocarcinoma transcript 1 (MALAT1) are nuclear lncRNA which are overexpressed in various cancers. NEAT1 regulate the gene expression by nuclear editing while MALAT1 causes the localization of nuclear speckles in tumours. NEAT1 and MALAT1 are considered oncogenic in nature. They form complex with miR-335-3p which is tumour suppressive in nature. MALAT1 is shown to regulate the expression of various oncogenic genes such as as P53, SF2/ASF, SFPQ, SRSF1, cyclin D1, cyclin E and CDK6. Further, NEAT1 is reported to be expressed under hypoxic conditions and causes increased cellular proliferation by inhibition of apoptosis [16].

Colorectal neoplasia differentially expressed (CRNDE)

Colorectal neoplasia differentially expressed (CRNDE) is a long non-coding RNA located at locus—hCG_1815491 on chromosome 16 and was first reported to be activated in colorectal cancer. CRNDE is also shown to be overexpressed in various other cancers such as lung cancer, breast cancer, gastric cancer and cervical cancer [68–72]. It is shown to be associated to various pathways such as Wnt/β-catenin, PI3K/AKT/mTOR, Ras/mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) and Notch1 signaling pathways [70, 73–75]. A study by Shehata et al. aimed to check the role of CRNDE in chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CML) showed that CRNDE is downregulated in CML patients and is associated with poor prognostic markers such as high levels of serum beta-2 microglobulin and lactic dehydrogenase, and the presence of del17p [76]. Recent study by Sharma et al. showed that CRNDE shows higher expression in both pediatric T and B-ALL patients as compared to healthy controls. Further, it shows lineage specific expression in T-ALL subtypes and is significantly highly expressed in T-ALL as compared to B-ALL [42].

RP11-620J15.3 lncRNA

RP11-620J15.3 located on chromosome 12 was first reported by Liu et al. in a study focused on hepatocellular carcinoma. Its high expression was shown to be associated with poor prognosis. Further in vivo and in vitro studies showed that RP11-620J15.3 plays role in cancer proliferation and metastasis by enhancing the Warburg effect through glucose-6-phosphate isomerase in HCC cells. However further detailed studies of pathways in other cancers are still awaited [77]. A recent study has shown that RP11-620J15.3 lncRNA show high expression in pediatric T-ALL patients. However, in contrast to previous study, overexpression of RP11-620J15.3 was linked to better overall survival in T-ALL patients [42]. These contradictory reports of RP11-620J15.3 on disease prognosis can be solved by further studies on larger cohorts of specific malignancies.

Long non-coding RNAs as therapeutic targets

As our understanding of LncRNAs deepens, researchers are focussing on exploring their prognostic and therapeutic potentials in leukemia. Prostate cancer antigen 3 (PCA3) is a lncRNA overexpressed in patients with prostate cancer. FDA approval of PCA3 based test highlighted the diagnostic potentials of lncRNAs [78]. This approval proved that lncRNAs can serve as valuable biomarkers for disease detection and management, which extends beyond just prostate cancer. LncRNA MALAT1 is overexpressed in various cancers and knockdown studies of MALAT1 have shown to inhibit tumor progression [16, 79, 80]. Study published by Donlic et al. focused on targeting MALAT1 with small molecule ligands has led to discovery of selective ligand for MALAT1 triple helix [81]. Also, si-MALAT1 in combination with curcumin have shown to significantly inhibit invasion and migration of colon cancer SW480 cells. Further studies in leukemia are still awaited. Recent study by Ostini et al. showed that Myocardial Infarction Associated Transcript (MIAT) is a nuclear lncRNA which act as oncogene in T cell and Chronic myeloid leukaemia [82]. Silencing of MIAT using siRNA in leukemic cells resulted in decreased cell survival and increased apoptosis. Expression of various tumour related genes such as CDK6, the growth factor receptor Fms Related Receptor Tyrosine Kinase 1 (FLT-1), Hypoxia Inducible Factor 1 Alpha Subunit (HIF-1A), c-MYC was downregulated in MIAT silenced leukemic cells while the expression of RELA and Nucleotide Binding Oligomerization Domain Containing 1 (NOD1) was upregulated which was responsible for apoptosis. These findings suggest that MIAT can be used as target therapy to silence various tumor associated genes after more detailed investigations [82]. Resveratrol and berberine currently in trials are also shown to target lncRNAs although they are in trails for other metabolic diseases [83]. Thus, lncRNAs is a promising research candidate in developing lncRNA-based therapies and in-depth studies can provide robust targets for therapeutic utility.

Conclusion and future perspectives

LncRNAs are the most abundant non-coding RNA. They were initially thought to be of transcriptional noise but new advanced studies have shown them to be an important regulators of cancer development and progression. They play an important role in leukemic progression, chemoresistance and decreased apoptosis in leukemic cells. Discovery of lncRNAs in T-ALL has shown new dimensions in understanding disease pathogenesis. Initial reports of lncRNA role in T-ALL was published by Zang et al. which showed T-ALL-R-LncR1 expression in leukemic cells [14]. Further studies helped in discovering various lncRNAs such as ANRIL, ARIEL, LUNAR1, EBLN3P, etc. [24, 25, 45]. LncRNAs such as XLOC_030252, XLOC_001561 and XLOC_005968 shows differential expression in TAL-1 positive patients [15]. LUNAR1 is also shown to be only expressed in T-ALL patients and high expression linked to poor prognosis [45]. However, most of these studies are focused on the expression profiles of lncRNAs and does not provide the detailed account of signaling pathways involved. These expression profiles can be used to refine the risk stratification and treatment options in T-ALL patients after more in-depth studies. Silencing of NALT and LINC00511 results in inhibition of leukemic cell growth and increased apoptosis indicates therapeutic potentials of lncRNAs [47, 62]. Further, their presence in serum and blood plasma makes them an appropriate candidate for marker of diagnostic and prognostic indicator.

Although there is immense potential in the field of lncRNAs research however, there are multiple challenges in exploring its role across different cancers. Some lncRNAs show tissue specific expression which makes lncRNA discovery difficult. The sample of choice in most of the studies is either blood, bone marrow or FFPE tissues. Each of these samples has their own set of disadvantages for detection of lncRNAs. The tissue specific lncRNAs are difficult to detect from blood while degradation of RNA in FFPE poses difficulty in detecting them from such samples. Apart from these, further characterization involves using NGS or expression arrays. The lower abundance of lncRNAs make it difficult to quantify them accurately. Also, the downstream processing involves complex bioinformatics tools for data analysis. T-ALL being heterogenous disease harbors many different mutations making it difficult to draw any direct correlation between expression of lncRNAs and mutations. Differential expression of certain long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) is observed in different forms of cancer, which emphasizes the necessity of having extensive databases that record these expression patterns. These libraries are essential for clarifying the complex genetic landscape of malignancies and supporting focused research projects. Moreover, the lack of lncRNA annotation databases makes it more difficult to characterize newly found transcripts functionally. This scarcity impedes the progress in comprehending novel lncRNAs contributions to cellular physiology and disease pathology. Due to rarity of related reports the prediction the biological roles and mechanisms underlying becomes difficult to assess. Thus, improving our understanding of lncRNA-mediated regulatory networks and their implications in cancer biology requires the creation of strong annotation tools. Overcoming these challenges will help in maximize the exploitation of lncRNA in T-ALL and other cancers for better disease management and to pave the way from bench to bedside.

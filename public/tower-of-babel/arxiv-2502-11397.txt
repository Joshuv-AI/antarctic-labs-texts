Angle structures on pseudo 3-manifolds
Huabin Ge, Longsong Jia, Faze Zhang

Abstract
It is still not known whether a hyperbolic 3 3 -manifold admits an angle structure or not. We consider angle structures with area-curvature ( A , k ) (A,k) on triangulated pseudo 3-manifolds M M in this article. A sufficient and necessary condition for the existence of such angle structures is established. As a consequence, any compact hyperbolic 3 3 -manifold with totally geodesic boundary admits an angle structure. We also derive certain topological information of M M from the existence of such angle structures.

Introduction

In the 1990s, Casson and Rivin (see
[
12
,
20
]
or
[
4
]
for a self-contained exposition) discovered a powerful technique for solving Thurston’s hyperbolic gluing equations. By introducing the concept of angle structures on an ideally triangulated
3
-manifold
M
, they proved that if a maximal volume angle structure exists, it provides solutions to Thurston’s gluing equations, thereby yielding a hyperbolic structure on
M
. Using theories and tools such as normal surfaces, the connection between angle structures and three-dimensional topology is increasingly being discovered. We refer to
[
8
,
10
,
11
,
13
,
20
]
for examples. For a triangulated pseudo
3
-manifold
(M,\mathcal{T})
, Luo-Tillmann
[
16
]
further developed the concept of (generalized) angle structures with area-curvature
(A,\kappa)
.
Assuming
A\leq 0
, a necessary condition for the existence of such angle structures was established in
[
16
]
.

In this article, we show that the necessary condition in
[
16
]
is also sufficient for the existence of such angle structures, by assuming that
(M,\mathcal{T})
admits a semi-angle structure. Let
\{\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n}\}
be the tetrahedra in a triangulated compact pseudo
3
-manifold
(M,\mathcal{T})
. A
normal surface

F
in
(M,\mathcal{T})
is an embedded surface, so as its intersection with any tetrahedron
\sigma_{i}
is a disjoint union of normal disks. There are 7 types of normal disks in each tetrahedron
\sigma_{i}
, including 3 quadrilaterals and 4 triangles. The
normal coordinate
of
F
is an ordered normal disk types
(q_{1},\cdots,q_{3n},t_{1},\cdots,t_{4n})\in\mathbb{R}^{7n}
. These coordinates satisfy a system of compatibility equations with solution space denoted by
\mathcal{C}(M,\mathcal{T})
.

By taking the contributions of each normal disk
t_{i}
or
q_{j}
to the Euler characteristic of
F
, a function
\chi^{\ast}(t_{j})
or
\chi^{\ast}(q_{j})
can be introduced. Linearly extending those
\chi^{\ast}(t_{i})
and
\chi^{\ast}(q_{j})
, the
generalized Euler characteristic function

\chi^{\ast}(\cdot)
is well-defined on
\mathbb{R}^{7n}
(see (
2.6
)-(
2.8
)). It agrees with the Euler characteristic on embedded and immersed normal surfaces, and gives an upper bound for the Euler characteristic of a branched normal surface. Each generalized angle structure
\alpha
determines an area-curvature function
(A,k)
as follows. The
combinatorial area

A(d)
of a normal disk
d\subset\sigma_{i}
is
\sum_{j}\alpha(e_{j})-(k-2)\pi
, where
d
is a
k
-side polygon, and intersects the edge
e_{1},\cdots,e_{k}
of
\sigma_{i}
. In addition, for an interior (boundary, resp.) edge
e
, its
combinatorial curvature

k(e)
is
2\pi
(
\pi
, resp.) minus the sum of the angles surrounding it. Similar to
\chi^{\ast}
, let
\chi^{(A,\kappa)}(\cdot)
(see (
2.9
) for details) be the
Euler characteristic function with area-curvature
(A,\kappa)
, our first result is:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,\mathcal{T})
be a triangulated compact pseudo
3
-manifold. Suppose it admits a semi-angle structure with area-curvature
(A,\kappa)
satisfying
A\leq 0
. If
\chi^{\ast}(s)<\chi^{(A,\kappa)}(s)
for those
s\in C(M,\mathcal{T})
with all quadrilateral coordinates non-negative and at least one quadrilateral coordinates positive, then
(M,\mathcal{T})
admits an angle structure with area-curvature
(A,\kappa)
.

Combining Theorem
1.1
with Proposition
17
of Luo-Tillmann
[
16
]
, we actually provide the following necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of angle structures.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
(M,\mathcal{T})
be a triangulated compact pseudo
3
-manifold. Suppose it admits a semi-angle structure with area-curvature
(A,\kappa)
satisfying
A\leq 0
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

1.

(M,\mathcal{T})
admits an angle structure with area-curvature
(A,\kappa)
;

2.

\chi^{\ast}(s)<\chi^{(A,\kappa)}(s)
for those
s\in C(M,\mathcal{T})
with all quadrilateral coordinates non-negative and at least one quadrilateral coordinates positive.

Given an area-curvature
(A,k)
with
A\leq 0
, if
A=0
occurs precisely on the
(0,0,\pi)
-normal triangles and
A<0
on the other normal triangles, we refer to such a pair
(A,\kappa)
a
flat area-curvature function
. This leads us to:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M,\mathcal{T})
be a triangulated compact pseudo
3
-manifold, and
(A,\kappa)
be a flat area-curvature function. If
(M,\mathcal{T})
admits a semi-angle structure with area-curvature
(A,\kappa)
and at least one dihedral angle around an edge of
\mathcal{T}
lies within
(0,\pi)
, then there exists an angle structure with area-curvature
(A^{\prime},\kappa)
where
A^{\prime}<0
.

As an application of Theorem
1.3
, we can establish the following:

Corollary 1.4
.

Suppose
M
is a compact hyperbolic
3
-manifold with totally geodesic boundary. There is an ideal triangulation
\mathcal{T}
so that
(M,\mathcal{T})
admits an angle structure.

Conversely, the existence of an angle structure with area-curvature
(A,\kappa)
on
(M,\mathcal{T})
reflects certain topological properties of

. Inspired by the work of Jaco-Rubinstein
[
9
]
, Lackenby
[

,
14
]
and Garoufalidis-Hodgson-Rubinstein-Segerman
[
5
]
, we have:

Theorem 1.5
.

For
A\leq 0
and
\kappa\leq 0
, let
M
be a compact orientable pseudo
3
-manifold with non-

boundary, and let
\mathcal{T}
be an ideal triangulation of the interior of
M

implies that

contains no essential spheres, essential tori, essential disks, or essential annuli. Moreover, the ideal triangulation
\mathcal{T}
is
0

It must be mentioned that, there had been several closely related concepts derived from angle structures, e.g. the semi-angle structure
[
10

11
]
, the taut angle structure
[

]
and the generalized angle structure with area-curvature

showed that for

are equivalent. Following that, Hodgson-Rubinstein-Segerman

proved that each cusped hyperbolic

and Hodgson-Rubinstein-Segerman
[

[

.

2.1
Triangulations

Following Jaco-Rubinstein
[
9
]
and Luo
[
15
]
, we have the following contents.

Let
X=\{\sigma_{i},i=1,2,...,n\}
be a collection of pairwise disjoint
3
-simplices, and let
\Phi
be a set of affine isomorphisms between the faces of
3
-simplices in
X
. If
\phi\in\Phi

\phi
maps the point

on a face of
\sigma_{i}
to a point

on a face of
\sigma_{j}

. The isomorphisms of

. The resulting quotient space

is a pseudo
3
-manifold. We denote the quotient mapping from
X
to
X/\Phi
by
p
. It is noted that the non-manifold points in
X/\Phi
only exist in the images under
p
of the centers of some edges and the vertices of
\sigma_{i}
for
i=1,2,...,n
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be the collection of images under
p
of all faces of
\sigma_{i}
for
i=1,2,...,n
in
X
and denote

. Then
\mathcal{T}
is called a

of

. Usually, we also denote that

. Under the quotient mapping

, all images of
3
-simplices in
X
are also denoted by
\{\sigma_{i},i=1,2,...,n\}
and
the images of
0
-faces,
1
-faces,
2
-faces and
3
-faces of
\sigma_{i}(i=1,2,...,n)
are called
vertices, edges, faces and tetrahedra
of
\mathcal{T}
respectively.

We also use
T^{(i)}
for

to denote the the vertices, edges, faces and tetrahedra of
\mathcal{T}
respectively. By Moise
[
19
]
and Bing
[
2
]
, every compact
3
-manifold has a triangulation.

Definition 2.2
.

If the link of each vertex in
|\mathcal{T}|
is a closed non-sphere surface, then

is called an
ideal triangulation
of
M=|\mathcal{T}|\backslash|\mathcal{T}^{(0)}|
. Where the vertices are called the
ideal vertices
.

It is noted that with an ideal triangulation

,

is the interior of a compact pseudo
3
-manifold with non-sphere boundaries. The following are the precisely description.

Suppose
M
is a compact pseudo
3
-manifold with non-empty boundary whose genera are larger than or equal to

. Let
C(M)
be the compact pseudo
3
-manifold obtained by coning off each boundary component of

to a point. In
particular, if
\partial M
has

components, then

has exactly

cone points,
denoted by

. Hence

. The ideal triangulation
\mathcal{T}
of

is actually the ideal triangulation of

as defined in Defintion

. Observed that the link of each

for

in
\mathcal{T}
, up to isotopy, is exactly the corresponding boundary component of

. By Moise
[
19
]
, the interior of every compact

Definition 2.3
.

Let

be a compact pseudo
3
-manifold with non-sphere boundaries and

be an ideal triangulation of the interior of

2.2
Normal surfaces and the compatibility equations

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulation of a pseudo
3
-manifold

and
\sigma

\mathcal{T}
. A properly embedded arc in a

.

A compact surface

in

in

[

Let

x_{i}+y_{j}=x_{i^{\prime}}+y_{j^{\prime}}.

(2.1)

All the equations at every such face of
\mathcal{T}
are called the

. We denote

For

\big\{W_{\sigma_{i}},\,W_{e_{j}}\,|\,i=1,\cdots,n,\,j=1,\cdots,m\big\},

(2.2)

The above definitions can be deduced into the other triangulation cases.

2.4
Area-curvature function

Let
(M,\mathcal{T})
be a pseudo
3
-manifold with a triangulation
\mathcal{T}
and
F
be an embedded or immersed normal surface
F
in
(M,\mathcal{T})
. If the coordinate of
F
is
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{3n},y_{1},\cdots,y_{4n})
where
n
is the number of tetrahedra in
\mathcal{T}
. Then the
generalized Euler characteristic
function
\chi^{*}:\mathbb{R}^{7n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined as

\chi^{*}(x_{1},...,x_{3n},y_{1},...,y_{4n})=\sum_{i=1}^{3n}x_{i}\chi^{*}(q_{i})+\sum_{j=1}^{4n}y_{j}\chi^{*}(t_{j}),

(2.6)

where
q_{i}
and
t_{j}
are the normal quadrilaterals and normal triangles in
F
respectively.

In the above, for a normal triangle
t
in a tetrahedra
\sigma_{i}
, if
b(t)
is the number of normal arcs in
t\cap\partial M
and
t\cap\sigma_{i}
consists of three edges
e_{ik},(k=1,2,3)
with the edges valence
d_{k}
where
1\leq k\leq 3
, then

\chi^{*}(t)=-\frac{1}{2}\big(1+b(t)\big)+\sum_{k=1}^{3}\frac{1}{d_{k}}.

(2.7)

For a normal quadrilateral
q
in a tetrahedra
\sigma_{i}
, if
b(q)
is the number of normal arcs in
q\cap\partial M
and
q\cap\sigma_{i}
consists of four edges
e_{ik^{\prime}},(k^{\prime}=1,2,3,4)
with the edges valence
d_{k^{\prime}}
where
1\leq k^{\prime}\leq 4
, then

\chi^{*}(q)=-\frac{1}{2}\big(2+b(q)\big)+\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{4}\frac{1}{d_{k^{\prime}}}.

(2.8)

If we denote
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{3n},y_{1},\cdots,y_{4n})
by
\overline{F}
, then there is a fact that
\chi^{*}(\overline{F})=\chi(F)
. See Kang-Rubinstain
[
11
]
.

If
\overline{F}
corresponds with a element of
\mathcal{C}(M,\mathcal{T})

, then the
Area-curvature function
on
s
is defined as

\chi^{(A,\kappa)}(s)=\frac{1}{2\pi}(\sum_{t\in\bigtriangleup}y_{t}(s)A(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}2z_{j}\kappa(e_{j})),

(2.9)

where
\bigtriangleup
is the set of all the normal triangles of
s
, and
y_{t}
is the corresponding normal triangle coordinate of

.

Luo-Tillmann (
[
16
]
, Lemma 15) got the following relationship between
\chi^{*}
and
\chi^{(A,\kappa)}

Lemma 2.12
.

Suppose
(M,\mathcal{T})
admits an angle (or semi-angle) structure
\alpha
with area-curvature
(A,\kappa)
. If
s\in\mathcal{C}(M,\mathcal{T})
, then

\chi^{(A,\kappa)}(s)=\chi^{*}(s)-\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{q\in\square}A(q)x_{q}(s),

(2.10)

where

and

is the combinatorial area of

\alpha
.

2.5
Farkas’s lemma

If
x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, we use
x>0
(
x\geq 0
,
x<0
,
x\leq 0
resp.) to mean that all components of
x_{i}
are positive (non-negative, negative, non-positive resp.). To approach our main results, we need the following duality result from linear programming, which is known as Farkas’s lemma, and can be found, for instance, in
[
23
]
. In the following lemma, vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
\mathbb{R}^{l}
are considered to be column vectors.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
A
be a real
k\times t
matrix,
b\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, and
\cdot
be the inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
.

1.

\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{t}|Ax=b\}\neq\emptyset
if and only if for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
such that
A^{T}y=0
, one has
y\cdot b=0
.

2.

\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{t}|Ax=b,x\geq 0\}\neq\emptyset
if and only if for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
such that
A^{T}y\leq 0
, one has
y\cdot b\leq 0
.

3.

\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{t}|Ax=b,x>0\}\neq\emptyset
if and only if for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
such that
A^{T}y\neq 0
and
A^{T}y\leq 0
, one has
y\cdot b<0
.

2.6
Kojima decomposition

To prove Theorem
1.5
, we need to demonstrate Kojima’s decomposition of a hyperbolic
3
-manifold with totally geodesic boundary into truncated hyperideal polyhedra.

Following Bao-Bonahon
[
1
]
, in the projective space model
B^{3}\subset RP^{3}
of
\mathbb{H}^{3}
, a
hyperideal polyhedra
refers to the intersection
\hat{P}
of
\mathbb{H}^{3}
with a compact convex polyhedra
\tilde{P}
of
RP^{3}
such that

1.

each vertex of
\tilde{P}
lies outside of
\mathbb{H}^{3}
which is called the
hyperideal vertex
, and

2.

each edge of
\tilde{P}
intersects
\mathbb{H}^{3}
non-empty.

Truncation
of a hyperideal polyhedra
\hat{P}
at the hyperideal vertex
v
in
\tilde{P}
is defined as cutting off the thick end towards
v
from
\hat{P}
by a geodesic plane
H_{v}
which is perpendicular to the adjacent geodesic faces at
v
. According to Kojima’s lemma
2.1

[
18
]
, this truncation is unique. The resulting
3
-cell
P
after truncation at every hyperideal vertex of
\tilde{P}
is called a
truncated hyperideal polyhedron
. The boundary of
P
consists of the intersections of
P
with the faces of
\tilde{P}
and the intersections of
\tilde{P}
with the planes
H_{v}
. The former are referred as the
internal faces
and the latter as the
external faces
of
P
. The internal faces of
P
are all right-angled hyperbolic polygons, while the external faces of
P
are hyperbolic polygons.

It is noteworthy that a truncated hyperideal tetrahedron is a specific type of truncated hyperideal polyhedron where its untruncated polyhedron is a tetrahedron in
\mathbb{RP}^{3}
. See Figure
1
for an example.

Figure 1:
Truncated Hyperideal Tetrahedron

In
[
18
]
, Kojima established the following theorem:

Theorem 2.14

(Kojima)
.

Every hyperbolic
3
-manifold with totally geodesic boundary is a quotient of finitely many hyperideal polyhedra in
\mathbb{H}^{3}
by isometries between pairs of faces.

Proof.

. The details are as follows which is combinatorially the same as that in Lackenby
[

[

.

All the polyhedra and polygons of
\mathcal{P}
will respectively mean untruncated hyperideal polyhedra and untruncated hyperideal polygons. We use the projective model
\mathbb{H}^{3}\subset\mathbb{RP}^{3}.
Recall that a polyhedron is a

if its faces consist of an
n
-gon and
n

n
-gon to a point
v\in\mathbb{RP}^{3}.
The point
v
and the
n
-gon are respectively called the

and the

of the pyramid. Let

be an untruncated hyperideal polyhedron and

be the untruncated polyhedron of
\mathcal{P}
. We arbitrarily pick an vertex
v
of

Then there is a decomposition of

into pyramids with tips at
v
and bases the faces of

disjoint from
v
by taking cone at
v.
For the base
D
of each of the pyramids, we arbitrarily pick an vertex
w
and decompose
D
into triangles by taking cone at
w.
The decomposition of
D
extends to a decomposition of the pyramid into tetrahedra. In this way,
Q
is decomposed into a union of tetrahedra
\{\sigma_{i}\}.
In turns, the intersections of
\{\sigma_{i}\}
with

give the decomposition of
P

. See Figure

For all the pillows, we insert enough flat truncated hyperideal tetrahedra into them. Then the resulting is an ideal truncated triangulation
\mathcal{T}
of

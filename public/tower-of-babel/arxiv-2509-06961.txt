Equivalence of Quaternionic Heisenberg Homogeneous Quasi-norms
O. A. Ariyo, M. E. EgweDepartment of Mathematics, University of Ibadan, Ibadan, Nigeria

Abstract
Let ℍ q \mathbb{H}_{q} denote the quaternionic Heisenberg group of dimension ( 4 ​ n + 3 ) (4n+3) with ℝ 4 × ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{4}\times\mathbb{R}^{3} stratification. We identify certain homogeneous norms on the group and show that any two quasi-norms on ℍ q \mathbb{H}_{q} are equivalent for n < ∞ n<\infty .



Keywords:
Quaternionic Heisenberg Quasi-norms; Homogeneous Norm; Equivalence of Norms.

2
The Homogeneous quasi-norms and Equivalence

The quasi-norms on Quaternionic Heisenberg group are homogeneous norms and are compatible with the group’s stratification. Moreover, these norms respect the non-Euclidean geometry and dilations on the group as well as the Carnot-Carathéodory metric resulting from the horizontal vector fields.

Definition 2.1
.

A quasi-norm on the quaternionic Heisenberg group is a function

|\cdot|_{\mathbb{H}_{q}}:\mathbb{H}_{q}\longrightarrow[0,\infty)

(2.1)

satisfying;

(i)

\displaystyle{|\delta_{\rho}\nu|_{\mathbb{H}_{q}}=\rho^{Q}|\nu|_{\mathbb{H}_{q}}}
,
\rho>0
; where
Q
is the degree of homogeneity

(ii)

\displaystyle{|\nu|_{\mathbb{H}_{q}}\geq 0}
and
\displaystyle{|\nu|_{\mathbb{H}_{q}}=0\iff\nu=0}
(non-negativity)

(iii)

\displaystyle{|\nu^{-1}|_{\mathbb{H}_{q}}=|\nu|_{\mathbb{H}_{q}}}

(iv)

\displaystyle{|\nu_{1}\nu_{2}|_{\mathbb{H}_{q}}\leq K\left(|\nu_{1}|_{\mathbb{H}_{q}}+|\nu_{2}|_{\mathbb{H}_{q}}\right)},\penalty\ K\geq 1
(quasi-triangle inequality)

for all
\nu:=(u,t)\in\mathbb{H}_{q}
.

Note that in quasi-norms, the triangle inequality property of norms is replaced with

\|x\cdot y\|\leq K(\|x\|+\|y\|)\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for\penalty\ some}\penalty\ K>1

(2.2)

where norm is implied when
K=1
. So we shall call (
2.2
) the quasi-triangle inequality.

Let (
2.1
) be a homogeneous quasi-norm on
\mathbb{H}_{q}
and
\delta_{\rho}
a dilation on
\mathbb{H}_{q}

2.1
) is said to be dilation invariant if

\|\delta_{\rho}(q,t)\|_{\mathbb{H}_{q}}=\rho\|(q,t)\|

Any norm on the Quaternionic Heisenberg group is homogeneous and of degree
Q=4n+6
with respect to the dilation of the group, i.e.,
\displaystyle{|\delta_{\rho}\nu|_{\mathbb{H}_{q}}=\rho^{Q}|\nu|_{\mathbb{H}_{q}}}
for any
\nu\in\mathbb{H}_{q}

The quaternionic quasi-norms include;

1.

The Korányi or the gauge norm is defined by

\|(q,t)\|_{\mathbb{H}_{q}}=\left(|q|^{4}+|t|^{2}\right)^{1/4}.

Note that
\displaystyle{|q|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|q_{i}|^{2}}
and
\displaystyle{|q_{i}|}
defines the classical quaternionic norm;
|t|=\left(\displaystyle{\sum_{i=1}^{3}t_{i}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
is the usual Euclidean norm on
t\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. This Korányi-type norm is a homogeneous norm of degree
1
in close relation to the dilation of the Quaternionic Heisenberg group defined earlier. It is known that this norm is smooth away from the origin and satisfies the following conditions;

(a)

\displaystyle{|(q,t)^{-1}|=|(q,t)|}

(b)

\displaystyle{|(q,t)|=0\implies q=0,\penalty\ t=0}

This norm satisfies the quasi-triangle inequality being symmetric and sub-additive up to a multiplicative constant.

To see this, (a) is trivial since
(q,t)^{-1}=(-q,-t)
and for (b), we show that
\displaystyle\|(q,t)\cdot(q^{\prime},t^{\prime})\|\leq K\left(\|(q,t)\|+\|(q^{\prime},t^{\prime})\|\right);\penalty\ \penalty\ K\geq 1

Recall that the product of any two elements
\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in\mathbb{H}_{q}
is given by

\nu\cdot\nu^{\prime}=(q,t)\cdot(q^{\prime},t^{\prime})=(q+q^{\prime},t+t+2\Im(q\cdot\bar{q^{\prime}})).

Then

\displaystyle\|(q,t)\cdot(q^{\prime},t^{\prime})\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|(q+q^{\prime}),t+t^{\prime}+2\Im(q\cdot\bar{q^{\prime}})\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\|q+q^{\prime}\|^{4}+\|t+t^{\prime}+2\Im(q\cdot\bar{q^{\prime}})\|^{2}\right)^{1/4}

Note that
\displaystyle|q+q^{\prime}|^{4}\leq 8(|q|^{4}+|q^{\prime}|^{4})
and

\displaystyle|t+t+2\Im(q\cdot\bar{q^{\prime}})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left(|t+t^{\prime}|^{2}+16|\Im(q\cdot\bar{q^{\prime}})|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left(|t|^{2}+|t^{\prime}|^{2}+16|q|^{2}|q^{\prime}|^{2}\right)

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\|(q,t)\cdot(q^{\prime},t^{\prime})\|^{4}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K^{\prime}\left(|q|^{4}+|q^{\prime}|^{4}+|t|^{2}+|t^{\prime}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K\left(\|(q,t)\|+\|(q^{\prime},t^{\prime})\|\right)

Definition 2.3
.

Any two quasi-norms
\|\cdot\|_{u}
and
\|\cdot\|_{v}
on Quaternionic Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}_{q}:=\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
are said to be equivalent if there exists constants
k_{1},k_{2}>0
such that
\displaystyle{k_{1}\|(q,t)\|_{u}\leq\|(q,t)\|_{v}\leq k_{2}\|(q,t)\|_{u},\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall\penalty\ (q,t)\in\mathbb{H}_{q}.}

To prove equivalence of norms, for instance, the Korányi and the max-type quasi-norms are equivalent since we can find an upper and lower bounds as follows;

\displaystyle\|(q,t)\|_{\mathbb{H}_{q}}=(|q|^{4}+|t|^{4})^{1/4}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(2\max(|q|^{4},|t|^{2})\right)^{1/4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{1/4}\max\left(|q|,|t|^{1/2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{1/4}\|(q,t)\|_{\max}

This defines the upper bound; and

\displaystyle\|(q,t)\|_{\mathbb{H}_{q}}=\left(|q|^{4}+|t|^{2}\right)^{1/4}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\max(|q|^{4},|t|^{2})\right)^{1/4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max(|q|,|t|^{1/2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|(q,t)\|_{\max}

defines the lower bound.

Hence, the equivalence is expressed as
\displaystyle{\|(q,t)\|_{\max}\leq\|(q,t)\|_{K}\leq 2^{1/4}\|(q,t)\|_{\max}}.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
|\nu|_{\mathbb{H}_{q_{1}}}
and
|\nu|_{\mathbb{H}_{q_{2}}}
be any two continuous homogeneous norms on
\mathbb{H}_{q}
invariant under dilation. Then
|\nu|_{\mathbb{H}_{q_{1}}}
and
|\nu|_{\mathbb{H}_{q_{2}}}
are equivalent.

Proof.

The statement of the theorem implies that we seek constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that
\forall\penalty\ \nu:=(u,t)\in\mathbb{H}_{q}
we have

C_{1}|\nu|_{\mathbb{H}_{q_{1}}}\leq|\nu|_{\mathbb{H}_{q_{2}}}\leq C_{2}|\nu|_{\mathbb{H}_{q_{1}}}.

Let
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
be unit spheres defined by
\displaystyle S_{1}=\{(u,t)\in\mathbb{H}_{q}:|(u,t)|_{\mathbb{H}_{q_{1}}}=1\}
and
\displaystyle S_{2}=\{(u,t)\in\mathbb{H}_{q}:|(u,t)|_{\mathbb{H}_{q_{2}}}=1\}
.

The spheres so defined are compact in
\mathbb{H}_{q}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
since
|(u,t)|_{\mathbb{H}_{q}}
is continuous and positive away from zero. Now define
\displaystyle\varphi:S_{2}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
by
\displaystyle\varphi\left((u,t)\right)=|(u,t)|^{\delta_{\rho}}=\rho^{Q}|(u,t)|,\penalty\ \penalty\ Q\geq 1
. Then by continuity property of the distance function
|(u,t)|_{\mathbb{H}_{q_{2}}}
and compactness of
S_{1}
,
\varphi
attains minimum on
S_{1}
and maximum on
S_{2}
denoted by
m
and
M
respectively. If we let
\rho:=|(u,t)|_{\mathbb{H}_{q_{1}}}
, we will have
\delta_{\rho}(u,t)\in S_{1}
, so that
\displaystyle{|(u,t)|_{\mathbb{H}_{q_{2}}}=|\delta_{\rho}(u,t)|_{\mathbb{H}_{q_{2}}}=\rho|(u,t)|_{\mathbb{H}_{q_{2}}}}

\displaystyle\implies\penalty\ m|(u,t)|_{\mathbb{H}_{q_{1}}}\leq|(u,t)|_{\mathbb{H}_{q_{2}}}\leq M|(u,t)|_{\mathbb{H}_{q_{1}}};\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall\penalty\ (u,t)\in\mathbb{H}\setminus(0,0)
.
∎

Theorem 2.5
.

The Box norm is a Euclidean-type norm on
\mathbb{H}_{q}
and is nonhomogeneous with respect to the Quaternionic Heisenberg group dilation.

Proof.

It suffices to show the non-homogeneity of this norm. To do this, we see by definition that

\displaystyle\|\delta_{\rho}(q,t)\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{|\rho q|^{2}+|\rho^{2}t|^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\rho^{2}|q|^{2}+\rho^{4}|t|^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\rho^{2}\left(|q|^{2}+\rho^{2}|t|^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho\sqrt{|q|^{2}+\rho^{2}|t|^{2}}\neq\rho\|(q,t)\|.

Therefore, the Box norm on
\mathbb{H}_{q}
is non-homogeneous on
\mathbb{H}_{q}
.
∎

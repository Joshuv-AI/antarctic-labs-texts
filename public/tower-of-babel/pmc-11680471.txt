Stem cells in pulmonary hypertension: Current understanding and future challenges
Authors: Xiaoyi Hu, Jinming Liu, Xiao Song, Ping Yuan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractStem cells possess the unique ability to develop into different cell types within the body. Researchers are exploring the use of different types of stem cells to potentially repair damaged blood vessels, reduce inflammation, and improve overall vascular function, all of which are crucial factors in pulmonary hypertension (PH). While it is important to acknowledge that further clinical studies and trials are necessary to fully understand the efficacy and safety of stem cell therapy for PH, ongoing research and initial findings present promising avenues for potentially developing new treatments or therapeutic strategies for PH.Keywords: pulmonary hypertension, stem cells

CURRENT KNOWLEDGE OF STEM CELLS IN PULMONARY HYPERTENSION (PH)

PH

Pulmonary hypertension (PH) is a clinical and pathophysiological syndrome characterized by structural or functional changes in the pulmonary arteries caused by various heterogeneous diseases and different pathogenic mechanisms. This results in elevated pulmonary vascular resistance and increased pulmonary arterial pressure, eventually leading to right heart failure and potentially death. 1 PH is categorized into 5 groups: pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH, group 1), PH associated with left heart disease (group 2), PH associated with lung diseases and/or hypoxia (group 3), PH associated with pulmonary artery obstructions (group 4), PH with unclear and/or multifactorial mechanisms (group 5). 1 While targeted therapies such as calcium channel blockers, endothelin receptor antagonists, phosphodiesterase 5 inhibitors, soluble guanylate cyclase, and prostacyclin analogues can improve quality of life for patients with PH, the 5‐year mortality rate still remains high. 1 However, the introduction of innovative stem cell technologies has brought new hope for addressing these issues. 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 , 7

Stem cells

Stem cells, known for their undifferentiated nature, possess a remarkable ability for self‐renewal, high proliferation, and the potential to differentiate into various cell types. These cells play crucial roles in the body's growth and development, and the onset of disease, making them highly sought‐after in cell therapy. 8 Among the different types of stem cells, mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs), endothelial progenitor cells (EPCs), and induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) have emerged as the most commonly used for the treating of PH. 2

MSCs are primarily located in various tissues and organs such as adipose tissue, bone marrow, dental pulp, placenta, and umbilical cord. These cells possess a wide range of biological characteristics, including the secretion of numerous cytokines, immunomodulatory properties, the capacity for multipotent differentiation, tissue repair and regeneration abilities, as well as anti‐inflammatory and anti‐apoptotic effects. 2 , 3 , 4 , 5

EPCs are a type of stem cell derived from the bone marrow, and possess the unique ability to repair and rejuvenate vascular endothelium. They play a vital role in maintaining the health of the heart and blood vessels, being involved in important physiological functions such as angiogenesis, repairing damaged endothelium, and regulating vascular tone. 2 , 3 , 4 , 5

Induced pluripotent stem cells (IPSCs) are a type of pluripotent stem cell that can be generated through the artificial reprogramming of non‐pluripotent adult somatic cells. This transformation is achieved by inducing the expression of specific genes and transcription factors that are responsible for maintaining the pluripotent state of embryonic stem cells. 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 This technique holds great promise in the field of regenerative medicine and research, as it enables the creation of patient‐specific pluripotent stem cells. These cells have the potential to be used for tissue transplantation or for personalized studies of various diseases.

Current studies of stem cells in PH

The effect of EPCs on PH

Previous studies have demonstrated that EPC infusion therapy shows promise in reversing PH in various models, such as chronic thromboembolic PH models and monocrotaline‐induced PH (MCT‐PH) rats. 2 , 9 Specifically, in MCT‐PH rats, EPC infusion therapy has been observed to improve hemodynamic parameters and increase survival rates. 9 Additionally, one study has reported significant improvements in abnormal hemodynamics in the piglet model of chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension with the use of EPCs. 10 The mechanisms underlying the beneficial effects of EPC infusion therapy include vascular function repair, apoptosis inhibition, and the potential application of gene‐edited EPC infusion therapy. 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 These findings highlight the potential of EPCs in mitigating PH and emphasize the diverse mechanisms through which this therapy may exert its beneficial effects.

It is encouraging to observe substantial advancements in the application of EPCs within the realm of PH. Six clinical trials have been conducted, each with distinct objectives concerning EPCs and their potential in treating PH. Notably, three of these trials were dedicated to demonstrating the clinical feasibility of autologous EPC transplantation therapy in patients with idiopathic PAH. Two trials concurrently aimed to explore the relationship between EPCs and PH, while another trial sought to establish the clinical viability of endothelial nitric oxide synthase‐EPC transplantation therapy in patients with progressive PH. 2 Among the six trials, three have reached completion. The outcomes of these trials demonstrated enhancements in hemodynamic parameters and the 6‐min walk distance among PH patients who underwent EPC infusion. These results strongly suggest the potential of EPCs as a therapeutic target for PH. 11 , 12 , 13

The effect of MSCs on PH

MSC infusion therapy has shown promise in enhancing hemodynamic parameters in models of PH. 2 , 14 Luo et al. 15 observed that MSC treatment attenuated hypoxia‐induced PH in murine models, while the administration of MSCs markedly ameliorated the aberrant hemodynamics observed in an MCT‐induced PH model. 16 The beneficial effects of MSC infusion therapy are attributed to a range of mechanisms, including the restoration of vascular functionality, the inhibition of endothelial‐to‐mesenchymal transition, the reduction of inflammation and apoptosis, the modulation of the microbiota, and the application of gene‐edited MSCs. 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 Collectively, these mechanisms contribute to the observed improvement in the hemodynamic parameters of PH models.

Investigation of the potential of MSCs in treating PH has led to the initiation of three clinical trials. These trials aimed to demonstrate the potential clinical effectiveness of autologous adipose tissue‐derived MSC transplantation therapy in patients with COPD‐PH, to establish the clinical viability of autologous MSC transplantation therapy in individuals with systemic sclerosis‐related PH, and to assess the clinical viability of autologous MSC transplantation therapy in preterm infants with bronchopulmonary dysplasia. 2 Unfortunately, the results of these trials remain undisclosed. 2

The effect of iPSCs on PH

In rats of MCT‐PH, the application of iPSC therapy has resulted in an improvement in right heart dysfunction, which in turn led to enhanced hemodynamic parameters. 2 , 17 The mechanisms underlying the effects of iPSCs infusion are principally centered on the restoration of vascular function and the suppression of inflammation. 2 , 3 , 4 , 5

FUTURE CHALLENGES AND PROSPECTS OF STEM CELL THERAPY IN PH

There are challenges associated with the clinical application of stem cells in the future. One of the significant challenges is ensuring rigorous regulatory oversight and establishing high clinical and ethical standards for stem cell therapies. It is essential to develop clear guidelines for the safe and effective use of stem cells in various treatments, and it is important to highlight that ongoing clinical trials primarily focus on patients with severe PH. Currently, there is no evidence supporting the effectiveness of stem cell therapy in treating mild PH, as individuals with mild PH are typically advised to manage disease progression with conventional drugs before considering stem cell therapy. Therefore, a systematic evaluation of indications for stem cell therapy is imperative. 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 Additionally, the potential risk of tumor formation is a significant concern, as even a single cell out of a million with harmful mutations could lead to catastrophic consequences due to the robust self‐renewal and differentiation capabilities of stem cells. Furthermore, continued investment in research and development is necessary to fully explore the potential of stem cell therapies, including personalized treatment and the development of novel stem cell‐based interventions. Overall, while challenges persist, the prospects for stem cell therapy in patients with PH are promising, particularly with increased research, infrastructure development, and a focus on ethical and accessible use of stem cell treatments.

CONCLUSION

Stem cells possess the unique ability to differentiate into various cell types within the body. Researchers are investigating the use of various types of stem cells to potentially repair damaged blood vessels, mitigate inflammation, and enhance overall vascular function, all of which are crucial factors in PH. Although it is essential to recognize that additional clinical studies and trials are necessary to fully comprehend the efficacy and safety of stem cell therapy for PH, ongoing research and preliminary findings offer promising avenues for developing novel treatments and therapeutic strategies for PH.

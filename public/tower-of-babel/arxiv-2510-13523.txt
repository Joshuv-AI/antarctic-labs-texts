Weak unipotence and Langlands duality
Jia-jun Ma, Shilin Yu

Abstract
. Weak unipotence of primitive ideals is a crucial property in the study of unitary representations of reductive groups. We establish a sufficient condition, referred to as mild unipotence , which guarantees weak unipotence and is more accessible in practice. We establish mild unipotence for both the q q -unipotent ideals defined by McGovern [ McG94 ] and unipotent ideals attached to nilpotent orbit covers defined by Losev-Mason-Brown-Matvieievskyi [ LMBM24 ] . Our proof is conceptual and uses the bijection between special orbits in type D D and metaplectic special orbits in type C C found in [ BMSZ23 ] in an essential way.



1.
Introduction

In
[
Vog84
]
, Vogan defined the notion of weak unipotence of primitive ideals of the universal enveloping algebra of a reductive Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, see
Definition

2.1
. This notion plays a crucial role in the study of unitary representations of reductive groups (see
[
Vog84
]
and
[
DMB25
]
).
In
[
BV85
, Proposition 5.10]
, Barbasch and Vogan proved weak unipotence of special unipotent ideals attached to even orbits in the Langlands dual Lie algebra
{\check{\mathfrak{g}}}
of
\mathfrak{g}
. It is natural to ask if there are more weakly unipotent primitive ideals that arise naturally from nilpotent orbits.
In
[
McG94
]
, McGovern introduced the notion of
q
-unipotent infinitesimal characters and
q
-unipotent ideals for classical Lie algebras. In
[
LMBM24
]
, Losev, Mason-Brown, and Matvieievskyi attached unipotent ideals to nilpotent orbit covers. For linear classical groups of type
B
,
C
and
D
, their unipotent ideals are special cases of
q
-unipotent ideals, while for type
A
, spin and exceptional groups, the unipotent ideals in
[
LMBM24
]
provide new examples. In all these cases, the primitive ideals are maximal primitive ideals with certain infinitesimal characters. Therefore we will also speak of weakly unipotence of an infinitesimal character, which just means the weakly unipotent of the maximal primitive ideal with this infinitesimal character.

The main result of this paper is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a complex semisimple Lie algebra.

(i)

if
{\mathfrak{g}}
is of classical type, then q-unipotent infinitesimal characters (cf.
Definition

3.6
) and unipotent ideals attached to covers of nilpotent orbits in
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
are weakly unipotent.

(ii)

if
{\mathfrak{g}}
is of exceptional type, then all unipotent ideals attached to birational rigid covers of nilpotent orbits in
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
are weakly unipotent.

To prove the above theorem, we introduce the concept of mild unipotence (
Definition

2.4
) of a two-sided ideal in
\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}
, which has already been implicitly used in
[
BV85
]
. This concept is based on the theory of cells developed by Kazhdan-Lusztig and Barbasch-Vogan (see
[
BMSZ25
, Section 3]
for an exposition of the theory). We then show that mild unipotence implies weak unipotence (
Corollary

2.6
) which is equivalent to some containment conditions of nilpotent orbits in the Lie algebra attached to the dual of the integral root system of
\lambda
(
Corollary

2.11
).

Then we show that McGovern’s
q
-unipotent ideals are all mildly unipotent (
Theorem

3.13
). The proof is based on the bijection between special orbits in type
D
and metaplectic special orbits in type
C
found in
[
BMSZ23
]
, as well as the Springer duality between special orbits in type
B
and type
C
. Both will be recalled in
Section

3.2
. Using these bijections, we are able to reduce the case of
q
-unipotent infinitesimal characters to the case of special unipotent infinitesimal characters, for which
[
BV85
, Lemma 5.7]
can again be applied (see
Proposition

2.12
). Since the original proof of
[
BV85
, Lemma 5.7]
is uniform and case-free, our proof of mild/weak unipotence of
q
-unipotent ideals is conceptually simple and does not require any complicated combinatorial computation.

We also study more general mildly unipotent infinitesimal characters in the case of type
A
in
Section

3.5
, for which a light combinatorial computation is needed. Combining with the case of
q
-unipotent infinitesimal characters, we can prove mild/weak unipotence of a larger class of infinitesimal characters in
Section

3.6
, which can be regarded as deformations of
q
-unipotent infinitesimal characters. This allows us to prove mild/weak unipotence of all unipotent ideals attached to nilpotent orbit covers of classical Lie algebras in
Corollary

3.26
, which includes the case of covers of orthogonal Lie algebras
\so(N)
that are equivariant under the spin groups
\Spin(N)
but not the orthogonal groups
\SO(N)
.

The case of exceptional groups is analyzed in
Section

4
and uses the
atlas
software
[
atl
]
.

2.
Weak and mild unipotence

2.1.
Weak unipotence

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a complex reductive Lie algebra and
\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}
be the universal enveloping algebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.
By the Harish-Chandra isomorphism, the center
\mathcal{Z}\mathfrak{g}
of
\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}
is isomorphic to the algebra
(S\mathfrak{h})^{W}\simeq\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}^{*}/W]
of
W
-invariant polynomials on
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, where
\mathfrak{h}
is the abstract Cartan subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
W
is the abstract Weyl group. We identify the set of infinitesimal characters of
\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}
with
\mathfrak{h}^{*}/W
and use
\chi_{\lambda}
to denote the infinitesimal character of
\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}
corresponding to an orbit
W\cdot\lambda
in
\mathfrak{h}^{*}/W
.
It is well known that there is a maximal primitive ideal
J_{\mathrm{max}}(\lambda)
of
\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}
for each infinitesimal character
\chi_{\lambda}
. Let
G
be a connected complex reductive group with Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Let
X^{*}\subset\mathfrak{h}^{*}
be the corresponding weight lattice of
G
and
\mathfrak{h}^{*}_{\mathbb{R}}\subset\mathfrak{h}^{*}
be the real span of
X^{*}
. We fix a
W
-invariant inner product on
\mathfrak{h}^{*}_{\mathbb{R}}
and write
\lVert\cdot\rVert
for the associated norm.

Suppose
M
is a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module and
\gamma\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}/W
. We define

(1)

{\mathrm{Pr}}_{\gamma}(M):=\{m\in M|\forall z\in\mathcal{Z}({\mathfrak{g}}),(z-\chi_{\gamma}(z))^{k}m=0\text{ for some positive integer }k\}

to be the generalized eigenspace of
M
with respect to the infinitesimal character
\chi_{\gamma}
.
Now suppose
M
has generalized infinitesimal character
\chi_{\lambda}
. For any finite-dimensional representation
F
of
G
, we can form the tensor product
M\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}F=M\otimes F
which is again a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module. By
[
Vog81
, Corollary 7.1.13]
(cf.
[
Kos75
, Theorem 5.1]
) we have a (finite) direct sum decomposition

M\otimes F=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}/W}{\mathrm{Pr}}_{\gamma}(M\otimes F).

In fact,
[
Vog81
, Corollary 7.1.13]
says that
{\mathrm{Pr}}_{\gamma}(M\otimes F)\neq 0
only if
\gamma=\lambda+\mu
for some weight
\mu
of
F
.

Definition 2.1

(c.f.
[
Vog84
, Definition 8.16]
)
.

Let
M
be a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module with generalized infinitesimal character
\chi_{\lambda}
for
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}
. We say that
M
is
weakly unipotent
with respect to
G
(or its weight lattice
X^{*}
) if, for any finite-dimensional representation
F
of
G
(or equivalently,
F
with weights in
X^{*}
) and any
\nu\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}/W
such that
\lVert\nu\rVert<\lVert\lambda\rVert
, we always have
{\mathrm{Pr}}_{\nu}(M\otimes F)=0
.

We call
\chi_{\lambda}
a
weakly unipotent infinitesimal character
if
J_{\mathrm{max}}(\lambda)
is weakly unipotent.

When
{\mathfrak{g}}
is semisimple, we will often take
G
to the adjoint group
G_{ad}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. There are primitive ideals that are weakly unipotent with respect to root lattices but not weight lattices, see
Example

3.15
.

The following result is the analogue of
[
DMB25
, Proposition 4.13]
and the proof is exactly the same. It means that the notion of weak unipotence in fact only depends on the annihilator ideal of the
\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}
-module. Therefore we can talk about weak unipotence of a two-sided ideal in
\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Fix
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}_{\mathbb{R}}
and let
I\subset\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}
be a two-sided ideal with generalized infinitesimal character
\chi_{\lambda}
. Let
G
be a connected algebraic group with Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
.
Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

Every
\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}
-module annihilated by
I
is weakly unipotent with respect to
G
.

(ii)

There exists a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module
M
with
\Ann_{\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}}(M)=I
such that
M
is weakly unipotent with respect to
G
.

(iii)

If
\lVert\nu\rVert<\lVert\lambda\rVert
for some
\nu\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}_{\mathbb{R}}
and
F
is a finite dimensional algebraic representation of
G
, then
\Ann_{\mathcal{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})}(\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}/I\otimes F)\not\subset\ker\chi_{\nu}
.

2.2.
Coherent families and Goldie rank representations

Coherent families of group representations were introduced by Schmid
[
Sch77
]
. See also
[
Zuc77
]
and
[
SV80
]
.
We refer the reader to
[
Vog81
, Chapter 7]
as a general reference for coherent families in this setting. We refer the reader to
[
BMSZ25
, § 3 & § 4]
for the notations.

Fix a coset
\Lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}/X^{*}
where
\mathfrak{h}
and let
Q
be the root lattice of
\mathfrak{g}
.
The integral Weyl group

W(\Lambda)
is defined by

W(\Lambda):=\{w\in W|\langle{\lambda-w\lambda,}{\check{\alpha}}\rangle\in\mathbb{Z},\text{ for all }\lambda\in\Lambda\text{ and coroot }\check{\alpha}\}=\{w\in W|\lambda-w\lambda\in Q\}.

It is known that
W(\Lambda)
is the Weyl group of a root system

R(\Lambda):=\{\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})|\langle\check{\alpha},\lambda\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ for all }\lambda\in\Lambda\}.

Fix a positive system
R^{+}(\Lambda)
of
R(\Lambda)
.
We call an element
\lambda\in\Lambda

dominant
if
\langle\lambda,\check{\alpha}\rangle\geq 0
for all
\alpha\in R^{+}(\Lambda)
.
Let
\Lambda^{+}
be the cone of all dominant elements in
\Lambda
.
On the other hand, we define

W_{\Lambda}:=\{w\in W|w\lambda\in\Lambda\text{ for all }\lambda\in\Lambda\}=\{w\in W|\lambda-w\lambda\in X^{*}\}

Therefore, the coset
W_{\Lambda}/W(\Lambda)
space has a set of representatives

B:=\set{b_{1},\cdots,b_{k}}

such that
b_{i}R^{+}(\Lambda)=R^{+}(\Lambda)
for all
i=1,\cdots,k
.

We record the following easy lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\lambda\in\Lambda
be a dominant element. Then
b^{-1}\lambda
is also dominant for every
b\in B
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in R^{+}(\Lambda)
. Then
b\alpha\in R^{+}(\Lambda)
by the definition of
B
.
Therefore,

\langle b^{-1}\lambda,\check{\alpha}\rangle=\langle\lambda,b\check{\alpha}\rangle\geq 0.\qed

We fix a Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}
of
\mathfrak{g}
and consider the category
\mathrm{Rep}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{b})
of finitely generated
\mathfrak{g}
-modules that are unions of finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{b}
-submodules. Let
\mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{b})
be the Grothendieck group of
\mathrm{Rep}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{b})
.
Let
\Coh_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{K}({\mathfrak{g}},\mathfrak{b}))
be the space of coherent families with value in
\mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{b})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}
based on
\Lambda
, see
[
BMSZ25
, Definition 3.3]
. It is a
W_{\Lambda}
-module with the action given by
w\cdot\Psi(\nu)=\Psi(w^{-1}\nu)
for all
\Psi\in\Coh_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{K}({\mathfrak{g}},\mathfrak{b}))
,
\nu\in\Lambda
and
w\in W_{\Lambda}
. We restrict the action to
W(\Lambda)
and therefore regard it as a
W(\Lambda)
-action. Moreover, there is also a
W
-action on
\Coh_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{K}({\mathfrak{g}},\mathfrak{b}))
commuting with the
W_{\Lambda}
-action. In summary,
\Coh_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{K}({\mathfrak{g}},\mathfrak{b}))
is a
W\times W(\Lambda)
-module. See
[
BMSZ25
, Section 3.2]
.

An element
\Psi\in\Coh_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{K}({\mathfrak{g}},\mathfrak{b}))
is called
basal
if
\Psi(\nu)
is either zero or irreducible for all
\nu\in\Lambda^{+}
.
For a basal element, let
\braket{\Psi}_{L}
(resp.
\braket{\Psi}_{R}
,
\braket{\Psi}_{LR}
) be the smallest
W
-invariant (resp.
W(\Lambda)
-invariant,
W\times W(\Lambda)
-invariant) basal subspace of
\Coh_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{K}({\mathfrak{g}},\mathfrak{b}))
containing
\Psi
, called the left (resp. right, two-sided) cone (representation) of
\Psi
. This defines left (resp. right, two-sided) preorders
\leq_{L}
(resp.
\leq_{R}
,
\leq_{LR}
) on the set of basal elements. The equivalence relations associated to these preorders are denoted by
\approx_{L}
,
\approx_{R}
and
\approx_{LR}
(we will mostly use
\approx_{LR}
only). The equivalence classes are called left, right and two-sided cells respectively. Relevant to cone representations, we also have the notions of left, right and two-sided cell representations. The notion of two-sided cell representations induces a partial order
\leq_{LR}
and equivlanece relation
\approx_{LR}
on the set
\Irr(W(\Lambda))=\widehat{W(\Lambda)}
of isomorphism classes of all irreducible representations of
W(\Lambda)
. A equivalence class for
\approx_{LR}
on
\Irr(W(\Lambda))
is called a two-sided cell of
\Irr(W(\Lambda))
. It is known that each two-sided cell of
\Irr(W(\Lambda))
contains a unique Goldie rank/special representation of
W(\Lambda)
, hence gives a bijection between two-sided cells and special representations of
W(\Lambda)
.
We refer the reader to
[
Jos80
, Section 5]
and
[
BV83
, Section 2]
, as well asSection 3.4, Definition 3.20, Proposition 3.22 of
[
BMSZ25
]
for more details.

Let
p_{J}
denote the Goldie rank polynomial of the primitive ideal
J
. Joseph
[
Jos80
]
showed that
\sigma_{J}:=\spn\Set{W(\Lambda)p_{J}}
is an irreducible
W(\Lambda)
-subrepresentation occurring in
S(\mathfrak{h})
, called the Goldie rank representation attached to
J
. By
[
BV83
, Corollary 2.16]
, each double cell in
\Irr(W(\Lambda))
contains a unique Goldie rank representation of
W(\Lambda)
.
Moreover, by
[
BV83
, Theorem 2.29]
,
\sigma_{J}
is a
special representation
of
W(\Lambda)
in the sense of Lusztig (
[
Lus79
,
Lus82
]
), which means that its fake degree is the same as its generic degree. Let
\Irr(W(\Lambda))^{sp}\subset\Irr(W(\Lambda))
denote the set of isomorphism classes of all special representations of
W(\Lambda)
.
By Duflo
[
Duf77
, Theorem 1]
, all primitive ideals of infinitesimal character
\lambda
are of the form
\Ann(L(w\lambda))
for some
w\in W(\Lambda)
.
For
w_{1},w_{2}\in W(\Lambda)
,
\sigma_{\Ann(L(w_{1}\lambda))}=\sigma_{\Ann(L(w_{2}\lambda))}
if and only if
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
are in the same double cell of
W(\Lambda)
, see
[
BV83
, Proposition 2.28]
.

2.3.
Special nilpotent orbits and dualities

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a reductive Lie algebra over
\mathbb{C}
with Weyl group
W
, and
{\check{\mathfrak{g}}}
be its Langlands dual Lie algebra with the same Weyl group
W
. The
{\check{\mathfrak{h}}}
be the abstract Cartan subalgebra of
{\check{\mathfrak{g}}}
, which is identified as the linear dual
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
of the abstract Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Let
\mathcal{N}_{o}
and
\check{\mathcal{N}}_{o}
denote the set of all nilpotent orbits in
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
and
{\check{\mathfrak{g}}}^{*}
, respectively. For any
\mathbb{O}\in\mathcal{N}_{o}
, let
\mathrm{Loc}(\mathbb{O})
be the set of isomnorphism classes of irreducible
G_{ad}
-equivariant local systems over
\mathbb{O}
. The Springer correspondence is an injective map

\spr_{\mathfrak{g}}:\widehat{W}\hookrightarrow\{(\mathbb{O},\rho)\,|\,\mathbb{O}\in\mathcal{N}_{o},\,\rho\in\mathrm{Loc}(\mathbb{O})\}.

We will also abbreviate
\spr_{\mathfrak{g}}
to
\spr
when the relevant Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
is clear from the context.
For any special representation
\sigma
, it is known that
\spr_{\mathfrak{g}}(\sigma)
is of the form
(\mathbb{O}_{\sigma},\mathbbm{1})
, where
\mathbbm{1}
stands for the trivial local system over
\mathbb{O}_{\sigma}
(
[
Lus79
]
). We say that
\mathbb{O}_{\sigma}
is the
special orbit
associated with the special representation
\sigma
. Let
\mathcal{N}_{o}^{sp}
denote the set of all special nilpotent orbits in
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
. Then we can regard
\spr_{\mathfrak{g}}
as a bijection

(2)

\spr_{\mathfrak{g}}:\Irr(W)^{sp}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{N}_{o}^{sp}.

Note that the notion of special representations is intrinsic to the Weyl group
W
, hence we also have

(3)

\spr_{{\check{\mathfrak{g}}}}:\Irr(W)^{sp}\xrightarrow{\sim}\check{\mathcal{N}}_{o}^{sp},

where
\check{\mathcal{N}}_{o}^{sp}
is the set of all special nilpotent orbits in
{\check{\mathfrak{g}}}^{*}
.

By
[
BV85
, Proposition 3.24]
, there is an order-reversing involution on the set of two-sided cells in
W
, which we denote by
d
. Since there is a bijection between two-sided cells and special representations of
W
, we also have an order-reversing involution on the set of special representations of
W
, which we also denote by
d(\sigma)=\check{\sigma}
. At the level of special representations, this involution is given by sending a special representation
\sigma
to the unique special representation
\check{\sigma}
in the same double cell as
\sigma\otimes\sgn
, where
\sgn
denotes the sign representation of
W
. Therefore we have
\check{\sigma}\approx_{LR}\sigma\otimes\sgn
. Note that in classical case,
\sigma\otimes\sgn
is always a special, so
\check{\sigma}=\sigma\otimes\sgn
. This is almost also true for exceptional types, except for three cases in type
E_{7}
and
E_{8}
(see
[
BV85
, Definition 4.5]
).

Combing with (
2
), we have an order-reversing involution
\mathcal{N}_{o}^{sp}
, found by Lusztig and Spaltenstein (
[
Spa82
]
),

(4)

d_{LS}=\spr_{\mathfrak{g}}\circ\,d\circ\spr_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}:\mathcal{N}_{o}^{sp}\to\mathcal{N}_{o}^{sp},\quad\mathbb{O}_{\sigma}\mapsto\spr_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbb{O}_{\check{\sigma}}).

Barbasch and Vogan
[
BV85
]
also defined a second order-reversing bijection

(5)

d_{BV}=\spr_{\mathfrak{g}}\circ\,d\circ\spr_{{\check{\mathfrak{g}}}}^{-1}:\check{\mathcal{N}}_{o}^{sp}\to\mathcal{N}_{o}^{sp},\quad\mathbb{O}_{\sigma}\mapsto\spr_{{\check{\mathfrak{g}}}}(\mathbb{O}_{\check{\sigma}}).

Moreover,
d_{BV}
can be extended to an order-reversing map

(6)

d_{BV}:\check{\mathcal{N}}_{o}\to\mathcal{N}_{o}^{sp}\subset\mathcal{N}_{o}

as follows. Given any
\check{\mathbb{O}}\in\check{\mathcal{N}}_{o}
, we choose a point
e\in\check{\mathbb{O}}
. By Jacobson-Morozov theorem,
e
can be completed into an
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}
-triple
(e,h,f)
with
f
nilpotent and the semisimple element
h
lying in
{\check{\mathfrak{h}}}
. The element
h
is uniquely determined up to conjugation by
W
and is independent of the choice of
e
or the
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}
-triple. We write
h_{\check{\mathbb{O}}}=h\in{\check{\mathfrak{h}}}/W
and define
\lambda_{\check{\mathbb{O}}}=\frac{1}{2}h_{\check{\mathbb{O}}}\in{\check{\mathfrak{h}}}/W
. Since
{\check{\mathfrak{h}}}\simeq\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, we can regard
\lambda_{\check{\mathbb{O}}}\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}/W
as a character of the center
\mathcal{Z}\mathfrak{g}
of the universal enveloping algebra
\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}
of
\mathfrak{g}
. We consider the associated variety
\mathcal{V}(J_{\mathrm{max}}(\lambda_{\check{\mathbb{O}}}))
of the maximal primitive ideal
J_{max}(\lambda_{\check{\mathbb{O}}})
of
\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}
with infinitesimal character
\lambda_{\check{\mathbb{O}}}
. By
[
Jos
, Theorem 3.10]
, the associated variety of any primitive ideal in
\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}
is the Zariski closure of a unique nilpotent orbit
\mathbb{O}
in
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
. We define
d_{BV}(\check{\mathbb{O}})=\mathbb{O}
. It is shown in
[
BV85
, Proposition A2]
that the image of
d_{BV}
is precisely
\mathcal{N}_{o}^{sp}
. Following
[
BV85
]
, we call
\lambda_{\check{\mathbb{O}}}
and
J_{\mathrm{max}}(\lambda_{\check{\mathbb{O}}})
the special unipotent infinitesimal character and special unipotent ideal, respectively, attached to
\check{\mathbb{O}}
.

2.4.
Mildly unipotence

Let
J
be a primitive ideal of
\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}
with infinitesimal character
\chi_{\lambda}
.
Let
\sigma_{J}
be the Goldie rank representation of
W(\Lambda)
attached to
J
, which is also the special representation attached to the double cell in
W(\Lambda)
containing
w
. We also write
\sigma_{\lambda}:=\sigma_{J_{max}(\lambda)}
for the maximal primitive ideal
J_{max}(\lambda)
with infinitesimal character
\chi_{\lambda}
.
Fix a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
\mathfrak{g}
. For any finite dimensional representation
F
of
\mathfrak{g}
, let
\wt(F)\subset\mathfrak{h}^{*}
be the set of all weights of
F
with respect to
\mathfrak{h}
.

We now introduce a variant of the notion of weak unipotence for primitive ideals, which will be justified by
Lemma

2.5
and
Corollary

2.6
below.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}
and
J
be a primitive ideal of
\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}
with infinitesimal character
\lambda
. Let
\sigma_{J}
be the Goldie rank representation of
W(\Lambda)
attached to
J
. We say that
J
is
mildly unipotent
with respect to an algebraic group
G
(or its weight lattice
X^{*}
) with Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
, if whenever
\sigma_{J}\leq_{LR}\sigma_{\nu}
for some element
\nu\in\Lambda=\lambda+X^{*}
, we must have
\lVert\nu\rVert\geq\lVert\lambda\rVert
, .

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
F
be a finite dimensional representation of
\mathfrak{g}
with weights in
\Lambda
. Let
\Psi
be a basal coherent family in
\Coh_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{b}))
such that
J=\Ann(\Psi(\lambda))
.
If
(F\otimes\Psi(\lambda))_{\nu}\neq 0
, then
\sigma_{J}\leq_{LR}\sigma_{b^{-1}\nu}
for some
b\in B
.

Proof.

By the definition of coherent families, we have

\begin{split}{\mathrm{Pr}}_{\nu}(F\otimes L(w\lambda))&={\mathrm{Pr}}_{\nu}(F\otimes\Psi(\lambda))\\
&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\in\wt(F),\\
\mu+\lambda\in W_{\Lambda}\cdot\nu\end{subarray}}\Psi(\lambda+\mu)\\
&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\in\wt(F),\,b\in B,\\
\lambda+\mu\in W(\Lambda)\cdot b^{-1}\cdot\nu\end{subarray}}\Psi(\lambda+\mu)\end{split}

If
{\mathrm{Pr}}_{\nu}(F\otimes L(w\lambda))\neq 0
, then there exists some weight
\mu

F

\Psi(\lambda+\mu)\neq 0

\lambda+\mu=u^{-1}b^{-1}\cdot\nu

and

where

is given by the right
W(\Lambda)
-action on
\Coh_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{b}))
. Write

as a linear combination of basal elements,

u\cdot\Psi=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}\Psi_{i},

where
0\neq a_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
and
\Psi_{j}\in\Coh_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{b}))
are distinct basal element. Then there exists some

such that
\Psi_{j}(b^{-1}\nu)\neq 0
.
Again by
[
BMSZ25
, Corollary 3.17]
,
\Psi_{j}(d^{-1}\nu)
is an irreducible
\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}
-module, with the corresponding primitive ideal denoted by

and the Goldie rank representation
\sigma_{I}=\sigma_{\Psi_{j}}
.
Note that
\Psi_{j}\in\braket{\Psi}_{R}
by definition. In particular,
\Psi_{j}\in\braket{\Psi}_{LR}
and hence
\sigma_{J}\leq_{LR}\sigma_{I}
.

On the other hand, we have

. Since
b^{-1}\nu
is dominant, by the translation principle again (
[
BMSZ25
, Corollary 3.17]
and
[
BJ77
, Theorem 2.12]
), there exists a basal element
\Psi^{\prime}\in\Coh_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{b}))
such that

and
\Psi^{\prime}\in\braket{\Psi_{j}}_{L}

\sigma_{I}\leq_{LR}\sigma_{b^{-1}\nu}
.
We thus conclude that

which is the desired result.
∎

Lemma 3.18

Lemma 3.19
.

Let
\mathbf{q}_{1}
and
\mathbf{q}_{2}
be two partitions of
n
such that
\mathbf{q}_{1}\preceq\mathbf{q}_{2}
. Then for any
r\in(-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}]
, we have

\lVert\xi_{r}(\mathbf{q}_{1})\rVert\leq\lVert\xi_{r}(\mathbf{q}_{2})\rVert.

Moreover, when
r\notin\{0,\tfrac{1}{2}\}
, the equality holds if and only if
\mathbf{q}_{1}=\mathbf{q}_{2}
.

Proof.

Set
s(\mathbf{q}_{i}):=\lVert\xi_{r}(\mathbf{q}_{i})\rVert^{2}
,
i=1,2
. Let
L
be the maximum of numbers of columns of
\mathbf{q}_{i}
,
i=1,2
. Write the columns of
\mathbf{q}_{i}
as
c_{1}(\mathbf{q}_{i})\geq c_{2}(\mathbf{q}_{i})\geq\cdots c_{L}(\mathbf{q}_{i})
,
i=1,2
. Here we allow
c_{j}(\mathbf{q}_{i})=0
for some
j
. Define the non-decreasing sequence
a_{1},\ldots,a_{L}
by

a_{j}:=\left(r+(-1)^{j-1+\lfloor r\rfloor}\left\lfloor\frac{j}{2}\right\rfloor\right)^{2}.

Set
C_{j}(\mathbf{q}_{i}):=\sum_{k=1}^{j}c_{k}(\mathbf{q}_{i})
for
j\geq 0
and
i=1,2
(note that
C_{0}(\mathbf{q}_{i})=0
).
Then summation by parts gives

(24)

s(\mathbf{q}_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{L}c_{j}(\mathbf{q}_{i})a_{j}=C_{L}(\mathbf{q}_{i})a_{L}+\sum_{j=1}^{L-1}C_{j}(\mathbf{q}_{i})(a_{j}-a_{j+1})=na_{L}+\sum_{j=1}^{L-1}C_{j}(\mathbf{q}_{i})(a_{j}-a_{j+1}).

Therefore

s(\mathbf{q}_{1})-s(\mathbf{q}_{2})=\sum_{j=1}^{L-1}(C_{j}(\mathbf{q}_{1})-C_{j}(\mathbf{q}_{2}))(a_{j}-a_{j+1}).

Since
\mathbf{q}_{1}\preceq\mathbf{q}_{2}
, taking transpose gives
\mathbf{q}_{1}^{t}\succeq\mathbf{q}_{2}^{t}
and hence
C_{j}(\mathbf{q}_{1})-C_{j}(\mathbf{q}_{2})\geq 0
for all
j
. On the other hand,
a_{j}-a_{j+1}\leq 0
since
a_{j}
is non-decreasing. These two facts together with (
24
) gives
s(\mathbf{q}_{1})\leq s(\mathbf{q}_{2})
.

When
r\notin\{0,\tfrac{1}{2}\}
, the sequence

is strictly increasing. Therefore the equality

holds if and only if

for all

, which is equivalent to
\mathbf{q}_{1}=\mathbf{q}_{2}
.
∎

attached to a connected cover

of any nilpotent orbit in

.

4.
The case of exceptional groups

Instead of checking that all unipotent ideals for simple exceptional Lie algebra are weakly unipotent, we describe a very simple algorithm to check the mild unipotence of the unipotent ideals
J_{max}(\lambda)
attached to birationally rigid covers for exceptional simple Lie algebras, as studied in
[
MBM23
, §  4.3.1 & 4.3.2]
. This is already enough for the applications in
[
DMB25
]
, see Corollary 5.23 and Theorem 5.25 there.
We have implemented this algorithm in the
atlas
software.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be an exceptional simple Lie algebra. Let
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}_{\mathbb{R}}
be the infinitesimal character of the unipotent ideal
I(\widetilde{\mathbb{O}})=J_{max}(\lambda)
attached to a birationally rigid cover
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}
of a nilpotent orbit
\mathbb{O}
in
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. For any
\nu\in\Lambda
, let
n_{\nu}
be the number of roots of the reductive subalgebra
{\check{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\nu}=\mathfrak{z}_{{\check{\mathfrak{g}}}}(\nu)
.
We run through all
\nu\in\Lambda
such that
\lVert\nu\rVert\leq\lVert\lambda\rVert
, and check whether
n_{\nu}>n_{\lambda}
. If this is the case, then

\dim\left(\Ind_{{\check{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\lambda}}^{{\check{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\Lambda}}\mathbf{0}\right)>\dim\left(\Ind_{{\check{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\nu}}^{{\check{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\Lambda}}\mathbf{0}\right)

and hence

\Ind_{{\check{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\lambda}}^{{\check{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\Lambda}}\mathbf{0}\not\preceq\Ind_{{\check{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\nu}}^{{\check{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\Lambda}}\mathbf{0}.

By
Corollary

2.11
, this implies that the unipotent ideal
I(\widetilde{\mathbb{O}})
is mildly unipotent, with respect to the choice of
X^{*}
. We have checked this for both root lattice and weight lattice for all simple exceptional Lie algebras and all unipotent ideals attached to birationally rigid covers.

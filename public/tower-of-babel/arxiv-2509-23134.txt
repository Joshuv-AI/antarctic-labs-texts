Vector Horndeski black holes in nonlinear electrodynamics Preprint: RIKEN-iTHEMS-Report-25, YITP-25-152, WUCG-25-11
Che-Yu Chen 1, Antonio De Felice 2, Shinji Tsujikawa 3, Taishi Sano 3

Abstract
On a spherically symmetric and static background, we study the existence of linearly stable black hole (BH) solutions in nonlinear electrodynamics (NED) with a Horndeski vector-tensor (HVT) coupling, with and without curvature singularities at the center ( r = 0 r=0 ). Incorporating the electric charge q E q_{E} and the magnetic charge q M q_{M} , we first show that nonsingular BHs can exist only if q M = 0 q_{M}=0 . We then study the stability of purely electric BHs by analyzing the behavior of perturbations in the metric and the vector field. Nonsingular electric BHs are unstable due to a Laplacian instability in the vector perturbation near the regular center. In the absence of the HVT coupling ( β = 0 \beta=0 ), singular BHs in power-law NED theories can be consistent with all linear stability conditions, while Born-Infeld BHs encounter strong coupling due to a vanishing propagation speed as r → 0 r\to 0 . In power-law NED and Born-Infeld theories with β ≠ 0 \beta\neq 0 , the electric fields for singular BHs are regular near r = 0 r=0 , while the metric functions behave as ∝ r − 1 \propto r^{-1} . Nevertheless, we show that Laplacian instabilities occur for regions inside the outer horizon r h r_{h} , unless the HVT coupling constant β \beta is significantly smaller than r h 2 r_{h}^{2} . For β ≠ 0 \beta\neq 0 , we also reconstruct the NED Lagrangian so that one of the metric functions takes the Reissner-Nordström form. In this case, there exists a branch where all squared propagation speeds are positive, but the ghost and strong coupling problems are present around the BH center. Thus, the dominance of the HVT coupling generally leads to BH instability in the high-curvature regime.

Introduction

The existence of black holes (BHs) is a fundamental prediction of Einstein’s General Relativity (GR)
Schwarzschild 1916
.
In the absence of rotation, the BH solution in Einstein-Maxwell theory is described by the
Reissner-Nordström (RN) spacetime

, characterized by a mass
M
and electric and/or magnetic charges

The stability of RN solutions can be analyzed by considering linear perturbations of both the metric and the electromagnetic field

;
Moncrief 1974b
;
Zerilli 1974
;
Moncrief 1975

timelike and spacelike regions.
Consequently, the RN BH solution is stable against linear perturbations on a static and spherically
symmetric (SSS) background.

In Einstein-Maxwell theory, the Lagrangian is given by the
Einstein-Hilbert term,

M_{\rm Pl}^{2}R/2

is the reduced Planck mass,

denotes the field strength of a vector field
A_{\mu}
.
One can consider BH solutions in a more general framework where

In Einstein-NED theory, the behavior of the electromagnetic field on the SSS background is modified compared to that in Einstein-Maxwell theory, leading to corresponding changes in the background metric.
This allows one to distinguish between the two theories through observations of gravitational waves

In Einstein-NED theory, there have been attempts to construct nonsingular BHs for which the curvature scalars remain finite at the center (

However, an analysis of BH perturbations near the regular center reveals that vector-field perturbations invariably develop a Laplacian instability in such nonsingular BHs

;
De Felice and Tsujikawa 2025b

Since this instability triggers the rapid growth of metric perturbations, nonsingular BHs in Einstein-NED theory cannot remain stable.

In Einstein-NED theory, the vector field

is not directly coupled to gravity.
To overcome the difficulties in constructing regular BHs without instabilities, one may introduce couplings between the vector field and the curvature tensors.
It is desirable to construct an interacting Lagrangian whose Euler-Lagrange equations remain second order, thereby avoiding Ostrogradsky instabilities

Moreover, maintaining the

The HVT coupling does not eliminate the curvature singularity at

However, it remains to be seen whether the stability of BHs is also ensured inside the outer horizon under the bounds derived in Ref.
Chen et al. 2024

In this paper, we study the BH solutions and their stabilities against linear perturbations in
Einstein-NED-HVT theory given by
the total Lagrangian

{\cal L}_{\rm T}=M_{\rm Pl}^{2}R/2+{\cal L}(F)+\beta L^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}F_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}

First, we aim to clarify whether linearly stable, nonsingular BHs can be realized by incorporating both the NED and HVT couplings.
For this purpose, we consider the magnetic charge
q_{M}
in addition to the electric
charge
q_{E}
.
We will show that the realization of nonsingular BHs at the background level requires
q_{M}=0
.
Next, we investigate the linear stability of purely electrically charged BHs that possess regular centers. We find that the squared propagation speed of vector-field perturbations in the even-parity sector,
c_{\Omega 4}^{2}
, is always negative for such nonsingular electric BHs.
Thus, in Einstein-NED-HVT theory, there are no regular charged BH solutions that satisfy all theoretically consistent conditions.

Although nonsingular BHs without theoretical pathologies do not exist in Einstein-NED-HVT theory, it may still allow the presence of linearly stable singular BHs with curvature singularities at
r=0
. To address this issue, we focus on purely electrically charged BHs and study their stability against linear perturbations.
For this purpose, we formulate a BH perturbation theory valid in both timelike and spacelike regions. In the presence of a singularity at
r=0
, the linear perturbation theory is expected to break down below the effective-field-theory (EFT) length scale

r_{\rm EFT}
.
Since nonlinear perturbations are significant only close to
r=0
, the EFT scale should lie well below the outer event horizon radius,
r_{h}
. The linear perturbation theory is valid not only for distances
r>r_{h}
but also in the regime
r_{\rm EFT}<r<r_{h}
, where
0<r_{\rm EFT}\ll r_{h}
.

We will first revisit the stability of BHs in Einstein-Maxwell-HVT theory by analyzing the behavior of perturbations in the spacelike region.
We find that there exists a characteristic distance
r_{g}
, dependent on
\beta
, below which both ghost and Laplacian instabilities appear.
To avoid these instabilities, we require that

r_{g}<r_{\rm EFT}
.
This puts a tight constraint on the coupling
\beta
, typically
|\beta|\lesssim r_{\rm EFT}^{3}/r_{h}
, so that
|\beta|\ll r_{h}^{2}
for
r_{\rm EFT}\ll r_{h}
.
In such cases, the observational signatures of the HVT coupling for
r>r_{h}
, such as in BH quasinormal modes, are likely to be difficult to detect.

In Einstein-NED theory without the HVT coupling (
\beta=0
), we also investigate the stability of BHs in power-law NED, with
{\cal L}(F)=F+a_{p}F^{p}
, and in Born-Infeld theory, with
{\cal L}(F)=(2/b)\left(1-\sqrt{1-bF}\right)
,
where
a_{p}
,
p
, and
b
are
constants. In power-law NED with an integer
p\geq 2
, we show that all linear stability conditions are satisfied for
a_{p}>0
at any distance
r
, in both timelike and spacelike regions. In Born-Infeld theory,

c_{\Omega 4}^{2}
approaches 0 as
r\to 0
, giving rise to
a strong coupling problem. To avoid this issue, the Born-Infeld coupling is constrained to be small,
i.e.,
b<2r_{\rm EFT}^{4}/q_{E}^{2}
.
When the HVT coupling is present (
\beta\neq 0
), we find that
c_{\Omega 4}^{2}<0
near
r=0
for both power-law NED and Born-Infeld theories. Therefore, unless the coupling
\beta
is sufficiently small, as mentioned above, the presence of the HVT coupling renders the BH solutions unstable. For
\beta\neq 0
, we also reconstruct the NED Lagrangian
{\cal L}(F)
so that one of the metric functions takes the same form as the RN BH. In this case, Laplacian instabilities are absent for all
r>0
, but ghosts appear, along with strong coupling near
r=0
.
Therefore, when the HVT coupling dominates at high curvature, it typically induces ghosts, Laplacian instabilities, or strong coupling.

For nonsingular BH solutions, this equation can be used
to fix the series coefficients of
f
in Eq. (
19
)
in terms of those of

(or vice versa).
Following this procedure, we obtain

0=-3q_{M}^{2}f_{0}r^{-4}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-2})\,.

(23)

Since

q_{M}=0\,,

(24)

independently of the value of
q_{E}
.
This argument excludes both purely magnetic (
q_{E}=0
,
q_{M}\neq 0
)
and dyonic (
q_{E}\neq 0
,
q_{M}\neq 0
) BHs.
The absence of nonsingular BHs for
q_{M}\neq 0
arises
from the nonvanishing right-hand side of Eq. (
11
)
when
\beta\neq 0
. For the purely magnetic BH with

A_{0}^{\prime}=0
, we have

\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}-\frac{h^{\prime}}{h}=-\frac{24\beta q_{M}^{2}}{r(M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r^{4}+4\beta q_{M}^{2})}\,.

Integrating this equation with respect to

\frac{f}{h}={\cal C}\left(1+\frac{4\beta q_{M}^{2}}{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r^{4}}\right)^{3/2}\,,

{\cal C}
is an integration constant.
We require

from
vanishing when
h(r)\neq 0

Using the expansion of
h(r)
in Eq. (
19
),
Eq. (
26
) yields the following expansion
for
f(r)
around the BH center:

f(r)={\cal C}\left(\frac{4\beta q_{M}^{2}}{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}\right)^{3/2}r^{-6}+{\cal O}(r^{-4})\,.

(27)

This behavior is incompatible with the requirement
for the metric component
f(r)
in Eq. (
19
).
As seen from Eq. (
26
), this arises from the nonvanishing HVT coupling
\beta
.
In the absence of the HVT coupling (
\beta=0
), nonsingular magnetic BHs can exist.

For the dyon BH, the electric
field
A_{0}^{\prime}
contributes to Eq. (
11
).
As long as
A_{0}^{\prime}(r)
behaves as
A_{0}^{\prime}(r)\propto r^{p}

around

, the behavior on the right-hand side of Eq. (

differs from that in Eq. (

In this case, near
r=0
, Eq. (

can be approximated as

\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}-\frac{h^{\prime}}{h}\simeq\frac{2r^{3}hA_{0}^{\prime 2}}{fq_{M}^{2}}\simeq\frac{2{\cal C}_{0}^{2}r^{2p+3}}{f_{0}q_{M}^{2}}\,,

(28)

where we have substituted
A_{0}^{\prime}={\cal C}_{0}r^{p}
,
with
{\cal C}_{0}
being a constant.
The integrated solution around
r=0
is

\frac{f}{h}={\cal C}_{1}\exp\left[\frac{{\cal C}_{0}^{2}}{f_{0}q_{M}^{2}(p+2)r^{-2(p+2)}}\right]\,,

(29)

where
{\cal C}_{1}
is an integration constant.
For
p<-2
, the right-hand side of Eq. (
29
)
exponentially vanishes as

, so a solution with

A_{0}^{\prime}(r)\propto r^{p}
is incompatible with the regular
metric ansatz in Eq. (
19
).
We note that Eq. (
29
) applies to dyonic BHs
with
q_{M}\neq 0
but not to purely electric BHs with
q_{M}=0
,
which will be discussed separately in Sec.
III.2
.

III.2
Purely electric BHs

For purely electric BHs, we use Eq. (
14

estimate the behavior of the electric field near
r=0

By substituting Eq. (
19
) with
h_{3}\neq 0

into Eq. (
14

r=0

the
\pm

\displaystyle A_{0+}^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q_{E}\sqrt{f_{0}}}{2\beta h_{3}}r^{-3}-\frac{q_{E}\sqrt{f_{0}}h_{4}}{\beta h_{3}^{2}}r^{-2}+{\cal O}(r^{-1})\,,

(30)

\displaystyle A_{0-}^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\sqrt{f_{0}}h_{3}}{q_{E}}r^{3}+\frac{5M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\sqrt{f_{0}}h_{4}}{q_{E}}r^{4}+{\cal O}(r^{5})\,,

(31)

respectively.
While

The latter property is similar to that of
nonsingular BHs in NED with

From Eq. (
11

\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}-\frac{h^{\prime}}{h}=\frac{8\beta hA_{0}^{\prime 2}}{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}rf}=\frac{q_{E}^{2}(1\pm\sqrt{1-\xi})^{2}}{2M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\beta r(2h-2-rh^{\prime})^{2}}\,.

Using the expansions in Eqs. (
30
) and (
31
)
around
r=0
and integrating Eq. (
32
) with respect to
r
,
the leading-order contribution to

can be estimated as

\displaystyle\frac{f}{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal C}\exp\left(-\frac{q_{E}^{2}}{3M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\beta h_{3}^{2}r^{6}}\right)\,,\qquad{\rm for}\quad A_{0}^{\prime}=A_{0+}^{\prime}\,,

(33)

\displaystyle\frac{f}{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal C}\exp\left(\frac{16M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\beta h_{3}^{2}r^{6}}{3q_{E}^{2}}\right)\,,\qquad~{\rm for}\quad A_{0}^{\prime}=A_{0-}^{\prime}\,,

where
{\cal C}
is an integration constant.
In the limit

19
) at

{\cal C}=f_{0}
.
Accordingly, it is possible to realize regular electric BHs
in the branch
A_{0}^{\prime}=A_{0-}^{\prime}
.

In the following, we will focus on the branch
A_{0}^{\prime}=A_{0-}^{\prime}
.
Taking the minus branch of Eq. (
14
) and
substituting it into Eqs. (
5
) and (
12
),
the field strength
F
and the Lagrangian
{\cal L}
are expressed as

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q_{E}^{2}(1-\sqrt{1-\xi})^{2}}{32\beta^{2}(2h-2-rh^{\prime})^{2}}\,,

(35)

\displaystyle{\cal L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}(rh^{\prime}+h-1)}{r^{2}}-\frac{q_{E}^{2}[rh^{\prime}(\sqrt{1-\xi}-1)+\xi(h-1)]}{4\beta r^{2}(2h-2-rh^{\prime})^{2}}\,,

(36)

both of which depend on
h
but not on
f
.
Substituting the expanded solution
h(r)=1+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}h_{n}r^{n}

around
r=0

and

, we obtain

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2M_{\rm Pl}^{4}h_{3}^{2}}{q_{E}^{2}}r^{6}+\frac{10M_{\rm Pl}^{4}h_{3}h_{4}}{q_{E}^{2}}r^{7}+{\cal O}(r^{8})\,,

(37)

\displaystyle{\cal L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3M_{\rm Pl}^{2}h_{2}+6M_{\rm Pl}^{2}h_{3}r+{\cal O}(r^{2})\,,

(38)

which are both finite at
r=0
.

For a given nonsingular metric function

, the field strength

and the Lagrangian
{\cal L}
are determined as functions of

using Eqs. (

) and (
36
).
As long as

{\cal L}
as a function of

.
We note that many of the regular metrics proposed in the literature have

;
Ayon-Beato and Garcia 1998
;
Dymnikova 2004
;
Hayward 2006
.
In this case, the dominant term of

behaves as

which leads to

regular metric functions in Eq. (
19
) at
r=0

For
h_{3}=0
, the other branch
A_{0-}^{\prime}
has the following
behavior around
r=0

\displaystyle A_{0-}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{5M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\sqrt{f_{0}}h_{4}}{q_{E}}r^{4}+\frac{9M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\sqrt{f_{0}}h_{5}}{q_{E}}r^{5}+{\cal O}(r^{6})\,,

(39)

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{25M_{\rm Pl}^{4}h_{4}^{2}}{2q_{E}^{2}}r^{8}+\frac{45M_{\rm Pl}^{4}h_{4}h_{5}}{q_{E}^{2}}r^{9}+{\cal O}(r^{10})\,,

(40)

\displaystyle{\cal L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3M_{\rm Pl}^{2}h_{2}+10M_{\rm Pl}^{2}h_{4}r^{2}+{\cal O}(r^{3})\,,

(41)

in Eq. (
19

Moreover,
A_{0-}^{\prime}

We have thus shown that, for both

It remains to be seen whether such regular BHs are stable against linear perturbations.

IV
Singular electric BHs

In this section, we study the properties of background
BH solutions that have curvature singularities
at
r=0
.
In the following, we focus on the purely
electric case:

q_{E}\neq 0\,,\qquad q_{M}=0\,.

(42)

From Eqs. (
5
) and (
10
), we obtain

F=\frac{q_{E}^{2}}{2\left[r^{2}{\cal L}_{,F}-8\beta\left(h-1\right)\right]^{2}}\,.

(43)

Equations (
7
) and (
11
) can then be recast as

M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\left[r(1-h)\right]^{\prime}=2F\left[4\beta(1-h)+r^{2}{\cal L}_{,F}\right]-r^{2}{\cal L}\,,

(44)

and

\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}-\frac{h^{\prime}}{h}=\frac{16\beta F}{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r}\,,

(45)

respectively.

In general, for a given
{\cal L}(F)
, the right-hand sides of Eqs. (
44
) and (
45
) can be expressed in terms of
F
and
r
. By combining this with the implicit relation between
F
and
r
given by Eq. (
43
), the metric functions
h
and
f
can then be determined numerically or perturbatively. Analytic solutions may be possible, particularly in the case
\beta=0
, where an explicit expression for
F(r)
can be obtained from Eq. (
43
) for certain forms of
{\cal L}(F)
.

IV.1
Maxwell-HVT theory

We begin with singular BH solutions in Maxwell-HVT
theory with the Einstein-Hilbert term, where the
vector sector is described by the standard Maxwell Lagrangian

{\cal L}=F\,,

(46)

with
\beta\neq 0
. The solutions outside the outer BH horizon
have already been investigated in Ref.
Horndeski 1978
;
Mueller-Hoissen and Sippel 1988
;
Balakin et al. 2008
;
Verbin 2022
;
Chen et al. 2024
.
Here, we extend the analysis to include the spacelike regions.
The background equations for
h

and
f
obey Eqs. (
44
) and (
45
),
with
{\cal L}_{,F}=1
.
Using Eq. (
43
),
Eqs. (
44
) and (
45
)
can be rewritten, respectively, as

\displaystyle M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\left[r(1-h)\right]^{\prime}=\frac{q_{E}^{2}}{2[r^{2}-8\beta(h-1)]}\,,

(47)

\displaystyle\Delta\equiv\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}-\frac{h^{\prime}}{h}=\frac{8\beta q_{E}^{2}}{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r[r^{2}-8\beta(h-1)]^{2}}\,.

(48)

To derive the solution to
h(r)
near
r=0
, we expand it as

h(r)=\sum_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}c_{i}r^{i}\,,

(49)

where
c_{i}
are constants and
i
is an integer.
Substituting Eq. (
49
) into
Eq. (
47
) and expanding around
r=0
,
we can determine the coefficients order by order.
The resulting solution around the BH center
is given by

h(r)=-\frac{2m}{r}+1-\frac{q_{E}^{2}}{64\beta mM_{\rm Pl}^{2}}r+{\cal O}(r^{3})\,,

(50)

where we have set
c_{-1}=-2m
.
A special case with
m=0
is possible,
but in the following
discussion we focus on
m\neq 0
.
Substituting the solution (
50
)
into Eq. (
48
), we find that the
leading-order term near
r=0
on the right-hand side of
Eq. (
48
) is proportional to
r
.
Hence, the difference between
f^{\prime}/f
and
h^{\prime}/h

vanishes as
r\to 0
.
The solution for
f(r)
, expanded around
r=0
, is given by

f(r)=f_{0}\left[-\frac{2m}{r}+1-\frac{3q_{E}^{2}}{64\beta mM_{\rm Pl}^{2}}r+{\cal O}(r^{2})\right]\,,

(51)

where
f_{0}
is an integration constant.
From Eqs. (
10
) and (
43
), we obtain

A_{0}^{\prime}(r)\propto r\,,\qquad F(r)\propto r^{2}\,,

(52)

near
r=0
.
Thus, the electric field is regularized by the HVT coupling
in the vicinity of the BH center.

The solutions expanded at spatial infinity,
which satisfy the boundary condition

f(r\to\infty)=1
, are
given by
Horndeski 1978
;
Chen et al. 2024

\displaystyle h(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{q_{E}^{2}}{2M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r^{2}}-\frac{2\beta Mq_{E}^{2}}{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r^{5}}+\frac{2\beta q_{E}^{4}}{5M_{\rm Pl}^{4}r^{6}}+{\cal O}(r^{-8})\,,

(53)

\displaystyle f(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{q_{E}^{2}}{2M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r^{2}}-\frac{2\beta q_{E}^{2}}{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r^{4}}+\frac{2\beta Mq_{E}^{2}}{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r^{5}}-\frac{3\beta q_{E}^{4}}{5M_{\rm Pl}^{4}r^{6}}+{\cal O}(r^{-7})\,,

(54)

where
M
is an integration constant.
The integration constant
f_{0}

so that the metric satisfies the asymptotic condition

r\to\infty

At spatial infinity, the electric field behaves as

.

Near
r=0

This indicates that the variations of

Moving from the distant region toward the BH center, the solutions for

r_{h}
.
If
|\beta|
is chosen to be large, the quantity
r^{2}-8\beta(h-1)
can cross 0 for

\beta<0
, which leads to divergence in the right-hand sides of Eqs. (
47
) and (
48
).
The plot in Fig.
2
corresponds to the case where no such crossing occurs.
Therefore, the magnitude of
\beta
is constrained from above to ensure consistent background solutions
(see also Fig. 2 in Ref.
Chen et al. 2024
).

The parameter ranges for

\beta
and
q_{E}
that are compatible with linear stability
in the region
r>r_{h}

have been studied in Ref.
Chen et al. 2024
.
In Sec.
VII
, we address stability for
r<r_{h}

to examine further constraints on the HVT coupling
\beta
.

IV.2
Power-law NED theory
with
\beta=0

Let us consider the power-law NED theory in
which the Lagrangian

{\cal L}
is given by

{\cal L}(F)=F+a_{p}F^{p}\,,

(58)

where
a_{p}
and
p
are constants.
We assume that
p
is an integer in the range
p\geq 2
.
In this section, we focus on the case without the HVT coupling (
\beta=0
).
The effect of the nonvanishing HVT coupling on the background BH solution will be discussed in Sec.
IV.4
.

Since we are considering the case
\beta=0
, Eq. (
45
)
admits the integrated solution

f=Ch
, where
C
is a constant.
Imposing the boundary conditions

f(r\to\infty)=h(r\to\infty)=1
fixes
C=1
,
and hence
f(r)=h(r)
.
We now consider the case
p=2
.
From Eq. (
10
), we obtain

A_{0}^{\prime}=\frac{q_{E}}{r^{2}(a_{2}A_{0}^{\prime 2}+1)}\,.

(59)

This is an algebraic equation that allows us
to express
A_{0}^{\prime}
as a function of
r
.
The metric component
h
obeys

h^{\prime}=\frac{1-h}{r}-\frac{A_{0}^{\prime 2}}{2M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}r-\frac{3a_{2}A_{0}^{\prime 4}}{4M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}r\,.

(60)

In the following, we assume
q_{E}>0

without loss of generality.
We will also consider the case
a_{2}>0
,
which is required for the linear stability
of BHs (see Sec.
VII.2
).
The solutions expanded around
r=0

are given by

\displaystyle A_{0}^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{q_{E}}{a_{2}}\right)^{1/3}\frac{1}{r^{2/3}}-\frac{r^{2/3}}{3(q_{E}a_{2}^{2})^{1/3}}+{\cal O}(r^{10/3})\,,

(61)

\displaystyle h(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(r)=-\frac{2m}{r}-\frac{9}{4M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}\left(\frac{q_{E}^{4}}{a_{2}}\right)^{1/3}\frac{1}{r^{2/3}}+1+{\cal O}(r^{2/3})\,,

(62)

where
m
is an integration constant.
While Einstein-Maxwell theory yields

A_{0}^{\prime}(r)\propto r^{-2}
,
the presence of the NED term
a_{2}F^{2}

alters the radial dependence to
A_{0}^{\prime}(r)\propto r^{-2/3}

near
r=0
. This also modifies the behavior of the metric functions, such that
h(r)=f(r)\propto r^{-1}
near
r=0
,
in contrast to
h(r)=f(r)\propto r^{-2}
for the RN BH.

At spatial infinity, the solutions can be expanded as

\displaystyle A_{0}^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q_{E}}{r^{2}}-\frac{a_{2}q_{E}^{3}}{r^{6}}+{\cal O}(r^{-10})\,,

(63)

\displaystyle h(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(r)=1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{q_{E}^{2}}{2M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r^{2}}-\frac{a_{2}q_{E}^{4}}{20M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r^{6}}+{\cal O}(r^{-10})\,.

(64)

In this regime, the coupling
a_{2}
acts
as corrections to the electric field and metric
functions of the RN solution.

(Left panel) Metric component

h~(=f)
as a function of
r/r_{h}

for the power-law NED with

and
q_{E}=2.15\times 10^{-2}M_{\rm Pl}r_{h}

.

and
q_{E}=2.15\times 10^{-2}M_{\rm Pl}r_{h}
,
with the same boundary condition as in the left panel.

To quantify the effect of the nonlinear
Lagrangian
a_{2}F^{2}
, we define

R_{a_{2}}=a_{2}A_{0}^{\prime 2}\,.

(65)

In the regime where
|R_{a_{2}}|>1
, the contribution
from
a_{2}F^{2}
dominates over the Maxwell term
F
.

In Fig.
3
, we present a numerical example of
h~(=f)
(left) and of
A_{0}^{\prime}
and
R_{a_{2}}
(right) as functions of
r/r_{h}
,
with
r_{h}
denoting the radius of the single horizon.
This corresponds to the case with
a_{2}>0
and
m>0
in the expansion of Eq. (
62
).
As seen from the solid line in the left panel of Fig.
3
, the metric function undergoes a transition from the solution
h(r)\simeq 1-2M/r
at large distances to
h(r)\simeq-2m/r
at small distances. Unlike the RN BH, where
h(r)
rises again toward
h(r\to 0)=+\infty
, the metric function for
a_{2}\neq 0
decreases toward
h(r\to 0)=-\infty
.
In the right panel of Fig.
3
, we see that
R_{a_{2}}
exceeds 1 for
r<r_{a_{2}}=2.7\times 10^{-2}r_{h}
. In this regime, the radial dependence of the electric field changes from
A_{0}^{\prime}(r)\propto r^{-2}
to
A_{0}^{\prime}(r)\propto r^{-2/3}
. This nontrivial behavior of
A_{0}^{\prime}(r)
for
r<r_{a_{2}}
, in turn, modifies the metric functions compared to those of the RN BH.

The above result corresponds to the case
p=2
, but
one can also consider a general integer power
p
.
At small distances, the leading behavior of the electric field
scales as
A_{0}^{\prime}(r)\propto r^{-2/(2p-1)}
. The metric
functions near
r=0
have the dependence
h(r)=f(r)=-2m/r+c_{p}r^{-2/(2p-1)}+\cdots
,
where
c_{p}
is a constant.
Therefore, for
p\geq 2
,
the leading-order contributions
to
h(r)
and
f(r)
are
-2m/r
.
Defining

R_{a_{p}}=2^{1-p}pa_{p}A_{0}^{\prime 2(p-1)}
,
the regime in which the small-distance solutions are realized
is characterized by the condition
|R_{a_{p}}|\gg 1
.
In contrast, in the regime
|R_{a_{p}}|\ll 1
, the leading-order contributions of the metric functions reduce to the RN ones, with
A_{0}^{\prime}(r)=q_{E}/r^{2}
, up to small corrections induced by the coupling
a_{p}
.
The small- and large-distance solutions are smoothly connected
around
|R_{a_{p}}|=1
, as illustrated in Fig.
3
.
For increasing
p
, the variation of
A_{0}^{\prime}(r)
in the
small-distance regime becomes less significant,
approaching
A_{0}^{\prime}(r)\propto\text{constant}

in the limit
p\to\infty
.

IV.3
Born-Infeld theory
with
\beta=0

We proceed to Born-Infeld theory without the HVT coupling.
The NED sector is given by the Lagrangian

{\cal L}(F)=\frac{2}{b}\left(1-\sqrt{1-bF}\right)\,,

(66)

where
b
denotes a constant parameter.
We will focus on the case
b>0
, in which

F~(>0)
is bounded from above.

The electric field is simply given by

A_{0}^{\prime}(r)=\frac{q_{E}}{\sqrt{r^{4}+r_{b}^{4}}}\,,

(67)

where
r_{b}^{4}\equiv bq_{E}^{2}/2
, and we have assumed

q_{E}>0
and
A_{0}^{\prime}>0
without loss of generality.
In the limit

r\to 0
, the electric field
approaches a constant value
q_{E}/r_{b}^{2}
.
Since we are now
considering the case
\beta=0
, we have

f^{\prime}/f=h^{\prime}/h
and hence
f(r)=h(r)
upon imposing the boundary conditions

f(\infty)=h(\infty)=1
.
Introducing the mass function
\mu(r)

in the form

h(r)=1-\frac{2\mu(r)}{r}\,,

(68)

it satisfies the differential equation

\mu^{\prime}(r)=\frac{1}{bM_{\rm Pl}^{2}}\left(\sqrt{r^{4}+r_{b}^{4}}-r^{2}\right)\,.

(69)

For small distances
r\ll r_{b}
,
integrating Eq. (
69
) yields

\mu(r)=m+\frac{q_{E}}{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\sqrt{2b}}r-\frac{r^{3}}{3M_{\rm Pl}^{2}b}+{\cal O}(r^{5})\,,

(70)

where
m
is an integration constant.
This translates to the metric functions

h(r)=f(r)=-\frac{2m}{r}+1-\frac{q_{E}}{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{b}}+\frac{2r^{2}}{3bM_{\rm Pl}^{2}}+{\cal O}(r^{4})\,.

(71)

Unlike the RN BH, the leading-order terms
of
h(r)
and
f(r)
are modified to

-2m/r
, as a result of the regularization
of
A_{0}^{\prime}(r)
near
r=0
.

At large-distances
r\gg r_{b}
, Eq. (
69
)
is integrated to give

\mu(r)=M-\frac{q_{E}^{2}}{4M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r}+\frac{bq_{E}^{4}}{160M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r^{5}}+{\cal O}(r^{-9})\,,

(72)

where
M
is another integration constant.
This leads to

h(r)=f(r)=1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{q_{E}^{2}}{2M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r^{2}}-\frac{bq_{E}^{4}}{80M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r^{6}}+{\cal O}(r^{-10})\,,

(73)

which shows that the coupling
b
works as
a correction to the RN metric. The solutions
(
71
) and (
73
) connect smoothly around
r=r_{b}
.
The mass function
\mu(r)
increases from its value
m
at
r=0
to the ADM mass
M
at spatial infinity.

IV.4
Power-law or
Born-Infeld NED theories with

\beta\neq 0

We now turn to NED theories with the HVT coupling, examining in turn the power-law and
Born-Infeld cases.

We first consider the power-law NED with
{\cal L}(F)=F+a_{p}F^{p}
, without imposing any restriction on the range of
p

(in contrast to Sec.
IV.2
,
where
p
was restricted to
p\geq 2
).
For
p<1
, the theory does not admit a proper Maxwell limit,
{\cal L}(F)\to F
as
F\to 0
, which can lead to significant
deviations from the RN solution at large distances.
Here, we are primarily interested in the modifications
induced by the nonlinear term
a_{p}F^{p}
near
r=0

(as in the case of nonsingular BHs in NED).
Therefore, we do not exclude the possibility
of
p<1
.

From Eqs. (
5
) and (
10
),
the equation of motion for
A_{0}^{\prime}

is given by

2^{1-p}a_{p}p\left(\frac{f}{h}\right)^{1-p}r^{2}A_{0}^{\prime 2p-1}-q_{E}\sqrt{\frac{f}{h}}+A_{0}^{\prime}\left[r^{2}+8\beta(1-h)\right]=0\,.

(74)

For
a_{p}=0
, we have already shown that the leading-order solutions for
h(r)
and
f(r)
near
r=0
scale as
r^{-1}
.
The same property holds for
\beta=0
with
p\geq 2
.
Since the metric components are expected to exhibit a similar behavior for
a_{p}\neq 0
and
\beta\neq 0
, we look for
leading-order solutions of the form

h(r)=\frac{h_{1}}{r}\,,\qquad f(r)=\frac{f_{1}}{r}\,,

(75)

in the vicinity of
r=0
. Substituting
Eq. (
75
) into Eq. (
74
),
we find that the solution
to
A_{0}^{\prime}(r)
depends on the power
p
.
When
p>-1/2
, the last two terms
in Eq. (
74
) dominate, and the short-distance
solution takes the form

A_{0}^{\prime}(r)=-\frac{q_{E}}{8\beta h_{1}}\sqrt{\frac{f_{1}}{h_{1}}}\,r\,,

(76)

which is analogous to the behavior in Eq. (
52
).
This corresponds to the regime where the HVT coupling
dominates over the power-law NED coupling
a_{p}F^{p}
.

If
p=-1/2
, all three terms in Eq. (
74
) contribute.
In this case, the electric field near the origin
r=0

has the following behavior:

A_{0}^{\prime}(r)=C_{-1/2}r\,,

(77)

where the coefficient
C_{-1/2}
can be obtained by
solving the leading-order contribution of Eq. (
74
).

When
p<-1/2
, the first and third terms in Eq. (
74
) dominate, and hence the short-distance solution is given by

A_{0}^{\prime}(r)=\left(\frac{2^{1-p}a_{p}p}{8\beta h_{1}}\right)^{1/[2(1-p)]}\sqrt{\frac{f_{1}}{h_{1}}}\,r^{3/[2(1-p)]}\,.

(78)

The exponent of the
r
-dependent term in Eq. (
78
)
lies in the range
0<3/[2(1-p)]<1
.
Thus, the electric field remains regular in the limit
r\to 0
.
For
p\geq-1/2
, we have
A_{0}^{\prime}(r)\propto r
, so that the electric field is more strongly regularized
by the HVT coupling than in the case
p<-1/2
.
Substituting Eq. (
78
) into the gravitational field
Eqs. (
44
) and (
45
), the leading-order metric functions of the form (
75
) are found to be consistent with them. The same property also
holds for the electric-field solutions given
in Eqs. (
76
) and (
77
).

The solutions derived above are valid in
the small-distance regime where the HVT coupling
and/or the NED power-law coupling contribute
to the electric-field profile.
As we will see in Sec.
VII.4
, these solutions suffer from Laplacian instabilities of even-parity perturbations.
Therefore, we will not study how they are connected to the solutions in the large-distance regime.

In Born-Infeld theory, characterized by
the Lagrangian
{\cal L}(F)=2/b\left(1-\sqrt{1-bF}\right)
, we discuss the
short-distance solutions in the presence
of the HVT coupling.
In this case, the electric field obeys

q_{E}\sqrt{\frac{f}{h}}+8\beta(h-1)A_{0}^{\prime}=\frac{\sqrt{2}r^{2}A_{0}^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2-bhA_{0}^{\prime 2}/f}}\,.

(79)

Under the assumption that the metric functions are given by
the forms (
75
), the leading-order term of
A_{0}^{\prime}
is determined by setting the right-hand side of
Eq. (
79
) to 0, yielding

A_{0}^{\prime}(r)=-\frac{q_{E}}{8\beta h_{1}}\sqrt{\frac{f_{1}}{h_{1}}}\,r\,,

(80)

which is of the same form as Eq. (
76
) and
Eq. (
52
). The Born-Infeld coupling
b
does not
affect the leading-order solution for
A_{0}^{\prime}(r)
.
One can also verify that, with the electric field given by Eq. (
80
), the leading-order metric components
h(r)=h_{1}/r
and
f(r)=f_{1}/r
are consistent with Eqs. (
44
) and (
45
).
For the same reason as in the case of the power-law NED, we will not discuss the behavior of the
large-distance solutions.

We note that the solutions found in Eqs. (
76
) and (
80
), corresponding to the power-law NED with
p>-1/2

and the Born-Infeld NED, respectively, exhibit the same behavior
as those in the Einstein-Maxwell-HVT theory,
namely,
A_{0}^{\prime}(r)\propto r
near
r=0
.
In fact, for any NED theory with a proper Maxwell limit,
i.e.,
\mathcal{L}(F)\rightarrow F
as
F\rightarrow 0
,
one can see from Eq. (
43
) that there always exists
a branch of solutions in which
h(r)\propto 1/r
,

f(r)\propto 1/r
, and
A_{0}^{\prime}(r)\propto r
around
r=0
.
This highlights the significant role of the HVT coupling
\beta

in dominating over the NED contributions near the BH center.
It also implies that if the solutions in Maxwell-HVT
theory exhibit instabilities near
r=0
, as we will
demonstrate in Sec.
VII.1
,
then solutions in general NED theories with
\beta\neq 0
, which behave similarly to those in Maxwell-HVT theory near
r=0
, would likely suffer from the same
instability, provided the NED theory has a proper Maxwell limit (see Sec.
VII.4
).

However, nontrivial solutions can exist if the assumption
of a proper Maxwell limit for the NED theory is relaxed.
An example is provided by the solutions in Eq. (
78
),
which correspond to the power-law NED with
p<-1/2
.
Another possibility for obtaining nontrivial solutions
is to consider branches in which
F
does not approach
0 at the origin, as we will demonstrate
in the next subsection.

IV.5
Reconstructed NED theories with
\beta\neq 0

The previous methods focused on solutions derived from
physically motivated NED Lagrangians,
{\cal L}(F)
.
Alternatively, one can adopt a different approach by
reconstructing the form of
{\cal L}(F)
from a given metric function
h(r)
.
The idea is that certain forms of
{\cal L}(F)
may have implications for the stability of BH solutions.
Let us consider the case in which one of the metric
functions,
h(r)
, takes the form

h(r)=1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{b_{1}}{r^{2}}\,,

(81)

for arbitrary
r
, where
M
and
b_{1}
are constants.
3
3

3

We reconstruct the theory corresponding to a given function
h(r)
. Although this theory may in principle admit infinitely many other solutions, they are generally inaccessible in analytic form.
Furthermore, quantities such as
M
, which physically represent the ADM mass, cannot be derived from first principles within the theory,
since their origin depends on the full set of solutions, which remains unknown. However, this method is interesting because it can generate
solutions without Laplacian instabilities, as we will see
later in Sec.
VII.5
.

This form has been chosen because the term
b_{1}/r^{2}

dominates over
-2M/r
as
r\to 0
.
If there is a NED Lagrangian compatible with such a behavior, it would have a different phenomenology from the other theories
discussed above, i.e.,
h(r)\propto 1/r
for
r\to 0
.

We define a function
\tilde{f}
as
\tilde{f}\equiv f/h
,
with
\lim_{r\to\infty}\tilde{f}=1
.
We can solve Eqs. (
45
) and (
44
) for
F
and
\mathcal{L}_{,F}
to obtain

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}{\tilde{f}^{\prime}}r}{16\beta\tilde{f}}\,,

(82)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{,F}

\displaystyle=-\frac{4\beta\bigl[rM_{\rm Pl}^{2}\left(2Mr-b_{1}\right){\tilde{f}^{\prime}}-2\left(r^{4}{\mathcal{L}}+b_{1}M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\right)\tilde{f}\bigr]}{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}{\tilde{f}^{\prime}}r^{5}}\,,

(83)

where we have assumed

\tilde{f}^{\prime}\neq 0
.
Substituting these expressions into Eq. (
43
)
yields an equation that determines
\mathcal{L}
, as follows:

\mathcal{L}=\frac{\bigl(-4M_{\rm Pl}\tilde{f}^{\prime}\beta M\,r^{2}+2M_{\rm Pl}\tilde{f}^{\prime}b_{1}\beta r\pm\sqrt{2}\,q_{E}r^{2}\sqrt{\tilde{f}^{\prime}\tilde{f}\beta r}-4M_{\rm Pl}\tilde{f}b_{1}\beta\bigr)M_{\rm Pl}}{4\beta\tilde{f}r^{4}}\,,

(84)

where we have
further assumed that

\tilde{f}^{\prime}\tilde{f}\beta>0
.
The plus/minus sign generally corresponds
to two branches of solutions/theories.
At this point, employing the previous
three relations together with the integrability condition
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(r)=\mathcal{L}_{,F}F^{\prime}(r)
,
we obtain a first-order differential equation
for
\tilde{f}
, which can be written as

2\left[r\left(3Mr-2b_{1}\right)\tilde{f}^{\prime}+4b_{1}\tilde{f}\right]M_{\rm Pl}\sqrt{\tilde{f}^{\prime}\tilde{f}\beta r}\mp\sqrt{2}\tilde{f}^{\prime}\tilde{f}q_{E}r^{3}=0\,.

(85)

Then, the profile for
\tilde{f}

is determined as follows:

\tilde{f}(r)=c_{1}\exp\!\left[\int^{r}\frac{q_{E}^{2}r^{4}+16M_{\rm Pl}^{2}b_{1}\beta(2b_{1}-3Mr)\mp q_{E}r^{2}\sqrt{q_{E}^{2}r^{4}+32M_{\rm Pl}^{2}b_{1}\beta(2b_{1}-3Mr)}}{4M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\left(3Mr-2b_{1}\right)^{2}\beta r}\,{\rm d}r\right],

(86)

where the constant
c_{1}
needs to be determined by the condition
\lim_{r\to\infty}\tilde{f}(r)=1
.
The minus/plus signs in Eq. (
86
)
give rise to two further branches, with the same minus/plus choices
also serving as solutions for the two branches of
\mathcal{L}
.
Thus, in total, four branches may arise: two associated
with the choice of
\tilde{f}
and two with the choice of
\mathcal{L}
.
At lowest order, around the origin, we find that

\tilde{f}^{\prime}\tilde{f}\beta>0
leads to
2c_{1}^{2}\beta r^{3}>0
,
and hence
\beta>0
(for all branches). Since

\tilde{f}=c_{1}r^{2}+\mathcal{O}(r^{3})
near
r=0
,
the metric function
f
has the following dependence:

f(r)=b_{1}c_{1}+Mc_{1}r+\frac{c_{1}[\mp\sqrt{\beta}\,|b_{1}|q_{E}+M_{\rm Pl}\beta(3M^{2}+4b_{1})]}{4M_{\rm Pl}b_{1}\beta}r^{2}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(r^{3}\right).

(87)

In what follows, we denote the cases with the minus and plus signs in Eq. (
86
) as branch A and branch B, respectively.
For branch A, we also refer to the cases with the plus and minus signs in Eq. (
84
) as branch A1 and branch A2, respectively.
For branch A, the expansions of
F
and

{\cal L}
near
r=0
are given by

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{8\beta}+\frac{3M_{\rm Pl}^{2}M}{16b_{1}\beta}r+\frac{9M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\beta\,M^{2}-q_{E}M_{\rm Pl}|b_{1}|\sqrt{\beta}}{32b_{1}^{2}\beta^{2}}r^{2}+\mathcal{O}(r^{3})\,,

(88)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=-\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}M}{2}r^{-3}-\frac{M_{\rm Pl}[3\beta M^{2}M_{\rm Pl}-(2b_{1}\epsilon-|b_{1}|)q_{E}\sqrt{\beta}]}{4b_{1}\beta}+{\cal O}(r^{-1})\,,

(89)

where
\epsilon=+1

(\epsilon=-1)
represents the branch A1 (A2).
This implies that
F
approaches the finite value
M_{\rm Pl}^{2}/(8\beta)

as
r\to 0
. In the vicinity of the BH center,
the NED Lagrangian behaves at leading order as

{\cal L}(F)\propto[F-M_{\rm Pl}^{2}/(8\beta)]^{-3}
.
Close to
r=0
, the electric field behaves
as
A_{0}^{\prime}(r)\propto r
, and is thus regular.
Using the properties

\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(r)=\mathcal{L}_{,F}F^{\prime}(r)
and

\mathcal{L}_{,F}^{\prime}(r)=\mathcal{L}_{,FF}F^{\prime}(r)
,
we can further estimate
\mathcal{L}_{,F}
and

\mathcal{L}_{,FF}
near
r=0
for branch A as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{,F}

\displaystyle=\frac{8\beta b_{1}}{r^{4}}-\frac{16\beta M}{r^{3}}+\epsilon\frac{2\sqrt{\beta}\,q_{E}}{M_{\rm Pl}r^{2}}-\epsilon\frac{3\sqrt{\beta}\,q_{E}M}{2M_{\rm Pl}b_{1}r}+\mathcal{O}(r^{0})\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{,FF}

and
\delta A
, we assume solutions of the
perturbation equations in the form

\vec{\mathcal{X}}_{\rm A}^{t}=\vec{\mathcal{X}}_{0}^{t}\,e^{-i(\omega r-kt)}
.
The squared radial propagation speeds,
measured with respect to the proper time

\tau=\int{\rm d}r/\sqrt{-h}
and the
rescaled radial coordinate

\tilde{r}=\int\sqrt{-f}\,{\rm d}t
, are given by

c_{r1}^{2}=-fh(K_{\rm A})_{11}/(G_{\rm A})_{11}=1
and

c_{r2}^{2}=-fh(K_{\rm A})_{22}/(G_{\rm A})_{22}=1
,
so that both are luminal.
The squared angular propagation speeds, measured
using the proper time, are expressed as

c_{\Omega 1}^{2}=hr^{2}(M_{\rm A})_{11}/[L(G_{\rm A})_{11}]

and

c_{\Omega 2}^{2}=hr^{2}(M_{\rm A})_{22}/[L(G_{\rm A})_{22}]
.
In the limit
l\to\infty
, these reduce to the same values
as those given in Eqs. (
114
) and (
115
).
Hence, the Laplacian instability is absent
if
{\cal G}_{1}>0
and
{\cal G}_{3}>0
.

For even-parity perturbations
\chi_{2}
and
V
, the ghost-free conditions correspond to

{\rm det}\,{\bm{G}}_{\rm B}=(G_{\rm B})_{11}(G_{\rm B})_{22}-(G_{\rm B})_{12}^{2}>0
and
(G_{\rm B})_{22}>0
.
For
f<0
and
h<0
, these conditions are
satisfied if
{\cal G}_{1}>0
and
{\cal G}_{2}>0
.
The radial propagation speeds
c_{r}

can be derived by solving

{\rm det}(c_{r}^{2}{\bm{G}}_{\rm B}+fh{\bm{K}}_{\rm B})=0
. Taking the limit

l\gg 1
, we obtain the same two luminal
values of
c_{r}^{2}
as those given in Eq. (
124
).
The angular propagation speeds

c_{\Omega}
can be found by solving

{\rm det}(Lc_{\Omega}^{2}{\bm{G}}_{\rm B}-hr^{2}{\bm{M}}_{\rm B})=0
with the limit

l\gg 1
. This equation leads to the
same values of
c_{\Omega 3}^{2}
and

c_{\Omega 4}^{2}
as those given in
Eqs. (
157
) and (
127
).

In summary, the stability of electric BHs in the space-like region is ensured if

{\cal G}_{1}>0\,,\qquad{\cal G}_{2}>0\,,\qquad{\cal G}_{3}>0\,,\qquad c_{\Omega 3}^{2}>0\,,\qquad c_{\Omega 4}^{2}>0\,.

(129)

These conditions are the same as those derived
in the time-like region.

VI
Instability of nonsingular electric BHs

In this section, we study the linear stability of nonsingular electric BHs with regular centers.
The consistent solution for
A_{0}^{\prime}(r)

that has a continuous limit to NED
as
\beta\to 0
corresponds to the minus branch of Eq. (
14

A_{0-}^{\prime}(r)=-\frac{q_{E}}{4\beta(2h-2-rh^{\prime})}\sqrt{\frac{f}{h}}\left(1-\sqrt{1-\xi}\right)\,.

(130)

We also recall that the Lagrangian

{\cal L}(F)
is expressed in the form (
12
),
which contains
A_{0}^{\prime}
,
f
,
h
,
and their
r
derivatives.
Near
r=0
, the metric components

f
and
h
of regular BHs can be expanded as Eq. (
19
).

In NED with
\beta=0
, the Laplacian instability arises from the negativity of the squared angular
propagation speed
c_{\Omega 4}^{2}
,
given by Eq. (
127
).
For
\beta\neq 0
, we examine whether
a similar property holds.
In Eq. (
127

F

{\cal L}

{\cal L}_{,F}={\cal L}^{\prime}(r)/F^{\prime}(r)

{\cal L}_{,FF}=[{\cal L}^{\prime\prime}(r)F^{\prime}(r)-{\cal L}^{\prime}(r)F^{\prime\prime}(r)]/F^{\prime}(r)^{3}

where
F=hA_{0}^{\prime 2}/(2f)

Using Eqs. (
130
) and (
12

(
19
) of
metric functions around
r=0

we find that
c_{\Omega 4}^{2}

is expanded as

\displaystyle c_{\Omega 4}^{2}=\begin{cases}-\dfrac{3}{2}-\dfrac{5h_{4}}{4h_{3}}r+{\cal O}(r^{2})&({\rm for}~h_{3}\neq 0)\,,\\
-2-\dfrac{9h_{5}}{10h_{4}}r+{\cal O}(r^{2})&({\rm for}~h_{3}=0,~h_{4}\neq 0)\,,\\
-\dfrac{5}{2}-\dfrac{7h_{6}}{9h_{5}}r+{\cal O}(r^{2})&({\rm for}~h_{3}=0,~h_{4}=0,~h_{5}\neq 0)\,,\end{cases}

(131)

whose leading-order terms are always negative.
The leading-order contribution to

As discussed in Ref.

where

Since

is coupled to the gravitational
perturbation
\chi_{2}

subject to exponential growth.
This implies that the regular metric of the form
(

steady state, so that the nonsingular electric
BH is ruled out by the angular instability.
Since regular BHs with magnetic charges cannot exist at the background level,
we have excluded the presence of all stable nonsingular BHs in theories described by the action (

VII
Stability of singular electric BHs

We now analyze the linear stability of the singular electric BH solutions, considering in turn the five classes of theories presented in Sec.

VII.1
Maxwell-HVT theory

In Maxwell-HVT theory, the metric

VII.2
Power-law NED theory
with
\beta=0

We now turn to the stability analysis of BHs in power-law NED theories,
described by the Lagrangian (
58
) with

p\geq 2
. Using the background
Eq. (
10
) with
f=h
, we find
that the quantities associated with
the linear stability of BHs reduce to

{\cal G}_{1}=1\,,\qquad{\cal G}_{2}={\cal G}_{3}=1+2^{1-p}p\,a_{p}A_{0}^{\prime 2(p-1)}\,,

(138)

and

c_{\Omega 1}^{2}=c_{\Omega 2}^{2}=c_{\Omega 3}^{2}=1\,,\qquad c_{\Omega 4}^{2}=\frac{1+2^{1-p}p\,a_{p}A_{0}^{\prime 2(p-1)}}{1+2^{1-p}(2p-1)p\,a_{p}A_{0}^{\prime 2(p-1)}}\,.

(139)

In the small-distance regime where the condition

|a_{p}F^{p}|\gg F
is satisfied, the electric
field behaves as
A_{0}^{\prime}(r)\propto r^{-2/(2p-1)}
.
In this region, the contribution
of the term
R_{a_{p}}=2^{1-p}p\,a_{p}A_{0}^{\prime 2(p-1)}
dominates over 1 for
p\geq 2
,
so that
{\cal G}_{2}={\cal G}_{3}\simeq 2^{1-p}p\,a_{p}A_{0}^{\prime 2(p-1)}
.
Provided that

a_{p}>0\,,

(140)

the conditions
{\cal G}_{2}={\cal G}_{3}>0
are always satisfied.
In the regime
R_{a_{p}}\gg 1
,
the leading-order term of

c_{\Omega 4}^{2}
is given by

c_{\Omega 4}^{2}\simeq\frac{1}{2p-1}\,,

(141)

which is positive for
p\geq 2
.
In contrast, in the large-distance regime characterized by
R_{a_{p}}\ll 1
,
the asymptotic value of
c_{\Omega 4}^{2}
approaches 1. Numerically, we computed
c_{\Omega 4}^{2}
for several values of

p
with
p\geq 2
, and confirmed that it increases smoothly from

1/(2p-1)
in the small-distance region
to 1 in the large-distance region.
Therefore, as long as
a_{p}>0
and
p\geq 2
,
neither ghost nor Laplacian instabilities appear in timelike or spacelike regions.

VII.3
Born-Infeld theory

In Born-Infeld theories with the HVT coupling,
the solution for
A_{0}^{\prime}(r)
is given by Eq. (
67
).
Using this background solution, the quantities
relevant for the linear stability of BHs
reduce to

{\cal G}_{1}=1\,,\qquad{\cal G}_{2}={\cal G}_{3}=\frac{\sqrt{r^{4}+r_{b}^{4}}}{r^{2}}\,,

(142)

and

c_{\Omega 1}^{2}=c_{\Omega 2}^{2}=c_{\Omega 3}^{2}=1\,,\qquad c_{\Omega 4}^{2}=\frac{r^{4}}{r^{4}+r_{b}^{4}}\,.

(143)

For
r>0
, all of these quantities are positive, and therefore ghost and Laplacian instabilities are absent.
However,
c_{\Omega 4}^{2}\to 0
as
r\to 0
, indicating the emergence of a strong coupling problem. To avoid this issue near the
singular point
r=0
, we need to assume
that the linear perturbation theory starts
to lose validity below an EFT scale
r_{\rm EFT}
.
In other words, we require the condition

r_{b}<r_{\rm EFT}
, i.e.,

b<\frac{2r_{\rm EFT}^{4}}{q_{E}^{2}}\,.

(144)

Since
r_{\rm EFT}
can be much smaller than the horizon radius
r_{h}
, the coupling
b
is constrained by
b\ll 2r_{h}^{4}/q_{E}^{2}
.
Under such a bound, the metric functions outside the horizon, as well as the electric field, are almost indistinguishable from those of the RN BH.

VII.4
Power-law or
Born-Infeld NED theories with

\beta\neq 0

In power-law NED theories with the HVT coupling, the leading-order metric functions

and

(145)

Near
r=0
, the electric-field strength
behaves as
F=hA_{0}^{\prime 2}/(2f)\simeq h_{1}A_{0}^{\prime 2}/(2f_{1})\propto A_{0}^{\prime 2}

When

(146)

(147)

as well in this case.
For

\beta

(148)

-1/2<c_{\Omega 4}^{2}<0
.
Since
c_{\Omega 4}^{2}
is negative

, the BH solution exhibits
a Laplacian instability near
r=0

To avoid this problem, the couplings
\beta
and

must be chosen sufficiently small so that the radius

that ensures the validity of linear perturbation theory. While we have considered only the angular propagation of

, the fact that

always becomes negative near

.
We also note that this instability arises as a consequence of the regularization of

in the vicinity of
r=0
.

In Born-Infeld theory with the HVT coupling, the metric functions near
r=0
are given by Eq. (
75

A_{0}^{\prime}(r)
of the form (
80

c_{\Omega 4}^{2}=-\frac{r(1-bF)(r-4\beta\sqrt{1-bF}\,h^{\prime})}{8\beta(1-bF)^{3/2}(h-1)-r^{2}}\,.

(149)

Near
r=0

(150)

showing the presence of Laplacian instability.
This instability manifests even in the region close to the horizon

, unless
\beta
is much smaller than
r_{h}^{2}
.

The above results show that the presence of the HVT coupling induces Laplacian instabilities in both the power-law NED and Born-Infeld theories.
To avoid this problem, one must either set
\beta=0
or choose
\beta
sufficiently small such that
r_{g}
lies within the EFT scale
r_{\rm EFT}
.
In addition, as noted at the end of Sec.
IV.4
, for any NED with
a proper Maxwell limit, i.e.,

\mathcal{L}(F)\to F
as
F\to 0

there is always a branch of solutions for
which

,
and
A_{0}^{\prime}(r)\propto r
near the origin. For this branch of solutions, the HVT effect dominates over NED contributions,
and the behavior of solutions, including
at the perturbation level, should be similar to that in
Maxwell-HVT theory.
Our results in this section, particularly the squared propagation speed
c_{\Omega 4}^{2}

for power-law NED theories with

and for
Born-Infeld theory,
support this expectation.
Nontrivial solutions can be obtained either by considering the NED without a proper Maxwell limit,
such as power-law NED theories
with
p<-1/2
,
or by taking branches of solutions where
F
does not approach 0 at the origin
(see Sec.
IV.5
). In the next subsection, we present the stability analysis of the solutions
derived in Sec.

VIII
Conclusions

In this paper, we have studied the existence and stability of BH solutions on the SSS background in theories described by the action (
1
).
In NED without the HVT coupling, it is known that nonsingular BHs with electric or magnetic charge are prone to Laplacian instabilities
near the center
De Felice and Tsujikawa 2025a
;
De Felice and Tsujikawa 2025b
. The primary motivation of this work is to clarify whether the presence of the HVT coupling in NED allows for the existence of linearly stable, nonsingular BHs.
By including both electric and magnetic charges, we found that the existence of regular BHs compatible with the metric ansatz (
19
) requires the magnetic charge
q_{M}
to vanish.
Therefore, we restricted our analysis to purely electric BHs and investigated their linear stability for both nonsingular and singular cases.

In Sec.
III.2
, we showed that purely electric BHs with regular centers can exist at the background level. There is a branch where the electric field remains finite near
r=0
, with
A_{0-}^{\prime}(r)\propto r^{3}
. The NED Lagrangian
{\cal L}(F)
can then be reconstructed to yield the regular metric functions expanded as in Eq. (
19
) around the origin. Depending on whether
h_{3}
vanishes, the resulting functional form of
{\cal L}(F)
near
r=0
differs.

In Sec.
IV
, we studied the background profiles of singular BHs
for five classes of
theories with an Einstein-Hilbert
term:
(A) Maxwell-HVT theory,
(B) Power-law NED theory with
\beta=0
,
(C) Born-Infeld theory with
\beta=0
,
(D) Power-law or
Born-Infeld NED theories with

\beta\neq 0
, and
(E) Reconstructed NED theories with
\beta\neq 0
.
In theories (A)–(D), the metric functions behave as
h(r)\propto r^{-1}
and
f(r)\propto r^{-1}
near
r=0
, exhibiting properties that
are different from those of the
RN solution.
This behavior is related to the regularization of the electric field near the origin, as summarized in
Table
1
. In theory (E), we reconstructed
f(r)
and
A_{0}^{\prime}(r)
by assuming the other metric function
to take the form

h(r)=1-2m/r+b_{1}/r^{2}
.
In this case, we showed that
f(r)

approaches a constant as
r\to 0
,
with the regular electric field

A_{0}^{\prime}(r)
proportional to
r
.

In Sec.
V
, we derived the second-order action for perturbations on the SSS background for electric BHs in NED with the HVT coupling.
There are four dynamical perturbations originating from the gravitational and vector-field sectors, which can be classified into two odd-parity and two even-parity modes.
We showed that neither ghosts nor Laplacian instabilities appear in odd- and even-parity perturbations under the conditions

{\cal G}_{1}>0
,
{\cal G}_{2}>0
,

{\cal G}_{3}>0
,

c_{\Omega 3}^{2}>0
, and

c_{\Omega 4}^{2}>0
.
These stability criteria are valid in both timelike and spacelike regions.

In Sec.
VI
, we applied the linear stability conditions to nonsingular BHs and found that
c_{\Omega 4}^{2}
, which corresponds to the squared propagation speed of the even-parity vector perturbation
V
, is always negative near the regular center. Since the even-parity perturbation
\chi_{2}
is subject to Laplacian instabilities through its coupling with
V
, the regular metric cannot be maintained in a steady state. Thus, as in NED, linearly stable nonsingular BHs cannot exist, even with the inclusion of the HVT coupling.

In Sec.
VII
, we studied
the linear stability of singular BHs
for the five classes of theories discussed in Sec.
IV
.
In Maxwell-HVT theory (A), we found that
c_{\Omega 2}^{2}
and
c_{\Omega 4}^{2}
are negative near
r=0
, leading to Laplacian instabilities unless the transition distance
r_{g}
is smaller than the EFT scale
r_{\rm EFT}
, which marks the limit of validity for linear perturbation theory.
This imposes a bound on the HVT coupling,
|\beta|\lesssim r_{\rm EFT}^{3}/r_{h}
, where
r_{h}
is the outer horizon radius.
Since
r_{\rm EFT}
is expected to be
much smaller than
r_{h}
, the coupling

|\beta|
is constrained to be

|\beta|\ll r_{h}^{2}
.
This fact was not recognized in Ref.
Chen et al. 2024
, as the stability analysis there was restricted to the region outside the outer horizon.

In power-law NED with
\beta=0
, i.e.,
theory (B) with
{\cal L}(F)=F+a_{p}F^{p}
, we showed that neither ghost nor Laplacian instabilities occur for
a_{p}>0
and
p>2
in both timelike and spacelike regions.
In Born-Infeld theory with
\beta=0
, i.e., theory (C) with

{\cal L}(F)=(2/b)(1-\sqrt{1-bF})
, all linear stability conditions are satisfied for
r>0
. However, a strong coupling can arise due to
c_{\Omega 4}^{2}
vanishing as
r\to 0
.
To avoid this problem, the coupling
b
is constrained to be
b<2r_{\rm EFT}^{4}/q_{E}^{2}\ll 2r_{h}^{4}/q_{E}^{2}
.
Under such a bound, the metric outside the outer horizon is almost indistinguishable from that of the RN BH.
In theories (D), i.e., power-law or Born-Infeld NED with
\beta\neq 0
, the HVT coupling dominates over the NED terms near
r=0
, leading to Laplacian instabilities due to negative values of
c_{\Omega 4}^{2}
.
Thus, unless the HVT coupling is sufficiently small, as in theory (A), i.e.,
|\beta|\lesssim r_{\rm EFT}^{3}/r_{h}
, the BH solutions in theories (D) become unstable.

In theories (E), Laplacian instabilities near
r=0
are absent for
M>0
and
b_{1}<0
.
As shown in Fig.
6
, there exists a branch of solutions with
\beta>0
where all squared angular propagation speeds remain positive
for all
r>0
.
This property is different from
those in theories (A) and (D).
However,
c_{\Omega 1}^{2}
and
c_{\Omega 4}^{2}
approach 0
as
r\to 0
, giving rise to a strong coupling issue around the BH center. Since
{\cal G}_{2}
is negative, a ghost
is also present. The Laplacian instabilities can be avoided
for classical perturbations,
but the presence of ghosts
can be problematic at the quantum level.

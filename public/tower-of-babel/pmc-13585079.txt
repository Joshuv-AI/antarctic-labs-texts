Evaluation of Clinical Efficiency of Two Single-file Ni-Ti Rotary Systems in Performing Root Canal Treatment in Primary Teeth: A Randomized Clinical Trial

Introduction

Primary teeth guide the appropriate eruption of permanent teeth and serve as natural space maintainers, making their preservation essential for maintaining function, esthetics, speech, and psychological well-being. Pulpectomy is the preferred course of treatment for deciduous teeth with symptoms of pulpal disease, aiming to retain the tooth until normal exfoliation while maintaining periapical health.1,2

Effective biomechanical preparation and canal disinfection are critical for success. Traditionally, stainless steel hand K-files were used, but their rigidity and inability to adapt resulted in prolonged chairside time and inadequate biomechanical preparation.

In order to address these issues, nickel-titanium (Ni-Ti) rotary instruments were introduced, offering enhanced flexibility, better canal shaping, and reduced treatment time. Among the available single-file Ni-Ti rotary instruments, the Kedo-S file system has been extensively explored since its inception. It is especially designed for primary teeth, namely, D1, E1, and U1.3 In contrast, XP-Endo Shaper, which is designed for oval canals, has shown promising results in permanent teeth. It improves canal shaping while preserving the natural anatomy and minimizing procedural errors. It has good canal-centering ability4 with less removal of canal dentin. Hence, these systems were considered for evaluation in this study.

The aim of the current study was to assess and compare instrumentation time and quality of obturation using Kedo-S and XP-Endo Shaper in pulpectomies of primary molars.

The research hypothesis stated that there is a difference in instrumentation time and quality of obturation using two single-file Ni-Ti rotary systems, that is, Kedo-S and XP-Endo Shaper.

Materials and Methods

This double-blinded, randomized clinical trial was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board and Ethics Committee (IEVDC/23/PG01/PPD/IVV/27) and registered under the Clinical Trials Registry of India (CTRI), CTRI Reg. No.: CTRI/2025/04/084040. The study followed the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) guidelines (Fig. 1). G*Power 3.1.9.7 software was used to compute the sample size based on the findings of the study by Pawar et al. The calculated sample size, with 80% power and a 95% confidence interval, was 25 teeth in each group.

CONSORT flowchart

Children aged 4–9 years were screened. Based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria, a total of 50 teeth were included after obtaining informed consent. Those requiring single- or two-visit pulpectomy were equally distributed into two groups, that is, group I (Kedo-S) and group II (XP-Endo Shaper), with 25 teeth in each group (Fig. 2). A computer-generated block randomization mechanism was employed to ensure randomization using opaque, sequentially numbered, sealed envelopes, and allocation concealment was achieved. These envelopes were then used to randomly assign each participant to one of the two groups. In the Kedo-S group, canal-specific files were utilized in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions, with settings of 2.2 Ncm torque and 250 rpm. XP-Endo Shaper was employed for all canals using long in-and-out pecking motions at 800 rpm with a torque of 1 Ncm.

(A) XP-Endo Shaper file, (B) obturation in the XP-Endo Shaper group, (C) Kedo-S file, (D) obturation in the Kedo-S group

A digital timer was used to record the instrumentation time in seconds, while the quality of obturation was assessed radiographically using the Coll and Sadrian criteria (Fig. 3) by two independent, trained assessors who were blinded to the study groups.

Fill characteristics. Radiographs showing (A) underfill, (B) Optimal fill, and (C) overfill

Statistical analysis was performed using Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) version 25.0 [International Business Machines Corporation (IBM), Armonk, NY]. The obtained data were subjected to the Mann–Whitney U test for instrumentation time and Pearson's Chi-squared test for quality of obturation.

Results

Demographic Data

The mean age of the children included in the study was 6 ± 1.7 years. The 50 teeth were contributed by 41 children: 32 underwent single-tooth pulpectomy and nine underwent two-tooth pulpectomies, representing any of the four quadrants, were included, contributing to a total of 41 children. Of the 41 children, 21 (51.21%) were males and 20 (48.79%) were females.

In the Kedo-S group, of the 25 teeth included, seven were primary first molars, and the remaining 18 were primary second molars. In the XP-Endo Shaper group, 10 were primary first molars and 15 were primary second molars.

The XP-Endo Shaper and Kedo-S groups differed statistically significantly in mean instrumentation time when compared between the groups (Fig. 4).

Intergroup comparison of instrumentation time

While assessing the quality of obturation (Fill Characteristics). (Fig. 5), a greater percentage of optimal fills (84%) and a fewer number of voids were evident in the XP-Endo Shaper group. On intergroup comparison of the quality of obturation, the majority of cases in both groups showed optimal fills, whereas underfills were more common in the XP-Endo Shaper group (12%) and overfills were more common in the Kedo-S group (16%). On evaluating the fourth criterion of the Coll and Sadrian criteria, that is, the existence or absence of voids, 13 (52%) teeth in the Kedo-S group and eight (32%) teeth in the XP-Endo Shaper group demonstrated the presence of voids. Overall, when pulpectomies were performed, primary first molars exhibited a higher percentage of voids.

Discussion

Pulpectomy is the main treatment option for primary teeth with pulpal involvement or dentoalveolar abscess. The main objective is to effectively clean and debride the root canal while maintaining a healthy, noninfected state until the tooth naturally exfoliates. This can be achieved by adhering to the biologic principles essential for cleaning and promoting healing of the root canal.5

Traditionally, root canal shaping is performed using Ni-Ti or non-Ni-Ti hand instruments, such as H-files, K-files, and Flex-R files, which provide good tactile control and are cost-effective. However, this method is very time-consuming and is associated with several drawbacks, such as inadequate cleaning, apical transportation, and ledge formation.6 In pediatric patients, the duration of therapy is especially important without compromising treatment quality. Treatment time is a priority because of the associated behavioral issues as well as immature development, which poses coping difficulties during invasive procedures.

To overcome the disadvantages of stainless steel hand files, rotary files were introduced, offering their own advantages and disadvantages. This system enhances the effectiveness of biomechanical preparation, resulting in more uniform preparation.7 Their use has evolved over the years from a sequential filing system to a single-file system, with the latest being the adaptive type.

Oval-shaped root canals are common in primary teeth, posing a challenge for rotary files with a central metal core. Since these files have a round cross-section, they tend to create a round-shaped space within the canal, leaving certain recesses uninstrumented and thereby resulting in ineffective canal shaping and debridement.8

Drukteinis and Balciuniene highlighted the challenge of inadequate cleaning of fins and the isthmus in primary teeth due to the centering effect of rotary files. They recommended the use of hand files in conjunction with rotary instruments to effectively remove infected tissue from ribbon-shaped canals. This further increases chairside time.9

Reduced instrumentation time and superior-quality fillings are two benefits of the rotary technique. Barr et al. introduced the use of Ni-Ti files for cleaning and shaping primary teeth in 2000 using ProFile 0.04 taper rotary instruments. They stated that using Ni-Ti rotary files to prepare root canals in deciduous teeth was less expensive, quicker, and produced predictable, uniform fillings. However, the risk of excessive instrumentation and perforation of thin dentin walls was also highlighted.10

Nickel-titanium instruments offer several advantages over stainless steel instruments. Their distinctive features include shape memory, superelasticity, and biocompatibility.11 These properties are due to reversible phase transformations between martensite and austenite. There are seven generations of NiTi instruments, with the latest generation (e.g., XP-Endo Shaper) using advanced shape-setting technology for improved performance.

Over time, there has been a significant increase in the use of rotary instrumentation in deciduous teeth using Ni-Ti files originally designed for permanent teeth. The literature demonstrates that pulpectomies of primary teeth, when performed with rotary file systems such as ProFile 0.04, ProTaper, Mtwo, FlexMaster, and WaveOne, are quicker and produce consistently uniform, predictable fills.12

However, because of limited mouth opening in children, rotary instruments designed for permanent teeth have additional drawbacks, including an increased risk of file breakage. This led to the introduction of pediatric rotary systems to overcome these drawbacks while providing better obturation quality, improved canal centricity, and conservation of root dentin. Nevertheless, the introduction of the Kedo-S Ni-Ti rotary file system by Jeevanandan et al. in 2017 pioneered the concept of a rotary file designed exclusively for children. The Kedo-S rotary file system, tailored for pediatric endodontics, includes five generations: Kedo-S, Kedo SG, Kedo-SG Blue, Kedo-S Square, and Kedo-S Plus.

The initial generation of the Kedo-S rotary file system included three Ni-Ti files of 16 mm in length. These files have a triangular cross-section and a noncutting tip, with a 12 mm active portion and a taper that progressively increases from 0.04 to 0.08, with different tip diameters: D1, 0.25; E1, 0.30; and U1, 0.40. This design allows efficient preparation of the root canal without overinstrumenting the fragile walls of primary teeth.

With the introduction of adaptive technology in file systems, XP-Endo Shaper was developed specifically to address the challenges posed by oval root canals in permanent teeth.13 It features a helical shaft made from a size 30 wire with a 0.01 taper. Hence, this file system was evaluated in primary molars, which have oval canals.

According to Silva et al.,14 hand preparation required more time than Ni-Ti rotary preparation in extracted primary teeth. The study conducted by Makarem et al. found that chairside time was reduced while using rotary instrumentation (FlexMaster system) compared with hand instrumentation.15 These findings are consistent with those reported in other in vitro and in vivo studies conducted by Nagaratna et al.,11 Pinheiro et al.,16 Kummer et al.,17 Ochoa-Romero et al.,18 and various others. Hence, hand files were not considered, and the two single-file Ni-Ti rotary systems, representing the older and the latest generations, were evaluated.

Outcomes

The outcomes evaluated were as follows: (1) instrumentation time and (2) quality of obturation.

Instrumentation Time

In this study, XP-Endo Shaper showed a marked reduction in instrumentation time of <1 minute compared with the Kedo-S rotary file, which required nearly 1.5 minutes. XP-Endo Shaper appeared to be the most effective in reducing instrumentation time.

According to the literature, only two studies have been conducted by the same author, Bhagyashri A. Pawar et al., in primary teeth in both in vitro and in vivo settings. XP-Endo Shaper demonstrated decreased instrumentation time in both settings and reduced apical debris extrusion in the in vitro setting.

Pawar et al.19 conducted an in vitro study that included 45 extracted primary canines. Group I (XP-Endo Shaper), group II (Kedo-S; D1 and E1), and group III (hand K-files) were evaluated. The results showed that XP-Endo Shaper resulted in decreased instrumentation time (2.38 ± 0.58 minutes) compared with the other two groups (hand K-files, 7.33 ± 1.20 minutes; Kedo-S, 4.61 ± 0.73 minutes).

In the present study, XP-Endo Shaper demonstrated a shorter instrumentation time than Kedo-S. The present results are in agreement with the study conducted by Pawar et al.20; however, their study also included hand files, whereas these were not considered in the present study. The instrumentation times were 10.9 ± 0.7 minutes and 14.8 ± 1.2 minutes for XP-Endo Shaper and Kedo-S, respectively. In the present study, the values were much lower, that is, 0.854 minutes and 1.698 minutes for XP-Endo Shaper and Kedo-S, respectively.

A review of the literature on instrumentation time using Kedo-S identified four studies comparing it with hand K-files, Kedo-SH, and rotary systems, including ProTaper, Kedo-SG Blue, and Pro AF Gold, in primary molar pulpectomy procedures.

Rajain et al.21 found that instrumentation with Kedo-S demonstrated significantly reduced time (8.03 ± 0.823 minutes) relative to K-files (11.27 ± 0.928 minutes).

Kalita et al.22 compared Kedo-S, ProTaper, and hand K-files for instrumentation time and found that Kedo-S showed the shortest duration, that is, 2.53 minutes, which was greater than that observed in the present study. It was stated that Kedo-S files provided superior cleaning efficiency in the coronal and middle thirds of root canals compared with ProTaper and K-files.

Shah et al.23 conducted a study using K-files, Kedo-S files, and Pro-AF Baby Gold files and found that Kedo-S files required the least instrumentation time (19.25 ± 2.98 minutes), followed by Pro-AF (21.89 ± 2 minutes).

In contrast, a study by Priyadarshini et al.24 comparing K-files with Kedo-SH, Kedo-S, and Kedo-SG Blue found that the shortest mean instrumentation time was achieved with Kedo-SG Blue (2.7840 ± 0.34217 minutes), followed by Kedo-S (3.4827 ± 0.48657 minutes), Kedo-SH (5.8800 ± 0.48345 minutes), and hand K-files (6.2167 ± 0.30978 minutes).

Quality of Obturation

Various criteria have been proposed for evaluating the quality of obturation in primary teeth, including the Coll and Sadrian criteria, the KEDOO classification, and the criteria proposed by Memarpour et al. The gold standard is the Coll and Sadrian criteria, proposed in 1996, which categorize obturation as optimal, underfilled, or overfilled.

Navit et al. concluded in their study that zinc oxide eugenol (ZOE) has the highest antimicrobial property after Endoflas.25 In the present study, zinc oxide eugenol was used as the obturating material because ZOE paste is widely used owing to its advantages. The quantities of eugenol released at the periapical area immediately after placement range from 10-⁴ to 10-⁶ drops after 24 hours and become zero after 1 month. Eugenol is believed to possess analgesic and anti-inflammatory properties within these concentrations, which are advantageous following pulpectomy. It is inexpensive and has been used by numerous researchers.

Considering the quality of obturation, an optimal fill is the most desirable outcome, followed by underfill, whereas overfill is undesirable when zinc oxide eugenol is used as the obturating material.

On intergroup comparison, an optimal fill was the predominant outcome in both groups, with group II (XP-Endo Shaper) showing a marginally higher rate. Moreover, overfill was observed more frequently in the Kedo-S group (16%); however, the difference was not statistically significant. The incidences of underfill and overfill remained relatively low overall.

Because overfill is more undesirable than underfill, teeth with overfills were scheduled for follow-up every 6 months until the normal exfoliation of the deciduous teeth, because overextruded zinc oxide eugenol material can cause deflection of the eruption path of permanent teeth. In such situations, extraction of the tooth followed by appropriate space management until eruption of the permanent tooth is recommended.

In all cases of underfill, the obturating material extended only 2–4 mm short of the apex, indicating a desirable outcome with the rotary files.

When voids are present in obturated root canals, the survival rate of the tooth decreases markedly. On evaluating the fourth criterion of the Coll and Sadrian criteria, that is, the presence or absence of voids, 13 (52%) teeth in the Kedo-S group and eight (32%) teeth in the XP-Endo Shaper group demonstrated the presence of voids. Voids were more common in pulpectomies performed in primary first molars. This indicates the better efficiency of XP-Endo Shaper over Kedo-S.

As the sample size and the type of file systems used in the current study and the study by Pawar et al. were similar, the results are comparable. Higher percentages of optimal fills were observed in the present study in both groups.

Regarding the quality of obturation with Kedo-S, the other file systems evaluated included K-files, H-files, Kedo-SH, ProTaper, Kedo-SG Blue, and K3 rotary files.

Priyadarshini et al., Panchal et al., and Divya et al. evaluated obturation using the Coll and Sadrian criteria, and Metapex was used as the obturating material.7,24,26

In studies by Panchal et al. and Divya et al., Kedo-S demonstrated higher percentages of optimal fills than the other file systems. However, in the study by Priyadarshini et al., Kedo-S demonstrated the lowest percentage of optimal fills compared with the other rotary files but performed better than hand K-files.

An in vitro study by Pawar et al. reported that XP-Endo Shaper resulted in reduced apical debris extrusion.19 A randomized clinical trial conducted by Thakur et al. demonstrated reduced postoperative pain in deciduous molars using XP-Endo Shaper compared with Kedo-SG and manual K-files.27 Because postoperative pain is multifactorial and extruded material can also contribute to postoperative pain, leading to potential bias, postoperative pain was not considered for evaluation in the current study. Supporting this, a systematic review and meta-analysis by Dedhia et al.28 highlighted that the instrument's flexible design contributed to both decreased apical debris extrusion and reduced postoperative pain. Therefore, future studies can evaluate these parameters in deciduous teeth.

Limitations

The current study used two-dimensional intraoral periapical radiographs to evaluate obturation quality because cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) is not recommended for children according to the American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry (AAPD) guidelines unless it has a major impact on diagnosis and treatment planning. Therefore, CBCT evaluation was not considered in the present study. The primary disadvantage of Ni-Ti single-file rotary systems is their high cost. As the cutting efficiency of these systems declines, the frequency of file replacement increases, thereby further increasing the cost.

Future Scope

Further studies can be planned using various other single-file Ni-Ti pediatric rotary file systems along with three-dimensional radiographic techniques [micro-computed tomography (microCT) and cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT)] to evaluate the quality of obturation. Very few in vitro and randomized clinical trials have evaluated XP-Endo Shaper in primary anterior teeth; therefore, further research can be conducted in both in vitro and in vivo settings. Similar studies can also be conducted using the newer KEDOO classification for assessing the quality of obturation. Long-term follow-up studies are needed to determine the clinical and radiographic success of pulpectomy performed using various Ni-Ti single-file systems.

Clinical Significance

Both single-file systems performed well in terms of instrumentation time. This supports the efficacy of single-file pediatric rotary systems, which can be considered in situations such as pulpectomies performed under general anesthesia, where time is a constraint. Considering the quality of obturation, XP-Endo Shaper performed better, with fewer voids and a greater number of optimal fills.

Conclusion

The limitations and observations of this study led to the following conclusions:

The instrumentation time for the evaluated single-file Ni-Ti rotary systems ranged between 0.854 and 1.692 minutes.

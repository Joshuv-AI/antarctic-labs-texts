Taeuk Nam

Abstract

Introduction

0.1.
Motivation

0.1.1.

In
[
DG15
]
, Drinfeld and Gaitsgory showed that the DG category
\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
enjoys a certain smallness property called
compact generation
. Roughly speaking, the
compact objects
of a category
{\mathbf{C}}
are those objects
c\in{\mathbf{C}}
such that mapping out of
c
is well-behaved (i.e. commutes with colimits), and the category
{\mathbf{C}}
is said to be
compactly generated
if every object of
{\mathbf{C}}
can be written as a colimit of compact objects.

0.1.2.

Being compactly generated is a highly desirable finiteness condition on a DG category for the following two reasons, one internal and one external.

0.1.3.

The first reason is that if
{\mathbf{C}}
is compactly generated, then it suffices to understand maps between compact objects to understand maps between arbitrary objects. More concretely, if
c,d\in{\mathbf{C}}
, we can write

c=\underset{i\in I}{\operatorname{colim}}\ c_{i},d=\underset{j\in J}{\operatorname{colim}}\ d_{j}

where the
c_{i}
and
d_{j}
are compact objects. Then we have

(0.1)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}aps}_{{\mathbf{C}}}(c,d)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{M}aps}_{{\mathbf{C}}}(\underset{i\in I}{\operatorname{colim}}\ c_{i},\underset{j\in J}{\operatorname{colim}}\ d_{j})

(0.2)

\displaystyle=\underset{i\in I}{\operatorname{lim}}\ {\mathcal{M}aps}_{{\mathbf{C}}}(c_{i},\underset{j\in J}{\operatorname{colim}}\ d_{j})

(0.3)

\displaystyle=\underset{i\in I}{\operatorname{lim}}\ \underset{j\in J}{\operatorname{colim}}\ {\mathcal{M}aps}_{{\mathbf{C}}}(c_{i},d_{j}).

0.1.4.

The second reason is that if
{\mathbf{C}}
is compactly generated, then (continuous) functors out of
{\mathbf{C}}
are determined by where they send the compact objects. In other words, if
{\mathbf{D}}
is another (not necessarily compactly generated) cocomplete DG category, then we have an identification

\operatorname{Fun}_{cts}({\mathbf{C}},{\mathbf{D}})=\operatorname{Fun}_{exact}({\mathbf{C}}^{c},{\mathbf{D}})

where
{\mathbf{C}}^{c}
is the full subcategory of compact objects.

0.1.5.

The following is one source of motivation for wanting to study
\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
in particular.

0.1.6.

The (de Rham, global, critical level) unramified geometric Langlands conjecture states that there is an equivalence of DG categories

\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\xrightarrow{\sim}{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{LocSys}_{G^{\vee}})

that satisfies a number of conditions.

0.1.7.

The right hand side of the equivalence,
{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{LocSys}_{G^{\vee}})
, is equivalent to the ind-completion
\operatorname{Ind}({\operatorname{Coh}}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{LocSys}_{G^{\vee}}))
, so it is
a priori
compactly generated. Thus, the compact generation of
\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
is a necessary condition for the geometric Langlands conjecture to hold.

0.1.8.

The conjecture was recently proven in a series of five papers
[
GR24a
]
,
[
ABC+24a
]
,
[
CCF+24
]
,
[
ABC+24b
]
,
[
GR24b
]
by nine authors: Arinkin, Beraldo, Campbell, Chen, Faergeman, Gaitsgory, Lin, Raskin, and Rozenblyum.

0.1.9.

The first step of the proof, contained in
[
GR24a
]
, is the construction of the Langlands functor

\mathbb{L}_{G}:\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\to{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{LocSys}_{G^{\vee}}).

The point at which the compact generation of
\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
is used is as follows: there is a monoidal action

{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LocSys}_{G^{\vee}})\otimes\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\to\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

called the
spectral action
. By fixing a certain object

\operatorname{Poinc}_{G,!}^{\operatorname{Vac,glob}}\in\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

called the
vacuum Poincare sheaf
, we obtain a functor

{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LocSys}_{G^{\vee}})\to\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}).

This functor has a continuous right adjoint, which we denote

\mathbb{L}_{G,\operatorname{coarse}}:\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\to{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LocSys}_{G^{\vee}}).

Then it is shown in
[
GR24a
, Theorem 1.6.2]
that
\mathbb{L}_{G,\operatorname{coarse}}
sends compact objects in
\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
to eventually coconnective objects in
{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LocSys}_{G^{\vee}})
. In other words, we have an exact functor

\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})^{c}\to{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LocSys}_{G^{\vee}})^{>-\infty}

and since the functor

\Psi_{\operatorname{Nilp},\{0\}}:{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{LocSys}_{G^{\vee}})\to{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LocSys}_{G^{\vee}})

is an equivalence on the eventually coconnective full subcategories, this yields

\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})^{c}\to{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{LocSys}_{G^{\vee}})^{>-\infty}\subset{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{LocSys}_{G^{\vee}})

and finally, because
\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
is compactly generated, we obtain a continuous functor

\mathbb{L}_{G}:\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\to{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{LocSys}_{G^{\vee}}).

0.1.10.

A natural direction of further inquiry and generalization is to go from the unramified setting to the ramified setting. This means that instead of considering the stack
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
, we instead consider the moduli stack of principal
G
-bundles on
X
with
level structure
at a point
x\in X
.

0.1.11.

Let
D_{x}
be the formal disc around
x\in X
. Restricting a principal
G
-bundle
{\mathcal{F}}_{G}
along
D_{x}\to X
yields a principal
G
-bundle
{\mathcal{F}}_{G,D_{x}}
on
D_{x}
. Then we can interpret
G({\mathcal{O}})
as the group of sections of the constant group scheme
D_{x}\times G
over
D_{x}
and consider
{\mathcal{F}}_{G,D_{x}}
as a
G({\mathcal{O}})
-torsor. For a subgroup
H\subset G({\mathcal{O}})
,
H
-level structure (at
x
) on
{\mathcal{F}}_{G}
is the data of a reduction of
{\mathcal{F}}_{G,D_{x}}
to
H
. The moduli stack of principal
G
-bundles on
X
with
H
-level structure at
x\in X
is denoted by
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{H}
.

0.1.12.

A side remark is that we could of course consider
H
-level structure at any arbitrary finite collection of points
x_{1},...,x_{k}\in X
(or even level structure for a different subgroup at each point). However, for the sake of ease of exposition, here and in the sequel, we will discuss the single point case and only bring up the multiple point case as necessary.

0.1.13.

Let us take a moment to note that in the case of
H=G({\mathcal{O}})
,
H
-level structure is no additional structure at all, i.e.
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{G({\mathcal{O}})}=\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
. The
smaller
the subgroup
H
, the
wilder
the ramification; for example, the case of
H=\{e\}
is called
full level structure
, and the corresponding stack is denoted
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\infty x}
.

0.1.14.

This leads to the natural question: for any subgroup
H\subset G({\mathcal{O}})
, can we describe the category
\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{H})
in spectral terms? i.e. is there a ramified geometric Langlands equivalence?

0.1.15.

For an arbitrary subgroup
H\subset G({\mathcal{O}})
, this question is out of reach with current technology. Thus as a first step, it makes sense to consider large subgroups of
G({\mathcal{O}})
(or in other words,
tame
ramification). More precisely, there is a (surjective) group homomorphism

G({\mathcal{O}})\to G

by evaluation at
t=0
, and we call its kernel the
first congruence subgroup

G^{(1)}({\mathcal{O}})
. We say a subgroup
H\subset G({\mathcal{O}})
is
tame
if it contains
G^{(1)}({\mathcal{O}})
.

0.1.16.

The geometric significance of tameness is that for a tame subgroup
H
,
H
-level structure is the same as the data of a
H/G^{(1)}({\mathcal{O}})
-reduction at
x
. In plain language, tame level structure lives
only
at the point itself,
not
on the formal neighborhood.

0.1.17.

We will focus on the tame subgroup
\operatorname{I}
, called the
Iwahori
subgroup. This is the preimage of the borel
B\subset G
under the homomorphism
G({\mathcal{O}})\to G
. Thus Iwahori level structure is the data of a
B
-reduction at
x\in X
.

0.1.18.

As a step towards the Iwahori ramified geometric Langlands conjecture, in the body of this paper we will show that
\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I}})
is compactly generated.

0.2.
Main Result

0.2.1.

In
[
DG15
]
, Drinfeld and Gaitsgory proved that the DG category of D-modules on the algebraic stack
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
is compactly generated.

0.2.2.

This paper builds upon
[
DG15
]
in the following way: instead of
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
, we consider the algebraic stack
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I}}
classifying principal
G
-bundles on
X
together with Iwahori level structure at a fixed point
x\in X
(i.e. a
B
-reduction at a fixed point
x\in X
). Our main result states that

Theorem 0.2.3
.

\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I}})
is compactly generated.

0.2.4.

More generally, for a finite collection of points
x_{1},...,x_{k}\in X
, we can consider the algebraic stack
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\operatorname{I};x_{1},...,x_{k})}
classifying principal
G
-bundles on
X
together with Iwahori level structure at each point
x_{j}
. Our argument will go through essentially verbatim in this generality, showing that

Theorem 0.2.5
.

\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\operatorname{I};x_{1},...,x_{k})})
is compactly generated.

0.2.6.

It has been pointed out to us that an unpublished article
[
AG09
]
by Gaitsgory and Arkhipov sketches the proof of compact generation in both the unramified and Iwahori ramified case. However, this argument has a deficiency of a combinatorial nature which was fixed in
[
DG15
]
for the unramified case. Our result shows that a similar modification is sufficient for the Iwahori ramified case.

0.3.
The Structure of the Paper

0.3.1.

Here is a description of how the paper is organized.

0.3.2.

In Section
1
, we begin by introducing the definition of compactly generated DG category in Subsection
1.1
. Then we summarize Drinfeld and Gaitsgory’s proof in
[
DG15
]
that
\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
is compactly generated. Here is a very brief overview of the argument:

0.3.3.

First, a prior result in
[
DG13
]
, by the same authors, says that QCA algebraic stacks, i.e. those that are quasicompact (”QC”) and have affine automorphism groups (”A”), have compactly generated DG categories of D-modules. We give a summary of the discussion in Subsection
1.2
.

0.3.4.

The complication is that
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
is not QCA because it fails to be quasicompact. However,
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
does have affine automorphism groups, so it is
locally
QCA. This means that
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
is a union of quasicompact QCA open substacks, so
\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
is a limit of compactly generated DG categories, under
!
-pullback.

0.3.5.

If these
!
-pullbacks had well defined left adjoints, this limit would be equivalent to taking the colimit where the connecting morphisms are the left adjoints of the
!
-pullbacks, the
!
-pushforward functors. Then since these
!
-pushforward functors would have continuous right adjoints, the colimit would automatically be compactly generated for formal reasons. Unfortunately,
!
-pushforward functors are not well defined along all maps.

0.3.6.

A property of open substacks called
co-truncativeness
on
U
guarantees all
!
-pushforward functors along inclusions
U\to U^{\prime}
are well-defined. This is discussed in Subsection
1.3
.

0.3.7.

We then review in Subsection
1.4
a geometric condition on the closed complement of
U
called
contractiveness
that is sufficient for
U
to be co-truncative.

0.3.8.

The explicit stratification of
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
into quasicompact open substacks given in
[
DG15
]
, roughly speaking, is called the Harder-Narasimhan stratification and has to do with the degrees of the reductions of the principal
G
-bundles to parabolic subgroups. We describe this stratification in Subsection
1.5
.

0.3.9.

Finally, we sketch the proof that the closed complements of the quasicompact open substacks above are indeed contractive (and therefore that the quasicompact open substacks are indeed co-truncative) in Subsection
1.6
.

0.3.10.

In Section
2
, we go through the proof of compact generation, in both the unramified setting (in Subsection
2.1
) and the Iwahori ramified setting (in Subsection
2.2
), for
G=\operatorname{SL}_{2}
. Most of the conceptual ideas of the proof appear already in this toy example, so a reader wanting a broad understanding of how the proof works without thinking too carefully about the combinatorics on first pass can skip Subsections
1.5
and
1.6
to proceed directly to Section
2
.

0.3.11.

In Section
3
, we prove the main result of the paper, Theorem
0.2.3
, that
\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I}})
is compactly generated.

0.3.12.

We spend Subsections
3.1
and
3.2
giving an analogous stratification of
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I}}
. Very roughly speaking, we stratify first by the strata of the underlying principal
G
-bundle in the Harder-Narasimhan stratification, then stratify further by the relative position of the Iwahori level structure (i.e. the
B
-reduction at a point) and the
P
-reduction.

0.3.13.

We then spend Subsections
3.3
to
3.7
showing that the closed complements of the quasicompact open substacks of our stratification are actually contractive.

0.3.14.

Finally, we discuss the proof of Theorem
0.2.5
, the compact generation of
\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\operatorname{I},x_{1},...,x_{k})})
, in Subsection
3.8
, explaining that our entire argument goes through modulo only a single change relating to a certain numerical bound in the definition of the stratification.

1.
Compact Generation of
\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

In this section, we give a brief summary of what has already been proven by Drinfeld and Gaitsgory in
[
DG15
]
, i.e. the compact generation of
\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
. We emphasize that none of the results in this section are due to us.

1.1.
Compact Generation of DG Categories

1.1.1.

Our setting is
\operatorname{DGCat}_{\operatorname{cont}}
; in other words our DG categories are assumed to be cocomplete and the functors between them are assumed to be continuous unless otherwise noted.

1.1.2.

An object
{\mathbf{c}}\in{\mathbf{C}}
is said to be
compact
if

{\mathcal{M}aps}_{{\mathbf{C}}}({\mathbf{c}},-):{\mathbf{C}}\to\operatorname{Vect}

is a continuous functor.

Definition 1.1.3
.

[
DG15
, 1.2.2]

Let
{\mathbf{C}}
be a DG category. We say that
{\mathbf{C}}
is
compactly generated
if there exists a collection of compact objects
\{{\mathbf{c}}_{\alpha}\in{\mathbf{C}}\}_{\alpha\in I}
such that

{\mathcal{M}aps}_{{\mathbf{C}}}({\mathbf{c}}_{\alpha},{\mathbf{c}}^{\prime})=0\ \forall\alpha

implies
{\mathbf{c}}^{\prime}=0
.

1.1.4.

The next two propositions concern stability properties of being compactly generated.

Proposition 1.1.5
.

Let
{\mathbf{C}}
and
{\mathbf{D}}
be a pair of DG categories, and let

{\mathsf{F}}:{\mathbf{C}}\to{\mathbf{D}}

be a (continuous) functor between them. Then
{\mathsf{F}}
has a (not necessarily continuous) right adjoint

{\mathsf{G}}:{\mathbf{D}}\to{\mathbf{C}}.

Suppose
{\mathsf{G}}
is continuous and conservative. If
{\mathbf{C}}
is compactly generated, then so is
{\mathbf{D}}
.

1.1.6.

Now consider a functor

\Psi:I\to\operatorname{DGCat}_{\operatorname{cont}}

that sends

(i\in I)\mapsto{\mathbf{C}}_{i},\ (i\to j\in I)\mapsto(\psi_{i,j}:{\mathbf{C}}_{i}\to{\mathbf{C}}_{j}).

For each
i\to j\in I
,

\psi_{i,j}:{\mathbf{C}}_{i}\to{\mathbf{C}}_{j}

has a right adjoint

\phi_{j,i}:{\mathbf{C}}_{j}\to{\mathbf{C}}_{i}.

Suppose
\phi_{j,i}
is continuous for all
i\to j
. Then we obtain a functor

\Phi:I^{\operatorname{op}}\to\operatorname{DGCat}_{\operatorname{cont}}

and it is a fact (see
[
DG15
, Proposition 1.7.5]
) that

\underset{i\in I}{\underset{\longrightarrow}{colim}}\ ({\mathbf{C}}_{i},\psi_{i,j})

is canonically equivalent to

\underset{i\in I^{\operatorname{op}}}{\underset{\longleftarrow}{lim}}\ ({\mathbf{C}}_{i},\phi_{j,i}).

Proposition 1.1.7
.

[
DG15
, Corollary 1.9.4]

In the above situation, if
{\mathbf{C}}_{i}
is compactly generated for all
i\in I
, then
\underset{i\in I}{\underset{\longrightarrow}{colim}}\ ({\mathbf{C}}_{i},\psi_{i,j})
is compactly generated.

1.2.
QCA Stacks

1.2.1.

In this subsection, we will give an extremely brief summary of the parts of
[
DG13
]
that pertains to the compact generation of
\operatorname{D-mod}
.

1.2.2.

First, let us recall the definition of
{\operatorname{IndCoh}}
. Given a quasicompact (locally noetherian and almost of finite type) DG scheme
Z
, we can consider

{\operatorname{Coh}}(Z)\subset{\operatorname{QCoh}}(Z)

and define

{\operatorname{IndCoh}}(Z):=\operatorname{Ind}({\operatorname{Coh}}(Z)).

1.2.3.

Given a map
f:Z_{1}\to Z_{2}
, there is a (continuous) functor

f^{!}:{\operatorname{IndCoh}}(Z_{2})\to{\operatorname{IndCoh}}(Z_{1})

defined in
[
Gai
]
. This data upgrades to a functor

{\operatorname{IndCoh}}^{!}:(\operatorname{DGSch}_{\operatorname{aft}})^{\operatorname{op}}\to\operatorname{DGCat}_{\operatorname{cont}}

which sends

(Z\in\operatorname{DGSch}_{\operatorname{aft}})\mapsto{\operatorname{IndCoh}}(Z),\ (f:Z_{1}\to Z_{2})\mapsto(f^{!}:{\operatorname{IndCoh}}(Z_{2})\to{\operatorname{IndCoh}}(Z_{1})).

1.2.4.

Let
\operatorname{PreStk}
denote the category of (locally almost of finite type) prestacks. We can left Kan extend
{\operatorname{IndCoh}}^{!}
along the inclusion

\operatorname{DGSch}_{\operatorname{aft}}^{\operatorname{op}}\to\operatorname{PreStk}^{\operatorname{op}}

to obtain a functor, also denoted

{\operatorname{IndCoh}}^{!}:\operatorname{PreStk}^{\operatorname{op}}\to\operatorname{DGCat}_{\operatorname{cont}}

by a slight abuse of notation. More concretely, we have

{\operatorname{IndCoh}}({\mathcal{Y}})=\underset{Z\to{\mathcal{Y}}}{\underset{\longleftarrow}{lim}}{\operatorname{IndCoh}}(Z)

where the limit is taken over quasicompact DG schemes
Z
mapping to
{\mathcal{Y}}
. In other words, the data of an ind-coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
on a prestack
{\mathcal{Y}}
is equivalent to the data of a compatible family of ind-coherent sheaves
g^{!}{\mathcal{F}}\in{\operatorname{IndCoh}}(Z)
for each map
g:Z\to{\mathcal{Y}}
from a quasicompact DG scheme.

1.2.5.

Now let
{\mathcal{Y}}
be an algebraic stack. We can consider the full subcategory

{\operatorname{Coh}}({\mathcal{Y}})\subset{\operatorname{IndCoh}}({\mathcal{Y}})

consisting of
{\mathcal{F}}\in{\operatorname{IndCoh}}({\mathcal{Y}})
such that
g^{!}{\mathcal{F}}\in{\operatorname{Coh}}(Z)
for all smooth maps
g:Z\to{\mathcal{Y}}
from quasicompact DG schemes.

1.2.6.

The inclusion

{\operatorname{Coh}}({\mathcal{Y}})\subset{\operatorname{IndCoh}}({\mathcal{Y}})

yields a functor

\operatorname{Ind}({\operatorname{Coh}}({\mathcal{Y}}))\to{\operatorname{IndCoh}}({\mathcal{Y}})

and if
{\mathcal{Y}}
is a quasicompact
DG
scheme, by definition of
{\operatorname{IndCoh}}
this functor is an equivalence. However, in general it need not be.

Definition 1.2.7
.

[
DG15
, Definition 2.2.2]

Let
{\mathcal{Y}}
be an algebraic stack.
{\mathcal{Y}}
is
locally QCA
if the automorphism groups of geometric points of
{\mathcal{Y}}
are affine.
{\mathcal{Y}}
is
QCA
if it is quasicompact and locally QCA.

Proposition 1.2.8
.

[
DG13
, Theorem 3.3.5]

Let
{\mathcal{Y}}
be a QCA algebraic stack. Then the functor

\operatorname{Ind}({\operatorname{Coh}}({\mathcal{Y}}))\to{\operatorname{IndCoh}}({\mathcal{Y}})

defined above is an equivalence. In particular, for a QCA algebraic stack
{\mathcal{Y}}
,
{\operatorname{IndCoh}}({\mathcal{Y}})
is compactly generated by
{\operatorname{Coh}}({\mathcal{Y}})
.

1.2.9.

Now we will discuss the relationship between
{\operatorname{IndCoh}}
and
\operatorname{D-mod}
. Recall that by definition, for a (locally almost of finite type) prestack
{\mathcal{Y}}
,

\operatorname{D-mod}({\mathcal{Y}}):={\operatorname{IndCoh}}({\mathcal{Y}}_{\operatorname{dR}}).

The map
\iota:{\mathcal{Y}}\to{\mathcal{Y}}_{dR}
gives rise to a continuous and conservative functor

\iota^{!}:\operatorname{D-mod}({\mathcal{Y}})={\operatorname{IndCoh}}({\mathcal{Y}}_{\operatorname{dR}})\to{\operatorname{IndCoh}}({\mathcal{Y}})

which we will denote by
{\mathbf{oblv}}_{\operatorname{D-mod}({\mathcal{Y}})}
.

1.2.10.

For a general prestack,
{\mathbf{oblv}}_{\operatorname{D-mod}({\mathcal{Y}})}
need not admit a left adjoint. However, when
{\mathcal{Y}}
is an algebraic stack,
{\mathbf{oblv}}_{\operatorname{D-mod}({\mathcal{Y}})}
admits a left adjoint

{\mathbf{ind}}_{\operatorname{D-mod}({\mathcal{Y}})}:{\operatorname{IndCoh}}({\mathcal{Y}})\to\operatorname{D-mod}({\mathcal{Y}})

(see
[
DG13
, 6.3]
). Proposition
1.1.5
and Proposition
1.2.8
together imply

Proposition 1.2.11
.

Let
{\mathcal{Y}}
be a QCA algebraic stack. Then
\operatorname{D-mod}({\mathcal{Y}})
is compactly generated.

1.3.
Truncativeness and Truncatability

1.3.1.

Recall that the goal of this section is to summarize the argument that
\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
is compactly generated. If
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
were QCA, we would be done by Proposition
1.2.11
. Unfortunately,
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
is not QCA because it is not quasicompact. However, it is
locally
QCA, i.e. its field valued points have affine automorphism groups.

1.3.2.

Let
{\mathcal{Y}}
be a locally QCA algebraic stack. Denote by
\operatorname{Open}({\mathcal{Y}})
the poset of quasicompact open substacks
U\subset{\mathcal{Y}}
. We have

\operatorname{D-mod}({\mathcal{Y}})=\underset{U_{i}\in\operatorname{Open}({\mathcal{Y}})^{\operatorname{op}}}{\underset{\longleftarrow}{lim}}\operatorname{D-mod}(U_{i})

where for an inclusion
j_{i,j}:U_{i}\hookrightarrow U_{j}
the connecting functor is

j_{i,j}^{!}:\operatorname{D-mod}(U_{j})\to\operatorname{D-mod}(U_{i}).

If the functors
j^{!}
admitted well-defined left adjoints, we would be in the situation of Proposition
1.1.7
and we could conclude that

\operatorname{D-mod}({\mathcal{Y}})=\underset{U_{i}\in\operatorname{Open}({\mathcal{Y}})^{\operatorname{op}}}{\underset{\longleftarrow}{lim}}\operatorname{D-mod}(U_{i})=\underset{U_{i}\in\operatorname{Open}({\mathcal{Y}})}{\underset{\longrightarrow}{colim}}\operatorname{D-mod}(U_{i})

is compactly generated. Unfortunately,
j_{i,j}^{!}
does not always admit a left adjoint.

Definition 1.3.3
.

[
DG15
, Definition 3.1.5]

Let
{\mathcal{Y}}
be a QCA algebraic stack. Let
i:{\mathcal{Z}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{Y}}
be a closed substack and let
j:U\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{Y}}
be its complementary open. We say that
{\mathcal{Z}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{Y}}
is
truncative
and that
U\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{Y}}
is
co-truncative
if
i_{*}
admits a well-defined left adjoint

i^{*}:\operatorname{D-mod}({\mathcal{Y}})\to\operatorname{D-mod}({\mathcal{Z}})

or equivalently, if
j^{!}
admits a well-defined left adjoint

j_{!}:\operatorname{D-mod}(U)\to\operatorname{D-mod}({\mathcal{Y}}).

1.3.4.

For a proof that the two conditions in the above definition, as well as a couple of other conditions, are equivalent, see
[
DG15
, Proposition 3.1.2]
.

Definition 1.3.5
.

[
DG15
, Definition 3.8.2]

Now let
{\mathcal{Y}}
be a locally QCA algebraic stack. We say that a closed substack
i:{\mathcal{Z}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{Y}}
is
truncative
if for every quasicompact open substack
\overset{\circ}{{\mathcal{Y}}}\subset{\mathcal{Y}}
,

{\mathcal{Z}}\cap\overset{\circ}{{\mathcal{Y}}}\hookrightarrow\overset{\circ}{{\mathcal{Y}}}

is truncative. Similarly, we say that an open substack
j:U\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{Y}}
is
co-truncative
if for every quasicompact open substack
\overset{\circ}{{\mathcal{Y}}}\subset{\mathcal{Y}}
,

U\cap\overset{\circ}{{\mathcal{Y}}}\hookrightarrow\overset{\circ}{{\mathcal{Y}}}

is co-truncative.

1.3.6.

Suppose that
{\mathcal{Z}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{Y}}
is now only locally closed. Then we can factor the inclusion as

{\mathcal{Z}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{V}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{Y}}

where
{\mathcal{Z}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{V}}
is a closed embedding and
{\mathcal{V}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{Y}}
is an open embedding. Then we say that
{\mathcal{Z}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{Y}}
is truncative if
{\mathcal{Z}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{V}}
is.

1.3.7.

One can formally deduce that even in the case where
{\mathcal{Y}}
is only locally QCA,
j:U\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{Y}}
being cotruncative implies that
j^{!}
has a well-defined left adjoint, which we still denote by
j_{!}
.

Definition 1.3.8
.

[
DG15
, Definition 4.1.1]

A locally QCA algebraic stack
{\mathcal{Y}}
is
truncatable
if it can be covered by co-truncative quasicompact open substacks. Alternatively,
{\mathcal{Y}}
is truncatable if
\operatorname{CTrnk}({\mathcal{Y}})
, the subposet of
\operatorname{Open}({\mathcal{Y}})
consisting of the co-truncative quasicompact open substacks, is cofinal in
\operatorname{Open}({\mathcal{Y}})
.

1.3.9.

The discussion in subsubsection
1.3.2
implies that

Proposition 1.3.10
.

Let
{\mathcal{Y}}
be a truncatable locally QCA algebraic stack. Then
\operatorname{D-mod}({\mathcal{Y}})
is compactly generated.

1.3.11.

One might object that for
U\in\operatorname{CTrnk}({\mathcal{Y}})
, we only have that
j_{!}
is defined for
j:U\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{Y}}
, whereas in the discussion in subsubsection
1.3.2
, we needed
j_{i,j!}
to be defined for
j_{i,j}:U_{i}\hookrightarrow U_{j}
. However, note that we have a pullback square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{Y}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j_{12}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j_{1}}

and by base change for
\operatorname{D-mod}
, we have that
j_{1,2}^{!}=j_{1}^{!}j_{2*}
, so

j_{1,2!}=j_{2}^{*}j_{1!}.

1.4.
Contractive Substacks

1.4.1.

In this subsection, we give a sufficient condition of a geometric nature for a substack to be truncative. Consider, as in
[
DG15
, 5.1.1]
, the following setup.

Definition 1.4.2
.

Consider
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
as a monoid under multiplication, and let
W
be an
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
-scheme. Let
p:W\to S
be an
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
-equivariant affine morphism of schemes, where the action of
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
on
S
is trivial. Let
\iota:S\to W
be a section of
p
such that the composition

W\to S\to W

corresponds to the action of
0\in{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
. Let us denote
{\mathcal{Y}}:=W/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
and
{\mathcal{Z}}:=S/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}=S\times\operatorname{pt}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
. We have maps

i:{\mathcal{Z}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{Y}}

and

\pi:{\mathcal{Y}}\to{\mathcal{Z}}

obtained from
\iota
and
p
respectively. We will call a closed substack of the form of
i:{\mathcal{Z}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{Y}}

globally contractive
.

1.4.3.

The contraction principle (see
[
DG15
, Proposition 5.3.2]
) says that in the above situation, we have an adjunction

\pi_{*}:\operatorname{D-mod}(W/{\mathbb{G}}_{m})\rightleftarrows\operatorname{D-mod}(S/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}):i_{*}

so we can identify
i^{*}
with
\pi_{*}
, which is well-defined. In other words, a globally contractive substack is automatically truncative. We can generalize this notion slightly and define

Definition 1.4.4
.

[
DG15
, 5.2.1]

A locally closed substack
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{Y}}^{\prime}
is called
contractive
if there exists a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{Z}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{Y}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{Y}}^{\prime}}

such that

(i)

{\mathcal{Z}}\to{\mathcal{Y}}
is globally contractive,

(ii)

the morphism
{\mathcal{Z}}\to{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime}\underset{{\mathcal{Y}}^{\prime}}{\times}{\mathcal{Y}}
is an open embedding,

(iii)

{\mathcal{Z}}\to{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime}
is surjective, and

(iv)

{\mathcal{Y}}\to{\mathcal{Y}}^{\prime}
is smooth.

1.4.5.

If we have a commutative diagram as in the above definition, except the top arrow is only contractive instead of globally contractive, then we can still conclude that
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{Y}}^{\prime}
is contractive.

Proposition 1.4.6
.

[
DG15
, Corollary 5.2.3]

If a locally closed substack is contractive, then it is truncative.

1.4.7.

We finish this subsection by giving a convenient way to show that a given locally closed substack is contractive.

Proposition 1.4.8
.

[
DG15
, Lemma 5.4.3]

Let
\pi:{\mathcal{W}}\to{\mathcal{S}}
be an affine schematic morphism of stacks, such that
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
acts on
{\mathcal{W}}
by
{\mathcal{S}}
-endomorphisms. Suppose that

(i)

the
{\mathcal{S}}
-endomorphism of
{\mathcal{W}}
corresponding to
0\in{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
factors as
i\circ\pi
for some section
i:{\mathcal{S}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{W}}
of
\pi
and

(ii)

the
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-action on
{\mathcal{W}}
is trivial over a point (however, this does not imply that the
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-action on
{\mathcal{W}}
is trivial over
{\mathcal{S}}
).

Then
i:{\mathcal{S}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{W}}
is contractive.

1.5.
Stratification of
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}

1.5.1.

In this subsection, we will record some important facts about (but not define) the Harder-Narasimhan stratification of
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
, as well as some related combinatorics. The Harder-Narasimhan stratification of
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
is due to Harder and Narasimhan for
G=\operatorname{GL}_{n}
and was defined by Schieder in
[
Sch15
]
for an arbitrary reductive group
G
.

1.5.2.

There are quasicompact locally closed substacks

\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\lambda)}\subset\operatorname{Bun}_{G}

called the
Harder-Narasimhan Strata
, indexed over
\lambda\in\Lambda_{G}^{+,{\mathbb{Q}}}
, the rational dominant coweights of
G
. A definition is given in
[
DG15
, Theorem 7.4.3]
. Given a subset
S\subset\Lambda_{G}^{+,{\mathbb{Q}}}
, we will denote

\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(S)}:=\underset{\lambda\in S}{\bigcup}\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\lambda)}.

1.5.3.

The
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\lambda)}
form a stratification of
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
. In other words, they are disjoint and their union is all of
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
. See
[
DG15
, Theorem 7.4.3]
for details.

1.5.4.

The subset
{\mathcal{A}}\subset\Lambda_{G}^{+,{\mathbb{Q}}}
on which
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\lambda)}
is nonempty is discrete. In particular, given
\theta\in\Lambda_{G}^{+,{\mathbb{Q}}}
, the subset

\{\lambda\in{\mathcal{A}}\mid\lambda\leq\theta\}

is finite, so

\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\leq\theta)}:=\underset{\lambda\leq\theta}{\bigcup}\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\lambda)}

is quasicompact.

1.5.5.

The topology of the Harder-Narasimhan Stratification is described in
[
DG15
, Corollary 7.4.11]
. The punchline is that for
\lambda\leq\lambda^{\prime}
,
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\lambda^{\prime}}
is contained in the closure of
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\lambda}
. Combined with the discreteness of
{\mathcal{A}}
above, this implies that
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\leq\theta)}
is an open substack of
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
.

1.5.6.

Now we shift our focus to
\Lambda_{G}^{+,{\mathbb{Q}}}
itself. First, let us note that for any standard parabolic
P
with Levi
M
, we can consider

\Lambda_{G,P}^{++,{\mathbb{Q}}}:=\{\lambda\in\Lambda_{G}^{+,{\mathbb{Q}}}\mid\langle\lambda,\check{\alpha}_{i}\rangle=0\ \forall i\in\Gamma_{M},\langle\lambda,\check{\alpha}_{i}\rangle>0\ \forall i\notin\Gamma_{M}\}

(Here and in the sequel,
\Gamma_{H}
for a reductive group
H
will denote the set of nodes of the Dynkin diagram of
H
. We will identify it with the set of simple roots of
H
and with the set of simple coroots of
H
.)

We call a coweight
\lambda\in\Lambda_{G,P}^{++,{\mathbb{Q}}}

P
-regular
.

Definition 1.5.7
.

[
DG15
, Definition 8.2.2]

A subset
S\subset\Lambda_{G}^{+,{\mathbb{Q}}}
is
P
-admissible
if

(1)

there exists some
P
-regular
\mu
such that

S\subset\mu+\underset{i\in\Gamma_{M}}{\Sigma}{\mathbb{Q}}_{\geq 0}\alpha_{i},

(2)

if
\lambda_{1}\in S
and
\lambda_{2}\in\Lambda_{G}^{+,{\mathbb{Q}}}
, then
\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\in\underset{i\in\Gamma_{M}}{\Sigma}{\mathbb{Q}}_{\geq 0}\alpha_{i}
implies that
\lambda_{2}\in S
, and

(3)

for all
\lambda\in S
,
\langle\lambda,\check{\alpha}_{i}\rangle>0
whenever
i\notin\Gamma_{M}
.

Furthermore, we will say that
S
is
contractively
P
-admissible
if
S
is
P
-admissible and for all
\lambda\in S
,

\langle\lambda,\check{\alpha}_{i}\rangle>2g-2

whenever
i\notin\Gamma_{M}
.

1.6.
Truncatability of
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}

1.6.1.

In this subsection we will finally conclude that
\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
is compactly generated. We will do this by writing
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
as a union of quasicompact co-truncative open substacks, and we will see that the open substacks are co-truncative by observing that their complements are contractive, and therefore truncative.

Proposition 1.6.2
.

[
DG15
, Theorem 9.1.2]

The open substacks

\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\leq\theta)}=\underset{\lambda\leq\theta}{\bigcup}\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\lambda)}

are co-truncative whenever

\langle\theta,\check{\alpha}_{i}\rangle\geq 2g-2

for all simple roots
\check{\alpha}_{i}\in\Gamma_{G}
.

Proposition 1.6.3
.

[
DG15
, Lemma 11.3.4]

Let
S\subset\Lambda_{G}^{+,{\mathbb{Q}}}
be a contractively
P
-admissible subset. Then we have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Bun}_{M}^{(S)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Bun}_{P^{-}}^{(S)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Bun}_{P}^{(S)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Bun}_{G}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota^{(S)}_{P^{-}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota^{(S)}_{P}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{(S)}_{P^{-}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{(S)}_{P}}

such that

(a)

\operatorname{Bun}_{M}^{(S)}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{P}^{(S)}\underset{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}}{\times}\operatorname{Bun}_{P^{-}}^{(S)}
is an open embedding.

(b)

\operatorname{Bun}_{M}^{(S)}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{P}^{(S)}
is surjective.

(c)

\operatorname{Bun}_{P^{-}}^{(S)}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
is smooth.

(d)

\operatorname{Bun}_{M}^{(S)}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{P^{-}}^{(S)}
is a section of the affine schematic map
\operatorname{Bun}_{P^{-}}^{(S)}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{M}^{(S)}
, and therefore it is a closed embedding.

(e)

\operatorname{Bun}_{P}^{(S)}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
is an isomorphism onto
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(S)}\subset\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
.

1.6.4.

[
DG15
, Proposition 11.3.7]

Furthermore, there exists an action of
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
on
\operatorname{Bun}_{P^{-}}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{M}
that satisfies the assumptions of Proposition
1.4.8
, and this action preserves
\operatorname{Bun}_{P^{-}}^{(S)}
. For the construction of this action, see
[
DG15
, 11.2]
. This implies that
\operatorname{Bun}_{M}^{(S)}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{P^{-}}^{(S)}
is contractive, and stability properties of contractiveness imply that
\operatorname{Bun}_{P}^{(S)}=\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(S)}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
is also contractive.

1.6.5.

We want to see that
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\leq\theta)}
is co-truncative in
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
whenever
\theta
is “deep enough” in every direction as in the statement of Proposition
1.6.2
. In other words, we want to check that
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\leq\theta)}\cap\overset{\circ}{{\mathcal{Y}}}
is co-truncative in
\overset{\circ}{{\mathcal{Y}}}
for all quasicompact open substacks
\overset{\circ}{{\mathcal{Y}}}
of
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
. However, it actually suffices to check this for a family of quasicompact open substacks that covers
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
, and a convenient such family is

\{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\leq\theta^{\prime})}\}_{\theta^{\prime}\geq\theta}.

1.6.6.

Now we observe that

\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\leq\theta^{\prime})}\setminus\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\leq\theta)}=\underset{\lambda\nleq\theta,\lambda\leq\theta^{\prime}}{\bigcup}\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\lambda)}

and we write this as a finite union of
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(S)}
for contractively
P
-admissible subsets
S\subset\Lambda_{G}^{+,{\mathbb{Q}}}
(where
P
is allowed to vary).

1.6.7.

[
DG15
, 9.3.2]

Let

(\theta,\theta^{\prime}]=\{\lambda\in\Lambda_{G}^{+,{\mathbb{Q}}}\mid\lambda\nleq\theta,\lambda\leq\theta^{\prime}\}.

For each
\lambda\in(\theta,\theta^{\prime}]
we will describe how to produce a parabolic subgroup
P_{\lambda}
and a contractively
P_{\lambda}
-admissible subset
S_{\lambda}\subset(\theta,\theta^{\prime}]
containing
\lambda
.

1.6.8.

Let
P_{\lambda}
be the parabolic subgroup whose Levi
M_{\lambda}
corresponds to the subset

\{i\in\Gamma_{G}\mid\langle\lambda,\check{\alpha}_{i}\rangle\leq 2g-2\}.

Now we take
S_{\lambda}
to be the contractively
P_{\lambda}
-admissible subset

\{\mu\in\Lambda_{G}^{+,{\mathbb{Q}}}\mid\lambda-\mu\in\underset{i\in\Gamma_{M_{\lambda}}}{\Sigma}{\mathbb{Q}}_{\geq 0}\alpha_{i}\}.

1.6.9.

Note that
{\mathcal{A}}\cap(\theta,\theta^{\prime}]
is finite, so

\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\leq\theta^{\prime})}\setminus\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\leq\theta}=\underset{\lambda\in{\mathcal{A}}\cap(\theta,\theta^{\prime}]}{\bigcup}\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(S_{\lambda})}

is a finite union of truncative substacks. Since finite unions of truncative locally closed substacks are truncative, this is sufficient to conclude Proposition
1.6.2
.

1.6.10.

Clearly the
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\leq\theta)}
cover
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
, so Proposition
1.6.2
implies that

Proposition 1.6.11
.

\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
is truncatable. Since
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
is locally QCA, by Proposition
1.3.10
,
\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
is compactly generated.

2.
The Case
G=\operatorname{SL}_{2}

In this section, we will examine the case of
G=\operatorname{SL}_{2}
. In both the unramified and Iwahori ramified versions, most of the conceptual ideas of the proof of compact generation can be seen in this case.

2.1.
Compact Generation of
\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{\operatorname{SL}_{2}})

2.1.1.

In this subsection, we will reproduce the discussion from
[
DG15
, Section 6]
that describes the argument that
\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
is compactly generated for
G=\operatorname{SL}_{2}
.

2.1.2.

The locally QCA algebraic stack
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
represents principal
\operatorname{SL}_{2}
-bundles on
X
, i.e. rank
2
vector bundles
{\mathcal{M}}
with trivial determinant.

2.1.3.

\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
is an increasing union of the quasicompact open substacks
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\leq m)}
.
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\leq m)}
represents
\operatorname{SL}_{2}
-bundles
{\mathcal{M}}
such that every line subbundle of
{\mathcal{M}}
has degree at most
m
.

2.1.4.

To see that the
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\leq m)}
are co-truncative for
m\geq\operatorname{max}(g-1,0)
, we want to show that
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(n)}=\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\leq n)}\setminus\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\leq n-1)}
is truncative for
n>\operatorname{max}(g-1,0)
.

2.1.5.

Now consider the stack
\operatorname{Bun}_{B}
that classifies short exact sequences

0\to{\mathcal{L}}\to{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}\to 0

and the substacks
\operatorname{Bun}_{B}^{n}
that classify such short exact sequences with
\operatorname{deg}({\mathcal{L}})=n
.

2.1.6.

We have a fiber square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Bun}_{T}^{n}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Bun}_{B}^{-n}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Bun}_{B}^{n}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\leq n)}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{-}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{-}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

where the stack
\operatorname{Bun}_{T}
classifies line bundles
{\mathcal{L}}
on
X
, and the substacks
\operatorname{Bun}_{T}^{n}
classify degree
n
line bundles, and the maps
i
and
i^{-}
sends
{\mathcal{L}}
to

0\to{\mathcal{L}}\to{\mathcal{L}}\oplus{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}\to{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}\to 0

and

0\to{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}\to{\mathcal{L}}\oplus{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}\to{\mathcal{L}}\to 0

respectively.

2.1.7.

The map
p
is an isomorphism onto the substack
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(n)}\subset\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\leq n)}
essentially because an
\operatorname{SL}_{2}
-bundle can have at most one line subbundle of degree
n
, since if
{\mathcal{L}},{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}
are two such subbundles, we obtain a map

{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}\in\operatorname{Hom}({\mathcal{L}}^{\prime},{\mathcal{L}}^{-1})=\operatorname{H}^{0}(X,{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime-1}\otimes{\mathcal{L}}^{-1})=0

as
\operatorname{deg}({\mathcal{L}}^{\prime-1}\otimes{\mathcal{L}}^{-1})=-2n<0
(and vice versa), so
{\mathcal{L}}={\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}
.

2.1.8.

The map
i
is a surjection because every
\operatorname{SL}_{2}
-bundle with a line subbundle of degree
n
decomposes as a direct sum, since
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}({\mathcal{L}}^{-1},{\mathcal{L}})=\operatorname{H}^{1}(X,{\mathcal{L}}^{\otimes 2})=0
, as
\operatorname{deg}({\mathcal{L}}^{\otimes 2})=2n>2g-2
.

2.1.9.

The map
p^{-}
is smooth because the cofiber of the differential of
p^{-}
at a point

0\to{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}\to{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{L}}\to 0

is
\operatorname{H}^{1}(X,{\mathcal{L}}^{\otimes 2})=0
.

2.1.10.

Finally, here is a description of the map
\mathfrak{i}^{-}
. There is a vector bundle
{\mathcal{V}}
over
\operatorname{Bun}_{T}^{n}
with fiber over
{\mathcal{L}}\in\operatorname{Bun}_{T}^{n}
equal to
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}({\mathcal{L}},{\mathcal{L}}^{-1})=\operatorname{H}^{1}(X,{\mathcal{L}}^{\otimes-2})
. Now let
\operatorname{Pic}^{n}
denote the scheme that classifies degree
n
line bundles on
X
together with a trivialization at some fixed point
x\in X
. Now consider the map

s:\operatorname{Pic}^{n}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{T}^{n}

given by forgetting the choice of realization. This realizes
\operatorname{Pic}^{n}
as a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
torsor over
\operatorname{Bun}_{T}^{n}
.
Pulling the vector bundle
{\mathcal{V}}
back along
s
yields a vector bundle over
Pic^{n}
. Taking the zero section

\operatorname{Pic}^{n}\to s^{*}{\mathcal{V}}

and quotienting by
\mathbb{G}_{m}

\operatorname{Pic}^{n}/\mathbb{G}_{m}\to s^{*}{\mathcal{V}}/\mathbb{G}_{m}

identifies with the map

i^{-}:\operatorname{Bun}_{T}^{n}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{B}^{-n}

so
i^{-}
is contractive.

2.2.
Compact Generation of
\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{\operatorname{SL}_{2}}^{\operatorname{I}})

2.2.1.

Now we move on to consider the
G=\operatorname{SL}_{2}
case in the Iwahori ramified setting.

2.2.2.

The locally QCA algebraic stack
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I}}
represents pairs
({\mathcal{M}},L)
where
{\mathcal{M}}
is a principal
\operatorname{SL}_{2}
-bundle on
X
, and
L
is a one-dimensional subspace of the two-dimensional vector space
x^{*}{\mathcal{M}}
. There is of course a map
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I}}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
given by forgetting
L
.

2.2.3.

The stack
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I}}
is an increasing union of the quasicompact open substacks
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I},(\leq m)}
, which is the base change of
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\leq m)}
along
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I}}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
.

2.2.4.

To see that the
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I},(\leq m)}
are co-truncative for
m\geq\operatorname{max}(g-1,0)
, we want to show that
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I},(n)}=\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I},(\leq n)}\setminus\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I},(\leq n-1)}
is truncative for
n>\operatorname{max}(g-1,0)
.

2.2.5.

The stack
\operatorname{Bun}_{B}^{\operatorname{I}}
classifies pairs

(0\to{\mathcal{L}}\to{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}\to 0,L),

and for
n>\operatorname{max}(g-1,0)

\operatorname{Bun}_{B}^{\operatorname{I},n}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I},(n)}

is an isomorphism.

2.2.6.

The stack
\operatorname{Bun}_{B}^{\operatorname{I}}
has two natural strata. For a given point of
\operatorname{Bun}_{B}^{\operatorname{I}}
, we can ask whether or not
L
is equal to the fiber
x^{*}{\mathcal{L}}\subset x^{*}{\mathcal{M}}
. The closed strata, denoted by
\prescript{\backslash}{}{\operatorname{Bun}_{B}^{\operatorname{I}}}
, can be identified with
\operatorname{Bun}_{B}
, and the open strata we will denote by
\prescript{\circ}{}{\operatorname{Bun}_{B}^{\operatorname{I}}}
.

2.2.7.

First, let us consider the open strata. There is a fiber square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Bun}_{T}^{n}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Bun}_{B}^{-n}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\prescript{\circ}{}{\operatorname{Bun}_{B}^{\operatorname{I},n}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I},(\leq n)}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{-}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{-}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

where
i
sends
{\mathcal{L}}
to

(0\to{\mathcal{L}}\to{\mathcal{L}}\oplus{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}\to{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}\to 0,x^{*}{\mathcal{L}}^{-1})\in\prescript{\circ}{}{\operatorname{Bun}_{B}^{\operatorname{I},n}}.

2.2.8.

We claim that
i
is surjective. Let
(0\to{\mathcal{L}}\to{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}\to 0,L)
be a point of
\prescript{\circ}{}{\operatorname{Bun}_{B}^{\operatorname{I},n}}

{\mathcal{M}}
splits as
{\mathcal{L}}\oplus{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}
, so we may consider
L\in x^{*}{\mathcal{L}}\oplus x^{*}{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}
. Let

be a basis of
x^{*}{\mathcal{M}}
.
Since
L\neq x^{*}{\mathcal{L}}
, there exists some vector space automorphism

\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(x^{*}{\mathcal{M}})

of the form

\phi(v)=v,\phi(w)=cv+w

that sends
L
to
x^{*}{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}
.

We will now see that
\phi
can be lifted to an automorphism of
{\mathcal{M}}
. We have

\operatorname{End}({\mathcal{M}})=\operatorname{Hom}({\mathcal{L}},{\mathcal{L}})\oplus\operatorname{Hom}({\mathcal{L}}^{-1},{\mathcal{L}})\oplus\operatorname{Hom}({\mathcal{L}},{\mathcal{L}}^{-1})\oplus\operatorname{Hom}({\mathcal{L}}^{-1},{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}).

\operatorname{Hom}({\mathcal{L}},{\mathcal{L}}^{-1})=\operatorname{H}^{0}(X,{\mathcal{L}}^{\otimes-2})=0
since
\operatorname{deg}({\mathcal{L}}^{\otimes-2})<0
. On the other hand,
\operatorname{Hom}({\mathcal{L}}^{-1},{\mathcal{L}})=\operatorname{H}^{0}(X,{\mathcal{L}}^{\otimes 2})
, and we know that since
\operatorname{deg}({\mathcal{L}}^{\otimes 2})>2g-1
, there exists a global section
f
that does not vanish at
x\in X
. Without loss of generality we may normalize it so that its value at

is
c
. Now the automorphism of
{\mathcal{M}}
given by

\Phi=(\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{L}}},f,0,\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}})\in\operatorname{Hom}({\mathcal{L}},{\mathcal{L}})\oplus\operatorname{Hom}({\mathcal{L}}^{-1},{\mathcal{L}})\oplus\operatorname{Hom}({\mathcal{L}},{\mathcal{L}}^{-1})\oplus\operatorname{Hom}({\mathcal{L}}^{-1},{\mathcal{L}}^{-1})

restricts to
\phi
.

Thus
\Phi
yields an isomorphism

(0\to{\mathcal{L}}\to{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}\to 0,L)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{\sim}}(0\to{\mathcal{L}}\to{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}\to 0,x^{*}{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}),

the latter of which is clearly in the image of
i
.

2.2.9.

The map
p^{-}
is smooth, because the cofiber of the differential of
p^{-}
at a point

is
\operatorname{H}^{1}(X,{\mathcal{L}}^{\otimes 2}(-x))=0
since
\operatorname{deg}({\mathcal{L}}^{\otimes 2}(-x))\geq 2g-1
.

2.2.10.

The map
i^{-}
is the same map as in the unramified case, and we have already seen that it is contractive.

2.2.11.

There is also a fiber square for the closed strata:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Bun}_{T}^{n}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\prescript{\circ}{}{\operatorname{Bun}_{B}^{\operatorname{I},-n}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Bun}_{B}^{n}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I},(\leq n)}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{-}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{-}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

Here

is the same map as in the unramified case, so we know that it is surjective.

2.2.13.

The map

\prescript{\circ}{}{\operatorname{Bun}_{B}^{\operatorname{I},-n}}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{B}^{\operatorname{I},-n}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I},(\leq n)}.

The first map is an open embedding, and the second map is a base change of the smooth map
\operatorname{Bun}_{B}^{-n}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\leq n)}
, so we conclude that

is smooth.

2.2.14.

Here is a description of the map
i^{-}
. There is a vector bundle
{\mathcal{V}}
over
\operatorname{Bun}_{T}^{n}
with fiber over
{\mathcal{L}}\in\operatorname{Bun}_{T}^{n}
equal to
\operatorname{H}^{1}(X,{\mathcal{L}}^{\otimes-2}(-x))
. Again, we can take the pullback of
{\mathcal{V}}
along
s:\operatorname{Pic}^{n}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{T}^{n}
. This time taking the zero section

\operatorname{Pic}^{n}\to s^{*}{\mathcal{V}}

and quotienting by

\operatorname{Pic}^{n}/\mathbb{G}_{m}\to s^{*}{\mathcal{V}}/\mathbb{G}_{m}

identifies with

so the latter is contractive.

3.
Compact Generation of
\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I}})

In this section, we will prove our main result, i.e. that
\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I}})
is compactly generated.

3.1.
Reduction Steps

3.1.1.

Like in the unramified case, our strategy will be to show that
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I}}
is truncatable by writing
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I}}
as a union of quasicompact co-truncative open substacks. Since
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I}}
is locally QCA, we may then invoke Proposition
1.3.10
to conclude that
\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I}})
is compactly generated.

3.1.2.

From the beginning, we fix a point
x\in X
(where the Iwahori level structure will live), and this induces a map

\operatorname{Bun}_{G}=\operatorname{Maps}(X,\operatorname{pt}/G)\to\operatorname{Maps}(\operatorname{pt},\operatorname{pt}/G)=\operatorname{pt}/G.

Using this map, we define

Definition 3.1.3
.

The
moduli stack of principal
G
-bundles on
X
with Iwahori level structure at
x\in X
is given by the fiber product

\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I}}:=\operatorname{Bun}_{G}\underset{\operatorname{pt}/G}{\times}\operatorname{pt}/B.

Note that there is a canonical map

\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I}}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{G}

given by forgetting the Iwahori level structure.

3.1.4.

Denote for
\lambda\in\Lambda_{G}^{+,{\mathbb{Q}}}

\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I},(\lambda)}:=\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\lambda)}\underset{\operatorname{pt}/G}{\times}\operatorname{pt}/B=\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\lambda)}\underset{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}}{\times}\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I}}

and for
S\subset\Lambda_{G}^{+,{\mathbb{Q}}}

\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I},(S)}:=\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(S)}\underset{\operatorname{pt}/G}{\times}\operatorname{pt}/B=\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(S)}\underset{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}}{\times}\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I}}.

3.1.5.

Since the map

\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I}}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{G}

is finite type, the open substacks

\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I},(\leq\theta)}:=\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\leq\theta)}\underset{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}}{\times}\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I}}

are quasicompact and we have

\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I}}=\underset{\theta\in\Lambda_{G}^{+,{\mathbb{Q}}}}{\bigcup}\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I},(\leq\theta)}.

Therefore, similarly to the non-Iwahori level structure case, it is sufficient to show that

Proposition 3.1.6
.

\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I},(\leq\theta)}
is co-truncative whenever

\langle\theta,\check{\alpha}_{i}\rangle\geq 2g-1

for all simple roots
\check{\alpha}_{i}\in\Gamma_{G}
.

3.1.7.

Note that the bound has changed from
2g-2
to
2g-1
. The reason why will become clear in the sequel. For now, we define

Definition 3.1.8
.

A
P
-admissible subset
S\subset\Lambda_{G}^{+,{\mathbb{Q}}}
is Iwahori-contractively
P
-admissible if it is
P
-admissible and for all
\lambda\in S
,

\langle\lambda,\check{\alpha}_{i}\rangle>2g-1

whenever
i\notin\Gamma_{M}
.

3.1.9.

In order to show that
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I},(\leq\theta)}
is co-truncative, we note that for
\theta^{\prime}\geq\theta
, as in Section
1.6.6
, we can write

\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I},(\leq\theta^{\prime})}\setminus\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I},(\leq\theta)}

as a finite union of

\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I},(S)}

for Iwahori-contractively
P
-admissible subsets
S\subset\Lambda_{G}^{+,{\mathbb{Q}}}
(where
P
is allowed to vary).

3.1.10.

Now we come to the first point of divergence from the proof of the unramified case. We will further stratify the substacks
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I},(S)}
above using
relative position
.

3.1.11.

Let
S
be Iwahori-contractively
P
-admissible as above. Just as we have defined
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I}}
, we define

\operatorname{Bun}_{P}^{\operatorname{I}}:=\operatorname{Bun}_{P}\underset{\operatorname{pt}/G}{\times}\operatorname{pt}/B

and similarly we denote

\operatorname{Bun}_{P}^{\operatorname{I},(S)}:=\operatorname{Bun}_{P}^{(S)}\underset{\operatorname{pt}/G}{\times}\operatorname{pt}/B.

Observe that we have a map

\operatorname{Bun}_{P}^{\operatorname{I},(S)}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I}}

obtained by base change from
\operatorname{Bun}_{P}^{(S)}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
. This map induces an isomorphism

\operatorname{Bun}_{P}^{\operatorname{I},(S)}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{\sim}}\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I},(S)}.

3.1.12.

The stack

\operatorname{pt}/P\underset{\operatorname{pt}/G}{\times}\operatorname{pt}/B=P\backslash G/B

is stratified by locally closed substacks

P\backslash PwB/B\subset P\backslash G/B

where
w
ranges over
W_{M}\backslash W
. The map

\operatorname{Bun}_{P}^{\operatorname{I},(S)}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{P}^{\operatorname{I}}\to\operatorname{pt}/P\underset{\operatorname{pt}/G}{\times}\operatorname{pt}/B

allows us to define

Definition 3.1.13
.

The
piece of
\operatorname{Bun}_{P}^{\operatorname{I},(S)}
having relative position
w
is given by the fiber product

\prescript{w}{}{\operatorname{Bun}_{P}^{\operatorname{I},(S)}}=\operatorname{Bun}_{P}^{\operatorname{I},(S)}\underset{P\backslash G/B}{\times}P\backslash PwB/B.

Using the isomorphism
\operatorname{Bun}_{P}^{\operatorname{I},(S)}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{\sim}}\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I},(S)}
, we also define
\prescript{w}{}{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I},(S)}}
as the image of
\prescript{w}{}{\operatorname{Bun}_{P}^{\operatorname{I},(S)}}
.

3.1.14.

Since finite unions of truncative locally closed substacks are truncative we have reduced Proposition
3.1.6
to

Proposition 3.1.15
.

The locally closed substack

\prescript{w}{}{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I},(S)}}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I}}

is contractive, and therefore truncative.

3.2.

3.2.1.

The rest of this section will be dedicated to proving Proposition

-admissible subset

3.3.
Global Generation of
{\mathfrak{n}}(P)_{{\mathcal{F}}_{M}}

3.3.1.

In this subsection, we will show that
{\mathfrak{n}}(P)_{{\mathcal{F}}_{M}}
is globally generated given some conditions on
{\mathcal{F}}_{M}\in\operatorname{Bun}_{M}
. First let us review the notion of global generation.

Definition 3.3.2
.

A vector bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
on
X
is said to be
globally generated
if

\operatorname{H}^{0}(X,{\mathcal{E}})\to{\mathcal{E}}_{x}

is surjective for all
x\in X
.

3.3.3.

Here are some useful facts for checking that a vector bundle on a curve is globally generated.

Lemma 3.3.4
.

The vector bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
is globally generated if and only if
\operatorname{H}^{1}(X,{\mathcal{E}}(-x))=0
for all
x\in X
.

Lemma 3.3.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
have some filtration

0={\mathcal{E}}_{0}\subset{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\subset...\subset{\mathcal{E}}_{m}={\mathcal{E}}

whose subquotients
{\mathcal{E}}_{i}/{\mathcal{E}}_{i-1}
are vector bundles, and let

\operatorname{gr}({\mathcal{E}})=\bigoplus{\mathcal{E}}_{i}/{\mathcal{E}}_{i-1}

be the associated graded of
{\mathcal{E}}
with respect to that filtration. If
\operatorname{H}^{1}(X,\operatorname{gr}({\mathcal{E}}))=0
, then
\operatorname{H}^{1}(X,{\mathcal{E}})=0
.

3.3.6.

The following now follows immediately from the preceding two lemmas.

Lemma 3.3.7
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be as above. If
\operatorname{H}^{1}(X,\operatorname{gr}({\mathcal{E}}))=0
and
\operatorname{gr}({\mathcal{E}})
is globally generated, then
{\mathcal{E}}
is globally generated.

3.3.8.

We will spend the rest of the subsection using the above basic facts about global generation to show that
{\mathfrak{n}}(P)_{{\mathcal{F}}_{M}}
is globally generated.

Lemma 3.3.9
.

Let
\tilde{G}
be a reductive group,
V
a finite dimensional
\tilde{G}
-representation on which
\operatorname{Z}_{0}(\tilde{G})
acts via
\check{\mu}
, and
{\mathcal{F}}_{\tilde{G}}\in\operatorname{Bun}_{\tilde{G}}^{\lambda,ss}
. If

\langle\lambda,\check{\mu}\rangle>2g-1,

then

V_{{\mathcal{F}}_{\tilde{G}}}
is globally generated.

Proof.

We note that by Serre duality, we have

\operatorname{H}^{1}(X,V_{{\mathcal{F}}_{\tilde{G}}}(-x))=\operatorname{H}^{0}(X,(V^{*})_{{\mathcal{F}}_{\tilde{G}}}(x)\otimes\omega_{X})^{*}.

By
[
DG15
, Lemma 10.3.2]
, every line subbundle of
(V^{*})_{{\mathcal{F}}_{\tilde{G}}}
has degree
\leq-\langle\lambda,\check{\mu}\rangle<-(2g-1)
, so every line subbundle of
(V^{*})_{{\mathcal{F}}_{\tilde{G}}}(x)\otimes\omega_{X}
has degree
<0
. Thus

\operatorname{H}^{0}(X,(V^{*})_{{\mathcal{F}}_{\tilde{G}}}(x)\otimes\omega_{X})=0

and we are done by Lemma
3.3.4
.
∎

Proposition 3.3.10
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}_{M}\in\operatorname{Bun}_{M}^{(\lambda)}
be such that
\langle\lambda,\check{\alpha}_{i}\rangle>2g-1
whenever
i\notin\Gamma_{M}
. Then the associated vector bundle
{\mathfrak{n}}(P)_{{\mathcal{F}}_{M}}
has
\operatorname{H}^{1}
equal to
0
and is globally generated.

Proof.

As in the proof of
[
DG15
, Proposition 10.4.5]
, let
P_{\lambda}
be the parabolic of
M
corresponding to the subset

\{i\in\Gamma_{M}\mid\langle\lambda,\check{\alpha}_{i}\rangle=0\}\subset\Gamma_{M}

and let
M_{\lambda}
be its levi. Since
{\mathcal{F}}_{M}\in\operatorname{Bun}_{M}^{(\lambda)}
, by
[
DG15
, Theorem 7.4.3]

{\mathcal{F}}_{M}
has a semistable reduction to
P_{\lambda}
of degree
\lambda
which we will denote by
{\mathcal{F}}_{P_{\lambda}}
. Denote by
{\mathcal{F}}_{M_{\lambda}}
the induced principal
M_{\lambda}
-bundle along
P_{\lambda}\to M_{\lambda}
. Then
{\mathfrak{n}}(P)_{{\mathcal{F}}_{M_{\lambda}}}
is an associated graded of
{\mathfrak{n}}(P)_{{\mathcal{F}}_{M}}={\mathfrak{n}}(P)_{{\mathcal{F}}_{P_{\lambda}}}
. It is shown in
loc.cit.
that
\operatorname{H}^{1}(X,{\mathfrak{n}}(P)_{{\mathcal{F}}_{M_{\lambda}}})=0
, so by Lemma
3.3.5
, we conclude that
\operatorname{H}^{1}(X,{\mathfrak{n}}(P)_{{\mathcal{F}}_{M}})=0
.

For global generation, by Lemma
3.3.7
, it suffices to show that
{\mathfrak{n}}(P)_{{\mathcal{F}}_{M_{\lambda}}}
is globally generated. Now observe that, as in
[
DG15
, Subsection 10.4.1]
,
{\mathfrak{n}}(P)
can be written as a direct sum of
M_{\lambda}
-representations

\bigoplus V_{M_{\lambda},\check{\alpha}}

where the direct sum is over positive roots
\check{\alpha}
of
G
such that there exists some
i\notin\Gamma_{M}
with
\operatorname{coeff}_{i}(\check{\alpha})>0
.
\operatorname{Z}_{0}(M_{\lambda})
acts on
V_{M_{\lambda},\check{\alpha}}
via
\check{\alpha}

3.8.

3.8.1.

Throughout the paper, we have considered
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{\operatorname{I}}
, the moduli stack of principal
G
-bundles on a curve
X
with Iwahori level structure at a single point
x\in X
. However, we could have also considered Iwahori level structure at
k
distinct points
x_{1},...,x_{k}\in X
. In this subsection, we will discuss what changes need to be made to the proof in order to show that the category of D-modules on the analogous moduli stack is compactly generated.

Definition 3.8.2
.

Let
x_{1},...,x_{k}\in X
be
k
distinct points. Then we define

\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\operatorname{I};x_{1},...,x_{k})}=\operatorname{Bun}_{G}\underset{(\operatorname{pt}/G)^{k}}{\times}(\operatorname{pt}/B)^{k}

where the map is

(x_{1}^{*},...,x_{k}^{*}):\operatorname{Bun}_{G}\to(\operatorname{pt}/G)^{k}.

Proposition 3.8.3
.

\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\operatorname{I};x_{1},...,x_{k})})
is compactly generated.

3.8.4.

It turns out that conceptually, the proof of Proposition
3.8
is identical to the proof of the single point case; the only difference is in the numerology. Just as the correct bound when going from the Iwahori ramification at no points case (i.e.
[
DG15
]
) to the single point case increased from
2g-2
to
2g-1
, the correct bound in the
k
points case will be
2g-2+k
.

3.8.5.

In other words, the proof consists of showing that the quasicompact open substacks

\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\operatorname{I};x_{1},...,x_{k}),(\leq\theta)}

are co-truncative for
\langle\theta,\check{\alpha}_{i}\rangle\geq 2g-2+k
. This is done by writing

\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\operatorname{I};x_{1},...,x_{k}),(\leq\theta^{\prime})}\setminus\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\operatorname{I};x_{1},...,x_{k}),(\leq\theta)}

as a finite union of
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\operatorname{I};x_{1},...,x_{k}),(S)}
for
k
-Iwahori contractively
P
-admissible subsets
S
(where
P
is allowed to vary), then stratifying further by

\prescript{(w_{1},...,w_{k})}{}{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\operatorname{I};x_{1},...,x_{k}),(S)}}=\operatorname{Bun}_{G}^{(\operatorname{I};x_{1},...,x_{k}),(S)}\underset{(P\backslash G/B)^{k}}{\times}(P\backslash Pw_{1}B/B\times...\times P\backslash Pw_{k}B/B).

3.8.6.

The reason for this boils down to the fact that instead of global generation of
{\mathfrak{n}}(P)_{{\mathcal{F}}_{M}}
, we want to have
k
-global generation of
{\mathfrak{n}}(P)_{{\mathcal{F}}_{M}}
; i.e. we want

\operatorname{H}^{0}(X,{\mathfrak{n}}(P)_{{\mathcal{F}}_{M}})\to\bigoplus_{j=1}^{k}({\mathfrak{n}}(P)_{{\mathcal{F}}_{M}})_{x_{j}}

to be surjective for any
k
points of
X
.

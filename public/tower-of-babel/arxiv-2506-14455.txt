Unified numerical analysis for thermoelastic diffusion and thermo-poroelasticity of thin plates
Neela Nataraj, Ricardo Ruiz-Baier, Aamir Yousuf

Abstract
We investigate a coupled hyperbolic-parabolic system modeling thermoelastic diffusion (resp. thermo-poroelasticity) in plates, consisting of a fourth-order hyperbolic partial differential equation for plate deflection and two second-order parabolic partial differential equations for the first moments of temperature and chemical potential (resp. pore pressure). The unique solvability of the system is established via Galerkin approach, and the additional regularity of the solution is obtained under appropriately strengthened data. For numerical approximation, we employ the Newmark method for time discretization of the hyperbolic term and a continuous interior penalty scheme for the spatial discretization of displacement. For the parabolic equations that represent the first moments of temperature and chemical potential (resp. pore pressure), we use the Crank–Nicolson method for time discretization and conforming finite elements for spatial discretization. The convergence of the fully discrete scheme with quasi-optimal rates in space and time is established. The numerical experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of the 2D Kirchhoff–Love plate model in capturing thermoelastic diffusion and thermo-poroelastic behavior in specific materials. We illustrate that as plate thickness decreases, the two-dimensional simulations closely approximate the results of three-dimensional problem. Finally, the numerical experiments also validate the theoretical rates of convergence.

Main contributions.

In this paper, we analyze the unique solvability and numerical approximation for an asymptotic model for
ted
and
tpe
plate models consisting of a coupled PDE system of one hyperbolic fourth-order PDE for the plate’s vertical deflection, and two second-order parabolic PDEs for the thickness-averaged (first moment) temperature distribution, and chemical potential/pore pressure. The unique solvability of the continuous formulation is based on the classical Galerkin approach (see, for example,
[

,
5
,
45
,
39
]

For the spatial discretization,

P_{1}
elements for temperature and chemical potential (or pore pressure) are employed. In terms of temporal discretization, we adopt Newmark’s scheme for the first hyperbolic equation and apply the Crank–Nicolson method for the remaining parabolic equations, ensuring quadratic convergence in time.
Following our recent work
[
43
]
, we utilize a modified Ritz projection for the analysis, based on the companion operator
[
18
]
. In conjunction with this, we employ the standard

-conforming Ritz projections for temperature and chemical potential/pore pressure to obtain the error estimates.

2
Well-posedness and regularity results

In this section, we establish the well-posedness of the problem through the finite Galerkin approach, which follows these steps: (i) construct a sequence of approximate solutions to the continuous problem, (ii) derive a priori bounds on these approximations based on the initial data, (iii) use a compactness argument to show the existence of a limit for a subsequence in the weak topology, and (iv) prove that this limit is the weak solution. After this, we also prove the additional regularity of continuous solution given the extra regularity conditions on given data, which is required in later sections for error analysis.

.

Let
{\rm HCT}(\mathcal{T})
denote the Hsieh–Clough–Tocher element.
There exists a linear mapping
J:{\rm M}(\mathcal{T})\to({\rm HCT}(\mathcal{T})+P_{8}(\mathcal{T}))\cap H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
such that any
w_{\rm M}\in{\rm M}(\mathcal{T})
satisfies

\displaystyle\text{(i) }Jw_{\rm M}(z)=w_{\rm M}(z)\quad\text{ for }z\in\mathcal{V},

\displaystyle\text{(ii) }\nabla({J}w_{\rm M})(z)=|\mathcal{T}(z)|^{-1}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}(z)}(\nabla w_{\rm M}|_{K})(z)\quad\text{ for }z\in\mathcal{V}(\Omega),

\displaystyle\text{(iii) }\fint_{e}\frac{\partial Jw_{\rm M}}{{\partial{\boldsymbol{n}}}}\,\mathrm{d}s=\fint_{e}\frac{\partial w_{\rm M}}{{\partial{\boldsymbol{n}}}}\,\mathrm{d}s\text{ for any }e\in\mathcal{E},

\displaystyle\text{(iv) }|\!|\!|w_{\rm M}-Jw_{\rm M}|\!|\!|_{\rm pw}\lesssim\min_{v\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}|\!|\!|w_{\rm M}-v|\!|\!|_{\rm pw},

\displaystyle\text{(v) }\|{v_{h}-Qv_{h}}\|_{H^{s}({\cal T})}\leq C_{1}h^{2-s}\min_{v\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}\lVert v-v_{h}\rVert_{h}\quad\text{ for }v_{h}\in V_{h},\ C_{1}>0,\text{ and }0\leq s\leq 2.

Here
Q=JI_{\rm M}
is a smoother operator defined from
V_{h}
to
H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
.

The error control associated with the fully discrete approximation employs Ritz projection operators defined from
H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
(resp.
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
into
V_{h}
(resp.
W_{h}
) for

(resp.
\theta\text{ and }p
). It should be noted that since

is not a subspace of

a_{h}({\cal R}_{h}w,v_{h})=(\nabla^{2}w,\nabla^{2}v_{h})\qquad\text{for all $v_{h}\in V_{h}$},

does not hold for
v_{h}\in V_{h}\subset H^{2}({\mathcal{T}})
for the nonstandard
C^{0}
IP scheme proposed herein.

Alternative approaches that define Ritz projections for nonstandard methods (see, e.g.,
[
22
,
29
]
for the fourth-order nonlinear parabolic extended Fisher–Kolmogorov equation) often require higher regularity
u\in H^{3}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
, which might not hold for non-convex domains. See the discussion in Section
2.2
for
non-convex polygons. Our recent work
[
43
]
addresses this issue by means of the
modified
Ritz projection (see Definition
3.2
below), which utilizes a smoother operator
Q:V_{h}\rightarrow H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
defined as
JI_{\rm M}
, where
J
(resp.
I_{\rm M}
) denotes the companion (resp. extended Morley interpolation) operator from Lemma
3.1
(resp. Lemma
3.1
).
The
modified Ritz projection

\mathcal{R}_{h}:H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow V_{h}
for the displacement variable is defined as follows:

(3.2)

a_{h}({\cal R}_{h}w,v_{h})=(\nabla^{2}w,\nabla^{2}Qv_{h})\qquad\text{ for all }v_{h}\in V_{h},\,w\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega).

Lemma 3.2

(Approximation properties for
{\cal R}_{h}

[
41
, Appendix]
)
.

Let
w\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)\cap H^{2+\sigma}(\Omega)
, where
\sigma\in(1/2,1]
, and let
\mathcal{R}_{h}w
be its Ritz projection defined in (

). Then, there exists a constant
C_{2}>0
such that

(3.3)

\|{w-\mathcal{R}_{h}w}\|+\|{\nabla(w-\mathcal{R}_{h}w)}\|+h^{\sigma}\lVert w-\mathcal{R}_{h}w\rVert_{h}\leq C_{2}h^{2\sigma}\lVert w\rVert_{H^{2+\sigma}(\Omega)}.

Next, we define the
H^{1}
-
conforming elliptic projection

\Pi_{h}\hskip-2.84544pt:H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow W_{h}

[
24
]
for the first moments of temperature and pressure as:

(3.4)

(\nabla\Pi_{h}\chi,\nabla\chi_{h})=(\nabla\chi,\nabla\chi_{h})\qquad\text{ for all }\chi_{h}\in W_{h}.

Lemma 3.3

(Approximation properties for
\Pi_{h}

[
24
, Theorem 32.15]
)
.

Let
\chi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap H^{1+\sigma}(\Omega)
for some
\sigma\in(1/2,1]
. Then, there exists a constant
C_{3}>0
such that

(3.5)

\|{\chi-\Pi_{h}\chi}\|+h^{\sigma}\|\nabla(\chi-\Pi_{h}\chi)\|\leq C_{3}h^{2\sigma}\|\chi\|_{H^{1+\sigma}(\Omega)}.

3.2
Fully discrete scheme

This subsection discusses a fully discrete scheme for (
2.6
). To the best of our knowledge, this is the first fully discrete scheme for a hyperbolic-parabolic coupled problem with explicit initial error estimates. In contrast with
[
40
]
, where the initial error is assumed to be bounded with the required convergence and the formulation begins from the second time step, our approach starts from the initial step and provides a general framework for defining the fully discrete formulation for any coupled hyperbolic-parabolic system.

For a positive integer
N
, consider the partition
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots<t_{N}=T
of the interval
[0,T]

with
t_{n}=n\Delta t
, and
\Delta t=T/N
being the time step.
For any function
\upsilon(\boldsymbol{x},t)
, the following notations are adopted:

\displaystyle\upsilon^{n}:=\upsilon(\boldsymbol{x},t_{n})=\upsilon(t_{n}),\quad\upsilon^{n+1/2}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\upsilon^{n+1}+\upsilon^{n}\right),\quad\upsilon^{n,1/4}:=\frac{1}{4}\left(\upsilon^{n+1}+2\upsilon^{n}+\upsilon^{n-1}\right)={\frac{1}{2}\left(v^{n+1/2}+v^{n-1/2}\right)},

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}_{t}\upsilon^{n+1/2}:=\frac{\upsilon^{n+1}-\upsilon^{n}}{\Delta t},\quad{\bar{\partial}}^{2}_{t}\upsilon^{n}:=\frac{\upsilon^{n+1}-2\upsilon^{n}+\upsilon^{n-1}}{(\Delta t)^{2}},\quad\delta_{t}\upsilon^{n}:=\frac{\upsilon^{n+1}-\upsilon^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}.

Let
(U^{n},\Theta^{n},P^{n})=(U(t_{n}),\Theta(t_{n}),P(t_{n}))
denote the approximation of the continuous solution
(u,\theta,p)
at time
t_{n}
. Considering the following approximation of the initial solution

(3.6)

(U^{0},\Theta^{0},P^{0})=({\mathcal{R}_{h}}u^{0},\Pi_{h}\theta^{0},\Pi_{h}p^{0}),

we compute
(U^{1},\Theta^{1},P^{1})\in V_{h}\times W_{h}\times W_{h}
by solving the following elliptic system for all
(v_{h},\psi_{h},q_{h})\in V_{h}\times W_{h}\times W_{h}

\displaystyle 2(\Delta t)^{-1}\big[(\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{1/2}-u^{*0},v_{h})+a_{0}(\nabla\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{1/2}-\nabla u^{*0},\nabla v_{h})\big]

(3.7a)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\;+d_{0}a_{h}(U^{1/2},v_{h})-\alpha(\nabla\Theta^{1/2},\nabla v_{h})-\beta(\nabla P^{1/2},\nabla v_{h})=(f^{1/2},v_{h}),

(3.7b)

\displaystyle a_{1}(\bar{\partial}_{t}{\Theta}^{1/2},\psi_{h})-\gamma\bar{\partial}_{t}{P^{1/2}},\psi_{h})+b_{1}(\Theta^{1/2},\psi_{h})+c_{1}(\nabla\Theta^{1/2},\nabla\psi_{h})+\alpha(\nabla\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{1/2},\nabla\psi_{h})=(\phi^{1/2},\psi_{h}),

(3.7c)

\displaystyle a_{2}(\bar{\partial}_{t}P^{1/2},q_{h})-\gamma(\bar{\partial}_{t}{\Theta}^{1/2},q_{h})+\kappa(\nabla P^{1/2},\nabla q_{h})+\beta(\nabla\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{1/2},\nabla q_{h})=(g^{1/2},q_{h}).

The solution is calculated at
t_{1}
using (
3.7a
)-(
3.7c
) in order to align the two numerical schemes, since the Newmark scheme (
3.8a
) requires solutions at
t_{0}
and
t_{1}
to compute the solution at
t_{2}
, while the Crank–Nicolson scheme begins its computation from
t_{1}
.
The idea of the discrete equation (
3.7a
) is based on the discretisation of biharmonic wave
[
43
]
for coupled system, and (
3.7b
)-(
3.7c
) are based on the Crank–Nicolson method to determine the solution at
t_{1}
. The construction of (
3.7
) guarantees quadratic convergence in time, implying that also the fully discrete scheme is quadratically convergent.

For
n=1,2,\cdots,N-1
, the fully discrete scheme consists in finding
(U^{n+1},\Theta^{n+1},P^{n+1})\in V_{h}\times W_{h}\times W_{h}
such that for all
(v_{h},\psi_{h},q_{h})\in V_{h}\times W_{h}\times W_{h}

(3.8a)

\displaystyle(\bar{\partial}_{t}^{2}U^{n},v_{h})+a_{0}(\nabla\bar{\partial}_{t}^{2}U^{n},\nabla v_{h})+d_{0}a_{h}(U^{n,1/4},v_{h})-\alpha(\nabla\Theta^{n,1/4},\nabla v_{h})-\beta(\nabla P^{n,1/4},\nabla v_{h})

\displaystyle=(f^{n,1/4},v_{h}),

\displaystyle a_{1}(\bar{\partial}_{t}{\Theta}^{n+1/2},\psi_{h})-\gamma(\bar{\partial}_{t}{P^{n+1/2}},\psi_{h})+b_{1}(\Theta^{n+1/2},\psi_{h})+c_{1}(\nabla\Theta^{n+1/2},\nabla\psi_{h})

(3.8b)

\displaystyle+\alpha(\nabla\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2},\nabla\psi_{h})

\displaystyle=(\phi^{n+1/2},\psi_{h}),

(3.8c)

\displaystyle a_{2}(\bar{\partial}_{t}P^{n+1/2},q_{h})-\gamma(\bar{\partial}_{t}{\Theta}^{n+1/2},q_{h})+\kappa(\nabla P^{n+1/2},\nabla q_{h})+\beta(\nabla\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2},\nabla q_{h})

\displaystyle=(g^{n+1/2},q_{h}).

This section and the rest of the paper uses the discrete Gronwall Lemma that is stated below.

Lemma 3.4

(Discrete Gronwall Lemma
[
30
]
)
.

Let
\{v_{n}\}
,
\{w_{n}\}
, and
\{y_{n}\}
be three non-negative sequences, with
\{y_{n}\}
monotone, that satisfy

\displaystyle v_{m}+w_{m}\leq y_{m}+\nu\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}v_{n},\quad\nu>0,\ v_{0}+w_{0}\leq y_{0}
. Then for
m\geq 0,
it holds that

v_{m}+w_{m}\leq y_{m}e^{m\nu}.

Remark 3.5

(Identities)
.

Before proceeding further, we state the following identities, which lead to telescopic sums and are used in Theorem
3.6
and Theorem
4.4
. For any discrete functions
Q^{n}\in V_{h}
and
S^{n}\in W_{h}
,
n=0,1,2,\cdots,N
there hold

(3.9a)

\displaystyle 2\Delta t(\bar{\partial}_{t}^{2}Q^{n},\delta_{t}Q^{n})

\displaystyle=\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}Q^{n+1/2}\rVert^{2}-\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}Q^{n-1/2}\rVert^{2},

(3.9b)

\displaystyle 2\Delta t(\nabla\bar{\partial}_{t}^{2}Q^{n},\nabla\delta_{t}Q^{n})

\displaystyle=\lVert\nabla\bar{\partial}_{t}Q^{n+1/2}\rVert^{2}-\lVert\nabla\bar{\partial}_{t}Q^{n-1/2}\rVert^{2},

(3.9c)

\displaystyle 2\Delta ta_{h}(Q^{n,1/4},\delta_{t}Q^{n})

\displaystyle=a_{h}(Q^{n+1/2},Q^{n+1/2})-a_{h}(Q^{n-1/2},Q^{n-1/2}),

(3.9d)

\displaystyle 2\Delta t(\bar{\partial}_{t}S^{n+1/2},S^{n+1/2})

\displaystyle=\lVert S^{n+1}\rVert^{2}-\lVert S^{n}\rVert^{2}.

3.3
Stability

Here we demonstrate the stability of the fully discrete scheme
in (
3.8
)
and establish a uniform bound of the solution
(U^{m+1},\Theta^{m+1},P^{m+1})
at
t_{m+1}
for

in terms of the solution at
t_{0}
,
t_{1}
, and the load/source functions. For any
\chi_{h}^{n},Q_{h}^{n}\in W_{h};n\in\{1,2,\cdots,m\}

\lVert(\chi_{h}^{m},Q_{h}^{m})\rVert^{2}_{H}:=\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{m}\Big[{b_{1}}\lVert\chi_{h}^{n+1/2}\rVert^{2}+c_{1}\lVert\nabla\chi_{h}^{n+1/2}\rVert^{2}+{\kappa}\lVert\nabla Q_{h}^{n+1/2}\rVert^{2}\Big].

Also, we define

\displaystyle((U^{0},U^{1},f))

\displaystyle:=6\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{1/2}\rVert^{2}+4a_{0}\lVert\nabla\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{1/2}\rVert^{2}+4d_{0}C_{\rm{Cont}}\lVert U^{1/2}\rVert_{h}^{2}+4T^{2}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2},

\displaystyle((\Theta^{0},\Theta^{1},\phi))

\displaystyle:={\frac{1}{2}(3a_{1}+|\gamma|/\gamma_{0})}\lVert\Theta^{1}\rVert^{2}+{c_{1}}\Delta t\lVert\nabla\Theta^{1/2}\rVert^{2}+\frac{T^{2}}{a_{1}-|\gamma|/\gamma_{0}}\lVert\phi\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))},

\displaystyle((P^{0},P^{1},g))

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}{(3a_{2}+|\gamma|\gamma_{0})}\lVert P^{1}\rVert^{2}+{\kappa}\Delta t\lVert\nabla P^{1/2}\rVert^{2}+\frac{T^{2}}{a_{2}-|\gamma|\gamma_{0}}\lVert g\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}.

In relation with (
2.3
), we define the discrete energy of (
1.1
) at time
t_{n}
, for

m=1,\cdots,N
, as

\displaystyle E_{h}(U^{m+1},\Theta^{m+1},P^{m+1})

\displaystyle:=\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{m+1/2}\rVert^{2}+a_{0}\lVert\nabla\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{m+1/2}\rVert^{2}+d_{0}C_{\rm{Coer}}\lVert U^{m+1/2}\rVert^{2}_{h}

\displaystyle\qquad+({a_{1}-|\gamma|/\gamma_{0}})\lVert\Theta^{m+1}\rVert^{2}+{(a_{2}-|\gamma|\gamma_{0})}\lVert P^{m+1}\rVert^{2}+\lVert(\Theta^{m},P^{m})\rVert^{2}_{H}.

Then, as in Theorem

Theorem 3.6

(Stability)
.

Let
f,\phi,g\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
,
u^{0}\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega),u^{*0}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, and both
\theta^{0},p^{0}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Then, the scheme (
3.8
) is unconditionally stable. Moreover, for
1\leq m\leq N-1
, the following bound holds:

\displaystyle E_{h}(U^{m+1},\Theta^{m+1},P^{m+1})\lesssim((U^{0},U^{1},f))+((\Theta^{0},\Theta^{1},\phi))+((P^{0},P^{1},g)).

The constant absorbed in ”
\lesssim
” above depends on
T
and on the model coefficients
a_{0},c_{1},\alpha,\beta,\kappa
.

Proof
.

The proof follows in six steps as outlined below.

Step 1 (Key inequality).
We multiply (
3.8a
) by
8\Delta t
, then choose
v_{h}=\delta_{t}U^{n}
in (
3.8a
), and utilize (
3.9a
)-(
3.9c
) to show that

\displaystyle 4

\displaystyle\big[\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2}\rVert^{2}-\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n-1/2}\rVert^{2}+a_{0}\lVert\nabla\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2}\rVert^{2}-a_{0}\lVert\nabla\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n-1/2}\rVert^{2}\big]+4d_{0}a_{h}(U^{n+1/2},U^{n+1/2})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad-4d_{0}a_{h}(U^{n-1/2},U^{n-1/2})=8\Delta t(\alpha\nabla\Theta^{n,1/4}+\beta\nabla P^{n,1/4},\nabla\delta_{t}U^{n})+8\Delta t(f^{n,1/4},\delta_{t}U^{n})

(3.11)

\displaystyle\quad\quad=4\Delta t(\alpha\nabla\Theta^{n,1/4}+\beta\nabla P^{n,1/4},\nabla(\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2}+\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n-1/2}))+4\Delta t(f^{n,1/4},\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2}+\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n-1/2}),

with the identity
2\delta_{t}U^{n}=\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2}+\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n-1/2}
in the last equality.
Next we choose
\psi_{h}=2\Delta t\Theta^{n+1/2}
in (
3.8b
),
q_{h}=2\Delta tP^{n+1/2}
in (
3.8c
), employ the identity (
3.9d
) and add the two resulting equations to obtain

\displaystyle a_{1}\Delta t\bar{\partial}_{t}\lVert\Theta^{n+1/2}\rVert^{2}+2\Delta t\big[{b_{1}}\lVert\Theta^{n+1/2}\rVert^{2}+c_{1}\lVert\nabla\Theta^{n+1/2}\rVert^{2}+\kappa\lVert\nabla P^{n+1/2}\rVert^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+2\gamma\big[(P^{n},\Theta^{n})-(P^{n+1},\Theta^{n+1})\big]+a_{2}\Delta t\bar{\partial}_{t}\lVert P^{n+1/2}\rVert^{2}

(3.12)

\displaystyle=-2\Delta t\big[(\alpha\nabla\Theta^{n+1/2}+\beta\nabla P^{n+1/2},\nabla\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2})\big]+2\Delta t\big[(\phi^{n+1/2},{\Theta}^{n+1/2})+(g^{n+1/2},{P}^{n+1/2})\big].

We also combine the coupling terms on the right-hand sides of (

3.12

\Theta^{n,1/4}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\Theta^{n+1/2}+\Theta^{n-1/2}\right)
twice (analogously for
P^{n,1/4}

\displaystyle 4(\alpha\nabla\Theta^{n,1/4}+\beta\nabla P^{n,1/4},\nabla(\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2}+\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n-1/2}))-2(\alpha\nabla\Theta^{n+1/2}+\beta\nabla P^{n+1/2},\nabla\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2})

(3.14)

Using the definition

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{m}A^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{m}4(\alpha\nabla\Theta^{n,1/4}+\beta\nabla P^{n,1/4},\nabla\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n-1/2})

\epsilon=2/c_{1}
(resp.

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}2(\alpha\nabla\Theta^{n-1/2},\nabla\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2})

), respectively. Then, under the assumptions of Theorem
4.4
, for
1\leq m\leq N-1
, the following error estimate holds

\displaystyle\lVert u^{m+1}-U^{m+1}\rVert^{2}+a_{0}\lVert\nabla(u^{m+1}-U^{m+1})\rVert^{2}\lesssim h^{4\sigma}+(\Delta t)^{4}.

Proof
.

Ignoring the last four non-negative terms on the left-hand side of (
4.37
), we can obtain

\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}\zeta^{m+1/2}\rVert^{2}+a_{0}\lVert\nabla\bar{\partial}_{t}\zeta^{m+1/2}\rVert^{2}+\lVert\zeta^{m+1/2}\rVert^{2}_{h}\lesssim h^{{4\sigma}}+\Delta t^{4},\qquad\text{ for }1\leq m\leq N-1.

Note that
\zeta^{m+1}=\zeta^{m+1/2}+\frac{1}{2}\Delta t\bar{\partial}_{t}\zeta^{m+1/2}
(resp.
\nabla\zeta^{m+1}=\nabla\zeta^{m+1/2}+\frac{1}{2}\Delta t\nabla\bar{\partial}_{t}\zeta^{m+1/2}
).
Then, by the discrete Poincaré inequality we have
\lVert\zeta^{m+1/2}\rVert\leq\lVert\zeta^{m+1/2}\rVert_{h}
(resp.
\lVert\nabla\zeta^{m+1/2}\rVert\leq\lVert\zeta^{m+1/2}\rVert_{h}
), and hence

\lVert\zeta^{m+1}\rVert^{2}+a_{0}\lVert\nabla\zeta^{m+1}\rVert\lesssim\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}\zeta^{m+1/2}\rVert^{2}+\lVert\zeta^{m+1/2}\rVert^{2}_{h}+a_{0}\lVert\nabla\bar{\partial}^{2}_{t}\zeta^{m+1/2}\rVert+a_{0}\lVert\zeta^{m+1/2}\rVert_{h}\lesssim(1+a_{0})(h^{4\sigma}+(\Delta t)^{4}).

Therefore, simply using triangle inequality we can obtain

\lVert u^{m+1}-U^{m+1}\rVert^{2}+a_{0}\lVert\nabla(u^{m+1}-U^{m+1})\rVert^{2}\lesssim\|\rho^{m+1}\|^{2}+\|\zeta^{m+1}\|^{2}+a_{0}\|\nabla\rho^{m+1}\|^{2}+a_{0}\|\nabla\zeta^{m+1}\|^{2}.

A combination of last three inequalities and (
3.3
) for
\|\rho^{m+1}\|^{2}+a_{0}\|\nabla\rho^{m+1}\|^{2}
lead to the desired result.
∎

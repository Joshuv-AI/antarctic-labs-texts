Introduction

Let
{\mathbb{Z}}_{s}
be the ring of
s
-adic integers, where
s
is a positive integer, not necessarily a prime. Let
\mathcal{V}
be the set of balls in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{s}
. The corresponding Hilbert space
\ell^{2}(\mathcal{V})
carries a natural, diagonal representation of the algebra of continuous functions on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{s}
. The purpose of this paper and the companion paper
[
12
]
is to study natural
C^{*}
-algebras in
\ell^{2}(\mathcal{V})
generated by
C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})
along with specific shifts. Those shift algebras are interesting examples of
C^{*}
-algebras with connections to number theory and combinatorics, a subject that has gained some popularity, see for example
[
9
,
25
]
.

Our motivation to study
C^{*}
-algebras associated with shifts on trees partially comes from trying to extend the results from
[
17
,
19
,
20
]
on
p
-adic spectral triples as well as ideas from
[
23
]
on noncommutative geometry of ultrametric Cantor sets. A heuristic goal is to understand “quantum” non-archimedean spaces and their geometry with seemingly many candidates for such concepts.

We review all the definitions and the key results from the companion paper
[
12
]
so that the present paper is self-contained. The main purpose of
[
12
]
was to determine how each of those shift algebras can be identified with a crossed product by an endomorphism, in fact a monomorphism with a hereditary range. In this paper, we delve deeper into the structure of the shift algebras and compute their
K
-theory.

There are several competing concepts of crossed product by an endomorphism
[
1
,
3
,
11
,
22
,
29
]
but they all agree for monomorphisms with a hereditary range. In
[
12
]
, we took a pragmatic approach to crossed products by finding enlargements of the initial algebra
C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})
up to a better behaved “coefficient”
C^{*}
-subalgebras of the shift algebras which makes the corresponding monomorphisms to have hereditary range. Those coefficient algebras are the starting point of the current paper.

To understand the structure of the shift algebras, we consider natural ideals in each of the algebras and then describe their quotients. The main technique is an analog of a basic construction for usual crossed products by automorphisms involving invariant ideals, see
[
30
, Section 3.4]
. In particular, we prove a counterpart to
[
30
, Proposition 3.19]
which gives a short exact sequence of crossed products obtained from an invariant ideal.

Another key technique used in this paper to understand the structure of some crossed products is to make them concrete algebras through faithful representations. Faithfulness is established using a version of the O’Donovan condition easily implied by the existence of gauge actions.

A short exact sequence of shift
C^{*}
-algebras induces a
6
-term exact sequence in
K
-theory which we then, in each case, analyze and reduce to short exact sequences. This is enough to determine the K-groups in all but the Bunce–Deddens shift case for which the sequence does not split. Instead, in this case, we describe the
K
-theory as a direct limit of finitely generated free Abelian groups.

The paper is organized as follows. Preliminary Section
2
contains the definitions of shift algebras and a gauge action for each. Crossed products by endomorphisms, coefficient algebras with gauge actions and invariant ideals are the subject of Section
3
. Each of the following four sections is devoted to one of the shift algebras. Those sections are structured similarly: we recall the coefficients algebras from
[
12
]
, describe the coefficient algebra ideals, their quotients and the quotient endomorphisms, followed by the identification of the corresponding shift algebra ideals and their quotients. Each section is then concluded with a calculation of the
K
-theory.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
\boldsymbol{s}
-adic integers

For
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
s\geq 2
, the space of
s
-adic integers
{\mathbb{Z}}_{s}
is defined as consisting of infinite sums

{\mathbb{Z}}_{s}=\Biggl\{x=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}x_{j}s^{j}\mid 0\leq x_{j}\leq s-1,\,j=0,1,2,\dots\Biggr\}.

We call the above expansion of
x
its
s
-adic expansion. Note that
{\mathbb{Z}}_{s}
is isomorphic with the countable product
\prod{\mathbb{Z}}/s{\mathbb{Z}}
and so, when the product is equipped with the Tychonoff topology, it is a Cantor set. It is also a metric space with the usual norm
|\cdot|_{s}
, which is defined as follows

|0|_{s}=0
and, if
x\neq 0
,
|x|_{s}=s^{-n}
,
where
x_{n}
is the first nonzero term in the above
s
-adic expansion.
Moreover,
{\mathbb{Z}}_{s}
is an Abelian ring with unity with respect to addition and multiplication with a carry and we have

{\mathbb{Z}}_{s}\cong\lim\limits_{\longleftarrow}{\mathbb{Z}}/s^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Using the division algorithm modulo
s
repeatedly, we can associate an
s
-adic expansion to any integer and so
{\mathbb{Z}}_{s}
naturally contains a copy of
{\mathbb{Z}}
which is dense in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{s}
.

Any ball in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{s}
is of the form

\left\{y\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{s}\mid|y-x|_{s}\leq\frac{1}{s^{n}}\right\},

where
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
and
0\leq x<s^{n}
. Here
x
is a particular center of this ball. Consequently, we can write the set of balls
\mathcal{V}
in the following way

\mathcal{V}=\{(n,x)\mid n=0,1,2,\dots,\,0\leq x<s^{n}\}
.

2.2
Hilbert spaces and shifts

Throughout this paper, we use the Hilbert space
H=\ell^{2}(\mathcal{V})
with canonical basis
\{E_{(n,x)}\}
, where
{n=0,1,2,\dots}
and
0\leq x<s^{n}
. Let
f\in C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})
and define a bounded operator
M_{f}\colon H\to H
by

M_{f}E_{(n,x)}=f(x)E_{(n,x)}
.
Due to the density of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{s}
, we have that

\|M_{f}\|=\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}}{|f(x)|}=\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{s}}{|f(x)|}

and so
f\mapsto M_{f}
is a faithful representation of the
C^{*}
-algebra
C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})
.

There are four main shifts in
H
that are the focus of this paper and which we call the Bunce–Deddens shift, the Hensel shift, the Bernoulli shift, and the Serre shift. All four shifts are defined on
H
with formulas given on the basis elements of
H
as follows:

(1)

The Bunce–Deddens shift:

U\colon H\to H
via

UE_{(n,x)}=E_{(n+1,x+1)}
.

(2)

The Hensel shift:

V\colon H\to H
via

VE_{(n,x)}=E_{(n+1,sx)}
.

(3)

The Bernoulli shift:

S\colon H\to H
via

SE_{(n,x)}=\frac{1}{s}\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}E_{(n+1,sx+j)}.

(4)

The Serre shift:

W\colon H\to H
via

WE_{(n,x)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}E_{(n+1,x+js^{n})}.

A computation yields their respective adjoints

\displaystyle(1)\quad U^{*}E_{(n,x)}=\begin{cases}E_{(n-1,x-1)}&\textrm{if}\ n\geq 1,\ 0<x\leq s^{n-1},\\
0&\textrm{if}\ n=0\ \textrm{or}\ x=0\ \textrm{or}\ s^{n-1}<x<s^{n},\end{cases}

\displaystyle(2)\quad V^{*}E_{(n,x)}=\begin{cases}E_{(n-1,x/s)}&\textrm{if}\ n\geq 1,\ s\mid x,\\
0&\textrm{if}\ n=0\ \textrm{or}\ s\nmid x,\end{cases}

\displaystyle(3)\quad S^{*}E_{(n,x)}=\begin{cases}E_{(n-1,(x-x\textrm{ mod }s)/s)}&\textrm{if}\ n\geq 1,\\
0&\textrm{if}\ n=0,\end{cases}

\displaystyle(4)\quad W^{*}E_{(n,x)}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{s}}E_{(n-1,x\textrm{ mod }s^{n-1})}&\textrm{if}\ n\geq 1,\\
0&\textrm{if}\ n=0.\end{cases}

It is easy to verify that

U^{*}U=V^{*}V=S^{*}S=W^{*}W=I
,
hence each one is an isometry. For each of these four shifts
\mathcal{J}\in\{U,V,S,W\}
, we have the corresponding shift
C^{*}
-algebras

A_{\mathcal{J}}:=C^{*}(\mathcal{J},M_{f}\mid f\in C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})),

which are the main objects of interest in this paper.

By analogy with graph algebras, a gauge action is an important structure shared by all those shift algebras. For
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
, consider the following strongly continuous one-parameter family
\{\mathcal{U}_{\theta}\}
of unitary operators on
\ell^{2}(\mathcal{V})
defined on basis elements by

\mathcal{U}_{\theta}E_{(n,x)}={\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}n\theta}E_{(n,x)}
.
We define a continuous one-parameter family
\{\rho_{\theta}\}
of automorphisms of
A_{\mathcal{J}}
, the gauge action
{\rho_{\theta}\colon A_{\mathcal{J}}\to A_{\mathcal{J}}}
, by the formula

\rho_{\theta}(a)=\mathcal{U}_{\theta}a\mathcal{U}_{\theta}^{-1}
.
Then on generators of
A_{\mathcal{J}}
we have

\rho_{\theta}(\mathcal{J})={\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}\theta}\mathcal{J},\qquad\rho_{\theta}(\mathcal{J}^{*})={\rm e}^{-2\pi{\rm i}\theta}\mathcal{J}^{*},\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\rho_{\theta}(M_{f})=M_{f}.

Throughout the rest of this paper, we describe the structure of each algebra and study its
K
-theory.

3
Coefficient algebras with a gauge action

3.1
Coefficient algebras

The notion of a coefficient algebra was introduced in
[
21
]
. A review of this concept, with additional material suitable for our purposes, is the main theme of this section.

Given a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, let
{\mathcal{J}}\in B(\mathcal{H})
be an isometry and let
B_{\mathcal{J}}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H})
be a
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
B(\mathcal{H})
containing the identity
I
. We use the notation
A_{\mathcal{J}}
for the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by
B_{\mathcal{J}}
and
{\mathcal{J}}
. We call
B_{\mathcal{J}}
a
coefficient algebra
of the
C^{*}
-algebra
A_{\mathcal{J}}
if
B_{\mathcal{J}}
and
{\mathcal{J}}
satisfy the following conditions

\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}(a):={\mathcal{J}}a{\mathcal{J}}^{*}\in B_{\mathcal{J}}

and

\beta_{\mathcal{J}}(a):={\mathcal{J}}^{*}a{\mathcal{J}}\in B_{\mathcal{J}}
,
for all
a\in B_{\mathcal{J}}
.

It is easy to see that
\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}
is an endomorphism of
B_{\mathcal{J}}
and we have the following properties
{\beta_{\mathcal{J}}\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}(a)=a}
,
\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}\beta_{\mathcal{J}}(a)=\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}(I)a\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}(I)
, and
\beta_{\mathcal{J}}(I)=I
.
Moreover, the map
\beta_{\mathcal{J}}\colon B_{\mathcal{J}}\to B_{\mathcal{J}}
is linear, continuous, positive and has the “transfer” property
{\beta_{\mathcal{J}}(\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}(a)b)=a\beta_{\mathcal{J}}(b)}
,
and thus is an example of Exel’s transfer operator
[
11
]
. Also notice that if the algebra
B_{\mathcal{J}}
is commutative, or more generally if
\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}(I)
is in the center of
B_{\mathcal{J}}
, then
\beta_{\mathcal{J}}
is also an endomorphism. Indeed, notice that
\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}(I)={\mathcal{J}}{\mathcal{J}}^{*}
and we have that

\beta_{\mathcal{J}}(a)\beta_{\mathcal{J}}(b)={\mathcal{J}}^{*}a{\mathcal{J}}{\mathcal{J}}^{*}b{\mathcal{J}}={\mathcal{J}}^{*}a\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}(I)b{\mathcal{J}}={\mathcal{J}}^{*}\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}(I)ab{\mathcal{J}}={\mathcal{J}}^{*}{\mathcal{J}}{\mathcal{J}}^{*}ab{\mathcal{J}}=\beta_{\mathcal{J}}(ab).

Clearly,
\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}(I)
is a projection and notice that
\textrm{Ran}(\alpha_{\mathcal{J}})
, the range of
\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}
, is a hereditary subalgebra of
B_{\mathcal{J}}
and we have

\Ran(\alpha_{\mathcal{J}})=\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}(I)B_{\mathcal{J}}\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}(I)
.

3.2
Gauge action

We now consider additional structure called a gauge action. Assume that there is a one-parameter family
\{\mathcal{U}_{\theta}\}
, for
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
, of unitary operators on
\mathcal{H}
satisfying the following commutation properties

\mathcal{U}_{\theta}a=a\mathcal{U}_{\theta}
,
\mathcal{U}_{\theta}\mathcal{J}={\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}\theta}\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{\theta}

for all
a\in B_{\mathcal{J}}
.
We define a one-parameter family
\{\rho_{\theta}\}
of automorphisms
\rho_{\theta}\colon B(\mathcal{H})\to B(\mathcal{H})
by the formula

\rho_{\theta}(a)=\mathcal{U}_{\theta}a\mathcal{U}_{\theta}^{-1}
.
It follows that on generators of
A_{\mathcal{J}}
we have

\rho_{\theta}(\mathcal{J})={\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}\theta}\mathcal{J},\qquad\rho_{\theta}(\mathcal{J}^{*})={\rm e}^{-2\pi{\rm i}\theta}\mathcal{J}^{*},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\rho_{\theta}(a)=a,\quad a\in B_{\mathcal{J}}.

Clearly, the map
\theta\mapsto\rho_{\theta}(x)
is continuous for all
x
which are polynomials in
\mathcal{J}
and
\mathcal{J}^{*}
with coefficients in
B_{\mathcal{J}}
. It follows by an approximation argument that it is continuous for every
{x\in A_{\mathcal{J}}}
.

As a consequence of the existence of a gauge action, we have an expectation

E\colon A_{\mathcal{J}}\to A_{\mathcal{J}}

given by the formula

E(x):=\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{\theta}(x){\rm d}\theta.

(3.1)

The authors of
[
12
]
noted that
C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})
is not a coefficient algebra for the shift algebras currently considered. Instead, coefficient algebra extensions of
C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})
were constructed as the invariant subalgebras
A_{\mathcal{J},\textrm{inv}}
of
A_{\mathcal{J}}
with respect to the automorphism
\rho_{\theta}
.
This is because
C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})\subseteq A_{\mathcal{J},\textrm{inv}}
and, for every
a\in A_{\mathcal{J},\textrm{inv}}
, we also have

\mathcal{J}^{*}a\mathcal{J}\in A_{\mathcal{J},\textrm{inv}}
and
\mathcal{J}a\mathcal{J}^{*}\in A_{\mathcal{J},\textrm{inv}}
.

3.3
O’Donovan condition

Using the commutation relation

{\mathcal{J}}a=\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}(a){\mathcal{J}}
,
one can arrange the polynomials in
{\mathcal{J}}
,
{\mathcal{J}}^{*}
with coefficients in
B_{\mathcal{J}}
so that the coefficients are in front of the powers of
{\mathcal{J}}
and behind the powers of
{\mathcal{J}}^{*}
. This leads to the following observation (see
[
21
, Proposition 2.3]
):
the vector space
\mathcal{F}(B_{\mathcal{J}},{\mathcal{J}})
consisting of finite sums

x=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}{\mathcal{J}}^{n}+\sum_{n<0}({\mathcal{J}}^{*})^{-n}a_{n},

(3.2)

where
a_{n}\in B_{\mathcal{J}}
, is a dense
*
-subalgebra of
A_{\mathcal{J}}
.
Additionally, using
{\mathcal{J}}^{n}={\mathcal{J}}^{n}({\mathcal{J}}^{*})^{n}{\mathcal{J}}^{n}
and its adjoint
one can always choose coefficients
a_{n}
such that

a_{n}=a_{n}{\mathcal{J}}^{n}({\mathcal{J}}^{*})^{n}
,
n\geq 0
and
a_{n}={\mathcal{J}}^{-n}({\mathcal{J}}^{*})^{-n}a_{n}
,
n<0
.

Then, applying the expectation defined in equation (
3.1
), we obtain

a_{n}=E(x({\mathcal{J}}^{*})^{n}),\quad n\geq 0\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad a_{n}=E({\mathcal{J}}^{-n}x),\quad n<0,

(3.3)

and thus such coefficients
a_{n}
are unique. More generally, by the above formulas we can associate “Fourier” coefficients
a_{n}\in B_{\mathcal{J}}
to any
x\in A_{\mathcal{J}}
and, by the usual Cesaro summation argument
[
15
]
, the coefficients
a_{n}
fully determine
x
.

We also have

\|a_{0}\|=\|E(x)\|\leq\|x\|
,
which is called the O’Donovan condition.
A key result (see
[
21
, Theorem 2.7]
) says that since
A_{\mathcal{J}}
satisfies the O’Donovan condition then
A_{\mathcal{J}}
is isomorphic to the crossed product
C^{*}
-algebra
{B_{\mathcal{J}}\rtimes_{\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}}{\mathbb{N}}}

A_{\mathcal{J}}\cong B_{\mathcal{J}}\rtimes_{\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}}{\mathbb{N}},

(3.4)

see
[
3
]
and the appendix of
[
13
]
. For concreteness, we use Stacey’s “multiplicity-one crossed product”, see
[
3
]
. Namely,

B_{\mathcal{J}}\rtimes_{\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}}{\mathbb{N}}
is defined to be the universal
C^{*}
-algebra generated by a copy of
B_{\mathcal{J}}
and an isometry
\mathcal{L}
such that for all
a\in B_{\mathcal{J}}
,

\mathcal{L}a\mathcal{L}^{*}=\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}(a)
.

The significance of the equation (
3.4
) is that the crossed product on the right-hand side is a universal object, defined in terms of generators and relations, and thus it gives us a way to construct convenient, concrete representations. A representation of the crossed product
{B_{\mathcal{J}}\rtimes_{\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}}{\mathbb{N}}}
is faithful if it is faithful on
B_{\mathcal{J}}
and satisfies the O’Donovan condition
[
3
]
. In particular, such a representation is faithful if it is faithful on
B_{\mathcal{J}}
and admits a gauge action.

3.4
Invariant ideals

In this section, we consider invariant ideals in coefficient algebras and the structure they impose on the crossed products, analogously to the usual crossed products by automorphisms, see
[
30
, Section 3.4]
. While this is standard material, for completeness we provide short proofs.

Let
I_{\mathcal{J}}
be an
\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}
and
\beta_{\mathcal{J}}
invariant ideal in
B_{\mathcal{J}}
and let
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
be the subalgebra in
A_{\mathcal{J}}
defined as

\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}:=C^{*}(a{\mathcal{J}}^{n}\mid a\in I_{\mathcal{J}},\,n=0,1,\dots)
.
Note that
I_{\mathcal{J}}\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
. Alternatively, one can define
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
by using the Fourier coefficients
a_{n}
from equations (
3.2
) and (
3.3
)

x\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
if and only if for all
{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\colon a_{n}\in I_{\mathcal{J}}}
.

We have the following elementary fact.

Proposition 3.1
.

The algebra
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
is an ideal in
A_{\mathcal{J}}
.

Proof.

We write the generators more explicitly
A_{\mathcal{J}}=C^{*}(b,{\mathcal{J}},{\mathcal{J}}^{*}\mid b\in B_{\mathcal{J}})
and similarly
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}=C^{*}(a{\mathcal{J}}^{n},({\mathcal{J}}^{*})^{n}a\mid a\in I_{\mathcal{J}},\,n=0,1,\dots)
. It is sufficient to verify that the products of generators are in
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
. This requires checking multiple cases.

Let
a\in I_{\mathcal{J}}
,
b\in B_{\mathcal{J}}
. It is clear that
ba{\mathcal{J}}^{n},a{\mathcal{J}}^{n}{\mathcal{J}}
, and
{\mathcal{J}}a{\mathcal{J}}^{n}
are in
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
.
Next, note that
{a{\mathcal{J}}^{n}b=a\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}^{n}(b){\mathcal{J}}^{n}\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}}
since
a\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}^{n}(b)\in I_{\mathcal{J}}
.

For adjoints, we have

{\mathcal{J}}^{*}a{\mathcal{J}}^{n}=\begin{cases}{\mathcal{J}}^{*}a{\mathcal{J}}{\mathcal{J}}^{n-1},&n>0,\\
{\mathcal{J}}^{*}a,&n=0\end{cases}=\begin{cases}\beta_{\mathcal{J}}(a){\mathcal{J}}^{n-1},&n>0,\\
{\mathcal{J}}^{*},a&n=0\end{cases}\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}

since
\beta_{\mathcal{J}}(a)\in I_{\mathcal{J}}
. Further, note that
{\mathcal{J}}{\mathcal{J}}^{*}=\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}(1)\in B_{\mathcal{J}}
so

a{\mathcal{J}}^{n}{\mathcal{J}}^{*}=\begin{cases}a{\mathcal{J}}^{n-1}\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}(1),&n>0,\\
a{\mathcal{J}}^{*},&n=0\end{cases}=\begin{cases}a\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}^{n}(1){\mathcal{J}}^{n-1},&n>0,\\
{\mathcal{J}}^{*}\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}(a),&n=0\end{cases}\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}.

Finally,
({\mathcal{J}}^{*})^{n}ab\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
,
b({\mathcal{J}}^{*})^{n}a=({\mathcal{J}}^{*})^{n}\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}^{n}(b)a\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
,
so
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
is an ideal completing the proof.∎

Next consider the factor maps
[\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}],[\beta_{\mathcal{J}}]\colon B_{\mathcal{J}}/I_{\mathcal{J}}\to B_{\mathcal{J}}/I_{\mathcal{J}}
defined by

[\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}](a+I_{\mathcal{J}})=\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}(a)+I_{\mathcal{J}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad[\beta_{\mathcal{J}}](a+I_{\mathcal{J}})=\beta_{\mathcal{J}}(a)+I_{\mathcal{J}}.

Proposition 3.2
.

[\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}]
is a monomorphism of
B_{\mathcal{J}}/I_{\mathcal{J}}
with hereditary range.

Proof.

Note that
\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}
is an endomorphism of
B_{\mathcal{J}}
, thus
[\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}]
is an endomorphism of
B_{\mathcal{J}}/I_{\mathcal{J}}
. Further,
\beta_{\mathcal{J}}\circ\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}=I
, so
[\beta_{\mathcal{J}}]\circ[\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}]=[I]
, thus
[\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}]
is a monomorphism.

For the range of
[\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}]
, we know from
[
12
]
that

\mathrm{Ran}\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}=\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}(I)B_{\mathcal{J}}\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}(I)
.
This implies that

\mathrm{Ran}[\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}]=[\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}][I]B_{\mathcal{J}}/I_{\mathcal{J}}[\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}][I]

and so
[\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}]
has hereditary range.
∎

We now investigate the structure of the factor algebra
A_{\mathcal{J}}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

We have the following isomorphism of algebras:

B_{\mathcal{J}}/I_{\mathcal{J}}\rtimes_{[\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}]}{\mathbb{N}}\cong A_{\mathcal{J}}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}.

Proof.

B_{\mathcal{J}}\rtimes_{[\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}]}{\mathbb{N}}
is the universal
C^{*}
-algebra generated by a copy of
B_{\mathcal{J}}/I_{\mathcal{J}}
and an isometry
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{J}}
such that

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{J}}[a]\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{J}}^{*}=[\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}][a]
.

Define the map
\varphi_{\mathcal{J}}\colon B_{\mathcal{J}}/I_{\mathcal{J}}\rtimes_{[\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}]}{\mathbb{N}}\to A_{\mathcal{J}}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
on generators by

\varphi_{\mathcal{J}}(a+I_{\mathcal{J}})=a+\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\varphi_{\mathcal{J}}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{J}})=\mathcal{J}+\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}.

This is well-defined as
I_{\mathcal{J}}\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
. Since
\mathcal{J}
is an isometry, so is
\varphi_{\mathcal{J}}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{J}})
. Furthermore, the map
\varphi_{\mathcal{J}}
preserves the crossed product relation

\varphi_{\mathcal{J}}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{J}})\varphi_{\mathcal{J}}(a+I_{\mathcal{J}})\varphi_{\mathcal{J}}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{J}})^{*}=\mathcal{J}a\mathcal{J}^{*}+\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}=\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}(a)+\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}=[\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}]\varphi_{\mathcal{J}}(a+I_{\mathcal{J}}).

By universality,
\varphi_{\mathcal{J}}
extends to a homomorphism from
B_{\mathcal{J}}/I_{\mathcal{J}}\rtimes_{[\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}]}{\mathbb{N}}
to
A_{\mathcal{J}}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
.

By uniqueness of the Fourier coefficients of
a
, see equation (
3.3
), we see that if
a\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
with
{a\in B_{\mathcal{J}}}
, then
a\in I_{\mathcal{J}}
. This means that the map
\varphi_{\mathcal{J}}
is faithful on
B_{\mathcal{J}}/I_{\mathcal{J}}
. To verify the map is faithful on the whole crossed product, we observe that the algebra
A_{\mathcal{J}}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
admits a gauge action.
Indeed, since
\rho_{\theta}
preserves
I_{\mathcal{J}}\subseteq B_{\mathcal{J}}
it factors to the quotient
[\rho]_{\theta}\colon A_{\mathcal{J}}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}\to A_{\mathcal{J}}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
.
We have that
[\rho]_{\theta}
is a 1-parameter family of automorphisms with

[\rho]_{\theta}(a+\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}})=a+\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}\quad\text{for }a\in B_{\mathcal{J}},\qquad[\rho]_{\theta}(\mathcal{J}+\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}})={\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}\theta}\mathcal{J}+\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}

and therefore
\varphi_{\mathcal{J}}
is faithful by
[
3
]
.

Finally, notice that
\varphi_{\mathcal{J}}
is onto because its range is closed and it contains the generators of
A_{\mathcal{J}}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
.
∎

As a consequence, we get the following short exact sequence of
C^{*}
-algebras, which is the basis for all structural results in the following sections:

0\to\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}\to A_{\mathcal{J}}\to B_{\mathcal{J}}/I_{\mathcal{J}}\rtimes_{[\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}]}{\mathbb{N}}\to 0.

(3.5)

4
The Bunce–Deddens shift

4.1
The algebra
\boldsymbol{A_{U}}

In this section, we consider the first shift algebra

A_{U}=C^{*}(U,M_{f}\mid f\in C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s}))
,
where the shift
U
is given on the basis elements of
H=\ell^{2}(\mathcal{V})
by
UE_{(n,x)}=E_{(n+1,x+1)}
. The corresponding maps on
A_{U}
are

\alpha_{U}(a):=UaU^{*}\in A_{U}
and
\beta_{U}(a):=U^{*}aU\in A_{U}
, for

.

We define the maps
\mathfrak{a}_{U}
and
\mathfrak{b}_{U}
on
C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})
as follows:

\mathfrak{a}_{U}f(x)=f(x-1)
and
\mathfrak{b}_{U}f(x)=f(x+1)
.
Both are automorphisms on
C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})
and we have

\mathfrak{a}_{U}(1)=\mathfrak{b}_{U}(1)=1
and
{\mathfrak{a}_{U}\circ\mathfrak{b}_{U}=\mathfrak{b}_{U}\circ\mathfrak{a}_{U}=1}
.
The operators
U
and
M_{f}
satisfy the following relations:

M_{f}U=UM_{\mathfrak{b}_{U}f}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\alpha_{U}(M_{f})=\allowbreak M_{\mathfrak{a}_{U}f}UU^{*}.

It follows additionally that

UM_{f}=M_{\mathfrak{a}_{U}f}U
and
\beta_{U}(M_{f})=M_{\mathfrak{b}_{U}f}
.

In parallel, we will also describe and analyze the subalgebras

A_{U,n}:=C^{*}(U,M_{(n,x)}\mid 0\leq x<s^{n}),

where
M_{(n,x)}
are the multiplication operators by the characteristic functions of balls

M_{(n,x)}E_{(m,y)}=\begin{cases}E_{(m,y)}&\textrm{if}\ |y-x|_{s}\leq s^{-n},\\
0&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We have the following formulas:

\mathfrak{a}_{U}M_{(n,x)}=M_{(n,x+1)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathfrak{b}_{U}M_{(n,x)}=M_{(n,x-1)}.

Notice that
A_{U,n}
is a subalgebra of
A_{U,n+1}
and
A_{U}
is the
C^{*}
-algebras direct limit of
A_{U,n}
via the inclusion maps

A_{U}=\smash{\lim\limits_{\longrightarrow}A_{U,n}}
.

4.2
The coefficient algebra of
\boldsymbol{A_{U}}

Define the mutually orthogonal projections

By

is isomorphic to

.

Put

Consider the algebra

generated by the products

Clearly,

is an ideal in

and, using the above Toeplitz map

where

is consisting of those sequences

for which

.

Similarly, define

I_{U,n}:=C^{*}(P_{k}M_{(n,x)}\mid k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},\,0\leq x<s^{n})\cong c_{0}\bigl({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},{\mathbb{C}}^{s^{n}}\bigr).

Then
I_{U,n}
is an ideal in
B_{U,n}
.

Consider the morphisms
\tilde{\alpha}_{U}
,
\tilde{\beta}_{U}
on

given by

(\tilde{\alpha}_{U}F)_{n}(x)=\begin{cases}f_{n-1}(x-1)&\textrm{if }n\geq 0,\\
0&\textrm{if }n=0\end{cases}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\bigl(\tilde{\beta}_{U}F\bigr)_{n}(x)=f_{n+1}(x+1).

A simple calculation shows that we have the following relations:

\alpha_{U}(T_{U}(F))=T_{U}\bigl(\tilde{\beta}_{U}F\bigr)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\beta_{U}(T_{U}(F))=T_{U}(\tilde{\alpha}_{U}F).

It follows that the ideals
I_{U}
and
I_{U,n}
are
\alpha_{U}
and
\beta_{U}
invariant.

We have the following short exact sequence

0\to c_{0}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s}))\to c({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s}))\to C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})\to 0,

where the quotient map is

.
It follows that the factor algebra

is isomorphic to
C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})

B_{U}/I_{U}\cong C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})
.

The morphisms

preserve the ideal

and so they factor to morphisms on

which are exactly the automorphisms

and

. We also get

4.3

From

It is easily seen that

is the kernel of this representation

Next, using a convenient representation, we deduce the structure of the ideal

as described in the following statement.

Proposition 4.1

Let
\mathcal{K}
be the

-algebra of compact operators on a separable Hilbert space.
We get the following isomorphisms of

-algebras

We faithfully represent

in

is zero. In the above short exact sequence, this corresponds to the fact that the diagonal subgroup of
K_{0}(\mathcal{I}_{U,n})
gives a zero class in
K_{0}(A_{U,n})
. Thus, we choose
[M_{(n,x)}P_{0}]
with
x\neq 0
for generators of the range of the map
{{\mathbb{Z}}^{s^{n}}/{\mathbb{Z}}\to K_{0}(A_{U,n})}
. To summarize, we get the following explicit description of
K_{0}(A_{U,n})

K_{0}(A_{U,n})\cong\Biggl\{\sum_{j=1}^{s^{n}-1}c_{j}[M_{(n,j)}P_{0}]+k[M_{(n,0)}]\mid c_{j},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\Biggr\}.

(4.4)

With those preliminaries we can now compute the maps
\phi_{n}\colon K_{0}(A_{U,n})\to K_{0}(A_{U,n+1})
. Notice that the
M_{(n,x)}
’s are indicators for balls in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{s}
so we can decompose these indicators as

M_{(n,x)}=\sum_{l=0}^{s-1}M_{(n+1,x+ls^{n})},

each of which are mutually orthogonal projections. Thus an element in equation (
4.4
) can be written as

\sum_{j=1}^{s^{n}-1}c_{j}[M_{(n,j)}P_{0}]+k[M_{(n,0)}]=\sum_{j=1}^{s^{n}-1}\sum_{l=0}^{s-1}c_{j}[M_{(n+1,j+ls^{n})}P_{0}]+k\sum_{l=0}^{s-1}[M_{(n+1,ls^{n})}].

(4.5)

To compute
\phi_{n}
, we need to write the above as combinations of generators of
K_{0}(A_{U,n+1})
as in equation (
4.4
).

Notice that by Murray–von Neumann equivalence

\displaystyle[M_{(n+1,0)}]

\displaystyle=\big[U^{ls^{n}}M_{(n+1,0)}(U^{*})^{ls^{n}}\big]=\big[M_{(n+1,ls^{n})}U^{ls^{n}}(U^{*})^{ls^{n}}\big]

\displaystyle=[M_{(n+1,ls^{n})}]-\big[M_{(n+1,ls^{n})}(I-U^{ls^{n}}(U^{*})^{ls^{n}})\big].

Thus equation (
4.5
) may be written as

\sum_{j=1}^{s^{n}-1}\sum_{l=0}^{s-1}c_{j}[M_{(n+1,j+ls^{n})}P_{0}]+ks[M_{(n+1,0)}]+k\sum_{l=1}^{s-1}\big[M_{(n+1,ls^{n})}(I-U^{ls^{n}}(U^{*})^{ls^{n}})\big].

(4.6)

Further, notice that for
l>0

I-U^{ls^{n}}(U^{*})^{ls^{n}}=P_{0}+P_{1}+\cdots+P_{ls^{n}-1}=\sum_{i=0}^{ls^{n}-1}U^{i}P_{0}(U^{*})^{i}.

By using Murray–von Neumann equivalence, the last sum in equation (
4.6
) is equal to

k\sum_{l=1}^{s-1}\sum_{i=0}^{ls^{n}-1}\big[M_{(n+1,ls^{n})}U^{i}P_{0}(U^{*})^{i}\big]=k\sum_{l=1}^{s-1}\sum_{i=0}^{ls^{n}-1}[M_{(n+1,ls^{n}-i)}P_{0}]

and, reindexing
ls^{n}-i\to i
, we get equation (
4.6
) as

\sum_{j=1}^{s^{n}-1}\sum_{l=0}^{s-1}c_{j}[M_{(n+1,j+ls^{n})}P_{0}]+ks[M_{(n+1,0)}]+k\sum_{l=1}^{s-1}\sum_{i=1}^{ls^{n}}[M_{(n+1,i)}P_{0}].

Swapping the second double sum in the above equation, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{s^{n}-1}\sum_{l=0}^{s-1}c_{j}[M_{(n+1,j+ls^{n})}P_{0}]+ks[M_{(n+1,0)}]+k\sum_{i=1}^{s^{n}-1}(s-1)[M_{(n+1,i)}P_{0}]

\displaystyle\qquad{}+k\sum_{i=s^{n}}^{(s-1)s^{n}}\left(s-\left\lceil\dfrac{i}{s^{n}}\right\rceil\right)[M_{(n+1,i)}P_{0}].

Reindexing, we write this formula as

\sum_{i=0}^{s^{n+1}-1}c_{i}^{\prime}[M_{(n+1,i)}P_{0}]+k^{\prime}[M_{(n+1,0)}],

where
k^{\prime}=ks
and

c_{i}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}c_{(i\bmod s^{n})}+k(s-1),&\text{if }1\leq i<s^{n},\\
c_{(i\bmod s^{n})}+k\left(s-\left\lceil\dfrac{i}{s^{n}}\right\rceil\right),&\text{if }s^{n}\leq i\leq(s-1)s^{n},\\
c_{(i\bmod s^{n})},&\text{if }(s-1)s^{n}<i<s^{n+1}.\end{cases}

(4.7)

Thus the map
\phi_{n}\colon K_{0}(A_{U,n})\to K_{0}(A_{U,n+1})
, using the identification
K_{0}(A_{U,n})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{s^{n}}
from equation (
4.4
), is given by

\phi_{n}((c_{1},\dots,c_{s^{n}-1},k))=(c_{1}^{\prime},\dots,c_{s^{n+1}-1}^{\prime},k^{\prime}).

As a consequence, we get the following proposition.

Proposition 4.5
.

The
K
-theory of
A_{U}
is given by

K_{0}(A_{U})=\lim\limits_{\longrightarrow}{\mathbb{Z}}^{s^{n}}

with maps
\phi_{n}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{s^{n}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}^{s^{n+1}}
given by

\phi_{n}((c_{1},\dots,c_{s^{n}-1},k))=(c_{1}^{\prime},\dots,c_{s^{n+1}-1}^{\prime},ks),

where
c_{i}^{\prime}
are described in equation (
4.7
).

6
The Bernoulli shift

6.1
The algebra
\boldsymbol{A_{S}}

Recall that the Bernoulli shift
S\colon H\to H
is defined as

SE_{(n,x)}=\frac{1}{s}\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}E_{(n+1,sx+j)}.

In this section, we study the algebra

A_{S}=C^{*}(S,M_{f}\mid f\in C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s}))
.
The corresponding maps on
A_{S}
are

\alpha_{S}(a):=SaS^{*}\in A_{S}
and
\beta_{S}(a):=S^{*}aS\in A_{S}
,
for
a\in A_{S}
.

Like in the other cases we have maps
\mathfrak{a}_{S},\mathfrak{b}_{S}\colon C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})\to C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})
defined by

\mathfrak{a}_{S}f(x)=f\left(\frac{x-x\textrm{ mod }s}{s}\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathfrak{b}_{S}f(x)=\frac{1}{s}\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}f(sx+j).

It is clear that
\mathfrak{a}_{S}
is an endomorphism but
\mathfrak{b}_{S}
is not a homomorphism as it was before, however
\mathfrak{b}_{S}
is positive and linear. We also have that
\mathfrak{b}_{S}(I)=I=\mathfrak{a}_{S}(I)

\mathfrak{a}_{S}
has trivial kernel.
We also have that
\mathfrak{b}_{S}\circ\mathfrak{a}_{S}=I
and that
\Ran\mathfrak{b}_{S}=C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})
.

Given
f\in C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})
,
S
and
M_{f}
satisfy the following relations

\beta_{S}(M_{f})=M_{\mathfrak{b}_{S}f}

SM_{f}=M_{\mathfrak{a}_{S}f}S

M_{f}S
is not equal to

.

For
j=0,1,\dots,s-1

\chi_{j}
be the characteristic function for the ball in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{s}
with center
j
and radius
s^{-1}

\chi_{j}(x)=\begin{cases}1&\textrm{if }x\textrm{ mod }s=j,\\
0&\textrm{else.}\end{cases}

Notice that for each
j
,
\chi_{j}
is locally constant and hence is a continuous function on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{s}
, see
[
26
]
. We define operators

(6.1)

By
[
12
, Proposition 6.2]

C^{*}
-algebras

A_{S}=C^{*}(S_{j}\mid j=0,\dots,s-1)

Two key relations between the
S_{j}

S_{j}^{*}S_{k}=\delta_{jk}I\quad\text{for all}\ j,k=0,\dots,s-1\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}S_{j}S_{j}^{*}=I-P_{(0,0)},

with the Cuntz–Toeplitz algebra, see
[
12
]
.

It is clear that

is a diagonal compact operator, and in fact by
[
12

The coefficient algebra of
\boldsymbol{A_{S}}

Since

(see

K_{1}(A_{S})\cong 0
.
From the
6

K_{0}(A_{S})
,

0\to K_{0}(\mathcal{K}(H))\to K_{0}(A_{S})\to K_{0}(\mathcal{O}_{s})\to 0.

Recall that
K_{0}(\mathcal{K}(H))\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
, and is generated by the class of any rank
1

[P_{(0,0)}]_{0}=\left[I-\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}S_{i}S_{i}^{*}\right]_{0}.

On the other hand, we have

K_{0}(\mathcal{O}_{s})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/(s-1){\mathbb{Z}}

and it is generated by the class of the identity
[I]_{0}

and

is generated by
[I]_{0}

The Serre shift

7.1
The algebra
\boldsymbol{A_{W}}

In this final section, the goal is to study the algebra

A_{W}=C^{*}(W,M_{f}\mid f\in C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s}))
.
Recall that the Serre shift is given by

WE_{(n,x)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}E_{(n+1,x+js^{n})}.

Consider the algebra
\mathcal{F}
of functions on
\mathcal{V}
defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}:=\{F\colon\mathcal{V}\to{\mathbb{C}}\mid\textrm{there is}\,f_{F}\in C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s}),\,\textrm{so that}\,\lim_{n}\underset{0\leq x<s^{n}}{\textrm{sup }}|F(n,x\textrm{ mod }s^{n})-f_{F}(x)|=0\}.

If
F\in\mathcal{F}
, we define
the operator
M_{F}
acting on
\ell^{2}(\mathcal{V})
by

M_{F}E_{(n,x)}=F(n,x)E_{(n,x)}
.
By
[
12
, Proposition 7.2]
, for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
we have
M_{F}\in A_{W}
. Additionally, by
[
12
, Proposition 7.3]
, the
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(W,M_{F})
generated by
W
and
M_{F}
with
F\in\mathcal{F}
is equal to
A_{W}
.

If
F\in\mathcal{F}
, we define

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{W}F(n,x)=\begin{cases}F\bigl(n-1,x\textrm{ mod }s^{n-1}\bigr)&\textrm{if }n\geq 1,\\
0&\textrm{else},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}_{W}F(n,x)=\frac{1}{s}\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}F(n+1,x+js^{n}).

With those definitions, we get the following useful relations

\beta_{W}(M_{F})=M_{\mathfrak{b}_{W}F}
and
WM_{F}=M_{\mathfrak{a}_{W}F}W
.

7.2
The coefficient algebra of
\boldsymbol{A_{W}}

Consider the mutually orthogonal projections
\{\mathcal{P}_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}}
given by

\mathcal{P}_{0}=I-WW^{*}
and
\mathcal{P}_{n}=W^{n}\mathcal{P}_{0}(W^{*})^{n}
.
By
[
12
, Proposition 7.8]
, we know that the
C^{*}
-algebra
B_{W}
generated by
M_{F}
’s and
\{\mathcal{P}_{n}\}

B_{W}:=C^{*}(M_{F},\mathcal{P}_{n}\mid F\in\mathcal{F},\,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})

is a coefficient algebra of
A_{W}
.

It was noticed in
[
12
, Propositions 7.4 and 7.8]
that the algebra
\mathcal{K}(H)_{\mathrm{inv}}
of gauge invariant compact operators is a subalgebra, in fact an ideal of
B_{W}
. Thus, we take

I_{W}:=\mathcal{K}(H)_{\mathrm{inv}}

and we see from the definitions that the ideal
I_{W}
is
\alpha_{W}
and
\beta_{W}
invariant.

The space
c({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s}))
of uniformly convergent sequences of continuous functions on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{s}
appeared in the discussion of the algebra
A_{U}
and it also is needed here. Consider the following Toeplitz-like map:

c({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s}))\ni G\mapsto T_{W}(G):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{n}(M_{g_{n}}-M_{g_{\infty}})\mathcal{P}_{n}+M_{g_{\infty}}\in B_{W},

where
g_{\infty}
is defined in equation (
4.1
).
Then, by
[
12
, Proposition 7.7]
every
a\in B_{W}
can be uniquely written as

a=T_{W}(G)+c
,
for some
c\in\mathcal{K}(H)_{\mathrm{inv}}
and
G\in c({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s}))
. It follows, see also
[
12
, Proposition 7.8]
, that the factor algebra
B_{W}/I_{W}
is isomorphic to
c({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s}))
,

B_{W}/I_{W}\cong c({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s}))
.

By
[
12
, Proposition 7.8]
, we have the following relation

\alpha_{W}(T_{W}(G))=T_{W}(G^{\prime})+c
,
where
c
is compact and
G^{\prime}=(g_{n}^{\prime})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}}
with
g_{n}^{\prime}=g_{n-1}
and
taking
g_{-1}=0
.
It follows that the factor endomorphism
[\alpha_{W}]
on
B_{W}/I_{W}\cong c({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s}))\cong c({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})\otimes C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})
is identified with endomorphism
\alpha\otimes I
on
c({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})\otimes C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})
where
\alpha\colon c({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})\to c({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})
is a monomorphism given by

\alpha((x_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty})=(x_{n-1})_{n=0}^{\infty}

(7.1)

with
x_{-1}:=0
.

7.3
The structure of
\boldsymbol{A_{W}}

The classical and motivating example of a crossed product by an endomorphism is the Toeplitz algebra
[
6
]

\mathcal{T}=c({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{N}}

with
\alpha
defined in equation (
7.1
). The goal is to relate
A_{W}
with the above Toeplitz algebra.

Let
\mathcal{I}_{W}:=C^{*}(aW^{n}\mid a\in I_{W},\,n=0,1,\dots)
. Then from Section
3
, we know that
\mathcal{I}_{W}
is an
\alpha_{W}
and
\beta_{W}
invariant ideal in
A_{W}
.
We have the following observation.

Proposition 7.1
.

The
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{I}_{W}
is equal to the
C^{*}
-algebra of compact operators on
H

\mathcal{I}_{W}=\mathcal{K}(H).

Proof.

Clearly,
\mathcal{I}_{W}\subseteq\mathcal{K}(H)
. Consider the matrix units for the canonical base of
H

P_{(n,x),(m,y)}E_{(k,z)}=\begin{cases}E_{(n,x)},&(m,y)=(k,z),\\
0,&\text{else}.\end{cases}

They generate
\mathcal{K}(H)
and by
[
12
, Proposition 7.4]
,
\mathcal{K}(H)\subseteq A_{W}
, so
P_{(n,x),(m,y)}\in A_{W}
.
Computing the gauge action on the matrix units gives

\rho_{\theta}(P_{(n,x),(m,y)})={\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}\theta(n-m)}P_{(n,x),(m,y)}.

If
n>m
, we can write

P_{(n,x),(m,y)}=P_{(n,x),(m,y)}(W^{*})^{n-m}W^{n-m}

and notice that
P_{(n,x),(m,y)}(W^{*})^{n-m}
is gauge invariant and compact. A similar argument works for
n<m
. It follows that
P_{(n,x),(m,y)}\in\mathcal{I}_{W}
.
∎

We now consider the factor algebra
A_{W}/\mathcal{I}_{W}
, which is isomorphic to the crossed product of
B_{W}/I_{W}\cong c({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})\otimes C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})
by the monomorphism
\alpha\otimes I
.

Proposition 7.2
.

We have an isomorphism of
C^{*}
-algebras

A_{W}/\mathcal{I}_{W}\cong\mathcal{T}\otimes C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})
,
where
\mathcal{T}
is the Toeplitz algebra.

Proof.

This result can be inferred from general considerations on tensor products of crossed products. However, we give a direct argument specific to our situation slightly modifying the arguments from Proposition
4.1
.

If
\{E_{k,l}\}
is the canonical basis in
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})
, then a faithful representation of the crossed product
c({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s}))\rtimes_{\alpha\otimes I}{\mathbb{N}}
is given by operators
\mathcal{U}
and
\widehat{M}_{F}
for functions
F=(f_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}}\in c({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s}))
given by the following formulas:

\mathcal{U}E_{k,l}=E_{k,l+1},\qquad\mathcal{U}^{*}E_{k,l}=\begin{cases}E_{k,l-1}&\textrm{if }l\geq 1,\\
0&\textrm{if }l=0,\end{cases}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widehat{M}_{F}E_{k,l}=f_{l}(k)E_{k,l}.

It is easy to verify that these operators satisfy the defining relations for the crossed product. Additionally, as before, the operators
\mathbb{U}_{\theta}
from equation (
4.2
) implement the gauge action in this representation. Since
F\mapsto\widehat{M}_{F}
is faithful, it follows that the above representation is a faithful representation of the crossed product
c({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s}))\rtimes_{\alpha\otimes I}{\mathbb{N}}
.

Since
c({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s}))\cong c({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})\otimes C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})
, finite linear combinations of functions of the form
fc
with
f\in C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})
and
c\in c({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})
are dense in
c({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})\otimes C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})
.
We get a factorization

\widehat{M}_{fc}E_{k,l}=f(k)c(l)E_{k,l}.

Denoting

\mu_{f}E_{k,l}=f(k)E_{k,l}
and
\nu_{c}E_{k,l}=c(l)E_{k,l}
,
we see that
\widehat{M}_{fc}=\mu_{f}\nu_{c}
. Moreover, for all
{f\in C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})}
and all
c\in c({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})
the operators
\mu_{f}
commute with
\nu_{c}
and with
\mathcal{U}
. Clearly,

C^{*}(\mu_{f}\mid f\in C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s}))\cong C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})

and, since both
\mathcal{U}
and
\nu_{c}
depend only on the second coordinate
l\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
, we have

C^{*}(\mathcal{U}^{m}\nu_{c}\mid m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},\,c\in c({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}))\cong\mathcal{T}\bigl(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})\bigr),

where
\mathcal{T}\bigl(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})\bigr)
is the Toeplitz algebra of operators in
B\bigl(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})\bigr)
. Finally, this implies that

\displaystyle A_{W}/\mathcal{I}_{W}

\displaystyle\cong C^{*}\big(\mathcal{U}^{m}\widehat{M}_{F}\mid m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},\,F\in c({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s}))\big)

\displaystyle=C^{*}\big(\mathcal{U}^{m}\nu_{c}\mu_{f}\mid m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},\,c\in c({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}),\,f\in C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})\big)\cong C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})\otimes\mathcal{T}\bigl(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})\bigr).

∎

The main result of this section in the theorem below is then a direct consequence of equation (
3.5
), Propositions

and
7.2
.

Theorem 7.3
.

We have the short exact sequence of
C^{*}
-algebras

0\rightarrow\mathcal{K}\rightarrow A_{W}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}\otimes C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})\rightarrow 0.

\displaystyle K_{1}(\mathcal{T}\otimes C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s}))\cong(K_{0}(\mathcal{T})\otimes K_{1}(C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})))\oplus(K_{1}(\mathcal{T})\otimes K_{0}(C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s}))).

The

.
Using this, we can compute

from the

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{0}(\mathcal{K})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{0}(A_{W})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{0}(\mathcal{T}\otimes C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{1}(\mathcal{T}\otimes C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s}))}

Since both

To compute

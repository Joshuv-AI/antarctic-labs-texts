Enhancing environmental sustainability in Somalia through water conservation, pollution reduction, energy saving, and waste management efficiency
Authors: Bile Abdisalan Nor, Zakarie Abdi Warsame, Abdikarim Abdullahi Farah, Zakaria Hassan Mohamed, Mohamed Ali Farah
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSomalia faces severe environmental challenges, including soil erosion, deforestation, overgrazing, and land degradation, which diminish agricultural productivity and exacerbate food insecurity. Vulnerable populations are increasingly affected by droughts, floods, and climate-related disasters that limit livelihood opportunities and threaten long-term sustainability. This study investigates the key factors contributing to environmental sustainability in Somalia. The research data for this study were gathered using a structured questionnaire developed based on previous studies to collect primary data from the targeted respondents. A survey was conducted with 240 respondents, of whom 50(20.8) completed it online, and 190(79.80) participated face-to-face. The data collected were analyzed using SmartPLS. The findings reveal that Conservation of Water, Reduction of Pollution, Saving Energy, and Waste Management Efficiency are all positively and significantly related to Environmental sustainability in Somalia. These results highlight the importance of effective water use, pollution reduction, energy conservation, and efficient waste management in mitigating ecological damage. The study suggests that sustainable environmental practices can improve resilience to climate-related impacts and support broader development goals. The practical implications of this research encourage policymakers, environmental agencies, and stakeholders to implement targeted strategies aimed at enhancing environmental protection. A stronger focus on these factors can contribute significantly to promoting sustainability and improving the quality of life for communities across Somalia.Keywords: Environmental sustainability, Sustainable development, Conservation of water, Reduction of pollution, Savings energy, Waste management efficiency, SomaliaSubject terms: Climate sciences, Environmental sciences, Environmental social sciences, Environmental studies

Introduction

Climate change is one of the twenty-first century’s most pressing challenges for human beings1. It may affect the environment, including increased temperatures, sea level rise, drought, floods, and more. These events impact everything we rely on and value, including water, electricity, transportation, wildlife, agriculture, ecosystems, and human health.

Environmental protection has become a critical global concern2. It contributes to several important goals in society, including long-term sustainability, a cleaner environment, less climate change, and better food3. It also develops new sectors and technologies, which may improve economic performance in the long term4.

Environmental sustainability refers to the appropriate use of natural resources to satisfy current needs while ensuring future generations’ capacity to meet their own. It balances environmental, economic, and social objectives, such as lowering carbon emissions, increasing renewable energy, and guaranteeing fair resource access. Environmental degradation due to human activities refers to the deterioration of natural resources, including water, oxygen, and soil, resulting from human activities5. Policymakers and academic communities have been paying significant attention to the growing issue of environmental degradation6.

Due to its significant local and global impacts, environmental protection is critical in Somalia. An increased rise in carbon dioxide emissions promotes global warming and climate change, which affect food production7. Somalia suffers from severe land degradation due to soil erosion, deforestation, and overgrazing. These environmental pressures limit agricultural production, worsening food insecurity8. Somalia is one of the nations experiencing the most severe effects of climate change. The vulnerable population suffers from the consequences of drought and floods, deteriorating ecosystems, and unsustainable resource usage9. The nation is dealing with catastrophic weather conditions that have significantly affected communities around the country. Climate change in Somalia has significantly affected people’s livelihoods.

The collapse of the state and governance systems has significantly influenced the Somali climate. Furthermore, Somalia’s most significant environmental difficulties are connected to environmental deterioration and the collapse of state and governance institutions in all their forms. The environment of Somalia is the subject of a limited amount of scientific knowledge and research because the country has been at war for decades.

In addition, studying the Factors that influence the protection of the environment for sustainability in Somalia provides valuable insights into broader environmental impacts, such as soil degradation and water scarcity. These factors are crucial for sustainable agricultural practices and environmental management. By tackling environmental concerns, Somalia may contribute to the sustainability of its agricultural economy5. Finally, empirical evidence on the Factors that influence the protection of the environment for sustainability is essential for formulating effective policy measures. Such results may help to influence plans for reducing environmental problems and implementing adaptation approaches to prevent climate change. Policymakers may utilize this information to create comprehensive strategies that seek economic and ecological goals, guaranteeing a balanced approach to growth.

Solid and liquid waste are significant environmental concerns in urban areas of Somalia, and waste generation is a major environmental issue. Furthermore, it has become increasingly clear that low environmental quality has negatively impacted human health in the entire country10. Rapid urbanization and the country’s lack of effective waste management systems create an increasingly solid and liquid waste problem10. Therefore, waste is a major environmental issue in Somalia, affecting the socio-economic conditions of the people and the health of people and animals. A well-managed waste collection and disposal system is necessary to address waste. Despite the importance of environmental protection, there is a lack of empirical evidence on this topic in the Somali context. To the best of our knowledge, no research has examined the relationships between Conservation of Water, Reduction of Pollution, Savings in Energy, Waste Management Efficiency, and Protection of the Environment in Somalia. A few studies have used the Reduction of pollution, waste management, and other factors to investigate factors influencing environmental sustainability11,12. However, these studies focus on countries other than Somalia, which faces severe land degradation due to soil erosion, deforestation, and overgrazing13. Given Somalia’s particular environmental challenges, examining the factors that influence environmental protection in the country’s specific context is crucial. Another study by has provided valuable insights into environmental sustainability, particularly examining the relationship between environmental degradation and factors like renewable energy consumption, GDP, FDI inflows, and population. A significant gap exists in understanding the specific determinants of environmental protection in Somalia. This study aims to address this gap by investigating the key factors that contribute to environmental protection for sustainability in Somalia. Specifically, it analyses how Conservation of Water affects environmental sustainability, evaluates the impact of the reduction of pollution on environmental sustainability, examines the influence of Saving Energy on environmental sustainability, and examines the impact of waste management efficiency on environmental sustainability. In line with these objectives, the study addresses the following questions: how does the conservation of Water affect environmental sustainability in Somalia? What is the impact of the reduction of pollution on environmental sustainability in Somalia? What is the influence of Saving Energy on environmental sustainability in Somalia? and the impact of waste management efficiency on environmental sustainability in Somalia?

This study contributes to the literature in several ways. Firstly, Environmental protection contributes to several important societal goals, including long-term sustainability, a cleaner environment, less climate change, and better food. To our knowledge, no research has examined this topic in the Somali context. The study fills an essential gap in the existing body of knowledge by examining the causal relationships between Conservation of Water, Reduction of pollution, Savings Energy, waste management efficiency, and environmental sustainability in Somalia. This allows a deeper understanding of how these variables interact with the Protection of the environment. Secondly, this study contributes to the existing empirical literature by presenting information on how the Conservation of Water interacts with environmental sustainability. These results highlight that utilizing water effectively to prevent unnecessary water consumption may help the environment. Finally, the study’s findings give policy-relevant insights for Somalia and other developing nations facing similar environmental challenges. Thus, the research has worldwide implications and adds to a more extensive discussion on sustainable development.

Literature review

Environmental Sustainability (ES) represents the attitudes and normative beliefs of people towards the protection of the environment and the practice of environmental sustainability for the current and future generations. Items measure both individual and policy-based orientation towards environmental sustainability. Based on the scale of the pro-environmental attitude concept14, ES represents the attitudinal orientation of individuals towards environmental protection.

Environmental Sustainability prevents unwanted changes to ecosystems and their constituent parts. It has become a critical global concern2. Environmental protection contributes to several important societal goals, including long-term sustainability, a cleaner environment, less climate change, and better food3. It develops new sectors and technologies, which may improve economic performance in the long term4. The concept of a circular economy, as discussed by15, offers a promising approach to environmental sustainability. This approach aims to reduce the extraction of natural resources, enhance waste management, and maximize the economic, social, and environmental value of products throughout their lifecycle. This literature review investigates the primary factors influencing environmental sustainability, including water conservation, pollution reduction, and energy savings.

Reduction of pollution and environmental sustainability

Pollution Reduction (ROP) represents the environmental beliefs and awareness of individuals regarding pollution-reducing behavior and activities. The concept is associated with the cognitive-beliefs orientation of pollution reduction and recognizes the association between people’s actions and pollution outcomes.

Environmental sustainability refers to natural systems’ ability to maintain their ecological integrity, biodiversity, and resource availability over time, despite man-made pressure16. One of the key challenges in this pressure is the problem of pollution, which covers issues of air pollution, water pollution, and even plastic pollution. This problem negatively affects the operation of ecosystems, poses risks to public health, and hinders sustainable development. The impact of pollution on environmental degradation is well-supported by empirical studies from various disciplines. In their study, Jiyinget al.17, through empirical analysis based on the CS-ARDL panel approach, found that green energy technology innovation can lower levels of carbon emission and particulate matter emission, whereas non-renewable energy use can lead to higher emissions. Such a study is useful for putting into context the problem of air pollution and sustainability in the perspective of African developing countries, where mechanisms of change are similar but often more pressing than in developed economies. Another study by18 demonstrates that comprehensive policy regulation can have a positive effect on improving air quality. Yet, enforcement capabilities are rarely equal throughout the entire world of developing countries, and mechanisms can fall apart in fragile states with poor infrastructures.

Another significant area where the challenge lies is that of water pollution, especially in Sub-Saharan Africa, due to the absence of adequate sanitation systems, agricultural runoff, and unregulated release of pollutants by industries. According to Jones et al., Sub-Saharan Africa will turn out to be the most significant hotspot for water pollution globally until 2100 AD because of population increase, urbanization, and poor sewage treatment systems. Furthermore19, highlighted that less than 10% of Sub-Saharan Africa’s wastewater was being treated before releasing it into the environment; therefore, most of the pollutants would enter freshwater and coastal ecosystems, leading to the destruction of biodiversity and the ecosystem on which the people depended.

The challenge of plastic pollution is also present because20, stated that 91% of global plastics were not being recycled, whereas African ecosystems received an outsized amount of plastics. Somalia has been threatened by this because of its long Indian Ocean coastlines.

Somali’s situation is radically different from that of high-income countries. It must be noted that pollution control in high-income countries depends mainly on regulation and mandatory emission standards, whereas in Somalia, until 2022, there was no proper body responsible for the same. Its degradation is driven not by industrial manufacturing emissions but by using traditional biomass sources, charcoal, and deforestation16.

Air pollution results from the combustion of fossil fuels, transportation, and industrial sites, which elevates temperatures and causes damage to the environment and human health. It has the potential to induce respiratory issues, including lung cancer and asthma. According to21 Fossil fuel combustion by-products are the world’s most significant threat to children’s health and a major contributor to global and environmental injustice. The most significant human-produced climate-altering greenhouse gas is carbon dioxide (CO2), which is composed of many noxious air pollutants. Similarly, water pollution results from the presence of harmful substances, which can negatively impact aquatic life and disturb the natural balance of ecosystems. Freshwater is a vital natural resource for drinking, cleaning, and cooking. However, water contamination by insecticides, heavy metals, plastics, and chemicals seriously threatens the environment22. It will also affect food security by contaminating the food supply chain, which commences with the procurement of basic materials and concludes with the production of final products.

Empirical studies emphasize the significance of pollution reduction in the pursuit of environmental sustainability. For example, Ali et al.23 demonstrates that the integration of Environmental Management Accounting procedures with pollution prevention strategies, including energy conservation and waste minimization, substantially enhances sustainability outcomes in small and medium-sized enterprises. Additionally, environmental proactivity contributes to these benefits. Du and Li24 evaluates the influence of environmental regulations on reducing pollution and collective emissions in Chinese industrial enterprises. Employing panel data and sophisticated modeling techniques, the investigation determines that environmental regulations substantially reduce carbon dioxide production and pollutant emissions. Both studies have emphasized that a key component of environmental sustainability is a reduction of pollution. They demonstrate that addressing pollution benefits the environment and human health while boosting economic and social consequences. The findings from this research have important implications for Somalia, as the country’s cities are experiencing rising pollution as a result of population expansion and rising energy use.

Plastic pollution is a serious worldwide concern, with 400 million tons of plastic generated yearly. Over 2,000 trash trucks dump plastic waste daily into our seas, rivers, and lakes, yet only a third of it is recycled25. Plastic pollution is a particularly urgent problem in Somalia, where growing urbanization and coastal contamination threaten the environment and human health. Furthermore, plastic production exacerbates the climate catastrophe by extensively relying on fossil fuels25. Numerous research studies have revealed efficient techniques for reducing plastic pollution by 80%, such as reusing and recycling, and investigating alternatives to plastic. Stoett et al.26 investigate its asymmetrical impacts on the Global South, which are further exacerbated by insufficient waste management and unequal trading practices. Their research advocates for “plastic justice,” which equates the pursuit of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) with promoting efficient refuse management. The localized initiatives in Brazil illustrate the potential of coordinated efforts to mitigate the detrimental effects of plastic pollution on environmental and socio-economic systems. Hence, we propose the following hypothesis:

H1

Reduction of pollution has a significant impact on Environmental sustainability.

Conservation of water and environmental sustainability

Conservation of Water (COW) is the measure of environmental beliefs of respondents regarding the importance of water conservation and its effect on ecological sustainability. Specifically, it captures the cognitive-belief dimension of water conservation, as respondents perceive the efficiency of water use to serve environmental ends. As indicated by the Value-Belief-Norm (VBN) model, Environmental beliefs concerning resource conservation are regarded as antecedents to environmental intentions27.

Water is an essential resource for the preservation of human activity and existence. It must be utilized and conserved efficiently. Approximately 2.2 billion individuals currently require access to pure drinking water services, despite the Sustainable Development Goal (SDG6) of ensuring water availability and sustainable management by 203028. Freshwater accounts for less than 2% of the Earth’s water supply, which is unevenly distributed and mostly salty29. The world is expected to experience a 40% decline in water supplies by 2050, increasing water-related diseases and disasters30.

In Somalia, where water scarcity is a significant problem, conservation efforts focus on sustainable management of surface and groundwater resources. The absence of permanent streams and the dry environment of the nation mean that the sole supply of water in the majority of Somalia is groundwater. Surface and groundwater resources are depleted as a result of poor water management. Therefore, the conjunctive use of surface and groundwater is a fundamental technique in water supply management that must be adopted to improve water resource growth, management, and conservation within a basin. Somalia implemented a water conservation and management policy to combat water scarcity, drought, and climate change. Key initiatives include installing solar-powered boreholes and building dams, and contracting for water infrastructure31. The solar-powered boreholes provide thousands of people with safe and consistent water32. This achievement highlights Somalia’s national priorities for improving lives, health, and economic opportunities. The national water conservation policy will assist the local water supply system in reducing demands, increasing system efficiency, and promoting sustainable water usage without compromising the future requirement for water to support continuous development31. Water resource management and water security still persist as important issues in many developing countries. In the context of Somalia, Ali et al.33 Confirmed that sustainable strategies such as Integrated Water Resource Management (IWRM) and rainwater harvesting are among the vital techniques that should be implemented to enhance water security.

Water conservation is the process that involves using water resources efficiently and responsibly to minimize waste, preserve supply, and safeguard the environment. It consists of reducing consumption and enhancing water recycling to maintain a consistent supply through these measures34. The literature underscores the significance of water conservation in fulfilling fundamental human requirements35, promoting environmental sustainability36 and addressing water scarcity37. Water conservation is crucial for developing the economy, public health, safety, and overall well-being, as it increases the availability of drinking water38.

Aina et al.39 argue that water conservation is influenced by various factors, including the growing demand for water due to urbanization, industry, and agriculture, the scarcity of freshwater resources, pollution from chemicals and trash, and soil erosion caused by heavy rainfall. These factors have presented challenges to the environment that must be addressed. Deforestation and urbanization may be the primary causes, as they reduce the soil’s water-holding capacity in higher areas40. Climate change exacerbates conservation efforts by facilitating saltwater intrusion and increasing evapotranspiration41. A study done by42 revealed that water conservation is the cornerstone of environmental sustainability. Caputo et al.43 revealed that water Conservation and Environmental Sustainability are influenced by people’s attitudes, emotions, and behaviors. The results of this study support the importance of emotions, perceptions of others’ attitudes, and defenses against susceptibility and future uncertainty regarding water scarcity. Ali et al.33 highlighted that water conservation methods include Integrated Water Resource Management (IWRM) and rainwater harvesting improves water availability and environmental sustainability. This research suggests using rainwater gathering to improve agricultural resilience in Somalia’s dry zones. The findings from this research have important implications for Somalia, since water scarcity is a rising concern in Somalia, particularly in the Juba and Shabelle river basins. Overreliance on upstream flows from Ethiopia, frequent droughts, insufficient infrastructure, and weak governance systems all contribute to chronic water shortages. Hence, we propose the following hypothesis:

H2

Conservation of water has a significant impact on Environmental sustainability.

Saving energy and environmental sustainability

Saving Energy (SE) can be defined as individual beliefs and awareness in relation to energy-saving technology and practices. These beliefs play an important role in the process of climate sustainability. Conceptualisation of this construct is grounded on the VBN theory27 and the notion of environmental awareness, which acknowledges that beliefs in regard to energy conservation serve as cognitive antecedents for sustainable energy consumption.

Saving energy improves the environment by lowering carbon dioxide levels and other hazardous pollutants in the atmosphere. Somalia has considerable renewable energy potential, notably in solar and wind power. Renewable energy sources such as solar and wind have less environmental impact. For many years, most Somalian families, both urban and rural, have relied on firewood and charcoal44,45. The nation’s energy comes from traditional biomass sources. Charcoal is the primary fuel used for domestic cooking in Somalia’s capital, Mogadishu, and other urban areas. Exports are legally prohibited, and its industrial uses are somewhat limited. The production of charcoal for local cooking had a detrimental effect on deforestation. The country’s deforestation has resulted in land deterioration, food insecurity, susceptibility to floods, and drought. Between 2000 and 2021, Somalia had an average yearly deforestation rate of 429,000 hectares. This equates to an average annual deforestation rate of 4.9%. Despite the potential worth of Somalia’s natural resources, its natural capital is now at risk. Deforestation and Land degradation are now estimated to be between 23 and 30%. Between 2000 and 2015, Somalia lost 147,704 square kilometers due to land degradation. Several factors, including soil erosion, ecological decline, and stream erosion, are responsible for this loss46. Simultaneously, Somalia has significant energy insecurity; the majority of its population relies on biomass energy to meet their needs45,47. The majority of Somali families cook using charcoal (47.9%) and firewood (41.3%). Gas and electricity are rarely used48,49. The use of traditional biomass, such as charcoal and firewood, as a major energy source can have a substantial environmental impact. When traditional biomass is used, it releases considerable volumes of greenhouse gas CO2 into the atmosphere. Global warming is exacerbated by greenhouse gases, which retain heat in the atmosphere50. Additionally, Somalia’s surface and groundwater resources are at risk due to climate change and the country’s rapidly expanding population. Another major issue is inadequate waste management. Currently, the majority of waste produced in Somalia is legally allocated to landfills that are controlled by Local Authorities. In some locations, all or a portion of the construction waste stream is illegally deposited on land or in natural drainage systems, including water, in violation of laws designed to safeguard people’s health, the economy, and the environment51. Mogadishu faces major issues due to inadequate waste management, resulting in environmental deterioration, public health hazards, and poor living circumstances52. There is still a lack of community involvement in waste management programs, reducing the effectiveness of existing projects and allowing inappropriate garbage dumping to persist.

Energy is essential for executing critical operations, including transportation, food preparation, water purification, movement, and communication53. However, growing global populations and economic development have significantly boosted energy use, increasing environmental challenges. Energy conservation is crucial for mitigating climate change, resolving energy-related issues, and improving environmental sustainability. Renewable energy technologies offer a significant opportunity to reduce greenhouse gas emissions and global warming by substituting conventional energy sources. Panwar et al.54 and Farghali et al.55 underscore the critical need for cutting-edge technology, innovative systems, and progressive policies to boost energy efficiency and conservation. This urgency is further exacerbated by the COVID-19 epidemic, geopolitical conflicts (e.g., the Ukraine crisis), and climate change.

While all energy sources have an environmental impact, improved energy efficiency has the potential to mitigate numerous adverse effects by reducing the use of resources and associated pollutants. Dincer and Rosen56 emphasizes the substantial correlation between environmental conservation and energy efficiency, arguing that efficient energy systems can simultaneously meet energy needs and reduce ecological impacts. This is consistent with international initiatives, such as the European Union’s sustainability objectives for 2050, prioritizing energy reduction and efficiency as essential environmental sustainability components55. Using renewable energy sources significantly reduces carbon dioxide emissions and other harmful contaminants, highlighting its important role in achieving sustainable development. This relationship is significant in the Somali context, since the majority of Somalis have historically depended on charcoal and firewood as their main energy sources. Deforestation was negatively impacted by the manufacturing of charcoal for regional cooking. Hence, we propose the following hypothesis:

H3

Saving Energy has a significant impact on Environmental sustainability.

Waste management and environmental sustainability

Waste Management Efficiency (WME) is a variable that implies that individual beliefs and attitudes towards the role of waste management practices in achieving environmental stability and preserving resources are captured. Conceptualization of the WME construct is based on the environmental attitude theory57 and the theory of pro-environmental beliefs27.

Waste is defined as unusable material. All waste disposal requirements must be met to ensure that hazardous waste is handled safely and environmentally sustainably12. E-waste is increasing at a rate of 20–25% annually. It is the most rapidly rising waste stream because of market expansion, replacement needs, and a high rate of obsolescence. Pollution prevention is a method that helps decrease hazardous and non-hazardous materials, equipment, and energy use58. Waste management or waste disposal encompasses the activities that occur from the moment of its creation to its ultimate disposal, including the storage, collection, transportation, processing, recycling, or treatment, and disposal of waste. Additionally, it involves the monitoring and regulation of the waste management process59.

Effective waste management strategies are crucial to environmental sustainability. Studies by12 Investigate the influence of e-waste management strategies on promoting environmental sustainability, particularly emphasizing the primary treatment procedures, e-waste generation, and awareness levels in educational institutions. The study illustrates a significant gap in the public’s comprehension of the processes involved in creating and processing e-waste. Nevertheless, extended producer responsibility has emerged as an effective tool for addressing e-waste issues. Despite outstanding efforts by competent authorities, the survey discovered that only a small percentage of the population is aware of effective e-waste disposal methods, emphasizing the need for more public education and targeted legislative measures.

The literature on environmental sustainability performance emphasizes the need to use indicators and metrics to track progress. Metrics, including carbon footprint, waste diversion rates, and resource efficiency, are widely used globally to evaluate environmental performance60. Organizations and societies may improve their environmental performance by incorporating recycled materials into industrial processes, using clean manufacturing techniques, and establishing sustainable administrative procedures. These techniques limit trash creation and pollution emissions, helping to preserve the environment61,62.

Empirical studies have consistently demonstrated that waste management practices significantly enhance environmental sustainability performance. For instance, Quartey et al.63 proved that waste management practices positively affect environmental performance. Similarly, 64 and65 found that sustainable waste practices reduce carbon footprints and improve circular economy initiatives. The findings of this previous research are especially noteworthy in nations like Somalia, where rapid urbanization and population increase have worsened waste management issues. Waste management is a big concern in most Somali cities, including Muqdishu. Waste management issues, particularly in Muqdishu, Somalia, are accompanied by a poor integrative process of handling and transporting waste from its source to disposal, which has an impact on people’s health and the sustainability of the built environment as a whole.

Furthermore, E-waste poses a substantial sustainability challenge for developing countries. Srivastava and Pathak66 suggest that fostering collaboration between the formal and informal sectors could substantially enhance the effectiveness of e-waste management practices. These partnerships can facilitate waste collection, processing, and recycling, thereby promoting a more comprehensive and efficient waste management ecosystem. The findings from these previous studies have important implications for Somalia, where Waste is one of the main environmental problems in Somalia. It has become more obvious that poor environmental quality has an adverse impact on human health across the nation. Hence, we propose the following hypothesis:

H4

Waste management has a significant impact on Environmental sustainability.

Materials and methods

Study design

This study aims to analyse the relationships between Conservation of Water (COW), Reduction of pollution (ROP), Savings Energy (SE), Waste Management Efficiency (WME), and Environmental Sustainability (ES). A comprehensive empirical study is proposed to address the identified research gaps. To ensure accuracy, this research used purposive sampling to select participants who met specific criteria, including their awareness and involvement in environmental protection practices. Additionally, the respondents were required to be at least 18 years old and residents of Somalia. To ensure eligibility, a screening question was utilized prior to survey administration, with only respondents who admitted to their awareness of and participation in environmental sustainability practices being included in the sample. The sampling frame is composed of individuals from key stakeholder groups in Muqdishu, such as municipal offices, environmental non-governmental organizations, universities, and designated corporate communities that are involved in environmental management activities. Muqdisgu was chosen as the study area because it is the capital city and the greatest urban center in Somalia, the region with the most severe environmental challenges. Inadequate waste management poses severe issues for the city, resulting in environmental deterioration, public health threats, and bad living circumstances52. The sample size for this study is 300. The recommended minimum sample size for this study is 92, which was calculated using G*power, a program to calculate the required sample size recommended by67. The sample size estimation was based on a linear multiple regression model (fixed model, R² deviation from zero), which was appropriate for testing the relationship between multiple variables. The parameters utilized in this calculation were based on four independent variables with F-square values of 0.073, 0.09, 0.061, and 0.125, an alpha level of 0.05, and a target power of 0.80. A total of 300 questionnaires were distributed to respondents. Out of 260 questionnaires were returned. However, after filtering out the responses, 20 respondents were excluded due to missing data. Therefore, 240 data-valid questionnaires were retained for the final analysis, resulting in an effective response rate of 80%. According to68 A minimum sample size ensures the model’s results are robust and generalizable. Thus, the final sample size for this study is 240. Survey instrument and data collection

A structured questionnaire was employed to gather data from respondents in Somalia regarding their awareness and involvement in environmental sustainability practices. Before the main study, a pilot test was conducted using a convenience sample of 50 respondents not included in the primary survey. According to the pilot test results, all of the variables, including conservation of water (COW), reduction of pollution (ROP), savings energy (SE), waste management efficiency (WME), and environmental sustainability (ES) have Cronbach’s alphas of 0.706, 0.720, 0.754, 0.807, and 0.793, respectively. At the pilot test stage, no items were removed due to the fact that all items exhibited satisfactory reliability levels. However, during the main data analysis, certain items were later deleted due to low factor loadings. All constructs in this study are operationalized at the cognitive-belief level, capturing respondents’ environmental beliefs and awareness as the antecedents to environmental intentions. Specifically, this is based on the Value-Belief-Norm (VBN) framework27 and the theory of environmental knowledge69, which suggests that environmental beliefs are cognitive predecessors to environmental behavior. All measures were constructed using a 5-point Likert scale (ranging from 1 “Strongly Disagree” to 5 “Strongly Agree”). All items were grouped in 5 blocks as follows: COW 5 items, ES 5 items, ROP 5 items, SE 5 items, and WME 5 items, demonstrating the constructs included in the theoretical model (Table). First, demographic profiles of the respondents were collected, which encompassed their gender, age, education, and employment status. The study variables, which included Conservation of Water (COW), Reduction of pollution (ROP), Savings Energy (SE), waste management efficiency (WME), and Environmental Sustainability (ES), were measured in the second section. All the variables are confirmed to be reliable in that the Cronbach’s alpha of the variables, such as Conservation of Water (COW), Reduction of pollution (ROP), Savings Energy (SE), waste management efficiency (WME), and Environmental Sustainability (ES), are 0.806, 0.750, 0.854, 0.857, and 0.893, respectively. Data collection was obtained through a survey conducted between December 2024 and April 2025 in Mogadishu, Somalia. Although the data collection period was five months, it was anticipated that seasonal variations would not affect the perceptions of respondents, as the study concentrates on attitudinal variables that remain consistent despite short-term seasonal fluctuations. Respondents were recruited from universities, environmental groups, and public institutions to ensure access to individuals aware of environmental sustainability practices. Conducting a survey in Muqdishu created numerous obstacles, such as transportation constraints and difficulty reaching certain respondents. Both online and physical methods were used to overcome these obstacles. The research data for this study were gathered using a structured questionnaire developed based on previous studies to collect primary data from the targeted respondents. The questionnaires were distributed to individuals aged 18 and above who were involved in environmental protection practices. All methods were conducted according to relevant guidelines and regulations. The research protocol, survey tools, and consent process were approved by the Ethics Committee of the Centre for Research and Development at SIMAD University (Approval Number: EC000137). Informed consent was obtained from all respondents before data collection. Participation was voluntary, and all respondents were assured of the confidentiality and anonymity of their responses.

Data analysis

The data gathered were analyzed utilizing SmartPLS version 3, a sophisticated statistical software designed for partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM), which is well-suited for investigating the relationships among variables within the study. The validity and reliability of the measurement scales were evaluated through measurement model assessment (outer model evaluation) using PLS-SEM, while hypotheses were examined using structural equation modelling (SEM). Consequently, this research utilised a measurement model (CFA) to assess the validity and reliability of the data. Additionally, SEM was employed to quantify and test the hypothesised relationships among the Conservation of Water, Reduction of pollution, Savings of Energy, Waste Management Efficiency, and Environmental Sustainability.

Data analysis and discussions

Respondents’ profile

The demographic profile of the respondents is summarised in Table 2. Regarding the total number of respondents, 55.83% are male and 44.17% are female. This result demonstrates that, in Somalia, males participate in public activities, the workforce, and higher education at a higher rate than females. Discussion

In Somalia, environmental protection is essential because of its profound effects on the local and global levels. Growing carbon dioxide emissions contribute to climate change and global warming, which have an impact on food production. Somalia experiences significant land degradation resulting from soil erosion, deforestation, and overgrazing. Food insecurity is exacerbated by these environmental factors that restrict agricultural output. This study investigates the key factors contributing to environmental sustainability in Somalia. Regarding the findings presented in Table 7, the reduction of pollution (ROP) positively affects environmental sustainability. This indicates that for every unit change in ROP, the Environmental sustainability increases by 0.075 units (see Table 7). The findings indicate that improvements in pollution reduction are linked to a small but favourable rise in environmental sustainability in Somalia. Reduced pollution promotes environmental sustainability in Somalia since the country’s economy is mainly reliant on agriculture77. Reduced pollution improves the quality of the air, water, and soil, increasing the sustainability of the environment. Moreover, reduced pollution, for example. CO2 emissions lessen the effects of climate change and environmental deterioration. These findings have important implications for Somalia, as the country’s cities are experiencing rising pollution as a result of population expansion and rising energy use. Somalia faces pressing environmental challenges, including air pollution, Plastic pollution, and deforestation. Plastic pollution is a particularly urgent problem in Somalia, where growing urbanisation and coastal contamination threaten the environment and human health. Furthermore, plastic production exacerbates the climate catastrophe by extensively relying on fossil fuels. Reduction of pollution leads to cleaner air, healthier soil, and a stable climate, benefiting both human and natural ecosystems. It also improves the quality of life for all humankind. Land degradation in Somalia has persisted at an alarming and escalating rate over the past two decades. The primary causes of this land degradation are unsustainable land use practices, such as deforestation, charcoal production, and overgrazing. With the help of the international community, the government of Somalia is focusing on developing and implementing a national plan 2025–2029 for the prevention of climate change pollution, including promoting sustainable practices in key sectors of agriculture, energy, and waste management. This study’s findings that pollution reduction improves environmental protection are in line with earlier research conducted in developing nations. For example, a study by24 discovered that effective environmental policies can reduce pollution while improving overall environmental results. Additionally, Ali et al.23 shows that integrating Environmental Management Accounting methods with pollution control strategies, such as energy conservation and waste minimization, significantly improves sustainability outcomes.

Our second hypothesis is that the Conservation of Water (COW) has a positive and statistically significant effect on environmental sustainability. This result shows that for every unit change in the Conservation of Water (COW), the Environmental sustainability increases by 0.254 units (see Table 7). This result indicates that, in Somalia, where water scarcity is a significant problem, conservation efforts improve water availability and environmental sustainability using rainwater gathering to improve agricultural resilience in Somalia’s dry zones. There is a severe water shortage experienced in Somalia due to climate change, deforestation, and a lack of water management facilities. The substantial influence of the conservation of water on environmental sustainability noted in the study aligns with evidence from comparable dryland regions in Sub-Saharan Africa in which water harvesting, water-conservative farming practices, and sustainable water management systems have contributed positively to plant life, groundwater recharging, and agriculture78. Climate change exacerbates water scarcity through changes in rainfall patterns, increased temperature, and evaporation, leading to droughts and floods. The beneficial effects of water conservation may be due to the fact that Somalia’s environmental sustainability can be enhanced by a modest increase in water conservation. The importance of water conservation in meeting basic human needs, promoting environmental sustainability36, and managing water shortages37. Water conservation is critical for economic development, public health, safety, and general well-being since it enhances the supply of drinking water38. In Somalia, where water shortage is a significant concern, water conservation seeks to sustainably manage the freshwater supply, safeguard the hydrosphere, and fulfill both present and future human demands. Water conservation prevents water shortages. It encompasses all policies, plans, and actions to achieve these objectives. Population, household size, and growth all impact water use. This study’s findings that Conservation of Water improves environmental sustainability are in line with earlier research conducted in developing nations. For example, Mahmoud and Abdallh36 concludes that water conservation has a positive and statistically significant effect on Environmental sustainability.

With regards to Saving Energy, we find that Saving Energy (SE) has a positive effect on Environmental Sustainability. This shows that for every unit change in Savings Energy (SE), the Environmental sustainability increases by 0.248 units (see Table 7). This result demonstrates that saving energy helps environmental sustainability in Somalia, because a lot of the energy in Somalia comes from charcoal and biomass. Thus, saving energy lowers the need for these resources, which promotes environmental sustainability and lessens land damage. Saving energy also lessens the need for conventional biomass sources, which minimizes deforestation and encourages the use of renewable energy sources, improving environmental sustainability. The positive effect on Environmental Sustainability may be due to the fact that Somalia’s environmental sustainability can be enhanced through the adoption of renewable energy. This is due to Energy sources in Somalia, such as solar and wind, having less environmental impact. However, most Somalian families, both urban and rural, have relied on firewood and charcoal45. Charcoal and firewood remain the primary energy sources in rural and urban areas. The nation’s energy comes from traditional biomass sources. Traditional biomass sources generate 82% of the nation’s energy79. Saving energy improves the environment in Somalia by lowering carbon dioxide levels and other hazardous pollutants in the atmosphere. Charcoal production in Somalia has resulted in significant deforestation, soil erosion, and biodiversity loss, harming both local populations and Somalia’s natural environments. The use of renewable energy sources, including solar power, assists in restoring damaged ecosystems. Somalia has considerable renewable energy potential, notably in solar and wind power. Renewable energy sources such as solar and wind have less environmental impact. This result is consistent with international initiatives, such as the European Union’s sustainability objectives for 2050, prioritizing energy reduction and efficiency as essential environmental sustainability components. This study’s findings that saving energy improves environmental sustainability are in line with earlier research. For example, Dincer and Rosen56 emphasizes the substantial correlation between environmental conservation and energy efficiency, arguing that efficient energy systems can simultaneously meet energy needs and reduce ecological impacts.

Our fourth hypothesis is that waste management efficiency positively affects environmental sustainability. This indicates that for every unit change in WMW, the Environmental sustainability increases by 0.344 units (see Table 7). This suggests minimizing waste generation, ensuring proper disposal, and recycling reduces environmental impacts in Somalia, where rapid urbanization and population increase have worsened waste management issues In this country. Based on this finding, Somalia has the potential to enhance environmental sustainability by mitigating the adverse impacts of solid waste on both the environment and public health through reducing waste generation and improving resource recovery efficiency. Waste management encompasses the processes and activities necessary to oversee waste from its origin to its final disposal. This encompasses waste collection, transportation, treatment, and disposal, along with the oversight and control of the waste management process and waste-related legislation, technology, and economic mechanisms. Waste management aims to mitigate the harmful impacts of waste on the environment and human health. A significant aspect of waste management is municipal solid waste generated by industrial, commercial, and residential activities. Before the civil war in Somalia, Mogadishu was known for its broad beaches, fresh sea air, and clear blue ocean water. However, vast amounts of trash have been spreading everywhere in recent decades. Major cities in Somalia lack formal garbage disposal facilities or trash recycling centers. Instead, individuals have left garbage on the streets and dumped it into the Indian Ocean or along the shore. Waste management shows a stronger effect compared to other variables because in Somalia, urban areas produce an increasing amount of solid waste due to population growth. The World Bank estimates that Somalia’s urban regions create more than 1,500 tons of solid trash daily80. This exposure to air pollution leads to negative health consequences and diseases. Rapid urbanization and the country’s lack of effective waste management systems create an increasingly solid and liquid waste problem10. Therefore, Solid and liquid waste are significant environmental concerns in urban areas of Somalia, and waste generation is a major environmental issue. Additionally, it is becoming more and more obvious that poor environmental quality has a detrimental effect on people’s health throughout the entire nation10. This study’s findings that Waste management strongly improves environmental sustainability are in line with earlier research conducted in developing nations. For example, Studies by12 investigate the influence of e-waste management strategies on promoting environmental sustainability. They found that e-waste management strategies improve environmental sustainability.

Conclusions and recommendations

This study investigates the key factors contributing to Environmental Sustainability in Somalia. Specifically, it analyses the causal relationships between Conservation of Water, Reduction of Pollution, Saving Energy, Waste Management Efficiency, and Environmental Sustainability. This research used purposive sampling to select participants who met specific criteria, including their awareness and involvement in environmental protection practices. The sample size for this study is 300. However, after filtering out the responses, the data study centers on 240 samples. The findings reveal that COW, ROP, SE, and WME are all positively and significantly related to Environmental Sustainability in Somalia. The result indicates that environmental sustainability in Somalia can be improved through integrated environmental practice, such as pollution reduction, conservation efforts, saving energy, and waste management. The results show that reducing pollution, conserving water by collecting rainwater to strengthen agricultural resilience in Somalia’s arid regions, saving energy, since charcoal and biomass provide a large portion of the country’s energy, and minimizing waste generation through appropriate disposal and recycling all help to lessen environmental impacts and improve environmental sustainability in Somalia. These results highlight the importance of effective water use, pollution reduction, energy conservation, and efficient waste management in mitigating ecological damage.

This study has made numerous contributions to the field of environmental Protection. Firstly, Environmental protection contributes to several important societal goals, including long-term sustainability, a cleaner environment, less climate change, and better food. To our knowledge, no research has examined this topic in the Somali context. The study fills an essential gap in the existing body of knowledge by examining the causal relationships between Conservation of Water, Reduction of pollution, Savings Energy, waste management efficiency, and environmental sustainability in Somalia. This allows a deeper understanding of how these variables interact with the Protection of the environment. Secondly, this study contributes to the existing empirical literature by presenting information on how the Conservation of Water interacts with the Protection of the Environment. These results highlight that utilizing water effectively to prevent unnecessary water consumption may help the environment. Finally, the study’s findings give policy-relevant insights for Somalia and other developing nations facing similar environmental challenges. Thus, the research has worldwide implications and adds to a larger discussion on sustainable development.

The practical implications of this study suggest that numerous strategies focus on enhancing environmental Protection. This paper finds that lowering pollution levels is good for ecosystems and well-being. Hence, the Ministry of Environment and Climate Change (MoECC), together with the Ministry of Petroleum and Mineral Resources, must enforce a National Pollution Control Policy by June 2026, which will include mandatory emissions reductions for industries, agriculture, and urban areas. It should draw on provisions already set out in NEP and international legal requirements as laid out in the UN Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD). Policy makers should commit 15% of all climate finance revenue from the Green Climate Fund (GCF) and bilateral donor countries to the transition towards renewable energy and reforestation projects of 50,000 hectares of degraded land by 2030.

The result of this research confirms that water conservation practices play a positive role in achieving environmental sustainability goals. In line with the conclusions of this study, the Ministry of Energy and Water Resources (MoEWR), together with the municipal water authorities, should design and adopt a National Water Conservation Action Plan based on the National Water Strategy 2021–2025. This plan shall entail a goal of achieving a 20% reduction in the urban water loss rate through rehabilitation of distribution networks in Mogadishu, Hargeisa, and Kismayo by December 2027. The funding for such an action plan should come from water sector grants by the World Bank, climate adaptation grants by UNEP, and municipal water tariffs. Furthermore, the plan should involve conducting public awareness programs to reach out to no less than 500,000 households living in areas suffering from droughts, proper demand forecast systems, and water conservation indicators measured every year by the MoEWR.

This study finds that energy saving positively affects environmental sustainability. The Ministry of Energy and Water Resources, together with the private sector actors and international donors, must develop a Somali Renewable Energy Expansion Plan by January 2026. It should seek to raise the proportion of renewable energy, especially solar, in the national energy mix to at least 30% by 2030, in line with the government’s Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) outlined in the Paris Agreement. Financial resources required for capacity building among electricity providers should be provided via energy financing projects of the African Development Bank (AFDB) and USAID.

This research clearly shows that efficient waste management is essential for protecting the environment. The Ministry of Environment and Climate Change, together with the Benadir Regional Administration (BRA) and other municipal governments of Hargeisa and Kismayo, need to construct three landfills within Somalia’s three biggest towns. This should be completed by December 2028. The landfills have to be built according to WHO/UNEP regulations on solid waste management, which will not allow any unregulated dumping of garbage within the municipalities. This can be achieved through funding from European Union humanitarian and development assistance and the Islamic Development Bank (IsDB), along with waste collection fees. Also, a national law on solid waste management needs to be developed under the auspices of the Ministry of Justice in close cooperation with the MoECC, which would be adopted before the middle of 2027. Compliance checks need to be done annually.

There are numerous limitations associated with this study that need to be acknowledged. Firstly, a cross-sectional study design cannot determine any causation, and even though some correlations were found between different variables, the causality cannot be proven definitively. Secondly, the study is limited to Mogadishu, and thus, the findings may not be applied to the rest of Somalia or even other countries. Thirdly, the use of a questionnaire could lead to social desirability effects, where people might try to give socially accepted answers rather than honest ones. Fourthly, purposive sampling was used, and therefore, the results may not necessarily be applicable to a wider population. Lastly, because common method variance is a threat when all the variables are assessed with the help of only one survey tool, there is a risk that the correlations were somewhat exaggerated. In addition, the COW, ROP, SE, and WME factors represent the cognitive-belief component of each of the environmental domains rather than actions. In future research, it is necessary to expand these cognitive belief-based measures by adding behavior-focused measures like the ones used in14 and57. Additionally, future research might want to include more variables in its analysis. Environmental awareness and attitudes related to the climate were out of scope for the present research, but they can certainly be considered in future analyses.

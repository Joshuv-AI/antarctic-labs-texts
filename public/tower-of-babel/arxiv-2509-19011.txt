Addition theorems for Ziegler pairs of hyperplane arrangements
Takuro Abe, Lukas Kühne and Piotr Pokora

Abstract
. Inspired by Terao’s freeness conjecture, we examine Ziegler pairs, which are pairs of hyperplane arrangements that share the same underlying matroid but have different modules of logarithmic derivations. In this paper, we present a general construction that yields the first known families of Ziegler pairs in arbitrary dimension and size, starting from examples in the complex projective plane.



1.
Introduction

A hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{A}
can be studied from two perspectives: a combinatorial one, via its intersection lattice
L(\mathcal{A})
, which is equivalent to a simple matroid; and an algebraic one, via its module of logarithmic derivations
D(\mathcal{A})
. A long-standing open conjecture tries to unify these two points of view.

Conjecture 1.1

(Terao’s conjecture
[
OT92
]
)
.

If
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
are two central arrangements defined over the same field with the same combinatorics, that is
L({\mathcal{A}}_{1})\cong L({\mathcal{A}}_{2})
, then the derivation module
D({\mathcal{A}}_{1})
is free if and only if
D({\mathcal{A}}_{2})
is free.

This conjecture is still wide open, even for line arrangements in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
Note that the assumption on the field is essential as Ziegler observed in
[
Zie90
]
that there exists a non-free arrangement of
9
lines over
\mathbb{F}_{3}
with underlying matroid the dual Hesse configuration whereas this matroid also admits free realizations over fields of characteristic not
3
.
However, we know that Terao’s conjecture (in this stronger form that fixes the field) holds for line arrangements in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
over any field with up to
14
lines
[
BK23
]
.

Ziegler was the first to provide evidence against this conjecture, describing a pair of line arrangements with the same combinatorics, but with non-isomorphic modules of derivations
[
Zie89
]
.
Pairs of arrangements with these properties are therefore nowadays called
Ziegler pairs
, which is the main focus of this paper.
To the best of our knowledge, these are the only known Ziegler pairs so far:

•

The Ziegler pair described in
[
Zie89
]
consisting of two line arrangements with
9
lines each that intersect in
6
triple points.
The degrees of a minimal generating system of their derivation modules are
(1,5,6,6,6)
and
(1,6,6,6,6,6,6)
.
It was later pointed out by Schenck that the former case is characterized by the condition that the
6
triples points lie on a conic
[
Sch12
]
.
This example was recently studied in detail by Dimca and Sticlaru in
[
DS24
]
.

•

This Ziegler pair was generalized through concepts from
rigidity theory
in
[
DST25
]
. In particular, the authors propose a construction of an infinite family of Ziegler pairs of line arrangements.

•

It was observed in
[
KLP24
]
that the matroids with a
singular realization space
found in
[
CL25
]
also yield Ziegler pairs.
The derivation module differs when the realization, a line arrangement with
12
lines, is in the singular locus of the realization space in these examples.

•

The
elliptic matroid

E_{n}
is the rank
3
matroid on the ground set
\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
where a triple is a non-basis if and only if the sum of these numbers is zero modulo
n
.
The name of this matroid stems from the fact that for
n\geq 10
, the realization space is birational to the elliptic modular curve
X_{1}(n)

[
BR24
]
.
The matroid
E_{11}
also appeared prominently in
[
BHKL25
]
as a counter-example regarding the space of Lorentzian polynomials.
Moreover, Cuntz and the third author observed in
[
CP25
]
that the realization space of
E_{10}
also yields a Ziegler pair as the derivation module differs on a rational point of this realization space, i.e., the elliptic modular curve
X_{1}(10)
.

Let us mention that in all of these examples there is a
hidden collinearity
in one part of the pair, that is there are collinear intersection points of the arrangement which are not forced to be collinear by the underlying matroid.

The main result of the paper is a description of the degrees of a minimal system of generators of the derivation of a line arrangement after adding a generic line (
Theorem

4.1
) or after coning the arrangement several times and then adding a generic hyperplane (
Theorem

5.1
).
This yields new families of Ziegler pairs:

Theorem 1.2

(see Corollary
5.2
)
.

There exist Ziegler pairs of irreducible arrangements of arbitrarily large size and arbitrarily large dimension.

In particular, these are the first irreducible (non-trivial) Ziegler pairs in dimensions greater than three.

This article is organized as follows: Section
2
presents the necessary preliminaries related to hyperplane arrangements and their derivation modules. Section
3
focuses on two notions of generic hyperplanes which is the technical heart of our paper. Section
4
provides a general recipe for constructing Ziegler pairs in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
by adding suitably chosen hyperplanes. The final Section
5
describes a general construction of non-trivial examples of Ziegler pairs in arbitrary dimensions.

2.
Preliminaries

We begin with recalling the definitions of the main players of this article. Most of these notions are taken from
[
OT92
]
.

Let
V=\mathbb{C}^{\ell}
and consider
S={\rm Sym}^{*}(V^{*})\cong\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}]
.
For
\ell=3
, we will assume , starting from Section 4, that our coordinates are
x,y,z
, and for
\ell=4
, we add a new variable,
w
, to this list.
Define
{\rm Der}(S)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}S\cdot\partial_{x_{i}}
. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a central arrangement of hyperplanes in
V
, i.e., a finite number of linear hyperplanes in
V
.
Throughout this article, for each
H\in\mathcal{A}
we fix a linear form
\alpha_{H}
such that
{\rm ker}(\alpha_{H})=H
, and denote by
Q(\mathcal{A})=\prod_{H\in\mathcal{A}}\alpha_{H}
the defining equation of
\mathcal{A}
.
The logarithmic derivation module
D(\mathcal{A})
of
\mathcal{A}
is defined as

D(\mathcal{A})=\{\theta\in{\rm Der}(S)\mid\theta(\alpha_{H})\in S\cdot\alpha_{H}\,\text{ for all }\,H\in\mathcal{A}\}.

Recall that
D(\mathcal{A})
is an
S
-graded reflexive module of rank
\ell
.
We say that the arrangement
\mathcal{A}
is
free
with the exponents
{\rm exp}({\mathcal{A}})=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell})
if there exists a homogeneous
S
-basis
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{\ell}
for
D(\mathcal{A})
such that
{\rm deg}(\theta_{i})=d_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,\ell
.
For
\theta\in{\rm Der}(S)
, we say that
\theta
is homogeneous of degree
d
if

\theta(\alpha)
is zero or a homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
for all
\alpha\in V^{*}
.

Observe that if
\mathcal{A}
is non-empty, every generating set of
D(\mathcal{A})
contains at least one element of degree
1
, which can always be chosen as the well-know Euler derivation

\theta_{E}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}x_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}.

We define

D_{0}(\mathcal{A})=\{\theta\in{\rm Der}(S)\mid\theta(\alpha_{H})=0\,\text{ for all }\,H\in\mathcal{A}\},

and let us recall that there is the following decomposition as a direct sum

D(\mathcal{A})=S\cdot\theta_{E}\oplus D_{0}(\mathcal{A}),

hence the freeness of
\mathcal{A}
boils down to checking the freeness of
D_{0}(\mathcal{A})
.

Given an arrangement
{\mathcal{A}}
and a hyperplane
H\in{\mathcal{A}}
one often considers the
deletion-restriction-triple
of
H
in
{\mathcal{A}}
.
The deletion
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}:={\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{H\}
is
{\mathcal{A}}
without
H
.
The restriction
{\mathcal{A}}^{H}
is an arrangement in the hyperplane
H
defined as

{\mathcal{A}}^{H}:=\{K\cap H\mid K\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}\}.

This triple yields an exact sequence of derivation modules, the well-studied
Euler sequence
, see
[
OT92
, Prop. 4.45]
:

0\to D({\mathcal{A}}^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot\alpha_{H}}}{{\longrightarrow}}D({\mathcal{A}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho}}{{\longrightarrow}}D({\mathcal{A}}^{H}),

where
\rho
is the map induced by the quotient map
S\to S/\alpha_{H}S
, more precisely
\rho(\theta)(\overline{f})=\overline{\theta(f)}
. The map
\rho
is called the
Euler restriction map
.
This sequence is not right exact in general. However, assuming that
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
is free, the first author has recently proven that the Euler sequence is, in fact, right exact, i.e.,
\rho
is surjective
[
Abe24
]
. A crucial step in our proofs below is to show that this sequence is right exact under certain genericity assumptions. In the course of our proofs we will use the following result.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
ADP24
, Theorem 2.5]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an arrangement in
{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
.

With the above notation, let

0\rightarrow\oplus_{i=1}^{m-2}S[-c^{\prime}_{i}]\rightarrow\oplus_{i=1}^{m}S[-d_{i}]\rightarrow D_{0}({\mathcal{A}}^{\prime})\rightarrow 0

be a minimal free resolution of
D_{0}({\mathcal{A}}^{\prime})
. If
|{\mathcal{A}}^{H}|>c_{i}^{\prime}-1
for all
i
, then
the Euler exact sequence

0\rightarrow D({\mathcal{A}}^{\prime})[-1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot\alpha_{H}}}{{\longrightarrow}}D({\mathcal{A}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho}}{{\longrightarrow}}D({\mathcal{A}}^{H})

is right exact.

Consider a direct sum decomposition
V=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
of the vector space
V
. Let
\mathcal{A}_{i}
be an arrangement in
V_{i}
and denote by
S_{i}
be the coordinate ring of
V_{i}
with
i\in\{1,2\}
.
Then
S_{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}S_{2}=S
, and we can define

\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{A}_{2}:=\{H_{1}\oplus V_{2}\,:\,H_{1}\in\mathcal{A}_{1}\}\cup\{V_{1}\oplus H_{2}\,:\,H\in\mathcal{A}_{2}\}.

We call an arrangement
irreducible
if it cannot be written as a product of two non-empty arrangements.

In this setting, the following result holds.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
OT92
, Proposition 4.14]
)
.

D(\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{A}_{2})=S\cdot D(A_{1})\oplus S\cdot D(\mathcal{A}_{2}).

Denote by
L(\mathcal{A})
the intersection lattice of
\mathcal{A}
, i.e.,

L(\mathcal{A})=\{\cap_{H\in\mathcal{B}}H\,:\,\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{A}\},

which is ordered by reverse inclusion.
Recall that if
\mathcal{A}
is the product arrangement of the form
\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{A}_{2}
, we have a natural isomorphism of the lattices

(2.1)

L(\mathcal{A}_{1})\times L(\mathcal{A}_{2})\cong L(\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{A}_{2}).

A special case of the direct product of an arrangement is its coning which we will use to construct high-dimensional examples.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an arrangement in
{\mathbb{C}}^{k}
for some
k\geq 3
defined by a polynomial
f\in{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{k}]
.
The
coning

c{\mathcal{A}}
of
{\mathcal{A}}
is the arrangement defined by the polynomial
x_{k+1}\cdot f\in{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{k+1}]
.

The coning of
{\mathcal{A}}
is thus the direct product of
{\mathcal{A}}
with the
1
-dimensional arrangement consisting of a point on a line.

Let us now define the main object of our studies, namely a Ziegler pair.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}\subset\mathbb{C}^{\ell}
be two central hyperplane arrangements such that
L(\mathcal{A}_{1})\cong L(\mathcal{A}_{2})
. We say that the pair
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
is a
Ziegler pair
if the associated modules of logarithmic derivations
D(\mathcal{A}_{1})
and
D(\mathcal{A}_{2})
have different minimal free resolutions and thus are not isomorphic.

To study the homological properties of hyperplane arrangements, we need the following crucial definition.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an arrangement in
{\mathbb{C}}^{\ell}
. We say that

(d_{1},\ldots,d_{s})
is a
degree sequence
of
{\mathcal{A}}
, denoted by
\exp({\mathcal{A}})
, if
the
0
-th syzygy of a minimal free resolution of
D({\mathcal{A}})
is of the form

\oplus_{i=1}^{s}S[-d_{i}]
.
We denote the degrees of a minimal generating set of
D_{0}({\mathcal{A}})
by
\exp_{0}({\mathcal{A}})
.

Let
\theta_{E},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{s}
be a minimal set of generators of
D({\mathcal{A}})
for a given arrangement in
{\mathbb{C}}^{k}
.
The module of derivations
D(c{\mathcal{A}})
of the coning is then minimally generated by
\theta_{E},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{s},x_{k+1}\partial_{x_{k}+1}
.
Thus it holds that

\exp(c{\mathcal{A}})=(1,\deg\theta_{E},\deg\theta_{2},\dots,\deg\theta_{s}).

Finally, let us recall the following important bound on the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of
D({\mathcal{A}})
that we will use in the forthcoming sections, here for an
S
-graded module
M
and its minimal free resolution

0\rightarrow\oplus_{i=1}^{n_{s}}S[-d_{i}^{s}]\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\oplus_{i=1}^{n_{1}}S[-d_{i}^{0}]\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0,

the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity
of
M
is defined by

\reg(M):=\max\{d_{i}^{j}-j\mid 0\leq j\leq s,\ 1\leq i\leq n_{j}\}.

Proposition 2.6

(
[
Sai19
, Proposition 1.2]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an arrangement in
{\mathbb{C}}^{\ell}
. Then

\reg D({\mathcal{A}})\leq|{\mathcal{A}}|-\ell+1
.

Remark 2.7
.

The above result for the case of
\ell=3
was proved earlier by Schenck in
[
Sch03
, Corollary 3.5]
.

3.
Generic hyperplanes

In this section we collect a number of lemmas devoted to different notions of generic hyperplanes.

We firstly study combinatorial genericity conditions and subsequently genericity conditions arising from the module of logarithmic derivations.

Definition 3.1
.

We call an arrangement in
{\mathbb{C}}^{\ell}

combinatorially generic
if for every subset
\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{p}\}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
with
p\leq\ell

we have
\codim(H_{1}\cap\dots\cap H_{p})=p
and
H_{1}\cap\dots\cap H_{p}=\{0\}
for
p>\ell
.

Definition 3.2
.

A hyperplane
H
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{\ell}
is
combinatorially generic with respect to a hyperplane arrangement

\mathcal{A}
if the localization
({\mathcal{A}}\cup\{H\})_{X}
is combinatorially generic for all flats
0\neq X\in L(\mathcal{A}\cup\{H\})
containing
H
.

One reason to consider combinatorially generic hyperplanes stems from the fact that they already impose strong algebraic conditions.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an arrangement in
{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
and let
{\mathcal{B}}
be the arrangement obtained by adding a combinatorial generic hyperplane
H\notin{\mathcal{A}}
with defining equation
\alpha_{H}
to
{\mathcal{A}}
.
Then it holds that:

(1)

The Euler sequence

0\rightarrow D({\mathcal{A}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot\alpha_{H}}}{{\rightarrow}}D({\mathcal{B}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho}}{{\rightarrow}}D({\mathcal{B}}^{H})\rightarrow 0.

is exact.

(2)

For any minimal set of generators
\theta_{E},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{s}
of
D({\mathcal{A}})
it holds that
\theta_{i}(\alpha_{H})\notin\alpha_{H}S
for all
2\leq i\leq s
.

Proof.

Set
|{\mathcal{A}}|=:d
and let

0\rightarrow\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n-3}S[-b_{j}]\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}S[-a_{j}]\rightarrow D({\mathcal{A}})\rightarrow 0

be a minimal free resolution of
D({\mathcal{A}})
with

a_{1}\leq\ldots\leq a_{n},\ b_{1}\leq\ldots\leq b_{n-2}
.
Note that,
a_{n}\leq d-2
and

b_{n-2}\leq d-1
since the Castelnouvo-Mumford regularity of
D({\mathcal{A}})
is at most
d-2
, and this follows from Theorem
2.6
.
Since
H
is combinatorially generic with respect to
{\mathcal{A}}
, we know that
|{\mathcal{A}}^{H}|=d
, hence
|{\mathcal{A}}^{H}|>b_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq n-2
.
Thus Theorem
2.1
implies that the Euler sequence

(3.1)

0\rightarrow D({\mathcal{A}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot\alpha_{H}}}{{\rightarrow}}D({\mathcal{B}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho}}{{\rightarrow}}D({\mathcal{B}}^{H})=S[-1]\oplus S[-d+1]\rightarrow 0.

is exact which proves (1).

To prove (2), let
\theta_{E}=\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{s}
be a minimal set of generators for
D({\mathcal{A}})
with
\deg\theta_{j}=a_{j}
.

We now show that
\theta_{j}\not\in D({\mathcal{B}})
for all
j>1
.
Assume that
\theta_{j}\in D({\mathcal{B}})
.
The above regularity argument implies that
\deg\theta_{j}=a_{j}\leq d-2
, so after replacing

\theta_{j}
by
\theta_{j}-f\theta_{E}
, if necessary, it holds that
\rho(\theta_{j})=0
for all
j>1
.
Thus the exactness of the sequence (
3.1
) implies that
\theta_{j}/\alpha_{H}\in D({\mathcal{A}})
, contradicting the minimality of the generators of
D({\mathcal{A}})
.
∎

We also need an algebraic notion of generic hyperplanes:

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a hyperplane arrangement in
{\mathbb{C}}^{\ell}
and let
\theta_{E}
,
\theta_{2},\ldots,\theta_{s}
be a minimal set of generators of
D(\mathcal{A})
.
A hyperplane
H
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{\ell}
with the defining equation
\alpha_{H}
is
algebraically generic
with respect to
\mathcal{A}
and its generators
\theta_{E},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{s}
if
\theta_{i}(\alpha_{H})\notin S\alpha_{H}
for all
i=2,\ldots,s
.

One can show that for an irreducible arrangement
{\mathcal{A}}
and a fixed derivation
\theta\in D({\mathcal{A}})
which is not a multiple of the Euler derivation, the set of hyperplanes
\{\alpha_{H}=0\}
in the dual space
(\mathbb{P}^{\ell-1})^{*}
such that
\theta(\alpha_{H})\notin S\alpha_{H}
is open and dense.
Since the intersection of finitely many open dense subsets is non-empty, algebraically generic hyperplanes always exist.
Since we do not use this fact below, we omit the proof of this statement.

The notion of an algebraically generic hyperplane might however depend on the chosen generators as the next example shows.

Example 3.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be the deleted
A_{3}
arrangement in
{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
with the defining equation
xyz(x-y)(x-z)=0
.
One basis of
D({\mathcal{A}})
is

\theta_{1}:=\theta_{E},\quad\theta_{2}:=y(x-y)\partial_{y},\quad\theta_{3}:=z(x-z)\partial_{z}.

The hyperplane
H:\{y-z=0\}
is algebraically generic with respect to the generators
\theta_{2}
and
\theta_{3}
.
This hyperplane is however not algebraically generic with respect to the basis
\theta_{E},\theta_{2},\theta_{2}+\theta_{3}
as
(\theta_{2}+\theta_{3})(y-z)\in S(y-z)
.

In the case of combinatorial generic hyperplanes in rank
3
, the notion of algebraically generic hyperplanes is however independent of the chosen minimal generators.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an essential arrangement in
{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
and let
H\notin{\mathcal{A}}
a combinatorially generic hyperplane with respect to
{\mathcal{A}}
. Let
\theta_{E},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{s}
be a minimal set of generators of
D({\mathcal{A}})
. Then
H
is also algebraically generic with respect to
{\mathcal{A}}
and the generators
\theta_{E},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{s}
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Lemma
3.3
(2). ∎

In the scenario of successive conings, we deduce the following corollary.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an arrangement in
{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
.
Suppose that
\theta_{E},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{s}
are minimal generators of
D({\mathcal{A}})
.
For
k=3,\dots,\ell
, let
{\mathcal{A}}_{k}
be the arrangement obtained from
{\mathcal{A}}
by taking
(k-3)
-times the coning operation.
Let
H
be a combinatorially generic hyperplane with respect to
{\mathcal{A}}_{\ell}
in
\mathbb{C}^{\ell}
and set
H_{k}:=H\cap\bigcap_{j=k+1}^{\ell}\{x_{j}=0\}
for
k=3,\dots,\ell
.
Then it holds that

(i)

H_{k}
is combinatorially generic with respect to
{\mathcal{A}}_{k}
for all
k=3,\dots,\ell
.

(ii)

H_{k}
is algebraically generic with respect to
{\mathcal{A}}_{k}
and its minimal set of generators
\theta_{E},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{s},x_{4}\partial_{x_{4}},\dots,x_{k}\partial_{x_{k}}
for all
k=3,\dots,\ell
.

Proof.

Let
L_{1},\dots,L_{p}
be hyperplanes in
{\mathcal{A}}_{k}
with
p\geq 1
.
By construction, these hyperplanes are also contained in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\ell}
and
H_{k}=H\cap\bigcap_{j=k+1}^{\ell}\{x_{j}=0\}
.
The first claim follows from the fact that
\codim_{{\mathbb{C}}^{k}}(H_{k}\cap L_{1}\cap\dots\cap L_{p})
equals

\codim_{{\mathbb{C}}^{\ell}}(H\cap\{x_{k+1}=0\}\cap\dots\cap\{x_{\ell}=0\}\cap L_{1}\cap\dots\cap L_{p}).

For the second claim, suppose
H_{k}
is defined by the equation
\alpha_{H_{k}}
.
Since the derivations
\theta_{i}
only involve the variables
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}
by construction, it follows that
\theta_{i}(\alpha_{H_{k}})=\theta_{i}(\alpha_{H_{3}})
for all
i=2,\dots,s
.
Hence Lemma
3.6
implies that
H_{k}
is algebraically generic with respect to
\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{s}
.
Lastly note that
H_{k}
is algebraically generic with respect to
x_{4}\partial_{x_{4}},\dots,x_{k}\partial_{x_{k}}
since
H_{k}
is combinatorially generic with respect to
{\mathcal{A}}_{k}
.
∎

4.
Ziegler pairs in
\mathbb{C}^{3}

Let us present our first main result of the paper.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
be a Ziegler pair of central hyperplane arrangements in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
. Let
{\mathcal{B}}_{i}
be the arrangement obtained by adding a generic hyperplane to
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
with
i\in\{1,2\}
. Then
{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{B}}_{2}
form a new Ziegler pair.

The proof of this result relies on a detailed understanding of the free resolution of the derivation module of the arrangement after adding a generic hyperplane.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an arrangement in
{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
and let
{\mathcal{B}}
be the arrangement obtained by adding a combinatorial generic hyperplane
H
to
{\mathcal{A}}
.
If
{\mathcal{A}}
has exponents
\exp({\mathcal{A}})=(1,a_{2},\dots,a_{n})
then the exponents of
{\mathcal{B}}
are

\exp({\mathcal{B}})=(1,a_{2}+1,\dots,a_{n}+1,|{\mathcal{A}}|-1).

Proof.

Set
d:=|{\mathcal{A}}|
. Following Lemma
3.3
(1), there is an exact sequence

(4.1)

0\rightarrow D({\mathcal{A}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot\alpha_{H}}}{{\rightarrow}}D({\mathcal{B}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho}}{{\rightarrow}}D({\mathcal{B}}^{H})=S[-1]\oplus S[-d+1]\rightarrow 0.

This sequence enables us to determine the degrees of a minimal set of generators for
D({\mathcal{B}}).

Let
\theta_{E}=\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\ldots,\theta_{n}
be a minimal set of generators for
D({\mathcal{A}})
with
\deg\theta_{j}=a_{j}
.
Let
\overline{\theta_{E}},\varphi
be a basis for
D({\mathcal{B}}^{H})
with
\deg\varphi=d-1
.
Note that
\overline{\theta_{E}}=\rho(\theta_{E})
.
Since
\rho
is surjective, there exists
\phi\in D({\mathcal{B}})_{d-1}
such that

\rho(\phi)=\varphi
.

By Lemma
3.3
(2), we obtain that
\theta_{j}\not\in D({\mathcal{B}})
for all
j>1
.

Hence, we know that
\theta_{E},\alpha_{H}\theta_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{H}\theta_{n},\phi\in D({\mathcal{B}})
, and since the sequence (
4.1
) is exact we can deduce that these derivations actually generate
D({\mathcal{B}})
.

Let us now show that they form in fact a minimal set of generators for
D({\mathcal{B}})
.
If they generate
D({\mathcal{B}})
after removing
\phi
, then the image of
\rho
consists of only
\overline{S}\overline{\theta_{E}}
, so
\rho
cannot
be surjective.
Next, assume that
\alpha\theta_{j}
is removable for some
j>1
, where
\alpha:=\alpha_{H}
.
There are
f,f_{k},g\in S
such that

\alpha\theta_{j}=f\theta_{E}+\sum_{k\neq j}f_{k}\alpha\theta_{k}+g\phi.

Since
\deg\alpha\theta_{j}\leq d-1=\deg\phi
, it holds that
g\in{\mathbb{C}}
.
If
g\neq 0
we could generate
D({\mathcal{B}})
without
\phi
which is impossible by the above argument.
Hence it must hold that
g=0
and thus

\alpha\theta_{j}=f\theta_{E}+\sum_{k\neq j}f_{k}\alpha\theta_{k}.

Since
\alpha\nmid\theta_{E}
we must have that
\alpha\mid f
, which shows

\theta_{j}=\frac{f}{\alpha}\theta_{E}+\sum_{k\neq j}f_{k}\theta_{k},

which is a contradiction to the minimality of the generating set
\{\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n}\}
for
D({\mathcal{A}})
.
Thus
\{\theta_{E},\alpha_{H}\theta_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{H}\theta_{n},\phi\}
is a minimal set of generators of
D({\mathcal{B}})
and we obtain
\exp({\mathcal{B}})=(1,a_{2}+1,\dots,a_{n}+1,d-1)
as claimed.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

4.1
.

Since
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
form a Ziegler pair, the set of exponents of
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
are different.
Thus
Lemma

4.2
implies that also set of exponents of
{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{B}}_{2}
is different, hence these two arrangements form a Ziegler pair too.
∎

Example 4.3
.

Let us start with the classical Ziegler example. Consider the two line arrangements

\mathcal{A}_{1}:f_{1}=xy(x-y-z)(x-y+z)(2x+y-2z)(x+3y-3z)(3x+2y+3z)\\
\times(x+5y+5z)(7x-4y-z)=0,

\mathcal{A}_{2}:f_{2}=xy(4x-5y-5z)(x-y+z)(16x+13y-20z)(x+3y-3z)(3x+2y+3z)\\
\times(x+5y+5z)(7x-4y-z)=0.

Geometrically both arrangements consist of
9
lines that meet in
6
triple points.
In
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
these
6
triple points lie on a smooth conic and in the case of
{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
they do not lie on a conic.
Thus we know that
L(\mathcal{A}_{1})\cong L(\mathcal{A}_{2})
.
The exponents of these line arrangements are the following:

{\rm exp}_{0}(\mathcal{A}_{1})=(5,6,6,6),{\quad\rm exp}_{0}(\mathcal{A}_{2})=(6,6,6,6,6,6).

Let
H:13x+171y+232z=0
and consider now
\mathcal{B}_{i}=\mathcal{A}_{i}\cup H
with
i\in\{1,2\}
.
Since
H
is general with respect to both configurations
\mathcal{A}_{i}
, then
L(\mathcal{B}_{1})\cong L(\mathcal{B}_{2})
, and by Theorem
4.1
the exponents of these line arrangements are

{\rm exp}_{0}(\mathcal{B}_{1})=(6,7,7,7,8),\quad{\rm exp}_{0}(\mathcal{B}_{2})=(7,7,7,7,7,7,8).

Example 4.4
.

Let us consider a Ziegler pair with the property that for each line arrangement the first three exponents are the same. Consider

\mathcal{A}_{1}:f_{1}=xyz(x+2z)(y+z)(x+2y)(x+5y+3z)(x+y+3z)\\
\times(x+y+z)(3x+5y+3z)=0

and

\mathcal{A}_{2}:f_{2}=xyz(x+y+z)(y+z)(x+(\sqrt{5}+2)z)(4x+5y+4z)(4x+(\sqrt{5}+3)y)\\
\times(x+y+(\sqrt{5}+3)z)(4x+(5\sqrt{5}+15)y+(4\sqrt{5}+12)z)=0.

We know that the arrangements
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
A_{2}
form a Ziegler pair since they have different minimal free resolutions of the derivation modules, namely

{\rm exp}_{0}(\mathcal{A}_{1})=(5,6,6),\quad{\rm exp}_{0}(\mathcal{A}_{2})=(5,6,6,7),

see
[

]
for details regarding this example.
Let
H:x+17y+131z=0
and define
\mathcal{B}_{i}=\mathcal{A}_{i}\cup H
. Since

is generic with respect to both arrangements
\mathcal{A}_{i}
, then
L(\mathcal{B}_{1})=L(\mathcal{B}_{2})
, and the arrangements
\mathcal{B}_{1}
,
\mathcal{B}_{2}

{\rm exp}_{0}(\mathcal{B}_{1})=(6,7,7,9),\quad{\rm exp}_{0}(\mathcal{B}_{2})=(6,7,7,8,9).

Using the techniques presented in Theorem
4.1
, we can derive another result that characterizes Ziegler pairs via restrictions.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}={\mathcal{A}}_{i}^{\prime}\cup\{H\}
be an arrangement in
{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
and let

0\rightarrow F_{1}^{i}\rightarrow F_{0}^{i}\rightarrow D({\mathcal{A}}_{i}^{\prime})\rightarrow 0

be minimal free resolutions. Assume that
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}^{\prime},{\mathcal{A}}_{2}^{\prime}
form a Ziegler pair. If
F_{0}^{i}=\oplus S[-a_{j}^{i}],\ F_{1}^{i}=\oplus S[-b_{j}^{i}]
and

\max\{a_{j}^{i}\}=a^{i},\ \max\{b_{j}^{i}\}=b^{i}
, then
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
form a Ziegler pair if

|{\mathcal{A}}_{i}^{H}|>\max\{a^{1},a^{2},b^{1},b^{2}\}
.

Proof.

The same proof as in Lemma
3.3
works since the condition on
|{\mathcal{A}}_{i}^{H}|
forces the vanishing

H^{2}(\widetilde{D({\mathcal{A}}_{i}^{\prime})}(-1-b^{i}+|{\mathcal{A}}_{i}^{H}|-1))=0,

which is the key observation in the proof of Lemma
3.3
.
∎

Example 4.6
.

We will explain how to use the above result in the setting of Example

.
Let us compute the minimal free resolutions of the derivation modules of the arrangements
\mathcal{A}_{1}
,

0\rightarrow S[-8]\oplus S[-7]\rightarrow S^{3}[-6]\oplus S[-5]\oplus S[-1]\rightarrow D(\mathcal{A}_{1})

and

Since line
H:13x+171y+232z=0
is generic with respect to arrangements

,

, then

, and

which confirms that the pair

5.
Ziegler pairs in higher dimensions

In this section we construct the first non-trivial Ziegler pairs in
\mathbb{C}^{\ell}
starting with a Ziegler pair of hyperplane arrangements in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
.
The foundation of our construction is describing the structure of the generators of the derivation module obtained by coning and adding one generic hyperplane.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an irreducible arrangement in
{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
. Let
{\mathcal{A}}_{\ell}
be the arrangement obtained from
{\mathcal{A}}
by taking
(\ell-3)
-times the coning operation, i.e.,
{\mathcal{A}}_{\ell}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{\ell}
with new coordinates
x_{4},\ldots,x_{\ell}
. Let
H\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{\ell}
be a combinatorially generic hyperplane with respect to
{\mathcal{A}}_{\ell}
.
Then for
{\mathcal{B}}_{\ell}:={\mathcal{A}}_{\ell}\cup\{H\}
, we have

\exp_{0}({\mathcal{B}}_{\ell})=((2)^{(\ell-3)+\binom{\ell-4}{2}},(\exp_{0}({\mathcal{A}})+1)^{\ell-2},n),

where for
k\geq 3
we set
H_{k}:=H\cap\bigcap_{j=k+1}^{\ell}\{x_{j}=0\}
, which is a hyperplane in
{\mathbb{C}}^{k}
, and
n+1:=|{\mathcal{A}}^{H_{3}}|
.

Proof.

Let
\theta_{E},\theta_{2},\ldots,\theta_{s}
be a minimal set of generators for
D({\mathcal{A}})
with
\deg\theta_{i}=d_{i}
. Then it is clear that
D({\mathcal{A}}_{\ell})
has a minimal set of generators

\theta_{E},\theta_{2},\ldots,\theta_{s},x_{4}\partial_{x_{4}},\ldots,x_{\ell}\partial_{x_{\ell}}.

For
k\geq 3
, we define
{\mathcal{A}}_{k}
to be the
(k-3)
–times coned arrangement of
{\mathcal{A}}
and

{\mathcal{B}}_{k}:={\mathcal{A}}_{k}\cup\{H_{k}\}
.
By the construction of the coned arrangement and the generic hyperplane
H
, we have

{\mathcal{B}}_{\ell}^{H_{k}}={\mathcal{B}}_{k}^{H_{k}}={\mathcal{B}}_{k-1}.

Let
\rho_{k}^{k-1}:D({\mathcal{B}}_{k})\to D({\mathcal{B}}_{k}^{H_{k}})=D({\mathcal{B}}_{k-1})
be the Euler restriction map, and for
2\leq j<i\leq\ell
we denote by
\rho_{i}^{j}:D({\mathcal{B}}_{i})\to D({\mathcal{B}}_{j})
the composition of these maps.

Claim 1:
For every
3\leq k\leq\ell
, the map
\rho_{k}^{k-1}:D({\mathcal{B}}_{k})\to D({\mathcal{B}}_{k-1})
is surjective and therefore the following Euler sequence is exact:

(5.1)

0\rightarrow D({\mathcal{A}}_{k})[-1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot\alpha_{H_{k}}}}{{\rightarrow}}D({\mathcal{B}}_{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho_{k}^{k-1}}}{{\rightarrow}}D({\mathcal{B}}_{k-1})\rightarrow 0.

Proof of Claim 1:

The case
k=3
was proved in
Theorem

4.1
.
Now assume
k>3
.
Since
\pd_{S}D({\mathcal{A}}_{k})\leq 1
, this module has a free resolution

0\rightarrow\bigoplus_{j=1}^{p-k}S[-b_{j}]\rightarrow\bigoplus_{j=1}^{p}S[-a_{j}]\rightarrow D({\mathcal{A}}_{k})\rightarrow 0

for some numbers
a_{j},b_{j}
and
p=s+k-3
.
Since we assume
k>3
, we know that
H^{1}({\mathcal{O}}(-a_{j}))=0
for all
1\leq j\leq p
and
H^{2}({\mathcal{O}}(-b_{j}))=0
for all
1\leq j\leq p-k
.
Thus the long exact sequence in cohomology corresponding to the above free resolution shows that
H^{1}(\widetilde{D({\mathcal{A}}_{k})(-1)})=0
.

Since
H_{k}
is combinatorially generic with respect to
{\mathcal{A}}_{k}
, this arrangement is locally free along
H_{k}
.
Hence, there is an exact sequence of sheaves

0\rightarrow\widetilde{D({\mathcal{A}}_{k})}(-1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot\alpha_{H_{k}}}}{{\rightarrow}}\widetilde{D({\mathcal{B}}_{k})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho_{k}^{k-1}}}{{\rightarrow}}\widetilde{D({\mathcal{B}}_{k-1})}\rightarrow 0.

The above cohomology-vanishing argument now shows that taking global sections preserves exactness and thus the map
\rho_{k}^{k-1}
is surjective, which proves Claim 1.

The module
D({\mathcal{B}}_{\ell}^{H_{3}})
is a free module of rank
2
with a basis
\theta_{E},\phi_{2}
with
\deg\phi_{2}=n
.
Since the map
\rho_{k}^{k-1}
is surjective for every
k\geq 3
we can find successive preimages of
\phi_{2}
, namely
\phi_{k}\in D({\mathcal{B}}_{k})
such that
\rho_{k}^{2}(\phi_{k})=\phi_{2}
for every
k\geq 3
.

After a change of coordinates, we may assume that
\partial_{x_{j}}(\alpha_{H})=1
for all
4\leq j\leq\ell
.
For
1\leq i\leq s
and
4\leq j\leq\ell
, consider the derivation

\varphi_{i}^{j}:=x_{j}\theta_{i}-\theta_{i}(\alpha_{H})(x_{j}\partial_{x_{j}}).

Then
\varphi_{i}^{j}(\alpha_{H})=0
for all
1\leq i\leq s,\ 4\leq j\leq\ell
, and
\varphi_{i}^{j}\in D({\mathcal{B}}_{\ell})
with
\deg\varphi_{i}^{j}=d_{i}+1
.
We also define the derivation for
4\leq i<j

\eta_{i}^{j}:=x_{i}x_{j}(\partial_{x_{i}}-\partial_{x_{j}}).

Then clearly
\eta_{i}^{j}\in D({\mathcal{B}}_{k})
for
j\leq k
.

For
k\geq 3
, consider the set of derivations

G_{k}:=\{\theta_{E},\{\alpha_{H_{k}}x_{j}\partial_{x_{j}}\}_{4\leq j\leq k},\{\alpha_{H_{k}}\theta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{s},\{\varphi_{i}^{j}\}_{1\leq i\leq s,\ 4\leq j\leq k},\{\eta_{i}^{j}\}_{4\leq i<j\leq k},\phi_{k}\}.

Claim 2:
For each
k\geq 3
, the set
G_{k}
generates
D({\mathcal{B}}_{k})
.

Proof of Claim 2:
We proceed by induction, where the base case
k=3
was again proved in
Theorem

4.1
.
Now assume the claim holds for some
3\leq k-1
.

The derivations
\theta_{i}
only involve the variables
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}
, and by Corollary
3.7
, it holds that
\theta_{i}(\alpha_{H_{k}})\notin S\alpha_{H_{k}}
for all
1\leq i\leq s
and
3\leq k\leq\ell
.
Therefore
\rho_{k}^{k-1}(\varphi_{i}^{j})=\varphi_{i}^{j}\in D({\mathcal{B}}_{k-1})
for all
4<k,j\leq\ell
and
1\leq i\leq s
. Now let us check the images of
\varphi_{i}^{k}
and
\eta_{i}^{k}
under the map
\rho:=\rho_{k}^{k-1}
.

First, we show that
\rho(\varphi_{i}^{j})=\varphi_{i}^{j}
if
j<k
, and

\rho(\varphi_{i}^{k})=-\alpha_{H_{k-1}}\theta_{i}
.
For
j<k
, there are no
x_{k}
-terms in
\varphi_{i}^{j}
. Thus

\rho(\varphi_{i}^{j})=\varphi_{i}^{j}
. Let
j=k
, now for
t<k

\displaystyle\rho(\varphi_{i}^{k})(x_{t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{k}\overline{\theta_{i}(x_{t})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\alpha_{H_{k-1}}\overline{\theta_{i}(x_{t})}=(-\alpha_{H_{k-1}}\theta_{i})(x_{t})

since modulo
x_{k}
does not change
\theta_{i}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\rho(\varphi_{i}^{k})(x_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\overline{\theta_{i}(\alpha_{H})}x_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\alpha_{H_{k-1}}\theta_{i}(-\alpha_{H_{k-1}}).

Since
\overline{x_{k}}=-\alpha_{H_{k-1}}
, we know that

\rho(\varphi_{i}^{k})=-\alpha_{H_{k-1}}\theta_{i}
.

Next, let us show that
\rho(\eta_{i}^{k})=-\alpha_{H_{k-1}}x_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}
.
Clearly
\rho(\eta_{i}^{k})(x_{t})=0
if
t\neq i,k
. We compute

\displaystyle\rho(\eta_{i}^{k})(x_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle=\overline{x_{i}x_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\alpha_{H_{k-1}}x_{i}=(-\alpha_{H_{k-1}}x_{i}\partial_{x_{i}})(x_{i}).

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\rho(\eta_{i}^{k})(x_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{x_{i}\alpha_{H_{k-1}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{H_{k-1}}x_{i}=(-\alpha_{H_{k-1}}x_{i}\partial_{x_{i}})(-\alpha_{H_{k-1}}),

which means that
\rho(\eta_{i}^{k})=-\alpha_{H_{k-1}}x_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}
.
We have also
\rho(\eta_{i}^{j})=\eta_{i}^{j}
for
4\leq i<j<k
.
Hence, by
\rho_{k}^{k-1}
,

\{\varphi_{i}^{j}\}_{1\leq i\leq s,4\leq j\leq k}\mapsto\{\varphi_{i}^{j},\alpha_{H_{k-1}}\theta_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq s,4\leq j\leq k-1}

and

\{\eta_{i}^{k}\}_{4\leq i\leq k-1}\mapsto\{\alpha_{H_{k-1}}x_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}\}_{4\leq i\leq k-1}

up to signs, so we arrive at
\rho_{k}^{k-1}(G_{k})=G_{k-1}
.
Using the exact sequence (
5.1
), we obtain a generating set of
D({\mathcal{B}}_{k})
by mapping the generators of
D({\mathcal{A}}_{k})
and selecting preimages of the generators of
D({\mathcal{B}}_{k-1})
under the surjective map
\rho_{k}^{k-1}
.
The latter module is generated by
G_{k-1}
by our induction assumption.

Claim 3:
The set
G_{k}
is a minimal generating set of
D({\mathcal{B}}_{k})
. Since the degrees of the set
G_{k}
for
k=\ell
match the claimed exponents, this completes the proof the theorem once this claim is proved.

Proof of Claim 3:
We again proceed by induction where the base case
k=3
was proved in
Theorem

4.1
.
Suppose that
G_{k-1}
minimally generates
{\mathcal{B}}_{k-1}
for some
3\leq k-1
.

Since
\rho_{k}^{k-1}
is surjective, we cannot remove

\theta_{E}
,
\varphi_{i}^{j}
for
1\leq i\leq s,4\leq j\leq k
,
\eta_{i}^{j}
for
4\leq i<j\leq k
or
\phi_{k}
.
Also, since
{\mathcal{A}}
is irreducible, the derivations
\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{s}
are all of degree at least
2
.
Thus the derivations
\varphi_{i}^{j},\alpha_{H}\theta_{i}
have degree at least
3
.
As
{\mathcal{A}}
contains at least
4
hyperplanes, also the derivation
\phi_{k}
has degree at least
3
.

Suppose
x_{t}\alpha_{H_{k}}\partial_{x_{t}}
is removable, then we have a relation involving the other derivations in
G_{k}
of degree
2
, namely

x_{t}\alpha_{H}\partial_{x_{t}}=\sum_{i\neq t}c_{i}x_{i}\alpha_{H_{k}}\partial_{x_{i}}+\sum_{4\leq i<j\leq k}e_{ij}x_{i}x_{j}(\partial_{x_{i}}-\partial_{x_{j}}),

where
c_{i}
and
e_{ij}
are constants.
Restricting this equation to
x_{k}=0
yields a linear relation among the generators
G_{k-1}
, which is a contradiction to our induction assumption.

Moreover, suppose we can express
\alpha_{H_{k}}\theta_{t}
, for
1\leq t\leq s
, via the relation

(5.2)

\alpha_{H_{k}}\theta_{t}=f\theta_{E}+\sum_{i\neq t}f_{i}\alpha_{H_{k}}\theta_{i}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq s,\\
4\leq j\leq k\end{subarray}}g_{ij}\varphi_{i}^{j}+\sum_{i=4}^{k}h_{i}x_{i}\alpha_{H_{k}}\partial_{x_{i}}+\sum_{4\leq i<j\leq k}q_{ij}\eta_{i}^{j}+c\phi_{k},

for polynomials
f,f_{i},g_{ij},h_{i},q_{ij}\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}]
.

The regularity argument from above tells us that for each
i=1,\ldots,s
the degree of
\theta_{i}
is at most
|{\mathcal{A}}|-2
.
As
\phi_{k}
has degree
|{\mathcal{A}}|-1
, we can conclude that
c
must be a constant.
Restricting this relation to
H_{3}
, i.e.,
\alpha_{H_{k}}=\alpha_{H_{k-1}}=\dots=\alpha_{H_{3}}
, yields the relation

0=\overline{f}\theta_{E}+\overline{c}\phi_{2}.

Since these two derivations form a basis of
D({\mathcal{B}}_{2})
this yields
c=0
.

Note that, by construction,
\varphi_{i}^{k}|_{x_{k}=0}=0
and
\varphi_{i}^{j}|_{x_{k}=0}=\varphi_{i}^{j}
for
j<k
.
The same conclusion holds for the restriction of
\eta_{i}^{j}
.
Furthermore, it holds that
\theta_{i}|_{x_{k}=0}=\theta_{i}
and
\alpha_{H_{k}}|_{x_{k}=0}=\alpha_{H_{k-1}}
.
Now restricting the relation (
5.2
) onto
x_{k}=0
yields

\displaystyle\alpha_{H_{k-1}}\theta_{t}=

\displaystyle\overline{f}\theta_{E}+\sum_{i\neq t}\overline{f_{i}}\alpha_{H_{k-1}}\theta_{i}+

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq s,\\
4\leq j\leq k-1\end{subarray}}g_{ij}\varphi_{i}^{j}+\sum_{i=4}^{k-1}\overline{h_{i}}x_{i}\alpha_{H_{k-1}}\partial_{x_{i}}+\sum_{4\leq i<j\leq k-1}\overline{q_{ij}}\eta_{i}^{j}.

This is impossible as these are all derivations in
G_{k-1}
and hence form a minimal generating set of
D({\mathcal{B}}_{k-1})
by our induction hypothesis.

∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
be a Ziegler pair in
{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
. Then there exists a Ziegler pair in
{\mathbb{C}}^{\ell}
determined by
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
such that each arrangement in the pair is irreducible.

Proof.

Since the
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
’s are a Ziegler pairs in
{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
, the hyperplane arrangements obtained by applying the construction in Theorem
5.1
have the same combinatorics and different sequences of exponents.
∎

Concretely, we describe an irreducible Ziegler pair in
{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
stemming from our construction below.

Example 5.3
.

Let us consider these arrangements of hyperplanes in
\mathbb{C}^{4}
:

\mathcal{A}_{1}:f_{1}=xy(x-y-z)(x-y+z)(2x+y-2z)(x+3y-3z)\\
\times(3x+2y+3z)(x+5y+5z)(7x-4y-z)w=0

and

\mathcal{A}_{2}:f_{2}=xy(4x-5y-5z)(x-y+z)(16x+13y-20z)\\
\times(x+3y-3z)(3x+2y+3z)(x+5y+5z)(7x-4y-z)w=0.

These are the conings of the usual Ziegler of
9
lines and thus we have

\exp({\mathcal{A}}_{1})=(1,5,(6)^{3}),\quad\exp({\mathcal{A}}_{2})=(1,(6)^{6}).

Let
H:x+13y+27z+42w=0
and consider
\mathcal{B}_{i}=\mathcal{A}_{i}\cup H
with
i\in\{1,2\}
. We can see that
H
is general position for both arrangements and thus it holds that
L(\mathcal{B}_{1})\cong L(\mathcal{B}_{2})
.
Now Theorem
5.1
implies (
n=8
in this case):

\displaystyle\exp_{0}(\mathcal{B}_{1})

\displaystyle=(2,(6)^{2},(7)^{6},8),

\displaystyle\exp_{0}(\mathcal{B}_{2})

\displaystyle=(2,(7)^{12},8).

Hence we have constructed a non-trivial example of a Ziegler pair of two hyperplane arrangements in
\mathbb{C}^{4}
since
\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2}
are irreducible with distinct degree sequences.

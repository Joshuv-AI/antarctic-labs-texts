Self-normalized Cramér-type Moderate Deviation of Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics
Hongsheng Dai, Xiequan Fan, Jianya Lu ∗

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the self-normalized Cramér-type moderate deviation of the empirical measure of the stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics (SGLD). Consequently, we also derive the Berry-Esseen bound for SGLD. Our approach is by constructing a stochastic differential equation (SDE) to approximate the SGLD and then applying Stein’s method, as developed in Fang et al. [2019] ; Lu et al. [2022] , to decompose the empirical measure into a martingale difference series sum and a negligible remainder term. Key words : Self-normalized Cramér-type Moderate Deviation; Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics; Stein’s method; Diffusion approximation; Berry-Esseen bound MSC2020 : 60F10; 62E20; 60E05; 62F12; 62L20



1.
Introduction

For a nonconvex stochastic loss function
\psi(\omega,\zeta):\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{r}\to\mathbb{R}
, where
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
is a random variable with probability distribution
\nu
, we consider the following optimization problem

\displaystyle\omega^{*}=\text{argmin}_{\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}P(\omega),\quad P(\omega)=\mathbb{E}_{\zeta\sim\nu}\psi(\omega,\zeta).

To find the minimizer
\omega^{*}
,
Welling and Teh [2011]
proposed the stochastic gradient Langevin dynamic (SGLD) algorithm, which has been widely applied to optimization problems. The iteration of the SGLD is given as follows,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\omega_{k}=\omega_{k-1}-\eta\nabla\psi(\omega_{k-1},\zeta_{k})+\sqrt{\eta\delta}\xi_{k},

where
\eta>0
is the step size,
\delta>0
is the inverse temperature parameter,
(\xi_{k})_{k\geq 1}
are sequence of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) standard
d
-dimensional normal random vectors and
(\zeta_{k})_{k\geq 1}
are i.i.d. samples from
\nu
.

As the number of iterations
k
tends to infinity,
Raginsky et al. [2017]
showed that (
1.1
) can find the approximate global minimizer. See
Xu et al. [2018]
;
Lamperski [2021]
;
Chen et al. [2020]
for more details on the convergence of the SGLD. Unlike the stochastic gradient descent (SGD) algorithm, which may converge to local minima in non-convex optimization problems, the SGLD algorithm benefits from the inclusion of Gaussian noise in its iterations. This added noise allows SGLD to more effectively explore the parameter space, making it well-suited for solving non-convex problems

Chen et al. [2020]
;
Zhang et al. [2017]
.

For the iteration (
1.1
), it is natural to consider it as a discretization to a continuous dynamic for a given step size
\eta
. We consider the following stochastic differential equation (SDE) to approximate the SGLD algorithm,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}X_{t}=-\nabla P(X_{t})\mathrm{d}t+Q_{\eta,\delta}(X_{t})\mathrm{d}B_{t},

where
B_{t}
is a
d-
dimensional standard Brownian motion and the diffusion matrix
Q_{\eta,\delta}(\cdot)\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
will be defined later. Significant work has been done in
Feng et al. [2020]
;
Hu et al. [2019]
on comparing stochastic algorithms to their corresponding SDE approximations, and on establishing the diffusion approximation bound of

(1.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{h\in\mathcal{H}}|\mathbb{E}h(\omega_{k})-\mathbb{E}h(X_{k\eta})|

for a family
\mathcal{H}
of test functions
h
. Different choices of
\mathcal{H}
correspond to different distance metrics, such as the Wasserstein
-1
distance for the Lipschitz function
h
and the total variation distance for bounded
h
. The diffusion approximation provides valuable insights into algorithms by viewing them as continuous dynamics. Acting as a bridge, it enables the application of continuous dynamic analysis methods to study the properties of stochastic algorithms. See
Li et al. [2017]
;
Li et al. [2019]
;
Chen et al. [2022]
for more details.

Under suitable conditions on
\psi
, (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) are exponential ergodic with invariant measures
\pi_{\eta}
and
\pi
respectively. For the SGLD and its invariant measure
\pi_{\eta}
, we construct an empirical measure

\Pi_{\eta}
as a statistic of
\pi_{\eta}
, where

\displaystyle\Pi_{\eta}(\cdot)=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\delta_{\omega_{k}}(\cdot).

Here
\delta_{\omega_{k}}(\cdot)
is the Dirac measure of
\omega_{k}
. Since (
1.3
) converges to zero as
k\to\infty
and
\eta\to 0
, given test function
h:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, it is natural to consider the asymptotic property of
\Pi_{\eta}(h)
, i.e.,
\int h\mathrm{d}\Pi_{\eta}
.

The study of self-normalized Cramér type moderate deviation (SNCMD) explores the deviation properties of random variables and has developed over the last decades, see
Shao [1999]
;
Jing et al. [2003]
for the results of independent random variables. For dependent random variables,
Chen et al. [2016]
studied the moderate deviation under mixing conditions,
Fan et al. [2019]
focused on the self-normalized Cramér type moderate deviation for martingales, and
Fan et al. [2020]
analysed stationary sequences. We refer the reader to
Jiang et al. [2022]
;
Shao and Zhou [2016]
;
Zhang [2023]
for further reference. However, for the iterative output of a stochastic algorithm, which is a sequence of dependent random variables, there has been limited analysis of SNCMD for it. See
Teh et al. [2016]
;
Gao and Yiu [2023]
for the fluctuation analysis of stochastic algorithms.

In this paper, we analyse the self-normalized Cramér type moderate deviation of
\Pi_{\eta}(h)
with Lipschitz test function
h
. Specifically, given a normalized term
\mathcal{Y}_{\eta}
, we compare the tail probability of
\Pi_{\eta}(h)/\sqrt{\mathcal{Y}_{\eta}}

after scaling and centralization, i.e.,
\sqrt{m\eta}(\Pi_{\eta}(h)-\pi(h))/\sqrt{\delta\mathcal{Y}_{\eta}}
,
with the tail probability of a standard normal distribution
N(0,I_{d})
. Consequently, we also derive the Berry-Esseen bound.

Using the diffusion approximation and Stein’s method, we have, for the first time, investigated the self-normalized Cramér type moderate deviation of the SGLD algorithm, which provides a novel approach and perspective on the analysis of the asymptotic properties of the SGLD algorithm. Although
Lu et al. [2022]
;
Fan et al. [2024]
carried out a similar analysis, i.e. examining the SNCMD of the Langevin dynamics, their results are based on the relatively restrictive assumption that both the gradient and test functions are bounded.
In contrast, our results extend this assumption by replacing the boundedness assumption with a Lipschitz condition. To relax this condition, we employ a truncation technique in the proof. Additionally, our work provides a new application of Stein’s method within the realm of machine learning.

The approach to proving the main results relies on Stein’s method and a standard decomposition of
\Pi_{\eta}(h)
, with similar ideas found in
Lu et al. [2022]
;
Teh et al. [2016]
. The strategy of the proof begins with a diffusion approximation for the stochastic algorithm, constructing a corresponding SDE. Under some mild conditions, the SDE has an ergodic measure
\pi
, and its associated Stein’s equation has a solution with good regularity properties. Using Stein’s equation, we decompose
\Pi_{\eta}(h)
into a martingale difference series sum
\mathcal{H}_{\eta}
and a remainder
\mathcal{R}_{\eta}
. For
\mathcal{H}_{\eta}
, we apply the martingale SNCMD theorem in
Fan et al. [2024]
to compare it with the standard normal distribution. Additionally, we show that the remainder
\mathcal{R}_{\eta}
is exponentially negligible using concentration inequalities.

The paper is organized as follows. Diffusion approximation and our main results are stated in Section
2
. In Section
3
, we provide some preliminary lemmas. In Section
4
, we give the proof of SNCMD and Berry-Esseen bound. The details of the proof of preliminary lemmas are deferred to Sections
5
and
6
.

We finish this section by introducing some notations which will be frequently used in the sequel. The Euclidean norm and the inner product of vectors
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
are denoted by
|x|
and
\langle x,y\rangle
, respectively. For higher-rank tensors, we denote their norm by
\|\cdot\|
. For any two matrices
A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, the Hilbert-Schmidt norm is denoted by

\|A\|_{\text{HS}}=\sqrt{\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}A_{ij}^{2}}=\sqrt{\textrm{Tr}(A^{\top}A)}
and their inner product by

\langle A,B\rangle_{\text{HS}}:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}A_{ij}B_{ij}
, where
\top
is the transpose operator. The symbols
C
and
c
represent positive constants whose values may vary from line to line. Let
\text{Lip}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the family of Lipschitz function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with Lipschitz constant
1
. We denote the conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}[\cdot|\omega_{k}]
and conditional probability
\mathbb{P}(\cdot|\omega_{k})
by
\mathbb{E}_{k}[\cdot]
and
\mathbb{P}_{k}(\cdot)
, respectively. Finally,
\Phi(x)
represents the cumulative distribution function for standard normal random variables.

2.
Diffusion approximation and Main results

We first construct the diffusion approximation. Rewriting (
1.1
), we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle\omega_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{k-1}-\eta\nabla P(\omega_{k-1})+\eta\nabla P(\omega_{k-1})-\eta\nabla\psi(\omega_{k-1},\zeta_{k})+\sqrt{\eta\delta}\xi_{k}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\omega_{k-1}-\eta\nabla P(\omega_{k-1})+\sqrt{\eta}V_{\eta,\delta}(\omega_{k-1},\zeta_{k},\xi_{k}),

where

\displaystyle V_{\eta,\delta}(\omega_{k-1},\zeta_{k},\xi_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\eta}\nabla P(\omega_{k-1})-\sqrt{\eta}\nabla\psi(\omega_{k-1},\zeta_{k})+\sqrt{\delta}\xi_{k}.

As
\mathbb{E}\psi(\cdot,\zeta)=P(\cdot)
, a straightforward calculation implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k-1}[V_{\eta,\delta}(\omega_{k-1},\zeta_{k},\xi_{k})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{cov}[V_{\eta,\delta}(\omega_{k-1},\zeta_{k},\xi_{k})|\omega_{k-1}]=\eta\Sigma(\omega_{k-1})+\delta I_{d},

where

\displaystyle\Sigma(x)=\mathbb{E}[\nabla\psi(x,\zeta)\nabla\psi(x,\zeta)^{\top}]-\nabla P(x)\nabla P(x)^{\top}

and
I_{d}
is the
d-
dimensional identity matrix. Thus it is natural to consider the following SDE to approximate (
1.1
),

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}X_{t}=-\nabla P(X_{t})\mathrm{d}t+Q_{\eta,\delta}(X_{t})\mathrm{d}B_{t},

where

\displaystyle Q_{\eta,\delta}(x)=\big(\eta\Sigma(x)+\delta I_{d}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

is a positive definite matrix and
B_{t}
is a
d-
dimensional standard Brownian motion. For the cost function and random variable
\zeta
, we introduce the following conditions.

Assumption 2.1
.

There exist constants
L,K_{1}>0
and
K_{2}\geq 0
such that for every
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
z\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
,

(2.3)

\displaystyle|\nabla\psi(x,z)-\nabla\psi(y,z)|\leq L|x-y|,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\langle x-y,-\nabla\psi(x,z)+\nabla\psi(y,z)\rangle\leq-K_{1}|x-y|^{2}+K_{2}.

Assumption 2.2
.

The random variable
\nabla\psi(x,\zeta)
is sub-Gaussian for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, that is, there exist positive constants
K_{\zeta}
and
C
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\exp\{K_{\zeta}|\nabla\psi(x,\zeta)|^{2}\}\leq C.

Remark 2.3
.

For the ease of the proof, we assume that
K_{\zeta}=1
.

For the SGLD algorithm (
1.1
) and its corresponding SDE (
2.2
), Lemma
3.2
below implies that they are exponential ergodic with invariant measures
\pi_{\eta}
and
\pi
respectively. Then we have the following Wasserstein-1 distance bound between
\pi
and
\pi_{\eta}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Suppose Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
hold, one has

(2.5)

\displaystyle W_{1}(\pi,\pi_{\eta})\leq C\eta^{1/2},

where

\displaystyle W_{1}(\pi,\pi_{\eta})=\sup_{h\in\mathrm{Lip_{1}}}|\pi(h)-\pi_{\eta}(h)|

is the Wasserstein-
1
distance.

Let
f
be the solution to the following Stein’s equation:

(2.6)

\displaystyle h-\pi(h)=\mathcal{L}f,

where
\mathcal{L}
is the generator of (
2.2
) given by

(2.7)

\mathcal{L}g(x)=\langle-\nabla P(x),\nabla g(x)\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle Q_{\eta,\delta}(x),\nabla^{2}g(x)\rangle_{\text{HS}}\,,\quad g\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L}).

Denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{\eta}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}|\nabla f(\omega_{k})|^{2},\quad\mathcal{W}_{\eta}=\frac{\sqrt{m\eta}(\Pi_{\eta}(h)-\pi(h))}{\sqrt{\delta\mathcal{Y}_{\eta}}}.

We have the following SNCMD of the SGLD algorithm.

Theorem 2.5
.

Suppose Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
hold. Let
\omega_{0}\sim\pi_{\eta}
and
h\in\mathrm{Lip}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
, set
m=o(\eta^{-2})
with
m\eta\to\infty
. Then we have:

(i) as
m\leq\eta^{-\frac{13}{8}}\delta^{-\frac{9}{8}}
,

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{W}_{\eta}>x)}{1-\Phi(x)}-1\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\big(x^{3}m^{-\frac{1}{4}}+(1+x)m^{-\frac{1}{4}}\ln m+x^{6}\eta^{\frac{1}{2}}\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big)

uniformly for
0\leq x=o(\eta^{{-\frac{1}{12}}}\delta^{\frac{1}{12}})
as
\eta
tends to zero and
m
tends to infinity.

(ii) as
m>\eta^{-\frac{13}{8}}\delta^{-\frac{9}{8}}
,

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{W}_{\eta}>x)}{1-\Phi(x)}-1\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\big(x^{3}(m\eta\delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\sqrt{m}\eta\delta x+m^{-\frac{1}{4}}\ln m\big)

uniformly for
0\leq x=o((m\eta\delta)^{\frac{1}{6}}\wedge(\sqrt{m}\eta\delta)^{-1})
as
\eta
tends to zero and
m
tends to infinity.

Moreover, the same results hold when
\mathcal{W}_{\eta}
is replaced by
-\mathcal{W}_{\eta}
.

Based on Theorem
2.5
, we also derive the Berry-Esseen bound for the SGLD algorithm.

Theorem 2.6
.

Under the assumption of Theorem
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{W}_{\eta}\leq x)-\Phi(x)|\leq Cm^{-\frac{1}{4}}\ln m.

Further assume
m=C\eta^{-2}/|\ln\eta|
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{W}_{\eta}\leq x)-\Phi(x)|\leq C\eta^{\frac{1}{2}}|\ln\eta|^{5/4}.

By choosing a specific connection between the step size
\eta
and the number of iterations
m
, we can achieve the bound
\sqrt{\eta}|\ln\eta|^{5/4}
which is close to the convergence rate in Theorem
2.4
, up to a logarithmic correction. This result also has the same order as that in
Fan et al. [2024]
up to a logarithmic correction, though their result is under stronger conditions.

Remark 2.7
.

The assumption
\omega_{0}\sim\pi_{\eta}
in Theorems
2.5
and
2.6
is not essential. Due to the exponential ergodicity of the SGLD algorithm, one can extend it to the case in which
\omega_{0}
is subgaussian distributed. The advantage of taking
\omega_{0}\sim\pi_{\eta}
is that in the calculation, the terms describing the difference between the distribution of
\omega_{k}
and
\pi_{\eta}
will vanish, while in the general case, one has to use exponential ergodicity of
\omega_{k}
to bound the difference. Since
\omega_{k}
converges to
\pi_{\eta}
exponentially fast, the difference will not put an essential difficulty. For the ease of calculation, we only considered the case of
\omega_{0}\sim\pi_{\eta}
.

4.
Proof of Main result

In this section, we present the proof of our main result. The proof of Theorem
2.4
is based on the Stein method as developed in
[
Fang et al., 2019
, Theorem 2.5]
. For Theorems
2.5
and

, we analyze the normalized Cramér-type moderate deviations for martingales and demonstrate that the remainder term

Proof of Theorem
2.4
.

Let
(\omega_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be the Markov Chain with initial value
\omega_{0}\sim\pi_{\eta}
. The Taylor expansion implies that

(4.1)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

h\in C^{\alpha}(\bar{\Omega})
satisfy
\mathcal{L}f=h
in a bounded domain
\Omega
where

\mathcal{L}f(x)=\langle a(x),\nabla^{2}f(x)\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}+\langle b(x),\nabla f(x)\rangle,

is strictly elliptic and its coefficients are in
C^{\alpha}(\bar{\Omega})
. Then if
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
with
\text{dist}(\Omega^{\prime},\partial\Omega)\geq\bar{d}
, there is a constant
C
such that

(5.1)

\bar{d}\|\nabla f\|_{0;\Omega^{\prime}}+\bar{d}^{2}\|\nabla^{2}f\|_{0;\Omega^{\prime}}+\bar{d}^{2+\alpha}[\nabla^{2}f]_{\alpha;\Omega^{\prime}}\leq C(\|f\|_{0;\Omega}+\|h\|_{0,\alpha;\Omega}),

where the positive constant
C
depends only on the ellipticity constant and the
C^{\alpha}(\bar{\Omega})
norms of the coefficients of
\mathcal{L}
.

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

The existence and the expression of the solution
f
can be proved similarly as in
[
Fang et al., 2019
, Proposition 6.1]
. Now we show the regularities of it. According to (
3.6
),

\displaystyle|f(x)|\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}|\mathbb{E}[h(X_{t}^{x})]-\pi(h)|\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}C(1+|x|^{2})e^{-ct}\mathrm{d}t\leq C(1+|x|^{2}),

where the second inequality follows (
B.1
).

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, define
r(x)=\frac{1}{2(1+|x|)}\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
and

B_{r(x)}(x)=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x-z|\leq r(x)\}.

Consider
\Omega=B_{r(y)}(y)
and
\Omega^{\prime}=B_{r(y)/2}(y)
for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
in Lemma
5.1
. Then we have

dist(\Omega^{\prime},\partial\Omega)\geq\frac{r(y)}{2}=\frac{1}{4(1+|y|)}.

Therefore, we take
\bar{d}=\frac{1}{4(1+|y|)}
.
Taking
\alpha=1
in Lemma
5.1
and considering operator (
2.7
),

\mathcal{L}f=\langle-\nabla P,\nabla f\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle Q_{\eta,\delta},\nabla^{2}f\rangle_{\text{HS}}.

The notation of
Q_{\eta,\delta}
implies that
\mathcal{L}
is strictly elliptic, thus (
3.1
) and (
3.3
) yield that its coefficients are Lipschitz functions in
\bar{\Omega}
which satisfy the condition of Lemma
5.1
. Then we have

(5.2)

\displaystyle r(y)\|\nabla f\|_{0;\Omega^{\prime}}\leq C(\|f\|_{0;\Omega}+\|h\|_{0,1;\Omega}),

(5.3)

\displaystyle r(y)^{2}\|\nabla^{2}f\|_{0;\Omega^{\prime}}\leq C(\|f\|_{0;\Omega}+\|h\|_{0,1;\Omega}),

(5.4)

\displaystyle r(y)^{3}[\nabla^{2}f]_{1;\Omega^{\prime}}\leq C(\|f\|_{0;\Omega}+\|h\|_{0,1;\Omega}).

For the equality (
3.8
), since

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r(x)\mathrm{d}x=\infty,

for any
0<r_{0}\leq 1
, we have

B_{r_{0}}(x)\subset\bigcup_{y\in B_{r_{0}(x)}}B_{r_{(y)/2}}(y)=\bigcup_{y\in B_{r_{0}(x)}}\Omega^{\prime}.

Combining with (
5.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\nabla f\|_{0;B_{r_{0}}(x)}\leq\sup_{y\in B_{r_{0}}(x)}\|\nabla f\|_{0;B_{r(y)/2}(y)}\leq\sup_{y\in B_{r_{0}}(x)}C(1+|y|)(\|f\|_{0;\Omega}+\|h\|_{0,1;\Omega}).

(
3.7
) implies that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{0;\Omega}\leq\sup_{z\in B_{r(y)}(y)}|f(z)|\leq\sup_{z\in B_{r(y)}(y)}C(1+|z|^{2})\leq C(1+|y|^{2}).

Since
h
is the Lipschitz function, then we have

\displaystyle\|h\|_{0,1;\Omega}\leq\sup_{z\in B_{r(y)}(y)}|h(z)|+\sup_{z_{1},z_{2}\in B_{r(y)}(y)}\frac{|h(z_{1})-h(z_{2})|}{|z_{1}-z_{2}|}\leq C(1+|y|^{2}).

Thus,

\displaystyle\|\nabla f\|_{0;B_{r_{0}}(x)}\leq\sup_{y\in B_{r_{0}}(x)}C(1+|y|)(1+|y^{2}|)\leq C(1+|x|^{3}),

which yields

\displaystyle|\nabla f(x)|\leq C(1+|x|^{3}).

Similarly, (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) imply

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}f\|_{0;B_{r_{0}}(x)}\leq C(1+|x|^{4}),

\displaystyle[\nabla^{2}f]_{1;B_{r_{0}}(x)}\leq C(1+|x|^{5}).

Thus, we can obtain (
3.9
) and (
3.10
).
∎

6.
Estimation of the remainder
\mathcal{R}_{\eta}

We will give in this section several lemmas of
\mathcal{R}_{\eta}
which play a crucial role in proving the main results. In order to estimate the tail probability of
\mathcal{R}_{\eta}
, we need the following four lemmas, the first three lemmas paving the way for proving Lemma
3.4
.

Lemma 6.1
.

For small enough
\gamma>0
, one has

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\exp\{\gamma|\omega_{k}|^{2}\}\leq C,

for any
k
.

Proof.

For small enough
\gamma>0
and any constant
k
, (
1.1
) implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\exp\{\gamma|\omega_{k+1}|^{2}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\big\{\gamma(|\omega_{k}|^{2}+|\eta\nabla\psi(\omega_{k},\zeta_{k+1})|^{2}+2\langle\omega_{k},-\eta\nabla\psi(\omega_{k},\zeta_{k+1})\rangle)\big\}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad\mathbb{E}_{k}[\exp\{\eta\delta\gamma|\xi_{k+1}|^{2}+2\gamma\langle\omega_{k}-\eta\nabla\psi(\omega_{k},\zeta_{k+1}),\sqrt{\eta\delta}\xi_{k+1}\rangle\}|\zeta_{k+1}]\right]

A straight calculation to the conditional expectation with respect to the Gaussian random variable
\xi_{k+1}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\exp\{\eta\delta\gamma|\xi_{k+1}|^{2}+2\gamma\langle\omega_{k}-\eta\nabla\psi(\omega_{k},\zeta_{k+1}),\sqrt{\eta\delta}\xi_{k+1}\rangle\}|\zeta_{k+1}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-2\eta\delta\gamma}}\exp\big\{\frac{2\eta\delta\gamma^{2}}{1-2\eta\delta\gamma}\left|\omega_{k}-\eta\nabla\psi\left(\omega_{k},\zeta_{k+1}\right)\right|^{2}\big\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-2\eta\delta\gamma}}\exp\big\{\frac{4\eta\delta\gamma^{2}}{1-2\eta\delta\gamma}(|\omega_{k}|^{2}+\eta^{2}|\nabla\psi\left(\omega_{k},\zeta_{k+1}\right)|^{2})\big\}.

Here
\gamma
is chosen to be small enough that
1-2\eta\delta\gamma>0
. Then one has,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\exp\{\gamma|\omega_{k+1}|^{2}\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-2\eta\delta\gamma}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\big\{(1+\frac{4\eta\delta\gamma}{1-2\eta\delta\gamma})\gamma|\omega_{k}|^{2}+(1+\frac{4\eta\delta\gamma}{1-2\eta\delta\gamma})\gamma\eta^{2}|\nabla\psi(\omega_{k},\zeta_{k+1})|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+2\gamma\langle\omega_{k},-\eta\nabla_{k}(\omega_{k},\zeta_{k+1})\big\}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\exp\{2\gamma\eta K_{2}\}}{\sqrt{1-2\eta\delta\gamma}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\big\{\big(1+\frac{4\eta\delta\gamma}{1-2\eta\delta\gamma}+2(1+\frac{4\eta\delta\gamma}{1-2\eta\delta\gamma})\eta^{2}L^{2}-K_{1}\eta\big)\gamma|\omega_{k}|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\big(2\gamma\eta^{2}(1+\frac{4\eta\delta\gamma}{1-2\eta\delta\gamma})+\frac{\gamma\eta}{K_{1}}\big)|\nabla\psi(0,\zeta_{k+1})|^{2}\big\}\Big].

Since
\omega_{k}
and
\zeta_{k+1}
are independent, and
\nabla\psi(0,\zeta_{k+1})
is sub-Gaussian, we can choose small enough
\gamma
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\exp\{\gamma|\omega_{k+1}|^{2}\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\exp\{2\gamma\eta K_{2}\}}{\sqrt{1-2\eta\delta\gamma}}\mathbb{E}\big[\exp\big\{(1-\frac{1}{2}K_{1}\eta)\gamma|\omega_{k}|^{2}\big\}\big]

\displaystyle\times\Big(\mathbb{E}\big[\exp\big\{(2\gamma\eta(1+\frac{4\eta\delta\gamma}{1-2\eta\delta\gamma})+\frac{\gamma}{k_{1}})|\nabla\psi(0,\zeta_{k+1})|^{2}\big\}\big]\Big)^{\eta}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C^{\eta}}{\sqrt{1-2\eta\delta\gamma}}\big(\mathbb{E}\exp\{\gamma|\omega_{k}|^{2}\}\big)^{1-\frac{1}{2}K_{1}\eta},

where the last line follows the Hölder inequality. Inductively, we can get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\exp\{\gamma|\omega_{k+1}|^{2}\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C^{\eta}}{\sqrt{1-2\eta\delta\gamma}}\big(\mathbb{E}\exp\{\gamma|\omega_{k}|^{2}\}\big)^{1-\frac{1}{2}K_{1}\eta}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{C^{\eta}}{\sqrt{1-2\eta\delta\gamma}}\Big)^{\frac{c}{\eta}}\big(\mathbb{E}\exp\{\gamma|\omega_{0}|^{2}\}\big)^{(1-K_{1}\eta/2)^{k+1}}\leq C.

Thus the exponential moment of
|\omega_{k}|^{2}
exists for any
k
and small enough
\gamma
.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Let Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
hold, considering the martingale difference

(\Psi(\omega_{k},\theta_{k+1}),\mathcal{F}_{k+1})_{k\geq 0}
with

(6.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}|\Psi(\omega_{k},\theta_{k+1})|^{i}\leq C^{i}(1+|\omega_{k}|^{\alpha i}+i!),

where
\alpha\geq 0
and
i
is any positive integer. Then for
\sqrt{m}=o(x)
, we have

(6.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\langle\nabla f(\omega_{k}),\Psi(\omega_{k},\theta_{k+1})\rangle>x\Big)\leq C\exp\{c(x^{2}/m)^{\frac{1}{4+\alpha}}\}.

Similarly,

(6.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\langle\nabla^{2}f(\omega_{k}),\Psi(\omega_{k},\theta_{k+1})\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}>x\Big)\leq C\exp\{c(x^{2}/m)^{\frac{1}{5+\alpha}}\}.

Here
f
is the solution of Stein’s equation given in Lemma
3.3
.

Proof.

Denote
\hat{\omega}_{k}=\omega_{k}1_{\{|\omega_{k}|\leq y\}}
for large enough
y
to be chosen later,
A=\{|\omega_{k}|\leq y,k=0,1,...,m-1\}
and
A^{C}
as its complement, then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\langle\nabla f(\omega_{k}),\Psi(\omega_{k},\theta_{k+1})\rangle>x\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\langle\nabla f(\omega_{k}),\Psi(\omega_{k},\theta_{k+1})\rangle>x,A\Big)+\mathbb{P}(A^{C})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\langle\nabla f(\hat{\omega}_{k}),\Psi(\hat{\omega}_{k},\theta_{k+1})\rangle>x\Big)+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{P}\big(|\omega_{k}|>y\big)

(6.4)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{-\lambda x}\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\lambda\langle\nabla f(\hat{\omega}_{k}),\Psi(\hat{\omega}_{k},\theta_{k+1})\rangle\Big\}+e^{-\gamma y^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{E}\exp\{\gamma|\omega_{k}|^{2}\},

where the last inequality follows from the Markov inequality,
\lambda
is a positive constant to be chosen later, and
\gamma
is a sufficiently small positive constant. For the second term of (
6.4
), Lemma
6.1
implies

\displaystyle e^{-\gamma y^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{E}\exp\{\gamma|\omega_{k}|^{2}\}\leq mCe^{-\gamma y^{2}}.

For the first term of (
6.4
), it is easy to see that,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\lambda\langle\nabla f(\hat{\omega}_{k}),\Psi(\hat{\omega}_{k},\theta_{k+1})\rangle\Big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\big\{\sum_{k=0}^{m-2}\lambda\langle\nabla f(\hat{\omega}_{k}),\Psi(\hat{\omega}_{k},\theta_{k+1})\rangle\big\}\mathbb{E}_{m-1}\exp\{\lambda\langle\nabla f(\hat{\omega}_{m-1}),\Psi(\hat{\omega}_{m-1},\theta_{m})\rangle\}\Big].

Noticing

\lambda\mathbb{E}_{m-1}\langle\nabla f(\hat{\omega}_{m-1}),\Psi(\hat{\omega}_{m-1},\theta_{m})\rangle=0,

by the Taylor expansion to the conditional expectation above, (
3.8
) and (
6.1
) imply

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{m-1}\exp\{\lambda\mathbb{E}_{m-1}\langle\nabla f(\hat{\omega}_{m-1}),\Psi(\hat{\omega}_{k},\theta_{m})\rangle\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{i}}{i!}\mathbb{E}_{m-1}\langle\nabla f(\hat{\omega}_{m-1}),\Psi(\hat{\omega}_{k},\theta_{m})\rangle^{i}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\frac{(C\lambda)^{i}}{i!}(1+y^{3})^{i}(1+y^{\alpha i}+i!)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1+\frac{(C\lambda y^{3+\alpha})^{2}}{1-C\lambda y^{3+\alpha}},

if
C\lambda y^{3+\alpha}<1
. By induction, we can get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\exp\Big\{\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\lambda\langle\nabla f(\hat{\omega}_{k}),\Psi(\hat{\omega}_{k},\theta_{k+1})\rangle\Big\}\leq\Big(1+\frac{(C\lambda y^{3+\alpha})^{2}}{1-C\lambda y^{3+\alpha}}\Big)^{m}.

Thus for (
6.4
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\langle\nabla f(\omega_{k}),\Psi(\omega_{k},\theta_{k+1})\rangle>x\Big)\leq\Big(1+\frac{(C\lambda y^{3+\alpha})^{2}}{1-C\lambda y^{3+\alpha}}\Big)^{m}e^{-\lambda x}+mCe^{-\gamma y^{2}}.

Let
\lambda=\frac{x}{2mC^{2}y^{6+2\alpha}}
and
y=(\frac{x^{2}}{2mC^{2}})^{\frac{1}{8+2\alpha}}
,
for large enough
m
and
\sqrt{m}=o(x)
, one can get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\langle\nabla f(\omega_{k}),\Psi(\omega_{k},\theta_{k+1})\rangle>x\Big)\leq Ce^{-c(x^{2}/m)^{\frac{1}{4+\alpha}}}.

Similarly, we can show (
6.3
). The details are omitted here.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.4
.

Recalling the definition of
\mathcal{R}_{\eta}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|\mathcal{R}_{\eta}|>y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{4}\mathbb{P}(|\mathcal{R}_{\eta,i}|>\frac{y}{4}),

and shall prove below that the following estimates hold:

(6.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|\mathcal{R}_{\eta,1}|>y/4)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ce^{-c\sqrt{m\eta\delta}y},

(6.6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\mathcal{R}_{\eta,2}\right|>y/4\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ce^{-c\delta^{\frac{1}{5}}y^{\frac{2}{5}}\eta^{-\frac{1}{5}}},

(6.7)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\mathcal{R}_{\eta,3}\right|>y/4\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ce^{-cy^{\frac{2}{9}}\eta^{-\frac{2}{9}}\delta^{-\frac{2}{9}}},

(6.8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\mathcal{R}_{\eta,4}\right|>y/4\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ce^{-cy^{\frac{2}{7}}\delta^{\frac{1}{7}}\eta^{-\frac{3}{7}}}+Ce^{-cy^{\frac{2}{5}}\delta^{-\frac{1}{5}}\eta^{-\frac{1}{5}}}.

Combining these estimates, we immediately get

\mathbb{P}(|\mathcal{R}_{\eta}|>y)\leq C\Big(e^{-cy\eta^{\frac{1}{2}}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}m^{\frac{1}{2}}}+e^{-cy^{\frac{2}{5}}\delta^{\frac{1}{5}}\eta^{-\frac{1}{5}}}+e^{-cy^{\frac{1}{6}}\eta^{-\frac{5}{12}}\delta^{-\frac{5}{12}}}+e^{-cy^{\frac{2}{7}}\delta^{\frac{1}{7}}\eta^{-\frac{3}{7}}}\Big),

for
c(\eta^{\frac{1}{2}}\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\vee m^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta\delta)\leq y\leq C\eta^{-\frac{7}{2}}\delta^{-\frac{7}{2}}
. Now we show (
6.5
)-(
6.8
) below.

a) Control of
\mathcal{R}_{\eta,1}
.

By the Markov inequality and (
3.7
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|\mathcal{R}_{\eta,1}|>y/4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\gamma|f(\omega_{0})-f(\omega_{m})|>\gamma\sqrt{m\eta\delta}y/4)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\exp\{C\gamma(1+|\omega_{0}|^{2}+|\omega_{m}|^{2})\}e^{-\gamma\sqrt{m\eta\delta}y/4}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\mathbb{E}\exp\{2C\gamma|\omega_{0}|^{2}\})^{1/2}(\mathbb{E}\exp\{2C\gamma|\omega_{m}|^{2}\})^{1/2}e^{-\gamma\sqrt{m\eta\delta}y/4+C\gamma},

where
\gamma
is a positive constant. Lemma
6.1
implies that the exponential moments of
\omega_{0}
and
\omega_{m}
are finite for small enough
\gamma
. Thus

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|\mathcal{R}_{\eta,1}|>y/4)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ce^{-c\sqrt{m\eta\delta}y}.

b) Control of
\mathcal{R}_{\eta,2}
.

According to the definition of
\mathcal{R}_{\eta,2}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{R}_{\eta,2}>y/4\right)=\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\langle\nabla f(\omega_{k}),\nabla P(\omega_{k})-\nabla\psi(\omega_{k},\zeta_{k+1})\rangle>\frac{\sqrt{m\delta}y}{4\sqrt{\eta}}\Big).

Since
\nabla\psi(0,\zeta_{k+1})
is sub-Gaussian from Assumption
2.2
, we have

\mathbb{E}|\nabla\psi(0,\zeta_{k+1})|^{i}\leq Ci!.

By (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}|\nabla P(\omega_{k})-\nabla\psi(\omega_{k},\zeta_{k+1})|^{i}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}\big[2L|\omega_{k}|+|\nabla P(0)|+|\nabla\psi(0,\zeta_{k+1})|\big]^{i}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{i}(1+|\omega_{k}|^{i}+i!),

which satisfies the condition of Lemma
6.2
with
\alpha=1
. Thus, (
6.2
) yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{R}_{\eta,2}>y/4\right)\leq C\exp\{-c\delta^{\frac{1}{5}}y^{\frac{2}{5}}\eta^{-\frac{1}{5}}\},

under the condition
y>\sqrt{\eta/\delta}
.

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{R}_{\eta,2}<-y/4\right)
can be estimated similarly. Thus (
6.6
) is proved.

c) Control of
\mathcal{R}_{\eta,3}
.
Let
A=\{|\Delta\omega_{k}|<y_{1}\leq 1,k=0,1,...,m-1\}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{R}_{\eta,3}>y/4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}s\big\langle\frac{\nabla^{2}f(\omega_{k}+ss^{\prime}\Delta\omega_{k})-\nabla^{2}f(\omega_{k})}{|ss^{\prime}\Delta\omega_{k}|},\Delta\omega_{k}\Delta\omega_{k}^{\top}\big\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}|ss^{\prime}\Delta\omega_{k}|\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}\mathrm{d}s>\frac{\sqrt{m\eta\delta}y}{4}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|\nabla^{2}f(\omega_{k}+ss^{\prime}\Delta\omega_{k})-\nabla^{2}f(\omega_{k})|}{|ss^{\prime}\Delta\omega_{k}|}|\Delta\omega_{k}|^{3}\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}\mathrm{d}s>\frac{\sqrt{m\eta\delta}y}{4},A\Big)+\mathbb{P}(A^{C})

For the first term, (
3.10
) implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|\nabla^{2}f(\omega_{k}+ss^{\prime}\Delta\omega_{k})-\nabla^{2}f(\omega_{k})|}{|ss^{\prime}\Delta\omega_{k}|}|\Delta\omega_{k}|^{3}\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}\mathrm{d}s>\frac{\sqrt{m\eta\delta}y}{4},A\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}C(1+|\omega_{k}|^{5})|\Delta\omega_{k}|^{3}1_{\{|\Delta\omega_{k}|<y_{1}\}}\geq\sqrt{m\eta\delta}y\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}C(1+|\omega_{k}|^{5})|\Delta\omega_{k}|^{3}1_{\{|\Delta\omega_{k}|<y_{1}\}}\geq\sqrt{m\eta\delta}y,|\omega_{k}|<y_{2}\text{ for any }k\big)

\displaystyle~~+\mathbb{P}(\max_{k\in\{0,...,m-1\}}|\omega_{k}|\geq y_{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\big\{-\frac{C(\sqrt{m\eta\delta}y-m(\eta\delta)^{\frac{3}{2}})^{2}}{my_{2}^{10}y_{1}^{6}}\big\}+Cme^{-y_{2}^{2}},

where the last inequality follows
[
Dedecker and Gouëzel, 2015
, Theorem 2]
and the fact
\mathbb{E}|\Delta\omega_{k}|^{3}\leq C(\eta\delta)^{\frac{3}{2}}
.

For the second term, a straight calculation implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(A^{C})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{P}(|\Delta\omega_{k}|>y_{1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{P}(\eta|\nabla\psi(\omega_{k},\zeta_{k+1})|)>y_{1}/2)+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{P}(\sqrt{\eta\delta}|\xi_{k+1}|>y_{1}/2)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{P}(|\omega_{k}|>\frac{Cy_{1}}{\eta})+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{P}(|\nabla\psi(0,\zeta_{k+1})|)>\frac{Cy_{1}}{\eta})+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{P}(|\xi_{k+1}|>\frac{Cy_{1}}{\sqrt{\eta\delta}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2me^{-Cy_{1}^{2}/\eta^{2}}+me^{-Cy_{1}^{2}/(\eta\delta)},

where the second inequality follows the iteration of
\omega_{k}
, the last inequality follows Lemma
6.1
and Assumption
2.2
. Combining the calculation above, we can get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{R}_{\eta,3}>y/4)\leq\exp\big\{-\frac{C(\sqrt{m\eta\delta}y-m(\eta\delta)^{\frac{3}{2}})^{2}}{my_{2}^{10}y_{1}^{6}}\big\}+Cme^{-y_{2}^{2}}+2me^{-Cy_{1}^{2}/\eta^{2}}+me^{-Cy_{1}^{2}/(\eta\delta)}.

Taking
y_{1}=y^{1/9}\eta^{7/18}\delta^{7/18}
and
y_{2}=y^{1/9}\eta^{-1/9}\delta^{-1/9}
, we complete the proof of (
6.7
), that is,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\mathcal{R}_{\eta,3}\right|>y/4\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ce^{-cy^{\frac{2}{9}}\eta^{-\frac{2}{9}}\delta^{-\frac{2}{9}}},

for
c(\sqrt{m}\eta\delta\vee\eta\delta)<y<C\eta^{-\frac{7}{2}}\delta^{-\frac{7}{2}}
.

d) Control of
\mathcal{R}_{\eta,4}
.
Following the notations of
\Sigma(\omega_{k})
and
\Delta\omega_{k}
, we have

(6.9)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{R}_{\eta,4}>y/4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{m\eta\delta}}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\langle\nabla^{2}f(\omega_{k}),\eta^{2}I_{1,k}+\eta\delta I_{2,k}+\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}I_{3,k}+\eta^{2}I_{4,k}\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}>y/4\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\langle\nabla^{2}f(\omega_{k}),I_{1,k}\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}>Cm^{\frac{1}{2}}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{-\frac{3}{2}}y\big)+\mathbb{P}\big(\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\langle\nabla^{2}f(\omega_{k}),I_{2,k}\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}>Cm^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}y\big)

Proof of Lemma

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Since
\nabla P(x)=\mathbb{E}[\nabla\psi(x,\zeta)]
, it is easy to see that
\nabla P
has the same properties, that is,

\displaystyle|\nabla P(x)-\nabla P(y)|\leq L|x-y|,

\displaystyle\langle x-y,-\nabla P(x)+\nabla P(y)\rangle\leq-K_{1}|x-y|^{2}+K_{2},

for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Following the assumptions (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), we can further have the bounds for
\nabla\psi(x,z)
and
\nabla P(x)
, that is,

\displaystyle|\nabla\psi(x,\zeta)|\leq L|x|+|\nabla\psi(0,\zeta)|,

\displaystyle|\nabla P(x)|\leq L|x|+|\nabla P(0)|.

Assumptions (
2.4
), (
3.2
) and Young’s inequality imply

\displaystyle\langle x,-\nabla\psi(x,\zeta)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle x-0,-\nabla\psi(x,\zeta)+\nabla\psi(0,\zeta)\rangle-\langle x,\nabla\psi(0,\zeta)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-K_{1}|x|^{2}+K_{2}+\frac{K_{1}}{2}|x|^{2}+\frac{1}{2K_{1}}|\nabla\psi(0,\zeta)|^{2}

(A.1)

\displaystyle=-\frac{K_{1}}{2}|x|^{2}+K_{2}+\frac{1}{2K_{1}}|\nabla\psi(0,\zeta)|^{2}.

Similarly,

(A.2)

\displaystyle\langle x,-\nabla P(x)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{K_{1}}{2}|x|^{2}+K_{2}+\frac{1}{2K_{1}}|\nabla P(0)|^{2}

Moreover,

(A.3)

\displaystyle\|\Sigma(x)\|\leq 2\mathbb{E}|\nabla\psi(x,\zeta)|^{2}\leq 4L^{2}|x|^{2}+C.

For the Lipschitz property of
Q_{\eta,\delta}
, recall that

Q_{\eta,\delta}(x)=\big(\mathbb{E}[V_{\eta,\delta}(x,\zeta,\xi)V_{\eta,\delta}(x,\zeta,\xi)^{\top}]\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

By Assumption
2.3
and
3.1
, the definition of
V_{\eta,\delta}(x,\zeta,\xi)
implies that

\displaystyle|V_{\eta,\delta}(x,\zeta,\xi)-V_{\eta,\delta}(y,\zeta,\xi)|\leq 2\sqrt{\eta}L|x-y|,

\displaystyle Q_{\eta,\delta}(x)=\|V_{\eta,\delta}(x,\zeta,\xi)V_{\eta,\delta}(x,\zeta,\xi)^{\top})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L^{2}}

We first give the proof of the ergodicity of
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. For the Lyapunov function
V(x)=|x|^{2}+1
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, (
2.7
), (
A.2
) and (
A.3
) imply

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}V(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle\nabla P(x),2x\rangle+\langle\eta\Sigma(x)+\delta I_{d},I_{d}\rangle_{\textrm{HS}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-K_{1}|x|^{2}+4\eta L^{2}|x|^{2}+C.

For small enough
\eta\leq K_{1}/(8L^{2})
, one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}V(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{K_{1}}{4}V(x)+(C+\frac{K_{1}}{4})1_{\{|x|^{2}\leq K_{1}+4C\}}.

By
[
Meyn and Tweedie, 1993
, Theorem 6.1]
,
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is exponential ergodic with invariant measure
\pi
, that is, there esxist constant
C
and
c
such that

(B.1)

\displaystyle\sup_{|h|\leq V}|\mathbb{E}h(X_{t}^{x})-\pi(h)|\leq CV(x)e^{-ct}.

The ergodicity of
(\omega_{k})_{k\geq 0}
follows
[
Tuominen and Tweedie, 1994
, Theorem 2.1]
. Notice that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[V(\omega_{k+1})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\mathbb{E}_{k}|\omega_{k}-\eta\nabla\psi(\omega_{k},\zeta_{k+1})+\sqrt{\eta\delta}\xi_{k+1}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+|\omega_{k}|^{2}+\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}|\nabla\psi(\omega_{k},\zeta_{k+1})|^{2}+\eta\delta d-2\eta\langle\omega_{k},\nabla P(\omega_{k})\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+2\eta^{2}L^{2}-\eta K_{1})|\omega_{k}|^{2}+1+C\eta.

Denote the transition probability of
(\omega_{k})_{k\geq 0}
by
P(x,\mathrm{d}y)
for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let

\displaystyle\quad V^{n}(x)=e^{c_{1}n\eta}V(x),\quad r(n)=c_{1}\eta e^{c_{1}n\eta}.

A straightforward calculation implies

\displaystyle PV^{n+1}(x)+r(n)V(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{c_{1}(n+1)\eta}PV(x)+c_{1}\eta e^{c_{1}n\eta}V(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{c_{1}(n+1)\eta}\big((1+2\eta^{2}L^{2}-\eta K_{1})|x|^{2}+1+C\eta\big)+c_{1}\eta e^{c_{1}n\eta}V(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{c_{1}n\eta}V(x)+c_{1}\eta e^{c_{1}n\eta}\big[\big(\frac{e^{c_{1}\eta}}{c_{1}\eta}(1+2\eta^{2}L^{2}-\eta K_{1})+1-\frac{1}{c_{1}\eta}\big)V(x)+\frac{e^{c_{1}\eta}}{c_{1}\eta}(C\eta+\eta K_{1}-2\eta^{2}L^{2})\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V^{n}(x)+r(n)\big[\frac{1}{c_{1}\eta}\big(e^{c_{1}\eta}(1+2\eta^{2}L^{2}-\eta K_{1})+c_{1}\eta-1\big)V(x)+\frac{e^{c_{1}\eta}}{c_{1}\eta}(C\eta+\eta K_{1}-2\eta^{2}L^{2})\big].

Choosing
\eta
small enough such that
e^{c_{1}\eta}(1+2\eta^{2}L^{2}-\eta K_{1})+c_{1}\eta<1
, we can get

\displaystyle PV^{n+1}(x)+r(n)V(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle V^{n}(x)+br(n)1_{\{x\in\mathcal{C}\}},

where
b=\frac{e^{c_{1}\eta}}{c_{1}\eta}(C\eta+\eta K_{1}-2\eta^{2}L^{2})
and
\mathcal{C}=\{x:V(x)\leq\frac{e^{c_{1}\eta}(C\eta+\eta K_{1}-2\eta^{2}L^{2})}{1-e^{c_{1}\eta}(1+2\eta^{2}L^{2}-2\eta K_{1})-c_{1}\eta}\}
.
[
Tuominen and Tweedie, 1994
, Theorem 2.1]
implies that
(\omega_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is ergodic with invariant measure
\pi_{\eta}
, that is, there exist constant
C
and
c
such that

(B.2)

\displaystyle\sup_{|h|\leq V}|\mathbb{E}h(\omega_{k}^{x})-\pi_{\eta}(h)|\leq C\eta^{-1}V(x)e^{-ck\eta}.

∎

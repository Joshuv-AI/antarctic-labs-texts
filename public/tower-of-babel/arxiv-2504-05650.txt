Introduction

At present, there are still some gaps between the physical observables detected by many experiments and the predictions of the Standard Model (SM). Many interesting observables require an accurate Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa (CKM) matrix element, which is a unitary
3\times 3
matrix that encodes the probability for quark flavor transition and is important to the search for
CP
violation beyond the SM. Generally, compared with other rows of CKM matrix, the test of first row CKM unitarity
|V_{ud}|^{2}+|V_{us}|^{2}+|V_{ub}|^{2}=1
is more attractive, because it has achieved a very good accuracy in the current experimental and theoretical prediction
Seng:2018yzq
. Based on the average results of
|V_{ud}|
,
|V_{us}|
, and
|V_{ub}|
provided by the 2024 Particle Data Group (PDG)
ParticleDataGroup:2024cfk
, one can deduce the corresponding accuracies
|\delta V_{\rm{CKM}}|/|V_{\rm{CKM}}|
as
0.033\%
,
0.38\%
, and
5.2\%
. Therefore, the uncertainty in the unitarity of the first row of the CKM matrix is mainly dominated by
|V_{ub}|
. Currently, the
|V_{ub}|
can be determined by the exclusive charmless semileptonic
B
-meson decays which can describe the decay process more precisely, thereby providing better kinematic constraints and more effective background suppression than inclusive decays, and aiding in more accurately identifying signals in experiments. Such as
B\to\pi\ell\nu
,
B\to\rho\ell\nu
, and
B\to\omega\ell\nu
, etc
BaBar:2010efp
;
Belle:2006hlt
;
CLEO:1999mif
;
BaBar:2010npl
;
LHCb:2020ist
. Among them, the world average result of
|V_{ub}|
is mainly extracted from
B\to\pi\ell\nu
decay. However, if we study the processes of decay into other mesons, we can investigate observables from another perspective. This is highly beneficial for us to further test theoretical calculations, improve accuracy, and enhance our understanding of charmless semileptonic decay components. Thus, in this work, we will present a study for the semileptonic
B^{+}\to\omega\ell^{+}\nu
decay, in which the
\omega
is a neutral vector meson.

(1.3\pm 0.4\pm 0.2\pm 0.3)\times 10^{-4}
. Additionally, since 2008, with the continuously improving detection accuracy of the BaBar detector, the BaBar Collaboration has conducted four measurements of

BaBar:2008vqc
;
BaBar:2012dvs

BaBar:2012thb

Therefore, based on the existing experimental data, the theoretical predictions for
B^{+}\to\omega\ell^{+}\bar{\nu}

In the exclusive decay processes, there are various hadronic matrix elements involved. When calculating them, we must consider physical mesons rather than free quarks. Therefore, to facilitate calculations, we can introduce a set of Lorentz-invariant form factors, also are transition form factors (TFFs), to provide a comprehensive description of these QCD processes. The TFFs of

;

;

;

Specifically, a precise understanding of the internal quark structure of mesons is one of the primary conditions in theoretical studies. The neutral mesons
\omega
and
\rho
share similar properties as vector meson with spin-parity
J^{P}=1^{-}
. But, the constituent parts of
\omega
-meson are somewhat special in that it may possess a hidden flavor. This is because it can undergo mixing effects with other neutral mesons that also possess hidden flavors and have the same quantum numbers, through both strong and electromagnetic interactions. For example, the
\omega-\phi

\omega

BESIII:2015kin
;
Gronau:2009mp
;
Li:2020ylu
. However, the
B^{+}\to\omega
transition discussed in this paper is induced by

\omega-\phi
mixing on the components of the flavor WF of
\omega
-meson. At present, there are two main schemes to deal with
\omega-\phi

\omega
-meson and the
\phi
-meson are mixtures of the SU(3) singlet
\omega_{0}

\omega_{8}

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\phi\cr\omega\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta_{V}&-\sin\theta_{V}\cr\sin\theta_{V}&~\cos\theta_{V}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\omega_{8}\cr\omega_{0}\end{pmatrix},

(1)

where
\omega_{8}=(u\bar{u}+d\bar{d}-2s\bar{s})/\sqrt{6}
and
\omega_{0}=(u\bar{u}+d\bar{d}+s\bar{s})/\sqrt{3}

\omega

\displaystyle|\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sin\varphi_{V}|\omega_{q}\rangle-\cos\varphi_{V}|\omega_{s}\rangle,

(2)

\displaystyle|\omega\rangle

\displaystyle=\cos\varphi_{V}|\omega_{q}\rangle+\sin\varphi_{V}|\omega_{s}\rangle.

(3)

The quark flavor bases are
|\omega_{q}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(u\bar{u}+d\bar{d})
and
|\omega_{s}\rangle=s\bar{s}
. The above equation clearly demonstrates that the mixing angle

For this, the experimental and theoretical studies have been conducted. The Ref.
Kucukarslan:2006wk

. As can be seen from Eq. (
3

In LCSR approach, the operator product expansion (OPE) is carried out near the light-cone
x^{2}\rightsquigarrow 0
. The nonperturbative effects are replaced by physically meaningful hadronic light-cone distribution amplitudes (LCDAs) with the progressively increasing twists. Compared with pseudoscalar mesons, such as
\pi,K
etc., the LCDAs of vector mesons are more complex, which contain both chiral-odd and chiral-even LCDAs arising from chiral-odd and chiral-even operators in the matrix elements, respectively. Meanwhile, it inherently possesses two polarization states. We denote the longitudinal part by the symbol ‘
\|
’ and the transverse part by the symbol ‘
\perp
’ in this paper. Therefore, to effectively address such issues, the Ref.
Ball:1998sk
proposed a process-dependent kinematic parameter
\delta
to classify the relevance of different LCDAs’ contributions to TFFs
Ball:2004rg
;
Ball:1998sk
. For vector meson
\omega
,
\delta\simeq m_{\omega}/m_{b}\sim 16\%
. Up to the
\delta^{3}
-order, the
\omega
-meson has fifteen LCDAs. The current understanding of the behavior of high-twist LCDAs for
\omega
-meson is not yet well-defined. If all these LCDAs are taken into account within the TFFs, it undoubtedly increases the difficulty in calculating and discussing the contributions of the LCDAs. Despite the fact that they may suffer from
\delta^{1}
-order or higher suppression, in order to better avoid situations where higher twists might contribute non-negligibly, we will adopt an improved LCSR method. This method involves selecting chiral currents to replace traditional currents when constructing the correlation function
Fu:2014pba
;
Wang:2002aje
;
Huang:2001xb
;
Huang:1998gp
, which can achieve the effect of highlighting the contributions from LCDA that we primarily focus on. In this work, we will employ left-handed chiral current to directly or indirectly make twist-2 LCDA
\phi^{\|}_{2;\omega}(x,\mu)
contributions dominant.

Generally, the vector meson’s LCDA
\phi^{\|}_{2;\omega}(x,\mu)
can be expanded with a series of Gegenbauer coefficients, and it is common to adopt a truncated form retaining only the first few terms

;
LatticeParton:2022zqc

and

\phi^{\|}_{2;\omega}(x,\mu)

\omega

Zhong:2011rg
;
Wu:2007rt
;
Huang:2004su

\pi,K,\eta^{(\prime)}

Zhong:2022ecl
;
Zhong:2021epq
;
Hu:2023pdl
, scalar mesons
a_{0}(980),K_{0}^{*}(1430)

II
Theoretical framework

The effective hamiltonian for semileptonic
b\to u\ell^{+}\nu
from the four-Fermi interaction in SM can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\rm{SM}}_{\rm{eff}}=\frac{4G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{ub}(\bar{u}\gamma_{\mu}P_{L}b)(\bar{\nu}\gamma^{\mu}P_{L}\ell^{+}),

(4)

where the Fermi coupling constant
G_{F}=1.166\times 10^{-5}~{\rm GeV}^{-2}
and the chiral projects
P_{L}=(1-\gamma_{5})/2
. In order to obtain the free quark amplitude
\mathcal{M}(B^{+}\to\omega\ell^{+}\nu)
explicitly, we need to sandwich Eq. (
4
) between the initial and final meson states,
i.e.,

\langle\omega(p,\lambda)|\bar{u}\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})b|B^{+}(p+q)\rangle
. And this matrix element must be calculated between physical final hadronic states that contain nonperturbative strong interaction contributions. These difficult-to-calculate quantities can be parameterized in terms of the Lorentz-invariant TFFs as follows:

\displaystyle\langle\omega(p,\lambda)|\bar{u}\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})b|B^{+}(p+q)\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=-ie_{\mu}^{*(\lambda)}(m_{B^{+}}+m_{\omega})A_{1}(q^{2})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+i(e^{*(\lambda)}\cdot q)\frac{A_{2}(q^{2})(2p+q)_{\mu}}{m_{B^{+}}+m_{\omega}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+iq_{\mu}(e^{*(\lambda)}\cdot q)\frac{2m_{\omega}}{q^{2}}[A_{3}(q^{2})-A_{0}(q^{2})]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}e^{*(\lambda)\nu}q^{\alpha}p^{\beta}\frac{2V(q^{2})}{m_{B^{+}}+m_{\omega}},

(5)

where
m_{B^{+}}
,
m_{\omega}
,
p
and
q=(p_{B^{+}}-p_{\omega})
are the masses of the
B^{+}
-meson and
\omega
-meson,
\omega
-meson momentum and the 4-momentum transfer between those two mesons, respectively. The
e^{*(\lambda)}
stands for the
\omega
-meson polarization vector with
\lambda
being its transverse
(\perp)
or longitudinal
(\|)
polarization. In addition, the
A_{0,1,2}(q^{2})
and
V(q^{2})
are four semileptonic TFFs, and
A_{3}(q^{2})
is not independent which satisfies the following relationship:

\displaystyle A_{3}(q^{2})=\frac{m_{B^{+}}+m_{\omega}}{2m_{\omega}}A_{1}(q^{2})-\frac{m_{B^{+}}-m_{\omega}}{2m_{\omega}}A_{2}(q^{2}).

(6)

In the SM, differential decay rate after neglecting the masses of charged lepton can be written as
Gao:2019lta
;
Gilani:2003hf

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma(B^{+}\to\omega\ell^{+}\nu)}{dq^{2}d\cos\theta_{W\ell}}

\displaystyle=|V_{ub}|^{2}\frac{G_{F}^{2}|\boldsymbol{p}_{\omega}|}{128\pi^{3}m_{B^{+}}^{2}}\frac{q^{2}}{c_{\omega}^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\bigg[(1-\cos\theta_{W\ell})^{2}\frac{|H_{+}(q^{2})|^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle+(1+\cos\theta_{W\ell})^{2}\frac{|H_{-}(q^{2})|^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle+\sin^{2}\theta_{W\ell}|H_{0}(q^{2})|^{2}\bigg].

(7)

In which the
\theta_{W\ell}
is the angle between the direction of the charged lepton in the virtual
W
-gauge boson rest frame and the direction of the virtual
W
in the
B^{+}
-meson rest frame. The isospin factor

is equal to
\sqrt{2}
for
B^{+}\to\omega\ell^{+}\nu

Ball:2004rg
. And the 3-momentum
|\boldsymbol{p}_{\omega}|
of
\omega
-meson has the following form:

\displaystyle|\boldsymbol{p}_{\omega}|=\frac{1}{2m_{B^{+}}}\lambda^{1/2}(m_{B^{+}}^{2},m_{\omega}^{2},q^{2}),

\displaystyle\lambda(a,b,c)=a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}-2ab-2ac-2bc.

(8)

Moreover, in the standard helicity basis, the three helicity amplitudes
H_{i}(q^{2})
with

can in turn be related to the axial-vector TFFs
A_{1}(q^{2})
and
A_{2}(q^{2})

,

\displaystyle H_{\pm}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=(m_{B^{+}}+m_{\omega})\Big[A_{1}(q^{2})\mp\frac{2m_{B^{+}}|\boldsymbol{p}_{\omega}|}{(m_{B^{+}}+m_{V})^{2}}

\displaystyle\times V(q^{2})\Big],

\displaystyle H_{0}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{B^{+}}+m_{\omega}}{2m_{\omega}\sqrt{q^{2}}}\bigg[(m_{B^{+}}^{2}-m_{\omega}^{2}-q^{2})A_{1}(q^{2})

\displaystyle-\frac{4m_{B^{+}}^{2}|\boldsymbol{p}_{\omega}|^{2}}{(m_{B^{+}}+m_{\omega})^{2}}A_{2}(q^{2})\bigg].

(9)

From the equations above, we can see that
A_{2}(q^{2})
contributes only to
H_{0}(q^{2})
,
V(q^{2})
contributes only to
H_{\pm}(q^{2})
, while

contributes to all helicity amplitudes
H_{\pm,0}(q^{2})
. In the high

-region, the
A_{1}(q^{2})
will dominate the contributions. After integrating Eq. (
7

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma(B^{+}\rightarrow\omega\ell^{+}\nu)}{dq^{2}}=|V_{ub}|^{2}\frac{G_{F}^{2}q^{2}|\boldsymbol{p}_{\omega}|}{96\pi^{3}m_{B}^{2}c_{\omega}^{2}}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~\times\bigg[|H_{0}(q^{2})|^{2}+|H_{+}(q^{2})|^{2}+|H_{-}(q^{2})|^{2}\bigg].

(10)

According to polarization states for these helicity amplitudes, the differential decay width can be decomposed into longitudinal polarization state and transverse polarization state,
i.e.
,

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma_{\rm{L}}(B^{+}\rightarrow\omega\ell^{+}\nu)}{dq^{2}}

\displaystyle=|V_{ub}|^{2}\frac{G_{F}^{2}q^{2}|\boldsymbol{p}_{\omega}|}{96\pi^{3}m_{B}^{2}c_{\omega}^{2}}|H_{0}(q^{2})|^{2}.

(11)

and

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma_{\pm}(B^{+}\rightarrow\omega\ell^{+}\nu)}{dq^{2}}

In which the specific transverse polarization part is

For the next step, we can start from the vacuum to meson correlation function to derive the LCSR of the TFFs firstly. The detailed form can be written as:

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mu}(p,q)

\displaystyle=i\int d^{4}xe^{iq\cdot x}\langle\omega(p,\lambda)|\mathrm{T}\{\bar{q}_{1}(x)\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})b(x),

\displaystyle\times j_{B^{+}}^{\dagger}(0)\}|0\rangle.

For

13

-meson in timelike

-region, the Eq. (
13

x^{2}\rightsquigarrow 0
, which corresponds to
(p+q)^{2}-m_{b}^{2}\ll 0
with the momentum transfer
q^{2}\sim\mathcal{O}(1~{\rm GeV}^{2})\ll
m_{b}^{2}
and ensures the validity of OPE. Finally, by utilizing the Borel transform to suppress contributions from highly twists and continuum states, and employing quark-hadron duality to match the OPE result with hadronic representation, we can obtain the analytic expression for the TFFs within the framework of LCSR. The corresponding result is similar to our previous work on
B\to\rho

Fu:2014cna
, which has been verified through our recalculation. Then, under the LCSR method, the final analytic expression of TFFs can be written as follows:

\displaystyle A_{1}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{2m_{b}^{2}m_{\omega}f_{\omega}^{\|}e^{m_{B^{+}}^{2}/M^{2}}}{m_{B^{+}}^{2}(m_{B^{+}}+\!m_{\omega})f_{B^{+}}}\bigg\{\int_{u_{0}}^{1}\!due^{-s(u)/M^{2}}\!\left[\frac{1}{u}\phi_{3;\omega}^{\bot}(u)\!-\!\frac{m_{\omega}^{2}}{u^{2}M^{2}}C_{\omega}^{\|}(u)\right]\!-\!e^{-s_{0}/M^{2}}\!\frac{m_{\omega}^{2}}{m_{b}^{2}+\!u_{0}^{2}m_{\omega}^{2}-q^{2}}

\displaystyle\times C_{\omega}^{\|}(u_{0})-m_{\omega}^{2}\int{\mathcal{D}\underline{\alpha}\int{dve^{-s(X)/M^{2}}\frac{1}{X^{2}M^{2}}\Theta(c(X,s_{0}))[\Phi_{3;\omega}^{\|}(\underline{\alpha})}}+\widetilde{\Phi}_{3;\omega}^{\|}(\underline{\alpha})]\bigg\},

(14)

\displaystyle A_{2}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{b}^{2}\,m_{\omega}(m_{B^{+}}+m_{\omega})f_{\omega}^{\|}e^{m_{B^{+}}^{2}/M^{2}}}{m_{B^{+}}^{2}f_{B^{+}}}\,\biggl\{2\int_{u_{0}}^{1}du\,e^{-s(u)/M^{2}}\bigg[\frac{1}{u^{2}M^{2}}\,A_{\omega}^{\|}(u)+\frac{m_{\omega}^{2}}{u^{2}M^{4}}\,C_{\omega}^{\|}(u)+\frac{m_{b}^{2}m_{\omega}^{2}}{4u^{4}M^{6}}

\displaystyle\times\widetilde{\widetilde{\widetilde{\Theta}}}(c(u,s_{0}))B_{\omega}^{\|}(u)\bigg]+2e^{-s_{0}/M^{2}}\bigg[\frac{A_{\omega}^{\|}(u_{0})}{m_{b}^{2}+u_{0}^{2}m_{\omega}^{2}-q^{2}}+\frac{m_{\omega}^{2}}{M^{2}}\frac{C_{\omega}^{\|}(u_{0})}{m_{b}^{2}+u_{0}^{2}m_{\omega}^{2}-q^{2}}-\frac{m_{\omega}^{2}u_{0}^{3}}{m_{b}^{2}+u_{0}^{2}m_{\omega}^{2}-q^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\frac{d}{du}\,\bigg(\frac{C_{\omega}^{\|}(u)}{u(m_{b}^{2}\,+u^{2}m_{\omega}^{2}\,-q^{2})}\bigg)\bigg|_{u=u_{0}}\,\bigg]\,+\,m_{\omega}^{2}\int{\mathcal{D}\underline{\alpha}\,\int{dve^{-s(X)/M^{2}}}}\,\frac{1}{X^{3}M^{4}}\,\Theta(c(X,s_{0}))\,[\Phi_{3;\omega}^{\|}(\underline{\widetilde{\alpha}})

\displaystyle+\widetilde{\Phi}_{3;\omega}^{\|}(\underline{\widetilde{\alpha}})]\bigg\},

(15)

\displaystyle V(q^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{b}^{2}m_{\omega}(m_{B^{+}}+m_{\omega})f_{\omega}^{\|}e^{m_{B^{+}}^{2}/M^{2}}}{2m_{B^{+}}^{2}f_{B^{+}}}\bigg[\,\int_{u_{0}}^{1}due^{-s(u)/M^{2}}\frac{1}{u^{2}M^{2}}\psi_{3;\omega}^{\bot}(u)+e^{-s_{0}/M^{2}}\frac{\psi_{3;\omega}^{\bot}(u_{0})}{m_{b}^{2}+u_{0}^{2}m_{\omega}^{2}-q^{2}}\,\bigg],

(16)

with

\displaystyle u_{0}

\displaystyle=\bigg[\sqrt{(q^{2}-s_{0}+m_{\omega}^{2})^{2}+4m_{\omega}^{2}(m_{b}^{2}-q^{2})}

\displaystyle+q^{2}-s_{0}+m_{\omega}^{2}\bigg]/(2m_{\omega}^{2}).

(17)

Where
s(u)=[m_{b}^{2}-\bar{u}(q^{2}-um_{\omega}^{2})]/u
with
\bar{u}=1-u
,
X=a_{1}+va_{3}
, and
c\left(u,s_{0}\right)=us_{0}-m_{b}^{2}+\overline{u}q^{2}-u\overline{u}m_{\omega}^{2}
.
\Theta(c(X,s_{0}))
is the usual step function, and
\widetilde{\widetilde{\widetilde{\Theta}}}(c(u,s_{0}))
is surface terms of two particle twist-4 LCDA
\phi^{\|}_{4;\omega}(x,\mu)
, which contribute quite small and can be safely neglected. For two-particle twist-3 LCDAs
\psi^{\bot}_{3;\omega}(x,\mu)
and
\phi^{\bot}_{3;\omega}(x,\mu)
, due to the existence of polarization states of the vector mesons and the non-negligible mass of the meson, the expression is more complex compared to that of the pseudoscalar meson, making the treatment process more challenging. To address this, P. Ball and V. M. Braun presented a systematic study of twist-3 LCDA for vector in 1998
Ball:1998sk
. Based on conformal symmetry and QCD equations of motion, they demonstrated that the
\psi^{\bot}_{3;\omega}(x,\mu)
and
\phi^{\bot}_{3;\omega}(x,\mu)
can be eliminated in favour of independent dynamical degrees of freedom, transforming into the leading twist LCDA, three-particle LCDA, and quark mass correction terms.

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt\phi_{3;\omega}^{\bot}(x,\mu)=\phi_{3;\omega}^{\bot WW}(x,\mu)\!+\!\phi_{3;\omega}^{\bot g}(x,\mu)\!+\!\phi_{3;\omega}^{\bot m}(x,\mu),

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt\psi_{3;\omega}^{\bot}(x,\mu)=\psi_{3;\omega}^{\bot WW}(x,\mu)\!+\!\psi_{3;\omega}^{\bot g}(x,\mu)\!+\!\psi_{3;\omega}^{\bot m}(x,\mu),

(18)

where contribution from the three-particle LCDA
\phi_{3;\omega}^{\bot g}(x,\mu)
is particularly small and can reasonably be ignored. The quark mass correction terms
\phi_{3;\omega}^{\bot m}(x,\mu)
has a coefficient
\widetilde{\delta}_{\pm}
, which is proportional to the quark mass in the meson components system. For
\omega
-meson, this coefficient tends to zero
Ball:1998sk
. Then, by using the Wandzura-Wilczek (WW) approximation, the twist-3 LCDAs
\psi^{\bot}_{3;\omega}(x,\mu)
and
\phi^{\bot}_{3;\omega}(x,\mu)
can be defined as

\displaystyle\phi_{3;\omega}^{\bot}(x,\mu)\!=\!\frac{1}{2}\left[\int_{0}^{x}{dv\frac{\phi_{2;\omega}^{\|}(v,\mu)}{\bar{v}}}+\int_{x}^{1}{dv\frac{\phi_{2;\omega}^{\|}(v,\mu)}{v}}\right]\!,

\displaystyle\psi_{3;\omega}^{\bot}(x,\mu)\!=\!2\!\left[\!\bar{x}\!\int_{0}^{x}dv\frac{\phi_{2;\omega}^{\|}(v,\!\mu)}{\bar{v}}\!+\!x\int_{x}^{1}\!dv\frac{\phi_{2;\omega}^{\|}(v,\!\mu)}{v}\right]\!.

(19)

And the simplified function
A^{\|}_{\omega}(u)=\int_{0}^{u}dv[\phi_{2;\omega}^{\|}(v)-\phi^{\bot}_{3;\omega}(v)]
can be further written as

\displaystyle A_{\omega}^{\|}(x,\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\bar{x}\int_{0}^{x}{dv\frac{\phi_{2;\omega}^{\|}(v,\mu)}{\bar{v}}}+x\int_{x}^{1}{dv\frac{\phi_{2;\omega}^{\|}(v,\mu)}{v}}\right].

(20)

The remaining simplified LCDA is

\displaystyle C^{\|}_{\omega}(u)=\int^{u}_{0}dv\int^{v}_{0}dw[\psi^{\|}_{4;\omega}+\phi^{\|}_{2;\omega}-2\psi^{\bot}_{3;\omega}(w)],

(21)

which can be found in Ref.
Fu:2014cna
.

Moreover, we need to construct the twist-2 LCDA
\phi_{2;\omega}^{\|}(x,\mu)
by using LCHO model. The relationship between twist-2 LCDA of
\omega
-meson and WF is defined as

\displaystyle\phi_{2;\omega}^{\|}(x,\mu)=\frac{2\sqrt{3}}{\widetilde{f}_{\omega}^{\|}}\int_{|\mathbf{k}_{\bot}|^{2}\leq\mu_{0}^{2}}{\frac{d^{2}\mathbf{k}_{\bot}}{16\pi^{3}}\psi_{2;\omega}^{\|}(x,\mathbf{k}_{\bot})},

(22)

where
\widetilde{f}_{\omega}^{\|}=f_{\omega}^{\|}/\sqrt{5}
and
\mathbf{k}_{\bot}
is the
\omega
-meson transverse momentum. Based on the BHL description
Zhong:2011rg
;
Wu:2007rt
;
Huang:2004su
, the light meson WF
\psi_{2;\omega}^{\|}(x,\mathbf{k}_{\bot})
will include the spin WF
\chi_{2;\omega}(x,\mathbf{k}_{\bot})
and spatial WF
\Psi^{R}_{2;\omega}(x,\mathbf{k}_{\bot})
,
i.e.,

\displaystyle\psi_{2;\omega}^{\|}(x,\mathbf{k}_{\bot})=\chi_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}^{L}(x,\mathbf{k}_{\bot})\Psi^{R}_{2;\omega}(x,\mathbf{k}_{\bot}).

(23)

In which
L
indicates the longitudinal spin projection for
\omega
-meson.
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are the different helicities of quark and antiquark.
\chi_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}^{L}(x,\mathbf{k}_{\bot})
can be determined by the Wigner-Melosh rotation, which establishes a connection between the spin states transforming from the instant form to the light-front form to address the spin structure of the WF. Based on this, the longitudinal and transverse spin form of
\rho
-meson is derived by light-front holographic model
Kaur:2020emh
. Here, since the
\omega
-meson and the
\rho
-meson possess the same spin projection and this work focuses on the longitudinal twist-2 LCDA, using the same treatment, we can consider the following form of the spin WF:

\displaystyle\chi_{2;\omega}(x,\mathbf{k}_{\bot})=\frac{\hat{m}_{q}(\mathcal{M}+2\hat{m}_{q})+2\mathbf{k}_{\bot}^{2}}{(\mathcal{M}+2\hat{m}_{q})\sqrt{2(\mathbf{k}_{\bot}^{2}+\hat{m}_{q})}}.

(24)

with
\mathcal{M}=\sqrt{(\mathbf{k}_{\bot}^{2}+\hat{m}_{q}^{2})/(x\bar{x})}
. Additionally,
\Psi^{R}_{2;\omega}(x,\mathbf{k}_{\bot})
can be separated into two components: the
x
-dependent part
\varphi(x)
and the
\mathbf{k}_{\bot}
-dependent part, the latter arising from the harmonic oscillator solution for the meson in its rest frame,

\displaystyle\Psi^{R}_{2;\omega}(x,\mathbf{k}_{\bot})

\displaystyle=A_{2;\omega}^{\|}\varphi(x)\exp\left(-b_{2;\omega}^{\|2}\frac{\mathbf{k}_{\bot}^{2}+\hat{m}_{q}^{2}}{x\bar{x}}\right).

(25)

Where
A^{\|}_{2;\omega}
is the normalization constant,
b^{\|}_{2;\omega}
is the harmonic parameter, and
\hat{m}_{q}\simeq 300~\rm{MeV}
is constituent quark mass. The function
\varphi(x)=1+B^{\|}_{2;\omega}C^{3/2}_{2}(2x-1)
with Gegenbauer polynomials
C^{3/2}_{2}(2x-1)
. Among them,
B^{\|}_{2;\omega}
dominates the longitudinal distribution. Then, the twist-2 LCDA
\phi_{2;\omega}^{\|}(x,\mu)
can be written as

\displaystyle\phi_{2;\omega}^{\|}(x,\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\sqrt{3}}{\widetilde{f}_{\omega}^{\|}}\int_{|\mathbf{k}_{\bot}|^{2}\leq\mu_{0}^{2}}{\frac{d^{2}\mathbf{k}_{\bot}}{16\pi^{3}}}A^{\|}_{2;\omega}\varphi(x)

\displaystyle\times\frac{\hat{m}_{q}(\mathcal{M}+2\hat{m}_{q})+2\mathbf{k}_{\bot}^{2}}{(\mathcal{M}+2\hat{m}_{q})\sqrt{2(\mathbf{k}_{\bot}^{2}+\hat{m}_{q})}}

\displaystyle\times\exp\left[-b_{2;\omega}^{\|2}\frac{\mathbf{k}_{\bot}^{2}+\hat{m}_{q}^{2}}{x\bar{x}}\right].

(26)

The remaining three unknown parameters,
A^{\|}_{2;\omega}
,
b_{2;\omega}^{\|}
and
B^{\|}_{2;\omega}
need to be determined using additional conditions:

•

The normalization condition of
\omega
-meson twist-2 LCDA

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}dx\phi_{2;\omega}^{\|}(x,\mu)=1.

(27)

•

The average value of the squared transverse momentum
\langle\mathbf{k}_{\bot}^{2}\rangle_{2;\omega}

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{k}_{\bot}^{2}\rangle_{2;\omega}=\frac{\int{dxd^{2}\mathbf{k}_{\bot}|\mathbf{k}_{\bot}|^{2}|\psi_{2;\omega}^{\|}(x,\mathbf{k}_{\bot})|^{2}}}{\int{dxd^{2}\mathbf{k}_{\bot}|\psi_{2;\omega}^{\|}(x,\mathbf{k}_{\bot})|^{2}}}.

(28)

Here we use
\langle\mathbf{k}_{\bot}^{2}\rangle_{2;\omega}^{1/2}=0.37~{\rm GeV}

Guo:1991eb
to do numerical calculation.

•

The Gegenbauer moments
a_{n;\omega}^{\|}(\mu)
can be derived by the following way

\displaystyle a_{n;\omega}^{\|}(\mu)=\frac{\int_{0}^{1}{dx\phi_{2;\omega}^{\|}(x,\mu)C_{n}^{3/2}(\xi)}}{\int_{0}^{1}{6x\bar{x}[C_{n}^{3/2}(\xi)]^{2}}},

(29)

with
\xi=(2x-1)
. Here, we adopt
a_{2;\omega}^{\|}(\mu_{0})=0.15\pm 0.12
as provided in Ref.
Dimou:2012un
mentioned in the previous section, to fix these unknown parameters.

IV
Summary

In the framework of the LCSR method, we have calculated the semileptonic decay
B^{+}\to\omega\ell^{+}\nu
. Firstly, for twist-2 LCDA
\phi_{2;\omega}^{\|}(x,\mu)
, we construct the LCHO model to describe its behavior, whose model parameters are determined by additional three conditions. The Fig.
1
shows that LCHO model of
\phi_{2;\omega}^{\|}(x,\mu_{0})
has two peaks. And the Fig.
2
also shows the twist-3 LCDAs
\phi_{3;\omega}^{\bot}(x,\mu_{0})
and
\psi_{3;\omega}^{\bot}(x,\mu_{0})
under the WW approximation. Our predictions are very close to QCDSR(WW)
Ball:1998sk
. The results of
A_{1}(0)
,
A_{2}(0)
, and
V(0)
at large recoil point are listed in Table
2
. Meanwhile, for the convenience of the experimental group, two important ratios
r_{V}
and
r_{2}
are also presented in Table
3
. Due to the different methods and the lack of experimental evidence, the predictions of different theoretical groups for these two ratios cannot reach a high degree of consistency.

After extrapolating the TFFs to whole physical
q^{2}
-region, the complete behavior of TFFs are shown in Fig.
3
. Our predictions for the overall trends of
A_{1}(q^{2})
,
A_{2}(q^{2})
, and
V(q^{2})
each show good consistency with those of other theoretical groups, particularly for
A_{1}(q^{2})
. Immediately following that, some interesting physical observables of
B^{+}\to\omega\ell^{+}\nu
can be determined. Firstly, the differential decay width as a function of
q^{2}
has been shown in Fig.
4

Overall, our calculated results show good consistency with others. Currently, the experimental data for

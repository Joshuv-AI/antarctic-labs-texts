King’s Conjecture and the Cox category
Matthew R. Ballard, Christine Berkesch, Michael K. Brown, Lauren Cranton Heller, Daniel Erman, David Favero, Sheel Ganatra, Andrew Hanlon, Jesse Huang

Abstract
We state and prove a realization of King’s Conjecture for a category glued from the derived categories of all of the toric varieties arising from a given Cox ring. Our perspective extends ideas of Beilinson and Bondal to all semiprojective toric varieties.

These properties of the Cox category offer a novel and concrete instantiation of the philosophy connecting derived categories and birational geometry.

Symplectic motivation

While our results, methods, and applications are algebraic,
some of the motivation for our definition of
D_{\operatorname{Cox}}
comes from the symplectic side of mirror symmetry, as we now explain.
There are at least two approaches to homological mirror symmetry for toric varieties in the literature: one is to pass through a microlocal sheaf category initiated in
[
39
,
40
,
41
]
and completed in
[
68
]
, and the other is to work directly with Floer theory and Fukaya categories as in
[
1
,
2
,
55
]
.
These are tied together by the main result of
[
48
]
, which implies that both categories on the symplectic side can be modeled with a partially wrapped Fukaya category.
Namely, there is an (appropriately derived) equivalence of categories between each
D({\mathcal{X}}_{i})
and a partially wrapped Fukaya category
\mathcal{W}(T^{*}T^{\dim{\mathcal{X}}_{i}},\mathfrak{f}_{i})
, where
\mathfrak{f}_{i}
is a closed subset of the boundary at infinity introduced in
[
39
]
.
Objects in this Fukaya category are supported on exact cylindrical at infinity Lagrangian submanifolds that do not intersect the stop
\mathfrak{f}_{i}
at infinity, and morphisms involve Lagrangian Floer cohomology of certain large perturbations of these submanifolds that never cross
\mathfrak{f}_{i}
.
4
4

4

While Fukaya categories are
A_{\infty}
-categories, this is not relevant for this motivational discussion.

Algebraic constructions are often more rigid than analogous constructions in topology and geometry, and this is the case here; there is flexibility on the symplectic side that is not obviously present on the algebraic side.
For example, one can define a wrapped Fukaya category by simply taking the union of all of the stop data:
\mathcal{W}(T^{*}T^{\dim{\mathcal{X}}_{i}},\bigcup_{i}\mathfrak{f}_{i})
, see Figure
1.2
.
Working out examples suggested that this category had nice properties, and it led us to ask: is there a natural algebraic analogue of this “union of stop data” category?

Thus, our definition of

A parallel motivation came from the commutative algebra

Outline of the proof of
Theorem

A

After we establish basic facts about
D_{\operatorname{Cox}}
,
the proof of our main result amounts to two major steps.
The first is to study how the Bondal–Thomsen collection behaves with respect to the Fourier–Mukai transforms
\Phi_{ij}\colon D({\mathcal{X}}_{i})\to D({\mathcal{X}}_{j})
, as introduced shortly after Definition
1.1
.

Lemma 1.6

(
\Theta
-Transform Lemma)
.

Let
-d\in\Theta_{X}
be an element whose image in
\Sigma_{GKZ}
lies in the chamber corresponding to
{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
. For any
j
,
\Phi_{ij}({\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}}(-d))={\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{j}}(-d)
.

This is a subtle property, which fails if one alters the hypotheses even
slightly.
The challenge has nothing to do with stackiness, as the statement seems equally subtle when both
{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
and
{\mathcal{X}}_{j}
are smooth toric varieties.
It does not appear to easily follow from known vanishing results from toric or birational geometry.

Lemma

1.6
captures a unique feature of the Bondal–Thomsen collection and its behavior under birational Fourier–Mukai transforms and so provides the foundation for our understanding of
D_{\operatorname{Cox}}
.

The
\Theta
-Transform Lemma almost immediately implies that the objects
{\mathcal{O}}_{\operatorname{Cox}}(-d)
form a strong exceptional collection when
X
is projective, and that they sum to a tilting bundle more generally.
Namely, we must compute
\operatorname{Hom}({\mathcal{O}}_{\operatorname{Cox}}(-d),{\mathcal{O}}_{\operatorname{Cox}}(-d^{\prime}))
for two elements
-d,-d^{\prime}\in\Theta
.
By adjunction and the
\Theta
-Transform Lemma, this reduces to computing

\operatorname{Hom}({\mathcal{O}}_{\operatorname{Cox}}(-d),{\mathcal{O}}_{\operatorname{Cox}}(-d^{\prime}))\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}}({\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}}(-d),{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}}(-d^{\prime}))\cong H^{*}({\mathcal{X}}_{i},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}}(d-d^{\prime})).

The element
d
lies in the nef cone of
{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
and since
-d^{\prime}
is an element of
\Theta
, it can be written as a toric
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor with all coefficients in
(-1,0]
. The vanishing of the higher cohomology groups then follows from a stacky version of Demazure Vanishing for
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors (
Theorem

2.11
).

Fullness of the Bondal–Thomsen collection for each
{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
was proven in
[
54
]
*Corollary D and
[
44
]
; the second step is to extend those results to show that
\Theta
generates
D_{\operatorname{Cox}}
. For this, we combine the fact that
\Theta_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}}
generates
D(\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}})
with
Lemma

1.6
and explicit computations regarding the behavior of Bondal–Thomsen bundles with respect to pushforward.

Related results

We briefly discuss how our construction and resxults relate to some previous constructions and results in the literature.
As noted in
Section

The construction in

There is a natural link between the elements of

of

-graded

is equivalent to the

Previous literature on King’s conjecture includes the cases where

,

]
, where

There are also many more papers inspired by King’s Conjecture as mentioned in the first paragraph of the introduction.

The idea of gluing together all the toric varieties in the secondary fan has appeared previously in the context of hypertoric varieties and (topological) cohomology rings
[
78
]
. Although these ideas align philosophically with our definition of the Cox category, we do not currently know how to make a precise mathematical connection.

In a broader context, our results fit into distinct streams of
efforts to better understand derived categories of toric varieties—including
the special role of
\Theta
—in algebraic geometry, commutative algebra, and
homological mirror symmetry. In algebraic geometry, a number of works
investigated the Rouquier dimension of derived categories of toric varieties and
whether the Bondal–Thomsen collection generates
D(X)
. Within commutative
algebra, our results fit into the recent burst of activity on extending
homological results from a standard graded polynomial ring to a multigraded
setting. Homological mirror symmetry for toric varieties is particularly
well-studied due to the central role of toric varieties in the subject. For
instance, large complex structure limit degenerations of Calabi–Yau varieties
can be glued from toric varieties along toric strata. Our work builds on and
extends concrete descriptions of derived categories of toric varieties arising
from work on homological mirror symmetry.

Preliminaries

This section serves two purposes. First, we establish our notation. Second, we collect facts about toric varieties and stacks that are needed to establish our results. In many cases, we need a version of a statement that is not clearly stated elsewhere in the literature. For instance, standard references like
[
34
]
do not cover toric stacks, and so we provide independent proofs of the necessary cohomological vanishing theorems. The subsections of this section are largely modular and can be read independently on an as-needed basis.

[

]
*Chapters 14–15. In

Section

2.3
defines the Bondal–Thomsen collection and collects some of its basic properties.
In

Throughout, we work over an arbitrary algebraically closed field

As in Theorem

The birational geometry of toric varieties

The birational geometry of a semiprojective toric variety
X
is described by its secondary fan
\Sigma_{GKZ}(X)
and by an associated GIT problem.
This is covered in detail in
[
34
]
*Chapters 14–15.
This subsection will establish our notation regarding the secondary fan and provide a terse summary of the main ideas and results that we will need.

The Cox ring of
X
is
S\coloneqq\bigoplus_{d\in\operatorname{Cl}(X)}H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(d))
, and this is a polynomial ring
S=\Bbbk[x_{\rho}\mid\rho\in\Sigma_{X}(1)]
.
The ring
S
comes with a natural grading by the class group,
\operatorname{Cl}(X)
.
The
\operatorname{Cl}(X)
-grading induces an action of a group
G
on the affine space
\operatorname{Spec}(S)

[
34
]
*Section 5.1, and this action leads to a GIT framework for describing the birational geometry of
X
as detailed in
[
34
]
*Chapter 14.
Here, we focus on a more combinatorial description.

The secondary fan
\Sigma_{GKZ}=\Sigma_{GKZ}(X)
is a fan that is supported on the effective cone of
X
. For each cone
\Gamma\in\Sigma_{GKZ}
, there is an associated toric variety, and it is convenient to describe these varieties in terms of
generalized fans
, meaning a set of cones satisfying all properties of a fan except that the cones are not required to be strongly convex
[
34
]
*Section 6.2.

Every cone
\Gamma\in\Sigma_{GKZ}
is the image in
\operatorname{Cl}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
of a subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{\Sigma(1)}
of the form

\left\{a\in\mathbb{R}^{\Sigma(1)}\ \bigg|\ \begin{array}[]{l}\text{there is a concave}\text{ support function }F\text{ on }\overline{\Sigma}_{\Gamma}\text{ such that}\\
F(u_{\rho})=-a_{\rho}\text{ for all }\rho\not\in I_{\Gamma}\text{ and }F(u_{\rho})\geqslant-a_{\rho}\text{ for all }\rho\in\Sigma(1)\end{array}\right\}

for some
I_{\Gamma}\subset\Sigma(1)

and generalized fan
\overline{\Sigma}_{\Gamma}
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
.
6
6

6

We have changed the notation from
[
34
]
from
I_{\emptyset}
to
I_{\Gamma}
so that this set keeps track of the cone
\Gamma
.

The associated
X_{\Gamma}
is the toric variety of the generalized fan
\overline{\Sigma}_{\Gamma}
; see
[
34
]
*Section 6.2.
In particular, if
L_{\Gamma}
is the lineality space
7
7

7

The lineality space of a generalized fan is the minimal cone in the generalized fan, which must be a subspace and a face of all other cones. It is denoted by
\sigma_{0}
in
[
34
]
.
of
\overline{\Sigma}_{\Gamma}
, the fan of
X_{\Gamma}
is

\Sigma_{\Gamma}=\{\sigma/L_{\Gamma}\mid\sigma\in\overline{\Sigma}_{\Gamma}\}
,
which is a fan on
(N_{\Gamma})_{\mathbb{R}}
, where
N_{\Gamma}\coloneqq N/N\cap L_{\Gamma}
.

The toric varieties
X_{\Gamma}
are always
semiprojective
.
By
[
34
]
*Proposition 7.2.9, one of several equivalent conditions for a toric variety to be semiprojective is that it is the toric variety associated to a full-dimensional lattice polyhedron.
In terms of the fan, this characterization means that the support
|\Sigma_{\Gamma}|
is full-dimensional and convex and that there exists a strictly convex piecewise linear function on
\Sigma_{\Gamma}
.

We enumerate the maximal cones of
\Sigma_{GKZ}
as
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{r}
with some arbitrary ordering. By
[
34
]
*Proposition 14.4.9, the corresponding
\Sigma_{1},\ldots,\Sigma_{r}
on

are honest (not generalized) fans that are simplicial, allowing for the following definition.

Definition 2.2

(Cox construction)
.

For each maximal chamber
\Gamma_{i}
of
\Sigma_{GKZ}
, the associated fan
\Sigma_{i}
determines an irrelevant ideal
B_{i}\subseteq S
(see
[
34
]
*Chapter 14.5). We
define
{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
as the smooth toric Deligne–Mumford stack
{\mathcal{X}}_{i}\coloneqq[\operatorname{Spec}(S)-V(B_{i})/G]
. Equivalently,
{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
can be defined in terms of a stacky fan (see
Section

2.2
for a review), given by the fan
\Sigma_{i}
and the homomorphism
\beta\colon\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma_{i}(1)}\to N
satisfying
\beta(e_{\rho})=u_{\rho}
for all
\rho\in\Sigma_{i}(1)
.

Example 2.3
.

If
\Sigma
is the fan for
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, then Definition
2.2
yields that
{\mathcal{X}}=[\mathbb{A}^{2}-\{0\}/\mathbb{G}_{m}]
, which is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
because
\mathbb{P}^{1}=X_{\Sigma}
is smooth.
If
X_{\Sigma}
is simplicial but not smooth, then this toric stack from the Cox construction will be a smooth Deligne–Mumford stack
[
20
]
.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

As is common in the investigation of derived categories of toric varieties (for example,
[
7
,
12
,
21
,
26
]
), we focus on the stack
{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
, instead of on the variety
X_{i}
, because the derived category of the stack is better behaved. For instance, the stack is smooth while the variety may not be.
As a consequence, when
X_{i}
is projective, (an enhancement of)
D({\mathcal{X}}_{i})
is a smooth, proper dg-category, whereas
D(X_{i})
fails to be proper.

In more concrete terms, each fan
\Sigma_{i}
determines a corresponding irrelevant ideal
B_{i}\subseteq S
(see
[
34
]
*Chapter 14.5), and
D({\mathcal{X}}_{i})
has a simple algebraic description.
Since
{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
equals the quotient stack
[\operatorname{Spec}(S)-V(B_{i})/G]
, the derived category
D({\mathcal{X}}_{i})
equals the derived category of finitely generated,
\operatorname{Cl}(X)
-graded
S
-modules modulo the subcategory generated by
B_{i}
-torsion modules.
In the smooth case,
{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
and
X_{i}
are equal, and so the derived categories are equal as well. In the simplicial case, the derived categories remain closely related. While one needs to distinguish between Cartier and non-Cartier elements of
\operatorname{Cl}(X_{i})
, one can still use
{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
to effectively study most homological questions about
X_{i}
.
∎

The secondary fan
\Sigma_{GKZ}
also parametrizes toric morphisms among the corresponding varieties.
Specifically, if
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma^{\prime}
are cones in
\Sigma_{GKZ}
, then there is an induced toric morphism
\pi\colon X_{\Gamma^{\prime}}\to X_{\Gamma}
coming from the fact that the generalized fan
\overline{\Sigma}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}
is a refinement of
\overline{\Sigma}_{\Gamma}
.

Remark 2.5
.

It is natural to ask why we don’t make
Definition

2.2
for arbitrary cones of
\Sigma_{GKZ}
. The short answer is that the lower dimensional cones of
\Sigma_{GKZ}
can correspond to non-simplicial toric varieties, and the related stacks are more subtle in those cases.
∎

Cohomology on toric Deligne–Mumford stacks

The goal of this section is to take a number of standard results (e.g., Demazure Vanishing) and techniques (e.g., the use of support functions) about sheaf cohomology of line bundles, and to extend them from toric varieties to toric Deligne–Mumford stacks, as will be required for our main results.
Instead of providing the most general version of each statement, we focus on versions that we will use.
We first briefly recall notation regarding such toric stacks.

Definition 2.6
.

In
\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma(1)}

e_{\rho}
.
Given a homomorphism
\beta\colon\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma(1)}\to N

\beta(e_{\rho})=b_{\rho}u_{\rho}

*Section 2 (see also

By
[

*Proposition 3.2,

is a smooth Deligne–Mumford stack when

is simplicial.

Now fix a simplicial fan
\Sigma
on
N_{\mathbb{R}}
and

for some

Let

As in the previous equation, we will often conflate elements

with torus invariant divisors
\sum a_{\rho}D_{\rho}
.
Going further, there is a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma(1)}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma(1)}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta^{*}}

where

is the toric variety of the fan

For any

\displaystyle H^{p}({\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}}(\widehat{D}))

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{m\in M}\widetilde{H}^{p-1}(V_{\widehat{D},m}^{{\mathcal{X}}}),

The Bondal–Thomsen collection

We now define
\Theta_{{\mathcal{X}}}

\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{X}})

{\mathcal{X}}={\mathcal{X}}_{\Sigma,\beta}

Definition 2.12
.

The

is the set
\Theta_{{\mathcal{X}}}

\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\lfloor\langle-\theta,\beta(e_{\rho})\rangle\rfloor D_{\rho}.

corresponding to

The element

depends only on the image of
\theta
in the torus
M_{\mathbb{R}}/M

There is also a Bondal–Thomsen collection
\Theta_{X}\subset\operatorname{Cl}(X)
for the toric variety

Namely,
\Theta_{X}
is the set of all classes described by (

\beta(e_{\rho})
replaced by
u_{\rho}
.

Example 2.14

The set

Definition 2.19
.

We will call a divisor
A=\sum a_{\rho}D_{\rho}
on
{\mathcal{X}}

numerically effective (nef)
if there is a concave piecewise linear function
F
on
\Sigma
such that
F(\beta(e_{\rho}))=-a_{\rho}
for all
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
.

Note that in
Definition

2.19

F
may not take integral values on the primitive generators of
\Sigma
.
However, any nef divisor
D
on
X_{\Sigma}
pulls back to a nef divisor on
{\mathcal{X}}_{\Sigma,\beta}
.
We will often use the following fact about vanishing of higher cohomology for Bondal–Thomsen line bundles. The corresponding statement for toric varieties is a corollary of Demazure Vanishing for
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors (see, for example,
[
34
]
*Theorem 9.3.5).

Lemma 2.20
.

Suppose that
|\Sigma|
is convex,
8
8

8

We put the convexity assumption here for clarity as it is used in the proof. As all toric varieties in this paper are assumed to be semiprojective, this assumption is always satisfied for us.

\Sigma
is simplicial, and
A=\sum a_{\rho}D_{\rho}
is a nef divisor on
{\mathcal{X}}={\mathcal{X}}_{\Sigma,\beta}
. Then for any
-d\in\Theta_{{\mathcal{X}}}
and any

,
H^{p}({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}}(A-d))=0
.

Proof.

Choose
\theta\in M_{\mathbb{R}}
so that
-d=-d(\theta)
.
Applying
[
34
]
*Theorem 9.1.3(a) as in
Section

2.2
,

H^{p}({\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}}(A-d))=\bigoplus_{m\in M}\widetilde{H}^{p-1}(V_{A-\theta,m}),

\displaystyle\text{where}\quad V_{A-\theta,M}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\mathrm{conv}\{e_{\rho}\mid\rho\in\sigma(1)\text{ and }\langle m,\beta(e_{\rho})\rangle<\lceil\langle\theta,\beta(e_{\rho})\rangle\rceil-a_{\rho}\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\mathrm{conv}\{e_{\rho}\mid\rho\in\sigma(1)\text{ and }\langle m,\beta(e_{\rho})\rangle<\langle\theta,\beta(e_{\rho})\rangle+F(\beta(e_{\rho}))\},

using that
\langle m,\beta(e_{\rho})\rangle,a_{\rho}
are in
\mathbb{Z}
and letting
F
be a concave support function for
A
. Then,
\beta_{\mathbb{R}}
maps
V_{A-\theta,M}
homeomorphically (see
Section

2.2
) onto the set

\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\mathrm{conv}\{\beta(e_{\rho})\mid\rho\in\sigma(1)\text{ and }\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle<\langle\theta,u_{\rho}\rangle+F(u_{\rho}))\},

which, since the inequality
\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle<\langle\theta,u_{\rho}\rangle+F(u_{\rho})
is invariant under positive scaling, is homotopic to

\left(\bigcup\{\sigma\in\Sigma\mid\langle m-\theta,u\rangle<F(u)\text{ for all nonzero }u\in\sigma\right)\setminus\{0\}.

(2.21)

Now, we observe that (
2.21
) is a deformation retract of

V_{F-\theta,m}=\{u\in|\Sigma|\mid\langle m-\theta,u\rangle<F(u)\},

which is a convex set since
F
is concave.
Namely, for any
u\in|\Sigma|
, there is a minimal cone in
\Sigma
containing
u
which has a maximal face
\sigma_{u}
such that
\sigma_{u}\setminus\{0\}\in V_{F-\theta,m}
.
Moreover,
\sigma_{u}
must be nonempty if
u\in V_{F-\theta,m}
.
Let
P_{\sigma_{u}}
be the orthogonal projection onto
\sigma_{u}
for some chosen inner product on
N_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then, a deformation retraction

is given by

H(u,t)=P_{\sigma_{u}}(u)+(1-t)\left(u-P_{\sigma_{u}}(u)\right)
.
Putting this all together,
V_{A-\theta,m}
is contractible when it is nonempty, yielding the desired result.
∎

Remark 2.22
.

On a toric stack in any characteristic, there is a toric Frobenius endomorphism

induced by the linear map of fans given by multiplication by
\ell
.
In
[

is the set of all possible summands of

for a smooth toric variety

(see also
[

,
3
]
).
Thomsen’s result was extended to toric Deligne–Mumford stacks in

.

for varieties uses that

includes
into

We will need several results on how elements of
\Theta
push forward.
These results are closely related, and they all follow from a more general statement about pushforwards under appropriately defined birational toric morphisms of toric stacks, but we have chosen to isolate the instances we use for clarity.
First,
\Theta
pushes forward in the naive way from
{\mathcal{X}}
to
X
.

Proposition 2.23

(Coarse moduli pushforwards of
\Theta
)
.

If
\Sigma
is simplicial and
\pi\colon{\mathcal{X}}_{\Sigma,\beta}\to X_{\Sigma}
is the natural coarse moduli space map, then for all
\theta\in M_{\mathbb{R}}
,

\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{\Sigma,\beta}}(-d(\theta))={\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\Sigma}}(-d(\theta)).

Proof.

The higher direct images all vanish as a consequence of
Lemma

2.20
with
A=0
.
Thus we must compute only
R^{0}\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}}(-d(\theta))
.
For any
\sigma\in\Sigma
,
\pi^{-1}(U_{\sigma})={\mathcal{X}}_{\sigma,\beta|_{\mathbb{Z}^{\sigma(1)}}}
.
Therefore

\displaystyle H^{0}(U_{\sigma},R^{0}\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{\sigma,\beta}}(-d(\theta)))

\displaystyle=H^{0}\left({\mathcal{X}}_{\sigma,\beta|_{\mathbb{Z}^{\sigma(1)}}},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}}(-d(\theta))\right)

\displaystyle=\Bbbk\left\langle m\in M\mid\langle m,\beta(e_{\rho})\rangle\geqslant\lceil\langle\theta,\beta(e_{\rho})\rangle\rceil\text{ for all }\rho\in\sigma(1)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\Bbbk\left\langle m\in M\mid\langle m,\beta(e_{\rho})\rangle\geqslant\langle\theta,\beta(e_{\rho})\rangle\text{ for all }\rho\in\sigma(1)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\Bbbk\left\langle m\in M\mid\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle\geqslant\langle\theta,u_{\rho}\rangle\text{ for all }\rho\in\sigma(1)\right\rangle,

using that
\langle m,\beta(e_{\rho})\rangle
is always an integer and that
\beta(e_{\rho})
is a positive multiple of
u_{\rho}
.
Thus this calculation shows that for all
\sigma\in\Sigma
,

H^{0}(U_{\sigma},R^{0}\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{\sigma,\beta}}(-d(\theta)))=H^{0}(U_{\sigma},{\mathcal{O}}_{U_{\sigma}}(-d(\theta)))

in a fashion that respects restriction, which is given by inclusion of polytopes.
∎

We also need to understand the pushforwards of Bondal–Thomsen elements under toric morphisms induced by refinements of fans.
We start with toric varieties, but due to their natural appearance in
Section

2.1
, we do so in the context of generalized fans.
Note that if
\overline{\Sigma}
is a generalized fan with lineality space
L
, then the character lattice of
X_{\overline{\Sigma}}
is

L^{\perp}\cap M=\{m\in M\mid\langle m,u\rangle=0\text{ for all }u\in L\},

and each
d\in-\Theta_{X_{\overline{\Sigma}}}
can be represented via some
\theta\in L^{\perp}
as

-d(\theta):=\sum_{\rho\in\overline{\Sigma}(1)}\lfloor\langle-\theta,u_{\rho}\rangle\rfloor D_{\rho}.

(2.24)

Let
\overline{\Sigma}(1)
denote the set of cones that correspond to rays in the quotient fan, and for each
\rho\in\overline{\Sigma}(1)
, let
u_{\rho}
denote the primitive generator of the quotient ray.

Proposition 2.25

(Pushforwards of
\Theta
along refinements)
.

Suppose that
\Sigma^{\prime}
is a fan on
N_{\mathbb{R}}
refining a generalized fan
\overline{\Sigma}
on
N_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Let
X^{\prime}=X_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
,
X=X_{\overline{\Sigma}}
, and let
\pi\colon X^{\prime}\to X
be the induced toric morphism.
If
L
is the lineality space of
\overline{\Sigma}
and
\theta\in L^{\perp}+M
, then

\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{\prime}}(-d(\theta))={\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-d(\theta)),

and the derived pushforward of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{\prime}}(-d(\theta))
is zero for all
\theta\not\in L^{\perp}+M
.

Proof.

For any
\sigma\in\overline{\Sigma}
, there is a fan
\sigma^{\prime}\subset\Sigma^{\prime}
refining
\sigma
.
The higher direct images vanish as a consequence of the analogue of
Lemma

2.20
with
A=0
for toric varieties applied to the
X_{\sigma^{\prime}}
.
To compute
R^{0}\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{\prime}}(-d(\theta))
,

\displaystyle H^{0}(U_{\sigma},R^{0}\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{\prime}}(-d(\theta)))

\displaystyle=H^{0}(X_{\sigma^{\prime}},{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{\prime}}(-d(\theta)))

\displaystyle=\Bbbk\left\langle m\in M\mid\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle\geqslant\lceil\langle\theta,u_{\rho}\rangle\rceil\text{ for all }\rho\in\sigma^{\prime}(1)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\Bbbk\left\langle m\in M\mid\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle\geqslant\langle\theta,u_{\rho}\rangle\text{ for all }\rho\in\sigma^{\prime}(1)\right\rangle

(2.26)

using that
\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle
is always an integer.

First, suppose that
\theta\not\in L^{\perp}+M
.
That is, for any
m
, there exists
u\in L
such that

\langle\theta-m,u\rangle>0
.
In addition,
\theta\not\in L^{\perp}+M
implies that
L\neq\{0\}
; moreover,
\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}\cdot\sigma^{\prime}(1)
contains
L
.
Thus,

can be written as a nonnegative combination of
u_{\rho}
with
\rho\in\sigma^{\prime}(1)
, and the fact that
\langle\theta-m,u\rangle>0
implies that it is not possible to have
\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle\geqslant\langle\theta,u_{\rho}\rangle
for all
\rho\in\sigma^{\prime}(1)
.
We conclude that
\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{\prime}}(-d(\theta))
has no local sections on an affine cover.

Now we turn to the case that
\theta\in L^{\perp}+M
.
Since adding an element of
M
does not affect the isomorphism class of the line bundle, assume that
\theta\in L^{\perp}
.
If
m\not\in L^{\perp}
, then there exists
u\in L
such that
\langle m,u\rangle<0
, and by the same argument as above, it is impossible to have
\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle\geqslant\langle\theta,u_{\rho}\rangle
for all
\rho\in\sigma^{\prime}(1)
.
Thus, the nonzero sections on

must correspond to

that lie in

. Continuing from (

),

\displaystyle H^{0}(U_{\sigma},R^{0}\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{\prime}}(-d(\theta)))

\displaystyle=\Bbbk\left\langle m\in L^{\perp}\cap M\mid\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle\geqslant\langle\theta,u_{\rho}\rangle\text{ for all }\rho\in\sigma^{\prime}(1)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\Bbbk\left\langle m\in L^{\perp}\cap M\mid\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle\geqslant\langle\theta,u_{\rho}\rangle\text{ for all }\rho\in\sigma(1)\right\rangle,

where the second equality follows from the fact that the image of every primitive generator of
\sigma^{\prime}(1)
in the quotient by

is a linear combination of primitive generators of
\sigma(1)
.
That is, our calculation shows that

H^{0}(U_{\sigma},R^{0}\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{\prime}}(-d(\theta)))=H^{0}(U_{\sigma},{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-d(\theta)))

for all
\sigma\in\Sigma
, and in a fashion that respects restriction, which is given by inclusion of polytopes.
∎

Lemma 2.29

If

Proof.

Fix the Laurent monomial

Then

\displaystyle H^{0}\left(U_{\sigma},S(-d(\theta))\right)=S(-d(\theta))[(x^{\overline{\sigma}^{\mathsf{c}}})^{-1}]_{0}\cong S[(x^{\overline{\sigma}^{\mathsf{c}}})^{-1}]_{-d(\theta)},

(2.30)

with monomial generators in bijection with the set of Laurent monomials

such that the exponent of

where the exponent of

for all

By the argument in the proof of

The Cox category

In this section, we define the Cox category and establish some of its core properties.

Construction of
\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}

In this subsection, we construct an explicit smooth toric DM stack
\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}
satisfying the necessary requirements of Definition
1.1
. The underlying coarse moduli space is simply the toric variety associated to a common simplicial refinement of the fans
\Sigma_{1},...,\Sigma_{r}

to lie over all the associated DM stacks, we need to “refine” the stacky structure as well.
Recall that
X_{i}
is the toric variety associated to
{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
with fan
\Sigma_{i}
.
Let
\Lambda
be a simplicial fan refining all of the

be the toric DM stack obtained from

via

It may not be the case that
{\mathcal{X}}_{\Lambda}
admits a morphism to each
{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
.
At the level of stacky fans, the obstacle is that when we lift the map of fans to a map of stacky fans, we might end up sending the generator
u_{\rho}
of a ray to a non-lattice point. We return to the running example of the Hirzebruch surface to illustrate this.

Example 3.1
.

We wish to find a toric DM stack
\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}
that maps to both
\mathbb{P}(1,1,3)
and

Since the fan for
\mathcal{H}_{3}

\mathcal{H}_{3}
.

does not map to the stack
\mathbb{P}(1,1,3)
. Nevertheless, as there are only 2 chambers in

in this example, it is not difficult to see what to do. Let

The Cox category

In this subsection, we establish some basic properties about
D_{\operatorname{Cox}}
;
see Appendix
A
for an alternative colimit description. The following is well-known for varieties, and the same essential proof goes through in our context.

Lemma 3.7
.

If
\alpha\colon{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{X}}
is a proper, birational morphism of smooth toric Deligne–Mumford stacks such that
\alpha_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}}={\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}}
, then
\alpha^{*}\colon D({\mathcal{X}})\to D({\mathcal{X}}^{\prime})
is fully faithful.
It follows that the functors
\pi_{i}^{*}\colon D({\mathcal{X}}_{i})\to D(\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}})
are fully faithful for all
i
.

Proof.

For any
\mathcal{F}\in D({\mathcal{X}})
,

\alpha_{\ast}\alpha^{\ast}\mathcal{F}\cong\mathcal{F}\otimes\alpha_{\ast}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}}
by the projection formula, which can be applied as
\mathcal{F}
is quasi-isomorphic to a bounded complex of locally free sheaves since
{\mathcal{X}}
is smooth.
Hence the unit map
\mathrm{id}\to\alpha_{\ast}\alpha^{\ast}
is an
isomorphism.
Consequently,
\alpha^{\ast}
is fully faithful. The application to the
\pi_{i}
from
Proposition

3.5
is immediate.
∎

With the standard notation for thick subcategories generated by a
collection of of subcategories, Definition
1.1
can be rewritten as

D_{\operatorname{Cox},\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}}\coloneqq\langle\pi_{1}^{\ast}D({\mathcal{X}}_{1}),\ldots,\pi_{r}^{\ast}D({\mathcal{X}}_{r})\rangle,

which is the smallest thick subcategory generated by the pullback categories from
each chamber. For the moment, we include the choice of
\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}
in the notation for
D_{\operatorname{Cox}}
though, as we will confirm in a moment, the category
D_{\operatorname{Cox}}
does not depend on the choice of
\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}
. First, we show the following:

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\alpha\colon\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}^{\prime}\to\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}
be a proper birational map of smooth toric
stacks induced by a stacky refinement of fans. Write

\rho_{i}=\pi_{i}\circ\alpha\colon\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
. Then
\alpha^{*}
is an
equivalence of categories between
\langle\pi_{1}^{*}D({\mathcal{X}}_{1}),\dots,\pi_{r}^{*}D({\mathcal{X}}_{r})\rangle
and
\langle\rho_{1}^{*}D({\mathcal{X}}_{1}),\dots,\rho_{r}^{*}D({\mathcal{X}}_{r})\rangle
.

Proof.

The functor
\alpha^{*}
is fully faithful by

Lemma

3.7
. It induces an equivalence since it essentially
surjects onto the generating set.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Given two such
\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}^{\prime}
and
\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}
, there is a natural equivalence

D_{\operatorname{Cox},\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}^{\prime}}\simeq D_{\operatorname{Cox},\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}}
.

Proof.

By
Proposition

3.6
, there exists
\widetilde{{\mathcal{Y}}}
with
\pi\colon\widetilde{{\mathcal{Y}}}\to\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}
and
\pi^{\prime}\colon\widetilde{{\mathcal{Y}}}\to\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}^{\prime}
both birational and proper.
Then apply
Lemma

3.8
to obtain the chain of equivalences.
∎

In view of

, we will drop any mention of
\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}
from

.

Definition 3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}\subseteq D(\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}})
be the subcategory of elements

, where
\pi_{i*}\mathcal{E}=0

We prove in

.
For now, we simply observe the following weaker results:

Lemma 3.11

The category

is the right

.

Proof.

Consider an element in

of the form

for some

.
By adjunction,
\operatorname{Hom}_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}}(\pi_{i}^{*}\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})=\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}}(\mathcal{E},\pi_{i*}\mathcal{F})=0

is generated by the elements of the form

for all

Conversely, if

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F}\in D_{\operatorname{Cox}}

and

.

Proof.

The cone

for all

. ∎

Recall that

The duality functor

If

The duality from

∎

The Bondal–Thomsen collection for the Cox category

We now define a line bundle

Proposition 4.2

\pi_{i}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}}(-d)=\pi_{j}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{j}}(-d)

Choose

in
\operatorname{Cl}(X)

be the support function of

Since the image of

\geqslant-\sum_{\rho\in\sigma(1)}c_{\rho}a_{\rho}\\

Remark 4.8
.

We caution the reader
that there are some categories which might appear similar to
D_{\operatorname{Cox}}
but are in fact not equivalent.
For instance, again consider the Hirzebruch surface
\mathcal{H}_{3}
.
The Cox ring is
S=\Bbbk[x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]
and the
irrelevant ideal is
B_{1}\coloneqq(x_{0},x_{2})\cap(x_{1},x_{3})
. Thus
D(\mathcal{H}_{3})=D(S)/D_{B_{1}}
where
D_{B_{1}}
is the bounded derived category of finitely generated

S
-modules whose homology is
B_{1}
-torsion.
If
B_{2}\coloneqq(x_{1})\cap(x_{0},x_{2},x_{3})
, then similarly
D(\mathbb{P}(1,1,3))=D(S)/D_{B_{2}}
.
However, it is not that case
D_{\operatorname{Cox}}
equals
D(S)/D_{B_{1}+B_{2}}
. In
fact,
D(S)/D_{B_{1}+B_{2}}
is the derived category of the non-separated
stack one obtains by gluing
\mathcal{H}_{3}
and
\mathbb{P}(1,1,3)
along their birational
locus; it is not even a proper dg-category.

Similarly, there is generally not some
\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}
with
D_{\operatorname{Cox}}=D(\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}})
. This
is perhaps easiest to see for the Atiyah Flop example: with notation as in

Example

4.6
,
D_{\operatorname{Cox}}\subseteq D(\widetilde{Y})
.
But
K_{0}(D_{\operatorname{Cox}})=\mathbb{Z}^{3}
by Theorem
A
, whereas
K_{0}(D(\widetilde{Y}))=\mathbb{Z}^{4}

by
[
21
]
.

We also note that
D_{\operatorname{Cox}}
is not a monoidal category in the “obvious way;” it is not closed with respect to the induced monoidal structure from
D(\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}})
.
For instance, in
Example

4.6
, tensor products of the line bundles

\pi_{+}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Y_{+}}(-1)
and
\pi_{-}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Y_{-}}(1)
generate all of the line
bundles on
\widetilde{Y}
. Since
D(\widetilde{Y})
is strictly larger than
D_{\operatorname{Cox}}
, it follows that
that
D_{\operatorname{Cox}}
is not closed with respect to this monoidal structure,
as it would be if it were the category supported along subvariety or the
pullback from some minimal rational resolution.

One must also be careful in passing from
S
-modules
to elements of
D_{\operatorname{Cox}}
. For instance, in
Example

4.6
, there is no
element of
D_{\operatorname{Cox}}
that pushes forward to both
{\mathcal{O}}_{Y_{+}}(-2)
and

{\mathcal{O}}_{Y_{-}}(-2)
. Said another way, it is not the case that
D_{\operatorname{Cox}}
can
always be expressed as the Verdier quotient of
D(S)
along a torsion
subcategory.

Proof of the
\Theta
-Transform Lemma

Recall that for any
1\leqslant i,j\leqslant r
, there is a commutative diagram as below, where the horizontal arrow is a birational map.

\textstyle{\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{j}}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{X}}_{j}.}

Our goal is to understand how elements of the Bondal–Thomsen collection behave with respect to these birational maps, and the associated Fourier–Mukai transforms.
For convenience, we prove the following restatement of
Lemma

1.6
.

Lemma 4.9
.

If
-d\in\Theta
and has image in
\Gamma_{i}
, then

\pi_{j\ast}\pi_{i}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}}(-d)={\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{j}}(-d)

As discussed after

4.6
, the hypotheses provide a delicate balance. Since

has image in
\Gamma_{i}

is anti-nef, and this factors into our proof that

is the right line bundle. The fact that

implies that it is “close to
0

Our proof involves testing the object
\pi_{j\ast}\pi_{i}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}}(-d)
against nef line bundles.
This allows us to reduce to a cohomology computation for line bundles that can be expressed as a difference of nef line bundles, meaning those of the form
D_{1}-D_{2}

D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are nef.
(Recall that nef line bundles on a toric DM stack are defined in

2.19

Then
[
5
]
*Theorem III.6 can be used to analyze such divisors in terms of their polytopes.
To reduce to a set of cohomology computations, we rely on the following lemma.

Lemma 4.10
.

Suppose that
\pi\colon{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{X}}
is a proper morphism of smooth toric DM stacks with quasi-projective coarse moduli spaces. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on

{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}

If

H^{p}({\mathcal{X}},\pi_{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}}(A))\cong H^{p}({\mathcal{X}},(R^{0}\pi_{*}\mathcal{F})\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}}(A))

\pi_{*}\mathcal{F}=R^{0}\pi_{*}\mathcal{F}

The statement is likely known to experts, but as we could not find a precise reference that implied this result, we provide a separate proof based on a pair of elementary lemmas.

Lemma 4.12

If
{\mathcal{X}}
is a smooth toric DM stack with coarse moduli morphism
\pi\colon\mathcal{X}\to X
, where

is

Proof.

By

such that

is a generating sheaf. By definition,

\mathcal{F}
.
(Note that
\pi_{\ast}
is exact.)
There are enough line bundles on

, so there is a surjection
\mathcal{V}\to\pi_{\ast}\mathcal{H}om(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})
with

Since

{\mathcal{X}}
is smooth and

amounts to the fact that

To do this, choose an ample line bundle

Proof of

Since

If

Definition 6.3

To simplify notation, write

Proposition 6.4
.

For any

\operatorname{H}_{S,S}|_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}\times{\mathcal{X}}_{j}}

is homotopy equivalent to
\operatorname{H}_{i,j}
as complexes of sheaves on
{\mathcal{X}}_{i}\times{\mathcal{X}}_{j}
. Moreover, if both chambers
\Gamma_{i}
and
\Gamma_{j}
for
{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
and
{\mathcal{X}}_{j}
are in the moving cone of

Proof.

The key idea is to iteratively apply
[
54
]
*Lemma 3.3, which provides a statement about the functorial behavior of
\operatorname{H}_{\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{X}}

with respect to pullback along an open inclusion. While
[
54
]
*Lemma 3.3 is only stated for removal of a single toric divisor, it can clearly be iterated. Moreover, the statement also holds when the map
\alpha
from the cited lemma involves removing higher codimension toric strata, since in that case the complex
\alpha^{*}\operatorname{H}_{\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{X}}
coincides with
\operatorname{H}_{\mathcal{Y}^{\prime},\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}

Now, since

can be obtained from

by removing the toric strata

[

only depends on the rays of the stacky fan.

Proof of

By Theorem

is isomorphic to
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}}(-d)

By adjunction, the identity morphism from

Now choose some other

\pi_{j*}(f)\colon\pi_{j*}{\mathcal{O}}_{\operatorname{Cox}}(-d)\to\pi_{j*}G

and the
\Theta
-Transform Lemma, this is simply a map

\pi_{j*}({\mathcal{O}}_{\operatorname{Cox}}(-d))={\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{j}}(-d)\xrightarrow{\ \pi_{j*}f\ }{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{j}}(-d)=\pi_{j*}G.

and

must agree when we pass to the open torus inside of
{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
and
{\mathcal{X}}_{j}
.
Since the restriction from

to the torus

must also equal the identity.
We conclude that the map

and

The Fourier–Mukai transform

\textstyle{D_{\operatorname{Cox}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

In this brief subsection, we provide some basic language for comparing these objects and the associated Fourier–Mukai transforms.

Recall from

There are natural functors with

For each pair of functors

.

\operatorname{H}_{S,S}

Cox rings and Bondal–Thomsen free monads

In this section, we show that
D_{\operatorname{Cox}}
can be used to provide uniform free monads across all toric birational models of a given toric variety.
Recall from
Section

2.4
that, given an

S
-module
P
and a cone
\Gamma\in\Sigma_{GKZ}(X)
, there is a corresponding
sheaf
P|_{X_{\Gamma}}
on the variety
X_{\Gamma}
.
This assignment is functorial and hence extends to complexes and derived categories.

For a sheaf
\mathcal{E}
on a toric variety or toric stack
{\mathcal{Y}}
, a
free monad
is a graded complex
F
of free modules over the Cox ring such that
F|_{{\mathcal{Y}}}\cong\mathcal{E}

F|_{{\mathcal{Y}}}
as a free monad as well. The Beilinson collection produces free monads for
\mathbb{P}^{n}
that have been used in many contexts; see, e.g.,
[
37
]
*§8 and related citations, or the complexes arising from one of the Beilinson spectral sequences, e.g.,
[
29
]
*§3.7. Similar monads for weighted projective stacks also appear in
[
25
]
*Corollary 1.8.

Recall from
Convention

2.1
that
K_{\Theta}(S)
is the homotopy category of free
S
-modules whose generators have degrees lying in
\Theta
.
From
Corollary

5.4
, the functor
\Psi\colon K_{\Theta}(S)\to D_{\operatorname{Cox}}
is an equivalence, with inverse
\Phi_{\operatorname{Cox},S}\colon D_{\operatorname{Cox}}\to K_{\Theta}(S)

-modules

On

[S^{2}\longleftarrow S(-1)^{3}]_{0}\cong[k^{2}\longleftarrow 0]\cong H^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-3)).

Generalizing to the Bondal–Thomsen collection on

For each

.

Let
\mathcal{E}\in D_{\operatorname{Cox}}

In addition to their influence on mirror symmetry, the ideas from Bondal’s Oberwolfach report

[

Throughout this section, assume that

given by

\beta(e_{\rho})=u_{\rho}
for all
\rho\in\Sigma(1)

\Gamma

\Sigma_{GKZ}(X)

12

12

If
\Sigma_{GKZ}

4.4
where
\Theta

[
34
]
*Definition 5.1.15, and we summarize a few key properties.
Each primitive collection

[

]

Definition 7.25

Set

To prove

Corollary 7.27
.

If
-d\in\Theta_{\Gamma}^{\circ}
, then the twisted Koszul complex

has generators lying entirely in

.

Proof.

The statement is a corollary of Lemma

. The free summands of the Koszul complex

are of the form

, where

and

. In particular, each

lies in

Since

, it follows that

. By Lemma

, this lies in

, and hence

. Thus,

is a Koszul complex where all summands are of the form

, with

and with
-e_{I}\in\overline{Z_{+}}
.
∎

Example 7.28
.

In our Hirzebruch surface example, if

Proof of Theorem
7.26
.

Since
\Theta
generates
D({\mathcal{X}})
, it suffices to prove that each
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}}(-d)
with
-d\in\Theta_{\Gamma}^{\circ}
can be generated by
\Theta\setminus\Theta_{\Gamma}^{\circ}
.
Continuing with notation from the proof of
Corollary

7.27
, since
\deg_{\Gamma}(x_{\rho})>0
for all
\rho\in P
,
\deg_{\Gamma}(d+e_{I})>\deg_{\Gamma}(d)
for
\emptyset\neq I\subseteq P
.
Thus, for each
-d\in\Theta_{\Gamma}^{\circ}
, the line bundle
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}}(-d)
can be generated by the elements in
\Theta
of strictly larger degree with respect to
\deg_{\Gamma}
.
In particular, iterating this argument eventually generates all elements of
\Theta_{\Gamma}^{\circ}
by those in
\Theta\setminus\Theta_{\Gamma}^{\circ}
. The final claim of the theorem follows from the fact that the coarse moduli morphism realizes
D(X)
as a quotient of
D({\mathcal{X}})
.
∎

Remark 7.29
.

The results in this section give an indication of both what can go right and what go wrong in the search for full strong exceptional collections for
D({\mathcal{X}})
on its own. In the case of the Hirzebruch surface, the elements of
\Theta\setminus\Theta_{\Gamma}^{\circ}
all lie in the chamber corresponding to the Hirzebruch surface because there is only one wall. Therefore, the generators from
Theorem

7.26
coincide with a well-known full strong exceptional collection of line bundles for the Hirzebruch surface.

On the other hand, imagine that the nef cone is bounded on both sides by interior walls of
\Sigma_{GKZ}
. Then we will have to consider a pair of primitive collections
P
and
P^{\prime}
. The corresponding reductions could interfere with one another. For instance, for
-d\in\Theta_{\Gamma}^{\circ}
, the Koszul complex
K_{P}(-d)
might spill into
\Theta_{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{\circ}
, and we might then be unable to generate with only elements from
\Theta\setminus(\Theta_{\Gamma}^{\circ}\cup\Theta_{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{\circ})
.
∎

construction of

such that the following diagrams commute for all triples of composable morphisms

, and

in
I

(A.1)

The

consists of functors

Given

The objects and morphisms are as follows:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Obj}\int\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{(i,A)\mid A\in\operatorname{Obj}\mathcal{C}(i)\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\int\mathcal{C}}((i,A),(j,B))

Composition of morphisms is given by

).
The category

]
*Theorem 10.2.3, which realizes it as a lax colimit of

.
For algebraic geometers, the Grothendieck construction is

prestacks and fibered categories; see, e.g.,
[
76
]
*Chapter 3.
It provides a flexible framework for gluing diagrams of
categories. In particular, it is straightforward to
realize a semiorthogonal decomposition, interpreted as
two categories glued along a bimodule, as the
pretriangulated envelope of
a Grothendieck construction, where the indexing category
is the

-quiver.

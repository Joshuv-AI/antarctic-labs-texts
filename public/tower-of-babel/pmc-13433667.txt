Integrating machine learning and spatiotemporal transcriptomics to build a diagnostic model for osteosarcoma metastasis and to decipher the role of necroptosis genes
Authors: Sun Jiahao, Cui Xu, Gong Rui, Xie Wenpeng, Zhang Yongkui
Published: 2026

Introduction

Osteosarcoma is a primary malignant bone tumor that frequently occurs in children and adolescents. Despite the progress made in the comprehensive treatment strategy combining surgery and chemotherapy, the prognosis of patients remains unsatisfactory, mainly due to the high metastatic tendency of the tumor. Data show that the 5-year survival rate of patients with localized osteosarcoma can reach 65–70%, but once metastasis occurs, the survival rate drops rapidly to below 30% [1, 2]. The treatment of metastatic osteosarcoma remains a major challenge in clinical practice.

In recent years, necroptosis, a regulated form of necrotic cell death, has gradually attracted attention for its role in tumor progression. This process has a dual role: on the one hand, it can induce tumor cell death, inhibit primary tumor growth and metastasis; on the other hand, the release of endogenous molecules such as damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) during necroptosis may mediate the activation of inflammatory signals and the remodeling of the immune microenvironment, thereby promoting tumor escape and distant colonization [3–6]. This complexity in mechanism suggests that necroptosis may play a key role in osteosarcoma metastasis [7, 8]. However, traditional transcriptome-based studies have difficulty fully revealing cell type-specific gene expression changes and spatiotemporal dynamics within the tumor microenvironment. With the advent of new technologies such as single-cell sequencing and spatial transcriptomics, researchers can now dissect tumor heterogeneity and intercellular communication within the microenvironment at higher resolution. Pseudotemporal analysis can reconstruct the trajectory of cell state transitions [3], while spatial transcriptomics can localize the regional characteristics of gene expression [5], providing powerful tools for revealing the dynamics of necroptosis-related genes during the metastatic process. Meanwhile, machine learning methods can integrate multi-omics data to uncover key patterns in the microenvironment, thereby assisting in the construction of more accurate predictive models.

This study integrates bulk transcriptome, single-cell sequencing and spatial transcriptome data, and combines machine learning strategies to construct a prediction model for osteosarcoma metastasis, screen key genes and evaluate their prognostic value. Further, molecular docking is used to predict targeted drugs, and experiments are conducted to verify the expression of key genes. The focus is on revealing the dynamic evolution rules of necroptosis-related genes during the metastasis process through temporal reconstruction and spatial positioning analysis, with the aim of generating novel hypotheses and providing new candidate targets for the early diagnosis and targeted treatment of osteosarcoma metastasis.

Research routes

Data source and processing

Osteosarcoma RNA-seq data were downloaded from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database, which provides a comprehensive resource of genomic data from various cancer types. According to whether the patient’s primary osteosarcoma had metastasis, they were divided into primary osteosarcoma non-metastasis group (NMOS) and primary osteosarcoma with metastasis group (MOS), and one patient with unknown metastasis was excluded. Finally, a total of 64 NMOS patients and 22 MOS patients were included. DESeq2 was used for differential expression analysis, and the screening criteria were: adjusted p < 0.05 and |log2 FC| ≥ 1. At the same time, GSE21257 and GSE32981 chip data sets were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database, a public repository for high-throughput gene expression data, and normalized by limma package as the verification set (Supplementary Table 1). Immune infiltration analysis was performed to assess the proportion of 22 immune cells by CIBERSORT. All pairwise comparisons for immune cell infiltration differences between groups were performed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, with BH false discovery rate (FDR) correction for multiple testing. Statistical significance was defined as adjusted p < 0.05, and effect sizes (median difference with 95% CIs) were reported for all significant comparisons. The unit of analysis for all bulk transcriptome and clinical correlation analyses was individual patient sample.

Single-cell sequencing and spatial transcriptome data processing

Single-cell sequencing data analysis: Single-cell sequencing data associated with NMOS and MOS were downloaded from the GEO database (GSE250015), including 1 treatment-naive primary non-metastatic OS sample and 1 treatment-naive primary metastatic OS sample, with no biological replicates. Data preprocessing and quality control were performed using Limma, Seurat, Dplyr, and Magrittr R packages. The quality control criteria were set as follows: cells with detected gene number between 200 and 10,000, mitochondrial gene percentage <20%, and at least 3 genes detected per cell. Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed on the filtered data, and the first 20 PCs were selected for tSNE and UMAP cluster analysis, with the resolution set to 0.2. Cell clusters were annotated according to canonical marker gene expression, and DESeq2 and Wilcoxon rank-sum test were used to calculate DEGs between cell clusters. All scRNA-seq analyses are strictly exploratory and hypothesis-generating, with no definitive clinical or mechanistic conclusions drawn. Individual cells were not treated as independent statistical replicates for patient-level inferences, and all statistical tests were only used to describe the heterogeneity of cell populations within the tested samples.

Time trajectory analysis: The combined analysis strategy of Monocle1 and Monocle3 was used [3]. Monocle1 constructed the overall cell differentiation trajectory and calculated the pseudo-time series by DDRTree algorithm, and Monocle3 identified gene expression modules based on partition graph abstraction (PAGA). The dynamic changes of trajectory related genes were analyzed by Moran’s I.

Spatial transcriptome data analysis: Spatial transcriptome raw data were obtained from two independent, non-paired sources: 1 primary OS tissue sample from the Code Ocean platform, and 1 OS peritumoral lymphoid tissue sample from the GEO database (GSE237070). Both samples were sequenced using the 10× Genomics Visium platform to ensure basic data comparability. The datasets included gene expression matrices and spatial coordinate information. Data preprocessing was completed by Seurat and SpatialExperiment packages. First, strict quality control was carried out to eliminate low-quality capture points, and tissue edges or image blurred areas were excluded. Subsequently, SCTransform was used to correct the technical noise and standardize the expression data, and the spatial domain was identified by combining principal component analysis and graph-based clustering. To resolve the spatial specificity, we further combined the results of single-cell annotation, mapped cell types by marker gene colocalization, and used SPARK algorithm to detect spatially variable genes (FDR < 0.05). All spatial transcriptome analyses are strictly exploratory and hypothesis-generating, with no definitive paired comparisons between primary tumor and metastatic niche drawn due to the non-paired, single-case sample design.

Machine learning model construction

This model was developed and reported in full compliance with the Transparent Reporting of a multivariable prediction model for Individual Prognosis Or Diagnosis with Artificial Intelligence (TRIPOD-AI) guidelines.

Data preparation

The batch-effect corrected TCGA-OS dataset was integrated as the training set (64 non-metastatic cases and 22 metastatic cases), and GSE21257 and GSE32981 were used as independent stratified external validation sets (stratified matching by clinical stage of osteosarcoma and patient age). The metastatic status of the sample was coded as a binary outcome variable (0 = non-metastatic, 1 = metastatic). No missing data were present in the training or validation datasets for the included features and outcome.

Selection and combination of algorithms

In this study, a variety of machine learning algorithms such as Ridge, Lasso, SVM, Logistic Regression, Random Forest and glmBoost were used, and combined with Elastic Net regularization, XGBoost and other algorithms to construct 113 algorithm combination strategies. To avoid model selection bias, all algorithms adopted unified feature input, preprocessing workflow, and evaluation criteria. The area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) was used as the primary optimization index, and a comprehensive evaluation system was constructed combined with sensitivity, specificity, precision, recall and F1-score to achieve accurate differentiation of metastatic and non-metastatic samples.

Data segmentation, cross validation, and data leakage prevention

To eliminate data leakage and ensure unbiased model evaluation, all feature preprocessing steps (including Z-score normalization, variance filtering) and feature selection (differentially expressed gene filtering) were performed exclusively within the training set of each resampling fold during the nested cross-validation procedure. The held-out test set in the outer fold was never used for feature engineering, preprocessing, or hyperparameter tuning at any point.

A nested tenfold cross-validation framework was used to evaluate model performance:

Outer tenfold cross-validation: The training dataset was split into ten stratified folds to maintain the consistent ratio of metastatic/non-metastatic samples in each fold, addressing the class imbalance in the dataset. For each iteration, ninefolds were used as the training set, and onefold was held out as the unseen test set for unbiased model performance evaluation and final model selection.

Outer tenfold cross-validation: The training dataset was split into ten stratified folds to maintain the consistent ratio of metastatic/non-metastatic samples in each fold, addressing the class imbalance in the dataset. For each iteration, ninefolds were used as the training set, and onefold was held out as the unseen test set for unbiased model performance evaluation and final model selection.

Inner tenfold cross-validation: Performed exclusively on the nine training folds from the outer loop, the inner tenfold cross-validation was dedicated to hyperparameter tuning via grid search, with no access to the held-out outer test set.

Inner tenfold cross-validation: Performed exclusively on the nine training folds from the outer loop, the inner tenfold cross-validation was dedicated to hyperparameter tuning via grid search, with no access to the held-out outer test set.

Meanwhile, 3 repetitions of the full nested tenfold cross-validation were performed to reduce random errors. To further avoid overfitting, the maximum tree depth was limited for all tree-based models, an early stopping strategy was adopted when validation set performance no longer improved, and the regularization penalty coefficient was optimized for linear models. Finally, the glmBoost+Ridge combined model with the optimal and most stable generalization performance across all cross-validation folds and external validation sets was selected as the final diagnostic model.

Hyperparameter tuning and range

The grid search method was used to optimize the key hyperparameters within the inner cross-validation loop: the alpha parameter of Elastic Net regularization (0.1–1, step size 0.1), the maximum tree depth of glmBoost (3–10, step size 1) and the learning rate (0.01–0.2, step size 0.01). The hyperparameter combination with the highest AUC value in the inner validation set was selected for each outer fold.

Model evaluation and validation

Model performance was evaluated on the held-out test set from each outer cross-validation fold, with the following metrics recorded: AUC, sensitivity, specificity, precision, recall, F1-score, and negative predictive value. A confusion matrix was constructed for the final model on the full training dataset. Two completely independent external validation sets (GSE21257 and GSE32981) that were never used in model training or tuning were used to evaluate the out-of-sample generalization performance of the final model, with AUC and classification accuracy calculated. The full model development pipeline is visualized in https://github.com/sjhjoint/MSM-Original-code.git.

Functional enrichment analysis

Based on the intersection results of differentially expressed genes, we used Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) for systematic enrichment analysis. GO analysis covered the three dimensions of Biological Process, Molecular Function and Cellular Component, and KEGG analysis focused on the enrichment characteristics of genes in metabolic and signaling pathways.

Downloading and processing of clinical data

The clinical data of OS were downloaded from TCGA database (Supplementary Table 1). According to whether the patient had primary osteosarcoma metastasis, the patients were divided into primary osteosarcoma non-metastasis group (NMOS) and primary osteosarcoma metastasis group (MOS), and 2 patients with unknown survival status and 1 patient with unknown survival time were excluded. Finally, 63 NMOS patients and 21 MOS patients were included.

Survival analysis and prognostic model construction

The clinical data of OS were downloaded from TCGA database. In order to evaluate the effect of intersection genes on the prognosis of patients with osteosarcoma, Cox regression model was used for univariate and multivariate analysis. The gene expression level, survival time and survival status factors were included in the model to screen out the gene prognostic factors related to the overall survival of patients. In addition, Kaplan–Meier curves were used to analyze survival differences among patients with different gene expression levels, and the significance of curves was compared by log-rank test. At the same time, the receiver operating characteristic (ROC)–AUC was constructed to evaluate the predictive efficacy of the genetic risk score for the prognosis of patients.

Drug screening and molecular docking

Based on core gene drug screening, using DSigDB database (https://maayanlab.cloud/Enrichr/enrich), look for potential drugs. Selected clinical commonly used drugs and predictions from Pubchem (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) for the chemical structure of active ingredients, the 3D structure of the target Protein was obtained from RCSB Protein Data Bank (PDB) (http://www.pdb.org/). Then uploaded to the CB—Dock2 (https://cadd.labshare.cn/cbdock2/php/blinddock.php), the calculation combined with affinity (kcal/mol) [5, 9], the stronger the binding energy of the lower tips of interaction between proteins and molecules. Binding energies less than −4.25 kcal/mol indicate good docking affinity, and binding energies less than −7 kcal/mol indicate excellent docking affinity.

Experimental validation

To validate the role of the core genes (MYC and TNFRSF21) identified through bioinformatics analysis in osteosarcoma metastasis, we conducted the following experiments at both the cellular model and omics levels.

RT-qPCR validation

All RT-qPCR experiments were performed and reported in full compliance with the Minimum Information for Publication of Quantitative Real-Time PCR Experiments (MIQE) guidelines. Comparative groups were established based on the metastatic potential of osteosarcoma cell lines. The high-metastatic potential group comprised the highly invasive 143B and MG63 cell lines, while the low-metastatic potential group included the U2OS and Saos-2 cell lines [10–13]. Total RNA was isolated from the cells using RNA ISO Plus reagent (Takara Bio, Japan; Cat#9108). RNA integrity was verified via 1.5% agarose gel electrophoresis, with A260/A280 ratios between 1.8 and 2.1 and A260/A230 ratios >2.0 for all RNA samples. Isolated RNA was treated with DNase I (Takara Bio, Japan; Cat#2270A) to eliminate genomic DNA contamination. Reverse transcription and quantitative PCR amplification were performed in one-step reactions using the One Step PrimeScript™RT-PCR Kit (Takara Bio, Japan; Cat#RR064A) on an ABI 7500 Real-Time PCR System. Gene-specific primer sequences were designed using NCBI Primer-BLAST, with all primer pairs spanning exon-exon junctions to avoid genomic DNA amplification. Primer sequences were as follows:

MYC: Forward 5′-TTCTCTCCGTCCTCGGATTC-3′, Reverse 5′-CCAGCAGAAGGTGATCCA GAC-3′; TNFRSF21: Forward 5′-TTCGTGGGCTGATGGAAGAC-3′, Reverse 5′-GAGCCGCTGGA TGTAGAGTC-3′; GAPDH (reference gene): Forward 5′-CTGGGCTACACTGAGCACC-3′, Reverse 5′-AAGTGGTCGTTGAGGGCAATG-3′.

The amplification efficiency of all primer pairs was verified to be between 90 and 110% via 5-point tenfold serial dilution standard curve analysis, with R2 > 0.99 for all standard curves. The stability of the reference gene GAPDH across all experimental groups was verified via geNorm analysis, with a stability value M < 0.5, confirming its suitability for normalization. Relative gene expression was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method with normalization to GAPDH. Three independent biological replicates were performed for each experiment, with technical triplicates in every qPCR run.

Gene expression and correlation analysis based on self-sequenced transcriptomic data

To further validate the expression patterns and potential associations of the aforementioned genes in independent omics data, we performed transcriptome sequencing on the representative high-metastatic potential cell line MG63 and the low-metastatic potential cell line U2OS. These two cell lines were selected because they stably represent high and low metastatic phenotypes, respectively, with well-defined in vitro growth characteristics, making them suitable for modeling metastasis-related transcriptomic features. Each group consisted of four biological replicates. Following total RNA extraction and quality control, mRNA was enriched using Oligo (dT) beads. Sequencing libraries were constructed and subjected to paired-end 150 bp sequencing on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform. Raw data underwent quality control via FastQC and filtering via Fastp. Clean reads were aligned to the human reference genome GRCh38 using HISAT2, and gene counts were quantified using featureCounts. Gene expression levels were normalized using FPKM. Based on this data, we conducted the following analyses:

Differential Expression Validation: DESeq2 was used to compare the expression levels of MYC and TNFRSF21 between the MG63 and U2OS groups (screening criteria: adjusted p value <0.05 and |log2 fold change| ≥ 1).

Differential Expression Validation: DESeq2 was used to compare the expression levels of MYC and TNFRSF21 between the MG63 and U2OS groups (screening criteria: adjusted p value <0.05 and |log2 fold change| ≥ 1).

Expression Correlation Analysis: The Pearson correlation coefficient between the expression of MYC and TNFRSF21 was calculated within this dataset.

Expression Correlation Analysis: The Pearson correlation coefficient between the expression of MYC and TNFRSF21 was calculated within this dataset.

Results

Transcriptome data analysis

The transcriptional profile data related to OS were obtained, including 64 patients with primary osteosarcoma without metastasis and 22 patients with primary osteosarcoma with metastasis, and 28 DEGs were screened, including 20 up-regulated DEGs and 8 down-regulated DEGs (Fig. 2A, B). Immune infiltration analysis showed that regulatory T cells, M2 macrophages and neutrophils were increased in the metastasis group. However, the percentages of T cells CD8, NK cells resting/activated and Macrophages M1 were significantly decreased, suggesting that the metastatic microenvironment was characterized by immunosuppression. In addition, there were significant differences in the activation of dendritic cells and mast cells between the two groups (p < 0.05). Correlation heatmaps further revealed a remodeling of synergistic or antagonistic relationships among immune cells in the metastatic group, such as the positive correlation between NK cells and T cells CD4 memory resting state was weakened, and the synergistic effect between macrophages M2 and Tregs was enhanced. This suggests that tumor cells may promote metastasis by disrupting the intercellular immune balance (Fig. 2C–E).

Single-cell sequencing data analysis

This in-depth analysis of the cellular heterogeneity of metastatic versus nonmetastatic osteosarcoma samples by single-cell sequencing revealed significant differences in cell-type composition, gene-expression signatures, and dynamic behavior between the two groups (Fig. 3). At the gene expression level, nFeature_RNA and nCount_RNA were more widely distributed in the Metastatic group, indicating that the transcriptional activity and heterogeneity of cells in the metastatic state were enhanced, and some cells may enter a hypermetabolic or stress state. The difference in metabolic-related indicators was particularly prominent. The proportion of mitochondrial genes in the Metastatic group was significantly increased, suggesting that the transferred cells may adapt to microenvironmental stress by enhancing oxidative phosphorylation or mitochondria-dependent metabolic pathways.

Cell clustering analysis showed that the Metastatic group showed higher cellular heterogeneity and a more dispersed distribution of cell subsets, suggesting that the process of metastasis may be accompanied by the emergence of new cell states or the functional remodeling of original cell types. The cell clusters in the Control group showed a relatively concentrated pattern, while multiple subsets in the Metastatic group showed obvious spatial separation, especially the significant expansion of cell clusters related to invasion and migration in the umap MAP. Cell type annotation further revealed that the proportion of fibroblast-like cells and myeloid derived immune cells was significantly increased in the Metastatic group, while the proportion of normal bone stromal cells was decreased.

Pseudotime trajectory analysis revealed increased trajectory complexity in the metastatic sample, characterized by multi-branching patterns. This suggests that a subset of cells may undergo a deviation from the primary differentiation path, potentially acquiring a state associated with invasiveness. This observed dynamic heterogeneity may be linked to the process of metastasis.

In-depth analysis of osteosarcoma metastatic group (Treat) versus non-metastatic group (Control) by single-cell sequencing constructed cell communication networks and revealed significant dysregulation of immune cell interactions in metastatic samples (Fig. 4). Cell communication network analysis showed that the signal intensity of pro-angiogenic and immunosuppressive pathways was significantly increased in the metastasis group, and these interactions were mainly dominated by endothelial cells, macrophages, and fibroblasts. The abnormal activation of key molecular pairs further drives the disorder of the immune microenvironment. The correlation analysis further revealed that the positive correlation between NK cells and CD4+ memory resting T cells was weakened, while the synergistic effect between M2 macrophages and Tregs was enhanced in the metastatic group, suggesting that tumors promote immune escape by remodeling the immune network.

Machine learning model construction and enrichment analysis of intersection genes

Through cross-analysis of TCGA database and single-cell sequencing gene differential data of osteosarcoma with and without metastasis, 8 differential genes were obtained. The glmBoost+Ridge model selected FOS, GZMA, PMAIP1, TNFRSF21, FBL, FOSB, and MYC as core genes, which all contributed significantly to the diagnostic performance of the model. The glmBoost+Ridge model performed best on the training set, with an AUC of 0.861. Meanwhile, the AUC of the model in the external validation set GSE32981 was 0.873, showing a strong generalization ability, but the AUC in the validation set GSE21257 was 0.642, indicating that the small sample size or the difference in feature distribution in this dataset may limit the performance of the model. The confusion matrix showed that the model had a high accuracy in the training set and the GSE32981 validation set, while the misclassification rate in GSE21257 was relatively high (Fig. 5A–C).

Enrichment analysis results showed that (Fig. 5D–F), in terms of GO analysis, FOS was mainly involved in cell responses to corticosterone, hypoxia, progesterone, and other stimuli, and had RNA polymerase II-specific transcription activator activity. FOSB and FOS partially overlapped, involving the response to mineralocorticoid, progesterone, etc. MYC is widely involved in the positive regulation of p53 mediator signal transduction, positive regulation of miRNA transcription, positive regulation of intrinsic apoptosis signaling pathway and other biological processes. Myc exists in the RNA polymerase II transcription regulatory complex, and has molecular functions such as core promoter sequence-specific DNA binding. PMAIP1 is mainly involved in the positive regulation of intrinsic apoptosis signaling pathway mediated by p53 mediator and exists in the Bcl-2 family protein complex. FBL was found in the ribonucleoprotein complex containing sno(s)RNA and had rRNA methyltransferase activity. GZMA is involved in the regulation of deoxyribonuclease activity, pyroptosis and other biological processes, and exists in immune synapses. TNFRSF21 is involved in biological processes such as cell response to tumor necrosis factor, negative regulation of B cell proliferation, and regulation of interleukin production. KEGG analysis showed that these genes were involved in colorectal cancer, amphetamine addiction, IL-17 signaling pathway, apoptosis, osteoclast differentiation, breast cancer, hepatitis B, Kaposi’s sarcoma-associated herpesvirus infection, chemical carcinoma-receptor activation, human T-cell leukemia virus 1 infection, Salmonella infection, MAPK signaling pathway and so on. Enrichment analysis showed that FOS was mainly involved in cell response to corticosterone, hypoxia, progesterone and other stimuli, and had RNA polymerase II-specific transcriptional activation activity. MYC is widely involved in the positive regulation of p53 mediator signal transduction, miRNA transcription and other biological processes. Enrichment analysis of these genes in osteosarcoma showed that they were mainly involved in the osteoclast differentiation and bone matrix remodeling pathways, but not in the pan-cancer cell proliferation pathway, which confirmed that they were specific regulatory genes of osteosarcoma metastasis, but not simply pan-cancer oncogenes.

Clinical prognostic value of ML core genes

In this study, Kaplan–Meier curve and log-rank test analysis showed that only the low expression level of GZMA in a single gene had an impact on the survival of patients (p = 0.049). However, Cox multivariate analysis showed that the combination of FBL, MYC, PMAIP1 and TNFRSF21 had a statistically significant predictive value for patient survival (p = 0.0056). The further constructed ROC curve showed that the area under the curve of the ML core gene combined risk score (AUC = 0.79), indicating that the synergy of multiple genes was excellent in predicting the prognosis of patients (Fig. 6).

Drug prediction and molecular docking based on ML core genes

The top 10 potential anti-osteosarcoma metastatic drugs (AMG900, Ganetespib, etc.) were screened by drug-gene interaction network analysis, all of which showed highly significant statistical associations (p < 0.001, adj-p = 0.01) (Fig. 7A). AMG900, Ganetespib, Dinaciclib, Estrone, Calcitriol, Methylprednisolone, and Sulindac targeted the MYC gene (OR = 256.15–832.88). Baclofen, Pilocarpine, and Nimodipine targeted the FOS gene (OR = 277.51–416.35), with combined scores ranging from 1362.86 to 5287.72. These results suggest that calcitriol and sulindac have potential binding affinity with osteosarcoma metastasis signature genes at the molecular docking level. These findings are purely predictive based on bioinformatics analysis, and the actual anti-metastatic efficacy, specific pharmacological mechanism, and clinical translational value of these candidate drugs require systematic validation in subsequent in vitro and in vivo experiments.

A Results of drug screening; B results of molecular docking

Based on the predicted top 10 candidate drugs, a secondary screening was performed based on their clinical translation potential and mechanistic risk. The specific criteria for drug exclusion were as follows: AMG900 was excluded due to its reported high toxicity risk in preclinical models; Dinaciclib and Estrone were excluded because literature suggests they may promote metastasis in specific cancers; Ganetespib was not prioritized as its inhibitory effects in osteosarcoma have been well-studied。Nimodipine, Baclofen, and Pilocarpine were excluded as their primary mechanisms of action are related to the nervous or muscular systems, showing less direct relevance to the tumor necroptosis/immune axis central to this study [12–16]. Calcitriol and Sulindac were retained as core candidates based on: (1) their well-established clinical safety profiles; (2) their known anti-inflammatory properties, which align with the inflammatory microenvironment features revealed in this study; and (3) existing independent studies suggesting their potential anti-tumor or immunomodulatory effects [17, 18], making their prospects for drug repurposing more compelling. Concurrently, the clinically commonly used drugs Tazemetostat [19] and Cisplatin [20] were included as a new targeted therapy reference and a cytotoxic drug benchmark, respectively.

Binding energy data (kcal/mol) from the molecular docking results show (Fig. 7B) that Tazemetostat, a clinically common drug, exhibited the strongest binding affinity for the key necroptosis receptor TNFRSF21 (−9.4 kcal/mol) and the immune-cytotoxic gene GZMA (−9.2 kcal/mol), significantly better than for other genes such as MYC (−8.0). In contrast, Cisplatin showed weak binding to all genes (−1.1 to −1.5). Among the predicted drugs, Calcitriol showed high binding affinity for TNFRSF21 (−8.7) and the transcription factor FOS (−8.4), while Sulindac showed moderate binding affinity for TNFRSF21 (−8.4) and FOS (−8.0), but weaker binding to MYC (−6.6).

Role of necroptosis gene and its clinical prognostic value

Two key genes, MYC and TNFRSF21, were screened by cross analysis of the core genes of ML and the necrotizing apoptosis gene set [21]. GO functional enrichment analysis showed that MYC was significantly involved in cell proliferation regulation, apoptosis signal activation and hypoxic stress response, and had the function of transcription repression complex formation. TNFRSF21 was mainly enriched in the pathways related to negative immune regulation and neuronal axon development. KEGG pathway analysis further revealed that MYC played a core driving role in thyroid cancer, bladder cancer and Wnt signaling pathway, and dominated the process of tumor metabolic reprogramming. TNFRSF21 mediates immune microenvironment remodeling through JAK-STAT signaling pathway and regulation of T cell activation (Fig. 8A–C). The synergistic function of the two proteins suggests that they jointly promote the metastasis of osteosarcoma by promoting metastasis and immune escape.

In this study, the Kaplan–Meier curve and log-rank test analysis showed that the high/low expression level of single gene (MYC or TNFRSF21) had no significant effect on the survival of patients. However, Cox multivariate analysis showed that the combination of MYC and TNFRSF21 had a statistically significant predictive value for patient survival (p = 0.008). The further constructed ROC curve showed that the area under the curve of the combined risk score of MYC and TNFRSF21 (AUC = 0.696), indicating that the synergy of the two had excellent predictive efficacy for the prognosis of patients (Fig. 8D–G).

Temporal and spatial evolution of necroptosis genes

Pseudotime-series analysis of necroptosis genes

To dissect the dynamic changes of key necrotizing apoptosis-related genes MYC and TNFRSF21 in osteosarcoma metastasis progression, we performed a pseudotime analysis based on single-cell transcriptome data (GSE250015) (Fig. 9). This analysis aims to reconstruct the potential trajectory of cell evolution from a nonmetastatic to a metastatic state and to assess the expression dynamics of target genes during this process.

Pseudo-time-series analysis of the nonmetastatic versus metastatic groups. A,G Distribution of cell types in UMAP space for two groups based on SingleR annotation. B,H Overall cell time sequence evolution in both groups. C,I Contents of MYC and TNFRSF21 in the two groups. D,E,J,K Pseudotemporal expression dynamics of MYC and TNFRSF21 in different cell types and the expression trajectories of MYC and TNFRFSF21 with pseudotime in two groups. F,L Visualization of two groups of MYC and TNFRFSF21 gene expression distributions based on UMAP dimensionality reduction algorithm

In the non-metastatic state, UMAP dimensionality reduction visualization showed that the cells of non-metastatic samples presented a clear clustering pattern, reflecting the inherent heterogeneity of cell composition in osteosarcoma, and MYC and TNFRSF21 showed differential expression in specific cell subsets. TNFRSF21 was expressed at a relatively high level in some stromal or immune-related cell clusters, while MYC was mainly expressed at a moderate or low level in active proliferating cell clusters. This expression pattern is consistent with its potential role in maintaining tissue homeostasis. The cell differentiation trajectory showed that the cells of the non-metastatic samples were mainly distributed along a relatively single trajectory, suggesting that their differentiation status was relatively stable.

In the metastatic state, compared with the non-metastatic state, the UMAP map of the metastatic samples showed a completely different cell distribution pattern. Dynamic up-regulation of MYC expression: quasi-temporal analysis clearly revealed the key role of MYC in the progression of metastasis. As the cells followed the pseudo-temporal trajectory, the expression of MYC showed a significant and continuous dynamic up-regulation trend. Notably, regions with elevated MYC expression were often accompanied by significant shifts in cell differentiation trajectories, suggesting that high MYC expression is a key molecular event driving cells to depart from the normal differentiation pathway and acquire a prometastatic phenotype. Progressive loss of TNFRSF21 expression: In contrast to MYC upregulation, TNFRSF21 showed a progressive loss of expression during metastatic progression. TNFRSF21 may still retain some expression in the early trajectory region close to the nonmetastatic state, but its expression level shows a systematic decrease as cells evolve to the more aggressive late metastatic state, reaching a nadir at the end of the metastatic trajectory or on specific prometastatic branches. Increased trajectory complexity: The pseudo-time series scatter plot of the transferred samples shows that the cell trajectories of the transferred samples show multi-branching characteristics, which is significantly more complex than the single trajectory of the non-transferred state.

By comparison, the expression of MYC was continuously up-regulated during the metastasis process, which was the key factor driving the cell differentiation trajectory deviation and the acquisition of invasive phenotype. However, the expression of TNFRSF21 decreased gradually until it disappeared, and its loss of function may reach its peak in the late stage of metastasis. The up-regulation of MYC and the down-regulation of TNFRSF21 form a reverse dynamic change pattern in the time dimension, and jointly shape the microenvironment conducive to metastasis.

Spatial distribution of necroptosis genes

By comparing and analyzing the spatial transcriptome data of osteosarcoma tissues and lymphoid tissues surrounding osteosarcoma, it was found that there were significant differences in the immune microenvironment, matrix characteristics and functional pathways between the two (Fig. 10A, B). In osteosarcoma tissue, tumor proliferation-related genes and osteoclast differentiation genes are highly expressed in specific spatial regions, reflecting the malignant biological characteristics of uncontrolled tumor cell proliferation and bone matrix destruction. However, the surrounding lymphoid tissue of osteosarcoma showed abnormally activated inflammatory microenvironment, which was characterized by disordered expression of keratinocyte marker genes and significant enrichment of S100A family inflammatory factors, suggesting that chronic inflammation and immune cell infiltration may drive tumor-associated matrix remodeling and lymph node metastasis. In addition, the high expression of tumor metabolic activity genes in osteosarcoma tissue was replaced by complement pathway genes and immunosuppressive factors in the surrounding lymphoid tissue, suggesting that the tumor metastatic microenvironment may promote immune escape by inhibiting mitochondrial function and activating the complement system. This contrast reveals the molecular reprogramming mechanism of osteosarcoma cell invasion and metastasis to adjacent lymphoid tissues, and provides a potential intervention direction for targeting tumor-stroma interaction in the metastatic niche.

A,B Spatial transcript abundance distribution, spatial domain identification and UMAP mapping, and spatial domain-specific expression in osteosarcoma tissue and lymphoid tissue surrounding the metastatic front of osteosarcoma; C,D distribution of MYC and TNFRSF21 genes expression in the spatial domain of osteosarcoma tissue and lymphoid tissue surrounding the metastatic front of osteosarcoma

Analysis of spatial transcriptome slice images showed that MYC and TNFRSF21 genes showed significant spatial expression heterogeneity in osteosarcoma tissues (Fig. 10C). The expression of MYC gene was mainly concentrated in the tumor core proliferation region and highly overlapped with the cell cycle-related gene expression region, suggesting that MYC may be involved in driving the uncontrolled proliferation of osteosarcoma cells and maintaining the malignant growth of tumors. TNFRSF21 gene showed a low and focal expression pattern in osteosarcoma tissue, especially in the tumor stroma interface region, suggesting that it may be involved in the weak regulation of cell adhesion or apoptosis related pathways in the primary tumor microenvironment. Notably, there was spatial complementarity in the expression of the two genes at specific tumor regions, possibly reflecting a functional antagonism in the osteosarcoma microenvironment. Notably, there was no significant overlap in spatial distribution between the two, which may reflect the independent regulatory pathways of the two genes in the primary osteosarcoma tissue and their joint participation in shaping the malignant biological characteristics of the tumor.

By analyzing the spatial transcriptome data of the lymphoid tissues surrounding osteosarcoma, MYC and TNFRSF21 genes showed unique spatial expression patterns in the lymphoid tissues surrounding osteosarcoma (Fig. 10D). The expression intensity of MYC gene in the germinal center of lymphoid tissue and the tumor invasion margin showed a gradient increase, especially in the lymphocyte-rich region adjacent to the tumor, suggesting that MYC may promote immune escape in the tumor microenvironment by regulating cell proliferation and metabolism. TNFRSF21 gene is mainly distributed in the stroma of lymphoid tissue, and its expression intensity is positively correlated with the density of stromal cells. Tnfrsf21 gene may participate in tumor-stroma interaction by mediating cell adhesion and apoptosis signals. Notably, the two genes showed a complementary trend of expression in the co-localized regions at the tumor invasion margin, possibly reflecting the inhibitory regulation of TNFRSF21 in Myc-driven proliferationally active regions, thereby weakening the immune surveillance function. These findings reveal the spatial heterogeneous expression characteristics of MYC and TNFRSF21 in the lymphoid tissues surrounding osteosarcoma, and provide a molecular basis for combined therapeutic strategies targeting the tumor immune microenvironment.

Through comparative analysis of necrotizing apoptosis-related genes MYC and TNFRSF21, we observed distinct spatial expression patterns of MYC and TNFRSF21 between the primary osteosarcoma tissue and the OS peritumoral lymphoid tissue (metastatic front). In the primary osteosarcoma tissue section, TNFRSF21 showed low and focal expression in the tumor core and tumor-stroma interface region, while MYC was highly enriched in the tumor proliferative region, with a complementary spatial distribution pattern between the two genes. In the OS peritumoral lymphoid tissue section, MYC expression showed a gradient increase at the tumor invasion margin, while TNFRSF21 expression was significantly decreased in the stromal region of the lymphoid tissue. These exploratory findings from non-paired spatial transcriptome samples suggest that the dysregulated expression of MYC and TNFRSF21 may form a synergistic effect from the primary tumor to the metastatic front, potentially promoting osteosarcoma progression via the modulation of necroptosis and the immunosuppressive microenvironment. This hypothesis requires further validation in paired, large-cohort spatial transcriptome samples and functional experiments.

Results of experimental validation

RT-qPCR validation of MYC and TNFRSF21 expression in osteosarcoma cell lines

To validate the core genes identified through bioinformatics analysis, we first examined the expression levels of MYC and TNFRSF21 in osteosarcoma cell lines with differing metastatic potential using RT-qPCR. The results (Fig. 11A, B) showed that the mRNA expression level of MYC was significantly higher in high-metastatic-potential cell lines (143B and MG63) compared to low-metastatic-potential cell lines (U2OS and Saos-2) (p < 0.05). Conversely, the expression level of TNFRSF21 was significantly lower in high-metastatic-potential cell lines (p < 0.05). These results confirm at the cellular model level that the upregulation of MYC and the downregulation of TNFRSF21 are associated with a high-metastatic phenotype in osteosarcoma.

A Differential expression of MYC; B difference in TNFRSF21 expression. C Volcano plot of differential genes; D the expression levels of MYC and TNFRSF21 genes were different; E,F the expression levels of MYC and TNFRSF21 genes were correlated

Validation of gene expression and association using self-sequenced transcriptomic data

To further validate these findings at the omics level, we performed transcriptome sequencing on the high-metastatic-potential cell line MG63 and the low-metastatic-potential cell line U2OS (Fig. 11C–E). The sequencing data passed quality control. Differential expression analysis (adjusted p value <0.05 and |log2 fold change| ≥ 1) identified a total of 7027 differentially expressed genes. Among these, MYC was significantly upregulated in the MG63 group, while TNFRSF21 was significantly downregulated, consistent with the trends observed in the RT-qPCR analysis and prior public database analyses.

Furthermore, we performed a correlation analysis of the FPKM-normalized expression levels of MYC and TNFRSF21 across all samples (n = 8.4 biological replicates per cell line). The results revealed a strong negative correlation between the two genes (Pearson r = −0.931, 95% CI [−0.982, −0.765], p = 0.001). This negative correlation remained highly significant even in subgroup analyses (within the U2OS group: r = −0.989, 95% CI [−0.999, −0.921], p = 0.001; within the MG63 group: r = −0.988, 95% CI [−0.999, −0.913], p = 0.001). To further validate the correlation between MYC and TNFRSF21 in clinical samples, we performed correlation analyses in the TCGA-OS patient cohort (n = 86). In the full unadjusted analysis, MYC and TNFRSF21 showed a moderate negative correlation (Pearson r = −0.212, 95% CI [−0.408, −0.001], p = 0.049). We further performed partial correlation analysis to adjust for confounding factors including metastasis status, patient age, sex, and clinical stage. After adjustment, the negative correlation between MYC and TNFRSF21 remained statistically significant (partial r = −0.158, 95% CI [−0.392, −0.003], p = 0.047), indicating the association between the two genes is independent of these clinical confounding factors.

Discussion

Osteosarcoma is a highly metastatic primary malignant bone tumor with a dismal prognosis. Early detection of metastatic potential and in-depth dissection of the underlying mechanisms are critical to improving the survival of affected patients. Existing studies have not yet established robust diagnostic models for osteosarcoma metastasis, and the spatiotemporal dynamic role of necroptosis in the metastatic cascade remains poorly characterized. To address these gaps, this study integrates bulk transcriptome, single-cell sequencing, spatial transcriptomics, and machine learning approaches, focusing on constructing a diagnostic model for osteosarcoma metastasis and deciphering the spatiotemporal evolutionary mechanism of necroptosis-related genes in disease progression.

Machine learning-based construction of osteosarcoma metastasis diagnostic model, drug prediction, and molecular docking

Construction of the diagnostic model and prognostic value of core genes

The primary goal of this study was to construct a robust diagnostic model for osteosarcoma metastasis. To mitigate model selection bias and overfitting—key concerns associated with the evaluation of multiple algorithm combinations—we implemented strict methodological safeguards throughout the modeling process. We systematically integrated TCGA and multiple GEO datasets, and evaluated 113 machine learning algorithm combinations for screening and optimization. Nested tenfold cross-validation was applied: the outer fold was used for unbiased model selection, while the inner fold was dedicated to hyperparameter tuning, with strict separation of training, validation, and test sets to eliminate data leakage. All models were evaluated using consistent metrics (AUC, sensitivity, specificity) to avoid selective reporting of optimal results. Finally, the glmBoost+Ridge model was identified as the optimal diagnostic tool, based on its robust generalization performance across datasets.

This model exhibited promising diagnostic performance in the training dataset (AUC = 0.861), effectively distinguishing between metastatic and non-metastatic primary osteosarcoma. More importantly, the model achieved an AUC of 0.873 in the independent external validation set GSE32981, supporting its potential clinical applicability. Although performance in the GSE21257 validation set was more limited (AUC = 0.642), this likely reflects the impact of inter-cohort heterogeneity in clinical characteristics and feature distribution, and highlights the need for further validation in larger, more diverse multicenter cohorts.

The seven core genes (FOS, MYC, GZMA, PMAIP1, TNFRSF21, FBL, FOSB) identified by the model form the cornerstone of the diagnostic framework, and functional enrichment analysis (GO/KEGG) revealed their specific association with osteosarcoma metastasis, rather than generic pan-cancer oncogenic processes. While these genes have been reported in other malignancies, our analysis shows they are predominantly enriched in osteosarcoma-relevant pathways, including osteoclast differentiation, bone matrix remodeling, IL-17 signaling, and MAPK signaling, which are core to the unique metastatic niche of bone tumors. They also participate in key biological processes including cellular stress response, transcriptional regulation, apoptosis, and immune modulation, confirming that the model captures biologically relevant driver genes of osteosarcoma metastasis, not just statistical associations.

We further explored the clinical prognostic value of these core genes. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis showed that only low expression of GZMA among individual core genes was significantly associated with inferior patient survival (p = 0.049). However, the most robust prognostic effect was derived from the synergistic combination of multiple genes: Cox multivariate analysis confirmed that the risk score model constructed by the combination of FBL, MYC, PMAIP1, and TNFRSF21 had independent and significant predictive value for overall survival in our cohort (p = 0.0056). The predictive efficiency of this combined risk score (AUC = 0.79) was substantially higher than that of any single gene, highlighting the advantage of machine learning in identifying synergistic gene combinations for prognostic stratification. This provides a proof-of-concept for a molecular marker-based tool to identify osteosarcoma patients at high risk of metastasis, which, with further validation, may guide more personalized treatment and follow-up strategies.

Drug prediction and molecular docking

Drug screening based on the core gene network directly links our diagnostic markers to potential targeted therapeutic strategies, addressing the unmet clinical need for effective treatments for metastatic osteosarcoma. Molecular docking results revealed distinct binding profiles of candidate agents: Tazemetostat, a clinically approved drug, exhibited strong binding affinity to the key necroptosis receptor TNFRSF21 (−9.4 kcal/mol) and the immune effector gene GZMA (−9.2 kcal/mol), suggesting it may reverse immunosuppression in the metastatic niche by restoring TNFRSF21 function [22]. In contrast, Cisplatin showed weak binding to all core genes (−1.1 to −1.5 kcal/mol), consistent with its known mechanism of broad-spectrum cytotoxicity via non-specific DNA damage, and lack of targeted activity against the pathways identified in this study [23].

Among the predicted repurposing candidates, Calcitriol showed high binding affinity for TNFRSF21 (−8.7 kcal/mol) and the stress-response gene FOS (−8.4 kcal/mol), indicating potential dual therapeutic effects: inhibiting FOS-mediated inflammatory microenvironment (e.g., the COX-2/PGE2 axis) and enhancing TNFRSF21-dependent necroptosis. Of note, while Sulindac showed moderate targeting of FOS (−8.0 kcal/mol, OR = 256.15), which may reduce metastatic burden in combination with chemotherapy, its weak binding to the oncogene MYC (−6.6 kcal/mol) suggests that combination with MYC inhibitors may be required to achieve a synergistic therapeutic effect.

Prognostic role and spatiotemporal evolution of necroptosis-related genes

Based on the core genes screened by the machine learning model, we further focused on necroptosis-related genes, and conducted an in-depth investigation of the prognostic value and dynamic spatiotemporal evolution of the two key genes, MYC and TNFRSF21, in osteosarcoma metastasis.

Prognostic value of MYC and TNFRSF21

In this study, univariate analysis showed that the expression level of MYC or TNFRSF21 alone was not significantly associated with patient survival. However, Cox multivariate analysis confirmed that the combined risk score of MYC and TNFRSF21 had independent and significant predictive value for patient survival (p = 0.008), and the ROC curve showed an AUC of 0.696, demonstrating the clinical predictive value of the synergistic effect of the two genes. This is consistent with previous reports: the NecroScore model developed by Zhong et al. [24] showed that high necroptosis-related gene signatures are closely associated with basal-like differentiation, T cell dysfunction, and high expression of immune checkpoints (such as PD-L1 and CTLA4), serving as important prognostic factors in bone tumors. This further supports the application potential of multi-dimensional necroptosis-related molecular markers in the prognostic stratification of osteosarcoma.

The synergistic prognostic effect of MYC and TNFRSF21 may be attributed to the imbalance between MYC-driven tumor cell proliferation and TNFRSF21-mediated immune microenvironment regulation, which together amplify pro-survival signals in the tumor microenvironment. Recent studies have shown that osteosarcoma metastasis is closely related to M2-type TAM polarization, exosome-mediated immunosuppressive signal transmission, high expression of immune checkpoints, and infiltration of immunosuppressive cells in the tumor microenvironment [25], all of which are core mechanisms of metastatic progression. Our experimental validation further substantiates the clinical relevance of these two genes: RT-qPCR confirmed that MYC expression was significantly elevated, while TNFRSF21 was markedly suppressed, in osteosarcoma cell lines with high metastatic potential (143B, MG63) compared to low-metastatic lines (U2OS, Saos-2). This consistent expression pattern across independent in vitro models underscores their pivotal role in defining the metastatic phenotype of osteosarcoma.

Synergistic mechanism of MYC and TNFRSF21: the “necroptosis-immune exemption” axis

Necroptosis plays a complex bidirectional role in osteosarcoma metastasis. On one hand, DAMPs released during necroptosis can recruit immune cells and induce local inflammatory responses by activating the NLRP3 inflammasome [26]. On the other hand, tumor cells can hijack the necroptosis pathway to remodel the immunosuppressive microenvironment and promote metastasis [27]. Recent studies have pointed out that the immunosuppressive microenvironment in osteosarcoma metastases (characterized by increased infiltration of Tregs and M2 macrophages) is closely related to DAMPs released by tumor cells via necroptosis, which can activate the NLRP3 inflammasome and recruit immunosuppressive cells, forming a vicious cycle of inflammation and immune escape [28].

Functional enrichment analysis in this study provides a molecular basis for this bidirectional effect. KEGG analysis showed that MYC plays a central role in tumor metabolic reprogramming and cell cycle progression, and its high expression is significantly associated with the activation of the core necroptosis pathway (RIPK1/MLKL) [29], which may lead to impaired antigen presentation function of dendritic cells and blocked activation of anti-tumor T cells [30]. Meanwhile, low expression of TNFRSF21 abolishes its inhibitory effect on immune checkpoint pathways such as CXCL12-CXCR4, leading to the expansion of Tregs and depletion of cytotoxic CD8+ T cells. Functional analysis further showed that TNFRSF21 participates in the maintenance of microenvironment homeostasis by negatively regulating B cell proliferation, while DAMPs released by dysregulated necroptosis can induce fibrotic responses and promote tumor cell invasion by enhancing extracellular matrix stiffness. This synergistic effect of immunosuppression and fibrotic microenvironment provides a key adaptive advantage for osteosarcoma lymph node metastasis. In addition, the enrichment of complement pathway genes in the stromal region revealed by spatial transcriptome suggests that tumors may evade immune surveillance by mimicking a chronic inflammatory microenvironment, providing a theoretical basis for combined targeting of the complement system and necroptosis pathway [31].

Our self-generated transcriptome sequencing data provide direct molecular evidence for the interaction between MYC and TNFRSF21. Analysis of MG63 (highly metastatic) versus U2OS (low-metastatic) cells not only reproduced the differential expression pattern of MYC upregulation and TNFRSF21 downregulation, but more importantly, revealed a strong inverse correlation between their expression levels across all samples (Pearson r = −0.931, p = 0.001). This inverse relationship suggests a tightly coordinated regulatory or functional antagonism between MYC and TNFRSF21 during osteosarcoma metastasis. Based on these findings, we propose a “necroptosis-immune exemption” axis as a testable scientific hypothesis to conceptualize the potential coordinated prometastatic role of MYC and TNFRSF21. This hypothesized axis describes a potential dual mechanism: MYC upregulation may drive pronecroptotic and inflammatory signaling to form a pre-metastatic niche, while TNFRSF21 downregulation may simultaneously impair anti-tumor immune surveillance, jointly promoting immunosuppression and metastatic progression. It should be noted that this hypothesis is only supported by expression correlation analysis and multi-omics exploratory findings, with no functional perturbation experiments performed to confirm the causal relationship between this axis and osteosarcoma metastasis. The causal mechanism requires further validation in systematic in vitro and in vivo functional experiments.

Temporal evolution of the necroptosis-related genes during metastasis

Using single-cell sequencing data, we delineated the dynamic expression trajectories of MYC and TNFRSF21 during osteosarcoma metastasis at single-cell resolution for the first time, providing temporal validation for the “necroptosis-immune exemption” axis. The analysis clearly showed that as cells evolve from a non-metastatic to a metastatic state, MYC expression exhibits a significant and continuous dynamic upregulation, which is accompanied by a marked deviation in the cell differentiation trajectory, indicating that high MYC expression is a key molecular event driving cells to acquire a pro-metastatic phenotype. In contrast, TNFRSF21 expression shows a progressive loss during metastatic progression, which is particularly pronounced in the late stage of the metastatic trajectory. This reverse dynamic pattern of MYC upregulation and TNFRSF21 downregulation accurately describes the temporal evolutionary process of the synergistic pro-metastatic effect of the two genes, and further supports their coordinated role in the “necroptosis-immune exemption” axis.

Spatial evolution of the necroptosis-related genes in the metastatic niche

In this study, comparative analysis of the spatial expression profiles of primary osteosarcoma tissues and the peritumoral lymphoid tissues (the metastatic front of osteosarcoma) revealed significant spatial heterogeneity of MYC and TNFRSF21, providing spatial validation for our proposed axis. In primary osteosarcoma tissues, MYC expression is highly enriched in the tumor core proliferation region, and its function is closely related to driving tumor cell metabolic reprogramming and uncontrolled proliferation. TNFRSF21 is expressed at low levels in primary osteosarcoma tissue, scattered in the tumor-stroma interface, with a weak role in regulating cell adhesion and apoptosis homeostasis.

However, at the invasive edge of osteosarcoma in the peritumoral lymphoid tissue, MYC expression is further upregulated and closely associated with metabolic reprogramming, EMT activation, and enrichment of pro-metastatic genes, suggesting that it drives invasive and metastatic growth of tumor cells via the Wnt/β-catenin pathway. Meanwhile, TNFRSF21 is further significantly downregulated in the metastatic microenvironment, and its loss leads to severe impairment of immune regulation and apoptotic signaling in the metastatic niche, forming a typical immunosuppressive “immune-exempt zone”. This spatial complementarity (gradual upregulation of MYC and gradual downregulation of TNFRSF21 from primary osteosarcoma tissue to the metastatic lymphoid tissue) is consistent with the “synergism of metabolism and immune escape” model reported in previous studies [32], and this study is the first to identify the central regulatory role of necroptosis-related genes in the spatial evolution of osteosarcoma metastasis.

Limitations

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged.

First, the single-cell sequencing analysis included only 1 non-metastatic and 1 metastatic primary OS sample, with no biological replicates. The spatial transcriptome analysis was based on non-paired, single-case samples from different sources. All analyses based on these datasets are strictly exploratory and hypothesis-generating, which may not fully capture the inter- and intra-tumor heterogeneity of osteosarcoma, thus limiting the generalizability of our spatiotemporal evolutionary findings of necroptosis-related genes. No definitive mechanistic conclusions are drawn from these analyses, and larger cohorts of single-cell and spatial transcriptome samples with biological replicates are needed to validate our observations in future studies.

Second, although we implemented strict methodological safeguards including nested tenfold cross-validation and independent external validation to minimize overfitting and model selection bias during machine learning model construction, the training and validation cohorts included in this study are retrospective public datasets with relatively small sample sizes. The generalization performance of the glmBoost+Ridge diagnostic model and the combined gene risk score still need to be verified in large-scale, prospective multicenter clinical cohorts. In addition, while we demonstrated the osteosarcoma metastasis-specific enrichment of the seven core genes, their specific functional roles beyond pan-cancer oncogenic regulation need to be further validated by gene perturbation experiments.

Third, our mechanistic conclusions are mainly based on integrated multi-omics bioinformatics analysis and preliminary in vitro cell line validation. The causal relationship between MYC/TNFRSF21 and osteosarcoma metastasis, as well as the functional role of the proposed “necroptosis-immune exemption” axis, lack direct in vivo verification in animal models. Meanwhile, the specific molecular mechanism of the regulatory interaction between MYC and TNFRSF21, and the dynamic crosstalk between the necroptosis pathway and the immunosuppressive microenvironment, have not been fully elucidated.

Finally, the potential therapeutic drugs screened in this study only have theoretical support from molecular docking and database prediction. No in vitro or in vivo pharmacological experiments were performed to validate their actual anti-osteosarcoma metastatic effects. Their specific anti-metastatic mechanism, efficacy, and clinical translational applicability need to be systematically verified in subsequent pharmacological experiments and clinical studies.

Conclusions

By integrating multi-omics data and 113 machine learning algorithms, this study has constructed a robust osteosarcoma metastasis diagnosis model, screened 7 core genes such as FOS and MYC, and suggested that their combined risk scores have predictive value for the prognosis of patients. In addition, Sulindac and Calcitriol are identified as having potential value in drug screening based on core genes. Further focusing on the key genes of necroptosis MYC and TNFRSF21, our analyses suggest that they may drive metastasis through a unique spatiotemporal synergistic mechanism. The temporal analysis revealed that MYC expression was dynamically up-regulated and accompanied by the deviation of cell differentiation trajectory during the metastasis process, while TNFRSF21 showed a progressive loss. The spatial transcriptome showed that MYC was highly expressed in the lymphocyte-rich region at the edge of tumor invasion and co-localized with the pro-metastasis genes, while TNFRSF21 was deleted in the matrix region to form the complement pathway enriched immune exempt region. RT-qPCR experiment provided preliminary validation, showing that MYC was significantly higher and TNFRSF21 was significantly lower in the metastasis group.

This study not only provides a diagnostic tool and prognostic stratification model with potential for osteosarcoma metastasis, but also lays the molecular basis for the combined treatment strategy of targeting necroptosis pathway and immune microenvironment. Collectively, our spatiotemporal analyses propose a novel “necroptosis-immune exemption” axis, which serves as a testable hypothesis and a conceptual framework for understanding osteosarcoma metastasis, meriting further investigation.

CTRP9 attenuates peripheral nerve injury-induced mechanical allodynia and thermal hyperalgesia through regulating spinal microglial polarization and neuroinflammation mediated by AdipoR1 in male mice
Authors: Tianzhu Liu, Longqing Zhang, Wei Mei
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractPeripheral nerve injury triggers rapid microglial activation, promoting M1 polarization within the spinal cord, which exacerbates the progression of neuropathic pain. C1q/TNF-related protein 9 (CTRP9), an adiponectin homolog, is known to suppress macrophage activation and exhibit anti-inflammatory properties through the activation of adiponectin receptor 1 (AdipoR1) in various disease contexts. Nevertheless, the involvement of CTRP9 in microglial polarization in the context of neuropathic pain is still unclear. Our study aimed to how CTRP9 influences spinal microglial polarization, neuroinflammation, and pain hypersensitivity, as well as the underlying mechanism, using a neuropathic pain model in male mice with spared nerve injury (SNI) of sciatic nerve. Our findings revealed SNI elevated the spinal CTRP9 and AdipoR1 levels in microglia. Furthermore, intrathecal administration of recombinant CTRP9 (rCTRP9) substantially weakened mechanical hypersensitivity and heat-related pain response triggered by SNI. On the other hand, rCTRP9 mediated a phenotypic switch in microglia, from the pro-inflammatory M1 state to the anti-inflammatory M2 state, by influencing the spinal AMPK/NF-κB mechanism in SNI mice. Additionally, treatment with AdipoR1 siRNA or an AMPK-specific antagonist both reversed the effects of CTRP9 on the phenotypic switching of spinal microglia and pain hypersensitivity. Collectively, these results indicate that CTRP9 ameliorates mechanical hypersensitivity and heat-related pain response, shifted the balance of microglia towards the anti-inflammatory M2 state, and suppresses neuroinflammatory responses by modulating the AMPK/NF-κB pathway, mediated by AdipoR1 activation, in mice with SNI.Graphical Abstract Keywords: Neuropathic pain, Spinal cord, Neuroinflammation, Microglial polarization, CTRP9, AdipoR1

Introduction

Neuropathic pain affects millions of individuals globally, yet the pathophysiological mechanisms underlying this condition remain not fully elucidated (Kim et al. 2023; Li et al. 2023a; Thouaye and Yalcin 2023). In clinical practice, a paucity of effective therapeutic strategies for neuropathic pain exists (Andre-Obadia et al. 2023; Gilron et al. 2024). Consequently, there is a pressing requirement to discover innovative targets to address and treat this debilitating condition.

Microglia exhibits phenotypic plasticity, adopting either the M1 or M2 phenotype in response to distinct stimuli (Liu et al. 2020; Guo et al. 2024; Li et al. 2024a). Marked by the release of numerous pro-inflammatory mediators, the M1 phenotype worsens inflammation (Liu et al. 2020; Long et al. 2024). In contrast, the M2 phenotype correlates with generating anti-inflammatory factors that aid in the resolution of inflammation (Bi et al. 2023; Yang et al. 2023a). A growing body of research has suggested a role for spinal microglia in the development of neuropathic pain (Wu et al. 2022; Zhang et al. 2022b; Li et al. 2023b). Damage to peripheral nerves trigger rapid microglial activation and promotes M1 polarization in the spinal cord, which, through neuron-microglia interactions, exacerbates neuropathic pain progression (Tsuda et al. 2023; Wu et al. 2023). Our previous work (Zhang et al. 2022b), along with that of others (Wu et al. 2022; Li et al. 2023b) has shown that promoting the transition of microglia toward the M2 phenotype can significantly alleviate neuropathic pain, suggesting that targeting microglial polarization could be a therapeutic strategy.

The C1q/tumor necrosis factor-related proteins (CTRPs), a family of 15 adiponectin-like molecules, exhibits diverse physiological functions (Rezabakhsh et al. 2020). Except for CTRP4, these proteins are characterized by four distinct domains (Sadat-Ebrahimi et al. 2022). Notably, CTRP9 shares the highest sequence homology with adiponectin, particularly within its globular domain, where it exhibits 54% amino acid identity (Rezabakhsh et al. 2020; Sadat-Ebrahimi et al. 2022). Besides its metabolic role, CTRP9, as a secreted glycoprotein, exerts a significant influence on modulating inflammation through adiponectin receptor 1 (AdipoR1) in multiple diseases (Kambara et al. 2015; Zhao et al. 2018; Wang et al. 2023a). For example, CTRP9 mitigates brain edema, improves neurological function, and reduces neuroinflammation in intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) models through the AdipoR1/ AMPK (Zhao et al. 2018). Besides, CTRP9 safeguards against acute cardiac injury by dampening inflammatory responses via an AdipoR1/AMPK-dependent mechanism (Kambara et al. 2015). In vitro studies have shown that CTRP9 shields neurons from oxygen–glucose deprivation/reoxygenation injury by attenuating neuronal apoptosis, oxidative stress, and inflammation through AdipoR1 activation (Wang et al. 2023a). Furthermore, CTRP9 is crucial in macrophage polarization, as evidenced by its ability to ameliorate cardiac dysfunction, curb inflammation, and encourage a shift towards M2 macrophage activation in myocardial infarction models through the AMPK/NF-κB pathway (Liu et al. 2019b). Despite these insights, the specific contribution of CTRP9 to microglial polarization in neuropathic pain awaits further investigation.

Besides contributing to the regulation of metabolic homeostasis, evidence from previous studies suggests that AMPK activation can mitigate neuropathic pain (Zheng et al. 2021; Jiang et al. 2023; Yang et al. 2023b; Zhang et al. 2024a). Our published research has shown that spinal AMPK mediated HSP 22 triggered-the amelioration of neuropathic pain (Zhang et al. 2024a). Furthermore, evidence points to AMPK's involvement in guiding microglial polarization (Han et al. 2021; Xu et al. 2021; Lu et al. 2024). A research has identified that AMPK activation can suppress the NF-κB pathway, promoting M2 polarization of microglia and reducing central sensitization during chronic migraine (Lu et al. 2024). Conversely, CTRP9 has been shown to trigger a decrease in various pro-inflammatory mediators through AMPK activation (Kambara et al. 2015; Zhao et al. 2018; Liu et al. 2019b; Zhang et al. 2019). Despite this, the potential mediation of AMPK in the effects of CTRP9 on microglial polarization and pain hypersensitivity in neuropathic pain models awaits further investigation.

The aim of our research was to assess whether CTRP9 could mitigate mechanical allodynia and thermal hyperalgesia, shift microglial activation towards the M2 phenotype from M1, and weaken neuroinflammatory reactions in mice. Specifically, we explored whether these effects were facilitated by the AdipoR1/AMPK.

Materials and methods

Animals

The C57BL/6 mice (male, 7–8 weeks) were brought in for our study from the Laboratory Animal Center of Tongji Hospital, Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology. Housed under standardized environmental conditions (temperature: 22–25 °C, humidity: 45–65%, and a 12-h light–dark cycle), the animals had unrestricted access to water and were fed a standard laboratory diet.

Neuropathic pain model

In accordance with established methodologies (Gao et al. 2024; Konnova et al. 2024), mice with neuropathic pain were achieved by conducting a spared nerve injury (SNI) procedure on the sciatic nerve. Briefly, under anesthesia with isoflurane (induction: 3% isoflurane; maintenance: 2% isoflurane), a surgical incision in the skin allowed for the visualization of the right sciatic nerve. Following this, the surrounding common peroneal and tibial nerves were carefully separated, tied with 6–0 silk sutures, and cut distally to the secured points. In sham-operated mice, the right sciatic nerve was carefully revealed, avoiding any need for ligating or severing the nerve.

Mechanical allodynia

As detailed in our previous work (Zhang et al. 2024b), paw withdrawal threshold (PWT) was measured using Stoelting's von Frey filaments. Before testing, mice were given a 30-min acclimation period in Plexiglass chambers. In the subsequent testing phase, the underside of the mice's ipsilateral hind paw was probed with a range of von Frey filaments, from 0.02 to 4.0 g in force, employing the up and down paradigm. A rapid reflex of paw retraction or orality directed at the hind paw was scored as an affirmative nociceptive reaction. The calculation of the PWT was performed based on the response pattern and the force of the final filament applied. Data obtained for PWT were subjected to logarithmic transformation for analysis.

Thermal hyperalgesia

Consistent with prior methodology (Wang et al. 2020), the quantification of thermal hyperalgesia was achieved by employing the Hargreaves method. Mice were initially allowed to acclimate for 30 min within a Plexiglass box. Subsequently, the thermal threshold of the ipsilateral hind paw, indicated by paw withdrawal latency (PWL), was quantified using an infrared radiometer. To prevent potential tissue damage, the infrared stimulus was automatically terminated after a maximum exposure time of 30 s.

Western blotting (WB)

Analysis using the WB was performed in line with the previously outlined experimental protocol (Wang et al. 2023b). Briefly, protein extracts were obtained from the ipsilateral L4-L6 spinal sections and quantified. An equal protein load of 40 μg per well was subjected to SDS-PAGE (10%) and electroblotted onto PVDF membranes (Millipore). The membranes were first occupied by 5% BSA for 1.5 h at 25 °C, prior to being soaked with primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C. Following the washing step, the membranes were then exposed to a secondary antibody for 1 h at 25 °C. Blot intensities were analyzed through the use of Bio-Rad's Image Lab program. Immunofluorescence (IF) staining

After mice were rendered unconscious using isoflurane anesthesia, they underwent intracardiac perfusion with chilled phosphate-buffered saline, followed by a solution of 4% paraformaldehyde in the same buffer. Segments of the spine between L4 and L6 were harvested and sectioned into 20-μm-thick slices utilizing a cryogenic microtome from Leica. A blocking step involving a mixture of 5% donkey serum and 0.3% Triton X-100 was performed on the sections for 60 min at a temperature of 25 °C. Post-blocking, incubation of the sections with the primary antibodies was carried out at 4 °C for an extended period overnight. Once the washing step was completed, the sections were then treated with matching secondary antibodies for 60 min at 25 °C. DAPI was used to visualize the nucleus. Fluorescence microscopy with an Olympus BX53 microscope was employed to acquire the images. The count of positively stained cells in the dorsal horn on the side of nerve injury was determined using ImageJ. The antibodies utilized in IF are presented in Table 1.

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)

qRT-PCR was conducted according to previous methodology (He et al. 2023; Zhao et al. 2023). RNA was obtained from segments of the spine between L4 and L6 on the nerve injury side utilizing TRIzol reagent from Vazyme, and subsequently converted into cDNA adhering to the provided protocol. Analysis by qRT-PCR was carried out using a LightCycler 480 sequence detection system (Roche). Gene expression was quantified using the 2−ΔΔCT formula, standardized against β-actin. Data obtained via qRT-PCR were log-transformed for analysis. Primer sequences utilized in this study are presented in Table 2.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

ELISA was carried out following protocol (Li et al. 2024b). Briefly, ipsilateral segments of the spine between L4 and L6 were processed by homogenization followed by centrifugation at 3000 g for 30 min at a temperature of 4 °C. After collection, the supernatant was subjected to analysis using a series of ELISA kits from Boster Biological Technology, following the provided protocols.

Intrathecal injection

Intrathecal injection was administered under brief anesthesia induced by isoflurane (1.5%) to minimize distress to the animals. A 30-gauge, 0.5-inch needle attached to a 10-μl syringe was carefully inserted into the intervertebral space at the L5 or L6 level, approaching at an initial angle of approximately 20° to the vertebral column. The needle was then gently advanced into the space between the spinous and transverse processes. The syringe's angle was subsequently adjusted to about 10°, and the needle was meticulously maneuvered forward into the intervertebral space. A tail flick response confirmed the correct placement of the needle tip within the subarachnoid space.

Drug treatment

PBS was used to dissolve rCTRP9 (Cat# 6537-TN), obtained from Novus Biologicals. A one-off intrathecal administration of rCTRP9 was performed (10, 40, or 100 μg/5 μl) was administrated on day 7 post-SNI. The selected dosages were based on preliminary experiments. To investigate the potential therapeutic effects of repeated rCTRP9 on neuropathic pain, mice received intrathecal injections of rCTRP9 on days 7–11 post-SNI. The AMPK antagonist, Compound C (Cat# HY-13418A, MedChemExpress; CC), was prepared in a solution containing 10% DMSO. CC (25 μg/5 μl) was administered via intrathecal injection to mice on days 7–11 post-SNI. The dosage of CC was determined based on our previously published research (Sun et al. 2022).

Intrathecal siRNA administration

AdipoR1 siRNA (Tsingke Biotechnology) was prepared at a concentration of 2.0 μg/μl in RNase-free water. The intrathecal injection of siRNA was executed following a procedure similar to that previously described (Li et al. 2022). A mixture of AdipoR1 siRNA and a transfection reagent was administered to the mice via intrathecal injection on days 1–6 post-SNI. qRT-PCR examined the efficiency of spinal AdipoR1 gene silencing.

Statistical analysis

Results are depicted as the mean values with their SEM and were processed utilizing GraphPad Prism for statistical analysis. The Shapiro–Wilk test was employed to verify the normal distribution of the data, and the outcomes are detailed in Tables 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11 and 12. Unpaired t-tests addressed pairwise comparisons, and ANOVA (both one-way and two-way) handled multiple group analyses, with subsequent Bonferroni correction. A threshold P-value of 0.05 or lower was set to denote statistical significance.

SNI elevates the level of spinal CTRP9 and AdipoR1

In comparison to sham-operated mice, SNI led to a significant reduction in the PWT (Fig. 1A) and PWL (Fig. 1B) on the ipsilateral hind paw. Then, qRT-PCR, WB, and IF were employed to assess the changes of the ipsilateral spinal CTRP9 and AdipoR1 in mice post-SNI. As depicted in Fig. 1C-G, the levels of CTRP9 and AdipoR1, as determined by qRT-PCR and WB, were markedly elevated after SNI. Besides, SNI elevated the ipsilateral spinal CTRP9 levels in neurons and microglia, as determined by IF staining (Fig. 1H-K). Additionally, SNI also induced an upregulation of the ipsilateral spinal AdipoR1 levels in neurons, astrocytes, and microglia (Fig. 1L-O). The observed outcomes point towards a potential role of spinal CTRP9 and AdipoR1 as critical mediators in modulating neuropathic pain induced by SNI.

SNI up-regulated the expression of CTRP9 and AdipoR1 in the spinal cord of mice. (A-B) In comparison to sham-operated mice, SNI significantly reduced PWT and PWL in the ipsilateral hind paw from days 3 to 14 post-surgery. (C-G) mRNA and protein levels of CTRP9 and AdipoR1 were found to be higher in the ipsilateral spinal cord of SNI mice compared to the sham group. (H–K) IF results revealed that SNI upregulated CTRP9 expression in neurons and microglia within the ipsilateral spinal dorsal horn of mice, in contrast to the sham group. (L-O) Post-SNI, AdipoR1 expression was elevated in neurons, astrocytes, and microglia in the ipsilateral spinal dorsal horn of mice. *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001, ****P < 0.0001, vs. sham group, n = 5

Administration of rCTRP9 induced the analgesic impacts in SNI mice

On day 7 post-SNI, mice received a one-off intrathecal administration of rCTRP9 (10, 40, or 100 μg) to assess the effective analgesic dose. As depicted in Fig. 2A-B, a solitary injection of 40 μg or 100 μg rCTRP9 reversed SNI-triggered decreases in PWT and PWL on the ipsilateral hind paw, with comparable effects at the 3-h post-injection assessment. Consequently, 40 μg of rCTRP9 was selected for subsequent experiments. To assess the changes in microglial polarization at 3 and 6 h following a single administration of rCTRP9 (40 μg), the ipsilateral L4-L6 spinal segments were harvested at these time points. The results indicated that rCTRP9 administration reversed the SNI-induced increases in M1 markers, including CD86 (Fig. 2C) and iNOS (Fig. 2D), as well as the decreases in M2 markers, such as CD206 (Fig. 2E) and Arg1 (Fig. 2F), in the ipsilateral spinal cord at 3 h post-injection. However, these changes were not sustained at 6 h post-injection. To further investigate repeated rCTRP9 injections could alleviate pain hypersensitivity caused by SNI, intrathecal injections of rCTRP9 were administered to mice from days 7 to 11 following-SNI. As illustrated in Fig. 2G-H, repeated administrations of rCTRP9 significantly dampened mechanical hypersensitivity and heat-related pain response triggered by SNI. These findings indicate that CTRP9 plays a crucial role in ameliorating pain responses caused by SNI.

The AMPK/NF-κB mediates the impacts of rCTRP9 on spinal microglia phenotype in mice with SNI

Subsequently, we assessed how CTRP9 might be influencing the spinal AMPK/NF-B in the context of neuropathic pain. Mice received intrathecal injections of rCTRP9 from days 7 to 11 post-SNI, and collection of the ipsilateral spinal sections occurred at 3 h after final administration of rCTRP9. The outcomes revealed that SNI downregulated the ipsilateral spinal levels of p-AMPK, an effect that was reversed by multiple administrations of rCTRP9 (Fig. 3A-B). Conversely, multiple administrations of rCTRP9 also mitigated the SNI-induced increase in the ipsilateral spinal p-p65 levels (Fig. 3C). Moreover, SNI promoted the translocation of the ipsilateral spinal p65-expressing microglia to the nucleus, as determined by IF staining, a response that was attenuated by multiple administrations of rCTRP9 (Fig. 3D-E). Subsequently, the outcomes demonstrated that multiple administrations of rCTRP9 reversed the SNI-induced increases in M1 markers, including CD86 (Fig. 3F-G) and iNOS (Fig. 3H-I), and the decreases in M2 markers, including CD206 (Fig. 3J-K) and Arg1 (Fig. 3L-M). Additionally, IF staining implied that multiple administrations of rCTRP9 reversed SNI-induced the increase in CD86-positive microglia (Fig. 3N-O) and reduce in CD206-positive microglia (Fig. 3P-Q). Our research findings imply that rCTRP9 may be responsible for mediating a phenotypic switch in microglia, from the pro-inflammatory M1 state to the anti-inflammatory M2 state, by influencing the spinal AMPK/NF-κB mechanism in SNI mice.

rCTRP9 contributes to the reduction of spinal neuroinflammation triggered by SNI

In this study, CTRP9's role in modulating the neuroinflammation was evaluated using qRT-PCR and ELISA. Mice received intrathecal injections of rCTRP9 from days 7 to 11 post-SNI, and collection of the ipsilateral spinal sections occurred at 3 h after final administration of rCTRP9. As depicted in Fig. 4A-F, SNI elevated the ipsilateral spinal IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α, but this increase was mitigated by multiple administrations of rCTRP9. Conversely, it was observed that multiple administrations of rCTRP9 reversed the SNI-induced reduction in the ipsilateral spinal IL-10 (Fig. 4G-H). Our study's data point to the possibility that CTRP9 could lessen the spinal inflammatory response in SNI mice.

The amelioration of neuropathic pain caused by rCTRP9 is reversed by AdipoR1 siRNA

To better understand how AdipoR1 influences the pain-relief provided by CTRP9, siRNA targeting AdipoR1 was intrathecally delivered between day 1 and day 6 after SNI surgery. qRT-PCR analysis demonstrated a substantial decrease in the spinal AdipoR1 gene expression in AdipoR1 siRNA-injected mice versus the negative siRNA-injected group (Fig. 5A). Furthermore, AdipoR1 siRNA negated the amelioration of neuropathic pain caused by repeated treatments with rCTRP9 in SNI mice (Fig. 5B-C). This outcome suggests that CTRP9's pain-relief effects are potentially facilitated through AdipoR1. Besides, AdipoR1 siRNA was also utilized to investigate the mediation of AdipoR1 on the rCTRP9-induced modulation of the spinal AMPK/NF-κB pathway. Mice received intrathecal injections of rCTRP9 from days 7 to 11 post-SNI, and collection of the ipsilateral spinal samples occurred at 3 h after final administration of rCTRP9. WB demonstrated that multiple administrations of rCTRP9 increased the ipsilateral spinal levels of p-AMPK and decreased the ipsilateral spinal p-p65, effects that were abrogated by AdipoR1 siRNA (Fig. 5D-F). Additionally, IF staining showed that multiple administrations of rCTRP9 reversed the SNI-induced translocation of the ipsilateral spinal p65-expressing microglia to the nucleus, an effect that was mitigated by AdipoR1 siRNA (Fig. 5G-H).

AdipoR1 siRNA abolishes the rCTRP9-induced alterations in the phenotypic switching of microglia

In this experiment, we utilized AdipoR1 siRNA to investigate its potential role in mediating the effects of rCTRP9 on spinal microglial polarization in a mouse model of SNI. Mice received intrathecal injections of rCTRP9 from days 7 to 11 post-SNI, and collection of the ipsilateral spinal samples occurred at 3 h after final administration of rCTRP9. qRT-PCR and WB analyses indicated that repeated administrations of rCTRP9 reduced the ipsilateral spinal CD86 (Fig. 6A-C) and iNOS (Fig. 6D-E) while increasing the expression of CD206 (Fig. 6F-G) and Arg1 (Fig. 6H-I). However, upon AdipoR1 siRNA administration, the observed effects were nullified. IF staining confirmed that AdipoR1 siRNA counteracted the multiple administrations of rCTRP9-induced decrease in the ipsilateral spinal CD86-positive microglia (Fig. 6J-K) and the increase in CD206-positive microglia (Fig. 6L-M). Collectively, these findings suggest that CTRP9 modulates the AMPK/NF-κB, leading to a reduction in M1 microglial state and an increase in M2 microglial state through spinal AdipoR1 activation in SNI mice.

Discussion

This study established the following: (1) SNI led to enhanced expression of CTRP9 and AdipoR1 among microglial cells in the mice's spinal cord; (2) Treatment with rCTRP9 helped reduce mechanical hypersensitivity and heat-related pain response triggered by SNI; (3) rCTRP9 administration shifted the balance of microglia towards the anti-inflammatory M2 state while suppressing the pro-inflammatory M1 state, and decreased spinal inflammation responses by modulating the spinal AMPK/NF-κB in mice with SNI; (4) Silencing AdipoR1 with siRNA or inhibiting AMPK with CC nullified the pain-relieving and anti-inflammatory impacts of rCTRP9 in SNI mice. In conclusion, CTRP9 ameliorates mechanical hypersensitivity and heat-related pain responses, modulates microglial phenotype, and reduces neuroinflammation through the AMPK/NF-κB pathway, which is mediated by spinal AdipoR1 activation in SNI mice.

CTRPs, including CTRP 1–15, are structurally similar to adiponectin (Rezabakhsh et al. 2020). Except for CTRP4, these proteins have a similar structural composition (Sadat-Ebrahimi et al. 2022). Notably, CTRP9 exhibits the highest amino acid sequence similarity to adiponectin, particularly in the globular domain with 54% identity (Rezabakhsh et al. 2020). Evidence suggests CTRP9 has an active part in diminishing inflammatory responses in diverse settings (Zhang et al. 2016; Li et al. 2019; Liu et al. 2019a). For example, it mitigates diabetic retinopathy by reducing pro-inflammatory cytokines and preserving tight junction proteins in db/db mice (Li et al. 2019). It also alleviates atrial inflammation and fibrosis post-myocardial infarction by inhibiting TLR4/NF‐κB pathway in rats (Liu et al. 2019a). In macrophages, CTRP9 reduces oxLDL-induced inflammation by activating AMPK, suppressing the phosphorylation of NF-κB p65 and nuclear translocation (Zhang et al. 2016). However, the capacity of CTRP9 to modulate pain sensitivity has remained an enigma. Our study found that a solitary injection of 40 μg or 100 μg rCTRP9 reversed SNI-triggered reductions in PWT and PWL, with comparable effects at the 3-h post-injection assessment. Furthermore, repeated injections of rCTRP9 resulted in the mitigation of mechanical hypersensitivity and heat-related pain responses in SNI-induced mice. Collectively, the study indicates that CTRP9 ameliorates SNI-induced mechanical hypersensitivity and heat-related pain responses in mice.

CTRP9, interacting with AdipoR1, plays a multifaceted role in various tissues and organs. Research has shown that CTRP9 reduces brain edema, improves neurological function, and mitigates neuroinflammation by activating the AdipoR1/ AMPK pathway in an ICH model (Zhao et al. 2018). Additionally, CTRP9 has been found to protect against acute cardiac injury by inhibiting inflammatory responses through an AdipoR1/AMPK-dependent mechanism (Kambara et al. 2015). A previous study also highlighted that spinal AdipoR1 expression in microglia increases and that adiponectin is crucial for maintaining normal thermal sensitivity and inducing analgesia by inhibiting neuroinflammation in mice with neuropathic pain (Sun et al. 2018). In line with this (Sun et al. 2018), our study observed that SNI triggered an increase in CTRP9 and AdipoR1 expression in microglia in the corresponding spinal cord region of the mice. Furthermore, we found that silencing AdipoR1 negated the pain-relieving impact of rCTRP9 in SNI mice. The upregulation of CTRP9 and AdipoR1 in the spinal cord may represent a protective mechanism against SNI-induced neuropathic pain, and knocking down CTRP9 or AdipoR1 in mice may aggravate SNI-induced symptoms of mechanical sensitivity and heat-related pain.

Neuropathic pain often arises from peripheral nerve damage, which instigates immediate activation of microglia and their inclination towards the M1 phenotype within the spinal cord—a key factor in pain development (Atta et al. 2023; Gao et al. 2024; Wang et al. 2024). These M1 polarized microglia discharge a plethora of pro-inflammatory cytokines, which in turn, stimulate spinal neurons, exacerbating neuropathic pain through dynamic neuron-microglia crosstalk (Atta et al. 2023). As a result, targeting microglial polarization is now seen as a potential avenue for therapeutic intervention in neuropathic pain conditions (Gao et al. 2024; Wang et al. 2024). Furthermore, it has been demonstrated that CTRP9 mitigates cardiac dysfunction, reduces inflammatory responses, and promotes M2 macrophage polarization through the modulation of AMPK/NF-κB pathway in experimental myocardial infarction (Liu et al. 2019b). However, the specific contribution of CTRP9 to the polarization of microglia in the context of neuropathic pain remains to be determined. This study reveals that intrathecal injection of rCTRP9 shifts the balance of microglia towards the anti-inflammatory M2 state while suppressing the pro-inflammatory M1 state, which results in decreased neuroinflammation via AdipoR1 activation. The results identify that CTRP9 facilitates a transition in microglial cells towards the M2 phenotype, which helps in lessening spinal neuroinflammation in SNI mice.

The AMPK/NF-κB signaling is believed to be involved in the process that dictates the phenotype of microglia (Han et al. 2021; Lu et al. 2024). A study revealed that AMPK activation mitigates chronic migraines by promoting M2 polarization of microglia and inhibiting the NF-κB pathway (Lu et al. 2024). Additionally, research has revealed that mesenchymal stem cell-derived extracellular vesicles enhance AMPK activation, which in turn facilitates M2 microglial polarization by suppressing NF-κB activation in rats with subarachnoid hemorrhage (Han et al. 2021). The regulatory effect of AMPK on microglial polarization is a key determinant in the pathogenesis of neuropathic pain. For instance, naringenin alleviated pain hypersensitivity by enhancing spinal M2 microglia polarization through the spinal AMPK/PGC-1α signaling axis (Ge et al. 2022). Conversely, substantial evidence has demonstrated that CTRP9 modulates inflammatory responses by activating AMPK (Kambara et al. 2015; Zhao et al. 2018; Zhang et al. 2019). However, the specific role of AMPK in mediating CTRP9's effects on pain hypersensitivity and microglial polarization remains unclear. The current investigation demonstrates that treating with rCTRP9 ameliorates symptoms of mechanical sensitivity and heat-related pain, enhances a transition towards the M2 microglial state, and reduces inflammation within the spinal cord by regulating the AMPK/NF-κB pathway in SNI mice. Moreover, our findings indicate that the pharmacological inhibition of AMPK with CC reverses rCTRP9's effects on reducing hypersensitivity to pain, modulating microglial phenotype, and regulating inflammatory cytokine profiles in SNI mice. Nevertheless, this research has its inherent limitations that warrant consideration. The use of genetically modified mice could help to control for genetic variability, potentially providing a clearer understanding of CTRP9's role in microglial polarization within a neuropathic pain model. Additionally, while a previous study has shown that CTRP9 can reduce high glucose-induced trophoblast cell damage by alleviating endoplasmic reticulum stress and oxidative stress (Zhang et al. 2022a), the current study did not investigate the impact of CTRP9 on these stressors in the context of neuropathic pain. Furthermore, the dorsal root ganglion's CTRP9 levels may significantly influence the progression of neuropathic pain, and future studies are needed to unravel the intricate mechanisms involved.

In summary, our findings demonstrate that CTRP9 effectively reduces the mechanical sensitivity and heat-related pain response triggered by SNI. Furthermore, in SNI mice, CTRP9 shifts the balance of microglia towards the anti-inflammatory M2 state while suppressing the pro-inflammatory M1 state, which results in decreased neuroinflammation via the AMPK/NF-κB pathway. While CTRP9 showed promise in influencing microglial polarization and alleviating pain hypersensitivity, these effects were nullified in the presence of AdipoR1 siRNA or when an AMPK inhibitor was employed. The implications of these results are that CTRP9 may be essential in the amelioration of neuropathic pain, and it could emerge as a hopeful candidate for therapeutic strategies aimed at neuropathic pain.

Bowditch representations in Gromov-hyperbolic spaces : characterizations, dynamics of Out ⁡ ( 𝔽 2 ) \mathrm{Out}(\mathbb{F}_{2}) and recognition.
Suzanne Schlich

Abstract
. We study a generalization of the B ​ Q BQ -conditions, introduced by Bowditch and further developed by Tan-Wong-Zhang, for representations of the free group of rank two into isometry groups of Gromov-hyperbolic spaces. We show the existence of an explicit constant K δ K_{\delta} , depending only on the hyperbolicity constant δ \delta of the space, such that the hyperbolicity of the images of primitive elements together with the finiteness of the set of (conjugacy classes of) primitive elements whose images have lengths bounded by K δ K_{\delta} imply a linear growth of the lengths with respect to the word length on primitive elements. We give several characterizations of Bowditch representations, and the framework developed allows us to prove that they form an open domain of discontinuity in the character variety. As a corollary, we also obtain a new characterization of primitive-stable representations, introduced by Minsky. Finally, we explain how our results can be used to obtain finite certificate for the recognition of Bowditch representations.



1.
Introduction

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group and
\mathcal{X}
be a metric space. The group of isometries
\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X})
of
\mathcal{X}
endowed with the compact-open topology is a topological group and acts by conjugation on the space of representations
\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X}))
from
\Gamma
to
\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X})
. On may consider the Hausdorffization of the quotient of
\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X}))
by the action of
\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X})
(that is, its largest Hausdorff quotient). It is commonly referred to as the character variety and denoted
\chi(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X}))
. Character varieties have been extensively studied, from many different points of view including their geometric, dynamical, algebraic (when
G
is an algebraic group) or analytical aspects. Numerous dynamical questions on character varieties still remain widely open. In this paper, we will study some specific subspaces of the character varieties
\chi(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X}))
, where
\mathbb{F}_{2}
is the free group on two generators and
\mathcal{X}
is a
\delta
-hyperbolic space, consisting of representations with good geometric behavior and which will give informations about the dynamic of the outer automorphism group
\mathrm{Out}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
of
\mathbb{F}_{2}
on
\chi(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X}))
.

An important class of representations from
\Gamma
to
\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X})
is the set of
convex-cocompact
representations, which we define in this setting to be the set of representations
\rho
so that the map from
\Gamma
to
\mathcal{X}
which sends
\gamma\in\Gamma
to
\rho(\gamma)x_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
is a quasi-isometric embedding, for
x_{0}
any basepoint in the space
\mathcal{X}
. The finitely generated group
\Gamma
here is endowed with any word metric, and note that this definition depends neither on the choice of the word metric on
\Gamma
nor on the choice of the basepoint
x_{0}
in
\mathcal{X}
. For
\gamma\in\Gamma
, denote
\|\gamma\|
its cyclically reduced word length (for a chosen set of generators), and
l(\rho(\gamma))=\underset{x\in\mathcal{X}}{\inf}d(\rho(\gamma)x,x)
the displacement of
\rho(\gamma)
. Delzant-Guichard-Labourie-Mozes (
[
5
]
) proved that
\rho
is convex-cocompact if and only if there exist two constants
C>0
and
D\geq 0
such that for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
,
l(\rho(\gamma))\geq\frac{1}{C}\|\gamma\|-D
. A fundamental example arises when
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{H}^{2}
and
\Gamma
is the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(S)
of a closed oriented surface
S
with genus at least 2. In this setting, the set of convex-cocompact representations coincide with two copies of the Teichmüller space of
S
in the character varitety
\chi(\pi_{1}(S),\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R}))
. Another rich example appears when
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{H}^{3}
: the set of convex-cocompact representations agrees with the set of quasi-fuchsian representations from
\pi_{1}(S)
to
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})
. The set of (conjugacy classes of) convex-cocompact representations is known to be an open domain of discontinuity for the action of
\mathrm{Out}(\pi_{1}(S))
on the character variety
\chi(\pi_{1}(S),\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X}))
. Whether or not it is the largest remains in general open. In the two examples above, Goldman conjectured that the action is ergodic on the complement of the set of convex-cocompact representations
[
8
]
.

The situation is different when
\Gamma=\mathbb{F}_{2}
and
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{H}^{3}
. Indeed, Bowditch
[
2
]
, followed by Tan-Wong-Zhang
[
21
]
, found a subspace in the character variety
\chi(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C}))

strictly
larger than the set of convex-cocompact representations and which form an open domain of discontinuity. This subspace is defined using some conditions on the images of the primitive element in
\mathbb{F}_{2}
by the representation. We say that
u\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
is
primitive
if there exists a free basis of
\mathbb{F}_{2}
containing
u
. The set of primitive elements in
\mathbb{F}_{2}
is invariant under conjugation and inversion and we denote
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
the set of conjugacy and inversion classes of primitive elements. We denote
[\gamma]
the class of the primitive element
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
in
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
.

Definition 1.1

(Bowditch
[
2
]
, Tan-Wong-Zhang
[
21
]
)
.

Let
\rho:\mathbb{F}_{2}\to\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
be a representation. We say that
\rho
satisfies the
BQ
-conditions
if the two following conditions hold :

•

For all
[\gamma]\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
,
\mathrm{Tr}(\rho(\gamma))\in\mathbb{C}\backslash[-2,2]
.

•

The set
\{[\gamma]\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\;|\;|\mathrm{Tr}(\rho(\gamma))| \leq 2\}
is finite.

Note that since these two conditions are invariant under inversion of the trace, the
BQ
-conditions are well-defined for representations with values in
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})
.

The work of Tan-Wong-Zhang
[
21
]
, generalizing that of Bowditch
[
2
]
, gives a characterization of the
BQ
-conditions above in term of the growth of the logarithm of the traces of the images of primitive elements with respect to the (cyclically reduced) word length. Precisely, they proved that a representation
\rho
satisfies the
BQ
-conditions if and only if there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
[\gamma]\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
,
\log^{+}|\mathrm{Tr}(\rho(\gamma))|:=\max\left\{\log|\mathrm{Tr}(\rho(\gamma))|,0\right\}\geq\frac{1}{C}\|\gamma\|
. They proved that the set of representations satisfying the
BQ
-conditions is open, and that the action of
\mathrm{Out}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
on it is properly discontinuous. Their work makes essential use of the trace formulas which hold in
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
.

1.1.
Characterizations of Bowditch representations in Gromov-hyperbolic spaces

The aim of this paper is to define such a set of representations in the more general setting of Gromov-hyperbolic spaces and to investigate its properties. We will assume that the space is
geodesic
, which means that there exists a geodesic segment between any two distinct points in
\mathcal{X}
and that it is a
visibility space
, which means that there exists a geodesic between any two distinct points in the boundary of
\mathcal{X}
. Since traces of isometries do not exist in this broader context, we will replace them by the
stable-norm

l_{S}(A)
of an isometry
A
of
\mathcal{X}
, defined as follows :
\displaystyle l_{S}(A)=\underset{n\to\mathbb{N}}{\lim}\frac{1}{n}d(A^{n}x_{0},x_{0})
, for
x_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
being any basepoint in
\mathcal{X}
(see section
2.4
for more informations on the stable-norm). Note that in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, the stable-norm and the usual displacement
l(A):=\underset{x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}}{\inf}d(Ax,x)
coincide.
Let us also recall that in
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
, which is the isometry group of the hyperbolic plane
\mathbb{H}^{2}
, the stable-norm and the traces are linked by the following formula :
\mathrm{Tr}(A)=2\cosh(\frac{l_{S}(A)}{2})
, and that in
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})
, we have the following inequality :
\displaystyle e^{\frac{l_{S}(A)}{2}}-1\leq|\mathrm{Tr}(A)|\leq e^{\frac{l_{S}(A)}{2}}+1
. We will work in our setting with the stable-norm which will be more convenient that the displacement. We prove that the following conditions are equivalent :

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a geodesic,
\delta
-hyperbolic, visibility space. Let
K_{\delta}=433\delta
.

Let
\rho:\mathbb{F}_{2}\to\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X})
be a representation.
The following conditions are equivalent :

(BQa)

•

For all
[\gamma]\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
,
\rho(\gamma)
is hyperbolic.

•

The set
\{[\gamma]\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\,|\,l_{S}(\rho(\gamma))\leq K_{\delta})\}
is finite.

(BQb)

•

For all
[\gamma]\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
,
\rho(\gamma)
is hyperbolic.

•

For all
K\geq 0
, the set
\{[\gamma]\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\,|\,l_{S}(\rho(\gamma))\leq K)\}
is finite.

(BQc)

•

For all
K\geq K_{\delta}
, the tree
T_{\rho}(K)
(defined in section
7.1
) is finite.

(BQd)

•

There exists
C>0
and
D\geq 0
such that for all
[\gamma]\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
,
l_{S}(\rho(\gamma))\geq\frac{1}{C}\|\gamma\|-D
.

When one (hence all) of these conditions is satisfied, we say that
\rho
is a
Bowditch representation
.

Remark 1.2
.

In particular, this theorem covers the case where

is a rank one symmetric space : the real hyperbolic

Lawton-Maloni-Palesi studied in

Using an explicit parametrization of the character variety

When

1.2.
Dynamics of the outer automorphism group

Let us recall that the
outer automorphism group

\mathrm{Out}(\Gamma)
of a group
\Gamma
is the quotient of the automorphism group
\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
of
\Gamma
by the set of inner automorphisms of
\Gamma
. There is a natural action of
\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
on the space of representations
\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X}))
by precomposition : for
\rho\in\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X}))
and
\Phi\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
, we set
\Phi.\rho=\rho\circ\Phi^{-1}
. This action induces an action of the outer automorphism group
\mathrm{Out}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
on the character variety
\chi(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X}))
. We say that the action of a group
\Gamma
on a topological space
\mathcal{T}
is
properly discontinuous
if for every compact subset
K
of
\mathcal{T}
,
the set
\left\{\Phi\in\Gamma\;|\;\Phi(K)\cap K\neq\emptyset\right\}
is finite. The techniques developed in this article allow us to study the action of the outer automorphism group
\mathrm{Out}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
of
\mathbb{F}_{2}
on the set of Bowditch representations.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a geodesic,
\delta
-hyperbolic, visibility space. Let
K_{\delta}=433\delta
.
The set of (conjugacy classes of) Bowditch representations is open in the character variety
\chi(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X}))
and the action of the outer automorphism group
\mathrm{Out}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
is properly discontinuous on it.

Remark 1.5
.

It is not hard to see that the set of primitive-stable representations is open and that the action of the outer automorphism group on it is properly discontinuous. Minsky
[
13
]
proved it for
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})
-representations but no difficulty is added when passing to
\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X})
for
\mathcal{X}
Gromov-hyperbolic (see also Proposition 4.5 in
[
17
]
for a proof of the openness). Therefore, we could also obtain Theorem
1.4
using Corollary
1.3
. However, we will explain how the framework developed for proving Theorem
1.1
can be used to obtain a direct proof of this result which does not rely on the equivalence with primitive-stable representations.

1.3.
Recognition

Finally, we will discuss how our work enables us to obtain finite certificates for Bowditch representations. More precisely, we will write an explicite algorithm which ends in finite time if
\rho
is a Bowditch representation.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a geodesic,
\delta
-hyperbolic, visibility space. Let
\rho:\mathbb{F}_{2}\to\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X})
be a representation. Then, there exists an algorithm, described in Algorithm
3
in section
7.3
, such that

•

If
\rho
is a Bowditch representation, then the algorithm ends in finite time.

•

If the algorithm ends in finite time, then it decides whether
\rho
is a Bowditch representation.

1.4.
An ingredient of the proof

Part of this work is very much inspired by the work of Bowditch
[
2
]
and its generalization by Tan-Wong-Zhang
[
21
]
. However, their work in

\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})
crucially relies on the existence of trace relations (as in Lawton-Maloni-Palesi’s work in
\mathrm{SU}(2,1)

[
11
]
), and in particular on the two relations :

(1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(AB)+\mathrm{Tr}(AB^{-1})=\mathrm{Tr}(A)\mathrm{Tr}(B)

(2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(A)^{2}+\mathrm{Tr}(B)^{2}+\mathrm{Tr}(AB)^{2}=\mathrm{Tr}(A)\mathrm{Tr}(B)\mathrm{Tr}(AB)+\mathrm{Tr}([A,B])+2.

These two equalities, which can be interpreted as an
edge relation
and a
vertex relation
in their framework, are extensively used in their papers. In order to prove Theorem
1.1
without such relations, we will need to replace them by new large-scale geometry arguments.
In particular, we will prove the following inequality on the length of a product of two isometries.

Proposition 1.7

(Proposition
4.1
in section
4
)
.

Let
C_{\delta}=432\delta
.

Let
A
and
B
be two hyperbolic isometries such that
l_{S}(A)>C_{\delta}
and
l_{S}(B)>C_{\delta}
. Then

(3)

\max\{l_{S}(AB),l_{S}(AB^{-1})\}\geq l_{S}(A)+l_{S}(B)-C_{\delta}.

Note that in
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
, Proposition
1.7
follows from equation (
1
). Indeed, from (
1
) :

\displaystyle|\mathrm{Tr}(A)|\;|\mathrm{Tr}(B)|=|\mathrm{Tr}(AB)+\mathrm{Tr}(AB^{-1})|

\displaystyle\leq|\mathrm{Tr}(AB)|+|\mathrm{Tr}(AB^{-1})|\leq 2\max\{|\mathrm{Tr}(AB)|,|\mathrm{Tr}(AB^{-1})|\},

and now taking the
\log
gives :

\displaystyle\max\{\log|\mathrm{Tr}(AB)|,\log|\mathrm{Tr}(AB^{-1})|\}

\displaystyle\geq\log|\mathrm{Tr}(A)|+\log|\mathrm{Tr}(B)|-\log 2.

Now recall that
\log|\mathrm{Tr}(A)|
and
l_{S}(A)
are comparable in
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
(up to an additive constant) to obtain the claim. Also note that if one is interested in the study of the
\log
of the modulus of the trace rather than on the stable-norm
l_{S}
(or displacement), then Proposition
1.7
holds with constant
\log 2
and for all hyperbolic isometries, without having to assume that the modulus of the trace is large enough.

2.
Preliminaries on hyperbolic spaces

In this section, we gather the required material on
\delta
-hyperbolic spaces needed for our work. The content of this section is classical. For a more detailed introduction or further results on
\delta
-hyperbolic spaces, we refer the interested reader, among other references, to
[
4
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
6
]
.

In this article, we will work in the context of geodesic
\delta
-hyperbolic spaces. Let
(\mathcal{X},d)
be a metric space. The metric space
\mathcal{X}
is said to be
geodesic
if there exists a geodesic segment between any two points in
\mathcal{X}
. We will write
[x,y]
for a geodesic segment joining the points
x
and
y
in
\mathcal{X}
. Note that such a segment is not necessary unique. For
x
,
y
and
z
three points in
\mathcal{X}
, a
triangle

T(x,y,z)
with vertices
x
,
y
and
z
is the union of three geodesic segments
[x,y]
,
[y,z]
and
[z,x]
which are called the sides of the triangle. Let
\delta\geq 0
be a constant. We say that a triangle is
\delta
-thin
if each side of the triangle is contained in the
\delta
-neighborhood of the union of the other two. A geodesic metric space
\mathcal{X}
is said to be
\delta
-hyperbolic
if every triangle in
\mathcal{X}
is
\delta
-thin. This definition of hyperbolicity is sometimes called
hyperbolicity in the sense of Rips
. There are (many) other equivalent definitions of hyperbolicity, among them the notion of
hyperbolicity in the sense of Gromov
, which we will define and compare to the one in the sense of Rips in Lemma
2.18
.

In all that follows, we fix
(\mathcal{X},d)
a geodesic
\delta
-hyperbolic space.

2.1.
Shapes of triangles and quadrilaterals

We can be more precise about the geometry of
\delta
-thin triangles. It is useful to compare the triangle
T(x,y,z)
with its associated tripod, which is the tripod with the same lengths of sides as
T(x,y,z)
. Lemma
2.1
finds three points, one on each side of the triangle, which correspond to the center of the associated tripod and which remain at bounded distance from each other.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
T(x,y,z)
be a
\delta
-thin triangle in
\mathcal{X}
. Then there exist three points
p
,
q
and
r
respectively on
[x,y]
,
[y,z]
and
[z,x]
such that
d(x,p)=d(x,r)
,
d(y,p)=d(y,q)
,
d(z,q)=d(z,r)
and
d(r,p)\leq 4\delta
,
d(p,q)\leq 4\delta
,
d(q,r)\leq 4\delta
.

Proof.

See proof of implication (1)
\implies
(3) in Proposition 1.17 in
[
3
]
. The statement of the Proposition just gives the existence of a bound on
d(r,p)
,
d(p,q)
and
d(q,r)
but the proof computes the constant
4\delta
explicitly.
∎

For
x
,
y
,
z
and
w
four points in
\mathcal{X}
, a
quadrilateral

Q(x,y,z,w)
is the union of four geodesic segments
[x,y]
,
[y,z]
,
[z,w]
and
[w,x]
, which are called the
sides
of the quadrilateral. The geometry of quadrilateral is also controlled. Indeed, it is a classical fact that in
\delta
-hyperbolic spaces, quadrilaterals are
2\delta
-thin.

Lemma 2.2

(Quadrilaterals are
2\delta
-thin.)
.

Let
x,y,z,w
be four points in
\mathcal{X}
and
Q(x,y,z,w)
be a quadrilateral. Then the quadrilateral
Q(x,y,z,w)
is
2\delta
-thin, which means that
[w,x]
is contained in the
2\delta
-neighborhood of
[x,y]\cup[y,z]\cup[z,w]
.

Proof.

See Lemma 11.6 in
[
6
]
or Lemma 1.5 in
[
4
]
for a more general statement for polygons.
∎

From the
2\delta
-thinness of quadrilaterals, we easily deduce, in the configuration where one side of the quadrilateral remains far from its opposite side, that we can find a point on it that is close to a point on each of its two adjacent sides.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
x,y,z,w
be four points in
\mathcal{X}
and
[x,y]
,
[y,z]
,
[z,w]
and
[w,x]
be four geodesic segments between these points. Assume moreover that for all
t\in[w,x]
,
d(t,[y,z])>2\delta
. Then there exist
t\in[w,x]
,
t_{1}\in[x,y]
and
t_{2}\in[z,w]
such that
d(t,t_{1})\leq 2\delta
and
d(t,t_{2})\leq 2\delta
.

Proof.

It is a consequence of Lemma
2.2
. For a detailed proof, see Lemma A.3 in
[
17
]
.
∎

2.2.
Gromov-boundary and shape of ideal-bigons and asymptotic geodesics

Let
x
,
y
and
z
be three points in
\mathcal{X}
. The
Gromov-product
of
x
and
y
with respect to
z
is defined by :

(4)

(x|y)_{z}=\frac{1}{2}(d(z,x)+d(z,y)-d(x,y)).

We say that a sequence of points
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

converges at infinity
if
(x_{n}|x_{m})_{z}\underset{n,m\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}+\infty
. This notion does not depend on the choice of
z
and notice that this implies that
d(x_{n},z)\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}+\infty
. When two sequences
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converge at infinity, we say that they are
equivalent
when
(x_{n}|y_{n})\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}+\infty
. The equivalence relation between sequences of points gives rise to the notion of the Gromov-boundary
\partial\mathcal{X}
of
\mathcal{X}
by considering the equivalence classes of this relation. When
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence in
\mathcal{X}
and
x\in\partial\mathcal{X}
, we denote
x_{n}\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}x
to say that
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is in the equivalent class
x\in\partial\mathcal{X}
.

When
l:[0,+\infty)\to\mathcal{X}
is a geodesic ray in
\mathcal{X}
(we will denote again, by a slight abuse of notation, its image by
l
) and
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is an unbounded sequence on
l
, then
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
automatically converges at infinity. Moreover, any two unbounded sequences on
l
are equivalent, giving rise to a well-defined point at infinity in
\partial\mathcal{X}
, which we call the
endpoint at infinity
of
l
. If
x\in\mathcal{X}
and
l_{\infty}\in\partial\mathcal{X}
, we say that
l
is a geodesic ray or a
half geodesic
with endpoints
x
and
l_{\infty}
or joining
x
and
l_{\infty}
if
l_{\infty}
is the endpoint of
l
and
l(0)=x
, and we denote
[x,l_{\infty})
. When two geodesic rays have the same endpoint, we say that they are
asymptotic
. Note that two asymptotic geodesic rays remain at bounded distance from each other. If
l:\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{X}
is a bi-infinite geodesic, it has two well-defined distinct points in the boundary
\partial\mathcal{X}
of
\mathcal{X}
, which we also call the
endpoints
of
l
.

Let
l
be either a geodesic, a geodesic ray, or a geodesic segment, and
x
be a point in
\mathcal{X}
. We say that
x^{\prime}
is a
projection
of
x
on
l
if
d(x,x^{\prime})=d(x,l)
. The projection is not necessarily unique. We will say more on projections in
\delta
-hyperbolic spaces in section
2.3
.

The following lemma states that geodesics with the same endpoints are uniformly close. It is an exercise in
[
6
]
. For completeness, we include a detailed proof here.

Lemma 2.4

(Ideal bigons are
2\delta
-thin,
[
6
]
Exercise 11.85)
.

Let
l
and
l^{\prime}
be two geodesic of
\mathcal{X}
which share the same endpoints. Then, for all
x\in l
,

d(x,l^{\prime})\leq 2\delta.

Proof.

Let
x\in l
. We consider, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the points
x_{n}
and
y_{n}
on
l
such that, denoting
[x_{n},y_{n}]\subset l
the geodesic segment from
x_{n}
to
y_{n}
included in
l
,
x\in[x_{n},y_{n}]
and
d(x_{n},x)=d(x,y_{n})=n
. Now consider
x^{\prime}_{n}
and
y^{\prime}_{n}
some projections of
x_{n}
and
y_{n}
on
l^{\prime}
. Consider
[x^{\prime}_{n},y^{\prime}_{n}]\subset l^{\prime}
the geodesic segment from
x^{\prime}_{n}
to
y^{\prime}_{n}
included in
l^{\prime}
,
[x_{n},x^{\prime}_{n}]
a geodesic segment between
x_{n}
and
x^{\prime}_{n}
and
[y_{n},y^{\prime}_{n}]
a geodesic segment between
y_{n}
and
y^{\prime}_{n}
. The quadrilateral made up with these four geodesic segments is
2\delta
-thin by Lemma
2.2
, so we deduce that
d(x,[x_{n},x^{\prime}_{n}]\cup[x^{\prime}_{n},y^{\prime}_{n}]\cup[y_{n},y^{\prime}_{n}])\leq 2\delta
. But recall that
l
and
l^{\prime}
have the same endpoints and that
x^{\prime}_{n}
is a projection of
x_{n}
on
l^{\prime}
, so
d(x_{n},x^{\prime}_{n})
is bounded. Therefore, using that
d(x,x_{n})\to\infty
, we obtain that
d(x,[x_{n},x^{\prime}_{n}])\to\infty
. By the same reasoning, we also deduce that
d(x,[y_{n},y^{\prime}_{n}])\to\infty
. Then, for
n
sufficiently large, we have
d(x,[x_{n},x^{\prime}_{n}]\cup[y_{n},y^{\prime}_{n}])>2\delta
. Then it follows that
d(x,[x^{\prime}_{n},y^{\prime}_{n}])\leq 2\delta
and since
[x^{\prime}_{n},y^{\prime}_{n}]\subset l^{\prime}
, we deduce
d(x,l^{\prime})\leq 2\delta
, which proves the lemma.
∎

Using similar techniques, we can also prove that geodesics sharing one endpoint are uniformly close, sufficiently close to the common endpoint.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
l
,
l^{\prime}
be two geodesics of
\mathcal{X}
that share one endpoint
l_{\infty}\in\partial\mathcal{X}
. Then there exists
x\in l
such that, denoting
[x,l_{\infty})
the half geodesic contained in
l
with endpoints
x\in\mathcal{X}
and
l_{\infty}\in\partial\mathcal{X}

y\in[x,l_{\infty})
:

d(y,l^{\prime})\leq 2\delta.

Proof.

Let
x_{0}\in l
. Denote
[x_{0},l_{\infty})
the half-geodesic contained in
l
with endpoints
x_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
and
l_{\infty}\in\partial\mathcal{X}
. For all
t>0
, let
y_{t}\in[x_{0},l_{\infty})
be the point on the half-geodesic
[x_{0},l_{\infty})
such that
d(x_{0},y_{t})=2t
, and let
x_{t}
be the midpoint of
[x_{0},y_{t}]
, where
[x_{0},y_{t}]
denotes the geodesic segment contained in
l
with endpoints
x_{0}
and
y_{t}
. Hence
d(x_{0},x_{t})=d(x_{t},y_{t})=t
. Let
x^{\prime}_{0}
and
y^{\prime}_{t}
be, respectively, some projections on the geodesic
l^{\prime}
of
x_{0}
and
y_{t}
. Now consider
[x_{0},x^{\prime}_{0}]
and
[y_{t},y^{\prime}_{t}]
be, respectively, some geodesic segments between
x_{0}
and
x^{\prime}_{0}
and
y_{t}
and
y^{\prime}_{t}
. By Lemma
2.2
, the quadrilateral formed by these four geodesic segments is

-thin, so
d(x_{t},[x_{0},x^{\prime}_{0}]\cup[x^{\prime}_{0},y^{\prime}_{t}]\cup[y_{t},y^{\prime}_{t}])\leq 2\delta
. Now note that since
d(x_{t},x_{0})=t\underset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
, we also have
d(x_{t},[x_{0},x^{\prime}_{0}])\underset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
. On the other hand, since
y_{t}\underset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}l_{\infty}
and

is an endpoint of both

and
l^{\prime}
, we deduce that
d(y_{t},y^{\prime}_{t})
is bounded. Therefore, using
d(x_{t},y_{t})=t\underset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
, we also have
d(x_{t},[y_{t},y^{\prime}_{t}])\underset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
. Thus, we necessarily have
d(x_{t},[x^{\prime}_{0},y^{\prime}_{t}])\leq 2\delta
when

for some

, and consequently,

gives the Lemma.
∎

Let us be a bit more precise and give some conditions to ensure that a point on a geodesic ray is sufficiently far along the ray to be uniformly close to another asymptotic geodesic ray.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
l
be a geodesic in
\mathcal{X}
and
y_{0}\in l
. Denote
l_{\infty}
one of the endpoints of
l
in
\partial\mathcal{X}
and
[y_{0},l_{\infty})
the half-geodesic contained in
l
with endpoints
y_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
and
l_{\infty}\in\partial\mathcal{X}
. Let
x_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
and consider
[x_{0},l_{\infty})
a half-geodesic from
x_{0}
to
l_{\infty}
. Let
x\in[x_{0},l_{\infty})
. Then :

•

If
d(x,y_{0})>d(y_{0},x_{0})+2\delta
, then there exists
y\in[y_{0},l_{\infty})
such that
d(x,y)\leq 2\delta
.

•

Assume moreover that
d(y_{0},x)>6\delta
, then any projection of
x
on
l
belongs to
[y_{0},l_{\infty})
.

Proof.

Since the geodesic rays
[x_{0},l_{\infty})
and
[y_{0},l_{\infty})
are asymptotic, there exist two sequences
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
x_{n}\in[x_{0},l_{\infty})
and
y_{n}\in[y_{0},l_{\infty})
for all

,
d(x_{n},x)\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}+\infty
and

is bounded. Let
[x_{n},y_{n}]
be a geodesic segment joining
x_{n}
and
y_{n}
. Then
d(x,[x_{n},y_{n}])\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}+\infty
. Take

d(x,[x_{n},y_{n}])>2\delta
and
x_{n}\in[x,l_{\infty})
, where
[x,l_{\infty})
is the half-geodesic contained in
[x_{0},l_{\infty})
joining
x
and
l_{\infty}
. Now consider
[x_{0},y_{0}]
a geodesic segment joining
x_{0}
and
y_{0}
and let
z_{0}\in[x_{0},y_{0}]
. By the triangle inequality we have
d(x,z)\geq d(x,y_{0})-d(y_{0},x_{0})
and so using our asumption on

we deduce that
d(x,[x_{0},y_{0}])>2\delta
. Consider
[x_{0},x_{n}]
and

and

and joining respectively
x_{0}
and

and
y_{0}
and

and

Now, let us assume, moreover, that

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
l
and

be two geodesics in
\mathcal{X}
which share one endpoint

. Let

. Then there exists a geodesic parametrization of

such that for all

d(l(t),l^{\prime}(t))\leq 6\delta
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.5
, there exists
x\in l
such that, denoting
[x,l_{\infty})
the geodesic ray contained in
l
with endpoints
x\in\mathcal{X}
and
l_{\infty}\in\partial\mathcal{X}
, we have for all
y\in[x,l_{\infty})
,
d(y,l^{\prime})\leq 2\delta
. Let
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
be such that

and let

be a projection of
x
on
l^{\prime}
. Now consider
l^{\prime}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{X}
the geodesic parametrization of

such that

and
l^{\prime}(t)\underset{t\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}l_{\infty}
. Let

be a projection map on

. For all

, we have, on the one hand :

\displaystyle d(x^{\prime},p(l(t)))

\displaystyle\leq d(x^{\prime},x)+d(x,l(t))+d(l(t),p(l(t)))\text{ by the triangle inequality}

\displaystyle\leq 2\delta+d(l(t_{0}),l(t))+2\delta\text{ since $l(t)\in[x,l_{\infty})$}

\displaystyle\leq 4\delta+d(l^{\prime}(t_{0}),l^{\prime}(t))=4\delta+d(x^{\prime},l^{\prime}(t)).

On the other hand, we have :

\displaystyle d(x^{\prime},l^{\prime}(t))-d(x^{\prime},p(l(t)))

\displaystyle\leq d(l^{\prime}(t_{0}),l^{\prime}(t))-d(x,l(t))+d(x,x^{\prime})+d(l(t),p(l(t)))

\displaystyle\leq d(l^{\prime}(t_{0}),l^{\prime}(t))-d(l(t_{0}),l(t))+4\delta

Therefore, we have

(5)

|d(x^{\prime},p(l(t)))-d(x^{\prime},l^{\prime}(t))|\leq 4\delta

, we deduce that for

. Therefore

\displaystyle d(p(l(t)),l^{\prime}(t))\leq|d(x^{\prime},p(l(t)))-d(x^{\prime},l^{\prime}(t))|\leq 4\delta\text{ by \eqref{eq: |d(x',p(l(t)))-d(x',l'(t))}}

2.3.
Projections

We now give some results about projections in
\delta
-hyperbolic spaces.

We first check the following :

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
l
be a geodesic in
\mathcal{X}
and
x\in\mathcal{X}
. Consider
z
a projection of
x
on the geodesic
l
. Let
[x,z]
be a geodesic segment between
x
and
z
and
y\in[x,z]
. Then
z
is also a projection of
y
on
l
.

Proof.

Let
t\in l
. We have
d(y,z)=d(x,z)-d(x,y)\leq d(x,t)-d(x,y)
since
z
is a projection of
x
on
l
and then, by the triangle inequality,
d(y,z)\leq d(y,t)
, which proves the Lemma.
∎

The following Lemma studies triangles with a “right angle” and states that the triangle inequality corresponding to this right angle is coarsely an equality in this case.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
l
be a geodesic in
\mathcal{X}
,
x\in\mathcal{X}
and
y\in l
. Consider
z
a projection of
x
on the geodesic

. Then
d(x,y)\geq d(x,z)+d(z,y)-12\delta

Proof.

Consider
[x,z]
a geodesic segment between
x
and
z
,
[x,y]
a geodesic segment between
x
and
y
, and
[y,z]
the geodesic segment between
y
and

contained in

. The triangle made with these three geodesic segments is
\delta
-thin since
\mathcal{X}
is
\delta
-hyperbolic and thus consider
p\in[x,z]
,

and

given by Lemma

. Then, using the properties on

(7)

\displaystyle d(x,y)=d(x,r)+d(r,y)=d(x,p)+d(q,y)=d(x,z)-d(p,z)+d(z,y)-d(z,q).

Moreover, since

Using this last bound and the bound on
d(p,q)
, we can now also bound
d(z,q)
:

(9)

d(z,q)\leq d(z,p)+d(p,q)\leq 4\delta+4\delta=8\delta.

The lemma then follows from combining (
7
), (
8
) and (

).
∎

If
l
is a geodesic, we say that a map
p:\mathcal{X}\to l
is a
projection
on
l
if
p(x)
is a projection on
l
for all

. Such a map is not necessarily unique nor continuous. However, the next lemma asserts that it is always coarsely

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
l
be a geodesic in
\mathcal{X}
and
p:\mathcal{X}\to l
a projection on
l
. For all

, we have :

(10)

d(p(x),p(y))\leq\max(8\delta,12\delta+d(x,y)-d(x,p(x))-d(y,p(y))).

In particular :

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of Lemma A.5 in

]
.

2.4.
Hyperbolic isometries and stable norm

Let
A
be an isometry of
\mathcal{X}
. By invariance of the Gromov-product under isometries, two sequences
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of points in
\mathcal{X}
are equivalent if and only if the two sequences
(Ax_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(Ay_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are, and therefore we obtain a well-defined action of the isometry
A
on
\partial\mathcal{X}
.

Let
x\in\mathcal{X}
. An isometry
A
of
\mathcal{X}
is said to be
hyperbolic
if the sequence
(A^{n}x)_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a quasi-geodesic in
\mathcal{X}
(this notion does not depend on the choice of the basepoint
x
). In this case,
A
has exactly two fixpoints in
\partial\mathcal{X}
which are the equivalent classes of
(A^{n}x)_{n\geq 0}
and
(A^{n}x)_{n\leq 0}
. These two (distinct) points are respectively called the
attracting
and
repelling
fixpoints of
A
and respectively denoted
A^{+}
and
A^{-}
. If
L_{A}
is a geodesic with endpoints
A^{+}
and
A^{-}
, it is not necessarily
A
-invariant, but it is close to be. Indeed, as a consequence of Lemma
2.4
, we obtain :

Corollary 2.11

(Corollary of Lemma
2.4
)
.

Let
A
be a hyperbolic isometry of
\mathcal{X}
. Let
L_{A}
be an infinite geodesic with endpoints
A^{+}
and
A^{-}
and
x\in L_{A}
, then

d(Ax,L_{A})\leq 2\delta.

Proof.

Observe that
A(L_{A})
is a geodesic with endpoints
A(A^{+})=A^{+}
and
A(A^{-})=A^{-}
and that
Ax\in A(L_{A})
. Lemma
2.4
then gives
d(Ax,L_{A})\leq 2\delta
.
∎

We define the
stable norm
of an isometry
A
as :

(12)

l_{S}(A)=\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}\frac{1}{n}d(A^{n}x,x).

One can check that
l_{S}(A)
is well-defined, do not depend on the choice of
x
, and is invariant under conjugation and inversion of
A
. Moreover, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
d(A^{n}x,x)\geq nl_{S}(A)
. As a consequence, the hyperbolicity of an isometry can be characterized using the stable-norm : an isometry
A
is hyperbolic if and only if
l_{S}(A)>0
.

We also introduce the
displacement
of an isometry
A
:

(13)

l(A)=\underset{x\in\mathcal{X}}{\inf}\,d(Ax,x).

We can easily check that
l_{S}(A)\leq l(A)
. The converse is coarsely true :

Lemma 2.12

(Proposition 6.4 in
[
4
]
)
.

l_{S}(A)\leq l(A)\leq l_{S}(A)+16\delta
.

Therefore, these two quantities can be interchanged in our results up to the addition of an additive constant depending on
\delta
. In the following, we will work with the stable-norm which will be more convenient.

When
A
is a hyperbolic isometry and
L_{A}
an
A
-invariant geodesic with endpoints
A^{+}
and
A^{-}
, then it is not hard to see that for all
x\in L_{A}
, its image
Ax\in[x,A^{+})
, where
[x,A^{+})
denotes the geodesic ray with endpoints
x
and
A^{+}
. If
L_{A}
is not assumed to be invariant anymore, we prove that the projection of
Ax
still satisfies this property assuming that the stable norm is large enough.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
A
be a hyperbolic isometry of
\mathcal{X}
. Let
L_{A}
be a geodesic between
A^{-}
and
A^{+}
and
x\in L_{A}
. Let
p(Ax)
be a projection of
Ax
on
L_{A}
.
If
l_{S}(A)>6\delta
, then
p(Ax)\in[x,A^{+})
.

Proof.

Let us define the following sequence of points on
L_{A}
by induction :

\displaystyle p_{0}=x

\displaystyle p_{n+1}\text{ is a projection of $Ap_{n}$ on $L_{A}$}.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the point
p_{n}
belongs to
L_{A}
, so by Corollary
2.11
,

(14)

d(Ap_{n},p_{n+1})\leq 2\delta.

Now let us prove that
d(p_{n},A^{n}x)\leq 2n\delta
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
by induction on
n
.

•

If
n=0
,
d(p_{0},A^{0}x)=d(x,x)=0
.

•

Suppose
d(p_{n},A^{n}x)\leq 2n\delta
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\displaystyle d(p_{n+1},A^{n+1}x)

\displaystyle\leq d(p_{n+1},Ap_{n})+d(Ap_{n},A^{n+1}x)

\displaystyle\leq 2\delta+d(p_{n},A^{n}x)\text{ by \eqref{eq:d(Apn,p_{n+1})<2d}}

\displaystyle\leq(n+1)2\delta.

We can now show that the sequences
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(A^{n}x)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are equivalent :

\displaystyle(A^{n}x|p_{n})_{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(d(x,A^{n}x)+d(x,p_{n})-d(A^{n}x,p_{n}))

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}(nl_{S}(A)-2n\delta)=\frac{n}{2}(l_{S}(A)-2\delta).

Since
l_{S}(A)>2\delta
, the Gromov product
(A^{n}x|p_{n})_{x}\underset{n\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}+\infty
. As a consequence,
p_{n}\underset{n\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}A^{+}
using that
A^{n}x\underset{n\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}A^{+}
since
A
is hyperbolic.

Now assume by contradiction that
p_{1}\in(A^{-},x)
. Then let us prove that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, the projection
p_{n}\in(A^{-},p_{n-1})
by induction on
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
.

•

For
n=1
, it is our assumption.

•

Suppose that for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
p_{n}\in(A^{-},p_{n-1})
. We apply the isometry
A
:
Ap_{n}
belongs to
AL_{A}\cap(A^{-},Ap_{n-1})
. We would like to apply Lemma
2.6
with
l=L_{A}
,
l_{\infty}=A^{-}
,
y_{0}=p_{n}
,
x_{0}=Ap_{n-1}
and
x=Ap_{n}
. Let us check the required inequalities :

\displaystyle d(Ap_{n},p_{n})

\displaystyle\geq l_{S}(A)>6\delta>2\delta+2\delta\geq d(Ap_{n-1},p_{n})+2\delta\text{ by \eqref{eq:d(Apn,p_{n+1})<2d}}

\displaystyle d(p_{n},Ap_{n})

\displaystyle\geq l_{S}(A)>6\delta.

Hence, Lemma
2.6
applies, and we deduce that
p_{n+1}
belongs to
[p_{n},A^{-})
.

Therefore, since for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
p_{n+1}
belongs to
[p_{n},A^{-})
, we deduce that the sequence
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
cannot converge to
A^{+}
, which is absurd. As a consequence,
p_{1}=p(Ax)\in[x,A^{+})
.

∎

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
A
be a hyperbolic isometry and
L_{A}
a geodesic between
A^{+}
and
A^{-}
. Let
x\in L_{A}
. Then
d(x,A^{2}x)\geq 2d(x,Ax)-20\delta
.

Proof.

We denote
p_{L_{A}}:\mathcal{X}\to L_{A}
be a projection on
L_{A}
. Note that the inequality of the Lemma is automatically satisfied when
d(x,Ax)\leq 10\delta
. Thus, in the following, we may assume that

(15)

d(x,Ax)>10\delta.

Consider the projection
p_{L_{A}}(Ax)
, we have
p_{L_{A}}(Ax)\in[x,A_{\infty})
, where
A_{\infty}\in\{A^{+},A^{-}\}
and
[x,A_{\infty})
denotes the geodesic ray between
x
and
A_{\infty}
contained in
L_{A}
. By applying the isometry
A
, we then also obtain that
Ap_{L_{A}}(Ax)\in[Ax,A_{\infty})
, with
[Ax,A_{\infty})
the geodesic ray between
Ax
and
A_{\infty}
contained in
A(L_{A})
. Now let us bound by below
d(Ap_{L_{A}}(Ax),p_{L_{A}}(Ax))
:

\displaystyle d(Ap_{L_{A}}(Ax),p_{L_{A}}(Ax))

\displaystyle\geq d(Ap_{L_{A}}(Ax),Ax)-d(p_{L_{A}}(Ax),Ax)\text{ by the triangle inequality}

\displaystyle\geq d(Ax,x)-4\delta\text{ by Corollary \ref{cor:axe-quasi-inv}}

\displaystyle>6\delta\text{ by \eqref{eq:12delta} }.

We now apply Lemma
2.6
with
l=L_{A}
,
y_{0}=p_{L_{A}}(Ax)
,
l_{\infty}=A_{\infty}
,
x_{0}=Ax
,
x=Ap_{L_{A}}(Ax)
. The inequality above, together with
d(Ax,p_{L_{A}}(Ax))\leq 2\delta
(Corollary
2.11
), ensures that the hypotheses of the Lemma are satisfied; therefore, we deduce that the projection
p_{L_{A}}(Ap_{L_{A}}(Ax))
on
L_{A}
belongs to
[p_{L_{A}}(Ax),A_{\infty})
. Now let us bound
d(p_{L_{A}}(Ap_{L_{A}}(Ax)),p_{L_{A}}(A^{2}x))
:

\displaystyle d(p_{L_{A}}(Ap_{L_{A}}(Ax)),p_{L_{A}}(A^{2}x))

\displaystyle\leq d(Ap_{L_{A}}(Ax),A^{2}x)+4\delta\text{ by the triangle inequality and Corollary \ref{cor:axe-quasi-inv}}

\displaystyle\leq d(p_{L_{A}}(Ax),Ax)+4\delta

\displaystyle\leq 6\delta.

\displaystyle\text{Also :}\qquad d(p_{L_{A}}(Ax),p_{L_{A}}(A^{2}x))

\displaystyle\geq d(Ax,A^{2}x)-4\delta\text{ by Corollary \ref{cor:axe-quasi-inv}}

\displaystyle\geq d(x,Ax)-4\delta

\displaystyle>6\delta.

Therefore, we deduce that
p_{L_{A}}(A^{2}x)\in[p_{L_{A}}(Ax),A_{\infty})
. We now have that
x,p_{L_{A}}(Ax)
and
p_{L_{A}}(A^{2}x)
are aligned on this order on
L_{A}
, and thus we can write :

\displaystyle d(x,A^{2}x)

\displaystyle\geq d(x,p_{L_{A}}(A^{2}x))-2\delta\text{ using Corollary \ref{cor:axe-quasi-inv}}

\displaystyle\geq d(x,p_{L_{A}}(Ax))+d(p_{L_{A}}(Ax),p_{L_{A}}(A^{2}x))\text{ because of the order of the points}

\displaystyle\geq d(x,Ax)+d(Ax,A^{2}x)-6\delta\text{ using Corollary \ref{cor:axe-quasi-inv}}

\displaystyle\geq 2d(x,Ax)-6\delta.\qed

From the previous lemma we now deduce the following Proposition, which says that the minimal displacement, or the stable norm, is nearly attained on the axis of
A
:

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
A
be a hyperbolic isometry and
L_{A}
a geodesic between
A^{+}
and
A^{-}
. Then, for all
x\in L_{A}
,

d(x,Ax)\leq l_{S}(A)+20\delta\leq l(A)+20\delta.

Proof.

First note that we always have
l_{S}(A)\leq l(A)
, so the right inequality is direct. Let us prove the left one.
Lemma
2.14
states that
d(x,A^{2}x)\geq 2d(x,Ax)-20\delta
. Note that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
L_{A}
is a geodesic between
(A^{n})^{+}=A^{+}
and
(A^{n})^{-}=A^{-}
. So a direct induction using Lemma
2.14
shows that, for all integers
n\geq 1
:

d(x,A^{2^{n}}x)\geq 2^{n}d(x,Ax)-(2^{n}-1)20\delta.

We then divide this inequality by
2^{n}
and take the limit. The left-hand side tends to
l_{S}(A)
while the right-hand side tends to
d(x,Ax)-20\delta
, which gives the Lemma.

∎

Theorem 2.16

(Theorem 1.9 in
[
15
]
)
.

Let
\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X})

\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X})
.

2.5.
Local-global Lemma

We end these preliminaries with the so-called local-global Lemma. There are several versions of it in the literature and we will use the one from
[
1
]
.

Lemma 2.17

(Local-to-Global,
[
1
]
)
.

.
Let
\mathcal{X}
be
\delta
-hyperbolic,
A>0
and suppose
x_{0},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathcal{X}

are such that
(x_{i-1}|x_{i+1})_{x_{i}}\leq A
and
d(x_{i},x_{i+1})>L:=3A+26\delta
for each
i
. Then any
geodesic joining
x_{0}
and
x_{n}
passes within
D:=A+10\delta
of each point
x_{i}
and

d(x_{0},x_{n})\geq d(x_{0},x_{1})+\dots+d(x_{n-1},x_{n})-2D(n-1).

Proof.

See Lemma 2.1 in
[
1
]
. The constants
3A+26\delta
and
D=A+10\delta
in Lemma
2.17
differ from those given by the authors in Lemma 2.1 in
[
1
]
; we will now explain why and how we obtain those for Lemma
2.17
.

First, notice that the definition of hyperbolicity used in
[
1
]
differs from ours. The authors assumed that the space is
\delta
-hyperbolic in the sense of Gromov. A metric space is
\delta
-hyperbolic in the sense of Gromov
if for all
x,y,z,w
in
\mathcal{X}
, we have :
(x|y)_{w}\geq\min((x|z)_{w},(y|z)_{w})-\delta
.
Hyperbolicity in the sense of Gromov and in the sense of Rips (our Definition, see section
2
) agrees, up to a change of the constant
\delta
. Next lemma from
[
6
]
explicits the constant needed to go from Rips hyperbolicity to Gromov hyperbolicity :

Lemma 2.18

(Lemma 11.27 in
[
6
]
)
.

If
\mathcal{X}
is a geodesic metric space that is
\delta
–hyperbolic in the sense of Rips, then
\mathcal{X}
is 3
\delta
–hyperbolic in the sense of Gromov.

Therefore, we deduce that the equation
A+2\delta\geq\min\{(x_{i-1}|x_{0})_{x_{i}},(x_{0}|x_{n})_{x_{i}},(x_{n}|x_{i+1})_{x_{i}})\}
in the proof of Lemma 2.1 in
[
1
]
becomes
A+6\delta\geq\min\{(x_{i-1}|x_{0})_{x_{i}},(x_{0}|x_{n})_{x_{i}},(x_{n}|x_{i+1})_{x_{i}})\}
for us. However, equation (2.3) in
[
1
]
, which is
d(z,[x,y])-4\delta\leq(x|y)_{z}\leq d(z,[x,y])
is still true in our context without altering the constants
4\delta
. Indeed, this inequality is a consequence of our Lemma
2.1
. No more changes are needed to follow the proof of
[
1
]
.

Finally, note that in Lemma 2.1 in
[
1
]
, the authors also prove in their Lemma that
\sum_{j=1}^{n}d(x_{j-1},x_{j})\leq 2d(x_{0},x_{n})
(last part of Lemma 2.1). This requires increasing the constant compared to the one that one could obtained without asking for this last inequality. Since we don’t need this inequality in Lemma
2.17
, we end up with the constants
D=A+10\delta
and
L=3A+26\delta
.
∎

3.
Irreducibility of Bowditch representations

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a geodesic
\delta
-hyperbolic space and
\rho:\Gamma\to\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X})
a representation of a finitely generated group
\Gamma
in
\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X})
. We say that
\rho
is
irreducible
if
\rho
does not globally fix a point in the boundary
\partial\mathcal{X}
of
\mathcal{X}
. If
\rho
is not irreducible, we say that it is
reducible
.

Remark 3.1
.

If
\Gamma=\mathbb{F}_{2}
is the free group of rank two and
\{a,b\}
is a free generating set of
\mathbb{F}_{2}
such that
A=\rho(a)
and
B=\rho(b)
are hyperbolic, this is equivalent to saying that
\{A^{+},A^{-}\}\cap\{B^{+},B^{-}\}=\emptyset
.

The goal of this section is to prove the following :

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a
\delta
-hyperbolic, geodesic, and visibility space, and
\rho:\mathbb{F}_{2}\to\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X})
satisfy
(BQa)
. Then
\rho
is irreducible.

3.1.
Busemann functions

In the following, we will use the Busemann functions. Let us introduce them now.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a metric space. Let
l:\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{X}
be a parametrized geodesic in
\mathcal{X}
and
x\in\mathcal{X}
. We define :

(16)

\mathrm{Bus}_{l}(x)=\underset{t\to+\infty}{\lim}(d(l(t),x)-t).

We now establish the following classical facts.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a metric space and
x\in\mathcal{X}
.

(a)

For all parametrized geodesic
l:\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{X}
,
\mathrm{Bus}_{l}(x)
is well-defined.

(b)

Let
c\geq 0
. If
l_{1}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{X}
and
l_{2}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{X}
are two parametrized geodesics such that for
t
sufficiently large,
d(l_{1}(t),l_{2}(t))\leq c
, then
\mathrm{Bus}_{l_{2}}(x)-c\leq\mathrm{Bus}_{l_{1}}(x)\leq\mathrm{Bus}_{l_{2}}(x)+c
.

Now assume moreover that
\mathcal{X}
is a geodesic
\delta
-hyperbolic space.

(c)

Let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{X}
. If
\mathrm{Bus}_{l}(x_{n})\underset{n\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}-\infty
, then
x_{n}\underset{n\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}l(+\infty)\in\partial\mathcal{X}
, where
l(+\infty)
denotes the limit in the boundary
\partial\mathcal{X}
of
l(t)
when
t\to+\infty
.

(d)

Let
A
an hyperbolic isometry and
L_{A}
a geodesic between its fixpoints
A^{+}
and
A^{-}
. Denote (with a slight abuse of notation) again
L_{A}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{X}
a parametrization of the geodesic
L_{A}
such that
L_{A}(t)\underset{t\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}A^{+}
. Then

\mathrm{Bus}_{L_{A}}(x)-l_{S}(A)-32\delta\leq\mathrm{Bus}_{L_{A}}(Ax)\leq\mathrm{Bus}_{L_{A}}(x)-l_{S}(A)+32\delta.

Proof.

(a)

By the triangle inequality, for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
and all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
d(l(t),x)-t=d(l(t),x)-d(l(0),l(t))\geq-d(l(0),x)
so the map
t\mapsto d(l(t),x)-t
is bounded from below. In addition, for all
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
, we have :

\displaystyle d(l(t_{2}),x)-t_{2}\leq d(l(t_{2}),l(t_{1}))+d(l(t_{1}),x)-t_{2}\leq d(l(t_{1}),x)+|t_{2}-t_{1}|-t_{2}=d(l(t_{1}),x)-t_{1}

so the map
t\mapsto d(l(t),x)-t
is non-increasing. Hence the limit exists.

(b)

We have for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
d(l_{1}(t),x)-t\leq d(l_{1}(t),l_{2}(t))+d(l_{2}(t),x)-t
so by taking the limit, we have
\mathrm{Bus}_{l_{1}}(x)\leq\mathrm{Bus}_{l_{2}}(x)+c
. The reverse inequality
\mathrm{Bus}_{l_{2}}(x)\leq\mathrm{Bus}_{l_{1}}(x)+c
is obtained by exchanging the role of
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
.

(c)

First note that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and for all
t>0
, we have by definition of the Gromov product,
d(l(t),x_{n})-t=d(l(t),x_{n})-d(l(0),l(t))=d(l(0),x_{n})-(x_{n}\,|\,l(t))_{l(0)}
. Then, letting
t\to+\infty
, we obtain

\mathrm{Bus}_{l}(x_{n})=d(l(0),x_{n})-(x_{n}\,|\,l(+\infty))_{l(0)},

where
(x_{n}\,|\,l(+\infty))_{l(0)}
is the limit of
(x_{n},\,|\,l(t))_{l(0)}
when
t\to+\infty
. Now if we assume that
\mathrm{Bus}_{l}(x_{n})\underset{n\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}-\infty
, then it implies that
(x_{n}\,|\,l(+\infty))_{l(0)}\underset{n\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}+\infty
, which, by definition of the boundary of
\mathcal{X}
, implies that
x_{n}\underset{n \to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}l(+\infty)
.

(d)

Let us first bound
d(A(L_{A}(t)),L_{A}(t+l_{S}(A)))
.

Consider
p(A(L_{A}(t)))
a projection of
A(L_{A}(t))
on
L_{A}
. Then, there exists
t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
p(A(L_{A}(t)))=L_{A}(t^{\prime})
. Moreover, since
L_{A}(t)\in L_{A}
, Corollary
2.11
ensures that
d(A(L_{A}(t)),L_{A}(t^{\prime}))\leq 2\delta
. From this we deduce, using the triangle inequality and the fact that
L_{A}
is an isometry :

(17)

d(AL_{A}(t),L_{A}(t+l_{S}(A)))\leq 2\delta+|t^{\prime}-t-l_{S}(A)|.

Let us now bound
||t^{\prime}-t|-l_{S}(A)|
. On the one hand we have :

\displaystyle l_{S}(A)-|t^{\prime}-t|\leq d(L_{A}(t),A(L_{A}(t)))-d(L_{A}(t^{\prime}),L_{A}(t))\leq d(L_{A}(t^{\prime}),A(L_{A}(t)))\leq 2\delta.

And on the other hand we have :

\displaystyle|t^{\prime}-t|-l_{S}(A)

\displaystyle\leq d(L_{A}(t^{\prime}),L_{A}(t))-d(A(L_{A}(t)),L_{A}(t))+20\delta\quad\text{ by Proposition \ref{prop:lS atteint sur l'axe}}

\displaystyle\leq d(L_{A}(t^{\prime}),A(L_{A}(t)))+20\delta\quad\text{ by the triangle inequality}

\displaystyle\leq 22\delta.

Therefore

(18)

||t^{\prime}-t|-l_{S}(A)|\leq 22\delta.

Now let us assume that
l_{S}(A)>6\delta
. Then Lemma
2.13
ensures that
L_{A}(t^{\prime})\in[L_{A}(t),A^{+})
, and so that
|t^{\prime}-t|=t^{\prime}-t
. From this, together with equation (

) and (

), we conclude

If

, then

\displaystyle d(AL_{A}(t),L_{A}(t+l_{S}(A)))

\displaystyle\leq d(AL_{A}(t),L_{A}(t))+d(L_{A}(t),L_{A}(t+l_{S}(A)))

\displaystyle\leq l_{S}(A)+20\delta+l_{S}(A)\text{ by Proposition \ref{prop:lS atteint sur l'axe}}

\displaystyle \leq 32\delta.

Then we proved that in any case

(19)

d(AL_{A}(t),L_{A}(t+l_{S}(A)))\leq 32\delta.

Moreover, reversing the orientation of the geodesic
L_{A}
we obtain also :

(20)

d(A^{-1}L_{A}(t),L_{A}(t-l_{S}(A)))\leq 32\delta.

Finally we can compute the Busemann function at

\displaystyle\mathrm{Bus}_{L_{A}}(Ax)

\displaystyle=\underset{t\to+\infty}{\lim}(d(L_{A}(t),Ax)-t)=\underset{t\to+\infty}{\lim}(d(A^{-1}(L_{A}(t)),x)-t)

\displaystyle\leq\underset{t\to+\infty}{\lim}(d(A^{-1}(L_{A}(t)),L_{A}(t-l_{S}(A)))+d(L_{A}(t-l_{S}(A)),x)-t+l_{S}(A)-l_{S}(A))

\displaystyle\leq\underset{t\to+\infty}{\lim}(d(L_{A}(t-l_{S}(A)),x)-t+l_{S}(A))-l_{S}(A)+32\delta\text{ by \eqref{eq:bound d(A^{-1}L_A(t),L_A(t-l_S(A)))}}

\displaystyle\leq\underset{t\to+\infty}{\lim}(d(L_{A}(t),x)-t)-l_{S}(A)+32\delta

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{Bus}_{L_{A}}(x)-l_{S}(A)+32\delta.

A similar computation of
\mathrm{Bus}_{L_{A}}
at
x
, using
A(L_{A}(t))
instead of

and (

∎

j\in\mathbb{N}
, then

d(x_{i},x_{i+1})=d(x_{4j+2},x_{4j+3})=d((AB)^{j}Ax_{B},(AB)^{j+1}x_{B})=d(x_{B},Bx_{B})\geq l_{S}(B)
.

•

Suppose that
i=4j+3
, with
j\in\mathbb{N}
, then

d(x_{i},x_{i+1})=d(x_{4j+3},x_{4(j+1)})=d((AB)^{j+1}x_{B},(AB)^{j+1}x_{A})=d(x_{B},x_{A})=d(L_{A},L_{B})
.

Then, using our hypotheses on
d(L_{A},L_{B})
,
l_{S}(A)
and
l_{S}(B)
and Lemma
4.3
we deduce that our sequence of points
(x_{i})_{0\leq i\leq n}
satisfies the hypothesis of the local-global Lemma
2.17
, and thus we obtain :

\displaystyle d(x_{0},x_{4n})

\displaystyle\geq d(x_{0},x_{1})+\dots+d(x_{4n-1},x_{4n})-2D(4n-1)

\displaystyle d(x_{A},(AB)^{n}x_{A})

\displaystyle\geq nd(x_{A},Ax_{A})+nd(x_{A},x_{B})+nd(x_{B},Bx_{B})+nd(x_{B},x_{A})-36\delta(4n-1)

\displaystyle d(x_{A},(AB)^{n}x_{A})

\displaystyle\geq n(d(x_{A},Ax_{A})+d(x_{B},Bx_{B})+2d(L_{A},L_{B}))-36\delta(4n-1).

To obtain the inequality on
l_{S}(AB)
, divide the previous one by
n
then take the limit, and use
\frac{1}{n}d(x_{A},(AB)^{n}x_{A})\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}l_{S}(AB)
,
d(x_{A},Ax_{A})\geq l_{S}(A)
and
d(x_{B},Bx_{B})\geq l_{S}(B)
.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

In Lemma
4.4
, we did not assume that
l_{S}(AB)\geq l_{S}(AB^{-1})
. In particular, in this case (
d(L_{A},L_{B})>50\delta
), the inequality of Proposition
4.1
is true both for
l_{S}(AB)
and
l_{S}(AB^{-1})
, not only the maximum.

In fact, adapting the proof of Lemma
4.4
, we could also prove that for all cyclically reduced word
W
on the alphabet
\{A,A^{-1},B,B^{-1}\}
, we have
l_{S}(W)\geq(\min(l_{S}(A),l_{S}(B))-72\delta)|W|
, where
|W|
is the word length of
W
. This in particular would prove that a representation of
\mathbb{F}_{2}
in
\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X})
is automatically convex-cocompact when
l_{S}(A)>50\delta
,
l_{S}(B)>50\delta
and
d(L_{A},L_{B})>50\delta
.

4.2.
When the axes come close at some point

Recall that
A
and
B
are two hyperbolic isometries which satisfy
\{A^{+},A^{-}\}\cap\{B^{+},B^{-}\}=\emptyset
. In this section, we assume moreover that
d(L_{A},L_{B})\leq 50\delta
and we denote
K_{\delta}=50\delta
.

Let
\varphi_{A}
be an isometry between
\mathbb{R}
and
L_{A}
such that
\varphi_{A}(t)\underset{t\to\pm\infty}{\longrightarrow}A^{\pm}
and
\varphi_{B}
an isometry between
\mathbb{R}
and
L_{B}
such that
\varphi_{B}(t)\underset{t\to\pm\infty}{\longrightarrow}B^{\pm}
.
We define the four following elements of
\mathcal{X}
:

Definition 4.1
.

\displaystyle x_{A}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{A}(\inf\{t\in\mathbb{R}\mid d(\varphi_{A}(t),L_{B})\leq K_{\delta}\})

\displaystyle y_{A}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{A}(\sup\{t\in\mathbb{R}\mid d(\varphi_{A}(t),L_{B})\leq K_{\delta}\})

\displaystyle x_{B}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{B}(\inf\{t\in\mathbb{R}\mid d(\varphi_{B}(t),L_{A})\leq K_{\delta}\})

\displaystyle y_{B}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{B}(\sup\{t\in\mathbb{R}\mid d(\varphi_{B}(t),L_{A})\leq K_{\delta}\})

Note that the isometries
\varphi_{A}
and
\varphi_{B}
induce an order on the geodesic
L_{A}
and
L_{B}
coming from the order on
\mathbb{R}
. We can extend this order to the endpoints of
L_{A}
and
L_{B}
. We make the following observations on the order of the elements
x_{A}
,
y_{A}
,
x_{B}
and
y_{B}
defined previously.

Lemma 4.6
.

We have

\displaystyle A^{-}<x_{A}\leq y_{A}<A^{+},

\displaystyle B^{-}<x_{B}\leq y_{B}<B^{+}.

Moreover, the two infimum and the two supremum in Definition
4.1
are attained, and then

(29)

d(x_{A},L_{B})=d(y_{A},L_{B})=d(x_{B},L_{A})=d(y_{B},L_{A})=K_{\delta}.

Proof.

First notice that the assumption
d(L_{A},L_{B})\leq K_{\delta}
implies that
x_{A}\neq A^{+}
,
y_{A}\neq A^{-}
,
x_{B}\neq B^{+}
and
y_{B}\neq B^{-}
. The inequalities
x_{A}\leq y_{A}
and
x_{B}\leq y_{B}
follow immediately from the definitions.

The fact that
x_{A}\neq A^{-}
,
y_{A}\neq A^{+}
,
x_{B}\neq B^{-}
and
y_{B}\neq B^{+}
is a consequence of the hypothesis
\{A^{-},A^{+}\}\neq\{B^{-},B^{+}\}
. Indeed, if
x_{A}=A^{-}
, then we could find a sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of elements of
L_{A}
which would stay at bounded distance from
L_{B}
and such that
x_{n}\to A^{-}
. We could then conclude that
A^{-}\in\{B^{-},B^{+}\}
which is a contradiction. The proof of the other inequalities are similar.

Finally, the fact that the infimum and the supremum are attained and that equation (
29
) holds is just a consequence of the continuity of the distance.
∎

Denote
[x_{A},y_{A}]
a geodesic segment between
x_{A}
and
y_{A}
contained in
L_{A}
and
[x_{B},y_{B}]
a geodesic segment between
x_{B}
and
y_{B}
contained in
L_{B}
. Recall that we introduced two projection maps :
p_{A}:\mathcal{X}\to L_{A}
and
p_{B}:\mathcal{X}\to L_{B}
. An easy consequence of Lemma
4.6
on the projections of
x_{A},y_{A},x_{B}
and
y_{B}
is the following :

Corollary 4.7
.

The projections
p_{B}(x_{A})
and
p_{B}(y_{A})
belong to
[x_{B},y_{B}]
and the projections
p_{A}(x_{B})
and
p_{A}(y_{B})
belong to
[x_{A},y_{A}]
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.6
,
d(p_{B}(x_{A}),x_{A})\leq K_{\delta}
and
d(p_{B}(y_{A}),x_{A})\leq K_{\delta}
, and so it immediately follows from the definition of
x_{B}
and
y_{B}
(Definition
4.1
) that
x_{B}\leq p_{B}(x_{A})\leq y_{B}
and
x_{B}\leq p_{B}(y_{A})\leq y_{B}
, since
p_{B}(x_{A}),p_{B}(y_{A})\in L_{B}
and
x_{A}\in L_{A}
. We obtain
p_{A}(x_{B}),p_{A}(y_{B})\in[x_{B},y_{B}]
similarily by exchanging the roles of
A
and
B
.
∎

We can also verify that the segment
[x_{A},y_{A}]
stays close to its projection on
L_{B}
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
x\in[x_{A},y_{A}]
, then
d(x,L_{B})\leq K_{\delta}+2\delta
.

Proof.

Consider the quadrilateral
[x_{A},y_{A}]\cup[y_{A},p_{B}(y_{A})]\cup[p_{B}(x_{A}),p_{B}(y_{A})]\cup[x_{A},p_{B}(x_{A})]
. By Lemma
2.2
, there exists
y\in[y_{A},p_{B}(y_{A})]\cup[p_{B}(x_{A}),p_{B}(y_{A})]\cup[x_{A},p_{B}(x_{A})]
such that
d(x,y)\leq 2\delta
. Then :

•

If
y\in[p_{B}(x_{A}),p_{B}(y_{A})]
, then
y\in L_{B}
and the lemma is proved.

•

If
y\in[x_{A},p_{B}(x_{A})]
, then
d(x,p_{B}(x_{A}))\leq d(x,y)+d(y,p_{B}(x_{A}))\leq 2\delta+K_{\delta}
, and
p_{B}(x_{A})\in L_{B}
so the lemma is again proved.

•

If
y\in[y_{A},p_{B}(y_{A})]
, then similarily
d(x,p_{B}(y_{A}))\leq 2\delta+K_{\delta}
, hence the lemma.

∎

Let
[y_{A},A^{+})
be the half-geodesic from
y_{A}
to
A^{+}
contained in
L_{A}
,
[y_{A},p_{B}(y_{A})]
a geodesic segment between
y_{A}
and
p_{B}(y_{A})
and
[p_{B}(x_{A}),p_{B}(y_{A})]
the geodesic segment between
p_{B}(x_{A})
and
p_{B}(y_{A})
contained in
L_{B}
. For all
x\in L_{B}
, we also denote by
(B^{-},x]
the half-geodesic from
x
in
\mathcal{X}
to
B^{-}
in
\partial\mathcal{X}
contained in
L_{B}
.

We now want to prove that the Gromov-product between a point
q
on
[y_{A},A^{-})
and a point
p
on
(B^{-},x]
, seen from a basepoint on
[p_{B}(x_{A}),p_{B}(y_{A})]
, is bounded by a constant depending only on
\delta
.

Lemma 4.9
.

Assume that
x_{B},p_{B}(x_{A}),p_{B}(y_{A})
and
y_{B}
are aligned on this order on
L_{B}
. Choose
x\in[p_{B}(x_{A}),p_{B}(y_{A})]
. Let
p\in(B^{-},x]
and
q\in[y_{A},A^{+})
. Then :

(30)

d(p,q)\geq d(p,x)+d(x,q)-16\delta.

Proof.

Let
[p,p_{B}(y_{A})]
be the geodesic segment from
p
to
p_{B}(y_{A})
contained in
L_{B}
and
[y_{A},q]
the geodesic segment from
y_{A}
to
q
contained in
L_{A}
. Consider the quadrilateral made up by the four geodesic segments
[p,p_{B}(y_{A})]
,
[p_{B}(y_{A}),y_{A}]
,
[y_{A},q]
and
[q,p]
. Assume by contradiction that for all
t\in[p,q]
,
d(t,[p_{B}(y_{A}),y_{A}])>2\delta
. Then by Lemma
2.3
, there exist
t\in[p,q]
,
t_{1}\in[p,p_{B}(y_{A})]
and
t_{2}\in[y_{A},q]
such that
d(t,t_{1})\leq 2\delta
and
d(t,t_{2})\leq 2\delta
. So
d(t_{1},t_{2})\leq 4\delta<K_{\delta}
. But recall that
p\in L_{B}
and
q\in(y_{A},A^{+})
so this contradicts the definition of
y_{A}
. So we deduce the existence of
r\in[p,q]
such that
d(r,[p_{B}(y_{A}),y_{A}])\leq 2\delta
. Let
s\in[p_{B}(y_{A}),y_{A}]
such that

(31)

d(r,s)\leq 2\delta.

In addition, observe that since
s\in[p_{B}(y_{A}),y_{A}]
, Lemma
2.8
ensures that
p_{B}(y_{A})
is a projection of
s
on
L_{B}
. Then, using Lemma
2.9
, we have :

(32)

d(p,s)\geq d(p,p_{B}(y_{A}))+d(p_{B}(y_{A}),s)-12\delta.

We can now derive the following inequalities :

\displaystyle d(p,q)

\displaystyle=d(p,r)+d(r,q)\text{ since $r\in[p,q]$ }

\displaystyle\geq d(p,s)+d(s,q)-4\delta\text{ by the triangle inequality and \eqref{eq:d(r,s)} }

\displaystyle\geq d(p,p_{B}(y_{A}))+d(p_{B}(y_{A}),s)+d(s,q)-16\delta\text{ by \eqref{eq:right-angle}}

\displaystyle\geq d(p,p_{B}(y_{A}))+d(p_{B}(y_{A}),q)-16\delta\text{ by the triangle inequality}

\displaystyle\geq d(p,x)+d(x,p_{B}(y_{A}))+d(p_{B}(y_{A}),q)-16\delta\text{ because $x\in[p,p_{B}(y_{A})]$}

\displaystyle\geq d(p,x)+d(x,q)-16\delta\text{ by the triangle inequality.}

∎

Our goal is now to obtain an analogous lemma to Lemma
4.9
, but in the case where
q
is no longer beyond
y_{A}
on the axis. For this purpose, we will now distinguish between two cases. Consider the quadrilateral
[x_{A},y_{A}]\cup[y_{A},p_{B}(y_{A})]\cup[p_{B}(y_{A}),p_{B}(x_{A})]\cup[p_{B}(x_{A}),x_{A}]
. Lemma
2.2
ensures that we have the following alternative :

(Alt1)

There exists
x\in[p_{B}(x_{A}),p_{B}(y_{A})]
and
y\in[x_{A},y_{A}]
such that
d(x,y)\leq 2\delta
.

(Alt2)

For all
x\in[p_{B}(x_{A}),p_{B}(y_{A})]
,
d(x,[x_{A},y_{A}])>2\delta
.

The following Lemma deals with case
(Alt1)
.

Lemma 4.10
.

Assume that condition
(Alt1)
is true and let
x\in[p_{B}(x_{A}),p_{B}(y_{A})]
and
y\in[x_{A},y_{A}]
be such that
d(x,y)\leq 2\delta
. Denote
[y,y_{A}]
the geodesic segment from
y
to
y_{A}
contained in
L_{A}
, let
q\in[y,y_{A}]
and
p\in(B^{-},x]
. Then :

(33)

d(p,q)\geq d(p,x)+d(x,q)-16\delta.

Proof.

Let
[x,y]
be a geodesic segment between
x
and
y
,
[x,p_{B}(y_{A})]
be the geodesic segment between
x
and
p_{B}(y_{A})
contained in
L_{B}
. By considering the quadrilateral
[x,y]\cup[y,y_{A}]\cup[y_{A},p_{B}(y_{A})]\cup[x,p_{B}(y_{A})]
and applying Lemma
2.2
, we deduce the existence of
q^{\prime}\in[x,y]\cup[x,p_{B}(y_{A})]\cup[y_{A},p_{B}(y_{A})]
such that
d(q,q^{\prime})\leq 2\delta
. Then :

•

If
q^{\prime}\in[x,y]
, then
d(x,q^{\prime})\leq 2\delta
and so
d(q,x)\leq 4\delta
. Therefore :

\displaystyle d(p,q)

\displaystyle\geq d(p,x)-4\delta

\displaystyle\geq d(p,x)+d(x,q)-d(x,q)-4\delta

\displaystyle\geq d(p,x)+d(x,q)-8\delta.

•

If
q^{\prime}\in[x,p_{B}(y_{A})]
, then

\displaystyle d(p,q)

\displaystyle\geq d(p,q^{\prime})-2\delta

\displaystyle\geq d(p,x)+d(x,q^{\prime})-2\delta\text{ since $x\in[p,q^{\prime}]$}

\displaystyle\geq d(p,x)+d(x,q)-4\delta.

•

If
q^{\prime}\in[y_{A},p_{B}(y_{A})]
, then by Lemma
2.8
, the point
p_{B}(y_{A})
is a projection of
q^{\prime}
on
L_{B}
and so by Lemma
2.9
,
d(p,q^{\prime})\geq d(p,p_{B}(y_{A}))+d(p_{B}(y_{A}),q^{\prime})-12\delta
and therefore :

\displaystyle d(p,q)

\displaystyle\geq d(p,q^{\prime})-2\delta

\displaystyle\geq d(p,p_{B}(y_{A}))+d(p_{B}(y_{A}),q^{\prime})-14\delta

\displaystyle\geq d(p,x)+d(x,p_{B}(y_{A}))+d(p_{B}(y_{A}),q)-16\delta\text{ since $x\in[p,p_{B}(y_{A})]$ and $d(q,q^{\prime})\leq 2\delta$}

\displaystyle\geq d(p,x)+d(x,q)-16\delta\text{ using the triangle inequality.}

∎

Now we adress the case
(Alt2)
. We will use the particular geometry of this configuration and so we start by a classical lemma which has already been established in previous work (
[
17
]
).

Lemma 4.11
.

If for all
x\in[p_{B}(x_{A}),p_{B}(y_{A})]
,
d(x,[x_{A},y_{A}])>2\delta
, then

(34)

\displaystyle d(p_{B}(x_{A}),p_{B}(y_{A}))\leq 8\delta,

(35)

\displaystyle d(x_{A},y_{A})\leq 2K_{\delta}+4\delta.

Proof.

Inequality
34
is proven in Lemma A.5 in
[
17
]
and inequality
35
is proven in Lemma A.4 in
[
17
]
using that
d(x_{A},p_{B}(x_{A}))=d(y_{A},p_{B}(y_{A}))=K_{\delta}
.
∎

We will now fix
y
to be the midpoint of
[x_{A},y_{A}]
and
q\in[y,y_{A}]
. We will now use Lemma
2.10
to prove that the projection of
q
on
L_{B}
must not be far from the projection of
y_{A}
on
L_{B}
.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
y
be the midpoint of the segment
[x_{A},y_{A}]
. Denote by
[y,y_{A}]
the geodesic segment between
y
and
y_{A}
contained in
L_{A}
and let
q\in[y,y_{A}]
. Then :

(36)

d(p_{B}(q),p_{B}(y_{A}))\leq 14\delta.

Proof.

We apply Lemma
2.10
to
l=L_{B}
,
p=p_{B}
,
x=q
and
y=y_{A}
to obtain :

d(p_{B}(q),p_{B}(y_{A}))\leq\max(8\delta,12\delta+d(q,y_{A})-d(q,p_{B}(q))-d(y_{A},p_{B}(y_{A})))

Recall that
q\in[y,y_{A}]
, so we deduce that
d(q,y_{A})\leq d(y,y_{A})=\frac{1}{2}d(x_{A},y_{A})
since
y
is the midpoint of the segment
[x_{A},y_{A}]
. But (
35
) gives a bound on
d(x_{A},y_{A})
from which we deduce that
d(q,y_{A})\leq K_{\delta}+2\delta
. Also recall that

. Therefore :

d(p_{B}(q),p_{B}(y_{A}))\leq\max(8\delta,12\delta+K_{\delta}+2\delta-K_{\delta}) \leq 14\delta.

∎

We now obtain the equivalent of Lemma
4.10
in the case of
(Alt2)
.

Lemma 4.13
.

Assume that condition
(Alt2)
holds. Let
x\in[p_{B}(x_{A}),p_{B}(y_{A})]
, denote
(B^{-},x]
the half-geodesic between
x\in\mathcal{X}
and
B^{-}
in
\partial\mathcal{X}
contained in
L_{B}
and let
p\in(B^{-},x]
. Let
y
be the midpoint of
[x_{A},y_{A}]
, denote
[y,y_{A}]
the geodesic segment between
y
and
y_{A}
contained in
L_{A}
and let
q\in[y,y_{A}]
. Then :

(37)

d(p,q)\geq d(p,x)+d(x,q)-40\delta.

Moreover :

(38)

d(x,y)\leq K_{\delta}+24\delta.

Proof.

\displaystyle d(p,q)

\displaystyle\geq d(p,p_{B}(q))+d(p_{B}(q),q)-12\delta\text{ by Lemma \ref{lem:bound-Gromov-product-right-angle}}

\displaystyle\geq d(p,p_{B}(y_{A}))+d(p_{B}(y_{A}),q)-40\delta\text{ by Lemma \ref{lem:proj-y-y_A-proche} }

\displaystyle\geq d(p,x)+d(x,p_{B}(y_{A}))+d(p_{B}(y_{A}),q)-40\delta\text{ since $x\in[p,p_{B}(y_{A})]$}

\displaystyle\geq d(p,x)+d(x,q)-40\delta.

Now let us bound

. The triangle inequality gives :

(39)

d(x,y)\leq d(x,p_{B}(y_{A}))+d(p_{B}(y_{A}),p_{B}(y))+d(p_{B}(y),y)

First recall that
x\in[p_{B}(x_{A}),p_{B}(y_{A})]
and then by (

) we have

Since

, Lemma
4.8
bounds

:

(41)

d(y,p_{B}(y))\leq K_{\delta}+2\delta.

Thus, the bound on

comes from combining (
39
) with (

), (
36
) and (
41
).
∎

Combining Lemma
4.9
, Lemma
4.10

, we proved :

.

x_{B},p_{B}(x_{A}),p_{B}(y_{A})

.

and such that, for all

and

, we have :

d(p,q)\geq d(p,x)+d(x,q)-40\delta.

Moreover, equality (

) still holds when
p\in[x,B^{+})
and
q\in(A^{-},y]
.

Proof.

We distinguish two cases :

•

is true, then take

and

.

If

if

.

∎

We now introduce four points,

and

and

and

.

Lemma 4.15
.

. Let

. Then :

.

.

There exists

.

Let

Remark 6.1
.

Let us make an observation that will be used extensively in this section.
Let
\rho:\mathbb{F}_{2}\to\mathrm{Isom}(X)
be an irreducible representation.
Let

be an edge in the tree
\Sigma
satisfying

and

. Then
\rho(P(X))
and
\rho(P(Y))
are hyperbolic. Moreover recall that the set

is a free basis of

, so by irreducibility of

4.1
applies, and we can write :

\max(l_{\rho}(Z),l_{\rho}(W))\geq l_{\rho}(X)+l_{\rho}(Y)-C_{\delta}.

If moreover we know that
\alpha_{\rho}(e)\leftrightarrow(X,Y;Z\to W)

l_{\rho}(Z)\geq l_{\rho}(X)+l_{\rho}(Y)-C_{\delta}
.

Starting from this observation, we derive a first easy and very useful result to study the orientation
\alpha_{\rho}
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\rho:\mathbb{F}_{2}\to\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X})
be an irreducible representation.
Let

be a vertex of
\Sigma
. Assume that
l_{\rho}(X)>C_{\delta},l_{\rho}(Y)>C_{\delta}
and
l_{\rho}(Z)>C_{\delta}
. Then, no two arrows among
\{\alpha_{\rho}(X\cap Y),\alpha_{\rho}(Y\cap Z),\alpha_{\rho}(X\cap Z)\}
are pointing away from the vertex

.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that two arrows point away from
v
, and without loss of generality assume that these arrows are
\alpha_{\rho}(X\cap Y)
and
\alpha_{\rho}(X\cap Z)
. Then, as in Remark
6.1
, we can apply Proposition
4.1
to
X
and
Y
to obtain :

(62)

l_{\rho}(X)+l_{\rho}(Y)\leq l_{\rho}(Z)+C_{\delta}.

Similarly, the same Proposition
4.1
applied to

and

gives :

(63)

l_{\rho}(X)+l_{\rho}(Z)\leq l_{\rho}(Y)+C_{\delta}.

By summing these two inequalities we deduce that

(64)

2l_{\rho}(X)+l_{\rho}(Y)+l_{\rho}(Z)\leq l_{\rho}(Z)+l_{\rho}(Y)+2C_{\delta},

and then we have

, which is a contradiction.
∎

Using Lemma

, we can study the sublevet sets

Lemma 6.3

Let
\rho:\mathbb{F}_{2}\to\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X})

For all

Proof.

The proof of this Lemma will very much follow the proof of the corresponding proposition in

2

.

First notice that since

, Proposition
4.1
is still true when replacing

by

. Assume by contradiction that this subset of the plane is not connected, and consider two regions

,

. If the length of this path is

and

When

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\rho:\mathbb{F}_{2}\to\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X})

Let

Let us first prove that for all edge

•

The property is true by assumption for

•

Suppose now that

Definition 6.1

Theorem 6.5

\mathcal{X}
be a

\iff

\rho

The implication

\implies

(BQa)

The implication

(BQb)

The implication

v
cannot both point away from
v
. If they are both pointing toward
v
, then we found a sink. If not, exactly one of them points away from
v
. Repeat the procedure with this new edge. If there was no sink, we could continue the procedure forever and then find an escaping ray for
\alpha_{\rho}
, which is forbidden by Lemma
6.6
. Hence the procedure stops, and we find a sink for
\alpha_{\rho}
.

Let us show unicity. If they were two sinks for
\alpha_{\rho}
, then consider a path of edges between these two sinks. Because of the orientation
\alpha_{\rho}
on the first and last edges of the path (which must be distinct), we deduce that we could find a vertex on the path with two arrows pointing away from it. This is forbidden by Lemma
6.2
.
∎

Proposition 6.8
.

Let
\rho:\mathbb{F}_{2}\to\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X})
satisfying
(BQa)
. If
\Omega_{\rho}(C_{\delta})=\emptyset
, then there exists
C>0
, such that for all
X\in\Omega
,
l_{\rho}(X)\geq\frac{1}{C}F_{e_{0}}(X)
.

Proof.

Let
v
be the unique sink given by Proposition
6.7
and denote by
e_{1}
,
e_{2}
and
e_{3}
the three edges which meet at
v
. Notice that
\Omega=\Omega^{0-}(\alpha_{\rho}(e_{1})) \cup\Omega^{0-}(\alpha_{\rho}(e_{2})) \cup\Omega^{0-}(\alpha_{\rho}(e_{3}))
. Then we apply Lemma
6.4
to
e_{1}
,
e_{2}
and
e_{3}
and we obtain the desired growth (recall that for all edges
e,e^{\prime}
, there exists
K>0
such that
\frac{1}{K}F_{e}\leq F_{e^{\prime}}\leq KF_{e}
).

∎

6.7.
The tree
\mathcal{T}_{\rho}

Before studying the case where
\Omega_{\rho}(C_{\delta})\neq\emptyset
, let us define a subtree
\mathcal{T}_{\rho}
of
\Sigma
which will help us handle situations involving small regions.

Fix
\rho:\mathbb{F}_{2}\to\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X})
an irreducible representation.
Let
E_{\rho}(C_{\delta})=\{e\in E(\Sigma):\Omega^{0}(e)\subset\Omega_{\rho}(C_{\delta})\}
.

Remark 6.9
.

Note that if
\rho
satisfy
(BQa)
, then
E_{\rho}(C_{\delta})
is finite because
\Omega_{\rho}(C_{\delta})=\{X\in\Omega:l_{\rho}(X)\leq C_{\delta}\}
is finite. Indeed,
\Omega^{0}
gives a map from
E_{\rho}(C_{\delta})
to the finite set
\Omega_{\rho}(C_{\delta})^{(2)}
(the set of distincts pairs in
\Omega_{\rho}(C_{\delta})
) and this map is injective because two regions intersect along at most one edge.

Let us assume that
\Omega_{\rho}(C_{\delta})\neq\emptyset
and suppose first that
E_{\rho}(C_{\delta})\neq\emptyset
. Then define
\mathcal{T}_{\rho}
to be the tree spanned by
E_{\rho}(C_{\delta})
. It is a finite tree when
E_{\rho}(C_{\delta})
is finite. If
E_{\rho}(C_{\delta})=\emptyset
but still
\Omega_{\rho}(C_{\delta})\neq\emptyset
, the connectedness of
\Omega_{\rho}(C_{\delta})
shown in Lemma
6.3
implies that
\Omega_{\rho}(C_{\delta})
consist of a single region. In this case, we take
\mathcal{T}_{\rho}
to be any edge on the boundary of this region.

Let us give a simple and useful observation.

Lemma 6.10
.

Let
\rho:\mathbb{F}_{2}\to\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X})
be an irreducible representation.
Let
e=X\cap Y\in\mathcal{T}_{\rho}
. Then either
l_{\rho}(X)\leq C_{\delta}
or
l_{\rho}(Y)\leq C_{\delta}
.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of the connectedness of the union of the regions in
\Omega_{\rho}(C_{\delta})
(see Lemma
6.3
).
∎

When
T
is a subtree of
\Sigma
, we define its
circular set

C(T)\subset\vec{E}(\Sigma)
to be the subset of oriented edge
\vec{e}
such that
\vec{e}\cap T
is exactly the head of
\vec{e}
. Note that if
T
is finite, its circular set
C(T)
is also finite.

Let us give some basic properties on the edges of the circular set
C(\mathcal{T}_{\rho})
.

Lemma 6.11
.

Let
\rho: \mathbb{F}_{2}\to\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X})
be an irreducible representation and assume
\Omega_{\rho}(C_{\delta})\neq\emptyset
.

Let
\vec{e}\leftrightarrow(X,Y;Z\to W)
be an edge in
C(\mathcal{T}_{\rho})
. Then

(a)

Either
l_{\rho}(X)>C_{\delta}
or
l_{\rho}(Y)>C_{\delta}
.

(b)

l_{\rho}(W)\leq C_{\delta}
.

(c)

Let
X^{\prime}\in\Omega^{-}(\vec{e})
. Then
l_{\rho}(X^{\prime})>C_{\delta}
.

(d)

\alpha_{\rho}(e)=\vec{e}
.

Proof.

(a)

Assume by contradiction that
l_{\rho}(X)\leq C_{\delta}
and
l_{\rho}(Y)\leq C_{\delta}
, then this means that
e\in E_{\rho}(C_{\delta})\subset\mathcal{T}_{\rho}
which is impossible by definition of
C(\mathcal{T}_{\rho})
.

(b)

Denote
e_{1}=X\cap W
and
e_{2}=Y\cap W
. Then, since
\vec{e}\in C(\mathcal{T}_{\rho})
, by definition of the circular set, either both
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
belong to
\mathcal{T}_{\rho}
or exactly one of them belong to
\mathcal{T}_{\rho}
. If both belong to
\mathcal{T}_{\rho}
and
l_{\rho}(W)>C_{\delta}
, then by Lemma
6.10
, both
l_{\rho}(X)\leq C_{\delta}
and
l_{\rho}(Y)\leq C_{\delta}
, but this would imply that
e\in\mathcal{T}_{\rho}
which contradicts the fact that
\vec{e}\in C(\mathcal{T}_{\rho})
. Now assume without loss of generality that
e_{1}\in\mathcal{T}_{\rho}
and
e_{2}\neq\mathcal{T}_{\rho}
, then it implies that
e_{1}\in E_{\rho}(C_{\delta})
and thus that
l_{\rho}(X)\leq C_{\delta}
and
l_{\rho}(W)\leq C_{\delta}
.

(c)

Assume by contradiction that
X^{\prime}\in\Omega^{-}(\vec{e})
is such that
l_{\rho}(X^{\prime})>C_{\delta}
. Then, by connectedness of
\underset{X\in\Omega_{\rho}(C_{\delta})}{\bigcup}X
(see Lemma
6.3
), we would find an edge
e^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{-}(\vec{e})
such that
e^{\prime}\in E_{\rho}(C_{\delta})\subset\mathcal{T}_{\rho}
. Now, using the same notations as in
(b)
, namely
e_{1}=X\cap W
and
e_{2}=Y\cap W
, recall that either
e_{1}
or
e_{2}
belongs to
\mathcal{T}_{\rho}
. By connectedness of the tree
\mathcal{T}_{\rho}
, this implies that also
e\in\mathcal{T}_{\rho}
which is false since
\vec{e}\in C(\mathcal{T}_{\rho})
.

(d)

This is immediate from the previous points. Indeed,
Z\in\Omega{-}(\vec{e})
so by
(c)
,
l_{\rho}(Z)>C_{\delta}
and by
(b)
,
l_{\rho}(W)\leq C_{\delta}
.

∎

6.8.
The case
\Omega_{\rho}(C_{\delta})\neq\emptyset

We now prove in this section implication
(BQa)

\implies

(BQd)
in the case
\Omega_{\rho}(C_{\delta})\neq\emptyset
. The key point is the following Lemma :

Lemma 6.12
.

Let
\rho:\mathbb{F}_{2}\to\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X})
be a representation satisfying
(BQa)
. Assume that
\Omega_{\rho}(C_{\delta})\neq\emptyset
.

Let
\vec{e}\leftrightarrow(X,Y;Z\to W)
be an oriented edge in
C(\mathcal{T}_{\rho})
. Assume moreover that
l_{\rho}(X)\leq C_{\delta}
. Then, there exists
C>0
such that for all
X^{\prime}\in\Omega^{0-}(\vec{e})
, we have
l_{\rho}(X^{\prime})\geq\frac{1}{C}F_{e}(X^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Denote by
\vec{e_{n}}
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the edges on the boundary of the region
X
such that
\vec{e_{0}}=\vec{e}
and
the head of
\vec{e_{n+1}}
is the tail of
\vec{e_{n}}
. Denote
Y_{n}
, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the regions such that
\Omega^{0}(e_{n})=\{X,Y_{n}\}
and let
\vec{f_{n}}\in\vec{E}(\Sigma)
and
W_{n}\in\Omega
be such that
\vec{f_{n}}\leftrightarrow(Y_{n},Y_{n+1};W_{n}\to X)
.

First notice that for all
n\geq 1
,
Y_{n}\in\Omega^{-}(\vec{e})
and then we deduce by Lemma
6.11

(c)
that
l_{\rho}(Y_{n})>C_{\delta}
. In addition,
\vec{e}\in C(\mathcal{T}_{\rho})
and
l_{\rho}(X)\leq C_{\delta}
, so by Lemma
6.11

(a)
we also have
l_{\rho}(Y)=l_{\rho}(Y_{0})>C_{\delta}
. Now let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let us justify that
\alpha_{\rho}(f_{n})=\vec{f_{n}}
. The region
W_{n}\in\Omega^{-}(\vec{e})
so by Lemma
6.11

(c)
, we have
l_{\rho}(W_{n})>C_{\delta}
. Since
l_{\rho}(X)\leq C_{\delta}
by assumption, we have
l_{\rho}(W_{n})>l_{\rho}(X)
, that is
\alpha_{\rho}(f_{n})=\vec{f_{n}}
. As a consequence,
\vec{f_{n}}
satisfy the hypothesis of Lemma
6.4
, and we deduce :

\text{ For all }X^{\prime}\in\Omega^{0-}(\vec{f_{n}}),\quad l_{\rho}(X^{\prime})\geq(m_{n}-C_{\delta})F_{f_{n}}(X^{\prime})+C_{\delta},\quad\text{ with }m_{n}=\min\{l_{\rho}(Y_{n}),l_{\rho}(Y_{n+1})\}.

Note that
m_{n}>C_{\delta}
and that the maps
F_{f_{n}}
and
F_{e}
are related as follows :
F_{f_{n}}\geq\frac{1}{n+2}F_{e}
. Then,

(68)

\text{ For all }X^{\prime}\in\Omega^{0-}(\vec{f_{n}}),\quad l_{\rho}(X^{\prime})\geq\frac{m_{n}-C_{\delta}}{n+2}F_{e}(X^{\prime}),\quad\text{ with }m_{n}=\min\{l_{\rho}(Y_{n}),l_{\rho}(Y_{n+1})\}.

Moreover, denoting
a=P(X)
,
b=P(Y)
,
A=\rho(a)
and
B=\rho(b)
, we have that
P(Y_{n})
is conjugated to
a^{n}b
. Moreover, since
\rho
satisfies
(BQa)
and
a^{n}b
is primitive for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, Proposition
5.3
implies that
B(A^{+})\neq A^{-}
. Therefore, by Proposition
5.1
, there exists
N
and
k
such that for all
n\geq N
,
l_{\rho}(Y_{n})=l_{S}(BA^{n})\geq l_{S}(A)n-k
. Therefore,
\displaystyle\frac{m_{n}-C_{\delta}}{n+2}\geq\frac{l_{S}(A)n-k-C_{\delta}}{n+2}\underset{n\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}l_{S}(A)
, and so for
n
large enough, we deduce that

(69)

\text{ For all }X^{\prime}\in\Omega^{0-}(\vec{f_{n}}),\quad l_{\rho}(X^{\prime})\geq\frac{l_{S}(A)}{2}F_{e}(X^{\prime}).

Finally note that we can decompose
\Omega^{0-}(\vec{e})
as
\Omega^{0-}(\vec{e})=\{X\}\bigsqcup\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\bigcup}\Omega^{0-}(\vec{f_{n}})
, so combining (
69
) for
n
large and (
68
) for
n
small, we find a constant
C>0
so that the inequalities
l_{\rho}(X^{\prime})\geq\frac{1}{C}F_{e}(X^{\prime})
hold for all
X^{\prime}\in\Omega^{0-}(\vec{e})
.
∎

We deduce the following Proposition :

Proposition 6.13
.

Let
\rho:\mathbb{F}_{2}\to\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X})
be a representation satisfying
(BQa)
.
If
\Omega_{\rho}(C_{\delta})\neq\emptyset
, then there exists
C>0
such that for all
X\in\Omega
,
l_{\rho}(X)\geq\frac{1}{C}F_{e_{0}}(X)
.

Proof.

Consider the tree
\mathcal{T}_{\rho}
and its circular set
C(\mathcal{T}_{\rho})
. Since
E_{\rho}(C_{\delta})
is finite (see Remark
6.9
), so are
\mathcal{T}_{\rho}
and
C(\mathcal{T}_{\rho})
. Moreover, note that
\Omega=\underset{\vec{e}\in C(\mathcal{T}_{\rho})}{\bigcup}\Omega^{0-}(\vec{e})
. Since this is a finite union, it is sufficient to show that for all
\vec{e}\in C(\mathcal{T}_{\rho})
, there exists
C_{\vec{e}}>0
such that for all
X\in\Omega^{0-}(\vec{e})
,
l_{\rho}(X)\geq\frac{1}{C_{\vec{e}}}F_{e}(X)
. This is done in Lemma
6.4
in the case where the two regions meeting at
e
have lengths greater than
C_{\delta}
(note that we can apply this Lemma since
\alpha_{\rho}(e)=\vec{e}
by Lemma
6.11

(d)
) and in Lemma
6.12
in the case where the length of one of two regions is smaller or equal to
C_{\delta}
.
∎

7.
Openness, dynamics of
\mathrm{Out}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
and recognition

In this section, we explain how we can use the framework developed in the previous section to show that the set of Bowditch representation is open and that the action of the outer automorphism group of
\mathbb{F}_{2}
acts properly discontinuously on it. We will proceed along lines similar to those in the corresponding parts of previous work (
[
2
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
11
]
); however, the large-scale behavior of our setting necessitates some adjustments which we will try to highlight.

7.1.
The tree
T_{\rho}(K)

Let us choose, for every region
X\in\Omega
, another region
Y_{X}\in\Omega
such that
X\cap Y_{X}
is an edge of
\Sigma
, equivalently
X\cap Y_{X}\in\partial X
. Now denote
a=P(X)
and
b=P(Y_{X})
, we have that
\{a,b\}
form a basis of
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Let
Y_{n}
, for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, be the region such that
P(Y_{n})
is a conjugate of
a^{n}b
. The regions
Y_{n}
are exactly the regions which intersect
X
in an edge on the boundary
\partial X
of
X
. Note that by definition
Y_{0}=Y_{X}
.

Let
\rho:\mathbb{F}_{2}\to\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X})
be a representation and
K>0
.
Let us denote
A=\rho(a)
and
B=\rho(b)
and assume that
A
is hyperbolic. If
B(A^{+})\neq A^{-}
, then Proposition
5.1
applies, and in this case denote
N_{+}
and
k_{+}
the constants given by the Proposition
5.1
applied to
A
and
B
. Similartly, if
B(A^{-})\neq A
, denote
N_{-}
and
k_{-}
the constants given by Proposition
5.1
applied to
A^{-1}
and
B
. Now define :

Definition 7.1
.

\displaystyle J_{\rho}^{+}(K,X)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bigcup\left\{X\cap Y_{n}\;|\;0\leq n\leq\max\left\{N_{+},\frac{K+k_{+}}{l_{\rho}(X)}\right\}\right\}&\text{ if $A$ is hyperbolic and }B(A^{+})\neq A^{-},\\
\bigcup\left\{X\cap Y_{n}\;|\;n\geq 0\right\}&\text{ if $A$ is hyperbolic and }B(A^{+})=A^{-},\\
\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle J_{\rho}^{-}(K,X)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bigcup\left\{X\cap Y_{n}\;|\;0\geq n\geq-\max\left\{N_{-},\frac{K+k_{-}}{l_{\rho}(X)}\right\}\right\}&\text{if $A$ is hyperbolic and }B(A^{-})\neq A^{+},\\
\bigcup\left\{X\cap Y_{n}\;|\;n\leq 0\right\}&\text{if $A$ is hyperbolic and }B(A^{-})=A^{+}.\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle J_{\rho}(K,X)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}J_{\rho}^{+}(K,X)\cup J_{\rho}^{-}(K,X)&\text{if $A$ is hyperbolic}\\
\partial X&\text{if $A$ is not hyperbolic.}\end{array}\right.

Remark 7.1
.

Assume that
A
is hyperbolic,
B(A^{+})\neq A^{-}
and
B(A^{-})\neq A^{+}
. Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
X\cap Y_{n}
does not belong to
J_{\rho}(K,X)
. Assume
n\geq 0
. Then
n>N_{+}
and therefore by Proposition
5.1
,
l_{S}(A^{n}B)\geq l_{S}(A)n-k_{+}
. But the integer
n
is also bigger than
\frac{K+k_{+}}{l_{S}(A)}
, so
l_{\rho}(Y_{n})=l_{S}(A^{n}B)>K
. Similarly if
n\leq 0
, we also deduce
l_{\rho}(Y_{n})>K
. Therefore, if
Z
is a region which intersect
X
and
l_{\rho}(Z)\leq K
, then
Z\cap X\subset J_{\rho}(K,X)
.

Definition 7.2
.

T_{\rho}(K)=\underset{X\in\Omega_{\rho}(K)}{\bigcup}J_{\rho}(K,X)
.

Remark 7.2
.

It is straighforward to see that
T_{\rho}(K_{1})\subset T_{\rho}(K_{2})
, whenever
K_{1}\leq K_{2}
.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
\rho:\mathbb{F}_{2}\to\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X})
be an irreducible representation and
K\geq C_{\delta}
. The tree
T_{\rho}(K)
is connected.

Proof.

Note that by construction, the subtrees
J_{\rho}(K,X)
are connected for all
X\in\Omega
: they are arcs in the boundary of the region
X
. Let
e
and
e^{\prime}
be two edges in
T_{\rho}(K)
. Then there exists
X
and
X^{\prime}
in
\Omega_{\rho}(K)
such that
e\in J_{\rho}(K,X)
and
e^{\prime}\in J_{\rho}(K,X^{\prime})
. Since
\bigcup\{X\;|\;X\in\Omega_{\rho}(K)\}
is connected (Lemma
6.3
), we deduce the existence of a sequence of regions
(X_{i})_{0\leq i\leq n}
such that
X=X_{0}
,
X^{\prime}=X_{n}
and
X_{i}\cap X_{i+1}\neq\emptyset
for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
. Denote
e_{i}=X_{i}\cap X_{i+1}
the edge at which
X_{i}
and
X_{i+1}
meet. Then
e_{i}\in J_{\rho}(K,X_{i})\cap J_{\rho}(K,X_{i+1})
. Therefore, we deduce that there exists a path from
e
to
e_{0}
which stays in
J_{\rho}(K,X_{0})
, a path from
e_{i}
to
e_{i+1}
which stays in
J_{\rho}(K,X_{i+1})
for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
, and a path from
e_{n-1}
to
e
which stays in
J_{\rho}(K,X_{n})
. This proves that
T_{\rho}(K)
is connected.

∎

Remark 7.4
.

Building on Remark
7.1
, we deduce that if
e
is an edge such that
\Omega^{0}(e)=\{X,Y\}
, with
l_{\rho}(X)\leq K
and
l_{\rho}(Y) \leq K
, then
e\subset T_{\rho}(K,X)
. As a consequence, using the connectedness of
T_{\rho}(K)
, we deduce that the tree
T_{\rho}(C_{\delta})
contains the tree
\mathcal{T}_{\rho}
defined in the previous section (
6.7
).

We say that a tree
T
is
attracting
for
\rho
, if for every edge
e
which does not belong to
T
, the orientation
\alpha_{\rho}(e)
points toward
T
.
Unlike in the previous works in
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})
(
[
2
]
,
[
21
]
) or in
\mathrm{PU}(2,1)
(
[
11
]
), the tree
T_{\rho}(K)
constructed here in this large-scale geometry context may not be attracting. In particular, if
X
is a region such that
l_{\rho}(X)\leq C_{\delta}
, and
e\leftrightarrow(X,Y_{n};Y_{n-1},Y_{n+1})
(with the notation from above) is an edge in the boundary of
X
which does not belong to
J_{\rho}(K,X)
, then a priori we could have
l_{\rho}(Y_{n+1})<l_{\rho}(Y_{n-1})
, which would mean that the orientation
\alpha_{\rho}(e)
doesn’t point toward
T_{\rho}(K)
. Indeed,
l_{\rho}(Y_{n+1})=l_{S}(AA^{n}B)
,
l_{\rho}(Y_{n-1})=l_{S}(A^{-1}A^{n}B)
, and the length
l_{S}(A^{n}B)
is big whereas the length
l_{S}(A)
could be arbitrarily small. Since that length
l_{S}(A)
is under the scale of precision of our space, we cannot deduce which inequality holds.

However, this bad phenomena can only happen in the boundary of a small region (
l_{\rho}(X)\leq C_{\delta}
) and the tree is attracting elsewhere as noted in the following proposition :

Proposition 7.5
.

Let
\rho:\mathbb{F}_{2}\to\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X})
and irreducible representation and
K>0
.

If an edge
e
of
\Sigma
does not belong to the tree
T_{\rho}(K)
and does not belong to the boundary of a region
X
with
l_{\rho}(X)\leq K
, then the orientation
\alpha_{\rho}(e)
points toward
T_{\rho}(K)
.

Proof.

The edge
e
belong to some
E^{0-}(\vec{e_{0}})
, for some oriented edge
\vec{e_{0}}\leftrightarrow(X_{0},Y_{0};Z\to W)
in the circular set
C(T_{\rho}(K))
.

•

Assume first that
l_{\rho}(X_{0})>K
and
l_{\rho}(Y_{0})>K
. Then, similarly as in the proof of Lemma
6.11

(b)
, we obtain that
l_{\rho}(W)\leq K
and similarly as in Lemma
6.11

(a)
that
l_{\rho}(Z)>K
. As a consequence
\alpha_{\rho}(e_{0})=\vec{e_{0}}
. Therefore we can apply Lemma
6.4
to conclude that
\alpha_{\rho}(e)
points toward
e_{0}
, hence toward
T_{\rho}(K)
.

•

Assume now that
l_{\rho}(X_{0})\leq K
. Since we assumed that
e
does not belong to the boundary of a region of length not bigger that
K
, we deduce that
e\in E^{0-}(\vec{e_{1}})
, where
\vec{e_{1}}\leftrightarrow(X_{1},Y_{1};Z_{1}\to X_{0})
is in
E^{-}(\vec{e_{0}})
. Note that by construction we also have
l_{\rho}(X_{1})>K
,
l_{\rho}(Y_{1})>K
and
l_{\rho}(Z_{1})>K
. Then
\alpha_{\rho}(e_{1})=\vec{e_{1}}
and therefore using Lemma
6.4
we obtain that the orientation on
\alpha_{\rho}(e)
points toward
e_{1}
, hence
e_{0}
, hence the tree
T_{\rho}(K)
.

∎

In addition, along the boundary of a region
X
such that
l_{\rho}(X)\leq K
, we can also argue that the orientation cannot be “escaping”, meaning that if
(\vec{e_{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of oriented edges lying in the boundary of
X
and not in the tree
T_{\rho}(K)
such that the head of
\vec{e_{n}}
is the tail of
\vec{e_{n+1}}
, then the orientation
\alpha_{\rho}(e_{n})
cannot enventually coincide with that of
\vec{e_{n}}
. Indeed, first note that the existence of the sequence
(\vec{e_{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in the boundary of
X
and not in
T_{\rho}(K)
implies that
A:=\rho(P(X))
is hyperbolic and that, denoting
B=\rho(P(Y_{X}))
,
B(A^{+})\neq A^{+}
or
B(A^{-})\neq A^{+}
depending on which side of the boundary of
X
the sequence
(\vec{e_{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
lies in. Then, in view of Proposition
5.1
, we have
l_{S}(A^{n}B)\geq l_{S}(A)|n|-k
for
k\geq 0
a constant and
|n|
large enough. But if
\alpha_{\rho}(e_{n})=\vec{e_{n}}
for
n

Proposition 7.6
.

Let

. Let
\rho:\mathbb{F}_{2}\to\mathrm{Isom}(\mathcal{X})
be an irreducible representation.

\rho
satisfy

is finite.

Proof.

First assume that
\rho
satisfies

. We proved in Theorem

that this implies that
\rho
satisfies
(BQb)

of
\mathbb{F}_{2}
,
B(A^{+})\neq A^{-}
and

Proposition 7.7

Let

•

7.3.

defined in section

Algorithm 4

n\geq N
,
d(l_{1}(n),p_{2}(l_{1}(n)))\leq 2\delta
and hence the algorithm runs forever.

∎

Remark 7.14
.

In
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
, the trace of the commutator characterizes irreducibility. Indeed, a representation
\rho:\mathbb{F}_{2}\to\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
is irreducible if and only if
\mathrm{Tr}(\rho([a,b]))=2
, for any free generating set
\{a,b\}
of
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Thus, this condition can be computed easily. Irreducibility is an
\mathrm{Out}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
-invariant condition which in this case corresponds to the slice
\mathrm{Tr}(\rho([a,b]))=2
of the
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
-character variety.

Let us also remark that our work allows us to certify the condition
B(A^{+})\neq A^{-}
by computing only the lengths
l_{S}(A^{n}B)
.

(

Proposition 7.15
.

Let
A
be a hyperbolic isometry and
B
be an isometry.

Algorithm
5

Remark 7.16
.

In

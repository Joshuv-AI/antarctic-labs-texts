Lemma 2.4

Let
n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}
be three integers such that
\min\{n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\}\geq 5

H
be
3
-partite 3-graph with vertex partition

\nu(H)=1
, then
\tau(H)=1
.

Proof.
By contradiction. Suppose that
\tau(H)\geq 2
. Then
E(H-v)\neq\emptyset
for any
v\in V(H)
. Let
f_{0}\in E(H)
. Since
e(H)\geq n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}-1
, and every edge of
H
intersects
f_{0}
, then
\sum_{x\in f_{0}}d_{H}(x)\geq n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}-1
. Thus
f_{0}
contains a vertex, saying
u_{1}\in V_{1}
such that
d_{H}(u_{1})\geq 5
. Let
f_{1}=\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\}\in E(G-u_{1})
such that
v_{i}\in V_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq 3
.
Since
d_{H}(u_{1})\geq 5
and
\nu(H)=1
, by symmetry, we may assume there exist two vertices
a_{3},b_{3}
in
V_{3}
such that
\{u_{1},v_{2},a_{3}\},\{u_{1},v_{2},b_{3}\}\in E(H)
. Write
f_{2}:=\{u_{1},v_{2},a_{3}\}
and
f_{3}:=\{u_{1},v_{2},b_{3}\}
.

Claim 1.
For any
f\in E(H)
,
|f\cap\{u_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\}|\geq 2
.

Firstly, consider
x\in V_{2}\setminus\{v_{2}\}
. We claim
N_{H}(x)\subseteq\{\{u_{1},v_{3}\}\}
. Otherwise, there exists
g\in N_{H}(x)\setminus\{\{u_{1},v_{3}\}\}
. If
u_{1}\notin g
, then either

\{f_{2},g\cup\{x\}\}
or
\{f_{3},g\cup\{x\}\}
is a matching of size two in
H
, contradicting to that
\nu(H)=1
; else if
u_{1}\in g
and
v_{3}\notin g
, then
\{g\cup\{x\},f_{1}\}
is a matching of size two in
H
, a contradiction. Since
\tau(H)\geq 2
, there exists an edge
f_{4}=\{u_{1},a_{2},v_{3}\}\in E(H)
such that
a_{2}\in V_{2}-v_{2}
.

Secondly, consider
y\in V_{1}\setminus\{u_{1}\}
. We claim
N_{H}(y)\subseteq\{\{v_{2},v_{3}\}\}
. Otherwise, there exists
g\in N_{H}(y)\setminus\{\{v_{2},v_{3}\}\}
. Since
\nu(H)=1
,
(g\cup\{y\})\cap f_{1}\neq\emptyset
. If
v_{2}\notin g
, then either
\{g\cup\{y\},f_{2}\}
or
\{g\cup\{y\},f_{3}\}
is a matching of size two in
H
, a contradiction; else if
v_{2}\in g
and
v_{3}\notin E(H)
, then
\{g\cup\{y\},f_{4}\}
is a matching of size two in
H
, a contradiction.

Finally, consider
z\in V_{3}-v_{3}
. We claim
N_{H}(z)\subseteq\{\{u_{1},v_{2}\}\}
. Otherwise, there exists
g\in N_{H}(z)\setminus\{\{u_{1},v_{2}\}\}
. Since
\nu(H)=1
, then
g\cap\{u_{1},v_{2}\}\neq\emptyset
, otherwise, either
\{g\cup\{z\},f_{2}\}
or
\{g\cup\{z\},f_{3}\}
is a matching of size two in
H
, a contradiction. If
u_{1}\notin g
and
v_{2}\in g
, then
\{f_{4},g\cup\{z\}\}
is a matching of size two in
G
, a contradiction; else if
u_{1}\in g
and
v_{2}\notin g
, then
\{f_{1},g\cup\{z\}\}
is a matching of size two in
H
, a contradiction. This completes the proof of Claim 1.

By Claim 1, every edge of
G

\{u_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\}

Lemma 2.5

Let
n\geq 2
be an integer.
Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two bipartite graphs with
n
vertices in each class. If for
i\in[2]
,
e(G_{i})>0
and
e(G_{1})+e(G_{2})>2n
, then
\{G_{1},G_{2}\}
admits a rainbow matching.

Proof.
Suppose that
e(G_{1})\leq e(G_{2})
. Then
e(G_{2})>n
. Let
uv\in E(G_{1})
. If
G_{2}-\{u,v\}
contains an edge
f
, then
\{uv,f\}
is a rainbow matching of
\{G_{1},G_{2}\}
. So we may assume that
\{u,v\}
is a vertex cover of
G_{2}
. Thus we may infer that
d_{G_{2}}(u)\geq 2
and
d_{G_{2}}(v)\geq 2
. Consider
e(G_{1})\geq 2
. Then we may choose an edge
f^{\prime}\in E(G_{1})-uv
. Without loss of generality, suppose that
v\notin f^{\prime}
. Since
d_{G_{2}}(v)\geq 2
and
G_{2}
is a bipartite graph, then there exists an edge
f^{\prime\prime}\in E(G_{2})
such that
v\in f^{\prime\prime}
and
f^{\prime}\cap f^{\prime\prime}=\emptyset
. It follows that
\{f^{\prime},f^{\prime\prime}\}
is a rainbow matching of
\{G_{1},G_{2}\}
. Next we may assume that
E(G_{1})=\{uv\}
, which implies that
e(G_{2})\geq 2n
, which contradicts to that
\{u,v\}
is a vertex cover of
G_{2}
. This completes the proof.

Next we give the stability lemma for smaller matching.

Lemma 2.6

Let
n,m
be two integers such that
m\geq 2
and
n\geq 12m
. Let
H
be a 3-partite 3-graph with
n
vertices in each class. If
e(H)\geq(m-1)n^{2}+3n-m
and
\nu(H)=m
, then
\tau(H)=m
.

Proof.
We prove the following claim.

Claim 1.
Let
s<n/2
be an integer and let
G
be a
3
-partite
3
-graph with
n
vertices in each class. If
G
contains a vertex
v
such that
\nu(G-v)=s
and
d_{G}(v)>2sn
, then
\nu(G)\geq s+1
.

Let
M
be a matching of size
s
in
G-v
. Note that the number of edges containing
v
and intersecting
V(M)
is at most
2sn
. So there exists an edge
f
such that
v\in f
and
f\cap V(M)=\emptyset
. Then
M\cup\{f\}
is a matching of size
s+1
in
G
. This completes the proof of Claim 1.

Claim 2.

H
contains
m-1
vertices, say
v_{1},\ldots,v_{m-1}
such that
d_{H-A_{i}}(v_{i})>2(m-i)n
and
\nu(H-A_{i+1})=m-i+1
, where
A_{1}:=\emptyset
and
A_{i}:=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{i-1}\}
for
2\leq i\leq m
.

Let
M_{0}
be a maximum matching of
H
. Then we have
|M_{0}|=m
. Note that

\sum_{v\in V(M_{0})}d_{H}(v)\geq e(H)>(m-1)n^{2}+2n,

Hence there exists
v_{1}\in V(M_{0})
such that

d_{H}(v_{1})>\frac{1}{3m}((m-1)n^{2}+2n)>2mn,

where the second inequality holds since
n\geq 12m
and
m\geq 2
.
Since
\nu(H)=m
, then by Claim 1, we have
\nu(H-v_{1})=m-1
. Recall that
e(H)\geq(m-1)n^{2}+3n-m
. So we have
e(H-v_{1})\geq(m-2)n^{2}+3n-m
. Suppose that we have found
v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}
such that for
1\leq i\leq r\leq m-2
, the following statements hold.

(i)

\nu(H-\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{i}\})=m-i
;

(ii)

d_{H-A_{i}}(v_{i})>2(m-i)n
for
1\leq i\leq r
, where
A_{1}=\emptyset
and
A_{i}=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{i-1}\}
for
2\leq i\leq r
.

Suppose that
r\leq m-2
. Note that
e(H-\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\})>(m-1-r)n^{2}+2n
. Let
M_{r}
be a matching of size
m-r
in
H-\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\}
. Then we have

\sum_{v\in V(M_{r})}d_{H}(v)>(m-1-r)n^{2}+2n.

Hence there exists
v_{r+1}\in V(M_{r})
such that

d_{H}(v_{r+1})>\frac{1}{3(m-r)}((m-1-r)n^{2}+2n)>2(m-r)n,

where the second inequality holds since
n\geq 12m
and
r\leq m-2
.
Moreover, by Claim 1, we have
\nu(H-\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{r+1}\})=m-1-r
. Continuing the process, we may find the desired
m-1
vertices. This completes the proof of Claim 2.

By Claims 1 and 2, we may choose
v_{1},\ldots,v_{m-1}
such that for
1\leq j\leq m-1
,
\nu(H-\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{j}\})=m-j
. Write
A:=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{m-1}\}
and
n_{i}:=n-|V_{i}\cap\{A\}|
for
1\leq i\leq 3
. Note that

\min\{n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\}\geq n-m\geq 5
.
One can see that
\nu(H-A)=1
and

e(H-A)\geq 3n-m=n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}-1.

By Lemma
2.4
,
H-A
has a vertex cover of size one denoted by
A^{\prime}
. Then
A\cup A^{\prime}
is a vertex cover of size
m
in
H
. This completes the proof.

. For
i\in[3]
, we choose
t
vertices from
V_{i}-V(M_{1}^{\prime})
, saying
v_{i1},\ldots,v_{it}
such that
\max\{d_{H^{\prime}}(v_{ij})\ |\ j\in[t]\}\leq\min\{d_{H^{\prime}}(y)\ |\ y\in V_{i}-(V(M_{1}^{\prime})\cup\{v_{ij}\ |\ j\in[t]\})\}
. Write
T:=\cup_{i\in[3]}\{v_{ij}\ |\ j\in[t]\}
and
H^{\prime\prime}:=H-(V(M_{1}^{\prime})\cup T)
. If
\delta_{1}(H^{\prime\prime})>\frac{2}{3}(n-t-2)^{2}
, then by Theorem
3.2
,
H^{\prime\prime}
has a perfect matching of size
n-t-2
, which means that
H
has a matching of size
m+1
, a contradiction. Hence we may assume
\delta_{1}(H^{\prime\prime})\leq\frac{2}{3}(n-t-2)^{2}
. We claim that
\delta_{1}(H^{\prime\prime})>4n
, otherwise, for some
i\in\{2,3\}
, there exist
t+1
vertices
y\in V_{i}-V(M_{1}^{\prime})
such that
d_{H}(y)\leq 4n+(t+2)n
. So we have

\displaystyle(m-2)n^{2}+2n+2

\displaystyle\leq(m-2)n^{2}+3n-m

\displaystyle\leq e(H-\{u,v\})

\displaystyle\leq(m-1)n^{2}-(t+1)(m-1)n+(4n+(t+2)n)(t+1)

(since
S\backslash\{u,v\}
) is a vertex cover of
H-\{u,v\}

\displaystyle\leq(m-1)n^{2}-2(m-1)n+14n,\quad\mbox{(since $m\geq 9$ and $1\leq t\leq 2$)}

\displaystyle\leq(m-1)n^{2}-2(n-4)n+14n,

which implies that

\displaystyle n^{2}-20n+2\leq 0,

contradicting to that
n\geq 20
. Put
B:=\{y\ |\ d_{H^{\prime\prime}}(y)<2(n-t-2)^{2}/3+4n\}

\displaystyle(m-2)n^{2}+2n+2

\displaystyle\leq e(H-\{u,v\})

\displaystyle<(m-1)n^{2}-(t+|B^{\prime}|)(m-1)n+(|B^{\prime}|+t)(2(n-t-2)^{2}/3+4n)+(|B^{\prime}|+t)(t+2)n

\displaystyle\leq(m-1)n^{2}-(t+3)(m-1)n+(t+3)(2(n-t-2)^{2}/3+4n)+(t+3)(t+2)n

\displaystyle\leq(m-1)n^{2}-4(m-1)n+4(2(n-t-2)^{2}/3+4n)+12n

\displaystyle\leq(m-1)n^{2}-4(n-4)n+8(n-3)^{2}/3+28n

\displaystyle=(m-2)n^{2}-n^{2}/3+28n+24,

contradicting to that

\delta_{1}(H^{\prime\prime})>4n
and

Subcase 2.2

|W\cap(V_{2}\cup V_{3})|=1
.

Without loss of generality, suppose that

Note that

\displaystyle(m-1)n^{2}+3n-m

\displaystyle\leq e(G)

\displaystyle\leq(m-2)n^{2}+d_{G}(x)+d_{G\setminus\{W\setminus\ \{w\}\}}(w)

\displaystyle\leq(m-2)n^{2}+d_{G}(x)+(n-m+1)n,

\displaystyle d_{H-(W\backslash\{u\})}(u)+d_{H-(W\backslash\{u\})}(v)\geq e(H)-(m-1)n^{2}\geq 3n-m>2n.

\displaystyle=e(G-V(\mathcal{M}_{1}))

EPSO-based rigid robotic arm for obstacle avoidance object grasping
Authors: Tong Xu, Xiaomin Han, Honghui Liu, Yuhong Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The smart orchard tournament of the robot developer competition requires participants to utilise a robotic arm equipped with a monocular camera to grasp ping-pong balls bearing fruit patterns. Common issues include low object recognition accuracy, inefficient performance, and grasping failures due to collisions with obstacles. This paper proposes a novel object grasping framework, EPSO-TPPP-YOLOv8, based on a rigid robotic arm for the task of fruit picking. In the safe distance analysis section, the two obstacles, cylinder and sphere, are first modelled. The robotic arm is then considered as an object consisting of several cylinders. The formula for calculating the safe distance between the robot arm and the obstacle is provided, and the design of the obstacle avoidance path is guided according to the calculation results. In the robot arm path planning section, the hyper-parameters of the angles of each robot arm joint are first determined, and then particle swarm optimization based on a novel set of strategies and evaluation criteria is used to generate the desired paths. The findings reveal that, in the absence of human intervention, the proposed EPSO algorithm reliably identifies paths that adhere to safety boundary limits within 50 iterations. The EPSO-TPPP framework, when operated under human guidance, has been demonstrated to achieve this within 10 iterations. In the subsequent phase of vision-based object recognition phase, a distinctive dataset is constructed based on the specified task. It is an established fact that simulation and field experiments have demonstrated the efficacy of the robot arm’s obstacle-avoidance grasping functionality.

Introduction

Motivation

This article draws inspiration from the smart orchard tournament of the robot developer competition, which requires participants to grasp ping-pong balls with fruit labels using a robotic arm equipped with a monocular camera. The task can be subdivided into two distinct subtasks: first, how to localize the target objects with the monocular camera, and second, how to grasp the object efficiently and accurately with the robotic arm.

Related work

In the domain of robotic arm path planning, tournament demands frequently manifest as inverse kinematic control problems. The objective of this study is to ascertain the values of all joint angle variables that are necessary for the end effector to be able to achieve the desired attitude given the relative attitude of the robot end effector. This is a redundant multivariate strongly coupled nonlinear problem, and is also a common problem faced by robotic arm control. Two principal methodologies are available for the investigation of inverse kinematic solutions: numerical and analytical. Among the most prevalent numerical solution methods are the pseudo-inverse method1,2, the Jacobi transpose method3 and the damped least squares method4. These methods are employed to approximate the inverse of the Jacobi matrix. However, numerical solution methods may yield significant oscillations and numerical stability issues when the end-effector is unable to reach the specified object position. The analytic method necessitates the construction of distinct inverse kinematic analytic expressions for each robot, which is inherently limited in its generality and applicability. For instance, Pieper’s criterion must be satisfied. In response to the aforementioned limitations, some scholars have transformed the inverse kinematic solution problem into a path planning problem under multiple constraints, such as polynomial interpolation5 and spline trajectory planning6. The mathematical models of these algorithms can be considered as polynomials. However, if the order is too small, it will lead to a large error in the motion of the end of the robot arm and a certain potential risk of collision between the robot arm and the obstacle. Conversely, if the order is too large, it will easily lead to the problems of tedious computation and superparameters that cannot be easily determined. The most significant challenge associated with this type of algorithm is that it solely concentrates on the kinematic characteristics of the robotic arm, with no consideration for the surrounding environment. This renders this type of control method incapable of avoiding obstacles, and the robotic arm is susceptible to contact with obstacles when grasping objects, resulting in grasp failures. A plethora of alternative path search algorithms have been proposed, including sampling-based search algorithms7, swarm optimization-based search algorithms8 and heuristic search-based algorithms9. The particle swarm algorithm (PSO) is an exemplary multi-parameter optimization algorithm that has been enhanced by researchers. For instance, Chen et al.10 propose the constant inertia weight and equally spaced initial particle position strategy based on PSO, and demonstrate the efficacy of the algorithm on eight known benchmark test functions. The algorithm requires 1000 iterations with 30 initial particles. The proposal of PSO with equally spaced inertia weights is also put forward for the optimisation of sensor networks11 and engineering structure design12. The focus of this type of literature is exclusively on the quality of the planned paths. However, in the context of a competition, the efficiency of the object grasping process must also be considered. Additionally, given that the robotic arm cannot be regarded as a mass, it is also necessary to construct a robotic arm model and consider the safe distance during its movement.

In the field of image recognition, traditional vision-based methods encompass a multitude of factors, including geometry, shape, color, and texture. The template matching method employed by the majority of teams in the competition is also based on this concept. For example, the transform line detection method, as described in13, is employed by the mobile campus intelligent robot. However, this type of algorithm in question is neither valid nor reliable, being significantly affected by the angle of light, and thus time-consuming for grasping. Consequently, some researchers have employed a deep learning-based approach for object detection. For example, Chen et al.14 propose a faster regression convolutional neural network framework and demonstrate its efficacy for vehicle location. Wei et al.15 present an online multiple object tracking method based on a minimum cost flow in the temporal window for autonomous driving. It is evident that existing object detection frameworks are capable of achieving accurate object detection. Nevertheless, the viability of devising an obstacle avoidance trajectory for the object to be captured remains uncertain.

Theory

Framework

The proposed robot arm object grasping framework comprises two stages: the simulation stage and the field experiments stage, as illustrated in Fig. 1. In the initial stage of the proposed framework, the obstacles and the robotic arm are first modelled, and a strategy for calculating the safe distance is proposed. Subsequently, the optimal parameters are obtained by the proposed enhanced PSO (EPSO) algorithm, and finally, the planned path is evaluated. In the field experiment stage, a unique dataset is first constructed based on the given task and trained on that dataset to obtain the trained model. Subsequently, the target object is to be captured by a monocular camera, the purpose of which is determine its relative position in relation to the robotic arm. Subsequently, the safe distance calculation and path planning are performed.Fig. 1The robotic arm object grasping framework.

Hyperparameter determination

It is acknowledged that there are a number of distinct modelling approaches for rigid robotic arms. These include the D-H parametric method and the URFD-based modelling method. Following the establishment of the geometry of the robotic arm model has been established, the spatial position of the linkage and end effector can be determined, contingent on the determination of the respective joint angles. This finding indicates that the robotic arm’s efficacy in grasping an object is contingent upon the joint angles, thereby establishing the joint angles as the hyperparameters to be optimized in this paper.

Enhanced particle swarm optimization for path planning

(1) Particle swarm optimization.

From the kinematic characteristics of the robot arm, it can be seen that the optimal parameters are six joint angles, which represents a class of multi-parameter optimization problem. Particle swarm optimization (PSO) is a well-known swarm optimization algorithm. The fundamental principle of the algorithm is to identify the optimal solution through the collective collaboration and information sharing among individuals within a population. For the purposes of this discussion, we will consider a population of m particles in the D-dimensional search space. The RRT* algorithm has been demonstrated to exhibit no discernible advantage in terms of expediting the generation of paths in conventional path planning tasks. This may be due to the fact that the algorithm permits the robotic arm to search freely within three-dimensional space, resulting in a broad search domain. However, the feasible solution range is quite restrictive (with a fixed search step size equal to the length of the robotic arm’s four limbs, a fixed number of search iterations set to four, and constraints on joint angles). Consequently, it is challenging to identify a viable solution within a constrained timeframe.

CHOMP is a gradient-based trajectory optimisation programme. Within the CHOMP framework, the minimum distance from obstacles can be engineered. Consequently, should a feasible solution be found, a 100% probability of safety margin can be achieved. However, the presence of local minima resulting from random initial values has the potential to compromise the efficacy of the algorithm, thereby impeding the search for collision-free paths.

DMP is a classical method that is applied to the process of robotic arm path planning. The optimisation process is comprised of two stages. Initially, the robot arm is guided by an experienced operator to generate a collision-free path. Secondly, the system state is altered via a self-stabilising second-order dynamic system, thus optimising the planned path. The findings indicate that, despite DMP’s proficiency in generating collision-free paths, the probability of safety margin is less than expected. The phenomenon under investigation is that of DMP enhancing the smoothness of the path, in a manner comparable to the implementation of a PD controller. However, it does not regulate the convergence process variable, i.e. trajectory shape. Consequently, this may result in the safety margin being unlikely to fall below the desired value. When considered in conjunction with inherent control accuracy errors in the hardware, this has the potential to result in collisions between the real vehicle and the robotic arm during experiments.

TP is a handheld device that can be used for manual robot manipulation, program writing, parameter configuration, and monitoring. The TP-based path planning (TPPP) algorithm is analogous to the first stage of the DMP algorithm. A total of 20 experiments were conducted by five students participating in the competition by using the TPPP. The findings of the study demonstrated that, while the demonstration system had the capacity to assist in the generation of collision-free initial paths, such paths exhibited a low probability of meeting safety margin criteria.

A comparison of the aforementioned algorithms reveals that EPSO exhibits a considerable advantage in terms of probability of collision-free path and the safety margin not less than \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$d_{{{\text{ob}}}}$$\end{document}, irrespective of the utilisation of TPPP. Furthermore, it has been demonstrated that with TPPP, EPSO can attain favourable outcomes with a limited number of iterations.

(3) Computational cost analysis.

A total of ten experiments are conducted, with five each for the traditional PSO algorithm (which does not take into account the relationship between the robotic arm and the obstacles) and the strategy proposed in this paper. In a scenario comprising four cylindrical obstacles (cf. Fig. 6, in which picking one fruit cluster treats the vine and the other three fruit clusters as obstacles), with 200 particles and 50 iterations, the PSO algorithm completed the task on average in approximately 0.3 s. In contrast, the EPSO algorithm required an average of approximately 7.1 s. The augmented computational expense can be attributed to the EPSO algorithm’s imperative to examine the spatial interrelationships between the robotic arm’s joints and obstacles, resulting in substantial delays in algorithm simulation. It has been demonstrated that the number of obstacles in the system is directly correlated with the time required for completion. It is evident that an increase in obstacles results in a corresponding increase in delay. However, given that the simulation branches utilised in the competition are identical, and the QR code situated beneath the base facilitates the robot’s ability to ascertain its own distance and orientation relative to the fruit branch (cf. Fig. 3). Consequently, during the competition, there is no need for real-time monitoring of obstacles within the robot’s field of vision; it is sufficient to detect the location, variety, and ripeness of the fruit.

Discussion

This paper presents a robotic system for fruit recognition and picking, which has been designed for robot competitions. The model integrates a robotic inverse kinematics model with an improved PSO algorithm for path planning. A dataset was collected and trained to identify fruit locations and varieties using YOLOv8, meeting competition requirements.

However, it should be noted that the paper’s achievements are subject to certain limitations. Firstly, the robotic arm path planning problem examined herein is fundamentally an optimisation problem involving six joint angle parameters, without consideration of the sequential relationships between different joint movements. The simplification of this strategy and its successful application are predicated upon the absence of any obstacles between the robotic arm and the fruit. Secondly, the fruit recognition problem addressed in this project is minimally affected by external factors, thereby enabling the existing framework to perform effectively. However, in real outdoor scenarios, lighting conditions, morphological characteristics, weather conditions, and complex backgrounds significantly impact fruit detection. This finding suggests a potentially fruitful research direction for future studies.

Conclusions

In the context of the simulation test stage, this paper proposes a novel safe distance calculation strategy for path safety analysis and a novel strategy and evaluation criteria based on the particle swarm optimization. The results of the comparison demonstrate that the proposed robotic arm path planning strategy exhibits superior convergence properties. In the field test stage, the comparison results demonstrate that the proposed robotic arm grasping framework exhibits enhanced stability and accuracy.

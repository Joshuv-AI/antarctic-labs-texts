Degradation of enrofloxacin by Fe0 activated PDS
Authors: Yaru Zhang, Zhenguo Li, Wenlong Bi, Bo Sun, Huanhuan Pei, Junmei Qin, Fenwu Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
In this paper, the effect of zero-valent iron (Fe0) activated persulfate (PDS) on the removal of enrofloxacin (ENR) was investigated, and the effect and mechanism were analyzed by exploring the effects of Fe0 concentration, PDS concentration, pH, and the influence of anion and aqueous matrix on the removal of ENR by the Fe0/PDS system. The results showed that when [ENR] = 20 µmol/L, [Fe0] = 0.15 g/L, [PDS] = 0.4 mmol/L, the removal rate of ENR was 85.3% at 90 min, the mainradicals were HO•, SO4•− and O2•−. At the same time, the system had a good mineralization effect (TOC removal rate > 40%), in addition, the system did not show obvious toxicity to soil microorganisms after the reaction, furthermore the Fe0/PDS system had a good removal effect on ENR in a wide pH range (4 ≤ pH ≤ 10). There is basically no difference in the removal rate of Fe0/PDS system in ultrapure water and river water. The results of this experiment could provide a reference for the removal of antibiotics based on advanced oxidation techniques based on SO4•−.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-78510-3.

Introduction

Since the introduction of the first antibiotic, penicillin, antibiotic drugs have been widely used in the medical, livestock and aquaculture industries due to their highly effective bacteriostatic and bactericidal effects1. But large amounts of antibiotics and their metabolites discharged into water bodies can cause serious environmental problems of water pollution2,3. The persistence of antibiotics in the environment not only causes direct harm to water microorganisms, plants and animals, but also threatens soil microorganisms when it flows into the soil4,5. Fluoroquinolone antibiotics (FQs) as a broad-spectrum class of antibiotics are among the most consumed antibiotics in the world6, of these, enrofloxacin (ENR) is frequently used because of its effectiveness7. In addition 26 antibiotics from five classes in the Fen River basin were analysed and it was found that enrofloxacin had a significant risk of antibiotic resistance throughout the basin, with a mean concentration of 47.78 ng/L8. When antibiotics enter the natural environment, they not only pollute the environment, but also cause harm to human health through the food chain9,10. It is reported that antibiotics kill more people each year indirectly than AIDS and malaria11. The massive use of antibiotics can lead to drug resistance in humans, posing a great threat to human health and the ecological environment12.

Currently, the main methods for removing antibiotics are adsorption, ozone oxidation and advanced oxidation, of which adsorption is the simplest, but it is unable to degrade the pollutants, and ozone oxidation technology has a rapid response, but requires high costs13. In addition, advanced oxidation process (AOPs) can efficiently degrade organic pollutants14–16, AOPs includes advanced oxidation technologies based on sulphate radicals (SO4•−) and hydroxyl radicals (HO•), the latter of which is mainly activated by H2O2, but operate at a narrow pH (3–4) and is less utilized, increasing economic costs in practice17–19. Furthermore, advanced oxidation technology based on SO4•− has advantages such as high redox potential, wide pH range, long half-life in the environment, and long duration20, making it more suitable for practical operation21. In this study, SO4•− advanced oxidation technology was selected to degrade ENR. Moreover, the structures of peroxodisulfate (PDS) and permonosulfate (PMS) contain O-O bonds, both of which could produce SO4•−, while PDS has a symmetrical molecular structure, is more stable and persists longer in the environment22,23. PDS can be activated by various means such as electrical, optical, thermal, ultrasonic, magnetic and transition metal activation24. In addition, transition metal iron is rich in content and is the most studied and used metal species, but iron ions often form difficult to treat iron sludge, and iron salts introduce anions, which have an impact on system degradation. While Fe0 as a transition metal catalyst has been used extensively due to its low price, environmental friendliness and ability to mineralize pollutants25,26. Fe0 in the PDS system will dissolve Fe2+, Fe2+ and PDS after the reaction of Fe3+ will react with zero-valent iron to form Fe2+27,28. This reduces the hydrolysis and precipitation caused by the accumulation of Fe3+ in the system.

In this study, we used the Fe0/PDS system for the removal of enrofloxacin with enrofloxacin as the target substance and analyzed the factors influencing the removal effect of ENR, including the initial concentration of PDS and Fe0, the initial pH before the reaction, the interfacial effects, the anions and so on. This study could provide a reference for the removal of quinolone antibiotics from the water environment and is important for the development and utilization of ecological water resources.

Materials and methods

Chemicals and materials

ENR (C19H22FN3O3) was purchased from Shanghai McLean Biochemical Science and Technology Co., Ltd; Fe0 was purchased from Tianjin Komeo Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd; Sodium persulfate, sodium bicarbonate, sodium chloride, ferrous sulfate were all produced by Tianjin Zhiyuan Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd; Concentrated sulfuric acid was produced by China National Pharmaceutical Chemical Reagent Co. The water is taken from the Wuma River, a tributary of the Fen River in Taigu County, Shanxi Province. All the reagents in this experiment were analytically pure, and all the water used in the experiment was deionised water.

Experiments and analyses

The initial concentration of ENR was 20 µmol/L (Figure S1 shows the standard curve of ENR). Different concentrations of Fe0 were weighed and placed in quartz tubes, 50 mL of prepared enrofloxacin solution was added to each quartz tube, and then different concentrations of PDS were added separately, and the whole reaction was carried out in a constant temperature water bath photochemical reaction chamber (Nanjing Xujiang Electromechanical Factory) under light-free conditions, with the temperature controlled at 20 ± 0.5°C, and the pH was adjusted using 0.5 mol/L H2SO4 and 0.5 mol/L NaOH for pH adjustment. The response surface method in JMP software was used to derive the optimal concentrations of Fe0 and PDS. The samples were analysed for their mineralisation effects using a Total Organic Carbon (TOC) analyser. Samples were taken at 5 min, 10 min, 20 min, 30 min, 45 min, 60 min, 90 min and 1 mL was passed through a 0.45 μm filter membrane and burst with 100 µL methanol. The ENR concentration was then detected by a high performance liquid chromatograph (LC-20AD) equipped with a C18 column (150 mm×4.6 mm, 5 μm), with a mobile phase of 0.1% formic acid: acetonitrile = 70:30 (V/V), a total flow rate set to 0.8 mL/min, and a detection wavelength set to 278 nm, for a detection time of 5 min. To determine the product toxicity, soil microbial community was used as a source of mixed strains, the solution after addition of the reaction was incubated for a fixed time for sampling and the OD600 value was determined spectrophotometrically to indicate the biotoxicity29,30. The experimental data were analysed using Microsoft Excel 2019 to calculate the mean and standard deviation.

Results and discussion

Removal of ENR by Fe0/PDS system

Effect of PDS and Fe0 dosage on the degradation of ENR

The effect of different concentrations of PDS on the removal of ENR was explored (Fig. 1a), after 90 min, the concentration change of ENR was minimal and almost negligible when the PDS concentration was 0.2, 0.3, 0.4 mmol/L, respectively, and the results showed that PDS alone had basically no effect on the removal of ENR. The removal effect of Fe0 on ENR was shown in Fig. 1b, where no PDS was added to the system, besides, the residual rates of ENR were 68.9% and 64.6% at Fe0 concentrations of 0.1 g/L and 0.2 g/L, respectively, within 90 min, which proved that Fe0 alone had a certain removal effect on ENR2. Meanwhile, EPR electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) experiments proved that the system produced a small amount of HO•,but no SO4•−(Fig. 1c), no obvious O2•− production was detected (Figure S2). Fe2+ dissolved in aqueous solution of Fe0 reacts with the PDS to produce HO•, SO4•−, which promotes the removal efficiency of ENR31. The residual rates were 66.4% and 67.6% when the dosage continued to increase to 0.3 g/L and 0.4 g/L, respectively. It may be due to the excess Fe0 dissolved too much Fe2+ thanradicals, and Fe2+ reacted with the radicals to reduce the radical content and slow down the ENR removal.

Concentration optimisation of Fe0/PDS system for ENR removal

The concentration ranges of Fe0 and PDS were determined by analysing the results of ENR removal in Fe0, PDS and Fe0/PDS systems. PDS concentrations were 0.2–0.4 mmol/L and Fe0 concentrations were 0.1–0.4 g/L, and then the ENR removal rate at 90 min was fitted by JMP software (Fig. 2). In addition, the visualisation analysis yielded that the removal of ENR was in the range of 80-93% at 90 min when the [Fe0] = 0.15 g/L and [PDS] = 0.4 mmol/L. With the increase of Fe0 concentration, the removal of ENR firstly increased and then decreased, which may be due to the fact that with the increase of Fe0 concentration, the dissolved Fe2+ was more and more, and moreradicals were produced, but when the Fe2+ concentration was too high than the PDS, the Fe2+ would compete with the ENR for the consumption of SO4•−, and the inhibition of the reaction by the Fe2+ would occur32.

Free radical contribution to ENR removal by Fe0/PDS system

In order to investigate which radicals were mainly produced in the Fe0/PDS system, ethanol, tert-butanol, and p-benzoquinone were used to burst the radicals, respectively; ethanol could burst both HO• and SO4•−, tert-butanol could burst HO•34, and p-benzoquinone could burst O2•− (pH before and after reaction was shown in Table S2). As shown in Fig. 4, the results proved that after 90 min of reaction of Fe0/PDS system, the residual rate of ENR in the Fe0/PDS system with the addition of tert-butanol was 29.8%, and compared with the control, HO• played a role of 14.3% in the system, and the residual rate of ENR with the addition of ethanol was 81.1%, and it can be concluded that the contribution of SO4•− contributed 51.3% to the ENR removal, and in summary, the radicals that played a major role in the system were HO• and SO4•−. It should be that the Fe2+ dissolved from Fe0 caused the O-O single bond in PDS to break to produce a large amount of SO4•− through activation of PDS (Eq. 1), at the same time, the SO4•− produced could react with the aqueous solution to form HO• (Eq. 4)35. The EPR and burst experiments demonstrated that some O2•− was also produced in the simultaneous system (Fig. 4a and c), presumably by the reaction of Fe0 or SO4•− with dissolved oxygen36,37. It was also possible that the reaction of persulfate ions with water produced O2•− 28, whichaccelerated the removal of ENR from the system.

Effect of pH on the removal of ENR by Fe0/PDS system

In order to investigate the effect of different pH on the removal rate of ENR in Fe0/PDS system, the changes in the concentration of ENR were measured under different pH conditions respectively (Fig. 5, besides, pH before and after the reaction was shown in Table S3) and pH in the degradation of ENR in Fe0/PDS system (Figure S4). CK in the Fig. 5 indicates unadjusted pH. The results showed that, except at pH = 2, the removal rate of ENR was 55.2% in 90 min, which was low, moreover, in a wide range (pH = 4–10) the removal rate was around 80%, all of them had good removal effect. Overall, the removal rate of ENR under alkaline conditions was better than that under acidic conditions, and the best results were obtained under alkaline conditions (pH = 8), which may be attributed to the formation of hydroxide complexes between the trivalent iron and water under alkaline conditions, which adsorbed a part of the ENR, and the removal rate of ERN was increased38,39. In addition, ENR was well removed under alkaline conditions due to the predominance of the ENR amphipathic ionic state under alkaline conditions, pKa1 ≈ 6.0, pKa2 ≈ 8.540. When pH was lower than pKa1, ENR existed mainly in cationic form in the solution, while pH was higher than pKa2, ENR dominated in anionic form in the solution, with higher quantum yield and better reaction effect. Furthermore, when the solution pH was kept between pKa1 and pKa2, ENR existed as an amphipathic ion in the solution and was more easily to be adsorbed. Besides, at pH 2–6, the removal rate was reduced due to the strong electrostatic repulsion between ENR and positively charged Fe2+. Similarly, the removal of ENR was relatively high when the pH was higher than 8, which was attributed to the electrostatic attraction between positively charged Fe2+ and ENR. In addition, acidic conditions favoured the dissolution of Fe2+ and promoted ROS production41, however, in an overly acidic environment H+ becomed a scavenger of HO• and SO4•− leading to a reduction in ENR removal42. It is also possible that the rapid dissolution of Fe2+ consumed PDS in the early stage and produced excessive SO4•− and HO•, and the large amount of SO4•− and HO• produced caused some of them not to participate in the reaction in time, and then self-burst and accumulation occurred, resulting in excessive consumption of PDS in the early stage and insufficient production of SO4•− and HO• in the late stage which slowed down the removal of ENR, and also hindered the conversion of Fe2+ to Fe3+ under over-acidic conditions, and Fe2+ competed with ENR for ROS, resulting in a reduction of ROS content43.

Influence of interfacial effects

From Figure S5, it can be seen that neither ferrous nor total iron content exceeded 24 mg/L. Based on this, in order to further investigate the interfacial effect of zero-valent iron, 4 mmol/L PDS and 24 mg/L of Fe2+ were added to the 20 µmol/L ENR solution to form the Fe2+/PDS homogeneous system (Fig. 6).

From Fig. 6, it can be seen that the removal trend of ENR in the Fe2+/PDS system was rapid and then flat, the rapid removal in the first 10 min may be due to the rapid generation of a large amount of degradation of ENR by PDS under the action of the high concentration of activator in the early stage. However, the removal trend gradually slowed down in 10–90 min, probably because there were a large amount of ENR intermediates which could competed with ENR for SO4•−. In addition, the removal rate of ENR was only 67.8% after 90 min of reaction in Fe2+/PDS system, which was much lower than that of 85.3% in Fe0/PDS system, indicating that the solid-liquid interfacial effect of Fe0/PDS system also had a certain effect on the removal of ENR. It was hypothesised that Fe0 could react with Fe3+ to reduce Fe3+ to Fe2+ (Eq. 3), which in turn reacted with PDS to produce more SO4•− and HO• to degrade ENR44, hence for the same Fe2+ concentration, the non-homogeneous phase showed 20% higher removal than the homogeneous phase.

Effect of anions on ENR removal in Fe0/PDS system

Anions are widely present in the water column and deplete SO4•− and HO• in advanced oxidation processes, affecting the removal of pollutants from the system45,46. In this study, the effects of Cl−, NO3−, and HCO3− ions on ENR removal (pH before and after the anionic reaction, as shown in Table S4) were investigated. To investigate the effect of ion concentrations of 0, 0.1, 1, 10 and 100 times the concentration of ENR on the degradation of ENR in the system (Fig. 7).

The results showed that when the concentration ratios of Cl− to ENR were 1:0.1, 1:1, and 1:10, the removal rate in 90 min was not significantly different from that of 1:0, however, in the case of a concentration ratio of Cl− and ENR of 1:100, the ENR removal increased (Fig. 7a), probably because Cl− reacted with SO4•− and HO•, to produce Cl•, Cl2, Cl2•− and HClO (Eqs. 5–8), which can oxidize the pollutants to a certain extent then accelerate the removal of ENR47. Removal increased with increasing Cl− concentration because SO4•− could oxidize more Cl− and produce more chlorine radicals14.

The inhibitory effect of NO3− on the Fe0/PDS system on ENR increases with the concentrations of NO3− (Fig. 7b), probably due to the fact that the NO3− could react with the PDS and consume a part of the SO4•− (Eq. 9), which reduced the removal of ENR48. Furthermore, when the solution was acidic, NO3− could oxidize Fe2+ which may have led to a slight inhibition of ENR removal in the Fe0/PDS system.

With the increase of HCO3− concentration, the residual rate of ENR was lower, and the removal rate of ENR was 92.7% after 90 min (Fig. 7c). The reason may be that HCO3− will seize free radicals and inhibit the system48,49. However, after adding HCO3− ions, the aqueous solution was weakly alkaline (Table S4), and the higher the concentration of HCO3−, the closer the pH value to 8, and The system degraded best at pH = 8 (Fig. 5). Effect of Fe0/PDS system on ENR removal in different water matrices

In order to investigate the application effect of Fe0/PDS system in real water bodies, river water (the basic physicochemical properties of the water body were shown in Table S5), tap water and ultrapure water were used as the water matrix for the experiments, respectively (Fig. 8). The results shown that ENR removal in tap water was the worst (the removal rate of 73.7%), ultrapure water and river water in the removal rate was basically no difference (85.5%, 85.3%, respectively), however the removal of ENR in the tap water and river water was not as fast as in ultrapure water, which may be the tap water and river water environment was more complex than that of ultrapure water, and the existence of a number of anions compete for the radicals. The final difference in ENR removal between river water and ultrapure water was not significant, probably due to the higher salt content of river water, which corroded Fe0 and accelerated the dissolution of Fe2+50, while tap water contained more anions and microorganisms and less salt, the anions could consume some of the SO4•−, which could decrease the ENR removal rate.

Changes in soil microbial toxicity by the Fe0/PDS system

The experiments showed that the Fe0/PDS system was able to effectively mineralize ENR to CO2 and H2O (Figure S6). Inoculate the solution with bacterial solution after reaction and incubate it in a constant temperature shaking chamber for 24 h. The OD600 values of different time periods were measured to indicate the growth of soil microorganisms (Fig. 9), so as to speculate the difference of its toxicity to soil microorganisms, besides, the experiments were adopted to compare the different systems to observe the toxicity. From Fig. 9, it can be seen that after 24 h of reaction of water, ENR and Fe0/PDS, the toxicity of Fe0/PDS system is less than that of water and ENR, and the growth trend of each treatment was steadily increasing, which indicated that there was no significant difference in the number of microorganisms between treatments, and the toxicity change was not obvious, and the Fe0/PDS system will not increase the toxicity of the environmental microorganisms29,30.

The Fe0/PDS system removed ENR with little environmental biotoxicity. This will provide technical support for the efficient removal of quinolone antibiotics from wastewater using high valence oxidants based on sulfate radicals.

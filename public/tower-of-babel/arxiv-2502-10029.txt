Abstract
. If X X is a topological space and κ \kappa is a cardinal then 𝖡𝖠 κ ​ ( X ) \mathsf{BA}_{\kappa}(X) is the statement that for each pair A , B ⊆ X A,B\subseteq X of κ \kappa -dense subsets there is an autohomeomorphism h : X → X h:X\to X mapping A A to B B . In particular 𝖡𝖠 ℵ 1 ​ ( ℝ ) \mathsf{BA}_{\aleph_{1}}(\mathbb{R}) is equivalent the celebrated Baumgartner axiom on isomorphism types of ℵ 1 \aleph_{1} -dense linear orders. In this paper we consider two natural weakenings of 𝖡𝖠 κ ​ ( X ) \mathsf{BA}_{\kappa}(X) which we call 𝖡𝖠 κ − ​ ( X ) \mathsf{BA}^{-}_{\kappa}(X) and 𝖴 κ ​ ( X ) \mathsf{U}_{\kappa}(X) for arbitrary perfect Polish spaces X X . We show that the first of these, though properly weaker, entails many of the more striking consequences of 𝖡𝖠 κ ​ ( X ) \mathsf{BA}_{\kappa}(X) while the second does not. Introduction

Cantor’s celebrated theorem on countable dense linear orders states (in part) that given any two countable dense

Already it was known from the work of Abraham and Shelah

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In the remainder of this section we recall some preliminaries we will need. 6.
Open Questions

We feel that this general view of “Baumgartner type topological axioms” is underdeveloped in the literature and therefore we are intentionally generous in the number of questions we ask in a shameless plea to incite more researchers to consider them. In what follows we list these questions roughly in the order the relevant ideas appeared in the paper.

The first of these comes from the study of the weak Baumgartner axioms for different cardinalities. For instance, the following basic question is open.

Question 2
.

If
\kappa<\lambda
does
\mathsf{BA}^{-}_{\kappa}
follow from
\mathsf{BA}_{\lambda}^{-}
? Similarly, can Theorem
3.2
be improved to
\mathsf{BA}_{\kappa}^{-}
implies
\mathfrak{b}>\kappa
?

We note, as discussed in
[
17
]
, that for the full Baumgartner axioms for Polish spaces this is also open. For instance it is not known if
\mathsf{BA}_{\aleph_{1}}(2^{\omega})
follows from
\mathsf{BA}_{\aleph_{2}}(2^{\omega})
.

Also left open from Section 2 is whether the
\mathsf{BA}^{-}
axioms were ever equivalent to the full
\mathsf{BA}
axioms. A precise question along these lines is the following.

Question 3
.

Is
\mathsf{BA}_{\kappa}^{-}(2^{\omega})
equivalent to
\mathsf{BA}_{\kappa}(2^{\omega})
? What about for
\omega^{\omega}
?

Turning to cardinal characteristics there are many questions left open about the application of Baumgartner type axioms to the study of cardinal characteristics. In fact little is known beyond Theorem
3.2
above, and in particular aside from the bounding number we have very little information. A sample question along these lines is the following.

Question 4
.

Does
\mathsf{BA}^{-}_{\kappa}
imply
{\rm cov}(\mathcal{M})\neq\kappa
? Does
\mathsf{BA}
imply
{\rm cov}(\mathcal{M})>\aleph_{1}
?

Moving to the universal axioms we note that we showed many non-implications of the
\mathsf{U}_{\kappa}
axioms but few implications. Thus a general question is what do the
\mathsf{U}_{\kappa}
axioms actually imply? A test question along these lines is the following.

Question 5
.

What consequences of
\mathsf{U}_{\kappa}
or
\mathsf{U}_{\kappa,\lambda}(X)
hold? For instance do any of these axioms have an effect on the cardinal
\mathfrak{d}
?

We note here that in Shelah’s model discussed in Theorem
4.5
we have
{\rm non}(\mathcal{M})<{\rm cov}(\mathcal{M})
which is in some sense the defining cardinal characteristic in the Cohen model so
\mathsf{U}_{\aleph_{1}}
is independent of this inequality. However I don’t know of a model
\mathsf{U}_{\kappa}
for some
\kappa>\mathfrak{d}
except for the trivial case where
\kappa=2^{\aleph_{0}}
. Similarly one can ask whether
\mathsf{U}_{\aleph_{1}}
holds in other well known models given its failure in the Cohen and random models. For instance the following is open.

Question 6
.

Does
\mathsf{U}_{\aleph_{1}}
hold in the Miller, Sacks or Laver models?

Along the same lines we observed that Miller’s theorem from

-dense

. However I do not know whether there are
\aleph_{1}
-dense such in this model.

Question 7
.

Are there
\aleph_{1}
-dense
X,Y\subseteq 2^{\omega}
which strongly avoid each other in the Miller, Sacks or Laver models?

Since the Miller and Laver models are also models of
{\rm cov}(\mathcal{N})={\rm cov}(\mathcal{M})=\aleph_{1}

Question 8
.

Are there
2^{\aleph_{0}}
-dense
X,Y\subseteq 2^{\omega}
with

We also still do not fully understand the difference between strongly avoiding and totally avoiding. As such we ask the following.

Question 9
.

Is it consistent that there are

which are
2^{\aleph_{0}}
-dense and strongly avoid one another but no such which totally avoid one another?

As a test question to unravel the above we can also look at the following.

Question 10

How are avoiding, strongly avoiding and totally avoiding affected by forcing axioms like
\mathsf{MA}
and
\mathsf{PFA}

On the face of it the conjecture is somewhat ambitious, yet we make it in part to highlight how little is known.

Note that the condition described in Part 1 of the conjecture is a simple topological obstruction to a space satisfying
\mathsf{BA}_{\kappa}(X)
since any dense set intersecting the closed nowhere dense set cannot be mapped to one not intersecting it. Part of the conjecture therefore is that this simple obstruction in fact characterizes the provable failure of a Baumgartner like axiom.

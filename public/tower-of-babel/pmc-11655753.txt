Production of high purity 47Sc from proton irradiation of natural vanadium targets
Authors: Shelbie Jaylene Cingoranelli, Emily E. Putnam, Jean Pierre Appiah, Jason Rider, Logan Burnett, Suzanne E. Lapi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundScandium-47 is the therapeutic counterpart to the diagnostic radionuclides, 43Sc and 44Sc. Together, these form elementally matched theranostic nuclide pairs, but their incorporation into radiopharmaceuticals requires developing production techniques leading to radioscandium isotopes with high chemical and radionuclidic purity. Previous 47Sc production methods involved expensive, enriched titanium targets that require additional procedures for target recovery. This work investigates the irradiation of natural vanadium targets and the development of purification methods for high-purity 47Sc. Natural vanadium foils were used in cyclotron target configurations. Targets were irradiated with 24 MeV protons at currents of up to 80 µA. A purification method was developed by determining the Kd values of Sc, Cr, and V using MP-50 resin. The final purification method used MP-50 and CM resin columns to isolate the 47Sc from natV and co-produced 51Cr. Inductively Coupled Plasma Mass Spectrometry (ICP-MS), gamma-ray spectroscopy, and a DOTA titration were used to characterize the 47Sc product. ResultsTwo cyclotron targets were designed, a small-scale target for developing a purification procedure and a high-power target for scaled-up production. The high-power target maximum current was 80 µA of 24 MeV protons. The yield for an 8 h irradiation at 80 µA of 24 MeV protons, was 128.02 ± 11.1 MBq of 47Sc at End of Bombardment. The radionuclidic purity of 47Sc was 99.5 ± 0.2%. The purification using MP-50 and CM columns resulted in the removal of natV target and 51Cr contaminate in the final 47Sc product, with an average recovery of 72 ± 2.1% and a DOTA apparent molar activity of 7733 ± 155 MBq/µmol. ICP-MS results showed that all top-row transition metals were below the limit of detection (< 1 ppb) with the exception of Zn, which was 64.6 ± 10.3 ppb.ConclusionsA high-power cyclotron target capable of withstanding a proton current of 80 µA was developed. A novel separation method was developed for isolating the 47Sc from the vanadium target and the co-produced 51Cr contaminate. The final product characterization resulted in a chemically and radionuclidically pure 47Sc product with high recovery yields.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s41181-024-00321-8.

Background

Theranostic (therapeutic + diagnostic) strategies combine diagnostic and therapeutic radioisotopes that share the same target (e.g., PSMA) (Huclier-Markai et al. 2018; Jones et al. 2020). The diagnostic partner is either a positron-emitting (β +) agent for use in Positron Emission Tomography (PET), for example, 68Ga, or a low energy photon-emitting agent (ɣ) for use in Single Photon Emission Computed Tomography (SPECT), such as 99mTc (Brookhaven National Laboratory website 2021). The diagnostic imaging agent can effectively identify patients who would benefit most from the targeted therapy, which uses a radiopharmaceutical with the same targeting moiety but includes a therapeutic isotope, for example, 177Lu. The success of theranostic applications, exemplified by clinical pairs such as [68Ga]Ga-PSMA-11 and [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617, underscores the efficacy of this approach and continues to drive exploration into potential theranostic radionuclides (Jones et al. 2020). Using two radioisotopes of the same element could be advantageous as this would create chemically identical radiopharmaceuticals, resulting in identical pharmacokinetics (Huclier-Markai et al. 2018; Kilian and Pyrzyńska 2023).

Scandium radionuclides have emerged as promising candidates due to their potential to form isotopically matched theranostic pairs (Huclier-Markai et al. 2018; Brookhaven National Laboratory website 2021; Kilian and Pyrzyńska 2023; Chernysheva et al. 2021) Table 1 provides the different radioscandium nuclides and their modes of decay (Huclier-Markai et al. 2018; Brookhaven National Laboratory website 2021; Kilian and Pyrzyńska 2023; Chernysheva et al. 2021; Mikolajczak et al. 2021). Scandium-43 (t1/2 = 3.89 h, β+ = 476 keV, 88.1%) and 44gSc (t1/2 = 3.97 h, β+ = 632 keV, 94.2%) are two radioscandium nuclides that can be used for PET imaging (Brookhaven National Laboratory website 2021; Becker et al. 2023; Greene and Hillman 1967; Meulen et al. 2015). Scandium-47 (t1/2 = 3.35 d, β− = 162 keV, 100%) is a therapeutic nuclide that can be employed in targeted radiotherapy (Huclier-Markai et al. 2018; Brookhaven National Laboratory website 2021; Kilian and Pyrzyńska 2023; Chernysheva et al. 2021). Scandium is chemically similar to lutetium, with both being trivalent rare earth metals, and has a comparable β− decay energy but shorter half-life than the clinically used 177Lu(β− = 133.6 keV, t1/2 = 6.64 d, 100%) (Brookhaven National Laboratory website 2021). The therapeutic potential of 47Sc has been shown to be comparable to 177Lu, but using 47Sc enables the creation of chemically identical radiopharmaceuticals when partnered with 43Sc or 44Sc, instead of chemically similar (but not identical) compounds using 43Sc/44Sc with 177Lu (Brookhaven National Laboratory website 2021; Müller et al. 2014, 2013; Siwowska et al. 2019; Umbricht et al. 2017). Creating chemically identical compounds supports precise, personalized medicine, as the diagnostic agent can reliably predict the localization and potentially therapeutic response of the therapeutic radiopharmaceutical. These often lead to additional co-produced radio contaminants and other undesired radioscandium isotopes, as listed in Table 1 and Supplemental Table S1 (Huclier-Markai et al. 2018; Brookhaven National Laboratory website 2021; Kilian and Pyrzyńska 2023; Chernysheva et al. 2021). Many production routes also require expensive enriched target materials that require high recovery recycling techniques (Becker et al. 2023; Majkowska-Pilip and Bilewicz 2011; Cingoranelli et al. 2023; Loveless et al. 2021, 2019). An alternative production route for 47Sc uses vanadium targets. This route produces 47Sc via nuclear reactions on 51V, particularly the 51V(p,p + α)47Sc reaction with protons below 40 MeV (Meiers et al. 2022; Snow et al. 2021; Barbaro et al. 2021; Hontzeas and Yaffe 2011; Jafari et al. 2019; Pupillo et al. 2019). Unlike other methods, this route eliminates the need for target material enrichment. The 51V isotope has a natural abundance of 99.75% and holds promise for achieving high radionuclidic purity 47Sc, representing a significant advantage over more conventional production routes. Additionally, 43Sc and 44gSc are more readily produced onsite, predominantly via medical cyclotrons and a potential generator via 44Ti/44Sc (Huclier-Markai et al. 2018; Chernysheva et al. 2021; Becker et al. 2023; Meulen et al. 2015; Cingoranelli et al. 2023; Benabdallah et al. 2023; Domnanich et al. 2017a; Eppard et al. 2017). These radionuclides are produced from enriched calcium or titanium targets, with recycling methods which enable routine production onsite for both preclinical and clinical applications. In contrast, 47Sc production is generally limited to large facilities capable of providing higher particle energies or fast/thermal neutrons, which results in limited availability of the radionuclide and logistical consideration for shipment and experimental planning. Production of both the diagnostic and therapeutic radionuclide onsite may facilitate more access for theranostic applications. Recent studies by Pupillo et al. and Barbaro et al. have reported cross-section results for 51V(p,p + α)47Sc with 50 MeV protons, while Ditrói et al. published cross-sections for 51V(γ,α)47Sc (Barbaro et al. 2021; Pupillo et al. 2019; Ditrói et al. 2016). The cross-sections reported in these papers demonstrate that 47Sc (in production yields of 1.01 MBq/µA/h) can be produced from proton-irradiated natV in high radionuclidic purity (exceeding 99%) using natural abundance targets. Meier et al. demonstrated the purification of 47Sc from photon-irradiated vanadium targets. (Meiers et al. 2022) However, production via V proton bombardment routes presents an additional challenge of removing the co-produced radionuclide 51Cr (t1/2 = 27.7 d, ε 100%).

The work presented here investigated the production of 47Sc using natV foils with 24 MeV protons. This included developing a target suitable for high-current irradiation on a medical cyclotron, developing a robust purification method, and determining the radionuclidic and chemical purity of the produced 47Sc.

Materials and methods

Unless otherwise stated, all chemicals used were analytical or trace metal grade. The supplemental information provides all supplier information, theoretical calculations, distribution coefficients (Kd) methods, and column preparation.

Target design

This study utilized two target configurations, as illustrated in Fig. 1. A 10.0 mm diameter (effective target size) foil was punched out of a 0.254 mm thick natV foil for the first target configuration. The resulting foil disc was carefully placed in a 1.0 mm deep divot cut-out at the center of a Ø 24 mm × 2.0 mm thick Ta coin. The V foil disc was secured using a retaining ring. The target assembly was then loaded into an Advanced Cyclotron Systems Inc. (ACSI, Richmond BC, Canada) 90° solid target holder) (Queern et al. 2017; Sayed et al. 2019). These targets were irradiated using an ACSI’s TR24 cyclotron at 24 MeV for 1 to 4 h with currents up to 40 µA.Fig. 1Target configurations used for 47Sc production from irradiation of natV targets

Target configurations used for 47Sc production from irradiation of natV targets

The second target design was developed to take advantage of the higher current, which could be accommodated using an in-house modified ACSI’s 30° solid target holder. A 24.0 mm × 40.0 mm × 2 mm aluminum target plate with a 0.25 mm recess was machined to accommodate a 0.25 mm thick sheet of natV without interfering with the helium, water cooling, and the load/unload mechanism. The target assembly was irradiated at 24 MeV for 4, 6, and 8 h with current ranges varying from 40 to 80 µA.

Target dissolution

Design B targets were cut after irradiation to 23 × 15 mm (the diameter of the beam window). V targets were dissolved in 7 M HNO3 at room temperature in a 50 mL glass beaker. The solution was slowly dried on a hot plate set at 160 °C. The dried sample was allowed to cool, 8 M HCl was added, and targets were reconstituted by heating using a hot plate set at 160 °C. The final dissolution volume for design A targets was 10 mL and for design B targets was 30 mL. After the material was fully reconstituted, the solution was set aside to cool.

Development of MP-50 separation

Small-scale studies were used to develop a procedure for removing the 51Cr contaminate from 47Sc. Design A (n = 3) was irradiated with 24 MeV protons at 30 µA for 1 h. Targets were dissolved into 8 M HCl, the solution was loaded onto the MP-50 column, and the flow through was collected (FT). The first wash (E1) consisted of 20 mL of 8 M HCl, collected in 10 mL fractions. The second wash (E2) was 10 mL of 4 M HCl, collected in 5 mL fractions. The last wash (E3) was 10 mL of Chelex water, collected in 5 mL fractions. The 47Sc elution (E4) was performed using 35 mL 1 M ammonium acetate pH 4 in 5 mL fractions. The first targetry scale-up used design A, irradiated at 40 µA for 4 h, using the same elution volumes. Addition of CM column

The incorporation of a second column was tested to exchange the purified 47Sc from the buffer solution into HCl. The separation using the MP-50 was performed as previously, and the CM resin column was fitted to the MP-50 column before the elution of 47Sc in 1 M ammonium acetate. After the elution of 47Sc in 1 M ammonium acetate directly onto the CM column, the 47Sc was eluted in 1 mL 2 M HCl (E5). The overall separation process is shown in Fig. 2B.

Scaled up production and separation using design B targets

The dissolved target in 8 M HCl was loaded onto an MP-50 column. Elutions of 20 mL 8 M HCl, 4 M HCl, and Chelex water were collected, respectively. The 47Sc was eluted 100 mL 1 M ammonium acetate pH 4, in two 50 mL fractions (E4 and E5), directly onto the 50 mg CM column. The CM column was washed with 20 mL Chelex water (E6) and 0.5 mL 0.1 M HCl (E7), before collecting 47Sc in 1 mL 2 M HCl (E8). The overall separation process is represented in Fig. 2C.

Final product characterization

All dissolved target solutions and subsequent elution fractions were analyzed by gamma spectroscopy using a High-Purity Germanium detector (HPGe). A 10 µL aliquot was taken and diluted in 990 µL water. The HPGe calibration was used using the source reported in Cingoranelli et al. (2023) All gamma-ray measurements had a deadtime < 5% and had counts ≥ 500 under each photopeak. Quantification of 46Sc was conducted at time points > 4 weeks post-irradiation. The radionuclidic purity was calculated as percentage of total radioactivity.

ICP-MS measurements were used to analyze fractions from the MP-50 separation, the adaptation of the CM column studies, and the scaled-up production studies. Samples were prepared using 20 µL aliquots of 2% nitric acid. Dissolved target solutions, the flow-through of the MP-50 column, and the 8 M HCl were diluted to 15 mL, where 500 µL target solution and flow-through ICP-MS samples were diluted a second time to 10 mL. All other ICP-MS samples were diluted to 5 mL of 2% nitric acid. ICP-MS standards were as in Cingoranelli et al. (2023).

The final quality test was performed using the 47Sc produced using target design B by performing a DOTA titration (Cingoranelli et al. 2023). The collected 47Sc was evaporated to dryness using a Smart Evaporator in a 5 mL PFA vial followed by reconstitution in 20 µL of 0.1 M HCl Cingoranelli et al. (2023)). Briefly, various concentrations of DOTA were labeled using the same volume and activity of 47Sc in 0.25 M ammonium acetate pH 4 at 95 °C, 800 RPM for 30 min. The yields were analyzed using spotted SG-iTLC plates developed in 1 M citric acid, where the RF of unlabeled 47Sc was 1, and the RF of the 47Sc-DOTA complex was 0.65.

Results

Theoretical and production yields

The theoretical yield for the targets irradiated with 24 MeV protons using design A, irradiated at 40 µA for 4 h was 30.3 MBq at end of bombardment (EOB), and experimental results under the same conditions were 30.6 ± 3.1 MBq at EOB. Distribution coefficient (Kd)

Supplemental Table S2 provides the reported Kd values. The Kd values demonstrate that Sc has a high affinity for MP-50 resin in HCl solutions, while V and Cr have a lower Kd value in high-molarity HCl solutions. These Kd differences were exploited to retain the Sc on the MP-50 column in high-molar HCl as V and Cr ions pass through.

Dissolution

Vanadium targets were readily dissolved in 7 M HNO3 at room temperature within 30 min into a blue solution, as shown in Fig. 3A. The target solution in nitric acid was brought to dryness by bring the solution to a gentle boil with a hot plate set at 160 °C, which took approximately 1.5 h for design A targets and 5 h for design B targets. A setpoint above 160 °C resulted in bumping, leading to a loss in target material and product. The dried solution had an orange appearance, as shown in Fig. 3B. After adding the 8 M HCl, the solution was set on a hot plate at 130 °C until the material was fully dissolved. Attempts at lower temperatures did not result in reconstitution. The color of the solution during reconstitution was yellow, as shown in Fig. 3C. Finally, after allowing the solution to cool, the final color of the solution was green, as shown in Fig. 3D. Increasing the dissolution and reconstitution solutions from 10 to 30 mL for targets from design B ensured complete dissolution.Fig. 3Target dissolution and reconstitution. Design A targets (n = 3) were irradiated for 1 h to produce 47Sc and 51Cr for small-scale studies to evaluate the elution profile of these two radionuclides using MP-50 resin. The 47Sc was eluted using 1 M ammonium acetate pH 4, with 59.5 ± 5% in the first 5 mL, 25.3 ± 2.7% in the second 5 mL, and 8.1 ± 5.4% in the third 5 mL. 47Sc characterization

The decay mode of 49Cr includes a 152 keV gamma-ray emission. Therefore, all 47Sc purification and quantification were performed after the decay of 49Cr, > 12 h after EOB. Quantification of long-lived 46Sc (t1/2 = 83.8 d) was performed > 4 weeks after EOB. The average radionuclidic purity of 47Sc at the end of the bombardment was 99.5 ± 0.2%, with representative gamma-ray spectra for the dissolved and purified solutions shown in Fig. 8. The average percentages of 46Sc and 48Sc in the final product were 0.05 ± 0.01% and 0.3 ± 0.03%, respectively. The ICP-MS trace metal analysis results for the recovered 47Sc showed that all the top-row transition metals were below the detection limit (< 1 ppb) except for Zn, which was 64.6 ± 10.3 ppb. Discussion

Scandium radionuclides are of interest for medical applications, particularly as a matched theranostic pair. The principal challenges lie in the production of high radionuclidic purity 47Sc. Current methods often rely on expensive enriched targets, necessitating recycling procedures to mitigate costs (Huclier-Markai et al. 2018; Kilian and Pyrzyńska 2023; Chernysheva et al. 2021; Mikolajczak et al. 2021; Becker et al. 2023; Cingoranelli et al. 2023). There has been growing interest in using natV targets as an alternative for 47Sc production, given that the target isotope is 99.75% naturally abundant (Meiers et al. 2022; Snow et al. 2021; Barbaro et al. 2021; Pupillo et al. 2019). Efforts have concentrated on understanding the nuclear cross-sections associated with this production route Snow et al. 2021; Barbaro et al. 2021; Jafari et al. 2019; Pupillo et al. 2019; Ditrói et al. 2016; Cingoranelli et al. 2024). However, exploration of separation chemistry is required to recover the 47Sc in a suitable form for downstream applications. The present study is centered on two primary objectives: first, the development of stable vanadium cyclotron targets capable of withstanding currents up to 80 µA, and second, the establishment of a separation method for purifying 47Sc from vanadium targets and the co-produced 51Cr.

Previous studies have utilized MP-50 resin for the purification of radioscandium from titanium targets, leveraging scandium's high affinity for this resin in HCl solutions (Loveless et al. 2019; Kolsky et al. 1999; Strelow 1984; Pietrelli et al. 1992). However, these studies lacked data concerning vanadium (in higher molarity acids) or chromium on MP-50 resins. Kd values were measured to devise a purification method for separating scandium from vanadium and chromium. In the HCl solution, Sc demonstrated a higher Kd value than V or Cr, indicating the potential utility of MP-50 as a column to capture the Sc ions effectively, while the lower affinity for V and Cr allows for immediate elution of these elements. For example, in 8 M HCl, the Kd value of Sc is 600, while the Kd values for V and Cr are 67 and 16, respectively. Strelow reported that in 0.1–6 M HCl, V(IV) Kd values ranged from 238 to 0.9, comparable to this work where V Kd values ranged from 300 to 25, both indicating that the V affinity for MP-50 in HCl decreases as the HCl molarity increases (Loveless et al. 2019; Kolsky et al. 1999; Strelow 1984; Pietrelli et al. 1992). Based on these findings, the loading solution in 8 M HCl was selected for high Sc retention and low V and Cr retention. Additional HCl washes were employed to remove residual V and Cr without losing Sc ions. This is supported by the data illustrated in the separation profile using MP-50, where the elution of natV and 51Cr is observed in the flow-through and 8 M HCl wash fractions.

Achieving effective separation with MP-50 resin hinges on the target material being in a high molar HCl solution, but vanadium foils do not readily dissolve in such conditions. A dissolution, dry down, and reconstitution process was devised to overcome this obstacle. All targets underwent complete reconstitution in 8 M HCl, with a final volume of 10 mL for design A targets and 30 mL for design B targets. Although the reconstitution process for design B targets took longer due to the increased volume, future refinements could expedite this process. For instance, implementing additional steps, such as adding the solution dropwise or altering design B targets with a target thickness of 0.127 mm instead of 0.254 mm, effectively halving the mass but maintaining an effective thickness for this slanted configuration, which would still capture the entire cross-section under 24 MeV, could reduce dissolution time.

The separation of 47Sc from both natV targets and 51Cr contaminants was achieved consistently using MP-50 resin for both target designs. The majority of the natV and 51Cr passed through the column directly during the loading process. This aspect proved pivotal for the purification method during targetry scale-up, particularly as the natV mass and 51Cr yields increased. By developing a purification method where the unwanted elements have a low affinity to the column during loading, the scale-up of the targetry was streamlined without necessitating alterations to column masses. This scalability is demonstrated with design B targets, where both column dimensions remain consistent throughout all experiments, and the scaled-up design B targets with larger masses still yielded similar results with natV and 51Cr still passing through the column. Increasing the 4 M HCl elution volume effectively removed the residual natV and 51Cr from these larger targets. The quantitative removal of these contaminates before the final 47Sc elution was verified by gamma-ray spectroscopy and ICP-MS analysis.

Subsequently, a second column was integrated into the system, following a procedure akin to that reported by Saini et al., which effectively traps the desired radionuclide onto a CM resin from a buffer solution (Saini et al. 2023). The 47Sc was immobilized onto the CM column from 1 M ammonium acetate elution from 40 to 100 mL elution volumes. Water and 0.1 M HCl washes were added to remove any residual buffer before elution of 47Sc using a 1 mL solution of 2 M HCl. The dried 47Sc collected from the separation in design A targets resulted in a small amount of dried ammonium acetate buffer < 50 mg. However, when the CM was washed with water and 0.1 M HCl before 47Sc elution, there was no residue in the dried 47Sc product.

Future refinement of this purification method will prioritize reducing the mass and volume of the MP-50 column. As demonstrated in this study, high molar HCl effectively traps 47Sc while allowing contaminants to pass through. Reducing the column mass could be achieved by employing a 1 mL SPE column, maintaining the column length at 4 cm but decreasing the diameter. These changes could decrease elution volumes, reduce purification time, and increase the 47Sc recovery. This study builds on work by Meier et al., which outlined a procedure for isolating 47Sc from natV targets irradiated with photons Meiers et al. (2022). This purification method used H2O2 added to the dissolved vanadium foil in 7 M HNO3 with a 6% H2O2 loading solution, followed by purification using three tandem BDGA resin columns Meier et al. (2022) reported yields as low as 50% for the first column, ranging from 71 to 93% for the second two BDGA columns, which is lower than the recovery yields reported here (Meiers et al. 2022). The current method employs two columns instead of three and consistently recovers ~ 70% of the 47Sc. However, the Meier et al. method includes a reduced dissolution and reconstitution time (< 10 min).

The final 47Sc quality was analyzed using ICP-MS and gamma-ray spectroscopy. The 47Sc radionuclidic purity was 99.5 ± 0.2%, surpassing previous cyclotron routes using enriched [50Ti]TiO2 targets, which reported purities of 91.5 ± 0.3% (Cingoranelli et al. 2023). The only other co-produced radioscandium nuclides from these targets were 46Sc and 48Sc, at low radionuclidic purities of 0.05 ± 0.01% and 0.3 ± 0.03%, respectively, which is produced in lower amounts than targets composed of enriched [50Ti]TiO2 at 2.3 ± 0.4% and 0.42 ± 0.6%, respectively (Cingoranelli et al. 2023). Notably, the most important decrease is the 46Sc amount, as it the contaminate of concern, being a longer-lived (83 d), β− emitting nuclide (β− = 111.8, 100%) with high gamma ray emission (889 keV I = 99%, 1120 keV I = 99%), and would pose for dosimetry concerns if present in significant quantities (Brookhaven National Laboratory website 2021). Photonuclear production using natTi targets resulted in 90.0 ± 0.1% pure 47Sc, while the photonuclear route using natV also yielded a higher radionuclidic purity at > 99.998% (albeit at low yields of 5.09 ± 0.1 nCi/g/µA/hr), indicating that natV targets can lead to a higher radionuclidic purity for either production route (Loveless et al. 2019; Snow et al. 2021; Mamtimin et al. 2015).

The ICP-MS results also indicate a high chemical purity of 47Sc, with Zn being the only measurable contaminate. The Zn contaminate could be removed by optimizing this purification method by finding a wash that could remove Zn without losing 47Sc. The chemical purity is reflected in the AMA reported here at 7733 ± 155 MBq/µmol using 47Sc produced during 6 h irradiations using 80 µA of 24 MeV protons at production yields of 82.9 ± 2.2 MBq at EOB. This AMA is higher than the previously reported AMA of 3367 MBq/µmol using enriched [50Ti]TiO2 targets with production yields of 84.7 MBq after 8 h irradiation with 20 µA of 24 MeV protons (Cingoranelli et al. 2023; Carbo-Bague et al. 2023).

The significance of this route lies in using naturally abundant vanadium targets, which yielded 47Sc in high radionuclidic purity without using enriched targets. These targets eliminate the extensive recycling procedures required with the enriched targets. Additionally, these targets can be obtained in foil form and can withstand higher beam energies and intensities, enhancing production efficiency. As technology improves, the ability to increase the proton intensity will directly lead to an increase in 47Sc yields, and the vanadium foils can be modified to withstand the increased heat deposition.

Conclusion

Scandium-47 was produced using natV foils irradiated with 24 MeV protons. These targets were developed to withstand a proton intensity of 80 µA for up to 8 h. A separation procedure was developed where MP-50 resin was used to isolate 47Sc from natV and 51Cr, and a CM column was employed to concentrate the scandium in 2 M HCl, which is suitable for downstream radiochemistry. The purity of the produced 47Sc was exceptional, with a radionuclidic purity of 99.5 ± 0.2%, high chemical purity, and a DOTA AMA of 7733 ± 155 MBq/µmol. Future directions should optimize the purification method developed here by reducing MP-50 resin mass and exploring strategies to enhance production yields, potentially using higher proton energies.

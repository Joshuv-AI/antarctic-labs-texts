Extending Linear Convergence of the Proximal Point Algorithm: The Quasar-Convex Case
José de Brito, Felipe Lara, Di Liu

Abstract
This work investigates the properties of the proximity operator for quasar-convex functions and establishes the convergence of the proximal point algorithm to a global minimizer with a particular focus on its convergence rate. In particular, we demonstrate: (i) the generated sequence is minimizing and achieves an 𝒪 ⁡ ( ε − 1 ) \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-1}) complexity rate for quasar-convex functions; (ii) under strong quasar-convexity, the sequence converges linearly and attains an 𝒪 ⁡ ( ln ⁡ ( ε − 1 ) ) \mathcal{O}(\ln(\varepsilon^{-1})) complexity rate. These results extend known convergence rates from the (strongly) convex to the (strongly) quasar-convex setting. To the best of our knowledge, some findings are novel even for the special case of (strongly) star-convex functions. Numerical experiments corroborate our theoretical results. Keywords : Nonconvex optimization; Proximal point algorithms; Linear convergence; Quasar-convexity Mathematics Subject Classification: 90C26; 90C30.

Introduction

Let
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
be a proper function. We are interested in solving the problem

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h(x),

(1)

via the
Proximal Point Algorithm
(PPA henceforth)
[
36
,
37
,
46
]
, a scheme which given
x^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a sequence of positive parameters
\{\beta_{k}\}_{k}
, generates a sequence as follows:

x^{k+1}\in{\rm argmin}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,\left(h(x)+\frac{1}{2\beta_{k}}\lVert x^{k}-x\|^{2}\right),~\forall~k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.

(2)

Despite its simplicity, PPA is a powerful tool in continuous optimization, as it allows us to simplify the original problem by solving better-conditioned subproblems. For instance, when
h
is convex, the function
x\mapsto h(x)+\frac{1}{2\beta_{k}}\lVert x^{k}-x\rVert^{2}
is strongly convex. This property makes solving the subproblems easier because proper, lower semicontinuous (lsc henceforth) strongly convex functions possess remarkable properties, such as a unique minimizer and quadratic growth. In fact, when
h
is convex, the sequence
\{x^{k}\}_{k}
generated by (
2
) is a minimizing sequence for problem (
1
). Specifically, it is decreasing, converges to the minimum (though not linearly), and achieves a complexity rate of
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-1})
. Moreover, if
h
is strongly convex, then
\{x^{k}\}_{k}
converges linearly to the unique solution with an improved complexity rate of
\mathcal{O}(\ln(\varepsilon^{-1}))
.

Due to these appealing properties, significant research efforts in recent decades have been devoted to extending the convergence guarantees of the PPA to broader function classes. Notable contributions include extensions to weakly convex and hypomonotone operators
[
13
,
26
,
44
]
; developments in DC programming
[
2
,
4
,
7
,
47
]
; and studies of (strongly) quasiconvex functions
[
17
,
30
,
43
]
. However, these frameworks fail to fully preserve the desirable properties of the convex case. For instance, PPA achieves only sublinear convergence for weakly convex and DC functions. Although linear convergence is possible for strongly quasiconvex functions, there is no evidence that PPA even converges to a minimizer in the general quasiconvex (not strongly) case
[
43
]
.

This paper bridges this gap by presenting a comprehensive extension of PPA from convex to quasar-convex functions. We establish global convergence to a minimizer and provide a detailed analysis of its convergence rate. This extension is compelling for three key reasons:

First, the quasar-convex class is a strict generalization of fundamental convexity classes. It encompasses all convex functions with a nonempty solution set
[
20
,
23
]
, all strongly convex functions as well as all (strongly) star-convex functions
[
42
]
.

Second, quasar-convexity arises naturally in numerous nonconvex applications, including machine learning
[
23
,
32
,
34
]
and stochastic optimization
[
10
,
53
]
. Furthermore, its favorable geometry is also crucial for accelerating gradient-based methods (see
[
20
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
50
]
and references therein).

Third, as we demonstrate, quasar-convexity provides a natural modeling framework for novel problems in microeconomics, statistics, and machine learning that are beyond the reach of convex models (see Subsection
3.1
).

Our Contribution
: We study (strongly) quasar-convex functions with modulus
\gamma\geq 0
. To that end, first we establish sufficient conditions for (nonsmooth) functions to be (strongly) quasar-convex. In particular, we show that the
\ell_{p}
-regularization (
0<p<1
)
[
52
]
and the CES (Constant Elasticity of Substitution)
[
3
]
, Cobb-Douglas
[
12
]
, and Leontief
[
33
]
production/utility functions are quasar-convex (and strongly quasar-convex on bounded convex sets). Second, we investigate the proximity operator of quasar-convex functions by proving that it is always nonempty (i.e., existence of iterates is guaranteed) and demonstrating cases where it is not a singleton. Furthermore, we establish a useful relationship between the minimizers of the proximity operator and the minimizers of the function. Third, we prove that the set of fixed points of the proximity operator coincides with the set of minimizers of a quasar-convex function, which provides the stopping criterion for PPA. Fourth, we implement PPA for quasar-convex functions (
\gamma\geq 0
) and show that the generated sequence is minimizing with a complexity rate of
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-1})
when
\gamma=0
, matching the rate for convex functions
[
19
]
. Fifth, for the special case of strongly quasar-convex functions (
\gamma>0
), we prove that PPA converges linearly to the unique solution and achieves an
\mathcal{O}(\ln(\varepsilon^{-1}))
complexity rate, exactly as in the strongly convex case. Finally, we present numerical experiments with nonconvex examples to demonstrate the potential of our results.

The paper is structured as follows: In Section
2
, we introduce preliminary concepts and basic definitions related to generalized convexity. In Section
3
, we study nonsmooth quasar-convex functions, analyze key properties of the proximity operator, and present illustrative examples with applications in machine learning and economics. In Section
4
, we implement the proximal point algorithm (PPA) and analyze its convergence behavior and complexity rates for both strongly quasar-convex and quasar-convex cases. In Section
5
, we provide numerical experiments on nonconvex problems, demonstrating the practical implications of our theoretical results. Finally, in Section
6
, we conclude with a discussion of our findings and outline potential directions for future research.

Preliminaries and Basic Definitions

The inner product of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the Euclidean norm are denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and
\lVert\cdot\rVert
, respectively. The open ball with center at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and radius
\varepsilon>0
is denoted by
\mathbb{B}(x_{0},\varepsilon)
. We denote
\mathbb{R}_{+}:=[0,+\infty[
and
\mathbb{R}_{++}:=\,]0,+\infty[
, hence
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}:=[0,+\infty[\,\times\ldots\times[0,+\infty[
and
\mathbb{R}_{++}^{n}:=\,]0,+\infty[\,\times\ldots\times\,]0,+\infty[
(
n
times), respectively. Let
K
be a nonempty set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, its closure is denoted by
\overline{K}

and its asymptotic (recession) cone by

K^{\infty}:=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:~\exists~t_{k}\rightarrow+\infty,~\exists~x_{k}\in K,~\frac{x_{k}}{t_{k}}\rightarrow u\}
. Furthermore, for any set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it follows from
[
5
, Proposition 2.1.2]
that

K^{\infty}=\{0\}
if and only if
K
is bounded.

Given any
x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, the following relations hold:

\displaystyle~~~~~\langle x-z,y-x\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\lVert z-y\rVert^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\lVert x-z\rVert^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\lVert y-x\rVert^{2},

(3)

\displaystyle\lVert\beta x+(1-\beta)y\rVert^{2}=\beta\lVert x\rVert^{2}+(1-\beta)\lVert y\rVert^{2}-\beta(1-\beta)\lVert x-y\rVert^{2}.

(4)

Given any extended-valued function
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, the effective domain of
h
is defined by
{\rm dom}\,h:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:h(x)<+\infty\}
. It is said that
h
is proper if
{\rm dom}\,h
is nonempty and
h(x)>-\infty
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The notion of properness is important when dealing with minimization problems.

We denote by
{\rm epi}\,h:=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}:h(x)\leq t\}
the epigraph of
h
, by
S_{\lambda}(h):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:h(x)\leq\lambda\}
the sublevel set of
h
at the height
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and by
{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h
the set of all minimal points of
h
. A function
h
is lsc at
\overline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
if for any sequence
\{x_{k}\}_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
x_{k}\rightarrow\overline{x}
, we have
h(\overline{x})\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}h(x_{k})
.

A function
h
with convex domain is said to be

(a)

(strongly) convex on
{\rm dom}\,h
, if there exists
\gamma\geq 0
such that, for all
x,y\in{\rm dom}\,h
and all
\lambda\in[0,1]
, we have

h(\lambda y+(1-\lambda)x)\leq\lambda h(y)+(1-\lambda)h(x)-\lambda(1-\lambda)\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert x-y\rVert^{2},

(5)

(b)

(strongly) quasiconvex on
{\rm dom}\,h
, if there exists
\gamma\geq 0
such that if for all
x,y\in{\rm dom}\,h
and all
\lambda\in[0,1]
, we have

h(\lambda y+(1-\lambda)x)\leq\max\{h(y),h(x)\}-\lambda(1-\lambda)\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert x-y\rVert^{2}.

(6)

A function is strongly convex (resp. quasiconvex) when
\gamma>0
, and convex (resp. quasiconvex) when
\gamma=0
. Hence, every (strongly) convex function is (strongly) quasiconvex, while the reverse statements do not hold in general (see
[
9
,
21
,
30
]
).

A proper function
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is said to be:

(i)

2-supercoercive, if

\liminf_{\lVert x\rVert\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{h(x)}{\lVert x\rVert^{2}}>0,

(7)

(ii)

coercive, if

\lim_{\lVert x\rVert\rightarrow+\infty}h(x)=+\infty.

(8)

or equivalently, if
S_{\lambda}(h)
is bounded for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.

Clearly,
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
, but the reverse statement does not hold as the function
h(x)=\lvert x\rvert
shows.

The following generalized convexity notion has been used in several applications from machine learning in virtue of its properties for fast convergence of gradient-type method (see
[
20
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
50
]
and references therein).

Motivated by
[
25
, Lemma 10]
, we define quasar-convexity as follows:

Definition 1
.

Let
\kappa\in\,]0,1]
,
\gamma\geq 0
and
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a function. Then
h
is said to be
(\kappa,\gamma)
-strongly quasar-convex with respect to
\overline{x}\in{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
if

\displaystyle h(\lambda\overline{x}+(1-\lambda)x)\leq\kappa\lambda h(\overline{x})+(1-\kappa\lambda)h(x)-\lambda\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{2-\kappa}\right)\frac{\kappa\gamma}{2}\lVert x-\overline{x}\rVert^{2},

for all
\lambda\in[0,1]
and all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Furthermore, when
\gamma=0
, we simply say that
h
is
\kappa
-quasar-convex with respect to
\overline{x}\in{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
.

Note that
{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
is a singleton when
h
is strongly quasar-convex (
\gamma>0
), thus in this case we say that
h
is strongly quasar-convex with modulus
\kappa\in\,]0,1]
and
\gamma>0
. If
h
is quasar-convex (
\gamma=0
), then
{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
is
star-shaped
(see
[
25
, Observation 3]
), i.e., there exists an
\overline{x}_{0}\in{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
such that for all
\overline{x}\in{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
and all
\lambda\in[0,1]
, we have
\lambda\overline{x}_{0}+(1-\lambda)\overline{x}\in{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
. Furthermore, if
\kappa=1
, then (strongly) quasar-convex functions are
(strongly) star-convex

[
42
]
. In particular, every (strongly) convex function is
(1,\gamma)
-strongly quasar-convex (
\gamma\geq 0
).

The relationship between convex/star-convex/quasar-convex notions is summarized below:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}{\rm strongly~convex}&\overset{*}{\Longrightarrow}&{\rm strongly~star{\rm-}convex}&\overset{*}{\Longrightarrow}&{\rm strongly~quasar{\rm-}convex}\\
\Downarrow&&\Downarrow&&\Downarrow\\
{\rm convex}&\overset{*}{\Longrightarrow}&{\rm star{\rm-}convex}&\overset{*}{\Longrightarrow}&{\rm quasar{\rm-}convex},\end{array}

where “
*
” denotes that
{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\,}h\neq\emptyset
is required. All the reverse statements do not hold in general (see
[
20
,
24
,
25
,
42
]
).

Remark 2
.

There is no relationship between (strongly) quasiconvex and (strongly) quasar-convex functions. Indeed, the function in
[
22
, Example 9]
is strongly quasiconvex but not quasar-convex, while the function in Example
28
(below) is strongly quasar-convex but not quasiconvex. For differentiable functions, sufficient conditions under which strong quasiconvexity implies quasar-convexity are provided in
[
22
,
32
]
.

Note that if
h
is strongly quasar-convex with modulus
\kappa\in\,]0,1]
and
\gamma>0
, then (see
[
25
, Corollary 1]
)

h(\overline{x})+\frac{\kappa\gamma}{2(2-\kappa)}\lVert y-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}\leq h(y),~\forall~y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(10)

where
\overline{x}\in{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
, thus strongly quasar-convex functions satisfy a quadratic growth condition with modulus
\frac{\kappa\gamma}{2(2-\kappa)}>0
.

In the differentiable case, we have the following useful characterization: A differentiable function
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
(\kappa,\gamma)
-strongly quasar-convex (
\kappa\in\,]0,1]
and
\gamma\geq 0
) with respect to
\overline{x}\in{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
if and only if (see
[
25
, Lemma 10]
)

h(\overline{x})\geq h(y)+\frac{1}{\kappa}\langle\nabla h(y),\overline{x}-y\rangle+\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert y-\overline{x}\rVert^{2},~\forall~y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(11)

Hence, quasi-strongly convex functions
[
41
]
are related to strongly quasar-convex.
Further properties for differentiable (strongly) quasar-convex functions may be found in
[
20
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
50
]
among others.

Given a nonempty, closed and convex set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the proximity operator on
K
of parameter
\beta>0
of a proper lsc function
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
at
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined as the operator
{\rm Prox}_{\beta h}(K,\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
for which

{\rm Prox}_{\beta h}(K,z)=\mathrm{argmin}_{x\in K}\left\{h(x)+\frac{1}{2\beta}\|z-x\rVert^{2}\right\}.

(12)

When
K=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we simply write
{\rm Prox}_{\beta h}(z):={\rm Prox}_{\beta h}(\mathbb{R}^{n},z)
. If
h
is proper, lsc and convex, then
{\rm Prox}_{\beta h}
turns out to be a single-valued operator (see, for instance,
[
8
, Proposition
12.15]
).

For a further study on generalized convexity and proximal point type methods we refer to
[
6
,
8
,

,

,
16
,
18
,
21
,

,

,

,

]
and references therein.

While quasar-convex functions are well-understood in the differentiable setting (see

,

,

,

3.1
Properties and Examples

We begin this subsection with the following simple results.

Lemma 3
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a nonempty closed convex set with
0\in K
, and let
\alpha>0
. Assume that
\kappa\in~]0,\alpha[
if
\alpha\leq 1
, and
\kappa\in~]0,1]
if
\alpha>1
. Consider the functions
\theta_{\alpha}:\,]0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
and
Q_{\alpha,\kappa}:\,]0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\theta_{\alpha}(\lambda):=\frac{1-(1-\lambda)^{\alpha}}{\lambda},\quad Q_{\alpha,\kappa}(\lambda):=\frac{\theta_{\alpha}(\lambda)-\kappa}{\left(1-\dfrac{\lambda}{2-\kappa}\right)\dfrac{\kappa}{2}}.

(13)

For
\kappa=1
, we extend continuously
Q_{\alpha,1}(1)
by taking
Q_{\alpha,1}(1):=2
. Then,

(a)

The function
\theta_{\alpha}
is strictly increasing for
\alpha\in\,]0,1[
, strictly decreasing for
\alpha>1
, and identically equal to
1
for
\alpha=1
. Moreover,

\inf_{\lambda\in\,]0,1]}\theta_{\alpha}(\lambda)=\begin{cases}\alpha&{\rm if}~\alpha\in\,]0,1],\\
1&{\rm if}~\alpha>1.\end{cases}

(b)

The function
Q_{\alpha,\kappa}(\lambda)
is strictly increasing on
\,]0,1]
for
\alpha\in\,]0,2[
, and strictly decreasing for
\alpha>2
. Moreover,

\inf_{\lambda\in\,]0,1]}Q_{\alpha,\kappa}(\lambda)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{2(\alpha-\kappa)}{\kappa}&{\rm if}~\alpha\in\,]0,2[,\\
\displaystyle\frac{2(2-\kappa)}{\kappa}&{\rm if}~\alpha\geq 2.\end{cases}

Lemma 4
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a nonempty closed convex set with
0\in K
, and
h:K\to\mathbb{R}
be a function satisfying

h(tx)=t^{\alpha}h(x),~\forall~x\in K,~\forall~t\in[0,1],

(14)

for some
\alpha>0
, with
0\in{\rm argmin}_{K}\,h
. Consider the functions
\theta_{\alpha}
and
Q_{\alpha,\kappa}
given in (
13
). Then the following assertions hold:

(a)

The function
h
is
\kappa
-quasar-convex with respect to
\overline{x}=0
if and only if

0<\kappa\leq\theta_{\alpha}(\lambda),~\forall~\lambda\in\,]0,1].

(15)

(b)

The function
h
is
(\kappa,\gamma)
-strongly quasar-convex with respect to
\overline{x}=0
if and only if there exist
\kappa\in\,]0,1]
and
\gamma>0
such that
(
15
) holds and

0<\gamma\leq Q_{\alpha,\kappa}(\lambda)\frac{h(x)}{\lVert x\rVert^{2}},~\forall~x\in K\setminus\{0\},~\forall~\lambda\in\,]0,1].

(16)

Proof.

Since
\overline{x}=0\in{\rm argmin}_{K}\,h
and
h
satisfies (
14
),
h(0)=0
. Thus,
h
is
(\kappa,\gamma)
-strongly quasar-convex with respect to
\overline{x}=0
if and only if there exist
\kappa\in\,]0,1]
and
\gamma>0
with

\displaystyle h((1-\lambda)x)\leq(1-\kappa\lambda)h(x)-\lambda\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{2-\kappa}\right)\frac{\kappa\gamma}{2}\lVert x\rVert^{2},

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow(1-\lambda)^{\alpha}\leq(1-\kappa\lambda)-\lambda\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{2-\kappa}\right)\frac{\kappa\gamma}{2}\dfrac{\lVert x\rVert^{2}}{h(x)}.

(17)

If
\gamma=0
, then
h
is
\kappa
-quasar-convex with respect to
\overline{x}=0
if and only if
0<\kappa\leq\theta_{\alpha}(\lambda)
, and
(a)
follows. If
\gamma>0
, then inequality (

) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\theta_{\alpha}(\lambda)-\kappa\geq\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{2-\kappa}\right)\frac{\kappa\gamma}{2}\frac{\lVert x\rVert^{2}}{h(x)}~\Longrightarrow~\gamma\leq Q_{\alpha,\kappa}(\lambda)\frac{h(x)}{\lVert x\rVert^{2}},

for all

and all

Sufficient conditions for ensuring strong quasar-convexity are given below.

Proposition 6
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a nonempty compact convex set with
0\in K
, and suppose there exists
c>0
such that

x\in K\backslash\{0\},~~\lVert x\rVert<c~\Longrightarrow~\dfrac{cx}{\lVert x\rVert}\in K.

(18)

Let
h:K\to\mathbb{R}
be a lsc function satisfying (
14
) with
{\rm argmin}_{K}\,h=\{0\}
. Define
M:=\sup\{\lVert x\rVert:x\in K\}<+\infty
and
S_{c}:=\min\{h(x):x\in K,\lVert x\rVert=c\}
. Then the following assertions hold:

(a)

If
0<\alpha\leq 1
, then
h
is
(\kappa,\gamma)
-strongly quasar-convex with respect to
\overline{x}=0
for every

\kappa\in\,]0,\alpha[
and

\gamma:=\frac{2(\alpha-\kappa)\,S_{c}}{\kappa\,c^{\alpha}\,M^{2-\alpha}}>0.

(b)

If
1<\alpha<2
, then
h
is
(\kappa,\gamma)
-strongly quasar-convex with respect to
\overline{x}=0
for every

\kappa\in\,]0,1]
and

\gamma:=\frac{2(\alpha-\kappa)\,S_{c}}{\kappa\,c^{\alpha}\,M^{2-\alpha}}>0.

(c)

If
\alpha=2
, then
h
is
(\kappa,\gamma)
-strongly quasar-convex with respect to
\overline{x}=0
for every

\kappa\in\,]0,1]
and

\gamma:=\frac{2(2-\kappa)\,S_{c}}{\kappa\,c^{2}}>0.

Proof.

Since
K_{c}:=\{x\in K:\lVert x\rVert=c\}
is compact and
h
is lsc, there exists
x_{0}\in K_{c}
such that

h(x_{0})=\min_{x\in K_{c}}h(x)=S_{c}.

As
x_{0}\neq 0
and
\mathrm{argmin}_{K}\,h=\{0\}
, it follows that
S_{c}>0
.
Let
x\in K\setminus\{0\}
and take
v:=\frac{cx}{\lVert x\rVert}
. By assumption (
18
), we have
v\in K
whenever
\lVert x\rVert\leq c
, thus the convexity of
K
ensures
v\in K
when
\lVert x\rVert>c
.
Then we have two cases:

(i)

If
\lVert x\rVert\leq c
, then
\frac{\lVert x\rVert}{c}\leq 1
, and using (
14
) we have

h(x)=\left(\frac{\lVert x\rVert}{c}\right)^{\alpha}h(v)~\Longrightarrow~\frac{h(x)}{\lVert x\rVert^{2}}=\frac{h(v)}{c^{\alpha}\,\lVert x\rVert^{2-\alpha}}\geq\frac{S_{c}}{c^{\alpha}M^{2-\alpha}}.

(ii)

If
\lVert x\rVert>c
, then
\frac{c}{\lVert x\rVert}<1
, and again by (
14
) we have

h(v)=\left(\frac{c}{\lVert x\rVert}\right)^{\alpha}h(x)~\Longrightarrow~\frac{h(x)}{\lVert x\rVert^{2}}=\frac{h(v)}{c^{\alpha}}\cdot\lVert x\rVert^{\alpha-2}\geq\frac{S_{c}}{c^{\alpha}M^{2-\alpha}}.

Hence, in both cases,

\frac{h(x)}{\lVert x\rVert^{2}}\geq\frac{S_{c}}{c^{\alpha}M^{2-\alpha}},~\forall~x\in K\setminus\{0\}.

Therefore, the conclusion follows from Lemmas
3
(b)
and
4
(b)
.
∎

Remark 7
.

Note that for
0<\alpha<2
, the boundedness of
K
is required. For
\alpha=2
, this assumption is no longer necessary. However, when
\alpha>2
, we have

\lim_{x\to 0}\ \frac{h(x)}{\lVert x\rVert^{2}}=\lim_{x\to 0}\left(h\left(\frac{x}{\lVert x\rVert}\right)\lVert x\rVert^{\alpha-2}\right)=0,

which shows that
h
is not strongly quasar-convex in this case.

The previous results have interesting consequences, illustrated by the following examples of nonsmooth (strongly) quasar-convex functions.

Notably, the
\ell_{p}
-regularization (

) is quasar-convex. As far as we know, this is the first result describing the generalized convexity notion related to
\ell_{p}
-regularization functions.

Example 8
.

(The
\ell_{p}
-regularization with
0<p<1
)
Let

(19)

\kappa\in\,]0,p[~~{\rm and}~~\gamma_{p}=\dfrac{2(p-\kappa)}{\kappa}>0.

It is known that the

In the following example, we show that the CES (Constant Elasticity of Substitution) production/utility function

Example 9
.

(The CES production/utility function)
Let
\beta\neq 0
and
\alpha_{i}>0
for all

. Then the CES function is

(20)

For

, the function defined in (

) can be continuously extended to
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
by setting
h_{C}(x):=0
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\setminus\mathbb{R}^{n}_{++}
.

It is known that
h_{C}
is quasiconcave if and only if
\beta\leq 1
, and it is convex if and only if
\beta\geq 1
(see
[
9
, Theorem 2.4.2]
).

On the other hand, it follows from Proposition
5
that
h_{C}
is
\kappa
-quasar-convex with respect to
0\in{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\,h_{C}
with modulus
\kappa\in\,]0,1]
for all
\beta\neq 0
. Furthermore, for
\beta>0
,
h_{C}
is strongly quasar-convex on bounded convex sets in virtue of Proposition
6
. In particular, for
K=\overline{\mathbb{B}}(0,1)\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
and
c=1
, we have:

(i)

If
\beta\in\,]0,2]
and
x\in K
with
\lVert x\rVert=1
, then

h_{C}(x)\geq\left(\alpha_{1}x^{2}_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{n}x^{2}_{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\geq\min\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\}^{\frac{1}{\beta}}.

Thus,
S_{1}=\min\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\}^{\frac{1}{\beta}}
, and by Proposition

\gamma_{C}=\frac{2(1-\kappa)\,\min\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\}^{\frac{1}{\beta}}}{\kappa}>0.

(ii)

If
\beta>2
and
x\in K
with
\lVert x\rVert=1

\displaystyle 1=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}^{\frac{2}{\beta}}x_{i}^{2}\alpha_{i}^{-\frac{2}{\beta}}\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}x_{i}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{2}{\beta}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}^{-\frac{2}{\beta-2}}\right)^{1-\frac{2}{\beta}}

\displaystyle\hskip 48.36958pt\Longleftrightarrow~h_{C}(x)\geq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}^{-\frac{2}{\beta-2}}\right)^{-\frac{\beta-2}{2\beta}}.

Furthermore, equality in Hölder’s inequality occurs on the unit sphere for
x_{i}=\frac{\alpha_{i}^{-1/(\beta-2)}}{\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}^{-2/(\beta-2)}\right)^{1/2}}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, which leads to

\gamma_{C}=\frac{2(1-\kappa)}{\kappa}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}^{-\frac{2}{\beta-2}}\right)^{-\frac{\beta-2}{2\beta}}>0.

The importance of the CES function is well-known in economics in virtue of its applications in production and utility theory among others (see

6

,

,

,

In particular, the Cobb-Douglas

Example 10
.

(The Leontief production/utility function)
Let
\alpha>0
and
\alpha_{i}>0
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Then the Leontief (see
[

) production/utility function is
h_{L}:\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by

h_{L}(x)=\min\left\{\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}}x_{1},\ldots,\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}x_{n}\right\}^{\alpha}.

It is known that

On the other hand, since

The Leontief function is an important tool in economics in virtue of its applications in production and utility theory based on the relationship between different commodities/goods (see

.

3.2
On the Proximity Operator

The next result provide an analysis of the behaviour at infinity of strongly quasar-convex functions.

Proposition 12
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed convex set and
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper lsc function such that
K\subset{\rm dom}\,h
. If
h
is a strongly quasar-convex function with modulus
\kappa\in\,]0,1]
and
\gamma>0
, then
h
is
2
-supercoercive.

Proof.

Let
\{x_{k}\}_{k}\subset K
be such that
\lVert x_{k}\rVert\rightarrow+\infty
. Then,
we may assume that
\frac{x_{k}}{\lVert x_{k}\rVert}\rightarrow u\in K^{\infty}
with
\lVert u\rVert=1
. Since
h
has a minimum
\overline{x}\in K
,
h(\overline{x})\leq h(x_{k})
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Using relation (
10
), we have

\displaystyle h(x^{k})\geq h(\overline{x})+\frac{\kappa\gamma}{2(2-\kappa)}\lVert x_{k}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\frac{h(x_{k})}{\lVert x_{k}\rVert^{2}}\geq\frac{h(\overline{x})}{\lVert x_{k}\rVert^{2}}+\frac{\kappa\gamma}{2(2-\kappa)}\left\|\frac{x_{k}}{\lVert x_{k}\rVert}-\frac{\overline{x}}{\lVert x_{k}\rVert}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{h(x_{k})}{\lVert x_{k}\rVert^{2}}\geq\frac{\kappa\gamma}{2(2-\kappa)}>0,

thus
h
is 2-supercoercive.
∎

As a consequence, we have the following result (which includes also the case
\gamma=0
). Since the proof is standard, it is omitted.

Corollary 13
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed convex set and
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper, lsc and
(\kappa,\gamma)
-strongly quasar-convex function with respect to
\overline{x}\in{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
(
\kappa\in\,]0,1]
and
\gamma\geq 0
) on
K\subset{\rm dom}\,h
. Then
h(\cdot)+\frac{1}{2\beta}\lVert\cdot\rVert^{2}
is lsc and
2
-supercoercive on
K
for all
\beta>0
.
As a consequence,
{\rm Prox}_{\beta h}(K,z)
is nonempty and compact for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and all
\beta>0
.

Remark 14
.

The proximity operator of a strongly quasar-convex function is not necessarily a singleton even in one dimension. Indeed, take
K=[-2,2]
,
\beta=1
,
z=\frac{3}{2}
and
h:K\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by
h(x)=\sqrt{\lvert x\rvert}
. Then, it follows from Proposition
6
(a)
that
h
is strongly quasar-convex, but (see
[
30
, Remark 19
(ii)
]
)

{\rm Prox}_{h}\left([-2,2],\tfrac{3}{2}\right)=\{0,1\},

i.e.,
{\rm Prox}_{h}
is not necessarily a singleton.

The following result will be useful in the sequel.

Proposition 15
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed convex set and
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper, lsc and
(\kappa,\gamma)
-strongly quasar-convex function with respect to
\overline{x}\in{\rm argmin}_{K}\,h
(
\kappa\in\,]0,1]
and
\gamma\geq 0
) on
K\subset{\rm dom}\,h
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
x^{*}\in{\rm Prox}_{\beta h}(K,z)
, then

\displaystyle h(x^{*})-h(\overline{x})\leq\frac{1}{\kappa\beta}\langle x^{*}-z,\overline{x}-x^{*}\rangle-\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert x^{*}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}.

(23)

Proof.

Since
x^{*}\in{\rm Prox}_{\beta h}(K,z)
, we have

\displaystyle h(x^{*})+\frac{1}{2\beta}\lVert x^{*}-z\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq h(x)+\frac{1}{2\beta}\lVert x-z\rVert^{2},~\forall~x\in K.

(24)

Since
h
is strongly quasar-convex with respect to
\overline{x}\in{\rm argmin}_{K}\,h
(
\kappa\in\,]0,1]
and
\gamma\geq 0
), taking
x=\lambda\overline{x}+(1-\lambda)x^{*}\in K
for all
\lambda\in[0,1]
, we have

\displaystyle h(x^{*})+\frac{1}{2\beta}\lVert x^{*}-z\rVert^{2}\leq h(\lambda\overline{x}+(1-\lambda)x^{*})+\frac{1}{2\beta}\lVert\lambda(\overline{x}-z)+(1-\lambda)(x^{*}-z)\rVert^{2},

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\kappa h(\overline{x})+(1-\lambda\kappa)h(x^{*})-\lambda\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{2-\kappa}\right)\frac{\kappa\gamma}{2}\lVert x^{*}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2\beta}\lVert z-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle~~~+\frac{(1-\lambda)}{2\beta}\lVert x^{*}-z\rVert^{2}-\frac{\lambda(1-\lambda)}{2\beta}\lVert x^{*}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2},~\forall~\lambda\in[0,1],

in virtue of (
4
). Then, it follows by (
3
) that

\displaystyle~\,\lambda\kappa(h(x^{*})-h(\overline{x}))\leq\frac{\lambda}{\beta}\langle x^{*}-z,\overline{x}-x^{*}\rangle+\frac{\lambda\kappa}{2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{\kappa\beta}-\gamma+\frac{\lambda\gamma}{2-\kappa}\right)\lVert x^{*}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2},

and the result follows by multiplying by
\frac{1}{\lambda\kappa}
, and then taking
\lambda\downarrow 0
.
∎

Remark 16
.

(i)

In contrast to the convex and (strongly) quasiconvex cases (see
[
8
, Proposition 16.44]
and
[
30
, Proposition 7]
, respectively), relation (
23
) holds only for
\overline{x}\in{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
. Surprisingly, as we will see in the next section, the information provided by (
23
) is enough to guarantee (linear) convergence to the optimal solution of a (strongly) quasar-convex function and to provide a detailed complexity analysis.

(ii)

Recall that if
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a closed set and
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is a proper function with
K\cap{\rm dom},h\neq\emptyset
, then
h
is said to be prox-convex on
K
(see
[
15
,
16
]
) if there exists
\alpha>0
such that for every
z\in K
,
{\rm Prox}_{h}(K,z)\neq\emptyset
, and

x^{*}\in{\rm Prox}_{h}(K,z)~\Longrightarrow~h(x^{*})-h(x)\leq\alpha\langle x^{*}-z,x-x^{*}\rangle,~\forall~x\in K.

(25)

It follows from Proposition
15
that prox-convex functions with modulus
\alpha\geq 1
are quasar-convex with modulus
\kappa=\frac{1}{\alpha}
and
\gamma=0
.

The following result provides a connection between fixed points of the proximity operator and minimal points of quasar-convex functions.

Proposition 17
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed convex set and
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper, lsc and
(\kappa,\gamma)
-strongly quasar-convex function with respect to
{\rm argmin}_{K}\,h
(
\kappa\in\,]0,1]
and
\gamma\geq 0
) on
K\subset{\rm dom}\,h
, and
\beta>0
. Then

{\rm Fix}\left({\rm Prox}_{\beta h}(K,\cdot)\right)={\rm argmin}_{K}\,h.

(26)

Proof.

(\supset)
Straightforward.

(\subset)
Let
x^{*}\in{\rm Prox}_{\beta h}(K,x^{*})
. Then,
h(x^{*})\leq h(x)+\frac{1}{2\beta}\lVert x^{*}-x\rVert^{2}
for all
x\in K
. Take
x=\lambda\overline{x}+(1-\lambda)x^{*}\in K
with
\lambda\in[0,1]
and
\overline{x}\in{\rm argmin}_{K}\,h
. Since
h
is strongly quasar-convex with modulus
\kappa\in\,]0,1]
and
\gamma\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle h(x^{*})\leq h(\lambda\overline{x}+(1-\lambda)x^{*})+\frac{1}{2\beta}\lVert\lambda(\overline{x}-x^{*})\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 24.18501pt\leq\lambda\kappa h(\overline{x})+(1-\lambda\kappa)h(x^{*})-\lambda\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{2-\kappa}\right)\frac{\kappa\gamma}{2}\lVert x^{*}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2\beta}\lVert x^{*}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}.

Then

\displaystyle h(x^{*})-h(\overline{x})\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{\kappa\beta}-\gamma+\frac{\lambda\gamma}{2-\kappa}\right)\lVert x^{*}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2},~\forall~\lambda\in\,]0,1].

Taking
\lambda\downarrow 0
, we obtain
h(x^{*})\leq h(\overline{x})
, thus
x^{*}\in{\rm argmin}_{K}\,h
.
∎

The previous result extends
[
8
, Proposition 12.29]
from the convex to the quasar-convex case.

4
The Proximal Point Algorithm

Motivated by the lack of algorithms for nonsmooth quasar-convex functions, we develop a proximal point algorithm that builds directly on the foundational properties of proximity operators established in the previous section.

The Proximal Point Algorithm (PPA) that we consider is based on the classical version of Rockafellar
[
46
]
(see also
[
36
,
37
]
).

Algorithm 1

PPA for Quasar-Convex Functions (PPA-Quasar)

Step 0.

Take
x^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
k=0
and
\{\beta_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a sequence of positive numbers.

Step 1.

Take

x^{k+1}\in{\rm Prox}_{\beta_{k}h}(x^{k}).

(27)

Step 2.

If
x^{k+1}=x^{k}
, then STOP,
x^{k}\in{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
. Otherwise, take
k=k+1
and go to Step 1.

Note that the iterative steps are well-defined by Corollary
13
while the stopping criteria holds by Proposition
17
. Furthermore, we implemented PPA on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, but it could be easily extended to any closed convex set.

4.1
Convergence Analysis

We proceed directly with the convergence result.

Theorem 18
.

Let
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper, lsc and strongly quasar-convex with modulus
\kappa\in\,]0,1]
and
\gamma>0
on
{\rm dom}\,h
. Suppose that there exists
\beta^{\prime}>0
such that

0<\beta^{\prime}\leq\beta_{k},~\forall~k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(28)

Then the sequence
\{x^{k}\}_{k}
, generated by Algorithm
1
, is a minimizing sequence of
h
, i.e.,
h(x^{k})\downarrow\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h(x)
and, moreover, converges linearly to the unique solution
\overline{x}\in{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
with convergence rate of at least

\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\kappa\beta^{\prime}\gamma+(\frac{\kappa^{2}\beta^{\prime}\gamma}{2-\kappa})}}\,\in~]0,1[.

(29)

Proof.

If
x^{k+1}=x^{k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then the algorithm stops and
x^{k}\in{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
by Proposition
17
. So, we assume that
x^{k+1}\neq x^{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Since
x^{k+1}\in{\rm Prox}_{\beta_{k}h}(x^{k})
, taking
z=x^{k}
,
x^{*}=x^{k+1}
and
x=x^{k}
in (
24
), and since we are assuming
x^{k+1}\neq x^{k}
, we obtain

\displaystyle h(x^{k+1})<h(x^{k+1})+\frac{1}{2\beta_{k}}\lVert x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}\leq h(x^{k}).

(30)

Hence,
\{h(x^{k})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is a decreasing sequence.

Now, let us prove that the sequence
\{x^{k}\}_{k}
converges to the unique solution. Indeed, since
h
is strongly quasar-convex and
x^{k+1}\in{\rm Prox}_{\beta_{k}h}(x^{k})
, it follows from Proposition
15
with
\overline{x}\in{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
that

\displaystyle 2\langle x^{k+1}-x^{k},x^{k+1}-\overline{x}\rangle\leq 2\kappa\beta_{k}(h(\overline{x})-h(x^{k+1}))-\kappa\beta_{k}\gamma\lVert x^{k+1}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}.

(31)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\lVert x^{k+1}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\lVert x^{k+1}-x^{k}+x^{k}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\lVert x^{k+1}-x^{k}\rVert^{2}+\lVert x^{k}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}+2\langle x^{k+1}-x^{k},x^{k}-\overline{x}\rangle

\displaystyle=\lVert x^{k}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}-\lVert x^{k+1}-x^{k}\rVert^{2}+2\langle x^{k+1}-x^{k},x^{k+1}-\overline{x}\rangle.

Then, using (
31
),

(1+\kappa\beta_{k}\gamma)\lVert x^{k+1}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}\leq\lVert x^{k}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}-\lVert x^{k+1}-x^{k}\rVert^{2}+2\kappa\beta_{k}(h(\overline{x})-h(x^{k+1})).

(32)

Using now (
10
) (with
y=x^{k+1}
) in (
32
), we have

\displaystyle(1+\kappa\beta_{k}\gamma)\lVert x^{k+1}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}\leq\lVert x^{k}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}-\lVert x^{k+1}-x^{k}\rVert^{2}-\frac{\kappa^{2}\beta_{k}\gamma}{2-\kappa}\lVert x^{k+1}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\,\left(1+\kappa\beta_{k}\gamma+\frac{\kappa^{2}\beta_{k}\gamma}{2-\kappa}\right)\lVert x^{k+1}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}\leq\lVert x^{k}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}-\lVert x^{k+1}-x^{k}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\,\overset{\eqref{param:cond1}}{\Longrightarrow}\,\lVert x^{k+1}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{1}{1+\kappa\beta^{\prime}\gamma+(\frac{\kappa^{2}\beta^{\prime}\gamma}{2-\kappa})}\lVert x^{k}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}.

(33)

Hence,
\{x^{k}\}_{k}
converges to the unique solution
\overline{x}\in{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
, and using (
30
) we conclude that it is a minimizing sequence, that is,
h(x^{k})\downarrow\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h(x)
.

Finally, it follows from (
33
) that
\{x^{k}\}_{k}
converges linearly
\overline{x}\in{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
with convergence rate of at least
\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\kappa\beta^{\prime}\gamma+(\frac{\kappa^{2}\beta^{\prime}\gamma}{2-\kappa})}}\in\,]0,1[
.
∎

If in particular the function
h
is strongly star-convex (
\kappa=1
), we obtain:

Corollary 19
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
18
. If the function
h
is strongly star-convex (
\kappa=1
,
\gamma>0
), then the sequence
\{x^{k}\}_{k}
, generated by Algorithm
1
, is a minimizing sequence of
h
and converges linearly to the unique solution
\overline{x}\in{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
with convergence rate of at least

\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+2\beta^{\prime}\gamma}}\,\in~]0,1[.

(34)

In the case when
\gamma=0
, i.e.,
h
is quasar-convex, we can also ensure that the generated sequence is a minimizing for the optimization problem.

Theorem 20
.

Let
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper, lsc and
\kappa
-quasar-convex with respect to
{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
(
\kappa\in\,]0,1]
,
\gamma=0
). Suppose that condition (
28
) holds. Then the sequence
\{x^{k}\}_{k}
, generated by Algorithm
1
, is a minimizing sequence of
h
, i.e.,
h(x^{k})\downarrow\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h(x)
.

Proof.

Since
h
is quasar-convex (
\gamma=0
) and
x^{k+1}\in{\rm Prox}_{\beta_{k}h}(x^{k})
, it follows from (
30
) that
\{h(x^{k})\}_{k}
is decreasing. Also, by repeating the proof of Theorem
18
with
\overline{x}\in{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
and
\gamma=0
, it follows from (
32
) that

\displaystyle\lVert x^{k+1}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert x^{k}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}+2\kappa\beta_{k}(h(\overline{x})-h(x^{k+1}))

\displaystyle\leq\lVert x^{k}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}+2\kappa\beta^{\prime}(h(\overline{x})-h(x^{k+1})).

(35)

Hence, the sequence
\{x^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is Fejér monotone with respect to
{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
(see
[
8
, Definition 5.1]
) and

0\leq 2\kappa\beta^{\prime}\sum_{k=0}^{p}(h(x^{k})-h(\overline{x}))\leq\lVert x^{0}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}-\lVert x^{p+1}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}.

(36)

Then the series
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(h(x^{k})-h(\overline{x}))
converges and, in particular,
\lim_{k\to\infty}h(x^{k})=h(\overline{x})=\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x)
.

Since the sequence
\{\lVert x^{k}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is monotonically decreasing and thus bounded, the sequence
\{x^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
possesses a convergent subsequence
\{x^{j_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
with limit point
\hat{x}
. By the lower semicontinuity of
h
, we obtain

h(\hat{x})\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}h(x^{j_{k}})=\lim_{k\to\infty}h(x^{k})=\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x).

(37)

Consequently,
\hat{x}\in{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
.

Thus, the sequence
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is Fejér monotone with respect to
{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
and has at least one cluster point belonging to
{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
. Applying
[
8
, Theorem 5.5]
, we conclude that
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
converges to some point in
{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
.
∎

In particular, for the star-convex case we have:

Corollary 21
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
18
. If the function
h
is star-convex (
\kappa=1
,
\gamma=0
), then the sequence
\{x^{k}\}_{k}
, generated by Algorithm
1
, is a minimizing sequence of
h
.

\beta^{{}^{\prime}}>0
in (
28
) can be as big as we want, the convergence rate in (
29
) and (
34
) can be as fast as we want, exactly as in the strongly convex case.

(b)

18
is a proper extension since when

\kappa=1
in (

), the linear rate is exactly as good as the rate for strongly convex ones (see, for instance,

(ii)
]
).

(c)

is strongly quasar-convex on bounded convex sets without being neither weakly convex nor DC nor strongly quasiconvex, i.e., the results from
[
2

4
,

,
13
,
26
,

44
,
47
]
and references therein can not be applied to this function.

4.2
Complexity Analysis

In the following result, we present non-asymptotic estimates for both functional values
h(x^{k})-\min h
and the distance
\lVert x^{k}-\overline{x}\rVert
when the objective function is strongly quasar-convex with
\gamma>0
.

Proposition 23
.

Suppose that the assumptions of Theorem
18
hold and
\overline{x}\in{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
. Let
\{x^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be the sequence generated by Algorithm
1
. Then, given a tolerance
\varepsilon>0
, the following assertions hold:

(a)

At most

\mathcal{O}(\ln(\varepsilon^{-1}))=\dfrac{\ln(\varepsilon^{-1})+\ln(\lVert x^{0}-\overline{x}\rVert)}{\ln\left(\sqrt{1+\kappa\beta^{\prime}\gamma+\frac{\kappa^{2}\beta^{\prime}\gamma}{2-\kappa}}\right)}.

iterations are needed to satisfy
\lVert x^{k}-\overline{x}\rVert\leq\varepsilon
.

(b)

At most

\mathcal{O}(\ln(\varepsilon^{-1}))=\dfrac{\ln\left(\varepsilon^{-1}\right)+\ln\left(\dfrac{\lVert x^{0}-\overline{x}\rVert}{{2\kappa\beta^{\prime}}}\right)}{\ln\left(\sqrt{1+\kappa\beta^{\prime}\gamma+\frac{\kappa^{2}\beta^{\prime}\gamma}{2-\kappa}}\right)}+1.

iterations are needed to satisfy
h(x^{k})-h(\overline{x})\leq\varepsilon
.

Proof.

(a)
It follows from Theorem
18
that

\lVert x^{k}-\overline{x}\rVert\leq\mu^{k}\lVert x^{0}-\overline{x}\rVert
with
\mu^{k}:=\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\kappa\beta^{\prime}\gamma+(\frac{\kappa^{2}\beta^{\prime}\gamma}{2-\kappa})}}\right)^{k}
, thus

k\geq\dfrac{\ln(\varepsilon^{-1})+\ln(\lVert x^{0}-\overline{x}\rVert)}{\ln(\sqrt{1+\kappa\beta^{\prime}\gamma+\frac{\kappa^{2}\beta^{\prime}\gamma}{2-\kappa}})}
, and
\lVert x^{k}-\overline{x}\lVert\leq\varepsilon
is guaranteed within
\mathcal{O}(\ln(\varepsilon^{-1}))
.

(b)
From (
32
), it follows that, for every
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\lVert x^{k}-x^{k-1}\rVert^{2}\leq\lVert x^{k-1}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}-(1+\kappa\beta_{k-1}\gamma)\lVert x^{k}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}+2\kappa\beta_{k-1}(h(\overline{x})-h(x^{k}))

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\Longrightarrow~h(x^{k})-h(\overline{x})\leq\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\kappa\beta^{\prime}\gamma+(\frac{\kappa^{2}\beta^{\prime}\gamma}{2-\kappa})}}\right)^{k-1}\dfrac{1}{2\kappa\beta^{\prime}}\lVert x^{0}-\overline{x}\lVert,

and the result follows by solving
\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\kappa\beta^{\prime}\gamma+(\frac{\kappa^{2}\beta^{\prime}\gamma}{2-\kappa})}}\right)^{k-1}\dfrac{1}{2\kappa\beta^{\prime}}\lVert x^{0}-\overline{x}\lVert\leq\varepsilon
. ∎

Corollary 24
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
23
. If in particular the function
h
is strongly star-convex (
\kappa=1
,
\gamma>0
), then:

(a)

At most
\mathcal{O}(\ln(\varepsilon^{-1}))=\dfrac{\ln(\varepsilon^{-1})+\ln(\lVert x^{0}-\overline{x}\rVert)}{\ln\left(1+2\beta^{\prime}\gamma\right)}
, iterations are needed to satisfy
\lVert x^{k}-\overline{x}\rVert\leq\varepsilon
.

(b)

At most
\mathcal{O}(\ln(\varepsilon^{-1}))=\dfrac{\ln\left(\varepsilon^{-1}\right)+\ln(\dfrac{\lVert x^{k}-\overline{x}\lVert}{{2\beta^{\prime}}})}{\ln\left(1+2\beta^{\prime}\gamma\right)}+1
, iterations are needed to satisfy
h(x^{k})-h(\overline{x})\leq\varepsilon
.

Now, we analyze the general quasar-convex case (
\gamma=0
).

Proposition 25
.

Let
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper, lsc and
\kappa
-quasar-convex with respect to
{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
(
\kappa\in\,]0,1]
,
\gamma=0
). Suppose that the sequence
\{x^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is generated by Algorithm
1
, with step sizes satisfying

0<\beta^{\prime}\leq\beta_{k}\leq\beta^{\prime\prime},~\forall~k\in\mathbb{N}.

(38)

Then, given a tolerance
\varepsilon>0
, the following assertions hold:

(a)

At most

\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})=\frac{2\beta^{\prime\prime}(h(x^{0})-\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x))}{\varepsilon^{2}},

(39)

iterations are needed to satisfy the stopping criterion
\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|\leq\varepsilon
.

(b)

At most

\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-1})=\frac{\lVert x^{0}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}}{2\kappa\beta^{\prime}\varepsilon},

(40)

iterations are needed to satisfy the stopping criterion
h(x^{k})-h(\overline{x})\leq\varepsilon
.

Proof.

(a)
The proof is standard (see, for instance,
[
1
, Proposition 4.8]
).

(b)
Using (
32
), we have

\displaystyle\hskip 46.94687pt2\kappa\beta^{\prime}(h(x^{k+1})-h(\overline{x}))\leq\lVert x^{k}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}-\lVert x^{k+1}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2},~\forall~k\geq 0

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow~2\kappa\beta^{\prime}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\left(h(x^{k+1})-h(\overline{x})\right)\leq\lVert x^{0}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}-\lVert x^{N}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}\leq\lVert x^{0}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}.

(41)

Since
\{h(x^{k})\}_{k}
is nonincreasing,

h(x^{N})-h(\overline{x})\leq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\left(h(x^{k+1})-h(\overline{x})\right)
.
Combining this with (
41
), we conclude

h(x^{N})-h(\overline{x})\leq\frac{\lVert x^{0}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}}{2\kappa\beta^{\prime}N},

(42)

and since this holds for all
N
, the result (
40
) follows.
∎

Remark 26
.

Note that our complexity rates for (strongly) quasar-convex function preserves the same order than for (strongly) convex functions (see
[
19
, Theorem 2.1]
and
[
8
, Example 23.40]
for instance), even when several classes of nonconvex functions are including in our analysis.

In the examples below, we illustrate our results for classes of functions which are neither convex nor quasiconvex, since their sublevel sets are not convex, which represent a hard problem for first-order algorithms.

Example 27
.

(Strongly quasar-convex illustration)

In the first example, we consider a family of strongly quasar-convex functions of the type:
h:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, defined as:

\displaystyle h(x)=h_{1}(\lVert x\rVert)h_{2}\left(\frac{x}{\lVert x\rVert}\right),

(43)

where

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38092pth_{1}(\lVert(x_{1},x_{2})\rVert)=\max\{q_{1}\lVert(x_{1},x_{2})\rVert^{2},q_{2}\lVert(x_{1},x_{2})\rVert^{2}-k\},~q_{2}>q_{1}>0,~k\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle h_{2}\left(\frac{(x_{1},x_{2})}{\lVert(x_{1},x_{2})\rVert}\right)=\frac{1}{4N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(a_{i}\sin^{2}(b_{i}x_{1})+c_{i}\cos^{2}(d_{i}x_{2})\right)+1,

with

{a_{i}}
and

[0,20]

{b_{i}}
and

are independently and uniformly distributed on
[-25,25]
for

.

Clearly, the minimum point of

on

is nonsmooth because

is nonsmooth as the maximum of two functions. Furthermore,

6
Conclusions

We contribute to the extension of the remarkable properties of the PPA from (strongly) convex functions to a broader class of functions, known as (strongly) quasar-convex. By generalizing the standard properties of proximity operators, we prove that PPA generates a minimizing sequence for quasar-convex functions and converges linearly in the strongly quasar-convex case, achieving a rate matching that of the strongly convex case. Additionally, we present several noteworthy examples of nonsmooth (strongly) quasar-convex functions, which have practical applications in machine learning, microeconomic theory, and beyond.

Our developments open the door to new research directions, as algorithms for nonsmooth quasar-convex optimization remain largely unexplored. A natural next step would be to extend these results to proximal-gradient (splitting) methods
[
4
]
and high-order PPA variants
[
1
]
. We leave these promising extensions for future work.

Normality criterion for a family of holomorphic curves that partially share wandering hyperplanes with their derivatives, and holomorphic functions lifted to curves in ℙ 2 ​ ( ℂ ) \mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{C})
Sonam Mehta, Kuldeep Singh Charak

Abstract
In this paper we generalize a result of Ye, Pang and Yang [ 12 ] on the normality of a family of holomorphic curves in ℙ N ​ ( ℂ ) \mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C}) . Further we obtain a normality criteria for family of meromorphic functions that partially share wandering holomorphic functions with their derivatives. We also devise a tractable representation of complex valued holomorphic functions on D D as functions from D D to ℙ 2 ​ ( ℂ ) \mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{C}) to obtain a normality criterion that leads to a counterexample to the converse of Bloch’s principle.

Introduction

A family
\mathcal{F}
of holomorphic functions on a domain
D
in the complex plane is said to be normal if any sequence
\{f_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{F}
has a subsequence which converges locally uniformly in
D
to an analytic function
f
or a subsequence which converges locally uniformly in
D
to
\infty
. Normality for families of meromorphic function is defined likewise with convergence of the subsequence required in the spherical metric of the Riemann sphere.

A central result in the theory of normal families is the
Critére fondamental
proved by Montel in 1912.

Theorem A

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of meromorphic functions on a domain
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}
which omit three distinct values
a.b,c
in
\hat{\mathbb{C}}
. Then
\mathcal{F}
is normal in
D
.

Recall that two nonconstant meromorphic functions
f
and
g
on a domain
D

share
the value
a\in\hat{\mathbb{C}}
if
\{f^{-1}(a)\}=\{g^{-1}(a)\}

(ignoring multiplicity).
W. Schwick
in 1992, drew a connection between normality and shared values with the following theorem:

Theorem B

(
[
9
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of meromorphic functions on a domain
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}
, and
a,b,c
be three distinct finite complex numbers. If for every
f\in\mathcal{F}
,
f
and
f^{\prime}
share the values
a,b,c
, then
\mathcal{F}
is normal in
D
.

Soon enough the Theorem
A
was generalized to the case of sharing of values by Sun
[
10
]
where instead of omitting the three values it is only required that the three distinct values be shared by all the functions of the family.

Theorem
A
has also been extended to the family of holomorphic maps into complex projective space as follows:

Theorem C

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of holomorphic maps of a domain
D
into
\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})
. Let
H_{1},H_{2},......,H_{2N+1}
be hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})
in general position. If for each
f\in\mathcal{F}
,
f
omits
H_{1},H_{2},......,H_{2N+1}
, then
\mathcal{F}
is normal in
D
.

The requirement of omission
2N+1
hyperplanes in Theorem
C

was reduced to merely sharing of such hyperplanes by Yang et al
[
11
]
. An adequate extension of the theorem
B
to the case of holomorphic maps into complex projective space could only be achieved after a proper extension of the notion of derivatives for such maps which was accomplished by Ye et al
[
12
]
.

Let
f
and
g
be two meromorphic functions on a domain
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}
. Then
f
and
g
are said to share a function
\alpha
, in
D
, ignoring multiplicities, if
f(z)-\alpha(z)=0\iff g(z)-\alpha(z)=0
. If
f(z)-\alpha(z)=0\implies g(z)-\alpha(z)=0
then we say that
f
and
g
partially share
\alpha
in
D
.

In 2014 Grahl and Nevo, using a simultaneous rescaling version of the Zalcman Lemma, obtained a normality criterion for a family of meromorphic functions whose each member shared three functions with its derivative.

Theorem D
.

[
[
6
]
]
Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of functions meromorphic in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
and
\epsilon>0
. Assume that for each
f\in\mathcal{F}
there exist functions
a_{1}^{(f)},a_{2}^{(f)},a_{3}^{(f)}
that are either meromorphic or identically infinity in
\mathbb{D}
, and do not have any common poles with
f
, such that
f
and
f^{\prime}
share the functions
a_{1}^{(f)},a_{2}^{(f)},a_{3}^{(f)}
, and such that

\sigma(a_{j}^{(f)(z)},a_{k}^{(f)(z)})\geq\epsilon\ \forall z\in\mathbb{D},\ \text{j,k}\ \in\{1,2,3\}\ \text{with}\ j\neq k

Then
\mathcal{F}
is normal in
\mathbb{D}.

The functions
a_{1}^{(f)},a_{2}^{(f)},a_{3}^{(f)}
are called
wandering shared functions
as they vary for different functions of the family
\mathcal{F}.
After proving our first main result we shall be in a position to replace sharing in Theorem
D
by partial sharing. But it shall entail some extra restrictions on the wandering shared functions; that they must be bounded holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}.

The extension of results in theory of meromorphic functions to that of holomorphic functions into
\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})
has been a regular feature in the field of normal families. The results obtained by various authors are more general and can be reduced to the case of meromorphic functions by just taking
N=1
. In our approach to the last result of this paper we have followed a different path by considering a family of complex valued holomorphic functions and lifting them to functions into
\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{C})
. A choice of proper hyperplanes has enabled us to formulate a nice criteria for normality of complex valued holomorphic functions on
D
. Now we introduce the necessary preliminaries.

1.1
Projective spaces and hyperplanes

An
N-
dimensional complex projective space is
\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})=\left(\mathbb{C}^{N+1}\setminus\{0\}\right)/\sim
, where
(a_{0},...,a_{N})\sim(b_{0},...,b_{N})
if and only if
(a_{0},...,a_{N})=\lambda(b_{0},...,b_{N})
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. We denote by
[a_{0}:\cdots:a_{N}]
the equivalence class of
(a_{0},...,a_{N})
. The mapping
\pi:\mathbb{C}^{N+1}\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})
given by
\pi(a_{0},...,a_{N})=[a_{0}:\cdots:a_{N}]
is called the standard projection mapping.
For details see
[
8
, pp.99-102]
and
[
5
]
.

A subset
H
of
\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})
is called a hyperplane if there is an
N-
dimensional linear subspace
\tilde{H}
of
\mathbb{C}^{N+1}
such that
\pi(\tilde{H}\setminus\{0\})=H
. For a fixed system of homogeneous coordinates
z=[z_{0}:z_{1}:\cdots:z_{N}]
, a hyperplane
H
of
\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})
can be written as

H=\{z\in\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})|<z,\alpha>=0\}

where
<z,\alpha>:=a_{0}z_{0}+\cdots+a_{N}z_{N}

and
\alpha=(a_{0},\ldots,a_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N+1}
is a non-zero vector.
We write it as

H=\{<z,\alpha>=0\}

(1)

for convenience. We also define

||H||:=\max_{0\leq i\leq N}|a_{i}|.

Henceforth in this paper, we only consider normalized hyperplane representations so that
||H||=1.

If
a_{j}(z),j=0,1,\ldots,N,
are holomorphic functions on a domain
D
not all simultaneously zero at any point
z\in D
, then at each point
z\in D
there is a hyperplane given by

H=\{z\in\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})|a_{0}(z)z_{0}+\cdots+a_{N}(z)z_{N}=0\}

We call it a moving hyperplane,
and write it as

H(z)=\{<z,\alpha(z)>=0\}

(2)

Let
H_{1},H_{2},\ldots,H_{N+1}
be hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})
represented as
H_{j}=\{<z,\alpha_{j}>=0\}
for
j=1,\ldots,N+1.

Then

\mathcal{D}(H_{1},\ldots,H_{N+1}):=|\det(\alpha_{1}^{t},\ldots,\alpha_{N+1}^{t})|

depends only on
H_{j}
and not on any choice of
\alpha_{j}
for normalized representations of hyperplanes.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
H_{1},\ldots,H_{q}
with
q\geq N+1
, be hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})
. Define

\mathcal{D}(H_{1},\ldots,H_{q}):=\prod_{1\leq j_{1}<\cdots<j_{N+1}\leq q}|\det(\alpha_{j_{1}}^{t},\ldots,\alpha_{j_{N+1}}^{t})|.

We say the hyperplane family
H_{1},\ldots,H_{q},

q\geq{N+1}
, in
\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})
is in general position if
\mathcal{D}(H_{1},\ldots,H_{q})>0.

1.2
Reduced representation

Let
D
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}
,
f:D\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})
be a holomorphic map and
U
be an open set in
D
. Any holomorphic map
\tilde{f}:U\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{N+1}
such that
\pi(\tilde{f}(z))\equiv f(z)
in
U
is called a
representation
of
f
on
U
, where
\pi
is the standard projection map.

Definition 1.2
.

For an open set
U
of
D
we call
\tilde{f}=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{N})
a
reduced representation
of
f
on
U
if
\tilde{f}
is a representation of
f
and

f_{0},\ldots,f_{N}
are holomorphic functions on
U
without common zeroes.

For any such representation we put
||\tilde{f}(z)||:=\left(\ \sum_{i=0}^{N}|f_{i}(z)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

Remark 1
.

Every holomorphic map of
D
into
\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})
has a reduced representation on
D

[
4
, section 5]
.

Remark 2
.

Let
\tilde{f}=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{N})
be a reduced representation of
f
on a domain D. Then for any arbitrary nowhere zero holomorphic function
h
on
D
,
(hf_{0},\ldots,hf_{N})
is also a reduced representation of
f
. Conversely, for any other reduced representation
(g_{0},\ldots,g_{N})
of
f
, each
g_{i}
can be written as
g_{i}=hf_{i}
for some nowhere zero holomorphic function
h
on
D
.

Definition 1.3
.

A function
f:D\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})
with reduced representation
\tilde{f}=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{N})
is said to intersect a hyperplane
H
given by (
1
) at a point
z_{0}\in D
if

<\tilde{f},H>\ :=a_{0}f_{0}+\cdots+a_{N}f_{N}

has a zero at
z_{0}
and we then write
f(z_{0})\in H.

If
<\tilde{f},H>\equiv 0
on
D
then we say that
f(D)\subset H
and if
<\tilde{f}(z),H>\neq 0\ \forall z\in D
then
f
is said to omit
H
on
D.

1.3
Normal family

Definition 1.4
.

A family
\mathcal{F}
of holomorphic maps from a domain
D
into
\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})
is said to be normal in
D
if any sequence in
\mathcal{F}
contains a subsequence which converges uniformly on compact subsets of
D
to a holomorphic map of
D
into
\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})
; and
\mathcal{F}
is said to be
normal at a point
a
in
D
if
\mathcal{F}
is normal on some neighbourhood of
a
in
D
.

Using the Fubini-Study metric on
\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})
, we see that a sequence
\{f_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
of holomorphic maps of
D
into
\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})
converges uniformly on compact subsets of
D
to a holomorphic map
f
if and only if, for any
a\in D
each
f_{n}
has a reduced representation

\tilde{f_{n}}=(f_{n0},\ldots,f_{nN})

on some fixed neighbourhood
U
of
a
in
D
such that

\{f_{ni}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges uniformly on
U
to a holomorphic function

f_{i}
(for
i=0,1,\ldots,N
) on
U
with the property that

\tilde{f}=(f_{0},f_{1},\ldots,f_{N})

is a reduced representation of
f
on
U
.

1.4
Extension of derivative

Now we proceed to the extension of the concept of derivative of meromorphic functions to that of functions from
D
into
\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})
. Note that any sort of spherical derivative using Fubini Study metric would have been inadequate to handle the problem of sharing where we require the derivative again to be a function into
\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})
.

In a successful attempt at extension of Theorem
B
a suitable notion of derivative for this setting was provided by Ye et al
[
12
]
as follows:

Let
f=[f_{0}:\cdots:f_{N}]
be a holomorphic map of
D
into
\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})
,
\mu\in\{0,1,\ldots,N\}
with
f_{\mu}\not\equiv 0
, and
d(z)
be a holomorphic function in
D
such that
f_{\mu}^{2}/d
and
W(f_{\mu},f_{i})/d
,
(i=0,1,\ldots,N;i\neq\mu)
are holomorphic functions without common zeroes. Here, as usual,

W(f_{\mu},f_{i})=\begin{vmatrix}f_{\mu}&f_{i}\\
f^{\prime}_{\mu}&f^{\prime}_{i}\end{vmatrix}

denotes the Wronskian of
f_{\mu}
and
f_{i}
.

Definition 1.5
.

[
12
]

The holomorphic map induced by the map

(W(f_{\mu},f_{0}),\ldots,W(f_{\mu},f_{\mu-1}),f_{\mu}^{2},W(f_{\mu},f_{\mu+1}),\ldots,W(f_{\mu},f_{N})):D\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{N+1}

is called the
\mu
th
derived holomorphic map
of
f
and we write

\nabla_{\mu}f=[W(f_{\mu},f_{0})/d,\ldots,W(f_{\mu},f_{\mu-1})/d,f_{\mu}^{2}/d,W(f_{\mu},f_{\mu+1})/d,\ldots,W(f_{\mu},f_{N})/d].

For simplicity, we shall write
\nabla_{0}f
as
\nabla f
.

Remark 3
.

The definition of
\nabla_{\mu}f
does not depend on the choice of a reduced representation of
f
.

Remark 4
.

A meromorphic function
f=\displaystyle\frac{f_{1}(z)}{f_{0}(z)}
on
D
can be regarded as a holomorphic map from
D
to
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
given by
f(z)=[f_{0}(z):f_{1}(z)]

and
\nabla f
is exactly the ordinary derivative of
f(z)
.

In 2015 Ye et al obtained the following result which led them to an extension of the Schwick’s theorem.

Theorem E

(
[
12
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of holomorphic maps of a domain
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}
into
\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})
,
H_{1},\ldots,H_{2N+1}
be hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})
in general position, and
\delta
be a real number with
0<\delta<1
. Suppose that for each
f\in\mathcal{F}
the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

If
\nabla f(z)\in H_{j}
, then
f(z)\in H_{j}
, for
j=1,\ldots,2N+1
.

(ii)

If
f(z)\in\bigcup_{j=1}^{2N+1}H_{j}
, then

\frac{|<f(z),H_{0}>|}{||f(z)||.||H_{0}||}\geq\delta

where
H_{0}=\{x_{0}=0\}
is a coordinate hyperplane.

(iii)

If
f(z)\in\bigcup_{j=1}^{2N+1}H_{j}
, then

\frac{|<\nabla f(z),H_{k}>|}{|f_{0}(z)|^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\delta}

for
k=1,\ldots,2N+1
.

Then
\mathcal{F}
is normal in
D
.

1.5
Sharing of functions and hyperplanes

We now give the definition of sharing hyperplanes, which extends the definition of sharing values.

Definition 1.6
.

Suppose
f
and
g
are two holomorphic functions from domain
D
into
\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})
and
H
be a hyperplane in
\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})
. If there exists some (and thus all) reduced representations
\tilde{f}
and
\tilde{g}
of
f
and
g
respectively such that
<\tilde{f},H>
and
<\tilde{g},H>
share(or partially share) zero on
D
, we say that
f
and
g
share(respectively partially share)
H
on
D
.

By remark
2
,
<\tilde{f}(z),H>=0
is independent of the choice of reduced representation of
f
. Therefore sharing(or partial sharing) of hyperplanes is well defined.

We can similarly define sharing(or partial sharing) of a moving hyperplane
H(z)
by the functions
f
and
g
if
<\tilde{f},H(z)>
and
<\tilde{g},H(z)>
share(respectively partially share) zero on
D
.

Main results

In this paper we generalize the theorem
E
to the case where we can take moving hyperplanes that can vary for different functions of the family
\mathcal{F}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of holomorphic maps of a domain

into

(i)

If
\nabla f(z)\in H_{j}^{f}(z)

f(z)\in H_{j}^{f}(z)
, for

.

(ii)

If
f(z)\in\bigcup_{j=1}^{2N+1}H_{j}^{f}(z)

\frac{|<f(z),H_{0}>|}{||f(z)||.||H_{0}||}\geq\delta

(iii)

We need the following lemma to relate the normality of families of complex valued holomorphic functions with the normality of corresponding functions lifted to
\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{C})
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let

be a domain in
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathcal{F}
be a family of complex valued holomorphic functions on
D
. The family
\mathcal{F}^{*}=\{F:D\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{C})\ \text{with}\ \tilde{F}(z)=(1,z,f(z)):f\in\mathcal{F}\}
is normal in
D
if and only if
\mathcal{F}
is normal in
D
.

Using our representation of complex valued holomorphic functions as holomorphic curves in
\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{C})
, and a judicious choice of hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{C})
we obtain the following normality criterion which requires among other things a boundedness condition on derivatives at fixed points of the respective functions.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of complex valued holomorphic functions on a domain
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}
. Let
A
and
B
be two disjoint compact subsets of unit disk
\mathbb{D}
not containing the origin and
M
be a positive number. Suppose for each
f\in\mathcal{F}
there exist two holomorphic functions
a_{1}^{f}(z)
and
a_{2}^{f}(z)
defined on
D
and having range in
A
and
B
respectively such that the following conditions are satisfied :

(i)

f^{\prime}(z)=a_{j}^{f}(z)
implies
f(z)=a_{j}^{f}(z)
and
f(z)=a_{j}^{f}(z)
implies
|f^{\prime}(z)|\leq M
for

.

(ii)

z
is a fixed point of
f
wherever
f^{\prime}
assumes 1 in
D
.

(iii)

|f^{\prime}(z)|\leq M
, whenever
z
is a fixed point of

.

Then
\mathcal{F}
is a normal family.

That condition
(i)
is essential can be seen by the following example:

Example 4
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\{f_{n}(z)=e^{nz}+z:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
defined on
\mathbb{C}
. Here each

for any
z\in\mathbb{C}
. Also it is clear that no member of this family can have any fixed point. But
\mathcal{F}
is not a normal family.

Theorem

enables us to formulate a counterexample to
the converse of Bloch’s principle
[
2
]
:
A family of holomorphic functions which have property P in common in a domain

is (apt to be) a normal family in

if P can not be possessed by non-constant entire functions.

Counterexample

Consider the function

. Then taking

we see that

satisfies all the conditions required in Theorem

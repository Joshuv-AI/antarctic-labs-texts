A ​ d ​ S 3 × S 3 AdS_{3}\times S^{3} Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude with KK modes
Hongliang Jiang, and De-liang Zhong

Abstract
We study the first curvature correction to the string amplitude of four Kaluza–Klein (KK) modes on A ​ d ​ S 3 × S 3 × M 4 AdS_{3}\times S^{3}\times M_{4} , with M 4 = K ​ 3 M_{4}=K3 or T 4 T^{4} , in type IIB string theory, which is holographically dual to the four-point correlator ⟨ 𝒪 p 1 ​ 𝒪 p 2 ​ 𝒪 p 3 ​ 𝒪 p 4 ⟩ \langle\mathcal{O}_{p_{1}}\mathcal{O}_{p_{2}}\mathcal{O}_{p_{3}}\mathcal{O}_{p_{4}}\rangle of certain half-BPS operators in the boundary D1-D5 CFT. The result takes the form of an integral over the Riemann sphere, analogous to the flat-space Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude, but with insertions of single-valued multiple polylogarithms of weight three. Our results are obtained in two steps. First, we derive the A ​ d ​ S 3 × S 3 AdS_{3}\times S^{3} Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude in the special case ⟨ 𝒪 p ​ 𝒪 p ​ 𝒪 1 ​ 𝒪 1 ⟩ \langle\mathcal{O}_{p}\mathcal{O}_{p}\mathcal{O}_{1}\mathcal{O}_{1}\rangle , by matching the CFT block expansion with an ansatz based on single-valued multiple polylogarithms. We then employ the A ​ d ​ S × S {AdS}\times S Mellin formalism to generalize the result to the general case of four arbitrary KK modes ⟨ 𝒪 p 1 ​ 𝒪 p 2 ​ 𝒪 p 3 ​ 𝒪 p 4 ⟩ \langle\mathcal{O}_{p_{1}}\mathcal{O}_{p_{2}}\mathcal{O}_{p_{3}}\mathcal{O}_{p_{4}}\rangle . Our analysis yields an infinite set of results for operator anomalous dimensions and OPE data in D1-D5 CFT at strong coupling. In particular, the resulting scaling dimensions of certain operators are shown to be consistent with classical string theory computations.

Introduction

The AdS/CFT correspondence has become an indispensable tool for studying quantum gravity, offering a quantitative, precise, and non-perturbative framework to probe the fundamental nature of gravity. Establishing a rigorous proof of the AdS/CFT correspondence remains a central challenge in theoretical physics. Among various cases, one of the most promising candidates is the duality between
\mathcal{N}=4
Super Yang–Mills (SYM) theory and type IIB string theory on
{AdS}_{5}\times S^{5}
, due to the maximal supersymmetry it preserves. Over the past few decades, significant progress has been made in understanding
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM through various approaches, including localization
Pestun:2016zxk
and integrability
Beisert:2010jr
;
Gromov:2013pga
. However, directly computing physical observables from the worldsheet formulation of type IIB string theory on
{AdS}_{5}\times S^{5}
remains a formidable challenge, primarily due to the difficulty of handling Ramond–Ramond (RR) flux backgrounds.

Nevertheless, remarkable progress has been made in the past few years by providing a worldsheet description of graviton scattering in AdS space. Instead of deriving the worldsheet formulation directly from string theory, an ansatz was proposed for the worldsheet integral involving single-valued multiple polylogarithms (SVMPLs), which was fully fixed by comparison with the flat space Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude and consistency with CFT expectations
Alday:2022uxp
;
Alday:2022xwz
;
Alday:2023jdk
;
Alday:2023mvu
. The resulting expression is therefore referred to as the AdS Virasoro-Shapiro (VS) amplitude. This approach circumvents the difficulties associated with Ramond–Ramond fluxes and is powerful enough to determine the first non-trivial curvature correction in various AdS backgrounds
Alday:2024yax
;
Alday:2024ksp
;
Chester:2024wnb
;
Chester:2024esn
.

One can also consider the case

supergravity coupled to

tensor multiplets, where

Further compactifying on

{AdS}_{3}

The underlying symmetry is given by
PSU(1,1|2)_{L}\times PSU(1,1|2)_{R}

4

The symmetry algebra is further enhanced to the small
\mathcal{N}=4

The bosonic subgroup is
SL(2,\mathbb{R})_{L}\times SU(2)_{L}\times SL(2,\mathbb{R})_{R}\times SU(2)_{R}
, which matches the isometry group
SO(2,2)\times SO(4)
of the

Each operator is labeled by the quantum numbers
h,j,\bar{h},\bar{j}
, corresponding to the Cartan generators of the bosonic subgroup. The scaling dimension
\Delta

\Delta=h+\bar{h},\ell=h-\bar{h}
.

In this paper, we are interested in the
1/2
-BPS operators coupling to the tensor multiplets, whose superconformal primaries are denoted as
s_{p}^{I}
, where
p=1,2,\cdots
labels the KK modes under the reduction on
S^{3}
, and

labels the vector representation of the flavor symmetry group
SO(h_{1,1}+1)
. The quantum numbers are given by
h=j=\bar{h}=\bar{j}=p/2
.

The flavor symmetry
SO(h_{1,1}+1)

SO(h_{1,1})
. See e.g.
Taylor:2007hs
. We will thus focus on the unique singlet under the
SO(h_{1,1})

\mathcal{O}_{p}
. For
p=1
, the multiplet
\mathcal{O}_{1}
includes a marginal operator that couples to the string coupling, which is dual to the dilaton of string theory. In the planar limit that we consider, this multiplet does not care about the compact spacetime
M_{4}
and is invariant under certain duality symmetries, including T-duality. For higher
p>1
,
\mathcal{O}_{p}
can be regarded as the cousins of
\mathcal{O}_{1}
with higher KK modes under the reduction on
S^{3}
. The goal of this paper is to study the correlation function of four arbitrary
\mathcal{O}_{p}
’s beyond the supergravity limit.

2.2
Superconformal kinematics in position space

The operator
\mathcal{O}_{p}
transforms as spin-
(p/2,p/2)
representation under the
SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{R}
R-symmetry, and carries
p
symmetric vector indices under
SO(4)
. To simplify notation, we introduce an index-free form

\mathcal{O}_{p}(\mathsf{x};\mathsf{y})\coloneq\mathcal{O}_{p}^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{p}}(\mathsf{x})\mathsf{y}_{\mu_{1}}\cdots\mathsf{y}_{\mu_{p}}~,

(1)

where
\mathsf{y}_{\mu}
is an
SO(4)
null vector satisfying
\mathsf{y}_{\mu}\mathsf{y}^{\mu}=0
. Using the isomorphism
SO(4)\simeq SU(2)\times SU(2)
, we can parametrize
\mathsf{y}^{\mu}=\sigma_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}^{\mu}y^{\alpha}\bar{y}^{\dot{\alpha}}
and label
\mathcal{O}
in spinor representation

\mathcal{O}_{p}(\mathsf{x};\mathsf{y})=\mathcal{O}_{p}(\mathsf{x};y,\bar{y})=\mathcal{O}_{p}^{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{p},\dot{\alpha}_{1}\cdots\dot{\alpha}_{p}}y_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots y_{\alpha_{p}}\;\bar{y}_{\dot{\alpha}_{1}}\cdots\bar{y}_{\dot{\alpha}_{p}}~.

(2)

Similarly, instead of using the real Euclidean coordinate
\mathsf{x}^{1},\mathsf{x}^{2}
to label the position of operators in the dual 2d CFT, we introduce complex coordinates
z=\mathsf{x}^{1}+\mathrm{i}\,\mathsf{x}^{2},\bar{z}=\mathsf{x}^{1}-\mathrm{i}\,\mathsf{x}^{2}
. The Lorentz-invariant distance between two points is then given by

\mathsf{x}_{ij}^{2}=(\mathsf{x}^{1}_{i}-\mathsf{x}_{j}^{1})^{2}+(\mathsf{x}^{2}_{i}-\mathsf{x}_{j}^{2})^{2}=z_{ij}\bar{z}_{ij}~,\quad\text{with}\quad z_{ij}=z_{i}-z_{j}~,\;\bar{z}_{ij}=\bar{z}_{i}-\bar{z}_{j}\,~.

(3)

For the R-symmetry polarization vectors, we define analogously
\mathsf{y}_{ij}=\mathsf{y}_{i}^{\mu}\mathsf{y}_{j\,\mu}=y_{ij}\bar{y}_{ij}
, where
y_{ij}=\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}y_{i}^{\alpha}y_{j}^{\beta},\bar{y}_{ij}=\epsilon_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}y_{i}^{\dot{\alpha}}y_{j}^{\dot{\beta}}
.
5
5

5

By rescaling, one can choose
y_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
\mathrm{y}_{i}\end{pmatrix}
, then
y_{ij}=\mathrm{y}_{i}-\mathrm{y}_{j}
.

We can construct conformal and R-symmetry cross ratios from the kinematic building blocks introduced above. There are two conformal cross ratios,
U
and
V
, and two R-symmetry cross ratios,
\sigma
and
\tau
, defined as

U=\frac{\mathsf{x}_{12}^{2}\mathsf{x}_{34}^{2}}{\mathsf{x}_{13}^{2}\mathsf{x}_{24}^{2}}=z\bar{z}~,\qquad V=\frac{\mathsf{x}_{14}^{2}\mathsf{x}_{23}^{2}}{\mathsf{x}_{13}^{2}\mathsf{x}_{24}^{2}}=(1-z)(1-\bar{z})~,

(4)

and

\sigma=\frac{\mathsf{y}_{13}\mathsf{y}_{24}}{\mathsf{y}_{12}\mathsf{y}_{34}}=\alpha\bar{\alpha}~,\qquad\tau=\frac{\mathsf{y}_{14}\mathsf{y}_{23}}{\mathsf{y}_{12}\mathsf{y}_{34}}=(1-\alpha)(1-\bar{\alpha})~.

(5)

In terms of spinor variables, the cross ratios are equivalently given by:

z=\frac{z_{12}z_{34}}{z_{13}z_{24}}~,\quad\bar{z}=\frac{\bar{z}_{12}\bar{z}_{34}}{\bar{z}_{13}\bar{z}_{24}}~,\quad\alpha=\frac{1}{y}=\frac{y_{13}y_{24}}{y_{12}y_{34}}~,\quad\bar{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\bar{y}}=\frac{\bar{y}_{13}\bar{y}_{24}}{\bar{y}_{12}\bar{y}_{34}}~.

(6)

It is also convenient to introduce the following alternative R-symmetry cross ratios:

\displaystyle\widetilde{U}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathsf{y}_{12}\mathsf{y}_{34}}{\mathsf{y}_{13}\mathsf{y}_{24}}=y\bar{y}=\frac{1}{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}=\frac{1}{\sigma}~,\qquad

(7)

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathsf{y}_{14}\mathsf{y}_{23}}{\mathsf{y}_{13}\mathsf{y}_{24}}=(1-y)(1-\bar{y})=\frac{(\alpha-1)(\bar{\alpha}-1)}{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}=\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-\bar{\alpha})}{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}=\tau/\sigma~.

(8)

Finally, in superconformal kinematics, it is useful to define the combinations:

g_{ij}=\frac{y_{ij}}{z_{ij}}~,\qquad\bar{g}_{ij}=\frac{\bar{y}_{ij}}{\bar{z}_{ij}}~,\qquad\bm{g}_{ij}=g_{ij}\bar{g}_{ij}~.

(9)

Without loss of generality, we will assume
p_{1}\geq p_{2}\geq p_{3}\geq p_{4}
, and study the four-point function:

\langle\mathcal{O}_{p_{1}}(\mathsf{x}_{1};\mathsf{y}_{1})\mathcal{O}_{p_{2}}(\mathsf{x}_{2};\mathsf{y}_{2})\mathcal{O}_{p_{3}}(\mathsf{x}_{3};\mathsf{y}_{3})\mathcal{O}_{p_{4}}(\mathsf{x}_{4};\mathsf{y}_{4})\rangle={\bf K}\mathcal{G}_{p_{1}p_{2}p_{3}p_{4}}(z,\bar{z};\alpha,\bar{\alpha})~,

(10)

which will be abbreviated as
\langle p_{1}p_{2}p_{3}p_{4}\rangle
. The prefactor
\mathbf{K}
captures the kinematic dependence and is given by

\displaystyle{\bf K}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bm{g}_{12}^{\frac{p_{1}+p_{2}-p_{3}-p_{4}}{2}}\bm{g}_{13}^{\frac{p_{1}+p_{3}-p_{2}-p_{4}}{2}}\bm{g}_{14}^{p_{4}}\bm{\;}\bm{g}_{23}^{\frac{p_{2}+p_{3}+p_{4}-p_{1}}{2}}\Big(\frac{\bm{g}_{12}\bm{g}_{34}}{\bm{g}_{14}\bm{g}_{23}}\Big)^{L}

(11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bm{g}_{12}^{\frac{p_{1}+p_{2}-p_{3}-p_{4}}{2}}\bm{g}_{13}^{\frac{p_{1}+p_{3}-p_{2}-p_{4}}{2}}\bm{\;}\bm{g}_{23}^{\frac{p_{2}+p_{3}-p_{4}-p_{1}}{2}}(\bm{g}_{14}\bm{g}_{23})^{p_{4}}\Big(\frac{V}{U\tau}\Big)^{L}~.

(12)

Here we used the relations:

\frac{\bm{g}_{12}\bm{g}_{34}}{\bm{g}_{14}\bm{g}_{23}}=\frac{V\tilde{U}}{U\tilde{V}}=\frac{V/\sigma}{U\tau/\sigma}=\frac{V}{U\tau}~,\qquad\frac{\bm{g}_{14}\bm{g}_{23}}{\bm{g}_{13}\bm{g}_{24}}=\frac{\tilde{V}}{V}=\frac{\tau}{V\sigma}~.

(13)

The function
\mathcal{G}
satisfies the superconformal Ward identities and admits the decomposition
Rastelli:2019gtj
:

\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}_{0}+(1-z\alpha)(1-\bar{z}\bar{\alpha})\mathcal{H}(U,V;\sigma,\tau)~,

(14)

where
\mathcal{G}_{0}
is the protected part that obeys

\mathcal{G}_{0}(z,\bar{z};\alpha,\bar{\alpha}=1/\bar{z})=f(z,\alpha)~,\qquad\mathcal{G}_{0}(z,\bar{z};\alpha=1/z,\bar{\alpha})=f(\bar{z},\bar{\alpha})~,

(15)

and survives in the free limit due to non-renormalization theorems.

The reduced correlator
\mathcal{H}
receives contributions from both protected and unprotected operators, coming respectively from the short and long multiplets of the SCFTs. In this work, we will focus on contributions from the unprotected long multiplets. Importantly,
\mathcal{H}
is a polynomial in
\alpha
and
\bar{\alpha}
of degree
L-1

Rastelli:2019gtj
, where the
extremality

L
is defined as

L\coloneq\frac{p_{4}-p_{1}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\min(p_{1}+p_{4},p_{2}+p_{3})=\begin{cases}p_{4}&\text{if}\ p_{4}-p_{3}\leq p_{2}-p_{1}\\
\frac{p_{2}+p_{3}+p_{4}-p_{1}}{2}&\text{if}\ p_{4}-p_{3}\geq p_{2}-p_{1}\\
\end{cases}~.

(16)

2.3
Mellin amplitude and Borel transformation

The Mellin amplitude for the reduced correlator is defined by the following integral transform
Rastelli:2019gtj
;
Behan:2024srh

\displaystyle\hskip-34.44434pt\mathcal{H}(U,V,\sigma,\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\mathrm{i}\infty}^{\mathrm{i}\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t}{(4\pi\mathrm{i})^{2}}\,U^{\frac{s-p_{3}-p_{4}}{2}+L}\,V^{\frac{t+p_{4}-p_{1}}{2}-L}\,M(s,t,\sigma,\tau)

(17)

\displaystyle\times\Gamma\Big(\frac{p_{1}+p_{2}-s}{2}\Big)\Gamma\Big(\frac{p_{3}+p_{4}-s}{2}\Big)\Gamma\Big(\frac{p_{1}+p_{4}-t}{2}\Big)\Gamma\Big(\frac{p_{2}+p_{3}-t}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\times\Gamma\Big(\frac{p_{1}+p_{3}-\tilde{u}}{2}\Big)\Gamma\Big(\frac{p_{2}+p_{4}-\tilde{u}}{2}\Big)~.

Here, the variables
s,t,\tilde{u}
are analogous to the standard Mandelstam variables in flat-space four-point amplitudes, and they satisfy
s+t+\tilde{u}=p_{1}+p_{2}+p_{3}+p_{4}-2
.

To obtain the curvature expansion near flat space, one has to perform an additional Borel transformation to the Mellin amplitude, which defines the AdS Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude
A
as
Alday:2024ksp
;
Penedones:2010ue

A(S,T,\sigma,\tau)=\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(\Sigma)\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\alpha}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{e^{\alpha}}{\alpha^{\Sigma+1}}M\Big(\frac{2\sqrt{\lambda}S}{\alpha}+\frac{2\Sigma-2}{3},\frac{2\sqrt{\lambda}T}{\alpha}+\frac{2\Sigma-2}{3},\sigma,\tau\Big)~,

(18)

where we define

\Sigma=\frac{1}{2}(p_{1}+p_{2}+p_{3}+p_{4})~.

(19)

Physically, the Borel transformation provides a resurgent resummation of the asymptotic strong coupling expansion of
\mathcal{M}
, yielding a well-defined amplitude that reorganizes the curvature corrections in power series of
\lambda^{-1/2}
in the flat-space limit.

In the strong coupling or large AdS radius limit,
A
reduces to the flat space Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude:

6

\langle p_{1}p_{2}p_{3}p_{4}\rangle
, since all KK modes become massless in that limit. In the simplest case of
\langle 1111\rangle

Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude for
\langle pp11\rangle

In this section, we derive the Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude for
\langle pp11\rangle
by employing a worldsheet ansatz involving SVMPL, and by imposing consistency with the conformal block expansion in CFT, the flat space limit, and the supergravity limit.
For the correlator
\langle pp11\rangle
considered here, the extremality parameter is
L=1
(see (
16
)). As a result, the reduced correlator
\mathcal{H}
, the Mellin amplitude
M
, and the Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude
A
are all
independent
of the R-symmetry cross ratios
\sigma
and
\tau

3.1
Supergravity limit

Let us first understand the supergravity limit of the AdS VS amplitude, which has been studied in Mellin space.

Given the Mellin amplitude, we can consider its Borel transformation (
18
) which, in the current case, reduces to

A(S,T)=\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(p+1)\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\alpha}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{e^{\alpha}}{\alpha^{p+2}}\,M\left(\frac{2\sqrt{\lambda}S}{\alpha}+\frac{2p}{3},\frac{2\sqrt{\lambda}T}{\alpha}+\frac{2p}{3}\right)~.

(21)

It satisfies the crossing relation:

A(S,T)=A(S,U)~,\qquad\text{where}\qquad S+T+U=0~.

(22)

The AdS amplitude can be expanded around flat space as

\displaystyle A(S,T)

\displaystyle=A^{(0)}(S,T)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}A^{(1)}(S,T)+\cdots~,

(23)

where
A^{(0)}(S,T)
is the flat-space VS amplitude (
20
), and the rest of terms are curvature corrections.

At each order
A^{(k)}
in (
23
), the leading term corresponds to the supergravity contribution and is singular in the small momentum limit. It can be obtained from the Mellin amplitude in the supergravity limit, which has been studied extensively
Giusto:2019pxc
:

M_{\text{SG}}(s,t)=-\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{t+1-p}-\frac{1}{\tilde{u}+1-p}~,\qquad\tilde{u}=2p-s-t~.

(24)

Inserting it to (
21
) yields

\displaystyle A_{\text{SG}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{\text{SG}}^{(0)}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}A_{\text{SG}}^{(1)}+\cdots

(25)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{S^{2}+T^{2}+U^{2}}{4STU}+\frac{p}{12\sqrt{\lambda}}\Big(\frac{2p}{S^{2}}-\frac{p-3}{T^{2}}-\frac{p-3}{U^{2}}\Big)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)~.

As a result, we can write both amplitudes as
M=M_{\text{SG}}+M_{\text{string}}
and
A=A_{\text{SG}}+A_{\text{string}}
, where the stringy non-supergravity parts are all polynomials in the Mandelstam-like variables.

From the CFT perspective,
the Mellin amplitude
M
receives contributions from both the short and long multiplets, just like the reduced correlator
\mathcal{H}
which is related to
M
via (
17
).
The protected operators completely determine the supergravity part of the amplitude, while long multiplet leads to the stringy corrections to the CFT data. In the planar or large-
N
limit, those single-trace stringy operators have large anomalous dimension or twist of order
\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}})
. One goal in the rest of the section is to study the CFT data associated with these stringy operators.

3.2
Poles from block expansion

To find those stringy corrections, we need to consider the block expansion in CFT. In Mellin space, we can expand the Mellin amplitude in terms of the Mellin blocks corresponding to
S
-channel and

Conclusion

In this paper, we generalize the flat-space Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude to AdS space and derive the leading curvature correction to string amplitudes involving four arbitrary KK modes on
{AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}\times M_{4}
in type IIB string theory. The result takes the form of an integral over the Riemann sphere, analogous to the flat-space Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude, but with insertions of single-valued multiple polylogarithms. Our derivation is fully self-consistent and allows us to extract an infinite amount of CFT data in the D1-D5 CFT, which could be useful for future integrability and bootstrap studies.

L_{a_{1}a_{2}\ldots a_{r}}(z)\coloneq\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t-a_{1}}L_{a_{2}\ldots a_{r}}(t)~,

(99)

where the words
a_{i}
take values in
\{0,1\}
. Explicitly, the multiple polylogarithms up to weight three are given in terms of combinations of logarithm, polylogarithms and zeta values as follows:

\displaystyle L_{0^{p}}(z)=

\displaystyle\frac{\log^{p}z}{p!}~,

(100)

\displaystyle L_{1^{p}}(z)=

\displaystyle\frac{\log^{p}(1-z)}{p!}~,

\displaystyle L_{01}(z)=

\displaystyle-\operatorname{Li}_{2}(z)~,

\displaystyle L_{10}(z)=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Li}_{2}(z)+\log(1-z)\log(z)~,

\displaystyle L_{010}(z)=

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{Li}_{3}(z)-\operatorname{Li}_{2}(z)\log(z)~,

\displaystyle L_{001}(z)=

\displaystyle-\operatorname{Li}_{3}(z)~,

\displaystyle L_{100}(z)=

\displaystyle-\operatorname{Li}_{3}(z)+\operatorname{Li}_{2}(z)\log(z)+\frac{1}{2}\log(1-z)\log^{2}(z)~,

\displaystyle L_{101}(z)=

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{Li}_{3}(1-z)-2\zeta(3)-\log(1-z)\left(2\operatorname{Li}_{2}(1-z)\right.\left.+\operatorname{Li}_{2}(z)+\log(1-z)\log(z)\right)~,

\displaystyle L_{110}(z)=

\displaystyle-\operatorname{Li}_{3}(1-z)+\frac{1}{6}\pi^{2}\log(1-z)+\zeta(3)~,

\displaystyle L_{011}(z)=

\displaystyle-\operatorname{Li}_{3}(1-z)+\operatorname{Li}_{2}(1-z)\log(1-z)+\frac{1}{2}\log(z)\log^{2}(1-z)+\zeta(3)~,

where
\operatorname{Li}_{n}(z)
denotes the polylogarithms of order
n
.

MPLs are holomorphic functions of
z
but they are not singe-valued. The single-valued version of MPLs can be constructed following the method in
Brown:2004ugm
. For our purposes, it suffices to know that up to weight three, single-valued multiple polylogarithms (SVMPLs)
\mathcal{L}
are constructed from MPLs as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{000}(z)=L_{000}(z)+L_{000}(\bar{z})+L_{00}(z)L_{0}(\bar{z})+L_{0}(z)L_{00}(\bar{z}),

(101)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{001}(z)=L_{001}(z)+L_{100}(\bar{z})+L_{00}(z)L_{1}(\bar{z})+L_{0}(z)L_{10}(\bar{z}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{010}(z)=L_{010}(z)+L_{010}(\bar{z})+L_{01}(z)L_{0}(\bar{z})+L_{0}(z)L_{01}(\bar{z}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{100}(z)=L_{100}(z)+L_{001}(\bar{z})+L_{10}(z)L_{0}(\bar{z})+L_{1}(z)L_{00}(\bar{z}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{110}(z)=L_{110}(z)+L_{011}(\bar{z})+L_{11}(z)L_{0}(\bar{z})+L_{1}(z)L_{01}(\bar{z}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{101}(z)=L_{101}(z)+L_{101}(\bar{z})+L_{10}(z)L_{1}(\bar{z})+L_{1}(z)L_{10}(\bar{z}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{011}(z)=L_{011}(z)+L_{110}(\bar{z})+L_{01}(z)L_{1}(\bar{z})+L_{0}(z)L_{11}(\bar{z}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{111}(z)=L_{111}(z)+L_{111}(\bar{z})+L_{11}(z)L_{1}(\bar{z})+L_{1}(z)L_{11}(\bar{z}).

Appendix B
Mack Polynomials

Following
Dolan:2011dv
, see also
Mack:2009mi
;
Fardelli:2023fyq
;
Dey:2017fab
;
Mack:2009mi
, the Mack polynomial
Q^{\Delta_{12},\Delta_{34},\tau}_{\ell,m}(s)
admits the following double sum form:

\displaystyle Q^{\Delta_{12},\Delta_{34},\tau}_{\ell,m}(s)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\ell}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\ell-k}(-m)_{k}\left(m+\frac{s+\tau}{2}\right)_{n}\mu(\ell,k,n,\Delta_{12},\Delta_{34},\tau)~,

(102)

where
(a)_{b}
denotes the Pochhammer symbol, and
\mu
is given by

\displaystyle\mu(\ell,k,n,\Delta_{12},\Delta_{34},\tau)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k+n+\ell}\frac{2^{\ell}\,\ell!\,\Gamma(\ell+\tau-1)}{\Gamma(2\ell+\tau-1)}\frac{(\ell+\tau-1)_{n}}{k!\,n!\,(-k-n+\ell)!}\left(n+\frac{d}{2}-1+\frac{\Delta_{34}-\Delta_{12}}{2}\right)_{k}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(k+n-\frac{\Delta_{12}}{2}+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)_{-k-n+\ell}\left(k+n+\frac{\Delta_{34}}{2}+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)_{-k-n+\ell}

\displaystyle\quad\times{}_{4}F_{3}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-k,\,3-d-n-\ell,\,1-\frac{d}{2}+\frac{\Delta_{12}}{2}+\frac{\tau}{2},\,1-\frac{d}{2}-\frac{\Delta_{34}}{2}+\frac{\tau}{2}\\
2-\frac{d}{2}-\ell,\,2-\frac{d}{2}+\frac{\Delta_{12}}{2}-\frac{\Delta_{34}}{2}-k-n,\,2-d+\tau\end{array};1\right)\,.

The normalization of Mack polynomial is chosen such that
Q_{\ell,m}^{\tau,d}(s)=s^{\ell}+\cdots
. For
m=0
, the Mack polynomial reduces to

Q_{\ell,0}^{\tau,d}(s)=\frac{(-2)^{\ell}\left(\frac{\tau}{2}-\frac{\Delta_{12}}{2}\right)_{\ell}\left(\frac{\Delta_{34}}{2}+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)_{\ell}}{(\ell+\tau-1)_{\ell}}\,_{3}F_{2}\left(-\ell,\frac{s}{2}+\frac{\tau}{2},\ell+\tau-1;\frac{\tau}{2}-\frac{\Delta_{12}}{2},\frac{\Delta_{34}}{2}+\frac{\tau}{2};1\right)~.\\

(105)

The normalization factor
\kappa
used in the main text reads

\displaystyle\kappa_{\ell,m,\tau,d}^{\left(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{-2^{1-\ell}(\ell+\tau-1)_{\ell}\Gamma(2\ell+\tau)}{m!(1-\frac{d}{2}+\ell+\tau)_{m}}\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(-m+\frac{p_{1}+p_{2}}{2}-\frac{\tau}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(-m+\frac{p_{3}+p_{4}}{2}-\frac{\tau}{2}\right)}

(106)

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(-\frac{p_{1}-p_{2}}{2}+\ell+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{p_{1}-p_{2}}{2}+\ell+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(-\frac{p_{3}-p_{4}}{2}+\ell+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{p_{3}-p_{4}}{2}+\ell+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)}\,.

Cytokinin-mediated enhancement of potato growth and yield by Verticillium Dahliae effector VDAL under low temperature stress
Authors: Jun Chun, Min Wan, Hongwei Guo, Qingpei Zhang, Yan Feng, Yunchuan Tang, Bo Zhu, Youshun Sang, Shenglin Jing, Tao Chen, Zixian Zeng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe pathogen Verticillium dahliae secreted effector V. dahliae Aspf2-like protein (VDAL) has been found to cause leaf wilting in cotton, but the ectopic expression of VDAL-encoding gene enhances the resistance to V. dahliae in cotton and Arabidopsis. The application of the VDAL protein powder with optimal dosage promotes the growth and yield in multiple crop species, such as rice and wheat. However, the promotive effects of VDAL on these aspects are sporadically reported in asexually propagated species, including potato, while the molecular regulatory network involved in the process remains unclear. In this study, we observed that VDAL promotes sprouting of the potato pre-basic seed (PBS) tubers and enhances the development of both above-ground and below-ground tissues. Strikingly, VDAL increases the tuber yield in both greenhouse and field trials by up to 18.97%. The time-course transcriptomic analysis and the endogenous phytohormone detection revealed that cytokinin may play an important role in response to VDAL-promoted growth. Interestingly, VDAL-treated PBS tubers show higher resistance to cold temperature (late-spring cold), a phenomenon that is diminished when the lovastatin, a cytokinin inhibitor is applied, indicating that the VDAL-promoted potato growth, particularly under low temperature, is associated with cytokinin.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-024-05840-9.Keywords: Potato, VDAL, Tuber yield, Low temperature, Transcriptome

Introduction

The protection from diverse pathogens for plants requires effective immune system. Upon infection, the immune system is activated by two types of the pathogen-derived molecules, including the immune response pathogen-associated molecular pattern (PAMPs)-triggered immunity (PTI) and the effector-triggered immunity (ETI) [1, 2]. PTI generally occurs when pathogen initially contact the plant cell, activating a series of rapid immune responses, for instance, hormonal changes, alternation in the expression of resistance gene, activation of mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) and reactive oxygen species (ROS) burst [1, 3]. The delivery of the pathogen secreted effectors into plant cells triggers ETI, causing reprogramming of the transcriptional profile in the host cells [4, 5], including changes in phytohormone network [6]. Further, PTI can interconnect with ETI to potentially enhance the plant resistance to pathogens [7].

Verticillium dahliae Aspf2-like protein (VDAL) is an effector secreted by V. dahliae, which is a soil-borne hemi-biotrophic fungal pathogen causing one of the devastating diseases Verticillium wilt in multiple agronomical important plants, such as cotton [8, 9] and potato [10, 11]. However, the ectopic expression of VDAL in Arabidopsis and cotton significantly enhanced the resistance to V. dahliae infection without affecting the plant development [12], through the competitive binding to the plant U-box 25 (PUB25) and PUB26 with the transcription factor MYB6, which promotes resistance to Verticillium wilt. Besides, the application of VDAL with optimal concentration not only enhances plant tolerance to abiotic stresses [13, 14], but it also promotes plant growth and yield [13, 15, 16]. For example, foliar spray of the VDAL solution substantially enhanced wheat resistance to drought, while it significantly promotes the growth during the winter irrigation period [13].

Potato is the fourth largest staple crop in China [10]. As the top producer, China produced one fourth of the world’s production, accounting for 95.6 million tons in about 5.7 million hectares (FAOSTAT, 2022). One of the most commonly used early-maturity cultivars, “Favorita”, has been cultivated in 5.4 million hectares from the year of 2003 to 2019 in China. The other widely planted cultivar “Xisen 6” with late maturation has been produced in one million hectares in China from 2017 to 2023 (https://www.natesc.org.cn/). Both cultivars have been adapted to the main cultivation season of potato (winter cropping areas), which usually starts before winter and ends before summer. However, abiotic stresses, such as drought, frost and late-spring cold, are frequently occurred during this period, due to unpredictably extreme weather. These stresses dramatically impede the sprouting of tuber apical bud [17], plant development [18] and resulting in yield reduction [19]. Similar to the sexually propagated species, the foliar spray of VDAL solution in the early-flowering period enhanced the potato resistance to Verticillium wilt and late blight, while it promoted the tuber yield [20]. However, the molecular role of VDAL on potato seed tubers, the major means of propagation for potato, remains unclear.

In this study, we conducted both greenhouse experiments and field trials using multiple cultivars, including both early- and late-maturity cultivars which have been commonly used in China and Sichuan Province. We observed consistently promotive effect of VDAL on sprouting, stolon development, total yield and other agronomic traits. The time-course transcriptome analysis suggested the phytohormone, particularly cytokinin, may play an important role in apical bud sprouting, which is supported by the evidence of the endogenous phytohormones detected from the VDAL-treated tubers. Interestingly, we found that VDAL promotes apical bud sprouting under low temperatures, likely with the assistance of cytokinin. We hypothesize that the transcription factor WRKY33 may be involved in connecting cytokinin signaling with cold tolerance, though further validation is required.

Results

Evaluation of optimal VDAL concentration for tuber sprouting

Pre-basic seed (PBS) tubers with uniform weight (3 g/tuber) were stored at 2–4℃ in the dark for two months to break dormancy. PBS tubers with 0–2 mm apical bud were treated with the VDAL in the concentrations of 0 ppm, 1 ppm, 3 ppm, 5 ppm, 7 ppm (designated as CK, V1, V3, V5 and V7 tubers), respectively, and sprouted under the temperature of 12–14℃ in the dark. Three biological replicates with a total of 30 PBS tubers were evaluated for each treatment. Three days after the VDAL treatment (DAT), PBS tubers from V1-V7 displayed significantly longer apical buds compared to the mock group (CK) (Fig. 1a, Table 1). However, longer apical buds were only observed for the V3 and V5 tubers 7 DAT (Table 1). The apical buds from the V1 and V7 tubers were significantly shorter than those from the CK tubers (Fig. 1b, Table 1), suggesting both low and high VDAL concentrations suppress the development of the apical buds, while 3–5 ppm VDAL promotes the sprouting. This result is supported by the study in potato [20] and rice [15]. Since the apical buds were consistently longer in the V3 tubers than those in the V5 tubers at all observed time points (Table 1), the optimal VDAL concentration for the PBS tuber sprouting appears to be 3 ppm.

VDAL enhances plant development and tuber yield

We further evaluated the agronomic traits using a larger number of PBS tubers (150 tubers/biological replicate/treatment). The PBS tubers were treated with 3 ppm VDAL and immediately potted in the soil in the greenhouse. It is interesting that the V3 tubers displayed the highest sprouting rate than other groups at each tested time point, while it reached 96% sprouting rate 30 DAT (Fig. 2a). The sprouting rates of the V3 tubers were 13.34%, 16.66% and 13.34% higher than those of the CK tubers at 26 DAT, 28 DAT and 30 DAT, respectively. Similar to the sprouting rate, the plants derived from the V3 tubers were generally taller than those from other groups, especially 49 DAT (Fig. 2b). The V3 tuber-derived plants were 19.05%, 13.05% and 16.38% taller than CK at each observed time point, respectively.

For below-ground traits, the plants derived from all groups of the VDAL-treated PBS tubers produced significantly more stolon than the CK tubers 49 DAT (Fig. 2c, Figure S1). In addition, the V3 tuber-derived plants generated the largest number of stolon (average of 3.7), which is substantially more than other groups (Fig. 2c). Besides, both V3 and V5 tuber-derived plants produced apparently more swollen stolon than other groups (Fig. 2c). This result indicates that 3 ppm VDAL appears to play a promotive role on the development of stolon. We also observed that the V3 tuber-derived plants produced the largest number of tubers per plant (28.1% more than CK), as well as the large/medium-size tubers (weight > 50 g) per plant (44.4% more than CK), compared to other groups (Fig. 2d). In contrast, the plants derived from all groups produced similar number of small tubers (weight < 50 g) (Fig. 2d). The deduced total yield of the V3 tuber-derived plants reached the highest (19,649.6 kg/ha) among all test groups (Fig. 2e), which is approximately 8.77% more than CK. These results collectively suggest that VDAL, particularly with concentration of 3 ppm, may enhance the development of both above- and below-ground traits.

VDAL enhances sprouting and tuber yield in the multi-year field trials

To confirm the promotive effect of VDAL for the tuber sprouting, we grown the same cultivar “Favorita” in the field (three replicates of 40 m2 for each treatment) in the winter season of 2019. Consistently, the sprouting rate of the V3 tubers was approximately 20% more than that of the CK tubers (Fig. 2f). In addition, the V3 tubers performed vigorous growth in the field compared to the CK tubers (Fig. 3), including longer apical bud and plant height, higher rooting rate and more yielded tubers per plant (Table 2). We also assessed the other cultivar “Xisen 3” (three replicates of 50 m2 for each treatment) in 2020. Similarly, the V3 tubers of “Xisen 3” were associated with better performance on those agronomical traits in the field compared to the CK tubers (Fig. 2f, Table 2).

In addition, we conducted three-year field trials in Yang Ma Village using three cultivars, including “Favorita”, “Xisen 3” and “Xingjia 2”. Intriguingly, the VDAL-treated tubers of all tested cultivars were consistently associated with significantly higher yield than the CK tubers (Table 3). The V3 tuber-derived plants produced 12.99%-18.97% more tubers in weight than the CK tuber-derived plants (Table 3). The two-year field trails in Bai Yu He Village also showed that the VDAL-treated tubers of another cultivar “Xisen 6” produced significantly higher tuber yield than the CK tubers (Table 3). Unexpectedly, small magnitude of the increase in yield (5.06%) was observed in the season of 2021–2022. It is possible that the promotive effect of VDAL on the yield might be compromised at some extend by higher planting density (112,500 plants/ha), comparing to the same cultivar with the planting density of 75,000 plants/ha. Noticeably, the marketable tuber rates derived from the VDAL-treated tubers were similar to the rates derived from the CK tubers in all tested years (Table 3), suggesting the application of VDAL has no harm on the market-acceptable tuber weight. Collectively, these results provide field evidence to support the promotive effect of VDAL on tuber sprouting and the yield of multiple cultivars.

VDAL induces the alternation of the gene expression associated with phytohormone and temperatures

To investigate the effect of VDAL on gene expression, we developed a total of 21 RNA-seq data, including three biological replicates of the V3 and CK tubers at time points of 0, 1, 3 and 7 DAT, respectively (Table S1). An average of 35.7 million (M) uniquely mapped reads were obtained from each data. The RNA-seq data among biological replicates were highly correlated (Pearson’s correlation, R2 = 0.93–0.95, p < 2.2e−16) (Figure S2). After the comparison to the CK tubers at each time point (CKd1, CKd3 and CKd7), we identified a total of 49, 90 and 27 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) (FDR < 0.01, log2 Fold Change (FC) > 1) in the V3 tubers at 1 DAT (V3d1), 3 DAT (V3d3) and 7 DAT (V3d7), respectively. We found 15 up- and 34 down-regulated genes from V3d1 Vs. CKd1 comparison. These up-regulated genes were mainly enriched in the gene ontology (GO) term of purine ribonucleotide binding (Figure S3a), which has been frequently reported in plant response to fungi [21–23]. The down-regulated genes were associated with the process of photosynthesis (Figure S3b). Down-regulation of photosynthetic genes is also observed in rice with the treatment of the VDAL [15]. In comparison between V3d3 and CKd3, a total of 57 up-regulated genes were identified, which were enriched in the process of response to wounding (Figure S3c), whilst 33 down-regulated genes were found to be associated with the metabolic processes and catalytic activities (Figure S3d). No GO enrichment was found for the up- (22) and down-regulated genes (5) from V3d7 Vs. CKd7 comparison.

Further we compared the expression of the genes in the V3 tubers relative to those in CK tubers in a time-course manner. The clustering analysis revealed that seven clusters of the genes displayed distinct expression profiles (Fig. 4a). In cluster 1–3, the genes were expressed at higher levels in the V3 tubers than in the CK tubers 1 DAT, 3 DAT and 7 DAT, respectively (Fig. 4a). In contrast, the genes in cluster 4–6 displayed the opposite expression patterns (Fig. 4a). The cluster 7 showed distinct expression pattern with higher levels in the V3 tubers Vs. the CK tubers 1 DAT and 3 DAT (Fig. 4a). The GO analysis revealed that all clusters of the genes were enriched in the categories related to responses to stimuli and chemicals (Fig. 4b). Interestingly, the genes from the cluster 1–3 were uniquely associated with defense responses, immune responses and multiple hormone responses, suggesting the VDAL treatment may trigger the up-regulation of the genes involved in phytohormones, possibly leading to the promotive effect on sprouting. In comparison, the genes from the cluster 4–6 were specifically enriched in light responses and cytokinin (Fig. 3b), implying that VDAL may promote sprouting via cytokinin. Noticeably, the genes in the cluster 1, 2, 6 and 7 were found to be associated with the biological process of temperature responses, including cold and heat (Fig. 3b), providing a clue that VDAL may also play a role on the temperature-responsive genes.

VDAL promotes tuber sprouting in the low temperature

To explore the potential promotive role of the VDAL on tuber sprouting in the low temperature, we treated the V3 and CK tubers under warm (18–20℃) and low (5–8℃) temperatures. As expected, the warm temperature-treated PBS tubers (V3W and CKW) consistently displayed longer apical buds than the low temperature-treated tubers (V3L and CKL), regardless of the VDAL treatment (Fig. 5a, Figure S4). Surprisingly, the V3L showed significantly longer apical buds than the CKL in the low temperature condition 7 and 14 DAT (Fig. 5a). In contrast, in the warm temperature condition, the V3W displayed similar or shorter apical buds compared to the CKW (Fig. 5a). This result indicates that VDAL may promote tuber sprouting in the low temperatures and this promotive effect seems to be compromised when the temperatures are optimal for sprouting.

Noticeably, in the field trails, the sprouting rates of the V3 tubers were constantly higher than those of the CK tubers observed 42 DAT (immediate planting after VDAL treatment) in both tested years (Fig. 2f). When observed 49 DAT in the season of 2019–2020, the V3 tubers displayed higher sprouting rate than the CK tubers; however, this difference was not significant in the season of 2020–2021 (Fig. 2f). The difference of sprouting rate is coincident with the variations in the trend of the temperatures between two years. The daily maximum temperature was substantially higher in the season of 2020–2021 than 2019–2020 year since 43 DAT, detected by an on-site temperature monitor system (Figure S5). The average maximum daily temperatures were 16.7℃ and 11.8℃ in the years of 2020–2021 and 2019–2020, respectively, calculated from 43 to 49 DAT. Thus, the field evidence further supports the promotive effect of VDAL on tuber sprouting in the low temperature.

Higher cytokinin levels detected in V3 tubers upon the VDAL treatment

The GO analysis of each gene cluster suggests that VDAL may affect the tuber sprouting through phytohormones, including cytokinin (Fig. 4b). This prompted us to evaluate the endogenous phytohormones in both V3 and CK tubers in a time-course manner. Abscisic acid (ABA), which inhibits tuber sprouting and growth [24], was constantly decreased in both V3 and CK upon the VDAL treatment (Fig. 6a). In contrast, gibberellins (GAs), which are positively associated with various developmental processes [25, 26], were increased in both V3 and CK (Fig. 6b). However, they decreased in the CK tubers starting from 7 DAT, but remained at similar levels in the V3 tubers from 3 to 14 DAT (Fig. 6b). We found that the levels of the auxins, including Indole-3-acetic acid (IAA) and Indole-3-butyric acid (IBA), were generally comparable between the V3 and CK tubers (Fig. 6c, d). Interestingly, higher levels of the cytokinin, including the growth promoters of isopentenyladenosine (iPA), trans-zeatin riboside (tZR) and zeatin, were detected in the V3 tubers than in the CK tubers since 3 DAT (Fig. 6e-g), which is coincident with the longer apical bud length observed in the V3 tubers compared to the CK tubers (Fig. 1, Table 1), indicating that the difference of the tuber sprouting between the V3 and CK tubers might be associated with the levels of the cytokinin.

In plants, isopentenyltransferases (IPTs) and LONELY GUY (LOG) genes play a key role in the cytokinin biosynthesis, while cytokinin oxidase/dehydrogenases (CKXs) are the key genes in the cytokinin degradation [27]. To investigate the expression of these genes in the V3 and CK tubers, we identified the closest Arabidopsis thaliana homologs [27] of the potato genes (Table S2). The expression levels of the IPTs and LOGs were generally similar between the V3 and CK tubers or lower in the V3 Vs. CK tubers from 0 to 7 DAT (Figure S6). Interestingly, the CKX genes (CKX1, CKX3 and CKX4) were expressed at lower levels in the V3 tubers compared to those in the CK tubers at both 3 DAT and 7 DAT (Fig. 7), indicating that the higher levels of cytokinin in the V3 tubers might be maintained by lower expression levels of the cytokinin degradation-associated genes. However, all CKXs genes expressed at low levels (TPM < 5.5). It seems that higher cytokinin levels in V3 tubers are maintained with additional factors. Collectively, the better sprouting performance of the VDAL-treated tubers is likely associated with the cytokinin.

Cytokinin is involved in the VDAL-promoted tuber sprouting, particularly under low temperature

To demonstrate the possible regulatory role of cytokinin in the sprouting of the VDAL-treated tubers under different temperature conditions, we applied lovastatin, an inhibitor of the cytokinin biosynthesis [28], to both V3 and CK tubers. Under low temperature condition (L), the sprouting of both V3 (V3L + lovastatin) and CK tubers (CKL + lovastatin) with 5 mM lovastatin treatments was completely inhibited observed at 7 DAT (Fig. 5b). In contrast, V3L without the lovastatin treatment successfully sprouted and displayed significantly longer apical buds than V3L + lovastatin (Fig. 5b), suggesting that the lovastatin interferes the promotive effect of the VDAL on the tuber sprouting under low temperature condition. Similar results were observed under warm temperature condition (W) 7 DAT. The V3 tubers (V3W) without the lovastatin treatment showed the longest apical buds compared to the V3 (V3W + lovastatin) and CK tubers (CKW + lovastatin) with the lovastatin treatment (Fig. 5b). Interestingly, the interfering effect of the lovastatin on the tuber sprouting was compromised when observed at 14 DAT under both temperature conditions (Fig. 5b), suggesting that the VDAL-induced cytokinin may play a promotive role at the early stage of the tuber sprouting. Additionally, the V3L tubers showed a large magnitude of elongation on the apical bud compared to the V3L + lovastatin tubers 7 DAT (Fig. 5c). However, the V3W tubers displayed similar magnitude of elongation compared to the V3W + lovastatin tubers (Fig. 5c), indicating that the VDAL-induced cytokinin may be associated with better promotive effect on tuber sprouting under low temperature. Collectively, the VDAL likely promotes the tuber sprouting via cytokinin, particularly under low temperature condition.

Discussion

The secretory elicitor protein VDAL has been demonstrated to activate immune response and further enhance resistance to the infection of its own secreting-fungus V. dahliae in Arabidopsis and cotton [12], as well as the fungus Rhizoctonia solani (sheath blight) in rice [15] and the fungus Sclerotinia sclerotiorum (sclerotinia stem rot) in Brassica rapa [29]. Thus, VDAL holds great potential to enhance resistance to fungal diseases in multiple crops. VDAL also enhances tolerance to abiotic stresses in wheat, such as salt [14], drought and late-spring cold [13]. Besides, the application of VDAL with suitable dosage promotes seed germination, rooting and plant growth in major crops [13, 15] and vegetable [16], while VDAL enhances total yield from several percent to over twenty percent in rice [15], wheat [13] and peanut [30]. These results suggest that VDAL may offer benefits to other agronomically important crops in terms of growth and yield improvement. In this study, we found that VDAL promoted potato tuber sprouting, rooting and total yield in both greenhouse (Fig. 2a-e) and field trials (Fig. 2f, Fig. 3). The highest yield increase reached to 18.97% when compared the VDAL-derived plant to the control in the field. Surprisingly, VDAL has promotive effect on tuber sprouting under low temperature evidenced in both laboratory test (Fig. 5) and field test under late-spring cold (Fig. 2f), suggesting that VDAL could not only promote plant growth and tuber yield, but also protect sprouts from growth inhibition caused by unpredictable late-spring cold.

The application of VDAL on crops triggers multiple molecular responsive cascades, mainly including phytohormones, abiotic stimulus responses, defense responses and the MAPK [12, 15, 29]. Interestingly, in this study, we found the cytokinin (growth promoters iPA, tZR and zeatin) were constantly higher in V3 tubers than those in CK under 12–14℃ temperatures (Fig. 6), indicating that VDAL may promote potato tuber sprouting through the maintenance of cytokinin at certain levels. In addition, previous studies indicate that cytokinin also plays a role in cold stress response in Arabidopsis [31]. The additional test with lower temperature (5–8℃) confirmed the promotive effect of VDAL on tuber sprouting, whereas warm temperature (18–20℃) compromised the effect of VDAL (Fig. 5a). Besides, the promotive effect of VDAL on tuber sprouting under low temperatures was diminished when treated V3 tubers with the lovastatin (7 DAT), a cytokinin inhibitor (Fig. 5b), further confirming that VDAL promotes tuber sprouting under low temperatures with the involvement of cytokinin. The enhanced tuber sprouting under low temperatures induced by VDAL could also be associated with its interaction with the E3 ligases PUB25 and PUB26 [12], which promote Arabidopsis freezing tolerance [32]. A recent study indicates that WRKY33 not only contributes cold tolerance in tomato [33], but also responses to the infection of Botrytis cinerea through participating in hormone signaling [33, 34], including cytokinin [35]. Surprisingly, we found that WRKY33 is the only gene, which was associated with GO terms of cold response, hormone and defense response in this study. These evidences suggest that WRKY33 might be involved in VDAL-cytokinin-cold network.

Further, we evaluated the expression of the WRKY33 homologs (RHC06 on chromosome 6 and RHC09 on chromosome 9) in both V3 and CK tubers under the temperature 12–14℃. Each allele of WRKY33 homologs displayed similar expression pattern with higher expression levels in V3 tubers compared to those in CK tubers 3 DAT, detected using both RNA-seq data (Figure S7a) and real time-quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) (Figure S7b). The alleles of RHC09H1G0869.2 and RHC09H2G0978.2 showed larger difference in expression levels between V3 and CK tubers 3 DAT than the alleles of RHC06H1G1897.2 and RHC06H2G1800.2 (Figure S7). Interestingly, the WRKY33 homolog on chromosome 9 expressed at significantly higher level in V3 tubers than that in either V3 + lovastatin tubers or CK tubers 3 DAT under low temperature (5–8℃), detected using RT-qPCR (Figure S8), implying that VDAL induces, but the cytokinin inhibitor lovastatin suppresses the expression of WRKY33. This expression pattern is consistent with the phenotype of the apical bud length (Fig. 5b). Collectively, these results suggest that WRKY33 may serve as a bridge to connect cold tolerance, hormone (at least cytokinin) and pathogen defense. However, the underlying molecular mechanisms governing VDAL-induced cytokinin signaling require further investigation through targeted molecular and genetic studies.

Materials and methods

Plant materials

Virus-free plantlets of an early-maturity cultivar “Favorita” (2n = 4x = 48, Solanum tuberosum, obtained from Zhangbei Bayuan Potato Industry Development Co., LTD) were transplanted into the coconut coir medium and grown in the greenhouse for PBS tubers. The harvested PBS tubers with uniform weight (3 g/tuber) were surfaced-dried and stored at 2–4℃ in the dark to break dormancy. The cylindrical tissue (7 mm × 10 mm, circumference × height of cylinder) near the apical bud of PBS tubers was taken by using a hole punch, followed by flash frozen in liquid nitrogen and ground for RNA-seq and endogenous phytohormone detection. Basic tubers from early-maturity cultivars, including “Favorita”,“Xisen 3”(2n = 4x = 48, S. tuberosum, derived from Favorita × K9304, obtained from Xisen Potato Industry Group Co., LTD) and “Xingjia 2” (2n = 4x = 48, S. tuberosum, derived from Gloria × 21–36-27–31, obtained from Zhangbei Bayuan Potato Industry Development Co., LTD), and a late-maturity cultivar “Xisen 6” (2n = 4x = 48, S. tuberosum, derived from Shepody × XS9304, obtained from Xisen Potato Industry Group Co., LTD) were used for the field trials. The PBS tubers of “Xisen 3” were used for evaluation under various temperatures. The powder with 1.5% VDAL protein was provided by State Key Laboratory of Plant Physiology and Biochemistry in China Agricultural University.

Greenhouse evaluation

For the optimal VDAL concentration assessment, the PBS tubers of “Favorita” with apical bud 0–2 mm were treated with VDAL in the concentrations of 0 ppm, 1 ppm, 3 ppm, 5 ppm, 7 ppm, respectively, and stored under the temperature of 12℃-14℃ in the dark. The PBS tubers from each treatment were randomly taken out from the dark condition for the measurement of the apical bud length at 0, 1, 3, 7, 14 DAT, respectively. The length of apical bud was measured using a vernier caliper.

For the evaluation of the agronomical traits and yield, the PBS tubers were treated with VDAL, and immediately planted (single tuber/pot) and grown in the greenhouse under the photoperiod of 16 h light at 18℃ and 8 h dark at 12℃.

Assessment of sprouting in low temperature

PBS tubers of “Xisen 3” with uniform weight (3 g/tuber) were treated the same as “Favorita” over two months to break dormancy. PBS tubers with apical bud 0–2 mm were treated with VDAL in the concentrations of 0 ppm and 3 ppm, and then divided into two groups, one followed by warm temperature treatment (18–20℃, designated as CKW and V3W) and the other followed by low temperature treatment (average 5–8℃, designated as CKL and V3L) in the dark. Similarly, the PBS tubers of “Xisen 3” were subject to the same treatment with additional chemical, cytokinin inhibitor lovastatin (Solarbio, Cat.# SL8280), for the evaluation of sprouting in low temperature with lovastatin. The length of apical bud of ten PBS tubers were measured by using a vernier caliper for each biological replicate, and three biological replicates were included for each treatment. The cylindrical tissue (7 mm × 10 mm, circumference × height of cylinder) near the apical bud of PBS tubers was taken out for the gene expression analysis.

Field evaluation

The field trials were conducted at two locations, including the Chong zhou Experimental Base in Yang Ma Village of Chong Zhou City and the Shi Fang Base in Bai Yu He Village of Shi Fang City, where the soil type is brown paddy with pH 6.01 and pH 6.58, respectively. The base fertilizer was N15-P5-K25 compound fertilizer with 1200 kg/ha in Yang Ma Village and 1500 kg/ha in Bai Yu He Village.

Virus-free basic tubers were cut into 50 g per cube and treated with VDAL 3 days before sowing. The basic tubers were sowed in the single ridge-double row covered by plastic film. Only the tuber cube with apical bud were chosen for this experiment. The cultivars, including “Favorita”, “Xisen 3” and “Xingjia 2” were planted in Yang Ma Village and their yield were measure in three replicates of 40m2, 50m2 and 20m2 for each treatment, respectively. The other cultivar “Xisen 6” were planted in Bai Yu He Village and its yield was evaluated in three replicates of 20m2 for each treatment. The harvested tubers were sorted by weight with > 100 g as large tuber, 50-100 g as medium tuber and < 50 g as small tuber. Tubers greater than 50 g were defined as marketable potato.

Quantification of endogenous plant growth regulators in tubers

Both CK and V3 tubers of “Favorita” selected from each time point (0, 1, 3, 7, 14 DAT) were subject to endogenous phytohormones evaluation. The cylindrical tissue near the apical bud of fifteen PBS tubers were assessed for each biological replicate and three biological replicates were included for each treatment. The samples were extracted using isopropyl alcohol-water-hydrochloric acid method. The contents of endogenous hormones ABA, GA, IAA, IBA, iPA, tZR and Zeatin were determined by a HPLC–MS (Agilent 1290 high performance liquid chromatograph series AB Sciex QTRAP 6500 + mass spectrometer) along with internal standard substances in Convinced-Test (Nanjing).

RNA-seq and data analysis

The cylindrical tissue near the apical bud of fifteen PBS tubers was pooled as a biological replicate. RNA-seq libraries were developed from three biological replicates for each treatment/time point. RNA isolation, library construction and sequencing were conducted by the Biomarker Technologies (Beijing). The RNA-seq libraries were sequencing on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform in 150 bp pair-end mode.

Raw reads were trimmed by fastp (v0.23.2) with default parameter. The HISAT2 program (v2.2.1) was used for aligning the clean reads to the reference RH89-039–16 genome assembly (version 3) [36]. The gene expression levels were calculated as the transcripts per million using Stringtie (v2.2.1). Differentially expressed genes were filtered if they showed at least two-fold difference with FDR < 0.01, by using DEseq2 (version 1.32.0). TC-seq (v1.16.0) was employed to analyze the gene expression pattern in time-course and clustering analysis. Genes that were differentially expressed in comparison to CK0 were used in TC-seq analysis.

Gene ontology enrichment

Homologous sequences of the potato genes in Arabidopsis thaliana were identified using the BLASTp program (BLAST v2.9.0). The homologous protein sequences with the highest similarity to the potato genes were subject to the GO enrichment analysis, using agriGO v2.0 [37]. GO terms from all annotated Arabidopsis genes were set as background for each enrichment test.

RT-qPCR

Total RNA of each sample was isolated by using an OminiPlant RNA Kit (CWBIO, Cat.# CW2598). cDNA was synthesized by using a EasyScript® reverse transcription kit (TransGen Biotech, Cat.# AE341). Three biological replicates from each treatment were used to measure the expression level of each gene. RT-qPCR was performed by using the SuperStar Universal SYBR Master Mix (CWBIO, Cat.# CW3888), and the procedure was 95℃ 30 s for initial denaturation, followed by 45 cycles of 95℃ 10 s for denaturation and 58℃ 10 s for annealing/extension. StEF1α was used as the reference gene for normalization using 2−∆Ct method (Zeng et al. 2019). Gene-specific primers used in this study were presented in Table S3. Statistical significance between treatments was tested using the paired t-test or Duncan’s New Multiple Range test.

Data visualization

All data were visualized using R program (https://www.r-project.org).

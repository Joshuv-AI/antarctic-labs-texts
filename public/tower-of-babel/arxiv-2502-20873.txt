Fusion systems related to polynomial representations of SL 2 ⁡ ( q ) \operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)
Valentina Grazian, Chris Parker, Jason Semeraro, Martin van Beek

Abstract
. Let q q be a power of a fixed prime p p . We classify up to isomorphism all simple saturated fusion systems on a certain class of p p -groups constructed from the polynomial representations of SL 2 ⁡ ( q ) \operatorname{SL}_{2}(q) , which includes the Sylow p p -subgroups of GL 3 ​ ( q ) \mathrm{GL}_{3}(q) and Sp 4 ​ ( q ) \mathrm{Sp}_{4}(q) as special cases. The resulting list includes all Clelland–Parker fusion systems, a simple exotic fusion system discovered by Henke–Shpectorov, and a new infinite family of exotic examples.

1.
Introduction

For a prime
p
, the analysis of
p
-local structure in finite groups and their modular representation theory is naturally expressed in the language of saturated fusion systems on
p
-groups. Since
p
-groups of a given order are overwhelmingly dominated by those of nilpotency class
2
, the work of the fourth author
[
44
]
implies that almost all
p
-groups admit no reduced saturated fusion system. In fact, the proportion of
p
-groups supporting a reduced saturated fusion system appears to be vanishingly small; this expectation is reinforced by computational evidence (see, for example,
[
34
]
). Consequently, recognizing the
p
-groups that do support reduced fusion systems, and classifying the fusion systems that occur on them, is an interesting and difficult problem. The results of this paper contribute to this programme (see
[
2
, Questions III.7.4, IV.7.1]
).

A saturated fusion system on a finite
p
–group
S
is said to be
exotic
if it does not arise as the fusion system of any finite group with Sylow
p
-subgroup
S
. As we shall see, the fusion systems studied in this paper are predominantly exotic.

One source of
p
-groups which support an exotic fusion system are the Sylow
p
-subgroups of non-abelian simple groups. In particular, a substantial body of work has examined the saturated fusion systems on a
p
-group
S
which is the Sylow
p
-subgroup of a Lie type group of characteristic
p
and rank
2

[
40
,
10
,
33
,
4
,
26
,
42
,
18
]
, culminating in the classification obtained in
[
43
]
. This direction of research has produced several families of exotic fusion systems (see, for example,
[
40
,
33
,
18
]
). For
q
a power of
p
, the
p
-groups considered here, which we christen
polynomial
p
-groups
, are generalisations of the Sylow
p
-subgroups of
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(q)
and
\mathrm{Sp}_{4}(q)
and have their origins in polynomial representations of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)
. Thus they lie directly in the rank
2
Lie type landscape. Moreover, polynomial
p
-groups are known to support reduced fusion systems and therefore provide a natural source of
p
-groups for the general programme described above. From a different perspective, the polynomial
p
-groups denoted
S_{n}(q)
below are remarkably like “
q
-versions” of maximal class groups (see Lemma
2.8
). Thus this work also complements the work in
[
15
]
.

In this article, we provide a complete classification of saturated fusion systems
\mathcal{F}
on polynomial
p
-groups with
O_{p}(\mathcal{F})=1
(core-free) and thus a determination of all the reduced saturated fusion systems on such groups. Our key results, Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
, form an important step toward a broader project carried out with collaborators in which we determine all reduced saturated fusion systems
\mathcal{F}
on a
p
-group
S
that possess an
\mathcal{F}
-essential subgroup that is both abelian and normal in
S
. This project extends the work initiated in
[
31
,
11
,
28

, and the classification obtained here supplies input required for the principal families of fusion systems arising in that extension.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a finite field of characteristic
p
and order
q
, and
G=\mathrm{GL}_{2}(q)
. For
n\geq 1
, define
V_{n}(q)
to be the set of homogeneous polynomials over
\mathbb{K}
of degree

in two commuting variables. Then

becomes a
\mathbb{K}G
-module of dimension
n+1
with respect to the natural polynomial action of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(q)
. The modules

are irreducible precisely when
0\leqslant n\leqslant p-1
. Writing
\Lambda(q)
for the dual module of
V_{p}(q)
, define:

P_{n}(q)=G\ltimes V_{n}(q),\hskip 5.69054ptS_{n}(q)\in\mathrm{Syl}_{p}(P_{n}(q)),\hskip 5.69054ptP_{\Lambda}(q)=G\ltimes\Lambda(q),\hskip 5.69054pt\mbox{ and }\hskip 5.69054ptS_{\Lambda}(q)\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(P_{\Lambda}(q)).

Note that

|S_{n}(q)|=q|V_{n}(q)|=q^{n+2}

and

|S_{\Lambda}(q)|=q|\Lambda(q)|=q^{p+2}.

In this paper, we refer to the
p
-groups
S_{n}(q)
and

as
polynomial
p
-groups
. The primary objective of this work is to determine up to isomorphism all core-free saturated fusion systems
\mathcal{F}
on
S_{\Lambda}(q)
and
S_{n}(q)
when
1\leqslant n\leqslant p-1
.

In
[
9
]
, Clelland and the second author introduced two infinite families of saturated fusion systems on the groups

with

which can be viewed as generalisations of the

-fusion systems of
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(q)
and, when

is odd,

. Indeed, these groups have parabolic subgroups which are closely related to

and

respectively, and the Clelland–Parker fusion systems possess an essential subgroup normaliser of the form

. Other than in some very small cases, the fusion systems exhibited in
[
9
]
are exotic (see
[
9
, Theorems 5.1 and 5.2]

[
17

. Collectively, we refer to all of these fusion systems as

Suppose

is a Sylow

-subgroup of

-module for

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that

This gives a characterisation of the Henke–Shpectorov systems as the only exceptional core-free exotic fusion systems on polynomial
p
-groups which occur when

. Theorem

subsumes the results in
[

]
and
[

]
mentioned above, but we rely on the former since the structure of

which have the property that

-module. This turns out to be a rather delicate calculation which takes up a good part of Section 5. After that, in Section 6, we determine up to

In a later paper the authors, along with other collaborators, will demonstrate that the

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that

is an odd prime, and

. Then

supports a simple, exotic fusion system

The system

Suppose that

In Section
4
, we explore various relationships between polynomial fusion systems. For example, suppose

is such a system on

and

, then pruning

As pointed out earlier, the polynomial fusion systems that are realisable are generically the fusion systems on

Our notation for groups is mostly standard and follows

2.
The structure of polynomial
p
-groups and their automorphism group

Throughout this work, we let
p
be an odd prime, although several of the constructions and results in this section are also applicable when
p=2
. Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field of characteristic
p
with group of units
\mathbb{K}^{*}
and order
q=p^{m}
. Set
A:=\mathbb{K}[x,y]
to be the ring of polynomials in two commuting indeterminates
x,y
with coefficients in
\mathbb{K}
.

Let
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{K})
. Then
\phi
acts on
A
by acting on the coefficients of
f(x,y)\in A
yielding the polynomial
f^{\phi}(x,y)
. This is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear map on
A
and so
A
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{K})
-module.

Define
D=\mathbb{K}^{*}\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{K})
and make
A
into a
\mathbb{K}D
-module as follows: for
g=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{K})
define
x\cdot g=ax+by
and
y\cdot g=cx+dy
and then, for
(\lambda,g)\in D
and
f(x,y)\in A
, define

f(x,y)\cdot(\lambda,g)=\lambda f(x\cdot g,y\cdot g).

This action makes
A
into a
\mathbb{K}D
-module.

Finally, regarding
A
as a
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-space, both
D
and
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{K})
act on
A
and we can check that, for
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{K})
and
(\lambda,\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right))\in D
we have
(\lambda,\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right))^{\phi}=(\lambda^{\phi},\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a^{\phi}&b^{\phi}\\
c^{\phi}&d^{\phi}\end{smallmatrix}\right))\in D
. In particular, we can form the semidirect product
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{K})\ltimes D
and this group acts
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linearly on
A
by acting on the coefficients of the polynomials.

.

Set

D:=\mathbb{K}^{*}\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{K}),

D^{*}:=O^{p}(\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{K}))\ltimes D=O_{p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{K}))\ltimes D,\mbox{ and }

.

as an

-module and observe that the action of

on

maps polynomials of degree

. For

Lemma 2.4
.

Regard
V=V_{p}(q)
as a
\mathbb{K}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{K})
-module. The
\mathbb{K}
-linear map

\displaystyle\psi:V_{p}(q)

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle V_{p-2}(q)

\displaystyle x^{i}y^{j}

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle ix^{i-1}y^{j-1}

is a surjective
\mathbb{K}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{K})
-module homomorphism with kernel
\langle x^{p},y^{p}\rangle_{\mathbb{K}}
.

Proof.

Set
X=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{K})
and observe that

X=\left\langle\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right),\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\theta\\
-\theta^{-1}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\mid\theta\in\mathbb{\mathbb{K}}^{*}\right\rangle.

For
i+j=p
, we have

i=-j\pmod{p},

ix^{i-1}y^{j-1}=-jx^{i-1}y^{j-1}\pmod{p},\,\,\text{and}

i\binom{j-1}{k}=(k+i)\binom{j}{k}\pmod{p}.

Hence

\displaystyle(x^{i}y^{j}\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right))\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x^{i}(x+y)^{j})\psi=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{j}\binom{j}{k}x^{k+i}y^{j-k}\right)\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\binom{j}{k}(k+i)x^{k+i-1}y^{j-k-1}=x^{i-1}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}(k+i)\binom{j}{k}x^{k}y^{j-k-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ix^{i-1}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\binom{j-1}{k}x^{k}y^{j-1-k}=ix^{i-1}(x+y)^{j-1}=(x^{i}y^{j})\psi\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right).

Similarly, for
\theta\in\mathbb{K}^{*}
,

\displaystyle(x^{i}y^{j}\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\theta\\
-\theta^{-1}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right))\psi

Polynomial fusion systems

In this section, we describe the polynomial fusion systems which appear in the conclusions of our main theorems. For this, as in Section

, we require certain extensions of the Clelland-Parker fusion systems by field automorphisms of
p^{\prime}
-order. We begin by recalling the definitions of these systems, following the presentation in

from Notation

and

Notation 4.1
.

We write
\mathcal{F}^{*}(n,q,R)
, respectively
\mathcal{F}^{*}(n,q,Q)

, respectively

It is clear that
\mathcal{F}(n,q,X)

\mathcal{F}^{*}(n,q,X)

Proposition 4.2
.

The fusion systems
\mathcal{F}^{*}(n,q,Q)
and
\mathcal{F}^{*}(n,q,R)
are saturated and respectively contain
\mathcal{F}(n,q,Q)
and
\mathcal{F}(n,q,R)

-index. Moreover,

\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{F}^{*}_{\Lambda}(q))=\{V\}\cup R^{S}\mbox{ and }\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{F}^{*}(n,q,X))=\{V\}\cup X^{S}\mbox{ for $X\in\{R,S\},$}

and for all such systems
\mathcal{F}
, we have
\mathcal{F}^{frc}=\{S\}\cup\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{F})

Proof.

Note that neither the isomorphism type of

nor the set of
\mathcal{F}_{S}(S)
-centric subgroups is affected by appending

-automorphisms to automisers of subgroups in a fusion system. Furthermore, the essential subgroups remain unchanged when passing from
\mathcal{F}_{S}(P_{n}(q))
to
\mathcal{F}_{S}(P^{*}_{n}(q))
, and from
\mathcal{F}_{N_{S}(X)}(P_{X})
to
\mathcal{F}_{N_{S}(X)}(P_{X}^{*})

[
9

applies to this situation too. Since

2

has

-index in
\mathcal{F}^{*}(n,q,X)

∎

We have that
\mathcal{F}^{*}(n,p,X)=\mathcal{F}(n,p,X)
for
X\in\{R,Q\}
. Let

. One can show that
\mathcal{F}^{*}(1,q,R)\cong\mathcal{F}_{S}(O^{p}(\Gamma\mathrm{L}_{3}(q)))
,
\mathcal{F}^{*}(2,q,Q)\cong\mathcal{F}_{S}(O^{p}(\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{K}))\ltimes\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(q))
and that

is exotic in every other case, following the same proof as
[
9
, Section 5]
.

Remark 4.3
.

The authors expect that one can replace the group

with the full automorphism group

in the construction of

for

to make amalgams with a larger Sylow

-subgroup. By adapting the methods used in

Now observe that by

the fusion systems

and

determine further systems via a process known as “pruning”. More precisely, a subset of the automorphisms of the essential subgroup

can be removed in either case to form, respectively, two new saturated fusion systems.

Notation 4.4
.

Define the saturated fusion systems

and
\mathcal{F}^{*}(n,q,R)_{P}
on

to be those obtained by pruning

(see Notation

) so that

.

Proposition 4.5
.

Suppose that
1<n\leqslant p-1
and
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}^{*}(n,q,R)_{P}
. Then the following hold:

(1)

O^{p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})
is exotic;

(2)

|\mathrm{Out}_{\mathcal{F}}^{0}(S)|=q-1
; and

(3)

O^{p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})
is simple unless

and

Proof.

We observe that
R\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{F}^{*}(n,q,R)_{P})
and, as

, we see that
R\not\trianglelefteq S
. Since
O^{p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{F}^{*}(n,q,R)}(R))\cong\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)
acts irreducibly on

,
[
2
, Proposition I.4.5]
implies that

. We observe that
{\mathcal{F}^{*}(n,q,R)_{P}}^{frc}=\{S\}\cup R^{S}
and so, in order to calculate

[

Let

be a proper non-trivial strongly closed subgroup of

so that

so that

so that
X[V,S]=\langle X^{S}\rangle R
. Let

be an element of

. Then we may arrange that

and so acts as

on

and as
\lambda
on

for some

. Recall the map
\delta
from Notation

when restricted to

-elements from Lemma

.

Now if
\mathcal{N}\trianglelefteq O^{p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}^{*}(n,q,R)_{P})

[

. But in this case, if

.

Proposition

Proposition 4.7
.

The following statements hold:

(1)

Suppose

[

Suppose that

[

∎

5.
Recognition and uniqueness results for groups and modules

Having described the polynomial
p
-groups and polynomial fusion systems of interest in this paper, we now embark on the proof that, other than the two exotic Henke–Shpectorov systems, these systems are the unique core-free saturated fusion systems supported on
S
, for
S\in\{S_{n}(q),S_{\Lambda}(q)\mid 1\leqslant n\leqslant p-1,q\neq p\}

The following proposition, which depends on the Classification of Finite Simple Groups, will be used to restrict the possibilities for
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{F}}(E)
when

is
\mathcal{F}
-essential in the fusion system
\mathcal{F}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose that
p
is a prime,
X
is a group and
T\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(X)
is elementary abelian of order
p^{m}
with

. Assume that
X
has a strongly
p
-embedded subgroup. Then
O_{p}(X)=1
and for
K=O^{p^{\prime}}(X)=\langle T^{K}\rangle
,
\overline{K}:=K/O_{p^{\prime}}(K)

Lemma 5.4
.

Suppose that

,

\mathbb{K}G
-module. If

Proof.

By the Steinberg Tensor Product Theorem

Define

It follows that there is a unique

Proposition 5.7
.

Suppose that

,

, and

is an indecomposable
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-module. Assume the following:

(1)

of

(3)

.

Consider the module

. Applying an extension of the arguments in
[
3
, (26.2)]
, we see that there is a
\mathbb{K}
-submodule

m
Frobenius twists of

is a direct sum of
m
Frobenius twists of

Let

. By

, (25.7)(1)]
, regarded as a

\mathbb{K}G
-submodule of

. Then for

is also an irreducible

-submodule. Set

. Then

-invariant and has dimension

of

. Since the only proper submodule of

is

, we conclude that

.

It remains to examine when

that if

and

. Furthermore,

Assume now that

[

to see that

Lemma 5.8

Suppose that

The remainder of this section is concerned with certain uniqueness results needed to show that the polynomial fusion systems are uniquely determined up to isomorphism. The proofs are long and technical. The reader is encouraged to work through them slowly, and may consider skipping some of the details on a first pass.

The irreducibility requirement in parts (1) and (2) of Lemma
5.11
is too limiting for our purposes and this prevents Lemma
5.11
from being applied directly. To remedy this we prove following proposition which is specific to the cases we consider, first viewing the modules
V_{i}(q)
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}

Proposition 5.14
.

Suppose that
H
is a finite group with
S\in\mathrm{Syl}_{p}(H)
. Assume that
S\cong S_{n}(q)
for
q=p^{m}>p
and
1\leqslant n<p
, and
H/O_{p}(H)\cong\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)
. Then, writing
V:=O_{p}(H)
and recognizing
V
as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)
-module, we have that
V\cong V_{n}(q)|_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
.

Proof.

We prove this by induction on
n
. For
n=1
, since
q>p
we have that
S\leq O^{p}(H)V
and as
C_{V}(S)<V
we have that
[V,O^{p}(H)]\neq 1
. Observe that
|S/V|=|V/C_{V}(S)|=|C_{V}(S)|
and so that by Lemma
5.5
, we have that
C_{G}(V)=V
,
H/V\cong\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)
and
V
may be viewed as a natural
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)
-module. Thus, the result holds when
n=1
. So assume from now that
1<n<p
.

Recall from Notation
2.2

S_{n}(q)=U\ltimes V_{n}(q)
, where
U
is elementary abelian of order
q
and
V_{n}(q)
is elementary abelian, self-centralising in
S_{n}(q)
and of order
q^{n+1}
. We aim to prove that the action of
H/V\cong\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)
on
V
is irreducible, for then the result holds by Lemma
5.11
. We observe that
C_{H}(V)\trianglelefteq H
and that
[S,V]\neq 1
. Since
H/V\cong\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)
and
q>p
, we have that
H/C_{H}(V)\cong\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)
or
H/C_{H}(V)\cong\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(q)
. Set
Z_{i}=Z_{i}(S)
for
0\leq i\leq n
where
Z_{0}=1
and
Z_{n+1}=V
.

In pursuit of proving that
H/V
acts irreducibly on
V
as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-module we assume, aiming for a contradiction, that there is
W\trianglelefteq H
with
1\neq W<V
. Choose
W
maximal subject to this. We either have that
W\leq C_{V}(S)=Z_{1}
, or there is some maximal
i\in\{2,\dots,n+1\}
such that
W\cap Z_{i}\not\leq Z_{i-1}
. Then Lemma
2.14
yields
Z_{i-1}\leq W<Z_{i}
.

(5.14.1)

i=n+1
.

Suppose otherwise so that
i\leqslant n
and
W\leq Z_{n}=[V,S]
. In particular,
[V/W,S]=[V,S]/W
has index
q
in
V/W
. If

, then
H
centralises
V/W
and this is impossible as

and
W
was chosen maximally. Hence
W<[V,S]
. Write
\mathbb{L}
for the largest extension of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
for which
V/W
is writable as an
\mathbb{L}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)
-module. Then by Lemma
5.2
, we have that
(V/W)/[V/W,S]\cong V/[V,S]
is irreducible as an
\mathbb{L}N_{H/V}(S/V)
-module. Hence, by Lemma
5.3
we deduce that
\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{K}:=\mathbb{F}_{q}
. But then
|V/W|=q^{a}
for some

and we conclude that
W=Z_{i}
. Now, by Lemma
2.10
we have that
S/Z_{i}\cong S_{n-i}(q)
and by induction we have that
V/W\cong V_{n-i}(q)
. Since
|C_{V}(S)|=q
, an application of Lemma
5.8
yields that
i>1
. Hence
W=Z_{i}
for some

.

Now, take
W_{0}<W
with
W_{0}\trianglelefteq H
and
W_{0}
maximal subject to this. Then
W/W_{0}
is an irreducible module and arguing as above, we either have that
W_{0}\leq C_{V}(S)=Z_{1}
,
Z_{i-1}\leq W_{0}
or
W_{0}=Z_{k}
for some

where

. If

then

is a trivial module and, as

and

is maximal, we have

Z_{i-1}<W_{0}<Z_{i}=W<Z_{n}=[V,S]

with

and Lemma

implies that
C_{V/W_{0}}(S)=Z_{i+1}/W_{0}
, a contradiction to Lemma

. Thus,

.

If

, then

and Lemma

to deduce that

for some

and we conclude that either

or

, and so
=Z_{k}
for some

We have that

and by induction, using that

is reducible, we deduce that

and
W
is irreducible. Applying Lemma
5.4
we deduce that

. As

,

and by Proposition
5.7
, using that

, we have a contradiction. This proves the claim.

We have shown that
i=n+1
so that
[V,S]<W
and
H
centralises
V/W
. Then
[W,S]=[V,S]<W
by Lemma

. Let

be of order

, set

has order

, or

.

Observe that

induces an automorphism on

which induces an action on

. But by Lemma
2.10
,
S/[V,S,S]\cong S_{1}(q)
is isomorphic to a Sylow

-subgroup of
\mathrm{PSL}_{3}(q)
. Since

,

so acts non-trivially on

. By Proposition

,

is a

-space for

and we conclude that

.

Recall the definition of

from Notation

. We recognise that

contains a Hall
p^{\prime}
-subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(S_{n}(q))
and so for

,
\mathrm{Aut}_{K}(S)
is
\mathrm{Aut}(S)
-conjugate to an element of order at least
\frac{q-1}{2}
which acts like an element of
\mathrm{Aut}_{P_{n}^{*}(q)}(S_{n}(q))
. Since
q>p
, Lemma
2.11
implies that
K
acts irreducibly on
C_{V}(S)=Z_{1}
as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}K
-module.

Let
1\neq V_{0}\leq W<V
with
V_{0}
chosen minimally such that
V_{0}\trianglelefteq H
. In particular,
V_{0}
is an irreducible
H/V
-module. Since
K
acts irreducibly on

, we see that
C_{V}(S)=\langle(C_{V}(S)\cap V_{0})^{K}\rangle\leq V_{0}
. As before, applying Lemma
5.2
and Lemma
5.3
we conclude that
V_{0}
is writable as a
\mathbb{K}
-module and so

for some
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
by Lemma
5.4
. If
i<n
, then

and by induction we have that
V/V_{0}
is irreducible which is impossible as
V_{0}<W
. Hence
V_{0}=[V,S]\cong V_{n-1}(q)|_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
and
V/V_{0}
is centralised by

. An application of Lemma

(2) then yields the unforgivable

. This completes the proof.
∎

Our next module recognition result concerns
S_{\Lambda}(q)
and
V_{\Lambda}(q)
.

Proposition 5.15
.

Suppose that
H
is a finite group with
S\in\mathrm{Syl}_{p}(H)
. Assume that
S\cong S_{\Lambda}(q)
for
q>p
, where
p
is an odd prime, and
H/O_{p}(H)\cong\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)
. Then, writing
V:=O_{p}(H)
and recognising
V
as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)
-module, we have that
V\cong\Lambda(q)|_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
.

Proof.

We observe that
Z(S)=C_{V}(S)
has order
q^{2}
and
V/[V,S]
has order
q
by Lemma
2.16
. Let
W\leq V
be such that
W
corresponds to the
(p-1)
-dimensional
\mathbb{K}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)
-submodule in the construction of

. In particular,

,
[W,S]=[V,S,S]
,
C_{W}(S)=C_{[V,S,S]}(S)
and
[V,S]=WC_{V}(S)
.

As in Proposition
5.14
, we have that
H/C_{H}(V)\cong\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)
or
H/C_{H}(V)\cong\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(q)
and we let
k\in N_{H}(S)
be of order
q-1
so that
\mathrm{Aut}_{\langle k\rangle}(S)
has order
q-1
or
\frac{q-1}{2}
respectively. Write
K:=\langle k\rangle
. Then
\mathrm{Aut}_{K}(S)
is an abelian subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(S)
of order at least
\frac{q-1}{2}
. Recall the definition of
P_{\Lambda}^{*}(q)
from Notation
2.5
. Since
\mathrm{Aut}_{P_{\Lambda}^{*}(q)}(S_{\Lambda}(q))
contains a Hall
p^{\prime}
-subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(S_{\Lambda}(q))
,
\mathrm{Aut}_{K}(S)
is conjugate in
\mathrm{Aut}(S)
to a subgroup of the unique abelian subgroup of order
(q-1)^{2}
in a Hall
p^{\prime}
-subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}_{P_{\Lambda}^{*}(q)}(S_{\Lambda}(q))
by Lemma
2.19
.

Let
tV\in H/V\cong\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)
with

an involution. In particular, we may arrange that

is the unique involution in
K
. Then
C_{V}(t)\geq C_{V}(H)\cap C_{W}(S)\neq 1
and

is an
H
-invariant decomposition of

. If

, then
[V,t]\geq[V,S]
and if
C_{V}(t)\not\leq[V,S]
, then
C_{V}(t)\geq[V,S]
. Since
C_{V}(t)\cap[V,t]=1
, we deduce that either

or

.

We split the analysis into two cases.

Case 1:

C_{V}(H)\cap C_{W}(S)\neq 1
.

Since
K
centralises
C_{V}(H)\cap C_{W}(S)
, and a Hall
p^{\prime}
-subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}_{P_{\Lambda}^{*}(q)}(S_{\Lambda}(q))
acts irreducibly on
C_{W}(S)
, we deduce that
K
centralises
C_{W}(S)
. It follows that
C_{V}(K)=C_{W}(S)
by Lemma
2.20
. Then, as
C_{V}(t)\neq 1
, we also know that
C_{V}(t)=V
and
H/C_{H}(V)\cong\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(q)
.

Let
V_{M}\trianglelefteq H
with
V_{M}<V
, and choose
V_{M}
maximally with respect to these conditions. Then
V/V_{M}
is an irreducible module for

. If
V_{M}\not<[V,S]
then
[V,S]\leq V_{M}
so that
H
centralises

. Indeed, as
V_{M}<V
and
K
centralises
V/V_{M}
, we must have that
C_{V}(K)\neq C_{W}(S)
, a contradiction. Hence,
V_{M}<[V,S]
and so
[V/V_{M},S]
has index
q
in
V/V_{M}
. Applying Lemma
5.2
, we see that
|V/V_{M}|=q^{a}
, for some
a\in\mathbb{N}
and
C_{V/V_{M}}(S)
has order
q
. Furthermore, both
C_{V/V_{M}}(S)
and
V/[V,S]
are irreducible as
\mathbb{F}_{p}K
-modules. Since
C_{V/V_{M}}(S)
is irreducible as a
\mathbb{F}_{p}K
-module, we must have that either

or
C_{V/V_{M}}(S)=C_{V}(S)V_{M}/V_{M}
.

Assume first that
C_{V}(S)\leq V_{M}
. Then, as

by Lemmas

and
2.18
, we have that

.

Now, again applying Lemmas

and
2.18
, we have that

and it follows that

, we ascertain that

and as

is even. As above, this implies that

.

Case 2:

C_{V}(H)\cap C_{W}(S)=1

,

and

so that

. On the other hand, if

then
[Y,S]\leq[V,S,S]
. Indeed, since
[Y,S]\neq 1
, we must have that
C_{W}(S)\leq[Y,S]
. In either case, applying Lemma
5.2
we conclude that
K
acts irreducibly on
C_{W}(S)
and
|Y/C_{Y}(H)|=q^{a}
for some
a\in\mathbb{N}
.

Assume first that
C_{Y}(H)\neq 1
so that
V=C_{V}(t)
and
H/C_{H}(V)\cong\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(q)
. Since a Hall

-subgroup of

. Since

.

-module. In particular,

. If

Thus, for the remainder of the proof we assume, aiming for a contradiction, that

. Let

be such that

and since

for some

, we deduce that

,

and

. Set

. Then

acts irreducibly on

and normalises

and

. Moreover,

\overline{Y}\neq\overline{[V,S;i]}
. Then
\overline{[V,S;i]}
and

-modules, and as

\overline{C_{V}(S)}
. Since

\mathrm{Aut}(S)

and

Hence,
Y=[V,S;i]
. Let

. Then

,

and
H/C_{H}(V)\cong\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(q)
so that
a

-subgroup of

and

. But

. Hence,
[V_{0},S]\not\leq Y
from which we deduce that

and

.

From Lemma

|V_{0}/Y|=q^{a}
for some

. If

. Since

Lemma 5.16
.

Suppose that
p
is odd and
m>1
. Let
H
be a
p^{\prime}
-order subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{m}(p)
which acts transitively on vectors with
|H|\geqslant p^{m}-1
and
|Z(H)|\geqslant\frac{p^{m}-1}{p}
. Then
H\cong\mathrm{GL}_{1}(p^{m})
is cyclic of order
p^{m}-1
.

Proof.

We appeal to the classification of transitive finite linear groups, due to Hering
[
20
]
. Then the restrictions on
H
promise that
H\leq\Gamma\mathrm{L}_{1}(p^{m})
. Set
K:=\mathrm{GL}_{1}(p^{m})
and assume that
|HK/K|=c\leqslant m
. Then
|C_{K}(HK)|=p^{m/c}-1
and
|Z(H)\cap K|\geqslant\frac{p^{m}-1}{pc}
. We deduce that
\frac{p^{m}-1}{pc}\leqslant p^{m/c}-1
so that

. Moreover, if

and
|H\cap K|=\frac{p^{2}-1}{2}>p-1
and so

. But now

and we obtain a contradiction. Hence, we conclude that

and

, and as
|H|=p^{m}-1=|K|
, the result is true.
∎

Lemma 5.17

Ledrappier-Young entropy formula for C 1 C^{1} diffeomorphisms with dominated splitting Part 1: unstable entropy formula and invariance principle
Shaobo Gan 1, Yao Tong 2, Jiagang Yang 3

Abstract
. We study the unstable entropy of C 1 C^{1} diffeomorphisms with dominated splittings. Our main result shows that when the zero Lyapunov exponent has multiplicity one, the center direction contributes no entropy, and the unstable entropy coincides with the metric entropy. This extends the celebrated work of Ledrappier–Young [ 18 ] for C 2 C^{2} diffeomorphisms to the C 1 C^{1} setting under these assumptions. In particular, our results apply to C 1 C^{1} diffeomorphisms away from homoclinic tangencies due to [ 20 ] . As consequences, we obtain several applications at C 1 C^{1} regularity. The Avila–Viana invariance principle [ 7 , 33 ] holds when the center is one-dimensional. Results on measures of maximal entropy due to Hertz–Hertz–Tahzibi–Ures [ 25 ] , Tahzibi–Yang [ 33 ] , and Ures–Viana–Yang–Yang [ 34 , 35 ] also remain valid for C 1 C^{1} diffeomorphisms.



1.
Introduction and main results

1.1.
Introduction

The notion of metric entropy, introduced by Kolmogorov and Sinai in the 1950s, provides a way to quantify the complexity of chaotic dynamical systems. In the 1960s, Oseledets’ theorem
[
22
]
introduced Lyapunov exponents, another fundamental tool in smooth ergodic theory. These two concepts are deeply connected: the inequality of Margulis and Ruelle, see
[
27
]
, shows that the metric entropy of an invariant measure for diffeomorphisms is upper bounded by the sum of the positive Lyapunov exponents, counting multiplicity; moreover, for
C^{1+\mathrm{H\"{o}lder}}
volume-preserving diffeomorphisms, the Pesin’s entropy formula
[
23
]
establishes equality, namely the entropy of the volume measure is equal to the sum of its positive Lyapunov exponents.

In a series of remarkable works
[
16
]
,
[
14
]
, and
[
18
]
in the 1980s, it was proven that for
C^{2}
diffeomorphisms, the Pesin entropy formula holds if and only if the measure admits absolutely continuous conditional measures along Pesin’s unstable manifolds. To achieve this, the authors of these papers introduced the concept of unstable entropy, i.e., entropy along Pesin unstable manifolds, and the main step in
[
18
]
is to prove that the unstable entropy is equal to the metric entropy for any
C^{2}
diffeomorphisms.

The assumption of
C^{2}
regularity is essential in the classical results described above. The key reason is that the holonomy maps induced by unstable foliations within center–unstable sets are Lipschitz.
This Lipschitz property also holds for
C^{1+\mathrm{H\"{o}lder}}
diffeomorphisms, as shown in recent works of Brown in
[
4
]
and Saghin in
[
28
]
, thereby extending the Ledrappier-Young theory to the
C^{1+\mathrm{H\"{o}lder}}
setting.

In contrast,
C^{1}
diffeomorphisms may fail to admit stable manifolds
[
24
,
3
]
. Nevertheless, several results
[
8
,
32
,
31
,
1
]
suggest the possibility of a parallel theory for “
C^{1}
+ domination,” analogous to the
C^{1+\mathrm{H\"{o}lder}}
or
C^{2}
cases.
Along this direction, a central and challenging problem is to establish the Ledrappier–Young entropy formula for
C^{1}
diffeomorphisms with dominated splitting. In this setting, the unstable holonomy maps inside center–unstable sets are generally not Lipschitz (rather they are Hölder). In this paper, we provide a partial positive answer to this problem in the case where the center bundle is one-dimensional.

We now briefly describe the framework. Let
M
be a compact
C^{\infty}
Riemannian manifold without boundary, and let
f\in\mathrm{Diff}^{1}(M)
be a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism. Denote by
m
an
f
-invariant ergodic Borel probability measure on
M
. The main result of this paper is that if the Oseledets splitting
E^{u}\oplus E^{c}\oplus E^{s}
corresponding to the positive, zero and negative exponents bundles is a dominated splitting on the support of
m
, and if the zero exponent has multiplicity one, then the center contributes no entropy.

Main Theorem

(Section 1.2,
Section

1.2
)
.

Let
f\in\mathrm{Diff}^{1}(M)
preserve an ergodic Borel probability
m
. If the Oseledets splitting
E^{u}\oplus E^{c}\oplus E^{s}
is dominated on the support of
m
and
\dim E^{c}\leq 1
, then we have
h^{u}_{m}(f)=h_{m}(f)
, where
h_{m}(f)
denotes the metric entropy and
h_{m}^{u}(f)
the unstable entropy.

See
Section

1.2
for precise definitions. In particular, the assumptions of the Main Theorem hold for any ergodic measure of diffeomorphisms away from homoclinic tangencies. See
Section

1.3
for details.

We now turn to the invariance principle, first established by Ledrappier in
[
15
]
for linear cocycles and later extended to the general setting by Avila and Viana in
[
2
]
. It is a powerful tool in smooth ergodic theory with numerous important applications.
Although not explicitly stated in Avila-Viana’s work, the Lipschitz regularity for the unstable holonomy maps inside center-stable manifolds (as in the Ledrappier-Young’s theory) is in fact a necessary assumption.

As an application of our Main Theorem, we show that the Avila–Viana invariance principle continues to hold in the
C^{1}
category when the center is one-dimensional.
Indeed, Tahzibi and Yang
[
33
, Corollary 2.1]
proved that a measure is
u
-invariance if and only if its entropy along unstable foliation coincides with the entropy of the quotient measure along the unstable foliation of the quotient space.
From this perspective, the invariance principle follows from our Main Theorem, which ensures that zero Lyapunov exponents contribute no entropy. Consequently, several recent works that rely on the invariance principle also remain valid for
C^{1}
diffeomorphisms, including
[
25
]
,
[
33
]
,
[
36
]
,
[
35
]
, and
[
34
]
; see
Section

6
for details.

Finally,we emphasize that the Lipschitz regularity of holonomy maps is a fundamental assumption for many remarkable results in smooth ergodic theory, such as
[
5
]
.
In future works, we aim to relax the Lipschitz regularity to Hölder regularity in some important cases. In particular, in Part 2 of this paper,
we will establish a
C^{1}
version of the Ledrappier-Young entropy formula from their seminal work
[
19
]
.

Strategy of proof:

We now outline the strategy used to overcome the lack of regularity.

In the
C^{1}
setting, the Lyapunov charts in
[
18
]
are no longer available, since the sub-exponential variation on the size of the charts under iteration depends heavily on the
C^{1+\mathrm{H\"{o}lder}}
regularity.
Instead, the presence of a dominated splitting allows us to construct fake foliations in local chart neighborhoods. Moreover, the results of Abdenur, Bonatti and Crovisier in
[
1
]
enables the construction of fake foliation charts, see
Definition

3
, to replace the Lyapunov charts. It is worth noting that the size of the fake foliation charts are non-uniform, scaling as the reciprocal of the hyperbolicity function of the base point (
Section

2.1
). Although these charts lack the one-step length estimates as for Lyapunov charts in
[
18
, Section 2.1]
, they still provide sufficient asymptotic length control (
Remark

3
), which is sufficient for our purpose.

To address the absence of Lipschitz regularity for unstable holonomies, we first show that the fake unstable holonomies inside the fake center-unstable leaves are bi-Hölder continuous, with Hölder exponent arbitrarily close to one; see
Section

2.2
. While this fact seems to be well-known, we are not aware of an explicit reference for our precise setting. Next, we carefully modify the convergence argument in
[
18
, Section 5]
. Instead of centered balls, we utilize the uncentered maximal function, where the balls are allowed to shift only along
E^{c}
. The one-dimensionality of
E^{c}
is crucial here, as it enables us to establish the required convergence arguments for uncentered balls (
Section

4
,
Section

5.2
), thereby recovering the rest of the proof in
[
18
, Section 5]
.

Organization:

Section 2 contains the construction of the fake foliation charts and provides the necessary geometric preparations, including the Hölder continuity of fake unstable holonomies inside fake center–unstable leaves, as well as properties of unstable manifolds and center–unstable sets.
Section 3 introduces the notion of unstable entropy for
C^{1}
diffeomorphisms with dominated splitting, in parallel with
[
19
]
, and establishes the quotient structure of two special partitions. In Section 4 we present the uncentered maximal function, where the one-dimensional assumption plays a crucial role. In Section 5 we complete the proof of the Main Theorem. Finally, in Section
6
we state and prove the
C^{1}
invariance principle, namely
Section

6.1
.

1.2.
Main results

In this section, we provide the technical definitions and state the main results of this paper.
As before,
f
is a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism on a compact Riemannian manifold
M
without boundary, and
m
is an
f
-invariant ergodic Borel probability measure.

A point
x\in M
is said to be a regular point if there exist numbers
\lambda_{1}(x)>\lambda_{2}(x)>\cdots>\lambda_{r(x)}(x)
and a decomposition of the tangent space

T_{x}M=E_{1}(x)\oplus E_{2}(x)\oplus\cdots\oplus E_{r(x)}(x)

such that for each
v\neq 0\in E_{i}(x)
, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\pm\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|Df^{n}_{x}v|=\lambda_{i}(x),

and

\lim_{n\rightarrow\pm\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|\mathrm{Jac}(Df^{n}_{x})|=\sum^{r(x)}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}(x)\dim E_{i}(x).

By the Oseledets theorem
[
22
]
, the set of regular points, denoted as
\Gamma
, is a set of full
m
measure and
\lambda_{i}(x),r(x)
and
\dim E_{i}(x)
are all
f
-invariant measurable functions. When
m
is ergodic, for
m
-a.e.
x\in M
,

\lambda_{i}(x)=\lambda_{i}
are called the Lyapunov exponents of
f
, and
\dim E_{i}(x)=\dim E_{i}
is called the multiplicity of
\lambda_{i}
. We denote the smallest positive exponent by
\lambda^{u}
and the largest negative exponent by
\lambda^{s}
.

We recall some basic properties of dominated splittings (see
[
1
]
). A
Df
-invariant splitting
T_{\Lambda}M=F\oplus E
of the tangent bundle over an
f
-invariant set
\Lambda
is dominated if there exists
N>0
such that given any
x\in\Lambda
and any unit vectors
v\in E(x),u\in F(x)
, we have

|Df_{x}^{N}(v)|\leq\frac{1}{2}|Df_{x}^{N}(u)|.

More generally, a
Df
-invariant splitting
T_{\Lambda}M=E_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus E_{t}
is dominated if given any
\ell\in\{1,\cdots,t-1\}
the splitting
(E_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus E_{\ell})\oplus(E_{\ell+1}\oplus\cdots\oplus E_{t})
is dominated. Recall that if an
f
invariant set
\Lambda
admits a dominated splitting
T_{\Lambda}M=E_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus E_{t}
, then the splitting depends continuously on
x\in\Lambda
and extends uniquely to a dominated splitting over the closure of
\Lambda
. Moreover, the angles between different bundles are uniformly bounded away from zero.

Define
E^{s}(x)=\bigoplus_{\lambda_{i}<0}E_{i}(x)
,
E^{c}(x)=\bigoplus_{\lambda_{i}=0}E_{i}(x)
and
E^{u}(x)=\bigoplus_{\lambda_{i}>0}E_{i}(x)
for any
x\in\Gamma
. We say that the splitting
E^{u}\oplus E^{c}\oplus E^{s}
is dominated on the support of
m
, if
E^{u}\oplus E^{c}\oplus E^{s}
is a dominated splitting on the invariant set
\Gamma\cap\mathrm{supp}(m)
. In this case, the splitting can be uniquely extended to a dominated splitting on
\mathrm{supp}(m)
, since
\Gamma\cap\mathrm{supp}(m)
is dense inside
\mathrm{supp}(m)
.

For any
x\in\Gamma\cap\mathrm{supp}(m)
we define the (global) stable and unstable manifolds as

W^{s}(x)=\left\{y\in M:\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log d(f^{n}x,f^{n}y)<0\right\}

and

W^{u}(x)=\left\{y\in M:\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log d(f^{-n}x,f^{-n}y)<0\right\},

where
d
is the Riemannian metric on
M
. When
E^{u}\oplus E^{c}\oplus E^{s}
is dominated on the support of
m
, Abdenur, Bonatti, and Crovisier proved in
[
1
, Section 8]
that
W^{u}(x)
(resp.
W^{s}(x)
) is an injectively immersed
C^{1}
submanifold of dimension
\dim E^{u}(x)
(resp.
\dim E^{s}
).

When
E^{u}\oplus E^{c}\oplus E^{s}
is dominated on the support of
m
, we define the
u
-entropy of
f
following the ideas of
[
18
]
,
[
12
]
and
[
40
]
. We will use the concepts of measurable partitions, disintegration and entropy, for which the reader can find more details in
[
26
]
. Similar to
[
18
]
, we define the
u
-entropy through special partitions:

Definition 1

(Subordinate partition)
.

Let
\xi
be a measurable partition of
M
. We say
\xi
is subordinate to
W^{u}
, if for
m
-a.e.
x
we have:

a)
\xi(x)\subseteq W^{u}(x)
and
\xi(x)
contains an open neighborhood of
x
within
W^{u}(x)
.

b)
\xi
is increasing, meaning that
f\xi\prec\xi
.

c)
\bigvee_{n=0}^{\infty}f^{-n}\xi
is the partition into points.

The
u
-entropy, denoted by
h^{u}_{m}(f)
, is defined as the conditional entropy
h^{u}_{m}(f):=H(\xi|f\xi)
for any
\xi
subordinate to
W^{u}
. We will see that the
u
-entropy does not depend on the choice of
\xi
. More details will be given in
Section

3
.

We now state our main result in this paper:

{theorem}
[Main Theorem]
Let
f\in\mathrm{Diff}^{1}(M)
preserve an ergodic Borel probability
m
. If the splitting
E^{u}\oplus E^{c}\oplus E^{s}
is dominated on the support of
m
and
\dim E^{c}\leq 1
, then
h^{u}_{m}(f)=h_{m}(f)
, where
h_{m}(f)
denotes the metric entropy of
f
.

Remark 1
.

We only need to prove the theorem for
\dim E^{c}=1
. In the case
\dim E^{c}=0
, i.e.,
m
is an ergodic hyperbolic measure, the result is proven by Ures, Viana, Yang and Yang in
[
35
, Proposition A.4]
. Although their result was proven for partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms, the proof is still valid under our setting with minor adjustments and is indeed easier than the case
\dim E^{c}=1
.

1.3.
Diffeomorphisms away from tangencies

We say that
f\in\mathrm{Diff}^{1}(M)
is away from homoclinic tangencies if there exists a
C^{1}
neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
of
f
such that for any
g\in\mathcal{U}
and any hyperbolic periodic orbit
P
of
g
,
W^{s}(P)
is transverse to
W^{u}(P)
.

The following is a simple consequence of
[
20
, Proposition 3.4]
.

{proposition}

Assume that
f\in\mathrm{Diff}^{1}(M)
is away from homoclinic tangencies and
m
is an ergodic measure of
f
. Then there exists a dominated splitting
T_{\mathrm{supp}(m)}M=E^{u}\oplus E^{c}\oplus E^{s}
, such that
\dim E^{c}\leq 1
, the Lyapunov exponents in
E^{u}
are positive and the Lyapunov exponents in
E^{s}
are negative.

Hence the zero exponent has multiplicity at most one.
By the Main Theorem, we have
h^{u}_{m}(f)=h_{m}(f)
. This is summarized in the following theorem.

{theorem}

If
f\in\mathrm{Diff}^{1}(M)
is away from homoclinic tangencies and
m
is an ergodic measure of
f
, then
h^{u}_{m}(f)=h_{m}(f)
.

1.4.
The non-ergodic case

If the measure
m
is not an ergodic measure, we can use the Ergodic Decomposition Theorem to reduce to the ergodic case. Such an argument was used in
[
18
, Section 6.2]
to reduce the non-ergodic case to the ergodic case in the

setting. The same technique applies to our setting, and we omit the details here.

However, one should note that by the Oseledets theorem, there exists a splitting of positive, zero and negative exponents bundles
E^{u}(x)\oplus E^{c}(x)\oplus E^{s}(x)
for
x\in\Gamma
as before, where
\Gamma
is the regular point set, but the dimension of each bundle need not be constant for
m
-a.e.
\ x\in M
if
m
is not ergodic. If this splitting is dominated on the support of
m
and
\dim E^{c}(x)\leq 1
for
m
-a.e.
x\in M
, then for
m
-a.e.
x\in M
, the ergodic component
m_{x}
of

satisfies the assumptions of
Section

1.2
, i.e., the splitting
E^{u}\oplus E^{c}\oplus E^{s}
is dominated on the support of
m_{x}
and
\dim E^{c}(y)\leq 1
for
m_{x}-
a.e.
y\in M
. In this case, we define the
u
-entropy of

as the integral of the

-entropy of its ergodic component

, i.e.,

h^{u}_{m}(f):=\int h^{u}_{m_{x}}(f)\,\mathrm{d}m(x).

Then we obtain a non-ergodic version of the main Theorem.

{theorem}

Let
f\in\mathrm{Diff}^{1}(M)
preserve a Borel probability
m
. If the splitting
E^{u}\oplus E^{c}\oplus E^{s}
is dominated on the support of
m
and
\dim E^{c}(x)\leq 1
for
m
-a.e.
x\in M
, then we have
h^{u}_{m}(f)=h_{m}(f)
.

Proceeding as above, one also obtains a non-ergodic version of
Section

1.3
.

{theorem}

Assume that
f\in\mathrm{Diff}^{1}(M)
is away from homoclinic tangencies and
m
is an invariant measure of

, then
h^{u}_{m}(f)=h_{m}(f)
.

Further applications, in particular, the
C^{1}
invariance principle, are postponed to Section

2.1.
Fake foliation

In this section, we consider fake foliations arising from dominated splittings. More details on fake foliations can be found in

Throughout this paper, we assume that

\mathrm{e}^{-\epsilon N}\leq\frac{\|D{\tilde{f}}_{x}^{\pm N}(0)|_{V^{i}(x)}\|}{\|D{\tilde{f}}_{x}^{\pm N}(0)|_{E^{i}(x)}\|}\leq\mathrm{e}^{\epsilon N},

Now we consider the splitting

We fixed a constant

{lemma}

[Fake foliations]
For each

and

b)

Remark 2
.

It is worth pointing out that fake foliations are defined on the entirety of

and are invariant under

For simplicity, when

.

2.2.
Uniformly Hölder holonomy

In this section, we consider the regularity of the fake
u
-holonomies insider the fake
cu
-leaves. When
f
is only
C^{1}
, we lose the Lipschitz property of the holonomy maps as in
[
18
, Section 4.2]
; however, we can prove the uniformly Hölder property with Hölder exponent arbitrarily close to
1
. We first define
c
-transversal.

Definition 2

(
c
-transversal)
.

For each
x\in\Gamma^{\prime}
,
U\subseteq T_{x}M
is called a
c
-transversal inside
{\widetilde{W}}^{cu}_{x}(x)\subseteq T_{x}M
with slope
\leq\frac{1}{C}
, if it is a
c
-dimensional submanifold of
\hat{U}=\mathrm{graph}(\varphi)\subseteq{\widetilde{W}}^{cu}_{x}(x)
where the function
\varphi:E^{c}_{x}\rightarrow E^{us}_{x}
satisfies
|D\varphi|\leq\frac{1}{C}
.

Then we can prove that the fake
u
-holonomies inside the fake
cu
-leaves are uniformly Hölder. Similar results can be found in
[
39
]
and
[
6
]
. Recall
\epsilon
from Lemma
2.1
which, with
\eta=\frac{\epsilon}{2}
, determines
\Gamma^{\prime}
.

{lemma}

[Uniformly Hölder]
For each
x\in\Gamma^{\prime}
, given two
c
-transversals
U
and
V
with slope
\leq\frac{1}{C_{1}}
inside
{\widetilde{W}}^{cu}_{x}(x)\subseteq T_{x}M
, if the fake
u
-foliation
{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}^{u}_{x}
induces an injective holonomy
{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{x}:U\rightarrow V
, then the fake
u
-holonomy is uniformly Hölder. Specifically, for every
r>0
, if we have for any
p\in U,|p-{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{x}(p)|\leq r
, then for
\alpha=1-\frac{7\epsilon}{\lambda^{u}}
,
C_{2}=C_{2}(A(x),r)
,

C_{2}^{-1}|p-q|^{\alpha^{-1}}\leq|{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{x}(p)-{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{x}(q)|\leq C_{2}|p-q|^{\alpha},\ \forall p,q\in U.

Proof.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
whose choice (depending on
x,p
and
q
) will be specified later.
We define
U_{n}:={\tilde{g}}_{x}^{-n}U
,
V_{n}:={\tilde{g}}_{x}^{-n}V
and note that
U_{n},V_{n}\subseteq{\widetilde{W}}^{cu}_{f^{-n}x}(f^{-n}x)
. By the definition of
C_{1}
(see the discussion after Equation (
3
)),
U_{n},V_{n}
stay as
c
-transversals with slope
\leq\frac{1}{C_{0}}
. We denote
p_{n}={\tilde{g}}^{-n}_{x}p
,
q_{n}={\tilde{g}}^{-n}_{x}q
, and note that by invariance,
{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{f^{-n}x}p_{n}={\tilde{g}}_{x}^{-n}{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{x}p
and
{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{f^{-n}x}q_{n}={\tilde{g}}^{-n}_{x}{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{x}q
.

We denote
\hat{U}_{n}:={\tilde{g}}_{x}^{-n}\hat{U}
and
\hat{V}_{n}:={\tilde{g}}_{x}^{-n}\hat{V}
where
\hat{U},\hat{V}
are given in Definition
2
. In general, we write
d_{R}(\cdot,\cdot)
for the distance in the submanifold
R
. Since
\hat{U}_{n}
and
\hat{V}_{n}
are both graphs, we have
\frac{1}{2}|p-q|\leq d_{\hat{U}_{n}}(p,q)\leq 2|p-q|
and
\frac{1}{2}|p-q|\leq d_{\hat{V}_{n}}(p,q)\leq 2|p-q|
. Then we have

(4)

\displaystyle|p_{n}-q_{n}|

\displaystyle=|{\tilde{g}}^{-n}_{x}p-{\tilde{g}}^{-n}_{x}q|\leq 2d_{\hat{U}_{n}}({\tilde{g}}^{-n}_{x}p,{\tilde{g}}^{-n}_{x}q)

\displaystyle\leq 2\int^{p}_{q}|D{\tilde{g}}^{-n}_{x}(y)|_{T_{y}\hat{U}}|\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}C_{f}^{N}\cdot\int^{p}_{q}|D{\tilde{g}}^{-KN}_{x}(y)|_{T_{y}\hat{U}}|\,\mathrm{d}y\ \ \ (\text{where }K=[\frac{n}{N}])

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}C^{N}_{f}\cdot\int^{p}_{q}|D{\tilde{f}}^{-KN}_{x}(0)|_{E^{c}_{x}}|\cdot\mathrm{e}^{2KN\epsilon}\,\mathrm{d}y\ \ \ (\text{by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{local argument 1} and \ref{local argument 2}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}C^{N}_{f}\mathrm{e}^{2n\epsilon}A(x)\mathrm{e}^{KN\epsilon}d_{U}(p,q)\ \ \ (\text{by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{ABC ergodic lemma}})

\displaystyle\leq A(x)C^{N}_{f}\mathrm{e}^{3n\epsilon}|p-q|.

In the same way, we can estimate

(5)

\displaystyle|p_{n}-{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{f^{-n}x}(p_{n})|

\displaystyle=|{\tilde{g}}^{-n}_{x}p-{\tilde{g}}^{-n}_{x}{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{x}p|\leq 2d_{{\widetilde{W}}^{u}_{f^{-n}x}(p_{n})}({\tilde{g}}^{-n}_{x}p,{\tilde{g}}^{-n}_{x}{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{x}p)

\displaystyle\leq 2\int^{p}_{{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{x}p}|D{\tilde{g}}^{-n}_{x}(y)|_{T_{y}{\widetilde{W}}^{u}_{x}(p)}|\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}C_{f}^{N}\cdot\int^{p}_{{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{x}p}|D{\tilde{g}}^{-KN}_{x}(y)|_{{T_{y}{\widetilde{W}}^{u}_{x}(p)}}|\mathrm{d}y\ \ \ (\text{where }K=[\frac{n}{N}])

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}C^{N}_{f}\cdot\int^{p}_{{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{x}p}|D{\tilde{f}}^{-KN}_{x}(0)|_{E^{u}_{x}}|\cdot\mathrm{e}^{2KN\epsilon}\mathrm{d}y\ \ \ (\text{by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{local argument 1} and \ref{local argument 2}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}C^{N}_{f}\mathrm{e}^{2n\epsilon}A(x)\mathrm{e}^{KN(-\lambda^{u}+\epsilon)}d_{{\widetilde{W}}^{u}_{x}(p)}(p,{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{x}p)\ \ \ (\text{by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{ABC ergodic lemma}})

\displaystyle\leq A(x)C^{N}_{f}\mathrm{e}^{n(-\lambda^{u}+3\epsilon)}|p-{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{x}p|

\displaystyle\leq A(x)C^{N}_{f}\mathrm{e}^{n(-\lambda^{u}+3\epsilon)}\cdot r,

and similarly,

|q_{n}-{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{f^{-n}x}(q_{n})|\leq A(x)C^{N}_{f}\mathrm{e}^{n(-\lambda^{u}+3\epsilon)}\cdot r.

We now choose a smooth foliation
{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}^{\bot}
in
T_{f^{-n}x}M
with parallel leaves, such that each leaf is a plane parallel to the
E^{us}_{f^{-n}x}
plane. This foliation induces a holonomy
h^{\bot}:\hat{U}_{n}\rightarrow\hat{V}_{n}
which is a homeomorphism since
\hat{U}_{n}
and
\hat{V}_{n}
are graphs of functions with slopes less than
\frac{1}{C_{0}}
. Since the angle of the holonomy and the fake leaf is uniform, there exists
C_{3}>0
such that

|h^{\bot}(p_{n})-h^{\bot}(q_{n})|\leq C_{3}|p_{n}-q_{n}|\leq C_{3}A(x)C^{N}_{f}\mathrm{e}^{3n\epsilon}|p-q|,

\displaystyle|{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{f^{-n}x}(p_{n})-h^{\bot}(p_{n})|

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}|{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{f^{-n}x}(p_{n})-h^{\bot}(p_{n})|_{(E^{us}_{x})^{\bot}}

\displaystyle=C_{3}|p_{n}-{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{f^{-n}x}(p_{n})|_{(E^{us}_{x})^{\bot}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}|p_{n}-{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{f^{-n}x}(p_{n})|

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}A(x)C^{N}_{f}\mathrm{e}^{n(-\lambda^{u}+3\epsilon)}\cdot r,

and in the same way,

|{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{f^{-n}x}(q_{n})-h^{\bot}(q_{n})|\leq C_{3}A(x)C^{N}_{f}\mathrm{e}^{n(-\lambda^{u}+3\epsilon)}\cdot r.

Thus, we have that

|{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{f^{-n}x}(p_{n})-{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{f^{-n}x}(q_{n})|\leq C_{3}A(x)C^{N}_{f}\mathrm{e}^{3n\epsilon}|p-q|+2C_{3}A(x)C^{N}_{f}\mathrm{e}^{n(-\lambda^{u}+3\epsilon)}\cdot r.

Then we write

(6)

\displaystyle|{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{x}(p)-{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{x}(q)|

\displaystyle=|{\tilde{g}}^{n}_{f^{-n}x}{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{f^{-n}x}(p_{n})-{\tilde{g}}^{n}_{f^{-n}x}{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{f^{-n}x}(q_{n})|

\displaystyle\leq 2d_{\hat{V}_{n}}({\tilde{g}}^{n}_{f^{-n}x}{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{f^{-n}x}(p_{n}),{\tilde{g}}^{n}_{f^{-n}x}{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{f^{-n}x}(q_{n}))

\displaystyle\leq 2\int^{{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{f^{-n}x}(p_{n})}_{{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{f^{-n}x}(q_{n})}|D{\tilde{g}}^{n}_{f^{-n}x}(y)|_{T_{y}\hat{V}_{n}({\tilde{h}}^{u}_{f^{-n}x}(p_{n}))}|\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}C_{f}^{N}\cdot\int^{{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{f^{-n}x}(p_{n})}_{{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{f^{-n}x}(q_{n})}|D{\tilde{g}}^{KN}_{f^{-n}x}(y)|_{T_{y}\hat{V}_{n}({\tilde{h}}^{u}_{f^{-n}x}(p_{n}))}|\,\mathrm{d}y\ \ \ (\text{where }K=[\frac{n}{N}])

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}C^{N}_{f}\cdot\int^{{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{f^{-n}x}(p_{n})}_{{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{f^{-n}x}(q_{n})}|D{\tilde{f}}^{KN}_{f^{-n}x}(0)|_{E^{u}_{f^{-n}x}}|\cdot\mathrm{e}^{2KN\epsilon}\,\mathrm{d}y\ \ \ (\text{by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{local argument 1} and \ref{local argument 2}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}C^{N}_{f}\mathrm{e}^{2n\epsilon}A(f^{-n}x)\mathrm{e}^{KN\epsilon}d_{\hat{V}_{n}}({\tilde{h}}^{u}_{f^{-n}x}(p_{n}),{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{f^{-n}x}(q_{n}))\ \ \ (\text{by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{ABC ergodic lemma}})

\displaystyle\leq A(f^{-n}x)C^{N}_{f}\mathrm{e}^{3n\epsilon}|{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{f^{-n}x}(p_{n})-{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{f^{-n}x}(q_{n})|

\displaystyle\leq A(x)C^{N}_{f}\mathrm{e}^{4n\epsilon}(C_{3}A(x)C^{N}_{f}\mathrm{e}^{3n\epsilon}|p-q|+2C_{3}A(x)C^{N}_{f}\mathrm{e}^{n(-\lambda^{u}+3\epsilon)}r).

Hence there exists a constant
C_{4}=C_{4}(A(x))
such that

|{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{x}(p)-{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{x}(q)|\leq C_{4}(\mathrm{e}^{7n\epsilon}|p-q|+\mathrm{e}^{(-\lambda^{u}+7\epsilon)n}r).

Letting
n=[\frac{\log|p-q|}{-\lambda^{u}}]
,
C_{2}=C_{4}C_{f}(r+1)
and recalling the choice of
\alpha=1-7\epsilon/\lambda^{u}
, we have

|{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{x}(p)-{\tilde{h}}^{u}_{x}(q)|\leq C_{2}|p-q|^{\alpha}.

With this, we finished the proof for the right-hand side of the lemma. The left-hand side can be proven similarly.
∎

Remark 3

.

Equation

and

2.3.
Unstable manifolds and center unstable sets

In this section we discuss the geometric structure of the unstable manifolds and center unstable sets. We introduce the concept of fake foliation charts. For this purpose, let
\epsilon>0
be fixed, and
\Gamma^{\prime}
be the full measure set given by Lemma
2.1
. Note that
R_{x}(\rho)
is the ball centered at
0
with radius
\rho
under the box norm.

Definition 3

(Fake foliation charts)
.

For
x\in\Gamma^{\prime}
, the
\delta
-fake foliation chart of
x
is the pair
\{R_{x}(\delta A(x)^{-1}),\exp_{x}\}
where
R_{x}(\delta A(x)^{-1})\subseteq T_{x}M
and
\exp_{x}:R_{x}(\delta A(x)^{-1})\rightarrow M
.

In Remark
2
we established that fake foliations carry true dynamical information only within the uniform size
r_{0}
, inside which we have
\tilde{g}=\tilde{f}
. To guarantee that orbits stay within this scale, we will only consider foliation charts of the size
\delta A(x)^{-1}\ll r_{0}
, as indicated by the local argument (see for instance the proof of Lemma
2.2
and
Section

2.3
below). This will also be the scale for which unstable/center unstable manifolds are defined. Remarkably, this is precisely the size of the Lyapunov charts in
[
18
]
as the reciprocal of the hyperbolic function
A(x)
.

Recall that
C_{f}
was chosen by Equation (
1
) and
C_{0}
was taken such that (
3
) holds. For
0<\delta\leq\delta_{0}:=\frac{r_{0}}{100C^{N}_{f}C_{0}^{10}}
, we define the center unstable set

S^{cu}_{\delta}(x):=\{v\in T_{x}M:|{\tilde{f}}^{-n}_{x}v|^{\prime}\leq\delta A(f^{-n}x)^{-1},\forall n\geq 0\}.

The global unstable manifold of
x\in\Gamma^{\prime}

W^{u}(x)=\{y\in M:\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log(d(f^{-n}x,f^{-n}y))<0\}

is an injectively immersed
C^{1}
manifold with dimension
u
by
[
1
, Proposition 8.9]
.
For any
y\in\Gamma^{\prime}\cap\exp_{x}S^{cu}_{\delta}(x)
we denote

W^{u}_{x,2\delta}(y):=\exp^{-1}_{x}(\text{the component of }W^{u}(y)\cap\exp_{x}R_{x}(2\delta A(x)^{-1})\text{ that contains $y$})

as the local unstable manifold (in the
\delta
-fake foliation charts of
x
). We first prove the following lemma, which characterizes the size of the fake unstable leaves that coincide with the true unstable manifolds inside the center unstable sets.

{lemma}

For any
y\in\Gamma^{\prime}\cap\exp_{x}S^{cu}_{\delta}(x)
,
W^{u}_{x,2\delta}(y)={\widetilde{W}}^{u}_{x}(y)\cap R_{x}(2\delta A(x)^{-1})
.

Proof.

Since
W^{u}_{x,2\delta}(y)
is a
u
-dimensional connected injectively immersed
C^{1}
manifold and
{\widetilde{W}}^{u}_{x}(y)\cap R_{x}(2\delta A(x)^{-1})
is a graph of a function
\varphi:R_{x}^{u}(2\delta A(x)^{-1})\rightarrow R_{x}^{cs}(2\delta A(x^{-1}))
with slope
\leq\frac{\gamma_{0}}{C_{1}}
, we only need to prove that
{\widetilde{W}}^{u}_{x}(y)\cap R_{x}(2\delta A(x)^{-1})\subseteq W^{u}_{x,2\delta}(y)
, which only requires us to prove that
{\widetilde{W}}^{u}_{x}(y)\cap R_{x}(2\delta A(x)^{-1})\subseteq\exp_{x}^{-1}W^{u}(y)
.

If
z\in{\widetilde{W}}^{u}_{x}(y)\cap R_{x}(2\delta A(x)^{-1})
, we consider
{\tilde{g}}^{-n}_{x}z\in{\widetilde{W}}^{u}_{f^{-n}x}(f^{-n}y)
. By the local argument, we have

(7)

\displaystyle|{\tilde{g}}^{-n}_{x}z-{\tilde{g}}^{-n}_{x}\exp_{x}^{-1}y|

\displaystyle\leq C^{N}_{f}\mathrm{e}^{(-\lambda^{u}+3\epsilon)n}A(x)|z-\exp_{x}^{-1}y|

\displaystyle\leq 4C_{0}\delta C^{N}_{f}\mathrm{e}^{(-\lambda^{u}+3\epsilon)n}\leq\frac{r_{0}}{10},

which goes to zero exponentially when
n\to+\infty
.

Since
y\in\exp_{x}S^{cu}_{\delta}(x)
, for any
n\geq 0
,
{\tilde{f}}^{-n}_{x}\exp_{x}^{-1}y
stays in the local charts
\{|v|\leq r_{0}\}
. Since
{\tilde{f}}_{x}={\tilde{g}}_{x}
when
|v|\leq r_{0}
, we have
|{\tilde{g}}_{x}^{-n}\exp_{x}^{-1}y|\leq\frac{r_{0}}{10}
and hence
|{\tilde{g}}^{-n}_{x}z|<r_{0}
. Thus, for any
n\geq 0
,
{\tilde{g}}_{x}^{-n}z
stays in the local charts
\{|v|\leq r_{0}\}
. Together with
Equation

7
we have
|{\tilde{f}}^{-n}_{x}z-{\tilde{f}}^{-n}_{x}\exp_{x}^{-1}y|
goes to zero exponentially when
n\to+\infty
. This means
z\in\exp_{x}^{-1}W^{u}(y)
, and consequently
{\widetilde{W}}^{u}_{x}(y)\cap R_{x}(2\delta A(x)^{-1})\subseteq\exp_{x}^{-1}W^{u}(y)
.
∎

For
0<\delta\leq\delta_{0}
, we prove the following lemma, which is partially parallel to
[
18
, Lemma 2.2.3]
.

{lemma}

A. For any
x\in\Gamma^{\prime},\ y\in\Gamma^{\prime}\cap\exp_{x}S^{cu}_{\delta}(x)
:

1)
W^{u}_{x,2\delta}(y)

4.
Uncentered maximal functions

Recall that for general Radon measures, the Lebesgue density theorem is proven using centered maximal functions as in
[
18
, Section 4]
. In this paper, however, we consider the
uncentered maximal operator
as in
[
30
, Chapter 3]
to bypass the lack of Lipschitz regularity for the holonomy maps. In dimension one, it is well-known (see, for instance,
[
29
]
) that the uncentered maximal operator is of weak type
(1,1)
, and therefore Lebesgue density theorem holds for uncentered balls. This is precisely where the one-dimensional hypothesis (or the multiplicity-one property of the Lyapunov exponents) is used.

Below, we only consider open coverings on
\mathbb{R}
, that is,
n=1
. In this case, an uncentered ball w.r.t.
y
is just a connected open interval that contains
y
. The next lemma has been used to prove
[
16
, Proposition 3.2]
; the proof is omitted.

{lemma}

[One-dimensional covering lemma] Assume that
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}
or
E\subseteq S^{1}
and
{\mathcal{A}}
is a finite covering of
E
by open intervals. Then there exists a subcovering
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
of
E
such that every
x\in E
is contained in at most two intervals.

Now let
m
be a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
,
g\in L^{1}(m),g\geq 0
. We define the
uncentered
average, maximal, and minimal function as:

g^{B}(x)=\frac{1}{m(B)}\int_{B}g(z)\,\mathrm{d}m(z)\,\mbox{ where $B$ is an uncentered ball w.r.t.\ $x$,}

and

g^{*}(x)=\sup_{B\ni x}g^{B}(x)

g_{*}(x)=\inf_{B\ni x}g^{B}(x)

It is easy to see that
g^{*}
(resp.
g_{*}
) is lower (resp. upper) semi-continuous and hence measurable.

{lemma}

[Maximal-minimal functions estimates] For any
\lambda>0
, the following hold:

a)
\displaystyle m(g^{*}>\lambda)\leq\frac{2}{\lambda}\int g\,\mathrm{d}m
.

b)
\displaystyle\int_{\{g_{*}<\lambda\}}g\,\mathrm{d}m\leq 2\lambda
.

Proof.

a) is essentially
[
29
, Theorem (a)]
(with the constant
2
instead of
5
on the right-hand side due to Lemma
4
) and is omitted. Below we prove b).

Let
\mathfrak{B}=\{g_{*}<\lambda\}
. For every
x\in\mathfrak{B}
, we take
B_{x}\ni x
such that
g^{B_{x}}(x)<\lambda
, that is,
\int_{B_{x}}g\,\mathrm{d}m<\lambda m(B_{x})
. Then
\{B_{x}:x\in\mathfrak{B}\}
is an open covering of
\mathfrak{B}
, and there exists a countable subcovering
\{B_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N}\}
.

Now
\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{B}}g\,\mathrm{d}m\leq\int_{\cup^{\infty}_{i=1}B_{i}}g\,\mathrm{d}m
and we have
\displaystyle\int_{\cup^{N}_{i=1}B_{i}}g\,\mathrm{d}m\to\int_{\cup^{\infty}_{i=1}B_{i}}g\,\mathrm{d}m
as
N\to\infty
. For each
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we treat
\{B_{1},B_{2}\dots B_{N}\}
as a finite subcovering of
\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}B_{i}
, and apply Lemma
4
to obtain a finite subcovering
\{B_{i_{1}},\cdots,B_{i_{M(N)}}\}
of
\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}B_{i}
that covers each point at most twice. We have

\displaystyle\int_{\cup^{N}_{i=1}B_{i}}g\,\mathrm{d}m\leq\sum_{j=1}^{M(N)}\int_{B_{i_{j}}}g\,\mathrm{d}m\leq\sum_{j=1}^{M(N)}\lambda m(B_{i_{j}})\leq 2\lambda

for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Sending
N\to\infty
, we have
\int_{\{g_{*}<\lambda\}}g\,\mathrm{d}m\leq 2\lambda
as required.
∎

With this lemma, we can prove the following lemma as in
[
18
, Section 4.1]
.

{lemma}

Let
(X,m)
be a Lebesgue space and

5.
Proof of Main Theorem

In this section, we finish the proof of the Main Theorem. In

5.1
we give requirements for the size of a set

Section

we prove the convergence arguments (

Before starting the proof, we briefly compare our proof in
Section

5
with
[
18
, Section 5]
and explain how we circumvent the lack of Lipschitz regularity for the holonomy maps.

In
[
18
]
, the authors use the Besicovitch covering lemma for centered balls; consequently, when they use the transverse metrics to define balls and iterate along the orbit of

, they need to use holonomy maps whenever

(defined in
Section

3.3
as in

In our proof, the holonomy is only used twice (at

6.1.

Following
[

can be joined by a curve formed by finitely many arcs that are contained in leaves of

or

Definition 6

(Special partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms)
.

Denote by

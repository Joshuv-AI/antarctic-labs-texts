Inteval Analysis for two spherical functions arising from robust Perspective-n-Lines problem
Xiang Zheng, Haodong Jiang, Junfeng WU

Abstract
This report presents a comprehensive interval analysis of two spherical functions derived from the robust Perspective-n-Lines (PnL) problem. The study is motivated by the application of a dimension-reduction technique to achieve global solutions for the robust PnL problem. We establish rigorous theoretical results, supported by detailed proofs, and validate our findings through extensive numerical simulations.

1.3
Accelerating Consensus Maximization

Consider the following 1d CM problem:

\max_{b\in\mathcal{X}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{1}\{|f(b|\mathbf{s}_{k})|\leq\epsilon\},

(3)

where
b
is a scalar parameter that belongs to
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbf{s}_{k}
is data, and
f(b|\mathbf{s}_{k})
is the residual function continuous in
b
. Suppose we can obtain
|f(b|\mathbf{s}_{k})|\leq\epsilon\Leftrightarrow b\in\bigcup_{l}[b_{kl}^{l},b_{kl}^{r}]
, the authors of
Zhang et al. (2024)
observe that problem (
3
) is equivalent to the following interval stabbing problem:

\max_{b\in\mathcal{X}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{l}\mathbf{1}\{b\in[b_{kl}^{l},b_{kl}^{r}]\}.

(4)

Next, consider a n-d CM problem:

\max_{\mathbf{b}\in\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{1}\{|f(\mathbf{b}|\mathbf{s}_{k})|\leq\epsilon\}.

(5)

The ACM method distinguishes one parameter with others,
\mathbf{b}=(\mathbf{b}_{1:n-1},b_{n})
, and branches only the space of
\mathbf{b}_{1:n-1}
. This is achieved by revising the bound-seeking procedure. Suppose we are seeking bounds for
\mathbf{b}_{1:n-1}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}_{1:n-1}
and
b_{n}
.

1.3.1
Lower Bound

Denote the consensus maximizer as
(\mathbf{b}^{*}_{1:n-1},b_{n}^{*})
, ACM finds a lower bound as follows,
\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{1}\{|f(\mathbf{b}_{1:n-1}^{*},b_{n}^{*}|\mathbf{s}_{k})|\leq\epsilon\}

\geq~\max_{b_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{1}\{|f(b_{n}|\mathbf{b}_{1:n-1}^{(c)},\mathbf{s}_{k})|\leq\epsilon\},

where
\mathbf{b}_{1:n-1}^{(c)}
is the center point of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}_{1:n-1}
. Notice that the lower bound corresponds to a 1-d CM problem, and it can be efficiently solved by interval stabbing as we do in (
4
).

1.3.2
Upper Bound

If a consensus problem can be written as

\max_{\mathbf{b}\in\mathcal{X}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{1}\{|\sum_{i}f_{i}(b_{n},h_{i}(\mathbf{b}_{1:n-1},\mathbf{s}_{k})|\mathbf{s}_{k})|\leq\epsilon\}

(6)

where
h_{i}(\mathbf{b}_{1:n-1})
is a function of
\mathbf{b}_{1:n-1}
. And if all
f_{i}
are monotonically increasing(
it’s similar to decreaseing condition
) in
h_{i}
, set that

h_{i}^{L}\leq h_{i}(\mathbf{b}_{1:n-1},\mathbf{s}_{k})\leq h_{i}^{U}

(7)

we can get

f_{L}(b_{n}|\mathbf{s}_{k})=\sum_{i}f_{i}(b_{n}|h_{i}^{L},\mathbf{s}_{k})\leq\sum_{i}f_{i}(b_{n},h_{i}(\mathbf{b}_{1:n-1},\mathbf{s}_{k})|\mathbf{s}_{k})\leq\sum_{i}f_{i}(b_{n}|h_{i}^{U},\mathbf{s}_{k})=f_{U}(b_{n}|\mathbf{s}_{k})

(8)

Given these bounding functions, ACM finds an upper bound as follows,
\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{1}\{|f(\mathbf{b}_{1:n-1}^{*},b_{n}^{*}|\mathbf{s}_{k})|\leq\epsilon\}

\leq\max_{b_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{1}\left[\{f_{L}(b_{n}|\mathbf{s}_{k})\leq\epsilon\}\bigcap\{f_{U}(b_{n}|\mathbf{s}_{k})\geq-\epsilon\}\right]

(9)

which can be solved by interval stabbing.

Readers can refer to
Zhang et al. (2024)
’s work for the detailed introduction and other applications of the accelerating consensus maximization algorithm.

Basic problem

Given a set of 3D lines

\mathop{\rm max}\limits_{\mathbf{R}\in SO(3)}~~\sum_{k}\boldsymbol{1}\{|\mathbf{}{}^{\mathcal{C}}\vec{\mathbf{n}}_{k}\bullet\mathbf{R}^{\top}{}^{\mathcal{W}}\vec{\mathbf{v}}_{k}|<=\epsilon_{r}\},

(10)

where
\vec{\mathbf{v}}_{k}

\vec{\mathbf{n}}_{k}

Modified rational six vertex model on a rectangular lattice : new formula, homogeneous and thermodynamic limits
Matthieu Cornillault, Samuel Belliard

Abstract
We continue the work of [ BPS24 ] studying the modified rational six vertex model. We find another formula of the partition function for the inhomogeneous model, in terms of a determinant that mix the modified Izergin one and a Vandermonde one. This expression enables us to compute the partition function in the homogeneous limit for the rectangular lattice, and then to study the thermodynamic limit. It leads to a new result, we obtain the first order of free energy with boundaries effects in the thermodynamic limit.



Introduction

Following on from the work of one of the authors and collaborator’s
[
BPS24
]
, we study the XXX six vertex model on a rectangular lattice with general boundary conditions (GBC), called the modified rational six vertex (MR6V) model.

For
boundary conditions already studied in the literature
, the partition function of six-vertex models is represented by determinants. In the case of XXX or XXZ models on the square lattice with domain wall boundary conditions (DWBC), it is the so-called
Izergin determinant

[
Ize87
,
ICK92
,
Kor82
,
KIB93
]
. More complex boundary conditions exist : as example we quote ones related to reflection algebra, which lead to the
Tsuchiya determinant

[
Tsu98
]
.

A first generalization to rectangular lattice was the so called six vertex model with partial domain wall boundary conditions (pDWBC), introduced in
[
FW12
]
.
This model enabled to recover the
Kostov’s determinant

[
Kos12
,
Kos12a
]
, linked with the
\mathcal{N}=4
supersymmetric Yang-Mills theory.
This model combines boundaries with same spin (up or down) and others that mixed up and down spins.

The GBC are a generalization of the pDWBC : the boundaries are not up or down spins but are linear combination of up and down spins. Related to quantum algebra, the MR6V model is connected to the XXX
\frac{1}{2}
-spin chain with a general twist, which can be represented by
modified Bethe vectors
, constructed from the
modified algebraic Bethe ansatz

[
BP15
,
BSV18
]
. Its partition function, linked with the modified Bethe vectors, is represented by the
modified Izergin determinant
(MID). This determinant is similar to the Izergin determinant but it depends also on an additional parameter and a normalization factor.

In this paper, we consider in particular the thermodynamic limit of this model. This limit is studied in two cases : first, when the lattice tends to a very long lattice, as in
[
MP21
]
with a rectangular lattice with partial domain wall boundary conditions, and then when it tends to an infinite lattice in both directions, as in
[
KZJ00
]
with a square lattice with domain wall boundary conditions.

This paper is organized as follows. In the section
1
, after recalling the MR6V model, the definition and the known formulae of its partition function, we present a new formula for this partition function.
This formula was proved in the appendix
A
, first using a similar reasoning as in
[
FW12
]
and then, in an alternative proof, showing that the new formulae is equal to the known ones.
Moreover, in the appendix
B
, we make the link with the partial domain wall partition function introduced in
[
FW12
]
.
Then, in the section
2
, we use the new formula of the partition function to compute first the homogeneous limit of the model and, then, its thermodynamic limit. The appendix
C
explains in details the computations for the infinite lattice.
Finally, the appendix
D
presents some useful mathematical tools for our computations.

1
Modified rational six vertex (MR6V) model

1.1
Definition

First, we recall the description of the inhomogeneous six-vertex model on the
n\times m
rectangular lattice in the framework of the Quantum Inverse Scattering Method (QISM).
According to the figure
1(a)
, we assign to each line and to each column a vector space,
V_{a_{i}}
for the lines
i\in[\![1,n]\!]
and
V_{b_{j}}
for columns
j\in[\![1,m]\!]
, and a free parameter,
u_{i}
for rows and
v_{j}
for columns, also called inhomogeneity. We order the lines from bottom to top and orient them from right to left. And we order the columns from right to left and orient them from bottom to top.

We assign to each segment of the lattice a spin which can be up or down. Each spin can have only two possible states so the vector spaces
V_{a_{i}}
and
V_{b_{j}}
are isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
Each vertex is labeled by a pair of variable
(u,v)
, linked with a pair of vector spaces
(V_{a},V_{b})
and is described by a
R
-matrix
R_{ab}(u,v)
(see the figure
1(b)
) whose entries are the statistical weights of the model. These entries are determined using the orientation of the line and the column.

(a)

The inhomogeneous six-vertex model on the
n\times m
rectangular lattice.

(b)

Graphical picture of a vertex and the
R
-matrix.

Figure 1
:
Modelisation of the lattice and vertices. The open arrows indicate the orientation of the lines.

In the case of the six-vertex model, the vertices of the lattice can have only six possible forms (see the figure
2
). We denote the energy of each possible vertex
\epsilon_{i}
,
i\in[\![1,6]\!]
, and their statistical weights
w_{i}=\exp(-\epsilon_{i}/(k_{B}T))
. Considering the zero field assumption (see
[
Bax82
]
for details), we have

a:=w_{1}=w_{2},\quad b:=w_{3}=w_{4}\quad\text{and}\quad c:=w_{5}=w_{6}.

(1.1)

Figure 2
:
The six different vertices in the QISM.

For the rational case, using the inhomogeneities
u
and
v
, the statistical weight of the vertex labeled by
(u,v)
can be equal to

a(u,v)=\frac{u-v+c}{c},\quad b(u,v)=\frac{u-v}{c}\quad\text{or}\quad c(u,v)=1.

(1.2)

The corresponding
R
-matrix is given by the formula

R_{ab}(u,v)=R_{ab}(u-v)=\frac{u-v}{c}I+P_{ab}

(1.3)

where
I
is the identity matrix and
P_{ab}
the permutation matrix.
R_{ab}(u,v)
is an operator acting on
V_{a}\otimes V_{b}
so we can represent it by a
4\times 4
matrix :

R_{ab}(u,v)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{u-v+c}{c}&0&0&0\\
0&\frac{u-v}{c}&1&0\\
0&1&\frac{u-v}{c}&0\\
0&0&0&\frac{u-v+c}{c}\end{pmatrix}.

(1.4)

It is one of the simplest solution of the Yang-Baxter equation

R_{ab}(u,v)R_{ac}(u,w)R_{bc}(v,w)=R_{bc}(v,w)R_{ac}(u,w)R_{ab}(u,v)

(1.5)

that ensures the integrability of the model (cf definition 7.5.2 in
[
CP00
]
).
This
R
-matrix has the property

[R_{ab}(u,v),B_{a}B_{b}]=[R_{ab}(u,v),\hat{C}_{a}\hat{C}_{b}]

(1.6)

with
B
and
\hat{C}
are
2\times 2
complex matrices. This property has motivated the following definition of the twisted inhomogeneous partition function.

Definition 1.1
.

The partition function of the inhomogeneous six vertex model on the
n\times m
rectangular lattice with general boundary conditions is

Z_{nm}(\bar{u}|\bar{v}|B|\hat{C})=\underset{\bar{a},\bar{b}}{\mathrm{tr}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}B_{a_{i}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\hat{C}_{b_{j}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{m}R_{a_{i}b_{j}}(u_{i}-v_{j})\right).

(1.7)

Remark 1.1
.

The first subscript of
Z_{nm}(\bar{u}|\bar{v}|B|\hat{C})
indicates the cardinal of
\bar{u}
and the second one the cardinal of
\bar{v}
. It will be useful in the following part.

We recall that
Z_{nm}(\bar{u}|\bar{v}|B|\hat{C})
is symmetric on the elements of
\bar{u}
and on the elements of
\bar{v}
.

Depending on the nature of the twists
\{B,\hat{C}\}
, we have different boundary conditions for the partition function. If both
B
and
\hat{C}
are invertible, the lattice is a torus. If only one of the twists is invertible, we have a cylinder. Finally, if
B
and
\hat{C}
are not invertible, we have a plane. Let us focus on this last case.
We consider a plane with general boundary conditions (see the figure
3
), defined by the four vectors

\ket{x}=x_{1}\ket{1}+x_{2}\ket{2}=\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{pmatrix}

(1.8)

with
x\in\{n,s,e,w\}
.
(\ket{1},\ket{2})
forms the canonical basis of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, the first vector representing the up spin and the second the down spin. We denote the dual vector as
\bra{x}
and we have
\braket{i|j}=\delta_{ij}
for
i,j\in\{1,2\}
.

Definition 1.2
.

We define the following twist

B=\ket{e}\otimes\bra{w}\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{C}=\ket{s}\otimes\bra{n}.

(1.9)

We can rewrite the partition function with the following formula

Z_{nm}(\bar{u}|\bar{v}|B|\hat{C})=\bra{W}\otimes\bra{N}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{m}R_{a_{i}b_{j}}(u_{i}-v_{j})\right)\ket{E}\otimes\ket{S}

(1.10)

with

\bra{N}=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{m}\bra{n}_{b_{j}},\quad\bra{W}=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\bra{w}_{a_{i}},\quad\ket{S}=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{m}\ket{s}_{b_{j}}\quad\text{and}\quad\ket{E}=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\ket{e}_{a_{i}}.

(1.11)

Figure 3
:
The
n\times m
rectangular lattice with general boundary conditions.

1.2
Known results

In
[
BPS24
]
, using a linear system approach, the authors constructed a formula for the partition function with the modified Izergin determinant
K_{nm}^{(\beta)}(\bar{u}|\bar{v})
(see
[
BSV18
]
for more details).

Proposition 1.1
.

[
BPS24
]
The partition function of the inhomogeneous rational six-vertex model with general boundary conditions is

Z_{nm}(\bar{u}|\bar{v}|B|\hat{C})=\frac{\mathrm{tr}(B)^{n}\mathrm{tr}(\hat{C})^{m}}{(1-\beta)^{m}}\frac{1}{g(\bar{u},\bar{v})}K_{nm}^{(\beta)}(\bar{u}|\bar{v})

(1.12)

with

\beta=1-\frac{\mathrm{tr}(B)\mathrm{tr}(\hat{C})}{\mathrm{tr}(B\hat{C})},\quad\mathrm{tr}(B)=\braket{w|e},\quad\mathrm{tr}(\hat{C})=\braket{n|s}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{tr}(B\hat{C})=\braket{n|e}\braket{w|s}.

(1.13)

The modified Izergin determinant can be expressed with two formulae
[
BSV18
]

\displaystyle K_{nm}^{(\beta)}(\bar{u}|\bar{v})

\displaystyle=\underset{1\leqslant k,l\leqslant m}{\mathrm{det}}\left(-\beta\delta_{kl}+\frac{f(\bar{u},v_{k})f(v_{k},\bar{v}_{k})}{h(v_{k},v_{l})}\right)

(1.14)

\displaystyle=(1-\beta)^{m-n}\underset{1\leqslant k,l\leqslant n}{\mathrm{det}}\left(f(u_{k},\bar{v})\delta_{ij}-\beta\frac{f(u_{k},\bar{u}_{k})}{h(u_{k},u_{l})}\right).

(1.15)

For the special case of a square lattice (
m=n
), we have an additional formula
[
BPS24
]

K_{n}^{(\beta)}(\bar{u}|\bar{v})=\frac{h(\bar{u},\bar{v})}{\Delta(\bar{u})\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{v})}\underset{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n}{\mathrm{det}}\left(\phi_{\beta}(u_{i}-v_{j})\right).

(1.16)

Remark 1.2
.

For specific values of the parameters (
m=n
,
\bra{n}=\bra{2}
,
\bra{w}=\bra{1}
,
\ket{e}=\ket{2}
and
\ket{s}=\ket{1}
),
\mathrm{tr}(B)=\mathrm{tr}(\hat{C})=0
and
\mathrm{tr}(B\hat{C})=1
so
\beta=1
and one can recover the Izergin determinant.

\beta
can be any complex : we can take
n_{2}=s_{2}=0
,
e_{1}=e_{2}=w_{1}=1
to obtain
w_{2}=-\beta
.

1.3
A new expression for rectangular lattice

Using the method developed by Foda and Wheeler in
[
FW12
]
for the partial domain wall boundary conditions, we found a new expression for the partition function for rectangular lattice that generalizes the formula (
1.12
) with (
1.16
).

We start from the formula (
1.12
) with (
1.16
) considering a square lattice whose the size is the length of the desired rectangle. Then, we remove the extra parameters doing them tend toward infinity to obtain the desired width. This operation consists in remove lines or columns in the lattice, due to the fact that

\frac{c}{u}R(u,v)\underset{u\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}I\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;\frac{c}{-v}R(u,v)\underset{v\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}I.

(1.17)

Doing this for all the extra parameters, we can express the partition function for the rectangular lattice with the partition function for the larger square lattice (using also the definition
1.1
of the partition function).

Proposition 1.2
.

For
n\leqslant m
,

Z_{nm}(\bar{u}|\bar{v}|B|\hat{C})=\lim\limits_{u_{m},\dots,u_{n+1}\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{tr}(B)^{m-n}}\prod_{i=n+1}^{m}\left(\frac{c}{u_{i}}\right)^{m}Z_{mm}(\bar{u}|\bar{v}|B|\hat{C})\right).

(1.18)

and for
n\geqslant m
,

Z_{nm}(\bar{u}|\bar{v}|B|\hat{C})=\lim\limits_{v_{n},\dots,v_{m+1}\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{tr}(\hat{C})^{n-m}}\prod_{j=m+1}^{n}\left(\frac{c}{-v_{j}}\right)^{n}Z_{nn}(\bar{u}|\bar{v}|B|\hat{C})\right).

(1.19)

The limits are to be taken sequentially, starting respectively with
u_{m}
and
v_{n}
.

Proposition 1.3
.

Starting from the previous proposition and following the steps in section 2 of
[
FW12
]
, we obtained a new explicit formula for
Z_{nm}(\bar{u}|\bar{v}|B|\hat{C})
in terms of the determinant of a block matrix :

\displaystyle Z_{nm}(\bar{u}|\bar{v}|B|\hat{C})=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{tr}(B)^{n}\mathrm{tr}(\hat{C})^{m}}{(1-\beta)^{\min(n,m)}}\frac{h(\bar{u},\bar{v})}{g(\bar{u},\bar{v})\Delta(\bar{u})\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{v})}

\displaystyle\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\underset{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant m}{\mathrm{det}}\left(\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\phi_{\beta}(u_{i}-v_{j})&\text{if}\;\;i\leqslant n\\
\psi_{m-i}(-v_{j})&\text{if}\;\;i\geqslant n+1\end{array}\right.\right)&\text{if}\;\;n\leqslant m\\
\underset{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n}{\mathrm{det}}\left(\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\phi_{\beta}(u_{i}-v_{j})&\text{if}\;\;j\leqslant m\\
\psi_{n-j}(u_{i})&\text{if}\;\;j\geqslant m+1\end{array}\right.\right)&\text{if}\;\;n\geqslant m\end{array}\right.

Remark 1.3
.

The previous formulae are indeed a generalization of the formula (
1.12
) with (
1.16
) : they are
\max(n,m)\times\max(n,m)
determinant where the first
\min(n,m)
rows or columns are of the modified Izergin determinant-type and the other are of Vandermonde determinant type.
Moreover, we obtain a new expression for the modified Izergin-determinant.

Homogeneous limit

2.2
Physical interpretation for completely homogeneous finite lattice

To have a global physical interpretation, we consider the statistical weights non-dimensionaless (only in this part)

a(x)=x+c,\qquad b(x)=x\qquad\text{and}\qquad c(x)=c=\exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon}{k_{B}T}\right).

(2.19)

With these statistical weights,

Z_{nm}(0|B|\hat{C})=Z_{nm}^{0}(B|\hat{C})c^{nm}.

(2.20)

Assuming that we are in the canonical ensemble, the total free energy of the completely homogeneous lattice is

\displaystyle F_{nm}^{tot}

\displaystyle=-k_{B}T\ln(Z_{nm}(0|B|\hat{C}))

(2.21)

\displaystyle=nm\epsilon-k_{B}T\left(\min(n,m)\ln(\mathrm{tr}(B\hat{C}))+d\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\ln(\mathrm{tr}(\hat{C}))&\text{if}\;\;n\leqslant m\\
\ln(\mathrm{tr}(B))&\text{if}\;\;n\geqslant m\end{array}\right.\right)

We see bulk and boundary effects in the expression of
F_{nm}^{tot}
because
B
and
\hat{C}
are directly linked to the boundaries. Moreover, the boundary effects are linear in the number of rows and columns, contrary to the bulk effects which are quadratic.

Assuming moreover that the boundaries do not depend on the temperature
T
, we can compute others physical characteristics.
The average energy
\bar{E}^{c}_{nm}
, the fluctuation of the energy
\Delta(E^{c}_{nm})
, the heat capacity at constant volume
C_{V,nm}
and the canonical entropy
S^{c}_{nm}
are respectively

\bar{E}_{nm}^{c}=F_{nm}^{tot}-T\frac{\partial F_{nm}^{tot}}{\partial T}=nm\epsilon,\quad\Delta(E^{c}_{nm})^{2}=-k_{B}T^{3}\frac{\partial^{2}F_{nm}^{tot}}{\partial T^{2}}=0,

(2.24)

C_{V,nm}=-T\frac{\partial F_{nm}^{tot}}{\partial T}=k_{B}T\left(\min(n,m)\ln(\mathrm{tr}(B\hat{C}))+d\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\ln(\mathrm{tr}(\hat{C}))&\text{if}\;\;n\leqslant m\\
\ln(\mathrm{tr}(B))&\text{if}\;\;n\geqslant m\end{array}\right.\right),

(2.25)

S^{c}_{nm}=-\frac{\partial F_{nm}^{tot}}{\partial T}=k_{B}\left(\min(n,m)\ln(\mathrm{tr}(B\hat{C}))+d\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\ln(\mathrm{tr}(\hat{C}))&\text{if}\;\;n\leqslant m\\
\ln(\mathrm{tr}(B))&\text{if}\;\;n\geqslant m\end{array}\right.\right).

(2.26)

In the average energy, only the bulk effects appear. However, in the heat capacity and the canonical entropy, only the boundary effects appear.

2.3
Thermodynamic limit

For a rectangular lattice, we can consider two different thermodynamic limits : in the first part, when
\max(n,m)\rightarrow+\infty
and, in the second part, when
n,m\rightarrow+\infty
where
d=|n-m|
remains constant. We consider that
c,x>0
(as Boltzmann weights) in the following part.

2.3.1
The semi-infinite lattice

This question was treated by Pronko, Pronko and Minin in
[
PP19
,
MP21
]
for the partial domain wall boundary conditions.
We consider the limit
\max(n,m)\rightarrow+\infty
(so
d\rightarrow+\infty
but
\min(n,m)
is constant).
In the formula (
2.17
), we factorize the term
\left(1+\frac{x}{c}\right)^{d+i+j-1}
out of the determinant and we obtain

\displaystyle Z_{nm}(x|B|\hat{C})=

\displaystyle Z_{nm}^{0}(B|\hat{C})\left(1+\frac{x}{c}\right)^{nm}

\displaystyle\times\underset{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant\min(n,m)}{\mathrm{det}}\left(\binom{d+i+j-2}{i-1}\left(1-\beta\left(\frac{x}{x+c}\right)^{d+i+j-1}\right)\right).

(2.27)

Because
x,c>0
,
\frac{x}{x+c}<1
and
\left(\frac{x}{x+c}\right)^{d+i+j-1}=\mathcal{O}\left(\left(\frac{x}{x+c}\right)^{d}\right)
. So

\displaystyle\underset{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant\min(n,m)}{\mathrm{det}}

\displaystyle\left(\binom{d+i+j-2}{i-1}\left(1-\beta\left(\frac{x}{x+c}\right)^{d+i+j-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\underset{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant\min(n,m)}{\mathrm{det}}\left(\binom{d+i+j-2}{i-1}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\left(\frac{x}{x+c}\right)^{d}\right)\right)\right)

(2.28)

\displaystyle=\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\left(\frac{x}{x+c}\right)^{d}\right)\right)^{\min(n,m)}\underset{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant\min(n,m)}{\mathrm{det}}\left(\binom{d+i+j-2}{i-1}\right)

(2.29)

\displaystyle=\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\left(\frac{x}{x+c}\right)^{d}\right)\right)\times 1.

(2.30)

using the fact that
\min(n,m)
is a constant, that
0<\frac{x}{x+c}<1
and the proposition
C.1
. Therefore

Z_{nm}(x|B|\hat{C})=Z_{nm}^{0}(B|\hat{C})\left(1+\frac{x}{c}\right)^{nm}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{x}{x+c}\right)^{d}\right).

(2.31)

This expression allows us to obtain an asymptotically equivalent (once again because
0<\frac{x}{x+c}<1
) :

Z_{nm}(x|B|\hat{C})\underset{\max(n,m)\rightarrow+\infty}{\sim}Z_{nm}^{0}(B|\hat{C})\left(1+\frac{x}{c}\right)^{nm}.

(2.32)

Remark 2.1
.

Asymptotically, it is like all vertices were of type
a
. This Boltzmann weight corresponds to the lower energy level (because
a(x)=b(x)+c(x)
and
b(x),c(x)\geqslant 0
). So, it is like the lattice was at the fundamental energy. We see also the boundary effect.

2.3.2
The infinite lattice

We consider the limit
m,n\rightarrow+\infty
with
d
constant. The first part of the calculation is based on

We consider the expression of

Because

is a Hankël determinant and satisfies the Toda equation (in Hirota form)

\tau_{n+1,m+1}\tau_{n-1,m-1}=\tau_{n,m}\tau^{\prime\prime}_{n,m}-(\tau^{\prime}_{n,m})^{2}

(2.34)

where the prime denotes the
x

When

where

Expressing

and the solutions of this equation have the form

\exp(f(x))=\frac{\alpha}{\sinh(\alpha(x-x_{0}))}.

(2.38)

So we can write the bulk free energy as

-\frac{F(x)}{k_{B}T}=\ln\left(e^{f(x)}\frac{c}{\phi_{1}(x)}\right)=\ln\left(\frac{\alpha}{\sinh(\alpha(x-x_{0}))}\frac{x(x+c)}{c}\right)

(2.39)

Now we go to determine
\alpha
and

with knowns values of

(2.42)

Conclusion

Thanks to the method developed by Foda and Wheeler
[
FW12
]
for the partial domain wall, based on computations using the determinant properties, we have found a new expression (
1.3
) for the partition function of the XXX six vertex model with general boundary conditions, introduced in
[
BPS24
]
.

This new expression has enabled us to compute the homogeneous limit of this model, using the historical technique of the Taylor expansion, introduced in
[
ICK92
]
.
Then, we have computed the thermodynamic limit in two cases. In the case of the infinite lattice (limits in both directions), using some computations of
[
KZJ00
]
, we have found that the free energy of the system and the linked physical characteristics depend on the parameter
\beta
(
1.13
), in other words, on the boundary conditions. However, our asymptotic approximation (
2.35
) may miss important effects for strong boundary fields and other orders should be considered.

These results have to be generalized to the trigonometric six vertex model, related to the XXZ spin chain, and further to the elliptic case, related to the eight vertex model. The key step is to consider the MABA in these cases to find the associated Bethe vectors, that allows us to obtain an algebraic expression of the partition function for general boundary conditions.
The existence of the elliptic and trigonometric source identities
[
MO24
]
, in the theory of multivariable special functions, strengthens our belief that there exists formulae of these partition functions in terms of Bethe vectors for trigonometric and elliptic cases. Indeed, the authors make a link between the Modified Izergin determinant in the rational case and the rational source identity. Moreover, a formula already exists for the elliptic SOS model with DWBC
[
Gal18
,
PRS08
,
Ros08
]
(this model is very close to the 8 vertex ones).

Moreover, we suppose that the physical results in the thermodynamic limit will be more interesting. In itself, the results might depend on the boundary conditions.
Furthermore, for DWBC, the free energy in the thermodynamic limit depends on the phase of the system (see
[
KZJ00
,
ZJ00
]
), phases depending on the anisotropy of the system. In particular, for the anti-ferroelectric phase and at zero temperature limit, it appears an arctic curve that separate the central disordered phase of the four ordered corners. This arctic curve appears also for other boundary conditions (for pDWBC, reflective end … see for example
[
LKRV18
]
for numerical results and graphics).

Finally, we can consider the linear system approach to the computation of the partition function of the six vertex model with reflecting boundary conditions (in the rational, trigonometric and elliptic case), related to the reflection algebra
[
Skl88
,
Tsu98
]
.

Appendix A
Proofs of the new formula for the partition function

A.1
Using the method of Foda and Wheeler

This proof follows the same reasoniong as in
[
FW12
]
. We begin to prove the proposition for the case
n\leqslant m
. We prove the following statement by induction over
k\in[\![0,m-n]\!]

\displaystyle Z_{m-k,m}(\bar{u}|\bar{v}|B|\hat{C})=\frac{\mathrm{tr}(B)^{m-k}\mathrm{tr}(\hat{C})^{m}}{(1-\beta)^{m-k}}

\displaystyle\frac{h(\bar{u},\bar{v})}{g(\bar{u},\bar{v})\Delta(\bar{u})\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{v})}

\displaystyle\times\underset{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant m}{\mathrm{det}}\left(\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\phi_{\beta}(u_{i}-v_{j})&\text{if}\;\;i\leqslant m-k\\
\psi_{m-i}(-v_{j})&\text{if}\;\;i\geqslant m-k+1\end{array}\right.\right).

For
k=0
, there is nothing to prove because the explicit formula is already known with this form (see equations (
1.12
)-(
1.16
)).
We detail now the case
k=1
to see the construction of the determinant. By definition,

Z_{nm}(\bar{u}|\bar{v}|B|\hat{C})=\lim\limits_{u_{m}\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{tr}(B)}\left(\frac{c}{u_{m}}\right)^{m}Z_{mm}(\bar{u}|\bar{v}|B|\hat{C})\right).

(A.3)

and

Z_{mm}(\bar{u}|\bar{v}|B|\hat{C})=\frac{\mathrm{tr}(B)^{m}\mathrm{tr}(\hat{C})^{m}}{(1-\beta)^{m}}\frac{h(\bar{u},\bar{v})}{g(\bar{u},\bar{v})\Delta(\bar{u})\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{v})}\underset{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant m}{\mathrm{det}}\left(\phi_{\beta}(u_{i}-v_{j})\right).

(A.4)

So

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathrm{tr}(B)}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{c}{u_{m}}\right)^{m}Z_{mm}(\bar{u}|\bar{v}|B|\hat{C})=\frac{\mathrm{tr}(B)^{m-1}\mathrm{tr}(\hat{C})^{m}}{(1-\beta)^{m}}\frac{h(\bar{u}_{m},\bar{v})}{g(\bar{u}_{m},\bar{v})\Delta(\bar{u}_{m})\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{v})}

\displaystyle\times\left[\left(\frac{c}{u_{m}}\right)^{m}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{(u_{m}-v_{j}+c)(u_{m}-v_{j})}{c^{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}\frac{c}{u_{m}-u_{i}}\right]\underset{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant m}{\mathrm{det}}\left(\phi_{\beta}(u_{i}-v_{j})\right).

(A.5)

We make the change of variable
u_{m}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
and we inject the factor between brackets in the last row of the determinant (the row
m
). The coefficients
d_{m,\tilde{\jmath}}
, for
\tilde{\jmath}\in[\![1,m]\!]
, become

\displaystyle d_{m,\tilde{\jmath}}

\displaystyle=(\varepsilon c)^{m}\left(\frac{\varepsilon c}{1-v_{\tilde{\jmath}}\varepsilon}-\frac{\beta\varepsilon c}{1-(v_{\tilde{\jmath}}-c)\varepsilon}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{(1-(v_{j}-c)\varepsilon)(1-v_{j}\varepsilon)}{(\varepsilon c)^{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}\frac{\varepsilon c}{1-u_{i}\varepsilon}

(A.6)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{1-v_{\tilde{\jmath}}\varepsilon}-\frac{\beta}{1-(v_{\tilde{\jmath}}-c)\varepsilon}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1-(v_{j}-c)\varepsilon)(1-v_{j}\varepsilon)\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}\frac{1}{1-u_{i}\varepsilon}

(A.7)

So
d_{m,\tilde{\jmath}}\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\rightarrow 0]{}1-\beta
. Factorizing by
1-\beta
, we prove the statement for
k=1
.

We suppose now that the statement is true for one
k\in[\![0,m-n-1]\!]
and we consider the statement for
k+1
. By definition,

\displaystyle Z_{m-(k+1),m}(\bar{u}|\bar{v}|B|\hat{C})

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{u_{m},\dots,u_{m-k}\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{tr}(B)^{m-n}}\prod_{i=n+1}^{m}\left(\frac{c}{u_{i}}\right)^{m}Z_{mm}(\bar{u}|\bar{v}|B|\hat{C})\right)

(A.8)

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{u_{m-k}\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{tr}(B)}\left(\frac{c}{u_{m-k}}\right)^{m}Z_{m-k,m}(\bar{u}|\bar{v}|B|\hat{C})\right).

(A.9)

According the induction statement,

\displaystyle Z_{m-k,m}(\bar{u}|\bar{v}|B|\hat{C})=\frac{\mathrm{tr}(B)^{m-k}\mathrm{tr}(\hat{C})^{m}}{(1-\beta)^{m-k}}

\displaystyle\frac{h(\bar{u},\bar{v})}{g(\bar{u},\bar{v})\Delta(\bar{u})\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{v})}

\displaystyle\times\underset{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant m}{\mathrm{det}}\left(\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\phi_{\beta}(u_{i}-v_{j})&\text{if}\;\;i\leqslant m-k\\
\psi_{m-i}(-v_{j})&\text{if}\;\;i\geqslant m-k+1\end{array}\right.\right).

So

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathrm{tr}(B)}\left(\frac{c}{u_{m-k}}\right)^{m}

\displaystyle Z_{m-k,m}(\bar{u}|\bar{v}|B|\hat{C})=\frac{\mathrm{tr}(B)^{m-(k+1)}\mathrm{tr}(\hat{C})^{m}}{(1-\beta)^{m-k}}\frac{h(\bar{u}_{m-k},\bar{v})}{g(\bar{u}_{m-k},\bar{v})\Delta(\bar{u}_{m-k})\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{v})}

\displaystyle\times\left[\left(\frac{c}{u_{m-k}}\right)^{m}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{(u_{m-k}-v_{j}+c)(u_{m-k}-v_{j})}{c^{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{m-k-1}\frac{c}{u_{m-k}-u_{i}}\right]

\displaystyle\times\underset{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant m}{\mathrm{det}}\left(\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\phi_{\beta}(u_{i}-v_{j})&\text{if}\;\;i\leqslant m-k\\
\psi_{m-i}(-v_{j})&\text{if}\;\;i\geqslant m-k+1\end{array}\right.\right).

We make the change of variable
u_{m-k}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
and we inject the factor between brackets in the row
m-k
of the determinant. The coefficients
d_{m-k,\tilde{\jmath}}
, for
\tilde{\jmath}\in[\![1,m]\!]
, become

\displaystyle d_{m-k,\tilde{\jmath}}

\displaystyle=(\varepsilon c)^{m}\left(\frac{\varepsilon c}{1-v_{\tilde{\jmath}}\varepsilon}-\frac{\beta\varepsilon c}{1-(v_{\tilde{\jmath}}-c)\varepsilon}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{(1-v_{j}\varepsilon)(1-(v_{j}-c)\varepsilon)}{(\varepsilon c)^{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{m-k-1}\frac{\varepsilon c}{1-u_{i}\varepsilon}

(A.14)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\varepsilon c)^{k}}\left(\frac{1}{1-v_{\tilde{\jmath}}\varepsilon}-\frac{\beta}{1-(v_{\tilde{\jmath}}-c)\varepsilon}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1-v_{j}\varepsilon)(1-(v_{j}-c)\varepsilon)\prod_{i=1}^{m-k-1}\frac{1}{1-u_{i}\varepsilon}.

(A.15)

We use the elementary
e_{l}
and complete symmetric functions
h_{l}
defined in the subsection
D.1
with their generating function (
D.3
).
Therefore

\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1-v_{j}\varepsilon)(1-(v_{j}-c)\varepsilon)=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}e_{l}\left(\frac{\bar{v}}{c}\bigcup\left(\frac{\bar{v}}{c}-1\right)\right)(\varepsilon c)^{l},

(A.16)

\prod_{i=1}^{m-k-1}(1-u_{i}\varepsilon)^{-1}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}h_{l}\left(\frac{\bar{u}_{m-k}}{c}\right)(\varepsilon c)^{l},

(A.17)

\frac{1}{1-v_{\tilde{\jmath}}\varepsilon}-\frac{\beta}{1-(v_{\tilde{\jmath}}-c)\varepsilon}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\left(\left(\frac{v_{\tilde{\jmath}}}{c}\right)^{l}-\beta\left(\frac{v_{\tilde{\jmath}}}{c}-1\right)^{l}\right)(\varepsilon c)^{l}.

(A.18)

So

\displaystyle d_{m-k,\tilde{\jmath}}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{l_{1}+l_{2}+l_{3}=l}f_{l_{1},l_{2}}(\bar{u}_{m-k}|\bar{v})\left(\left(\frac{v_{\tilde{\jmath}}}{c}\right)^{l_{3}}-\beta\left(\frac{v_{\tilde{\jmath}}}{c}-1\right)^{l_{3}}\right)\right)(\varepsilon c)^{l-k}

(A.19)

with

(A.20)

However,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{v_{\tilde{\jmath}}}{c}\right)^{l_{3}}-\beta\left(\frac{v_{\tilde{\jmath}}}{c}-1\right)^{l_{3}}=(1-\beta)\left(\frac{v_{\tilde{\jmath}}}{c}\right)^{l_{3}}-\beta\sum_{l_{4}=0}^{l_{3}-1}\binom{l_{3}}{l_{4}}\left(\frac{v_{\tilde{\jmath}}}{c}\right)^{l_{4}}(-1)^{l_{3}-l_{4}}.

(A.21)

So the terms for

in

, only the term
(l_{1}=0,l_{2}=0,l_{3}=k)
contribute to the determinant for the same reasons. And for

, the terms tend to
0
when
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. We can therefore write the row
m-k
with the term
(1-\beta)\left(\frac{v_{\tilde{\jmath}}}{c}\right)^{k}
for all
\tilde{\jmath}\in[\![1,m]\!]
when
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. Factorizing

outside the determinant, we obtain

\displaystyle Z_{m-(k+1),m}(\bar{u}|\bar{v}|B|\hat{C})=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{tr}(B)^{m-(k+1)}\mathrm{tr}(\hat{C})^{m}}{(1-\beta)^{m-(k+1)}}\frac{h(\bar{u},\bar{v})}{g(\bar{u},\bar{v})\Delta(\bar{u})\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{v})}

\displaystyle\times\underset{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant m}{\mathrm{det}}\left(\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\phi_{\beta}(u_{i}-v_{j})&\text{if}\;\;i\leqslant m-(k+1)\\
\psi_{m-i}(-v_{j})&\text{if}\;\;i\geqslant m-k\end{array}\right.\right)

which proves the statement for

and finishes the induction proof.

Applying the formula for

, we obtain

\displaystyle Z_{n,m}(\bar{u}|\bar{v}|B|\hat{C})=\frac{\mathrm{tr}(B)^{n}\mathrm{tr}(\hat{C})^{m}}{(1-\beta)^{n}}

\displaystyle\frac{h(\bar{u},\bar{v})}{g(\bar{u},\bar{v})\Delta(\bar{u})\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{v})}

\displaystyle\times\underset{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant m}{\mathrm{det}}\left(\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\phi_{\beta}(u_{i}-v_{j})&\text{if}\;\;i\leqslant n\\
\psi_{m-i}(-v_{j})&\text{if}\;\;i\geqslant n+1\end{array}\right.\right)

To prove the formula for

,

,
u_{i}\leftrightarrow-v_{j}
and

. Then, the proof follows the same reasoning.

A.2
An alternative proof

Now we will prove that each formula of (
1.3
) is equal to one of the two known formulae ((
1.12
) with (
1.14
) or (
1.15
)). Contrary to the previous proof, this method is original.

To do this, as in
[
BPS24
]
, we consider that
g(\bar{u},\bar{v})\Delta(\bar{u})\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{v})
is a determinant of some
“Cauchy”
matrix and we multiply the matrix in the determinant by the inverse of the
“Cauchy”
matrix. Then, we will transform the obtained matrix using the properties of the determinant.
We denote the Cauchy matrix in quotation marks because, for the real Cauchy matrix, we have the same number of
u_{i}
and
v_{j}
. Here, it is not necessary the case. First of all, we have to define this partial Cauchy matrix and compute its inverse. We name it as partial in reference to the partial domain wall boundary conditions partition function.

A.2.1
The partial Cauchy matrix and its inverse

To define the partial Cauchy matrix, we use the same form as the one of the partition function because the real Cauchy matrix corresponds to the matrix inside the determinant for
\beta=0
(in the case of a square lattice).

Definition A.1
.

We define the partial Cauchy matrix
\mathcal{C}_{nm}(\bar{u}|\bar{v})
by

\mathcal{C}_{nm}(\bar{u}|\bar{v})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\left(\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}g(u_{i}-v_{j})&\text{if}\;i\leqslant n\\
\psi_{m-i}(-v_{j})&\text{if}\;i\geqslant n+1\end{array}\right.\right)_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant m}&\text{for}\;n\leqslant m\\
\left(\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}g(u_{i}-v_{j})&\text{if}\;j\leqslant m\\
\psi_{n-j}(u_{i})&\text{if}\;j\geqslant m+1\end{array}\right.\right)_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n}&\text{for}\;n\geqslant m\end{array}\right.

(A.26)

Proposition A.1
.

The determinant of the partial Cauchy matrix
\mathcal{C}_{nm}(\bar{u}|\bar{v})
has the same form as the one of the real Cauchy matrix

\mathrm{det}\left(\mathcal{C}_{nm}(\bar{u}|\bar{v})\right)=g(\bar{u},\bar{v})\Delta(\bar{u})\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{v}).

(A.27)

Proof.

The proof has the construction as the one of the formula
1.3
. We will focus on the case
n\leqslant m
. We obtain the other case exchanging
n\leftrightarrow m
,
u_{i}\leftrightarrow-v_{j}
and
i\leftrightarrow j
.

We will prove the following statement by induction over
k\in[\![0,m-n]\!]

\mathrm{det}\left(\mathcal{C}_{m-k,m}(\bar{u}|\bar{v})\right)=g(\bar{u},\bar{v})\Delta(\bar{u})\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{v})

(A.28)

For
k=0
, the statement is automatically true because the partial Cauchy matrix is the real Cauchy matrix and its determinant is well known : it is
g(\bar{u},\bar{v})\Delta(\bar{u})\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{v})
. The interested reader can find a proof, for example, in the french book
[
Gou21
]
.

We suppose now that the statement is true for one
k\in[\![0,m-n-1]\!]
and we want to prove the statement for
k+1
. So we have to eliminate the variable
u_{m-k}
: we will do this tending it toward infinity.
So, according to the induction statement,

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{g(\bar{u},\bar{v})\Delta(\bar{u})\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{v})}\mathrm{det}\left(\mathcal{C}_{m-k,m}(\bar{u}|\bar{v})\right)

(A.29)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{g(\bar{u}_{m-k},\bar{v})\Delta(\bar{u}_{m-k})\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{v})}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{u_{m-k}-v_{j}}{c}\prod_{i=1}^{m-k-1}\frac{c}{u_{m-k}-u_{i}}\right]\mathrm{det}\left(\mathcal{C}_{m-k,m}(\bar{u}|\bar{v})\right).

(A.30)

We make the change of variable

and we inject the factor between brackets in the row
m-k
of the determinant. The coefficients

, for
\tilde{\jmath}\in[\![1,m]\!]

(A.31)

Using again

and

and their generating functions, we obtain

(A.32)

Doing the same reasoning that in the previous proof, we can write the row
m-k
with the term
\left(\frac{v_{\tilde{\jmath}}}{c}\right)^{k}
for all
\tilde{\jmath}\in[\![1,m]\!]
when

. So we obtain

1=\frac{1}{g(\bar{u}_{m-k},\bar{v})\Delta(\bar{u}_{m-k})\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{v})}\mathrm{det}\left(\mathcal{C}_{m-(k+1),m}(\bar{u}|\bar{v})\right),

(A.33)

because

is independent of
u_{m-k}
, which proves the statement for
k+1
.

Finally, by induction over
k\in[\![0,m-n]\!]
,

\mathrm{det}\left(\mathcal{C}_{m-k,m}(\bar{u}|\bar{v})\right)=g(\bar{u},\bar{v})\Delta(\bar{u})\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{v}).

(A.34)

For

, we obtain

\mathrm{det}\left(\mathcal{C}_{nm}(\bar{u}|\bar{v})\right)=g(\bar{u},\bar{v})\Delta(\bar{u})\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{v}).

(A.35)

∎

Proposition A.2
.

The inverse matrix of the partial Cauchy matrix

\mathcal{C}_{nm}(\bar{u}|\bar{v})^{-1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\left(\begin{matrix}\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}g(u_{j},v_{i})\frac{g(\bar{u}_{j},u_{j})g(v_{i},\bar{v}_{i})}{g(u_{j},\bar{v})g(\bar{u},v_{i})}&\text{if}\;j\leqslant n\\

where

Definition C.1

Proof.

For

,
D_{n-1}^{(k,l)}(d)
satisfies the following inductive relation

D_{n-1}^{(k,l)}(d)=D_{n-2}^{(k-1,l-1)}(d+1)+D_{n-2}^{(k-1,l)}(d+1).

(C.10)

Proof.

If
k>n
,
l>n
or
l<1
, each term of the previous relation is equal to
0
because, in the first case, we have also
k-1>n-1
, in the second case, we have also
l-1>n-1
and
l>n-1
and in the last case, we have also
l-1<1
.
The inductive relation is therefore satisfied.

Now, we suppose that
1<k\leqslant n
. First, we treat the case
n=2
. We have, for all
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
D_{1}^{(2,1)}(d)=D_{1}^{(2,2)}(d)=1
and, by convention,
D_{0}^{(1,1)}(d)=1
. And, according to the extended definition
C.3
,
D_{0}^{(1,2)}(d)=D_{0}^{(1,0)}(d)=0
. So the inductive relation is automatically satisfied.

Now, we consider the case
n>2
. To make the computations, we suppose also that
1<l<n
. We will consider the cases
l=1
and
l=n
at the end of the proof.
To have this relation, we follow the same reasoning as in the proof of the proposition
C.1
. We replace each column
C_{j}
for
j\in[\![2,n-1]\!]
by
C_{j}-C_{j-1}
and we obtain a determinant with the same form as (
C.5
) : each column
C_{j}
, for
j\in[\![2,n-1]\!]
and
\neq l
, has now the form

\left(\begin{matrix}0\\
\binom{d+1+j-1}{1}-\binom{d+1+j-1-1}{1}\\
\vdots\\
\binom{d+k-2+j-1}{k-2}-\binom{d+k-2+j-1-1}{k-2}\\
\binom{d+k+j-1}{k}-\binom{d+k+j-1-1}{k}\\
\vdots\\
\binom{d+n-1+j-1}{n-1}-\binom{d+n-1+j-1-1}{n-1}\end{matrix}\right)\;\;\;\text{or}\;\;\;\left(\begin{matrix}0\\
\binom{d+1+j}{1}-\binom{d+1+j-1}{1}\\
\vdots\\
\binom{d+k-2+j}{k-2}-\binom{d+k-2+j-1}{k-2}\\
\binom{d+k+j}{k}-\binom{d+k+j-1}{k}\\
\vdots\\
\binom{d+n-1+j}{n-1}-\binom{d+n-1+j-1}{n-1}\end{matrix}\right),

(C.11)

depending on wether
j<l
or
j>l
, and
C_{l}
has the form

\left(\begin{matrix}0\\
\binom{d+1+l}{1}-\binom{d+1+l-1-1}{1}\\
\vdots\\
\binom{d+k-2+l}{k-2}-\binom{d+k-2+l-1-1}{k-2}\\
\binom{d+k+l}{k}-\binom{d+k+l-1-1}{k}\\
\vdots\\
\binom{d+n-1+l}{n-1}-\binom{d+n-1+l-1-1}{n-1}\end{matrix}\right).

(C.12)

As in the proof of the proposition
C.1
, we use the Pascal’s formula
\binom{p+1}{q+1}-\binom{p}{q+1}=\binom{p}{q}
to simplify the expression of the column
C_{j}
,
j\neq l
and
1
, and we obtain

\left(\begin{matrix}0\\
\binom{d+0+j-1}{0}\\
\vdots\\
\binom{d+k-3+j-1}{k-3}\\
\binom{d+k-1+j-1}{k-1}\\
\vdots\\
\binom{d+n-2+j-1}{n-2}\end{matrix}\right)\;\;\;\text{or}\;\;\;\left(\begin{matrix}0\\
\binom{d+0+j}{0}\\
\vdots\\
\binom{d+k-3+j}{k-3}\\
\binom{d+k-1+j}{k-1}\\
\vdots\\
\binom{d+n-2+j}{n-2}\end{matrix}\right).

(C.13)

However, for
j=l
, we have to use the formula
\binom{p+2}{q+1}-\binom{p}{q+1}=\binom{p+1}{q}+\binom{p}{q}
, also derived from the Pascal’s one. We obtain therefore

\left(\begin{matrix}0\\
\binom{d+0+l}{0}+\binom{d+0+l-1}{0}\\
\vdots\\
\binom{d+k-3+l}{k-3}+\binom{d+k-3+l-1}{k-3}\\
\binom{d+k-1+l}{k-1}+\binom{d+k-1+l-1}{k-1}\\
\vdots\\
\binom{d+n-2+l}{n-2}+\binom{d+n-2+l-1}{n-2}\end{matrix}\right).

(C.14)

Because the first row contains one
1
at the first position and
0
at the others positions, we expand
D_{n-1}^{(k,l)}(d)
along the first row and we obtain a determinant of size
n-2
whose columns
C_{j}
,
j\in[\![1,n-2]\!]
, are, respectively for
j<l-1,j=l-1
and
j>l-1
,

\left(\begin{matrix}\binom{d+1+0+j-1}{0}\\
\vdots\\
\binom{d+1+k-2+j-2}{k-3}\\
\binom{d+1+k-1+j-1}{k-1}\\
\vdots\\
\binom{d+1+n-2+j-1}{n-2}\end{matrix}\right),\;\left(\begin{matrix}\binom{d+1+0+l-1}{0}+\binom{d+1+0+l-1-1}{0}\\
\vdots\\
\binom{d+1+k-3+l-1}{k-3}+\binom{d+1+k-3+l-1-1}{k-3}\\
\binom{d+1+k-1+l-1}{k-1}+\binom{d+1+k-1+l-1-1}{k-1}\\
\vdots\\
\binom{d+1+n-2+l-1}{n-2}+\binom{d+1+n-2+l-1-1}{n-2}\end{matrix}\right),\;\left(\begin{matrix}\binom{d+1+0+j}{0}\\
\vdots\\
\binom{d+1+k-3+j}{k-3}\\
\binom{d+1+k-1+j}{k-1}\\
\vdots\\
\binom{d+1+n-2+j}{n-2}\end{matrix}\right).

(C.15)

With the rewriting of the columns, we remark that
d
has been increased by

and the cut between rows takes place now between rows
k-2
and
k-1
. Moreover, we can separate this new determinant in two ones, because of the sum in

D_{n-1}^{(k,l)}(d)=D_{n-2}^{(k-1,l-1)}(d+1)+D_{n-2}^{(k-1,l)}(d+1).

(C.16)

Finally, we consider now the cases
l=1
and

. Indeed, for both cases, there is no cut between columns and we only have to use the Pascal’s formula
\binom{p+1}{q+1}-\binom{p}{q+1}=\binom{p}{q}
to simplify the expression of
D_{n-1}^{(k,l)}(d)
. So we obtain

D_{n-1}^{(k,1)}(d)=D_{n-2}^{(k-1,1)}(d+1)\;\;\text{and}\;\;D_{n-1}^{(k,n)}(d)=D_{n-2}^{(k-1,n-1)}(d+1).

(C.17)

And, according to the extended definition
C.3
,
D_{n-2}^{(k-1,0)}(d+1)=D_{n-2}^{(k-1,n)}(d+1)=0
. So the inductive relation is satisfied again, which concludes the proof.
∎

Proposition C.3
.

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
k\in[\![1,n]\!]
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
,

D_{n-1}^{(k,l)}(d)=\sum_{p=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{p}D_{n-k}^{(1,l-p)}(d+k-1).

(C.18)

Remarks C.2
.

We consider only the case
k\leqslant n
. Else, we will make appear some determinant of negative size, which is not well defined.

This relation will give us an explicit formula for
D_{n-1}^{(k,l)}(d)
when we will have
D_{n-1}^{(1,l)}(d)
.

Proof.

Let be
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. For
n=1
, the equality is automatically satisfied because the sum contains only one term. Now, for
n>1
, we will prove by induction over
k\in[\![1,n]\!]
the following statement

\forall d\in\mathbb{N},l\in\mathbb{Z},\;\;D_{n-1}^{(k,l)}(d)=\sum_{p=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{p}D_{n-k}^{(1,l-p)}(d+k-1).

(C.19)

For
k=1
, the equality is automatically satisfied and the base case is proved.
Now, we suppose that the statement is true for one
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and we consider
D_{n-1}^{(k+1,l)}(d)
. By the inductive relation (
C.10
),

D_{n-1}^{(k+1,l)}(d)=D_{n-2}^{(k,l-1)}(d+1)+D_{n-2}^{(k,l)}(d+1)

(C.20)

and using the inductive statement twice,

D_{n-1}^{(k+1,l)}(d)=\sum_{p=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{p}D_{n-1-k}^{(1,l-1-p)}(d+k-1)+\sum_{p=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{p}D_{n-1-k}^{(1,l-p)}(d+k-1).

(C.21)

By changing the index of the first sum (
p^{\prime}=p+1
), we can rewrite the previous expression as

\displaystyle D_{n-1}^{(k+1,l)}(d)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=1}^{k}\binom{k-1}{p-1}D_{n-1-k}^{(1,l-p)}(d+k-1)+\sum_{p=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{p}D_{n-1-k}^{(1,l-p)}(d+k-1)

(C.22)

\displaystyle=D_{n-1-k}^{(1,l-k)}(d+k-1)+\sum_{p=1}^{k-1}\left[\binom{k-1}{p-1}+\binom{k-1}{p}\right]D_{n-1-k}^{(1,l-p)}(d+k-1)

\displaystyle+D_{n-1-k}^{(1,l)}(d+k-1)

(C.23)

\displaystyle=D_{n-1-k}^{(1,l-k)}(d+k-1)+\sum_{p=1}^{k-1}\binom{k}{p}D_{n-1-k}^{(1,l-p)}(d+k-1)+D_{n-1-k}^{(1,l)}(d+k-1)

(C.24)

using the Pascal’s formula. We obtain finally the inductive statement for
k+1

D_{n-1}^{(k+1,l)}(d)=\sum_{p=0}^{k}\binom{k}{p}D_{n-1-k}^{(1,l-p)}(d+k-1)

(C.25)

which concludes the proof.
∎

Proposition C.4
.

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
k,l\in[\![1,n]\!]
,
D_{n-1}^{(k,l)}(d)
is defined uniquely with the equality

\forall k,j\in[\![1,n]\!],\;\;\sum_{l=1}^{n}(-1)^{k+l}D_{n-1}^{(k,l)}(d)\binom{d+l-1+j-1}{j-1}=\delta_{kj}.

(C.26)

Proof.

Because
D_{n-1}^{(k,l)}(d)
is the
(k,l)
minor of
M_{n}(d)
,
(-1)^{k+l}D_{n-1}^{(k,l)}(d)
is one coefficient of the comatrix of
M_{n}(d)
,
\mathrm{Com}(M_{n}(d))
.
Moreover, we know that

\displaystyle(M_{n}(d)^{T})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathrm{det}(M_{n}(d)^{T})}(\mathrm{Com}(M_{n}(d)^{T}))^{T}=\frac{1}{D_{n}(d)}\mathrm{Com}(M_{n}(d))=\mathrm{Com}(M_{n}(d)).

(C.27)

So
\mathrm{Com}(M_{n}(d))
can be defined uniquely as the inverse matrix of
M_{n}(d)^{T}
and their coefficient can be defined uniquely also with the relations

\forall k,j\in[\![1,n]\!],\;\;\sum_{l=1}^{n}(-1)^{k+l}D_{n-1}^{(k,l)}(d)\binom{d+l-1+j-1}{j-1}=\delta_{kj}.

(C.28)

∎

Proposition C.5
.

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
,
D_{n-1}^{(k,l)}(d)
is given by

•

D_{n-1}^{(1,l)}(d)=\frac{\mathds{1}_{1\leqslant l\leqslant n}}{(l-1)!(n-l)!}\prod_{1\leqslant q\leqslant n,\,q\neq l}(d+q)
,

•

D_{0}^{(k,l)}(d)=\mathds{1}_{k=l=1}

•

and, for
n>1
,
D_{n-1}^{(k,l)}(d)=D_{n-2}^{(k-1,l-1)}(d+1)+D_{n-2}^{(k-1,l)}(d+1)

where
\mathds{1}
is the usual indicator function.

Proof.

We will prove by induction over
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, using the previous expressions, the following statement

\forall d\in\mathbb{N},\,n\in\mathbb{N}^{*},\,j\in[\![1,n]\!],\;\;\;\sum_{l=1}^{n}(-1)^{k+l}D_{n-1}^{(k,l)}(d)\binom{d+l-1+j-1}{j-1}=\delta_{kj}.

(C.29)

For
k=1
, let be
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. We have to prove

\sum_{l=1}^{n}\left(\frac{(-1)^{1+l}}{(l-1)!(n-l)!}\underset{q\neq l}{\prod_{q=1}^{n}}(d+q)\right)=1

(C.30)

and, if
n>1
,

\forall j\in[\![2,n]\!],\;\;\sum_{l=1}^{n}\left(\frac{(-1)^{1+l}}{(l-1)!(n-l)!}\underset{q\neq l}{\prod_{q=1}^{n}}(d+q)\binom{d+l-1+j-1}{j-1}\right)=0.

(C.31)

For the first equation, it is equivalent to prove that

\prod_{q=1}^{n}\frac{1}{d+q}=\sum_{l=1}^{n}\left(\frac{(-1)^{1+l}}{(l-1)!(n-l)!}\frac{1}{d+l}\right)

(C.32)

which corresponds to the partial fraction expansion of the rational fraction
\prod_{q=1}^{n}\frac{1}{d+q}
. We will compute this expansion. According to the theory, the partial fraction expansion of this rational fraction has the form

\prod_{q=1}^{n}\frac{1}{d+q}=\sum_{l=1}^{n}\frac{\gamma_{l}}{d+l}

(C.33)

where

\gamma_{l}=\lim\limits_{d\rightarrow-l}\underset{q\neq l}{\prod_{q=1}^{n}}\frac{1}{d+q}=\prod_{q=1}^{l-1}\frac{-1}{l-q}\prod_{q=l+1}^{n}\frac{1}{q-l}=\frac{(-1)^{l-1}}{(l-1)!(n-l)!}

(C.34)

which proves the first equation because
(-1)^{l-1}=(-1)^{l+1}
. Now, for the second equation (and
n>1
), it is equivalent to prove that

\forall j\in[\![2,n]\!],\;\;\sum_{l=1}^{n}\left(\frac{(-1)^{1+l}}{(l-1)!(n-l)!}\frac{1}{d+l}\binom{d+l-1+j-1}{j-1}\right)=0

(C.35)

or

\forall j\in[\![2,n]\!],\;\;\sum_{l=1}^{n}\left(\frac{(-1)^{1+l}}{(l-1)!(n-l)!}\prod_{q=1}^{j-2}(d+l+q)\right)=0

(C.36)

developing the binomial coefficient. Multiplying this sum by
(n-1)!
and making the index change
l^{\prime}=l-1
, it is equivalent to prove that

\forall j\in[\![2,n]\!],\;\;\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\left((-1)^{l}\binom{n-1}{l}\prod_{q=1}^{j-2}(d+l+1+q)\right)=0.

(C.37)

However, for
j\in[\![2,n]\!]
,

\prod_{q=1}^{j-2}(d+l+1+q)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x^{j-2}}\left.\left(x^{d+l+j-1}\right)\right|_{x=1}

(C.38)

so

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\left((-1)^{l}\binom{n-1}{l}\prod_{q=1}^{j-2}(d+l+1+q)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x^{j-2}}\left.\left(x^{d+j-1}\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{l}(-x)^{l}\right)\right|_{x=1}

(C.39)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x^{j-2}}\left.\left(x^{d+j-1}(1-x)^{n-1}\right)\right|_{x=1}.

(C.40)

1
is a order
n-1
root of the polynom
X^{d+j-1}(1-X)^{n-1}
. But
0\leqslant j-2<n-1
because
j\in[\![2,n]\!]
. So
1
is still a root of its
(j-2)^{\text{th}}
derivative and the second equation is true. The base case is now proved.

We consider now that the statement is true for one
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Let be
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. If
n=1
, because the case
k=1
has been proved just before, the equality is automatically verified for
k+1
, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. We suppose now that
n>1
and we consider the sum for
k+1
. For
j\in[\![1,n]\!]
, using the inductive relation,

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{n}(-1)^{k+1+l}

\displaystyle D_{n-1}^{(k+1,l)}(d)\binom{d+l-1+j-1}{j-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{n}(-1)^{k+1+l}\left(D_{n-2}^{(k,l-1)}(d+1)+D_{n-2}^{(k,l)}(d+1)\right)\binom{d+l-1+j-1}{j-1}

(C.41)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=2}^{n}(-1)^{k+1+l}D_{n-2}^{(k,l-1)}(d+1)\binom{d+l-1+j-1}{j-1}

\displaystyle+\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{k+1+l}D_{n-2}^{(k,l)}(d+1)\binom{d+l-1+j-1}{j-1}

(C.42)

because for all
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
D_{n-2}^{(i,n)}(d)=D_{n-2}^{(i,0)}(d)=0
. So, making a change of index in the first sum, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{n}(-1)^{k+1+l}

\displaystyle D_{n-1}^{(k+1,l)}(d)\binom{d+l-1+j-1}{j-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{k+l}D_{n-2}^{(k,l)}(d+1)\left[\binom{d+l+j-1}{j-1}-\binom{d+l-1+j-1}{j-1}\right].

(C.43)

If
j=1
, this sum is therefore equal to
0
, that is ww want to prove because
k+1>1
. Else,

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{n}(-1)^{k+1+l}

\displaystyle D_{n-1}^{(k+1,l)}(d)\binom{d+l-1+j-1}{j-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{k+l}D_{n-2}^{(k,l)}(d+1)\binom{d+l+j-2}{j-2}

(C.44)

using the Pascal’s formula. Remarking that
\binom{d+l+j-2}{j-2}=\binom{d+1+l-1+j-2}{j-2}
, we obtain, using the inductive statement for
k
,

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{n}

\displaystyle(-1)^{k+1+l}D_{n-1}^{(k+1,l)}(d)\binom{d+l-1+j-1}{j-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{k+l}D_{n-2}^{(k,l)}(d+1)\binom{d+1+l-1+j-2}{j-2}=\delta_{k,j-1}=\delta_{k+1,j}

(C.45)

which concludes the proof.
∎

Now, we use all the previous propositions to obtain an explicit expression to
D_{n-1}^{(k,l)}(d)
.

Proposition C.6
.

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
,

D_{n-1}^{(k,l)}(d)=\frac{\mathds{1}_{1\leqslant k,l\leqslant n}}{(n-k)!}\prod_{q=k}^{n}(d+q)\sum_{p=\mathrm{max}(l,k)}^{\mathrm{min}(k+l-1,n)}\binom{k-1}{p-l}\binom{n-k}{p-k}\frac{1}{d+p}.

(C.46)

Proof.

We consider directly
k,l\in[\![1,n]\!]
. Else, we already know that
D_{n-1}^{(k,l)}(d)=0
. Using the equation (
C.18
) and the expression of
D_{n-1}^{(1,l)}(d)
, we obtain

D_{n-1}^{(k,l)}(d)=\sum_{p=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{p}\frac{\mathds{1}_{1\leqslant l-p\leqslant n-k+1}}{(l-p-1)!(n-k+1-l+p)!}\underset{q\neq l-p}{\prod_{q=1}^{n-k+1}}(d+k-1+q).

(C.47)

Making successively changes of indices
q^{\prime}=q+k-1
,
p^{\prime}=k-1-p
and then
p^{\prime}=l+p
(with a rewriting before), we obtain

\displaystyle D_{n-1}^{(k,l)}(d)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{p}\frac{\mathds{1}_{1\leqslant l-p\leqslant n-k+1}}{(l-p-1)!(n-k+1-l+p)!}\underset{q\neq l-p+k-1}{\prod_{q=k}^{n}}(d+q)

(C.48)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{p}\frac{\mathds{1}_{1\leqslant l+p-k+1\leqslant n-k+1}}{(l+p-k)!(n-l-p)!}\underset{q\neq l+p}{\prod_{q=k}^{n}}(d+q)

(C.49)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{p}\frac{\mathds{1}_{k-l\leqslant p\leqslant n-l}}{(p-(k-l))!(n-l-p)!}\underset{q\neq l+p}{\prod_{q=k}^{n}}(d+q)

(C.50)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=l}^{k+l-1}\binom{k-1}{p-l}\frac{\mathds{1}_{k\leqslant p\leqslant n}}{(p-k)!(n-p)!}\underset{q\neq p}{\prod_{q=k}^{n}}(d+q).

(C.51)

Injecting the bounds of the indicator function with its of the sum and rewriting again the expression, we obtain the wanted expression.
∎

D.3
Complex analysis

Proposition D.3
.

We consider
F
a complex rational function
F(z)=P(z)/Q(z)
where
P
and
Q
are complex polynomials with
\mathrm{deg}\,Q>\mathrm{deg}\,P+1
. We denote
a_{1},\dots,a_{q}
the poles of
F
(in other words the zeros of
Q
). We assume that there are only one order pole. Then

\sum_{k=1}^{q}\mathrm{Res}(F,a_{k})=0.

(D.8)

Proof.

We take
R>\mathrm{max}|a_{k}|
. There exists an open set
U
of
\mathbb{C}
that contains the circle of radius
R
and center
z=0
but not the poles of
F
.
F
is continuous on
U
so, according to the theorem 2.3 of the section III §2 of
[
Lan99
]
,

\left|\oint_{|z|=R}F(z)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}z\right|\leqslant\underset{|z|=R}{\text{max}}|F(z)|\times 2\pi R.

(D.9)

We can factorize
P
and
Q
in irreducible elements

P(z)=A\prod_{j=1}^{p}(z-b_{j})\;\;\text{with }A\neq 0\;\;\text{and}\;\;Q(z)=\prod_{k=1}^{q}(z-a_{k}).

(D.10)

and using the triangle inequality, for
|z|=R
we have

|z-b_{j}|\leqslant|z|+|b_{j}|=R+|b_{j}|\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;|z-a_{k}|\geqslant||z|-|a_{k}||=R-|a_{k}|.

(D.11)

So

\left|\oint_{|z|=R}F(z)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}z\right|\leqslant|A|\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{p}(R+|b_{j}|)}{\prod_{k=1}^{q}(R-|a_{k}|)}\times 2\pi R.

(D.12)

By hypothesis,
q>p+1
so
q-p-1>0
and the right term goes to zero when
R
goes to infinity.
In addition, by the residue theorem,

\oint_{|z|=R}F(z)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}z=2i\pi\sum_{k=1}^{q}\mathrm{Res}(F,a_{k})

(D.13)

and the sum is independent of

as long as
R>\mathrm{max}|a_{k}|
. So
\sum_{k=1}^{q}\mathrm{Res}(F,a_{k})=0
.

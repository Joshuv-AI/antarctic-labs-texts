Ancillary ligand modulation in fluorinated β-diketonate Dy(iii) complexes: spectroscopic and photophysical investigations
Authors: Sonia Redhu, Devender Singh, Anuj Dalal, Shri Bhagwan, Sangeeta Yadav, Sumit Kumar, Rajender Singh Malik, Parvin Kumar, Jayant Sindhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A series of semiconducting and luminescent dysprosium(iii) complexes (DB1–DB4) were synthesized using 4,4,4-trifluoro-1-phenyl-1,3-butanedione as a fluorinated β-diketone sensitizer along with bidentate ancillary ligands (BPy, B1, B2 and B3). FT-IR and 1H NMR spectroscopic analyses support effective ligand coordination and the formation of the proposed Dy(iii) complexes. Thermogravimetric analysis reveals good thermal stability with high decomposition temperatures, demonstrating suitability for device processing and operational durability. Optical and electrochemical investigations were performed to establish structure–property correlations. The HOMO–LUMO energy gaps of DB1–DB4 were found in the range of 2.88–3.14 eV, consistent with electrochemical data and indicative of wide-band-gap semiconducting behavior. Photoluminescence studies exhibit a dominant emission band around 575 nm, assigned to the characteristic 4F9/2 → 6H13/2 transition of Dy(iii), producing intense yellow emission. The combined contribution of ligand-centered blue emission and Dy(iii)-centered yellow emission results in near-white light output, as confirmed by CIE chromaticity coordinates. The integration of semiconducting electronic structure, efficient ligand to metal energy transfer, near-white emission and robust thermal stability highlights the potential of these Dy(iii) complexes as promising materials for optoelectronic applications, particularly in white light-emitting diodes and display technologies.

Introduction

Since the first report of a strongly luminescent europium complex by Weissman in 1942,1 lanthanide-based organic complexes have become a major focus of optical materials research. Over the decades, significant progress has been made in designing lanthanide complexes for diverse applications, including polymer doped optical amplifiers2,3 and biomedical diagnostics,4–6 where their sharp emission lines and long lifetimes enable sensitive detection of trace biomolecules.7,8 Lanthanide coordination complexes continue to receive considerable interest due to their exceptional optical, electronic and photophysical properties, which make them highly attractive for next generation optoelectronic and photonic devices.9–11 Among trivalent lanthanide ions, dysprosium(iii) (Dy3+) is of particular significance due to its characteristic dual band emission in blue–yellow spectral region. This emission originates from 4F9/2 → 6H15/2 (blue, ∼480 nm) and 4F9/2 → 6H13/2 (yellow, ∼570–590 nm) transitions, whose complementary nature enables effective intrinsic color mixing.12,13 The simultaneous presence of these narrow-band emissions, together with long luminescence lifetimes and high color purity, allows Dy3+ complexes to generate near-white light using a single emitting centre, thereby eliminating the need for multiple dopant ions.14–16 Owing to these advantageous photophysical characteristics, Dy3+ based materials have emerged as promising candidates for white light emitters,17,18 display technologies19,20 and solid-state lighting applications.21–23 Despite their attractive emission properties, lanthanide ions inherently possess very low molar absorption coefficients (ε ≤ 1 M−1 cm−1).24 To overcome this limitation, antenna effect (Fig. 1) is widely employed, wherein suitable organic chromophores absorb incident light and transfer harvested energy to the lanthanide ion, enabling highly efficient sensitized emission. β-Diketonates represent one of the most effective classes of antenna ligands due to their strong chelating ability, thermal robustness and favourable intramolecular energy transfer pathways.25–28

Schematic illustration of ligand-to-metal energy transfer (antenna effect) leading to sensitized Dy3+ emission.

Fluorination of β-diketonate ligands further enhances luminescence efficiency by replacing high energy C–H vibrations with lower-energy C–F bonds. Heavier fluorine atom reduces vibrational quenching, suppresses non-radiative decay and significantly improves emission intensity.29,30 Beyond this, the incorporation of an appropriate secondary (ancillary) ligand offers an additional strategy to fine tune the photophysical behaviour of the complexes. Such ligands influence the coordination geometry, reduce the number of high frequency solvent molecules near the metal centre and modulate electronic interactions that govern energy transfer efficiency, emission intensity and overall optical performance.31–33 In present study, we report synthesis and detailed characterization of a series of Dy3+ complexes employing 4,4,4-trifluoro-1-phenyl-1,3-butanedione (TFPD) as the primary β-diketonate ligand and 2,2′-bipyridine (Bpy) along with its derivatives 2,2′-bipyridine dihydrobromide (B1), 5-bromo-5′-(3,4-ethylenedioxythien-2-yl)-2,2′-bipyridine (B2) and 5,5′-bis(3,4-ethylenedioxythien-2-yl)-2,2′-bipyridine (B3) as secondary ligands. Structural modifications introduced in the ancillary ligands allow systematic evaluation of their influence on optical, electronic and photoluminescent properties of resulting complexes. Comprehensive studies using FTIR, NMR and photoluminescence spectroscopy were conducted to establish structure property relationships. By correlating electronic features and steric characteristics of secondary ligands with optoelectronic responses of the complexes, this work provides meaningful insights into rational ligand design strategies for developing high performance lanthanide based luminescent materials.

Experimental materials and methods

Chemical used

Analytical grade (AR) reagents, including 25% NH4OH, hexane and ethanol, were used as received without additional purification. Dysprosium chloride hexahydrate (DyCl3·6H2O), 4,4,4-trifluoro-1-phenyl-1,3-butanedione (TFPD) and 2,2′-bipyridine were procured from Sigma-Aldrich and utilized directly for synthesis. Substituted derivatives of 2,2′-bipyridine designated as B1, B2 and B3 were synthesized in research facility following standard laboratory procedures.34

Synthetic procedure

Synthesis of the trivalent dysprosium complexes was achieved by employing DyCl3·6H2O, TFPD and the ancillary ligand in a 1 : 3 : 1 stoichiometric ratio. Initially, TFPD (6.48 mmol) was dissolved in ethanol, followed by the gradual addition of 25% NH4OH. The mixture was allowed to stand until the release of ammonia vapours ceased, indicating the formation of the corresponding ammonium diketone salt. Subsequently, ethanolic solutions of DyCl3·6H2O (2.16 mmol) and the respective ancillary ligand (2.16 mmol) were added to the reaction mixture. pH was maintained between 6 and 7 by dropwise addition of ammonium hydroxide when necessary. The reaction mixture was stirred overnight to promote complete complex formation. After this, the solution was left undisturbed to allow slow ethanol evaporation, leading to the formation of solid complexes. The precipitates obtained were repeatedly washed first with ethanol and then with hexane to eliminate impurities. Instrumentation

The CHN composition of the synthesized dysprosium complexes was analysed using a PerkinElmer 2400 elemental analyzer. UV-vis absorption measurements were performed in dichloromethane (DCM) using the Shimadzu 2450 spectrophotometer over the 200–600 nm range. Vibrational characteristics of complexes were investigated with a PerkinElmer 5700 FT-IR spectrometer, employing potassium bromide as the reference and collecting data in the mid-IR region. To obtained proton NMR spectra Bruker Avance II 400 MHz spectrometer, using tetramethylsilane (TMS) as internal standard and CDCl3 as solvent. Optical properties have been further evaluated using Horiba Jobin Yvon Fluorolog FL-3-11 spectrophotometer, while luminescence decay lifetimes were measured using an F-7000 FL spectrophotometer in powdered form. Electrochemical behavior was examined through cyclic voltammetry (CV) using Potentiostat 4000, operated at a scan rate of 0.1 V s−1. Hitachi STA 7300 simultaneous thermal analyzer was used at a programmed heating rate of 10 °C min−1 to assess thermal robustness of synthesized complexes under a nitrogen atmosphere.

Powder XRD analysis

Powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurements were performed to assess the structural order and crystallinity of the synthesized ternary Dy(iii) complexes. The diffraction patterns of DB1–DB4 (Fig. S1, SI) were collected over a 2θ range of 10–80°. Notable differences in peak intensity and distribution among DB1–DB4 indicate variations in their crystallinity. Notably, the ternary Dy(iii) complex (DB1) exhibits sharper and better-defined diffraction peaks, reflecting its higher crystalline order compared with the other synthesized complexes. In line with earlier studies, diminished or broadened diffraction features are typically associated with reduced structural order in such coordination systems.39,40

UV-vis absorption studies

Electronic absorption spectra of free TFPD ligand and dysprosium complexes (DB1–DB4) were recorded in dichloromethane (DCM) solution and the results are presented in Fig. 4. All samples exhibit intense absorption bands in UV region, originating from π–π* transitions of conjugated organic framework. Free TFPD ligand shows a broad absorption maximum at 314 nm, whereas the dysprosium complexes display red-shifted maxima at 356 nm (DB1), 367 nm (DB2), 376 nm (DB3) and 384 nm (DB4).

This progressive bathochromic shift upon complexation reflects increased electron delocalization and strong metal–ligand interactions, suggesting successful coordination of the ligand to the Dy(iii) center and the resulting modulation of the electronic structure. These variations arise from the pronounced magnetic anisotropy of the Dy3+ center and are collectively referred to as dipolar shifts (Δp). The direction and magnitude of the shift (upfield or downfield) depend on local magnetic environment generated by the Dy3+ ion.47 As predicted, protons located closer to the metal centre show more pronounced shifts, consistent with the inverse dependence of Δp on the distance r.

Luminescence properties analysis

The photoluminescence properties of DB1–DB4 complexes were examined in the powdered form at ambient temperature through excitation and emission spectral analysis. Fig. 6 illustrates the excitation profiles of complexes DB1–DB4, recorded by observing emission about ∼575 nm, which corresponds to prominent 4F9/2 → 6H13/2 transition of Dy3+ ion. All complexes display a wide excitation envelope originating from the electronic absorption of the coordinated organic ligands.48 The observation of this ligand-centred band indicates effective ligand-to-metal energy transfer, indicating that the organic framework efficiently sensitizes the Dy(iii) center and facilitates its characteristic emission through the antenna mechanism. The photoluminescence behaviour of the synthesized dysprosium complexes was investigated by recording their emission spectra under excitation at ∼370 nm. As shown in Fig. 7, two dominant emission features appear in the blue and yellow regions at approximately 482 nm and 575 nm, respectively. These emissions arise from the characteristic 4f–4f transitions of Dy(iii), namely 4F9/2 → 6H15/2 and 4F9/2 → 6H13/2.49 The blue emission centred at 482 nm is associated with a magnetic-dipole-allowed transition, unaffected by variations in the coordination geometry. Conversely, the yellow emission near 575 nm originates from an electric-dipole-allowed transition (ΔL = 2, ΔJ = 2) and exhibits strong sensitivity to the symmetry of the local environment surrounding the Dy3+ ion.50

Excitation spectra for DB1–DB4 complexes.

Luminescence spectra of DB1–DB4 complexes.

In present series, the comparatively higher intensity of the yellow emission relative to blue counterpart signifies that the Dy3+ ions reside in coordination environments with reduced symmetry. This observation follows the well-established photophysical behaviour of Dy(iii) systems, where magnetic dipole allowed transitions are favoured in highly symmetric sites, whereas electric dipole allowed transitions become more prominent as the local symmetry decreases.51 Additionally, a weak emission band observed near 654 nm is attributed to the 4F9/2 → 6H11/2 (ΔJ = 1) transition. Notably, emission profiles of complexes DB3 and DB4 display residual ligand-centered bands, suggesting incomplete ligand to metal energy transfer and consequently, less efficient sensitization of the Dy(iii) center. Excellent mono-exponential fitting indicates the presence of a single, dominant Dy3+ emissive center in each complex, reflecting a well-defined coordination environment.64 A systematic decrease in lifetime values (Table 6) was observed across the series in the order DB1 > DB2 > DB3 > DB4, indicating a progressive increase in non-radiative deactivation processes. In comparison, the present dysprosium complexes exhibit relatively lower luminescence intensity, quantum yield and luminescence lifetime values, which may be attributed to less efficient ligand-to-dysprosium energy transfer.65–67

Thermogravimetric investigation

Thermal stability is a key factor that influences the performance and applicability of lanthanide based materials. To evaluate this characteristic, thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) was conducted, which revealed a distinct single step decomposition pattern. Since all synthesized complexes exhibited comparable thermal profiles, the thermogram of DB4 is presented as a representative case (Fig. 12).

TGA profile of DB4 complex.

The complex remained stable up to 198 °C, indicating that no coordinated or lattice water molecules are present an observation further supported by FTIR results. A major decomposition phase occurred between 198–382 °C, resulting in an overall mass loss of 86.93%, which closely matches the theoretically calculated value of 86.04%. After this decomposition step, no additional mass loss was observed, suggesting the formation of a stable inorganic residue and exhibiting the good thermal stability of the synthesized dysprosium complexes.71,72

Conclusions

This study demonstrates that systematic substituent modulation of ancillary ligands effectively governs the optoelectronic properties of heteroleptic mononuclear Dy(iii) complexes. The spectroscopic and analytical results are consistent with the proposed composition of the Dy(iii) complexes containing three TFPD ligands and one bidentate nitrogen-donor ancillary ligand, providing a stable framework for tuning the electronic and photophysical properties. Optical absorption and electrochemical investigations reveal wide band gap semiconducting characteristics, with HOMO–LUMO energy gaps ranging from 2.88 to 3.14 eV, showing strong agreement between spectroscopic and electrochemical estimations. The tunability of these band gaps through ligand substitution highlights the potential of these complexes as molecular semiconducting materials for optoelectronic processing. Importantly, the DB4 complex demonstrates thermal stability up to 198 °C, indicating suitability for thermal fabrication techniques and thin-film device integration. Efficient ligand to metal energy transfer produces dominant yellow emission (4F9/2 → 6H13/2 at 575 nm), while the presence of ligand based band results in dual blue-yellow emission in DB3 and DB4. Overall, rational ligand engineering enables precise control over band gap, energy transfer dynamics and emission behavior, positioning these complexes as promising materials for semiconducting photonic applications.

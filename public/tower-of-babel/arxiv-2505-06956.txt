On Siegel–Eisenstein series of level p p and their p p -adic properties
Siegfried Böcherer, Keiichi Gunji, Toshiyuki Kikuta

Abstract
We construct a Siegel–Eisenstein series of level p p with a quadratic character mod p p which is a U ⁡ ( p ) U(p) -eigenfunction with eigenvalue 1 1 , and calculate its Fourier coefficients explicitly. We show that this Siegel–Eisenstein series is a p p -adic Siegel–Eisenstein series, i.e., it is a p p -adic limit of a sequence of Siegel–Eisenstein series of level 1 1 . We prove also that the Siegel–Eisenstein series with a nonquadratic character mod p p constructed by Takemori is also a p p -adic Siegel–Eisenstein series.

1
Introduction

p
-Adic limits of Eisenstein series are formally defined by
a Fourier series, whose coefficients are uniform
p
-adic limits of
the Fourier coefficients for the sequence of Eisenstein series
in question. One may ask whether such a formal series actually defines
a classical modular form. In most works on this subject, it is an issue
how to “guess” a candidate for such a classical modular form.
In our papers
[
1
,
2
]
the candidate
came up naturally as a linear combination of
genus theta series for quadratic forms of level
p
(after some work!).
By Siegel’s theorem this candidate can then be rewritten as a
Siegel–Eisenstein series of level
p
and one can show that it automatically
has to be an eigenfunction for the Hecke operator
U(p)
with eigenvalue
1
.

The first aim of this paper is to provide a direct
construction of such a Siegel–Eisenstein series of level
p
with a quadratic character mod
p
,
which is
U(p)
-eigenfunction with eigenvalue
1
.
This is accomplished by using the functional equation between
two Eisenstein series corresponding to some cusps, presented by
[
3
]
.
This method also allows us to give its Fourier coefficients completely explicitly.

The second aim of this paper is to show that a simple comparison of the
Fourier coefficients of
p
-adic limits of the Eisenstein series (of level
1
)
on one hand and
with those of the level
p
Eisenstein series with
U(p)
-eigenvalue
1
on the other hand
give the most direct approach to
the problem described above (“guessing a candidate”) for many cases, in fact in a surprisingly simple manner.
We will later (Remark
4.2
) comment on the strength and applicability of the
different methods.

Notation

Let
n
be a positive integer,

\mathbb{H}_{n}
the Siegel upper half space of degree
n
,
and
\Gamma_{n}
the Siegel modular group of degree
n
.
Let
N
be a positive integer and
\Gamma_{0}^{(n)}(N)
the congruence subgroup of
\Gamma_{n}
defined as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}^{(n)}(N):=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_{n}\>\Big|\>C\equiv 0_{n}\bmod{N}\right\}.

For a positive integer
k
and a Dirichlet character

\chi
mod
N
, we denote by

the space of Siegel modular forms of weight
k
with character
\chi
for
\Gamma_{0}^{(n)}(N)
.
When

(trivial character mod

we write simply
M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}^{(n)}(N))
for

.

Any

has a Fourier expansion of the form

F(Z)=\sum_{0\leq T\in\Lambda_{n}}a_{F}(T)q^{T},\quad q^{T}:=e^{2\pi i{\rm Tr}(TZ)},\quad Z\in\mathbb{H}_{n},

where

\Lambda_{n}:=\{T=(t_{ij})\in{\rm Sym}_{n}(\mathbb{Q})\;|\;t_{ii},\;2t_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

For the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
or the Dirichlet

with character

\zeta^{(p)}(s)
or

the functions removing the Euler

For Dirichlet characters

2.1
Siegel–Eisenstein series and the
U(p)
-operator

Let
p

\chi_{p}
. Let
\psi={\boldsymbol{1}}_{p}

\chi_{p}
. For an integer

\psi(-1)=(-1)^{k}

E^{n,(n)}_{k,\psi}(Z,s)=(\det Y)^{s-k/2}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\Gamma_{\infty}w_{n}\Gamma_{0}^{(n)}(p)}\psi(\det C)\det(CZ+D)^{-k}|\det(CZ+D)|^{k-2s}.

for

and

. Here we write
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}
as usual. The right hand side converge for
\re(2s)>n+1

s

E^{n,(n)}_{k,\psi}(Z,k/2)\in M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}^{(n)}(p),\psi)
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{k,s}(\Gamma^{(n)}_{0}(p),\psi)
be the space of Siegel–Eisenstein series of weight
k
, with complex parameter
s
(cf.
[
3
, §§2.1]

f=\sum_{T\in\Lambda_{n}}b(T,Y,s)e^{2\pi i\mathrm{tr}(TX)}\in\mathcal{E}_{k,s}(\Gamma_{0}^{(n)}(p),\psi)
, we have

U(p)f=\sum_{T\in\Lambda_{n}}b(pT,p^{-1}Y,s)e^{2\pi i\mathrm{tr}(TX)}.

Then by
[
3
, Proposition 2.10]
,
E^{n,(n)}_{k,\psi}(Z,s)
is a
U(p)
-eigenfunction with the eigenvalue
p^{n(k/2-s)+2sn-n(n+1)/2}
. We know the existence and the uniqueness up to scalar of such a function by
[
3
, Corollary 2.5]
.

Let
E^{n,(0)}_{k,\psi}(Z,s)\in\mathcal{E}_{k,s}(\Gamma^{(n)}_{0}(p),\psi)
be the
U(p)
-eigenfunction with eigenvalue
p^{n(k-s/2)}
, normalized so that the constant term of the Fourier expansion is
\det(Y)^{s/2-k}
. Then there is a functional equation between
E^{n,(0)}_{k,\psi}(Z,s)
and
E^{n,(n)}_{k,\psi}(Z,(n+1)/2-s)
as follows.

We put

\delta=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $k$ is even,}\\
1&\text{if $k$ is odd,}\end{cases}\quad\varepsilon_{p}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $p\equiv 1\bmod 4$,}\\
i&\text{if $p\equiv 3\bmod 4$.}\end{cases}

We define the completed Riemann zeta function and the Dirichlet
L
-function by

\xi(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\zeta(s),\quad\xi(s,\chi_{p})=\left(\frac{p}{\pi}\right)^{(s+\delta)/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{s+\delta}{2}\right)L(s,\chi_{p}).

Then
\xi(1-s)=\xi(s)
and
\xi(1-s,\chi_{p})=\xi(s,\chi_{p})
holds. By abuse of language, we understand
\xi(s,\psi)=\xi(s)
for
\psi={\boldsymbol{1}}_{p}
. We set
\Gamma_{m}(s)=\pi^{m(m-1)/4}\prod_{i=0}^{m-1}\Gamma(s-i/2
).

Now we put

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k,\psi}^{n,(0)}(Z,s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma_{n}\left(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{n}\left(s+\dfrac{\delta}{2}\right)}\xi(2s,\psi)\prod_{j=1}^{[n/2]}\xi(4s-2j)E_{k,\psi}^{n,(0)}(Z,s),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k,\psi}^{n,(n)}(Z,s)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{n}(s,\psi)\frac{\Gamma_{n}\left(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{n}\left(s+\dfrac{\delta}{2}\right)}\xi(2s,\psi)\prod_{j=1}^{[n/2]}\xi(4s-2j)E_{k,\psi}^{n,(n)}(Z,s),

here

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}(s,{\boldsymbol{1}}_{p})

\displaystyle=\frac{1-p^{-2s}}{1-p^{n-2s}}\prod_{i=1}^{[n/2]}\frac{1-p^{2i-4s}}{1-p^{2n+1-2i-4s}},

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}(s,\chi_{p})

\displaystyle=(\varepsilon_{p}p^{-1/2})^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{[n/2]}\frac{1-p^{2i-4s}}{1-p^{2n+1-2i-4s}}.

Then by
[
3

the following functional equation holds.

\mathbb{E}^{n,(0)}_{k,\psi}(Z,s)=\mathbb{E}^{n,(n)}_{k,\psi}\left(Z,\frac{n+1}{2}-s\right).

Thus we can write

\displaystyle E^{n,(0)}_{k,\psi}(Z,s)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{n}\Bigl(\frac{n+1}{2}-s,\psi\Bigr)\frac{\Gamma_{n}\Bigl(s+\dfrac{\delta}{2}\Bigr)\Gamma_{n}\Bigl(\dfrac{n+1}{2}-s+\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)}{\Gamma_{n}\Bigl(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)\Gamma_{n}\Bigl(\dfrac{n+1+\delta}{2}-s\Bigr)}

(2.1)

\displaystyle\times\frac{\xi(n+1-2s,\psi)}{\xi(1-2s,\psi)}\prod_{j=1}^{[n/2]}\frac{\xi(2n+2-4s-2j)}{\xi(1-4s+2j)}E^{n,(n)}_{k,\psi}\Bigl(Z,\frac{n+1}{2}-s\Bigr),

here we use
\xi(2s,\psi)=\xi(1-2s,\psi)
and
\xi(4s-2j)=\xi(1-4s+2j)
.

In this section we shall show the following.

Theorem 2.1
.

For any positive integer
k
such that
\psi(-1)=(-1)^{k}
,
E^{n,(0)}_{k,\psi}(Z,k/2)
is a non-zero holomorphic Siegel modular form of level
p
, weight

-eigenfunction with eigenvalue

.

In order to prove the theorem, we shall study the Fourier expansion of
E^{n,(0)}_{k,\psi}(Z,s)
. For
T\in\Lambda_{n}
and a prime number

, we define the local Siegel series by

S^{n}_{q}(T,q^{-s})=\sum_{R\in{\rm Sym}_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{q}/\mathbb{Z}_{q})}\mu_{q}(R)^{-s}e^{2\pi i\Tr(TR)},

where
\mu_{q}(R)=q^{\ord_{q}\mu(R)}

\mu(R)

. It is known that
S^{n}_{q}(T,q^{-s})
is a rational function in

.

Using Siegel series, the Fourier expansion of

\displaystyle\quad E^{n,(n)}_{k,\psi}\Bigl(Z,\frac{n+1}{2}-s\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\det(Y)^{(n+1-k)/2-s}\sum_{T\in\Lambda_{n}}\biggl\{\Xi_{n}(Y,T;s_{1},s_{2})\prod_{q\neq p}S^{n}_{q}(T,\psi(q)q^{2s-n-1})\biggr\}e^{2\pi i\Tr(TX)},

with
s_{1}=(n+1)/2-s+k/2
and
s_{2}=(n+1)/2-s-k/2
. Here,
\Xi_{n}(Y,T;\alpha,\beta)
is the confluent hypergeometric function investigated by Shimura (
[
9
]
). We use the notation
\Xi
instead of
\xi
, in order to avoid confusions with completed zeta functions.

First we study
\Xi_{n}(Y,T;s_{1},s_{2})
.
For
T\in\Lambda_{n}
we denote the sign of
T
by
(a,b)
, when the number of the positive (resp. negative) eigenvalues of
T
is
a
(resp.
b
). By

\rank T=r

(r-t,t)

\begin{split}&\Xi_{n}(Y,T;s_{1},s_{2})=i^{-nk}2^{u}\pi^{v}\frac{\Gamma_{n-r}\Bigl(\dfrac{n+1}{2}-2s\Bigr)}{\Gamma_{n-t}\Bigl(\dfrac{n+1}{2}-s+\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)\Gamma_{n-r+t}\Bigl(\dfrac{n+1}{2}-s-\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)}\\
&\ \ \times\det(Y)^{2s-(n+1)/2}d_{+}(TY)^{-s+k/2+t/4}d_{-}(TY)^{-s-k/2+(r-t)/4}\omega_{n}(2\pi Y,T;s_{1},s_{2}).\end{split}

(2.2)

Here

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\frac{r(n+1)}{2}+2(n-r)s+k(r-2t)+\frac{(r-t)t}{2}-\frac{n(n-1)}{2},

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle=\frac{(k-2s)r}{2}-kt+\frac{n(n+1)}{2}-\frac{r(n-r)}{2}-\frac{t(r-t)}{2},

(resp.

) is the product of the absolute values of all positive (resp. negative) eigenvalues of

\omega_{n}(2\pi Y,T,\alpha,\beta)

\omega_{n}\left(2\pi Y,T;\frac{n+1}{2},\beta\right)=2^{-r(n+1)/2}\pi^{r(n-r)/2}e^{-2\pi\Tr(TY)}.

(2.3)

Next we investigate the Siegel series. Assume that
T\in\Lambda_{n}
is non-degenerate. If

\displaystyle(1-q^{-s})\left(\prod_{i=1}^{(n-1)/2}(1-q^{2i-2s})\right)F_{q}^{n}(T,q^{-s})&\text{$n$ is odd.}\end{cases}

Note that

is given by Katsurada (

For

with lower rank, we have the following.

(

Let

\displaystyle S_{q}^{n}(T,q^{-s})=\frac{\displaystyle(1-q^{-s})\prod_{j=1}^{[n/2]}(1-q^{2j-2s})}{\displaystyle(1-\chi_{T_{1}}(q)q^{n-r/2-s})\prod_{j=1}^{[(n-r)/2]}(1-q^{2n-2j-r+1-2s})}F_{q}^{r}(T_{1},q^{n-r-s}).

If

is odd then

\displaystyle S_{q}^{n}(T,q^{-s})=\frac{\displaystyle(1-q^{-s})\prod_{j=1}^{[n/2]}(1-q^{2j-2s})}{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{[(n-r+1)/2]}(1-q^{2n-2j-r+2-2s})}F_{q}^{r}(T_{1},q^{n-r-s}).

2.2
The case of trivial character

In this subsection we treat the case of
\psi={\boldsymbol{1}}_{p}
. In that case the weight
k
is even. Let

E^{n,(0)}_{k,{\boldsymbol{1}}_{p}}(Z,s)=\sum_{T\in\Lambda_{n}}C_{{\boldsymbol{1}}_{p}}(T;Y,s)e^{2\pi i\Tr(TX)}

be the Fourier expansion of
E^{n,(0)}_{k,{\boldsymbol{1}}_{p}}
.

First assume that
T
is positive semi-definite; the sign of
T
is
(r,0)
(
0\leq r\leq n
). Let
T=\diag(T_{1},0)
with
T_{1}\in\Lambda_{r}
and
T_{1}>0
.
We can write
C_{{\boldsymbol{1}}_{p}}(T;Y,s)
explicitly using (
2.1
), (
2.2
) and Proposition
2.2
. The term in (

) is rewritten as

\displaystyle\quad\ \ \frac{\xi(n+1-2s)}{\xi(1-2s)}\prod_{j=1}^{[n/2]}\frac{\xi(2n+2-4s-2j)}{\xi(1-4s+2j)}

\displaystyle=\pi^{-n(n+1)/4}\frac{\Gamma\Bigl(\dfrac{n+1}{2}-s\Bigr)}{\Gamma\Bigl(\dfrac{1}{2}-s\Bigr)}\prod_{j=1}^{[n/2]}\frac{\Gamma(n+1-2s-j)}{\Gamma\Bigl(\dfrac{1}{2}-2s+j\Bigr)}

\displaystyle\quad\ \ \times\frac{\zeta(n+1-2s)}{\zeta(1-2s)}\prod_{j=1}^{[n/2]}\frac{\zeta(2n+2-4s-2j)}{\zeta(1-4s+2j)}.

Together with (

C_{{\boldsymbol{1}}_{p}}(T;Y,k/2)=\lim_{s\to k/2}G_{n,r}(s)Z_{n}({\boldsymbol{1}}_{p},T,s)e^{-2\pi\Tr(TY)}.

Here,
G_{n,r}(s)
is the term corresponding to Gamma functions, defined by

\displaystyle G_{n,r}(s)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{nk/2}2^{2ns-n(n-1)/2}\pi^{n(n+1)/4}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\Gamma_{n}(s)\Gamma_{n-r}\Bigl(\dfrac{n+1}{2}-2s\Bigr)}{\Gamma_{n}\Bigl(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)\Gamma_{n}\Bigl(\dfrac{n}{2}-s\Bigr)\Gamma_{n-r}\Bigl(\dfrac{n+1}{2}-s-\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{j=1}^{[n/2]}\frac{\Gamma(n+1-2s-j)}{\Gamma\Bigl(\dfrac{1}{2}-2s+j\Bigr)},

where we use the relation
\dfrac{\displaystyle\Gamma\Bigl(\frac{n+1}{2}-s\Bigr)}{\displaystyle\Gamma\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}-s\Bigr)\Gamma_{n}\Bigl(\frac{n+1}{2}-s\Bigl)}=\dfrac{1}{\Gamma_{n}\Bigl(\dfrac{n}{2}-s\Bigr)}

If

Z_{n}({\boldsymbol{1}}_{p},T,s)=\frac{L^{(p)}(1+r/2-2s,\chi_{T_{1}})}{\displaystyle\zeta^{(p)}(1-2s)\prod_{j=1}^{r/2}\zeta^{(p)}(1-4s+2j)}\prod_{q\neq p}F^{r}_{q}(T_{1},q^{2s-r-1}),

We study

more precisely. If

\prod_{j=1}^{[n/2]}\frac{\Gamma(n+1-2s-j)}{\Gamma\Bigl(\dfrac{1}{2}-2s+j\Bigr)}=\prod_{j=0}^{n/2-1}\frac{\Gamma(n-2s-j)}{\Gamma\Bigl(\dfrac{n+1}{2}-2s-j\Bigr)}=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{\Gamma(n-2s-j)}{\Gamma\Bigl(\dfrac{n+1}{2}-2s-\dfrac{j}{2}\Bigr)},

\displaystyle G_{n,r}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma_{n}\Bigl(\dfrac{n+1}{2}-s-\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)\Gamma_{n-r}\Bigl(\dfrac{n+1}{2}-2s\Bigr)}{\Gamma_{n-r}\Bigl(\dfrac{n+1}{2}-s-\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)\Gamma_{n}\Bigl(\dfrac{n+1}{2}-2s\Bigr)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}\frac{\Gamma\Bigl(\dfrac{r+1}{2}-s-\dfrac{k}{2}-\dfrac{j}{2}\Bigr)}{\Gamma\Bigl(\dfrac{r+1}{2}-2s-\dfrac{j}{2}\Bigr)}.

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma_{n}\Bigl(\dfrac{n+1}{2}-s+\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)\Gamma_{n-r}\Bigl(\dfrac{n+1}{2}-s-\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)}{\Gamma_{n-t}\Bigl(\dfrac{n+1}{2}-s+\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)\Gamma_{n-r+t}\Bigl(\dfrac{n+1}{2}-s-\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\displaystyle\pi^{*}\prod_{i=0}^{t-1}\Gamma\Bigl(\frac{t+1}{2}-s+\frac{k}{2}-\frac{i}{2}\Bigr)}{\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{t-1}\Gamma\Bigl(\frac{r+1}{2}-s-\frac{k}{2}-\frac{i}{2}\Bigr)}.

The numerator is finite at

. Thus in order to show the vanishing of Fourier coefficients, it suffices to consider the case

as we have seen above.
Then thanks to the denominator, it vanishes at
s=k/2
unless
t=1
and
r
is even.

Let us consider the remaining case,
t=1
and
r
is even. We see that the term
L^{(p)}(1+r/2-2s,\chi_{T_{1}})
in
Z_{n}({\boldsymbol{1}}_{p},T,s)
vanishes at
s=k/2
for
r\leq 2k-2
. Indeed, then
r/2+1-k
is a non-positive integer. Since
\sgn T_{1}=(r-1,1)
we have
\chi_{T_{1}}(-1)=(-1)^{r/2+1}
, thus
L^{(p)}(r/2+1-k,\chi_{T_{1}})=0
, for we have assumed
k
is even. If
r=2k
, then as we have seen above,
Z_{n}({\boldsymbol{1}}_{p},T,k/2)
survives only for the
T
such that
\chi_{T_{1}}={\boldsymbol{1}}
, but it does not happen since
\chi_{T_{1}}(-1)=-1
. As a consequence we have shown that
C_{{\boldsymbol{1}}_{p}}(T,Y,k/2)=0
for non positive semi-definite
T
.

Now we have proved Theorem
2.1
. More precisely we can write down the Fourier coefficients as follows.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
T=\diag(T_{1},0)\in\Lambda_{n}
with
T_{1}\in\Lambda_{r}
positive definite. Then the Fourier coefficient
a(T)
of
E^{n,(0)}_{k,{\boldsymbol{1}}_{p}}(Z,k/2)
at
T
is given as follows.

(1)

Assume that
k\geq\dfrac{n+1}{2}
. If
r
is even then

a(T)=\frac{2^{r/2}\,L^{(p)}(r/2+1-k,\chi_{T_{1}})}{\displaystyle\zeta^{(p)}(1-k)\prod_{j=1}^{r/2}\zeta^{(p)}(1-2k+2j)}\,\prod_{q\neq p}F_{q}^{r}(T_{1},q^{k-r-1}).

If
r
is odd then

a(T)=\frac{2^{(r+1)/2}}{\displaystyle\zeta^{(p)}(1-k)\prod_{j=1}^{(r-1)/2}\zeta^{(p)}(1-2k+2j)}\,\prod_{q\neq p}F_{q}^{r}(T_{1},q^{k-r-1}).

(2)

Assume that
k\leq\dfrac{n}{2}
. Then
a(T)=0
unless
r\leq 2k
. If
r<2k
then
a(T)
is the
same as above. For the case
r=2k
,
a(T)\neq 0
only if
\chi_{T_{1}}={\boldsymbol{1}}
and in that case,

a(T)=\frac{2^{k}}{\displaystyle\zeta^{(p)}(1-k)\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\zeta^{(p)}(1-2k+2j)}\,\prod_{q\neq p}F_{q}^{r}(T_{1},q^{-k-1}).

2.3
The case of quadratic character

In this subsection we treat the case
\psi=\chi_{p}
the quadratic character modulo
p
. The necessary calculations are quite similar to the case of trivial character. We write the Fourier expansion

E^{n,(0)}_{k,\chi_{p}}(Z,s)=\sum_{T\in\Lambda_{n}}C_{\chi_{p}}(T;Y,s)e^{2\pi i\Tr(TX)}.

If
T=\diag(T_{1},0)
is positive semi-definite, then we have

C_{\chi_{p}}(T;Y,k/2)=\lim_{s\to k/2}G^{\chi_{p}}_{n,r}(s)Z_{n}(\chi_{p},T,s)e^{-2\pi\Tr(TY)}

with

\displaystyle G^{\chi_{p}}_{n,r}(s)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{p}^{n}\,i^{-nk}2^{2ns-n(n-1)/2}\pi^{n(n+1)/4}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\Gamma_{n}\Bigl(s+\dfrac{\delta}{2}\Bigr)\Gamma_{n-r}\Bigl(\dfrac{n+1}{2}-2s\Bigr)}{\Gamma_{n}\Bigl(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)\Gamma_{n}\Bigl(\dfrac{n+\delta}{2}-s\Bigr)\Gamma_{n-r}\Bigl(\dfrac{n+1}{2}-s-\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)}

(2.6)

\displaystyle\times\prod_{j=1}^{[n/2]}\frac{\Gamma(n+1-2s-j)}{\Gamma\Bigl(\dfrac{1}{2}-2s+j\Bigr)}

and
Z_{n}(\chi_{p},T,s)
defined as

Z_{n}(\chi_{p},T,s)=\frac{L^{(p)}(1+r/2-2s,\chi_{T_{1}}\chi_{p})}{\displaystyle L(1-2s,\chi_{p})\prod_{j=1}^{r/2}\zeta^{(p)}(1-4s+2j)}\prod_{q\neq p}F^{r}_{q}(T_{1},\chi_{p}(q)q^{2s-r-1}),

or

Z_{n}(\chi_{p},T,s)=\frac{1}{\displaystyle L(1-2s,\chi_{p})\prod_{j=1}^{(r-1)/2}\zeta^{(p)}(1-4s+2j)}\prod_{q\neq p}F^{r}_{q}(T_{1},\chi_{p}(q)q^{2s-r-1})

according as
r
is even or odd. We recall that
\chi_{T_{1}}\chi_{p}
stands for the primitive character arising from
\chi_{T_{1}}\cdot\chi_{p}
.
By the similar way to show (
2.2
), we can prove the equality

G_{n,r}^{\chi_{p}}(s)=\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}\frac{\Gamma\Bigl(\dfrac{r+1}{2}-s-\dfrac{k}{2}-\dfrac{j}{2}\Bigr)}{\Gamma\Bigl(\dfrac{r+1}{2}-2s-\dfrac{j}{2}\Bigr)}.

(2.7)

Indeed, if
k
is even, then (
2.3
) is completely same as (
2.2
).
If
k
is odd, then we have
p\equiv 3\bmod 4
and
\varepsilon_{p}^{k}\,i^{-n(k+1)}=(-1)^{n(k-1)/2}
in (
2.3
). Moreover by
[
6
, Lemma 6.1]
we have the functional equation

\frac{\Gamma_{n}\Bigl(s+\dfrac{1}{2}\Bigr)}{\Gamma_{n}\Bigl(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)}=(-1)^{n(k-1)/2}\frac{\Gamma_{n}\Bigl(\dfrac{n+1}{2}-s-\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)}{\Gamma_{n}\Bigl(\dfrac{n}{2}-s\Bigr)}.

Using it one can show the equation (
2.7
).

Therefore we have for
r\leq 2k-1

\lim_{s\to k/2}G^{\chi_{p}}_{n,r}(s)=\begin{cases}2^{r/2}&\text{if $r$ is even, }\\
2^{(r+1)/2}&\text{if $r$ is odd.}\end{cases}

Moreover if
\sgn T=(r-t,t)
with
1\leq t\leq r\leq n
, then
\lim_{s\to k/2}G^{\chi_{p}}_{n,r}(s)=0
unless
t=1
and
r
is even.

The term
Z_{n}(\chi_{p},T,s)
is also similar to the case of the trivial character. It has zero at
s=k/2
for
r\geq 2k+1
and if
r=2k
, it survives only for
T=(T_{1},0)
with
T_{1}\in\Lambda_{2k}
that satisfies
\chi_{T_{1}}=\chi_{p}
. In the case that
\sgn T_{1}=(r-1,1)
with even
r
, then
\chi_{T_{1}}\chi_{p}(-1)=(-1)^{r/2-k+1}
, thus
L^{(p)}(1+r/2-k,\chi_{T_{1}}\chi_{p})=0
for
r\leq 2k-1
, and
\chi_{T_{1}}=\chi_{p}
does not happen if
r=2k
. Thus we have shown that
C_{\chi_{p}}(T;Y,k/2)=0
for non positive semi-definite
T
.

Now we have proved Theorem
2.1
. The Fourier coefficients are explicitly given as follows. We remark that in the case of

, the same formula was given by Takemori (

), who used the functional equation of the Siegel series.

.

If

3
p
-Adic limits of the Siegel–Eisenstein series and their Fourier coefficients

Let
p
be an odd prime and
v_{p}
the additive valuation on the
p
-adic field
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
normalized such that
v_{p}(p)=1
.
For a formal power series
F
of the form
F=\sum_{T\in\Lambda_{n}}a_{F}(T)q^{T}
with
a_{F}(T)\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
,
we define

\displaystyle v_{p}(F):=\inf\{v_{p}(a_{F}(T))\;|\;T\in\Lambda_{n}\}.

For a sequence of formal power series

F_{m}=\sum_{T\in\Lambda_{n}}a_{F_{m}}(T)q^{T}
with
a_{F_{m}}(T)\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
,
and a formal power series
G=\sum_{T\in\Lambda_{n}}a_{G}(T)q^{T}

with
a_{G}(T)\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
,
we write

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}F_{m}=G\quad(p\text{-adic\ limit})

if
v_{p}(F_{m}-G)\to\infty
when
m\to\infty
.

We put

{\boldsymbol{X}}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}/(p-1)\mathbb{Z}.

We regard
\mathbb{Z}\subset{\boldsymbol{X}}
via the embedding
n\mapsto(n,\widetilde{n})
with
\widetilde{n}:=n
mod
p-1
.
Let
E_{k}^{n}=\sum_{T}a_{k}(T)q^{T}
be the Siegel–Eisenstein series of degree
n
, weight
k
, level
1

and the constant term
1
.
For
(k,a)\in{\boldsymbol{X}}
, the
p
-adic Siegel–Eisenstein series
\widetilde{E}^{n}_{(k,a)}

of degree
n
and weight
(k,a)
is defined (if convergent) as

\widetilde{E}^{n}_{(k,a)}:=\lim_{m\to\infty}E_{k_{m}}^{n}\quad(p\text{-adic\ limit}),

where
k_{m}
is a sequence of even integers such that
k_{m}\to\infty
(
m\to\infty
) in the usual topology of
\mathbb{R}

and
k_{m}\to(k,a)
(
m\to\infty
) in
{\boldsymbol{X}}
.
We write

\widetilde{E}^{n}_{(k,a)}=\sum_{T}\widetilde{a}_{(k,a)}(T)q^{T}.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
p
be an odd prime.
Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
with
0\leq\alpha\leq p-2
and

k
be a positive integer with
k\geq\frac{n+1}{2}
such that
k+\alpha
is even.
Let
\{k_{m}\}
a sequence of even integers such that

k_{m}\to(k,k+\alpha)\in{\boldsymbol{X}},\quad k_{m}\to\infty\quad(m\to\infty).

Then the Fourier coefficients of
\widetilde{E}^{n}_{(k,k+\alpha)}
are given as follows.
For
T={\rm diag}(T_{1},0)
with
T_{1}\in\Lambda_{r}
positive definite, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{a}_{(k,k+\alpha)}(T)=2^{[(r+1)/2]}L^{(p)}(1-k,\omega^{\alpha})^{-1}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{[r/2]}L^{(p)}(1+2j-2k,\omega^{2\alpha})^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle~~~~~\times\prod_{q\neq p}F^{r}_{q}(T_{1},\omega^{\alpha}(q)q^{k-r-1})\times\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\ r\ \text{odd},\\
L^{(p)}(1+r/2-k,\chi_{T_{1}}\omega^{\alpha})&\text{if}\ r\ \text{even}.\end{cases}

Here
\omega
is the Teichmüller character on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

Proof.

We may suppose that
k_{m}=k+a(m)p^{b(m)}
with
a(m)\equiv\alpha
mod
(p-1)
,
b(m)\to\infty
(
m\to\infty
).
Let
T={\rm diag}(T_{1},0)
with
T_{1}\in\Lambda_{r}
positive definite.
Recall that

\displaystyle a_{k_{m}}(T)=2^{[(r+1)/2]}L(1-k_{m},{\boldsymbol{1}})^{-1}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{[r/2]}L(1+2i-2k_{m},{\boldsymbol{1}})^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle~~~~~\times\prod_{q}F^{r}_{q}(T,q^{k_{m}-r-1})\times\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\ r\ \text{odd},\\
L(1+r/2-k,\chi_{T_{1}})&\text{if}\ r\ \text{even}.\end{cases}

Let
L_{p}(s,\varphi)
be the Kubota–Leopoldt
p
-adic
L
-function for a primitive Dirichlet character
\varphi
.
It is known that
L_{p}(s,\varphi)
is a
p
-adic meromorphic (analytic if
\varphi\neq{\boldsymbol{1}}
) function satisfying

\displaystyle L_{p}(1-s,\varphi)=-(1-\varphi\omega^{-s}(p)p^{s-1})\frac{B_{s,\varphi\omega^{-s}}}{s}\qquad(1\leq s\in\mathbb{Z}),

(3.1)

where
B_{s,\chi}
is the
s
-th generalized Bernoulli number with a character
\chi
.
Note also that
L_{p}(s,{\boldsymbol{1}})
is analytic except for a pole at
s=1
.
Then we have

\displaystyle L^{(p)}(1-s,\varphi\omega^{-s})=L_{p}(1-s,\varphi)\qquad(1\leq s\in\mathbb{Z}).

(3.2)

First we show that

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}L(1-k_{m},{\boldsymbol{1}})=L^{(p)}(1-k,\omega^{\alpha}).

(3.3)

If we put
s=k_{m}
,
\varphi=\omega^{k+\alpha}
in (
3.1
), then we have
\varphi\omega^{-s}=\omega^{0}={\boldsymbol{1}}
and therefore

\displaystyle L_{p}(1-k_{m},\omega^{k+\alpha})=-(1-p^{k_{m}-1})\frac{B_{k_{m}}}{k_{m}}.

Here
B_{k_{m}}=B_{k_{m},\boldsymbol{1}}
is the
k_{m}
-th Bernoulli number.
This implies

\displaystyle L(1-k_{m},{\boldsymbol{1}})

\displaystyle=-\frac{B_{k_{m}}}{k_{m}}=\frac{L_{p}(1-k_{m},\omega^{k+\alpha})}{1-p^{k_{m}-1}}.

Taking a
p
-adic limit of the both sides, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}L(1-k_{m},{\boldsymbol{1}})

\displaystyle=L_{p}(1-k,\omega^{k+\alpha})=L^{(p)}(1-k,\omega^{\alpha}),

because of
k_{m}\to k
(
m\to\infty
)
p
-adically.
Here the last equality follows from (
3.2
). This proves (
3.3
).
Note that
L^{(p)}(1-k,\omega^{\alpha})\neq 0
since
k+\alpha
is even.

In a similar way, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{[r/2]}L(1+2j-2k_{m},{\boldsymbol{1}})^{-1}=\prod_{j=1}^{[r/2]}L^{(p)}(1+2j-2k,\omega^{2\alpha})^{-1},

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}L_{p}(1+r/2-k_{m},\chi_{T_{1}}\omega^{k-r/2+\alpha})=L^{(p)}(1+r/2-k,\chi_{T_{1}}\omega^{\alpha})

(3.4)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~(\text{for}\ r\ \text{even\ with}\ 0\leq r\leq n).

Here, since
k\geq\frac{n+1}{2}
implies
2j-2k<0
, the first equation above can be obtained using (
3.2
).
Again, it is necessary to note that
L^{(p)}(1+2j-2k,\omega^{2\alpha})\neq 0
for each
j
with
1\leq j\leq[r/2]
.

Next we show that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\prod_{q}F^{r}_{q}(T_{1},q^{k_{m}-r-1})=\prod_{q\neq p}F^{r}_{q}(T_{1},\omega^{\alpha}(q)q^{k-r-1}).

It suffices to show that

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}F^{r}_{q}(T_{1},q^{k_{m}-r-1})=F^{r}_{q}(T_{1},\omega^{\alpha}(q)q^{k-r-1}),

(3.5)

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}F^{r}_{p}(T_{1},p^{k_{m}-r-1})=1.

(3.6)

Since

q^{k_{m}-r-1}\equiv q^{a(m)p^{b(m)}}q^{k-r-1}\equiv\omega^{\alpha}(q)q^{k-r-1}\bmod{p^{b(m)+1}}

and the constant term of
F^{r}_{q}(T_{1},X)
is
1
,
we have

\displaystyle F^{r}_{q}(T_{1},q^{k_{m}-r-1})\equiv F^{r}_{q}(T_{1},\omega^{\alpha}(q)q^{k-r-1})\bmod{p^{b(m)+1}},

(3.7)

\displaystyle F^{r}_{p}(T_{1},p^{k_{m}-r-1})\equiv 1\bmod{p^{b(m)+1}}\quad(\text{for}\ m\text{\ large}).

(3.8)

This shows (
3.5
), (
3.6
).

Finally, we need to check that this convergence is uniform in
T
.
The main issue is dependence on the quadratic character
\chi_{T_{1}}
.
If the conductor of
\chi_{T_{1}}\omega^{k-r/2+\alpha}
is divisible by a prime
q
other than
p
,
then there exists a formal power series
\Phi_{T_{1}}(X)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![X]\!]
such that

L_{p}(s,\chi_{T_{1}}\omega^{k-r/2+\alpha})=\Phi_{T_{1}}((1+p)^{s}-1)
(cf. Washington
[
12
, Theorem 7.10]
).
By this fact and (
3.7
), (
3.8
), we see that the convergence is uniform in
T
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

For the case of trivial character, one can avoid the use of Kubota–Leopoldt
p
-adic
L
-function
(Kummer type congruences would be enough).

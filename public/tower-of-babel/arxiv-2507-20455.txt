Ribbon knots and iterated cables of fibered knots
Jennifer Hom, JungHwan Park

Abstract
. We define a knot to be γ 0 \gamma_{0} -sharp if its Seifert genus is detected by the concordance invariant γ 0 \gamma_{0} , which arises from the immersed curve formalism in bordered Heegaard Floer homology. We show that a connected sum of γ 0 \gamma_{0} -sharp fibered knots is ribbon exactly when it is of the form K # − K K\mathbin{\#}-K . Consequently, either iterated cables of tight fibered knots are linearly independent in the smooth concordance group, or the slice–ribbon conjecture fails.

Introduction

A knot in the three-sphere
S^{3}
is called

if it bounds a smoothly embedded disk in the four-ball
B^{4}
, and
ribbon
if it bounds an immersed disk in
S^{3}
with only ribbon singularities. Since resolving the ribbon singularities naturally yields a smooth slicing disk, every ribbon knot is necessarily slice. One of the most significant open problems in low-dimensional topology, originally posed by Fox
[
14
, Problem 25]
, asks whether the converse is also true. This question is known as the
slice-ribbon conjecture
.

By applying the celebrated theorem of Casson and Gordon
[
8
]
, which states that a fibered knot in a homology sphere is homotopically ribbon

A knot in a homology sphere is called
homotopically ribbon
if it bounds a smooth disk in a homology four-ball, where the inclusion of the knot complement into the disk complement induces a surjective map on the fundamental group. Note that every ribbon knot in the three-sphere is homotopically ribbon.
if and only if its closed monodromy extends over a handlebody, many interesting non-ribbon knots have been constructed. For a large number of them, the sliceness problem remains unresolved to this day.

For instance, Miyazaki

is a knot and

states that the set of algebraic knots, which are in particular iterated cables of torus knots, is linearly independent in the smooth knot concordance group.

About ten years ago, Miyazaki’s result was strengthened by Baker

, who proved that the same holds for connected sums of fibered knots supporting the tight contact structure. For simplicity, we will refer to such knots as

In this article, we provide additional examples of fibered knots that are not ribbon, using the results of Casson and Gordon

Theorem 1.1

A connected sum of

Corollary 1.2
.

Either distinct iterated cables of tight fibered knots are linearly independent in the smooth knot concordance group, or the slice-ribbon conjecture is false.

Background on knot Floer complexes and immersed curves

We assume the reader is familiar with the knot Floer homology package of
[
48
]
and
[

; for survey articles on these invariants, we recommend
[

]

[
30

, and
[

\mathit{CFK}(K)
as a chain complex over the ring
\mathbb{F}[U,V]
. The knot Floer complex can be endowed with the additional structure of an endomorphism
\iota_{K}
, coming from involutive Heegaard Floer homology
[

]
.

If two knots
K
and
J
are concordant, then their knot Floer complexes
\mathit{CFK}(K)
and
\mathit{CFK}(J)
are
locally equivalent
: there exist graded chain maps

f\colon\mathit{CFK}(K)\to\mathit{CFK}(J)\qquad\textup{ and }\qquad g\colon\mathit{CFK}(J)\to\mathit{CFK}(K)

that induce isomorphisms in
U^{-1}H_{*}
.
We will also say that two knots
K
and
J
are
locally equivalent
if their knot Floer complexes are locally equivalent.

Local equivalence can be considered in both weaker and stronger settings. The weaker setting is to quotient by the ideal
(UV)
, and consider the knot Floer complex over
\mathbb{F}[U,V]/(UV)
, as in
[
12
, Definition 2.3]
; this is the same as
\varepsilon
-equivalence, considered in
[
29
, Section 4.1]
. The stronger setting is to require that the maps
f
and
g
homotopy-commute with
\iota_{K}
, as in
[
62
, Definition 2.4]
, which we will refer to as
\iota_{K}
-local equivalence
.

The local equivalence class of
\mathit{CFK}(K)
over
R=\mathbb{F}[U,V]
or
\mathbb{F}[U,V]/(UV)
admits a unique minimal (with respect to rank) representative, called the
connected complex over
R
, which has antecedents in the Pin(2)-monopole setting
[
61
, Section 2.5]
and the involutive Heegaard Floer setting
[
23
, Section 3]
; see also
[
65
, Section 6]
and
[
26
, Section 5]
.

As observed in
[
20
, Proposition 2]
, the connected complex over
\mathbb{F}[U,V]/(UV)
is exactly the data of the component
\gamma_{0}
coming from the immersed curve associated to
K

[
19
]
. Moreover, following
[
12
, Section 4]
, the connected complex over
\mathbb{F}[U,V]/(UV)
, or equivalently

, can be completely described by a finite sequence of nonzero integers, specifying its standard complex representative. In Section

, we will say that a knot

is locally equivalent to a sequence if its complex

is locally equivalent to the standard complex associated to that sequence.

Remark 2.1
.

The connected complex is a concordance invariant and in fact is an invariant of concordances in homology cobordisms

]
. It follows from the proof of

that

is an invariant of concordances in homology cobordisms as well.

Recall from

that the knot Floer complex

detects the genus of

. We say that

is
\gamma_{0}
-sharp
if the genus of

is the maximum Alexander grading (i.e., the maximum vertical coordinate) in the support of

Observe that the maximum Alexander grading in the support of

3.
Proof of the main theorem

We now prove the main theorem, whose statement we recall below.
The argument follows closely that of Baker
[
3
, Theorem 3]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

A connected sum of
\gamma_{0}
-sharp fibered knots is ribbon if and only if it is of the form
K\mathbin{\#}-K
.

Proof.

Assume that
J
is a
\gamma_{0}
-sharp fibered knot.
We will show that
J
is minimal with respect to homotopy ribbon concordance among all fibered knots in
S^{3}
, thereby generalizing
[
3
, Lemma 2]
.
Suppose that
J
is homotopy ribbon concordant to a fibered knot
J^{\prime}
in
S^{3}
.
Then, by
[
17
, Lemma 3.4]
, we have either
J=J^{\prime}
or
g(J)>g(J^{\prime})
.
Although the result is stated for ribbon concordance, the same argument applies to homotopy ribbon concordance.
By the assumption that
J
is
\gamma_{0}
-sharp and by Remark
2.1
, we have
g(J)\leq g(J^{\prime})
, which implies that
J=J^{\prime}
.

We now apply
[
40
, Theorem 5.5]
, which states that if a connected sum of knots that are minimal with respect to homotopy ribbon concordance among all fibered knots in homology spheres is ribbon, then it must be of the form
K\mathbin{\#}-K
. On the other hand, the remark following
[
40
, Lemma 1.3]
and the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 3]
state that the condition “among all fibered knots in homology spheres” can be relaxed to “among all fibered knots in
S^{3}
” using the Geometrization Conjecture
[
52
,
53
,
54
]
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

If we consider ribbon concordance instead of homotopy ribbon concordance,
then each
\gamma_{0}
-sharp fibered knot

is minimal not only among all fibered knots in

,
but also among all knots in
S^{3}
.
This follows from an observation of Miyazaki
[

]
, which builds on results of Silver
[

]
and Kochloukova
[

]
,
showing that if there is a ribbon concordance from

to

and

must also be fibered.

Next, we prove Corollary
1.2
. By iterated cables, we mean knots obtained by performing at least one nontrivial cabling operation. The statement is as follows:

Corollary 3.3
.

Either distinct iterated cables of tight fibered knots are linearly independent in the smooth knot concordance group, or the slice-ribbon conjecture is false.

Proof.

Assume the slice–ribbon conjecture is true.
Let
K_{1},K_{2},\dots,K_{n}
be distinct iterated cables of tight fibered knots, and suppose that

K:=c_{1}K_{1}\#c_{2}K_{2}\#\cdots\#c_{n}K_{n}

is slice, and hence ribbon, where
c_{i}K_{i}
denotes the
c_{i}
-fold connected sum of
K_{i}
if
c_{i}>0
, the
(-c_{i})
-fold connected sum of
-K_{i}
if
c_{i}<0
, and the unknot if
c_{i}=0
.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
c_{1}\geq 0
. It suffices to show that
c_{1}=0
.

Recall that a tight fibered knot
J
satisfies
\tau(J)=g(J)
by
[
38
, Theorem 4]
.
Moreover, by
[
28
, Corollary 4 and Theorem 2]
, we have
\varepsilon(J)=1
, and any iterated cable of
J
also satisfies
\varepsilon=1
.
In particular, for each
i
, we have
\varepsilon(K_{i})=1
and
\varepsilon(-K_{i})=-1

[
28
, Proposition 3.6]
. If
c_{1}>0
, then since
K
is ribbon and the knots
K_{1},\dots,K_{n}
are assumed to be distinct, Theorem
1.1
implies that
K_{1}=-K_{i}
for some
i
.
However, this leads to a contradiction, since
\varepsilon(K_{1})=1
while
\varepsilon(-K_{i})=-1
.
Therefore, we must have
c_{1}=0
.
∎

Finally, we prove Corollary
1.3
, restated below for convenience:

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
K
and
J
be distinct iterated cables of tight fibered knots. Then, for each pair of distinct integers
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
that are relatively prime to
p
, the knot

P(K,J,p,q_{1},q_{2}):=K_{p,q_{1}}\mathbin{\#}-K_{p,q_{2}}\mathbin{\#}J_{p,q_{2}}\mathbin{\#}-J_{p,q_{1}}

is algebraically slice but not ribbon.

Proof.

By Corollary
1.2
and the fact that
P(K,J,p,q_{1},q_{2})
is known to be algebraically slice by
[
37
]
, it suffices to show that
K_{p,q_{1}}
,
K_{p,q_{2}}
,
J_{p,q_{1}}
, and
J_{p,q_{2}}
are distinct. This will follow in particular from showing that
K_{p,q_{1}}
is not isotopic to any of
K_{p,q_{2}}
,
J_{p,q_{1}}
, or
J_{p,q_{2}}
and then applying symmetry.

The first case is immediate, since we are assuming
q_{1}\neq q_{2}
, which implies that
K_{p,q_{1}}
and
K_{p,q_{2}}
have distinct Alexander polynomials. For the remaining cases, recall that if
K_{p,q}
is isotopic to
J_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
for some
p,p^{\prime}>1
, then
K
is isotopic to
J
. This follows because any homeomorphism between the complements of the cabled knots induces a homeomorphism between the complements of
K
and
J
, by the uniqueness of the JSJ decomposition
[
32
,
33
]
, and knot complements determine knot types
[
16
]
. This completes the proof.
∎

The goal of this section is to prove Proposition

P(K,U,2,q_{1},q_{2}):=K_{2,q_{1}}\mathbin{\#}-K_{2,q_{2}}\mathbin{\#}T_{2,q_{2}}\mathbin{\#}-T_{2,q_{1}}.

In Section

\mathbb{F}[U,V]/(UV)
of

. The following proposition describes the effect of
(2,q)
-cabling on an L-space knot

Proposition 4.1
.

Let

be an L-space knot whose knot Floer complex is described by the integer sequence

(a_{1},b_{1},\dots,a_{n},b_{n}).

(1)

If

, then the knot Floer complex of the cable

, then the connected complex of the cable

Example 4.2
.

Consider the L-space knot

. The sequence description for the knot Floer complex of

(1,-3,2,-2,3,-1)

in terms of immersed curves.

\gamma
for

\gamma^{\prime}_{1}
. Now to the right of
\gamma^{\prime}_{1}
, take a second copy of the immersed curve
\gamma
, stretch it by a factor of two, and shift down by
q

\gamma^{\prime}_{2}
. Connect the right endpoint of
\gamma^{\prime}_{1}
to the left endpoint of
\gamma^{\prime}_{2}
by an arc

. Now compress the picture horizontally so that the pegs for
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
lie in a single vertical line. Let
\gamma_{i}
,

We first consider the case

. Consider
\gamma_{1}

a_{i}
into a sequence of right and left arcs of length one, where there are

a_{i}-1

\gamma

Next, we consider
\gamma_{1.5}

\gamma^{\prime}_{1.5}
lies in a vertical strip between the pegs of
\gamma^{\prime}_{1}
and
\gamma^{\prime}_{2}

.

Lastly, we consider
\gamma_{2}
, which follows as for
\gamma_{1}
, with the roles of

and

The case

is similar.
∎

It is straightforward to observe that
X,Y_{i,j},Z_{i,j},Y^{\prime}_{i,j},
and
Z^{\prime}_{i,j}
form a basis for
\mathit{CFK}(K\#T_{2,q})
.

Lemma 4.4
.

The subcomplex
X
is described by the sequence

(a_{1},b_{1},\dots,a_{n},b_{n},\underbrace{1,-1,1,-1,\dots,1,-1}_{q-1\textup{ entries}},-b_{n},-a_{n},\dots,-b_{1},-a_{1}),

and the map
\iota_{K}
on
X
is the basic involution.

Proof.

Computing the differential on
X
, one sees that
X
has the sequence description above.

We now consider
\iota_{K}
on
X
. Note that since
X
consists of elements of the form
x_{i}y_{j}
,
x_{i}y^{\prime}_{j}
, and
x^{\prime}_{i}y^{\prime}_{j}
where at least one of the subscripts is even, the
(\Phi\otimes\Psi)\circ(\iota_{1}\otimes\iota_{2})
term always vanishes. Hence we have that
\iota_{K}
sends the
\ell^{\textup{th}}
entry of (
4.1
) to the
\ell^{\textup{th}}
from the end, which is exactly the basic involution.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

The map
\iota_{K}
takes
Y_{i,j}
to
Y^{\prime}_{i,j}
and
Z_{i,j}
to
Z^{\prime}_{i,j}
.

Proof.

We first consider
Y_{i,j}
and
Y^{\prime}_{i,j}
. Recall that
i
and
j
are odd. On
Y_{i,j}
, we have

\displaystyle\iota_{K}(x_{i}y_{j})

\displaystyle=x^{\prime}_{i}y^{\prime}_{j}+b_{i}U^{b_{i}-1}x^{\prime}_{i+1}y^{\prime}_{j-1}

\displaystyle\iota_{K}(x_{i-1}y_{j}+x_{i}y_{j-1})

\displaystyle=x^{\prime}_{i-1}y^{\prime}_{j}+x^{\prime}_{i}y^{\prime}_{j-1}

\displaystyle\iota_{K}(x_{i}y_{j+1}+V^{b_{i}-1}x_{i+1}y_{j})

\displaystyle=x^{\prime}_{i}y^{\prime}_{j+1}+U^{b_{i}-1}x^{\prime}_{i+1}y^{\prime}_{j}

\displaystyle\iota_{K}(x_{i-1}y_{j+1}+V^{b_{i}-1}x_{i+1}y_{j-1})

\displaystyle=x^{\prime}_{i-1}y^{\prime}_{j+1}+U^{b_{i}-1}x^{\prime}_{i+1}y^{\prime}_{j-1},

and on
Y^{\prime}_{i,j}
, we have

\displaystyle\iota_{K}(x^{\prime}_{i}y^{\prime}_{j}+b_{i}U^{b_{i}-1}x^{\prime}_{i+1}y^{\prime}_{j-1})

\displaystyle=x_{i}y_{j}+2b_{i}V^{b_{i}-1}x_{i+1}y_{j-1}

\displaystyle\iota_{K}(x^{\prime}_{i-1}y^{\prime}_{j}+x^{\prime}_{i}y^{\prime}_{j-1})

\displaystyle=x_{i-1}y_{j}+x_{i}y_{j-1}

\displaystyle\iota_{K}(x^{\prime}_{i}y^{\prime}_{j+1}+U^{b_{i}-1}x^{\prime}_{i+1}y^{\prime}_{j})

\displaystyle=x_{i}y_{j+1}+U^{b_{i}-1}x_{i+1}y_{j}

\displaystyle\iota_{K}(x^{\prime}_{i-1}y^{\prime}_{j+1}+U^{b_{i}-1}x^{\prime}_{i+1}y^{\prime}_{j-1})

\displaystyle=x_{i-1}y_{j+1}+U^{b_{i}-1}x_{i+1}y_{j-1}.

Similarly, we consider
\iota_{K}
on
Z_{i,j}
and
Z^{\prime}_{i,j}
. Again, recall that
i
and
j
are odd. On
Z_{i,j}
, we have

\displaystyle\iota_{K}(x_{i}y^{\prime}_{j})

\displaystyle=x^{\prime}_{i}y_{j}+b_{i}U^{b_{i}-1}x^{\prime}_{i+1}y_{j-1}

\displaystyle\iota_{K}(x_{i-1}y^{\prime}_{j}+x_{i}y^{\prime}_{j+1})

\displaystyle=x^{\prime}_{i-1}y_{j}+x^{\prime}_{i}y_{j+1}

\displaystyle\iota_{K}(x_{i}y^{\prime}_{j-1}+V^{b_{i}-1}x_{i+1}y^{\prime}_{j})

\displaystyle=x^{\prime}_{i}y_{j-1}+U^{b_{i}-1}x^{\prime}_{i+1}y_{j}

\displaystyle\iota_{K}(x_{i-1}y^{\prime}_{j-1}+V^{b_{i}-1}x_{i+1}y^{\prime}_{j+1})

\displaystyle=x^{\prime}_{i-1}y_{j-1}+U^{b_{i}-1}x^{\prime}_{i+1}y_{j+1},

and on
Z^{\prime}_{i,j}
, we have

\displaystyle\iota_{K}(x^{\prime}_{i}y_{j}+b_{i}U^{b_{i}-1}x^{\prime}_{i+1}y_{j-1})

\displaystyle=x_{i}y^{\prime}_{j}

\displaystyle\iota_{K}(x^{\prime}_{i-1}y_{j}+x^{\prime}_{i}y_{j+1})

\displaystyle=x_{i-1}y^{\prime}_{j}+x_{i}y^{\prime}_{j+1}

\displaystyle\iota_{K}(x^{\prime}_{i}y_{j-1}+U^{b_{i}-1}x^{\prime}_{i+1}y_{j})

\displaystyle=x_{i}y^{\prime}_{j-1}+V^{b_{i}-1}x_{i+1}y^{\prime}_{j}

\displaystyle\iota_{K}(x^{\prime}_{i-1}y_{j-1}+U^{b_{i}-1}x^{\prime}_{i+1}y_{j+1})

\displaystyle=x_{i-1}y^{\prime}_{j-1}+V^{b_{i}-1}x_{i+1}y^{\prime}_{j+1},

which completes the proof.
∎

Proposition
4.3
now follows readily:

Proof of Proposition
4.3
.

It follows from Lemmas
4.4
and
4.5
that we have an
\iota_{K}
-equivariant splitting of
\mathit{CFK}(K\#T_{2,q})
with
X
as a direct summand. The result now follows from the description of
X
.

The statement for
K\#T_{2,-q}
follows from an analogous choice of basis; however, since
K\#T_{2,-q}
is not locally equivalent to an L-space knot, we do not have a notion of a basic involution.
∎

Finally, we are ready to prove Proposition
4.6
:

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
K
be a nontrivial L-space knot, and let
U
denote the unknot. Then, for each pair of distinct odd integers
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
, the knot

P(K,U,2,q_{1},q_{2}):=K_{2,q_{1}}\mathbin{\#}-K_{2,q_{2}}\mathbin{\#}T_{2,q_{2}}\mathbin{\#}-T_{2,q_{1}}

is algebraically slice but not ribbon.
Moreover, its knot Floer complex is locally equivalent to that of the unknot if and only if the pair
(q_{1},q_{2})
satisfies one of the following:

•

q_{1},q_{2}>4g(K)
,

•

0<q_{1},q_{2}<4g(K)
, or

•

q_{1},q_{2}<0
,

where
g(K)
is the Seifert genus of
K
. In particular, in all of the above cases, all concordance invariants derived from the knot Floer complex vanish.

Furthermore, when
q_{1},q_{2}>4g(K)
, the obstruction coming from the involutive knot Floer complex vanishes. When
q_{1},q_{2}<0
, the obstruction coming from the Ozsváth–Szabó
d
-invariant, together with the Hendricks–Manolescu
\overline{d}
- and
\underline{d}
-invariants of the double branched covers, vanish.

Proof.

We will first show that the knot Floer complexes of
K_{2,q_{1}}\#T_{2,q_{2}}
and
K_{2,q_{2}}\#T_{2,q_{1}}
are locally equivalent if and only if the pair
(q_{1},q_{2})
satisfies one of the conditions listed above.

For the “if” direction, let
K
be an L-space knot described by the integer sequence

(a_{1},b_{1},\dots,a_{n},b_{n}).

Suppose that
q_{1},q_{2}>4g(K)
. Observe that if
q_{1}>4g(K)
, then
K_{2,q_{1}}
is an L-space knot described by the sequence

(a^{\prime}_{1},b^{\prime}_{1},\dots,a^{\prime}_{n},b^{\prime}_{n},\underbrace{1,-1,1,-1,\dots,1,-1}_{q_{1}-4g(K)-1\textup{ entries}},-b^{\prime}_{n},-a^{\prime}_{n},\dots,-b^{\prime}_{1},-a^{\prime}_{1})

where
a^{\prime}_{i},b^{\prime}_{i}
are as in Proposition
4.1
(
1
). Since this sequence satisfies the hypothesis of Proposition
4.3
, we have that
K_{2,q_{1}}\#T_{2,q_{2}}
is
\iota_{K}
-locally equivalent to

(a^{\prime}_{1},b^{\prime}_{1},\dots,a^{\prime}_{n},b^{\prime}_{n},\underbrace{1,-1,1,-1,\dots,1,-1}_{q_{1}+q_{2}-4g(K)-2\textup{ entries}},-b^{\prime}_{n},-a^{\prime}_{n},\dots,-b^{\prime}_{1},-a^{\prime}_{1}).

Reversing the roles of
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
gives the desired result.

The non-involutive proof for
q_{1},q_{2}<4g(K)
is similar. Indeed, let
K
be an L-space knot described by the integer sequence

(a_{1},b_{1},\dots,a_{n},b_{n}).

The connected complex of
K_{2,q_{1}}
is given by

(a^{\prime}_{1},b^{\prime}_{1},\dots,a^{\prime}_{n},b^{\prime}_{n},\underbrace{-1,1,-1,1,\dots,-1,1}_{4g(K)-q_{1}-1\textup{ entries}},-b^{\prime}_{n},-a^{\prime}_{n},\dots,-b^{\prime}_{1},-a^{\prime}_{1}),

where
a^{\prime}_{i},b^{\prime}_{i}
are as in Proposition
4.1
(
2
).

Suppose that
0<q_{2}<4g(K)
. The complex for
K_{2,q_{1}}
is given by

(4.2)

(a^{\prime}_{1},b^{\prime}_{1},\dots,a^{\prime}_{n},b^{\prime}_{n},\underbrace{-1,1,-1,1,\dots,-1,1}_{4g(K)-q_{1}-1\textup{ entries}},-b^{\prime}_{n},-a^{\prime}_{n},\dots,-b^{\prime}_{1},-a^{\prime}_{1})

while the complex for
T_{2,q_{2}}
is given by

(4.3)

(\underbrace{1,-1,1,-1,\dots,1,-1}_{q_{2}-1\textup{ entries}}).

If
q_{2}\geq 4g(K)-q_{1}
, then the number of consecutive
\pm 1
’s in the middle of (
4.2
) is at most equal to that in (
4.3
), effectively annihilating
4g(J)-q_{1}-1
of the entries, yielding a middle sequence of length
q_{2}-1-(4g(K)-q_{1}-1)
and so the connected complex of
K_{2,q_{1}}\#T_{2,q_{2}}
is

(a^{\prime}_{1},b^{\prime}_{1},\dots,a^{\prime}_{n},b^{\prime}_{n},\underbrace{1,-1,1,-1,\dots,1,-1}_{q_{1}+q_{2}-4g(K)\textup{ entries}},-b^{\prime}_{n},-a^{\prime}_{n},\dots,-b^{\prime}_{1},-a^{\prime}_{1}).

On the other hand, if
0<q_{2}<4g(K)-q_{1}
, then the middle sequence of
\pm 1
in (
4.2
) is longer than the sequence in (
4.3
), yielding the connected complex of
K_{2,q_{1}}\#T_{2,q_{2}}
as

(a^{\prime}_{1},b^{\prime}_{1},\dots,a^{\prime}_{n},b^{\prime}_{n},\underbrace{-1,1,-1,1,\dots,-1,1}_{4g(K)-q_{1}-q_{2}\textup{ entries}},-b^{\prime}_{n},-a^{\prime}_{n},\dots,-b^{\prime}_{1},-a^{\prime}_{1}).

Now suppose that
q_{2}<0
. Then the connected complex of
K_{2,q_{1}}\#T_{2,q_{2}}
is given by

(a^{\prime}_{1},b^{\prime}_{1},\dots,a^{\prime}_{n},b^{\prime}_{n},\underbrace{-1,1,-1,1,\dots,-1,1}_{4g(K)-q_{1}-q_{2}-2\textup{ entries}},-b^{\prime}_{n},-a^{\prime}_{n},\dots,-b^{\prime}_{1},-a^{\prime}_{1}).

By reversing the roles of
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
, we see that if
0<q_{1},q_{2}<4g(K)
, or if
q_{1},q_{2}<0
, then
K_{2,q_{1}}\#T_{2,q_{2}}
and
K_{2,q_{2}}\#T_{2,q_{1}}
are locally equivalent, as desired.

For the “only if” direction, first consider the case where
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
have different signs. As mentioned in the introduction, applying the cabling formula
[
28
]
for the
\tau
-invariant allows us to conclude that the
\tau
of
P(K,U,2,q_{1},q_{2})
is nonzero. Thus, it suffices to consider the case where both
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
are positive. This case follows from comparing the previous computations, noting that
b^{\prime}_{n}
from Proposition
4.1
equals
2b_{n}-1
if
q>4g(K)
, and
2b_{n}
if
q<4g(K)
.

Lastly, we now assume that the odd integers
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
satisfy
q_{1},q_{2}<0
, and consider the double branched cover of
P(K,U,2,q_{1},q_{2})
, which is homeomorphic to

\Sigma_{2}(P(K,U,2,q_{1},q_{2})):=S^{3}_{q_{1}}(K\#K^{r})\mathbin{\#}-S^{3}_{q_{2}}(K\#K^{r})\#S^{3}_{q_{2}}(U)\mathbin{\#}-S^{3}_{q_{1}}(U),

where
K^{r}
denotes the reverse of
K
,
U
is the unknot, and
S^{3}_{m}(J)
denotes the
m
-framed Dehn surgery on a knot
J
.
The Ozsváth–Szabó
d
-invariant is additive under connected sum and behaves naturally under orientation reversal
[
45
]
. Moreover, the
d
-invariant of a positive Dehn surgery is determined by the knot’s
V_{i}
-sequence
[
56
,
42
]
, and each
V_{i}
vanishes when the
\nu^{+}
-invariant vanishes
[
27
]
. It is also known that
\nu^{+}(-(K\#K^{r}))=\nu^{+}(-(U))=0
(see e.g.
[
4
, Theorem 1.5]
).
In particular, for each negative integer
m
, there is a natural identification between the spin
c
structures of
S^{3}_{m}(K\#K^{r})
and those of
S^{3}_{m}(U)
, under which the corresponding
d
-invariants coincide. Hence, the same holds for the
d
-invariants of
\Sigma_{2}(P(K,U,2,q_{1},q_{2}))
and those of

S^{3}_{q_{1}}(U)\mathbin{\#}-S^{3}_{q_{2}}(U)\#S^{3}_{q_{2}}(U)\mathbin{\#}-S^{3}_{q_{1}}(U),

which bounds a spin rational homology ball. This implies that the obstruction coming from the
d
-invariant vanishes. Furthermore, the vanishing of the obstruction given by the
\overline{d}
- and
\underline{d}
-invariants follows from
[
24
, Theorem 1.6]
, which states that the local equivalence class of the
\iota
-complex
(\mathit{CF}^{-},\iota)
associated to
S^{3}_{q_{1}}(J)
coincides with that of
S^{3}_{q_{2}}(J)
, with respect to the unique spin structure, for any choice of knot
J
, as long as the signs of
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
agree. This concludes the proof.
∎

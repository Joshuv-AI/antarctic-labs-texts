Central Message

In a patient with HOCM, a relatively thin septum, and severe MR, a complex MVr avoided the implantation of a prosthetic valve into an incidentally infected field.

In patients with hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy (HOCM), systolic anterior motion (SAM) of the mitral valve can cause severe left ventricular outflow tract obstruction (LVOTO) in the absence of significant septal hypertrophy. Historically, mitral valve replacement (MVR) has been considered necessary in these cases; however, we have found that mitral valve repair (MVr) using pathoanatomically guided techniques, with or without limited septal myectomy, can effectively relieve LVOTO and avoid MVR.1 We present a case wherein these reparative techniques were particularly valuable to avoid implantation of a prosthetic valve into a field with incidentally discovered Borrelia burgdorferi infective endocarditis (IE). Written consent for publication was obtained. Institutional review board approval was waived.

Case Summary

A 67-year-old female patient with HOCM and new-onset exertional dyspnea was referred to our multidisciplinary Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy Center for surgical evaluation. Preoperative transesophageal echocardiography demonstrated multifactorial LVOTO with septal hypertrophy (maximum diameter 14 mm) and mitral valve SAM, with resting and provoked gradients of 66 and 102 mm Hg, respectively. After median sternotomy, cardiopulmonary bypass was established with aortic and bicaval cannulation, and the heart was arrested with antegrade and retrograde Buckberg cardioplegia. A left atriotomy was performed through the interatrial groove, and the mitral valve was inspected, revealing bileaflet myxomatous degeneration and a large AML perforation suspicious for IE (Figure 2). First, all infected- and necrotic-appearing tissues were debrided and sent for histopathologic and microbiologic examination. A patch of autologous pericardium was harvested to reconstruct the AML defect (Figure 3). Next, triangular resection and sliding posterior valvuloplasty were performed to reduce the posterior mitral leaflet height, and a 35-mm flexible annuloplasty band was placed. A limited transaortic myectomy was then performed (3 g) and a redundant segment of the free margin of the AML was plicated (Figure 4). Last, a 32-mm rigid tricuspid annuloplasty band was placed. Initial postoperative course was uneventful. Blood and tissue cultures remained negative, an Infectious Diseases consultant was engaged, and empiric broad-spectrum antibiotics were administered. On day 5, mitral valve polymerase chain reaction analysis identified B burgdorferi consistent with Lyme IE. She was discharged home on 6 weeks of intravenous ceftriaxone and oral doxycycline. At 6-month follow-up, she remained asymptomatic. Echocardiography demonstrated normal biventricular function, no LVOTO (peak gradient 11 mm Hg with Valsalva) or SAM, trivial MR with mean gradient of 4 mm Hg, and trivial TR.

Discussion

Our case highlights 2 key points regarding the management of complex mitral valve pathology in patients with HOCM. First, in those with a relatively thin septum, durable relief of LVOTO and MVr can be achieved by following key surgical principles and using pathoanatomically guided techniques. Second, comprehensive histopathologic and microbiologic analysis with polymerase chain reaction is important for unusual valve pathology concerning for IE, because negative blood and tissue culture results are not uncommon.

Our approach to MVr in patients with LVOTO and mild septal hypertrophy aims to counteract abnormal flow dynamics and restore a posteriorly positioned coaptation zone.1, 2, 3 In this patient, a tailored MVr strategy comprehensively addressed multifactorial severe MR and LVOTO.1,2 The perforated AML was reconstructed with an autologous pericardial patch to leverage native tissue and mitigate infection risk. Triangular resection and sliding posterior valvuloplasty reduced the height of all posterior leaflet segments to <15 mm and shifted the coaptation zone posteriorly. The annulus was stabilized with a large annuloplasty band to avoid aggressive downsizing and SAM. Limited septal myectomy and plication of the redundant AML free margin through a transaortic approach eliminated abnormal SAM-inducing flow vortices. Integrating these techniques, a durable MVr was achieved, avoiding implantation of a prosthetic valve into an infected field and preserving the well-recognized long-term benefits of MVr over MVR.

In the absence of classic IE symptoms, recognition of unusual mitral valve pathology (ie, leaflet perforation) raised concern for infectious etiology, prompting comprehensive microbiological testing.4,5 It is possible that turbulent flow caused by LVOTO contributed to valvular trauma and endothelial damage and created a nidus for the development of IE, and accordingly, relief of LVOTO with restoration of normal flow dynamics was key to comprehensively treatment of this pathology.E1 Although a tailored MVr strategy acutely addressed this patient's MR and LVOTO, identification and treatment of the causal organism was equally important to eradicate infection and achieve durable results.E2,E3

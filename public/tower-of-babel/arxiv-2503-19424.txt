A linear, unconditionally stable, second order decoupled method for the nematic liquid crystal flows with SAV approach
Ruonan Cao † and Nianyu Yi ‡,§

Abstract
. In this paper, we present a second order, linear, fully decoupled, and unconditionally energy stable scheme for solving the Erickson-Leslie model. This approach integrates the pressure correction method with a scalar auxiliary variable technique. We rigorously demonstrate the unconditional energy stability of the proposed scheme. Furthermore, we present several numerical experiments to validate its convergence order, stability, and computational efficiency.



1.
Introduction

Liquid crystals represent the fourth state of matter, distinct from gases, liquids, and solids.
They are four types of liquid crystals: nematic, smectic, cholesteric, and disotic, which are differentiated by the alignments of the molecular. Furthermore, they also can be categorized into thermotropic or lyotropic depending on their production conditions. Nematic liquid crystals are a common type of the thermotropic class.

Ericksen
[
3
,
4
]
and Leslie
[
10
,
9
]
developed the hydrodynamic theory of the nematic liquid crystals. They proposed the Ericksen-Leslie model, which consists of an convective harmonic map heat flow equation for the evolution of the director field coupled with an incompressible Navier-Stokes equation for the velocity and the pressure with a certain additional stress tensor.
Due to the complexity of the original Ericksen-Leslie model with some reaction-coupling terms, Lin
[
11
]
proposed the following simplified Ericksen-Leslie model:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathrm{\textbf{d}}}{\partial t}+\mathrm{\textbf{u}}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{\textbf{d}}-\gamma\Delta\mathrm{\textbf{d}}-\gamma\lvert\nabla\mathrm{\textbf{d}}\rvert^{2}\mathrm{\textbf{d}}=0,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathrm{\textbf{u}}}{\partial t}+\mathrm{\textbf{u}}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{\textbf{u}}-\nu\Delta\mathrm{\textbf{u}}+\nabla p+\lambda\nabla\cdot\left(\left(\nabla\mathrm{\textbf{d}}\right)^{t}\nabla\mathrm{\textbf{d}}\right)=0,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mathrm{\textbf{u}}=0,

(1.4)

\displaystyle|\mathrm{\textbf{d}}|=1,

in
\Omega_{T}=\Omega\times(0,T]
, where
\Omega\subset R^{d}\ (d=2,3)
is a bounded domain with a Lipschitz continuous boundary
\partial\Omega
. The unknowns are the velocity field
u
, pressure
p
, and director field
d
that characterizes the orientation of liquid crystal molecules.
The positive physical constant parameters are the fluid viscosity
\nu
, elasticity constant
\lambda
and relaxation time constant
\gamma
.

Let
\|\cdot\|
denotes the
L^{2}
norm of scalars, vectors or tensors, and
(\cdot,\cdot)
means the
L^{2}
inner product. Taking the inner products of (
1.1
) with
\lambda\left(-\Delta\mathrm{\textbf{d}}-\lvert\nabla\mathrm{\textbf{d}}\rvert^{2}\mathrm{\textbf{d}}\right)
, (
1.2
) with
u
, and summing up the two equations with some algebraic manipulation, we obtain the following energy dissipation law

\frac{d}{dt}W_{s}(\mathrm{\textbf{u}},\mathrm{\textbf{d}})=-\nu\lVert\nabla u\rVert^{2}-\lambda\gamma\|\Delta\mathrm{\textbf{d}}+|\nabla\mathrm{\textbf{d}}|^{2}\mathrm{\textbf{d}}\|^{2}\leq 0.

where

W_{s}(\mathrm{\textbf{u}},\mathrm{\textbf{d}})=\frac{1}{2}\lVert u\rVert^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\lVert\nabla\mathrm{\textbf{d}}\rVert^{2}.

Lin and Liu
[
12
]
established its well-posedness of the simplified Ericksen-Leslie model (
1.1
)-(
1.4
). Indeed, they prove global existence of weak solutions as well as local existence of strong solutions to system (
1.1
)-(
1.4
).
For numerical approximation we refer to
[
1
,
2
,
14
]
.
Du, Guo and Shen
[
2
]
proposed a Fourier-spectral method and derived an error estimation, which demonstrated the spectral accuracy of the proposed method.
In
[
14
]
, Lin and Liu presented the
C^{0}
finite element method for
2D
hydrodynamic liquid crystal model.

The Ginzburg-Landau penalty function
[
12
]
is commonly used to relax the constraint
|\mathrm{\textbf{d}}|=1
, then obtain a modified Ericksen-Leslie model:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathrm{\textbf{d}}}{\partial t}+\mathrm{\textbf{u}}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{\textbf{d}}+\gamma\left(-\Delta\mathrm{\textbf{d}}+f_{\varepsilon}\left(\mathrm{\textbf{d}}\right)\right)=0,\;|\mathrm{\textbf{d}}|\leq 1,

(1.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathrm{\textbf{u}}}{\partial t}+\mathrm{\textbf{u}}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{\textbf{u}}-\nu\Delta\mathrm{\textbf{u}}+\nabla p+\lambda\nabla\cdot\left(\left(\nabla\mathrm{\textbf{d}}\right)^{t}\nabla\mathrm{\textbf{d}}\right)=0,

(1.7)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mathrm{\textbf{u}}=0,

where
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
is the penalty parameter.
Here, the Ginzburg-Landau penalty function
f_{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{\textbf{d}})
is defined as
f_{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{\textbf{d}}):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(|\mathrm{\textbf{d}}|^{2}-1\right)\mathrm{\textbf{d}}
, which is the gradient of the Ginzburg-Landau potential function
F_{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{\textbf{d}})=\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}\left(|\mathrm{\textbf{d}}|^{2}-1\right)^{2}
, i.e.
f_{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{\textbf{d}})=\nabla_{\mathrm{\textbf{d}}}F_{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{\textbf{d}})
.

Multiplying (
1.5
) by
\lambda\left(-\Delta\mathrm{\textbf{d}}+f_{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{\textbf{d}})\right)
and (
1.6
) by
u
, after the integration in
\Omega
, we obtain the following energy law:

(1.8)

\frac{d}{dt}W(\mathrm{\textbf{u}},\mathrm{\textbf{d}})=-\nu\lVert\nabla u\rVert^{2}-\lambda\gamma\|-\Delta\mathrm{\textbf{d}}+f_{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{\textbf{d}})\|^{2},

where

W=\frac{1}{2}\lVert u\rVert^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\lVert\nabla\mathrm{\textbf{d}}\rVert^{2}+\lambda\int_{\Omega}F_{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{\textbf{d}})\;d\mathrm{\textbf{x}}

represents the total energy of the system (
1.5
)-(
1.7
), consisting of the kinetic energy
W_{kin}=\frac{1}{2}\lVert u\rVert^{2}
, the elastic energy
W_{ela}=\frac{\lambda}{2}\lVert\nabla\mathrm{\textbf{d}}\rVert^{2}
, and the penalty energy
W_{pen}=\lambda\int_{\Omega}F_{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{\textbf{d}})\;d\mathrm{\textbf{x}}
.

Liu and Walkington
[
13
]
developed a finite element scheme for (
1.5
)-(
1.7
) and simulated the dynamical behaviors of defects in liquid crystals.
Becker and Feng et al.
[
1
]
proposed two fully discretized schemes: one for the system (
1.5
)-(
1.7
), which is unconditionally stable and satisfies the discrete energy law, and the other for direct discretization of system (
1.1
)-(
1.4
).
Girault and Guillén-González
[
7
]
investigated a fully discrete scheme based on
C^{0}
finite elements in space and a semi-implicit Euler scheme in time. They established its unconditional stability and convergence theories.
In
[
5
]
, Guillén-González and Gutiérrez-Santacreu introduced a linear, unconditionally stable, semi-implicit scheme that satisfies the energy law for the modified system (
1.5
)-(
1.7
).
Some further contributions are found in
[
15
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
.

In view of the energy decay property of the modified Ericksen-Leslie model (
1.5
)-(
1.7
), it is of advantage to design numerical schemes that preserve the discrete version of energy dissipation law (
1.8
)
This paper aims to develop a linear, unconditionally energy stable, and fully decoupled strategy for the modified Ericksen-Leslie model.
The main difficulty in designing such efficient numerical scheme comes from the strongly nonlinear and coupled terms.
We use the Lagrange multipliers
[
5
]
to linearize the nonlinear term and the rotational pressure correction (PC) method
[
6
]
to decouple the velocity and pressure.
Furthermore, we implement the scalar auxiliary variable (SAV)
[
8
]
decoupling strategy to handle the convective terms in the system, enhancing the overall efficiency and stability of the numerical scheme.
We then apply the second-order backward differentiation formula (BDF2) to the modified Ericksen-Leslie model (
1.5
)-(
1.7
) and establish the unconditional energy stability of proposed schemes.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we develop a semi-discrete scheme based on the pressure correction method and the scalar auxiliary variable approach (PCSAV), prove it unconditional energy stability, and describe an efficient implementation. Section
3
introduces a PCSAV method with explicit time-stepping scheme for convection term (PCSAV-ECT). In Section
4
, we present several numerical experiments to verify the accuracy, stability and computational efficiency of two schemes.

2.
PCSAV method for the modified Ericksen-Leslie model

In this section, we propose a linear and decoupled semi-discrete scheme for the modified Ericksen-Leslie model.
A scalar auxiliary variable is introduced to reformulate the equations (
1.5
)-(
1.7
) into an equivalent system, then we apply BDF2 scheme with the pressure correction method to the equivalent system to obtain the semi-discrete scheme.
We prove that the proposed scheme satisfies the energy dissipation law at the discrete level.
In the end, we present a detailed procedure to efficiently implement the scheme.

To simplify the analysis, we assume the following boundary conditions:

(2.1)

\mathrm{\textbf{d}}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\quad\partial_{n}\mathrm{\textbf{u}}|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

With only minor modifications the analysis can be extended to other boundary conditions.
The initial conditions are prescribed as follows:

(2.2)

\mathrm{\textbf{d}}\left(\mathrm{\textbf{x}},0\right)=\mathrm{\textbf{d}}_{0}\left(\mathrm{\textbf{x}}\right),\quad\mathrm{\textbf{u}}\left(\mathrm{\textbf{x}},0\right)=\mathrm{\textbf{u}}_{0}\left(\mathrm{\textbf{x}}\right),\quad\text{for}\;\mathrm{\textbf{x}}\in\Omega,

where
\mathrm{\textbf{u}}^{0}:\Omega_{T}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathrm{\textbf{d}}^{0}:\Omega_{T}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
are given functions.

Notice that the elastic tensor

1.6

(2.3)

where
\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\mathrm{\textbf{d}}|^{2}+F_{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{\textbf{d}})
can be incorporated as part of the pressure.
Let
\mathrm{\textbf{w}}=-\Delta\mathrm{\textbf{d}}+f_{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{\textbf{d}})
, the modified Ericksen-Leslie model (
1.5
)-(
1.7

(2.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathrm{\textbf{d}}}{\partial t}+\mathrm{\textbf{u}}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{\textbf{d}}+\gamma\mathrm{\textbf{w}}=0,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathrm{\textbf{w}}=-\Delta\mathrm{\textbf{d}}+f_{\varepsilon}\left(\mathrm{\textbf{d}}\right),

(2.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathrm{\textbf{u}}}{\partial t}+\mathrm{\textbf{u}}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{\textbf{u}}-\nu\Delta\mathrm{\textbf{u}}+\nabla p-\lambda\left(\nabla\mathrm{\textbf{d}}\right)^{t}\mathrm{\textbf{w}}=0,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mathrm{\textbf{u}}=0.

Let

, then

and the total energy is

(2.8)

W=\frac{1}{2}\lVert u\rVert^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\lVert\nabla\mathrm{\textbf{d}}\rVert^{2}+\lambda\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}q^{2}\;d\mathrm{\textbf{x}}.

Define a scalar auxiliary variable

by

s(t)=\exp(-\frac{t}{T}).

The system (
2.4
)-(
2.7

(2.9)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathrm{\textbf{d}}}{\partial t}+\frac{s\left(t\right)}{\exp\left(-\frac{t}{T}\right)}\mathrm{\textbf{u}}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{\textbf{d}}+\gamma\mathrm{\textbf{w}}=0,

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mathrm{\textbf{w}}=-\Delta\mathrm{\textbf{d}}+q\mathrm{\textbf{d}},

(2.11)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial q}{\partial t}=\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\mathrm{\textbf{d}}\right)^{t}\frac{\partial\mathrm{\textbf{d}}}{\partial t},

(2.12)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathrm{\textbf{u}}}{\partial t}+\left(\mathrm{\textbf{u}}\cdot\nabla\right)\mathrm{\textbf{u}}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla\cdot\mathrm{\textbf{u}}\right)\mathrm{\textbf{u}}-\nu\Delta\mathrm{\textbf{u}}+\nabla p=\lambda\frac{s\left(t\right)}{\exp\left(-\frac{t}{T}\right)}\left(\nabla\mathrm{\textbf{d}}\right)^{t}\mathrm{\textbf{w}},

(2.13)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mathrm{\textbf{u}}=0,

(2.14)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial s}{\partial t}=-\frac{s}{T}+\frac{1}{\exp\left(-\frac{t}{T}\right)}\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathrm{\textbf{u}}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{\textbf{d}}\right)\mathrm{\textbf{w}}-\left(\left(\nabla\mathrm{\textbf{d}}\right)^{t}\mathrm{\textbf{w}}\right)\mathrm{\textbf{u}}\;d\mathrm{\textbf{x}}.

Here, the additional term
\frac{1}{\exp\left(-\frac{t}{T}\right)}\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathrm{\textbf{u}}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{\textbf{d}}\right)\mathrm{\textbf{w}}-\left(\left(\nabla\mathrm{\textbf{d}}\right)^{t}\mathrm{\textbf{w}}\right)\mathrm{\textbf{u}}\;d\mathrm{\textbf{x}}
in (
2.14

Taking the inner products of (

The scheme (

Hengqin-RA-v1: Advanced Large Language Model for Diagnosis and Treatment of Rheumatoid Arthritis with Dataset based Traditional Chinese Medicine
Yishen Liu, Shengda Luo, Zishao Zhong, Tongtong Wu, Jianguo Zhang, Peiyao Ou, Yong Liang, Liang Liu, Hudan Pan

Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) primarily trained on English texts, often face biases and inaccuracies in Chinese contexts. Their limitations are pronounced in fields like Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM), where cultural and clinical subtleties are vital, further hindered by a lack of domain-specific data, such as rheumatoid arthritis (RA). To address these issues, this paper introduces Hengqin-RA-v1 , the first large language model specifically tailored for TCM with a focus on diagnosing and treating RA. We also present HQ-GCM-RA-C1 , a comprehensive RA-specific dataset curated from ancient Chinese medical literature, classical texts, and modern clinical studies. This dataset empowers Hengqin-RA-v1 to deliver accurate and culturally informed responses, effectively bridging the gaps left by general-purpose models. Extensive experiments demonstrate that Hengqin-RA-v1 outperforms state-of-the-art models, even surpassing the diagnostic accuracy of TCM practitioners in certain cases.

During the training of
Hengqin-RA-v1

, raw data comprised original medical record corpora. Input prompts were designed based on the principles of ”four diagnostic information extraction, theory, method, prescription, and medicine.” These prompts enabled the application of large models to organize raw data into structured medical records. Building on the structured medical records from
Hengqin-RA-v1

Medical logic was extracted from both ”single medical records” and ”multiple medical records.” Analyzing single records is vital, as each is unique and provides detailed insights into dialectical treatment strategies tailored to specific cases. These records encompass comprehensive patient information and the complete diagnosis and treatment process by the physician.

Figure 3:
The sliding window approach enhances corpus context logic by extracting BibTeX entries via pdf2bib, aligning journal names using fuzzy matching, and organizing paper IDs, journal names, and categories into a structured table for improved data context.

We propose a five-step process for handling journal data. First, a target journal list is read, and BibTeX entries are extracted using pdf2bib. Journal names are then isolated and matched to the target list using fuzzy matching with thefuzz library to ensure accuracy despite inconsistencies. Matched journal names, paper IDs, and categories are compiled into a structured table. After classification, journals are divided into blocks, generating 270,000 dialogue sets. A sliding window approach is applied to extract ”within” and ”between” segment relationships, with step sizes proportional to journal importance (e.g., step size 1 for category 1 journals). To offset the higher cost of dialogue set generation, question-answer pairs are added, preserving the original cost while capturing detailed segment relationships.

Finally, we introduce an instance-oriented retrieval enhancement method to retain the base model’s knowledge. This approach integrates external examples with model generation capabilities, dynamically providing context and examples to improve accuracy and relevance. By incorporating RA patient records or similar cases, it enhances diagnostic recommendations and adapts to specific tasks and domains. Additionally, it aids doctors in teaching medical students by combining examples with generation capabilities to demonstrate diagnostic reasoning and treatment plans.

Our approach to data input differs from other large TCM models
(
Zhang et al. 2023

Experimental Results

As shown in
5
, the TCM recommendations generated by
Hengqin-RA-v1
demonstrate an ability to summarize patient symptoms and provide preliminary diagnoses, effectively integrating modern medical data with TCM theory. For example, the model identifies damp-heat symptoms like a ”greasy yellow and dark” complexion and thick, greasy tongue coating, linking TCM concepts such as ”heat syndrome” and ”damp-heat” with laboratory markers like CRP and ALT levels. However, it lacks coverage of auscultation and palpation, omitting key details like odor characteristics and pulse information. While inspection and questioning provide basic insights, they fail to capture personalized nuances.

The recommended Chinese medicines, such as ”Bupleurum” and ”Coptis chinensis,” align with damp-heat syndrome differentiation but lack clear explanations for dosage adjustments or compatibility logic, resulting in overly broad and generalized recommendations. Additionally, the language, while fluent, often lacks fine-grained TCM-specific terminology, reducing its precision.

Improvements should focus on expanding coverage of the four diagnostic methods, especially auscultation and palpation, enhancing syndrome differentiation, and personalizing treatment recommendations. Better integration of modern medical data and optimization of language expression to reduce generalizations and emphasize professionalism are also essential. While the model demonstrates a logical framework for generating TCM recommendations, significant improvements are needed in diagnostic comprehensiveness, recommendation depth, and personalization.

Conclusion

In this paper, we introduce
Hengqin-RA-v1
, the first Chinese large language model for diagnosing and treating rheumatoid arthritis using traditional Chinese medicine (TCM). The model generates personalized, expert-validated treatment plans that surpass the precision and specificity of other Chinese and English LLMs, such as GPT and Gemini. We also present
HQ-GCM-RA-C1
, the first TCM corpus dataset for rheumatoid arthritis, designed for training LLMs and developing specialized report generation models. This dataset complements English medical models, reducing bias and promoting fairness, while advancing corpus completeness and equitable representation in Chinese and English LLMs.

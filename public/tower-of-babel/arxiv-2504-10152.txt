NEO BALCOBALANCING NUMBERS
Ahmet Tekcan

Abstract
. In this work, we defined neo balcobalancing numbers, neo Lucas-balcobalancing numbers, neo balcobalancers and neo Lucas-balcobalancers and derived the general terms of these numbers in terms of balancing numbers. Conversely we deduced the general terms of balancing, cobalancing, Lucas-balancing and Lucas-cobalancing numbers in terms of these numbers. We also deduced some relations on Binet formulas, recurrence relations, relationship with Pell, Pell-Lucas, triangular, square triangular numbers, Pythagorean triples and Cassini identities. We also formulate the sum of first n n -terms of these numbers and obtained some formulas for the sums of Pell, Pell-Lucas, balancing and Lucas-cobalancing numbers in terms of these numbers.

2.
Neo Balcobalancing Numbers

In
[
4
]
, Chailangka and Pakapongpun said that a positive integer
n

is called a neo balancing number if the Diophantine equation

(2.1)

1+2+\cdots+(n-1)=(n-1)+(n-0)+(n+1)+(n+2)+\cdots+(n+r)

holds for some positive integer
r
which is called neo balancer.

Later in
[
34
]
, Tekcan and Yıldız defined neo cobalancing
numbers. They said that a positive integer
n
is called a neo cobalancing
number if the Diophantine equation

(2.2)

1+2+\cdots+n=(n-1)+(n-0)+(n+1)+(n+2)+\cdots+(n+r)

holds for some positive integer
r
which is called neo cobalancer
corresponding to
n
.

As we did in
[
30
,
31
]
, if we sum both sides of (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), then we get

(2.3)

\displaystyle 1+2+\cdots+(n-1)+1+2+\cdots+n=

\displaystyle 2[(n-1)+(n-0)+(n+1)+(n+2)+\cdots+(n+r)]

and said that a positive integer
n
is called a neo balcobalancing number
if the Diophantine equation in (
2.3
) verified for some positive
integer
r
which is called neo balcobalancer.

From (
2.3
), we get

(2.4)

r=\frac{-2n-1+\sqrt{8n^{2}-12n+9}}{2}

and

(2.5)

n=2+r+\sqrt{2r^{2}+5r+2}.

Let
B_{n}^{neobc}
denote the
n^{\text{th}}
neo balcobalancing number and
let
R_{n}^{neobc}
denote the
n^{\text{th}}
neo balcobalancer. Then from (
2.4
),
B_{n}^{neobc}
is a neo balcobalancing number if and only if
8(B_{n}^{neobc})^{2}-12B_{n}^{neobc}+9
is a perfect square. Thus

(2.6)

C_{n}^{neobc}=\sqrt{8(B_{n}^{neobc})^{2}-12B_{n}^{neobc}+9}

is an integer which is called the
n^{\text{th}}
neo Lucas-balcobalancing
number and from (
2.5
),
R_{n}^{neobc}
is a neo balcobalancer if
and only if
2(R_{n}^{neobc})^{2}+5R_{n}^{neobc}+2
is a perfect square.
Thus

(2.7)

CR_{n}^{neobc}=\sqrt{2(R_{n}^{neobc})^{2}+5R_{n}^{neobc}+2}

is an integer which is called the
n^{\text{th}}
neo Lucas-balcobalancer
number.

In order to determine the general terms of neo balcobalancing numbers, neo
Lucas-balcobalancing numbers, neo balcobalancers and neo
Lucas-balcobalancers, we have to determine the set of all (positive)
integer solutions of some specific Pell equation. Since
B_{n}^{neobc}
is a neo balcobalancing number if and only if
8(B_{n}^{neobc})^{2}-12B_{n}^{neobc}+9
is a perfect square, we set

8(B_{n}^{neobc})^{2}-12B_{n}^{neobc}+9=y^{2}

for some positive integer
y
. Then

16(B_{n}^{neobc})^{2}-24B_{n}^{neobc}+18=2y^{2}

and hence

(4B_{n}^{neobc}-3)^{2}+9=2y^{2}.

Taking
x=4B_{n}^{neobc}-3
, we get the Pell equation (see
[
1
,
12
]
)

(2.8)

x^{2}-2y^{2}=-9.

Let
\Omega
denotes the set of all (positive )integer solutions of (
2.8
). Then we can give the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

The set of all integer solutions of
x^{2}-2y^{2}=-9
is

\Omega=\{(21B_{n-1}-3B_{n-2},15B_{n-1}-3B_{n-2}):n\geq 1\}.

Proof.

For the Pell equation in (
2.8
), the indefinite quadratic form is
F=(1,0,-2)
of discriminant
\Delta=8
. So
\tau_{\Delta}=3+2\sqrt{2}
. Thus the set of representatives is
\text{Rep}=\{[\pm 3\ \ \ 3]\}
and
M=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}3&2\\
4&3\end{array}\right]
(see
[
6
, p. 118]
). Here we note that

[x_{n}\ \ \ y_{n}]=[3\ \ \ \ 3]M^{n-1}

for
n\geq 1
. Also it can be easily seen that the
n^{\text{th}}
power of
M
is

M^{n}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}3B_{n}-B_{n-1}&2B_{n}\\
4B_{n}&3B_{n}-B_{n-1}\end{array}\right]

for
n\geq 1
. So

[x_{n}\ \ \ \ y_{n}]=[3\ \ \ \ 3]M^{n-1}=[21B_{n-1}-3B_{n-2}~\ \ \ \ 15B_{n-1}-3B_{n-2}].

Thus
\Omega=\{(21B_{n-1}-3B_{n-2},15B_{n-1}-3B_{n-2}):n\geq 1\}
as we
claimed.
∎

Before determining the general terms of neo balcobalancing numbers, neo
Lucas-balcobalancing numbers, neo balcobalancers and neo
Lucas-balcobalancers, we need the following Lemma first.

Lemma 2.1
.

For balancing numbers
B_{n}
, we have

(1)

\frac{441B_{2n-1}^{2}}{2}-63B_{2n-1}B_{2n-2}+\frac{9B_{2n-2}^{2}}{2}+\frac{9}{2}=(15B_{2n-1}-3B_{2n-2})^{2}

(2)

\frac{81B_{2n-1}^{2}}{8}-\frac{27B_{2n-1}B_{2n-2}}{4}+\frac{9B_{2n-2}^{2}}{8}-\frac{9}{8}=9B_{2n-1}^{2}

for
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

Applying Binet formula
B_{n}=\frac{\alpha^{2n}-\beta^{2n}}{4\sqrt{2}}
,
we get the desired result.
∎

Now from Theorem
2.1
, we can give the following result.

Theorem 2.2
.

The general terms of neo balcobalancing numbers, neo
Lucas-balcobalancing numbers, neo balcobalancers and neo
Lucas-balcobalancers are

\displaystyle B_{n}^{neobc}

\displaystyle=\frac{21B_{2n-1}-3B_{2n-2}+3}{4}

\displaystyle C_{n}^{neobc}

\displaystyle=15B_{2n-1}-3B_{2n-2}

\displaystyle R_{n}^{neobc}

\displaystyle=\frac{9B_{2n-1}-3B_{2n-2}-5}{4}

\displaystyle CR_{n}^{neobc}

\displaystyle=3B_{2n-1}

for
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

Since
x=4B_{n}^{neobc}-3
, we get from Theorem 2.1 that

B_{n}^{neobc}=\frac{21B_{2n-1}-3B_{2n-2}+3}{4}.

So from (
2.6
), we get

\displaystyle C_{n}^{neobc}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{8(B_{n}^{neobc})^{2}-12B_{n}^{neobc}+9}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{8(\frac{21B_{2n-1}-3B_{2n-2}+3}{4})^{2}-12(\frac{21B_{2n-1}-3B_{2n-2}+3}{4})+9}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{441B_{2n-1}^{2}}{2}-63B_{2n-1}B_{2n-2}+\frac{9B_{2n-2}^{2}}{2}+\frac{9}{2}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{(15B_{2n-1}-3B_{2n-2})^{2}}

\displaystyle=15B_{2n-1}-3B_{2n-2}

by (1) of Lemma
2.1
and from (
2.4
)

\displaystyle R_{n}^{neobc}

\displaystyle=\frac{-2B_{n}^{neobc}-1+C_{n}^{neobc}}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{-2(\frac{21B_{2n-1}-3B_{2n-2}+3}{4})-1+(15B_{2n-1}-3B_{2n-2})}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{9B_{2n-1}-3B_{2n-2}-5}{4}.

Finally from (
2.7
), we deduce that

\displaystyle CR_{n}^{neobc}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2(R_{n}^{neobc})^{2}+5R_{n}^{neobc}+2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2(\frac{9B_{2n-1}-3B_{2n-2}-5}{4})^{2}+5(\frac{9B_{2n-1}-3B_{2n-2}-5}{4})+2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{81B_{2n-1}^{2}}{8}-\frac{27B_{2n-1}B_{2n-2}}{4}+\frac{9B_{2n-2}^{2}}{8}-\frac{9}{8}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{9B_{2n-1}^{2}}

\displaystyle=3B_{2n-1}

by (2) of Lemma

. This completes the proof.
∎

Here we note that neo balcobalancing numbers should be positive from
definition. But from (

), we see that

is
a perfect square. So we accepted

is,
B_{0}^{neobc}=0
. In this case
R_{0}^{neobc}=1
and
C_{0}^{neobc}=CR_{0}^{neobc}=3.

3.
Binet Formulas and Recurrence Relations

Theorem 3.1
.

Binet formulas for neo balcobalancing numbers, neo
Lucas-balcobalancing numbers, neo balcobalancers and neo
Lucas-balcobalancers are

\displaystyle B_{n}^{neobc}

\displaystyle=\frac{3(\alpha^{4n-1}+\beta^{4n-1})+6}{8}

\displaystyle C_{n}^{neobc}

\displaystyle=\frac{3(\alpha^{4n-1}-\beta^{4n-1})}{2\sqrt{2}}

\displaystyle R_{n}^{neobc}

\displaystyle=\frac{3(\alpha^{4n-2}+\beta^{4n-2})-10}{8}

\displaystyle CR_{n}^{neobc}

\displaystyle=\frac{3(\alpha^{4n-2}-\beta^{4n-2})}{4\sqrt{2}}

for
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

We note that
B_{n}=\frac{\alpha^{2n}-\beta^{2n}}{4\sqrt{2}}
. Thus from
Theorem
2.2
, we deduce that

\displaystyle B_{n}^{neobc}

\displaystyle=\frac{21B_{2n-1}-3B_{2n-2}+3}{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{21(\frac{\alpha^{4n-2}-\beta^{4n-2}}{4\sqrt{2}})-3(\frac{\alpha^{4n-4}-\beta^{4n-4}}{4\sqrt{2}})+3}{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{\alpha^{4n}(21\alpha^{-2}-3\alpha^{-4})-\beta^{4n}(21\beta^{-2}-3\beta^{-4})}{4\sqrt{2}}+3}{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{\alpha^{4n}(-3+3\sqrt{2})-\beta^{4n}(3+3\sqrt{2})}{2}+3}{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{3(\alpha^{4n-1}+\beta^{4n-1})+6}{8}.

The others can be proved similarly.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Recurrence relations for neo balcobalancing numbers, neo
Lucas-balcobalancing numbers, neo balcobalancers and neo
Lucas-balcobalancers are

\displaystyle B_{n}^{neobc}

\displaystyle=35B_{n-1}^{neobc}-35B_{n-2}^{neobc}+B_{n-3}^{neobc}

\displaystyle C_{n}^{neobc}

\displaystyle=35C_{n-1}^{neobc}-35C_{n-2}^{neobc}+C_{n-3}^{neobc}

\displaystyle R_{n}^{neobc}

\displaystyle=35R_{n-1}^{neobc}-35R_{n-2}^{neobc}+R_{n-3}^{neobc}

for
n\geq 3
and

\displaystyle CR_{n}^{neobc}

\displaystyle=35CR_{n-1}^{neobc}-35CR_{n-2}^{neobc}+CR_{n-3}^{neobc}

for
n\geq 4
.

Proof.

First we notice that
35B_{2n-3}-35B_{2n-5}+B_{2n-7}=B_{2n-1}
and
35B_{2n-4}-35B_{2n-6}+B_{2n-8}=B_{2n-2}
. Thus from Theorem
2.2
, we
get

\displaystyle 35B_{n-1}^{neobc}-35B_{n-2}^{neobc}+B_{n-3}^{neobc}

\displaystyle=35(\frac{21B_{2n-3}-3B_{2n-4}+3}{4})-35(\frac{21B_{2n-5}-3B_{2n-6}+3}{4})

\displaystyle\ \ \ +(\frac{21B_{2n-7}-3B_{2n-8}+3}{4})

\displaystyle=\frac{21(35B_{2n-3}-35B_{2n-5}+B_{2n-7})-3(35B_{2n-4}-35B_{2n-6}+B_{2n-8})+3}{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{21B_{2n-1}-3B_{2n-2}+3}{4}

\displaystyle=B_{n}^{neobc}.

The others can be proved similarly.
∎

4.
From Neo Balcobalancing Numbers to Balancing Numbers

In Theorem
2.2
, we deduced the general terms of neo balcobalancing
numbers, neo Lucas-balcobalancing numbers, neo balcobalancers and neo
Lucas-balcobalancers in terms of balancing numbers. Conversely, we can
give the general terms of balancing, cobalancing, Lucas-balancing and
Lucas-cobalancing numbers in terms of neo balcobalancing numbers and neo
Lucas-balcobalancing numbers as follows.

Theorem 4.1
.

The general terms of balancing, cobalancing, Lucas-balancing
and Lucas-cobalancing numbers

\displaystyle B_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}4B_{\frac{n+1}{2}}^{neobc}-C_{\frac{n+1}{2}}^{neobc}-3&n\geq 1\text{ odd }\\
&\\
20B_{\frac{n+2}{2}}^{neobc}-7C_{\frac{n+2}{2}}^{neobc}-15&n\geq 2\text{
even}\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle b_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{12}\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}-16B_{\frac{n+1}{2}}^{neobc}+6C_{\frac{n+1}{2}}^{neobc}+6&n\geq 1\text{
odd }\\
&\\

6.
Relationship with Triangular and Square Triangular Numbers

Recall that triangular numbers denoted by
T_{n}
are the numbers of the
form

T_{n}=\frac{n(n+1)}{2}.

It is known that there is a correspondence between balancing numbers and
triangular numbers. From (
1.1
), we see that
n
is a balancing number if and only if
n^{2}
is a triangular number since

\frac{(n+r)(n+r+1)}{2}=n^{2}.

So

(6.1)

T_{B_{n}+R_{n}}=B_{n}^{2}.

As in (
6.1
), we can give the following theorem.

Theorem 6.1
.

B_{n}^{neobc}
is a neo balcobalancing number if and only if
(B_{n}^{neobc})^{2}-\frac{3B_{n}^{neobc}}{2}+1
is a triangular number, that
is,

T_{B_{n}^{neobc}+R_{n}^{neobc}}=(B_{n}^{neobc})^{2}-\frac{3B_{n}^{neobc}}{2}+1.

Proof.

Let
n
be a neo balcobalancing number. Then from (
2.3
), we get

\frac{(n-1)n}{2}+\frac{n(n+1)}{2}=2\left[2n-1+nr+\frac{r(r+1)}{2}\right]

and hence

\frac{(n+r)(n+r+1)}{2}=n^{2}-\frac{3n}{2}+1.

Thus

T_{B_{n}^{neobc}+R_{n}^{neobc}}=(B_{n}^{neobc})^{2}-\frac{3B_{n}^{neobc}}{2}+1

as we claimed.
∎

There are infinitely many triangular numbers that are also square numbers
which are called square triangular numbers and is denoted by
S_{n}
. Notice
that

S_{n}=s_{n}^{2}=\frac{t_{n}(t_{n}+1)}{2},

where
s_{n}
and
t_{n}
are the sides of the corresponding square and
triangle. Their Binet formulas are

(6.2)

.

\displaystyle=3s_{2n-1}

.

Predicting plant stress using SAM-L: novel self-adaptive-meta learner with XAI based on soil moisture and chlorophyll analysis
Authors: Tawfeeq Alsanoosy, Javaid Ahmad Malik
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractRecent advancements in precision agriculture have introduced innovative approaches to addressing plant stress, a critical factor influencing crop productivity and agricultural sustainability. Accurate, real-time prediction of plant stress has become essential for optimizing water utilization and promoting healthy crop development. While existing machine learning methods have demonstrated efficacy, they often lack the adaptability required to accommodate the dynamic conditions of agricultural environments. Prior research has identified soil moisture and chlorophyll content as key indicators of plant health and stress, with conventional models relying on simplistic algorithms for stress prediction. However, these models exhibit limitations in scalability, adaptability and interpretability. To overcome these challenges, this study employed sparse additive models with learning (SAM-L) algorithms, integrated with explainable artificial intelligence (XAI), to provide a flexible and transparent solution. In this paper, we proposed a novel framework that integrates SAM-L and XAI to predict plant stress using soil moisture and chlorophyll content. The SAM-L algorithm is a machine learning method that focuses on sparsely selecting relevant features through additive models. It aims to enhance model interpretability while maintaining high prediction accuracy by learning sparse representations of input data. The SAM-L algorithm enhances interpretability while preserving high predictive accuracy by learning sparse feature representations from input data. Additionally, XAI was incorporated to ensure interpretable decision-making, enabling farmers and stakeholders to comprehend the rationale behind irrigation recommendations. The model’s architecture incorporates a three-layer Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network to process sequential data effectively. The proposed framework achieved a high performance on publicly available dataset, yielding an overall accuracy of 89.2% on the multi-class classification task. Further analysis of the results across the three predefined stress categories (healthy, moderate stress, and high stress) revealed strong performance, with the model obtaining a macro F1-score of 0.88 and a macro recall of 0.88. The proposed framework not only can enhance prediction accuracy but also can promote sustainable farming practices by reducing water wastage and improving crop resilience.Subject terms: Climate change, Computer science, Ecology

Introduction

With the world’s growing population, the agricultural sector faces increasing pressure to meet food demands while simultaneously addressing climate change and resource scarcity1. Water management, in particular, has become a critical challenge, as conventional irrigation methods often lead to water overuse and waste, exacerbating scarcity in many regions2. plant stress is one of the most influential factors that influence on crop health and yield3. Plant stress, caused by environmental factors such as drought, high temperatures, soil issues, and nutrient imbalances. This stress directly impacts the physiological processes of crops, leading to inhibited growth, reduced yield, and in severe cases, complete crop failure. Accurate and timely prediction of plant stress is essential for sustainable agriculture4. Such predictions enable farmers to implement appropriate measures, like optimizing water use, applying suitable fertilizers, and adjusting other farming practices, to mitigate negative impacts and improve crop outcomes. Thus, real-time monitoring and identification of plant stress could help in early disease diagnosis and prevention.

In agricultural, conventional methods for assessing plant health, such as visual inspections or manual measurements, are often inefficient and fail to provide timely, data-driven insights5. Understanding the need to advance highly data-orientated approaches is essential for raising the bar for irrigation scheduling and resource management. Recent advancements in precision agriculture, including the use of sensors, remote sensing, and data analytics, have demonstrated significant value. These technologies predict factors like soil moisture, ambient temperature, and light intensity, which are crucial indicators of plant stress6. Such developments allow farmers to get real-time reports that can predict stress levels and inform optimal agricultural practices7. This paper introduces a novel framework that integrates Sparse Additive Models with Learning (SAM-L) and explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) to predict plant stress using soil moisture and chlorophyll content. Soil moisture is one of the main factors in the hydrological cycle, crucial for driving weather conditions, plant growth, and groundwater storage8. Chlorophyll content is defined as the amount of chlorophyll—the green pigment vital for photosynthesis—in a plant, making it a key indicator of plant health, stress levels, and nutrient status9. The SAM-L algorithm utilizes a self-adaptive learning framework, which allows it to continuously adjust its parameters in response to dynamic environmental conditions common in agricultural settings. While it does not fit into traditional categories like ensemble or reinforcement learning, it draws from principles of meta-learning and online learning. This unique approach enables the model to adapt to new data without requiring a complete retraining cycle. The model’s architecture incorporates a three-layer Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network to process sequential data effectively. The innovation of SAM-L lies in its integration of sparsity for feature selection and its mechanism for adaptive model refinement using real-time sensor data. This design allows it to efficiently manage the large, dynamic datasets typical of agricultural monitoring. A key differentiator is the inclusion of XAI techniques, which ensure that predictions are transparent and easy for end-users, such as farmers, to interpret. This transparency is vital for building trust in automated decision-making systems, particularly in critical areas like water management and crop health prediction. The primary motivation for this research is to leverage the SAM-L algorithm to create a more adaptive and accurate model for predicting plant stress. The goal is to reduce water waste, increase crop yield, and support sustainable farming practices. This framework is designed to be sustainable agricultural, addressing the growing needs of modern agriculture10.

The scheduling of irrigation underwent significant transformations over the past few decades due to the increasing demand for water conservation and improved crop management11. Traditional irrigation systems primarily rely on time-based or simplistic calculations, which failed to account for the dynamic requirements of crops or fluctuating environmental conditions12. Consequently, substantial water is wasted through over-irrigation or under-irrigation, leading to crop stress and diminished yields13. Recently, precision agriculture emerged as a promising solution, incorporating advanced technologies such as soil moisture sensors, remote sensing, and climate data to monitor crop needs in real time14. Soil moisture sensors represent a critical advancement in irrigation scheduling, enabling precise determination of irrigation timing and volume, thereby eliminating much of the uncertainty associated with conventional methods. A key feature of these sensors is their ability to continuously monitor soil conditions, which play a vital role in optimizing irrigation schedules and ensuring adequate water supply at appropriate intervals15. Sensor-based irrigation systems demonstrate notable efficacy in enhancing water-use efficiency, reducing water consumption, and improving crop productivity across multiple studies. Another significant development in irrigation scheduling was the adoption of data-driven models that analyzed various environmental variables, such as temperature, humidity, and light intensity, to estimate plant stress and irrigation demands16. Machine learning (ML) algorithms, including decision trees, support vector machines, and neural networks, are employed to predict stress levels and optimize irrigation systems. Deep learning (DL) and ensemble methods further contributed to generating accurate predictions of plant health and stress. However, these remained insufficient in addressing the rapid variability of environmental conditions, posing challenges for traditional models in delivering flexible solutions17. The integration of advanced techniques, such as SAM-L, might be essential to improve both irrigation scheduling and plant stress detection. These methods offered enhanced accuracy, mitigated issues such as overfitting and interpretability, and provided a more comprehensive approach to addressing modern agricultural challenges.

Standard methods for plant stress prediction have long been employed in agriculture, but their limitations render them inadequate for practical farming applications. A significant drawback is their inability to adapt to dynamic and evolving conditions. Traditional approaches typically rely on static thresholds and predefined input values, such as fixed moisture and temperature levels, to identify plant stress18. However, these criteria fail to account for the complex interactions among environmental conditions, plant physiology, and growth stages. As a result, such models often prove unreliable in predicting stress levels across diverse weather patterns and geographically dispersed agricultural regions. Another critical limitation of conventional models is their reliance on limited data inputs, often restricted to basic parameters like temperature, humidity, and soil moisture19. These models neglect other vital environmental factors influencing plant health, such as light intensity, soil pH, and nutrient availability. The absence of comprehensive data significantly diminishes their predictive accuracy. Furthermore, traditional plant stress prediction models are often non-interpretable, hindering farmers and agronomists from understanding the underlying decision-making processes20. This lack of explainability poses a major barrier to practical adoption, as stakeholders require transparent insights to inform critical decisions regarding irrigation, fertilization, and pest management. Additionally, many conventional models lack flexibility and scalability, making them unsuitable for modern agricultural systems, particularly precision farming, where real-time and adaptive predictions are essential21. These models also often exhibit declining accuracy over time because new variables emerge, and their inability to include big-data from IoT sensors and remote sensing technologies further diminishes their effectiveness. Therefore, there is the urgent need for advanced techniques of ML which are capable of dynamically adapting to the changing conditionswhile providing accurate predictions and explainable solutions that farmers can easily interpret. This problem can be addressed by developing models such as the SAM-L framework integrated with XAI, which offers improved scalability, adaptability, and transparency in plant stress prediction.

The main contribution of this paper is that we proposed a novel framework that integrates SAM-L and XAI to predict plant stress using soil moisture and chlorophyll content. The model’s architecture incorporates a three-layer LSTM network to process sequential data effectively. SAM-L dynamically adapts to environmental changes by learning from diverse datasets, enabling real-time and precise predictions. By incorporating XAI, this approach ensures transparent decision-making, allowing farmers to interpret the model’s reasoning effectively. We applied two widely used XAI techniques, Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations (LIME) and Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP), to analyze feature contributions. LIME provides localized explanations for individual predictions, allowing researchers to understand each feature’s contribution to a specific outcome. Concurrently, SHAP generates global feature importance rankings, which reveals how variations in soil moisture and chlorophyll influence stress classification across the dataset The proposed framework achieved a high performance on publicly available dataset, yielding an overall accuracy of 89.2% on the multi-class classification task. Further analysis of the results across the three predefined stress categories (healthy, moderate stress, and high stress) revealed strong performance, with the model obtaining a macro F1-score of 0.88 and a macro recall of 0.88.

The outcomes of this work advance the field of precision agriculture by introducing an interpretable and adaptive framework for plant stress prediction. By integrating SAM-L with XAI, the outcomes of this research can enhance prediction accuracy under dynamic environmental conditions while providing actionable insights and explanation for farmers. The proposed model addresses key limitations of traditional approaches, such as scalability and transparency, thereby facilitating broader adoption of data-driven decision-making in agricultural practices.

Plant stress prediction

ML has been widely employed in plant stress detection and prediction, utilizing features such as soil moisture, temperature, chlorophyll content, and spectral indices. Algorithms including support vector machines, random forests, and gradient boosting demonstrated promising results in identifying stress symptoms before visual manifestation. These models contributed to optimized irrigation schedules, reduced yield loss, and improved precision agriculture decision-making. Early stress classification relied on supervised learning algorithms such as support vector machines and decision trees, which operated on limited datasets. However, these models lacked the adaptability required for the complex and dynamic nature of agricultural environments32. More recently, DL architectures, including recurrent neural networks and convolutional neural networks, successfully modeled temporal dependencies and spatial patterns in irrigation scheduling34,35. By leveraging real-time data from IoT-enabled field sensors, these models enabled dynamic, real-time predictions. Reinforcement learning also demonstrated potential, where an agent learned optimal irrigation strategies through trial and error, minimizing water waste while maintaining optimal crop health36. Additionally, ensemble methods such as random forests and boosting techniques enhanced model robustness by combining multiple predictors, effectively handling noisy and incomplete data31. These advancements not only improved stress prediction capabilities but also enabled early detection and more precise management of irrigation, nutrient application, and other agricultural practices37.

Temporal models in irrigation

The integration of ML revolutionized irrigation scheduling by enabling more accurate and adaptive water management solutions. Traditional irrigation methods depended on fixed schedules or farmer intuition, often resulting in over- or under-irrigation, which wasted water and compromised crop health38. With the rise of precision agriculture, ML techniques were increasingly adopted to optimize water usage prior to harvest. Supervised learning methods, including linear regression, decision trees, and support vector machines, were commonly employed to predict irrigation demands based on environmental and soil parameters such as soil moisture, ambient temperature, and humidity33. DL approaches, particularly temporal models such as LSTM networks and Temporal Convolutional Networks, proved highly effective for sequential agricultural data analysis. These models captured complex temporal dependencies between environmental variables and crop responses, leading to more precise irrigation scheduling and early stress detection. By integrating multi-source sensor data, they provided adaptive, site-specific irrigation recommendations. These models were trained on historical data to learn the relationships between environmental variables and plant water requirements. Advanced techniques such as random forests and boosting algorithms outperformed conventional models due to their ability to handle nonlinear relationships and mitigate overfitting39.

Explainable AI in agricultural decision support

XAI methods became essential for fostering trust in agricultural AI systems. These methods provided transparent reasoning for model predictions, enabling farmers and agronomists to identify which environmental or physiological factors contributed most to predicted stress conditions. XAI not only enhanced interpretability but also facilitated compliance with data-driven policy-making. XAI has received significant momentum in agricultural Decision Support Systems, offering interpretable models that improved trustworthiness and usability. With more and more complex and integrated ML models deployed for irrigation scheduling, crop management, and plant health prediction, the need for explainability became paramount27. Conventional black-box AI models, while effective in prediction accuracy, lacked clear explanations for their decisions, hindering farmers and agricultural experts from relying on them for critical choices25. XAI techniques addressed this limitation by providing interpretable explanations for AI predictions, thereby enhancing model transparency and accessibility for stakeholders. In agricultural applications, XAI played a crucial role in ensuring that decision-making processes such as determining irrigation timing,or water allocation were not only accurate but also understandable28. With the help of techniques like LIME and SHAP, XAI enabled to identify the most influential parameters in model decisions, including soil moisture levels, temperature, humidity, and nutrient concentration26. So, farmers could understand the rationale behind AI-generated recommendations, increasing their trust in such systems. By incorporating XAI into agricultural decision support systems, decision is made easy to suit the actual needs of farmers without advanced technical expertise29. This allowed agriculturalists to understand recommendations, promoting sustainable practices and regional suitability. Through supported interpretation, XAI might encourage more AI adoption among farmers.

Self-adaptive meta-learning in precision agriculture

Self-Adaptive Meta-Learning (SAM) emerged as a meta-learning paradigm designed to enable models to adapt rapidly to new tasks or changing environments with minimal retraining. Unlike conventional meta-learning, which optimized for generalization across tasks, or continual learning, which focused on retaining knowledge across sequential tasks, SAM incorporated adaptive mechanisms that dynamically adjusted learning parameters in response to environmental shifts. In this study, SAM was implemented as a meta-learning framework based on LSTM architectures, allowing the model to adapt to variations in soil moisture and chlorophyll dynamics over time. This targeted adaptability improved the accuracy and robustness of stress predictions under diverse agricultural conditions. Claims regarding SAM’s adaptability were strictly confined to empirical evidence from experiments, avoiding extrapolation beyond tested scenarios. SAM algorithms demonstrated flexible adaptation to any environments and datasets, making them well-suited for precision agriculture. Data-driven models played a pivotal role in advancing farming practices, particularly in irrigation scheduling, crop health monitoring, and pest detection40. However, due to the variation in crops and growing regions, traditional ML techniques often struggled to generalize across different agricultural settings. Self-adaptive meta-learning offers effective solution to this challenge41. SAM algorithms autonomously adjusted their learning processes based on environmental and data inputs, improving prediction accuracy and decision-making. In agriculture, SAM algorithms rely on prior knowledge from related tasks to accelerate learning in new contexts and thus prove high efficient in the case of scarce labelled data. For instance, SAM could learn from data that is acquired from different farms or crops types and rapidly adapt to the new conditions without mass requests for training data possesses42. This valuable in precision agriculture, where timely responses to dynamic factors like soil moisture, temperature, and nutrient availability were critical for effective crop management43. Furthermore, SAM excellent in transfer learning, where knowledge from one task (e.g., irrigation prediction for a specific crop) could be applied to related tasks (e.g., irrigation management for another crop). The self-adaptive capabilities of SAM algorithms were especially beneficial for real-time decision-making, which demanded continuous updates and rapid adjustments. Integrating SAM into precision agriculture systems enabled farmers to optimize resource utilization, enhance crop health, and promote sustainable land use, thereby strengthening agricultural resilience in the face of global challenges.

Proposed work

The objective of this study was to develop a novel plant stress prediction framework that integrated SAM-L and XAI techniques to enhance prediction accuracy in smart farming. The proposed model was designed to adaptively predict plant stress based on soil moisture and chlorophyll content. SAM-L, an innovative meta-learning algorithm, was employed to enable the model to generalize across diverse environmental conditions, making it applicable to various farms, crops, and weather patterns. This approach eliminated the need for manual parameter adjustments, as the model continuously improved with additional data.

Explainable AI

Explainable AI (XAI) encompassed methods and techniques in AI designed to render the decision-making processes of models transparent and interpretable to humans. Its primary objective is to elucidate how AI models derived specific predictions or classifications, thereby fostering trust and accountability. XAI plays a critical role in enhancing model interpretability, ensuring fairness, and enabling users to scrutinize and contest AI decisions in high-stakes domains such as healthcare and finance. To assess interpretability, two widely adopted XAI techniques were used LIME and SHAP. There were applied to examine feature contributions in individual predictions and across the model. SHAP was utilized to generate global feature importance rankings, which elucidated the influence of soil moisture and chlorophyll variations on stress classification throughout the dataset. LIME provided localized explanations for specific predictions, enabling users to discern the contribution of each feature to a particular outcome. Beyond their standard implementation, the explanation process was tailored to emphasize domain-specific insights. For example, SHAP plots (Fig. 11) not only ranked features by importance but also illustrated their directional impact on stress levels. Concurrently, LIME outputs were integrated into an interactive dashboard to facilitate real-time interpretation for agronomists. This combined approach enhances trust and decision support in precision agriculture contexts by linking model outputs to actionable agricultural parameters.

Conclusion

The agricultural sector faces increasing pressure to meet food demands while simultaneously addressing climate change and resource scarcity. Plant stress is a critical factor influencing crop productivity and agricultural sustainability. While existing studies demonstrated efficacy, they often lacked the adaptability required to accommodate the dynamic conditions of agricultural environments. Prior research identified soil moisture and chlorophyll content as key indicators of plant health and stress, with conventional models relying on simplistic algorithms for stress prediction. However, these models exhibited limitations in scalability, adaptability, and interpretability. To overcome these challenges, we proposed the SAM-L framework, which integrated LSTM-based modeling with XAI techniques, achieving an overall accuracy of 89.2%, a macro F1-score of 0.88, and a macro recall of 0.88 across three stress categories. These quantitative results validated the framework’s predictive capability under the tested conditions. The model demonstrated stable performance as training data increased, with reduced variance between training and validation scores, indicating improved generalization. We employed LIME and SHAP approaches to enhance interpretability, improving trust and transparency in the model’s decision-making process. The analysis revealed that the learning model’s performance improved with larger datasets, exhibiting reduced overfitting.

Future work should expand the input dataset, incorporate temporal data for time-series analysis, and explore advanced machine learning techniques to uncover additional relationships between plant stress and environmental factors. Further validation is necessary to confirm the model’s applicability in real-world farming scenarios and its robustness under varying agricultural and climatic conditions. Integrating IoT devices and real-time forecasting could provide farmers with actionable insights for crop management. These advancements would enable the development of more intelligent farming techniques, enhancing crop health protection and yield prediction under resource constraints and climate variability. Although the model was designed to handle diverse datasets, overfitting remained a challenge due to the complexity of environmental data and the difficulty of accurately predicting plant stress across all potential scenarios.

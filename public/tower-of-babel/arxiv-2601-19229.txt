Benling Li, Wei Zhao

Abstract
. In the Riemannian setting, every flat Cartan–Hadamard manifold is isometric to Euclidean space, the canonical model that underlies the theory of Sobolev spaces and guarantees the sharpness/rigidity of the Hardy inequality, the uncertainty principle, and the Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg (CKN) inequality. In this paper, we show that on a flat Finsler Cartan–Hadamard manifold — Berwald’s metric space — the classical picture alters radically: the Nash embedding theorem fails, the Sobolev space becomes nonlinear, and the Hardy and uncertainty inequalities break down completely, whereas the CKN inequality exhibits a sharp threshold in its validity depending on a parameter. By contrast, on Funk metric spaces — another class of Finsler Cartan–Hadamard manifolds — this threshold behavior disappears, although all the other non-Riemannian features persist. We trace this divergence to the lower bound of the S S -curvature. As a consequence, the failure of the aforementioned functional inequalities is established for a broad class of Finsler manifolds.

1.
Introduction

In 1996, S. S. Chern remarked that Finsler geometry may be regarded, in essence, as Riemannian geometry freed from the quadratic constraint
[
8
]
.
This liberation from the quadratic mold is precisely what unlocks a far richer geometric landscape, wherein profound distinctions from the Riemannian setting arise as natural and inherent features.

A quintessential non-Riemannian feature afforded by this greater generality is the asymmetry of Finsler metrics — a property that leads to a variety of unexpected phenomena.
A striking illustration is provided by the Funk metric on the Euclidean unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,|x|<1\}
, defined by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathsf{F}(x,y)=\frac{\sqrt{(1-|x|^{2})|y|^{2}+\langle x,y\rangle^{2}}}{1-|x|^{2}}+\frac{\langle x,y\rangle}{1-|x|^{2}},\qquad\forall(x,y)\in T\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}).

where
|\cdot|
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denote the
n
-dimensional Euclidean norm and inner product, respectively (cf. Funk
[
12
]
). On the one hand,
(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}),\mathsf{F})
is a Finsler Cartan–Hadamard manifold of constant negative curvature
-1/4
, thus serving as a Finslerian analogue of the Poincaré disc. On the other hand, it exhibits extreme asymmetry: for the radial direction
\mathsf{y}:=x/|x|
,

\lim_{|x|\rightarrow 1^{-}}\mathsf{F}(x,\mathsf{y})=+\infty,\qquad\lim_{|x|\rightarrow 1^{-}}\mathsf{F}(x,-\mathsf{y})=1.

Consequently, the Nash embedding theorem fails in this setting; that is,

(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}),\mathsf{F})
admits no isometric embedding into any Minkowski space.
Moreover, the asymmetry is also reflected in the associated distance function, which satisfies

\lim_{|x|\rightarrow 1^{-}}d_{\mathsf{F}}(\mathbf{0},x)=+\infty,\qquad\lim_{|x|\rightarrow 1^{-}}d_{\mathsf{F}}(x,\mathbf{0})=\ln 2.

These consequences of asymmetry further extend to functional-analytic structures: the associated Sobolev spaces become nonlinear, as shown by Farkas–Kristály–Varga
[
10
]
and Kristály–Radus
[
24
]
.

A second fundamental distinction is the absence of a canonical measure on a Finsler manifold. Unlike the Riemannian setting, various natural choices exist, and their analytic properties can differ substantially (see, e.g., Álvarez Paiva–Berck
[
1
]
and Álvarez Paiva–Thompson
[
2
]
).
This is vividly illustrated by sharp functional inequalities.
On any Riemannian Cartan–Hadamard manifold endowed with its canonical Riemannian measure, the Heisenberg–Pauli–Weyl (HPW) uncertainty principle, the Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg (CKN) interpolation inequality, and the Hardy inequality are all sharp (cf. Kristály
[
21
]
).
Recently, in contrast, Kristály–Li–Zhao
[
22
]
showed that all three inequalities fail on the Finsler Cartan–Hadamard manifold
(\mathbb{B}^{n}(\mathbf{0}),\mathsf{F})
equipped with the Busemann–Hausdorff measure. Since on this manifold the Busemann–Hausdorff measure coincides with the standard Lebesgue measure

\mathscr{L}^{n}

up to a multiplicative constant, one has

•

HPW uncertainty principle/CKN interpolation inequality: for
p=2
and
q=0
, or for
0<q<2<p
with
2<n<2(p-q)/(p-2)
,

(1.2)

\inf_{u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}))\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}\mathsf{F}^{2}(\nabla u){\dd}x\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}\frac{|u|^{2p-2}}{d_{\mathsf{F}}(\mathbf{0},\cdot)^{2q-2}}{\dd}x\right)}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}\frac{|u|^{p}}{d_{\mathsf{F}}(\mathbf{0},\cdot)^{q}}{\dd}x\right)^{2}}=0;

•

Hardy inequality: for any
p\in(1,n)
,

(1.3)

\inf_{u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}))\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}\mathsf{F}^{p}(\nabla u){\dd}x}{\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}\frac{|u|^{p}}{d_{\mathsf{F}}(\mathbf{0},\cdot)^{p}}{\dd}x}=0.

The phenomenon, in fact, extends to the whole class of Funk metric spaces. Recall that a Finsler manifold
(\Omega,\mathsf{F})
is called a Funk metric space if
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

x+\frac{y}{\mathsf{F}(x,y)}\in\partial\Omega,\qquad\forall x\in\Omega,\ y\in T_{x}\Omega\backslash\{0\}.

Such spaces have constant curvature
-1/4
. When
\Omega=\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})
,
the metric
\mathsf{F}

reduces to the one given in (
1.1
). The underlying reason for the failure of these inequalities
is that the
S
-curvature (associated with the Lebesgue measure, cf. Shen
[
33
,
34
]
) — a quantity with no Riemannian analogue — satisfies the following dominance condition over the Ricci curvature:

(1.4)

\inf_{(x,y)\in T\Omega\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\mathbf{S}(x,y)}{\mathsf{F}(x,y)}>\sup_{(x,y)\in T\Omega\backslash\{0\}}\sqrt{(n-1)\max\left\{0,\frac{-\mathbf{Ric}(x,y)}{\mathsf{F}(x,y)}\right\}}.

For further properties of Funk metric spaces we refer to Faifman
[
9
]
, Li
[
25
]
, Kajántó–Kristály
[
18
]
and Shen
[
35
,
36
,
37
]
.

The discussion thus far has focused on the setting of negative curvature. In the case of zero curvature, the Riemannian situation is rigid: every complete, simply connected, flat Riemannian manifold is isometric to Euclidean space. This canonical model underpins the theory of Sobolev spaces and the sharpness/rigidity of all the functional inequalities aforementioned (see e.g. Cazacu–Flynn–Lam–Lu
[
7
]
, Hardy
[
13
]
, Hebey
[
14
]
and Kristály
[
21
]
).
In contrast to Funk metric spaces, a natural question arises in the Finsler setting:

Does there exist a flat Finsler Cartan–Hadamard manifold that exhibits the same unexpected non-Riemannian features
under a weaker
S
-curvature condition than (
1.4
)?

This paper provides a complete affirmative answer.
The candidate is furnished by the classical Berwald’s metric — a projectively flat, zero-curvature Finsler metric on
\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})
, originally constructed by Berwald in relation to Hilbert¡¯s fourth problem
[
4
,
5
]
. It is explicitly given by

\B(x,y):=\frac{(\sqrt{(1-|x|^{2})|y|^{2}+\langle x,y\rangle^{2}}+\langle x,y\rangle)^{2}}{(1-|x|^{2})^{2}\sqrt{(1-|x|^{2})|y|^{2}+\langle x,y\rangle^{2}}},\qquad\forall(x,y)\in T\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}).

Thus
(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}),\B)

serves as a flat Finslerian counterpart of the Poincaré disc.

In what follows, let
p^{\prime}

denote the Hölder conjugate exponent of

p
. Our first main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Berwald’s metric space
(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}),\B)
is a flat Cartan–Hadamard manifold satisfying:

(i)

(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}),\B)
admits no isometric embedding into any Minkowski space;

(ii)

the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}),\B,\mathscr{L}^{n})
is not a vector space for all
p\in(1,+\infty)
;

(iii)

the
L^{p}
-Hardy inequality fails for all
p\in(1,n)
, i.e.,

\inf_{f\in C^{\infty}_{0}({\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})})\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}{\B}^{p}(\nabla f){\dd}x}{\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}\frac{|f|^{p}}{d_{\B}(\mathbf{0},\cdot)^{p}}{\dd}x}=0.

(iv)

the generalized uncertainty principle fails, i.e., for any
-p+1<s\leq 1<p<n
,

\inf_{f\in C^{\infty}_{0}({\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})})\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\left(\int_{{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}}{\B}^{p}(\nabla f){\dd}x\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}}|f|^{p}d_{\B}(\mathbf{0},\cdot)^{p^{\prime}s}{\dd}x\right)^{1/{p^{\prime}}}}{\int_{{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}}|f|^{p}d_{\B}(\mathbf{0},\cdot)^{s-1}{\dd}x}=0.

Kajántó–Kristály–Peter–Zhao
[
19
]
established the sharp generalized uncertainty principle on flat Riemannian Cartan–Hadamard manifolds. However, Theorem
1.1
/(
iv
) demonstrates that it
fails completely
in the flat Finsler setting; consequently, all known forms of the HPW uncertainty principle (cf. Kombe–Özaydin
[
20
]
, Kirstály
[
21
]
, and Nguyen
[
29
]
) and the hydrogen uncertainty principle (cf. Cazacu–Flynn–Lam
[
6
]
and Frank
[
11
]
) — both of which holds in the Riemannian setting — also fail there.

A natural extrapolation would suggest that the generalized Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg (CKN) inequality — which holds in the flat Riemannian case
[
19
]
— should likewise fail on
(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}),\B)
. Surprisingly, the actual picture is more delicate.

Theorem 1.2
.

On Berwald’s metric space
(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}),\B)
, let
1<p<m
and
p(n+s-1)>m(n-p)>0
.

(i)

If
1-p<s\leq 2
, then the generalized Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg inequality fails:

\inf_{f\in C^{\infty}_{0}({\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})})\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\left(\int_{{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}}{\B}^{p}(\nabla f){\dd}x\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}}{|f|^{p^{\prime}(m-1)}}{d_{\B}(\mathbf{0},\cdot)^{p^{\prime}s}}{\dd}x\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}}{\int_{{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}}{|f|^{m}}{d_{\B}(\mathbf{0},\cdot)^{s-1}}{\dd}x}=0.

(ii)

If
s>2
, then the generalized Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg inequality holds: for any
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}))
,

{\left(\int_{{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}}{\B}^{p}(\nabla f){\dd}x\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}}{|f|^{p^{\prime}(m-1)}}{d_{\B}(\mathbf{0},\cdot)^{p^{\prime}s}}{\dd}x\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}}\geq\frac{s-2}{4m}{\int_{{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}}{|f|^{m}}{d_{\B}(\mathbf{0},\cdot)^{s-1}}{\dd}x},

with equality if and only if
f=0
.

Note that the hypotheses
1<p<m
and
p(n+s-1)>m(n-p)>0
excludes the case

s\leq 1-p
.
Thus, in contrast to the complete failure of the uncertainty principle, the generalized CKN inequality exhibits a sharp threshold behavior in the parameter
s=2
, separating regimes of validity from those of failure. This further underscores how non-Riemannian geometry can alter the very structure of classical functional inequalities.

To our knowledge, this threshold phenomenon is observed here for the first time even in a Finsler setting. Its occurrence is notable because, on Finsler manifolds with extreme asymmetry (i.e., infinite reversibility), the relevant functional inequalities typically cannot be established in a global form; see Huang–Kristály–Zhao
[
15
]
, Kajántó
[
17
]
, and Mester–Peter–Varga
[
28
]
. For example, while the conclusions of Theorem
1.1
extend to all Funk metric spaces (see Section
4
), the generalized CKN inequality fails unconditionally there, as the following result shows.

Theorem 1.3
.

On a Funk metric space
(\Omega,\mathsf{F})
, for any
1<p<m
and
p(n+s-1)>m(n-p)>0
,

\inf_{f\in C^{\infty}_{0}({\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})})\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\left(\int_{{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}}{\mathsf{F}}^{p}(\nabla f){\dd}x\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}}{|f|^{p^{\prime}(m-1)}}{d_{\mathsf{F}}(\mathbf{0},\cdot)^{p^{\prime}s}}{\dd}x\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}}{\int_{{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}}{|f|^{m}}{d_{\mathsf{F}}(\mathbf{0},\cdot)^{s-1}}{\dd}x}=0.

The decisive difference lies in the
S
-curvature. On a Funk metric space it admits a uniform positive lower bound (
1.4
), while on Berwald’s metric space it decays polynomially (of order
(1+d_{\B}(\mathbf{0},x))^{-1}
), and in particular

(1.5)

\inf_{(x,y)\in T\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\mathbf{S}(x,y)}{\mathsf{B}(x,y)}=\sup_{(x,y)\in T\Omega\backslash\{0\}}\sqrt{(n-1)\max\left\{0,\frac{-\mathbf{Ric}(x,y)}{\mathsf{B}(x,y)}\right\}}=0.

Crucially, (
1.5
) alone is not enough to support the threshold behavior in Theorem
1.2
: for instance, every Minkowski space endowed with the Lebesgue measure satisfies (
1.5
) yet the generalized CKN inequality holds there, and none of the pathologies in Theorem
1.1
occur.

This contrast motivates a general investigation. We obtain the following characterization.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(M,F)
be an
n
-dimensional forward complete Finsler manifold with
\mathbf{Ric}\geq-(n-1)k^{2}
for some
k\geq 0
and let
\m
be a smooth positive measure on
M
. Suppose that there exists a point
o\in M
such that

(1.6)

\mathbf{S}(\nabla r)\geq(n-1)k+\frac{C}{1+r},

where
r(x)=d_{F}(o,x)
is the distance function from
o
and
C
is a constant satisfying

C>1\ \text{ if }\ k>0;\qquad C\geq n\ \text{ if }\ k=0.

Then the following statements are true:

(i)

The
L^{p}
-Hardy inequality fails for all
p\in(1,n)
, i.e.,

\inf_{f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(M)\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{M}{F}^{*p}({\rm d}f)\dm}{\int_{M}{|f|^{p}}{r^{-p}}\dm}=0.

(ii)

The generalized uncertainty principle fails, i.e., for any
-p+1<s\leq 1<p<n
,

\inf_{f\in C^{\infty}_{0}({M})\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\Big(\int_{{M}}F^{*p}({\rm d}f){\dm}\Big)^{1/p}\Big(\int_{{M}}|f|^{p}r^{p^{\prime}s}{\dm}\Big)^{1/{p^{\prime}}}}{\int_{{M}}|f|^{p}r^{s-1}{\dm}}=0.

(iii)

The generalized Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg inequality fails for certain parameters. Specifically, if
1<p<m
,
p(n+s-1)>m(n-p)>0
,
and

s<\begin{cases}C,&\text{if }k>0,\\[4.0pt]
C-n+1,&\text{if }k=0,\end{cases}

then

(1.7)

\inf_{f\in C^{\infty}_{0}({M})\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\Big(\int_{{M}}F^{*p}({\rm d}f){\dm}\Big)^{1/p}\Big(\int_{{M}}{|f|^{p^{\prime}(m-1)}}{r^{p^{\prime}s}}{\dm}\Big)^{1/p^{\prime}}}{\int_{{M}}{|f|^{m}}{r^{s-1}}{\dm}}=0.

(iv)

If the
S
-curvature is strengthened to

\inf_{(x,y)\in TM\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\mathbf{S}(x,y)}{F(x,y)}>(n-1)k,

then
the generalized Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg inequality fails completely, i.e., (
1.7
) holds for any
1<p<m
and
p(n+s-1)>m(n-p)>0
.

Taking

o=\mathbf{0}
and endowing the space with the Lebesgue measure, Berwald’s metric space satisfies
\mathbf{Ric}\equiv 0
and
\mathbf{S}(\nabla r)=\frac{n+1}{1+r}
whereas the Funk metric space satisfies
\mathbf{Ric}\equiv-\frac{(n-1)}{4}
and
\mathbf{S}\equiv\frac{(n+1)}{2}
. Therefore,
Theorem
1.4
accounts for the failure of the functional inequalities on both settings through their curvature profiles. Notably, the

S
-curvature condition in Theorem
1.4
is considerably weaker than that in
[
22
]
, yet its conclusions are stronger.
Finally, Theorem
1.4
remains valid even when the condition (
1.6
) is weakened to

(1.8)

\mathbf{S}(\nabla r)\geq(n-1)k+\frac{C}{1+r}\quad\text{ in \ }M\backslash B^{+}_{R}(o),

for some
R>0
; see Remark
5.2
for details.

Since the analysis of PDEs on manifolds relies on the functional inequalities governing Sobolev spaces, our results indicate that the functional-analytic foundation for studying elliptic and parabolic equations on Finsler manifolds is sensitive to non-Riemannian geometric data. The breakdown of classical inequalities calls for new methods to establish existence, regularity, and stability of solutions to Finslerian PDEs – a direction ripe for future work.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
collects the necessary preliminaries on Finsler geometry and Sobolev spaces. Section
3
studies the geometry of Berwald’s metric space and contains the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. Section
4
presents geometric analysis of Funk metric spaces, providing results parallel to those in Theorem
1.1
and proving Theorem
1.3
. In both Sections
3
and
4
we avoid Finslerian comparison theory and give direct calculations to keep the presentation self-contained and transparent. Finally, Section
5

1.4
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Elements from Finsler geometry

In this section, we recall some definitions and properties from Finsler geometry; for details see
Bao–Chern–Shen
[
3
]
, Ohta
[
30
]
and Shen
[
35
,
34
]
. Naturally, Einstein’s summation convention is used throughout the paper.

Let
V
be an
n
-dimensional vector space with
n\geq 2
. A
Minkowski norm
on
V
is a nonnegative function
\phi:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying:

(i)

\phi(y)\geq 0
with equality if and only if
y=0
;

(ii)

\phi
is positively
1
-homogeneous, i.e.,
\phi(\lambda y)=\lambda\phi(y)
for all
\lambda>0
;

(iii)

the Hessian matrix
\left([\phi^{2}]_{y^{i}y^{j}}(y)\right)
is positive definite for all
y\in V\backslash\{0\}
.

The pair
(V,\phi)
is then called a
Minkowski space
.

For any Minkowski norm
\phi
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the following inequality holds (see
[
3
, (1.2.3)]
):

(2.1)

y^{i}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial{y^{i}}}(x)\leq\phi(y),\qquad\forall y=y^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{i}},\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\},

with equality if and only if
y=\lambda x
with
\lambda\geq 0
. Here
(y^{i})
are the standard Cartesian coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional connected smooth manifold with tangent bundle
TM
.
A
Finsler metric

F=F(x,y)
is a
C^{\infty}
-function defined on
TM\backslash\{0\}
such
that
F(x,\cdot)
is a Minkowski norm on
T_{x}M
for every
x\in M
. The pair
(M,F)
is called a
Finsler manifold
. Thus, a Minkowski space
(V,\phi)
is the simplest model of Finsler manifolds by setting
M:=V
and
F(x,y):=\phi(y)
.

The
fundamental tensor

g_{y}=(g_{ij}(x,y))
is defined by

(2.2)

\displaystyle g_{ij}(x,y):=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}F^{2}}{\partial y^{i}\partial y^{j}}(x,y),\qquad\forall(x,y)\in TM\backslash\{0\}.

By Euler’s theorem,
F^{2}(x,y)=g_{ij}(x,y)y^{i}y^{j}
for all
(x,y)\in TM\backslash\{0\}
. Note that
g_{ij}
can be viewed as a local function on
TM\backslash\{0\}
, but it cannot be defined on
y=0
unless
F
is Riemannian, in which case
g_{ij}(x,y)
is independent of
y
.

A distinctive non-Riemannian feature is the possible asymmetry of
F
.
Its
reversibility
is defined by

(2.3)

\lambda_{F}(M):=\sup_{x\in M}\lambda_{F}(x)\ \ {\rm with}\ \ \lambda_{F}(x)=\sup_{y\in T_{x}M\setminus\{0\}}\frac{F(x,-y)}{F(x,y)},

see Rademacher
[
31
,
32
]
. Hence,
\lambda_{F}(M)=1
if and only
F
is
reversible
, i.e.,
F(x,y)=F(x,-y)
.

The
Legendre transformation

\mathfrak{L}:TM\rightarrow T^{*}M
is defined
by

(2.4)

\mathfrak{L}(x,y):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}g_{y}(y,\cdot),&\text{ if }(x,y)\in TM\backslash\{0\},\\[4.0pt]
0,&\text{ if }(x,y)=0\in TM,\end{array}\right.

where
g_{y}(y,\cdot):=g_{ij}(x,y)y^{i}{\dd}x^{j}
.
For a smooth function
f:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, its
gradient
is given by
\nabla f:=\mathfrak{L}^{-1}({\dd}f)
; consequently,
{\dd}f(X)=g_{\nabla f}(\nabla f,X)
on the set
\{{\dd}f\neq 0\}
.

The
co-metric
(or
dual metric
)
F^{*}
of
F
is
defined by

(2.5)

F^{*}(x,\eta):=\underset{y\in T_{x}M\backslash\{0\}}{\sup}\frac{\langle\eta,y\rangle}{F(x,y)},\qquad\forall\eta\in T_{x}^{*}M,

which itself is a Finsler metric on
T^{*}M
. Here
\langle\eta,y\rangle:=\eta(y)

denotes the canonical pairing between
T^{*}_{x}M

and
T_{x}M
. A Cauchy–Schwarz type inequality then holds:

(2.6)

\langle\eta,y\rangle\leq F^{*}(x,\eta)F(x,y),\qquad\forall\eta\in T^{*}_{x}M,\ y\in T_{x}M,

with equality if and only if
\eta=\alpha\mathfrak{L}(x,y)
for some
\alpha>0
. In particular,

(2.7)

F^{*}(\mathfrak{L}(x,y))=F(x,y),\qquad\forall(x,y)\in TM,

A smooth curve
t\mapsto\gamma(t)
in
M
is called a (constant-speed)
geodesic
if it satisfies

\frac{{\dd}^{2}\gamma^{i}}{{\dd}t^{2}}+2G^{i}\left(\gamma,\frac{{\dd}\gamma}{{\dd}t}\right)=0,

where the
geodesic coefficient

G^{i}
is defined by

\displaystyle G^{i}(x,y):=\frac{1}{4}g^{il}(x,y)\left\{2\frac{\partial g_{jl}}{\partial x^{k}}(x,y)-\frac{\partial g_{jk}}{\partial x^{l}}(x,y)\right\}y^{j}y^{k}.

In this paper,
\gamma_{y}(t)
always denotes a geodesic with
\dot{\gamma}_{y}(0)=y
.
A Finsler manifold
(M,F)
is
forward complete
if every geodesic
t\mapsto\gamma(t)
,
0\leq t<1
, can be extended to a geodesic defined on
0\leq t<+\infty
; similarly,
(M,F)
is
backward complete
if every geodesic
t\mapsto\gamma(t)
,
0<t\leq 1
, can be extended to a geodesic defined on
-\infty<t\leq 1
.

The
Riemannian curvature

R_{y}
of
F
is a family of linear transformations on tangent spaces. Explicitly, it is given by

R_{y}:=R^{i}_{k}(x,y)\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\otimes{\dd}x^{k}
, where

R^{i}_{\,k}(x,y):=2\frac{\partial G^{i}}{\partial x^{k}}-y^{j}\frac{\partial^{2}G^{i}}{\partial x^{j}\partial y^{k}}+2G^{j}\frac{\partial^{2}G^{i}}{\partial y^{j}\partial y^{k}}-\frac{\partial G^{i}}{\partial y^{j}}\frac{\partial G^{j}}{\partial y^{k}}.

For a plane
\Pi:=\text{Span}\{y,v\}\subset T_{x}M
, the
flag curvature
is defined by

\mathbf{K}(y,\Pi):=\mathbf{K}(y,v):=\frac{g_{y}\left(R_{y}(v),v\right)}{g_{y}(y,y)g_{y}(v,v)-g^{2}_{y}(y,v)}.

For a Riemannian metric, the flag curvature reduces to the sectional curvature.
A
Cartan–Hadamard manifold
is a simply connected, forward complete Finsler manifold
with non-positive flag curvature.

The
Ricci curvature
of
y\in T_{x}M\backslash\{0\}
is defined by

\mathbf{Ric}(x,y):=\frac{1}{F^{2}(x,y)}\sum_{i}\mathbf{K}(y,e_{i}),

where
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
is a
g_{y}
-orthonormal basis of
T_{x}M
. A Finsler manifold
(M,F)
is said to
satisfy
\mathbf{Ric}\geq k
for some
k\in\mathbb{R}
if

\mathbf{Ric}(x,y)\geq kF(x,y),\qquad\forall(x,y)\in TM.

Let
\zeta:[0,1]\rightarrow M
be a Lipschitz continuous path. Its
length
is defined by

L_{F}(\zeta):=\int^{1}_{0}F({\zeta}(t),\dot{\zeta}(t))dt.

The associated
distance function

d_{F}:M\times M\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
is defined as

d_{F}(x_{1},x_{2}):=\inf L_{F}(\zeta)
,
where the infimum is taken over all
Lipschitz continuous paths
\zeta:[0,1]\rightarrow M
with

\zeta(0)=x_{1}
and
\zeta(1)=x_{2}
. Generally
d_{F}(x_{1},x_{2})\neq d_{F}(x_{2},x_{1})
unless
F
is reversible.

The
forward
and
backward metric balls
centered at
x\in M
with radii
R>0
are defined respectively by

(2.8)

B^{+}_{R}(x):=\{z\in M:\,d_{F}(x,z)<R\},\qquad B^{-}_{R}(x):=\{z\in M:\,d_{F}(z,x)<R\}.

The closure of a forward (resp., backward) metric ball with finite radius is compact if
(M,F)
is forward (resp., backward) complete.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a smooth positive measure on
M
; in a local coordinate system
(x^{i})
we
express
{\dd}\mathfrak{m}=\sigma(x){\dd}x^{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge{\dd}x^{n}
. In particular,
the
Busemann–Hausdorff measure

\mathfrak{m}_{BH}
is defined by

\displaystyle{\dd}\mathfrak{m}_{BH}:=\frac{\omega_{n}}{\vol(B_{x}M)}{\dd}x^{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge{\dd}x^{n},

where
B_{x}M:=\{y\in T_{x}M:F(x,y)<1\}
and
\omega_{n}:=\vol(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1})
is the volume of Euclidean
n
-dimensional unit ball. In the Riemannian setting,
\mathfrak{m}_{BH}
coincides with the canonical Riemannian measure.

For convenience, a triple
(M,F,\m)
is called a
Finsler metric measure manifold
(
\FMMM
) if
(M,F)
is a forward complete Finsler manifold endowed with a smooth positive measure
\m
.

Let
(M,F,\m)
be a
\FMMM
.
Define the
distortion
as

\tau(x,y):=\log\frac{\sqrt{\det g_{ij}(x,y)}}{\sigma(x)},\qquad\forall y\in T_{x}M\backslash\{0\},

and the
S
-curvature
as

(2.9)

\mathbf{S}(x,y):=\left.\frac{\dd}{{\dd}t}\right|_{t=0}\tau(\gamma_{y}(t),\dot{\gamma}_{y}(t)).

Equivalently (cf. Shen
[
34
]
),

(2.10)

\mathbf{S}(x,y)=\frac{\partial G^{i}}{\partial y^{i}}(x,y)-y^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\ln\sigma(x).

And
(M,F,\m)
is said to
satisfy
\mathbf{S}\geq h
for some
h\in\mathbb{R}
if

\mathbf{S}(x,y)\geq h\,F(x,y),\qquad\forall(x,y)\in TM\backslash\{0\}.

The remainder of this subsection is devoted to the polar coordinate system in the Finsler setting.
Fix a point
o\in M
and set
S_{o}M:=\{z\in T_{o}M\,:\,F(o,z)=1\}
.
The
cut value

i_{y}
of
y\in S_{o}M
and the
injectivity radius

\mathfrak{i}_{o}
at
o
are defined by

\displaystyle i_{y}:=\sup\{t>0:\text{the geodesic }\gamma_{y}|_{[0,t]}\text{ is globally minimizing}\},\qquad\mathfrak{i}_{o}:=\inf_{y\in S_{o}M}i_{y}>0,

where
\gamma_{y}(t)
is a (unit-speed) geodesic with
\dot{\gamma}_{y}(0)=y
.

Let
(r,y)
be the
polar coordinate system
around some point
o\in M
. That is, if
x=(r,y)
, then
r=r(x)=d_{F}(o,x)
and
y=y(x)\in S_{o}M
such that

(2.11)

r\leq i_{y},\quad\gamma_{y}(r)=(r,y),\quad\dot{\gamma}_{y}(r)=\nabla r|_{(r,y)}.

Moreover,
it follows from
[
34
, Lemma 3.2.3]
that
F(\nabla r)=F^{*}({\dd}r)=1
for
\m
-a.e.
x\in M
.

In the polar coordinate system around
o
, the measure
\m
decomposes as

\dm|_{(r,y)}=:\hat{\sigma}_{o}(r,y)\,{\dd}r\wedge{\dd}\nu_{o}(y),

where
{\dd}\nu_{o}
denotes the Riemannian volume form of the indicatrix
S_{o}M
. Consequently,

(2.12)

\int_{M}f\dm=\int_{S_{o}M}{\dd}\nu_{o}(y)\int_{0}^{i_{y}}f(r,y)\,\hat{\sigma}_{o}(r,y)\,{\dd}r,\quad\forall f\in L^{1}(M,\m).

For convenience, we also introduce the

integral of distortion
(cf. Huang–Kristály–Zhao
[
15
]
)

(2.13)

\mathscr{I}_{\m}(o):=\int_{S_{o}M}e^{-\tau(o,y)}{\dd}\nu_{o}(y)<+\infty.

To state the volume comparison result of Zhao–Shen
[
39
, Theorem 3.6]
, we first set

\mathfrak{s}_{k}(t):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{\sin(\sqrt{k}t)}{\sqrt{k}},&&\text{ if }k>0,\\[3.0pt]
\ \ \ \ t,&&\text{ if }k=0,\\
\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{-k}t)}{\sqrt{-k}},&&\text{ if }k<0.\end{array}\right.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
39
]
)
.

Let
(M,F,\m)
be an
n
-dimensional
\FMMM
. If
\mathbf{Ric}\geq(n-1)k
for some
k\in\mathbb{R}
, then for every
o\in M
and
y\in S_{o}M
, the function

(2.14)

H_{y}(r):=\frac{\hat{\sigma}_{o}(r,y)}{e^{-\tau\big(\gamma_{y}(r),\dot{\gamma}_{y}(r)\big)}\mathfrak{s}_{k}^{n-1}(r)},\qquad\forall r\in(0,i_{y}),

is monotonically decreasing in r and satisfies
\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}H_{y}(r)=1
,
where
(r,y)
is the polar coordinate system around
o
and
\gamma_{y}(t)
is a geodesic with
\dot{\gamma}_{y}(0)=y
.

Remark 2.2
.

Since
\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}H_{y}(r)=1
and
S_{o}M
is compact, there exists
\epsilon\in(0,\min\{1,\mathfrak{i}_{o}\})
such that

(2.15)

2^{-1}e^{-\tau(o,y)}r^{n-1}\leq\hat{\sigma}_{o}(r,y)\leq 2e^{-\tau(o,y)}r^{n-1},\quad\forall r\in(0,\epsilon),\ y\in S_{o}M.

2.2.
Sobolev spaces induced by Finsler structure

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(M,F,\m)
be an
\FMMM
.
For
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(M)
and
p\in[1,+\infty)
, define a pseudo-norm as

(2.16)

\|u\|_{W^{1,p}_{\m}}:=\left(\int_{M}|u|^{p}\dm\right)^{1/p}+\left(\int_{M}F^{*p}({\dd}u)\dm\right)^{1/p}.

The
Sobolev space

W^{1,p}_{0}(M,F,\m)
is defined as the closure of
C^{\infty}_{0}(M)
with respect to the backward topology induced by
\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,p}_{\m}}
, i.e.,

u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(M,F,\m)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists\,(u_{k})\subset C^{\infty}_{0}(M)\text{ \ with }\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|u-u_{k}\|_{W^{1,p}_{\m}}=0.

Remark 2.4
.

If a function
f
is differentiable at
x\in M
, then the definition of gradient and (
2.7
) imply

(2.17)

F^{*}(x,{\dd}f)=F(x,\nabla f).

Hence,
W^{1,p}_{0}(M,F,\m)
admits an equivalent characterization in terms of the gradient, i.e.,

\|u\|_{W^{1,p}_{\m}}=\left(\int_{M}|u|^{p}\dm\right)^{1/p}+\left(\int_{M}F^{p}(\nabla u)\dm\right)^{1/p}.

Consequently,
W^{1,p}_{0}(M,F,\m)
is the standard Sobolev space when
F
is Riemannian.
In the general Finsler setting,
however, since the gradient is nonlinear (
\nabla(u_{1}+u_{2})\neq\nabla u_{1}+\nabla u_{2}
), it is therefore
more convenient to define Sobolev spaces via the differential.
The triangle inequality for the co-metric
F^{*}
implies that for any
u,v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(M,F,\m)
:

(i)

\|u\|_{W^{1,p}_{\m}}\geq 0
with equality if and only if
u=0
;

(ii)

\|\lambda\,u\|_{W^{1,p}_{\m}}=\lambda\|u\|_{W^{1,p}_{\m}}
for any
\lambda\geq 0
;

(iii)

\|u+v\|_{W^{1,p}_{\m}}\leq\|u\|_{W^{1,p}_{\m}}+\|v\|_{W^{1,p}_{\m}}
.

Remark 2.5
.

The pseudo-norm
\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,p}_{\m}}
induces a “metric” on
W^{1,p}_{0}(M,F,\m)
by

\mathsf{D}_{p}(u,v):=\|v-u\|_{W^{1,p}_{\m}}
.
Since
\mathsf{D}_{p}
could be not symmetric, one defines
forward
and
backward balls
in the same way as (
2.8
).
The
forward topology

\mathcal{T}_{+}
(resp.,
backward topology

\mathcal{T}_{-}
) is the topology
generated by forward balls (resp., backward balls).
Consequently, a sequence
(u_{k})\subset W^{1,p}_{0}(M,F,\m)
is convergent to
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(M,F,\m)
under
\mathcal{T}_{+}
(resp.,
\mathcal{T}_{-}
) if
\mathsf{D}_{p}(u,u_{k})\rightarrow 0
(resp.,
\mathsf{D}_{p}(u_{k},u)\rightarrow 0
).
In particular,
W^{1,p}_{0}(M,F,\m)
is constructed by the backward topology
\mathcal{T}_{-}
.

Definition 2.6
.

Given a set
U\subset M
, a function
f:U\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called
Lipschitz
if there exists a constant
C\geq 0
such that

|f(x_{1})-f(x_{2})|\leq C\,d_{F}(x_{1},x_{2}),\quad\forall x_{1},x_{2}\in U.

The minimal
C
satisfying the above inequality is called the
dilatation
(or
Lipschitz constant
) of
f
, denoted by
\mathsf{dil}(f)
.

Let
\mathsf{Lip}_{0}(U):=C_{0}(U)\cap\mathsf{Lip}(U)
denote the collection of compactly supported Lipschitz functions on
U
.
The following approximation result is standard; a proof can be found in Kristály–Li–Zhao
[
22
, Section 3]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
(M,F,\m)
be an
\FMMM
. For every
u\in\mathsf{Lip}_{0}(M)
, there exists a sequence of smooth Lipschitz functions
(u_{k})\subset C^{\infty}_{0}(M)\cap\mathsf{Lip}_{0}(M)
such that

\displaystyle\supp u\cup\supp u_{k}\subset\mathscr{K},\quad|u_{k}|\leq C,\quad F^{*}({\dd}u_{k})\leq\mathsf{dil}(u_{k})\leq C,

\displaystyle\quad\sup_{M}|u_{k}-u|\rightarrow 0,\qquad\|u_{k}-u\|_{W^{1,p}_{\m}}+\|u-u_{k}\|_{W^{1,p}_{\m}}\rightarrow 0,

where
\mathscr{K}\subset M
is a compact set and
C>0
is a constant. In particular, if
u
is nonnegative, so are
u_{k}
’s.

Remark 2.8
.

The last convergence in Lemma
2.7
is equivalent to
\|u_{k}-u\|_{W^{1,p}_{\m}}\to 0
. Indeed,

\|u_{k}-u\|_{W^{1,p}_{\m}}\leq\|u_{k}-u\|_{W^{1,p}_{\m}}+\|u-u_{k}\|_{W^{1,p}_{\m}}\leq 2\lambda_{F}(\mathscr{K})\,\|u_{k}-u\|_{W^{1,p}_{\m}}.

The local equivalence of
d_{F}
to the Euclidean metric
[
3
, (6.2.3)]
guarantees the validity of Rademacher’s theorem in Finsler geometry; thus every Lipschitz function is differentiable almost everywhere, which motivates the next definition.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
(M,F,\m)
be an
n
-dimensional
\FMMM
. Fix a point
o\in M
and let
r(x):=d_{F}(o,x)
denote the distance function from
o
.
Define three functionals on
\mathsf{Lip}_{0}(M)\backslash\{0\}
as follows:

(i)

for
p\in(1,n)
, set

\mathscr{H}_{o,p}(f):=\frac{\int_{M}{F}^{*p}({\dd}f)\dm}{\int_{M}{|f|^{p}}{r^{-p}}\dm};

(ii)

for
1-p<s\leq 1<p<n
, set

\mathscr{U}_{o,p,s}(f):=\frac{\Big(\int_{M}F^{*p}({\rm d}f)\dm\Big)^{1/p}\left(\int_{M}|f|^{p}r^{p^{\prime}s}\dm\right)^{1/{p^{\prime}}}}{\int_{M}|f|^{p}r^{s-1}\dm};

(iii)

for
1<p<m
and
p(n+s-1)>m(n-p)>0
, set

\mathscr{C}_{o,p,m,s}(f):=\frac{\Big(\int_{{M}}F^{*p}({\rm d}f){\dm}\Big)^{1/p}\left(\int_{{M}}{|f|^{p^{\prime}(m-1)}}{r^{p^{\prime}s}}{\dm}\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}}{\int_{{M}}{|f|^{m}}{r^{s-1}}{\dm}}.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
(M,F,\m)
be an
n
-dimensional
\FMMM
and let
\mathbf{J}
denote any of the functionals
\mathscr{H}_{o,p}
,
\mathscr{U}_{o,p,s}
or
\mathscr{C}_{o,p,m,s}
from Definition
2.9
. Then
\mathbf{J}
is well-defined on
\mathsf{Lip}_{0}(M)\backslash\{0\}
, and

(2.18)

\displaystyle\inf_{f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(M)\backslash\{0\}}\mathbf{J}(f)=\inf_{f\in\mathsf{Lip}_{0}(M)\backslash\{0\}}\mathbf{J}(f).

Proof.

Given
f\in\mathsf{Lip}_{0}(M)\backslash\{0\}
, a direct calculation combined with (
2.6
) yields

\displaystyle\limsup_{z\rightarrow x}\frac{|f(z)-f(x)|}{d_{F}(x,z)}=\sup_{y\in S_{x}M}\left(\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{|f(\gamma_{y}(t))-f(\gamma_{y}(0))|}{t}\right)=\sup_{y\in S_{x}M}|\langle{\dd}f,y\rangle|=\max\{F^{*}(x,\pm{\dd}f)\},

where
\gamma_{y}(t)
denotes a unit-speed geodesic from
x
with
\dot{\gamma}_{y}(0)=y
.
Hence,

F^{*}(x,{\dd}f)\leq\limsup_{z\rightarrow x}\frac{|f(z)-f(x)|}{d_{F}(x,z)}\leq\mathsf{dil}(f),\qquad\text{for $\m$-a.e. $x\in M$},

which together with the compactness of
\supp(f)
yields
\int_{M}{F}^{*p}({\dd}f)\dm<+\infty
. Choose
R>1
such that
\supp(f)\subset B^{+}_{R}(o)
. Note that
\overline{B^{+}_{R}(o)}
is compact since
(M,F)
is forward complete. Choose a small
\epsilon>0
as in Remark
2.2
. Then, by (
2.12
)–(
2.15
) we have

\displaystyle 0<\int_{M}{|f|^{p}}{r^{-p}}\dm

\displaystyle\leq\max|f|\int_{B^{+}_{R}(o)}r^{-p}\dm\leq\max|f|\left(\int_{B^{+}_{\epsilon}(o)}+\int_{B^{+}_{R}(o)\backslash B^{+}_{\epsilon}(o)}r^{-p}\dm\right)

(2.19)

\displaystyle\leq\max|f|\left(2\mathscr{I}_{\m}(o)\int^{\epsilon}_{0}r^{n-p-1}{\dd}r+\epsilon^{-p}\m\left[B^{+}_{R}(o)\right]\right)<+\infty.

Thus,
\mathscr{H}_{o,p}
is well-defined; the other two functionals are treated similarly. Equality (
2.18
) follows from Lemma
2.7
and an estimate analogous to (
2.19
).
∎

3.
Berwald’s metric space

This section investigates the Finsler manifold introduced by Berwald
[
4
,
5
]
, which arose from Hilbert’s fourth problem. Subsection
3.1
presents its basic geometric properties, while Subsection
3.2
establishes the nonlinearity of its Sobolev spaces and the failure of several classical functional inequalities. These results collectively provide the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. All arguments in this and the following section proceed by direct computation, which yields stronger and more explicit results than would be possible by invoking advanced tools such as volume comparison theorems.

3.1.
Background of Berwald’s metric space

Definition 3.1

(
[
4
,
5
]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})
be the Euclidean unit ball centered at
\mathbf{0}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Berwald’s metric
is defined by

(3.1)

\B(x,y):=\frac{(\sqrt{(1-|x|^{2})|y|^{2}+\langle x,y\rangle^{2}}+\langle x,y\rangle)^{2}}{(1-|x|^{2})^{2}\sqrt{(1-|x|^{2})|y|^{2}+\langle x,y\rangle^{2}}},\qquad\forall(x,y)\in T\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}).

The pair
(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}),\B)
is called
Berwald’s metric space
.

Remark 3.2
.

According to
[
3
,
34
]
, a Finsler metric is said to be
of Berwald type
if the Chern connection can be viewed as a linear connection on the underlying manifold; such metrics form an intermediate class between Riemannian and general Finsler structures.
The metric
\B
defined in (
3.1
), however, is not of Berwald type. In what follows, the term “Berwald’s metric” will always refer to the metric given by (
3.1
).

The manifold
(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}),{\B})
is
projectively flat
: the image of every geodesic is a straight line (cf.
[
35
,
37
]
). Its geodesic coefficients are given by
G^{i}(x,y)=P(x,y)y^{i}
with

(3.2)

P(x,y)=\frac{\sqrt{(1-|x|^{2})|y|^{2}+\langle x,y\rangle^{2}}+\langle x,y\rangle}{1-|x|^{2}}.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}),\B,\mathscr{L}^{n})
be Berwald’s metric space endowed with the Lebesgue measure. The following statements are true:

(i)

the distance functions
{d}_{\B}(\mathbf{0},x)
and
{d}_{\B}(x,\mathbf{0})
are given by

(3.3)

d_{\B}(\mathbf{0},x)=\frac{|x|}{1-|x|},\qquad d_{\B}(x,\mathbf{0})=\frac{|x|}{1+|x|};

(ii)

the Finsler manifold
(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}),\B)
is forward complete (but not backward complete) with

\lambda_{\B}(x)=\left(\frac{1+|x|}{1-|x|}\right)^{2},\qquad\lambda_{\B}\left(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})\right)=+\infty;

(iii)

the gradient of the distance function
r(x):=d_{\B}(\mathbf{0},x)
is given by

(3.4)

\nabla r(x)=\frac{(1-|x|)^{2}}{|x|}x;

(iv)

(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}),\B,\mathscr{L}^{n})
is a Cartan–Hadamard manifold with
\mathbf{K}=0
and
\mathbf{S}\in(0,2(n+1))
. In particular,

(3.5)

\mathbf{S}(x,\nabla r)=\frac{n+1}{1+r(x)}.

Proof.

(
i
)

The projective flatness implies that the geodesic starting from
\mathbf{0}
to
x
has the same trajectory as the radial segment
\gamma(t)=tx
,
t\in[0,1]
.
Hence

\displaystyle{d}_{\B}(\mathbf{0},x)

\displaystyle=\int^{1}_{0}\B(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t)){\dd}t=\int^{1}_{0}\frac{|x|}{(1-t|x|)^{2}}{\dd}t=\frac{|x|}{1-|x|},

\displaystyle{d}_{\B}(x,\mathbf{0})

\displaystyle=\int^{1}_{0}\B(\gamma(t),-\dot{\gamma}(t)){\dd}t=\int^{1}_{0}\frac{|x|}{(1+t|x|)^{2}}{\dd}t=\frac{|x|}{1+|x|}.

(
ii
)

By the definition of reversibility (
2.3
), we have

\lambda_{\B}(x)=\sup_{y\in T_{x}\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})\setminus\{0\}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{(1-|x|^{2})|y|^{2}+\langle x,y\rangle^{2}}+\langle x,y\rangle}{\sqrt{(1-|x|^{2})|y|^{2}+\langle x,y\rangle^{2}}-\langle x,y\rangle}\right)^{2}=\left(\frac{1+|x|}{1-|x|}\right)^{2},

which yields
\lambda_{\B}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}))=+\infty
.

Let
c(t)
,
t\in[0,1)
be a unit-speed geodesic. The projective flatness combined with the triangle inequality and (
3.3
) yields

(3.6)

t=d_{\B}(c(0),c(t))\geq d_{\B}(\mathbf{0},c(t))-d_{\B}(\mathbf{0},c(0))=\frac{|c(t)|}{1-|c(t)|}-\frac{|c(0)|}{1-|c(0)|},

which implies
\limsup_{t\rightarrow 1^{-}}|c(t)|<1
.
Set
c(1):=\lim_{t\rightarrow 1^{-}}c(t)
; the limit exists because
c(t)
is a ray. The geodesic ODE then implies that
c(t)
can be extended to an interval
[0,1+\varepsilon)
for some
\varepsilon>0
with
\lim_{t\rightarrow(1+\varepsilon)^{-}}|c(t)|<1
.
Inductively we can extend
c(t)
along the direction
\dot{c}(0)
until
|c(t)|\rightarrow 1
,
which by the estimate above in (
3.6
) occurs only as
t\rightarrow+\infty
. Thus
(\Omega,F)
is forward complete.

On the other hand, (
3.3
) implies
{d}_{\B}(x,\mathbf{0})\rightarrow 1/2
as
|x|\rightarrow 1
.
Consequently, the backward bounded closed ball
\overline{B^{-}_{1/2}(\mathbf{0})}=\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})
is not compact,
so backward completeness fails.

(
iii
)

Let
(r,y)
denote the polar coordinate system of
(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}),\B)
around
\mathbf{0}
.
For
x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\}
, write
x=(r,y)
and let
\gamma_{y}(t)
be the unit-speed geodesic with
\dot{\gamma}_{y}(0)=y\in S_{\mathbf{0}}\Omega
. Then
\gamma_{y}(r)=x
, and
x
is parallel to
y
since
F
is projectively flat.
Hence, we may write
x^{i}=h(r,y)y^{i}
for some nonnegative function
h(r,y)
.
Substituting this into (
3.3
) yields

r=\frac{|x|}{1-|x|}=\frac{h(r,y)|y|}{1-h(r,y)|y|},

which implies

h(r,y)=\frac{r}{(1+r)|y|},\quad x^{i}=\frac{r}{(1+r)|y|}y^{i},\quad\gamma_{y}(t)=\frac{ty}{(1+t)|y|}.

Using (
2.11
) in combination with the relations above, we obtain

\nabla r(x)=\dot{\gamma}_{y}(r)=\left.\frac{{\dd}\gamma_{y}^{i}}{{\dd}t}\right|_{t=r}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}=\frac{y^{i}}{(1+r)^{2}|y|}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}=\frac{x^{i}}{r(1+r)}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}=\frac{(1-|x|)^{2}}{|x|}x.

(
iv
)

The flatness (i.e.,
\mathbf{K}=0
) follows from
[
5
,
37
]
. Using (
2.10
), (
3.2
), and (
3.4
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{S}(x,y)}{\B(x,y)}=(n+1)\frac{P(x,y)}{\B(x,y)}=(n+1)\sqrt{(1-|x|^{2})+\frac{\langle x,y\rangle^{2}}{|y|^{2}}}\left(\sqrt{(1-|x|^{2})+\frac{\langle x,y\rangle^{2}}{|y|^{2}}}-\frac{\langle x,y\rangle}{|y|}\right),

which implies
\mathbf{S}\in(0,2(n+1))
and (
3.5
).
∎

Now we study the co-metric
\B^{*}
of
\B
. Given the complexity of the formula for
\B
, it seems impossible to express
\B^{*}
in an explicit way. Instead, we investigate this problem from the perspective of
(\alpha,\beta)
-metric. Recall that
a Finsler metric
F
is called an
(\alpha,\beta)
-metric
if there exists a smooth function
\phi(s)
such that

F=\alpha\,\phi(\beta/\alpha)
,
where
\alpha=\sqrt{a_{ij}(x)y^{i}y^{j}}
is a Riemannian metric and
\beta
is a
1
-form.
Such metrics, introduced by Matsumoto
[
27
]
, form a rich and widely studied family in Finsler geometry.
Berwald’s metric
\B
is a special
(\alpha,\beta)
-metric:

(3.7)

\B=\alpha(1+s)^{2},\qquad s=\frac{\beta}{\alpha},

with

(3.8)

\alpha=\alpha(x,y)=\frac{\sqrt{(1-|x|^{2})|y|^{2}+\langle x,y\rangle^{2}}}{(1-|x|^{2})^{2}},\qquad\beta=\beta(x,y)=\frac{\langle x,y\rangle}{(1-|x|^{2})^{2}}.

Following the approach of Masca–Sabau–Shimada
[
26
]
, we derive a quartic equation satisfied by
\B^{*}
. The proof is given in Appendix
A
.

Lemma 3.4
.

The co-metric
\B^{*}=\B^{*}(x,\xi)
of Berwald’s metric
\B
satisfies the quartic equation

(3.9)

\begin{split}&16|x|^{2}(1-|x|^{2})^{2}\Big\{(1-|x|^{2})\alpha^{*2}+\beta^{*2}\Big\}{\B}^{*4}+8\Big\{(10|x|^{2}-1)(1-|x|^{2})\alpha^{*2}\beta^{*}+(9|x|^{2}-1)\beta^{*3}\Big\}{\B}^{*3}\\
&+\Big\{(1-20|x|^{2}-8|x|^{4})\alpha^{*4}+6(6|x|^{2}-5)\alpha^{*2}\beta^{*2}-27\beta^{*4}\Big\}{\B}^{*2}+12\alpha^{*4}\beta^{*}{\B}^{*}-\alpha^{*6}=0,\end{split}

where
\alpha^{*2}=\alpha^{*2}(x,\xi)=a^{ij}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}
,
\beta^{*}=\beta^{*}(x,\xi)=b^{i}\xi_{i}
and

(3.10)

a^{ij}=(1-|x|^{2})^{3}(\delta^{ij}-x^{i}x^{j}),\qquad b^{i}=(1-|x|^{2})^{2}x^{i}.

Example 1
.

In this example, we apply Lemma
3.4
to verify that the differential of the distance function has unit length, i.e.,

(3.11)

{\B}^{*}(x,{\dd}r)=1,\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\},

where
r(x):=d_{\B}(\mathbf{0},x)
.
In fact, owing to (
3.3
), we get

(3.12)

{\dd}r=\sum_{i}^{n}\frac{x^{i}}{(1-|x|)^{2}|x|}{\dd}x^{i},\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\}.

By the definition of co-metric (
2.5
), we have

(3.13)

\displaystyle\B^{*}(x,{\dd}r)=\sup_{y\in T_{x}\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\langle{\dd}r,y\rangle}{\B(x,y)}=(1+|x|)^{2}\sup_{|y|=1}\frac{\langle x,y\rangle}{|x|}\frac{\sqrt{(1-|x|^{2})+\langle x,y\rangle^{2}}}{\left[\sqrt{(1-|x|^{2})+\langle x,y\rangle^{2}}+\langle x,y\rangle\right]^{2}}.

Thus, it suffices to consider the case when
\langle x,y\rangle\geq 0
and
|y|=1
.
By setting
\theta:=\angle x\mathbf{0}y
(the angle subtended by
x
and
y
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\langle x,y\rangle}{|x|}\frac{\sqrt{(1-|x|^{2})+\langle x,y\rangle^{2}}}{\left[\sqrt{(1-|x|^{2})+\langle x,y\rangle^{2}}+\langle x,y\rangle\right]^{2}}=\frac{\cos\theta}{\sqrt{1-|x|^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}}\frac{1}{\left[1+\frac{|x|\cos\theta}{\sqrt{1-|x|^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}}\right]^{2}}\leq 1.

Hence, (
3.13
) yields
\B^{*}(x,{\dd}r)\leq 2
for
x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\}
.
On the other hand, (
3.10
) and (
3.12
) imply

(3.14)

\displaystyle\alpha^{*2}(x,{\dd}r)=(1+|x|)^{4},\qquad\beta^{*}(x,{\dd}r)=|x|(1+|x|)^{2}.

Substituting the above expressions into (
3.9
)
\div(1+|x|)^{6}
yields

({\B}^{*}(x,{\dd}r)-1)\Big\{({\B}^{*}(x,{\dd}r)(1-|x|)^{2}+(1+|x|)^{2}\Big\}\Big\{4|x|{\B}^{*}(x,{\dd}r)-(1+|x|)^{2}\Big\}^{2}=0,

which can be simplified into

(3.15)

\left({\B}^{*}(x,{\dd}r)-1\right)\Big\{4|x|{\B}^{*}(x,{\dd}r)-(1+|x|)^{2}\Big\}=0.

Since
{\B}^{*}(x,{\dd}r)\leq 2
, by letting
|x|\rightarrow 0
in (
3.15
) we obtain
{\B}^{*}(x,{\dd}r)=1
for small
|x|
.

Suppose that the second factor of (
3.15
) will vanish in
\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})
. Let
x_{0}
be such a point with the smallest Euclidean norm. Then
{\B}^{*}(x,{\dd}r)=1
for any
0<|x|<|x_{0}|
. By the continuity, we have two equations:

{\B}^{*}(x_{0},{\dd}r)=1,\qquad 4|x_{0}|{\B}^{*}(x_{0},{\dd}r)-(1+|x_{0}|)^{2}=0,

which gives
|x_{0}|=1
, contradicting
x_{0}\in\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})
.
Consequently, the first factor in (
3.15
) vanishes identically and hence (
3.11
) follows.

3.2.
Analytic properties of Berwald’s metric space

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}),{\B},\mathscr{L}^{n})
be Berwald’s metric space endowed with the Lebesgue measure.
Define a function
w
on
\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})
by

(3.16)

w(x):=-\left[\ln\left(\frac{2-|x|}{1-|x|}\right)\right]^{-\frac{1}{n}}.

Then
w\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}),\B,\mathscr{L}^{n})
for every
p\in[1,+\infty)
.

Proof.

The proof proceeds in two steps.

Step 1.
We show
\|w\|_{W^{1,p}_{\mathscr{L}^{n}}}<+\infty
.

Let
x=(t,y)
denote the Euclidean polar coordinates. Using
\ln(\frac{2-t}{1-t})\geq\ln 2
for
t\in[0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}|w|^{p}{\dd}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}\left[\ln\left(\frac{2-|x|}{1-|x|}\right)\right]^{-\frac{p}{n}}{\dd}x=n\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{1}\left[\ln\left(\frac{2-t}{1-t}\right)\right]^{-\frac{p}{n}}t^{n-1}{\dd}t

(3.17)

\displaystyle\leq n\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{1}(\ln 2)^{-\frac{p}{n}}t^{n-1}{\dd}t=\frac{\omega_{n}}{(\ln 2)^{\frac{p}{n}}}<+\infty.

By (
3.3
), note that
w(x)=-[\ln(2+r(x))]^{-\frac{1}{n}}
, where
r(x)=d_{\B}(\mathbf{0},x)=|x|/(1-|x|)
. A direct calculation combined with (
3.12
) gives

(3.18)

\displaystyle{\dd}w

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial w}{\partial r}{\dd}r=\frac{1-|x|}{n(2-|x|)}\left[\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{1-|x|}\right)\right]^{-\frac{1}{n}-1}\sum^{n}_{i=1}\frac{x^{i}}{|x|(1-|x|)^{2}}{\dd}x^{i}.

Together with (
3.11
) this yields

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}{\B}^{*p}({\dd}w){\dd}x&=\frac{1}{n^{p}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}\left(\frac{1-|x|}{2-|x|}\right)^{p}\left[\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{1-|x|}\right)\right]^{-\frac{p}{n}-p}{\dd}x\\
&=\frac{n\omega_{n}}{n^{p}}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{1-t}{2-t}\right)^{p}\left[\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{1-t}\right)\right]^{-\frac{p}{n}-p}t^{n-1}{\dd}t.\end{split}

Since
\frac{1-t}{2-t}\leq\frac{1}{2}
and
\ln(1+\frac{1}{1-t})\geq\ln 2
for
t\in[0,1)
, we get

(3.19)

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}{\B}^{*p}({\dd}w){\dd}x\leq\frac{\omega_{n}}{2^{p}n^{p}}(\ln 2)^{-\frac{p}{n}-p}<+\infty.

Hence,
\|w\|_{W^{1,p}_{\mathscr{L}^{n}}}<+\infty
follows by (
3.17
) and (
3.19
).

Step 2.
We prove
w\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}),\B,\mathscr{L}^{n})
.

For every small
\varepsilon>0
, set

w_{\varepsilon}:=\begin{cases}-[\ln(2+\varepsilon)]^{-\frac{1}{n}},&\text{ if }r<\varepsilon,\\[4.0pt]
-[\ln(2+r)]^{-\frac{1}{n}}=w,&\text{ if }r\geq\varepsilon.\end{cases}

One checks directly that

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\|w-w_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{1,p}_{\mathscr{L}^{n}}}=0.

Now define
w_{\varepsilon,\delta}:=\min\{0,w_{\varepsilon}+\delta\}
for
\delta\in(0,(\ln 2)^{-1/n})
. If we show

(3.20)

w_{\varepsilon,\delta}\in\mathsf{Lip}_{0}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})),\qquad\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\|w_{\varepsilon}-w_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{W^{1,p}_{\mathscr{L}^{n}}}=0,

then a diagonal argument together with Lemma
2.7
implies that
w
can be approximated by smooth functions with compact supports;
consequently,
w\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}),\B,\mathscr{L}^{n})
.

To verify (
3.20
), note first that by projective flatness,
one can directly check
w_{\varepsilon,\delta}\in\mathsf{Lip}_{0}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}))
with

\supp(w_{\varepsilon,\delta})=\overline{\{w_{\varepsilon,\delta}<0\}}=\overline{\{r<\exp(\delta^{-n})-2\}}=\left\{|x|\leq\frac{\exp(\delta^{-n})-2}{\exp(\delta^{-n})-1}\right\}.

Moreover,
a direct calculation yields

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}|w_{\varepsilon}-w_{\varepsilon,\delta}|^{p}{\dd}x

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\{r<\exp(\delta^{-n})-2\}}\delta^{p}{\dd}x+\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\{r\geq\exp(\delta^{-n})-2\}}|w_{\varepsilon}|^{p}{\dd}x

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}\delta^{p}{\dd}x+\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\left\{|x|\geq\frac{\exp(\delta^{-n})-2}{\exp(\delta^{-n})-1}\right\}}|w|^{p}{\dd}x=0.

Similarly, since
\mathsf{B}^{*}({\dd}w)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}),\mathscr{L}^{n})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}\mathsf{B}^{*p}({\dd}w_{\varepsilon}-{\dd}w_{\varepsilon,\delta}){\dd}x\leq\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\left\{|x|\geq\frac{\exp(\delta^{-n})-2}{\exp(\delta^{-n})-1}\right\}}\mathsf{B}^{*p}({\dd}w){\dd}x=0.

Then (
3.20
) follows, which concludes the proof.
∎

Based on the above lemma, we have the following result.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}),{\B},\mathscr{L}^{n})
be Berwald’s metric space endowed with the Lebesgue measure.
Then the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}),\mathscr{L}^{n})
is not a vector space for all
p\in(1,+\infty)
.

Proof.

Let
w
be defined as in (
3.16
).
In view of Lemma
3.5
, it suffices to show
\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}{\B}^{*p}(-{\dd}w){\dd}x=+\infty
.
By (
3.14
) and (
3.18
), we have

\displaystyle\alpha^{*2}(x,-{\dd}w)=w_{r}^{2}\alpha^{*2}(x,{\dd}r)=w_{r}^{2}(1+|x|)^{4},\quad\beta^{*}(x,-{\dd}w)=-w_{r}\beta^{*}(x,{\dd}r)=-w_{r}|x|(1+|x|)^{2},

where

w_{r}:=\frac{\partial w}{\partial r}=\frac{1-|x|}{n(2-|x|)}\left[\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{1-|x|}\right)\right]^{-\frac{1}{n}-1}>0.

Substituting the above expressions into (
3.9
)
\div[(1+|x|)^{6}w_{r}^{2}]
gives

\displaystyle 16|x|^{2}(1-|x|)^{2}\,{\B}^{*4}(x,-{\dd}w)+8|x|(|x|^{4}-10|x|^{2}+1)w_{r}\,{\B}^{*3}(x,-{\dd}w)

\displaystyle+(1+|x|)^{2}(|x|^{4}-50|x|^{2}+1)w_{r}^{2}\,{\B}^{*2}(x,-{\dd}w)-12|x|(1+|x|)^{4}w_{r}^{3}\,{\B}^{*}(x,-{\dd}w)-(1+|x|)^{6}w_{r}^{4}=0,

which factors as

\displaystyle({\B}^{*}(x,-{\dd}w)+w_{r})\Big\{(1-|x|)^{2}\,{\B}^{*}(x,-{\dd}w)-(1+|x|)^{2}w_{r}\Big\}\Big\{4|x|\,{\B}^{*}(x,-{\dd}w)+(1+|x|)^{2}w_{r}\Big\}^{2}=0.

Because
{\dd}w\neq 0
, we have
{\B}^{*}(x,-{\dd}w)>0
; hence the second factor must vanish. Consequently,

(3.21)

\B^{*}(x,-{\dd}w)=\frac{(1+|x|)^{2}}{(1-|x|)^{2}}w_{r}=\frac{(1+|x|)^{2}}{n(1-|x|)(2-|x|)}\left[\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{1-|x|}\right)\right]^{-\frac{1}{n}-1}.

Given
p>1
, there exists a small
\vartheta>0
such that

(3.22)

p-\vartheta p\left(\frac{1}{n}+1\right)>1.

Moreover, choose a number
\delta=\delta(\vartheta)\in(0,1)
such that for
|x|\in(\delta,1)
,

\left[\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{1-|x|}\right)\right]^{-\frac{1}{n}-1}>\left[\frac{1}{1-|x|}-1\right]^{\vartheta(-\frac{1}{n}-1)}.

Combined with (
3.21
), these choices yield

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}{\B}^{*p}(x,-{\dd}w){\dd}x

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2^{p}n^{p}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}\frac{(1+|x|)^{2p}}{(1-|x|)^{p}}\left[\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{1-|x|}\right)\right]^{-\frac{p}{n}-p}{\dd}x,

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2^{p}n^{p}}\int_{\{\delta<|x|<1\}}\frac{(1+|x|)^{2p}}{(1-|x|)^{p}}\left[\frac{1}{1-|x|}-1\right]^{-\vartheta p(\frac{1}{n}+1)}{\dd}x

(3.23)

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{n}}{2^{p}n^{p-1}}\int_{\delta}^{1}\frac{(1+t)^{2p}t^{n-1-\vartheta p(\frac{1}{n}+1)}}{(1-t)^{p-\vartheta p(\frac{1}{n}+1)}}{\dd}t=+\infty,

where the last equality follows from (
3.22
).
This completes the proof.
∎

The nonlinearity of
W^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}),\B,\mathscr{L}^{n})
has its underlying reason in the incompatibility of the relevant topologies (see Remark
2.5
).

Corollary 3.7
.

For each
p\in(1,+\infty)
, the forward and backward topologies of

W^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}),\B,\mathscr{L}^{n})
are incompatible. In particular, there exist a sequence
(w_{k})
and
w
in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}),\B,\mathscr{L}^{n})
such that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|w-w_{k}\|_{W^{1,p}_{\mathscr{L}^{n}}}=0,\quad\|w_{k}-w\|_{W^{1,p}_{\mathscr{L}^{n}}}=+\infty,\ \forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

Let
w
be as in (
3.16
).
By Lemma
3.5
, there exists a sequence
(w_{k})\subset C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}))
such that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|w-w_{k}\|_{W^{1,p}_{\mathscr{L}^{n}}}=0
. It remains to show
\|w_{k}-w\|_{W^{1,p}_{\mathscr{L}^{n}}}=+\infty
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Suppose by contradiction that
\|w_{k_{0}}-w\|_{W^{1,p}_{\mathscr{L}^{n}}}<+\infty
for some
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the triangle inequality of
\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,p}_{\mathscr{L}^{n}}}
forces

\|-w\|_{W^{1,p}_{\mathscr{L}^{n}}}=\|0-w\|_{W^{1,p}_{\mathscr{L}^{n}}}\leq\|0-w_{k_{0}}\|_{W^{1,p}_{\mathscr{L}^{n}}}+\|w_{k_{0}}-w\|_{W^{1,p}_{\mathscr{L}^{n}}}<+\infty,

which contradicts the fact that
\|-w\|_{W^{1,p}_{\mathscr{L}^{n}}}=+\infty
by (
3.2
). This concludes the proof.
∎

In the remainder of this subsection,
we demonstrate the failure of several classical functional inequalities on Berwald’s metric space.
This phenomenon cannot be deduced from the results of Kristály–Li–Zhao
[
22
]
, because here the

S
-curvature does not admit a uniform positive lower bound (see (
3.5
)).

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}),\B,\mathscr{L}^{n})
be Berwald’s metric space endowed with the Lebesgue measure. Denote by
r(x)=d_{\B}(\mathbf{0},x)
the distance function from the origin.
Then the following statements are true:

(i)

the
L^{p}
-Hardy inequality fails for every
p\in(1,n)
, i.e.,

\inf_{f\in C^{\infty}_{0}({\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})})\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}{\B}^{*p}({\rm d}f){\dd}x}{\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}{|f|^{p}}{r^{-p}}{\dd}x}=0;

(ii)

the generalized uncertainty principle fails, i.e., for any
-p+1<s\leq 1<p<n
,

\inf_{f\in C^{\infty}_{0}({\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})})\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\left(\int_{{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}}{\B}^{*p}({\rm d}f){\dd}x\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}}|f|^{p}r^{p^{\prime}s}{\dd}x\right)^{1/{p^{\prime}}}}{\int_{{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}}|f|^{p}r^{s-1}{\dd}x}=0.

Proof.

Given
\iota\in(0,1)
, define a function
u_{\iota}
on
\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})
by

(3.24)

u_{\iota}(x):=-e^{-\iota r(x)}=-\exp\left({\frac{-\iota|x|}{1-|x|}}\right).

Since
\B^{*}({\dd}r)=1
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}{\B}^{*p}({\dd}u_{\iota}){\dd}x

\displaystyle=\iota^{p}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}|u_{\iota}|^{p}{\dd}x=\iota^{p}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}e^{\frac{-\iota p|x|}{1-|x|}}{\dd}x

(3.25)

\displaystyle=\iota^{p}n\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{1}e^{\frac{-\iota pt}{1-t}}t^{n-1}{\dd}t\leq\iota^{p}n\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{1}t^{n-1}{\dd}t={\iota^{p}\omega_{n}}.

(
i
)

Choose a small
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

e^{\frac{-t}{1-t}}>2^{-1}
for
t\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
.
A direct calculation then yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}\frac{|u_{\iota}|^{p}}{r^{p}}{\dd}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}\frac{(1-|x|)^{p}}{|x|^{p}}e^{\frac{-\iota p|x|}{1-|x|}}{\dd}x=n\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{p}t^{n-1-p}e^{\frac{-\iota pt}{1-t}}{\dd}t

(3.26)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n\omega_{n}(1-\varepsilon_{0})^{p}}{2^{\iota p}}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon_{0}}t^{n-1-p}{\dd}t\geq\frac{n\omega_{n}(1-\varepsilon_{0})^{p}\varepsilon_{0}^{n-p}}{2^{p}(n-p)}.

On the other hand, for every small
\epsilon>0
, set

u_{\iota,\epsilon}:=\begin{cases}-e^{-\iota\epsilon},&\text{ if }r<\epsilon,\\
-e^{-\iota r},&\text{ if }r\geq\epsilon,\end{cases}\qquad\tilde{u}_{\iota,\epsilon}:=\min\{0,u_{\iota,\epsilon}+\epsilon\}.

Then
\tilde{u}_{\iota,\epsilon}\in\mathsf{Lip}_{0}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}))
.
Now a direct calculation
yields

(3.27)

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{0},p}(\tilde{u}_{\iota,\epsilon})=\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{0},p}(u_{\iota})<+\infty,

where
\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{0},p}
is defined in Definition
2.9
.
Now Lemma
2.10
combined with (
3.25
)–(
3.27
) implies

\displaystyle\inf_{f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}))}\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{0},p}(f)=\inf_{f\in\mathsf{Lip}_{0}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}))}\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{0},p}(f)\leq\lim_{\iota\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{0},p}(u_{\iota})\leq\lim_{\iota\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{2^{p}(n-p)\iota^{p}}{n(1-\varepsilon_{0})^{p}\varepsilon_{0}^{n-p}}=0.

Hence, the Hardy inequality fails for
p\in(1,n)
.

(
ii
)

As before, choose a small
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

e^{\frac{-t}{1-t}}>2^{-1}
for
t\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
. Since
1-s\geq 0
and
n+s-1>0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}|u_{\iota}|^{p}r^{s-1}{\dd}x

\displaystyle=n\omega_{n}\int^{1}_{0}e^{-\iota p\left(\frac{t}{1-t}\right)}\left(\frac{t}{1-t}\right)^{s-1}t^{n-1}{\dd}t

(3.28)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n\omega_{n}}{2^{\iota p}}\int^{\varepsilon_{0}}_{0}(1-t)^{1-s}t^{n+s-2}{\dd}t\geq\frac{n\omega_{n}(1-\varepsilon_{0})^{1-s}\varepsilon_{0}^{n+s-1}}{2^{p}(n+s-1)}=:C_{1}(s,p,n,\varepsilon_{0}).

For the numerator factor containing
r^{p^{\prime}s}
we write

(3.29)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0})}}|u_{\iota}|^{p}r^{p^{\prime}s}{\dd}x=n\omega_{n}\int^{1}_{0}e^{-\frac{\iota pt}{1-t}}\left(\frac{t}{1-t}\right)^{p^{\prime}s}t^{n-1}{\dd}t.

For a small positive
\delta<1
, since
p^{\prime}s+n>0
,

(3.30)

\displaystyle\int^{\delta}_{0}e^{-\frac{\iota pt}{1-t}}\left(\frac{t}{1-t}\right)^{p^{\prime}s}t^{n-1}{\dd}t\leq\max\left\{1,(1-\delta)^{-p^{\prime}s}\right\}\int^{\delta}_{0}t^{p^{\prime}s+n-1}{\dd}t=:C_{2}(\delta,s,p,n).

For the remaining part, we have

(3.31)

\displaystyle\int^{1}_{\delta}e^{-\frac{\iota pt}{1-t}}\left(\frac{t}{1-t}\right)^{p^{\prime}s}t^{n-1}{\dd}t\leq\max\left\{1,\delta^{p^{\prime}s+n-1}\right\}\int^{1}_{\delta}e^{-\frac{\iota p\delta}{1-t}}(1-t)^{-p^{\prime}s}{\dd}t.

Case 1:
p^{\prime}s\leq 1
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\int^{1}_{\delta}e^{-\frac{\iota p\delta}{1-t}}(1-t)^{-p^{\prime}s}{\dd}t=

Funk metric spaces

This section investigates Funk metric spaces, a fundamental class of projectively flat Finsler manifolds with constant negative curvature. Subsection
4.1
collects their basic geometric properties, while Subsection
4.2
establishes the nonlinearity of their Sobolev spaces and the failure of the uncertainty principle and CKN inequality. Theorem

is established, which restates Theorem
4.10

4.1.
Background of Funk metric spaces

Definition 4.1
.

A Finsler metric
\mathsf{F}
on a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called a
Funk metric
if

(4.1)

x+\frac{y}{\mathsf{F}(x,y)}\in\partial\Omega,

for every
x\in\Omega
and
y\in T_{x}\Omega\backslash\{0\}
. The pair
(\Omega,\mathsf{F})
is called a
Funk metric space
.

In (
4.1
) both
x
and
y/\mathsf{F}(x,y)
are interpreted as vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with their sum emanating from the origin.
The following characterization of Funk metrics is due to Li
[
25
]
and Shen
[
37
]
.

Theorem 4.2

(
[
25
,
37
]
)
.

A pair
(\Omega,\mathsf{F})
is a Funk metric space if and only if
there is a (unique) Minkowski norm
\phi
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

(4.2)

\partial\Omega=\phi^{-1}(1),\qquad\mathsf{F}(x,y)=\phi{\left(y+x\mathsf{F}(x,y)\right)}.

In particular,
\Omega
is a bounded strongly convex domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
given by

(4.3)

\Omega=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\phi(x)<1\}.

Example 2
.

Let
\phi(x)=|x|
be the Euclidean norm of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then
\Omega=\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}({\mathbf{0}})
, and the corresponding Funk metric is

\displaystyle\mathsf{F}(x,y)=\frac{\sqrt{(1-|x|^{2})|y|^{2}+\langle x,y\rangle^{2}}}{1-|x|^{2}}+\frac{\langle x,y\rangle}{1-|x|^{2}},\qquad\forall(x,y)\in T\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1}(\mathbf{0}),

which coincides with the metric (
1.1
) discussed in the Introduction.

We collect several fundamental properties of Funk metric spaces that will be used repeatedly.

Theorem 4.3

(
[
35
]
)
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathsf{F},\m_{BH})
be an
n
-dimensional Funk metric space equipped with the Busemann–Hausdorff measure. Then the following statements are true:

(i)

(\Omega,\mathsf{F})
is a Cartan–Hadamard manifold with
\mathbf{K}=-\frac{1}{4}
;

(ii)

the S-curvature is constant, i.e.,
\mathbf{S}=\frac{n+1}{2}
;

(iii)

\m_{BH}=\sigma\mathscr{L}^{n}
with

(4.4)

\sigma=\frac{\vol(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{1})}{\vol(\Omega)}=\frac{\omega_{n}}{\vol(\Omega)}.

The explicit form of the co-metric for a Funk metric was given by Huang–Mo
[
16
]
and Shen
[
36
]
.

Lemma 4.4

(
[
16
,
36
]
)
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathsf{F})
be a Funk metric space and
\phi
the Minkowskian norm satisfying (
4.2
). The dual metric
\mathsf{F}^{*}
is given by

(4.5)

\mathsf{F}^{*}(x,\eta)=\phi^{*}(\eta)-\langle\eta,x\rangle,

where
\displaystyle\phi^{*}(\eta):=\underset{y\in T_{x}\Omega\backslash\{0\}}{\sup}\frac{\eta(y)}{\phi(y)}
and
\langle\eta,x\rangle=\eta(x)
is the canonical pairing between
T_{x}^{*}\Omega
and
T_{x}\Omega
.

Every Funk metric space is projectively flat; this yields concrete formulas for distances and reversibility.

Theorem 4.5

(
[
22
]
)
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathsf{F})
be a Funk metric space and
\phi
the Minkowskian norm satisfying (
4.2
). Then the following statements are true:

(i)

the distance function
d_{\mathsf{F}}(\mathbf{0},x)
is given by

(4.6)

d_{\mathsf{F}}(\mathbf{0},x)=-\ln[1-\phi(x)];

(ii)

the Funk metric space
(\Omega,\mathsf{F})
is forward complete (but not backward complete) with

\lambda_{{\mathsf{F}}}(x)\in\left[\frac{1+\phi(x)}{1-\phi(x)},\frac{\lambda_{\phi}+\phi(x)}{1-\phi(x)}\right],\qquad\lambda_{F}(\Omega)=+\infty.

where
\lambda_{\phi}:=\sup_{y\neq 0}\phi(-y)/\phi(y)
is the reversibility of
\phi
.

4.2.
Analytic properties of Funk metric spaces

Lemma 4.6

(\Omega,\mathsf{F})
be an
n
-dimensional Funk metric space and

the Minkowskian norm satisfying (

If

such that

, then

(4.7)

\int_{\Omega}f\circ\phi\dm_{BH}=n\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{1}f(t)t^{n-1}{\dd}t=n\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{+\infty}f(1-e^{-r})e^{-r}(1-e^{-r})^{n-1}{\dd}r,

where the variable
r
denotes the distance from the origin given by (
4.6
).

Proof.

The Minkowski norm
\phi
induces a Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\}
(cf.
[
3
, Sections 14.2]
), i.e.,

\hat{g}:=\hat{g}_{ij}(x){\dd}x^{i}\otimes{\dd}x^{j}:=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\phi^{2}}{\partial x^{i}\partial x^{j}}(x){\dd}x^{i}\otimes{\dd}x^{j}.

It should be remarked that

\hat{g}_{ij}(x)=\hat{g}_{ij}(\lambda x)
for any
\lambda>0
.

Let
(t,v)
denote the polar coordinate system around
\mathbf{0}
induced by
\hat{g}
, i.e.,

(4.8)

t:=\phi(x),\qquad v:=\frac{x}{\phi(x)}\in S_{\mathbf{0}}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\phi(y)=1\}.

According to
[
3
, Sections 14.9]
, the canonical Riemannian metric satisfies

(4.9)

{\dd}{\vol_{\hat{g}}}(x)=\sqrt{\det\hat{g}_{ij}(x)}\,{\dd}x=t^{n-1}{\dd}t\,{\dd}\nu_{\mathbf{0}}(v),

where
{\dd}\nu_{\mathbf{0}}
is the canonical Riemannian measure of
S_{\mathbf{0}}
(see
[

, (1.4.8)]
), i.e.,

(4.10)

{\dd}\nu_{\mathbf{0}}|_{x}=\sqrt{\det\hat{g}_{ij}(x)}\,\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j-1}x^{i}{\dd}x^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge{\dd}x^{j-1}\wedge{\dd}x^{j+1}\wedge\cdots{\dd}x^{n}.

Set
\sigma:=\frac{\omega_{n}}{\vol(\Omega)}
as in (
4.4
). It is straightforward to check that

\Psi(x):=\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{\det\hat{g}_{ij}(x)}}

is a scalar function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\}
with
\Psi(x)=\Psi(x/\phi(x))
; thus it may be regarded as a function on
S_{\mathbf{0}}
.
By Stokes’ theorem and (
4.10
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{S_{\mathbf{0}}}\Psi(v){\dd}\nu_{\mathbf{0}}(v)

\displaystyle=\sigma\int_{S_{\mathbf{0}}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j-1}x^{i}{\dd}x^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge{\dd}x^{j-1}\wedge{\dd}x^{j+1}\wedge\cdots{\dd}x^{n}

(4.11)

\displaystyle=n\sigma\int_{\{\phi<1\}}{\dd}x=n\sigma\vol(\Omega)=n\omega_{n}.

The assumption combined with Theorem
4.3
/(
iii
) implies
f\circ\phi\in L^{1}(\Omega,\m_{BH})
.
Let
(t,v)
be the polar coordinate system as in (
4.8
). A direct calculation combined with (
4.8
), (
4.9
), and (
4.2
) yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f\circ\phi\dm_{BH}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\}}f(\phi(x))\sigma{\dd}x=\int_{\Omega\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\}}f(\phi(x))\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{\det\hat{g}(\frac{x}{\phi(x)})}}{\sqrt{\det\hat{g}(x)}}{\dd}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\}}f(\phi(x))\Psi\left(\frac{x}{\phi(x)}\right)\dvol_{\hat{g}}(x)=\int_{S_{\mathbf{0}}}\left(\int^{1}_{0}f(t)\Psi(v)t^{n-1}{\dd}t\right){\dd}\nu_{\mathbf{0}}(v)

\displaystyle=n\omega_{n}\int^{1}_{0}f(t)t^{n-1}{\dd}t.

Finally, (
4.6
) implies
r=-\ln(1-t)
with
r=d_{\mathsf{F}}(\mathbf{0},x)
.
Hence, we have
t=1-e^{-r}
, and

\int_{\Omega}f\circ\phi\dm_{BH}=n\omega_{n}\int^{1}_{0}f(t)t^{n-1}{\dd}t=n\omega_{n}\int^{+\infty}_{0}f(1-e^{-r})e^{-r}(1-e^{-r})^{n-1}{\dd}r,

which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathsf{F})
be an
n
-dimensional Funk metric space and
\phi
the Minkowski norm satisfying (
4.2
).
For
\iota\in(0,1)
, define

Then
u_{\iota}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathsf{F},\m_{BH})
and

(4.12)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u_{\iota}|^{p}\dm_{BH}

\displaystyle=\frac{n!\,\omega_{n}}{(\iota p+1)(\iota p+2)\cdot\cdot\cdot(\iota p+n)},

(4.13)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathsf{F}^{*p}({\dd}u_{\iota})\dm_{BH}

\displaystyle=\frac{\iota^{p}n!\,\omega_{n}}{(\iota p+1)(\iota p+2)\cdot\cdot\cdot(\iota p+n)}.

Proof.

For simplicity, write
\m:=\m_{BH}
.
By Lemma
4.6
, we have

\int_{\Omega}|u_{\iota}|^{p}\dm=n\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{1}t^{n-1}(1-t)^{\iota p}{\dd}t=n\omega_{n}\mathfrak{B}(n,\iota p+1)=\frac{n!\,\omega_{n}}{(\iota p+1)(\iota p+2)\cdot\cdot\cdot(\iota p+n)},

where

denotes the Beta function. This gives (
4.12
).

On the other hand, for any

, by (

) we have

(4.14)

\phi^{*}({\dd}\phi|_{x})=\sup_{y\neq 0}\frac{\langle{\dd}\phi|_{x},y\rangle}{\phi(y)}=1,\qquad\langle{\dd}\phi|_{x},x\rangle=x^{i}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x^{i}}(x)=\phi(x).

Then by Lemma
4.4
, we obtain

\mathsf{F}^{*}(x,{\dd}u_{\iota})=\phi^{*}({\dd}u_{\iota}|_{x})-\langle{\dd}u_{\iota}|_{x},x\rangle=\iota[{1-\phi(x)}]^{\iota}=\iota|u_{\iota}|(x),

which together with (

The proof of

follows the same pattern as Lemma

and is therefore omitted.
∎

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathsf{F},\m_{BH})
be an
n
-dimensional Funk metric space endowed with the Busemann-Hausdorff measure.
Then the following statements are true:

(i)

W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega,\mathsf{F},\m_{BH})
is not a vector space for each
p\in(1,+\infty)
;

(ii)

the forward and backward topologies of
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathsf{F},\m_{BH})
are incompatible.
In particular, there exist a sequence
(u_{k})
and
u
in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega,\mathsf{F},\m_{BH})
such that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|u-u_{k}\|_{W^{1,p}_{\m_{BH}}}=0,\qquad\|u_{k}-u\|_{W^{1,p}_{\m_{BH}}}=+\infty,\ \forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

For simplicity, write
\m:=\m_{BH}

and let
\phi
be the Minkowski norm satisfying (
4.2
).
Fix
p\in(1,+\infty)
and choose
\iota\in(0,1-1/p)
. Set

u_{\iota}(x):=-[{1-\phi(x)}]^{\iota},\qquad\forall x\in\Omega.

Lemma
4.7
implies
u_{\iota}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega,\mathsf{F},\m)
.
Now we show
-u_{\iota}\not\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega,\m)
.
Indeed, the second equation in (
4.14
) yields

\langle{\dd}u_{\iota}|_{x},x\rangle=\iota[1-\phi(x)]^{\iota-1}\phi(x)\geq 0.

Using the expression for the co-metric (
4.5
) and the integral formula (
4.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|-u_{\iota}\|^{p}_{W^{1,p}_{\m}}\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathsf{F}^{*p}(x,-{\dd}u_{\iota})\dm=\int_{\Omega}\left[\phi^{*}(-{\dd}u_{\iota})+\langle{\dd}u_{\iota},x\rangle\right]^{p}\dm\geq\int_{\Omega}\langle{\dd}u_{\iota},x\rangle^{p}\dm

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{n\omega_{n}\iota^{p}}\int_{0}^{1}t^{p+n-1}(1-t)^{p(\iota-1)}{\dd}t=\mathfrak{B}\big(p+n,p(\iota-1)+1\big)=+\infty,

where the last equality follows by
p(\iota-1)+1<0
.
Hence
-u_{\iota}\not\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega,\mathsf{F},\m_{BH})
, proving (
i
).

Statement (
ii
) follows from the same argument used to prove Corollary
3.7
.
∎

The failure of the Hardy inequality on Funk metric spaces was proved by Kristály–Li–Zhao
[
22
]
.
Using the test function
u_{\iota}(x):=-[{1-\phi(x)}]^{\iota}
, we shall show in
Theorem
4.10
that both the uncertainty principle and the generalized Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg inequality fail on Funk metric spaces.
The proof relies on a basic estimate for a certain exponential integral, which we record first.

Lemma 4.9
.

For every

b>-n
, there exists a constant
C(b,n)>0
such that

(4.15)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}r^{b}e^{-r}(1-e^{-r})^{n-1}{\dd}r\leq C(b,n)<+\infty.

Proof.

Note that

1-e^{-r}\leq r
for all
r\in[0,+\infty)
. This together with
b+n>0
yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}r^{b}e^{-r}(1-e^{-r})^{n-1}{\dd}r

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-r}r^{b+n-1}{\dd}r=\Gamma(b+n)=:C(b,n)<+\infty,

which competes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathsf{F},\m_{BH})
be an
n
-dimensional Funk metric space endowed with the Busemann–Hausdorff measure and set
r(x):=d_{\mathsf{F}}(\mathbf{0},x)
.
Then the following statements are true:

(i)

the generalized uncertainty principle fails, i.e., for any
-p+1<s\leq 1<p<n
,

\inf_{f\in C^{\infty}_{0}{\Omega}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\Big(\int_{{\Omega}}{\mathsf{F}}^{*p}({\rm d}f){\dd}{\m_{BH}}\Big)^{1/p}\left(\int_{{\Omega}}|f|^{p}r^{p^{\prime}s}{\dd}{\m_{BH}}\right)^{1/{p^{\prime}}}}{\int_{{\Omega}}|f|^{p}r^{s-1}{\dd}{\m_{BH}}}=0;

(ii)

the generalized Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg inequality fails, i.e., if
1<p<m
and
p(n+s-1)>m(n-p)>0
,
then

\inf_{f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\Big(\int_{{M}}F^{*p}({\rm d}f){\dm_{BH}}\Big)^{1/p}\left(\int_{\Omega}{|f|^{p^{\prime}(m-1)}}{r^{p^{\prime}s}}{\dm_{BH}}\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}}{\int_{\Omega}{|f|^{m}}{r^{s-1}}{\dm_{BH}}}=0.

Proof.

Write
\m:=\m_{BH}

and let
\phi
be the Minkowski norm satisfying (
4.2
).
Set

u_{\iota}(x):=-[{1-\phi(x)}]^{\iota}
for
\iota\in(0,1)
.
Lemma
4.7
shows that

and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathsf{F}^{*p}({\dd}u_{\iota})\dm

\displaystyle=\frac{\iota^{p}n!\,\omega_{n}}{(\iota p+1)(\iota p+2)\cdot\cdot\cdot(\iota p+n)}<\frac{\iota^{p}n!p!\,\omega_{n}}{(p+n)!}.

(
i
)

By (
4.6
) and (
4.7
), a direct computation gives

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}{|u_{\iota}|^{p}}{r^{s-1}}{\dd}{\m}

\displaystyle=n\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-(\iota p+1)r}r^{s-1}(1-e^{-r})^{n-1}{\dd}r

\displaystyle\geq n\omega_{n}\int_{1/2}^{1}e^{-(p+1)r}r^{s-1}(1-e^{-r})^{n-1}{\dd}r=:n\omega_{n}C_{1}(p,s,n)>0.

On the other hand, since

, a direct calculation yields

. Then Lemma

yields a constant

\displaystyle\leq n\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{+\infty}r^{p^{\prime}s}e^{-r}(1-e^{-r})^{n-1}{\dd}r\leq n\omega_{n}C_{2}(p,s,n).

Now it follows from (

which establishes (
i
).

(

)

First, there exists a constant

with

(4.19)

\int_{{M}}{|u_{\iota}|^{m}}{r^{s-1}}{\dm}\geq n\omega_{n}C_{3}(s,m,n)>0.

,

While both the Berwald and Funk metric spaces are Cartan–Hadamard manifolds with infinite reversibility and have many properties in common, their behavior regarding the generalized CKN inequality is strikingly different: the sharp threshold phenomenon present in the Berwald setting disappears entirely for Funk metrics.

The proof relies repeatedly on the integral decomposition (

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(M,F,\mathfrak{m})
be an
n
-dimensional
\FMMM
with
\mathbf{Ric}\geq-(n-1)k^{2}
for some

. Assume that there exists a point

such that

(5.1)

\mathbf{S}(\nabla r)\geq(n-1)k+\frac{C}{1+r},

where

is the distance function from

satisfies

C>1\ \text{ if }\ k>0;\qquad C\geq n\ \text{ if }\ k=0.

For
p\in(1,n)
, let

be two constants satisfying

-n<b<p^{\prime}(C-n+1),&\text{if }k=0.\end{cases}

v_{\iota}(x):=-e^{-\iota r^{1+\frac{\mu}{p}}},
with constants
\iota,\mu
satisfying

0<\iota<\min\left\{a^{-1},p^{-1}\right\},\qquad\begin{cases}C-1<\mu<C,&\text{if }k>0,\\

Then there exist constants

and

\displaystyle\int_{M}F^{*p}({\dd}v_{\iota})\dm

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}\mathscr{I}_{\m}(o)\iota^{\frac{p(p+C-1)}{p+\mu}}C_{1},&\text{ if }k>0,\\
\mathscr{I}_{\m}(o)\iota^{\frac{p(p+C-n)}{p+\mu}}C_{1},&\text{ if }k=0,\end{cases}

(5.3)

\displaystyle\int_{M}|v_{\iota}|^{a}r^{b}\dm\leq\mathscr{I}_{\m}(o)\iota^{\xi}

\displaystyle\,C_{2}\quad\text{with}\quad\xi>\begin{cases}-p^{\prime}\left(\frac{p+C-1}{p-\mu}\right),&\text{ if }k>0,\\[4.0pt]
-p^{\prime}\left(\frac{p+C-n}{p-\mu}\right),&\text{ if }k=0,\end{cases}

where
\mathscr{I}_{\m}(o)
is the integral of distortion (
2.13
). In particular,

(5.4)

\lim_{\iota\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left(\int_{M}F^{*p}({\dd}v_{\iota})\dm\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{M}|v_{\iota}|^{a}r^{b}\dm\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}=0.

Proof.

Since (
5.4
) follows directly from (
5.2
) and (

Let
(r,y)
be the polar coordinate system around

By (

Together with Theorem
2.1
this yields the key comparison

(5.5)

\hat{\sigma}_{o}(r,y)\leq e^{-\tau(\gamma_{y}(r),\dot{\gamma}_{y}(r))}\mathfrak{s}^{n-1}_{-k^{2}}(r)\leq e^{-\tau(o,y)-(n-1)kr}\mathfrak{s}^{n-1}_{-k^{2}}(r)\,(r+1)^{-C},\quad\forall r\in(0,i_{y}),\ y\in S_{o}M.

The remainder of the proof is divided into two cases:
k>0
and
k=0
.

Case (i)

.

Since

, we have

(5.6)

e^{-(n-1)kr}\mathfrak{s}_{-k^{2}}^{n-1}(r)\leq(2k)^{1-n}.

Since
F^{*}({\dd}r)=1
, a direct calculation together with (

), (
2.13

5.5

\displaystyle\int_{M}F^{*p}({\dd}v_{\iota})\dm

\displaystyle=\iota^{p}\left(1+\frac{\mu}{p}\right)^{p}\int_{M}r^{\mu}e^{-\iota pr^{1+\frac{\mu}{p}}}\dm

\displaystyle\leq\iota^{p}\left(2k\right)^{1-n}\left(1+\frac{\mu}{p}\right)^{p}\int_{S_{o}M}\left(\int^{+\infty}_{0}e^{-\tau(o,y)}e^{-\iota pr^{1+\frac{\mu}{p}}}r^{\mu}(r+1)^{-C}{\dd}r\right){\dd}\nu_{o}(y)

\displaystyle\leq\iota^{p}\left(2k\right)^{1-n}\left(1+\frac{\mu}{p}\right)^{p}\mathscr{I}_{\m}(o)\left(\int^{1}_{0}r^{\mu}(r+1)^{-C}{\dd}r+\int_{1}^{+\infty}e^{-\iota pr^{1+\frac{\mu}{p}}}r^{\mu}(r+1)^{-C}{\dd}r\right)

\displaystyle\leq\iota^{p}\left(2k\right)^{1-n}\left(1+\frac{\mu}{p}\right)^{p}\mathscr{I}_{\m}(o)\left(1+\int_{1}^{+\infty}e^{-\iota pr^{1+\frac{\mu}{p}}}r^{\mu-C}{\dd}r\right).

\displaystyle=\iota^{\frac{p(C-\mu-1)}{p+\mu}}\frac{p^{\frac{p(C-\mu)+\mu}{p+\mu}}}{p+\mu}\Gamma\left(\frac{p(\mu-C)-\mu}{p+\mu}+1\right)=:\iota^{\frac{p(C-\mu-1)}{p+\mu}}~\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{1}(p,C,\mu).

\displaystyle\int_{M}F^{*p}({\dd}v_{\iota})\dm

\displaystyle=\iota^{\frac{p(p+C-1)}{p+\mu}}\left(\iota^{\frac{p(\mu-C+1)}{p+\mu}}+\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{1}(p,C,\mu)\right)\left(2k\right)^{1-n}\left(1+\frac{\mu}{p}\right)^{p}\mathscr{I}_{\m}(o)

\displaystyle\leq\iota^{\frac{p(p+C-1)}{p+\mu}}\left(1+\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{1}(p,C,\mu)\right)\left(2k\right)^{1-n}\left(1+\frac{\mu}{p}\right)^{p}\mathscr{I}_{\m}(o)

\displaystyle\int_{M}{|v_{\iota}|^{a}}{r^{b}}\dm

\displaystyle\leq\int_{S_{o}M}\left(\int^{+\infty}_{0}e^{-\tau(o,y)}e^{-\iota ar^{1+\frac{\mu}{p}}}r^{b}e^{-(n-1)kr}\mathfrak{s}^{n-1}_{-k^{2}}(r)(r+1)^{-C}{\dd}r\right){\dd}\nu_{o}(y)

\displaystyle\leq\mathscr{I}_{\m}(o)\left(\frac{2}{b+n}+\frac{\max\{1,\varepsilon_{1}^{b}\}}{(2k)^{n-1}}+(2k)^{1-n}\int_{1}^{+\infty}e^{-\iota ar^{1+\frac{\mu}{p}}}r^{b-C}{\dd}r\right).

The remainder of the proof of (

) is divided into two subcases:
b\leq C
and
C<b<p^{\prime}C
.

Subcase 1.1:

In this subcase,

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{+\infty}e^{-\iota ar^{1+\frac{\mu}{p}}}r^{b-C}{\dd}r\leq\int_{1}^{+\infty}e^{-\iota ar}{\dd}r\leq\iota^{-1}a^{-1}.

\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon_{1}(k,n)

Efficacy and safety of extended-release carbidopa–levodopa (IPX203) versus immediate-release carbidopa–levodopa for Parkinson's disease: A systematic review
Authors: Ahmed Attar, Hasen H Aljadani, Khalid F Barwais, Abdalmajed A Alqsair, Faisal A Alharbi, Harith M Alharith, Abdullah A Fallatah, Mumen H Halabi, Alqassem Y Hakami, Nirmeen Zagzoog
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveIPX203 is a newly approved oral carbidopa–levodopa formulation designed to prolong the therapeutic effect and reduce complications associated with the standard immediate-release formulation. Immediate-release carbidopa–levodopa remains the current standard of care but has a short duration of action. This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of IPX203 compared with immediate-release carbidopa–levodopa in patients with advanced Parkinson's disease.MethodsThis systematic review followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines and was registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (registration number: CRD42024623230). A comprehensive search of PubMed/MEDLINE, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials, Web of Science, ScienceDirect, Scopus, and Embase identified randomized controlled trials comparing IPX203 with immediate-release carbidopa–levodopa. The primary outcomes were changes from baseline in “Good On-time” (periods of effective symptom control with minimal dyskinesia) and “Off-time” (periods when medication effects wear off and symptoms return). Secondary outcomes included scores on Part III of the Movement Disorder Society–Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale and adverse events.ResultsA total of 466 records were screened, resulting in the inclusion of three randomized controlled trials involving 560 participants. Compared with immediate-release carbidopa–levodopa, IPX203 increased total “Good On-time” and reduced “Off-time.” Scores on Part III of the Movement Disorder Society–Unified Parkinson’s Disease Rating Scale demonstrated greater improvement in motor function with IPX203. The incidence of adverse events was comparable between the two groups, although treatment-emergent adverse events and treatment discontinuations were more frequent in the IPX203 group.ConclusionCompared with immediate-release carbidopa–levodopa, IPX203 increased “Good On-time,” reduced “Off-time,” improved motor function as measured by Part III of the Movement Disorder Society–Unified Parkinson’s Disease Rating Scale, and demonstrated a comparable safety profile.

Background

Parkinson's disease (PD) is a chronic neurodegenerative movement disorder that primarily affects older adults. It is characterized by both motor and non-motor symptoms. The motor symptoms, collectively referred to as parkinsonism, include bradykinesia, muscle rigidity, and resting tremor. 1 These symptoms are present in approximately 80% of patients with early-stage PD, whereas drug-related motor complications affect 40%–50% of patients with advanced PD. 2 Non-motor symptoms include dementia, cognitive impairment, and a wide range of other manifestations that can substantially affect daily life. 3 PD is characterized by degeneration of the substantia nigra, a dopamine-producing region of the basal ganglia, 4 leading to dopamine depletion and the associated motor symptoms. PD is the second most common neurodegenerative disorder. 5 According to the World Health Organization (WHO), approximately 8.5 million people worldwide are living with PD. Among adults older than 65 years, the prevalence has increased by 81% since 2000. 6 Furthermore, mortality has doubled since 2000, 6 indicating that current prevention and treatment strategies have been insufficient to address the growing burden of PD.

Among the available treatment strategies, PD is most commonly managed with a combination of levodopa and carbidopa, which remains the standard pharmacological therapy according to the WHO. 6 Levodopa, a precursor of dopamine, is the first-line treatment for PD because it replenishes depleted dopamine levels and alleviates the motor symptoms of parkinsonism by reducing their severity. 7 However, levodopa undergoes extensive peripheral metabolism before reaching the central nervous system, which contributes to adverse effects and reduces the amount of drug available to the brain. Carbidopa is a peripheral dopa decarboxylase inhibitor that prevents the peripheral conversion of levodopa to dopamine, thereby increasing levodopa availability in the central nervous system, allowing lower doses to be used, and reducing peripheral adverse effects. 8 Immediate-release carbidopa–levodopa (IR CD–LD), commonly marketed as Sinemet, remains the standard treatment for the symptomatic management of PD. Initially, Sinemet provides symptomatic relief for approximately 6 h; however, its duration of action gradually decreases with continued use, necessitating more frequent dosing and increasing the risk of adverse effects. The long-term use of levodopa in patients with PD may lead to motor complications, including wearing-off phenomena (the return symptoms before the next scheduled dose) and peak-dose dyskinesias. These complications can substantially impair daily functioning and overall quality of life. 9 These limitations highlight the need for alternative formulations of IR CD–LD that prolong therapeutic benefit while minimizing treatment-related adverse effects. Controlled-release formulations, including Sinemet CR (Nacom Retard in Europe) and Madopar Depot, were developed to prolong levodopa delivery. However, their pharmacokinetic profiles are characterized by delayed and variable absorption, resulting in inconsistent clinical responses.10,11

A newly approved CD–LD combination, IPX203, is an oral extended-release formulation. It consists of IR granules containing CD–LD, together with extended-release beads comprising a levodopa core surrounded by polymer coatings that slow drug release. 12 IPX203 was developed to prolong levodopa availability and reduce wearing-off periods between doses, a common motor complication experienced by patients receiving CD–LD therapy. Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) have demonstrated these benefits in patients with PD. A study reported that IPX203 provided a longer duration of “Good On-time,” reflecting improved control of parkinsonian symptoms, compared with IR CD–LD. 13 Two additional studies similarly demonstrated prolonged therapeutic effects and concluded that IPX203 produced a longer pharmacodynamic effect than IR CD–LD.14,15

Because IPX203 has only recently been approved for the treatment of PD, comprehensive evidence synthesizing its efficacy and safety relative to current standard therapy remains limited. Therefore, a systematic evaluation of the available RCTs is warranted. This study aimed to systematically review the existing evidence on the efficacy of IPX203 compared with IR CD–LD in patients with advanced PD.

Methods

This systematic review followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines to assess the efficacy and safety of IPX203 compared with IR CD–LD in patients with PD. 16 The study protocol was registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO) under registration number CRD42024623230.

Data sources and search strategy

RCTs were identified through searches of PubMed/MEDLINE, the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL), Web of Science, ScienceDirect, Scopus, and Embase from database inception to 5 December 2024. To ensure a comprehensive and sensitive search strategy, both keywords and Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) terms were used. These included “Parkinson's disease,” “PD” “IPX203,” “extended-release carbidopa–levodopa,” “carbidopa–levodopa,” and “immediate-release carbidopa–levodopa.”

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Studies were included if they met the following criteria: RCTs evaluating the efficacy and safety of IPX203 compared with IR CD–LD in adults aged 40 years or older with advanced PD experiencing motor fluctuations. RCTs were excluded if they (a) were published in a language other than English; (b) included patients with atypical or secondary parkinsonism; (c) included patients with a history of prior neurosurgical treatment for PD; (d) included patients who did not respond to CD–LD treatment; or (e) included patients with medical conditions that could interfere with drug absorption, such as malabsorption or severe hepatic or renal insufficiency.

Study selection and data extraction

Duplicate records were removed, and the remaining articles were screened independently by three reviewers in pairs. Each pair screened the studies by title and abstract, followed by full-text review. Data, including the duration of the intervention, change from baseline in “Good On-time” (h), and the number of treatment-emergent adverse events, were extracted using a standardized data collection form. In addition, two reviewers independently performed data extraction, and any disagreements or discrepancies were resolved by a third reviewer.

Outcomes

The primary outcomes were changes in “Good On-time” (periods of effective symptom control with minimal dyskinesia) and “Off-time” (periods when the effects of medication wear off and symptoms return). Changes in “Good On-time” refer to the duration of symptom control without troublesome dyskinesia and were consistently reported across all three included studies. This outcome is particularly relevant in the management of PD because it directly reflects motor function stability and patients’ quality of life. Changes in “Off-time” represent periods during which the effects of PD medication diminish, resulting in the re-emergence or worsening of motor and non-motor symptoms. Secondary outcomes included changes in scores on Movement Disorder Society–Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale Part III (MDS–UPDRS III), overall safety, and adverse events. MDS–UPDRS III assesses motor function in patients with PD by evaluating symptoms such as tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia, and postural instability. Scores range from 0 to 132, with higher scores indicating greater motor impairment.

Risk of bias and quality assessment

Two investigators independently assessed the risk of bias of the included RCTs using the Revised Cochrane Risk of Bias Tool (RoB 2.0). This tool comprises five main domains, each of which is assessed separately, and classifies the overall risk of bias for each study as low risk, some concerns, or high risk. Any disagreements were resolved by a third investigator. 17

Results

This systematic search identified 780 records, of which 314 were removed as duplicates. Following title and abstract screening, 444 records were excluded. Of the remaining 22 articles, 12 were excluded for not meeting the inclusion criteria, and seven were excluded because they were conference abstracts. Ultimately, three RCTs were included in the systematic review. The study selection process is illustrated in Figure 1.

Study and participant characteristics

All three studies compared IPX203 with IR CD–LD for the treatment of PD. Hauser et al. 13 conducted a double-blind, double-dummy, Phase III trial involving 506 participants. Modi et al.14,15 conducted two rater-blinded, crossover pharmacokinetic studies: one single-dose study (n = 26) and one multiple ascending-dose study (n = 28). Overall, 560 participants were enrolled between 2019 and 2023. The mean age of the participants was 66 ± 9 years, with a mean age at disease onset of 58 ± 9 years. Overall, 70% of participants were men, and 91.4% had Hoehn and Yahr stage II–IV disease. Baseline patient diaries showed a mean of 9.6 ± 2.2 h of “Good On-time” and 5.9 ± 2.2 h of “Off-time” per day. Study quality and risk of bias

The risk of bias assessment is presented in Figures 2 and 3. Overall, the Phase III trial by Hauser et al. was adjusted to have a low risk of bias, whereas both studies by Modi et al. were judged to have a high overall risk of bias, despite demonstrating a low risk of bias across most assessed domains. Specifically, bias arising from deviations from the intended interventions, measurement of the outcome, and missing outcome data was judged to be at low risk in all three studies. However, all three studies were judged to have some concerns regarding the randomization process, whereas the two studies by Modi et al.14,15 also had some concerns regarding the selection of the reported results. Consequently, both Modi et al. studies were judged to have a high overall risk of bias because concerns related to the randomization process and selective outcome reporting reduced confidence in the estimated effects, particularly for MDS–UPDRS III and pharmacokinetic outcomes. In contrast, the trial by Hauser et al. 13 demonstrated an overall low risk of bias despite some concerns related to the randomization process.

Change in “Good On-time”

In the study by Hauser et al., 13 baseline “Good On-time” was 9.52 ± 2.21 h in the IPX203 group and 9.60 ± 2.34 h in the IR CD–LD group. After treatment, the least-squares (LS) mean change from baseline was −0.50 h for IPX203 and −1.03 h for IR CD–LD, resulting in an LS mean difference of 0.53 h (95% CI, 0.09–0.97; P = 0.02) in favor of IPX203. Patients receiving IPX203 also experienced a longer “Good On-time” per dose (LS mean, 3.76 h; 95% CI, 3.62–3.91) than those receiving IR CD–LD (LS mean, 2.21 h; 95% CI, 2.07–2.36), representing an increase of 1.55 h per dose (95% CI, 1.37–1.73; P < 0.001). Furthermore, the median “Good On-time” was 11.50 h with IPX203 and 10.88 h with IR CD–LD, corresponding to a difference of 0.62 h in favor of IPX203. Treatment frequency was lower in the IPX203 group (3.04 ± 0.40 doses/day) than in the IR CD–LD group (5.01 ± 1.19 doses/day), consistent with the longer duration of action of the extended-release formulation. Additional dosing information is provided in Appendix 1.

In the single-dose study by Modi et al. 14 , the mean “Good On-time” was 5.32 h following IPX203 administration compared with 2.76 h for IR CD–LD, representing a difference of 2.56 h (95% CI, 1.82–3.31; P < 0.0001). Although the dosing frequency for IPX203 was not reported in this study, patients receiving IR CD–LD administered the medication an average of 4.8 times per day.

In the multiple-dose study by Modi et al., 15 baseline “Good On-time” was 9.98 ± 2.3 h. Following treatment, the mean “Good On-time” increased to 12.07 h in the IPX203 group and 10.17 h in the IR CD–LD group, resulting in an LS mean difference of 1.91 h in favor of IPX203. Patients receiving IPX203 took the medication approximately 3.1 times per day, compared with 4.7 times per day for those receiving IR CD–LD.

Change in “Off-time”

Significant improvements in “Off-time” were reported in the study by Hauser et al. 13 Baseline “Off-time” was 6.12 ± 2.16 h in the IPX203 group and 6.08 ± 2.18 h in the IR CD–LD group. Following treatment, “Off-time” decreased by 1.94 h in the IPX203 group and by 1.33 h in the IR CD–LD group. This resulted in a between-group difference of −0.57 h, indicating a statistically significant benefit in favor of IPX203 (Table 2).

The single-dose study by Modi et al. 14 reported similar findings. Baseline “Off-time” was 6.1 h in both treatment groups. Following treatment, the IPX203 group experienced a 1.62-h reduction in “Off-time,” whereas the IR CD–LD group showed a 1.12-h increase. This resulted in a between-group difference of −2.74 h, favoring IPX203.

The multiple ascending-dose study by Modi et al. 15 reported a baseline “Off-time” of 5.2 h in both treatment groups. Following treatment, the IPX203 group demonstrated a 1.98-h reduction in “Off-time,” whereas the IR CD–LD group exhibited a 0.27-h increase. This resulted in a between-group difference of −2.25 h, favoring IPX203.

Changes in MDS–UPDRS III

In the RISE-PD study by Hauser et al., 13 baseline MDS–UPDRS III (“On”) scores were 24.7 ± 15.96 in the IPX203 group and 24.9 ± 15.56 in the IR CD–LD group. Following treatment, both groups demonstrated a mean improvement of 0.8 points, with no statistically significant difference between them. These findings suggest comparable short-term improvements in motor function with IPX203 and IR CD–LD.

In contrast, Modi et al. 14 reported that patients treated with IPX203 experienced a greater reduction in MDS–UPDRS III scores than those receiving IR CD–LD (−12.7 vs. −6.62; P < 0.0001). Hour-by-hour analysis demonstrated significant improvements with IPX203 from 3 to 10 h after dosing (P ≤ 0.029), suggesting a longer duration of therapeutic effect.

Similarly, Modi et al. 15 reported that the change in MDS–UPDRS III scores from Day 1 to Day 15 was −9.3 in the IPX203 group compared with 0.1 in the IR CD–LD (LS mean difference, −9.0; P = 0.0087). Additionally, over a 10-h post-dose period, IPX203 demonstrated greater improvement (−24.6) than IR CD–LD (−19.4; P = 0.0226), indicating a more sustained motor benefit. Adverse events

Adverse events were reported across all three studies. Among the combined 560 participants, 273 treatment-emergent adverse events were reported, of which 168 (61.5%) occurred in the IPX203 group and 105 (38.5%) in the IR CD–LD group (Table 3). Discontinuations due to adverse events occurred in 15 participants receiving IPX203 and 3 participants receiving IR CD–LD. The most common treatment-emergent adverse events reported with IPX203 were nausea (14 events, 8.3%), dyskinesia (10 events, 6.0%), and dizziness (8 events, 4.8%). Discussion

This systematic review evaluated the efficacy and safety of IPX203, a novel extended-release formulation of CD–LD, compared with IR CD–LD in patients with PD experiencing motor fluctuations. Across the three included RCTs, IPX203 consistently demonstrated superiority over IR CD–LD by increasing “Good On-time,” reducing “Off-time,” and requiring less frequent dosing. These findings suggest improved motor symptom control and the potential to enhance treatment adherence. Importantly, IPX203 demonstrated a safety profile comparable to that of IR CD–LD.

A recent systematic review and meta-analysis by Alnajjar et al. 18 evaluating IPX203 in patients with PD reached conclusions that are generally consistent with those of the present review regarding its efficacy and safety. However, several methodological differences between the two reviews should be acknowledged, including differences in the study eligibility criteria, the handling of publications primarily focused on pharmacokinetic and formulation data, and the inclusion of crossover studies in the quantitative synthesis. These methodological differences rly explain the discrepancy in the number of included studies and the analytical approaches used, although the overall conclusions of both reviews remain largely consistent.

Considerable efforts have been made to improve currently available therapies for PD. Much of this work has focused on developing extended-release formulations of CD–LD to prolong therapeutic effects and reduce motor fluctuations. Before the development of IPX203, several strategies were used to prolong levodopa exposure and reduce motor fluctuations, including the addition of catechol-O-methyltransferase (COMT) inhibitors and the use of controlled-release formulations such as the previously mentioned Madopar Depot (a controlled-release formulation of levodopa and benserazide) and Nacom 200 Retard (a controlled-release combination of carbidopa and levodopa). Comparative studies have shown that, under certain conditions, controlled-release levodopa formulations produced pharmacokinetic profiles similar to those of levodopa/carbidopa/entacapone combinations. However, these formulations remained limited by delayed absorption and variable clinical responses. 19

Nacom 200 Retard and Madopar Depot provided a slower release of levodopa and maintained therapeutic plasma concentrations for longer than IR formulations, thereby reducing dosing frequency and improving overnight symptom control. However, their clinical effectiveness was limited by delayed absorption, lower peak levodopa concentrations, and reduced bioavailability compared with IR formulations. Furthermore, because these formulations depend on gastric emptying for drug delivery, levodopa absorption may be inconsistent, resulting in variable clinical responses, particularly in patients with advanced PD and impaired gastrointestinal motility.10,11

The limitations of earlier controlled-release formulations directly informed the development of newer extended-release levodopa formulations, including IPX203, which was specifically designed to achieve more rapid onset of action while maintaining sustained plasma levodopa concentrations. 12 The observed clinical benefits of IPX203 are likely attributable to its unique pharmacokinetic design, which combines an IR component for rapid symptom control with extended-release granules that maintain plasma levodopa concentrations over time. This dual-release mechanism addresses key limitations of IR CD–LD, including its short duration of action and variable pharmacokinetic profile, both of which contribute to motor complications in patients with advanced PD. Consistent with this mechanism, the RISE-PD trial by Hauser et al. 13 demonstrated that IPX203 significantly prolonged “Good On-time” while requiring fewer daily doses than IR CD–LD. Similarly, the crossover studies by Modi et al.14,15 reported longer “Good On-time” together with greater improvements in MDS–UPDRS III scores, further supporting the sustained therapeutic effect of IPX203. Notably, in the single-dose crossover study, Modi et al.14,15 reported that IPX203 demonstrated superior pharmacodynamic performance not only to IR CD–LD but also to Rytary, another extended-release CD–LD formulation. IPX203 extended “Good On-time” by approximately 1 h, particularly during the late post-dose period (5–10 h), highlighting its ability to sustain motor benefits beyond the duration achieved with the comparator formulations. 14 These findings are consistent with previous evidence indicating that fluctuating plasma levodopa concentrations associated with IR CD–LD contribute to motor complications, including wearing-off phenomena and dyskinesia. 20 Continuous dopaminergic stimulation (CDS) has been proposed as an important therapeutic objective in PD to minimize these complications. 21 Although other extended-release formulations, such as Rytary, were developed to provide more CDS, frequent dosing may still be required because of inter-dose fluctuations. The available evidence suggests that IPX203 provides more sustained levodopa exposure while reducing dosing frequency compared with IR CD–LD.13–15

Despite these promising findings, this review has several limitations. First, only studies published in English were included, and all eligible studies were conducted in the United States, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to other populations and healthcare settings. In addition, two of the included RCTs had relatively small sample sizes (n = 27 and n = 25), which may further limit the external validity of the findings. Second, a quantitative meta-analysis was not performed because of substantial clinical and methodological heterogeneity among the included studies, including differences in study design, duration, sample size, clinical objectives, outcome assessment timing, and outcome definitions. Consequently, variations in the reported treatment effects may partly reflect differences in study methodology rather than treatment efficacy alone. Third, although the two crossover studies provided valuable insights into the short-term pharmacokinetic and motor effects of IPX203, they did not evaluate longer-term clinical outcomes, such as disease progression, health-related quality of life, or sustained treatment effectiveness. Furthermore, more than 90% of the included participants were enrolled in the Phase III trial by Hauser et al. 13 Consequently, the overall conclusions regarding clinical efficacy are largely driven by this study, which may limit the generalizability of the findings across different study designs and populations. Finally, studies were not excluded on the basis of methodological quality, although the risk of bias of each included study was formally assessed and considered during interpretation of the findings.

Despite these limitations, this systematic review has several important strengths. The inclusion of a large, multicenter, double-blind, double-dummy, Phase III trial 13 involving 506 participants across 105 centers substantially strengthens the evidence base. In addition, the two crossover studies provided valuable pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic data under blinded conditions.14,15 Another strength is the consistency of the findings across studies despite differences in study design. Overall, IPX203 demonstrated clinically meaningful benefits without major safety concerns. Although treatment-emergent adverse events were reported more frequently with IPX203 than with IR CD–LD, the overall safety profile remained broadly comparable between treatment groups.

The clinical implications of these findings are noteworthy. IPX203 was associated with improved motor symptom control, reduced dosing frequency, and the potential to enhance treatment adherence. In the RISE-PD trial, IPX203 reduced the mean daily dosing frequency from approximately five doses to three while improving clinical outcomes. These findings suggest that IPX203 may represent a useful therapeutic option for patients with PD who experience motor fluctuations despite treatment with IR CD–LD. Future research should include longer-term studies to assess the sustained efficacy and safety of IPX203, its effects on disease progression, and real-world outcomes such as hospitalization, healthcare utilization, and caregiver burden. Studies involving more diverse populations, including individuals with cognitive impairment and those from different geographical regions, are also needed to improve the generalizability of the evidence. In addition, investigating the role of IPX203 in earlier stages of PD and in combination with other therapeutic strategies may further define its place in clinical practice.

Conclusion

This systematic review suggests that IPX203 provides more sustained motor benefits than IR CD–LD in patients with PD experiencing motor fluctuations. Compared with IR CD–LD, IPX203 was associated with longer “Good On-time,” reduced “Off-time,” less frequent daily dosing, and a broadly comparable safety profile. Further long-term, high-quality studies involving more diverse populations are warranted to assess the sustained efficacy and safety of IPX203 and to better defined its role in the long-term management of PD.

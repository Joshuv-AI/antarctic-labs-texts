Introduction

Variational models of phase transitions consider integrals depending on double-well potentials
W
with two (or more) minimizers. These simple models, as such admit highly discontinuous unphysical solutions; a selection of minimizers can be ensured by a singular-perturbation approach in which a higher-order term is added, the form of which is sometimes dictated by modeling considerations. In the Cahn–Hilliard theory of phase separations
[
13
]
(but earlier models leading to similar formulations date back to Landau
[
21
]
, and even to van der Waals
[
31
]
) the corresponding energy is of the form

\int_{\Omega}W(u)dx+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx,

with a first-order perturbation depending on the small parameter
\varepsilon
. Analytically, the description of the solutions for
\varepsilon
small translates in an asymptotic analysis, which leads to a sharp-interface model in which the interaction of the two integral terms gives rise to an interfacial energy separating two phases characterized by the minimization of
W
, and minimum problems for
\varepsilon>0
are approximated by corresponding minimum problems for interfacial energies. This
minimal-interface criterion
was conjectured e.g. by Gurtin
[
20
]
. Its rigorous statement was made possible by the development of the theory of sets of finite perimeter and of functions of bounded variation, which allowed to rigorously define the limit interfacial functionals. The proof of the minimal-interface criterion was then achieved by Modica
[
24
]
, following earlier results in a seminal paper by Modica and Mortola
[
25
]
, showing the
\Gamma
-convergence of the scaled energies

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}W(u)dx+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx

to an interfacial energy, which is simply given by

m_{W}{\rm Per}(E,\Omega),

where
E
is a set of finite perimeter, and
{\rm Per}(E,\Omega)
denotes the perimeter of
E
in
\Omega
. In the case where the minimizers of
W
are
-1
and
1
(which can be assumed up to a change of variables), minimal functions
u_{\varepsilon}
can be described as approximating
u=-1+2\chi_{E}
, where
E
is a minimal set.
In this process, an interesting observation is that functions
u_{\varepsilon}
approximating
\chi_{E}
tend to follow an optimal profile in the direction orthogonal to the interface.
This remark is highlighted by the fact that the constant
m_{W}
can be expressed as a one-dimensional
optimal-profile problem

m_{W}=\inf\Big\{\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}(W(v)+|v^{\prime}|^{2})dt:v\in H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}),\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}v(t)=\pm 1\Big\}.

The Modica result has been generalized and revisited in many ways and in different contexts, leading also to more complex non one-dimensional descriptions (see e.g.
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
26
]
). However, even when dealing with higher-dimensional problems, a reduction to the one-dimensional case is often useful to ensure compactness by a slicing argument (see e.g.
[
1
,
10
]
). We note moreover that the analysis of perturbed double-well energies is the building block for many “phase-field” approximation procedures of free-discontinuity problems starting from the seminal work by Ambrosio and Tortorelli
[
5
]
(see
[
10
]
for a presentation of different approximations).

The higher-order perturbation term of the double-well energy can be taken of different forms involving
higher-order derivatives
, justified either by the necessity of higher regularity (useful for the approximation procedures mentioned above), or by modeling assumptions (e.g. from atomistic theories with long-range interactions as in
[
11
]
). In this case the energies take the form

\int_{\Omega}W(u)dx+\varepsilon^{2k}\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla^{(k)}u\|^{2}dx,

the second term denoting a norm of the
k
-th order tensor of derivatives of order
k
. Such functionals have been considered by Fonseca and Mantegazza
[
18
]
for
k=2
and by Brusca, Donati, and Solci
[
12
]
for arbitrary
k
. They showed that, taking
\|\cdot\|
the operator norm, the scaled functionals

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}W(u)dx+\varepsilon^{2k-1}\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla^{(k)}u\|^{2}dx,

still
\Gamma
-converge to a sharp-interface energy
m_{k}{\rm Per}(E,\Omega)
, where now the (one-dimensional) optimal-profile problem giving
m_{k}
is

m_{k}=\inf\Big\{\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}(W(v)+|v^{(k)}|^{2})dt:v\in H^{k}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}),\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}v(t)=\pm 1\Big\}.

(1)

We note that in this case even the proof of the inequality
m_{k}>0
is not trivial if
k\neq 1
.

In a different direction, it is also natural in some problems to consider perturbations of the form

\int_{\Omega}W(u)dx+\varepsilon^{2s}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}dx\,dy,

where
s\in(0,1)
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The additional term is a Gagliardo
fractional
s
-seminorm
, which can appear as a boundary term from a higher-dimensional problem (see e.g.
[
4
]
). Under different scaling, these problems have been studied by Savin and Valdinoci
[
29
]
(see also Palatucci and Vincini
[
27
]
, earlier results by Alberti, Bouchitté and Seppecher
[
3
]
and Alberti and Bellettini
[
2
]
, and a recent review paper by Dipierro and Valdinoci
[
17
]
). In particular if
2s>1
they note that the scaled energies

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}W(u)dx+\varepsilon^{2s-1}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}dx\,dy,

still define a singularly-perturbed functional, and show their convergence to a sharp-interface functional.

In this paper we give a complete description which comprises both the higher-order and the fractional cases. We restrict our analysis to the one-dimensional case, which contains the relevant new technical ideas for the proofs. The treatment of the
d
-dimensional case is postponed to future work, since it requires additional techniques such as slicing (see
[
10
,
1
]
), which allows one to obtain coerciveness from the one-dimensional analysis, and blow-up (see
[
19
]
), which leads to a description of the surface energy density as a limit of minimum problems on cubes of diverging size (see in particular the work by Dal Maso, Fonseca and Leoni
[
15
]
where the representation formula is obtained for a similar problem). The higher-dimensional issues are overall standard but rather complex and technically challenging, and need the use of some adaptations to the fractional case (see for example the fractional slicing result
[
22
, Theorem 6.47]
). Note that in the higher-dimensional case in the limit we will have an anisotropic perimeter functional
depending on the norm that we choose in the terms of the form
\|D^{(k)}u(x)-D^{(k)}u(y)\|
.

The main result of the paper is the following.
We consider energies defined in the higher-order fractional Sobolev space
H^{k+s}
(see e.g. the recent monograph
[
22
]
and the ‘guide’
[
16
]
). We suppose that
W
is a non-negative continuous function with minima in
-1
and
1
, and larger than a constant at infinity.
For fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\in(0,1)
with
k+s>\frac{1}{2}
(so that the non-local part is still a singular perturbation after scaling by
1/\varepsilon
) and a bounded interval
I
, we consider
F_{\varepsilon}
defined by

F_{\varepsilon}(u)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{I}W(u(x))dx+\varepsilon^{2(k+s)-1}\int_{I\times I}\frac{|u^{(k)}(x)-u^{(k)}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}dx\,dy,

and show that the
\Gamma
-limit of
F_{\varepsilon}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
with respect to the convergence in measure and in
L^{1}(0,1)
is the sharp-interface functional given by

F(u)=m_{k+s}\#S(u)
, where
S(u)
is the set of discontinuity points of
u
defined on
BV(I;\{-1,1\})
, with

\displaystyle m_{k+s}=\inf\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}}W(v)dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|v^{(k)}(x)-v^{(k)}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}dx\,dy:

\displaystyle\hskip 128.0374ptv\in H^{k+s}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}),\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}v(x)=\pm 1\Big\}

(2)

defining a strictly positive constant.

The proofs of the result mainly rely on the interpolation techniques first developed in
[
30
]
to treat the more complex case of singular perturbations producing free-discontinuity problems, and subsequently adapted to phase-transition problems in
[
12
]
. Since the technical results in those papers are tailored for local integral energies, they must be modified for the present situation with non-trivial changes. The techniques mainly focus on estimating the size of intervals where functions are close to
\pm 1
, and at the same time have large derivatives of all orders between
1
and
k
, in terms of the energy on the corresponding interval. Since this energy contains the
s
-seminorm of the
k
-th derivative and an integral that can be estimated by the
L^{2}
-distance from a well, suitable interpolation inequalities must be proved in terms of these quantities. These estimates allow to provide bounds in terms of the whole
H^{k+s}
-norm, showing that either a function oscillates, and hence contains points where derivatives of each order vanish, or has small derivative of all orders at some point close to the wells. In both cases, we can use a Poincaré-inequality argument to bound the total
H^{k+s}
-norm (and hence ensure that compactness properties hold). This allows one to bound the number of transitions between the wells along sequences
u_{\varepsilon}
for which
F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})
is equibounded, and hence to prove the equicoerciveness of the energies. Note that, differently from
[
12
]
, where the existence of points with small derivatives and close to the wells was used to extend functions outside an interval to prove a lower bound of the energies, in the non-local case such an argument is not sufficient, and we use instead a cut-off argument together with an extensive use of interpolation inequalities.

We have taken care to use minimal hypotheses on
W
; namely only that it has a quadratic behaviour at
\pm 1
and is bounded at infinity. The same arguments can be applied if
W(z)
behaves as
||z|-1|^{q}
close to
\pm 1
with
q>1
, by modifying the interpolation inequalities. We have limited our analysis to
q=2
for ease of notation. Moreover, the compactness result is trivially improved to convergence in some
L^{p}
if
W
has a polynomial growth at infinity.

If we compare the fractional case as stated above with the integer case, we note a divergence issue at integer points,
highlighted by the fact that for each
k
the function
s\mapsto m_{k+s}
, even though continuous on
(0,1)
, diverges as
s
tends to
0^{+}
or
1^{-}
. This is a consequence of results by Maz’ya–Shaposhnikova
[
23
]
(see also
[
14
, Section 1]
) and by Bourgain, Brezis and Mironescu
[
9
]
(see also Ponce
[
28
]
) which, reformulated for
k
-th derivatives, state that
the functionals

G_{s}(u)=s(1-s)\int_{I\times I}\frac{|u^{(k)}(x)-u^{(k)}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}dx\,dy

\Gamma
-converge to
2\int_{I}|u^{(k)}|^{2}dx
and to
\int_{I}|u^{(k+1)}|^{2}dx
in the
L^{2}
-topology, as
s\to 0^{+}
and
s\to 1^{-}
, respectively. With this remark in mind, we can correct this dependence by modifying
F_{\varepsilon}
above for
s\in(0,1)
, setting

F^{k,s}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{I}W(u(x))dx+\frac{s(1-s)}{2^{1-s}}\varepsilon^{2(k+s)-1}\int_{I\times I}\frac{|u^{(k)}(x)-u^{(k)}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}dx\,dy.

These functionals are of the same form as
F_{\varepsilon}
and their
\Gamma
-limit has the coefficient

\displaystyle m_{k}(s)=\inf\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}}W(v)dx+\frac{s(1-s)}{2^{1-s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|u^{(k)}(x)-u^{(k)}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}dx\,dy,:

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826ptv\in H^{k+s}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}),\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}v(x)=\pm 1\Big\}.

(3)

With this correction, the above-mentioned results by Maz’ya–Shaposhnikova
[
23
]
and Ponce
[
28
]
, together with a compactness argument in which we again use the interpolation techniques, ensure that
m_{k}(s)\to m_{k}
as
s\to 0^{+}
and
m_{k}(s)\to m_{k+1}
as
s\to 1^{-}
, respectively, where
m_{k}
is defined by (
1
) on integer
k
, thus obtaining a continuous function on
(\frac{1}{2},+\infty)
. We note that the same correction argument for
m_{s}
in the particular case
k=0
and
s\to 1^{-}
can be found in
[
27
]
.

2
Setting of the problem

We preliminarily introduce some notation. We will use standard notation for Sobolev and Lebesgue spaces, and for spaces of differentiable functions. In particular we will use
\|u\|_{L^{p}(I)}
and
\|u\|_{C^{k}(I)}
for the norm of
u
in the corresponding spaces. The space
BV(I;\{-1,1\})
is the set of piecewise-constant function on
I
taking only the values
-1
and
1
. For such a function
S(u)
will denote its set of discontinuity points.
The letter
C
will denote a generic strictly positive constant depending only on fixed parameters.

We will deal with singular-perturbation problems defined in fractional Sobolev spaces
H^{r}
, with
r>0
for whose definition, notation and main properties we refer to
[
22
]
. For ease of notation we will write
r=k+s
with
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\in[0,1)
. We recall that if
I
is an interval and
s\in(0,1)
, a function
u
belongs to
H^{k+s}(I)
if
u
belongs to the classical Sobolev space
H^{k}(I)
and
[u]^{2}_{k+s}(I)<+\infty
, where

[u]^{2}_{k+s}(I)=\int_{I\times I}\frac{|u^{(k)}(x)-u^{(k)}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}dxdy.

Note that
[u]^{2}_{k+s}(I)=[u^{(k)}]_{s}(I)
. If
s=0
we set
[u]^{2}_{k}(I)=\int_{I}|u^{(k)}|^{2}dx
.

Let
W\colon\mathbb{R}\to[0,+\infty)
be a usual
double-well potential
; that is, a non-negative continuous function with
W(z)=0
only if
z\in\{-1,1\}
. As growth conditions are concerned, we only require that there exist
\alpha_{W},\beta_{W}>0
such that

W(z)\geq\alpha_{W}\min\{(z+1)^{2},(z-1)^{2},\beta_{W}\}
(H1)

for all
z\in\mathbb{R}
, and that there exists
\overline{\eta}>0
and
\gamma_{W}
such that

W(z)\leq\gamma_{W}\min\{(z+1)^{2},(z-1)^{2}\}
(H2)

if
||z|-1|\leq\overline{\eta}
.

Conditions (H1) and (H2) are satisfied if there exist
W^{\prime\prime}(1),W^{\prime\prime}(-1)>0
, and
W
is larger than a positive constant at infinity.

Our main result is the following.
Note that the condition
k+s>\frac{1}{2}
ensures that the coefficient of the
perturbation tends to
0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
k+s>\frac{1}{2}
and let
F_{\varepsilon}
be defined by

F_{\varepsilon}(u)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{I}W(u(x))dx+\varepsilon^{2(k+s)-1}[u]^{2}_{k+s}(I).

Then the
\Gamma
-limit of
F_{\varepsilon}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
with respect to the convergence in measure and in
L^{1}(0,1)
is the sharp-interface functional

F(u)=m_{k+s}\#S(u)
defined on
BV(I;\{-1,1\})
, with

m_{k+s}=\inf\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}}W(v)dx+[v]^{2}_{k+s}(\mathbb{R}):v\in H^{k+s}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}),\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}v(x)=\pm 1\Big\}.

(4)

This theorem is complemented by the corresponding
equi-coerciveness result
Theorem
9
.

Remark 2

(convergence of minimum problems)
.

If
W(z)\geq c_{1}|z|^{p}-c_{2}
for some positive constants
c_{1},c_{2}
and
p\geq 1
, then from the coerciveness in measure we deduce that the functionals
F_{\varepsilon}
are equi-coercive in
L^{1}(I)
. As a consequence, we deduce the convergence of minimum problems of the form

\min\bigg\{F_{\varepsilon}(u)-\int_{I}fu\,dt\bigg\}\qquad\hbox{ and }\qquad\min\bigg\{F_{\varepsilon}(u):\int_{I}u\,dt=m\bigg\},

with
f\in L^{p^{\prime}}(I)
and
m\in(0,|I|)
, to the corresponding problems for
F
. Analogously, one obtains the convergence with suitably defined boundary data (see
[
29
]
).

3
Preliminary results

In this section we gather some results that will be used in the proofs. The first part contains interpolation results for fractional Sobolev spaces, the second part contains properties of sequences of functions with bounded energies
F_{\varepsilon}
deriving from those interpolation results.

3.1
Interpolation results in fractional Soboles spaces

The following two propositions are derived from known results in the theory of fractional Sobolev spaces.

Proposition 3

(Interpolation inequality)
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
k\geq 1
and
s\in(0,1)
.
There exists a constant
R_{k,s}>0
such that for all

I\subset\mathbb{R}
bounded interval, and
u\in H^{k+s}(I)
, the interpolation inequality

\|u^{(\ell)}\|_{L^{2}(I)}\leq R_{k,s}\big(|I|^{-\ell}\|u\|_{L^{2}(I)}+\|u\|^{\theta}_{L^{2}(I)}[u]^{1-\theta}_{k+s}(I)\big)

(5)

holds for all
\ell\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, where
\theta=1-\frac{\ell}{k+s}
.

Proof.

Theorem 5.8 and Exercise 5.9 in
[
22
]
show that

\|u^{(\ell)}\|_{L^{2}(I)}\leq C\big(t^{-\ell}\|u\|_{L^{2}(I)}+t^{k-\ell}[u^{(k)}]_{s}(I)\big)

for all
0<t\leq|I|
.
An algebraic manipulation of this inequality (see e.g.
[
22
, Exercise 1.26(ii)]
) gives (
5
).
∎

Lemma 4

(Estimate of
\|u\|_{L^{2}}
with the
s
-seminorm)
.

There exists
C>0
such that
for any
I\subset\mathbb{R}
open bounded interval and
u\in H^{s}(I)
the following inequality holds:

\|u\|_{L^{2}(I)}\leq C\Big(|I|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bigg|\int_{J}u(x)\,dx\bigg|+|I|^{s}[u]_{s}(I)\Big),

(6)

where
J\subseteq I
is any interval such that
2|J|\geq|I|
.

Proof.

Let
s^{\prime}<\min\{\frac{1}{2},s\}
be fixed, and let
p=p(s^{\prime})=\frac{2}{1-2s^{\prime}}
.
Then (see
[
22
, Corollary 2.3]
) there exists a positive constant
C
such that for any
I\subset\mathbb{R}
an open bounded interval and
u\in H^{s}(I)
we can estimate

\|u\|_{L^{p}(I)}\leq C\Big(|I|^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\Big|\int_{J}u(x)\,dx\Big|+[u]_{s^{\prime}}(I)\Big),

(7)

where
J\subseteq I
is any interval such that
2|J|\geq|I|
.
By Hölder’s inequality we get

\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I)}\leq|I|^{2s^{\prime}}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{p}(I)},

so that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}(I)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C|I|^{s^{\prime}}\bigg(|I|^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\Big|\int_{J}u(x)\,dx\Big|+[u]_{s^{\prime}}(I)\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\bigg(|I|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big|\int_{J}u(x)\,dx\Big|+|I|^{s^{\prime}}[u]_{s^{\prime}}(I)\bigg).

Lemma 2.6 in
[
22
]
ensures that

[u]_{s^{\prime}}(I)\leq|I|^{s-s^{\prime}}[u]_{s}(I)
,
and the proof of the claim is complete.
∎

3.2
Analysis of functions with values close to the wells

This section contains the main technical results that will be used in the sequel.
For ease of notation, we will consider functions defined on
(0,1)
, for which we drop the dependence on the interval, simply writing
[u]_{k+s}
in the place of
[u]_{k+s}(0,1)
. All results will be independent of this choice.

The letter
I
will be then used for a generic interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, for which we set

F_{\varepsilon}(u;I)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{I}W(u(x))dx+\varepsilon^{2(k+s)-1}[u]^{2}_{k+s}(I).

Let
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
be a sequence of functions in
H^{k+s}(0,1)
such that

\sup_{\varepsilon>0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=S<+\infty.

(8)

We can suppose that
u_{\varepsilon}\in H^{k+s}(0,1)\cap C^{\infty}([0,1])
by the density of such functions with respect to the convergence in
H^{k+s}(0,1)
(see e.g.
[
22
, Theorem 1.64]
).

With fixed
\eta\in(0,\min\{1,\sqrt{\beta_{W}}\})
we set

A_{\varepsilon}^{\eta}=\{t\in(0,1):\big||u_{\varepsilon}(t)|-1\big|<\eta\}.

(9)

Hypothesis (H1) implies that we have

W(z)\geq\alpha_{W}(z-1)^{2}\ \hbox{\rm in }[1-\eta,1+\eta]\ \ \hbox{\rm and}\ \ W(z)\geq\alpha_{W}(z+1)^{2}\ \hbox{\rm in }[-1-\eta,-1+\eta]

for all
\eta\in(0,\min\{1,\sqrt{\beta_{W}}\})
.
Hence also

|\{t\in(0,1):||u_{\varepsilon}(t)|-1|\geq\eta\}|\leq\frac{S\varepsilon}{\alpha_{W}}\Big(\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}+\frac{1}{\beta_{W}}\Big)<\frac{2S\varepsilon}{\alpha_{W}\eta^{2}}.

(10)

Lemma 5

(Bound on the derivatives)
.

Let
k\neq 0
and
\eta\in(0,\min\{1,\sqrt{\beta_{W}}\})
be fixed.
Then there exists a constant
R=\widehat{R}_{k,s}>0
such that for any interval
I\subset A_{\varepsilon}^{\eta}
with
|I|\geq R\varepsilon
we have

\Big|\Big\{t\in I:|u_{\varepsilon}^{(\ell)}(t)|<\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{\ell}}\ \hbox{\rm for all }\ell\in\{1,\dots,k\}\Big\}\Big|>0.

Proof.

It is not restrictive to assume that
I\subset A_{\varepsilon}^{\eta}
is such that
|u_{\varepsilon}(t)-1|<\eta

(11)

Fixing

AB\leq\frac{1}{p}A^{p}+\frac{1}{q}B^{q}

obtaining

\varepsilon^{2\ell-1}\|u_{\varepsilon}-1\|^{2\theta}_{L^{2}(I)}[u_{\varepsilon}]^{2(1-\theta)}_{k+s}(I)\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\|u_{\varepsilon}-1\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I)}+\varepsilon^{2(k+s)-1}[u_{\varepsilon}]^{2}_{k+s}(I).

(12)

By Proposition

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}^{(\ell)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle R_{k,s}^{2}\Big(|I|^{-\ell}\|u_{\varepsilon}-1\|_{L^{2}(I)}+\|u_{\varepsilon}-1\|^{\theta}_{L^{2}(I)}[u_{\varepsilon}]^{1-\theta}_{k+s}(I)\Big)^{2}

(13)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2R_{k,s}^{2}\Big(|I|^{-2\ell}\|u_{\varepsilon}-1\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I)}+\|u_{\varepsilon}-1\|^{2\theta}_{L^{2}(I)}[u_{\varepsilon}]^{2(1-\theta)}_{k+s}(I)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2R^{2}_{k,s}\max\{1,\alpha_{W}^{-1}\}S(|I|^{-2\ell}+\varepsilon^{-2\ell})\varepsilon

for all
\ell\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, where

is the constant in (

We now suppose that

\Big|\Big\{t\in I:|u_{\varepsilon}^{(\ell)}(t)|<\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{\ell}}\ \hbox{\rm for all }\ell\in\{1,\dots,k\}\Big\}\Big|=0.

(14)

Then, there exists
\ell\in\{1,\dots,k\}
such that

\Big|\Big\{t\in I:|u_{\varepsilon}^{(\ell)}(t)|\geq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{\ell}}\Big\}\Big|\geq\frac{|I|}{k},

so that by (

\frac{|I|}{k}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2\ell}}\leq 2R^{2}_{k,s}\max\{1,\alpha_{W}^{-1}\}S(|I|^{-2\ell}+\varepsilon^{-2\ell})\varepsilon,

which implies that either

|I|^{1+2\ell}\leq 4R^{2}_{k,s}k\max\{1,\alpha_{W}^{-1}\}S\varepsilon^{1+2\ell}

(15)

or

|I|\leq 4R^{2}_{k,s}k\max\{1,\alpha_{W}^{-1}\}S\varepsilon.

(16)

We have then shown that if (

independent of
\eta
,

and

∎

Lemma 7

(Bound of the length of intervals with “large” derivatives)
.

Let

, and let

(17)

Then, for all
\varepsilon_{r}\to 0
as

and

sequence of intervals satisfying

Let

4
Compactness

In this section we prove that the sequence
F_{\varepsilon}
is equicoercive; more precisely, that sequences
u_{\varepsilon}
with equibounded energies are pre-compact in measure. We recall that we consider functions defined on
(0,1)
for ease of notation.

Theorem 9

(Compactness)
.

Let
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
be a family in
H^{k+s}(0,1)
such that
\sup_{\varepsilon>0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\leq S<+\infty
. Then, there exist
u\in BV((0,1);\{-1,1\})
and a subsequence
\varepsilon_{r}\to 0
such that
u_{\varepsilon_{r}}\to u
in measure. Furthermore, if
W
satisfies
W(z)\geq c_{1}|z|^{p}-c_{2}
for some
c_{1},c_{2}
and
p\geq 1
, then
u_{\varepsilon_{r}}\to u
in
L^{p}(0,1)
.

Before proving Theorem
9
, we give the definition of transition intervals (between two values
\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}
) of the sequence
u_{\varepsilon}
. Let
v
be a continuous function; we say that an open interval
I\subset(0,1)
is a
transition interval for
v
between
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
if
v(t)\in(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
for
t\in I
, and
\{v(\inf I),v(\sup I)\}=\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\}
. Note that different transition intervals are disjoint.
With given a sequence
u_{\varepsilon}
, the set of the transition intervals for
u_{\varepsilon}
between
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
is denoted by
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
.

We will prove a bound on the number of transition intervals “not crossing the wells”; that is, between two values
\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}
such that
[\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}]\cap\{-1,1\}=\emptyset
. In particular, with fixed

\eta\in(0,\min\{1,\sqrt{\beta_{W}}\})
, we will consider transition intervals between
-1+\eta
and
1-\eta
, which will be called
\eta
-transition intervals
.
With given a sequence
u_{\varepsilon}
, the set of the
\eta
-transition intervals for
u_{\varepsilon}
is denoted by
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\eta}
.

In the following result we prove that the number of transitions between two values as above is equibounded. In particular, we will prove the compactness theorem by using the fact the number of the
\eta
-transitions is equibounded, while the general case will be used in the proof of the lower-bound estimate.

Proposition 10

(Bound on the number of transitions)
.

Let
k+s>\frac{1}{2}
and let
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}\subset H^{k+s}(0,1)\cap C^{\infty}([0,1])
with
F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\leq S<+\infty
. Let
\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}
be such that

[\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}]\cap\{-1,1\}=\emptyset
, and let
\varepsilon_{r}\to 0
. Then,

\sup_{r}\#\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon_{r}}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})<+\infty
.

Proof.

We first treat the case
k=0
and
s>\frac{1}{2}
as it uses a simpler argument.
Suppose by contradiction that there exist
M_{r}
transition intervals for
u_{r}:=u_{\varepsilon_{r}}
with
M_{r}\to+\infty
. Then there exists a transition interval
I_{r}
between
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
such that

F_{\varepsilon_{r}}(u_{r};I_{r})=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{r}}\int_{I_{r}}W(u_{r})\,dx+\varepsilon_{r}^{2s-1}[u_{r}]^{2}_{k+s}(I_{r})\leq\frac{S}{M_{r}}=o(1).

(19)

Since
[\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}]\cap\{-1,1\}=\emptyset
we have
W(u_{r})\geq C>0
on
I_{r}
, with
C
depending only on
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
, so that in particular

\frac{|I_{r}|}{\varepsilon_{r}}\leq\frac{1}{C\varepsilon_{r}}\int_{I_{r}}W(u_{r})\,dx\leq\frac{1}{C}F_{\varepsilon_{r}}(u_{r};I_{r})=o(1).

If
I_{r}=(\alpha_{r},\beta_{r})
, we set
v_{r}(t)=u_{r}(\alpha_{r}+(\beta_{r}-\alpha_{r})t)-u_{r}(\alpha_{r})
. A change of variables gives

[v_{r}]^{2}_{s}(0,1)=(\beta_{r}-\alpha_{r})^{2s-1}[u_{r}]^{2}_{s}(I_{r})\leq\Big(\frac{|I_{r}|}{\varepsilon_{r}}\Big)^{2s-1}F_{\varepsilon_{r}}(u_{r};I_{r})=o(1).

The compact embedding of
H^{s}(0,1)
in
C^{0,s^{\prime}}(0,1)
for
s^{\prime}\leq s-\frac{1}{2}
and the fact that
v_{r}(0)=0
imply the uniform convergence of
v_{r}
to
0
, which gives a contradiction since
|v_{r}(1)|=\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}
.

If
k\geq 1
an argument using the compact embedding of
H^{k+s}
is not immediately possible.
In this case, we suppose by contradiction that there exist
\widehat{M}_{r}\to+\infty
, and

(a_{r}^{j},b_{r}^{j})\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon_{r}}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
for
j\in\{1,\ldots,\widehat{M}_{r}\}
transition intervals such that
a_{r}^{j}<a_{r}^{j+1}
for all
r
and for all
j\in\{1,\dots,\widehat{M}_{r}^{\eta}-1\}
. We set

M_{r}=\bigg\lfloor\frac{\widehat{M}_{r}}{6(k+2)}\bigg\rfloor,

and define the intervals

I_{r}^{n}=(a_{r}^{1+6n(k+2)},b_{r}^{6(n+1)(k+2)}),\ \ n\in\big\{0,\dots,M_{r}\big\}.

By construction each
I_{r}^{n}
contains at least
6(k+2)
transition intervals for
u_{r}
.

We then choose
n_{r}\in\{0,\dots,M_{r}\}
such that, setting
I_{r}=I_{r}^{n_{r}}
,

F_{\varepsilon_{r}}(u_{r};I_{r})=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{r}}\int_{I_{r}}W(u_{r})\,dx+\varepsilon_{r}^{2(k+s)-1}[u_{r}]^{2}_{k+s}(I_{r})\leq\frac{S}{M_{r}}.

(20)

The argument by contradiction in the following will consist in a scaling argument producing a sequence of functions with equibounded
H^{k+s}
norm on a common interval and uniformly converging to a constant. Since the same functions are transition functions this will prove the contradiction. In order to bound the
H^{k+s}
norm we make an iterated use of the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, which will be possible since for each
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}
we will find points of the reference intervals where the
\ell
-th derivative vanishes. This in turn is possible by the simple observation that in an interval with
k
transitions there are at least
k-1
extremal points, and hence
k-1
points where the first derivative vanishes. Repeating the argument, the
\ell
-th derivative vanishes in at least
k-\ell
points.

We note that this argument can be directly applied to the interval
I_{r}
if it is of length
of order
\varepsilon_{r}
, while otherwise it will be applied to suitably chosen subintervals by use of interpolation arguments (for which a higher number of transition intervals is needed, which explains the number
6(k+2)
above).
We separately treat the two cases.

Case
1
:
\sup_{r}\frac{|I_{r}|}{\varepsilon_{r}}\leq T<+\infty
.

We divide
I_{r}
in three subintervals, each one containing (at least)
2k+3
transition intervals between
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}

I^{-}_{r}

\lambda_{2}
,
there exist
\alpha^{1}_{r}\in I^{-}_{r}

.
Moreover both
I^{-}_{r}

, and, if

Setting

\displaystyle[v_{r}]^{2}_{k+s}(0,1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\beta^{k}_{r}-\alpha^{k}_{r})^{2(k+s)-1}[u_{r}]^{2}_{k+s}(\alpha^{k}_{r},\beta^{k}_{r})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\beta^{k}_{r}-\alpha^{k}_{r}}{\varepsilon_{r}}\Big)^{2(k+s)-1}\frac{S}{M_{r}}\leq\frac{{T}^{2(k+s)-1}S}{M_{r}},

Noting that by construction

k

\lambda_{1}

.

4

{H^{k}(0,1)}

Case

:
\sup_{r}\frac{|I_{r}|}{\varepsilon_{r}}=+\infty

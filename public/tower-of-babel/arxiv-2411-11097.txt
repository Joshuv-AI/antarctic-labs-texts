The algebraic semantics for the one-variable monadic fragment of the predicate logic 𝒢 ∀ ∼ {\cal G}\forall_{\sim}
Diego Castaño, Valeria Castaño, José Patricio Díaz Varela and Marcela Muñoz Santis

Abstract
In this article we characterize the equivalent algebraic semantics for the one-variable monadic fragment of the first-order logic 𝒢 ∀ ∼ {\cal G}\forall_{\mathop{\sim}} defined by F. Esteva, L. Godo, P. Hájek and M. Navara in Residuated fuzzy logics with an involutive negation , Archive for Mathematical Logic 39 (2000). To this end, we first introduce the variety 𝕄 ​ 𝔾 ∼ \mathbb{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}} as a certain class of Gödel algebras endowed with two monadic operators and a De Morgan negation. We study its basic properties, determine its subdirectly irreducible members and prove that this variety has the finite embeddabilty property. In particular, we prove that a special subvariety ℂ ​ 𝕄 ​ 𝔾 ∼ \mathbb{CMG}_{\mathop{\sim}} of 𝕄 ​ 𝔾 ∼ \mathbb{MG}_{\sim} is exactly the desired equivalent algebraic semantics; this is done via a functional representation of finite subdirectly irreducible algebras.

Introduction and preliminares

BL
-algebras were introduced by P. Hájek in his book
[
7
]
as the algebraic semantics of his basic fuzzy logic (
{\cal BL}
). One of the most important extensions of
{\cal BL}
is Gödel Logic (
{\cal G}
), obtained by adding the axiom schema
\varphi\to\varphi^{2}
, whose algebraic semantics, Gödel algebras, is also given in
[
7
]
. Recall that the variety of Gödel algebras (
\mathbb{G}
) coincides with the subvariety of Heyting algebras determined by the prelineality equation
(x\to y)\vee(y\to x)\approx 1
and
\mathbb{G}
is generated by the Gödel t-norm
[0,1]_{\mathbb{G}}
.

Later, in
[
6
]
, F. Esteva, L, Godo, P. Hájek and M. Navara introduced the logic
{\cal G}_{\mathop{\sim}}
as an expansion of Gödel logic adding an arbitrary independent involutive negation given by the following axiom schemata:

(\mathop{\sim}1)

\mathop{\sim}(\mathop{\sim}\varphi)\equiv\varphi

(\mathop{\sim}2)

\neg\varphi\to\mathop{\sim}\varphi

(\mathop{\sim}3)

\Delta(\varphi\to\psi)\to\Delta(\mathop{\sim}\psi\to\mathop{\sim}\varphi)

(\Delta 1)

\Delta\varphi\vee\neg\Delta\varphi

(\Delta 2)

\Delta(\varphi\vee\psi)\to(\Delta\varphi\vee\Delta\psi)

(\Delta 5)

\Delta(\varphi\to\psi)\to(\Delta\varphi\to\Delta\psi)

where
\Delta\varphi
is
\neg\mathop{\sim}\varphi
and
\varphi\equiv\psi
is
(\varphi\to\psi)\&(\psi\to\varphi)
. The deduction rules of
{\cal G}_{\mathop{\sim}}
are
modus ponens
(
\displaystyle\frac{\varphi,\varphi\to\psi}{\psi}
) and
necessitation
for
\Delta
(
\displaystyle\frac{\varphi}{\Delta\varphi}
).

They also defined the predicate calculus based on this logic
({\cal G}\forall_{\mathop{\sim}})
by taking as axioms those of
{\cal G}_{\mathop{\sim}}
plus the following five axioms for quantifiers given by P. Hájek in his basic predicate logic:

(\forall 1)

(\forall x)\varphi(x)\to\varphi(t)
(
t
substitutable for
x
in
\varphi(x)
)

(\exists 1)

\varphi(t)\to(\exists x)\varphi(x)
(
t
substitutable for
x
in
\varphi(x)
)

(\forall 2)

(\forall x)(\psi\to\varphi)\to(\psi\to(\forall x)\varphi)
(
x
not free in
\psi
)

(\exists 2)

(\forall x)(\psi\to\varphi)\to((\exists x)\psi\to\varphi)
(
x
not free in
\varphi
)

(\forall 3)

(\forall x)(\psi\vee\varphi)\to((\forall x)\psi\vee\varphi)
(
x
not free in
\varphi
)

The inference rules of this calculus are modus ponens, necessitation (
\displaystyle\frac{\varphi}{\Delta\varphi}
) and generalization (
\displaystyle\frac{\varphi}{\forall\varphi}
).

In recent articles, D. Castaño, C. Cimadamore, J. P. Díaz Varela and L. Rueda introduced and studied the variety
\mathbb{MG}
of monadic Gödel algebras as an expansion of Gödel algebras by two monadic operators
\exists
and
\forall
. They showed that these algebras constitute the equivalent algebraic semantics of the one-variable monadic fragment of the predicate Gödel logic
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
. Following this line of research we intend to find the equivalent algebraic semantics of the one-variable monadic fragment of the first-order logic
{\cal G}\forall_{\mathop{\sim}}
, which is the main result of this paper.
We start by introducing an expansion
\mathbb{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
of the variety
\mathbb{MG}
by a unary operation
\mathop{\sim}
. In Sections 2 and 3, we make a standard study of the basic properties of the algebras in this variety, which includes the characterization of congruences and subdirectly irreducible algebras. We also prove that
\mathbb{MG}_{\sim}
has the finite embeddability property (FEP for short) and so it is generated by its finite members. In the next sections, we introduce an important subvariety of
\mathbb{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
, the variety
\mathbb{CMG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
, characterized within the variety
\mathbb{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
by the identity
\exists(x\wedge\mathop{\sim}x)\to\forall(x\vee\mathop{\sim}x)\approx 1
, that is,
\exists(x\wedge\mathop{\sim}x)\leq\forall(x\vee\mathop{\sim}x)
. We are interested in this subvariety because the formula
(\exists x)(\varphi\wedge\mathop{\sim}\varphi)\to(\forall x)(\varphi\vee\mathop{\sim}\varphi)
is a theorem in the predicate calculus based on the logic
{\cal G}_{\mathop{\sim}}
. We give a complete characterization of the range of the monadic operators in algebras within this variety, which allows us to show that
\mathbb{CMG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
is precisely the variety generated by the functional monadic
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebras. Finally, in Section 6, taking into account that functional algebras encode the information of the models used to interpret the one-variable monadic fragment of the first-order logic
{\cal G}\forall_{\mathop{\sim}}
, we prove that
\mathbb{CMG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
is the equivalent algebraic semantics for this calculus.

Recall (see
[
3
]
) that the algebras in
\mathbb{MG}
(monadic Gödel algebras, which we call
MG
-algebras in this article, for short) are Gödel algebras endowed with two unary operations
\forall
and
\exists
that satisfy the following identities:

(M1)

\forall x\to x\thickapprox 1,

(M2)

\forall(x\to\forall y)\thickapprox\exists x\to\forall y,

(M3)

\forall(\forall x\to y)\thickapprox\forall x\to\forall y,

(M4)

\forall(\exists x\lor y)\thickapprox\exists x\lor\forall y.

Note that in
[
3
]
there is an additional identity (M5) that holds trivially in this case.

We are interested in studying
MG
-algebras with an involutive negation. The following definition is motivated by the axioms of the predicate calculus based on the logic
{\cal G}_{\mathop{\sim}}
.

Definition 1.1
.

An algebra
\langle A,\vee,\wedge,\to,\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall,0,1\rangle
is an
MG_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra
if
\langle A,\vee,\wedge,\to,\exists,\forall,0,1\rangle
is a monadic Gödel algebra,
\langle A,\vee,\wedge,\mathop{\sim},0,1\rangle
is a De Morgan algebra and the following identities hold:

(N)

\neg x\to\mathop{\sim}x\approx 1
, that is,
\neg x\leq\mathop{\sim}x
,

(Q)

\forall x\approx\mathop{\sim}\exists\mathop{\sim}x
,

where
\neg x:=x\to 0
.

We denote by
\mathbb{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
the variety of
MG_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebras and by
\mathbb{G}_{\mathop{\sim}}
the variety of Gödel algebras with a De Morgan negation that satisfies

.

{\bf A}

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
be an algebra in
\mathbb{G}_{\mathop{\sim}}

(1)

\neg a\vee\neg\neg a=1

(2)

\Delta(a\to b)=\Delta(\mathop{\sim}b\to\mathop{\sim}a)

(3)

\Delta a\lor\neg\Delta a=1

(4)

\Delta(a\lor b)=\Delta a\lor\Delta b

(5)

\Delta a\land\Delta(a\to b)\leq\Delta b

(6)

\Delta a\leq a

\Delta\Delta a=\Delta a
,

where

Observe that
(1)

\mathbb{G}_{\mathop{\sim}}

-algebras (see
[
6

Recall that a
filter
of a
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is an increasing nonempty set

whenever

a\in F
or
b\in F
. We have the following important properties of the variety
\mathbb{G}_{\mathop{\sim}}
.

Lemma 1.3
.

Let
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
be an algebra in
\mathbb{G}_{\mathop{\sim}}
. Then:

(1)

The condition
(N)

in the definition of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathop{\sim}}
can be replaced by the Kleene condition
(K)
:
x\wedge\mathop{\sim}x\leq y\vee\mathop{\sim}y
.

(2)

The set of prime filters of
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is a disjoint union of chains. Hence, if
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is a finite algebra,
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is a product of totally ordered algebras.

(3)

The congruence lattice and the lattice of regular filters of
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
are isomorphic, where
F
is a regular filter if
F
is a filter that satisfies
\Delta x\in F
whenever
x\in F
(see
[
9
]
).

(4)

\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is a subdirectly irreducible algebra if and only if
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is totally ordered if and only if
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is simple.

Next we summarize some of the basic properties of monadic Gödel algebras; all the proofs can be found in

Lemma 1.4
.

Let
\langle{\bf A},\exists,\forall\rangle
be an algebra in

. Then:

(1)

\exists A=\forall A
.

(2)

\exists{\bf A}
is a subalgebra of
{\bf A}
.

(3)

The congruence lattice and the lattice of monadic filters of
\langle{\bf A},\exists,\forall\rangle
are isomorphic, where
F
is a monadic filter if

is a filter that satisfies

whenever
x\in F
.

(4)

The congruence lattices of
\langle{\bf A},\exists,\forall\rangle
and
\exists{\bf A}

(5)

\langle{\bf A},\exists,\forall\rangle
is finitely subdirectly irreducible if and only if
\exists{\bf A}
is totally ordered.

Some arithmetical properties of
MG
-algebras useful for this work are the following.

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
\langle{\bf A},\exists,\forall\rangle
be an algebra in
\mathbb{MG}
. Then, for any

(1)

\forall 1=\exists 1=1

\forall(a\vee\forall b)=\forall a\vee\forall b

(5)

2
Subdirectly irreducible
MG_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebras

Let
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
be an algebra in the variety
\mathbb{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
. In this section we prove that
\langle\exists{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is a subalgebra of
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
and that the congruence lattices of
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
and
\langle\exists{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
are isomorphic.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
is an algebra in
\mathbb{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
, then
\langle\exists{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is a subalgebra of
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
.

Proof.

Since
\langle{\bf A},\exists,\forall\rangle
is an
MG
-algebra, by Lemma
1.4
, we have that
\exists{\bf A}
is a Gödel algebra and
\exists A=\forall A
. We now consider
x\in\exists A
, then
x=\exists z
, for some
z\in A
. From
(Q)
, we obtain
\forall\mathop{\sim}z=\mathop{\sim}\exists z
and thus
\mathop{\sim}x=\forall\mathop{\sim}z
. This proves that
\langle\exists{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is a subalgebra of
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
.

\quad\hfill\blacksquare

Let us define the following lattices

•

\mbox{{\bf Con}}({\bf A})
: the congruence lattice of an algebra
{\bf A}
.

•

{\bf F}_{r}(\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle)
: the lattice of regular filters of an algebra
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
in
\mathbb{G}_{\mathop{\sim}}
.

•

{\bf F}_{m}(\langle{\bf A},\exists,\forall\rangle)
: the lattice of monadic filters of an algebra
\langle{\bf A},\exists,\forall\rangle
in
\mathbb{MG}
.

•

{\bf F}_{mr}(\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle)
: the lattice of monadic regular filters of an algebra
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
in
\mathbb{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
, where
F\subseteq A
is a
monadic regular filter
if
F
is both, monadic and regular.

In
[
3
, Th. 2.7]
it was proved that
{\bf F}_{m}(\langle{\bf A},\exists,\forall\rangle)
is isomorphic to
{\bf Con}(\langle{\bf A},\exists,\forall\rangle))
, for each algebra
\langle{\bf A},\exists,\forall\rangle
in
\mathbb{MG}
, and the isomorphism is given by
F\longmapsto\theta_{F}=\{(a,b)\in A^{2}:(a\to b)\wedge(b\to a)\in F\}.

Similarly, in
[
9
, Th. 3.3]
it was proved that
{\bf F}_{r}(\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle)
is isomorphic to
{\bf Con}(\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle)
, for each algebra
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
in
\mathbb{G}_{\mathop{\sim}}
, and the isomorphism is given by
F\longmapsto\theta^{\prime}_{F}=\{(a,b)\in A^{2}:a\wedge f=b\wedge f,\mbox{ for some }f\in F\}.

Taking these results into account as well as the fact that
\theta_{F}=\theta^{\prime}_{F}
in an
MG_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra the following proposition is immediate.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
be an
{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra. The correspondence
\mbox{{\bf F}}_{mr}(\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle)\to\mbox{{\bf Con}}(\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle)
defined by
F\longmapsto\theta_{F}=\{(a,b)\in A^{2}:(a\to b)\wedge(b\to a)\in F\}
is an order isomorphism whose inverse is given by
\theta\longmapsto 1/\theta
.

In
[
9
]
H.P. Sankappanavar defined the following terms:

t_{k}(x):=\Delta^{0}x\wedge\Delta^{1}x\wedge\Delta^{2}x\wedge\ldots\wedge\Delta^{k}x,

where
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\Delta^{n}x
is defined recursively:
\Delta^{0}x:=x
and
\Delta^{n+1}x:=\Delta^{n}(\Delta x)
, for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. He used them to prove that given a De Morgan Heyting algebra
{\bf L}
and
X\subseteq L
,
X\neq\emptyset
, the regular filter generated by
X
is the set

Fg_{r}(X)=\{a\in L:t_{k_{1}}(x_{1})\wedge\ldots\wedge t_{k_{n}}(x_{n})\leq a,\mbox{ for some }x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X,k_{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0},n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

Observe that algebras in the variety
\mathbb{G}_{\mathop{\sim}}
satisfy
\Delta x\leq x
and
\Delta^{2}x=\Delta x
(Lemma
1.2
). Then, on any of these algebras we have
t_{0}(x)=x
and
t_{k}(x)=\Delta x
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, for
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
in
\mathbb{G}_{\mathop{\sim}}
and
\emptyset\neq X\subseteq A
:

\displaystyle Fg_{r}(X)=\{a\in A:\Delta x_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\Delta x_{n}\leq a,\mbox{ for some }x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X,n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

(1)

Lemma 2.3
.

Given an
{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
and
X\subseteq A
,
X\neq\emptyset
, the monadic regular filter generated by
X
is the set

\begin{array}[]{lcl}Fg_{mr}(X)&=&Fg_{r}(\{\forall x:x\in X\})\\
&=&\{a\in A:\Delta\forall x_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\Delta\forall x_{n}\leq a,\mbox{ where }x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\in X,n\in\mathbb{N}\}.\par\end{array}

Proof.

Let
S=\{a\in A:\Delta\forall x_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\Delta\forall x_{n}\leq a,\mbox{ where }x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\in X\}
. By (
1
) we have that
S=Fg_{r}(\{\forall x,x\in X\}
in the algebra
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
. Thus, if
a\in S
,
\Delta a\in S
.

Let us prove that
\forall a\in S
whenever
a\in S
. Given
a\in S
, there exist
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
such that
\Delta\forall x_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\Delta\forall x_{n}\leq a
. By Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
1.4
(1) we know that
\langle\forall{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is a subalgebra of
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
. Then, there exists
y\in A
such that
\Delta\forall x_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\Delta\forall x_{n}=\forall y
. From this
\forall y\to a=1
and so,
\forall(\forall y\to a)=\forall 1=1
. Finally, by
(M3)
we obtain
\forall y\to\forall a=1
, or equivalently
\Delta\forall x_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\Delta\forall x_{n}\leq\forall a
.

We have proved that
S
is a monadic regular filter, and it is clearly the smallest one that contains
X
.

\quad\hfill\blacksquare

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
be an
MG_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra. The correspondence
{\bf F}_{mr}(\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle)\to{\bf F}_{r}(\langle\exists{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle)
defined by
F\longmapsto F\cap\exists A
is an order isomorphism whose inverse is given by
S\mapsto Fg_{mr}(S)
.

Proof.

Let
F
be a monadic regular filter of
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
. Since
\langle\exists{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is a subalgebra of
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
, we have that
F\cap\exists A
is a regular filter of
\langle\exists{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
.

Let us prove that
Fg_{mr}(F\cap\exists A)=F
, for all
F\in\mbox{F}_{mr}(\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle)
. From
F\cap\exists A\subseteq F
, it follows that
Fg_{mr}(F\cap\exists A)\subseteq F
. Now suppose
x\in F
. Since
\exists A=\forall A
and
F
is a monadic filter we have that
\forall x\in F\cap\exists A
. By Lemma
1.5
(2) we know that
\forall x\leq x
and consequently
x\in Fg_{mr}(F\cap\exists A)
.

Let us prove that
Fg_{mr}(S)\cap\exists A=S
for each
S\in\mbox{F}_{r}(\langle\exists{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle)
. Using Lemma
2.3
and
\forall S=S
we obtain that

Fg_{mr}(S)\cap\exists A=Fg_{r}(\forall S)\cap\exists A=Fg_{r}(S)\cap\exists A.

Moreover,
Fg_{r}(S)\cap\exists A=S
. Indeed, from
(\ref{filtro monádico regular})
we know that if
a\in Fg_{r}(S)\cap\exists A
then
a\geq\Delta x_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\Delta x_{n}
, for some
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in S
. Now, since
S
is a regular filter,
a\in\exists A
and
S
is an increasing set on
\exists A
, we have that
a\in S
. Thus,
Fg_{r}(S)\cap\exists A\subseteq S
. The other inclusion is trivial.

Hence, it is clear that the correspondence
F\mapsto F\cap\exists A
is an order isomorphism.

\quad\hfill\blacksquare

1.3

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle\in\mathbb{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}

(1)

\mbox{{\bf Con}}(\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle)\cong{\bf F}_{mr}(\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle)\cong{\bf F}_{r}(\langle\exists{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle)\cong{\bf Con}(\langle\exists{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle)

(2)

The following are equivalent:

(a)

\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
is subdirectly irreducible.

(b)

\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
is simple.

(c)

\langle\exists{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is subdirectly irreducible.

(d)

\langle\exists{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is simple.

(e)

\langle\exists{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is totally ordered.

Remark 2.6
.

In particular, if
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
is a finite subdirectly irreducible algebra in

we have, by Corollary

(2), that
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle\cong\prod_{i=1}^{r}\langle{\bf C}_{n_{i}},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
, where
\langle{\bf C}_{n_{i}},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is the
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-chain with

\langle\exists{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is a totally ordered subalgebra of
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
.

As a consequence of the results given in this section we obtain an important property of the variety
\mathbb{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
.

Theorem 2.7
.

\mathbb{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
is a discriminator variety.

Proof.

We consider the term
T(x):=\Delta\forall x
. Since
\langle\exists{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is totally ordered for each subdirectly irreducible algebra
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
in
\mathbb{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
, it follows that:

T(a)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{ if }a=1\\
0&\mbox{ if }a\neq 1,\end{array}\right.

for all
a\in A
.
Then, it is immediate that a discriminator term for each subdirectly irreducible algebra is given by

t(x,y,z):=(T((x\to y)\wedge(y\to x))\wedge z)\vee(\neg(T((x\to y)\wedge(y\to x))\wedge x).

\quad\hfill\blacksquare

Corollary 2.8
.

\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\exists,\forall\rangle\in\mathbb{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
is subdirectly irreducible if and only if
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
is simple if and only if
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
is directly indescomposable.

3
Generations by finite members

We note that the variety
\mathbb{G}_{\mathop{\sim}}
is locally finite. In fact, since all of the subdirectly irreducible algebras in this variety are totally ordered (Lemma
1.3
) it is easy to see that each
n
-generated chain has at most
2n+2
elements. This implies that the class of subdirectly irreducible algebras in
\mathbb{G}_{\mathop{\sim}}
is uniformly locally finite and so, using
[
2
, Th. 3.7]
we have that
\mathbb{G}_{\mathop{\sim}}
is locally finite. This property is not true for the
variety
\mathbb{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
, as we will show later.
Nevertheless, we prove next that the variety
\mathbb{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
has the FEP and thus, it is generated by its finite members. In order to show this it will be useful to describe which subalgebras of a
G_{\sim}
-algebra can be the range of the quantifiers.

First recall that, in
[
3
]
, D. Castaño et al. give a characterization of the range of the quantifiers of a monadic
BL
-algebra
{\bf L}
by means of certain types of subalgebras called
m
-relatively complete subalgebras
. Given a
BL
-algebra
{\bf L}
, a subalgebra
{\bf C}
of
{\bf L}
is
m
-relatively complete
if it satisfies:

(s_{1})

For every
a\in L
, the set
\{c\in C:c\leq a\}
has a greatest element and
\{c\in C:c\geq a\}
has a least element.

(s_{2})

For every
a\in L
and
c_{1},c_{2}\in C
such that
c_{1}\leq c_{2}\vee a
, there exists
c_{3}\in C
such that
c_{1}\leq c_{2}\vee c_{3}
and
c_{3}\leq a
.

(s_{3})

For every
a\in L
and
c_{1}\in C
such that
a\ast a\leq c_{1}
, there exists
c_{2}\in C
such that
a\leq c_{2}
and
c_{2}\ast c_{2}\leq c_{1}
.

Given a
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
, a subalgebra
\langle{\bf C},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
of
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is
m
-relatively complete
if it satisfies
(s_{1})
-
(s_{3})
from the above definition. In this case, since
\wedge
coincides with
\ast
, the condition
(s_{3})
trivially holds. Moreover, if
\langle{\bf C},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is totally ordered,
(s_{2})
may be replaced by the following simpler equivalent condition (see
[
3
]
):

(s_{2}^{\prime})

If
1=c\vee a
for some
c\in C
and
a\in A
, then
c=1
or
a=1
.

The following characterization of the range of the quantifier
\exists
in an
MG_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra is analogous to the result given in
[
3
]
for
MG
-algebras, and it is useful to build
MG_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebras from a given
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
be a
{G}_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra and
\langle{\bf C},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
an
m
-relatively complete subalgebra of
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
. We define on
A
the operations

\exists a:=\min\{c\in C:c\geq a\},\ \ \ \ \forall a:=\max\{c\in C:c\leq a\},

then
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
is an
MG_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra such that
\langle\forall{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle=\langle\exists{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle=\langle{\bf C},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
. Conversely, if
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
is an
MG_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra, then
\langle\forall{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle=\langle\exists{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is an
m
-relatively complete subalgebra of
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\langle{\bf C},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is an
m
-relatively complete subalgebra of
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
and
\forall
and
\exists
are defined as in the statement of the theorem. In
[
3
]
it was proved that
\langle{\bf A},\exists,\forall\rangle
is a monadic
BL
-algebra and consequently, it is an
MG
-algebra. Moreover,
\exists A=\forall A=C
. It only remains to show that
\forall a=\mathop{\sim}\exists\mathop{\sim}a
for each
a\in A
.
We write
d=\forall a=\max\{c\in C:c\leq a\}
and
m=\exists\mathop{\sim}a=\min\{c\in C:c\geq\mathop{\sim}a\}
.
From
m\geq\mathop{\sim}a
we have that
\mathop{\sim}m\leq a
. Since
\langle{\bf C},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is a subalgebra of
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
and
m\in C
we obtain
\mathop{\sim}m\in C
and so,
\mathop{\sim}m\leq d
. Dually, from
d\leq a
,
\mathop{\sim}d\geq\mathop{\sim}a
and since
\mathop{\sim}d\in C
, we have that
m\leq\mathop{\sim}d
and so
\mathop{\sim}m\geq d
.
Therefore,
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
is an
MG_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra such that
\langle\forall{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle=\langle\exists{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle=\langle{\bf C},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
.

The converse follows from Lemma
2.1
and
[
3
, Th. 3.1]
.

\quad\hfill\blacksquare

Example 3.2
.

Fix two natural numbers
n
and
r
, and consider the chain
\langle{\bf C}_{n},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
with
n
elements in
\mathbb{G}_{\mathop{\sim}}
and the
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle:=\langle{\bf C}_{n},\mathop{\sim}\rangle^{r}
. It is easy to check that
\langle{\bf D}_{1},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
and
\langle{\bf D}_{2},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
are
m
-relatively complete subalgebras of
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
, where
D_{1}:=\{x\in A:x(i)=x(j),\mbox{ for all }i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\}
,
D_{2}:=\{0,1\}
and
0
,
1
are the least and greatest elements of
{\bf A}
, respectively. Then, by Theorem
3.1
we have that
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists_{1},\forall_{1}\rangle
and
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists_{2},\forall_{2}\rangle
are both
MG_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebras, where
\exists_{1},\forall_{1}
are the quantifiers associated with
\langle{\bf D}_{1},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
and
\exists_{2},\forall_{2}
are the quantifiers associated with
\langle{\bf D}_{2},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
.

Theorem
3.1
also allows us to build an example showing that the variety
\mathbb{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
is not locally finite. Consider the
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra
\langle[0,1]_{\mathbb{G}},\mathop{\sim}\rangle^{\mathbb{N}}
, where
[0,1]_{\mathbb{G}}
is the standard Gödel algebra on the real interval and
\mathop{\sim}
is the unary operation
defined by
\mathop{\sim}x:=1-x
. Let
C
be the set of constant sequences in
\langle[0,1]_{\mathbb{G}},\mathop{\sim}\rangle^{\mathbb{N}}
. It is easy to check that
\langle{\bf C},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is an
m
-relatively complete subalgebra of
\langle[0,1]_{\mathbb{G}},\mathop{\sim}\rangle^{\mathbb{N}}
. Thus, by the above theorem we obtain that
\langle\langle[0,1]_{\mathbb{G}},\mathop{\sim}\rangle^{\mathbb{N}},\exists,\forall\rangle
is an
MG_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra. The same argument as in
[
4
]
shows that the subalgebra of
\langle\langle[0,1]_{\mathbb{G}},\mathop{\sim}\rangle^{\mathbb{N}},\exists,\forall\rangle
generated by the element
a(n):=1-\frac{1}{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, is infinite. This shows that the variety
\mathbb{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
is not locally finite.

We now use Theorem
3.1
to prove the FEP.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
is a subdirectly irreducible
{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra and
N:=\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{n}\}
is a finite set contained in
A
, then there exists a finite subalgebra
\langle{\bf A}_{1},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
of
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
and operations
\forall_{1}
and
\exists_{1}
with the following properties:

(1)

\langle{\bf A}_{1},\mathop{\sim},\exists_{1},\forall_{1}\rangle
is a finite subdirectly irreducible
{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra.

(2)

N\subseteq A_{1}
.

(3)

If
a\in A_{1}
and
\forall a\in A_{1}
, then
\forall_{1}a=\forall a
.

(4)

If
a\in A_{1}
and
\exists a\in A_{1}
, then
\exists_{1}a=\exists a
.

Proof.

Let
\langle{\bf A}_{1},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
be the subalgebra of
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
generated by
N
. Of course,
N\subseteq A_{1}
and since the variety
\mathbb{G}_{\mathop{\sim}}
is locally finite,
A_{1}
is finite.

Let us consider
C:=A_{1}\cap\forall A
. Observe that
\langle{\bf C},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is a totally ordered subalgebra of
\langle{\bf A}_{1},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
. We claim that it is an
m
-relatively complete subalgebra. In fact, it is clear that
(s_{1})
holds since
C
is a finite chain. To show
(s^{\prime}_{2})
, suppose that
1=c\vee a
, with
c\in C
and
a\in A_{1}
. Since
\langle\exists{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is an
m
-relatively complete subalgebra of
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
,
c=1
or
a=1
.

Since
\langle{\bf C},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is an
m
-relatively complete subalgebra of
\langle{\bf A}_{1},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
, by Theorem
3.1
we have that
\langle{\bf A}_{1},\mathop{\sim},\exists_{1},\forall_{1}\rangle
is an
MG_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra where
\exists_{1}
and
\forall_{1}
are defined by:

\exists_{1}a:=\min\{c\in C:c\geq a\},\ \ \ \ \forall_{1}a:=\max\{c\in C:c\leq a\},

Moreover, since
\langle{\bf C},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is totally ordered,
\langle{\bf A}_{1},\mathop{\sim},\exists_{1},\forall_{1}\rangle
is subdirectly irreudible by Corollary
2.5
.

To prove
(3)
, suppose
a\in A_{1}
and
\forall a\in A_{1}
. Let us prove that
\forall a=\forall_{1}a=\max\{c\in C:c\leq a\}
. We know that
\forall a\in C
and
\forall a\leq a
. Suppose that
m\in C
and
m\leq a
. Since
m\in C=\forall A\cap A_{1}
, there exists
m^{\prime}\in A
such that
m=\forall m^{\prime}
. Then
m\leq a
implies
\forall\forall m^{\prime}\leq\forall a
and thus
m\leq\forall a
. This shows that
\forall_{1}a=\forall a
.

To prove
(4)
, let
a\in A_{1}
and
\exists a\in A_{1}
. Since
\langle{\bf A}_{1},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
satisfies
(Q)
we obtain
\mathop{\sim}\forall\mathop{\sim}a\in A_{1}
. Then,
\mathop{\sim}a,\forall\mathop{\sim}a\in A_{1}
. Using
(3)
we obtain
\forall\mathop{\sim}a=\forall_{1}\mathop{\sim}a
and consequently,
\exists a=\mathop{\sim}\forall\mathop{\sim}a=\mathop{\sim}\forall_{1}\mathop{\sim}a=\exists_{1}a
and the proof is complete.

\quad\hfill\blacksquare

Theorem 3.4
.

\mathbb{MG}_{\sim}
has the FEP.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.3
we have that the class of finite subdirectly irreducible algebras in
\mathbb{MG}_{\sim}
has the F.E.P. and so
\mathbb{MG}_{\sim}
has the FEP too.

\quad\hfill\blacksquare

Corollary 3.5
.

The variety
\mathbb{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
is generated as a quasivarity by its finite subdirectly irreducible members.

Proof.

Let
{\cal S}_{fin}
be the class of all
finite subdirectly irreducible algebras in
\mathbb{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
. Suppose we have terms
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},t
in variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}
such that
{\cal S}_{fin}\models(t_{1}\approx 1\wedge\ldots\wedge t_{n}\approx 1)\Rightarrow t\approx 1
but
\mathbb{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}\not\models(t_{1}\approx 1\wedge\ldots\wedge t_{n}\approx 1)\Rightarrow t\approx 1
. Then, there exists a subdirectly irreducible algebra
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle\in\mathbb{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
with
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle\not\models(t_{1}\approx 1\wedge\ldots\wedge t_{n}\approx 1)\Rightarrow t\approx 1
. Let
T
be the set of all subterms of
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},t
, and let
\overline{a}:=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})\in A^{k}
be such that
t_{1}(\overline{a})=1,\ldots,t_{n}(\overline{a})=1
but
t(\overline{a})\neq 1
. Further, let
\langle{\bf A}_{1},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
be the finite subdirectly irreducible algebra in
\mathbb{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
of Lemma
3.3
considering
N:=\{s(\overline{a}):s\in T\}
. Then, we have that
\langle{\bf A}_{1},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle\not\models(t_{1}\approx 1\wedge\ldots\wedge t_{n}\approx 1)\Rightarrow t\approx 1
, contradicting our assumption.

\quad\hfill\blacksquare

4
The subvariety
\mathbb{CMG}_{\mathop{\sim}}

In this section we focus our attention on a particular subvariety of
\mathbb{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
, namely
\mathbb{CMG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
, whose members are characterized within the variety
\mathbb{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
by the following condition:

\exists(x\wedge\mathop{\sim}x)\leq\forall(x\vee\mathop{\sim}x).

(
C
)

\mathbb{CMG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
is a proper subvariety of
\mathbb{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
since the subalgebras in Example
3.2
with
D_{2}=\{0,1\}
do not satisfy
(C)
for
n\geq 3
.

In Section
6
we show that the variety
\mathbb{CMG}_{\sim}
is the equivalent algebraic semantics of the one-variable monadic fragment of the first-order logic
{\cal G}\forall_{\sim}
. Condition
(C)
arises from the fact that
(\exists x)(\varphi\wedge\mathop{\sim}\varphi)\to(\forall x)(\varphi\vee\mathop{\sim}\varphi)
is a theorem of
{\cal G}\forall_{\sim}
. To show this, take a formula
\varphi
in
{\cal G}\forall_{\sim}
, a variable
w
such that
\varphi
does not contain
w
and
\psi:=\varphi[w/x]
(the formula that results from substituting all the free occurrences of
x
with
w
). Since
{\cal G}_{\sim}
proves
(\psi\wedge\mathop{\sim}\psi)\to(\varphi\vee\mathop{\sim}\varphi)
(easy consequence of
[
6
, Theorem 7]
) we have that
{\cal G}\forall_{\sim}\vdash(\psi\wedge\mathop{\sim}\psi)\to(\varphi\vee\mathop{\sim}\varphi)
. Using generalization we obtain
{\cal G}\forall_{\sim}\vdash(\forall x)((\psi\wedge\mathop{\sim}\psi)\to(\varphi\vee\mathop{\sim}\varphi))
and from the fact that
\psi
does not contain
x
freely and
(\forall 2)
we have that
{\cal G}\forall_{\sim}\vdash(\psi\wedge\mathop{\sim}\psi)\to(\forall x)(\varphi\vee\mathop{\sim}\varphi)
. Now, generalization, the fact that
\varphi
does not contain
w
and
(\exists 2)
yield
{\cal G}\forall_{\sim}\vdash(\exists w)(\psi\wedge\mathop{\sim}\psi)\to(\forall x)(\varphi\vee\mathop{\sim}\varphi)
. Finally,
{\cal G}\forall_{\sim}\vdash(\exists x)(\varphi\wedge\mathop{\sim}\varphi)\to(\forall x)(\varphi\vee\mathop{\sim}\varphi)
follows from
[
7
, Theorem 5.1.17.]
and Modus Ponens.
It is worth noting that even when
x
is the only variable in
\varphi
, the proof of
(\exists x)(\varphi\wedge\mathop{\sim}\varphi)\to(\forall x)(\varphi\vee\mathop{\sim}\varphi)
requires an auxiliary variable different from
x
. Moreover, if a proof including only
x
existed,
(C)
should hold on the whole variety
\mathbb{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
.

Next, we characterize the finite subdirectly irreducible algebras in
\mathbb{CMG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
by means of the range of their monadic operators. We start by showing some basic results. Here, and subsequently,
\langle{\bf C}_{n},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
denotes the
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-chain with
n
elements.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
be a
CMG_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra and
a\in A
. Then,
a\leq\mathop{\sim}a
if and only if
a\leq\exists a\leq\mathop{\sim}a
.

Proof.

Let
a\in A
such that
a\leq\mathop{\sim}a
. Using conditions (

C

),
(Q)
and Lemma
1.5
(2) we obtain the following equivalence:

\exists(a\land\mathop{\sim}a)\leq\forall(a\vee\mathop{\sim}a)\mbox{ if and only if }a\leq\exists a\leq\forall\mathop{\sim}a\leq\mathop{\sim}a.

\quad\hfill\blacksquare

An element
d
in an
MG_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra is called a
fixed point
if it satisfies
d=\mathop{\sim}d
.

Corollary 4.2
.

If
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
is a
CMG_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra with a fixed point
d
, then
\exists d=d
.

Remark
2.6
states that, if
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
is a finite subdirectly irreducible algebra in
\mathbb{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
, then
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle\cong\prod_{i=1}^{r}\langle{\bf C}_{n_{i}},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
, for some natural numbers
n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}
. If
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
belongs to
\mathbb{CMG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
, the following stronger result holds.

Proposition 4.3
.

If
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
is a finite subdirectly irreducible
CMG_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra such that
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\langle{\bf C}_{n_{i}},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
, then
n_{i}
is odd for all
i=1,\ldots,r
or
n_{i}
is even for all
i=1,\ldots,r
.

Proof.

Assume
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
as in the statement of the lemma, and suppose that there exists a direct factor of
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
with an even number of elements and a direct factor with an odd number of elements.

Observe that if
n_{i}
is even, there exists an element
a_{i}
in
C_{n_{i}}
such that
\mathop{\sim}a_{i}
covers
a_{i}
, that is, if
a_{i}\leq c\leq\mathop{\sim}a_{i}
, then
c=a_{i}
or
c=\mathop{\sim}a_{i}
. If
n_{i}
is odd, there exists an element
d\in C_{n_{i}}
such that
d_{i}=\mathop{\sim}d_{i}
.

Taking this into account, consider the element
t\in A
such that

t(i):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}a_{i}&\mbox{ if }&n_{i}\mbox{ is even, }\\
d_{i}&\mbox{ if }&n_{i}\mbox{ is odd,}\end{array}\right.

where
a_{i}
and
d_{i}
are the elements described above.
We claim that
\exists t=t
. In fact, since by construction
t\leq\mathop{\sim}t
, by Proposition
4.1
we have that
t\leq\exists t\leq\mathop{\sim}t
. If
n_{i}
is odd,
t(i)=d_{i}=\mathop{\sim}d_{i}=\mathop{\sim}t(i)
and so
t(i)=(\exists t)(i)
. Suppose that
n_{i}
is even, from the fact that
t\leq\exists t\leq\mathop{\sim}t
,
(C)
and
(Q)
we obtain

a_{i}=t(i)\leq(\exists t)(i)=(\exists(t\wedge\mathop{\sim}t))(i)\leq\forall(t\vee\mathop{\sim}t))(i)=(\mathop{\sim}\exists t)(i)\leq(\mathop{\sim}t)(i)=\mathop{\sim}a_{i}.

Since
\langle{\bf C}_{n_{i}},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
has no fixed points it follows that
(\exists t)(i)<\mathop{\sim}(\exists t)(i)
and so
a_{i}\leq(\exists t)(i)<\mathop{\sim}(\exists t)(i)\leq\mathop{\sim}a_{i}
. Finally, since
\mathop{\sim}a_{i}
covers
a_{i}
, we have that
t(i)=(\exists t)(i)
. Both cases show that
t(i)=(\exists t)(i)
, for all
i=1,\ldots,r
and consequently,
\exists t=t
. Hence,
t\in\exists A
.

Consider now the elements
\mathop{\sim}t\to t
and
\mathop{\sim}(\mathop{\sim}t\to t)
in
\exists A
. Note that

(\mathop{\sim}t\to t)(i)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}a_{i}&\mbox{ if }n_{i}\mbox{ is even,}\\
1&\mbox{ if }n_{i}\mbox{ is odd,}\end{array}\right.\mbox{ \ and \ }(\mathop{\sim}(\mathop{\sim}t\to t))(i)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathop{\sim}a_{i}&\mbox{ if }n_{i}\mbox{ is even,}\\
0&\mbox{ if }n_{i}\mbox{ is odd.}\end{array}\right.

Clearly,
\mathop{\sim}t\to t
and
\mathop{\sim}(\mathop{\sim}t\to t)
are non-comparable elements in
\langle\exists{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
contradicting the fact that
\langle\exists{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is totally ordered.

\quad\hfill\blacksquare

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
be a finite subdirectly irreducible algebra in
\mathbb{CMG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
such that
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\langle{\bf C}_{n_{i}},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
.

(1)

If
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
has a fixed point, then
n_{i}
is odd, for all
i=1,\ldots,r
.

(2)

If
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
has no fixed points, then
n_{i}
is even, for all
i=1,\ldots,r
.

The following result characterizes the finite subdirectly irreducible algebras
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
in the variety
\mathbb{CMG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
by means of
\exists A
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
be a finite subdirectly irreducible algebra in
\mathbb{MG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
such that
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\langle{\bf C}_{n_{i}},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
.

(1)

If
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
has a fixed point
d
, then

\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle\in\mathbb{CMG}_{\sim}
if and only if
d\in\exists A.

(2)

If
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
has no fixed points, then
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle\in\mathbb{CMG}_{\sim}
if and only if there exists
t\in\exists A
such that
(\mathop{\sim}t)(i)
covers
t(i)
, for all
i=1,\ldots r
.

Proof.

Assume
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
as in the statement of the theorem. The forward implication in
(1)
follows immediately from Corollary
4.2
.

Conversely, suppose that
d\in\exists A
and let
x\in A
. Since
d
is a fixed point of
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
,
d(i)
is a fixed point of
\langle{\bf C}_{n_{i}},\mathop{\sim}\rangle

satisfies (

C

).

In order to prove

\quad\hfill\blacksquare

5
Functional monadic
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebras

In this section we consider an important example of
CMG_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebras. Our interest in these algebras arises from the fact that they encode the information of the models used to interpret the one-variable monadic fragment of the first-order logic
{\cal G}\forall_{\mathop{\sim}}
. We call them
functional monadic
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebras
and we prove that they generate the variety
\mathbb{CMG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
.

We define functional monadic
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebras in the same way that functional monadic
BL
-algebras are defined in
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
. Let
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
be a totally ordered
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra and let
X
be a non-empty set. Consider the
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra
\langle{\bf A}^{X},\mathop{\sim}\rangle:=\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle^{X}
and let
S
be the set of functions
f\in A^{X}
such that
\mbox{inf}\{f(x):x\in X\}
and
\mbox{sup}\{f(x):x\in X\}
both exist in
{\bf A}
. For every
f\in S
, we define:
(\forall_{\wedge}f)(x):=\mbox{inf}\{f(y):y\in X\}
and
(\exists_{\vee}f)(x):=\mbox{sup}\{f(y):y\in X\}
. Note that
\forall_{\wedge}f
and
\exists_{\vee}f
are constant maps. We write
C
for the set of constant maps of
A^{X}
. Let
\langle{\bf B},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
be a subalgebra of
\langle{\bf A}^{X},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
such that
B\subseteq S
and
B
is closed under
\exists_{\vee}
and
\forall_{\wedge}
. In
[
3
]
it was shown that the structure
\langle{\bf B},\exists_{\vee},\forall_{\wedge}\rangle
is a monadic
BL
-algebra,
C\cap B
is the universe of an
m
-relatively complete subalgebra of
{\bf B}
and
C\cap B=\exists_{\vee}B=\forall_{\wedge}B
. We claim that
\langle{\bf B},\mathop{\sim},\exists_{\vee},\forall_{\wedge}\rangle
is an
MG_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra. Indeed, it is clear that
\langle{\bf C}\cap{\bf B},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is a
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-subalgebra of
{\bf B}
and so, it is an
m
-relatively complete subalgebra of
\langle{\bf B},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
. Thus, by Theorem
3.1
,
\langle{\bf B},\mathop{\sim},\exists_{\vee},\forall_{\wedge}\rangle
is an
MG_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra. We call the algebras
\langle{\bf B},\mathop{\sim},\exists_{\vee},\forall_{\wedge}\rangle
that arise in this way

G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebras.
The main example of these algebras is obtained considering
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
to be the standard
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra
\langle[0,1]_{\mathbb{G}},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
and

.

Proposition 5.1
.

Every functional monadic
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra satisfies
(

C

)
.

Proof.

Let
\langle{\bf B},\mathop{\sim},\exists_{\vee},\forall_{\wedge}\rangle
be a functional monadic
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra whit
\langle{\bf B},\mathop{\sim}\rangle\leq\langle{\bf A}^{X},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
for a suitable totally ordered
G_{\sim}
-algebra
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
and a non-empty set
X
. Given
f\in B
, since by Lemma
1.3
(1)
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
satisfies the Kleene condition, we have that
f(x)\wedge\mathop{\sim}f(x)\leq f(y)\vee\mathop{\sim}f(y)
, for all
x,y\in X
.
Therefore, we have that

\exists_{\wedge}(f\wedge\mathop{\sim}f)=\mbox{sup}\{f(x)\wedge\mathop{\sim}f(x),x\in X\}\leq\mbox{inf}\{f(y)\vee\mathop{\sim}f(y),y\in X\}=\forall_{\wedge}(f\vee\mathop{\sim}f).

\quad\hfill\blacksquare

Next we show that all finite subdirectly irreducible
CMG_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebras with a fixed point are isomorphic to a functional monadic
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra. We first prove this result for all finite totally ordered

Lemma 5.2
.

Every finite totally ordered
{CMG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra with a fixed point is isomorphic to a functional monadic
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra.

Proof.

Let
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle

-chain with an odd number of elements and
\exists A:=\{c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{k},\mathop{\sim}c_{k-1},\ldots,\mathop{\sim}c_{0}\}

.

Put
A_{i}:=\{x\in A:c_{i-1}<x<c_{i}\}
and
\mathop{\sim}A_{i}:=\{x\in A:\mathop{\sim}c_{i}<x<\mathop{\sim}c_{i-1}\}
for
1\leq i\leq k
, we have that
\exists A_{i}=\{c_{i}\}
,
\forall A_{i}=\{c_{i-1}\}
,
\exists(\mathop{\sim}A_{i})=\{\mathop{\sim}c_{i-1}\}
and
\forall(\mathop{\sim}A_{i})=\{\mathop{\sim}c_{i}\}
whenever
A_{i}\neq\emptyset
.

Consider the functional monadic
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra
\langle[0,1]_{\mathbb{G}}^{\mathbb{N}},\mathop{\sim},\exists_{\vee},\forall_{\wedge}\rangle

\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
is embeddable in
\langle[0,1]_{\mathbb{G}}^{\mathbb{N}},\mathop{\sim},\exists_{\vee},\forall_{\wedge}\rangle
.
In order to show that, take the constant sequences
g_{i}
in
[0,1]_{\mathbb{G}}^{\mathbb{N}}
given by

g_{i}(n):=\frac{i}{2k},

for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq i\leq k
.

Note that
g_{k}
is the fixed point in
\langle[0,1]_{\mathbb{G}}^{\mathbb{N}},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
and
0\equiv g_{0}<g_{1}<\ldots<g_{k}\equiv\frac{1}{2}

1\leq i\leq k
and

f_{i}^{(j)}\in[0,1]_{\mathbb{G}}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that

f_{i}^{(j)}(n):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{i}{2k}-\frac{1}{2k}\left(1-\frac{1}{n+1}\right)^{j}&\mbox{ if }n\mbox{ es even,}\\
\frac{i}{2k}-\frac{1}{2k}\left(\frac{1}{n+1}\right)^{j}&\mbox{ if }n\mbox{ is odd.}\\
\end{array}\right.

Observe that

0\equiv g_{0}<f_{i}^{(j)}<g_{k}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\mbox{ \ \ \ and \ \ \ }g_{i-1}<f_{i}^{(1)}<f_{i}^{(2)}<\cdots<f_{i}^{(j)}<f_{i}^{(j+1)}<\cdots<g_{i}.

Thus,

\frac{1}{2}<\mathop{\sim}f_{i}^{(j)}<1\mbox{ \ \ \ and \ \ \ }\mathop{\sim}g_{i}<\cdots<\mathop{\sim}f_{i}^{(j+1)}<\mathop{\sim}f_{i}^{(j)}<\cdots<\mathop{\sim}f_{i}^{(2)}<\mathop{\sim}f_{i}^{(1)}<\mathop{\sim}g_{i-1}.

Moreover, an easy computation shows that
\forall_{\wedge}f_{i}^{(j)}=g_{i-1}
,
\forall_{\wedge}\mathop{\sim}f_{i}^{(j)}=\mathop{\sim}g_{i}=1-g_{i}
,
\exists_{\vee}f_{i}^{(j)}=g_{i}
and
\exists_{\vee}\mathop{\sim}f_{i}^{(j)}=\mathop{\sim}g_{i-1}=1-g_{i-1}.

Finally, let
A_{i}:=\{a_{i}^{(1)},a_{i}^{(2)},\ldots,a_{i}^{(r_{i})}\}
and

\mathop{\sim}A_{i}:=\{\mathop{\sim}a_{i}^{(r_{i})},\ldots,\mathop{\sim}a_{i}^{(1)}\}
for
1\leq i\leq k
where
a_{i}^{(1)}<a_{i}^{(2)}<\ldots<a_{i}^{(r_{i})}
and
\mathop{\sim}a_{i}^{(r_{i})}<\mathop{\sim}a_{i}^{(r_{i}-1)}<\ldots<\mathop{\sim}a_{i}^{(1)}
and consider the map
\varphi:A\to[0,1]_{{\cal\mathbb{G}}}^{\mathbb{N}}
given by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\varphi(c_{i}):=g_{i}&\mbox{ for }0\leq i\leq k,\\
\varphi(\mathop{\sim}c_{i}):=\mathop{\sim}g_{i}&\mbox{ for }0\leq i\leq k,\\
\varphi(a_{i}^{(j)}):=f_{i}^{(j)}&\mbox{ for }1\leq i\leq k\mbox{ and }1\leq j\leq r_{i},\\
\varphi(\mathop{\sim}a_{i}^{(j)}):=\mathop{\sim}f_{i}^{(j)}&\mbox{ for }1\leq i\leq k\mbox{ and }1\leq j\leq r_{i}.\\
\end{array}\right.

It follows immediately that
\varphi
is an embedding.

\quad\hfill\blacksquare

In
[
4
]
the authors built a monadic Gödel algebra
\langle{\bf A}^{n},\overline{\exists},\overline{\forall}\rangle
by means of a monadic Gödel chain
\langle{\bf A},\exists,\forall\rangle
and a natural number
n
where the quantifiers are defined as follows:

\overline{\exists}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})(i):=\exists(a_{1}\vee\ldots\vee a_{n})\mbox{ \ \ \ and \ \ \ }\overline{\forall}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})(i):=\forall(a_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge a_{n}),

for
1\leq i\leq n
. Here,
\overline{\exists}(A^{n})=\overline{\forall}(A^{n})=\{(c,\ldots,c):c\in\exists A\}
is the universe of an
m
-relatively complete subalgebra of
{\bf A}^{n}
.

If we now consider a finite
CMG_{\mathop{\sim}}
-chain
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
with a fixed point, we have that
\langle{\bf A}^{n},\mathop{\sim}\rangle=\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle^{n}
is a
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra. A straightforward verification shows that
\langle\overline{\exists}{\bf A}^{n},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is an
m
-relatively complete subalgebra of
\langle{\bf A}^{n},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
that contains the fixed point of
{\bf A}^{n}
. Thus, we obtain that
\langle{\bf A}^{n},\mathop{\sim},\overline{\exists},\overline{\forall}\rangle
is an algebra in the variety
\mathbb{CMG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

For each finite
CMG_{\mathop{\sim}}
-chain
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
with a fixed point and every natural number
n
, the algebra
\langle{\bf A}^{n},\mathop{\sim},\overline{\exists},\overline{\forall}\rangle
is isomorphic to a functional monadic
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra.

Proof.

Let
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
be a finite totally ordered
CMG_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra with a fixed point. By the previous lemma there exists an embedding
\varphi
from
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
to
\langle{\bf B}^{X},\mathop{\sim},\exists_{\vee},\forall_{\wedge}\rangle
where
\langle{\bf B},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is a
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-chain. Thus,
\varphi(\exists a)=\exists_{\vee}\varphi(a)
,
\varphi(\forall a)=\forall_{\wedge}\varphi(a)
and
\varphi(\mathop{\sim}a)=\mathop{\sim}\varphi(a)
, for all
a\in A
.
It was proved in
[
4
]
that
\varphi
induces an embedding
\psi:{\bf A}^{n}\to{\bf B}^{X\times\{1,\ldots,n\}}
given by

\psi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})(x,i):=\varphi(a_{i})(x)

such that
\psi(\overline{\exists}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))=\exists_{\vee}\psi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
and
\psi(\overline{\forall}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))=\forall_{\wedge}\psi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
. We claim that
\psi
also satisfies
\psi(\mathop{\sim}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))=\mathop{\sim}\psi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\mathop{\sim}\psi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})(x,i)

\displaystyle=\mathop{\sim}\varphi(a_{i})(x)

\displaystyle=\varphi(\mathop{\sim}a_{i})(x)

\displaystyle=\psi(\mathop{\sim}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))(x,i).

Therefore,
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\overline{\exists},\overline{\forall}\rangle
is isomorphic to a functional monadic
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra.

\quad\hfill\blacksquare

The following basic property of finite subdirectly irreducible
CMG_{\sim}
-algebra is the last ingredient we need to prove the main theorem of this section.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
be a finite subdirectly irreducible
CMG_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra with a fixed point where
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\langle{\bf A}_{i},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
. Then,
\pi_{i}|_{\exists{\bf A}}
is injective, where
\pi_{i}:{\bf A}\to{\bf A}_{i}
are the projection maps, for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
. Thus,
|\exists{\bf A}|=|\pi_{i}({\exists{\bf A}})|
.

Proof.

Suppose that there exist
a,b\in\exists A
such that
a\neq b
and
a(i)=b(i)
for some
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,r\}
. Observe that
a(i)=b(i)\neq 1
. Since otherwise, by Lemma
4.6
(1) we have that
a=b=1
which is a contradiction. Since
\exists{\bf A}
is totally ordered we can suppose, without loss of generality, that
a\leq b
and from
a\neq b
we have that
a(j)<b(j)
for some
j\in\{1,\ldots r\}
. We claim that the elements
b\to a
and
b
are no comparable. Indeed, we have that
b(i)<(b\to a)(i)=1
and
(b\to a)(j)=a(j)<b(j)
. This completes the proof.

\quad\hfill\blacksquare

Theorem 5.5
.

Every finite subdirectly irreducible
{CMG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra with a fixed point is isomorphic to a monadic functional
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra.

Proof.

Let
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
be a finite subdirectly irreducible
CMG_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra with a fixed point. By Proposition
4.3
, we can assume there are finite
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-chains
{\bf A}_{i}
with a fixed point such that
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\langle{\bf A}_{i},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
. By the previous lemma, we can assume without loss of generality that
C:=\pi_{i}(\exists A)=\{c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{k-1},c_{k},\mathop{\sim}c_{k-1},\ldots,\mathop{\sim}c_{0}\}
, for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
, where

0=c_{0}<c_{1}<\ldots<c_{k}=\mathop{\sim}c_{k}<\mathop{\sim}c_{k-1}<\ldots<\mathop{\sim}c_{0}=1.

We further assume
A_{i}\cap A_{j}=C
, for
i\neq j
. Note that
\exists A=\{(x,x,\ldots,x)\in A:x\in C\}
.

Put
A_{ij}:=\{x\in A_{i}:c_{j-1}<x<c_{j}\}
and
\mathop{\sim}A_{ij}:=\{x\in A_{i}:\mathop{\sim}c_{j}<x<\mathop{\sim}c_{j-1}\}
for
1\leq i\leq r
,
1\leq j\leq k
. We can write the underlying structure of distributive lattice of
{\bf A}_{i}
in terms of lattice ordinal sum as follows:

\{c_{0}\}\oplus A_{i1}\oplus\{c_{1}\}\oplus\ldots\oplus A_{ik}\oplus\{c_{k}\}\oplus\mathop{\sim}A_{ik}\oplus\ldots\oplus\{\mathop{\sim}c_{1}\}\oplus\mathop{\sim}A_{i1}\oplus\{\mathop{\sim}c_{0}\}.

Let
B:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}A_{i}
and consider the Gödel algebra
{\bf B}
with universe
B
given by the following ordinal sum:

\{c_{0}\}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}A_{i1}\oplus\{c_{1}\}\oplus\ldots\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}A_{ik}\oplus\{c_{k}\}

\oplus\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r-1}\mathop{\sim}A_{(r-i)k}\oplus\ldots\oplus\{\mathop{\sim}c_{1}\}\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r-1}\mathop{\sim}A_{(r-i)1}\oplus\{\mathop{\sim}c_{0}\}.

Let
\sim
be the natural involutive anti-isomorphism such that
\langle{\bf B},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is a
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra. In addition, for
i=1,\ldots r
, we have that
\langle{\bf A}_{i},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is a
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-subalgebra of
\langle{\bf B},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
.

It is easy to check that
C
is the universe of an
m
-relatively complete subalgebra of
\langle{\bf B},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
. Hence, by Theorems
3.1
and
4.5
we can define quantifiers in
{\bf B}
given by
\exists x:=\min\{c\in C:x\leq c\}
and
\forall x:=\max\{c\in C:c\leq x\}
such that
\langle{\bf B},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
is a
CMG_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra.
Therefore, we can apply the construction described before Lemma
5.3
and obtain the
CMG_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra
\langle{\bf B}^{r},\mathop{\sim},\overline{\exists},\overline{\forall}\rangle
.

Since
\langle{\bf A}_{i},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is a subalgebra of
\langle{\bf B},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
, we obtain that
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim}\rangle=\langle\prod_{i=1}^{r}{\bf A}_{i},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
is a subalgebra of
\langle{\bf B}^{r},\mathop{\sim}\rangle
. We claim that
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
is a subalgebra of
\langle{\bf B}^{r},\mathop{\sim},\overline{\exists},\overline{\forall}\rangle
. Indeed, for
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\in A
we have

\displaystyle(\exists(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}))(i)

\displaystyle=\min\{c\in C:a_{j}\leq c,\mbox{ for all }j=1,\ldots,r\}

\displaystyle=\min\{c\in C:a_{1}\vee\ldots\vee a_{r}\leq c\}

\displaystyle=(\overline{\exists}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}))(i).

In the same way we obtain that
\forall a=\overline{\forall}a
, for all
a\in A
. Finally, since by Lemma
5.3
we have that
\langle{\bf B}^{r},\mathop{\sim},\overline{\exists},\overline{\forall}\rangle
is isomorphic to a functional monadic
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra, so is
\langle{\bf A},\mathop{\sim},\exists,\forall\rangle
.

\quad\hfill\blacksquare

This theorem and Corollary
4.11
yield the following result.

Corollary 5.6
.

\mathbb{CMG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
is generated as a quasivariety by its finite functional monadic
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebras with a fixed point.

Taking into account the construction given in the proof of Lemma
5.2
we obtain the following stronger result.

Corollary 5.7
.

\mathbb{CMG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
is generated as a quasivariety by the functional monadic
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra
\langle[0,1]_{\mathbb{G}}^{\mathbb{N}},\mathop{\sim},\exists_{\vee},\forall_{\wedge}\rangle
.

6
A completeness result

In this section we show that the class
\mathbb{CMG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
is the equivalent algebraic semantics of the one-variable monadic fragment of the predicate
logic
{\cal G}\forall_{\mathop{\sim}}
.
To prove this, we introduce the logics
{\cal S}5({\mathbb{G}}_{\mathop{\sim}})
, defined semantically, and the Hilbert-style syntactic calculus

{\cal S}5({\cal G}_{\mathop{\sim}})
.
We show that
{\cal S}5({\cal G}_{\mathop{\sim}})
is complete with respect to the class
\mathbb{CMG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
and
{\cal S}5({\mathbb{G}}_{\mathop{\sim}})
is equivalent to the one-variable monadic fragment of the first-order logic
{\cal G}\forall_{\mathop{\sim}}
. Finally, using a completeness result via functional algebras analogous to the one given in
[
4
]
we obtain that the equivalent algebraic semantics of the one-variable monadic fragment of the predicate logic
{\cal G}\forall_{\mathop{\sim}}
is the variety
\mathbb{CMG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
.

Next, we define the logic
{\cal S}5({\mathbb{G}}_{\mathop{\sim}})
in the same way that P. Hájek defined the logic
{\cal S}5(\mathbb{BL})
in
[
7
]
. The language of this logic is the language of the logic
{\cal G}_{\mathop{\sim}}
augmented with the unary connectives
\square
and
\lozenge
. The formulas in
{\cal S}5({\mathbb{G}}_{\mathop{\sim}})
are interpreted in a structure given by a triple
{\bf K}=\langle W,e,{\bf L}\rangle
where
W
is a non-empty set,
{\bf L}
is a totally ordered
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebra and
e:W\times Prop\to L
is a function defined on pairs
(w,p)
consisting of an element
w\in W
and a propositional variable
p
. The truth degree
||\varphi||_{{\bf K},w}
of a formula
\varphi
in
{\bf K}
at
w
is defined by setting
||\varphi||_{{\bf K},w}=e(w,\varphi)
if
\varphi\in Prop
and extending its definition for any formula
\varphi
as follows:

•

||0||_{{\bf K},w}:=0^{\bf L}
,
||1||_{{\bf K},w}:=1^{\bf L}
,

•

||\varphi\wedge\psi||_{{\bf K},w}:=||\varphi||_{{\bf K},w}\wedge^{\bf L}||\psi||_{{\bf K},w}
, and the same for
\vee
and
\to
,

•

||\mathop{\sim}\varphi||_{{\bf K},w}:=\mathop{\sim}^{\bf L}||\varphi||_{{\bf K},w},

•

||\square\varphi||_{{\bf K},w}:=\inf\{||\varphi||_{{\bf K},v}:v\in W\}
,

•

||\lozenge\varphi||_{{\bf K},w}:=\sup\{||\varphi||_{{\bf K},v}:v\in W\}
.

Observe that
||\square\varphi||_{{\bf K},w}
and
||\lozenge\varphi||_{{\bf K},w}
may be undefined. Hence, we only consider
safe
structures, that is, structures
{\bf K}
where
||\square\varphi||_{{\bf K},w}
and
||\lozenge\varphi||_{{\bf K},w}
are defined for any formula
\varphi
.

We now define the consequence relation
\models_{{\cal S}5({\mathbb{G}}_{\mathop{\sim}})}
. Given a set of formulas
\Gamma
, a
model
of
\Gamma
is a safe structure
{\bf K}=\langle W,e,{\bf L}\rangle
such that
||\varphi||_{{\bf K},w}=1
for every
\varphi\in\Gamma
and every
w\in W
. Thus, given a set of formulas
\Gamma
we define

\Gamma\models_{{\cal S}5({\mathbb{G}}_{\mathop{\sim}})}\varphi\mbox{ if and only if }||\varphi||_{{\bf K},w}=1\mbox{ for every model }{\bf K}=\langle W,e,{\bf L}\rangle\mbox{ of }\Gamma\ \mbox{ and every }w\in W.

In
[
7
]
the author proved that the modal logic
{\cal S}5(\mathbb{BL})
is equivalent to the monadic fuzzy predicate calculus
m{\cal BL}\forall
with unary predicates and just one object variable
x
. The atomic formula
P_{i}(x)
corresponds to the propositional variable
p_{i}
, modalities
\square
and
\lozenge
correspond to the quantifiers
\forall x
and
\exists x
, respectively, and the triples
{\bf K}=\langle W,e,{\bf L}\rangle
correspond to models for the
m{\cal BL}\forall
.
In the same way we can obtain that
{\cal S}5(\mathbb{G}_{\mathop{\sim}})
is actually equivalent to the one-variable monadic fragment of the predicate logic
{\cal G}\forall_{\mathop{\sim}}
because there is a natural correspondence between formulas of both logics, between corresponding models and between corresponding truth degrees.

We now introduce the Hilbert-style syntactic calculus
{\cal S}5({\cal G}_{\mathop{\sim}})
as an expansion of
{\cal S}5^{\prime}(BL)
given in
[
3
]
enriched with a unary connective
\mathop{\sim}
. The axioms of
{\cal S}5({\cal G}_{\mathop{\sim}})
are the axioms in
{\cal S}5^{\prime}(BL)
plus the following axiom schemata:

(G)

\varphi\to\varphi\&\varphi

(\mathop{\sim}1)

\mathop{\sim}(\mathop{\sim}\varphi)\equiv\varphi

(\mathop{\sim}2)

\neg\varphi\to\mathop{\sim}\varphi

(\mathop{\sim}3)

\Delta(\varphi\to\psi)\to\Delta(\mathop{\sim}\psi\to\mathop{\sim}\varphi)

(\Delta 1)

\Delta\varphi\lor\neg\Delta\varphi

(\Delta 2)

\Delta(\varphi\lor\psi)\to(\Delta\varphi\lor\Delta\psi)

(\Delta 5
)

\Delta(\varphi\to\psi)\to(\Delta\varphi\to\Delta\psi)

(\lozenge 1)

\square\varphi\ \equiv\ \mathop{\sim}\lozenge\mathop{\sim}\varphi

(\lozenge 2)

\lozenge(\varphi\wedge\mathop{\sim}\varphi)\to\ \square(\varphi\vee\mathop{\sim}\varphi)

where
\neg\varphi
is
\varphi\to\overline{0}
and
\Delta\varphi
is
\neg\mathop{\sim}\varphi
, and the inference rules of
{\cal S}5({\cal G}_{\mathop{\sim}})
are those of
{\cal S}5^{\prime}(BL)
(modus ponens and generalization) and necessitation for
\Delta
(
\displaystyle\frac{\varphi}{\Delta\varphi}
). Recall that
(G)
implies that
\varphi\&\psi
is equivalent to
\varphi\wedge\psi
.

Proposition 6.1
.

{\cal S}5({\cal G}_{\mathop{\sim}})
is an implicative logic.

Proof.

Since
{\cal S}5^{\prime}(BL)
is an implicative logic (see
[
3
, Lemma 4.7]
) it only remains to prove that the condition
\varphi\to\psi,\psi\to\varphi\vdash\mathop{\sim}\psi\to\mathop{\sim}\varphi
holds in
{\cal S}5({\cal G}_{\mathop{\sim}})
. In fact, by necessitation,
\varphi\to\psi\vdash\Delta(\varphi\to\psi)
and using
(\mathop{\sim}3)
we obtain
\varphi\to\psi\vdash\Delta(\mathop{\sim}\psi\to\mathop{\sim}\varphi)
. Finally, using the definition of
\Delta
and
(\mathop{\sim}2)
we have that
\varphi\to\psi\vdash\mathop{\sim}(\mathop{\sim}(\mathop{\sim}\psi\to\mathop{\sim}\varphi))
and so,
\varphi\to\psi\vdash\mathop{\sim}\psi\to\mathop{\sim}\varphi
.

\quad\hfill\blacksquare

Recall that given an implicative logic
{\cal L}
in the language
L
it is possible to define the class
Alg^{\ast}{\cal L}
of all algebras
{\bf A}
of type
L
with an element
1\in A
that satisfy:

(Alg1)

For all
\Gamma\subseteq Fm(L)
and
h
homomorphism from
Fm(L)
to
{\bf A}
, if
\Gamma\vdash_{\cal L}\varphi
and
h[\Gamma]\subseteq\{1\}
then
h(\varphi)=1
.

(Alg2)

For all
a,b\in A
, if
a\to b=1
and
b\to a=1
, then
a=b
.

Moreover, it is well known that
Alg^{\ast}{\cal L}
is the equivalent algebraic semantics of the logic
{\cal L}
. Hence, we have that

\displaystyle\Gamma\vdash_{{\cal L}}\varphi\mbox{ if and only if }\Gamma\models_{Alg^{\ast}{\cal L}}\varphi,

(2)

where
\Gamma\models_{Alg^{\ast}{\cal L}}\varphi
means that
h(\varphi)=1
, for every homomorphism
h
from
Fm(L)
to
{\bf A}
such that
h[\Gamma]\subseteq\{1\}
.

The following result characterizes the class
Alg^{\ast}{\cal S}5({\cal G}_{\mathop{\sim}})
.

Proposition 6.2
.

The equivalent algebraic semantics of
{\cal S}5({\cal G}_{\mathop{\sim}})
is the variety
\mathbb{CMG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
.

Proof.

In
[
6
]
, F. Esteva, L. Godo, P. Hájek and M. Navara proved that
Alg^{\ast}{\cal G}_{\mathop{\sim}}
is the class of Gödel algebras that satisfy the equations of the form
\varphi\approx 1
where
\varphi
ranges over the axiom schemata
(\mathop{\sim}1)
,
(\mathop{\sim}2)
,
(\mathop{\sim}3)
,
(\Delta 1)
,
(\Delta 2)
and
(\Delta 5)
. Note that, by Lemma
1.2
and
[
6
, Lemma 4]
, these algebras are precisely the
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebras defined in this article.

On the other hand, D. Castaño et al. proved that
Alg^{\ast}{\cal S}5^{\prime}(BL)
is the class of monadic
BL
-algebras. Taking these results into account, and the fact that the axioms
(\mathop{\sim}2)
,
(\lozenge 1)
and
(\lozenge 2)
correspond to the conditions
(N)
,
(Q)
and
(C)
, the proof of this result is immediate.

\quad\hfill\blacksquare

Now, using (
2
) for the case
{\cal L}={\cal S}5({\cal G}_{\mathop{\sim}})
we have the following completeness result.

Theorem 6.3
.

For every formula
\varphi
and set of formulas
\Gamma
in
{\cal S}5({\cal G}_{\mathop{\sim}})
:

\Gamma\vdash_{{\cal S}5({\cal G}_{\mathop{\sim}})}\varphi\mbox{ \ \ if and only if \ \ }\Gamma\models_{\mathbb{CMG}_{\mathop{\sim}}}\varphi,

We next prove a result connecting completeness with functional algebras similar to the one given in
[
4
]
.

Theorem 6.4
.

The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

For any formula
\varphi
and any set of formulas
\Gamma
, we have

\Gamma\vdash_{{\cal S}5({\cal G}_{\mathop{\sim}})}\varphi\mbox{ \ \ if and only if \ \ }\Gamma\models_{{\cal S}5({\mathbb{G}}_{\mathop{\sim}})}\varphi

(2)

The variety
\mathbb{CMG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
is generated, as a quasivariety, by its functional monadic
G_{\mathop{\sim}}
-algebras.

Proof.

The proof runs in a similar way to the corresponding proof in
[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
taking into account that
{\cal S}5({\mathbb{G}}_{\mathop{\sim}})
is finitary because it is equivalent to the one-variable monadic fragment of the predicate calculus
{\cal G}\forall_{\mathop{\sim}}
and this latter is finitary, and
\mathbb{CMG}_{\mathop{\sim}}
is the equivalent algebraic semantics of the logic
{\cal S}5({\cal G}_{\mathop{\sim}})
(Proposition
6.2
).

\quad\hfill\blacksquare

Finally, since
(2)
in Theorem
6.4
holds (see Corollary
5.6
) we have the following completeness result for
{\cal S}5({\cal G}_{\mathop{\sim}})
.

Theorem 6.5

(Completeness)
.

For any formula
\varphi
and any set of formulas
\Gamma
, it follows that

\Gamma\vdash_{{\cal S}5({\cal G}_{\mathop{\sim}})}\varphi\mbox{ \ \ if and only if \ \ }\Gamma\models_{{\cal S}5({\mathbb{G}}_{\mathop{\sim}})}\varphi\mbox{ \ \ if and only if \ \ }\Gamma\models_{\mathbb{CMG}_{\mathop{\sim}}}\varphi.

Thus, we have characterized the equivalent algebraic semantics for the one-variable monadic fragment of the first-order logic
{\cal G}\forall_{\sim}
as the variety
\mathbb{CMG}_{\sim}
. As a consequence, there exists a correspondence between the axiomatic extensions of this calculus and the subvarieties of
\mathbb{CMG}_{\sim}
. For this reason, it would be important to describe all subvarieties of this variety. However, considering the complexity of this problem, we believe that a way to proceed with our work would be to intend to resolve this problem for the subvariety of
\mathbb{CMG}_{\sim}
generated by totally ordered algebras.

To carry this out, we believe it would be very useful to use the results obtained in
[
5
]
from the variety
\mathbb{MG}
and to obtain a suitable topological duality from
\mathbb{CMG}_{\sim}
that allows us to deepen the study of this variety.

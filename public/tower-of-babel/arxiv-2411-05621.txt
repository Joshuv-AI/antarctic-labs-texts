Second-order hyperfine correction to H, D, and 3 He energy levels
Krzysztof Pachucki, Vojtěch Patkóš, Vladimir A. Yerokhin

Abstract
The complete second-order hyperfine-interaction correction is calculated for centroid energy levels of H, D, and 3 He atoms. For 3 He, the corrections of − 2.075 -2.075 kHz and − 0.305 -0.305 kHz beyond the leading hyperfine-mixing contribution are obtained for the 2 1 ​ S 2^{1}S and 2 3 ​ S 2^{3}S states, respectively. These results shift the nuclear charge radii difference derived from the 3 He – 4 He isotope shift and largely resolve the previously reported disagreement between the muonic and electronic helium determinations [van der Werf et al. , arXiv:2306.02333 (2023); Schuhmann et al. , arXiv:2305.11679 (2023)].



I
Introduction

The nuclear magnetic moment causes a shift of atomic energy levels, depending on the total angular momentum

\vec{F}=\vec{I}+\vec{J}
, where
\vec{I}
is the nuclear spin and
\vec{J}
represents the electron angular momentum

[
1
]
. This effect is known as hyperfine splitting (HFS). In hydrogen-like atoms, it is relatively small compared to
the fine structure, which arises from the electron’s spin-orbit coupling.

Due to its small magnitude, hyperfine interaction is typically treated using first-order perturbation theory. The second-order
HFS correction has rarely been addressed in the literature, and when it has, it is often treated incorrectly, primarily due
to the presence of crossed diagrams
[
2
]
and the fact that it diverges in the point-nucleus limit.
A proper analysis of the second-order HFS correction has been conducted
only for hydrogen and muonic hydrogen (
\mu
H) atoms so far, in the context of the two-photon exchange contribution
[
3
,
4
]
.
This was required because the hydrogen HFS has been measured precisely
[
5
]
and accurate measurements
of HFS in light muonic atoms are underway
[
7
,
8
,
6
,
9
]
.

The second-order HFS corrections modify not only the HFS but also the centroid energies. The corresponding
correction has usually been neglected due to its small size, with the leading order
in hydrogenic systems starting at
(Z\alpha)^{6}m^{3}/M^{2}
, where
M
is the nuclear mass,
m
is the electron mass,

Z
is the nuclear charge number, and
\alpha
is the fine-structure constant. This correction forms part of a complete
quadratic recoil correction, which has recently been derived in Ref.
[
10
]
.

In helium-like atoms, the hyperfine interaction causes mixing between triplet and singlet states, which results in significant corrections.
For instance, the HFS of the helium
2^{3}S
state is impacted by it by as much as 60 kHz
[
11
,
12
]
,
exceeding even the relativistic correction
[
12
]
. Similarly, the
2^{3}S
–
2^{1}S
mixing contributes a substantial correction
to the
3
He –
4
He isotope shift, around 80 kHz
[
13
]
.

In light of the discrepancy in the nuclear charge radii difference between
3
He and
4
He, as determined by electronic
versus muonic measurements
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
,
examining all possible corrections to the
3
He –
4
He isotope shift
has become critically important.
Recently, Qi and collaborators
[
17
]
found that the mixing with
n>2
excited states yields an additional correction to
the helium isotope shift of about 1.37 kHz, which partially explains this discrepancy. In this work, we calculate the complete second-order HFS
correction, finding an even larger correction of 1.77 kHz. Combined with an improved value of the field shift contribution,
this result brings the electronic and muonic
determinations of the nuclear charge radii difference
[
14
,
15
]
into a

1.3\,\sigma
agreement.

II
Hydrogenic atom

Let us begin by considering the HFS interaction in hydrogenic systems.
The Dirac Hamiltonian in the external electromagnetic field is (in natural units
\hbar=c=1
)

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{EM}}=

\displaystyle\ \vec{\alpha}\cdot(\vec{p}-e\,\vec{A}_{\mathrm{hfs}})+\beta\,m+e\,A^{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ H_{\mathrm{D}}+V_{\mathrm{hfs}},

(1)

where
e
is the electron charge,
A_{0}
is the Coulomb field from the nucleus

\displaystyle V\equiv e\,A^{0}=-\frac{Z\,\alpha}{r}\,,

(2)

and
\vec{A}_{\mathrm{hfs}}
is the vector potential from the nuclear magnetic moment
\vec{\mu}

\displaystyle\vec{A}_{\mathrm{hfs}}=\frac{1}{4\,\pi}\,\vec{\mu}\times\frac{\vec{r}}{r^{3}}\,.

(3)

The corresponding hyperfine interaction for a point nucleus is

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{hfs}}=

\displaystyle\ -e\,\vec{\alpha}\cdot\vec{A}_{\mathrm{hfs}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{e}{4\,\pi}\,\vec{\mu}\cdot\vec{\alpha}\times\frac{\vec{r}}{r^{3}}\,.

(4)

The second-order hyperfine correction to energy is uv divergent for a point nucleus.
Therefore, we cannot ignore the charge and magnetic moment nuclear form factors,
but for simplicity we assume that they are equal to each other, thus

\displaystyle\rho_{E}(r)=\rho_{M}(r)=\rho(r)\,,

(5)

and neglect the higher order nuclear electromagnetic moments. Thus, potentials take the general form

\displaystyle V=

\displaystyle\ -Z\,\alpha\,\biggl[\frac{1}{r}\biggr]_{\mathrm{fs}}\equiv-\frac{Z\alpha}{r}\,F_{C}(r)\,,

(6)

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{hfs}}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{e}{4\,\pi}\,\vec{\mu}\cdot\vec{\alpha}\times\biggl[\frac{\vec{r}}{r^{3}}\biggr]_{\mathrm{fs}}\equiv\frac{e}{4\,\pi}\,\vec{\mu}\cdot\,\frac{\vec{\alpha}\times\vec{r}}{r^{3}}\,F_{\rm hfs}(r)\,,

(7)

and
F_{C}(r)=F_{\rm hfs}(r)=1
for the point nucleus.

The second-order hyperfine correction to energy in the hydrogenic system is

\displaystyle E_{\rm hfs2}=

\displaystyle\ \langle\phi|V_{\mathrm{hfs}}\,\frac{1}{(E_{D}-H_{D})^{\prime}}\,V_{\mathrm{hfs}}|\phi\rangle\,.

(8)

The product of the nuclear magnetic moments in the above is to be replaced by

\mu^{a}\,\mu^{b}\rightarrow\delta^{ab}\,\vec{\mu}^{\,2}/3
, because here we are interested
in corrections to energy levels but not to the hyperfine structure.
Using the spectral representation of the Green function, we express Eq. (
8
) as

\displaystyle E_{\rm hfs2}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{3}\,(Z\alpha)^{2}\,m^{3}\,\left(\frac{g}{2M}\right)^{2}\,I(I+1)\,\sum_{n,\kappa_{n},\mu_{n}}\!\!\!^{{}^{\prime}}\,\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{a}-\varepsilon_{n}}

\displaystyle\times\langle n_{a}\kappa_{a}\mu_{a}|F_{\rm hfs}(r)\,\frac{(\vec{\alpha}\times\vec{r})_{i}}{r^{3}}|n\kappa_{n}\mu_{n}\rangle\,

\displaystyle\times\langle n\kappa_{n}\mu_{n}|F_{\rm hfs}(r)\,\frac{(\vec{\alpha}\times\vec{r})_{i}}{r^{3}}|n_{a}\kappa_{a}\mu_{a}\rangle\,,

(9)

where we introduced the nuclear
g
-factor
g
as

\displaystyle\vec{\mu}=\frac{q}{2\,M}\,g\,\vec{I},

(10)

where
q=Z\,|e|
.
Performing the angular-momentum algebra in Eq. (
9
), we obtain, for the
1s
reference state
(
\kappa_{a}=-1
),

\displaystyle E_{\rm hfs2}=\frac{2}{3}\,(Z\alpha)^{2}\,m^{3}\,\left(\frac{g}{2M}\right)^{2}\,I(I+1)\,{\cal E}\,,

(11)

where
{\cal E}
is

\displaystyle{\cal E}=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{\kappa_{n}=-1,2}\,a_{\kappa_{n}}\,\sum_{n}\,\!^{{}^{\prime}}\,\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{a}-\varepsilon_{n}}

\displaystyle\ \times\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{2}dx\,\frac{F_{\mathrm{hfs}}(x)}{x^{2}}\,[g_{a}(x)\,f_{n}(x)+f_{a}(x)\,g_{n}(x)]\right\}^{2}\,.

(12)

Here,
a_{-1}=2/3
,
a_{2}=1/3
, and
g_{a}(x)
and
f_{a}(x)
are the upper and the lower radial
components of the Dirac wave function of the reference state.
For the dipole parametrization with
r_{M}=r_{C}
, the
magnetic and electric distribution functions are

\displaystyle F_{\rm hfs}(x)=

\displaystyle\ 1-e^{-\lambda x}\Big[1+\lambda x+\frac{1}{2}(\lambda x)^{2}\Big]\,,

\displaystyle F_{C}(x)=

\displaystyle\ 1-\Big(1+\frac{\lambda x}{2}\Big)\,e^{-\lambda x}\,,

(13)

with
\lambda=2\sqrt{3}/r_{C}
.
We evaluate numerically Eq. (
12
) for the dipole nuclear model by representing the
summation over the spectrum as a sum over the pseudostates
in the basis of B-splines
[
18
,
19
]
.
A series of calculations were performed for a fixed nuclear charge
Z
but for different
nuclear radii
r_{C}
. The obtained results were fitted to the form

\displaystyle{\cal E}(r_{C})=(Z\alpha)^{4}\,\big[a\ln r_{C}+b+c\,r_{C}+\ldots\big]\,,

(14)

which yielded the following result for the leading logarithmic coefficient,

\displaystyle a=5.00\,(1)\,,

(15)

in agreement with the result of analytical calculations in Eq. (
74
).
Next, we performed numerical calculations of
{\cal E}
for different values of
Z
with a
fixed value of
r_{C}
and fitted the results to the form

\displaystyle{\cal E}(Z\alpha)=(Z\alpha)^{4}\,\big[5\ln\left(Z\alpha\,m\,r_{C}\right)+B+Z\alpha\,C+\ldots\big]\,.

(16)

We obtained the nonlogarithmic coefficient
B
as

\displaystyle B=2.700\,(2)\,,

(17)

again in agreement with the analytical result in Eq. (
74
).
The numerical results for the leading
Z\alpha
-expansion coefficients
serve as a valuable test of the correctness of the analytical derivation for few-electron atoms.

III
NRQED approach for a few electron systems

For light few-electron systems, the best computational approach is that based on nonrelativistic quantum electrodynamics (NRQED),
because we can account accurately for relativistic effects and electron correlations on the same footing.
In fact, the derivation is similar to that of the internuclear spin-spin interaction in molecules by Ramsey
[
20
]
,
with the difference that here we have the same nucleus and thus have to include the hard electron-nucleus interaction,
which is highly singular.
Therefore, we will use the dimension regularization for the derivation of NRQED operators, see Appendix
A
for details.
Because we know that the shift is proportional to
\vec{I}^{\,2}
, we can assume initially that
I=1/2

and later after cancellation of
1/\varepsilon
terms, replace it by an arbitrary
I
.
Therefore, in
d=3-2\,\varepsilon
dimensions the vector potential takes the form

\displaystyle A_{\mathrm{hfs}}^{i}(\vec{q})=

\displaystyle\ -i\,Z\,e\,\frac{g}{4\,M}\,\sigma_{N}^{ij}\,\frac{q^{j}}{q^{2}}\,\tilde{\rho}(q^{2})\,,

(18)

where
\tilde{\rho}
is a Fourier transform of
\rho
in Eq. (
5
) and
\sigma_{N}^{ij}
is
d
-dimensional spin operator for the nucleus defined in Eq. (
84
).
In the coordinate representation it is

\displaystyle A_{\mathrm{hfs}}^{i}(\vec{r})=

\displaystyle\ -i\,Z\,e\,\frac{g}{4\,M}\,\sigma_{N}^{ij}\,\int\frac{d^{d}q}{(2\,\pi)^{d}}\,e^{i\,\vec{q}\cdot\vec{r}}\,\frac{q^{j}}{q^{2}}\,\tilde{\rho}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{Z\,e}{4\,\pi}\,\frac{g}{4\,M}\,\sigma_{N}^{ij}\,\biggl[\frac{r^{j}}{r^{3}}\biggr]_{\mathrm{fs,\varepsilon}}.

(19)

In
d=3
dimensions,
\sigma^{ij}=\epsilon^{ijk}\,\sigma^{k}
and for a point nucleus
A_{\mathrm{hfs}}^{i}(\vec{r})
becomes

\displaystyle\vec{A}_{\mathrm{hfs}}(\vec{r})=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{Z\,e}{4\,\pi}\,\frac{g}{2\,M}\,\vec{I}\times\frac{\vec{r}}{r^{3}}=\frac{1}{4\,\pi}\,\vec{\mu}\times\frac{\vec{r}}{r^{3}}\,,

(20)

as it should.

The leading second-order hyperfine-interaction correction is of the order
m\,\alpha^{6}\,(m/M)^{2}
.
We represent it as a sum of three terms

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{hfs2}}=

\displaystyle\ E_{\mathrm{sec}}+E_{\mathrm{diam}}+E_{\mathrm{high}}\,,

(21)

which we calculate in the following three sections.
Similarly to the first-order HFS correction, which is proportional to the Fermi coefficient
E_{F}
,
the second-order HFS correction is proportional to the
second-order Fermi coefficient
E_{F2}
,

\displaystyle E_{F2}=\alpha^{6}\,m\,\Bigl(\frac{Z\,g\,m}{2\,M}\Big)^{2}\,I(I+1)\,.

(22)

IV
E_{\mathrm{sec}}

E_{\mathrm{sec}}
is a second-order correction of the form

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{sec}}=

\displaystyle\ \bigg\langle H_{\mathrm{hfs}}\,\frac{1}{E-H}\,H_{\mathrm{hfs}}\bigg\rangle\,,

(23)

where
H
is the nonrelativistic Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H=\frac{p_{1}^{2}}{2\,m}+\frac{p_{2}^{2}}{2\,m}+\biggl[\frac{\alpha}{r_{12}}-\frac{Z\,\alpha}{r_{1}}-\frac{Z\,\alpha}{r_{2}}\biggr]_{\varepsilon},

(24)

and
H_{\mathrm{hfs}}
is the leading hyperfine interaction.
It is obtained from the nonrelativistic expansion of
V_{\mathrm{hfs}}
for a point nucleus

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{hfs}}=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{Z\,\alpha\,g}{4\,M}\,\alpha^{i}\,\sigma_{N}^{ij}\,\biggl[\frac{r^{j}}{r^{3}}\biggr]_{\mathrm{\varepsilon}}\,,

(25)

namely

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{hfs}}=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{Z\,\alpha\,g}{4\,M}\,\sigma_{N}^{ij}\,\biggl\{\sigma^{i}\,\biggl[\frac{r^{j}}{r^{3}}\biggr]_{\mathrm{\varepsilon}}\,,\,\frac{\sigma^{k}\,p^{k}}{2\,m}\biggr\}\,,

(26)

where
\{A\,,\,B\}=A\,B+B\,A
. In
d
-dimensions

\displaystyle\sigma^{i}\,\sigma^{k}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\,\{\sigma^{i}\,,\,\sigma^{k}\}+\frac{1}{2}\,[\sigma^{i}\,,\,\sigma^{k}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \delta^{ik}+i\,\sigma^{ik}\,,

(27)

thus

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{hfs}}=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{Z\,\alpha\,g}{4\,m\,M}\,\sigma_{N}^{ij}\,\biggl(\sigma^{ik}\,\frac{i}{2}\,\biggl[\frac{r^{j}}{r^{3}}\,,\,p^{k}\biggr]_{\mathrm{\varepsilon}}+\biggl[\frac{r^{j}}{r^{3}}\biggr]_{\mathrm{\varepsilon}}p^{i}\biggr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ H_{\mathrm{hfsA}}+H_{\mathrm{hfsB}}+H_{\mathrm{hfsC}}\,,

(28)

where after summing over all electrons

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{hfsA}}=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{a}\frac{Z\,\alpha\,g}{2\,m\,M}\,\sigma_{N}^{ij}\,\sigma_{a}^{ij}\,\frac{\pi}{d}\,\delta^{d}(r_{a})\,,

(29)

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{hfsB}}=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{a}\frac{Z\,\alpha\,g}{8\,m\,M}\,\sigma_{N}^{ij}\,\sigma_{a}^{ik}\,\biggl[\frac{\delta^{jk}}{r_{a}^{3}}-3\,\frac{r_{a}^{j}\,r_{a}^{k}}{r_{a}^{5}}\biggr]_{\mathrm{\varepsilon}}\,,

(30)

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{hfsC}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d=3}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\ \sum_{a}\frac{Z\,\alpha\,g}{2\,m\,M}\,\vec{I}\cdot\frac{\vec{r}_{a}}{r_{a}^{3}}\times\vec{p}_{a}\,.

(31)

The corresponding second-order contributions

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{sec}}=

\displaystyle\ E_{\mathrm{secA}}+E_{\mathrm{secB}}+E_{\mathrm{secC}}

(32)

are angular averaged [see Eqs. (
85
) and (
86
) in Appendix
A
] and expressed in atomic units.

We proceed first with the calculation of
E_{\mathrm{secA}}
,

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{secA}}=

\displaystyle\ \bigg\langle H_{\mathrm{hfsA}}\,\frac{1}{(E-H)^{\prime}}\,H_{\mathrm{hfsA}}\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{E_{F2}}{d^{3}\,(d-1)}\,\sum_{a,b}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\ \bigg\langle\sigma_{a}^{ij}\,4\,\pi\,\delta^{d}(r_{a})\,\frac{1}{(E-H)^{\prime}}\,\sigma_{b}^{ij}\,4\,\pi\,\delta^{d}(r_{b})\bigg\rangle\,,

(33)

where atomic units are used for the matrix elements, and
E_{F2}
is assumed to be the
d
-dimensional generalization of Eq. (
22
) with
I(I+1)\to\langle\vec{I}^{\,2}\rangle_{\varepsilon}
.
The expression in Eq. (
33
) is singular and needs to be transformed to the regular form
by pulling out the
1/\varepsilon
singularity from the second-order matrix elements.
This is achieved by the use of the identity

\displaystyle H_{a}|\phi\rangle\equiv

\displaystyle\ \{H-E\,,\,Q_{a}\}|\phi\rangle+V_{a}|\phi\rangle\,,

(34)

where
\phi
is the reference state,

\displaystyle H_{a}=

\displaystyle\ 4\,\pi\,\delta^{d}(r_{a})\,,

(35)

\displaystyle Q_{a}=

\displaystyle\ 2\,\biggl[\frac{1}{r_{a}}\biggr]_{\varepsilon}\,,

(36)

\displaystyle V_{a}=

\displaystyle\ -2\,\biggl[\frac{\vec{r}_{a}}{r_{a}^{3}}\biggr]_{\varepsilon}\cdot\vec{\nabla}_{a}\,,

(37)

to obtain

\displaystyle\bigg\langle H_{a}\,\frac{1}{(E-H)^{\prime}}\,H_{b}\bigg\rangle=

\displaystyle\ \bigg\langle V^{+}_{a}\,\frac{1}{(E-H)^{\prime}}\,V_{b}\bigg\rangle+\langle H_{a}\rangle\,\langle Q_{b}\rangle

\displaystyle\ +\langle H_{b}\rangle\,\langle Q_{a}\rangle-\,\langle H_{a}\,Q_{b}\rangle-\,\langle H_{b}\,Q_{a}\rangle

\displaystyle\ +\langle Q_{a}\,(H-E)\,Q_{b}\rangle\,,

(38)

where
V^{+}_{a}
is a Hermitian conjugation of
V_{a}
.
The resulting form of
E_{\mathrm{secA}}
is

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{secA}}=

\displaystyle\ E_{F2}\,\frac{1}{d^{3}\,(d-1)}\,\sum_{a,b}\sigma_{a}^{ij}\,\sigma_{b}^{ij}\,\biggl[\bigg\langle V^{+}_{a}\,\frac{1}{(E-H)^{\prime}}\,V_{b}\bigg\rangle+\langle 4\,\pi\,\delta^{3}(r_{a})\rangle\,\bigg\langle\frac{2}{r_{b}}\bigg\rangle+\langle 4\,\pi\,\delta^{3}(r_{b})\rangle\,\bigg\langle\frac{2}{r_{a}}\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle\ -\,\bigg\langle 4\,\pi\,\delta^{d}(r_{a})\,2\,\biggl[\frac{1}{r_{b}}\biggr]_{\varepsilon}\bigg\rangle-\,\bigg\langle 4\,\pi\,\delta^{d}(r_{b})\,2\,\biggl[\frac{1}{r_{a}}\biggr]_{\varepsilon}\bigg\rangle+2\,\delta_{ab}\,\bigg\langle\biggl[\frac{\vec{r}_{a}}{r_{a}^{3}}\biggr]_{\varepsilon}\cdot\biggl[\frac{\vec{r}_{b}}{r_{b}^{3}}\biggr]_{\varepsilon}\bigg\rangle\biggr]\,.

(39)

For the second-order tensor contribution
E_{\mathrm{secB}}
we use Eq. (
86
) to contract indices, and

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{secB}}=

\displaystyle\ \bigg\langle H_{\mathrm{hfsB}}\,\frac{1}{E-H}\,H_{\mathrm{hfsB}}\bigg\rangle=E_{F2}\,\frac{(d-2)}{2\,d^{2}\,(d-1)^{2}}\,\sum_{a,b}\,\bigg\langle\sigma_{a}^{kl}\,\biggl[\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r_{a}^{3}}-3\,\frac{r_{a}^{i}\,r_{a}^{j}}{r_{a}^{5}}\biggr]_{\mathrm{\varepsilon}}\,\frac{1}{E-H}\,\sigma_{b}^{kl}\,\biggl[\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r_{b}^{3}}-3\,\frac{r_{b}^{i}\,r_{b}^{j}}{r_{b}^{5}}\biggr]_{\mathrm{\varepsilon}}\bigg\rangle\,,

(40)

we use the following identity to pull out
1/\varepsilon
singularity from the matrix elements:

\displaystyle H_{a}^{ij}|\phi\rangle\ \equiv

\displaystyle\ \{H-E,Q_{a}^{ij}\}|\phi\rangle+V_{a}^{ij}|\phi\rangle\,,

(41)

where

\displaystyle H_{a}^{ij}=

\displaystyle\ \bigg[\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r_{a}^{3}}-3\frac{r_{a}^{i}r_{a}^{j}}{r_{a}^{5}}\bigg]_{\varepsilon}=\Big(-\nabla^{i}\,\nabla^{j}+\frac{\delta^{ij}}{d}\,\vec{\nabla}^{\,2}\Big)\,\mathcal{V}(r_{a})\,,

(42)

\displaystyle Q_{a}^{ij}=

\displaystyle\ 2\,\biggl[\frac{\delta^{ij}}{6\,r_{a}}-\frac{r_{a}^{i}\,r_{a}^{j}}{2\,r_{a}^{3}}\biggr]_{\mathrm{\varepsilon}}=2\,\Big(-\nabla^{i}\,\nabla^{j}+\frac{\delta^{ij}}{d}\,\vec{\nabla}^{\,2}\Big)\,\mathcal{V}^{(2)}(r_{a})\,,

(43)

\displaystyle V_{a}^{ij}|\phi\rangle=

\displaystyle\ \bigg[-\frac{\delta^{ij}r_{a}^{k}+3\,\delta^{ik}r_{a}^{j}+3\,\delta^{jk}r_{a}^{i}}{3\,r_{a}^{3}}+3\frac{r_{a}^{i}r_{a}^{j}r_{a}^{k}}{r_{a}^{5}}\bigg]\,\nabla_{a}^{k}|\phi\rangle\,,

(44)

with
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{V}^{(2)}
defined in Appendix
A
. We obtain

\displaystyle\bigg\langle H_{a}^{ij}\,\frac{1}{E-H}\,H_{b}^{ij}\bigg\rangle=

\displaystyle\ \bigg\langle V_{a}^{+ij}\,\frac{1}{E-H}\,V_{b}^{ij}\bigg\rangle-\,\langle H_{a}^{ij}\,Q_{b}^{ij}\rangle-\,\langle H_{b}^{ij}\,Q_{a}^{ij}\rangle+\langle Q_{a}^{ij}\,(H-E)\,Q_{b}^{ij}\rangle\,.

(45)

The result for
E_{\mathrm{secB}}
is then

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{secB}}=

\displaystyle\ E_{F2}\,\frac{(d-2)}{2\,d^{2}\,(d-1)^{2}}\,\sum_{a,b}\,\sigma_{a}^{st}\,\sigma_{b}^{st}\,\biggl[\bigg\langle V_{a}^{+ij}\,\frac{1}{E-H}\,V_{b}^{ij}\bigg\rangle-2\,\bigg\langle\bigg[\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r_{a}^{3}}-3\frac{r_{a}^{i}r_{a}^{j}}{r_{a}^{5}}\bigg]_{\varepsilon}\,\biggl[\frac{\delta^{ij}}{6\,r_{b}}-\frac{r_{b}^{i}\,r_{b}^{j}}{2\,r_{b}^{3}}\biggr]_{\mathrm{\varepsilon}}\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle\ -2\,\bigg\langle\bigg[\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r_{b}^{3}}-3\frac{r_{b}^{i}r_{b}^{j}}{r_{b}^{5}}\bigg]_{\varepsilon}\,\biggl[\frac{\delta^{ij}}{6\,r_{a}}-\frac{r_{a}^{i}\,r_{a}^{j}}{2\,r_{a}^{3}}\biggr]_{\mathrm{\varepsilon}}\bigg\rangle+2\,\delta_{ab}\,\bigg\langle\nabla_{a}^{k}\,\biggl[\frac{\delta^{ij}}{6\,r_{a}}-\frac{r_{a}^{i}\,r_{a}^{j}}{2\,r_{a}^{3}}\biggr]_{\mathrm{\varepsilon}}\,\nabla_{b}^{k}\biggl[\frac{\delta^{ij}}{6\,r_{b}}-\frac{r_{b}^{i}\,r_{b}^{j}}{2\,r_{b}^{3}}\biggr]_{\mathrm{\varepsilon}}\bigg\rangle\biggr]\,.

(46)

Spin-orbit contribution
E_{\mathrm{secC}}
does not require any regularization,

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{secC}}=

\displaystyle\ \bigg\langle H_{\mathrm{hfsC}}\,\frac{1}{E-H}\,H_{\mathrm{hfsC}}\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ E_{F2}\,\frac{1}{3}\,\sum_{a,b}\bigg\langle\frac{\vec{r}_{a}}{r_{a}^{3}}\times\vec{p}_{a}\,\frac{1}{E-H}\,\frac{\vec{r}_{b}}{r_{b}^{3}}\times\vec{p}_{b}\bigg\rangle\,,

(47)

and can be calculated directly in
d=3
dimensions.

V
E_{\mathrm{diam}}

The diamagnetic contribution in natural units is

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{diam}}=\sum_{a}\frac{e^{2}}{2\,m}\,\bigl\langle\vec{\mathcal{A}}(\vec{r}_{a})^{2}\bigr\rangle\,,

(48)

where

\displaystyle e\,\vec{\mathcal{A}}(\vec{r}_{a})=\frac{Z\,\alpha\,g}{4\,M}\,\sigma_{N}^{ij}\,\biggl[\frac{r_{a}^{j}}{r_{a}^{3}}\biggr]_{\mathrm{\varepsilon}}\,.

(49)

After transforming to atomic units and performing the angular average, it becomes

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{diam}}=

\displaystyle\ E_{F2}\,\frac{1}{d}\,\sum_{a}\biggl\langle\frac{1}{r_{a}^{4}}\biggr\rangle_{\mathrm{\varepsilon}}\,.

(50)

VI
E_{\mathrm{high}}

The high-energy contribution is obtained from the scattering amplitude, and is essentially the same as for the hydrogenic case.
In momentum space and natural units, we have

\displaystyle V(\vec{q})=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{4\,\pi\,Z\,\alpha}{\vec{q}^{\,2}}\,\tilde{\rho}(q^{2})\,,

(51)

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{hfs}}(\vec{q})=

\displaystyle\ i\,4\,\pi\,Z\,\alpha\,\frac{g}{4\,M}\,\alpha^{i}\,\sigma_{N}^{ij}\,\frac{q^{j}}{q^{2}}\,\tilde{\rho}(q^{2})\,.

(52)

Then,

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{high}}=

\displaystyle\ \phi^{2}(0)\,\int\frac{d^{d}q_{1}}{(2\,\pi)^{d}}\,\int\frac{d^{d}q_{2}}{(2\,\pi)^{d}}\,V(\vec{q}_{1}-\vec{q}_{2})

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\ \langle t|\gamma^{0}\,S_{F}(q_{2}-q_{1})\,\gamma^{0}\,V_{\mathrm{hfs}}(-\vec{q}_{1})\,S_{F}(q_{2})\,\gamma^{0}\,V_{\mathrm{hfs}}(\vec{q}_{2})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\ \gamma^{0}\,V_{\mathrm{hfs}}(-\vec{q}_{1})\,S_{F}(q_{1})\,\gamma^{0}\,S_{F}(q_{2})\,\gamma^{0}\,V_{\mathrm{hfs}}(\vec{q}_{2})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\ \gamma^{0}\,V_{\mathrm{hfs}}(-\vec{q}_{1})\,S_{F}(q_{1})\,\gamma^{0}\,V_{\mathrm{hfs}}(\vec{q}_{2})\,S_{F}(q_{1}-q_{2})\,\gamma^{0}|t\rangle\,,

(53)

where
S_{F}
is the fermion propagator and
t=(m,\vec{0})
. Performing traces with Dirac matrices, one obtains

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{high}}=

\displaystyle\ -\phi^{2}(0)\,(4\,\pi\,Z\,\alpha)^{3}\,\biggl(\frac{g}{2\,M}\biggr)^{2}\,\frac{4}{d\,(d-1)}\,\langle\vec{I}^{\,2}\rangle_{\varepsilon}\,

\displaystyle\ \times\int\frac{d^{d}q_{1}}{(2\,\pi)^{d}}\,\int\frac{d^{d}q_{2}}{(2\,\pi)^{d}}\,\frac{\tilde{\rho}(q_{1}^{2})\,\tilde{\rho}(q_{2}^{2})\,\tilde{\rho}(q_{3}^{2})}{q_{1}^{2}\,q_{2}^{2}\,q_{3}^{2}}

\displaystyle\ \times\bigg(1+(d-2)\,\frac{(\vec{q}_{1}\cdot\vec{q}_{2})\,(\vec{q}_{2}\cdot\vec{q}_{3})\,(\vec{q}_{3}\cdot\vec{q}_{1})}{q_{1}^{2}\,q_{2}^{2}\,q_{3}^{2}}\bigg)\,,

(54)

where
\vec{q}_{3}=\vec{q}_{1}-\vec{q}_{2}
. After transforming integral to the coordinate representation, it becomes

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{high}}=

\displaystyle\ -\phi^{2}(0)\,(Z\,\alpha)^{3}\,\biggl(\frac{g}{4\,M}\biggr)^{2}\,\frac{16\,\langle\vec{I}^{\,2}\rangle_{\varepsilon}}{d\,(d-1)}\int d^{d}r\bigl[\mathcal{V}_{\rho}(r)^{3}-(d-2)\,\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}\mathcal{V}_{\rho}^{(2)}(r)\,\nabla^{j}\nabla^{k}\mathcal{V}_{\rho}^{(2)}(r)\,\nabla^{k}\nabla^{i}\mathcal{V}_{\rho}^{(2)}(r)\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ E_{\mathrm{highA}}+E_{\mathrm{highB}}\,,

(55)

where
\mathcal{V}_{\rho}(r)
and
\mathcal{V}_{\rho}^{(2)}(r)
are defined in Appendix
A
.

E_{\mathrm{highA}}
includes the
\vec{r}
integral over a small region of the nuclear charge distribution,
where we can set
d=3
. It is evaluated as

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{highA}}=

\displaystyle\ -\phi^{2}(0)\,(Z\,\alpha)^{3}\,\biggl(\frac{g}{4\,M}\biggr)^{2}\,\frac{8}{3}\,\langle\vec{I}^{\,2}\rangle\int^{R}d^{3}r\bigl[{V}_{\rho}(r)^{3}-\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}{V}_{\rho}^{(2)}(r)\,\nabla^{j}\nabla^{k}{V}_{\rho}^{(2)}(r)\,\nabla^{k}\nabla^{i}{V}_{\rho}^{(2)}(r)\big]\,.

(56)

It is convenient at this point to use the explicit form of the nuclear charge distribution,
which we chose to be dipole parametrization, the same as used in the hydrogenic case in Sec. II,

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}(p^{2})=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\lambda^{4}}{(\lambda^{2}+p^{2})^{2}}\,.

(57)

Thus,
d=3
potentials become

\displaystyle 4\,\pi\,\rho(r)=

\displaystyle\ 4\pi\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\tilde{\rho}(p^{2})\,e^{i\vec{p}\cdot\vec{r}}=\frac{\lambda^{3}}{2}\,e^{-\lambda\,r}\,,

(58)

\displaystyle{V}_{\rho}(r)=

\displaystyle\ 4\pi\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{\tilde{\rho}(p^{2})}{p^{2}}e^{i\vec{p}\cdot\vec{r}}=\frac{1}{r}-\frac{e^{-\lambda\,r}}{r}-\frac{\lambda}{2}\,e^{-\lambda\,r}\,,

(59)

\displaystyle{V}_{\rho}^{(2)}(r)=

\displaystyle\ 4\pi\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{\tilde{\rho}(p^{2})\,e^{i\vec{p}\cdot\vec{r}}-1}{p^{4}}=-\frac{r}{2}-\frac{2}{\lambda^{2}\,r}+\frac{1}{2\,\lambda}\,e^{-\lambda\,r}+\frac{2}{\lambda^{2}}\,\frac{e^{-\lambda\,r}}{r}\,.

(60)

Now the
r
-integration in Eq. (
56
) can be performed analytically, with the result

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{highA}}=

\displaystyle\ -\phi^{2}(0)\,(4\,\pi\,Z\,\alpha)^{3}\,\biggl(\frac{g}{4\,M}\biggr)^{2}\,\frac{8}{3}\,\langle\vec{I}^{\,2}\rangle\,5\,\pi\,\biggl(-\frac{467}{540}+\frac{2\,\ln 2}{5}+\frac{3\,\ln 3}{10}-\ln\frac{r_{C}}{R}+\gamma\biggr)\,,

(61)

where
r_{C}=2\,\sqrt{3}/\lambda
. It should be mentioned that the description of the nucleus through the elastic form-factors
\rho_{C}
and
\rho_{M}
here
is not fully justified. It is because for high momenta
\sim\nicefrac{{\hbar}}{{r_{C}}}
the electron sees individual nucleons, rather than the nucleus as a whole.
The detailed analysis of this contribution is complicated and should be performed together with other three-photon exchange corrections
[
21
]
.
We will use the above form-factor result for comparison with direct numerical calculations for hydrogenic systems described in Sec. II,
keeping in mind that for the
2^{3}S-2^{1}S
transition in
3
He it is negligible.

The part
E_{\mathrm{highB}}
of the high energy contribution involves integral outside of the nuclear region, thus we can assume the
point nucleus and obtain

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{highB}}=

\displaystyle\ -\phi^{2}(0)\,(Z\,\alpha)^{3}\,\biggl(\frac{g}{4\,M}\biggr)^{2}\,\frac{16}{d\,(d-1)}\,\langle\vec{I}^{\,2}\rangle_{\varepsilon}\int_{R}d^{d}r\bigl[\mathcal{V}(r)^{3}-(d-2)\,\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}\mathcal{V}^{(2)}(r)\,\nabla^{j}\nabla^{k}\mathcal{V}^{(2)}(r)\,\nabla^{k}\nabla^{i}\mathcal{V}^{(2)}(r)\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \phi^{2}(0)\,(Z\,\alpha)^{3}\,\biggl(\frac{g}{4\,M}\biggr)^{2}\,\langle\vec{I}^{\,2}\rangle_{\varepsilon}\,[(4\,\pi)^{\varepsilon}\Gamma(1+\varepsilon)]^{2}\,\frac{40}{3}\,\pi\,\biggl(\frac{23}{30}+\frac{1}{4\,\varepsilon}+\ln R+\gamma\biggr)\,,

(62)

and by convention the factor
[(4\,\pi)^{\varepsilon}\Gamma(1+\varepsilon)]^{2}
is consistently pulled out from all the terms.

The sum of both parts is independent on arbitrary cutoff
R
, and is

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{high}}=

\displaystyle\ (Z\,\alpha)^{3}\,\biggl(\frac{g}{2\,M}\biggr)^{2}\,\langle\vec{I}^{\,2}\rangle_{\varepsilon}\frac{10}{3}\,\pi\biggl(\frac{881}{540}-\frac{2\,\ln 2}{5}-\frac{3\,\ln 3}{10}+\frac{1}{4\,\varepsilon}+\ln r_{C}\biggr)\,\langle\delta^{d}(r)\rangle\,.

(63)

Transforming to atomic units by
\vec{r}\rightarrow(m\,\alpha)^{-1/(1+2\,\varepsilon)}\,\vec{r}
, one pulls out the factor

m^{(1-2\,\varepsilon)/(1+2\varepsilon)}\,\alpha^{2/(1+2\,\varepsilon)}

from
H
and obtains the nonrelativistic Hamiltonian in atomic units.
Similarly, for
H^{(6)}
the common factor in atomic units

\eta=m^{(1-10\,\varepsilon)/(1+2\varepsilon)}\,\alpha^{6/(1+2\,\varepsilon)}

(64)

is pulled out from all the terms, which corresponds to the replacement

m\rightarrow 1,\alpha\rightarrow 1
, and

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{high}}=

\displaystyle\ E_{F2}\,\frac{10}{3}\,\pi\biggl(\frac{881}{540}-\frac{2\,\ln 2}{5}-\frac{3\,\ln 3}{10}+\frac{1}{4\,\varepsilon}+\ln m\,\alpha\,r_{C}\biggr)\,\sum_{a}\,Z\,\langle\delta^{d}(r_{a})\rangle\,.

(65)

VII
Complete result

In order to eliminate
1/\varepsilon
singularity, we transform all singular operators (in atomic units) to the same form,

\displaystyle\bigg\langle\bigg[\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r^{3}}-3\frac{r^{i}r^{j}}{r^{5}}\bigg]_{\varepsilon}\,\biggl[\frac{\delta^{ij}}{6\,r}-\frac{r^{i}\,r^{j}}{2\,r^{3}}\biggr]_{\mathrm{\varepsilon}}\bigg\rangle=

\displaystyle\ (1-\varepsilon)\,\bigg\langle\frac{1}{r^{4}}\bigg\rangle_{\varepsilon}\,,

(66)

\displaystyle\bigg\langle\nabla^{k}\,\biggl[\frac{\delta^{ij}}{6\,r}-\frac{r^{i}\,r^{j}}{2\,r^{3}}\biggr]_{\mathrm{\varepsilon}}\,\nabla^{k}\biggl[\frac{\delta^{ij}}{6\,r}-\frac{r^{i}\,r^{j}}{2\,r^{3}}\biggr]_{\mathrm{\varepsilon}}\bigg\rangle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{(d-1)(d^{2}-2d+4)}{4d}\bigg\langle\frac{1}{r^{4}}\bigg\rangle_{\varepsilon}=\biggl(\frac{7}{6}-\frac{31}{18}\,\varepsilon\biggr)\,\bigg\langle\frac{1}{r^{4}}\bigg\rangle_{\varepsilon}+O(\varepsilon)\,,

(67)

and use
[
22
]

\displaystyle\bigg\langle\frac{1}{r^{4}}\bigg\rangle_{\varepsilon}=

\displaystyle\ \bigg\langle\frac{1}{r^{4}}\bigg\rangle+Z\,\langle\pi\delta^{d}(r)\rangle\,\biggl(-\frac{2}{\varepsilon}+8\biggr)+O(\varepsilon)\,,

(68)

where
\langle 1/r^{4}\rangle
is defined as integral from a small radius
a
to infinity with the

1/a
and
\ln a+\gamma
terms subtracted out.
The
1/\varepsilon
singularity cancels out, and we obtain the final result for the hydrogenic atom as

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{hfs2}}=

\displaystyle\ E_{\mathrm{secA}}+E_{\mathrm{secB}}+E_{\mathrm{diam}}+E_{\mathrm{high}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ E_{F2}\,\biggl[\frac{1}{9}\,\bigg\langle V^{+}\,\frac{1}{(E-H)^{\prime}}\,V\bigg\rangle+\frac{1}{12}\,\bigg\langle V^{+ij}\,\frac{1}{E-H}\,V^{ij}\bigg\rangle+\frac{16}{9}\,\langle\pi\,\delta^{3}(r)\rangle\,\bigg\langle\frac{1}{r}\bigg\rangle+\frac{5}{12}\,\bigg\langle\frac{1}{r^{4}}\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle\ +\frac{Z^{4}}{n^{3}}\,\frac{10}{3}\biggl(\frac{1223}{540}-\frac{2\,\ln 2}{5}-\frac{3\,\ln 3}{10}+\ln m\,\alpha\,r_{C}\biggr)\biggr]\,.

(69)

Using the following expectation values with hydrogenic
nS
-state wave functions,

\displaystyle\biggl\langle\frac{1}{r^{4}}\biggr\rangle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{8\,Z^{4}}{n^{3}}\,\biggl[-\frac{5}{3}+\frac{1}{2\,n}+\frac{1}{6\,n^{2}}+\gamma+\Psi(n)-\ln\frac{n}{2}+\ln Z\biggr]\,,

(70)

\displaystyle\bigg\langle V^{+}\,\frac{1}{(E-H)^{\prime}}\,V\bigg\rangle=

\displaystyle\ Z^{4}\,\biggl(-\frac{56}{3\,n^{5}}-\frac{24}{n^{4}}+\frac{8}{3\,n^{3}}\biggr)\,,

(71)

\displaystyle\bigg\langle V^{+ij}\,\frac{1}{(E-H)}\,V^{ij}\bigg\rangle=

\displaystyle\ Z^{4}\,\biggl(\frac{2}{9\,n^{5}}-\frac{2}{3\,n^{3}}\biggr)\,,

(72)

we obtain for the hydrogenic
nS
state

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{hfs2}}(n)=

\displaystyle\ m\,(Z\,\alpha)^{6}\,\Big(\frac{g\,m}{2\,M}\Big)^{2}\,I(I+1)\,\frac{10}{3\,n^{2}}\biggl[\frac{181}{270}-\frac{3}{10\,n}+\frac{1}{12\,n^{2}}+\frac{3}{10}\,\ln\frac{4}{3}-\ln(n)+\gamma+\Psi(n)+\ln m\,Z\,\alpha\,r_{C}\biggr]\,.

(73)

Specifically, for
1S
state it reduces to

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{hfs2}}(1)=

\displaystyle\ m\,(Z\,\alpha)^{6}\,\Big(\frac{g\,m}{2\,M}\Big)^{2}\,I(I+1)\,\frac{10}{3}\,(0.540\,008+\ln m\,Z\,\alpha\,r_{C})\,,

(74)

which is in perfect agreement with the results of our numerical calculations described in Sec. II.
Namely, the analytical constant term
0.540\,008
in Eq. (
73
)
agrees with the result obtained by fitting the all-order numerical data,
2.700(2)/5=0.540\,0\,(4)
.

The final result for the helium atom takes the form (in atomic units)

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{hfs2}}=

\displaystyle\ E_{F2}\,\biggl\{\sum_{a}\biggl[\frac{5}{12}\,\bigg\langle\frac{1}{r_{a}^{4}}\bigg\rangle+\frac{10}{3}\biggl(\frac{1223}{540}-\frac{2\,\ln 2}{5}-\frac{3\,\ln 3}{10}+\ln m\,\alpha\,r_{C}\biggr)\,Z\,\pi\,\langle\delta^{3}(r_{a})\rangle\biggr]

\displaystyle\ +\sum_{a,b}\biggl[\frac{1}{27}\,\bigg\langle\vec{\sigma}_{a}\,V^{+}_{a}\,\frac{1}{(E-H)^{\prime}}\,\vec{\sigma}_{b}\,V_{b}\bigg\rangle+\frac{1}{36}\,\bigg\langle\vec{\sigma}_{a}\,V_{a}^{+ij}\,\frac{1}{E-H}\,\vec{\sigma}_{b}\,V_{b}^{ij}\bigg\rangle+\frac{1}{3}\,\bigg\langle\frac{\vec{r}_{a}}{r_{a}^{3}}\times\vec{p}_{a}\,\frac{1}{E-H}\,\frac{\vec{r}_{b}}{r_{b}^{3}}\times\vec{p}_{a}\bigg\rangle\biggr]

\displaystyle\ +\frac{16}{27}\,\sum_{a,b}\vec{\sigma}_{a}\cdot\vec{\sigma}_{b}\,\langle\pi\,\delta^{3}(r_{a})\rangle\,\bigg\langle\frac{1}{r_{b}}\bigg\rangle-\frac{16}{27}\,\sum_{a\neq b}\vec{\sigma}_{a}\cdot\vec{\sigma}_{b}\,\bigg\langle\frac{\pi\,\delta^{3}(r_{a})}{r_{b}}\bigg\rangle+\frac{1}{18}\,\sum_{a\neq b}\,\vec{\sigma}_{a}\cdot\vec{\sigma}_{b}\,\bigg\langle\frac{1}{r_{a}^{3}\,r_{b}}-3\frac{(\vec{r}_{a}\cdot\vec{r}_{b})^{2}}{r_{a}^{5}\,r_{b}^{3}}\bigg\rangle\biggr\}\,,

(75)

where
E_{F2}
is defined in Eq. (
22
), the operator
V_{a}
is defined in Eq. (

),
and
V^{ij}_{a}
is defined in Eq. (

IX
{2^{3}S}

In our previous calculation of the isotope shift of the

transition energy of helium
[

]

and

in Eqs. (

It was recently pointed out by Qi and coworkers
[
17
]
that these contributions are in fact significant
and shift the theoretical predictions for the isotope shift beyond the previously estimated error bars.
We now extend our previous theory by accounting for the complete second-order HFS correction
calculated in the previous sections.

The individual theoretical contributions to the
{}^{3}\textrm{He}-{}^{4}\textrm{He}
isotope shift of
the
2^{1}S
–
2^{3}S
centroid energies are summarized in Table
3
, in comparison with results of
Qi and coworkers
[
17
]
. All numerical values apart from
\delta{\cal E}_{\rm mix}
are taken from
our previous investigation
[
22
]
. Note that the entries
\alpha^{6}
,
\alpha^{7}
, and

\alpha^{5}{\cal E}_{\rm pol}
were not independently reproduced by Qi and coworkers but taken from

[

]
.
The deviation from Qi and coworkers for
\delta{\cal E}_{\rm mix}
is due to the fact that they
did not calculate the complete second-order HFS correction but used an approximate representation for it.
Otherwise, we observe very good agreement with their calculation.

The primary source of uncertainty arises from the
\alpha^{7}

A_{72}
coefficient. A calculation of the full
\alpha^{7}
correction for the helium atom is quite challenging and has not
been attempted so far. Another open challenge is the inelastic second-order HFS contribution. However,
given that the leading (electric dipole) polarizability correction is
only 0.2 kHz, it can be argued to be negligible at the current level of precision.

The theory for the point nucleus summarized in Table

needs to be complemented by
the description of the energy shift arising due to the nuclear mean square charge radius
r_{C}
.
Generalizing the expression for the finite nuclear size (fs) effect for the hydrogenic energy levels

[

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{fs}}[{}^{A}\textrm{He}]=

\displaystyle\ \frac{2}{3}\,Z\,\alpha^{4}\,m\,\frac{r^{2}_{C}}{\not\!\lambda^{2}}\,\sum_{a}\langle\,\pi\,\delta^{3}(r_{a})\rangle_{M}\,

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\ \Big\{1-(Z\,\alpha)^{2}\Big[\ln\Big(Z\,\alpha\,\frac{r_{C}}{\not\!\lambda}\Big)+\beta\Big]

\displaystyle+\alpha\,(Z\,\alpha)\,(4\,\ln 2-5)\Big\}\equiv C_{A}\,r^{2}_{C}\,,

(82)

where
\not\!\!\lambda=386.159
fm is the reduced Compton wavelength,
the expectation value of the
\delta

relativistic correction

[

[

proportional to the difference of the squared radii (as was assumed in Ref.

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{fs}}[{}^{3}\textrm{He}\!-\!{}^{4}\textrm{He}]=

\displaystyle\ C_{3}\,r^{2}_{3}-C_{4}\,r^{2}_{4}

\displaystyle\ \frac{C_{3}+C_{4}}{2}\,\big[r^{2}_{3}-r^{2}_{4}\big]+\frac{C_{3}-C_{4}}{2}\,\big[r^{2}_{3}+r^{2}_{4}\big]

\displaystyle\ C\,\big[r^{2}_{3}-r^{2}_{4}\big]+D\,\big[r^{2}_{3}+r^{2}_{4}\big]\,,

C

Summary

We have derived the complete elastic second-order hyperfine-interaction correction
of order
\alpha^{6}
to the centroid energy levels
of hydrogen-like and helium-like ions. The resulting formulas have been
verified through comparisons with our all-order numerical calculations for hydrogen-like ions.
Numerical calculations have been performed for the
2^{1}S
and
2^{3}S
states of atomic helium,
extending the previous approximate treatment of this effect by Qi
et al.

[
17
]
.

The obtained results impact the determination of the nuclear charge radii difference between

3
He and
4
He, largely resolving the previously reported disagreement
between the muonic and electronic helium determinations
[
15
,
14
]
.
From the measured isotope shift of the
2^{1}S
–
2^{3}S
transition energy
we obtain the mean square charge radius difference of
3
He and
4
He,
which is only
1.3\,\sigma
away from the muonic value.
Given the difficulty of estimating the nuclear polarizability uncertainties in muonic atoms,
we consider this to be good agreement.

Remarkably, the determination of
\delta r^{2}
from
the electronic helium spectroscopy is four times more accurate than that from the muonic
helium. This is the consequence of the fact that the inelastic nuclear effects for muonic atoms
are much more significant than in the electronic ones.
The observed agreement between electronic and muonic measurements supports the reliability
of nuclear charge radius determinations from muonic atoms, suggesting that they could
be successfully extended to heavier nuclei, which is the objective of the QUARTET collaboration
[
29
]
.

Appendix A
Dimensional regularization

This appendix is based on Ref.
[
22
]
.
In order to extend spin-
\frac{1}{2}
into
d
dimensions, we define antisymmetric tensor

\sigma^{ij}=\frac{i}{2}[\gamma^{i},\gamma^{j}]\,,

(84)

which in the three-dimensional limit simplifies to

\sigma^{ij}\overset{d\rightarrow 3}{=}2\,\epsilon^{ijk}s^{k}.

(85)

Since we consider only corrections to energy and neglect the hyperfine splitting,
we may perform the angular average

\displaystyle\sigma_{a}^{ij}\,\sigma_{b}^{kl}\rightarrow

\displaystyle\ \sigma_{a}^{ij}\,\sigma_{b}^{ij}\,\frac{\delta^{ik}\,\delta^{jl}-\delta^{il}\,\delta^{jk}}{d\,(d-1)}\,\,,

(86)

\displaystyle\sigma_{a}^{ij}\,\sigma_{b}^{il}\rightarrow

\displaystyle\ \sigma_{a}^{ij}\,\sigma_{b}^{ij}\,\frac{\delta^{jl}}{d}\,.

(87)

For
a=b
,

\displaystyle\sigma^{ij}\,\sigma^{ij}=d\,(d-1)\,.

(88)

We will not always have spin 1/2 for the nucleus.
In the dimensional regularization, we will pretend that
I=1/2
and use
\sigma_{N}^{ij}
.
However, instead of the above identity, we will consistently use the notation

\displaystyle\sigma_{N}^{ij}\sigma_{N}^{ij}\equiv 8\,\langle\vec{I}^{\,2}\rangle_{\varepsilon}\,,

(89)

and drop the subscript
\varepsilon
once all
1/\varepsilon
terms are canceled out.

Throughout our calculations, we extensively used the following result for the general
d
-dimensional integral,

\displaystyle\ \Omega_{d}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{2\,\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2)}\,,

(90)

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{d}p}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{4\pi}{p^{n}}e^{i\vec{p}\cdot\vec{r}}=

\displaystyle\ 2^{2-n}\pi^{1-d/2}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{d-n}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)}r^{n-d}\,.

(91)

Two special cases are of particular importance (with
d=3-2\,\varepsilon
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}(r)=

\displaystyle\ \int\frac{d^{d}p}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{4\pi}{p^{2}}e^{i\vec{p}\cdot\vec{r}}=\frac{C_{1}}{r^{1-2\varepsilon}},

(92)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{(2)}(r)=

\displaystyle\ \int\frac{d^{d}p}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{4\pi}{p^{4}}e^{i\vec{p}\cdot\vec{r}}=C_{2}\,r^{1+2\varepsilon}\,,

(93)

where

\displaystyle C_{1}=

\displaystyle\ \pi^{\varepsilon-1/2}\Gamma(1/2-\varepsilon)\,,

(94)

\displaystyle C_{2}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{4}\pi^{\varepsilon-1/2}\Gamma(-1/2-\varepsilon)\,.

(95)

We define also the associated potentials:

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\rho}=

\displaystyle\ 4\pi\int\frac{d^{d}p}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{\tilde{\rho}(p^{2})}{p^{2}}e^{i\vec{p}\cdot\vec{r}},

(96)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\rho}^{(2)}=

\displaystyle\ 4\pi\int\frac{d^{d}p}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{\tilde{\rho}(p^{2})}{p^{4}}e^{i\vec{p}\cdot\vec{r}},

(97)

and in
d=3

\displaystyle{V}_{\rho}=

\displaystyle\ 4\pi\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{\tilde{\rho}(p^{2})}{p^{2}}e^{i\vec{p}\cdot\vec{r}},

(98)

\displaystyle{V}_{\rho}^{(2)}=

\displaystyle\ 4\pi\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{\tilde{\rho}(p^{2})\,e^{i\vec{p}\cdot\vec{r}}-1}{p^{4}}.

(99)

d=3

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}+\text{local terms},

Coarsening dynamics for spiral and nonspiral waves in active Potts models
Hiroshi Noguchi

Abstract
This study examines the domain-growth dynamics of q q -state active Potts models ( q = 3 q=3 – 8 8 ) under the cyclically symmetric conditions using Monte Carlo simulations on square and hexagonal lattices. By imposing active cyclic flipping of states, finite-length waves emerge in the long-term limit. This study focuses on coarsening dynamics from an initially random mixture of states to these moving-domain states. When spiral waves appear in the final state, the correlation length follows the Lifshitz–Allen–Cahn (LAC) law ( ∝ t 1 / 2 \propto t^{1/2} ) until saturation is observed at the characteristic wavelength. By contrast, in the case of nonspiral waves, the growth rate is raised prior to the saturation, leading to a transient increase in the coarsening exponent. Moreover, the mean cluster size exhibits a similar form of transient increase under most of the conditions. In factorized symmetry modes at q = 6 q=6 , domains composed of two or three states similarly follow the LAC law. Finally, this study confirmed that the choice of lattice type (square or hexagonal) and update scheme (Metropolis or Glauber) does not alter the dynamic behavior.



I
Introduction

Domain coarsening is a well-known phenomenon observed during spinodal decomposition in a rapidly quenched systems,
and its dynamics are theoretically well established
Puri and Wadhawan 1982
;
Furukawa 1985
;
Bray 1994
;
Grest et al. 1988
;
Rogers et al. 1988
;
Tanaka and Araki 1997
;
Noguchi and Gompper 2006
;
Ferrero and Cannas 2007
;
Yang et al. 2025
.
In particular, for surface-tension-driving coarsening in a nonconserved order parameter,
the domain length grows proportionally to
t^{1/2}
, following the Lifshitz–Allen–Cahn (LAC) law
Lifshitz 1962
;
Allen and Cahn 1979
. By contrast, diffusion-dominated conserved systems exhibit the growth in accordance with the Lifshitz–Slyozov law (
t^{1/3}
)
Lifshitz and Slyozov 1961
.
However, coarsening dynamics leading to nonequilibrium states have been far less explored
Oppo and Kapral 1987
;
Al Hammal et al. 2005
;
Vazquez and López 2008
;
Latoski et al. 2022
;
Elder et al. 1992
;
Cross and Meiron 1995
;
Dey et al. 2012
;
Mishra et al. 2014
;
Saha et al. 2020
;
Katyal et al. 2020
;
Pattanayak et al. 2021
;
Dittrich et al. 2023
;
Bandyopadhyay et al. 2024
;
Rouzaire et al. 2025
.
Under nonequilibrium conditions, the final states can be nonstatic and manifest as spatiotemporal patterns, such as traveling waves and global oscillations
Nicolis and Prigogine 1977
;
Haken 2004
;
Mikhailov 1994
;
Rabinovich et al. 2000
;
Kuramoto 1984
;
Murray 2003
;
Beta and Kruse 2017
;
Bailles et al. 2022
;
Noguchi 2025a
.
Recently, increasing attention has been directed toward the coarsening to such nonstatic states

;

;
Dey et al. 2012
;
Mishra et al. 2014
;
Saha et al. 2020
;

;

;
Bandyopadhyay et al. 2024
;

.

II
Models and Methods

For the 2D square and hexagonal lattices,
square and rhombus simulation cells with a side length of
L
are employed, respectively, under periodic boundary conditions.
The total number of sites is
N=L^{2}
.
In the
q
-state Potts model, each site possesses a state
s\in[0,q-1]
.
The nearest neighboring sites (
i
and
j
) interact with contact energies
J_{s_{i},s_{j}}
:
Wu 1982
;
Potts 1952

H_{\mathrm{int}}=-\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}J_{s_{i}s_{j}}.

(1)

In the square and hexagonal lattices, each site has four and six nearest neighbors, respectively.
In equilibrium systems, each state may additionally possess self-energy
\varepsilon_{s}
,
and the ratio of the forward and backward flip rates for flipping of a single site from
s
to
s^{\prime}
is given by
\exp(-\Delta H_{s_{i}s^{\prime}_{i}})
, where
\Delta H_{s_{i}s^{\prime}_{i}}=\Delta H_{\mathrm{int}}-h_{s,s^{\prime}}
is the energy difference between the two states,

h_{s,s^{\prime}}=\varepsilon_{s}-\varepsilon_{s}^{\prime}
is the flipping energy,
and the thermal energy
k_{\mathrm{B}}T
is normalized to unity.
At equilibrium, the cyclic sum of the flipping energies is
\sum_{k}^{q}h_{k,[k+1]}=0
, where
[k]
denotes
k\bmod q
.
This model is extended to a nonequilibrium framework, where
\sum_{k}^{q}h_{k,[k+1]}>0
, while maintaining
h_{ss^{\prime}}=-h_{s^{\prime}s}

Noguchi et al. 2024
.
Therefore, the detailed balance can be locally satisfied for flips between
s
and
s^{\prime}
, but not globally over a full cycle (
s=0\to 1\ ...\to(q-1)\to 0
). For
q=3
, this corresponds to the rock–paper–scissors relationship.
Such dynamics can be realized experimentally
Noguchi et al. 2024
;
Noguchi 2025c
;
Noguchi 2025d
, for example, in reactions on a catalytic surface
Ertl 2008
;
Brär et al. 1994
;
Gorodetskii et al. 1994
;
Barroo et al. 2020
;
Zeininger and et al. 2022
and molecular transport across membranes
Tabe and Yokoyama 2003
;
Miele et al. 2020
;
Holló et al. 2021
;
Noguchi 2023
.

In this study, the analysis is restricted to cyclically symmetric conditions: constant flipping energy
h_{k,[k+1]}=h
, and
J_{k,k^{\prime}}
depends solely on the state distance
|k-k^{\prime}|
. For example, a six-state Potts model has three independent contact energy parameters:
J_{k,k}-J_{k,[k+1]}
,
J_{k,[k+2]}-J_{k,[k+1]}
, and
J_{k,[k+3]}-J_{k,[k+1]}
.
A site is randomly selected, and
its flip to a neighboring state (
s=k\to[k+1]
or
[k-1]
) is performed using the Metropolis MC algorithm with the acceptance ratio calculated as
p_{s_{i}s^{\prime}_{i}}=\min[1,\exp(-\Delta H_{s_{i}s^{\prime}_{i}})]
.
This flip is attempted
N
times per MC step (time unit).
We previously verified that the choice between the Metropolis and Glauber schemes for the MC update introduces only a minor difference in the long-term dynamics at
q=3

Noguchi et al. 2024
.
In Sec.
III.6
, we further discuss the effects of the update schemes on the coarsening dynamics at
q=3
and
6
.

In this study,
we set
J_{k,k}=2
and
1.2
for the square and hexagonal lattices, respectively, and
J_{k,[k+1]}=0
for both lattices.
We use the standard Potts models (
J_{k,[k+n]}=0
)
and repulsive nonflip-contact Potts models (
J_{k,[k+n]}=-J_{k,k}
) with
2\leq n\leq q/2

to examine
q
dependence.
Note that these two models are identical at
q=3
.
When
q
is a factorizable number, modes with factorized symmetry can emerge
Noguchi 2025c
.
As representative modes, the coarsening dynamics to two wave modes are simulated with factorized symmetry at
q=6
.
i) M2W3 mode: three types of mixed domains form spiral waves under conditions
J_{k,[k+2]}=0
and
J_{k,[k+3]}\sim 2
.
The neighboring pairs of diagonal states (
s=k
and
[k+3]
)
have stronger attractive interactions than the other pairs of different states, resulting in a mixed domain.
Here, we use
J_{k,[k+3]}=1.5
and refer to this as the attractive diagonal-contact condition.
ii) W3 mode: three odd- or even-numbered states form spiral waves under conditions
J_{k,[k+2]}=0
and
J_{k,[k+3]}\simeq-1
.
These two spiral waves temporally coexist through stochastic switching in the long term,
whereas they coexist spatially during coarsening.
Here, we use
J_{k,[k+3]}=-1
and refer to this as the repulsive diagonal-contact condition.

Domain lengths are calculated from the spatial correlation and mean cluster size (area).
The spatial correlation function
C_{\mathrm{r}}({\mathbf{r}})=(1/N)\sum_{i,j}\delta_{s_{i},s_{j}}\delta({\mathbf{r}}_{i,j}-{\mathbf{r}})
is
calculated along the axial [
(x,y)=(1,0)
and
(0,1)
] and diagonal [
(x,y)=(1,\pm 1)
] directions for the square lattices (see Fig. S1 of the Supplemental Material
SI
).
Three directions [
(x,y)=(1,0),(1/2,\pm\sqrt{3}/2)
] along the three sides of triangles are used for the hexagonal lattices.
The half-relaxation lengths along the axial directions are considered as the correlation lengths.
For single-state dominant domains,
the half-relaxation length
r_{\mathrm{cr1}}
is calculated as
C_{\mathrm{r}}(r_{\mathrm{cr1}})=(1+1/q)/2
,
since
C_{\mathrm{r}}(r)=1/q
at
r\to\infty
.
For domains comprising
n
states,

r_{\mathrm{cr}n}
is calculated as
C_{\mathrm{r}}(r_{\mathrm{cr}n})=(1+n/q)/2
.
The mean cluster size of the
s=k
state is given by

n_{k,\mathrm{cl}}=(\sum_{i_{k,{\mathrm{cl}}}=1}^{N_{k}}{i_{k,\mathrm{cl}}}^{2}n^{k,{\mathrm{cl}}}_{i})/N_{k}
,
where
n^{k,{\mathrm{cl}}}_{i}
is the number of clusters of size
i_{k,\mathrm{cl}}
, and

N_{k}=i_{k,\mathrm{cl}}n^{k,{\mathrm{cl}}}_{i}
is the total number of
s=k
sites.
Cluster sizes are averaged as
n_{\mathrm{cl}}=\langle n_{k,\mathrm{cl}}\rangle
,
where
\langle...\rangle
is the ensemble average for the
q
states and different simulation runs.
The characteristic length based on the domain area is given by
n_{\mathrm{cl}}^{1/2}
.
We primarily used
L=2048
(
N=4~194~304
). To check the finite size effects,
L=1024
was also used at several parameter sets.
Statistical errors are calculated from ten independent runs.
The coarsening exponents
\alpha_{\mathrm{cr}}
and
\alpha_{\mathrm{cl}}
are calculated by fitting functions
r_{\mathrm{cr1}}(t)=b_{1}t^{\alpha_{\mathrm{cr}}}
and
n_{\mathrm{cl}}(t)^{1/2}=b_{2}t^{\alpha_{\mathrm{cl}}}
, respectively.
The sum of the statistical and fitting errors is used for the errors of
\alpha_{\mathrm{cr}}
and
\alpha_{\mathrm{cl}}
.

III
Results and Discussion

III.1
Three-State Potts Model

It is well established that the coarsening dynamics to the thermal equilibrium state (
h=0
) at
q=3

follow the LAC law with the characteristic length scaling as
r\propto t^{1/2}

Bray 1994
;
Grest et al. 1988
.
Our simulations confirm this dynamic law, as shown in Fig.
1
.
Both the correlation length
r_{\mathrm{cr1}}
and cluster length
n_{\mathrm{cl}}^{1/2}
increase as
t^{1/2}
.
The contact probability
p_{\mathrm{cn}}
of different states (proportional to domain boundary length
r_{\mathrm{bd}}
)
follows as
p_{\mathrm{cn}}\propto t^{-1/2}
, because
r_{\mathrm{bd}}\propto N/r_{\mathrm{cr1}}

Ferrero and Cannas 2007
.
The cluster length is saturated to
n_{\mathrm{cl}}^{1/2}\simeq 600
and
1200
at
L=1024
and
2048
, respectively,
owing to the finite-size effect [see Fig.
1
(d)], whereas
r_{\mathrm{cr1}}
does not exhibit such saturation
within the simulated time range [see Fig.
1
(c)].

At
h>0
, spiral waves emerge at
t\to\infty
, where the boundaries between the
s=k
and
[k+1]
domains move ballistically in a rock–paper–scissors manner [see Fig.
1
(b)].
The number density of spirals increases linearly with
h

Noguchi et al. 2024
.
Consequently,
r_{\mathrm{cr1}}
,
n_{\mathrm{cl}}^{1/2}
, and
p_{\mathrm{cn}}
are saturated to finite values,
with saturation occurring earlier at higher
h
.
However, the temporal evolution of
r_{\mathrm{cr1}}
and
p_{\mathrm{cn}}
remains nearly identical to that at
h=0
until
the onset of saturation [see Fig.
1
(c) and (e)].
Hence, the coarsening exponent, obtained by fitting data in the range
2<r_{\mathrm{cr1}}<6
exhibits little dependence on
h

[see Fig.
2
(a)].
Since the exponent
1/2
is the asymptotic value for
t\to\infty
,
the exponent values obtained at finite times are slightly smaller.
Comparable deviations have been reported in previous simulations
Rogers et al. 1988
.

In contrast to
r_{\mathrm{cr1}}
and
p_{\mathrm{cn}}
,

n_{\mathrm{cl}}^{1/2}
exhibits more rapid growth prior to saturation [see Figs.
1
(d) and S2(a)], with the coarsening exponent transiently increasing with
h
[Fig.
2
(b)].
This behavior arises from the elongated crescent shapes of the domains in spiral waves (compare the snapshots in Figs.
1
(a)–(b) at the same time steps).
During this transient regime, the domain morphology evolves from approximately circular to crescent-shaped.
It is rather interesting that
r_{\mathrm{cr1}}
maintains its
t^{1/2}
development in this time range.

The domains in spiral waves have a rectangular shape elongating along the axial directions of the square lattice [
(x,y)=(1,0)
and
(0,1)
], as shown in Fig.
1
(b). As a result, correlation functions along the diagonal directions [
(x,y)=(1,\pm 1)
] decay slightly faster than those along the axial directions [Fig. S1(a)], with the corresponding correlation length being slightly (
\simeq 10
%) smaller [Fig. S2(b)]. However, this geometrical influence on coarsening dynamics is negligible, as discussed in Sec.
III.6
.
Overall, the LAC law is maintained at
q=3
, even under nonequilibrium conditions, except for a transient increase in
n_{\mathrm{cl}}^{1/2}
.

III.2
Standard Potts Models

Next, we describe the coarsening dynamics of the standard Potts models at
q=4
–
8
(Figs.
3
,
4
, and S3–S7).
During coarsening to equilibrium states (
h=0
),

r_{\mathrm{cr1}}
and
n_{\mathrm{cl}}^{1/2}
grow as
t^{1/2}
at
q=4
–
8
, similar to that at
q=3

(see Figs.
3
, S4, S5, S6, and S7 for
q=6
,
4
,
5
,
7
, and
8
, respectively)
Grest et al. 1988
.
The coarsening exponent
\alpha_{\mathrm{cr}}
, calculated for
2<r_{\mathrm{cr1}}<6
, is lower with increasing
q

compared with the exponent calculated for
30<r_{\mathrm{cr1}}<100

[see red solid and black dashed lines in Fig.
4
(a)].
Hence, the approach to the asymptotic value of
1/2
becomes slower with increasing
q
.

At
h>0
, nonspiral waves emerge, and the domains remain approximately circular (see Figs.
3
(a), S4(a), and S7(a) for
q=6
,
4
, and
8
, respectively)
Noguchi 2025b
;
Noguchi 2025c
.
Since flips do not occur at the boundaries between the domains of
s=k
and
[k+n]
with
2\leq n\leq q/2
,
the ballistic motion of domain boundaries occurs only between the
s=k
and
[k+1]
domains; therefore, the waves do not exhibit spiral shapes.
The correlation functions are isotropic [the dashed line of
C_{\mathrm{crd}}
overlaps with the red line of
C_{\mathrm{cr1}}

III.6
Robustness of Coarsening Dynamics

We simulated coarsening on the hexagonal lattice and using the Glauber update scheme to examine the robustness of coarsening dynamics.
It is known that the coarsening dynamics of Potts models at zero temperature
depend on lattice type, with
higher trap ratios into metastable states for square lattices
Olejarz et al. 2013
than for hexagonal lattices
Denholm 2021
.
In the present simulations of square lattices, spiral waves exhibit rectangular shapes elongating in the vertical and horizontal directions [
(x,y)=(0,\pm 1)
and
(\pm 1,0)
].
To examine the effects of this anisotropic domain structure,
simulation of Potts models are performed on hexagonal lattices at
q=3
and
6
.
In these cases, isotropic spiral waves emerge, as shown in Fig.
9
.
Nevertheless, coarsening dynamics closely matches those observed on square lattices (compare Figs. S15 and S16 with Figs.
1
and
5
, respectively),
with differences in the obtained exponent values being within the margin of error (see Figs.
2
and
6
).
Therefore, we conclude that the influence of lattice structures on coarsening dynamics is negligible.

In this study, we used the Metropolis MC update scheme.
Since the Glauber update scheme
p_{s_{i}s^{\prime}_{i}}=1/[1+\exp(\Delta H_{s_{i}s^{\prime}_{i}})]
is also widely used for coarsening dynamics,
we also simulated the three- and six-state Potts models using the Glauber update.
The resulting, coarsening dynamics are similar (Figs. S17 and S18), and the exponent values calculated prior to saturation are slightly smaller than those calculated using the Metropolis scheme (Figs.
2
and
6
). Since the acceptance ratio of the Glauber scheme is lower than that of the Metropolis scheme,
the approach to the asymptotic value is slightly slower.
Therefore, the choice of the update schemes does not result in a significant difference in the coarsening dynamics.

IV
Summary

We studied the coarsening dynamics of
q
-state active Potts models with
q=3
–
8
under cyclically symmetric conditions.
For equilibrium states (
h=0
), coarsening dynamics follows the LAC law (the characteristic length
\propto t^{1/2}
)
as reported in the previous studies.
When active flips (
h>0
) are introduced into the three-state Potts model and repulsive nonflip-contact Potts models with
q=4
–
8
,
spiral waves emerge in the long-term limit.
In such cases, the correlation length follows the LAC law until the onset of saturation to the wave mode,
whereas the cluster length (the root of the mean cluster size) exhibits a growth enhancement prior to saturation.
This discrepancy between the two lengths arises from the elongated crescent shape of spiral domains.
By contrast, in the standard Potts models with
q=4
–
8
, where the final states are nonspiral waves (waves move separately without centers),
a comparable growth enhancement is obtained in the correlation length.
Therefore, the differences in the final spatiotemporal patterns modifies the coarsening dynamics near saturation.

When a factorizable number of states are used for the Potts models, a mode of the factorized symmetry can emerge.
In this study, we examined two types of factorized modes at
q=6
: spiral waves composed of three types of diagonal mixed domains,
and the coexistence of spiral waves comprising odd- and even-numbered states.
In the former and latter cases, large domains comprising two or three states exhibit the coarsening dynamics characteristic of
q=3
spiral waves
and two-phase spinodal decomposition, respectively.

Finally, this study examined the influence of the choice of lattice types (square vs. hexagonal lattices) and MC update schemes (Metropolis vs. Glauber).
Neither choice alters the coarsening dynamic behavior, demonstrating that the obtained dynamics are robust with respect to model and algorithmic details.

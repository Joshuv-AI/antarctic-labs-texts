Sensing of Pb2+ in real samples of dwarf copperleaf and Spinacia oleracea by a novel modified SBA/MWCNTs electrode
Authors: Jayagopi Gayathri, Sivakumar Sivalingam
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Recently, lead (Pb2+) has been detected in various food samples, including vegetables, leaves, meat, and fish. It is challenging to detect Pb2+ in leaves using analytical methods, such as electrochemical sensing techniques. In this research, the newly synthesized composite material of SBA/MWCNTs/GE was used as a modified electrode to ascertain the heavy metal ion (Pb2+) present in dwarf copperleaf and Spinacia oleracea samples in aqueous medium. The specificity of GE contributes to its reproducibility. The surface of MWCNTs provides stability and enhances the current of Pb2+. Preconcentration of Pb2+-SBA/MWCNTs/GE was observed in oxidation and reduction peaks of Pb2+ measured by cyclic voltammetry (CV). The morphology of Pb2+-SBA/MWCNTs and Pb2+-MWCNTs electrodes was analyzed by scanning electron microscope (SEM). The oxidation peaks of Pb2+ were investigated by square-wave anodic stripping voltammetry (SWASV). Using SWASV, the effect of pH, supporting electrolyte, and preconcentration time was examined. The composite material showed good recoveries of 100.1–102.3% by SWASV for dwarf copperleaf and Spinacia oleracea samples. The accuracy of the SWASV method was further validated by comparison with atomic absorption spectroscopy (AAS).

Introduction

Increasing amounts of toxic heavy metals are being released into the environment, where they accumulate in soil and humans.1,2 They are also a major problem because they contaminate waste discharged into wastewater outflows.3,4 Pb2+ and its congeners are difficult to separate from one another and remain stable in soil for extended periods, causing bioaccumulation in agricultural products and trophic transfer to humans.5–7 Even while plants absorb a significant amount of lead ions from the soil, very little of the Pb2+ load is transferred from the roots to the stems and leaves.8–11 Even though the earth's crust contains large amounts of heavy metals, human activities such as mining, smelting, burning coal, storing battery effluents, automobile exhaust, metal plating, tanning leather, finishing operations, and adding fertilizers, pesticides, and additives to paint, gasoline, and other products can increase their concentrations in food, causing them to exceed safe limits.12,13 Consequently, the widespread use of Pb2+ in various parts of the world has led to environmental problems and health risks for people.

Reactive oxygen species (ROS) produced by heavy metals in plant tissues disrupt the antioxidant defense mechanism.14,15 The purpose of these studies was to ascertain the physiological and biochemical traits of spinach (Spinacia oleracea L.) plants.16,17 When spinach plants were grown in contaminated soil rather than control soil, heavy metal contamination resulted in notable decreases in seed germination, seedling biomass, protein, and total nitrogen content.18–20 A wide range of bioactive chemicals with documented antioxidant, antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, hepatoprotective, and anticancer effects may be found in the dwarf copperleaf plant (Alternanthera paronychioides).21,22

Heavy metal ions can be detected using various techniques, such as atomic fluorescence spectroscopy (AFS),23–25 inductively coupled plasma-atomic emission spectrometry (ICP-AES),26–28 and inductively coupled plasma-mass spectrometry (ICP-MS).29 The above techniques provide accurate, sensitive and selective detection of heavy metal ions. However, these techniques are more expensive and require longer analysis times. Recently, research on the detection of many heavy metal ions has increased because sensitive methods such as square wave anodic stripping voltammetry (SWASV), a conventional electrochemical method, enable highly sensitive, rapid and cost-effective analysis.

In recent decades, the sensitivity of SWASV has been influenced by the development of better mercury-free electrode materials. A wide range of materials, such as complexes,30 nanomaterials,31 carbon-based compounds,32 and others, have been used as modifiers in the development of electrochemical sensors. By improving sensitivity, specific surface area, and conductivity, these modifications made SWASV a desirable alternative to traditional analytical techniques with a lower detection limit.33

Schiff base compounds were generated when an imine was created by the reaction of a primary amine with an aldehyde or a ketone.34–36 Schiff bases were prepared by choosing the appropriate amine, which is added to the carbonyl molecule. The presence of N and O donor atoms in the ligand is useful to make a molecule more rigid, symmetrical, and to control the biting angle, which leads to a number of structural variations. SBA ligand as a unique structural properties and ability to adhere to the surface of the electrode, Schiff base materials are frequently employed in electrochemical applications.37

The current study presents a new sensor for the first time, salicylidene-2-aminobenzyl alcohol (SBA)/multiwalled carbon nanotubes (MWCNTs), to detect Pb2+ ions at specific concentrations. Square wave anodic stripping voltammetry (SWASV) has been used to analyze Pb2+ at the SBA/MWCNTs electrode. In comparison to MWCNTs alone, the combination of SBA and MWCNTs demonstrated improved electron transfer behavior and exceptional stripping performance for the detection of Pb2+ at a highly effective rate. Salicylidene-2-aminobenzyl alcohol (SBA) contains N- and O-containing functional groups that chelate with metal cations. The SBA ligand increased the selectivity and sensitivity of the metal ion detection process because of its interaction with the metal ion. Low detection limits were observed in voltammetric measurements using the modified SBA/MWCNTs/graphite electrode as the working electrode. The modified working electrode displays exceptional Pb2+ sensitivity and selectivity. The results are correlated with atomic absorption spectroscopy (AAS), and the proposed method has been successfully applied to analyze Pb2+ in dwarf copperleaf and Spinacia oleracea.

Experimental section

Chemical reagents and instruments

Graphite rods and multiwalled carbon nanotubes (MWCNTs, particle size: 110 nm, purity >90%) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. Salicylaldehyde (99%), 2-aminobenzyl alcohol (98%), acetic acid (≥99.7%), sodium acetate (≥99%), and lead acetate trihydrate (≥99%) were purchased from SRL Pvt. Ltd, India. Throughout the experiment, analytical-grade chemicals and doubly distilled water were utilized. The CHI 660B Analyzer was used to measure the square wave anodic stripping voltammetry. Conventional three-electrode cells utilize a working electrode modified by SBA/MWCNTs. The HITACHI S3400N device was used to perform SEM and EDX analyses.

Salicylidene-2-aminobenzyl alcohol (SBA) synthesis

With minor modifications, the SBA ligand was prepared using a previously reported method.38,46 0.04 mmol of salicylaldehyde and 0.04 mmol of 2-aminobenzyl alcohol were combined with 30 mL of ethanolic solvent. A yellow-greenish precipitate was obtained by mechanically stirring the aforementioned mixture for 2 h. It was filtered, washed repeatedly with ethanol, and dried for 30 minutes in an oven. The synthesis mechanism is shown in Fig. 1.

Synthesis of salicylidene-2-aminobenzyl alcohol (SBA) ligand.

Development of a modified working electrode utilizing SBA/MWCNTs

Graphite electrodes (GE) saturated with paraffin wax have been utilized for electrode modification, as previously described in our studies.39,41 After polishing the electrodes with emery paper, they were cleaned with distilled water, dried in the air, and covered with 0.5 µm alumina silica. 10 µL of the 0.1 mg MWCNTs were dropcast onto the GE surface after being dissolved in 1 mL of ethanol. After dissolving the SBA ligand in 1 mM acetonitrile, 10 µL of the ligand was drop-cast onto the electrode surface modified by MWCNTs and allowed to air dry.

Utilizing the real samples of Spinacia oleracea and dwarf copperleaf

Sample-1 (dwarf copperleaf) and sample-2 (Spinacia oleracea) were prepared. 10 g of each sample (dwarf copperleaf and Spinacia oleracea) were individually submerged in distilled water, prepared at room temperature, and filtered. The filtrate was then utilized for further studies. The samples (1 and 2) were then combined with lead ions. Lead acetate (0.06 g) is mixed with 50 cc of the sample. As a result, the samples are utilized for Pb2+ ion measurement using square wave anodic voltammetry (SWASV), and atomic absorption spectroscopy (AAS) is performed to compare the obtained values.

Analysis of Pb2+ ion

For 180 s, the SBA/MWCNTs/GE electrode was submerged in 0.1 M acetate (pH 5.5) containing 26 µg per L Pb2+, with mechanical stirring. The electrode was submerged in a freshly prepared 0.1 M acetate solution. On the electrode surface, Pb2+ was reduced for 90 s at −1.2 V. Anodic oxidation at potentials between −1.2 and −0.2 V results in Pb2+ reduction.

The Rct values for GE, MWCNTs/GE, SBA/MWCNTs/GE, and Pb2+-SBA/MWCNTs/GE MWCNTs electrodes were 799 Ω, 290 Ω, 217 Ω, and 145 Ω, respectively. The schematic representation of the modified Randle's equivalent circuit for the electrodes is shown in the inset (Fig. 3). In the circuit, Rct is the charge-transfer resistance, Rs is the solution resistance, and Cdl is the capacitance. The Rct of Pb2+-SBA/MWCNTs/GE is smaller than that of SBA/MWCNTs/GE, MWCNTs/GE, and GE, further confirming the better conductivity of the SDA/MWCNTs modified electrode.

Optimization of experimental conditions

Effect of supporting electrolytes

The influence of supporting electrolyte on the preconcentration of 37 µg L−1 of Pb2+ on SBA/MWCNTs/GE modified electrode was studied using different electrolytes such as KNO3, HNO3, NaNO3, and acetate buffer. Fig. 5A shows the stripping voltammograms for Pb2+, which was preconcentrated from these different electrolytes. It is evident from the figure that 0.1 M acetate buffer is the most suitable medium for the preconcentration of Pb2+. Hence, in subsequent experiments, 0.1 M acetate buffer was used for the preconcentration of Pb2+.

The influence of supporting electrolytes: pH (B) and preconcentration time (C) for 37 µg L−1 of Pb2+ ion at the modified electrode; 0.1 M solutions (A) of (a) NH4NO3, (b) KNO3, (c) NaNO3, and (d) acetate buffer solution.

Effect of pH

The effect of pH on the preconcentration of Pb2+ on the modified electrode in 0.1 M acetate buffer solution containing 37 µg L−1 of Pb2+ was studied in the pH range 4.0 to 6.5, and the results obtained are shown in Fig. 5B. Maximum stripping current was observed at pH 5.5. In the lower pH conditions, the protonation of the SBA ligand could occur and might result in poor stability for the metal complex, while at high pH, the metal could form hydroxides. Hence, pH 5.5 was considered the most suitable pH for the preconcentration of Pb2+ on the electrode surface. Hence, subsequent experiments have been carried out using a 0.1 M acetate buffer solution at pH 5.5.

Accumulation time

The effect of preconcentration time on the electrochemical response for Pb2+ measurement was also investigated by SWASV in a 0.1 M acetate buffer solution containing Pb2+. The various stripping currents of Pb2+ were studied at different time intervals from 60 to 360 s, and the obtained data are given in Fig. 5C. The figure shows that the stripping currents increased sharply up to 180 s, and then decreased slightly up to 360 s. Therefore, 180 s was chosen as the optimal time for sensing Pb2+ ions.

Electrochemical sensing of Pb2+

The parameters of SBA/MWCNTs for different concentrations of Pb2+ using SWASV are shown in Fig. 6A. The observed anodic peak shows a linear increase with an increase in the concentration of Pb2+. The calibration graph is shown in Fig. 6B. Different concentrations ranging from 3 to 105 µg L−1, with a correlation coefficient of 0.99 for Pb2+, were obtained. The linear regression equation is ip/µA = 0.0139x + 0.520 µg L−1 for Pb2+, and the limit of detection was 1 ng per L Pb2+. The sensitivity of Pb2+ is 0.0148 µA µM−1.

Reproducibility and stability measurement

The reproducibility and long-term stability of the SBA/MWCNTs modified electrode towards Pb2+ detection were evaluated using SWASV. The stability of the modified electrode was examined over a period of one month under ambient conditions. The voltammetric currents of 28 µg L−1 of Pb2+ in 0.1 M acetate buffer (pH 5.5) with the modified electrode were found to be almost constant (Fig. 8C). The relative standard deviation (RSD) values for Pb2+ were determined to be 2.0%, and no variations in the voltammetric currents were taken into consideration. The results showed that the redesigned electrode exhibits good stability for the electrochemical determination of Pb2+. It was observed that the voltammetric currents for 28 µg per L Pb2+ in 0.1 M acetate buffer (pH 5.5) with the modified electrode remained nearly constant for 28 days. No changes in the voltammetric currents were observed, and the relative standard deviation (RSD) values for Pb2+ were calculated to be 2.0%. The result indicated that the modified electrode exhibits favorable stability for the repetitive electrochemical determination of Pb2+. The reproducibility of the developed electrode, five SBA/MWCNT-modified working electrodes in 0.1 M acetate buffer (pH 5.5) solution containing 28 µg L−1 of Pb2+, is shown in Fig. 8A and B, which present the bar diagram of the reproducibility of the five electrodes. The RSD was 1.2% for Pb2+. Hence, the SBA/MWCNTs modified working electrode shows better stability and reproducibility for the voltammetric measurement of Pb2+.

Investigative application to real samples

Pb2+ ions were found in a variety of heavy metal samples using the SBA/MWCNTs modified electrode. Samples 1 (dwarf copperleaf) and 2 (Spinacia oleracea) were collected at the Avadi market in Tamil Nadu, India. The samples were filtered and diluted 1 : 100 with 0.1 M acetate at pH 5.5. SWASV conducted the three tests of the metal ions in the different samples. Table 3 and SWASV analysis of Pb2+ in Spinacia oleracea (Fig. 9B) and dwarf copperleaf (Fig. 9A) provided the observed values. Atomic absorption spectrometry (AAS) was used to confirm the results; for Pb2+, the RSD values were 0.9% and 2.3%. The results show good recovery when Pb2+ is present for analysis. All samples showed a good recovery (between 100.1% and 102.3%). Therefore, this prepared method can be employed to measure Pb2+ ions in dwarf copperleaf and Spinacia oleracea.

Conclusion

For the highly sensitive electrochemical determination of Pb2+, a novel Schiff base ligand (SBA)-functionalized multi-walled carbon nanotube (SBA/MWCNTs) modified electrode was effectively created. By offering numerous Pb2+ binding sites through the imine (–CHN–) and hydroxyl (–OH) functional groups, the Schiff base ligand and MWCNTs worked in concert to greatly improve electrochemical performance. Meanwhile, the MWCNT network enhanced electron-transfer kinetics, electrical conductivity, and electrode stability. The advantageous function of the Schiff base ligand in Pb2+ preconcentration was therefore confirmed by the SBA/MWCNTs electrode, showing a significantly larger stripping current than the MWCNTs-modified electrode. The sensor demonstrated exceptional sensitivity for trace Pb2+ analysis, achieving a good detection limit of 1 ng L−1 under optimised SWASV conditions. By demonstrating satisfactory analytical performance in determining Pb2+ in dwarf copperleaf and Spinacia oleracea samples, the developed sensor's practical application was effectively confirmed, demonstrating its reliability for real-sample analysis. All things considered, the suggested SBA/MWCNTs sensing platform offers a simple, sensitive, and affordable method for detecting harmful lead exposure in environmental and food samples.

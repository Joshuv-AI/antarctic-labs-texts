Abstract
. Both the function and its normal derivative on the element boundary are Q k Q_{k} polynomials for the Bogner-Fox-Schmit C 1 C^{1} - Q k Q_{k} finite element functions. Mathematically, to keep the optimal order of approximation, their spaces are required to include P k P_{k} and P k − 1 P_{k-1} polynomials respectively. We construct a Bell type C 1 C^{1} - Q k Q_{k} finite element on rectangular meshes in 2D and 3D, which has its normal derivative as a Q k − 1 Q_{k-1} polynomial on each face, for k ≥ 4 k\geq 4 . We show, with a big reduction of the space, the C 1 C^{1} - Q k Q_{k} Bell finite element retains the optimal order of convergence. Numerical experiments are performed, comparing the new elements with the original elements.

2.
The 2D
C^{1}
-
Q_{k}
Bell finite elements

Let
\mathcal{Q}_{h}=\{T\}
be a square-of-size-
h
mesh in 2D,
or a cube-of-size-
h
mesh in 3D, on the polygonal or polyhedral
domain
\Omega
in (
1.1
).

On a square or a cube, we define, for
k\geq 4
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle V_{T}=\{v\in Q_{k}(T):\partial_{{\bf n}}v|_{e}\in Q_{k-1}(e),\ e\in\partial T\},

where
\partial_{{\bf n}}
denotes a normal derivative on the edge or the face-square
e
,
and
Q_{k}=\hbox{\rm{span}}\{x_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots x_{d}^{k_{d}}:0\leq k_{i}\leq k\}
for
d=1,2,3
.

For 2-dimensional
V_{T}
on a size
h
square
T
,
the degrees of freedom of the Bell element are defined by, cf.
Figure
2
,

(2.2)

\displaystyle F_{m}(v)=\begin{cases}v,&\hbox{\rm{at }}\ {\bf x}_{1}+\frac{h}{k-2}(i,j),\;i,j=0,\dots,k-2,\\
\partial_{x}v,&\hbox{\rm{at }}\ {\bf x}_{1}+h(i,\frac{j}{k-3}),\;i=0,1,\ j=0,\dots,k-3,\\
\partial_{y}v,&\hbox{\rm{at }}\ {\bf x}_{1}+h(\frac{i}{k-3},j),\;i=0,\dots,k-3,\ j=0,1,\\
\partial_{xy}v,&\hbox{\rm{at }}\ {\bf x}_{1}+h(i,j),\;i,j=0,1.\end{cases}

The degrees of freedom of the

-

).

Lemma 2.1
.

The set of degrees of freedom in (
2.2
) uniquely define a
V_{T}
function in
(
2.1
).

Proof.

The number of degrees of freedom in (
2.2
) is

(2.3)

\displaystyle N_{d}

\displaystyle=(k-1)^{2}+2(2(k-2))+4.

The number of constraint equations in (
2.1
) is, one derivative restriction each
edge,

(2.4)

\displaystyle N_{e}

\displaystyle=4.

Adding (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), we get

\displaystyle N_{d}+N_{e}

\displaystyle=(k-1)^{2}+4(k-1)+4=(k+1)^{2}=\dim Q_{k}.

Thus, to determine a
v_{h}\in V_{T}
, we have a square system of linear equations of
(k+1)^{2}

unknowns.
The uniqueness implies the existence of the solution.

Let
v_{h}\in V_{T}
and
F_{m}(v_{h})=0
for all dofs in (
2.2
). We need to show
v_{h}=0
.

Because
v
vanishes at
k-1
points on an edge
{\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{2}
and
\partial_{x}v
vanishes at
the two end points, the degree-
k
polynomial
v|_{{\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{2}}=0
.
Which implies

\displaystyle v_{h}=(y-({\bf x}_{1})_{2})q_{k,k-1}

for some
\ q_{k,k-1}\in\hbox{\rm{span}}\{x^{i}y^{j},\ 0\leq i\leq k,\ 0\leq j\leq k-1\}
,
where
({\bf x}_{1})_{2}
is the
y
-coordinate of
{\bf x}_{1}
.

Because the degree
k-1
polynomial
\partial_{y}v|_{{\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{2}}
vanishes at
k-2
points on

{\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{2}
and its tangential derivative (
\partial_{xy}v_{h}
) vanishes at
the two end points, we
\partial_{y}v|_{{\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{2}}=0
.
Thus, we can factor out another factor that

\displaystyle v_{h}=(y-({\bf x}_{1})_{2})^{2}q_{k,k-2}

for some
\ q_{k,k-2}\in\hbox{\rm{span}}\{x^{i}y^{j},\ 0\leq i\leq k,\ 0\leq j\leq k-2\}
.

Repeating the calculation on the four edges, we have

\displaystyle v_{h}=(x-({\bf x}_{1})_{1})^{2}(x-({\bf x}_{3})_{1})^{2}(y-({\bf x}_{1})_{2})^{2}(y-({\bf x}_{3})_{2})^{2}q_{k-4}

for some
\ q_{k-4}\in Q_{k-4}
. Evaluating
v_{h}
at the inner
(k-3)^{2}

points inside
T
, cf. Figure
2
, we conclude that
q_{k-4}=0
and
v_{h}=0
.
The lemma is proved.
∎

Lemma 3.1
.

The set of degrees of freedom in (
3.1
) uniquely define a
V_{T}
function in
(
2.1
).

Proof.

The number of degrees of freedom in (
3.1
) is

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} N_{d}&=64+12(4k-15)+6((k-4)^{2}+(k-3)^{2})+(k-3)^{3}\\
&=k^{3}+3k^{2}-9k+7.\end{aligned}

To reduce a 2D
Q_{k}
polynomial’s degree by 1, we need to post
2k-1
constraints for

x^{k},x^{k}y,\dots,x^{k}y^{k},\dots,xy^{k}
and
y^{k}
.
Thus the number of constraint equations in (
2.1
) is,

(3.3)

\displaystyle N_{e}

\displaystyle=6(2k-1).

Adding (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), we get

\displaystyle N_{d}+N_{e}

\displaystyle=k^{3}+3k^{2}+3k+1

\displaystyle=(k+1)^{3}=\dim Q_{k}.

Thus, to determine a
v_{h}\in V_{T}
in 3D,
we have a square system of linear equations of
(k+1)^{3}
unknowns.
The uniqueness implies the existence of the solution.

Let
v_{h}\in V_{T}
and
F_{m}(v_{h})=0
for all dofs in (
3.1
). We need to show
v_{h}=0
.
It would use the proof in 2D several times.

On the top square
{\bf x}_{5}{\bf x}_{6}{\bf x}_{7}{\bf x}_{8}
, the restriction of
v_{h}
is a 2D Bell
C^{1}
-
Q_{k}
element.
By the degrees of freedom
\{v({\bf n}_{l}^{(0)}),\partial_{x}v({\bf n}_{l}^{(1)}),\partial_{y}v({\bf n}_{l}^{(2)}),\partial_{xy}v({\bf n}_{l}^{(4)})\}
and Lemma
2.1
,
we have
v_{h}|_{{\bf x}_{5}{\bf x}_{6}{\bf x}_{7}{\bf x}_{8}}=0
and

\displaystyle v_{h}=(z-({\bf x}_{5})_{3})q_{k,k,k-1}

for some
q_{k,k,k-1}\in\hbox{\rm{span}}\{x^{i}y^{j}z^{l},\ 0\leq i,j\leq k,\ 0\leq l\leq k-1\}
.

On the top square
{\bf x}_{5}{\bf x}_{6}{\bf x}_{7}{\bf x}_{8}
, the restriction of
\partial_{z}v_{h}
is a 2D
BFS
C^{1}
-
Q_{k-1}
element because of the normal derivative restriction in (
2.1
).
By the degrees of freedom
\{\partial_{z}v({\bf n}_{l}^{(3)}),\partial_{xz}v({\bf n}_{l}^{(5)}),\partial_{yz}v({\bf n}_{l}^{(6)}),\partial_{xyz}v({\bf n}_{l}^{(7)})\}
and
the uni-solvency of the 2D BFS element,
we have
\partial_{z}v_{h}|_{{\bf x}_{5}{\bf x}_{6}{\bf x}_{7}{\bf x}_{8}}=0
and

\displaystyle v_{h}=(z-({\bf x}_{5})_{3})^{2}q_{k,k,k-2}

for some
q_{k,k,k-2}\in\hbox{\rm{span}}\{x^{i}y^{j}z^{l},\ 0\leq i,j\leq k,\ 0\leq l\leq k-2\}
.

Repeating above analysis on the other five face squares, we obtain

(3.4)

\displaystyle v_{h}=b^{2}q_{k-4}

for some
q_{k-4}\in Q_{k-4}
, where
b=(x-x_{1})(x_{1}+h-x)(y-y_{1})(y_{1}+h-y)(z-z_{1})(z_{1}+h-z)
and
{\bf x}_{1}=(x_{1},y_{1},z_{1})
.

For the
Q_{k-4}
polynomial
q_{k-4}
in (
3.4
),
it and
v_{h}
vanish at
(k-3)^{3}
internal points, defined in (
3.1
).
Thus
q_{k-4}=0
and
v_{h}=0
.
The lemma is proved.
∎

4.
The finite element solution and convergence

The
C^{1}
-
Q_{k}
(
k\geq 4
) Bell finite element spaces are defined by

(4.1)

\displaystyle V_{h}=\{v_{h}\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega):v_{h}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}\phi_{i}\},

where
\{\phi_{i}\}
is a basis of the global space which combines the local basis functions
dual to the degrees of freedom (
2.2
) or (
3.1
).

The finite element discretization of the biharmonic equation (
1.1
) reads:
Find
u\in V_{h}
such that

(4.2)

\displaystyle(\Delta u,\Delta v)=(f,v)\quad\forall v\in V_{h},

where
V_{h}
is defined in (
4.1
).

Lemma 4.1
.

The finite element equation
(
4.2
) has a unique solution.

Proof.

By letting
v_{h}=u_{h}
and
f=0
in the linear equation
(
4.2
), we get
\Delta u_{h}=0
on every
T
.
Because
u_{h}\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
,
u_{h}
is a global solution of the Laplace equation,

\displaystyle\Delta u=0\ \hbox{\rm{in }}\ \Omega;\quad u=0\ \hbox{\rm{on }}\ \partial\Omega.

By the uniqueness of the solution of the Laplace equation,
u_{h}=0
.
Thus, (
4.2
) has a unique solution.
∎

In order to have the optimal order
L^{2}
convergence, we need to assume the
domain
\Omega
in (
1.1
) is regular enough, for example, a square or a
cube, such that

(4.3)

\displaystyle|u|_{4}\leq C\|f\|_{0}.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
u\in H^{k+1}\cap H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
be
the exact solution of the biharmoic equation (
1.1
).
Let
u_{h}
be the
C^{1}
-
P_{k}
finite element solution of (
4.2
).
Assuming the full-regularity (
4.3
), it holds

\displaystyle\|u-u_{h}\|_{0}+h^{2}|u-u_{h}|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{k+1}|u|_{k+1},\quad k\geq 4.

Proof.

The proof is standard. A proof can be found in
[
22
]
.

Subtracting (
4.2
) from (
1.1
), we get

\displaystyle(\Delta(u-u_{h}),\Delta v_{h})=0\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}.

In
H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
, the
H^{2}
semi-norm
|u|_{2}
is equivalent to the norm
\|\Delta u\|_{0}
.
For simplicity, we denote
\|\Delta u\|_{0}
by
|u|_{2}
, i.e., omitting the
norm equivalence constants.
Applying the Schwarz inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle|u-u_{h}|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=(\Delta(u-u_{h}),\Delta(u-I_{h}u))

\displaystyle\leq|u-u_{h}|_{2}|u-I_{h}u|_{2}\leq Ch^{k-1}|u|_{k+1}|u-u_{h}|_{2},

where
I_{h}u
is the nodal interpolation defined by the degrees of freedom, i.e,

\displaystyle I_{h}u|_{T}=\sum_{n=1}^{N_{T}}F_{n}(u)\phi_{n}

with
F_{n}
and
\phi_{n}
defined in (
2.2
) or (
3.1
) and
their dual basis functions, respectively.
As
V_{T}\supset P_{k}(T)
in (
2.1
),

I_{h}
preserves
P_{k}
polynomials locally.
We can use the spline theory in
[
10
]
to prove the
H^{2}
-stability of
I_{h}
.
Or we can modify the theory of

[
9
]
to prove the
H^{2}
-stability of
I_{h}
.
Then It is routing to get
the quasi-optimal approximation of the operator, cf.
[
13
]
.

To prove the
L^{2}
convergence, we introduce a dual problem,

(4.4)

\displaystyle(\Delta w,\Delta v)

\displaystyle=(u-u_{h},v),\quad w\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega),\ \forall v\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega).

Thus, by (
4.4
),

\displaystyle\|u-u_{h}\|_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle=(\Delta w,\Delta(u-u_{h}))=(\Delta(w-w_{h}),\Delta(u-u_{h}))

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}|w|_{4}h^{k-1}|u|_{n}\leq Ch^{k+1}|u|_{k+1}\|u-u_{h}\|_{0},

where
w_{h}
is the finite element solution to the equation (
4.4
).
We obtain the
L^{2}
error bound. The theorem is proved.
∎

Regulators of the Fixed Elliptic Curve over Rank-One Imaginary Quadratic Fields
Shenghao Hua

Abstract
. We establish a one-sided central limit theorem for the logarithms of regulators of a fixed rational non-CM elliptic curve E E over imaginary quadratic fields of rank one, motivated by the Gross–Zagier formula and a result of Radziwiłł and Soundararajan. We also establish the existence of many imaginary quadratic fields whose regulators grow at least as fast as the square root of the conductor of the field. When the central value of the L L -function of E E is non-vanishing, these results also hold for the regulator of the quadratic twist E ( d ) E^{(d)} over ℚ \mathbb{Q} . Moreover, assuming the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture, we obtain the uniform boundedness of the Shafarevich–Tate groups and Tamagawa numbers for these rank-one quadratic twists.

1.
Introduction

The field of arithmetic statistics is a fast-moving area of number theory, the goal of arithmetic statistics is to understand the average behavior of families of arithmetic objects such as elliptic curves and number fields.
Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over the rational numbers
\mathbb{Q}
, and let
K
be a number field containing
\mathbb{Q}
. The base change of
E
to
K
allows us to consider the group of
K
-rational points
E(K)
.
When
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
is an imaginary quadratic field with square-free integer
d<0
, the base change of
E
from
\mathbb{Q}
to
K
lifts its classical modular form to a Bianchi modular form over
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
via Langlands base change theory. These base change forms have a clear structural role within Bianchi modular forms, helping to better understand modularity and Galois representations of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
, and linking classical modular forms to the Langlands program.
On the other hand, extending the base field to
K
typically increases the set of
K
-rational points on
E
, and arithmetic invariants such as the fundamental units of the Mordell–Weil group may change, thereby enriching the curve’s arithmetic properties.

The regulator
\Reg(E/K)
of
E
over
K
is defined as the volume of a fundamental domain for the maximal free subgroup of its Mordell–Weil group over
K
, calculated using the Néron–Tate canonical height pairing relative to
K
, which is invariant modulo torsion.
It is a fundamental arithmetic invariant that can be nontrivial only when the Mordell–Weil group has positive rank, i.e., positive algebraic rank.
It is natural to ask:
How does the regulator vary as the imaginary quadratic field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
changes?

Specifically, we consider the case when the algebraic rank is 1; in this situation, the regulator coincides with the Néron–Tate height of the fundamental unit in
E(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))
.

Here, we take the central point to be
s=1
.
For
\operatorname{Re}s>\frac{3}{2}
, the normalized Hasse–Weil
L
-function of
E
over
\mathbb{Q}
is

L(s,E)=L(s,E/\mathbb{Q}):=\prod_{p\nmid N}\left(1-a_{p}p^{-s}+p^{1-2s}\right)^{-1}\prod_{p\mid N}L_{p}(p^{-s})^{-1}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{n^{s}},

where
a_{p}=p+1-\#E(\mathbb{F}_{p})
for primes
p
of good reduction, and the local factors
L_{p}(p^{-s})
at bad primes are determined by the type of reduction of
E
at
p
.
From Hasse’s bound, we know that

|a_{p}|\leq 2\sqrt{p}.

The completed
L
-function of
E/\mathbb{Q}
is defined by

\Lambda(s,E)=N^{s/2}(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)L(s,E),

where
N
is the conductor of
E
. Thanks to the modularity theorem proved by Wiles and Taylor, with crucial contributions from Breuil, Conrad, and Diamond,
\Lambda(E,s)
admits analytic continuation to the entire complex plane and satisfies the functional equation

\Lambda(s,E)=w\,\Lambda(2-s,E),

with
w=\pm 1
the root number.

Any real primitive character to the modulus
n
must be of the form
\chi_{n}(\nu)=(\frac{n}{\nu})
where
n
is a fundamental discriminant
[
12
, Theorem 9.13]
, i.e., a product of pairwise coprime integers of the form
-4
,
\pm 8
,
(-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}p
where
p
is an odd prime.
Given a fundamental discriminant
d
, the quadratic twist
E^{(d)}
over
\mathbb{Q}
has Hasse–Weil
L
-function

L(s,E^{(d)})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}\left(\frac{d}{n}\right)}{n^{s}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}\chi_{d}(n)}{n^{s}}.

The completed
L
-function of
E^{(d)}
is defined by

\Lambda(s,E^{(d)})=N_{d}^{s/2}(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)L(s,E^{(d)}),

where
N_{d}
is the conductor of the twist
E^{(d)}
.
When
(d,2N)=1
,
N_{d}=d^{2}N
.
We also have

\Lambda(s,E^{(d)})=w_{d}\,\Lambda(2-s,E^{(d)}),

with root number

(1.1)

w_{d}=w\chi_{d}(-N).

For
d<0
a fundamental discriminant, the base change
L
-function satisfies

L(s,E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))=L(s,E)\cdot L(s,E^{(d)}).

For
\epsilon=\pm 1
, let

\Omega_{\epsilon}:=\{d:\textrm{fundamental discriminant }d<0,~(d,2N)=1,~w\chi_{d}(-N)=\epsilon\}.

Let
N_{0}=[8,N]
.
Let
a\bmod N_{0}
be a residue class, define

\Omega_{\epsilon}(a):=\{d\in\Omega_{\epsilon}:\ d\equiv a\pmod{N_{0}},\ d<0\}.

We have the following theorems.

Theorem 1.1

(One-sided central limit theorem)
.

Let
E
be a non-CM elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with conductor
N
and root number
w
, and the vanishing order of the
L(s,E)
at the central point is at most 1.
Fix a residue class
a\bmod N_{0}
be a residue class such that
a
is a quadratic residue modulo
N
,
a\equiv 1
or
5\pmod{8}
,
and for any fundamental discriminant
d<0
with
d\equiv a\pmod{N_{0}}
,
w\chi_{d}(-N)=\epsilon
.
Let
V\in\mathbb{R}
.
For large
X
we have

\Bigg|\Bigg\{d\in\Omega_{-w}(a)\mid 20<-d\leq X,~L^{\prime}(1,E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))\neq 0,\\
\frac{\log\left(\Reg(E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))/\sqrt{|d|}\right)-\tfrac{w}{2}\log\log|d|}{\sqrt{\log\log|d|}}\geq V\Bigg\}\Bigg|

is at most

\left|\{d\in\Omega_{-w}(a):-d\leq X\}\right|\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{V}^{\infty}e^{-x^{2}/2}\,dx+o(1)\right).

Moreover, when
L(1,E)\neq 0
, this result also applies to
\Reg(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})
.

It is known that, in this setting, the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH) implies that the quantity
\Reg(E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))/\sqrt{d}
is
O(|d|^{\varepsilon})
for any
\varepsilon>0
, whereas Theorem
1.1
shows that, with probability 1,
\log\left(\Reg(E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))/\sqrt{|d|}\right)
does not exceed
\tfrac{w+o(1)}{2}\log\log|d|
.

Motivated by the Katz–Sarnak philosophy and the Keating–Snaith conjecture arising from random matrix models, one can similarly study the central derivatives of the family of quadratic twists of elliptic curve
L
-functions.
We conjecture that, with probability 1,

\log\left(\Reg(E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))/\sqrt{|d|}\right)

is also bounded below by
\tfrac{w-o(1)}{2}\log\log|d|
.
Under GRH, it may be possible to prove this result, as well as a full version of the central limit theorem, following the approach in
[
15
]
.
Here we can unconditionally show that there exist infinitely many imaginary quadratic fields
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
for which
\Reg(E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))
satisfies this condition.

Theorem 1.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, there exist positive constants
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}
such that for any small
\varepsilon>0
and sufficiently large
X
, we have

\left|\left\{d\in\Omega_{1}(a)\mid 20<-d\leq X,\ \Reg(E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))\geq c_{1}\sqrt{|d|}\right\}\right|\gg X^{1-\varepsilon},

\left|\left\{d\in\Omega_{-1}(a)\mid 20<-d\leq X,\ \Reg(E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))\geq\left(c_{2}\log|d|+c_{3}\right)\sqrt{|d|}\right\}\right|\gg X^{1-\varepsilon}.

Moreover, when
L(1,E)\neq 0
, this result also applies to
\Reg(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})
.

Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
will be established in the next section using the Gross–Zagier formula and a result of Radziwiłł and Soundararajan, along with the distribution of quadratic twist
L
-functions or their derivatives.
Moreover, assuming the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture, we obtain the uniform boundedness of the Shafarevich–Tate groups and Tamagawa numbers for these rank-one quadratic twists, see Theorem
2.6
.

2.
Gross–Zagier theory

To impose a constraint on
\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))
, we consider the quadratic twist
E^{(d)}
, which is another elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
, defined (up to isomorphism) by:

E^{(d)}:y^{2}=x^{3}+ad^{2}x+bd^{3},

if
E
is given by
y^{2}=x^{3}+ax+b
.

Let Galois group
G=\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})/\mathbb{Q})=\{1,\sigma\}
.
Then
E(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))
decomposes as a direct sum of two subgroups:

E(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))=E^{+}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))\oplus E^{-}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})),

where

E^{+}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))=\{P\in E(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})):\sigma(P)=P\}=E(\mathbb{Q})

is the subgroup fixed by
G
, and

E^{+}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))=\{P\in E(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})):\sigma(P)=-P\}\cong E^{(d)}(\mathbb{Q}).

Thus we have

\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))=\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb{Q})+\operatorname{rank}E^{(d)}(\mathbb{Q}).

For an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
, when the vanishing order of its
L
-function at the central point, i.e., the analytic rank, is at most 1, then the results of Gross–Zagier
[
5
]
and Kolyvagin
[
10
]
imply that the analytic rank coincides with the algebraic rank.
Therefore, for
i=0,1
, if the analytic rank of
E
over
\mathbb{Q}
is
i
, we require that the analytic rank of its quadratic twist
E^{(d)}
over
\mathbb{Q}
be
1-i
.

The Gross–Zagier formula provides a bridge connecting it to the derivative of the
L
-function at the central point.
Let
f_{E}\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
be the normalized newform associated to
E
via modularity.
Let
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
satisfying the Heegner hypothesis: every prime dividing
N
splits in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
, i.e.
d
is a quadratic residue modulo
[4,N]
.
Let
\phi_{E}\colon X_{0}(N)\to E
be a modular parametrization of
E
. Then one can construct a Heegner point
P_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})}\in E(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))
as follows.
Let
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})}
be an order of discriminant
d
. Consider the set of CM points on the modular curve
X_{0}(N)
parameterizing isomorphism classes of pairs
(E^{\prime},C)
, where
E^{\prime}
is an elliptic curve with complex multiplication by
\mathcal{O}
, and
C\subset E^{\prime}[N]
is a cyclic subgroup of order
N
that is stable under the action of
\mathcal{O}
. These CM points are defined over the Hilbert class field
H
of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
, and the Galois action on them factors through
\mathrm{Gal}(H/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))\cong\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal{O})
, the ideal class group.
Let
z\in X_{0}(N)(H)
be such a CM point, and define

P_{K}:=\mathrm{Tr}_{H/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})}(\phi_{E}(z))\in E(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})),

the trace of the image of
z
under the modular parametrization. Then
P_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})}
is rational over
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
and is called a Heegner point on
E
over
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
.
Then the celebrated Gross–Zagier formula tells us the following:

Lemma 2.1

(Gross–Zagier formula over imaginary quadratic fields
[
5
]
)
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be a non-CM elliptic curve of conductor
N
, and
let
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
satisfying the Heegner hypothesis.
Assume that
\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))=1
, so that
P_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})}
is of infinite order. Then the derivative of the Hasse–Weil
L
-function of
E
over
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
satisfies

L^{\prime}(1,E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))=\frac{32\pi^{2}\,\|f_{E}\|^{2}}{|\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})}^{\times}|^{2}\,\sqrt{|d|}\cdot\deg\phi_{E}}\cdot\widehat{h}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})}(P_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})}),

where:

•

\|f_{E}\|^{2}
is the Petersson norm of
f_{E}
, defined as

\|f_{E}\|^{2}=\int_{\Gamma_{0}(N)\backslash\mathbb{H}}|f_{E}(z)|^{2}\,y^{2}\,\frac{dx\,dy}{y^{2}},

•

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})}^{\times}
is the unit group of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
, and for
d<-3
, this group is precisely
\{\pm 1\}
,

•

\deg\phi_{E}
is the degree of the modular parameterization
\phi_{E}
,

•

\widehat{h}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})}(P_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})})
is the Néron–Tate height of the Heegner point
P_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})}\in E(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))
.

Due to the invariance of the Néron–Tate height modulo torsion, when
\mathrm{rank}\,E(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))=1
, the value
\widehat{h}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})}(P_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})})
coincides with the regulator
\Reg(E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))
.
Therefore, when the sum of the analytic ranks of
E(\mathbb{Q})
and
E^{(d)}(\mathbb{Q})
is 1, understanding the distribution of

(2.1)

L^{\prime}(1,E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))=L(1,E)L^{\prime}(1,E^{(d)})+L^{\prime}(1,E)L(1,E^{(d)})

allows us to deduce the distribution of
\Reg(E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))
.

Recall the Galois group
G=\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})/\mathbb{Q})=\{1,\sigma\}
. Define the point

P_{d}:=P_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})}-\sigma(P_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})})\in E(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})).

Since
\sigma^{2}=\mathrm{id}
, we have

\sigma(P_{d})=\sigma(P_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})})-\sigma^{2}(P_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})})=\sigma(P_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})})-P_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})}=-P_{d}.

Thus,
P_{d}
is anti-invariant under the action of
\sigma
.

By the theory of quadratic twists, the points in

\{P\in E(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})):\sigma(P)=-P\}

correspond bijectively to points in

E^{(d)}(\mathbb{Q}).

Therefore, the point
P_{d}
defines a rational point on the quadratic twist
E^{(d)}
; that is,

P_{d}\in E^{(d)}(\mathbb{Q}).

Moreover, the Gross–Zagier formula applies to its Néron–Tate height over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Lemma 2.2

(Gross–Zagier formula over rational field
[
5
]
)
.

With notations as Lemma
2.1
, and
\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb{Q})=0
,

\operatorname{rank}E^{(d)}(\mathbb{Q})=1
, so that
P_{d}
is of infinite order in
E^{(d)}(\mathbb{Q})
. Then the derivative of the Hasse–Weil
L
-function of
E^{(d)}
over
\mathbb{Q}
satisfies

L^{\prime}(1,E^{(d)})=\frac{32\pi^{2}\,\|f_{E}\|^{2}}{|\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})}^{\times}|^{2}\,\sqrt{|d|}\cdot\deg\phi_{E}}\cdot\widehat{h}_{\mathbb{Q}}(P_{d}),

where
\widehat{h}_{\mathbb{Q}}(P_{d})
is the Néron–Tate height of the Heegner point
P_{d}\in E^{(d)}(\mathbb{Q})
.

We prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
by establishing the following results on
L
-functions.

Theorem 2.3
.

Under the conditions as in Theorem
1.1
, for large
X
we have

\left|\left\{d\in\Omega_{-w}(a)\mid 20<-d\leq X,~\frac{\log L^{\prime}(1,E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))-\tfrac{w}{2}\log\log|d|}{\sqrt{\log\log|d|}}\geq V\right\}\right|

is at most

\left|\{d\in\Omega_{-w}(a):-d\leq X\}\right|\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{V}^{\infty}e^{-x^{2}/2}\,dx+o(1)\right).

Moreover, when
L(1,E)\neq 0
, this result also applies to
L^{\prime}(1,E^{(d)})
.

Remark 2.4
.

Here, we mainly employ the method of Radziwiłł and Soundararajan to establish a one-sided central limit theorem.
For the large deviations problem, it may be possible to adapt techniques similar to those of Arguin and Bailey
[
1
]
and Creighton
[
2
]
to control the number of twists with large values of
\log L^{\prime}(1,E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))
, say of size comparable to
\alpha\log\log|d|
, for
0<\alpha<2
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, there exist positive constants
c_{1}^{\prime},c_{2}^{\prime},c_{3}^{\prime}
such that for any small
\varepsilon>0
and sufficiently large
X
, we have

\left|\left\{d\in\Omega_{1}(a)\mid 20<-d\leq X,\ L^{\prime}(1,E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))\geq c_{1}^{\prime}\right\}\right|\gg X^{1-\varepsilon},

\left|\left\{d\in\Omega_{-1}(a)\mid 20<-d\leq X,\ L^{\prime}(1,E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))\geq c_{2}^{\prime}\log|d|+c_{3}^{\prime}\right\}\right|\gg X^{1-\varepsilon}.

Moreover, when
L(1,E)\neq 0
, this result also applies to
L^{\prime}(1,E^{(d)})
.

The Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer (BSD) conjecture relates the first non-zero term in the Taylor expansion of the
L
-function at the central point to various arithmetic quantities, including the order of the Shafarevich–Tate group, the Tamagawa numbers, and the regulator.

Conjecture 1

(BSD conjecture)
.

For
E
over number field
K
,

\lim_{s\to 1}\frac{L(s,E/K)}{(s-1)^{r}}=\frac{\#\Sh(E/K)\Reg(E/K)\Omega_{E/K}\prod_{v\text{ finite}}c_{v}(E(K))}{\left(\#E(K)_{\text{tors}}\right)^{2}},

where:

•

r=\text{ord}_{s=1}L(E/K,s)
is the analytic rank of
E
,

•

\Sh(E/K)
is the Shafarevich–Tate group,

•

\Omega_{E/K}=\prod_{v\mid\infty}\int_{E(K_{v})^{0}}|\omega|
is the product of the real or complex periods at the archimedean places,

•

c_{v}(E(K))
are the Tamagawa numbers at the finite places
v
of
K
,

•

E(K)_{\text{tors}}
is the torsion subgroup of
E(K)
.

Here we note that, in this case, the ratio of the
L
-derivative to the regulator is a fixed constant. Thus, combining the BSD conjecture with the Gross–Zagier formula, we expect that the Shafarevich–Tate groups and the Tamagawa numbers should be uniformly bounded in this family.

Theorem 2.6

(Uniform boundedness of the Shafarevich–Tate groups and the Tamagawa numbers)
.

Let
E
be a non-CM elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with conductor
N
and root number
w
, and the vanishing order of the
L(s,E)
at the central point is at most 1.
Fix a residue class
a\bmod N_{0}
be a residue class such that
a
is a quadratic residue modulo
N
,
a\equiv 1
or
5\pmod{8}
,
and for any fundamental discriminant
d<0
with
d\equiv a\pmod{N_{0}}
,
w\chi_{d}(-N)=\epsilon
.
For
-3>d\in\Omega_{-w}(a)
, we have

(i)

L^{\prime}(1,E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))/\Reg(E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))=\frac{8\pi^{2}\,\|f_{E}\|^{2}}{\sqrt{|d|}\cdot\deg\phi_{E}}.

Moreover, when
L(1,E)\neq 0
, we have

L^{\prime}(1,E^{(d)})/\Reg(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})=\frac{8\pi^{2}\,\|f_{E}\|^{2}}{\sqrt{|d|}\cdot\deg\phi_{E}}.

(ii)

Under the BSD conjecture, the Shafarevich–Tate groups and Tamagawa numbers of this family obtained by base change to imaginary quadratic fields satisfying the Heegner condition are uniformly bounded, i.e., there exists a constant
C
depending on
E
such that

\#\Sh(E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))\prod_{v\text{ finite}}c_{v}(E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))\leq C.

Moreover, when
L(1,E)\neq 0
, this result also applies to
\#\Sh(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})\prod_{v\text{ finite}}c_{v}(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})
.

Proof.

(i)

This follows directly from the Gross–Zagier formula.

(ii)

For imaginary quadratic field
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
, define the following periods:

\Omega_{E}^{+}:=\int_{E(\mathbb{R})^{0}}|\omega|,\quad\Omega_{E}^{-}:=\int_{E(\mathbb{R})^{0}}\omega,

where
\omega
is a Néron differential.
The period associated to
E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
is given by the product of periods of
E
and its quadratic twist:

\Omega_{E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})}=\Omega_{E}^{+}\cdot\Omega_{E^{(d)}}^{+}.

Moreover, from Pal
[
13
, Proposition 2.5]
we know that for negative square-free
(d,2N)=1
,

\Omega_{E^{(d)}}^{+}=\frac{\tilde{u}(d)}{\sqrt{|d|}}c_{\infty}(E^{(d)})\Omega_{E}^{-}

with some
\tilde{u}\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
decided by
E
,
and
c_{\infty}(E^{(d)})
denotes the number of connected components of
E^{(d)}(\mathbb{R})
, which is either 1 or 2, depends on whether the cubic polynomial of the elliptic curve has one real root or three real roots.

Thus, under the BSD conjecture,

\#\Sh(E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))\prod_{v\text{ finite}}c_{v}(E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))=\frac{8\pi^{2}\,\|f_{E}\|^{2}\left(\#E(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))_{\mathrm{tors}}\right)^{2}}{\tilde{u}c_{\infty}(E^{(d)})\Omega_{E}^{+}\Omega_{E}^{-}\deg\phi_{E}}.

Moreover, when
L(1,E)\neq 0
, we have

\#\Sh(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})\prod_{v\text{ finite}}c_{v}(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})=\frac{8\pi^{2}\,\|f_{E}\|^{2}\left(\#E^{(d)}(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}\right)^{2}}{\tilde{u}c_{\infty}(E^{(d)})\Omega_{E}^{-}\deg\phi_{E}}.

Mazur
[
11
]
showed that
1\leq|E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}|\leq 12
,
and for quadratic fields, Kenku–Momose
[
9
]
and Kamienny
[
8
]
showed that
1\leq|E(K)_{\mathrm{tors}}|\leq 18
.
Thus, we obtain the uniform boundedness of the Shafarevich–Tate groups and Tamagawa numbers for this family.
∎

Preparation

Let
\Phi(\cdot)\leq 1
be a smooth non-negative Schwartz class function supported on
[\frac{1}{2},\frac{5}{2}]

\Phi(x)=1
for

(3.1)

\check{\Phi}(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(x)x^{s}\mathrm{d}x.

Similarly to Radziwiłł–Soundararajan
[
14
]

[

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u
be a positive integer such that
(u\ell,N_{0})=1

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

From (
2.1
), when
w=-1
,
applying
[
14
, Proposition 2]
, there exists a constant
D_{1}>0
, depending on
E
, such that

\sum_{d\in\Omega_{1}(a)}L^{\prime}(1,E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))\Phi\left(\frac{-d}{X}\right)=L^{\prime}(1,E)\sum_{d\in\Omega_{1}(a)}L(1,E^{(d)})\Phi\left(\frac{-d}{X}\right)\\
=D_{1}X+O\left(X^{\frac{7}{8}+\varepsilon}\right).

When
w=1
, using Lemma
3.1
, there exist constants
D_{2},D_{3}>0
, depending on
E
, such that

\sum_{d\in\Omega_{-1}(a)}L^{\prime}(1,E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))\Phi\left(\frac{-d}{X}\right)=L(1,E)\sum_{d\in\Omega_{1}(a)}L^{\prime}(1,E^{(d)})\Phi\left(\frac{-d}{X}\right)\\
=D_{2}X\log X+D_{3}X+O\left(X^{\frac{3}{4}+\varepsilon}\right).

Moreover, by Heath-Brown’s quadratic large sieve
[
6
]
, in both cases we have

\sum_{d\in\Omega_{-w}(a)}L^{\prime}(1,E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))^{2}\Phi\left(\frac{-d}{X}\right)\ll X^{1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}.

Let
c_{i}^{\prime}<\frac{D_{i}}{2}
for
i=1,2,3
. Then for
w=-1
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in\Omega_{1}(a)\\
L^{\prime}(1,E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))<c_{1}^{\prime}\end{subarray}}L^{\prime}(1,E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))\Phi\left(\frac{-d}{X}\right)<2c_{1}^{\prime}X,

and thus,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in\Omega_{1}(a)\\
L^{\prime}(1,E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))\geq c_{1}^{\prime}\end{subarray}}L^{\prime}(1,E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))\Phi\left(\frac{-d}{X}\right)>(D_{1}-2c_{1}^{\prime})X.

A similar argument applies for
w=1
.

Now let

. By Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in\Omega_{1}(a)\\
L^{\prime}(1,E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))\geq c_{1}^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\left|L^{\prime}(1,E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))\Phi\left(\frac{-d}{X}\right)\right|^{k}\\
\gg\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in\Omega_{1}(a)\\

\times\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in\Omega_{1}(a)\\

5.
Proof of Proposition
3.6

Proof.

Our proof follows a similar strategy to that of
[
14
, Proposition 5]
,
but with an additional logarithmic factor, as in
[
3
]
.

For any integer
n
, we write
n=(n)_{1}(n)_{2}^{2}
,
where
(n)_{1}
is square-free.
Let
\tilde{a}(\cdot)
be a completely multiplicative function defined by
\tilde{a}(p^{\alpha})=a(p)^{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\geq 0
.
Let
\Omega(n)
denote the total number of prime factors of
n
, counted with multiplicity.
Let
\omega(\cdot)
be a multiplicative function defined by
\omega(p^{\alpha})=\alpha!
.
Define the function
b(n)
to be 1 if
n
has at most
\ell
prime factors, all of which lie in
\mathcal{P}
, and 0 otherwise.
Then we have

E_{\ell}(-\mathcal{P}(d))=\sum_{n}\frac{\tilde{a}(n)}{n}\frac{(-1)^{\Omega(n)}}{\omega(n)}b(n)\chi_{d}(n).

Using Lemma
3.1
, we obtain

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in\Omega_{-1}(a)\end{subarray}}L^{\prime}(1,E^{(d)})E_{\ell}(-\mathcal{P}(d))\Phi\left(\frac{-d}{X}\right)\\
=\frac{\check{\Phi}(0)X}{4\pi^{2}N_{0}}\sum_{n}\frac{\tilde{a}(n)a((n)_{1})}{n(n)_{1}}\frac{(-1)^{\Omega(n)}}{\omega(n)}b(n)\\
\times\left(\log\frac{X}{(n)_{1}}+C_{1}+\sum_{p\mid n}\frac{C_{2}(p)}{p}\log p\right)+O\left(X^{\frac{3}{4}+2\varepsilon}\right).

We shall concentrate on the terms involving

\log\left(\frac{X}{(n)_{1}}\right)
. The remaining terms, involving

C_{1}+\sum_{p\mid n}\frac{C_{2}(p)}{p}\log p,

can be shown, using the same argument, to contribute
at most
O\left(X(\log X)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log\log X\right)
.

Define

S_{1}=\sum_{n}\frac{\tilde{a}(n)a((n)_{1})}{n(n)_{1}}\frac{(-1)^{\Omega(n)}}{\omega(n)}b(n),

and

S_{2}=\sum_{n}\frac{\tilde{a}(n)a((n)_{1})}{n(n)_{1}}\frac{(-1)^{\Omega(n)}}{\omega(n)}b(n)\log(n)_{1}.

Then we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in\Omega_{-1}(a)\end{subarray}}L^{\prime}(1,E^{(d)})E_{\ell}(-\mathcal{P}(d))\Phi\left(\frac{-d}{X}\right)\ll|S_{1}|X\log X+|S_{2}|X+X^{\frac{3}{4}+2\varepsilon}.

For
S_{1}
, we use Rankin’s trick to remove the restriction imposed by
b(n)
.
When
\Omega(n)>\ell
, we have
2^{\Omega(n)-\ell}>1
.
Thus, we obtain the bound

|S_{1}|\leq\left|\sum_{n}\frac{\tilde{a}(n)a((n)_{1})}{n(n)_{1}}\frac{(-1)^{\Omega(n)}}{\omega(n)}\right|+\left|\sum_{n}\frac{|\tilde{a}(n)a((n)_{1})|}{n(n)_{1}}\frac{2^{\Omega(n)-\ell}}{\omega(n)}\right|.

For the first part, we have

\left|\sum_{n}\frac{\tilde{a}(n)a((n)_{1})}{n(n)_{1}}\cdot\frac{(-1)^{\Omega(n)}}{\omega(n)}\right|=\left|\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{a(p)^{2i}}{p^{2i}(2i)!}-\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{a(p)^{2i+2}}{p^{2i+2}(2i+1)!}\right)\right|\\
\ll\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\left(1-\frac{a(p)^{2}}{2p^{2}}+O\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)\right).

For the second part, using the inequality
1+x\leq e^{x}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we obtain

\left|\sum_{n}\frac{|\tilde{a}(n)a((n)_{1})|}{n(n)_{1}}\frac{2^{\Omega(n)-\ell}}{\omega(n)}\right|\leq 2^{-\ell}\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{a(p)^{2i}2^{2i}}{p^{2i}(2i)!}+\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{a(p)^{2i+2}2^{2i+1}}{p^{2i+2}(2i+1)!}\right)\\
\leq 2^{-\ell}\exp\left(2\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\left(\frac{a(p)^{2}}{p^{2}}+O\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)\right)\right).

Using Lemma
3.3
, we have

|S_{1}|\ll(\log X)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log\log X.

By expanding the logarithmic term, we have

S_{2}=-\sum_{q\in\mathcal{P}}\sum_{l\geq 0}\frac{a(q)^{2l+2}\log q}{q^{2l+2}(2l+1)!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\tilde{a}(n)a((n)_{1})}{n(n)_{1}}\frac{(-1)^{\Omega(n)}}{\omega(n)}b(nq^{2l+1}).

For the inner sum, when
l\leq\frac{\ell}{2}
, we have

\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\tilde{a}(n)a((n)_{1})}{n(n)_{1}}\frac{(-1)^{\Omega(n)}}{\omega(n)}b(nq^{2l+1})\right|\ll(\log X)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log\log X\left(1+\frac{a(q)^{2}}{2q^{2}}+O\left(\frac{1}{q^{2}}\right)\right).

When
l>\frac{\ell}{2}
, by applying Stirling’s formula to the factorial term, we obtain

\frac{1}{(2l+1)!}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\tilde{a}(n)a((n)_{1})}{n(n)_{1}}\frac{(-1)^{\Omega(n)}}{\omega(n)}b(nq^{2l+1})\right|\\
\ll\left((\log X)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log\log X+\frac{2^{2l+1}}{(2l+1)!}2^{-\ell}(\log X)^{2}(\log\log X)^{-4}\right)\\
\times\left(1+\frac{a(q)^{2}}{2q^{2}}+O\left(\frac{1}{q^{2}}\right)\right)\\
\ll(\log X)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log\log X\left(1+\frac{a(q)^{2}}{2q^{2}}+O\left(\frac{1}{q^{2}}\right)\right).

Then, applying Lemma
3.3
, we conclude that

|S_{2}|\ll\sum_{q\in\mathcal{P}}\left(\frac{a(q)^{2}}{q^{2}}+O\left(\frac{1}{q^{2}}\right)\right)(\log X)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log\log X\ll(\log X)^{\frac{1}{2}}(\log\log X)^{-1}.

This completes the proof.
∎

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn the context of global preparedness and response strategies for Mpox outbreaks, we develop a comprehensive mathematical model to investigate its transmission dynamics, integrating critical factors such as vaccination coverage and environmental contamination. The model is analytically examined to ensure well-posedness, including the positivity of solutions and the existence of a biologically invariant region. A global sensitivity analysis, supported by contour and surface plots, identifies critical parameters affecting disease transmission. Furthermore, we formulate and analyze an optimal control problem incorporating vaccination and sanitation efforts to evaluate intervention effectiveness. Introduction

Zoonotic diseases remain significant global public health threats, as highlighted by the worldwide outbreak of monkeypox-hereafter referred to as ‘Mpox’ in line with the World Health Organization’s updated terminology1. The outbreak began in early May 2022, when a case of Mpox with no travel history to endemic regions was reported in the UK2. Mpox is caused by the monkeypox virus (MPXV), a double-stranded DNA virus from the Poxviridae family. It is transmissible through direct contact with an infected individual, contact with contaminated materials or through exposure to infected animals. Vertical transmission may occur during pregnancy, with the virus potentially passing to the fetus transplacentally, or to the neonate during or shortly after childbirth3. In May 2022, outbreaks of Mpox were detected in the United Kingdom, Portugal, and Italy, with the majority of cases occuring among men who have sex with men (MSM)4. Human-to-human transmission occurs through direct contact with biological fluids, skin lesions, or mucous membranes, as well as inhalation of respiratory particles from infected individuals5. The clinical course of Mpox in humans includes three strategies: an incubation period from 7- 17 days; a prodromal phase lasting 1–4 days marked by fever, fatigue, and swollen lymph nodes; and an eruptive phase lasting 14-28 days, characterized by skin lesions primarily appearing on the face and extremities6.

The World Health Organization officially designated the Mpox outbreak as a “public health emergency of international concern”7, emphasizing its broad international transmission and the increased burden it imposed on health systems worldwide, many of which were already under significant strain due to the ongoing challenges posed by the COVID-19 pandemic8. Although Mpox is generally regarded as a mild and self-limiting illness, it can become severe, particularly in children, pregnant individuals, and those with weakened immune systems9. Historically, most Mpox outbreaks were confined to neglected communities in West and Central Africa, where the disease is endemic10. Between 1967 and 1979, widespread smallpox vaccination provided partial protection against Mpox. However, following smallpox eradication, vaccination efforts ceased, leading to a gradual decline in population immunity11. The current global Mpox outbreak is concerning due to this reduced immunity combined with the virus’s evolving epidemiological patterns.

In 2022, more than 30,000 Mpox cases were identified in the U.S., with reported between May and July, and daily infections reaching a peak on August 1st12. The CDC advised administering two JYNNEOS vaccine doses, spaced four weeks apart, targeting high-risk groups-mainly gay, bisexual, and other men who have sex with men (MSM) with multiple recent partners13. However, demand soon surpassed vaccine supply and distribution capacity. In response, New York City adopted a “first-dose priority” approach, aiming to provide broad protection through initial single doses, with second doses deferred until availability improved. This strategy diverged from CDC recommendations and was implemented under limited evidence on the comparative efficacy of one versus two doses, but was necessary given the outbreak’s urgency12. In the study14, the authors demonstrate that, in addition to vaccination, enhancing awareness, treatment, and quarantine measures effectively reduce Mpox transmission and offer insights for broader disease control. Many studies15,16 account for environmental contamination, recognizing its significant role in disease transmission dynamics due to the virus’s ability to persist in the environment and facilitate indirect spread. A deterministic model of Mpox is developed, and in the study17, it is shown that backward bifurcation may occur, and isolating infected individuals significantly reduces disease transmission.

Using time series analysis and a deterministic model, the study18 highlights the critical role of Mpox vaccination and the need to adjust key factors like contact rates in high-risk groups. We examined several existing models19–22 to gain deeper insights into the transmission dynamics and control strategies of infectious diseases. Due to the inherent randomness in disease transmission, many studies23–27 implement a stochastic approach, as it captures unpredictable variations more accurately and provides a robust framework for analyzing complex disease dynamics.

The ongoing global concern around Mpox highlights the need for advanced epidemiological models capable of capturing complex disease dynamics. Although previous compartmental and fractional-order models have significantly advanced our understanding, gaps remain in integrating multiple transmission pathways, control interventions, and stochastic effects into a cohesive framework. This study addresses these gaps by introducing a novel model that explicitly incorporates Mpox transmission via human, animal, and environmental routes, and vaccination interventions. The incorporation of vaccination and sanitation as dynamic control measures provides practical insights into managing outbreaks. Moreover, our approach extends beyond deterministic frameworks to include stochastic modeling techniques-specifically Continuous-Time Markov Chains (CTMC) and Stochastic Differential Equations (SDE)-to more realistically capture random fluctuations and rare events inherent in disease spread. This comprehensive integration, coupled with rigorous calibration to recent outbreak data and extensive sensitivity analyses, marks the novelty and practical utility of our model, positioning it uniquely within existing Mpox epidemiological literature.

Building upon insights from previous studies, we formulate a comprehensive model to analyze mpox dynamics, incorporating key factors such as vaccination, environmental transmission, and stochastic effects through CTMC and SDE approaches. Model formulation

While classical models such as the SIR or SIRS frameworks have been valuable for understanding general infectious disease dynamics, they typically represent a single-host population and overlook external reservoirs or indirect transmission routes. These models are limited in their ability to capture the complexity of Mpox transmission. In contrast, our model incorporates rodent-to-human and environment-to-human pathways, which are essential for representing the zoonotic and environmental persistence characteristics of Mpox. By including environmental virus concentration and animal reservoir interactions, the model enables a more realistic assessment of control strategies-not only vaccination and sanitation, but also measures aimed at minimizing human contact with infected rodents. This expanded framework allows for more comprehensive scenario planning than traditional models can offer.

We analyze Mpox data from 2022, the peak year of the outbreak in the U.S., with 30,088 confirmed cases and 53 deaths (Fig. 3). This contrasts sharply with 2023 (1771 cases, 7 deaths) and 2024 (2368 cases, 3 deaths), reflecting a significant decline in disease burden39. Focusing on 2022 allows us to study the most intense phase of the epidemic, better understand transmission and healthcare impact, and evaluate the effectiveness of public health responses during the period of greatest concern.

Local sensitivity analysis

This analysis not only offers a quantitative measure of parameter sensitivity but also serves as a strategic tool for guiding effective disease control and management policies.

Global sensitivity analysis

We perform a global sensitivity analysis of the reproduction number using Partial Rank Correlation Coefficients (PRCC) and Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS). PRCC quantifies the strength and direction of the monotonic relationship between model parameters and the output, while corresponding p-values assess the statistical significance of these associations43. PRCC requires monotonocity between inputs and outputs44; therefore, we verify this condition before proceeding with this analysis. Sensitivity results are obtained using the baseline parameter values listed in Table 2, with LHS performed using 1000 simulations and variation around each input parameter.

Figure 16b shows the role of the virus decay rate in shaping the dynamics of environmental contamination over time. Higher decay rates lead to earlier peaks and faster reductions in contamination, while lower decay rates prolong the persistence of contamination in the environment. This analysis underscores the critical role of enhancing virus decay through regular and efficient decontamination practices. Recommendations include routine disinfection of surfaces in high-risk areas, such as healthcare facilities and public spaces, to accelerate the reduction of environmental contamination.

The numerical findings highlights the multifaceted drivers of Mpox transmission and underscore key intervention targets. A higher human-to-human transmission rate accelerates and intensifies outbreaks, emphasizing the need to reduce direct contact. Although zoonotic spillover from rodents is not the primary driver, controlling initial introductions remains essential. Environmental contamination emerges as a critical factor, influenced by viral shedding and decay rates; thus, maintaining hygiene, sanitation, and surface decontamination is vital to curb indirect transmission. Lower disease-induced mortality may prolong outbreaks, yet reducing fatalities remains a core public health priority. Vaccination proves highly effective in mitigating spread, while recovery dynamics reveal long-term stabilization through acquired immunity. Overall, the findings underscore the need for an integrated approach that combines behavioral, environmental, and immunological strategies to effectively control Mpox outbreaks.

Stochastic dynamics of Mpox

Stochastic analysis involves modeling systems that incorporate inherent randomness, where outcomes are influenced by probabilities and uncertainty. This approach is particularly useful for studying phenomena such as disease spread, where variability plays a key role. On the other hand, deterministic analysis focuses on systems with predictable behavior, where future states are determined by initial conditions and governing equations, without any randomness involved. This method provides precise predictions in scenarios where the system’s behavior can be accurately modeled without considering uncertainty. Both approaches offer valuable insights depending on the nature of the system being studied.

Incorporating stochasticity is especially important for diseases like Mpox, where outbreaks may be sporadic, case numbers are often low, and transmission routes can vary. The random nature of contact patterns, environmental exposures, and behavioral factors introduces uncertainty that can dramatically influence outbreak dynamics. By modeling these random effects, the stochastic framework provides a more nuanced understanding of disease spread, helping to identify the conditions under which the disease might persist, fade out, or re-emerge unexpectedly. This added layer of analysis enhances the predictive power and reliability of the model in public health planning and risk assessment.

The stochastic differential equation (SDE) framework offers notable advantages over the continuous-time Markov chain (CTMC) model by incorporating continuous-time stochasticity to reflect natural variability in population dynamics. This results in smoother, biologically plausible transitions that more accurately represent real-world epidemic behavior, particularly in large populations. In contrast, the CTMC model’s discrete transitions may oversimplify complex processes. Thus, the SDE approach provides a more robust and realistic foundation for epidemiological modeling and intervention analysis.

Comprehensive summary and conclusion

This study conducts a rigorous analysis of Mpox transmission dynamics through a deterministic compartmental model that integrates vaccination strategies, environmental transmission pathways, and essential behavioral and epidemiological parameters influencing disease propagation. The model’s mathematical integrity is verified through positive and invariant region analysis. Stability analysis reveals that the disease-free equilibrium is both locally and globally stable when the basic reproduction number , while the unique endemic equilibrium exists for Using U.S. outbreak data (May-December 2022), model parameters are estimated via least squares method. The model shows strong agreement with observed daily, cumulative, and death cases, supported by residual assessment. Sensitivity analysis, aided by contour and surface plots, identifies effective vaccination rates, reduced failure in vaccination, minimized environmental contamination, and increased individual-level preventive behavior as major drivers in controlling disease spread.

From a public health perspective, the optimal control analysis incorporating vaccination and sanitation as intervention, underscores that a combined intervention−with appropriately chosen control weights− is essential for optimal disease mitigation− vaccination directly protects individuals, while sanitation curtails environmental sources of reinfection. While the vaccination-only control strategy results in a substantial number of averted infections, its impact on environmental contamination remains limited. In contrast the combined approach not only reduces infections but also effectively suppresses environmental contamination.

Stochastic extensions using CTMC and SDE frameworks incorporate the inherent randomness of disease transmission, offering a more realistic representation of epidemic dynamics than deterministic models. CTMC captures discrete state transitions and is ideal for small populations, while SDE provides continuous approximations suitable for large populations with fluctuating transmission rates. SDE models are computationally efficient and capture noise effects more smoothly. Both approaches enhance predictive accuracy, support probabilistic risk assessment, and inform robust public health decision-making under uncertainty.

These findings provide actionable insights for policymakers, suggesting that Mpox control requires not only sufficient vaccine coverage but also focused efforts to reduce environmental transmission, promote public health education, ensure prompt treatment of infected individuals to reduce Mpox related mortality, mitigate clinical complications, and alleviate the overall disease burden. Effective control of Mpox requires routine disinfection in high-risk settings, including healthcare facilities and public spaces, to limit viral shedding and environmental contamination. Reinforcing hygiene protocols, isolating infectious individuals, and regularly sanitizing high-touch surfaces are essential to disrupting transmission chains. The sustained and coordinated implementation of these measures can substantially reduce disease spread and strengthen the effectiveness of evidence-based public health response systems.

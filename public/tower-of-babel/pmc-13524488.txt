The involvement of CgIRF1 in regulating LPS-induced haemocyte proliferation in the Pacific oyster Crassostrea gigas
Authors: Yiqing Wang, Mengjia Chen, Zhuo Yu, Xiaoyu Gu, Yuhao Jin, Yuqing Zeng, Jiayu Yu, Xue Qiao, Lingling Wang, Linsheng Song
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractInterferon regulatory factor 1 (IRF1) is an important transcription regulator involving in immune regulation. A homolog of IRF1 (CgIRF1) was previously identified to be involved in the antiviral immune response of Pacific oyster Crassostrea gigas. In the present study, the role of CgIRF1 in regulating haemocyte proliferation in the immune response of LPS stimulation was further investigated. CgIRF1 mRNA was detectable in all the stages of oyster larvae, with the highest level in four-cell stage. LPS stimulation significantly enhanced the CgIRF1 expression in both RNA and protein level. And the nuclear translocation of CgIRF1 protein was also observed in haemocyte after LPS stimulation. The recombinant CgIRF1 showed interaction activity with CgIRF8, which has been previously identified to involve in haemocyte proliferation. Consistently, after the CgIRF1 expression was inhibited by RNAi, the percentage of EdU positive haemocytes as well as the expression level of hematopoiesis-related transcription factors (CgGATA and CgRunx) and cell cycle regulatory proteins (CgCDK2, CgCDC6 and CgCDC45) significantly reduced in haemocytes. These results indicated CgIRF1 might involve in regulating haemocyte proliferation by binding CgIRF8 and further regulating expression of cell proliferation-related factors during immune response.Keywords: Crassostrea gigas, Haemocytes, IRF1, Cell proliferation

Introduction

The interferon regulatory factor (IRF) family of transcription factors are well known for their essential roles in regulating the expression of genes underly interferon (IFN) responses [1]. There are nine IRF members (IRF1-9) in mammals, which feature conserved domains essential for interactions with other transcription factors and cofactors [2]. These transcription factors collectively orchestrate the host's innate immune response, bridging the detection of pathogens to the activation of antiviral genes. IRF-1 was originally identified as a regulator of virus-inducible enhancer-like elements on the human IFN-β gene [3]. It was subsequently identified as a central regulator of diverse physiological processes, including host defense, hematopoietic development, cell development and differentiation [4].

IRF1 is a structurally conserved transcription factor with the essential ability to activate transcription from specific promoters [5]. The IRF1 protein consists of a conserved DNA binding domain (DBD) and a C-terminal IRF-associated domain 2 (IAD2), which is responsible for recognizing DNA motifs and interacting with other transcription factors, respectively [6,7]. IRF1 is basally expressed at low levels in human cells, but it can be rapidly induced by key inflammatory and antiviral signals, such as IFN, TNF, and IL [8]. For instance, IFN activate IRF1 transcription via the JAK/STAT pathway, and the activated IRF1 further bind to interferon-stimulated response element (ISRE), where it forms a complex protein-protein interaction with key transcription factors such as IRF8, IRF2, STAT1 and NF-κB [9,10]. These interactions allow IRF1 to modulate a broad spectrum of gene expression, such as IFN-stimulated genes (ISGs) and IFN-stimulated gene factor 3 (ISGF3) [11]. IRF1 collaborates with IRF8 to initiate transcription of IL-1β in reactive microglia [12]. IFN-γ induced IRF1 can interact with MyD88 upon Toll-like receptor activation, which licenses its rapid translocation into the nucleus to regulate the production of IFN-β, iNOS, and IL-12p35 [13,14]. Therefore, IRF1 is one of the critical transcription factors involving in cytokine signaling and immune response by cooperation with other factors.

IRF1 exerts a multifaceted role that extends far beyond immune regulation, encompassing processes such as immune cell development and differentiation. Specifically, IRF1 expression is upregulated during myeloid differentiation, and upon exposure to external stimuli including viral infection, LPS and IFNs [15,5]. IRF1 plays a critical role in the differentiation of CD8+ T and T helper 1 (Th1) cells [16,17]. Moreover, IRF1−/− mice display increased frequencies of granulocytic precursors with impaired granulocytic development in the bone marrow [18]. IRF1 is an important regulator governing both self-renewal and stress responses in hematopoietic stem cells [19]. IRF1 was also reported to act as a tumor suppressor by blocking cell cycle progression, while IRF2 competitively binds to the same ISRE to counteract this inhibition and promote cell proliferation [4]. Thus, IRF1 plays an important role in regulating cell development and differentiation in a context-dependent manner.

Phylogenomic analysis places the origin of the IRF gene family at the base of Metazoa, predating the divergence of major bilaterian lineages and coinciding with the emergence of animal multicellularity [2]. IRFs present in all major metazoan lineages from sponges to mammals, including sea sponges, placozoans, comb jellies, cnidarians, and bilaterians [2]. For instance, BbtIRF1 and BbtIRF8 from amphioxus Branchiostoma belcheri tsingtauense was identified to bind ISRE and were upregulated upon poly (I:C) stimulation [20]. LvIRF from Litopenaeus vannamei exhibited antiviral activity by binding ISRE elements and upregulating Vago4 [21]. IRF homologs are widely identified in mollusc, such as oyster Crassostrea gigas [22], scallop Chlamys farreri [23] and mussel Bathymodiolus platifrons [24]. CfIRF1 and CfIRF2 from C. farreri showed conserved structure characteristics of the IRF family, and could recognize and bind to ISRE elements, and function as transcriptional regulators in innate immunity [23]. Our previous work has characterized an IRF1 homolog (CgIRF1) in Pacific oyster C. gigas, which was highly expressed in haemocytes, induced by immune challenges, and capable of binding ISRE and activating the CgIFNLP promoter [22]. Moreover, CgIRF8 was involved in the regulation of haemocyte proliferation by regulating the expression of CgGATA and other related genes during oyster immune response [25]. However, the immunological roles of IRF family proteins in invertebrates remain largely unknown.

The Pacific oyster C. gigas is a cornerstone species in global shellfish aquaculture, valued for its economic contribution and ecological functions. However, the sustainability of oyster aquaculture is increasingly threatened by recurrent outbreaks of pathogen infectious diseases, such as Vibrio splendidus and ostreid herpesvirus 1 (OsHV-1) [26]. Oyster haemocytes are considered as the main immune cells, which are primarily responsible for the defense against pathogens. There are significant interspecific differences in hematopoiesis of among invertebrate. For instance, hematopoietic tissue of crustaceans was identified as a thin and non-transparent sheet located at the dorsolateral side of the stomach, such as freshwater crayfish and Chinese mitten crab [27,28]. The irregularly folded structure (IFS) of the gills is regarded as the site of haemocyte formation in oyster [29]. VEGF-VEGFR signaling has been identified to direct the migration of newborn haemocytes from hematopoietic site to the circulating haemolymph in oyster [30]. Haemocyte proliferation was significantly upregulated during the immune response, reflecting rapid cellular mobilization and clonal expansion in response to pathogen challenge [31,32]. The oyster hematopoiesis was regulated by many transcription factors, such as Fos [33], GATA [34], Runx [35], Brachyury [31] and IRF8 [25]. In the present study, the high expression of CgIRF1 in haemocytes was detected after LPS stimulation, and its relationship with LPS induced haemocyte proliferation was further studied. The main objectives were to (1) verify the expression profile of CgIRF1 in oyster haemocytes following LPS stimulation, (2) examine expression changes of proliferation related genes in CgIRF1-RNAi oyster and (3) elucidate interactive relationship of CgIRF1 and CgIRF8 in regulating haemocyte proliferation.

Materials and methods

Animals, immune stimulation and sample collection

The adult Pacific Oysters with an average with shell length of 13 cm were collected from a local aquaculture farm Dalian, Liaoning province, China. Oysters were then cultured for one week in aerated seawater with enhanced oxygen content at 15 ± 2 °C, and the seawater was renewed once every day. Seventy-two oysters were randomly divided into two groups, and injected with 100 μL of 0.5 mg/mL LPS (L2630, Sigma, USA) or 100 μL of sterile seawater (SW), respectively. Total haemocytes were collected for RNA and protein extraction at 0, 12, 24 and 48 h according to previous description [25]. Nine oysters were randomly collected from each group, and three of them were pooled together as one sample. Eight samples of oyster larvae were collected from untreated oyster seedlings, including zygote, four-cells, morula, blastula, gastrula, early trochophore, trochophore and D-veliger.

RNA extraction, cDNA synthesis and qRT-PCR analysis

Total RNA was extracted from haemocytes and larvae with the TRIzol™ reagent (SM129, Sevenbio, China). The RNA was reversely transcribed by using the SevenClever ™ First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (with dsDNase) (SM136, Sevenbio, China) according to manufacturer’s instruction. The qRT-PCR was performed to detect the mRNA level of target genes with the primers in Table 1 (P1–P14) as our previous study [36]. A fragment of CgEF (NM_001305313.2) was amplified by primers P15 and P16 (Table 1) and used as an internal reference. The reaction was performed use SYBR Green qPCR Master Mix II (SM143-01, Sevenbio, China). The relative expression level was calculated by 2-ΔΔCT method [37].

Protein extraction and western blot

The protein expression of CgIRF1 was detected by Western blot as our previous work [38]. Total protein was extracted from oyster haemocytes by RIPA lysis buffer containing protease and phosphatase inhibitors (P0013B, Beyotime, China). The protein samples were separated by 12.5% SDS-PAGE, transferred onto nitrocellulose membranes, blocked with 5% skim milk (A600669-0250, Sangon, China), and incubated with primary antibodies against CgIRF1 (1:1000, RC-5332, rabbit anti-IRF1, Absea Biotechnology, China) and β-tubulin (1:1000, AF2835, Beyotime, China), respectively. After three TBST washes, the membranes were incubated with HRP-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG (1:1000, A0210, Beyotime, China). The membrane was incubated in Western lighting ECL substrate system (Thermo Scientific, USA) in dark, and images were then captured using the Amersham Imager 600 system (GE Healthcare, USA).

Immunofluorescence assay

The subcellular localization of CgIRF1 in haemocytes was determined by immunocytochemistry as previous description [39]. After LPS stimulation, the haemocytes were obtained and adsorbed on the slide. Then the cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA), permeabilized with 0.5% Triton-100 and blocked with 3% BSA. The cells further were incubated with the CgIRF1 antibody (1:500, RC-5332, rabbit anti-IRF1, Absea Biotechnology, China) and secondary antibody Alexa Fluor 488-labeled Goat anti-rabbit antibody (1:1000, A0423, Beyotime, China), respectively. The nuclei were stained with DAPI (1:1000, C1006, Beyotime, China) for 10 min. Finally, haemocytes were observed under a laser scanning confocal microscope (Zeiss, Germany).

RNA interference (RNAi) of CgIRF1 and EdU staining

Specific siRNAs (Table 1) were designed and synthesized by GenePharma (China) to interfere with CgIRF1 expression. Twenty-seven oysters were randomly divided into three groups: Blank, NC-RNAi and CgIRF1-RNAi. Each group contained nine oysters, and three of them were pooled together as one sample. The NC-RNAi and CgIRF1-RNAi groups were injected with 100 µL of NC-siRNA and CgIRF1-siRNA, respectively. At 6 h post siRNA injection, each oyster was injected with 100 μL EdU working solution (0.2 mg/mL, dissolved in filtered seawater). At 12 h post siRNA injection, 100 μL LPS (0.5 mg/mL) was injected into each oyster subsequently. Haemocytes were collected for total RNA extraction and cell proliferation detection after 12 h LPS stimulation. EdU labeling was performed using the BeyoClick™ EdU Cell Proliferation Kit with Alexa Fluor 488 (C0071S, Beyotime, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The positive signals of EdU indicated the newly generated haemocytes, which was subsequently analyzed by flow cytometry (BD Bio-sciences, USA).

The molecular interaction between recombinant CgIRF1 and CgIRF8

The recombinant protein of CgIRF1 and CgIRF8 (rCgIRF1 and rCgIRF8) used in this study were obtained from our previous studies [22]. The interaction between rCgIRF1 and rCgIRF8 was detected by BLI assay according to the previous description [34]. The rCgIRF1 protein was biotinylated followed by a Biotinylation Kit (G-MM-IGT, GENEMORE, China). The biotinylated rCgIRF1 was then immobilized on streptavidin-coated sensors for baseline equilibration. The rCgIRF8 was serially diluted with PBST to four gradient concentrations (3.299 µM, 0.825 µM, 0.412 µM, and 0.206 µM). The diluted recombinant proteins were aliquoted into individual wells of a 96-well plate, and the plate was then loaded into the Octet BLI system (Octet, Sartorius, USA) for operation and the raw data were analyzed by the Octet system software.

Statistical analysis

The Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS) 26.0 was used to analyze all data, which are expressed as mean ± SD (N = 3). One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by multiple comparisons was performed. Differences among groups were considered significant at p < 0.05 (* or a, b, c), p < 0.01 (**), and p < 0.001 (***).

Results

The expression patterns of CgIRF1 in oyster larvae and haemocytes after LPS stimulation

The IRF1 has been reported to regulated the development and immune response [5]. Here we found CgIRF1 mRNA was also widely expressed in all stages of oyster larvae, including zygote, four-cells, morula, blastula, gastrula, early trochophore, trochophore and D-veliger (Fig. 1A). The mRNA level of CgIRF1 showed high expression in cleavage stage, especially in four-cell stage, which were 22.33-fold of that in early trochophore larvae (p < 0.001). Then the mRNA expression of CgIRF1 was gradually declined with the embryonic development (Fig. 1A). Our previous work has demonstrated increased expression of CgIRF1 in oyster haemocytes after poly (I:C) stimulation [22]. In the present study, the RNA expression levels of CgIRF1 in haemocytes were also up-regulated upon LPS stimulation. As shown in Fig. 1B, the mRNA transcripts of CgIRF1 showed dynamic changes in expression levels from 12 to 48 h. The mRNA level peaked at 12 h with 6.76-fold induction (p < 0.001), and showed sustained upregulation of 2.02-fold (p < 0.01) and 2.10-fold (p < 0.01) of that in SW group at 24 h and 48 h, respectively (Fig. 1B). The protein expression level of CgIRF1 was examined by Western blot with a specific antibody. A distinct band at approximately 38 kDa was detected, which was consistent with the predicted molecular weight of CgIRF1 protein (Fig. 1C). The CgIRF1 protein level was also significantly increased in haemocytes after LPS stimulation (Fig. 1D&E), which was 6.61-fold (p < 0.001), 7.17-fold (p < 0.001) and 1.28-fold (p < 0.01) of that in SW group at 12 h, 24 h and 48 h, respectively (Fig. 1E). The results indicated the enhanced activation of CgIRF1 during LPS-induced immune responses.

The subcellular localization of CgIRF1 in haemocytes after LPS stimulation

The immunocytochemical assay was conducted to detect the subcellular localization of CgIRF1 protein in haemocytes after LPS stimulation. The CgIRF1 protein was indicated as green, and the nucleus stained by DAPI shown in blue signals. In the control group, the green signals indicating CgIRF1 showed weak fluorescence intensity and mainly distributed in the cytoplasm of haemocyte (Fig. 2). Following 12 and 24 h after LPS stimulation, green fluorescent signals were stronger than that in control group and showed increased distribution in nucleus of haemocytes (Fig. 2). These results indicate that LPS induced CgIRF1 translocation from the cytoplasm to the nucleus, suggesting its potential role as a transcriptional regulator in haemocytes during LPS stimulation.

The interactive relationship of CgIRF1 with CgIRF8

IRF1 and IRF8 have been reported to directly interact physically through their IAD (IRF association domain) regions to form heterodimers, and synergistically regulate many downstream gene expression [6]. In order to identify the interaction of CgIRF1 and CgIRF8, their recombinant proteins (rCgIRF1 and rCgIRF8) were obtained by prokaryotic expression and detected by SDS-PAGE. The purified rCgIRF1 and rCgIRF8 showed distinct single band of approximately 47 kDa (with His tag) (Fig. 3A) and 97 kDa (with MBP tag) (Fig. 3B), respectively, which was consistent with their predicted molecular mass. The binding activity of rCgIRF1 and rCgIRF8 was detected and analyzed using BLI in vitro. As shown in Fig. 3C, significant binding responses between rCgIRF1 and rCgIRF8 were detected across different concentrations with a KD value of 5.2 × 10–7 M, indicating a potential physical interaction between the two proteins.

The proportion of EdU positive haemocytes in CgIRF1-RNAi oysters after LPS stimulation

Our previous work has identified CgIRF8 as a key transcription factor involved in haemocyte proliferation during the LPS induced immune response [25]. The interaction between CgIRF1 and CgIRF8 implies a cooperative mechanism in regulating haemocyte proliferation. To explore whether CgIRF1 is involved in the regulation of haemocytes proliferation, the specific siRNA of CgIRF1 were designed and employed to interference its expression. After injection of CgIRF1-siRNA, the mRNA expression level of CgIRF1 in haemocytes was significantly down-regulated after LPS injection, which was about 0.08-fold of that in the NC group (p < 0.001) (Fig. 4A), achieving a knockdown efficiency of over 85%. The EdU positive signals indicating the renewal circulating haemocytes were determined by flow cytometry. The percentage of EdU positive haemocytes in CgIRF1-RNAi oyster was decreased significantly, which was 0.37-fold of that in NC group (Fig. 4B&C). The above results suggest that CgIRF1 might plays a role in regulating haemocyte proliferation by binding with CgIRF8.

The expression of hematopoiesis related genes in CgIRF1-RNAi oysters after LPS stimulation

To clarify the regulated roles of CgIRF1 in the proliferation of haemocytes, the expression levels of hematopoietic transcription factor (CgGATA, CgRunx and CgSCL) and cell cycle regulatory protein (CgCDK2, CgCDC6 and CgCDC45) in CgIRF1-RNAi group were detected by qRT-PCR. The expression level of hematopoietic transcription factor CgGATA and CgRunx decreased significantly in the CgIRF1-RNAi group, which was about 0.14-fold (p < 0.001) and 0.11-fold (p < 0.05) of that in the NC group, respectively (Fig. 5A&B). While expression level of CgSCL showed an upward trend in the CgIRF1-RNAi group compared with the control group, which was about 1.58-fold (p < 0.001) of that in the NC group (Fig. 5C). The expression level of cell regulatory protein CgCDK2, CgCDC6 and CgCDC45 was significantly decreased in the CgIRF1-RNAi group, which were 0.61 (p < 0.01), 0.32 (p < 0.001) and 0.40-fold (p < 0.001) of that in the NC group, respectively (Fig. 5D-F). The results showed suggested the important roles of CgIRF1 in haemocyte proliferation by regulating proliferation related gene expression.

Discussion

IRF1 is a key transcription factor that critically regulates immune cell development and differentiation, as well as antiviral and antibacterial innate immune responses [4]. IRF1 homologs have been identified in mollusc, such as CfIRF1 from C. farreri [23] and CgIRF1 from C. gigas [22]. They responded to immune stimulation and showed ISRE-binding activity, which was consistent with their putative roles in the IFN-like system. Our previous findings revealed that CgIRF1 and CgIRF8 played critical roles in CgIFNLP-mediated antiviral responses in the Pacific oyster C. gigas [22,40]. And CgIRF8 serves as a key regulator of haemocyte proliferation during LPS-induced immune responses [25]. In the present study, an interaction between CgIRF1 and CgIRF8 was observed, as well as the high expression of CgIRF1 after LPS stimulation. Hence, the involvement of CgIRF1 in haemocyte proliferation was further examined, aiming to advance current knowledge of invertebrate hematopoiesis.

IRF1 is an evolutionarily conserved protein known to regulate various aspects of innate immunity, including antiviral defense [5]. Human IRF1 harbors a N-terminal DBD for target gene recognition, and a C-terminal IAD2 involving in protein–protein interactions [41]. Like its homologs, oyster CgIRF1 features a conserved N-terminal DBD and a less conserved IAD2 domain [22], suggesting functional divergence in downstream signaling. IRF1 is constitutively expressed at low levels in a wide range of normal cells but can be rapidly induced by diverse pathogen-associated molecular patterns, including poly (I:C) and LPS [5]. IRF1 is highly expressed in a variety of immune-related organs and tissues, as well as in rapidly dividing cell populations, including malignant tumors [9]. For instance, IRF1 from teleost such as golden pompano Trachinotus ovatus was rich expression in known immune-associated tissues, including whole blood, gill, and head-kidney [42]. CgIRF1 is most highly expressed in haemocytes, suggesting its core role in oyster immune defense [22]. IRF-1 expressed in the inner cell mass (ICM) of porcine early blastocysts enhances the pluripotency of porcine induced pluripotent stem cells (piPSCs), in part by promoting the JAK-STAT pathway [43]. Our findings reveal that CgIRF1 showed relatively high expression in oyster cleavage stages, followed by downregulation in later developmental phases. IRF9 from blunt snout bream (Megalobrama amblycephala) appears to play important roles around hatching and during the larval development [44]. IRF-1 functions as a negative regulator of Il23α in LPS-stimulated bone marrow-derived macrophages (BMMs) [45]. In the present study, LPS challenge induced upregulation of CgIRF1 in both mRNA and protein levels in haemocyte, suggesting its potential roles in LPS induced immune responses. IRF1 deficiency attenuated LPS-induced mRNA expression of TLR3, TLR6 and TLR9 in murine macrophages [46]. IRF1 is diffusely distributed in the cytoplasm, but could be instantly activated upon stimulation and translocated into the nucleus [47]. Consistently, the nuclear translocation of CgIRF1 was observed in haemocytes after LPS stimulation. These findings suggest that CgIRF1 is functionally implicated in the LPS triggered immune response in oyster haemocytes.

IRF1 is a key hematopoietic transcription factor involved in immune cell proliferation and differentiation [48]. Upregulation of IRF1 suppressed cell proliferation and colony formation, induced G1 cell cycle arrest, promoted cell apoptosis, and increased the sensitivity of CRC cells to X-ray irradiation [49]. Oyster haemocytes are considered as the main immune cells, which are primarily responsible for the defense against pathogens [26]. The anatomical sites for hematopoiesis differ greatly across mollusc taxa. Haemocytes in gastropods are produced in the pericardial area, typically the anterior pericardial wall [50]. Haemocytes in cephalopods are believed to derive from the white body, a multilobed organ surrounding the optic bundle [51]. While in bivalves, the IFS of gills has been proposed as the hematopoietic site for haemocyte generation [29]. However, hematopoietic sites in most mollusc have not been clearly documented, and multiple primary hematopoietic sites may exist within each group [52]. CgIRF8 was identified as a key regulator of haemocyte proliferation in oysters [25]. Consistently, CgIRF1-RNAi led to a significant reduction in haemocyte production upon LPS stimulation. Moreover, interaction of CgIRF1 and CgIRF8 was observed by BLI. IRF1 commonly mediates gene regulation by forming homo or heterodimers via its IAD2 domain, partnering with other IRF proteins such as IRF8, as well as co-regulatory transcription factors [9]. IRF1 functionally interact with IRF8 to mediate IFN-γ induced activation of genes that are critical for macrophage antimicrobial defense [53]. IRF8 and IRF1 synergistically regulate basal and IFN-γ-induced transcriptional programs in macrophages, and participate in the proliferation and differentiation of myeloid cells [54]. Therefore, CgIRF1 might regulate haemocyte proliferation by binding with CgIRF8. CgIRF1-RNAi downregulated the hematopoietic transcription factor CgGATA and CgRunx as well as the mRNA expression of cell cycle proteins, but upregulated CgSCL. GATA-2 is critical for the maintenance and expansion of immature hematopoietic progenitors, whereas GATA-1 is essential for erythroid progenitor survival and terminal erythroid differentiation [55]. Runx family are highly conserved transcription factors essential for hematopoiesis and lineage specification in both development and adulthood [56]. SCL/TAL1 is a master transcriptional regulator essential for both normal hematopoiesis and its pathological dysregulation [57]. Definitive HSC emerge from the embryo proper via an endothelial-to-hematopoietic transition controlled by Runx1, acting with SCL and GATA2 [57]. Recent studies have revealed an evolutionarily conserved role for GATA, Runx and SCL in immune cell development in invertebrates, such as the sea urchin and the Pacific oyster [[58], [59], [60]]. CgGATA, CgRunx and CgSCL in Pacific oyster showed essential roles in haemocyte proliferation, and CgGATA was the target gene of CgIRF8 [59,25,60]. Therefore, CgIRF1 might translocated into cell nucleus to co-work with CgGATA, CgRunx to mediated haemocyte proliferation. However, the expression of CgSCL was increased after CgIRF1-RNAi, hinting the unknown pathway involving the CgIRF1 mediated immune response. Cell cycle proteins form the core machinery for DNA replication and cell-cycle progression: CDC6 and CDC45 are responsible for assembling the pre-replication complex and initiating replication. CDK2 provides cell-cycle-dependent kinase activity to drive entry into S phase [61], and PCNA acts as the sliding clamp for DNA polymerase [62], ensuring efficient elongation of the replication process. Therefore, CgIRF1 might cooperate with CgIRF8 to mediate haemocyte proliferation by inducing expression of key proliferation-related factors.

In conclusion, LPS stimulation induced the activation and nuclear translocation of CgIRF1 in oyster haemocytes. CgIRF1 might exhibit binding activity with CgIRF8, and mediate haemocyte proliferation by transcriptionally regulating multiple hematopoietic transcription factors and cell cycle–related proteins. Collectively, these findings identify CgIRF1 as an important immune regulator that involve in regulation of haemocyte homeostasis in Pacific oyster C. gigas.

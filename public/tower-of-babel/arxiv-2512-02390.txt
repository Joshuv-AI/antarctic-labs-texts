Error estimates for semi-Lagrangian schemes with higher-order interpolation for conservation laws with dispersive terms
Haruki Takemura

Abstract
. We establish error estimates for semi-Lagrangian schemes for the initial value problem of one-dimensional conservation laws with a dispersive term, including the Korteweg–de Vries equation. The schemes considered in this paper are based on the semi-Lagrangian technique combined with spatial discretization by higher-order interpolation operators. For the semi-Lagrangian schemes equipped with the spline or Hermite interpolation operators of order 2 ​ s − 1 2s-1 , we derive an L 2 L^{2} -error estimate of O ⁡ ( Δ ​ t r + h 2 ​ s / Δ ​ t ) O(\Delta t^{r}+h^{2s}/\Delta t) and an H s H^{s} -error estimate of O ⁡ ( Δ ​ t r + h s / Δ ​ t ) O(\Delta t^{r}+h^{s}/\sqrt{\Delta t}) , where h h and Δ ​ t \Delta t denote the spatial mesh size and the time step size, respectively, and r ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] r\in\lparen 0,1] is a parameter determined by the discretization of the dispersive term. A key step in the analysis is to establish the stability of the interpolation operators. Under suitable assumptions, interpolation operators of order 2 ​ s − 1 2s-1 are stable with respect to the H s H^{s} -norm as well as a weighted H s H^{s} -norm. The weighted H s H^{s} -norm depends on h h and Δ ​ t \Delta t , and it reduces to the L 2 L^{2} -norm in the limit h → 0 h\to 0 .



1.
Introduction

The initial value problem of the dispersive conservation law is given by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}F(u)+\nu\partial_{x}^{3}u=0\quad

\displaystyle\text{in}\quad\mathbb{T}\times(0,T),

(1.2)

\displaystyle u(0)=u_{0}\quad

\displaystyle\text{in}\quad\mathbb{T},

where
\nu
is a positive constant,
\mathbb{T}
denotes
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, and
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a sufficiently smooth function. A typical example is the Korteweg–de Vries (KdV) equation obtained by substituting
F(u)=\frac{1}{2}u^{2}
. Throughout this paper, suppose that the problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) admits a unique smooth solution.

We consider the fully semi-Lagrangian (SL) schemes for the initial value problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) proposed in
[
5
]
. Before considering schemes for (
1.1
)–(
1.2
), we briefly recall the main ideas underlying fully SL approaches. The fully SL schemes have been extensively studied as numerical methods for diffusive conservation laws; see, for example,
[
4
,
3
]
. The derivation of the schemes for diffusive conservation laws is based on the probabilistic representation of solutions via the Feynman–Kac formula, as explained in
[
7
,
11
]
, where the schemes are introduced as layer methods. As emphasized in
[
11
]
, although the derivation is based on stochastic discussion, the resulting schemes are entirely deterministic. Moreover, problems with the Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions have been treated in this framework in
[
10
,
9
]
.

In
[
5
]
, the fully SL schemes are generalized to conservation laws containing higher-order derivative terms

(1.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}F(u)+\nu\partial_{x}^{l}u=0\quad

\displaystyle\text{in}\quad\mathbb{T}\times(0,T),

(1.4)

\displaystyle u(0)=u_{0}\quad

\displaystyle\text{in}\quad\mathbb{T},

where
l\geq 3
is an integer. For the case
l=3
, two fully SL schemes were proposed as follows. Let
N_{x},N_{t}\in\mathbb{N}
,
0=x_{0}<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{N_{x}}=1
,
\Delta t=T/N
, and
t_{n}=n\Delta t
. We denote the numerical approximation for
u(x_{j},t_{n})
by
u_{j}^{n}
and denote the vector
(u_{1}^{n},\ldots,u_{N_{x}}^{n})
by
U^{n}
. Let
h=\max_{j}|x_{j}-x_{j-1}|
, and let
\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{h}:\mathbb{R}^{N_{x}}\to C(\mathbb{T})
be an interpolation operator satisfying
\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{h}[(v_{1},\ldots,v_{N_{x}})](x_{j})=v_{j}
for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,N_{x}\}
. Thus,
\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{h}[U^{n}]:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
represents the numerical solution at
t=t_{n}
. In the numerical experiments of
[
5
]
, the linear interpolation and the cubic spline interpolation are used as
\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{h}
. In addition to these, we also consider the higher-order spline and Hermite interpolations in the present paper.

We denote
\partial_{u}F(u)
by
f(u)
. Then we can rewrite (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) as

(1.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+f(u)\partial_{x}u+\nu\partial_{x}^{3}u=0\quad

\displaystyle\text{in}\quad\mathbb{T}\times(0,T),

(1.6)

\displaystyle u(0)=u_{0}\quad

\displaystyle\text{in}\quad\mathbb{T}.

The fully SL schemes for the problem (
1.5
)–(
1.6
) can be written as

(1.7)

\displaystyle u_{j}^{0}

\displaystyle=u_{0}(x_{j}),

(1.8)

\displaystyle u_{j}^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\gamma,\lambda)\in\Lambda}\gamma\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{h}[U^{n-1}](x_{j}-f(u_{j}^{n})\Delta t+\lambda(\nu\Delta t)^{1/3}).

In
[
5
]
, two choices for the parameter set
\Lambda
are proposed. One is given by

(1.9)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{4}=\left\{\left(-\frac{1}{4},\sqrt[3]{4}\right),\left(\frac{1}{4},0\right),\left(\frac{3}{4},\sqrt[3]{4}\right),\left(-\frac{1}{4},2\sqrt[3]{4}\right)\right\},

and the other is given by

(1.10)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{5}=\left\{\left(\frac{3}{16},-2\right),\left(\frac{3}{8},0\right),\left(\frac{3}{4},2\right),\left(-\frac{3}{8},4\right),\left(\frac{1}{16},6\right)\right\}.

The derivation of the scheme (
1.7
)–(
1.8
) does not rely on the Feynman–Kac formula. Instead, the parameter sets
\Lambda_{4}
and
\Lambda_{5}
are chosen so that the resulting schemes satisfy appropriate consistency and stability conditions. See Section 3 of
[
5
]
for details on the construction of the parameter set
\Lambda
. As shown in
[
5
]
, the local truncation error of the scheme (
1.7
)–(
1.8
) with a
q
-th order interpolation operator is
O(\Delta t^{1/3}+h^{q}/\Delta t)
for
\Lambda_{4}
and
O(\Delta t^{2/3}+h^{q}/\Delta t)
for
\Lambda_{5}
.

In the literature, both explicit and implicit fully SL schemes have been studied (see, for example,
[
8
,
7
]
). However, in this paper, we restrict ourselves to the implicit one, which enables us to treat solutions with large gradients. Due to the implicit nature of the scheme, we use an iteration method to solve (
1.8
) for
u_{j}^{n}
. However, this does not cause significant difficulty, since the unknown of (
1.8
) is a single scalar
u_{j}^{n}
for each
(j,n)\in\{1,\ldots,N_{x}\}\times\{1,\ldots,N_{t}\}
. For fixed
n
, once
U^{n-1}
has been obtained, the values
u_{j}^{n}
(
j\in\{1,\ldots,N_{x}\}
) can be computed in parallel.

SL schemes possess advantages that are well suited for nonlinear dispersive conservation laws. As a first example, SL methods can use relatively large time steps. Moreover, according to the numerical experiments reported in
[
5
]
, the implicit SL scheme with cubic spline interpolation is more efficient than certain finite difference schemes, that is, it achieves smaller errors for the same CPU time.

Several types of interpolation have been employed in the construction of SL schemes. SL schemes with spline interpolation have been discussed in a number of earlier studies
[
14
,
1
,
16
]
. Whereas spline interpolation is global, one may use local interpolations such as the Hermite interpolations. The cubic interpolated pseudo-particle (CIP) scheme is a SL scheme that employs the cubic Hermite interpolation. Early works
[
18
,
19
]
studied the CIP schemes for advection equations and hydrodynamic equations. A CIP scheme for the KdV equation was later proposed in
[
21
]
, although it is different from (
1.7
)–(
1.8
). Even for diffusive conservation laws, there are only a few theoretical results concerning fully SL schemes equipped with the Hermite interpolation operators. Mathematical analysis of the CIP scheme for the linear advection equation has been conducted in
[
12
,
20
,
6
]
. Convergence of SL schemes with Hermite interpolations for Vlasov–Poisson equations was investigated in
[
2
]
.

In the present paper, we establish
L^{2}
- and
H^{s}
-error estimates for the scheme (
1.7
)–(
1.8
) with higher-order interpolation operators. We impose a few conditions on the interpolation operator
\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{h}
, and these conditions are satisfied when the spline or Hermite interpolations are employed. In our previous paper
[
17
]
, we derived error estimates for fully SL schemes for diffusive conservation laws. For the scheme (
1.7
)–(
1.8
), we can adopt a similar strategy. A key lemma asserts that the spline or Hermite interpolations of order
2s-1
are stable with respect to the
H^{s}
-norm and the weighted
H^{s}
-norm defined later. We note that the weighted
H^{s}
-norm becomes the
L^{2}
-norm in the limit
h\to 0
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we reformulate the scheme (
1.7
)–(
1.8
) in terms of continuous functions on
\mathbb{T}
as a preparation for error analysis. In Section
3
, we provide error estimates. In Section
4
, we give the proofs of the main theorems in Section
3
. Numerical results are presented in Section
5
.

2.
Reformulation of the fully semi-Lagrangian scheme

2.1.
Derivation of the scheme

We introduce some notation. We denote the norms of Sobolev spaces
L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
and
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{T})
by
\left\|\,\cdot\,\right\|_{0,p}
and
\left\|\,\cdot\,\right\|_{s,p}
, respectively. We also use the Sobolev seminorm
\left|\,\cdot\,\right|_{s,p}=\left\|\partial_{x}^{s}(\,\cdot\,)\right\|_{0,p}
. For a positive integer
s
, we define

(2.1)

\displaystyle\left\|v\right\|_{s,2,\ast}=\left(\left\|v\right\|_{0,2}^{2}+\left|v\right|_{s,2}^{2}\right)^{1/2},

which is equivalent to the standard
H^{s}
-norm. We further define the norm
\left\|\,\cdot\,\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}
, which depends on the discretization parameter
\Delta=(h,\Delta t)
, by

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left\|v\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}=\left(\left\|v\right\|_{0,2}^{2}+\frac{h^{2s}}{\Delta t}\left|v\right|_{s,2}^{2}\right)^{1/2},

and refer to it as the weighted
H^{s}
-norm. We denote the Bochner space
W^{k,p}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathbb{T}))
by
W^{k,p}(H^{s})
.

In this paper,
C
denotes generic positive constants, independent of
h
and
\Delta t
, whose value may change from line to line. We say that a positive quantity
Q
depends polynomially on
A_{1},\ldots,A_{l}
when there exists a polynomial
\Phi(A_{1},\ldots,A_{l})
such that

(2.3)

\displaystyle Q\leq\Phi(A_{1},\ldots,A_{l}).

We use
C_{\mathrm{P}}(A_{1},\ldots,A_{l})
to denote a generic constant which depends polynomially on
A_{1},\ldots,A_{l}
.

Let us briefly recall the derivation of the fully SL scheme (
1.7
)–(
1.8
). Let a function
w:\mathbb{T}\times[0,T]\ni(x,t)\mapsto w(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}
be Lipschitz continuous with respect to
x
. For
(x,t)\in\mathbb{T}\times[0,T]
, let us define the trajectory
X[w]:(0,T)\to\mathbb{T}
by

(2.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}X[w](s;x,t)=w(X[w](s;x,t),s)\quad

\displaystyle\text{in}\quad\mathbb{T}\times(0,T),

(2.5)

\displaystyle X[w](t;x,t)=x.

Let
u
be a smooth solution to (
1.5
)–(
1.6
), and let
x\in\mathbb{T}
be fixed.
For any
s\in[0,T]
and a sufficiently smooth function
v:\mathbb{T}\times(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}
, we have

(2.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}u(X_{s},s)=\partial_{t}u(X_{s},s)+f(u(X_{s},s))\partial_{x}u(X_{s},s),

where
X_{s}=X[f\circ u](s;x,t_{n})
. We denote
u(\,\cdot\,,t_{n})
by
u^{n}
. Integrating (
2.6
) with respect to
s
, we have

(2.7)

\displaystyle u^{n}(x)

\displaystyle=u^{n-1}(X[f\circ u](t_{n-1};x,t_{n}))

(2.8)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\left[\partial_{t}u(X_{s},s)+f(u(X_{s},s))\partial_{x}u(X_{s},s)\right]\mathrm{d}s.

Using (
1.5
), we have

(2.9)

\displaystyle u^{n}(x)

\displaystyle=u^{n-1}(X[f\circ u](t_{n-1};x,t_{n}))-\nu\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\partial_{x}^{3}u(X[f\circ u](s;x,t_{n}),s)\mathrm{d}s.

We now approximate the second term on the right-hand side of (
2.9
). By (
1.9
),
\Lambda_{4}
satisfies

(2.10)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{(\gamma,\lambda)\in\Lambda_{4}}\gamma\lambda^{k}=\begin{cases}1&(k=0),\\
0&(k=1,2),\\
-1&(k=3),\end{cases}

where we use
0!=1
. Similarly, (
1.10
) implies

(2.11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{(\gamma,\lambda)\in\Lambda_{5}}\gamma\lambda^{k}=\begin{cases}1&(k=0),\\
0&(k=1,2,4),\\
-1&(k=3).\end{cases}

By (
2.10
), (
2.11
) and Taylor’s theorem, we have

(2.12)

\displaystyle\sum_{(\gamma,\lambda)\in\Lambda}\gamma v(x+\lambda(\nu\Delta t)^{1/3})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{4}\sum_{(\gamma,\lambda)\in\Lambda}\frac{1}{k!}\gamma\lambda^{k}(\nu\Delta t)^{k/3}\partial_{x}^{k}v(x)+O((\nu\Delta t)^{5/3})

(2.13)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}v(x)-\nu\Delta t\partial_{x}^{3}v(x)+O((\nu\Delta t)^{4/3})&(\Lambda=\Lambda_{4}),\\
v(x)-\nu\Delta t\partial_{x}^{3}v(x)+O((\nu\Delta t)^{5/3})&(\Lambda=\Lambda_{5}).\end{cases}

Thus, we have

(2.14)

\displaystyle u^{n-1}(X[f\circ u](t_{n-1};x,t_{n}))-\nu\Delta t\partial_{x}^{3}u^{n-1}(X[f\circ u](t_{n-1};x,t_{n}))

(2.15)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\gamma,\lambda)\in\Lambda}\gamma u^{n-1}(X[f\circ u](t_{n-1};x,t_{n})+\lambda(\nu\Delta t)^{1/3})+O((\nu\Delta t)^{1+r}),

where

(2.16)

\displaystyle r=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{3}&(\Lambda=\Lambda_{4}),\\
\frac{2}{3}&(\Lambda=\Lambda_{5}).\end{cases}

We also use the approximation

(2.17)

\displaystyle\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\partial_{x}^{3}u(X[f\circ u](s;x,t_{n}),s)\mathrm{d}s=\Delta t\partial_{x}^{3}u^{n-1}(X[f\circ u](t_{n-1};x,t_{n}))+O(\Delta t^{2}).

Applying (
2.15
) and (
2.17
) to (
2.9
), we obtain

(2.18)

\displaystyle u^{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\gamma,\lambda)\in\Lambda}\gamma u^{n-1}(X[f\circ u](t_{n-1};x,t_{n})+\lambda(\nu\Delta t)^{1/3})+O(\Delta t^{1+r}).

Furthermore, by the backward Euler method, we have the approximation

(2.19)

\displaystyle X[f\circ u](t_{n-1};x,t_{n})\approx x-f(u^{n}(x))\Delta t.

Applying (
2.19
) to (
2.18
), and then replacing
u^{n}
by
\bar{u}^{n}
, we obtain the semi-discrete scheme in time

(2.20)

\displaystyle\bar{u}^{n}(x)=\sum_{(\gamma,\lambda)\in\Lambda}\gamma\bar{u}^{n-1}(x-f(\bar{u}^{n}(x))\Delta t+\lambda(\nu\Delta t)^{1/3}).

By introducing the spatial interpolation
\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{h}
, we obtain the fully discrete scheme (
1.7
)–(
1.8
). This completes the derivation of the scheme.

2.2.
Reformulation of the scheme

The scheme (
1.7
)–(
1.8
) is written in terms of the numerical solution at the discrete grid points
(x_{j},t_{n})
. In this subsection, we rewrite this scheme in terms of the numerical solutions
u_{h}^{n}
defined on the domain
\mathbb{T}
. For any
v\in C(\mathbb{T})
, we define
X_{\Delta t}^{1}[v]:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}
by

(2.21)

\displaystyle X_{\Delta t}^{1}[v](x)=x-v(x)\Delta t.

The function
X_{\Delta t}^{1}[f\circ u^{n}]:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}
is the first-order approximation of
X[f\circ u](t_{n-1};\,\cdot\,,t_{n})
obtained by the backward Euler method. Let
s\geq 2
,
\Delta t\in(0,1)
,
f\in W^{s,\infty}(\mathbb{T})
and
D_{f,\Delta t}=\{v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})\,|\,\left|v\right|_{1,\infty}<(3\left|f\right|_{1,\infty}\Delta t)^{-1}\}
. For
v\in D_{f,\Delta t}
, there exists a unique function
w\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
such that

(2.22)

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle=v\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ w].

We define the operator
\mathcal{S}_{f,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}:D_{f,\Delta t}\to H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
by
\mathcal{S}_{f,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}(v)=w
with (
2.22
). The existence and uniqueness of
w
satisfying (
2.22
) are ensured by the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1

(Lemmas 2.1 and A.1 of
[
17
]
)
.

Let
s\geq 2
be an integer. Assume that
v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
and
f\in W^{s,\infty}(\mathbb{T})
. Suppose that

(2.23)

\displaystyle 3\Delta t\left|f\right|_{1,\infty}\left|v\right|_{1,\infty}\leq 1

is satisfied. Then the following three statements hold:

(i)

There exists a unique function
w\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
such that

(2.24)

\displaystyle w=v\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ w].

In the remainder of this lemma, we denote
w
by
\mathcal{S}_{f,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}(v)
.

(ii)

The mapping
X_{\Delta t}^{1}[f\circ\mathcal{S}_{f,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}(v)]:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}
is a bijection.

(iii)

There exists a positive constant
C_{\mathrm{P}}^{(1)}
which depends polynomially only on
\left\|f\right\|_{s,\infty}
and
\left\|v\right\|_{s,2,\ast}
such that

(2.25)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{S}_{f,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}(v)\right\|_{s,2,\ast}\leq\left(1+C_{\mathrm{P}}^{(1)}\Delta t\right)\left\|v\right\|_{s,2,\ast}.

For
\Lambda=\Lambda_{4}
or
\Lambda_{5}
, let
\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{D}}:L^{2}(\mathbb{T})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
be defined by

(2.26)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{D}}v)(x)=\sum_{(\gamma,\lambda)\in\Lambda}\gamma v(x+\lambda(\nu\Delta t)^{1/3}).

In the following, we reformulate the scheme (
1.7
)–(
1.8
) using the operators
\mathcal{S}_{f,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}
and
\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{D}}
. Let
u_{h}^{n}=\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{h}[U^{n}]
. For
n\geq 1
, we have

(2.27)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{D}}u_{h}^{n-1})(x)=\sum_{(\gamma,\lambda)\in\Lambda}\gamma u_{h}^{n-1}(x+\lambda(\nu\Delta t)^{1/3}),

and

(2.28)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{f,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}(\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{D}}u_{h}^{n-1})(x)

(2.29)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\gamma,\lambda)\in\Lambda}\gamma u_{h}^{n-1}(x-f(\mathcal{S}_{f,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}(\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{D}}u_{h}^{n-1})(x))\Delta t+\lambda(\nu\Delta t)^{1/3}).

Suppose that (
1.8
) admits a unique solution
u_{j}^{n}
for each
(j,n)\in\{1,\ldots,N_{x}\}\times\{1,\ldots,N_{t}\}
. Comparing (
1.8
) and (
2.29
), we have

(2.30)

\displaystyle u_{j}^{n}=\mathcal{S}_{f,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}(\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{D}}u_{h}^{n-1})(x_{j}).

Therefore, we have

(2.31)

\displaystyle u_{h}^{n}=\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{h}\left[\Big(\mathcal{S}_{f,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}(\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{D}}u_{h}^{n-1})(x_{j})\Big)_{j=1}^{N_{x}}\right].

Let the interpolation operator
\mathcal{I}_{h}:C(\mathbb{T})\to C(\mathbb{T})
be defined by

(2.32)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{h}v=\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{h}[(v(x_{1}),v(x_{2}),\ldots,v(x_{N_{x}}))].

Then, the fully SL scheme (
1.7
)–(
1.8
) can be rewritten as

(2.33)

\displaystyle u_{h}^{0}=\mathcal{I}_{h}u_{0},

(2.34)

\displaystyle u_{h}^{n}=\mathcal{I}_{h}\left(\mathcal{S}_{f,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}(\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{D}}u_{h}^{n-1})\right).

By Lemma
2.1
, the function
u_{h}^{n}
is uniquely determined by (
2.34
) provided that

(2.35)

\displaystyle\Delta t\left|f\right|_{1,\infty}\left|\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{D}}u_{h}^{n}\right|_{1,\infty}\leq\frac{1}{3}.

As we will see in the proof of Theorem
3.1
later, if the initial value problem (
1.5
)–(
1.6
) admits a sufficiently smooth solution and
\Delta t
is sufficiently small,
\left\|u_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2}
(
s\geq 2
) is bounded by a constant independent of
h
,
\Delta t
and
n
. Under this condition, we obtain the bound

(2.36)

\displaystyle\left|u_{h}^{n}\right|_{1,\infty}\leq C,

where
C
is independent of
h
,
\Delta t
and
n
. Therefore, the condition (
2.35
) is satisfied for all
n
if
\Delta t
is sufficiently small.

We introduce two choices for the interpolation operator
\mathcal{I}_{h}
in the scheme (
2.33
)–(
2.34
). For positive integer
l
, let
\mathbb{P}^{l}(\lparen x_{j-1},x_{j}])
be the set of all polynomials of degree at most
l
on the interval
\lparen x_{j-1},x_{j}]
, and the space of piecewise polynomials
\mathbb{P}_{h}^{l}(\mathbb{T})
be defined by

(2.37)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{h}^{l}(\mathbb{T})=\left\{v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}v|_{\lparen x_{j-1},x_{j}]}\in\mathbb{P}^{l}(\lparen x_{j-1},x_{j}])\text{ for all }j=1,\ldots,N_{x}\right\}.

For
s\geq 2
, the spline interpolation operator
\mathcal{I}_{h,\mathrm{spl}}^{2s-1}:C(\mathbb{T})\to\mathbb{P}_{h}^{2s-1}(\mathbb{T})\cap C^{2s-2}(\mathbb{T})
is defined by

(2.38)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{I}_{h,\mathrm{spl}}^{2s-1}v)(x_{j})=v(x_{j}),\text{ for all }j\in\{1,\ldots,N_{x}\}.

In this paper, when we use the spline interpolation, we suppose that
\mathcal{I}_{h,\mathrm{spl}}^{2s-1}v
is uniquely defined by (
2.38
) for any
v\in C(\mathbb{T})
. The linear and cubic spline interpolations used in
[
5
]
are identical to
\mathcal{I}_{h,\mathrm{spl}}^{2s-1}
with
s=1
and
s=2
, respectively.

Let us introduce another interpolation. We define the Hermite interpolation operator
\mathcal{I}_{h,\mathrm{Her}}^{2s-1}:C^{s-1}(\mathbb{T})\to\mathbb{P}_{h}^{2s-1}(\mathbb{T})\cap C^{s-1}(\mathbb{T})
by

(2.39)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{k}(\mathcal{I}_{h,\mathrm{Her}}^{2s-1}v)(x_{j})=\partial_{x}^{k}v(x_{j}),\text{ for all }(j,k)\in\{1,\ldots,N_{x}\}\times\{0,\ldots,s-1\}.

If Hermite interpolation is used in the scheme (
2.33
)–(
2.34
), the resulting scheme cannot be represented within the framework of (

)–(
1.8
), since we need the numerical approximation of derivatives
\partial_{x}^{k}u(x_{j},t_{n})
(
k=1,\ldots,s-1
).
As a simple example, we exhibit the scheme with the cubic Hermite interpolation operator.
Denote the numerical approximation of
\partial_{x}u(x_{j},t_{n})
by
v_{j}^{n}
, and let
\tilde{U}^{n}=(u_{1}^{n},\ldots,u_{N_{x}}^{n},v_{1}^{n},\ldots,v_{N_{x}}^{n})
.
Let us define the cubic interpolation operator
\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{h,\mathrm{Her}}^{3}:\mathbb{R}^{2N_{x}}\to\mathbb{P}_{h}^{3}(\mathbb{T})\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{T})
be defined by

(2.40)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{h,\mathrm{Her}}^{3}[A](x_{j})=a_{j},\quad\partial_{x}\left(\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{h,\mathrm{Her}}^{3}[A]\right)(x_{j})=b_{j}

for
A=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{N_{x}},b_{1},\ldots,b_{N_{x}})
. Then, the scheme can be written as

(2.41)

\displaystyle u_{j}^{0}

\displaystyle=u_{0}(x_{j}),\quad v_{j}^{0}=\partial_{x}u_{0}(x_{j}),

(2.42)

\displaystyle u_{j}^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\gamma,\lambda)\in\Lambda}\gamma\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{h,\mathrm{Her}}^{3}[\tilde{U}^{n-1}](x_{j}-f(u_{j}^{n})+\lambda(\nu\Delta t)^{1/3}),

(2.43)

\displaystyle v_{j}^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{w_{j}^{n}}{1+w_{j}^{n}f^{\prime}(u_{j}^{n})\Delta t},

where

(2.44)

\displaystyle w_{j}^{n-1}=\sum_{(\gamma,\lambda)\in\Lambda}\gamma\partial_{x}(\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{h,\mathrm{Her}}^{3}[\tilde{U}^{n-1}])(x_{j}-f(u_{j}^{n})+\lambda(\nu\Delta t)^{1/3}).

The numerical experiments in Section
5
will be performed using this scheme.

We prepare some assumptions for the interpolation operators.

(P1)

\tau_{\Delta t}^{n}

\displaystyle\tau_{\Delta t}^{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left[u^{n}(x)-\sum_{(\gamma,\lambda)\in\Lambda}\gamma u^{n-1}(x-f(u^{n}(x))\Delta t+\lambda\delta)\right].

By Taylor’s theorem, we have

(4.10)

\displaystyle u^{n-1}(x-f(u^{n}(x))\Delta t+\lambda\delta)

(4.11)

\displaystyle=u^{n-1}(x)+(-f(u^{n}(x))\Delta t+\lambda\delta)\partial_{x}u^{n-1}(x)

(4.12)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}(-f(u^{n}(x))\Delta t+\lambda\delta)^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}u^{n-1}(x)+\frac{1}{6}(-f(u^{n}(x))\Delta t+\lambda\delta)^{3}\partial_{x}^{3}u^{n-1}(x)

(4.13)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\Delta t^{4/3}}{6}\int_{0}^{-f(u^{n}(x))\Delta t^{2/3}+\lambda\nu^{1/3}}y^{3}\partial_{x}^{4}u(x+\Delta t^{1/3}y,t_{n-1})\,\mathrm{d}y.

1.5
) and (

) to (

), we have

(4.14)

\displaystyle\tau_{\Delta t}^{n}=\tau_{\Delta t,1}^{n}+\tau_{\Delta t,2}^{n}+\tau_{\Delta t,3}^{n}+\tau_{\Delta t,4}^{n},

where

(4.15)

\displaystyle\tau_{\Delta t,1}^{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left(u^{n}(x)-u^{n-1}(x)\right)-\partial_{t}u(x,t_{n-1}),

(4.16)

\displaystyle\tau_{\Delta t,2}^{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(f(u^{n}(x))-f(u^{n-1}(x))\right)\partial_{x}u(x,t_{n-1}),

(4.17)

\displaystyle\tau_{\Delta t,3}^{n}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Delta t}{2}f(u^{n}(x))^{2}+\frac{\Delta t^{2}}{6}f(u^{n}(x))^{3}\partial_{x}^{3}u(x,t_{n-1}),

(4.18)

\displaystyle\tau_{\Delta t,4}^{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\gamma,\lambda)\in\Lambda}\tau_{\Delta t,4,\gamma,\lambda}^{n}(x),

and

(4.19)

\displaystyle\tau_{\Delta t,4,\gamma,\lambda}(x)=\frac{\gamma\Delta t^{1/3}}{6}\int_{0}^{-f(u^{n}(x))\Delta t^{2/3}+\lambda\nu^{1/3}}y^{3}\partial_{x}^{4}u(x+\Delta t^{1/3}y,t_{n-1})\,\mathrm{d}y.

\displaystyle\tau_{\Delta t,1}^{n}(x)

4.3.

The following lemma concerns the hypothesis (D
1
), which is related to the stability of the operator
\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{D}}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
s
be a positive integer, and suppose that
\Lambda
is either
\Lambda_{4}
or
\Lambda_{5}
. For any
v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
, the following inequalities hold:

(4.28)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{D}}v\right\|_{s,2,\ast}\leq\left\|v\right\|_{s,2,\ast},

and

(4.29)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{D}}v\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}\leq\left\|v\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}.

Proof.

For any
y\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the translation operator
\mathcal{T}_{y}:L^{2}(\mathbb{T})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
by
(\mathcal{T}_{y}v)(x)=v(x-y)
(
v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
).
Then, the operator
\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{D}}
can be written as

(4.30)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{D}}v=\sum_{(\gamma,\lambda)\in\Lambda}\gamma\mathcal{T}_{-\lambda\delta}v.

For any
y_{1}
,
y_{2}
,
y_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

(4.31)

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{T}_{y_{1}+y_{2}}v,\mathcal{T}_{y_{1}+y_{3}}v\right)_{\mathbb{T}}=\left(\mathcal{T}_{y_{2}}v,\mathcal{T}_{y_{3}}v\right)_{\mathbb{T}},

where the
L^{2}
inner product
\left(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\right)_{\mathbb{T}}
is defined by
\left(v_{1},v_{2}\right)_{\mathbb{T}}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}v_{1}(x)v_{2}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x
.

First, we consider the case
\Lambda=\Lambda_{4}
. Using (
4.31
), we have

(4.32)

\displaystyle\left(\left\|v\right\|_{0,2}^{2}-\left\|\frac{1}{4}\left(\mathcal{T}_{y}v+v+3\mathcal{T}_{-y}v-\mathcal{T}_{-2y}v\right)\right\|_{0,2}^{2}\right)_{\mathbb{T}}

(4.33)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left(v,v\right)_{\mathbb{T}}-\frac{1}{8}\left(v,\mathcal{T}_{y}v\right)_{\mathbb{T}}-\frac{1}{8}\left(v,\mathcal{T}_{2y}v\right)_{\mathbb{T}}+\frac{1}{16}\left(v,\mathcal{T}_{3y}v\right)_{\mathbb{T}}

(4.34)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16}\left\|\mathcal{T}_{y}v-v-\mathcal{T}_{-y}v+\mathcal{T}_{-2y}v\right\|_{0,2}^{2}\geq 0.

Substituting
y=\sqrt[3]{4}\delta
, we obtain

(4.35)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{D}}v\right\|_{0,2}\leq\left\|v\right\|_{0,2}.

Let
w\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
. Substituting
v=\partial_{x}^{s}w
in (
4.34
), we have

(4.36)

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{D}}w\right|_{s,2}\leq\left|w\right|_{s,2}.

Combining (
4.34
) and (
4.36
), we obtain (
4.28
) and (
4.29
).

Next, we consider the case
\Lambda=\Lambda_{5}
. Using (
4.31
), we have

(4.37)

\displaystyle\left\|v\right\|_{0,2}^{2}-\left\|\frac{3}{16}\mathcal{T}_{2\delta}v+\frac{3}{8}v+\frac{3}{4}\mathcal{T}_{-2\delta}v-\frac{3}{8}\mathcal{T}_{-4\delta}v+\frac{1}{16}\mathcal{T}_{-6\delta}v\right\|_{0,2}^{2}

(4.38)

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{256}\left(5(v,v)_{\mathbb{T}}-4(v,\mathcal{T}_{2\delta}v)_{\mathbb{T}}-4(v,\mathcal{T}_{4\delta}v)_{\mathbb{T}}+4(v,\mathcal{T}_{6\delta}v)_{\mathbb{T}}-(v,\mathcal{T}_{8\delta}v)_{\mathbb{T}}\right)

(4.39)

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{256}\left\|v-2\mathcal{T}_{2\delta}v+2\mathcal{T}_{6\delta}v-\mathcal{T}_{8\delta}v\right\|_{0,2}^{2}\geq 0,

which implies

(4.40)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{D}}v\right\|_{0,2}\leq\left\|v\right\|_{0,2}.

By substituting
w=\partial_{x}^{s}v
to (
4.40
), we obtain

(4.41)

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{D}}w\right|_{s,2}\leq\left|w\right|_{s,2}.

From (
4.40
) and (
4.41
), we deduce (
4.28
) and (
4.29
) for the case
\Lambda=\Lambda_{5}
.
∎

In the following, we give the proof of Theorem
3.1
. The proof consists of three steps:

Step 1.

We first establish the estimate

(4.42)

\displaystyle\left\|u^{n}-u_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+C(u,u_{h}^{n-1})\Delta t\right)\left\|u^{n-1}-u_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast}^{2}

(4.43)

\displaystyle\quad+C(u,u_{h}^{n-1})\Delta t^{1+\bar{\varepsilon}},

for sufficiently small
\Delta t
, where
C(u,u_{h}^{n-1})
and
\bar{\varepsilon}
are positive constants independent of
\Delta t

\displaystyle=\left\|e_{h}^{n}\right\|_{0,2}^{2}+\frac{h^{2s}}{\Delta t}\left|e_{h}^{n}\right|_{s,2}^{2}

(4.120)

\displaystyle\leq(1+3\Delta t)\left\|\mathcal{I}_{h}\eta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}^{2}+\frac{h^{2s}}{\Delta t}\left|\rho_{h}^{n}\right|_{s,2}^{2}

(4.121)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{6}{\Delta t}\left(\left\|\mathcal{I}_{h}\theta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}^{2}+\Delta t^{2}\left\|\mathcal{I}_{h}\tau_{\Delta t}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}^{2}+\left\|\rho_{h}^{n}\right\|_{0,2}^{2}\right).

By (

), we have

\displaystyle\left\|e_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+C\Delta t)\left\|\eta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}^{2}+\frac{C}{\Delta t}\left\|\theta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}^{2}

(4.123)

\displaystyle\quad+6\Delta t\left\|\tau_{\Delta t}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}^{2}+\frac{6}{\Delta t}\left\|\rho_{h}^{n}\right\|_{0,2}^{2}+\frac{h^{2s}}{\Delta t}\left|\rho_{h}^{n}\right|_{s,2}^{2}.

We estimate
\eta_{h}^{n}
. By (
4.2
) and (

), we have

(4.124)

\displaystyle\left\|\eta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}

\displaystyle=\left\|(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}e_{h}^{n}\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ(\mathcal{S}_{f,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u_{h}^{n-1})])\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}

(4.125)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+C_{\mathrm{P}}(\left\|\mathcal{S}_{f,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast})\Delta t\right)\left\|\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}e_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}.

Applying (
4.29
) and (
4.72
) to (
4.125
), we have

(4.126)

\displaystyle\left\|\eta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}\leq\left(1+C_{\mathrm{P}}(\left\|u_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast})\Delta t\right)\left\|e_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}.

Let
h_{0}
and
\Delta t_{0}
be the constants introduced in Step 2 of the proof of Theorem
3.1
. In the following, we assume that
(h,\Delta t)\in(0,h_{0})\times(0,\Delta t_{0})
. Then, we have the bound (
4.100
). Therefore, by (
4.126
), there exists a positive constant
C_{\mathrm{P}}^{(8)}
depending only on

such that

(4.127)

\displaystyle\left\|\eta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}\leq\left(1+C_{\mathrm{P}}^{(8)}\Delta t\right)\left\|e_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}.

Let us estimate
\tau_{\Delta t}^{n}
. From (
3.10
) and (
3.11
), we deduce that

(4.128)

\displaystyle\left\|\tau_{\Delta t}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}

(4.130)

\displaystyle\leq CK(u)\Delta t^{r}.

We estimate
\theta_{h}^{n}
. By (

), we have

(4.131)

\displaystyle\left\|\theta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}

(4.132)

\displaystyle=\left\|(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u^{n-1})\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ u^{n}]-(\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u^{n-1})\circ{X_{\Delta t}^{1}}[f\circ(\mathcal{S}_{f,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u_{h}^{n-1})]\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{P}}(\left\|u^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\ast},\left\|\mathcal{S}_{f,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\ast})\Delta t\left\|\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u^{n-1}\right\|_{s+1,\infty}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\left\|u^{n}-\mathcal{S}_{f,\Delta t}^{\mathrm{A}}\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\mathrm{V}}u_{h}^{n-1}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}.

3.9
), (

) and (

(4.136)

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot(\left\|\eta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}+\left\|\theta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}+\Delta t\left\|\tau_{\Delta t}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}).

) for the numerical solution, we deduce that there exists a positive constant

, independent of the numerical solution

, such that

\displaystyle\left\|\theta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}\leq C^{(9)}\Delta t(\left\|\eta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}+\left\|\theta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}+\Delta t\left\|\tau_{\Delta t}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}).

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-C^{(9)}\Delta t}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

4.138
) to (

), we have

(4.139)

\displaystyle\left\|\theta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}\leq\frac{1}{2}C^{(9)}\Delta t(\left\|\eta_{h}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}+\Delta t\left\|\tau_{\Delta t}^{n}\right\|_{s,2,\Delta}).

) becomes

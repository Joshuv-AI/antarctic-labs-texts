An aqueous in situ approach to discover crystalline coordination materials for adsorptive separation
Authors: Qingqing Yan, Chenyang Nie, Yafei Du, Bochun Zhang, Yi Cheng, Peng Guo, Sujing Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCoordination materials are pivotal for advancing multidisciplinary science. Conventional synthetic methods, solid-, liquid-, and gas-phase, suffer from limited reactant scope and poor control over product formation, hindering fundamental research and applications. Here, we introduce a heterogeneous in situ approach using zero-valent metals as cation sources in water, combined with linker supersaturation. This enables universal, eco-friendly, and scalable synthesis of crystalline coordination materials. We demonstrate its power by solving the challenge of synthesizing titanium coordination compounds, previously inaccessible via conventional routes. The resulting titanium materials display diverse dimensionalities, structural variety, and broad functional-group tolerance, highlighting their promise for adsorptive separation. Notably, the method extends broadly across metals, including rare earths, transition and main-group metals. Thus, it establishes a general paradigm for heterogeneous in situ synthesis in coordination chemistry and enables energy-efficient production of advanced separation materials. Our work redefines coordination material synthesis and accelerates the development of inorganic–organic hybrid solids.Subject terms: Ligands, Organic-inorganic nanostructures, Organic-inorganic nanostructures, Chemical bonding

Introduction

Coordination materials that combine inorganic and organic chemistry have advanced significantly, facilitating progress in multidisciplinary research across physics, materials science, biology, pharmacy, and various branches of chemistry1–6. The synthesis of crystalline coordination materials is crucial for understanding their atomic structures and properties, which supports both scientific inquiry and practical applications7–10. However, current synthesis methods—the gas phase, liquid phase, and solid phase—have notable limitations (Fig. 1a). Gas- and solid-phase syntheses often face restricted reactant scopes due to compatibility issues related to the chemical and physical properties of the reactants used with specific apparatuses11,12. In contrast, liquid-phase synthesis is commonly favoured for its broad applicability13–15, but it poses challenges in controlling product crystallization due to the homogeneous nature of reactions at suitable reactant concentrations when highly reactive chemicals are used or significant side reactions are presented16,17. Therefore, developing alternative synthesis methods that effectively manage reaction rates and crystallization while ensuring good reactant compatibility is essential—a need widely acknowledged in the field.

Approaches for synthesizing coordination materials.

a Traditional synthetic methods face several limitations: both solid and gas phase syntheses are characterized by significantly restricted reactant scopes and an inability to control the reaction process effectively. Furthermore, managing the rates of reaction and crystallization in liquid phase synthesis poses considerable challenges. b The heterogeneous in situ synthetic approach described in this study comprises four key steps: 1) Metal solids remain inert in a linker-saturated solution at room temperature, alongside undissolved linker solids, until the mixture is heated to initiate the reaction; 2) Upon heating, linker molecules in solution coordinate with dissolved metal cations. This interaction shifts the equilibrium, facilitating coordination product formation while promoting the dissolution of both metal and linker into the solution; 3) Nucleation of coordination products occurs as reactant solids diminish. At this stage, three distinct solid phases are present; 4) The final coordination material is formed once all reactants have been completely consumed (blue spheres represent metal particles; light blue spheres indicate dissolved metal cations; aggregated purple needles denote insoluble linker molecules; separated purple needles represent dissolved linker molecules; polyhedra with two colours illustrate crystalline coordination products that have formed).

Among the myriads of coordination materials, the synthesis of titanium-based crystalline coordination compounds in solution poses significant challenges because of their high reactivity and propensity for side reactions18–21. Titanium ions are particularly sensitive to moisture from both atmospheric humidity and solvents, leading to rapid hydrolysis and the formation of titanium oxides as byproducts. As a result, conventional methods for producing these materials often require anhydrous solvents and inert atmospheres, limiting their applicability and scalability. This situation renders the field particularly challenging within coordination chemistry19, thereby establishing it as one of the most appropriate subfields for developing new synthetic methods to tackle the aforementioned challenges. In light of these issues, we propose that alternative heterogeneous in situ strategies—especially aqueous scalable methods—may offer promising solutions for broadening the range of applicable reactants and conditions. If designed to address reaction kinetics and crystallization rates during synthesis, such innovative techniques could pave new paths in this complex field.

Here, we present an in situ strategy for the discovery of crystalline coordination materials by employing a fully heterogeneous synthesis approach, utilizing titanium compounds as a model system. This approach addresses high reactivity challenges by employing zero-valent titanium metal instead of salts and complexes to generate cations through in situ reactions in aqueous acidic media (Fig. 1b). The gradual transformation of titanium metal into trivalent titanium (Ti3+) cations create in situ reaction sites, allowing stepwise reactions with organic linkers and enhancing reactant activity modulation. To address crystallization rate issues, we increase the concentration of organic components to a heterogeneous level. This adjustment maintains a saturated linker concentration throughout the reaction, maximizing coordination reaction competitiveness over hydrolysis side reactions and shifting equilibrium towards coordination product formation. Consequently, various titanium-based coordination compounds can be synthesized using different organic linkers with oxygen coordinative sites, including zero-dimensional (0D) complexes and metal-organic polyhedra (MOPs), one-dimensional (1D) chains, two-dimensional (2D) layers, and three-dimensional (3D) frameworks. The synthesis strategy shows robust tolerance to functional groups on the linkers, enabling the reticular fabrication of related 3D coordination polymers (CPs). Additionally, these innovative coordination materials exhibit broad, valuable adsorptive separation properties, improving gas and vapor separation efficiency and selectivity. This encompasses the selective separation of carbon dioxide from nitrogen (CO2/N2), carbon dioxide from methane (CO2/CH4), methane from nitrogen (CH4/N2), as well as the targeted capture of water vapor over other gases and vapours, and the separation of hexane isomers along with cyclic C6 hydrocarbons. Notably, this in situ approach not only aids in discovering new structures but also enables more efficient synthesis of known compounds. Our findings suggest that this methodology can be easily extended beyond titanium-based materials to include other zero-valent metals from various periodic table groups—such as yttrium (rare-earth), chromium (transition), and tin (main group)—demonstrating a broad scope for both inorganic and organic reactants.

Results

The synthesis of titanium coordination compounds using a specific organic linker involves key parameters such as metal precursors, solvents, temperatures, and pressures. Traditional methods rely on sensitive titanium salts and hazardous organic solvents to create a homogeneous reactant mixture. These processes typically require anhydrous conditions with appropriate temperatures and pressures, necessitating precise control over each parameter to consistently achieve the desired product. This complexity often poses significant challenges for reproduction and scaling up synthesis.

Notably, water, recognized as an environmentally friendly solvent for chemical reactions, is typically excluded from synthesizing titanium compounds because of the extreme sensitivity of titanium cations to water. Additionally, the reported synthesis conditions show limited compatibility with the variety and structural types of available linkers. Therefore, tailored reaction conditions must be developed for each specific linker and titanium precursor combination. Furthermore, controlling the reaction rates and crystallization in titanium-organic systems presents significant challenges, especially when highly crystalline coordinative products suitable for structural analysis are desired.

In response to these complexities, our goal is to develop a water-based in situ approach for synthesizing titanium coordination materials. This method uses inert solid-state titanium precursors while ensuring compatibility with various linkers and structures. Furthermore, it provides enhanced tunability to effectively control reaction rates and crystallization processes, indicating significant advancements in synthesis.

The choice of water as the primary reaction solvent limits stable solid-state titanium precursors to those that do not hydrolyse in water, excluding compounds such as titanium sulfate (Ti(SO4)2), titanium oxysulfate (TiOSO4), and titanium oxide-acetylacetonate (TiO(acac)2). Thus, only titanium oxide and zero-valent titanium metal are viable reactants. Titanium oxide is known for its exceptional stability under standard conditions but can transform into other compounds in concentrated acids and bases, which are unsuitable for coordination reactions. In contrast, titanium metal shows tunable reactivity in diluted acidic aqueous solutions, with its reaction rate adjustable by varying the heating temperature. This makes it an ideal solid precursor for coordination reactions, despite being overlooked in previous reports on the preparation of coordinative compounds. Diluted hydrochloric acid (HCl) was ultimately selected to dissolve titanium metal because of its cost-effectiveness, coordination capability, and safety compared with other inorganic acids, such as sulfuric acid.

To effectively react with in situ generated Ti3+ ions in acidic aqueous solutions, selecting an appropriate organic linker is crucial. The linker must form strong coordination bonds with Ti3+ ions and exhibit suitable solubility in diluted HCl to facilitate in situ reactions. According to Ralph G. Pearson’s hard-soft-acid-base theory, titanium ions are typical hard acids that prefer strong coordination with oxygen-containing hard bases such as carboxylic acids, phenols, alcohols, and phosphonates22. This classification expands the range of applicable linkers since oxygen-containing compounds are more common than those with other coordinating heteroatoms, especially in natural products. We began our synthesis via the use of oxygen-containing linkers with medium to good solubility—specifically, pyromellitic acid, gallic acid, and squaric acid. After optimizing the conditions through experience, we introduced various small-quantity additives and explored additional linkers with different functional groups for comparable results. This approach led us to identify a diverse array of representative oxygen-based linkers suitable for fabricating titanium coordination compounds under similar reaction conditions (Fig. 2 and Supplementary Figs. 1-6).

Molecular structures of the organic linkers employed in this work.

A diverse array of organic molecules featuring oxygen coordination sites can be utilized for this synthetic approach. These include pure carboxylic acids, linkers based solely on hydroxyl groups, bifunctional linkers that incorporate both carboxylate and hydroxyl functionalities, and phosphonates. The linker moiety may possess either aromatic or aliphatic characteristics. Notably, the primary structure of the linker has extensive coverage of four-membered, five-membered, and six-membered rings. Heterocyclic linkers also represent promising candidates for this application (with carboxylate groups highlighted in red, hydroxyl groups highlighted in purple, phosphonate groups highlighted in pink, halogen-containing groups highlighted in light blue, and nitrogen atoms highlighted in dark blue).

Organic linkers employed in this work can be categorized into four types on the basis of their coordination site characteristics: pure carboxylic acids, pure hydroxyl compounds, bifunctional molecules with both carboxylate and hydroxyl groups, and phosphonates. Pure carboxylic acids such as functionalized terephthalic acids (BDCs) with mono-, di-, and tetra-substitutions vary in size and shape while remaining inert under acidic conditions, allowing the formation of corresponding coordination materials. Aromatic dicarboxylic acids featuring five-membered heterocycles also serve as effective linkers alongside aliphatic dicarboxylic acids such as oxalic acid and 1,4-cyclohexanedicarboxylic acid (1,4-CDA), which perform well under these reaction conditions. Hydroxyl groups can further functionalize carboxylic acids to act as efficient bifunctional linkers. The parent structures have one to four hydroxyl groups—illustrated by mono-substituted BDC and isophthalic acid (IPA), di-substituted BDC and IPA, tri-substituted gallic acid, and tetra-substituted quinic acid. The position of the hydroxyl groups significantly affects the binding affinity for the metal centres in the resulting coordination compound because of differences between the carboxylate and hydroxyl functionalities. When pure hydroxyl linkers are used, aromatic phenolic compounds are effective in this synthesis method. In contrast, aliphatic alcohols do not produce sufficiently crystalline coordination products; this limitation likely arises from rapid coordination processes combined with significant structural flexibility of the linker molecules. The phosphonate linker, which reacts with Ti3+ ions to form the 3D CP MIL-9123 (MIL: Material of Institute Lavoisier), is expected to introduce alternative binding modes and coordination structures.

The coordination reactions under these conditions are entirely heterogeneous. The dispersion of titanium metal particles and excess linker solid in cold diluted HCl showed no noticeable changes before heating. As the temperature increases, titanium gradually dissolves in the diluted HCl, forming Ti3+ ions that disperse homogeneously in the solution and are ready for coordination reactions. This increased solubility due to heating allows for a saturated concentration of the linker at any given temperature, maximizing the coordination efficiency with in situ formed Ti3+ ions while minimizing competitive hydrolysis into titanium oxide. Since the dissolution of titanium metal in diluted HCl is relatively slow and can be controlled by adjusting the heating temperature, it enables controlled slow crystallization of the coordinative product. During this process, the metal solid, linker solid, and coordinative product solid can coexist simultaneously. As more coordinative products form over time, the reactants gradually diminish. Thus, this entire reaction process exhibits significant in situ characteristics that define its inherently heterogeneous nature within this synthetic approach.

It is worthy to note that during the synthesis employing carboxylic acid linkers as reactants, a control experiment was performed using a commercially available TiCl3-HCl aqueous solution as an alternative titanium source. As shown in Supplementary Fig. 7, this preformed TiCl3-HCl solution delivered significantly inferior performance compared to the in situ system comprising metallic titanium and hydrochloric acid, highlighting the essential role of in situ-generated Ti(III) species in achieving optimal catalytic or synthetic outcomes.

Coordination compounds can be classified by their structural dimensionality into 0D complexes, 1D chains, 2D layers, and 3D networks. A compatible synthetic approach allows for the creation of coordinative products across all dimensions using various reactants under similar conditions. We identified all solvable crystal structures obtained through this method—using conventional single-crystal X-ray diffraction (SCXRD) for larger crystals (>100 μm) or continuous rotation electron diffraction (cRED) for smaller samples24–26 (Supplementary Figs. 8–10 and Supplementary Tables 1–19)—and categorized them by structural dimensionality, as shown in Figs. 3 and 4. It should be noted that the reported crystal structure corresponds to the thermodynamically stable, fully oxidized form.

First, 0D coordination compounds, including mononuclear complexes and multinuclear MOPs, are formed by bifunctional linkers with carboxylate and adjacent phenolic hydroxyl groups. This is due to their strong two-site chelating ability, which leads to linker rigidity with limited rotational and stretching freedom. In the structures of USTC-705 (Ti-4,6-2OH-IPA, Ti(C8O6H4)2(H2O)2) and USTC-706 (Ti-4-OH-IPA, Ti(C8O5H4)2(H2O)2) (USTC stands for University of Science and Technology of China), which were constructed with 4,6-dihydroxyisophthalic acid (4,6-2OH-IPA) and 4-hydroxyisophthalic acid (4-OH-IPA) linkers, respectively, the half-salicylate portions coordinate with the Ti4+ centre. This arrangement occupies four sites in the coordination environment to form an equatorial plane while leaving two polar positions available for terminal water molecules (Fig. 3a, b). In contrast, the scenarios in MOP USTC-707 (Ti-2,6-2OH-BDC, Ti2(C8O6H3)3)—composed of 2,6-dihydroxyterephthalic acid (2,6-2OH-BDC) and eight Ti4+ ions—are notably different (Fig. 3c). All oxygen atoms forming the coordination octahedron come from binding sites on the linker; one linker connects two adjacent Ti4+ ions to create a molecular cage with an accessible rectangular window measuring 4 Å × 5 Å. To the best of our knowledge, no octanuclear Ti–MOP material—such as USTC-707, which features potentially extendable binding sites that could be amenable to future structural modifications—has been reported to date27. Notably, the binding functionalities on the opposite side of each benzene ring in all three materials remain uncoordinated. This arrangement promotes intramolecular hydrogen bonding, resulting in a three-dimensional network through weak interactions. These can be seen as examples of hydrogen-bonded organic frameworks (HOFs) based on metalate building blocks28 (Supplementary Figs. 11–13).

Furthermore, titanium coordination polymers with low structural dimensionality, such as 1D chains and 2D layers, can be synthesized via this method. For example, the reaction of benzene-1,2,4-tricarboxylic acid (1,2,4-BTC) as a linker produces a single-strand TiO chain (USTC-708: Ti-1,2,4-BTC, TiO(C9O6H5)2), which is anchored by pairs of carboxylate groups from the BDC moiety and arranged alternately along the chain direction. This configuration enables para-positioned carboxylates to engage in intramolecular weak interactions between adjacent chains (Fig. 3d and Supplementary Fig. 14). Notably, USTC-708 shares a similar linking pattern and structural features with MIL-17829—an Fe3+-based chain structure that connects adjacent metal ions via hydroxyl bridges instead of oxo groups, as in USTC-708.

In addition, a double-layered ladder structure of USTC-709 (Ti-gallic acid, Ti2O(C7O5H3)2(H2O)2), featuring Ti4 cluster building blocks, was synthesized through a reaction with gallic acid (Fig. 3e and Supplementary Fig. 15). Notably, gallate molecules exhibit two distinct connection modes: one mode acts as a bridge within the Ti4 cluster by coordinating its three phenolic sites to two adjacent Ti4+ ions while leaving the carboxylate group uncoordinated; the other mode links neighbouring Ti4 clusters via both carboxylate groups and their two meta-substituted hydroxyl groups. Consequently, the outer surface of this 1D structure is rich in accessible carboxylates and phenolic hydroxyls that facilitate dimensional expansion via hydrogen bonding. Importantly, in both 0D and 1D materials, free coordination sites remaining after forming coordinative structures enable weak intramolecular interactions such as hydrogen bonding and allow potential reactions with other metal ions or clusters (Supplementary Figs. 11–15). This presents significant opportunities for enhancing structural diversity as advanced building blocks.

The 2D layered structure of USTC-710 (Ti-1,4-CDA, TiO(C8O4H8)) was synthesized using cyclohexane-1,4-dicarboxylic acid (1,4-CDA) as a linker, providing inherent backbone flexibility (Fig. 3f). Adjacent Ti4+ ions are connected through shared corner oxo groups, forming one-dimensional TiO chains that serve as secondary building units (SBUs). These chains are linked by two sets of linker molecules in an alternating up and down arrangement to create the final 2D layer (Supplementary Fig. 16). Similar to the difference between MIL-53 and MIL-47 structures, USTC-710 has comparable connectivity and structural patterns with the reported Fe-CDA compound30; however, USTC-710 differs mainly in its ability to substitute hydroxyl groups in the Fe structure with oxo groups in its titanium counterpart.

A total of eleven unreported 3D CPs with unique structures, which can be elucidated through diffraction data, have been successfully identified via this method. This approach demonstrates a broad tolerance for linker diversity within the realm of Ti-based 3D CP synthesis. Among these discoveries, nine distinct structures are derived from BDC linkers featuring various substitutions, including chlorine (Cl), bromine (Br), trifluoromethyl (CF3), hydroxyl (OH), and carboxylate groups. Additionally, one structure is synthesized from IPA-type camphoric acid, whereas the other is constructed via a phosphonate linker employed in the synthesis of MIL-91.

Importantly, all the materials constructed from carboxylate linkers exhibit similar corner-sharing straight 1D (TiO)n chain SBUs. The carboxylate groups of the linker chelate two adjacent Ti4+ ions within the chain, thereby completing the 3D network. As illustrated in Fig. 4a–e, the five structures (USTC-711-714: Ti-2-Br-BDC, TiO(C8O4H3Br); Ti-2,5-2Br-BDC, TiO(C8O4H2Br2); Ti-2-CF3-BDC, TiO(C9O4H3F3); Ti-2,5-2CF3-BDC, TiO(C10O4H2F6); Ti-2,5-2Cl-BDC, TiO(C8O4H2Cl2);), derived from mono- and di-substituted linkers containing halogen functional groups that are less prone to forming intramolecular weak interactions, display analogous connection details and pore opening characteristics comparable to those of the MIL-53 open structure (Supplementary Fig. 17 and Supplementary Table 20)31. This similarity persists despite differences in space groups and unit cell parameters, with the exception of the two USTC-713 compounds, which are isostructural. Like those of MIL-53, the cavities of these as-synthesized samples are occupied by free linker molecules, which facilitate an expanded open pore configuration while simultaneously obstructing pore accessibility. Notably, electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) measurements (Supplementary Fig. 18) indicate the presence of Ti3+ ions in the as-synthesized sample—an occurrence rarely observed in air-stable Ti-based 3D CPs. During thermal treatment aimed at removing trapped free linker molecules from the cavity, Ti3+ ions gradually convert into Ti4+ ions. This transformation allows for a metastable intermediate state characterized by mixed-valence dual presence; under these conditions, the partially activated porous structure exhibits reversible breathing flexibility akin to that seen in MIL-5331,32. However, once all Ti3+ ions have been fully oxidized to Ti4+, this structural flexibility becomes irreversibly lost (Supplementary Figs. 19, 20).

In contrast, crystal structures exhibiting narrow pore configurations were observed when the linkers were capable of generating intramolecular weak interactions or possessed significant steric hindrance. This is exemplified by USTC-715 (Ti-Btec, TiO(C10O8H4)), (Fig. 4f), which is derived from pyromellitic acid (Btec) and forms hydrogen bonds between neighbouring uncoordinated carboxylate groups. Similarly, in the case of USTC-716 (Ti-4Cl-BDC, TiO(C8O4Cl4)) (Fig. 4g), which is derived from tetrachloroterephthalic acid (4Cl-BDC), the linkers maintain a considerable distance from one another to minimize steric repulsion. A notably denser structure (USTC-717: Ti-BHA, Ti2O2(C12O12H2), Fig. 4h) constructed using benzene-1,2,3,4,5,6-hexacarboxylic acid (BHA)—a functionalized BDC linker with the highest substitution level serving as a 4-connected node—displays a markedly different linkage pattern among adjacent TiO chains. Each BHA molecule contributes four carboxylates that effectively bind four closely spaced TiO chains together while leaving two carboxylates at the para position free from binding; these remain within the cavity formed. This arrangement resembles that of the MIL-116 structure33 (Supplementary Fig. 21). Notably, BDC-type linkers lead to a unreported structural type in all reported Ti-based 3D CPs through this synthetic approach. This differs significantly from previously reported Ti-based 3D CP structures using BDC-type linkers34–36.

In addition to the aforementioned structures constructed from derived BDC linkers, IPA-type linkers, such as camphoric acid featuring a semirigid aliphatic cyclic ring moiety (USTC-718: Ti- camphoric acid, TiO(C10O4H14), Fig. 4i), can effectively assemble with this type of linear 1D TiO chain, in contrast to the helical chains observed in the MIP-208 structure generated by 5-NH2-IPA37. Furthermore, bifunctional carboxylate linkers containing phenolic hydroxyl groups, such as 2-OH-BDC (USTC-719: Ti-2-OH-BDC, TiO(C8O5H4), Fig. 4j), surprisingly contribute only carboxylate groups for network formation while leaving the commonly regarded more reactive hydroxyl groups uncoordinated. This results in a structure similar to that of USTC-711-714 but with slightly smaller pore openings (Supplementary Fig. 17 and Supplementary Table 20). These two examples potentially underscore the critical role of reaction conditions in preferentially generating straight 1D TiO chain SBUs. This highlights the considerable potential of this approach for designing and discovering targeted coordination materials through the use of specific carboxylate linkers.

Unlike the exclusive formation of the MIL-91 product23, which features a 1D corner-sharing TiO chain as its SBU and exhibits limited structural flexibility in previous syntheses utilizing the [(piperazine-bis(phosphonic acid)] (PBP) linker, regardless of the titanium precursors employed, this approach yields a flexible framework constructed from chains of monodispersed Ti4+ octahedra (USTC-720: Ti-PBP, Ti2O(C6O6H12N2P2)3). These octahedra are surrounded by phosphonate tetrahedra that are interconnected through naturally dynamic piperazine cycles. The structure demonstrates reversible phase transitions contingent upon the inclusion and removal of guest water molecules (Supplementary Fig. 22), likely due to significantly increased rotational and stretching freedom of the Ti4+ centres compared with those constrained within TiO chains by phosphonates in MIL-91.

This heterogeneous in situ synthetic methodology not only is highly effective in the exploration of coordination compounds with innovative architectures but also has a remarkable capacity for yielding well-established materials. For example, one can synthesize highly crystalline products such as the squarate-pillared titanium oxide 3D framework (USTC-700, Fig. 4l) and a dense titanium coordination network derived from oxalic acid (Supplementary Fig. 23) through this technique under ambient-pressure reflux conditions.

Importantly, this approach transcends the mere production of titanium-based coordination materials; its exceptional versatility facilitates an expansive array of reactants that include various other metals. This encompasses transition metals exemplified by chromium, rare-earth elements represented by yttrium, and main group metals typified by tin. Both previously documented and coordination compounds with unreported new structures can be synthesized via this sophisticated synthetic strategy (Supplementary Note 1, Supplementary Figs. 24–26).

The titanium-based complex materials characterized in this study predominantly exhibit microporous architectures. Their sorption isotherms and corresponding surface areas are presented in Supplementary Figs. 27–32 and Supplementary Table 21, respectively. Furthermore, these materials display excellent thermal and hydrolytic stability (Supplementary Figs. 33–46). Collectively, these favourable physicochemical properties position them as highly promising candidates for diverse functional applications, including adsorption-based separations, catalytic transformations, molecular sensing, proton conduction, and biomedical applications. Within this work, we focus primarily on adsorption-based separation; Fig. 5 highlights several representative performance metrics for both single-function and multifunctional materials, thereby underscoring the practical relevance and versatility of our synthetic strategy in energy- and environment-related technologies.

Adsorptive separation performance of the selected titanium coordination materials discovered in this work.

a USTC-714 selectively adsorbs CO2 over CH4 at 298 K, achieving an IAST selectivity of 196. b USTC-711 effectively separates CO2 from N2 with decent selectivity (IAST 60) and good adsorption capacity. c USTC-707 differentiates between CH4 and N2, showing an IAST selectivity of 4.7. d USTC-720 excels in recognizing water molecules, outpacing interactions with other vapours and gases. e The multifunctional USTC-711 accurately identifies cyclohexane over benzene and cyclohexene at 363 K, demonstrating high uptake ratios linked to high adsorption selectivity. f The multifunctional architecture of USTC-707 distinguishes dibranched C6 isomers from their linear or monobranched counterparts effectively.

For instance, USTC-714, synthesized from 2,5-2Cl-BDC and characterized by an abundant chlorine environment adorning the inner walls of its structural cavity, manifests a significant affinity for carbon dioxide (CO2) over methane (CH4) during adsorptive separation processes. This material achieves a high ideal adsorbed solution theory (IAST) selectivity of 196, positioning it among the most selective 3D CP materials available for such applications38 (Fig. 5a and Supplementary Fig. 47). In a similar vein, USTC-711 demonstrates commendable performance in the adsorptive separation of CO2 and nitrogen (N2) mixtures with an IAST selectivity favouring CO2 over N2 reaching 60 (Fig. 5b and Supplementary Figs. 48, 49), comparable to that exhibited by MIL-53 materials31. Furthermore, USTC-707 shows a pronounced preference for CH4 over N2 with an IAST selectivity of 4.7 (Fig. 5c and Supplementary Fig. 50), aligning closely with other 3D CP materials constructed using simple linkers and economically viable metals38.

In addition to facilitating gas separations, the adsorptive separation of diverse vapours can be adeptly accomplished through the utilization of the porous solids developed in this study. For instance, the ultramicroporous material USTC-720 exhibits notable structural flexibility that empowers it to selectively differentiate water molecules. The adsorption capacity for water vapour can surpass 200 cm³ g−1 under saturated pressure, with a pronounced step observed in its isotherm—likely attributable to the pore opening effect. In stark contrast, other species, whether gaseous or vaporous, are unable to infiltrate the structural cavity, resulting in negligible uptake across the entire pressure spectrum (Fig. 5d and Supplementary Fig. 51). Crucially, such exclusive recognition capabilities for water over alternative substances are seldom encountered in porous materials, particularly within coordination solids39,40. Consequently, USTC-720’s significant proficiency in discerning water molecules positions it as a promising candidate for moisture removal and the synthesis of anhydrous products.

It is noteworthy that materials developed through this synthetic approach not only exhibit single functional applications, as exemplified by USTC-714 and USTC-720, but also demonstrate the potential for multifunctional applications among certain candidates within this material family. For instance, in addition to their commendable ability to separate CO2 from N2, USTC-711 showcases exceptional performance in the challenging adsorptive separation of cyclic C6 mixtures in vapor phases, which include benzene (Bz), cyclohexene (Cye), and cyclohexane (Cya; see Fig. 5e). This structure effectively discriminates against Cya, nearly preventing its entry into the structural pores while simultaneously exhibiting a strong capacity to adsorb both Bz and Cye with high uptake at an elevated working temperature of 363 K, primarily due to the size-exclusion effect exerted by the activated USTC-711 pore structure on Cya molecules. This results in a significantly enhanced combination of high selectivity associated with uptake ratios and substantial adsorption capacity (Supplementary Fig. 52 and Supplementary Table 22), positioning USTC-711 as one of the most effective sorbents available for this separation application—particularly under high working temperatures that closely resemble practical conditions41,42.

Similarly, the multifunctional USTC-707 demonstrates good kinetic separation capabilities in distinguishing the di-branched hexane isomer 2,2-dimethylbutane (22DMB) from its linear counterpart n-hexane (nHEX), as well as from the monobranched 3-methylpentane (3MP) during the adsorptive separation of their vapours (Fig. 5f and Supplementary Fig. 53). Furthermore, its proficiency in adsorptive separation has been supported by its preferential adsorption of xenon over krypton, along with an effective capacity for recognizing iodine (I2) molecules within solution environments (Supplementary Figs. 54, 55).

In addition, UV-vis spectroscopy of the solid samples reveals that phenolic hydroxyl-containing ligands effectively tune the optical properties of the resulting materials43–46 (Supplementary Fig. 56). Specifically, USTC-705 and USTC-706, based on 4,6-2OH-IPA and 4-OH-IPA, exhibit distinct absorption peaks between 280 and 650 nm. USTC-707, derived from 2,6-2OH-BDC, shows absorption in the 280–600 nm range. USTC-709 (gallic acid) and USTC-719 (2-OH-BDC) both display strong absorption across 280–700 nm. In contrast, USTC-700, assembled from fully hydroxylated ligands, absorbs only within 280–500 nm. These results underscore the key role of phenolic hydroxyl ligands in tailoring visible-light responsiveness in titanium-based functional materials, providing a foundation for their potential use in light-driven applications such as photocatalysis and photoelectric conversion.

Consequently, this innovative in situ synthetic methodology not only significantly broadens the structural diversity of coordination compounds but also lays a robust foundation for realizing valuable functionalities that cater to the practical exigencies associated with next-generation coordination materials.

Discussion

The work presented in this paper establishes an efficient synthetic approach that is universally applicable, environmentally friendly, and scalable. We assert that this method offers significant advantages over traditional methodologies. Our results demonstrate that the aqueous in situ technique using zero-valent metals and hydrophilic oxygen linkers effectively regulates both the coordination reaction rate and crystal growth rate. This allows us to achieve target products that are difficult to obtain through classical methods. This innovative strategy has led to the discovery of coordinative materials with diverse structures across all dimensionalities, showing promising potential in various adsorptive separation applications.

For the exemplified system comprising titanium 3D CPs presented in this study, which are characterized by a limited number of published structures and a lack of design and predictive synthesis methodologies, our findings not only significantly enhance structural diversity and tolerance to substituted functional groups but also indicate that the level of design and structure prediction can be substantially improved through the application of this Ti3+ in situ forming method. This approach draws on insights from state-of-the-art principles for synthesizing iron- and aluminium-based coordinative materials, potentially facilitating rapid advancements in the development of titanium coordinative compounds. Furthermore, the extensive range of metals applicable within this methodology opens up possibilities for an explosive discovery rate of hundreds of thousands of new coordinative materials constructed with various metals and linkers. This is particularly relevant for those assembled using natural linkers featuring oxygen-binding sites alongside metals commonly found in biological systems, thereby mimicking biological units and functions.

Furthermore, the applicability of this synthetic approach can be significantly enhanced by substituting HCl with alternative acids, whether inorganic or organic. This is particularly pertinent for those acids that exhibit strong and diverse coordination and linkage capabilities, such as sulfuric acid, nitric acid, phosphoric acid, formic acid, acetic acid, and trifluoroacetic acid—each of which is currently under investigation within our research group. Consequently, the range of potential products can be greatly expanded to encompass a broader variety of inorganic-organic hybrid materials beyond merely coordinative types. This expansion will represent a substantial advancement in both fundamental research and practical applications across multiple scientific disciplines.

Methods

Synthesis of crystalline coordination materials

The general solvothermal synthesis method was as follows

In a 25 mL autoclave reactor, the linker, diluted HCl (3 M) and additives were introduced and stirred at room temperature (RT). While stirring, titanium powder was added to the mixture. The reaction mixture was subsequently heated in an oven at a given temperature for a designated period. After cooling to RT, the product was collected via filtration, washed with ethanol, and then air-dried. The detailed reaction conditions for synthesizing each polymer are provided in Supplementary Note 1.

The general ambient-pressure reflux synthesis method was as follows

In a round bottom flask, the linker and diluted HCl (3 M) or water were introduced and stirred at room temperature (RT). While stirring, zero-valent metal was added to the mixture. The reaction mixture was subsequently refluxed at a given temperature for a designated period. After cooling to RT, the product was collected via filtration, washed with ethanol, and then air-dried. The detailed reaction conditions for synthesizing each polymer are provided in Supplementary Note 1.

Adsorptive separation

Sorption of CO2, N2 and CH4

Single-component adsorption isotherms of CO2, N2 and CH4 at various temperatures were collected on a Micromeritics 3Flex analyser. The sample was activated under dynamic vacuum for 8 h prior to data collection.

Sorption of vapours

Single-component adsorption isotherms of hexane isomers, cyclic C6 hydrocarbons, water, methanol and ethanol vapours were collected on a Belsorp Max X analyser. The adsorbates were degassed through five freeze pump-thaw cycles before analysis. The sample was activated under dynamic vacuum for 8 h prior to data collection.

Iodine adsorption of USTC-707

Adsorption studies were performed by immersing 20 mg activated USTC-707 samples in 10 mL of a 0.01 mmol L−1 iodine/hexane solution. The supernatant solution was used for UV-vis absorbance measurement. The absorbance at the maximum wavelength of iodine (λmax = 523 nm) was selected to calculate the iodine content.

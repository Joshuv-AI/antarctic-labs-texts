Channel Resolvability Using Multiplicative Weight Update Algorithm
Koki Takahashi and Shun Watanabe

Abstract
We study channel resolvability, which is a key problem to proving a strong converse of identification via channel. The literature has only proved channel resolvability using random coding. We prove channel resolvability using the multiplicative weight update algorithm, the first approach to channel resolvability using non-random coding.

In addition to the converse proof technique for the identification, channel resolvability also plays an important role in the context of the wiretap channel

The problem of identification via channel and channel resolvability have been studied extensively

For point-to-point channel, the identification capacity is well understood.

Thus we need to deepen our understanding of identification; see also a recent survey

As a first step toward that direction, we prove channel resolvability with non-random coding. As far as we know, there is no paper about channel resolvability with non-random coding, although various analysis techniques have been developed

In this paper, we use the multiplicative weight update (MWU) algorithm to prove channel resolvability. The MWU algorithm is a widely used algorithm in game theory and learning theory, and it finds a certain minimax value of two-player game
[

The key idea is to use the MWU algorithm instead of the Chernoff bound used in Alswede’s proof

II
Problem Formulation of channel resolvability

Consider an input distribution
P\in{\cal P(X)},
and a channel
W
from input alphabet
{\cal X}
to output alphabet
{\cal Y}
. The goal is to simulate the output distribution

W_{P}:=\sum_{{x}\in{\cal X}}P({x})W_{x}.

Let
{\cal C}=\{{x}_{1},{x}_{2},...,x_{L}\}
be a codebook of size
L
and
W_{{\cal C}}
be the output distribution where inputs are codewords which are converted from uniform random numbers with the codebook
{\cal C}
, i.e.,

W_{{\cal C}}:=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{l=1}^{L}W_{x_{l}}.

A simulation error is measured by the total variation distance
between the output distribution
W_{P}
and
the output distribution
W_{{\cal C}}
of the codebook:

d_{\mathrm{var}}(W_{P},W_{{\cal C}}):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{y\in{\cal Y}}|W_{P}(y)-W_{{\cal C}}(y)|.

The problem is how to construct a codebook
{\cal C}
such that for arbitrary small
\eta
, the total variation distance
d_{\mathrm{var}}(W_{P},W_{{\cal C}})
is smaller than
\eta,
as small size
L
as possible.

For the asymptotic setting of discrete memoryless channel and the worst input distribution, the optimal rate for channel resolvability is given by the Shannon capacity
C(W)

[
20
]
. Our goal of this paper is to achieve this result using non-random coding.

III
Multiplicative weight update algorithm

In this section, we review the MWU algorithm in notations that are compatible with
channel resolvability.
We consider an
L
rounds game between
{\cal X}
-player and
{\cal Y}
-player;

{\cal X}
-player seeks to maximize the cost while
{\cal Y}
-player seeks to minimize
the cost. For
{\cal X}
-player’s strategy
x\in{\cal X}
and
{\cal Y}
-player’s strategy

y\in{\cal Y}
, cost
0\leq m(x,y)\leq 1
is incurred.
In each round
1\leq l\leq L
,
{\cal Y}
-player select a (mixed) strategy, i.e.,
a distribution
f(l,\cdot)
on
{\cal Y}
; then
{\cal X}
-player responds with a
strategy
x_{l}\in{\cal X}
. Our goal is to construct a sequence of mixed strategies

f(1,\cdot),\ldots,f(L,\cdot)
such that the total cost

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{L}\bigg(\sum_{y^{\prime}\in{\cal Y}}f(l,y^{\prime})m(x_{l},y^{\prime})\bigg)

is not too much more than the cost of the
{\cal Y}
-player’s best strategy in hindsight, i.e.,

\displaystyle\min_{y\in{\cal Y}}\sum_{l=1}^{L}m(x_{l},y).

MWU algorithm is an iterative algorithm that obtains approximately the best strategies. MWU algorithm has a few variations, and we use the so-called Hedge algorithm
[
5
]
.
The MWU algorithm reviewed in Algorithm 1 is a classical version of the one presented in
[
22
]
, and its performance is evaluated in the following lemma. For readers’ convenience, we provide a proof of
the lemma in Appendix
-A
; see also
[
5
, Theorem 2.3]
.

Algorithm 1

Multiplicative weight update algorithm

Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2}],
and initialize the weight
w(1,y)=1
for all
y\in{\cal Y}
.
For each step
l=1,2,...,L
:
1. Calculate
{\cal Y}
-player’s mixed strategy as follows:

f(l,y)=\frac{w(l,y)}{\sum_{y^{\prime}\in{\cal Y}}w(l,y^{\prime})},~y\in{\cal Y}.

2.
{\cal X}
-player selects a pure strategy
x_{l}\in{\cal X}
.3. For the cost
(m(x_{l},y):y\in{\cal Y})
, update the weight as follows:

w(l+1,y)=\exp\bigg(-\varepsilon\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{l}m(x_{l^{\prime}},y)\bigg),~y\in{\cal Y}

Lemma 1
.

(MWU algorithm performance)

Fix an arbitrary
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
.
The mixed strategies
f(1,\cdot),\ldots,f(L,\cdot)
of
{\cal Y}
-player obtained
by Algorithm
1
satisfies for every
y\in{\cal Y}
,

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)\sum_{l=1}^{L}\bigg(\sum_{y^{\prime}\in{\cal Y}}f(l,y^{\prime})m(x_{l},y^{\prime})\bigg)\leq\sum_{l=1}^{L}m(x_{l},y)+\frac{\ln{|{\cal Y}|}}{\varepsilon}

IV
Single-shot bound

In this section, we derive a single-shot bound for channel resolvability using the MWU algorithm.
For the later convenience to apply the single-shot bound for the asymptotic setting, we consider truncated measures known as smoothing; e.g. see
[
36
]
.

Definition 2
.

(Truncated measures)

For an arbitrary set
{\cal S}\subseteq{\cal X\times Y}
, and a given channel
W
,
we define a truncated measure as

W_{x}^{\cal S}(y)=W_{x}(y)\mathbf{1}[(x,y)\in{\cal S}],

and a truncated measure with input distribution
P
as

W_{P}^{\cal S}(y)=\sum_{x\in{\cal X}}P(x)W_{x}(y)\mathbf{1}[(x,y)\in{\cal S}].

We define
W_{\cal C}^{\cal S}
similarly.
For the complement
{\cal S}^{c}
, we define
W_{x}^{{\cal S}^{c}},W_{P}^{{\cal S}^{c}},W_{\cal C}^{{\cal S}^{c}}
similarly.

We also consider the support set

\mathsf{s}(W_{P}^{\cal S}):=\{y:W_{P}^{\cal S}(y)>0\},

to define a cost properly.
The following choice of cost enables us to apply the MWU algorithm for channel resolvability.

Definition 3
.

(Cost)

For an input distribution
P
, a given channel
W
, and an arbitrary set
{\cal S}
,
we define cost as

m(x,y)=\begin{cases}\frac{W_{x}^{\cal S}(y)}{W_{P}^{\cal S}(y)}e^{-D_{\mathrm{max}}}&y\in\mathsf{s}(W_{P}^{\cal S})\\
\mathrm{undefined}&y\notin\mathsf{s}(W_{P}^{\cal S})\end{cases},

where

D_{\mathrm{max}}=\max_{(x,y)\in{\cal S}:\atop y\in\mathsf{s}(W_{P}^{\cal S})}\ln{\frac{W_{x}^{\cal S}(y)}{W_{P}^{\cal S}(y)}}.

D_{\mathrm{max}}
ensures that the cost
m(x,y)
is smaller than or equals to
1
for all strategy
(x,y)\in{\cal X}\times\mathsf{s}(W_{P}^{\cal S})
. We run Algorithm
1
for
\mathsf{s}(W_{P}^{\cal S})
instead of
{\cal Y}
; then,
{\cal Y}
-player’s mixed strategy
f(l,\cdot)
is a distribution on
\mathsf{s}(W_{P}^{\cal S})
.
Finally, we define
{\cal X}
-player’s strategy at each round
l
.

Definition 4
.

(
{\cal X}
-player’s strategy)

At each round
1\leq l\leq L
,
{\cal Y}
-player selects a mixed strategy
f(l,y)
for
y\in\mathsf{s}(W_{P}^{\cal S})
. For cost
m(x,y)
,
{\cal X}
-player selects
x_{l}\in{\cal X}
such that

x_{l}=\underset{x}{\mathrm{argmax}}\sum_{y\in\mathsf{s}(W_{P}^{\cal S})}f(l,y)m(x,y).

Note that such
{\cal X}
-player’s strategy
x_{l}
satisfies Lemma
5
.

Lemma 5
.

(
{\cal X}
-player’s strategy performance)

At each round
l
, after
{\cal Y}
-player’s mixed strategy
f(l,y)
for
y\in\mathsf{s}(W_{P}^{\cal S})
,
{\cal X}
-player’s strategy
x_{l}
satisfies

\sum_{y\in\mathsf{s}(W_{P}^{\cal S})}f(l,y)m(x_{l},y)\geq e^{-D_{\mathrm{max}}}.

(1)

Proof.

We get

\begin{split}\max_{x}\sum_{y\in\mathsf{s}(W_{P}^{\cal S})}f(l,y)\frac{W_{x}^{\cal S}(y)}{W_{P}^{\cal S}(y)}&\geq\mathbb{E}_{X\sim P}\bigg[\sum_{y\in\mathsf{s}(W_{P}^{\cal S})}f(l,y)\frac{W_{X}^{\cal S}(y)}{W_{P}^{\cal S}(y)}\bigg]\\
&=\sum_{y\in\mathsf{s}(W_{P}^{\cal S})}f(l,y)\frac{W_{P}^{\cal S}(y)}{W_{P}^{\cal S}(y)}\\
&=\sum_{y\in\mathsf{s}(W_{P}^{\cal S})}f(l,y)\\
&=1.\end{split}

Multiplying
e^{-D_{\mathrm{max}}}
to both sides, we get equation (
1
) for
y\in\mathsf{s}(W_{P}^{\cal S})
.
∎

For our choice of cost and
{\cal X}
-player’s strategy, we get the following performance of channel resolvability code
{\cal C}=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{L}\}
obtained from
{\cal X}
-player’s strategies.

Lemma 6
.

(Channel resolvability performance)

For an input distribution
P
, a given channel
W
, an arbitrary set
{\cal S}
, and arbitrary small

obtained by the MWU algorithm satisfies

\forall y\in{\cal Y}\qquad W_{P}^{\cal S}(y)-W_{\cal C}^{\cal S}(y)\leq 2\varepsilon W_{P}^{\cal S}(y),

(2)

as long as the size
L
satisfies that

L\geq\frac{e^{D_{\mathrm{max}}}\ln{|{\cal Y}|}}{\varepsilon^{2}}.

(3)

Proof.

First, we derive the equation

\forall y\in{\cal Y}\qquad W_{{\cal C}}^{\cal S}(y)\geq\bigg(1-\varepsilon-\frac{e^{D_{\mathrm{max}}}\ln{|{\cal Y}|}}{L\varepsilon}\bigg)W_{P}^{\cal S}(y),

(4)

with two cases,
y\notin\mathsf{s}(W_{P}^{\cal S})
and

Case

For all

Case

Applying Lemma

From Definition

\forall y\in{\cal Y}\qquad W_{\cal C}^{\cal S}(y)\geq(1-2\varepsilon)W_{P}^{\cal S}(y).

(5)

as long as the size

satisfies assumption (
3
).

Theorem 7
.

(Single-shot bound for channel resolvability)

For an input distribution
P
, a given channel
W
, an arbitrary set
{\cal S}
, and arbitrary small
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
, the codebook
{\cal C}
obtained by the MWU algorithm satisfies

d_{\mathrm{var}}(W_{P},W_{\cal C})\leq 2\varepsilon+\sum_{(x,y)\in{\cal S}^{c}}P(x)W_{x}(y),

as long as the size
L
satisfies that

L\geq\frac{e^{D_{\mathrm{max}}}\ln{|{\cal Y}|}}{\varepsilon^{2}}.

Proof.

We measure the total variation distance and get

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{var}}(W_{P},W_{\cal C})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{y\in{\cal Y}}|W_{P}(y)-W_{\cal C}(y)|

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in{\cal Y}:\atop W_{P}(y)\geq W_{\cal C}(y)}\Big(W_{P}(y)-W_{\cal C}(y)\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in{\cal Y}:\atop W_{P}(y)\geq W_{\cal C}(y)}\Big(W_{P}^{\cal S}(y)+W_{P}^{{\cal S}^{c}}(y)\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{y\in{\cal Y}:\atop W_{P}(y)\geq W_{\cal C}(y)}\Big(W_{\cal C}^{\cal S}(y)+W_{\cal C}^{{\cal S}^{c}}(y)\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in{\cal Y}:\atop W_{P}(y)\geq W_{\cal C}(y)}\Big(W_{P}^{\cal S}(y)-W_{\cal C}^{{\cal S}}(y)\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{y\in{\cal Y}:\atop W_{P}(y)\geq W_{\cal C}(y)}\Big(W_{P}^{{\cal S}^{c}}(y)-W_{\cal C}^{{{\cal S}}^{c}}(y)\Big).

(6)

Lemma
6
implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in{\cal Y}:\atop W_{P}(y)\geq W_{\cal C}(y)}\Big(W_{P}^{\cal S}(y)-W_{\cal C}^{{\cal S}}(y)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon\sum_{y\in{\cal Y}:\atop W_{P}(y)\geq W_{\cal C}(y)}W_{P}^{\cal S}(y)

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon.

(7)

By combining equation (
IV
) and (
IV
), we get

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{var}}(W_{P},W_{\cal C})

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon+\sum_{y\in{\cal Y}:\atop W_{P}(y)\geq W_{\cal C}(y)}\Big(W_{P}^{{\cal S}^{c}}(y)-W_{\cal C}^{{{\cal S}}^{c}}(y)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon+\sum_{y\in{\cal Y}:\atop W_{P}(y)\geq W_{\cal C}(y)}W_{P}^{{\cal S}^{c}}(y)

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon+\sum_{y\in{\cal Y}}W_{P}^{{\cal S}^{c}}(y).

Note that

\sum_{y\in{\cal Y}}W_{P}^{{\cal S}^{c}}(y)=\sum_{(x,y)\in{\cal S}^{c}}P(x)W_{x}(y).

Thus, we get Theorem
7
as long as the size
L
satisfies assumption (
3
).
∎

V
Asymptotic result for fixed type

In this section, we consider channel resolvability for block length
n
and use type method; see
[
15
, Section 2]
.
We fix a type
T
on
{\cal X}
. Let
{\cal X}_{T}^{n}
be a set of all sequences
x^{n}
in
{\cal X}^{n}
that have type
T
.
We consider an input distribution
P_{n}^{T}
on
{\cal X}_{T}^{n}
. We simulate the output distribution
W_{P_{n}^{T}}
for an input distribution
P_{n}^{T}
using the MWU algorithm. We define the set
{\cal A}
on the strong conditional typical set of the space
{\cal(X}_{T}^{n}{\cal,Y}^{n})
.

Definition 8
.

(Strong conditional typical set)

For block length
n
, a type
T
, a given i.i.d channel
W
, and small error threshold
\alpha
, we define the strong conditional typical set

\begin{split}{\cal T}_{W,\alpha}^{n}(x^{n}):=&\bigg\{y^{n}:\bigg|\frac{1}{n}N(a,b|x^{n},y^{n})-T(a)W_{a}(b)\bigg|<\alpha\\
&\qquad\forall a\in{\cal X},b\in{\cal Y}\bigg\},\end{split}

where
N(a,b|x^{n},y^{n})
outputs the number of occurrences of set
(a,b)
in
(x^{n},y^{n})
.
We also define set

{\cal A}:=\{(x^{n},y^{n}):x^{n}\in{\cal X}_{T}^{n},\;y^{n}\in{\cal T}_{W,\alpha}^{n}(x^{n})\}.

Note that if
y^{n}
is in the strong conditional typical set
{\cal T}_{W,\alpha}^{n}
,
y^{n}
satisfies the property of the weak conditional typical sequence such that

\displaystyle\bigg|-\frac{1}{n}\ln W_{x^{n}}(y^{n})-H(W|T)\bigg|<\alpha^{\prime}\quad\forall x^{n}\in{\cal X}_{T}^{n},

(8)

where
\alpha^{\prime}=c_{W}\alpha
for constant
c_{W}

[
15
, Problem 2.5]
. We also define the set
{\cal B}
on the strong typical set of the output space
{\cal Y}^{n}
.

Definition 9
.

(Strong typical set)
For block length
n
, a type
T
, a given i.i.d channel
W
, and small error threshold
\beta=|{\cal X}|\alpha
, we define the strong typical set

\begin{split}{\cal T}_{W_{T},\beta}^{n}:=\bigg\{y^{n}:\bigg|\frac{1}{n}N(b|y^{n})-W_{T}(b)\bigg|\leq\beta\quad\forall b\in{\cal Y}\bigg\}\end{split}

where
N(b|y^{n})
outputs the number of occurrences of
b
in
y^{n}
.
For an input distribution
P_{n}^{T}
, we also define set

{\cal B}:=\bigg\{y^{n}\in{\cal T}_{W_{T},\beta}^{n}:W_{P_{n}^{T}}(y^{n})\geq\frac{\tau}{|{\cal T}_{W_{T},\beta}^{n}|}\bigg\},

where
\tau
is a small positive value.

Note that the number of strong typical sequences satisfies

\bigg|\frac{1}{n}\ln|{\cal T}_{W_{T},\beta}^{n}|-H(W_{T})\bigg|\leq\beta^{\prime},

(9)

for arbitrarily small positive
\beta^{\prime}
and sufficiently large
n

[
15
, Lemma 2.13]
.
Using these sets
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
, we prove channel resolvability for a fixed type.

Theorem 10
.

(Channel resolvability for fixed type)

For a fixed type
T
, an input distribution
P_{n}^{T}
on
{\cal X}_{T}^{n}
, a given i.i.d. channel
W
, arbitrarily small
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
, small positive
\tau,\delta,\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}>0
, and sufficiently large
n
, the codebook
{\cal C}_{n}^{T}
obtained by the MWU algorithm satisfies

d_{\mathrm{var}}(W_{P_{n}^{T}},W_{{\cal C}_{n}^{T}})\leq 2\varepsilon+\tau+\delta,

as long as the size
L_{n}
satisfies that

L_{n}\geq\frac{\exp{\Big(n(I(T,W)+\alpha^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime})-\ln{\tau}\Big)}\;\ln{|{\cal Y}|^{n}}}{\varepsilon^{2}},

where
I(T,W)
is mutual information of type
T
and channel
W
.

Proof.

Fix sufficiently small
\alpha
and let
\beta=|{\cal X}|\alpha
so that equation (
8
) and (
9
) are satisfied for the given
\alpha^{\prime}
and
\beta^{\prime}
.
We define a set

{\cal S}:={\cal A}\cap({\cal X}_{T}^{n}\times{\cal B}).

To apply Theorem
7
for the set
{\cal S}
, we have to calculate
D_{\mathrm{max}}
, and
\sum_{(x^{n},y^{n})\in{\cal S}^{c}}P_{n}^{T}(x^{n})W_{x^{n}}(y^{n}).

From Definition
3
, we get

\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{max}}

\displaystyle=\max_{(x^{n},y^{n})\in{\cal S}:\atop y^{n}\in\mathsf{s}\big(W_{P_{n}^{T}}^{\cal S}\big)}\ln{\frac{W_{x^{n}}^{{\cal S}}(y^{n})}{W_{P_{n}^{T}}^{{\cal S}}(y^{n})}}

\displaystyle=\max_{(x^{n},y^{n})\in{\cal S}:\atop y^{n}\in\mathsf{s}\big(W_{P_{n}^{T}}^{\cal S}\big)}\Big(\ln{W_{x^{n}}^{{\cal S}}(y^{n})}-\ln{W_{P_{n}^{T}}^{{\cal S}}(y^{n})}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq-n(H(W|T)-\alpha^{\prime})+n(H(W_{T})+\beta^{\prime})-\ln{\tau}

\displaystyle=n(I(T,W)+\alpha^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime})-\ln{\tau},

(10)

where the inequality follows from equation (
8
) i.e.,

-\frac{1}{n}\ln{W_{x^{n}}^{\cal S}(y^{n})}\geq H(W|T)-\alpha^{\prime},

and the property of the set
{\cal B}
with equation (
9
) i.e.,

\begin{split}-\frac{1}{n}\ln{W_{P_{n}^{T}}^{\cal S}(y^{n})}&\leq-\frac{1}{n}\ln{\frac{\tau}{|{\cal T}_{W_{T},\beta}|}}\\
&\leq H(W_{T})+\beta^{\prime}-\frac{1}{n}\ln{\tau}.\end{split}

Next, for
{\cal S}^{c}:=({\cal X}_{T}^{n}\times{\cal Y}^{n})\backslash{\cal S}
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{(x^{n},y^{n})\in{\cal S}^{c}}P_{n}^{T}(x^{n})W_{x^{n}}(y^{n})

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sum_{(x^{n},y^{n})\in({\cal X}_{T}^{n}\times{\cal Y}^{n})\backslash{\cal A}}P_{n}^{T}(x^{n})W_{x^{n}}(y^{n})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\sum_{(x^{n},y^{n})\in{\cal A}\cap({\cal X}_{T}^{n}\times({\cal Y}^{n}\backslash{\cal B}))}P_{n}^{T}(x^{n})W_{x^{n}}(y^{n}),

(11)

where the inequality follows from
{\cal S}^{c}=\{({\cal X}_{T}^{n}\times{\cal Y}^{n})\backslash{\cal A}\}\cup\{{\cal A}\cap({\cal X}_{T}^{n}\times({\cal Y}^{n}\backslash{\cal B}))\}

with the union bound. Note that the probability of non-typical set is small
[
15
, Lemma 2.12]
, i.e.,
for sufficient large
n
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{(x^{n},y^{n})\in({\cal X}_{T}^{n}\times{\cal Y}^{n})\backslash{\cal A}}P_{n}^{T}(x^{n})W_{x^{n}}(y^{n})\leq\delta.

(12)

Note also that the set
{\cal A}\cap\big({\cal X}_{T}^{n}\times({\cal Y}^{n}\backslash{{\cal T}_{W_{T},\beta}^{n}})\big)
is empty set, because for
\beta=|{\cal X}|\alpha
, each sequence
y^{n}
in
{\cal A}
is included in
{\cal T}_{W_{T},\beta}^{n}

[
15
, Lemma 2.10]
. Thus, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{(x^{n},y^{n})\in{\cal A}\cap({\cal X}_{T}^{n}\times({\cal Y}^{n}\backslash{\cal B}))}P_{n}^{T}(x^{n})W_{x^{n}}(y^{n})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\sum_{y^{n}\in{{\cal T}_{W_{T},\beta}^{n}}:\atop W_{P_{n}^{T}}(y^{n})<\frac{\tau}{|{\cal T}_{W_{T},\beta}^{n}|}}\sum_{x^{n}\in{\cal X}_{T}^{n}}P_{n}^{T}(x^{n})W_{x^{n}}(y^{n})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\sum_{y^{n}\in{{\cal T}_{W_{T},\beta}^{n}}:\atop W_{P_{n}^{T}}(y^{n})<\frac{\tau}{|{\cal T}_{W_{T},\beta}^{n}|}}W_{P_{n}^{T}}(y^{n})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\tau.

(13)

Substituting equation (

) and (

) to equation (

), we get

\sum_{(x^{n},y^{n})\in{\cal S}^{c}}P_{n}^{T}(x^{n})W_{x^{n}}(y^{n})\leq\tau+\delta.

Applying Theorem

.

VI
Asymptotic result for general input distribution

In this section, we consider channel resolvability for a general input distribution. Let
{\cal T}
be the set of all types. For a given distribution
P_{n}
on
{\cal X}^{n}
, we consider the type distribution

P_{{\cal T}}(T):=P_{n}({\cal X}_{T}^{n})

(14)

The type distribution distributes a general input distribution by each type
T
as

P_{n}(x^{n})=\sum_{T\in{\cal T}}{P_{\cal T}(T)}{P_{n}^{T}(x^{n})},

for each symbol
x^{n}\in{\cal X}^{n}
where

P_{n}^{T}(x^{n}):=\frac{P_{n}(x^{n})}{P_{n}({\cal X}_{T}^{n})}\mathbf{1}[x^{n}\in{\cal X}_{T}^{n}]
.
We first simulate the type distribution
P_{\cal T}
. For readers’ convenience, we provide a proof of the proposition in Appendix
-B
; This simulation is known as source resolvability, which is already proved by deterministic code construction
[
18
,
35
,
26
]
. However, we prove source resolvability using the MWU algorithm so that our channel resolvability code is constructed solely by the MWU algorithm.

Proposition 11
.

(Source resolvability with type)
For type distribution
P_{\cal T}
, arbitrary small
\varepsilon^{\prime}\in(0,\frac{1}{2}],
small positive
\tau^{\prime}>0
, and block length
n
, the codebook
{\cal C^{\prime}}=\{T_{1},T_{2},...,T_{L_{n}^{\prime}}\}\subset{\cal T}
obtained by the MWU algorithm makes a distribution

R_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}(T)=\frac{1}{L_{n}^{\prime}}\sum_{l=1}^{L_{n}^{\prime}}\mathbf{1}[T_{l}=T]
. The distribution
R_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}(T)

satisfies that

d_{\mathrm{var}}(P_{\cal T},R_{{\cal C}^{\prime}})\leq 2\varepsilon^{\prime}+\tau^{\prime},

as long as the size
L_{n}^{\prime}
satisfies

L_{n}^{\prime}\geq\frac{(n+1)^{|{\cal X}|}|{\cal X}|\ln{(n+1)}}{\tau\varepsilon^{\prime 2}}.

(15)

Select one of
L_{n}^{\prime}
types uniformly at random. For the chosen type
T
, select a codeword uniformly at random from
L_{n}
codewords.
In Proposition
11
, sufficiently large
L_{n}^{\prime}
ensures the outcome distribution approximates
P_{\cal T}
. Similarly, in Theorem
10
, sufficiently large
L_{n}
ensures the output distribution approximates the one induced by
P_{n}^{T}(x^{n})
. These insights lead to channel resolvability for a general input.

Theorem 12
.

(Channel resolvability for general input distribution)

For a general input distribution
P_{n}
, a given i.i.d. channel
W,
and small constants
\eta,\kappa
, the codebook
{\cal C}_{n}
obtained by the MWU algorithm satisfies that

d_{\mathrm{var}}(W_{P_{n}},W_{{\cal C}_{n}})\leq\eta

for sufficiently large
n
, as long as the size
L_{n}^{\prime\prime}
satisfies that

L_{n}^{\prime\prime}\geq\exp(n(C(W)+\kappa)).

Proof.

From Proposition
11
, we get type distribution
R_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}
and from Theorem
10
, we get distribution
W_{{\cal C}_{n}^{T}}
for a fixed type
T
. Thus, we get

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{var}}(W_{P_{n}},W_{{\cal C}_{n}})

\displaystyle\quad=d_{\mathrm{var}}\bigg(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}}P_{\cal T}({T})W_{P_{n}^{T}},\sum_{T\in{\cal T}}R_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}({T})W_{{\cal C}_{n}^{T}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad\leq d_{\mathrm{var}}\bigg(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}}P_{\cal T}({T})W_{P_{n}^{T}},\sum_{T\in{\cal T}}P_{\cal T}({T})W_{{\cal C}_{n}^{T}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad+d_{\mathrm{var}}\bigg(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}}P_{\cal T}({T})W_{{\cal C}_{n}^{T}},\sum_{T\in{\cal T}}R_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}({T})W_{{\cal C}_{n}^{T}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sum_{T\in{\cal T}}P_{\cal T}({T})d_{\mathrm{var}}\big(W_{P_{n}^{T}},W_{{\cal C}_{n}^{T}}\big)+d_{\mathrm{var}}(P_{\cal T},R_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}),

(16)

where the first inequality follows from triangular inequality and the last inequality follows from data processing inequality. When we consider the worst case of type
T
in Theorem
10
, we get
d_{\mathrm{var}}(W_{P_{n}^{T}},W_{{\cal C}_{n}^{T}})\leq 2\varepsilon+\tau+\delta
as long as the size
L_{n}
satisfies that for channel capacity
C(W)=\max_{T}I(T,W)
,

\begin{split}L_{n}&\geq\frac{\exp{\Big(n(C(W)+\alpha^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime})-\ln{\tau}\Big)}\;\ln{|{\cal Y}|^{n}}}{\varepsilon^{2}}.\end{split}

Thus, we get

\begin{split}d_{\mathrm{var}}(W_{P_{n}},W_{{\cal C}_{n}})\leq 2\varepsilon+\tau+\delta+2\varepsilon^{\prime}+\tau^{\prime},\end{split}

as long as the size
L_{n}^{\prime\prime}
satisfies that

\begin{split}L_{n}^{\prime\prime}&\geq L_{n}\frac{(n+1)^{|{\cal X}|}|{\cal X}|\ln{(n+1)}}{\tau^{\prime}\varepsilon^{\prime 2}}.\end{split}

By setting
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime},\tau,\tau^{\prime},\delta,\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}
to small values,
for sufficiently large
n
,

\eta
and
\kappa
satisfies Theorem
12
.
∎

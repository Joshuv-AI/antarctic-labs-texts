Pandey-Upadhyay’s wavelet transform and microlocal Sobolev singularities of functions
Akira Lee, Shinya Moritoh

Abstract
The aim of the paper is to define the microlocal Sobolev singularities of functions using Pandey-Upadhyay’s wavelet transform and provide a comparison with Hörmander’s microlocal singularities.



keywords
wavelet transform, microlocal singularity

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification:

Primary 42B15; Secondary 42C40

1
Introduction

The aim of the paper is to define the microlocal Sobolev
singularities of functions using Pandey-Upadhyay’s
wavelet transform and provide a comparison
with Hörmander’s
microlocal singularities.

Let
f
be a square integrable function, i.e.
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
Let the wavelet function
\psi
be rapidly decreasing, i.e.
\psi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, and its Fourier transform

\hat{\psi}
be compactly supported in the cube
[1/2,2]^{n}
.
Then the wavelet transform of
f
is defined as follows (see
Pandey-Upadhyay
PU
):

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle W_{\psi}f(x,\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}f(t)\,|\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n}|\overline{\psi}(\xi_{1}(t_{1}-x_{1}),\ldots,\xi_{n}(t_{n}-x_{n}))dt,\end{array}

where
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\,\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots,\xi_{n})\in({\mathbb{R}}-\{0\})^{n}
.
The inversion formula for Pandey-Upadhyay’s
wavelet transform reads as follows:

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle f(t)=C_{\psi}^{-1}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times({\mathbb{R}}-\{0\})^{n}}\!\!\!W_{\psi}f(x,\xi)|\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n}|\times\\
\displaystyle\hskip 99.58464pt\times\psi(\xi_{1}(t_{1}-x_{1}),\ldots,\xi_{n}(t_{n}-x_{n}))dxd\xi/|\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n}|,\end{array}

(1)

where
C_{\psi}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\hat{\psi}(\omega)|^{2}d\omega/|\omega_{1}\cdots\omega_{n}|

is a finite, nonzero constant.

It is easily seen that the Fourier transform of

W_{\psi}f(x,\xi)
with respect to
x
can be calculated as follows:

\left(W_{\psi}f(\cdot,\xi)\right)^{\wedge}(\tau):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}W_{\psi}f(x,\xi)e^{-ix\cdot\tau}dx=\hat{f}(\tau)\overline{\hat{\psi}}(\xi_{1}^{-1}\tau_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}^{-1}\tau_{n}).

Since
\hat{\psi}
is compactly supported in the cube
[1/2,2]^{n}
,
the function

\left(W_{\psi}f(\cdot,\xi)\right)^{\wedge}(\tau)
is compactly
supported in rectangular parallelepiped

[(1/2)\xi_{1},2\xi_{1}]\times\cdots\times[(1/2)\xi_{n},2\xi_{n}]
.

We note that, in the definitions of wavelet transforms,
Pandey-Upadhyay
PU
uses 1-dimensional dilations
in all coordinate directions,
while Moritoh
M
uses
n
-dimensional rotation
and 1-dimensional dilation in the radial direction.

In the following section 2, as an anlogue of the
singularities considered in
Moritoh
M
, we define the microlocal Sobolev
singularities of functions using Pandey-Upadhyay’s
wavelet transform, and state our main theorem
providing a comparison with Hörmander’s
microlocal singularities. In the proof, we
will focus on the corresponding key parts of
M
.

2
Definitions, main theorem, and the proof

Let us first recall the definition of Hörmander’s microlocal
singularities.

Definition 1

(Hörmander
H
)
.

Let
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,

(x_{0},\xi^{0})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times({\mathbb{R}}^{n}-\{0\})
,
and
s\geq 0
.
Then,
f
is said to belong to the
Sobolev space

H^{s}
microlocally at
(x_{0},\xi^{0})
in the sense of
Hörmander if
there exist a cutoff function

\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
identically
equal to 1 in a neighborhood of
x_{0}
and a conical
neiborhood
\Gamma(\xi^{0})
of
\xi^{0}
such that

\int_{\Gamma(\xi^{0})}|(\phi f)^{\wedge}(\xi)|^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s}d\xi<\infty.

(2)

We now define the microlocal singularities of functions
using Pandey-Upadhyay’s wavelet transform.

Definition 2
.

Let
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,

(x_{0},\xi^{0})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times({\mathbb{R}}-\{0\})^{n}
,
and
s\geq 0
. Then,
f
is said to belong to the
Sobolev space

H^{s}
microlocally at
(x_{0},\xi^{0})
in the
sense of PU if
there exist a neiborhood
U(x_{0})
of
x_{0}
and a conical
neiborhood
\Gamma(\xi^{0})
of
\xi^{0}
such that

\iint_{U(x_{0})\times\Gamma(\xi^{0})}|W_{\psi}f(x,\xi)|^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s}dxd\xi/|\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n}|<\infty.

(3)

In order to state the main theorem, let us assume,
for simplicity, that
n=2
and that
both coordinates of
\xi^{0}=(\xi^{0}_{1},\xi^{0}_{2})
are positive, i.e.

\xi^{0}\in({\mathbb{R}}_{>0})^{2}
, and
define the conical neighborhood

\Gamma_{\psi}(\xi^{0})
of
\xi^{0}
, with some margin
depending on the wavelet
\psi
, as follows.

\Gamma_{\psi}(\xi^{0})=\{\tau=(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\in({\mathbb{R}}_{>0})^{2}|\,(1/5)(\xi^{0}_{1}/\xi^{0}_{2})<\tau_{1}/\tau_{2}<5(\xi^{0}_{1}/\xi^{0}_{2})\}.

(4)

Theorem 1
.

Let
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
,
(x_{0},\xi^{0})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times({\mathbb{R}}_{>0})^{2}
, and
s\geq 0
.

(a)

If
(
2
)
holds for a cutoff function

\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
identically
equal to 1 in a neighborhood of
x_{0}
and the conical neighborhood

\Gamma_{\psi}(\xi^{0})
of
\xi^{0}
defined by
(
4
)
,
then
f
belongs to the
Sobolev space

H^{s}
microlocally at
(x_{0},\xi^{0})
in the sense of PU.

(b)

Conversely, if
(
3
)
holds for a neighborhood

U(x_{0})
of
x_{0}
and the conical neighborhood

\Gamma_{\psi}(\xi^{0})

defined by
(
4
)
, then
f
belongs to the Sobolev space

H^{s}
microlocally at
(x_{0},\xi^{0})
in the sense of Hörmander.

Remark 1
.

In Moritoh
M
, a comparison theorem is stated
using wave front sets and considers enlarging the
wave front sets only in frequency space.
This corresponds to how
\Gamma_{\psi}(\xi^{0})
is defined by
(
4
). We also note that
\Gamma_{\psi}(\xi^{0})
needs to
be a conical neighborhood that is slightly larger than
the smallest conical one containing

[(1/2)\xi_{1}^{0},2\xi_{1}^{0}]\times[(1/2)\xi_{2}^{0},2\xi_{2}^{0}]
.

Proof.

We use the method employed in the proof of
theorem 1 in
M
.
We also note that proposition 2 (local property)
in
M
also holds true for Pandey-Upadhyay’s
wavelet transform:
If
x_{0}
does not
belong to
\text{supp}\,f
, then there exists a neighbourhood

U(x_{0})
of
x_{0}
such that
W_{\psi}f(x,\xi)
is rapidly
decreasing in
\xi
uniformly in
x\in U(x_{0})
.

First, let us prove (a). Our assumption is that, for a cutoff function

\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
identically
equal to 1 in a neighborhood of
x_{0}
, the following holds true.

\int_{\Gamma_{\psi}(\xi^{0})}|(\phi f)^{\wedge}(\xi)|^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s}d\xi<\infty.

(5)

Let a neighborhood
U(x_{0})
of
x_{0}
be contained in the set

\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}|\phi(x)=1\}
and let
a conical neighborhood
\Gamma(\xi^{0})
of
\xi^{0}
be
defined by

\{\tau=(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\in({\mathbb{R}}_{>0})^{2}|\,(4/5)(\xi^{0}_{1}/\xi^{0}_{2})<\tau_{1}/\tau_{2}<(5/4)(\xi^{0}_{1}/\xi^{0}_{2})\}
.
Using the same argument as for the first half of
the proof of theorem 1 in
M
, it suffices to
show only the following.

\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times\Gamma(\xi^{0})}|W_{\psi}(\phi f)(x,\xi)|^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s}dxd\xi/|\xi_{1}\xi_{2}|<\infty.

This can be rewritten as

\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times\Gamma(\xi^{0})}|{(\phi f)}^{\wedge}(\tau)\hat{\psi}(\xi_{1}^{-1}\tau_{1},\xi_{2}^{-1}\tau_{2})|^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s}d\tau d\xi/|\xi_{1}\xi_{2}|<\infty.

(6)

Changing the variable
\xi
to the variable
\omega
, where

\omega_{1}=\xi_{1}^{-1}\tau_{1}
and
\omega_{2}=\xi_{2}^{-1}\tau_{2}
,
and taking the support conditon for
\hat{\psi}(\omega)
into
account, we can see that
d\xi/|\xi_{1}\xi_{2}|=d\omega/|\omega_{1}\omega_{2}|
and
1+|\xi|^{2}\leq 4(1+|\tau|^{2})
.
Moreover, when
\xi\in\Gamma(\xi^{0})
, from the equality

\tau_{1}/\tau_{2}=(\omega_{1}/\omega_{2})(\xi_{1}/\xi_{2})
we see that

\tau_{1}/\tau_{2}
is between (1/4)(4/5) and 4(5/4) times the
ratio
\xi^{0}_{1}/\xi^{0}_{2}
, i.e.

\tau\in\Gamma_{\psi}(\xi^{0})
.
Therefore, the left side of (
6
) is bounded from above by

\int_{\Gamma_{\psi}(\xi^{0})}|{(\phi f)}^{\wedge}(\tau)|^{2}\,4^{s}(1+|\tau|^{2})^{s}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\hat{\psi}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})|^{2}d\omega/|\omega_{1}\omega_{2}|\right)d\tau,

which is finite from our assumption (
5
).

Next, let us prove (b). Our assumption is that, for
a neighborhood
U(x_{0})
of
x_{0}
, the following holds true.

\iint_{U(x_{0})\times\Gamma_{\psi}(\xi^{0})}|W_{\psi}f(x,\xi)|^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s}dxd\xi/|\xi_{1}\xi_{2}|<\infty.

(7)

Let
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})

be compactly supported in
U(x_{0})
and be identically equal to 1
in some neighborhood of
x_{0}
. Then, taking the inversion formula
(
1
) into account, and
using the same argument as for the second half of
the proof of theorem 1 in
M
, it suffices to
show only the following: The function defined as

\begin{array}[]{l}f_{1}(t)\\
\displaystyle=C_{\psi}^{-1}\!\!\!\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times\Gamma_{\psi}(\xi^{0})}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\phi(x)W_{\psi}f(x,\xi)|\xi_{1}\xi_{2}|\psi(\xi_{1}(t_{1}-x_{1}),\xi_{2}(t_{2}-x_{2}))dxd\xi/|\xi_{1}\xi_{2}|\end{array}

(8)

belongs to the Sobolev space
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
.

By putting

g(x,\xi)=\phi(x)W_{\psi}f(x,\xi)(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s/2}
,
our assumption (
7
) can be rewritten as

\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times\Gamma_{\psi}(\xi^{0})}|g(x,\xi)|^{2}dxd\xi/|\xi_{1}\xi_{2}|<\infty.

(9)

The Fourier transform of (
8
) is equal to

\widehat{f_{1}}(\tau)=C_{\psi}^{-1}\int_{\Gamma_{\psi}(\xi^{0})}\left(g(\cdot,\xi)\right)^{\wedge}(\tau)\hat{\psi}(\xi_{1}^{-1}\tau_{1},\xi_{2}^{-1}\tau_{2})(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-s/2}d\xi/|\xi_{1}\xi_{2}|.

Therefore, by using the Schwarz inequality, the same
argument immediately following (
6
),
the Fubini theorem, the Plancherel theorem and
our assumption (
9
), we obtain

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\widehat{f_{1}}(\tau)|^{2}(1+|\tau|^{2})^{s}d\tau\\
\displaystyle\leq C_{\psi}^{-2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\left(\int_{\Gamma_{\psi}(\xi^{0})}|\left(g(\cdot,\xi)\right)^{\wedge}(\tau)|^{2}d\xi/|\xi_{1}\xi_{2}|\right)\times\\
\displaystyle\hskip 99.58464pt\times\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\hat{\psi}(\xi_{1}^{-1}\tau_{1},\xi_{2}^{-1}\tau_{2})|^{2}\left(\frac{1+|\tau|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\right)^{s}d\xi/|\xi_{1}\xi_{2}|\right)d\tau\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\leq C_{\psi}^{-2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\left(\int_{\Gamma_{\psi}(\xi^{0})}|\left(g(\cdot,\xi)\right)^{\wedge}(\tau)|^{2}d\xi/|\xi_{1}\xi_{2}|\right)\times\\
\displaystyle\hskip 99.58464pt\times 4^{s}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\hat{\psi}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})|^{2}d\omega/|\omega_{1}\omega_{2}|\right)d\tau\\
\displaystyle=4^{s}C_{\psi}^{-1}\int_{\Gamma_{\psi}(\xi^{0})}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|g(x,\xi)|^{2}dx\right)d\xi/|\xi_{1}\xi_{2}|<\infty.\end{array}

∎

Remark 2
.

From the above, it is easy to see that
function spaces with dominating mixed smoothness are
worth considering by using Pandey-Upadhyay’s
wavelet transform.

Remark 3
.

We note the paper
HPS
in relation to our study.
In Han-Paul-Shukla
HPS
,
continuous shearlet transforms are used
to characterize the microlocal Sobolev wave front sets,
the 2-microlocal spaces, and local Hölder spaces. See also
the original reference Guo-Kutyniok-Labate
GKL
.

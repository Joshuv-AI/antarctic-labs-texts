Quasi-conformal VS quasi-isometric equivalence in spaces with controlled growth
Katrin Fässler, Enrico Le Donne, Sebastiano Nicolussi Golo, Alessandro Ottazzi, Pierre Pansu

Abstract
. We study conditions under which quasi-conformal homeomorphisms are quasi-isometries. We show that if two nilpotent geodesic Lie groups are quasi-conformally homeomorphic, then they are quasi-isometrically equivalent. We also give more general results beyond the nilpotent case. In particular, we show that quasi-conformal homeomorphisms between geodesic Lie groups are quasi-isometries whenever the spaces have strict parabolic or hyperbolic conformal type. As a consequence, quasi-conformal homeomorphisms between geodesic Lie groups with infinite fundamental group are quasi-isometries. The statements for Lie groups are deduced from a more general study on metric measure spaces with uniformly locally bounded geometry.

Introduction

1.1.
Overview

In Geometry and Analysis, quasi-conformal homeomorphisms and quasi-isometries play a widespread role; see for instance
[

Quasi-conformality (QC) is an infinitesimal property, while quasi-isometries (QI) preserve large-scale structures.
A typical interplay between these two types of maps is found in a crucial step of Mostow Rigidity Theorem: there, a quasi-isometry between hyperbolic spaces induces a quasi-conformal map between the visual boundaries.

It turns out there is a deeper connection:
In
[
6
, Theorem 9.8]
, Bonk, Heinonen, and Koskela proved
that, under specific geometric conditions
1

1

The geometric conditions on the space are: having bounded geometry, being roughly starlike, and having a Gromov boundary that is a nondegenerate continuum.
, quasi-conformal homeomorphisms between Gromov hyperbolic spaces are themselves quasi-isometries.
As an application, they obtained a boundary extension result for quasi-conformal maps between Gromov hyperbolic
n
-dimensional Hadamard manifolds (
n\geq 2
) with Ricci bounded geometry; see
[
6
, Theorem 1.15]
.
A special case of
[
6
, Theorem 9.8]
(for quasi-conformal diffeomorphisms between Hadamard manifolds of pinched negative sectional curvature) was obtained earlier in
[
42
]
.

The Bonk--Heinonen--Koskela result suggests a ‘‘QC-implies-QI Principle’’ for some types of geodesic spaces.
More recently, Pansu studied a notion of large-scale conformal maps and showed that such maps are quasi-isometries in the setting of manifolds with bounded geometry; see
[
40
, Theorem 2]
.

We investigate similar results for Lie groups endowed with left-invariant (Riemannian or non-Riemannian) geodesic distances.
We show the validity of the “QC-implies-QI Principle” in a large class of geodesic Lie groups; see Theorem
B
.
On each geodesic Lie group, the top-dimensional Hausdorff measure is a Haar measure, which is locally Ahlfors regular and supports a local Poincaré inequality.
Hence, we consider each geodesic Lie group as a metric measure space with
uniformly locally bounded geometry
, in the sense of PI spaces; see, for instance,
[
24
,
26
,
27
,
28
]
, and Definition
2.1
and Proposition
8.1
below.

The common core of our arguments is to obtain bounds on the conformal capacity of connected sets, compact or unbounded, which lead to bornologous comparisons between the original distance and some
conformal gauges
, that is, distance-like functions that are bi-Lipschitz preserved by quasi-conformal maps.
Our main inspiration is the profound work by J. Ferrand on Riemannian manifolds; see for instance
[
18
,
34
]
, and
[
47
]
for a survey.
Once we have such comparisons, we obtain that quasi-conformal maps are bi-bornologous.
The conclusion that quasi-conformal maps are quasi-isometries follows from the observation that bi-bornologous maps between geodesic spaces are quasi-isometries.

The strategy we have just outlined clearly cannot work in every geodesic Lie group.
For example, the Euclidean plane admits plenty of quasi-conformal homeomorphisms that are not quasi-isometries.
This example suggests that we need additional conditions on the space, such as being Gromov hyperbolic, as mentioned in the result by Bonk, Heinonen, and Koskela above.
In the non-Riemannian case, curvature bounds are too restrictive.
However, other large-scale conditions allow us to run the appropriate capacity estimates.
These extra conditions are of two kinds, depending on whether the space has
parabolic
or
hyperbolic conformal type
, as in Definition
4.1
.
In the parabolic case, we use bounds on the volume growth at infinity and the existence of
quasi-straight sequences
of points, as in Definition
5.10
, to obtain bounds on the conformal capacity of condensers made by pairs of unbounded connected sets.
In the hyperbolic case, we use the isoperimetric inequality at large scale to obtain bounds on the conformal capacity of so-called Grötzsch condensers, that is, compact connected sets paired with
\infty
.

In geodesic Lie groups, these different conditions are governed by the difference between the exponent
N
of volume growth and the Hausdorff dimension
Q
; see, for instance,
[
47
]
, or
[
53
,
55
,
56
]
.
Our methods apply whenever
N\neq Q
, while they leave open the situation when
N=Q
, as expected.
The Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an example where
N=Q=n
.

1.2.
Main results

We next present our main results in detail.
Our focus will be on geodesic Lie groups.
However, we try to pinpoint minimal assumptions for each argument: for this reason, we prove most of our results in the setting of metric measure spaces.
Besides geodesic Lie groups, other spaces to which our Theorems
F
and
G
apply are locally compact isometrically homogeneous geodesic spaces, such as metric quotients of Lie groups, or Lie groups endowed with quasi-geodesic left-invariant distances, only to mention a few examples.

We call
geodesic Lie group

(G,d,\mu)
every Lie group
G
endowed with a geodesic left-invariant distance
d
, inducing the manifold topology, and a left Haar measure
\mu
.
From the work of Berestowski
[
4
]
, every geodesic left-invariant distance on a Lie group is a Carnot-Carathéodory distance, also referred to as a sub-Finsler metric.
Examples of geodesic Lie groups are Riemannian and sub-Riemannian connected Lie groups.

For nilpotent Lie groups, we show that quasi-conformal equivalence implies quasi-isometric equivalence, with no extra conditions.

Theorem A

(see Theorem
8.14
)
.

Let
G,H
be nilpotent geodesic Lie groups.
If there is a metrically quasi-conformal map
G\to H
, then there is a quasi-isometry
G\to H
.

To prove Theorem
A
, we distinguish two cases: spaces of parabolic and of hyperbolic conformal type.
A metric measure space has
parabolic conformal type
if all compact sets have zero
Q
-capacity,
Q
being the Hausdorff dimension of the space; otherwise the space has
hyperbolic conformal type
.
The conformal type depends on the metric measure structure and, for spaces with bounded geometry, it is preserved under quasi-conformal maps.

In the nilpotent case, the starting point is to show that a simply connected nilpotent geodesic Lie group has parabolic conformal type if and only if it is a Carnot group; see Proposition
8.13
.
By the Differentiation Theorem
[
35
]
, at every point of intrinsic differentiability, the differential of a quasi-conformal map
G\to H
between Carnot groups is a bi-Lipschitz map
G\to H
, and thus, in particular, we recover a quasi-isometry
G\to H
between the same spaces.

In the hyperbolic case, we show that every quasi-conformal map
G\to H
is a quasi-isometry, while the non-simply connected case is studied by passing to the universal covering.
In fact, the hyperbolic case is treated in a more general setting, beyond nilpotent Lie groups.

The distinction between hyperbolic conformal type and parabolic conformal type in geodesic Lie groups needs to be made finer.
On a geodesic metric measure space
(X,d,\mu)
, one may define the
p
-capacity
\operatorname{Cap}_{p}(E)
of each subset
E
of
X
, for every
p\in[1,+\infty)
; see Definition
2.4
.
If
(X,d,\mu)
has locally
Q
-bounded geometry, then the
Q
-capacity is preserved by quasi-conformal maps: for this reason, the distinction between hyperbolic and parabolic conformal type is done in terms of the
Q
-capacity.
For
p\neq Q
, the
p
-capacity is not necessarily preserved by quasi-conformal maps.
If we define the
parabolic dimension
of
X
by

(1)

\dim_{\rm par}(X):=\inf\{p\in[1,+\infty):\operatorname{Cap}_{p}(E)=0,\ \forall E\Subset X\},

then this number may not be a quasi-conformal invariant.
Surprisingly, it turns out that, for geodesic Lie groups, the value
\dim_{\rm par}(X)
is a quasi-conformal invariant; see Corollary
8.7
.

We prove that a geodesic Lie group with Hausdorff dimension
Q
has parabolic conformal type if and only if
\dim_{\rm par}(G)\leq Q
, and has hyperbolic conformal type if and only if
Q<\dim_{\rm par}(G)
; see Corollary
8.3
.
We say that
G
has
strictly parabolic conformal type
if
\dim_{\rm par}(G)<Q
, and we call the threshold case when
\dim_{\rm par}(G)=Q
as
liminal parabolic conformal type
.
We are in the latter case exactly when the geodesic Lie group is globally Ahlfors regular.

Theorem B

(see Theorem
8.6
)
.

Quasi-conformal maps between non-compact geodesic Lie groups preserve both the Hausdorff and the parabolic dimensions of the groups; in particular, they maintain the distinction between hyperbolic, parabolic, and strictly parabolic conformal types.

Moreover, quasi-conformal maps between geodesic Lie groups of hyperbolic or strictly parabolic conformal type are quasi-isometries.

Theorem
B
implies in particular that the sub-Riemannian Heisenberg group and the sub-Riemannian rototranslation group are not quasi-conformally equivalent, as was already shown in
[
16
]
.

In the second part of Theorem
B
, we have not included the liminal parabolic case, because it would not be true.
An example of a geodesic Lie group with liminal parabolic conformal type is the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
: we know that in this case, there are plenty of quasi-conformal maps that are not quasi-isometries.
One can find more examples among Carnot groups: in particular, the sub-Riemannian Heisenberg group, which also admits quasi-conformal transformations that are not quasi-isometries.

An example of a geodesic Lie group with strictly parabolic conformal type is the direct product
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
, since the Hausdorff dimension is
n+1
, while its parabolic dimension is
n
, as shown in Theorem
D
below.
Theorem
B
implies that every quasi-conformal map from
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
onto itself is a quasi-isometry.
This fact was not known before, to the authors’ knowledge.
We generalize this example as follows:

Theorem C

(see Theorem
8.10
)
.

Let
f:G_{1}\to G_{2}
be a quasi-conformal map between geodesic Lie groups.
If the fundamental group of
G_{1}
is infinite, then
f
is a quasi-isometry.

For maps between nilpotent geodesic Lie groups, Theorem
B
with a few algebraic and metric observations implies Theorem
A
.
Indeed, for simply connected nilpotent geodesic Lie groups, the Hausdorff dimension
Q
and the degree
N
of volume growth at large scale are computable algebraically from the structure of the Lie algebra.
With this fact, we show in Proposition
8.13
that
Q\leq N
on simply connected nilpotent geodesic Lie groups, with equality holding only for Carnot groups.
See Section
8.5
for more details.

However, beyond nilpotent Lie groups, it is not clear that QC equivalence implies QI equivalence for geodesic Lie groups of liminal parabolic conformal type.
An example is the
\ell^{2}
product
(G,d)=(\mathbb{H}^{1},d_{R})\times(\mathscr{R},d_{sR})
of the Riemannian Heisenberg group
(\mathbb{H}^{1},d_{R})
and the sub-Riemannian rototranslation group
(\mathscr{R},d_{sR})
:
G
has parabolic and Hausdorff dimension 7, so, it is a liminal parabolic geodesic Lie group but it is not nilpotent.
This scenario will be studied in a forthcoming work.

Theorem
B
is proven in several steps, which we describe in the following sections.

1.3.
Conformal type, volume growth, and isoperimetric inequalities

Quasi-conformal maps between spaces with
Q
-bounded geometry preserve both topological properties and
Q
-capacities.
It is reasonable, then, to define distance-like functions on metric measure spaces that combine these two types of information.
We will use in particular two of them.
In the hyperbolic case, we use what we call the
hyperbolic Ferrand distance

\mathbbmss{h}
:
for every two points
x,y
, the quantity
\mathbbmss{h}(x,y)
is the infimum of
Q
-capacity of closed and connected sets
E
that contain
x
and
y
; see Definition
7.1
.
These sets
E
are also known as
Grötzsche capacitors
.
In the parabolic case, we use instead what we call the
parabolic Ferrand distance

\mathbbmss{p}
:
for every two points
x,y
, the quantity
\mathbbmss{p}(x,y)
is the
\frac{1}{Q}
-power of the inverse of the infimum of
Q
-capacity of capacitors
(E,F)
made of closed connected unbounded sets with
x\in E
and
y\in F
; see Definition
78
.
These capacitors
(E,F)
are also known as
Teichmüller
2

capacitors
.

To get estimates of Ferrand distances, we use two geometric properties: the isoperimetric profile at large scale and the volume growth at large scale.

We say that a metric measure space
(X,d,\mu)
supports the
isoperimetric inequality at large scale of order
\frac{N-1}{N}
, for some
N\geq 1
, if there exists
C\in\mathbb{R}
such that,
for every
E\subset X
measurable,

(2)

\mu(E)\geq 1\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad\mu(E)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\leq C\operatorname{Per}(E),

where
\operatorname{Per}(E)
denotes the perimeter, i.e., the total variation of the characteristic function of
E
.
The supremum of all such
N
is the
isoperimetric dimension

\dim_{\rm isp}(X)
of
X
.

We say that a metric measure space
(X,d,\mu)
has
degree of large-scale growth at most
N
, for some
N\geq 1
if there exists
C\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(3)

\mu(B(x,R))\leq CR^{N},\qquad\forall x\in X,\ \forall R>1.

The
growth dimension

\dim_{\rm grt}(X)
of
X
is the infimum of all such
N
.
If
\dim_{\rm grt}(X)<\infty
, then
X
has
polynomial (volume) growth
.
Under some mild additional hypothesis on the spaces, we have that
\dim_{\rm grt}(X)
is a quasi-isometric invariant; see Proposition
5.2
.

Using the coarea inequality, one easily checks that
\dim_{\rm isp}(X)\leq\dim_{\rm grt}(X)
.
We will show in Corollary
6.13
and Proposition
4.4
that,

if
X
is a metric measure space with compact sets of arbitrarily large measure, then

(4)

\dim_{\rm isp}(X)\leq\dim_{\rm par}(X)\leq\dim_{\rm grt}(X).

To prove Theorem
B
, we first characterize the conformal type of geodesic Lie groups, in terms of their growth and isoperimetric dimensions.
Sobolev and isoperimetric inequalities and their relation with heat diffusion and volume growth on Riemannian Lie groups have been studied thoroughly; see, for instance
[
49
,
50
,
47
]
.
In particular, Pittet proved in
[
43
, Theorem 2.1]

that the isoperimetric profile at large scale of a Riemannian Lie group
is determined by its volume-growth type and whether it is amenable and unimodular or not.
We need a similar statement for geodesic Lie groups:
since every two geodesic left-invariant distances on a Lie group are quasi-isometric equivalent to each other, our task is to show that the isoperimetric dimension is preserved under quasi-isometries.
We will do this following a strategy of Kanai from
[
32
]
; see Theorem
6.6
.
As a consequence, we obtain the following characterization of isoperimetric profiles at large scale for geodesic Lie groups.

Theorem D

(see Theorem
8.2
)
.

Let
(G,d,\mu)
be a non-compact geodesic Lie group.
Then exactly one of the following cases happens:

(a)

G
is non-amenable or non-unimodular, it has exponential volume growth,
and there is
C
such that, for every measurable
E\subset G
,
if
\mu(E)\geq 1
then
\mu(E)\leq C\operatorname{Per}(E)
.
In particular,
\dim_{\rm isp}(X)=\dim_{\rm grt}(X)=\infty
.

(b)

G
is amenable, unimodular, it has exponential volume growth,
and for every
N>1
there is
C
such that, for every measurable
E\subset G
,
if
\mu(E)\geq 1
then
\mu(E)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\leq C\operatorname{Per}(E)
.
In particular,
\dim_{\rm isp}(X)=\dim_{\rm grt}(X)=\infty
.

(c)

G
is amenable, unimodular, it has polynomial growth of degree
N
, for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
and there is
C
such that, for every measurable
E\subset G
,
if
\mu(E)\geq 1
, then
\mu(E)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\leq C\operatorname{Per}(E)
.
In particular,
\dim_{\rm isp}(X)=\dim_{\rm grt}(X)<\infty
.

As a consequence, for each non-compact geodesic Lie group
G
, one has equalities in (
4
), that is,

(5)

\dim_{\rm isp}(G)=\dim_{\rm par}(G)=\dim_{\rm grt}(G),

Recall that all Lie groups of polynomial growth are amenable and unimodular; see
[
15
, II.4.5]
.
Note that Theorem
D
provides a complete characterization of the parabolic conformal type of geodesics Lie groups in terms of their volume growth.

Theorem E

(see Corollary
8.3
)
.

A geodesic Lie group
(G,d,\mu)
has parabolic conformal type if and only if there exists
C>0
such that

(6)

\mu(B(x,r))\leq Cr^{Q},\qquad\forall r\geq 1,\ \forall x\in G,

where
Q
is the Hausdorff dimension of
(G,d)
.

Once we have established the conformal type of geodesic Lie groups, we can use the quasi-conformal invariance of capacity to prove Theorem
B
.
The two cases, parabolic and hyperbolic, are treated quite differently.

1.4.
QC maps in the hyperbolic conformal type

As we have described earlier, on each metric measure space, one can consider the hyperbolic Ferrand distance
\mathbbmss{h}
.
By construction, the function
\mathbbmss{h}
satisfies the triangular inequality and quasi-conformal maps are bi-Lipschitz maps for
\mathbbmss{h}
.
In the parabolic case, we have
\mathbbmss{h}\equiv 0
.
However, temporarily omitting some extra condition, if
(X,d,\mu)
is a metric measure space with hyperbolic conformal type,
the identity map
(X,d)\to(X,\mathbbmss{h})
is
bi-bornologous
, that is,
\lim_{n\to\infty}d(x_{n},y_{n})=\infty
if and only if
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbbmss{h}(x_{n},y_{n})=\infty
,
for every sequences
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
X
.

In all crucial capacity estimates, we make use of
monotone functions
.
Following the strategy outlined by Ferrand in
[
18
]
, we observe that the capacity can be obtained by infimising over monotone functions; see Proposition
3.15
.
Moreover, we recover from the work
[
24
]
of Hajłasz and Koskela upper bounds for the oscillation of monotone functions; see Section
3.3
.

At this point, we can regard each quasi-conformal map
f:(X,d)\to(Y,d)
as the composition of bornologous functions

(7)

(X,d)\overset{\mathrm{Id}}{\longrightarrow}(X,\mathbbmss{h})\overset{f}{\longrightarrow}(Y,\mathbbmss{h})\overset{\mathrm{Id}}{\longrightarrow}(Y,d).

Consequently,
f:(X,d)\to(Y,d)
is bornologous.
If
X
and
Y
are quasi-geodesic spaces, bornologous functions are large-scale Lipschitz;
see Proposition
2.12
.

The above strategy needs extra assumptions on the space
(X,d,\mu)
, which will be satisfied for geodesic Lie groups.
See Section
2
for the definition of uniformly locally bounded geometry.
A more general result is the following:

Theorem F

(see Theorem
7.8
)
.

Let
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
be metric measure spaces such that:

(1)

they are quasi-geodesic and boundedly compact;

(2)

they have uniformly locally bounded geometry, with Hausdorff dimension
Q>1
;

(3)

for each
r>0
, the measure of balls of radius
r
is uniformly bounded from above.

If
\dim_{\rm isp}(X_{1})>Q
and
\dim_{\rm isp}(X_{2})>Q
,
then every quasi-conformal map
X_{1}\to X_{2}
is a quasi-isometry.

1.5.
QC maps in the parabolic conformal type

In the parabolic case, the above hyperbolic Ferrand distance
\mathbbmss{h}
is the zero function.
So, we use the parabolic Ferrand distance
\mathbbmss{p}
.
We show that, under some additional hypothesis on the space, for every pair of sequences
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,
we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}d(x_{n},y_{n})=\infty
if and only if
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbbmss{p}(x_{n},y_{n})=\infty
.
Hence, a strategy similar to the one applied in the hyperbolic case allows us to show that quasi-conformal maps are bornologous, with bornologous inverse, and thus quasi-isometries.

For the argument, we need both lower and upper bounds to
\mathbbmss{p}
.
Upper bounds are based on properties of monotone functions; see Proposition
5.8
.
For the lower bounds, we need to construct, for every pair of points
x
and
y
, two appropriate connected and unbounded sets
E
and
F
such that
x\in E
and
y\in F
.
The intuitive idea is to take a straight line passing through the points and consider the two unbounded half-lines separated by the points.

To do this, we use what we call
quasi-straight sequences
:
roughly speaking, a quasi-straight sequence is a function
\zeta:\mathbb{Z}\to X
unbounded in both directions, so that its image is made of roughly evenly distributed points that are almost aligned, quantitatively; see Definition
5.10
.
We note that quasi-straight sequences form a larger class than infinite quasi-geodesics; see Remark
5.11
.

A metric space is
quasi-straightenable
if every two points lie close to a quasi-straight sequence, quantitatively with constants that are independent of the points;
see Definition
5.10
.
We can show that every non-compact geodesic Lie group with polynomial growth is quasi-straightenable, because simply connected nilpotent Lie groups are quasi-straightenable (using straight lines in exponential coordinates), and because every geodesic Lie group with polynomial growth is quasi-isometric to a simply connected nilpotent Lie group.

With these notions, we can prove the second part of Theorem
B
, when both spaces are strictly parabolic.

Theorem G

(see Theorem
5.19
)
.

Let
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
be metric measure spaces such that:

(1)

they are quasi-geodesic and boundedly compact;

(2)

they have uniformly locally bounded geometry, with Hausdorff dimension
Q>1
;

(3)

they are quasi-straightenable.

If
\dim_{\rm grt}(X_{1})<Q
and
\dim_{\rm grt}(X_{2})<Q
,
then every quasi-conformal map
X_{1}\to X_{2}
is a quasi-isometry.

Observe that the above Theorem
G
can only be applied to strictly parabolic geodesic Lie groups.
This hypothesis is used in the lower bound for
\mathbbmss{p}
; see Proposition
5.16
.
However, we can show that geodesic Lie groups that are liminal parabolic are globally Loewner spaces and thus the parabolic Ferrand distance
\mathbbmss{p}
is
0
at every pair of points;
see Proposition
5.7
.
Therefore, a strictly parabolic geodesic Lie group cannot be quasi-conformal equivalent to a liminal parabolic geodesic Lie group.
The latter argument is shadowing ideas from
[
16
]
.

2.
Spaces and maps

In this section, we introduce various properties of metric spaces and of their maps.

A metric space is said to be
locally compact
if every point has a compact neighborhood.
A metric space is
boundedly compact
if bounded sets are pre-compact.
If
(X,d)
is a metric space, we denote the
(open) ball
with center
x\in X
and radius
r>0
as
B(x,r):=\{y\in X:d(x,y)<r\}
,
and the
closed ball
as
\bar{B}(x,r):=\{y\in X:d(x,y)\leq r\}
.
If
E
is a subset of a metric space and
r>0
, we define

(8)

B(E,r):=\bigcup_{x\in E}B(x,r).

A subset
\mathscr{K}
of a metric space
(X,d)
is
r
-separated
for some
r\in(0,+\infty)
if
d(x,y)\geq r
for all
x,y\in\mathscr{K}
distinct.
An
r
-separated subset
\mathscr{K}\subset X
is
maximal
if it is maximal among
r
-separated sets with respect to inclusion of sets.
Equivalently, an
r
-separated subset
\mathscr{K}\subset X
is maximal if for every
z\in X
there exists
x\in\mathscr{K}
with
d(x,z)<r
.
In every metric space, for every
r\in(0,+\infty)
, there exists a maximal
r
-separated set by Zorn’s Lemma.

A
metric measure space
is a triple
(X,d,\mu)
where
(X,d)
is a metric space and
\mu
is a Borel regular measure on
X
.
Given a metric measure space
(X,d,\mu)
, the
volume functions of
X
are

\mu_{X}^{+},\mu_{X}^{-}:(0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty]
, defined by

(9)

\displaystyle\mu_{X}^{+}(r):=\sup\{\mu(B(x,r)):x\in X\},

\displaystyle\mu_{X}^{-}(r):=\inf\{\mu(B(x,r)):x\in X\},

for all

A Borel function

is an

of a function

If

.

Definition 2.1

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B(x,r)}|u-u_{B}|\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq C_{\rm P}r\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B(x,\sigma r)}g^{Q}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{1/Q},

.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric measure space.
A

is a pair
(E;F)
of subsets
E,F\subset X
.
The set of
admissible functions for the capacitor
(E;F)
is

(13)

\cal A(E;F):=\left\{u:X\to\mathbb{R}:\text{$u$ measurable, }E\subset\{u\geq 1\}\text{ and }F\subset\{u\leq 0\}\right\}.

For
p\in[1,\infty)
, the
p
-capacity
of a capacitor
(E;F)
is

(14)

\operatorname{Cap}_{p}(E;F):=\inf\left\{\int_{X}g^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu:\text{$g$ upper gradient of $u\in\cal A(E;F)$}\right\}.

The set of
admissible functions for the set
E\subset X
is

(15)

\cal A(E;\infty):=\left\{u:X\to\mathbb{R}:\begin{array}[]{c}\text{$u$ measurable, with compact support,}\\
\text{and }E\subset\{u\geq 1\}\end{array}\right\}.

For
p\in[1,\infty)
, the
p
-capacity (at infinity)
of a set
E\subset X
is

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cap}_{p}(E)

\displaystyle:=\operatorname{Cap}_{p}(E;\infty)

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{\int_{X}g^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu:\text{$g$ upper gradient of $u\in\cal A(E;\infty)$}\right\}.

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{\operatorname{Cap}_{p}(E;F):X\setminus F\text{ is compact}\right\}.

Remark 2.5
.

The capacity
E\mapsto\operatorname{Cap}_{p}(E)
is sub-additive and monotone.
Indeed, if
E_{1},E_{2}\subset X
and
u_{1},u_{2}:X\to\mathbb{R}
are functions with compact support and
E_{j}\subset\{u_{j}\geq 1\}
, then
u=\max\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
is such that
E_{1}\cup E_{2}\subset\{u\geq 1\}
and an upper gradient of
u
is
g=\max\{g_{1},g_{2}\}
, where
g_{j}
is an upper gradient of
u_{j}
.
Thus
\operatorname{Cap}_{p}(E_{1}\cup E_{2})\leq\operatorname{Cap}_{p}(E_{1})+\operatorname{Cap}_{p}(E_{2})
.
The monotonicity is clear from the definition as an infimum:
E_{1}\subset E_{2}
implies
\operatorname{Cap}_{p}(E_{1})\leq\operatorname{Cap}_{p}(E_{2})
.

We use the capacity to introduce a first notion of quasi-conformal maps.

Definition 2.6

(Quasi-conformal map: geometric definition)
.

A map
f:X\to Y
between metric measure spaces is
geometrically quasi-conformal of index
Q
or, simply,
quasi-conformal
, if it is a homeomorphism and if there exists
K\in\mathbb{R}
such that, for every

,

(16)

\frac{1}{K}\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(E;F)\leq\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(f(E);f(F))\leq K\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(E;F).

A related and more elementary definition is the so-called
metric definition of quasi-conformal maps
:

Definition 2.7

(Quasi-conformal map: metric definition)
.

A map
f:X\to Y
between metric spaces is
metrically quasi-conformal
if it is a homeomorphism and if there exists
C\in\mathbb{R}
such that, for every
x\in X
,

(17)

\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\sup\{d(f(x),f(x^{\prime})):x^{\prime}\in B(x,r)\}}{\inf\{d(f(x),f(x^{\prime})):x^{\prime}\notin B(x,r)\}}\leq C.

Remark 2.8
.

In the literature, it is known that the metric definition of quasi-conformal map implies the above geometric definition for homeomorphisms between spaces with
Q
-bounded geometry; see
[
27
, Theorem 9.10]
or
[
27
, Theorem 9.8]
with
[
26
, Proposition 2.17]
and
[
6
, Proposition 9.4]
.
See also
[
51
]
.
The above Definition
2.6
is a priori weaker than a more standard geometric definition, which is stated in terms of the modulus of curve families. Our definition would correspond to considering only families of curves connecting two sets (instead of arbitrary curve families); for the equivalence in the Euclidean setting, see
[

.
In our work, we will mostly use the geometric Definition

.
Later, in the case of nilpotent geodesic Lie groups (i.e., in Theorem

), we will consider the metric Definition

and make use of the fact that it implies the geometric Definition

.

Definition 2.9

(Quasi-isometry)
.

A function
f:X\to Y
between metric spaces is
large-scale Lipschitz
if there exist
L,C\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(18)

d(f(p),f(q))\leq Ld(p,q)+C,\qquad\forall p,q\in X.

A function
f:X\to Y
between metric spaces is a
quasi-isometry
if there exist
L,C\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(19)

\frac{1}{L}d(x_{1},x_{2})-C\leq d(f(x_{1}),f(x_{2}))\leq Ld(x_{1},x_{2})+C,\qquad\forall x_{1},x_{2}\in X,

and such that, for every
y\in Y
there exists
x\in X
with
d(f(x),y)\leq C
.

Definition 2.10

(Quasi-geodesic space)
.

A metric space
(X,d)
is
(\ell,r)
-quasi-geodesic
for some
\ell,r\in\mathbb{R}
,
if, for every
p,q\in X
there are
x^{0}=p,x^{1},\dots,x^{k}=q
with
d(x^{j-1},x^{j})\leq r

and
\sum_{j=1}^{k}d(x^{j-1},x^{j})\leq\ell d(p,q)
.

Definition 2.11

(Bornologous function)
.

A function
f:X\to Y
between metric spaces is
bornologous
if the following holds:
if
\{p_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}},\{q_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X
are sequences in
X
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}d(f(p_{n}),f(q_{n}))=\infty
, then
\lim_{n\to\infty}d(p_{n},q_{n})=\infty
.
For extra information, see
[
45
, p.6]
.

Compositions of bornologous maps are bornologous.
Large-scale Lipschitz maps are bornologous.
It is classic that for geodesic spaces the inverse is also valid; see
[
45
, Lemma 1.10]
.
For completeness, we include a proof of this exercise.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
X
and
Y
be metric spaces.
If
X
is quasi-geodesic and if
f:X\to Y
is bornologous, then
f
is large-scale Lipschitz.

Proof.

Suppose
X
is
(\ell,r)
-quasi-geodesic but
f
does not satisfy (
18
) for every
L,C>0
.
Then, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
there are
p_{n},q_{n}\in X
such that

(20)

d(f(p_{n}),f(q_{n}))>nd(p_{n},q_{n})+n.

Since
X
is
(\ell,r)
-geodesic,
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
there are
k_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
and

x_{n}^{0}=p_{n},x_{n}^{1},\dots,x_{n}^{k_{n}}=q_{n}
such that
d(x_{n}^{j-1},x_{n}^{j})\leq r

for all
j\in\{1,\dots,k_{n}\}
,
and
\sum_{j=1}^{k_{n}}d(x_{n}^{j-1},x_{n}^{j})\leq\ell d(p_{n},q_{n})
.

Since
f
is bornologous and by the assumption (
20
), we know that
d(p_{n},q_{n})\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
: in particular, up to passing to a subsequence, we can assume
d(p_{n},q_{n})\geq r
for all
n
.

If, for some
j
, we have
d(x_{n}^{j-1},x_{n}^{j})<r/2
, then we can skip a few points and still obtain a chain of points so that

r/2\leq d(x_{n}^{j-1},x_{n}^{j})\leq 3r
for all
j\in\{1,\dots,k_{n}\}
.
It follows that we can assume

(21)

\ell d(p_{n},q_{n})\geq\frac{k_{n}r}{2}.

Further, by the triangle inequality,

(22)

\sum_{j=1}^{k_{n}}d\left(f(x_{n}^{j-1}),f(x_{n}^{j})\right)\geq d(f(p_{n}),f(q_{n})).

Consequently, for each
n
there exists
j_{n}
such that
d(f(x_{n}^{j_{n}-1}),f(x_{n}^{j_{n}}))\geq\frac{d(f(p_{n}),f(q_{n}))}{k_{n}}
.
For such points, we have

(23)

d(f(x_{n}^{j_{n}-1}),f(x_{n}^{j_{n}}))\geq\frac{d(f(p_{n}),f(q_{n}))}{k_{n}}\overset{\eqref{eq67f62298}}{>}n\frac{d(p_{n},q_{n})}{k_{n}}+\frac{n}{k_{n}}\overset{\eqref{eq67f62053}}{\geq}\frac{rn}{2\ell}+\frac{n}{k_{n}}.

We conclude that

\lim_{n\to\infty}d(f(x_{n}^{j_{n}-1}),f(x_{n}^{j_{n}}))=\infty

even though
d(x_{n}^{j_{n}-1},x_{n}^{j_{n}})\leq 3r
,
in contradiction with
f
being bornologous.
∎

Corollary 2.13
.

A bijection between quasi-geodesic metric spaces that is bornologous and with inverse bornologous, is a quasi-isometry.

3.
Monotone functions

3.1.
Topological notions

Given a subset
Y
of a topological space
X
, we denote by
by
\operatorname{cl}(Y)
the closure of
Y
within
X
,
by
\operatorname{int}Y
the interior of
Y
within
X
, and
by
\partial Y
the topological boundary of
Y
within
X
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a topological space.
A subset
K\subset X
is a
continuum
if it is compact and connected.
A subset
C\subset X
is an
\infty
-continuum
if it is closed and every connected component of
C
is not compact.
Equivalently, a closed set
C\subset X
is an
\infty
-continuum if the closure of
C
in the Alexandrov 1-point compactification of
X
is a continuum.

In
[
18
]
, Ferrand called
\infty
-continua with the name “r-continuum”, where “r” stands for “relative”.
From Ferrand’s work, we isolated the following remark, which will be useful to study monotone functions.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X
be a Hausdorff topological space and
C\subset X
an
\infty
-continuum.
If
Y\subset X
is compact with
\operatorname{int}Y\cap C\neq\emptyset
, then
\partial Y\cap C\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

Up to passing to a connected component of
C
that contains a point of
\operatorname{int}Y\cap C
, we can assume that
C
is connected.
Define
Y_{1}=\operatorname{int}Y\cap C
and
Y_{2}=C\setminus Y=C\cap(X\setminus Y)
.
Since
Y
is a compact subset of a Hausdorff space, then
Y
is closed;
thus,
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
are both open in
C
.
The set
Y_{1}
is not empty by assumption.
The set
Y_{2}
is not empty, because otherwise
C=C\cap Y
would be a closed set in a compact set, thus compact; this is impossible since
\infty
-continua are assumed not compact.
Moreover,
Y_{1}\cap Y_{2}=\emptyset
.
Since
C
is connected, then
C\neq Y_{1}\cup Y_{2}
, i.e., there exists
c\in C\setminus(Y_{1}\cup Y_{2})
.
This means that
c\in\partial Y
,
because
X=\operatorname{int}Y\sqcup\partial Y\sqcup(X\setminus Y)
.
∎

3.2.
Monotone functions on topological spaces

Definition 3.3
.

Let
X
be a topological space.
A function
u:X\to\mathbb{R}
is
monotone
if, for every compact
K\subset X
,

(24)

\sup_{\partial K}u=\sup_{K}u\quad\text{ and }\quad\inf_{\partial K}u=\inf_{K}u.

Further references for monotone functions are
[
37
,
18
,
34
]
.
Notice that, since
\sup\emptyset=-\infty
, if there exists
K\subset X
compact with
\partial K=\emptyset
, then there are no monotone functions
X\to\mathbb{R}
.

The following two lemmas are due to Ferrand, who proved the analog for Riemannian manifolds.
For completeness, we include her argument showing the validity for topological spaces.

Lemma 3.4

(Ferrand)
.

Let
X
be a Hausdorff topological space.
Let
C\subset X
be an
\infty
-continuum and
u:X\to\mathbb{R}
continuous.
Suppose that
u
is monotone in
X\setminus C
and that
C\subset\{u=\sup_{X}u\}\cup\{u=\inf_{X}u\}
.
Then, the function
u
is monotone on
X
.

Proof.

The argument for this proof is inspired by
[
18
, (2.4)]
.
We start considering the case
C\subset\{u=\sup_{X}u\}
.
Set
M:=\sup_{X}u
and let
K\subset X
compact.

First, assume
\partial K\cap C=\emptyset
.
Then
K\cap C=\emptyset
by the contrapositive of Lemma
3.2
.
So, we have the identities (
24
) on
K
because
u
is monotone on
X\setminus C
.

Second, assume
\partial K\cap C\neq\emptyset
.
Since
M=\sup_{X}u\geq\sup_{K}u\geq\sup_{\partial K}u\geq\sup_{\partial K\cap C}u=M
, then we have
\sup_{K}u=\sup_{\partial K}u
.

We need to show that
\inf_{K}u=\inf_{\partial K}u
.
If
\inf_{K}u=M
, then
K\subset\{u=M\}
and thus
\inf_{\partial K}u=M=\inf_{K}u
.
So, suppose that
\inf_{K}u<M-\epsilon
for some
\epsilon>0
.
Define
K_{\epsilon}:=K\cap\{u\leq M-\epsilon\}
.
Since
u
is continuous, then
K_{\epsilon}
is the intersection of a compact set and a closed set, so
K_{\epsilon}
is compact.
Since
\partial K_{\epsilon}\subset\{u\leq M-\epsilon\}
and
\{u\leq M-\epsilon\}\cap C=\emptyset
,
then
\partial K_{\epsilon}\cap C=\emptyset
and thus, by the first part of the proof,

\inf_{K}u=\inf_{K_{\epsilon}}u=\inf_{\partial K_{\epsilon}}u
.
Let
x\in\partial{K_{\epsilon}}
with
u(x)=\inf_{\partial K_{\epsilon}}u
.
If
x\in\operatorname{int}(K)
, then

x\in\operatorname{int}(K)\cap\{u<M-\epsilon\}\subset\operatorname{int}(K_{\epsilon})
: contradiction.
Thus,
x\in\partial K
and we conclude that
\inf_{K}u=\inf_{\partial K}u
.

We have shown the lemma whenever
C\subset\{u=\sup_{X}u\}
.
Clearly, we directly obtain the same statement for
C\subset\{u=\inf_{X}u\}
,
by substituting
u
with
-u
.
If
C\subset\{u=\sup_{X}u\}\cup\{u=\inf_{X}u\}
, then
C=C_{\rm s}\sqcup C_{\rm i}
with

C_{\rm s}:=C\cap\{u=\sup_{X}u\}
and
C_{\rm i}:=C\cap\{u=\inf_{X}u\}
.
Then, if a continuous function
u:X\to\mathbb{R}
is monotone in
X\setminus C=(X\setminus C_{\rm i})\setminus C_{\rm s}
,
then it is monotone on
X\setminus C_{\rm i}
be the first part of the proof, so
u
is monotone on
X
by the subsequent observation.
∎

Lemma 3.5

(Ferrand)
.

Let
X
be a Hausdorff topological space.
Let
K\subset X
be a continuum and
u:X\to\mathbb{R}
continuous such that
K\subset\{u=\sup_{X}u\}
or
K\subset\{u=\inf_{X}u\}
.
If
u
is monotone in
X\setminus K
, then, for every
x\in K
, the function
u
is monotone in
X\setminus\{x\}
.

Proof.

The argument is taken from
[
18
, (2.5)]
.
We claim that, in the topological space
X\setminus\{x\}
, the set
K\setminus\{x\}
is an
\infty
-continuum.
Indeed, let
C
be a connected component of
K\setminus\{x\}
.
Since
X
is Hausdorff, then
X\setminus\{x\}
is open and thus
C
, which is open in
K\setminus\{x\}
, is open in
K
.
If
C
were compact, it would be closed in
K
because
K
is Hausdorff: since
K
is connected, we would conclude
C=K
, but
x\in K\setminus C
.
Therefore, we conclude that
C
is not compact, and this shows the claim.
The statement finally follows from Lemma
3.4
. ∎

3.3.
Estimate of the oscillation for monotone functions

Definition 3.6
.

Given a space
X
, define the

of a function
u:X\to\mathbb{R}
over a set

as

(25)

\operatorname{osc}_{D}u:=\sup_{D}u-\inf_{D}u={\rm diam}(u(D)).

We will prove the following result, which is an adaptation of
[
24
, Theorem 7.2]
.

Theorem 3.7

(after Hajłasz and Koskela)
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric measure space with uniformly locally
Q
-bounded geometry with data
(Q,R,C_{\rm A},C_{\rm P},\sigma)
and with

.
There is a constant
C_{1}\geq 1
such that the following holds.
For every
x_{0}\in X
, for every
r\in(0,R/C_{1})
, for every

continuous with upper gradient

,
there is

such that for every

with
d(x_{0},x_{1})=d(x_{0},x_{2})=t

(26)

|u(x_{1})-u(x_{2})|\leq C_{1}\left(\frac{d(x_{1},x_{2})}{r}\right)^{1/Q}\left(\int_{B(x_{0},C_{1}r)\setminus B(x_{0},r/C_{1})}g^{Q}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{Q}}.

Before the proof of Theorem
3.7
, we state a special case of
[

, Theorem 7.2]
(for continuous

, with

), and then discuss the adaptations needed to deduce Theorem

.

Theorem 3.8

(Theorem 7.2 in
[

]
)
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric measure space, and

.
Assume that

such that

(27)

\mu(B(z,s))\geq C_{\rm b}\mu(B(z_{0},s_{0}))\left(\frac{s}{s_{0}}\right)^{Q},

for all

,

,

, and
0<s\leq s_{0}
. Let

be a continuous function and
g:X\to[0,+\infty]
a measurable function such that there exist constants
C_{\rm P}>0
and
\sigma\geq 1

3.4.
Lebesgue Straightening Lemma

The aim of this subsection is Proposition
3.14
, which is an integration of the Lebesgue Straightening Lemma as proven by Mostow in
[
37
]
with a statement about upper-gradients.
For this, we need to revise the notions introduced by Mostow, so as to prove Lemma
3.12
.
Ferrand proved Proposition
3.14
in the context of Riemannian manifolds; see
[
34
, §3.5]
and
[
18
, §1.8]
.

Let
X
be a topological space,
U\subset X
open, and
u:U\to\mathbb{R}
continuous.
For
a\in\mathbb{R}
, denote
aUu
the union of all connected components of
U\setminus u^{-1}(a)
whose closures lie in
U
.
For
a\in\mathbb{R}
, define the function
u.a:U\to\mathbb{R}
,

(47)

(u.a)(x):=\begin{cases}a&\text{ if }x\in aUu,\\
u(x)&\text{ if }x\notin aUu.\end{cases}

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
X
be a metric space,
u:X\to\mathbb{R}
continuous, and
U\subset X
open.
Let
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
(u.a):X\to\mathbb{R}
be the function modified as in (
47
) on
U
.
If
g:X\to[0,+\infty]
is an upper gradient for
u
, then
g
is an upper gradient also for
(u.a)
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma:[0,1]\to X
be a rectifiable curve.
We need to show (
10
) for
u.a
, that is,

(48)

|(u.a)(\gamma(1))-(u.a)(\gamma(0))|\leq\int_{\gamma}g.

We have three cases:
If
\gamma(1),\gamma(0)\in aUu
, then (
48
) is trivial, because the left-hand side of (
48
) is zero.
If
\gamma(1),\gamma(0)\notin aUu
, then (
48
) is trivial, because
g
is an upper gradient of
u
.
Up to reversing the direction of
\gamma
, the only remaining case is
\gamma(0)\in aUu
and
\gamma(1)\notin aUu
.
Then, there is
t\in[0,1]
such that
\gamma(t)\in\partial(aUu)
, so that
(u.a)(\gamma(0))=a=u(\gamma(t))
.
Therefore,

|(u.a)(\gamma(1))-(u.a)(\gamma(0))|=|u(\gamma(1))-u(\gamma(t))|\leq\int_{\gamma|_{[t,1]}}g\leq\int_{\gamma}g.

∎

Mostow proved the following statement.

Lemma 3.13

(Lebesgue Straightening Lemma –
[
37
, p.67]
)
.

Let
U
be an open proper subset of a connected locally connected topological space and let
u:\operatorname{cl}U\to[m,M]
be a continuous function, for some
m,M\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
a:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{Q}\cap[m,M]
be an enumeration of the rational numbers in
[m,M]
.
For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, set

(49)

u_{n}=(\dots((u.a_{1}).a_{2})\dots).a_{n}.

Then
u_{n}
converges uniformly on
\operatorname{cl}U
to a monotone function.

Lemma
3.13
can be strengthened by keeping track of the upper gradients of the functions
u_{n}
.

Proposition 3.14

(Lebesgue Straightening Lemma – version 2)
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a connected locally connected metric measure space.
Let
U\subset X
be open. Let
u:X\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous, and bounded on
U
.
Then there exists
v:X\to\mathbb{R}
continuous such that

(1)

v
is monotone in
U
;

(2)

u=v
on
X\setminus U
;

(3)

upper gradients of
u
are upper gradients of
v
too.

Proof.

Let
v
be the function given by Lemma
3.13
.
Then
v
is monotone in
U
and
u=v
on
X\setminus U
.
We claim that, if
g:X\to[0,+\infty]
is an upper gradient for
u
, then
g
is an upper gradient also for
v
.
Indeed, by Lemma
3.12
, the function
g
is an upper gradient for each approximating function
u_{n}
defined in (
49
).
Since
u_{n}
converges uniformly to
v
on
X
, then
g
is an upper gradient of
v
as well.
This proves the last part of the proposition. ∎

Using Proposition

, we show that the capacity can be estimated using monotone admissible functions.
For this reason, define for

and

\cal A_{\rm m}(E;F):=\{u\in\cal A(E;F):u\text{ monotone on }X\setminus(E\cup F)\}.

.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric measure space and

For every

closed, or

\operatorname{Cap}_{p}(E;F)=\inf\left\{\int_{X}g^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu:\text{$g$ upper gradient of $u\in\cal A_{\rm m}(E;F)$}\right\}.

Let

Remark 3.16
.

Among the properties of quasi-conformal maps, we will strongly use two properties:
First, they bi-Lipschitz preserve the capacity as by (
16
).
Second, they are homeomorphisms, and hence they preserve the topology.
In particular, quasi-conformal maps preserve monotone functions.
For this reason, we could define the capacity using only monotone admissible functions, and consider maps that preserve this reduced capacity.
Proposition
3.15
ensures that this choice is not restrictive.

4.2.

The following Lemma
4.3
is a standard exercise, and we will use this result for capacity estimates.
We include the proof for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric measure space and
o\in X
.
Let
0<\bar{r}<\bar{R}<\infty
and suppose that there are
q>1
and
C>0
such that

(53)

\mu(B(o,r))\leq Cr^{q},\qquad\forall r\in[\bar{r},\bar{R}].

Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cap}_{q}(B(o,\bar{r});X\setminus B(o,\bar{R}))

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1+q(\log(\bar{R})-\log(\bar{r}))}{(\log(\bar{R})-\log(\bar{r}))^{q}}

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cap}_{p}(B(o,\bar{r});X\setminus B(o,\bar{R}))

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\left(-\frac{q}{p-q}\bar{R}^{q-p}+\frac{p}{p-q}\bar{r}^{q-p}\right)}{(\log(\bar{R})-\log(\bar{r}))^{p}},

\displaystyle\forall p\in[1,+\infty)\setminus\{q\}.

Proof.

Define the function

(54)

u(x):=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }x\in B(o,\bar{r}),\\
\frac{\log\bar{R}-\log(d(o,x))}{\log\bar{R}-\log\bar{r}}&\text{ if }x\in B(o,\bar{R})\setminus B(o,\bar{r}),\\
0&\text{ if }x\in X\setminus B(o,\bar{R}).\end{cases}

The function
u
is an admissible function for the capacitor
B(o,\bar{r});X\setminus B(o,\bar{R})
.
Using the chain rule and the fact that the distance function is 1-Lipschitz,
one can easily show that an upper gradient of
u
is the function
g:X\to[0,+\infty]
,

(55)

g(x):=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }x\in B(o,\bar{r}),\\
\frac{1}{\log\bar{R}-\log\bar{r}}\frac{1}{d(o,x)}&\text{ if }x\in B(o,\bar{R})\setminus B(o,\bar{r}),\\
0&\text{ if }x\in X\setminus B(o,\bar{R}).\end{cases}

Set
L:=\frac{1}{\log\bar{R}-\log\bar{r}}
and let
p\in[1,\infty)
.
Notice that
(L/\bar{R})^{p}\leq g^{p}\leq(L/\bar{r})^{p}
on
B(o,\bar{R})\setminus B(o,r)
,
and that
\{x:g(x)^{p}>t\}=B(o,L/t^{1/p})
for every
t\in[(L/\bar{R})^{p},(L/\bar{r})^{p})
.
Therefore

\displaystyle\int_{X}g(x)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

\displaystyle[\text{Cavalieri}]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(g^{p}>t)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{L^{p}/\bar{R}^{p}}(\mu(B(o,\bar{R}))-\mu(B(o,\bar{r}))\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{L^{p}/\bar{R}^{p}}^{L^{p}/\bar{r}^{p}}\mu(B(o,L/t^{1/p}))\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle[\text{by\penalty\ \eqref{eq67f8cd13}}]

\displaystyle\leq CL^{p}\bar{R}^{q-p}+CL^{q}\int_{L^{p}/\bar{R}^{p}}^{L^{p}/\bar{r}^{p}}t^{-q/p}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle[\text{set }s^{p}=t]

\displaystyle=CL^{p}\bar{R}^{q-p}+pCL^{q}\int_{L/\bar{R}}^{L/\bar{r}}s^{p-1-q}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=CL^{p}\bar{R}^{q-p}+pCL^{q}\begin{cases}\log(\bar{R}/\bar{r})&\text{ if }p=q\\
\frac{L^{p-q}}{p-q}(1/\bar{r}^{p-q}-1/\bar{R}^{p-q})&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}C\frac{1+q(\log(\bar{R})-\log(\bar{r}))}{(\log(\bar{R})-\log(\bar{r}))^{q}}&\text{ if }p=q,\\
\frac{C}{(\log(\bar{R})-\log(\bar{r}))^{p}}\left(-\frac{q}{p-q}\bar{R}^{q-p}+\frac{p}{p-q}\bar{r}^{q-p}\right)&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

∎

4.3.
Volume growth VS parabolicity

There are several results on Riemannian manifolds that link volume growth at large scale with parabolicity.
See for instance
[
55
, §5.2]
, or
[
47
, Cor.5.6]
.
The next result is a general one:

Proposition 4.4
.

Suppose that
(X,d,\mu)
is a metric measure space with
degree of large-scale growth at most
N\in(1,+\infty)
,
that is,
there exist
C,N>1
such that

(56)

\mu(B(x,R))\leq CR^{N}\qquad\forall x\in X,\forall R>1.

Then
X
is
p
-parabolic for every
p\geq N
, and thus
\dim_{\rm par}(X)\leq N
.

Proof.

Let
x\in X
,
r>0
, and
p\geq N
.
We give an upper bound to
\operatorname{Cap}_{p}(B(x,r))

using the function given by Lemma
4.3
.
If
p=N>1
, then

(57)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cap}_{p}(B(o,r))\leq\liminf_{R\to\infty}C\frac{1+N(\log(R)-\log(r))}{(\log(R)-\log(r))^{N}}=0.

If
p>N
, i.e.,
N-p<0
, then

(58)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cap}_{p}(B(o,r))\leq\liminf_{R\to\infty}C\frac{\left(R^{N-p}\frac{N}{N-p}-r^{N-p}\frac{p}{N-p}\right)}{(\log(R)-\log(r))^{p}}=0.

∎

4.4.
Sobolev inequality VS hyperbolicity

We give a sufficient condition for hyperbolicity of a metric measure space.
In Proposition
4.5
, we show that a Sobolev inequality at large scale implies hyperbolicity.
Since we can deduce a Sobolev inequality from the isoperimetric inequality, we will show in Corollary
6.13
that a large-scale isoperimetric inequality implies hyperbolicity.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric measure space
with compact sets of arbitrarily large measure.
Suppose also that there are
N\geq 1
,
C_{N}>0
, and
C_{\infty}\geq 0
such that,
for every
u:X\to\mathbb{R}
measurable with upper gradient
g:X\to[0,+\infty]
,

(59)

\left(\int_{X}|u|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\leq C_{N}\int_{X}g\,\mathrm{d}\mu+C_{\infty}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Then
X
is
p
-hyperbolic for every
p\in[1,N)
, and thus
\dim_{\rm par}(X)\geq N
.

Proof.

Let
K\Subset X
.
Let
u:X\to[0,1]
with
K\subset\{u=1\}
, and
g:X\to[0,+\infty]
an upper gradient of
u
.
For
q\geq 1
,
we apply (
59
) to
u^{q}
,
noticing that an upper gradient of
u^{q}
is
qu^{q-1}g
.
Therefore, for
q\in(1,+\infty)
and
p\in(1,N)
,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{X}(u^{q})^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq67f05467}}{\leq}C_{N}\int_{X}qu^{q-1}g\,\mathrm{d}\mu+C_{\infty}

\displaystyle[\text{by H\"{o}lder inequality}]

\displaystyle\leq C_{N}q\left(\int_{X}u^{(q-1)\frac{p}{p-1}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\int_{X}g^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+C_{\infty}.

We want
(q-1)\frac{p}{p-1}=q\frac{N}{N-1}
, which means

q=\frac{1}{1-\frac{N(p-1)}{(N-1)p}}=\frac{(N-1)p}{(N-1)p-N(p-1)}=\frac{(N-1)p}{N-p}.

Since
p\in[1,N)
, then
q\geq 1
.
So, we get

\left(\int_{X}u^{\frac{qN}{N-1}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{\frac{N-1}{N}-\frac{p-1}{p}}\leq C_{N}q\left(\int_{X}g^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+C_{\infty}\left(\int_{X}u^{\frac{qN}{N-1}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{-\frac{p-1}{p}}.

Since

\int_{X}u^{\frac{qN}{N-1}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\geq\mu(K),

we obtain

\mu(K)^{\frac{N-p}{Np}}-C_{\infty}\frac{1}{\mu(K)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}}\leq C_{N}q\left(\int_{X}g^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Since we can choose
K
with arbitrarily large measure,
we conclude that, for every
p\in[1,N)
, there exists
K\subset X
compact with
\operatorname{Cap}_{p}(K)>0
.
∎

If (

) holds with

, then the proof of Proposition

does not need compact sets of arbitrarily large measure,
but only one compact set with positive measure.

5.1.
Volume growth and growth dimension

In this subsection, we turn to volume growth conditions and their interaction with quasi-isometries.

Definition 5.1

(Growth at infinity)
.

A metric measure space
(X,d,\mu)
has
degree of large-scale growth at most
N
, for
N\in[0,\infty)
, if there exists
C\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(60)

\mu(B(x,R))\leq CR^{N},\qquad\forall x\in X,\ \forall R>1.

The
growth dimension
of
X
is

(61)

\dim_{\rm grt}(X):=\inf\left\{N\geq 0:\text{$X$ has degree of large-scale growth at most\penalty\ $N$}\right\}.

In Proposition
4.4
, we have seen that, if a metric measure space
X
has degree of large-scale growth at most
N\in(1,\infty)
, then
X
is
p
-parabolic for every
p\in[1,N]
.
In particular,

(62)

\dim_{\rm par}(X)\leq\dim_{\rm grt}(X).

In Proposition
5.2
below, we will show that, under certain geometric conditions, the growth dimension is a quasi-isometric invariant.
This is a standard exercise, and we include the proof for completeness.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be separable metric measure spaces,
and suppose that their volume functions, defined as in (
9
), are valued in
(0,+\infty)
.
Assume
X
and
Y
are quasi-isometric and let
N\in[1,+\infty)
.
Then, the space
X
has degree of large-scale growth at most
N
if and only if
Y
does.

The proof is postponed after the following lemma.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
(X,d_{X},\mu_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y})
be separable metric measure spaces.
Let
f:X\to Y
be a map such that there are
L,C\in\mathbb{R}
with

(63)

d_{Y}(f(a),f(b))\leq Ld_{X}(a,b)+C,\qquad\forall a,b\in X.

For
r,t\in(0,\infty)
, define

(64)

\displaystyle\cal C_{f}(r,t)

\displaystyle:=\sup\left\{\frac{\mu_{Y}(B(f(x),t))}{\mu_{X}(B(x,r))}:x\in X\right\}.

Then, for every
E\subset X
and every
r,s\in(0,\infty)
,

(65)

\mu_{Y}(B(f(E),s))\leq\cal C_{f}(r,2Lr+C+s)\mu_{X}(B(E,r)).

Proof.

Fix
E\subset X
and
r\in(0,+\infty)
.
Let
\mathscr{K}\subset E
be a maximal set of points that are
2r
-separated.
Since
X
is separable, then
\mathscr{K}
is countable.
Since the points are
2r
-separated, then

(66)

\bigsqcup_{k\in\mathscr{K}}B(k,r)\subset B(E,r).

By maximality, we have,

(67)

E\subset\bigcup_{k\in\mathscr{K}}B(k,2r).

Hence, for every
s\geq 0
we have

(68)

\displaystyle B(f(E),s)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq68ce54d8}}{\subset}\bigcup_{k\in\mathscr{K}}B(f(B(k,2r)),s)\overset{\eqref{eq6866a4ee}}{\subset}\bigcup_{k\in\mathscr{K}}B(B(f(k),2Lr+C),s)

\displaystyle\subset\bigcup_{k\in\mathscr{K}}B(f(k),2Lr+C+s).

In what follows, we assume that, for all
k\in\mathscr{K}
, we have
\mu_{Y}(B(f(k),2Lr+C+s))<\infty
and
\mu_{X}(B(k,r))>0
,
because such special cases imply directly (
65
) using the convention
0\cdot\infty=\infty
.
Therefore,

(69)

\displaystyle\mu_{Y}(B(f(E),s))

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq68375526}}{\leq}\sum_{k\in\mathscr{K}}\mu_{Y}(B(f(k),2Lr+C+s))

(70)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathscr{K}}\frac{\mu_{Y}(B(f(k),2Lr+C+s))}{\mu_{X}(B(k,r))}\mu_{X}(B(k,r))

(71)

\displaystyle\leq\cal C_{f}(r,2Lr+C+s)\sum_{k\in\mathscr{K}}\mu_{X}(B(k,r))

(72)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq683755ad}}{\leq}\cal C_{f}(r,2Lr+C+s)\mu_{X}(B(E,r)).\qed

Proof of Proposition
5.2
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a
(L,C)
-quasi-isometry.
It is immediate to show that, for every
x\in X
and
R>0
,

(73)

B(f(x),R)\subset B(f(B(x,L(R+2C))),C).

Indeed, if
z\in B(f(x),R)
, then there exists
y\in X
with
d(f(y),z)\leq C
.
It follows that
d(x,y)\leq Ld(f(x),f(y))+LC\leq L(d(f(x),z)+d(z,f(y)))+LC\leq L(R+C)+LC=L(R+2C)
.

Using Lemma
5.3
, we get

(74)

\displaystyle\mu_{Y}(B(f(x),R))

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq6866ab6e}}{\leq}\mu_{Y}(B(f(B(x,L(R+2C))),C))

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq68377070}}{\leq}\cal C_{f}(1,2L+2C)\mu_{X}(B(x,L(R+2C)))

\displaystyle\leq\cal C_{f}(1,2L+2C)\mu_{X}^{+}(L(R+2C)).

Since the volume functions are valued in
(0,+\infty)
,
\cal C_{f}(1,2L+2C)<\infty
.

If
z\in Y
and
R>0
, then there is
x\in X
with
d(f(x),z)\leq C
.
Hence,

(75)

\mu_{Y}(B(z,R))\leq\mu_{Y}(B(f(x),R+C))\overset{\eqref{eq6866afe4}}{\leq}\cal C_{f}(1,2L+2C)\mu_{X}^{+}(L(R+3C)).

We conclude that

(76)

\mu_{Y}^{+}(R)\leq\cal C_{f}(1,2L+2C)\mu_{X}^{+}(LR+3LC),\qquad\forall R\in(0,\infty).

The latter estimate easily implies that if
X
has degree of large-scale growth at most
N\in[1,+\infty)
, then so does
Y
.
Since quasi-isometries have quasi-inverses, the reversed implication also holds.
∎

5.2.
The parabolic Ferrand distance

We study quasi-conformal maps between spaces of parabolic conformal type using what we call the parabolic Ferrand distance
\mathbbmss{p}
, which we soon introduce.
This gauge-type function is defined in terms of capacitors made by pairs of
\infty
-continua.
The parabolic Ferrand distance is a power of a function, denoted below by
\mathscr{P}
, that has been studied by Ferrand in
[
34
,
18
]
.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric measure space with Hausdorff dimension
Q\geq 1
.
For
x,y\in X
, we define the
Ferrand gauge
by

(77)

\mathscr{P}(x,y):=\inf\left\{\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(E;F):\begin{array}[]{c}x\in E,\ y\in F,\\
\text{$E$ and $F$ $\infty$-continua}\end{array}\right\}.

For
x,y\in X
, we define the
parabolic Ferrand distance
by

(78)

\mathbbmss{p}(x,y):=\frac{1}{\mathscr{P}(x,y)^{\frac{1}{Q}}}=\sup\left\{\frac{1}{\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(E;F)^{\frac{1}{Q}}}:\begin{array}[]{c}x\in E,\ y\in F,\\
\text{$E$ and $F$ $\infty$-continua}\end{array}\right\}.

Remark 5.5
.

Despite the name, we do not claim that the function
\mathbbmss{p}:X\times X\to[0,+\infty]
in Definition
5.4
is a distance in complete generality.
Ferrand proved for Riemannian manifolds in
[
34
, Théorème 7.1 & §8.2]
that,
if
X
is a Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
, then
\mathbbmss{p}:(x,y)\mapsto\frac{1}{\mathscr{P}(x,y)^{1/n}}
satisfies the triangle inequality.
In
[
19
, Proposition 3.2]
, Ferrand improved the result showing that

\mathbbmss{p}^{\frac{n}{n-1}}
satisfies the triangle inequality.

We will show that
\mathbbmss{p}
is bi-Lipschitz preserved by quasi-conformal maps,
and then, under certain conditions on the space, we will give an upper bound for
\mathbbmss{p}

in Proposition
5.8
and later a lower bound to
\mathbbmss{p}
as a consequence of the upper bounds in Proposition
5.16
.
The final result will be to show that, under specific conditions on the spaces, quasi-conformal maps are bornologous; see Theorem
5.19
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
X
and
Y
be metric measure spaces with locally
Q
-bounded geometry,
and let
f:X\to Y
be a quasi-conformal map.
Then there exists
K>1
such that the parabolic Ferrand distance
\mathbbmss{p}
satisfies

(79)

\frac{1}{K}\mathbbmss{p}(x_{1},x_{2})\leq\mathbbmss{p}(f(x_{1}),f(x_{2}))\leq K\mathbbmss{p}(x_{1},x_{2}),\qquad\forall x_{1},x_{2}\in X.

Proof.

We equivalently show that there exists
K>1
such that for the Ferrand gauge
\mathscr{P}
we have

(80)

\frac{1}{K}\mathscr{P}(x_{1},x_{2})\leq\mathscr{P}(f(x_{1}),f(x_{2}))\leq K\mathscr{P}(x_{1},x_{2}),\qquad\forall x_{1},x_{2}\in X.

We focus on the second inequality in (
80
).
If
\mathscr{P}(x_{1},x_{2})=\infty
, then the inequality is true.
Suppose
\mathscr{P}(x_{1},x_{2})<\infty
and let
\epsilon>0
.
Then there are
\infty
-continua
E,F\subset X
such that
x\in E
,
y\in F
and

\mathscr{P}(x_{1},x_{2})+\epsilon\geq\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(E;F)
.

Since
f
is a homeomorphism, then
f(E),f(F)\subset Y
are
\infty
-continua
with
f(x_{1})\in f(E)
,
f(x_{2})\in f(F)
.
Therefore,
\mathscr{P}(f(x_{1}),f(x_{2}))\leq\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(f(E);f(F))
.

By the Geometric Definition
2.6
of quasi-conformal map, there is
K>0
such that

(81)

\mathscr{P}(f(x_{1}),f(x_{2}))\leq\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(f(E);f(F))\leq K\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(E;F)\leq K\mathscr{P}(x_{1},x_{2})+K\epsilon.

Since
\epsilon
is an arbitrary positive number, we obtain the second inequality in (
80
).
The first inequality in (
80
) follows from the fact that the above statement also applies to
f^{-1}
.
∎

The main application of the Ferrand distance will be Theorem
5.19
, which is itself based on the above Proposition
5.6
and Proposition
5.18
.
One of the hypothesis of Proposition
5.18
, which then reverberate in Theorem
5.19
, is that the growth dimension
N
is strictly smaller than the Hausdorff dimension
Q
.
The case
N=Q
has to be singled out with the following proposition, which is a direct application of the study of Loewner spaces made in
[
26
]
.
In these spaces, we have a global Poincaré inequality and a global two-sided volume-growth bound.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric measure space with
Q
-bounded geometry, as
in Definition
2.1
with
R\equiv+\infty
.
Suppose also that
X
is geodesic and boundedly compact, and that
Q>1
.
Then, the parabolic Ferrand distance
\mathbbmss{p}
satisfies

(82)

\mathbbmss{p}(x,y)=0,\qquad\forall x,y\in X.

Proof.

A geodesic, boundedly compact space with
Q
-bounded geometry,
Q>1
, is a Loewner space by
[
26
, Theorem 5.7]
; see also
[
25
, Theorem 9.10]
.
Let
\psi_{Q}:(0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty]
be the Lowener function of
X
, that is,

(83)

\psi_{Q}(t)=\inf\left\{\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(E;F):\begin{array}[]{c}E,F\subset X\text{ continua with }\\
0<\operatorname{dist}(E,F)\leq t\min\{\operatorname{diam}E,\operatorname{diam}F\}\end{array}\right\}.

By
[
26
, Theorem 3.6]
,
\psi_{Q}(t)\approx\log\frac{1}{t}
as
t\approx 0
; see also
[
25
, Theorem 8.23]
.

Let
x,y\in X
and
E,F\subset X
be
\infty
-continua with
x\in E
and
y\in F
.
For every
R>1
, let
E_{R}
and
F_{R}
be the connected components of
E\cap B(x,R)
and
F\cap B(y,R)
containing
x
and
y
, respectively.
Then
\operatorname{dist}(E_{R},F_{R})\leq d(x,y)
for all
R
, and
\lim_{R\to\infty}\operatorname{diam}E_{R}=\lim_{R\to\infty}\operatorname{diam}F_{R}=\infty
.
It follows that

(84)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(E;F)

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{R\to\infty}\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(E_{R};F_{R})

(85)

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{R\to\infty}\psi_{Q}\left(\frac{\operatorname{dist}(E_{R},F_{R})}{\min\{\operatorname{diam}E_{R},\operatorname{diam}F_{R}\}}\right)=\infty.

We infer that
\mathscr{P}(x,y)=+\infty
, for all
x,y\in X
, and hence we conclude (
82
).
∎

5.3.
Upper bound to the parabolic Ferrand distance

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric measure space with uniformly locally
Q
-bounded geometry, with
Q>1
.
Suppose that
X
is quasi-convex.
Then, there exists
C>0
such that

(86)

\mathbbmss{p}(x,y)\leq C\max\left\{1,{d(x,y)}\right\},\qquad\forall x,y\in X.

The constant
C
only depends on the geometric data of
X
.

Proof.

To show (
86
) we equivalently show

(87)

\mathscr{P}(x,y)\geq C\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{d(x,y)^{Q}}\right\}.

By Corollary
3.11
, there exist
C_{0},r_{0}>0
depending only on the data of
X
such that, for every
u:X\to\mathbb{R}
monotone with upper gradient
g
we have

(88)

\operatorname{osc}_{B(z,r)}u\leq C_{0}\|g\|_{L^{Q}(X)},\qquad\forall z\in X,\ \forall r\in(0,r_{0}).

Let
x,y\in X
and assume
\mathscr{P}(x,y)<\infty
.
Then there are
\infty
-continua
E,F\subset X
with
x\in E
and
y\in F

such that,
\mathscr{P}(x,y)\geq\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(E;F)/2
.
By Proposition
3.15
, there is
u:X\to[0,1]
with upper gradient
g
such that
E\subset\{u\leq 0\}
,
F\subset\{u\geq 1\}
,
u
is monotone on
X\setminus(E\cup F)
, and
\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(E;F)\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}g^{Q}\,\mathrm{d}\mu
.
In particular,

u
is monotone on
X
by Lemma
3.4
and

(89)

\mathscr{P}(x,y)\geq\frac{1}{4}\|g\|_{L^{Q}(X)}^{Q}.

We separate two cases, depending on the size of
d(x,y)
.
First case:
d(x,y)<r_{0}
.
Then

(90)

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=u(y)-u(x)\overset{\eqref{eq686fb595}}{\leq}C_{0}\|g\|_{L^{Q}(X)}\overset{\eqref{eq686fbccd}}{\leq}C_{0}4^{1/Q}\mathscr{P}(x,y)^{1/Q}.

This shows (
87
) with
C=\frac{1}{4C_{0}^{Q}}
.

Second case:
d(x,y)\geq r_{0}
.
Since
X
is assumed to be
C_{1}
-quasi-convex for some
C_{1}\geq 1
,
there is a Lipschitz
\gamma:[0,1]\to X
from
x
to
y
with length at most
\operatorname{Length}(\gamma)\leq C_{1}d(x,y)
.
Let
n=\lceil\frac{\operatorname{Length}(\gamma)}{r_{0}}\rceil\geq 1
, so that

(91)

n\leq\frac{\operatorname{Length}(\gamma)}{r_{0}}+1\leq\frac{C_{1}d(x,y)}{r_{0}}+\frac{d(x,y)}{r_{0}}=d(x,y)\frac{C_{1}+1}{r_{0}},

and let
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{n}=1
such that, for each
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,
we have
d(\gamma(t_{j-1}),\gamma(t_{j}))\leq\operatorname{Length}(\gamma|_{[t_{j-1},t_{j}]})<r_{0}
.
Then

(92)

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=u(\gamma(1))-u(\gamma(0))\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}|u(\gamma(t_{j-1}))-u(\gamma(t_{j}))|\overset{\eqref{eq686fb595}}{\leq}nC_{0}\|g\|_{L^{Q}(X)}

(93)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq68737758}}{\leq}d(x,y)C_{0}\frac{C_{1}+1}{r_{0}}\|g\|_{L^{Q}(X)}\overset{\eqref{eq686fbccd}}{\leq}d(x,y)C_{0}\frac{C_{1}+1}{r_{0}}4^{1/Q}\mathscr{P}(x,y)^{1/Q}.

We conclude (
87
) with
C=\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{r_{0}}{C_{0}(C_{1}+1)}\right)^{Q}
, which only depends on the geometric data of
X
.

Remark 5.9
.

for all

, and
n\leq C_{2}d(x,y)
,
where
r_{0}
is the radius given by Corollary
3.11
.
In our case, we had
C_{2}=\frac{C_{1}+1}{r_{0}}
; see (

).
So, we need

to be
(\ell,r)
-quasi-geodesic for some

and for some

.

5.4.
Quasi-straight sequences

In order to give a lower bound to the Ferrand distance
\mathbbmss{p}(x,y)
of two points
x
and
y
, we need to find a “good” pair of
\infty
-continua
E
and
F
containing
x
and
y
, respectively; see Proposition
5.16
.
We will construct these continua from bi-infinite sequences of points that are quasi-aligned to straight lines, and that pass close to
x
and
y
.
We call such sequences
quasi-straight sequences
; see Definition
5.10
.
We call
quasi-straightenable
a space where every pair of points lies close to a quasi-straight sequence, quantitatively and uniformly; see Definition
5.13
.
The advantage of quasi-straight sequences is that their existence is (quantitatively) preserved by quasi-isometries.
For this reason, we can prove their existence in geodesic Lie groups with polynomial growth; see Proposition
8.4
.

Definition 5.10
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space.
A (bi-infinite) sequence
\zeta:\mathbb{Z}\to X
is a
K
-quasi-straight sequence
, with
K\geq 0
,
if both
\zeta(\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0})
and
\zeta(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})
are unbounded sets in
X
,

(94)

d(\zeta_{k},\zeta_{k+1})\leq 1+K,\qquad\forall k\in\mathbb{Z},

and

(95)

\begin{gathered}d(\zeta_{i},\zeta_{j})+d(\zeta_{j},\zeta_{k})-d(\zeta_{i},\zeta_{k})\leq Kd(\zeta_{i},\zeta_{k})+K,\\
\qquad\hskip 142.26378pt\forall i,j,k\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ with }i\leq j\leq k.\end{gathered}

Notice that (
95
) is equivalent to

(
95
’)

\begin{gathered}d(\zeta_{i},\zeta_{j})+d(\zeta_{j},\zeta_{k})\leq(1+K)d(\zeta_{i},\zeta_{k})+K,\\
\qquad\hskip 142.26378pt\forall i,j,k\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ with }i\leq j\leq k,\end{gathered}

which we will use often.

Remark 5.11
.

If a function
\zeta:\mathbb{Z}\to X
is a quasi-isometry, where
\mathbb{Z}
is endowed with the Euclidean distance, then
\zeta
is a quasi-geodesic and a quasi-straight sequence.
However, there are quasi-straight sequences that do not arise from quasi-geodesics.
For example, consider
\mathbb{Z}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}
where
\mathbb{R}
is endowed with the snowflaked Euclidean distance:
d(x,y):=\sqrt{|x-y|}
, for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
.
One can easily check that
\mathbb{Z}
is a quasi-straight sequence in
(\mathbb{R},d)
.
Such example appears crucially in the first Heisenberg group.
See Lemma
8.5
for a generalization of this example.

Lemma 5.12
.

Quasi-isometries preserve quasi-straight sequences, quantitatively.

Proof.

Let
(X,d)
and
(Y,d)
be metric spaces,
f:X\to Y
a
(L,C)
-quasi-isometry
and
\zeta:\mathbb{Z}\to X
a
K
-straight sequence, for some
L,C,K\geq 0
.

First, for every
m\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

(96)

d(f(\zeta_{m}),f(\zeta_{0}))\overset{\eqref{eq68ce76a1}}{\geq}\frac{1}{L}d(\zeta_{m},\zeta_{0})-C.

Since
\limsup_{m\to+\infty}d(\zeta_{m},\zeta_{0})=+\infty

and
\limsup_{m\to-\infty}d(\zeta_{m},\zeta_{0})=+\infty
,
then
f\circ\zeta(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})
and
f\circ\zeta(\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0})
are unbounded.

Second, for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
we estimate

(97)

\displaystyle d(f(\zeta_{k}),f(\zeta_{k+1}))\overset{\eqref{eq68ce76a1}}{\leq}Ld(\zeta_{k},\zeta_{k+1})+C\overset{\eqref{eq6870e51e}}{\leq}L(1+K)+C.

Third, if
i,j,k\in\mathbb{Z}
with
i\leq j\leq k
, then

(98)

\displaystyle d(f(\zeta_{i}),f(\zeta_{j}))+d(f(\zeta_{j}),f(\zeta_{k}))

(99)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq68ce76a1}}{\leq}Ld(\zeta_{i},\zeta_{j})+Ld(\zeta_{j},\zeta_{k})+2C

(100)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq68ce7545}}{\leq}L(K+1)d(\zeta_{i},\zeta_{k})+LK+2C

(101)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq68ce76a1}}{\leq}L(K+1)(Ld(f(\zeta_{i}),f(\zeta_{k}))+LC)+LK+2C

(102)

\displaystyle=L^{2}(K+1)d(f(\zeta_{i}),f(\zeta_{k}))+L^{2}C(K+1)+LK+2C.

We conclude that
f\circ\zeta:\mathbb{Z}\to Y
is a
K^{\prime}
-quasi-straight sequence with

(103)

K^{\prime}=\max\{L(1+K)+C,L^{2}(K+1)-1,L^{2}C(K+1)+LK+2C\}.\qed

Definition 5.13
.

A metric space
(X,d)
is
K
-quasi-straightenable
with
K\geq 0
if, for every
x,y\in X
, there exists an
K
-quasi-straight sequence
\zeta:\mathbb{Z}\to X
with
x,y\in B(\zeta(\mathbb{Z}),K)
.
We say that
X
is
quasi-straightenable
if it is
K
-quasi-straightenable for some
K\geq 0
.

Lemma 5.14
.

Quasi-straightenability is preserved under quasi-isometry, quantitatively.

Proof.

Let
(X,d)
and
(Y,d)
be metric spaces and
f:X\to Y
a
(L,C)
-quasi-isometry.
Suppose that
X
is
K
-quasi-straightenable and let
y_{1},y_{2}\in Y
.
Then there are
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
such that
d(f(x_{j}),y_{j})\leq C
for both

.
Since
X
is
K
-quasi-straightenable, there is a
K
-quasi-straight sequence
\zeta:\mathbb{Z}\to X
, and

with

for both

.

, the function

.
Notice that, for both

(105)

Since

,
then

and

We conclude that

is

-quasi-straightenable with

5.5.

We use quasi-straight sequences to give a lower bound to the parabolic Ferrand distance
\mathbbmss{p}
. The key property is an upper bound in the capacities of some distinguished sets; see Proposition
5.16
.
For the proof, we will need the following lemma.

Lemma 5.15
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and
\zeta:\mathbb{Z}\to X
a
K
-quasi-straight sequence, with
K\geq 0
.
Let
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
with
m\leq n

Then, for every

with

and

Proof.

Let

be integers.
Then

(108)

\displaystyle d(\zeta_{m},\zeta_{n})

\displaystyle\leq d(\zeta_{i},\zeta_{m})+d(\zeta_{m},\zeta_{n})+d(\zeta_{n},\zeta_{j})

(109)

\displaystyle[\text{by\penalty\ \eqref{eq68ce7545} and $K\geq 0$}]

\displaystyle\leq(K+1)d(\zeta_{i},\zeta_{n})+K+(K+1)d(\zeta_{n},\zeta_{j})

(110)

\displaystyle[\text{by\penalty\ \eqref{eq68ce7545}}]

\displaystyle\leq(K+1)((K+1)d(\zeta_{i},\zeta_{j})+K)+K

\displaystyle=(K+1)^{2}d(\zeta_{i},\zeta_{j})+K(K+2).

Rearranging, we get (

).
∎

Proposition 5.16

and

By Lemma

, we have

\frac{1}{(1+K)^{2}}(\bar{R}-2K)-\frac{K(K+2)}{(1+K)^{2}}>2K.

,
with

, an upper gradient of

We claim that, for every

and

Next, we claim that, for every

(118)

d_{E}(z)+d_{F}(z)\geq\frac{d(x,y)}{K+1}-\left(2K+\frac{4K}{K+1}\right)-2d(o,z).

Indeed, if

,

and

\displaystyle d(z,x^{\prime})+d(z,y^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq d(\zeta_{i},o)-d(x^{\prime},\zeta_{i})-d(o,z)+d(\zeta_{j},o)-d(y^{\prime},\zeta_{j})-d(o,z)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq6870e535}}{\geq}\frac{d(\zeta_{i},\zeta_{m})+d(\zeta_{m},o)}{K+1}-\frac{K}{K+1}+\frac{d(\zeta_{j},\zeta_{n})+d(\zeta_{n},o)}{K+1}-\frac{K}{K+1}-2d(o,z)-2K

From (

(119)

R_{xy}:=\frac{d(x,y)}{4(K+1)}-\frac{1}{2}\left(2K+\frac{4K}{K+1}\right),

\forall z\in B(o,R_{xy}),\qquad d_{E}(z)+d_{F}(z)\geq\frac{d(x,y)}{2(K+1)}.

\displaystyle\int_{X}g(z)^{Q}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(z)

Lemma 5.17
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space.
For
K\subset X
and
x\in X
, define
d_{K}(x):=\operatorname{dist}(x,K)=\inf\{d(x,k):k\in K\}
.
Let
E,F\subset X
be such that
\operatorname{cl}(E)\cap\operatorname{cl}(F)=\emptyset
.
Define
u:X\to[0,1]
by

(137)

u(x):=\frac{d_{E}(x)}{d_{E}(x)+d_{F}(x)},\qquad\forall x\in X.

Then
u
is locally Lipschitz, and in particular, for every
x,y\in X

(138)

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq d(x,y)\max\left\{\frac{1}{d_{E}(y)+d_{F}(y)},\frac{1}{d_{E}(x)+d_{F}(x)}\right\}.

Moreover, for every
x\in X
,

u(x)=0
if and only if
x\in\operatorname{cl}(E)
,
and
u(x)=1
if and only if
x\in\operatorname{cl}(F)
.
Finally, the function
g(x):=\frac{1}{d_{E}(x)+d_{F}(x)}

is an upper gradient of
u
.

Proof.

For every
x,y\in X
, we have

(139)

\displaystyle u(x)-u(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{E}(x)}{d_{E}(x)+d_{F}(x)}-\frac{d_{E}(y)}{d_{E}(y)+d_{F}(y)}

(140)

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{E}(x)(d_{E}(y)+d_{F}(y))-d_{E}(y)(d_{E}(x)+d_{F}(x))}{(d_{E}(x)+d_{F}(x))(d_{E}(y)+d_{F}(y))}

(141)

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{E}(x)d_{F}(y)-d_{E}(y)d_{F}(x)}{(d_{E}(x)+d_{F}(x))(d_{E}(y)+d_{F}(y))}

(142)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{d_{E}(x)(d_{F}(x)+d(x,y))-(d_{E}(x)-d(x,y))d_{F}(x)}{(d_{E}(x)+d_{F}(x))(d_{E}(y)+d_{F}(y))}

(143)

\displaystyle=d(x,y)\frac{d_{E}(x)+d_{F}(x)}{(d_{E}(x)+d_{F}(x))(d_{E}(y)+d_{F}(y))}

\displaystyle=d(x,y)\frac{1}{d_{E}(y)+d_{F}(y)}.

This shows (

).

If

,

and

, then
d_{E}(x)+d_{F}(x)\geq d_{E}(z)+d_{F}(z)-2r\geq\max\{d_{E}(z),d_{F}(z)\}-2r
.
Since
\operatorname{cl}(E)\cap\operatorname{cl}(F)=\emptyset
, then
\max\{d_{E}(z),d_{F}(z)\}>0
.
Therefore, there are

and

such that

for all

.
From (

), we conclude that

is

Lipschitz on

.

The last statements are easy.
In particular, recall that the pointwise Lipschitz constant is an upper gradient; see
[
11
, Proposition 1.11]
.

5.6.

.

The first inequality in (

The second inequality is shown as follows.

6.
Isoperimetric inequalities at large scale

6.1.
Perimeter and coarea inequality

For generalizing the theory of BV functions and of sets of finite perimeter,
in
[
36
,
2
]
,
Miranda Jr., Ambrosio, and Di Marino
developed a notion of
total variation for locally integrable functions
in the setting of metric measure spaces.
We refer to those two papers for a complete introduction.
If
X
is a metric measure space, we denote by

\|\mathrm{D}f\|(U)

the
total variation
of
f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(X;\mathbb{R})
on
U\subset X
.
Of the total variation, we only recall the following facts:
The map
U\mapsto\|\mathrm{D}f\|(U)
is a Borel outer measure on
X

and, if
g
is an upper gradient of
f
then, for every open set
U\subset X
,

(148)

\|\mathrm{D}f\|(U)\leq\int_{U}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x).

The
perimeter measure
of a set
E
is the total variation of the characteristic function
{\mathds{1}\!}_{E}
of
E
, that is, the measure
\operatorname{Per}(E;\cdot):=\|\mathrm{D}{\mathds{1}\!}_{E}\|
.
We will denote by
\operatorname{Per}(E)
the
perimeter
of a measurable set
E
, i.e.,

\operatorname{Per}(E):=\operatorname{Per}(E;X)=\|\mathrm{D}{\mathds{1}\!}_{E}\|(X)
.

The following coarea formula is proven by Miranda Jr. in
[
36
, Proposition 4.2]
for spaces of locally bounded geometry, and extended to general metric measure spaces by Ambrosio and Di Marino in
[
2
]
(see the end of the introduction there).

Theorem 6.1

(Coarea formula,
[
36
, Proposition 4.2]
)
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric measure space and
f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(X;\mathbb{R})
.
For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, define
E_{t}:=\{x\in X:f(x)>t\}
.
Then, for every
U\subset X
open,

(149)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{Per}(E_{t};U)\,\mathrm{d}t=\|\mathrm{D}f\|(U).

In particular, by (
148
), if
g:X\to[0,+\infty]
is an upper gradient of
f
, then, for every
U\subset X
open,

(150)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{Per}(E_{t};U)\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{U}g\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

In
[

, Theorem 4.5]
,
Miranda Jr. proved also the following local isoperimetric inequality.

Theorem 6.2

(Local isoperimetric inequality)

Let

-bounded geometry with data

Let

6.2.
Isoperimetric inequality at large scale

Definition 6.4
.

We say that a metric measure space
(X,d,\mu)
supports
an isoperimetric inequality at large scale of order
\alpha\in[0,1]
if there exist
V_{\alpha},C_{\alpha}\in(0,+\infty)
such that,
for every
E\subset X
measurable,

(153)

\mu(E)\geq V_{\alpha}\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad\mu(E)^{\alpha}\leq C_{\alpha}\operatorname{Per}(E).

The
large-scale isoperimetric dimension
of
(X,d,\mu)
is

(154)

\dim_{\rm isp}(X):=\sup\left\{N\in[1,\infty):\begin{array}[]{c}\text{$X$ supports a large-scale }\\
\text{isoperimetric inequality of order $\frac{N-1}{N}$ }\end{array}\right\}.

Remark 6.5
.

Notice that, if
X
supports a large-scale isoperimetric inequality of order
\alpha\in[0,1]
, then
X
supports a large-scale isoperimetric inequality of order
\beta
for every
\beta\in[0,\alpha]
.
Indeed, taking
V_{\beta}:=\max\{V_{\alpha},1\}
, we obtain that,
if
\mu(E)\geq V_{\beta}
, then

\mu(E)^{\beta}\leq\mu(E)^{\alpha}\leq C_{\alpha}\operatorname{Per}(E)
.
For this reason, for every
N<\dim_{\rm isp}(X)
, the space
X
supports a large-scale isoperimetric inequality of order
\frac{N-1}{N}
.

Kanai showed in
[
32
]
that every quasi-isometry between two Riemannian manifolds with certain curvature bounds, preserve the order of isoperimetric inequalities at large scale.
A careful adaptation of Kanai’s proof leads to the following generalization to metric measure spaces with uniformly locally bounded geometry.
In fact,
the key properties used in Kanai’s setting
are uniform bounds from above and below for the volume of small balls and a local isoperimetric inequality.

Theorem 6.6

(after Kanai
[
32
]
)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two quasi-geodesic metric measure spaces with uniformly locally bounded geometry, with Hausdorff dimension equal to or greater than 1.
Assume
X
and
Y
are quasi-isometric and let
\alpha\in[0,1]
.
Then,
X
supports an isoperimetric inequality at large scale of order
\alpha
if and only if does
Y
.

We shall prove Theorem
6.6
in Section

Kanai’s Theorem

allows us to obtain Theorem

, extending the result
[

, Theorem 2.1]
by Pittet from Riemannian Lie groups to geodesic Lie groups; see Theorem
8.2
.

Kanai proves Theorem

Firstly, they show a similar statement for graphs.
Secondly, they show that the isoperimetric inequality at large scale on a “good” Riemannian manifold is equivalent to a similar inequality on an approximating graph.
We will follow the same strategy.

The first step goes as follows.
A graph is a pair

(192)

\mu^{+}(b):=\sup\{\mu(B(x,b)):x\in X\}.

Then, for every
x\in X
there exists

such that

(193)

\operatorname{Per}(B(x,r))\leq\frac{\mu(B(x,b)\setminus B(x,a))}{b-a}.

Proof.

Let
x\in X
and set
d_{x}(y)=d(x,y)
.

(194)

\displaystyle\mu^{+}(b)\geq\mu(B(x,b)\setminus B(x,a))\overset{\eqref{eq68559ec1}}{\geq}\int_{a}^{b}\operatorname{Per}(B(x,t))\,\mathrm{d}t.

It follows that there is

6.4.
The isoperimetric inequality at large implies the Sobolev inequality

We will show that an isoperimetric inequality at large scale, as in (
195
), implies a Gagliardo–Nirenberg–Sobolev type inequality, as in (
196
).
Proposition
6.12
is inspired by
[
17
, Proposition 4.5.3]

and
[
39
, Proof of Theorem 1.3 and Proposition 3.1]
.

Proposition 6.12
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric measure space.
Suppose that
X
supports a large scale isoperimetric inequality of order
\frac{N-1}{N}
for some
N>1
,
that is, that there are
C_{\rm I},V_{\rm I}
such that, for all
E\subset X
measurable,

(195)

\text{if $\mu(E)\geq V_{\rm I}$, then }\mu(E)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\leq C_{\rm I}\cdot\operatorname{Per}(E).

Then, for every
q\in[1,N)
there exist constants
C_{q}
and
C_{q,\infty}
such that,
for every
u:X\to\mathbb{R}
locally integrable with upper gradient
g:X\to[0,+\infty]
,

(196)

\left(\int_{X}|u|^{\frac{Nq}{N-q}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{\frac{N-q}{Nq}}\leq C_{q}\left(\int_{X}g^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}+C_{q,\infty}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}.

Proof.

Before diving into the proof, recall the following formula from real analysis, see
[
25
, (3.34)]
:
If
\delta\in(0,1)
and
F:(0,+\infty)\to(0,+\infty)
is non-decreasing, then

(197)

\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{\frac{1}{\delta}-1}F(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\delta\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}F(s)^{\delta}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{1/\delta}.

Let
u:X\to\mathbb{R}
be locally integrable with upper gradient
g:X\to[0,+\infty]
.
We will consider the following condition:

(198)

\text{for all $t\geq 0$, either $\mu(\{|u|>t\})\geq V_{\rm I}$ or $\mu(\{|u|>t\})=0$}.

We claim that, if
u
satisfies the condition (
198
), then

(199)

\left(\int_{X}|u|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\leq C_{\rm I}\int_{X}g\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

Indeed, setting
\alpha=\frac{N}{N-1}
, we have

\displaystyle\int|u|^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle[\text{Cavalieri}]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(\{|u|^{\alpha}>t\})\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle[s=t^{1/\alpha},\ s^{\alpha}=t,\ \alpha s^{\alpha-1}\,\mathrm{d}s=\,\mathrm{d}t]

\displaystyle=\alpha\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(\{|u|>s\})s^{\alpha-1}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle[\text{by\penalty\ \eqref{eq67dbf7e6} with $\delta=1/\alpha$}]

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(\{|u|>s\})^{1/\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle[\text{since $\alpha=\frac{N}{N-1}$}]

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(\{|u|>s\})^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{N}{N-1}}

\displaystyle[\text{\eqref{eq67dc177c} and isop. ineq.\penalty\ \eqref{eq67dbf890}}]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}C_{\rm I}\cdot\operatorname{Per}(\{|u|>s\})\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{N}{N-1}}

\displaystyle[\text{coarea inequality\penalty\ \eqref{eq68559ec1}}]

\displaystyle\leq C_{\rm I}^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\left(\int_{X}g\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{\frac{N}{N-1}}.

We thus have obtained (
199
) under the condition (
198
).

Next, we claim that, if
u
satisfies the condition (
198
) and
q\in[1,N)
, then

(200)

\left(\int_{X}|u|^{\frac{Nq}{N-q}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{\frac{N-q}{Nq}}\leq C_{\rm I}\frac{(N-1)q}{N-q}\left(\int_{X}g^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Indeed, we first notice that also
|u|^{p}
satisfies the condition (
198
), for every
p>0
.
Second, if
p\geq 1
then
p|u|^{p-1}g
is an upper gradient of
|u|^{p}
.
Therefore, applying (
199
) to
|u|^{p}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\int_{X}|u^{p}|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq68558ec5}}{\leq}C_{\rm I}\int_{X}p|u|^{p-1}g\,\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle[\text{H\"{o}lder inequality}]

\displaystyle\leq C_{\rm I}p\left(\int_{X}|u|^{(p-1)q^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{X}g^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where
q^{\prime}=\frac{q}{q-1}
is the Hölder exponent conjugate to
q
.
Since we want
(p-1)q^{\prime}=p\frac{N}{N-1}
, we obtain

p=\frac{N-1}{N-q}q
.
Then we have
p\frac{N}{N-1}=\frac{Nq}{N-q}
.
Assuming
u\neq 0
, then obtain on the left-hand side the integral of
|u|^{\frac{Nq}{N-q}}
exponentiated to
\frac{N-1}{N}-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=\frac{N-q}{Nq}
.
This concludes the proof of (
200
) under the condition (
198
).

Finally, suppose that
u:X\to\mathbb{R}
is a locally integrable function that may not satisfy (
198
).
Let
\xi\geq 0
be such that
\mu(\{|u|>t\})\geq V_{\rm I}
for
t\in(0,\xi)
and
\mu(\{|u|>t\})\leq V_{\rm I}
for
t\geq\xi
.
Then
|u|=u_{1}+u_{2}
, where
u_{1}=\min\{|u|,\xi\}
and
u_{2}=|u|-u_{1}
.
We have that
u_{1}
satisfies the condition (
198
):
indeed, if
t\in(0,\xi)
, then
\mu(\{|u_{1}|>t\})=\mu(\{|u|>t\})\geq V_{\rm I}
;
if
t>\xi
, then
\mu(\{|u_{1}|>t\})=0
.
Notice also that
g
is an upper gradient of
u_{1}
,
and that
u_{2}
is supported on the set
\{|u|>\xi\}
with
\mu(\{|u|>\xi\})\leq V_{\rm I}
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{X}|u|^{\frac{Nq}{N-q}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{\frac{N-q}{Nq}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{X}|u_{1}|^{\frac{Nq}{N-q}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{\frac{N-q}{Nq}}+\left(\int_{X}|u_{2}|^{\frac{Nq}{N-q}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{\frac{N-q}{Nq}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq685599b5}}{\leq}C_{\rm I}\frac{(N-1)q}{N-q}\left(\int_{X}g^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}+\left(\int_{\{|u|>\xi\}}|u_{2}|^{\frac{Nq}{N-q}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{\frac{N-q}{Nq}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\rm I}\frac{(N-1)q}{N-q}\left(\int_{X}g^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}+V_{\rm I}^{\frac{N-q}{Nq}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}.

So, we have (
196
) with
C_{q}=C_{\rm I}\frac{(N-1)q}{N-q}
and
C_{q,\infty}=V_{\rm I}^{\frac{N-q}{Nq}}
.
∎

The hyperbolic case

7.1.
The hyperbolic Ferrand distance

Definition 7.1
.

Let
X
be a metric measure space with Hausdorff dimension
Q
.
The
hyperbolic Ferrand (pseudo)distance
on
X
is the function
\mathbbmss{h}:X\times X\to[0,+\infty]
,

(202)

\mathbbmss{h}(x,y):=\inf\left\{\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(K):K\text{ continuum with }x,y\in K\right\},\qquad\forall x,y\in X.

Here
\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}
denotes the
Q
-capacity as in Definition
2.4
.

The map
\mathbbmss{h}
is a pseudo-distance, that is, it satisfies the triangle inequality.
Indeed, if
x,y,z\in X
and
K_{xy}
,
K_{yz}
are continua with
x,y\in K_{xy}
and
y,z\in K_{yz}
, then
K_{xy}\cup K_{yz}
is a continuum containing
x
and
z
, and
\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(K_{xy}\cup K_{yz})\leq\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(K_{xy})+\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(K_{yz})
,
because capacity is sub-additive; see Remark
2.5
.
It follows that
\mathbbmss{h}(x,z)\leq\mathbbmss{h}(x,y)+\mathbbmss{h}(y,z)
.

The Ferrand pseudo-distance
\mathbbmss{h}
is not a distance when there is a pair
a,b\in X
with
\mathbbmss{h}(a,b)=0
and
a\neq b
.
The following result shows that, under certain hypothesis on the space
X
, there are only two cases: either
\mathbbmss{h}
is a distance, i.e.,
\mathbbmss{h}(a,b)>0
whenever
a\neq b
,
or
\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(K)=0
for every continuum
K\subset X
, i.e.,
X
is parabolic.

Theorem 7.2

(
[
18
, Theorem 6.8]
)
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a path-connected metric measure space with uniformly locally
Q
-bounded geometry.
If there are
a,b\in X
distinct such that
\mathbbmss{h}(a,b)=0
, then
\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(K)=0
for every compact connected subset
K
of
X
.

Proof.

\mathbbmss{h}(a,b)=0
means that, for every
\epsilon>0
there exists a continuum
K_{\epsilon}\subset X
with
a,b\in K_{\epsilon}
, and a function
u_{\epsilon}:X\to[0,1]
continuous with compact support,
K_{\epsilon}\subset\{u_{\epsilon}\geq 1\}
, and

\int_{X}g_{\epsilon}^{Q}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\epsilon
, where
g_{\epsilon}
is an upper gradient of
u_{\epsilon}
.
By Proposition
3.15
and Lemma
3.5
, we can assume that
u_{\epsilon}
is monotone in
X\setminus\{x\}
for every
x\in K_{\epsilon}
.

Let
K\subset X
be a nonempty continuum.

Suppose that
a\notin K
but
b\in K
.
Since
a\in K_{\epsilon}
,
u_{\epsilon}
is monotone in
X\setminus\{a\}
.
By Corollary
3.11
, there are
C,R\in\mathbb{R}
such that, whenever
r\in(0,R]
and
x\in X
are such that
B(x,Cr)\subset X\setminus\{a\}
, we have

\operatorname{osc}_{B(x,r)}u_{\epsilon}\leq C\epsilon^{1/Q}
, for every
\epsilon>0
.
Take
r\in(0,R)
such that
Cr<{\rm dist}(a,K)
, and cover
K
with a finite number, say
n
, of balls of radius
r
centered in
K
.
Then,
\operatorname{osc}_{K}u_{\epsilon}\leq nC\epsilon^{1/Q}
.
Since
u_{\epsilon}(b)=1
and
b\in K
, then

u_{\epsilon}(x)\geq 1-nC\epsilon^{1/Q}
for all
x\in K
.
Take
\epsilon
small enough so that
1-nC\epsilon^{1/Q}>1/2
.
Then the function
v(x)=2u_{\epsilon}(x)
is continuous with compact support and with
K\subset\{v\geq 1\}
.
Moreover, an upper-gradient for
v
is
2g_{\epsilon}
.
So,

\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(K)\leq\int_{X}(2g_{\epsilon})^{Q}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq 2^{Q}\epsilon
.
Since
\epsilon
is arbitrarily small, we conclude that
\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(K)=0
.

Suppose that
a,b\notin K
.
Let
\gamma:[0,1]\to X
be a path with
\gamma(0)\in K
and
\gamma(1)=b
.
Let
\bar{t}=\inf\{t\in[0,1]:\gamma(t)\in\{a,b\}\}
.
Then,
\bar{t}>0
,
\gamma(\bar{t})\in\{a,b\}
, and
\gamma(t)\notin\{a,b\}
for all
t<\bar{t}
.
Without loss of generality,
\gamma(\bar{t})=b
and
a\notin\gamma([0,\bar{t}])
.
Then
K\cup\gamma([0,\bar{t}])
is a continuum containing
b
but not
a
, and thus, by the previous argument and the monotonicity of capacity,

\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(K)\leq\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(K\cup\gamma([0,\bar{t}]))=0
.

If
a,b\in K
, we can take
x\in X\setminus K
and, as we did above, a path
\gamma\subset X\setminus\{a\}
from
x
to
b
.
By the previous paragraph,
\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(\gamma)=0
and thus
\mathbbmss{h}(x,b)=0
.
Applying the previous paragraph switching
a
with
x
, we conclude
\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(K)=0
.
∎

Corollary 7.3
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a path-connected metric measure space with uniformly locally
Q
-bounded geometry.
If there exists a continuum
K\subset X
such that
\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(K)\neq 0
,
then
\mathbbmss{h}(a,b)=0
if and only if

.
In particular, the pseudodistance
\mathbbmss{h}
is a distance.

On spaces with locally bounded
Q
-geometry, the
Q
-capacity is bi-Lipschitz preserved by quasi-conformal maps, by the geometric Definition
2.6
.
For this reason, the
Q
-Ferrand distance plays a role in the study of quasi-conformal maps.

.

Let
X,Y
be metric measure spaces with uniformly locally
Q
-bounded geometry,
and
f:X\to Y
a quasi-conformal map.
Then there is
L
such that

(203)

\frac{1}{L}\mathbbmss{h}(p,q)\leq\mathbbmss{h}(f(p),f(q))\leq L\mathbbmss{h}(p,q).

7.2.
Upper bound to the hyperbolic Ferrand distance

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a boundedly compact metric measure space with Hausdorff dimension
Q
.
Suppose that there is
\rho:(0,+\infty)\to(0,+\infty)
such that for every
x,y\in X
there is a continuum joining
x
and
y
in
B(x,\rho(d(x,y)))
.
Define
\mu^{+}:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty]
by

(204)

\mu^{+}(r):=\sup\{\mu(B(x,r)):x\in X\},\qquad\text{ for }r\in[0,+\infty).

Then, we have

(205)

\mathbbmss{h}(x,y)\leq\mu^{+}(\rho(d(x,y))+1),\qquad\forall x,y\in X.

In particular, if
\mu^{+}(r)<\infty
for every
r
, then whenever
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbbmss{h}(x_{n},y_{n})=\infty
, we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}d(x_{n},y_{n})=\infty
.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in X
and
r=d(x,y)
.
Since
x,y
are joined by a continuum in
B(x,\rho(r))
, then

(206)

\mathbbmss{h}(x,y)\leq\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(B(x,\rho(r))).

Consider the function
u(z)=\max\{\rho(r)+1-d(x,z),0\}
.
Then
B(x,\rho(r))\subset\{u\geq 1\}
and
\operatorname{spt}(u)\subset B(x,\rho(r)+2)
.
Since
X
is boundedly compact,
u
has compact support and thus it is admissible for
B(x,\rho(r))
.
An upper gradient of
u
is
g={\mathds{1}\!}_{B(x,\rho(r)+1)}
.
Therefore,

(207)

\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(B(x,\rho(r))\leq\mu(B(x,\rho(r)+1))\leq\mu^{+}(\rho(d(x,y))+1).

∎

7.3.
Lower bound to the hyperbolic Ferrand distance

Theorem 7.6
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a boundedly compact metric measure space with uniformly locally
Q
-bounded geometry,
Q>1
.
Assume that
X
is of hyperbolic conformal type
and that there exist
p\geq 1
,
C_{Q}>0
and
C_{\infty}\geq 0
such that the following Gagliardo–Nirenberg–-Sobolev type inequality,

(208)

\left(\int_{X}|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{1/p}\leq C_{Q}\left(\int_{X}g^{Q}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{Q}}+C_{\infty}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(X)},

holds for every
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(X)
with upper gradient
g
.

Then, there is
C_{\rm F}\geq 1
such that
for every
a,b\in X
with
d(a,b)>C_{\rm F}
,

(209)

\mathbbmss{h}(a,b)\geq\frac{1}{C_{\rm F}}d(a,b)^{\frac{1}{pQ}},

where
\mathbbmss{h}
denotes the hyperbolic Ferrand distance.

Proof.

Let
R
and
C
be as in Corollary
3.11
, and fix
r\in(0,R/C)
. Fix
a,b\in X
with
d(a,b)>4CR
.

Since
X
is hyperbolic, then
\mathbbmss{h}(a,b)>0
by Corollary
7.3
.
Let
K\subset X
be a continuum with
a,b\in K
and
\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(K)\leq 2^{Q}\mathbbmss{h}(a,b)
.
Let
u:X\to[0,1]
be an admissible function for
K
, monotone on
X\setminus\{z\}
for every
z\in K
,
and
g:X\to[0,+\infty]
an upper-gradient of
u
,
such that
\int_{X}g^{Q}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq 2^{Q}\operatorname{Cap}_{Q}(K)
.

Let
\{x_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}\subset K
be a
(2Cr)
-separated set
with

(210)

\frac{d(a,b)}{3}\leq NCr.

Notice that, since
d(a,b)>4C_{1}r
, for each
j
we have
K\setminus B(x_{j},C_{1}r)\neq\emptyset
, and thus,
u
is monotone on
B(x_{j},C_{1}r)
.

CASE 1:
\int_{X}g^{Q}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\geq\frac{N}{2}\frac{1}{(2C)^{Q}}
.
Then

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}(a,b)

\displaystyle\geq 4^{-Q}\int_{X}g^{Q}\,\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle\geq 4^{-Q}\frac{N}{2}\frac{1}{(2C)^{Q}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq67f6777f}}{\geq}4^{-Q}\frac{d(a,b)}{6Cr}\frac{1}{(2C)^{Q}}.

Since
pQ>1
, this lower bound implies (
209
) for
d(a,b)\geq 1
.

CASE 2:
\int_{X}g^{Q}\,\mathrm{d}\mu<\frac{N}{2}\frac{1}{(2C)^{Q}}
.
Then there are
N/2
points in
\{x_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
such that
\int_{B(x_{j},Cr)}g^{Q}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq\frac{1}{(2C)^{Q}}
.
Then, if
x_{j}
is one of such points, for every
y\in B(x_{j},r)
we have

(211)

\displaystyle u(y)

\displaystyle\geq 1-\operatorname{osc}_{B(x_{j},r)}u

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq67efd746}}{\geq}1-C\left(\int_{B(x_{j},Cr)}g^{Q}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{1/Q}

\displaystyle[\text{by the choice of }j]

\displaystyle\geq 1-C\frac{1}{2C}=\frac{1}{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}(a,b)^{1/Q}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\left(\int_{X}g^{Q}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{1/Q}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq67f66fec}}{\geq}\frac{1}{4C_{Q}}\left(\int_{X}u^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{1/p}-\frac{C_{\infty}}{4C_{Q}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}

\displaystyle[\text{balls are disjoint and $|u|\leq 1$}]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4C_{Q}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{B(x_{j},r)}u^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{1/p}-\frac{C_{\infty}}{4C_{Q}}

\displaystyle[\text{by Ahlfors regularity and\penalty\ \eqref{eq67f00c04}}]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4C_{Q}}\left(\frac{N}{2}\frac{1}{C_{\rm A}}r^{Q}\right)^{1/p}\frac{1}{2}-\frac{C_{\infty}}{4C_{Q}}

\displaystyle[\text{by\penalty\ \eqref{eq67f6777f}}]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{8C_{Q}}\left(\frac{r^{Q-1}}{6C_{\rm A}C}\right)^{1/p}d(a,b)^{1/p}-\frac{C_{\infty}}{4C_{Q}}.

This completes the proof of (
209
).
∎

Corollary 7.7
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a boundedly compact metric measure space with uniformly locally
Q
-bounded geometry,
Q>1
.
Suppose that
X
supports a large-scale isoperimetric inequality of order
\frac{N-1}{N}
for some
N>Q
, as in (

).
Suppose also that

Then there is
C_{\rm F}>0
such that, for every

with

(212)

\mathbbmss{h}(a,b)\geq\frac{1}{C_{\rm F}}d(a,b)^{\frac{1}{pQ}},

where

and
\mathbbmss{h}
is the hyperbolic Ferrand distance.

Proof.

We apply Proposition
6.12
to obtain (
196
) for
q=1
, that is (
59
);
thus, Proposition
4.5
implies that
X
has hyperbolic conformal type, because
Q<N
.
We apply again Proposition
6.12
to obtain (
208
) for
q=Q
,
because
Q<N
;
hence, Theorem
7.6
implies (

∎

7.4.
QC implies QI in the hyperbolic case

Theorem 7.8
.

Let
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
be metric measure spaces
with uniformly locally
Q
-bounded geometry with
Q>1
.
For both
j\in\{1,2\}
, suppose that
X_{j}
is boundedly compact, and quasi-convex, and that the function
\mu^{+}_{X_{j}}:r\in(0,+\infty)\mapsto\sup\{\mu(B(x,r)):x\in X\}
is finite valued and
\lim_{r\to\infty}\mu^{+}_{X_{j}}(r)=\infty
.
If
\dim_{\rm isp}(X_{1})>Q
and
\dim_{\rm isp}(X_{2})>Q
,
then every quasi-conformal map
X_{1}\to X_{2}
is a quasi-isometry.

Proof.

Let
\mathbbmss{h}_{j}
be the hyperbolic Ferrand distance on
X_{j}
, for
j\in\{1,2\}
.

First, we claim that the identity map
(X_{j},d_{j})\to(X_{j},\mathbbmss{h}_{j})
is bornologous.
To prove this claim, fix
(X,d,\mathbbmss{h})\in\{(X_{1},d_{1},\mathbbmss{h}_{1}),(X_{2},d_{2},\mathbbmss{h}_{2})\}
.
By assumption, there is
C>0
such that
X
is
C
-quasi-convex.
Therefore, for every
r>0
and for every
x,y\in X
with
x,y\in B(o,r)
,
there is a curve
\gamma
from
x
to
y
with
\operatorname{Length}(\gamma)\leq Cd(x,y)
, and thus,
\gamma\subset B(x,Cd(x,y)+1)
.
We can then apply Lemma
7.5
with
\rho(r):=Cr+1
:
if
\{(x_{n},y_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X\times X
is a sequence with
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbbmss{h}(x_{n},y_{n})=\infty
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}d(x_{n},y_{n})=\infty
.
This shows our claim that the identity map
(X_{j},d_{j})\to(X_{j},\mathbbmss{h}_{j})
is bornologous.

Second, also the hypotheses of Corollary
7.7
are met for both spaces.
We obtain that, for
j\in\{1,2\}
, if
\{(x_{n},y_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X_{j}\times X_{j}
is a sequence with
\lim_{n\to\infty}d(a_{n},b_{n})=\infty
, then
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbbmss{h}(a_{n},b_{n})=\infty
.
We conclude that the identity map
(X_{j},d_{j})\to(X_{j},\mathbbmss{h}_{j})
is bornologous, for both
j\in\{1,2\}
.

Next, since quasi-conformal maps are biLipschitz for the hyperbolic Ferrand distance,
a quasi-conformal map
f:(X_{1},d_{1})\to(X_{2},d_{2})
is composition of bornologous maps

(213)

(X_{1},d_{1})\overset{\mathrm{Id}}{\to}(X_{1},\mathbbmss{h}_{1})\overset{f}{\to}(X_{2},\mathbbmss{h}_{2})\overset{\mathrm{Id}}{\to}(X_{2},d_{2}),

and thus bornologous.
Since the inverse of
f
is also quasi-conformal, it is bornologous too.

Notice that quasi-convex spaces are quasi-geodesic.
Therefore, we conclude that
f
is a quasi-isometry by Corollary
2.13
.
∎

8.
Geodesic Lie groups

8.1.
Geodesic Lie groups

A
geodesic Lie group
is a Lie group endowed with a left-invariant geodesic distance
d
inducing the manifold topology, and it is equipped with a left Haar measure
\mu
.
In fact, every geodesic Lie group is a sub-Finsler Lie group
G
:
it is equipped with a bracket-generating polarization
\Delta
of its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and a norm
\|\cdot\|
on
\Delta
.
The sub-Finsler structure defines the
geodesic distance
(a.k.a.
Carnot-Carathéodory distance
):

d(x,y):=\inf\left\{\int_{0}^{1}\|{\rm d}L_{\gamma(t)}^{-1}\dot{\gamma}(t)\|\,\mathrm{d}t:\begin{array}[]{l}\gamma:[0,1]\to G\text{ absolutely continuous},\\
\gamma(0)=x,\ \gamma(1)=y,\\
{\rm d}L_{\gamma(t)}^{-1}\dot{\gamma}(t)\in\Delta\text{ for a.e.\penalty\ }t\in[0,1]\end{array}\right\},

which is a geodesic distance that induces the manifold topology, as
\Delta
is assumed to be bracket generating; see
[
33
]
for a detailed exposition of sub-Finsler Lie groups.

Recall that a Lie group is
unimodular
if every left-invariant Haar measure is also right invariant.
Lie groups with polynomial growth are unimodular.

Proposition 8.1
.

A geodesic Lie group
(G,d,\mu)
is a boundedly compact metric measure space with uniformly locally
Q
-bounded geometry,
where
Q
is the Hausdorff dimension of
(G,d)
and it equals

(214)

Q=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}k\dim(\Delta^{[k]}/\Delta^{[k-1]}),

with
\Delta^{[0]}:=\{0\}
,
\Delta^{[1]}:=\Delta
, and
\Delta^{[k+1]}:=\Delta^{[k]}+[\Delta,\Delta^{[k]}]
for all
k\geq 1
.

If
G
is unimodular, then a
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality holds at all scales.

Proof.

Since geodesic Lie groups are locally compact geodesic complete metric spaces, they are boundedly compact; see
[
33
, Theorem 7.1.7]
.

It is well known that geodesic Lie groups have locally
Q
-bounded geometry,
with
Q
as in (
214
).
Indeed, the local Ahlfors regularity is proven in
[
38
, Ch 1, Sec 3]
.
Local Poincaré inequalities
(G,d,\mu)
are proven in
[
31
]
, with
[
24
, Theorem 11.12]
, for the sub-Riemannian case; the general case of geodesic Lie groups is deduced from the fact that every geodesic Lie group is bi-Lipschitz equivalent to a sub-Riemannian Lie group.
The uniformity of constants is a consequence of the left-invariance of both the metric
d
and the measure
\mu
.
So, we conclude that geodesic Lie groups have uniformly locally
Q
-bounded geometry.

Another source for a proof of the (1,1)-Poincaré inequality in geodesic Lie groups is
[
46
, p.461]
, which clearly shows that, when
G
is unimodular, then the inequality holds at all scales.
∎

Pittet
[
43
]
characterized the isoperimetric profiles at large scales of Riemannian Lie groups.
Kanai’s Theorem
6.6
allows us to extend the result by Pittet from Riemannian to geodesic Lie groups.
The isoperimetric inequality at large scale implies a Gagliardo–Nirenberg–Sobolev type inequality, as we have shown in Proposition
6.12
.
Sobolev inequalities are one of our main tools to study capacity on geodesic Lie groups,
as we have shown in Proposition
4.5
and Theorem
7.6
.
Sobolev inequalities on nilpotent geodesic Lie groups have been shown also by Varopoulos
[
49
]
, or
[
50
, Theorem IV.7.2]
,
and on Lie groups with polynomial growth by
Alexopoulos and Lohoué in
[
1
]
.

Theorem 8.2

(after Pittet
[
43
, Theorem 2.1]
)
.

Let
(G,d,\mu)
be a non-compact geodesic Lie group.
Then, exactly one of the following cases happens:

(a)

G
is non-amenable or non-unimodular, with exponential volume growth,
and it supports an isoperimetric inequality at large scale of order
\alpha
,
for every
\alpha\in[0,1]
.
In this case,
\dim_{\rm grt}(G)=\infty
.

(b)

G
is amenable, unimodular, with exponential volume growth,
and it supports an isoperimetric inequality at large scale of order
\alpha
,
for every
\alpha\in[0,1)
.
In this case,
\dim_{\rm grt}(G)=\infty
.

(c)

G
is amenable, unimodular, and there is
N\in\mathbb{N}

such that
G
has polynomial growth of degree
N
,
and it supports an isoperimetric inequality at large scale of order
\alpha
,
for every
\alpha\in[0,\frac{N-1}{N}]
.
In this case,
\dim_{\rm grt}(G)=N
.

As a consequence,

(215)

\dim_{\rm isp}(G)=\dim_{\rm par}(G)=\dim_{\rm grt}(G).

Proof.

Let
\rho
be a left-invariant Riemannian metric on
G
.
Pittet’s result
[
43
, Theorem 2.1]

implies that one of the three cases listed in the theorem hold for
(G,\rho,\mu)
.

We claim that the same case holds for
(G,d,\mu)
.
Indeed, since both
\rho
and
d
are left-invariant (quasi-)geodesic distances on
G
, they are quasi-isometric equivalent.
By Proposition
5.2
, the volume growth of
(G,d,\mu)
and
(G,\rho,\mu)
are equivalent, that is, they are either both exponential or both polynomial with the same degree.
Theorem
6.6
tells us that, for every
\alpha\in[0,1]
,
either both
(G,d,\mu)
and
(G,\rho,\mu)
support an isoperimetric inequality at large scale of order
\alpha
, or none.
Our claim is proven.

To deduce (
215
), first recall that, by (
62
) and (
201
), we have

(216)

\dim_{\rm isp}(G)\leq\dim_{\rm par}(G)\leq\dim_{\rm grt}(G).

If
G
falls in one of the first two cases, then
\dim_{\rm isp}(G)=\infty
and thus equalities hold.
If
G
has polynomial growth, then case (c) claims that there is
N
such that
\dim_{\rm grt}(G)\leq N
and
N\leq\dim_{\rm isp}(G)
.
Therefore, we have necessarily
\dim_{\rm grt}(G)=N=\dim_{\rm isp}(G)
and (
215
).
∎

Corollary 8.3
.

Geodesic Lie groups are boundedly compact spaces with uniformly locally bounded geometry.
Moreover, if
G
is a non-compact geodesic Lie group with Hausdorff dimension
Q
and growth dimension
N\in[1,\infty]
,
then one and only one of the following two cases is true:

(I)

Either
N\leq Q
, and
G
has parabolic conformal type;

(II)

Or
Q<N
, and
G
has hyperbolic conformal type.

The identities in (
215
) have been proven for Riemannian manifolds by Coulhon and Saloff-Coste in
[
12
]
.

8.2.
Quasi-straightenable geodesic Lie groups

Proposition 8.4
.

Every geodesic Lie group with polynomial growth is quasi-straightenable
in the sense of Section
5.4
.

Proof.

Every geodesic Lie group with polynomial growth is quasi-isometric to a simply connected nilpotent Riemannian Lie group; see, for instance,
[
13
, Corollary 101]
.
By Lemma
8.5
, simply connected nilpotent Riemannian Lie groups are quasi-straightenable.
By Lemma
5.14
, quasi-straightenability is preserved by quasi-isometries.
This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 8.5
.

Simply connected nilpotent Riemannian Lie groups are quasi-straightenable.

Proof.

We will make use of the fact that

(217)

\alpha^{\theta}+\beta^{\theta}\leq 2(\alpha+\beta)^{\theta},\qquad\forall\theta,\alpha,\beta\in(0,+\infty).

Let
(N,d)
be a simply connected nilpotent Riemannian Lie group, with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}
and exponential map
\exp:\mathfrak{n}\to N
.
Denote by
\|\cdot\|
the Riemannian norm on
\mathfrak{n}
.
By Guivarc’h Theorem, see for instance
[
33
, Theorem 10.4.3]
,
there is a splitting
\mathfrak{n}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{s}V_{j}
of
\mathfrak{n}
into subspaces such that, for every
v_{1},\dots,v_{s}
with
v_{j}\in V_{j}
for all
j
,

(218)

\frac{1}{C}\sum_{m=1}^{s}a_{m}\|v_{m}\|^{1/m}-C\leq d(1_{N},\exp(\sum_{m=1}^{n}v_{m}))\leq C\sum_{m=1}^{s}a_{m}\|v_{m}\|^{1/m}+C,

for some constants
C>0
and
\{a_{j}\}_{j=1}^{s}\subset(0,+\infty)
.
Moreover, up to increasing the constant
C
,
we also assume that, if
v=\sum_{m=1}^{s}v_{m}\in\mathfrak{n}
with
v_{m}\in V_{m}
for each
m\in\{1,\dots,s\}
, then

(219)

\sum_{m=1}^{s}\|v_{m}\|\leq C\|v\|\leq C\sum_{m=1}^{s}\|v_{m}\|.

Set

(220)

K=\max\left\{2C^{2},\,2C^{3}+C,\,\sup\{d(1_{N},\exp(u)):\|u\|\leq 1\}\right\}.

We claim that, for every vector
v\in\mathfrak{n}
with
\|v\|=1
,
the sequence
\zeta:\mathbb{Z}\to N
,
\zeta_{k}:=\exp(kv)
, is an
K
-quasi-straight sequence.
Indeed, write
v=\sum_{m=1}^{s}v_{m}
with
v_{m}\in V_{m}
for each
m\in\{1,\dots,s\}
.
First we have that
\zeta(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})
and
\zeta(\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0})
are unbounded, because
\exp:\mathfrak{n}\to N
is a homeomorphism and
v\neq 0
.
Second,

(221)

d(\zeta_{k},\zeta_{k+1})=d(1_{N},\exp(v))\overset{\eqref{eq6874f56f}}{\leq}K.

Third, if
i,j,k\in\mathbb{Z}
are such that
i\leq j\leq k
, then

(222)

\displaystyle d(\zeta_{i},\zeta_{j})+d(\zeta_{j},\zeta_{k})

\displaystyle=d(1_{N},\exp((j-i)v))+d(1_{N},\exp((j-k)v))

(223)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq6874da38}}{\leq}C\sum_{m=1}^{s}a_{m}(|j-i|^{1/m}+|k-j|^{1/m})\|v_{m}\|^{1/m}+C

(224)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq6874d78e}}{\leq}C\sum_{m=1}^{s}a_{m}2|k-i|^{1/m}\|v_{m}\|^{1/m}+C

(225)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq6874da38}}{\leq}2C^{2}d(1_{N},\exp(\sum_{m=1}^{s}(k-i)v_{m}))+2C^{3}+C

(226)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq6874f56f}}{\leq}Kd(\zeta_{i},\zeta_{k})+K.

This shows the claim.

Finally, let
x,y\in N
be distinct points.
Define
w:=\exp^{-1}(x^{-1}y)
, so that
y=x\exp(w)
, and let
v=\frac{w}{\|w\|}
.
Then
v
is a unit vector and so the function
\zeta:\mathbb{Z}\to N
,
\zeta_{k}:=x\exp(kv)
, for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, defines a
K
-quasi-straight sequence with
\zeta_{0}=x
.
Moreover, if
k=\lfloor\|w\|\rfloor
, then

(227)

\displaystyle d(y,\zeta_{k})

\displaystyle=d(x\exp(w),x\exp(kv))=d(1_{N},\exp((\|w\|-k)v))

(228)

\displaystyle\leq\sup\{d(1_{N},\exp(u)):\|u\|\leq 1\}\overset{\eqref{eq6874f56f}}{\leq}K.

Therefore,
x,y\in B(\zeta(\mathbb{Z}),K)
.
This shows that
(N,d)
is quasi-straightenable.
∎

8.3.
Quasi-conformal maps between geodesic Lie groups

We are now ready to prove our main Theorem
B
, which we spell out for the sake of clarity.

Theorem 8.6
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be non-compact geodesic Lie groups of the same Hausdorff dimension
Q
, and let
f:G_{1}\to G_{2}
be a quasi-conformal map.
For each
j\in\{1,2\}
, denote by
N_{j}
the growth dimension of
G_{j}
.
Then
N_{1}=N_{2}
and one of the following cases happens:

(1)

Q>N_{1}
and
f
is a quasi-isometry:
This is the strictly parabolic case.

(2)

Q=N_{1}
:
This is the liminal parabolic case.

(3)

Q<N_{1}
and
f
is a quasi-isometry:
This is the hyperbolic case.

Proof.

If the Hausdorff dimension
Q
equals 1, then the spaces are the Euclidean line, so we are trivially in case (2). Next, we assume
Q>1
.

In cases (1) and (2), when
N_{1}\leq Q
,
G_{1}
has parabolic conformal type by Corollary
8.3
.
Hence,
G_{2}
has also parabolic conformal type, see Remark
4.2
, and thus
N_{2}\leq Q
by Corollary
8.3
.
Since
Q<\infty
, both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
have polynomial volume growth.
By Proposition
8.4
, they are both quasi-straightenable.

We claim that
Q=N_{1}
if and only if
Q=N_{2}
.
Notice that a geodesic Lie group with liminal parabolic type
is Ahlfors regular at all scales and it supports the Poincaré inequality at all scales by Proposition
8.1
; see also
[
46
, p.461]
.
Therefore, combining Proposition
5.7
and Proposition
5.18
,
we conclude that
N_{1}<Q
(or
N_{2}<Q
) if and only if the parabolic Ferrand distance of
G_{1}
(or
G_{2}
) takes finite values.
The quasi-conformal invariance of the parabolic Ferrand distance, as shown in Proposition
5.6
, implies that
Q=N_{1}
if and only if
Q=N_{2}
.

In case (1), when
N_{1}<Q
and
N_{2}<Q
,
we can apply Theorem
5.19
and obtain that
f
is a quasi-isometry.
Since the growth dimension is a quasi-isometric invariant by Proposition
5.2
, we conclude
N_{1}=N_{2}
.

In case (3), when
Q<N_{1}
, then
G_{1}
has hyperbolic conformal type by Corollary
8.3
.
Hence,
G_{2}
has also hyperbolic conformal type and thus
Q<N_{2}
, again by Corollary
8.3
.
We conclude by Theorem
7.8
that
f
is a quasi-isometry.
Again by Proposition
5.2
, we conclude
N_{1}=N_{2}
.
∎

For large classes of spaces, growth dimension is a quasi-isometric invariant, but need not be quasi-conformally invariant.
It turns out to be quasi-conformally invariant in the realm of geodesic Lie groups, as a consequence of the following Corollary.

Corollary 8.7
.

Among non-compact geodesic Lie groups, the growth dimension, the large-scale isoperimetric dimension, and the parabolic dimension are quasi-conformal and quasi-isometric invariants.

Proof.

By (
215
), the three dimensions are equal to each other for non-compact geodesic Lie groups.
As we have shown in Proposition
5.2
and Theorem
6.6
,
\dim_{\rm grt}
and
\dim_{\rm isp}
are quasi-isometric invariant, hence also
\dim_{\rm par}
is quasi-isometric invariant.
By Theorem
8.6
, the existence of a quasi-conformal homeomorphism implies either that the function is a quasi-isometry, or that both domain and codomain are liminal parabolic.
In both cases, the three dimensions are preserved.
∎

Remark 8.8
.

The proof of Theorem
8.6
uses, even though not explicitly, that the liminal parabolic case corresponds to the case when the geodesic Lie group is a Loewner space.

8.4.
Lie groups with infinite fundamental group

Recall that a map
\pi:X\to Y
between metric spaces is a
submetry
if

(229)

\pi(\bar{B}(x,r))=\bar{B}(\pi(x),r)\qquad\forall x\in X,\forall r>0.

For more information on submetries, see
[

, §3.1.7]
.

Lemma 8.9
.

For each
j\in\{1,2\}
, let
\pi_{j}:(\tilde{X}_{j},\tilde{d}_{j})\to(X_{j},d_{j})
be a submetry between metric spaces.
Let
f:X_{1}\to X_{2}
and
\tilde{f}:\tilde{X}_{1}\to\tilde{X}_{2}
be homeomorphisms such that
\pi_{1}\circ\tilde{f}=f\circ\pi_{1}
, i.e., the following diagram commutes:

(230)

\textstyle{\tilde{X}_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\tilde{f}}
\textstyle{\tilde{X}_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{2}}
\textstyle{X_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{X_{2}}

For every

,
if
\tilde{f}
is an
(L,C)
-quasi-isometry, then
f
is an
(L,C)
-quasi-isometry too.

Proof.

Let
z_{1},z_{2}\in X_{1}
.
Since
\pi_{1}
is a submetry, there are
\tilde{z}_{1},\tilde{z}_{2}\in X_{1}
such that
\pi_{1}(\tilde{z}_{k})=z_{k}
for each

and

.
Since
\tilde{f}
is an
(L,C)
-quasi-isometry and since
\pi_{2}
is 1-Lipschitz, we have

(231)

\displaystyle d_{2}(f(z_{1}),f(z_{2}))

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq68d44523}}{=}d_{2}(\pi_{2}(\tilde{f}(\tilde{z}_{1})),\pi_{2}(\tilde{f}(\tilde{z}_{2})))\leq\tilde{d}_{2}(\tilde{f}(\tilde{z}_{1}),\tilde{f}(\tilde{z}_{2}))

\displaystyle\leq L\tilde{d}_{1}(\tilde{z}_{1},\tilde{z}_{2})+C=Ld_{1}(z_{1},z_{2})+C.

Since the inverse of
f
and
\tilde{f}
satisfy the same conditions, we conclude that also the inequality
d_{2}(f(z_{1}),f(z_{2}))\geq 1/Ld_{1}(z_{1},z_{2})-C
is also fulfilled.
We conclude that
f
is an
(L,C)
-quasi-isometry.
∎

Theorem 8.10
.

Let
f:G_{1}\to G_{2}
be a quasi-conformal map between non-compact geodesic Lie groups.
If the fundamental group of
G_{1}
is infinite, then
f
is a quasi-isometry.

Proof.

Let
Q
be the Hausdorff dimension of
G_{1}
and
N
the growth dimension of
G_{1}
.
If
N\neq Q
, then
f
is a quasi-isometry by Theorem
8.6
,
so we assume
N=Q
.

For
j\in\{1,2\}
, let
\tilde{G}_{j}
be the universal covering of
G_{j}
,
endowed with the geodesic distance induced by the one on
G_{j}
.
The quotient maps
\pi_{j}:\tilde{G}_{j}\to G_{j}
are submetries.
Since
f
is a quasi-conformal homeomorphism, then it lifts to a quasi-conformal homeomorphism
\tilde{f}:\tilde{G}_{1}\to\tilde{G}_{2}
.
Let
Q
be the Hausdorff dimension and growth dimension of
G_{1}
.
Then
Q
is also the Hausdorff dimension of
\tilde{G}_{1}
,
but, by Lemma
8.11
, the growth dimension of
\tilde{G}_{1}
is strictly larger than

because the fundamental group of

is infinite.
Therefore,
\tilde{G}_{1}
is of hyperbolic type, and thus
\tilde{f}
is a quasi-isometry by Theorem
8.6
.
By Lemma

, also

is a quasi-isometry.
∎

Lemma 8.11
.

Let
\pi:\tilde{G}\to G
be a covering map and submetry of connected metric Lie groups with infinite kernel.
Let
\tilde{\mu}
be a Haar measure on
\tilde{G}
and

.
Then there are
C>0
and

such that

(233)

\tilde{\mu}(B_{\tilde{G}}(1_{\tilde{G}},R))\geq C\mu(B_{G}(1_{G},R))\cdot R^{k},\qquad\forall R>1.

Proof.

We denote by
d
the metric on
G
, and by
\tilde{d}
the metric on
\tilde{G}
.
Let
Z:=\ker(\pi)
.
We claim that there are

and

such that

(234)

Indeed, since

is a normal and discrete subgroup of

, which is connected,
then

So, there are a finite group
T\subset Z
and
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}\subset Z
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
Z\simeq T\times\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{Z}\cdot e_{i}
.
Since
Z
is infinite, then
k\geq 1
.
If
z\in Z
, then there are
t\in T
and
\{z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}\subset\mathbb{Z}
such that

z=t+\sum_{i=1}^{k}z_{i}e_{i}
.
Therefore,
\tilde{d}(1_{\tilde{G}},z)\leq\tilde{d}(1_{\tilde{G}},t)+\sum_{i=1}^{k}|z_{i}|\tilde{d}(1_{\tilde{G}},e_{i})
.
Set
A:=\max\{\tilde{d}(1_{\tilde{G}},e_{i})\}_{i=1}^{k}
.
If we take
t=1_{\tilde{G}}
and
|z_{i}|<\frac{R}{Ak}
, we obtain

\tilde{d}(1_{\tilde{G}},z)<R
.
Therefore, the set
B_{\tilde{G}}(1_{\tilde{G}},R)\cap Z
contains at least
\left(\frac{R}{Ak}\right)^{k}
elements and we get (
234
).

Fix
r>0
, e.g.,
r=1
.
For
R>1
, let
\{x_{j}^{R}\}_{j=1}^{N(R)}\subset G
be a maximal
r
-separated set in
B_{G}(1_{G},R)
, for some
N(R)\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then we have, for every
R>1
,

(235)

\mu(B_{G}(1_{G},R))\leq N(R)\cdot\mu(B_{G}(1_{G},r)).

Since
\pi
is a submetry, we can lift these sets to
\{\tilde{x}_{j}^{R}\}_{j=1}^{N(R)}\subset\tilde{G}
such that
\pi(\tilde{x}_{j}^{R})=x_{j}^{R}
and
d(1_{\tilde{G}},\tilde{x}_{j}^{R})=d(1_{G},x_{j}^{R})
, for every
R
and
j
.
For
R>1
, consider the set

(236)

\cal X(R):=\left\{\tilde{x}_{j}^{R}z:j\in\{1,\dots,N(R)\},\ z\in B_{\tilde{G}}(1_{\tilde{G}},R)\cap Z\right\}.

If
i\neq j
and
z,z^{\prime}\in B_{\tilde{G}}(1_{\tilde{G}},R)\cap Z
, then

\tilde{d}(\tilde{x}_{i}^{R}z,\tilde{x}_{j}^{R}z^{\prime})\geq d(x_{i}^{R},x_{j}^{R})\geq r
,
and, if
z\neq z^{\prime}
, then
\tilde{d}(\tilde{x}_{i}^{R}z,\tilde{x}_{i}^{R}z^{\prime})=\tilde{d}(1_{\tilde{G}},z^{-1}z^{\prime})\geq\epsilon
,
where
\epsilon:=\min\{\tilde{d}(1_{\tilde{G}},z):z\in Z\setminus\{1_{\tilde{G}}\}\}>0
.
Moreover,
\tilde{d}(1_{\tilde{G}},\tilde{x}_{j}^{R}z)\leq d(1_{G},x_{j}^{R})+\tilde{d}(1_{\tilde{G}},z)\leq 2R
.
Therefore, for
\delta:=\min\{r,\epsilon\}
,

(237)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}(B_{\tilde{G}}(1_{\tilde{G}},2R+\delta))

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{y\in\cal X(R)}\tilde{\mu}(B_{\tilde{G}}(y,\delta/2))=\#\cal X(R)\tilde{\mu}(B_{\tilde{G}}(1_{\tilde{G}},\delta/2))

(238)

\displaystyle=N(R)\cdot\#(B_{\tilde{G}}(1_{\tilde{G}},R)\cap Z)\overset{\eqref{eq68db6e37}\&\eqref{eq68db6dd4}}{\geq}\frac{\mu(B_{G}(1_{G},R))}{\mu(B_{G}(1_{G},r))}\cdot CR^{k}.

We thus get (
233
).
∎

8.5.
Nilpotent geodesic Lie groups

Theorem
8.6
leaves an incomplete picture for the “QC implies QI” for geodesic Lie groups of liminal parabolic type.
In the class of nilpotent geodesic Lie groups, we are able to fill this gap.
We show in Theorem
8.14
that nilpotent geodesic Lie groups that are quasi-conformally equivalent are also quasi-isometric.

Nilpotent groups have polynomial growth.
In particular, the growth dimension
N
of a simply connected nilpotent Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}

can be computed algebraically by the
Bass–Guivarc’h formula

(239)

N=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\dim(\mathfrak{g}^{k}),

where
\mathfrak{g}^{1}=\mathfrak{g}
, and
\mathfrak{g}^{k+1}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{k}]
;
see
[
23
, Théorèm II.4]
or
[
3
, Theorem 2]
.
See also
[
8
, Corollary 2.9]
.

Definition 8.12
.

A geodesic Lie group
(G,\Delta,\|\cdot\|)
is a
Carnot group
if
G
is simply connected and
\Delta
is the first layer of a stratification,
which means that
the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
has a splitting
\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{j=1}^{s}V_{j}
with
V_{j+1}=[V_{1},V_{j}]
for all
j
, and with
\Delta=V_{1}
;
see for instance
[
33
, Chapter 11]
.

The following proposition describes the dichotomy for simply connected nilpotent geodesic Lie groups.
Afterwards, we will treat the general case of nilpotent geodesic Lie groups.

Proposition 8.13
.

Let
G
be a simply connected nilpotent geodesic Lie group with Hausdorff dimension
Q
and growth dimension
N
.
Then
Q\leq N
.
Moreover,

(I)

Either
Q=N
,
G
is a Carnot group and
G
has liminal parabolic conformal type;

(II)

Or
Q<N
,
G
is not a Carnot group and
G
has hyperbolic conformal type.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie algebra of
G
and
\Delta\subset\mathfrak{g}
the bracket-generating polarization of
G
.
Therefore,
Q
is given by (
214
), while
N
by (
239
).

The assumption that
\Delta
is bracket generating implies

(240)

\forall k\geq 0,\ \Delta^{[k]}+\mathfrak{g}^{k+1}=\mathfrak{g}.

Indeed, if (
240
) were false, then there would exist
k\geq 1
with
\Delta^{[k]}+\mathfrak{g}^{k+1}\neq\mathfrak{g}
(the case
k=0
is trivial).
Taking the quotient
\mathfrak{h}:=\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}^{k+1}
of
\mathfrak{g}
by the ideal
\mathfrak{g}^{k+1}
, we obtain a nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
of step
k
with
\Delta^{[k]}/\mathfrak{g}^{k+1}\neq\mathfrak{h}
.
Since
\mathfrak{h}
has nilpotency step
k
, then
\Delta^{[m]}/\mathfrak{g}^{k+1}=\Delta^{[k]}/\mathfrak{g}^{k+1}
for all
m>k
.
Therefore,
\Delta/\mathfrak{g}^{k+1}
would be not bracket generating, in contradiction with
\Delta
being bracket generating in
\mathfrak{g}
.
This shows our claim (
240
).

Let
s
be the nilpotency step of
\mathfrak{g}
.
From (
240
), we get
\Delta^{[s]}=\mathfrak{g}
, because
\mathfrak{g}^{s+1}=\{0\}
.
If
s=1
, i.e.,
\mathfrak{g}
is abelian, then clearly
Q=N
and
G
is a Carnot group.
Assume
s\geq 2
.
Again from (
240
), we obtain for each
k\in\{0,\dots,s-1\}

(241)

\displaystyle\dim\mathfrak{g}^{k+1}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq67f78c42}}{\geq}\dim\mathfrak{g}-\dim\Delta^{[k]}=\sum_{j=k+1}^{s}(\dim\Delta^{[j]}-\dim\Delta^{[j-1]})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=k+1}^{s}\dim(\Delta^{[j]}/\Delta^{[j-1]}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle Q\overset{\eqref{eq684e8752}}{=}\sum_{j=1}^{s}j\dim(\Delta^{[j]}/\Delta^{[j-1]})=\sum_{k=1}^{s}\sum_{j=k}^{s}\dim(\Delta^{[j]}/\Delta^{[j-1]})\overset{\eqref{eq684e87d8}}{\leq}\sum_{k=1}^{s}\dim\mathfrak{g}^{k}=N.

We have thus shown that
Q\leq N
.

Equality
Q=N
holds if and only if, for each
k\in\{0,\dots,s-1\}
,
there is equality also in (
241
),
i.e.,
\dim\mathfrak{g}=\dim\Delta^{[k]}+\dim\mathfrak{g}^{k+1}
.
Therefore,
Q=N
holds if and only if the sum in (
240
) is a direct sum.
Consequently, equality
Q=N
holds if and only if
\Delta
is the first layer of a stratification of
\mathfrak{g}
.

We conclude that, if
Q=N
then
G
is a Carnot group,
and
G
is liminal parabolic by Corollary
8.3
.
If
Q\neq N
then
Q<N
and
G
is not a Carnot group,
and
G
is hyperbolic by Corollary
8.3
.
∎

Theorem 8.14
.

Let
f:G_{1}\to G_{2}
be a metrically quasi-conformal map between nilpotent geodesic Lie groups.
Then
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
have the same Hausdorff dimension, which we denote by
Q
.
Denote by
N_{j}
the growth dimension of
G_{j}
, for each
j\in\{1,2\}
.
Then
N_{1}=N_{2}
and one of the following two exclusive cases happens:

(I)

Either both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are simply connected and have liminal parabolic conformal type, and thus they are algebraically isomorphic and metrically bi-Lipschitz Carnot groups.

(II)

Or
f
is a quasi-isometry.

Proof.

First of all, metrically quasi-conformally equivalent geodesic Lie groups have the same Hausdorff dimension by
[
35
, Corollary 6.4]
. If the Hausdorff dimension
Q
equals 1, then the spaces are either the Euclidean line or a circle, so the conclusions are trivial. Next, we assume
Q>1
.

From Remark
2.8
, the map
f
is a geometrically quasi-conformal map.
By Theorem
8.6
, we obtain that
N:=N_{1}=N_{2}
and that whenever
Q<N
or
Q>N
, then
f
is a quasi-isometry.
If
N=Q
, that is, if
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
have liminal parabolic conformal type, then we distinguish two cases.

On the one hand, if
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are simply connected, then they are Carnot groups by Proposition
8.13
; since by
[
41
, Theorem 2]
differentiation of metrically quasi-conformal maps between Carnot groups induces bi-Lipschitz isomorphisms of Lie groups, we conclude that
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are algebraically isomorphic and metrically bi-Lipschitz Carnot groups.

On the other hand, suppose that
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are not simply connected.
Since they are nilpotent, their fundamental groups are infinite (this is because simply connected Lie groups have no torsion; see
[
33
, Proposition 9.4.26.i]
).
By Theorem
8.10
, the quasi-conformal map
f
is a quasi-isometry.
∎

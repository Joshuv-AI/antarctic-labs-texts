Investigating (Non)-Integrability and Pulsating String in D3‐Brane Background
Rashmi R. Nayak, Kamal L. Panigrahi, Manoranjan Samal, Balbeer Singh

Abstract
This work explores the (non)-integrability and chaotic dynamics of classical strings in the background of a D3-brane with a non-commutative parameter, within the framework of the AdS/CFT correspondence. Using the Polyakov action, we derive the equations of motion and constraints for pulsating strings and analyze their stability through perturbation theory. In the high-energy limit, the first-order perturbed equation simplifies to the Pöschl-Teller equation, solvable via associated Legendre or hypergeometric functions, while numerical methods are employed for generic energy values. We demonstrate that the non-commutative parameter enhances chaotic behavior, as evidenced by the Largest Lyapunov Exponent (LLE). Furthermore, we investigate the integrability of geodesic motion and identify two distinct string modes: captured at and escape to infinity. Finally, we study pulsating strings in the deformed ( A ​ d ​ S 3 × S 2 ) ϰ (AdS_{3}\times S^{2})_{\varkappa} background, deriving dispersion relations for both short and long strings.

5.1
(Largest) Lyapunov Exponent

For a dynamical system, consider two points in phase space that are initially separated by a small distance
\delta\mathbf{x}_{0}
. As the system evolves, the separation between these points typically grows or shrinks exponentially over time. The Lyapunov exponent
\lambda
quantifies this exponential rate of separation:

\displaystyle|\delta\mathbf{x}(t)|\approx|\delta\mathbf{x}_{0}|e^{\lambda t},

(40)

A positive
\lambda
implies exponential divergence and is indicative of chaos, while a negative value indicates that trajectories converge toward an attractor. In a dynamical system with
n
dimensions, there are typically
n
Lyapunov exponents, each corresponding to a different direction in phase space. The Largest Lyapunov Exponent (LLE) is the maximum of these exponents and describes the most rapid rate of separation (or convergence) of infinitesimally close trajectories.

In this section, we numerically check the chaos by calculating the associated (Largest) Lyapunov exponent values. Throughout the numerical calculations, without loss of generality, we have set
\alpha^{\prime}=1/\pi,~g=0.1,~N=500
and
n_{5}=1
. The energy is held constant while
\theta
is varied as a parameter. Depending on the initial location of the string we observe two kinds of string modes: captured at and escape to infinity. The capture scenario is usually observed when the string is placed near the horizon whereas the escape to infinity is observed for the string located at away from the horizon. In Fig

, for instance, we first show the possible string trajectories and then plot the corresponding Lyapunov exponent values in Fig

6
Perturbation analysis in D3-brane background

In this section, we shall embark on studying the D3-brane without the involvement of the NS-NS flux. We will effectively reduce the system into three dimensions and perform the perturbation analysis.

The metric is given by

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=f^{-1/2}\Big(-dx_{0}^{2}+dx_{1}^{2}+h(dx_{2}^{2}+dx_{3}^{2})\Big)+f^{1/2}(dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega_{5}^{2}),

(41)

where,
d\Omega_{5}^{2}=d\phi_{1}^{2}+\cos^{2}\phi_{1}d\phi_{2}^{2}+\sin^{2}\phi_{1}\Big(d\phi_{3}^{2}+\cos^{2}\phi_{3}d\phi_{4}^{2}+\sin^{2}\phi_{3}d\phi_{5}^{2}\Big)
.

Consider the following ansatz:
\phi_{1}=\phi_{3}=\frac{\pi}{2}
with

\displaystyle t=t(\tau)\ ,r=r(\sigma)\ ,\phi_{5}=\phi_{5}(\tau)=\omega\tau,

(42)

while the rest of the coordinates are set to some arbitrary constant values.

The corresponding Polyakov action becomes

\displaystyle S_{P}=-\frac{1}{4\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\int d\tau d\sigma(\frac{\dot{t}^{2}}{\sqrt{f}}-r^{2}\sqrt{f}\dot{\phi}^{2}+r^{\prime 2}\sqrt{f}).

(43)

The induced metric is given by,

\displaystyle ds^{2}_{ind}

\displaystyle=G_{\alpha\beta}d\sigma^{\alpha}d\sigma^{\beta},

(44)

\displaystyle=(-\frac{\dot{t}^{2}}{\sqrt{f}}+\dot{\phi}_{5}^{2}r^{2}\sqrt{f})d\tau^{2}+\sqrt{f}r^{\prime 2}d\sigma^{2}.

(45)

The energy is given by
\mathcal{E}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{t}}=\frac{-\dot{t}}{\sqrt{f}}
, whereas the equation of motion for
t
is :
\ddot{t}=0
.

The equation of motion along
r
is

\displaystyle 2r^{\prime\prime}\sqrt{f}+\frac{r^{\prime 2}f^{\prime}}{2\sqrt{f}}+\frac{\dot{t}^{2}}{2f^{3/2}}+2r\dot{\phi}_{5}^{2}+\frac{\dot{\phi}_{5}r^{2}f^{\prime}}{2\sqrt{f}}=0.

(46)

Also, the Virasoro constraint or the conformal gauge constraint is given by

\displaystyle-\frac{\dot{t}^{2}}{\sqrt{f}}+\dot{\phi}_{5}^{2}r^{2}\sqrt{f}+\sqrt{f}r^{\prime 2}=0.

(47)

the above ansatz is consistent with the equation of motion (by differentiating wrt
\tau
, we get
\ddot{t}=0
and by differentiating wrt
\sigma
, we get the
r
-equation).

Next, we consider the first-order perturbations along this string.
The solution
r(\sigma)
is given by

\displaystyle r(\sigma)=\frac{\mathcal{E}}{\omega}\sin(\omega\sigma+C),

(48)

which is oscillatory.
Next, we determine the tangent vectors

\dot{X}=(\dot{t},0,\omega),~~~~~X^{\prime}=(0,r^{\prime},0)

. (where we have used here the specific form
\phi=\omega\tau
).

Then the normal vectors are

\displaystyle N^{\mu}=\Big(\frac{r^{2}\omega\sqrt[4]{f(r)}}{\sqrt{E^{2}r^{2}-r^{4}\omega^{2}}},0,-\frac{E}{\sqrt[4]{f(r)}\sqrt{r^{2}\left(E^{2}-r^{2}\omega^{2}\right)}}\Big).

(49)

The components of the extrinsic curvature tensor are given by

\displaystyle K_{\tau\tau}=0,

(50)

\displaystyle K_{\tau\sigma}=-\frac{E\sqrt[4]{f}rw\left(rf^{\prime}(r)+4f(r)\right)}{4f(r)},

(51)

\displaystyle K_{\sigma\tau}=-\frac{E\sqrt[4]{f}rw\left(rf^{\prime}(r)+4f(r)\right)}{4f(r)},

(52)

\displaystyle K_{\sigma\sigma}=0.

(53)

By using Mathematica we found that the normal fundamental forms vanish. Next, we find the first-order perturbations equation from

\Box\varphi^{i}+2\mu^{\alpha}_{ij}\partial_{\alpha}\varphi^{j}+(\nabla_{\alpha}\mu^{\alpha}_{ij})\varphi^{j}-\mu^{\alpha}_{il}\mu^{l}_{j\alpha}\varphi^{j}+\frac{2}{G_{cc}}K^{\alpha\beta}_{i}K_{\alpha\beta}^{j}\varphi^{j}-h^{\alpha\beta}R^{\mu}_{\rho\sigma\nu}N^{\rho}_{i}N^{\sigma}_{j}X^{\mu}_{,\alpha}X^{\nu}_{,\beta}\varphi^{j}=0.

(54)

Computing the term
\frac{2}{G_{cc}}K^{\alpha\beta}_{i}K_{\alpha\beta}^{j}
which is given by

\displaystyle\frac{2}{G^{c}_{c}}K^{\alpha\beta}_{i}K_{\alpha\beta}^{j}=-\frac{E^{2}\omega^{2}\left(rf^{\prime}(r)+4f(r)\right)^{2}}{8f(r)^{2}(E-r\omega)(E+r\omega)},

(55)

and the term

\displaystyle h^{\alpha\beta}R^{\mu}_{\rho\sigma\nu}N^{\rho}_{i}N^{\sigma}_{j}X^{\mu}_{,\alpha}X^{\nu}_{,\beta}\varphi^{j}=\frac{4f(r)\left(E^{2}f^{\prime\prime}(r)+r\omega^{2}\left(rf^{\prime\prime}(r)+f^{\prime}(r)\right)\right)-5f^{\prime}(r)^{2}\left(E^{2}+r^{2}\omega^{2}\right)}{16f(r)^{2}}.

(56)

Thus, we get the first-order perturbations equation as

\displaystyle-\ddot{\varphi}+\varphi^{\prime\prime}-\frac{E^{2}\omega^{2}\left(rf^{\prime}(r)+4f(r)\right)^{2}}{8f(r)^{2}(E-r\omega)(E+r\omega)}\varphi

\displaystyle-\frac{4f(r)\left(E^{2}f^{\prime\prime}(r)+r\omega^{2}\left(rf^{\prime\prime}(r)+f^{\prime}(r)\right)\right)-5f^{\prime}(r)^{2}\left(E^{2}+r^{2}\omega^{2}\right)}{16f(r)^{2}}\varphi=0.

(57)

Now, we can take the Fourier series expansion to be
\varphi=\sum_{n}\alpha_{n}(\sigma)e^{in\tau}
, we get

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime\prime}(\sigma)+\alpha(\sigma)\Big(n^{2}-\frac{4f(r)\left(E^{2}f^{\prime\prime}(r)+r\omega^{2}\left(f^{\prime}(r)+rf^{\prime\prime}(r)\right)\right)-5f^{\prime}(r)^{2}\left(E^{2}+r^{2}\omega^{2}\right)}{16f(r)^{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{E^{2}\omega^{2}\left(rf^{\prime}(r)+4f(r)\right)^{2}}{8f(r)^{2}(E-r\omega)(E+r\omega)}\Big)=0.

(58)

Next, we numerically solve this equation using solution
r(\sigma)=\frac{\mathcal{E}}{\omega}\sin(\omega\sigma+C)
.
We plot

in Fig.

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime\prime}(\sigma)+\left[n^{2}-2\omega^{2}\sec^{2}(c+\sigma\omega)\right]\alpha(\sigma)=0.

The solutions are expressed in terms of associated Legendre functions or hypergeometric functions

\displaystyle\alpha(\sigma)=C_{1}P_{\nu}^{\mu}(\tan(c+\sigma\omega))+C_{2}Q_{\nu}^{\mu}(\tan(c+\sigma\omega)).

Except for the few special cases discussed in the section, solving the equations analytically is futile. The proper approach is to solve them numerically.
The problem can be viewed as solving the second-order differential equation

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime\prime}(\sigma)+G(\sigma)\alpha(\sigma)=0,~~\alpha(a_{0})=a,~~\alpha^{\prime}(a_{0})=b,

\displaystyle G(\sigma)=n^{2}-\frac{2E^{8}\omega^{2}\sec^{2}(c+\sigma\omega)}{\left(\text{$\alpha^{\prime}$}^{2}R^{2}\omega^{4}\csc^{4}(c+\sigma\omega)+E^{4}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\text{$\alpha^{\prime}$}^{2}R^{2}\omega^{6}\csc^{4}(c+\sigma\omega)\left(-5E^{4}\csc^{2}(c+\sigma\omega)+\text{$\alpha^{\prime}$}^{2}R^{2}\omega^{4}\csc^{4}(c+\sigma\omega)-4E^{4}\right)}{\left(\text{$\alpha^{\prime}$}^{2}R^{2}\omega^{4}\csc^{4}(c+\sigma\omega)+E^{4}\right)^{2}},

(62)

where
a_{0},~a,~b
are reals.
Ideally, one would test the stability of the equations for an infinite range of initial conditions. However, as this is impractical, we have restricted our analysis to a finite set of initial conditions. We now present the numerical results

7
Pulsating string in deformed
(AdS_{3}\times S^{2})_{\varkappa}

This section will consider the semiclassical quantization of a closed pulsating string in the deformed
(AdS_{3}\times S^{2})_{\varkappa}
subsector of the deformed
(AdS_{5}\times S^{5})_{\varkappa}
.
The metric of deformed
(AdS_{3}\times S^{2})_{\varkappa}
is given by
[
71
,
66
]

ds^{2}_{({AdS_{3}\times S^{2}})_{\varkappa}}=-h(\rho)dt^{2}+f(\rho)d\rho^{2}+\rho^{2}d\psi_{1}^{2}+\tilde{h}(r)d\phi^{2}+\tilde{f}(r)dr^{2},

(63)

where,

\displaystyle h=\frac{1+\rho^{2}}{1-\varkappa^{2}\rho^{2}},~~~f=\frac{1}{(1+\rho^{2})(1-\varkappa^{2}\rho^{2})},

(64)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}=\frac{1-r^{2}}{1+\varkappa^{2}r^{2}},~~~\tilde{f}=\frac{1}{(1-r^{2})(1+\varkappa^{2}r^{2})},

(65)

where the coordinates
t,~\rho,~\psi_{1}
are the coordinates of the deformed metric
AdS_{3}
whereas the coordinates
\phi,~r
belong to the deformed sphere
S^{2}
.
We do the following substitution in the metric:

\rho=\sinh\rho,~~r=\cos\psi,

(66)

then we obtain

\displaystyle ds^{2}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\cosh^{2}\rho}{1-\varkappa^{2}\sinh^{2}\rho}dt^{2}+\frac{1}{1-\varkappa^{2}\sinh^{2}\rho}d\rho^{2}+\sinh^{2}\rho d\psi_{1}^{2}+\frac{\sin^{2}\psi}{1+\varkappa^{2}\cos^{2}\psi}d\phi^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{1+\varkappa^{2}\cos^{2}\psi}d\psi^{2}.

(67)

We rename the coordinate
\psi_{1}\equiv\varphi
for notational conveniences.
Consider the following pulsating string ansatz:

\displaystyle t=t(\tau),~~\rho=\rho(\tau),~~\phi=\phi(\tau),~~\psi=\psi(\sigma).

(68)

Then, the Polyakov action becomes

\displaystyle S_{P}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\sqrt{1+\varkappa^{2}}\int d\tau d\sigma\Bigg(-\frac{\cosh^{2}\rho}{1-\varkappa^{2}\sinh^{2}\rho}\dot{t}^{2}+\frac{1}{1-\varkappa^{2}\sinh^{2}\rho}\dot{\rho}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\sin^{2}\psi}{1+\varkappa^{2}\cos^{2}\psi}\dot{\phi}^{2}-\frac{1}{1+\varkappa^{2}\cos^{2}\psi}\psi^{{}^{\prime}2}\Bigg).

(69)

Therefore the Lagrangian is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\Bigg(-\frac{\cosh^{2}\rho}{1-\varkappa^{2}\sinh^{2}\rho}\dot{t}^{2}+\frac{1}{1-\varkappa^{2}\sinh^{2}\rho}\dot{\rho}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\sin^{2}\psi}{1+\varkappa^{2}\cos^{2}\psi}\dot{\phi}^{2}-\frac{1}{1+\varkappa^{2}\cos^{2}\psi}\psi^{{}^{\prime}2}\Bigg).

(70)

The equation of motion for t is :

\displaystyle\cosh^{2}\rho~\ddot{t}+\frac{\left(1+\varkappa^{2}\right)\sinh 2\rho}{1-\varkappa^{2}\sinh^{2}\rho}\dot{t}\dot{\rho}=0.

(71)

The equation of motion for
\rho
is given by

\displaystyle\ddot{\rho}\left(2+\varkappa^{2}-\varkappa^{2}\cosh 2\rho\right)+\sinh 2\rho\left(\varkappa^{2}\dot{\rho}^{2}+\left(\varkappa^{2}+1\right)\dot{t}^{2}\right)=0.

(72)

The equation for
\phi
is

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}=\omega~~\textit{(constant)},

(73)

and the equation for
\psi
is

\displaystyle\sin 2\psi\left(w^{2}\left(\varkappa^{2}+1\right)-\varkappa^{2}\psi^{\prime}\right)^{2}-\psi^{\prime\prime}\left(\varkappa^{2}\cos 2\psi+\varkappa^{2}+2\right)=0.

(74)

The first Virasoro constraint is trivially satisfied, whereas the second Virasaro constraint is given by

-\frac{\cosh^{2}\rho}{1-\varkappa^{2}\sinh^{2}\rho}\dot{t}^{2}+\frac{1}{1-\varkappa^{2}\sinh^{2}\rho}\dot{\rho}^{2}+\frac{\sin^{2}\psi}{1+\varkappa^{2}\cos^{2}\psi}\dot{\phi}^{2}+\frac{1}{1+\varkappa^{2}\cos^{2}\psi}\psi^{{}^{\prime}2}=0.

(75)

From the equation of motion of
t
:

\displaystyle\dot{t}=\frac{A(1-\varkappa^{2}\sinh^{2}\rho)}{\cosh^{2}\rho},

(76)

where A is a constant.
Next, we substitute the value of
\dot{t}
and
\dot{\phi}
into the Virasoro constraint
75
to obtain

\displaystyle-\frac{A^{2}(1-\varkappa^{2}\sinh^{2}\rho)}{\cosh^{2}\rho}+\frac{\dot{\rho}^{2}}{1-\varkappa^{2}\sinh^{2}\rho}+\frac{w^{2}\sin^{2}\psi}{1+\varkappa^{2}\cos^{2}\psi}+\frac{1}{1+\varkappa^{2}\cos^{2}\psi}\psi^{{}^{\prime}2}=0.

(77)

which is a coupled differential equation in variables
\rho(\tau)
and
\psi(\sigma)
. To solve this coupled equation, we split the equation into the variables as follows

\displaystyle-\frac{A^{2}(1-\varkappa^{2}\sinh^{2}\rho)}{\cosh^{2}\rho}+\frac{\dot{\rho}^{2}}{1-\varkappa^{2}\sinh^{2}\rho}+C^{2}

\displaystyle=0,

(78)

\displaystyle\frac{w^{2}\sin^{2}\psi}{1+\varkappa^{2}\cos^{2}\psi}+\frac{1}{1+\varkappa^{2}\cos^{2}\psi}\psi^{{}^{\prime}2}-C^{2}

\displaystyle=0,

(79)

where
C
is an arbitrary constant.
On solving the above equations, we obtain

\displaystyle\sinh\rho=\sqrt{\frac{R_{-}sn^{2}\left(2C\varkappa\sqrt{R_{+}}\tau,\frac{R_{-}}{R_{+}}\right)}{1+\varkappa^{2}\left[1+R_{-}sn^{2}\left(2C\varkappa\sqrt{R_{+}}\tau,\frac{R_{-}}{R_{+}}\right)\right]}},

(80)

\displaystyle\sin\theta=\sqrt{\frac{C^{2}(1+\varkappa^{2})}{w^{2}+\varkappa^{2}C^{2}}}sn\left(\sqrt{w^{2}+\varkappa^{2}C^{2}}\sigma,\frac{C^{2}(1+\varkappa^{2})}{w^{2}+\varkappa^{2}C^{2}}\right),

(81)

where,

\displaystyle R_{\mp}=\frac{-3C^{2}-A^{2}\varkappa^{2}\mp\sqrt{A^{4}\varkappa^{4}+2A^{2}C^{2}\varkappa^{2}(5+2\varkappa^{2})+C^{4}(9+4\varkappa^{2}-4\varkappa^{4})}}{2C^{2}\varkappa^{2}}.

(82)

The conserved charges associated to the string can be given by

\displaystyle E=-\int^{2\pi}_{0}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{t}}=A\sqrt{1+\varkappa^{2}}/\alpha^{\prime},

(83)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J_{\phi}}=\int^{2\pi}_{0}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{\phi}}=\frac{\sqrt{1+\varkappa^{2}}}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\frac{w\sin^{2}\psi}{1+\varkappa^{2}\cos^{2}\psi}.

(84)

We define,

\displaystyle\alpha\equiv\sinh\xi_{\textit{max}}=\sqrt{R_{-}},

(85)

\displaystyle\beta\equiv\sin\theta_{\textit{max}}=\sqrt{\frac{C^{2}(1+\varkappa^{2})}{w^{2}+\varkappa^{2}C^{2}}}.

(86)

From the periodicity condition of
\theta
, we obtain

\displaystyle 2\pi\sqrt{w^{2}+\varkappa^{2}C^{2}}=4\textbf{K}\left[\frac{C^{2}(1+\varkappa^{2})}{w^{2}+\varkappa^{2}C^{2}}\right].

(87)

where
K
(..) is complete elliptic integral of first kind.
Thus, consequently, we get

\displaystyle C=\frac{2\beta\textbf{K}(\beta^{2})}{\pi\sqrt{1+\varkappa^{2}}},~~~w=\frac{2}{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{1+\varkappa^{2}(1-\beta^{2})}{1+\varkappa^{2}}}\textbf{K}(\beta^{2}).

(88)

Next, substituting the value of
\sin\theta
into eq
84
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\phi}=\frac{2\sqrt{1+\varkappa^{2}-\varkappa^{2}\beta^{2}}}{\pi\alpha^{\prime}\varkappa^{2}}\Big[\Pi\Big(\frac{\varkappa^{2}\beta^{2}}{1+\varkappa^{2}},\beta^{2}\Big)-\textbf{K}(\beta^{2})\Big].

(89)

For solving the expression of
E
, we first find
A
, then substitute the value of
C
from eq

\displaystyle E=\frac{8\beta\textbf{K}\left(\beta^{2}\right)\sqrt{1+\varkappa^{2}}\cosh\xi}{\alpha^{\prime}\sqrt{1+\varkappa^{2}}},

(90)

\displaystyle=\frac{8}{\alpha^{\prime}}\beta\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}\textbf{K}\left(\beta^{2}\right).

(91)

In this section, we take the limit

whereas in the limit

\displaystyle E\approx\frac{4}{\alpha^{\prime}}\alpha~\textit{ln}\frac{16}{1-\beta^{2}}.

8
Conclusions and Future directions

In this work, we start by investigating the (non)-integrability and chaotic behaviour of the D3-brane in the presence of an effective non-commutative parameter. Additionally, we examine the point-particle case, where the dynamics are shown to be integrable. Notably, the non-commutative parameter intensifies the chaotic effects within the system. Moreover, we identify two distinct string modes: one leading to captured at and the other escaping to infinity. The (largest) Lyapunov exponents are found to be slightly higher in the capture scenario compared to the escape-to-infinity case.

Next, using the Polyakov action, with the help of geometric quantities like the extrinsic curvature tensor, we formulate perturbations equations. With a pulsating string ansatz, solutions to the motion and constraints were derived. In the high-energy limit, the first-order equation simplifies to the Pöschl-Teller equation, solvable via Legendre or hypergeometric functions, while numerical methods address generic
\mathcal{E}
cases. This aids in finding first-order energy corrections linked to anomalous dimensions in strongly-coupled gauge theory. Moreover, the numerical results show that for oscillatory/non-oscillatory solutions,
\omega
and
\mathcal{E}
depend on initial conditions. As
\omega
increases,
\mathcal{E}
must decrease to maintain oscillations. Small
\omega
ensures oscillatory solutions regardless of
\mathcal{E}
or initial conditions, while certain parameter values lead to instability.

Lastly, we have studied pulsating string in the so-called deformed
(AdS_{3}\times S^{2})_{\varkappa}
background. To the best of our knowledge, the analysis of the string dispersion relation in the undeformed case has not been done before. To obtain pulsating string solutions in the deformed background, we have employed a suitable ansatz for a circular pulsating string configuration. By considering the bosonic sector of the string action in the conformal gauge, we derived the corresponding equations of motion and Virasoro constraints. We have further found out the short string energy as a function of
\mathcal{J}_{\phi}
and
\varkappa
, and for the long string, in the limit of
\alpha>>1
it is an involved logarithmic function of
\varkappa
. Thus, the energy and the computed corrections serve as the first examples of contributions from this string sector to the anomalous dimensions in the dual gauge theory. In future, it would be interesting to include the effect of the B-field using a different string ansatz and analyze its influence on chaos in the system, as this could reveal new dynamical features. Further, it is crucial to investigate field theory duals of these theories, which would provide a deeper understanding of the gauge-gravity correspondence and its implications.

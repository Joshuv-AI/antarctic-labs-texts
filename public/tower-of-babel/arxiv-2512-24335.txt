Backpropagation from KL Projections: Differential and Exact I-Projection Correspondences
Manish Krishan Lal

Abstract
We establish two exact correspondences between reverse-mode automatic differentiation (backpropagation evaluated at a fixed forward pass) and compositions of projection maps in Kullback–Leibler (KL) geometry. In both settings, belief-propagation-style message passing enforces agreement and factorization constraints via KL projections. In the first setting, backpropagation arises as the differential of a KL projection map on a delta-lifted factorization. In the second setting, on complete and decomposable sum–product networks, backpropagation coincides with exact probabilistic inference, and the backward values admit an interpretation as Lagrange multipliers of a KL I-projection problem.

1
Introduction

Modern deep learning is most often analyzed through optimization and
dynamical-systems perspectives: stochastic gradient dynamics, curvature and
Hessian spectra, implicit bias, and overparameterization.
These viewpoints describe
how
parameters evolve during training, but
they largely treat the
backpropagation
algorithm itself as a black box—
an instance of reverse-mode automatic differentiation whose internal structure
is rarely examined, despite the fact that this structure determines how
gradients are produced and propagated through the model.

This paper takes a complementary viewpoint.
We study backpropagation as an
operator on messages and beliefs

associated with a computation graph, and we ask whether this operator admits a
geometric description with explicit projection, fixed-point, and stability
structure.
Our starting point is the observation that belief propagation (BP) admits a
precise formulation in Kullback–Leibler (KL) geometry as an alternation of
information projections.
We show that, in two distinct regimes, backpropagation is not merely compatible
with this geometric structure but is determined by it exactly.

1.1
Geometric setting (informal)

Throughout the paper, we work in finite probability simplices equipped with KL
Bregman geometry. The message-passing objects we manipulate—messages, beliefs,
and region tables—are strictly positive probability vectors (elements of
interior simplices), represented in log-coordinates when convenient. All
projections are the associated Bregman
I-projections
and

reverse-KL (M-)projections
induced by the negative-entropy generator.
Precise definitions of the spaces, projection operators, and normalization
conventions are given in
Section

2
.

In the settings considered in this paper, the underlying computation is a
scalar-valued map: either a
C^{1}
deterministic computation

F:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
evaluated at an input
x
, or a network polynomial

S:(0,\infty)^{N}\to(0,\infty)
evaluated at evidence
e
. In both cases, we adopt
a
region view
, standard in variational and projection-based formulations
of message passing

[
9
,
7
]
.
Rather than working with a single global object, inference is expressed in terms
of collections of local objects, each associated with a subset of variables.
Formally, each node
n
carries a
scope

\mathrm{sc}(n)
consisting of the
variables below it, and the
region family

\mathcal{R}
is the multiset of
all such scopes. Consistency between overlapping regions is enforced by

consensus
constraints (equal-scope marginals must agree), while
independence structure is enforced by
factorization
constraints across
decomposed regions. Depending on the setting, the underlying state is either a

single joint object
coordinated by these constraints or a

collection of region-level objects
linked by them.

1.2
Two regimes

We study two regimes.

(A)

A
delta-lifted
factorization of a smooth computation directed acyclic
graph (DAG) with scalar output
z\coloneqq F(x)
.
Each assignment
y=f(\cdot)
is replaced by a Dirac constraint

\delta(y-f(\cdot))
, and a positive
C^{1}
output factor

\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)
is attached.

(B)

A
complete and decomposable sum–product network (SPN)
, i.e., an
arithmetic circuit with strictly positive leaf indicators.
We denote by
\lambda=(\lambda_{i,t})_{i,t}\in(0,\infty)^{N}
the collection of
indicator values associated with variables
X_{i}
taking state
t
, and by
e

the corresponding evidence; throughout, we identify
e
with
\lambda
and
write
e\coloneqq\lambda
, where
N=\sum_{i=1}^{M}|\mathcal{X}_{i}|
.
For fixed positive circuit weights, the SPN defines a scalar multilinear map

S:(0,\infty)^{N}\to(0,\infty),

called the
network polynomial
, whose value at the root node
r
is
denoted
S(e)=S(r)
.
Each node
n
in the circuit has an associated upward value
S(n)\in(0,\infty)

and a downward (reverse-mode) value

D(n)\;\coloneqq\;\frac{\partial S(r)}{\partial S(n)}.

In Regime (A), the region family
\mathcal{R}
encodes the replicated copies
introduced by the delta-lift.
In Regime (B), it is the collection of SPN scopes (sum, product, and singleton
regions).

1.3
Main results (informal)

Regime (A): backpropagation as the differential of a KL projection map.

Normalized belief propagation on the delta-lifted factorization coincides with
the KL projection operator

\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Proj}}\;\coloneq\;\overrightarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}}\circ\overleftarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\mathcal{Q}},

which enforces agreement across replicated variables (i.e., equal-scope copies)
and product structure in dual coordinates (
Proposition

3.1
).
Here
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Proj}}
acts on a strictly positive joint table over the
replicated variables, encoding the current BP messages.
For a single forward evaluation, let
b_{\downarrow}(x)
denote the product of all
downward BP messages entering a variable
x
.
Then, at the forward point,

\nabla_{x}\log b_{\downarrow}(x)\;=\;\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(z)}{\varphi(z)}\,\nabla_{x}z,

so backpropagation is the
Jacobian
of the KL projection operator (after
message extraction) evaluated at the forward point (
Theorem

3.3
).

Regime (B): backpropagation equals exact marginals on SPNs.

In complete and decomposable SPNs, a single forward–backward sweep yields
reverse-mode derivatives that, after normalization by
S(e)
,
equal

exact marginal probabilities. In particular, for each variable
X_{i}
and state

t\in\mathcal{X}_{i}
,

\frac{\partial S(e)}{\partial\lambda_{i,t}}\;=\;\Pr(X_{i}=t\mid e)\,S(e),

with analogous identities for sum-gate children. Thus, one upward and one
downward pass compute all marginals exactly
(
Theorem

4.2
). Equivalently, the forward–backward sweep realizes the two-step KL projection
operator

\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{SPN}}\;\coloneq\;\overrightarrow{\pi}^{f_{\mathcal{R}}}_{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{R}}}\;\circ\;\overleftarrow{\pi}^{f_{\mathcal{R}}}_{\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{R}}},

which enforces scope-consensus followed by scope-factorization induced by
decomposability (
Proposition

4.4
).

1.4
Additional consequences

Regime (A).

Identifying backpropagation with the differential of the KL projection map

\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Proj}}
yields quantitative guarantees beyond the exact
identity of
Theorem

3.3
.
In particular, we:
(i) bound the error of backprop adjoints obtained after finitely many
Walsh–Regalia projection steps in terms of the distance to an effectively
acyclic factorization (
Theorem

3.6
);
(ii) construct convergent surrogate backprop gradients by replacing the KL
generator with a symmetric (Mahalanobis) Bregman divergence
(
Proposition

3.7
); and
(iii) make explicit the gauge-invariance of adjoints under state-independent
message rescaling (
Proposition

3.8
).
These results are stated and proved in
Section

3.4
.

Regime (B).

In the SPN setting, downward values admit a convex-analytic interpretation:
backpropagation computes the Lagrange multipliers (shadow prices) enforcing the
consensus and factorization constraints of the underlying KL I-projection
(
Theorem

4.12
).
Moreover, the map from log-evidence to log-derivatives is locally Lipschitz, with
constants controlled by the circuit structure and local parameter ranges
(
Proposition

4.13
).

1.5
A boundary case: when belief propagation truly generalizes backpropagation

Although this paper focuses on backpropagation as a deterministic operator, it
is useful to isolate the precise setting in which belief propagation
(sum–product) provides a genuine probabilistic extension of backpropagation.

If the delta-lift is augmented with a proper prior
p(x)
on the inputs and a
proper likelihood
\ell:\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)
at the scalar output

z=z(x,\theta)
, then on tree-structured graphs belief propagation computes

posterior-expected
sensitivities:

\nabla_{\theta}\log p(y_{\mathrm{obs}})\;=\;\mathbb{E}_{p(x\mid y_{\mathrm{obs}};\theta)}\!\left[s(z)\,\partial_{\theta}z(x,\theta)\right],\qquad s(z)\coloneq\nabla_{z}\log\ell(z).

Here the expectation is with respect to the exact posterior distribution

p(x\mid y_{\mathrm{obs}};\theta)
.
In the Dirac-prior limit
p(x)=\delta_{x^{*}}
, this reduces to the algebraic
delta-lift identity of Regime (A) (
Theorem

3.9
), cleanly
separating the algebraic correspondence from the probabilistic statement valid
for normalized tree models.

2
Preliminaries and notation

For a scalar function
h
,
\nabla h
denotes the gradient; for vector-valued

F
,
JF
denotes the Jacobian. The vector
\mathbf{1}
denotes an all-ones
vector of appropriate dimension. We write
a\propto b
to indicate equality up
to a positive, state-independent constant, followed by normalization to land
in a simplex.

Probability simplices and interiors.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a finite set with
|\mathcal{X}|=n
. We identify
distributions on
\mathcal{X}
with vectors in the
n
-simplex

\Delta(\mathcal{X})\;\coloneq\;\big\{{p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}}~\big|~{\mathbf{1}^{\top}p=1}\big\},\qquad\Delta^{\circ}(\mathcal{X})\;\coloneq\;\big\{{p\in\Delta(\mathcal{X})}~\big|~{p_{i}>0~\forall i}\big\}.

Whenever we take logarithms (for messages or beliefs) we work on

\Delta^{\circ}(\mathcal{X})
so that all coordinates are strictly positive.

Bregman divergences and KL geometry.

Let
f:\mathcal{D}\to\mathbb{R}
be strictly convex and differentiable on a convex
domain
\mathcal{D}
. The Bregman divergence is

D_{f}(r,q)\;\coloneq\;f(r)-f(q)-\langle\nabla f(q),\,r-q\rangle.

When
f
is of Legendre type, the Fenchel conjugate
f^{*}
is well-defined,

\nabla f
is a bijection between primal and dual coordinates, and

D_{f}(r,q)=D_{f^{*}}(\nabla f(q),\nabla f(r))
. Throughout, we work in KL geometry with the negative-entropy generator

f(p)\;\coloneq\;\sum_{i}p_{i}\log p_{i},\qquad p\in\Delta^{\circ}(\mathcal{X}),

and conjugate
f^{*}(\theta)=\log\sum_{i}e^{\theta_{i}}
. Then
\nabla f(p)=\bigl(1+\log p_{i}\bigr)_{i}
,
\nabla f^{*}(\theta)=\mathrm{softmax}(\theta)\in\Delta^{\circ}(\mathcal{X})
, and
D_{f}(r,q)=\mathrm{KL}(r\|q)
is the Kullback–Leibler divergence.

Left (I-) and right (reverse-KL) projections.

Let
C\subseteq\Delta(\mathcal{X})
be nonempty, convex, and closed in the
relative topology of the simplex, and write
\widehat{C}=\nabla f(C)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

for its image in dual coordinates. We use:

•

the
left
(I-) projection

\overleftarrow{\pi}^{f}_{C}(q)\;\in\;\arg\min_{r\in C}D_{f}(r,q)\;=\;\arg\min_{r\in C}\mathrm{KL}(r\|q);

•

the
right
projection

\overrightarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\widehat{C}}(q)\;\coloneq\;\nabla f^{*}\!\Bigl(\arg\min_{u\in\widehat{C}}D_{f^{*}}\bigl(u,\nabla f(q)\bigr)\Bigr).

When
\widehat{C}=\nabla f(C)
,
\overrightarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\widehat{C}}(q)
is the usual
reverse-KL
M
-projection of
q
onto
C
expressed in dual coordinates

[
3
]
. For KL, both projections
are unique on
\Delta^{\circ}(\mathcal{X})
by strict convexity of
f
.

Product families.

For variables
X_{1},\dots,X_{M}
with finite alphabets
\mathcal{X}_{i}
, write

\Omega\coloneq\prod_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{X}_{i}
and

\Delta(\Omega)
for the joint simplex. The (nonconvex)
product
family
is

\mathcal{C}\;\coloneq\;\left\{{\prod_{i=1}^{M}q_{i}(x_{i})\;}~\Big|~{\;q_{i}\in\Delta^{\circ}(\mathcal{X}_{i})}\right\}\;\subset\;\Delta^{\circ}(\Omega),

and we write
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}=\nabla f(\mathcal{C})\subset\mathbb{R}^{|\Omega|}

for its image in dual coordinates. Right (reverse–KL) projections onto such sets enforce product structure by
mapping a joint object to the closest element of the product family. Variants of this
construction encode
factorization constraints and are denoted as
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}
, in Regime (A)
and Regime (B), respectively.

Factor graphs and normalized BP.

A factor graph is a bipartite graph
G=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})

with variable nodes
X_{i}\in\mathcal{V}
, factor nodes
g\in\mathcal{F}
, and
edges
(g,X_{i})\in\mathcal{E}
when
g
depends on
X_{i}
. Each factor

g:\prod_{i\in\mathsf{nb}(g)}\mathcal{X}_{i}\to(0,\infty)
is strictly positive,
and
\mathsf{nb}(u)
denotes the neighbors of
u
.

A
message
on edge
(g,X_{i})
is a vector

m_{g\to i}\in\Delta^{\circ}(\mathcal{X}_{i})
(similarly
m_{i\to g}
). The
normalized sum–product/BP updates are

\displaystyle m_{g\to i}(x_{i})

\displaystyle\propto\sum_{x_{\mathsf{nb}(g)\setminus i}}g(x_{\mathsf{nb}(g)})\prod_{j\in\mathsf{nb}(g)\setminus i}m_{j\to g}(x_{j}),

\displaystyle m_{i\to g}(x_{i})

\displaystyle\propto\prod_{h\in\mathsf{nb}(i)\setminus g}m_{h\to i}(x_{i}),

followed by normalization in
\Delta^{\circ}(\mathcal{X}_{i})
. The
belief

at
X_{i}
is

b_{i}(x_{i})\;\propto\;\prod_{g\in\mathsf{nb}(i)}m_{g\to i}(x_{i})\;\in\;\Delta^{\circ}(\mathcal{X}_{i}).

Messages are defined only up to positive, state-independent per-edge scalings;
all of our identities use
log-derivatives
, which are invariant to such
gauge transformations.

Consensus sets.

When a variable
X
is replicated into
K
copies

X^{(1)},\dots,X^{(K)}
with common alphabet
\mathcal{X}
, the
consensus
set
is the diagonal face

\mathcal{D}\;\coloneq\;\left\{{q\in\Delta(\Omega^{K})}~\Big|~{q(x^{(1)},\dots,x^{(K)})=0\ \text{unless}\ x^{(1)}=\cdots=x^{(K)}}\right\}.

The set
\mathcal{D}
is a closed convex face of
\Delta(\Omega^{K})
.
The I-projection
\overleftarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\mathcal{D}}
enforces equality by zeroing
off-diagonal mass and renormalizing on the diagonal. Variants of this
construction (denoted
\mathcal{Q}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{R}}
) encode
scope-wise equality constraints in Regime (A)
and Regime (B), respectively.

Regime (A): Delta-lifted backpropagation as the differential of a KL projection algorithm

3.1
Projection algorithm: BP as a hybrid of KL projections

Let
G=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})
be a discrete factor graph with strictly
positive factors. Following the Walsh–Regalia replication
construction
[
8
]
, let
\Omega
be the finite product alphabet of a
single replica of the variables, so that joint tables live on
\Omega^{K}
for
some replication index
K
. Let
\mathcal{Q}\subset\Delta(\Omega^{K})
be the
diagonal face and let
\Delta^{\circ}(\mathcal{Q})
denote the
relative
interior
of this face, i.e., distributions supported on the diagonal with
strictly positive diagonal entries. Let
\mathcal{P}
be the (nonconvex) family
of product distributions on the replicated variables in the sense
of
[
8
]
, and define its dual-coordinate image

\widehat{\mathcal{P}}\coloneq\nabla f(\mathcal{P})\subset\mathbb{R}^{|\Omega|^{K}}
.

Projections with faces.

The I-projection
\overleftarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\mathcal{Q}}
maps
\Delta^{\circ}(\Omega^{K})
into

\Delta^{\circ}(\mathcal{Q})
by zeroing off-diagonal mass and renormalizing on the
diagonal. Since elements of
\Delta^{\circ}(\mathcal{Q})
have zeros outside the
diagonal, we interpret dual coordinates on
\mathcal{Q}
using the
relative
gradient
of
f
restricted to the affine hull of
\mathcal{Q}
. Equivalently, we
may identify
\Delta(\mathcal{Q})
with the simplex on the diagonal outcomes and
use the same entropy generator on that smaller simplex. With this convention,
the right projection
\overrightarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}}
is well-defined on

\Delta^{\circ}(\mathcal{Q})
.

For
q\in\Delta^{\circ}(\Omega^{K})
define the two-step operator

\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Proj}}(q)\;\coloneq\;\overrightarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}}\!\big(\,\overleftarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\mathcal{Q}}(q)\,\big).

(1)

This operator first enforces consensus (onto
\mathcal{Q}
), then enforces
product structure (onto
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}
in dual coordinates).

Walsh–Regalia hybrid scheme.

Let
q_{-1}\in\Delta^{\circ}(\Omega^{K})
be the normalized product of the replicated
factor tables. Walsh and Regalia
[
8
, Thm. 1]
give a Dykstra-like
hybrid scheme:

\displaystyle k_{n}

\displaystyle=\overrightarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}}\!\Big(\nabla f^{*}\big(\nabla f(q_{n-1})+\sigma_{n-1}\big)\Big),

(2)

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}

\displaystyle=\nabla f(q_{n-1})+\sigma_{n-1}-\nabla f(k_{n}),

(3)

\displaystyle r_{n}

\displaystyle=\overleftarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\mathcal{Q}}\!\Big(\nabla f^{*}\big(\nabla f(k_{n})+\tau_{n-1}\big)\Big),

(4)

\displaystyle q_{n}

\displaystyle=\overrightarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}}(r_{n}),

(5)

\displaystyle\tau_{n}

\displaystyle=\nabla f(k_{n})+\tau_{n-1}-\nabla f(q_{n}),

(6)

initialized with
\sigma_{-1}=\mathbf{0}
and
\tau_{-1}=\mathbf{0}
.

Proposition 3.1

(Normalized BP as a hybrid of KL projections
[
8
, Thm. 1]
)
.

For a finite factor graph
G
with strictly positive factors and initialization
q_{-1}
as above, the iterates of
(
2
)–(
6
) coincide with normalized sum–product (BP) on
G
.
In particular:

(i)

if
G
is a tree, one upward–downward BP pass computes
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Proj}}(z_{-1})
, and the resulting beliefs are exact marginals;

(ii)

any fixed point of normalized BP is a fixed point of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Proj}}
.

3.2
Delta factors and a message-level chain rule

We now pass to the smooth delta-lift. Let
y=\psi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})
with
\psi\in C^{1}
on an open set

\mathcal{U}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{r}}
, and consider the deterministic factor

g(y,x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\;=\;\delta\big(y-\psi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\big).

Assume the incoming message
m_{y\to g}(y)
is strictly positive and
C^{1}
near the forward value
y^{*}
, and that all other arguments
x_{-j}
are clamped to
x_{-j}^{*}
.

Lemma 3.2

(Delta-factor chain rule)
.

Under these assumptions,

\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\log m_{g\to x_{j}}(x_{j})\right|_{x_{j}^{*}}\;=\;\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\log m_{y\to g}(y)\right|_{y^{*}}\cdot\left.\frac{\partial y}{\partial x_{j}}\right|_{x^{*}}.

Proof
. By the continuous factor-to-variable update,

m_{g\to x_{j}}(x_{j})\;\propto\;\int\delta\big(y-\psi(x_{j},x_{-j}^{*})\big)\,m_{y\to g}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y=m_{y\to g}\!\big(\psi(x_{j},x_{-j}^{*})\big).

Differentiating
\log m_{g\to x_{j}}
and evaluating at the forward point gives the claim.

\hfill\quad\blacksquare

3.3
Backpropagation as the differential of a KL projection map

Let
z
denote the scalar output node of the computation DAG, and let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)
be a
C^{1}
output factor.
Run one upward pass (collapsing messages at delta factors to Dirac masses at forward values) and one downward pass seeded by

m_{\varphi\to z}(z)\;=\;\varphi(z).

For each variable
v
define the downward belief

b_{\downarrow}(v)\;\coloneq\;\prod_{g\in\mathrm{nb}(v)\cap\text{downstream}}m_{g\to v}(v),\qquad s(v)\;\coloneq\;\left.\partial_{v}\log b_{\downarrow}(v)\right|_{v^{*}}.

Theorem 3.3

(Backpropagation as log-derivatives of downward BP messages)
.

With the setup above,
s(z)=\varphi^{\prime}(z^{*})/\varphi(z^{*})
, and for any variable
x
,

\left.\nabla_{x}\log b_{\downarrow}(x)\right|_{x^{*}}\;=\;\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(z^{*})}{\varphi(z^{*})}\ \left.\nabla_{x}z\right|_{x^{*}}.

Equivalently, for
\varphi(z)=e^{\alpha z}
one has

\nabla_{x}\log b_{\downarrow}(x)=\alpha\,\nabla_{x}z
at the forward point
x^{*}
.

Proof
. At each deterministic factor
g
corresponding to
y=\psi(\cdot)
,
Lemma

3.2
yields

\partial_{x}\log m_{g\to x}\;=\;\partial_{y}\log m_{y\to g}\cdot\partial_{x}y\quad\text{at the forward point}.

Since
b_{\downarrow}(x)
is the product of all factor-to-variable messages into
x
,

s(x)\;=\;\partial_{x}\log b_{\downarrow}(x)\;=\;\sum_{y\in\mathrm{Ch}(x)}s(y)\,\partial_{x}y,

where
\mathrm{Ch}(x)
denotes the children of
x
in the computation DAG, with base case
s(z)=\varphi^{\prime}(z^{*})/\varphi(z^{*})
from
m_{\varphi\to z}=\varphi
.
This is exactly the reverse-mode recursion for
\nabla_{x}z
, multiplied by
\varphi^{\prime}(z^{*})/\varphi(z^{*})
.
Reverse induction along a topological order of the DAG gives the result.

\hfill\quad\blacksquare

Remark 3.4

(Proper likelihoods)
.

If
\varphi(z)=e^{-L(z)/T}
with
L\in C^{1}
and
T>0
, then
s(z)=-L^{\prime}(z^{*})/T
and

\nabla_{x}\log b_{\downarrow}(x)\;=\;-\frac{1}{T}L^{\prime}(z^{*})\,\nabla_{x}z\quad\text{at }x^{*},

the usual backpropagation gradient for the scalar loss
L(z)
, scaled by
1/T
.

Remark 3.5

(Differential of the KL projection map)
.

By
Proposition

3.1
, normalized BP on the delta-lifted factor graph applies

\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Proj}}=\overrightarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}}\circ\overleftarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\mathcal{Q}}
.

Theorem

3.3
states that, at the forward point and in log-coordinates,
the Jacobian of the map that sends the output seed
\varphi
through

\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Proj}}
and reads off downward messages agrees (up to the scalar
\varphi^{\prime}(z^{*})/\varphi(z^{*})
)
with the reverse-mode adjoint map. In this sense backpropagation is the

differential
of the KL projection map that implements BP on the delta-lift.

3.4
New consequences for backprop from the KL projection connection

We now record quantitative and structural consequences of identifying
backpropagation with the differential of the KL projection map

\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Proj}}
.

Notation.

Let
q_{-1}\in\Delta^{\circ}(\Omega^{K})
be the normalized product of the replicated
factor tables, and write

\zeta_{-1}\;\coloneq\;\nabla f(q_{-1})

for its log-coordinates.
Let
\mathcal{F}
denote the union of linear subspaces in log-coordinates
corresponding to (effectively) acyclic factorizations, as characterized in

[
8
]
.
For
L\in\mathbb{N}
, let
C_{L}
and
\gamma
be the constants from

[
8
, Prop. 1, Thm. 3]
controlling the deviation between
the length-
L
Walsh–Regalia iteration and the two-step projection

\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Proj}}
in log-coordinates.

For a variable
x
, define

g^{(L)}(x)\ \coloneq\ \nabla_{x}\log b_{\downarrow}^{(L)}(x),\qquad g^{\star}(x)\ \coloneq\ \frac{\varphi^{\prime}(z^{*})}{\varphi(z^{*})}\,\nabla_{x}z,

where
b_{\downarrow}^{(L)}
is the product of downward messages formed after

L
Walsh–Regalia outer iterations, and
g^{\star}
is the exact backprop adjoint
from
Theorem

3.3
.

Theorem 3.6

(Finite-pass accuracy of backprop via WR iterations)
.

Assume the setup of
Sections

3.2
and
3.1
.
Fix
L\in\mathbb{N}
and suppose

\mathrm{dist}(\zeta_{-1},\mathcal{F})\leq\delta,

where

denotes Euclidean distance in

(effectively acyclic factorization), then

for all

, and a single forward/backward pass
suffices.

Proof
. By
[

\|\zeta^{(L)}-\zeta^{\star}\|\leq(C_{L}+\gamma)\delta

and

are the log-coordinates of the

The map

The claim follows by the mean-value inequality.

3.5
Probabilistic generalization on trees

The algebraic identity of
Theorem

3.3
does not, by itself, define a
normalized probabilistic model: the delta-lift uses singular constraints and
the output factor
\varphi
need not be integrable.
When the delta-lift is augmented with proper priors on inputs and a proper
likelihood at the output, a genuine probabilistic generalization becomes
available. In this setting, backpropagation is replaced by

posterior-expected sensitivities
.

Theorem 3.9

(Posterior-expected sensitivities on trees)
.

Let
x
denote all non-output variables and let
\theta\in\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be
a parameter.
Assume:

(a)

p(x)
is a proper prior density on
x
that does not depend on
\theta
;

(b)

z=z(x,\theta)
is a
C^{1}
map of
(x,\theta)
into
\mathbb{R}
;

(c)

the likelihood
\ell:\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)
is
C^{1}
, and we write

s(z)\coloneq\nabla_{z}\log\ell(z)
;

(d)

the marginal likelihood

p(y_{\mathrm{obs}};\theta)\;=\;\int\ell\big(z(x,\theta)\big)\,p(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

is finite for all
\theta\in\Theta
;

(e)

differentiation under the integral sign is justified, i.e., for each

\theta\in\Theta
there exists an integrable function
h_{\theta}(x)
such that

\big\|\nabla_{\theta}\big(\ell(z(x,\theta))\,p(x)\big)\big\|\;\leq\;h_{\theta}(x)\quad\text{for a.e.\ }x,\qquad\int h_{\theta}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

Assume further that the factor graph induced by
p(x)
and

\ell(z(x,\theta))
is a tree.
Then

\nabla_{\theta}\log p(y_{\mathrm{obs}};\theta)\;=\;\mathbb{E}\Big[s\big(z(x,\theta)\big)\,\partial_{\theta}z(x,\theta)\,\Big|\,y_{\mathrm{obs}}\Big],

where the expectation is taken with respect to the exact posterior

p(x\mid y_{\mathrm{obs}};\theta)
.
Moreover, on a tree this posterior expectation is computed exactly by a single
upward–downward sum–product (BP) pass, equivalently by one application of the
two-step KL operator

\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Proj}}=\overrightarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}}\circ\overleftarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\mathcal{Q}}
applied to the normalized model.

Proof
. By definition,

p(y_{\mathrm{obs}};\theta)\;=\;\int\ell\big(z(x,\theta)\big)\,p(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

By assumption (e) and the dominated convergence theorem,

\nabla_{\theta}p(y_{\mathrm{obs}};\theta)\;=\;\int\nabla_{\theta}\Big(\ell\big(z(x,\theta)\big)\,p(x)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\;=\;\int\ell\big(z(x,\theta)\big)\,s\big(z(x,\theta)\big)\,\partial_{\theta}z(x,\theta)\,p(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Dividing by
p(y_{\mathrm{obs}};\theta)
yields

\nabla_{\theta}\log p(y_{\mathrm{obs}};\theta)\;=\;\frac{\displaystyle\int\ell\big(z(x,\theta)\big)\,p(x)\,s\big(z(x,\theta)\big)\,\partial_{\theta}z(x,\theta)\,\mathrm{d}x}{\displaystyle\int\ell\big(z(x,\theta)\big)\,p(x)\,\mathrm{d}x},

which is exactly the posterior expectation stated.

For the second claim, note that on a tree a single sum–product pass computes
all exact posterior marginals of
p(x\mid y_{\mathrm{obs}};\theta)
.
The integrand

s(z(x,\theta))\,\partial_{\theta}z(x,\theta)
decomposes as a sum of local terms
associated with nodes and factors of the computation graph.
Hence the posterior expectation can be evaluated exactly from these marginals
in one upward–downward pass.
In the discrete setting, this pass coincides with one application of the
Walsh–Regalia two-step KL operator

\overrightarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}}\circ\overleftarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\mathcal{Q}}
applied to the
normalized model.

\hfill\quad\blacksquare

Corollary 3.10

(Dirac-prior specialization and recovery of the algebraic identity)
.

Under the assumptions of
Theorem

3.9
, suppose instead that the
prior on
x
is a Dirac measure
\delta_{x^{*}}
at a forward configuration
x^{*}
.
Then the posterior is also a Dirac measure at
x^{*}
and

\nabla_{\theta}\log p(y_{\mathrm{obs}};\theta)\;=\;s\big(z(x^{*},\theta)\big)\,\partial_{\theta}z(x^{*},\theta).

If we choose an output factor
\varphi
such that

\varphi^{\prime}(z^{*})/\varphi(z^{*})=s(z^{*})
, this recovers exactly the algebraic identity of

Theorem

3.3
.

Proof
. If
p(x)=\delta_{x^{*}}
, then

p(y_{\mathrm{obs}};\theta)\;=\;\ell\big(z(x^{*},\theta)\big),

and therefore

\nabla_{\theta}\log p(y_{\mathrm{obs}};\theta)\;=\;\nabla_{\theta}\log\ell\big(z(x^{*},\theta)\big)\;=\;s\big(z(x^{*},\theta)\big)\,\partial_{\theta}z(x^{*},\theta)

by the chain rule.
Since the posterior is supported at
x^{*}
, the posterior expectation in

Theorem

3.9
reduces to point evaluation.
Choosing
\varphi
so that
\varphi^{\prime}(z^{*})/\varphi(z^{*})=s(z^{*})
yields

Theorem

3.3
.

\hfill\quad\blacksquare

4
Regime (B): Sum–product circuits — backpropagation equals exact marginals and realizes a KL I-projection

Let
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{M}\}
be discrete variables with finite alphabets
\mathcal{X}_{i}
and joint space
\Omega\;\coloneq\;\prod_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{X}_{i}.
As in
Section

2
, distributions on
\Omega
live in the simplex
\Delta(\Omega)
with interior
\Delta^{\circ}(\Omega)
. Throughout this section, we assume strictly positive objects (all indicators and weights are
>0
), so that every normalization and logarithm is well-defined.

4.1
Sum–product networks and upward/downward values

An SPN is a DAG
G=(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{E})
whose internal nodes partition into
sum
nodes
\mathsf{Sum}
and
product
nodes
\mathsf{Prod}
, with leaf nodes
\mathcal{L}
being indicator functions of the form
\mathbf{1}[X_{i}=t]
for
t\in\mathcal{X}_{i}
.
Each node
n\in\mathcal{N}
has a
scope

\mathrm{sc}(n)\subseteq\{1,\ldots,M\}
(the set of variables appearing below
n
).
We impose the standard structural assumptions:

•

Completeness:
for every
s\in\mathsf{Sum}
and
c\in\mathrm{ch}(s)
,
\mathrm{sc}(c)=\mathrm{sc}(s)
.

•

Decomposability:
for every
p\in\mathsf{Prod}
and distinct
c,c^{\prime}\in\mathrm{ch}(p)
,

\mathrm{sc}(c)\cap\mathrm{sc}(c^{\prime})=\varnothing
.

•

Positive weights:
each edge
(s\to c)
from a sum has a weight
w_{sc}\in(0,\infty)
.
When
\sum_{c}w_{sc}=1
we say the sum is
normalized
.

Soft evidence on
X_{i}
is encoded by indicator leaves taking strictly positive values

\lambda_{i,t}\in(0,\infty)
for
t\in\mathcal{X}_{i}
.
We interpret
\lambda_{i,t}
as a unary evidence potential (or likelihood term) for the event
X_{i}=t
.
Hard evidence is recovered by setting
\lambda_{i,t}\in\{0,1\}
and working on the induced support (relative interiors).
In this general soft-evidence regime, posterior marginals are obtained from
log-derivatives
:

\Pr(X_{i}=t\mid e)=\frac{\partial\log S(e)}{\partial\log\lambda_{i,t}}=\frac{\lambda_{i,t}}{S(e)}\frac{\partial S(e)}{\partial\lambda_{i,t}}.

The
network polynomial

S(e)
is defined by the usual bottom-up evaluation rules
[
4
,
6
]
:

\displaystyle\text{Leaf }\ell=\mathbf{1}[X_{i}=t]:

\displaystyle\quad S(\ell)=\lambda_{i,t},

\displaystyle\text{Product }p:

\displaystyle\quad S(p)=\prod_{c\in\mathrm{ch}(p)}S(c),

\displaystyle\text{Sum }s:

\displaystyle\quad S(s)=\sum_{c\in\mathrm{ch}(s)}w_{sc}\,S(c).

Let
r
be the root. We write
S(e)\;\coloneq\;S(r)\;>\;0.

Downward (reverse-mode) values.

Define the
downward value
at each node
n
by

D(n)\;\coloneq\;\frac{\partial S(r)}{\partial S(n)},\qquad D(r)\equiv 1.

Under our standing positivity assumptions,
S(n)>0
for all nodes and hence all
partial derivatives are well-defined; in particular
D(n)>0
for all
n
(see

Lemma

4.6
).
At each sum node
s
we define a
local gating distribution

b_{s}\in\Delta^{\circ}(\mathrm{ch}(s))
, and at each variable
X_{i}
a
variable belief

b_{i}\in\Delta^{\circ}(\mathcal{X}_{i})
; explicit formulas are given in

Theorem

4.2
.

4.2
Local reverse-mode rules

The reverse-mode rules on an SPN are local and follow directly from the chain rule.

Lemma 4.1

(Local reverse-mode rules)
.

Let
s\in\mathsf{Sum}
and
p\in\mathsf{Prod}
.

(a)

For a sum node
s
and child
c\in\mathrm{ch}(s)
,
D_{s\to c}(c)\;=\;D(s)\,w_{sc}.

(b)

For a product node
p
and child
c\in\mathrm{ch}(p)
,
D_{p\to c}(c)\;=\;D(p)\!\!\!\prod_{c^{\prime}\in\mathrm{ch}(p)\setminus\{c\}}\!\!\!S(c^{\prime}).

Consequently,

D(c)\;=\;\sum_{s\in\mathrm{pa}(c)\cap\mathsf{Sum}}D(s)\,w_{sc}\;+\;\sum_{p\in\mathrm{pa}(c)\cap\mathsf{Prod}}D(p)\!\!\!\prod_{c^{\prime}\in\mathrm{ch}(p)\setminus\{c\}}\!\!\!S(c^{\prime}).

Proof
. By definition,

D_{s\to c}(c)\;=\;\frac{\partial S(r)}{\partial S(s)}\,\frac{\partial S(s)}{\partial S(c)}\;=\;D(s)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial S(c)}\Big(\sum_{c^{\prime}}w_{sc^{\prime}}S(c^{\prime})\Big)\;=\;D(s)\,w_{sc},

which proves (a).
Similarly,

D_{p\to c}(c)\;=\;\frac{\partial S(r)}{\partial S(p)}\,\frac{\partial S(p)}{\partial S(c)}\;=\;D(p)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial S(c)}\Big(\prod_{c^{\prime}}S(c^{\prime})\Big)\;=\;D(p)\!\!\!\prod_{c^{\prime}\neq c}S(c^{\prime}),

which proves (b).
Summing edge contributions over parents of
c
gives the stated formula for
D(c)
.

\hfill\quad\blacksquare

4.3
Derivatives equal marginals

We now recall Darwiche’s differential semantics
[
4
]
specialized to SPNs and record the formulas we use.

Theorem 4.2

(Log-derivatives equal marginals)
.

Assume the SPN is complete and decomposable with positive weights and positive indicators.

(a)

Variable marginals (soft evidence).

For each
i
and state
t\in\mathcal{X}_{i}
,

\Pr(X_{i}=t\mid e)\;=\;\frac{\lambda_{i,t}}{S(e)}\,\frac{\partial S(e)}{\partial\lambda_{i,t}}\;=\;\frac{\partial\log S(e)}{\partial\log\lambda_{i,t}}\in(0,1).

Equivalently, the variable belief
b_{i}\in\Delta^{\circ}(\mathcal{X}_{i})
defined by

b_{i}(t)\;\coloneq\;\frac{\lambda_{i,t}}{S(e)}\,\frac{\partial S(e)}{\partial\lambda_{i,t}}

equals this marginal.

(b)

Gate marginals at sums.

For each sum node
s
and child
c\in\mathrm{ch}(s)
,

\Pr(\text{parse visits }s\text{ and selects }c\mid e)\;=\;\frac{D(s)\,w_{sc}\,S(c)}{S(e)}.

Moreover, the local gating distribution
b_{s}\in\Delta^{\circ}(\mathrm{ch}(s))
given by

b_{s}(c)\;\coloneq\;\frac{w_{sc}\,S(c)}{S(s)}

is the conditional distribution of the gate given that the parse visits
s
, and

\Pr(\text{visit }s\mid e)\;=\;\frac{D(s)S(s)}{S(e)},\qquad\Pr(\text{select }c\mid e,\text{visit }s)\;=\;b_{s}(c).

Consequently, one upward pass computing the values
\{S(n)\}
and one reverse pass computing
\{D(n)\}
recover all variable marginals and all sum-gate quantities above.

Remark 4.3

(Beliefs as normalized derivatives)
.

The beliefs
b_{i}
and
b_{s}
are obtained from circuit derivatives as follows:

b_{i}(t)=\frac{\lambda_{i,t}}{S(e)}\,\frac{\partial S(e)}{\partial\lambda_{i,t}}
(equivalently, a log-derivative),
and
b_{s}(c)=\frac{w_{sc}S(c)}{S(s)}
is a local conditional gate distribution at sums.
These coincide with the beliefs obtained by running sum–product on the circuit viewed as a factor graph with unary evidence potentials.

4.4
Projection interpretation: a two-step KL I-projection

We now express the upward–downward computation as a two-step projection in KL geometry, parallel to the construction in Regime (A).

Region family and ambient space.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be the multiset of scopes
\mathcal{R}\;\coloneq\;\big\{{\mathrm{sc}(n)}~\big|~{n\in\mathcal{N}}\big\}
, and for
R\in\mathcal{R}
write
\mathcal{X}_{R}\coloneq\prod_{i\in R}\mathcal{X}_{i}
.
We work on the product space of region tables

\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{R}}\;\coloneq\;\prod_{R\in\mathcal{R}}\Delta^{\circ}(\mathcal{X}_{R}).

On
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{R}}
we use the separable negative-entropy generator

f_{\mathcal{R}}(q)\;\coloneq\;\sum_{R\in\mathcal{R}}\sum_{x_{R}\in\mathcal{X}_{R}}q_{R}(x_{R})\log q_{R}(x_{R}),\qquad q=(q_{R})_{R\in\mathcal{R}}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{R}},

so that the associated Bregman divergence is the sum of KL divergences,

D_{f_{\mathcal{R}}}(r,q)=\sum_{R\in\mathcal{R}}\mathrm{KL}(r_{R}\|q_{R})
.

Product constraints.

Define the
product family

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}\subset\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{R}}
by

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}\;\coloneq\;\left\{{q\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{R}}}~\Big|~{q_{\mathrm{sc}(p)}=\bigotimes_{c\in\mathrm{ch}(p)}q_{\mathrm{sc}(c)}\ \text{for all }p\in\mathsf{Prod}}\right\}.

In log-coordinates
\theta_{R}=\nabla f(q_{R})=1+\log q_{R}
, these constraints are affine
(up to additive normalizations), hence
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{R}}\coloneq\nabla f_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}})
is an affine set in dual coordinates.

Consensus constraints.

Define the
consensus set

\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{R}}\subset\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{R}}
by requiring that equal-scope copies agree:
whenever two indices
R,R^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}
represent the same scope (e.g., along a sum edge
(s\to c)
with
\mathrm{sc}(s)=\mathrm{sc}(c)
or due to sharing), we impose
q_{R}=q_{R^{\prime}}
.

Two-step KL operator.

Let
z_{-1}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{R}}
denote the collection of local region
tables. Define

\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{SPN}}(z_{-1})\;\coloneq\;\overrightarrow{\pi}^{f_{\mathcal{R}}}_{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{R}}}\Bigl(\overleftarrow{\pi}^{f_{\mathcal{R}}}_{\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{R}}}(z_{-1})\Bigr).

(7)

Proposition 4.4

(SPN pass realizes the KL two-step projection)
.

Let the SPN be complete and decomposable with positive weights and evidence.
Then the region marginals encoded by the beliefs of

Theorem

4.2
coincide with the region marginals of

\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{SPN}}(z_{-1})
.
In particular, for SPN trees (no DAG sharing),

\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{SPN}}
reduces to the Walsh–Regalia map

\overrightarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}}\circ\overleftarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\mathcal{Q}}

on the associated factor graph
[
8
]
.

Proof
. For an SPN tree (no sharing), the induced factor graph on region variables is a
tree, and the constraint sets
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{R}}
and

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}
coincide with the Walsh–Regalia consensus and product
sets.
By
Proposition

3.1
, one upward–downward sum–product pass computes

\overrightarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}}\circ\overleftarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\mathcal{Q}}
, whose exact marginals
agree with the beliefs of
Theorem

4.2
.

For a general DAG SPN, unrolling shared subgraphs yields an equivalent SPN tree
with preserved completeness and decomposability.
Beliefs on the DAG coincide with the corresponding marginals on the unrolled
tree, and the two-step projection on the tree produces a unique family of region
marginals.
Identifying equal-scope copies recovers the DAG marginals, which therefore agree
with
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{SPN}}(z_{-1})
.

\hfill\quad\blacksquare

Remark 4.5

(Messages, projections, and beliefs)
.

Upward values provide the sufficient statistics for the right projection

\overrightarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{R}}}
, encoding products across
decomposed scopes.
Downward values act as Lagrange multipliers enforcing consensus, corresponding to
the I-projection onto
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{R}}
.
Normalizing these quantities yields the beliefs of

Theorem

4.2
.

4.5
Consequences and caveats

We collect some structural properties that will be used later.

Lemma 4.6

(Positivity and interior beliefs)
.

Assume completeness and decomposability with strictly positive weights
w_{sc}>0
and strictly positive indicators
\lambda_{i,t}>0
.
Then for every node
n
,

S(n)>0,\qquad D(n)>0.

Consequently,
b_{i}\in\Delta^{\circ}(\mathcal{X}_{i})
and
b_{s}\in\Delta^{\circ}(\mathrm{ch}(s))
for all
i
and all sum nodes
s
.

Proof
. We prove positivity of
S
by induction bottom-up.
At leaves,
S(\ell)=\lambda_{i,t}>0
.
Products of positive numbers are positive, and weighted sums with positive weights are positive, so
S(n)>0
for all
n
.

For
D
, we have
D(r)=1>0
.
Suppose
D(u)>0
for all ancestors
u
of
n
.
For any parent
s\in\mathrm{pa}(n)\cap\mathsf{Sum}
,
Lemma

4.1
(a) gives

D_{s\to n}(n)=D(s)w_{sn}>0
.
For any parent
p\in\mathrm{pa}(n)\cap\mathsf{Prod}
,
Lemma

4.1
(b) and positivity of the
S
-values give

D_{p\to n}(n)=D(p)\prod_{c^{\prime}\neq n}S(c^{\prime})>0
.
Summing over parents yields
D(n)>0
.
The formulas for
b_{i}
and
b_{s}
in
Theorem

4.2
are ratios of positive quantities and hence lie in the interiors.

\hfill\quad\blacksquare

Proposition 4.7

(Uniqueness of the KL projections in
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{SPN}}
)
.

Under the assumptions of
Lemma

4.6
:

(a)

\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{R}}
is a closed convex subset of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{R}}
; hence the I-projection

\overleftarrow{\pi}^{f_{\mathcal{R}}}_{\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{R}}}
is unique.

(b)

\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{R}}
is an affine set in dual coordinates; hence the right projection

\overrightarrow{\pi}^{f_{\mathcal{R}}}_{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{R}}}
is unique.

Proof
. (a) The constraints defining
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{R}}
are linear equalities of region marginals across duplicated scopes.
The intersection of an affine subspace with the simplex is convex and closed, hence
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{R}}
is convex and closed.
Strict convexity of KL in its first argument then implies uniqueness of the minimizer of
D_{f}(\cdot,z_{-1})
over
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{R}}
.

(b) Let
q\in\mathsf{Prod}_{\mathcal{R}}
and write
\theta=\nabla f(q)
.
For negative entropy,
\theta_{R}
is (up to an additive constant) the vector of
\log q_{R}
.
The constraint
q_{\mathrm{sc}(p)}=\bigotimes_{c\in\mathrm{ch}(p)}q_{\mathrm{sc}(c)}
becomes

\theta_{\mathrm{sc}(p)}=\sum_{c\in\mathrm{ch}(p)}\theta_{\mathrm{sc}(c)}+c_{p}\mathbf{1},

for some scalar
c_{p}
enforcing normalization.
Thus
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{R}}
is an affine subspace in
\theta
.
The dual Bregman divergence
D_{f^{*}}
is strictly convex in its first argument, so projection of any
\theta
onto this affine subspace is unique.
Mapping back by
\nabla f^{*}
yields uniqueness of
\overrightarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{R}}}
.
Existence and uniqueness of such forward Bregman projections are classical; see, e.g.,
[
2
]
.

\hfill\quad\blacksquare

Proposition 4.8

(Local versus global normalization at sums)
.

For a sum node
s
and child
c\in\mathrm{ch}(s)
define

p_{s}^{\mathrm{glob}}(c)\;\coloneq\;\frac{D(s)\,w_{sc}\,S(c)}{S(e)},\qquad b_{s}(c)\;\coloneq\;\frac{w_{sc}\,S(c)}{S(s)}.

Then

p_{s}^{\mathrm{glob}}(c)\;=\;\pi(s)\,b_{s}(c),\qquad\pi(s)\;\coloneq\;\frac{D(s)\,S(s)}{S(e)}\in(0,1],

and
\pi(s)
is the posterior probability that a parse visits
s
.
In particular,
\sum_{c}p_{s}^{\mathrm{glob}}(c)=\pi(s)
and
\sum_{c}b_{s}(c)=1
.

Proof
. By
Theorem

4.2
(b),

p_{s}^{\mathrm{glob}}(c)=\Pr(Y_{s}=c\mid e)
and
b_{s}(c)
is the conditional probability of choosing child
c
given that a parse has reached
s
.
Thus

\Pr(Y_{s}=c\mid e)\;=\;\Pr(\text{parse visits }s\mid e)\,\Pr(Y_{s}=c\mid e,\text{visit }s),

so
\pi(s)\coloneq\Pr(\text{visit }s\mid e)
satisfies

p_{s}^{\mathrm{glob}}(c)=\pi(s)b_{s}(c)
.
By definition,

\pi(s)\;=\;\sum_{c}\Pr(Y_{s}=c\mid e)\;=\;\sum_{c}\frac{D(s)w_{sc}S(c)}{S(e)}\;=\;\frac{D(s)S(s)}{S(e)}.

The remaining statements follow immediately.

\hfill\quad\blacksquare

Corollary 4.9

(Tree case: equality of local and global gates)
.

Suppose the SPN is a tree (no DAG sharing) and
s
lies on every root–to–leaf path.
Then
\pi(s)=1
and hence

p_{s}^{\mathrm{glob}}(c)\;=\;b_{s}(c)\qquad\text{for all }c\in\mathrm{ch}(s).

Proof
. In a tree SPN, each parse corresponds to a single root–to–leaf path (with one choice at each encountered sum).
If
s
lies on every root–to–leaf path, then every parse visits
s
, hence
\Pr(\text{visit }s\mid e)=1
and therefore
\pi(s)=1
.
The claim follows from
Proposition

4.8
.

\hfill\quad\blacksquare

Proposition 4.10

(Reduction to tree BP and Walsh–Regalia two-step)
.

If the SPN is a tree (no sharing), the operator
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{SPN}}
in (
7
) coincides with the Walsh–Regalia two-step map
\overrightarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}}\circ\overleftarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\mathcal{Q}}
on the associated factor graph, and the upward–downward computation equals standard tree BP.
Moreover, for each sum node
s
the local gate distribution
b_{s}
is the conditional distribution of the gate given that the parse visits
s
, and the corresponding global gate marginals satisfy

p_{s}^{\mathrm{glob}}(c)=\pi(s)\,b_{s}(c)
with
\pi(s)=D(s)S(s)/S(e)
.
In particular, at the root sum node one has
\pi(r)=1
and hence
p_{r}^{\mathrm{glob}}=b_{r}
.

Proof
. As noted in the proof of
Proposition

4.4
, when the SPN is a tree the induced factor graph on region variables is a tree whose consensus/product sets are exactly
\mathcal{Q}
and
\mathcal{P}
in
[
8
]
.
By
Proposition

3.1
, one BP pass computes the two-step projection

\overrightarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}}\circ\overleftarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\mathcal{Q}}
and yields exact marginals.
The equality of gate distributions is
Corollary

4.9
.

\hfill\quad\blacksquare

Remark 4.11

(Hard evidence)
.

If some indicators are zero (hard evidence), the computation restricts to the induced support.
All statements above remain valid after replacing simplices by their relative interiors on that support; strict convexity of KL on each relative interior guarantees uniqueness of the projections in
Proposition

.

4.6
New results on backpropagation

We finally record two structural properties of backpropagation on SPNs in the projection language.

Theorem 4.12

(KKT interpretation of SPN backpropagation)
.

Consider the composite projection

\overrightarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{R}}}\circ\overleftarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{R}}}

associated with a complete and decomposable SPN with positive weights and
evidence.
Let
\hat{q}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{R}}
denote its unique fixed point.
Then there exists a choice of Lagrange multipliers for the consensus and
factorization constraints such that, for each sum node
s
and each product edge

(p,c)
,

\pi(s)\coloneq\frac{D(s)\,S(s)}{S(e)}\in(0,1],\qquad\mu_{p\to c}\coloneq\frac{1}{S(e)}\,D_{p\to c}(c)>0,

where

D_{p\to c}(c)=D(p)\prod_{c^{\prime}\in\mathrm{ch}(p)\setminus\{c\}}S(c^{\prime}).

These dual variables satisfy

\frac{D(s)}{S(e)}=\frac{\pi(s)}{S(s)},\qquad\frac{D_{p\to c}(c)}{S(e)}=\mu_{p\to c}.

Proof
. We work on
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{R}}
with the separable generator

f_{\mathcal{R}}
and consider the convex optimization problem

\min_{q\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{R}}}D_{f_{\mathcal{R}}}(q,z_{-1})\quad\text{s.t.}\quad q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{R}},\quad q\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}.

By
Proposition

4.7
, both projections are unique, and hence the optimizer

\hat{q}
is unique. We write the consensus constraints as linear equalities
Aq=0
and the
factorization constraints as affine equalities in dual coordinates

B\theta=0
, where
\theta=\nabla f_{\mathcal{R}}(q)
.
Introducing Lagrange multipliers
\lambda
and
\mu
, the Lagrangian takes the
form

\mathcal{L}(q,\lambda,\mu)=D_{f_{\mathcal{R}}}(q,z_{-1})+\langle\lambda,Aq\rangle+\langle\mu,B\nabla f_{\mathcal{R}}(q)\rangle.

Stationarity of
\mathcal{L}
at
\hat{q}
yields

\nabla f_{\mathcal{R}}(\hat{q})-\nabla f_{\mathcal{R}}(z_{-1})+A^{\top}\lambda+J(\nabla f_{\mathcal{R}})(\hat{q})^{\top}B^{\top}\mu=0.

The multipliers associated with
Aq=0
enforce consensus of replicated region
tables, while those associated with
B\nabla f_{\mathcal{R}}(q)=0
enforce
factorization in dual coordinates.
The upward–downward quantities of the SPN provide an explicit parametrization of
these dual variables. For each sum node
s
we define

\pi(s)\coloneq\frac{D(s)\,S(s)}{S(e)},

and for each product node
p
and child
c\in\mathrm{ch}(p)
we define the
edge-level quantity

\mu_{p\to c}\coloneq\frac{1}{S(e)}\,D_{p\to c}(c),\qquad D_{p\to c}(c)=D(p)\!\!\prod_{c^{\prime}\in\mathrm{ch}(p)\setminus\{c\}}\!\!S(c^{\prime}).

A direct substitution using
Lemma

4.1
shows that these quantities
satisfy the stationarity condition and therefore constitute valid KKT
multipliers.

\hfill\quad\blacksquare

Proposition 4.13

(Local Lipschitz continuity of SPN adjoints)
.

Consider a complete and decomposable SPN with positive weights and evidence.
Let
\Lambda
be a compact set of log-evidence vectors

\log\lambda=(\log\lambda_{i,t})_{i,t}
such that all
\lambda_{i,t}
are bounded
away from
0
and
\infty
.
Then the map

\log\lambda\ \longmapsto\ \Bigl(\frac{\partial\log S(e)}{\partial\log\lambda_{i,t}}\Bigr)_{i,t}

is Lipschitz on
\Lambda
.

Proof
. Write
u_{i,t}\coloneq\log\lambda_{i,t}
.
By
Theorem

4.2
(a),

\frac{\partial\log S(e)}{\partial u_{i,t}}=\Pr(X_{i}=t\mid e).

Define
p(u)\coloneq\bigl(\Pr(X_{i}=t\mid e)\bigr)_{i,t}.
For a complete and decomposable SPN with positive weights and indicators, the map

u\mapsto p(u)
is
C^{1}
on the open set
\{\lambda_{i,t}>0\}
.
Since
\Lambda
is compact and bounded away from the boundary

\{\lambda_{i,t}=0\}
, the Jacobian
Jp(u)
is uniformly bounded on
\Lambda
.
Thus

L\coloneq\sup_{u\in\Lambda}\|Jp(u)\|_{\mathrm{op}}<\infty.

By the mean value theorem, for all
u,u^{\prime}\in\Lambda
,
\|p(u)-p(u^{\prime})\|_{2}\leq L\,\|u-u^{\prime}\|_{2}
, which proves the claim.

\hfill\quad\blacksquare

5
Examples

5.1
Example A: two operations under a delta-lift

Example 5.1

(Logistic unit with exponential output; explicit messages and derivatives)
.

This example instantiates the projection operator

\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Proj}}=\overrightarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}}\circ\overleftarrow{\pi}^{f}_{\mathcal{Q}}

on a minimal delta-lifted computation graph.

Variables and domains.

Let
x,w\in\mathbb{R}
(input and parameter),
u\in\mathbb{R}
, and
z\in(0,1)
with

u\;=\;w\,x,\qquad z\;=\;\sigma(u)\;\coloneq\;\frac{1}{1+e^{-u}},

and let the output factor be
\varphi(z)=e^{\alpha z}
with
\alpha>0

(hence
\varphi\in C^{1}((0,1);\mathbb{R}_{>0})
).

Delta-lift factorization.

Introduce deterministic factors

g_{1}(u,w,x)\;=\;\delta\big(u-wx\big),\qquad g_{2}(z,u)\;=\;\delta\big(z-\sigma(u)\big).

Assume
x
is clamped at a given forward value
x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
w^{*}\in\mathbb{R}
be the evaluation point and set

u^{*}=w^{*}x^{*}
and
z^{*}=\sigma(u^{*})
.

Upward pass.

Because
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
are Dirac constraints, upward messages into
u
and
z

collapse to point masses at
u^{*}
and
z^{*}
, respectively.

Downward seeding at the output.

Seed the downward pass with

m_{\varphi\to z}(z)\;=\;\varphi(z)=e^{\alpha z},\qquad s(z)\coloneq\partial_{z}\log m_{\varphi\to z}(z)\big|_{z^{*}}=\alpha.

Message through
g_{2}
(from
z
to
u
).

m_{g_{2}\to u}(u)\;\propto\;\int\delta\big(z-\sigma(u)\big)\,m_{\varphi\to z}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z=e^{\alpha\sigma(u)},

hence

\partial_{u}\log m_{g_{2}\to u}(u)\big|_{u^{*}}=\alpha\,\sigma^{\prime}(u^{*})=\alpha\,\sigma(u^{*})(1-\sigma(u^{*})).

Message through
g_{1}
(from
u
to
w
and
x
).

With
x
clamped at
x^{*}
,

m_{g_{1}\to w}(w)\;\propto\;m_{g_{2}\to u}(wx^{*})=e^{\alpha\sigma(wx^{*})},

so

\partial_{w}\log m_{g_{1}\to w}(w)\big|_{w^{*}}=\alpha\,\sigma^{\prime}(u^{*})\,x^{*}.

Similarly,

\partial_{x}\log m_{g_{1}\to x}(x)\big|_{x^{*}}=\alpha\,\sigma^{\prime}(u^{*})\,w^{*}.

Action of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Proj}}
.

Since
g_{1}
is the only downstream factor,

b_{\downarrow}(w)=m_{g_{1}\to w}
and
b_{\downarrow}(x)=m_{g_{1}\to x}
.
Evaluating the downward log-beliefs yields

\nabla_{w}\log b_{\downarrow}(w)\big|_{w^{*}}=\alpha\,\sigma^{\prime}(u^{*})\,x^{*},\qquad\nabla_{x}\log b_{\downarrow}(x)\big|_{x^{*}}=\alpha\,\sigma^{\prime}(u^{*})\,w^{*}.

Because

\nabla_{w}z=\sigma^{\prime}(u^{*})x^{*}
and

\nabla_{x}z=\sigma^{\prime}(u^{*})w^{*}
,
these quantities coincide with the differential of

\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Proj}}
at the forward configuration, recovering
backpropagation as stated in
Theorem

3.3
with

\varphi^{\prime}(z^{*})/\varphi(z^{*})=\alpha
.

Variant with proper likelihood.

If
\varphi(z)=e^{-L(z)/T}
with
L\in C^{1}
and
T>0
, then

s(z)=-L^{\prime}(z^{*})/T
and the same calculation yields the standard backpropagation
gradient scaled by
1/T
.

5.2
Example B: a tiny SPN

Example 5.2

(Two-variable SPN with one root sum and two product children)
.

Variables and evidence.

Let
X,Y
be binary with alphabets
\{0,1\}
.
Soft evidence is encoded by indicator leaves

\lambda_{X,0},\lambda_{X,1},\lambda_{Y,0},\lambda_{Y,1}\in(0,1]
.

Circuit (complete & decomposable).

Define two product nodes

P_{1}=\mathbf{1}[X=0]\mathbf{1}[Y=0],\qquad P_{2}=\mathbf{1}[X=1]\mathbf{1}[Y=1],

and the root sum
r=w_{1}P_{1}+w_{2}P_{2},~w_{1},w_{2}>0
. The circuit is complete, decomposable, and a tree.

Upward values.

S(P_{1})=\lambda_{X,0}\lambda_{Y,0},\qquad S(P_{2})=\lambda_{X,1}\lambda_{Y,1},

S(e)=S(r)=w_{1}\lambda_{X,0}\lambda_{Y,0}+w_{2}\lambda_{X,1}\lambda_{Y,1}.

Downward values.

Initialize
D(r)=1
.
Then

D(P_{1})=w_{1},\qquad D(P_{2})=w_{2},

and

D(\mathbf{1}[X=0])=w_{1}\lambda_{Y,0},\quad D(\mathbf{1}[X=1])=w_{2}\lambda_{Y,1},

D(\mathbf{1}[Y=0])=w_{1}\lambda_{X,0},\quad D(\mathbf{1}[Y=1])=w_{2}\lambda_{X,1}.

Exact marginals.

By
Theorem

4.2
,

\Pr(X=0\mid e)=\frac{\lambda_{X,0}}{S(e)}\frac{\partial S(e)}{\partial\lambda_{X,0}}=\frac{w_{1}\lambda_{X,0}\lambda_{Y,0}}{S(e)},\qquad\Pr(X=1\mid e)=\frac{w_{2}\lambda_{X,1}\lambda_{Y,1}}{S(e)},

\Pr(Y=0\mid e)=\frac{w_{1}\lambda_{X,0}\lambda_{Y,0}}{S(e)},\qquad\Pr(Y=1\mid e)=\frac{w_{2}\lambda_{X,1}\lambda_{Y,1}}{S(e)}.

Gate beliefs.

At the root,

b_{r}(P_{1})=\frac{w_{1}S(P_{1})}{S(e)},\qquad b_{r}(P_{2})=\frac{w_{2}S(P_{2})}{S(e)}.

Since the circuit is a tree, these coincide with the global gate marginals.

Projection viewpoint.

This SPN realizes, in one upward–downward pass, the two-step KL operator

\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{SPN}}=\overrightarrow{\pi}^{f_{\mathcal{R}}}_{\widehat{\mathsf{Prod}}_{\mathcal{R}}}\circ\overleftarrow{\pi}^{f_{\mathcal{R}}}_{\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{R}}}

from
Proposition

4.4
, acting on the product space of region tables

\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{R}}
with the separable negative-entropy generator

f_{\mathcal{R}}=\sum_{R\in\mathcal{R}}f(\cdot)
.
Here the I-projection enforces consensus of equal-scope regions (the two product
children of
r
), and the reverse-KL projection enforces factorization across
decomposed scopes, as witnessed by
S(P_{k})=\lambda_{X,k}\lambda_{Y,k}
and the
exact marginal identities above.

6
Conclusion

This note examined backpropagation through the lens of KL projection geometry,
with the goal of making explicit the operator-theoretic structure underlying
message passing and reverse-mode differentiation.

Scope and limitations.

Regime (A) is algebraic: the delta–lift includes Dirac constraints and typically
an improper output factor, so the correspondence concerns the local structure
of messages and their differentials rather than normalized marginals.
Regime (B) is probabilistic: completeness, decomposability, and strict
positivity ensure interior beliefs and uniqueness of the KL projections.
In both regimes, our analysis concerns a single evaluation of the relevant
projection operators; the dynamics induced by parameter updates depend on the
chosen optimization scheme and are not addressed here.

When BP truly generalizes backpropagation.

Theorem

3.9
isolates the regime in which sum–product strictly
generalizes backpropagation. With proper priors, a proper likelihood, and a
tree–structured graph, belief propagation computes posterior–expected
sensitivities exactly. In the Dirac–prior limit, this reduces to the algebraic
delta–lift identity of Regime (A); on loopy graphs, the same expression is
evaluated in the usual Bethe/EP approximation regime.

Outlook.

Three directions appear particularly natural.

•

Training dynamics from projection–derived gradients.

Since each backprop evaluation arises from a KL projection operator or its
differential, tools from Bregman geometry and operator splitting may be used to
study stability, implicit regularization, and alternative update schemes.

•

Projection–modified message passing and architectures.

The projection viewpoint suggests modifying BP schemes (e.g., via symmetric
generators, damping, or explicit consensus enforcement) and designing
architectures that encode consensus and factorization constraints directly.

•

Sampling and KL gradient flows.

Sampling methods can be viewed as dynamical processes whose stationary
distributions solve related KL variational problems, often expressible as
gradient flows in KL geometry. Understanding how projection operators and their
differentials interact with such flows, and how they relate to the projection
structures identified here, is a natural direction for future work.

Perspective.

More broadly, Bregman projections provide a unifying geometric language for
learning, inference, and sampling. Our results place standard backpropagation
and SPN message passing within this projection calculus by identifying specific
projection operators whose differentials or KKT multipliers coincide with the
adjoints used in practice. Exploiting this shared geometric interface remains an
open and promising direction.

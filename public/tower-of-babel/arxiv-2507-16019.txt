Covariant reggeization framework for diffraction. Part I: Hadronic tensors in Minkovsky space-time of any dimension.
R.A. Ryutin e1,addr1

Abstract
In this paper we consider the general structure of irreducible tensor representations of the Poincare group of arbitrary dimension with multiple sets of Lorentz indices and different ways to construct them from basic elements (Lorentz vectors and the metric tensor). Then we apply the same methods to obtain the expansion of general hadronic tensors in terms of these irreducible tensors. We propose to use an effective approach in hadronic diffraction, which was usually called covariant reggeization, and obtain basic functions to calculate all the diffractive cross-sections.

Basics of the covariant reggeization framework

Basic elements of the covariant reggeization approach are vertex functions (irreducible
tensor representations of the Poincare group with multiple groups of Lorentz indices). As examples
we can consider the vertex “scalar-scalar-spin-
J
tensor” in momentum space:

{\mathscr{V}}^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{J}}(k,q)
, where

\mathscr{V}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{J}}(p,q)=<p-q|\mathscr{I}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{J}}|p>,

(11)

and propagator

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{J}\nu_{1}...\nu_{J}}(q)=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\;e^{iq(x-y)}\left<0|\;\Phi^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{J}}(x)\Phi^{\nu_{1}...\nu_{J}}(y)\;|0\right>\quad{,}

\displaystyle=\Pi^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{J},\nu_{1}...\nu_{J}}(q)/(m^{2}(J)-q^{2}),

(12)

which have the poles at

m^{2}(J)-q^{2}=0,\;\mbox{i.e.}\;J=\alpha_{{\mathbb{R}}}(q^{2})\;,

(13)

after an appropriate analytic continuation of the signatured amplitudes
in
J
. We assume that this pole, where
\alpha_{\mathbb{R}}
is the reggeon
trajectory.

The reggeization prescription for the amplitude after all contractions of Lorentz indexes is

\sum_{J}\frac{F^{J}}{(q^{2}-m^{2})}\to\frac{\alpha_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\prime}}{2}\eta_{{\mathbb{R}}}(q^{2})\Gamma(-\alpha_{{\mathbb{R}}}(q^{2}))F^{\alpha_{{\mathbb{R}}}(q^{2})}.

(14)

\mathscr{I}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{J}}
is the current operator related to the hadronic spin-
J
Heisenberg field operator,

\left(\square+m_{J}^{2}\right)\Phi^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{J}}(x)=\mathscr{I}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{J}}(x)\;,

(15)

and

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\mathscr{I}^{\mu_{1}...\mu...\mu_{J}}=0\;{;}

(16)

\displaystyle\mathscr{I}^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{J}}=\mathscr{I}^{\left(\mu_{1}...\mu_{J}\right)}\;{;}\;

(17)

\displaystyle g_{\mu_{i}\mu_{k}}\mathscr{I}^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{i}...\mu_{k}...\mu_{J}}=0.

(18)

Here
\left(\mu_{1}...\mu_{J}\right)
denotes symmetrization in all indices. In
q
-space conditions (
16
)-(
18
)
look like Rarita-Schwinger conditions for irreducible representations of the Poincaré algebra (transverse-symmetric-traceless, TST):

\displaystyle q_{\lambda}\mathscr{V}^{\mu_{1}...\lambda...\mu_{J}}=0\;{;}

(19)

\displaystyle\mathscr{V}^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{J}}=\mathscr{V}^{\left(\mu_{1}...\mu_{J}\right)}\;{;}

(20)

\displaystyle g_{\mu_{i}\mu_{k}}\mathscr{V}^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{i}...\mu_{k}...\mu_{J}}=0{.}

(21)

Similar conditions are imposed on all other irreducible tensors considered in this paper. Here we use usual Einstein prescription for summation in the identical indices.

6
Discussions and conclusions

Based on the picture of the reggeon as a “reggeized” tensor quantum field of arbitrary integer spin in a
D
-dimensional Minkowski space, this work derives the had-ronic irreducible tensors that represent the amplitudes of all the most important diffractive processes. The corresponding cross sections are obtained by various contractions of these tensors.

A generalization to the case of non‑conserved currents is also considered, in which the final tensors can be decomposed into irreducible tensors and transferred momenta.

It should be noted that the “non‑conservation” of currents may be an artifact of higher-dimensional space-time. For this reason we have considered here a “toy” model of Minkowski space with an arbitrary number of dimensions, in order to understand
how the number of dimensions affects the behavior of diffractive cross
sections. For example, if the currents are conserved, then the differential cross
section for single dissociation as a function of the momentum transfer squared
must exhibit a minimum
at very small values of the momentum‑transfer squared, as was shown in Refs.
mySDandPomCS
;
myVisualization
. Those
works also demonstrated that unitarization can somewhat
soften this behavior, but the trend of decreasing cross section at small momentum‑transfer
squared remains. Consequently, some authors
CloseIn
,
CloseOut
have studied a case
with a non‑conserved current which, however, may be conserved in a space with extra dimensions, so that its projection onto
our four‑dimensional space appears effectively “non‑conserved.” Of course, in future
work one should consider more realistic scenarios with curved extra dimensions, for example an anti-de Sitter bulk.

As already mentioned in the Introduction, we apply a tensorial formalism here for transparency. In
principle one could obtain similar results using approaches based on “continuous” spin and Wigner’s
equations
CSPin
-
CSPout
, on higher‑spin
field theories, hyperfields and string methods, or by using the helicity formalism
helicityIn
-
helicityOut
to
compute amplitudes (which will
be done later to generalize this approach to vertices with half‑integer spins and their reggeized analogs). The helicity
formalism becomes especially important for multi‑Reggeon vertices, since the number of recurrent equations
grows with the number of Reggeon legs in amplitudes and the solutions for the coefficient functions become cumbersome.

Particular attention must be paid to the possible behavior of the form-factors at small momentum transfer, which is
characteristic of diffractive processes. As will be shown in the second part of this study, the properties of the form factors at low momentum
transfer (finiteness of the hadronic tensors) impose additional constraints on the hadronic tensors, allowing one to simplify the cross‑section expressions significantly and to attempt to extract these form-factors by the use of the existing experimental data.

The solutions for the irreducible tensors obtained in this work may be applied not only to diffractive processes but also to the amplitudes of processes in effective models for higher‑spin particles, since the approach presented here is mathematically quite general.

Tricks for generating functions

If we have some generating function

f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}x^{n}

then we can obtain useful relations which someone can apply to different recurrent relations:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n+k}x^{n}=\frac{f(x)-\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k}a_{i}x^{i}}{x^{k}}\equiv f^{+}_{k}(x),

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}a_{n-k}x^{n}=x^{k}f(x)\equiv f^{-}_{k}(x),

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n^{m}a_{n+k}x^{n}=\left\{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left\{m\atop i\right\}x^{i}\frac{\partial^{i}}{\partial x^{i}}\right\}f^{+}_{k}(x),

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}n^{m}a_{n-k}x^{n}=\left\{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left\{m\atop i\right\}x^{i}\frac{\partial^{i}}{\partial x^{i}}\right\}f^{-}_{k}(x),

\displaystyle\left\{m\atop i\right\}=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{i}\frac{(-1)^{i-j}j^{m}}{(m-j)!j!},\;\left\{0\atop 0\right\}=1,

\displaystyle n^{m}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m}\left\{m\atop i\right\}(n)_{m},\;(n)_{m}=\frac{n!}{(n-m)!},

(205)

where
\left\{m\atop i\right\}
are Stirling numbers of the second kind.

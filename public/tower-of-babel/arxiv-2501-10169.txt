Rates of memory loss for null recurrent Markov chains
Ilya Chevyrev, Alexey Korepanov

Abstract
Orey (1962) proved that for an irreducible, aperiodic, and recurrent Markov chain with transition operator P P , the sequence P n ​ ( μ − ν ) P^{n}(\mu-\nu) converges to zero in total variation for any two probability measures μ \mu and ν \nu . In other words, all such Markov chains exhibit memory loss . While the rates of memory loss have been extensively studied for positive recurrent chains, there is a surprising lack of results for null recurrent chains. In this work, we prove the first estimates of memory loss rates in the null recurrent case.



1
Introduction

Consider a Markov chain
X_{0},X_{1},\ldots
taking values in a measurable space
\Omega
with transition operator
P\colon{\mathcal{P}}(\Omega)\to{\mathcal{P}}(\Omega)
,
where
{\mathcal{P}}(\Omega)
is the space of probability measures on
\Omega
.
That is, if
X_{n}\sim\mu
with
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}(\Omega)
, then
X_{n+1}\sim P\mu
.
A question of fundamental interest is whether the chain is
mixing
, and if so, what is the
mixing rate
.
A well studied notion of mixing is
memory loss:

the convergence
\|P^{n}\mu-P^{n}\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
for
\mu,\nu\in{\mathcal{P}}(\Omega)
that act as initial distributions for
X
,
where
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
is the total variation norm.

A celebrated result of Orey
[
Ore59
,
Ore62
]
(see also
[
JO67
,
Ore71
]
) states that if
X
is recurrent, irreducible and aperiodic, then
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|P^{n}\mu-P^{n}\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=0
for all
\mu,\nu\in{\mathcal{P}}(\Omega)
.
This result is remarkable in its scope, but of course, at this level of generality, comes with no rate of convergence.

In the case that
X
is
positive recurrent
, and thus admits an invariant probability measure,
there are well-known results that quantify memory loss,
e.g. for so-called
regular
measures, one has
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|P^{n}\mu-P^{n}\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}<\infty
, so in particular
\|P^{n}\mu-P^{n}\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\lesssim n^{-1}
, see
[
Num84
, Theorem 6.11]
.
There is an abundance of criteria for polynomial and geometric rates of convergence, e.g. based on estimates of first return times to nice sets, or the existence of Lyapunov functions, see

[
NT78
,
NT82
,
NT83
]

for early works
as well as the books

[
Lin02
,
Num84
,
BH22
,
MT09
]
and the references therein.
For results in the case of subgeometric mixing rates, see for example
[
TT94
,
Ver97
,
JR02
,
DFMS04
,
Bax05
,
Hai21
,
CnM23
]

and the book
[
DMPS18
]
.
To our knowledge, all of these conditions are restricted to the
positive recurrent
case.

The purpose of this paper is to provide the first rates in convergence
\|P^{n}\mu-P^{n}\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\to 0

for
null recurrent
Markov chains.
We present three results which use different strategies and work under different assumptions, and which are applicable to general Harris chains.

Remark 1.1
.

The Markov chain (
1.1
) and our choice of initial distributions are far more than a convenient illustration.
As we show in Section
3
,
estimates on
\|P^{n+\ell}\delta_{0}-P^{n}\delta_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
for (
1.1
)
lead directly to memory loss rates for Markov chains on a general state space with relaxed recurrence
properties, specifically for Harris chains.

For
n\geq 1
let

\displaystyle p_{n}=\P(\inf\{k\geq 1\,:\,X_{k}=0\}=n\mid X_{0}=0)=(1-q_{0})\cdots(1-q_{n-2})q_{n-1}\;.

Suppose that

\sum_{k>n}p_{k}\leq Cn^{-\alpha}

with some
C,\alpha>0
. If
\alpha>1
, then
X_{n}
is positive recurrent,
and it is generally null recurrent otherwise.

A standard result (see Appendix
A
) for positive recurrent Markov chain is that for
\alpha>1
,

\|P^{n+\ell}\delta_{0}-P^{n}\delta_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\lesssim\frac{\ell}{n^{\alpha}}\;,

(1.2)

where
\lesssim
means a bound up to a multiplicative constant that does not depend on
n
or
\ell
.
In general, this bound is sharp for
\ell\leq n
, e.g. when
\sum_{k>n}p_{k}=(n+1)^{-\alpha}
,
see Corollary
2.4
.

In the null recurrent case
\alpha\leq 1
, we are not aware of any prior results which give a convergence rate.
Our main results in this situation can be summarised as follows.

(a)

If
\alpha\in(0,3/2)
, then
\displaystyle\|P^{n+\ell}\delta_{0}-P^{n}\delta_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\lesssim\frac{\ell^{2/3}}{n^{\alpha/3}}

for
\ell
sufficiently small, for example for
\ell\leq n^{3/2-\alpha}(\log(1+n))^{-3/2}
.
See Theorem
2.6
.

(b)

If
p_{n}
satisfies the
decreasing failure rate
(DFR), i.e. the sequence
p_{n}/(p_{n}+p_{n+1}+\cdots)
is non-increasing, then

\|P^{n+\ell}\delta_{0}-P^{n}\delta_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
is given by an explicit expression,
regardless of the assumption on the tails
\sum_{k>n}p_{k}
.
In particular,

\|P^{n+1}\delta_{0}-P^{n}\delta_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=2\sum_{k>n+1}p_{k}
.
See Theorem
2.8
and Lemma
2.9
.

(c)

Assume a corresponding lower bound
\sum_{k>n}p_{k}\geq C^{-1}n^{-\alpha}
.
Then, for
\alpha\in(1/2,1)
,

\|P^{n+\ell}\delta_{0}-P^{n}\delta_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\lesssim\frac{\ell^{2\alpha-1}}{n^{2\alpha-1}}\;.

Assume furthermore an upper bound on increments
\sum_{k>n}|p_{k}-p_{k+1}|\lesssim n^{-\alpha-1}
.
Then, for
\alpha\in(0,1)
,

\|P^{n+\ell}\delta_{0}-P^{n}\delta_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\lesssim\frac{\ell^{\alpha}}{n^{\alpha}}\;.

(1.3)

See Theorem
2.10
.

Remark 1.2
.

If
n\mapsto\sum_{k>n}p_{k}
is regularly varying, then (
1.3
) is sharp in the sense that

\|P^{n+\ell}\delta_{0}-P^{n}\delta_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\gtrsim\ell^{\alpha}n^{-\alpha}
,
see Corollary
2.4
.
Furthermore, in this case, the obvious upper bound

\|P^{n+\ell}\delta_{0}-P^{n}\delta_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\lesssim 1
is sharp for
\ell>n
.

Remark 1.3
.

The bound (
1.2
) for
\alpha>1
follows from the case
\ell=1
and the triangle inequality.
This is
not
the case for the results in
(a)
-
(c)
.

Remark 1.4
.

As mentioned in Remark
1.1
, bounds on
\|P^{n+\ell}\delta_{0}-P^{n}\delta_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}

translate directly to memory loss rates for general Harris chains.
As a simple example, consider a Markov chain
X
on a measurable space
\Omega

with a recurrent point
x\in\Omega
.
Define
\tau=\inf\{k\geq 1\,:\,X_{k}=x\}

and
p_{k}=\P(\tau=k\mid X_{0}=x)
.
Suppose that

\|P^{n+\ell}\delta_{0}-P^{n}\delta_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\lesssim\ell^{\beta}n^{-\alpha}

for some
\beta\geq\alpha\geq 0
uniformly in
\ell,n\geq 1
,
where
P
is the transition operator for the Markov chain (
1.1
) built with these
p_{k}
.
For
\mu\neq\nu\in{\mathcal{P}}(\Omega)
, define
\|\mu-\nu\|_{\beta}=\E_{X_{0}\sim\lambda}\tau^{\beta}
where
\lambda=|\mu-\nu|(\Omega)^{-1}|\mu-\nu|\in{\mathcal{P}}(\Omega)
.
Then
\|Q^{n}\mu-Q^{n}\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\lesssim n^{-\alpha}\|\mu-\nu\|_{\beta}
where
Q
is the transition operator of
X
.
See Proposition
3.8
for details.

Our proofs of
(a)
-
(c)
are self-contained and independent of each other.
Moreover, the proofs are elementary yet at times delicate, using only
basic facts about regularly varying functions, Hoeffding’s inequality, etc.
We make the following additional remarks.

(a)

is based on the famous and elegant coupling of Ornstein
[
Orn69a
,
Orn69b
]
,
where memory loss is drawn from the times when a symmetric random walk on
{\mathbb{Z}}
reaches
0
.
The proof is short, the assumptions are very general, yet the rate is weaker than in
(b)
or
(c)
, and than in (
1.2
) for
\alpha>1
.

(b)

has an even shorter, essentially algebraic proof, although the assumption is very restrictive.
The decreasing failure rate is used to verify that the renewal sequence
u_{n}=\P(X_{n}=0\mid X_{0}=0)

is monotone, which in turn allows completely bypassing otherwise substantial difficulties.
Such monotonicity conditions were considered in
[
Isa88
,
HF91
]
.
We highlight, however, that we give a simple and possibly new proof of the fact that a decreasing failure rate implies
monotonicity of
u_{n}
(see Lemma
2.9
).

(c)

is, we believe, the main contribution of this paper.
The rate (
1.3
) is sharp (see Remark
1.2
) while the assumptions
are still practical to verify.
Our proof of
(c)
is the longest part of the paper and is based on analysing
the renewal sequence

using Tauberian arguments, inspired by but not relying on
[
GL63
]
.
Some key differences are that we bound the increments
|u_{n}-u_{n+\ell}|
rather than
u_{n}
itself,
and that we require only upper/lower bounds on the tails
\sum_{k>n}p_{k}
instead of assuming
that the tails are regularly varying.
(Regular variation of

is a standard assumption in renewal theory with infinite mean, see e.g.
[
GL63
,
Teu68
,
Eri70
,
Fre82
,
Don97
,
CD19
,
Ber19
]
,
and we are not aware of any results that do not use this assumption.)
See Remark
2.11
for possible generalisations of
(c)
.

We conclude the introduction by outlining the motivation for our study.
Mixing rates are a fundamental property of Markov chains,
as evidenced by the body of results in the positive recurrent case,
thus we believe our findings carry intrinsic interest.

Our motivation originates in smooth ergodic theory, where so-called
non-uniformly expanding

and
non-uniformly hyperbolic
dynamical systems have an intimate relation to
Markov chains similar to (
1.1
). Various statistical properties have been proved by
representing these systems as
Young towers
, topological Markov chains with
some nice structure
[
You98
,
You99
]
. Further, connections with
renewal-type Markov chains have been established via Lipschitz continuous and measure preserving
semi-conjugacies
[
Kor18
,
CDKM24
]
.

The landscape is much like in the probabilistic literature. Memory loss is known to
occur in very general conditions
[
Lin71
]
, see
[
Aar97
, Theorem 1.3.3]
.
In the positive recurrent case, sharp rates of memory loss have been obtained
using coupling
[
You98
,
You99
,
KL21
]
(c.f.
[
Pit74
,
Lin79
]
)
and renewal theory
[
Sar02
,
Gou04
]
. In the null recurrent case,
renewal sequences have been accurately estimated
[
MT12
,
Ter16
]
.
However, no results currently address rates of memory loss in the null recurrent case.

Extension of our results to ergodic theory will be a subject of a future study.
In particular, the ‘practical’ assumptions in
(c)
have been chosen with such applications in mind.

2.1
Notation and preliminaries

Recall that we suppose that
X_{n}
is aperiodic and recurrent.
That is,
\sum_{n\geq 1}p_{n}=1
and there exist
c_{\#}>0
and
N_{\#}\geq 1
so that

\gcd\{1\leq k\leq N_{\#}\,:\,p_{k}\geq c_{\#}\}=1\;.

(2.1)

If
\sum_{n\geq 1}np_{n}<\infty
, then
X_{n}
is positive recurrent, and
X_{n}
is null recurrent otherwise.

Let
u_{n}
,
n\geq 0
, denote the renewal probabilities

u_{n}=\P(X_{n}=0\mid X_{0}=0)\;.

Equivalently, we can define
u_{n}
by
u_{0}=1
and the
renewal equation

u_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}p_{k}u_{n-k}\;,\quad n\geq 1\;.

(2.2)

For
n\geq 0
let

z_{n}=\sum_{k>n}p_{k}\;.

Remark 2.1
.

It is a direct verification that
z_{n}=z_{n-1}(1-q_{n-1})=(1-q_{0})\cdots(1-q_{n-1})
for
n\geq 1
.
In particular
z_{n}q_{n}=p_{n+1}
for
n\geq 0
and
\sum_{n\geq 0}z_{n}q_{n}=1=z_{0}
. That is,

z_{n}
are the weights of the unique (up to scaling) stationary measure on
\Omega
.

Our notation allows a convenient expression

P^{n}\delta_{0}=\sum_{m=0}^{n}u_{n-m}z_{m}\delta_{m}\;.

Subsequently,

P^{n+\ell}\delta_{0}-P^{n}\delta_{0}=\sum_{m=n+1}^{n+\ell}u_{n+\ell-m}z_{m}\delta_{m}+\sum_{m=0}^{n}(u_{n+\ell-m}-u_{n-m})z_{m}\delta_{m}\;.

(2.3)

The problem of bounding
\|P^{n+\ell}\delta_{0}-P^{n}\delta_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}

is therefore closely tied to the asymptotics of the increments of the renewal sequence
u_{n}
.

We use
X\lesssim Y
to denote
X\leq CY
for a constant
C>0
that does not depend on
X,Y
.
We write
X\asymp Y
if
X\lesssim Y
and
Y\lesssim X
.
We also write
Y\gg X
whenever
Y\geq CX
for some
C
sufficiently large.
For
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we let
\lfloor x\rfloor\in{\mathbb{Z}}
denote the floor of
x
.

For probability measures
\mu,\nu
on a general measurable space
(\Omega,\Sigma)
, recall the identity

\|\mu-\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=2\sup_{A\in\Sigma}|\mu(A)-\nu(A)|\;.

(2.4)

2.1.1
Regularly varying functions

Recall that a function
\rho\colon[a,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is called
regularly varying (RV) with index
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
if
\lim_{x\to\infty}\rho(\lambda x)/\rho(x)=\lambda^{\alpha}
for all
\lambda>0
.
A RV function with index
0
is called slowly varying.
For more details on RV functions, we refer to
[
BGT89
]
.
We will use the following basic facts without explicit mention.
Suppose
\rho
is RV with index
\alpha
.

•

One has
\rho(x)=x^{\alpha}L(x)
where
L
is slowly varying.

•

For any compact
I\subset(0,\infty)
, the limit
\lim_{x\to\infty}\rho(\lambda x)/\rho(x)=\lambda^{\alpha}
is uniform over
\lambda\in I
.
In particular, for
x,y
sufficiently large,
\rho(x)\asymp\rho(y)
whenever
x\asymp y
.

•

Karamata’s theorem

[
BGT89
, Section 1.5.6]
. Suppose
\rho
is locally bounded.
If
\alpha>-1
, then
\lim_{x\to\infty}x\rho(x)/\int_{a}^{x}\rho(y)\,dy=\alpha+1
.
If
\alpha<-1
, then
\lim_{x\to\infty}x\rho(x)/\int_{x}^{\infty}\rho(y)\,dy=-\alpha-1
.
If
\alpha=-1
, then
\int_{a}^{x}\rho(y)\,dy
is slowly varying.

We will often use these facts in the following form: if
\alpha>-1
, then
\int_{a}^{x}\rho(y)\,dy\asymp x\rho(x)
and
\int_{0}^{\delta}\rho(x^{-1})^{-1}\,dx\asymp\delta\rho(1/\delta)

uniformly in
x
sufficiently large and
\delta
sufficiently small.
Likewise, if
\alpha<-1
, then

\int_{x}^{\infty}\rho(y)\,dy\asymp x\rho(x)
and
\int_{\delta}^{1/a}\rho(x^{-1})^{-1}\,dx\asymp\delta\rho(1/\delta)
.

If
\rho
is defined on
\{k,k+1,\ldots\}
, we say that
\rho
is RV if
x\mapsto\rho(\lfloor x\rfloor)
is RV.

2.2
Lower bounds

We begin with an easy
lower bound
on the memory loss for the Markov chain (
1.1
).

Lemma 2.2
.

For all
n,\ell\geq 0

\|P^{n+\ell}\delta_{0}-P^{n}\delta_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\geq 2\sum_{m=n+1}^{n+\ell}u_{n+\ell-m}z_{m}\;.

(2.5)

Proof.

The result follows from (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) by taking
A=\{n+1,\ldots,n+\ell\}
in the latter.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

\|P^{n+1}\delta_{0}-P^{n}\delta_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\geq 2z_{n+1}
.

Corollary 2.4
.

Suppose
n\mapsto z_{n}
is RV with index
-\alpha
.
If
\alpha>1
, then for
n\geq 0
and
\ell\geq 1
,

\|P^{n+\ell}\delta_{0}-P^{n}\delta_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\gtrsim\sum_{m=n+1}^{n+\ell}z_{n}\;.

(2.6)

so in particular,
\|P^{n+\ell}\delta_{0}-P^{n}\delta_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\gtrsim\ell z_{n}
for
\ell\leq n
.
If
\alpha\in(0,1)
, then for
n\geq 0
and
\ell\geq 1
,

\|P^{n+\ell}\delta_{0}-P^{n}\delta_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\gtrsim 1\wedge\frac{z_{n}}{z_{\ell}}\;.

If
\alpha=1
, then for
\ell\leq n
,

\|P^{n+\ell}\delta_{0}-P^{n}\delta_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\gtrsim z_{n}\sum_{k=1}^{\ell-1}m(k)^{-1}\;,\qquad\text{where}\qquad m(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}z_{k}\;.

Proof.

If
\alpha>1
then
u_{n}\gtrsim 1
for sufficiently large
n
.
Hence (
2.6
) follows from (
2.5
) and
\|P^{n+\ell}\delta_{0}-P^{n}\delta_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\gtrsim\ell z_{n}
for
\ell\leq n
follows since
z
is RV.

Suppose
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Then by
[
GL63
, Theorem 1.1]
,
\liminf_{n\to\infty}nz_{n}u_{n}>0
.
Let
r>0
such that
u_{n}\gtrsim n^{-1}z_{n}^{-1}
for all
n\geq r
.
It suffices to consider
r<n
.
In this case, for
\ell\leq n
, by (
2.5
) and Karamata’s theorem,

\displaystyle\|P^{n+\ell}\delta_{0}-P^{n}\delta_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{m=n+1}^{n+\ell}u_{n+\ell-m}z_{m}\asymp z_{n}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell-1}u_{k}\gtrsim\sum_{k=r}^{\ell-1}\frac{z_{n}}{kz_{k}}\asymp\frac{z_{n}}{z_{\ell}}\;,

while for
\ell>n
,

\displaystyle\|P^{n+\ell}\delta_{0}-P^{n}\delta_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{m=n+1}^{n+\ell-r}u_{n+\ell-m}z_{m}\gtrsim 1\;,

where we used
u_{n+\ell-m}\gtrsim\ell^{-1}z_{\ell}^{-1}

and
z_{m}\gtrsim z_{\ell}
.

Suppose
\alpha=1
. By
[
Eri70
, Theorem 1]
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}m(n)u_{n}=1
.
Then there is
r>0
so that
u_{n}\geq\frac{1}{2}m(n)^{-1}
for all
n\geq r

and, repeating the calculation for
\alpha\in(0,1)
, for
r<\ell\leq n
,

\displaystyle\|P^{n+\ell}\delta_{0}-P^{n}\delta_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim z_{n}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell-1}u_{k}\gtrsim z_{n}\sum_{k=r}^{\ell-1}m(k)^{-1}\;.\qed

Remark 2.5
.

Suppose that
\sum_{k>n}p_{k}\lesssim n^{-\alpha}
with
\alpha>0
.
For
\alpha>1
(when
X_{n}
is positive recurrent),

\|P^{n+1}\delta_{0}-P^{n}\delta_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\lesssim n^{-\alpha}
by (

).
By Corollary

, this is sharp.
A question of special interest and the initial motivation for this work was to investigate
whether the bound

can be extended to

.

2.3
Upper bounds

Under very relaxed assumptions, using only an upper bound on the tails

, we obtain:

.

Suppose that
\sum_{k>n}p_{k}\leq\rho(n)
, where

.

(DFR), i.e. that the sequence

.

) and on

.

.
Remark that (

.

Remark 2.11
.

The only place where we use the lower bound on

is Lemma

.
It is possible to assume a bound of the form

)

Remark 2.12
.

Condition (

Given

(3.1)

Throughout this section, let

,

3.1
Markov chains with a recurrent atom

Suppose that
X_{n}
has a recurrent atom
S\in\Sigma
.
That is,
there exists a probability measure
\beta
on
\Omega
such that

(a)

\P(X_{n}\in S\text{ infinitely often}\mid X_{0}=x)=1
for each
x\in\Omega
, and

(b)

\P(X_{n+1}\in A\mid X_{n}=x)=\beta(A)
for all
A\in\Sigma
and
x\in S
.

Define

\tau=\inf\{k\geq 1\,:\,X_{k-1}\in S\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad p_{n}=\P(\tau=n\mid X_{0}\sim\beta)\;.

(3.2)

Since
X_{n}
is recurrent,
\sum_{n\geq 1}p_{n}=1
.

Let
\beta_{m}
,
m\geq 0
, be probability measures on
\Omega

\displaystyle\beta_{m}(A)

\displaystyle=\P(X_{m+1}\in A\mid X_{0}\in S\text{ while }X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\notin S)

\displaystyle=\P(X_{m}\in A\mid X_{0}\sim\beta\text{ and }\tau\geq m+1)\;.

For a measure
\zeta
on
\{0,1,\ldots\}
, let
\pi\zeta
be its projection on
\Omega
obtained by

\pi\zeta=\sum_{m\geq 0}\zeta(m)\beta_{m}\;.

The following lemma links
Q^{n}\mu
, for
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}(\Omega)
, with
the evolution of
P^{n}\delta_{0}
on (
1.1
) for which
the
p_{n}
are given by (
3.2
), up to a small remainder.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X_{0}\sim\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}(\Omega)
and
A\in\Sigma
. Then

\P(X_{n}\in A)=Q^{n}\mu(A)=\pi\Bigl[\sum_{t=1}^{n}\P(\tau=t)P^{n-t}\delta_{0}\Bigr](A)+\P(X_{n}\in A\,,\tau>n)\;.

Proof.

Let

Z_{n}=\begin{cases}-1&\text{if }n=0\text{ or }X_{0},\ldots X_{n-1}\notin S\;,\\
\min\{k\geq 0\,:\,X_{n-1-k}\in S\}&\text{otherwise}\;.\end{cases}

Then
(X_{n},Z_{n})
is a Markov chain on
\Omega\times\{-1,0,\ldots\}
, and, for
m\geq 0
,

\beta_{m}(A)=\P(X_{n}\in A\mid Z_{n}=m)\;,\qquad\tau=\inf\{k\geq 1\,:\,Z_{k}=0\}\;.

Observe that
Z_{\tau},Z_{\tau+1},\ldots
behaves as the Markov chain (
1.1
) defined by
p_{n}
in (
3.2
),
with the initial condition
Z_{\tau}=0
. Consequently,

\sum_{m=0}^{n}\P(Z_{t+n}=m\mid Z_{t}=0)\beta_{m}=\pi P^{n}\delta_{0}\;.

Suppose that
X_{0}\sim\mu
.
For
A\in\Sigma
,
n\geq t\geq 1
,
0\leq m<n
, we have

\displaystyle\P(X_{n}\in A,\,Z_{n}=m,\,\tau=t)

\displaystyle=\P(X_{n}\in A\mid Z_{n}=m,\,\tau=t)\P(Z_{n}=m\mid\tau=t)\P(\tau=t)

\displaystyle=\beta_{m}(A)\P(Z_{n}=m\mid Z_{t}=0)\P(\tau=t)\;.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\P(X_{n}\in A)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n-t}\P(X_{n}\in A,\,Z_{n}=m,\,\tau=t)+\P(X_{n}\in A,\,Z_{n}=-1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n-t}\beta_{m}(A)\P(Z_{n}=m\mid Z_{t}=0)\P(\tau=t)+\P(\tau>n,X_{n}\in A)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{n}\P(\tau=t)\pi[P^{n-t}\delta_{0}](A)+\P(\tau>n,X_{n}\in A)\;.\qed

Lemma
3.1
can be used to estimate memory loss, for example:

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{P}}(\Omega)
. Then

\|Q^{n}(\mu-\mu^{\prime})\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\sum_{t=1}^{n}\bigl|\mu(\tau=t)-\mu^{\prime}(\tau=t)\bigr|\,b_{t-1}(n-t)+2\mu(\tau>n)+2\mu^{\prime}(\tau>n)\;,

where
b_{\ell}(n)
are as in (
3.1
)
and
\mu(\tau=k)
is a shorthand for
\P(\tau=k\mid X_{0}\sim\mu)
.

Proof.

Let
X_{0}\in\mu
and let
X^{\prime}_{n}
be a copy of
X_{n}
with
X^{\prime}_{0}\sim\mu^{\prime}
.
Let
\tau^{\prime}
denote the copy of
\tau
.
By Lemma
3.1
,

\begin{split}Q^{n}\mu(A)-Q^{n}\mu^{\prime}(A)&=\pi\Bigl[\sum_{t=1}^{n}\bigl(\P(\tau=t)-\P(\tau^{\prime}=t)\bigr)P^{n-t}\delta_{0}\Bigr](A)\\
&\qquad+\P(X_{n}\in A\,,\tau>n)-\P(X^{\prime}_{n}\in A\,,\tau^{\prime}>n)\;.\end{split}

Furthermore
\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\bigl(\P(\tau=t)-\P(\tau^{\prime}=t)\bigr)P^{n-1}\delta_{0}=0
and thus

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{n}\bigl(\P(\tau=t)-\P(\tau^{\prime}=t)\bigr)P^{n-t}\delta_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{n}\bigl(\P(\tau=t)-\P(\tau^{\prime}=t)\bigr)(P^{n-t}\delta_{0}-P^{n-1}\delta_{0})

\displaystyle-(\P(\tau>n)-\P(\tau^{\prime}>n))P^{n-1}\delta_{0}\;.

The result follows readily.
∎

Corollary
3.2
can be strengthened when
\mu
and
\mu^{\prime}
overlap, i.e. when
|\mu-\mu^{\prime}|(\Omega)<2
.
For
\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{P}}(\Omega)
let

|\mu-\mu^{\prime}|(\tau=n)=|\mu-\mu^{\prime}|(\Omega)\,\P(\tau=n\mid X_{0}\sim\lambda)\;,

(3.3)

where
\lambda
is the normalized to probability difference
|\mu-\mu^{\prime}|
, namely

\lambda=\frac{|\mu-\mu^{\prime}|}{|\mu-\mu^{\prime}|(\Omega)}
, if
\mu\neq\mu^{\prime}
, and an arbitrary measure
otherwise.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{P}}(\Omega)
. Then

\|Q^{n}(\mu-\mu^{\prime})\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\sum_{t=1}^{n}|(\mu-\mu^{\prime})(\tau=t)|\,b_{t-1}(n-t)+2|\mu-\mu^{\prime}|(\tau>n)\;,

where
b_{\ell}(n)
are as in (
3.1
)
and
|\mu-\mu^{\prime}|(\tau=k)
is as in (
3.3
).

Proof.

Write
\mu-\mu^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}|\mu-\mu^{\prime}|(\Omega)\,(\kappa-\kappa^{\prime})
, where
\kappa
and
\kappa^{\prime}
are mutually
singular probability measures.
Then
\lambda=\frac{1}{2}|\kappa-\kappa^{\prime}|=\frac{1}{2}(\kappa+\kappa^{\prime})
and
\P(\tau=n\mid X_{0}\sim\lambda)=\frac{1}{2}(\kappa(\tau=n)+\kappa^{\prime}(\tau=n))
.
The result follows from Corollary
3.2
applied to
\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}
.
∎

3.2
Harris chains

Suppose now that
X_{n}
is a
Harris chain

[
Lin02
,
Dur19
]
, i.e. there exists
S\in\Sigma
,
\varepsilon>0
and a probability measure
\beta
on
\Omega
such that

(a)

\P(X_{n}\in S\text{ infinitely often}\mid X_{0}=x)=1
for each
x\in\Omega
, and

(b)

\P(X_{n+1}\in A\mid X_{n}=x)\geq\varepsilon\beta(A)
for all
A\in\Sigma
and
x\in S
.

Remark 3.4
.

If
\varepsilon=1
, then
S
is an atom and we can work with
X_{n}
as in Section
3.1
.
For
\varepsilon<1
the situation is not too different: using Nummelin’s splitting technique
[
Num78
]

we can extend
X_{n}
to a Markov chain with an atom.

Remark 3.5
.

There are different definitions of Harris (recurrent) chains in the literature, see e.g.
[
Num84
,
MT09
,
BH22
]
.
We work with a definition that is simple for our purposes.
See e.g.
[
NT82
]

that relates our definition to others.

Let
\xi_{n}
,
n\geq 0
, be a sequence of independent (also from the Markov chain
X_{n}
) Bernoulli random variables
with
\P(\xi_{n}=1)=\varepsilon
and
\P(\xi_{n}=0)=1-\varepsilon
.
Denote

\tau=\inf\{k\geq 1\,:\,X_{k-1}\in S\text{ and }\xi_{k-1}=1\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad p_{n}=\P(\tau=n\mid X_{0}\sim\beta)\;.

(3.4)

This way,
p_{n}
and
\tau
are geometrically compounded versions of (
3.2
).
Again,
\sum_{n\geq 1}p_{n}=1
.

Let
\beta_{m}
,
m\geq 0
, be probability measures on
\Omega
given by

\beta_{m}(A)=\P(X_{m}\in A\mid X_{0}\sim\beta\text{ and }\tau\geq m+1)\;.

For a measure
\zeta
on
\{0,1,\ldots\}
, let
\pi\zeta
be the measure on
\Omega
obtained by

\pi\zeta=\sum_{m\geq 0}\zeta(m)\beta_{m}\;.

The exact copy of Lemma
3.1
holds:

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
X_{0}\sim\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}(\Omega)
and
A\in\Sigma
. Then

\P(X_{n}\in A)=Q^{n}\mu(A)=\pi\Bigl[\sum_{t=1}^{n}\P(\tau=t)P^{n-t}\delta_{0}\Bigr](A)+\P(X_{n}\in A,\,\tau>n)\;.

Proof.

The strategy, following
[
Num78
]
, is to extend
X_{n}
to a Markov chain with an atom and then apply Lemma
3.1
.
To this end, let
H(x,A)=\P(X_{n+1}\in A\mid X_{n}=x)
denote the kernel of
X_{n}
.
Define
H_{0}(x,A)
by

H(x,A)=\begin{cases}\varepsilon\beta(A)+(1-\varepsilon)H_{0}(x,A)&\text{if }x\in S\;,\\
H_{0}(x,A)&\text{otherwise}\;,\end{cases}

where, if
\varepsilon=1
, we set
H_{0}(x,\cdot)
arbitrarily for
x\in S
.

Let
{\widehat{X}}_{n}
be the Markov chain on
{\widehat{\Omega}}=\Omega\times\{0,1\}
with kernel

{\widehat{H}}((x,0),(A,t))=\begin{cases}(1-\varepsilon)H_{0}(x,A\cap S)+H_{0}(x,A\setminus S)&\text{if }t=0\;,\\
\varepsilon H_{0}(x,A\cap S)&\text{if }t=1\;,\end{cases}

{\widehat{H}}((x,1),(A,t))={\hat{\beta}}(A,t):=\begin{cases}(1-\varepsilon)\beta(A\cap S)+\beta(A\setminus S)&\text{if }t=0\;,\\
\varepsilon\beta(A\cap S)&\text{if }t=1\;.\end{cases}

(3.5)

Recall that
X_{0}\sim\mu
and let
{\widehat{X}}_{0}\sim{\hat{\mu}}
where

{\hat{\mu}}(A\times\{0\})=\mu(A\setminus S)+(1-\varepsilon)\mu(A\cap S)\quad\text{and}\quad{\hat{\mu}}(A\times\{1\})=\varepsilon\mu(A\cap S)\;.

Observe that
\P({\widehat{X}}_{n}=(x,1)\mid{\widehat{X}}_{n}\in\{x\}\times\{0,1\})=\varepsilon\mathbf{1}_{x\in S}
.
It is then a direct verification that

\P\bigl({\widehat{X}}_{n+1}\in A\times\{0,1\}\mid{\widehat{X}}_{n}\in\{x\}\times\{0,1\}\bigr)=\P(X_{n+1}\in A\mid X_{n}=x)\;.

That is, the first component marginal process of
{\widehat{X}}_{n}
has the same law as
X_{n}
.
Moreover
{\widehat{S}}=S\times\{1\}
is an atom for
{\widehat{X}}_{n}
, which thus falls within the framework of Section
3.1

with
{\hat{\tau}}=\inf\{k\geq 1\,:\,{\widehat{X}}_{k-1}\in{\widehat{S}}\}
,
{\hat{\beta}}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\widehat{\Omega}})
as in (
3.5
),
and the corresponding projection
{\hat{\pi}}
.
Moreover,
\P(\tau=n\mid X_{0}\sim\beta)=\P({\hat{\tau}}=n\mid{\widehat{X}}_{0}\sim{\hat{\beta}})
,
so the distributions
p_{n}
for
X_{n}
and
{\widehat{X}}_{n}
coincide.
Thus, by Lemma
3.1
, writing
{\widehat{A}}=A\times\{0,1\}
,

\P({\widehat{X}}_{n}\in{\widehat{A}})={\hat{\pi}}\Bigl[\sum_{t=1}^{n}\P({\hat{\tau}}=t)P^{n-t}\delta_{0}\Bigr]({\widehat{A}})+\P({\widehat{X}}_{n}\in{\widehat{A}},\,{\hat{\tau}}>n)\;.

The hats can be removed, yielding the desired result.
∎

With the same proof as Corollary
3.3
, we obtain the following.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{P}}(\Omega)
. Then

\|Q^{n}(\mu-\mu^{\prime})\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\sum_{t=1}^{n}|(\mu-\mu^{\prime})(\tau=t)|\,b_{t-1}(n-t)+2|\mu-\mu^{\prime}|(\tau>n)\;,

where
b_{\ell}(n)
are as in (
3.1
)
and
|\mu-\mu^{\prime}|(\tau=k)
is as in (
3.3
).

3.3
An example

Suppose we are in the setting of Section
3.2
. In particular,
X
is a Harris chain.
Recall (
3.3
) where
\tau
is from (
3.4
). Corollary
3.7
provides a convenient way to estimate memory loss:

Proposition 3.8
.

Suppose that

b_{\ell}(n)=\|P^{n}\delta_{0}-P^{n+\ell}\delta_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\lesssim\rho(n)g(\ell)

uniformly in
n\geq\ell\geq 1
,
where
\rho,g
are RV with
g
increasing.
Denote

\|\mu-\mu^{\prime}\|_{g}=\sum_{\ell\geq 0}g(\ell)|\mu-\mu^{\prime}|(\tau=\ell)=|\mu-\mu^{\prime}|(\Omega)\,\E_{X_{0}\sim\lambda}g(\tau)\;,

(3.6)

where
\lambda=\frac{|\mu-\mu^{\prime}|}{|\mu-\mu^{\prime}|(\Omega)}
.
Then

\|Q^{n}\mu-Q^{n}\mu^{\prime}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\lesssim\{\rho(n)+1/g(n)\}\|\mu-\mu^{\prime}\|_{g}\;.

(3.7)

Proof.

Denote
\mu_{\ell}=\mu(\tau=\ell),\mu^{\prime}_{\ell}=\mu^{\prime}(\tau=\ell)
.
Remark that

|\mu_{\ell}-\mu^{\prime}_{\ell}|=\Bigl|\int_{\Omega}\P(\tau=\ell\mid X_{0}=x)(\mu-\mu^{\prime})(dx)\Bigr|\leq|\mu-\mu^{\prime}|(\tau=\ell)\;.

By Corollary
3.7
,

\|Q^{n}(\mu-\mu^{\prime})\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\bigl|\mu_{\ell}-\mu^{\prime}_{\ell}\bigr|\,b_{\ell-1}(n-\ell)+2|\mu-\mu^{\prime}|(\tau>n)\;.

Then, using
b_{\ell}(n)\lesssim 1\wedge g(\ell)\rho(n)
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}|\mu_{\ell}-\mu^{\prime}_{\ell}|b_{\ell-1}(n-\ell)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n/2<\ell\leq n}|\mu_{\ell}-\mu^{\prime}_{\ell}|+\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq n/2}|\mu_{\ell}-\mu^{\prime}_{\ell}|g(\ell)\rho(n-\ell)

\displaystyle\lesssim(g(n)^{-1}+\rho(n))\|\mu-\mu^{\prime}\|_{g}\;,

where we used
\sum_{n/2<\ell\leq n}|\mu_{\ell}-\mu^{\prime}_{\ell}|\lesssim g(n)^{-1}\|\mu-\mu^{\prime}\|_{g}
.
Finally, since
g
is increasing, we clearly have
|\mu-\mu^{\prime}|(\tau>n)\leq g(n)^{-1}\|\mu-\mu^{\prime}\|_{g}
.
∎

4
Ornstein’s coupling

In this section we prove Theorem
2.6
, adapting Ornstein’s proof
[
Orn69a
,
Orn69b
]

of Orey’s theorem. The proof is based on reduction to a symmetric random walk on integers and coupling
when the random walk reaches
0
.

4.1
Meeting times and coupling inequality

Consider probability distributions
\mu,\nu
on a state space
\Omega
and a transition operator
P\colon{\mathcal{P}}(\Omega)\to{\mathcal{P}}(\Omega)
.
We recall how to bound
\|P^{n}\mu-P^{n}\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
by coupling.
Consider
\Omega
-valued Markov chains
X,Y
, defined on the same probability space, with initial distributions
X_{0}\sim\mu,Y_{0}\sim\nu
.
Suppose that exists a random time
T>0
such that
X_{n}=Y_{n}
for all
n\geq T
.
Then

\P(X_{n}\neq Y_{n})\leq\P(T>n)

which in particular implies that, for any measurable
A\subset\Omega
,

\displaystyle\P(X_{n}\in A)

\displaystyle=\P(X_{n}\in A,\,T\leq n)+\P(X_{n}\in A,\,T>n)\leq\P(Y_{n}\in A,\,T\leq n)+\P(T>n)

\displaystyle\leq\P(Y_{n}\in A)+\P(T>n)\;.

By symmetry,
\P(Y_{n}\in A)\leq\P(X_{n}\in A)+\P(T>n)
.
We thus obtain the
coupling inequality,
a simple yet very useful tool
for estimating total variation (see e.g.
[
Lin02
, Chapter I]
):

\|P^{n}\mu-P^{n}\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=2\sup_{A}|\P(X_{n}\in A)-\P(Y_{n}\in A)|\leq 2\P(T>n)\;.

(4.1)

4.2
Proof of Theorem
2.6

By the aperiodicity condition (
2.1
), there exists
A\geq 1
such that
p_{A},p_{A+1}>0
.
Let
X
be the Markov chain (
1.1
) with
X_{0}=0
.
Let
a_{0}=\inf\{j\geq\ell\,:\,X_{j}=0\}
and
a_{k+1}=\inf\{j>a_{k}:X_{j}=0\}
index visits of
X
to
0
after time
\ell
.
Let
\Delta
denote backward increments, so
\Delta a_{k}=a_{k}-a_{k-1}

and
\P(\Delta a_{k}=n)=p_{n}
for all
k\geq 1
.

On the same (possibly enriched) probability space as
X
,
we now construct another Markov chain
Y
with the same transition probabilities but with initial state
Y_{0}=X_{\ell}\sim P^{\ell}\delta_{0}
.
To define
Y
, it suffices to specify the times
b_{0}=\inf\{j\geq 0\,:\,Y_{j}=0\}
and
b_{k+1}=\inf\{j>b_{k}\,:\,Y_{j}=0\}

that index the visits of
Y
to
0
.
First, let
b_{0}=a_{0}-\ell
so that
Y_{0}=X_{\ell},Y_{1}=X_{\ell+1},\ldots,Y_{b_{0}}=X_{a_{0}}
.
Then we define
b_{k}
for
k\geq 1
by

\Delta b_{k}=\begin{cases}\Delta a_{k}&\text{if }a_{k-1}=b_{k-1}\text{ or }\Delta a_{k}\notin\{p_{A},p_{A+1}\}\;,\\
s_{k}&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where
s_{k}
is a sequence of independent (also from
X
) and identically distributed
random variables with
\P(s_{k}=A)=p_{A}/(p_{A}+p_{A+1})
and
\P(s_{k}=A+1)=p_{A+1}/(p_{A}+p_{A+1})
.
This completes the construction of
Y
.
Remark that, for
k\geq 1
,
\Delta b_{k}
has the same distribution as
\Delta a_{k}
, thus
Y
indeed has the same
transition operator as
X
.

Denote
d_{k}=a_{k}-b_{k}
. Let
k_{0}=0
and let
k_{i}
,
i\geq 1
, index when
d_{k}
can change, namely

k_{i+1}=\inf\{j>k_{i}\,:\,\Delta a_{j}\in\{p_{A},p_{A+1}\}\}
.
Observe that
\Delta a_{k_{i}}=a_{k_{i}}-a_{k_{i-1}}
are i.i.d. with
\P(\Delta a_{k_{i}}\geq j)\leq\rho(j)
.
Furthermore,
d_{k_{0}},d_{k_{1}},\ldots
is a symmetric random walk on integers starting at
d_{k_{0}}=d_{0}=\ell\geq 1

and stopping when it reaches
0
, with
\Delta d_{k_{i}}=d_{k_{i}}-d_{k_{i-1}}\in\{-1,0,1\}
and

\P(\Delta d_{k_{i}}=\pm 1\mid d_{k_{i-1}}\neq 0)=p_{A}p_{A+1}/(p_{A}+p_{A+1})^{2}\;.

Let
\sigma=\inf\{j>0\,:\,d_{k_{j}}=0\}
.
Consider a walk
Z_{i}
with the same transition probabilities as
d_{k_{i}}
but without stopping,
such that
Z_{i}=\ell-d_{k_{i}}
for
i\leq\sigma
.
Note that
\sigma=\{j>0\,:\,Z_{j}=\ell\}
.
By the reflection principle,

\P(\sigma\leq n)=\P(\max_{i\leq n}Z_{i}\geq\ell)=\P(Z_{n}=\ell)+2\P(Z_{n}>\ell)=\P(Z_{n}=\ell)+\P(|Z_{n}|>\ell)

and thus, uniformly in
n,\ell
,
\P(\sigma>n)\leq\P(|Z_{n}|\leq\ell)\lesssim\ell n^{-1/2}
.

Now let
m,n\geq 0
.
Using (
4.1
) and that
X_{n}=Y_{n}
for all
n\geq a_{k_{\sigma}}
, we obtain

\begin{split}\|P^{n}\delta_{0}-P^{n+\ell}\delta_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}&\leq 2\P(a_{k_{\sigma}}>n)\leq 2\P(a_{k_{m}}\geq n)+2\P(\sigma\geq m)\\
&\lesssim\P(a_{k_{m}}\geq n)+\ell m^{-1/2}\;.\end{split}

(4.2)

It remains to bound
\P(a_{k_{m}}\geq n)
and choose a suitable
m
.
Let
c_{i}=\Delta a_{k_{i}}\mathbf{1}\{\Delta a_{k_{i}}\leq B\}
with
0<B\leq n
.
Then

\P(a_{k_{m}}\geq n)\leq\P\Bigl(\max_{i\leq m}\Delta a_{k_{i}}\geq B\Bigr)+\P\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\geq n\Bigr)\;.

(4.3)

For the first probability, we have

\P\Bigl(\max_{i\leq m}\Delta a_{k_{i}}\geq B\Bigr)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\P(\Delta a_{k_{i}}\geq B)\leq m\rho(B)\;.

(4.4)

For the second probability, we use Hoeffding’s inequality.
For this, using
\P(\Delta a_{k_{n}}=i)=p_{i}
, write

\E c_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{B}ip_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{Var}(c_{1})\leq\E c_{1}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{B}i^{2}p_{i}\;.

Recall that we assume that
\alpha<3/2
.
Then
\mathrm{Var}(c_{1})\lesssim B^{2}\rho(B)
.
By
[
Hoe63
, Theorem 3]
, for
m\E c_{1}\leq n/2
,

\P\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\geq n\Bigr)\leq\P\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}-m\E c_{1}\geq n/2\Bigr)\leq\exp\Bigl[-\frac{n}{2B}h\Bigl(\frac{2m\mathrm{Var}(c_{1})}{nB}\Bigr)\Bigr]

where
h(z)=(1+z)\log(1+z^{-1})-1
.
Let
\gamma=\inf_{z\in(0,1)}\frac{h(z)}{\log(z^{-1})}
and note that
\gamma>0
. Thus

\displaystyle\P\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\geq n\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\Bigl[-\frac{n}{2B}\gamma\log\Bigl(\frac{nB}{2m\mathrm{Var}(c_{1})}\Bigr)\Bigr]=\Bigl(\frac{2m\mathrm{Var}(c_{1})}{nB}\Bigr)^{\frac{\gamma n}{2B}}

\displaystyle\leq K^{\gamma n/B}\Bigl(\frac{mB\rho(B)}{n}\Bigr)^{\frac{\gamma n}{2B}}\;,

where
K
is uniform in
n,m
and where we used that, if
\frac{2m\mathrm{Var}(c_{1})}{nB}\geq 1
, then the first bound trivially holds.
Taking
B=\frac{n}{2p}
for
p\geq 1
, we obtain

\P\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\geq n\Bigr)\lesssim(m\rho(n))^{\gamma p}

and therefore, by (
4.2
), (
4.3
), and (
4.4
), for fixed
p\geq\gamma^{-1}

and uniformly in
m,n
such that
m\E c_{1}\leq n/2
,

\begin{split}\|P^{n}\delta_{0}-P^{n+\ell}\delta_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}&\lesssim\ell m^{-1/2}+m\rho(n)+(m\rho(n))^{\gamma p}\\
&\lesssim\ell m^{-1/2}+m\rho(n)\;.\end{split}

(4.5)

Now we treat the cases
\alpha<1
,
\alpha=1
and
\alpha\in(1,\frac{3}{2})
separately.

First suppose that
\alpha<1
. The result is trivial if
\ell^{2/3}\rho(n)^{1/3}>1
,
so we assume that
\ell^{2/3}\rho(n)^{1/3}\leq 1
.
To optimise, we take
m=1+\lfloor\ell^{2/3}\rho(n)^{-2/3}\rfloor
,
which implies
\ell m^{-1/2}\asymp m\rho(n)\asymp\ell^{2/3}\rho(n)^{1/3}
.
Note that

m\E c_{1}\lesssim mB\rho(B)\ll mn\rho(n)\asymp\ell^{2/3}\rho(n)^{1/3}n\leq n\;,

where the asymptotic ratio can be made arbitrarily large by taking
p
large.
Hence with a suitably large
p
we achieve
m\E c_{1}\leq n/2

for all sufficiently large
n
.
Therefore (
4.5
) holds and we conclude

\|P^{n}\delta_{0}-P^{n+\ell}\delta_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\lesssim\ell^{2/3}\rho(n)^{1/3}
.

Suppose now that
\alpha=1
. Then
\E c_{1}\lesssim M(B)=1+\int_{1}^{B}\rho(t)\,dt
, which is slowly varying in
B
and increasing.
We take
m=1+\lfloor\delta\ell^{2/3}\rho(n)^{-2/3}\rfloor
for some small
\delta>0
.
Now the constraint
m\E c_{1}\leq n/2
is satisfied for sufficiently large
n
if

\ell\ll\rho(n)M(n)^{-3/2}n^{3/2}
, and the proof proceeds as for
\alpha<1
.
The case
\ell\leq\rho(n)M(n)^{-3/2}n^{3/2}
as in the statement of the theorem
follows from a bounded number of applications of the triangle inequality.

Suppose now that
\alpha\in(1,\frac{3}{2})
. Then
\E c_{1}\lesssim 1
.
To optimise, we take
m
as a small multiple of
1+\lfloor\ell^{2/3}\rho(n)^{-2/3}\rfloor
,
so that
\ell m^{-1/2}\asymp m\rho(n)\asymp\ell^{2/3}\rho(n)^{1/3}
.
Then, for
n
sufficiently large,
m\E c_{1}\leq\ell^{2/3}\rho(n)^{-2/3}\leq n/2

by the assumption that
\ell\leq\rho(n)n^{3/2}
.
Once again (
4.5
) holds and we conclude
\|P^{n}\delta_{0}-P^{n+\ell}\delta_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\lesssim\ell^{2/3}\rho(n)^{1/3}
.
The proof of Theorem
2.6
is complete.

Proof of Theorem

5.1
Proof of Lemma
2.9

Recall that
z_{0}=1
and

Assume the DFR in this subsection.
Excluding the trivial case
p_{1}=1

p_{n}=0
for

for all

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose that
b_{k}
,
k\geq 0
, is a non-negative sequence with
\frac{b_{k+1}}{b_{k}}\leq\frac{z_{k+1}}{z_{k}}

for all
k
. Let
c_{k}=b_{k+1}+p_{k+1}b_{0}
for
k\geq 0
. Then

\frac{c_{k+1}}{c_{k}}\leq\frac{z_{k+1}}{z_{k}}
for all
k
.

Proof.

Fix
k\geq 0
and write

\frac{c_{k+1}}{c_{k}}=\frac{b_{k+2}+p_{k+2}b_{0}}{b_{k+1}+p_{k+1}b_{0}}=\lambda\frac{b_{k+2}}{b_{k+1}}+(1-\lambda)\frac{p_{k+2}}{p_{k+1}}\;,

where
\lambda=\frac{b_{k+1}}{b_{k+1}+p_{k+1}b_{0}}\in(0,1]
.
Since
\frac{p_{k+2}}{p_{k+1}}\leq\frac{z_{k+1}}{z_{k}}
,
it suffices to consider the case
\frac{b_{k+2}}{b_{k+1}}>\frac{z_{k+1}}{z_{k}}
.
Then, since
\lambda
is monotone decreasing function of
b_{0}
(with all other variables fixed),
so is
\frac{c_{k+1}}{c_{k}}
.
In turn, from
\frac{b_{k+1}}{b_{0}}\leq\frac{z_{k+1}}{z_{0}}
we obtain
b_{0}\geq\frac{b_{k+1}}{z_{k+1}}

and

\frac{c_{k+1}}{c_{k}}\leq\frac{b_{k+2}+p_{k+2}\frac{b_{k+1}}{z_{k+1}}}{b_{k+1}+p_{k+1}\frac{b_{k+1}}{z_{k+1}}}=\frac{\frac{b_{k+2}}{b_{k+1}}+\frac{p_{k+2}}{z_{k+1}}}{1+\frac{p_{k+1}}{z_{k+1}}}\leq\frac{\frac{z_{k+2}}{z_{k+1}}+\frac{p_{k+2}}{z_{k+1}}}{1+\frac{p_{k+1}}{z_{k+1}}}=\frac{z_{k+1}}{z_{k}}\;.\qed

Corollary 5.2
.

u_{n}\geq u_{n+1}
for all
n\geq 0
.

Proof.

Consider a Markov chain
Y_{n}
on non-negative integers with the connection graph (
1.1
)
but all arrows reversed, starting at
Y_{0}=0
with transition probabilities

\P(Y_{n+1}=k\mid Y_{n}=k+1)=1\quad\text{and}\quad\P(Y_{n+1}=k\mid Y_{n}=0)=p_{k+1}\quad\text{for}\quad k\geq 0\;.

Set
b_{k}^{n}=\P(Y_{n}=k)
. Note that
b_{k}^{n+1}=b_{k+1}^{n}+p_{k+1}b_{0}^{n}
.
Observe that the renewal probabilities
\P(Y_{n}=0\mid Y_{n}=0)
coincide with those for the Markov chain (
1.1
),
hence
u_{n}=b_{0}^{n}
. By Lemma
5.1
applied inductively,

\frac{b_{k+1}^{n}}{b_{k}^{n}}\leq\frac{z_{k+1}}{z_{k}}
for all
n,k
,
and in particular
\frac{b_{1}^{n}}{b_{0}^{n}}\leq\frac{z_{1}}{z_{0}}
.
Then

\frac{u_{n+1}}{u_{n}}=\frac{b_{1}^{n}+p_{1}b_{0}^{n}}{b_{0}^{n}}=\frac{b_{1}^{n}}{b_{0}^{n}}+p_{1}\leq\frac{z_{1}^{n}}{z_{0}^{n}}+p_{1}=1\;.\qed

,

|A(x)|\lesssim\frac{\ell}{\rho(|x|^{-1})}\;,

|B(x)|\lesssim\frac{\ell|x|\wedge 1}{\rho(|x|^{-1})^{2}}\;.

(6.27)

Therefore, using (
6.21
),

|H^{\prime}(x)|\lesssim\frac{\ell}{\rho(|x|^{-1})}+\frac{\ell|x|\wedge 1}{|x|\rho(|x|^{-1})}\lesssim\frac{\ell}{\rho(|x|^{-1})}

and thus, since
\rho
is RV with index
-\alpha>-1
,

\int_{-2\delta}^{2\delta}|H^{\prime}(x+\delta)-H^{\prime}(x)|\,dx\lesssim\int_{-2\delta}^{2\delta}\frac{\ell}{\rho(|x|^{-1})}\,dx\lesssim\frac{\ell\delta}{\rho(\delta^{-1})}\;.

Suppose now that
|x|>2\delta
.
To handle the second term in (
6.26
),
by (
6.21
) and Lemma
6.9
,

|B^{\prime}(x)|\lesssim\frac{\ell}{\rho(|x|^{-1})^{2}}+\frac{\ell|x|\wedge 1}{|x|\rho(|x|^{-1})^{2}}\lesssim\frac{\ell}{\rho(|x|^{-1})^{2}}\;.

Therefore, by (
6.21
)-(
6.22
) and (
6.27
),

|B(x+\delta)P^{\prime}(x+\delta)-B(x)P^{\prime}(x)|\lesssim\frac{\delta\ell}{|x|\rho(|x|^{-1})}+\frac{(\ell|x|\wedge 1)\rho(\delta^{-1})}{|x|\rho(|x|^{-1})^{2}}\;.

The integral of the first term over
|x|>2\delta
is
\ell\delta/\rho(\delta^{-1})
as earlier,
while the integral of the second term over
|x|>2\delta
is

\int_{1/\ell}^{\pi}\frac{\rho(\delta^{-1})}{|x|\rho(|x|^{-1})^{2}}\,dx+\int_{2\delta}^{1/\ell}\frac{\ell\rho(\delta^{-1})}{\rho(|x|^{-1})^{2}}\,dx\;.

The first integral is proportional to
\rho(\delta^{-1})/\rho(\ell)^{2}
.
The second integral, recalling that
\rho(z)=z^{-\alpha}L(z)
and
\delta<\frac{1}{4\ell}
, is equal to

\ell\rho(\delta^{-1})\int_{\ell}^{\frac{1}{2\delta}}\rho(y)^{-2}y^{-2}\,dy\asymp\begin{cases}\rho(\delta^{-1})\rho(\ell)^{-2}&\alpha<1/2\;,\\
\ell\rho(\delta^{-1})\{K(\delta^{-1})-K(\ell)\}&\alpha=1/2\;,\\
\ell\delta\rho(\delta^{-1})^{-1}&\alpha>1/2\;.\end{cases}

(6.28)

To handle the first term in (
6.26
),
we write

\displaystyle A(x+\delta)-A(x)

\displaystyle=\ell e_{-\ell}(x+\delta)\bigl(U(x+\delta)-U(x)\bigr)+\ell\bigl(e_{-\ell}(x+\delta)-e_{-\ell}(x)\bigr)U(x)

\displaystyle=A_{1}(x,\delta)+A_{2}(x,\delta)\;.

By (
6.24
),
|U^{\prime}(x)|\lesssim|x|^{-1}\rho(|x|^{-1})^{-1}
,
hence
|U(x+\delta)-U(x)|\lesssim\delta|x|^{-1}\rho(|x|^{-1})^{-1}
, and thus

\int_{|x|>2\delta}|A_{1}(x,\delta)|\,dx\lesssim\frac{\ell\delta}{\rho(\delta^{-1})}\;.

To handle
A_{2}(x,\delta)=\ell\bigl(F(x)-F(x+\delta)\bigr)U(x)
, note that, for
z\in[2\delta,\pi]
, by (
6.13
),

\int_{|x|\in[2\delta,z]}|A_{2}(x,\delta)|\,dx\lesssim\frac{\ell^{2}\delta z}{\rho(z^{-1})}\;.

(6.29)

Furthermore, similar to (
6.14
),

\begin{split}\Big|\int_{|x|>z}A_{2}(x,\delta)e^{-\mathbf{i}(n-\ell)x}\,dx\Big|&\leq 2\ell\Big|\int_{|x|>z}U(x)e^{-\mathbf{i}(n-\ell)x}\,dx\Big|\\
&\lesssim\frac{\ell\delta}{\rho(z^{-1})}+\int_{z}^{\pi}\frac{\ell\delta}{x\rho(x^{-1})}\,dx\lesssim\frac{\ell\delta}{\rho(z^{-1})}\;,\end{split}

(6.30)

where in the 2nd bound we used Lemma
6.11
with
h(y)=y^{-1}
and (
6.24
).
Taking
z=\ell^{-1}\geq 2\delta
,
both (
6.29
) and (
6.30
) yield a contribution of order
\ell\delta\rho(\ell)^{-1}\lesssim\ell\delta\rho(\delta^{-1})^{-1}
.

Finally, note that
\ell\delta/\rho(\delta^{-1})
and
\rho(\delta^{-1})/\rho(\ell)^{2}
, for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
are bounded by a multiple of (
6.28
) (for
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
we use

K(\delta^{-1})-K(\ell)\gtrsim L(\delta^{-1})^{-2}+L(\ell)^{-2}
since
\delta^{-1}\gg\ell
, as in (
6.17
)).
Therefore (
6.25
) is bounded by the right-hand side of (
6.28
), concluding the proof.
∎

Appendix A
Memory loss for positive recurrent Markov chains

Here we justify (
1.2
). We use notation from Section
2.1
,
in particular the sequences
u_{n}=\P(X_{n}=0\mid X_{0}=0)

and
z_{n}=\sum_{k>n}p_{k}
.
We start by recalling the following special case of
[
MO14
, Theorem 2.2]
:

Theorem A.1
.

Suppose that
\alpha>1
. If
z_{n}\lesssim n^{-\alpha}
, then
|u_{n}-u_{n+1}|\lesssim n^{-\alpha}
.

To prove (
1.2
), consider
\alpha>1
.
It suffices to consider
\ell=1
and apply the triangle inequality.
By (
2.3
) and Theorem
A.1
,

\displaystyle\|P^{n+1}\delta_{0}-P^{n}\delta_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}

\displaystyle=z_{n+1}+\sum_{m=0}^{n}|u_{n+1-m}-u_{n-m}|z_{m}\

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{-\alpha}+\sum_{m=0}^{n}(n-m+1)^{-\alpha}(m+1)^{-\alpha}\lesssim n^{-\alpha}\;,

as desired.

Remark A.2
.

, the proof of which in

]

There are other ways to prove (
1.2
), for example we were able to adapt
[

,

) under the strong moment assumption

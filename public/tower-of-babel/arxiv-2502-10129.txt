Planckian Bounds From Local Uncertainty Relations
Zohar Nussinov, Saurish Chakrabarty

Abstract
We introduce “local uncertainty relations” in thermal many-body systems, from which fundamental bounds in quantum systems can be derived. These lead to universal non-relativistic speed limits (independent of interaction range) and transport coefficient bounds (e.g., those of the diffusion constant and viscosity) that are compared against experimental data.

Viscosity bounds may be derived similar to those derived for the diffusion using the methods of Sections
V
and
VI
.
These will generally depend on system details such as the interactions
{\cal V}
. In what follows, we review and discuss Eyring inspired bounds for the viscosity
[
23
,
24
,
11
,
5
,
15
,
16
]
that lead to a form that depends only on the number density (number of particles per unit volume)
{\sf n}
. Here, we will further part from our more general recipe of Sections
III
and
IV
. The form of these bounds was inspired (yet treated very differently from his model for “hole” motion in liquids
[
24
]
in our own work) by Eyring’s studies of chemical reaction rates
[
23
]
.

What now follows is a review of an intuitive argument for such a bound. As is well known, in conventional “classical” systems, the shear viscosity exhibits a minimum as a function of the temperature
T
at the lower temperature end of the gaseous phase. This minimum appears since in the liquid the viscosity generally drops with increasing temperature while in the gas the viscosity is monotonically increasing. To obtain a trivial bound, similar to other works, e.g.,
[
21
]
we examined the viscosity in the gas phase
[
11
,
5
,
67
]
. The shear viscosity
\eta
of a classical gas is related to the collision time
\tau_{\sf coll}
by

\eta={\sf n}k_{B}T\tau_{\sf coll}.

(16)

We next write a rather trivial and general bound on the collision time in a classical gas. In classical gases, the mean free path exceeds the de-Broglie wavelength and thus the thermal ratio between de-Broglie wavelength
h/{|\bf{p}}|
of the particles and their speed
|{\bf{p}}|/m
is a lower bound on
\tau_{\sf coll}
. We return to the non-relativistic Hamiltonians of Eq. (
4
) having a potential energy depending only on particle position. Regardless of the interaction strengths, particle density, and all other details, the classical thermal average of the latter ratio is readily evaluated being, similar to the theme of this article, a local (here, a single particle) average,

\frac{\int d^{3}{\bf{p}}\penalty\ \penalty\ e^{-\beta{\bf{p}}^{2}/(2m)}\penalty\ \penalty\ (hm/{\bf{{p}}}^{2})}{\int d^{3}{\bf{p}}\penalty\ \penalty\ e^{-\beta{\bf{p}}^{2}/(2m)}}=\frac{h}{k_{B}T}.

(17)

Plugging this into Eq. (
16
) leads to a trivial viscosity bound
[
5
]
of a non-degenerate classical gas (and thus, as explained above, of conventional classical gases in general) that is independent of
all details apart from the particle density
,

\displaystyle\boxed{\eta\geq{\sf n}h.}

(18)

This bound is similar to more rigorous inequalities derived from the local uncertainty relations
[
5
]
yet is generally tighter than these. We reiterate and underscore that the average of Eq. (
17
)
always
applies in classical systems regardless of interaction details and particle density. By contrast, Eq. (
16
) along with the condition of the mean free path being smaller than the de Broglie wavelength only holds for non degenerate classical gases. As we discuss next the bound of Eq. (
18
) may be violated in low temperature helium gases.

with

. To examine the effect of disorder and general athermal systems, we return to our diffusion constant bounds of Sections
V
and
VI

[
5
]
. Towards this end, we start with the time integral of Eq. (
5
),
D=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\langle v^{H}_{i\ell}(t)v^{H}_{i\ell}(0)\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}G_{v}(t)dt
, yet now
do not
specialize to averages taken with the canonical probability density
\rho^{\sf canonical}_{\Lambda}
. In the fully localized system of immobile impurities, the diffusion constant vanishes,

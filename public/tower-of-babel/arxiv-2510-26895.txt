Exact and approximate conditions of tabletop reversibility: when is Petz recovery cost-free?
Minjeong Song, Hyukjoon Kwon, Valerio Scarani

Abstract
Channels 𝒩 \mathcal{N} that describe open quantum dynamics are inherently irreversible: it is impossible to undo their effect completely, but one can study partial recovery of the information. The Petz recovery map 𝒩 ^ γ ( P ) \hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\gamma}^{(\texttt{P})} is a systematic construction that depends only on 𝒩 \mathcal{N} and on a reference state γ \gamma , which will be recovered exactly. If the real input state was different from γ \gamma , the recovery is partial, with a guarantee of near-optimality. Generically, an implementation of the Petz recovery map would look very different from the implementation of the channel. It is natural to study under which conditions the two maps require similar or even identical resources. The noisy forward channel 𝒩 \mathcal{N} is called tabletop time-reversible for a given γ \gamma when the corresponding Petz recovery map is realizable in such a way. First, we study the exact tabletop reversibility (TTR) conditions. We show in particular that a time-sensitive control of an ancilla system is needed. Second, we present the approximate TTR conditions, which do not require such a time-sensitive control. Third, we derive Lindbladian TTR conditions under a random-time collision model.



I
Introduction

All practical quantum information tasks confront dissipative noise from the surrounding bath over time
Breuer and Petruccione 2007
. This inevitable phenomenon leads to irreversibility of noisy quantum dynamics. Nonetheless, such noisy quantum dynamics can be recovered by considering recovery channels for some quantum states. A recovery channel, known as a
Petz recovery map
, has particularly proven useful in many research areas. Quantum error correction is one of its prominent applications
since a Petz recovery map for a code space is shown to be the optimal recovery when the code is perfectly correctable
Barnum and Knill 2002
. Moreover, it performs nearly optimal compared to existing quantum error correcting codes together with recovery algorithms when the code is not too far from perfect codes
Barnum and Knill 2002
;
Ng and Mandayam 2010
;
Chen et al. 2020
;
Cree and Sorce 2022
;
Zheng et al. 2024
;
Biswas et al. 2025
;
Li et al. 2025
.

It is a natural figure of merit for a recovery what states can be exactly recovered, and if approximate, how close the recovered state is from the initial state before noise.
The former has been answered in the original paper by Petz
Petz 1986
;
Petz 1988
(see also Ref.
Petz 2003
for a less technical proof). It was shown that a Petz recovery map successfully recovers states when they satisfy some entropic conditions, i.e., when the relative entropy between the state to recover and a so-called reference state remains unchanged under the noisy dynamics
Hayden et al. 2004
. An approximation of this condition concerns a question as to how the changes in the relative entropy affect the distance between the initial state and the recovered state. A Petz recovery map and its variants have then enabled an information theoretic formulation of recoverability in terms of relative entropies
Wilde 2015
;
Sutter et al. 2016a
;
Junge et al. 2018
. They also made appearance in a plethora of studies on recoverability in the special case concerning a tripartite system in terms of the conditional mutual information
Fawzi and Renner 2015
;
Sutter et al. 2016b
;
Kim 2021
;
Hu and Zou 2024
;
Kim et al. 2024
. Namely, if a conditional mutual information between two subsystems conditioning the other is zero, a Petz recovery map can recover a global state from a local state of the two subsystems only and the global state is called a quantum Markov chain
Hayden et al. 2004
. A Petz recovery map manifests in many other quantum foundation. To name a few, it has been adopted as a crucial tool for formulating quantum fluctuation theorems
Åberg 2018
;
Kwon and Kim 2019
;
Aw et al. 2021
;
Buscemi and Scarani 2021
and has appeared as one of the promising candidates of quantum Bayesian retrodiction in developing quantum Bayes’ rule
Leifer and Spekkens 2013
;
Tsang 2022
;
Parzygnat and Buscemi 2023
;
Parzygnat and Fullwood 2023
;
Cenxin et al. 2023
;
Scandi et al. 2025
;
Surace and Scandi 2023
;
Bai et al. 2025
;
Liu et al. 2025a
;
Liu et al. 2025b
;
Liu et al. 2025c
.

Apart from its fundamental importance, the implementation of Petz recovery maps is substantial to make it practically useful. There have been a few developments toward this direction. The first attempt to implement Petz recovery maps approximately was given in Ref.
Gilyén et al. 2022
. Later, circuit-design approaches have been proposed based on the resource availability in experimental setups (e.g. the circuit depth or the number of ancilla qubits) in the context of quantum error correction focusing on a code space
Biswas et al. 2024
. Subsequently, an experimental realization of Petz recovery maps has been recently developed for a qubit decoherence channel by designing exact quantum circuits in trapped ion platforms
Png and Scarani 2025
, and in NMR platforms
Singh et al. 2025
.

Here, we approach this problem in a different perspective— we ask when a Petz recovery map can be implemented without requiring additional devices than the existing ones on the table. Specifically, we ask when a Petz recovery map can be realized with the same devices that were available for the forward dynamics, thus enabling ‘cost-free’ but still nearly optimal reversal. This approach was first introduced in Ref.
Aw et al. 2024
, and the forward dynamics is said to be
tabletop time-reversible
(TTR) when such a realization is possible.

In this work, we study conditions of TTR in detail. We present the exact conditions of TTR in a directly usable form and we demonstrate it with illustrative examples. These conditions provide an insightful connection with the sufficient condition of TTR, product preservation, previously found in Ref.
Aw et al. 2024
—it provides a straightforward explanation of why the product preservation is a strictly sufficient, but not necessary condition. Yet, the exact TTR conditions require a control of an ancilla system in a timely manner in general. We thus turn to approximate TTR conditions by which we circumvent such a time-sensitive control and present the first two non-trivial approximate TTR conditions; first-order approximate TTR and second-order approximate TTR. While such approximate TTR conditions contain complex terms in general, we present simpler forms of approximate TTR conditions for some special choices of a reference state. This includes the first-order approximate implementation of the class of Petz recovery maps relevant to quantum error correction. In addition, we present Lindbladian conditions of approximate TTR, with which one can implement Petz recovery maps sequentially. TTR-based approaches enable the implementation of Petz recovery maps, which does not require excessive resources as the unitary operations for the reversal are considered accessible and require only at most the same number of ancilla qubits as that of ancilla for the forward dynamics.

Definition 1

Given a channel

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\gamma}^{(\texttt{P})}(\bullet)\equiv\sqrt{\gamma}\mathcal{N}^{\dagger}\quantity(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\N(\gamma)}}

Notably, choosing a pure prior state always makes the Petz recovery map trivial—it becomes an erasure channel that outputs the prior state, regardless of its input. That is, if one holds a too strong belief in the prior state, the reversal always outputs the prior state. In what follows, we will consider full rank priors only so as to avoid such trivial cases. In addition, we assume that the prior state after the forward dynamics remains full rank, as otherwise the Petz recovery maps are ill-defined or not uniquely defined

Though written as a sequential composition of three maps, the Petz recovery map should not be implemented as such a composition, because those separate maps are not, in general, trace-preserving, while their composition is. Rather, one should find its form as in Eq. (
1
), which is known only for a few cases. A prominent example is thermal operations from quantum thermodynamics:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(\bullet)=\Tr_{\texttt{E}}\left[U_{\texttt{SE}}(\bullet\otimes\rho_{\texttt{th}}^{(\beta)}(H_{\texttt{E}}))U_{\texttt{SE}}^{\dagger}\right],

\commutator{U_{\texttt{SE}}}{H_{\texttt{S}}+H_{\texttt{E}}}=0

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\rho_{\texttt{th}}^{(\beta)}(H_{\texttt{S}})}^{(\texttt{P})}(\bullet)=\Tr_{\texttt{E}}\left[U_{\texttt{SE}}^{\dagger}(\bullet\otimes\rho_{\texttt{th}}^{(\beta)}(H_{\texttt{E}}))U_{\texttt{SE}}\right].

Here, implementing the Petz recovery map
does not require building additional devices that carry out a joint unitary for the reversal— it simply uses the existing devices from the forward dynamics.

Generalizing from this example, we introduce the following definition:

Definition 2

(Tabletop reverse map)

Given a channel

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\xi^{\prime}}^{(\texttt{T})}(\bullet)\equiv\Tr_{\texttt{E}}\left(U_{\texttt{SE}}^{\dagger}(\bullet\otimes\xi^{\prime}_{\texttt{E}})U_{\texttt{SE}}\right).

(3)

This is depicted in

1

such that its Petz recovery map

is given by

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\gamma}^{(\texttt{P})}=\hat{\mathcal{N}}^{(\texttt{T})}_{\xi^{\prime}_{\gamma}}.

(4)

In other words, if the Petz recovery map with prior
\gamma
can be implemented by appending an ancilla and running
the same evolution

U_{\texttt{SE}}
backwards.

Once the dynamics is given by
(U,\xi)
and a prior
\gamma

\xi^{\prime}

(U,\xi)

Not

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \text{Minimize}&\quad 0\\
\text{subject to}&\quad\xi^{\prime}\geq 0\\
&\,\,\Tr\xi^{\prime}=1\\
&\,\,\chi_{\xi^{\prime}}^{(\texttt{T})}=\chi_{\gamma}^{(\texttt{P})}\,,\end{aligned}

(5)

where
\chi_{\gamma}^{(\texttt{P})}
and
\chi_{\xi^{\prime}}^{(\texttt{T})}
are the Choi matrices

of
\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\gamma}^{(\texttt{P})}
and
\hat{\mathcal{N}}^{(\texttt{T})}_{\xi^{\prime}}
, respectively.

While the problem of TTR can be solved with SDP numerically for any given example, a much deeper understanding is gained by studying it analytically. The first work on tabletop reversibility
Aw et al. 2024
identified
product preservation
as a sufficient condition of tabletop reversibility. Product preservation holds for thermal operations and Gibbs states at the same temperature:

\displaystyle U_{\texttt{SE}}\left(\rho_{\texttt{th}}^{(\beta)}(H_{\texttt{S}})\otimes\rho_{\texttt{th}}^{(\beta)}(H_{\texttt{E}})\right)U^{\dagger}_{\texttt{SE}}=\rho_{\texttt{th}}^{(\beta)}(H_{\texttt{S}})\otimes\rho_{\texttt{th}}^{(\beta)}(H_{\texttt{E}})\,.

(6)

By generalizing this, a unitary
U_{\texttt{SE}}
is said to be product preserving with respect to
\gamma\otimes\xi
if it satisfies

\displaystyle U_{\texttt{SE}}(\gamma\otimes\xi)U^{\dagger}_{\texttt{SE}}=\gamma^{\prime}\otimes\xi^{\prime}

(7)

for some states
\gamma^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}
. Product preservation has been fully characterized for the case when both the system and the environment are one qubit
Aw et al. 2024
. In the same paper, some counterexamples showed that product preservation is not a necessary condition: there are tabletop time-reversible dynamics that do not arise from product preservation.

III
Exact TTR

The channel
\mathcal{N}
defined by
(U,\xi)
can be written as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{N}(\bullet)&=\Tr_{\texttt{E}}\left(U_{\texttt{SE}}(\bullet\otimes\xi_{\texttt{E}})U_{\texttt{SE}}^{\dagger}\right)\\

(9)

for any orthonormal basis
\{\ket*{f_j}\}_{j}
of system
E

to represent
\mathds{1}_{\texttt{S}}\otimes\ket{e_k}_{\texttt{E}}

\displaystyle N_{jk}=\sqrt{p_{k}}\bra*{e'_j}_{\texttt{E}}U_{\texttt{SE}}\ket*{e_k}_{\texttt{E}}.

(10)

Similar to the forward channel

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \hat{N}_{jk}^{(\texttt{P})}=&\sqrt{p_{k}}\sqrt{\gamma}\bra*{e_k}_{\texttt{E}}U^{\dagger}_{\texttt{SE}}\ket*{e'_j}_{\texttt{E}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma^{\prime}}},\mbox{\quad and\quad}\\
\hat{N}_{jk}^{(\texttt{T})}=&\sqrt{p^{\prime}_{j}}\bra*{e_k}_{\texttt{E}}U^{\dagger}_{\texttt{SE}}\ket*{e'_j}_{\texttt{E}}\end{aligned}

respectively. The product preservation condition (
7

\displaystyle U_{\texttt{SE}}(\gamma\otimes\xi)U^{\dagger}_{\texttt{SE}}=\gamma^{\prime}\otimes\xi^{\prime}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle(\gamma\otimes\xi)U_{\texttt{SE}}^{\dagger}=U_{\texttt{SE}}^{\dagger}(\gamma^{\prime}\otimes\xi^{\prime})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\sqrt{p_{k}}\sqrt{\gamma}\bra*{e_k}_{\texttt{E}}U^{\dagger}_{\texttt{SE}}\ket*{e'_j}_{\texttt{E}}=\sqrt{p^{\prime}_{j}}\bra*{e_k}_{\texttt{E}}U^{\dagger}_{\texttt{SE}}\ket*{e'_j}_{\texttt{E}}\sqrt{\gamma^{\prime}},\,\forall\,j,k

\displaystyle\hat{N}_{jk}^{(\texttt{P})}=\hat{N}_{jk}^{(\texttt{T})},\,\forall\,j,k.

4

m_{1},m_{2},n_{1},n_{2}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{jk}\left(p^{\prime}_{j}\sqrt{r^{\prime}_{m_{1}}r^{\prime}_{m_{2}}}-p_{k}\sqrt{r_{n_{1}}r_{n_{2}}}\right)\Phi_{m_{1}j\leftarrow n_{1}k}\Phi^{*}_{m_{2}j\leftarrow n_{2}k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{jk}\left(p^{\prime}_{j}\sqrt{r^{\prime}_{m_{1}}r^{\prime}_{m_{2}}}-p_{k}\sqrt{r_{n_{1}}r_{n_{2}}}\right)\delta_{m_{1},n_{1}}\delta_{j,k\oplus n_{1}}\delta_{m_{2},n_{2}}\delta_{j,k\oplus n_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}r_{n_{1}}(p^{\prime}_{k\oplus n_{1}}-p_{k})=r_{n_{1}}\Big(\sum_{k}p^{\prime}_{k\oplus n_{1}}-\sum_{k}p_{k}\Big)=0.

Example 3

(
XX
Hamiltonian)
.

Consider the dynamics defined by
H_{\texttt{tot}}=gX\otimes X
, or
gXX

\xi

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\theta}(\bullet)=\Tr_{\texttt{E}}\left(e^{-iXX\theta}(\bullet\otimes\xi)e^{iXX\theta}\right)

(18)

\commutator{\gamma}{X}=0

depends on

(thermal channel) or

The dynamics under study is

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &e^{-iXX\theta}(\gamma\otimes\xi)e^{iXX\theta}\\
=&(\cos{\theta}\mathds{1}-i\sin{\theta}XX)(\gamma\otimes\xi)(\cos{\theta}\mathds{1}+i\sin{\theta}XX)\\

&+i(\cos{\theta}\sin{\theta})X\gamma\otimes\commutator{X}{\xi},\end{aligned}

(19)

where the condition
\commutator{\gamma}{X}=0
was used in the last line. Three facts follow from this: First, any

that commutes with

is a steady state of
\mathcal{N}_{\theta}
for all

. Second,

preserves the product

whenever

for any integer

Let us look next at the case
\commutator{\xi}{Z}=0
, which defines dephasing channels

. We first compute the transition matrix

for

:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\Phi_{mj\leftarrow nk}\\
\equiv&\bra{\lambda_m}_{\texttt{S}}\bra*{e'_j}_{\texttt{E}}U_{\theta}\ket*{\lambda_n}_{\texttt{S}}\ket*{e_k}_{\texttt{E}}\\
=&\bra{\lambda_m}_{\texttt{S}}\bra*{j}_{\texttt{E}}(\cos{\theta}\mathds{1}-i\sin{\theta}XX)\ket*{\lambda_n}_{\texttt{S}}\ket*{k}_{\texttt{E}}\\

Having this, the TTR condition is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{jk}\left(p^{\prime}_{j}\sqrt{r^{\prime}_{m_{1}}r^{\prime}_{m_{2}}}-p_{k}\sqrt{r_{n_{1}}r_{n_{2}}}\right)\Phi_{m_{1}j\leftarrow n_{1}k}\Phi^{*}_{m_{2}j\leftarrow n_{2}k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{jk}r_{n_{1}}\left(p_{j}-p_{k}\right)\Big((\cos^{2}\theta)\delta_{j,k}+(\sin^{2}\theta)\delta_{j,k\oplus 1}\Big)

\displaystyle=r_{n_{1}}\cos^{2}\theta\Big(\sum_{k}p_{k}-\sum_{k}p_{k}\Big)+r_{n_{1}}\sin^{2}\theta\Big(\sum_{k}p_{k\oplus 1}-\sum_{k}p_{k}\Big)

\displaystyle=0.

(21)

Finally, we show that
\mathcal{N}_{\theta}(\bullet)

whatever the choice of
\xi

depends on

as
\xi^{\prime}=u_{\theta}\xi u_{\theta}^{\dagger}
with

. Notice that this implies
\Tr\left[\xi^{\prime}X\right]=\Tr\left[\xi X\right]
.

The general proof is given in
Appendix

A
; here we show the case
\theta=\frac{\pi}{4}
. First, let us compute the transition matrix
\Phi_{mj\leftarrow nk}
:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\Phi_{mj\leftarrow nk}\\
\equiv&\bra{\lambda_m}_{\texttt{S}}\bra*{e'_j}_{\texttt{E}}U_{\theta}\ket*{\lambda_n}_{\texttt{S}}\ket*{e_k}_{\texttt{E}}\\
=&\bra{\lambda_m}_{\texttt{S}}\bra*{e'_j}_{\texttt{E}}(\cos{\theta}\mathds{1}-i\sin{\theta}XX)\ket*{\lambda_n}_{\texttt{S}}\ket*{e_k}_{\texttt{E}}\\
=&\bra{\lambda_m}\ket*{\lambda_n}\bra*{e'_j}\Big(\underbrace{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\ket*{e_k}+i\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{\sqrt{2}}X\ket*{e_{k}}}_{=:\ket*{f_{n,k}}}\Big).\end{aligned}

(22)

Let us then choose
\xi^{\prime}
such that
\Tr\left[\xi^{\prime}X\right]=\Tr\left[\xi X\right]
and its eigenbasis is given by
\ket*{e'_{j}}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\ket*{e_j}-iX\ket*{e_{j}}\right)

\bra*{e_j'}\ket*{f_{n,k}}
as follows,

\displaystyle\bra*{e_j'}\ket*{f_{n,k}}=\begin{cases}\delta_{j,k},\quad&\text{if}\,\,n=0\\
i\bra*{e_j}X\ket*{e_k},\quad&\text{if}\,\,n=1.\end{cases}

(23)

Using

\displaystyle\Phi_{mj\leftarrow nk}=\begin{cases}\delta_{m,n}\delta_{j,k},\quad&\text{if}\,\,n=0\\
i\delta_{m,n}\bra*{e_j}X\ket*{e_k},\quad&\text{if}\,\,n=1.\end{cases}

(a) when

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sum_{jk}\left(p^{\prime}_{j}\sqrt{r^{\prime}_{m_{1}}r^{\prime}_{m_{2}}}-p_{k}\sqrt{r_{n_{1}}r_{n_{2}}}\right)\Phi_{m_{1}j\leftarrow n_{1}k}\Phi^{*}_{m_{2}j\leftarrow n_{2}k}\\

(b) when

(c) when

(27)

(d) when

IV
Approximate TTR

Figure 2:
Approximate Tabletop Time-reversibility.

So far, TTR has been defined for a given channel. Example
3
showed that there are cases in which TTR holds for a whole family of channels indexed by the interaction time, but usually at the price of having to choose
\xi^{\prime}
differently for each time. We now switch the focus and search for situations in which TTR holds for
\xi
independent of the interaction time, at least approximately.

We first define the first- and second-order approximations. Then use this knowledge to discuss TTR for a random-time collision model, and finish with the application to the amplitude-damping channel for a qubit.

Consider a dynamics
\mathcal{N}_{\Delta t}
for a short time
\Delta t
. A direct approach to get approximate conditions of TTR is to expand
\hat{{\cal N}}^{(\texttt{P})}_{\Delta t,\gamma}
and
\hat{{\cal N}}^{(\texttt{T})}_{\Delta t,\xi^{\prime}}
in powers of
\Delta t
up to some desired order and match terms order by order (sketched in
Fig.

2
):

Definition 3
.

Given
(H_{\texttt{tot}},\xi)
that define the channel
{\cal N}_{\Delta t}
for any evolution time
\Delta t
, we consider the expansions of
\hat{{\cal N}}^{(\texttt{P})}_{\Delta t,\gamma}
and
\hat{{\cal N}}^{(\texttt{T})}_{\Delta t,\xi^{\prime}}
as a power series in
\Delta t
. We say that
(H_{\texttt{tot}},\xi)
is
k^{\texttt{th}}
order approximate tabletop time-reversible for a prior
\gamma
with regard to
\xi^{\prime}
if the
n^{\texttt{th}}
order terms of the two series are the same for all
n\leq k
.

As desired, the condition of approximate TTR does not depend on
\Delta t
; neither will
\xi^{\prime}
. When
k=0
, the condition is trivial. The limit
k\to\infty
corresponds to the case of exact TTR with regard to
the same

\xi^{\prime}
for any
\Delta t
. This is a strong condition, but we know that thermal operations satisfy it; we leave the question open as to whether there exist non-thermal operations that are
\infty^{\texttt{th}}
-order approximate TTR.

In what follows, we will present first two non-trivial approximation of TTR conditions. For convenience
X_{\texttt{S}}
and
X_{\texttt{E}}
will be used as shorthand notations for
X_{\texttt{S}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\texttt{E}}
and
\mathds{1}_{\texttt{S}}\otimes X_{\texttt{E}}
, respectively. Calligraphic fonts are used only for channels and superoperators.
We will also adopt the natural unit
\hbar:=1
, and often suppress the subscript
t
from states when it is clear that we are concerned with a single dynamics.

IV.1
First-order approximation

Let us begin with the first-order approximation of the TTR condition:

Theorem 2

(First-order approximation)
.

(H_{\texttt{tot}},\xi)
is first-order approximate tabletop time-reversible for a prior
\gamma
with regard to
\xi^{\prime}
if it holds that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\Tr_{\texttt{E}}\left(\left(\gamma_{\texttt{S}}^{\frac{1}{2}}H_{\texttt{tot}}\gamma_{\texttt{S}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\xi_{\texttt{E}}\right)-iA\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\Tr_{\texttt{E}}\left(H_{\texttt{tot}}\xi^{\prime}_{\texttt{E}}\right)\\
&\Tr_{\texttt{E}}\left(\left(\gamma_{\texttt{S}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}H_{\texttt{tot}}\gamma_{\texttt{S}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\xi_{\texttt{E}}\right)+i\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}A=\Tr_{\texttt{E}}\left(H_{\texttt{tot}}\xi^{\prime}_{\texttt{E}}\right),\end{aligned}

(28)

where we define
A\equiv-i\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x\sqrt{\gamma}}\commutator{H}{\gamma}e^{-x\sqrt{\gamma}}dx
and
H\equiv\Tr_{\texttt{E}}\left(H_{\texttt{tot}}\xi_{\texttt{E}}\right)
. When
\gamma
is a steady state or the maximally mixed state,
A
vanishes.

Proof.

Here, we will give a rough sketch of proof when
\gamma
is a steady state (see
Appendix

B
for the complete proof).

We start with the Kraus representation of
\mathcal{N}_{\Delta t}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\Delta t}(\rho)=\sum_{j,k}p_{k}\bra*{e_j}_{\texttt{E}}e^{-iH_{\texttt{tot}}\Delta t}\ket*{e_k}_{\texttt{E}}\rho\bra*{e_k}_{\texttt{E}}e^{iH_{\texttt{tot}}\Delta t}\ket*{e_j}_{\texttt{E}}.

Accordingly, the Petz recovery map for prior being a steady state can be expressed as

\displaystyle\hat{{\cal N}}^{(\texttt{P})}_{\Delta t,\gamma}(\rho)

\displaystyle\!\!=

\displaystyle\sum_{j,k}p_{k}\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\bra*{e_k}_{\texttt{E}}e^{iH_{\texttt{tot}}\Delta t}\ket*{e_j}_{\texttt{E}}\!\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\!\bra*{e_j}_{\texttt{E}}e^{-iH_{\texttt{tot}}\Delta t}\ket*{e_k}_{\texttt{E}}\!\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\!\!=

\displaystyle\sum_{j,k}p_{k}\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\bra*{e_k}_{\texttt{E}}(\mathds{1}+i\Delta tH_{\texttt{tot}})\ket*{e_j}_{\texttt{E}}\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathds{1}-i\Delta tH_{\texttt{tot}})\ket*{e_k}_{\texttt{E}}\!\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho+i\Delta t\Tr_{\texttt{E}}\left((\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}\otimes\mathds{1})H_{\texttt{tot}}(\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\otimes\xi)\right)\rho

\displaystyle-i\Delta t\rho\Tr_{\texttt{E}}\left((\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\otimes\mathds{1})H_{\texttt{tot}}(\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}\otimes\xi)\right)+\order{(\Delta t)^2}.

(29)

Similarly, we get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{\Delta t,\xi^{\prime}}^{(\texttt{T})}(\rho)\\
=&\sum_{j,k}p^{\prime}_{j}\bra*{e_k'}_{\texttt{E}}e^{iH_{\texttt{tot}}\Delta t}\ket*{e_j'}_{\texttt{E}}\rho\bra*{e_j'}_{\texttt{E}}e^{-iH_{\texttt{tot}}\Delta t}\ket*{e_k'}_{\texttt{E}}\\
=&\rho+i\Delta{t}\sum_{j}p^{\prime}_{j}\bra*{e_j'}_{\texttt{E}}H_{\texttt{tot}}\ket*{e_j'}_{\texttt{E}}\rho\\
&-i\Delta{t}\rho\sum_{j}p^{\prime}_{j}\bra*{e_j'}_{\texttt{E}}H_{\texttt{tot}}\ket*{e_j'}_{\texttt{E}}+\order{(\Delta t)^2}\\
=&\rho+i(\Delta t)\Tr_{\texttt{E}}\left(H_{\texttt{tot}}(\mathds{1}\otimes\xi^{\prime})\right)\rho\\
&-i(\Delta t)\rho\Tr_{\texttt{E}}\left(H_{\texttt{tot}}(\mathds{1}\otimes\xi^{\prime})\right)+\order{(\Delta t)^2}.\end{aligned}

∎

From
2
, we can make some useful observations. For the case where
\gamma
is the maximally mixed state, conditions (
28
) reduce to

\displaystyle\Tr_{\texttt{E}}\left(H_{\texttt{tot}}\xi_{\texttt{E}}\right)=\Tr_{\texttt{E}}\left(H_{\texttt{tot}}\xi^{\prime}_{\texttt{E}}\right).

(30)

In particular, any dynamics is first-order approximate TTR with regard to
\xi^{\prime}=\xi
when
\gamma=\mathds{1}/d
. As such, one can approximately implement a Petz recovery map for
\gamma=\mathds{1}/d
by running
U_{\Delta t}=e^{-iH_{\texttt{tot}}\Delta t}
backwards, i.e.,
U_{(-\Delta t)}=U_{\Delta t}^{\dagger}
. This is similar to how thermal operations are TTR by running the same operation backwards. The same conclusion can be derived when
\gamma
is a steady state and satisfies
[\gamma_{\texttt{S}},H_{\texttt{tot}}]=0
.

As an example, recall
3
where we considered
H_{\texttt{tot}}=gXX
and a steady state
\gamma
. In this case,
\gamma
satisfies
[\gamma_{\texttt{S}},H_{\texttt{tot}}]=0
. The dynamics under consideration is then TTR for any evolution time, but
\xi^{\prime}
depends on an evolution time unless
\xi
is such that the dynamics becomes the trivial case of thermal operations or dephasing channels. Nonetheless,
2
says that the dynamics can be TTR by using the same ancilla
\xi^{\prime}=\xi
regardless of an evolution time
\Delta t
, while sacrificing accuracy in implementing the Petz recovery map up to an error
\order{(\Delta t)^2}
. Besides, since the effect of a unitary for the interaction is periodic with respect to an evolution time, there exists
\Delta\hat{t}>0
such that
U_{\Delta\hat{t}}=U_{(-\Delta t)}
.

Moreover,
2
holds an useful implication in quantum error correction. A Petz recovery map for prior being the maximally mixed state on a code space (also known as ‘transpose’ channel or code-specific Petz recovery map) is often of interest, and our results imply that such Petz recovery maps are approximately implementable by running the same environment backwards with an error of the second order of the evolution time.

IV.2
Second-order approximation

Similarly, we can find the conditions for the second-order approximate TTR by the second-order matching of the two maps. The final form of the second-order terms of a Petz recovery map is complicated in general: it can be found in
Appendix

C
, together with its derivation. Here we present the conditions for the second-order approximate TTR in the special case of a steady state
\gamma
satisfying
[\gamma_{\texttt{S}},H_{\texttt{tot}}]=0
:

Theorem 3

(Second-order approximation)
.

If a prior state
\gamma
is a steady state and
[\gamma_{\texttt{S}},H_{\texttt{tot}}]=0
, then
(H_{\texttt{tot}},\xi)
is second-order approximate tabletop time-reversible with regard to
\xi^{\prime}
if it holds that

\displaystyle\Tr_{\texttt{E}}\left(H_{\texttt{tot}}\xi_{\texttt{E}}\right)=\Tr_{\texttt{E}}\left(H_{\texttt{tot}}\xi^{\prime}_{\texttt{E}}\right)

(31)

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\Tr_{\texttt{E}}\left(H_{\texttt{tot}}\rho_{\texttt{S}}H_{\texttt{tot}}\xi_{\texttt{E}}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(\Tr_{\texttt{E}}(H_{\texttt{tot}}^{2}\xi_{\texttt{E}})\rho_{\texttt{S}}+\rho_{\texttt{S}}\Tr_{\texttt{E}}(H_{\texttt{tot}}^{2}\xi_{\texttt{E}})\right)\\
&=\Tr_{\texttt{E}}(H_{\texttt{tot}}(\rho_{\texttt{S}}\otimes\xi^{\prime}_{\texttt{E}})H_{\texttt{tot}})\\
&\quad-\frac{1}{2}\left(\Tr_{\texttt{E}}(H_{\texttt{tot}}^{2}\xi^{\prime}_{\texttt{E}})\rho_{\texttt{S}}+\rho_{\texttt{S}}\Tr_{\texttt{E}}(H_{\texttt{tot}}^{2}\xi^{\prime}_{\texttt{E}})\right),\end{aligned}

(32)

for any
\rho
.

So far, we have considered dynamics for a small evolution time
\Delta t
. Now imagine a dynamics
\mathcal{N}_{T}
for a time
T
which is not sufficiently small. One can attempt to recover the noisy dynamics
\mathcal{N}_{T}
by executing Petz recovery maps that recover dynamics
\mathcal{N}_{\Delta t}
for a smaller time
\Delta t\equiv\frac{T}{N}
.
If the Lindbladian
Lindblad 1976
;
Gorini et al. 1976
;
Manzano 2020
;
Stefanini et al. 2025
of the dynamics is known, the entire dynamics can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{T}=\circ_{n=1}^{N}\mathcal{N}_{\Delta t}=e^{{\Delta t}\mathcal{L}}\circ\cdots\circ e^{{\Delta t}\mathcal{L}},

(33)

where
\mathcal{L}
is the Lindbladian of the dynamics of interest. Then, one can apply conditions of approximate TTR to each dynamics
\mathcal{N}_{\Delta t}
up to some desired order. For instance, under the conditions of the second order approximate TTR, Petz recovery maps can be realized by implementing tabletop reverse maps sequentially, thus
sequential TTR
or
composable TTR

Aw et al. 2024
with an error
\order{\frac{T^3}{N^2}}
, namely, for any
\rho

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\norm{\left(\circ_{n=1}^{N} \hat{\N}^{(\texttt{P})}_{\Delta t,\gamma_{n-1}}
-
\circ_{n=1}^{N} \hat{\N}^{(\texttt{T})}_{\Delta t,\xi'_{n-1}}\right)(\rho)
}_{1}\leq\order{N(\Delta t)^3}=\order{\frac{T^3}{N^2}},

where
\gamma_{n}
and
\xi^{\prime}_{n}
represent a prior state of a Petz recovery map and an ancilla state of the corresponding tabletop reverse map at each time step
t_{n}=n\Delta t
.

IV.3
Lindbladian from a random-time collision model

Figure 3:
Random-time Collision Model.

The typical derivation of the Lindbladian, however, uses a continuous-time formalism from the outset, although it is implicitly acknowledged that the Born-Markov approximation cannot hold for arbitrarily short times (there must be some time of real interaction, during which the evolution is non-Markovian). Besides, the final formulas are hard to cast as an explicit dependence from the system-bath interaction. In what follows, we consider a collision model
Scarani et al. 2002
;
Alicki and Lendi 2007
;
Ciccarello et al. 2022
;
Lacroix et al. 2025
, where we do not rely on the exact Lindbladian of a given dynamics in order to derive conditions of such a sequential TTR.

Consider a random-time collision model, i.e. a sequential interaction of the system with individual components of the bath (c.f., Fig.
3
), where the collision time may vary. Let
gH_{\texttt{tot}}
be the total Hamiltonian, where we singled out the relevant energy scale
g
for the problem under study. Without loss of generality, we set
H_{\texttt{tot}}=H_{\texttt{S}}+H_{\texttt{E}}+H_{\texttt{I}}
. A single collision with
\xi
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \rho_{n}\to\rho_{n+1}&=\Tr_{\texttt{E}}\left(e^{-igH_{\texttt{tot}}{\Delta t}}(\rho_{n}\otimes\xi_{\texttt{E}})e^{igH_{\texttt{tot}}{\Delta t}}\right),\\
&\approx\rho_{n}+g\Delta t\mathcal{H}\rho_{n}+(g\Delta t)^{2}\sum_{j,k}p_{k}\mathcal{D}[L_{jk}]\rho_{n},\end{aligned}

(34)

having defined the superoperators

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\rho

\displaystyle\equiv-i\commutator{H_{\texttt{S}}+H_{\texttt{Ls}}(\xi)}{\rho},

(35)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}[L_{jk}]\rho

\displaystyle\equiv L_{jk}\rho L_{jk}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\anticommutator{L_{jk}^\dagger L_{jk}}{\rho},

(36)

with

\displaystyle H_{\texttt{Ls}}(\xi)

\displaystyle\equiv\Tr_{\texttt{E}}\left((\mathds{1}_{\texttt{S}}\otimes\xi_{\texttt{E}})H_{\texttt{I}}\right),

(37)

\displaystyle L_{jk}

\displaystyle\equiv\bra*{f_j}_{\texttt{E}}{H_{\texttt{tot}}}\ket{e_k}_{\texttt{E}},

(38)

for any orthonormal basis
\{\ket*{f_j}\}_{j}
. Similar to the exact TTR, we will choose
\ket*{f_j}:=\ket*{e'_j}
the eigenvectors of
\xi^{\prime}
. As such, Eq. (
34
) motivates the Lindbladian

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=g\mathcal{H}+\Gamma\sum_{j,k}p_{k}\mathcal{D}[L_{jk}],

(39)

with the rate
\Gamma
of the event of a collision. When collision times are finite and
\Gamma
is sufficiently small such that
\Gamma\sim g^{2}\expectationvalue{\Delta t}
, this Lindbladian captures a coarse-grained behavior of the scattering process
(H_{\texttt{tot}},\xi)
, with the superoperators
\mathcal{H}
and
\Gamma p_{k}\mathcal{D}[L_{jk}]
. The superoperator
\mathcal{H}
accounts for a closed dynamics governed by the system Hamiltonian moderated by a so-called Lamb shift Hamiltonian
H_{\texttt{Ls}}(\xi)
, which describes a shift of the system’s energy levels due to its interaction with the ancilla. The superoperator
\Gamma p_{k}\mathcal{D}[L_{jk}]
, called dissipator, accounts for a coarse-grained dissipation in the direction of jump operators
L_{jk}
with its weight
\Gamma p_{k}
.

Under the same collision model, the tabletop reverse Lindbladian of the reverse collisional dynamics with ancilla
\xi^{\prime}
is given by

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{L}}^{(\texttt{T})}_{\xi^{\prime}}=g\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(\texttt{T})}_{\xi^{\prime}}+\Gamma\sum_{j,k}p^{\prime}_{j}\mathcal{D}[\hat{L}_{jk}^{(\texttt{T})}]

(40)

with a Hamiltonian and jump operators

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\xi^{\prime}}^{(\texttt{T})}\rho=

\displaystyle-i\commutator{ -H_{\texttt{S}}-H_{\texttt{Ls}}(\xi')}{\rho}

(41)

\displaystyle\hat{L}^{(\texttt{T})}_{jk}=

\displaystyle\,L_{jk}^{\dagger},

(42)

describes tabletop reverse collisions given by
(-H_{\texttt{tot}},\xi^{\prime})
.

Following Ref.
Kwon et al. 2022
, for a prior state
\gamma_{t}

whose spectral decomposition
\gamma_{t}\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\sum_{\lambda_{t}}\lambda_{t}\outerproduct{\lambda_t}{\lambda_t}
at time
t
, we compute a Lindbladian

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{L}}^{(\texttt{P})}_{\gamma_{t}}\equiv g\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(\texttt{P})}_{\gamma_{t}}+\Gamma\sum_{j,k}p_{k}\mathcal{D}[\hat{L}^{(\texttt{P})}_{jk}]

(43)

with a Hamiltonian and jump operators

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\gamma_{t}}^{(\texttt{P})}\rho

\displaystyle\equiv-i\commutator{-H_{\texttt{S}} -H_{\texttt{Ls}}(\xi) + H_{\texttt{C}}}{\rho}

(44)

\displaystyle\hat{L}^{(\texttt{P})}_{jk}

\displaystyle=\hat{L}^{(\texttt{P})}_{jk}(\gamma_{t})\equiv\gamma_{t}^{1/2}L_{jk}^{\dagger}\gamma_{t}^{-1/2},

(45)

defining the correction Hamiltonian
H_{\texttt{C}}=H_{\texttt{C},\gamma_{t}}
as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \!\!\!\!\!H_{\texttt{C},\gamma_{t}}\equiv\frac{1}{2i}\sum_{\lambda_{t},\lambda_{t}^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\lambda_{t}}-\sqrt{\lambda_{t}^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{\lambda_{t}}+\sqrt{\lambda_{t}^{\prime}}}\right)\bra{\lambda_t}M(\gamma_{t})\ket*{\lambda_t'}\outerproduct{\lambda_t}{\lambda_t'},\end{aligned}

(46)

with
M(\gamma_{t})\equiv\Gamma\sum_{jk}p_{k}(L_{jk}^{\dagger}L_{jk}+\gamma_{t}^{-1/2}L_{jk}\gamma_{t}L_{jk}^{\dagger}\gamma_{t}^{-1/2})
. The Lindbladian
\hat{{\cal L}}^{(\texttt{P})}_{\gamma_{t}}
is not the exact Lindbladian of the Petz recovery map of the forward collisional dynamics unless
\gamma_{t}
is a steady state, but its Lindblad dynamics is sufficiently close to the Petz recovery map. The following lemma shows this (see
Section

D.1
for the proof):

Lemma 4
.

Consider a collisional dynamics
\mathcal{N}_{\Delta t}:=e^{\Delta t\mathcal{L}}
for an evolution time
\Delta t
. Let
\hat{{\cal N}}^{(\texttt{P})}_{\Delta t,\gamma_{t}}
be the Petz recovery map of the dynamics
\mathcal{N}_{\Delta t}
for a prior
\gamma_{t}
at a time
t
. The Lindblad dynamics
e^{\Delta t\hat{{\cal L}}^{(\texttt{P})}_{\gamma_{t}}}
is close to the Petz recovery map
\hat{{\cal N}}^{(\texttt{P})}_{\Delta t,\gamma_{t}}
up to an error
{\cal O}\left((\Delta t)^{2}\right)
, i.e.,

\frac{1}{2}\left\|\hat{{\cal N}}^{(\texttt{P})}_{\Delta t,\gamma_{t}}-e^{\Delta t\hat{{\cal L}}^{(\texttt{P})}_{\gamma_{t}}}\right\|_{\diamond}={\cal O}\left((\Delta t)^{2}\right),

(47)

where
\norm{\bullet}_{\diamond}
is the diamond norm
Aharonov et al. 1998
.

This immediately leads to the Lindbladian conditions of approximate TTR (see
Section

D.2
for the proof):

Theorem 5
.

Consider a collisional dynamics
\mathcal{N}_{\Delta t}:=e^{\Delta t\mathcal{L}}
and a collisional tabletop reverse dynamics
e^{\Delta t\hat{\mathcal{L}}^{(\texttt{T})}_{\xi^{\prime}}}
. A Petz recovery map
\hat{{\cal N}}^{(\texttt{P})}_{\Delta t,\gamma_{t}}
of the dynamics
\mathcal{N}_{\Delta t}
can be approximately implemented by
e^{\Delta t\hat{\mathcal{L}}^{(\texttt{T})}_{\xi^{\prime}}}
up to an error
\order{(\Delta t)^2}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\norm{
\hat{{\cal N}}^{(\texttt{P})}_{\Delta t,\gamma_t} - e^{\Delta t \hat{\mathcal{L}}^{(\texttt{T})}_{\xi'}}
}_{\diamond}=\order{(\Delta t)^2},

(48)

if
\hat{\mathcal{L}}^{(\texttt{P})}_{\gamma_{t}}=\hat{\mathcal{L}}^{(\texttt{T})}_{\xi^{\prime}}+\order{\Delta t}
or equivalently,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &H_{\texttt{Ls}}(\xi)-H_{\texttt{C}}=H_{\texttt{Ls}}(\xi^{\prime})+\alpha\mathds{1}+\order{\Delta t}\\
&\sum_{jk}p_{k}\mathcal{D}[\gamma_{t}^{1/2}\bra*{e_k}_{\texttt{E}}H_{\texttt{tot}}\ket*{e'_j}_{\texttt{E}}\gamma_{t}^{-1/2}]\\
&\qquad=\sum_{jk}p^{\prime}_{j}\mathcal{D}[\bra*{e_k}_{\texttt{E}}H_{\texttt{tot}}\ket*{e'_j}_{\texttt{E}}]+\order{\Delta t},\end{aligned}

(49)

for some number
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.
Moreover, the complex term of the correction Hamiltonian
H_{\texttt{C}}=H_{\texttt{C},\gamma_{t}}
vanishes if

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \gamma_{t}^{1/2}\bra*{e_k}_{\texttt{E}}{H_{\texttt{tot}}}\ket*{e'_j}_{\texttt{E}}\gamma_{t}^{-1/2}=c_{jk}\bra*{e_k}_{\texttt{E}}{H_{\texttt{tot}}}\ket*{e'_j}_{\texttt{E}},\end{aligned}

(50)

for some number
c_{jk}
.

Under this collision model, one can proceed to an approximate implementation of Petz recovery maps through a sequential TTR when the evolution time
T
of the noisy dynamics is not sufficiently small:

Corollary 6
.

The concatenation of Petz recovery maps for each collision and that of tabletop reverse maps satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\norm{\circ_{n=1}^{N} \hat{\N}^{(\texttt{P})}_{\Delta t,\gamma_{n-1}} - \circ_{n=1}^{N} e^{\Delta t \hat{\mathcal{L}}^{(\texttt{T})}_{\xi'_{n-1}}}}_{\diamond}=\order{\frac{T^2}{N}}

(51)

if it holds that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &H_{\texttt{Ls}}(\xi)=H_{\texttt{Ls}}(\xi^{\prime}_{n})+\alpha\mathds{1}+\order{\Delta t}\\
&\sqrt{p_{k}}\gamma_{n}^{1/2}\bra*{e_k}_{\texttt{E}}{H_{\texttt{tot}}}\ket*{e'_j(n)}_{\texttt{E}}\gamma_{n}^{-1/2}\\
&\quad\quad=\sqrt{p^{\prime}_{j}(t_{n})}\bra*{e_k}_{\texttt{E}}{H_{\texttt{tot}}}\ket*{e'_j(n)}_{\texttt{E}}+\order{\Delta t},\end{aligned}

(52)

for some number
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\xi^{\prime}_{n}\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\sum_{j}p^{\prime}_{j}(n)\outerproduct*{e'_j(n)}{e'_j(n)}
.

Proof.

Following
5
, if Eq. (
52
) holds for every
n
, the difference between a Petz recovery map and tabletop reverse map for each time intervals scales as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{\mathcal{N}}^{(\texttt{P})}_{\Delta t,\gamma_{n-1}}-e^{\Delta t\hat{\mathcal{L}}^{(\texttt{T})}_{\xi^{\prime}_{n-1}}}\right)(\rho)=\order{(\Delta t)^2}

(53)

for any
\rho
. Then it immediately follows that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{2}\left(\circ_{n=1}^{N}\hat{\mathcal{N}}^{(\texttt{P})}_{\Delta t,\gamma_{n-1}}-\circ_{n=1}^{N}e^{\Delta t\hat{\mathcal{L}}^{(\texttt{T})}_{\xi^{\prime}_{n-1}}}\right)(\rho)\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\left(\circ_{n=1}^{N}\hat{\mathcal{N}}^{(\texttt{P})}_{\Delta t,\gamma_{n-1}}-\circ_{n=1}^{N}\left(e^{\Delta t\hat{\mathcal{L}}^{(\texttt{T})}_{\xi^{\prime}_{n-1}}}-2{\cal O}((\Delta t)^{2})\right)\right)(\rho)\\
=&\underbrace{{\cal O}((\Delta t)^{2})+{\cal O}((\Delta t)^{2})+\cdots+{\cal O}((\Delta t)^{2})}_{N}.\end{aligned}

(54)

Since
\Delta t=\frac{T}{N}
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\norm{\circ_{n=1}^{N} \hat{\N}^{(\texttt{P})}_{\Delta t,\gamma_{n-1}} - \circ_{n=1}^{N} e^{\Delta t \hat{\mathcal{L}}^{(\texttt{T})}_{\xi'_{n-1}}}}_{\diamond}=\order{\frac{T^2}{N}}.

(55)

∎

It is worth noting that
\gamma_{n}
’s have been merely referred to the prior states at time
t_{n}
. Yet, when it comes to sequential reversal, it is important that the prior states must be chosen such that they follow the trajectory determined by the forward dynamics. To facilitate TTR along the trajectory, one thus needs to
take into account propagated prior states.

V
Discussion

The Petz recovery map has proven to be a powerful mathematical tool in many applications of quantum information. However, its physical realization has been considered challenging due to its complex form.
In this work, we study the exact and approximate conditions for tabletop time-reversibility (TTR), under which the Petz recovery map can be implemented using resources similar to, or even identical to, those accessible from the forward dynamics. Thus, TTR-based approaches enable the implementation of a Petz recovery map to be less resource-intensive.

Lastly, we highlight several open questions and possible future research directions, both general and technical.

1.

Our definition of TTR, introduced in Ref.
Aw et al. 2024
, assumes that what is difficult is to create a new dynamics, and thus requests that the Petz map be implemented with a dilation involving
U^{\dagger}
. The preparation of the ancilla
\xi^{\prime}
is considered as a free operation. In the lab, whether an operation is considered free or resourceful depends strongly on the experimental platform that is being used. Thus one may want to consider alternative definitions of TTR, perhaps platform-specific ones.

2.

In all of the examples we considered, a dynamics appears to be tabletop time-reversible whenever a prior state is a steady state. We do not have a proof that this is true in general, and leave it for a future investigation.

3.

The whole inspiration for the TTR condition came from the observation that thermal operations satisfy it
Alhambra et al. 2018
. In the language introduced here, they are
\infty^{\texttt{th}}
-order approximate TTR: the Petz recovery map is just the backward process with the same ancilla as the forward process, and regardless of the evolution time. We conjecture that thermal operations are the only operations with this property.

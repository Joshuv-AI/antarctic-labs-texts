High blood glucose and shoulder pain in middle-aged and older Chinese adults: a secondary analysis of the China health and retirement longitudinal study (CHARLS)
Authors: Yi Duan, Yan Zhou, Chong Wei, Fusheng Xiong, Zhi Liu, Haizhu Xu, Qiang Li, Qun Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractUsing nationally representative data from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS), in this study, we examined cross-sectional and retrospective cohort study associations between blood glucose status and shoulder pain in middle-aged and older Chinese adults. A total of 13,523 participants aged ≥ 45 years were included in the cross-sectional analysis and classified into high or normal blood glucose groups. A total of 8,440 participants without shoulder pain were recruited from the CHARLS at baseline in 2011 were followed up in 2015. The prevalence of shoulder pain was 13.0% in the overall population, with an increased prevalence of 18.0% among individuals with high blood glucose. High blood glucose was significantly associated with shoulder pain, with an odds ratio = 1.41 (95% CI 1.15–1.73). Over 4 years, 996 new cases of shoulder pain were identified, and high blood glucose increased risk with an odds ratio = 1.58 (95% CI 1.20–2.07). High blood glucose is a risk factor for shoulder pain, supporting the importance of blood sugar control to reduce shoulder pain among patients with high blood glucose in middle-aged and older Chinese adults.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-47880-1.Keywords: High blood glucose, Shoulder pain, Middle-aged and older Chinese adults, China Health and Retirement Longitudinal StudySubject terms: Diseases, Endocrinology, Health care, Medical research, Risk factors

Introduction

Shoulder pain is a common health concern, particularly among middle-aged and older adults1. It shows a high prevalence and is associated with chronic conditions, such as diabetes and osteoporosis2–6. In addition to impairing patients’ quality of life, shoulder pain imposes a considerable economic burden on healthcare systems3,7. Although physical therapy, pharmacological interventions, and surgical procedures are available, their ability to relieve pain and restore function remains limited by variable outcomes and high recurrence rates8–13. Therefore, from the perspective of disease prevention, in order to reduce the incidence of shoulder pain, research on the risk factors of shoulder pain is necessary.

Hyperglycemia is recognized as a major factor in the development of chronic diseases and it also contribute to the occurrence of shoulder pain14–16. Evidence supports a link between hyperglycemia and shoulder pain in many studies17–20. However, it remains to be clarified whether this link holds in China’s large middle-aged and elderly population. In this study, we applied cross-sectional and retrospective cohort analyses to assess the relationship between hyperglycemia and shoulder pain using large datasets to strengthen reliability. Potential covariates, including age, sex, body mass index, and comorbidities, are controlled through logistic regression model to ensure a comprehensive evaluation. This research aims to guide clinical strategies for managing patients with diabetes in middle-aged and older Chinese adults, improving outcomes and reducing healthcare costs.

This study’s findings clarify the link between hyperglycemia and shoulder pain in middle-aged and older Chinese adults and support early recognition and intervention for shoulder symptoms in individuals with hyperglycemia. Additionally, these results could inform the development of targeted preventive and therapeutic approaches for shoulder complications, providing useful direction for clinical practice and future research.

Methods

Study population and ethical review

The China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS) was approved by the Institutional Review Committee of Peking University (Approval Number: IRB00001052-11015). All respondents signed written informed consent before participation. This study used the anonymous public CHARLS dataset; therefore, additional ethical approval was not required. Data from the 2011 and 2015 CHARLS cycles were used to analyze diagnostic status related to blood glucose and shoulder pain. Participants were categorized into two groups based on self-report in the Health Status Questionnaire: the high blood glucose group, including those with diabetes or impaired glucose tolerance, and the normal blood glucose group with normal glucose levels. In the shoulder pain questionnaire, respondents “answering yes” were identified as having shoulder pain.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria were: (1) participants aged ≥ 45 years in CHARLS 2011, and (2) complete data on blood glucose and shoulder pain. The exclusion criteria were: (1) missing physical examination and questionnaire data (n = 3740); (2) age < 45 (n = 269) or no age information (n = 21); (3) no information on gender (n = 9); and (4) missing data on diabetes and impaired glucose tolerance (n = 143). This study consisted of two components: (1) a cross-sectional analysis including 13,523 participants in CHARLS 2011, and (2) a retrospective cohort study that further excluded 1,749 individuals with shoulder pain in 2011 and 3,334 individuals lacking shoulder pain data in 2015. Finally, 8,440 participants in CHARLS 2011 were fully followed up in 2015. Figure 1 shows the flowchart of participant selection.

Flow diagram for participants included in the study.

Covariate selection

Covariates were selected based on known factors related to shoulder pain and the characteristics of CHARLS. The covariates included: (1) demographic factors: gender, age, educational background; (2) lifestyle factors: Body Mass Index, smoking, and alcohol consumption; and (3) disease factors: hypertension and cardiovascular diseases.

Statistical software and analysis

All analyses were performed using R version 4.3.3 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Continuous variables were compared using t-tests, and categorical variables using chi-square tests to evaluate differences between those with and without high blood glucose. For subgroup analyses, numerical variables were converted to categorical variables to facilitate stratified analyses. Age was divided into five groups: under 50 years old, 50 to 59 years old, etc. Body mass index was divided into four groups: lean, normal, overweight, and Obesity. Logistic regression models estimated odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (95% CIs) for the association between high blood glucose and shoulder pain. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

Results

Baseline characteristics of the cross-sectional study participants in 2011

A total of 13,523 participants were included in the cross-sectional analysis in 2011. The prevalences of shoulder pain in the overall population, normal blood glucose group, and high blood glucose group were 12.9% (1,749/13,523), 12.6% (1,603/12,732), and 18.5% (146/791), respectively. High blood glucose was more common among females, older adults, individuals with elevated body mass index, and those with hypertension or cardiovascular diseases (Table 1).

Retrospective cohort study between baseline high blood glucose and incident shoulder pain at follow-up 2011–2015

There were 8,440 participants included in the retrospective cohort study. During 4 years of follow-up, the incidence of new shoulder pain was higher among those with high blood glucose (46.14 vs. 28.61 per 1,000 person-years) (Table 3). After adjusting for relevant covariates, the results showed that the high blood glucose group was 1.58 times to have shoulder pain than the normal blood glucose group (Table 3).

Discussion

This cross-sectional study identified high blood glucose as a risk factor for shoulder pain in middle-aged and older Chinese adults, and this association remained consistent in subgroup analyses. Retrospective Cohort Study also showed that the incidence of shoulder pain in patients with hyperglycemia was significantly higher than in patients with normoglycemia. These findings emphasize the need for strengthened monitoring and evaluation of shoulder pain during hyperglycemia management and provide new perspectives for prevention and treatment aimed at improving patient quality of life.

In the cross-sectional analysis (Table 1), participants with hyperglycemia were older, had higher body mass index (BMI), included more females, and were more likely to have cardiovascular disease, consistent with typical clinical characteristics of patients with hyperglycemia21,22. Previous studies have confirmed links between high blood glucose and multiple chronic diseases, such as cardiovascular diseases, hypothyroidism, and osteoporosis5,6,23–25. Numerous studies have shown that several factors influence shoulder pain, including age, BMI, depressive symptoms, stroke, hypothyroidism, biomechanical and psychosocial factors26–33. In our analysis (Fig. 2), cardiovascular disease, diabetes, female gender, and smoking were relevant to shoulder pain, aligning with earlier research17,27,29,34. Participants with older age and higher BMI were less likely to report shoulder pain, which differs from previous findings35–37 and suggests that regional and ethnic differences may influence shoulder pain and require further study. Higher education levels were associated with a lower incidence of shoulder pain, indicating that individuals with higher education may pay closer attention to health38,39. Shoulder pain may arise from diverse disorders, such as rotator cuff disease, adhesive capsulitis, shoulder instability, shoulder osteoarthritis, stroke, and even laparoscopic surgery40–44. Because the study relied on reported symptoms, specific causes could not be identified, which may introduce bias. To address this, subgroup analyses were performed (Table 2), and the results remained robust.

To confirm the association between high blood glucose and shoulder pain further, we performed a retrospective cohort analysis using baseline data and follow-up records (Table 3). Patients with hyperglycemia had a markedly higher risk of new shoulder pain, with an annual incidence of 46.14 vs. 28.61 per 1,000 person-years. This result is consistent with previous longitudinal studies19,45. After adjusting for relevant factors, the incidence rate of new shoulder pain in the high blood glucose group was 1.58 times that of the normal blood glucose group, confirming that hyperglycemia is a risk factor for shoulder pain. Carvalho-e-Silva reported nearly consistent results, as their longitudinal analysis showed that the incidence of shoulder pain among patients with diabetes was 1.56 times that of those without diabetes46.To further confirm reliability, we conducted subgroup analyses, and as shown in Fig. 3, the results remained stable across subgroups. However, because of the short follow-up period, the number of new shoulder pain cases in the high blood glucose group was relatively small. In some subgroups, such as age and smoking, case numbers were insufficient for meaningful analysis. Future research may need to examine the effects of age and smoking on shoulder pain separately. Improving lifestyle factors and quality of life among patients with hyperglycemia and reducing shoulder pain incidence may help lessen the economic burden on individuals and society. These findings offer clinical reference for intervention and important information for policymakers developing management strategies for diabetes and related diseases.

This study uses data from the CHARLS to conduct both cross-sectional and retrospective cohort analyses on the relationship between hyperglycemia and shoulder pain in middle-aged and older Chinese adults, making the results more robust. Despite the clinical relevance, certain limitations exist. The underlying data came from a single public database, and was not verified by external data or our own clinical data, which may influence generalizability. Reliance on self-reported questionnaires may introduce response bias affecting accuracy. Some related covariates were not collected, such as thyroid diseases, Parkinson’s disease and the application of related drugs, etc. No causal model has been provided to distinguish confounding factors from mediating factors, potential overadjustment bias remains unaddressed (e.g., the inclusion of body-mass index and cardiovascular disease). Future study should incorporate diverse and sufficient data, longer follow-up, and varied research designs to improve reliability and applicability.

In conclusion, this study identifies a significant positive association between high blood glucose and shoulder pain, highlighting the importance of blood sugar control to reduce the occurrence of shoulder pain among patients with high blood glucose in middle-aged and older Chinese adults.

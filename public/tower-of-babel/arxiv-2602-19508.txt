On factorization of matrix of Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials
Aritra Bhattacharya, Ashish Mishra, Shraddha Srivastava

Abstract
. Let ℋ = ℋ ⁡ ( W , S ) \mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}(W,S) be the Hecke algebra of the Coxeter system ( W , S ) (W,S) over ℤ ⁡ [ q ± 1 ] \mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}] , where W W is the Weyl group of a symmetrizable Kac-Moody algebra. In this paper, we show that the matrix of Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials of ℋ \mathcal{H} factorizes into a product of | S | |S| many matrices, each of which has entries as polynomials in q q with nonnegative coefficients. To achieve this goal, we use hybrid basis T ​ C J TC^{J} for J ⊆ S J\subseteq S of ℋ \mathcal{H} , defined by Grojnowski-Haiman. The intermediate matrices in the aforementioned factorization turn out to be the transition matrices from T ​ C J TC^{J} -basis to T ​ C I TC^{I} -basis for I ⊂ J I\subset J . Equivalently, these coefficients can be computed using a natural restriction map from ℋ \mathcal{H} to the parabolic Hecke algebra ℋ J \mathcal{H}_{J} . Moreover, following the ideas from Grojnowski-Haiman, we also give a geometric proof of the positivity of these coefficients.

1.
Introduction

For Hecke algebra of a Coxeter system, Kazhdan and Lusztig
[
KL79
]
defined Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials as the integral polynomial coefficients for the change of basis from Kazhdan-Lusztig basis to standard basis and showed in
[
KL80
]
that for finite and affine Weyl groups, these polynomials have nonnegative coefficients. Later on, Elias and Williamson
[
EW14
]
showed the nonnegativity of these coefficients for arbitrary Coxeter systems.

While considering the question of positivity of coefficients for the multiplication by Kazhdan-Lusztig basis element, Grojnowski and Haiman introduced hybrid basis, generalizing well-known positivity theorems of Springer
[
Spr82
]
, Lusztig
[
Lus85
]
, and Dyer and Lehrer
[
DL90
]
. As hybrid basis interpolates between standard basis and Kazhdan-Lusztig basis, a natural question to explore in this more general setting is to study the positivity of coefficients for the change of basis between hybrid bases. We answer this question in
B
, which provides us a factorization of the matrix of Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials into a product of matrices, each of which has entries as polynomials with nonnegative coefficients, see
C
.

Consider a Coxeter system
(W,S)
, where
W
is the Weyl group of a symmetrizable Kac-Moody algebra and
S
is a set of simple reflections that generate
W
. The Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}
is a
\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]
-algebra that serves as a
q
-analogue of the group algebra
\mathbb{Z}[W]
of
W
.
For a subset
J\subseteq S
, one can consider the Coxeter subsystem
(W_{J},J)
, where
W_{J}
is the (parabolic) subgroup of
W
generated by
J
and let
\mathcal{H}_{J}
be its corresponding Hecke algebra. The Hecke algebras have a standard basis
\{T_{w}:w\in W\}
indexed by elements of the Weyl group. Consider a natural restriction map
|_{J}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}_{J}
by defining on standard basis elements

\displaystyle\qquad T_{w}|_{J}=\begin{cases}T_{w}&\hbox{ if }w\in W_{J},\\
0&\hbox{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

and extending linearly to
\mathcal{H}
.

Let
\{C_{w}:w\in W\}
denote the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis of
\mathcal{H}
(these are denoted
C^{\prime}_{w}
in
[
KL79
]
by Kazhdan and Lusztig). Then one can ask whether the coefficients of
C_{w}|_{J}
in the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis of
\mathcal{H}_{J}
are nonnegative. In this paper, we confirm that this is indeed true. Let
W^{J}
denote the minimal length left coset representatives of
W/W_{J}
. Our main result states that

Theorem A
.

For
u\in W^{J}
and
w\in W
, the coefficients of
(T_{u^{-1}}C_{w})|_{J}
with respect to the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis of
\mathcal{H}_{J}
are polynomials in
q
with nonnegative coefficients.

In what follows, the polynomials in
A
are referred to as restriction coefficients. In Section
6
we show that the parabolic Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials corresponding to the sign representation appear as a special case of these.
Positivity of coefficients in
A
can be equivalently reinterpreted in terms of coefficients of change of basis for a hybrid basis
TC^{J}:=\{TC^{J}_{w}:w\in W\}
of
\mathcal{H}
defined by Grojnowski and Haiman
[
GH07
]
. This hybrid basis interpolates between the standard basis (when
J=\emptyset
) and the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis (when
J=S
). Since the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis expands positively in the standard basis, one may ask a more general question: whether
TC^{J}
-basis expands positively in the
TC^{I}
-basis whenever
I\subseteq J\subseteq S
. We show that this is indeed the case in the following theorem.

Theorem B
.

For
I\subseteq J\subseteq S
and
w\in W
, the coefficients
h^{I,J}_{x,w}(q)
in the expansion

(1.1)

TC^{J}_{w}=\sum_{x\in W}h^{I,J}_{x,w}(q)TC^{I}_{x}

are polynomials in
q
with nonnegative coefficients.

See Section
5
for a proof of
A
and
B
. These are
Corollary 5.2
and
Theorem 5.1
, respectively.

B
immediately implies an interesting property of the matrix of Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials:

Theorem C
.

For a chain of subsets

(1.2)

\emptyset=J_{0}\subset J_{1}\subset\cdots\subset J_{k}=S,

the matrix of Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials of
\mathcal{H}
factorizes into a product of
k
many matrices, each of which has entries as polynomials in
q
with nonnegative coefficients. In particular, we can take the Chain
1.2
in such a manner that each subset
J_{i}
has
i
elements and
k=|S|.

We prove
Lemma 5.5
and
Lemma 5.6
, and use these along with
[
GH07
, Theorem 3.2]
to prove

B
. Grojnowski and Haiman have used the techniques from perverse sheaves and mixed Hodge modules in order to prove
[
GH07
, Theorem 3.2]
. In Section
8
, we give a geometric proof of
A
that is essentially the arguments of
[
GH07
]
. Since these geometric methods are available only for Weyl groups of symmetrizable Kac-Moody algebras, the corresponding statements of Theorems
A
,
B
and
C
for general Coxeter groups remain open. Computing explicitly the restriction coefficients in the case of dihedral groups in
Proposition 7.2
leads us to conclude that the positivity holds true for dihedral groups. In a subsequent work, we would like to study the corresponding positivity question for all Coxeter groups using the machinery of Soergel bimodules from
[
EW14
]
.

We have computed various examples of the restriction coefficients in Section
7
. For Weyl group
W
of type
A_{n}
, we compute the restriction coefficients
(T_{u^{-1}}C_{w})|_{[n-1]}
, where
[n-1]=\{1,2,\ldots,n-1\}
(for convenience of notation, we use
(T_{u^{-1}}C_{w})|_{[n-1]}
to mean
(T_{u^{-1}}C_{w})|_{J[n-1]}
, where
J[n-1]=\{s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{n-1}\}
), where

runs over the set
W^{[n-1]}=\{1,s_{n},s_{n-1}s_{n},\ldots,s_{2}\cdots s_{n},s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{n}\}
and

. We obtain explicit formulas for the restriction coefficients of
(T_{u^{-1}}C_{w})|_{[n-1]}
when
u=s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{n},s_{2}s_{3}\cdots s_{n}
and
s_{3}\cdots s_{n}
.

2.
Preliminaries of Hecke algebras

This section contains preliminaries of Hecke algebra of a Coxeter system. Our main reference for this section is
[
EMTW20
]
.

The
Hecke algebra

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}(W)
of a Coxeter system
(W,S)
is the
\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]
-algebra generated by
\{T_{s}:s\in S\}
with
T_{s}
satisfying the braid relations and the quadratic relations:

(2.1)

T_{s}^{2}=1+(q^{-1}-q)T_{s},\qquad\hbox{ for all }s\in S.

Given a reduced expression
w=s_{i_{1}}\ldots s_{i_{\ell}}
for
w\in W
, define
T_{w}=T_{s_{i_{1}}}\ldots T_{s_{i_{\ell}}}
. Since any two reduced expressions for
w
are related by the braid relations
T_{w}
is a well defined element of
\mathcal{H}
. The collection
\{T_{w}:w\in W\}
forms a basis of
\mathcal{H}
known as the
standard basis
.
Later, we also use the following multiplication rule

(2.2)

T_{s}T_{w}=\begin{cases}T_{sw}&\hbox{ if }sw>w,\\
T_{sw}+(q^{-1}-q)T_{w}&\hbox{ if }sw<w.\end{cases}

The
bar involution
is a ring automorphism
\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
given by

\overline{T_{x}}=(T_{x^{-1}})^{-1},\quad\hbox{ for all }x\in W,\quad\hbox{ and }\quad\overline{q^{\pm 1}}=q^{\mp 1}.

Recall the
Bruhat order
is a partial order on
W
defined by setting
u\leq v
if any (or some) reduced expression for
v
contains a subexpression for
u
.

For each
w\in W
, there is a unique self-dual element
C_{w}\in\mathcal{H}
such that if

(2.3)

C_{w}=\sum_{x\in W}h_{x,w}(q)T_{x},

then

(a)

h_{x,w}(q)=0
if
x\nleq w
, and
h_{w,w}(q)=1
,

(b)

h_{x,w}(q)\in q\mathbb{Z}[q]
for
x<w
,

where
<
denotes the Bruhat order. In particular, the elements
C_{w}
are unitriangular written in terms of standard basis with respect to the Bruhat order. Thus the set
\{C_{w}:w\in W\}
forms a basis of
\mathcal{H}
known as the
Kazhdan-Lusztig basis
. The Kazhdan-Lusztig basis was introduced in
[
KL79
]
, where a different normalization is used and their
C^{\prime}
is our
C
. We follow the normalization used in
[
EMTW20
]
, where
T_{w}
is denoted
\delta_{w}
and
C_{w}
is denoted
b_{w}
.
The coefficients
h_{x,w}(q)\in\mathbb{Z}[q]
in (
2.3
) are known as the
Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials
. Unless there is ambiguity, we suppress
q
in
h_{x,w}(q)
and write
h_{x,w}
for notational convenience.

Example 2.1
.

Two particular examples of Kazhdan-Lusztig basis elements are as follows:

(a)

For
s\in S
,
C_{s}=T_{s}+q.

(b)

Suppose
W
is finite and
w_{0}
the longest element in it. Then

C_{w_{0}}=\sum_{y\in W}q^{\ell(w_{0})-\ell(y)}T_{y}.

The Kazhdan-Lusztig basis elements can be recursively constructed in the following way: Start with
C_{1}=1
.
For
s\in S
and
w\in W
such that
ws>w
, we have

(2.4)

C_{ws}=C_{w}C_{s}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z<w\\
zs<z\end{subarray}}\mu(z,w)C_{z},

where
\mu(z,w):=
the coefficient of
q
in
h_{z,w}
.

The
\omega
-involution
is a ring anti-automorphism
\omega:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
given by

\omega(T_{x})=T_{x}^{-1},\qquad\hbox{ for all }x\in W,\qquad\hbox{ and }\qquad\overline{q^{\pm 1}}=q^{\mp 1}.

There is a standard
\mathbb{Z}
-bilinear form
(-,-):\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]
on
\mathcal{H}
defined by
(\overline{T_{x}},T_{y})=\delta_{x,y}
for
x,y\in\mathcal{H}
. The adjoint of multiplication by
a\in\mathcal{H}
under this form is given by multiplication by
\omega(a)
:

(2.5)

(ah,h^{\prime})=(h,\omega(a)h^{\prime})\qquad\hbox{ for }a,h,h^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}.

We recall the recursions for the Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials
h_{y,x}(q)
following
[
Cas16
]
.

Proposition 2.2

(Proposition 4.4
[
Cas16
]
)
.

Suppose
s\in S
such that
sx<x\mbox{ and }y\leq x
. Then

\displaystyle h_{y,x}=\begin{cases}\qquad\qquad\qquad 1&\hbox{ if }y=x,\\
h_{sy,sx}+q^{c}h_{y,sx}-\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\\
y\leq w<sx\\
sw<w\end{subarray}}\mu(w,sx)h_{y,w}&\hbox{ if }y\neq x,\end{cases}

where

c=\begin{cases}-1&\hbox{ if }sy<y,\\
+1&\hbox{ if }sy>y.\end{cases}

Proposition 2.3

(Corollary 4.5 of
[
Cas16
]
)
.

If
s\in S
is such that
sx<x,sy<y<x,y\nleq sx
, then

h_{y,x}=h_{sy,sx}.

Proposition 2.4

(Proposition 5.2 of
[
Cas16
]
)
.

If
s\in S
and
x,y\in W
such that
sx<x\mbox{ and }sy>y
, then
h_{y,x}=qh_{sy,x}
.

Notations.
We use the characteristic function
\chi_{-}
to which takes the value
1
on a statement if it is true and the value
0
on a statement if it is false. For example,

\chi_{w\in W_{J}}=\begin{cases}1&\hbox{ if }w\in W_{J},\\
0&\hbox{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

We also use the Kronecker delta function

\delta_{x,y}=\begin{cases}1&\hbox{ if }x=y,\\
0&\hbox{ if }x\neq y.\end{cases}

If
\mathcal{B}
is a basis, then for
b\in\mathcal{B}
we write
[b]h
to denote the coefficient of
b
in the
\mathcal{B}
expansion of
h
, i.e,

h=\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}([b]h)b.

3.
The restriction map

In this section, we define a restriction map and study its properties.

Let
J\subseteq S
and
W_{J}=\langle J\rangle
is the parabolic subgroup of
W
corresponding to
J
, with corresponding Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{J}=\mathcal{H}(W_{J})
. Using the standard basis
\{T_{w}:w\in W\}
of
\mathcal{H}
, we define a restriction map

\displaystyle|_{J}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}_{J}

\displaystyle\qquad T_{w}\mapsto\begin{cases}T_{w}&\hbox{ if }w\in W_{J},\\
0&\hbox{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

and extend linearly to
\mathcal{H}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The restriction map
|_{J}
is both left and right
\mathcal{H}_{J}
-linear: For
g\in\mathcal{H}_{J}
and
h\in\mathcal{H}
,

(3.1)

(hg)|_{J}=h|_{J}\cdot g\qquad\hbox{ and }\qquad(gh)|_{J}=g\cdot h|_{J}.

Proof.

We prove the left sided
\mathcal{H}_{J}
-linearity of the restriction map
|_{J}
by induction on the length of the elements of
W_{J}
.

Let
s\in J
and
w\in W
. If
sw>w
, then
T_{s}T_{w}=T_{sw}
, which gives

\displaystyle(T_{s}T_{w})|_{J}=T_{sw}|_{J}=\chi_{w\in W_{J}}T_{sw}=T_{s}\chi_{w\in W_{J}}T_{w}=T_{s}T_{w}|_{J}.

If
sw<w
, then by (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle(T_{s}T_{w})|_{J}=(T_{sw}+(q^{-1}-q)T_{w})|_{J}=\chi_{w\in W_{J}}(T_{sw}+(q^{-1}-q)T_{w})=T_{s}\chi_{w\in W_{J}}T_{w}=T_{s}T_{w}|_{J}.

By linearity, we conclude that for
s\in J
,
h\in\mathcal{H}
,

(T_{s}h)|_{J}=T_{s}h|_{J}.

Now by induction on length of
v\in W_{J}
, we prove that for
h\in\mathcal{H}
,

(T_{v}h)|_{J}=T_{v}h|_{J}.

Suppose
\ell(sv)=\ell(s)+\ell(v)
with
s\in J,h\in\mathcal{H}
. Then

\displaystyle(T_{sv}h)|_{J}=(T_{s}T_{v}h)|_{J}=T_{s}(T_{v}h)|_{J}=T_{s}T_{v}h|_{J}=T_{sv}h|_{J}.

This proves the left sided
\mathcal{H}_{J}
-linearity of the restriction map
|_{J}
.
Similarly, the right sided
\mathcal{H}_{J}
-linearity is obtained.
∎

Using
\mathcal{H}_{J}
-linearity, we compute the following examples.

Example 3.2
.

Since
C_{s}=T_{s}+q
for
s\in S
, the restriction

C_{s}|_{J}=\begin{cases}C_{s}&\hbox{ if }s\in J,\\
q&\hbox{ if }s\notin J.\end{cases}

Consider
\{s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{k}\}\subseteq S
and
J=\{s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{j}\}
for
j\leq k
.
Then

\displaystyle(C_{s_{1}}C_{s_{2}}\cdots C_{s_{k}})|_{J}=q^{k-j}C_{s_{1}}\cdots C_{s_{j}}=(C_{s_{j+1}}C_{s_{j+2}}\cdots C_{s_{k}}C_{s_{1}}\cdots C_{s_{j}})|_{J}.

Example 3.3
.

Since
{}^{J}W\cap W_{J}=\{1\}
, we get for
u\in W^{J}
and
s\in J
,
(T_{u^{-1}}C_{s})|_{J}=\delta_{u,1}C_{s}
.

Suppose
u,u^{\prime}\in W^{J}
and
v\in W_{J}
, then by
Proposition 3.1
,

\displaystyle(T_{u^{-1}}T_{u^{\prime}v})|_{J}=(T_{u^{-1}}T_{u^{\prime}}\cdot T_{v})|_{J}=(T_{u^{-1}}T_{u^{\prime}})|_{J}\cdot T_{v}.

Lemma 3.4
.

For
u,u^{\prime}\in W^{J}
,

(T_{u^{-1}}T_{u^{\prime}})|_{J}=\delta_{u,u^{\prime}}.

Proof.

The coefficient of
T_{v}
for
v\in W_{J}
in
(T_{u^{-1}}T_{u^{\prime}})|_{J}
equals

\displaystyle(\overline{T}_{v},T_{u^{-1}}T_{u^{\prime}})=(\overline{T}_{v},\omega(\overline{T}_{u})T_{u^{\prime}})=(\overline{T}_{u}\overline{T}_{v},T_{u^{\prime}})=(\overline{T}_{uv},T_{u^{\prime}})=\delta_{u,u^{\prime}}\delta_{1,v}.

∎

For
u\in W^{J},w\in W
, by
Lemma 3.4
,

(3.2)

(T_{u^{-1}}C_{w})|_{J}=\sum_{y\leq w}h_{y,w}(q)(T_{u^{-1}}T_{y})|_{J}=\sum_{v\in W_{J}}h_{uv,w}(q)T_{v}.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
u\in W^{J}
and
w\in W.
Then, for the parabolic factorization
w=w_{J}w^{J}
,

(T_{u^{-1}}C_{w})|_{J}=0\hbox{ if }u\nleq w^{J}.

Proof.

By (
3.2
), if
T_{v}
appears in the
T
-expansion of
(T_{u^{-1}}C_{w})|_{J}
then we must have
uv\leq w
. By
[
BB05
, Proposition 2.5.1]
parabolic factorization respects Bruhat order for quotients. Hence
u\leq w^{J}
.
∎

For
u\in W^{J},w\in W
, define
h^{J}_{uv,w}(q)
by

(3.3)

(T_{u^{-1}}C_{w})|_{J}=\sum_{v\in W_{J}}h^{J}_{uv,w}(q)C_{v}.

Combining (
3.2
), (
3.3
) and (
2.3
) we get

h_{uy,w}(q)=\sum_{v\in W_{J}}h^{J}_{uv,w}(q)h_{y,v}(q)\quad\hbox{ for }y\in W_{J}.

Since the matrix
(h_{y,v}(q))_{y,v\in W_{J}}
is unitriangular, this shows that

h^{J}_{uv,w}(q)\in\mathbb{Z}[q]

for
u\in W^{J},v\in W_{J}
and
w\in W
. Note that this also implies
h^{J}_{uv,w}(q)=0
if
uv\nleq w
.

The following proposition gives a condition for vanishing of
h^{J}_{uv,w}(q).

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
w\in W,u\in W^{J},v\in W_{J}
and
s\in J
be such that
vs>v,ws<w
. Then
h^{J}_{uv,w}(q)=0.

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle(q+q^{-1})(T_{u^{-1}}C_{w})|_{J}=(T_{u^{-1}}C_{w}C_{s})|_{J}=(T_{u^{-1}}C_{w})|_{J}C_{s}=\sum_{v^{\prime}\in W_{J}}h^{J}_{uv^{\prime},w}(q)C_{v^{\prime}}C_{s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v^{\prime}\in W_{J}\\
v^{\prime}s>v^{\prime}\end{subarray}}h^{J}_{uv^{\prime},w}(q)\bigg(C_{v^{\prime}s}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z<v^{\prime}\\
zs<z\end{subarray}}\mu(z,v^{\prime})C_{z}\bigg)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v^{\prime}\in W_{J}\\
v^{\prime}s<v^{\prime}\end{subarray}}h^{J}_{uv^{\prime},w}(q)\cdot(q+q^{-1})C_{v^{\prime}},

(T_{u^{-1}}C_{w})|_{J}
only has those
C_{v}
with
vs<v
appearing.
∎

4.
The hybrid basis

This section defines hybrid basis of Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}
. Also we establish a connection between hybrid basis and restriction map in
Proposition 4.2
. Furthermore, we show that the parabolic Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials are a special case of restriction coefficients.

Let
J\subseteq S
be a subset of the set
S
of simple reflections. We recall the definition of
hybrid basis

TC^{J}:=\{TC^{J}_{w}:w\in W\}
and
CT^{J}:=\{CT^{J}_{w}:w\in W\}
of the Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}
as defined by Grojnowski and Haiman in
[
GH07
]
. Let
W_{J}
be the parabolic subgroup of
W
generated by
J
. Let
W^{J}
be the minimal length left coset representatives in
W/W_{J}
and
{}^{J}\!W
the minimal length right coset representatives in
W_{J}\backslash W
. For
w\in W
, there are parabolic factorizations

\displaystyle w=w^{J}\cdot w_{J}\qquad\hbox{ and }\qquad w=_{J}\!\!w\cdot^{J}\!\!w

with
w_{J},_{J}\!w\in W_{J}
and
w^{J}\in W^{J},^{J}\!\!w\in^{J}\!W
.
[
BB05
, Proposition 2.4.4]
says that such a factorization exists and is unique, with
\ell(w)=\ell(w^{J})+\ell(w_{J})=\ell(_{J}w)+\ell(^{J\!}w)
. Define

(4.1)

TC^{J}_{w}=T_{w^{J}}C_{w_{J}}\qquad\hbox{ and }\qquad CT^{J}_{w}=C_{{}_{J}w}T_{{}^{J\!}w}.

On the extreme ends, we recover the standard and Kazhdan-Lusztig bases of
\mathcal{H}
: when
J=\emptyset
,
TC^{\emptyset}_{w}=T_{w}
and when
J=S
,
TC^{S}_{w}=C_{w}
.

There exists a ring anti-automorphism
\Psi:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
such that
\Psi(T_{w})=T_{w^{-1}}
and
\Psi
fixes
q
. Further,
\Psi
commutes with bar involution and therefore,
\Psi(C_{w})=C_{w^{-1}}
. Thus,

(4.2)

\Psi(TC^{J}_{w})=\Psi(C_{w_{J}})\Psi(T_{w^{J}})=C_{w_{J}^{-1}}T_{{(w^{J})}^{-1}}=CT^{J}_{w^{-1}}.

Because of this, we will get similar results for both types of hybrid bases. It will be enough to consider one or the other, depending on the context.

Now we show that
TC^{J}
is a basis of
\mathcal{H}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{J}
be the Hecke algebra of the Coxeter subsystem
(W_{J},J)
generated by
J\subseteq S
. Let
\mathcal{B}=\{b_{w}:w\in W_{J}\}
be a basis for
\mathcal{H}_{J}
. Then the set
\{T_{u}b_{v}\,|\,u\in W^{J},v\in W_{J}\}
is a basis for
\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

To show that
\{T_{u}b_{v}\,|\,u\in W^{J},v\in W_{J}\}
is a basis, it is enough to show that each
T_{w}:w\in W
can be uniquely written as a linear combination of
T_{u}b_{v}^{\prime}
s. By uniqueness of parabolic factorization, we have
T_{w}=T_{u}T_{v}
for unique
u\in W^{J},v\in W_{J}
. Also,
T_{v}\in\mathcal{H}_{J}
and hence, can be expressed uniquely as a linear combination of
\{b_{v}:v\in W_{J}\}
.
∎

By
[
BB05
, Proposition 2.4.4, (2.11),(2.12)]
, if
u\in W^{J}
and
v\in W_{J}
then
\ell(uv)=\ell(u)+\ell(v)
, so
T_{u}T_{v}=T_{uv}
. Since
\ell(uv)=\ell(u)+\ell(v)
, a reduced expression for
uv
is obtained by concatenating a reduced expression for
u
and a reduced expression for
v
. Then by subword criteria, if
x<v
then
ux<uv=w
.
Then

\displaystyle TC^{J}_{w}

\displaystyle=T_{u}C_{v}=T_{u}(T_{v}+\sum_{x<v}h_{x,v}(q)T_{x})=T_{uv}+\sum_{x<v}h_{x,v}(q)T_{ux}

\displaystyle=T_{w}+\sum_{x<v}h_{x,v}(q)T_{ux},

so
TC^{J}_{w}
is unitriangular with respect to the Bruhat order when expressed in terms of the standard basis.

The following proposition explains a connection between
TC^{J}
-basis and the restriction map defined in Section
3
.

Proposition 4.2
.

For
u\in W^{J}
,
v\in W_{J}
and
h\in\mathcal{H}
, the coefficient of
TC^{J}_{uv}
in the
TC^{J}
-expansion of
h
equals the coefficient of
C_{v}
in the Kazhdan-Lusztig -expansion of
(T_{u^{-1}}h)|_{J}
:

(4.3)

[TC^{J}_{uv}]h=[C_{v}](T_{u^{-1}}h)|_{J}.

Proof.

Suppose

h=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u^{\prime}\in W^{J}\\
v\in W_{J}\end{subarray}}b_{u^{\prime}v}(q)TC^{J}_{u^{\prime}v}.

Then using
\mathcal{H}_{J}
-linearity of the restriction map and
Lemma 3.4
,

\displaystyle(T_{u^{-1}}h)|_{J}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u^{\prime}\in W^{J}\\
v\in W_{J}\end{subarray}}b_{u^{\prime}v}(q)(T_{u^{-1}}T_{u^{\prime}}C_{v})|_{J}=\sum_{v\in W_{J}}b_{uv}(q)C_{v}.

Thus,
[C_{v}](T_{u^{-1}}h)|_{J}=b_{uv}(q)=[TC^{J}_{uv}]h
.
∎

Below we recall the following result from
[
GH07
, Corollary 3.9]
, which is the source of our main results.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(W,S)
be the Coxeter system of a Weyl group of a symmetrizable Kac-Moody algebra, and
\mathcal{H}
be its Hecke algebra and
J\subseteq S
. For
w,u\in W
, the product
C_{w}TC^{J}_{u}
expands positively in the
TC^{J}
-basis:

C_{w}TC^{J}_{u}=\sum_{v\in W}d^{w}_{u,v}(q)TC^{J}_{v}

with
d^{w}_{u,v}(q)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[q^{\pm 1}]
.

5.
Proofs of main results

In this section, we give a proof of
B
(restated as

) and a proof of

(restated as

Theorem 5.1
.

Let

We defer giving a proof of the above theorem until the end of this section. Now we state and prove its corollary.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
(W,S)
be the Coxeter system of a Weyl group of a symmetrizable Kac-Moody algebra, and

has coefficients in

:

(5.2)

(T_{u^{-1}}C_{w})|_{J}=\sum_{v\in W_{J}}h^{J}_{uv,w}(q)C_{v},

with
h^{J}_{uv,w}(q)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[q]
.

Proof.

By
Proposition 4.2
,

(5.3)

h^{J}_{uv,w}(q)=[C_{v}](T_{u^{-1}}C_{w})|_{J}=[TC^{J}_{uv}]C_{w}=h^{J,S}_{uv,w}(q).

∎

Below we give two examples illustrating

.

Example 5.3
.

When

and
I=\emptyset
, then

,
W^{J}=\{u\in W\,|\,us_{1}>u\}
. If

,

\displaystyle TC^{[1]}_{w}=T_{ws_{1}}C_{s_{1}}=T_{ws_{1}}(T_{s_{1}}+q)=T_{w}+qT_{ws_{1}}.

If
w\in W

, then

Example 5.4
.

For type A,
J=\{1,2\}
and
I=\{1\}
, the parabolic factorizations for

are

\displaystyle 1=1\cdot 1,s_{1}=1\cdot s_{1},s_{2}=s_{2}\cdot 1,s_{1}s_{2}=s_{1}s_{2}\cdot 1,s_{2}s_{1}=s_{2}\cdot s_{1},s_{1}s_{2}s_{1}=s_{1}s_{2}\cdot s_{1}.

Let

. Then

\displaystyle TC^{\{1,2\}}_{u}=T_{u}=TC^{\{1\}}_{u},

\displaystyle TC^{\{1,2\}}_{us_{1}}=T_{u}C_{s_{1}}=TC^{\{1\}}_{us_{1}},

\displaystyle TC^{\{1,2\}}_{us_{2}}=T_{u}C_{s_{2}}=T_{us_{2}}+qT_{u}=TC^{\{1\}}_{us_{2}}+qTC^{\{1\}}_{u},

\displaystyle TC^{\{1,2\}}_{us_{1}s_{2}}=T_{u}C_{s_{1}}C_{s_{2}}=T_{us_{1}s_{2}}+q(T_{us_{1}}+T_{us_{2}})+q^{2}T_{u}=TC^{\{1\}}_{us_{1}s_{2}}+qTC^{\{1\}}_{us_{2}}+qTC^{\{1\}}_{us_{1}},

\displaystyle TC^{\{1,2\}}_{us_{2}s_{1}}=T_{u}C_{s_{2}}C_{s_{1}}=T_{u}(T_{s_{2}}+q)C_{s_{1}}=T_{us_{2}}C_{s_{1}}+qT_{u}C_{s_{1}}=TC^{\{1\}}_{us_{2}s_{1}}+qTC^{\{1\}}_{us_{1}},

since

\displaystyle TC^{\{1\}}_{us_{1}}=T_{u}C_{s_{1}}=T_{us_{1}}+qT_{u},

\displaystyle TC^{\{1\}}_{us_{1}s_{2}s_{1}}=T_{us_{1}s_{2}}C_{s_{1}}=T_{us_{1}s_{2}s_{1}}+qT_{us_{1}s_{2}},

\displaystyle TC^{\{1\}}_{us_{2}s_{1}}=T_{us_{2}}C_{s_{1}}=T_{us_{2}s_{1}}+qT_{us_{2}}.

Now we prove two lemmas which will lead us to a proof of
Theorem 5.1
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
I\subseteq J\subseteq S
and
u\in W^{J},z\in W^{I}\cap W_{J}
. Then
uz\in W^{I}
.

Proof.

We must show that

\ell(uzs)>\ell(uz)\text{ for every }s\in I.

Fix
s\in I
. Since
z\in W_{J}
and
s\in W_{J}
,
zs\in W_{J}
. Because
u\in W^{J}
and
zs\in W_{J}
,

\ell(uzs)=\ell(u)+\ell(zs).

Since
z\in W^{I}
, no simple reflection in
I
is a right descent of
z
; hence

\ell(zs)=\ell(z)+1.

Also
u\in W^{J}
and
z\in W_{J}
imply that
\ell(u)+\ell(z)=\ell(uz).

Therefore

\ell(uzs)=\ell(u)+\ell(zs)=\ell(u)+\ell(z)+1=\ell(uz)+1>\ell(uz).

∎

Lemma 5.6
.

If
u\in W^{J},z\in W_{J}
and
I\subseteq J
then
T_{u}TC^{I}_{z}=TC^{I}_{uz}

Proof.

Suppose
z=z^{I}\cdot z_{I}
is the parabolic factorization of
z
with respect to
I
. Then
z^{I}\in W^{I}\cap W_{J}
. So by
Lemma 5.5
,
uz^{I}\in W^{I}
. Hence,
uz=uz^{I}\cdot z_{I}
is a parabolic factorization of
uz
with respect to
I
. Also
u\in W^{J}
and
z^{I}\in W_{J}
imply that
\ell(uz^{I})=\ell(u)+\ell(z^{I})
. Then
TC^{I}_{uz}=T_{uz^{I}}C_{z_{I}}=T_{u}T_{z^{I}}C_{z_{I}}=T_{u}TC^{I}_{z}
.
∎

Proof of
Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose
w=uv
with
u\in W^{J}
and
v\in W_{J}
. Then
TC^{J}_{w}=T_{u}C_{v}
.
Using (
5.1
) and (
5.3
) write

C_{v}=\sum_{x\in W}h^{I}_{x,v}(q)TC^{I}_{x}.

Since
v\in W_{J}
and the sum can only run over
x\leq v
in Bruhat order, the sum must be over
x\in W_{J}
. Then

TC^{J}_{w}=T_{u}C_{v}=T_{u}\sum_{x\in W}h^{I}_{x,v}(q)TC^{I}_{x}=\sum_{x\in W_{J}}h^{I}_{x,v}(q)TC^{I}_{ux}

by

.
Therefore,

(5.4)

[TC^{I}_{uv}]TC^{J}_{u^{\prime}v^{\prime}}=[TC^{I}_{v}]C_{v^{\prime}}\delta_{u,u^{\prime}}\quad\hbox{ for }u,u^{\prime}\in W^{J},\ v,v^{\prime}\in W_{J}.

By
Theorem 4.3
, we have
[TC^{I}_{v}]C_{v^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[q]
. This proves

. ∎

and

imply that

and

are equivalent.

6.
Parabolic Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials

In this section we show that the parabolic Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials associated to sign representations are special cases of restriction coefficients.
These polynomials were defined by Deodhar in
[
Deo87
]
, below we recall them using normalization from
[
Soe97
]
. As shown in
[
GH07
]
, the parabolic Kazhdan-Lusztig positivity can be derived from their main result
Theorem 4.3
. In fact, it may be deduced from our weaker statement
Theorem 5.1
or
Corollary 5.2
as we will show below that the parabolic Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials are special cases of restriction coefficients.

Consider the sign representation of
\mathcal{H}_{J}
:

\epsilon(T_{w})=(-q)^{\ell(w)}\text{ for }w\in W_{J}.

Then the induced representation
\text{Ind}_{\mathcal{H}_{J}}^{\mathcal{H}}(\epsilon)
is isomorphic to the
\mathcal{H}
-module
\mathcal{H}e_{J}^{-}
, where
e_{J}^{-}
satisfies

(6.1)

(T_{s}+q)e_{J}^{-}=0,\text{ for }s\in J.

The sets
\{T_{u}e_{J}^{-}\mid u\in W^{J}\}
and
\{C_{u}e_{J}^{-}\mid u\in W^{J}\}
are bases of
module
\mathcal{H}e_{J}^{-}
. The parabolic Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials
P^{J^{-}}_{u,u^{\prime}}
for
u,u^{\prime}\in W^{J}
are defined to be the change of basis coefficients

C_{u^{\prime}}e_{J}^{-}=\sum_{u\in W^{J}}P^{J^{-}}_{u,u^{\prime}}(q)T_{u}e_{J}^{-}.

Lemma 6.1
.

For
v\in W_{J}\setminus\{1\}
,

(6.2)

C_{v}e_{J}^{-}=0.

Proof.

Since for
s\in S
,
C_{s}=T_{s}+q
, (
6.1
) says that

C_{s}e_{J}^{-}=0\qquad\hbox{ for }s\in J.

Suppose that
C_{z}e_{J}^{-}=0
for
z\leq v\in W_{J}
with
z\neq 1
. By (
2.4
)

\displaystyle C_{vs}e_{J}^{-}=C_{v}C_{s}e_{J}^{-}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z<v\\
zs<z\end{subarray}}\mu(z,v)C_{z}e_{J}^{-}=0

since all
z\neq 1
in the last sum.
∎

Then for
w\in W
,

(6.3)

TC_{w}e_{J}^{-}=\begin{cases}T_{w}e_{J}^{-}&\text{ if }w\in W^{J},\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

By (
5.1
) and (
5.3
)

C_{w}=\sum_{x\in W}h^{J}_{x,w}(q)TC^{J}_{w}\,.

Then applying
e_{J}^{-}
to both sides and using (
6.3
), we get that

(6.4)

P^{J^{-}}_{u,u^{\prime}}(q)=h^{J}_{u,u^{\prime}}(q)\qquad\hbox{ for }u,u^{\prime}\in W^{J}.

Thus, the parabolic Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials are special cases of restriction coefficients.

Lemma 7.3

Let

For

v\in W_{[n-1]}

\displaystyle(T_{s_{n}\ldots s_{2}}C_{s_{1}\ldots s_{n}\cdot v})|_{[n-1]}=\sum_{y\in W_{[n-1]}}h_{s_{2}\ldots s_{n}\cdot y,s_{1}\ldots s_{n}\cdot v}T_{y}=q\sum_{y\in W_{[n-1]}}h_{y,v}T_{y}=qC_{v}.

∎

For
y,x\in W_{[n-1]}
, by
Proposition 2.4
and
Lemma 7.3
, we have

(7.5)

h_{s_{i+1}\ldots s_{n}y,s_{i}s_{i+1}\ldots s_{n}x}=qh_{s_{i}\ldots s_{n}y,s_{i}\ldots s_{n}x}=qh_{y,x}

and the same calculation as above shows that for
v\in W_{[n-1]}
,

(7.6)

(T_{s_{n}\ldots s_{i+1}}C_{s_{i}\ldots s_{n}\cdot v})|_{[n-1]}=qC_{v}.

Proposition 7.6
.

For
y,x\in W_{[n-1]}
and
i\in[n-2]
,

(7.7)

h_{s_{i+2}\ldots s_{n}y,s_{i}\ldots s_{n}x}=\begin{cases}q^{2}h_{y,x}&\hbox{ if }s_{i}x<x,\\
qh_{s_{i}y,x}+h_{y,x}&\hbox{ if }s_{i}y<y,s_{i}x>x,\\
qh_{s_{i}y,x}+q^{2}h_{y,x}&\hbox{ if }s_{i}y>y,s_{i}x>x.\end{cases}

Proof.

Using
Proposition 2.2
with
s=s_{i}
we get

\displaystyle h_{s_{i+2}\ldots s_{n}y,s_{i}\ldots s_{n}x}

\displaystyle=h_{s_{i+2}\ldots s_{n}s_{i}y,s_{i+1}\ldots s_{n}x}+q^{c}h_{s_{i+2}\ldots s_{n}y,s_{i+1}\ldots s_{n}x}

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\\
s_{i+2}\ldots s_{n}y\leq w<s_{i+1}\ldots s_{n}x\\
s_{i}w<w\end{subarray}}\mu(w,s_{i+1}\ldots s_{n}x)h_{s_{i+2}\ldots s_{n}y,w}

\displaystyle=qh_{s_{i}y,x}+q^{c+1}h_{y,x}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\\
s_{i+2}\ldots s_{n}y\leq w<s_{i+1}\ldots s_{n}x\\
s_{i}w<w\end{subarray}}\mu(w,s_{i+1}\ldots s_{n}x)h_{s_{i+2}\ldots s_{n}y,w}

Where
c=\pm 1
depending on
s_{i}y>y
or
s_{i}y<y
.
The last sum runs over
w=s_{i+2}\ldots s_{n}v
for

with
s_{i}v<v
. Then

\displaystyle\mu(w,s_{i+1}\ldots s_{n}x)=[q]h_{s_{i+2}\ldots s_{n}v,s_{i+1}\ldots s_{n}x}=[q]qh_{v,x}=\begin{cases}0,&\hbox{ if }v\neq x,\\
1&\hbox{ if }v=x.\end{cases}

Hence,

\displaystyle h_{s_{i+2}\ldots s_{n}y,s_{i}\ldots s_{n}x}

\displaystyle=qh_{s_{i}y,x}+q^{c+1}h_{y,x}-\chi_{s_{i}x<x}h_{s_{i+2}\ldots s_{n}y,s_{i+2}\ldots s_{n}x}

\displaystyle=qh_{s_{i}y,x}+q^{c+1}h_{y,x}-\chi_{s_{i}x<x}h_{y,x}

If
s_{i}y<y
and
s_{i}x<x
then the final two terms cancel, and we are left with

, where the last equality is by
Proposition 2.4
. If
s_{i}y>y
and
s_{i}x<x
then

by

, so the first and third terms cancel out. So we get

(7.8)

h_{s_{i+2}\ldots s_{n}y,s_{i}\ldots s_{n}x}=\begin{cases}q^{2}h_{y,x}&\hbox{ if }s_{i}x<x,\\
qh_{s_{i}y,x}+h_{y,x}&\hbox{ if }s_{i}y<y,s_{i}x>x,\\
qh_{s_{i}y,x}+q^{2}h_{y,x}&\hbox{ if }s_{i}y>y,s_{i}x>x.\end{cases}

∎

Corollary 7.7
.

For

and

,

\mu(s_{i+2}\ldots s_{n}y,s_{i}\ldots s_{n}x)=\begin{cases}\delta_{s_{i}y,x}+\mu(y,x)&\hbox{ if }s_{i}y<y,s_{i}x>x,\\

Proof.

By taking the coefficient of

in

, we get

\displaystyle\mu(s_{i+2}\ldots s_{n}y,s_{i}\ldots s_{n}x)=\begin{cases}0&\hbox{ if }s_{i}x<x,\\

\delta_{s_{i}y,x}&\hbox{ if }s_{i}y>y,s_{i}x>x.\end{cases}

If
s_{i}x>x
and
s_{i}y=x
then
s_{i}x=y>x=s_{i}y
. So the last case will give

always.
∎

Proposition 7.8
.

For

,

\displaystyle(T_{s_{n}\ldots s_{3}}C_{w})|_{[n-1]}=\begin{cases}C_{w_{[n-1]}}&\hbox{ if }w^{[n-1]}=s_{3}\ldots s_{n},\\

Proof.

The first two and the last cases follow from previous calculations. So we need to calculate when

.
When

and

, then by

and

we get

By (

) this gives the third case above. So the only remaining case to check is the fourth case.
Now,

\displaystyle C_{s_{1}}C_{x}

\displaystyle=(T_{s_{1}}+q)C_{x}=(T_{s_{1}}+q)(\sum_{y\leq x}h_{y,x}T_{y})=\sum_{y\leq x}h_{y,x}T_{s_{1}}T_{y}+q\sum_{y\leq x}h_{y,x}T_{y}

8.
A geometric proof of positivity of restriction coefficients

For the sake of readability, we prove
A
for finite Weyl groups following the proof of
Theorem 4.3
in
[
GH07
]
. It is useful to work with a different normalization of the Hecke algebra here, namely

T_{s}^{2}=(q-1)T_{s}+q\qquad\hbox{ for }s\in S.

This is obtained from the earlier relation by replacing
T_{s}
by
q^{-\frac{1}{2}}T_{s}
for all
s\in S
. Here one needs to work with the coefficient ring
\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}]
and make corresponding changes to the definition, for example, the bar involution will take
q^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}\mapsto q^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}
.

We work inside the setting of perverse sheaves and mixed Hodge modules, and use
[
Ach21
]
as our main reference. All geometric spaces considered here are quasiprojective complex algebraic varieties, in addition to their Zariski topology these varieties are equipped with analytic topology. We will always consider sheaves with respect to the analytic topology. Any such variety is locally compact in the analytic topology, so there is no difficulty in using results from Chapter 1 of
[
Ach21
]
, for example using proper base change theorems.
For a quasiprojective algebraic variety
X
, let
D^{b}_{c}(X;\mathbb{Q})
denote the derived category of bounded
\mathbb{Q}
-complexes with constructible cohomologies. If
X
has an action by an algebraic group
B
then we can also consider the equivariant version
D^{b}_{B}(X;\mathbb{Q})
. A map
f:X\to Y
of varieties induces maps on the derived categories: the derived pullback
f^{*}:D^{b}_{c}(Y)\to D^{b}_{c}(X)
and the derived proper pushforward
f_{!}:D^{b}_{c}(X)\to D^{b}_{c}(Y)
. There is a
t
-structure on
D^{b}_{c}(X;\mathbb{Q})
whose heart is the category
\mathrm{Perv}(X;\mathbb{Q})
of perverse sheaves. If
Y\subset X
is a smooth, connected, locally closed subvariety, and
\mathcal{L}
a local system of finite type on
Y
, then there is a intersection cohomology complex
\mathrm{IC}(Y,\mathcal{L})\in\mathrm{Perv}(X;\mathbb{Q})
. If
\mathcal{L}
is irreducible then
\mathrm{IC}(Y,\mathcal{L})
is a simple object in
\mathrm{Perv}(X;\mathbb{Q})
, and any simple object in
\mathrm{Perv}(X;\mathbb{Q})
is an intersection cohomology complex (
[
Ach21
, Theorem 3.4.5]
). An object of
D^{b}_{c}(X;\mathbb{Q})
is semisimple if it is isomorphic to a finite direct sum of shifts of simple perverse sheaves.

We fix necessary notations and recall the categorification theorem
[
Ach21
, Theorem 7.3.8]
which connects the Hecke algebra with geometry of flag varieties. Let
G
be a connected reductive complex algebraic group and choose a Borel subgroup
B
and a maximal torus
T\subset B
,
W
be the corresponding Weyl group. The flag variety
\mathcal{F}=G/B
is a projective variety. For each
w\in W
, the open Schubert cells are
Y_{w}=BwB/B
, and these form a good stratification of
\mathcal{F}
. There are natural inclusion maps

i_{w}:Y_{w}\to\mathcal{F}

for
w\in W
. The intersection cohomology complexes
\mathrm{IC}_{w}=\mathrm{IC}(Y_{w};\mathbb{Q})\in D^{b}_{c}(\mathcal{F};\mathbb{Q})
are
B
-equivariant. There is a convolution product that makes both the categories
\mathrm{Semis}_{B}(\mathcal{F};\mathbb{Q})\subset D^{b}_{B}(\mathcal{F};Q)
monoidal, where
\mathrm{Semis}_{B}(\mathcal{F};\mathbb{Q})
denotes the additive category of semisimple objects in
D^{b}_{B}(\mathcal{F};Q)
. The categorification theorem says that the split Grothendieck group
\mathcal{K}
of
\mathrm{Semis}_{B}(\mathcal{F};\mathbb{Q})
is isomorphic as rings with the Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}
of
W
, where elements
[\mathrm{IC}_{w}]
are mapped to the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis elements
C_{w}
. Under this isomorphism, the standard basis elements
T_{w}
correspond to
[i_{w!}\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_{Y_{w}}]
, the classes of the derived proper pushforwards of the derived versions of constant sheaves. Thus, to show an element of the Hecke algebra is
C
-positive is equivalent to show that the corresponding element on the side of
\mathcal{K}
is arising from a semisimple element.

Now we turn to our main goal in this section to show that for
w\in W
and
u\in W^{J}
, the element
(T_{u^{-1}}C_{w})|_{J}\in\mathcal{H}_{J}
is
C
-positive in the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis of
\mathcal{H}_{J}
. By (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) it is equivalent to showing that for
w\in W
and
u\in\!{}^{J}W
,
(C_{w}T_{u^{-1}})|_{J}\in\mathcal{H}_{J}
is
C
-positive. We choose to prove the later, since its more convenient to deal with left cosets than right cosets. We achieve our goal in two steps.
In the first step, we lift the map of multiplying by
T_{u^{-1}}
and restricting to
\mathcal{H}_{J}
at the level of functors between derived categories
D^{b}_{c}(\mathcal{F})\to D^{b}_{c}(\mathcal{F}_{L})
, where
\mathcal{F}_{L}=L/B_{L}
, and
B_{L}=B\cap L
is the Borel restricted to the Levi subgroup
L
of
G
corresponding to the subset
J\subset S
. Then in the second step, we show that this lifted map preserves semisimplicity.

For
J\subseteq S
, let
P
and
L
be the parabolic and Levi subgroups of
G
corresponding to
J
respectively. Then

P=LB=\underset{w\in W_{J}}{\bigcup}BwB.

There are natural inclusion maps

i_{w}:Y_{w}\xhookrightarrow{}\mathcal{F}\qquad\hbox{ and }\qquad i_{v}^{J}:Y^{J}_{v}\xhookrightarrow{}\mathcal{F}_{L},

where
v\in W_{J}
and
Y^{J}_{v}=B_{L}vB_{L}/B_{L}
.
Now fix
x\in\!{}^{J}W
. We suppress the dependence on the element
x
in the notation of the maps given below.
There is an isomorphism of varieties

\varphi:\mathcal{F}_{L}\to LxB/B\quad\hbox{ given by }\quad gB_{L}\mapsto gxB.

The well-definedness of
\varphi
comes from the fact that
\mathrm{stab}(xB)\cap L=B_{L}
, since for
b_{L},b^{\prime}_{L}\in L
,

\displaystyle b_{L}B_{L}=b^{\prime}_{L}B_{L}\iff{b^{\prime}_{L}}^{-1}b_{L}\in B_{L}\iff{b^{\prime}_{L}}^{-1}b_{L}xB=xB\iff b_{L}xB=b^{\prime}_{L}xB.

which proves injectivity as well as well-defined. The map
L\to Lx
given by
g\mapsto gx
is surjective, so
\varphi
is surjective as well.

Since
P=LB
we get a map

\pi:PxB/B\to LxB/B\quad\hbox{ given by }\quad(g_{L}\cdot b)xB\mapsto g_{L}xB.

It is well-defined since
\mathrm{stab}(xB)\cap P\subseteq B
.

Finally, there is inclusion

j:PxB/B\xhookrightarrow{}G/B=\mathcal{F}.

The next proposition realizes the map
h\mapsto(hT_{x^{-1}})|_{J}
from
\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}_{J}
as a functor.

Suppose

Proposition 8.2
.

For
x\in\!{}^{J}W
and notations as above the functor
\varphi^{*}\pi_{!}j^{*}:D^{b}_{c}(\mathcal{F})\to D^{b}_{c}(\mathcal{F}_{L})

Proof.

From

This gives a functor of derived categories

where the last equivalence is Beilinson’s theorem

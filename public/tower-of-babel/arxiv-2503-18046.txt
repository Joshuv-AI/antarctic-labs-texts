Non-(strong, geometrically) ergodicity criteria for discrete time Markov chains on general state spaces
Ling-Di Wang, Yu Chen, Yu-Hui Zhang

Abstract
For discrete-time Markov chains on general state spaces, we establish criteria for non-ergodicity and non-strong ergodicity, and derive sufficient conditions for non-geometric ergodicity via the theory of minimal nonnegative solutions. Our criteria are formulated based on the existence of solutions to inequalities involving the chain’s one-step transition kernel. Meanwhile, these practical criteria are applied to a type of examples, which can effectively characterize the non-ergodicity and non-strong ergodicity of a specific class of single birth (death) processes.

2.2
Lower bounds for moments of hitting times and sufficiency

In this section, we prove the sufficiency of Theorem
1.1
, Theorem
1.4
, and Proposition
1.6
using the minimal solution theory and Dynkin’s formula.

For any set
A\in\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{X})
, define the first return time
\tau_{A}^{+}
and first hitting time
\tau_{A}
on
A
as follows:

\tau_{A}^{+}:=\inf\{n\geq 1:X_{n}\in A\},\qquad\tau_{A}:=\inf\{n\geq 0:X_{n}\in A\}.

Denote

L(x,A)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{A}^{+}=n)=\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{A}^{+}<\infty).

Before proceeding further, let us briefly outline the main ideas of our proofs.

Taking non-strong ergodicity as an example, it has an alternative characterization (
[
10
, Theorem 16.0.2]
): for an aperiodic chain
\{X_{n}\}
, it is strongly ergodic if and only if there exists a petite set
C\in\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{X})
such that
\sup_{x\in\mathscr{X}}\mathbb{E}_{x}\tau_{C}^{+}<\infty
, and for every set
A\in\mathscr{B}^{+}(\mathscr{X})
, we have
\sup_{x\in\mathscr{X}}\mathbb{E}_{x}\tau_{A}^{+}<\infty
. To verify that a chain is non-strongly ergodic, it suffices to show that
\sup_{x\in\mathscr{X}}\mathbb{E}_{x}\tau_{A}^{+}=\infty
for some set
A\in\mathscr{B}^{+}(\mathscr{X})
. We first derive a lower bound for the expectation of the return time using the minimal solution theory (see Proposition
2.9
); an increasing sequence of such lower bounds then implies the desired result. On the other hand, a finite approximation method (see Proposition
2.11
and Lemmas
2.14
,
2.15
) guarantees the existence of an increasing sequence of lower bounds, thus establishing the necessity of our conditions.

Additionally, by incorporating appropriate Lyapunov-like conditions, one can derive lower bounds for the hitting times of the Markov chain using Dynkin’s formula. This allows us to obtain Lyapunov-like criteria for non-strong ergodicity.

The following assertion, which relaxes the nonnegativity condition, is a modified version of
[
10

Proposition 2.7

A
\psi
-irreducible Markov chain

Proof
The necessity follows directly from
[

[

, it suffices to show that there exist sets

and

such that

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{C}^{+}<\infty)<1\quad\text{for all }x\in D.

Without loss of generality, assume

is nonnegative (otherwise, replace
V(x)
by
V(x)-\inf_{x\in\mathscr{X}}V(x)
). For the set
C\in\mathscr{B}^{+}(\mathscr{X})
, let
a=\inf_{x\in C}V(x)
and define
D=\{x:V(x)<a\}
. By (
2.3
), we have
a>0
,

, and
D\subseteq C^{c}
. For

,

\displaystyle a>V(x)

\displaystyle\geq PV(x)=\int_{C}V(y)P(x,\mathrm{d}y)+\int_{C^{c}}V(y)P(x,\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle\geq aP(x,C)+\int_{C^{c}}PV(y)P(x,\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle\geq aP(x,C)+a\int_{C^{c}}P(y,C)P(x,\mathrm{d}y)+\int_{C^{c}}\int_{C^{c}}V(z_{1})P(y,\mathrm{d}z_{1})P(x,\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle=a\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{C}^{+}=1)+a\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{C}^{+}=2)+\int_{C^{c}}\int_{C^{c}}V(z_{1})P(y,\mathrm{d}z_{1})P(x,\mathrm{d}y).

\displaystyle a>V(x)>a\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{C}^{+}=k)+\int_{C^{c}}\cdots\int_{C^{c}}V(z_{n-1})P(z_{n-2},\mathrm{d}z_{n-1})\cdots P(x,\mathrm{d}y).

Letting

Proposition 2.8

For each
A\in\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{X})

Proposition 2.11

Assume that the Markov chain
\{X_{n}\}
with Markov semigroup
\{P(x,\cdot)\}
on a locally compact separable metric space
(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{X}))
is
\psi
-irreducible, aperiodic, Feller, and
\mathrm{supp}\,\psi
has nonempty interior. Let
E\subseteq\mathscr{X}
be a compact set with
0<\psi(E)<\infty
. For any
A\in\mathscr{B}^{+}(\mathscr{X})
with

being a compact set, define
\{\hat{P}(x,\cdot)\}
on

as follows:

\hat{P}(x,B)=\bigg[P(x,B)+P(x,E^{c})\frac{\psi(B\cap A)}{\psi(A)}\bigg]\mathbf{1}_{E\setminus A}(x)+\frac{\psi(B)}{\psi(E)}\mathbf{1}_{A}(x)

(2.13)

for all
B\in\mathscr{B}(E)
. Then
\{\hat{P}(x,\cdot):x\in E\}
is a transition probability, and the Markov chain generated by
\{\hat{P}(x,\cdot):x\in E\}
is
\psi
-irreducible, aperiodic, and strongly ergodic.

Proof
It is easy to verify that
\hat{P}(x,E)=1
and
\{\hat{P}(x,B):x\in E,B\in\mathscr{B}(E)\}
is a transition probability on
(E,\mathscr{B}(E))
. Let
\{\hat{X}_{n}\}
denote the Markov chain on the state space
(E,\mathscr{B}(E))
with one-step transition probability
\{\hat{P}(x,B):x\in E,B\in\mathscr{B}(E)\}
. For
n\geq 2
and
B\in\mathscr{B}(E)
, let
\hat{P}_{n}(x,B)=\mathbb{P}_{x}(\hat{X}_{n}\in B)
, with
\hat{P}_{1}=\hat{P}
.

We first prove that
\{\hat{X}_{n}\}
is
\psi
-irreducible, i.e., for all
x\in E
and
B\in\mathscr{B}(E)
with
\psi(B)>0
, there exists
n\geq 1
such that
\hat{P}_{n}(x,B)>0
(by
[
10
, Proposition 4.2.1]
).

1) For all
x\in A
and
B\in\mathscr{B}(E)
with
\psi(B)>0
, it is obvious that
\hat{P}(x,B)>0
.

2) We prove that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\hat{P}_{n}(x,A)>0
for all

(equivalently, for all
x\in E\cap A^{c}
, there exists
n\geq 1
such that
\hat{P}_{n}(x,A)>0

\hat{P}_{n}(x_{1},A)=0
for all

. We derive a contradiction as follows:

By (

),

, which implies

and

. Moreover,
P(x_{1},A^{c}\cap E)=1
and
\hat{P}(x_{1},A^{c}\cap E)=1
, with

\hat{P}(x_{1},A^{c}\cap E\cap C)=P(x_{1},A^{c}\cap E\cap C)\quad\text{for all }C\in\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{X}).

By the Chapman-Kolmogorov equation, for all
C\in\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{X})
,

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{2}(x_{1},A^{c}\cap E\cap C)

\displaystyle=\int_{E\cap A^{c}}\hat{P}(x_{1},\mathrm{d}y)\hat{P}(y,A^{c}\cap E\cap C)\quad(\text{since }\hat{P}(x_{1},A^{c}\cap E)=1)

\displaystyle=\int_{E\cap A^{c}}P(x_{1},\mathrm{d}y)P(y,A^{c}\cap E\cap C)\quad(\text{by }\eqref{constr})

(2.14)

and

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{k+1}(x_{1},A^{c}\cap E\cap C)

\displaystyle=\int_{E}\hat{P}_{k}(x_{1},\mathrm{d}y)\hat{P}(y,A^{c}\cap E\cap C)

\displaystyle=\int_{E\cap A^{c}}\hat{P}_{k}(x_{1},\mathrm{d}y)\hat{P}(y,A^{c}\cap E\cap C)\quad(\text{since }\hat{P}_{k}(x_{1},A)=0)

\displaystyle=\int_{E\cap A^{c}}P_{k}(x_{1},\mathrm{d}y)P(y,A^{c}\cap E\cap C)\quad(\text{by }\eqref{constr}\text{ and }\eqref{induc1})

for all

. This implies

, contradicting the irreducibility of
\{X_{n}\}
.

3) We prove that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\hat{P}_{n}(x,B)>0
for all

and

with
\psi(B)>0
. By part 2), there exists

such that

. Moreover,

\hat{P}_{n+1}(x,B)\geq\int_{A}\hat{P}_{n}(x,\mathrm{d}y)\hat{P}(y,B)=\frac{\psi(B)}{\psi(E)}\hat{P}_{n}(x,A)>0.

Thus,
\{\hat{X}_{n}\}
is
\psi
-irreducible.

is compact,
\{\hat{X}_{n}\}
is weakly Feller by definition. By
[

, Theorems 6.2.9, 16.2.5]
, it suffices to prove aperiodicity. Let

be a measure. For all

and

,

, so

is
\hat{\nu}_{1}
-small by definition (see
[
10
, Section 5.2]
). Since
\hat{\nu}_{1}(A)>0
,
\{\hat{X}_{n}\}

[
10

3

In this section, we investigate a type of examples to illustrate our criteria.

3.1
Example 1

Our criteria are especially effective for models like the following one.

Let
1\geq b(x)>0
and
1\geq\gamma(x)>0
be two continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
b(x)+\gamma(x)\leq 1
. Let
\beta(x)
be a density function with support

It is obvious that
\{X_{n}\}
is aperiodic and irreducible with respect to Lebesgue measure

By
[

\mathcal{X}
are petite.

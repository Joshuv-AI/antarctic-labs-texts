Band-like Exact Zero-energy Andreev Bound States and Superconducting Diode Effect in Mixed s+p -wave Josephson Junctions
Shu-Tong Guan, Jin An

Abstract
Topological Josephson junctions enable nonreciprocal transport involving Majorana fermions (MFs). Here we examine a topological Josephson junction with mixed s s + p p -wave pairing, where topological phase transition can be driven by adjusting the ratio between the pairing components. There exist two exact symmetrically positioned zero-energy level crossings for the Andreev-bound states, which can be shifted by external fields, and can be destroyed or recreated in pairs by a time-reversal breaking Zeeman field or inhomogeneities, exhibiting band-like structure. The dependence of the shift on the Zeeman field is linear when the two p p -wave 𝒅 \bm{d} -vectors on both sides are identical while quadratic when they are distinct. Near the topological phase transition, the topological p p -wave dominant junctions host MF-induced pronounced superconducting diode effect with high efficiency factor Q Q up to 30%, in contrast to the trivial s s -wave dominant junctions possessing relatively small Q Q .

APPENDIX A
JOSEPHSON CURRENT CALCULATION BY MATSUBARA GREEN’S FUNCTION

Here in this section we shall provide a method of calculating the Josephson current by making use of Matsubara Green’s function. Physically, the charge current flowing in either superconductor of a Josephson junction consists of both supercurrent and quasi-particle current. How to calculate them separately and accurately is a rather technical problem, especially for superconductors with spin-orbit coupling or mixed pairing parity. However, the analysis can be greatly simplified by focusing on the normal link if only the total current is concerned, since, according to current conservation, the total charge current through the normal region equals that through the superconducting regions. The hybrid lattice system we consider is an infinite one, where, apart from the normal link with finite length
L
, both superconductors on the two sides of the junction are assumed to be half-infinite, as schematically shown in Fig.
5
.

\varphi=\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}

\bm{T}_{S}

\bm{T}_{S}^{\prime}

\bm{T}_{j}

j+1

\displaystyle=\frac{e}{\hbar}\text{Im}\left[\langle\psi^{\dagger}(j+1)\tau_{z}\bm{T}_{j}\psi(j)\rangle\right],

where

\displaystyle\bm{T}_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}{T}_{j}&\\

(14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{j_{1},\,j_{2}}(\bm{\tau})

\displaystyle=-\langle\mathcal{T}_{\bm{\tau}}\psi_{j_{1}}(\bm{\tau})\psi_{j_{2}}^{\dagger}(0)\rangle

\displaystyle I=

(17)

\displaystyle-\frac{ie}{2\hbar\beta}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}\text{Tr}\left\{\tau_{z}\left[\bm{T}_{j}\mathcal{G}_{j,\,j+1}(i\omega_{n})-\bm{T}_{j}^{\dagger}\mathcal{G}_{j+1,\,j}(i\omega_{n})\right]\right\}.

\displaystyle g_{L}^{-1}=i\omega_{n}\mathbf{1}_{4\times 4}-(H_{\text{BdG}})_{-1,-1}-\bm{T}_{S}g_{L}\bm{T}_{S}^{\dagger},

\displaystyle g_{R}^{-1}=i\omega_{n}\mathbf{1}_{4\times 4}-(H_{\text{BdG}})_{L,L}-\bm{T}_{S}^{\prime\dagger}g_{R}\bm{T}_{S}^{\prime},

and
\mathcal{G}^{R}_{j,\,j}

(j+1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{j,\,j}^{L}=\left(i\omega_{n}\mathbf{1}_{4\times 4}-(H_{\text{BdG}})_{jj}-\bm{T}_{j-1}\mathcal{G}_{j-1,\,j-1}^{L}\bm{T}_{j-1}^{\dagger}\right)^{-1},

(19)

and in the
(L-j)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{j,\,j}^{R}=\left(i\omega_{n}\mathbf{1}_{4\times 4}-(H_{\text{BdG}})_{jj}-\bm{T}_{j}^{\dagger}\mathcal{G}_{j+1,\,j+1}^{R}\bm{T}_{j}\right)^{-1}.

(20)

Now by taking the self-energy corrections from both sides into account, we have the diagonal total Green’s function

at site

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{j,\,j}(i\omega_{n})=

\displaystyle\left(i\omega_{n}\mathbf{1}_{4\times 4}-(H_{\text{BdG}})_{jj}\right.

(21)

\displaystyle\left.-\bm{T}_{j-1}\mathcal{G}_{j-1,\,j-1}^{L}\bm{T}_{j-1}^{\dagger}-\bm{T}_{j}^{\dagger}\mathcal{G}_{j+1,\,j+1}^{R}\bm{T}_{j}\right)^{-1}.

Finally the needed off-diagonal total Green’s functions

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{j,\,j+1}(i\omega_{n})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{G}_{j,\,j}^{L}\bm{T}_{j}^{\dagger}\mathcal{G}_{j+1,\,j+1}=\mathcal{G}_{j,\,j}\bm{T}_{j}^{\dagger}\mathcal{G}_{j+1,\,j+1}^{R},

(22)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{j+1,\,j}(i\omega_{n})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{G}_{j+1,\,j+1}\bm{T}_{j}\mathcal{G}_{j,\,j}^{L}=\mathcal{G}_{j+1,\,j+1}^{R}\bm{T}_{j}\mathcal{G}_{j,\,j}.

APPENDIX B
JOSEPHSON CURRENT CALCULATION BY DIRECT HAMILTONIAN MATRIX DIAGONALIZATION

Another method of Josephson current calculation can be made if instead of considering half-infinite superconductors, the two superconductors at both sides of the normal link are treated as having finite size. Actually this method can be achieved by directly diagonalizing the whole model Hamiltonian
H_{\text{BdG}}
of the hybrid junction system. In the lattice space,
H_{\text{BdG}}
can be given by

\displaystyle(H_{\text{BdG}})_{ij}=\begin{pmatrix}t_{ij}-(\bm{h}_{i}\cdot\bm{\sigma}+\mu)\delta_{ij}&\mathbf{\Delta}_{ij}\\
(\mathbf{\Delta}_{ji})^{\dagger}&-t_{ij}^{*}+(\bm{h}_{i}\cdot\bm{\sigma}+\mu)\delta_{ij}\end{pmatrix}

(23)

where
\mu
is the chemical potential, being identical for the whole junction system, and
t_{ij}
is the hopping matrix from site
j
to
i
. A possible Zeeman field
\bm{h}_{i}
is also introduced, which can be different from site to site. Here we consider a general situation of pairing potential
\mathbf{\Delta}_{ij}
between site
i
and
j
, regardless of its pairing symmetry being singlet, triplet or their mixture. The real-space
\mathbf{\Delta}_{ij}
is assumed to vary as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbf{\Delta}_{ij}=\begin{cases}\Delta_{ij}e^{i\varphi_{1}},\;\;\;\;&-L_{S}\leq i,j<0,\\
\Delta_{ij}e^{i\varphi_{2}},\;\;\;\;\;\;\;&L\leq i,j<L+L_{S},\\
0,\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(24)

where
\varphi_{1}
,
\varphi_{2}
are the pairing phases for the superconductors, with
\varphi=\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}
the superconducting phase difference. So both superconductors contain
L_{S}
sites, and the whole open system has
N=2L_{S}+L
lattice sites. As before, the total Josephson charge current can be computed via a cross section between site
j
and
j+1
in the normal link:

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=I_{j+1\leftarrow j}=\frac{2e}{\hbar}\text{Im}\{\langle c^{\dagger}(j+1)T_{j}c(j)\rangle\}

(25)

\displaystyle=\frac{2e}{\hbar}\sum_{\alpha,\,\beta={\uparrow,\downarrow}}\text{Im}\{(T_{j})_{\alpha\beta}\langle c_{\alpha}^{\dagger}(j+1)c_{\beta}(j)\rangle\},

By diagonalizing the
2N\times 2N
real-space BdG Hamiltonian for the whole junction system, one has the eigenvalue equation:
H_{\text{BdG}}u_{n}=E_{n}u_{n}
, with
u_{n}=[u_{n\uparrow}(1),u_{n\downarrow}(1),\cdots,u_{n\uparrow}(2N),u_{n\downarrow}(2N)]^{T}
and
1\leq n\leq 2N
. Then the Bogoliubov quasi-particle excitation operators
{\gamma}_{n}
can be introduced as below by an expansion of electron annihilation operator via a unitary transformation:

c_{\sigma}(j)=\sum_{n}^{2N}{u}_{n\sigma}(j){\gamma}_{n},

(26)

where
{\gamma}_{n}
satisfy the anti-commutation relations:
\{{\gamma}_{n},{\gamma}_{m}^{\dagger}\}=\delta_{mn}
, with
1\leq n,m\leq 2N
. Therefore, we have
\left\langle{\gamma}_{n}^{\dagger}{\gamma}_{m}\right\rangle=f(E_{n})\delta_{nm}
, and
\left\langle{\gamma}_{m}{\gamma}_{n}^{\dagger}\right\rangle=f(-E_{n})\delta_{nm}
with
f(E)=1/(1+e^{\beta E})
the Fermi distribution function. But it is worth mentioning that here not all
{\gamma}_{n}
are annihilation operators, nor are they independent of each other. Since, due to the intrinsic particle-hole symmetry of the BdG Hamiltonian, each eigenvalue
E_{n}
must be accompanied by another eigenvalue
-E_{n}
, we have additionally
E_{n}=-E_{n-N}
and
\gamma_{n}=\gamma_{n-N}^{\dagger}
when
N<n\leq 2N
.
The thermodynamic average can then be recognized to be

\displaystyle\langle c_{\alpha}^{\dagger}(j+1)c_{\beta}(j)\rangle=\sum_{n,m=1}^{2N}{u}_{n\alpha}^{*}(j+1){u}_{m\beta}(j)\left\langle{\gamma}_{n}^{\dagger}{\gamma}_{m}\right\rangle

(27)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{2N}{u}_{n\alpha}^{*}(j+1){u}_{n\beta}(j)f(E_{n})

\displaystyle=-\langle c_{\beta}(j)c_{\alpha}^{\dagger}(j+1)\rangle=-\sum_{n=1}^{2N}{u}_{n\alpha}^{*}(j+1){u}_{n\beta}(j)f(-E_{n})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{2N}{u}_{n\alpha}^{*}(j+1){u}_{n\beta}(j)\tanh(\beta E_{n}/2).

Finally the Josephson current can be given by

\displaystyle I=

(28)

\displaystyle-\frac{e}{\hbar}\sum_{n=1}^{2N}\sum_{\alpha,\,\beta}\text{Im}\{(T_{j})_{\alpha\beta}{u}_{n\alpha}^{*}(j+1){u}_{n\beta}(j)\}\tanh(\beta E_{n}/2).

Compared to the Matsubara Green’s function method for an infinite hybrid system in Appendix
A
, this method of current calculation for a finite-size hybrid system is much simpler. What’s important is that it can handle the situation at the exact zero temperature. When the superconductor is topological, more attention should be paid since at each open end of the superconductors, one or more Majorana fermions will be introduced, which may cause non-negligible effect if the size is chosen too small. In actual numerical computation, as long as
L_{S}
of the superconductors is sufficiently large, numerical accuracy can be controlled within a reasonable range. But the cost is that the consuming time for this method would be generally longer than that for the Green’s function method. On the other hand, as a function of the superconducting phase difference
\varphi
, eigenvalues
E_{n}(\varphi)
obtained here give directly the energy spectrum of the junction, and in particular, give the Andreev-bound-state (ABS) energy spectrum, as has been used in the main text.

\displaystyle 4\text{det}\begin{pmatrix}i\sin{\Theta}\sin{k_{F}^{+}L}&-e^{i(\tilde{\varphi}+k_{F}(L-1))}\cos{\Theta}\cos{\frac{\varphi+\Delta k_{F}L}{2}}\\

where

level crossings exist, they must come in pairs, and obey the following exact equation:

\displaystyle\cos{\varphi}=-\cos{\Delta k_{F}L}+\tan^{2}{\Theta}(\cos{2k_{F}L}-\cos{\Delta k_{F}L}),

(57)

where
\varphi=\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}
. In the absence of the Zeeman field,
\Delta k_{F}=0
,
\Theta=0
, one has a doubly degenerate
E=0

\varphi_{0}=\pi
. In the presence of a Zeeman field within the
xy

\pi
, as long as

is not too large, which is shown in Fig.

h
is sufficiently large, the above equation can not ensure a real solution, indicating the absence of the ZEABSs, as shown in Fig.
9
(e), (f) and (h). However, the right side of the above equation exhibits an oscillating behavior as a function of
h
, resulting in the region of the ZEABSs occurring alternatively, no matter how large
h
is. Because the equation is exact, this band-like structure of the ZEABSs is also exact. As long as
h
is sufficiently small, the existence of the ZEABSs is always guaranteed. Now we consider in detail the limiting case of

, where one can adopt the approximation:
\tan{\Theta}\approx\sin{\Theta}\approx\Theta
,
\cos\Delta k_{F}L\approx 1-(\Delta k_{F}L)^{2}/2,\,\cos{\varphi}\approx-1+(\Delta\varphi)^{2}/2

\pm\varphi_{0}
, with
\varphi_{0}=\pi+\Delta\varphi
and the phase shift
\Delta\varphi

\displaystyle(\Delta\varphi)^{2}

\displaystyle=(\Delta k_{F}L)^{2}-4\sin^{2}{\Theta}\sin^{2}{k_{F}L},

(58)

where

\displaystyle\sin{\Theta}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\tan{(k_{F}^{+}/2)}-\tan{(k_{F}^{-}/2)}}{\tan{(k_{F}^{+}/2)}+\tan{(k_{F}^{-}/2)}}\approx\frac{\Delta k_{F}}{4\tan{\frac{k_{F}}{2}}\cos^{2}{\frac{k_{F}}{2}}}

(59)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta k_{F}}{2\sin k_{F}}\approx\frac{h}{2\sin^{2}{k_{F}}}.

Since
\Delta k_{F}\approx 2h/\hbar v_{F}=h/\sin{k_{F}}
, the phase shift
\Delta\varphi
of the ZEABSs is found to be

\displaystyle\Delta\varphi

\displaystyle=\frac{h}{\sin^{2}{k_{F}}}\sqrt{(L\sin k_{F})^{2}-\sin^{2}{k_{F}L}}.

(60)

The expression is meaningful since the quantity in the square root is positive-definite when

. This indicates that the shift of the superconducting phase difference
\Delta\varphi
is proportional to the magnitude of the Zeeman field and is also sensitive to the length of the normal link.

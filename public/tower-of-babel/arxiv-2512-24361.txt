Permutations with only reduced co-BPDs
Joshua Arroyo, Adam Gregory

Abstract
. Bumpless pipe dreams (BPDs) are combinatorial objects used in the study of Schubert and Grothendieck polynomials. Weigandt recently introduced a co-BPD object associated to each BPD and used them to give an analogue to the change of bases formulas of Lenart and Lascoux between these polynomials. She posed the problem of characterizing the set of permutations whose BPDs have only reduced co-BPDs. We give a pattern-avoidance characterization for these permutations using a set of seven patterns.

As an application of Theorem

This means that if we can state anything about the permutations of the co-BPDs of such a

The following two corollaries give an example of such a result.

Corollary 6.1
.

If

is a non-reduced BPD, then co(

) traces out a permutation that contains at least one of pattern in
\Pi^{r}
, which is the reverse of each permutation in
\Pi
.

Proof.

Note that for any co-BPD, co(

), flipping co(
B
) vertically gives a BPD, f(co(
B
)), whose permutation is the reverse of co(
B
)’s permutation.
If
B
is non-reduced, then f(co(
B
)) must have the configuration meaning f(co(
B
)) contains a pattern in
\Pi
, thus co(
B
) contains a pattern in
\Pi^{r}
.
∎

We can say a bit more in the case when, in addition to avoiding the patterns in
\Pi
,
w
also
contains
a 2143 pattern. Note that Lemma 7.2 in
[
9
]
gives that
\text{bpd}(w)=\text{BPD}(w)
whenever
w
avoids the 2143 pattern, i.e., when
w
is a
vexillary
permutation. The converse of this statement is true as well, which we now demonstrate via contrapositive.

Lemma 6.2
.

If
w
contains a 2143 pattern then
\text{bpd}(w)\subsetneq\text{BPD}(w)
, that is, there exist non-reduced bumpless pipe dreams for
w
.

Proof.

Suppose
w
contains a 2143 pattern. There could be multiple instances of this pattern, but among all such occurrences, fix
i<j<k<\ell
to be the one where
i
and
j
are as large as possible. Note that the pipes exit in the order
j\;i\;\ell\;k
. Consider the Rothe BPD
\widehat{B}
for
w
. The inversion
(k,\ell)
in
w
means that on
\widehat{B}
there will be a
tile strictly southeast of the (unique)
tiles of both
i
and
j
in
\widehat{B}
. We wish to droop
i
into this
tile, but there may be blocking pipes
j^{\prime}
with
j<j^{\prime}<k
that exit between
i
and
\ell
, obstructing this droop. If there are such pipes, droop them into the
tile in order from largest to smallest, moving the
tile strictly northwest until we arrive at a BPD
B^{\prime}\in\text{bpd}(w)
that has a
tile still strictly southeast of the
tile in
i
and
j
and no longer has such blocking pipes. We may now droop
i
into this
tile to get a BPD
B^{\prime\prime}\in\text{bpd}(w)
. The
tile of
B^{\prime}
that
i
drooped into is now a
tile in
B^{\prime\prime}
that has a
tile of
i
above it in the same column. This
tile of
i
is strictly southeast of the
tile of
j
. We wish to
K
-droop
j
into this
tile of
i
. In order to be a blocking pipe it would have to be some
j^{\prime}
with
j<j^{\prime}<k
that exits between
j
and
i
, but then
j^{\prime}\;i\;\ell\;k
forms a
2143
pattern, contradicting
j
chosen to be maximal.
So there are no obstructions blocking
j
from making a
K
-droop move into this tile, resulting in a (non-reduced)
B\in\text{BPD}(w)\setminus\text{bpd}(w)
as desired.
∎

We can use the previous two results to achieve the following corollary.

Corollary 6.3
.

If
w
contains a 2143 pattern and avoids each pattern in
\Pi
, then there exists
v
containing a pattern in
\Pi^{r}
such that
a_{w,v}>0
where

\mathfrak{G}_{w}=\sum_{v}(-1)^{\ell(v)-\ell(w)}a_{w,v}\mathfrak{S}_{v}.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.1
we have that for any
B\in\text{BPD}(w)
, co
(B)
is reduced. Therefore, by Theorem 1.1 in
[
10
]
(cf. Theorem
2.8
above), we have that if the permutation associated with co
(B)
is
v
then
a_{w,v}
is nonzero.
By Lemma
6.2
, we know
w
has some non-reduced BPD, say
B
.
By Corollary
6.1
we have that co
(B)
whose associated permutation,
v
, contains a pattern in
\Pi^{r}
meaning
a_{w,v}
is nonzero.
∎

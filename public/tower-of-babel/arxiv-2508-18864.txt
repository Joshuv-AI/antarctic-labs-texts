Calogero–Sutherland hyperbolic system and Heckman–Opdam 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n \mathfrak{gl}_{n} hypergeometric function


Abstract
We prove equivalence of two integral representations for the wave functions of hyperbolic Calogero–Sutherland system. For this we study two families of Baxter operators related to hyperbolic Calogero–Sutherland and rational Ruijsenaars models; the first one as a limit from hyperbolic Ruijsenaars system, while the second one independently. Besides, computing asymptotics of integral representations and also the value at zero point, we identify them with renormalized Heckman–Opdam 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n \mathfrak{gl}_{n} hypergeometric function.

1
Introduction

Recently, significant progress has been made in the study of the Ruijsenaars quantum hyperbolic model introduced in
[
R1
]
. In
[
R2
]
S. Ruijsenaars found the so called “kernel function” playing the role of reproducing kernel for the model. By use of the kernel function in
[
HR1
]
M. Hallnäs and S. Ruijsenaars constructed integral representation for wave functions of the model. Besides, in a series of papers
[
BDKK1
,
BDKK2
,
BDKK3
,
BDKK4
]
the technique of integral Baxter operators for the Ruijsenaars model was developed, which helped to establish bispectral and coupling constant dualities together with the orthogonality and completeness relations.

It is natural to expect that similar statements take place for nonrelativistic limit of Ruijsenaars hyperbolic model, the hyperbolic Calogero–Sutherland model, governed by the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{j}}^{2}+\sum_{1\leq j\not=k\leq n}\frac{\pi^{2}g(g-1)}{\sh^{2}\pi(x_{j}-x_{k})}.

(1.1)

Moreover, almost all ingredients for that are known. The following Euler type integral representation of wave functions was found by M. Hallnäs and S. Ruijsenaars in
[
HR2
]

\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{(2\pi\Gamma(g))^{k}}{k!}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n(n-1)/2}}\exp\biggl(2\pi\imath\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\biggl(\sum_{i=1}^{k}y_{ki}-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}y_{k-1,i}\biggr)\biggr)\\[6.0pt]
\times\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\,[4\sh^{2}\pi(y_{ki}-y_{kj})]^{g}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k+1}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\,[2\ch\pi(y_{k+1,i}-y_{kj})]^{g}}\,\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\prod_{i=1}^{k}dy_{ki},

(1.2)

where we denote
\bm{x}_{n}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
and identify
y_{ni}\equiv x_{i}
. The related Baxter operators were studied by M. Hallnäs
[
H
]
. The different expression by means of Mellin–Barnes integrals

\hat{\Psi}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{(2\pi\Gamma(g))^{-k}}{k!}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n(n-1)/2}}\exp\biggl(2\pi\imath\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}\biggl(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{ki}-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\gamma_{k-1,i}\biggr)\biggr)\\[6.0pt]
\times\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{k+1}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\,\Gamma\bigl(\imath\gamma_{k+1,i}-\imath\gamma_{kj}+\frac{g}{2}\bigr)\,\Gamma\bigl(\imath\gamma_{kj}-\imath\gamma_{k+1,i}+\frac{g}{2}\bigr)}{\prod_{1\leq i\not=j\leq k}\,\Gamma(\imath\gamma_{ki}-\imath\gamma_{kj})\Gamma(\imath\gamma_{kj}-\imath\gamma_{ki}+g)}\,\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\prod_{i=1}^{k}d\gamma_{ki},

(1.3)

where we identify
\gamma_{ni}\equiv\lambda_{i}
, was found in
[
KK1
]
.
Besides, there is a well developed theory of Heckman–Opdam hypergeometric functions
[
HO
,
O
]
, which analyses wave functions through their series representation. However, the precise correspondence between different parts of the story was not rigorously established, except for the case of two particles
[
BDKK6
]
, and the goal of this paper is to fill this gap.

First, in Section
2
we collect known results for relativistic hyperbolic Ruijsenaars model needed in the present paper. Then, in Section
3
we study its limit to nonrelativistic Calogero–Sutherland model. In this limit basic ingredients of all integrals — the kernel function and the measure function — tend pointwise to their nonrelativistic counterparts. However, we cannot declare the uniform limit of the measure function, needed for establishing limits of integrals. Instead we prove the uniform bound for the measure function and thus prove the basic integral identity which is behind the commutativity of Baxter operators. Then their theory goes in parallel to that of Baxter operators in relativistic model. In this way we reprove the results of M. Hallnäs
[
H
]
.

The hyperbolic Ruijsenaars model is self-dual in a sense that corresponding wave functions diagonalize the same difference operators with respect to coordinates and spectral parameters
[
BDKK2
]
. In the case of Calogero–Sutherland system the dual model on the space of spectral parameters is governed by rational Ruijsenaars–Macdonald operators, which we consider in Section
4
. The corresponding limiting procedure from hyperbolic model is different. Again, we have a pointwise limit of kernel and measure functions, but this is not a uniform limit, which can be used in integrals. Therefore here we develop the theory of Baxter operators and their properties independently. In particular, we give a direct proof of commutativity of dual Baxter operators, which is a simplified version of the hyperbolic derivation from
[
BDKK1
]
. Besides, using the Mellin–Barnes integral formula for the wave function (
1.3
) we describe the spectrum of dual Baxter operators.

The equivalence between two integral representations of the wave functions (
1.2
), (
1.3
)

\displaystyle\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\hat{\Psi}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}),

(1.4)

or bispectral duality, is proved in Section
5
. The proof is based on the diagonalization properties of two families of Baxter operators, acting in the spaces of spatial and spectral variables. Besides, it relies on the commutativity of these families with each other, which follows from the convergence of certain integrals and requires certain bounds on the wave functions. Here we follow the lines of the paper
[
HR4
]
and obtain the necessary bounds using Mellin–Barnes representation. In the rational limit the bounds are slightly more delicate because of power factors in the asymptotics of the gamma function.

In Appendix
B
we derive asymptotics of the considered wave function

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})&\sim e^{\pi g\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}(2i-n-1)}\\[6.0pt]
&\times\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}e^{2\pi\imath\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\lambda_{\sigma(i)}}\prod_{{1\leq i<j\leq n}}\Gamma(\imath\lambda_{\sigma(i)}-\imath\lambda_{\sigma(j)})\,\Gamma(\imath\lambda_{\sigma(j)}-\imath\lambda_{\sigma(i)}+g)\end{aligned}

(1.5)

in Weyl chamber of spatial parameters
x_{1}\gg x_{2}\gg\ldots\gg x_{n}
.
Comparing it with the known asymptotics of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
Heckman–Opdam hypergeometric function
F_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})
, normalized as
F_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(0,...,0)=1
, in Section
6
we show, using the uniqueness property of Heckman–Opdam hypergeometric function
[
HO
,
O
]
, that

\displaystyle F_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(kg)}{\Gamma(g)}\,\prod_{1\leq i\not=j\leq n}\Gamma^{-1}(\imath(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})+g)\,{\Psi}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}).

(1.6)

As a corollary, we state two dual integral representations for the Heckman–Opdam hypergeometric function, as well as precise form of its Harish-Chandra series, which we obtain by residue evaluation of Mellin–Barnes integrals.

In Appendix C we perform another check of the identification (
1.6
) calculating the wave function at zero point
x_{1}=\ldots=x_{n}=0
.

In conclusion, we note that a third integral representation of the
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
Heckman–Opdam hypergeometric function is given in
[
BG
]
. The related Baxter operators are considered in
[
KMS
]
in the context of trigonometric Calogero–Sutherland model.

2
Ruijsenaars hyperbolic system

The Ruijsenaars hyperbolic system is described by a set of commuting Ruijsenaars–Macdonald difference operators

M^{R}_{r}(\bm{x}_{n})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[n]\\
|I|=r\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I\\
j\notin I\end{subarray}}\frac{\sh\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}-\imath\mathrm{g}\right)}{\sh\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}\right)}\cdot T^{-\imath\omega_{1}}_{I,x},

(2.7)

which act on functions of
n
complex variables

\bm{x}_{n}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})

analytical in a proper vicinity of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Here
T^{a}_{x_{i}}
is the shift operator

T^{a}_{x_{i}}=e^{a\partial_{x_{i}}},\qquad\left(T^{a}_{x_{i}}\,f\right)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i},\ldots,x_{n})=f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}+a,\ldots,x_{n})

(2.8)

and for any subset
I\subset[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}

T^{a}_{I,x}=\prod_{i\in I}T_{x_{i}}^{a}.

(2.9)

The system is parametrized by three constants: periods
\bm{\omega}=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
and coupling constant
g
, subject to conditions

\mathrm{Re}\,\omega_{1}>0,\qquad\mathrm{Re}\,\omega_{2}>0,\qquad 0<\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{g}<\mathrm{Re}\,\omega_{1}+\mathrm{Re}\,\omega_{2}

(2.10)

and also

0<\mathrm{Re}\,\frac{\mathrm{g}}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}<\mathrm{Re}\,\frac{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}.

(2.11)

The operators
M^{R}_{r}
are symmetric with respect to the bilinear form

\left(f(\bm{x}_{n}),\,g(\bm{x}_{n})\right)_{\mu^{R}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(\bm{x}_{n})g(-\bm{x}_{n})\mu^{R}(\bm{x}_{n})d\bm{x}_{n}.

(2.12)

Here the measure function
\mu^{R}(\bm{x}_{n})
is defined as follows.
First, define the weight function

w^{R}(x)=S_{2}(\imath x)S_{2}^{-1}(\imath x+\mathrm{g})=S_{2}(\imath x)S_{2}(-\imath x+\mathrm{g}^{\ast}),

(2.13)

where
S_{2}(z)=S_{2}(z|\bm{\omega})
is the double sine function, see Appendix
A
, and

\mathrm{g}^{\ast}=\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-\mathrm{g}.

(2.14)

Besides, denote

\mu^{R}(x):=\mu^{R}(x|\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\mathrm{g})=w^{R}(x)w^{R}(-x).

(2.15)

Finally, we set

\begin{split}&w^{R}(\bm{x}_{n})=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}w^{R}(x_{i}-x_{j}),\\
&\mu^{R}(\bm{x}_{n})=w^{R}(\bm{x}_{n})w^{R}(-\bm{x}_{n})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\not=j\end{subarray}}^{n}w^{R}(x_{i}-x_{j})=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mu^{R}(x_{i}-x_{j}).\end{split}

(2.16)

To describe the wave functions and Baxter operators we also need the following function of a complex variable

K^{R}(x):=K^{R}(x|\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\mathrm{g})=S_{2}^{-1}\Bigl(\imath x+\frac{\mathrm{g}^{\ast}}{2}\Bigr)S_{2}^{-1}\Bigl(-\imath x+\frac{\mathrm{g}^{\ast}}{2}\Bigr).

(2.17)

The Ruijsenaars kernel function is defined as the product

K^{R}(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{m})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{m}K^{R}(x_{i}-y_{j}).

(2.18)

One of the key objects for the analysis of the system is a family of Baxter operators
Q^{R}_{n}(\lambda)
parameterized by
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. These are integral operators

\left(Q^{R}_{n}(\lambda)f\right)(\bm{x}_{n})=d^{R}_{n}(\mathrm{g})\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{y}_{n}\,Q^{R}(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n};\lambda)f(\bm{y}_{n})

(2.19)

with the kernel

Q^{R}(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n};\lambda)=e^{{2\pi\imath}\lambda(\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}-\underline{\bm{y}}_{n})}K^{R}(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n})\mu^{R}(\bm{y}_{n}),\qquad x_{j},y_{j}\in\mathbb{R},

(2.20)

and normalizing constant

d^{R}_{n}(\mathrm{g})=\frac{1}{n!}\left[\sqrt{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}S_{2}(\mathrm{g})\right]^{-n}.

(2.21)

Here and below we use the notation

\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}.

Baxter operators commute between themselves as well as with Hamiltonians
M^{R}_{r}

[
BDKK1
]
:

\displaystyle Q^{R}_{n}(\lambda)\,Q^{R}_{n}(\rho)=Q^{R}_{n}(\rho)\,Q^{R}_{n}(\lambda),

(2.22)

\displaystyle Q^{R}_{n}(\lambda)\,M^{R}_{r}(\bm{x}_{n})=M^{R}_{r}(\bm{x}_{n})\,Q^{R}_{n}(\lambda),\qquad r=1,\ldots,n.

(2.23)

Next, denote by
\Lambda^{R}_{n}(\lambda)
the integral operator similar to the Baxter
Q
-operator

\begin{split}\left(\Lambda^{R}_{n}(\lambda)f\right)(\bm{x}_{n})=d^{R}_{n-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}d\bm{y}_{n-1}\,\Lambda^{R}(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n-1};\lambda)f(\bm{y}_{n-1})\end{split}

(2.24)

with the kernel

\Lambda^{R}(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n-1};\lambda)=e^{{2\pi\imath}\lambda(\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}-\underline{\bm{y}}_{n-1})}K^{R}(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n-1})\mu^{R}(\bm{y}_{n-1}),\qquad x_{j},y_{j}\in\mathbb{R},

(2.25)

and constant
d^{R}_{n-1}
given by the formula (
2.21
).
According to
[
HR1
]
(see also
[
BDKK2
]
) the function

\Psi^{R}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}):=\Psi^{R}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n};\mathrm{g}|\bm{\omega})=\Lambda^{R}_{n}(\lambda_{n})\,\Lambda^{R}_{n-1}(\lambda_{n-1})\cdots\Lambda^{R}_{2}(\lambda_{2})\,e^{{2\pi\imath}\lambda_{1}x_{1}}

(2.26)

is a joint eigenfunction of Macdonald operators.

M^{R}_{r}(\bm{x}_{n})\,\Psi^{R}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=e_{r}\bigl(e^{{2\pi\lambda_{1}}{\omega_{1}}},\dots,e^{{2\pi\lambda_{n}}{\omega_{1}}}\bigr)\Psi^{R}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}).

(2.27)

Here
e_{r}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
is
r
-th elementary symmetric function,

e_{r}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\ldots<i_{r}\leq n}z_{i_{1}}\cdots z_{i_{r}}.

In what follows
\Lambda^{R}_{n}(\lambda)
is called raising operator. These operators satisfy exchange relation

\displaystyle\Lambda^{R}_{n}(\lambda)\Lambda^{R}_{n-1}(\rho)=\Lambda^{R}_{n-1}(\rho)\Lambda^{R}_{n}(\lambda),

(2.28)

see
[
BDKK2
]
, which leads to the symmetry of wave functions (
2.26
) with respect to spectral variables.

To describe further properties of Baxter operators we need the following notation

\hat{\bm{\omega}}=\frac{\bm{\omega}}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}=\left(\frac{1}{\omega_{2}},\frac{1}{\omega_{1}}\right),\qquad\hat{\mathrm{g}}=\frac{\mathrm{g}^{*}}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}=\hat{\omega}_{1}+\hat{\omega}_{2}-\frac{\mathrm{g}}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}.

(2.29)

Moreover, for any quantity
a
, which depends on parameters
\bm{\omega}
and
\mathrm{g}
we denote by
\hat{a}
the same quantity with parameters
\bm{\omega}
and
\mathrm{g}
replaced by
\hat{\bm{\omega}}
and
\hat{\mathrm{g}}
. For instance,

\begin{split}&\hat{w}^{R}(\lambda)=S_{2}(\imath\lambda|\hat{\bm{\omega}})S_{2}^{-1}(\imath\lambda+\hat{\mathrm{g}}|\hat{\bm{\omega}}),\\[6.0pt]
&\hat{K}^{R}(\lambda)=S_{2}^{-1}\Bigl(\imath\lambda+\frac{\hat{\omega}_{1}+\hat{\omega}_{2}-\hat{\mathrm{g}}}{2}\Big|\hat{\bm{\omega}}\Bigr)S_{2}^{-1}\Bigl(-\imath\lambda+\frac{\hat{\omega}_{1}+\hat{\omega}_{2}-\hat{\mathrm{g}}}{2}\Big|\hat{\bm{\omega}}\Bigr).\end{split}

(2.30)

A degeneration of commutativity relation (
2.22
) gives the exchange relation

Q^{R}_{n}(\lambda)\,\Lambda^{R}_{n}(\rho)=\hat{K}^{R}(\lambda-\rho)\;\Lambda^{R}_{n}(\rho)\,Q^{R}_{n-1}(\lambda),

(2.31)

which in its turn implies that
\Psi^{R}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})
diagonalizes Baxter operators

Q^{R}_{n}(\lambda)\,\Psi^{R}_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\hat{K}^{R}(\lambda-\lambda_{j})\,\Psi^{R}_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}),

(2.32)

the details are given in
[
BDKK2
]
.

Using the notations (
2.29
) let us introduce dual Baxter and raising operators, acting in the spaces of spectral variables

\displaystyle\bigl(\hat{Q}^{R}_{n}(x)f\bigr)(\bm{\lambda}_{n})=d^{R}_{n}(\hat{\mathrm{g}}|\hat{\bm{\omega}})\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{\gamma}_{n}\,\hat{Q}^{R}(\bm{\lambda}_{n},\bm{\gamma}_{n};x)f(\bm{\gamma}_{n}),

(2.33)

\displaystyle\bigl(\hat{\Lambda}^{R}_{n}(x)f\bigr)({\bm{\lambda}}_{n})=d^{R}_{n-1}(\hat{\mathrm{g}}|\hat{\bm{\omega}})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}d\bm{\gamma}_{n-1}\,\hat{\Lambda}^{R}({\bm{\lambda}}_{n},\bm{\gamma}_{n-1};x)f(\bm{\gamma}_{n-1})

with the kernels

\begin{split}&\hat{Q}^{R}({\bm{\lambda}}_{n},\bm{\gamma}_{n};x)=e^{{2\pi\imath}x(\underline{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}-\underline{\bm{\gamma}}_{n})}\hat{K}^{R}({\bm{\lambda}}_{n},\bm{\gamma}_{n})\hat{\mu}(\bm{\gamma}_{n}),\\[7.0pt]
&\hat{\Lambda}^{R}({\bm{\lambda}}_{n},\bm{\gamma}_{n-1};x)=e^{{2\pi\imath}x(\underline{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}-\underline{\bm{\gamma}}_{n-1})}\hat{K}^{R}({\bm{\lambda}}_{n},\bm{\gamma}_{n-1})\hat{\mu}(\bm{\gamma}_{n-1}).\end{split}

(2.34)

Dual Baxter operators commute between themselves and obey the corresponding exchange relation with dual raising operators

\displaystyle\hat{Q}^{R}_{n}(x)\,\hat{Q}^{R}_{n}(y)=\hat{Q}^{R}_{n}(y)\,\hat{Q}^{R}_{n}(x),

(2.35)

\displaystyle\hat{Q}^{R}_{n}(x)\,\hat{\Lambda}^{R}_{n}(y)=K^{R}(x-y)\;\hat{\Lambda}^{R}_{n}(y)\,\hat{Q}^{R}_{n-1}(x).

(2.36)

The duality theorem
[
BDKK2
]
states that the wave function (
2.26
) can be also constructed via dual raising operators

\Psi^{R}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\hat{\Lambda}^{R}_{n}(x_{n})\,\hat{\Lambda}^{R}_{n-1}(x_{n-1})\cdots\hat{\Lambda}^{R}_{2}(x_{2})\,e^{{2\pi\imath}\lambda_{1}x_{1}},

(2.37)

or in short,

\hat{\Psi}^{R}_{\bm{x}_{n}}({\bm{\lambda}}_{n})=\Psi^{R}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}).

(2.38)

3
Calogero–Sutherland hyperbolic system

The nonrelativistic counterparts of the kernel, weight and measure functions are

K(x)=(2\ch\pi x)^{-\mathit{g}},\qquad w(x)=|2\sh\pi x|^{\mathit{g}},\qquad\mu(x)=w^{2}(x)

(3.39)

and, correspondingly,

\begin{split}K(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{m})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{m}K(x_{i}-y_{j}),\qquad&w(\bm{x}_{n})=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}w(x_{i}-x_{j}),\\[3.0pt]
&\mu(\bm{x}_{n})=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mu(x_{i}-x_{j}).\end{split}

(3.40)

In these notations, the commuting Hamiltonians of (reduced) hyperbolic Calogero–Sutherland
n
-particle system can be written as follows
[
HR1
, eq. (11)]
:

\operatorname{H}_{k}=w^{-1}(\bm{x}_{n})\cdot{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\cdot w(\bm{x}_{n}),

(3.41)

where

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}_{k}&=\frac{1}{(n-k)!}\sum\limits_{0\leq l\leq\left[\frac{k}{2}\right]}\frac{1}{2^{l}l!(k-2l)!}\\
&\times\sum\limits_{\sigma\in S_{n}}s_{g}(x_{\sigma(1)}-x_{\sigma(2)})\cdots s_{g}(x_{\sigma(2l-1)}-x_{\sigma(2l)})\,p_{\sigma(2l+1)}\cdots p_{\sigma(k)},\end{split}

(3.42)

s_{g}(x)=\frac{\pi^{2}g(1-g)}{\sh^{2}\pi x},\qquad p_{j}=-\imath\partial_{x_{j}}.

(3.43)

Note that the Hamiltonian (
1.1
) is given by the combination
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{1}^{2}-2\mathcal{H}_{2}
.

All the results of this section hold under assumption on the coupling constant

\displaystyle g>0,

(3.44)

whereas starting from Section
4
we impose the stronger condition
g>1
. At the end this restriction can be relaxed after renormalization of the wave function and its identification with Heckman–Opdam
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
hypergeometric function, see Section 6.

Now define Baxter operators
Q_{n}(\lambda)
and raising operators
\Lambda_{n}(\lambda)
similarly to (
2.19
), (
2.24
):

\displaystyle\left(Q_{n}(\lambda)f\right)(\bm{x}_{n})=d_{n}(g)\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{y}_{n}\,Q(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n};\lambda)f(\bm{y}_{n}),

(3.45)

\displaystyle\left(\Lambda_{n}(\lambda)f\right)(\bm{x}_{n})=d_{n-1}(g)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}d\bm{y}_{n-1}\,\Lambda(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n-1};\lambda)f(\bm{y}_{n-1}),

(3.46)

where the kernels are given by

\displaystyle Q(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n};\lambda)=e^{{2\pi\imath}\lambda(\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}-\underline{\bm{y}}_{n})}K(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n})\mu(\bm{y}_{n}),

(3.47)

\displaystyle\Lambda(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n-1};\lambda)=e^{{2\pi\imath}\lambda(\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}-\underline{\bm{y}}_{n-1})}K(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n-1})\mu(\bm{y}_{n-1}),

(3.48)

and normalizing constant reads

\displaystyle d_{n}(g)=\frac{\left(2\pi\Gamma(g)\right)^{n}}{n!}.

(3.49)

Baxter operators commute with Hamiltonians
\operatorname{H}_{k}
, given by the relations (
3.41
)–(
3.43
):

\operatorname{H}_{k}Q_{n}(\lambda)=Q_{n}(\lambda)\operatorname{H}_{k}.

(3.50)

The proof of (
3.50
) copies the proof of analogous identity for the hyperbolic Calogero–Sutherland system, see e.g.
[
BDKK1
, Section 2]
.
It is a direct consequence of kernel function identity
[
HR2
, eq. (35)]
:

\operatorname{H}_{k}(\bm{x}_{n})K(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n})=\operatorname{H}_{k}(-\bm{y}_{n})K(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n}),

(3.51)

and of the symmetricity of Hamiltonians
\operatorname{H}_{k}
with respect to the bilinear pairing

\left(f(\bm{x}_{n}),\,g(\bm{x}_{n})\right)_{\mu}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(\bm{x}_{n})g(-\bm{x}_{n})\mu(\bm{x}_{n})d\bm{x}_{n},

(3.52)

which follows from the fact that operators
\mathcal{H}_{k}
given by (
3.42
) are manifestly symmetric with respect to the pairing with measure
d\bm{x}_{n}
. Note also that commutativity relation (
3.50
) on the space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)
is established in
[
H
, Section 4]
.

Using a degeneration of kernel function identity (
3.51
), M. Hallnäs and S. Ruijsenaars proved in
[
HR2
]
that the function

\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\Lambda_{n}(\lambda_{n})\,\Lambda_{n-1}(\lambda_{n-1})\cdots\Lambda_{2}(\lambda_{2})\,e^{{2\pi\imath}\lambda_{1}x_{1}}

(3.53)

is a joint eigenfunction of Hamiltonians (
3.41
)

\operatorname{H}_{k}\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=e_{k}(2\pi\lambda_{1},\ldots,2\pi\lambda_{n})\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}).

(3.54)

Recently, M. Hallnäs
[
H
]
proved that the functions (
3.53
) also diagonalize Baxter
Q
-operators (
3.45
):

Q_{n}(\lambda)\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\prod^{n}_{i=1}\hat{K}(\lambda-\lambda_{i})\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}),

(3.55)

where

\hat{K}(\lambda)=\Gamma\Bigl(\imath\lambda+\frac{g}{2}\Bigr)\Gamma\Bigl(-\imath\lambda+\frac{g}{2}\Bigr).

(3.56)

The proof given in

3.1
Calogero–Sutherland limit

In this section we establish local relations between Baxter and raising operators by using the nonrelativistic limit of Ruijsenaars hyperbolic system. As a corollary, we obtain alternative proof of the diagonalization property (
3.55
). The present approach gives new nontrivial integral identities corresponding to local relations between Baxter and raising operators.

Consider the Ruijsenaars hyperbolic system with fixed period
\omega_{1}
and renormalized coupling constant
\mathrm{g}

\omega_{1}\mapsto 1,\qquad\mathrm{g}\mapsto\mathit{g}\omega_{2}.

(3.57)

Let us describe the limits of kernel and measure functions, Baxter operators (
2.19
), raising operators (
2.24
) and wave functions (
2.26
) (without normalization (
2.21
)) as
\omega_{2}\to 0
.
The limiting procedure is based on the following statements, proved in Appendix
A.2
.

Proposition 3.1
.

For
g>0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
we have pointwise limits

\displaystyle\lim_{\omega_{2}\to 0}K^{R}(x|1,\omega_{2},g\omega_{2})=K(x),

(3.58)

\displaystyle\lim_{\omega_{2}\to 0}\mu^{R}(x|1,\omega_{2},g\omega_{2})=\mu(x).

(3.59)

Proposition 3.2
.

For
g>0
there exist constants
C_{1}(g,a),C_{2}(g,a)
such that

\displaystyle|K^{R}(x|1,\omega_{2},g\omega_{2})|<C_{1}e^{-\pi g|x|},

(3.60)

\displaystyle|\mu^{R}(x|1,\omega_{2},g\omega_{2})|<C_{2}e^{2\pi g|x|}

(3.61)

uniformly for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
\omega_{2}\in(0,a]

The statements of Proposition
3.1
are well known; they easily follow from integral representations of logarithms of double sine and sine functions. However the statements of Proposition
3.2
, especially the bound (
3.61

The proof is given in Appendix
A.2
.

For any
2n

(3.62)

\displaystyle Q_{n}(\bm{z}_{2n};\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{{2\pi\imath}\lambda\underline{\bm{y}}_{n}}\prod_{a=1}^{2n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}K(z_{a}-y_{i})\mu(\bm{y}_{n})d\bm{y}_{n}.

In
[
BDKK1
, Section 3]
the integral identity

\displaystyle Q^{R}_{n}(\bm{z}_{2n};\lambda)=e^{{2\pi\imath}\lambda\underline{\bm{z}}_{2n}}Q^{R}_{n}(\bm{z}_{2n};-\lambda)

is proven under assumption
|\operatorname{Im}\lambda|<\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{g}/\omega_{1}\omega_{2}=g
. This identity is equivalent to commutativity of relativistic Baxter operators (

) written in terms of kernels.
The following statement is its nonrelativistic limit.

Proposition 3.3
.

Assuming
|\operatorname{Im}\lambda|<g
we have the equality

Q_{n}(\bm{z}_{2n};\lambda)=e^{{2\pi\imath}\lambda\underline{\bm{z}}_{2n}}Q_{n}(\bm{z}_{2n};-\lambda).

(3.65)

Proof
. The stated equality follows from the formula (
3.64

\displaystyle\lim_{\omega_{2}\to 0}Q_{n}^{R}(\bm{z}_{2n};\lambda)=Q_{n}(\bm{z}_{2n};\lambda),

the integrand of (

\omega_{2}\to 0

\displaystyle\biggl|e^{{2\pi\imath}\lambda\underline{\bm{y}}_{n}}\prod_{a=1}^{2n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}K^{R}(z_{a}-y_{i})\mu^{R}(\bm{y}_{n})\biggr|\leq C(\bm{z}_{2n};g)\,\exp\biggl(2\pi(|\operatorname{Im}\lambda|-g)\sum_{j=1}^{n}|y_{j}|\biggr).

Hence, due to dominated convergence theorem we can interchange the limit
\omega_{2}\to 0
with integration and arrive at the formula (

\Box

The last proposition gives an alternative to

Theorem 3.1
.

Baxter operators commute

under assumption

4.1
Statements

Denote by

In this section and in what follows we assume

G_{1}(\bm{\gamma}_{n})\cdot G_{2}(\bm{\gamma}_{n},\nu_{n})\cdot G_{3}(\nu_{n})\cdot G_{4}(\bm{\gamma}_{n},\nu_{n}),

where

\begin{split}G_{1}(\bm{\gamma}_{n})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\left((\lambda_{i}-\gamma_{j})^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{g-1}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\left((\nu_{k}-\gamma_{j})^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{g-1}{2}}\\
\times\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n-1}\left((\gamma_{i}-\gamma_{j})^{2}+1\right)^{1-g}\,\left(\gamma_{n}^{2}+1\right)^{1-g}\!\!,\end{split}

\begin{split}G_{2}(\bm{\gamma}_{n},\nu_{n})=&\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\frac{(\gamma_{j}-\nu_{n})^{2}+1}{\nu_{n}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{g-1}{2}}\,\left(\frac{(\lambda_{n}-\gamma_{n}-\nu_{n})^{2}+1}{\nu_{n}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{g-1}{2}},\\
G_{3}(\nu_{n})=&\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\frac{(\nu_{j}-\nu_{n})^{2}+1}{\nu_{n}^{2}}\right)^{1-g},\\
G_{4}(\bm{\gamma}_{n},\nu_{n})=&\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\frac{\left((\lambda_{j}-\gamma_{n}-\nu_{n})^{2}+1\right)\left((\nu_{j}-\gamma_{n}-\nu_{n})^{2}+1\right)}{\left((\gamma_{j}-\gamma_{n}-\nu_{n})^{2}+1\right)^{2}}\right)^{\frac{g-1}{2}}.\end{split}

The products
G_{1}(\bm{\gamma}_{n})
,
G_{2}(\bm{\gamma}_{n},\nu_{n})
and
G_{3}(\nu_{n})
are bounded by the same arguments as
F_{1}(\bm{\gamma}_{n})
,
F_{2}(\bm{\gamma}_{n},\nu_{n})
and
F_{3}(\nu_{n})
above.
For the last product
G_{4}(\bm{\gamma}_{n},\nu_{n})
, we use the inequality

\frac{(x-a)^{2}+1}{(x-b)^{2}+1}<(a-b)^{2}+2

(4.116)

which follows from the analysis of the elementary function of real variable

f(x)=\frac{(x-a)^{2}}{x^{2}+1}.

Inequality (
4.116
) implies that the product
G_{4}(\bm{\gamma}_{n},\nu_{n})
can be bounded by positive power function of the variables
\bm{\gamma}_{n}
, which does not depend on
\nu_{n}
and thus can be suppressed by an exponent

C\exp(\delta\sum_{j}|\gamma_{j}|)

with any positive
\delta
.

We thus conclude that under condition (
4.113
) we have a uniform bound of the integrand by the integrable function and can interchange limit and integral.
The integral over
\gamma_{n}
separates,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}d\gamma_{n}\,e^{2\pi\imath\left(x-y+\frac{\imath}{2}\right)\gamma_{n}}\Gamma\left(\imath\gamma_{n}+\frac{g}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(-\imath\gamma_{n}+\frac{g}{2}\right)=2\pi\Gamma(g)\left(2\ch\pi\left(x-y+\frac{\imath}{2}\right)\right)^{-g},

(4.117)

that is

\int_{\mathbb{R}}d\gamma_{n}\,e^{2\pi\imath\left(x-y+\frac{\imath}{2}\right)\gamma_{n}}\hat{K}(\gamma_{n})=2\pi\Gamma(g)K\left(x-y+\frac{\imath}{2}\right).

(4.118)

Combining (
4.118
) with (
4.114
), (
4.104
), and (
4.82

\hat{Q}_{n}(x)\,\hat{\Lambda}_{n}\Bigl(y-\frac{\imath}{2}\Bigr)=K\Bigl(x-y+\frac{\imath}{2}\Bigr)\hat{\Lambda}_{n}\Bigl(y-\frac{\imath}{2}\Bigr)\hat{Q}_{n-1}(x),

(4.119)

valid under condition (
4.113
). Thus we can make a shift

and get the statement (
4.89
) of Theorem
4.3
.

5.1
Asymptotics and bounds

In
[
HR3
]
and
[
HR4
]
M. Hallnäs and S. Ruijsenaars got remarkable results on asymptotical behaviour and total bounds of the wave functions of hyperbolic Ruijsenaars model. These bounds were used in
[
BDKK2
]
for establishing the duality symmetry (
2.38
) of the wave functions of hyperbolic Ruijsenaars model.

The wave function
{\hat{\Psi}}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})
given by iterative Mellin–Barnes integral (
4.90
) admits analogous asymptotical analysis with arguments almost the same, as in
[
HR3
,
HR4
]
.

Following the lines of
[
HR3
,
HR4
]
, let us introduce renormalized wave functions

\Phi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\hat{w}({\bm{\lambda}}_{n}){\hat{\Psi}}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})

(5.1)

and (as shown below) their asymptotics

\Phi^{as}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=e^{-2\pi g(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{\rho}_{n})}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}e^{2\pi\imath\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\lambda_{\sigma(i)}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<j\\
\sigma(i)>\sigma(j)\end{subarray}}\frac{\hat{w}(\lambda_{\sigma(j)}-\lambda_{\sigma(i)})}{\hat{w}(\lambda_{\sigma(i)}-\lambda_{\sigma(j)})},

(5.2)

where

\bm{\rho}_{n}=\Big(\frac{n-1}{2},\ldots,\frac{1-n}{2}\Big).

(5.3)

Set also

\hat{\Delta}({\bm{\lambda}}_{n})=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(1+|\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}|).

(5.4)

Then we have the following statements. Assume that

x_{1}>x_{2}>\ldots>x_{n}

(5.5)

and denote by
m_{n}(\bm{x}_{n})
the minimum of distances
x_{i}-x_{i+1}
,

m_{n}(\bm{x}_{n})=\min_{i}(x_{i}-x_{i+1}).

(5.6)

Theorem 5.1
.

For
g>1
,
{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and arbitrary small
\varepsilon>0
there exist constants
N(g),C(g,\varepsilon)>0
such that in the region (
5.5
)

|\Phi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})-\Phi^{as}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})|<C(g,\varepsilon)\,\hat{\Delta}({\bm{\lambda}}_{n})^{N(g)}\,e^{-2\pi g(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{\rho}_{n})-(2\pi-\varepsilon)m_{n}(\bm{x}_{n})}.

(5.7)

Since wave function is symmetric with respect to
\bm{x}_{n}
(
4.94
), the above theorem in fact provides its asymptotics in all regions. Besides, we have a total bound on the function
\Phi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})
.

Theorem 5.2
.

For
g>1
and
\bm{x}_{n},{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
there exist

such that

|\Phi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})|<C(g)\,\hat{\Delta}({\bm{\lambda}}_{n})^{N(g)}\,e^{-\pi g\sum_{i<j}|x_{i}-x_{j}|}.

(5.8)

Note that, actually, in both theorems we can take

(5.9)

The proofs of these theorems, which are given in Appendix

) and (
4.108

5.2
Duality

Theorem 5.3
.

For
g>1
and
\bm{x}_{n},\bm{\lambda}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have the equality

{\Psi}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})={\hat{\Psi}}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}).

(5.10)

Proof
. The proof is by induction over
n
. For
n=1
the equality is immediate

\hat{\Psi}_{\lambda_{1}}(x_{1})=e^{2\pi\imath\lambda_{1}x_{1}}=\Psi_{\lambda_{1}}(x_{1}).

Now assume we proved the statement for
n-1
variables.
By definition (
3.53
),

\begin{split}\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\Lambda_{n}(\lambda_{n})\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n-1}}(\bm{x}_{n}).\end{split}

(5.11)

Comparing (
3.45
) and (
3.46
), we obtain

\begin{split}\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=e^{2\pi\imath\lambda_{n}x_{n}}Q_{n-1}(\lambda_{n})\prod^{n-1}_{j=1}K(x_{n}-x_{j})\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n-1}}(\bm{x}_{n-1}).\end{split}

(5.12)

According to (
4.92
)

\begin{split}\prod^{n-1}_{j=1}K(x_{n}-x_{j})\hat{\Psi}_{\bm{\lambda}_{n-1}}(\bm{x}_{n-1})=\hat{Q}_{n-1}(x_{n})\hat{\Psi}_{\bm{\lambda}_{n-1}}(\bm{x}_{n-1}),\end{split}

(5.13)

so applying induction assumption
\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n-1}}(\bm{x}_{n-1})=\hat{\Psi}_{\bm{\lambda}_{n-1}}(\bm{x}_{n-1})
we deduce

\begin{split}\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=e^{2\pi\imath\lambda_{n}x_{n}}Q_{n-1}(\lambda_{n})\hat{Q}_{n-1}(x_{n})\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n-1}}(\bm{x}_{n-1}).\end{split}

(5.14)

The next step is to change the order of Baxter operators

Q_{n-1}(\lambda_{n})\hat{Q}_{n-1}(x_{n})=\hat{Q}_{n-1}(x_{n})Q_{n-1}(\lambda_{n}).

To do it, we have to prove that the integral (
5.14
)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2(n-1)}}&d\bm{y}_{n-1}d\bm{\gamma}_{n-1}\,e^{2\pi\imath\lambda_{n}(\underline{\bm{x}}_{n-1}-\underline{\bm{y}}_{n-1})}\,e^{2\pi\imath x_{n}(\underline{{\bm{\lambda}}}_{n-1}-\underline{\bm{\gamma}}_{n-1})}\\[6.0pt]
&\times K(\bm{x}_{n-1},\bm{y}_{n-1})\hat{K}(\bm{\lambda}_{n-1},\bm{\gamma}_{n-1})\mu(\bm{y}_{n-1})\hat{\mu}(\bm{\gamma}_{n-1})\Psi_{\bm{\gamma}_{n-1}}(\bm{y}_{n-1})\end{split}

(5.15)

is absolutely convergent and then apply Fubini’s theorem.

To prove absolute convergence we use the bound on wave function (
5.8
). More precisely, we split the measure functions as

\begin{split}\mu(\bm{y}_{n-1})=w(\bm{y}_{n-1})w(-\bm{y}_{n-1}),\qquad\hat{\mu}(\bm{y}_{n-1})=\hat{w}(\bm{y}_{n-1})\hat{w}(-\bm{y}_{n-1}),\end{split}

(5.16)

and group factors from the integrand of (
5.15
) into three parts

\begin{split}&w(-\bm{y}_{n-1})K(\bm{x}_{n-1},\bm{y}_{n-1}),\qquad\hat{w}(-\bm{\gamma}_{n-1})\hat{K}(\bm{\lambda}_{n-1},\bm{\gamma}_{n-1}),\\[6.0pt]
&w(\bm{y}_{n-1})\hat{w}(\bm{\gamma}_{n-1})\Psi_{\bm{\gamma}_{n-1}}(\bm{y}_{n-1}).\end{split}

(5.17)

According to (
3.76
), we have

\begin{split}|w(-\bm{y}_{n-1})K(\bm{x}_{n-1},\bm{y}_{n-1})|&\leq C^{\prime}_{1}e^{\pi g\sum_{i<j}|y_{i}-y_{j}|}\cdot C^{\prime}_{2}e^{-\pi g\sum_{i,j}|x_{i}-y_{j}|}\\
&\leq C^{\prime}(g,\bm{\omega},\bm{x}_{n-1})e^{-\pi g\sum^{n-1}_{j=1}|y_{j}|},\end{split}

(5.18)

where we use inequalities
|y_{i}-y_{j}|\leq|y_{i}|+|y_{j}|
and
|x_{i}-y_{j}|\geq|y_{j}|-|x_{i}|
.

Similarly, from (
4.107
) and (
4.108
) we deduce

\begin{split}|\hat{w}(-\bm{\gamma}_{n-1})\hat{K}(\bm{\lambda}_{n-1},\bm{\gamma}_{n-1})|\leq\hat{C}^{\prime}(g,\bm{\omega},\bm{\lambda}_{n-1})p(|\bm{\gamma}_{n-1}|)e^{-\pi\sum^{n-1}_{j=1}|\gamma_{j}|}.\end{split}

(5.19)

Furthermore, applying (
5.8
) we have

\begin{split}|w(\bm{y}_{n-1})\hat{w}(\bm{\gamma}_{n-1})\Psi_{\bm{\gamma}_{n-1}}(\bm{y}_{n-1})|\leq C(g,\bm{\omega})\hat{\Delta}(\bm{\gamma}_{n-1})^{N^{\prime}(g)},\end{split}

(5.20)

where we again used induction assumption
\Psi_{\bm{\gamma}_{n-1}}(\bm{y}_{n-1})=\hat{\Psi}_{\bm{\gamma}_{n-1}}(\bm{y}_{n-1})
. Bounds (
5.18
), (
5.19
), (
5.20
) imply absolute convergence of the integral (
5.15
).

Changing order of
Q
-operators in (
5.14
), we have

\begin{split}\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})&=e^{2\pi\imath\lambda_{n}x_{n}}Q_{n-1}(\lambda_{n})\hat{Q}_{n-1}(x_{n})\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n-1}}(\bm{x}_{n-1})\\[6.0pt]
&=e^{2\pi\imath\lambda_{n}x_{n}}\hat{Q}_{n-1}(x_{n})Q_{n-1}(\lambda_{n})\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n-1}}(\bm{x}_{n-1}).\end{split}

(5.21)

Using the identity (
3.55
)

\begin{split}Q_{n-1}(\lambda_{n})\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n-1}}(\bm{x}_{n-1})=\prod^{n-1}_{j=1}\hat{K}(\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{j})\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n-1}}(\bm{x}_{n-1}),\end{split}

(5.22)

we rewrite the above expression as

\begin{split}\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=e^{2\pi\imath\lambda_{n}x_{n}}\hat{Q}_{n-1}(x_{n})K(\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{j})\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n-1}}(\bm{x}_{n-1}).\end{split}

(5.23)

Finally, due to explicit formulas (
4.84
), (
4.88
) and induction assumption we have

\begin{split}\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\hat{\Lambda}_{n}(x_{n})\hat{\Psi}_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n-1}).\end{split}

(5.24)

This is exactly the definition of Mellin–Barnes integral representation (
4.90
).

6
Heckman–Opdam hypergeometric function

In the previous sections we considered wave functions of Calogero–Sutherland model constructed using integral operators. Alternatively, Heckman and Opdam studied wave functions expressed in terms of so-called Harish-Chandra series
[
HO
]
. In particular, they proved that Calogero–Sutherland Hamiltonians admit unique eigenfunctions
F_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})
with normalization

F_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(0,\dots,0)=1,

(6.25)

which are symmetric and analytic in
\bm{x}_{n}
on a suitable neighbourhood of the origin.

The results of the previous section enable us to establish the precise correspondence between Heckman–Opdam
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
hypergeometric function and the constructed above wave function of the Calogero–Sutherland model.

First, we reformulate the statement of Theorem
5.1
directly for the function

{\Psi}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})={\hat{\Psi}}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}).

Define

{\Psi}^{as}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=e^{-2\pi g(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{\rho}_{n})}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}e^{2\pi\imath\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\lambda_{\sigma(i)}}\prod_{{i<j}}\hat{w}^{-1}(\lambda_{\sigma(i)}-\lambda_{\sigma(j)}).

(6.26)

Theorem
5.1
implies the following asymptotical behaviour of the function
{\Psi}^{as}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})
for real
{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}
.

Proposition 6.1
.

In the region
x_{1}>x_{2}>\ldots>x_{n}

|\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})-\Psi^{as}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})|<C(g,{\bm{\lambda}}_{n})\exp(-2\pi g(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{\rho}_{n})-(2\pi-\varepsilon)m_{n}(\bm{x}))

(6.27)

for any
{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\lambda_{i}\not=\lambda_{j}
.

Since
\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})
is symmetric in
\bm{x}_{n}
, in the region

x_{1}<x_{2}<\ldots<x_{n}

(6.28)

its asymptotics is described by the function

{\Psi^{\prime}}^{as}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=e^{2\pi g(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{\rho}_{n})}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}e^{2\pi\imath\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\lambda_{\sigma(i)}}\prod_{{i<j}}\hat{w}^{-1}(\lambda_{\sigma(j)}-\lambda_{\sigma(i)})

(6.29)

so that for any
{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
(with
\lambda_{i}\not=\lambda_{j}
for
i\not=j
) and
\bm{x}_{n}
in the region (
6.28
)

|\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})-{\Psi^{\prime}}^{as}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})|<C(g,{\bm{\lambda}}_{n})\exp(2\pi g(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{\rho}_{n})-(2\pi-\varepsilon)m^{\prime}_{n}(\bm{x})),

(6.30)

where

m^{\prime}_{n}=\min_{i}(x_{i+1}-x_{i}).

On the other hand, the asymptotics
F^{as}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})
of the Heckman–Opdam
A_{n-1}
hypergeometric function
F_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})
(extended to
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
) in the region (
6.28
) is given by
[
HO
,
O
]

F^{as}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\alpha_{n}(g)\,e^{2\pi g(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{\rho}_{n})}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}e^{2\pi\imath\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\lambda_{\sigma(i)}}\prod_{{i<j}}\frac{\Gamma\bigl(\imath(\lambda_{\sigma(j)}-\lambda_{\sigma(i)})\bigr)}{\Gamma\bigl(\imath(\lambda_{\sigma(j)}-\lambda_{\sigma(i)})+g\bigr)},

(6.31)

where

\alpha_{n}(g)=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(kg)}{\Gamma(g)}.

(6.32)

Comparing (
6.29
) and (
6.31
) (recall that
\hat{w}^{-1}(\lambda)=\Gamma(\imath\lambda)\Gamma(-\imath\lambda+g)
), we see that

F^{as}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\alpha_{n}\prod_{i\not=j}\Gamma^{-1}(\imath(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})+g)\,{\Psi^{\prime}}^{as}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}).

(6.33)

Thus, the functions

F_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\alpha_{n}\prod_{i\not=j}\Gamma^{-1}(\imath(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})+g)\,{\Psi}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})

diagonalize the same Calogero–Sutherland differential operators, are analytical in a neighbourhood of the origin (see
[
HR1
, Theorem 6.1]
) and have the same asymptotics in the region (
6.28
). Then the uniqueness theorem
[
HO
]
says that they coincide.

Theorem 6.1
.

F_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\alpha_{n}\prod_{i\not=j}\Gamma^{-1}(\imath(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})+g)\,{\Psi}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}).

(6.34)

In Appendix
C
we also verify by direct calculation that the normalization condition (
6.25
) is satisfied.

The conjugation by the product
\prod_{i\not=j}\Gamma^{-1}(\imath(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})+g)
transforms dual Hamiltonians (
4.78
)

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{r}=(-1)^{r(n-1)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[n]\\
|I|=r\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I\\
j\notin I\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}-\imath(1-g)}{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}}\prod_{i\in I}T_{\lambda_{i}}

(6.35)

to the same operators with reflected coupling constant (and without sign factor)

\hat{H}_{r}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[n]\\
|I|=r\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I\\
j\notin I\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}-\imath g}{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}}\prod_{i\in I}T_{\lambda_{i}},

(6.36)

that is

\hat{M}_{r}\,\prod_{i\not=j}\Gamma(\imath(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})+g)=\prod_{i\not=j}\Gamma(\imath(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})+g)\,\hat{H}_{r}.

This is in accordance with the known fact that Heckman–Opdam function satisfies difference equations
[
C
,
DE
]

\hat{H}_{r}{F}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=e_{r}(e^{2\pi x_{1}},\ldots,e^{2\pi x_{n}}){F}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}).

(6.37)

As a direct corollary of the relation (
6.34
), we have two integral representations for Heckman–Opdam hypergeometric function

\displaystyle{F}_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{n}\prod_{i\not=j}\Gamma^{-1}(\imath(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})+g)\ \Lambda_{n}(\lambda_{n})\,\Lambda_{n-1}(\lambda_{n-1})\cdots\Lambda_{2}(\lambda_{2})\,e^{{2\pi\imath}\lambda_{1}x_{1}}

(6.38)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{n}\prod_{i\not=j}\Gamma^{-1}(\imath(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})+g)\ \hat{\Lambda}_{n}(x_{n})\,\hat{\Lambda}_{n-1}(x_{n-1})\cdots\,{\hat{\Lambda}}(x_{2})\,e^{2\pi i\lambda_{1}x_{1}}.

(6.39)

Standard bounds on gamma function
\Gamma(\lambda)
(Stirling formula in the cone
|\arg\lambda|<\pi
together with reflection formula between the poles
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
) show that Mellin–Barnes integral (
4.90
) can be calculated by residue technique in the region
x_{1}>x_{2}>\ldots>x_{n}
with
m_{n}(x)>\delta
for some fixed
\delta>0
. The result is the convergent Harish-Chandra series, which we desribe now.

Let
M_{n}
be the set of lower triangular matrices with zeros on diagonal, whose elements are nonnegative intergers

M_{n}=\bigl\{M=(m_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}\,\big|\,m_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\;j<i;\;m_{ij}=0,\;i\leq j\bigr\}.

(6.40)

For a given
M=(m_{ij})\in M_{n}
define the meromorphic function

c_{M}({\bm{\lambda}}_{n})=\prod_{1\leq j<i\leq n}\frac{(-1)^{m_{ij}}\Gamma(g+m_{ij})}{m_{ij}!\Gamma(g)}\,\frac{\prod\limits_{k=3}^{n}\prod\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}\prod\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t=1}}{{t\not=s}}}^{k-1}\hat{w}(\lambda_{s}-\lambda_{t}+\imath(p_{ks}-p_{k,t}))}{\prod\limits_{k=2}^{n}\prod\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}\prod\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t=1}}{{t\not=s}}}^{k}\hat{w}(\lambda_{s}-\lambda_{t}+\imath(p_{ks}-p_{k+1,t}))}=

(6.41)

\!\prod_{1\leq j<i\leq n}\!\!\!\!\!\frac{(-1)^{m_{ij}}\Gamma(g+m_{ij})}{m_{ij}!\Gamma(g)}\,\frac{\prod\limits_{k=2}^{n}\prod\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}\prod\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t=1}}{{t\not=s}}}^{k}\!\Gamma(\imath(\lambda_{s}-\!\lambda_{t})-\!p_{ks}+\!p_{k+1,t})\Gamma(\imath(\lambda_{t}-\!\lambda_{s})+\!p_{ks}-\!p_{k+1,t}+\!g)}{\prod\limits_{k=3}^{n}\prod\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}\prod\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t=1}}{{t\not=s}}}^{k-1}\Gamma(\imath(\lambda_{s}-\lambda_{t})-p_{ks}+p_{k,t})\Gamma(\imath(\lambda_{t}-\lambda_{s})+p_{ks}-p_{k,t}+\!g)}.

Here

p_{i,j}=m_{i,j}+m_{i+1,j}+m_{i+2,j}+\ldots+m_{n,j}.

(6.42)

Let
\psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})
be the series

\psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=e^{2\pi\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\imath x_{i}\lambda_{i}-g(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{\rho}_{n})\right)}\sum\limits_{M\in M_{n}}c_{M}({\bm{\lambda}}_{n})\ e^{2\pi\sum\limits_{j<i}m_{ij}(x_{i}-x_{j})}.

(6.43)

The residue calculation shows that the function
\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})
is the symmetrization over spectral variables of the function
\psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})
.

Proposition 6.2
.

In the region
x_{1}>x_{2}>\ldots>x_{n}
we have

\Psi_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\psi_{\lambda_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,\lambda_{\sigma(n)}}(\bm{x}_{n}).

(6.44)

Correspondingly, Heckman–Opdam function
F_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})
in the region

(6.45)

This formula can be regarded as a rational counterpart of the Noumi–Shiraishi series
[
NS
]
.

Appendix A
Double sine function and its limits

A.1
Double sine function

The double sine function
S_{2}(z):=S_{2}(z|\bm{\omega})
, see
[
Ku
]
and references therein, is a meromorphic function that satisfies two functional relations

\frac{S_{2}(z)}{S_{2}(z+\omega_{1})}=2\sin\frac{\pi z}{\omega_{2}},\qquad\frac{S_{2}(z)}{S_{2}(z+\omega_{2})}=2\sin\frac{\pi z}{\omega_{1}}

(A.46)

and inversion relation

S_{2}(z)S_{2}(-z)=-4\sin\frac{\pi z}{\omega_{1}}\sin\frac{\pi z}{\omega_{2}},

(A.47)

or equivalently

S_{2}(z)S_{2}(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-z)=1.

(A.48)

The function
S_{2}(z)
has poles at the points

z=\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+m\omega_{1}+k\omega_{2},\qquad m,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

(A.49)

and zeros at

z=-m\omega_{1}-k\omega_{2},\qquad m,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

(A.50)

For
\omega_{1}/\omega_{2}\not\in\mathbb{Q}
all poles and zeros are simple.
In the region of analyticity
\mathrm{Re}\,z\in(0,\mathrm{Re}\,(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}))
we have the following integral representation for the logarithm of
S_{2}(z)

\displaystyle\log S_{2}(z)=\frac{\pi\imath}{2}B_{2,2}(z)+\int_{\mathbb{R}+\imath 0}\frac{e^{zt}}{(e^{\omega_{1}t}-1)(e^{\omega_{2}t}-1)}\frac{dt}{t},

(A.51)

where
B_{2,2}(z)
is the second order multiple Bernoulli polynomial

\displaystyle B_{2,2}(z)=\frac{1}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}\biggl(\Bigl(z-\frac{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}}{2}\Bigr)^{2}-\frac{\omega_{1}^{2}+\omega_{2}^{2}}{12}\biggr).

(A.52)

It is clear from this representation that the double sine function is homogeneous

S_{2}(\gamma z|\gamma\omega_{1},\gamma\omega_{2})=S_{2}(z|\omega_{1},\omega_{2}),\qquad\gamma\in(0,\infty)

(A.53)

and invariant under permutation of periods

S_{2}(z|\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=S_{2}(z|\omega_{2},\omega_{1}).

(A.54)

The double sine function can be expressed through the Barnes double gamma function
\Gamma_{2}(z|\bm{\omega})

[
B
]
,

S_{2}(z|\bm{\omega})=\Gamma_{2}(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-z|\bm{\omega})\Gamma_{2}^{-1}(z|\bm{\omega}),

(A.55)

and its properties follow from the corresponding properties of the double gamma function.

In the limit the double sine function reduces to the usual gamma function

\displaystyle S_{2}(z|\hat{\bm{\omega}})\sim\sqrt{2\pi}\,\biggl(\frac{\omega_{1}}{2\pi\omega_{2}}\biggr)^{\omega_{1}z-\frac{1}{2}}\,\Gamma^{-1}(\omega_{1}z),\qquad\omega_{2}\to 0^{+},

(A.56)

where
\hat{\bm{\omega}}=(\omega_{1}^{-1},\omega_{2}^{-1})
. Hence, we can compute nonrelativistic limits
of the dual weight and kernel functions from Section
2

\displaystyle\hat{w}^{R}(\lambda)=S_{2}(\imath\lambda|\hat{\bm{\omega}})\,S_{2}\Big(-\imath\lambda+\frac{\mathrm{g}}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}\,\Big|\hat{\bm{\omega}}\Big),

(A.57)

\displaystyle\hat{K}^{R}(\lambda)=S_{2}^{-1}\Bigl(\imath\lambda+\frac{\mathrm{g}}{2\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}\Big|\hat{\bm{\omega}}\Bigr)\,S_{2}^{-1}\Bigl(-\imath\lambda+\frac{\mathrm{g}}{2\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}\Big|\hat{\bm{\omega}}\Bigr).

(A.58)

Namely, setting
\omega_{1}=1
and
\mathrm{g}=g\omega_{2}
we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\hat{w}^{R}(\lambda)\sim\frac{2\pi}{(2\pi\omega_{2})^{g-1}}\,\Gamma^{-1}(\imath\lambda)\,\Gamma^{-1}(-\imath\lambda+g),\\[6.0pt]
&\hat{K}^{R}(\lambda)\sim\frac{(2\pi\omega_{2})^{g-1}}{2\pi}\,\Gamma\Bigl(\imath\lambda+\frac{g}{2}\Bigr)\,\Gamma\Bigl(-\imath\lambda+\frac{g}{2}\Bigr),\end{aligned}\qquad\omega_{2}\to 0^{+}.

(A.59)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial^{3}\beta_{g}(x)}{\partial x\partial g^{2}}&=xe^{gx}\,\frac{g(e^{x}-1)-e^{x}}{(e^{x}-1)^{2}}+\frac{e^{gx}}{e^{x}-1}\\

(A.97)

so that

(A.98)

We thus obtained that for

\begin{split}\frac{\partial^{3}\beta_{g}(x)}{\partial x\partial g^{2}}\geq 0,\qquad x<0.\end{split}

(A.99)

So

is monotone in
g
for
g\geq 1
,
x<0
.

In the case

\begin{split}\frac{\partial^{2}\beta_{g}(x)}{\partial x\partial g}\bigg|_{g=1}=\frac{1}{x^{2}}-\frac{1}{4\sh^{2}\frac{x}{2}}\geq 0\end{split}

(A.100)

since

. Hence, we obtain that

\begin{split}\frac{\partial^{2}\beta_{g}(x)}{\partial x\partial g}\geq\frac{\partial^{2}\beta_{g}(x)}{\partial x\partial g}\bigg|_{g=1}\geq 0,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ g\geq 1,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ x<0.\end{split}

(A.101)

Therefore,
\frac{\partial\beta_{g}(x)}{\partial x}
is monotone in
g
for
g\geq 1
and
x<0
.

Since
\beta_{1}(x)
is identically equal to zero, the derivative
\frac{\partial\beta_{1}(x)}{\partial x}
is also zero at any
x
,
which finally implies that

\beta_{g}(x)
is monotone for positive
x
for any
g\geq 1
.

By lemma assumption
z=\imath y\in\imath\mathbb{R}
and
|y|\geq\varepsilon
. Then

\int_{t\leq-1}\Omega=\int_{t\leq-1}\frac{\omega_{2}e^{\imath yt}dt}{1-e^{t}}\cdot\beta_{g}(t\omega_{2}).

(A.102)

Since

Appendix B
Asymptotics of wave function

The arguments presented here are similar to those used by M. Hallnäs and S. Ruijsenaars in
[
HR4
]
. We consider Mellin–Barnes integral representation (
4.90
) and shift the integration contour through the nearest poles. The residue term gives the asymptotics, while for the rest we find out the proper bounds.
The result of the contour shift is described by the following lemma.

Lemma B.1
.

Assume that
g>1
,
{\bm{\lambda}}_{n},\bm{x}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then we have the integral representation with shifted elementary contours

for

\begin{split}&\hat{\Psi}_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=e^{2\pi\imath x_{n}(\lambda_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{n})}\sum^{n-1}_{r=0}\hat{d}_{n-1-r}\int_{(\mathbb{R}+\imath\frac{g}{2}+\imath\varepsilon)^{n-1-r}}d\bm{t}_{n-1-r}\\[6.0pt]
&\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset{\{1,\ldots,n\}}\\
|J|=r\end{subarray}}\hat{\Psi}_{(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n-1-r};\{\lambda_{i}+\imath g/2\}_{i\in J})}(\bm{x}_{n-1}-x_{n}\bm{1})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\notin J\\

l=1,\ldots,n-1-r\end{subarray}}\hat{K}^{-1}(\lambda_{j}-t_{l}+\imath g).\end{split}

(B.1)

The proof is a direct residue calculation.

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma^{1-n}(g)}{(n-1)!}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\,\exp\biggl(2\pi\imath x_{n}\biggl(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\gamma_{i}\biggr)\biggr)

the form of the general expression

\displaystyle\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(0)=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(g)}{\Gamma(kg)}\,\prod_{1\leq i\not=j\leq n}\,\Gamma(\imath\lambda_{ij}+g)

C.55

We have

\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n+1}}(0)=\frac{\Gamma^{-n}(g)}{n!}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\,\Gamma\bigl(\imath\lambda_{i}-\imath\gamma_{j}+\frac{g}{2}\bigr)\,\Gamma\bigl(\imath\gamma_{j}-\imath\lambda_{i}+\frac{g}{2}\bigr)}{\prod_{1\leq i\not=j\leq n}\,\Gamma(\imath\gamma_{i}-\imath\gamma_{j})\Gamma(\imath\gamma_{j}-\imath\gamma_{i}+g)}\,\Psi_{\bm{\gamma}_{n}}(0)\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{d\gamma_{k}}{2\pi}\\[6.0pt]
=\Gamma^{-n}(g)\,\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(g)}{\Gamma(kg)}\,\frac{1}{n!}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\,\Gamma\bigl(\imath\lambda_{i}-\imath\gamma_{j}+\frac{g}{2}\bigr)\,\Gamma\bigl(\imath\gamma_{j}-\imath\lambda_{i}+\frac{g}{2}\bigr)}{\prod_{1\leq i\not=j\leq n}\,\Gamma(\imath\gamma_{i}-\imath\gamma_{j})}\,\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{d\gamma_{k}}{2\pi}\\[6.0pt]
=\Gamma^{-n}(g)\,\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(g)}{\Gamma(kg)}\,\frac{\Gamma^{n+1}(g)\,\prod_{1\leq i\not=j\leq n+1}\,\Gamma(\imath\lambda_{ij}+g)}{\Gamma((n+1)g)}=\prod_{k=1}^{n+1}\frac{\Gamma(g)}{\Gamma(kg)}\,\prod_{1\leq i\not=j\leq n+1}\,\Gamma(\imath\lambda_{ij}+g),

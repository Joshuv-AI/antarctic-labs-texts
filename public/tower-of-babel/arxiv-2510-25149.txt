Extending degree- 2 2 Azumaya algebras with C 2 C_{2} -actions and examples from character varieties of knot groups Thanks: We acknowledge the support of the Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada (NSERC), RGPIN-2021-02603.
Justin Lawrence, Nicholas Rouse, Ben Williams

Abstract
. We give criteria to determine when a degree- 2 2 Azumaya algebra with C 2 C_{2} -action over a dense open subvariety of a curve extends to the entire curve as an algebra with C 2 C_{2} -action. These consist of conditions for the extension of the algebra, combined with a new condition for the extension of the algebra with the action. The new condition is testable by computer algebra systems, and we explain how the result applies to the canonical components of the character varieties of certain hyperbolic knots with order- 2 2 symmetries. We conclude by carrying out the calculations for different symmetries of the Figure- 8 8 knot.

Let
W\subseteq Y
denote a scheme and a dense open subscheme. If
\mathcal{A}
is an Azumaya algbera, i.e., an étale sheaf of matrix algebras, defined over
W
, one may ask whether
\mathcal{A}
extends to an Azumaya algebra over
Y
. This question arises in the study of the
\mathrm{SL}(2)
-character variety of a hyperbolic knot group

, as detailed in
[
4
]
. In this application,

is a canonical component of the character variety, and is therefore a
1
-dimensional curve defined over a number field. The subvariety
W
is the dense open subvariety where the representation is irreducible, and the algebra
\mathcal{A}
over
W
is tautologically defined. There is a deep, and mysterious, conjecture
[
4

that the algebra
\mathcal{A}
extends if and only if the knot in question is not an

-space knot.

In general, canonical components of character varieties of hyperbolic knot groups are complicated entities, being curves of high degree. In
[
3
]
, the authors exploit the symmetry of symmetric (in this case, freely periodic) hyperbolic knots to make deductions about the canonical components of the character varieties of the knot groups. We hope that, in general, character varieties of symmetric knots may be more tractable by virtue of the symmetry of the knot.

This paper considers the following situation: suppose as before that
W\subseteq Y
denotes a scheme and a dense open subscheme, and that
\mathcal{A}
is an Azumaya algebra of degree
2
over

. Suppose further that
\mathcal{A}
carries a nontrivial action by

, the cyclic group of order

, i.e., there is a nontrivial order-

of algebras over

-action over all of

We then give example calculations where

-action and

over

1.
The structure group

Let
W
be a scheme. A
degree-
2
Azumaya algebra
(see
[
7
, §5]
) on
W
is a sheaf of algebras
\mathcal{A}
that is locally isomorphic in the étale topology to
\operatorname{Mat}_{2\times 2}(\mathcal{O}_{W})
: that is, for every
w\in W
, there exists an open
U\ni w
and a finite étale map
U^{\prime}\to U\subseteq W
for which the pullback of
\mathcal{A}
to
U^{\prime}
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{Mat}_{2\times 2}(\mathcal{O}_{U^{\prime}})
. In this circumstance, one says
U^{\prime}
is an
étale neighbourhood
of
w
.

A
C_{2}
-action
on a degree-
2
Azumaya algebra
\mathcal{A}
is a map
g:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
of sheaves of algebras on
W
for which
g^{2}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{A}}
. We emphasize that
g
preserves the order of multiplication, and is therefore not an involution of Azumaya algebras.

Remark 1.1
.

In our
C_{2}
-action, the underlying scheme
W
is fixed. One can give a more general definition, in which
W
itself has a
C_{2}
-action and
\mathcal{A}
has a compatible action. This is much more complicated, and not considered further here.

An Azumaya algebra over
W
is necessarily a coherent sheaf. If
R
is a ring, then an Azumaya algebra
\mathcal{A}
on
\Spec R
determines and is determined by the
R
-algebra of global sections. In a standard abuse of notation, we write
\mathcal{A}
for this
R
-algebra.

In
[
7
, Thm. 5.1]
, a number of different characterizations of Azumaya algebras over schemes are given. In the case of an affine scheme
\Spec R
, one such characterization amounts to the following:
\mathcal{A}
is an
R
-algebra that is finitely generated and projective as an
R
-module, and for which the canonical map
\mathcal{A}\otimes_{R}\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\operatorname{End}_{R}(\mathcal{A})
is an isomorphism. This is the definition used by
[
2
]
.

In the case where
R=F
is a field, an Azumaya algebra over
R
is a central simple algebra over
F
and
vice versa
. In particular, a degree-
2
Azumaya algebra
A
over a field
F
is either a
4
-dimensional central division algebra over
F
(the nonsplit case), or is isomorphic to
\operatorname{Mat}_{2\times 2}(F)
(the split case). In either case, we will call
A
a
quaternion algebra
over
F
.

Example 1.2
.

We define
J=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}
. Over any scheme
W
, there is a trivial degree-
2
Azumaya algebra
\operatorname{Mat}_{2\times 2}(\mathcal{O}_{W})
, which can be endowed with the
C_{2}
-action
\iota
given by conjugating by
J
.

Our first observation is that all nontrivial
C_{2}
-actions on degree-
2
Azumaya algebras are étale locally isomorphic to that of Example
1.2
.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
W
be a connected scheme on which
2
is invertible. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a degree-
2
Azumaya algebra over
W
and
g:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
a nontrivial order-
2
isomorphism of
\mathcal{A}
. Locally in the étale topology, the algebra with
C_{2}
-action
(\mathcal{A},g)
is isomorphic to
(\operatorname{Mat}_{2\times 2}(\mathcal{O}_{W}),\iota)
.

Proof.

First we make a preliminary observation about nontriviality of the action. Since
2
is invertible, we may decompose
\mathcal{A}
as a locally free sheaf
\mathcal{A}_{+}\oplus\mathcal{A}_{-}
, where
g
acts as
\pm 1
on
\mathcal{A}_{\pm}
. Since
W
is connected , the ranks of the locally free sheaves
\mathcal{A}_{+}
and
\mathcal{A}_{-}
are constant. In particular, this implies that
g
acts nontrivially on every stalk of
\mathcal{A}
.

Let
w\in W
be a point. We will prove that there is an étale neighbourhood
U^{\prime}
of
w
such that the pullback of
(\mathcal{A},g)
to
U^{\prime}
is isomorphic as an algebra with automorphism to
\operatorname{Mat}_{2\times 2}(\mathcal{O}_{U^{\prime}},\iota)
. We may suppose already that we have passed to an étale neighbourhood of
w
over which
\mathcal{A}
is isomorphic to a matrix algebra. That is to say, we may safely suppose
\mathcal{A}=\operatorname{Mat}_{2\times 2}(\mathcal{O}_{W})
.

Let
A
denote the pullback of
\mathcal{A}
to the (spectrum of the) residue field
\kappa(w)
of
w
. The Skolem–Noether theorem tells us that the automorphism induced by
g
on
A
is given by conjugation by some element of
\mathrm{GL}(2;\kappa(w))
. This matrix is actually defined in some subring of
\kappa(w)
that defines an affine open neighbourhood of
w
. That is, by passing to some neighbourhood
U_{0}
of
w
, we may suppose that the automorphism
g
is given by conjugation by some invertible matrix
C\in GL(2;\mathcal{O}_{U_{0}})
. Since
g^{2}=\mathrm{id}
, it must be the case that
C^{2}=dI_{2}
where
d\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{U_{0}}
. Working étale-locally we can assume that
d
has a square-root on some étale neighbourhood
U_{1}
of
w
, and so replace
C
by a matrix satisfying
C^{2}=I_{2}
, but where
C\neq I_{2}
in each stalk. In a neighbourhood of
w
, the matrix
C
may be brought to rational canonical form, which must be
C=J
as required.
∎

Proposition

\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\

c&d\end{bmatrix}

and

and

. The linear algebraic group

Being disconnected,

]

[

]
, and so

over

meets the condition of

for some integer

may be embedded as a closed subgroup in
\mathrm{GL}(4)
, and it is reductive since we assume the characteristic of

is not

\mathrm{GL}(4)\to\mathrm{GL}(4)/Q

-bundle now follows from
[
9
, Prop. 0.9 & Amplification 1.3]

.

The following lemma is an amalgamation of results of Auslander and Goldman. We state it here for ease of reference.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
R
is a discrete valuation ring (DVR) and
F
is its field of fractions. If
A
is a central simple algebra of degree

over
F
such that the Brauer class of
A
is in the image of
\Br(R)\to\Br(F)
, then every maximal order in

is Azumaya over

.

Proof.

Choose a maximal
R
-order

for
A
. It is the case that
O
is a projective
R
-module by
[
2
, Prop. 1.3 and the Corollary to Prop. 4.7]

It remains to show that

is central and separable over
R
. The Brauer group condition on
[A]
assures us that there exists some algebra
B
that is central and separable over
R
and for which
B\otimes_{R}F
is isomorphic to
A\otimes_{F}\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times n}(F)
for some integer

. Choose an identification
B\otimes_{R}F=A\otimes_{K}\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times n}(F)
. This makes

into a maximal order in

, and
\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times n}(O)
is also a maximal order in this

-algebra (see
[
2
, Thm 3.6]
), so that
[
2
, Prop. 3.5]
tells us these orders are conjugate. Then
[

, Thm 3.5]
implies that
O
is central and separable over
R
, completing the proof.
∎

3.1.
Interpreting the tautological algebra

We attempt to explain the sense in which

along

on

is a subset of
\operatorname{Mat}_{2\times 2}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})
, we may view
q^{*}(\mathcal{A})
as the sheaf of matrix algebras in which the representations of
\Gamma
take values.

A point

is a conjugacy class
[\phi]
of representations
\Gamma\to\mathrm{SL}(2;\bar{\mathbb{Q}})
. The pullback

is a sheaf of algebras over
\Spec\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
, i.e., an algebra. For any

, there exists a lift
\tilde{w}
making the following diagram commute:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Spec\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{w}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces w}

We may identify

and obtain an isomorphism

3.2.
The Algebra over the Fraction Field

Our main theorem, Theorem

, requires us to know something about the restriction of an Azumaya algebra

to the fraction field of the variety over which it is defined. In this section, we study this algebra.

We continue to assume that

and that the tautological algebra
\mathcal{A}
is defined over

denote the fraction field of

for the restriction of

to

. The algebra

is an Azumaya algebra of degree
2
over the field

, but

is nonsplit in all the examples considered in this paper.

A quaternion algebra
A
over a field

of characteristic different from

admits a presentation as a

, i.e., a fixed isomorphism with an algebra determined by a Hilbert symbol:

A\cong\left(\frac{a,b}{F}\right),\quad a,b\in F^{\times}.

This means that there are elements
i,j\in A
satisfying
i^{2}=a
,
j^{2}=b
and
ij=-ji
. We refer to
[
13
]
for general background on such algebras. A number of features of
A
, notably the reduced norm and reduced trace, are easily computed when
A
is presented as a symbol algebra. We shall give formulas for presenting the algebras that arise in this paper as symbol algebras.

Since
A
is a degree-
2
Azumaya algebra over a field, it carries a standard
involution:
\bar{x}=\operatorname{Trd}(x)1_{A}-x
. In these terms,
\operatorname{Nrd}(x)1_{A}=x\bar{x}
.
We may define an
F

\langle x,y\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Trd}(\bar{x}y)=\frac{1}{2}(\bar{x}y+\bar{y}x),

which is immediately seen to be symmetric.

We will refer below to

A
. These are elements for which
\bar{x}=-x
, so that
\operatorname{Trd}(x)=0
. For such an element,
x^{2}+\operatorname{Nrd}(x)1_{A}=0
, so that

. Two traceless elements are orthogonal if and only if they anticommute, since we can write

xy+yx=-\bar{x}y-\bar{y}x=2\langle x,y\rangle

under the assumption that the traces are
0
.

If
\gamma\in\Gamma
is a group element, then

was defined in Notation

. The global section

yields an element of

for this element in an abuse of notation. The coordinate ring of

, which contains the element

, is a subring of

is also an element of

.

S^{3}
. For the group
\Gamma

1
or
2

1

We take
X(\Gamma)_{0}
to be such a canonical component, which is so named because it contains the class of a lift
\phi:\Gamma\to\mathrm{SL}(2;\mathbb{C})
of the canonical representation of
\Gamma
as deck transformations of the universal cover
\widetilde{S^{3}\setminus K}\to S^{3}\setminus K
. The deck-transformation representation
\Gamma\to\Isom(\widetilde{S^{3}\setminus K})
amounts to an irreducible faithful representation
\Gamma\to\mathrm{PSL}(2;\mathbb{C})=\mathrm{PGL}(2;\mathbb{C})
, once an isomorphism of
\widetilde{S^{3}\setminus K}
with oriented hyperbolic

\phi
is actually defined over
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}

contains the class

of an absolutely irreducible faithful representation of
\Gamma

The variety

is smooth over
\mathbb{C}
, but
X(\Gamma)_{0}

X(\Gamma)_{0}
by

The

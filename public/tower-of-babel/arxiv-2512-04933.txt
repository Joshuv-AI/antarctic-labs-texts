Next-order asymptotics for the volume of Schatten balls
Mathias Sonnleitner

Abstract
. The volume of the unit balls of self-adjoint finite-dimensional Schatten p p -classes of n × n n\times n -matrices, 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞ 1\leq p\leq\infty , is only known exactly for p = 2 p=2 and p = ∞ p=\infty . We give an asymptotic expansion of the logarithmic volume to order o ⁡ ( n ) o(n) for general p ≥ 3 2 p\geq\frac{3}{2} . The proof rests on asymptotics for the partition function of β \beta -ensembles due to Leblé and Serfaty [Invent. Math. 210(3):645–757, 2017]. Independently, the case p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 was obtained by Dworaczek Guera, Memin and Pain [arXiv:2511.05386]. In the complex case the asymptotic expansion is continued to order O ⁡ ( 1 ) O(1) for all p ≥ 1 p\geq 1 .

1.
Introduction

The Schatten or Schatten-von Neumann class consists of all compact operators between two Hilbert spaces whose sequence of singular values belongs to the Lebesgue sequence space
\ell_{p}
. It can be seen a non-commutative version of
\ell_{p}
and is named after Schatten and von Neumann
Sch46
;
Sch50
;
SVN46
. We refer to
Pie07
;
PX03
for an overview of their role in functional analysis and Banach space geometry.

In this article, we study the geometry of finite-dimensional Schatten classes and in particular their unit balls. These have been investigated as test cases for conjectures in asymptotic geometric analysis
DFG+23
;
GMP25
;
GP07
;
KMP98
;
RV20
, in the context of low-rank matrix recovery and information-based complexity
CR12
;
CK15
;
HPV17
;
HPV21
;
PS22
or in quantum information theory
ASW11
;
Wil13
. Let
\mathbb{F}_{1}=\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{F}_{2}=\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{F}_{4}=\mathbb{H}
and denote the singular values of a matrix
A\in\mathbb{F}_{\beta}^{n\times n}
, where
\beta\in\{1,2,4\}
, by

s_{1}(A)\geq\cdots\geq s_{n}(A)\geq 0.

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, the
p
-Schatten norm of
A
is

\|A\|_{p}=\begin{cases}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}s_{j}(A)^{p}\Big)^{1/p}&\colon p<\infty\\
s_{1}(A)&\colon p=\infty.\end{cases}

The associated unit ball and its intersection with the subspace of self-adjoint matrices
\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{\beta})=\{A\in\mathbb{F}_{\beta}^{n\times n}\colon A^{*}=A\}
are denoted by

B_{p,\beta}^{n}=\{A\in\mathbb{F}_{\beta}^{n\times n}\colon\|A\|_{p}\leq 1\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n}=B_{p,\beta}^{n}\cap\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{\beta}).

For self-adjoint matrices the Schatten
p
-norm
\|A\|_{p}
becomes a
p
-norm of eigenvalues. In case of non-self adjoint matrices one can also study the rectangular case, see e.g.
JKP24
.

We shall identify
\mathbb{F}_{\beta}^{n\times n}
with
\mathbb{R}^{\beta n^{2}}
and
\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{\beta})
with
\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}}
, where

\displaystyle d_{n}:=\beta\frac{n(n-1)}{2}+n.

(1)

Then
B_{p,\beta}^{n}
and
\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n}
are convex bodies (i.e. compact convex sets with nonempty interior) in
\mathbb{R}^{\beta n^{2}}
and
\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}}
, respectively. In the following, we study their volumes
\mathrm{vol}(B_{p,\beta}^{n})
and
\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n})
. Our aim is to give more precise asymptotics of the volume of
\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n}
as
n\to\infty
.

The exact volume of
B_{p,\beta}^{n}
and
\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n}
is only known in the cases
p=2
and
p=\infty
. For
p=2
, the Schatten
2
-balls are Euclidean unit balls of corresponding dimension and thus

\mathrm{vol}(B_{2,\beta}^{n})=\frac{\pi^{\beta n^{2}/2}}{\Gamma(1+\frac{\beta n^{2}}{2})}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{B}_{2,\beta}^{n})=\frac{\pi^{d_{n}/2}}{\Gamma(1+\frac{d_{n}}{2})},

where
\Gamma(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{x-1}e^{-t}\mathrm{d}t
denotes the Gamma function. For
p=\infty
it follows from Saint Raymond’s work
SR84
and Selberg’s integral formula
And91
;
Sel44
that

\mathrm{vol}(B_{\infty,\beta}^{n})=\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\Gamma(1+j\frac{\beta}{2})}{\prod_{j=n}^{2n-1}\Gamma(1+j\frac{\beta}{2})}\pi^{\beta n^{2}/2},

(2)

and in the self-adjoint case it holds that

\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{B}_{\infty,\beta}^{n})=2^{d_{n}}(2\pi)^{\beta n(n-1)/4}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{\Gamma(1+j\frac{\beta}{2})^{2}\Gamma((j+1)\frac{\beta}{2})}{\Gamma(2+(n+j-1)\frac{\beta}{2})\Gamma(j\frac{\beta}{2})}.

(3)

For general
1\leq p\leq\infty
, exact volumes are unknown and instead their asymptotic behavior has been studied. Saint Raymond
SR84
derived
A(p)>0
such that, for
\beta\in\{1,2\}
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{1/2+1/p}\mathrm{vol}(B_{p,\beta}^{n})^{1/\beta n^{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\beta}}e^{3/4}A(p)

(4)

and determined
A(2)=e^{-1/4}
and
A(\infty)=\frac{1}{2}
. Guédon and Paouris
GP07
showed that
\mathrm{vol}(B_{p,4}^{n})^{1/4n^{2}}
and
\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n})^{1/d_{n}},\beta\in\{1,2,4\}
are of the same order
n^{-1/2-1/p}
. Kabluchko, Prochno and Thäle
KPT20a
determined

A(p)=\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\frac{p\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\frac{p}{2})}{\sqrt{e}\Gamma(\frac{p+1}{2})}\bigg)^{1/p}

(5)

and moreover showed in
KPT20b
that, for
\beta\in\{1,2,4\}
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{1/2+1/p}\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n})^{1/d_{n}}=\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{\beta}}e^{3/4}A(p).

(6)

We remark that one way to see that
A(p)
is decreasing in
p
is to observe that the same is true for the normalized volume
\mathrm{vol}(n^{1/p}\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n})
. Recently, Dadoun, Fradelizi, Guédon and Zitt
(
DFG+23
, Remark 4.8)
showed (
4
) for
\beta=4
. Thus, the asymptotics in (
4
) and (
6
) hold in all cases
\beta\in\{1,2,4\}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

In case of
p=\infty
, the quantity
A(\infty)=\frac{1}{2}
can be expressed in terms of the minimal logarithmic energy of probability measures supported in
[-1,1]
attained by the arcsine distribution, see
ST97
. We refer to
BDF+24
and
Bra24
for related asymptotics for discrete minimal logarithmic energy.

In case of
p<\infty
, the quantity
A(p)
in (
5
) can be expressed as

A(p)=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{p\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\frac{p}{2})}{\sqrt{e}\Gamma(\frac{p+1}{2})}\Big)^{1/p}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{e}\alpha_{p}}\Big)^{1/p},

where
\alpha_{p}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|x|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}(x)
is the
p
-th absolute moment of the Ullman distribution
\mu_{p}
which has density

f_{p}(x)=\frac{p}{\pi}\int_{|x|}^{1}\frac{t^{p-1}}{\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}}\mathrm{d}t,\qquad x\in[-1,1].

(7)

Our main contribution is the following asymptotic expansion for the logarithmic volume
\ln\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n})
. Recall that the differential entropy of a probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
is

\mathrm{Ent}(\mu)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\ln(f(x))f(x)\mathrm{d}x,

whenever
\mu
admits a Lebesgue density
f
and
\mathrm{Ent}(\mu)=-\infty
otherwise.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\beta\in\{1,2,4\}
. If
p\geq\frac{3}{2}
, then, as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\ln\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n})=

\displaystyle-\frac{\beta}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{p}\Big)n^{2}\ln n

\displaystyle+\frac{\beta}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{4\pi}{\beta}+\frac{3}{4}+\ln A(p)\Big)n^{2}

\displaystyle-\Big(1-\frac{\beta}{2}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{p}\Big)n\ln n

\displaystyle+\Big(1-\frac{\beta}{2}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{4}{\beta\pi}+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2p}+\ln A(p)+\mathrm{Ent}(\mu_{p})\Big)n+o(n).

(8)

Moreover, if
\beta=2
and
p\geq 1
, then, for some constant
M_{p}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\ln\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{B}_{p,2}^{n})=-\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{p}\Big)n^{2}\ln n+\Big(\frac{1}{2}\ln 2\pi+\frac{3}{4}+\ln A(p)\Big)n^{2}-\ln n+M_{p}+o(1).

(9)

In case of
p=2
it is known that
\mathrm{Ent}(\mu_{2})=\ln\pi-\frac{1}{2}
where
\mu_{2}
is the semi-circle law. Otherwise the entropy
\mathrm{Ent}(\mu_{p})
of the Ullman distribution appears to be unknown. Independently, the expansion in (
1
) was recently obtained by Dworaczek Guera, Memin and Pain
GMP25
in case of
p\geq 2
.

The proof of Theorem
1
relies on the fact that the volume of
\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n}
can be expressed in terms of the integral

Z_{n,p,\beta}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{\beta}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}n\,v_{p}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|^{p}}\mathrm{d}x,

(10)

where

v_{p}=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\frac{p}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{p+1}{2})}=\frac{1}{p\alpha_{p}}.

The quantity
Z_{n,p,\beta}
is the partition function of a one-dimensional
\beta
-ensemble with potential
V(x)=v_{p}|x|^{p}
, where eigenvalues/particles are distributed with joint density

f_{n,p,\beta}(x)=\frac{1}{Z_{n,p,\beta}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{\beta}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}n\,v_{p}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|^{p}}\mathrm{d}x,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(11)

The parameter
\beta
stands for the inverse temperature and may take any positive value, while for the matrix representation we restrict to
\beta\in\{1,2,4\}
. We refer to
AGZ10
and
For10
for more information on
\beta
-ensembles.

In this context the Ullman distribution arises as the corresponding equilibrium measure minimizing the functional

\mu\mapsto I_{p}(\mu)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\ln|x-y|\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\mathrm{d}\mu(y)+v_{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|x|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

over
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
, where
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
denotes the space of probability measures on
\mathbb{R}
. More precisely, if
X=(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
is distributed according to the density
f_{n,p,\beta}
, then the random empirical measure
\mu_{n,p}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{X_{i}}
tends to
\mu_{p}
as
n\to\infty
. Moreover, large deviation principles due to
BG97
and
HP00
yield

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{2}{\beta n^{2}}\ln Z_{n,p,\beta}=I_{p}(\mu_{p})=\ln 2+\frac{3}{2p}.

(12)

Earlier work
DPS95
;
Joh98
also identified the limiting constant but with different techniques. In
DFG+23
, the limit (
12
) is used to prove (
6
). The large deviation principle for tagged empirical fields due to Leblé and Serfaty
LS17
allows to refine (
12
). The following result forms the core of the proof of (
1
) in Theorem
1
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\beta>0
and
p\geq\frac{3}{2}
. As
n\to\infty
,

\ln Z_{n,p,\beta}=-\frac{\beta}{2}n^{2}I_{p}(\mu_{p})+\frac{\beta}{2}n\ln n-C(\beta)n+\Big(1-\frac{\beta}{2}\Big)\mathrm{Ent}(\mu_{p})n+o(n),

where

C(\beta)=\Big(1-\frac{\beta}{2}\Big)\ln\frac{\beta}{2}-\frac{\beta}{2}\ln 2\pi+\frac{\beta}{2}+\ln\Gamma\Big(\frac{\beta}{2}\Big).

(13)

Independently, in the recent work
GMP25
the authors deduced the statement of Theorem
2
in case of
p\geq 2
from a central limit theorem for linear eigenvalue statistics. The restriction to
p\geq 2
results from regularity requirements of the underlying approach, see also
DFG+23
. The slightly milder restriction on
p
in Theorem
2
is due to the fact that the Ullman density
f_{p}
is Hölder continuous of order
\frac{1}{2}
if and only if
p\geq\frac{3}{2}
. This is required to apply the results in
LS17
.

The term
\mathrm{Ent}(\mu_{p})
in Theorem
2
arises via a rescaling property of the specific entropy which is part of the rate function in the large deviation principle in
LS17
. In case of
\beta=2
, the contribution of
\mathrm{Ent}(\mu_{p})
vanishes and more precise asymptotics hold. The following is a consequence of
(
CKM23
, Prop. 1.1)
which is based on
KM99
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
p\geq 1
. There exists
M_{p}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
such that, as
n\to\infty
,

\ln Z_{n,p,2}=-n^{2}I_{p}(\mu_{p})+n\ln n+(\ln 2\pi-1)n+\frac{5}{12}\ln n+M_{p}^{\prime}+o(1).

Theorem
3
combined with Lemma
8
and Lemma
10
below yields (
9
) in Theorem
1
. In case of
p
an even integer, the potential
V(x)=v_{p}|x|^{p}
is an analytic function and results of
BG13
yield a full asymptotic expansion of
\ln Z_{n,p,\beta}
, and consequently of
\ln\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n})
. In case of
p=2
the integral
Z_{n,2,\beta}
is known via a Mehta-Selberg integral. For
p\neq 2
the coefficients in the full asymptotic expansion are defined in terms of integrals of the Stieltjes transform of
\mu_{p}
, see also
(
BG24
, Section 7)
and
Shc13
;
Shc14
.

In the case of non self-adjoint matrices, the volume of the unit ball
B_{p,\beta}^{n}
may be written in terms of

\tilde{Z}_{n,p,\beta}=\int_{(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{n}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{\beta}\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{\beta/2-1}e^{-\beta n\,v_{p}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{p/2}}\mathrm{d}x,

see e.g.
(
DFG+23
, Sec. 4.2)
. This corresponds to the partition function of a Laguerre
\beta
-ensemble with potential
V(x)=v_{p}|x|^{p/2}
. The leading order asymptotics for
\tilde{Z}_{n,p,\beta}
can again be obtained via large deviations, leading to (
4
). However, the results in
LS17
do not apply and it goes beyond the scope of the present work to derive next-order asymptotics for
\tilde{Z}_{n,p,\beta}
and
B_{p,\beta}^{n}
.

Remark 4
.

The unit balls
B_{p,\beta}^{n}
and
\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n}
can also be studied for
0<p<1
. Then (
4
) and (
6
) as well as Theorem
3
continue to hold. However, even neglecting regularity assumptions, the proof of Theorem
1
and the underlying large deviation principle in
LS17
are limited to
p\geq 1
. This is because, while the microscopic limit point process in the bulk is the universal sine
2
-process for
p\geq 1
, it becomes dependent on
p
for
p<1
, see
BEY14
;
CKM23
. Since the minimizer of the rate function is the free energy which is uniquely given by the sine
β
-process, see
EHL21
;
HM25
, the large deviation principle in
LS17
cannot hold in its present form if
p<1
.

Remark 5
.

Heuristically, the unit balls
B_{p,\beta}^{n}
and
\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n}
are non-commutative versions of
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|^{p}\leq 1\}
. To compare, note that

\mathrm{vol}(n^{1/p}\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n})=n^{n/p}\frac{2^{n}\Gamma(1+\frac{1}{p})^{n}}{\Gamma(1+\frac{n}{p})}=\frac{1+o(1)}{\sqrt{2\pi n/p}}e^{\mathrm{Ent}(\nu_{p})n},

where
\nu_{p}
has density proportional to
e^{-|x|^{p}/p}
and maximizes entropy over
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
with
\int|x|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\leq 1
, see also
KP21
. Similarly, the Ullman distribution
\mu_{p}
maximizes free entropy over
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
with
\int|x|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\leq\alpha_{p}
, see
(
HP00
, Prop. 5.34)
. In case of
p=\infty
, one can interpret
\nu_{\infty}
as the uniform distribution on
[-1,1]
, which maximizes entropy, and
\mu_{\infty}
as the arcsine distribution, which maximizes free entropy. Formally, see Lemma
8
below, the case
\beta=0
corresponds to the commutative case where a Poisson point process appears in the microscopic limit, see also
AD14
;
BP15
;
Leb16
.

Remark 6
.

Theorem
1
can be combined with the stochastic representation in [Cor. 4.3]KPT20b of a random matrix drawn uniformly from a Schatten ball
\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n}
to obtain information on critical intersections of Schatten
p
-balls in the spirit of
KPT20b
and
ST25
.

2.
Proofs

In the following, we provide the proofs of Theorems
1
and
2
. First, in Lemma
7
we prove Hölder regularity of the Ullman density. Then Theorem
2
is a direct consequence of
LS17
. Subsequently, in Lemma
8
we recall a Weyl type integration formula providing the link between
\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n})
and
Z_{n,p,\beta}
. Finally, we deduce Theorem
1
from Theorem
2
with the help of a known asymptotic expansion in Lemma
10
.

Corollary 1.1 in
LS17
states that the asymptotics in Theorem
2
hold, i.e.

\ln Z_{n,p,\beta}=-\frac{\beta}{2}n^{2}I_{p}(\mu_{p})+\frac{\beta}{2}n\ln n-C(\beta)n+\Big(1-\frac{\beta}{2}\Big)\mathrm{Ent}(\mu_{p})n+o(n),

(14)

provided that the function
V(x)=v_{p}|x|^{p}
satisfies certain assumptions (H1)-(H5). These involve regularity of the density of the equilibrium measure which in this case is the Ullman density

f_{p}(x)=\frac{p}{\pi}\int_{|x|}^{1}\frac{t^{p-1}}{\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}}\mathrm{d}t,\qquad x\in[-1,1].

It is known from [Thm. 1.34]DKM98 that if
V\in C^{3}
, that is if
p\geq 3
, then the density of the equilibrium measure is Hölder continuous of order
\frac{1}{2}
and behaves like
\sqrt{1-|x|}
near the boundary of its support. To include smaller values of
p
, we verify the following statement directly.

Lemma 7
.

Let
p\geq\frac{3}{2}
. Then
f_{p}
is Hölder continuous of order
\frac{1}{2}
on
[-1,1]
and there exist positive constants
c
and
C
such that

c\sqrt{1-|x|}\leq f_{p}(x)\leq C\sqrt{1-|x|}\qquad\text{for}\qquad|x|\geq\frac{1}{2}.

(15)

Proof.

First, we verify the behavior of
f_{p}
near the boundary points. By a change of variables, for
x\in(-1,1)\setminus\{0\}
,

\frac{\pi}{p}f_{p}(x)=\int_{|x|}^{1}\frac{t^{p-1}}{\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}}\mathrm{d}t=|x|^{p-1}\int_{0}^{\arccos|x|}\frac{1}{\cos^{p}\alpha}\mathrm{d}\alpha.

(16)

Combining (
16
) with the asymptotics
\arccos|x|=\sqrt{2(1-|x|)}+o(1-|x|)
as
|x|\to 1
proves (
15
) which in turn implies that
f_{p}
is Hölder continuous of order
\frac{1}{2}
at the boundary points
\pm 1
.

Next, we show that
f_{p}
is in fact continuously differentiable at
x\in(-1,1)\setminus\{0\}
. For symmetry reasons it is enough to consider
x\in(0,1)
. Let
0<|h|<\min\{x,1-x\}
and write

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{p}\big(f_{p}(x+h)-f_{p}(x)\big)=

\displaystyle\big((x+h)^{p-1}-x^{p-1}\big)\int_{0}^{\arccos x}\frac{1}{\cos^{p}\alpha}\mathrm{d}\alpha

\displaystyle-(x+h)^{p-1}\int_{\arccos(x+h)}^{\arccos x}\frac{1}{\cos^{p}\alpha}\mathrm{d}\alpha.

Using
\arccos(x+h)=\arccos x-\frac{h}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}+o(h)
and letting
h\to 0
shows that
f_{p}
is continuously differentiable at
x
with derivative

f_{p}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{p-1}{x}f_{p}(x)-\frac{p}{\pi}\frac{1}{x\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}.

It remains to check Hölder continuity at zero. Let
h\in(0,1)
and write

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{p}\big(f_{p}(h)-f_{p}(0)\big)

\displaystyle=\int_{h}^{1}\frac{t^{p-1}}{\sqrt{t^{2}-h^{2}}}\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{1}t^{p-2}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{h}^{1}t^{p-1}\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{2}-h^{2}}}-\frac{1}{t}\Big)\mathrm{d}t-\frac{h^{p-1}}{p-1}.

The latter integral can be estimated by

\displaystyle\int_{h}^{1}t^{p-1}\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{2}-h^{2}}}-\frac{1}{t}\Big)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq h^{2}\int_{h}^{1}\frac{t^{p-3}}{\sqrt{t^{2}-h^{2}}}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=h^{p-1}\int_{1}^{1/h}\frac{u^{p-3}}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}}\mathrm{d}u.

In particular, if
1<p<2
, then

0\leq\frac{\pi}{p}\frac{f_{p}(h)-f_{p}(0)}{h^{p-1}}\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{u^{p-3}}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}}\mathrm{d}u<\infty.

(17)

Thus, in this case we obtain that
f_{p}
is Hölder continuous of order
p-1
at
0
. For
p\geq 2
, a similar argument shows that
f_{p}
is continuously differentiable at
0
with
f_{p}^{\prime}(0)=0
. In conclusion, if
p\geq\frac{3}{2}
, the function
f_{p}
is Hölder continuous of order
\frac{1}{2}
on
[-1,1]
. This completes the proof.
∎

We can now deduce Theorem
2
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Let
p\geq\frac{3}{2}
. In order to apply
(
LS17
, Cor. 1.1)
to
V(x)=v_{p}|x|^{p}
, we verify assumptions (H1)-(H5) there. The function
V
is lower semi-continuous, bounded from below, everywhere finite and grows polynomially as
|x|\to\infty
. Thus, assumptions (H1) and (H2) in
LS17
are satisfied. By Lemma
7
the density
f_{p}
of the equilibrium measure
\mu_{p}
is Hölder continuous of order
\frac{1}{2}
on its support
\Sigma=[-1,1]
. Thus, assumption (H3) is satisfied. For assumption (H4) we note that
\Sigma
is connected and its boundary is given by
\{-1,1\}
. By Lemma
7
, the density
f_{p}
behaves like
c\sqrt{1-|x|}
in neighborhoods of the boundary points. Thus, assumption (H4) is satisfied. Assumption (H5) is satisfied since
x\mapsto\exp(-c(|x|^{p}-\ln|x|))
is integrable on
\mathbb{R}
for every
c>0
. Thus, we can apply
(
LS17
, Cor. 1.1)
to obtain (
14
). Finally, the constant
C(\beta)
can be obtained from the known full expansion of the partition function in the Gaussian case
p=2
, see e.g.
(
BG24
, Sec. 7)
.
∎

For the proof of Theorem
1
we provide the following well-known link between Schatten
p
-balls and
\beta
-ensembles. It is a combination of a Weyl type integration formula, see
AGZ10
;
SR84
, with a classical trick in convexity going back to
Dir39
for computing the volume of the unit ball of
\ell_{p}^{n}
, see
DFG+23
;
GP07
;
KMP98
. Recall that
d_{n}=\frac{\beta}{2}n^{2}+(1-\frac{\beta}{2})n
and
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|^{p}\leq 1\}
.

Lemma 8
.

Let
\beta\in\{1,2,4\}
and
1\leq p<\infty
. Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n})

\displaystyle=c_{n}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{\beta}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}x_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{n}}{\Gamma(1+\frac{d_{n}}{p})}\Big(\frac{n\beta v_{p}}{2}\Big)^{d_{n}/p}Z_{n,p,\beta}

where
Z_{n,p,\beta}
and
v_{p}
are as in (
10
) and

c_{n}=\frac{1}{n!}\bigg(\frac{\Gamma(\frac{\beta}{2})}{(2\pi)^{\beta/2}}\bigg)^{n}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(2\pi)^{\beta k/2}}{\Gamma(\frac{\beta k}{2})}.

Remark 9
.

For
p=\infty
, the statement of Lemma
8
becomes

\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{B}_{\infty,\beta}^{n})=c_{n}\int_{[-1,1]^{n}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{\beta}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}x_{n}.

Then (
3
) is a consequence of Selberg’s integral formula.

As a final ingredient, we shall need the following well-known asymptotics for
c_{n}
as in Lemma
8
.

Lemma 10
.

Let
\beta\in\{1,2,4\}
. Then, as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\ln c_{n}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\beta}{4}n^{2}\ln n+\frac{\beta}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{4\pi}{\beta}+\frac{3}{4}\Big)n^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\Big(1+\frac{\beta}{2}\Big)n\ln n

\displaystyle+\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big(1-\frac{\beta}{2}\Big)\ln\beta-\frac{1}{2}\Big(1+\frac{\beta}{2}\Big)(\ln\pi-1)-\ln 2+\ln\Gamma\Big(\frac{\beta}{2}\Big)\Big)n

\displaystyle-\frac{3+\frac{\beta}{2}+\frac{2}{\beta}}{12}\ln n+a_{\beta}+o(1),

where
a_{\beta}\in\mathbb{R}
is some constant.

Proof.

It follows from the definition of
c_{n}
that

c_{n}=\frac{1}{n!}\Gamma_{2}\Big(n+1;\frac{2}{\beta},1\Big)\bigg(\frac{\Gamma(\frac{\beta}{2})}{(\frac{\beta}{2})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}}\bigg)^{n}\Big(\frac{4\pi}{\beta}\Big)^{\frac{\beta}{4}n^{2}-(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\beta}{4})n},

where
\Gamma_{2}(x;\frac{2}{\beta},1)
is a particular instance of the Barnes double Gamma function, see
BG24
;
Spr09
. Adapting
(
BG24
, eq. (7.17))
, the following asymptotic expansion holds

\displaystyle\ln\Gamma_{2}\Big(n+1;\frac{2}{\beta},1\Big)=

\displaystyle-\frac{\beta}{4}n^{2}\ln n+\frac{3\beta}{8}n^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Big(1-\frac{\beta}{2}\Big)n\ln n-\frac{1}{2}\Big(1-\frac{\beta}{2}\Big)n

\displaystyle+\bigg(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3+\frac{\beta}{2}+\frac{2}{\beta}}{12}\bigg)\ln n+a_{\beta}^{\prime}+o(1),

where
a_{\beta}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
is some constant. Combined with Stirling’s approximation
\ln n!=n\ln n-n+\frac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi n)+o(1)
we deduce the claimed expansion.
∎

We are now ready to deduce the asymptotics for the volume of the self-adjoint Schatten classes
\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n}
given in Theorem
1
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let
\beta\in\{1,2,4\}
and
p\geq\frac{3}{2}
. Recall that
d_{n}=\frac{\beta}{2}n^{2}+(1-\frac{\beta}{2})n
. By Lemma
8
it holds that

\ln\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n})=\ln c_{n}+\frac{d_{n}}{p}\ln\Big(\frac{\beta nv_{p}}{2}\Big)-\ln\Gamma\Big(1+\frac{d_{n}}{p}\Big)+\ln Z_{n,p,\beta}.

(18)

By Stirling’s approximation, as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\ln\Gamma\Big(1+\frac{d_{n}}{p}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{n}}{p}\Big(\ln\frac{d_{n}}{p}-1\Big)+o(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{n}}{p}\Big(2\ln n+\ln\frac{\beta}{2p}-1+\Big(1-\frac{\beta}{2}\Big)\frac{2}{\beta n}\Big)+o(n).

(19)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{d_{n}}{p}\ln\Big(\frac{\beta nv_{p}}{2}\Big)-\ln\Gamma\Big(1+\frac{d_{n}}{p}\Big)=

\displaystyle-\frac{d_{n}}{p}\Big(\ln n-\ln(pv_{p})-1+\Big(1-\frac{\beta}{2}\Big)\frac{2}{\beta n}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\beta}{2p}n^{2}\ln n+\frac{\beta}{2p}\big(\ln(pv_{p})+1\big)n^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{p}\Big(1-\frac{\beta}{2}\Big)n\ln n+\frac{\ln(pv_{p})}{p}\Big(1-\frac{\beta}{2}\Big)n+o(n).

(20)

Combining Theorem
2
, Lemma
10
and (
2
) with (
18
) and collecting terms completes the proof of (
1
) in Theorem
1
.

For the proof of (
9
) in Theorem
1
we proceed analogously, replacing Theorem
2
by Theorem
3
.

ABSTRACT NUMBER: ESOC2026A1469 SOCIETAL COSTS AND HEALTH-RELATED QUALITY OF LIFE AT 2 AND 3 YEARS AFTER STROKE
Authors: Chi Nguyen, Jasper Daems, Hassan Al Ameer, Manon Kappelhof, Diederik Dippel, Yvo Roos, Durk-Jouke van der Zee, Erik Buskens, Maarten Uyttenboogaart, Maarten Lahr
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and aimsEvidence on costs and health-related quality of life (HRQoL) after stroke beyond the first year is limited. This study estimated societal costs over 3-month period and HRQoL at 2 and 3 years post-stroke, and examined differences by functional outcome in the Netherlands.MethodsStroke patients were recruited from the MR CLEAN MED and MR CLEAN NO IV trials. HRQoL and cost data were collected using telephone interviews and questionnaires. HRQoL was assessed using EQ-5D-5L and expressed as utility values. Functional outcome was assessed using the modified Rankin Scale (mRS). Costs were estimated from a societal perspective using non-parametric bootstrapping and categorized into healthcare and non-healthcare costs.ResultsIn total, 297 patients were included, with follow-up available for 108 patients at 2 years and 189 patients at 3 years post-stroke. Mean utility was 0.77 (SD 0.28) at 2 years and 0.79 (SD 0.27) at 3 years. Mean total 3-month costs per patient were €7,487 (95% CI €5,482–€9,808) at 2 years and €7,098 (95% CI €5,118–€9,521) at 3 years. Non-healthcare costs accounted for 37% of total costs at 2 years and 41% at 3 years. Total 3-month costs increased substantially with worse functional outcome, ranging from €2,288 (mRS0) to €29,879 (mRS5) at 2 years, and from €448 (mRS0) to €46,389 (mRS5) at 3 years.ConclusionsSocietal costs remain substantial at long-term follow-up after stroke, particularly among patients with severe disability.

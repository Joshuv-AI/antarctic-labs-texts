ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAccess to routine dental care is a cornerstone of preventive healthcare. Regular dental check-ups, which include professional cleanings, examinations, and preventive treatments, play a crucial role in preventing advanced dental diseases such as cavities, gum disease, and oral cancer. These check-ups help identify potential problems early, reducing the need for more invasive treatments and minimizing complications. This research aims to identify key determinants influencing patient behavior regarding dental care.MethodsTo identify influential factors affecting annual dental visits (ADV), we utilized the publicly available 2022 Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System (BRFSS) dataset, comprising survey records. This dataset captures health-related behaviors, chronic conditions, and access to preventive services among adults in the United States. We propose a hybrid method combining feature selection using the transformer with machine learning (ML) models to uncover the determinants of ADV behavior.ResultsThe proposed model was evaluated using various transformer architectures. Among them, RoBERTa, ELECTRA, and BERT demonstrated the highest performance. Features selected by these top-performing models were subsequently used to train several ML models. CatBoost and XGBoost achieved the highest accuracies at 76.0% and 75.6%, respectively, while the decision tree achieved the lowest accuracy at 64.8%.ConclusionsOur proposed method effectively reduced the feature space, thereby improving focus and reducing training and inference time without compromising accuracy. This fusion-based model provides valuable insights for healthcare providers, enabling the development of targeted interventions tailored to specific population needs. Understanding the factors contributing to irregular dental visits can guide evidence-based strategies to overcome barriers and improve overall oral health outcomes.Keywords: Dental care, Clinical decision support systems, Dental visits, Transformers, Feature selection, Machine learning

Introduction

Oral health is crucial for overall well-being as it affects all elements of an individual’s life, such as physical health, mental health, and social well-being [1]. Proper oral hygiene prevents dental issues such as cavities, gum disease, and oral cancer. Inadequate oral health can lead to discomfort, difficulties with eating and speaking, and a diminished quality of life. Furthermore, oral health has been linked to various systemic conditions such as heart disease [2–4], diabetes [5–7], mental disorders [8–10], and respiratory infections [11–13]. Prioritizing oral health can lower the risk of developing these conditions and improve overall health and well-being. Extensive healthcare data have become increasingly accessible to the public through national surveys. In the United States(US), several prominent surveys—including the Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System (BRFSS) [14], National Health Interview Survey (NHIS) [15], Current Population Survey (CPS) [16], American Community Survey (ACS) [17], and Medical Expenditure Panel Survey (MEPS) [18]—offer valuable insights into healthcare access, expenditure, and utilization. Among these surveys, BRFSS was selected for this study due to its large sample size, detailed coverage of behavioral risk factors, chronic conditions, and preventive health practices. These features make it particularly well-suited for identifying the factors influencing dental visits at the population level. Survey data from the BRFSS between 2018 and 2022 show that about 33% of adults in the US did not have a dental checkup during the past year. Regular dental check-ups, which include professional cleanings, examinations, and preventative treatments, play a crucial role in preventing advanced oral diseases such as cavities, gum disease, and oral cancer. By having regular dental check-ups, these conditions can be diagnosed early on, reducing the need for more invasive treatments and minimizing the risk of complications.

Understanding the factors influencing dental visits holds significant importance for various reasons. Primarily, this would help dental professionals and policymakers in recognizing impediments to accessibility and devising effective solutions. Understanding the factors associated with dental visits can guide policy decisions, enabling policymakers to formulate strategies that enhance dental care access and utilization [19–21]. This awareness facilitates the creation of targeted public health campaigns. This would also help dental professionals in providing more effective care [22–26]. By identifying groups at higher risk of some oral conditions, dentists can tailor their preventive care and early intervention approaches, providing more focused advice and treatment plans that can help improve patient oral health outcomes. Moreover, understanding these factors can help mitigate oral health disparities by ensuring equal access to high-quality dental care [27–32]. Preventive dental care is generally more cost-effective than treating advanced dental problems [22, 33–37]. This preventive approach not only saves patients from potential discomfort and inconvenience but also conserves resources and reduces the financial burden on healthcare systems.

Manual identification of factors influencing annual dental visits (ADV) is a time-consuming process prone to errors and misinterpretations. The possibility of overlooking or misinterpreting critical factors is high, leading to incomplete or inaccurate analyses. There is a need to automate this process to address these challenges. Automation can ensure the comprehensive identification and analysis of crucial features that influence ADV, thereby enhancing the accuracy and efficiency of decision making in dental care provision. Automating this process can lead to more informed decisions and better patient outcomes. In this study, explainable attentive transformer () models are employed to identify the pivotal factors that play a role in ADV. By harnessing the power of , we are able to analyze a wealth of data and pinpoint the variables most closely associated with regular dental check-ups. This approach allowed us to identify the factors and understand their relative importance in influencing an individual’s likelihood of attending dental check-ups. The insights gleaned from the provide valuable information for dental professionals and policymakers, enabling them to tailor interventions and public health campaigns that effectively address barriers to dental care access and utilization. The reduced set of features derived from can be employed to train supervised machine learning (ML) models, enabling a reduction in both training and testing time, while enhancing prediction accuracy. Furthermore, this could potentially enhance overall dental care and enable personalized precision dentistry. Literature review

Artificial intelligence (AI)-based techniques have become increasingly popular in the field of dentistry for various applications, such as predicting, detecting, and diagnosing dental diseases [38–42]. These models leverage the capabilities of ML and deep learning algorithms, to analyze vast amounts of data and identify patterns that may not be immediately apparent to human observers. AI-based models are trained on large datasets of patient information, including medical history [43], genetic factors [44, 45], lifestyle habits [46, 47], and dental images [48–52], to predict the likelihood of developing dental diseases such as periodontal disease, dental caries, and oral cancer.

AI-based models have been explored in various branches of dentistry, including endodontics [53–56], orthodontics [57–63], periodontics [64], prosthodontics [65–68], pedodontics [69], maxillofacial pathology and surgery [70–74], orofacial pain management [75, 76], and neck), and dental anesthesiology [77]. AI has shown promising outcomes in early lesion detection, covering a range of dental issues like cavities, vertical root fractures, and apical lesions. Furthermore, AI has been effectively deployed for the diagnosis and classification of various dental diseases. AI has been experimented with in the planning of treatments and forecasting treatment outcomes. This includes the simulation of facial appearance changes in pre- and post-oral treatment photographs. AI was used detect oral tumors and cancers through analysis radiographic, microscopic, and images from ultrasonography.

Recently, Luo et al. [78] conducted a static examination to explore the connections between acculturation, dental floss, and regular ADV, aiming to understand their relationship with oral health outcomes and access to dental care. Their findings revealed a significant association between acculturation and dental visits among insured individuals. Elani et al. [79] applied ML techniques to analyze 28 manually selected features from the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey data from 2011 to 2014. The results showed that age and regular dental care were strong predictors of tooth loss. The seminal work on identifying the relation between the last dental visit and tooth loss was studied by Bomfim et al. [80]. They employed the extreme gradient boosting algorithm (XGBoost) to assess the significance of seven co-variates in predicting the link between tooth loss and other factors. The authors underscored that the last dental visit acted as a mediator between socioeconomic status and severe tooth loss in older individuals. Additionally, they emphasized that age, level of education, and frequency of dental visits were the primary factors associated with severe tooth loss in older individuals. ML has been explored to enhance the general Electronic Health Records (EHRs) of patients [81, 82], but there is a need to improve the completeness of dental data to contribute to overall oral health improvement. Given the significant role of ADV, identifying the factors impacting regular dental visits is essential for improving dental data completeness and, consequently, enhancing overall dental care.

To the best of our knowledge, transformers have not been extensively utilized in identifying factors influencing ADV. In this study, we aim to elucidate the black-box decisions enabled by the proposed model by visualizing the associated factors contributing to the detection of regular ADV. While most existing works manually select features for training ML models, our proposed EAT adopts a systematic methodology to identify the top contributing features for further training ML models.

Data source

This study uses the most recent publicly accessible dataset of the Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System (BRFSS) for the year 2022. BRFSS is an annual nationwide survey conducted by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) in the US. It gathers state-level information on a variety of health-related risk behaviors, chronic health conditions, and preventive health practices among individuals ages 18 years and older. State health departments manage the BRFSS field operations, adhering to protocols established by each state and receiving technical support from the CDC. Collaborating during survey development, state health departments either conduct interviews internally or enlist contractors. Subsequently, the collected data undergo editing, processing, weighting, and analysis at the CDC. BRFSS plays a vital role in monitoring the health status of the US population, identifying disparities in health outcomes, and guiding public health interventions aimed at enhancing overall population health.

Study population

The study includes 443,770 adults aged 18 years or older living in the US. The BRFSS comprises telephone surveys focused on gathering information about health-related behaviors, chronic conditions, access to healthcare, and utilization of preventive services among adults living in the US and selected regions.

Data preprocessing

Cases missing ADV are excluded from the dataset in preparation for ML analysis, resulting in a reduced sample size of 434,554 adults and 328 features. Features containing over 25% missing values are eliminated, along with those comprising continuous or string values. Additionally, features containing alphanumeric values and those with over 1000 unique values were excluded, resulting in a final selection of 109 features from the BRFSS dataset. To address potential biases related to ADV frequency within 12 months (coded as 1) versus non-annual visits (coded as 0), Chi-square tests are performed. It should be noted that eleven features (‘CELLFON5’, ‘CTELNUM1’, ‘VETERAN3’, ‘PVTRESD3’, ‘HAVARTH4’, ‘_DRDXAR2’, ‘DROCDY4_’, ‘CHCKDNY2’, ‘COVIDPOS’, ‘CSTATE1’, ‘CVDINFR4’) exhibit high p-values, suggesting a strong correlation with ADV status and are thus unsuitable for model training. Consequently, these variables are omitted from the dataset. In total, 98 features display statistically significant relationships with the outcome variable, ADV status . An input text is constructed using the independent sixty-nine features along with the outcome label of ADV for further training using the proposed model.

for feature selection

The proposed feature selection model, EAT, incorporating label attention, extends the basic transformer architecture (refer to Fig. 2). Label attention mechanisms are employed to aggregate the outputs of transformer attention head layers, computing their mean. This aggregated result is then passed through a fully connected layer with sigmoid activation to predict the likelihood of ADV. Training of utilizes binary cross-entropy loss with the Adam optimizer, set at a learning rate of 0.001. To prevent overfitting, early stopping is implemented, leveraging precision on the validation split. This ensures the preservation of the best-performing model, effectively predicting ADV from unseen structured textual input.

ML approach for dental visit prediction

The top sixteen features selected through the proposed method are then employed to train ML models for predicting ADV. Upon preprocessing the dataset, it was observed that missing values () posed a challenge for the ML classifiers. Effective management of missing data is essential to ensuring the reliability of the results. Imputation is a widely accepted approach for handling missing values, involving the replacement of missing data with estimated or calculated values. In this study, we utilize the iterative imputer, which capitalizes on the internal structure and interrelationships within the dataset to estimate and fill in missing values. The iterative imputation method adopts an iterative modeling strategy, initially initializing missing values with preliminary estimates. It then iteratively refines these estimates by fitting a model that predicts missing values based on the relationships among observed features, employing a multiple linear regression equation. This iterative process continues until convergence, progressively refining prior imputations with each iteration. The final imputed values are determined by averaging imputations across multiple iterations, thereby optimizing missing value estimates by leveraging the most reliable available information.

ML classifiers analyze the training data, recognizing patterns and relationships between input features and desired outcomes. Throughout the training phase, the algorithm fine-tunes its parameters to minimize the disparity between its predicted ADV outcomes and the actual target dental visits. Subsequently, these learned parameters are applied to predict future outcomes for the test dataset. The performance of the ML classifiers is assessed through diverse metrics, which entail comparing predicted and actual ADV outcomes derived from the test dataset, distinct from the training phase. A total of 4,34,554 cases were used to train and test the ML classifiers. Among them, 3,47,643 cases (80% of 4,34,554) were randomly selected for training, while 86,911 cases (20% of 4,34,554) were reserved for testing purposes. In order to ensure fair results, identical splits (using a unique random seed) were utilized for all ML classifiers. The ML classifiers in this study were developed using Python 3.7.0 (Python Software Foundation). Evaluation metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, sensitivity, specificity, and AUC were calculated using the test dataset.

Minimizing false negatives is crucial to avoid overlooking any potential patients. Consequently, the focus lies on maximizing the recall metric, indicating the model’s ability to accurately detect all positive cases. A recall of 91.1% is achieved for identifying the ADV patients.

Prioritizing recall, the model aims to identify the highest number of patients to minimize the risk of missing individuals needing ADV attention. Striking a balance between false positives and false negatives is pivotal for maintaining the model’s overall efficacy. While false positives may occur, the importance of avoiding false negatives — which could result in neglecting patients requiring ADV — outweighs their impact. The overarching focus of the research model is on maximizing recall to mitigate false negatives and ensure potential ADV patients are not overlooked, while also considering false positives for a practical and efficient screening method. Discussion

Consistent dental care plays a crucial role in preventive healthcare. Lack of regular dental care can impede early diagnosis, prevention, and treatment, leading to poorer oral health outcomes. The focus of the study was on ADV due to its substantial impact on oral health. Identifying the factors influencing ADV involved a two-phase hybrid ML approach, which first employed feature selection through the proposed transformer, followed by training using conventional ML models. To assess how reducing the feature set affects the predictive performance of the ML model in predicting ADV, an experiment was conducted using both the full set of features and the selected feature set, with results presented in Table 4. To evaluate the significance of features in identifying ADV, feature importance scores (F-Scores) were computed using the CatBoost classifier, as illustrated in Fig. 7. Higher scores indicate a greater contribution to the accurate identification of ADV. Notably, the top six most influential variables were annual flu shot status, smoking habits, geographical location, number of permanent teeth removed, educational attainment, and age. Subsequently, other notable features include annual income, alcohol consumption, insurance coverage, body weight, and urban residency. Additionally, leveraging the interpretability of the proposed EAT values allows for the analysis of the values corresponding to each of these influential features. For instance, influenza vaccination status (FLUSHOT7) was positively associated with ADV. This observation aligns with prior studies [88–91] demonstrating that older adults are more likely to receive the influenza vaccine and are particularly susceptible to age-related degenerative changes, which often necessitate enhanced dental care to maintain oral health [89, 92]. Furthermore, a significant portion of current smokers schedule ADV, consistent with observations reported in recent literature [93–95]. Patients with an annual income exceeding $25,000 were predominantly associated with ADV, a trend supported by existing studies [96, 97]. Similarly, a majority of patients with a minimum education level of grade 12 (EDUCA levels 4 and above) had ADV, which aligns with findings from recent studies [98, 99]. The prevalence of ADV was notably higher among patients who had undergone the removal of one or more permanent teeth due to decay or gum disease. Respondents from all age and weight groups were associated with ADV. Additionally, a significant portion of those attending ADV reported consuming at least 93 alcoholic drinks per week, which aligns with trends observed in recent studies [100–102]. The majority possessed some form of health insurance (_HCVU652 = 1.0), a pattern also documented in recent studies [103–105]. Additionally noted was the trend where a majority of patients residing in Colorado, Connecticut, Maine, New Hampshire, North Carolina, Tennessee, and Vermont did not schedule their ADV. This may be attributable to rural residency and the associated disparities in access to dental facilities, as identified in previous research [106, 107]. By understanding and addressing the influencing factors that impact the completeness of dental data, stakeholders can implement targeted strategies to fill gaps and improve data quality. Ultimately, leveraging these factors can not only enhance dental data completeness but also inform evidence-based decision-making and improve oral health outcomes for individuals and communities.

Strengths and limitations

In this study, a fusion of the DL and ML methods is proposed for developing an interpretable and automated feature set. Several factors were found to influence the ADV. These analyses will enable healthcare providers to implement proactive strategies, which may improve oral health outcomes. By focusing on the prevention or early detection of dental issues, the necessity for expensive treatments or emergency care may be minimized. A comprehensive understanding of the factors impacting ADV can guide policy development aimed at advancing oral health and preventing dental diseases on a population scale. Furthermore, it can assist policymakers in refining strategies to mitigate dental costs and promote holistic well-being.

This study has some potential limitations. Employing imputation techniques to handle missing data could potentially affect the model’s performance. The prediction model was constructed solely based on data sourced from BRFSS, which might limit the applicability of our findings to broader populations. Furthermore, depending on self-reported data for behavioral aspects like smoking and alcohol consumption may introduce reliability concerns, potentially affecting the precision of our prediction model. The model was evaluated within an EHR framework utilizing longitudinal survey data, which may constrain its generalizability to various healthcare settings. Further studies could be conducted to validate the model using external datasets from a range of clinical environments and investigate its adaptability to real-time clinical workflows in order to enhance its broader applicability.

Future studies

In future research, the proposed model will undergo application and potential retraining with an expanded dataset such as the Medical Expenditure Panel Survey (MEPS) to understand the factors contributing to various dental visit reasons. Furthermore, investigation into various other neural network architectures will be conducted to bolster the model’s performance. Additionally, the exploration of incorporating new influencing factors will be pursued to better encapsulate the distribution and behavior of input features within the model. This study lays the groundwork for the development of a decision support tool aimed to enable dental professionals and policymakers to recognize barriers to ADV and effectively address them. The integration of multimodal data—encompassing dental history textual notes and imaging—will be examined to provide a more comprehensive understanding of the factors that influence ADV.

Conclusions

Regular dental visits are crucial for maintaining optimal oral health. These visits provide an opportunity for professional assessment, preventive care, and early detection of dental issues. It is important to identify the factors contributing to regular ADV. Most of the survey datasets include a large number of cases with noisy, duplicative information. In this study, we proposed a novel transformer to select the important features contributing to ADV. Our initial investigation identified influential factors by leveraging BRFSS data and ML techniques to predict the likelihood of ADV using these identified features. It could be observed that the proposed method substantially reduced the feature space there by enabling focus and requiring less training and inference time, without compromising on performance. The implementation of such a fusion based model holds the potential to provide healthcare providers with fresh insights into the factors associated with ADV and facilitate improved access to dental care by tailoring interventions to address specific needs, ensuring timely preventive and therapeutic services. Consequently, clinicians can adopt proactive measures informed by this knowledge. This, inturn, leads to enhanced oral health outcomes as interventions are targeted towards preventing dental problems or addressing them at an early stage, potentially reducing the need for costly treatments or emergency care. Understanding the ADV influential factors could enhance research efforts and policy development aimed at promoting oral health and preventing dental diseases at the population level. Overall, recognizing the factors influencing regular ADV has far-reaching implications for improving oral health outcomes and promoting overall well-being.

Revisiting the convergence rate of the Lasserre hierarchy for polynomial optimization over the hypercube
Sander Gribling, Etienne de Klerk, Juan Vera

Abstract
We revisit the problem of minimizing a given polynomial f f on the hypercube [ − 1 , 1 ] n [-1,1]^{n} . Lasserre’s hierarchy (also known as the moment- or sum-of-squares hierarchy) provides a sequence of lower bounds { f ( r ) } r ∈ ℕ \{f_{(r)}\}_{r\in\mathbb{N}} on the minimum value f ∗ f^{*} , where r r refers to the allowed degrees in the sum-of-squares hierarchy. A natural question is how fast the hierarchy converges as a function of the parameter r r . The current state-of-the-art is due to Baldi and Slot [SIAM J. on Applied Algebraic Geometry, 2024] and roughly shows a convergence rate of order 1 / r 1/r . Here we obtain closely related results via a different approach: the polynomial kernel method. We also discuss limitations of the polynomial kernel method, suggesting a lower bound of order 1 / r 2 1/r^{2} for our approach.



Keywords
: Polynomial kernel method, semidefinite programming, Positivstellensatz, Lasserre hierarchy

Introduction

We consider the problem of minimizing a given polynomial
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
on the hypercube
[-1,1]^{n}
, namely

f_{\mathrm{min}}:=\mathrm{min}_{x\in[-1,1]^{n}}f(x).

(1)

For given
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
, we say that a value
\hat{f}
is a (relative)
\varepsilon
-approximation of
f_{\mathrm{min}}
if

|\hat{f}-f_{\mathrm{min}}|\leq\varepsilon(f_{\mathrm{max}}-f_{\mathrm{min}}),

where
f_{\mathrm{max}}:=\mathrm{max}_{x\in[-1,1]^{n}}f(x)
; see e.g.
[
3
]
. Since we will only consider polynomials, we write
f\in\mathbb{R}[x]
to indicate a real polynomial, and
f\in\mathbb{R}[x]_{d}
if the degree of
f
is (at most)
d
.

Problem (
1
) is of enduring interest in the context of bound-constrained nonlinear optimization, and includes
the
\mathsf{NP}
-hard maximum cut problem as a special case: for a graph
G=(V,E)
with
|V|=n
, the size of a maximum cut in
G
is given by

\mathrm{max}_{x\in[-1,1]^{n}}\frac{1}{4}x^{\top}L_{G}x,

where
L_{G}
is the Laplacian matrix of
G
. While the maximum cut problem allows a polynomial-time
(1-0.878)
-approximation,
due to Goemans and Williamson
[
7
]
,
Bellare and Rogaway
[
3
]

showed that, in general, problem (
1
) does not allow a polynomial-time

\varepsilon
-approximation of
f_{\mathrm{min}}
for any
\varepsilon\in[0,1)
, unless
\mathsf{P}=\mathsf{NP}
.

Problem (
1
) is a special case of a polynomial optimization problem with a compact feasible set.
In a seminal work from 2001, Lasserre
[
10
]
introduced a hierarchy of semidefinite programming problems to solve such problems.
To introduce the Lasserre hierarchy for our special case, we view
[-1,1]^{n}
as the feasible set for constraints

g_{i}(x):=1-x_{i}^{2}\geq 0\quad(i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}=:[n]).

(2)

The quadratic module associated with this (or any) set of polynomials
\mathbf{g}=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})
is defined as

\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{g})=\Sigma[x]+\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}\Sigma[x],

(3)

where
\Sigma[x]
denotes the cone of sum-of-squares (SOS) polynomials in the variables
x
.
The truncated, quadratic module of order
r
is defined as

\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{g})_{r}=\Sigma[x]_{r}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}\Sigma[x]_{r-\deg(g_{i})},

where
\Sigma[x]_{r}
denotes the SOS cone of polynomials of (total) degree at most
r
.
The key idea is that membership of
\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{g})_{r}
may be formulated as a semidefinite programming (SDP) problem.
The Lasserre hierachy may now be defined as the sequence of SDP problems:

f_{(r)}:=\mathrm{sup}\left\{t\;:\;f-t\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{g})_{r}\right\}\quad r\in\mathbb{N}.

(4)

The convergence of the sequence
f_{(r)}\rightarrow f_{\mathrm{min}}
as
r\rightarrow\infty
was already shown
by Lasserre
[
10
]
for the case when the quadratic module satisfies the Archimedean assumption:

\exists g\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{g})\;\mbox{such that}\;\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;:\;g(x)\geq 0\}\mbox{ is compact.}

(5)

This is an immediate consequence
of the following Positivstellensatz by Putinar
[
18
]
.

Theorem 1

(Putinar Positivstellensatz
[
18
]
)
.

Assume a compact semi-algebraic set
K
is described by polynomials
\mathbf{g}
and that the Archimedean assumption (
5
) holds for
\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{g})
.
Then, if
f\in\mathbb{R}[x]
is (strictly) positive on
K
, one has
f\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{g})
.

The Archimedean property clearly holds for the polynomials that we use to describe the hypercube. Since we are only interested in this special case,

\mathbf{g}
will always refer to (
2
) from now on.

Following the seminal paper by Lasserre, there has been extensive research on cases where finite convergence holds
[
15
,
12
,
4
]
, as well as research into the
rate of convergence
[
16
,
6
,
2
]
.
For the case of the hypercube
[-1,1]^{n}
, the best result so far is due to Baldi and Slot
[
2
]
, who showed that

f_{(r)}-f_{\mathrm{min}}=O\left(\frac{1}{r}\right).

Here the big-O notation suppresses all dependence on
f
and
n
.

To understand this result better, we consider the main result of Baldi and Slot
[
2
]
in more detail.

Theorem 2

(Theorem 11 in Baldi and Slot
[
2
]
)
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{R}[x]
be a polynomial of degree
d
and assume
0<f_{\mathrm{min}}<f_{\mathrm{max}}
on
[-1,1]^{n}
. Then we
have
f\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{g})_{rn}
, with
\mathbf{g}
defined by (
2
), whenever

r\geq 4\mathfrak{c}\cdot d^{2}(\log n)\cdot\frac{f_{\mathrm{max}}}{f_{\mathrm{min}}}+\mathrm{max}\left\{\pi d\sqrt{2n},\ \left(2\mathfrak{c}\cdot\frac{f_{\mathrm{max}}}{f_{\mathrm{min}}}\cdot C(n,d)\right)^{1/2}\right\},

where
\mathfrak{c}\in[1,e^{5}]
is an absolute constant with
e\approx 2.71828
being Euler’s number, and
C(n,d)
is a constant that only depends on
(n,d)
, and satisfies
1
1

1

The bounds given here on the constant
C(n,d)
are from Laurent and Slot
[
14
, Section 3.3]
.

2\pi^{2}d^{2}n\binom{n+d}{d}\leq C(n,d)\leq\mathrm{min}\left\{2\pi^{2}d^{2}n2^{n/2}(d+1)^{n},2\pi^{2}d^{2}n2^{d/2}(n+1)^{d}\right\}.

Returning to problem (
1
), with
f\in\mathbb{R}[x]_{d}
being the objective function, one may apply the above theorem by considering the positive
function on
[-1,1]^{n}
defined by

x\mapsto f(x)-f_{\mathrm{min}}+\varepsilon_{f}(r)\left(f_{\mathrm{max}}-f_{\mathrm{min}}\right),

where
r\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\varepsilon_{f}(r)>0
is a suitable quantity, to be specified, that depends on
f
and
r
, and satisfies

\varepsilon_{f}(r)=O\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)
. This immediately leads to the following corollary.

Corollary 1

(cf. Corollary 15 in Baldi and Slot
[
2
]
)
.

Consider problem (
1
), and let
r\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy
n|r
as well as

r\geq 4\mathfrak{c}\cdot d^{2}(n\log n)+n+2n\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}C(n,d)}.

Then

f_{(r)}-f_{\mathrm{min}}\leq\varepsilon_{f}(r)(f_{\mathrm{max}}-f_{\mathrm{min}}),

where

\varepsilon_{f}(r)\leq\frac{4\mathfrak{c}\cdot d^{2}(n\log n)}{r}.

Thus, under the stated conditions on
r
,
f_{(r)}
is an
\varepsilon_{f}(r)
-approximation of
f_{\mathrm{min}}
.

In this paper, we will show a related result, by using the kernel polynomial method (see, e.g.
[
23
]
)
along the lines of
[
5
]
.
The kernel polynomial method has been used before to analyze the convergence of the Lasserre hierarchy, e.g. for the unit sphere by Fang and Fawzi
[
6
]
. It has also been used to analyze related SDP hierarchies, e.g. in
[
14
,
1
]
. In the conclusion of this paper we give a more detailed discussion of the relationships between these works.

We will show that the rate of convergence may be improved to
O\left(\frac{\log r}{r^{2}}\right)
if one only requires fixed relative accuracy: For
any fixed
\varepsilon>0
we will show that

f_{(r)}-f_{\mathrm{min}}-\varepsilon(f_{\mathrm{max}}-f_{\mathrm{min}})=O\left(\frac{\log r}{r^{2}}\right).

Thus we show that the Lasserre hierarchy converges at (at least) the rate of

O\left(\frac{\log r}{r^{2}}\right)
to an
\varepsilon
-approximation of
f_{\mathrm{min}}
; see Corollary
25
for a precise statement.

We emphasize that our construction achieves a Putinar-type certificate instead of a Schmüdgen-type certificate which is more common for kernel-based approaches. For this, it is crucial that we construct a univariate kernel that provides a good SOS-approximation of positive polynomials (as opposed to an approximation in the univariate quadratic module, cf.
[
5
]
). The work of Baldi and Slot
[
2
]
also obtains Putinar-type certificates, but along the way it makes use of Schmüdgen’s Positivstellensatz on the ball. We avoid Schmüdgen’s Positivstellensatz and its associated (large) constants in terms of
n
and
d
. We state our results with explicit constants that depend polynomially on
n
and
d
.

We measure the quality of an SOS-approximation in terms of the 1-norm of the coefficients of the difference in the Chebyshev basis, denoted
\|\cdot\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}
. We complement our construction of good SOS-approximations with a lower bound result, which might be of independent interest. In
Theorem

10
we show that if
q
is a degree-
r
sum of squares with
\|(1-x^{2})-q\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\leq\delta
, then we have
r=\Omega(1/\sqrt{\delta})
.

Outline of this paper

We start by reviewing some background material in Section
2
, namely properties of Chebyshev polynomials,

In Section

we describe our use of the polynomial kernel method. Thus we review Gaussian kernels that form the core of our approach in Section

, followed by truncated Gaussians in Section

6
.

Preliminaries

2.1
Basic properties of Chebyshev polynomials

The Chebyshev polynomials of degree
k
of the first and second kind are respectively defined by:

\displaystyle T_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\cos(k\arccos x),

(6)

\displaystyle U_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin((k+1)\arccos(x))}{\sin(\arccos(x))},

(7)

for
x\in[-1,1]
.

Defining the inner product for suitable
f,g:[-1,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,

\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{f(x)g(x)}{\pi\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}\mathrm{d}x

one obtains the orthogonality relations for the Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind:

\displaystyle\langle T_{k},T_{m}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1+\delta_{k,0}}{2}\delta_{k,m}.

(8)

One may generalize the Chebyshev polynomials to the multivariate case by taking
products of univariate Chebyshev polynomials. Setting

\mathrm{d}\mu(\textbf{x}):=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{1-x_{i}^{2}}}\mathrm{d}\textbf{x},

(9)

then, for
\alpha\in(\mathbb{N}_{0})^{n}
, where
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, the corresponding multivariate Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind is defined as

T_{\alpha}(\textbf{x})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}T_{\alpha_{i}}(x_{i}),

that form an orthogonal set of polynomials w.r.t. the inner product defined by
\mu
.

In what follows we present a number of classical properties of Chebyshev polynomials. For completeness, we provide proofs where we could not find
the exact statement in the literature. The properties we use without proof may readily be found in classic texts, like the one by Rivlin
[
19
]
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon>0
such that
\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{10k^{2}}
. Then we have

\mathrm{max}_{y\in[-1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon]}|T_{k}(y)|=T_{k}(1+\varepsilon)<2.

Proof.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have
|T_{k}(x)|=|T_{k}(|x|)|
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Thus, it is enough to consider only non-negative values for
x
.
For
0\leq x\leq 1
we have
|T_{k}(x)|\leq 1
.
Now we show
T_{k}(x)
is increasing for
x\geq 1
. In this case we have

T_{k}(x)=\cosh(k\arccosh(x))
. Now,
\arccosh(x)\geq 0
and increasing for
x\geq 1
and
\cosh(x)
is increasing for
x\geq 0
. Thus
T_{k}
is increasing for
x\geq 1
.
Summing up, we get
\mathrm{max}_{|x|\leq 1+\varepsilon}|T_{k}(x)|=T_{k}(1+\varepsilon)=\cosh(k\arccosh(1+\varepsilon))
.
For
0\leq\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{10k^{2}}
we have
\arccosh(1+\varepsilon)=\ln(1+\varepsilon+\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+2\varepsilon})\leq 3\sqrt{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{3}{\sqrt{10}k}
. Thus
T_{k}(1+\varepsilon)\leq\cosh(1)<2
.
∎

Lemma 3
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, one has
|T_{k}(y)|\leq|2y|^{k}
for
|y|\geq 1
.

Proof.

By the Pell equation one has

T_{k}(y)^{2}=1+(y^{2}-1)U^{2}_{k-1}(y),

where
U_{k-1}
is the
(k-1)
th Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind. It follows that

|T_{k}(y)|\geq 1\mbox{ if }|y|\geq 1.

Since
T_{k}(1)=1
, this means
T_{k}(y)\geq 1
if
y\geq 1
.
By the recursive relation

T_{k+1}(y)=2yT_{k}(y)-T_{k-1}(y),

if
y\geq 1
, one therefore has

|T_{k+1}(y)|=T_{k+1}(y)\leq 2yT_{k}(y)=|2yT_{k}(y)|.

If
y\leq-1
, the recursive relation together with the symmetry
T_{k}(-y)=(-1)^{k}T_{k}(y)
imply

(-1)^{k+1}T_{k+1}(|y|)=(-1)^{k+1}2|y|T_{k}(y)-(-1)^{k-1}T_{k-1}(|y|),

which is the same as

T_{k+1}(|y|)=2|y|T_{k}(|y|)-T_{k-1}(|y|),

so that

|T_{k+1}(y)|=T_{k+1}(|y|)=2|y|T_{k}(|y|)-T_{k-1}(|y|)\leq 2|y|T_{k}(|y|)=|2yT_{k}(y)|.

Thus, whenever
|y|\geq 1
, one has
|T_{k+1}(y)|\leq|2y||T_{k}(y)|
.
The proof now follows by induction on
k
, since the statement of the lemma holds trivially for
T_{0}
.
∎

We also need the following (well-known) result that relates the sup- and 1-norms for univariate polynomials.

Lemma 4
.

Let
p
be a univariate polynomial of degree
d
with expansion in the Chebyshev basis
p=\sum_{k=0}^{d}c_{k}T_{k}
. Then

\|p\|_{\infty}\leq\|p\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\leq\sqrt{2(d+1)}\|p\|_{\infty},

where

\|p\|_{\infty}=\mathrm{sup}_{t\in[-1,1]}|p(t)|
and
\|p\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}=\sum_{k=0}^{d}|c_{k}|
.

Proof.

Let
p=\sum_{k=0}^{d}c_{k}T_{k}
. Since
|T_{k}(t)|\leq 1
for
t\in[-1,1]
, we immediately obtain

\|p\|_{\infty}=\mathrm{sup}_{t\in[-1,1]}|p(t)|\leq\sum_{k=0}^{d}|c_{k}|
.
We now prove the second inequality. To do so, note that

\sum_{k=0}^{d}|c_{k}|\leq\sqrt{d+1}\sqrt{\sum_{k=0}^{d}c_{k}^{2}}

(10)

due to the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. The Chebyshev polynomials are known to be orthogonal with respect to the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
defined as

\langle p,q\rangle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}p(t)q(t)\frac{dt}{\sqrt{1-t^{2}}}.

The function
\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{1-t^{2}}}
is the probability density function of the Chebyshev measure with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
[-1,1]
, and therefore
\langle p,p\rangle\leq\|p\|_{\infty}^{2}
.
Together with the orthogonality of the Chebyshev polynomials with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
this yields the inequality

\sum_{k=0}^{d}c_{k}^{2}\langle T_{k},T_{k}\rangle=\langle p,p\rangle\leq\|p\|_{\infty}^{2}.

(11)

Combining
Eqs.

10
and
11
yields

\|p\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{d+1}}{\mathrm{min}_{k=0,...,d}\sqrt{\langle T_{k},T_{k}\rangle}}\|p\|_{\infty}.

Observing that
\langle T_{0},T_{0}\rangle=1
and
\langle T_{k},T_{k}\rangle=1/2
for
k>0
concludes the proof.
∎

The following classical result is due to V. Markov (1892).

Lemma 5
.

For
k,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in[-1,1]
, and any degree
k
polynomial
p
with
\|p\|_{\infty}=1
, we have

\left|\frac{d^{\ell}}{dx^{\ell}}p(x)\right|\leq\left.\frac{d^{\ell}}{dx^{\ell}}T_{k}(x)\right|_{x=1}=\frac{k^{2}[k^{2}-1^{2}]\ldots[k^{2}-(\ell-1)^{2}]}{1\cdot 3\cdots(2\ell-1)}<k^{2\ell}.

In particular, one has
\left\|\frac{d^{\ell}}{dx^{\ell}}T_{k}\right\|_{\infty}<k^{2\ell}
.

2.2
Technical lemmas related to quadratic modules

We start by recalling a classical result about univariate polynomials that are nonnegative on an interval.

Theorem 3

(Fekete, Markov-Lukácz (see, e.g.,
[
17
]
))
.

Let
p
be a univariate polynomial of degree

2m
. Then
p
is nonnegative on the interval
[a,b]
if and
only if

p(t)=q_{1}^{2}(t)+(t-a)(t-x)q_{2}^{2}(t),

for some polynomials
q_{1}
of degree
m
and
q_{2}
of degree
m-1
.
If the degree of
p
is
2m+1
then
p
is
nonnegative on
[a,b]
if and only if

p(t)=(t-a)q_{1}^{2}(t)+(b-t)q_{2}^{2}(t),

for some polynomials
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
of degree
m
.

Since
|T_{k}(x)|\leq 1
for all
x\in[-1,1]
, one immediately has the following corollary.

Corollary 6
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, one has
1-T_{k}(x)^{2}\in\mathcal{Q}(1-x^{2})_{2k}
.

We first recall a well-known lemma about the monomial basis.

Lemma 7

(cf.
[
8
, Lemma 23]
)
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
be such that
|\alpha|\leq 2t
for an integer
t\in\mathbb{N}
, then

1-x^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{Q}(1-x_{1}^{2},1-x_{2}^{2},\ldots,1-x_{n}^{2})_{2t}.

We now prove an analogous statement in the Chebyshev basis.

Lemma 8
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, then
1-T_{\alpha}(x),1+T_{\alpha}(x)\in\mathcal{Q}(1-x_{1}^{2},1-x_{2}^{2},\ldots,1-x_{n}^{2})_{2|\alpha|}
.

Proof.

We first use the identity

1\pm T_{\alpha}(x)=1\pm\prod_{i\in[n]}T_{\alpha_{i}}(x_{i})=\frac{\left(1\pm\prod_{i\in[n]}T_{\alpha_{i}}(x_{i})\right)^{2}+\left(1-\prod_{i\in[n]}T_{\alpha_{i}}(x_{i})^{2}\right)}{2}.

(Here the
\pm
should be interpreted as either consistently
+
or consistently
-
.) The first term on the right hand side is a square of degree
2|\alpha|
. It remains to show that the second term lies in the quadratic module. To do so, consider the identity

s_{n}\coloneqq 1-\prod_{i\in[n]}T_{\alpha_{i}}(x_{i})^{2}=\left(1-T_{\alpha_{n}}(x_{n})^{2}\right)+T_{\alpha_{n}}(x_{n})^{2}\left(1-\prod_{i\in[n-1]}T_{\alpha_{i}}(x_{i})^{2}\right).

The first term on the right hand side is a univariate polynomial of the form
1-T_{k}(y)^{2}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
; by
Corollary

6
it belongs to
\mathcal{Q}(1-y^{2})_{2k}
. The second term has degree
2|\alpha|
: it is a square of degree
2\alpha_{n}
times
s_{n-1}\coloneqq\left(1-\prod_{i\in[n-1]}T_{\alpha_{i}}(x_{i})^{2}\right)
, where
s_{n-1}
is defined by taking the product only over the first
n-1
variables. Repeating the argument for
s_{n-1}
allows us to iteratively reduce the number of variables until we are in the univariate case. At that point, we can again apply
Corollary

6
. ∎

Note that the statement we prove is slightly weaker compared to the one in the monomial basis: the degree bound becomes
2|\alpha|
instead of the least even integer greater than
|\alpha|
.

Corollary 9
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{R}[x]
, then
\|p\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}-p\in\mathcal{Q}(1-x_{1}^{2},1-x_{2}^{2},\ldots,1-x_{n}^{2})_{2\mathrm{deg}(p)}
.

Proof.

Write
p=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}p_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}
, then

\|p\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}-p=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}|p_{\alpha}|(1-\mathrm{sign}(p_{\alpha})T_{\alpha})
and observe that, by
Lemma

8
, each term
(1-\mathrm{sign}(p_{\alpha})T_{\alpha})
belongs to
\mathcal{Q}(1-x_{1}^{2},1-x_{2}^{2},\ldots,1-x_{n}^{2})_{2|\alpha|}
.
∎

The main result of this section is a simple consequence of the above.

Theorem 4
.

Let
f
and
q
be polynomials where
q\in\mathcal{Q}(1-x_{1}^{2},1-x_{2}^{2},\ldots,1-x_{n}^{2})_{r}
and
r\geq\mathrm{deg}(f)
, then

f+\|f-q\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\in\mathcal{Q}(1-x_{1}^{2},1-x_{2}^{2},\ldots,1-x_{n}^{2})_{2r}.

Proof.

Apply
Corollary

9
to
p=q-f
to obtain

f-q+\|f-q\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\in\mathcal{Q}(1-x_{1}^{2},1-x_{2}^{2},\ldots,1-x_{n}^{2})_{2r}
and add
q
on both sides.
∎

This result in turn has the following implication for the Lasserre hierarchy (
4
).

Corollary 10
.

Consider problem (
1
) and its associated Lasserre hierarchy (
4
).
One has, for
r\geq\mathrm{deg}(f)
,

f_{\mathrm{min}}-f_{(2r)}\leq\mathrm{min}_{q\in Q(1-x_{1}^{2},1-x_{2}^{2},\ldots,1-x_{n}^{2})_{2r}}\left\|f-f_{\mathrm{min}}-q\right\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}.

Proof.

Follows immediately from Theorem
4
, by replacing
f
in Theorem
4
by
f-f_{\mathrm{min}}
.
∎

Our analysis of the convergence rate of the Lasserre hierarchy will be based on Corollary
10
, in the sense that
we will construct a suitable
q\in Q(1-x_{1}^{2},1-x_{2}^{2},\ldots,1-x_{n}^{2})_{2r}
to approximate

, via the polynomial kernel method.
Our construction in the univariate case will in fact be SOS, which allows us to easily generalize to the multivariate setting.

3
An approximation kernel approach

The outline of this section is as follows.
We show, in three steps, how to construct a sequence of convolution operators
\mathcal{K}_{r}
with corresponding kernels

K_{r}(x,y)
for
r\in\mathbb{N}
, that satisfy the following two properties:

1.

if
f\in\mathbb{R}[x]_{d}
is positive on
[-1,1]
,

\mathcal{K}_{r}f\in\mathcal{Q}(1-x^{2})_{334r}\quad(r\geq\mathrm{deg}(f)),

2.

and

\left\|\mathcal{K}_{r}f-f\right\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}=O(\log(r)/r^{2})\quad\forall f\in\mathbb{R}[x]_{d},

for some fixed
d
.

Through
Theorem

4
, such a sequence of kernels would immediately imply that, for
f\in\mathbb{R}[x]_{d}
positive on
[-1,1]
, we have

f+\left\|\mathcal{K}_{r}f-f\right\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\in\mathcal{Q}(1-x_{1}^{2},1-x_{2}^{2},\ldots,1-x_{n}^{2})_{334r}.

We construct our kernels
K_{r}
in three steps.

First, we show that the convolution operator
\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}
corresponding to the normal distribution
\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
, has the property that
\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}
nearly preserves degree-
k
polynomials, under suitable assumptions on
k
and
\sigma
: we show

\|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}T_{k}-T_{k}\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\leq 2k^{15/2}\sigma^{2},

see
Lemma

14
for a formal statement.

Second, we write
\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma,R}
for the convolution operator obtained by truncating the domain of integration used by
\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}
to an interval
[-R,R]
. We show that if
R-1
is large enough compared to
\sigma^{2}
and
k
, then this leads to negligible error: in
Lemma

16
we show that if
R-1\geq\gamma(2+\sqrt{2})\sqrt{k}\sigma
, then

\|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}T_{k}-\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma,R}T_{k}\|_{\infty}\leq 2\sqrt{2}\exp(-\gamma^{2}).

It is important to note that here we measure the error in the sup-norm since
\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma,R}
does not map polynomials to polynomials.

Third, we approximate the kernel
K_{G}^{\sigma,R}
by an SOS kernel
K_{r}
, by using a degree-
O(r)
SOS approximation
s^{2}
of
\exp(-t)
on the interval
[0,b]
where
b=(R+1)^{2}/(2\sigma^{2})
. We show in
Lemma

20
that

\|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma,R}T_{k}-\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{k}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{24\delta}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma},

where
\delta
is such that
|\exp(-t)-s(t)^{2}|\leq\delta
for all
t\in[0,b]
.

Finally, in
Section

4.1
, we combine these three estimates to show that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{k}-T_{k}\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}T_{k}-T_{k}\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}+

\displaystyle\sqrt{2(r+1)}\left(\vphantom{\|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}T_{k}-\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma,R}T_{k}\|_{\infty}}\right.\|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}T_{k}-\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma,R}T_{k}\|_{\infty}+

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma,R}T_{k}-\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{k}\|_{\infty}\left.\vphantom{\|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}T_{k}-\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma,R}T_{k}\|_{\infty}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2k^{15/2}\sigma^{2}+

\displaystyle\sqrt{2(r+1)}\left(\vphantom{\|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}T_{k}-\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma,R}T_{k}\|_{\infty}}\right.2\sqrt{2}\exp(-\gamma^{2})\ \ +

\displaystyle\frac{24\delta}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\left.\vphantom{\|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}T_{k}-\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma,R}T_{k}\|_{\infty}}\right).

We arrive at an overall upper bound of
O(\log(r)/r^{2})
by balancing the three terms under the constraint that
r=O(\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/\sigma^{2}})
. Specifically, we choose

\sigma^{2}=O(\log(r)/r^{2}),\qquad R=1+O(\sqrt{d\log(r)}\sigma),\qquad\delta=1/\poly(r).

We refer to
Theorem

6
for a precise statement, and to Corollary
25
for its implication for the Lasserre hierarchy.

3.1
Kernels of the Gaussian type

Here we consider kernels of the Gaussian type. That is, for a parameter
\sigma\geq 0
, we consider the kernel
K_{G}^{\sigma}
defined via

K^{\sigma}_{G}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\exp{\left(-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)},

for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
. The associated linear operator on polynomials is known as the Gauss-Weierstrass convolution operator. It acts on
f\in\mathbb{R}[x]
as

\mathcal{K}^{\sigma}_{G}(f)(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}K^{\sigma}_{G}(x,y)f(y)dy.

The operator
\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}
is the convolution operator corresponding to the probability density function
p
of the normal distribution
\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
with mean
0
and variance
\sigma^{2}
. Our aim in this section is to show that
\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}T_{k}
is a good approximation of
T_{k}
, under suitable assumptions on
\sigma
and
k
. To do so, we first show that
\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}
maps degree-
k
polynomials to degree-
k
polynomials. We then show how to bound
\|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}-T_{k}\|_{\infty}
. We finally convert this to a bound on
\|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}-T_{k}\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}
.

To facilitate the analysis, we briefly recall the moments of
\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
. The odd-degree moments are equal to
0
due to the symmetry of the probability density function. The even-degree moments are

\mathbb{E}_{z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})}[z^{2\ell}]=\sigma^{2\ell}(2\ell-1)!!,

(12)

where
(2\ell-1)!!
denotes the double factorial of
(2\ell-1)
, that is, the product of all odd numbers between
1
and
2\ell-1
.

The following result states the well-known fact that the moments of the normal distribution are polynomials.

Lemma 11
.

Let
\sigma\geq 0
. Then
\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}
maps degree-
k
polynomials to degree-
k
polynomials.

Lemma 12
.

For
\sigma\geq 0
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\|T_{k}-\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}T_{k}\|_{\infty}\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}(2\ell-1)!!\,k^{4\ell}\sigma^{2\ell}

Proof.

Fix an
x\in[-1,1]
. We show how to bound
|T_{k}(x)-\int K_{G}^{\sigma}(x,y)T_{k}(y)dy|
. To do so, note that
K(x,\cdot)
corresponds to the probability density function of the normal distribution with mean
x
and variance
\sigma^{2}
. Writing
p
for the probability density function of
\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
, we have
K(x,y)=p(x-y)
. Using a change of variables
y=x+z
and the Taylor expansion of
T_{k}(x+z)
, we have

\displaystyle T_{k}(x)-\int K_{G}^{\sigma}(x,y)T_{k}(y)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}p(z)\left(T_{k}(x)-T_{k}(x+z)\right)dz

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}p(z)(2T_{k}(x)-T_{k}(x+z)-T_{k}(x-z))dz

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\lceil k/2\rceil}\frac{d^{2\ell}}{dx^{2\ell}}T_{k}(x)\mathbb{E}[z^{2\ell}],

where the expectation is taken with respect to
\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
. Using the triangle inequality, we obtain

|T_{k}(x)-\int K_{G}^{\sigma}(x,y)T_{k}(y)dy|\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{\lceil k/2\rceil}\mathbb{E}[z^{2\ell}]\left|\frac{d^{2\ell}}{dx^{2\ell}}T_{k}(x)\right|.

To conclude the proof, we substitute (
12
) for
\mathbb{E}[z^{2\ell}]
and use
Lemma

5
to bound
\left|\frac{d^{2\ell}}{dx^{2\ell}}T_{k}(x)\right|
.
∎

Using the (naive) bound
(2\ell-1)!!\leq(2\ell)^{2\ell}
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 13
.

Let
\sigma\geq 0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
k^{6}\sigma^{2}\leq 1
, then

\|T_{k}-\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}T_{k}\|_{\infty}\leq k^{7}\sigma^{2}

Proof.

We have

\sum_{\ell=1}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}(2\ell-1)!!\,k^{4\ell}\sigma^{2\ell}\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}(2\ell)^{2\ell}\,k^{4\ell}\sigma^{2\ell}\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}(k)^{2\ell}\,k^{4\ell}\sigma^{2\ell}\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}k^{6}\sigma^{2},

where in the last inequality we use the assumption
k^{6}\sigma^{2}\leq 1
.
∎

Lemma 14
.

Let
\sigma\geq 0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
k^{6}\sigma^{2}\leq 1
. Then
\mathcal{K}^{\sigma}_{G}T_{k}
is a degree-
k
polynomial that satisfies

\|\mathcal{K}^{\sigma}_{G}T_{k}-T_{k}\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\leq\sqrt{2(k+1)}k^{7}\sigma^{2}\leq 2k^{15/2}\sigma^{2}.

Proof.

Lemma

11
shows that
\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}
is a degree-
k
polynomial.
Corollary

13
shows
\|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}T_{k}-T_{k}\|_{\infty}\leq k^{7}\sigma^{2}
whenever
k^{6}\sigma^{2}\leq 1
. We finally use
Lemma

4
applied to the degree-
k
polynomial
K_{G}^{\sigma}T_{k}-T_{k}
to convert the sup-norm bound to a bound on the coefficient norm: we obtain
\|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}T_{k}-T_{k}\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\leq\sqrt{2(k+1)}\|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}T_{k}-T_{k}\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{2(k+1)}k^{7}\sigma^{2}
.
∎

3.2
Truncated Gaussian kernel

The next step towards an SOS kernel is to replace the Gaussian by a truncated Gaussian on
[-R,R]\supset[-1,1]
for some sufficiently large
R
. The moments of this truncated kernel are no longer polynomials. However, we will show that if we apply the truncated kernel to a Chebyshev polynomial, then we obtain a good approximation of that Chebyshev polynomial in the sup-norm.

Definition 15
.

For
\sigma\geq 0
and
R\geq 1
we define the truncated Gauss-Weierstrass operator
\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma,R}
as

\mathcal{K}^{\sigma,R}_{G}(f)(x):=\int_{[-R,R]}K^{\sigma}_{G}(x,y)f(y)dy.

Before we analyze this operator, we first recall a useful concentration inequality:
let
\mu,\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
and
X\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^{2})
, then, for all
c\geq 0
, we have

\mathbb{P}[|X-\mu|\geq c]\leq 2e^{-c^{2}/(2\sigma^{2})}.

(13)

Lemma 16
.

Let
\sigma\geq 0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
k\sigma^{2}\leq 1
. Assume
R\geq 1
and
\gamma\geq 1
are such that
R-1\geq\gamma(2+\sqrt{2})\sqrt{k}\sigma
. Then we have

\|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}T_{k}-\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma,R}T_{k}\|_{\infty}\leq 2\sqrt{2}\exp(-\gamma^{2}).

Proof.

Let
x\in[-1,1]
, then we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}K^{\sigma}_{G}(x,y)T_{k}(y)dy-\int_{[-R,R]}K^{\sigma}_{G}(x,y)T_{k}(y)dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R,R]}K^{\sigma}_{G}(x,y)|T_{k}(y)|dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R,R]}e^{-(x-y)^{2}/2\sigma^{2}}|T_{k}(y)|dy

Since
y\in\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R,R]
and
R\geq 1
, we have
|T_{k}(y)|\leq|2y|^{k}
. Let us write
y=x+\delta
and note that
|2y|^{k}\leq(2(1+|\delta|))^{k}\leq e^{k(1+|\delta|)}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R,R]}e^{-(x-y)^{2}/2\sigma^{2}}|T_{k}(y)|dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\int_{|\delta|\geq R-1}e^{-\frac{\delta^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}+k(1+|\delta|)}d\delta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\int_{|\delta|\geq R-1}e^{-\frac{\delta^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}}d\delta

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\,\mathbb{P}_{\delta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,2\sigma^{2})}[|\delta|\geq R-1]

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{2}e^{-(R-1)^{2}/(4\sigma^{2})}

where in the last inequality we use
Eq.

13
. The second inequality holds since
R-1\geq 2(k\sigma^{2}+\sqrt{k}\sigma\sqrt{k\sigma^{2}+1})
, which in turn is a consequence of the assumptions that
R-1\geq\gamma(2+\sqrt{2})\sqrt{k}\sigma
and
k\sigma^{2}\leq 1
. Indeed, under those assumptions we have
R-1\geq 2(\sqrt{k}\sigma+\sqrt{k}\sigma\sqrt{2})\geq 2(k\sigma^{2}+\sqrt{k}\sigma\sqrt{k\sigma^{2}+1})
. The lemma then follows by observing that
e^{-(R-1)^{2}/(4\sigma^{2})}\leq e^{-\gamma^{2}}
.
∎

3.3
An SOS kernel

The next step is to approximate the truncated Gaussian kernel by an SOS kernel.
To do so, we first approximate the function
t\mapsto\exp(-t)
by an SOS polynomial using the following lemma.

Lemma 17

(cf.
[
20
, Thrm 4.1]
)
.

For every
b>0
and
0\leq\delta<1
, there exists a polynomial
s_{b,\delta}
with degree

\mathrm{deg}(s_{b,\delta})=\left\lceil\sqrt{2t\log(4/\delta)}\right\rceil,\mbox{ where }t=\left\lceil\mathrm{max}\left\{\frac{1}{2}be^{2},\log(2/\delta)\right\}\right\rceil,

(14)

such that

|\exp(-t)-s_{b,\delta}(t)|\leq\delta\quad\forall t\in[0,b].

An immediate corollary to the lemma is obtained by approximating
t\mapsto\exp(-2t)
by
s_{b,\delta}^{2}
.

Corollary 18
.

For every
b>0
and
0\leq\delta<1
, there exists a polynomial
s_{b,\delta}
with degree as in (
14
),
such that

|\exp(-2t)-s_{b,\delta}^{2}(t)|\leq 2\delta+\delta^{2}\leq 3\delta
for all
t\in[0,b]
.

We now define the SOS kernel that we will use, namely:

K_{r}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}s^{2}_{b,\delta}\left(\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}\right),

for the following choices of parameters:

\delta=r^{-7/2},\quad\sigma=\frac{\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)}}{r},\quad\gamma=\sqrt{\frac{5}{2}\log(r)},\quad R-1=\gamma(2+\sqrt{2})\sqrt{d}\sigma,\quad b=\frac{(R+1)^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}.

(15)

For this parameter choice, by
Corollary

18
, we have

\displaystyle\deg(K_{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\deg(s_{b,\delta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\left\lceil\sqrt{2\left\lceil\frac{1}{2}be^{2}\right\rceil\log(4/\delta)}\right\rceil

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\sqrt{(be^{2}+2)[\log(1/\delta)+\log 4]}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\left(e\frac{R+1}{\sigma}+\sqrt{2}\right)(\sigma r+\sqrt{\log 4})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4e\cdot r(R+1)+4\sqrt{2}\sigma\cdot r+\sqrt{2\log 4}\cdot e\cdot\frac{R+1}{\sigma}+\sqrt{2\log 4}.

Using that

R+1=2+\sqrt{35}(1+1/\sqrt{2})\sqrt{d}\frac{\log r}{r}\leq 10\mbox{ if $r\geq d$},

as well as
\sigma=\frac{\sqrt{\frac{7}{2}\log r}}{r}
,
one obtains, for
r\geq d
,

\deg(K_{r})\leq 40e\cdot r+4\sqrt{7\log r}+10\sqrt{2\log 4}\cdot e\frac{r}{\sqrt{\frac{7}{2}\log r}}+\sqrt{2\log 4}\\
<167r=O(r).

(16)

The convolution operator associated with
K_{r}
is

\mathcal{K}_{r}(f)(x):=\int_{[-R,R]}K_{r}(x,y)f(y)dy.

By
Corollary

18
, we immediately obtain the following results.

Lemma 19
.

For the parameters
\delta,\sigma,R,b
as in
Eq.

15
one has

\left|K_{r}(x,y)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\exp{\left(-(x-y)^{2}/2\sigma^{2}\right)}\right|\leq\frac{3\delta}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\qquad\forall x\in[-1,1],\;\forall y\in[-R,R].

Proof.

Fix
x\in[-1,1]
and
y=[-R,R]
and set
t=\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}
. Note that
0\leq t=\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}\leq\frac{(R+1)^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}=b
. We therefore have

\left|K_{r}(x,y)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\exp{\left(-(x-y)^{2}/2\sigma^{2}\right)}\right|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}|\exp(-2t)-s_{b,\delta}(t)^{2}|\leq\frac{3\delta}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma},

where the last inequality follows from
Corollary

18
.
∎

Next we show that our SOS kernel approximates the truncated Gaussian kernel well enough, under suitable assumptions on
R
.

Lemma 20
.

Let the parameters
\delta,\sigma,R,b
be as in
Eq.

15
. For any fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\|\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{k}-\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma,R}T_{k}\|_{\infty}\leq 2R\frac{3\delta}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\cdot\mathrm{max}_{y\in[-R,R]}|T_{k}(y)|.

In particular, if
R\leq 1+1/(10k^{2})
, then we have
\|\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{k}-\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma,R}T_{k}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{24\delta}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}
.

Proof.

Fix
x\in[-1,1]
. We use the triangle inequality and
Lemma

19
to obtain

\displaystyle|\mathcal{K}_{r}(T_{k})(x)-\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma,R}(T_{k})(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{[-R,R]}[K_{r}(x,y)-K_{G}^{\sigma}(x,y)]T_{k}(y)dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{[-R,R]}\left|K_{r}(x,y)-K_{G}^{\sigma}(x,y)\right|\left|T_{k}(y)\right|dy

\displaystyle\leq 2R\frac{3\delta}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\cdot\mathrm{max}_{y\in[-R,R]}|T_{k}(y)|.

Finally we note that if
R\leq 1+1/(10k^{2})
, then
R\leq 2
and
\mathrm{max}_{y\in[-R,R]}|T_{k}(y)|\leq 2
, from which the last claim follows.
∎

We finally observe that this operator maps polynomials that are nonnegative on
[-R,R]
to SOS polynomials of degree
O(r)
.

Proposition 21
.

Let
R\geq 1,\sigma^{2}\geq 0,r\in\mathbb{N}
, and assume
f\in\mathbb{R}[x]_{d}
is nonnegative on the interval
[-R,R]
. Then
\mathcal{K}_{r}f
is an SOS polynomial of the same degree as
K_{r}
.

Proof.

Assume we have a quadrature rule of degree
O(r+d)
for the Lebesgue measure on
[-R,R]
that is given by nodes
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{N}\in[-R,R]
with corresponding weights
c_{1}>0,\ldots,c_{N}>0
, for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
.
We then have

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{r}(f)(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{[-R,R]}K_{r}(x,y)f(y)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}c_{j}K_{r}(x,\omega_{j})f(\omega_{j}).

By construction
K_{r}(\cdot,\omega_{j})
is an SOS polynomial of degree
\deg(K_{r})
for each
j
. Moreover, by assumption,
f(\omega_{j})\geq 0
for each
j\in[N]
. The result follows.
∎

4
Proof of main result

In this section we prove our main result, first for the univariate case, and then, in Section
4.2
, we
show how to leverage the univariate approach to establish our main result in the multivariate setting.

4.1
Proof in the univariate case

Theorem 5

(Approximate identity)
.

Fix
d\in\mathbb{N}
and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
k\leq d
. Let
r\in\mathbb{N}
and choose
\delta=r^{-7/2}
,

\sigma=\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)}/r
, and
R=1+\sqrt{\frac{5}{2}\log(r)}(2+\sqrt{2})\sqrt{d}\sigma
, as in
Eq.

15
.
Assume
r
is sufficiently large so that
k^{6}\sigma^{2}\leq 1
and
R\leq 1+1/(10d^{2})
, i.e. assume

\frac{r}{\log r}\geq\mathrm{max}\left\{10\sqrt{35}\left(1+1/\sqrt{2}\right)d^{5/2},\frac{7d^{6}}{2r}\right\}.

(17)

Then we have
\deg(K_{r})=O(r)
and

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{k}-T_{k}\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}

\displaystyle\leq 2k^{15/2}\frac{\log(r)}{r^{2}}+\sqrt{2(r+1)}\left(2\sqrt{2}r^{-5/2}+\frac{24}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{r^{-5/2}}{\sqrt{\frac{7}{2}\log(r)}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(2d^{15/2}+14\right)\frac{\log(r)}{r^{2}}.

Proof.

We follow the proof strategy outlined at the start of
Section

3
.
We first use the triangle inequality to obtain

\|\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{k}-T_{k}\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\leq\|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}T_{k}-T_{k}\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}+\|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}T_{k}-\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{k}\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}},

where we use that
\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}T_{k}
is a polynomial due to
Lemma

11
.
Lemma

14
shows that
\|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}T_{k}-T_{k}\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\leq 2k^{15/2}\sigma^{2}
, since, by assumption,
k^{6}\sigma^{2}\leq 1
.

It remains to bound
\|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}T_{k}-\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{k}\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}
. To do so, we first use
Lemma

4
to upper bound the
1
-norm by the sup-norm:

\|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}T_{k}-\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{k}\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\leq\sqrt{2(r+1)}\|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}T_{k}-\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{k}\|_{\infty}.

We now use the triangle inequality to obtain

\|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}T_{k}-\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{k}\|_{\infty}\leq\|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}T_{k}-\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma,R}T_{k}\|_{\infty}+\|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma,R}T_{k}-\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{k}\|_{\infty}.

Now, by
Lemma

16
, we can bound the first quantity by

\|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma}T_{k}-\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma,R}T_{k}\|_{\infty}\leq 2\sqrt{2}r^{-5/2},

where we use that
k\sigma^{2}\leq 1
and
R-1\geq\gamma(2+\sqrt{2})\sqrt{k}\sigma
for
\gamma=\sqrt{\frac{5}{2}\log(r)}
. Similarly, using
Lemma

20
, we have

\|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{\sigma,R}T_{k}-\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{k}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{24\delta}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}=\frac{24}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{r^{-5/2}}{\sqrt{\frac{7}{2}\log(r)}},

where we use that
R\leq 1+1/(10d^{2})
.

Combining the above bounds yields the claimed upper bound on
\|\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{k}-T_{k}\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}
.
∎

We finally arrive at our main theorem in the univariate setting, namely a weaker version of Theorem
3
that may — unlike
Theorem
3
—
readily be extended to the multivariate case.
For this we need a final ingredient: a lower bound on
\mathrm{min}_{x\in[-R,R]}f(x)
for degree-
d
polynomials that are nonnegative on
[-1,1]
.
Such a bound can be found for example in the work of Baldi and Slot
[
2
]
, even in the multivariate setting.

Lemma 22

(
[
2
]
)
.

Assume
f\in\mathbb{R}[x]_{d}
is positive on
[-1,1]^{n}
with minimum and maximum values
0<f_{\mathrm{min}}<f_{\mathrm{max}}
and that
R>1
.
Then there exists an absolute constant
c\in[1,e^{5}]
, where
e
is the base of the natural logarithm, such that

f-\frac{1}{2}f_{\mathrm{min}}
is nonnegative on
[-R,R]^{n}
if

R\leq 1+\frac{f_{\mathrm{min}}}{2cd^{2}f_{\mathrm{max}}}.

We may now prove a weaker version of the Markov-Lucacs theorem (Theorem
3
) that may readily be extended to the multivariate case.

Theorem 6
.

Assume
f\in\mathbb{R}[x]_{d}
is positive on
[-1,1]
with minimum and maximum values
0<f_{\mathrm{min}}<f_{\mathrm{max}}
. Let
r\in\mathbb{N}
be sufficiently large such that (
17
) holds as
well as

\frac{r}{\log r}\geq 150d^{5/2}\frac{f_{\mathrm{max}}}{f_{\mathrm{min}}}.

(18)

Then we have

f+\varepsilon\in\mathcal{Q}(1-x^{2})_{2t},

where
t=O(r)
and
\varepsilon=O(\|f\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\log(r)/r^{2})
. In particular, we may assume

t=167r,\mbox{ and }\varepsilon=\left(2d^{15/2}+14\right)\|f\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\log(r)/r^{2}.

Proof.

Let
r
meet the conditions in the statement of the theorem, and construct the associated convolution
operator
\mathcal{K}_{r}
, where the parameters
\delta,\sigma,R
are as in
Theorem

5
.
By
Lemma

22
and our assumption on
r
, we have that
f>0
on
[-R,R]
.
Therefore, by
Proposition

21
, we have that
\mathcal{K}_{r}f\in\mathcal{Q}(1-x^{2})_{t}
for some
t=O(r)
.
By
Theorem

5
we moreover have
\|\mathcal{K}_{r}f-f\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}=O(\|f\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\log(r)/r^{2})
.
To conclude the proof, it thus suffices to apply
Theorem

4
:

f+\|\mathcal{K}_{r}f-f\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\in\mathcal{Q}(1-x^{2})_{2t}.\qed

4.2
Extension to the multivariate setting

Here we show how to extend the results from
Section

4.1
to the multivariate setting.

For the sake of readability, let us assume the following.
Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed and
r\in\mathbb{N}
with
r
meets the conditions (
17
) and (
18
), and assume there exists a univariate kernel
K_{r}(x,y)
for all
x\in[-1,1]
,
y\in[-R,R]
for some
R>1
with the following properties:

1.

\|\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{k}-T_{k}\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\leq\varepsilon
for all
0\leq k\leq d
.

2.

K_{r}(x,y)=s_{r}(x,y)^{2}
for all
x\in[-1,1],y\in[-R,R]
, where
s_{r}\in\mathbb{R}[x]_{t}
for
t=O(r)
.

We now construct a multivariate kernel
K_{r,n}(x,y)
for
x\in[-1,1]^{n},y\in[-R,R]^{n}
by setting

K_{r,n}(x,y)=\prod_{i\in[n]}K_{r}(x_{i},y_{i}).

We first show that the first property of
\mathcal{K}_{r}
implies that
\mathcal{K}
approximately preserves multivariate Chebyshev polynomials
T_{\alpha}(x)=\prod_{i\in[n]}T_{\alpha_{i}}(x_{i})
in the following sense.

Lemma 23
.

Let
\alpha\in(\mathbb{N}_{0})^{n}
be such that
\alpha_{i}\leq d
for all
i\in[n]
. Then we have

\|\mathcal{K}_{r,n}T_{\alpha}-T_{\alpha}\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\leq\varepsilon\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(1+\varepsilon)^{i}.

Moreover, if
\varepsilon\leq 1/n
, then
\|\mathcal{K}_{r,n}T_{\alpha}-T_{\alpha}\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\leq e\varepsilon n
.

Proof.

We prove the lemma using induction on
n
. The base case
n=1
holds by our assumption on
\mathcal{K}_{r}
. Now assume that
n>1
and that the bound holds for up to
n-1
variables.

We first observe that

\mathcal{K}_{r,n}T_{\alpha}(x)=\int K_{r,n}(x,y)T_{\alpha}(y)dy=\prod_{i\in[n]}\left(\int K_{r}(x_{i},y_{i})T_{\alpha_{i}}(y_{i})dy_{i}\right)=\prod_{i\in[n]}\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{\alpha_{i}}(x_{i}).

Hence

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{r,n}T_{\alpha}-T_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\in[n]}\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{\alpha_{i}}-\prod_{i\in[n]}T_{\alpha_{i}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\in[n]}\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{\alpha_{i}}-\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{\alpha_{n}}\prod_{i\in[n-1]}T_{\alpha_{i}}+\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{\alpha_{n}}\prod_{i\in[n-1]}T_{\alpha_{i}}-\prod_{i\in[n]}T_{\alpha_{i}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{\alpha_{n}}\left(\prod_{i\in[n-1]}\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{\alpha_{i}}-\prod_{i\in[n-1]}T_{\alpha_{i}}\right)+\left(\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{\alpha_{n}}-T_{\alpha_{n}}\right)\prod_{i\in[n-1]}T_{\alpha_{i}}.

Using the triangle inequality and submultiplicativity of
\|\cdot\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}
we thus obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{K}_{r,n}T_{\alpha}-T_{\alpha}\right\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{\alpha_{n}}\left(\prod_{i\in[n-1]}\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{\alpha_{i}}-\prod_{i\in[n-1]}T_{\alpha_{i}}\right)\right\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}+\left\|\left(\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{\alpha_{n}}-T_{\alpha_{n}}\right)\prod_{i\in[n-1]}T_{\alpha_{i}}\right\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{\alpha_{n}}\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\left\|\prod_{i\in[n-1]}\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{\alpha_{i}}-\prod_{i\in[n-1]}T_{\alpha_{i}}\right\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}+\|\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{\alpha_{n}}-T_{\alpha_{n}}\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\left\|\prod_{i\in[n-1]}T_{\alpha_{i}}\right\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon)\left\|\prod_{i\in[n-1]}\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{\alpha_{i}}-\prod_{i\in[n-1]}T_{\alpha_{i}}\right\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}+\varepsilon,

where in the last inequality we have used
\|\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{\alpha_{n}}-T_{\alpha_{n}}\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\leq\varepsilon
,
\|\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{\alpha_{n}}\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\leq\|T_{\alpha_{n}}\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}+\|\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{\alpha_{n}}-T_{\alpha_{n}}\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\leq 1+\varepsilon
, and
\|\prod_{i\in[n-1]}T_{\alpha_{i}}\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}=1
. We finally use the induction hypothesis to conclude

\|\mathcal{K}_{r,n}T_{\alpha}-T_{\alpha}\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\left(\varepsilon\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}(1+\varepsilon)^{i}\right)+\varepsilon=\varepsilon\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(1+\varepsilon)^{i}.

∎

Next, we show that the second property of
\mathcal{K}_{r}
implies that
\mathcal{K}_{r,n}
maps positive polynomials to SOS polynomials.
Using the same argument as in the proof of
Proposition

21
, we have the following.

Proposition 24
.

Assume
f\in\mathbb{R}[x]_{d}
is nonnegative on
[-R,R]^{n}
, then
\mathcal{K}_{r,n}f
is an SOS polynomial of degree
2nt=O(nr)
.

We are now ready to prove our main result.

Theorem 7
.

Assume
f\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]_{d}
is positive on
[-1,1]^{n}
with minimum and maximum values
0<f_{\mathrm{min}}<f_{\mathrm{max}}
. Let
r\in\mathbb{N}
be such that (
17
) and (
18
) hold. Then we have

f+\widetilde{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{Q}(1-x_{1}^{2},\ldots,1-x_{n}^{2})_{2nt},

where
t=O(r)
and
\widetilde{\varepsilon}=O(\|f\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\log(r)/r^{2})
.
In particular, we may assume

t=167r,\mbox{ and }\tilde{\varepsilon}=e\cdot n\left(2d^{15/2}+14\right)\|f\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\log(r)/r^{2}.

Proof.

Let
r
satisfy (
17
) and (
18
), and let the univariate kernel
K_{r}
be as in
Theorem

5
, and note that by our assumption on
r
we have
\varepsilon\coloneqq\mathrm{max}_{k\in\mathbb{N}:0\leq k\leq d}\|\mathcal{K}_{r}T_{k}-T_{k}\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\leq 1/n
. Let the parameters
\delta,\sigma,R
be as in
Theorem

5
. By
Lemma

22
and our assumption on
r
, we have that
f>0
on
[-R,R]^{n}
.
Therefore, by
Proposition

24
, we have that
\mathcal{K}_{r,n}f\in\mathcal{Q}(1-x_{1}^{2},\ldots,1-x_{n}^{2})_{nt}
for some
t=O(r)
. By
Lemma

23
we moreover have
\|\mathcal{K}_{r}f-f\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\leq e\varepsilon n\|f\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}=O(\|f\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\log(r)/r^{2})
. To conclude the proof, it thus suffices to apply
Theorem

4
: we have

f+\|\mathcal{K}_{r,n}f-f\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\in\mathcal{Q}(1-x_{1}^{2},\ldots,1-x_{n}^{2})_{2nt}.\qed

We may now infer some results on the rate of convergence of the Lasserre hierarchy.

Corollary 25
.

Consider problem (
1
) of minimizing a polynomial
f
of degree
d
over
[-1,1]^{n}
, and the associated Lasserre hierarchy (
4
).
Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. If
r\in\mathbb{N}
satisfies

\frac{r}{\log r}\geq 300\frac{d^{5/2}}{\varepsilon},

then, for
t=167r
one has

f_{\mathrm{min}}-f_{(2nt)}\leq\varepsilon(f_{\mathrm{max}}-f_{\mathrm{min}})+e\cdot n\left(2d^{15/2}+14\right)\|f\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\log(r)/r^{2}.

Proof.

The result follows immediately by applying Theorem
7
to the function

\tilde{f}:=f-f_{\mathrm{min}}+\varepsilon(f_{\mathrm{max}}-f_{\mathrm{min}}),

and noting that

\frac{\tilde{f}_{\mathrm{max}}}{\tilde{f}_{\mathrm{min}}}=\frac{1+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}<\frac{2}{\varepsilon}.

∎

A few remarks on our results:

•

There are no constants in Corollary
25
with explicit exponential dependence on
d
and
n
, unlike the
C(n,d)
in Theorem
2
. The quantity
\|f\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}
may depend exponentially on
n
and
d
, but it also may not in some interesting cases.
For general
f\in\mathbb{R}[x]_{d}
, one has

\|f\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\leq\left(2^{d}\binom{n+d}{d}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{\infty},

that follows from the (straightforward) multivariate extension of Lemma
4
. On the other hand, for special cases like the maximum cut problem in graphs (as discussed in the introduction), one has
\|f\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\leq 2\|f\|_{\infty}
; see
[
5
]
for a detailed discussion of this.

•

One should note that, for fixed
\varepsilon>0
, the Lasserre
hierarchy yields an
\varepsilon
-approximation of
f_{\mathrm{min}}
after a
finite
number of steps, since it converges to
f_{\mathrm{min}}
.
For this reason,
Corollary
25
should not be seen as an asymptotic convergence result as
r\gg 0
, and we have therefore avoided big-O notation, i.e. we have made all constants explicit.

5
Limits to our proof technique

Our approach is based on the best degree-
r
SOS approximation of
f-f_{\mathrm{min}}
on
[-1,1]^{n}
in the
\|\cdot\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}
norm, as detailed in Corollary
10
.
Here we argue that
an SOS
\delta
-approximation in the Chebyshev basis requires degree
\Omega(1/\sqrt{\delta})
. Subsequently, we present a numerical investigation to get an indication whether this bound is tight.

5.1
Bounds based on examples by Stengle

We first recall degree lower- and upper bounds due to Stengle based on a non-standard algebraic description of the cube
[
22
]
.

Theorem 8

(Stengle, lower bound)
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(1-x^{2})+\varepsilon\in\mathcal{Q}((1-x^{2})^{3})_{r}\Rightarrow r=\Omega(1/\sqrt{\varepsilon}).

Theorem 9

(Stengle, upper bound)
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an
r=O(\log(1/\varepsilon)/\sqrt{\varepsilon})
such that

(1-x^{2})+\varepsilon\in\mathcal{Q}((1-x^{2})^{3})_{r}.

Our goal is to prove the following.

Theorem 10
.

If
q
is a degree-
r
sum of squares with
\|(1-x^{2})-q\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\leq\delta
, then we have
r=\Omega(1/\sqrt{\delta})
.

Before we prove the theorem, let us prove a useful lemma.

Lemma 26
.

If
R>0
and
d\in\mathbb{N}
are such that
R-x\in\mathcal{Q}((1-x^{2})^{3})_{d}
, then, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

R-x^{k},R+x^{k}\in\operatorname{\mathcal{Q}}((1-x^{2})^{3})_{dk}
and
R-T_{k}(x),R+T_{k}(x)\in\operatorname{\mathcal{Q}}((1-x^{2})^{3})_{dk}
.

Proof.

Assume
R-x\in\mathcal{Q}((1-x^{2})^{3})_{d}
. Note that then also
R+x\in\mathcal{Q}((1-x^{2})^{3})_{d}
, which follows from
R+x=R-(-x)
and the observation that
(1-x^{2})^{3}=(1-(-x)^{2})^{3}
.
Then for
\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}
, we have

R-\varepsilon x=(1-x^{2})^{3}\alpha(x)+\beta(x),

(19)

where
\deg\alpha\leq d-6
,
\deg\beta\leq d
and
\alpha
and
\beta
are SOS.
Substituting
x\mapsto x^{k}
in (
19
) we obtain

R-\varepsilon x^{k}=(1-x^{2k})^{3}\alpha(x^{k})+\beta(x^{k})=(1-x^{2})^{3}(1+x^{2}+\cdots+x^{2k-2})^{3}\alpha(x^{k})+\beta(x^{k})\in\operatorname{\mathcal{Q}}((1-x^{2})^{3})_{dk}.

This proves the lemma in the monomial case.

For the Chebyshev case, we use the cosine representation.
Substituting
x\mapsto\cos(kt)
in (
19
) we obtain,

R-\varepsilon\cos(kt)=(1-\cos^{2}(kt))^{3}\alpha(\cos(kt))+\beta(cos(kt))=\sin^{6}(kt)\alpha(\cos(kt))+\beta(\cos(kt)).

By an inductive argument
\sin(kt)=\sin(t)p_{k}(\cos(t))
, where
p_{k}
is a degree
k-1
polynomial. Then,

R-\varepsilon\cos(kt)=\sin^{6}(t)p_{k}(\cos(t))^{6}\alpha(\cos(kt))+\beta(\cos(kt)).

Equivalently,

R-\varepsilon T_{k}(x)=(1-x^{2})^{3}p_{k}(x)^{6}\alpha(T_{k}(x))+\beta(T_{k}(x))\in\operatorname{\mathcal{Q}}((1-x^{2})^{3})_{dk}.

This proves the lemma in the Chebyshev case.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

10
.

We will set
R=2
. By
Theorem

9
we have
3-x^{2}=1+2-x^{2}\in\mathcal{Q}((1-x^{2})^{3})_{d}
for some
d\in O(1)
. Using the identity
1+\varepsilon/2-x=\tfrac{1}{2}((1-x)^{2}+1+\varepsilon-x^{2})
, for
\varepsilon=1
, we obtain
R-x=2-x\in\operatorname{\mathcal{Q}}((1-x^{2})^{3})_{d}
for
d=O(1)
.
Lemma

26
then implies that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have
R-T_{k}(x),R+T_{k}(x)\in\mathcal{Q}((1-x^{2})^{3})_{dk}
.
This yields the following variant of
Corollary

9
: for any polynomial
p
, we have
2\|p\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}+p\in\mathcal{Q}((1-x^{2})^{3})_{d\deg(p)}
. In particular, for
p=(1-x^{2})-q
we have

2\|(1-x^{2})-q\|_{1,\mathrm{mon}}+(1-x^{2})-q\in\mathcal{Q}((1-x^{2})^{3})_{dr},

which, since
q
is a sum of squares, means

(1-x^{2})+2\|(1-x^{2})-q\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\in\mathcal{Q}((1-x^{2})^{3})_{dr}.

Note that
dr\in O(r)
since
d\in O(1)
. We can now apply
Theorem

8
with
\varepsilon=2\delta
to obtain that
r=\Omega(1/\sqrt{\delta})
.
∎

5.2
Numerical investigation

In this section we do a numerical investigation to construct kernels that give SOS approximations in the
\|\cdot\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}
norm, to give an indication
if it is possible to improve on our construction.
To this end, for fixed
d\in N
, we consider a family of kernels of the form

K_{r}(x,y):=1+2\sum_{k=1}^{d}\lambda_{k}T_{k}(x)T_{k}(y)+2\sum_{i,j=d+1}^{r}\alpha_{ij}T_{i}(x)T_{j}(y)\quad\quad r>d,

(20)

where the
\lambda_{k}\in[0,1]
and
\alpha_{ij}\in[0,1]
are fixed coefficients. As before, one has the associated approximation (convolution) operators

\mathcal{K}_{r}(f)(x)=\int_{-1}^{1}K_{r}(x,y)f(y)\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{1-y^{2}}}\mathrm{d}y\quad\quad f\in\mathbb{R}[x],\;r\in\mathbb{N}.

If
f\in\mathbb{R}[x]_{d}
, say
f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}f_{k}T_{k}
, and
r>d
, one therefore has

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{K}_{r}(f)-f\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{d}(\lambda_{k}-1)f_{k}T_{k}\right\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{d}|(\lambda_{k}-1)f_{k}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathrm{max}_{\ell\in[d]}|\lambda_{\ell}-1|\cdot\|f\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}.

In the spirit of the works
[
9
,
5
]
one may now construct an optimal kernel via semidefinite programming
by solving, for fixed
d<r\in\mathbb{N}
:

v_{r,d}:=\mathrm{min}_{\lambda_{k},\alpha_{ij}\in[0,1]}\mathrm{max}_{\ell\in[d]}\left\{|\lambda_{\ell}-1|\;:\;K_{r}(x,y)\in\Sigma[x,y]_{2r}\mbox{ of the form }\eqref{Kernel_Juan}\right\}.

(21)

By construction, one therefore has that if
f\in\mathbb{R}[x]_{d}
, then
\|\mathcal{K}_{r}(f)-f\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}\leq v_{r,d}\cdot\|f\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}
. Moreover,
if
f
is nonnegative in
[-1,1]
, then
\mathcal{K}_{r}(f)
is SOS.

6
Conclusion and discussion

It remains an open question as to what the exact rate of convergence of the Lasserre hierarchy is for problem (
1
).
As we recalled in Corollary
1
, Baldi and Slot
[
2
]
showed a convergence rate of
O(1/r)
, but in the same
paper they also showed a lower bound of
\Omega(1/r^{8})
.
There is therefore still quite some gap between the known lower and upper bounds.

There is some reason to expect the exact rate to be
O(1/r^{2})
. Indeed, this rate has been shown by Laurent and Slot
[
14
]
(see also
[
5
]
) for the related SDP
hierarchy where the quadratic module (
3
) is replaced by the pre-ordering

\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{g})=\Sigma[x]+\sum_{\mathrm{I}\subseteq[n]}\Sigma[x]\prod_{i\in\mathrm{I}}g_{i},

which is a superset of the quadratic module (
3
). SDP hierarchies of this type are sometimes called Schmüdgen hierarchies,
with reference to the celebrated Schmüdgen positivstellensatz
[
21
]
, which states that — if one replaces the quadratic module in the statement of
Theorem
1
by the pre-ordering — then one may omit the Archimedean assumption. (It is worth noting that Baldi and Slot
[
2
]
leveraged the results by Laurent and Slot
[
14
]

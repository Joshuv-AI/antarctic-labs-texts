Elementwise conservative actions and new constructions of boomerang subgroups
Yair Glasner, Tobias Hartnick, Waltraud Lederle

Abstract
. We show that countable non-abelian free groups admit uncountably many mutually singular elementwise conservative non-singular random subgroups, which are supported on infinite subgroups of infinite index and singular with respect to every invariant random subgroup. This complements recent rigidity results for elementwise-conservative random subgroups in higher rank lattices by the first- and third-named authors. Our proof is based on a study of representations of free groups into measurable full groups in which the action of the first generator of the free group is fixed. We show that elementwise conservativity is generic among such representations in the sense of Baire category.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Dynamical rigidity and flexibility

One of the main themes of modern group theory is the dichotomy between
flexibility
and
rigidity
. For example, lattices in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\R)
admit a rich space of deformations (closely related to Teichmüller space) whereas lattices in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
or
\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\R)
do not admit any non-trivial deformations at all - this is Mostow’s celebrated rigidity theorem. Lattices in higher rank simple Lie groups (such as
\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\R)
) satisfy a whole plethora of rigidity properties, from Kazhdan’s Property (T) and various strengthenings thereof to Margulis’ superrigidity and its generalizations.

It was observed at an early stage that rigidity properties of higher rank lattices
\Lambda
are closely connected to
dynamical rigidity properties
of the action of the ambient Lie group
G
on the homogeneous space
G/\Lambda
, which preserves an invariant probability measure. This lead to the study of rigidity properties of general
probability measure preserving (p.m.p.) actions
of higher rank Lie groups. Notably, Nevo, Stuck and Zimmer
[
SZ94
]
,
[
NZ02
]
established that every ergodic p.m.p. action of a center-free simple higher rank Lie group
G
is either essentially free or transitive.

The Nevo–Stuck–Zimmer theorem can be stated equivalently in the language of conjugation-invariant probability measures on the Chabauty space
\mathrm{Sub}(G)
of closed subgroups of
G
, a.k.a.
invariant random subgroups
(IRSs). Indeed, since every p.m.p. action
G\curvearrowright(X,\mu)
gives rise to an IRS by pushing
\mu
forward via the
stabilizer map

\mathrm{Stab}\colon X\to\mathrm{Sub(G)},\quad x\mapsto\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(x)

and since every IRS arises from a p.m.p. action in this way
[
AGV14
,
ABB+17
]
, the Nevo–Stuck–Zimmer theorem says that every ergodic IRS of a center-free simple higher rank Lie group is either trivial or supported on the conjugation orbit of a lattice. In particular, a generic point of such an IRS is either trivial or a lattice; similar rigidity results also hold for IRSs of higher rank lattices.

On the contrary, rank one Lie groups (and, more generally, hyperbolic locally compact groups) and their lattices admit plenty of non-trivial IRSs
[
ABB+20
]
- in this sense they are
dynamically flexible
. This flexibility already shows in the easiest non-trivial example, namely non-abelian finitely generated free groups.

In recent years it has become more and more apparent that dynamical rigidity results in higher rank concern not only p.m.p. actions but also other classes of non-singular actions. Notably, motivated by applications to Margulis’ unbounded injectivity radius conjecture, Gelander and Fraczyk
[
FG23
]
initiated a study of
stationary random subgroups
(SRSs), i.e. stationary probability measures on Chabauty spaces, and established a version of the Nevo–Stuck–Zimmer theorem in this context.

Similarly, the work of the first and the third author in
[
GL25
]
implies a version of the Nevo–Stuck–Zimmer theorem for a different class of non-singular random subgroups which we call
elementwise conservative random subgroups
(ECRSs). The present article, which can be seen as a companion article to this work, is devoted to an instance of
dynamical flexibility of ECRSs
. Namely, we are going to show that a countable non-abelian free group admits plenty of ECRSs which are singular with respect to every IRS.

1.2.
ECRSs and boomerang subgroups

In order to state our first main result, we now introduce the class of non-singular systems we will be working with; see Subsection
2.2
below for general background and terminology concerning non-singular transformations. Given a discrete group
\Lambda
, we denote by
\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)
the space of subgroups of
\Lambda
, which is a compact
\Lambda
-space when equipped with the action by conjugation and the topology as a closed subset of
\{0,1\}^{\Lambda}
. Recall that a non-singular transformation
T
is called
conservative
if every
T
-wandering set is a nullset (see Subsection
2.4
for various equivalent characterizations).

Definition
.

Let
\Lambda
be a countable discrete group.

(i)

A non-singular action of
\Lambda
on a standard probability space
(X,\mu)
is called
elementwise conservative
(or an
e.c. action
for short) if every
\gamma\in\Lambda
acts by a conservative transformation.

(ii)

A probability measure
\mu
on
\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)
is called an
e.c. random subgroup (ECRS)
if the conjugation action of
\Lambda
on
(\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda),\mu)
is non-singular and e.c..

(iii)

A subgroup
\Delta\in\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)
is called a
boomerang subgroup
if for every
\gamma\in\Lambda
there exists a sequence
n_{k}\to\infty
with
\gamma^{n_{k}}\Delta\gamma^{-n_{k}}\to\Delta
in
\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)
.

It follows from the Poincaré recurrence theorem that every p.m.p. action is e.c., and hence every IRS is an ECRS. Moreover, almost every instance of an ECRS (and hence of an IRS) is a boomerang subgroup. Schematically,

p.m.p. action
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{stabilizer}}
IRS
e.c. action
stabilizer
ECRS
almost surely

boomerang subgroup.

Note that while IRSs and ECRSs are concepts from non-singular dynamics, boomerang subgroups belong to the realm of topological dynamics.

Remark
.

Unlike ergodicity or stationarity, the e.c. property is an
elementwise
property of an action. Asking for the action to be
globally
conservative (in the sense that for every positive measure set
A
there is some
1\neq\gamma\in\Lambda
such that
\mu(A\cap\gamma(A))>0
) is a much weaker notion. In fact, as shown in Lemma
Lemma
,
every
non-singular random subgroup is globally conservative, whereas elementwise conservativity is much more restrictive.

On first sight, an elementary conservative action is just a countable collection of conservative
\Z
-actions, one for every element of the group. However, the fact that these different actions combine into an action of some given group
\Lambda
often puts severe constraints on possible e.c. actions of
\Lambda
as illustrated by the following rigidity result from
[
GL25
]
.

Theorem

(
[
GL25
, Corollary 1.3]
)
.

Let
\Lambda
be a lattice in a center-free simple Lie group
G
, and assume that
\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Lambda)\geq 2
. Then, every nontrivial ergodic ECRS of
\Lambda
is supported on finite index subgroups. In fact, every nontrivial boomerang subgroup of
\Lambda
is of finite index.

This rigidity result is in line with the general philosophy explained above: One expects ECRSs (and hence boomerang subgroups) to be rare in higher rank situations, but abundant in rank one. The goal of the present article is to partially confirm the expectation of dynamical flexibility of ECRSs in rank one by showing:

Theorem A

(Existence of exotic ECRSs)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable non-abelian free group. Then
\Gamma
admits uncountably many mutually singular ergodic ECRSs which are singular with respect to every IRS and supported on core-free, co-amenable and co-highly transitive subgroups.

Here, a subgroup
\Delta<\Gamma
is called
core-free
if
\bigcap_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\gamma\Delta\gamma^{-1}=\{1\}
; it is called
co-amenable
if the left action
\Gamma\curvearrowright\Gamma/\Delta
is amenable in the sense of Greenleaf, i.e. admits
(F,\varepsilon)
-almost invariant sets for every finite subset
F\subset\Gamma
and every
\varepsilon>0
. Finally it is called
co-highly transitive
if the action of
\Gamma\curvearrowright\Gamma/\Delta
is transitive on ordered
k
-tuples of distinct points, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Every core-free subgroup of an infinite group is of infinite index, every co-amenable subgroup of a non-amenable group is infinite, and every co-highly transitive group is maximal. Thus Theorem
A
produces exotic boomerangs which are infinite, of infinite index and maximal in the ambient free group.

1.3.
Representations into measurable full groups

We now outline a general strategy towards the proof of Theorem
A
. Thus let
\Gamma
be a countable non-abelian free group. Firstly, in order to construct an ECRS as in the statement of the theorem, it suffices to construct a non-singular action of
\Gamma
on a standard probability space
(X,\mu)
with the following additional properties:

(1)

The action is faithful and e.c., but there is no
\Gamma
-invariant probability measure in the measure class of
\mu
.

(2)

The stabilizer map
\mathrm{Stab}\colon X\to\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma)
is injective (hence a Borel isomorphism onto its image).

(3)

Almost all stabilizers are core-free, co-amenable and co-highly transitive.

Indeed, in this case the push-forward of
\mu
under the stablizer map will be an ECRS with the desired properties. Most of our work goes into constructing a non-singular action satisfying (1); showing that these actions also satisfy (2) and (3) is comparatively easy. The construction is based on three main ingredients.

The first ingredient is the technology of measurable full groups (see Subsection
2.3
and the references therein). We start from a hyperfinite and ergodic countable Borel equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}
on
a standard probability space
(X,\mu)
of Krieger type
\mathrm{II}_{\infty}
or
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
for some
\lambda\in[0,1]
(see
2.2.5
) and consider the associated measurable full group
G
(see
2.3.1
). Here the assumptions on the Krieger type are chosen such that
\mu
is non-atomic and such that there is no
G
-invariant probability measure in the class of
\mu
. Our goal is now to find representations
\rho\colon\Gamma\to G
with dense image such that
\rho(\gamma)
is conservative for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
; the corresponding
\Gamma
-actions on
(X,\mu)
will then satisfy (1). The question now becomes how to construct such representations.

Here is where the second ingredient comes in: Baire category theory. Instead of considering individual representations
\rho\colon\Gamma\to G
we consider the Polish space
\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,G)
of
all
such representations. We say that a
generic representation
has a Property (P) if the set of representations with this property contains a dense
G_{\delta}
-subset of
\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,G)
. By the Baire category theorem, if
(P_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a countable set of properties that hold for a generic representation, then a generic representation satisfies all of these properties simultaneously. In particular, there exists a representation satisfying
(P_{n})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and this allows for the construction of representations with peculiar properties. Unfortunately it turns out that, for our finitely-generated non-abelian free group, generic representations into measurable full groups have finite orbits; while this does imply that generic representations are e.c., the resulting boomerang subgroups are of finite index and thus rather boring.

To fix this problem, we use a third ingredient, which is inspired from
[
Bow15
,
EG16
]
: constraining representations.
We fix a generator
T
for
\mathcal{R}
and a (finite or countably infinite) basis
(a,b_{1},b_{2},\dots)
for
\Gamma
. We then say
that a representation
\rho\colon\Gamma\to G
is
T
-constrained
if
\rho(a)=T
(in particular almost all orbits of
a
are infinite). The closed subspace
\mathrm{Hom}_{T}(\Gamma,G)\subset\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,G)
of
T
-constrained representations is Polish, hence we can talk about
generic
T
-constrained representations
. Theorem
A
is now a consequence of the following main result:

Theorem B

(Generic constrained representations)
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a hyperfinite and ergodic countable Borel equivalence relation on a non-atomic standard probability space
(X,\mu)
with measurable full group
G
. Then for every choice of generator
T
of
\mathcal{R}
, a generic
T
-constrained representation
\rho\colon\Gamma\to G
has the following properties:

(1)

\rho
is faithful.

(2)

The action of
\Gamma
via
\rho
is elementwise conservative.

(3)

For almost every
x\in X
, the action of
\Gamma
on the equivalence class
[x]
is amenable (in the sense of Greenleaf), faithful and highly transitive.

(4)

The stabilizer map
\Stab\colon(X,\mu)\rightarrow(\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma),\Stab_{*}(\mu))
is an isomorphism of
\Gamma
-systems.

Moreover, if
\mathcal{R}
is not of Krieger type
\mathrm{II}_{1}
, then:

(5)

There is no
\Gamma
-invariant probability measure in
[\mathrm{Stab}_{*}\mu]
.

(6)

\rho(\Gamma)
is dense in
G
.

In fact, the constrained representations with these properties form a dense
G_{\delta}
-set.

Our main contribution is the proof of elementwise conservativity, which is essentially combinatorial and works by reduction to the study of special permutations of
\mathbb{Z}
. The remainder of the proof is
heavily inspired from work of Le Maître and his co-authors (see
[
LM24
]
and the references therein).

1.4.
Variants and refinements

In Theorem
B
we have assumed that
\mu
is non-atomic and ergodic; equivalently,
\mathcal{R}
is of Krieger type
\mathrm{II}_{1}
,
\mathrm{II}_{\infty}
or
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
for some
\lambda\in[0,1]
. Let us briefly explain what happens in the remaining Krieger types:

If
\mathcal{R}
is of Krieger type
\mathrm{I}_{n}
with
1\leq n<\infty
, then
[\mathcal{R}]=\mathrm{Sym}_{n}
is a finite symmetric group, and thus all representations
\Gamma\to[\mathcal{R}]
are finite.

The case of Krieger type
\mathrm{I}_{\infty}
yields something more interesting: Any system
(X,[\mu],T)
of type
\mathrm{I}_{\infty}
is conjugate to
(\mathbb{Z},\mu_{c},\sigma)
, with
\mu_{c}
equivalent to a counting measure on
\mathbb{Z}
and
\sigma\colon\Z\rightarrow\Z
given by

n\mapsto n+1
. We may (and will) thus assume that
(X,[\mu],T)=(\mathbb{Z},\mu_{c},\sigma)
, which implies that the associated measurable full group is given by
G=\Sym(\mathbb{Z})
, the full symmetric group of
\Z
. Note that the action of
\sigma
is
not
conservative, since every singleton is a wandering set, and hence
\sigma
-constrained representations into
\mathrm{Sym}(\mathbb{Z})
can never be elementwise conservative. However, the following variant of Theorem
B
still guarantees that stabilizers of generic constrained representations are boomerang subgroups; this has to do with the fact that the non-conservative elements do not stabilize any points.

Theorem C
.

A generic constrained representation
\rho\in\Hom_{\sigma}(\Gamma,\Sym(\mathbb{Z}))
and its induced
\Gamma
-action on
\Z
have the following properties:

(1)

For an element
w\in\Gamma
the following are equivalent:

(i)

w
is not conjugate to
a^{k}
for any
0\neq k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(ii)

All orbits of
\rho(w)
are finite.

(iii)

\rho(w)
is conservative.

(2)

The stabilizer map
\Stab\colon(X,\mu)\rightarrow(\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma),\Stab_{*}(\mu))
is an isomorphism of
\Gamma
-systems.

(3)

For every
x\in\Z
the stabilizer
\Stab_{\Gamma}(x)
is a boomerang subgroup of
\Gamma
.

(4)

\Gamma
acts amenably, faithfully and highly transitively on
\mathbb{Z}
.

Ien the case where
\Gamma
is of countably-infinite rank, Theorem
A
can be established by a much simpler argument. Namely one can work directly with generic representations (rather than restricted generic representations) and establish the following variant of Theorem
B
:

Theorem D
.

Let
\Gamma=\langle b_{1},b_{2},\ldots\rangle
be a countable non-abelian free group of infinite rank and let
\mathcal{R}
be a hyperfinite, ergodic, aperiodic countable Borel equivalence relation on a standard Borel space with measurable full group
G
. Then a generic representation
\rho\colon\Gamma\to G
and the induced action on
(X,\mu)
have the following properties:

(1)

For every finitely generated subgroup of
\Gamma
almost all orbits are finite. In particular every element of
\rho(\Gamma)
is periodic and hence the
\Gamma
-action is elementwise conservative.

(2)

\rho(\Gamma)
is dense in
G
.

(3)

The stabilizer map
\Stab\colon(X,\mu)\rightarrow(\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma),\Stab_{*}(\mu))
is an isomorphism of
\Gamma
-systems.
In particular,
\Gamma
does not preserve any invariant probability measure unless
\mathcal{R}
is of Krieger type
\mathrm{II}_{1}
.

(4)

\Gamma
acts amenably, faithfully and highly transitively on almost every orbit.

1.5.
Related results and open problems

§ 1.5.1
.

Theorem
A
provides examples of exotic boomerang subgroups which “do not arise from an IRS” in one possible sense. The following natural question remains open though:

Question
.

Does there exist an infinite boomerang subgroup of a countable non-abelian free group
\Gamma
whose orbit closure does not support an IRS?

A partial answer to this question follows from recent work of the third-named author
[
Led25
]
and work of Gekhtman–Levit
[
GL19
]
: There exist infinite boomerang subgroups of
\Gamma
whose orbit closure only supports a single IRS concentrated on the trivial subgroup.

More precisely, it was established in
[
GL19
, Thm. 1.1]
(respectively
[
GL23
, Thm. 1.4]
) that every non-trival IRS
\Delta
(respectively every nontrivial SRS
\Delta
with respect to a nice enough random walk) has critical exponent
\delta(\Delta)>\frac{\delta(\Gamma)}{2}
(and even
\delta(\Delta)=\delta(\Gamma)
if one further assumes that the relevant IRS or SRS is supported on groups of divergence type). On the other hand, the combinatorial construction from
[
Led25
]
yields boomerang subgroups of arbitrarily small critical exponent, which therefore do not admit any non-trivial IRS (or non-trivial nice SRS) on their orbit closure. Unfortunately, this argument is not quite strong enough to fully resolve the question since, by a result of Fraczyk
[
Fra19
, Corollary 3.2]
, the orbit closure of every group
\Delta\in\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma)
with
\delta(\Delta)\leq\frac{\delta(\Gamma)}{2}
necessarily contains the trivial group. In relation to the current article, also the following question remains open.

Question
.

Do the boomerang subgroups of small critical exponent constructed in
[
Led25
]
support a non-trivial ECRS on their orbit closure?

§ 1.5.2
.

In a different direction, we hope that the notion of an e.c. action, as introduced here and in
[
GL25
]
, will prove to be fruitful in nonsingular dynamics beyond the setting of nonfree actions considered here. For example, in a related upcoming paper, Le Maître and Stalder use a construction similar to ours to obtain free e.c. actions of a free group
\Gamma
that do not come from p.m.p. actions using Baire generic representations of a free group into the Polish group
\operatorname{Aut}(\R,\lambda)
of infinite measure preserving transformations of a standard Lebesgue space.
Typically such actions are essentially free and do not contribute new examples of boomerang subgroups, but they do provide a rich source of e.c. actions that are not probability measure preserving.
More generally, we propose the following question:

Question
.

Which groups admit interesting e.c. actions with no invariant probability measure?

One such example, suggested by Sasha Bontemps and the third named author, goes as follows: Let
\Lambda
be a nonamenable torsion group acting on its universal strongly proximal flow (a.k.a. its Furstenberg boundary)
\partial_{F}\Lambda
. For a torsion group every nonsingular action is e.c., but not in an interesting way. Strong proximality rules out the existence of any invariant Borel probability measure on
\partial_{F}\Lambda
.
Typically
\partial_{F}\Lambda
is far from being a standard Borel space, but it is well known that metric factors
\partial_{F}\Lambda\rightarrow X
separate points and one can always pass to such a factor
X
. Indeed take any nonconstant function
f\in C(X)
- the minimal closed self adjoint
\Lambda
-invariant algebra spanned by
f
inside
C(\partial_{F}\Lambda)
will give rise to such a factor.

The main result of
[
GL25
]
shows that for lattices of higher
\mathbb{Q}
-rank in simple Lie groups all e.c. actions are either finite or essentially free, but it remains open whether there are any e.c. actions of such groups that do not admit an equivalent invariant probability measure.

1.6.
Organization of the article

In Section
2
we fix our notation and collect various preliminaries concerning non-singular ergodic theory and measurable full groups.
In Section
3
we prove basic properties on representations into measurable full groups.
In Section
4
we consider restricted representations into the measurable full group of a system of Krieger type
\mathrm{I}_{\infty}
and establish Theorem
C
. It turns out that this case is much simpler than that of other Krieger types. Section

We first establish that generic constrained representations into the measurable full group are elementwise conservative by reduction to a combinatorial lemma on so-called zebra permutations. The structural insight into generic constrained representations gained by the proof is then exploited to establish further properties of such representations. In Section

\mathrm{II}_{\infty}

The theorems from the introduction then follow by combining all these results; for the convenience of the reader we have collected their proofs in Section
7
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Polish groups and constrained representations

§ 2.1.1
.

A topological space is called
Polish
if it is separable and completely metrizable. A topological group
G
is called a
Polish group
if the underlying space is Polish.

A subset
Y
of a Polish space
X
is called a
G_{\delta}
-set
if it is a countable intersection of open subsets of
X
; equivalently,
Y
is Polish with respect to the subspace topology. Pre-images of
G_{\delta}
-sets under continuous map are
G_{\delta}
, and the
Baire category theorem
states that the class of dense
G_{\delta}
subsets is closed under countable intersections.

A subset of a Polish space
X
is called
comeager
, or
residual
, if it contains a dense
G_{\delta}
set, and
meager
if its complement is comeager. We say that a property
\mathcal{P}
of elements of a Polish space
X
is
generic
if the set of elements of
X
with this property is comeager.

§ 2.1.2
.

Throughout this article,
\Gamma
denotes a countable non-abelian free group. We fix a basis of
\Gamma
of the form
(a,b_{1}\dots,b_{r-1})
if
\Gamma
has finite rank
r
, and of the form
(a,b_{1},b_{2},\dots)
if
\Gamma
has countable infinite rank. We then identify elements of
\Gamma
with reduced words over the alphabet consisting of the basis elements and their inverses. As indicated by the notation, the first generator
a
will often play a special role in our considerations. If
\Gamma
has rank at least
r
, then we denote by
\Gamma_{r}<\Gamma
the subgroup generated by the first
r
basis elements
(a,b_{1},\dots,b_{r-1})
.

§ 2.1.3
.

If
G
is a Polish group and
T\in G
, then we denote by
\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,G)
the space of homomorphisms of
\Gamma
to
G
and by
\mathrm{Hom}_{T}(\Gamma,G)
the subspace of those homomorphisms
\rho
with
\rho(a)=T
. We refer to elements of
\mathrm{Hom}_{T}(\Gamma,G)
as
T
-constrained representations
.

If
\Gamma
has finite rank
r
, then we topologize these spaces by demanding that the natural bijections
\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,G)\to G^{r}
and
\mathrm{Hom}_{T}(\Gamma,G)\to G^{r-1}
given respectively by
\rho\mapsto(\rho(a),\rho(b_{1}),\dots,\rho(b_{r-1}))
and
\rho\mapsto(\rho(b_{1}),\dots,\rho(b_{r-1}))
are homeomorphisms; if
\Gamma
has infinite rank, then we similarly identify
\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,G)
and
\mathrm{Hom}_{T}(\Gamma,G)
with the countable product
G^{\infty}
and equip them with the topology corresponding to the product topology on
G^{\infty}
. In either case,
\mathrm{Hom}(F_{\infty},G)
and
\mathrm{Hom}_{T}(\Gamma,G)
are Polish, and for every
w\in\Gamma
the
evaluation map

\mathrm{ev}_{w}\colon\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,G)\to G,\quad\rho\mapsto\rho(w)

is continuous.

By assumption, the topology on
G
is metrizable, and we will always fix a bounded metric
d
on
G
(not necessarily complete) which induces the given topology. Then for
1\leq r\leq\infty
the topology on
G^{r}
is induced by the metric

d((g_{i}),(h_{i}))=\sum_{i=1}^{r}d(g_{i},h_{i})/2^{i}
, and we equip
\Hom(\Gamma,G)
and
\Hom_{T}(\Gamma,G)
with the corresponding metrics.

§ 2.1.4
.

We are going to be interested in “generic properties” of constrained representations. If
\Gamma
is a countable free group,
G
is a Polish group,
T\in G
and
\mathcal{P}
is a property of representations in
\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,G)
, then we say that a
generic
T
-constrained representation satisfies
\mathcal{P}

if the subset
\{\rho\in\mathrm{Hom}_{T}(\Gamma,G)\mid\rho\text{ has }\mathcal{P}\}\subset\mathrm{Hom}_{T}(\Gamma,G)
is comeager. It follows from the Baire category theorem that if a generic
T
-constrained representation satisfies countably many properties
\mathcal{P}_{1},\mathcal{P}_{2},\dots
, then it satisfies all of these properties simultaneously.

2.2.
Non-singular ergodic theory

In order to define the target groups of our representations, we need some background from non-singular ergodic theory. A convenient reference is
[
DS23
]
.

§ 2.2.1
.

Throughout this article, all measurable spaces are assumed to be standard Borel spaces. We refer to a measurable bijection between standard Borel spaces as a
Borel isomorphism
; by
[
Kec95
, (15.2) Corollary]
the inverse of a Borel isomorphism is automatically measurable. If
(X,\mathcal{B})
is a standard Borel space and
\mu
denotes a
\sigma
-finite Borel measure on
X
, then we refer to
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
as a
standard measure space
; if
\mu
is moreover a probability measure, then it is called a
standard probability space
.

From now on let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
(or
(X,\mu)
for short) be a standard measure space. We denote by
[\mu]
the
measure class
of
\mu
, i.e. the collection of all
\sigma
-finite Borel measures
\nu
on
X
such that
\nu(A)=0\iff\mu(A)=0
for all
A\in\mathcal{B}
. Every measure class can be represented by a probability measure. Given
A,B\in\mathcal{B}
we define

A\sim B\;:\Longleftrightarrow\;\mu(A\triangle B)=0.

The quotient of
\mathcal{B}
by this equivalence relation is a Polish group of exponent
2
, where
\triangle
is the group operation. It is denoted
\mathrm{MAlg}(X,[\mu])
and called the
measure algebra
of
(X,[\mu])
.

§ 2.2.2
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
be a standard measure space. A Borel automorphism
T
of
X
is called
non-singular
if
[T_{*}\mu]=[\mu]
, and two such automorphisms are called
equivalent
if they coincide on a set of full measure. Equivalence classes of non-singular Borel automorphisms form a group
\mathrm{Aut}(X,[\mu])
under composition of representatives, and this group acts on
\mathrm{MAlg}(X,[\mu])
by
[T]([A])\coloneqq[T(A)]
. We will need the following consequence of the Radon–Nikodym theorem:

Lemma

(
[
Coh13
, Lemma 4.2.1]
)
.

If
T
is non-singular, then

\forall\,\varepsilon>0\;\exists\,\delta>0\;\forall A\in\mathcal{B}:\;\mu(A)<\delta\implies\mu(TA)<\varepsilon.

Remark
.

As is customary, we will often not distinguish notationally between non-singular Borel automorphisms and their equivalence classes in
\operatorname{Aut}(X,[\mu])
; or between elements of
\mathcal{B}
and their image in the measure algebra. For example, given
T\in\operatorname{Aut}(X,[\mu])
and
A\in\mathrm{MAlg}(X,[\mu])
we write
T(A)

for the element of the measure algebra obtained by applying a representative of
T
to a representative of
A
. This abuse of notation is uncritical as long as all the concepts involved are independent of representatives.

§ 2.2.3
.

If
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
is a standard probability space and
T\in\mathrm{Aut}(X,[\mu])
, then the triple
(X,[\mu],T)
is called a
non-singular dynamical system
. More generally, if
\Lambda
is a group, then a homomorphism
\Lambda\to\mathrm{Aut}(X,[\mu])
is called a
non-singular action
.

Clearly, if
[\mu]
contains a
T
-invariant probability measure
\nu
, then
(X,[\mu],T)
is non-singular; such a non-singular dynamical system is called
probability-measure preserving
(p.m.p.).

We say that
T\in\mathrm{Aut}(X,[\mu])
is
periodic
if almost every
T
-orbit is finite, and

aperiodic
if almost every
T
-orbit is infinite. We say that
T
is

if for every
A\in\mathcal{B}
with
T^{-1}(A)=A
we have
\mu(A)=0
or
\mu(X\setminus A)=0
; then
(X,[\mu],T)
is called an
ergodic system
and
\mu
is called
T
-ergodic
.

Lemma
.

If
T\in\mathrm{Aut}(X,[\mu])
is aperiodic and ergodic, then for every
A\subset X
of positive measure and all
\varepsilon>0
and
M>0
there exists a subset
A^{\prime}\subset A
of positive measure such that
A^{\prime},TA^{\prime},\dots,T^{M}A^{\prime}
are pairwise measurably disjoint, and
\mu(A^{\prime}\cup\dots\cup T^{M}A^{\prime})<\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Let
p>M
be a prime. By the Lehrer–Weiss version of the non-singular Rokhlin lemma
[
DS23
, Theorem 2.9]
there exists a subset
B\subset X
of positive measure such that
B,TB,\dots,T^{p-1}B
(and hence
T^{k}B,\dots,T^{k+p-1}B
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
) are pairwise measurably disjoint and
A\subset B\cup TB\cup\dots\cup T^{p-1}B
. Choose
k\in\{0,\dots,p-1\}
such that
A^{\prime\prime}:=T^{k}(B)\cap A
has positive measure; then
A^{\prime\prime},TA^{\prime\prime},\dots,T^{M}A^{\prime\prime}
are pairwise measurably disjoint, and by Lemma
2.2.2
we can choose
A^{\prime}\subset A^{\prime\prime}\subset A
such that
\mu(A^{\prime}\cup\dots\cup T^{M}A^{\prime})<\varepsilon
.
∎

§ 2.2.4
.

The
ratio set
of a nonsingular system
(X,[\mu],T)
is the multiplicative subsemigroup
R(T)\subset\R_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\}
consisting of all
r\in\R
such that for every measurable subset
A\subset X
of positive measure and every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a measurable subset

of positive measure such that

\left|\frac{dT^{k}\mu}{d\mu}(x)-r\right|<\varepsilon\quad\text{for all }x\in B.

It turns out that

depends only on

.

§ 2.2.5
.

Two non-singular dynamical systems
(X,[\mu],T)
and
(X^{\prime},[\mu^{\prime}],T^{\prime})
are
orbit equivalent
if there exists a Borel isomorphism
f\colon X\to X^{\prime}
such that
[f_{*}\mu]=[\mu^{\prime}]
and
\langle T\rangle x=\langle T^{\prime}\rangle f(x)
for almost every
x\in X
. Ergodic non-singular dynamical systems have been classified up to orbit equivalence by Dye and Krieger. A fundamental invariant of orbit equivalence is the so-called
Krieger type
; here a non-singular, ergodic dynamical system
(X,[\mu],T)
is said to have

•

type
\mathrm{I}_{n}
, with
1\leq n<\infty
, if
\mu
has precisely
n
atoms. In particular
[\mu]
contains the unique normalized counting measure on
n
atoms preserved by
T
,

•

type
\mathrm{I}_{\infty}
if
\mu
has countably many atoms. In particular
[\mu]
contains the unique counting measure on infinitely many atoms preserved by
T
,

•

type
\mathrm{II}_{1}
if there exists a non-atomic probability measure
\nu\in[\mu]
preserved by
T
,

•

type
\mathrm{II}_{\infty}
if there exists a non-atomic
\sigma
-finite infinite measure
\nu\in[\mu]
preserved by
T
.

In all of these cases, the given measure class contains an invariant measure, and hence
R(T)=\{1\}
. If the given measure class does
not
contain an invariant measure, then we say that the system is of

•

type
\mathrm{III}_{0}
if

,

•

type
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
if

for some
\lambda\in(0,1)
,

•

type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
if

.

The

is uniquely determined by its Krieger type, except in the case of systems of type
\mathrm{III}_{0}
, and that the orbit equivalence class of a system of type
\mathrm{III}_{0}
is determined by the isomorphism type of the associated Krieger flow. See
[
KW91
]
for details. We say that
T
is of Krieger type
\mathrm{I}
it is of Krieger type
\mathrm{I}_{n}
for some

and define Krieger types
\mathrm{II}
and

similarly.

§ 2.2.6
.

Concrete models for systems of different Krieger types are given by

§ 2.2.7
.

Definition
.

Let

(

)

For a non-singular, ergodic dynamical system

(ii)

§ 2.2.8
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a standard probability space. An equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}
on
X
is called
measurable
if it is a
\mu\times\mu
-measurable subset of
X\times X
.
We say that a measurable equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}
is
countable
(respectively
finite
) if almost every equivalence class of
\mathcal{R}
is countable (respectively finite). It is called
hyperfinite
if
\mathcal{R}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{R}_{n}
for finite measurable subequivalence relations
\mathcal{R}_{n}
and
ergodic
iff for every
A\in\mathcal{B}
the
\mathcal{R}
-saturation
\bigcup_{x\in A}[x]
has measure
0
or
1
.

Example
.

Every non-singular dynamical system
(X,[\mu],T)
defines a hyperfinite measurable equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}_{T}\coloneqq\{(x,y)\in X\times X\mid y\in\langle T\rangle x\}
called the
orbit equivalence relation
of
T
. Given a hyperfinite equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}
on
(X,[\mu])
we say that
T\in\operatorname{Aut}(X,[\mu])
is a
generator
for
\mathcal{R}
if
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}_{T}
almost everywhere. By the Ornstein–Weiss theorem, ergodic hyperfinite equivalence relations are precisely the orbit equivalence relations of ergodic non-singular transformations (up to almost everywhere coincidence). In particular,
T

is ergodic if and only if
\mathcal{R}_{T}
is ergodic.

Moreover,
T
is aperiodic if and only if almost every class of
\mathcal{R}_{T}
is infinite; we then also say that
\mathcal{R}_{T}
is aperiodic.
Moreover, the Krieger type of
T
depends only on
\mathcal{R}_{T}
and is thus also called the
Krieger type
of
\mathcal{R}_{T}
. Then an
ergodic
hyperfinite equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}
on
(X,[\mu])
is aperiodic
unless it is of Krieger type
\mathrm{I}_{n}
. In particular, this is the case if
\mathcal{R}
is ergodic and
\mu
is non-atomic (i.e.
\mathcal{R}
is not of Krieger type
\mathrm{I}
).

2.3.
Measurable full groups

§ 2.3.1
.

We are now in the position to define the target groups of our representations; these are measurable full groups of non-singular dynamical systems or, equivalently, the associated measurable equivalence relations. Throughout this subsection let
\mathcal{R}
be a hyperfinite ergodic equivalence relation on a standard probability space
(X,[\mu])
.

Definition
.

The
measurable full group
of
\mathcal{R}
is the subgroup

[\mathcal{R}]=\{g\in\operatorname{Aut}(X,[\mu])\mid(x,g(x))\in\mathcal{R}\text{ for almost all }x\in X\}<\operatorname{Aut}(X,[\mu]).

§ 2.3.2
.

By definition, if
T
is a generator of
\mathcal{R}
, then
g\in[\mathcal{R}]
if and only if almost every
g
-orbit is contained in the corresponding
T
-orbit. Thus there then exists a (non-strict) cocycle
\alpha:[\mathcal{R}]\times X\to\mathrm{Sym}(\mathbb{Z})
,
(g,x)\mapsto\alpha_{x}(g)
, unique up to almost everywhere equality, such that for all
g\in[\mathcal{R}]
and almost all
x\in X
we have

g(T^{n}x)=T^{\alpha_{x}(g)(n)}(x).

We refer to
\alpha
as the
action cocycle
of
[\mathcal{R}]\curvearrowright X
with respect to
T
.

On
[\mathcal{R}]
we define the
uniform metric
defined by
d(g,h)=\mu(\{x\in X\mid g(x)\neq h(x)\})
and equip
[\mathcal{R}]
with the corresponding topology; then
[\mathcal{R}]
is a Polish group and the Krieger type of
\mathcal{R}
(or
T
) depends only on the isomorphism class of
[\mathcal{R}]
as a topological group. If
\mathcal{R}
is of type
\mathrm{I}_{n}
or
\mathrm{II}_{1}
, then the uniform metric is complete, otherwise a complete metric for
[\mathcal{R}]
is given by
d^{\prime}(g,h)=d(g,h)+d(g^{-1},h^{-1})
. In the sequel we always equip
[\mathcal{R}]
with the uniform metric, and spaces of (constrained) representations into
[\mathcal{R}]
with the corresponding metric as in §
2.1.3
.

If
\mathcal{R}
is of type
\mathrm{I}_{n}
with
n<\infty
, then
[\mathcal{R}]\cong\mathrm{Sym}_{n}
is finite, and if
\mathcal{R}
is of type
\mathrm{I}_{\infty}
then
[\mathcal{R}]
is isomorphic to the group
\Sym(\Z)
of permutations on
\mathbb{Z}
with the permutation topology. Otherwise,
[\mathcal{R}]
is simple
[
Eig81
]
and contractible
[
Dan95
]
.

§ 2.3.3
.

In order to define an element of the full group we will usually only specify it on a conull set; this is possible due to the following proposition. Here, by a
partial automorphism
of
(X,[\mu])
we mean a Borel isomorphism
g_{o}\colon\dom(g_{0})\to\ran(g_{o})
between Borel subsets
\dom(g_{o}),\ran(g_{o})\subset X
.

Proposition
.

Let
g_{o}
be a partial automorphism of
(X,[\mu])
such that
\dom(g_{o})
and
\ran(g_{o})
are conull in
X
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

There exists
g\in[\mathcal{R}]
whose representatives agree with
g_{o}
almost everywhere.

(ii)

g_{o}(x)\in[x]_{\mathcal{R}}
for almost all
x\in\dom(g_{o})
.

In this case,
g
is uniquely determined by
g_{o}
.

Proof.

It is clear that (i)
\implies
(ii) and that
g
is uniquely determined by
g_{o}
. For the implication (ii)
\implies
(i) assume that
\dom(g_{o})=X\setminus N_{1}
and
\ran(g_{o})=X\setminus N_{2}
; we can enlarge
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
and may thereby assume without loss of generality that
g_{o}(x)\in[x]_{\mathcal{R}}
for all
x\in\dom(g_{o})
and that
N_{1}
,
N_{2}
and
g_{o}
are Borel. Then for
i\in\{1,2\}
the saturation
M_{i}\coloneqq\bigcup_{n\in\Z}T^{n}N_{i}
is a Borel nullset, and hence
M\coloneqq M_{1}\cup M_{2}
is a Borel nullset. We claim that

X\setminus M=\{x\in X\mid g_{o}|_{[x]}\colon[x]\to[x]\text{ well-defined and bijective}\}.

(2.1)

Indeed, the restriction of
g_{o}
to
[x]
is well-defined if and only if
[x]\cap N_{1}=\varnothing
, which is the case if and only if
x\notin M_{1}
, and in this case
g_{o}|_{[x]}
is injective. If
x\notin M_{1}
, then
g_{o}|_{[x]}
is surjective if and only if
g_{o}([x])=[g_{o}(x)]
, i.e.
g_{o}(x)\notin M_{2}
, hence (
2.1
) follows.

By (
2.1
) we have a well-defined restriction
g_{1}\coloneqq g_{o}|_{X\setminus M}\colon X\setminus M\to X\setminus M
, and since
g_{o}
is bijective and Borel, also
g_{1}
is a Borel automorphism of
X\setminus M
. Now extend
g_{1}
by the identity on
M
; this defines a class
g\in[\mathcal{R}]
with the desired properties.
∎

In other words, to specify an element of
[\mathcal{R}]
it is enough to specify it on a conull set and to ensure that it preserves the equivalence relation there. In some cases we can also extend partial isomorphisms which are defined on a smaller subset:

Lemma

(Extension lemma)
.

Assume that
\mathcal{R}
is non-atomic and let
g^{\prime}
be a partial automorphism of
(X,[\mu])
such that
g^{\prime}(x)\in[x]_{\mathcal{R}}
for almost all
x\in\dom(g^{\prime})
. If
0<\mu(\dom(g^{\prime})),\mu(\ran(g^{\prime}))<1
, then there exists
g\in[\mathcal{R}]
with
g|_{\dom(g)}=g^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{R}
is ergodic and non-atomic,
\mathcal{R}
is either of type
\mathrm{II}
or of type
\mathrm{III}
. In the former case, apply
[
LM24
, Lemma 7.16]
, and in the latter case apply
[
KLM15
, Prop. 11]
.
∎

§ 2.3.4
.

An important fact is that the set of
periodic
elements is a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
[\mathcal{R}]
. Here we call an element
g\in[\mathcal{R}]
periodic if almost every point is periodic, i.e. for almost every

with

.

2.4.
Conservative transformations and e.c. actions

§ 2.4.1
.

We have the following strengthening of the notion of a weakly wandering set:

Definition
.

Let
T\in\operatorname{Aut}(X,[\mu])
. A set
W\in\mathcal{B}
is called a
wandering set for
T
if
\mu(T^{n}(W)\cap W)=0
for all
n\in\Z\setminus\{0\}
.

Proposition

(
[
DS23
, Prop. 2.1]
)
.

Let
(X,[\mu],T)
be a nonsingular dynamical system. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

Every wandering set for
T
is a nullset.

(ii)

For every
A\in\mathcal{B}
with
\mu(A)>0
there exists
n>0
such that
\mu(A\cap T^{-n}A)>0
.

(iii)

For every
A\in\mathcal{B}
,

\mu\left(A\setminus\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}T^{-n}A\right)=0.

(iv)

T
is
incompressible
, i.e. if
C\in\mathcal{B}
with
T^{-1}C\subset C
, then
\mu(C\setminus T^{-1}C)=0
.

Definition
.

If a system
(X,[\mu],T)
satisfies the equivalent conditions from Proposition
Proposition
, then it is called
conservative
, and
T
is called a
conservative transformation
.
A non-singular action of a group
\Lambda
on a standard probability space
(X,\mu)
is
elementwise conservative
(e.c.) if every element of
\Lambda
acts by a conservative transformation.

Remark
.

Every nonsingular dynamical system
(X,[\mu],T)
admits a unique, up to nullsets, decomposition
X=C\sqcup D
into
T
-invariant Borel sets (called
Hopf decomposition
) such that
D=\bigsqcup_{n\in\Z}T^{n}W
for some wandering set
W\subset X
and such that
T|_{C}
is conservative (
[
DS23
, Thm. 2.2]
). The set
C
is then called the
conservative part
of
X
.

§ 2.4.2
.

Example
.

Let
(X,[\mu],T)
be a non-singular system.

(i)

If
T
is
periodic
, in the sense that almost every
T
-orbit is finite, then
T
is conservative.

(ii)

If
\mu
is non-atomic and
T
is ergodic, then
T
is conservative. In particular, if
T
is ergodic and of Krieger type
\mathrm{II}_{\infty}
or
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
for some
\lambda\in[0,1]
, then
T
admits a weakly wandering set of positive measure by Proposition
Proposition
, but no wandering set of positive measure by Proposition
Proposition
.

(iii)

If
X
admits an (essentially) disjoint decomposition
X=\bigsqcup X_{i}
into finitely many
T
-invariant pieces
X_{i}
, then
T
is conservative if each each of the restrictions
T|_{X_{i}}
is conservative.

(iv)

It follows from (i)-(iii) that if
\mu
is non-atomic and
X
decomposes into a part on which
T
acts periodically and finitely many subsets on which
T
acts ergodically, then
T
is conservative.

Remark
.

It
\mu
has atoms, then a non-singular ergodic transformation of
(X,[\mu])
need not be conservative. Namely, if
T
denotes the shift on
\mathbb{Z}
and
\mu
is equivalent to the counting measure, then
T
is ergodic, but every singleton is a wandering set for
T
.
This is essentially the only example of an ergodic transformation that is not conservative.
However, if
T
is
recurrent
in the sense that for every
A\in\mathcal{B}
with
\mu(A)>0
and almost every
x\in X
there exists

with

, then it is always conservative (and ergodic). By Poincaré recurrence, this is the case for any probability measure preserving ergodic transformation. Conversely, every ergodic and conservative transformation is recurrent.

§ 2.4.3
.

We record the following consequence of Example
Example
.(iv) for later use:

Proposition

(Conservativity criterion)
.

Assume that
(X,[\mu],T)
is an ergodic non-singular system and that
\mu
is non-atomic. If
g\in[\mathcal{R}_{T}]
and there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
almost every
T
-orbit contains at most
k
infinite
g
-orbits, then
g
is conservative.

Proof.

Choose
k
such that every
T
-orbit contains at most
k
infinite
g
-orbits and partition
X=X_{p}\sqcup X_{np}
into
g
-invariant measurable sets with
X_{p}
containing all the finite
g
-orbits and
X_{np}
containing all the infinite ones. By Example
Example
.(i) we may assume that
\mu(X_{np})>0
.

Assume that
X_{np}=X_{1}\sqcup X_{2}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup X_{L}
is a partition into
g
-invariant sets of positive measure. By ergodicity of
T
, almost every
T
-orbit
\mathcal{O}
intersects every
X_{i}
, and the intersection
\mathcal{O}\cap X_{i}
then contains at least one
g
-orbit
\mathcal{O}_{i}
, which is infinite by construction of
X_{np}
. Then
\mathcal{O}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{O}_{L}
are infinite
g
-orbits in
\mathcal{O}
, and thus
L\leq k
by assumption. This implies that we can decompose
X_{np}
into at most
k
subsets on which
g
acts ergodically, and hence the proposition follows from Example
Example
.(iv).
∎

2.5.
The Chabauty space and boomerang subgroups

§ 2.5.1
.

If
H
is a locally compact second countable group, then the space
\mathrm{Sub}(H)
of closed subgroups of
H
carries a canonical compact metrizable topology known as the
Chabauty–Fell topology
, which is invariant under the
H
-action by conjugation and hence gives rise to a topological dynamical system
H\curvearrowright\mathrm{Sub}(H)
. We will only need this topology in the case of a countable discrete group
\Lambda
, in which case it coincides with the restriction of the product topology via the embedding
\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)\subset\{0,1\}^{\Lambda}
, which can be shown to have closed image. In particular, for a countable discrete group
\Lambda
the
Chabauty space

\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)
is totally disconnected.

If
\Lambda\curvearrowright X
is a Borel action on a standard Borel space, then the stabilizer map
\mathrm{Stab}\colon X\to\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)
is Borel. In particular, if the stabilizer map is injective, then it is a Borel isomorphism onto its image. More generally, if
(X,\mu)
is a standard probability space and
\Lambda<\operatorname{Aut}(X,[\mu])
, then we say that
\Lambda
is
totally-non-free
if there is a full measure subset

such that
\Stab\colon X^{\prime}\to\mathrm{Sub}(H)
is injective; this then implies that
\mathrm{Stab}
induces an isomorphism
(X,\mu)\to(\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda),\Stab_{*}\mu)
.

§ 2.5.2
.

From now on
\Lambda
denotes a countable discrete group. We recall from the introduction that a subgroup
\Delta\in\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)
is a boomerang subgroup if for every
\gamma\in\Lambda
there exists a sequence
n_{k}\to\infty
with
\gamma^{n_{k}}\Delta\gamma^{-n_{k}}\to\Delta
in
\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)
. Elementary examples of boomerang subgroups are

(1)

normal subgroups,

(2)

finite index subgroups,

(3)

more generally, subgroups whose normalizer has finite index,

(4)

more generally, subgroups such that every group element has a power in the normalizer.

All of these examples of boomerang subgroups
\Delta<\Lambda
have in common that for every
\gamma\in\Lambda
, the
\langle\gamma\rangle
-orbit of
\Delta
is finite. In fact it is not hard to show that this is necessarily the case whenever
\Delta
is finitely generated, or whenever
\Delta
is not in the perfect kernel of
\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)
.

§ 2.5.3
.

The topology of
\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)
admits the following equivalent description, which is more convenient for our purposes. For any finite subset
F\subset\Lambda
, and any
\Delta\in\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)
, we denote

(1)

\Env(F)\coloneqq\{H\in\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)\mid F\subset H\}

(2)

\Miss(F)\coloneqq\{H\in\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)\mid F\cap H=\varnothing\}

(3)

\Nbh(\Delta,F)\coloneqq\{H\in\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)\mid F\cap H=F\cap\Delta\}=\Env(F\cap\Delta)\cap\Miss(F\setminus\Delta)
.

Then, as
F
varies over all finite subsets of
\Lambda

the sets
\Env(F)
and
\Miss(F)
form a basis for the topology on
\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)
and the sets
\Nbh(\Delta,F)
constitute a neighborhood basis of
\Delta
in
\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)
. This implies that
\Delta\subset\Lambda
is a boomerang if and only if for every
\gamma\in\Lambda
and every finite subset
F\subset\Lambda
there exists
n>0
with
\gamma^{n}\Delta\gamma^{-n}\in\Nbh(\Delta,F)
, i.e.

\gamma^{n}\Delta\gamma^{-n}\cap F=\Delta\cap F.

Using this terminology we prove the following lemma promised in Remark
Remark
, which can be seen as a consequence of the “locally essential lemma” (
[
Gla17
, Lemma 2.3]
)

Lemma
.

Let
\Lambda
be a countable group. Then
the conjugation action
\Lambda\curvearrowright\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)
is globally conservative with respect to any non-singular probability measure
\mu
on
\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)
.

Proof.

Let
B\subset\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)
with
\mu(B)>0
. We need to show that there exists
\gamma\in\Lambda
such that
\mu(\gamma B\gamma^{-1}\cap B)>0
. Write
\mu=\lambda\delta_{\{1\}}+(1-\lambda)\mu^{\prime}
with
\mu^{\prime}(\{\{1\}\})=0
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
. The statement is obvious if
\mu^{\prime}(B)=0
, hence we assume that
\mu^{\prime}(B)>0
.

By definition,
\mu^{\prime}
is supported on the countable union
\bigcup_{\gamma\neq 1}\Env(\{\gamma\})
, hence we can choose
\gamma\in\Lambda\setminus\{1\}
such that
B^{\prime}:=\Env(\{\gamma\})\cap B
satisfies
\mu^{\prime}(B^{\prime})>0
. Now
\gamma
acts trivially on
\Env(\{\gamma\})
, hence on
B^{\prime}
, and thus
\mu(\gamma B\gamma^{-1}\cap B)\geq\mu^{\prime}(\gamma B\gamma^{-1}\cap B)\geq\mu^{\prime}(B^{\prime})>0
.
∎

Basics on representations into measurable full groups

In this section
\Lambda
is any countable group,

\mathcal{R}
a hyperfinite Borel equivalence relation on
(X,\mathcal{B},[\mu])
with some generator
T\in\operatorname{Aut}(X,[\mu])
and
G=[\mathcal{R}]
denotes the corresponding measurable full group.

3.1.
G_{\delta}
-properties of generic representations

§ 3.1.1
.

Throughout this article we will establish that various sets of (constrained) representations are dense
G_{\delta}
-sets. The
G_{\delta}
-property is usually easy to establish, and one can use the following general principle:

Proposition
.

If
\Lambda
is a countable group and
P\subset\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)
is
G_{\delta}
, then also

\Xi(P)\coloneqq\{\rho\in\Hom(\Lambda,G)\ |\ \mu(\{x\ |\ \Lambda_{x}\in P\})=1\}\subset\Hom(\Lambda,G)

is
G_{\delta}
,
where
\Lambda_{x}
is the stabilizer of
x
in
\Lambda
with respect to the action induced by
\rho
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that for every open subset
P\subset\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)
and every
\varepsilon>0
the set

\Xi(P,\varepsilon)\coloneqq\{\rho\in\Hom(\Lambda,G)\ |\ \mu(\{x\ |\ \Lambda_{x}\in P\})>1-\varepsilon\}

is open. Indeed, if
P
is
G_{\delta}
then we can realize it as a descending intersection of open sets
P=\bigcap_{i\in\mathbb{N}}P_{i}
and
\Xi(P)=\bigcap_{n,i\in\mathbb{N}}\Xi(P_{i},1/n)
. Hence we assume that
P
is open and fix
\rho\in\Xi(P,\varepsilon)
.

Let
\Sigma\in\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)
and express
\Lambda=\bigcup B_{n}
as an ascending union of finite sets
B_{n}
.
Then the sets
\Nbh(\Sigma,B_{n})=\{\Delta\in\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)\ |\ \Sigma\cap B_{n}=\Delta\cap B_{n}\}

form a local basis of Chabauty open neighborhoods around
\Sigma
.

Now let
x\in X
. If
\Lambda_{x}\not\in P
we set
n(x,\rho)\coloneqq\infty
. Otherwise (using that
P
is open) there exists some

n=n(\rho,x)
such that

\Lambda_{x}\in\Nbh(\Lambda_{x},B_{n})\subset P
. By definition of
\Xi(P,\varepsilon)
we find
0<\eta=\eta(\rho)<\varepsilon/2
such that

n(x,\rho)=\infty
on a set of measure at most
\varepsilon-2\eta
. Thus
we can fix one global
N=N(\rho)\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\mu\left(B(\rho,N)\right)=1-\varepsilon+\eta>1-\varepsilon,\qquad{\text{where }}B(\rho,N)=\{x\in X\ |\ U(\Lambda_{x},B_{N})\subset P\}.

Now choose an open neighborhood
\rho\in O\subset\Hom(\Lambda,G)
small enough to satisfy

\mu\left(\{x\in X\mid\forall\rho^{\prime}\in O\,\forall\gamma\in B_{N}:\;\rho(\gamma)x\neq\rho^{\prime}(\gamma)x\ \}\right)<\frac{\eta}{\left|B_{N}\right|}.

Thus, for any
\rho^{\prime}\in O
we have

\displaystyle\mu\left(B(\rho^{\prime},N(\rho^{\prime}))\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mu\left(B\left(\rho,N(\rho)\right)\cup\bigcup_{\gamma\in B_{N}}\{x\ |\ \rho(\gamma)x\neq\rho^{\prime}(\gamma)x\}\right)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 1-\varepsilon+\eta-\sum_{\gamma\in B_{N}}\frac{\eta}{\left|B_{N}\right|}>1-\varepsilon,

hence
\rho\in O\subset\Xi(P,\varepsilon)
.
∎

If we specialize to the case of a countable non-abelian free group
\Gamma
and use the fact that
\Hom_{T}(\Gamma,G)
is a closed, and hence
G_{\delta}
, subset of
\Hom(\Gamma,G)
, then we obtain:

Corollary
.

If
P\subset\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma)
is
G_{\delta}
then

\Xi_{T}(P)\coloneqq\Hom_{T}(\Gamma,G)\cap\Xi(P)=\{\rho\in\Hom_{T}(\Gamma,G)\ |\ \mu\{x\ |\ \rho(\Gamma)_{x}\in P\}=1\},

is a
G_{\delta}
-subset of
\Hom_{T}(\Gamma,G)
.

§ 3.1.2
.

The following proposition shows that various properties of interest to us are
G_{\delta}
in the Chabauty space. Here and in the sequel we use the symbol
\subset_{f}
to denote finite subsets; given a subgroup
\Delta<\Lambda
we denote by
\pi_{\Delta}\colon\Lambda\to\Lambda/\Delta
the canonical quotient map.

Proposition
.

If
\Lambda
is a countable group, then the sets
\mathrm{Sub}^{\text{cf}}(\Lambda)
,
\mathrm{Sub}^{\text{coa}}(\Lambda)
and
\mathrm{Sub}^{\text{cht}}(\Lambda)
of core free subgroups, co-amenable subgroups and co-highly transitive subgroups respectively are

G_{\delta}
-subsets of
\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)
.

Proof.

Firstly, we can write

\mathrm{Sub}^{\text{cf}}(\Lambda)=\bigcap_{1\neq\gamma\in\Lambda}\left(\bigcup_{\eta\in\Lambda}\Miss(\{\eta\gamma\eta^{-1}\})\right),

which is a countable intersection of Chabauty-open subsets of
\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)
.

Secondly, given
A,F\subset_{f}\Lambda
and
\varepsilon>0
we denote by
\Omega_{A,F,\varepsilon}
the set of
\Delta\in\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)
such that
\pi_{\Delta}\colon\Lambda\to\Lambda/\Delta
is injective on
A
and
\pi_{\Delta}(A)\subset\Lambda/\Delta
is an
(F,\varepsilon)
-Følner subset. Explicitly, the latter means that for all
\gamma\in\Lambda
we have

|\gamma\pi_{\Delta}(A)\triangle\pi_{\Delta}(A)|/|\pi_{\Delta}(A)|<\varepsilon
, and by definition we have

\mathrm{Sub}^{\text{coa}}(\Lambda)=\bigcap_{n\geq 1}\bigcap_{F\subset_{f}\Lambda}\bigcup_{A\subset_{f}\Lambda}\Omega_{A,F,1/n}.

We thus fix
A,F\subset_{f}\Lambda
and
\varepsilon>0
; we have to show that
\Omega_{A,F,\varepsilon}
is open.
Concerning the first condition, we note that

\{\Delta\in\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)\mid\pi_{\Delta}|_{A}\text{ injective}\}=\Miss(A^{-1}A\setminus\{1\}),

and for every
\Delta
is in this set and all
\gamma\in\Lambda
we have
|A|=|A/\Delta|=|\gamma A/\Delta|
.
Next, take any
\Delta\in\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)
any
\gamma\in F,\omega,\omega^{\prime}\in A
and observe that
\gamma\omega^{\prime}\Delta=\omega\Delta\in\gamma A/\Delta\cap A/\Delta
if and only if
\omega^{-1}\gamma\omega^{\prime}\in\Delta
. So we see that
if
\Delta\in\Omega_{A,F,\varepsilon}
then also

\Nbh(\Delta,A^{-1}FA\cup A^{-1}A)\subset\Omega_{A,F,\varepsilon}.

This shows that the set of co-amenable subgroups is
G_{\delta}
.

Finally, given
\gamma\in\Lambda
,
\Omega\subset_{f}\Lambda
and
\sigma
a permutation of
\Omega
we set

H(\gamma,\Omega,\sigma)\coloneqq\{\Delta\in\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda)\ |\forall\omega\neq\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega\colon\,\omega^{-1}\omega^{\prime}\not\in\Delta,\,{\text{ and }}\forall\omega\in\Omega\colon\,\gamma\omega\Delta=\sigma(\omega)\Delta\}.

Then
\Delta\in H(\gamma,\Omega,\sigma)
if and only if
\pi_{\Delta}
is injective on
\Omega
and
\gamma
induces the permutation
\sigma
on
\pi_{\Delta}(\Omega)
, and
H(\gamma,\Omega,\sigma)
is Chabauty-open, since

H(\gamma,\Omega,\sigma)=\Miss(\Omega\setminus\{1\})\cap\bigcap_{\omega\in\Omega}\Env(\{\sigma(\omega)^{-1}\gamma\omega\})

The proposition then follows from the fact that

\mathrm{Sub}^{\text{cht}}(\Lambda)=\bigcap_{\Omega\subset_{f}\Lambda}\left(\bigcap_{\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega}\bigcap_{\sigma\in\Sym(\Omega^{\prime})}\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Lambda}H(\Lambda,\gamma,\Omega^{\prime},\sigma)\right).\qed

Combining this with Corollary
Proposition
we deduce:

Corollary
.

The subsets of
\Hom_{T}(\Gamma,G)
consisting of representations
\rho
such that for almost every
x\in X
the action of
\Gamma
on the orbit
\rho(\Gamma)x
is amenable, faithful or highly-transitive respectively are
G_{\delta}
in
\Hom_{T}(\Gamma,G)
.

3.2.
Faithful representations

§ 3.2.1
.

Given a representation
\rho\in\Hom(\Lambda,G)
we say that
\rho
is
faithful on almost every orbit
if and only if

\mu(\{x\in X\mid\forall\,\gamma\in\Lambda\setminus\{1\}:\rho(\gamma)|_{[x]}\neq 1\})=1.

This implies that
\rho
is faithful, and if
\mathcal{R}
is ergodic, then also the converse holds:

Lemma
.

Assume that
\mathcal{R}
is ergodic and let
\rho\in\Hom(\Lambda,G)
. Then
\rho
is faithful if and only if it is faithful on almost every orbit.

Proof.

For every
\gamma\in\Lambda
the set
X_{\gamma}\coloneqq\{x\in X\mid\rho(\gamma)|_{[x]}\neq 1\}
is a measurable union of
\mathcal{R}
-orbits, hence has measure either
0
or
1
. Thus

\rho(\gamma)\neq 1\iff\mu(X_{\gamma})\neq 0\iff\mu(X_{\gamma})=1.

Now assume that
\rho
is faithful; since
\Lambda
is countable we deduce that

\forall\gamma\in\Lambda\setminus\{1\}:\,\mu(X_{\gamma})=1\Longrightarrow\mu\left(\bigcap_{\gamma\in\Lambda\setminus\{1\}}X_{\gamma}\right)=1\Longrightarrow\rho\text{ faithful on every orbit.}\qed

§ 6.3.1

Recall that for subgroups of

Proposition
.

Let
\Lambda\leq G
be dense. Then
\Lambda
acts highly transitively on almost every orbit.

Proof.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\pi\in\Sym_{n+1}
.
For
g\in G
we say that
g_{x,n}=\pi

if
\alpha(g,x)|_{[0,n]}=\pi
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
.
It suffices to show that

\mu(\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Lambda}\{x\in X\mid\gamma_{x,n}=\pi\})>1-2\varepsilon.

There exists
g\in G
with
\mu(\{x\in X\mid g_{x,n}=\pi\})>0
.
By density, there exists
\gamma\in\Lambda
and
A\subset X
with
\mu(A)>0
and

\gamma_{x,n}=\pi
for all
x\in A
.
By ergodicity of
T
we know that
X=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}T^{k}A
.
The idea is now the following:
If a transformation
T_{k}\in\Lambda
is very close to
T^{k}
, then on a large part of
T^{k}A
we will have
(T_{k}\gamma T_{k}^{-1})_{x,n}=\pi
.

Recall that
d(g,h)=\mu(\{x\in X\mid g(x)\neq h(x)\})
.
Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and choose
T_{k}\in\Lambda
with
d(T_{k}^{-1}T^{n^{\prime}},T^{-k+n^{\prime}})<\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{k}(n+1)}
for all
0\leq n^{\prime}\leq n
. This implies that

\mu(\{x\in X\mid\exists 0\leq n^{\prime}\leq n\colon T_{k}^{-1}(T^{n^{\prime}}x)\neq T^{-k+n^{\prime}}x\})<\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{k}}
.

Set
\gamma_{k}:=T_{k}\gamma T_{k}^{-1}

Now

\displaystyle\{x\in T^{k}A\mid(\gamma_{k})_{x,n}=\pi\}

\displaystyle\supset\{x\in T^{k}A\mid\forall 0\leq n^{\prime}\leq n\colon T_{k}^{-1}(T^{n^{\prime}}x)=T^{-k}(T^{n^{\prime}}x)\}=:A_{k}

and by the above
\mu(T^{k}A\setminus A_{k})\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{k}}
.
Summarizing

\displaystyle\mu(\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Lambda}\{x\in X\mid\gamma_{x,n}=\pi\})

\displaystyle\geq\mu(\bigcup_{k\geq 0}\{x\in X\mid(\gamma_{k})_{x,n}=\pi\})

\displaystyle\geq\mu(\bigcup_{k\geq 0}A_{k})

\displaystyle\geq 1-\sum_{k\geq 0}\mu(T^{k}A\setminus A_{k})

\displaystyle>1-\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{k}}=1-2\varepsilon.

∎

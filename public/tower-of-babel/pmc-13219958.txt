Outcomes of Restorative Treatment for Isolated Patellar Cartilage Lesions: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis
Authors: Jelle P van der List, Tarik Taoufik, David C Flanigan, Robert A Magnussen, Nicholas A Apseloff
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground:Patellar cartilage lesions often cause debilitating symptoms, and several treatments exist. However, most review studies have combined patellar and trochlear lesions.Purpose:To assess the outcomes of different treatments of only patellar cartilage lesions.Study Design:Systematic review; Level of evidence, 4.Methods:The PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane Library databases were searched in March 2025 for clinical studies reporting outcomes of advanced cartilage treatments beyond microfracture or chondroplasty. The inclusion criteria consisted of isolated patellar lesions (no trochlear or bipolar lesions), regardless of concomitant procedures, with a minimum 1-year follow-up and at least 10 patients. Relevant outcomes were failure, reoperation, and complication rates, and patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs). Random-effect models were used, and outcomes were reported with 95% CIs.Results:A total of 36 studies (1058 patients; mean age, 32 years; follow-up, 5.1 years) were included. Most patients underwent (matrix-induced) autologous chondrocyte implantation (MACI/ACI; mean lesion, 4.3 cm2), followed by osteochondral autograft transfer (OAT/mosaicplasty; mean lesion, 1.5 cm2) and autologous matrix-induced chondrogenesis (AMIC; mean lesion, 2.9 cm2). Most studies were level 3 or 4 evidence and published since 2010. MACI/ACI resulted in an overall failure risk of 8.8% (4.4%-14%), a Lysholm score of 72.8 (68.7-76.8), a Kujala score of 74.8 (66.9-82.8), and an International Knee Documentation Committee (IKDC) score of 65.3 (60.7-69.9). OAT/mosaicplasty resulted in an overall failure risk of 1.7% (0.4%-6.7%), a Lysholm score of 82.7(76.1-89.3), a Kujala score of 77.6 (75.3-79.8), and an IKDC score of 84.2 (65.4-103). AMIC resulted in a failure risk of 8% (3.9%-15.9%), a Lysholm score of 87.7 (85.1-90.4), a Kujala score of 78.7 (63.9-93.5), and an IKDC score of 84.3 (73.9-94.7). Sensitivity analysis of lesions <3.5 cm2 showed equivalent failure rates among treatments, and mean IKDC scores were 84.2 (65.4-103), 67.2 (63.1-73.1), and 89.5 (84.2-94.9) after OAT/mosaicplasty, MACI/ACI, and AMIC, respectively.Conclusion:In this systematic review with meta-analysis, overall good to excellent outcomes were observed for all identified treatments, with mean estimated failure rates <10% and PROMs ranging from 70% to 85% of the maximum score. Treatment with OAT/mosaicplasty and AMIC resulted in the highest IKDC score for patients with lesions <3.5 cm2.Keywords: autologous chondrocyte implantation, cartilage lesion, patella, osteochondral allograft, osteochondral autograft transfer, tibial tubercle osteotomy

Patellar cartilage lesions commonly result in knee symptoms such as pain, joint effusions, catching or locking, and/or the inability to perform daily activities or sports. 54 The incidence of cartilage lesions during arthroscopy is approximately 60% at a mean age of 35 to 40 years, of which 10% to 25% have patellar lesions, and 5% to 10% of patients have full-thickness lesions.19,27,39 Patellar lesions often occur after patellar instability or patellar maltracking, and various underlying factors can contribute to the onset and persistence of these lesions, such as malalignment, lateral tuberosity, trochlear dysplasia, patellar instability, muscle imbalances, or patella alta.7,21,50,51,63,64,75

Initial treatment of cartilage lesions typically consists of nonoperative modalities, with microfracture or chondroplasty considered for those with persistent symptoms, but outcomes of this approach are disappointing for larger lesions and at longer-term follow-up.2,32,44,53,65,70 As a result, treatments have shifted toward more restorative procedures such as osteochondral autograft transplantation (OAT) or mosaicplasty, osteochondral allograft (OCA), or (matrix-associated) autologous chondrocyte implantation (MACI or ACI), which are often performed with concomitant procedures such as tibial tubercle osteotomy (TTO), trochleoplasty, medial patellofemoral ligament reconstruction (MPFLR), and lateral retinaculum lengthening or release (LRL).

Given the various treatment options for these cartilage lesions, it is important to assess the overall outcomes in the literature to guide treatment for surgeons and patients. However, most systematic reviews on this topic combine patellar and trochlear lesions together,3,16,38 or only report on specific procedures. 17 One study reported on isolated patellar chondral treatment 30 but several new studies have been published since, indicating that an updated and complete systematic review is of additional value.6,20,22,52

Therefore, this study aimed to assess the outcomes of advanced cartilage treatments for isolated patellar lesions. The hypothesis was that the various treatments would lead to satisfactory outcomes regarding failure, reoperation, return to sports, and subjective outcomes.

Methods

This systematic review with meta-analysis followed the PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) guidelines, and the study was registered in PROSPERO (number blinded). There was no funding or conflict of interest for this study.

Literature Search

The electronic databases PubMed, Cochrane Library, and Embase were searched on March 26, 2025, for studies reporting on the outcomes of surgical treatment for patellar cartilage lesions. The search terms included “(patella OR patellar OR patellofemoral) AND (cartilage OR osteochondral or chondral) AND (((osteochondral) AND (allograft OR autograft)) OR transplantation OR implantation OR restoration OR repair) AND (outcomes OR reoperation OR results) AND knee” and were limited to English studies with human subjects. In the first round, titles and abstracts of all identified studies were reviewed for potential inclusion; in the second round, the full texts of the potential studies were reviewed for inclusion. In addition, the references of 7 systematic reviews and meta-analyses on patellofemoral cartilage injuries were reviewed to ensure no studies were missed.14,23,24,30,38,56,71 Agreement was ultimately reached on inclusion and exclusion for all studies, and no third reviewer was required.

The inclusion criteria consisted of (1) isolated patellar cartilage lesion (ie, no bipolar or trochlear lesions); (2) advanced cartilage treatments (ie, no chondroplasty or microfracture); (3) outcomes at minimum 1 year follow-up; (4) minimum of 10 patients; (5) indication of chondral or osteochondral lesions; and (6) skeletal maturity. A minimum of 1 year and a minimum of 10 patients were chosen to ensure capturing all relevant studies on this topic. The exclusion criteria consisted of (1) animal or biomechanical studies; (2) concomitant other cartilage lesions treated; and (3) indication of osteoarthritis. Concomitant procedures such as TTO or MPFLR were not considered exclusion criteria, as they are frequently performed in patients with cartilage lesions (Figure 1).

Quality Assessment

The level of evidence of the included studies was assessed using the adjusted Oxford Centre for Evidence-Based Medicine. 80 The methodological quality of the included studies was assessed using the Methodological Index for Non-Randomized Studies (MINORS) criteria, as no randomized studies were identified. 69 Finally, the quality of evidence was graded using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) approach. 35

Data Collection

Extracted data were collected in Excel (Version 2021; Microsoft Corp). Baseline data consisted of author names, publication year, journal, number of knees, age, length of follow-up, sex, type of procedure, and concomitant procedures. Based on the available data, relevant outcomes consisted of failures (defined by authors and as revision of procedure or conversion to arthroplasty), reoperations, complications, return to sports (RTS), Lysholm score, Kujala, International Knee Documentation Committee (IKDC) score, Tegner activity level, and visual analog scale of pain (VAS).

Statistical Analysis

For statistical analysis, Excel and the R package (Version 4.2.3; R Foundation for Statistical Computing) were used. 72 Baseline characteristics were reported as weighted means, and means and standard deviations were calculated using predefined methods if not reported and possible.40,78 Given the lack of comparative studies, no comparative meta-analyses were performed. Heterogeneity was assessed using I2 values; values <25% were considered low. 10 Forest plots were generated for all outcomes by treatment modality, using random-effect models, and reported with 95% CIs. Additionally, plots were designed to assess the correlation between defect size and patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) regardless of treatments. Finally, sensitivity analysis was performed by only including lesions <3.5 cm2 for IKDC and failure rates.

Quality of Studies

The included studies had limited level of evidence with only 1 level 2 prospective 26 study, 8 level 3 studies,6,20,28,37,49,57,59,61 and the majority of level 4 studies. ¶ Included noncomparative studies had a mean MINORS criteria score of 12 out of 16 points, corresponding to 75% of the maximum score. In contrast, comparative studies had a mean score of 17.6 out of 24, corresponding to 73% of the maximum score (Table 1). Given the heterogeneity of studies and the lack of level 1 studies, the GRADE recommendation was very low. Of the studies included, 81% were published since 2010 and 36% since 2020.

Baseline Characteristics

A total of 36 studies were included, with 1058 patients with a mean age of 32 years (range, 13-64 years), a mean follow-up of 5.1 years (range, 1.7-17.6 years), and 54% were women. The overall mean treated location size was 3.6 cm2 (range, 1.1-6.3 cm2) (Table 2). A total of 27 studies reported concomitant procedures in 779 patients, with TTO most frequently performed (28%), followed by LRL or release (24%), MPFLR (13%), and trochleoplasty (4%) (Table 3).

Eighteen studies reported on the outcomes of ACI/MACI with 606 patients, who had a mean age of 32 years (range, 13-61 years), a mean follow-up of 6 years (range, 2-17.6 years), and the largest mean lesion size of all treatments (mean, 4.3 cm2). The second most common treatment was OAT/mosaicplasty, with 9 studies and 188 patients with comparable baseline characteristics (mean age, 32 years; mean follow-up, 3.1 years), although they had the smallest mean lesion size (mean, 1.5 cm2 [range 1.1-2.4 cm2]). The third most reported treatment is AMIC, with 7 studies and 131 patients with a mean age of 35 years (range, 16-54 years), a mean follow-up of 4.3 years, and a mean defect size of 2.9 cm2.

The 2 least frequently reported procedures were OCA (2 studies, 48 patients) and PJAC (2 studies, 85 patients). Those studies had similar baseline characteristics, with mean ages of 37 and 27 years, respectively, and mean lesion sizes of 3.1 and 2.7 cm2, respectively. The OCA cohorts had relatively long follow-up (3.9 and 9.7 years), whereas the PJAC cohorts did not have any studies beyond short-term follow-up (2.4 and 2.8 years).

ACI and MACI

Thirteen studies (476 patients) reported an overall estimated failure rate of ACI/MACI of 8.8% (95% CI, 4.4%-14%), and 8 studies (297 patients) reported an overall estimated reoperation rate of 9.7% (95% CI, 2.1%-34.5%). Ten studies (336 patients) reported an overall estimated complication rate was 16.4% (95% CI, 6%-37.7%). Only 2 studies (93 patients) reported an RTS rate of 71% (95% CI, 46.4%-87.5%), and 2 studies (105 patients) reported a Lysholm score of 72.8 (95% CI, 68.7%-76.8%), which was lower than that of other treatments. The estimated Kujala score was comparable to other treatments, with an estimated overall score of 74.8 (95% CI, 66.9%-82.8%) in 3 studies (141 patients). However, the IKDC was lower than that of other treatments, with an estimated overall score of only 65.3 (95% CI, 60.7%-69.9%) in 7 studies involving 289 patients. Similarly, the Tegner score was higher than the overall mean (3.5 [95% CI, 2.6%-4.4%]) in 2 studies with 52 patients, and the VAS pain was higher, although this was based on only 1 study with 25 patients 61 (Table 4). Sensitivity analysis with only lesions <3.5 cm2 revealed a failure rate of 2.6% (95% CI, 0.4%-13.9%) and IKDC of 67.2 (95% CI, 63.1%-71.3%).

OAT/Mosaicplasty

Six studies (115 patients) reported the lowest overall estimated failure rate for any treatment at 1.7% (95% CI, 0.4%-6.7%) and a comparable reoperation rate of 7.3% (95% CI, 2.8%-17.9%). Seven studies (133 patients) reported comparable complication rates with an estimated 8.8% (95% CI, 2%-31.9%). RTS was achieved in 100% of patients (2 studies, 29 patients). The Tegner level was higher than in other studies (5.5), but this was only reported in 1 study with 34 patients. 20 The Lysholm score (5 studies, 108 patients), Kujala score (4 studies, 98 patients) and VAS pain (1 study, 20 patients) were comparable to other treatments with estimated means of 82.7 (95% CI, 76.1%-89.3%), 77.6 (95% CI, 75.3%-79.8%), and 2.4 (95% CI, 1.2%-3.6%), respectively. The IKDC score (2 studies, 32 patients) was higher than ACI/MACI, with an estimated mean of 84.2 (95% CI, 65.4%-103%).

AMIC

Four studies (87 patients) reported an estimated failure rate of 8% (95% CI, 3.9%-15.9%) after AMIC, a reoperation rate of 0.4% (95% CI, 0%-75.1%), and 6 studies (119 patients) reported an estimated complication rate of 1.4% (95% CI, 0%-46.9%). Kujala and Tegner activity scores were equivalent to other treatments with 78.7 (95% CI, 63.9%-93.5%) and 4.4 (95% CI, 3.9%-4.9%), respectively, whereas RTS was lower compared with other treatments, with 2 studies (45 patients) reporting a rate of 35.6% (95% CI, 23.1%-50.4%). Finally, Lysholm and IKDC scores were relatively high compared with other treatments, and VAS pain was relatively low, with estimated means of 87.7 (95% CI, 85.1%-90.4%), 84.3 (95% CI, 73.9%-94.7%), and 1.8 (95% CI, 1.2%-2.4%), respectively. Sensitivity analysis with only lesions <3.5 cm2 revealed a failure rate of 9.6% (95% CI, 4.6%-18.8%) and an IKDC score of 89.5 (95% CI, 84.2%-94.9%).

OCA and PJAC

Only 2 studies reported on OCA and PJAC outcomes each, preventing a reliable meta-analysis of outcomes, except for PJAC failure rates. Two studies (85 patients) reported an overall estimated failure rate of 7.1% (95% CI, 3.2%-14.8%).

Discussion

Most studies of isolated patellar cartilage lesion treatment have been published on ACI/MACI procedures, followed by OAT/mosaicplasty and AMIC. Overall, good to excellent outcomes were observed across all treatments, with mean estimated failure rates <10% and PROMs ranging from 70% to 85% of the maximum score. AMIC had the lowest incidence of reoperations and complications, and OAT and AMIC reported the highest IKDC and Lysholm scores compared with ACI/MACI. However, OAT/mosaicplasty had the smallest defect size, and ACI/MACI had the largest defect size and the longest follow-up.

This study identified that ACI/MACI procedures were most reported in the literature. This is perhaps not surprising as the first study on ACI was published in 1994, and the technique has undergone several developments to improve outcomes.11-13,33,79 The indications for the different treatments in this study vary significantly, which should be considered when interpreting the results. Not only were there differences in defect size between the different treatments (ie, OAT/mosaicplasty had the smallest defect and ACI/MACI the largest), but the follow-up of ACI/MACI was also longer (Table 2), and the incidence of concomitant procedures differed between the cohorts as well (Table 3).

Regarding failure rates in this study, they were overall similar across treatments, without significant differences. However, the failure rate of OAT/mosaicplasty was lower (1.7%) than that of other treatments. This can likely be explained by the fact that patients in this cohort had smaller defects and shorter follow-up than those in other treatments. Nonetheless, these findings show that OAT/mosaicplasty can be an excellent treatment for patients with smaller lesions. These findings are consistent with the literature on tibiofemoral cartilage treatments. 62 Oeding et al 62 assessed the outcomes of OAT at 15-year follow-up in 12 studies with 495 patients and noted excellent outcomes and low rates of reoperation. Based on these studies, it seems that OAT for patellar lesions leads to excellent results and might be the preferred treatment for smaller lesions, even though cartilage thickness is different between the patella and other areas of the knee. 68

Interestingly, the PROMs (IKDC and Lysholm) were lowest in the ACI/MACI group and highest in the OAT/mosaicplasty and AMIC groups. For systematic reviews like this one, it is impossible to correct for baseline differences such as lesion size or length of follow-up. However, when reviewing the outcome scores by defect size (Figure 5), one can see that Kujala and Lysholm scorers did not differ between smaller and larger lesions, and that the IKDC score was only mildly worse in the larger group. In contrast, the VAS pain score was indeed worse at follow-up in patients with initially larger lesions. Therefore, it seems that defect size can only partially explain the inferior outcomes of MACI for patellar lesions. Among the AMIC studies, the study with the largest defect size (4.4 cm2) by Kusano et al 47 had indeed inferior IKDC outcomes compared with AMIC for smaller defect sizes (2.5 and 2.7 cm2), 57 but still had superior IKDC scores compared with 6 out of 7 ACI/MACI studies.

Although only a limited number of studies assessed RTS, OAT/mosaicplasty was associated with the highest rate, with all 29 patients returning to sports in 2 studies. This is similar to studies focusing on RTS rates for all cartilage lesions in the knee (with the majority being tibiofemoral lesions), as various systematic reviews have shown that the highest RTS rates are achieved after OAT/mosaicplasty.15,45,46

In this study, complications reported in the included studies were somewhat higher in the MACI/ACI cohort than in other studies, with rates ranging from 0% to 60%. Five out of 10 studies reported a complication rate of 31% or higher. When reviewing these studies, hypertrophy of the graft was the most common complication after ACI/MACI, followed by chondroplasty, arthrofibrosis, and periosteal patch complications in the older studies.6,29,37,43 The issues of graft hypertrophy were not seen with other treatments. However, the AMIC study by Kusano et al 47 reported a 45% stiffness rate, which could be attributed to a slower rehabilitation protocol, and the study by Waltenspül et al 77 mainly reported complications with the TTO screws.

Limitations

Limitations are present in this study. First, the study design, as a systematic review with meta-analysis, prevents the correction for potential confounders, such as defect size and length of follow-up, and limits conclusions about the direct comparison of treatments. Nonetheless, this study provides an overview of the available evidence in the literature for the outcomes of all treatments for their indications, and the sensitivity analysis with lesions <3.5 cm2 enables assessment of smaller lesions. Furthermore, different reviews may have used different criteria for outcomes of reoperations and complications, which may be explained by procedures that are indirectly related, such as TTO screw removal. Finally, this study cannot control for the role of concomitant procedures, year of procedure, and treatment indications.

Conclusion

In this systematic review with meta-analysis, overall good to excellent outcomes were observed across all identified treatments, with mean estimated failure rates <10% and PROMs ranging from 70% to 85% of the maximum score. Treatment with OAT/mosaicplasty and AMIC resulted in the lowest reoperation rates and highest IKDC and Lysholm scores, although these lesions generally had smaller defect sizes.

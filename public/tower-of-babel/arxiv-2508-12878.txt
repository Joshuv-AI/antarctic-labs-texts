Form factors of composite branch-point twist operators in the sinh-Gordon model on a multi-sheeted Riemann surface: semiclassical limit
Michael Lashkevich, Amir Nesturov, Landau Institute for Theoretical Physics, 142432 Chernogolovka, Russia Kharkevich Institute for Information Transmission Problems, 19 Bolshoy Karetny per., 127994 Moscow, Russia Department of Physics, Faculty of Mathematics and Physics, University of Ljubljana, Jadranska 19, Ljubljana SI-1000, Slovenia ML: lashkevi@landau.ac.ru, AN: amir.nesturov@fmf.uni-lj.si

Abstract
Quantum sinh-Gordon model in 1 + 1 1+1 dimensions is one of the simplest and best-studied massive integrable relativistic quantum field theories. We consider this theory on a multi-sheeted Riemann surfaces with a flat metric, which can be seen as a pile of planes connected to each other along cut lines. The cut lines end at branch points, which are represented by a twist operator 𝒯 n {\mathscr{T}}_{n} . Operators of such kind are interesting in the framework of the problem of computing von Neumann and Renyi entanglement entropies in the original model on the plane. The composite branch - point twist operators (CTO) are a natural generalization of the twist operators, obtained by placing a local operator to a branch point by means of a certain limiting procedure. Correlation function in quantum field theory can be, in principle, found by means of the spectral decomposition. It allows one to express them in terms of form factors of local operators, i.e. their matrix elements in the basis of stationary states. In integrable models complete sets of exact form factors of all operators can be found exactly as solutions of a system of bootstrap equations. Nevertheless, identification of these solution to the operators in terms of the basic fields remains problematic. In this work, we develop a technique of computing form factors of a class of CTO determined in terms of the basic field in the semiclassical approximation.

Any operator is uniquely defined by a complete set of its matrix elements with respect to the eigenstates of the Hamiltonian. In the case of a relativistic quantum field theory such eigenstates are characterized by sets of types, internal states and momenta of stable particles in the corresponding in
-

Correlation functions of local operators can be expressed in terms of form factors by means of the spectral decomposition. For example, the vacuum two

(2.23)

Explicitly it reads

\nabla^{2}\phi-m^{2}\sinh\phi=-8\pi n\nu\delta(x).

(2.24)

We are interested in the radial solutions. Let us fix the asymptotics such solutions at small values of the radial coordinate
r
. Transform eq. (
2.24
) to the variables
\zeta=\tilde{x}^{1}+{\rm i}\tilde{x}^{2}
,
{\bar{\zeta}}=\tilde{x}^{1}-{\rm i}\tilde{x}^{2}
:

\tilde{\nabla}^{2}\phi-m^{2}n^{2}|\tilde{x}|^{2n-2}\sinh\phi=-8\pi n\nu\delta(\tilde{x}).

(2.25)

For the asymptotics we may neglect the potential term, and thus we have

\phi=-4n\nu\log|\tilde{x}|+O(1)=-4\nu\log r+O(1)\quad\text{as $r\to 0$.}

(2.26)

The radial solution
\phi(x)=\phi_{\nu}(mr)
with this property reads
[
14
,
15
]

\phi_{\nu}(t)=\sum^{\infty}_{N\in 2{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}+1}\lambda^{N}\phi^{(N)}(t)\mathchar 59\relax\qquad\lambda={\sin\pi\nu\over\pi}\mathchar 59\relax

(2.27)

where

\phi^{(N)}(t)={4\over N}\int d^{N}\theta\,\prod^{N}_{i=1}{{\rm e}^{-t\cosh\theta_{i}}\over 2\cosh{\theta_i-\theta_{i+1}\over 2}}

(2.28)

with
\theta_{N+1}=\theta_{1}
. We see that the function satisfies the ‘reflection’ identities

\phi_{\nu}(t)=-\phi_{-\nu}(t)=\phi_{1-\nu}(t).

(2.29)

In fact, the asymptotic property (
2.26
) is valid in the region
-{1\over 2}<\nu<{1\over 2}
only. In the region
0\leq\nu\leq 1
the asymptotics is given by

{\rm e}^{\mp{1\over 2}\phi_{\pm\nu}(t)}=\beta_{\nu}t^{2\nu}+\beta_{1-\nu}t^{2-2\nu}+O(t^{2})\quad\text{as $t\to 0$,}

(2.30)

where

\beta_{\nu}=2^{-6\nu}{\Gamma({1\over 2}-\nu)\over\Gamma({1\over 2}+\nu)}.

(2.31)

It contains two concurrent contributions, and in the region
|\nu|<{1\over 2}
the first term dominates, while in the region
{1\over 2}<|\nu|<1
the second one dominates. There is no solution that have the asymptotic (
2.26
) with real
\nu
such that
|\nu|>{1\over 2}
. This is the classical counterpart of the reflection property known in the quantum field theory
[
11
,
12
]
. Thus we will always assume

|\nu|<\tfrac{1}{2}.

(2.32)

We will also need the large
t
asymptotic of the solution
\phi_{\nu}(t)
. It is dominated by the first term in the series (
2.28
) and reads

\phi_{\nu}(t)\approx\sqrt{8\pi}\lambda t^{-1/2}{\rm e}^{-t}\quad\text{as $t\to\infty$.}

(2.33)

Now decompose the field
\varphi(x)
into the classical background and quantum field
\chi(x)
:

\varphi(x)=b^{-1}\phi_{\nu}(mr)+\chi(x).

(2.34)

So the next step is to expand the action (
2.9
) in powers of the field
\chi
:

S[b^{-1}\phi_{\nu}+\chi]-S[b^{-1}\phi_{\nu}]=S_{0}[\chi]+S_{\text{int}}[\chi].

(2.35)

The first term in the r.h.s. is the quadratic contribution

S_{0}[\chi]={1\over 16\pi}\int d^{2}x\,\left((\partial_{\mu}\chi)^{2}+m^{2}\chi^{2}\cosh\phi_{\nu}(mr)\right).

(2.36)

The second term contains higher powers in
\chi
and will be considered as an interaction action:

\begin{gathered}{}S_{\text{int}}[\chi]=\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}S^{(k)}[\chi]\mathchar 59\relax\qquad S^{(k)}[\chi]=b^{k-2}\int d^{2}x\,\chi^{k}(x)H^{(k)}_{\nu}(x)\mathchar 59\relax\\
H^{(k)}_{\nu}(x)={m^{2}\over 16\pi\,k!}\quantity(\e^{\phi_\nu(mr)}+(-1)^k \e^{-\phi_\nu(mr)}).\end{gathered}

(2.37)

Each term
S^{(k)}
is of the order
b^{k-2}
and contains
k
th power of
\chi
, so that it is represented by a
k
-
leg vertex in the Feynman diagram technique.

2.3
Radial quantization

The perturbation expansion based on the decomposition (
2.35
)–(
2.37
) is performed against the rotationally symmetric background of the radial solution
\phi_{\nu}(mr)
. Let us start from the linear approximation, which corresponds to the action
S_{0}
. In this approximation the field
\chi
satisfies the equation of motion

\left(\partial_{r}^{2}+r^{-1}\partial_{r}+r^{-2}\partial_{\xi}^{2}-m^{2}\cosh\phi_{\nu}(mr)\right)\chi(x)=0.

(2.38)

The quantum field
\chi(x)
should be represented as a decomposition in solutions to this equation with operator coefficients. Due to the rotational symmetry it is natural to decompose
\chi(x)
in the eigenfunctions of the angular momentum.

In the vicinity of the origin this decomposition is known from the conformal field theory on the
(\zeta\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}{\bar{\zeta}})
plane in the radial quantization picture:

\displaystyle\chi(x)

\displaystyle\simeq{\mathbf{Q}}-2{\rm i}{\mathbf{P}}\log{\zeta\bzeta\over R^{2/n}}+\sum_{k\neq 0}\left({{\mathbf{a}}_{k}\over{\rm i}k}\zeta^{-k}+{\bar{\mathbf{a}}_{k}\over{\rm i}k}{\bar{\zeta}}^{-k}\right)

\displaystyle={\mathbf{Q}}-{4{\rm i}\over n}{\mathbf{P}}\log{r\over R}+\sum_{k\neq 0}\left({{\mathbf{a}}_{k}\over{\rm i}k}{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}{\tilde{k}}\xi}+{\bar{\mathbf{a}}_{k}\over{\rm i}k}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}{\tilde{k}}\xi}\right)r^{-{\tilde{k}}}\quad\text{as $r\to 0$;}\qquad\tilde{k}={k\over n}.

(2.39)

The sum is taken over integer values of
k
. We will often use the ‘tilde’ notation as a synonym to dividing by
n
. The scale
R
is arbitrary and drops out of all conformal correlation functions. The operators
{\mathbf{P}}
,
{\mathbf{Q}}
,
{\mathbf{a}}_{k}
,
\bar{\mathbf{a}}_{k}
form a Heisenberg algebra with the commutation relations

[{\mathbf{P}}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}{\mathbf{Q}}]=-{\rm i}\mathchar 59\relax\qquad[{\mathbf{a}}_{k}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}{\mathbf{a}}_{l}]=[\bar{\mathbf{a}}_{k}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\bar{\mathbf{a}}_{l}]=2k\delta_{k+l}.

(2.40)

All other commutators are equal to zero. We will also need the vacuum states
|0\rangle_{\nu}
,
\langle\infty|
defined as follows:

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathbf{P}}|0\rangle_{\nu}={\mathbf{a}}_{k}|0\rangle_{\nu}=\bar{\mathbf{a}}_{k}|0\rangle_{\nu}=0\mathchar 59\relax

(2.41)

\displaystyle\langle\infty|{\mathbf{Q}}=\langle\infty|{\mathbf{a}}_{-k}=\langle\infty|\bar{\mathbf{a}}_{-k}=0\mathchar 59\relax\qquad k>0.

The subscript
\nu
at the vacuum is not necessary mathematically, but it will be convenient to use it to mark the classical background.

In the radial quantization picture the radial variable
|\zeta|
plays the role of imaginary time, while the angular variable
\mathop{\rm Arg}\nolimits\zeta
plays the role of a space coordinate. The infinite past is supposed to be at the point
\zeta=0
, while the infinite future is supposed to be at the point
\zeta=\infty
.

We extend the decomposition (
2.39
) to all distances in the form

\chi(x)={\mathbf{Q}}f_{0}(x)+{4{\rm i}\over n}{\mathbf{P}}f_{*}(x)+\sum_{k\neq 0}\left({{\mathbf{a}}_{k}\over{\rm i}k}f_{k}(x)+{\bar{\mathbf{a}}_{k}\over{\rm i}k}\bar{f}_{k}(x)\right).

(2.42)

According to (
2.38
), the functions
f_{k}(x)
are solutions to the equation

\left(\partial_{r}^{2}+r^{-1}\partial_{r}+r^{-2}\partial_{\xi}^{2}-m^{2}\cosh\phi_{\nu}(mr)\right)f_{k}(x)=0\mathchar 59\relax\qquad k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\cup\{*\}\mathchar 59\relax

(2.43)

with fixed angular dependence:

f_{k}(x)={\rm e}^{-{\rm i}{\tilde{k}}\xi}m^{\tilde{k}}u_{k}(mr)\mathchar 59\relax\qquad f_{*}(x)=u_{*}(mr).

(2.44)

We assume the asymptotic conditions

u_{k}(t)=t^{-{\tilde{k}}}(1+o(t))\mathchar 59\relax\qquad u_{*}(t)=-\log t+O(1)\quad\text{as $t\to 0$}

(2.45)

to fit the expansion (
2.39
), and

u_{k}(t)=O\bigl(t^{-1/2}{\rm e}^{-t}\bigr)\quad\text{for $k>0$ or $k=*$ as $t\to\infty$}

(2.46)

to ensure that correlation functions decrease properly at infinity. The functions
u_{k}(t)
satisfy the sinh
-
Bessel equation
[
8
]

u^{\prime\prime}(t)+t^{-1}u^{\prime}(t)-\left(\cosh\phi_{\nu}(t)+\kappa^{2}t^{-2}\right)u(t)=0\mathchar 59\relax\qquad t>0\mathchar 59\relax

(2.47)

for

\kappa=\begin{cases}{\tilde{k}}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}&\text{if $k\in{\mathbb{Z}}$;}\\
0\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}&\text{if $k=*$.}\end{cases}

(2.48)

This equation generalizes the Bessel equation and reduces to it for
\nu=0
.

In what follows we will also use the sinh
-
Bessel functions
K_{\nu\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\kappa}(t)
,
I_{\nu\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\kappa}(t)
, which are solutions to the sinh
-
Bessel equation (
2.47
) with the asymptotic properties

K_{\nu\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\kappa}(t)=\begin{cases}2^{\kappa-1}\Gamma(\kappa)t^{-\kappa}(1+o(1))\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}&\text{if $\kappa>0$;}\\
-\log t+O(1)\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}&\text{if $\kappa=0$,}\end{cases}\qquad I_{\nu\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\kappa}(t)={t^{\kappa}\over 2^{\kappa}\Gamma(\kappa+1)}(1+o(t))

(2.49)

as
t\to 0
and

K_{\nu\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\kappa}(t)=\sqrt{\pi\over 2}{{\rm e}^{-t}\over t^{1/2}}(K^{\infty}_{\nu\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\kappa}+O(t^{-1}))\mathchar 59\relax\qquad I_{\nu\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\kappa}(t)={1\over\sqrt{2\pi}}{{\rm e}^{-t}\over t^{1/2}}\left((K^{\infty}_{\nu\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\kappa})^{-1}+O(t^{-1})\right)

(2.50)

as
t\to\infty
. The Wronskian of these solutions is

K_{\nu\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\kappa}(t)I^{\prime}_{\nu\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\kappa}(t)-K^{\prime}_{\nu\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\kappa}(t)I_{\nu\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\kappa}(t)=t^{-1}.

(2.51)

The connection coefficients read

K^{\infty}_{\nu\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\kappa}={\Gamma^{2}\left(\kappa+1\over 2\right)\over\Gamma\left({\kappa+1\over 2}+\nu\right)\Gamma\left({\kappa+1\over 2}-\nu\right)}.

(2.52)

It is evident that the functions
u_{k}
only differ from the sinh
-
Bessel functions by a normalization:

u_{k}(t)=\begin{cases}K_{\nu\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}0}(t)\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}&\text{if $k=*$;}\\
2^{1-{\tilde{k}}}\Gamma^{-1}({\tilde{k}})K_{\nu\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}{\tilde{k}}}(t)\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}&\text{if $k>0$;}\\
2^{-{\tilde{k}}}\Gamma(1-{\tilde{k}})I_{\nu\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}-{\tilde{k}}}(t)\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}&\text{if $k\leq 0$.}\end{cases}

(2.53)

The main point is that the correlation functions of the field
\chi(x)
in the unperturbed theory (with respect to the quadratic action
S_{0}
) is given by a matrix element in the radial quantization picture:

\langle\chi(x_{1})\cdots\chi(x_{N}){\mathscr{T}}_{n}V_{\nu}(0)\rangle_{0}=\langle\infty|\mathop{\rm T}\nolimits_{r}[\chi(x_{1})\cdots\chi(x_{N})]|0\rangle_{\nu}\mathchar 59\relax

(2.54)

where
\mathop{\rm T}\nolimits_{r}
is the radial ordering symbol:

\mathop{\rm T}\nolimits_{r}\quantity[\cO_1(x_1)\cdots\cO_N(x_N)]={\mathscr{O}}_{\sigma_{1}}(x_{\sigma_{1}})\cdots{\mathscr{O}}_{\sigma_{N}}(x_{\sigma_{N}})\mathchar 59\relax\qquad r_{\sigma_{1}}>\cdots>r_{\sigma_{N}}\mathchar 59\relax

(2.55)

for integer spin local operators
{\mathscr{O}}_{i}
. The consistency check of the radial quantization scheme and the proof of identity (
2.54
) are given in Appendix
A
.

2.4
Correlation functions and form factors

In the radial quantization picture the correlation function
G^{(n)}_{\mathscr{O}}
defined in (
2.14
) is given by

G^{(n)}_{\mathscr{O}}\quantity(\{x_i\}_N)=\bra{\infty}\mathop{\rm T}\nolimits_{r}\left[\varphi(x_{n})\ldots\varphi(x_{1}){\rm e}^{-S_{\text{int}}}\right]\ket{\cO}\mathchar 59\relax

(2.56)

where
\ket{\cO}
is a state that can be constructed from to the operator
{\mathscr{T}}_{n}{\mathscr{O}}
in a unique way. Basically, this construction can be described as follows. The state
|0\rangle_{\nu}=|V_{\nu}\rangle
defined in (
2.41
) on the background of the classical solution
\phi_{\nu}(mr)
corresponds to the operator
{\mathscr{T}}_{n}V_{\nu}
. The descendant operators (
2.17
) are obtained recursively from the exponential operators by the procedure (
2.7
). This procedure is transferred to the radial picture straightforwardly:

\displaystyle{}G^{(n)}_{\partial^{k}\varphi\,{\mathscr{O}}}(\{x_{i}\}_{N})

\displaystyle=\left[\oint_{|\zeta|^{n}=r_{0}}{d\zeta\over 2\pi{\rm i}\zeta}{\partial^{k}\over\partial\zeta^{k}}G^{(n)}_{\mathscr{O}}(\{x_{i}\}_{N}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,x)+(\text{c.t.})\right]_{r_{0}\to 0}\mathchar 59\relax

(2.57)

\displaystyle G^{(n)}_{\bar{\partial}^{k}\varphi\,{\mathscr{O}}}(\{x_{i}\}_{N})

\displaystyle=\left[\oint_{|\zeta|^{n}=r_{0}}{d\zeta\over 2\pi{\rm i}\zeta}{\partial^{k}\over\partial{\bar{\zeta}}^{k}}G^{(n)}_{\mathscr{O}}(\{x_{i}\}_{N}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,x)+(\text{c.t.})\right]_{r_{0}\to 0}.

Thus the calculation of each correlation function
G^{(n)}_{\mathscr{O}}(\{x_{i}\}_{N})
reduces to the calculation of the correlation functions
G^{(n)}_{\nu}(\{x_{i}\}_{N^{\prime}})
with
N^{\prime}\geq N
.

The form factors are related to the correlation functions according to the following reduction formula
[
8
]
:

F^{(n)}_{\mathscr{O}}(\{\theta_{i}\}_{N})=\left(-\sqrt{4\pi Z_{\varphi}}\right)^{-N}\lim_{r_{i}\to\infty}\left(\prod^{N}_{i=1}r_{i}\int_{C}d\xi_{i}\,{\rm e}^{{\rm i}mr_{i}\sinh(\theta_i+\i\xi_i)}\mathop{\mathord{\mathop{\partial}\limits^{\lower 1.50694pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\leftrightarrow$}}}}\nolimits_{r_{i}}\right)G^{(n)}_{\mathscr{O}}(\{x_{i}\}_{N}).

(2.58)

Here
Z_{\varphi}
is the wave function renormalization constant. Since
Z_{\varphi}=1+O(b^{4})
we will omit it in what follows. The left
-
right derivative
\mathop{\mathord{\mathop{\partial}\limits^{\lower 1.50694pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\leftrightarrow$}}}}\nolimits
is defined as

f(t)\mathop{\mathord{\mathop{\partial}\limits^{\lower 1.50694pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\leftrightarrow$}}}}\nolimits_{t}g(t)=\tfrac{1}{2}(f(t)g^{\prime}(t)-f^{\prime}(t)g(t)).

There is a subtlety regarding the definition of the contour
C
. In the case of a field theory on the plane and a local operator
{\mathscr{O}}
the correlation function is periodic in
\xi_{i}
with the period
2\pi
, so that
C
can be assumed to be the segment
[-\pi\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\pi]
. But for a twist operator corresponding to a branch point of a multi
-
sheeted manifold this periodicity is broken to the
2\pi n
periodicity. A consistent way to overcome this difficulty demands adding two integrals over the variable
r
on the banks of the cut to infinity. Instead, we continue the contour
C
in the complex plane to
\pm{\rm i}\infty
, but accept the rule described below.

The correlation functions
G^{(n)}_{\mathscr{O}}
have the form of Fourier series in the variables
\xi_{i}
, which come from the functions
{\rm e}^{\mp{\rm i}{\tilde{k}}\xi_{i}}u_{k}(mr_{i})
in the series (
2.42
). The upper sign corresponds to the coefficient at
{\mathbf{a}}_{k}
, while the lower sign to that at
\bar{\mathbf{a}}_{k}
. Correspondingly we have integrals of the form

I^{\pm}_{\alpha}(t\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\theta)=\int_{C^{\pm}}{d\xi\over 2\pi}\,{\rm e}^{{\rm i}t\sinh(\theta+\i\xi)+{\rm i}\alpha\xi}\mathchar 59\relax\qquad\alpha=\mp{\tilde{k}}.

(2.59)

Here the contour
C^{+}
is chosen so that
\zeta\to\infty
at its ends and
C^{-}
so that
{\bar{\zeta}}\to\infty
at its ends, namely

C^{\pm}=(\overrightarrow{-\pi\mp{\rm i}\infty\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}-\pi})\cup[\overrightarrow{-\pi\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\pi}]\cup(\overrightarrow{\pi\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\pi\mp{\rm i}\infty}).

(2.60)

The arrows indicate the direction of the contour. We will consider these integrals in the sense of analytic continuation from imaginary values of
\theta
in the vicinity of
-{{\rm i}\pi\over 2}
. Make a change
\xi\to\xi+{\rm i}\theta-{\pi\over 2}
. Then we have

I^{\pm}_{\alpha}(t\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\theta)=\int_{C^{\pm}}{d\xi\over 2\pi}\,{\rm e}^{t\cos\xi+{\rm i}\alpha\left(\xi+{\rm i}\theta-{\pi\over 2}\right)}={\rm e}^{-\alpha\left({{\rm i}\pi\over 2}+\theta\right)}\int_{C^{\pm}}{d\xi\over 2\pi}\,{\rm e}^{t\cos\xi+{\rm i}\alpha\xi}.

Rigorously speaking the contour
C^{\pm}
should be shifted by
-{\rm i}\theta+{\pi\over 2}
, but we assume this shift to be small enough and ignore it. We immediately recognize the
I
Bessel function in the r.h.s.:

I^{\pm}_{\alpha}(t\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\theta)={\rm e}^{-\alpha\left({{\rm i}\pi\over 2}+\theta\right)}I_{\mp\alpha}(t)={\rm e}^{\pm{\tilde{k}}\left({{\rm i}\pi\over 2}+\theta\right)}I_{\tilde{k}}(t).

(2.61)

The choice of the contour leads to the fact that the form factors are expressed in terms of the expressions
tI_{\tilde{k}}(t)\mathop{\mathord{\mathop{\partial}\limits^{\lower 1.50694pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\leftrightarrow$}}}}\nolimits_{t}u_{k}(t)
and
tI_{0}(t)\mathop{\mathord{\mathop{\partial}\limits^{\lower 1.50694pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\leftrightarrow$}}}}\nolimits\phi_{\nu}(t)
, which have finite limit as
t\to\infty
. The choice of the contour is not crucial in the leading order in
b
, where such expressions with
k>0
or
k=*
only enter the form factors, but we fix it for future studies of subleading contributions.

We considered the integration contours as lying on the
s=0
sheet of the manifold
{\mathscr{M}}_{n}
. Due to the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
symmetry of the manifold we may translate it to other sheets. Instead of translating them we will shift the variables
\theta_{i}
,
\theta^{\prime}_{i}
in (
1.1
), (
1.3
) assuming them to live on a set of
n
lines for the in
-
states:

\theta\in\bigcup^{n-1}_{s=0}({\mathbb{R}}-2\pi{\rm i}s).

(2.62)

It means that
\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\theta
is the rapidity of the particle, while
s=-\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\theta/2\pi
is the sheet number.

3
Properties of the sinh
-
Bessel functions

In this section we study the properties of the sinh
-
Bessel functions
K_{\nu\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}k}(t)
,
I_{\nu\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}k}(t)
. In
[
8
]
they were introduced and studied for integer values of the parameter
k
. The main instrument was the approach based on the Fredholm determinant formalism
[
16
]
. Here we use this formalism to study these functions for non
-
integer values of
k
. This formalism provides an explicit expression for the functions
K_{\nu\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}k}(t)
in terms of a series of multiple integrals with powers of the parameter
\lambda
as coefficients. The functions
I_{\nu\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}k}(t)
are defined indirectly, but we provide a construction of the series in powers of
t
for both types of functions.

In this section we consider the letters
k
,
l
as arbitrary real (or even complex after the analytic continuation) numbers, with no relation to the indices in sections
2
and
4
. Physically they correspond to
{\tilde{k}}
,
{\tilde{l}}
or
\kappa
there.

3.1
Definitions in terms of the Fredholm determinants

Consider the space
{\mathscr{F}}=L_{2}[0\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\infty)
and define the operator
\hat{K}
on
{\mathscr{F}}
with the kernel

K(u\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}v)={E(u)E(v)\over u+v}\mathchar 59\relax\qquad E(u)=\exp\left(-{1\over 2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{K}}t_{k}u^{k}\right).

(3.1)

Here
t_{k}\in{\mathbb{C}}
are parameters and
\mathbb{K}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
is an arbitrary finite or countable set. In application to the theories on the multi
-
sheeted surface with one branch point of the order
n
it is natural to set
\mathbb{K}={1\over n}{\mathbb{Z}}
, but generally it is not necessary. We will only assume that
0\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\pm 1\in\mathbb{K}
. Let us also define the functions

\phi({\vec{t}})=2\log\det(1+\hK)-2\log\det(1-\hK)\mathchar 59\relax\qquad\vec{t}=\{t_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{K}}

(3.2)

in terms of the Fredholm determinants
\det(1\pm\hK)
. More explicitly

\phi(\vec{t})=2\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits\log{1+\hK\over 1-\hK}=\sum_{r\in 2{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}+1}{4\over r}\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits{\hat{K}}^{r}=\sum_{r\in 2{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}+1}{4\over r}\int d^{r}u\prod^{r}_{i=1}{{\rm e}^{-\sum_{k\in\mathbb{K}}t_{k}u_{i}^{k}}\over u_{i}+u_{i+1}}\mathchar 59\relax\quad u_{r+1}=u_{1}.

(3.3)

For those values of the parameters
t_{k}
, for which the integrals converge, the function
\phi(\vec{t})
is proved
[
16
]
to satisfy the classical sinh
-
Gordon equation:

\partial_{1}\partial_{-1}\phi=\sinh\phi\mathchar 59\relax

(3.4)

where
\partial_{k}=\partial/\partial t_{k}
. For an arbitrary function
\Phi(\vec{t})
let us define the function
\Phi^{\text{r}}(z\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}{\bar{z}})=\Phi(\vec{t})
restricted to the values

t_{1}=mz/2\mathchar 59\relax\qquad t_{-1}=m{\bar{z}}/2\mathchar 59\relax\qquad t_{0}=-\log\lambda\mathchar 59\relax\qquad t_{k}=0\quad(k\neq 0\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\pm 1).

(3.5)

We will refer the function
\Phi^{\text{r}}
as to the basic reduction of the function
\Phi
. For the function
\phi^{\text{r}}
we have

\phi^{\text{r}}(z\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}{\bar{z}})=\phi_{\nu}(m|z|).

(3.6)

For
k_{1}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\ldots\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}k_{s}\in\mathbb{K}
define the functions

\Phi_{k_{1}\ldots k_{s}}(\vec{t})=(-1)^{s}\partial_{k_{1}}\cdots\partial_{k_{s}}\phi(\vec{t}).

(3.7)

In what follows we only discuss the cases
s=1\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}2
, since we restrict ourselves to the descendant operators with no more than two derivatives. The restricted functions
\Phi^{\text{r}}_{k_{1}\ldots k_{s}}
factorize

\Phi^{\text{r}}_{k_{1}\ldots k_{s}}(z\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}{\bar{z}})={\tilde{\Phi}}_{k_{1}\ldots k_{s}}(m|z|){\rm e}^{-{\rm i}\sum_{i}k_{i}\xi}.

(3.8)

Note that all these functions have the same large
t
asymptotics

{\tilde{\Phi}}_{k_{1}\ldots k_{s}}(t)=\sqrt{8\pi}\lambda t^{-1/2}{\rm e}^{-t}(1+O(t))\quad\text{as $t\to\infty$.}

(3.9)

Besides, all these functions are symmetric with respect to transposition of subscripts and simultaneous change of their signs:

{\tilde{\Phi}}_{k_{1}\ldots k_{s}}(t)={\tilde{\Phi}}_{k_{\sigma_{1}}\ldots k_{\sigma_{s}}}(t)={\tilde{\Phi}}_{-k_{1}\ldots-k_{s}}(t)\qquad(\forall\sigma\in S_{s}).

(3.10)

The
t_{k}
-
derivative of equation (
3.4
) provides a consistent equation for
\Phi_{k}
:

(\partial_{1}\partial_{-1}-\cosh\phi)\Phi_{k}=0.

(3.11)

After the basic reduction the radial part
{\tilde{\Phi}}_{k}
satisfies the sinh
-
Bessel equation (
2.47
) with
\kappa=k
. Since the function
{\tilde{\Phi}}_{k}(t)
decreases at infinity, it is proportional to the function
K_{\nu\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}k}(t)
. The proportionality factor is easily established by a comparison of the asymptotics (

4.3
Renormalization procedure and operator
{\mathscr{T}}_{n}(\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi\,V_{\nu})

From now on we discuss the operators that demand renormalization. The renormalization procedure is known from the conformal perturbation theory
[
17
]
. Recall the aspects of this theory that are relevant to our consideration. In the conformal perturbation theory the action
S
of a field theory is split as follows

S=S_{\text{CFT}}+\mu\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}d^{2}x\,\Phi_{\text{p}}(x)\mathchar 59\relax

(4.22)

where
S_{\text{CFT}}
is the action of the conformal field theory that corresponds to the ultraviolet fixed point of the field theory under consideration, and
\Phi_{\text{p}}(x)
is a primary spinless local operator of conformal dimension
\Delta_{\text{p}}<1
. Let
\{{\mathscr{O}}_{i}\}
be a complete set of linearly independent quasiprimary local operators in the ultraviolet conformal theory with conformal dimensions
(\Delta_{i}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\bar{\Delta}_{i})
. The second term in (
4.22
) is considered as a perturbation action. The terms of the perturbation expansion may contain ultraviolet (UV) divergences in the vicinity of the operators
{\mathscr{O}}_{i}(x)
. With the spacial UV cutoff
r_{0}
the term of the order
k
looks like

(-\mu)^{k}\int_{|y_{i}-x|>r_{0}}d^{2k}y\,\Phi_{\text{p}}(y_{1})\cdots\Phi_{\text{p}}(y_{k}){\mathscr{O}}_{i}(x)=\sum_{j:\,{\delta^{(k)}{}^{j}_{i}\leq 0}}U^{(k)}{}_{i}^{j}f^{(k)}{}^{j}_{i}(r_{0}){\mathscr{O}}_{j}(x)+\text{(UV-finite terms)}\mathchar 59\relax

(4.23)

where
U^{(k)}{}^{j}_{i}
are
c
-
number coefficients and

f^{(k)}{}^{j}_{i}(r)=\begin{cases}{r^{{2\delta^{(k)}{}^{j}_{i}}}\over 2\delta^{(k)}{}^{j}_{i}}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}&{\delta^{(k)}{}^{j}_{i}<0};\\
\log{\rho\over r}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}&\delta^{(k)}{}^{j}_{i}=0\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\end{cases}\qquad\delta^{(k)}{}^{j}_{i}=\Delta_{j}-\Delta_{i}+k(1-\Delta_{\text{p}}).

(4.24)

The scale
\rho
is arbitrary. Correlation functions can be made UV finite by adding counterterms to each operator. Namely, the renormalized operator has the form

{\mathscr{O}}^{\text{ren}}_{i}(x)={\mathscr{O}}_{i}(x)+\sum_{k>0}\>\sum_{i:\,{\delta^{(k)}{}^{j}_{i}\leq 0}}V^{(k)}{}^{j}_{i}f^{(k)}{}^{j}_{i}(r_{0}){\mathscr{O}}_{j}(x){}\biggr|_{r_{0}\to 0}

(4.25)

with finite coefficients
V^{(k)}{}^{j}_{i}
, which can be expressed in terms of the coefficients
U^{(k)}{}^{j}_{i}
. Note that the operators
{\mathscr{O}}_{j}
that enter the r.h.s. of (
4.23
), (
4.25
) with nonzero coefficient are not arbitrary. They must exist in the operator product expansion of
k
instances of
\Phi_{\text{p}}
and
{\mathscr{O}}_{i}
. In particular, they have the same spin:
\Delta_{j}-\bar{\Delta}_{j}=\Delta_{i}-\bar{\Delta}_{i}
.

The case
\delta^{(k)}{}^{j}_{i}=0
is special. The appearance of the arbitrary dimensional constant
\rho
leads to an ambiguity in the renormalization procedure. If either the theory or the operator depends on a continuous parameter
p
, and the quantity
\delta^{(k)}{}^{j}_{i}
vanishes at a certain value
p_{0}
of this parameter, this leads to a pole of correlation functions of the operator
{\mathscr{O}}_{i}
at
p=p_{0}
. This is why this case is called the resonant one.

In the case of the sinh
-
Gordon model the UV conformal field theory is the free massless field theory, while the perturbation is the hyperbolic cosine term:

\begin{gathered}{}S_{\text{CFT}}=\int_{{\mathscr{M}}_{n}}d^{2}x\,{(\partial_{\mu}\varphi)^{2}\over 16\pi}=\sum^{n}_{s=1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}d^{2}x\,{(\partial_{\mu}\varphi^{(s)})^{2}\over 16\pi}\mathchar 59\relax\\

(4.26)

The conformal perturbation theory describes correlation functions at the distances
r\ll m^{-1}
, while the semiclassical approximation is valid for
r\gg{\rm e}^{-b^{-2}}m^{-1}
. The validity regions of these two expansions overlap, but the semiclassical theory describes larger distances. It means that though the structure of the renormalization terms in the semiclassical theory should be the same, the functions

may differ from those of the conformal perturbation theory by terms of the order

. We will discuss it later on particular examples.

It can be checked that the exponential operators

5
Conclusion

In this work we extended the semiclassical approach to calculation of form factors
[
8
]
to the composite twist operators (CTOs) in the sinh
-
Gordon model on multi
-
sheeted Riemann surfaces.

Form factors of the exponential CTOs are already known exactly, and in the semiclassical limit
b\sim\hbar^{1/2}\to 0
they reduce to a product of simple constant one
-
particle contributions. However, the form factors of the descendant operators, obtained from the exponential ones by multiplication by products of space
-
time derivative of the basic field
\varphi
, are essentially quantum objects. Even in the leading order in
b
they are determined by the quantum fluctuations about the classical background, and, generally, beyond the quadratic approximation. Furthermore, non
-
chiral descendant operators demand renormalization. We study the renormalization procedure in detail and demonstrate its consistency with that in the conformal perturbation theory introduced by Al. Zamolodchikov
[
9
]
.

We would like to give several comments that could suggest directions for the future studies:

•

The form factors of local operators in an integrable model of quantum field theory can be obtained in two ways. First, they can be obtained exactly by solving a system of bootstrap equations. In this way the identification of a solution to a particular operator in terms of the basic field is a separate and complicated problem. Second, the form factors of a given operator defined in terms of the basic fields can be found approximately using a kind of perturbation theory. In our case, it is the perturbation theory on a classical background. Comparison of the results of the two methods can approach us to a solution of the identification problem. We are planning to perform such comparison in the upcoming paper.

•

Obtained form factors are expressed in terms of the asymptotic coefficients of the generalized sinh
-
Bessel functions. We present here a basic theory of these functions, but the problems formulated above demand a deeper theory and more explicit and general expressions for the coefficients. In particular, a more detailed study of the general functions
\Phi_{k_{1}\ldots k_{s}}
is necessary to investigate higher descendant operators (with
K+L>2
in (
2.17
)) and the subleading contributions in the parameter
b
.

•

It would be interesting to generalize the technique to the sine
-
Gordon model. Such generalization is not reduced to using solutions with imaginary values of the parameter
\nu
. To describe topological solitons (kinks and antikinks) rotationally non
-
invariant solutions should be taken into account.

It should be noted that the results of this paper simplify the derivation and extend the results of
[
8
]
in the case
n=1
. Another remark is that most of our results are not restricted to integer values of
n
. Though the non
-
integer values of
n
have no clear interpretation in terms of the Minkowski space, in the case of the Euclidean space they correspond to spaces with a conic singularity. This may facilitate application of the results to computing the von Neumann entanglement entropy.

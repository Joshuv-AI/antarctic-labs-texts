On the cohomology of simple Shimura varieties with non quasi-split local groups
Jingren Chi, Thomas J. Haines

Abstract
. We study the Scholze test functions for bad reduction of simple Shimura varieties at a prime where the underlying local group is any inner form of a product of Weil restrictions of general linear groups. Using global methods, we prove that these test functions satisfy a vanishing property of their twisted orbital integrals, and we prove that the pseudostabilization base changes of such functions exist (even though the local group need not be quasi-split) and can be expressed in terms of explicit distributions in the stable Bernstein center. We then deduce applications to the stable trace formula and local Hasse-Weil zeta functions for these Shimura varieties.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the bad reduction of the simple Shimura varieties attached to unitary similitude groups introduced in the work of Kottwitz
[
Kot92a
]
. Our goal is twofold. On the one hand, we extend the results of
[
SS13
]
that require the local group to be a product of Weil restrictions of general linear groups, instead allowing the local group to be any inner form of such a group. We thus systematically deal with issues arising from the failure of the local group to be quasi-split. This extension includes the proof of a vanishing property of twisted orbital integrals of various test functions and a theory of pseudostabilization base change transfer for such functions (both of these are new features arising for non-quasi-split groups). Further, it includes a comparison of certain generalizations of the Scholze test functions defined in
[
Sch13b
]
with certain standard test distributions in the stable Bernstein center detailed in
[
Hai14
]
. On the other hand, we use these local results to describe the cohomology and the semisimple Hasse-Weil local factors of Kottwitz-type simple Shimura varieties, generalizing previous works, such as
[
SS13
]
and
[
She18
]
, to more cases of bad reduction.

1.1.
Main results

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a CM field with totally real subfield
\mathbb{F}_{0}
. Let
(\mathbb{D},*)
be a division algebra with center
\mathbb{F}
together with an involution
*
of second kind, giving rise to the unitary similitude group
\mathbb{G}
and simple Shimura varieties
\mathrm{Sh}_{K}
as in
[
Kot92a
]
, where
K\subset\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
is sufficiently small compact open subgroup. Fix a prime number
p
. Let
\mathbb{E}
be the reflex field and
\mathfrak{p}
a prime of
\mathbb{E}
above the rational prime
p
. We assume that all places of
\mathbb{F}_{0}
above
p
are split in
\mathbb{F}
. In particular,
\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is a product of inner forms of Weil restrictions of general linear groups. Also we assume that
K=K^{p}K_{p}
for compact open subgroups
K_{p}\subset\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and
K^{p}\subset\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})
.

Fix another prime number
\ell\neq p
and let
\xi
be an algebraic representation of
\mathbb{G}
giving rise to an
\ell
-adic local system
\mathcal{F}_{\xi,K}
on
\mathrm{Sh}_{K}
. Consider the
\ell
-adic étale cohomology groups

H_{\xi}^{i}:=\varinjlim_{K}H_{\acute{e}t}^{i}(\mathrm{Sh}_{K}\otimes_{\mathbb{E}}\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathcal{F}_{\xi,K})

and form their alternating sum
H_{\xi}^{*}:=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}H_{\xi}^{i}
in the Grothendieck group of
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-representations of
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{E})\times\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
. The main global theorem concerns the restriction of this representation to the local Weil group
W_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
. We choose a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-model
\mathcal{G}
of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
such that
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
is a parahoric subgroup of
\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, and we always assume
K_{p}\subseteq\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.

Theorem 1.1.1
.

There is an identity in the Grothendieck group of
\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})\times\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\times W_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
representations

H^{*}_{\xi}\cong\sum_{\pi_{f}}a(\pi_{f})\pi_{f}\otimes(r_{-\mu}\circ\varphi_{\pi_{p}}|_{W_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}}})|\cdot|^{-\dim\mathrm{Sh}/2}.

We refer to Theorem
4.3.1
for the more precise statement. Here we only mention that
\varphi_{\pi_{p}}
is the semisimple
L
-parameter of the irreducible smooth representation
\pi_{p}
of
\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. Note that by our assumption
\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is an inner form of a product of Weil restrictions a general linear groups, hence the local Langlands correspondence is known for
\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
; see, e.g.,
[
Coh18
]
.

This result is proved by adapting Scholze’s generalization of the Langlands-Kottwitz method to bad reduction cases as in
[
Sch13b
]
. Roughly speaking, using an
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
-model of the Shimura variety with
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
-level
structure and the description of points in its special fiber, one gets the following expression from the Grothendieck-Lefschetz trace formula:

(1.1)

\mathrm{Tr}(\tau\times hf^{p}|H_{\xi}^{*})=\sum_{(A,u,\lambda)}\mathrm{vol}(I(\mathbb{Q})\backslash I(\mathbb{A}_{f}))~O_{\gamma}(f^{p})~TO_{\delta\sigma}(\phi_{\tau,h})\,\mathrm{tr}\xi(\gamma_{\ell}).

Here
\tau\in\mathrm{Frob}^{j}I_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}}\subset W_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
for some
j\geq 1
(where
\mathrm{Frob}\in W_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
is a geometric Frobenius element and
I_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}}\subset W_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
is the inertia subgroup),
h\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))
is a cut-off function at
p
and
f^{p}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p}))
is a function corresponding to a Hecke operator away from
p
. The sum runs over isomorphism classes of virtual polarized abelian varieties
(A,u,\lambda)
which, roughly speaking, parametrize isogeny classes in the set of
\kappa_{\mathfrak{p}}
-points of the special fiber of the integral model. For each isogeny class one has an associated
\sigma
-conjugacy class
\delta\in\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}})
where
\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}}
is the degree
r:=j[\kappa_{\mathfrak{p}}:\mathbb{F}_{p}]
unramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
\sigma
is the Frobenius automorphism on
\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}}
, and a conjugacy class
\gamma=(\gamma_{\ell})_{\ell\neq p}\in\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})
. See §
4.4.2
for details. The main ingredient in each summand is the twisted orbital integral of certain test function
\phi_{\tau,h}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}})
that is defined in a purely local way using certain deformation spaces of
p
-divisible groups with extra structures.

Next one would like to relate the right hand side of (
1.1
) with the geometric side of the Arthur-Selberg trace formula for
\mathbb{G}
. The first step would be to associate a global (stable) conjugacy class
\gamma_{0}\in\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q})
to each summand so that
(\gamma_{0};\gamma,\delta)
forms a Kottwitz triple. However, when the local group
\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is not quasi-split, there is an obstruction to the existence of
\gamma_{0}
. Namely, such a
\gamma_{0}
exists if and only if the naive norm
N\delta:=\delta\sigma(\delta)\dotsm\sigma^{r-1}(\delta)\in\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}})
is conjugate to an element in
\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
; see Proposition
4.5.2
. When
\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is quasi-split, as in
[
Kot92a
]
,
[
Sch13b
]
, and
[
SS13
]
, this is always true and there is no obstruction to finding
\gamma_{0}
. Now we can state our first local result, the aforementioned vanishing result.

Theorem 1.1.2
.

With notations as above, if
N\delta\in\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}})
is not
\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}})
-conjugate to an element of
\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, then
TO_{\delta\sigma}(\phi_{\tau,h})=0
.

The special case of this result where
\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is the unit group of a division algebra and the relevant cocharacter is
(1,0,\dotsc,0)
is proved in previous work of Shen
[
She18
]
. Our approach to the general case is different from Shen’s method. Vanishing statements like this were first conjectured by Rapoport, for test functions for certain Shimura varieties with parahoric level structure at
p
(see
[
Rap90
, Conj. 5.7]
). Special cases of his conjecture were proved by Rapoport in
loc. cit.
, and were later generalized by Waldspurger to some additional cases (private communication), also by passing to the spectral side. Rapoport made a related vanishing conjecture in the framework of the Langlands-Rapoport Conjecture, see
[
Rap05
, Conj. 10.2]
.

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.1.2
, only those isogeny classes to which we can associate a Kottwitz triple contribute to the sum (
1.1
) and we get

\mathrm{Tr}(\tau\times hf^{p}|H_{\xi}^{*})=\sum_{(\gamma_{0};\gamma,\delta)}c(\gamma_{0};\gamma,\delta)\,O_{\gamma}(f^{p})\,TO_{\delta\sigma}(\phi_{\tau,h})\,\mathrm{tr}\xi(\gamma_{0}).

To proceed further, we need to know the spectral information of the test function
\phi_{\tau,h}
. Let us introduce more notations. The Shimura data determines a conjugacy class of cocharacters
\mu
of
\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{C})
(whose field of definition is the reflex field
\mathbb{E}
) that we also view as characters of the maximal torus
\hat{\mathbb{T}}
in the Langlands dual group
\hat{\mathbb{G}}
. Let
r_{-\mu}
be the irreducible algebraic representation of
\hat{\mathbb{G}}\rtimes W_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
whose restriction to

\hat{\mathbb{G}}
has extreme weight
-\mu
, such that
W_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
acts trivially on the extreme weight spaces. For each
\tau\in W_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
, let
z_{\tau,-\mu}
be the element of the (stable) Bernstein center of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
that acts on any irreducible smooth representation
\pi
of
\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
by the scalar

\mathrm{Tr}(\tau|(r_{-\mu}\circ\varphi_{\pi}|_{W_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}}})|\cdot|_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{-\langle\rho,\mu\rangle})

where
\varphi_{\pi}:W_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\to\hat{\mathbb{G}}\rtimes\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is the semisimple Langlands parameter of
\pi
and
\rho
is half the sum of positive roots of
\mathbb{G}
. Here the notion of positive root is determined by a choice of Borel subgroup
\mathbb{B}
containing
\mathbb{T}
, and in forming
\langle\rho,\mu\rangle
we also choose a
\mathbb{B}
-dominant representative
\mu\in X_{*}(\mathbb{T})
in its Weyl-group orbit. Now we can state our second local result.

Theorem 1.1.3
.

The Scholze test function
\phi_{\tau,h}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}}))
has matching
(
twisted
)
orbital integrals with
z_{\tau,-\mu}*h\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}))
.

This establishes a key relation between the Scholze test functions and the stable Bernstein center. Similar relations of test functions for bad reduction with the stable Bernstein center were predicted by the second author and Kottwitz, and later by Scholze and Shin. We refer the reader to
[
Hai14
]
for some background on the test function conjecture and on the transfer conjectures for the stable Bernstein center; these ideas of the second author and Kottwitz were fleshed out during the period 1998-2011. Subsequently, Scholze and Shin formulated a more flexible version (in particular allowing for arbitrary cut-off functions
h
) in
[
SS13
]
. The version of Theorem
1.1.3
is adapted to the process of pseudostabilization which can be used in the setting of this article; the above references are in the framework of stabilization, which is more widely applicable. When
\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is quasi-split, Theorem
1.1.3
was proved in
[
SS13
]
. Our proof involves a global-local argument which has the same basis as the argument in
[
SS13
]
, but which is more complicated both in terms of local harmonic analysis and the global geometric argument, when the local group is allowed to be non-quasi-split. In particular, for our argument it is necessary to introduce two companion global groups
\mathbb{G}_{\beta}
and
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}
whose roles will be detailed later in this introduction. Roughly speaking, we will study the Shimura varieties attached to the group
\mathbb{G}_{\beta}
, and express the point-counting of their special fibers in terms of automorphic representations attached to the other group
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}
(instead of the group
\mathbb{G}_{\beta}
itself).

In fact, for many cut-off functions
h
we can also find an element in the stable Bernstein center of
\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}})
whose convolution with
certain cut-off functions on
\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}})
have matching twisted orbital integrals with
\phi_{\tau,h}
, see Corollary
3.4.5
. As a consequence, we verify the special case of the test function conjecture of the second author and Kottwitz in our setting; in particular this expresses the semisimple trace of Frobenius in terms of certain functions in the Bernstein center:

\mathrm{Tr}^{\mathrm{ss}}(\mathrm{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{j}\!\times\!f^{p}|H^{*}(\mathrm{Sh}_{K_{p}K^{p}}\otimes_{\mathbb{E}}\bar{\mathbb{Q}},\mathcal{F}_{\xi,K_{p}K^{p}}))=\sum_{(\gamma_{0};\gamma,\delta)}c(\gamma_{0};\gamma,\delta)\,O_{\gamma}(f^{p})\,TO_{\delta\sigma}(z^{(r)}_{-\mu}*e_{K_{p^{r}}})\,\mathrm{tr}\xi(\gamma_{0}).

Here the level
K_{p}\subset\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
at
p
can be arbitrarily small,
K_{p^{r}}\subset\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}})
is a compact open subgroup corresponding to
K_{p}
in a suitable sense, and
e_{K_{p^{r}}}
is the characteristic function of
K_{p^{r}}
divided by its volume. Further,
f^{p}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(K^{p}\backslash\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{A}^{p}_{f})/K^{p})
is any function. Finally the main ingredient is the element
z^{(r)}_{-\mu}
in the stable Bernstein center of
\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}})
, which is defined similarly to
z_{\tau,-\mu}
, using the semisimple trace (which coincides with trace on inertia invariants in the current setting) instead of the usual trace. See Corollary
4.6.2
for a complete statement and section §
4.6
for the details.

The proof of Corollary
3.4.5
involves more delicate harmonic analysis than what is needed for the proof of Theorem
1.1.3
: it requires a result on the pseudostabilization base-change of the stable Bernstein center, namely Theorem
2.5.7
. The rather involved proof of this result relies on the permanence of the vanishing property of twisted orbital integrals, with respect to convolution by elements in the stable Bernstein center (Proposition
2.5.5
), and all of these ingredients depend on our twisted local Jacquet-Langlands correspondence (Theorem
2.4.1
), which to our knowledge is a novel result. We refer to §
2
for the details.

Our debt to the ideas of Kottwitz, Rapoport, Harris-Taylor, Scholze, and Shin should be clear to the reader. Like the related articles
[
She18
,
Sch13b
,
SS13
]
, our method builds on Rapoport’s extension of the Langlands-Kottwitz method to bad reduction, which proposed the idea of counting points by weighting fixed points of Frobenius-Hecke correspondences by the (semisimple) trace of Frobenius on the stalk of the nearby cycles of appropriate local systems on the generic fiber (see
[
Rap05
, §10]
); but following Scholze, we avoid use of integral models except at the bottom of the tower, where we use an integral model for parahoric level structure. Further, like the earlier articles, we also rely heavily on the global-local arguments and Galois representation constructions of Harris-Taylor
[
HT01
]
, in the sense that we generalize their arguments to work in our specific context.

1.2.
Strategy of proof

Although the global result Theorem
1.1.1
will be an immediate consequence of the local results Theorem
1.1.2
and Theorem
1.1.3
, our proof of the local results is by a global method. Starting with the original Shimura data
(\mathbb{G},X)
, we construct a companion Shimura data
(\mathbb{G}_{\beta},X_{\beta})
whose localization at
p
is the same as that for
(\mathbb{G},X)
, and we show that in order to prove our local theorems, it is enough to work with
(\mathbb{G}_{\beta},X_{\beta})
(see Proposition
3.3.3
). More precisely, the group
\mathbb{G}={\rm GU}(\mathbb{D},*)
and the CM field
\mathbb{F}
are replaced by another pair, which we denote by
\mathbb{G}_{\beta}
and
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{K}\mathbb{F}_{0}
. The latter
\mathbb{F}
is a CM field containing a purely imaginary quadratic extension
\mathbb{K}
in which
p
splits, and
\mathbb{G}_{\beta}={\rm GU}(\mathbb{D}_{\beta},*_{\beta})
is a group like
\mathbb{G}
whose signature at the archimedean places is determined by the original
\mu
, as in our section
5.1.3
(especially Lemma
5.1.1
). For this unitary group
\mathbb{G}_{\beta}
, we have the ambient group
\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}_{\beta}
, the linear group of units of
\mathbb{D}_{\beta}
over its center
\mathbb{F}
. We establish the required theory of base change for these unitary groups (Theorem
5.2.2
) and use this to obtain the Galois representation of
{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{F}}/\mathbb{F})
attached to a cuspidal automorphic representation
\pi
of
\mathbb{G}_{\beta}(\mathbb{A})
(Theorem
5.3.1
), which by construction satisfies global-local compatibility. For each relevant
\pi
we induce the corresponding Galois representation using Shapiro’s Lemma to get something almost like a global Langlands parameter, namely an admissible homomorphism
\varphi_{\ell}(\pi)\,:\,{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{K})\rightarrow\,^{L}\mathbb{G}_{\beta}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
(see the proof of Corollary
5.3.2
). We restrict this further to
{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\tilde{\mathbb{E}})
where
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}=\mathbb{E}\mathbb{K}
(composite of the reflex field with
\mathbb{K}
), in order to compare it with the Galois representation
\sigma_{\beta,\xi}(\pi_{f})
appearing tautologically in the cohomology of the Shimura variety attached to
\mathbb{G}_{\beta}
.
Having done all that, we are in a position to use the results of Kottwitz at the good places, and the Chebotarev density theorem, in order to prove that Theorem
1.1.1
holds for
{\rm Sh}(\mathbb{G}_{\beta},X_{\beta})
, even without using the Langlands-Kottwitz-Scholze method for this special Shimura variety. The details are given in the proof of Corollary
5.3.2
.

For the next step, we run the Langlands-Kottwitz-Scholze method for
{\rm Sh}(\mathbb{G}_{\beta},X_{\beta})
. This connects the purely local test function
\phi_{\tau,h}
to global cohomology groups where the Galois action is now sufficiently pinned-down, and it would permit us to prove the desired properties of
\phi_{\tau,h}
, provided that the Langlands-Kottwitz-Scholze method could be pushed to the point of stabilization. As mentioned above, there are obstructions to converting the resulting Lefschetz trace formula of form (
1.1
) into an Arthur-Selberg trace formula without knowing the validity of the vanishing property of the test function
\phi_{\tau,h}
and without being able to construct, even when the vanishing property is known, the global elements
\gamma_{0}
in the desired Kottwitz triples
(\gamma_{0};\gamma,\delta)
. To surmount these obstructions, our main innovation is to find a replacement
(\gamma^{\prime}_{0};\gamma,\delta)
of the usual Kottwitz triples using an auxiliary group
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}
, also constructed from a division algebra with involution of the second kind, which is quasi-split at
p
and isomorphic to
\mathbb{G}_{\beta}
away from
p
and
\infty
. With this modification we can relate the right hand side of (
1.1
) to a simple trace formula for
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}
. Finally, by comparing simple trace formulas for
\mathbb{G}_{\beta}
and
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}
, we get the local results Theorems
1.1.2
and
1.1.3
and hence we deduce the global result Theorem
1.1.1
in full generality.

1.3.
Organization of the article

In §
2
, we establish several results in
p
-adic harmonic analysis. After reviewing various notions of transfer of local test functions, we prove a twisted version of the local Jacquet-Langlands correspondence (Theorem
2.4.1
) using a global method involving twisted trace formulae. As a consequence we deduce the technical results mentioned above concerning base change of the stable Bernstein center and the permanence of the vanishing property.

In §
3
, we study the deformation spaces of
p
-divisible groups with EL structures and use it to define the local test functions
\phi_{\tau,h}
, generalizing the constructions in
[
Sch13b
]
. Then we state our local results concerning its vanishing property (Theorem
3.4.3
) and its relation with certain elements in the stable Bernstein center of the local group (Theorem
3.4.4
).

In §
4
, we first review the construction of Kottwitz type simple Shimura varieties and their integral models with parahoric level structures. Then we state the
main global result Theorem
4.3.1
giving the description of the cohomology, review the Langlands-Kottwitz-Scholze method, and explain the extra ingredient needed to adapt it to our situation, that is, the vanishing property Theorem
3.4.3
of the local test function
\phi_{\tau,h}
. After that we deduce Theorem
4.3.1
from the main local result Theorem
3.4.4
concerning the spectral information of the test function
\phi_{\tau,h}
.

In §
5
, we prove the local results by embedding the local situation into a global simple Shimura variety, for which we can use several results on automorphic representations to establish the description of its cohomology directly, without using the Langlands-Kottwitz-Scholze method. From there we deduce the local results concerning the test function
\phi_{\tau,h}
by modifying the Langlands-Kottwitz-Scholze method using an auxiliary group
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}
.

1.4.
Notations

Since we work in both local and global setting and will pass back and forth between them, to minimize confusion we distinguish different settings by different fonts. We use Roman letters
F,D,B,G
etc. to denote objects over the local field
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. But note that in §
2
we use the boldface letter
\mathbf{G}
to denote an algebraic group over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, whereas we use the Roman letter
G=\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
to denote its set of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-points. We use the calligraphic font
\mathcal{G}
to denote a group scheme over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, while blackboard bold letters
\mathbb{F},\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B},\mathbb{G}
etc. are used to denote objects defined over the global field
\mathbb{Q}
.

2.
Preparations in local harmonic analysis

In this section we let
\mathbf{G}
be a reductive algebraic group over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
whose quasi-split inner form
\mathbf{G}^{*}
is isomorphic to a product of Weil restrictions of general linear groups. Let
r\geq 1
be an integer. Let
\mathbf{G}_{r}:=\mathrm{Res}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathbf{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}}}
, and let
\mathbf{G}_{r}^{*}:=\mathrm{Res}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathbf{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}}}^{*}
be the quasi-split inner form of
\mathbf{G}_{r}
. Fix a generator
\sigma
of the cyclic group
\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
which induces automorphisms of the
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-algebraic groups
\mathbf{G}_{r}
and
\mathbf{G}_{r}^{*}
that we also denote by
\sigma
.
We denote
G=\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
,
G_{r}=\mathbf{G}_{r}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}})
, and similarly
G^{*}=\mathbf{G}^{*}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
,
G_{r}^{*}=\mathbf{G}_{r}^{*}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=\mathbf{G}^{*}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}})
.

2.1.
Transfer of twisted orbital integrals

For each element
\delta\in G_{r}
, define its
naive norm
to be the element

N_{r}\delta:=\delta\sigma(\delta)\dotsm\sigma^{r-1}(\delta)\in G_{r}.

Then there exists an element
\mathcal{N}_{r}\delta\in G^{*}
that is stably conjugate to the naive norm
N_{r}\delta
. The stable conjugacy class of
\mathcal{N}_{r}\delta
only depends on the stable
\sigma
-conjugacy class of
\delta
and we call it
the norm of
\delta
in
G^{*}
; see
[
Kot82
,
\lx@sectionsign 5
]
. Similarly, for
\delta\in G_{r}^{*}
we have the naive norm
N_{r}^{*}\delta\in G_{r}^{*}
and the norm
\mathcal{N}_{r}^{*}\delta\in G^{*}
, the latter of which is, a priori, a stable conjugacy class in
G^{*}
. We say
\delta\in G_{r}
(resp.
\delta^{*}\in G_{r}^{*}
) is
\sigma
-semisimple
if
N_{r}\delta
(resp.
N_{r}\delta^{*}
) is semisimple.

For the groups and elements considered here, stable (
\sigma
-) conjugacy just coincides with ordinary (
\sigma
-) conjugacy.

Lemma 2.1.1
.

(1)

The map
\delta\mapsto\mathcal{N}_{r}\delta
defines an injection from the set of
\sigma
-conjugacy classes of
\sigma
-semisimple elements of
G_{r}
to the set of conjugacy classes of semisimple elements of
G^{*}
. A similar statement holds for
G_{r}^{*}
.

(2)

For each
\delta\in G_{r}
such that
N_{r}\delta
is
G_{r}
-conjugate to a regular semi-simple element in
G
, there exists
\delta^{*}\in G_{r}^{*}
such that
\mathcal{N}_{r}\delta=\mathcal{N}_{r}^{*}\delta^{*}
.

Proof.

The first part follows from
[
AC89

Definition 2.1.2

,

Since our group

The existence of stable base change in general is established in

2.2.
Reduction to matching at regular elements

It is well-known principle that in order to check the matching of (twisted) orbital integrals on all semisimple elements, it should suffice to check the matching on
regular
semisimple elements. We include a proof of this fact in our setting, following the arguments of
[
Clo90
, Prop. 7.2]
.

Proposition 2.2.1
.

Let
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G_{r})
and
\phi^{*}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G^{*})
. Suppose that for any
regular
semisimple element
\gamma^{*}\in G^{*}
we have
O_{\gamma^{*}}(\phi^{*})=0
if
\gamma^{*}
is not a norm from
G_{r}
, while if
\gamma^{*}=\mathcal{N}_{r}\delta
we have

O_{\gamma^{*}}(\phi^{*})=TO_{\delta\sigma}(\phi).

Then
\phi^{*}
is a stable base change transfer of
\phi
at all semisimple elements
\gamma^{*}
.
(
Note that the usual sign
e(\mathbf{G}_{\delta\sigma})
which would appear is trivial under our assumption that
\gamma^{*}
is regular semisimple, since
\mathbf{G}_{\delta\sigma}
is a torus.
)

Proof.

We start by recalling some facts about Shalika germs. Suppose
\gamma^{*}=\mathcal{N}_{r}\delta
is a semisimple element in
G^{*}
, not necessarily regular. By
[
Kot82
, Lem. 5.8]
, once we fix a
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-inner twisting
\psi:\mathbf{G}\rightarrow\mathbf{G}^{*}
there is an
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-inner twisting
\mathbf{G}_{\delta\sigma}\rightarrow\mathbf{G}^{*}_{\gamma^{*}}
, canonical up to inner automorphisms by
\mathbf{G}^{*}_{\gamma^{*}}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
. For any maximal
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-torus
{\mathbf{T}}\subset\mathbf{G}_{\delta\sigma}
(
{\mathbf{T}}
need not be
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-elliptic), we may assume our chosen inner twisting
\psi
gives a
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-embedding
\psi:{\mathbf{T}}\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}{\mathbf{T}}^{*}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{G}^{*}_{\gamma^{*}}\subset\mathbf{G}^{*}
, by
[
Kot82
, Cor. 2.2]
. We can arrange (by conjugating
\psi
by
\mathbf{G}^{*}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
) that
\psi(N_{r}\delta)=\gamma^{*}
. Then we can further assume (conjugating
\psi
if necessary by an element in
\mathbf{G}^{*}_{\gamma^{*}}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
and using
[
Kot82
, Cor. 2.2]
) that
\psi
gives a
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-isomorphism
\psi:{\mathbf{T}}\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}{\mathbf{T}}^{*}\subset\mathbf{G}^{*}_{\gamma^{*}}
. Then we have
N_{r}\delta\in T
and
\psi(N_{r}\delta)=\gamma^{*}
.

Fix a Haar measure
dt
on
T:={\mathbf{T}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. Let
\{u_{j}\}
be a set of representatives of unipotent conjugacy classes in
G_{\delta\sigma}
. It follows from
[
Rog90
, Proposition 8.1.1]
that there is a sufficiently small neighborhood
0\in\Omega\subset\mathrm{Lie}(T)
such that

•

the exponential map defines an isomorphism from
\Omega
to an open neighborhood of
1
in
T
;

•

Let
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
be the open subset consisting of elements
X\in\Omega
such that
\exp(X)\in T
is regular. Then
a\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega^{\prime}
for any
0\neq a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
;

•

The germ expansion holds on
\Omega^{\prime}
, i.e. for all
0\neq t\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and any
X\in\Omega^{\prime}
, denote
x_{t}:=\exp(tX)
, then we have

TO_{x_{t}\delta\sigma}(\phi)=\sum_{j}\Gamma_{u_{j}}^{G}(x_{t}\delta)\,TO_{u_{j}\delta\sigma}(\phi),\quad\forall\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G_{r})

where the Shalika germ
\Gamma_{u_{j}}
is a function on
\exp(\Omega^{\prime})\delta
that depends on the Haar measure on
T=G_{x_{t}\delta\sigma}=(G_{\delta\sigma})_{x_{t}}
and the Haar measure on
G_{u_{j}\delta\sigma}
used to define the orbital integrals.

By
[
Rog90
, Proposition 8.1.2(b)]
, the function
\Gamma_{u_{j}}^{G}
is homogeneous of degree
\frac{1}{2}d_{u_{j}}:=\frac{1}{2}\dim(G_{\delta\sigma}/G_{u_{j}\delta\sigma})
in the sense that for all
X\in\Omega^{\prime}
and
0\neq t\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
we have

\Gamma_{u_{j}}^{G}(\exp(tX)\delta)=|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}d_{u_{j}}}\Gamma_{u_{j}}^{G}((\exp X)\delta).

There is a similar Shalika germ expansion

O_{\psi(\exp(rtX))\gamma^{*}}(\phi^{*})=\sum_{j^{\prime}}\Gamma_{u^{*}_{j^{\prime}}}^{G^{*}}(\psi(\exp(rtX))\gamma^{*})\,O_{u^{*}_{j^{\prime}}\gamma^{*}}(\phi^{*}),\quad\forall 0\neq t\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

Here
\{u^{*}_{j^{\prime}}\}_{j^{\prime}}
ranges over the set of unipotent conjugacy classes in
G^{*}_{\gamma^{*}}
, and the other notation is as before. Like the above, the Shalika germs here enjoy a similar homogeneity property.

Now we prove the vanishing statement. From the definition we verify easily that for any
X\in\Omega^{\prime}
and
0\neq t\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
we have
N_{r}(\exp(tX)\delta)=\exp(rtX)N_{r}\delta
and hence under our embedding
T\hookrightarrow G^{*}_{\gamma^{*}}
we have
\mathcal{N}_{r}(\exp(tX)\delta)=\psi(\exp(rtX))\gamma^{*}
. Therefore if the semisimple element
\gamma^{*}\in G^{*}
is not a norm from
G_{r}
, then
\psi(\exp(rtX))\gamma^{*}
is also not a norm from
G_{r}
. Then by the definition of stable base change transfer we have
O_{\psi(\exp(rtX))\gamma^{*}}(\phi^{*})=0
. Looking at the degree
0
term in the germ expansion we get that
O_{\gamma^{*}}(\phi^{*})=0
.

Next we again assume that
\gamma^{*}=\mathcal{N}_{r}\delta
is a norm. We consider
\psi,{\mathbf{T}}
, and
{\mathbf{T}}^{*}
as above. We assume moreover that
{\mathbf{T}}
is a
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-elliptic torus in
\mathbf{G}_{\delta\sigma}
. Then
{\mathbf{T}}^{*}
is also a
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-elliptic torus in the inner form
\mathbf{G}_{\gamma^{*}}^{*}
of
\mathbf{G}_{\delta\sigma}
.

By Harish-Chandra descent of (twisted) orbital integrals, there exists
\phi_{1}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G_{\delta\sigma})
and
\phi_{1}^{*}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G_{\gamma^{*}}^{*})
such that for all
X\in\Omega^{\prime}
and
0\neq t\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
sufficiently close to
0
we have

(2.1)

TO^{G_{r}}_{\exp(tX)\delta\sigma}(\phi)=O_{\exp(tX)}^{G_{\delta\sigma}}(\phi_{1}),\quad O_{\psi(\exp(rtX))\gamma^{*}}(\phi^{*})=O_{\psi(\exp(rtX))}^{G^{*}_{\gamma^{*}}}(\phi_{1}^{*}).

See, for example, the proof of
[
Rog90
, p.114, proof of Proposition 8.1.2]
, or
[
AC89
, p.23-24]
.

By the descent property of Shalika germs (see
[
Rog90
, Proposition 8.1.2(a)]
) we have

(2.2)

\Gamma_{1}^{G}((\exp(tX))\delta)=\Gamma_{1}^{G_{\delta\sigma}}(\exp(tX)),\quad\Gamma_{1}^{G^{*}}(\psi(\exp(rtX))\gamma^{*})=\Gamma_{1}^{G^{*}_{\gamma^{*}}}(\psi(\exp(rtX))).

Comparing the degree
0
terms of the germ expansions in (
2.1
) we get

(2.3)

TO_{\delta\sigma}(\phi)=\phi_{1}(1),\quad O_{\gamma^{*}}(\phi^{*})=\phi_{1}^{*}(1).

On the other hand, by assumption we have
TO^{G_{r}}_{\exp(tX)\delta\sigma}(\phi)=O_{\psi(\exp(rtX))\gamma^{*}}(\phi^{*})
and hence by (
2.1
) we get

O_{\exp(tX)}^{G_{\delta\sigma}}(\phi_{1})=O_{\psi(\exp(rtX))}^{G^{*}_{\gamma^{*}}}(\phi_{1}^{*}).

Then looking at the degree
0
terms of the germ expansion and using (
2.2
), (
2.3
) we get

\Gamma_{1}^{G_{\delta\sigma}}(\exp X)\,TO_{\delta\sigma}(\phi)=\Gamma_{1}^{G^{*}_{\gamma^{*}}}(\psi(\exp(rtX)))\,O_{\gamma^{*}}(\phi^{*})

We note that
\mathbf{G}^{*}_{\gamma^{*}}
is the quasi-split inner form of
\mathbf{G}_{\delta\sigma}
. Now we claim: since
{\mathbf{T}}
is elliptic in
\mathbf{G}_{\delta\sigma}
,
\Gamma_{1}^{G_{\delta\sigma}}
and
\Gamma_{1}^{G^{*}_{\gamma^{*}}}
are constant functions of
X
and they differ by the factor
e(\mathbf{G}_{\delta\sigma})
. Then we would get that
O_{\gamma^{*}}(\phi^{*})=e(\mathbf{G}_{\delta\sigma})TO_{\delta\sigma}(\phi)
.

It remains to prove the claim above. Since
{\mathbf{T}}
is
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-elliptic in
\mathbf{G}_{\delta\sigma}
, the main theorem of
[
Rog81
]
shows that

\Gamma_{1}^{G_{\delta\sigma}}(\exp X)=(-1)^{q(G_{\delta\sigma})}d(G_{\delta\sigma})^{-1},\quad\forall X\in\Omega^{\prime}

where
q(G_{\delta\sigma})
is the
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-rank of the derived group of
\mathbf{G}_{\delta\sigma}
and
d(G_{\delta\sigma})
is the formal degree of the Steinberg representation of
G_{\delta\sigma}
(with respect to a chosen Haar measure on
G_{\delta\sigma}
). There is a similar result for
\Gamma_{1}^{G^{*}_{\gamma^{*}}}
. In
[
Kot88
]
, the measures on the groups are determined from Euler-Poincaré measures on the adjoint groups, in which case
d(G^{*}_{\gamma^{*}})=(-1)^{q(G^{*}_{\gamma^{*}})}
by
[
Bor76
]
(see also
[
GR10
, §7.1]
). Then by
[
Kot88
, Theorem 1]
, if the measures on
G_{\delta\sigma}
and
G^{*}_{\gamma^{*}}
are chosen as in
[
Kot88
]
, we have
d(G_{\delta\sigma})=d(G^{*}_{\gamma^{*}})
. Finally we note that since
\mathbf{G}^{*}_{\gamma^{*}}
is the quasi-split inner form of
\mathbf{G}_{\delta\sigma}
, we have
e(\mathbf{G}^{*}_{\gamma^{*}})=1
and
e(\mathbf{G}_{\delta\sigma})=(-1)^{q(G_{\delta\sigma})-q(G^{*}_{\gamma^{*}})}
. This completes the proof of the claim, and hence of the proposition.
∎

2.3.
A reinterpretation of stable base change transfer

When
\phi^{*}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(G^{*})
is the stable base change transfer of a function
\phi_{r}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(G_{r})
, we will write this as
\phi_{r}\longleftrightarrow\phi^{*}
. When
\phi^{*}
is the usual base-change of a function
\phi^{*}_{r}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(G^{*}_{r})
, then we write
{\rm BC}(\phi^{*}_{r})=\phi^{*}
. (By “usual base-change”, we just mean what is described in Definition
2.5.3
below, when all groups in sight are quasi-split.) We now define a variant of the first relation.

Definition 2.3.1
.

For
\phi_{r}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(G_{r})
and
\phi^{*}_{r}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(G^{*}_{r})
, we write
\phi_{r}\overset{\sigma-{\rm reg}}{\longleftrightarrow}\phi^{*}_{r}
when the following property holds for every
\sigma
-regular semisimple element
\delta^{*}\in G^{*}_{r}
:

•

If there is a
\delta\in G_{r}
such that
\mathcal{N}_{r}\delta=\mathcal{N}_{r}\delta^{*}
, then
TO^{G^{*}_{r}}_{\delta^{*}\sigma}(\phi^{*}_{r})=TO^{G_{r}}_{\delta\sigma}(\phi_{r})
.

•

If there is no such
\delta
, then
TO^{G^{*}_{r}}_{\delta^{*}\sigma}(\phi^{*}_{r})=0
.

Lemma 2.3.2
.

Fix
\phi_{r}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(G_{r})
and
\phi^{*}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(G^{*})
.

(1)

If
\phi_{r}\longleftrightarrow\phi^{*}
, then there exists a function
\phi^{*}_{r}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(G^{*}_{r})
such that
\phi_{r}\overset{\sigma-{\rm reg}}{\longleftrightarrow}\phi^{*}_{r}
and
{\rm BC}(\phi^{*}_{r})=\phi^{*}
.

(2)

Conversely, if there exists a
\phi^{*}_{r}
with
\phi_{r}\overset{\sigma-{\rm reg}}{\longleftrightarrow}\phi^{*}_{r}
and
{\rm BC}(\phi^{*}_{r})=\phi^{*}
, then
\phi_{r}\longleftrightarrow\phi^{*}
.

(3)

If
G_{r}^{*}=(G^{*})^{r}
, and if
\phi^{*}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(G^{*})
is a Jacquet-Langlands transfer of
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(G)
, then
\phi^{*}
is a stable base change transfer of
\phi_{r}:=\phi\otimes e\otimes\cdots\otimes e\in C^{\infty}_{c}(G^{r})
, where
e\in C^{\infty}_{c}(G)
is an idempotent such that
\phi*e=\phi
.

Proof.

(1). Suppose
\phi_{r}\longleftrightarrow\phi^{*}
. We first claim that
\phi^{*}={\rm BC}(\phi^{*}_{r})
for some
\phi^{*}_{r}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(G^{*}_{r})
. Suppose
\gamma^{*}\in G^{*}
is regular semisimple. If
\gamma^{*}
is not a norm from
G^{*}_{r}
, then it is not a norm from
G_{r}
(use Lemma
2.1.1
(2)) and hence
O^{G^{*}}_{\gamma^{*}}(\phi^{*})=0
. This proves the claim thanks to
[
AC89
, Prop. I.3.1]
. Next we claim that
\phi_{r}\overset{\sigma-{\rm reg}}{\longleftrightarrow}\phi^{*}_{r}
. Suppose that
\delta^{*}\in G^{*}_{r}
is
\sigma
-regular semisimple, and write
\mathcal{N}_{r}\delta^{*}=\gamma^{*}\in G^{*}
. If there is a
\delta\in G_{r}
with
\mathcal{N}_{r}\delta=\mathcal{N}_{r}\delta^{*}
, then we have

TO^{G_{r}}_{\delta\sigma}(\phi_{r})=O^{G^{*}}_{\gamma^{*}}(\phi^{*})=TO^{G^{*}_{r}}_{\delta^{*}\sigma}(\phi^{*}_{r}),

as desired. Now suppose that there is no such
\delta
. Then we claim that
\gamma^{*}
is not a norm from
G_{r}
, since if
\gamma^{*}=\mathcal{N}_{r}\delta
, then by Lemma
2.1.1
(2),
\delta
gives rise to
\delta^{**}\in G_{r}^{*}
with
\mathcal{N}_{r}\delta=\mathcal{N}_{r}\delta^{**}
, and then by injectivity of the
\mathcal{N}_{r}
for
G^{*}_{r}
, this forces
\delta^{*}
to be
G^{*}_{r}
-
\sigma
-conjugate to
\delta^{**}
, which then implies that
\delta^{*}
comes from
\delta
, a contradiction. The claim being proved, we see that
TO^{G^{*}_{r}}_{\delta^{*}\sigma}(\phi^{*}_{r})=O^{G^{*}}_{\gamma^{*}}(\phi^{*})=0
, the first equality following from
\phi^{*}={\rm BC}(\phi^{*}_{r})
. This completes the proof of (1).

(2). Suppose that
\phi^{*},\phi^{*}_{r},\phi_{r}
satisfy the given hypotheses. We need to verify the condition defining
\phi_{r}\longleftrightarrow\phi^{*}
at any semisimple element
\gamma^{*}\in G^{*}
. By Proposition
2.2.1
, we may assume
\gamma^{*}
is regular. Suppose
\gamma^{*}=\mathcal{N}_{r}\delta
for
\delta\in G_{r}
; let
\delta^{*}\in G^{*}_{r}
be an element associated to
\delta
as in Lemma
2.1.1
(2). Then
\mathcal{N}_{r}\delta^{*}=\gamma^{*}
and our hypotheses give

O^{G^{*}}_{\gamma^{*}}(\phi^{*})=TO^{G^{*}_{r}}_{\delta^{*}\sigma}(\phi^{*}_{r})=TO^{G_{r}}_{\delta\sigma}(\phi_{r}).

Now suppose that
\gamma^{*}
is not a norm from
G_{r}
. We claim that
O^{G^{*}}_{\gamma^{*}}(\phi^{*})=0
. If
\gamma^{*}
is not a norm from
G^{*}_{r}
, we are done using
\phi^{*}={\rm BC}(\phi^{*}_{r})
. If
\gamma^{*}=\mathcal{N}_{r}\delta^{*}
, then this
\delta^{*}
does not come from any
\delta\in G_{r}
, and hence
O^{G^{*}}_{\gamma^{*}}(\phi^{*})=TO^{G^{*}_{r}}_{\delta^{*}\sigma}(\phi^{*}_{r})=0
, as desired.

(3). Define
\phi^{*}_{r}=\phi^{*}\otimes e^{*}\otimes\cdots\otimes e^{*}
, where
e^{*}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(G^{*})
is an idempotent with
\phi^{*}*e^{*}=\phi^{*}
. By the proof of
[
AC89
,
\lx@sectionsign I.5
]
, we see that
\phi^{*}_{r}\overset{\sigma-{\rm reg}}{\longleftrightarrow}\phi_{r}
in the obvious sense adapted to functions in
C^{\infty}_{c}(G^{*r})
resp.,
C^{\infty}_{c}(G^{r})
. By
[
AC89
,
\lx@sectionsign I.5
]
again, we also have
{\rm BC}(\phi^{*}_{r})=\phi^{*}
, again in the obvious sense for the groups in question. Then by the same formal argument as in (2), the assertion follows.
∎

If a function

Theorem 2.4.1
.

Let
\pi^{*}
be an irreducible tempered representation of
G^{*}
and let
\Pi^{*}
be the base change lift of
\pi^{*}
, which is by definition a
\sigma
-stable representation of
G_{r}^{*}
with canonical intertwining operator
I_{\sigma}^{*}
, see
[
AC89
,
\lx@sectionsign I.2
]
.

(1)

If
\Pi^{*}
has a Langlands-Jacquet transfer to an irreducible tempered representation
\Pi
of

, then
\Pi
is
\sigma
-stable and we can choose an intertwining operator

on
\Pi

\mathrm{Tr}(\phi I_{\sigma}|\Pi)=e(\mathbf{G}_{r})\mathrm{Tr}(\phi^{*}|\pi^{*}).

(2)

\mathrm{Tr}(\phi^{*}|\pi^{*})=0.

2.4.1.
Remark on

2.4.6.
Remark on central characters

Our next task is to introduce the global method, relying on the Deligne-Kazhdan very simple trace formula. A technical point is that this formula (and its twisted version) is valid only for automorphic forms which transform by a unitary central character under translation of the argument by the center. The groups we work with are not adjoint, but have split connected centers. For these particular groups, by a twisting argument we can assume without loss of generality that the local representations

we start with, as well as all global automorphic representations, have trivial central characters, and that the functions we encounter are smooth, transform by the trivial character, and have compact support modulo center. (For more details on the twisting argument, see

for the Hecke algebra of smooth functions on

having compact modulo center support, and which transform by the trivial character of the center.

w\sigma^{-1}(L)w^{-1}=L
, together with an operator
I_{L}^{\sigma}
on
\pi_{L}
such that

I_{L}^{\sigma}\pi_{L}(w\sigma^{-1}(h)w^{-1})=\pi_{L}(h)I_{L}^{\sigma},\quad\forall h\in P.

Define an intertwining operator
I_{\sigma}
on the induced representation
\pi=i_{P}^{G}\pi_{L}
by

(I_{\sigma}f)(g):=I_{L}^{\sigma}f(w\sigma^{-1}(g)),\quad\forall g\in G_{r}.

Then we have
I_{\sigma}\pi(g)=\pi(\sigma(g))I_{\sigma}
for all
g\in G_{r}
. Moreover, the same formula defines an intertwining operator, still denoted
I_{\sigma}
, on (the Jacquet-Langlands transfers of) any irreducible generic representation in the component
V(L,D)
, since such representations are full induced modules by Zelevinsky’s theorem (
[
Zel80
, 9.7]
).

For any
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G_{r})
, the trace of
\phi I_{\sigma}
defines a regular function on the variety
V(L,D)
. Therefore the twisted character identity for any representation in
V(L,D)
can be reduced to representations in the Zariski dense subset
\mathcal{A}_{v_{0}}\cap V(L,D)
that we proved in Lemma
2.4.6
. Therefore we get

\mathrm{Tr}(\phi I_{\sigma}|\Pi)=e(\mathbf{G}_{r})\mathrm{Tr}(\phi^{*}|\pi^{*})

for any irreducible tempered representation
\pi^{*}
of
G^{*}
whose base change
\Pi^{*}
has a Langlands-Jacquet transfer
\Pi
(in the sense of Badulsecu), which is an irreducible admissisble representation of
G_{r}
.

2.5.
Transfer of the Bernstein center

We first recall some standard facts about the (stable) Bernstein center from
[
Hai14
]
. In what follows, we work over a
p
-adic field
F
, freely using the analogues of the statements above which were proved for the case
F=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. In this context
F_{r}
denotes the unique degree
r
unramified extension of
F
in a fixed algebraic closure of
F
. To simplify notation, we will use regular roman letters to denote both algebraic groups and their
F
-points (bearing in mind
G_{r}
is also the set of
F
-points for a Weil-restricted group over
F
).

Let
{}^{L}G=\hat{G}\rtimes W_{F}
be the Langlands dual group of
G
. A
semisimple
L
-parameter
for
G
is a continuous homomorphism

\lambda:W_{F}\to{}^{L}G
whose image consists of semisimple elements and whose projection to the second factor is the identity map. The set of
\hat{G}
-conjugacy classes of semisimple
L
-parameters has the structure of an infinite disjoint union of algebraic varieties, which we refer to as the
stable Bernstein variety
of
G
and denote by
\mathfrak{Y}_{G}^{\mathrm{st}}
. The
stable Bernstein center

\mathfrak{Z}_{G}^{\mathrm{st}}
is defined to be the ring of regular functions on
\mathfrak{Y}_{G}^{\mathrm{st}}
. In particular, the stable Bernstein center for
G
is canonically identified with the stable Bernstein center for its quasi-split inner form
G^{*}
. For any
Z\in\mathfrak{Z}_{G}^{\mathrm{st}}
, we sometimes denote by
Z^{*}
the corresponding element in
\mathfrak{Z}_{G^{*}}^{\mathrm{st}}
.

There is a morphism of algebraic varieties
\mathfrak{Y}_{G}^{\mathrm{st}}\to\mathfrak{Y}_{G_{r}}^{\mathrm{st}}
defined by restricting a semisimple
L
-parameter to the subgroup
W_{F_{r}}\subset W_{F}
. This induces the base change homomorphism of stable Bernstein centers:

(2.12)

b:\mathfrak{Z}_{G_{r}}^{\mathrm{st}}\to\mathfrak{Z}_{G}^{\mathrm{st}}

In our setting, since
G^{*}
is a product of Weil restrictions of general linear groups, the local Langlands correspondence is known and there is a quasi-finite map from the stable Bernstein center
\mathfrak{Z}_{G}^{\mathrm{st}}
to the Bernstein center
\mathfrak{Z}_{G}
which is an isomorphism if
G=G^{*}
. A good reference for these facts is
[
Coh18
]
. In particular, we can view an element
Z\in\mathfrak{Z}_{G}^{\mathrm{st}}
as a distribution on the group
G
acting on any irreducible smooth representation
\pi
of
G
by a scalar
Z(\pi)
. In particular, if
\pi^{*}
is the Jacquet-Langlands transfer of
\pi
, then
Z^{*}(\pi^{*})=Z(\pi)
. Moreover, for any
Z\in\mathfrak{Z}_{G_{r}}^{\mathrm{st}}
and any tempered irreducible representation
\pi^{*}
of
G^{*}
we have

Z(BC(\pi^{*}))=b(Z)(\pi^{*})

where
BC(\pi^{*})
is the base change of
\pi^{*}
whose existence is established in
[
AC89
]
.

Theorem 2.5.1
.

Let
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G_{r})
and let
\phi^{*}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G^{*})
be its stable base change transfer. Then for any
Z\in\mathfrak{Z}^{\mathrm{st}}_{G_{r}}
,
b(Z)^{*}*\phi^{*}
is a stable base change transfer of
Z*\phi
.

Proof.

Let
\phi^{*}
be a stable base change transfer of
\phi
and let
(Z*\phi)^{*}
be a stable base change transfer of
Z*\phi
. By Kazhdan’s density theorem it suffices to show that

\mathrm{Tr}(b(Z)^{*}*\phi^{*}|\pi^{*})=\mathrm{Tr}((Z*\phi)^{*}|\pi^{*})

for any irreducible tempered representation
\pi^{*}
of
G^{*}
.

Fix such a
\pi^{*}
. Let
\Pi^{*}
be the base change lifting of
\pi^{*}
to
G_{r}^{*}
as in
[
AC89
, Theorem 6.2]
. If
\Pi^{*}
does not have a Langlands-Jacquet transfer to
G_{r}
, then both sides are
0
by Theorem
2.4.1
(2).

Now suppose that
\Pi^{*}
has a Langlands-Jacquet transfer
\Pi
, which is an irreducible admissible representation of
G_{r}
. By part (1) of Theorem
2.4.1
we know that
\Pi
is
\sigma
-stable and we can choose the intertwining operator
I_{\sigma}
so that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}((Z*\phi)^{*}|\pi^{*})

\displaystyle=e(G_{r})\mathrm{Tr}((Z*\phi)I_{\sigma}|\Pi)=e(G_{r})Z(\Pi)\mathrm{Tr}(\phi I_{\sigma}|\Pi)

\displaystyle=e(G_{r})Z(\Pi^{*})\mathrm{Tr}(\phi I_{\sigma}|\Pi)=b(Z)^{*}(\pi^{*})\mathrm{Tr}(\phi^{*}|\pi^{*})=\mathrm{Tr}(b(Z)^{*}*\phi^{*}|\pi^{*}).

∎

Lemma 2.5.2
.

Let
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G(F_{r}))
and let
\phi^{*}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G^{*}(F))
be its stable base change transfer. Then
\phi
has the vanishing property if and only if
\mathrm{Tr}(\phi^{*}|\pi^{*})=0
for any irreducible tempered representation
\pi^{*}
of
G^{*}(F)
that does not have a Langlands-Jacquet transfer to
G(F)
.

Proof.

We start with some preliminary remarks. Any irreducible tempered representation
\pi
of
G(F)
(resp.
\pi^{*}
of
G^{*}(F)
) can be written as a normalized induction
i_{P(F)}^{G(F)}(\sigma)
(resp.
i_{P^{*}(F)}^{G^{*}(F)}(\sigma^{*})
) of an essentially discrete series representation
\sigma
(resp.
\sigma^{*}
) of
M(F)
(resp.
M^{*}(F)
) for a Levi factor
M\subset P
(resp.
M^{*}\subset P^{*}
); see
[
Tad90
]
. The property that
\pi^{*}
does not have a Langlands-Jacquet transfer to
G(F)
is equivalent to the condition that
M^{*}
does not transfer to
G
(comp.
[
Bad03
, p. 105]
). Indeed, if
M^{*}
does transfer to an
F
-Levi
M\subset G
, then
\sigma^{*}
has a discrete series transfer
\sigma
on
M(F)
, in which case
\pi^{*}
is the Jacquet-Langlands transfer of the tempered representation
i_{P(F)}^{G(F)}(\sigma)
for any
F
-parabolic
P
having
M
as
F
-Levi factor (see
[
Bad18
, Prop. 7.1(b)]
). Conversely, if
M^{*}
does not transfer, then
\pi^{*}
cannot be the Jacquet-Langlands transfer of a tempered irreducible representation
\pi=i_{Q(F)}^{G(F)}(\sigma)
, where
Q=LU
is a Levi factorization of an
F
-parabolic and
\sigma
is a discrete series on
L(F)
. If
\pi^{*}
were the transfer of such a
\pi
, then we would have
\pi^{*}=i_{Q^{*}(F)}^{G^{*}(F)}(\sigma^{*})
where
\sigma^{*}
is a discrete series transfer of
\sigma
, and where
Q^{*}=L^{*}U^{*}
is a transfer of
Q=LU
. By uniqueness of the discrete series inducing data of tempered representations of
G^{*}
(
[
Zel80
, Thm. 9.7]
), we must have
M=L^{*}
, contradicting our assumption that
M
does not transfer.

In a slightly different direction, Badulescu defined an injective map on representation rings

\mathrm{JL}:R(G)\rightarrow R(G^{*}),

and termed the condition that
\pi^{*}
not lie in the image by
\pi^{*}\in\mathbf{S}_{G^{*},G}
. Badulescu also proved that, for
\phi^{*}\in\mathcal{H}(G^{*}(F))
, the property that

{\rm Tr}(\phi^{*}|\pi^{*})=0,\hskip 36.135pt\forall\pi^{*}\in\mathbf{S}_{G^{*},G}

is equivalent to the property that

O_{\gamma^{*}}(\phi^{*})=0,\hskip 36.135pt\forall\gamma^{*}\in G^{*,\rm rs}(F)\backslash G(F)

that is, for all regular semisimple
\gamma^{*}
which do not transfer to
G(F)
;
see
[
Bad03
, Lem. 3.3]
. This statement is clearly related to what we wish to prove, but it is not quite sufficient.

We now give the proof, which is quite analogous to
[
Coh18
, Lem. 8.2]
.
First suppose that
\phi
has the vanishing property. Then the orbital integral of
\phi^{*}
vanishes on any regular semisimple conjugacy class
\gamma^{*}
of
G^{*}
that does not transfer to
G
. In fact, if such an element
\gamma^{*}
is not a norm from
G_{r}
then
O_{\gamma^{*}}(\phi^{*})=0
by the definition of stable base change transfer; while if
\gamma^{*}=\mathcal{N}_{r}\delta
for some
\delta\in G_{r}
then
\mathcal{N}_{r}\delta
is not
G_{r}
-conjugate to any element of
G
(since otherwise
\gamma^{*}
would transfer to
G
), so that
O_{\gamma^{*}}(\phi^{*})=TO_{\delta\sigma}(\phi)=0
by the vanishing property of
\phi
. By the Weyl integration formula, this implies that
\mathrm{Tr}(\phi^{*}|\pi^{*})=0
for any tempered irreducible representation
\pi^{*}
of
G^{*}
that does not have a Langlands-Jacquet transfer to
G
. In fact, by the preliminary discussion above, any such representation
\pi^{*}
is a representation induced from a Levi subgroup
M^{*}\subset G^{*}
that does not transfer to
G
, and the character of such a representation has (its regular-semisimple) support consisting of regular semisimple elements
\gamma^{*}
that are conjugate to elements in
M^{*}
and hence do not transfer to
G(F)
. Here we use the observation in
[
Bad03
, p. 105]
that if
M^{*}
does not transfer to
G(F)
, then no element of
G^{*,\rm rs}(F)\cap M^{*}
can transfer. (Alternatively, one can use the argument at the end of the proof of Lemma
2.4.2
.)

Next we prove the implication in the reverse direction. Assume that
\mathrm{Tr}(\phi^{*}|\pi^{*})=0
for any representation
\pi^{*}
of
G^{*}
that does not have a Langlands-Jacquet transfer to
G
. Suppose
\delta\in G(F_{r})
is a
\sigma
-semisimple element such that
N_{r}\delta\in G(F_{r})
is not conjugate to any element in
G(F)
. There is a semisimple element
\gamma^{*}\in G^{*}(F)
that has the same characteristic polynomial as
N_{r}\delta
. Then the conjugacy class of
\gamma^{*}
does not transfer to
G(F)
. Note that
\gamma^{*}
is an elliptic element in an
F
-Levi subgroup
M^{*}
of
G^{*}
, and
M^{*}
does not transfer to an
F
-Levi subgroup in
G
(because elliptic tori transfer to any inner form of
M^{*}
, see for example
[
Kot86b
, §10]
).

Let
P^{*}
be an
F
-parabolic subgroup of
G^{*}
with
F
-Levi factor
M^{*}
.
Any irreducible tempered representation
\pi^{*}
of
M^{*}
is induced from a discrete series representation of a Levi subgroup
L^{*}
of
M^{*}
. By induction in stages we see that
i_{P^{*}(F)}^{G^{*}(F)}\pi^{*}
is tempered, and it is irreducible by a result of Bernstein-Zelevinsky. It does not have a Langlands-Jacquet transfer to
G(F)
: this is because
M^{*}
does not transfer to
G(F)
; use
[
Bad18
, Prop. 7.1(a)]
. Hence

\mathrm{Tr}((\phi^{*})^{P^{*}})|\pi^{*})=\mathrm{Tr}(\phi^{*}|i_{P^{*}(F)}^{G^{*}(F)}\pi^{*})=0

by assumption and the character descent formula. This implies that
O^{M^{*}}_{\gamma^{*}}((\phi^{*})^{P^{*}}))=0
by Kazhdan’s density theorem and hence
O_{\gamma^{*}}(\phi^{*})=0
by the descent formula for orbital integrals. Then we get

TO_{\delta\sigma}(\phi)=\pm O_{\gamma^{*}}(\phi^{*})=0

from which we conclude that
\phi
has the vanishing property.
∎

Definition 2.5.3
.

Let
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G_{r})
. A function
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G)
is called a
base change transfer of
\phi
if for any semisimple element
\gamma\in G
, we have
e(G_{\gamma})O_{\gamma}(f)=e(G_{\delta\sigma})TO_{\delta\sigma}(\phi)
if
\gamma
is
G_{r}
-conjugate to the naive norm
N_{r}\delta
of some
\sigma
-semisimple element
\delta\in G_{r}
, and
O_{\gamma}(f)=0
otherwise.

Lemma 2.5.4
.

If
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G_{r})
has the vanishing property, then it has a base change transfer
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G)
.

Proof.

Let
\phi^{*}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G^{*})
be a stable base change transfer of
\phi
. Let
\gamma^{*}\in G^{*}
be a semisimple element that is not stably conjugate to any element of
G
. If
\gamma^{*}
is not the norm of any element in
G_{r}
, then by definition
O_{\gamma^{*}}(\phi^{*})=0
. If
\gamma^{*}=\mathcal{N}_{r}\delta
for some
\delta\in G_{r}
, then by the vanishing property we have
O_{\gamma^{*}}(\phi^{*})=\pm TO_{\delta\sigma}(\phi)=0
. Thus
\phi^{*}
has a Jacquet-Langlands transfer
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G)
by
[
DKV84
]
. Then
f
is a base change transfer of
\phi
by definition.
∎

Proposition 2.5.5
.

If
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G_{r})
has the vanishing property
(
in the sense of Definition
2.1.5
)
, then so does
Z*\phi
for any
Z\in\mathfrak{Z}^{\mathrm{st}}_{G_{r}}
.

Proof.

Let
\phi^{*}
be the stable base change transfer of
\phi
and let
(Z*\phi)^{*}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G(F))
be the stable base change transfer of
Z*\phi
. By Lemma
2.5.2
, it suffices to show that

\mathrm{Tr}((Z*\phi)^{*}|\pi^{*})=0

for any irreducible tempered representation
\pi^{*}
of
G^{*}
that does not have a Jacquet-Langlands transfer to
G
.

Fix such a
\pi^{*}
. Let
\Pi^{*}
be the base change of
\pi^{*}
to
G_{r}^{*}
as in
[
AC89
, Theorem 6.2]
. If
\Pi^{*}
does not have a Langlands-Jacquet transfer to
G_{r}
, then we are done by Theorem
2.4.1
.

Now suppose that
\Pi^{*}
has a Langlands-Jacquet transfer
\Pi
, which is an irreducible tempered representation of
G_{r}
. By part (1) of Theorem
2.4.1
we know that
\Pi
is
\sigma
-stable and we choose the intertwining operator
I_{\sigma}
as in
loc. cit
so that

\mathrm{Tr}((Z*\phi)^{*}|\pi^{*})=\mathrm{Tr}((Z*\phi)I_{\sigma}|\Pi).

This vanishes since the right hand side is a scalar multiple of

\mathrm{Tr}(\phi I_{\sigma}|\Pi)=e(G_{r})\mathrm{Tr}(\phi^{*}|\pi^{*})=0.

Here the first equality follows from Theorem
2.4.1
and the second equality follows from Lemma
2.5.2
.
∎

∎

Theorem 2.5.7
.

If

4.
Simple Shimura varieties of Kottwitz type

In this section we prove our main global result (Theorem

) on the description of the cohomology of the simple Shimura varieties studied by Kottwitz in
[

]
. Our result generalizes the main result from

4.1.
Basic setup

First we recall the basic setting from
[
Kot92a
]
, cf. also
[
Hai05
, §5.2]

4.1.1.
Shimura data

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a CM field with totally real subfield
\mathbb{F}_{0}
. Denote by
c\in\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{F}/\mathbb{F}_{0})
the complex conjugation. Let
\mathbb{D}
be a central division algebra of dimention

and

\mathbb{D}

\mathbb{G}
be the
\mathbb{Q}
-group whose points in any commutative

\mathbb{G}(R)=\{x\in\mathbb{D}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}R|xx^{*}\in R^{\times}\}

The map

whose kernel
\mathbb{G}_{0}
is an inner form of (the restriction of scalars of) a unitary group associated to the quadratic extension
\mathbb{F}/\mathbb{F}_{0}

Let

. Assume moreover that

\mathbb{D}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}

be the

of the restriction of

to
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and let

in
\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{R})
, where

is the centralizer of

in
\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{R})

\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}\cong\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}
where the first (resp. second) copy of
\mathbb{C}
on the right hand side corresponds to the identity (resp. complex conjugation). Restricting

to the first factor, we obtain a cocharacter
\mu:\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathbb{C}}\to\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{C}}

Then the pair
(\mathbb{G},X_{\infty})
is a Shimura datum. Let
\mathbb{E}
be its reflex field, i.e. the field of definition of the

Let

-module. Moreover, replacing

The cocharacter
\mu
induces a decomposition of

-modules

where

acts by

(resp.

) on

(resp.

-module

4.1.3.
Integral data

Fix a prime

-lattices

in

be the set of

-lattices in

The stabilizer

of

(resp.

4.2.
Integral models

We will define integral models of Shimura varieties with level
K=K^{p}K_{\mathcal{L}}
structure.

For any scheme
S
, let
AV_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{B}}}(S)
be the
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
-linear category in which:

•

The objects are pairs
(A,i)
where
A
is an abelian scheme over
S
and
i:\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{B}}\to\mathrm{End}(A)\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
-algebra homomorphism;

•

The morphisms between two objects
(A,i)
and
(A^{\prime},i^{\prime})
form the
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
-module
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{B}}}(A,A^{\prime})
consisting of elements in
\mathrm{Hom}(A,A^{\prime})\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
that commute with
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{B}}
.

For each object
(A,i)
in
AV_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{B}}}(S)
, let
A^{\vee}
be the dual abelian scheme and
i^{\vee}(b):=i(b^{\dagger})^{\vee}
for all
b\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{B}}
. Then
(A^{\vee},i^{\vee})
is also an object in
AV_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{B}}}(S)
. A
polarization
of
(A,i)
is an isomorphism
\lambda:A\to A^{\vee}
in
AV_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{B}}}(S)
such that
n\lambda
is induced by an ample line bundle on
A
for some positive integer
n
. In particular,
\lambda
induces the Rosati involution
\iota_{\lambda}
on
\mathrm{End}(A)\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
and
i(b^{\dagger})=i(b)^{\iota_{\lambda}}
for all
b\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{B}}
. Note that what we call a
polarization
is called
principal polarization
in
[
RZ96
, §6]
.

To define the moduli problem, recall from
[
RZ96
, Definition 6.5]
that an
\mathcal{L}
-set of abelian varieties
A_{\bullet}=\{A_{\Lambda},\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}\}
in
AV_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{B}}}(S)
is a functor from
\mathcal{L}
to
AV_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{B}}}(S)
satisfying a periodicity condition and a condition on the height of the isogenies among the
A_{\Lambda}
’s. For each
\mathcal{L}
-set
A_{\bullet}
, there is a dual
\mathcal{L}
-set
\tilde{A}_{\bullet}
and we have the notion of a polarization
\lambda:A_{\bullet}\to\tilde{A}_{\bullet}
, cf.
[
RZ96
, Definition 6.6]
.

We define the functor
\mathfrak{M}_{K_{\mathcal{L}}K^{p}}
on the category of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
-schemes that associates to a test scheme
S
the set of isomorphism classes of the following objects:

•

An
\mathcal{L}
-set of abelian varieties
A_{\bullet}=(A_{\bullet},i_{\bullet})
in
AV_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{B}}}(S)
;

•

A polarization
\lambda
of
A_{\bullet}
;

•

A
K^{p}
-level structure on
A_{\bullet}
, i.e. a
K^{p}
-orbit of isomorphisms of skew-Hermitian
\mathbb{B}
-modules

\bar{\eta}:H_{1}(A_{\bullet},\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})\cong\mathbb{V}\otimes\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p}\mod K^{p}

that respect the bilinear forms on both sides up to a constant in
(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})^{\times}
. More precisely, we consider such isomorphisms
\bar{\eta}:H_{1}(A_{\bullet,s},\mathbb{A}^{p}_{f})\cong V_{\mathbb{A}^{p}_{f}}
for every geometric point
s
of
S
, and we require that
\bar{\eta}
is fixed by
\pi_{1}(S,s)
(see
[
Kot92b
, p. 390]
).

Moreover we require
A_{\bullet}
to satisfy the usual Kottwitz determinant condition, i.e., an equality of polynomial functions

\det(b;\mathrm{Lie}A_{\Lambda})=\det(b;\mathbb{V}_{1})

for all
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}
and all
b\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{B}}
.

Proposition 4.2.1
.

The functor
\mathfrak{M}_{K_{\mathcal{L}}K^{p}}
is representable by a projective scheme over
\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
whose generic fiber is isomorphic to a disjoint union of
|\ker^{1}(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{G})|
copies of
\mathrm{Sh}_{K_{\mathcal{L}}K^{p}}
.

Proof.

Each lattice
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}
determines a maximal parahoric subgroup
K_{\Lambda}
whose corresponding moduli functor
\mathfrak{M}_{K_{\Lambda}K^{p}}
parametrizes a single object in
AV_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{B}}}(S)
with polarization and level
K^{p}
-structure, satisfying the determinant condition. By
[
Sch13b
, Theorem 5.2]
,
\mathfrak{M}_{K_{\Lambda}K^{p}}
is representable by a projective scheme
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
. Note that
\mathfrak{M}_{K_{\mathcal{L}}K^{p}}
embeds as a closed subscheme of a product of finitely many
\mathfrak{M}_{K_{\Lambda}K^{p}}
’s, hence is also a projective scheme over
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
. The description of the generic fibre of
\mathfrak{M}_{K_{\mathcal{L}}K^{p}}
follows from
[
Kot92b
, §8]
.
∎

4.3.
Description of cohomology

Let
\xi
be an irreducible finite dimensional algebraic representation of
\mathbb{G}
that is defined over a number field
L
. Fix a place
\lambda
of
L
above a rational prime
\ell\neq p
, then by the construction in
[
Kot92b
, §6]
we obtain
\ell
-adic local systems
\mathcal{F}_{\xi,K}
(resp.,
\mathcal{F}_{\xi,K_{\mathcal{L}}K^{p}}
) on
\mathrm{Sh}_{K}
(resp., the integral model
\mathfrak{M}_{K_{\mathcal{L}}K^{p}}
) together with Hecke correspondences among them. Taking cohomology in degree
i\geq 0
, we get
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{E})\times\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
-representations

H_{\xi}^{i}:=\varinjlim_{K}H_{\acute{e}t}^{i}(\mathrm{Sh}_{K}\otimes_{\mathbb{E}}\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathcal{F}_{\xi,K})

where
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
is the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{Q}
in
\mathbb{C}
. Here
K\subset\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
ranges over all neat compact open subgroups of the form
K=K_{p}K^{p}
, where
K_{p}\subset K_{\mathcal{L}}
is a normal compact open subgroup. We also form the virtual
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{E})\times\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
-representation

H_{\xi}^{*}:=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}H_{\xi}^{i}

where the alternating sum is taken in the corresponding Grothendieck group. Then we have a decomposition

H_{\xi}^{*}=\bigoplus_{\pi_{f}}\pi_{f}\otimes\sigma_{\xi}(\pi_{f})

where
\pi_{f}
ranges over irreducible admissible representations of
\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
and
\sigma_{\xi}(\pi_{f})
is a virtual finite dimensional representation of
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{E})
. Now we can state our global main result, which gives a nearly complete description of the restriction of
\sigma_{\xi}(\pi_{f})
to the local Weil group at
\mathfrak{p}
.

Theorem 4.3.1
.

For any self-dual
\mathcal{O}_{B}
-lattice chain
\mathcal{L}
in
V
with associated parahoric subgroup
K_{\mathcal{L}}\subset\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
as in §
4.1.3
, we have an equality in the Grothendieck group of
\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})\times K_{\mathcal{L}}\times W_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
representations

H^{*}_{\xi}=\sum_{\pi_{f}}a(\pi_{f})\pi_{f}\otimes(r_{-\mu}\circ\varphi_{\pi_{p}}|_{W_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}}})|\cdot|_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{-\dim\mathrm{Sh}/2}

where
a(\pi_{f})\in\mathbb{Z}
is defined in
[
Kot92a
, p.657]
.

Remark 4.3.2
.

As remarked in
[
SS13
, §5]
, we expect this to be an equality in the Grothendieck group of
\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{A}_{f})\times W_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
-representations. Under some extra assumptions the expectation is true. We refer to
[
SS13
, Theorem 5.2, Corollary 5.3]
for details.

The proof of Theorem
4.3.1
will be finished in §
4.5
, as a consequence of the local results Theorem
3.4.3
and Theorem
3.4.4
.

First we record its implications for the semisimple local
L
-factors of the Shimura varieties
\mathrm{Sh}_{K}
at
\mathfrak{p}
. We introduce more notations, following
[
Hai14
, §6.3]
. Let
r_{\mathfrak{p}}:\hat{G}\rtimes W_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\to\mathrm{GL}(V_{r_{\mathfrak{p}}})
be the algebraic representation defined by

r_{\mathfrak{p}}:=\mathrm{Ind}^{\hat{G}\rtimes W_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}_{\hat{G}\rtimes W_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}r_{-\mu}

where
r_{-\mu}:\hat{G}\rtimes W_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}}\to\mathrm{GL}(V_{-\mu})
is the representation defined in §
3.4
. For an irreducible smooth representation
\pi_{p}
of
\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, let
\varphi_{\pi_{p}}:W_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\to\hat{G}\rtimes W_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
be its
semisimple
L
-parameter
. Then the semisimple local
L
-factor of
\pi_{p}
with respect to the representation
r_{\mathfrak{p}}
is defined by

L^{ss}(s,\pi_{p},r_{\mathfrak{p}})=\det(\mathrm{Id}-p^{-s}r_{\mathfrak{p}}(\Phi_{p});V_{r_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{I_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}})

where
I_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\subset W_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is the inertia subgroup and
\Phi_{p}\in W_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is a geometric Frobenius element.

Corollary 4.3.3
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
be any sufficiently small compact open subgroup which factorizes in the form
K=K_{p}K^{p}
specified above. Then the semisimple local Hasse-Weil factor of
\mathrm{Sh}_{K}
at the place
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathbb{E}
is given by

\zeta_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathrm{ss}}(\mathrm{Sh}_{K},s)=\prod_{\pi_{f}}L^{\mathrm{ss}}(s-\frac{\dim\mathrm{Sh}_{K}}{2},\pi_{p},r_{\mathfrak{p}})^{a(\pi_{f})\dim\pi_{f}^{K}}.

Proof.

We denote
E=\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
d:=\dim\mathrm{Sh}_{K}
. Let
E_{0}
be the maximal unramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
in
E
. Write
K=K_{p}K^{p}
where
K^{p}\subset\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})
and
K_{p}
is contained in a parahoric subgroup
K_{\mathcal{L}}
in
\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
associated to some
\mathcal{O}_{B}
-stable lattice chain
\mathcal{L}
in
V
. Recall from
[
Hai05
, Definition 9.4]
that

\log\zeta_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathrm{ss}}(\mathrm{Sh}_{K},s)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{Tr}^{ss}(\Phi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{j},H^{*}(\mathrm{Sh}_{K,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}))\frac{N\mathfrak{p}^{-js}}{j}

where the semisimple trace
\mathrm{Tr}^{ss}
is (by definition) the trace of
\Phi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{j}
on the
I_{E}
-invariant subspace of the
semisimplification
of the corresponding
W_{E}
-representations.

We take
\xi
to be the trivial representation and apply Theorem
4.3.1
. Then for any
\tau\in\Phi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{j}I_{E}\subset W_{E}
, where
\mathrm{\Phi}_{\mathfrak{p}}\in W_{E}
is a geometric Frobenius element, we get that

\mathrm{Tr}(\tau,H^{*}(\mathrm{Sh}_{K,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}))=\sum_{\pi_{f}}a(\pi_{f})\dim(\pi_{f}^{K})\mathrm{Tr}(r_{-\mu}\circ\varphi_{\pi_{p}}(\tau))(N\mathfrak{p})^{-jd/2}.

Integrating both sides over the coset
\tau\in\Phi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{j}I_{E}
(against the Haar measure on
I_{E}
with total volume
1
) and taking the sum over
j
we get

(4.1)

\log\zeta_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathrm{ss}}(\mathrm{Sh}_{K},s-\frac{d}{2})=\sum_{\pi_{f}}a(\pi_{f})\dim(\pi_{f}^{K})\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{Tr}^{ss}(\varphi_{\pi_{p}}(\Phi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{j}),r_{-\mu})\frac{(N\mathfrak{p})^{-js}}{j}.

The semisimple trace on the right hand side of (
4.1
) coincides with the trace on the inertia invariant subspace since
\varphi_{\pi_{p}}
is the
semisimple

L
-parameter so that the representation
r_{-\mu}\circ\varphi_{\pi_{p}}|_{W_{E}}
is such that inertia acts through a finite quotient. In other words we have

\mathrm{Tr}^{ss}(\varphi_{\pi_{p}}(\Phi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{j}),r_{-\mu})=\mathrm{Tr}(\varphi_{\pi_{p}}(\Phi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{j})|r_{-\mu}^{I_{E}}).

On the other hand, recall from
[
Hai05
, Definition 9.8]
that

\log L^{\mathrm{ss}}(s,\pi_{p},r_{\mathfrak{p}})=\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{Tr}^{ss}(\varphi_{\pi_{p}}(\Phi_{p}^{r});V_{r_{\mathfrak{p}}})\frac{p^{-rs}}{r}.

By
[
Hai14
, Lemma 6.3.1]
we have

\mathrm{Tr}^{ss}(\varphi_{\pi_{p}}(\Phi_{p}^{r});V_{r_{\mathfrak{p}}})=\begin{cases}[E_{0}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]\mathrm{Tr}^{ss}(\varphi_{\pi_{p}}(\Phi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{j}),r_{-\mu}^{E_{0}})&\text{ if }r=j[E_{0}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]\\
0&\text{ if }[E_{0}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]\nmid r\end{cases}

where
r_{-\mu}^{E_{0}}:=\mathrm{Ind}_{\hat{G}\rtimes W_{E}}^{\hat{G}\rtimes W_{E_{0}}}r_{-\mu}
.
Since the extension
E/E_{0}
is totally ramified, we have

I_{E_{0}}/I_{E}=\frac{\hat{G}\rtimes W_{E_{0}}}{\hat{G}\rtimes W_{E}}

from which we deduce that
(r_{-\mu}^{E_{0}})^{I_{E_{0}}}=r_{-\mu}^{I_{E}}
and hence

\mathrm{Tr}^{ss}(\varphi_{\pi_{p}}(\Phi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{j}),r_{-\mu}^{E_{0}})=\mathrm{Tr}(\varphi_{\pi_{p}}(\Phi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{j}),(r_{-\mu}^{E_{0}})^{I_{E_{0}}})=\mathrm{Tr}(\varphi_{\pi_{p}}(\Phi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{j}),r_{-\mu}^{I_{E}})=\mathrm{Tr}^{ss}(\varphi_{\pi_{p}}(\Phi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{j}),r_{-\mu}).

Therefore we get

(4.2)

\log L^{\mathrm{ss}}(s,\pi_{p},r_{\mathfrak{p}})=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{Tr}^{ss}(\varphi_{\pi_{p}}(\Phi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{j}),r_{-\mu}^{E_{0}})\frac{p^{-j[E_{0}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]s}}{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{Tr}^{ss}(\varphi_{\pi_{p}}(\Phi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{j}),r_{-\mu})\frac{(N\mathfrak{p})^{-js}}{j}.

Now the result follows by comparing (
4.1
) and (
4.2
).
∎

4.4.
Counting points

Let
E=\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Fix an integer
j\geq 1
and an element
\tau\in\mathrm{Frob}_{E}^{j}I_{E}\subset W_{E}
. Denote
r:=j[\kappa_{E}:\mathbb{F}_{p}]
where
\kappa_{E}
is the residue field of
E
and set
k_{r}:=\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}
, which is a degree
j
extension of
\kappa_{E}
. Let
h\in C_{c}^{\infty}(K_{\mathcal{L}})
and
f^{p}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p}))
. Our goal is to calculate
\mathrm{Tr}(\tau\times hf^{p}|H_{\xi}^{*})
by the Grothendieck-Lefschetz trace formula.
We mostly follow the exposition of
[
Sch13b
]
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
f^{p}
is the characteristic function of a double coset
K^{p}g^{p}K^{p}
divided by the volume of
K^{p}
for a neat compact open subgroup
K^{p}\subset\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})
, and
h
is the characteristic function of
K_{p}g_{p}K_{p}
divided by the volume of
K_{p}
for some normal open subgroup
K_{p}\subset K_{\mathcal{L}}
and
g_{p}\in K_{\mathcal{L}}
.

4.4.1.
Frobenius-Hecke correspondence

We will analyze the cohomological correspondence on
\mathrm{Sh}_{K_{p}K^{p}}
that gives rise to the action of
\tau\times hf^{p}
on
H^{*}_{\xi}
. The first step is to consider the induced correspondence on the “base level”
\mathrm{Sh}_{K_{\mathcal{L}}K^{p}}
, which extends to the integral model
\mathfrak{M}_{K_{\mathcal{L}}K^{p}}
, and describe its fixed points in the special fiber.
More precisely the action of
hf^{p}
is defined by a correspondence

(4.3)

\mathfrak{M}_{K_{\mathcal{L}}K^{p}}\xleftarrow{p_{1}}\mathfrak{M}_{K_{\mathcal{L}}K^{p}_{g^{p}}}\xrightarrow{p_{2}}\mathfrak{M}_{K_{\mathcal{L}}K^{p}}

where

•

K^{p}_{g^{p}}:=K^{p}\cap g^{p}K^{p}(g^{p})^{-1}
;

•

p_{1}
is the composition of the natural projection
\mathfrak{M}_{K_{\mathcal{L}}K^{p}_{g^{p}}}\to\mathfrak{M}_{K_{\mathcal{L}},g^{p}K^{p}(g^{p})^{-1}}
with the isomorphism
\mathfrak{M}_{K_{\mathcal{L}},g^{p}K^{p}(g^{p})^{-1}}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathfrak{M}_{K_{\mathcal{L}}K^{p}}
induced by (“right multiplication by”)
g^{p}
;

•

p_{2}
is the natural projection.

Our convention is slightly different from both
[
Sch13b
]
and
[
Kot92b
]
.

4.4.2.
Fixed points of correspondence

Let
\mathrm{Fix}_{j,\mathcal{L}}(g^{p})
denote the set of fixed points of
\tau\times hf^{p}
in
\mathfrak{M}_{K_{\mathcal{L}}K^{p}_{g^{p}}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
. The notation is meant to suggest that it is independent of
g_{p}\in K_{\mathcal{L}}
and the choice of
\tau
in the coset
\mathrm{Frob}_{E}^{j}I_{E}
. To describe it we consider the following correspondence of the special fiber

(4.4)

\mathfrak{M}_{K_{\mathcal{L}}K^{p}}\otimes\kappa_{E}\xleftarrow{\mathrm{Fr}_{E}^{j}\circ p_{1}}\mathfrak{M}_{K_{\mathcal{L}}K^{p}_{g^{p}}}\otimes\kappa_{E}\xrightarrow{p_{2}}\mathfrak{M}_{K_{\mathcal{L}}K^{p}}\otimes\kappa_{E}

where
p_{1},p_{2}
are the special fibers of the corresponding maps in (
) and

\mathrm{Fr}_{E}
denotes the geometric Frobenius endomorphism of
\mathfrak{M}_{K_{\mathcal{L}}K^{p}}\otimes\kappa_{E}
. Then
\mathrm{Fix}_{j,\mathcal{L}}(g^{p})
is the fixed point set of the above correspondence, which consists of points
(A_{\bullet},\lambda,\bar{\eta})
in
\mathfrak{M}_{K_{\mathcal{L}}K^{p}_{g^{p}}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
such that
(A_{\bullet},\lambda,\bar{\eta})
and
\sigma^{r}(A_{\bullet},\lambda,\overline{\eta g^{p}})
define the same point in
\mathfrak{M}_{K_{\mathcal{L}}K^{p}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
. Here
\sigma^{r}(\cdot)
means base change along
p^{r}
-th power map of
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.
Then for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, there exists an isomorphism
u_{i}:\sigma^{r}(A_{i})\to A_{i}
in
AV_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{B}}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
sending
\sigma^{r}(\overline{\eta g^{p}})
to
\bar{\eta}
and compatible with the isogenies among the
A_{i}
’s. Moreover, there is a number
c_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}^{\times}\cap\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
u_{0}^{*}\lambda_{0}=c_{0}\sigma(\lambda_{0})
. Then
(A_{0},u_{0},\lambda_{0})
is a
p^{r}c_{0}
-polarized virtual
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{B}}
-abelian variety over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}
up to
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
-isogeny in the sense of
[
Kot92b
]
. The inverse of the Frobenius endomorphisms on
A_{j}
define a conjugacy class
\gamma=(\gamma_{l})_{l\neq p}\in\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})
and a

a(\pi_{f})=\sum_{\pi_{\infty}}m(\pi_{f}\otimes\pi_{\infty})\,\mathrm{tr}\,\pi_{\infty}(f^{\mathbb{G}}_{\xi,\infty}).

The third equality uses the definition of the element

in the stable Bernstein center of

4.6.

In this section we record some results of independent interest. We express the Lefschetz number in (

As usual, we fix an integer

\mathrm{Tr}(\tau\times hf^{p}|H_{\xi}^{*})=\sum_{(\gamma_{0};\gamma,\delta)}c(\gamma_{0};\gamma,\delta)\,O_{\gamma}(f^{p})\,TO_{\delta\sigma}(z_{\tau,-\mu}^{(r)}*\tilde{h})\,\mathrm{tr}\,\xi(\gamma_{0})

In

]
, for certain pairs of compact open subgroups

Let

over

We write the invariant of the local division algebra

5.1.3.
Unitary similitude groups

Above we consider the pair

where
\dagger

on
\mathbb{D}
(which is not necessarily positive).

For any invertible element

such that
\beta^{\dagger}=-\beta
we can associate the following objects:

•

An involution of the second kind

on
\mathbb{B}
defined by

b^{*_{\beta}}:=\beta b^{\dagger}\beta^{-1}\quad\forall b\in\mathbb{B}.

•

A non-degenerate alternating pairing

on

Then for all

(b_{1}x_{1}b_{2},x_{2})_{\beta}=(x_{1},b_{1}^{\dagger}x_{2}b_{2}^{*_{\beta}})_{\beta}
.

•

A
\mathbb{Q}
-algebraic group
\mathbb{G}_{\beta}
defined as the subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{B}}\mathbb{V}
preserving the form

up to a scalar in
\mathbb{Q}
. Since
\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{B}}(\mathbb{V})=\mathbb{D}
, for any
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra

\mathbb{G}_{\beta}(R)=\{g\in\mathbb{D}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}R\,|\,xx^{*_{\beta}}\in R^{\times}\}.

The map
g\mapsto gg^{*_{\beta}}
defines a homomorphism of
\mathbb{Q}
-algebraic groups
c:\mathbb{G}_{\beta}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
. Let
\mathbb{G}_{\beta,0}\subset\mathbb{G}_{\beta}
be its kernel. In other words, for any
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra

we have

\mathbb{G}_{\beta,0}(R)=\{g\in\mathbb{B}^{op}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}R\,|\,xx^{*_{\beta}}=1\}.

Let
\mathbb{G}_{\beta}^{\mathrm{ad}}
be the adjoint group of
\mathbb{G}_{\beta,0}
(and also
\mathbb{G}_{\beta}
). Sometimes we also view
\mathbb{G}_{\beta,0}
and
\mathbb{G}_{\beta}^{\mathrm{ad}}
as algebraic groups defined over
\mathbb{F}_{0}
.

Lemma 5.1.1
.

There exists
0\neq\beta\in\mathbb{B}^{\dagger=-1}
such that

•

At any finite prime that is non-split in
\mathbb{K}
,
\mathbb{G}_{\beta}
and
\mathbb{G}_{\beta,0}
are quasi-split;

•

For any infinite place
\tau:\mathbb{F}_{0}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the pairing
(\cdot\,,\cdot\,)_{\beta}
on

We follow the proof of
[
HT01
, Lemma I.7.1]
. First pick any
0\neq\beta_{0}\in\mathbb{B}^{\dagger=-1}
. In this proof, we view the associated groups
\mathbb{G}_{\beta_{0},0}
and
\mathbb{G}_{\beta_{0}}^{\mathrm{ad}}
as algebraic groups over
\mathbb{F}_{0}
. We aim to twist all the objects defined by
\beta_{0}
by a cohomology class in

with

and the twisted objects will be associated to

. In particular,
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha\beta_{0},0}
is the inner form of
\mathbb{G}_{\beta_{0},0}
classified by
[\alpha]\in H^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{0},\mathbb{G}_{\beta_{0}}^{\mathrm{ad}})

When

is odd, the natural map of pointed sets

\mathbb{F}_{0}

\beta
with prescribed local conditions.

From now on we assume that

is even. Then there is an exact sequence of pointed sets:

(5.2)

H^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{0},\mathbb{G}_{\beta_{0}}^{\mathrm{ad}})\to\bigoplus_{x}H^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{0,x},\mathbb{G}_{\beta_{0}}^{\mathrm{ad}})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Obs}}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\to 1

If
x
is a finite place of
\mathbb{F}_{0}
that splits into two different places

in
\mathbb{F}
, then

.

If
x
is a finite place of
\mathbb{F}_{0}

\mathbb{F}

.

If
x

\mathbb{F}_{0}

with

mod 2, where

\mathbb{G}_{\beta_{0}}
at

For any place
x

\mathbb{F}_{0}

5.2.1.
The Langlands dual group

Let

to
\mathbb{K}

\mathbb{Q}

\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}\cong\mathbb{B}^{{\rm op},\times}\times\mathrm{Res}_{\mathbb{K}/\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{G}_{m}

Using this isomorphism, the automorphism

\theta(g,\lambda):=(\lambda^{c}g^{-*},\lambda^{c})

The Langlands dual group

\Phi

\mathbb{G}^{\vee}=\prod_{\Phi}\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\times\mathbb{C}^{\times}

Similarly, the Langlands dual group of

{}^{L}\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}=\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}^{\vee}\rtimes\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})

\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})

\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{K}/\mathbb{Q})
with the complex conjugation acting by

Define an
L

w_{n}{}^{t}g_{c\varphi}^{-1}w_{n}^{-1}\quad&\text{ if }\varphi\notin\Phi.\end{cases}

to

Lemma 5.2.1
.

With notations as above, we have

Any

Let

\omega_{\pi,y}=\omega_{\pi_{y}}\omega_{\pi,y^{c}}

∎

We define the local base change of

where

Let

\mathbb{F}
above

Proof.

This is basically Theorem VI.2.1 of
[
HT01
]
, which improves upon Theorem A.5.2 of
[
Lab99
]
. We briefly review the argument here.

First one reduces to base change for the unitary group
\mathbb{G}_{1}
instead of the unitary similitude group
\mathbb{G}
. More precisely, one shows that there exists an automorphic representation
\pi^{\prime}
of
\mathbb{G}_{1}(\mathbb{A})
such that

•

x
of
\mathbb{Q}

\mathbb{K}

5.3.

The following result establishes the existence of Galois representations (satisfying suitable local-global compatibility conditions) associated to cohomological automorphic representations of
\mathbb{G}
.

.

\pi
be an irreducible automorphic representation of
\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{A})

\pi_{\infty}

\xi

\mathbb{F}

\mathbb{F}_{0}

, there is a place

of

above

Using Theorem

.

Let us explain the arguments in more detail. Let
\sigma_{\ell}(\pi)
be the
n
-dimensional representation of

from Theorem

. Under the Shapiro isomorphism,

corresponds to a (conjugacy class of) homomorphism(s):

\varphi_{\ell}(\pi):\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{K})\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})^{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathbb{F},\overline{\mathbb{Q}})}\rtimes\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{K})=\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})^{\Phi}\rtimes\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{K}).

Let
\omega_{\pi}
be the central character of
\pi
and let

be as in Theorem

. Then
\omega

be the associated (one-dimensional)
\ell
-adic Galois representation. Combining the restriction of

and

to

we get a homomorphism

By construction, for any finite prime
\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}
of
\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}
with residue characteristic

\tilde{\varphi}_{\ell}(\pi)|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}_{\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}}}\cong\varphi_{\pi_{q}}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}_{\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}}}

Consider the virtual representation

of
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\widetilde{\mathbb{E}})

For any prime

∎

5.4.
Fixed points via generalized Kottwitz triples

We would like to proceed from Corollary
4.4.2
and find an expression of the right hand side in terms of a trace formula, without knowing the vanishing property of the test function
\phi_{\tau,h}
. For this purpose, we will associate to each fixed point of the Frobenius-Hecke correspondence a substitute of the Kottwitz triple defined using an auxiliary group
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}
that is constructed in a similar way to
\mathbb{G}
.

5.4.1.
An auxiliary group

Let
\mathbb{B}^{\prime}
be the division algebra over
\mathbb{F}
that is split at
w,w^{c}
and isomorphic to
\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{D}^{\mathrm{op}}
at all other finite places of
\mathbb{F}
. Choose a
positive
involution
\dagger^{\prime}
of the second kind on
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}
. Analogously to the construction of
\mathbb{G}
in Lemma
5.1.1
, we can find an element
0\neq\beta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}^{\prime}
that, together with the positive involution
\dagger^{\prime}
, gives rise to an involution
*^{\prime}
, a
\dagger^{\prime}
-Hermitian pairing on
\mathbb{V}^{\prime}=\mathbb{B}^{\prime}
, and a group
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}
such that

•

\mathbb{G}^{\prime}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\cong\mathrm{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathbb{G}_{m}
is the quasi-split inner form of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
;

•

For any finite prime
\ell\neq p
, we have
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}\cong\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}
;

Note that we do not put any condition at the archimedean places.

5.4.2.
Generalized Kottwitz triples

Definition 5.4.1
.

Let
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and set
r:=j[\kappa_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}}:\mathbb{F}_{p}]
. A degree
j

generalized Kottwitz triple

(\gamma_{0}^{\prime};\gamma,\delta)
consists of

•

A semisimple stable conjugacy class
\gamma_{0}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{G}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})
that is elliptic in
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
,

•

a conjugacy class
\gamma\in\mathbb{G}^{\prime}(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})=\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})
that is stably conjugate to
\gamma_{0}^{\prime}
,

•

a
\sigma
-conjugacy class
\delta\in\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}})
whose naive norm
N_{r}\delta=\delta\sigma(\delta)\dotsm\sigma^{r-1}(\delta)
is stably conjugate to
\gamma_{0}^{\prime}
, such that
\kappa_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}(p\delta)=-\mu^{\#}
in
X^{*}(Z(\hat{\mathbb{G}})^{\Gamma(p)})
, where
\Gamma(p):=\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.

Remark 5.4.2
.

Recall that
\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}})\cong(\mathbb{D}_{w}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}})^{\times}\times\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}}^{\times}
. We call the image of
N_{r}\delta
in
\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}}^{\times}
its similitude factor. On the other hand, the first factor of
N_{r}\delta
has a characteristic polynomial which is a monic polynomial of degree
(\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\mathbb{D})^{\frac{1}{2}}
with coefficients in
F\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}}
(recall that
\mathbb{D}_{w}
has center
\mathbb{F}_{w}\cong F
). We refer to this simply as the characteristic polynomial of
N_{r}\delta
. Similarly, for any element in
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\cong\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)\times\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}
we have its characteristic polynomial (defined by the first factor) and its similitude factor. In the definition of generalized Kottwitz triples, the condition that
N_{r}\delta\in\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}})
is stably conjugate to
\gamma_{0}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{G}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
simply means that they have the same characteristic polynomial and similitude factor.

Let
x=(A_{\bullet},\lambda,\bar{\eta})\in\mathrm{Fix}_{j,\mathcal{L}}(g^{p})
be a fixed point in the closure of the generic fiber as in §
4.4.2
. Then we have associated to
x
a polarized virtual
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{B}}
-abelian variety
(A,u,\lambda)
over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}
, giving rise to a
conjugacy class
\gamma\in\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})=\mathbb{G}^{\prime}(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})
and a
\sigma
-conjugacy class
\delta\in\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}})
.

Lemma 5.4.3
.

There exists a unique semi-simple stable conjugacy class
\gamma_{0}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{G}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})
such that
(\gamma_{0}^{\prime};\gamma,\delta)
forms a degree
j
generalized Kottwitz triple.

Proof.

The arguments in
[
Kot92b
, §14]
go through in our situation with slight modifications.

First we construct a candidate
\gamma_{0}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{G}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})
. Starting with a maximal semisimple subalgebra
N
of
\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{B}}(A)
containing the Frobenius endomorphism
\pi_{A}
, we seek an algebra embedding from
N
into
C^{\prime}:=\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{B}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{V}^{\prime})=(\mathbb{B}^{\prime})^{\mathrm{op}}
compatible with the Rosati involution
*_{\lambda}
on
N
and the involution
*^{\prime}
on
C^{\prime}
, and take
\gamma_{0}^{\prime}
to be the image of
\pi_{A}^{-1}
under this embedding. To show the existence of such an embedding, one replaces the algebra
C
in
loc.cit.
by our
C^{\prime}
everywhere in pages 420-422. This works in our setting because
\mathbb{B}^{\prime}
and hence
C^{\prime}
splits at any place of
\mathbb{F}
above
p
.

Next we check that
(\gamma_{0}^{\prime};\gamma,\delta)
forms a generalized Kottwitz triple. For each
\ell\neq p
, over the algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
we have an isomorphism
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})\cong\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})^{\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{F}_{0},\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})}\times\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}^{\times}
. Under this isomorphism, each
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
factor of
\gamma_{\ell}
and
\gamma_{0}^{\prime}
have the same characteristic polynomial, which is the image of the characteristic polynomial of
\pi_{A}^{-1}
in the semisimple
\mathbb{F}
-algebra
N
under suitable embeddings of
\mathbb{F}
into
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
. Also they have the same similitude factor in
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}^{\times}
, which equals to
(\pi_{A}\pi_{A}^{*_{\lambda}})^{-1}
.
Hence
\gamma
and
\gamma_{0}^{\prime}
are stably conjugate in
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})=\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
.

At
p
, it is clear that the naive norm
N_{r}\delta\in\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}})
and
\gamma_{0}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{G}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
have the same similitude factor, which equals to
(\pi_{A}\pi_{A}^{*_{\lambda}})^{-1}
. Next we recall that there is an
\mathbb{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}}
-module
H_{r}
such that
H_{r}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}}}\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
is isomorphic to the covariant rational Dieudonné module of the
p
-divisible group
A[p^{\infty}]
. The element
\delta\in\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}})
is defined by choosing an isomorphism of
\mathbb{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}}
-modules
H_{r}\cong\mathbb{V}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}}
(which exists by the assumption that the fixed point
x=(A_{\bullet},\lambda,\bar{\eta})
lies in the closure of the generic fiber). Under the natural embedding
\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{B}}(A)\to\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{B}}(H_{r})\cong\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{B}}(\mathbb{V}\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}})
we have
N_{r}\delta=\pi_{A}^{-1}
. So the characteristic polynomials of
N_{r}\delta
and
\gamma_{0}^{\prime}
both equal to the characteristic polynomial of
\pi_{A}^{-1}
in the semisimple
\mathbb{F}
-algebra
N
. Hence
N_{r}\delta
and
\gamma_{0}^{\prime}
are stably conjugate.

Finally, the fact that
\kappa_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}(p\delta)=-\mu^{\#}
follows from the determinant condition for the fixed point
x=(A_{\bullet},\lambda,\bar{\eta})
.
∎

For each

(A,u,\lambda)
associated to a given triple
(\gamma_{0}^{\prime},\gamma,\delta)
is equal to
\ker[\ker^{1}(\mathbb{Q},I_{0})\to\ker^{1}(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{G}^{\prime})]
. Then the identity follows from Corollary
4.4.2
.
∎

Let
f_{\tau,h}^{*}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}))
be the stable base change transfer of
\phi_{\tau,h}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}_{\beta}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}}))
. Then for any conjugacy class
\gamma_{p}\in\mathbb{G}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\cong\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)\times\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}
which is the norm of
\delta\in\mathbb{G}_{\beta}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}})

SO_{\gamma_{p}}(f_{\tau,h}^{*})=O_{\gamma_{p}}(f_{\tau,h}^{*})=e(\delta)TO_{\delta\sigma}(\phi_{\tau,h})

where

is the Kottwitz sign; see §
4.5.3
.

Corollary 5.5.2
.

We have the following equality

\mathrm{Tr}(\tau\times hf^{p}|H_{\beta,\xi}^{*})=\sum_{\pi^{\prime}}m(\pi^{\prime})\mathrm{tr}\,\pi^{\prime}(f_{\tau,h}^{*}f^{p}f^{\mathbb{G}^{\prime}}_{\xi,\infty})

where the sum runs over automorphic representations
\pi^{\prime}
of
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}
and
m(\pi^{\prime})
denotes the automorphic multiplicity.

Proof.

This follows from Theorem
5.5.1
by the pseudo-stabilization process in §

.

Combining Corollary
5.3.3
and Corollary
5.5.2
, we will be able to deduce our main local theorems. We claim that for any irreducible generic representation
\pi_{p}^{\prime}
of
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
which is not transferred from
\mathbb{G}_{\beta}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
,
\mathrm{tr}\,\pi_{p}^{\prime}(f_{\tau,h}^{*})=0
. Since the
L^{2}
-Bernstein variety for
\mathbb{G}_{\beta}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is a union of connected components of the
L^{2}
-Bernstein variety for
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, the property of not being a transfer from
\mathbb{G}_{\beta}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is Zariski closed. Thus by
[
Shi12
, Proposition 3.1]
, to prove the claim we may assume that
\pi^{\prime}_{p}
is the local component of an automorphic representation
\pi^{\prime}
of
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}
such that
\mathrm{tr}\,\pi_{\infty}^{\prime}(f_{\xi,\infty}^{\mathbb{G}^{\prime}})\neq 0
. Choose
f^{p}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}^{\prime}(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p}))
such
that its trace on
(\pi^{\prime})^{p,\infty}
is nonzero and its trace on all other irreducible admissible representations of
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})\cong\mathbb{G}_{\beta}(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})
vanishes. Then by Corollary
5.5.2
,
\mathrm{Tr}(\tau\times hf^{p}|H^{*}_{\xi})
is a nonzero multiple of
\mathrm{tr}\,\pi_{p}^{\prime}(f_{\tau,h}^{*})
. If this is nonzero, then by Corollary
5.3.3
(with the same choice of
f^{p}
), there is an automorphic representation
\pi
of
\mathbb{G}_{\beta}
such that
\pi^{p,\infty}\cong(\pi^{\prime})^{p,\infty}
. But then by Theorem
5.2.2
and strong multiplicity one,
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
have the same transfer to an automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{n,\mathbb{F}}
and hence
\pi_{p}^{\prime}
is the Jacquet-Langlands transfer of
\pi_{p}
, contradicting our assumption at the beginning. This proves our claim and then Theorem
3.4.3
follows by Lemma
2.5.2
.

Next we consider an irreducible generic representation
\pi_{p}
of
\mathbb{G}_{\beta}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and let
\pi_{p}^{\prime}
be its Jacquet-Langlands transfer to
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. We claim that

\mathrm{tr}(z_{\tau,-\mu}*h|\pi_{p})=\mathrm{tr}(f_{\tau,h}^{*}|\pi_{p}^{\prime}).

We may assume that
\pi_{p}
is the local component of an automorphic representation
\pi
of
\mathbb{G}_{\beta}(\mathbb{A})
such that
\mathrm{tr}\,\pi_{\infty}(f_{\xi,\infty}^{\mathbb{G}})=1
. By Theorem
5.2.2
,
[
Bad08
, Theorem 5.1]
and
[
HT01
, Theorem VI.2.9]
,
\pi_{p}^{\prime}
is the local component of an automorphic representation
\pi^{\prime}
of
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}
such that
\mathrm{tr}\,\pi^{\prime}_{\infty}(f_{\xi,\infty}^{\mathbb{G}^{\prime}})=1
and
\pi_{f}^{p}\cong(\pi^{\prime}_{f})^{p}
. We choose
f^{p}
that singles out
\pi_{f}^{p}
as before, then the claim follows from Corollary
5.3.3
and Corollary
5.5.2
. Combined with the previous claim we proved (that
\mathrm{tr}\,\pi_{p}^{\prime}(f_{\tau,h}^{*})=0
if
\pi_{p}^{\prime}
is not transferred from
\mathbb{G}_{\beta}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
), we see that
f_{\tau,h}^{*}
is a Jacquet-Langlands transfer of
z_{\tau,-\mu}*h
. For any semisimple
\gamma\in\mathbb{G}_{\beta}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=G(F)
let
\gamma^{*}\in G^{*}(F)
be a matching element. If
\gamma=\mathcal{N}_{r}\delta
and hence
\gamma^{*}=\mathcal{N}_{r}^{*}\delta
for some

e(G_{\gamma})O_{\gamma}(z_{\tau,\mu}*h)=O_{\gamma^{*}}(f^{*}_{\tau,h})=e(G_{\delta\sigma})TO_{\delta\sigma}(\phi_{\tau,h}).

If
\gamma

G(F_{r})

\gamma^{*}

G(F_{r})
and hence we have

e(G_{\gamma})O_{\gamma}(z_{\tau,\mu}*h)=O_{\gamma^{*}}(f^{*}_{\tau,h})=0.

This shows that
z_{\tau,\mu}*h
is a base change of
\phi_{\tau,h}
in the sense of Definition
2.1.2
, and hence this finishes the proof of Theorem
3.4.4
.

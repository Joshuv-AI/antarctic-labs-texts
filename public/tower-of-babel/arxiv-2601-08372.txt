Data-Driven Time-Limited h 2 h^{2} Optimal Model Reduction for Linear Discrete-Time Systems
Hiroki Sakamoto, Kazuhiro Sato

Abstract
This paper develops a data-driven h 2 h^{2} model reduction method for discrete-time linear time-invariant systems. Specifically, we solve the h 2 h^{2} model reduction problem defined over a finite horizon using only impulse response data. Furthermore, we show that the proposed data-driven algorithm converges to a stationary point under certain assumptions. Numerical experiments demonstrate that the proposed method constructs a good reduced-order model in terms of the h 2 h^{2} norm defined over the finite horizon using a SLICOT benchmark (the CD player model).



I
Introduction

In engineering systems, the behavior over a finite horizon can govern system performance. In such cases, the input–output behavior over the finite horizon is evaluated using time-limited Gramians and finite horizon norms
[
7
]
.
In particular, when dealing with large-scale and complex systems, model order reduction (MOR) techniques are required to construct reduced-order models (ROMs) while preserving the input–output behavior over the finite horizon.

In general, MOR aims to efficiently handle real-world large-scale systems by
(i) approximating the original system sufficiently well with respect to a certain metric (e.g., the
h^{2}
norm),
(ii) preserving important properties of the original system, and
(iii) constructing ROMs via efficient numerical algorithms
[
2
,
4
]
.
When focusing on finite-interval behavior, one defines the performance metric over the finite horizon and performs MOR accordingly.
Well-known model-based (i.e., system matrices available) approaches for time-limited systems include time-limited balanced truncation
[
13
,
18
]
and time-limited
h^{2}
(or
\mathcal{H}^{2}
) MOR
[
9
,
6
]
.
On the other hand, in practice, one often encounters situations where the system matrices are unknown and only measured data are available, or where system identification
[
14
,
12
]
is difficult; in such cases, a data-driven framework is required, as shown in Figure
1
.

Representative data-driven MOR methods for linear time-invariant (LTI) systems include the Loewner framework, which constructs interpolatory models from frequency-response data
[
15
,
1
]
,
data-driven balanced truncation
[
8
]
, and the eigensystem realization algorithm (ERA), which constructs a realization from impulse response data
[
11
,
10
]
.
In particular, when only impulse response data are available, ERA
[
11
,
10
]
is a standard choice, but it does not guarantee optimality in the sense of the time-limited
h^{2}
norm ((discrete-time counterpart of
\mathcal{H}^{2}
norm).

Fig. 1:

Overview of the proposed data-driven time-limited
h^{2}
model reduction framework.

In this work, we aim to construct a ROM directly from measured impulse response data without identifying the original system matrices, as shown in Figure
1
.
The contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

•

We show that the objective function and its gradient in the model-based time-limited
h^{2}
MOR problem can be represented using only impulse response data.

•

We develop a gradient-based algorithm with convergence guarantees using only measured data.

Section

Section

II
Model-based Time-Limited
h^{2}
MOR for LTI systems

II-A

Discrete-Time LTI systems over a finite horizon

Consider the discrete-time LTI system

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} x_{k+1}&=Ax_{k}+Bu_{k},\\
y_{k}&=Cx_{k},\end{aligned}

(1)

where
x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
u_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and
y_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
denote the state, input, and output at time
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, respectively, and the matrices
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
,
B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
, and
C\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times n}
are constant system matrices.
The impulse response (Markov parameters) and the transfer function of system (
1
) are given by

\displaystyle h[k]

\displaystyle:=CA^{k}B\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times m},

(2)

\displaystyle H(z)

\displaystyle:=C(zI-A)^{-1}B\in\mathbb{C}^{p\times m}.

(3)

Using the impulse response sequence
\{h[k]\}
and assuming
x_{0}=0
, the input–output relation of system (
1
) over a finite horizon can be written as the convolution

y_{k+1}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}h[j]\>u_{k-j},\qquad k=0,1,\dots,L-1.

(4)

Under the assumption that system (
1
) is asymptotically stable, i.e.,
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is stable in the discrete-time sense (
\rho(A)<1
), the
h^{2}
norm of system (
1
) is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\|H\|_{h^{2}}^{2}:=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|h[k]\|^{2}.

(5)

Following
[
9
,
16
]
, the time-limited
h^{2}_{L}
norm over the finite horizon
\{0,1,\dots,L-1\}
is defined as

\|H\|_{h^{2}_{L}}^{2}:=\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}\|h[k]\|^{2}.

(6)

Note that (
6
) is well-defined even if
A
is not stable.

Next, we consider a ROM of system (
1
):

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \hat{x}_{k+1}&=\hat{A}\hat{x}_{k}+\hat{B}u_{k},\\
\hat{y}_{k}&=\hat{C}\hat{x}_{k},\end{aligned}

(7)

with transfer function
\hat{H}(z):=\hat{C}(zI-\hat{A})^{-1}\hat{B}
,
where
\hat{x}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
,
\hat{y}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, and
r\ll n
.
For the two systems (
1
) and (
7
), the following input–output error bound holds
[
9
]
:

\displaystyle\|y-\hat{y}\|_{\ell_{L}^{\infty}}\leq\|H-\hat{H}\|_{h^{2}_{L}}\cdot\|u\|_{\ell_{L}^{2}}.

(8)

Inequality (
8
) implies that, for inputs with bounded energy over the horizon, a sufficiently small time-limited
\|H-\hat{H}\|_{h^{2}_{L}}
error guarantees that the output error
\|y-\hat{y}\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{L}}
becomes correspondingly small.

II-B

Time-Limited
h^{2}
MOR

Motivated by the input–output inequality (
8
), we formulate the time-limited
h^{2}
model reduction problem as follows:

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm minimize}\limits

\displaystyle\|H-\hat{H}\|_{h^{2}_{L}}^{2}

(9)

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm subject\ to}\limits

\displaystyle(\hat{A},\hat{B},\hat{C})\in\mathcal{E},

where
\mathcal{E}:=\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}\times\mathbb{R}^{r\times m}\times\mathbb{R}^{p\times r}
is the feasible set.
The objective function in Problem (
9
) is given by

\displaystyle\|H-\hat{H}\|_{h^{2}_{L}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}\|CA^{k}B-\hat{C}\hat{A}^{k}\hat{B}\|^{2}

(10)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}\|CA^{k}B\|^{2}+f_{L}(\hat{\theta}),

(11)

where
\hat{\theta}:=(\hat{A},\hat{B},\hat{C})
and

\displaystyle f_{L}(\hat{\theta})

\displaystyle:=\tr(\hat{C}P_{L}\hat{C}^{\top})-2\tr(CR_{L}\hat{C}^{\top})

(13)

\displaystyle=\tr(\hat{B}^{\top}Q_{L}\hat{B})+2\tr(B^{\top}S_{L}\hat{B}),

(14)

and

\displaystyle P_{L}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}\hat{A}^{k}\hat{B}\hat{B}^{\top}(\hat{A}^{\top})^{k},

(15)

\displaystyle Q_{L}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}(\hat{A}^{\top})^{k}\hat{C}^{\top}\hat{C}\hat{A}^{k}

(16)

\displaystyle R_{L}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}A^{k}B\hat{B}^{\top}(\hat{A}^{\top})^{k},

(17)

\displaystyle S_{L}

\displaystyle:=-\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}(A^{\top})^{k}C^{\top}\hat{C}\hat{A}^{k}.

(18)

Therefore, Problem (
9
) can be equivalently rewritten as

\min_{\hat{\theta}\in\mathcal{E}}\ f_{L}(\hat{\theta}).

(19)

To solve Problem (
9
), we derive the gradients.
For the objective function
f_{L}(\hat{\theta})
, we denote the gradients with respect to
\hat{A}
,
\hat{B}
, and
\hat{C}
as
\nabla_{\hat{A}}f_{L}
,
\nabla_{\hat{B}}f_{L}
, and
\nabla_{\hat{C}}f_{L}
, respectively.
Here, we define the adjoint of the Fréchet derivative of the matrix-power map
\hat{A}\mapsto\hat{A}^{L}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{\ast}_{\hat{A},L}(X):=\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}(\hat{A}^{\top})^{j}\,X\,(\hat{A}^{\top})^{L-1-j}.

(20)

Proposition 1
.

The gradients of
f_{L}(\hat{\theta})
are

\displaystyle\nabla_{\hat{A}}f_{L}

\displaystyle=2(Q_{L}\,\hat{A}\,P+S_{L}^{\top}AR+\mathcal{T}^{\ast}_{\hat{A},L}(M))

(21)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\hat{B}}f_{L}

\displaystyle=2(Q_{L}\hat{B}+S_{L}^{\top}B)

(22)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\hat{C}}f_{L}

\displaystyle=2(\hat{C}P_{L}-CR_{L}),

(23)

where
P_{L}
,
Q_{L}
,
R_{L}
, and
S_{L}
are solutions of (
15
), (
16
), (
17
), and (
18
).

P
and
R
are obtained by solving the following discrete-time Lyapunov equation and Sylvester equation

\displaystyle\hat{A}P\hat{A}^{\top}+\hat{B}\hat{B}^{\top}

\displaystyle=P,

(25)

\displaystyle AR\hat{A}^{\top}+B\hat{B}^{\top}

\displaystyle=R.

(26)

Furthermore,

\displaystyle M:=R^{\top}(A^{\top})^{L}C^{\top}\hat{C}-P(\hat{A}^{\top})^{L}\hat{C}^{\top}\hat{C}.

(27)

Proof.

The proof follows
[
6
]
. See Appendix
A
.

III
Problem Formulation

The goal of this paper is to solve Problem (
19
) using only the impulse response sequence
\{h[k]\}
.
In particular, we assume that the length-
L
impulse response data generated from system (
1
) are available:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{L}:=\big\{\,h[0],\,h[1],\,\dots,\,h[L-1]\,\big\}.

(28)

Here, multiplying
R_{L}
and
S_{L}
from the left by
C
and
B^{\top}
, respectively, yields

\displaystyle CR_{L}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}CA^{k}B\hat{B}^{\top}(\hat{A}^{\top})^{k}

(29)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}h[k]\cdot\hat{B}^{\top}(\hat{A}^{\top})^{k}=:Z_{1},

(30)

\displaystyle B^{\top}S_{L}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}B^{\top}(A^{\top})^{k}C^{\top}\hat{C}\hat{A}^{k}

(31)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}h[k]^{\top}\cdot\hat{C}\hat{A}^{k}=:Z_{2}.

(32)

Consequently, the objective function
f_{L}(\hat{\theta})
can be rewritten equivalently using the impulse response data (
28
) as

\displaystyle f_{L,\text{data}}(\hat{\theta})

\displaystyle:=\tr(\hat{C}P_{L}\hat{C}^{\top})-2\tr(Z_{1}\hat{C}^{\top})

(33)

\displaystyle=\tr(\hat{B}^{\top}Q_{L}\hat{B})+2\tr(Z_{2}\hat{B}),

(34)

Therefore, Problem (
19
) is modified as

\min_{\hat{\theta}\in\mathcal{E}}\ f_{L,\text{data}}(\hat{\theta}).

(35)

Thus, the problem addressed in this paper is as follows.

Problem 1
.

Develop an algorithm for solving the time-limited
h^{2}
MOR problem (
35
) in a data-driven manner.
Specifically, construct a ROM using the impulse response data (
28
), under the condition that the matrices
A
,
B
, and
C
in (
1
) are unknown.

Note that, while we can utilize the gradients represented by Proposition
1
to solve Problem (
35
), we cannot compute them when
A
,
B
, and
C
are unknown.

In practice, it may be necessary to ensure the stability of ROMs for Problem (
35
).
For the data-driven MOR problem with stability constraints, please refer to Appendix
B
.

using the impulse response data.
Here, we denote the gradient of

\displaystyle\nabla f_{L,\text{data}}=(\nabla_{\hat{A}}f_{L,\text{data}},\nabla_{\hat{B}}f_{L,\text{data}},\nabla_{\hat{C}}f_{L,\text{data}}).

\displaystyle\nabla_{\hat{C}}f_{L,\text{data}}

\displaystyle=2(\hat{C}P_{L}-Z_{1}),

(39)

where

,
Q_{L}
,
R_{L}
, and
S_{L}
are solutions of (

), (
16
), (
17
), and (

Remark 1
.

In our setting, where

are unavailable but

VII
Conclusion

In this paper, we developed a data-driven time-limited
h^{2}
model reduction method for discrete-time LTI systems.
In particular, by computing the objective function and its gradient from impulse response data, the proposed method avoids using the original system matrices
(A,B,C)
.
Numerical experiments demonstrated that the proposed gradient-based optimization method produces better solutions than the initial point in terms of time-limited
h^{2}
norm.

A
Proof

Proof of Proposition
1
.

Since
\langle X,Y\rangle=\tr(X^{\top}Y)
, the objective function can be written as

\displaystyle f_{L}(\hat{\theta})=\langle\hat{C}^{\top}\hat{C},\ P_{L}\rangle-2\langle C^{\top}\hat{C},\ R_{L}\rangle.

(44)

Hence

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}f_{L}=2\langle\hat{C}P_{L}-CR_{L},\ \mathrm{d}\hat{C}\rangle+\langle\hat{C}^{\top}\hat{C},\ \mathrm{d}P_{L}\rangle-2\langle C^{\top}\hat{C},\ \mathrm{d}R_{L}\rangle.

(45)

Define the Stein and Sylvester operators

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(X):=X-\hat{A}X\hat{A}^{\top},\quad\mathcal{L}(X):=X-AX\hat{A}^{\top},

(46)

with adjoints

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\ast}(Y):=Y-\hat{A}^{\top}Y\hat{A},\quad\mathcal{L}^{\ast}(Y):=Y-A^{\top}Y\hat{A}.

(47)

Then, by the nature of the trace,

\displaystyle\langle Y,\mathcal{S}(X)\rangle=\langle\mathcal{S}^{\ast}(Y),X\rangle,\quad\langle Y,\mathcal{L}(X)\rangle=\langle\mathcal{L}^{\ast}(Y),X\rangle.

(48)

Next, to eliminate

and
\mathrm{d}R_{L}

and

Now, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(P_{L})

\displaystyle=\hat{B}\hat{B}^{\top}-(\hat{A})^{L}\hat{B}\hat{B}^{\top}(\hat{A}^{\top})^{L},

(49)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(R_{L})

\displaystyle=B\hat{B}^{\top}-A^{L}B\hat{B}^{\top}(\hat{A}^{\top})^{L}.

(50)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{d}P_{L})

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{d}\hat{B})\hat{B}^{\top}+\hat{B}(\mathrm{d}\hat{B})^{\top}+(\mathrm{d}\hat{A})P_{L}\hat{A}^{\top}

(51)

\displaystyle\quad+\hat{A}P_{L}(\mathrm{d}\hat{A})^{\top}-\mathrm{d}\big((\hat{A})^{L}\hat{B}\hat{B}^{\top}(\hat{A}^{\top})^{L}\big),

(52)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{d}R_{L})

\displaystyle=B(\mathrm{d}\hat{B})^{\top}+AR_{L}(\mathrm{d}\hat{A})^{\top}-\mathrm{d}\big(A^{L}B\hat{B}^{\top}(\hat{A}^{\top})^{L}\big).

(53)

Let
Q_{L}
and
S_{L}
be the solutions of the adjoint equations

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\ast}(Q_{L})=\hat{C}^{\top}\hat{C},\qquad\mathcal{L}^{\ast}(S_{L})=-C^{\top}\hat{C}.

(54)

Applying (
48

52

53

\displaystyle\langle\hat{C}^{\top}\hat{C},\ \mathrm{d}P_{L}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle Q_{L},\ \mathcal{S}(\mathrm{d}P_{L})\rangle,

(55)

\displaystyle\langle-C^{\top}\hat{C},\ \mathrm{d}R_{L}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle S_{L},\ \mathcal{L}(\mathrm{d}R_{L})\rangle.

(56)

Substituting (
52
) and (
53
) into (
55
) and (
56
),
and collecting the
\mathrm{d}\hat{B}

.
Similarly, the explicit
\mathrm{d}\hat{C}
-term in (

\nabla_{\hat{C}}f_{L}=2(\hat{C}P_{L}-CR_{L})

We decompose
\mathrm{d}f_{L}

\mathrm{d}\hat{A}
.
The interior terms in (
52
) and (
53
) give

\displaystyle 2\langle Q_{L}\hat{A}P,\ \mathrm{d}\hat{A}\rangle+2\langle S_{L}^{\top}AR,\ \mathrm{d}\hat{A}\rangle,

(57)

where

and
R

25
) and (
26
).
The remaining
\mathrm{d}\hat{A}
-dependence comes only through the endpoint terms

\mathrm{d}\big((\hat{A})^{L}\hat{B}\hat{B}^{\top}(\hat{A}^{\top})^{L}\big)
and

\mathrm{d}\big(A^{L}B\hat{B}^{\top}(\hat{A}^{\top})^{L}\big)
.
By using (
25
) and (
26
), these endpoint contributions can be written as

\displaystyle 2\tr\big(M^{\top}\,\mathrm{d}(\hat{A}^{L})\big).

(58)

Finally, the Fréchet derivative of the matrix-power map
\hat{A}\mapsto\hat{A}^{k}

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}(\hat{A}^{L})=\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}\hat{A}^{L-1-j}(\mathrm{d}\hat{A})\hat{A}^{j}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{T}^{\ast}_{\hat{A},L}(M),\ \mathrm{d}\hat{A}\rangle.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\hat{A}}f_{L}=2\big(Q_{L}\hat{A}P+S_{L}^{\top}AR+\mathcal{T}^{\ast}_{\hat{A},L}(M)\big),

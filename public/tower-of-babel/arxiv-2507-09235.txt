Explicit geometric construction of triangle-free Ramsey graphs
Matija Kocbek

Abstract
We describe an explicit geometric construction of a vast family of graphs without m m -cliques with bounded independence number generalizing triangle-free Ramsey graphs described by Codenotti, Pudlák and Resta and provide a new combinatorial proof for the upper bound on the independence number of the latter. We focus on triangle-free graphs and describe some families of such graphs with n n vertices and independence number O ⁡ ( n 2 3 ) O(n^{\frac{2}{3}}) which give us a constructive asymptotic lower bound Ω ⁡ ( t 3 2 ) \Omega(t^{\frac{3}{2}}) for Ramsey numbers R ⁡ ( 3 , t ) R(3,t) which achieves the best-known constructive lower bound. We describe an additional family of graphs that don’t match the best-known bound but still have a polynomial independence number with regards to the number of vertices and are based on Euclidean geometry. We also present a linear 1 2 \frac{1}{2} -approximation algorithm for finding the largest independent set that works for a significant subset of our family of graphs.



Keywords—
Ramsey number, triangle-free graphs, combinatorial designs, combinatorial geometry

Let’s first define the central object we will be working with.

Definition 2.1
.

Let

Any
m

2.

Every point is contained in at least one block.

3.

Every block is non-empty.

Then we say
\mathcal{B}
is a weakly
m
-wise balanced design over
\mathcal{A}
or an
m
-wise balanced packing over
\mathcal{A}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
m\geq 3
be a natural number, let
\mathcal{B}
be a weakly
(m-1)
-wise balanced design over some set
\mathcal{A}
. Let
<
be any strict linear ordering of the set
\mathcal{A}
. We then define a graph
\Gamma_{\mathcal{B},m}
in such way,
that we take take all incidence pairs for vertices and two vertices
(x,B_{1})
and
(y,B_{2})
have an edge between them if
x<y
,
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
are different and
x\in B_{2}
.

Our notation neglects the fact that the above defined graph is also dependent on the set
\mathcal{A}
and its linear ordering
<
. This is for simplicity of writing but we keep this fact in mind. Let’s explore properties of this graph:

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a weakly
(m-1)
-balanced design over
\mathcal{A}
. Then
\Gamma_{\mathcal{B},m}
does not contain a clique of size
m
.

Proof.

We prove by contradiction. Assume we have a clique
(x_{1},B_{1}),(x_{2},B_{2}),\ldots,\\
(x_{m},B_{m})
. Then all points and blocks are pairwise different. Without loss of generality we have
x_{1}<x_{2}<\ldots<x_{m}
. Then it follows by construction
that
x_{1},\ldots x_{m-1}\in B_{m-1}\cap B_{m}
. Because
m-1
pairwise distinct points are contained in at most one block, we have
B_{m-1}=B_{m}
which is a contradiction and so
\Gamma_{\mathcal{B},m}
really doesn’t contain an
m
-clique.

\hfill\Box

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a weakly
(m-1)
-balanced design over
\mathcal{A}
. Then
\alpha(\Gamma_{\mathcal{B},m})\leq|\mathcal{A}|+|\mathcal{B}|
.

Proof.

Let
H\subseteq V(\Gamma_{\mathcal{B},m})
be any independent set of vertices. Then we can write
H
as a disjoint union of two sets.

The first set
H_{1}
contais all incidence pairs in
H
for which the point appears in at least two different incidence pairs in
H
. Let’s take two such incidence pairs which share the same point, say
(x,B_{1})
and
(x,B_{2})
.
Because
H
is independent, for every
(y,B_{2})\in H,y\neq x
it must be
y<x
, as we would otherwise have an edge between
(x,B_{1})
and
(y,B_{2})
which would contradict independence of
H
. Similarly

(y,B_{1})\in H,y\neq x
it must be
y<x
, as we would otherwise have an edge between
(x,B_{2})
and
(y,B_{1})
. This means that among all points which appear in any pair with
B_{1}
in
H
,

is maximal.
It then follows that for every
(z,B)\in H_{1}
,
z
has to be maximal out of all points that appear in pair with
B
in
H
and so every block

can appear in

|H_{1}|\leq|\mathcal{B}|
.

The other set
H_{2}
contains all incidence pairs for which the point appears in precisely one incidence pair in
H

|H_{2}|\leq|\mathcal{A}|
.

Because

|H|\leq|\mathcal{A}|+|\mathcal{B}|
and so
\alpha(\Gamma_{\mathcal{B},m})\leq|\mathcal{A}|+|\mathcal{B}|
.

\hfill\Box

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a weakly
(m-1)
-balanced design over
\mathcal{A}
. Then
\chi(\Gamma_{\mathcal{B},m})\geq\frac{\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}|B|}{|\mathcal{A}|+|\mathcal{B}|}
.

Proof.

Graph
\Gamma_{\mathcal{B},m}
has
\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}|B|
vertices. Because
\chi(G)\alpha(G)\geq|V(G)|
for any graph
G
, we have
\chi(\Gamma_{\mathcal{B},m})\geq\frac{\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}|B|}{|\mathcal{A}|+|\mathcal{B}|}
.

\hfill\Box

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a weakly
(m-1)
-balanced design over
\mathcal{A}
. Then
|\mathcal{B}|\leq\alpha(\Gamma_{\mathcal{B},m})
.

Proof.

We will prove that every graph
\Gamma_{\mathcal{B},m}
contains an independent set of size
|\mathcal{B}|
. We prove by induction on
n=|\mathcal{B}|
.

For
n=0
, the statement is obvious and base of induction fulfilled. Let
n\geq 1
and suppose the statement is true for all smaller natural numbers. Let
\mathcal{B}
of power
n
be a weakly
(m-1)
-balanced design over
\mathcal{A}
. Let
x
be the smallest point in
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{C}
the set of all blocks which contain
x
. Let
\mathcal{I}_{1}
be the set of all incidence pairs which contain
x
and blocks in
\mathcal{C}
.
This set is obviously independent because all incidence pairs contain the same point in them. Let furthermore
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{B}\setminus\mathcal{C}
. Then by construction of
\mathcal{C}
,
x
isn’t included in any blocks in
\mathcal{D}
. Let
y\in\mathcal{A}\setminus\{p\}
and
B_{2}\in\mathcal{D}
such that
y\in B_{2}
and let
(x,B_{1})\in\mathcal{I}_{1}
.
Because of minimality of
x
, incidence pairs
(x,B_{1})
and
(y,B_{2})
can be connected only if
x\in B_{2}
, which is impossible because
x
isn’t contained in any block in
\mathcal{D}
. Thus
\mathcal{I}_{1}
doesn’t share an edge with any incidence pair with points in
\mathcal{A}
and blocks in
\mathcal{D}
and is different from every such pair. Let
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
be the
set of points from
A
which are contained in some block in
\mathcal{D}
. Then
\mathcal{D}
is a weakly
(m-1)
-wise balanced design over
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
and
\Gamma_{\mathcal{D},m}
is isomorphic to the subgraph
H
of
\Gamma_{\mathcal{B},m}
, which is induced on vertices which have points in
A^{\prime}
and blocks in
D
. We have proven that this subgraph is independent and disjoint from
\mathcal{I}_{1}
.
Because
x
is contained in at least one block,
C
is a non-empty set, and so
|D|<n
. Then
\Gamma_{\mathcal{D},m}
and with it

of size

. Then
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}_{1}\cup\mathcal{I}_{2}

|\mathcal{C}|+|\mathcal{D}|=|\mathcal{B}|

\hfill\Box

The proof of the previous theorem gives us an algorithm with which we can efficiently find an independent set of size
|\mathcal{B}|
in
\Gamma_{\mathcal{B},m}
. We start with an empty independent set. Then we take the smallest point and add all incidence pairs in which it appears to the independent set. From the set of all points and blocks we remove this point and all the blocks which contain it and again take the smallest
of the rest of the points and we add all the incidence pairs in which it appears with non-removed blocks to the independent set. We continue the procedure until we run out of blocks. If we appropriately store incidences, this algorithm has a
O(|\mathcal{A}|+|\mathcal{B}|)
time complexity.

The previous theorem also give us the following obvious corollary which tells us that if we have more blocks than points, the independence number is proportional to the number of points and blocks.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a weakly
(m-1)
-balanced design over
\mathcal{A}
. Suppose
|\mathcal{A}|\leq k|\mathcal{B}|
for some
k>0
. Then
\frac{|\mathcal{A}|+|\mathcal{B}|}{k+1}\leq\alpha(\Gamma_{\mathcal{B},m})
.

For many important classes of weakly
m
-wise balanced designs it is already known that

Weakly
m
-wise balanced design
\mathcal{B}
over
\mathcal{A}
is a Steiner system with parameters

if
|\mathcal{A}|=n
, every block contains exactly

m

[
5

tells us that if
\mathcal{B}

. This tells us the following:

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a Steiner system with parameters

over
\mathcal{A}
and let

. Then
\frac{|\mathcal{A}|+|\mathcal{B}|}{2}\leq\alpha(\Gamma_{\mathcal{B},m})\leq|\mathcal{A}|+|\mathcal{B}|
.

Likewise, weakly
2
-wise balanced design
\mathcal{B}
is a pairwise balanced system if every block contains at least two points and any two points are contained together in precisely one block. Erdős-de Bruijn theorem
[

tells us that if
\mathcal{B}
is a pairwise balanced design and no block contains all of

. From this we get:

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a pairwise balanced design over
\mathcal{A}
such that no block contains all of

. Then
\frac{|\mathcal{A}|+|\mathcal{B}|}{2}\leq\alpha(\Gamma_{\mathcal{B},3})\leq|\mathcal{A}|+|\mathcal{B}|

In these cases when there are less points than there are blocks, the previously described algorithm is an
\frac{1}{2}
-approximation algorithm.

We also have a different lower bound for the independence number of
\Gamma_{\mathcal{B},m}
which is useful, when we have more points than blocks:

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a weakly
(m-1)
-balanced design over
\mathcal{A}
. Then
\frac{|\mathcal{A}|}{|\mathcal{B}|}\leq\alpha(\Gamma_{\mathcal{B},m})
.

Proof.

We will prove that
\Gamma_{\mathcal{B},m}
contains an independent set of size at least
\frac{|\mathcal{A}|}{|\mathcal{B}|}

\frac{|\mathcal{A}|}{|\mathcal{B}|}

\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{B}}=A
. However, we also have
|\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{B}}|<|\mathcal{B}|\frac{|\mathcal{A}|}{|\mathcal{B}|}<|\mathcal{A}|

which is a contradiction. So we have a block

which contains at least
\frac{|\mathcal{A}|}{|\mathcal{B}|}

\mathcal{I}=\{(x,B);x\in B\}
is an independent set of size at least
\frac{|\mathcal{A}|}{|\mathcal{B}|}
which proves the theorem.

\hfill\Box

This, together with the previous lower bound, gives us the following corollary:

Corollary 2.11
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a weakly
(m-1)
-balanced design over
\mathcal{A}
. Suppose

for some

. Then
\frac{k|\mathcal{A}|^{\varepsilon}+|\mathcal{B}|}{2}\leq\alpha(\Gamma_{\mathcal{B},m})

3
Geometric construction of triangle-free graphs with bounded independence number

We saw that for many weakly
(m-1)
-wise balanced designs
\mathcal{B}
the independence number of
\Gamma_{\mathcal{B},m}
is proportional to the number of points and blocks. Let’s focus now on the case when
m=3
and let’s denote
\Gamma_{\mathcal{B},3}
simply as
\Gamma_{\mathcal{B}}
. The following statement due to Alexander Ravsky gives us an asymptotic lower bound for the number of points and blocks in a
weakly
2
-wise balanced design:

Theorem 3.1
.

(Alexander Ravsky)
Let
\mathcal{B}
be a weakly
2
-wise balanced design over
\mathcal{A}
. Let
n
be the number of incidence pairs. Then
|\mathcal{A}|+|\mathcal{B}|=\Omega(n^{\frac{2}{3}})
.

Proof.

Let
a=|\mathcal{A}|
and
b=|\mathcal{B}|
. Observe the grid
A\times B
in which we color the cell
(x,B)
if
x\in B
. Because two points are contained together in at most one block, cells
(x,B_{1}),(y,B_{1}),(x,B_{2})
and
(y,B_{2})
cannot be all colored for any distinct
x,y\in\mathcal{A}
and distinct
B_{1},B_{2}\in\mathcal{B}
. We say that the grid doesn’t contain axes-aligned rectangles.

Let’s show a more general inequality for grids without axes-aligned rectangles. Let’s have such a grid with
a
rows and
b
columns. Let’s denote its elements with
m_{ij}
. Then there are no four indices
i,j,k\text{ and }l
, such that
t_{ik},t_{il},t_{jk}
and
t_{jl}
are colored cells. We say that row
i

forbids pair of columns
\{j,k\}
if both
m_{ij}
and
m_{ik}
are colored. As we demonstrated previously, no two rows can forbid the same pair of colums. Let’s denote with
l_{i}
the number of colored cells in row
i
. Then row
i

forbids precisely
\binom{l_{i}}{2}
pairs of colums. Because no two rows can forbid the same pair of columns and because there are
\binom{b}{2}
pairs of columns altogether, we have
\sum_{i=1}^{a}\binom{l_{i}}{2}\leq\binom{b}{2}
or equivalently
b^{2}-b\geq\sum_{i=1}^{a}l_{i}^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{a}l_{i}
. By using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we get

b^{2}-b\geq\frac{1}{a}(\sum_{i=1}^{a}l_{i})^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{a}l_{i}

.

Returning to our context, we have
n=\sum_{i=1}^{a}l_{i}
and so
n^{2}-an-a(b^{2}-b)\leq 0
. Solving this as a quadratic inequality we get
a\geq\frac{n^{2}}{b^{2}-b+n}
and so
a+b\geq\frac{n^{2}}{b^{2}-b+n}+b
. Denote with
b_{0}
the point in which right hand side achieves its minimum on
[1,\infty)
. Then
a+b\geq\frac{n^{2}}{b_{0}^{2}-b_{0}+n}+b_{0}\geq b_{0}
.
If we write
f(b)=\frac{n^{2}}{b^{2}-b+n}+b
, we have
f^{\prime}(b)=\frac{(b-1)(b^{3}-b^{2}+2nb-2n^{2})}{(b^{2}-b+n)^{2}}
, so
f^{\prime}(b)=0
if
b=1
or
b^{3}-b^{2}+2nb-2n^{2}=0
. We can check that the latter equation has exactly one solution because
g(b)=b^{3}-b^{2}+2nb-2n^{2}
is an increasing cubic polynomial and so we must have
g(b_{0})=0
.
We can check
g(\sqrt[3]{2n^{2}}-\frac{2}{3}\sqrt[3]{n})<0
and because
g
is increasing we then know that
b_{0}>\sqrt[3]{2n^{2}}-\frac{2}{3}\sqrt[3]{n}
, and so
|\mathcal{A}|+|\mathcal{B}|=a+b\geq b_{0}>\sqrt[3]{2n^{2}}-\frac{2}{3}\sqrt[3]{n}
. This finally gives us
|\mathcal{A}|+|\mathcal{B}|=\Omega(n^{\frac{2}{3}})
.

\hfill\Box

So if we have
|\mathcal{A}|\leq k|\mathcal{B}|
for some
k>0
, the independence number of
\Gamma_{\mathcal{B}}
with
n
vertices will always be
\Omega(n^{\frac{2}{3}})
for any choice of
\mathcal{B}
. Let’s show that this asymptotic lower bound is tight.

Let’s look at a special case of this graph. If
k
is a power of some prime number, we construct
P_{k}
in such way that we take a finite projective plane of order
k
, that is finite projective plane with
k^{2}+k+1
points. For vertices we take all incidence pairs
(p,L)
. We take any linear ordering on the set of points and make an edge between
(p,L)
and
(p^{\prime},L^{\prime})
if
p<p^{\prime}
,
L\neq L^{\prime}
and
p\in L^{\prime}
.
Graph
P_{k}
is a special case of
\Gamma_{\mathcal{B}}
in which
\mathcal{A}
contains
k^{2}+k+1
points,
\mathcal{B}
contains
k^{2}+k+1
blocks and every block contains
k+1
points. Any two points are contained together in exactly one block. Results from the previous section give us:

Theorem 3.2
.

P_{k}
doesn’t contain any triangles. Furthermore
\alpha(P_{k})\leq 2\cdot(k^{2}+k+1)
and
\chi(P_{k})\geq\frac{k+1}{2}
.

Because
P_{k}
has
(k^{2}+k+1)(k+1)
vertices, we have
\alpha(P_{k})=O(n^{\frac{2}{3}})
where
n=|V(P_{k})|
and projective plane has the same number of points and lines, so the previous asymptotic lower bound for cases when
|\mathcal{A}|\leq|\mathcal{B}|
is tight.

In the same manner, if
k
is a power of some prime, we could build a graph
A_{k}
from a finite affine geometry of order
k
, that is finite affine geometry with
k^{2}
points. Such a plane has
k^{2}+k
lines and every line contains
k
points. Thus the following theorem holds:

Theorem 3.3
.

A_{k}
doesn’t contain any triangles. Furthermore
\alpha(A_{k})\leq 2k^{2}+k
and
\chi(A_{k})\geq\frac{k(k+1)}{2k+1}
.

Because
A_{k}
has
k(k^{2}+k)
vertices, we have
\alpha(A_{k})=O(n^{\frac{2}{3}})
where
n=|V(A_{k})|
.

We have given two different constructions of graphs on
n
vertices with independence number of order
O(n^{\frac{2}{3}})
but both these constructions are possible only for certain
n
. Let’s now construct a graph that has
n
vertices and independence number of order
O(n^{\frac{2}{3}})
where
n
is any natural number. By differentiation we see that
f(k)=\frac{(k+1)^{3}+(k+1)^{2}}{k^{3}+k^{2}}
is decreasing on
[1,\infty)
and so

\frac{(k+1)^{3}+(k+1)^{2}}{k^{3}+k^{2}}\leq 6
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus we have
k
such that
n\leq k^{3}+k^{2}\leq 6n
. By Bertrand’s postulate
[
10
]
there is a prime number
p
such that
k\leq p\leq 2k
. Let
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
be sets of points and lines in a finite affine geometry of order
p
. Then
\sum_{L\in B}|L|=p^{3}+p^{2}\geq n
. For every
L\in B
we can choose

L^{\prime}\subseteq L
so that
\sum_{L\in B}|L\setminus L^{\prime}|=n
. Let’s now define
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}=\{L\setminus L^{\prime};L\in\mathcal{B},L\neq L^{\prime}\}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
set of points which are contained in some block in
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
. Then
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
is a weakly
2
-wise balanced design over
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
with
n
incidence pairs. Let’s define
G_{n}=\Gamma_{\mathcal{B^{\prime}}}
. Then we have
\alpha(G_{n})\leq|\mathcal{A}^{\prime}|+|\mathcal{B}^{\prime}|\leq|\mathcal{A}|+|\mathcal{B}|=2p^{2}+p\leq 8k^{2}+2k\leq 8\cdot 6\frac{n}{k}
. Because
n\leq k^{3}+k^{2}\leq 2k^{3}
we get
\alpha(G_{n})\leq 48\sqrt[3]{2}n^{\frac{2}{3}}
and thus the following theorem:

Theorem 3.4
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
graph
G_{n}
has
n
vertices, is triangle-free and
\alpha(G_{n})=O(n^{\frac{2}{3}})
.

We should point out that the estimations we made in the previous paragraph are very crude. For every
k\geq 4
we have
\frac{(k+1)^{3}+(k+1)^{2}}{k^{3}+k^{2}}\leq 2
which is much tighter. When estimating the distribution of prime numbers we only used Betrand’s postulate but prime numbers are much denser. Furthermore, we didn’t take into account that instead of a prime
p
, we could have taken any power of a prime and the proof would still hold. And
those are even denser. High constant in the estimation could thus be significantly lower.

Finally, let’s take a look at a family of graphs we can get by using weakly
2
-wise balanced designs induced by points and lines in the Euclidean plane. Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathcal{A}=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2};1\leq a\leq N,1\leq b\leq 2N^{2}\}
and
\mathcal{B}=\{\{(x,mx+b);x\in\mathbb{N},1\leq x\leq N\};m,b\in\mathbb{N},1\leq m\leq N,1\leq b\leq N^{2}\}
. Then
|\mathcal{A}|=2N^{3},|\mathcal{B}|=N^{3}
and every block contains
N
points
so
\Gamma_{\mathcal{B}}
has
N^{4}
vertices and thus
\alpha(\Gamma_{\mathcal{B}})=O(n^{\frac{3}{4}})
where
n
is the number of vertices of the graph. Because we know we have an independent set of size
|\mathcal{B}|=n^{\frac{3}{4}}
we also know that this bound is tight. For any sets of points
\mathcal{A}
and set of lines
\mathcal{B}
in the Euclidean plane for which
|\mathcal{A}|\leq k|\mathcal{B}|
for some
k>0
, we have shown that the independence number
of
\Gamma_{\mathcal{B}}
will be asymptotically equal to
|\mathcal{A}|+|\mathcal{B}|
. By the Szemerédi–Trotter theorem
[
9
]
we then know that for such families of points and lines in the Euclidean plane, the previous construction has the asymptotically smallest possible independence number, as the theorem states that for any set of points and lines
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
in the Euclidean plane with
n
incidence pairs we have
|\mathcal{A}|+|\mathcal{B}|=\Omega(n^{\frac{3}{4}})
.

4
Discussion

In this article we proved that for any weakly
2
-wise balanced design
\mathcal{B}
containing at most
k|\mathcal{B}|
points for some
k>0
, graph
\Gamma_{\mathcal{B}}
has an independence number asymptotically bounded below with
\Omega(n^{\frac{2}{3}})
. But a question left open is whether we can get a family of triangle-free graphs with smaller independence number if we use some weakly
2
-wise balanced designs that have asymptotically more points than blocks. Another open question left in this article is whether we can get better
constructive lower bounds for
R(4,t)
and
R(5,t)
than those presented in
[
7
]
, if we use proper weakly
3
-wise or weakly
4
-wise balanced designs.

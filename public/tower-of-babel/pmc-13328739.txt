Vibration characteristic analysis of a rack-climbing lift platform based on a rigid–flexible coupled model
Authors: Lei Mou, Jinjin Sun, Jie Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To address the limitations of traditional multi-rigid-body models in accurately analyzing the vibration characteristics of rack-climbing lift platforms, this study proposes a rigid–flexible coupled dynamic equation and develops a flexible-body substructure reduction method to enhance simulation accuracy while reducing computational complexity. A rigid–flexible coupled simulation model is constructed by integrating ANSYS APDL with ADAMS, enabling an organic coupling between flexible components and rigid bodies. Axial vibration characteristics of the platform are simulated using four models—rigid, multi-flexible coupled, flexible-driven coupled, and rigid–flexible coupled. Results indicate that the Z-direction vibration is similar to the X-direction, with maximum amplitudes of 1.03mm and 1.34mm occurring during the startup phase, whereas the Y-direction amplitude reaches 7mm, representing increases of 579% and 422% relative to the Z- and X-directions, respectively. These findings demonstrate the inadequacy of rigid-body models for effective vibration analysis. Compared with the multi-flexible and flexible-driven coupled models, the rigid–flexible coupled model exhibits smoother transition in vibration response and more pronounced amplitudes, confirming its validity. Further parametric analysis under varying operating speed, load magnitude, eccentric loading, and braking time reveals that eccentric loading effects are condition-dependent, while load magnitude and operating speed significantly influence platform vibration. Finally, experimental validation of axial vibration and vibration acceleration confirms the accuracy of the proposed model. This study provides a theoretical foundation and reliable modeling framework for structural optimization, comfort design, and vibration behavior analysis of lift platforms.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-51186-7.

Introduction

Safety, reliability, and real-time lifting capacity are key foundations for ensuring the efficient and continuous operation of aerial work platforms. The guide frame lifting platform, as a product of the new era combining the functions of construction hoists and aerial work platforms, is widely used in the construction of prefabricated buildings and the façades of high-rise urban buildings due to its simple operation and safe, reliable advantages. It has gradually become an important direction for the future development of aerial work platform technology. In recent years, with the continuous increase in the demand for aerial work, lifting platforms have been developing towards automation, larger sizes, and intelligence, gradually breaking through traditional design frameworks1. However, most of these devices currently adopt integrated and standardized designs, which leads to significant vibrations and intermittent abnormal noises during practical applications, severely impacting engineering safety and construction quality. Therefore, a systematic analysis of the vibration characteristics of guide frame lifting platforms under different operating conditions is a prerequisite for achieving stable and reliable operation of the equipment.

At present, most scholars mainly focus on the structural characteristics, structural optimization, and static analysis of lifting platforms. Although these studies have improved the stability and safety of the platforms to some extent, research on the multibody dynamics analysis and vibration theory of lifting platforms remains relatively insufficient2–6. Although these studies have improved the stability and safety of the platform to some extent, the exploration of multi-body dynamic analysis and vibration theory for lifting platforms remains insufficient. However, vibration is a key factor affecting the operational safety, stability, and comfort of operators. Zheng et al.7 and others performed static analysis, modal analysis, and random vibration analysis of lifting platforms based on a five-degree-of-freedom dynamic model, determining the maximum deformation, maximum stress, and their locations. However, their research did not involve multi-body dynamics analysis or the impact of vibration parameters on the platform. Researchers such as Yang et al.8 and Bi et al.9 established dynamic simulation models of lifting platforms based on the principles of theoretical mechanics and engineering thermodynamics, and conducted systematic investigations into their dynamic responses and vibration characteristics. Florentin et al.10 and others established a flexible-rigid coupling model based on traditional flexible body modeling methods, driving the research on lifting equipment from static analysis to dynamic analysis, and further explored the main factors influencing vibration responses. Han11 and others further studied and derived the vibration differential equations for tower cranes under single operating conditions, providing a theoretical model foundation for solving such problems. Cibicik A et al.12 and others validated the accuracy of their model based on numerical analysis and multi-condition dynamic models. Han et al.13 and others clarified the vibration response laws through modal analysis and transient analysis of the model. Some scholars have explored the influence of rigid force and natural frequency on vibration characteristics based on numerical models and simulation analysis14–16. Liu et al.17 and Peng et al.18, and others analyzed transverse vibration, longitudinal vibration, and transverse-longitudinal coupled vibration one by one, revealing the coupling relationships between different directions of vibration. Yan et al.19 and others derived the relationship curve between natural frequency and vibration response.

In the aforementioned studies, scholars have conducted in-depth analyses of the influence of factors such as the structure, statics, multi-body dynamics, and natural frequencies of lifting platforms on their vibration characteristics, and have proposed various vibration models and corresponding optimization solutions. However, most of the research relies on single models, single operating conditions, or finite element simulation methods to solve vibration characteristics. These models often exhibit low accuracy, limited analysis depth, and fail to trace the root causes of vibration. To address this issue, this paper proposes a combined simulation method based on a multi-body dynamics model and finite element simulation, aiming to conduct a comprehensive analysis of the platform’s vibration characteristics. To further validate the model’s rationality and analysis results, an experimental setup for the guide frame lifting platform was designed. The study examined the effects of multiple operating conditions and factors, including startup, steady-state operation, braking, and off-center loading, on the vibration characteristics of the platform. This research provides an effective solution to improve the safety and reliability of lifting platforms and offers experimental and theoretical support for future vibration characteristic analysis of lifting platforms under multiple operating conditions.

Establishment of the flexible-rigid coupling model for the guide frame lifting platform

As a typical multi-body dynamics system, the traditional multi-rigid body dynamics models are insufficient to meet the practical engineering requirements for guide frame lifting platforms. Therefore, the development of new models has become an inevitable choice to address the challenges in such systems. This section constructs a flexible-rigid coupling model and its simulation model for the guide frame climbing lifting platform, based on the flexible-rigid coupling dynamics theory and substructure reduction theory in multi-body system dynamics.

Modal analysis of the rack-climbing lift platform

Modal analysis is one of the fundamental methods in structural dynamics and is primarily used to determine the natural frequencies and mode shapes of a structural system. These two parameters are essential indicators that characterize the free vibration behavior of the system. For any structural system, a set of inherent vibration frequencies exists under free vibration conditions, which are referred to as natural frequencies or resonant frequencies. Each natural frequency corresponds to a specific vibration pattern. When the excitation frequency approaches or coincides with the natural frequency of the structure, a significant resonance response may occur, and the structure exhibits a vibration pattern corresponding to that frequency, known as the vibration mode.

In modal analysis, the dynamic governing equations of the system are typically established based on Hamilton’s principle23,24, which can be expressed as follows:18\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{array}{c}M\ddot{x}+C\dot{x}+Kx=f\left(t\right)\end{array}$$\end{document}

In the above equation, M and K denote the mass matrix and stiffness matrix of the system, respectively, which are determined by the material properties and structural parameters of the platform. To address these issues, this study proposes a flexible body generation method that improves computational efficiency while maintaining computational accuracy.

Conclusions

The rack-climbing lift platform is modeled as a multi-body dynamic system. By applying rigid–flexible coupled dynamics theory and substructure reduction theory, a rigid–flexible coupled system dynamics model is constructed. This approach overcomes the limitations of traditional multi-rigid-body dynamic models, such as low computational accuracy and weak dynamic responses, while retaining the high computational efficiency of rigid-body models.A command stream softening method embedded in ANSYS Workbench is introduced. By replacing the original rigid components in the system with modal neutral files, rigid-flexible transformation is achieved, and a rigid-flexible coupled simulation model of the platform is established. Using modal theory, modal verification of the rigid-flexible coupled model is conducted in ADAMS, and the natural frequencies of the model are compared to ensure the accuracy of the transformation, with a maximum error of 0.198%. This method resolves potential data loss or corruption issues that may occur when importing ANSYS Workbench modal neutral files into ADAMS.Axial vibration characteristics of the platform are simulated and analyzed. The results show that the Z- and X-direction vibrations are similar, whereas Y-direction vibration is more pronounced, reaching amplitudes up to 7mm an increase of 579% and 422% compared with Z- and X-directions, respectively. Significant differences in vibration characteristics are observed between the platforms centroid and its end, indicating that vibration analysis is influenced by the choice of measurement points. The primary factors affecting platform vibration are analyzed under four operating conditions: travel speed, load magnitude, eccentric loading, and braking duration. The maximum vibration amplitudes are 6.14mm, 5.6mm, 11.74mm, and 3.18mm, respectively. When both travel speed and load magnitude are doubled, vibration amplitudes increase by 31.4% and 58.4%, respectively. With increasing load, the growth of vibration amplitude exceeds linear scaling, with eccentric loading having the most significant impact. Compared with other conditions, braking duration notably affects vibration characteristics, with an optimal braking time determined to be 0.5s. An experimental platform for analyzing the vibration characteristics of the rack-climbing lift platform was designed, along with a comparative scheme between axial vibration and vibration acceleration simulations and experiments. The results show that the simulation-experiment error for axial vibration amplitude is approximately 6%, and the average error for vibration acceleration is about 17.19%. These findings validate the effectiveness of the proposed rigid–flexible coupled model. Furthermore, multi-condition analysis of platform vibration characteristics provides a basis for optimizing operational protocols, improving platform structure, and enhancing stability, safety, and operator comfort in subsequent studies.

The rack-climbing lift platform is modeled as a multi-body dynamic system. By applying rigid–flexible coupled dynamics theory and substructure reduction theory, a rigid–flexible coupled system dynamics model is constructed. This approach overcomes the limitations of traditional multi-rigid-body dynamic models, such as low computational accuracy and weak dynamic responses, while retaining the high computational efficiency of rigid-body models.

A command stream softening method embedded in ANSYS Workbench is introduced. By replacing the original rigid components in the system with modal neutral files, rigid-flexible transformation is achieved, and a rigid-flexible coupled simulation model of the platform is established. Using modal theory, modal verification of the rigid-flexible coupled model is conducted in ADAMS, and the natural frequencies of the model are compared to ensure the accuracy of the transformation, with a maximum error of 0.198%. This method resolves potential data loss or corruption issues that may occur when importing ANSYS Workbench modal neutral files into ADAMS.

Nonparametric Bellman Mappings for Value Iteration in Distributed Reinforcement Learning
Yuki Akiyama, Konstantinos Slavakis 1

Abstract
This paper introduces novel Bellman mappings (B-Maps) for value iteration (VI) in distributed reinforcement learning (DRL), where agents are deployed over an undirected, connected graph/network with arbitrary topology—but without a centralized node, that is, a node capable of aggregating all data and performing computations. Each agent constructs a nonparametric B-Map from its private data, operating on Q-functions represented in a reproducing kernel Hilbert space, with flexibility in choosing the basis for their representation. Agents exchange their Q-function estimates only with direct neighbors, and unlike existing DRL approaches that restrict communication to Q-functions, the proposed framework also enables the transmission of basis information in the form of covariance matrices, thereby conveying additional structural details. Linear convergence rates are established for both Q-function and covariance-matrix estimates toward their consensus values, regardless of the network topology, with optimal learning rates determined by the ratio of the smallest positive eigenvalue (the graph’s Fiedler value) to the largest eigenvalue of the graph Laplacian matrix. A detailed performance analysis further shows that the proposed DRL framework effectively approximates the performance of a centralized node, had such a node existed. Numerical tests on two benchmark control problems confirm the effectiveness of the proposed nonparametric B-Maps relative to prior methods. Notably, the tests reveal a counter-intuitive outcome: although the framework involves richer information exchange—specifically through transmitting covariance matrices as basis information—it achieves the desired performance at a lower cumulative communication cost than existing DRL schemes, underscoring the critical role of sharing basis information in accelerating the learning process.

Following the nonparametric framework of
[

The RKHS
\mathcal{H}
is potentially infinite dimensional

33
,
34
]
, equipped with a
reproducing kernel
\kappa(\cdot,\cdot)\colon\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{R}\colon(\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{1},\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{2})\mapsto\kappa(\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{1},\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{2})

\kappa(\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{1},\cdot)\in\mathcal{H}
,
\forall\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{1}\in\mathcal{Z}
, an inner product
\langle\cdot\mathop{}|\mathop{}\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
, induced norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\coloneqq\langle\cdot\mathop{}|\mathop{}\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}^{1/2}
, and the feature mapping
\varphi\colon\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{H}\colon\bm{\mathrm{z}}\mapsto\varphi(\bm{\mathrm{z}})\coloneqq\kappa(\bm{\mathrm{z}},\cdot)
, so that the celebrated

holds true:
\forall Q^{(n)}\in\mathcal{H}
,
\forall\bm{\mathrm{z}}\in\mathcal{Z}
,
Q^{(n)}(\bm{\mathrm{z}})=\langle Q^{(n)}\mathop{}|\mathop{}\varphi(\bm{\mathrm{z}})\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

33
,
34
]
. An immediate
consequence of the reproducing property is that inner products can be computed directly through kernel function
evaluations:
\langle\varphi(\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{1})\mathop{}|\mathop{}\varphi(\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{2})\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\langle\kappa(\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{1},\cdot)\mathop{}|\mathop{}\kappa(\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{2},\cdot)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\kappa(\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{1},\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{2})
. A well-known example of an infinite
dimensional

is the RKHS associated with the Gaussian kernel
\kappa(\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{1},\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{2})\coloneqq\exp[-\lVert\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{1}-\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{2}\rVert^{2}/(2\tau^{2})]
, for some
\tau\in\mathbb{R}_{++}

To use familiar notations from linear algebra, the “dot-product”

will be used
for the inner product hereafter, where

stands for the transposition operator. To simplify notation for the

\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}\coloneqq[\,Q^{(1)},\ldots,Q^{(N)}\,]\in\bm{\mathscr{H}}
, where

\bm{\mathscr{H}}
stands for the

\mathcal{H}\times\ldots\times\mathcal{H}
, with inner
product:
\langle\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}_{1}\mathop{}|\mathop{}\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}_{2}\rangle_{\bm{\mathscr{H}}}\coloneqq\sum_{n=1}^{N}\langle Q_{1}^{(n)}\mathop{}|\mathop{}Q_{2}^{(n)}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
,
\forall\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}_{1},\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}_{2}\in\bm{\mathscr{H}}
. For convenience, square
brackets
[\cdot]
are used instead of parentheses
(\cdot)
to denote tuples

in

, and
thus allowing for the use of familiar linear algebra operations in the following sections.

III
The Centralized Bellman Mapping

Had there been a
centralized node

n_{\star}\in\mathcal{N}
, connected with every node of the graph

\mathcal{G}_{\star}=(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{E}_{\star})
, where
\mathcal{E}_{\star}
follows a
star topology,

able to
collect all
data
\{\mathcal{T}^{(n)}\}_{n\in\mathcal{N}}
of
Assumption

1(iii)
and to
perform all necessary computations, a centralized B-Map
T_{\odot}\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}\colon Q\mapsto T_{\odot}(Q)
could have been defined at
n_{\star}
as

\displaystyle T_{\odot}(Q)

\displaystyle{}\coloneqq{}

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\textnormal{av}}^{(n)}}\overbrace{[\,g_{i}^{(n)}+\alpha\inf_{a_{i}^{(n)\prime}\in\mathcal{A}}Q(\,\bm{\mathrm{z}}(\bm{\mathrm{s}}_{i}^{(n)\prime},a_{i}^{(n)\prime})\,)\,]}^{c_{i}^{(n)}(Q)}\,\psi_{\odot i}^{(n)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}}\bm{\mathrm{\Psi}}_{\odot}^{(n)}\,\bm{\mathrm{c}}^{(n)}(Q)\,,

(6)

where

\displaystyle g_{i}^{(n)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle g(\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{i}^{(n)})\,,\quad\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{i}^{(n)}\coloneqq\bm{\mathrm{z}}(\bm{\mathrm{s}}_{i}^{(n)},a_{i}^{(n)})\,,

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{c}}^{(n)}(Q)

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle[c_{1}^{(n)}(Q),\ldots,c_{N_{\textnormal{av}}^{(n)}}^{(n)}(Q)]^{\intercal}\,,

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{\Psi}}_{\odot}^{(n)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle[\psi_{\odot 1}^{(n)},\ldots,\psi_{\odot N_{\textnormal{av}}^{(n)}}^{(n)}]\,,

and
\{\{\psi_{\odot i}^{(n)}\}_{i=1}^{N_{\textnormal{av}}^{(n)}}\}_{n\in\mathcal{N}}
are user-defined

basis
functions/elements drawn from the RKHS
\mathcal{H}
, which is endowed with a reproducing kernel

\kappa
, a feature mapping
\varphi
, and an inner product
\langle\cdot\mathop{}|\mathop{}\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
, consistent with the
discussion at the end of
Section

II-A
.

Form (
6
) is inspired by the non-distributed design of
[
12
, (3b)]
, which was
introduced as a surrogate for the classical formulation (
1
) in settings where computing the
conditional expectation in (
1
) is infeasible; see
Assumption

1(iii)
. In
(
6
), this conditional expectation is approximated by a linear combination of user-defined functions
\{\psi_{\odot i}^{(n)}\}\subset\mathcal{H}
, with coefficients
c_{i}^{(n)}(Q^{(n)})
determined by evaluations of the
one-step cost
g
and the long-term
Q
-functions at
\{\mathcal{T}^{(n)}\}_{n\in\mathcal{N}}
.

Study
[
12
, Prop. 1]
develops a variational framework for designing
\{\psi_{\odot i}^{(n)}\}
. Remarkably, by selecting appropriate loss functions and regularization terms within that variational framework, one
recovers several well-known B-Map designs
[
12
, Prop. 1]
. This work, for the sake of clarity
and concreteness, adopts the following specific basis functions:

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{\Psi}}_{\odot}^{(n)}

\displaystyle{}\coloneqq{}

\displaystyle(\,\sum\nolimits_{n\in\mathcal{N}}\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}^{(n)}\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}^{(n)\intercal}+\sigma\Id\,)^{-1}\,\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}^{(n)}

(7a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\,\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}_{\mathcal{N}}\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\intercal}+\sigma\Id\,)^{-1}\,\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}^{(n)}\,,

(7b)

where

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}^{(n)}

\displaystyle{}\coloneqq{}

\displaystyle[\varphi(\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{1}^{(n)}),\ldots,\varphi(\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{N_{\textnormal{av}}^{(n)}}^{(n)})]\,,

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}^{(n)}\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}^{(n)\intercal}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{N_{\textnormal{av}}^{(n)}}\varphi(\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{i}^{(n)})\varphi^{\intercal}(\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{i}^{(n)})\,,

(7c)

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}_{\mathcal{N}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle[\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}^{(1)},\ldots,\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}^{(N)}]\,,

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}_{\mathcal{N}}\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\intercal}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{n\in\mathcal{N}}\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}^{(n)}\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}^{(n)\intercal}\,,

(7d)

\Id\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is the identity operator, and
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}_{++}
. Borrowing from the
signal-processing jargon, (
7c
) will be called the
nodal covariance operator,

while (
7d
) the
network-wide covariance operator.
Notice that (
7c
)
and (
7d
) operate in the feature space
\mathcal{H}
.

A certain degree of design flexibility is available, as any choice of
\bm{\mathrm{\Psi}}_{\odot}^{(n)}
from the framework of

[
12
, Prop. 1]
, other than (
7
), could in principle be
employed. However, (
7b
) is adopted here because it has a simpler form than the other alternatives offered
in
[
12
, Prop. 1]
, enables a direct extension of the design in
[
12
]

to the current distributed setting, and possesses rigorously established theoretical
properties
[
12
, Sec. II.D]
.

Under the specific (
7b
), (
6
) takes the following special form:

\displaystyle T_{\odot}(Q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{n\in\mathcal{N}}\bm{\mathrm{\Psi}}_{\odot}^{(n)}\,\bm{\mathrm{c}}^{(n)}(Q)

(8a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{n\in\mathcal{N}}(\,\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}_{\mathcal{N}}\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\intercal}+\sigma\Id\,)^{-1}\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}^{(n)}\,\bm{\mathrm{c}}^{(n)}(Q)

(8b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\,\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}_{\mathcal{N}}\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\intercal}+\sigma\Id\,)^{-1}\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}_{\mathcal{N}}\,\bm{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathcal{N}}(Q)

(8c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}_{\mathcal{N}}(\,\bm{\mathrm{K}}_{\mathcal{N}}+\sigma\Id\,)^{-1}\,\bm{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathcal{N}}(Q)\,,

(8d)

where

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathcal{N}}(Q)

\displaystyle{}\coloneqq{}

\displaystyle[\,\bm{\mathrm{c}}^{(1)\intercal}(Q),\ldots,\bm{\mathrm{c}}^{(N)\intercal}(Q)\,]^{\intercal}

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{K}}_{\mathcal{N}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\intercal}\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}_{\mathcal{N}}\,,

and the equality in (
8d
) can be easily verified.

In the presence of a centralized node
n_{\star}\in\mathcal{N}
, the computation of the centralized
T_{\odot}

in (
8
) and its fixed point, if it exists, is immediate. More precisely, the following two-step
algorithmic procedure suffices to compute and distribute a fixed point
Q_{\odot}
of
T_{\odot}(\cdot)
to all nodes
across the graph.

Algorithm 1

(Centralized solution)
.

1:

Graph
\mathcal{G}_{\star}=(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{E}_{\star})
with a centralized node
n_{\star}\in\mathcal{N}
(
\mathcal{E}_{\star}
follows a star topology), data
\{\mathcal{T}^{(n)}\}_{n\in\mathcal{N}}
,
reproducing kernel
\kappa
.

2:

Each node
n
sends its data
\mathcal{T}^{(n)}
to the centralized node
n_{\star}
.

3:

With all data
\{\mathcal{T}^{(n)}\}_{n\in\mathcal{N}}
available, the centralized node computes

T_{\odot}(\cdot)
via (
8
), identifies a fixed point
Q_{\odot}\in\Fix T_{\odot}
, and
distributes it to all nodes across the graph, so that actions per node are taken according to the “optimal”
policy
\mu_{\odot}\colon\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{A}\colon\bm{\mathrm{s}}\mapsto\mu_{\odot}(\bm{\mathrm{s}})\in\arg\inf\nolimits_{a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}}Q_{\odot}(\bm{\mathrm{s}},a^{\prime})
.

IV
A Novel Distributed Value-Iteration Algorithm

IV-A

Challenges in the absence of a centralized node

In the absence of a centralized node,
Algorithm

1
is infeasible. This section proposes a fully distributed
alternative which abides by
Assumptions

1
.

To this end, define first the following
nodal
B-Maps:
T^{(n)}\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}\colon Q\mapsto T^{(n)}(Q)
with

\displaystyle T^{(n)}(Q)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\textnormal{av}}^{(n)}}[\,g_{i}^{(n)}+\alpha\inf_{a_{i}^{(n)\prime}\in\mathcal{A}}Q(\bm{\mathrm{s}}_{i}^{(n)\prime},a_{i}^{(n)\prime})\,]\,\psi_{i}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\bm{\mathrm{\Psi}}^{(n)}\,\bm{\mathrm{c}}^{(n)}(Q)\,,

(9)

where
\bm{\mathrm{\Psi}}^{(n)}\coloneqq[\psi_{1}^{(n)},\ldots,\psi_{N_{\textnormal{av}}^{(n)}}^{(n)}]
and
\{\psi_{i}^{(n)}\}_{i=1}^{N_{\textnormal{av}}^{(n)}}
are basis functions in
\mathcal{H}
defined by the agent at
node
n
. Following the structure of (
7b
), a natural choice for
\bm{\mathrm{\Psi}}^{(n)}
is

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{\Psi}}^{(n)}=(\,\bm{\mathrm{C}}^{(n)}+\sigma\Id\,)^{-1}\,\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}^{(n)}\,,

(10)

where
\bm{\mathrm{C}}^{(n)}
is a nodal estimate of the network-wide covariance operator
\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}_{\mathcal{N}}\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\intercal}
; hence, (
10
) serves as an estimate of (
7b
). A
more detailed form appears in (
24
). However, such a design raises the following issues.

Challenges 2
.

(i)

(Distribute Q-functions)
A consensus-based distributed algorithm over
\mathcal{G}
is
needed to approximate the centralized computation of
\sum_{n^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}}\bm{\mathrm{\Psi}}_{\odot}^{(n^{\prime})}\,\bm{\mathrm{c}}^{(n^{\prime})}(\cdot)
in (
8a
) at every node
n
.

(ii)

(Distribute covariance operators)
A consensus-based distributed algorithm over

\mathcal{G}
is required to ensure that the estimate
\bm{\mathrm{C}}^{(n)}
in (
10
) accurately
approximates the network-wide covariance operator (
7d
) at every node
n
.

(iii)

(Curse of dimensionality)
Because
\mathcal{H}
may be infinite-dimensional,
addressing
Challenges

2(i)
and
2(ii)
involves sharing high- or even infinite-dimensional objects, such as
Q-functions and covariance operators. To mitigate the resulting communication bandwidth constraints, a
dimensionality-reduction scheme is required.

IV-B

Distributing Q-functions

To address
Challenge

2(i)
, gather first all nodal B-Maps into the following
in-network
B-Map:
\bm{\mathrm{T}}\colon\bm{\mathscr{H}}\to\bm{\mathscr{H}}\colon\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}=[Q^{(1)},\ldots,Q^{(N)}]\mapsto\bm{\mathrm{T}}(\bm{\mathfrak{Q}})
with

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{T}}(\bm{\mathfrak{Q}})\coloneqq[\,T^{(1)}(Q^{(1)}),\ldots,T^{(N)}(Q^{(N)})\,]\,.

(11)

This paper’s counter-proposition to (
4
) is to solve distributively the following task:

\displaystyle\arg\min_{\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{\bm{\mathscr{H}}}}\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert\,\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}^{\prime}-N\,\bm{\mathrm{T}}(\bm{\mathfrak{Q}})\,\rVert_{\bm{\mathscr{H}}}^{2}

(12a)

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{\bm{\mathscr{H}}}}\sum\nolimits_{n\in\mathcal{N}}\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert\,Q^{\prime(n)}-N\,T^{(n)}(Q^{(n)})\,\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left[\,\sum\nolimits_{n\in\mathcal{N}}T^{(n)}(Q^{(n)}),\ldots,\sum\nolimits_{n\in\mathcal{N}}T^{(n)}(Q^{(n)})\,\right]

(12b)

\displaystyle=[\,\bm{\mathrm{T}}(\bm{\mathfrak{Q}})\,\bm{\mathrm{1}}_{N},\ldots,\bm{\mathrm{T}}(\bm{\mathfrak{Q}})\,\bm{\mathrm{1}}_{N}\,]\,.

The closed-form solution (
12b
) of (
12a
) is straightforward for a centralized node, but in its
absence, a distributed algorithmic approach is required. To this end, the framework of
[
42
]
is
adopted due to its generality, flexibility, and simple recursive structure. Accordingly, the linear operators
A^{\textnormal{Q}},A_{\varpi}^{\textnormal{Q}}\colon\bm{\mathscr{H}}\to\bm{\mathscr{H}}
are defined by

\displaystyle A^{\textnormal{Q}}(\bm{\mathfrak{Q}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}(\bm{\mathrm{I}}_{N}-\gamma\bm{\mathrm{L}})\,,

(13a)

\displaystyle A_{\varpi}^{\textnormal{Q}}(\bm{\mathfrak{Q}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\varpi A^{\textnormal{Q}}(\bm{\mathfrak{Q}})+(1-\varpi)\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}\,,

(13b)

\forall\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}\in\bm{\mathscr{H}}
. In (
13
),
\varpi\in[1/2,1)
and
\gamma\in(\,0,1/\lVert\bm{\mathrm{L}}\rVert_{2}\,]
, where
\lVert\bm{\mathrm{L}}\rVert_{2}
is the spectral norm of the Laplacian matrix

\bm{\mathrm{L}}

[
43
]
.

Owing to the definition of the Laplacian matrix, notice that the
n
th entry of
A^{\textnormal{Q}}(\bm{\mathfrak{Q}})

in (
13a
) takes the following form:

\displaystyle(\,A^{\textnormal{Q}}(\bm{\mathfrak{Q}})\,)^{(n)}=(1-\gamma\lvert\mathcal{N}_{n}\rvert)\,Q^{(n)}+\gamma\sum_{n^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}_{n}}Q^{(n^{\prime})}\,.

(14)

This is a clear demonstration of the distributive nature of
A^{\textnormal{Q}}
, because not only the local
Q-function
Q^{(n)}
but also copies of
\{Q^{(n^{\prime})}\}_{n^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}_{n}}
need to be
transmitted from neighbors
\mathcal{N}_{n}
to node
n
to compute
(\,A^{\textnormal{Q}}(\bm{\mathfrak{Q}})\,)^{(n)}
. Because of
\gamma\in(\,0,1/\lVert\bm{\mathrm{L}}\rVert_{2}\,]
, it can be verified that
\lVert A^{\textnormal{Q}}\rVert=\lVert\bm{\mathrm{I}}_{N}-\gamma\bm{\mathrm{L}}\rVert_{2}\leq 1
, where
\lVert A^{\textnormal{Q}}\rVert
is the norm induced by
the inner product
\langle\cdot\mathop{}|\mathop{}\cdot\rangle_{\bm{\mathscr{H}}}
. Moreover, because
\gamma\leq 1/\lVert\bm{\mathrm{L}}\rVert_{2}
,
it can be verified that
\langle A^{\textnormal{Q}}(\bm{\mathfrak{Q}})\mathop{}|\mathop{}\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}\rangle_{\bm{\mathscr{H}}}\geq 0
,

\forall\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}\in\bm{\mathscr{H}}
, that is,
A^{\textnormal{Q}}
is positive
[
44
, §9.3]
. Now,
notice that
\bm{\mathrm{L}}\bm{\mathrm{1}}_{N}=\bm{\mathrm{0}}=0\cdot\bm{\mathrm{1}}_{N}
. By
Assumption

1(i)
and the fact that

\bm{\mathrm{L}}
is positive semidefinite
[
38
, Lem. 4.3]
, the rank of
\bm{\mathrm{L}}
is
N-1
with
0
being its
smallest eigenvalue, and the kernel space
\kernel\bm{\mathrm{L}}=\Span(\bm{\mathrm{1}}_{N})
, so that

\displaystyle\Fix(A^{\textnormal{Q}})\coloneqq\{\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}\in\bm{\mathscr{H}}\nonscript\,|\allowbreak\nonscript\,\mathopen{}A^{\textnormal{Q}}(\bm{\mathfrak{Q}})=\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}\}=\mathcal{C}_{\bm{\mathscr{H}}}\,.

(15)

As in (
3
),
k\in\mathbb{N}
serves as the VI index in this paper; see
Figure

1
. With
\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}[k]=[Q^{(1)}[k],\ldots,Q^{(N)}[k]\,]\in\bm{\mathscr{H}}
being the snapshot of all Q-function estimates across
\mathcal{G}

at VI iteration
k
, the aforementioned properties of
A^{\textnormal{Q}}
and (
15
) ensure that the sequence

(\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}_{m}[k])_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
generated by
\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}_{-1}[k]\coloneqq[0,\ldots,0]
and,
\forall m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}_{0}[k]

\displaystyle{}\coloneqq{}

\displaystyle A_{\varpi}^{\textnormal{Q}}(\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}_{-1}[k])-\eta(\,\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}_{-1}[k]-N\,\bm{\mathrm{T}}(\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}[k])\,)\,,

(16a)

\displaystyle\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}_{m+1}[k]

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}_{m}[k]-(\,A_{\varpi}^{\textnormal{Q}}(\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}_{m-1}[k])-\eta\,\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}_{m-1}[k]\,)

\displaystyle\phantom{\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}_{m}[k]}+(\,A^{\textnormal{Q}}(\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}_{m}[k])-\eta\,\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}_{m}[k]\,)\,,

(16b)

with
\eta\in(0,2(1-\varpi)\,)
, converges strongly (recall that
\mathcal{H}
may be infinite dimensional) to the
solution (
12b
) as
m\to\infty

[
42
, Lem. 3.4 and Cor. 3.5]
. Even more, a linear
convergence rate for
(\bm{\mathfrak{Q}}_{m}[k])_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
is established by
Theorem

5(ii)
.

Challenge

2(iii)
appears prominently in the previous discussion, because possibly infinite dimensional Q-functions need
to be shared among neighbors to compute (
14
). To surmount
Challenge

2(iii)
, dimensionality reduction is
needed. To this end, the feature mapping
\varphi\colon\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{H}
—recall the discussion at the end
of
Section

II-A
—will be replaced henceforth by the random-Fourier-feature-(RFF) mapping
[
37
]

\tilde{\varphi}\colon\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{R}^{D}\colon\bm{\mathrm{z}}\mapsto\tilde{\varphi}(\bm{\mathrm{z}})
, for a user-defined

D\in\mathbb{N}_{*}
, with

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}(\bm{\mathrm{z}})\coloneqq\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{D}}\,[\cos(\bm{\mathrm{v}}_{1}^{\intercal}\bm{\mathrm{z}}+u_{1}),\ldots,\cos(\bm{\mathrm{v}}_{D}^{\intercal}\bm{\mathrm{z}}+u_{D})]^{\intercal}\,,

(17)

\forall\bm{\mathrm{z}}\in\mathcal{Z}
, where
\{\bm{\mathrm{v}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{D}
and
\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{D}
are samples from the Gaussian
and uniform distributions, respectively. In other words, a general Q-function in the potentially infinite-dimensional

\mathcal{H}
, for instance
Q^{(n)}=\sum_{i}c_{i}^{(n)}\varphi(\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{i}^{(n)})
, will hereafter be represented by
the dimensionally reduced
D\times 1
vector
\tilde{Q}^{(n)}=\sum_{i}c_{i}^{(n)}\tilde{\varphi}(\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{i}^{(n)})
. Although
\tilde{\varphi}
formally replaces
\varphi
, the symbol
\varphi
will continue to be used for clarity
and notational simplicity, with the understanding that
\tilde{\varphi}
is implemented in the
background.

IV-C

Distributing covariance operators

Drawing now attention to
Challenge

2(ii)
, after the RFF dimensionality-reduction scheme has been applied, a
distributed scheme is needed to compute the
D\times D
network-wide covariance matrix
\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}_{\mathcal{N}}\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\intercal}
of (
7d
); recall that the RFF
\tilde{\varphi}
is
implemented now in computations. To this end, define the linear vector space of operators
\bm{\mathscr{O}}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}^{D\times ND}=\{\bm{\mathfrak{C}}\coloneqq[\bm{\mathrm{C}}^{(1)},\ldots,\bm{\mathrm{C}}^{(N)}]{\mathbin{}\mid\mathbin{}}\bm{\mathrm{C}}^{(n)}\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times D}\}
, equipped with the standard inner product
\langle\bm{\mathfrak{C}}_{1}\mathop{}|\mathop{}\bm{\mathfrak{C}}_{2}\rangle_{\bm{\mathscr{O}}}\coloneqq\trace(\bm{\mathfrak{C}}_{1}^{\intercal}\bm{\mathfrak{C}}_{2})
,
\forall\bm{\mathfrak{C}}_{1},\bm{\mathfrak{C}}_{2}\in\bm{\mathscr{O}}
. Observe then

\displaystyle\arg\min_{\bm{\mathfrak{C}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\bm{\mathscr{O}}}}\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert\,\bm{\mathfrak{C}}-N\,\bm{\mathfrak{C}}_{\mathcal{N}}\,\rVert_{\textnormal{F}}^{2}

(18a)

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{\bm{\mathfrak{C}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\bm{\mathscr{O}}}}\sum\nolimits_{n\in\mathcal{N}}\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert\,\bm{\mathrm{C}}^{(n)}-N\,\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}^{(n)}\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}^{(n)\intercal}\,\rVert_{\textnormal{F}}^{2}

\displaystyle=[\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}_{\mathcal{N}}\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\intercal},\ldots,\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}_{\mathcal{N}}\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\intercal}]\,,

(18b)

where
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\textnormal{F}}
is the Frobenius norm,

\displaystyle\bm{\mathfrak{C}}_{\mathcal{N}}\coloneqq[\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}^{(1)}\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}^{(1)\intercal},\ldots,\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}^{(N)}\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}^{(N)\intercal}]

(19)

gathers all nodal covariance operators, and the consensus set

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\bm{\mathscr{O}}}

\displaystyle{}\coloneqq{}

\displaystyle\{\bm{\mathfrak{C}}\in\bm{\mathscr{O}}{\mathbin{}\mid\mathbin{}}\bm{\mathrm{C}}^{(n)}=\bm{\mathrm{C}}^{(n^{\prime})},\forall\{n,n^{\prime}\}\in\mathcal{E}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\bm{\mathfrak{C}}\in\bm{\mathscr{O}}{\mathbin{}\mid\mathbin{}}\bm{\mathrm{C}}^{(1)}=\ldots=\bm{\mathrm{C}}^{(N)}\}\,.

Along the lines of (
13
), define the linear operators
A^{\textnormal{C}},A_{\varpi}^{\textnormal{C}}\colon\bm{\mathscr{O}}\to\bm{\mathscr{O}}
by

\displaystyle A^{\textnormal{C}}(\bm{\mathfrak{C}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\bm{\mathfrak{C}}\,(\,(\bm{\mathrm{I}}_{N}-\gamma\bm{\mathrm{L}})\otimes\bm{\mathrm{I}}_{D}\,)\,,

(20a)

\displaystyle A_{\varpi}^{\textnormal{C}}(\bm{\mathfrak{C}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\varpi A^{\textnormal{C}}(\bm{\mathfrak{C}})+(1-\varpi)\bm{\mathfrak{C}}\,,

(20b)

\forall\bm{\mathfrak{C}}\in\bm{\mathscr{O}}
, where
\otimes
stands for the Kronecker product,
\gamma\in(\,0,1/\lVert\bm{\mathrm{L}}\rVert_{2}\,]
, and
\varpi\in[1/2,1)
. It is not difficult to verify that per node
n
, only

\bm{\mathrm{C}}^{(n)}
and copies of
\{\bm{\mathrm{C}}^{(n^{\prime})}\}_{n^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}_{n}}
from the neighboring
agents need to be shared to compute

\displaystyle(\,A^{\textnormal{C}}(\bm{\mathfrak{C}})\,)^{(n)}=(1-\gamma\lvert\mathcal{N}_{n}\rvert)\,\bm{\mathrm{C}}^{(n)}+\gamma\sum_{n^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}_{n}}\bm{\mathrm{C}}^{(n^{\prime})}\,.

(21)

Moreover, similarly to the discussion following (
14
) and by using basic properties of
\otimes
, it can be
verified that
\lVert A^{\textnormal{C}}\rVert=\lVert(\bm{\mathrm{I}}_{N}-\gamma\bm{\mathrm{L}})\otimes\bm{\mathrm{I}}_{D}\rVert_{2}=\lVert\bm{\mathrm{I}}_{N}-\gamma\bm{\mathrm{L}}\rVert_{2}\leq 1
, that
A^{\textnormal{C}}
is positive, that
(\bm{\mathrm{L}}\otimes\bm{\mathrm{I}}_{D})(\bm{\mathrm{1}}_{N}\otimes\bm{\mathrm{I}}_{D})=(\bm{\mathrm{L}}\bm{\mathrm{1}}_{N})\otimes\bm{\mathrm{I}}_{D}=\bm{\mathrm{0}}
, that
\kernel(\bm{\mathrm{L}}\otimes\bm{\mathrm{I}}_{D})=\Span(\bm{\mathrm{1}}_{N}\otimes\bm{\mathrm{I}}_{D})
, and that

\displaystyle\Fix(A^{\textnormal{C}})\coloneqq\{\bm{\mathfrak{C}}\in\bm{\mathscr{O}}\nonscript\,|\allowbreak\nonscript\,\mathopen{}A^{\textnormal{C}}(\bm{\mathfrak{C}})=\bm{\mathfrak{C}}\}=\mathcal{C}_{\bm{\mathscr{O}}}\,.

(22)

Consequently, and similarly to (
16
), sequence

(\,\bm{\mathfrak{C}}_{l}=(\bm{\mathrm{C}}_{l}^{(1)},\ldots,\bm{\mathrm{C}}_{l}^{(N)})\,)_{l\in\mathbb{N}}

generated by
\bm{\mathfrak{C}}_{-1}\coloneqq(\bm{\mathrm{0}},\ldots,\bm{\mathrm{0}})
and

\displaystyle\bm{\mathfrak{C}}_{0}

\displaystyle{}\coloneqq{}

\displaystyle A_{\varpi}^{\textnormal{C}}(\bm{\mathfrak{C}}_{-1})-\eta(\bm{\mathfrak{C}}_{-1}-N\,\bm{\mathfrak{C}}_{\mathcal{N}})\,,

(23a)

\displaystyle\bm{\mathfrak{C}}_{l+1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\bm{\mathfrak{C}}_{l}-(\,A_{\varpi}^{\textnormal{C}}(\bm{\mathfrak{C}}_{l-1})-\eta\,\bm{\mathfrak{C}}_{l-1}\,)

\displaystyle\phantom{\bm{\mathfrak{C}}_{l}}+(\,A^{\textnormal{C}}(\bm{\mathfrak{C}}_{l})-\eta\,\bm{\mathfrak{C}}_{l}\,)\,,

(23b)

VI
Conclusions

A novel class of nonparametric Bellman mappings (B-Maps) was introduced for value iteration (VI) in distributed
reinforcement learning (DRL). This approach leveraged a reproducing kernel Hilbert space representation of the
Q-function, enabling a nonparametric formulation that supports flexible, agent-specific basis function design. Beyond
sharing Q-functions, agents also exchanged basis information without relying on a centralized node, facilitating
consensus. The proposed methodology was backed by rigorous theoretical analysis, and numerical evaluations on two
well-known control problems demonstrated its superior performance compared to existing methods. Interestingly, the
evaluations revealed a counter-intuitive insight: despite involving increased information exchange—specifically
through covariance matrix sharing—the approach achieved the desired performance with lower cumulative communication
cost than prior-art DRL schemes. This underscores the critical role of basis information in accelerating the learning
process.

Ongoing research aims to extend this framework in several directions. In particular, future work will investigate the
extension of
Assumptions

1
to MARL and multi-task RL, including scenarios that permit the sharing of state-action
information among agents; address the specific federated RL problem and its idiosyncrasies within a star-topology
network; consider online and streaming data scenarios; provide a theoretical analysis of the approximation error
introduced by the RFF approximation; and develop strategies to reduce the computational complexity of the matrix
inversion in (
24
).

Atomic decomposition for an affine Weyl group of type G 2 G_{2}
Bárbara Muniz, David Plaza, Claudia Rojas-Andías

Abstract
. We show that the elements of the Kazhdan–Lusztig basis of the spherical Hecke algebra of type G 2 G_{2} have an atomic decomposition. As a by-product, we obtain a new algorithm to compute generalized Kostka–Foulkes polynomials in type G 2 G_{2} .



1.
Introduction

Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials arise as the transition coefficients between the canonical (Kazhdan–Lusztig) basis and the standard basis of the Hecke algebra attached to a Coxeter system
[
4
]
.
They are fundamental objects in representation theory and geometry because they encode important structural information in both settings.
In this paper we work with an affine Weyl group of type
G_{2}
and focus on a distinguished family of elements indexed by dominant weights, which we call
spherical elements
.
They are obtained by translating the longest element of the finite Weyl group by a dominant weight (see Section 2 for the precise definition).
For spherical elements, Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials coincide with Kostka–Foulkes polynomials or
q
-weight multiplicities
[
8
,
3
]
, and in particular their coefficients are known to be non-negative.

In type A, Lascoux discovered a further positivity phenomenon for spherical elements
[
5
]
.
He introduced a basis now called the atomic basis and proved that the canonical basis elements expand in this basis with coefficients in
\mathbb{N}[q]
.
We refer to this phenomenon by saying that Kazhdan–Lusztig basis elements have an atomic decomposition.
Lecouvey and Lenart
[
6
]
later studied atomic decompositions in other types using the theory of crystal.
Their work provides evidence that atomicity extends beyond type
A
and naturally raises the problem of determining for which affine types, and for which dominant weights, the spherical elements admit atomic decompositions.

Beyond type
A
, atomicity may fail, and a precise characterization of the weights and affine types for which it holds is still out of reach. In this paper we solve this question for an affine Weyl group of type
G_{2}
.

For a dominant weight
\lambda
, we write
\underline{\mathbf{H}}_{\lambda}
for the spherical Kazhdan–Lusztig basis element and
\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}
for the corresponding element of the atomic basis. Our main result is the following.

Theorem A
.

For every dominant weight
\lambda
we have an expansion

\underline{\mathbf{H}}_{\lambda}=\sum_{\mu\leq\lambda}a_{\lambda,\mu}(q)\mathbf{N}_{\mu},

where
\leq
denotes the dominance order on weights and the polynomials
a_{\lambda,\mu}(q)
have non-negative coefficients. In other words, in type
G_{2}
, all the Kazhdan–Lusztig basis elements associated to spherical elements admits an atomic decomposition.

We give two proofs of this result.

The first proof relies on the step-by-step decompositions of the pre-canonical bases introduced in
[
7
]
.
These decompositions do not immediately give atomicity, since one of the intermediate transitions is not positive.
It is therefore necessary to verify that the negative terms that arise during the process cancel out, yielding the desired positive expansion.

The second proof is obtained by modifying the definition of the pre-canonical bases so that the step-by-step decompositions become positive at each stage, along the line of what was done in
[
9
]
for type
C_{2}
. This approach is particularly well-suited for the construction of crystalline realizations of the atomic decomposition, an ongoing line of work by the first author. Such concrete realizations are extremely valuable, as they can be used to combinatorially describe Kostka–Foulkes polynomials as
q
-weight multiplicities — a long-standing open problem beyond type
A
.
We expect that similar modifications of the pre-canonical bases may recover step-by-step positivity in other types as well.

As an application of our results, we provide an explicit algorithm for computing generalized Kostka–Foulkes polynomials in type
G_{2}
.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews the necessary background on the affine Weyl group of type
G_{2}
, the spherical Hecke algebra, the spherical elements, and the pre-canonical bases. Section 3 contains the first proof of
A
via step-by-step decompositions. Section 4 develops the adjusted bases and presents the second proof. Section 5 describes the computation of generalized Kostka–Foulkes polynomials. Finally, we add an appendix with a
SageMath
implementation of our algorithms to compute the atomic decomposition, the pre-canonical bases and the Kostka–Foulkes polynomials.

(2.3)

(2.4)

H_{\alpha,m}=\{\lambda\in V\mid\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle=m\}

\{s_{\alpha,0}\mid\alpha\in\Phi\}
.
Let
s_{1}=s_{\alpha_{1},0}

s_{2}=s_{\alpha_{2},0}
. It is well-known that
W_{f}

\{s_{1},s_{2}\}
.

W_{f}

(2.5)

W_{f}=\langle s_{1},s_{2}\mid s_{1}^{2}=s_{2}^{2}=1,\quad(s_{1}s_{2})^{6}=1\rangle.

So that
W_{f}
is isomorphic to the group of symmetries of a regular hexagon.
It longest element is
w_{0}=(s_{1}s_{2})^{3}=(s_{2}s_{1})^{3}
.

The affine Weyl group
W_{f}
attached to
\Phi
is the group generated by the reflections
\{s_{\alpha,m}\mid\alpha\in\Phi,\,m\in\mathbb{Z}\}
.
The connected components of the complement of hyperplanes

(2.6)

V\setminus\bigcup_{\alpha,m}H_{\alpha,m}

G_{2}

Figure

\mathcal{A}

(2.7)

A_{0}=\{\lambda\in V\mid-1<\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle<0,\mbox{ for all }\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\}

There is a bijection
W_{a}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
given by

.
This bijection allows us to uniquely identify elements in
W_{a}
with alcoves.

The walls of

are supported in the hyperplanes
H_{\alpha_{1},0}
,
H_{\alpha_{2},0}

H_{\beta,-1}

\beta

\beta=2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
. Let
s_{0}=s_{\beta,-1}
.
Then
W_{a}
is also a Coxeter system with presentation

(2.8)

W_{f}=\langle s_{0},s_{1},s_{2}\mid s_{0}^{2}=s_{1}^{2}=s_{2}^{2}=1,\,(s_{0}s_{1})^{3}=(s_{0}s_{2})^{2}=(s_{1}s_{2})^{6}=1\rangle.

Also, the affine Weyl group
W_{a}
can be realized as a semidirect product:
W_{a}=W_{f}\ltimes\mathbb{Z}\Phi
, where
\mathbb{Z}\Phi

In type
G_{2}

1

X=\mathbb{Z}\Phi
.
Of course, this is not always the case and in general we only have one containment
\mathbb{Z}\Phi\subset X
.

2.2.
Pre-canonical bases

To any Coxeter system we can attach a Hecke algebra.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be the Hecke algebra associated to
W_{a}
.
It is the
\mathbb{Z}[v,v^{-1}]
-algebra associative algebra with generators
\{\mathbf{H}_{0},\mathbf{H}_{1},\mathbf{H}_{2}\}
and relations

(2.9)

\mathbf{H}_{i}^{2}=(v-v^{-1})\mathbf{H}_{i}+1,\quad\mathbf{H}_{0}\mathbf{H}_{1}\mathbf{H}_{0}=\mathbf{H}_{1}\mathbf{H}_{0}\mathbf{H}_{1},\quad\mathbf{H}_{0}\mathbf{H}_{2}=\mathbf{H}_{2}\mathbf{H}_{0},\quad(\mathbf{H}_{1}\mathbf{H}_{2})^{3}=(\mathbf{H}_{2}\mathbf{H}_{1})^{3}.

It comes equipped with two distinguished bases:
The standard basis
\{\mathbf{H}_{w}\mid w\in W_{a}\}
and the canonical or Kazhdan–Lusztig basis
\{\underline{\mathbf{H}}_{w}\mid w\in W_{a}\}
(see for instance
[
1
]
).
The coefficients of the change of basis matrix between the canonical and standard basis are the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials.
More concretely, the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials,
h_{x,y}(v)\in\mathbb{Z}[v,v^{-1}]
, are given by

(2.10)

\underline{\mathbf{H}}_{w}=\sum_{x\in W_{a}}h_{x,w}(v)\mathbf{H}_{x}.

Indeed, it is known that
h_{x,w}(v)\in\mathbb{N}[v]
for arbitrary Coxeter systems
[
2
]
.
We have a third basis
\{\mathbf{N}_{w}\mid w\in W_{a}\}
of
\mathcal{H}
, which we refer as the atomic basis.
Their elements are given by

(2.11)

\mathbf{N}_{w}=\sum_{x\leq w}v^{\ell(w)-\ell(x)}\mathbf{H}_{x},

where
\leq
denotes the Bruhat order and
\ell(\cdot)
the length function.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathbf{H}\in\mathcal{H}
and write it in terms of the atomic basis:

(2.12)

\mathbf{H}=\sum_{x\in W_{a}}a_{x}(v)\mathbf{N}_{x}

for some
a_{x}(v)\in\mathbb{Z}[v,v^{-1}]
.
Then, we say that
\mathbf{H}
has an atomic decomposition if
a_{x}(v)\in\mathbb{N}[v]
for all
x\in W_{a}
.

Remark 2.3
.

It is known that, for any Coxeter system, the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials satisfy the
monotonicity property
(see,
[
11
]
):

(2.13)

h_{x,w}(v)-v^{\,\ell(y)-\ell(x)}\,h_{y,w}(v)\in\mathbb{N}[v]\qquad\text{for all }x\leq y\leq w.

Having an atomic decomposition is a substantially stronger condition than monotonicity.
However, it is also known that many elements do not admit an atomic decomposition.
In fact, the existence of an atomic decomposition appears to be a rather rare phenomenon.

For
\lambda\in X^{+}
we set
\underline{\mathbf{H}}_{\lambda}=\underline{\mathbf{H}}_{\theta(\lambda)}
and
\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}=\mathbf{N}_{\theta(\lambda)}
.
The spherical Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}^{\textbf{sph}}
lives inside
\mathcal{H}
.
It is the free
\mathbb{Z}[v,v^{-1}]
-submodule of
\mathcal{H}
with canonical basis
\{\underline{\mathbf{H}}_{\lambda}\mid\lambda\in X^{+}\}
and atomic basis
\{\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}\mid\lambda\in X^{+}\}
.
Although
\mathcal{H}^{\textbf{sph}}\subset\mathcal{H}
it is not a sub-algebra of
\mathcal{H}
since the multiplication in
\mathcal{H}^{\textbf{sph}}
is defined as a deformation of the usual multiplication in
\mathcal{H}
.
In this paper we only use the linear structure of
\mathcal{H}^{\textbf{sph}}
, so that to avoid unnecessary technicalities we left the multiplication in
\mathcal{H}^{\textbf{sph}}
undefined and refer the interested reader to
[
7
, Section 2.2]
.

We write

(2.14)

\underline{\mathbf{H}}_{\lambda}=\sum_{\mu\leq\lambda}a_{\lambda,\mu}(q)\mathbf{N}_{\mu},

where
q=v^{2}
and
\leq
denotes the dominance order on
X
.
In type
A
the polynomials
a_{\lambda,\mu}(q)
are the atomic polynomials introduced by Lascoux
[
5
]
.
The main result in this paper (
A
) is to prove that
a_{\lambda,\mu}(q)
have positive coefficients or, following Definition
2.2
, that the elements of the canonical basis have an atomic decomposition.

We now define pre-canonical bases and explain their connection with the atomic decomposition.
First, we need to give some notation.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\lambda\in X
.

(1)

We say that
\lambda
is singular if there is an element
w\in W_{f}
which fixes
\lambda+\rho
.
A non-singular weight is called regular.

(2)

The dot action of
W_{f}
in
V
is given by the formula:
w\cdot\lambda=w(\lambda+\rho)-\rho
.

(3)

If
\lambda\in X
is regular then we define
w_{\lambda}\in W_{f}
to be the unique element such that
w_{\lambda}\cdot\lambda\in X^{+}
.

(4)

For
\lambda\in X
we define

(2.15)

\tilde{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{w}=\begin{cases}(-1)^{\ell(w_{\lambda})}\underline{\mathbf{H}}_{w_{\lambda}\cdot\lambda},&\mbox{if }\lambda\mbox{ is regular}\\[4.0pt]
0,&\mbox{if }\lambda\mbox{ is singular.}\end{cases}

(5)

For
i\geq 1
we define

(2.16)

\Phi^{\geq i}=\{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\mid\operatorname{ht}(\alpha)\geq i\}.

(6)

For
I\subset\Phi
we set
\Sigma_{I}=\sum_{\alpha\in I}\alpha
.

We are now in position to define pre-canonical bases.

Definition 2.5
.

For
i\geq 2
and
\lambda\in X^{+}
we define

(2.17)

\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{i}=\sum_{I\subset\Phi^{\geq i}}(-q)^{|I|}\tilde{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{\lambda-\Sigma_{I}}.

We call
\mathcal{N}^{i}=\{\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}\mid\lambda\in X^{+}\}
the
i
-th pre-canonical basis of
\mathcal{H}^{\textbf{sph}}
.

The motivation behind this definition is the anti-atomic formula
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
which asserts that
\mathcal{N}^{2}
coincides with the atomic basis. This is,
\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}=\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{2}
for all
\lambda\in X^{+}
.
It is worth to note that the anti-atomic formula hold for arbitrary affine Weyl groups.
Retuning to the
G_{2}
-setting, it follows from
Table

1
that
\Phi^{\geq m}=\emptyset
for all
m\geq 6
.
Thus,
\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{m}=\underline{\mathbf{H}}_{\lambda}
for all
m\geq 6
and
\lambda\in X^{+}
.
Therefore, in order to compute
\underline{\mathbf{H}}_{\lambda}
in terms of the atomic basis it is enough to compute the expansion of an element
\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{i+1}
in terms of
\mathcal{N}^{i}
for
2\leq i\leq 5
.

Remark 2.6
.

In type
A
, it was conjectured in
[
7
, Conjecture 1.6]
that, for every
i\geq 2
, the coefficients appearing in the expansion of an element
\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{i+1}
in terms of the basis
\mathcal{N}^{i}
are polynomials with nonnegative coefficients.
This conjecture was recently proved by Yamil Sagurie and the first author
[
10
]
, using methods that are closely related to the ones developed in this paper.
We emphasize that this step-by-step positivity property implies the existence of an atomic decomposition.

In type
G_{2}
, however, this step-by-step positivity does
not
hold in general: as we will see in the next section, negative coefficients may appear in some of the intermediate expansions.
Nevertheless—and this is one of the striking features of type
G_{2}
—these negative terms ultimately cancel out when all steps are combined.
In other words, although the intermediate layers of the process fail to be positive, the
total
expansion remains positive, and the atomic decomposition holds in type
G_{2}
.

3.
First approach

3.1.
Inverse step-by-step decompositions

We begin this section by introducing certain elements that relax the definition of pre-canonical bases elements.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
A\subset\Phi
and
\lambda\in X
.
We define

(3.1)

\mathbf{M}^{A}_{\lambda}=\displaystyle\sum_{I\subset A}(-q)^{|I|}\mathbf{\underline{\widetilde{H}}}_{\lambda-\sum_{I}}.

In the following lemma we collect our two main tools to obtain inverse step-by-step decompositions.

Lemma 3.2
.

For all
A\subseteq\Phi
and
\lambda\in X
we have

(a)

If
\alpha\in A
and
B=A\setminus\{\alpha\}
then
\mathbf{M}^{A}_{\lambda}=\mathbf{M}^{B}_{\lambda}-q\mathbf{M}^{B}_{\lambda-\alpha}
.

(b)

\mathbf{M}^{A}_{\lambda}=-\mathbf{M}^{s_{i}(A)}_{s_{i}\cdot\lambda}
. In particular, if
\langle\lambda,\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\rangle=-1
and
s_{i}(A)=A
then
\mathbf{M}^{A}_{\lambda}=0
.

Proof.

The first claim follows directly by the definition of
\mathbf{M}
-elements.
The second one is the content of
[
7
, Proposition 4.3]
.
∎

We now obtain all the inverse step-by-step decompositions.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\lambda={(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})_{\varpi}}\in X^{+}
.
We have

(3.2)

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{5}=\scalebox{0.85}{\mbox{$\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{6}-q\mathbf{N}_{{(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}-1)_{\varpi}}}^{6},&\mbox{if }\lambda_{2}\geq 1;\\
&\\
\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{6},&\mbox{if }\lambda_{2}=0.\end{array}\right.$}}&\quad\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{4}=\scalebox{0.85}{\mbox{$\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{5}-q\mathbf{N}_{{(\lambda_{1}-3,\lambda_{2}+1)_{\varpi}}}^{5},&\mbox{if }\lambda_{1}\geq 3;\\
&\\
\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{5},&\mbox{if }\lambda_{1}=2;\\
&\\
\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{5}+q\mathbf{N}^{5}_{{(0,\lambda_{2})_{\varpi}}},&\mbox{if }\lambda_{1}=1;\\
&\\
\mathbf{N}^{5}_{\lambda}+q\mathbf{N}^{5}_{{(1,\lambda_{2}-1)_{\varpi}}},&\mbox{if }\lambda_{1}=0,\lambda_{2}\geq 1;\\
&\\
\mathbf{N}^{5}_{\lambda}&\mbox{if }\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=0.\end{array}\right.$}}\\[20.0pt]
&\\
\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{3}=\scalebox{0.85}{\mbox{$\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{4}-q\mathbf{N}_{{(\lambda_{1}-1,\lambda_{2})_{\varpi}}}^{4},&\mbox{if }\lambda_{1}\geq 1;\\
&\\
\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{4}-q^{2}\mathbf{N}^{4}_{{(2,\lambda_{2}-2)_{\varpi}}},&\mbox{if }\lambda_{1}=0,\lambda_{2}\geq 2;\\
&\\
\mathbf{N}^{4}_{\lambda},&{\text{else}}\end{array}\right.$}}&\quad\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{2}=\scalebox{0.85}{\mbox{$\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{3}-q\mathbf{N}_{{(\lambda_{1}+1,\lambda_{2}-1)_{\varpi}}}^{3},&\mbox{if }\lambda_{2}\geq 1;\\
&\\
\mathbf{N}^{3}_{\lambda},&\mbox{if }\lambda_{2}=0.\end{array}\right.$}}\end{array}

Proof.

All the decompositions follow by combining both claims in Lemma
3.2
.
We only prove the decomposition of
\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{4}
in
\mathcal{N}^{5}
, since this is the most interesting case.
The other cases are dealt with similarity and are left to the reader.

We recall from Table
1
that
\Phi^{\geq 5}=\Phi^{\geq 4}\cup\{{(3,-1)_{\varpi}}\}
and that
s_{1}(\Phi^{\geq 5})=\Phi^{\geq 5}
.

•

If
\lambda_{1}\geq 3
then
{(\lambda_{1}-3,\lambda_{2}+1)_{\varpi}}\in X^{+}
and the result follows from Lemma
3.2

(a)
.

•

If
\lambda_{1}=2
then
\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{4}=\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{5}-q\mathbf{N}_{{(-1,\lambda_{2}+1)_{\varpi}}}^{5}
. By Lemma
3.2

(b)
applied to
s_{1}
we obtain
\mathbf{N}_{{(-1,\lambda_{2}+1)_{\varpi}}}^{5}=0
.
Thus,
\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{4}=\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{5}
as we wanted to show.

•

If
\lambda_{1}=1
then
\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{4}=\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{5}-q\mathbf{N}_{{(-2,\lambda_{2}+1)_{\varpi}}}^{5}
. By Lemma
3.2

(b)
applied to
s_{1}
we obtain
\mathbf{N}_{{(-2,\lambda_{2}+1)_{\varpi}}}^{5}=-\mathbf{N}_{{(0,\lambda_{2})_{\varpi}}}^{5}
.
Therefore,
\mathbf{N}^{4}_{\lambda}=\mathbf{N}^{5}_{\lambda}+q\mathbf{N}^{5}_{{(0,\lambda_{2})_{\varpi}}}
.

•

If
\lambda_{1}=0
then
\mathbf{N}^{4}_{\lambda}=\mathbf{N}^{5}_{\lambda}-q\mathbf{N}^{5}_{{(-3,\lambda_{2}+1)_{\varpi}}}
. By Lemma
3.2

(b)
applied to
s_{1}
we obtain

\mathbf{N}^{5}_{{(-3,\lambda_{2}+1)_{\varpi}}}=-\mathbf{N}_{{(1,\lambda_{2}-1)_{\varpi}}}^{5}
. If
\lambda_{2}\geq 1
then
{(1,\lambda_{2}-1)_{\varpi}}\in X^{+}
and we are done.
Otherwise,
\lambda_{2}=0
and
\mathbf{N}_{{(1,-1)_{\varpi}}}^{5}=\tilde{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{{(1,-1)_{\varpi}}}-q\tilde{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{{(1,-2)_{\varpi}}}
. Since both
{(1,-1)_{\varpi}}
and
{(1,-2)_{\varpi}}
are singular weights, we conclude that
\mathbf{N}_{{(1,-1)_{\varpi}}}^{5}=0
and the result follows.

∎

3.2.
Step-by-step decompositions

In this section we invert the decompositions obtained in
3.3
.
Among these, the decompositions of
\mathcal{N}^{5}
inside
\mathcal{N}^{6}
and of
\mathcal{N}^{2}
inside
\mathcal{N}^{3}
are the simplest to analyze.
Indeed, in both situations the rule is the same: we subtract the corresponding root when the resulting weight remains dominant; if it is no longer dominant, the decomposition consists of a single term.
This simplicity is mirrored in the inverse decompositions: they are obtained by iteratively subtracting the relevant root, summing over all dominant weights encountered, until a non-dominant weight is reached.
We record these two cases in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\lambda={(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})_{\varpi}}\in X^{+}
. Then, we have

(3.3)

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\displaystyle\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{6}=\sum_{i=0}^{\lambda_{2}}q^{i}\mathbf{N}_{{(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}-i)_{\varpi}}}^{5}&\mbox{and }&\displaystyle\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{3}=\sum_{i=0}^{\lambda_{2}}q^{i}\mathbf{N}_{{(\lambda_{1}+i,\lambda_{2}-i)_{\varpi}}}^{2}.\\
\end{array}

In particular, both expressions are positive.

Proof.

Both identities follow by an easy inductive argument on
\lambda_{2}
together with Proposition
3.3
.
∎

We now move our attention to the decomposition of elements of
\mathcal{N}^{5}
in terms of
\mathcal{N}^{4}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\lambda={(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})_{\varpi}}\in X^{+}
and write
\lambda_{1}=3m+r
for
m\geq 0
and
r\in\{0,1,2\}
.
Then, we have

(3.4)

\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{5}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m}q^{i}\mathbf{N}_{{(\lambda_{1}-3i,\lambda_{2}+i)_{\varpi}}}^{4}-q^{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m+\lambda_{2}}q^{2i-1}\mathbf{N}_{{(1,m+\lambda_{2}-i)_{\varpi}}}^{3},&\mbox{if }r=0;\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}q^{i}\mathbf{N}_{{(\lambda_{1}-3i,\lambda_{2}+i)_{\varpi}}}^{4}+q^{m}\sum_{i=0}^{m+\lambda_{2}}q^{2i}\mathbf{N}_{{(1,m+\lambda_{2}-i)_{\varpi}}}^{3},&\mbox{if }r=1;\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m}q^{i}\mathbf{N}_{{(\lambda_{1}-3i,\lambda_{2}+i)_{\varpi}}}^{4},&\mbox{if }r=2,\end{array}\right.

where we make the convention that empty sums are set equal to
0
.

Proof.

Let
\hat{\mathbf{N}}^{5}_{\lambda}
be the right-hand side of (
3.4
).
We must show that
\hat{\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{5}}=\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{5}
for all
\lambda\in X^{+}
.
We proceed by induction on
\lambda_{1}
.
The base cases being
\lambda_{1}\in\{0,1,2\}
.

A direct and easy computation shows that
\hat{\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{5}}=\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{5}
for
\lambda={(0,0)_{\varpi}},{(1,0)_{\varpi}},{(2,0)_{\varpi}}
.
So that we fix
\lambda_{2}\geq 1
and assume that
\hat{\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{5}}=\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{5}
for all
\mu={(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})_{\varpi}}
with
\mu_{1}\in\{0,1,2\}
and
\mu_{2}<\lambda_{2}
.
By definition of the
\hat{\mathbf{N}}
-elements we have

(3.5)

\hat{\mathbf{N}}_{\lambda}^{5}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{4}-q\hat{\mathbf{N}}_{{(1,\lambda_{2}-1)_{\varpi}}}^{5}&\mbox{if }\lambda_{1}=0;\\
\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{3}+q^{2}\hat{\mathbf{N}}_{{(1,\lambda_{2}-1)_{\varpi}}}^{5}&\mbox{if }\lambda_{1}=1;\\
\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{4}&\mbox{if }\lambda_{1}=2.\\
\end{array}\right.

Now using our inductive hypothesis we have
\hat{\mathbf{N}}_{{(1,\lambda_{2}-1)_{\varpi}}}^{5}=\mathbf{N}_{{(1,\lambda_{2}-1)_{\varpi}}}^{5}
.
Then, using Proposition
3.3
to write
\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{3}
and
\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{4}
in terms of
\mathcal{N}^{5}
, we see that in the three cases the right-hand side of (

\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{5}
.
This completes the proof of our base cases.

We now fix
\lambda_{1}\geq 3
and suppose that
\hat{\mathbf{N}_{\mu}^{5}}=\mathbf{N}_{\mu}^{5}
for all

We have

(3.6)

\hat{\mathbf{N}}_{\lambda}^{5}=\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{4}+q\hat{\mathbf{N}}_{{(\lambda_{1}-3,\lambda_{2}+1)_{\varpi}}}^{5}=\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{4}+q\mathbf{N}_{{(\lambda_{1}-3,\lambda_{2}+1)_{\varpi}}}^{5}=\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{5},

where the first equality comes from the definition of the
\hat{\mathbf{N}}
-elements, the second by our inductive hypothesis and the third by Proposition
3.3
.
This finishes our induction and the proof of the lemma.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

The alert reader may have noticed the appearance of

Although one might expect only

Lastly, we describe the expansion of

Lemma 3.7

Let
\lambda={(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})_{\varpi}}\in X^{+}
and write

(3.7)

\displaystyle\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{4}=\sum_{i=0}^{\lambda_{1}}q^{i}\mathbf{N}^{3}_{{(\lambda_{1}-i,\lambda_{2})_{\varpi}}}+q^{\lambda_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}q^{4i-2}\left(\mathbf{N}^{3}_{{(2,\lambda_{2}-2i)_{\varpi}}}+q\mathbf{N}^{3}_{{(1,\lambda_{2}-2i)_{\varpi}}}+q^{2}\mathbf{N}^{3}_{{(0,\lambda_{2}-2i)_{\varpi}}}\right).

Proof.

Let
\hat{\mathbf{N}}^{4}_{\lambda}
be the right-hand side of (

We must show that
\hat{\mathbf{N}}_{\lambda}^{4}=\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{4}
for all
\lambda={(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})_{\varpi}}\in X^{+}
.
We proceed by induction on
\lambda_{2}
.
Let us further assume that
\lambda_{2}
is even.
Our base case is
\lambda_{2}=0
.
Notice that in this case the second row of the expression of

in
\mathcal{N}^{4}
in Proposition

is never required.
On the other hand, since

in this case, the element
\hat{\mathbf{N}}_{\lambda}^{4}
reduces to

(3.8)

\hat{\mathbf{N}}_{\lambda}^{4}=\sum_{i=0}^{\lambda_{1}}q^{i}\mathbf{N}^{3}_{{(\lambda_{1}-i,\lambda_{2})_{\varpi}}}.

Therefore, we are essentially in the same situation of Lemma

.
Thus, an easy inductive argument on
\lambda_{1}
shows that
\hat{\mathbf{N}}_{\lambda}^{4}=\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{4}
.

We now assume that
\lambda_{2}\geq 2
and that the result holds for all even numbers less than
\lambda_{2}

Let us suppose that
\lambda_{1}=0

By the definition of the

(3.9)

\hat{\mathbf{N}}_{\lambda}^{4}=\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{3}+q^{2}\hat{\mathbf{N}}_{{(2,\lambda_{2}-2)_{\varpi}}}^{4}=\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{3}+q^{2}{\mathbf{N}}_{{(2,\lambda_{2}-2)_{\varpi}}}^{4}={\mathbf{N}}_{\lambda}^{4},

(3.10)

This completes the proof of

The case when

3.3.
Atomic Decomposition

In this section we prove that the elements of the canonical basis
\underline{\mathbf{H}}_{\lambda}=\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{6}
have an atomic decomposition for all
\lambda\in X^{+}
.

Proof of
A
.

By Lemmas
3.4
,
3.5
, and
3.7
, it suffices to show that

\mathbf{N}^{5}_{\lambda}
has an atomic decomposition whenever
\lambda_{1}\equiv 0\pmod{3}
.
We first treat the case
\lambda_{1}=0
.
If
\lambda_{2}=0
then
\mathbf{N}^{5}_{\lambda}=\mathbf{N}^{4}_{\lambda}
and there is nothing to prove.
Thus we assume
\lambda_{2}\geq 1
.
By Lemma
3.5
we have

(3.11)

\mathbf{N}^{5}_{{(0,\lambda_{2})_{\varpi}}}=\mathbf{N}^{4}_{{(0,\lambda_{2})_{\varpi}}}-\sum_{i=1}^{\lambda_{2}}q^{2i-1}\,\mathbf{N}^{3}_{{(1,\lambda_{2}-i)_{\varpi}}}.

We now distinguish the parity of
\lambda_{2}
.
Assume that
\lambda_{2}=2m
is even.
(The odd case is analogous and omitted.)
Using
\lambda_{2}=2m
, equation (
3.11
) becomes

(3.12)

\mathbf{N}^{5}_{{(0,\lambda_{2})_{\varpi}}}=\mathbf{N}^{4}_{{(0,\lambda_{2})_{\varpi}}}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}q^{4i-1}\,\mathbf{N}^{3}_{{(1,\lambda_{2}-2i)_{\varpi}}}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}q^{4i-3}\,\mathbf{N}^{3}_{{(1,\lambda_{2}-2i+1)_{\varpi}}}.

On the other hand, Lemma
3.7
yields

(3.13)

\mathbf{N}^{4}_{{(0,\lambda_{2})_{\varpi}}}=\mathbf{N}^{3}_{{(0,\lambda_{2})_{\varpi}}}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}q^{4i-2}\Bigl(\mathbf{N}^{3}_{{(2,\lambda_{2}-2i)_{\varpi}}}+q\,\mathbf{N}^{3}_{{(1,\lambda_{2}-2i)_{\varpi}}}+q^{2}\,\mathbf{N}^{3}_{{(0,\lambda_{2}-2i)_{\varpi}}}\Bigr).

Substituting (

(3.14)

\mathbf{N}^{5}_{{(0,\lambda_{2})_{\varpi}}}=\sum_{i=0}^{m}q^{4i}\,\mathbf{N}^{3}_{{(0,\lambda_{2}-2i)_{\varpi}}}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}q^{4i-3}\,\mathbf{N}^{2}_{{(1,\lambda_{2}-2i+1)_{\varpi}}},

where Proposition

has been used to rewrite some differences of two
\mathbf{N}^{3}
–terms as a
\mathbf{N}^{2}
–term.

We now apply Lemma
3.4
to rewrite each
\mathbf{N}^{3}

\mathbf{N}^{2}
.
After a reordering of the terms, we obtain

(3.15)

\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{5}&=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m}\sum_{j=0}^{\lambda_{2}-2i}q^{4i+j}\mathbf{N}_{{(j,\lambda_{2}-2i+j)_{\varpi}}}^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}q^{4i-3}\mathbf{N}_{{(1,\lambda_{2}-2i+1)_{\varpi}}}^{2}\\

which is manifestly an atomic decomposition.

We now treat the case
\lambda_{1}\geq 3
with
\lambda_{1}\equiv 0\pmod{3}

Suppose inductively that

has an atomic decomposition for all

By Proposition
3.3

(3.16)

\mathbf{N}^{5}_{\lambda}=\mathbf{N}^{4}_{\lambda}+q\,\mathbf{N}^{5}_{{(\lambda_{1}-3,\lambda_{2}+1)_{\varpi}}}.

The element

∎

5.
Computing Kostka–Foulkes polynomials

In this section we explain how the atomic decomposition can be used to compute
Kostka–Foulkes polynomials.

We begin by observing that the two approaches developed in the previous
sections not only establish the atomic decomposition for canonical basis
elements, but in fact yield explicit algorithms for computing these
decompositions.

We now introduce the standard basis of the spherical Hecke algebra

\mathcal{H}^{\mathbf{sph}}
.
Unlike the canonical or atomic bases, the standard basis is not simply the set

\{\mathbf{H}_{\theta(\lambda)}\mid\lambda\in X^{+}\}
, since these elements do not lie in
\mathcal{H}^{\mathbf{sph}}
.
To obtain the correct definition, one must sum over the standard basis
elements of
\mathcal{H}
that lie in the double coset

W_{f}\theta(\lambda)W_{f}
, weighted by the length. Explicitly,

\mathbf{H}_{\lambda}\coloneqq\sum_{w\in W_{f}\theta(\lambda)W_{f}}v^{\ell(\theta(\lambda))-\ell(w)}\,\mathbf{H}_{w}.

With this definition, the standard basis of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathbf{sph}}
is
precisely
\{\mathbf{H}_{\lambda}\mid\lambda\in X^{+}\}
.

It is straightforward to verify that

\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}^{2}\;=\;\mathbf{N}_{\lambda}\;=\;\sum_{\mu\leq\lambda}q^{\operatorname{ht}(\lambda-\mu)}\,\mathbf{H}_{\mu}.

Moreover, if we expand the canonical basis element as

\underline{\mathbf{H}}_{\lambda}\;=\;\sum_{\mu\leq\lambda}K_{\lambda,\mu}(q)\,\mathbf{H}_{\mu},

then

K_{\lambda,\mu}(q)\;=\;h_{\theta(\mu),\,\theta(\lambda)}(v),\qquad\text{with }q=v^{2}.

In other words, the polynomials
K_{\lambda,\mu}(q)
are precisely the
Kostka–Foulkes polynomials.

We now summarize the procedure for computing a Kostka–Foulkes polynomial

K_{\lambda,\mu}(q)
from the atomic decomposition.

(1)

Compute the atomic expansion of the canonical basis element:

\underline{\mathbf{H}}_{\lambda}=\sum_{\nu\leq\lambda}a_{\lambda,\nu}(q)\,\mathbf{N}_{\nu}.

(2)

Determine the set of weights
\nu
appearing in this expansion such
that
\mu\leq\nu
.

(3)

Finally,

K_{\lambda,\mu}(q)=\sum_{\nu}q^{\operatorname{ht}(\nu-\mu)}\,a_{\lambda,\nu}(q),

where the sum runs over all
\nu
satisfying
\mu\leq\nu
in step (2).

We implemented this algorithm in
SageMath
10.6, and the corresponding
code can be found at (
github.com/bamuniz/AtomicDecompositionG2
). To obtain the atomic decomposition we have used the formulas in §
3.2
.
Although the code is largely
self-explanatory, we conclude this section with an example illustrating its
usage.

Abstract
. The cave polynomial of a polymatroid was recently introduced and used to study the syzygies of polymatroidal ideals. We study the combinatorial relationships between three formulas for the cave polynomial. As an application, we interpret the Snapper polynomial in terms of these three formulas.

1.
Introduction

Polymatroids are a generalization of matroids, and objects of interest in combinatorial optimization. For more about polymatroids, we point to
[
Sch03
]
. The cave polynomial of a polymatroid was recently introduced in
[
CMS25
]
and used to settle two conjectures regarding polymatroids. The first was a conjecture of Bandari, Bayati, and Herzog from 2018, stating that homological shift ideals of polymatroidal ideals are again polymatroidal ideals. The second was a conjecture of Castillo, Cid-Ruiz, Mohammadi, and Montaño, stating that the Möbius support of a polymatroid is a generalized polymatroid. The cave polynomial, which is relatively simple to compute given a polymatroid, connects and resolves both of these conjectures.

We study three known methods for computing the cave polynomial. While it is known that these three methods give the same result, prior proofs of the equivalences rely on deep machinery from algebra and algebraic geometry (see
§1.1
for a recollection of the realizable case and
§1.2
for the arbitrary case). The main results of this paper (
Theorem 1.5
,
Theorem 1.6
, and
Theorem 1.7
) give combinatorial relationships between the three methods.

We use
\mathbf{e}_{i}
to denote the
i
th standard basis vector in
\mathbb{N}^{p}
. For any point
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
, we set
|\mathbf{n}|\coloneq n_{1}+\cdots+n_{p}
. The base and independence polytopes of a polymatroid
\mathscr{P}
are denoted by
B(\mathscr{P})
and
I(\mathscr{P})
, respectively. We use
\rk(\mathscr{P})
to mean the rank of the polymatroid. See
Section 2
for precise definitions of polymatroids and related concepts.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathscr{P}
be a polymatroid on
[p]\coloneqq\{1,2,\ldots,p\}
.

(1)

The
indicator function
of
\mathscr{P}
is the function
\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{P}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{N}
given by

\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{n})=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }\mathbf{n}\in B(\mathscr{P}),\\
0&\text{ if }\mathbf{n}\notin B(\mathscr{P}).\end{cases}

(2)

The
cave polynomial
of
\mathscr{P}
is

\cave_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{t})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|\mathbf{n}|=\rk(\mathscr{P})\end{subarray}}\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{n})\prod_{i=1}^{p-1}\left(1-\max_{i<j}\{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{i}+\mathbf{e}_{j})\}t_{i}^{-1}\right)\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{n}}.

This definition of the cave polynomial is based on the combinatorial notion of caves, introduced in
[
CCRMM22
, Section 5]
. There are three other polynomials of interest from the literature. We compute an example of each of these polynomials in
Section 2
,
Section 3
, and
Section 3
. The first comes from the combinatorial ideas of caves and stalactites, introduced in
[
CCRMM22
]
(see
Section 2
for more detail).

Definition 1.2
.

Let
c_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathscr{P})
denote the number of stalactites of
\mathscr{P}
containing
\mathbf{n}
. We define the
stalactite polynomial
of
\mathscr{P}
to be

\rm{Stal}_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{t})=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in I(\mathscr{P})}(-1)^{\rk(\mathscr{P})-|\mathbf{n}|}c_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathscr{P})\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{n}}.

The second comes from the Snapper polynomial of a matroid whose restriction polymatroid is
\mathscr{P}
(see
§1.3
). The formula for the Snapper polynomial of a matroid was given in
[
LLPP24
, Corollary 7.5]
. This in fact depends only on
\mathscr{P}
, not on the matroid (see
[
EL23
, Corollary 2.9]
).

Definition 1.3
.

The
box polynomial
of
\mathscr{P}
is given by

\textrm{Box}_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{t})=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in I(\mathscr{P})}\prod_{i=1}^{p}(t_{i}^{n_{i}}-t_{i}^{\max\{0,\ n_{i}-1\}}).

The third polynomial describes the class of a multiplicity-free variety
X
in the Grothendieck ring
K(\mathbb{P})
(where
\mathbb{P}
is a product of projective spaces, see
§1.1
). The coefficients describing this class as a linear combination of Schubert classes are particular values of the Möbius function of a poset associated to the polymatroid
\mathscr{P}
(see
Section 3
for the precise definition). We use
\mu_{P}(-,-)
to denote the Möbius function of the poset, and write
\mu_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{n})\coloneqq-\mu_{P}(\mathbf{n},\hat{1})
.

Definition 1.4
.

The
Möbius polynomial
of
\mathscr{P}
is

\textrm{M\"{o}b}_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{t})=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in I(\mathscr{P})}\mu_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{n})\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{n}}.

We can now state the main result of the paper.

Theorem 1.5
.

The three polynomials above are all equal to the cave polynomial. That is, for any polymatroid
\mathscr{P}
, we have

\cave_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{t})=\Stal_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{t})=\mathrm{Box}_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{t})=\Mob_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{t}).

A certain class of polymatroids, called realizable polymatroids, are related to multiplicity-free varieties (in the sense of
[
Bri03
]
) in the following way: for any realizable polymatroid
\mathscr{P}
, one can construct a multiplicity-free variety
X_{\mathscr{P}}\subseteq\mathbb{P}\coloneqq\mathbb{P}^{m_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{m_{p}}
whose multidegree support is
\mathscr{P}
(see
[
CCRMM22
, proof of Proposition 7.15]
for the construction). This construction is essential to prior understanding of the cave polynomial in the realizable case. We will give a brief summary of the proof of this result in the realizable case, and then in the arbitrary case. These proofs rely heavily on algebraic geometry.

1.1.
The realizable case

Let
\mathscr{P}
be a polymatroid and
X_{\mathscr{P}}
the multiplicity-free variety with
\mathscr{P}
as its multidegree support. Then the coefficients of the three polynomials defined above appear in two places: as the coefficients of the multigraded Hilbert polynomial of
X_{\mathscr{P}}
written in terms of the binomial expressions
\binom{t_{1}+a_{1}}{a_{1}}\cdots\binom{t_{p}+a_{p}}{a_{p}}
, and as the coefficients of the expression of
[X_{\mathscr{P}}]
as a linear combination of the classes of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{a_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{a_{p}}}
in the Grothendieck ring
K(\mathbb{P})
. In
[
CCRMM22
, Theorem 6.9]
, it was shown that the coefficients of the stalactite polynomial of
\mathscr{P}
are also the Hilbert coefficients of the variety
X_{\mathscr{P}}
. The proof uses a shelling order of the simplicial complex
\Delta(J)
associated to the generic initial ideal
J=\mathrm{gin}(\mathfrak{P})
of the
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded prime ideal
\mathfrak{P}
associated to
X_{\mathscr{P}}
. Conca and Tsakiris proved in
[
CT22
, Theorem 3.9]
that the multigraded Hilbert function is given by

\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in I(\mathscr{P})}\prod_{i=1}^{p}\binom{t_{i}+n_{i}-1}{n_{i}},

i.e. that its coefficients are the same as those of the box polynomial. It was shown by Knutson in
[
Knu09
, Theorem 3]
that the coefficients describing the class of a multiplicity-free variety
X
in the Grothendieck ring as a linear combination of Schubert classes are given by the Möbius function of an order ideal in the Bruhat order. In
[
CCRMM22
, Theorem 6.12(iii)]
, it was also proven that the Hilbert coefficients satisfy a Möbius-like recurrence, i.e. that the stalactite polynomial is equal to the Möbius polynomial. This proof uses properties of multiplicity-free varieties, namely their behavior under cuts by generic hyperplanes and projections.

1.2.
The arbitrary case

Brion showed that any multiplicity-free variety
X_{\mathscr{P}}
has a flat degeneration to a variety
Y_{\mathscr{P}}

[
Bri03
]
. In
[
CMS25
, Definition 2.4]
we call this the polymatroidal multiprojective variety of
\mathscr{P}
. For any polymatroid
\mathscr{P}
, we can define its dual polymatroidal ideal
J_{\mathscr{P}}
, and then
Y_{\mathscr{P}}=V(J_{\mathscr{P}})
. The generic initial ideal
J
used in the proof that the stalactite coefficients are equal to the Hilbert coefficients coincides with
J_{\mathscr{P}}
, so this is true in the arbitrary case as well. It is known that the cave polynomial of a polymatroid is related to the Snapper polynomial of a polymatroid (see
§1.3
). Eur and Larson gave the following formula for the Snapper polynomial in
[
EL23
, Proposition 2.8]
:

\mathrm{Snapp}_{\mathscr{P}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in I(\mathscr{P})}\prod_{i=1}^{p}\binom{t_{i}+n_{i}-1}{n_{i}}.

This shows that the Box polynomial is equal to the cave polynomial in the arbitrary case. The flat degeneration of
X_{\mathscr{P}}
to
Y_{\mathscr{P}}
also means that we can use
Y_{\mathscr{P}}
to compute the Hilbert polynomial, so the fact that the Hilbert coefficients are given by a Möbius function is still true in the arbitrary case.

1.3.
Combinatorial Approach

The primary goal of this paper is to further combinatorialize the proof of
Theorem 1.5
. In particular, we give an explicit formula for the Möbius function and show that it computes the coefficients of the formula given in
Section 1
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\mathbf{m}\leq\mathbf{n}<\hat{1}
in
P
. Then

\mu_{P}(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n})=\begin{cases}(-1)^{j}&\text{if }\mathbf{m}=\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{i_{1}}-\cdots-\mathbf{e}_{i_{j}}\text{ for some distinct }i_{1},\ldots,i_{j}\in[p],\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We also show that the number of stalactites containing a point is given by a Möbius-like recurrence.

Theorem 1.7
.

For each
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
, the number of stalactites
c_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathscr{P})
obeys the following recurrence:

c_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathscr{P})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\mathbf{n}\in B(\mathscr{P}),\\
1-\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{m}>\mathbf{n}}c_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathscr{P})&\text{if }\mathbf{n}\in I(\mathscr{P})\setminus B(\mathscr{P}),\\
0&\text{if }\mathbf{n}\notin I(\mathscr{P}).\end{cases}

That is,
c_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathscr{P})=\mu_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{n}).

One application of these results is to the Snapper polynomial.

Remark 1.8
.

The augmented
K
-ring
K(M)
of a matroid was introduced by Larson, Li, Payne, and Proudfoot
[
LLPP24
]
. Let
M
be a matroid on ground set
E
with subsets
S_{1},\ldots,S_{p}
such that the restriction polymatroid is
\mathscr{P}
(with cage
\mathbf{m}
). That is,

(1)

the subsets
S_{1},\ldots,S_{p}
form a partition of
E
,

(2)

|S_{i}|=m_{i}
for all
i\in[p]
,

(3)

The rank function of the matroid,
\rk_{M}:2^{E}\to\mathbb{N}
is preserved by the product of symmetric groups
\mathfrak{S}_{S_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathfrak{S}_{S_{p}}
, and

(4)

For each
J\subseteq[p]
, we have

\rk_{\mathscr{P}}(J)=\rk_{M}\left(\bigcup_{j\in J}S_{j}\right).

Then, following the construction in
[
LLPP24
]
, the Snapper polynomial of
\mathscr{P}
is

\mathrm{Snapp}_{\mathscr{P}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})\coloneqq\chi\left(M,\mathcal{L}_{S_{1}}^{\otimes t_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{L}_{S_{p}}^{\otimes t_{p}}\right),

where
\chi(M,-)\colon K(M)\to\mathbb{Z}
denotes the Euler characteristic map and
\mathcal{L}_{S_{i}}
denotes a line bundle.

It was also shown in
[
CMS25
]
that
\mathrm{Snapp}_{\mathscr{P}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})=\mathfrak{b}(\cave_{\mathscr{P}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}))
, where
\mathfrak{b}
is the polynomial map sending monomials to their corresponding products of binomial expressions, that is,

\mathfrak{b}\colon\mathbb{Q}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]\to\mathbb{Q}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]\ \text{ is given by }\ \mathfrak{b}\colon t_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots t_{p}^{n_{p}}\mapsto\binom{t_{1}+n_{1}}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{t_{p}+n_{p}}{n_{p}}.

Recall that Eur and Larson showed that
\mathrm{Snapp}_{\mathscr{P}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})=\mathfrak{b}(\mathrm{Box}_{\mathscr{P}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}))
[
EL23
, Proposition 2.8]
.

From
Theorem 1.5
, we get the following equalities.

Corollary 1.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{b}
be the map defined above. We have

\mathrm{Snapp}_{\mathscr{P}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})=\mathfrak{b}(\textnormal{M\"{o}b}_{\mathscr{P}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}))=\mathfrak{b}(\Stal_{\mathscr{P}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})).

2.
Preliminaries

Here we state the relevant definitions for the remainder of the paper. We first establish some notation. For a positive integer
p
let
[p]
denote the set
\{1,2,\ldots,p\}
. We use
\mathbf{e}_{i}
to denote the
i
th standard basis vector in
\mathbb{N}^{p}
. Given a subset
J=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\}\subseteq[p]
we say
\mathbf{e}_{J}\coloneqq\mathbf{e}_{j_{1}}+\cdots+\mathbf{e}_{j_{k}}
. For any point
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
, we say
|\mathbf{n}|\coloneq n_{1}+\cdots+n_{p}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
polymatroid
on
[p]
with
cage

\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{p})
is given by a function
\rk_{\mathscr{P}}\colon 2^{[p]}\to\mathbb{N}
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

\rk_{\mathscr{P}}(\varnothing)=0
,

(2)

\rk_{\mathscr{P}}(\{i\})\leq m_{i}
,

(3)

If
I_{1}\subseteq I_{2}\subseteq[p]
, then
\rk_{\mathscr{P}}(I_{1})\leq\rk_{\mathscr{P}}(I_{2})
, and

(4)

For all
I_{1},I_{2}\subseteq[p]
,
\rk_{\mathscr{P}}(I_{1})+\rk_{\mathscr{P}}(I_{2})\geq\rk_{\mathscr{P}}(I_{1}\cup I_{2})+\rk_{\mathscr{P}}(I_{1}\cap I_{2})
.

Then
\rk_{\mathscr{P}}
is called the
rank function of
\mathscr{P}
,
and the
rank of the polymatroid
is
\rk(\mathscr{P})\coloneqq\rk_{\mathscr{P}}([p])
.

Under this definition, a
matroid
is a polymatroid with cage
(1,\ldots,1)
. There is a second definition which will be more convenient for us to use. The two definitions were shown to be equivalent in
[
HH02
, Theorem 3.4]
.

Definition 2.2
.

We say a finite set
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{p}
is
homogeneous
if, for all
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in A
, we have
|\mathbf{a}|=|\mathbf{b}|
. A
polymatroid
is a finite homogeneous set of points
\mathscr{P}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{p}
that is M-convex, i.e.,

For each
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\mathscr{P}
and
i\in[p]
such that
u_{i}>v_{i}
, there is
j\in[p]
such that
u_{j}<v_{j}
and
\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{e}_{i}+\mathbf{e}_{j}\in\mathscr{P}
.

If
\mathscr{P}
is actually a
matroid
, then all entries of each
\mathbf{n}\in\mathscr{P}
are either
0
or
1
.

Example 2.3
.

We will use the polymatroid
\mathscr{P}=\{(0,3),(1,2),(2,1)\}
as a running example throughout the rest of the paper.

The cave polynomial is nonhomogeneous, so its support cannot be a polymatroid. Instead we use a generalization of a polymatroid which need not be homogeneous.

Definition 2.4
.

A
generalized polymatroid
is a finite set
\mathscr{G}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{p}
whose homogenization is a polymatroid. Equivalently, following
Section 2
, a generalized polymatroid is a set
\mathscr{G}
with the following two properties:

(1)

For each
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\mathscr{G}
with
u_{i}>v_{i}
, one of the following is true:

(a)

There exists an index
j\in[p]
such that
u_{j}<v_{j}
and
\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{e}_{i}+\mathbf{e}_{j},\ \mathbf{v}+\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}\in\mathscr{G}
, or

(b)

|\mathbf{u}|>|\mathbf{v}|
and
\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{e}_{i},\ \mathbf{v}+\mathbf{e}_{i}\in\mathscr{G}
.

(2)

For each
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\mathscr{G}
with
|\mathbf{u}|>|\mathbf{v}|
, there is
j\in[p]
such that
u_{j}>v_{j}
and both
\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{e}_{j}
and
\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{e}_{j}
are in
\mathscr{G}
.

Definition 2.5
.

The
independence polytope of
\mathscr{P}
is

I(\mathscr{P})\coloneqq\Big\{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\bigm|\sum_{i\in I}n_{i}\leq\rk(I)\text{ for all }I\subseteq[p]\Big\}.

The
base polytope of
\mathscr{P}
is

B(\mathscr{P})\coloneqq I(\mathscr{P})\cap\Big\{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\bigm|\sum_{i=1}^{p}n_{i}=\rk(\mathscr{P})\Big\}.

Equivalently, following the M-convexity definition, the base polytope is the convex hull of the points in the polymatroid, i.e.
B(\mathscr{P})=\conv(\mathscr{P})
. The independence polytope is the set of points below
B(\mathscr{P})
in the nonnegative orthant, that is,
I(\mathscr{P})=(B(\mathscr{P})+\mathbb{R}^{p}_{\leq 0})\cap\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{p}
.

Now we give the definitions of neighbors, stalactites, and caves. The construction of the cave polynomial mimics the construction of a cave as the union of stalactites. These definitions were first stated in
[
CCRMM22
, Section 5]
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\mathscr{P}
be a polymatroid and
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in B(\mathscr{P})
.

(1)

We say that
\mathbf{u}
is a
neighbor
of
\mathbf{v}
in the
(-\ell,j)
direction if
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{e}_{\ell}+\mathbf{e}_{j}
for some
\ell,j\in[p]
.

(2)

Let
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{m}
be distinct elements of
\supp(\mathbf{u})
, that is,
u_{\ell_{i}}\neq 0
for
i=1,\ldots,m
. Define

\St(\mathbf{u};\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{m})=\Big\{\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{e}_{J}\mid J\subseteq[m]\Big\}.

(3)

Let
V\subseteq\mathscr{P}
. Consider the set
J
of indices
\ell
such that there is
\mathbf{w}\in V
which is a neighbor of
\mathbf{u}
in the
(-\ell,j)
direction for some
j\in[p]
. We define
\St(\mathbf{u};V)=\St(\mathbf{u};J)
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
A
be a finite set in
\mathbb{N}^{p}
.
Let
\max(A)
denote
\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf{a}\in A}\{|\mathbf{a}|\}
. The
top
elements of
A
are
A^{\rm{top}}\coloneqq\{\mathbf{a}\in A\mid|\mathbf{a}|=\max(A)\}
.
For any
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
, the
truncation of
A
at
\mathbf{b}
is
A_{\mathbf{b}}\coloneqq\{\mathbf{n}\in A\mid\mathbf{n}\geq\mathbf{b}\}
.

Definition 2.8
.

A finite set
C\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
is a
cave
if each of the following conditions is satisfied:

(1)

The top elements
C^{\mathrm{top}}
form a polymatroid.

(2)

Let
\prec
denote any lexicographic order on
\mathbb{N}^{p}
and order the elements of
C^{\mathrm{top}}
as
\mathbf{a}_{1}\prec\cdots\prec\mathbf{a}_{|C^{\mathrm{top}}|}
. Then

C=\bigcup_{i=1}^{|C^{\mathrm{top}}|}\St(\mathbf{a}_{i};\{\mathbf{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{a}_{i-1}\}).

(3)

For each nonzero
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
, the truncation
C_{\mathbf{b}}
is a generalized polymatroid.

Stalactites can be computed using any lex ordering. While the individual stalactites depend on the choice of ordering, the stalactite polynomial does not.

Theorem 2.9
.

For any polymatroid
\mathscr{P}
, we have
\cave_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{t})=\Stal_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{t})
.

Proof.

Here we prove the statement for the stalactite polynomial computed using the standard lex order. In
Theorem 4.3
we will show that the stalactite polynomial is equal to the Möbius polynomial, which does not depend on a lex ordering. Order the elements of
B(\mathscr{P})
as
\mathbf{a}_{1}\prec\cdots\prec\mathbf{a}_{r}
in the standard lex order. We show that the coefficient of
\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{m}}
in the cave polynomial is equal to
(-1)^{\rk(\mathscr{P})-|\mathbf{m}|}c_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathscr{P})
. First we observe that the formula for the cave polynomial is cancellation-free, because the sign of each term is determined by
|\mathbf{m}|
, and the coefficient of
\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{m}}
will have sign
(-1)^{\rk(\mathscr{P})-|\mathbf{m}|}
. The product

\prod_{i=1}^{p-1}\left(1-\max_{i<j}\{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{i}+\mathbf{e}_{j})\}t_{i}^{-1}\right)\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{n}}

appears in the sum if and only if
\mathbf{n}\in B(\mathscr{P})
. The monomial
\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{m}}
appears in the same product if and only if there exists a subset
J\subseteq[p]
such that
\mathbf{m}=\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{J}
and
\mathbf{n}
has neighbors of the form
\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{i}+\mathbf{e}_{j}
, with
j>i
, for each
i\in J
. On the other hand,
\mathbf{m}\in\St(\mathbf{a}_{k};\{\mathbf{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{a}_{k-1}\})
if and only if there exists a subset
J\subseteq[p]
such that
\mathbf{m}=\mathbf{a}_{k}-\mathbf{e}_{J}
and
\mathbf{a}_{k}
has neighbors of the form
\mathbf{a}_{k}-\mathbf{e}_{i}+\mathbf{e}_{j}\in\{\mathbf{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{a}_{k-1}\}
for all
i\in J
. Notice that this means
\mathbf{a}_{k}-\mathbf{e}_{i}+\mathbf{e}_{j}\prec\mathbf{a}_{k}
in the lex order, which is equivalent to the condition that
i<j
. So the coefficients of the two polynomials are equal.
∎

Example 2.10
.

For
\mathscr{P}=\big\{(0,3),(1,2),(2,1)\big\}
, we can use the ordering
(0,3)\prec(1,2)\prec(2,1)
and then we have

\displaystyle\St\big((0,3);\varnothing\big)

\displaystyle=\{(0,3)\}.

\displaystyle\St\big((1,2);\{(0,3)\}\big)

\displaystyle=\{(1,2),(0,2)\}.

\displaystyle\St\big((2,1);\{(0,3),(1,2)\}\big)

\displaystyle=\{(2,1),(1,1)\}.

So
\Stal_{\mathscr{P}}(t_{1},t_{2})=t_{2}^{3}+t_{1}t_{2}^{2}-t_{2}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}t_{2}-t_{1}t_{2}
.
By the definition of the cave polynomial,

\displaystyle\cave_{\mathscr{P}}(t_{1},t_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|\mathbf{n}|=\rk(\mathscr{P})\end{subarray}}\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{n})\left(1-\mathbbm{1}_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{1}+\mathbf{e}_{2})t_{1}^{-1}\right)t_{1}^{n_{1}}t_{2}^{n_{2}}.

\displaystyle=\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{P}}((0,3))\left(1-\mathbbm{1}_{\mathscr{P}}((-1,4))t_{1}^{-1}\right)t_{2}^{3}+\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{P}}((1,2))\left(1-\mathbbm{1}_{\mathscr{P}}((0,3))t_{1}^{-1}\right)t_{1}^{1}t_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{P}}((2,1))\left(1-\mathbbm{1}_{\mathscr{P}}((1,2))t_{1}^{-1}\right)t_{1}^{2}t_{2}^{1}+\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{P}}((3,0))\left(1-\mathbbm{1}_{\mathscr{P}}((2,1))t_{1}^{-1}\right)t_{1}^{3}

\displaystyle=t_{2}^{3}+(1-t_{1}^{-1})t_{1}t_{2}^{2}+(1-t_{1}^{-1})t_{1}^{2}t_{2}+0

\displaystyle=t_{2}^{3}+t_{1}t_{2}^{2}-t_{2}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}t_{2}-t_{1}t_{2}.

3.
Formula for the Möbius Function of a polymatroid

In this section, we define and study the Möbius function of a polymatroid. In particular, we prove an explicit formula for this Möbius function which allows us to show that
\textrm{Box}_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{t})=\textrm{M\"{o}b}_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{t})
. We define a poset related to each polymatroid
\mathscr{P}
. Let
P
be the poset with underlying set
I(\mathscr{P})\cap\mathbb{N}^{p}
and a maximal element
\hat{1}
adjoined, with relations given by the componentwise order. That is,

\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{p})\leq\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{p})\text{ if }a_{i}\leq b_{i}\text{ for each }i\in[p].

Then the Möbius function of
P
is the function
\mu_{P}\colon\mathrm{Int}(P)\to\mathbb{Z}
on the set of closed intervals of
P
, defined by the relation

\mu_{P}(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n})\coloneqq\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }\mathbf{m}=\mathbf{n},\\
-\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{m}\leq\mathbf{a}<\mathbf{n}}\mu_{P}(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{a})&\text{ if }\mathbf{m}<\mathbf{n},\\
0&\text{ if }\mathbf{m}\not\leq\mathbf{n}.\end{cases}

The only Möbius values which appear as coefficients in the Möbius polynomial are those of the form
\mu_{P}(\mathbf{n},\hat{1})
. In fact the coefficient of
\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{n}}
in
\Mob_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{t})
is
-\mu_{P}(\mathbf{n},\hat{1})
. For this reason we use the notation
\mu_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{n})\coloneqq-\mu_{P}(\mathbf{n},\hat{1})
. From the relation above we get

(1)

\mu_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{n})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\mathbf{n}\in B(\mathscr{P}),\\
1-\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{m}>\mathbf{n}}\mu_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{m})&\text{if }\mathbf{n}\in I(\mathscr{P})\setminus B(\mathscr{P}),\\
0&\text{if }\mathbf{n}\notin I(\mathscr{P}).\end{cases}

In order to compare the Möbius values to the coefficients of the box polynomial, we give an explicit formula for this Möbius function.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathbf{m}\leq\mathbf{n}<\hat{1}
in
P
. Then

\mu_{P}(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n})=\begin{cases}(-1)^{j}&\text{if }\mathbf{m}=\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{i_{1}}-\cdots-\mathbf{e}_{i_{j}}\text{ for some distinct }i_{1},\ldots,i_{j}\in[p],\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
P
be the poset described above, and let
\mathbf{m}\leq\mathbf{n}<\hat{1}
in
P
. Let
\ell
denote the length of the interval
[\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}]\subseteq P
. We will induct on
\ell
. If
\ell=0
then
\mathbf{m}=\mathbf{n}
, so
\mu_{P}(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n})=1=(-1)^{0}
. If
\ell=1
, then
\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{e}_{i}
for some
i\in[p]
. Substituting, we obtain

\mu_{P}(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n})=-\mu_{P}(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{m})=-1=(-1)^{1}.

Now suppose
\ell\geq 1
and that the formula is valid for intervals of length less than
\ell
. We have two cases. First suppose that
\mathbf{m}=\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{i_{1}}-\cdots-\mathbf{e}_{i_{\ell}}
for some
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{\ell}\}\subseteq[p]
. Then each element
\mathbf{a}
in the interval
[\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}]
is of the form
\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{e}_{J}
where
J\subseteq\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{\ell}\}
. Thus by induction,

\mu_{P}(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n})=-\sum_{\mathbf{m}\leq\mathbf{a}<\mathbf{n}}\mu_{P}(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{a})=-\sum_{J\subset\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{\ell}\}}\mu_{P}(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{e}_{J})=-\sum_{J\subset\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{\ell}\}}(-1)^{|J|}=-\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}(-1)^{j}\binom{\ell}{j}.

Since the alternating sum of binomial coefficients is equal to zero, we have

-\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}(-1)^{j}\binom{\ell}{j}=(-1)^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{\ell}=(-1)^{\ell},

which concludes the first case. In the second case, we assume
\mathbf{m}\neq\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{i_{1}}-\cdots-\mathbf{e}_{i_{\ell}}
for any subset
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{\ell}\}\subseteq[p]
. Let
A=\{i\in[p]\mid\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{e}_{i}<\mathbf{n}\}
. Notice that
|A|<\ell
. Let
B=\{\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{e}_{J}\in P\mid J\subseteq A\}
. If
\mathbf{m}\leq\mathbf{a}<\mathbf{n}
and
\mathbf{a}\notin B
, then
\mu_{P}(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{a})=0
by induction. So we have

\mu_{P}(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n})=-\sum_{\mathbf{m}\leq\mathbf{a}<\mathbf{n}}\mu_{P}(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{a})=-\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in B}\mu_{P}(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{a})=-\sum_{J\subseteq A}(-1)^{|J|}=-\sum_{j=0}^{|A|}(-1)^{j}\binom{|A|}{j}=0.

This completes the proof.
∎

Example 3.2
.

The polymatroid
\mathscr{P}=\big\{(0,3),(1,2),(2,1)\big\}
corresponds to the poset with the following Hasse diagram and Möbius values:

\hat{1}
(0,3)
(1,2)
(2,1)
(0,2)
(1,1)
(2,0)
(0,1)
(1,0)
(0,0)

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathscr{P}}\big((0,3)\big)

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathscr{P}}\big((1,2)\big)

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathscr{P}}\big((2,1)\big)

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathscr{P}}\big((0,2)\big)

\displaystyle=-1

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathscr{P}}\big((1,1)\big)

\displaystyle=-1

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathscr{P}}\big((2,0)\big)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathscr{P}}\big((0,1)\big)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathscr{P}}\big((1,0)\big)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathscr{P}}\big((0,0)\big)

\displaystyle=0

Therefore the Möbius polynomial of
\mathscr{P}
is
\Mob_{\mathscr{P}}(t_{1},t_{2})=t_{2}^{3}+t_{1}t_{2}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}t_{2}-t_{2}^{2}-t_{1}t_{2}
.

As a consequence of
Theorem 3.1
, we get the following result.

Theorem 3.3
.

For any polymatroid
\mathscr{P}
, we have
\mathrm{Box}_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{t})=\Mob_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{t})
.

Proof.

We will compare coefficients of the two polynomials. First, notice that, for a fixed
\mathbf{n}\in I(\mathscr{P})
, the coefficient of
\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{m}}
in the product

\prod_{i=1}^{p}\big(t_{i}^{n_{i}}-t_{i}^{\max\{0,n_{i}-1\}}\big)

is equal to
(-1)^{|J|}
if
\mathbf{m}=\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{J}\text{ for some }J\subseteq[p]
and
0
otherwise. That is, the coefficient of
\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{m}}
in the product is
\mu_{P}(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n})
. Thus when we sum over the independence polytope, we get that the coefficient of
\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{m}}
in
\mathrm{Box}_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{t})
is
\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in I(\mathscr{P})}\mu_{P}(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n})
. The coefficient of
\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{m}}
in the Möbius polynomial is
\mu_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{m})
, by definition. We have

\mu_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{m})=-\mu_{P}(\mathbf{m},\hat{1})=\sum_{\mathbf{m}\leq\mathbf{n}<\hat{1}}\mu_{P}(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}).

Since
\mu_{P}(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n})=0
for
\mathbf{n}\in I(\mathscr{P})
with
\mathbf{m}\not\leq\mathbf{n}
, this is the same as
\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in I(\mathscr{P})}\mu_{P}(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n})
. Hence the two polynomials have the same coefficients, so they are equal.

∎

Example 3.4
.

For
\mathscr{P}=\big\{(0,3),(1,2),(2,1)\big\}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Box}_{\mathscr{P}}(t_{1},t_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in I(\mathscr{P})}\big(t_{1}^{n_{1}}-t_{1}^{\max\{0,n_{1}-1\}}\big)\big(t_{2}^{n_{2}}-t_{2}^{\max\{0,n_{2}-1\}}\big)

\displaystyle=(1)(t_{2}^{3}-t_{2}^{2})+(t_{1}-1)(t_{2}^{2}-t_{2})+(t_{1}^{2}-t_{1})(t_{2}-1)+(1)(t_{2}^{2}-t_{2})+(t_{1}-1)(t_{2}-1)

\displaystyle\quad+(t_{1}^{2}-t_{1})(1)+(1)(t_{2}-1)+(t_{1}-1)(1)+(1)(1)

\displaystyle=t_{2}^{3}-t_{2}^{2}+t_{1}t_{2}^{2}-t_{1}t_{2}+t_{1}^{2}t_{2}.

4.
Stalactites are counted by a Möbius function

In order to prove that
\rm{Stal}_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{t})=\textrm{M\"{o}b}_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{t})
, we examine the coefficients of the stalactite polynomial. That is, we show that the number of stalactites containing some
\mathbf{n}\in I(\mathscr{P})
is counted by the Möbius function. Let
c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathscr{P})
denote the signed number of stalactites of the polymatroid
\mathscr{P}
containing
\mathbf{n}
, i.e.

c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathscr{P})=(-1)^{\rk(\mathscr{P})-|\mathbf{n}|}\#\Big\{i\mid\mathbf{n}\in\St\big(\mathbf{a}_{i};\{\mathbf{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{a}_{i-1}\}\big)\Big\}.

Then
c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathscr{P})
is also the coefficient of
\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{n}}
in the stalactite polynomial. We show that, for each
\mathbf{n}\in I(\mathscr{P})
,
c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathscr{P})=\mu_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{n})
. We first prove two lemmas that will simplify the proof of
Theorem 4.3
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathscr{P}
be a polymatroid and
\mathbf{n}\in I(\mathscr{P})
. Then
\mathscr{P}_{\mathbf{n}}\coloneqq\mathscr{P}\cap(\mathbf{n}+\mathbb{N}^{p})
is a polymatroid (called the truncation of
\mathscr{P}
at
\mathbf{n}
).

Proof.

Since
\mathscr{P}_{\mathbf{n}}
is a subset of the finite homogeneous set
\mathscr{P}
, we know that
\mathscr{P}_{\mathbf{n}}
is finite and homogeneous as well. Thus, it suffices to check M-convexity of
\mathscr{P}_{\mathbf{n}}
. Suppose
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\mathscr{P}_{\mathbf{n}}
with
u_{i}>v_{i}
. Then the same is true in
\mathscr{P}
, so by M-convexity of
\mathscr{P}
there is an index
j\in[p]
such that
u_{j}<v_{j}
and
\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{e}_{i}+\mathbf{e}_{j}\in\mathscr{P}
. All we need to check is that
\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{e}_{i}+\mathbf{e}_{j}\in\mathscr{P}_{\mathbf{n}}
, i.e., that
\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{e}_{i}+\mathbf{e}_{j}\geq\mathbf{n}
in the componentwise order. Since
\mathbf{u}\in\mathscr{P}_{\mathbf{n}}
by assumption, we know
\mathbf{u}\geq\mathbf{n}
. The only entry we need to check is the
i
th. Since
\mathbf{v}\in\mathscr{P}_{\mathbf{n}}
and
u_{i}>v_{i}
, we have that
u_{i}-1\geq v_{i}\geq n_{i}
. Thus
\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{e}_{i}+\mathbf{e}_{j}\geq\mathbf{n}
, as we wanted.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathscr{P}
and
\mathscr{P}_{\mathbf{n}}
be as above. Then for all
\mathbf{m}\in\mathscr{P}_{\mathbf{n}}
, we have
c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathscr{P})=c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathscr{P}_{\mathbf{n}})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{m}\in\mathscr{P}_{\mathbf{n}}
. We show that the stalactites which contain
\mathbf{m}
in
\mathscr{P}
also contain
\mathbf{m}
in
\mathscr{P}_{\mathbf{n}}
. First, note that
\mathscr{P}_{\mathbf{n}}\coloneqq\mathscr{P}\cap(\mathbf{n}+\mathbb{N}^{p})
must contain
\mathscr{P}\cap(\mathbf{m}+\mathbb{N}^{p})
. Therefore, in order to show that an element of
\mathscr{P}
is in
\mathscr{P}_{\mathbf{n}}
, it suffices to show that the element is greater than or equal to
\mathbf{m}
in the componentwise order. Suppose that
\mathbf{m}\in St(\mathbf{a}_{i};\{\mathbf{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{a}_{i-1}\})
in
\mathscr{P}
. Then
\mathbf{m}=\mathbf{a}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{J}
for some
J\subseteq[p]
, so
\mathbf{a}_{i}\geq\mathbf{m}
and thus
\mathbf{a}_{i}\in\mathscr{P}_{\mathbf{n}}
. The only neighbors of
\mathbf{a}_{i}
which must be in
\mathscr{P}_{\mathbf{n}}
in order for
\mathbf{m}
to be in the stalactite in
\mathscr{P}_{\mathbf{n}}
are those in the
(-\ell,j)
directions for
\ell\in J
. Each such neighbor is of the form
\mathbf{a}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{\ell}+\mathbf{e}_{j}
. We write
\mathbf{a}_{i}=(a_{i,1},\ldots,a_{i,p})
. Since
\mathbf{a}_{i}\geq\mathbf{m}
, we need only show that
a_{i,\ell}-1\geq m_{\ell}
. In fact, since
\ell\in J
, we have
m_{\ell}=a_{i,\ell}-1
. Therefore
\mathbf{a}_{i}
and its neighbors in the necessary directions are all in
\mathscr{P}_{\mathbf{n}}
, so
\mathbf{m}
is in the same number of stalactites in
\mathscr{P}_{\mathbf{n}}
as it is in
\mathscr{P}
.
∎

We can now prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\mathscr{P}
be a polymatroid. Then
\Stal_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{t})=\Mob_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{t})
.

Proof.

We show that the coefficients
c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathscr{P})
of the stalactite polynomial obey the same relation as
\mu_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{n})
(see
(1)
). For any
\mathbf{a}\in B(\mathscr{P})
, let
\St(\mathbf{a})\coloneqq\St(\mathbf{a};\{\mathbf{b}\in B(\mathscr{P})\mid\mathbf{b}\prec\mathbf{a}\})
, where
\prec
denotes a lex ordering. If
\mathbf{n}\in B(\mathscr{P})
, then
\mathbf{n}\in\St(\mathbf{n})
and
\mathbf{n}
cannot be in any other stalactites, so
c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathscr{P})=1
. If
\mathbf{n}\notin I(\mathscr{P})
, then
\mathbf{n}
is in no stalactites, since each element of a stalactite is of the form
\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{e}_{J}
, for some
\mathbf{u}\in B(\mathscr{P})
and
J\subseteq[p]
. Thus in this case
c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathscr{P})=0
. It remains to show that if
\mathbf{n}\in I(\mathscr{P})\setminus B(\mathscr{P})
, then
c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathscr{P})=1-\sum_{\mathbf{m}>\mathbf{n}}c_{\mathbf{m}}^{\prime}(\mathscr{P})
, i.e.
\sum_{\mathbf{m}\geq\mathbf{n}}c_{\mathbf{m}}^{\prime}(\mathscr{P})=1
. Let
\mathscr{P}_{\mathbf{n}}
be the truncation of
\mathscr{P}
at
\mathbf{n}
. Then by
Section 4
,
\sum_{\mathbf{m}\geq\mathbf{n}}c_{\mathbf{m}}^{\prime}(\mathscr{P})=\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in I(\mathscr{P}_{\mathbf{n}})}c_{\mathbf{m}}^{\prime}(\mathscr{P}_{\mathbf{n}})
. Thus it suffices to show that for any polymatroid
\mathscr{P}
, we have
\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in I(\mathscr{P})}c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathscr{P})=1
.

Let
c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{u}}(\mathscr{P})
denote the contribution of
\St(\mathbf{u})
to
c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{m}}
, that is,

c_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{u}}(\mathscr{P})\coloneqq\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\mathbf{m}\in\St(\mathbf{u})\text{ and }|\mathbf{u}|-|\mathbf{m}|\text{ is even,}\\
-1&\text{if }\mathbf{m}\in\St(\mathbf{u})\text{ and }|\mathbf{u}|-|\mathbf{m}|\text{ is odd,}\\
0&\text{if }\mathbf{m}\notin\St(\mathbf{u}).\end{cases}

Then
c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathscr{P})=\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in B(\mathscr{P})}c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{u}}(\mathscr{P})
. Therefore

\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in I(\mathscr{P})}c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathscr{P})=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in I(\mathscr{P})}\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in B(\mathscr{P})}c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{u}}(\mathscr{P})=\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in B(\mathscr{P})}\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in I(\mathscr{P})}c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{u}}(\mathscr{P}).

Let
B(\mathscr{P})=\mathbf{a}_{1}\prec\mathbf{a}_{2}\prec\cdots\prec\mathbf{a}_{n}
. We show that
\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in I(\mathscr{P})}c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{a}_{1}}(\mathscr{P})=1
and
\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in I(\mathscr{P})}c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{a}_{i}}(\mathscr{P})=0
for
i>1
.
First,
\St(\mathbf{a}_{1})=\St(\mathbf{a}_{1};\varnothing)=\mathbf{a}_{1}
, so
c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{a}_{1},\mathbf{a}_{1}}(\mathscr{P})=1
, and
c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{a}_{1}}(\mathscr{P})=0
for all other
\mathbf{m}\in I(\mathscr{P})
. Thus
\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in I(\mathscr{P})}c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{a}_{1}}(\mathscr{P})=1
.

Now let
i>1
and consider
\St(\mathbf{a}_{i})
. First we show that
\mathbf{a}_{i}
has at least one neighbor in
\{\mathbf{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{a}_{i-1}\}
, i.e. there is
j<i
such that
\mathbf{a}_{j}=\mathbf{a}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{\ell}+\mathbf{e}_{k}
. Since
\mathbf{a}_{1}\prec\mathbf{a}_{i}
, there is some
r\in[p]
with
a_{1,r}<a_{i,r}
. Pick the least such
r
. Then
a_{1,q}=a_{i,q}
for all
q<r
. Now by M-convexity of
\mathscr{P}
, there exists
s\in[p]
such that
a_{1,s}>a_{i,s}
and
\mathbf{a}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{r}+\mathbf{e}_{s}\in\mathscr{P}
. Notice that
s>r
, and thus
\mathbf{a}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{r}+\mathbf{e}_{s}\prec\mathbf{a}_{i}
. So
\mathbf{a}_{i}
has at least one neighbor in
\{\mathbf{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{a}_{i-1}\}
. Let
J
be the set of directions in which
\mathbf{a}_{i}
has neighbors in
\{\mathbf{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{a}_{i-1}\}
. Then

\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in I(\mathscr{P})}c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{a}_{i}}(\mathscr{P})=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\St(\mathbf{a}_{i})}c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{a}_{1}}(\mathscr{P})=\sum_{J^{\prime}\subseteq J}(-1)^{|J^{\prime}|}.

Since
J
is nonempty, this sum is equal to 0, as desired.

∎

By combining
Theorem 2.9
,
Theorem 3.3
, and
Theorem 4.3
, we obtain the main result:

Theorem 4.4
.

For any polymatroid
\mathscr{P}
, we have

\cave_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{t})={\rm Stal}_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{t})={\rm Box}_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{t})=\text{\rm M\"{o}b}_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{t}).

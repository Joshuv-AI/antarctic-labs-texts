Modular Counting over 3-Element and Conservative Domains
Andrei A. Bulatov, Amirhossein Kazeminia

Abstract
In the Constraint Satisfaction Problem (CSP for short) the goal is to decide the existence of a homomorphism from a given relational structure 𝒢 {\mathcal{G}} to a given relational structure ℋ {\mathcal{H}} . If the structure ℋ {\mathcal{H}} is fixed and 𝒢 {\mathcal{G}} is the only input, the problem is denoted CSP ⁡ ( ℋ ) {\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}}) . In its counting version, # ​ CSP ​ ( ℋ ) \#{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}}) , the task is to find the number of such homomorphisms. The CSP and # ​ CSP \#{\rm CSP} have been used to model a wide variety of combinatorial problems and have received a tremendous amount of attention from researchers from multiple disciplines. In this paper we consider the modular version of the counting CSPs, that is, problems of the form # p ​ CSP ​ ( ℋ ) \#_{p}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}}) of counting the number of homomorphisms to ℋ {\mathcal{H}} modulo a fixed prime number p p . Modular counting has been intensively studied during the last decade, although mainly in the case of graph homomorphisms. Here we continue the program of systematic research of modular counting of homomorphisms to general relational structures. The main results of the paper include a new way of reducing modular counting problems to smaller domains and a study of the complexity of such problems over 3-element domains and over conservative domains, that is, relational structures that allow to express (in a certain exact way) every possible unary predicate.

In a Constraint Satisfaction Problem (CSP) the goal is to decide the existence of a homomorphism from a given relational structure

to a given structure
{\mathcal{H}}

]

Approximate counting is another vast area of research that we do not touch in this paper. Statistical Physics often concerns with the problem of computing partition functions that is closely related to counting CSPs. In this paper we consider another variation of counting problems, counting modulo an integer.

The complexity class the Counting

Modular counting.

While the complexity of and algorithms for exact counting are now well understood, counting modulo an integer has received much attention in the past decade. For an integer
k

{\mathcal{H}}
the problem of counting homomorphisms from a given relational structure to
{\mathcal{H}}

\#_{k}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})

One of the most important features of relational structures affecting the complexity of the corresponding counting CSP is the automorphism group,

(or is a

\#_{p}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}}^{\pi})

denotes the substructure of

induced by the set
\mathsf{Fix}(\pi)

. Clearly, such a reduction by
p
-automorphisms can be repeated until the resulting structure is
p
-rigid
, that is, has no
p
-automorphisms. Such a derivative
p
-rigid structure is unique up to an isomorphism, as it is shown in
[
26
]
, and will be denoted by
{\mathcal{H}}^{*p}
.

A related problem that will be important in this paper is computing partition functions. Let

for a given (di)graph

,
17
]
and produced by elements of the field of a finite multiplicative order.

Existing results.

A systematic research on modular counting was initiated by Faben in
[
25
]
, who introduced the complexity classes
\#_{p}P
of problems of counting, modulo
p
, accepting paths of polynomial time Turing machines. In the same paper he also proved some key hardness results and gave a complexity classification of
\#_{p}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
for 2-element structures
{\mathcal{H}}
into polynomial time solvable and
\#_{p}P
-complete ones. The notion of reduction between modular counting problems is similar to that for exact counting: it is either polynomial time reduction, in the same sense as before, or parsimonious reductions, where the answers to the two instances must be equal modulo
p
.

Since then most of the effort has been directed at problems
\#_{p}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
where
{\mathcal{H}}
is a graph. Faben and Jerrum
[
26
]
investigated the case when
p=2
and
{\mathcal{H}}
is a tree, and, apart from classifying the complexity of the problem in this case, they also posed a conjecture stating that if
{\mathcal{H}}
is a
p
-rigid graph,
\#_{p}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
has the same complexity as the exact counting problem
\#{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
. In a series of papers
[
31
,
30
,
33
,
42
,
28
,
40
]
the Faben/Jerrum conjecture was confirmed for a number of graph classes and values of
p
, and then was proved in full generality by Bulatov and Kazeminia in
[
16
]
.

While modular counting of graph homomorphisms provides some insight into the more general case, many of the complications do not occur in that case as it was demonstrated in
[
41
]
. As we will be using some of the results from this paper, we discuss it in some detail. The first observation is that the more general version of the Faben/Jerrum conjecture is not true for general relational structures: for any prime
p
there exists a
p
-rigid structure
{\mathcal{H}}
such that
\#{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
is hard, but
\#_{p}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
is easy. This can be fixed to some extent by introducing and exploiting multi-sorted relational structures with a richer structure of automorphisms. We will be using this framework as well, see Section
2.1
. Although it is possible to modify the Faben/Jerrum conjecture for general multi-sorted structures in such a way that no counterexample is known so far, there is still little understanding of the general case.

There are two properties of graphs that made the result of
[
16
]
possible but fail for general relational structures. One is the structure of automorphisms of direct products of graphs. Due to the results of
[
35
,
16
]
every automorphism of a direct product of graphs essentially boils down to a combination of automorphisms of the factors and a permutation of the factors. This is no longer true in the general case, even in the case of digraphs, as was demonstrated in
[
41
]
. This leads to a failure of the second important property: reductions through primitive positive (pp-) definitions. Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a relational structure. Recall that a relation
\mathcal{R}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
on
H
, the base set of
{\mathcal{H}}
, is said to be
pp-definable
in
{\mathcal{H}}
if there is a first order formula that represents
\mathcal{R}
and has the following form

\mathcal{R}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})=\exists y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\ \Phi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}),

where
\Phi
is a conjunction of predicates from
{\mathcal{H}}
and equality predicates. If
{\mathcal{H}}
is a
p
-rigid graph, then the problem
\#_{p}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}}+\mathcal{R})
, where
{\mathcal{H}}+\mathcal{R}
denotes the
extension
of
{\mathcal{H}}
by adding the predicate
\mathcal{R}
, is polynomial time reducible to
\#_{p}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
. Such reductions are known to be a powerful tool in the CSP research, as they allow for the use of polymorphisms, that is, homomorphisms from powers
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
to
{\mathcal{H}}
, to describe the complexity of constraint problems, see
[
15
,
3
]
. In particular, the existence of a so-called
Mal’tsev polymorphism
, that is, a polymorphism
m:{\mathcal{H}}^{3}\to{\mathcal{H}}
satisfying the equations
m(x,y,y)=m(y,y,x)=x
, makes it possible to represent solutions to a CSP in a compact form that then in some cases can be used to find their number
[
11
,
23
]
. In the case of general relational structures pp-definitions no longer give rise to reductions between problems and need to be replaced with their modular counterpart. A relation
\mathcal{R}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
is said to be
p
-mpp-definable
in
{\mathcal{H}}
for a prime
p
, if can be represented by a formula

\mathcal{R}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})=\exists^{\equiv p}y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\ \Phi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}),

where the requirements on
\Phi
are the same as before, and which is true for certain values of
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}
if and only if the number of values of
y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}
that make
\Phi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})
true is not divisible by
p
. Then
[
41
]
proves that
\#_{p}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}}+\mathcal{R})
is polynomial time reducible to
\#_{p}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
for any relational structure
{\mathcal{H}}
and any relation
\mathcal{R}

p
-mpp-definable in
{\mathcal{H}}
.

Unfortunately,
p
-mpp-definitions cannot be captured by polymorphisms, and overcoming this difficulty will be an ongoing theme of this paper. In some cases, however, polymorphisms of
p
-mpp-definable relations play an important role. For instance, in
[
41
]
it was shown that if the set of 2-mpp-definable relations of a structure

has a Mal’tsev polymorphism, then
\#_{2}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
can be solved in polynomial time.

Our contribution.

In this paper we build upon the results of
[
41
]
to obtain a complexity classification of
\#_{p}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
for two classes of structures
{\mathcal{H}}
.
We start with a new version of an automorphism and the corresponding rigidity condition.

A binary polymorphism
f
of a relational structure
{\mathcal{H}}
is said to be an
automorphic polynomial
if for any
a\in H
,
f(a,x)
is a permutation of
H
. If, for a prime
p
, for any
a\in H
,
f(a,x)
is the identity mapping or a permutation of order
p
, and
f(a,x)
is a permutation of order
p
for at least one
a\in H
,
f
is said to be a
p
-automorphic polynomial
. The existence of a
p
-automorphic polynomial allows one to reduce a modular counting CSP to a CSP over a smaller structure. For a complete version of the following result see Proposition
3.1
and Corollary
3.3
.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let a relational structure
{\mathcal{H}}
have a
p
-automorphic polynomial
f
. Then there is
{\mathcal{H}}^{f}
such that
{\mathcal{H}}^{f}
has smaller cardinality than
{\mathcal{H}}
and
\#_{p}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
is polynomial time reducible to
\#_{p}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}}^{f})
. Moreover, if
{\mathcal{H}}
satisfies some additional conditions,
{\mathcal{H}}^{f}
can be chosen such that
\#_{p}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
and
\#_{p}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}}^{f})
are polynomial time equivalent.

In order to prove Proposition
1.1
, given an instance
(V,{\mathcal{C}})
of
\#_{p}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
we find permutations of the domain that are specific for each variable from
V
, have order
p
, and such that they map every tuple of every constraint to a tuple from the same constraint (so-called
consistent permutations
). These permutations allow us to reduce the domain
{\mathcal{H}}
to a smaller structure, because similar to automorphisms the elements that are shifted by the permutations can be eliminated from the problem. On the other hand, let
a\in H
be an element such that
f(a,x)
is a permutation of order
p
. Then, if permutations above do not exist, it implies that the element
a
cannot be a part of any solution, and therefore can be eliminated. Consistent permutations are found by constructing a derivative CSP, whose domain is the symmetric group on
H
, and the CSP itself is an instance of a Mal’tsev CSP over that group
[
11
,
5
]
.

Recall that a unary relation pp-definable in a structure
{\mathcal{H}}
is said to be a
subalgebra
of
{\mathcal{H}}
. In the case of modular counting we use
p
-subalgebras
, that is, unary relations
p
-mpp-definable in
{\mathcal{H}}
. The two types of relational structures we consider here are

•

3-element structures, which are the next step after a similar result for 2-element structures from
[
25
]
, and

•

p
-conservative
structures, i.e., structures such that every possible unary relation is a
p
-subalgebra.

The same types of the CSP have been major milestones in the study of the decision problem, see
[
6
,
7
,
1
,
9
]
. They introduced several key ideas that were then used to obtain a dichotomy theorem for the general CSP.

In this paper in both cases our complexity classification is incomplete. The remaining difficulty is the complexity of modular partition functions. When
p=2
every partition function has a 0-1 matrix, and therefore can be treated as the problem of counting homomorphism to a graph or a bipartite graph. The complexity of this problem is known, see,
[
16
]
. If
p\geq 3
this is no longer possible and the structure of matrices of partition functions becomes more intricate, requiring methods from finite fields. Some partial results have been obtained in
[
39
]
.

Let
\langle{\mathcal{H}}\rangle
and
\langle{\mathcal{H}}\rangle_{p}
denote the set of all relations that are, respectively, pp-definable and
p
-mpp-definable in
{\mathcal{H}}
. In the case of exact counting a necessary condition for tractability of
\#{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
is the existence of a Mal’tsev polymorphism of
{\mathcal{H}}
, mentioned above. No similar property is known to be true for modular counting. Our two main results claim that for 3-element and
p
-conservative structures
{\mathcal{H}}
if
\langle{\mathcal{H}}\rangle_{p}
has no Mal’tsev polymorphism then
\#_{p}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
is
\#_{p}P
-hard. If a Mal’tsev polymorphism exists then we identify certain cases, in which the problem is polynomial time solvable.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a
p
-conservative structure and
p
a prime. If
\langle{\mathcal{H}}\rangle_{p}
does not have a Mal’tsev polymorphism then
\#_{p}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
is
\#_{p}P
-hard. Otherwise, if
p=2
then
\#_{2}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
is solvable in polynomial time.

In order to state the result for 3-element structures let
{\mathcal{H}}^{f}
be the structure from Proposition
1.1
constructed from
{\mathcal{H}}
using a
p
-automorphic polynomial
f
. Note that if
{\mathcal{H}}
is a 3-element structure,
{\mathcal{H}}^{f}
has at most 2 elements.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a 3-element structure and
p
a prime. If one of the following conditions holds:

(a)

{\mathcal{H}}
has a
p
-automorphic polynomial
f
and
\langle{\mathcal{H}}^{f}\rangle_{p}
has a Mal’tsev polymorphism, or

(b)

{\mathcal{H}}
does not have a
p
-automorphic polynomial,
\langle{\mathcal{H}}\rangle_{p}
has a Mal’tsev polymorphism, and
p=2
,

then
\#_{p}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
is solvable in polynomial time. If

(1)

{\mathcal{H}}
has a
p
-automorphic polynomial
f
and
\langle{\mathcal{H}}^{f}\rangle_{p}
does not have a Mal’tsev polymorphism, or

(2)

{\mathcal{H}}
does not have a
p
automorphic polynomial,
\langle{\mathcal{H}}\rangle_{p}
does not have a Mal’tsev polymorphism,

then
\#_{p}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
is
\#_{p}P
-complete.

Since
{\mathcal{H}}^{f}
has at most 2 elements, the cases when
{\mathcal{H}}
has a
p
-automorphic polynomial can be dealt with using an extension of the results by Faben
[
25
]
, see Proposition
5.2
.

Apart from the introduction of
p
-automorphic, the main technical contribution of the paper is the following. The property of relations with a Mal’tsev polymorphism that makes Mal’tsev polymorphisms useful is rectangularity. A relation
\mathcal{R}
(at least binary) is said to be
rectangular
if for any partition of the coordinate set of
\mathcal{R}
into two nonempty parts for any tuples
{\bf a},{\bf b}
from the first part and
{\bf c},{\bf d}
from the second part, if
({\bf a},{\bf c}),({\bf b},{\bf c}),({\bf b},{\bf d})\in\mathcal{R}
, then
({\bf a},{\bf d})\in\mathcal{R}
. If a structure
{\mathcal{H}}
has a Mal’tsev polymorphism then any relation
\mathcal{R}\in\langle{\mathcal{H}}\rangle
is rectangular, a property that is sometimes referred to as
strong rectangularity
. It is the lack of rectangularity that often makes a counting CSP hard, as it was shown in
[
12
]
. In the case of modular counting a similar result is also true, although highly nontrivial, provided a non-rectangular relation is in
{\mathcal{H}}
, see
[
16
]
. The major difficulty though has been obtain hardness from non-rectangular relations in
\langle{\mathcal{H}}\rangle
. Here we found a way to use
\langle{\mathcal{H}}\rangle_{p}
instead, while still using a Mal’tsev polymorphism.

Preliminaries

Let
[n]
denote the set
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. Let
H^{n}
be the direct product of the set
H
with itself
n
times and
H_{1}\times\dots\times H_{n}
the direct product of sets
H_{1},\ldots,H_{n}
. We denote the members of

and

. The

is denoted by
\mathbf{a}[i]
.
For

, we write
{\rm pr}_{I}{\bf a}
for the tuple
({\bf a}[i_{1}],\dots,{\bf a}[i_{k}])
, and for a relation
\mathcal{R}\subseteq H^{n}
or
\mathcal{R}\subseteq H_{1}\times\dots\times H_{n}
, we define
{\rm pr}_{I}\mathcal{R}=\{{\rm pr}_{I}{\bf a}\mid{\bf a}\in\mathcal{R}\}
. The
arity
of
\mathcal{R}
, denoted by
\hbox{ar}(\mathcal{R})
, is defined to be
n
, the length of the tuples in
\mathcal{R}
.
For
{\bf a}\in H_{1}\times\dots\times H_{s}
,
s\in[n]
, let
\mathsf{Ext}_{\mathcal{R}}({\bf a})=\{{\bf b}\in H_{s+1}\times\dots\times H_{n}|({\bf a},{\bf b})\in\mathcal{R}\}
, and by
\mathsf{\#ext}_{\mathcal{R}}({\bf a})
we denote the cardinality of
\mathsf{Ext}_{\mathcal{R}}({\bf a})
. (Note that the order of elements in
{\bf a}
and
{\bf b}
and

might differ, hence we slightly abuse the notation here.) For a prime
p
we denote the cardinality of
\mathsf{Ext}_{\mathcal{R}}({\bf a})
mod

by
\mathsf{\#_{p}ext}_{\mathcal{R}}({\bf a})
. Moreover,
\mathsf{pr}^{p}_{I}\mathcal{R}
denotes the set

\{{\rm pr}_{I}{\bf a}\mid{\bf a}\in\mathcal{R}\text{ and }\mathsf{\#_{p}ext}_{\mathcal{R}}({\rm pr}_{I}{\bf a})\neq 0\}
.
Often, we treat relations

as predicates

.

2.1
Relational structures

We begin by introducing
multi-sorted
sets. Let
H=\{H_{i}\}_{{i\in[k]}}
be a collection of sets. We assume that the sets
H_{1},\ldots,H_{k}
are disjoint.
A mapping
\varphi
between two multi-sorted sets
G=\{G_{i}\}_{{i\in[k]}}
and
H=\{H_{i}\}_{{i\in[k]}}
is defined as a collection of mappings
\varphi=\{\varphi_{i}\}_{{i\in[k]}}
, where
\varphi_{i}:G_{i}\to H_{i}
, that is,
\varphi_{i}
maps elements of the sort
i
in
G
to elements of the sort
i
in
H
.
A mapping
\varphi=\{\varphi_{i}\}_{{i\in[k]}}
from
\{G_{i}\}_{{i\in[k]}}
to
\{H_{i}\}_{{i\in[k]}}
is injective (bijective), if for all
i\in[k]
,
\varphi_{i}
is injective (bijective).

A
multi-sorted relational signature

\sigma
over a set of types
[k]
is a collection of
relational symbols
, a symbol
\mathcal{R}\in\sigma
is assigned a positive integer
\ell_{\mathcal{R}}
, the
arity
of the symbol denoted
\hbox{ar}(\mathcal{R})
, and a tuple
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{\ell_{\mathcal{R}}})\in[k]^{\ell_{\mathcal{R}}}
, the
type
of the symbol. A
multi-sorted relational structure

{\mathcal{H}}
with signature
\sigma
is a multi-sorted set
\{H_{i}\}_{i\in[k]}
and an
interpretation

\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{H}}
of each
\mathcal{R}\in\sigma
, where
\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{H}}
is a relation or a predicate on
H_{i_{1}}\times...\times H_{i_{\ell_{\mathcal{R}}}}
.
The multi-sorted structure
{\mathcal{H}}
is finite if
H
contains finitely many finite domains, and
\sigma
is finite. All structures in this paper are finite. The set
H
is said to be the
base set
or the
universe
of
{\mathcal{H}}
. For the base set we will use the same letter as for the structure, only in the regular font.
Multi-sorted structures with the same signature and types are called
similar
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}=(\{H_{i}\}_{i\in[k]};\mathcal{R}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{R}_{\ell})
be a relational structure. We use
\mathsf{Sp}({\mathcal{H}})
to denote its
spectrum
, that is, an infinite sequence
(n_{1},n_{2},\dots)
where
n_{j}
is the number of domains of cardinality
j
. If the structure is finite,
\mathsf{Sp}({\mathcal{H}})
is essentially a finite sequence, and we will truncate it by removing the trailing zeroes. Let
\prec
denote the lexicographic order on the set of such sequences assuming later entries are more senior.

Let
{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{H}}
be similar multi-sorted structures with signature
\sigma
. A
homomorphism

\varphi
from
{\mathcal{G}}
to
{\mathcal{H}}
is a mapping
\varphi:G\to H
such that for any
\mathcal{R}\in\sigma
with type
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{\ell_{\mathcal{R}}})
, if
\mathcal{R}^{{\mathcal{G}}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell_{\mathcal{R}}})
is true for

2.2
The CSP and Modular Counting

There are two standard formulations of the Constraint Satisfaction Problem (CSP).
Let
{\mathcal{H}}=(\{H_{i}\}_{i\in[k]};\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{H}}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{H}}_{\ell})
be a multi-sorted relational structure. The problem
{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
asks, given a relational structure
{\mathcal{G}}=(\{G_{i}\}_{i\in[k]};\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{G}}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{G}}_{\ell})
similar to
{\mathcal{H}}
whether there exists a homomorphism
\varphi
from
{\mathcal{G}}
to
{\mathcal{H}}
.

The other standard definition does not involve relational structures. Let
H=\{H\}_{i\in[k]}
be a multi-sorted domain and
\Gamma
a set of multi-sorted relations over
H
, called a
constraint language
. An instance

of

consists of:

•

a finite set of variables

,

•

a type function
\tau:V\to[k]
assigning each variable a sort,

•

a finite set of constraints
{\mathcal{C}}
, where each constraint is a pair
\langle{\bf s},\mathcal{R}\rangle
with
\mathcal{R}\in\Gamma
of arity
m
and
{\bf s}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{m})
a tuple of variables whose sorts match the type of
\mathcal{R}
.

A
solution
is a mapping
\varphi:V\to\bigcup_{i\in[k]}H_{i}
such that for all
v\in V
,
\varphi(v)\in H_{\tau(v)}
, and for every constraint
\langle{\bf s},\mathcal{R}\rangle
, the tuple
(\varphi(v_{1}),\ldots,\varphi(v_{m}))
belongs to
\mathcal{R}
.

For a structure
{\mathcal{H}}=(\{H_{i}\}_{i\in[k]};\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{H}}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{H}}_{\ell})
, let
\Gamma_{\mathcal{H}}=\{\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{H}}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{H}}_{\ell}\}
. It is well known, see e.g.
[
27
]
and
[
3
]
, that the problems
{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
and
{\rm CSP}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{H}})
can be easily translated into each other. The same holds for the counting versions
\#{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
and
\#{\rm CSP}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{H}})
, as well as the modular counting versions
\#_{p}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
and
\#_{p}{\rm CSP}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{H}})
. The conversion procedure is as follows. Given an instance
{\mathcal{G}}
of
{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
with signature
\sigma
, construct an instance
{\mathcal{P}}=(V,\tau,{\mathcal{C}})
of
{\rm CSP}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{H}})
by setting
V=\bigcup_{i\in[k]}G_{i}
, with the type function
\tau
induced by the sorts of
{\mathcal{G}}
, and for every relation
\mathcal{R}\in\sigma
and tuple
{\bf s}\in\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{G}}
, include the constraint
\langle{\bf s},\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{H}}\rangle
in
{\mathcal{C}}
. Conversely, for a finite constraint language
\Gamma=\{\mathcal{R}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{R}_{\ell}\}
over
H
we can construct a structure
{\mathcal{H}}_{\Gamma}=(H,\mathcal{R}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{R}_{\ell})
with an appropriately selected signature. Then any instance of
{\rm CSP}(\Gamma)
can be transformed into an instance of
{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}}_{\Gamma})
by reversing this construction. This correspondence naturally extends to the counting and modular counting versions.

We will also use polymorphisms of relational structures and constraint languages. Let
{\mathcal{H}}=(H,\mathcal{R}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{R}_{\ell})

and

Lemma 2.4

(
[

]
, see also
[

]

Also, it was shown in

4
Tools for Reduction

This section introduces the main technical tools used in our reductions. First,
we revisit the framework of automorphism-stable sets and Möbius inversion, originally developed by Bulatov and Kazeminia
[
16
]
. We extend their approach to handle more general cases required in our setting.
Then, we define indicator problems and explain how the existence of a Mal’tsev polymorphism relates to the presence of a specific predicate in the set of all (multi-sorted) pp-definable relations.
Then, we apply all the constructions and tools that we developed in this section to two special classes of relational structures:
p
-conservative and three-element structures.

4.1
Automorphism-stable Sets

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a (multi-sorted) relational structure and
n
a natural number. We call a subset
A\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}^{n}

automorphism-stable
if there exists
{\bf a}\in A
such that the set

\mathsf{Stab}({\bf a},A)=\{\pi\in\mathsf{Aut}({\mathcal{H}}^{n})\mid\pi({\bf a})\in A\}

is a subgroup of
\mathsf{Aut}({\mathcal{H}}^{n})
. Note that
\mathsf{Stab}({\bf a},A)
is always nonempty since it contains the identity automorphism.

Given a collection
{\bf a}_{1},\ldots,{\bf a}_{s}
of tuples of elements from
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
, we define
\mathsf{Stab}({\bf a}_{1},\ldots,{\bf a}_{s})
to be the set of automorphisms
\pi\in\mathsf{Aut}({\mathcal{H}}^{n})
such that each
{\bf a}_{i}
is a fixed point of
\pi
, i.e.,

\mathsf{Stab}({\bf a}_{1},\ldots,{\bf a}_{s})=\{\pi\in\mathsf{Aut}({\mathcal{H}}^{n})\mid\pi({\bf a}_{i})={\bf a}_{i}\text{ for all }i=1,\ldots,s\}.

We also define a combination of these notions: For
{\bf a}_{1},\ldots,{\bf a}_{s}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
and
A\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
, the set

\mathsf{Stab}({\bf a}_{1},\ldots,{\bf a}_{s},A)=\{\pi\in\mathsf{Aut}({\mathcal{H}}^{n})\mid\pi({\bf a}_{i})={\bf a}_{i}\text{ for all }i,\text{ and }\pi(A)\subseteq A\}

consists of automorphisms that fix each element of
{\bf a}_{1},\ldots,{\bf a}_{s}
and map
A
into itself.
We start with a generalization of Lemma 3.8 from
[
16
]
. First, we introduce a specific type of homomorphism. For (multi-sorted) relational structures
{\mathcal{G}}
and
{\mathcal{H}}
, and distinguished elements
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\in{\mathcal{G}}
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in{\mathcal{H}}
, we define

\mathsf{Hom}\bigl(({\mathcal{G}},x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}),({\mathcal{H}},a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})\bigr)

to be the set of all homomorphisms
\varphi:{\mathcal{G}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
such that
\varphi
is a homomorphism from
{\mathcal{G}}
to
{\mathcal{H}}
and, in addition,
\varphi(x_{i})=a_{i}
for all
i\in[m]
.
Furthermore, for
A\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}
, we set

\mathsf{Hom}\bigl(({\mathcal{G}},x),({\mathcal{H}},A)\bigr)\;=\;\bigcup_{a\in A}\mathsf{Hom}\bigl(({\mathcal{G}},x),({\mathcal{H}},a)\bigr).

The counting versions of these definitions are defined analogously.

Lemma 4.1

(see Lemma 3.8,
[
16
]

Let

Proof.

Parts (1) and (2) follow from Lemma 3.8 of
[

in the form we require. Let

Then

•

•

for any partition

(3) We use the Möbius inversion formula on

\displaystyle N(\theta)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{inj}(({\mathcal{H}}/_{\theta},{\bf a}_{1}/_{\theta},\ldots,{\bf a}_{k}/_{\theta},{\bf a}/_{\theta}),({\mathcal{H}},{\bf a}_{1},\ldots,{\bf a}_{k},A)).

\displaystyle N(=_{H})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\theta\in\mathsf{Part}(H)}w(\theta)M(\theta)

•

For every

5
Complexity Classifications: Conservative Structures

In this section we give an (incomplete) complexity classification of
\#_{p}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
, where
{\mathcal{H}}
is a
p
-conservative structure. The missing case is
p>2
and
\langle{\mathcal{H}}\rangle_{p}
has a Mal’tsev polymorphism. The gap is due the lack of understanding of the complexity of partition functions over nontrivial finite fields. It is a highly nontrivial problem that requires a separate study, see
[
39
]
for some partial results.

We start with proving hardness for structures whose domains are 2-element.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a multi-sorted relational structure, all of whose domains contain at most 2 elements. If a
p
-indicator rectangularity obstruction is
p
-mpp-definable in
{\mathcal{H}}
, then
\#_{p}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
is
\#_{p}P
-hard.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{R}\subseteq{\rm pr}_{U}\Upsilon_{3}({\mathcal{H}})
is a
p
-mpp-definable
p
-indicator rectangularity obstruction, where
U\subseteq I_{\mathcal{H}}\cup J_{\mathcal{H}}
,
|U|\geq 2
, and both
I^{\prime}=U\cap I_{\mathcal{H}}
and
J^{\prime}=U\cap J_{\mathcal{H}}
are nonempty. Define the projections

{\bf a}^{\prime}={\rm pr}_{I^{\prime}}{\bf a}_{\mathcal{H}},\quad{\bf b}^{\prime}={\rm pr}_{I^{\prime}}{\bf b}_{\mathcal{H}},\quad{\bf c}^{\prime}={\rm pr}_{J^{\prime}}{\bf c}_{\mathcal{H}},\quad{\bf d}^{\prime}={\rm pr}_{J^{\prime}}{\bf d}_{\mathcal{H}},

and assume
({\bf a}^{\prime},{\bf c}^{\prime}),({\bf b}^{\prime},{\bf c}^{\prime}),({\bf b}^{\prime},{\bf d}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{R}
while
({\bf a}^{\prime},{\bf d}^{\prime})\notin\mathcal{R}
.

If
|U|=2
, then since each domain has at most 2 elements, the relation
\mathcal{R}
corresponds to a binary relation over two 2-element domains. In this case,
\#_{p}{\rm CSP}(\mathcal{R})
is equivalent to evaluating the partition function of a matrix

M=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&1\end{pmatrix},

which is known to be
\#_{p}\mathrm{P}
-complete by
[
25
]
. Thus, the result holds in this case.

We now consider the case
|U|\geq 3
and aim to reduce the arity of
\mathcal{R}
while preserving the rectangularity obstruction. That is, we want to find a subset
U^{\prime}\subset U
and a
p
-mpp-definable relation
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\subseteq{\rm pr}_{U^{\prime}}\mathcal{R}
that still encodes the obstruction, i.e.,

({\bf a}^{\prime\prime},{\bf c}^{\prime\prime}),\,({\bf b}^{\prime\prime},{\bf c}^{\prime\prime}),\,({\bf b}^{\prime\prime},{\bf d}^{\prime\prime})\in\mathcal{R}^{\prime},\quad({\bf a}^{\prime\prime},{\bf d}^{\prime\prime})\notin\mathcal{R}^{\prime},

where
{\bf a}^{\prime\prime}={\rm pr}_{I^{\prime\prime}}{\bf a}_{\mathcal{H}}
,
{\bf b}^{\prime\prime}={\rm pr}_{I^{\prime\prime}}{\bf b}_{\mathcal{H}}
,
{\bf c}^{\prime\prime}={\rm pr}_{J^{\prime\prime}}{\bf c}_{\mathcal{H}}
,
{\bf d}^{\prime\prime}={\rm pr}_{J^{\prime\prime}}{\bf d}_{\mathcal{H}}
, and
I^{\prime\prime}=U^{\prime}\cap I_{\mathcal{H}}
,
J^{\prime\prime}=U^{\prime}\cap J_{\mathcal{H}}
are nonempty.

Since
|U|\geq 3
, at least one of
|I^{\prime}|\geq 2
or
|J^{\prime}|\geq 2
must hold. Without loss of generality, assume
|J^{\prime}|\geq 2
and select a variable
v\in J^{\prime}
. Write
{\bf c}^{\prime}=({\bf c}^{\prime\prime},0)
and
{\bf d}^{\prime}=({\bf d}^{\prime\prime},1)
, arranging the coordinates so that
v
corresponds to the last position.

Let
B_{1},B_{2},B_{3}
be the sets of possible extensions of
({\bf a}^{\prime},{\bf c}^{\prime\prime}),({\bf b}^{\prime},{\bf c}^{\prime\prime}),({\bf b}^{\prime},{\bf d}^{\prime\prime})
in
\mathcal{R}
, respectively, over the coordinate
v
. Since the domains have at most 2 elements, each
B_{i}\subseteq\{0,1\}
has size at most 2.

If
p\geq 3
, then regardless of the structure of the
B_{i}
, we may define
U^{\prime}=U-\{v\}
and let

\mathcal{R}^{\prime}({\bf x}_{|U^{\prime}})=\exists^{\equiv p}{\bf x}_{|v}\,\mathcal{R}({\bf x}_{|U})={\rm pr}_{U^{\prime}}\mathcal{R},

which is still
p
-mpp-definable and preserves the obstruction (as the indicator condition is unaffected modulo
p
). This gives the desired result.

Now suppose
p=2
and we analyze based on the intersections of the
B_{i}
.

Case 1:

B_{1}\cap B_{2}\cap B_{3}\neq\emptyset
. Let
c\in B_{1}\cap B_{2}\cap B_{3}
. Then we define

\mathcal{R}^{\prime}({\bf x}_{|U^{\prime}})=\exists^{\equiv 2}{\bf x}_{|v}\,(\mathcal{R}({\bf x}_{|U})\wedge C_{c}({\bf x}_{|v}),

which restricts
v
to a constant and ensures the projection of
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
satisfies the required rectangularity obstruction over
U^{\prime}=U-\{v\}
.

Case 2:

B_{1}\cap B_{2}\neq\emptyset
and
B_{2}\neq B_{3}
. Without loss of generality we can assume that
B_{1}=\{1,0\},B_{2}=\{0\}
and
B_{3}=\{1\}
, suppose
1\in B_{1}-B_{2}
. Let
U^{\prime\prime}=I^{\prime}\cup\{v\}
and define

\mathcal{R}^{\prime}({\bf x}_{|U^{\prime\prime}})=\exists^{\equiv 2}{\bf x}_{|J^{\prime}-\{v\}}\,(\mathcal{R}({\bf x}_{|U})\wedge\bigwedge_{j\in J^{\prime}-\{v\}}C_{{\bf c}^{\prime\prime}_{|j}}({\bf x}_{|j}).

Then
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
contains
({\bf a}^{\prime},0),({\bf a}^{\prime},1),({\bf b}^{\prime},0)
but not
({\bf b}^{\prime},1)
, that is, it is a rectangularity obstruction. Although this is a “swapped” obstruction (i.e.,
{\bf a}^{\prime}
and
{\bf b}^{\prime}
trade roles), the structure still suffices to encode hardness, after relabeling.

Case 3:

B_{1}=B_{2}\neq B_{3}
. Without loss of generality
B_{1}=B_{2}=\{1\},B_{3}=\{0\}
. Then the value of
v
is fixed for all tuples in
\mathcal{R}
that match the patterns, and we may again let

\mathcal{R}^{\prime}({\bf x}_{|U-\{v\}})=\exists^{\equiv 2}{\bf x}_{|v}\,\mathcal{R}({\bf x}_{|U})={\rm pr}_{U-\{v\}}\mathcal{R},

which satisfies the same type of rectangularity obstruction.
We now argue that the case analysis covers all possible configurations of
B_{1},B_{2},B_{3}\subseteq\{0,1\}
with
B_{i}\neq\emptyset
. There are only seven relevant patterns (up to symmetry):

•

B_{1}=B_{2}=B_{3}
: handled in Case 1.

•

B_{1}=B_{2}\neq B_{3}
, with
B_{1}\cap B_{3}\neq\emptyset
: falls under Case 1.

•

B_{1}=B_{2}\neq B_{3}
, with
B_{1}\cap B_{3}=\emptyset
: handled in Case 3.

•

B_{1}=\{0,1\},B_{2}=\{0\},B_{3}=\{1\}
: handled in Case 2.

•

All
B_{i}
different but pairwise intersecting: reduces to Case 1.

•

Two equal, third intersects both: reduces to Case 1.

•

Any configuration where
B_{1}\cap B_{2}\cap B_{3}\neq\emptyset
: handled in Case 1.

Since
\{0,1\}
has only two elements, no three nonempty subsets can be pairwise disjoint. Therefore, the three cases exhaust all possibilities.

By iterating this reduction process, we eventually arrive at a relation of arity 2, and as argued above, such a relation yields a
\#_{p}\mathrm{P}
-complete problem. Therefore,
\#_{p}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
is
\#_{p}\mathrm{P}
-hard.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a multi-sorted relational structure, all of whose domains contain at most 2 elements.
The problem
\#_{p}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
is solvable in polynomial time if and only if
\langle{\mathcal{H}}\rangle_{p}
has a Mal’tsev polymorphism. Otherwise it is
\#_{p}P
-hard.

Proof.

Note that a relational structure with constant relations, each of whose domains is 2-element, is conservative. Therefore, by Theorem
4.8
if
\langle{\mathcal{H}}\rangle_{p}
does not have a Mal’tsev polymorphism then the
p
-indicator rectangularity obstruction is
p
-mpp-definable in
{\mathcal{H}}
. By Proposition
5.1

\#_{p}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
is
\#_{p}P
-hard.

Suppose that
\langle{\mathcal{H}}\rangle_{p}
has a Mal’tsev polymorphism
m
. From the description of clones on a 2-element set
[
43
]
we can conclude that for every domain
H_{i}
of
{\mathcal{H}}
there is a polymorphism
m_{i}
of
\langle{\mathcal{H}}\rangle_{p}
such that
m_{i}
acts as
x\oplus y\oplus z
(or the minority operation) on
H_{i}
, where
\oplus
is addition modulo 2. If we prove that there exists a polymorphism
m^{\prime}
of
\langle{\mathcal{H}}\rangle_{p}
that acts as a minority operation on every domain of
{\mathcal{H}}
then every relation of
\langle{\mathcal{H}}\rangle_{p}
is representable by a system of linear equations modulo 2, and therefore
\#{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
is solvable in polynomial time, and so is
\#_{p}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
.

For each
H_{i}=\{a_{i},b_{i}\}
, there are 4 options of how the operation
m
can act. Indeed, the values

m(a_{i},a_{i},b_{i}),m(a_{i},b_{i},b_{i}),m(b_{i},a_{i},a_{i}),m(b_{i},b_{i},a_{i})

are fixed, as
f
is a Mal’tsev operation that only leaves
m(a_{i},b_{i},a_{i}),m(b_{i},a_{i},b_{i})\in\{a_{i},b_{i}\}
. Depending on these values we call
m
on
H_{i}
type 0 if
m(a_{i},b_{i},a_{i})=b_{i}
and
m(b_{i},a_{i},b_{i})=a_{i}
(in this case
m
on
H_{i}
equals the minority operation
x\oplus y\oplus z
, exactly what we want to obtain);
m
on
H_{i}
is of type 1 if
m(a_{i},b_{i},a_{i})=a_{i}
and
m(b_{i},a_{i},b_{i})=b_{i}
; of type 2 if
m(a_{i},b_{i},a_{i})=m(b_{i},a_{i},b_{i})=a_{i}
; and of type 3 if
m(a_{i},b_{i},a_{i})=m(b_{i},a_{i},b_{i})=b_{i}
.

Now, consider the term

f(x,y,z)=m(m(x,y,z),x,m(x,z,y)).

As is easily seen,
f
acts as a minority operation whenever
m
on
H_{i}
is of type 1,2, or 3, and it is the first projection
f(x,y,z)=x
if
m
on
H_{i}
is of type 0. This means that

g(x,y)=f(x,x,y)

acts as the first projection if
m
on
H_{i}
is of type 0, and as the second projection otherwise. Finally, consider

h(x,y,z)=g(m(x,y,z),f(x,y,z)).

This operation acts as
m(x,y,z)
if
m
on
H_{i}
is of type 0, and it acts as
f(x,y,z)
otherwise. In both cases
h
is the minority operation.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a conservative structure and
p
a prime. If
\langle{\mathcal{H}}\rangle_{p}
does not have a Mal’tsev polymorphism then
\#_{p}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
is
\#_{p}P
-hard. If
\langle{\mathcal{H}}\rangle_{2}
has a Mal’tsev polymorphism then
\#_{2}{\rm CSP}({\mathcal{H}})
is solvable in polynomial time.

Proof.

The second statement of the theorem follows from Theorem 15 of
[
41
]
. For the first statement, by Theorem
4.8
a
p
-indicator rectangularity obstruction
\mathcal{R}
is
p
-mpp-definable in
{\mathcal{H}}
. Recall that

({\bf a}_{\mathcal{H}},{\bf c}_{\mathcal{H}}),({\bf b}_{\mathcal{H}},{\bf c}_{\mathcal{H}}),({\bf b}_{\mathcal{H}},{\bf d}_{\mathcal{H}})\in\mathcal{R},

while
({\bf a}_{\mathcal{H}},{\bf d}_{\mathcal{H}})\not\in\mathcal{R}
. As
{\mathcal{H}}
is conservative, for every
v\in I_{\mathcal{H}}
(
v\in J_{\mathcal{H}}
),
H_{i_{v}}=\{{\bf a}[v],{\bf b}[v]\}
(
H_{i_{v}}=\{{\bf c}[v],{\bf d}[v]\}
) is a
p
-subalgebra of
{\mathcal{H}}
. Set

\mathcal{R}^{\prime}({\bf x}_{|I_{\mathcal{H}}\cup J_{\mathcal{H}}})=\mathcal{R}({\bf x}_{|I_{\mathcal{H}}\cup J_{\mathcal{H}}})\wedge\bigwedge_{v\in I_{\mathcal{H}}\cup J_{\mathcal{H}}}H_{i_{v}}({\bf x}_{v}).

The relation
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
still contains
({\bf a}_{\mathcal{H}},{\bf c}_{\mathcal{H}}),({\bf b}_{\mathcal{H}},{\bf c}_{\mathcal{H}}),({\bf b}_{\mathcal{H}},{\bf d}_{\mathcal{H}})
and does not contain
({\bf a}_{\mathcal{H}},{\bf d}_{\mathcal{H}})

\#_{p}{\rm CSP}(\mathcal{R}^{\prime})
is
\#_{p}P
-hard.

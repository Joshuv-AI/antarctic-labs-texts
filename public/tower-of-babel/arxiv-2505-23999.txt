A topologically extendible mapping class that is not smoothly extendible
Shital Lawande, Kuldeep Saha

Abstract
. We give an example of a smooth characteristic embedding of a torus in 𝕊 2 × 𝕊 2 ​ # ​ 𝕊 1 × 𝕊 3 \mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\#\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{3} such that there exists no diffeomorphism of the ambient 4 4 -manifold that induces the Dehn twist along a meridian of the torus, but there exists a homeomorphism of the ambient 4 4 -manifold, isotopic to identity, that induces the Dehn twist. As an application of our methods, we provide examples of two proper smooth embeddings of an annulus in 𝕊 2 × 𝕊 2 ​ # ​ 𝕊 1 × 𝕊 3 ∖ i ​ n ​ t ​ ( 𝔻 4 ) \mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\#\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}\setminus int(\mathbb{D}^{4}) which are topologically isotopic, but not smoothly isotopic (relative to boundary).



1.
Introduction

Given a smooth embedding of an orientable surface in an orientable
4
-manifold, we consider the elements in the mapping class group of the surface which are induced by diffeomorphisms of the ambient
4
-manifold. We call such mapping classes
extendible
with respect to the given embedding. The problem of extendibility originated in the work of Montesinos
[
10
]
, who used embedding of torus in
\mathbb{S}^{4}
to construct diffeomorphism of
\mathbb{S}^{4}
known as the
Montesinos twist
. The general question of extendibility for closed oriented surfaces was later studied by Hirose
[
3
]

[
4
]

[
5
]
and Hirose–Yasuhara
[
6
]
. For a smooth characteristic embedding
\iota
of a closed orientable surface in a
4
-manifold such that
\iota
induces the zero map on the first integral homology, the Rokhlin quadratic form obstructs extendibility of some mapping class groups (see section
2.2
). In particular, if
\mathcal{T}
is such a characteristic smooth embedding of a torus in an orientable
4
-manifold so that its meridian
m
has vanishing Rokhlin qudratic form, then the Dehn twist along
m
,
\tau_{m}
, is not
smoothly
extendible. Therefore, there exists no smooth isotopy
h_{t}
(
t\in[0,1]
) of the ambient
4
-manifold such that
h_{1}\circ\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}\circ\tau_{m}
. On the other hand, we prove the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists a smooth characteristic embedding
f
of a torus in
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\#\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
and a topological isotopy
G_{t}
(
t\in[0,1]
) of
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\#\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
such that the following hold.

(a)

G_{0}=id
, and
G_{1}\circ f=f\circ\tau_{m}
.

(b)

q_{f}([m])=0
.

Here,
q_{f}([m])
denotes the Rokhlin qudratic form of the meridian
m
.

Theorem
1.1
is proved by explicitly constructing an embedding
f
and a topological isotopy
G_{t}
. One of the main ingredients in the proof is a
fibered Dehn twist

\textrm{T}_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
on
\mathbb{S}^{4}
, induced by the flow of an open book decomposition of
\mathbb{S}^{3}
(see section
2.4
and section
2.5
). The map
\textrm{T}_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
was introduced in a previous work by the authors
[
8
]
to describe various extendible mapping classes for an unknotted closed orientable genus
g
surface in
\mathbb{S}^{4}
(Theorem
6.1
in
[
8
]
). Another key element in the proof of Theorem
1.1
is the observation that
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\#\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
is obtained from
\mathbb{S}^{4}
by performing a
round

1
-surgery (see section
2.6
). In the context of
4
-manifolds, a round
1
-surgery is also known as a
torus surgery
or a
logarithmic transform
.

As an application of the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we get the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists a smooth characteristic proper embedding
h:\mathbb{S}^{1}\times[0,1]\rightarrow(\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\#\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{3})\setminus int(\mathbb{D}^{4})
, such that the following hold.

(a)

\textrm{T}_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\circ h
is topologically isotopic to
h
, relative to boundary.

(b)

\textrm{T}_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\circ h
is not smoothly isotopic to
h
, relative to boundary.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Embedding of surfaces and extendible mapping classes

Let
f
be an embedding of a closed surface
\Sigma
into a compact
4
-manifold
V^{4}
. Then
\phi\in\mathcal{MCG}(\Sigma)
is called
f
-extendible
if there exists an orientation preserving diﬀeomorphism
\Phi
of
V^{4}
such that
f\circ\phi=\Phi\circ\ f
. Let
\mathcal{MCG}(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma)
denote the relative mapping class group of
\Sigma
. We denote the Dehn twist along a simple closed curve
\gamma\subset\Sigma
by
\tau_{\gamma}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A mapping class
\phi\in\mathcal{MCG}(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma)
is called
f
-extendible
if there exists a diffeomorphism (up to isotopy)
H
of
V
(that restricts to identity near
\partial V
) such that
H\circ f=f\circ\phi
.

If we replace
diffeomorphism
by
homeomorphism
in Definition
2.1
, then we call
\phi

topologically

f
-extendible. It is not hard to see that extendibility is a property of a mapping class of the surface. In particular, we note the following.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f
be a proper embedding of a surface
F
in a
4
-manifold
V
. Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be two isotopic simple closed curves on
F
. If
\tau_{\alpha}
is
f
-extendible, then
\tau_{\beta}
is also
f
-extendible.

The set of all
f
-extendible mapping classes form a subgroup of
\mathcal{MCG}(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma)
. Moreover, extendibility is preserved under isotopy of an embedding.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
f_{0},f_{1}:F\rightarrow M
be proper embeddings such that
f_{0}
is isotopic to
f_{1}
(relative to boundary), and let
\gamma
be a simple closed curve on
F
. If
\tau_{\gamma}
is
f_{1}
-extendible then
\tau_{\gamma}
is
f_{0}
-extendible.

Proof.

Given that the embeddings
f_{0},f_{1}:F\rightarrow M
are isotopic, there exists an isotopy
h_{t}:F\rightarrow M
(
t\in[0,1]
) such that
h_{0}=f_{0}
and
h_{1}=f_{1}.
We extend this to an isotopy
H_{t}:M\rightarrow M
(
t\in[0,1]
) such that
H_{0}=id_{M}
and
H_{1}\circ f_{0}=f_{1}.
Since
\tau_{\gamma}
is
f_{1}
-extendible, there exists a diffeomorphism
\Psi:M\rightarrow M
such that
\Psi\circ f_{1}=f_{1}\circ\tau_{\gamma}.
Therefore,
({H_{1}}^{-1}\circ\Psi\circ H_{1})\circ f_{0}=f_{0}\circ\tau_{\gamma}
(up to isotopy). Hence,
\tau_{\gamma}
is
f_{0}
-extendible.

∎

Definition 2.4

(Flexible embedding)
.

A proper embedding
f
of a surface
\Sigma
in a
4
-manifold

is called flexible if every element of

-extendible.

Hirose–Yasuhara

gave a sufficient condition on a closed compact

Theorem 2.5

(
[

]
(Theorem

))
.

Let

be a

-manifold and
\mathbb{D}^{4}
a
4
-ball in

. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hopf link in
\partial(M\setminus int\mathbb{D}^{4})
. If
\mathcal{H}
bounds a disjoint union of two disks in

.

The proof of Theorem

uses the following fact.

Proposition 2.6

(The Hopf annulus trick
[

]
,
[

]
)
.

There exists a flexible proper embedding of an annulus
\mathcal{A}
in
\mathbb{S}^{3}\times[0,1]

.

The notion of

in an

, then every

2.2.
Characteristic embedding and Rokhlin quadratic form

Let
\Sigma
be a compact orientable surface and let
V
be a compact orientable
4
-manifold such that
H_{1}(V;\mathbb{Z})=0
. A proper embedding
f:(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma)\rightarrow(V,\partial V)
is called
chraracteristic
if
[f(\Sigma)]\cdot X\equiv X\cdot X\pmod{2}
for all
X\in H_{2}(V,\mathbb{Z}_{2})
. Equivalently,
[f(\Sigma)]\in H_{2}(V,\partial V;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is the Poincare dual of the second Stiefel–Whitney class of
V
. Given such a characteristic embedding
f
, one can define a quadratic form
q_{f}:H_{1}(F,\mathbb{Z}_{2})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}
in the following way.

For
x\in H_{1}(\Sigma,\mathbb{Z}_{2})
we choose a simple closed curve
C
on
\Sigma
representing
x
. As
H_{1}(V;\mathbb{Z}_{2})=0
,
f(C)
bounds a connected, orientable surface
D
embedded in
V^{4}
. We may assume that
D
meets
\Sigma
transversely. Since
D
is homotopy equivalent to an wedge of circles, the normal bundle
{\nu}_{D}
of
D
is trivial. A trivialization on
D
induces a unique trivialization on the restriction of
\nu_{D}
over
\partial D=f(C)
(see section
2
in
[
7
]
). The normal bundle of
f(C)
in
f(\Sigma)
then determines a
1
-dimensional subbundle of this trivialized
2
-disk bundle over
C
. Let
\mathcal{O}(D)
be the mod
2
number of full twists made by this
1
-dimensional subbundle as we go around
C
. Now define,

q_{f}(x)=D\cdot f(\Sigma)+\mathcal{O}(D)\mathrm{mod~2},

where
D\cdot f(\Sigma)
is the number of intersection points between the interior of
(D)
and
f(\Sigma)
. The quadratic form
q_{f}
is called the
Rokhlin quadratic form
of the embedding
f
. For
x,y\in H_{1}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
,
q_{f}
satisfies the relation:
q_{f}(x+y)=q_{f}(x)+q_{f}(y)+x\cdot y
, where
x\cdot y
is the
\pmod{2}
intersection number of
x
and
y
.

Recall that
\phi\in\mathcal{MCG}(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma)
is
f
-extendible, if there is an orientation preserving diﬀeomorphism
\Phi
of
V
(up to isotopy) such that
f\circ\phi=\Phi\circ f
. If
\phi
is
f
-extendible then, by definition of the Rokhlin quadratic form,
q_{f}([\phi(\alpha)])=q_{f}([\alpha])
for every simple closed curve
\alpha
on
\Sigma
(see
[
6
]
). Let
\Sigma_{g,b}
denote the compact orientable surface of genus
g
with
b
boundary components and let
W^{4}
be a compact orientable
4
-manifold with
H_{1}(W;\mathbb{Z})=0
. The following was proved in
[
8
]
.

Lemma 2.7

(Lemma
3.1
in
[
8
]
)
.

Let
f:(\Sigma_{g,b},\partial\Sigma_{g,b})\rightarrow(W^{4},\partial W^{4})
be a smooth characteristic proper embedding.

(a)

Let
\gamma_{1}
be an essential simple closed curve in the interior of
\Sigma_{g,b}
. If
\tau_{\gamma_{1}}
is
f
-extendible then
q_{f}([\gamma])=1
.

(b)

Assume that
\partial W
is an integral homology sphere. Let
\gamma_{2}
be a non-nullhomologous simple closed curve in the interior of
\Sigma_{g,b}
which is parallel to a boundary component of
\Sigma_{g,b}
. If
\tau_{\gamma}
is
f
-extendible, then
q_{f}([\gamma_{2}])=1
.

(c)

If
(g,b)\notin\{(1,1),(0,2),(1,0)\}
, then there exists a simple closed curve
\gamma
in the interior of
\Sigma_{g,b}
such that
q_{f}(\gamma)=0
and
\gamma
is either essential or parallel to a boundary component.

Thus, for a smooth characteristic embedding
f
as in Lemma
2.7
, if
q_{f}(\gamma)=0
for some simple closed curve
\gamma
, then
\tau_{\gamma}
is not
f
-extendible. Thus, by Lemma
2.7
, there exists no smooth embedding of
\Sigma_{g}
in
\mathbb{S}^{4}
(for
g\geq 2
) for which every element of
\mathcal{MCG}(\Sigma_{g})
is extendible. The non-existence of a such a smooth embedding of a torus in
\mathbb{S}^{4}
follows from the Rokhlin signature theorem (see remark
3.2
in
[
6
]
).

Example 2.1
.

Consider the trivial embedding
f_{0}
of a torus
T
in
\mathbb{S}^{3}
. This induces an embedding of
T
in
\mathbb{S}^{4}
via the standard inclusion map
\mathbb{S}^{3}\rightarrowtail\mathbb{S}^{4}
(as equator). Since the meridian (or longitude)
m
(or
l
) on
T
bounds a disk in
\mathbb{S}^{3}
, disjoint from
T\setminus m
,
q_{f_{0}}(m)=0
and
q_{f_{0}}(l)=0
. Thus,
q_{f_{0}}(\tau_{m}(l))=q_{f_{0}}([m]+[l])=1\neq 0=q_{f_{0}}(l)
. Hence,
\tau_{m}
is not
f_{0}
-extendible. Similarly, one can show that
\tau_{l}
is not
f_{0}
-extendible. However, the simple closed curve
\gamma=\tau_{m}(l)
is an unknot and has surface framing
1
. Thus, by Proposition
2.6
,
\tau_{\gamma}
is
f_{0}
-extendible.

Remark 2.8
.

We assumed
H_{1}(V^{4},\mathbb{Z})=0
while defining the Rokhlin quadratic form for a simple closed curve
c
on the embedded surface. We needed this for
c
to bound a surface in
V
. Thus, it is enough to assume that the embedding induces zero map on the first integral homology.

2.3.
Open book decomposition

An open book decomposition of a closed
(2n+1)
-manifold
M
consists of a codimension
2
closed submanifold
B
and a fibration map
\pi:M\setminus B\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}
, such that in a tubular neighborhood of
B\subset M
, the restriction map
\pi:B\times(\mathbb{D}^{2}\setminus\{0\})\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}
is given by
(b,r,\theta)\mapsto\theta
. The fibration
\pi
determines a unique fiber manifold
N^{2n}
whose boundary is
B
. The closure
\bar{N}
is called the
page
and
B
is called the
binding
. The monodromy of the fibration map
\pi
determines a diffeomorphism
\phi
of
\bar{N}
such that
\phi
is identity near the boundary
\partial\bar{N}
. In particular,
M=\mathcal{MT}(\bar{N},\phi)\cup_{id,\partial}\partial\bar{N}\times\mathbb{D}^{2}
, where
\mathcal{MT}(\bar{N},\phi)
denotes the mapping torus of
\phi
. We denote such an open book decomposition of
M
by
\textrm{OB}(\bar{N},\phi)
. The map
\phi
is called the
monodromy
of the open book. It is a well-known fact that every
M^{2n+1}
admits an open book decomposition.

2.4.
General Dehn twists along
3
-manifolds

Let
Y
be a closed oriented
3
-manifold, and
\rho:\mathbb{S}^{1}\rightarrow Diff^{+}(Y)
be a loop based at the identity. Given any
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-action on
Y
one can find such a loop. We can define a diffeomorphism
\textrm{T}_{Y}:Y\times[0,1]\rightarrow Y\times[0,1]
by
(y,t)\mapsto(\rho(t)(y),t)
for
(y,t)\in Y\times[0,1]
. Here, we think of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
as the quoitent
[0,1]/\{0\sim 1\}
.

Whenever there is an embedding of
Y
into a
4
-manifold
V^{4}
, we can extend
\textrm{T}_{Y}
to a diffeomorphism of
V^{4}
supported in a neighborhood of the embedded
Y
. We call
\textrm{T}_{Y}
a
Dehn twist on
V^{4}
along
Y
. Although
\textrm{T}_{Y}
depends on
\rho
, whenever
\rho
is clear from the context, we shall use
\textrm{T}_{Y}
to denote this Dehn twist.

A circle action on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
via open book

Consider the trivial open book of
\mathbb{S}^{3}
given by
\textrm{OB}(\Sigma=\mathbb{D}^{2},id)
. The open book induces an
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-action on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
via the flow of a vector field
X
on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
whose time-
1
map takes a page to itself and applies the identity map as monodromy on it. In a neighborhood of the binding,
(\partial\Sigma\times\mathbb{D}^{2},(x,r,\theta))
,
X
is given by
r\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}
. In particular, the flow of
X
fixes the binding unknot
U_{0}=\partial\Sigma
pointwise. Let
p
be a point in the interior of the collar neihghborhood

. Then the orbit of

under this

-action gives another unknot

in
\mathbb{S}^{3}

naturally bounds a
2
-disk in
S^{3}
that intersects the binding at a single point. Thus, we have an
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-action on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
that fixes the binding
U_{0}
and rotates
U_{1}
. Let
\textrm{T}_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
denote the corresponding fibered Dehn twist on
\mathbb{S}^{3}\times[0,1]
.

2.6.
Round handles and round surgery

The notion of a round handle and round surgery was introduced by Asimov
[
1
]
to study non-singular Morse-Smale flows. Recall that an
n
-dimensional handle of index
k
, denoted by
H_{k}
, is a copy of
\mathbb{D}^{k}\times\mathbb{D}^{n-k}
that is attached to the boundary of an
n
-manifold
M
. The attaching region is a framed tubular neighborhood of an embedded
\mathbb{S}^{k-1}
(i.e. a copy of
\mathbb{S}^{k-1}\times\mathbb{D}^{n-k}
) in
\partial M
.

Definition 2.9
.

A round handle of dimension
n
and index
k
attached to
M
is a pair
R_{k}=(\mathbb{D}^{k}\times\mathbb{D}^{n-k-1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1},f)
, where embedding
f:\partial\mathbb{D}^{k}\times\mathbb{D}^{n-k-1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\rightarrow\partial M
gives the attaching map. Let
M\cup R_{k}
denote the manifold obtained from
M
by attaching
\mathbb{D}^{k}\times\mathbb{D}^{n-k-1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
via
f
.

A
round surgery
corresponds to attaching a round handle to a cobordism. Let
N
be a manifold of dimension
n
. A round surgery of index
k
is defined as the operation that removes an embedded
int(\partial\mathbb{D}^{k}\times\mathbb{D}^{n-k}\times\mathbb{S}^{1})
from
N
and reglues a copy of
\mathbb{D}^{k}\times\partial\mathbb{D}^{n-k}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
by the identity map of
\partial\mathbb{D}^{k}\times\partial\mathbb{D}^{n-k}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Asimov proved the following relating a round handle with the usual handles.

Theorem 2.10

(Fundamental Lemma of Round Handles)
.

Let
M
and
\widetilde{M}
be manifolds such that
\widetilde{M}=M\cup H_{k}\cup H_{k+1}
, where
H_{k}
and
H_{k+1}
are attached independently (i.e. their attaching regions are disjoint) to the same boundary component of
M
and
k\geq 1
. Then
\widetilde{M}=M\cup R_{k}
.

3.1.
An embedding of a torus in
\mathbb{S}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\#\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}

Consider the trivial open book
\mathbb{S}^{3}=\textrm{OB}(\mathbb{D}^{2},id)
. Think of
\mathbb{S}^{4}
as a union of the cylinder
\mathbb{S}^{3}\times[-1,2]
with two
4
-balls
B^{4}_{-1}
and
B^{4}_{2}
(bounding
\mathbb{S}^{3}\times\{-1\}
and
\mathbb{S}^{3}\times\{2\}
respectively). To talk about the circle action on
\textrm{OB}(\mathbb{D}^{2},id)
, we fix a page given by
D=\{(x,y,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\cup\{\infty\}:x^{2}+y^{2}\leq 1\}
. Let
p\in int(D)
such that
\|p\|=\epsilon
for some
\epsilon\in[1-2\delta,1-\delta]
, where
\delta
is small and positive. Let
U_{0}
be the unknot obtained as the orbit of
p
under the circle action of the open book. Let
D_{1}=\{q\in D:\|q\|\leq 1-3\delta\}
and
U_{1}=\partial D_{1}
. Consider the union
(U_{0}\sqcup U_{1})\times[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2}]\subset\mathbb{S}^{3}\times[-1,2]
. Since
U_{0}
and
U_{1}
are unlinked unknots there exists a planar annulus
A\subset\mathbb{S}^{3}
such that

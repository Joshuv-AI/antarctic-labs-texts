On the pluricanonical map of projective 3-folds of general type with P 3 ≥ 2 P_{3}\geq 2
Yong Hu, Jianshi Yan

Abstract
. We prove that for all nonsingular projective 3-folds of general type with third plurigenus P 3 ≥ 2 P_{3}\geq 2 , the pluricanonical map φ m \varphi_{m} is birational onto its image for all m ≥ 14 m\geq 14 , which is optimal.



1.
Introduction

One of the most fundamental problems in algebraic geometry is to classify algebraic varieties. Concerning this problem, one very effective approach is to study the pluricanonical maps. A fundamental theorem established by Hacon-McKernan
[
13
]
, Takayama
[
18
]
and Tsuji
[
19
]
guarantees the existence of optimal constants
r_{n}
for any positive integer
n
and for any smooth projective
n
-fold of general type, such that the
m
-canonical map
\varphi_{m}
is birational onto its image for all
m\geq r_{n}
. Determining the exact values of
r_{n}
is an important open question. It is classically known that
r_{1}=3
. For surfaces of general type, Bombieri
[
2
]
proved that
r_{2}=5
. For
n=3
, it has been shown by Iano-Fletcher
[
14
]
, Chen-Chen
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
and Chen
[
11
]
that
27\leq r_{3}\leq 57
.

The pluricanonical map of
3
-folds with extra plurigenera conditions is expected to have better behavior. For instance, for
3
-folds of general type with
p_{g}\geq 2
, Chen
[
10
]
proved that
\varphi_{m}
is birational onto its image for all
m\geq 8
; for
3
-folds of general type with
P_{2}\geq 2
, Chen-Chen
[
6
]
proved that
\varphi_{m}
is birational onto its image for all
m\geq 11
. Both results are known to be optimal.

In this paper, we focus on nonsingular
3
-folds of general type with
P_{3}\geq 2
(see
[
6
, Remark 1.10]
for the discussion on this topic). Our main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
V
be a nonsingular projective
3
-fold of general type with
P_{3}(V)\geq 2
. Then:

(1)

\varphi_{m}
is birational onto its image for all
m\geq 14
;

(2)

if
\varphi_{13}
is not birational, then
P_{3}(V)=2
,
q(V)=0
and
|3K_{V}|
is composed with a pencil of
(1,2)
-surfaces.

The result is optimal due to the following example.

Example 1.2
.

A general hypersurface
X_{28}
of degree
28
in a weighted projective space
\mathbb{P}(1,3,4,5,14)
(see
[
14
, p. 151]
) is a minimal
3
-fold of general type with
P_{3}=2
and its
13
-canonical map is non-birational.

In the study of explicit birational geometry of
3
-folds of general type, a significant challenge arises when the fibration induced by
|3K_{V}|
has very small birational invariants. Our contribution is to study a special birational model constructed in
[
12
]
. Using this new model, we are able to explicitly extract information about the singularities of the minimal model. By combining this with a detailed analysis of the pluricanonical maps, we are able to prove the main results stated in Theorem
1.1
.

Throughout this paper, all varieties are defined over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
0
. We will frequently use the following symbols:

\diamond

\sim
’ denotes linear equivalence;

\diamond

‘
\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}
’ denotes
\mathbb{Q}
-linear equivalence;

\diamond

‘

’ denotes numerical equivalence;

\diamond

‘
|A|\succcurlyeq|B|
’ or, equivalently, ‘

’ means
|A|\supseteq|B|+
fixed effective divisors.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Varieties and divisors

Let
Z
be a normal projective variety of dimension
d
. The
geometric genus

p_{g}(Z)
and the
m
-th plurigenus

P_{m}(Z)
of
Z
are defined as

p_{g}(Z):=h^{0}(Z,K_{Z}),\ P_{m}(Z):=h^{0}(Z,mK_{Z}).

The
canonical volume
of
Z
is defined as

\mathrm{vol}(Z):=\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{h^{0}(Z,nK_{Z})}{n^{d}/d!}.

We say that
Z
is
minimal
, if
Z
has at worst
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial terminal singularities and
K_{Z}
is nef. If
Z
is birational to a smooth projective variety
Z^{\prime}
with
\mathrm{vol}(Z^{\prime})>0
, we say that
Z
is of general type.

If
Z
has at worst rational singularities and
q(Z):=h^{1}(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z})>0
, then
Z
is said to be
irregular
. Otherwise,
Z
is called
regular
.

For two integers
a>0
and
b\geq 0
, an
(a,b)
-surface
is a smooth projective surface
S
of general type with
\mathrm{vol}(S)=a
and
p_{g}(S)=b
.

2.2.
Birationality principle

Let
Z
be a normal projective variety on which
D
is a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Cartier Weil divisor with
h^{0}(Z,D)\geq 2
. We may define
a generic irreducible element of
|D|
in the following way. Write

|D|=\text{Mov}|D|+\text{Fix}|D|,

where
\text{Mov}|D|
and
\text{Fix}|D|
denote the moving part and the fixed part of
|D|
respectively. Consider the rational map
\varphi_{|D|}
induced by the linear system
|D|
.
|D|
is said to be composed with a pencil
if
\dim\overline{\varphi_{|D|}(Z)}=1
; otherwise,
|D|
is not composed with a pencil
.
A generic irreducible element of
|D|
is defined to be an irreducible component of a general member in
\text{Mov}|D|
if
|D|
is composed with a pencil or, otherwise, a general member of
\text{Mov}|D|
.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that
|D|
can
distinguish different generic irreducible elements
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
of a linear system
|M|
if neither
M_{1}
nor
M_{2}
is contained in
\text{Bs}|D|
, and if
\overline{\varphi_{|D|}(M_{1})}\nsubseteq\overline{\varphi_{|D|}(M_{2})},\overline{\varphi_{|D|}(M_{2})}\nsubseteq\overline{\varphi_{|D|}(M_{1})}
.

We will use the following type of birationality principle.

Theorem 2.2
.

(cf.
[
5
, §2.7]
)
Let
Z
be a nonsingular projective variety,
A
and
B
be two divisors on
Z
with
|A|
being a base point free linear system. Take the Stein factorization of
\varphi_{|A|}
:
Z\overset{h}{\longrightarrow}W\longrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{h^{0}(Z,A)-1}

where
h
is a fibration onto a normal variety
W
. Then the rational map
\varphi_{|B+A|}
is birational onto its image if one of the following conditions is satisfied:

(i)

\dim\varphi_{|A|}(Z)\geq 2
,
|B|\neq\emptyset
and
\varphi_{|B+A|}|_{D}
is birational for a
general member
D
of
|A|
.

(ii)

\dim\varphi_{|A|}(Z)=1
,
\varphi_{|B+A|}
can distinguish different general fibers of
h
and
\varphi_{|B+A|}|_{F}
is birational for a general fiber
F
of
h
.

2.4.
Plurigenera of threefolds of general type

Applying the proof of
[
5
, Lemma 3.2]
, we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
X
be a minimal
3
-fold of general type. Then
the following hold:

(1)

for all
n\geq 2
, we have

P_{n+2}(X)\geq P_{n}(X)+P_{2}(X)+(n^{2}+n)K_{X}^{3}-\chi({\mathcal{O}_{X}});

(2)

if
\chi({\mathcal{O}_{X}})\leq 2
and
P_{3}(X)\geq 2
, we have
P_{5}(X)\geq 1
;

(3)

if
\chi({\mathcal{O}_{X}})\leq 1
and
P_{3}(X)\geq 2
, we have
P_{n}(X)\geq 1
for all
n\geq 8
.

Proof.

(1) follows by the proof of
[
5
, Lemma 3.2]
. For (2), by (1) and our assumption, we have

\displaystyle P_{5}(X)

\displaystyle\geq P_{3}(X)+P_{2}(X)+12K_{X}^{3}-\chi({\mathcal{O}_{X}})

\displaystyle\geq P_{2}(X)+12K_{X}^{3}

\displaystyle>0.

Now suppose that
\chi({\mathcal{O}_{X}})\leq 1
. By (1), for all
k\geq 6
, we have

\displaystyle P_{k+2}(X)

\displaystyle\geq P_{k}(X)+P_{2}(X)+(k^{2}+k)K_{X}^{3}-\chi({\mathcal{O}_{X}})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{k^{2}+k}{36}-1

\displaystyle>0,

where the second inequality follows by
[
6
, Proposition 4.3]
and the last inequality holds by
k\geq 6
. It follows that we have
P_{n}(X)\geq 1
for all
n\geq 8
. (3) is proved. The proof is completed.

3.
Birationality of pluricanonical maps

According to the minimal model program (see
[
1
]
), a smooth projective variety of general type is always birational to a
minimal model
. To study the birationality of pluricanonical maps of varieties of general type, it suffices to study the pluricanonical maps of their minimal models.

Let
X
be a minimal
3
-fold of general type with
P_{3}(X)\geq 2
. The following lemma follows by
[
9
, Proposition 2.9]
,
[
8
, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
3
, Theorem 1.3]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X
be an irregular minimal
3
-fold of general type. Then
\varphi_{m}
is birational for all
m\geq 5
.

Proof.

By
[
9
, Proposition 2.9]
,
\varphi_{m}
is birational for all
m\geq 7
. The birationality of
\varphi_{6}
follows by
[
8
, Theorem 1.1]
.
\varphi_{5}
is birational by
[
3
, Theorem 1.3]
.
∎

Thus we only need to consider the case when
X
is regular. From now on, we always assume that
q(X)=0
. Take
\Lambda\subseteq|3K_{X}|
and
\pi:X^{\prime}\to X
as in §
2.3
. Keep the same notations as there. Recall that we have the fibration
f:X^{\prime}\to\Gamma
with a general fiber

. Since

, we have
\Gamma\cong{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

The linear system
|mK_{X^{\prime}}|
distinguishes different general fibers of
f
for all
m\geq 12
.

Proof.

When

, by
[
5
, Proposition 2.15]
, we have

for all

. So
mK_{X^{\prime}}\geq F
for all

. It follows that
|mK_{X^{\prime}}|
distinguishes different general fibers of
f
.

When

, by
[
5
, Lemma 2.32]
, we have
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})\leq 1
. By Lemma
2.3
(3), we have
P_{n}(X)\geq 1
for all

.
So for all

, we have
mK_{X^{\prime}}\geq F
, which implies that
|mK_{X^{\prime}}|
distinguishes different general fibers of
f
. The proof is completed.

∎

So by Theorem
2.2
, in order to consider the birationality of
\varphi_{m}
, we may just consider the birationality of the map induced by
|mK_{X^{\prime}}||_{F}
. Denote by
\sigma:F\to F_{0}
the contraction onto its minimal model. By

(take

,

-divisor

\displaystyle\pi^{*}(K_{X})|_{F}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\frac{1}{4}\sigma^{*}(K_{F_{0}})+H.

. It is clear that

is a nef and big divisor whose fractional part is simple normal crossing for any

. For all

\displaystyle|mK_{X^{\prime}}||_{F}\succcurlyeq|K_{X^{\prime}}+\ulcorner{L_{m}}\urcorner+F||_{F}\succcurlyeq|K_{F}+\ulcorner{L_{m}}\urcorner|_{F}|\succcurlyeq|K_{F}+\ulcorner{L_{m}|_{F}}\urcorner|,

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X
be a regular minimal
3
-fold of general type with
P_{3}(X)\geq 2

|K_{F}+\ulcorner{L_{m}|_{F}}\urcorner|
gives a birational map if one of the following holds:

(2)

Proof.

Note that we have

If

where the last inequality holds since

\xi\geq\frac{1}{2}
. It follows that the linear system
|K_{C}+\ulcorner{L_{m}|_{C}-\frac{5}{2}{\tilde{E}_{F}}|_{C}}\urcorner|

2.2
, Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
3.7
,
\varphi_{m}
is birational for all
m\geq 13
. The proof is completed.

Retuve: Automated Multi-Modality Analysis of Hip Dysplasia with Open Source AI Journal: Software Impacts
Adam McArthur, Stephanie Wichuk, Stephen Burnside, Andrew Kirby, Alexander Scammon, Damian Sol, Abhilash Hareendranathan, Jacob L. Jaremko

Abstract
Developmental dysplasia of the hip ( DDH ) poses significant diagnostic challenges, hindering timely intervention. Current screening methodologies lack standardization, and AI-driven studies suffer from reproducibility issues due to limited data and code availability. To address these limitations, we introduce Retuve, an open-source framework for multi-modality DDH analysis, encompassing both ultrasound ( US ) and X-ray imaging. Retuve provides a complete and reproducible workflow, offering open datasets comprising expert-annotated US and X-ray images, pre-trained models with training code and weights, and a user-friendly Python Application Programming Interface ( API ). The framework integrates segmentation and landmark detection models, enabling automated measurement of key diagnostic parameters such as the alpha angle and acetabular index. By adhering to open-source principles, Retuve promotes transparency, collaboration, and accessibility in DDH research. This initiative has the potential to democratize DDH screening, facilitate early diagnosis, and ultimately improve patient outcomes by enabling widespread screening and early intervention. The GitHub repository/code can be found here: https://github.com/radoss-org/retuve

Ultrasound

3.2
X-ray

Similar to ultrasound analysis, AI-based methods for X-ray analysis of
DDH
follow distinct approaches. One approach involves directly classifying a hip as either Normal or Dysplastic. For instance, one study utilized YOLOv5 to directly predict the presence of dysplasia with an associated confidence score
[
46
]
. This end-to-end approach offers a potentially rapid screening tool.

Alternatively, Landmark methods are frequently employed. These methods typically involve identifying key anatomical landmarks on the X-ray image to calculate various measurements, including the Acetabular Index. This information, combined with additional metadata, is then used to classify the hip as Normal or Dysplastic
[
47
]
. The accuracy of landmark detection is paramount for reliable classification.

Another approach involves segmenting the pelvis and femur, followed by the application of a specialized algorithm to identify the correct landmarks for calculating the acetabular angle
[
41
]
. This segmentation-based approach can improve the robustness of landmark detection, particularly in cases with subtle anatomical variations.

3.3
Open Source

Despite the growing body of research in AI-assisted
DDH
diagnosis, a significant gap remains in terms of reproducibility and accessibility. Currently, no research paper on
DDH
AI provides fully reproducible code and data for the AI models used. While some papers include code snippets
[
48
]
or utilize open-source data with limited labels
[
41
,
42
]
, a concerted effort to standardize methodologies and provide comprehensive open-source resources is lacking. This absence hinders independent validation, comparison of different approaches, and further development of the field.

Retuve distinguishes itself from existing methods through its comprehensive approach to addressing the challenges in
DDH
research, focusing on reproducibility, standardization, and accessibility. While previous studies have explored AI-driven analysis of ultrasound and X-ray images for
DDH
diagnosis, they often fall short in providing the necessary resources for independent validation and further development.

Retuve Overview

Retuve is a comprehensive open-source framework for automated multi-modality analysis of Hip Dysplasia, encompassing both ultrasound and X-ray imaging. The framework is designed to provide a complete and reproducible workflow for
DDH
analysis, offering open datasets, models (training code and weights), and a user-friendly Python API. Retuve’s modular architecture enables seamless integration of different AI models and algorithms, allowing for automated measurement of key diagnostic parameters such as the alpha angle and acetabular index. By adhering to open-source principles, Retuve promotes transparency, collaboration, and accessibility in
DDH
research, aiming to democratize
DDH
screening and facilitate early intervention. Using the same basic architecture, Retuve can be easily forked and adjusted for other medical imaging/radiomic tasks, including for
MRI
/CT.

Retuve can be split into two main components, as seen on
Figure
2

Figure 2:
Retuve Architecture Diagram, with Inputs, AI Outputs, and final outputs for the user.

1.

Segmentation AI
: A set of neural network models that take the raw image, and output the required geometry for analysis.

2.

Rule-Based Algorithm
: A set of algorithms that take the geometry from the AI, and output the final measurements.

Together, these components allow Retuve to analyze any of the above formats, and output the required measurements.
The Segmentation AI System is shared between all modalities, and the Rule-Based Algorithm is specific to each modality.

Retuve is being released before validation results have been published. Therefore all metrics have been marked as experimental when running Retuve. As metric validations are published, metrics will move through an alpha/beta/mature release system. The current release state and updates to the release plan can be found

Software Impact/Results

Retuve builds upon and differs from existing methods in the following ways:

1.

Complete Reproducibility:
Unlike many studies that lack publicly available code and datasets, Retuve provides a fully open-source framework, including:

(a)

Expert-annotated datasets for both ultrasound and X-ray images.

(b)

Pre-trained models with training code and weights, enabling researchers to replicate results and fine-tune models for their specific needs.

(c)

A user-friendly Python API for easy integration and experimentation.

This end-to-end reproducibility is crucial for building trust in AI-driven solutions and fostering collaborative innovation.

2.

Multi-Modality Approach:
Retuve supports both ultrasound and X-ray imaging modalities, recognizing that different clinical scenarios may require different imaging techniques. By providing tools for both modalities, Retuve offers a more versatile solution for
DDH
analysis.

3.

Focus on Standardization:
Retuve aims to promote standardization in
DDH
screening by providing a consistent framework for image analysis and diagnostic parameter measurement. This can help reduce inter-observer variability and improve the reliability of
DDH
diagnosis.

4.

Open-Source Philosophy:
Retuve is committed to the principles of open-source AI, adhering to the
OSI
definition. This means that the framework is freely available for anyone to use, modify, and distribute, fostering community-driven development and innovation.

5.

Beyond Landmark Detection and Segmentation:
While Retuve incorporates state-of-the-art segmentation and landmark detection models, it goes beyond these basic tasks by having modular design that allows for easy integration of new models and algorithms.

Retuve provides a foundational framework for

7.1.1

Retuve’s applications are diverse.

Aligned with efforts like OLMo 2

We encourage community contributions to improve accuracy and usability. Retuve’s extensible design allows adding new AI models and features. By making Retuve freely available, we aim to enable earlier

We believe Retuve can significantly improve

8
Conclusion

In this paper, we have introduced Retuve, the first fully open-source Hip Dysplasia Collaborative Framework for infant hip health using AI. Retuve supports both X-ray and ultrasound imaging and uniquely provides open datasets, models (training code and weights), and a Python API, for reproducibility and collaborative development. We demonstrated its modular architecture, allowing for easy integration of different AI models and algorithms, and we noted a novel landmark-based approach for X-ray analysis.

Future development will focus on enhancing Retuve’s robustness and accuracy, particularly in challenging scenarios such as
2DUS
sweep cases and images with poor scan quality. This includes exploring dedicated AI systems for scan quality assessment to improve performance across diverse imaging conditions. Crucially, ongoing efforts will prioritize expanding and refining expert-annotated datasets to mitigate bias and improve model calibration. Furthermore, we aim to investigate the application of landmark-based approaches to ultrasound analysis, potentially leading to more precise and reliable measurements.

By democratizing
DDH
diagnosis through open-source AI, Retuve empowers researchers, clinicians, and developers worldwide to collaboratively advance the field. We invite and encourage community participation in benchmarking Retuve’s performance, contributing novel AI models and algorithms, and expanding its capabilities to address unmet clinical needs. Through this collaborative effort, we can accelerate progress in early
DDH
detection, facilitate timely intervention, and ultimately improve outcomes for infants globally, reducing the long-term burden of
DDH
-related complications such as early-onset osteoarthritis. Retuve represents a significant step towards accessible, transparent, and equitable healthcare solutions for infant hip health - and a model for medical imaging AI tools to solve other clinical problems.

Outcomes of endovascular thrombectomy in acute ischemic stroke patients with insulin resistance
Authors: Yuanxiang Huang, Ximing Nie, Minghua Wang, Yiwen Xu, Zeyu Li, Lina Zheng, Xin Liu, Wei Chen, Hongyi Yan, Liping Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundFor patients with acute ischemic stroke (AIS) caused by large vessel occlusion, endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) serves as the standard treatment. However, a significant proportion of patients fail to achieve functional independence despite successful recanalization. While insulin resistance (IR) has been implicated in adverse stroke outcomes, its precise impact on AIS patients undergoing EVT remains incompletely characterized.MethodsThis multicenter cohort study acquired data from the RESCUE-RE registry to explore the association between IR, quantified via the triglyceride glucose (TyG) index, and 90-day functional outcomes in AIS patients undergoing EVT. The primary outcome was functional dependence, defined as a modified Rankin Scale score ≥3 at 90 days. Secondary outcomes included symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage, all-cause mortality, early neurological deterioration, and infarct volume at 24 hours, as assessed by follow-up imaging.ResultsAmong 1305 patients, higher TyG index quartiles were notably associated with unfavorable clinical outcomes. Patients with the highest TyG quartile (Q4) exhibited increased odds of functional dependence (adjusted odds ratio (aOR) 1.79, 95% confidence interval (95% CI) 1.26 to 2.54), early neurological deterioration (aOR 2.48, 95% CI 1.46 to 4.22), and mortality (OR 2.62, 95% CI 1.68 to 4.09) compared with those with the lowest TyG quartile (Q1). Stratified analyses revealed that these associations were more pronounced in younger patients, females, and individuals with hypertension, diabetes, and favorable collateral circulation. Mediation analyses identified inflammation as a partial explanatory factor in the observed relationships.ConclusionsInsulin resistance is independently associated with deteriorated functional outcomes in AIS patients undergoing EVT.Keywords: Stroke, Thrombectomy

Introduction

Stroke persists as a leading cause of death and disability around the world, and its burden has significantly increased over the past years.1 Endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) is a proven treatment for acute ischemic stroke (AIS) caused by large vessel occlusion, with robust evidence from randomized trials confirming its efficacy and safety within 24 hours from symptom onset.2 Despite advancements in AIS management making rapid progress, a substantial proportion of patients fail to achieve recovery even with effective reperfusion therapies. Over half of the patients who successfully underwent recanalization with EVT failed to achieve functional independence at 3 months, a phenomenon known as futile recanalization, which is observed in both acute anterior and posterior circulation occlusions.3

Insulin resistance (IR), one of the prevailing pathophysiological defects in type 2 diabetes mellitus, has emerged as an independent predictor of poor functional outcome after stroke.4 Notably, beyond its established association with stroke recurrence and mortality, previous studies indicated that IR severity correlates with unfavorable outcomes in stroke patients after intravenous thrombolysis.5 However, the relationship between IR and functional outcomes after EVT remains unclear. The Homeostasis Model Assessment of Insulin Resistance (HOMA-IR), developed in 1985, is commonly utilized to assess IR by evaluating fasting insulin and glucose levels.6 Although HOMA-IR is widely utilized in glucose metabolic research, its reliance on fasting insulin measurements limits clinical utility in non-metabolic studies. This gap has propelled the triglyceride-glucose (TyG) index, calculated as ln[fasting triglycerides (mg/dL) × fasting glucose (mg/dL)/2], to prominence as an alternative biochemical marker for IR.7 In AIS patients with hypertension, TyG is associated with symptomatic intracranial atherosclerosis and 90-day modified Rankin Scale score (mRS).8 Additionally, the TyG index is correlated with mRS in stroke patients undergoing intravenous thrombolysis.9 Inflammation assumes a crucial and central position in determining the prognosis of ischemic vascular diseases.10 Previous studies showed that IR was positively associated with inflammatory biomarkers such as high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP),11 suggesting that inflammatory responses may play a mediating role between IR and the pathological changes following ischemic stroke.

The objective of this study was to investigate the relationship between IR and clinical functional outcomes among AIS patients treated with EVT, while exploring the potential mediating role of inflammatory factors.

Methods

Study design and subjects

Subjects were recruited from the RESCUE-RE study (Registration Study for Critical Care of Acute Ischemic Stroke After Recanalization), which is an ongoing prospective observational cohort. This investigation encompassed adult individuals who have suffered from AIS due to large vessel occlusion and are undergoing EVT. These patients were from 18 comprehensive stroke centers across China.12 The cohort was registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR1900022154). The study was approved by the medical ethics committee of Beijing Tiantan Hospital, Capital Medical University (approval no. KY2018‐057‐01) and all the other participating centers. All patients or their legal representatives were informed of the research content and provided their consent. The handling and treatment of patients in every participating center were carried out in line with the local clinical care procedures and the most recent international standards and guidelines.13

Patients in the RESCUE-RE cohort who met the subsequent criteria were enrolled: (1) age >18 years; (2) AIS confirmed by cerebral imaging; (3) AIS patients treated by endovascular treatment within 24 hours from stroke onset; (4) patients were followed up for 90 days. Patients without a baseline biochemical test to assess IR were excluded from the current analysis.

Baseline data collection

Researchers with professional training collected clinical data of included patients through face-to-face interviews or electronic medical records following a standardized data collection protocol. The information included the patients’ population characteristics, current smoking status, medical histories of hypertension, diabetes, coronary atherosclerotic disease, atrial fibrillation, dyslipidemia, status of thrombolysis, site of occlusion, and the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score at admission. The collateral status was determined based on the ASITN/SIR (American Society of Intervention and Therapeutic Neuroradiology/Society of Interventional Radiology) collateral grading.14 The etiological classification of ischemic stroke was established using the criteria outlined in the TOAST (Trial of ORG 10172 in Acute Stroke Treatment) system.15

Outcome measurements

The primary outcome was functional dependence defined as an mRS score from 3 to 6 at 3 months follow-up. Secondary outcomes included 90-day death, symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH), early neurologic deterioration (END), and 24-hour infarct volume. Trained research coordinators, who were masked to the clinical information, collected 90-day follow-up outcomes through in-clinic interviews or telephone surveys. sICH was termed as any instance of ICH accompanied by neurological decline (≥4 point increase in the NIHSS score) within 7 days of symptom occurrence. END was referred to as an NIHSS ≥4 at 24 hours post EVT. The 24-hour infarct volume was defined as the infarct volume measured by CT scan 24 hours after EVT.

Blood sample measurements

Blood samples were obtained within a 24-hour window after EVT and were measured using standard laboratory procedures. The TyG index was computed according to the following formula: TyG=ln[fasting triglycerides (mg/dL) × fasting glucose (mg/dL)/2]. Neutrophil-to-platelet ratio (NPR) is the ratio of the number of neutrophils to platelets in the blood, while neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) is the ratio of the number of neutrophils to lymphocytes. The systemic immune inflammation index (SII) was calculated by using the following formula: (neutrophil counts × platelet counts)/lymphocyte counts. These ratios were used as markers to assess inflammation and immune response.

Statistical analyses

Statistical analyses were conducted using SAS software version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Inc, Cary, NC). Continuous variables were expressed as mean±SD or median with IQR, while categorical variables were reported as counts and percentages. For comparing continuous variables, Student’s t-tests or Mann-Whitney U tests were employed, whereas χ2 or Fisher’s exact tests were used to analyze categorical variables. Multivariate logistic regression was applied to estimate odds ratios (OR) and 95% confidence intervals (95% CI) for the independent associations between IR and clinical and imaging outcomes. This analysis adjusted for selected confounders based on their clinical relevance and results from univariable analyses. Subgroup analyses were conducted to evaluate heterogeneity in the TyG index on the primary outcome with statistical tests of interactions. In the mediation analysis, the TyG index was a predictor variable (X), inflammatory markers were mediators (M), and 90-day mRS was the outcome variable (Y). Receiver-operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to determine the performance of the TyG index and the cut-off value in predicting the primary outcome. All analyses were two-sided. Statistical significance was defined by P<0.05.

Results

Baseline characteristics

The current study cohort comprised 1305 patients undergoing EVT in RESCUE-RE between July 2018 and June 2022 (online supplemental figure 1). Among all patients (mean age 63.96±12.32 years, 68.20% males), the median NIHSS score at baseline was 14 (IQR 11–19), and the median time from onset to reperfusion was 8 hours 20 mins; 32.4% patients received intravenous thrombolysis before EVT, which aligns with previous registry-based reports.16 Baseline characteristics of the subjects according to the functional outcome status were presented in online supplemental table 1. Association between TyG index in quartiles and the outcomes

Among the patients, 798 (61.15%) were found to have the primary outcome of a poor functional prognosis and 217 (27.19%) patients died at the 3-month follow-up. A dose-dependent relationship emerged between TyG quartiles and poor functional prognosis, with higher quartiles demonstrating progressively worse outcomes. This trend extended to secondary outcomes, including worsening NIHSS scores at 24 hours and 7 days, 90-day mortality, and early neurological deterioration. No significant associations were observed for sICH or 24-hour infarct volume (table 1).

Figure 1 illustrates the distribution of mRS scores at 3 months stratified by TyG index quartiles. A significant shift was observed towards unfavorable functional outcomes in patients with higher TyG quartiles. A restricted cubic spline curve confirmed a linear dose-response relationship between TyG elevation and poor functional prognosis (online supplemental figure 2).

Distribution of functional outcomes at 90 days. Distribution of the modified Rankin Scale scores at 90 days in acute ischemic stroke receiving endovascular treatment according to triglyceride-glucose (TyG) index by quartile.

After adjustments for age, sex, history of smoking, hypertension, atrial fibrillation, and admission NIHSS, patients with the TyG index in Q4 were associated with increased risks of unfavorable functional outcome (adjusted OR (aOR) 1.79, 95% CI 1.26 to 2.54, P<0.01), early neurologic deterioration (aOR 2.48, 95% CI 1.46 to 4.22, P<0.01), and 90-day death (aOR 2.62, 95% CI 1.68 to 4.09, P<0.01) compared with patients with TyG index in Q1 (table 2). Neither sICH nor 24-hour infarct volume exhibited TyG-dependent associations. An ROC curve was employed to evaluate the predictive value of the TyG index and the unfavorable outcome (online supplemental figure 3 and table 3).

Subgroup analyses

The results derived from the subgroup analyses targeting the primary outcome are presented in online supplemental figure 4. The states of IR were differentiated based on the median of the TyG index (TyG=8.71). Notably, patients with a higher TyG index were significantly correlated with the functional outcome in certain subgroups of special interests, including patients aged <80 years (OR 1.52, 95% CI 1.20 to 1.91, P<0.01), female (OR 1.94, 95% CI 1.28 to 2.94, P<0.01), patients with hypertension (OR 1.49, 95% CI 1.11 to 2.00, P<0.01), diabetes (OR 1.95, 95% CI 1.18 to 3.22, P<0.01), good collateral status (OR 1.40, 95% CI 1.09 to 1.79, P<0.01), posterior circulation (OR 1.78, 95% CI 1.11 to 2.85, P=0.02), intracranial atherosclerosis (OR 1.45, 95% CI 1.10 to 1.92, P<0.01), and those without intravenous thrombolysis (OR 1.41, 95% CI 1.07 to 1.86, P=0.01). There was no significant heterogeneity in interaction observed among the remaining subgroups.

Mediation analysis

An increased quartile of TyG index was independently associated with clinical outcomes. In addition, the TyG index was associated with NPR and borderline associated with SII, indicating that IR was positively associated with inflammatory markers. Those inflammatory indices were also associated with unfavorable clinical outcomes. The mediation analysis (table 3) demonstrated a significant partial mediation effect for systemic inflammation on the association of IR with clinical outcomes. After adjustments for the potential covariates, IR estimated by the TyG index still had a substantial association with clinical outcomes. In the adjusted model, the proportion mediated by NPR was 11.76% for unfavorable functional prognosis. There was no evidence of SII mediating IR on clinical outcomes.

Discussion

In this multicenter registry study of AIS patients treated with EVT, patients with a higher TyG index quartile exhibited a higher prevalence of pre-existing diabetes mellitus, hypertension, and atrial fibrillation. We demonstrated a significant association between IR, quantified by the TyG index, and multiple clinical outcomes, including functional dependence, neurological severity, 90-day mortality, and early neurological deterioration following EVT.

In AIS patients without receiving thrombolysis or mechanical thrombolysis, previous studies have shown that TyG-based IR was strongly associated with the incidence of ischemic stroke.17 18 AIS patients with a higher TyG index were also associated with a higher risk of stroke recurrence in elderly patients.19 The correlation between the TyG index and poor functional outcomes remains controversial. While a meta-analysis of 18 studies (n=592 635) found no significant association between TyG and unfavorable functional outcome (OR 1.12, 95% CI 0.88 to 1.43),20 subsequent meta-analyses have reported contradictory findings, with one demonstrating an independent association (OR 1.37, 95% CI 1.11 to 1.69).21 Notably, emerging evidence increasingly supported the prognostic value of elevated TyG index for predicting unfavorable functional outcomes.22 In AIS patients undergoing reperfusion therapy, a multicenter prospective cohort research study indicated that TyG index elevation correlated with worse 90-day functional prognosis in intravenous thrombolysis-treated patients (OR 1.32, 95% CI 1.01 to 1.73).9 To our knowledge, the present research was among the first to highlight the association between IR and functional prognosis in EVT-treated AIS patients. These findings were consistent with previous studies in ischemic stroke patients with or without reperfusion therapy, collectively suggesting that IR, particularly when manifesting as high TyG burden, may universally exacerbate functional impairment in AIS populations.

Our results indicated that there is no association between sICH and TyG index, which is consistent with the other two independent studies among AIS patients treated with intravenous thrombolysis.9 23 Compared with patients without vessel recanalization, blood pressure control may be more important than hyperglycemia in influencing the risk of sICH in AIS patients undergoing reperfusion therapy. For infarct volume, there was only one small-sample study examining the relationship between IR and infarct volume.8 It showed that there was no significant association between IR and infarct volume, which is consistent with our finding. In subgroup analysis, we found that in patients with good collateral circulation, a higher TyG index was associated with unfavorable outcomes, suggesting that IR may influence post-EVT outcomes primarily via microcirculatory damage rather than by altering infarct volume.

Consistent with the established literature documenting IR’s frequent comorbidity with conventional vascular risk factors (hypertension, obesity, diabetes mellitus),24 our study revealed that TyG-associated risks were amplified in patients with these comorbidities. Our subgroup analyses also indicated that in patients with good collateral circulation, a higher TyG index was associated with unfavorable clinical prognosis, which suggested that the impact of IR is achieved by influencing the microcirculatory function. Previous research indicated that a significant association existed between IR and poor functional prognosis among stroke patients without diabetes.4 However, our study found that in AIS patients undergoing EVT, IR was more closely linked to prognosis in those with diabetes. Differences in the patients’ enrollment and the models to assess IR may explain this discrepancy.

The pathophysiological mechanisms underlying the prognostic significance of IR in EVT-treated patients may involve multiple interrelated pathways. Insulin resistance promotes prothrombotic states through platelet hyperreactivity and endothelial dysfunction,25 resulting in chronic inflammation and endothelium dysfunction.26 For ischemia–reperfusion injury, the proposed mechanisms stem from dysfunctional glucose metabolism and upregulated inflammatory cytokines, evidence of which has been found in multiple insulin-resistant obesity experimental models.27 Moreover, a previous study indicated that inflammatory cytokines partially mediated the association between IR and the prognosis of ischemic stroke without endovascular treatment.28 In the present study, we discovered that inflammatory markers were related not only to unfavorable outcomes following endovascular treatment but also acted as a mediator between IR and adverse functional outcomes. Other possible effects of IR on unfavorable functional outcomes include abnormal coagulation function. It is reported that IR is associated with a procoagulant plasma profile. Fibrinogen and von Willebrand factor (vWF) were increased in IR subjects, which reflects adipose tissue inflammation and increased hepatic production of coagulation factors.29 The role of coagulation-associated factors, such as vWF, in the mechanisms of IR-related inflammatory responses warrants further research.

Despite the significant findings of this study, there are several limitations that warrant our attention. First, as an observational study, we cannot establish a causal relationship between IR and functional outcomes in AIS patients treated with EVT. There may be unmeasured confounding factors influencing the results that were not accounted for in the analysis. Randomized controlled trials that are aimed at reducing IR, such as metformin or SGLT2 (sodium-glucose transport protein 2) inhibitors, are warranted to assess their potential benefits in improving functional outcomes after EVT. Second, while we included a large cohort of patients from multiple centers, the study’s generalizability may be limited by the fact that all participating centers were in China. Additional large-scale multicenter studies are needed to confirm the generalizability of these findings across different populations and settings. Lastly, compared with interleukins or tumor necrosis factor, the inflammatory markers in the current study lack specificity. In future research, a biobank could be established to measure more targeted inflammatory mediators across temporal dimensions.

In conclusion, this study reveals that the TyG index—an indicator of IR—is independently associated with unfavorable functional outcomes in AIS patients treated with EVT. Elevated TyG levels are linked to heightened risks of functional dependence, early neurological deterioration, and mortality. Additionally, inflammation partially mediates these relationships, pointing to its potential role as a therapeutic target. These findings emphasize the TyG index’s prognostic significance and call for further exploration of IR as a modifiable factor in stroke management after EVT.

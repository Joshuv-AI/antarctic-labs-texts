On the existence of a singular limit equation for a model of a self-propelled object motion


Abstract
In this paper, a phase‑field model is introduced to describe the evolution of a deformable, self‑propelled object driven by surface‑tension effects. The model couples an Allen–Cahn‑type equation, which distinguishes the body from the surrounding fluid, with a reaction-diffusion equation for the surfactant concentration. As the interface‑thickness parameter ε \varepsilon tends to zero, it is shown that the phase‑field model converges to a sharp-interface limit coupled with a reaction-diffusion equation. In particular, the normal velocity is given by the mean curvature, surface tension, and volume-preserving effect.

Introduction

A self-propelled system is a system in motion by the action of an object deforming or an object changing its field of motion.
The motion of bacteria and cells in living systems and the non-living systems, such as camphor and pentanol droplets, are considered to belong to self-propelled systems because they have internal mechanisms to generate motion.
In recent years, mathematical models have been used to analyze keratocyte motion

Lemma 2.2

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}(u^{\varepsilon})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

(I)

Next, we show that for each

(3.12)

Let

\begin{cases}\Delta w=\rho_{\delta}\ast G(\varphi^{\varepsilon})-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\delta}\ast G(\varphi^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}x&in $\Omega$,\\

(3.13)

Similar to the case of Neumann boundary condition,
the solution exists and is unique. Set
\vec{\zeta}=\nabla w

Then we have

\displaystyle\|\vec{\zeta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\|\nabla\vec{\zeta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\|w\|_{C^{2,\beta}(\Omega)}\leq C(\delta),

(3.14)

where

\delta

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}G(\varphi^{\varepsilon})\mathrm{div}\,\vec{\zeta}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}G(\varphi^{\varepsilon})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}(\rho_{\delta}\ast G(\varphi^{\varepsilon})-G(\varphi^{\varepsilon}))G(\varphi^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}x

(3.16)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\delta}\ast G(\varphi^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{\Omega}G(\varphi^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}x.

(3.17)

We estimate each term below.

(3.18)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\tilde{G}(\varphi^{\varepsilon})^{2}-\int_{\Omega}\tilde{G}(\varphi^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

(3.21)

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}G(\varphi^{\varepsilon})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{36}\int_{\Omega}\tilde{G}(\varphi^{\varepsilon})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\frac{1}{36}\left(\int_{\Omega}\tilde{G}(\varphi^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}x-44E(t)\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\int_{\Omega}G(\varphi^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{11}{9}E(t)\varepsilon.

(3.23)

(3.25)

for sufficiently small

(3.30)

(3.37)

(3.38)

(3.39)

, since
\gamma^{\prime}(u^{\varepsilon_{i}})
is bounded, by estimating
\vec{\eta}
by its
L^{\infty}
-norm and applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle L_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(G(\varphi^{\varepsilon_{i}})-G(\varphi))^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{\varepsilon_{i}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}.

(3.80)

Note that
\{\|\nabla u^{\varepsilon_{i}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T))}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is bounded,
|G(\varphi^{\varepsilon_{i}})-G(\varphi)|\leq C
since
\{\varphi^{\varepsilon_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
and
\varphi
are bounded.
Moreover,
\varphi^{\varepsilon_{i}}\to\varphi
for a.e.
(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
by Lemma
2.8
.
Thus, the Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem implies that
L_{1}\to 0
as
i\to\infty
.
As for
L_{2}
, since
G(\varphi)\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,T))
, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality yields that

\displaystyle L_{2}\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\gamma^{\prime}(u^{\varepsilon_{i}})-\gamma^{\prime}(u))^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{\varepsilon_{i}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}.

(3.81)

Since
\{\|\nabla u^{\varepsilon_{i}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T))}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is bounded and
\{u^{\varepsilon_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
strongly converges to
u
in
L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T))
, we have
L_{2}\to 0
as
i\to\infty
.
Finally, since
G(\varphi)\gamma^{\prime}(u)\vec{\eta}\in L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T))
and
\{u^{\varepsilon_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
weakly converges to
u
in
H^{1}(\Omega\times(0,T))
, we see that
L_{3}\to 0
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle K_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}G(\varphi^{\varepsilon_{i}})\gamma^{\prime}(u^{\varepsilon_{i}})\nabla u^{\varepsilon_{i}}\cdot\vec{\eta}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

(3.82)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}G(\varphi)\gamma^{\prime}(u)\nabla u\cdot\vec{\eta}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{1}{6}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}(\varphi,t)}\gamma^{\prime}(u)\nabla u\cdot\vec{\eta}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

(3.83)

holds, where we have used
G(u^{\varepsilon_{i}})\rightarrow G(u)
and
\gamma^{\prime}(u^{\varepsilon_{i}})\rightarrow\gamma^{\prime}(u)
as
i\to\infty
in
L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T))
.
For
K_{2}
, we obtain in a similar manner that

\displaystyle K_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}G(\varphi^{\varepsilon_{i}})\gamma(u^{\varepsilon_{i}})\operatorname{div}\vec{\eta}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

(3.84)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}G(\varphi)\gamma(u)\operatorname{div}\vec{\eta}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{1}{6}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}(\varphi,t)}\gamma(u)\operatorname{div}\vec{\eta}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

(3.85)

holds.
As a result, we obtain

\displaystyle K_{1}+K_{2}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\frac{1}{6}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}(\varphi,t)}\left[\gamma^{\prime}(u)\nabla u\cdot\vec{\eta}+\gamma(u)\operatorname{div}\vec{\eta}\right]\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

(3.86)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}(\varphi,t)}\operatorname{div}(\gamma(u)\vec{\eta})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

(3.87)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\partial^{\ast}\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}(\varphi,t)}\gamma(u)\vec{\eta}\cdot\vec{\nu}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\,\mathrm{d}t

(3.88)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\gamma(u)\vec{\phi}\cdot\vec{\nu}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}|_{\partial^{\ast}\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}(\varphi,t)}}{\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t.

(3.89)

Here, let us show that the following equality holds:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}(\varphi,t)}\operatorname{div}(\gamma(u)\vec{\eta})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\partial^{\ast}\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}(\varphi,t)}\gamma(u)\vec{\eta}\cdot\vec{\nu}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\,\mathrm{d}t.

(3.90)

Since
u\in H^{1}(\Omega\times(0,T))
, we see that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|u(x,t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,\ \int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x,t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty.

(3.91)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u(x,t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,\ \int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x,t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty\qquad\text{for a.e. }t\geq 0;

(3.92)

that is,
u(\cdot,t)\in H^{1}(\Omega)
for a.e.
t\geq 0
.
Choose
t\geq 0
such that
u(\cdot,t)\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and take an approximate sequence
\{u^{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
; that is,
u^{j}\to u(\cdot,t)
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
.
Since each
u^{j}
is smooth, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{in}(\varphi,t)}}\operatorname{div}(\gamma(u^{j})\vec{\eta})\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\partial^{\ast}\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}(\varphi,t)}\gamma(u^{j})\vec{\eta}\cdot\vec{\nu}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

(3.93)

It is clear that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}(\varphi,t)}\operatorname{div}(\gamma(u^{j})\vec{\eta})\,\mathrm{d}x\to\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}(\varphi,t)}\operatorname{div}(\gamma(u)\vec{\eta})\,\mathrm{d}x\qquad\text{as }j\to\infty.

(3.94)

Therefore, we consider the right-hand side of (
3.93
) and show that it is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathbb{R}
.
Choose a function
\zeta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\zeta=1
on
\Omega=[0,1)^{n}
.
Suppose for a moment that
D(t)
, defined by

\displaystyle D(t)\coloneqq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\Omega\\
r\in(0,1)\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{\ast}\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}(\varphi,t)\cap B_{r}(x))}{r^{n-1}},

(3.95)

is finite for a.e.
t>0
.
Then, it follows from
[
27
, Lemma 4.9.1]
that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\partial^{\ast}\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}(\varphi,t)}\gamma(u^{k})\vec{\eta}\cdot\vec{\nu}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\int_{\partial^{\ast}\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}(\varphi,t)}\gamma(u^{j})\vec{\eta}\cdot\vec{\nu}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right|

(3.96)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\partial^{\ast}\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}(\varphi,t)}|\gamma(u^{j})-\gamma(u^{k})|\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq C\int_{\partial^{\ast}\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}(\varphi,t)}|\zeta(\gamma(u^{k})-\gamma(u^{j}))|\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

(3.97)

\displaystyle\leq CD(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla(\zeta(\gamma(u^{k})-\gamma(u^{j})))|\,\mathrm{d}x.

(3.98)

Since
\{u^{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
converges to
u(\cdot,t)
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
, it is a Cauchy sequence in
H^{1}(\Omega)
, and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla(\zeta(\gamma(u^{k})-\gamma(u^{j})))|\,\mathrm{d}x

(3.99)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\zeta||\nabla(\gamma(u^{k})-\gamma(u^{j}))|\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla\zeta|\cdot|\gamma(u^{k})-\gamma(u^{j})|\,\mathrm{d}x\to 0

(3.100)

as
j,k\to\infty
.
Therefore, the right-hand side of (
3.93
) is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathbb{R}
.
Note that
the trace operator

T:W^{1,1}(\Omega)\to L^{1}(\partial^{\ast}\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}(\varphi,t))
is well-defined since one can show
\|g^{1}-g^{2}\|_{L^{1}(\partial^{\ast}\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}(\varphi,t))}\leq CD(t)\|g^{1}-g^{2}\|_{W^{1,1}(\Omega)}
for any
g^{1},g^{2}\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)
similarly.
Hence we see that

\displaystyle\int_{\partial^{\ast}\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}(\varphi,t)}\gamma(u^{j})\vec{\eta}\cdot\vec{\nu}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\to\int_{\partial^{\ast}\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}(\varphi,t)}\gamma(u)\vec{\eta}\cdot\vec{\nu}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

(3.101)

This completes the proof of (
3.90
).

We show that
D(t)
is finite for a.e.
t>0
.
To this end, we first prove that there exists a subsequence of
\{\varepsilon_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
, denoted by the same symbol, such that

\displaystyle\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{i}|\nabla\varphi^{\varepsilon_{i}}|^{2}}{2}+\frac{W(\varphi^{\varepsilon_{i}})}{2\varepsilon_{i}}\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon_{i}\left(\triangle\varphi^{\varepsilon_{i}}-\frac{W^{\prime}(\varphi^{\varepsilon_{i}})}{2\varepsilon_{i}}\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right\}<\infty

(3.102)

for a.e.
t>0
.
Since Lemma
2.3
(I) readily shows that

\displaystyle\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{i}|\nabla\varphi^{\varepsilon_{i}}|^{2}}{2}+\frac{W(\varphi^{\varepsilon_{i}})}{2\varepsilon_{i}}\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty,

(3.103)

we focus on the second term.
It follows from Lemma
2.3
(II) that

\displaystyle\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(\varepsilon_{i}\triangle\varphi^{\varepsilon_{i}}-\frac{W^{\prime}(\varphi^{\varepsilon_{i}})}{2\varepsilon_{i}}\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty

(3.104)

holds.
Applying the Fatou’s lemma, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon_{i}\left(\triangle\varphi^{\varepsilon_{i}}-\frac{W^{\prime}(\varphi^{\varepsilon_{i}})}{2\varepsilon_{i}}\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

(3.105)

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{i\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon_{i}\left(\triangle\varphi^{\varepsilon_{i}}-\frac{W^{\prime}(\varphi^{\varepsilon_{i}})}{2\varepsilon_{i}}\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

(3.106)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{T}\left(\liminf_{i\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon_{i}\left(\triangle\varphi^{\varepsilon_{i}}-\frac{W^{\prime}(\varphi^{\varepsilon_{i}})}{2\varepsilon_{i}}\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\,\mathrm{d}t.

(3.107)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\liminf_{i\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon_{i}\left(\triangle\varphi^{\varepsilon_{i}}-\frac{W^{\prime}(\varphi^{\varepsilon_{i}})}{2\varepsilon_{i}}\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

(3.108)

is finite for a.e.
t>0
.
Thus, taking a subsequence of
\{\varepsilon_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
if necessary, (
3.102
) holds.
We next prove that

\displaystyle D(t)\leq\frac{1}{3}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\Omega\\
r\in(0,1)\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu_{t}(B_{2r}(x))}{r^{n-1}}

(3.109)

holds.
Note that by applying the Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla G(\varphi^{\varepsilon})|

\displaystyle=|G^{\prime}(\varphi^{\varepsilon})\nabla\varphi^{\varepsilon}|=\sqrt{2W(\varphi^{\varepsilon})}|\nabla\varphi^{\varepsilon}|\leq\frac{\varepsilon|\nabla\varphi^{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{2}+\frac{2W(\varphi^{\varepsilon})}{2\varepsilon}

(3.110)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\frac{\varepsilon|\nabla\varphi^{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{2}+\frac{W(\varphi^{\varepsilon})}{2\varepsilon}\right).

(3.111)

Using lower semi-continuity, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{\ast}\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}(\varphi,t)\cap B_{r}(x))

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\chi_{B_{r}(x)}(y)\,\mathrm{d}\|\nabla\chi_{\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}(\varphi,t)}\|(y)

(3.112)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{i\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\chi_{B_{r}(x)}(y)\frac{1}{6}|\nabla G(\varphi^{\varepsilon_{i}}(y,t))|\,\mathrm{d}y

(3.113)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{3}\liminf_{i\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\chi_{B_{r}(x)}(y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}^{\varepsilon_{i}}

(3.114)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{3}\mu_{t}(B_{2r}(x)).

(3.115)

Since (
3.102
) holds, using
[
21
, Proposition 4.5]
, we see that
D(t)
is finite for a.e.
t>0
.

Summarizing the above calculations, we conclude that

\vec{v}=\vec{h}-\frac{1}{6}\tilde{S}[\varphi]\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}|_{\partial^{\ast}\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}(\varphi,t)}}{\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}}\right)\vec{\nu}+\frac{1}{6}\gamma(u)\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}|_{\partial^{\ast}\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}(\varphi,t)}}{\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}}\right)\vec{\nu}

(3.116)

in the sense of distribution.
Using the relation (
2.75
), we obtain

\vec{v}=\vec{h}-\sqrt{2}\tilde{S}[\varphi]\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}|_{\partial^{\ast}\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}(\varphi,t)}}{\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}}\right)\vec{\nu}+\sqrt{2}\gamma(u)\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}|_{\partial^{\ast}\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}(\varphi,t)}}{\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}}\right)\vec{\nu}.

(3.117)

∎

(4.2)

See

]
for formal derivation methods.
Here, we assume that the interface is a single layer in (

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}|_{\partial^{\ast}\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}(\varphi,t)}}{\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}}=1.

Then, (

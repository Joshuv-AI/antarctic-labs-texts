The Ax-Kochen-Ershov principles via the higher valued hyperfield
Junguk Lee

Abstract
. In this paper, we concern the model theory of finitely ramified henselian valued fields via higher valued hyperfields. Most of all, we provide a number of Ax-Kochen-Ershov Theorems for finitely ramified henselian valued fields relative to higher valued hyperfields. As corollaries, we deduce a transfer of decidability for full theories and existential theories of a finitely ramified henselian valued fields relative to higher valued hyperfields.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we concern model theory of finitely ramified henselian valued fields via higher valued hyperfields.

A valued field
(K,\nu)
of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
is called
finitely ramified
if the interval
(0,\nu(p)]
in the value group is finite, and its cardinality
e
is called the
initial ramification index
. If the initial ramification index
e
is one, then the valued fields is called
unramified
. Let
\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
be the valuation ring of
K
with the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}
. Let
\nu K
be the value group and we say
(K,\nu)
is
\mathbb{Z}
-valued field if
\nu K\cong\mathbb{Z}
. Let
K\nu
be the residue field of
K
and
\operatorname{res}_{\nu}:\mathcal{O}_{\nu}\rightarrow K\nu
be the natural projection map. For each
n\geq 1
, let
\mathcal{O}_{\nu,n}
be the quotient of
\mathcal{O}_{\nu,n}
by
\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}^{n}
.

In
[
3
]
, Anscombe, Dittmann, and Jahnke proved completeness, existential completeness, and model completeness results for theories of finitely ramified henselian valued fields relative to residue fields and value groups.

Fact 1.1
.

[
3
, Theorem 5.1, Corollary 5.6, Theorem 5.13]

If
(K,\nu)
and
(L,\omega)
are finitely ramified henselian valued fields of the same initial ramification index, then

\underbrace{(K,\nu)\equiv(L,\omega)}_{\mbox{ in }\mathcal{L}_{val}}\Leftrightarrow\underbrace{K\nu\equiv L\omega}_{\mbox{ in }\mathcal{L}_{p,e}}\mbox{ and }\underbrace{\nu K\equiv\omega L}_{\mbox{ in }\mathcal{L}_{oag}}

and

\underbrace{(K,\nu)\equiv_{\exists}(L,\omega)}_{\mbox{ in }\mathcal{L}_{val}}\Leftrightarrow\underbrace{K\nu\equiv_{\exists}L\omega}_{\mbox{ in }\mathcal{L}_{p,e}}

and,
in case
(K,\nu)\subseteq(L,\omega)
,

\underbrace{(K,\nu)\preceq_{\exists}(L,\omega)}_{\mbox{ in }\mathcal{L}_{val}}\Leftrightarrow\underbrace{K\nu\preceq_{\exists}L\omega}_{\mbox{ in }\mathcal{L}_{p,e}}\mbox{ and }\underbrace{\nu K\preceq_{\exists}\omega L}_{\mbox{ in }\mathcal{L}_{oag}},

where
\mathcal{L}_{val}
is the three-sorted language of valued fields,
\mathcal{L}_{oag}
the language of ordered abelian groups, and
\mathcal{L}_{p,e}
is an expansion of the ring language
\mathcal{L}_{ring}
by adding some finitely many relation symbols, which are existentially definable in
\mathcal{L}_{val}
(see the first paragraph of Section
2
for precise definitions of the languages
\mathcal{L}_{val}
,
\mathcal{L}_{oag}
, and
\mathcal{L}_{ring}
).

Most of all, their AKE principle for elementary equivalence strengths the results of Basarab and of Lee and the author. In
[
4
, Theorem 3.1]
, Basarab proved that

\underbrace{(K,\nu)\equiv(L,\omega)}_{\mbox{ in }\mathcal{L}_{val}}\Leftrightarrow\underbrace{\mathcal{O}_{\nu,n}\equiv\mathcal{O}_{\omega,n}}_{\mbox{ in }\mathcal{L}_{ring}}\mbox{ for all }n\geq 1\mbox{ and }\underbrace{\nu K\equiv\omega L}_{\mbox{ in }\mathcal{L}_{oag}}.

If residue fields are perfect, we in
[
10
, Theorem 5.2]
showed that it is enough to consider a single
n=n_{0}
depending only on
p
and
e
, not considering all
n
simultaneously.

Meanwhile, we in
[
9
]
proved the AKE principle for elementary equivalence relative to higher valued hyperfields when residue fields are perfect. Given a valued field
(K,\nu)
and a positive integer
n\geq 1
, the
n
th valued hyperfield
is
\mathcal{H}_{\nu,n}:=K/(1+\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}^{n})
. The higher valued hyperfield forms a certain valued field-like algebraic structure with a multivalued addition. We consider higher valued hyperfields as structures in the one-sorted language
\mathcal{L}_{vhf}
of valued hyperfields (see Subsection
2.2
for the detailed definition of the language). Given integers
n
and
m
, we write
n|m
if
n
divides
m
.

Fact 1.2
.

[
9
, Theorem 5.8, Corollary 5.9]

Let
(K,\nu)
and
(L,\omega)
be finitely ramified henselian valued fields of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
having the same initial ramification index
e
. Let
n
be a positive integer satisfying that

\begin{cases}n>0&\mbox{if }p\not|e,\\
n>e^{2}M_{\nu}&\mbox{if }p|e.\end{cases}

Then,

\underbrace{(K,\nu)\equiv(L,\omega)}_{\mbox{ in }\mathcal{L}_{val}}\Leftrightarrow\underbrace{(\mathcal{H}_{\nu,n},[p]_{n})\equiv(\mathcal{H}_{\omega,n},[p]_{n})}_{\mbox{ in }\mathcal{L}_{vhf}}.

Our main results in this paper are several versions of Ax-Kochen-Ershov principles for finitely ramified henselian valued fields relative to higher valued hyperfields without any restriction on residue fields, analogous to Fact
1.1
and generalizing Fact
1.2
.

Main Theorem

(Theorem
4.3
, Theorem
4.6
, Theorem
4.9
)
.

Let
(K,\nu)
and
(L,\omega)
be finitely ramified henselian valued fields of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
having the same initial ramification index
e
. Let
n
be a positive integer satisfying that

\begin{cases}n>0&\mbox{if }p\not|e,\\
n>e^{2}M_{\nu}&\mbox{if }p|e.\end{cases}

Then,

\underbrace{(K,\nu)\equiv(L,\omega)}_{\mbox{ in }\mathcal{L}_{val}}\Leftrightarrow\underbrace{(\mathcal{H}_{\nu,n},[p]_{n})\equiv(\mathcal{H}_{\omega,n},[p]_{n})}_{\mbox{ in }\mathcal{L}_{vhf}}

and

\underbrace{(K,\nu)\equiv_{\exists}(L,\omega)}_{\mbox{ in }\mathcal{L}_{val}}\Leftrightarrow\underbrace{(\mathcal{H}_{\nu,n},[p])\equiv_{\exists}(\mathcal{H}_{\omega,n},[p]_{n})}_{\mbox{ in }\mathcal{L}_{vhf}}

and,
in case
(K,\nu)\subseteq(L,\omega)
,

\underbrace{(K,\nu)\preceq_{\exists}(L,\omega)}_{\mbox{ in }\mathcal{L}_{val}}\Leftrightarrow\underbrace{\mathcal{H}_{\nu,n}\preceq_{\exists}\mathcal{H}_{\omega,n}}_{\mbox{ in }\mathcal{L}_{vhf}}.

As corollaries, we deduce a transfer of decidability for full theories (see Corollary
4.4
) and existential theories (see Corollary
4.7
). Note that Kartas proved that for henselian valued fields
(K,\nu)
and
(L,\omega)
tamely (and not necessarily finitely) ramified extensions of
(\mathbb{Q},\nu_{p})
with the
p
-adic valuation
\nu_{p}
,

\underbrace{(\mathcal{H}_{\nu,1},[p]_{1})\equiv_{\exists}(\mathcal{H}_{\omega,1},[p]_{1})}_{\mathcal{L}_{vhf}}\Rightarrow\underbrace{(K,\nu)\equiv_{\exists}(L,\omega)}_{\mathcal{L}_{ring}}

under a certain form of resolution of singularities (see
[
6
, Theorem A]
).

Now we briefly explain the structure of the paper. In Section
2
, we remind several facts on basic terminologies used throughout the paper. Most of all, we concern several facts on the strict Cohen subring and its representatives developed by Anscombe and Jahnke in
[
2
]
. Also, we consider basic properties on valued hyperfields. And we introduce the one-sorted language
\mathcal{L}_{vhf}
of valued hyperfields and concern the
\mathcal{L}_{vhf}^{+}
-homomorphism of valued hyperfields, which is crucially used for the AKE principles for existentially completeness and existentially closedness relative to higher valued hyperfields (see Remark
2.14
). In Section
3
, we prove two versions of embedding theorems of valued fields relative to higher valued hyperfields for complete
\mathbb{Z}
-valued fields: over structures and not over structures (see Theorem
3.3
and Lemma
3.5
). Finally, in Section
4
, we apply our embedding theorems to prove the AKE principles for finitely ramified valued fields relative to higher valued hyperfieds.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper, we consider valued fields as first-order structures in the three-sorted language
\mathcal{L}_{val}
: A sort
K
for the field, a sort
k
for the residue field, and a sort
\Gamma\cup\{\infty\}
for the value group with infinity. Two field sorts
K
and
k
are interpreted as rings in the language
\mathcal{L}_{ring}:=\{+,\cdot,0,1\}
and the value group sort
\Gamma\cup\{\infty\}
interpreted as an ordered abelian group with infinity in the language
\mathcal{L}_{oag}:=\{+,0,<,\infty\}
. And three sorts are connected by symbols for the residue map
\operatorname{res}:K\rightarrow k
, interpreted as the constant zero map outside the valuation ring, and the valuation map
\nu:K\rightarrow\Gamma\cup\{\infty\}
.

2.1.
The strict Cohen subring and Representatives

In this subsection, we assume that
(K,\nu)
is complete and
\mathbb{Z}
-valued. A
strict Cohen ring
is a complete unramified valuation ring of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
(c.f.
[
2
, Section 2]
). We recall that
\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
has a strict Cohen subring over which
\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
is totally ramified, and we call such a ring a
strict Cohen ring of
\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
. For a field
k
of characteristic
p>0
and a subfield
k_{0}
of
k
, a subset
B
of
k
is called
p-independent over
k_{0}
if

[k^{p}k_{0}(b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}):k^{p}k_{0}]=p^{r}

for all distinct elements
b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}\in B
and for all
r>0
, and
B
is called a
p-basis over
k_{0}
if
k=k^{p}k_{0}(B)
. If
k_{0}
is the prime field, then we omit ‘over
k_{0}
’.

Fact 2.1

(
[
5
, Theorem 11 and Theorem 13]
)
.

(1)

Each tuple
B
of representatives of a
p
-basis of
K\nu
is contained a strict Cohen subring
R
of
\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
.

(2)

Given tuples
B
and
B^{\prime}
of representatives of a same
p
-basis of
K\nu
and strict Cohen subrings
R
and
R^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
containing
B
and
B^{\prime}
respectively,
B=B^{\prime}
(as a set) if and only if
R=R^{\prime}
. Especially, if
K\nu
is perfect or
\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
is unramified, then such a strict Cohen subring is unique. So, given a tuple
B
of representatives of a
p
-basis of
K\nu
, we write
R(B)
for the strict Cohen subring of
\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
containing
B
.

We also recall the following theorem on lifting of homomorphism of residue fields into a homomorphism of valued fields.

Fact 2.2

(
[
3
, Lemma 4.1]
)
.

Let
(K,\nu)
and
(L,\omega)
be
\mathbb{Z}
-valued fields of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
. Suppose
K
is unramified and
L
is complete. Let
\varphi:K\nu\rightarrow L\omega
be a field embedding. Let
\beta
be a tuple of
p
-independent elements in
K\nu
,
B
a lift of
\beta
in
K
, and
B^{\prime}
a lift of
\varphi(\beta)
in
L
.

Then, there is an embedding
\Phi:K\rightarrow L
of valued fields sending
B
to
B^{\prime}
, compatible with
\varphi
, that is, given
x\in\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
,

\operatorname{res}_{\omega}(\Phi(x))=\varphi(\operatorname{res}_{\nu}(x)).

Now, we recall several facts on representatives of the residue field in the valuation ring.

Fact 2.3

(c.f.
[
5
, Lemma 6]
)
.

For
a,b\in\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
,

a\equiv b\pmod{\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}}\Rightarrow a^{p^{i}}\equiv b^{p^{i}}\pmod{\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}^{i}}

for each
i\geq 1
.

Fact 2.4

(c.f.
[
5
, Lemma 7]
and
[
2
, Lemma 3.4]
)
.

There is a unique choice of representatives

\lambda_{i+1}:(K\nu)^{(p^{i})}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\nu,i+1}^{(p^{i})},\alpha^{p^{i}}\mapsto a^{p^{i}}+\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}^{i+1}

where
a\in R
with
\alpha=\operatorname{res}(a)
for each
i\geq 0
.

Remark 2.5
.

For a strict Cohen subring
R
of
\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
, the restriction
\operatorname{res}_{\nu}|_{R}:R\rightarrow K\nu
is surjective and so we have

\operatorname{Im}(\lambda_{l+1})=\{c^{p^{l}}\pmod{\mathfrak{m}^{l+1}}:c\in R\}.

Given
m\geq 1
and
l\geq 0
, let

P_{m,l}:=\{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}):0\leq i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{m}<p^{l}\}

be the set of all multi-indices in
m
-many non-negative integers less than
p^{l}
. Given
m
-tuple
\bar{b}:=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})
in a ring and a multi-index
I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m})\in P_{m,l}
, we write

\bar{b}^{I}:=b_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots b_{m}^{i_{m}}.

Fact 2.6

(c.f.
[
2
, Proposition 3.6]
)
.

Fix
l\geq 0
. Let
\lambda_{l+1}:(K\nu)^{(p^{l})}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\nu,l+1}^{(p^{l})}
be the unique surjective choice of representatives and let
B
be a tuple of representatives of a fixed
p
-basis of
K\nu
. Let
R
be the strict Cohen subring of
\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
containing
B
.

(1)

\mathcal{O}_{\nu,l+1}
is generated by
\operatorname{Im}(\lambda_{l+1})\cup\operatorname{res}_{l+1}[B]\cup\{\operatorname{res}_{l+1}(\pi)\}
.
More precisely, for each

, there are

•

a tuple
\bar{b}:=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})\in B^{m}
for some
m\geq 1
; and

•

tuples
\bar{\alpha}_{i}:=(\alpha_{i,I})_{I\in P_{m,l}}\in(K\nu)^{|P_{m,l}|}

such that

a\equiv\sum_{i\leq l}\left(\sum_{I\in P_{m,l}}\lambda_{l+1}(\alpha_{i,I})^{p^{l}}\bar{b}^{I}\right)\pi^{i}\pmod{\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}^{l+1}}.

Equivalently, there are tuples
\bar{a}_{i}:=(a_{i,I})_{I\in P_{m,l}}\in\mathcal{O}_{\nu}^{|P_{m,l}|}
for
i\leq l
such that

a\equiv\sum_{i\leq l}\left(\sum_{I\in P_{m,l}}a_{i,I}^{p^{l}}\bar{b}^{I}\right)\pi^{i}\pmod{\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}^{l+1}}.

(2)

The subring
R\pmod{\pi^{l+1}}
of
\mathcal{O}_{\nu,l+1}
is generated by

\operatorname{Im}(\lambda_{l+1})\cup\operatorname{res}_{l+1}[B].

More precisely, let
s\geq 0
with
\nu(p^{s})<l+1\leq\nu(p^{s+1})
, and then for each
a\in R
, there are

•

a tuple
\bar{b}:=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})\in B^{m}
for some
m\geq 1
; and

•

\bar{\alpha}_{i}:=(\alpha_{i,I})_{I\in P_{m,l}}\in(K\nu)^{|P_{m,l}|}

a\equiv\sum_{i\leq s}\left(\sum_{I\in P_{m,l}}\lambda_{l+1}(\alpha_{i,I})^{p^{l}}\bar{b}^{I}\right)p^{i}\pmod{\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}^{l+1}}.

Equivalently, there are tuples
\bar{a}_{i}:=(a_{i,I})_{I\in P_{m,l}}\in R^{|P_{m,l}|}
for
i\leq l
such that

a\equiv\sum_{i\leq s}\left(\sum_{I\in P_{m,l}}a_{i,I}^{p^{l}}\bar{b}^{I}\right)p^{i}\pmod{\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}^{l+1}}.

Proof.

We provide a proof of
(2)
. We choose inductively tuples
\bar{a}_{i}:=(a_{i,I})_{I\in P_{m,l}}\in R^{|P_{m,l}|}
such that

a-\sum_{i\leq i_{0}}\left(\sum_{I\in P_{m,l}}a_{i,I}^{p^{l}}\bar{b}^{I}\right)p^{i}\in p^{i_{0}+1}R

for each
i_{0}\leq s
. For
i_{0}=0
, by Remark
2.5
, there is a tuple
\bar{a}_{0}:=(a_{i,I})_{I\in P_{m,l}}\in R^{|P_{m,l}|}
such that

a=\sum_{I\in P_{m,l}}a_{0,I}^{p^{l}}\bar{b}^{I}\pmod{\mathfrak{m}},

and so
a-\sum_{I\in P_{m,l}}a_{0,I}^{p^{l}}\bar{b}^{I}\in\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}\cap R=pR
. Suppose we have chosen tuples
\bar{a}_{0},\ldots,\bar{a}_{i_{0}}
such that

a-\sum_{i\leq i_{0}}\left(\sum_{I\in P_{m,l}}a_{i,I}^{p^{l}}\bar{b}^{I}\right)p^{i}\in p^{i_{0}+1}R

for
i_{0}<s
. Since
\left(a-\sum_{i\leq i_{0}}\left(\sum_{I\in P_{m,l}}a_{i,I}^{p^{l}}\bar{b}^{I}\right)p^{i}\right)/p^{i_{0}+1}\in R
, by Remark
2.5
, there is a tuple
\bar{a}_{i_{0}+1}:=(a_{i_{0}+1,I})_{I\in P_{m,l}}\in R^{|P_{m,l}|}
such that

\displaystyle\left(a-\sum_{i\leq i_{0}}\left(\sum_{I\in P_{m,l}}a_{i,I}^{p^{l}}\bar{b}^{I}\right)p^{i}\right)/p^{i_{0}+1}-\sum_{I\in P_{m,l}}a_{i_{0}+1,I}^{p^{l}}\bar{b}^{I}\in pR

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\ a-\sum_{i\leq i_{0}}\left(\sum_{I\in P_{m,l}}a_{i,I}^{p^{l}}\bar{b}^{I}\right)p^{i}-\left(\sum_{I\in P_{m,l}}a_{i_{0}+1,I}^{p^{l}}\bar{b}^{I}\right)p^{i_{0}+1}\in p^{(i_{0}+1)+1}R

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\ a-\sum_{i\leq i_{0}+1}\left(\sum_{I\in P_{m,l}}a_{i,I}^{p^{l}}\bar{b}^{I}\right)p^{i}\in p^{(i_{0}+1)+1}R

and so the chosen tuples

are desired ones.
∎

2.2.
Valued hyperfields

First, we introduce a notion of valued hyperfields.

Definition 2.7
.

[
7
, Definition 1.2 and 1.4]

(1)

A
hyeprfield
is an algebraic structure
(H,+,\cdot,0,1)
such that for
H^{\times}:=H\setminus\{0\}
,
(H^{\times},\cdot,1)
is an abelian group and there is a multivalued operation
+:\ H\times H\rightarrow 2^{H}
for the power set
2^{H}
of
H
satisfying the following:

(a)

0\cdot\alpha=0
for all
\alpha\in H
.

(b)

(Associative)
(\alpha+\beta)+\gamma=\alpha+(\beta+\gamma)
for all
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in H
.

(c)

(Commutative)
\alpha+\beta=\beta+\alpha
for all
\alpha,\beta\in H
.

(d)

(Distributive)
(\alpha+\beta)\cdot\gamma\subset\alpha\cdot\gamma+\beta\cdot\gamma
for all
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in H
.

(e)

(Identity)
\alpha+0=\{\alpha\}
for all
\alpha\in H
.

(f)

(Inverse) For any
\alpha\in H
, there is a unique
-\alpha\in H
such that
0\in\alpha+(-\alpha)
.

(g)

For all
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in H
,
\alpha\in\beta+(-\gamma)
if and only if
\beta\in\alpha+\gamma
.

(2)

A
valued hyperfield
is a hyperfield
(H,+,\cdot,0,1)
equipped with a map
\nu
from
H
to
\Gamma\cup\{\infty\}
for an ordered abelian group
\Gamma
such that

(a)

For
\alpha\in H
,
\nu(\alpha)=\infty
if and only if
\alpha=0
;

(b)

For all
\alpha,\beta\in H
,
\nu(\alpha\cdot\beta)=\nu(\alpha)+\nu(\beta)
;

(c)

For all
\alpha,\beta\in H
and
\gamma\in\alpha+\beta
,
\nu(\gamma)\geq\min\{\nu(\alpha),\nu(\beta)\}
;

(d)

For all
\alpha,\beta\in H
,
\nu(\alpha+\beta)
consists of a single element unless
0\in\alpha+\beta
; and

(e)

There is
\rho_{H}\in\Gamma
such that either
\alpha+\beta
is a closed ball of radius
\rho_{H}+\min\{\nu(\alpha),\nu(\beta)\}
for all
\alpha,\beta\in H
, or
\alpha+\beta
is a open ball of radius
\rho_{H}+\min\{\nu(\alpha),\nu(\beta)\}
for all
\alpha,\beta\in H^{\times}
.

For
B\subset H
and
\alpha\in H
, define
\alpha+B:=\bigcup_{\beta\in B}\alpha+\beta
(*). The associativity of
+
means that given
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in H
, we have
(\alpha+\beta),(\beta+\gamma)\subset H
and
\alpha+(\beta+\gamma)=(\alpha+\beta)+\gamma
in the sense of
(*)
.

For
\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\in H
, we write
\sum^{\mathcal{H}}\alpha_{i}
for
(\alpha_{0}+\cdots+\alpha_{k})\subset H
. Since the multivalued operation
+
is associative, the notion of
\sum^{\mathcal{H}}
is well-defined.

Definition 2.8
.

[
9
, Definition 2.5]

Let
(H_{i},+_{i},\cdot_{i},0_{i},1_{i},\nu_{i})
be a valued hyperfield for
i=1,2
. A map
f
from
H_{1}
to
H_{2}
is called a
homomorphsim
if the following hold:

(1)

f(0_{1})=0_{2}
and
f(1_{1})=1_{2}
.

(2)

f(\alpha\cdot_{1}\beta)=f(\alpha)\cdot_{2}f(\beta)
for all
\alpha,\beta\in H_{1}
.

(3)

f(\alpha+_{1}\beta)\subset f(\alpha)+_{2}f(\beta)
for all
\alpha,\beta\in H_{1}
.

(4)

For all
\alpha,\beta\in H_{1}
,

\nu_{1}(\alpha)\leq\nu_{1}(\beta)\Leftrightarrow\nu_{2}(f(\alpha_{1}))\leq\nu_{2}(f(\beta)).

Definition 2.9
.

[
9
, Definition 2.7]

Given
n\geq 1
, the
n
th valued hyperfield
of
(K,\nu)
is a hyperfield
(K/(1+\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}^{n}),+_{\mathcal{H}},\cdot_{\mathcal{H}})
, denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{\nu,n}
, such that for each
a(1+\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}^{n}),b(1+\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}^{n})\in K/1+\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}^{n}
,

(1)

a(1+\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}^{n})\cdot_{\mathcal{H}}b(1+\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}^{n}):=ab(1+\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}^{n})
,

(2)

a(1+\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}^{n})+_{\mathcal{H}}b(1+\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}^{n}):=\{(x+y)(1+\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}^{n})|\ x\in a(1+\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}^{n}),y\in b(1+\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}^{n})\}
, and

Conventionally, we write
0
for
0(1+\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}^{n})
and
1
for
1(1+\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}^{n})
. The valuation
\nu
of
K
induces a map
\nu_{\mathcal{H}}
on
K/(1+\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}^{n})
sending
a(1+\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}^{n})
to
\nu(a)
. We call
(K/(1+\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}^{n}),+_{\mathcal{H}},\cdot_{\mathcal{H}},\nu_{\mathcal{H}})
the
n
th valued hyperfield
.

For
A\subset K
, let
\mathcal{H}_{\nu,n}(A):=\{a(1+\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}^{n}):\ a\in A\}
and for
a\in K
, we write
[a]_{n}
for
a(1+\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}^{n})\in\mathcal{H}_{\nu,n}
. Let
S_{\nu}
be the set of units in the valuation ring and the zero, that is,

S_{\nu}:=\mathcal{O}^{\times}\cup\{0\}=\{x\in K:\nu(x)=0\mbox{ or }x=0\}.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
(K,\nu)

is a binary relation. For a valued hyperfield

4.
Ax-Kochen-Eroshov principle via hyperfields

We first recall several facts on the coarsening of valuation. Let
(K,\nu)
be a valued field, let
\nu^{\circ}
be the finest coarsening of
\nu
, and let
\bar{\nu}
be the valuation on the residue field
K\nu^{\circ}
. So, for the nontrivial smallest convex subgroup
(\nu K)^{\circ}
of
\nu K
,

\nu^{\circ}K\cong\nu K/(\nu K)^{\circ},\ \bar{\nu}K\nu^{\circ}\cong(\nu K)^{\circ}.

Fact 4.1
.

[
9
, Remark 5.4 and Lemma 5.5]

Fix a positive integer
n
. Let

\mathcal{H}_{\nu,n}^{\circ}:=\{\alpha\in\mathcal{H}_{\nu,n}:\nu_{\mathcal{H}}(\alpha)\in(\nu K)^{\circ}\}\cup\{0(\in\mathcal{H}_{\nu,n})\}.

Then,
\mathcal{H}_{\nu,n}^{\circ}
forms a valued hyperfield and it is isomorphic to
\mathcal{H}_{\bar{\nu},n}
via an isomorphism
f:\mathcal{H}_{\nu,n}^{\circ}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\bar{\nu},n}
given as follows: For each
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}_{\nu,n}^{\circ}
,

f(\alpha)=\begin{cases}[\operatorname{res}_{\nu^{\circ}}(a)]_{n}&\mbox{if }\alpha=[a]_{n},\\
0&\mbox{if }\alpha=0.\end{cases}

Remark 4.2
.

Let
(K,\nu)
and
(L,\omega)
be finitely ramified valued fields of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
. Given a positive integer
n
, a homomorphism over
p
from
\mathcal{H}_{\nu,n}
to
\mathcal{H}_{\omega,n}
induces a homomorphism over
p
from
\mathcal{H}_{\nu^{\circ},n}
to
\mathcal{H}_{\omega^{\circ},n}
.

Proof.

Let
f:\mathcal{H}_{\nu,n}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\omega,n}
be a homomorphism over
p
. Since
(K,\nu)
and
(L,\omega)
are finitely ramified and
f
is over
p
, given
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}_{\nu,n}
,

\nu_{\mathcal{H}}(\alpha)\in(\nu K)^{\circ}\Leftrightarrow\omega_{\mathcal{H}}(f(\alpha))\in(\omega L)^{\circ},

which implies that

. So, by Fact

,

induces a homomorphism

4.1.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(K,\nu)
and
(L,\omega)
be finitely ramified henselian valued fields of mixed characteristic

having the same initial ramification index

. Let

\begin{cases}n>0&\mbox{if }p\not|e,\\

(K,\nu)\equiv(L,\omega)\Leftrightarrow(\mathcal{H}_{\nu,n},[p]_{n})\equiv(\mathcal{H}_{\omega,n},[p]_{n}).

Proof.

It is enough to show the right-to-left direction. We follow the proof of
(3)\Rightarrow(1)
in
[
9
, Theorem 5.8]
. Suppose
(\mathcal{H}_{\nu,n},[p]_{n})\equiv(\mathcal{H}_{\omega,n},[p]_{n})
. By the Keisler-Shelah theorem, after taking the ultraproduct properly, we may assume that

\aleph_{1}
-saturated and

Since

, we have

(K\nu^{\circ},\bar{\nu})\cong(L\omega^{\circ},\bar{\omega})
and so

T_{1}\models T_{0}
, we have that
\operatorname{Th}_{\mathcal{L}_{val}}^{\exists}(K,\nu)
is decidable if and only if
\{\tilde{\theta}:\theta\in\operatorname{Th}_{\mathcal{L}_{vfh}}^{\exists^{+}}(\mathcal{H}_{\nu,n},[p]_{n})\}
is decidable if and only if
\operatorname{Th}_{\mathcal{L}_{vfh}}^{\exists^{+}}(\mathcal{H}_{\nu,n},[p]_{n})
is decidable.
∎

4.3.
Existential closedness

In this subsection, we aim to prove the AKE principle for existential closedness relative to higher valued hyperfields. We start with rewriting the embedding lemma
[
3
, Lemma 5.12]
using the valued hyperfield.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
(K,\nu)
and
(L,\omega)
be two extensions of a valued field
(K_{0},\nu_{0})
, all of which are henselian and finitely ramified of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
having the same initial ramification index
e
. Suppose that

•

\nu K/\nu_{0}K_{0}
is torsion-free;

•

K\nu/K_{0}\nu_{0}
is separable; and

•

(K_{0},\nu_{0})
and
(K,\nu)
are
\aleph_{1}
-saturated, and
(L,\omega)
is
|K|^{+}
-saturated.

Let
n
be a positive integer satisfying that

\begin{cases}n>0&\mbox{if }p\not|e,\\
n>e^{2}M_{\nu}&\mbox{if }p|e.\end{cases}

Then, if there is an
\mathcal{L}_{vhf}^{+}
-embedding
\mathcal{H}_{\nu,n}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\omega,n}
over
\mathcal{H}_{\nu_{0},n}
, then there is an embedding
K\rightarrow L
over
K_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi_{\mathcal{H}}:\mathcal{H}_{\nu,n}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\omega,n}
be an
\mathcal{L}_{vhf}^{+}
-embedding over
\mathcal{H}_{\nu_{0},n}
, which is an embedding of valued hyperfields by Remark
2.14
, and it induces an embedding
\varphi_{\Gamma}:\nu K\rightarrow\omega L
over
\nu_{0}K_{0}
. Also, by Lemma
3.5
with the inclusion
(K_{0},\nu_{0})\rightarrow(L,\omega)
, there is an embedding
\Phi_{\circ}:(K\nu^{\circ},\bar{\nu})\rightarrow(L\omega^{\circ},\bar{\omega})
over
(K_{0}\nu_{0}^{\circ},\bar{\nu}_{0})
. From the proof of
[
3
, Lemma 5.12]
, we have the desired embedding
K\rightarrow L
over
K_{0}
.

Indeed, let
\varphi_{\Gamma}^{\circ}:\nu^{\circ}K\rightarrow\omega^{\circ}L
be an embedding induced from the embedding
\varphi_{\Gamma}
. Then, the proof of
[
3
, Lemma 5.12]
gives an embedding
\psi:K^{\times}/1+\mathfrak{m}_{\nu^{\circ}}\rightarrow L^{\times}/1+\mathfrak{m}_{\omega^{\circ}}
to make the following diagram commute:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(K\nu^{\circ})^{\times}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K^{\times}/1+\mathfrak{m}_{\nu^{\circ}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nu^{\circ}K}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(L\omega^{\circ})^{\times}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L^{\times}/1+\mathfrak{m}_{\omega^{\circ}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega^{\circ}L}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{\circ}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{\Gamma}^{\circ}}

via the identifications
\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{\nu^{\circ}}/1+\mathfrak{m}_{\nu^{\circ}}\cong(K\nu^{\circ})^{\times}
and
\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{\omega^{\circ}}/1+\mathfrak{m}_{\omega^{\circ}}\cong(L\omega^{\circ})^{\times}
, and this gives an embedding
K\rightarrow L
over
K_{0}
inducing
\psi
, which is the desired one.
∎

Using Lemma
4.8
, we have an AKE principle for relative existential closedness relative to higher valued hyperfields.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
(K,\nu)\subseteq(L,\omega)
be finitely ramified henselian valued fields of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
having the same initial ramification index
e
. Let
n
be a positive integer satisfying that

\begin{cases}n>0&\mbox{if }p\not|e,\\
n>e^{2}M_{\nu}&\mbox{if }p|e.\end{cases}

If
\mathcal{H}_{\nu,n}\preceq_{\exists}\mathcal{H}_{\omega,n}
in
\mathcal{L}_{vhf}
, then
(K,\nu)\preceq_{\exists}(L,\omega)
in
\mathcal{L}_{val}
.

Proof.

We mimic the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 5.13]
. Taking ultrapowers properly, we may assume that
(K,\nu)
and
(L,\omega)
are
\aleph_{1}
-saturated, and consider an
|L|^{+}
-saturated elementary extension
(K^{*},\nu^{*})
of
(K,\nu)
.

Since
\mathcal{H}_{\nu,n}\preceq_{\exists}\mathcal{H}_{\omega,n}
, by Remark
2.17
,
\omega L/\nu K
is torsion-free and
L\omega/K\nu
is separable. Also, since
\mathcal{H}_{\nu,n}\preceq_{\exists}\mathcal{H}_{\omega,n}
and
(K^{*},\nu^{*})
is
|L|^{+}
-saturated, there is an
\mathcal{L}_{vhf}^{+}
-embedding
\mathcal{H}_{\omega,n}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\nu^{*},n}
over
\mathcal{H}_{\nu,n}
, which is an embedding of valued hyperfields. Therefore, by Lemma
4.8
, there is an embedding
(L,\omega)\rightarrow(K^{*},\nu^{*})
over
(K,\nu)
, which implies that
(K,\nu)\preceq_{\exists}(L,\omega)
.
∎

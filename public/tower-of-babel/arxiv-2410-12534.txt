Stability properties for subgroups generated by return words Thanks: The second author was supported by the CTU Global Postdoc Fellowship program.
France Gheeraert, Herman Goulet-Ouellet, Julien Leroy, Pierre Stas

Abstract
. Return words are a classical tool for studying shift spaces with low factor complexity. In recent years, their projection inside groups have attracted some attention, for instance in the context of dendric shift spaces, of generation of pseudorandom numbers (through the welldoc property), and of profinite invariants of shift spaces. Aiming at unifying disparate works, we introduce a notion of stability for subgroups generated by return words. Within this framework, we revisit several existing results and generalize some of them. We also study general aspects of stability, such as decidability or closure under certain operations.



1.
Introduction

Introduced in the 90’s by Durand
[
12
]
, return words find their origin in the induction operation on shift spaces. They are roughly defined as words separating two consecutive occurrences of a given word. This notion is used to construct
S
-adic representations
[
12
]
, to characterize families of shift spaces
[
36
,
3
,
18
]
, to obtain decision procedure for substitutive shift spaces
[
14
,
15
]
, and to study bifix codes
[
6
]
. Return words also have other applications in symbolic dynamics, combinatorics on words, and algebra: they play a key role in the study of Schützenberger groups, which are profinite groups naturally associated with minimal shift spaces (see
[
2
]
), and they appear when studying critical exponents
[
11
,
16
]
, and palindromic richness
[
19
]
.

It is natural to consider words as elements of the free group, and more generally to consider their projection into some group, such as the free Abelian group, or groups of the form
(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{d}
,
m\geq 2
. Several papers studying the behavior of return groups, i.e., subgroups generated by return words in a given group, can be found in the literature; we briefly survey some of them.

Return groups were first described in the study of Sturmian and strict episturmian shift spaces, where return words to any
w
form a basis of the free group over the alphabet (
[
23
,
5
]
). This is partially generalized in
[
8
]
by Berthé et al. to a much wider class called dendric shift spaces, which includes both strict episturmian words and codings of regular interval exchanges. Indeed, in a dendric shift space, return sets form bases of the ambient free group, a result called the
Return Theorem
. This condition is in fact a characterization, as shown in
[
18
]
. Berthé et al. also observed that the return words still form generating sets (but need not form bases) under the weaker assumption of
connectedness
; and in fact the even weaker assumption of suffix-connectedness suffices
[
20
]
.
An analogous property in finite Abelian groups appears in the work of Balková et al. on pseudorandom number generators
[
4
]
, in the form of the
welldoc property
. The general behavior of return groups in finite groups is also linked with ergodic properties of skew products based on minimal shift spaces
[
9
]
. Another work reveals that return groups in the Thue–Morse shift space have a peculiar behavior: they form infinite decreasing chains
[
7
]
.

Our aim is to initiate a systematic study of return groups in order to provide a coherent explanation for these disparate results. Indirectly, all of them study whether or not all return groups are equal, at least for sufficiently large words. This can be formalized using
eventual stability
defined as follows: we ask that the return group to a sufficiently long word
u
is equal to the return group to its prefix of some length bounded independently of
u
. The smallest such bound is called the
stability threshold
, and we also study
stability
defined as eventual stability of threshold 0. To emphasize the fact that (eventual) stability depends on the ambient group, we talk about (eventual)
\varphi
-stability, where
\varphi
is a morphism used to map the return words to the ambient group.

In this paper, we revisit the results mentioned above through the lens of (eventual) stability, we generalize some of them, and we study several new aspects of stability such as decidability or operations on shift spaces. More precisely, the paper is organized as follows.

The main notions from symbolic dynamics are recalled in
Section

2
while eventual stability is introduced in
Section

3
. We then study stability for the above-mentioned families of shift spaces. In particular, using a key theorem by Krawczyk and Müllner
[
25
]
, we show that no aperiodic automatic shift space is eventually stable (
Corollary

15
). The end of
Section

3
provides several equivalent definitions of eventual stability: using Rauzy graphs (
Proposition

7
) or in subgroup separable groups (
Theorem

21
).

In
Section

4
, we establish some decidability results in the context of shift spaces generated by a primitive substitution. First, the
limit
of the return groups read in a finite group can be effectively computed (
Proposition

27
). Moreover, at the cost of imposing additional conditions on the substitution, we obtain analogous results when return groups are read in the free group over the alphabet (
Proposition

31
).

Section

5
is dedicated to the study of the behavior of (eventual) stability under two natural operations on shift spaces: derivation and application of a substitution. For both, (eventual) stability is preserved whenever the group morphisms involved satisfy some mild conditions (
Proposition

33
,
Proposition

39
, and
Proposition

41
). A consequence of these results is that in the free group, eventual stability is preserved by application of substitutions (
Corollary

42
). This cannot be said about derivation however; we provide a counterexample (
Proposition

35
).

Afterwards, we show that stability in subgroup separable groups respects a local-global principle, in the sense that it is sufficient to consider stability in the finite quotients (
Proposition

43
). This is the object of
Section

6
. Using the local-global principle, we study the welldoc property in
Section

7
. We restate it in terms of Abelian stability (
Corollary

53
) and, as a consequence of this work, show that it is satisfied by a large family of shift spaces (
Corollary

55
).

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a finite alphabet. The symbol
\varepsilon
denotes the
empty word
, and
\mathcal{A}^{*}
is the
free monoid
over
\mathcal{A}
. We also let
\mathcal{A}^{+}=\mathcal{A}^{*}\setminus\{\varepsilon\}
be the set of all non-empty words over
\mathcal{A}
. For
u\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
,
|u|
denotes the length of
u
.

Let
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}
be respectively the sets of two-sided and right-sided infinite words over
\mathcal{A}
. Given
x\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}\cup\mathcal{A}^{*}
, we let
\mathcal{L}(x)\subseteq\mathcal{A}^{*}
denote the
language
of
x
, i.e., the set of all finite words appearing in
x
. We extend this notation to subsets
X\subseteq\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}
by
\mathcal{L}(X)=\bigcup_{x\in X}\mathcal{L}(x)
. We further write

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\leq n}(X)=\{u\in\mathcal{L}(X)\mid|u|\leq n\},\quad\mathcal{L}_{\geq n}(X)=\{u\in\mathcal{L}(X)\mid|u|\geq n\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{n}(X)=\{u\in\mathcal{L}(X)\mid|u|=n\}.

2.1.
Shift spaces

Let us equip the sets
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}
with the product topology, where
\mathcal{A}
is equipped with the discrete topology. The
shift
is the map
S\colon\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}
defined by
(x_{n})_{n}\mapsto(x_{n+1})_{n}
.

A non-empty subset
X
of
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}
is a
shift space
if it is closed and invariant under the shift map. The shift space
X
is
minimal
if it does not strictly contain any other shift space. It should be noted that this condition is equivalent to
\mathcal{L}(X)
being
uniformly recurrent
, which is to say that, for any
w\in\mathcal{L}(X)
, there is a
k
such that any
u\in\mathcal{L}_{\geq k}(X)
contains
w
as a factor.

A shift space is called
periodic
if it contains only purely periodic infinite words, and
aperiodic
if it contains no such words. Note that a minimal shift space is either periodic or aperiodic, and that the latter is equivalent to the shift space being infinite.

2.2.
Substitutions

Since we also work with group morphisms, to avoid any confusion, we call
substitution
a morphism between finitely generated free monoids, i.e., a monoid morphism of the form
\sigma\colon\mathcal{A}^{*}\to\mathcal{B}^{*}
. Such a morphism
\sigma
may be extended, whenever convenient, to maps
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{N}}
which are again denoted
\sigma
. We say that
\sigma
is
letter-to-letter
whenever
\sigma(a)
is a letter for all
a\in\mathcal{A}
.

A sequence
x\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}
is said to be
substitutive
if there exist substitutions
\sigma\colon\mathcal{B}^{*}\to\mathcal{B}^{*}
and
\theta\colon\mathcal{B}^{*}\to\mathcal{A}^{*}
and a letter
b\in\mathcal{B}
such that
\sigma^{n}(b)
converges in
\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{N}}
to
y
(which is a fixed point of
\sigma
) and
x=\theta(y)
.
In turn, a shift space
X
over
\mathcal{A}
is called
substitutive
if it is generated by a substitutive sequence
x
, i.e.,

X=\left\{z\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}\mid\mathcal{L}(z)\subseteq\mathcal{L}(x)\right\}.

We also say that
X
is
generated by

(\sigma,\theta)
.
Whenever
x
is substitutive and
\theta
is the identity, we say that
x
(as well as the shift space it generates) is
purely substitutive
.

In what follows, we mostly deal with substitutive shifts spaces where the substitution
\theta
is letter-to-letter and the substitution
\sigma
is
primitive
, that is, there exists a positive integer
k
such that for all
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
, the letter
a
occurs in
\sigma^{k}(b)
.
In that case, the generated shift space
X
is minimal and independent of the choice of the fixed point
y
of
\sigma
. More precisely, we have

X=\left\{z\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}\mid\mathcal{L}(z)\subseteq\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N},b\in\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{L}(\theta\sigma^{n}(b))\right\}.

We say that such shift spaces are
primitive substitutive
, and every minimal substitutive shift space is primitive substitutive
[
12
]
.

2.3.
Return words and return groups

For a shift space
X\subseteq\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}
and a word
u\in\mathcal{L}(X)
, a
return word
to
u
is an element of the set

\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)=\left\{r\in\mathcal{A}^{+}\mid ru\in\mathcal{L}(X)\cap(u\mathcal{A}^{*}\setminus\mathcal{A}^{+}u\mathcal{A}^{+})\right\}.

Note that
\mathcal{R}_{X}(\varepsilon)=\mathcal{A}
and if
u\neq\varepsilon
, the return words to
u
are the words
r
such that
ru\in\mathcal{L}(X)
and
ru
contains exactly two occurrences of
u
, one as a prefix and one as a suffix.

Example 1
.

Take the so-called
Tribonacci substitution

\sigma\colon\{a,b,c\}^{*}\to\{a,b,c\}^{*}
defined by

\sigma\colon a\mapsto ab,b\mapsto ac,c\mapsto a.

Let
X
be the shift space generated by
\sigma
. The set of return words to
aba
in
X
is given by
\mathcal{R}_{X}(aba)=\{ab,aba,abac\}
.
All three of these return words can be seen in the factor

\sigma^{5}(a)=\underline{abac}abaabac\underline{ab}abac\underline{aba}abac.

For an alphabet
\mathcal{A}
, let
F_{\mathcal{A}}
denote the
free group
over
\mathcal{A}
. Recall that it consists of all reduced words over the alphabet
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{A}^{-1}
, where
\mathcal{A}^{-1}=\{a^{-1}\mid a\in\mathcal{A}\}
is a disjoint copy of
\mathcal{A}
, and a word is
reduced
when it contains no factors of the form
aa^{-1}
or
a^{-1}a
with
a\in\mathcal{A}
. The cardinality of
\mathcal{A}
is called the
rank
of
F_{\mathcal{A}}
. For a general reference on free groups and their basic properties, we refer to the monograph
[
27
]
. We also recall, for context, the foundational Nielsen–Schreier theorem, which states that any subgroup of a free group is isomorphic to a free group (of a possibly different rank).

Given a group
G
and a subset
T\subseteq G
, we let
\left\langle T\right\rangle
denote the subgroup of
G
generated by
T
, i.e., the smallest subgroup of
G
containing
T
(where the dependency on
G
is implicit). We view
\mathcal{A}^{*}
as a subset of
F_{\mathcal{A}}
in the usual way, so we naturally extend any substitution
\sigma\colon\mathcal{A}^{*}\to\mathcal{B}^{*}
into a group morphism from
F_{\mathcal{A}}
to
F_{\mathcal{B}}
. We also consider subgroups of
F_{\mathcal{A}}
generated by words of
\mathcal{A}^{*}
, as in the following central notion.

Definition 2
.

Let
X
be a shift space over
\mathcal{A}
and let
u\in\mathcal{L}(X)
. The
return group to
u
in
X
is the subgroup
\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle
of
F_{\mathcal{A}}
.

More generally, we are interested in the study of images of return groups under group morphisms
\varphi\colon F_{\mathcal{A}}\to G
where the image
\varphi(F_{\mathcal{A}})
often has additional properties (such as being finite). In this case, it will be more convenient to assume that
\varphi
is onto and that
G
itself satisfies these properties. This is not a restriction since one can simply replace
G
by
\varphi(F_{\mathcal{A}})
if needed. The following simple lemma is used repeatedly.

Lemma 3
.

Let
X
be a minimal shift space over
\mathcal{A}
and let
\varphi\colon F_{\mathcal{A}}\to G
be any group morphism. If
w
is a factor of
u\in\mathcal{L}(X)
, then up to conjugacy,
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle
is a subgroup of
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(w)\right\rangle
. More precisely, if
pw
is a prefix of
u
, then

\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(w)\right\rangle\geq\varphi(p)^{-1}\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle\varphi(p).

In particular, if
w
is a prefix of
u
, then
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(w)\right\rangle\geq\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle
.

Proof.

Assume that
u=pws
. First observe that any return word to
u=pws
is a concatenation of return words to
pw
(
[
12
, Proposition 2.6, part 4]
). Likewise, any return word to
pw
is, up to conjugacy by
p
, a concatenation of return words to
w
. Therefore

\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(w)\right\rangle\geq p^{-1}\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(pw)\right\rangle p\geq p^{-1}\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle p.

As
\varphi
is a morphism, we further deduce that
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(w)\right\rangle\geq\varphi(p)^{-1}\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle\varphi(p)
.
∎

3.
Eventual stability

Lemma

3
shows that return groups are subgroups of one another when extending words on the right. We therefore consider the following version of stability.

Definition 4
.

Let
\varphi\colon X\to G
be any group morphism. A shift space
X
over
\mathcal{A}
has
eventually
\varphi
-stable return groups
if there exists
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for all
w\in\mathcal{L}_{M}(X)
and all
u\in w\mathcal{A}^{*}\cap\mathcal{L}(X)
, we have
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle=\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(w)\right\rangle
. If
M=0
, we moreover say that
X
has
\varphi
-stable return groups
.

We make two comments on this definition. First, the set of integers
M
satisfying the above property is either empty or an interval of the form
[K,\infty)
. In the latter case, the integer
K
is called the
threshold
. Second, since
\mathcal{R}_{X}(\varepsilon)=\mathcal{A}
, one could equivalently say that
X
has
\varphi
-stable return groups whenever
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle=\varphi(F_{\mathcal{A}})
for all
u\in\mathcal{L}(X)
.

Eventual stability is of particular interest when
\varphi
is the identity on
F_{\mathcal{A}}
, in which case we omit the mention of
\varphi
and say that
X
has
(eventually) stable return groups
. This omission is justified by the following simple result.

Proposition 5
.

Let
X
be a minimal shift space over
\mathcal{A}
with eventually stable return groups of threshold
M
. Then
X
has eventually
\varphi
-stable return groups of threshold at most
M
for every group morphism
\varphi
defined on
F_{\mathcal{A}}
.

Eventual stability can also be stated in terms of groups generated by Rauzy graphs. Let
X
be a shift space.
The
order-
n
Rauzy graph
of
X
, denoted
\Gamma_{n}(X)
, is the directed graph whose vertices are the elements of
\mathcal{L}_{n}(X)
, and there is an edge from
u
to
v
if there are letters
a
and
b
such that
ub=av\in\mathcal{L}(X)
; this edge is labeled by
a
. The label of a path is the concatenation of the labels of the edges.

The
Rauzy group
of
u\in\mathcal{L}(X)
, denoted
\operatorname{Gr}(u)
, is the subgroup of
F_{\mathcal{A}}
generated by the labels of paths based on
u
in
\Gamma_{|u|}(X)
, where by
path based on
u
we mean a path from
u
to
u
.

Proposition 7
.

Let
X
be a minimal shift space over
\mathcal{A}
and let
\varphi\colon F_{\mathcal{A}}\to G
be any group morphism. Then,
X
has eventually
\varphi
-stable return groups if and only if there exists
K\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for all
w\in\mathcal{L}_{K}(X)
and all
u\in w\mathcal{A}^{*}\cap\mathcal{L}(X)
, we have
\varphi(\operatorname{Gr}(u))=\varphi(\operatorname{Gr}(w))
. Moreover, we then have
\varphi(\operatorname{Gr}(u))=\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle
for all
u\in\mathcal{L}_{\geq K}(X)
.

In particular,
X
has
\varphi
-stable return groups if and only if
\varphi(\operatorname{Gr}(u))=\varphi(F_{\mathcal{A}})
for all
u\in\mathcal{L}(X)
.

Proof.

The proof relies on the following observations:
on the one hand, for any
u\in\mathcal{L}(X)
, every return word to
u
is the label of a path based on
u
, so
\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle\leq\operatorname{Gr}(u)
. On the other hand, if
u\in w\mathcal{A}^{*}\cap\mathcal{L}(X)
is of length at least
K_{w}=\max\{|vw|\mid v\in\mathcal{R}_{X}(w)\}
, then
\operatorname{Gr}(u)\leq\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(w)\right\rangle
by the proof of
[
8
, Theorem 4.7]
.
Consequently, for any
w\in\mathcal{L}(X)
and
u\in w\mathcal{A}^{*}\cap\mathcal{L}_{\geq K_{w}}(X)
,

(i)

if
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle=\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(w)\right\rangle
, then
\varphi(\operatorname{Gr}(u))\leq\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(w)\right\rangle=\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle\leq\varphi(\operatorname{Gr}(u))
so
\varphi(\operatorname{Gr}(u))=\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle
;

(ii)

if
\varphi(\operatorname{Gr}(u))=\varphi(\operatorname{Gr}(w))
, then
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(w)\right\rangle\leq\varphi(\operatorname{Gr}(w))=\varphi(\operatorname{Gr}(u))\leq\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(w)\right\rangle
so
\varphi(\operatorname{Gr}(w))=\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(w)\right\rangle
.

Assume that
X
has eventually
\varphi
-stable return groups of threshold
M
, and set
K=\max\{K_{v}\mid v\in\mathcal{L}_{M}(X)\}
. Since
K\geq M
, to conclude that
\varphi(\operatorname{Gr}(u))=\varphi(\operatorname{Gr}(w))
whenever
w
is the length-
K
prefix of
u
, it is sufficient to show that
\varphi(\operatorname{Gr}(u))=\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle
for all
u\in\mathcal{L}_{\geq K}(X)
.
Applying
Item

(i)
with
w
the length-
M
prefix of
u\in\mathcal{L}_{\geq K}(X)
, we have
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle=\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(w)\right\rangle
so
\varphi(\operatorname{Gr}(u))=\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle
. This shows one implication.

For the converse, assume that
\varphi(\operatorname{Gr}(u))=\varphi(\operatorname{Gr}(w))
whenever
w
is the length-
K
prefix of
u
. To conclude that
X
has eventually
\varphi
-stable return groups (of threshold at most
K
), it suffices once again to prove that
\varphi(\operatorname{Gr}(w))=\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(w)\right\rangle
for all
w\in\mathcal{L}_{\geq K}(X)
. This is a consequence of
Item

(ii)
since, taking any
u\in w\mathcal{A}^{*}\cap\mathcal{L}_{\geq K_{w}}(X)
, we have
\varphi(\operatorname{Gr}(u))=\varphi(\operatorname{Gr}(w))
so
\varphi(\operatorname{Gr}(w))=\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(w)\right\rangle
.

The characterization of
\varphi
-stability then directly follows from the fact that
\varphi(\operatorname{Gr}(u))=\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle
for all long enough
u
and that
\operatorname{Gr}(u)\leq\operatorname{Gr}(w)
and
\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle\leq\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(w)\right\rangle
whenever
w
is a prefix of
u
.
∎

3.1.
Behavior of known families

Return groups and their stabilization have already been studied, sometimes implicitly, for some well-known classes of shift spaces. As a consequence, we obtain some simple examples of shift spaces with (eventually) stable return groups.

3.1.1.
Eventually stable return groups

It is proved in

]
that minimal dendric shift spaces — or more generally, connected shift spaces — have stable return groups. The second author extended this result by considering a weaker version of connectedness called “suffix-connectedness” in

if there exists
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that all words
u\in\mathcal{L}_{\geq M}(X)
are dendric. The smallest
M
satisfying this property is called the
threshold for dendricity
. If
M=0
, we say that
X
is
dendric
.

Definition 10
.

A word
u\in\mathcal{L}(X)
is
suffix-connected
if there exists
1\leq d\leq|u|+1
such that the elements of
\{au^{\prime}\mid a\in\operatorname{E}^{-}_{1}(u)\}
lie in a single connected component of
\mathcal{E}_{d}(u^{\prime\prime})
, where
u=u^{\prime}u^{\prime\prime}
and
|u^{\prime}|=d-1
.
We then say that a shift space
X
is
eventually suffix-connected
if there exists
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that all words
u\in\mathcal{L}_{\geq M}(X)
are suffix-connected.
The smallest
M
satisfying this property is called the
threshold for suffix-connectedness
. If
M=0
, we say that
X
is
suffix-connected
.

Obviously, every eventually dendric shift space is also eventually suffix-connected and the threshold for suffix-connectedness cannot exceed the one for dendricity. Observe that, unlike the original definition in
[
20
]
, for a shift space to be suffix-connected, we also ask that the empty word is suffix-connected, leading to the following result.

Proposition 11

(
[
20
, Corollary 1.2]
)
.

Every minimal suffix-connected shift space has stable return groups.

The above proposition can be extended to the case where the empty word is not suffix-connected, as shown in
[
20
]
. More generally, the techniques used in
[
8
]
and
[
20
]
can be applied to the class of minimal eventually suffix-connected shift spaces, leading to the following natural extension of
Proposition

11
.

Proposition 12
.

Every minimal eventually suffix-connected shift space
X
has eventually stable return groups.
Furthermore, the threshold for the stability of return groups does not exceed the threshold for suffix-connectedness.

Proof.

Let
X
be a minimal eventually suffix-connected shift space of threshold
M
. By the proof of
[
20
, Proposition 5.3]
, for every
w\in\mathcal{L}_{M}(X)
and
u\in w\mathcal{A}^{*}\cap\mathcal{L}(X)
, we have
\operatorname{Gr}(u)=\operatorname{Gr}(w)
. By
Proposition

7
,
X
then has eventually stable return groups of threshold at most
M
.
∎

This proposition sheds some light on the behavior of return groups in eventually dendric shift spaces. It also gives a partial answer to
[
20
, Question 10.2]
. Indeed, as the Rauzy groups
\operatorname{Gr}(u)
and
\operatorname{Gr}(v)
are conjugate when
|u|=|v|
, we established that, whenever
X
is eventually suffix-connected, all but finitely many return groups of
X
lie in the same conjugacy class. We cannot precisely compute their rank however.

3.1.2.
Unstable return groups

On the other hand, Berthé and the second author recently showed that the Thue–Morse shift space does not have eventually stable return groups
[
7
]
. This result can be extended to any minimal automatic shift space using a result by Krawczyk and Müllner. Let us first recall the definition of automatic shift spaces.

Definition 13
.

A shift space
X
is
k
-automatic
for
k\geq 2
if it is generated by
(\sigma,\tau)
where
\sigma\colon\mathcal{B}^{*}\to\mathcal{B}^{*}
satisfies
|\sigma(b)|=k
for all
b\in\mathcal{B}
, and
\tau\colon\mathcal{B}^{*}\to\mathcal{A}^{*}
is letter-to-letter.

Theorem 14

(
[
25
, Theorem 3.2.3]
)
.

If
X
is an aperiodic minimal
k
-automatic shift space then, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a constant
K_{n}
such that, for every
u\in\mathcal{L}_{\geq K_{n}}(X)
, any return word to
u
has length multiple of
k^{n}
.

Corollary 15
.

If
X
is an aperiodic minimal
k
-automatic shift space over
\mathcal{A}
, then
X
does not have eventually
\varphi
-stable return groups, where
\varphi\colon F_{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathbb{Z}
is such that
\varphi(u)=|u|
for all
u\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
. In particular,
X
does not have eventually stable return groups.

Proof.

It is enough to prove that for every
w\in\mathcal{L}(X)
, there exists
u\in w\mathcal{A}^{*}\cap\mathcal{L}(X)
such that
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle\neq\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(w)\right\rangle
.
Let us fix such a word
w
.
There is a positive integer
n
such that
w
has a return word of length not multiple of
k^{n}
.
By
Theorem

14
, there exists
u
having
w
as a prefix and such that all return words to
u
have length multiple of
k^{n}
.
In other words,
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle\subseteq k^{n}\mathbb{Z}
while
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(w)\right\rangle\not\subseteq k^{n}\mathbb{Z}
, which implies that
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle\neq\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(w)\right\rangle
.
This shows that the return groups are not eventually
\varphi
-stable. In particular,
X
does not have eventually stable return groups by
Proposition

5
.
∎

Combined with
Proposition

12
, this implies the following result.

Corollary 16
.

A minimal eventually suffix-connected shift space that is
k
-automatic is periodic.

3.2.
Some equivalent conditions

When considering stability conditions for a minimal shift space, it is easy to see that it suffices to check that infinitely many words have “full” return groups, as detailed below.

Proposition 17
.

Let
X
be a minimal shift space over
\mathcal{A}
and let
\varphi
be a group morphism defined on
F_{\mathcal{A}}
. The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

the shift space
X
has
\varphi
-stable return groups;

(ii)

\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle=\varphi(F_{\mathcal{A}})
for infinitely many
u\in\mathcal{L}(X)
.

Proof.

Clearly, if
X
has
\varphi
-stable return groups, then
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle=\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(\varepsilon)\right\rangle=\varphi(F_{\mathcal{A}})
holds for infinitely many
u\in\mathcal{L}(X)
. Conversely, let
w\in\mathcal{L}(X)
. By minimality and by assumption, there exists
u\in\mathcal{L}(X)
having
w
as a factor and such that
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle=\varphi(F_{\mathcal{A}})
.
Lemma

3
then implies that
\varphi(F_{\mathcal{A}})
is, up to conjugacy by an element of
\varphi(F_{\mathcal{A}})
, a subgroup of
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(w)\right\rangle
, which in turns implies that
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(w)\right\rangle=\varphi(F_{\mathcal{A}})
. Therefore,
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(w)\right\rangle=\varphi(F_{\mathcal{A}})=\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(\varepsilon)\right\rangle
for all
w\in\mathcal{L}(X)
, proving
\varphi
-stability.
∎

Building on similar ideas, we provide in
Theorem

21
different equivalent definitions for eventual
\varphi
-stability. In order to do so, we ask for an additional algebraic condition known as
subgroup separability
. This condition has a rich history in group theory and, as we shall see, is satisfied by the morphisms classically considered in the context of return words. The definition of subgroup separability will be stated in terms of the following notion.

Definition 18
.

Let
G
be a group and let
H\leq G
. We say that
G
is
H
-separable
if, for every
g\notin H
, there exists a normal subgroup
N\trianglelefteq G
of finite index such that
gN
is not in the subgroup
H/N
of
G/N
.

In the terminology of Malcev
[
28
]
, who was among the firsts to study such properties, we would alternatively say that the subgroup
H
is a
finitely separable subgroup
of
G
.
The next lemma is a property which appeared in a paper of Raptis and Varsos (
[
31
, Proposition 1]
). We include a proof for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 19
.

Let
G
be a group and let
H\leq G
. If
G
is
H
-separable, then
H
does not strictly contain any of its conjugates.

Proof.

Suppose by contradiction that the subgroup
g^{-1}Hg
is strictly contained in
H
for some
g\in G
. Let
h\in H\setminus g^{-1}Hg
, we have
ghg^{-1}\notin H
. Therefore, there exists a normal subgroup
N\trianglelefteq G
of finite index such that
ghg^{-1}N\notin H/N
. In particular,
hN\notin g^{-1}Hg/N
.
Next, consider the two subgroups
H/N
and
g^{-1}Hg/N
of
G/N
. Those are conjugate subgroups in a finite group, thus they have the same cardinality. Moreover
g^{-1}Hg\leq H
, thus
g^{-1}Hg/N=H/N
. We deduce that

hN\in H/N=g^{-1}Hg/N,

contradicting the choice of
N
.
∎

Definition 20
.

A group
G
is
subgroup separable
if it is
H
-separable for every finitely generated subgroup
H\leq G
.

Subgroup separable groups are sometimes called
locally extended residually finite
(LERF), a terminology which can be traced back to a paper of Burns
[
10
]
who attributed it to Meskin. This alternative terminology is meant to highlight the fact that the property is a generalization of
residual finiteness
(RF), which may be defined as
\{1\}
-separability.

Many important families of groups are known to be subgroup separable. A first example is the family of finite groups. Indeed, in a finite group
G
, the trivial subgroup
\{1\}
has finite index, and if
H\leq G
is a proper subgroup with
g\notin H
, then
g\notin H=H/\{1\}
. A classical result of M. Hall, which is particularly important in the context of this paper, states that all free groups are subgroup separable (
[
21
, Theorem 5.1]
). Another result, due to Malcev, states that
virtually polycyclic groups
(i.e., finite extensions of groups with cyclic subnormal series) have finitely separable subgroups, and thus they are subgroup separable
[
28
]
. This includes all finitely generated Abelian groups; see
[
35
, Lemma 4.2]
for a direct proof of this. On the other hand, a simple example of a finitely generated group which is not subgroup separable is the direct product of two free groups of rank 2
[
1
]
.

We now provide equivalent points of view for eventual stability.

Theorem 21
.

Let
X
be a minimal shift space over
\mathcal{A}
and let
\varphi\colon F_{\mathcal{A}}\to G
be a group morphism onto a group
G
. Consider the following assertions:

(i)

X
has eventually
\varphi
-stable return groups;

(ii)

\{\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle\mid u\in\mathcal{L}(X)\}
is finite;

(iii)

the subgroups
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle
are conjugate for all but finitely many
u\in\mathcal{L}(X)
;

(iv)

the subgroups
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle
are conjugate for infinitely many
u\in\mathcal{L}(X)
.

Then:
(i)
implies
(ii)
and
(iii)
;
(ii)
and
(iii)
each imply
(iv)
. Moreover, all the assertions are equivalent when
G
is subgroup separable.

Proof.

Item

(i)
implies
Item

(ii)
since it suffices to consider return groups associated with the elements of
\mathcal{L}_{\leq M}(X)
for some
M
, which is finite. It also implies
Item

(iii)
by
Proposition

7
. Indeed, there exists
M
such that, for all
u\in\mathcal{L}_{\geq M}(X)
, the subgroup
\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle
is a Rauzy group for a length-
M
word. The conclusion then follows since, when
X
is uniformly recurrent, the Rauzy graphs are strongly connected so Rauzy groups associated with words of the same length are conjugate.
The fact that
Item

(ii)
implies
Item

(iv)
is a consequence of the pigeonhole principle, and the implication from
Item

(iii)
to
Item

(iv)
is direct.

Let us now prove that, when
G
is subgroup separable,
Item

(iv)
implies
Item

(i)
. Let
W
be an infinite subset of
\mathcal{L}(X)
such that, for all
v\in W
, the subgroup
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(v)\right\rangle
is conjugate to some given
H\leq G
. Fix
v\in W
. As
X
is minimal, there exists
M
such that all words
u\in\mathcal{L}_{\geq M}(X)
have
v
as a factor. Let
u\in\mathcal{L}_{\geq M}(X)
and let
w
be its length-
M
prefix. By Lemma
3
and by definition of
v
, there exists a conjugate
H_{1}
of
H
such that

\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle\leq\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(w)\right\rangle\leq H_{1}.

Similarly, as
X
is minimal and
W
infinite, there exists
t\in W
such that
u
is a factor of
t
. Therefore, there exists a conjugate
H_{2}
of
H
such that
H_{2}
is a subgroup of
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle
. Since
G
is subgroup separable and return groups are finitely generated, this implies that
H_{1}=H_{2}
by
Lemma

19
, so
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle=\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(w)\right\rangle
. As this is true for any
w\in\mathcal{L}_{M}(X)
and
u\in w\mathcal{A}^{*}\cap\mathcal{L}(X)
, this ends the proof.
∎

We do not know however if some or all of these assertions are still equivalent when
G
is not subgroup separable.

We deduce the following dichotomy as a direct consequence of
Lemma

3
and of
Theorem

21
.

Corollary 22
.

Let
X
be a minimal shift space over
\mathcal{A}
and let
\varphi\colon F_{\mathcal{A}}\to G
be a morphism onto a subgroup separable group
G
. Either
X
has eventually
\varphi
-stable return groups, or there exists a sequence
\left(u^{(n)}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{L}(X)^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}\left(u^{(n+1)}\right)\right\rangle<\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}\left(u^{(n)}\right)\right\rangle
for all
n
.

4.
Decidability in the case of substitutive shift spaces

Based on
Proposition

17
and
Theorem

21
, (eventual) stability of return groups reduces to the study of return groups for well-chosen infinite families of words. This is especially useful in the case of a minimal substitutive shift space
X
where, following the work of Durand on derivation, one can find a sequence of words
(u^{(n)})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, a finite alphabet
\mathcal{C}
, an endomorphism
\alpha
of
F_{\mathcal{C}}
, and a free group morphism
\psi
such that, for each
n
, we have
\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u^{(n)})\right\rangle=\psi(\alpha^{n}(F_{\mathcal{C}}))
(see
Proposition

25
). Moreover, the above morphisms
\alpha
and
\psi
are computable, leading to decidability results on eventual stability, both in finite groups and in the free group over the alphabet of
X
.

4.1.
Derived sequences

Assume that
X
is a minimal shift space over
\mathcal{A}
, and consider a word
u\in\mathcal{L}(X)
.
Let
\theta_{u}\colon\mathcal{B}^{*}\to\mathcal{A}^{*}
define a bijection between
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)
. There is a shift space
Y
over
\mathcal{B}
such that
\mathcal{L}(Y)=\theta_{u}^{-1}(\mathcal{L}(X))
. This shift space is minimal and is unique up to a renaming of the letters (because
\theta_{u}
and
\mathcal{B}
are not uniquely defined). It is called the
derived shift space of
X
with respect to
u
, and is denoted
D_{u}(X)
. The substitution
\theta_{u}
is called a
derivating substitution
(for
u
).

One can also define derivation of a right-infinite word
x\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}
with respect to one of its prefixes
u
. In this case, we have a canonical choice of
\theta_{u}
. More precisely, we naturally set

\mathcal{R}_{x}(u)=\left\{r\in\mathcal{A}^{+}\mid ru\in\mathcal{L}(x)\cap(u\mathcal{A}^{*}\setminus\mathcal{A}^{+}u\mathcal{A}^{+})\right\}

and
\mathcal{A}_{u}=\{1,\dots,\#\mathcal{R}_{x}(u)\}
. Then we define
\theta_{x,u}\colon\mathcal{A}_{u}^{*}\to\mathcal{A}^{*}
such that
\theta_{x,u}(\mathcal{A}_{u})=\mathcal{R}_{x}(u)
and for every
i\in\mathcal{A}_{u}
, if
k
is the smallest occurrence of
\theta_{x,u}(i)u
in
x
, then
x_{[0,k)}
belongs to
\{\theta_{x,u}(j)\mid 1\leq j<i\}^{*}
. In particular, this implies that
\theta_{x,u}(1)u
is a prefix of
x
.
The
derived sequence of
x
with respect to
u
, denoted
D_{u}(x)
, is then the unique
z\in\mathcal{A}_{u}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\theta_{x,u}(z)=x
. It satisfies (for the choice
\theta_{u}=\theta_{x,u}
)

D_{u}(X)=\left\{z\in\mathcal{A}_{u}^{\mathbb{Z}}\mid\mathcal{L}(z)\subseteq\mathcal{L}(D_{u}(x))\right\}.

Lemma 23

(
[
12
, Proposition 2.6]
)
.

Let
X
be a minimal shift space. If
\theta_{u}
is a derivating substitution for
u\in\mathcal{L}(X)
, then for every
w\in\mathcal{L}(D_{u}(X))
, we have

\theta_{u}(\mathcal{R}_{D_{u}(X)}(w))=\mathcal{R}_{X}(\theta_{u}(w)u).

Furthermore, if
x=y_{[0,\infty)}
for some
y\in X
,
u
is a prefix of
x
, and
w
is a prefix of
D_{u}(x)
, then
\theta_{u}(w)u
is a prefix of
x
,
D_{\theta_{u}(w)u}(x)=D_{w}(D_{u}(x))
, and
\theta_{x,\theta_{u}(w)u}=\theta_{x,u}\theta_{D_{u}(x),w}
.

The two following results are reformulations of Durand’s work in
[
12
,
13
]
. As
Proposition

25
was not explicitly stated, we give a short proof.

Theorem 24
.

Let
\sigma\colon\mathcal{B}^{*}\to\mathcal{B}^{*}
be a primitive substitution with a fixed point
y\in\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{N}}
, let
\tau\colon\mathcal{B}^{*}\to\mathcal{A}^{*}
be a letter-to-letter substitution, and let
x=\tau(y)
.

(i)

For every prefix
u
of
x
, the substitution
\theta_{x,u}
is computable and there exist some computable substitutions
\sigma_{u}\colon\mathcal{C}^{*}\to\mathcal{C}^{*}
and
\tau_{u}\colon\mathcal{C}^{*}\to\mathcal{A}_{u}^{*}
such that
\sigma_{u}
is primitive,
\tau_{u}
is letter-to-letter, and
\mathcal{D}_{u}(x)=\tau_{u}(z)
where
z\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}
is the fixed point of
\sigma_{u}
starting with 1.

(ii)

There is a computable constant
K
such that the set

\#\{(\sigma_{u},\tau_{u})\mid u\text{ prefix of }x\}\leq K.

In particular, the number of derived sequences of
x
(with respect to its prefixes) is at most
K
.

Proposition 25
.

Let
X
be a minimal substitutive shift space generated by
(\sigma,\tau)
, where
\sigma\colon\mathcal{B}^{*}\to\mathcal{B}^{*}
is primitive and
\tau\colon\mathcal{B}^{*}\to\mathcal{A}^{*}
is letter-to-letter. There exist two computable substitutions
\alpha\colon\mathcal{C}^{*}\to\mathcal{C}^{*}
and
\psi\colon\mathcal{C}^{*}\to\mathcal{A}^{*}
, and a sequence
(u^{(n)})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{L}(X)^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
u^{(n)}
is a proper prefix of
u^{(n+1)}
and
\mathcal{R}_{X}(u^{(n)})=\psi\alpha^{n}(\mathcal{C})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

We set
x=\tau(y)
, where
y
is a fixed point of
\sigma
.
Using
Lemma

23
, we inductively define a sequence
(v^{(n)})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of prefixes of
x
by

v^{(0)}=\varepsilon\quad\text{and}\quad v^{(n+1)}=\theta_{x,v^{(n)}}(1)v^{(n)}\text{ for }n\in\mathbb{N}.

By
Lemma

23
once again, the sequence of infinite words defined by
x^{(n)}=D_{v^{(n)}}(x)
satisfies
x^{(n+1)}=D_{1}(x^{(n)})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, if we set
\theta_{0}=\theta_{x,\varepsilon}
and
\theta_{n+1}=\theta_{x^{(n)},1}
for all
n
, then
\theta_{x,v^{(n)}}=\theta_{0}\dots\theta_{n}
.

Using
Theorem

24
and its notations, there exist computable
i<j
such that
(\sigma_{v^{(i)}},\tau_{v^{(i)}})=(\sigma_{v^{(j)}},\tau_{v^{(j)}})
, hence
x^{(i)}=x^{(j)}
.
This inductively implies that, for all
m\geq i+1
,
x^{(m+j-i)}=x^{(m)}
and
\theta_{m+j-i}=\theta_{m}
.
It then suffices to define the sequence
(u^{(n)})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
by
u^{(n)}=v^{(i+n(j-i))}
for every
n
and to consider
\psi=\theta_{0}\cdots\theta_{i}
,
\alpha=\theta_{i+1}\cdots\theta_{j}
, and
\mathcal{C}=\left\{1,\dots,\#\mathcal{R}_{x}(v^{(j)})\right\}
.
∎

Observe that in the case of a purely substitutive shift space, i.e.,
\tau=\mathrm{id}
, the morphisms
\tau_{v^{(i)}}
in the above proof are also identity morphisms. This is the case for the next example.

Example 26
.

Let us illustrate the above construction with the primitive substitution
\sigma\colon a\mapsto aab,b\mapsto acb,c\mapsto ba
and its fixed point

x=aabaabacbaabaabacbaabbaacbaaba\cdots

We start with
v^{(0)}=\varepsilon
. The values of
\theta_{0}
and
\sigma_{v^{(0)}}
are simply

\displaystyle\theta_{0}\colon\begin{cases}1\mapsto a\\
2\mapsto b\\
3\mapsto c\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{v^{(0)}}\colon\begin{cases}1\mapsto 112\\
2\mapsto 132\\
3\mapsto 21\end{cases}.

In particular
x^{(0)}
is the image of
x
under
a\mapsto 1,b\mapsto 2,c\mapsto 3
.

Next we find
v^{(1)}=\theta_{0}(1)v^{(0)}=a
. The values of
\sigma_{v^{(1)}}
and
\theta_{1}
may be computed using the procedure described by Durand
[
13
]
:

\displaystyle\theta_{1}\colon\begin{cases}1\mapsto 1\\
2\mapsto 12\\
3\mapsto 132\\
4\mapsto 122\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{v^{(1)}}\colon\begin{cases}1\mapsto 12\\
2\mapsto 123\\
3\mapsto 1413\\
4\mapsto 1233\end{cases}.

The next value is
v^{(2)}=\theta_{0}\theta_{1}(1)v^{(1)}=aa
. Using the same procedure as before, we find

\theta_{2}\colon\begin{cases}1\mapsto 12\\
2\mapsto 123\\
3\mapsto 14\\
4\mapsto 13\\
5\mapsto 1233\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{v^{(2)}}\colon\begin{cases}1\mapsto 12\\
2\mapsto 1234\\
3\mapsto 15\\
4\mapsto 134\\
5\mapsto 123434\end{cases}.

Finally,
v^{(2)}=\theta_{0}\theta_{1}\theta_{2}(1)v^{(2)}=aabaa
and

\displaystyle\theta_{3}\colon\begin{cases}1\mapsto 12\\
2\mapsto 1234\\
3\mapsto 15\\
4\mapsto 134\\
5\mapsto 123434\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{v^{(3)}}\colon\begin{cases}1\mapsto 12\\
2\mapsto 1234\\
3\mapsto 15\\
4\mapsto 134\\
5\mapsto 123434\end{cases}.

In particular we see that
\sigma_{v^{(3)}}=\sigma_{v^{(2)}}
. Therefore
x^{(k)}=x^{(k+1)}
for all
k\geq 2
, and thus
\theta_{k}=\theta_{k+1}
for all
k\geq 3
. Consequently, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the set of return words to the prefix
v^{(2+n)}
of
x
is
\psi\alpha^{n}(\{1,\dots,5\})
, where

4.2.
Eventual stability in finite groups

Observe that all shift spaces have eventually
\varphi
-stable return groups whenever the image of
\varphi
is a finite group by

. We now show that, up to conjugacy, the subgroup over which the images of the return groups stabilize is computable. We deduce in particular that the question of
\varphi
-stability

Proposition 27
.

Let

be a minimal substitutive shift space generated by
(\sigma,\tau)
, where

is primitive and

Let

be a morphism onto a finite group

. One can:

(i)

compute a subgroup

of

(ii)

decide whether

has
\varphi
-stable return groups.

Proof.

We prove the first claim.
By

, there exist computable substitutions

, and a sequence

of

∎

4.3.
Eventual stability in free groups

In some particular cases, the substitution
\psi
of
Proposition

25
is the identity, i.e., we obtain an infinite sequence of words whose return groups are given by
\alpha^{n}(F_{\mathcal{C}})
.
More generally, we sometimes have a group morphism
\varphi\colon F_{\mathcal{A}}\to F_{\mathcal{A}}
and a finitely generated subgroup
H\leq F_{\mathcal{A}}
such that, for all
n\geq 0
,
\varphi^{n}(H)
is the return group for some well-chosen
u^{(n)}\in\mathcal{L}(X)
(the
u^{(n)}
’s being distinct).
In these cases, we also obtain the decidability of (eventual) stability of return groups.

Lemma 29
.

Let
\varphi\colon G\to G
be a group endomorphism and let
H\leq G
. If
\varphi
is injective over
H
and
\varphi(H)<H
, then the subgroups
\varphi^{n}(H)
form an infinite decreasing chain.

Proof.

Towards a contradition, suppose that
\varphi^{n}(H)=\varphi^{n+1}(H)
, where
n
is minimal. Note that
n>0
as
\varphi(H)<H
. Fix
x\in H
. Then, as
\varphi^{n}(H)=\varphi^{n+1}(H)
, there exists
y\in H
such that
\varphi^{n+1}(y)=\varphi^{n}(x)
. By injectivity,
\varphi^{n}(y)=\varphi^{n-1}(x)
as
\varphi^{n}(y),\varphi^{n-1}(x)\in H
. Since this holds for every
x\in H
, we conclude that
\varphi^{n-1}(H)=\varphi^{n}(H)
, a contradiction to the minimality of
n
.
∎

The following result relies heavily on algorithmic techniques detailed in
[
24
]
, which are based on
Stallings foldings
introduced in Stallings’ seminal paper
[
34
]
.

Lemma 30
.

Let
X
be a minimal shift space over
\mathcal{A}
, let
\varphi\colon F_{\mathcal{A}}\to F_{\mathcal{A}}
be a group endomorphism, and let
S\subseteq F_{\mathcal{A}}
be a finite subset such that
\left\langle\varphi(S)\right\rangle\leq\left\langle S\right\rangle
.
If there exists pairwise distinct words
u^{(n)}\in\mathcal{L}(X)
such that
\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u^{(n)})\right\rangle=\left\langle\varphi^{n}(S)\right\rangle
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
then one can (from
\varphi
and
S
):

(i)

decide whether
X
has eventually stable return groups;

(ii)

then compute the subgroup over which the return groups stabilize;

(iii)

decide whether
X
has stable return groups.

Proof.

Before studying the sequence
\left\langle\varphi^{n}(S)\right\rangle
, let us recall the following generalities on finitely generated subgroups. Let
T
be a finite subset of
F_{\mathcal{A}}
. Then one can compute the rank of
\left\langle T\right\rangle
using
[
24
, Proposition 7.1]
and
[
24
, Proposition 8.2]
successively. Moreover,
\varphi
is injective over
\left\langle T\right\rangle
if and only if
\left\langle T\right\rangle
and
\varphi\left\langle T\right\rangle=\left\langle\varphi(T)\right\rangle
have the same rank, and otherwise the rank can only decrease when applying
\varphi
.
Applied to the sequence
(\left\langle\varphi^{n}(S)\right\rangle)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, this leads to the existence of a computable
k\leq\#S
such that
\sigma
is injective over
\left\langle\varphi^{k}(S)\right\rangle
.

Since
\left\langle\varphi(S)\right\rangle\leq\left\langle S\right\rangle
, we have
\left\langle\varphi^{k+1}(S)\right\rangle\leq\left\langle\varphi^{k}(S)\right\rangle
.
To decide whether this is an equality, it then suffices to determine whether
\varphi^{k}(s)\in\left\langle\varphi^{k+1}(S)\right\rangle
for all
s\in S
. This can be done using
[
24
, Proposition 7.1]
and
[
24
, Proposition 7.2]
.

•

If
\left\langle\varphi^{k+1}(S)\right\rangle=\left\langle\varphi^{k}(S)\right\rangle
then, by induction,
\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u^{(n)})\right\rangle=\left\langle\varphi^{n}(S)\right\rangle=\left\langle\varphi^{k}(S)\right\rangle
for all
n\geq k
. By
Item

(iv)
of
Theorem

21
, this implies that
X
has eventually stable return groups, and
\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle
is conjugate to
\left\langle\varphi^{k}(S)\right\rangle
for any long enough
u\in\mathcal{L}(X)
.

•

If
\left\langle\varphi^{k+1}(S)\right\rangle<\left\langle\varphi^{k}(S)\right\rangle
, then by
Lemma

29
, the subgroups
\left\langle\varphi^{n}(S)\right\rangle
form a decreasing chain and
X
does not have stable return groups by
Item

(ii)
of
Theorem

21
.

This ends the proof of the first claim. Notice that, if
X
has eventually stable return groups, the return groups then stabilize over
\left\langle\varphi^{k}(S)\right\rangle
which is computable. As in
Proposition

27
, we can then decide whether
X
has stable return groups.
∎

.

be a shift space generated by a primitive substitution

decide whether

Assume first that

,

By

∎

Before moving on to the next example, let us briefly give further details for the computation of the constant

from
[

which appears in the above proof. Moving forward we call

(whenever it exists) the

.

Since the preservation constant is related to

[
26
]
. Let
\sigma
be a primitive substitution and let

be the shift space generated by
\sigma
. Given a word

of

(p,w,s)
such that

and

. We say that an interpretation

and

must pass. Assume that

is aperiodic. Then Mossé’s recognizability theorem
[
29
]
is equivalent to the existence of a

5.
Closure properties

This section is devoted to the study of the behavior of (eventual) stability of the return groups under two fundamental operations: derivation and application of a substitution.

5.1.
With respect to derivation

Derivation being closely related to return words, it is a natural candidate when considering preservation of (eventual) stability of the return groups.

Proposition 33
.

Let
X
be a minimal shift space over
\mathcal{A}
, let
\varphi\colon F_{\mathcal{A}}\to G
be a group morphism, and let

. If
X
has eventually
\varphi
-stable return groups of threshold
M
, then
D_{u}(X)
has eventually
\varphi\theta_{u}
-stable return groups of threshold at most
\max\{0,M-|u|\}
, where
\theta_{u}
is the associated derivating substitution for
u
.

In particular, if
X
has
\varphi
-stable return groups, then
D_{u}(X)
has
\varphi\theta_{u}
-stable return groups.

Proof.

By
Lemma

23
,
\varphi\theta_{u}\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{D_{u}(X)}(w)\right\rangle=\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(\theta_{u}(w)u)\right\rangle
for all
w\in\mathcal{L}(D_{u}(X))
. Therefore, if
K
satisfies
(|w|\geq K\implies|\theta_{u}(w)u|\geq M)
, then
D_{u}(X)
has eventually
\varphi\theta_{u}
-stable return groups of threshold at most
K
. The conclusion follows since one can take
K=\max\{0,M-|u|\}
.
∎

Consequently, if
X
has eventually
\varphi
-stable return groups of threshold
M
, then
D_{u}(X)
has eventually
\psi
-stable return groups of threshold at most
\max\{0,M-|u|\}
for all

such that
\ker(\varphi\theta_{u})\leq\ker(\psi)
. Indeed,
\eta=\psi(\varphi\theta_{u})^{-1}\colon G\to H
is then a well-defined group morphism, and

. Since

is trivial precisely when

is a free subset of

, we deduce the following corollary.

.

Let
X
be a minimal shift space over
\mathcal{A}
and let

such that

, then

.

5.2.
With respect to substitutions

Another classical operation on shift spaces is to consider the image under a substitution. In the particular case where the substitution is a derivating substitution, this operation is the converse of derivation.
Lemma

23
then gives a strong link between the sets of return words. For a general substitution, there is a similar link but the return words in the image are related to the return words to the
set of pre-images
, which might contain more than one word. The study of the behavior of (eventual) stability under application of a substitution therefore relies heavily on the notion of return words to sets of words.

Definition 37
.

A set
S\subseteq\mathcal{A}^{*}
is a
factor code
if it is non-empty and no word is factor of another.

If
S\subseteq\mathcal{L}(X)
is a factor code, then one naturally defines the return words to
S
in
X
as follows:

\mathcal{R}_{X}(S)=\left\{r\in\mathcal{A}^{+}\mid\exists u\in S\text{ st. }ru\in\mathcal{L}(X)\cap(S\mathcal{A}^{*}\setminus\mathcal{A}^{+}S\mathcal{A}^{+})\right\}.

Note that, if
S,T
are two factor codes such that
S\subseteq T
, then
\mathcal{R}_{X}(S)\subseteq(\mathcal{R}_{X}(T))^{+}
.

Lemma 38
.

Let
X
be a minimal shift space over
\mathcal{A}
and let
\varphi\colon F_{\mathcal{A}}\to G
be a group morphism. Then
X
has eventually
\varphi
-stable return groups of threshold at most
M
if and only if, for all factor codes
T\subseteq\mathcal{L}(X)
, we have
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(T)\right\rangle=\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(S)\right\rangle
, where
S=\{u_{[0,\min\{|u|,M\})}\mid u\in T\}
.

Proof.

One implication directly follows from the fact that singletons are particular factor codes. Let us prove the converse, and let
S,T\subseteq\mathcal{L}(X)
be two factor codes such that
S=\{u_{[0,\min\{|u|,M\})}\mid u\in T\}
.

By definition of
S
and
T
,
\mathcal{R}_{X}(T)\subseteq(\mathcal{R}_{X}(S))^{*}
so the inclusion
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(T)\right\rangle\leq\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(S)\right\rangle
follows. Conversely, take
r\in\mathcal{R}_{X}(S)
. There exist
u,u^{\prime}\in S
such that
ru^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}(X)\cap u\mathcal{A}^{*}
. By definition of
S
, there exist
v,v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
such that
uv,u^{\prime}v^{\prime}\in T
. Since
X
is minimal, there exist
w,t\in\mathcal{L}(X)
such that
wrtu^{\prime}v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}(X)\cap uv\mathcal{A}^{*}
and
u^{\prime}
is a prefix of
tu^{\prime}v^{\prime}
. Then
wrt\in(\mathcal{R}_{X}(T))^{*}
,
w\in(\mathcal{R}_{X}(u))^{*}
, and
t\in(\mathcal{R}_{X}(u^{\prime}))^{*}
so
r\in\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(T)\right\rangle\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u^{\prime})\right\rangle
. Moreover, as
X
has eventually
\varphi
-stable return groups of threshold at most
M
,
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle=\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(uv)\right\rangle\leq\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(T)\right\rangle
. Indeed, either
v=\varepsilon
or
|u|=M
. Similarly,
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u^{\prime})\right\rangle\leq\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(T)\right\rangle
. Thus
\varphi(r)\in\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(T)\right\rangle
. As this is true for any
r\in\mathcal{R}_{X}(S)
, we conclude that
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(T)\right\rangle=\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(S)\right\rangle
.
∎

We can now prove the counterpart of
Proposition

33
and
Corollary

34
. For a shift space
X
over
\mathcal{A}
and a substitution
\sigma\colon\mathcal{A}^{*}\to\mathcal{B}^{*}
, we let
\sigma[X]
denote the shift-closure of
\{\sigma(x)\mid x\in X\}
.

Proposition 39
.

Let
X
be a minimal shift space over
\mathcal{A}
and let
\sigma\colon\mathcal{A}^{*}\to\mathcal{B}^{*}
be a substitution. Let
\varphi\colon F_{\mathcal{A}}\to G
and
\psi\colon F_{\mathcal{B}}\to H
be group morphisms satisfying
\ker(\varphi)\leq\ker(\psi\sigma)
. If
X
has eventually
\varphi
-stable return groups of threshold
M
, then
\sigma[X]
has eventually
\psi
-stable return groups of threshold at most
\max\{1,M|\sigma|\}
.

Proof.

First, recall that
|\sigma|=\max\{|\sigma(a)|\mid a\in\mathcal{A}\}
and observe that the hypothesis on the kernels is equivalent to
\bar{\sigma}=\psi\sigma\varphi^{-1}\colon\varphi(F_{\mathcal{A}})\to H
being a well-defined group morphism. This morphism satisfies
\bar{\sigma}\varphi=\psi\sigma
.

To study
\sigma[X]
, we first factorize the substitution
\sigma
into
\tau\theta
as follows.
Let
\mathcal{A}=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{d}\}
,
n_{i}=|\sigma(a_{i})|
, and
\mathcal{C}=\{a_{i,j}\mid 1\leq i\leq d,0\leq j\leq|\sigma(a_{i})|-1\}
where
a_{i,j}\neq a_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}
if
i\neq i^{\prime}
or
j\neq j^{\prime}
. We define
\theta\colon\mathcal{A}^{*}\to\mathcal{C}^{*}
and
\tau\colon\mathcal{C}^{*}\to\mathcal{B}^{*}
by

\theta(a_{i})=a_{i,0}\cdots a_{i,n_{i}-1}\quad\text{and}\quad\tau(a_{i,j})=\sigma(a_{i})_{j}.

They clearly satisfy
\sigma=\tau\theta
.

We consider the intermediary shift space
\theta[X]
and show that it is eventually
\psi\tau
-stable of threshold at most
\max\{1,M|\sigma|\}
. To do so, we first study the return group of any non-empty
v\in\mathcal{L}(\theta[X])
. Let
a_{i,j}
and
a_{k,l}
respectively be the first and last letters of
v
. Define
p_{v}=a_{i,0}\cdots a_{i,j-1}
and
s_{v}=a_{k,l+1}\cdots a_{k,n_{k}-1}
. Then
p_{v}vs_{v}=\theta(t_{v})
for a unique
t_{v}\in\mathcal{L}(X)
. Since
v
only appears as a factor of
\theta(t_{v})
and
\theta(t_{v})
only appears as the image of
t_{v}
, we have

(1)

\mathcal{R}_{\theta[X]}(v)=p_{v}^{-1}\mathcal{R}_{\theta[X]}(\theta(t_{v}))p_{v}=p_{v}^{-1}\theta(\mathcal{R}_{X}(t_{v}))p_{v}.

Now, take
w\in\mathcal{L}(\theta[X])
of length
\max\{1,M|\sigma|\}
and
u\in w\mathcal{C}^{*}\cap\mathcal{L}(\theta[X])
. We prove that
\psi\tau\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{\theta[X]}(w)\right\rangle=\psi\tau\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{\theta[X]}(u)\right\rangle
. By
Eq.

1
, we have

\displaystyle\psi\tau\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{\theta[X]}(w)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\psi\tau(p_{w})^{-1}\psi\tau\theta\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(t_{w})\right\rangle\psi\tau(p_{w})

\displaystyle=\psi\tau(p_{w})^{-1}\psi\sigma\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(t_{w})\right\rangle\psi\tau(p_{w})

\displaystyle=\psi\tau(p_{w})^{-1}\bar{\sigma}\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(t_{w})\right\rangle\psi\tau(p_{w}).

Similarly,
\psi\tau\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{\theta[X]}(u)\right\rangle=\psi\tau(p_{u})^{-1}\bar{\sigma}\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(t_{u})\right\rangle\psi\tau(p_{u})
.
As
p_{u}=p_{w}
by definition of
u
and
w
, it then suffices to show that
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(t_{w})\right\rangle=\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(t_{u})\right\rangle
. By definition of
u
and
w
once again,
t_{w}
is a prefix of
t_{u}
and

|t_{w}|\geq\frac{|\theta(t_{w})|}{|\sigma|}=\frac{|p_{w}wq_{w}|}{|\sigma|}\geq\frac{|w|}{|\sigma|}\geq M.

As
X
is eventually
\varphi
-stable of threshold
M
, this directly implies that
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(t_{w})\right\rangle=\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(t_{u})\right\rangle
, and therefore that
\psi\tau\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{\theta[X]}(w)\right\rangle=\psi\tau\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{\theta[X]}(u)\right\rangle
. This ends the proof that
\theta[X]
is eventually
\psi\tau
-stable of threshold
K\leq\max\{1,M|\sigma|\}
.

Let us now show that
\sigma[X]
is eventually
\psi
-stable of threshold at most
K
. Let
w\in\mathcal{L}_{K}(\sigma[X])
and let
u\in w\mathcal{B}^{*}\cap\mathcal{L}(\sigma[X])
. Since
\tau
is letter-to-letter, one easily sees that
\tau^{-1}(u)\cap\mathcal{L}(\theta[X])
is a factor code and

\mathcal{R}_{\sigma[X]}(u)=\tau(\mathcal{R}_{\theta[X]}(\tau^{-1}(u)\cap\mathcal{L}(\theta[X]))).

Set
T=\tau^{-1}(u)\cap\mathcal{L}(\theta[X])
and
S=\{v_{[0,K)}\mid v\in T\}
. Note that
S=\tau^{-1}(w)\cap\mathcal{L}(\theta[X])
thus we similarly have
\mathcal{R}_{\sigma[X]}(w)=\tau(\mathcal{R}_{\theta[X]}(S))
. As
\theta[X]
is eventually
\psi\tau
-stable of threshold
K
, we obtain by
Lemma

38

\psi\mathcal{R}_{\sigma[X]}(u)=\psi\tau(\mathcal{R}_{\theta[X]}(T))=\psi\tau(\mathcal{R}_{\theta[X]}(S))=\psi\mathcal{R}_{\sigma[X]}(w).

This shows that
\sigma[X]
is eventually
\psi
-stable of threshold at most
K\leq\max\{1,M|\sigma|\}
.
∎

Observe that the bound
\max\{1,M|\sigma|\}
can safely be replaced by
M|\sigma|
except when
M=0
. The fact that
\max\{1,M|\sigma|\}
is tight when
M=0
is made clear by the following example.

Example 40
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\{a,b\}
and let
\sigma\colon\mathcal{A}^{*}\to\mathcal{A}^{*}
be defined by
\sigma(a)=ab
and
\sigma(b)=abbb
. For any shift space
X
over
\mathcal{A}
, we have
\mathcal{R}_{\sigma[X]}(a)=\{ab,abbb\}
. In particular,
\sigma[X]
cannot be stable, even when
X
is.

The following shows that this phenomenon disapears when the morphism
\sigma
is surjective between free groups.

Proposition 41
.

Let
X
be a minimal shift space over
\mathcal{A}
, let
\sigma\colon\mathcal{A}^{*}\to\mathcal{B}^{*}
be a substitution, and let
\varphi\colon F_{\mathcal{A}}\to G
and
\psi\colon F_{\mathcal{B}}\to H
be group morphisms satisfying
\ker(\varphi)\leq\ker(\psi\sigma)
. If
X
has
\varphi
-stable return groups and
\sigma\colon F_{\mathcal{A}}\to F_{\mathcal{B}}
is surjective, then
\sigma[X]
has
\psi
-stable return groups.

Proof.

For conciseness, we re-use the notations from the proof of
Proposition

39
.
Let
u\in\mathcal{L}(\sigma[X])\setminus\{\varepsilon\}
. Then for any
w\in\tau^{-1}(u)\cap\mathcal{L}(\theta[X])
, we have

\psi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{\sigma[X]}(u)\right\rangle\geq\psi\tau\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{\theta[X]}(w)\right\rangle=\psi\tau(p_{w})^{-1}\psi\sigma\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(t_{w})\right\rangle\psi\tau(p_{w}).

Since
X
has
\varphi
-stable return groups and
\sigma
is surjective, we have

\psi\sigma\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(t_{w})\right\rangle=\bar{\sigma}\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(t_{w})\right\rangle=\bar{\sigma}\varphi(F_{\mathcal{A}})=\psi\sigma(F_{\mathcal{A}})=\psi(F_{\mathcal{B}}).

This shows that
\psi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{\sigma[X]}(u)\right\rangle
contains
\psi(F_{\mathcal{B}})
, up to conjugacy by an element of
\psi(F_{\mathcal{B}})
. Consequently,
\psi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{\sigma[X]}(u)\right\rangle=\psi(F_{\mathcal{B}})
, and as it is true for all non-empty
u\in\mathcal{L}(\sigma[X])
, the shift space
\sigma[X]
has
\psi
-stable return groups by
Proposition

17
.
∎

Finally, let us highlight two consequences of these propositions.

Corollary 42
.

(i)

The family of shift spaces with eventually stable return groups is closed under application of substitutions.

(ii)

The family of shift spaces with stable return groups is closed under application of substitutions that are surjective when seen as group morphisms.

Proof.

Indeed, we can apply
Proposition

39
and
Proposition

41
with
\varphi
the identity on
F_{\mathcal{A}}
,
\psi
the identity on
F_{\mathcal{B}}
, and any substitution
\sigma
since
\ker(\varphi)
is trivial.
∎

6.
Local-global principle for stability

As stated in
Section

4.2
, if the image of
\varphi
is finite, then any minimal shift space
X
has eventually
\varphi
-stable return groups. But, as shown in
Section

3
, not every shift space has eventually stable return groups. This shows that eventual stability of return groups cannot simply be reduced to a “local” property. On the other hand, when considering stability, we show that it is sufficient to consider morphisms
\varphi
with finite images, making this property easier to study. More precisely, we prove the following
local-global
principle for stability.

Proposition 43
.

Let
X
be a minimal shift space over
\mathcal{A}
and let
\varphi\colon F_{\mathcal{A}}\to G
be a group morphism onto a subgroup separable group
G
. For every
u\in\mathcal{L}(X)
, the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle=G
;

(ii)

\psi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle=H
for every onto group morphism
\psi\colon F_{\mathcal{A}}\to H
where
H
is finite and
\ker(\varphi)\leq\ker(\psi)
.

To simplify the proof of this result, we first obtain an alternative statement using the following simple observation.

Remark 44
.

By the first isomorphism theorem, statements about images of morphisms may be translated in terms of normal subgroups. More precisely, for an onto morphism
\varphi\colon G\to H
and a subset
S\subseteq G
, the property
\varphi(S)=H
is equivalent to
S\ker(\varphi)=G
. Moreover,
H
is finite if and only if
\ker(\varphi)
has finite index.

In light of this, the statement of
Proposition

43
can be rephrased as follows. Let
X
be a minimal shift space over
\mathcal{A}
and let
N
be a normal subgroup of
F_{\mathcal{A}}
such that
F_{\mathcal{A}}/N
is subgroup separable. Then for every
u\in\mathcal{L}(X)
,
\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle N=F_{\mathcal{A}}
if and only if
\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle N^{\prime}=F_{\mathcal{A}}
for every normal subgroup
N^{\prime}\trianglelefteq F_{\mathcal{A}}
of finite index such that
N\leq N^{\prime}
. The proof relies on a simple lemma.

Lemma 45
.

Let
G
be a group and let
H\leq G
such that
G
is
H
-separable. If
HN=G
for all normal subgroups
N\trianglelefteq G
of finite index, then
H=G
.

Proof.

Let us proceed by contraposition and assume that
H
is a proper subgroup of
G
. Let
g\in G\setminus H
. Since
G
is
H
-separable, we may take a normal subgroup
N\trianglelefteq G
of finite index such that
gN\not\in H/N
. Thus
H/N\neq G/N
, which means that
HN\neq G
.
∎

Proof of
Proposition

43
.

For the forward implication, we assume that
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle=G
, which is equivalent to
\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle\ker(\varphi)=F_{\mathcal{A}}
by
Remark

44
. Clearly for every morphism
\psi\colon F_{\mathcal{A}}\to H
onto a group
H
such that
\ker(\varphi)\leq\ker(\psi)
, we then have
F_{\mathcal{A}}=\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle\ker(\varphi)\leq\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle\ker(\psi)
, thus
\psi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle=H
.

For the converse, as
G
is subgroup separable, it is in particular
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle
-separable.
By
Lemma

45
, to show that
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle=G
, it suffices to prove that
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle N=G
for every normal subgroup
N\trianglelefteq G
of finite index. Let
N
be such a subgroup.
By the correspondence theorem, there exists a normal subgroup
N^{\prime}\trianglelefteq F_{\mathcal{A}}
of finite index containing
\ker(\varphi)
such that
N=\varphi(N^{\prime})
.
Let
\psi\colon F_{\mathcal{A}}\to F_{\mathcal{A}}/N^{\prime}
denote the canonical morphism. We get
\ker(\varphi)\leq N^{\prime}=\ker(\psi)
and
F_{\mathcal{A}}/N^{\prime}
is finite. Hence, by assumption,
\psi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle=F_{\mathcal{A}}/N^{\prime}
, or in other words,
\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle N^{\prime}=F_{\mathcal{A}}
. Applying
\varphi
, we obtain
\varphi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle N=G
. As this is true for every normal subgroup
N\trianglelefteq G
of finite index, we conclude by
Lemma

45
.
∎

The next corollary highlights the special case of
Proposition

43
when
\varphi
is the identity on
F_{\mathcal{A}}
.

Corollary 46
.

Let
X
be a minimal shift space over
\mathcal{A}
and let
u\in\mathcal{L}(X)
. The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle=F_{\mathcal{A}}
;

(ii)

\psi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle=H
for every onto morphism
\psi\colon F_{\mathcal{A}}\to H
where
H
is a finite group.

Next we turn to the case where
\varphi
is the Abelianization map, that is,

\operatorname{ab}\colon F_{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathbb{Z}^{\#\mathcal{A}}\quad u\mapsto(|u|_{a})_{a\in\mathcal{A}},

where
|u|_{a}
is the number of occurrences of
a
in
u
, with the inverse
a^{-1}
counting as a negative occurrence. In this context, we can improve slightly the statement of
Proposition

43
. To this end, consider the
k
-Abelianization
morphisms (
k>1
), defined by

\operatorname{ab}_{k}\colon F_{\mathcal{A}}\to(\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z})^{\#\mathcal{A}}\quad u\mapsto(|u|_{a}+k\mathbb{Z})_{a\in\mathcal{A}}.

Proposition 47
.

Let
X
be a minimal shift space over
\mathcal{A}
and let
u\in\mathcal{L}(X)
. The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

\operatorname{ab}\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle=\mathbb{Z}^{\#\mathcal{A}}
;

(ii)

\operatorname{ab}_{k}\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle=(\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z})^{\#\mathcal{A}}
for every
k\geq 1
.

The proof relies on the following elementary fact, of which we include a proof.

Lemma 48
.

For any
k>1
, the kernel
\ker(\operatorname{ab}_{k})
is the smallest normal subgroup of
F_{\mathcal{A}}
containing all commutators and
k
-th powers.

Proof.

The fact that
\ker(\operatorname{ab}_{k})
is a normal subgroup containing commutators and
k
-th powers is an easy consequence of the definition. The fact that it is the
smallest
such normal subgroup follows from the observation that every element of
g\in F_{\mathcal{A}}
can be written in the form

g=a_{1}^{\delta_{1}}a_{2}^{\delta_{2}}\cdots a_{d}^{\delta_{d}}c,

where
c
is a product of commutators,
\delta_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathcal{A}=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{d}\}
. It is then clear that, when
g\in\ker(\operatorname{ab}_{k})
, all
\delta_{i}
must be multiples of
k
, and thus
g
is a product of
k
-th powers and commutators.
∎

Proof of
Proposition

47
.

The fact that
(i)
implies
(ii)
is an immediate consequence of
Proposition

43
.

For the converse, in light of
Proposition

43
, we need to show that, for any onto morphism
\psi\colon F_{\mathcal{A}}\to H
such that
H
is finite and
\ker(\operatorname{ab})\leq\ker(\psi)
, we have
\psi\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle=H
. It is equivalent to show that
\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)\right\rangle\ker(\psi)=F_{\mathcal{A}}
by
Remark

44
, or, as we assume that
(ii)
holds, it suffices to prove that
\ker(\psi)
contains
\ker(\operatorname{ab}_{k})
for some
k\geq 1
.

Let us fix such a morphism
\psi
. As
\ker(\operatorname{ab})\leq\ker(\psi)
, the latter contains all commutators (and in particular,
H
is Abelian). Let us show that it contains all
k
-th powers for an appropriate choice of
k\geq 1
. For each
a\in\mathcal{A}
, let
k_{a}
be the order of
\psi(a)
in
H
, and let
k=\operatorname{lcm}\{k_{a}\mid a\in\mathcal{A}\}
. Take
g=a_{1}^{\delta_{1}}\cdots a_{d}^{\delta_{d}}c\in F_{\mathcal{A}}
, where
\delta_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
c
is a product of commutators. Since
H
is Abelian, we find (in additive notation for
H
) that

\psi(g^{k})=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\delta_{i}k\psi(a_{i})=0,

since
k
is a common multiple of the orders of the
\psi(a_{i})
. This shows that
\ker(\psi)
contains all
k
-th powers in
F_{\mathcal{A}}
, thus it contains
\ker(\operatorname{ab}_{k})
by
Lemma

48
.
∎

7.
Applications to the welldoc property

It turns out that Abelian stability already appeared in the literature in a disguised form. Namely, in a paper from 2016
[
4
]
, Balková et al. studied the
well distributed occurrence
property — or welldoc for short — in relation with pseudorandom number generation. The main purpose of this section is to show precisely how welldoc relates to Abelian stability. This also allows us to obtain some sufficient conditions for the welldoc property which generalize results of Balková et al.

The original definition of welldoc is framed within the setting of
one-sided
infinite words, i.e., elements of
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}
. It goes as follows: given a one-sided infinite word
y\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}
and
w\in\mathcal{L}(y)
, consider the set

P_{y}(w)=\{y_{[0,m)}\mid m\in\mathbb{N},y_{[m,m+|w|)}=w\}.

Then, in Balková et al.’s terminology,
y\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}
is said to have the welldoc property when, for all
k\geq 1
and all words
w\in\mathcal{L}(y)
,

\operatorname{ab}_{k}(P_{y}(w))=(\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z})^{\#\mathcal{A}}.

Let us extend the notion of welldoc to the setting of general group morphisms and bi-infinite words and shift spaces. To do so, it is useful to set
x_{[i,j)}=x_{[j,i)}^{-1}
when
j<i
, where the inverse is taken in the free group
F_{\mathcal{A}}
. Then, for
x\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}
and
w\in\mathcal{L}(x)
, we define the following subset of
F_{\mathcal{A}}
:

P_{x}(w)=\{x_{[0,m)}\mid m\in\mathbb{Z},x_{[m,m+|w|)}=w\}.

Note that if
y=x_{[0,\infty)}
, then
P_{y}(w)=P_{x}(w)\cap\mathcal{A}^{*}
.

Definition 49
.

Let
\varphi\colon F_{\mathcal{A}}\to G
be a group morphism. A word
x\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}
has
\varphi
-welldoc
if, for all
w\in\mathcal{L}(x)
,

\varphi(P_{x}(w))=\varphi(F_{\mathcal{A}}).

By extension, we say that a shift space
X
has
\varphi
-welldoc
when all its elements have it.

In this terminology, Balková et al.’s definition of welldoc for
y\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}
is equivalent to the conjunction of the
\operatorname{ab}_{k}
-welldoc properties over all integers
k\geq 1
.

In the case of finite groups, this two-sided version is coherent with the original one-sided definition. In fact, welldoc is a property of the language rather than the point, as detailed in the following lemma.
In particular, a minimal shift space has
\varphi
-welldoc if and only if
any
of its elements does.

Lemma 50
.

Let
\varphi\colon F_{\mathcal{A}}\to G
be a morphism onto a finite group
G
. Let
x,y\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}
be such that
\mathcal{L}(x)=\mathcal{L}(y)
. If
x
has
\varphi
-welldoc, then so does
y
.
More precisely, for any
w\in\mathcal{L}(x)
, if
\varphi(P_{x}(w))=G
, then
\varphi(P_{y}(w))=G
.

Proof.

Let
w\in\mathcal{L}(x)
. Since
G
is finite, there exists
M
such that the elements of
P_{x}(w)
of length at most
M
suffice to obtain
G
. Letting
M^{\prime}=-M
if
x\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}
and
M^{\prime}=0
if
x\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}
, this means that for every
g\in G
, there exists
i
in
[M^{\prime},M)
such that
\varphi(x_{[0,i)})=g
and
x_{[i,i+|w|)}=w
. Since
\mathcal{L}(y)=\mathcal{L}(x)
, there exists an index
k
such that
x_{[M^{\prime},M+|w|)}=y_{[k+M^{\prime},k+M+|w|)}
. Therefore,
G\subseteq\varphi(y_{[0,k)})^{-1}\varphi(P_{y}(w))
, which implies that
\varphi(P_{y}(w))=G
.
∎

Observe that if
w=x_{[0,n)}
, then the positive (resp., negative) elements of
P_{x}(w)
are concatenations of return words (resp., inverses of return words) to
w
in
x
. More generally, we have the inclusion

(2)

P_{x}(w)\subseteq x_{[0,i)}^{-1}\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{X}(w)\right\rangle,

whenever
x_{[i,i+|w|)}=w
.
This hints at a relationship between welldoc and stability. The following result, which was proved in
[
9
]
using tools from the theory of bifix codes, further confirms this.

Proposition 51

(
[
9
, Theorem 6.1]
)
.

Let
X
be a minimal shift space and
\varphi\colon F_{\mathcal{A}}\to G
be a morphism onto a finite group
G
. Then
X
is
\varphi
-stable if and only if, for every
x\in X
and
n\geq 0
,

\varphi(P_{x_{[0,\infty)}}(x_{[0,n)}))=G.

Combined with our own results, it can be strengthened as follows.

Theorem 52
.

Let
X
be a minimal shift space over
\mathcal{A}
and let
\varphi\colon F_{\mathcal{A}}\to G
be a morphism onto a subgroup separable group
G
. The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

X
has
\varphi
-stable return groups;

(ii)

X
has
\psi
-welldoc for every onto morphism
\psi\colon F_{\mathcal{A}}\to H
where
H
is finite and
\ker(\varphi)\subseteq\ker(\psi)
.

Proof.

Thanks to the local-global principle (
Proposition

43
), it is sufficient to show that, for any morphism
\varphi\colon F_{\mathcal{A}}\to G
onto a finite group
G
,
X
has
\varphi
-stable return groups if and only if it has
\varphi
-welldoc.
The fact that
\varphi
-welldoc implies
\varphi
-stable return groups follows from (
2
).

Next assume that
X
has
\varphi
-stable return groups. Fix
w\in\mathcal{L}(X)
.
By minimality of
X
, there exists
z\in X
such that
w=z_{[0,|w|)}
and
\mathcal{L}(X)=\mathcal{L}(z_{[0,\infty)})
. Using
Proposition

51
, we have
\varphi(P_{z_{[0,\infty)}}(w))=G
.
Therefore, by
Lemma

50
, for any
x\in X
,
\varphi(P_{x}(w))=G
. As it is true for any
w\in\mathcal{L}(X)
and any
x\in X
,
X
has
\varphi
-welldoc.
∎

\varphi
is the Abelianization map by using
Proposition

47
instead of the general the local-global principle (
Proposition

43
).
This leads to the following simple characterization of the original welldoc property of Balková et al.

Corollary 53
.

Let
X
be a minimal shift space over
\mathcal{A}
. The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

X
has
\operatorname{ab}
-stable return groups;

(ii)

X
has
\operatorname{ab}_{k}
-welldoc for all integers

Analogous results were obtained recently by Espinoza
[

]
and Savalev and Puzynina
[

]

-stable return groups. Note that this last result also implies that the shift space of

\operatorname{ab}
-stable, even though it is not eventually stable.

Another interesting application of

52

\varphi
is the identity on
F_{\mathcal{A}}
.

Corollary 54
.

A minimal shift space has stable return groups if and only if it has
\varphi
-welldoc for every morphism
\varphi

In particular, we deduce the following, which generalizes in multiple ways a theorem by Balková et al. stating that Arnoux–Rauzy words have the welldoc property

]
.

Corollary 55
.

Every minimal suffix-connected shift space, and in particular, every minimal dendric shift space, has
\varphi
-welldoc for every morphism
\varphi
onto a finite group.

8.
Open questions

Since this work is a first exploration of eventual stability of return groups, many questions remain unanswered. We would like to emphasize two that we believe are of particular interest.

(i)

We recently showed that return words can be used to characterize dendricity, a purely combinatorial property
[
18
]
. Namely, a minimal shift space
X
over
\mathcal{A}
is dendric if and only if
X
has stable return groups and, for every
u\in\mathcal{L}(X)
,
\#\mathcal{R}_{X}(u)=\#\mathcal{A}
. In light of this result and of the examples given in
Section

3.1
, it is of interest to investigate the interactions between the algebraic properties of return words and some combinatorial aspects of shift spaces. For instance, can (eventual)
\varphi
-stability be used to characterize
k
-automatic shift spaces, or eventually dendric shift spaces? On the other hand, can (eventual) stability be understood from a combinatorial perspective?

(ii)

In
Section

5
, we obtained results on closure properties of (eventual) stability with respect to derivation and substitutions. As for topological conjugacy, we know that stability is not preserved. Is eventual stability preserved by topological conjugacy? In other words, is it a dynamical property? Note that, by
Corollary

42
, it is equivalent to ask whether it is closed under topological factorization.

SAPPNet: students’ academic performance prediction during COVID-19 using neural network
Authors: Naveed Ur Rehman Junejo, Qingsheng Huang, Xiaoqing Dong, Chang Wang, Adnan Zeb, Mahammad Humayoo, Gengzhong Zheng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
A variety of reasons have made it more difficult for educators and tutors to anticipate students’ performance. Numerous researchers have used various predictive models to identify students who may be at-risk of dropping out early. Additionally, these methods were used to forecast final semester grades based on various datasets. However, these prediction models still fall short of meeting educational management requirements. In this paper, we propose the deep learning (DL) based model named students academic performance prediction network (SAPPNet) to predict the students’ grades. We consider the questionnaire-based Jordan University dataset which contains demographic information, usage of digital tools before and after COVID-19, sleep times before and after COVID-19, social interaction, psychological state, and academic performance. SAPPNet consists of spatial convolution modules which are designed to extract spatial dependencies includes categorical and numerical attributes that represent static features (gender, level/year, age, digital tools used before and after COVID-19, psychological condition using prolonged e-learning tools) and temporal module for temporal dependencies involves sequences that capture changes before and after COVID-19. Additionally, we also try to implement classical machine learning (ML) models including support vector machine, k nearest neighbor, decision tree, and random forest, and DL models named artificial neural network, convolutional neural network, long short-term memory, and students learning prediction network. Simulation results show that SAPPNet achieved the best performance compared to state-of-the-art methods, with an accuracy, precision, recall, and an F1-score of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$93\%$$\end{document}93%. The proposed model with spatial and temporal modules improves the prediction performance, and it implies new aspect of the educational dataset.

Introduction

The COVID-19 pandemic crisis has had an impact on student lifestyles and the educational system globally. In an effort to lower the likelihood of infection, the educational systems decided to switch from in-person instruction to online instruction during the pandemic lockdown1,2. The abrupt change from in-person instruction raises serious questions about the effectiveness of the online learning environment. Many students lack access to the internet and are unable to afford the electronic devices. Furthermore, it’s unclear how requiring online education would affect students psychologically. This and a host of other factors affect how satisfied students are with the effectiveness of the online learning environment.

Diverse viewpoints define what constitutes a student’s academic achievement, but in modern educational settings, quantitative assessments are essential. Predicting student success is important for supporting many stakeholders in the educational process. Student performance prediction helps students make informed decisions about which courses or activities to take and makes it possible for them to arrange their academic time effectively. Teachers/tutors gain by recognizing students who are at risk and modifying lesson plans and learning resources accordingly3,4. Student performance prediction is a valuable tool for educational managers to assess curricula and optimize course systems. In summary, by using the insights gained from student performance prediction, stakeholders in educational advancement can create better plans to increase overall educational performance. Early efforts in predicting student achievement centered on EDM methods. The effectiveness of conventional data mining techniques in identifying significant patterns from educational datasets has been proven. These methods demonstrated the value of feature selection, data pre-processing, and the contribution of domain expertise to the improvement of predictive models, laying the groundwork for later developments.

Improved predictive capabilities for online learning performance are also made feasible by the ongoing advancements in feature engineering and machine learning, which have allowed for more efficient educational data mining. But in the pandemic and post-pandemic era, there has been a sharp increase in online learning data, particularly in the area of student learning behavior, which has drawn the interest of educators and academics5,6. In this new environment, university students’ demand for online learning instruction has skyrocketed. It is also capable of being well-prepared for future pandemics. It’s now a fresh question for teachers to consider whether the initial worries and conclusions can still be applied to better instruct students, as there may be fluctuations in the possible effects of various learning behaviors and characteristics on students’ performance at the end of the semester7–9.

In today’s educational environments, forecasting student success has become an increasingly important goal. The purpose of this review of the literature is to summarize the main conclusions from a number of scholarly works that investigate various approaches, such as deep learning (DL) paradigms, machine learning (ML), and educational data mining (EDM) techniques, in order to improve comprehension and prediction abilities in student performance1,10–13. The goal of the relatively new field of EDM research is to identify latent patterns in various educational environments. This includes developing teachers’ curricula, analyzing learning behaviors, analyzing students’ knowledge, and organizing course times. The ultimate goal of all activities in this subject is the improvement of student learning performance, along with ancillary objectives like lower educational costs12. Researchers collected records including students’ demographic information, academic performance and learning behavior, then establish ML based prediction models including decision trees (DT), Naive Bayes (NB), logistic regression (LR), k-nearest neighbor (K-NN) and other algorithms to predict students’ performance9,13–15. The DL models are one of the powerful methods compared to ML methods, aims to learn deep features from original features for subsequent tasks and has become benchmarks in a wide range of applications in recent years. Different DL methods have been proposed including artificial neural network (ANN), convolutional neural network (CNN), recurrent neural network (RNN), long-short term memory (LSTM), and bidirectional long-short term memory (BiLSTM) to predict the student performance during the semester in online and offline education systems5,7,15–19.

One of the most significant features of EDM is its ability to predict student success based on past behavior. Over the years, several researchers have used statistics and discrete variables to estimate the students’ performance. The candidate’s prior activities may have included their CGPA, internal semester assessments, co-curricular and social performances, gender, financial level, and the kind of school attended by the kids. The aforementioned are regarded as significant indications that have been used to accurately forecast students’ academic progress in the past. Analyzing what happens when the conventional learning environment is altered, as was the case during the COVID-19 pandemic lockdown, is a crucial component. The current query is, “will the traditional DL and ML techniques be equally relevant to the online learning environment to accurately compute the student’s performance? If so, what qualities and metrics would be useful in creating the best prediction model to assess students’ performance? Research on the following primary difficulties is typically lacking, despite some encouraging early applications of deep neural network learning in learning analytics: Analyze in-depth the differences in performance between DL algorithms and other traditional ML methods to forecast students’ academic success. Furthermore, a lot of research lacks an assessment of how changing hyper-parameters affects prediction accuracy, making it difficult to pinpoint the precise variables that are the best indicators of students’ academic success. According to authors knowledge, there is no research work using ML or DL models to predict students performance on “questionnaire-based Jordan University dataset” which contain demographic information, usage of digital tools before and after COVID-19, sleep times before and after COVID-19, social interaction, psychological state, and academic. These days, there is a serious issue with students’ physical and mental health at higher education institutions that calls for a lot of attention and involvement. Students frequently suffer from mental health illnesses, and many difficulties including stress, anxiety, and depression have a big influence on their academic performance. It is very important to analyze that the student grading performance is affected by prolonged usage of digital tools, sleeping habits, and psychological conditions.

Based on above mentioned research questions, we propose the students academic performance prediction network (SAPPNet), which is designed to predict student academic performance by analyzing patterns within online educational dataset. SAPPNet is constructed from three key stages, two consisting of spatial convolution, one consisting of temporal module, and two fully connected layers that output the final prediction classes. In addition, we also tried to implement DL and classical ML models such as SVM, LR, KNN, DT, ANN, CNN, and LSTM where DL models achieved better performance compared to state-of-the-art ML models. The simulation results show that SAPPNet outperformed the traditional models in terms of accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score.

Literature work of student performance prediction

It is noted form previous research that in the literature two different classification schemes have been examined to predict students’ learning outcomes. The first is binary categorization, which limits writers to predicting a student’s likelihood of passing or failing (i.e., dropping out or not)20. The second type of classification is called multiclass; in this type of classification, authors have taken into account grades from several classes over the semester such as GPA grading (A+, A, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$A-$$\end{document}A-, B+, B, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$B-$$\end{document}B-, C+, C, C, D, and F) or pass, fail, distinction, and withdraw7,21. Scholars frequently undertake research from several angles including predicting students’ performance, predicting pass/fail/distinction/withdrawn, and predicting early or late (final) semesters5–9. Educational data such as student demographics (gender, age, financial status, mother’s job, father’s job, etc.), prior academic performance, extracurricular and in-class activities, use of e-devices (tablets, laptops, or mobile phones), health status, and classroom participation are frequently thought to be indicators of students’ performance at the end of the semester7,22–24.

As the subject developed, ML became a potent tool for forecasting student performance. Prominent research works have investigated the use of ensemble, SVM and DT. More sophisticated predictive models were made possible by these machine learning techniques, which demonstrated how to manage intricate relationships found in educational data. Mapping-based techniques, commonly used in ML, aim to create a relationship between course grades and student-course features25. In a standards-based grading course, authors26 evaluated the efficacy of several prediction models to identify students who were considered to be at-risk. The models were trained using only in-semester performance data and employed binary output classification (Pass/Fail). Among the seven investigated modeling techniques logistic regression (LR), K-NN, multi-layer perceptron (MLP), DT, SVM, Naive Bayes Classifier (NBC), and Ensemble, the best results were achieved by the NBC and the Ensemble model. To predict the student’s grade, authors27 used the K-NN and SVM algorithms on the dataset. They then compared the accuracy of each method. The University of Minho in Portugal provided the authors with a dataset (2005-2006) that consisted of 395 samples from reports and surveys from two secondary schools in Portugal. Students are assessed twice a year (in periods G1, G2), and the final score (the aim for prediction) is obtained in period G3, which combines the results of the two earlier periods. Three common decision tree algorithms; REPtree, J48, and random tree were examined for how well they might predict grades at post-secondary education institutions in a study13. The authors used survey data in the form of questions, which was used as input for the aforementioned models. Based on its performance in comparison to the random tree and REPTree algorithms, the J48 method was ultimately deemed the superior algorithm. According to simulation results, there has been a comparison of performance with respect to precision, recall, true positive (TP), and false positive (FP). In28, authors built two classifiers with RF using a dataset that included all of the courses completed by undergraduate students at Canada’s top universities. It has also been investigated to use RF to forecast academic success and student performance. The results include two reliable classifiers and a variable importance analysis that provides valuable insights for university administrations.

According to29, accurately and promptly predicting students’ involvement levels and offering customized support are key components in lowering dropout rates in virtual learning environments (VLEs). To examine the different feature roles on attrition at Open University in the UK, they suggested a survival modeling technique. The learning outcome was seen via the lens of student engagement, which holds that higher knowledge and skill acquisition results from more student involvement with VLE. In order to improve student productivity, the course instructor was given a novel method by which the instructor can intervene in the learning process of a student who is struggling by giving them the necessary learning resources and dialog prompts.

DL has transformed the prediction of student performance in recent years by enabling complex representations to be automatically learned from raw data. DL models are a powerful class of mapping-based techniques that are used to extract complex characteristics from source data in order to improve performance on a variety of tasks. These models have become standards in many different applications in recent years. In16, authors created a complex student performance prediction network (SPPN) that uses data from junior high schools that includes background information, school specifics, study records from the past, present study data, and personal information. The SPPN has six fully connected layers: one input layer, four hidden layers, and one output layer. Among the compared algorithms, SPPN achieves the highest accuracy, averaging 77.2%. Findings indicate that using SPPN to identify specific events, like pre-warning for children who are at risk, is useful in educational settings. In the study, ANN designed especially for e-learning contexts to predict student performance was employed9. By forecasting students’ performance at the conclusion of the term (Final Grades), this research sought to raise student accomplishment levels, give more tailored and efficient teaching tactics for students, and enhance their learning skills. The dataset contained the information of 3518 students. Only the historic performance and learning platform interaction categories were taken into consideration out of the five feature categories. In order to forecast students’ academic performance (failure and dropout chances), EDM research has progressively used ML algorithms since last decades36. While they were initially able to offer fundamental insights, classic statistical techniques such as LR and DT found it difficult to identify intricate patterns in educational data37. Through the use of more complex algorithms and greater datasets, ML models like SVMs, RF, K-NN, and Naive Bayes have improved accuracy26,27,38–40. Temporal data presented difficulties for these models, though, which prompted the development of DL models like ANN5,15, CNN11, and LSTM networks20,41. LSTMs performed exceptionally well in evaluating sequential time-dependent data, such as study habits during course41, whereas CNNs, which are generally employed in image processing, were modified to detect spatial patterns in educational data42. Within the EDM community, there is a continuous discussion regarding which models produce the most thorough outcomes. While some academics support DL models, which can extract deeper insights from data despite their complexity, others contend that more interpretable and sufficient older models, such as RFs, are more appropriate for many applications. In conclusion, the models and metrics employed in EDM have advanced significantly over the past ten years, with DL approaches being a major factor in this development. Nonetheless, the continuous discussion regarding the trade-offs between interpretability and model performance emphasizes the necessity of more study. It is advised that future studies concentrate on creating dynamic and ensemble approaches for automated remedial measures and more accurate student performance prediction.

As a result, we have suggested the SAPPNet model with performance enhancement in this research work. Furthermore, it offers a comparison analysis of traditional ML and DL techniques including KNN, SVM, DT, RF, CNN, GRU, and LSTM. Compared to existing approaches, our proposed SAPPNet relies on more accurate student performance predictors.

Dataset detail

In this section, the detailed overview of the dataset named “Dataset of Jordanian university students’ psychological health impacted by using e-learning tools during COVID-19” is provided, which was retrieved from23. COVID-19 affects all facets of society, but particularly education. Schools, universities, and other higher education establishments were impacted by the pandemic. Similar to other nations, Jordan’s government declared an emergency to limit travel and stop the virus from spreading. There won’t be any opportunity for social engagement because the virus has made all social gatherings illegal. After that, the pupils switched to an online learning platform and began using digital study aids like laptops, iPads, iPhones, and tablets. This has an effect on their psychological well-being and mental health. The dataset focuses on how university students are affected by this e-learning system and their use of digital tools. The COVID-19-related mental health and psychological impact status of students are included in the dataset. The dataset then investigates if there is a relationship between the prevalence of psychosomatic symptoms and disorders and students’ prolonged use of digital e-learning resources as a result of the COVID-19 pandemic. The dataset is organized into two main parts: the first part includes demographic data, and the second part describes the psychosomatic impacts of COVID-19’s e-learning digital tools on college students’ well-being. The second section covers the five primary constructions. The first concept is about using technological devices (such an iPad, laptop, and cell phone) before and after COVID-19. Data pre-processing

To ensure the reliability and quality of data used, data pre-processing is the critical step to train and evaluate the ML and DL models. The raw data need to be converted into a form in order for the machine language to comprehend them completely, to involve the series of steps including cleaning, transforming and organizing. In the data, one of the thirty feature columns is binary (gender: male/female); the other twenty-two are categorical values, and our objective/target value (GPA) is one of the remaining twenty-two survey responses based on Likert scale. To make the data machine-interpretable, the following dependencies have been converted to numerical or binary values:Assigning numerical values to each Likert response (’Strongly Disagree’:0, ’Disagree’:1, ’Uncertain’:2, ’Agree’:3, ’Strongly Agree’:4).Applying One-Hot Encoding to each and every category feature.The target values’ label encoding (GPA/Grading: ’Below 60 / Below 2.0’:0, ’60–69 / 2–2.49’:1, ’70-79 / 2.5–2.99’:2, ’80–89 / 3–3.49’:3, ’+90 / +3.5’:4) and Time spend (’1-3’:0, ’3–6’:1, ’6–9’:2, ’9–12’:3, ’+12’:4,).We have eliminated the four rows that had a GPA of less than 2.00.

System model

Figure 3 illustrates the students academic performance prediction network (SAPPNet), which is designed for grading performance prediction by analyzing patterns within educational data. SAPPNet consists of input layer, spatial convolution modules, temporal module and fully connected layer (output layer). The requirement for human feature engineering can be decreased by using spatial convolution modules, which can automatically learn and extract pertinent features from high-dimensional data. This is particularly helpful for our data, which includes a variety of aspects like academic performance, psychological variables, and the use of digital tools. Modules for spatial convolution are skilled at handling multivariate data and can reveal feature hierarchies in space. This is similar to how they apply pattern detection to structured data with several variables, but for image data. The purpose of the temporal module is to extract sequential patterns and temporal connections from data. knowledge the evolution of student behaviors and performances in our dataset requires a knowledge of changes across time, both before and after COVID-19. Temporal module is perfect for educational datasets, where the temporal aspect is important, such monitoring student performance and behavior changes over several semesters, because they can retain information over extended periods. The model makes use of the feature extraction skills of the spatial convolution module and the sequence modeling capabilities of the temporal module by integrating the two modules. The effective collection of both spatial and temporal patterns is ensured by this hybrid technique. Below is a thorough explanation of every part of the model:

Several approaches could be taken into consideration to improve the SAPPNet model’s applicability in real-time educational contexts and overcome its constraints. First, by include more diverse datasets that reflect various educational environments, student demographics, and learning contexts, the model’s robustness and generalizability could be enhanced. This would verify that the model is not unduly specialized to a particular dataset or institution and that it functions well in a variety of scenarios. Furthermore, real-time applications require the implementation of continuous learning and model adaption processes. The model can adjust to shifting student behaviors and instructional strategies by incorporating a feedback loop that retrains it on fresh data on a regular basis, ensuring accuracy over time. The model’s interpretability and educator-friendliness are additional factors to take into account. For the purpose of making decisions in real-time settings, educators and administrators must comprehend the forecasts and underlying logic of the model. Thus, it would be helpful to provide an interpretable version of SAPPNet or to provide tools that provide concise explanations of its predictions.

The results of the study demonstrate the better predictive performance of the SAPPNet model, providing notable improvements in EDM. Our study’s findings show that, when applied to the given educational dataset, the SAPPNet model predicts student outcomes with a promising degree of accuracy and efficacy. Future studies should concentrate on enhancing the interpretability and scalability of the model, nevertheless, to guarantee its usefulness in real-time learning environments. The model’s efficacy can be more broadly generalized and its inclusion into educational systems supported with additional validation in a variety of scenarios. Although the suggested SAPPNet model shows great promise for enhancing academic results, real-time deployment is severely hampered by its present limitations. The results of our findings indicate that further study in a number of crucial areas is necessary to fully fulfill the promise of the model. Firstly, increasing the variety of datasets utilized for model training will improve the model’s resilience and applicability. Secondly, the use of continuous learning methods will guarantee the model’s long-term adaptability and accuracy. Third, making the model’s predictions easier to understand will make it more useful and well-liked by teachers. Lastly, by increasing computing efficiency, the model will become more widely available to schools with different degrees of technological capability. Future research can aid in the creation of more reliable, flexible, and approachable models that can significantly improve teaching methods by overcoming these limitations. These improvements will open the door for more sophisticated and inclusive predictive models in the field of education in addition to increasing the SAPPNet model’s usefulness in real-time educational Conclusion

In the paper, we implemented various ML and DL techniques, including SVM, K-NN, RF, DT, ANN, SLPNet, LSTM, and the proposed method SAPPNet to predict student performance. These models were trained and tested using inputs based on demographic information, health conditions, psychological states, social relations, sleep habits, digital tool usage, and academic performance (quizzes and assignments in e-learning) before and after COVID-19. Herein, students’ GPA grading have been considered as target output of the deployed predictive models. These grades groups are organized as; grades below 60, between 60 and 70, between 70 and 80, between 80 and 90, and above 90. These thresholds were selected to resemble standard academic grading intervals. The results have proved that the proposed SAPPNet method outperformed SVM, DT, RF, K-NN, ANN, LSTM, and SLPNet in terms of accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score. Although the SAPPNet model has a great deal of promise to improve educational results, in order to assure its practical implementation in real-time situations, further research must concentrate on strengthening interpretability, scalability, and computing efficiency. Increasing the diversity of datasets and including continuous learning would improve the model’s resilience and flexibility even more. In future, authors will try to design Transformer based model for students’ performance prediction to make an intelligent educational system more accurate.

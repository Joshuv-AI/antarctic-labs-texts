Regional Anesthesia in Thumb Carpometacarpal Arthroplasty: Increased Costs Without Differences in Early Emergency Department Visits or Opioid Prescriptions
Authors: Patrick Burroughs, Joyce En-Hua Wang, Daniel Griffin, James Anundson, Eliana LeMaster, Curtis Henn, Michael Kessler
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeFirst carpometacarpal (CMC) osteoarthritis is increasingly treated with CMC arthroplasty. Perioperative regional nerve blocks are commonly used in upper-extremity surgery to improve postoperative pain control, but their effect on emergency department (ED) visits and opioid use after CMC arthroplasty remains unclear. We evaluated whether perioperative regional nerve blocks were associated with fewer postoperative ED visits and opioid prescriptions after CMC arthroplasty.MethodsUsing a Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act–compliant national database of 170 million patients, we identified individuals who underwent first CMC arthroplasty between 2010 and 2023 using Current Procedural Terminology codes. Patients who received a perioperative regional nerve block were matched 1:1 to those who did not by age, sex (male or female as recorded in the database), and Elixhauser Comorbidity Index. Outcomes included ED visits within 7 days, opioid prescriptions (measured in morphine milligram equivalents) within 14 days, and episode-of-care cost.ResultsWe identified 181,241 patients who underwent CMC arthroplasty and created two matched cohorts of 33,186 patients each (76.8% women; mean age = 61.48 [standard deviation, 8.42] years). Patients who received a regional nerve block had a higher episode-of-care cost than those who did not ($2,247.00 [quartile 1–quartile 3: $1,136.00 to $3,896.00] vs $1,419.50 [quartile 1–quartile 3: $629.50 to $2,609.90]). The rates of ED visits within 7 days were similar between groups (1.93% vs 1.90%). The numbers of opioid prescriptions within 14 days were also similar (median morphine milligram equivalents = 300.0 [quartile 1–quartile 3: 200.0 to 0.5] vs 300.0 [quartile 1–quartile 3: 200.0 to 500.0]).ConclusionsIn this matched national cohort, perioperative regional nerve blocks during CMC arthroplasty increased episode-of-care cost without reducing the numbers of short-term ED visits or postoperative opioid prescriptions.Type of study/level of evidenceRetrospective comparative study III.Key words: CMC arthroplasty, Cost analysis, ED visits, Opioids, Regional anesthesia

The overprescription of opioid drugs has been implicated as a contributor to the opioid addiction crisis in the United States.1 Although hand surgery was identified as one of the specialties that prescribed these medications the most frequently, recent efforts have focused on providing guidelines for opioid prescription for postoperative analgesia in hand and upper-extremity procedures.2,3 In a retrospective claim review study of hand surgeries, 13% of adult patients continued to fill opioid prescriptions 90 days after surgery. That study also found that carpometacarpal (CMC) arthroplasty/arthrodesis had the highest opioid dosage at initial prescription among common hand surgery procedures.4

Osteoarthritis affecting the first CMC joint is common in the US population and is increasingly being treated surgically with CMC arthroplasty.5,6 Inadequate postoperative pain control is associated with higher health care resource utilization and costs.7 Postoperative pain is also a known predictor of 30-day hospital readmission and emergency department (ED) visits.8 Unplanned hospital visits after outpatient surgeries have economic implications and are a metric for quality set by the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services.9,10

Regional anesthesia (RA) is a common anesthetic technique used in hand surgery; one method is a peripheral nerve block at the level of the brachial plexus for upper-extremity procedures.11 There is limited research comparing postoperative analgesia in patients undergoing CMC arthroplasty; however, general anesthesia (GA) alone and GA with a regional nerve block are common anesthetic techniques used for this procedure. When compared with GA alone, RA decreases postoperative nausea, shortens stay in the postoperative recovery unit, and decreases postoperative pain scores when paired with GA.12, 13, 14 Given the frequency with which CMC arthroplasty is used to treat first CMC arthritis, reducing the costs and unplanned hospital visits associated with this procedure is pivotal to improving the quality of care. Therefore, we aimed to determine whether GA with a preoperative nerve block compared with GA alone reduces the number of ED visits after ambulatory surgery for CMC arthroplasty within 7 days after surgery. Secondary outcomes evaluated included total costs associated with the procedure and postoperative opioid prescriptions. We hypothesized that we would observe a decrease in the number of ED visits and costs among patients treated with an additional upper-extremity nerve block when compared with those who received GA alone.

Materials and Methods

Subjects

The PearlDiver Application (PearlDiver Technologies) is a Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act-compliant national that contains containing both commercial and Medicare claims data for 170 million patients indexed by International Statistical Classification of Diseases and Current Procedural Terminology (CPT) codes. Patients who underwent CMC arthroplasty between January 2010 and April 2023 were identified using CPT code 25447 (n = 181,241). We excluded patients younger than 18 years of age or older than 79 years of age, as well as those who underwent inpatient surgery. We then further stratified these populations by identifying patients who received a regional block for perioperative RA using CPT codes 64415 and 64417 (n = 143,714; Fig. 1). These patients were then matched 1-to-1 on the basis of age, sex, and Elixhauser Comorbidity Index to patients who had the same surgery without RA (76.8% women; age = 61.5 [standard deviation, 8.4 years]; Fig. 2).8

Description of the matched study cohort derived from patients who underwent carpometacarpal arthroplasty identified within PearlDiver Mariner database using Current Procedural Terminology code 22547. The case and control groups were matched on age, sex, and Elixhauser Comorbidity Index (ECI). The two groups had an identical breakdown by sex (76.8% women and 23.2% men) and nearly identical age ranges (for the case group, mean age = 61.48 [standard deviation, 8.42] years; for the control group, mean age = 61.50 [standard deviation, 8.41] years).

With two matched patient groups identified, all medical encounters, prescriptions, and costs in the perioperative period were tracked. Incidence of ED visits, prescription medications, and associated costs were then compared between the two groups.

Statistical analysis

We used Fisher exact test in the PearlDiver Application to determine the statistical significance of the rate of return to ED between the two cohorts, with P < .05 denoting statistical significance. Statistical differences in the cost of episodes of care or in the morphine milligram equivalent (MME) of opioids prescribed were compared using the Wilcoxon-Mann-Whitney U test, Welch t test, or Kruskal-Wallis test when appropriate.

Results

Between 2010 and 2022, which was the final year with a complete data set, the average annual incidence of CMC arthroplasty in our cohort was 13,184 procedures (range: 12,652–4,155), rising from 1,383 blocks in 2010 to 3,237 blocks in 2021 (Fig. 3). On average, the annual incidence of regional nerve blocks increased by 169.5 blocks per year (R2 = 0.969) from 2010 to 2021.

The cost of care for patients who received a regional nerve block ($2,247.00; quartile 1–quartile 3: $1,136.00 to $3,896.00) was significantly higher than that for patients who did not ($1,419.50; quartile 1–quartile 3: $629.50 to $2,609.90; P < .0001). Discussion

Perioperative regional nerve blocks are increasingly being used in patients undergoing first CMC arthroplasty.15 The use of multimodal pain management and RA has been gaining generalized popularity in the field of hand surgery and specifically for patients undergoing outpatient hand surgery, including CMC arthroplasty.15, 16, 17 At this time, the evidence in the literature is mixed regarding the benefits of RA as an alternative anesthetic technique or add-on to GA. There is limited research comparing postoperative analgesia use in patients undergoing CMC arthroplasty. In a single-center, randomized, controlled clinical trial comparing pain scores in patients undergoing primary thumb CMC joint arthroplasty who received a brachial plexus block, a multimodal analgesic regimen including anti-inflammatory medications, acetaminophen, and gabapentin was statistically noninferior to an opioid-containing pain management pathway at 24 hours and 2 weeks after surgery.18 This finding is important, because our data suggest that a perioperative nerve block does not decrease postoperative opioid prescriptions. However, additional studies have shown that adding RA did not reduce postoperative opioid demand or improve pain control compared with GA alone in a cohort of 72 patients.19 Additionally, intraoperative liposomal bupivacaine in addition to a supraclavicular nerve block has been shown to lower opioid consumption for 5 days after surgery among patients who undergo CMC arthroplasty.20

To our knowledge, our study has the largest sample size used to date to compare these two anesthetic techniques, and our findings suggest that the theoretical benefits of an additional preoperative regional block to GA may not be durable after surgery. Notwithstanding, RA has its downsides. For patients, rebound pain is a phenomenon well-characterized after RA wears off and patients experience acute pain.21 Rebound pain has been attributed to increased postoperative ED utilization.22 A survey study found that hand surgeons had hesitations regarding the potential delay to induction and unpredictability of RA success.23 A prospective, randomized controlled trial evaluating postoperative pain control among patients who underwent CMC arthroplasty or open reduction and internal fixation of distal radius fractures with RA demonstrated that postoperative ketorolac may decrease narcotic usage in the first 4 postoperative days.24 The current research has not identified the optimal multimodal pain management regimen to decrease postoperative pain, opioid consumption, and rebound pain among patients undergoing CMC arthroplasty.

There are limitations associated with the present study, including limitations inherent to the PearlDiver database and the retrospective nature of the study. When using PearlDiver, the ability to extract data for analysis is limited to the queries designed by the research team and the original patient data that were entered into the medical record. Moreover, granular data from individual patient records cannot be accessed to protect patient privacy, which can limit the types of statistical analyses performed. However, the database contains data on nearly 170 million individuals, and it is thought to represent a broad spectrum of patients that is representative of the US population. Furthermore, the database and our results only comment on MME prescribed, rather than MME consumed.

This study demonstrates the addition of RA for CMC arthroplasty leads to an increased episode-of-care costs without affecting postoperative ED visits or opioid prescribing habits. Future research should prospectively investigate perioperative pain control, opioid consumption, patient-reported and clinical outcomes, and per-patient costs of care among patients who undergo CMC arthroplasty with and without a perioperative regional nerve block.

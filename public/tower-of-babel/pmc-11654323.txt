Dental students’ perceptions of integrating online learning in the dental curriculum
Authors: Banan Al-Natour, Rima Safadi, Abed Al-Hadi Hamasha, Reema Karasneh, Mohammad A Alkandari, Sarah A Hamasha
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionFollowing the worldwide lockdown due to COVID-19, education globally switched from classroom to online learning. This transition impacted the teaching techniques used in dental education. Thus, the objective of this study was to explore the perceptions and attitudes of undergraduate dental students’ experience with online learning. The benefits and drawbacks of integrating online learning into dental teaching were assessed as well as the factors that enhanced the online learning experience.MethodsThis is a cross sectional descriptive study employing a survey design. A web-based survey was distributed to the undergraduate dental students at Jordan University of Science and Technology. All dental undergraduate students of different academic levels were eligible to participate (n = 1500). The responses were collected anonymously through August 2022. Descriptive statistics and the Pearson Chi-square test were utilized.ResultsA total of 984 undergraduate dental students participated in this questionnaire. Females and year four students dominated the study population (64.7%, 31.3%). Internet connection was not a barrier to online learning for most students (61.4%). The preference for online learning over on campus for most students was due to time and money saving, better comprehension, and less social anxiety (n = 421, 45.7%). Drawbacks of online learning for most students (60%) were the difficulty of maintaining focus and the poor design of lectures (76.3%). The low cost of the internet was a factor that significantly encouraged students to favour online learning (P value = 0.01). Active interaction during lectures was a main factor that encouraged students to stay focused during online sessions (P value < 0.001). For a significant number of students, the lecturers demonstrated superior teaching skills during online sessions in comparison to the classroom (P < 0.001).ConclusionDespite the positive attitude of undergraduate dental students towards online learning, many improvements are required. Enhancing online lecture design which can be facilitated by implementing comprehensive training and workshop programs for lecturers. Actively engaging students during lectures is necessary to help students regain focus during online sessions. Finally, decreasing lecture times to match students’ attention spans is fundamental. By focusing on interface design and providing structured training for lecturers, a more effective online learning environment can be achieved.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12909-024-06497-6.Keywords: Education, Distance; Students, Dental; Curriculum

Introduction

Online learning offers access to a wide range of information without the need to be in the classroom. Different educational institutes have utilized this type of learning for the last few decades [1]. Online learning can be described as employing electronic technology to deliver teaching materials to students and to facilitate the communication between them and their teachers [2]. The term online teaching is used when 80% of course content is delivered online, while “Face-to-face” learning is termed when (0–29) % of the content is delivered online, but hybrid or blended teaching is when (30–80) % of the content is delivered online [3].

Prior to the outbreak of the infectious respiratory coronavirus (COVID-19), most teaching was considered face-to-face teaching [4]. Lectures were delivered on campus except for clinical training for medical or dental students which took place in hospitals or medical and dental teaching centres. In March 2020,a global pandemic was announced by the World Health Organization (WHO) and a worldwide lockdown was called to contain the COVID-19 viral spread and minimize its related deaths [5, 6]. This was critical in controlling outbreaks and protecting vulnerable populations. As a result, education at all levels faced unprecedented challenges and a steep transition in teaching methods took place where physical traveling to the learning facilities to obtain education was prohibited. This resulted in the interruption of medical and dental training as well as clinical placements [7]. Dental schools were largely impacted by the COVID-19 pandemic. Dental practice involves working in close proximity with patients and with the main sources of infective organisms, blood and saliva. Additionally, dental operative procedures result in the generation of aerosols and droplets which are regarded as a significant mode of viral transmission [8]. Dental students at both pre-clinical and clinical levels were profoundly affected by the pandemic and online learning was used as a replacement to mitigate the viral spread and to resume education [9]. In Jordan, the Dental bachelors consists of a 5-year program, in which the final two-three years are mainly clinically taught with their incorporated theoretical counterparts. Following the pandemic, most academic universities across the globe and in Jordan adopted and integrated online learning into their teaching style to reduce the risk of viral transmission [10–12].

Opposing views on the advantages of online over offline learning have been reported by several studies [13–15]. Benefits of online learning reported include time and money management, while drawbacks include family distractions, poor internet connection, and the overwhelming number of resources online [14, 15]. Some questions still arise about facilitating a better online learning experience for dental students.This study aimed to explore the perceptions and attitudes of undergraduate dental students regarding the integration of online learning into dental education post-COVID-19.

Addressing student’s concerns is vital to ensure successful learning outcomes, bridge any existing gaps in the online teaching process, and propose training initiatives for faculty members and lecturers where needed. Ultimately, this will lead to generating competent future dentists and oral healthcare providers that support a stronger dental healthcare system.

Methods

Design and study participants

A cross-sectional study was conducted at Jordan University of Science and Technology (JUST), Irbid, Jordan. The faculty of dentistry at JUST offers undergraduate and postgraduate programs. An average of 350 undergraduate students graduate each year. The number of females enrolling in the dental program is usually higher than males. In the year of the study, the number of females was three times higher than the number of males.

The questionnaire was built based on available literature which examine the advantages and disadvantages of online learning, the student’s preferred learning method, and the instructor’s knowledge and skill in online teaching [4, 9, 13, 16, 17]. The questionnaire was devised after being discussed with two experts in epidemiology from two different universities, who were required to provide feedback and indicate any necessary changes to the survey. The validity of the questionnaire was established using a pilot test by discussing and collecting data with ten undergraduate dental students not included in the study sample before being revised and edited. Wording of ambiguous or complex questions were revised and the layout of questions were modified to improve readability. The final questionnaire explored five major categories: [1] Demographic information [2], Effect of Internet characteristics and connectivity on online learning [3], Students’ learning preferences [4], Students’ engagement during online learning and the online lecture characteristics [5], Effect of lecturers’ demeanour and skill during online learning on students comprehension and academic performance. The questions used in this survey were yes or no or multiple choice questions applying the “required” and “Limit to one response” validation options. (supplementary material 1). The reason for choosing this type of questions was to provide clear quantitative data and to encourage high response rates. The internal consistency of the scale was assessed using Cronbach’s Alpha that demonstrated good reliability, with a Cronbach’s Alpha coefficient of 0.827.

Study participants

All undergraduate students (n = 1500) were invited to participate in the study via students’ E-learning and social media platforms (Email, Facebook, WhatsApp). Google forms link for the questionnaire was used for data collection. The objectives and aims of the questionnaire were explained to all participating students at the beginning of the questionnaire with a detailed description. Participants were required to read this information before proceeding to the questions. Participants were informed that proceeding with the survey indicated their consent to participate in the study. Ethical approval was obtained from the Institutional Research Board (IRB approval, 16/146/2021) to collect participants’ data.

Postgraduate dental students were excluded as they follow distinctive clinical programs that differ significantly from undergraduate students. This will ensure a homogenous sample allowing for accurate analysis and conclusions without potential bias from the differing experiences.

Data collection

The study utilized a cross-sectional study design to capture a snapshot of students’ perceptions and attitudes with regard of online learning following the worldwide lockdown due to COVID-19. The online survey method was selected due to its accessibility, enabling us to reach a broad sample of undergraduate students efficiently. The form was available from the 15th of August 2022 to the 30th with follow-up reminders sent after three and seven days. The obtained sample size represents 65.6% of the total population which provided confidence that the sample is representative to the target population and ensures reliable and generalizable results.

Data analysis

Data was exported as an Excel sheet from Google Forms, then refined to be used by IBM SPSS STATISTICS V23 for data analysis. Descriptive statistics were adopted, and Pearson chi-square analysis was used with a p-value < 0.05 suggesting statically significant differences among categorical parameters.

Results

Demographics and its impact on learning preference

A total of 984 dental students from different academic levels participated in this survey. Hence, the response rate was 65.6%. The majority of participants were females (n = 637, 64.7%). Regarding the level of study, the highest proportion of participants were year four (n = 308, 31.3%), and the lowest were year one students (n = 63, 6.4%). Concerning the method of transportation to the university campus, a higher percentage of students lived near the university and hence did not need to transport to campus (n = 524, 53.2%). Similar percentages of males and females preferred online teaching (61,62%) (P value = 0.476). The preclinical 3rd year (68%) and clinical 4th year (66%) significantly preferred online teaching compared to year one (52.4%) and year five students (53.4%) (P value < 0.05). Students who owned cars were more likely to prefer online teaching (69%), followed by students who used private transportation (62%). However, those who lived near the school were the least to favor online teaching (51.5%) (P value < 0.05). Table 1.1 and 1.2 describe the demographic data in detail.

The student preferences among different teaching methods

According to the preferred didactic learning method, most students chose online learning (n = 607, 61.6%). Regarding early morning or late evening lectures, most students favored online sessions (n = 766,77.8%) over the classroom (n = 219, 22.2%). The reasons for selecting online learning over classroom for most students, (n = 421, 45.7%) were related to a combination of factors. These factors included saving money and time, better comprehension of the study material, and minimal social anxiety associated with online learning. On the other hand, only a minority of students selected classroom learning for theoretical lectures (n = 378, 38.4%). The reasons for preferring classroom learning were mainly for the social interactions in the classroom (n = 334, 42.6%). When students were asked about their preferred teaching materials during online sessions, higher percentages of students preferred multiple resources (n = 612, 62.4%). These recourses included videos-lecture handouts-articles-eBooks. However, a smaller percentage preferred pre-recorded or live online lectures without supplemental study materials (n = 225, 22.9%).

Regarding utilizing case-based discussions as a partial replacement for missed oral medicine seminars, most students reported that online seminars were a beneficial replacement (n = 591, 60.4%). Additionally, most students indicated their desire to attend future online case-based discussions (CBD)(n = 658, 67.8%). These factors also influenced students to prefer online CBDs to on-campus CBD (P value < 0.005). Furthermore, most students revealed their preference for blended learning in the future (n = 499, 47%). Table 3 describes these findings in detail.

Discussion

The learning process has dramatically changed since the COVID-19 pandemic. Accessibility to education on campus became hindered, making online learning essential [6]. Modifying education delivery has been challenging, especially for low-income countries such as Jordan [7, 15]. Thus, an assessment of online teaching methods is needed to modify and enhance the existing teaching approaches. This study aimed to assess the benefits/drawbacks of integrating online learning into the dental curriculum according to the perceptions of undergraduate dental students and suggest recommendations to improve the online learning experience. This study reported many benefits, including time and money saving, less social anxiety, and readily accessible study material. However, it also reported several drawbacks, such as the inability to maintain total concentration and the poor lecture design.

The internet connection needed for online learning was not a barrier to learning; on the contrary, most students indicated the affordability and ease of connecting online. Unlike at the beginning of the pandemic, when most Jordanian universities struggled to shift their teaching to online due to the lack of supportive technological infrastructure and internet access [7, 15]. However, for high-income countries, such as Germany, connecting to lectures online never posed any problems amidst the pandemic [18].

Additionally, more than half of the students preferred online learning over classroom learning. This was due to several factors: cost-effectiveness, time-saving, better comprehension of study material, and less social anxiety. Studying at home is more affordable financially and saves time since it negates the need for transportation. The study material is also readily accessible since the lecture can be recorded thus, students can review them allowing to revisit complex topics at their own pace, enhancing retention and understanding. However, classroom learning does not allow this feature. The benefits of online education for dental and medical students has also been highlighted by numerous studies in various countries [14, 17, 19–21].

Although majority of students indicated their preference of online learning over classroom, this was not the case for first year or fifth year students. For First year students, this maybe due to the challenges of switching from the traditional online learning they have adapted to through out their school years. They also may have found it more challenging due to the absence of interaction and social engagement. As this primary reason for students to prefer classroom learning over online. Regarding fifth year students, reasons for disliking online learning may be due to the difficulty in acquiring practical skills via online learning as well as the stress of completing the larger clinical requirements in the final year as reported by Jordanian students in previous studies [11, 12].In regards to didactic materials, most dental students demonstrated interest in diverse learning styles, such as live or recorded lectures, handouts, videos, and e-books. Similarly, a high percentage of Romania students considered dentistry-related videos useful [22].

Case-based discussions are usually carried out during oral diagnosis and oral medicine seminars. In these CBD, an oral condition with the signs and symptoms of the patients involved is presented to the students. Before online learning, students would attend such seminars on campus, however, since the shift of learning to online, seminars have been conducted in a blended manner. Most students preferred attending CBD online instead of traveling to the campus. Furthermore, around half of the students favoured maintaining online CBD alongside clinics in the future. This was also the preference for dental students elsewhere [18, 22]. Additionally, the majority of students preferred the blended teaching method in the future, where clinical training can resume on campus while theoretical lectures can be delivered virtually. In agreement, a recent cross-sectional survey at JUST also found that most students favoured blended learning as the method of learning in the future [12].

Regarding students’ attentiveness during online lectures, a higher percentage of students indicated a loss of focus during sessions and less motivation to learn. This was one of the main disadvantages presented by this study. This concurs with a Jordanian study which also reported a lack of motivation during online sessions [11]. Indeed, this was also a complaint for students in India and China [23, 24]. To overcome the lack of motivation, recent research has highlighted several effective strategies which include, setting regular quizzes and, implementing frequent assessments of online study material [25, 26].

One of the drawbacks of online learning explored in this study was the lack of concentration and focus. Majority of students in this study complained of loosing overall concentration and focus during online lectures. This could occur for a variety of reasons such as becoming disinterested or reluctant to participate in online sessions. According to students in this study, the crucial factors that motivated them to maintain focus during online lectures were active participation and engagement between the student and lecturer and being notified by the lecturer which keeps the students attentive and involved in the learning process. Additionally, the visibility of the lecturer during online learning has been reported to positively impact the online learning process, as confirmed by students in this study. This visual connection may compensate for the lack of face-to-face interaction during online learning. Indeed, previous studies have emphasized the importance of such factors in improving online learning outcomes [27–29].

Another main disadvantage reported by students in this study was the poor design of online lectures. Several studies have also reported this disadvantage [12, 14, 16, 30]. To enhance the effectiveness of online lecture design, it is important to implement structured professional developmental programs for lecturers that focus on design principles, effective use of technology and assessment methods. In addition, supplying lecturers with high-tech laptops and digital pens may assist lecturers in designing more interactive lectures. Digital pens can create a more realistic experience for students mimicking the traditional classroom setting. As interaction between students and lecturers can occur in real-time hence maintaining student’s attention. It can also support various learning styles allowing for adjustments such as larger font size, different colours and the use of symbols. Using a digital pen has also been suggested by students in India as a solution to creating more immersive and interactive online lectures [31]. Regarding lecture duration, the optimum lecture duration reported by students was thirty minutes to forty five. Interestingly, a study in Turkey revealed that the optimum lecture duration should not exceed twenty minutes [32]. The study stated that beyond twenty minutes, the lecturer may project negative emotions onto their students. However, if more time is needed, the study proposed that instructors should place more effort into enhancing their lecture design. Although students reported a loss of focus during online learning and the poor design of online lectures, these factors did not affect the student’s grades as reported by the majority of students in this study. This result partially agrees with a study on medical students’ anatomy exam performance. As student’s performance in online exams was superior to exams conducted on campus [33]. This may be due to students’ increased social anxiety on campus or the ease of cheating during online exams as highlighted by several studies [34, 35].

Several factors related to the lecturer significantly enhanced the overall learning experience for students, hence, impacted their preferences towards online teaching. These factors included clear voice and language, better presentation of the lecture, and efficient time management. In online settings, students may not have the same visual cues as in the classroom hence, using well articulated language and clear voice can greatly improve comprehension. In addition, efficient time management can contribute to a positive learning experience by respecting student’s time which may lead to higher satisfaction and retention of the online study material. These factors were also reported by a recent study, proving them essential for an effective online learning experience [36]. Concerning lecturer familiarity with online learning, majority of students indicated so, This might be attributed to the fact that this study was conducted sometime after COVID-19 pandemic, a time when educators were compelled to quickly adapt to online teaching methods. Hence, gaining experience and comfort with digital learning platforms.

Regarding classroom learning, it was preferred by a smaller number of students. For most students, this was due to the social interaction between peers and lecturers. This was also reported by students in Croatia [19]. Indeed, social interaction remains a key to stimulating better participation and attentiveness among students, and the lack of social communication is a barrier to online learning as confirmed by previous studies [27, 37]. However, this can be mitigated during online learning by encouraging and motivating students to participate and engage with each other. Indeed, video conferencing software, such as breakout rooms on Microsoft Teams, allows students to be grouped into separate rooms, where they can carry out their group discussions together and alongside their instructor.

Keeping progress of the teaching process is central to delivering the maximum quality of teaching; thus, this study emphasizes the drawbacks of the current teaching methods and suggests improvements according to undergraduate dental students. This is the first in-depth study that explored the perceptions of undergraduate dental students’ regarding integrating online teaching in Jordan’s dental education post-COVID-19. The strength of this study is the large sample size of dental students across preclinical and clinical years. However, the cross-sectional design of the study would limit the ability to infer causality however, capturing data at a critical point of time provides valuable insight into students’ experiences. The convenience sampling technique is also a limitation of this study. Furthermore, this study was carried out across one university (JUST), other dental schools were not included. This was due to various reasons such as the limited funding and personal which restricted the ability to broaden the study scope. In addition to the complexity and time consuming process of gaining ethical approval.

In addition, the study relied on a self-reported questionnaire; thus, elements of recall bias might have been introduced. Additionally, while yes/no questions or multiple choice questions applying the “required” and “Limit to one response” validation options were the type of questions utilized in this survey, its crucial to recognize their limitations, as they may restrict the participants’ ability to express their thoughts fully and may limit the depth of the information obtained, however; these types of questions encouraged higher response rates in the study.

Conclusion

This study assessed online learning according to JUST undergraduate dental students. It identified areas of online learning that need further improvement and the existing benefits. The benefits of integrating online learning according to undergraduate students included the ease of lecture access, the ability to save finances, the flexibility of rescheduling lectures especially early morning or late evening, and the minimization of social anxiety accompanied by face-to-face learning. The drawbacks of integrating online learning included students’ distraction during lectures and the poor lecture design. Additionally, improvements suggested by this study include reducing lecture time to minutes and organizing training programs and workshops for lecturers and faculty members to strengthen their lecture design to deliver more interactive lectures. These suggestions could significantly improve the efficacy of dental education, thus yielding more confident and professional dentists, hence, upgrading the dental healthcare system.

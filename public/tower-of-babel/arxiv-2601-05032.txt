Abstract
The temporal evolution of the propagation environment plays a central role in integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) systems. A slow-time evolution manifests as channel aging in communication links, while a fast-time one is associated with structured clutter with non-zero Doppler. Nevertheless, the joint impact of these two phenomena on ISAC performance has been largely overlooked. This addresses this research gap in a network utilizing orthogonal frequency division multiplexing waveforms. Here, a base station simultaneously serves multiple user equipment (UE) devices and performs monostatic sensing. Channel aging is captured through an autoregressive model with exponential correlation decay. In contrast, clutter is modeled as a collection of uncorrelated, coherent patches with non-zero Doppler, resulting in a Kronecker-separable covariance structure. We propose an aging-aware channel estimator that uses prior pilot observations to estimate the time-varying UE channels, characterized by a non-isotropic multipath fading structure. The clutter’s structure enables a novel low-complexity sensing pipeline: clutter statistics are estimated from raw data and subsequently used to suppress the clutter’s action, after which target parameters are extracted through range–angle and range–velocity maps. We evaluate the influence of frame length and pilot history on channel estimation accuracy and demonstrate substantial performance gains over block fading in low-to-moderate mobility regimes. The sensing pipeline is implemented in a clutter-dominated environment, demonstrating that effective clutter suppression can be achieved under practical configurations. Furthermore, our results show that dedicated sensing streams are required, as communication beams provide insufficient range resolution.

RV

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}_{i,v^{\prime},s}=\sum_{v=1}^{V}e^{-\frac{j2\pi vv^{\prime}}{V^{\prime}}}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{i,v,s}\in\mathds{C}^{{M_{\rm{BS}}}},

(48)

where
v^{\prime}\in[1,V^{\prime}]
and
V^{\prime}\geq V
, accounting for zero padding. The delay of each target is quantized with a resolution of
1/\Delta_{f}V^{\prime}
.
The angle profiles associated with each range profile are then computed through
MUSIC

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}_{v^{\prime},s}=\frac{1}{I}\sum_{i=1}^{I}\mathbf{r}_{i,v^{\prime},s}\mathbf{r}_{i,v^{\prime},s}^{\mathsf{H}}\in\mathds{C}^{{M_{\rm{BS}}}\times{M_{\rm{BS}}}}.

(49)

39
), let us denote with
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{v^{\prime},s}
the noise subspace of
\mathbf{Q}_{v^{\prime},s}

v^{\prime}
-th bin is
P_{s,v^{\prime}}(\theta)=1/{\mathbf{a}(\theta)^{\mathsf{H}}\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{v^{\prime},s}\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{v^{\prime},s}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{a}(\theta)}
.
The next step is to compute the velocity profile associated with each range bin. We first project each range profile onto the angle with the highest
MUSIC
power within that range bin, denoted by
\theta^{\textrm{M}}_{v^{\prime}}
.
Then, an inverse DFT is applied to each column of this matrix

\displaystyle q_{i^{\prime},v^{\prime},s}=\sum_{i=1}^{I}e^{\frac{j2\pi ii^{\prime}}{I^{\prime}}}\mathbf{a}(\theta^{\textrm{M}}_{v^{\prime}})^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{r}_{i,v^{\prime},s}.

(50)

By collecting all the
q_{i^{\prime},v^{\prime},s}
into a matrix, the
RV

s
-th stream
\mathbf{V}_{s}\in\mathds{C}^{I^{\prime}\times V^{\prime}}
, where
I^{\prime}\geq I
accounts for zero padding.

Precoding Strategies

In this section, we will present the precoding strategies adopted by the system.
We assume that all the subcarriers within the same coherence block have the same precoding matrix. We’ll thus drop the subcarrier index from the subsequent derivations and derive the precoding matrix of an arbitrary block.
During pilot transmission, the system does not have
CSIT
, or rather assumes that the
\widehat{\mathbf{h}}_{i,k}
at the end of the previous frame has drifted too far from its actual value.
For this reason, during pilot slots, the BS relies solely on the second-order statistics of
\mathbf{h}_{i,k}
, enabling eigen-beamforming
[
50
]
. Indeed, the
n
-th largest eigenvalue of
\mathbf{C}_{{\textrm{Tx}},k}
shall be denoted by
\mathbf{u}_{{\textrm{Tx}},k,n}
.
When the
UE
feeds back the channel estimates
\widehat{\mathbf{H}}_{i,k}
to the transmitter,
thus making
CSIT
available, the
BS
employs
MMSE
precoding
[
51
, Ch. 5]
.
Let us collect all the pilot slot indices in the set
\mathcal{P}
, then the precoding matrix for
UE

k
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{i,k}=\begin{cases}[\mathbf{u}_{{\textrm{Tx}},k,1},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{{\textrm{Tx}},k,M_{\rm{UE}}}]&\textrm{if}\penalty\ i\in\mathcal{P},\\
\mathbf{T}_{i}^{-1}\alpha_{k}\widehat{\mathbf{H}}_{i,k}^{\top}&\textrm{otherwise},\end{cases}

(51)

where
\mathbf{T}_{i}\hskip-2.84526pt=\sum_{k=1}^{K}(\alpha_{k}^{2}\widehat{\mathbf{H}}_{i,k}^{\top}\widehat{\mathbf{H}}_{i,k}^{*}+\overline{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Xi}}}_{i,k})+\sigma^{2}_{k}\mathbf{I}_{{M_{\rm{BS}}}}
.
Here
\overline{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Xi}}}_{i,k}\in\mathds{C}^{{M_{\rm{BS}}}\times{M_{\rm{BS}}}}
is obtained by summing all the
{M_{\rm{BS}}}\times{M_{\rm{BS}}}
blocks along the main diagonal of
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Xi}}_{i,k}
.
The sensing beam definition is not dependent on whether
i
is a data or pilot slot: we are adopting a beam-sweeping strategy, discretizing the sensing interval
[\theta_{\textrm{start}},\theta_{\textrm{end}}]
into
\{\theta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{I}
.
The interference between the sensing stream and the communication ones must be eliminated; therefore, if some degree of
CSIT
is available, the sensing beams must be projected onto the null space of the communication channels, thus defining the sensing precoding vector as

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}_{i,v,S}=\begin{cases}\mathbf{a}(\theta_{i})&\textrm{if}\penalty\ i\in\mathcal{P},\\
\left(\mathbf{I}_{M}-\widehat{\mathbf{H}}_{i}^{\dagger}\widehat{\mathbf{H}}_{i}\right)\mathbf{a}(\theta_{i})&\textrm{otherwise},\\
\end{cases}

(52)

where
\widehat{\mathbf{H}}_{i}=\left[\alpha_{1}\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{i,1}^{\top},\dots,\alpha_{k}\widehat{\mathbf{H}}_{i,K}^{\top}\right]^{\top}\in\mathds{C}^{KM_{\rm{UE}}\times{M_{\rm{BS}}}}
.

VIII
Conclusions

This paper examined a
MU-MIMO
(
MU-MIMO
)
OFDM

ISAC
network that concurrently serves multiple
UE
and performs monostatic sensing. The temporal evolution of the propagation environment was shown to induce channel aging, motivating the development of an aging-aware channel estimator, as well as Doppler-affected clutter.
The clutter’s structure enabled us to develop a novel radar pipeline, which facilitates the estimation of multiple target positions in a clutter-dominated environment. Said pipeline’s low complexity makes it possible to be deployed on
ISAC
-capable
BS
operating in the presence of urban clutter, a scenario for which Kronecker-separability holds.
Our results show that, in low-to-moderate mobility regimes, reliance on a block-fading model leads to substantial performance degradation compared to aging-aware estimation. Moreover, we have shown that under reasonable assumptions, structured clutter can be effectively modeled and mitigated, enabling more accurate evaluation of
ISAC
performance. As part of future work, we plan on extending this model to multi-static
ISAC
scenarios, where the sensing geometry is more complex and perfect knowledge of the transmitted symbol is not always guaranteed.
Furthermore, non-homogeneous clutter models will be investigated.

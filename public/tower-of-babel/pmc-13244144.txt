Vascular Disease Prospective–Retrospective Observational Registry for Aortic Aneurysm and Peripheral Artery Disease in Korea: Study Rationale and Methodology

INTRODUCTION

Among arterial diseases, peripheral artery disease (PAD) and aortic aneurysm (AA) are two of the most common and clinically significant entities. Although their underlying pathophysiological mechanisms differ—PAD is characterized by progressive arterial stenosis and occlusion due to atherosclerotic plaque formation, whereas AA involves pathological dilatation of the arterial wall secondary to extracellular matrix degradation—both conditions represent major vascular disorders associated with substantial morbidity and mortality [1-3]. Despite their distinct anatomical and clinical manifestations, PAD and AA share several key risk factors, including smoking, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, and dyslipidemia, as well as overlapping biological pathways involving endothelial dysfunction, lipid accumulation, inflammatory activation, oxidative stress, and proteolytic imbalance [4-7]. These shared mechanisms suggest that PAD and AA may reflect different phenotypic expressions within the continuum of systemic atherosclerosis rather than entirely separate disease entities [8,9].

Both open surgical repair and endovascular interventions are widely used for the management of AA and PAD, and recent technological advances, such as conformable stent graft systems for AA and atherectomy combined with drug-coated balloon (DCB) strategies for PAD, have improved procedural feasibility and long-term durability [10,11]. However, in the early stages, both diseases are often asymptomatic, resulting in delayed diagnosis and missed opportunities for timely intervention. Such delays contribute to disease progression and adverse outcomes, including limb-threatening ischemia and aneurysm rupture [8,12,13].

Large-scale vascular registries have demonstrated the value of standardized data collection and long-term follow-up for outcome assessment and quality improvement in vascular care [14,15]. In Korea, however, integrated longitudinal data encompassing both AA and PAD remain limited, highlighting the need for a structured registry platform. The absence of such a system limits the understanding of disease heterogeneity, evaluation of treatment effectiveness, and development of tailored risk-management strategies in real-world settings [16,17].

Building on the established role of national and international vascular registries in generating real-world evidence, monitoring outcomes, and supporting quality improvement in vascular care [18,19], the Vascular disease Prospective–Retrospective Observational Registry (VAPOR) was established as a mixed retrospective–prospective cohort designed to systematically collect clinical, imaging, and procedural data from patients with AA and PAD. This registry aims to characterize shared and disease-specific risk factors, assess the influence of medical and revascularization therapies on outcomes, and develop predictive models for cardiovascular and limb-related events to support personalized vascular management and long-term prognostic evaluation using established vascular registry and guideline-based frameworks [20-23].

Through this registry platform, which was initiated at a single coordinating center and designed for multicenter expansion, we aim to elucidate the systemic nature of atherosclerosis, improve patient risk stratification and clinical decision-making, and generate robust real-world evidence to guide vascular practice [16,20,23]. Ultimately, this initiative aims to advance evidence-based guidelines and inform national strategies for the prevention, treatment, and policy development of vascular diseases.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

1) Organization and study objectives

The VAPOR was established to collect and analyze data from patients treated for AA or PAD, with the goal of evaluating treatment outcomes and long-term prognosis. This mixed retrospective–prospective registry aims to generate high-quality, real-world clinical evidence and assess the clinical efficacy and safety of various treatment modalities. The findings are expected to provide foundational data to enhance the quality of pharmacological and interventional therapies for vascular diseases in Korea.

2) Study design and population

The VAPOR is a mixed retrospective–prospective observational registry. Although it was designed as a multicenter observational platform, the initial phase of enrollment was conducted at a single coordinating center. The registry infrastructure was specifically developed to enable stepwise expansion to multiple participating institutions.

Eligible participants include adult patients aged ≥19 years who were diagnosed with AA or PAD and underwent aneurysm repair, endovascular aneurysm exclusion, or revascularization procedures. Patients with isolated vasculitis, connective tissue disorders, or other rare non-atherosclerotic vascular diseases are excluded. For the prospective cohort, all participants are required to provide written informed consent before enrollment. The retrospective component currently includes 4,438 unique patients treated between January 2011 and March 21, 2023, at the coordinating center. The requirement for informed consent was waived for this cohort.

Clinical outcomes, including the composite primary endpoints of major adverse limb events (MALE) or all-cause mortality, are verified through medical record review and entered into a standardized electronic case report form (eCRF). Although the registry includes both retrospective and prospective cohorts, data collection in both groups is based on post-procedural clinical records, thereby minimizing differences in data-acquisition processes between cohorts.

Enrollment for the prospective cohort began after approval was obtained from the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Asan Medical Center (IRB No. 2023-1089) and will continue for 10 years, targeting approximately 2,500 patients, with an expected enrollment of approximately 250 patients per year. In the prospective cohort, patient enrollment occurs immediately before the planned revascularization or aneurysm repair procedure. Clinical and procedural data are entered into the eCRF immediately after completion of the procedure. Consequently, all cases are recorded regardless of procedural success, including aborted or technically unsuccessful procedures.

Scheduled clinical follow-up visits are conducted at 1, 6, and 12 months and subsequently at 3, 5, and 10 years post-procedure. The total study duration, encompassing registration, follow-up, and data analysis, is estimated to be approximately 21 years (Fig. 1). Data are collected exclusively from routine clinical care and standard medical records. The recorded variables include demographic characteristics, medical history, imaging and procedural data, laboratory parameters, medications, and clinical outcomes. Because certain variables, including functional assessments, patient-reported outcomes, and serial imaging follow-up data, are not consistently available in the retrospective cohort, the minimum retrospective and expanded prospective datasets are presented separately in Tables 2 and 3. Patients are enrolled on the basis of findings from computed tomography angiography (CTA), lower extremity angiography, ultrasonography, echocardiography, and physical examinations. The definitions of AA and PAD are based on current international guideline criteria (Table 4).

The registry collects structured imaging variables derived from routine clinical imaging studies, including CTA, duplex ultrasonography, and echocardiography. Raw imaging files are not centrally stored in the registry database. Instead, imaging data remain archived within the institutional clinical imaging systems of the participating centers, whereas key imaging parameters are recorded in the registry database using standardized variables. To improve measurement consistency and reduce interobserver variability, future registry updates will incorporate the storage of original imaging data using dedicated analysis software (e.g., Aorta AVIEW), enabling standardized assessment across participating centers.

All data entered into the registry are de-identified before entry. Each participant is assigned a unique registry identification number, and no personally identifiable information is stored in the database. This anonymization process is applied to both the retrospective and prospective cohorts. Participating centers submit only de-identified data to the registry database, ensuring that patient confidentiality is preserved even at the coordinating center.

Procedure-related variables are entered by the physicians who performed the intervention. Trained research coordinators enter other clinical data, including demographic characteristics, laboratory values, and diagnostic test results. To ensure data accuracy and completeness, registry entries are reviewed weekly by center operators.

3) Statistical analysis

Baseline demographics and clinical characteristics will be summarized by treatment group. Continuous variables will be presented as mean±standard deviation or median with interquartile range (IQR), as appropriate, and compared using Student t-test or Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Categorical variables will be expressed as counts with percentages and compared using the chi-square or Fisher exact test. Survival analyses will be performed using the Kaplan–Meier method, with group comparisons using the log-rank test. Adjusted hazard ratios with 95% confidence intervals will be estimated using Cox proportional hazards regression models incorporating demographic, clinical, angiographic, and procedural covariates. The proportional hazards assumption will be assessed using log–log plots and Schoenfeld residuals. Multivariable models using forward stepwise selection will be constructed to identify independent predictors of primary and secondary outcomes. Variables with P-values <0.20 in univariate analyses or those considered clinically relevant will be included. All tests will be two-sided, and a P-value of <0.05 will be considered statistically significant. Since the primary outcomes are time-to-event endpoints, participants without a documented event will be censored at the date of their last confirmed follow-up. Follow-up completeness will be periodically evaluated, and baseline characteristics will be compared between participants with complete and incomplete follow-up to assess potential bias.

RESULTS

1) Primary outcomes

The primary outcomes of the registry include major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), MALE, and aneurysm-related or procedure-related adverse events after open surgical repair (OSR), endovascular aneurysm repair (EVAR), thoracic endovascular aortic repair (TEVAR), or percutaneous transluminal angioplasty (PTA). MACE is defined as a composite of cardiovascular death, non-fatal myocardial infarction (MI), and non-fatal stroke, adjudicated according to international standards (Universal Definition of Myocardial Infarction; American Heart Association/American Stroke Association [AHA/ASA] criteria) [24,25]. MALE is defined as major amputation above the ankle or major reintervention, including new bypass surgery, endovascular revascularization, thrombectomy/thrombolysis, or reintervention for restenosis.

All endpoint definitions are prespecified and based on established international guideline criteria. Aneurysm-related events in patients with AA include aneurysm-related death, rupture, or reintervention. Procedure-specific outcomes are also recorded: EVAR/TEVAR-specific events include persistent or recurrent type I/III endoleak, sac diameter increase of ≥5 mm, or graft limb occlusion; OSR-specific events include anastomotic pseudoaneurysm or reoperation for aneurysm recurrence. Applicable outcomes are evaluated in both the AA and PAD cohorts at 30 days and during long-term follow-up after the procedure.

2) Secondary outcomes

Secondary outcomes include technical success, hemodynamic and imaging parameters, safety endpoints, and patient-centered outcomes. Technical success is procedure-specific: for EVAR/TEVAR, accurate device deployment at the intended site without type I/III endoleak, conversion, or intraprocedural death; for OSR, completion of planned anastomosis with immediate flow restoration; and for PTA, <30% residual stenosis with restored distal flow.

Patency outcomes include primary, assisted primary, and secondary patency, as assessed by duplex ultrasound or CTA, and target lesion revascularization/target vessel revascularization rates. Imaging and hemodynamic parameters for AA include sac diameter change of ≥5 mm and endoleaks (type I-III), while those for PAD include ankle–brachial index or toe–brachial index improvement of ≥0.15, Rutherford category improvement of ≥1 grade, and restenosis defined as ≥50% diameter reduction or peak systolic velocity ratio ≥2.5.

Safety endpoints include 30-day adverse events such as death, stroke, MI, acute kidney injury according to Acute Kidney Injury Network/Kidney Disease Improving Global Outcomes (AKIN/KDIGO) criteria, clinically relevant bleeding (according to International Society on Thrombosis and Haemostasis [ISTH] criteria or Bleeding Academic Research Consortium [BARC] type ≥2 criteria), access-site complications, contrast-induced nephropathy, and graft or wound infection [26,27]. Patient-centered outcomes include quality of life (EQ-5D-5L and VascuQoL scores), functional test results (6-minute walk test, Walking Impairment Questionnaire), length of hospital stay, 30-day readmission, and reoperation.

Long-term outcomes include amputation-free survival, defined as freedom from death or major amputation, and aneurysm-related event-free survival, defined as freedom from aneurysm-related death, rupture, or reintervention. All event definitions are based on relevant international guidelines and reporting standards, including the European Society for Vascular Surgery guidelines, Society for Vascular Surgery reporting standards for lower-extremity peripheral arterial disease, Global Vascular Guidelines, and the AHA/American College of Cardiology (ACC) recommendations. Follow-up data on mortality, reintervention, and other clinical outcomes will be collected at scheduled post-procedural follow-up visits and verified using electronic health records and national mortality data. Participants may discontinue follow-up if they withdraw consent in the prospective cohort, die, or are lost to follow-up.

3) Study status

As of April 15, 2026, a total of 5,249 unique patients have been enrolled in the VAPOR, including 2,193 patients with AA and 3,287 patients with PAD. Because AA and PAD are not mutually exclusive disease entities within the registry, patients with both conditions are included in both disease-specific cohorts. A total of 231 patients had both AA and PAD.

The retrospective cohort included 4,438 unique patients, comprising 1,843 patients with AA and 2,793 patients with PAD, including 198 patients with both conditions. Since IRB approval was obtained on March 21, 2023, 811 patients have been prospectively enrolled, comprising 350 patients with AA and 494 patients with PAD, including 33 patients with both conditions (Table 5).

DISCUSSION

The VAPOR is a registry initiative in Korea designed to comprehensively characterize the clinical characteristics and long-term outcomes of two major yet distinct arterial diseases: AA and PAD. Because systemic atherosclerosis often affects multiple vascular territories, some patients in the registry are included in both the AA and PAD cohorts [4-7]. Although AA and PAD are included within a unified registry infrastructure, the two disease cohorts will be analyzed separately for disease-specific outcomes. Shared cardiovascular outcomes may be evaluated across the overall registry population when appropriate, with disease type incorporated as a stratification or adjustment variable in pooled analyses. Despite their shared vascular context, VAPOR is designed to analyze AA and PAD as independent disease entities with distinct therapeutic and prognostic considerations. This approach provides a robust, evidence-based foundation for developing disease-specific management strategies tailored to the Korean population.

A key strength of the registry is its hybrid retrospective–prospective design. The retrospective component captures extensive real-world data spanning more than a decade, encompassing 4,438 unique patients treated for AA or PAD. This longitudinal depth allows the evaluation of temporal trends in treatment, such as the transition from OSR to EVAR for AA and from bypass surgery to endovascular interventions, including PTA and DCB angioplasty, for PAD. In parallel, the prospective component enables standardized, high-quality data collection with predefined outcomes and structured follow-up protocols. Together, these complementary datasets provide a platform for real-world comparative analyses of both diseases.

From a clinical viewpoint, VAPOR is positioned to generate actionable, high-quality real-world evidence. In the AA cohort, the registry will elucidate the outcomes related to technical success, endoleak formation, sac changes, and reintervention following EVAR and OSR, particularly in patients with complex anatomies. In the PAD cohort, it will enable comprehensive comparisons of revascularization strategies in relation to patency, limb salvage, functional outcomes, and patient-reported outcomes. Such evidence is essential because randomized controlled trials often exclude elderly patients or those with multiple comorbidities, who represent a substantial proportion of real-world AA and PAD populations.

Importantly, the registry is committed to developing risk-prediction and outcome-modeling tools specifically tailored to the Korean population. Existing prognostic models for AA and PAD have largely been derived from Western cohorts and may not accurately reflect the risk profiles, vascular characteristics, treatment patterns, and clinical outcomes of East Asian patients. By integrating clinical, imaging, procedural, and longitudinal outcome data, VAPOR will support the development of locally calibrated models for predicting major vascular outcomes and guiding individualized treatment decisions.

Although VAPOR is currently based at a single coordinating center, it was designed as a multicenter registry platform intended for stepwise expansion to additional institutions. The coordinating center provides a substantial dataset owing to its large patient volume and procedural diversity. The scalable eCRF and integrated data infrastructure of the registry were specifically designed to facilitate seamless expansion to other centers across Korea. This planned transition to a multicenter framework is expected to improve external validity and generalizability, ultimately positioning the VAPOR to inform national health policy and guide the development of standardized, evidence-based clinical guidelines for both AA and PAD.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, although the registry was designed for multicenter expansion, the current dataset was derived from a single coordinating center, which may limit generalizability. Second, the retrospective component is subject to inherent limitations, including incomplete data capture and variability in follow-up assessments. Third, some variables, such as patient-reported outcomes, functional assessments, and serial imaging parameters, are more consistently available in the prospective cohort than in the retrospective cohort. Finally, raw imaging data are not yet centrally stored, although future registry updates are planned to incorporate standardized imaging storage and analysis. Despite these limitations, VAPOR provides a sustainable infrastructure for longitudinal vascular data integration and represents an important step toward real-world evidence generation, risk prediction, quality improvement, and policy development in Korean vascular surgery.

CONCLUSION

The VAPOR is a landmark initiative in Korean vascular surgery that employs a mixed retrospective–prospective design to systematically study AA and PAD. It aims to generate robust real-world evidence for developing locally calibrated risk models, refining treatment strategies, and enhancing patient outcomes. Initiated at a single high-volume center and structured for multicenter expansion, the VAPOR is poised to inform national health policy and contribute meaningfully to reducing the burden of vascular diseases in the Korean population.

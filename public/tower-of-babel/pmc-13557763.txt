Letter to the Editor: Great Minds Think Alike
Authors: Yuan-Ya Liao, Yu-Min Lin
Published: 2026

We read with great interest the article titled “Surgical Technique: Management of a Pathological Fracture in the Femoral Neck: Case Report and Literature Review” by Hashem et al.1 They described their technique for treating unicameral cyst in the femoral neck of young patients aged from 4 years 9 months to 8 years old with 2 mm Kirschner wires. Their technique was not unprecedented, and a similar technique was, to our knowledge, first described by Wagner2 in 1978 for proximal femur corrective osteotomies. Randall et al3 also reported using the Wagner transphyseal fixation technique treating unicameral cysts (seven of 18 cases) and aneurysmal bone cysts (three of 18 cases) in their series with three recurrences and one needing revision. Moreover, Randall et al3 described their techniques in detail, and Aljuhani et al4 expanded the indication. Comparing with a pediatric locking plate, Wagner transphyseal fixation is more suitable for cysts abutting the physes in the proximal femur in young patients with rare reports of physeal arrest.5 Great minds think alike.

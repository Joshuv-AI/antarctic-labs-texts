Multidimensional quadrangle condition and cuboctahedra in latin hypercubes
Anna A. Taranenko

Abstract
The well-known quadrangle criterion states that a latin square is istopic to the Cayley table of a group if and only if all quadrangles spanned by the same triple of symbols coincide in the fourth symbol. Gowers and Long (2020) reformulated it in the following way: the Cayley tables of the most associative quasigroups have the maximum number of octahedra. In the present paper, we state the multidimensional quadrangle condition for d d -dimensional latin hypercubes in terms of the reconstruction of subcubes of order 2 2 from a bundle of d + 1 d+1 entries and in terms of the maximal number of cuboctahedra. In particular, we show that the must associative d d -ary quasigroups have Cayley tables such that every 2 2 -dimensional plane is isotopic to a latin square principally isotopic to the Cayley table of a group. We also estimate the number of cuboctahedra in latin squares and hypercubes from below and provide some computational results. Keywords: quadrangle condition, latin hypercube, latin square, multiary quasigroup, associativity MSC2020: 15B15, 20N05

Introduction

A
latin square

L
of order
n
is an
n\times n
-array filled with
n
symbols so that each row and each column of
L
contains all
n
symbols. Every latin square can be considered as the Cayley (multiplication) table of a quasigroup: a binary operation with the left and right inverses.

There is a natural question about conditions under which a latin square is the multiplication table of some group, i.e., a quasigroup with associativity and an identity element. Permutations of the rows, columns and symbols of a latin square allows us to obtain another (isotopic) latin square that is the Cayley table of a quasiagroup with an identity element. About a century ago, Brandt
[
brandt.qudrangle
]
and Frolov
[
frolov.groupCayley
]
proposed a simple criterion for associative latin squares that is now known as the
quadrangle condition
.

Theorem 1

(
[
brandt.qudrangle
,
frolov.groupCayley
]
)
.

A latin square
L
is isotopic to the Cayley table of group if and only if for all
a,b,c
forming a subsquare

\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}

the entry
d
is uniquely defined.

In
[
GowLong.approxgroups
]
, Gowers and Long raised the question of how associative a quasigroup can be. As one possible measures of the degree of associativity, they proposed counting the number of pairs of identical rectangles in a latin square, which they called “octachedra”. Latin squares of order
n
isotopic to the Cayley table of a group have
n^{5}
“octachedra”, which is the maximum possible number, and a larger number of “octachedra” corresponds to greater associativity of a latin square.

In the present paper, we use term “cuboctachedron” instead of “octachedron”. It was introduced in
[
KwanSahSaw.SubstrctLS
]
by Kwan, Sah, Sawhney, and Simkin and refers to the geometric resemblance of this object to a cuboctachedron in the corresponding
3
-partite
3
-uniform hypergraph. In
[
KwanSahSaw.SubstrctLS
]
, it was shown that a random latin square of order
n
has
n^{4}(1+o(1))
cuboctahedra with high probability.

The main aim of the present paper is to state the quadrangle condition for latin hypercubes and multiary quasigroups. In Section
2
, we introduce the multidimensional analogue of a cuboctahedron, give the necessary definitions, and provide preliminary results. Section
3
is devoted to the proof of the multidimensional quadrangle condition. We show that the Cayley tables of the most associative
d
-ary quasigroups has
2
-dimensional planes isotopic to latin squares that are principally isotopic to the Cayley tables of groups. As in the
2
-dimensional case, we also state the multidimensional quadrangle condition in terms of the reconstruction of subcubes of order
2
from a bundle of entries and the maximality of the number of cuboctahedra in a latin hypercube.

In addition, in Section
4
we obtain several lower bounds on the number of cuboctahedra in latin squares and hypercubes. In Section
5
, we compare these bounds with the actual numbers of cuboctahedra in latin squares of orders
n\leq 6
and in
3
-dimensional and
4
-dimensional latin hypercubes of orders
4
and
5
.

From the definitions it is easily follows that isotopic latin hypercubes have the same number of cuboctahedra. Meanwhile, a conjugation of a latin hypercube may change its number of cuboctahedra. For example, we verified that every
4

Proposition 1
.

Let

*
-entry in subsquare

c&*\end{array}

. Then

are principally isotopic and they are isotopic to a Cayley table of a group.

Proof.

By Theorem

, the latin squares

and
L^{\prime}
are isotopic to the Cayley tables of groups. To show that they are principally isotopic, it is sufficient to permute rows and columns of
L

so that they have the same first row and the same first column. The condition on the subsquares implies that the resulting latin squares are identical.
∎

3
Multidimensional quadrangle condition

We start this section with a generalization of the quadrangle condition to a multidimensional case. For this purpose we need more definitions.

Let
Q
be a
d
-dimensional latin hypercube of order
n
. A
bundle
in
Q
is a collection of indices
(\alpha^{0},\alpha^{1},\ldots,\alpha^{d})
such that for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
a pair of indices
\alpha^{0}
,
\alpha^{i}
belong to line of direction
(i)
. A
d
-dimensional subcube
S
of order
2
is
spanned
by a bundle
(\alpha^{0},\alpha^{1},\ldots,\alpha^{d})
if it is composed by indices
\beta
such that if for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
we have
\beta_{i}\neq\alpha^{0}_{i}
, then
\beta_{i}=\alpha^{i}_{i}
. The filling of a bundle, subcube, or other submatrix
S
in
Q
is an indexed collection of symbols
\{q_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in S}
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
Q
be a
d
-dimensional latin hypercube of order
n
. The filling of a bundle uniquely determines the filling of a spanned subcube of order
2
if and only if every
2
-dimensional plane of
Q
of direction
(i,j)
is principally isotopic to a latin square
L^{i,j}
which is isotopic to a Cayley table of some group.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
d
. For
d=2
the statement is equivalent to Theorem
1
.

Assume that the filling of every bundle in
Q
uniquely determines the filing of the spanned subcube of order
2
. Let us fix some direction
(i,j)
of
2
-dimensional planes and consider a hyperplane
\Gamma
that either contains
2
-dimensional planes of this deriction or do not intersect them.

Since
\Gamma
is a
(d-1)
-dimensional latin hypercube, the filling of every bundle in
\Gamma
uniquely determines the filling of the spanned
(d-1)
-dimensional subcube. By the inductive assumption, all
2
-dimensional planes of direction
(i,j)
in
\Gamma
are principally isotopic to a latin square
L
which is isotopic to the Cayley table of some group.

Similarly, consider a hyperplane
\Gamma^{\prime}
parallel to hyperplane
\Gamma
. Then all
2
-dimensional planes of direction
(i,j)
in
\Gamma^{\prime}
are principally isotopic to a (possibly different) latin square
L^{\prime}
which is isotopic to the Cayley table of some group. In all
2
-dimensional planes of direction
(i,j)
in
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
subsquares of order
2
are defined uniquely by three other symbols, Consequently, from Proposition
1
we have that latin squares
L
and
L^{\prime}
are principally isotopic.

Assume now that for every direction
(i,j)
there is a latin square
L^{i,j}
which is isotopic to the Cayley table of some group such that every
2
-dimensional plane of direction
(i,j)
in
Q
is principally isotopic to
L^{i,j}
. Consider a
d
-dimensional subcube
S
of order
2
spanned by a bundle
(\alpha^{0},\alpha^{1},\ldots,\alpha^{d})
and filled by symbols
q^{0},q^{1},\ldots,q^{d}
. By the inductive assumption, we uniquely find the symbols in all
d
hyperplanes of the subcube
S
that contain the index
\alpha^{0}
. Using these symbols, we uniquely determine the filling of hyperplanes of
S
going through indices
\alpha^{1},\ldots,\alpha^{d}
. Thus we completely reconstruct the filling of the subcube
S
.
∎

Our next goal is to state the quadrangle condition of Theorem
2
in terms of the maximality of the number of cuboctahedra in a latin hypercube. We start with an upper on the numbers of
k
-dimensional cuboctahedra.

Proposition 2
.

Given
0\leq k\leq d
, every
d
-dimensional latin hypercube

of order

has no more than
{d\choose k}n^{2d-1}(n-1)^{k}

-dimensional cuboctahedra and

Proof.

Recall that a

-dimensional cuboctahedron is a collection of pairs
\{(a_{1}^{i},a_{2}^{i}),(b_{1}^{i},b_{2}^{i}):i=1,\ldots,d\}

k

(a_{1}^{i},a_{2}^{i})
for which
a_{1}^{i}\neq a_{2}^{i}
. We have
{d\choose k}n^{d-k}(n(n-1))^{k}
ways to choose
d
pairs
(a_{1}^{i},a_{2}^{i})
such that there are exactly
k
inequalities
a_{1}^{i}\neq a_{2}^{i}
. The condition
q_{a_{1}^{1},\ldots,a_{1}^{d}}=q_{b_{1}^{1},\ldots,b_{1}^{d}}
allows us to chose arbitrarily all except one

that can be done in

ways. The remaining values of
b_{2}^{1},\ldots,b_{2}^{d}
for a
k
-dimensional cuboctahedron are uniquely defined from equalities
q_{a_{j_{1}}^{1},\ldots,a_{j_{d}}^{d}}=q_{b_{j_{1}}^{1},\ldots,b_{j_{d}}^{d}}
for all
j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}\in\{1,2\}
. Thus the maximum number of

-dimensional cuboctahedra in a

is
{d\choose k}n^{2d-1}(n-1)^{k}
.

To find the total number of cuboctahedra, it is sufficient to sum these values from
0
to
d
.
∎

Now we are ready to prove the second form of the quadrangle condition.

Theorem 3
.

A

-dimensional hypercube

has

-dimensional plane of

of direction
(i,j)
is principally isotopic to a latin square

which is isotopic to a Cayley table of some group.

Proof.

By Proposition
2
,

is the maximum number of cuboctahedra in a

-dimensional latin hypercube of order

.

Al latin hypercube

has the maximum cuboctahedra if every
d
-dimensional subcube
S
order
2
spanned by a bundle
(\alpha^{0},\alpha^{1},\ldots,\alpha^{d})
and filled by symbols

coincides with a subcube

spanned by a bundle
(\beta^{0},\beta^{1},\ldots,\beta^{d})
and filled by the same symbols. By Theorem

, this condition is equivalent to that every

-dimensional plane of

of direction
(i,j)
is principally isotopic to a latin square
L^{i,j}
which is isotopic to a Cayley table of some group.
∎

We conclude this section with some comments on the obtained characterization of latin hypercubes with the maximum number of cuboctahedra. First, Theorem

implies that the Cayley tables of

-iterated groups

Next, in

and latin hypercubes of order

4
Lower bounds on the number of cuboctachedrons

In this section, we prove several lower bounds on the numbers of cuboctahedra in latin squares and hypercubes. We start with cuboctahedra of small dimensions.

Proposition 3
.

The number
c_{0}(Q)
of
0
-dimensional cuboctahedra in a
d
-dimensional latin hypercube
Q
of order
n
is
c_{0}(Q)=n^{2d-1}
.

Proof.

A
0
-dimensional cuboctahedron is an ordered pair of identical symbols of a latin hypercube. For each of
n
symbols, there are
n^{2d-2}
ordered pairs of entries of
Q
containing a given symbol.
∎

Proposition 4
.

The number
c_{1}(Q)
of
1
-dimensional cuboctahedra in a
d
-dimensional latin hypercube
Q
of order
n
is
c_{1}(Q)=dn^{2d-1}(n-1)
.

Proof.

Note that a
1
-dimensional cuboctahedron is a an ordered pair of identically filled ordered dominos (pairs of entries from one line). The number of ways to choose the first domino is equal to
dn^{d}(n-1)
: there are
d
ways to choose the direction of the domino,
n^{d}
ways to choose the first entry, and
(n-1)
ways to choose the second entry in the line of this direction. The number of ways to choose the second domino is
n^{d-1}
because it is uniquely defined by its first entry.
∎

Next, we find the number of degenerate cuboctahedra. A cuboctahedron
\{(a_{1}^{i},a_{2}^{i}),(b_{1}^{i},b_{2}^{i}):i=1,\ldots,d\}
in a
d
-dimensional latin hypercube
Q
is
degenerate
if
a_{j}^{i}=b_{j}^{i}
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
and
j\in\{1,2\}
. In other words, a degenerate cuboctahedron is a taken twice submatrix of
Q
.

Proposition 5
.

The number
\delta_{k}(Q)
of degenerate
k
-dimensional cuboctahedra in a
d
-dimensional latin hypercube
Q
of order
n
is
\delta_{k}(Q)={d\choose k}n^{d}(n-1)^{k}
.

Proof.

By the definition, the number of degenerate
k
-dimensional cuboctahedra is equal to the number of
k
-dimensional submatrices of order
2
in
Q
. There are
{d\choose k}
ways to choose the direction of the submatrix,
n^{d}
ways to choose the first entry, and
(n-1)^{k}
ways to choose other entries in selected
k
lines. So there are
{d\choose k}n^{d}(n-1)^{k}
degenerate
k
-dimensional cuboctahedra in total.
∎

The above statements imply that the total number of cuboctahedra in a given latin hypercube is determined by the numbers of non-degenerate cuboctahedra of dimensions
k\geq 2
. For latin squares, we give a better bound on the number of non-degenerate
2
-dimensional cuboctahedra (and hence on the total number of cuboctahedra), while for higher-dimensional latin hypercubes we give, for the sake of simplicity, only the essential parts of the lower bound.

Theorem 4
.

Every latin square
L
of order
n
contains at least
3n^{4}+n^{3}-9n^{2}+6n
cuboctahedra.

Proof.

By Propositions
3
and
4
, there are
2n^{4}-n^{3}
cuboctahedra of dimensions
0
and
1
in every latin square of order
n
. By Proposition
5
, we have in addition
n^{4}-2n^{3}+n^{2}
degenerate cuboctahedra of dimension
2
.

Let us estimate the number of non-degenerate
2
-dimensional cuboctahedra.
Given symbols
x,y,z\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
, let
t^{u}_{x,y,z}
denote the number of
2\times 2
subsquares
S^{u}_{x,y,z}
of the form

\begin{array}[]{cc}x&y\\
z&u\end{array}

in the latin square
L
. Since every pair of subsquares
S^{u}_{x,y,z}
in
L
is a non-degenerate
2
-dimensional cuboctahedron, the total number of non-degenerate
2
-dimensional cuboctahedra is equal to
\sum\limits_{x,y,z,u}t^{u}_{x,y,z}(t^{u}_{x,y,z}-1)
.
From the definition of a latin square, for all
x,y,z
, where
x\neq y
,
x\neq z
, we have that
\sum\limits_{u=0}^{n-1}t^{u}_{x,y,z}=n
and
t^{y}_{x,y,z}=t^{z}_{x,y,z}=0
.

Consider the case
y\neq z
. By the pigeonhole principle, either there exists
u
such that
t^{u}_{x,y,z}\geq 3
or there exist two different
u
and
u^{\prime}
such that
t^{u}_{x,y,z}\geq 2
and
t^{u^{\prime}}_{x,y,z}\geq 2
. In the first case, three subsquares
S^{u}_{x,y,z}
compose
6
non-degenerate
2
-dimensional cuboctahedra, and in the second case two pairs of
S^{u}_{x,y,z}
and
S^{u^{\prime}}_{x,y,z}
give
4
non-degenerate
2
-dimensional cuboctahedra. Assuming that for all
x,y,z
, where
x\neq y
,
x\neq z
,
y\neq z
, the second (worst) case is realized, we obtain at least
4n(n-1)(n-2)=4n^{3}-12n^{2}+8n
non-degeneral
2
-dimensional cuboctahedra of such type in total.

Assume now that
y=z
. Again, by the pigeonhole principle, we see that there exists
u
such that
t^{u}_{x,y,y}\geq 2
. Also we note that the number
t^{x}_{x,y,y}
is always even. So in this case the minimum number of the cuboctahedra is achieved when there are exactly two subsquares
S^{x}_{x,y,y}
for each
x,y
, where
x\neq y
, and all other subsquares
S^{u}_{x,y,y}
appears once. A pair of subsquares
S^{x}_{x,y,y}
gives us
2
non-degenerate
2
-dimensional cuboctahedra, and so in total we have
2n(n-1)=2n^{2}-2n
additional cuboctahedra.

Summing up, every latin square
L
of order
n
has at least

(2n^{4}-n^{3})+(n^{4}-2n^{3}+n^{2})+(4n^{3}-12n^{2}+8n)+(2n^{2}-2n)=3n^{4}+n^{3}-9n^{2}+6n

cuboctahedra.

∎

Using a similar method, we estimate from below the number of non-degenerate
k
-dimensional cuboctahedra in latin hypercubes.

Proposition 6
.

Let
d\geq 3
,
n>d
, and
2\leq k\leq n
.
Then every
d
-dimensional latin hypercube
Q
of order
n
contains at least
{d\choose k}n(n-d)^{2^{k}-1}r_{k}(r_{k}-1)
non-degenerate
k
-dimensional cuboctahedra, where
r_{k}=\lfloor n^{d-1}(n-1)^{k+1-2^{k}}\rfloor
.

Proof.

Fix a symbol
s\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
and one of
{d\choose k}
directions of
k
-dimensional planes in
Q
. Let estimate the number of non-degenerate
k
-dimensional cuboctahedra of a chosen direction with a corner symbol
s
.

By the definition, each cuboctahedron is a pair of
k
-dimensional submatrices of order
2
in
Q
with the same pattern of symbols. So if there are
t
different
k
-dimensional submatrices of order
2
with the fixed corner and filling by symbols, then they add
t(t-1)
non-degenerate
k
-dimensional cuboctahedra.

The number of
k
-dimensional submatrices of order
2
of a given direction and having corner in a fixed entry is
(n-1)^{k}
, and the total number of such submatrices with corner symbols
s
is
n^{d-1}(n-1)^{k}
.

On the other hand, the number of possible symbol patterns of
k
-dimensional matrices of order
2
in a latin hypercube of order
n
with a fixed corner symbol
s
does not exceed
(n-1)^{2^{k}-1}
and is not less than
(n-d)^{2^{k}-1}
. The minimum of the number of cuboctahedra is achieved when each possible symbol pattern of
k
-dimensional submatrices of order
2
appears as equal times as possible, i.e., when it appears at least
\lfloor n^{d-1}(n-1)^{k+1-2^{k}}\rfloor=r_{k}
times. So there are at least
(n-d)^{2^{k}-1}r_{k}(r_{k}-1)
non-degenerate
k
-dimensional cuboctahedra of a given direction and with the corner symbol
s
, and
{d\choose k}nr_{k}(r_{k}-1)
such cuboctahedra in total.

∎

Note that for small
n
the bounds
(n-1)^{2^{k}-1}
and
(n-d)^{2^{k}-1}
on the number of possible symbol patterns in
k
-dimensional matrices of order
2

can be quite far from the exact number. So in Section
5
we estimate them more precisely for latin hypercubes of small order and dimension.

At last, we are ready to state the lower bound on the number of cuboctahedra in latin hypercubes.

Theorem 5
.

Let
d\geq 3
and
n>d
. For
2\leq k\leq d
, define
r_{k}=\lfloor n^{d-1}(n-1)^{k+1-2^{k}}\rfloor
. Then every
d
-dimensional latin hypercube
Q
of order
n
contains at least

(d+1)n^{2d}-(d-1)(n^{2d-1}+n^{d})-dn^{d+1}+n\sum\limits_{k=2}^{d}{d\choose k}(n-d)^{2^{k}-1}r_{k}(r_{k}-1)

cuboctahedra.

Proof.

By Propositions
3
and
4
, there are
dn^{2d}-(d-1)n^{2d-1}
cuboctahedra of dimensions
0
and
1
in a
d
-dimensional latin hypercube of order
n
.
By Proposition
5
, we have in addition
\sum\limits_{k=2}^{d}{d\choose k}n^{d}(n-1)^{k}=n^{d}(n^{d}-d(n-1)-1)
degenerate cuboctahedra of dimension
k
. At last, for each
k\geq 2
, Proposition
6
gives
{d\choose k}n(n-d)^{2^{k}-1}r_{k}(r_{k}-1)

k
-dimensional non-degenerate cuboctahedra.
∎

Corollary 1
.

For
d\geq 3
, every
d
-dimensional latin hypercube
Q
of order
n
contains at least

\left(\frac{d(d-1)}{2}+d+1\right)n^{2d}+o(n^{2d})

cuboctahedra as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof.

In Theorem
5
we have
r_{2}=\lfloor\frac{n^{d-1}}{n-1}\rfloor
, therefore the corresponding summand gives asymptotically
\frac{d(d-1)}{2}n^{2d}
non-degenerate
2
-dimensional cuboctahedra.
∎

Ground-state blowing-up solutions for a Hardy-Sobolev equations on a manifold
Hussein Cheikh Ali, Frédéric Robert

Abstract
. We prove the existence of blowing-up families of solutions to an equation of Hardy-Sobolev type in high dimensions. These families are of minimal type. The sole condition is that the potential of the linear operator touches a critical potential at the singular point. This condition is sharp as shown by the first author in [ HCA:pjm ] .



1.
Introduction

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
without boundary,
x_{0}\in M
,
0<s<2
and
h\in C^{0}(M)
. We consider solutions
u\in H_{1}^{2}(M)\cap C^{0}(M)
to the Hardy-Sobolev equation

(1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{g}u+hu=\frac{u^{2^{\star}(s)-1}}{d_{g}(x_{0},x)^{s}}&\hbox{ in }M\\
u>0&\hbox{ in }M,\end{array}\right.

where
\Delta_{g}:=-\hbox{div}_{g}(\nabla)
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator with minus sign convention and
2^{\star}(s):=\dfrac{2(n-s)}{n-2}
. The space
H_{1}^{2}(M)
be the completion of
C^{\infty}(M)
for the norm
\|\cdot\|_{H_{1}^{2}}:=\|\nabla\cdot\|_{2}+\|\cdot\|_{2}
. The weak formulation of (
1
) makes sense due to the critical Sobolev embedding
H^{2}_{1}(M)\hookrightarrow L^{2^{\star}(s)}(M,d_{g}(x_{0},\cdot)^{-s})
, see Jaber
[
JaberNATM
,
jaber:jmaa
]
. Our first result is the existence of families of solutions to Hardy-Sobolev equations that blow-up along a standard bubble defined in (
3
):

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 7
. Let
h_{0}\in C^{p}(M)
,
p\geq 2
, be such that
\Delta_{g}+h_{0}
is coercive and assume that

(2)

h_{0}(x_{0})=c_{n,s}\operatorname{Scal}_{g}(x_{0}),\hbox{ where }c_{n,s}:=\frac{(n-2)(6-s)}{12(2n-2-s)},

where
\operatorname{Scal}_{g}
is the scalar curvature of
(M,g)
. Then there exists
(h_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\in C^{p}(M)
such that
\lim_{k\to+\infty}h_{k}=h_{0}
in
C^{p}(M)
and there exists
(u_{k})_{k}\in H_{1}^{2}(M)\cap C^{0}(M)
such that

\Delta_{g}u_{k}+h_{k}u_{k}=\frac{u_{k}^{2^{\star}(s)-1}}{d_{g}(x,x_{0})^{s}}\,,\,u_{k}>0\hbox{ in }M

and
u_{k}=Bubble+o(1)
in
H_{1}^{2}(M)
as
k\to+\infty
.

The condition on the scalar curvature is optimal. Indeed, the first author has proved in
[
HCA:pjm
]
that if a family blows-up with the profile of a bubble with
n\geq 7
, then
h_{0}(x_{0})=c_{n,s}\operatorname{Scal}_{g}(x_{0})
. The statement is more precise when a specific geometric quantity does not vanish:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 7
. Let
h_{0}\in C^{p}(M)
,
p\geq 2
, be such that
\Delta_{g}+h_{0}
is coercive and

h_{0}(x_{0})=c_{n,s}\operatorname{Scal}_{g}(x_{0}).

Assume that
L_{g}(h_{0},x_{0})\neq 0
where
L_{g}(h_{0},x_{0})
is defined in (
8
). Let
f\in C^{p}(M)
be such that
f(x_{0})L_{g}(h_{0},x_{0})<0
.
Then there exists
(u_{\epsilon})_{\epsilon>0}\in H_{1}^{2}(M)\cap C^{0}(M)
such that

\Delta_{g}u_{\epsilon}+(h_{0}+\epsilon f)u_{\epsilon}=\frac{u_{\epsilon}^{2^{\star}(s)-1}}{d_{g}(x,x_{0})^{s}}\,,\,u_{\epsilon}>0\hbox{ in }M

and
u_{\epsilon}=Bubble+o(1)
in
H_{1}^{2}(M)
as
\epsilon\to 0
.

The potential
c_{n,s}\operatorname{Scal}_{g}(x_{0})
first appeared in Jaber
[
JaberNATM
]
where it is proved that the condition
h(x_{0})<c_{n,s}\operatorname{Scal}_{g}(x_{0})
permits to obtain minimizing solutions to (
1
). Chen
[
chen
]
proved that when
h(x_{0})>c_{n,s}\operatorname{Scal}_{g}(x_{0})
, there are families
(u_{\epsilon})_{\epsilon}
of solutions to (
1
) where the exponent
2^{\star}(s)-1
is replaced by
2^{\star}(s)-1-\epsilon
and where
u_{\epsilon}
blows-up with a bubble profile as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
. Regarding blow-up with several bubbles on a manifold, we refer to CheikhAli-Mazumdar
[
HCA:SM
]
.

In the context of a bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there has been an abundance of studies of Hardy-Sobolev equations. For equations like (
1
) with the singularity interior to the domain, we refer to Ruiz-Willem
[
RW
]
and the recent Ghoussoub-Mazumdar-Robert
[
gmr:jde
]
for high energies. It is also possible to add a Hardy potential in the linear operator, which has been studied in Esposito-Ghoussoub-Pistoia-Vaira
[
egpv
]
and Ghoussoub-Mazumdar-Robert
[
gmr:jde
]
.

In order to prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, we use the finite-dimensional reduction. We follow the method developed in Robert-Vétois
[
RV:imrn
]
for the case
s=0
, see also Micheletti-Pistoia-Vétois
[
MPV
]
. For Hardy-Sobolev problems, the linearized problem is one dimensional, which permits to ignore the gradient of the potential. However, the singularity
d_{g}(\cdot,x_{0})^{-s}
breaks the conformal invariance of the equation on a manifold: this phenomenon does not happen on a domain of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
since the curvature vanishes. This is why, unlike in the case
s=0
, the classical Yamabe potential
\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}\operatorname{Scal}_{g}
is not relevant in our context. A consequence is that the remainder that comes from the finite-dimensional reduction is not neglictible in the energy expansion: we overcome this essential difficulty by performing a delicate analysis of the remainder. A similar phenomenon has been observed in Esposito-Pistoia
[
EP
]
and Robert-Vétois
[
RV:calcvar
]
.

The problem we consider is naturally stated in dimension
n\geq 3
, and, according to CheikhAli
[
HCA:pjm
]
, the equality
h_{0}(x_{0})=c_{n,s}\operatorname{Scal}_{g}(x_{0})
is expected to be the blow-up condition for
n\geq 4
. However, the dimensions
n=4,5,6
carry some additional complexity. First, for
n=6
, the first term in the expression of
L_{g}(h_{0},x_{0})
can be expressed in term of a log, but the final nonlocal term is implicit and not known, as in Esposito-Pistoia
[
EP
]
. For
n=4,5
, one expects to introduce a notion of ”mass” as in Robert-Vétois
[
RV:imrn
]
, but, as mentioned above, we cannot exploit the conformal invariance when
s>0
.

In all this paper,
C(a,b,...)
will denote any constant depending only on the parameters
a,b,...
. The value might change from one line to the other.

2.
Definition of a bubble and
L_{g}(h_{0},x_{0})

We fix
0<r_{0}<i_{g}(M)/3
where
i_{g}(M)>0
is the injectivity radius of
(M,g)
. Let us choose a cutoff function
\chi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\chi_{|(-\infty,r_{0}]}\equiv 1
and
\chi_{|[2r_{0},+\infty)}\equiv 0
. For any
\delta>0
, we define a
bubble
as

(3)

\displaystyle U_{\delta,x_{0}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa_{n,s}\,\chi(d_{g}(x_{0},x))\frac{\delta^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}{\left(\delta^{2-s}+d_{g}(x_{0},x)^{2-s}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2-s}}}

for all
x\in M
, where
\kappa_{n,s}:=\left((n-s)(n-2)\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2(2-s)}}
. In particular, we have that

U_{\delta,x_{0}}(x):=\chi(d_{g}(x_{0},x))U_{\delta}\left(\exp_{x_{0}}^{-1}(x)\right)\hbox{ for all }x\in M

where

(4)

U_{1}(X):=\kappa_{n,s}\left(\frac{1}{1+|X|^{2-s}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2-s}}\hbox{ and }U_{\delta}(X):=\delta^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}U_{1}\left(\delta^{-1}X\right)

for all
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. It follows from Chou-Chu
[
Chouchu
]
that the
U_{\delta}
’s,
\delta>0
, are the only nontrivial solutions
U\in D_{1}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
\mathbb{\|}\nabla\cdot\mathbb{\|}_{2}
) to

(5)

\Delta_{\xi}U=\frac{U^{2^{\star}(s)-1}}{|X|^{s}},U\geq 0\hbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
\xi
is the Euclidean metric. Since
\Delta_{\xi}U_{\delta}=\frac{U_{\delta}^{2^{\star}(s)-1}}{|X|^{s}}
for all
\delta>0
, differentiating with respect to
\delta
yields

(6)

\Delta_{\xi}Z_{0}=\left(2^{\star}(s)-1\right)\frac{U_{1}^{2^{\star}(s)-2}}{|X|^{s}}Z_{0}\hbox{ in }D_{1}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hbox{ where }Z_{0}:=\left(\partial_{\delta}U_{\delta}\right)_{|\delta=1}

It follows from Robert
[
RANA
]
(see also Dancer-Gladiali-Grossi
[
DGG
]
) that

(7)

K_{0}:=\left\{\varphi\in D_{1}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\Delta_{\xi}\varphi=\left(2^{\star}(s)-1\right)\frac{U_{1}^{2^{\star}(s)-2}}{|X|^{s}}\varphi\,\hbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\right\}=\operatorname{Span}\{Z_{0}\}.

We define

(8)

\displaystyle L_{g}(h_{0},x_{0})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4n}\left(\Delta_{g}(h_{0}-\Lambda_{n,s}\operatorname{Scal}_{g})(x_{0})-\mathcal{K}_{g}^{n,s}(x_{0})\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|X|^{4}|\nabla U_{1}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}W_{s,g,x_{0}}\hat{C}(W_{s,g,x_{0}})\,dX

with

(9)

\mathcal{K}_{g}^{n,s}(x_{0}):=\frac{\Lambda_{n,s}}{18}\Big(8|Ric_{g}(x_{0})|^{2}_{g}-3|Rm_{g}(x_{0})|^{2}_{g}-\frac{5(2-s)}{10-s}\operatorname{Scal}_{g}(x_{0})^{2}\Big)

where
\Lambda_{n,s}:=\frac{(n-2)(10-s)}{20(2n-2-s)}
and
Ric_{g}
and
Rm_{g}
are the Ricci and Riemann tensors. Note that for
s>0
,
\Lambda_{n,s}\neq c_{n,s}
, which is a difference with the nonsingular case
s=0
. The function
W_{s,g,x_{0}}
is defined as

W_{s,g,x_{0}}:=c_{n,s}\operatorname{Scal}_{g}(x_{0})U_{1}+\frac{1}{3}R_{ij}(x_{0})\sigma^{i}\sigma^{j}(r\partial_{r}U_{1})\in L^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hbox{ for }n\geq 7

where
R_{ij}
are the coordinates of
Ric_{g}
in the exponential map at
x_{0}
, and
(r,\sigma)
are polar coordinates. Finally,
\hat{C}=\hat{C}(W_{s,g,x_{0}})\in D_{1}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the unique solution to

\Delta_{\xi}\hat{C}-(2^{\star}(s)-1)\frac{U_{1}^{2^{\star}(s)-2}}{|X|^{s}}\hat{C}=-W_{s,g,x_{0}}+\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}W_{s,g,x_{0}}Z_{0}\,dx}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla Z_{0}|^{2}\,dx}\Delta_{\xi}Z_{0}\,;\,\hat{C}\in K_{0}^{\perp}.

3.
Preliminary notations

We fix
h_{0}\in C^{2}(M)
such that
\Delta_{g}+h_{0}
is coercive, that is the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(\Delta_{g}+h_{0})
is positive. Given
h\in L^{\infty}(M)
, the space
H_{1}^{2}(M)
is endowed with the bilinear form

(10)

\langle u,v\rangle_{h}:=\int_{M}\Big((\nabla u,\nabla v)_{g}+huv\Big)\,dv_{g}\hbox{ for all }u,v\in H_{1}^{2}(M).

We let
\epsilon>0
be such that for
h\in L^{\infty}(M)
such that
\mathbb{\|}h-h_{0}\mathbb{\|}_{\infty}<\epsilon
. The coercivity of
\Delta_{g}+h_{0}
yields the coercivity of that the operator
\Delta_{g}+h
for
\epsilon<<1
, with a coercivity constant depending only on
h_{0}
and
\epsilon<<1
. Therefore,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{h}
is positive definite and
\left(H_{1}^{2},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{h}\right)
is a Hilbert space. We let
\mathbb{\|}\cdot\mathbb{\|}_{h}
be the norm induced by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{h}
: this norm is equivalent to the standard norm
\|\cdot\|_{H_{1}^{2}}
. Solutions to (
1
) are critical points of the functional

J_{h}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\Big(|\nabla u|_{g}^{2}+hu^{2}\Big)\,dv_{g}-F(u)\hbox{ for all }u\in H_{1}^{2}(M),

where
dv_{g}
is the Riemannian element of volume and

F(u):=\frac{1}{2^{\star}(s)}\int_{M}\frac{u_{+}^{2^{\star}(s)}}{d_{g}(x_{0},x)^{s}}\,dv_{g}\hbox{ for all }u\in H_{1}^{2}(M),

with
u_{+}:=\max(u,0)
. Indeed,
F,J_{h}\in C^{1}(H_{1}^{2}(M))
and

J_{h}^{\prime}(u)[\phi]=\int_{M}\left((\nabla u,\nabla\phi)_{g}+hu\phi\right)\,dv_{g}-\int_{M}\frac{u_{+}^{2^{\star}(s)-1}\phi}{d_{g}(x_{0},x)^{s}}\,dv_{g}\hbox{ for all }u,\phi\in H_{1}^{2}(M).

We define the operator
(\Delta_{g}+h)^{-1}:\left(H_{1}^{2}(M)\right)^{\prime}\to H_{1}^{2}(M)
. In particular, for any
w\in L^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(M)=(L^{2^{\star}}(M))^{\prime}\subset(H_{1}^{2}(M))^{\prime}
where
2^{\star}:=\frac{2n}{n-2}
, the function
u=(\Delta_{g}+h)^{-1}w\in H_{1}^{2}(M)
is the unique solution of
\left(\Delta_{g}+h\right)u=w
in
M
. It follows from the continuity of the Sobolev embedding
H_{1}^{2}(M)\hookrightarrow L^{2^{\star}}(M)
, we get for
\epsilon<<1
that

\mathbb{\|}(\Delta_{g}+h)^{-1}w\mathbb{\|}_{H_{1}^{2}}\leq C(h_{0},\epsilon)\,\mathbb{\|}w\mathbb{\|}_{\frac{2n}{n+2}},

where
C(h_{0},\epsilon)>0
depends only on
(M,g)
,
h_{0}\in L^{\infty}(M)
, and
0<\epsilon<<1
. As one checks, differentiating the bubble
U_{\delta,x_{0}}
defined in (
3
), we get

(11)

\partial_{\delta}\left(U_{\delta,x_{0}}(x)\right)=\frac{n-2}{2}\kappa_{n,s}\chi(d_{g}(x_{0},x))\delta^{\frac{n-4}{2}}\frac{d_{g}(x_{0},x)^{2-s}-\delta^{2-s}}{\left(\delta^{2-s}+d_{g}(x_{0},x)^{2-s}\right)^{\frac{n-s}{2-s}}}.

For
\delta\in(0,+\infty)
, we define

K_{\delta}:=\operatorname{Span}\left\{Z_{\delta}\right\},

where

(12)

\displaystyle Z_{\delta}(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\chi(d_{g}(x_{0},x))\delta^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}Z_{0}(\delta^{-1}\exp_{x_{0}}^{-1}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n-2}{2}\kappa_{n,s}\chi(d_{g}(x_{0},x))\delta^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\frac{d_{g}(x_{0},x)^{2-s}-\delta^{2-s}}{\left(\delta^{2-s}+d_{g}(x_{0},x)^{2-s}\right)^{\frac{n-s}{2-s}}}\hbox{ for all }x\in M.

Let
K_{\delta}^{\perp}
as the orthogonal of
K_{\delta}
in
H_{1}^{2}(M)
, defined as

K_{\delta}^{\perp}=\{\phi\in H_{1}^{2}(M)\,;\langle\phi,Z_{\delta}\rangle_{h}=0\}

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{h}
is defined in (
10
). We let
\Pi_{K_{\delta}}:H_{1}^{2}(M)\mapsto H_{1}^{2}(M)
and
\Pi_{K_{\delta}^{\perp}}:H_{1}^{2}(M)\mapsto H_{1}^{2}(M)
be the orthogonal projection onto respectively
K_{\delta}
and
K_{\delta}^{\perp}
with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{h}
.

4.
The finite dimensional reduction

The goal of this section is to prove the following:

Theorem 4.1
.

We fix
h_{0}\in L^{\infty}(M)
such that
\Delta_{g}+h_{0}
is coercive. Then there exists
\epsilon>0
such that for any
h\in L^{\infty}(M)
satisfying
\mathbb{\|}h-h_{0}\mathbb{\|}_{\infty}<\epsilon
, there exists
\delta\mapsto\phi_{\delta}\in C^{1}\left((0,\epsilon),H_{1}^{2}(M)\right)
such that
u_{\delta}:=U_{\delta,x_{0}}+\phi_{\delta}
is a critical point of
J_{h}
if and only if
\delta\in(0,\epsilon)
is a critical point of
\delta\mapsto J_{h}(u_{\delta})
in
(0,\epsilon)
. Moreover, we have that
\mathbb{\|}\phi_{\delta}\mathbb{\|}_{H_{1}^{2}}\leq C\mathbb{\|}R_{\delta}\mathbb{\|}_{\frac{2n}{n+2}}
, where
C
is a constant depending on
(M,g)
,
h_{0}
and
\epsilon
. Here,
R_{\delta}
is defined as:

(13)

R_{\delta}:=\left(\Delta_{g}+h\right)U_{\delta,x_{0}}-\frac{U_{\delta,x_{0}}^{2^{\star}(s)-1}}{d_{g}(x_{0},x)^{s}}.

where the peak
U_{\delta,x_{0}}
is given by (
3
).

The proof is classical, and we only stress on the necessary changes due to our context. We prove the Theorem
4.1
by constructing a solutions
u\in H_{1}^{2}(M)
to equation (
1
), or equivalently to
u-(\Delta_{g}+h)^{-1}\left(F^{\,\prime}(u)\right)=0
. This is equivalent to find
u\in H_{1}^{2}(M)
such that

\displaystyle\Pi_{K_{\delta}}\left(u-(\Delta_{g}+h)^{-1}\left(F^{\,\prime}(u)\right)\right)=0,

(14)

\displaystyle\Pi_{K_{\delta}^{\perp}}\left(u-(\Delta_{g}+h)^{-1}\left(F^{\,\prime}(u)\right)\right)=0.

We first prove that for
0<\delta<<1
, small enough, there exists a unique
\phi_{\delta}\in K_{\delta}^{\perp}\cap H_{1}^{2}(M)
small enough such that
u_{\delta}=U_{\delta,x_{0}}+\phi_{\delta}
solves (
14
). For any
\delta>0
, we introduce the map
L_{\delta,h}:K_{\delta}^{\perp}\to K_{\delta}^{\perp}
defined by

(15)

L_{\delta,h}(\phi):=\Pi_{K_{\delta}^{\perp}}\left(\phi-(\Delta_{g}+h)^{-1}(F^{\,\prime\prime}(U_{\delta,x_{0}})\phi)\right)\hbox{ for all }\phi\in H_{1}^{2}(M).

We will begin this section by proving the invertibility of
L_{\delta,h}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

We fix
h_{0}\in L^{\infty}(M)
such that
\Delta_{g}+h_{0}
is coercive. Then, there exists
\epsilon>0
such that for any
h\in L^{\infty}(M)
satisfying
\mathbb{\|}h-h_{0}\mathbb{\|}_{\infty}<\epsilon
,
there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
x_{0}\in M
and any
\delta\in(0,\epsilon)
, we have that

(16)

\mathbb{\|}L_{\delta,h}(\phi)\mathbb{\|}_{H_{1}^{2}}\geq C\,\mathbb{\|}\phi\mathbb{\|}_{H_{1}^{2}}\hbox{ for all }\phi\in H_{1}^{2}(M).

In particular,
L_{\delta,h}
is a bi-continous isomorphism.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. We assume that there exist a sequence
(\delta_{\alpha})_{\alpha}>0
and a sequence
(h_{\alpha})_{\alpha}\in L^{\infty}(M)
such that
\delta_{\alpha}\to 0
,
\|h_{\alpha}-h_{0}\|_{\infty}\to 0
as
\alpha\to+\infty
and a sequence of functions
(\phi_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}\in H_{1}^{2}(M)
satisfying

(17)

\mathbb{\|}\phi_{\alpha}\mathbb{\|}_{H_{1}^{2}}=1,\quad\phi_{\alpha}\in K_{\delta_{\alpha}}^{\perp}\hbox{ and }\mathbb{\|}L_{\alpha}(\phi_{\alpha})\mathbb{\|}_{H_{1}^{2}}\to 0\hbox{ as }\alpha\to+\infty,

where
L_{\alpha}:=L_{\delta_{\alpha},h_{\alpha}}
. For any
\alpha
, we set
\bar{\chi}_{\alpha}(X):=\bar{\chi}\left(\delta_{\alpha}|X|\right)
where
\bar{\chi}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
is such that
\bar{\chi}_{|(-\infty,2r_{0}]}\equiv 1
and

\widetilde{\phi}_{\alpha}(X):=\bar{\chi}_{\alpha}(X)\delta_{\alpha}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\phi_{\alpha}\circ\exp_{x_{0}}(\delta_{\alpha}X)\hbox{ for all }X\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

By (
17
) and with change of variable, we get that the sequence
(\widetilde{\phi}_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Passing if necessary to a subsequence, we may assume that
\widetilde{\phi}_{\alpha}\rightharpoonup\widetilde{\phi}
weakly in
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Set
Z_{\alpha}:=Z_{\delta_{\alpha}}
when
Z_{\delta_{\alpha}}
is defined in (
12
). Since for any
\alpha
, the function
\phi_{\alpha}
belongs to
K_{\delta_{\alpha}}^{\perp}
, and using a change of variables, we obtain

(18)

\displaystyle 0=\langle Z_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha}\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}(\nabla Z_{\alpha},\nabla\phi_{\alpha})_{g}\,dv_{g}+\int_{M}h_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}\phi_{\alpha}\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\nabla(\chi_{\alpha}Z_{0}),\nabla\widetilde{\phi}_{\alpha})_{g_{\alpha}}\,dv_{g_{\alpha}}+\delta_{\alpha}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tilde{h}_{\alpha}\chi_{\alpha}Z_{0}\widetilde{\phi}_{\alpha}\,dv_{g_{\alpha}},

where
g_{\alpha}:=\exp_{x_{0}}^{\star}g(\delta_{\alpha}X)
,
\tilde{h}_{\alpha}(X):=h_{\alpha}(\exp_{x_{0}}(\delta_{\alpha}X))
for all
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
Z_{0}
as in (
6
) and
\chi_{\alpha}(X):=\chi(\delta_{\alpha}|X|)
for all
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For
r_{0}>0
, by Hölder’s inequality and since
\|h_{\alpha}-h_{0}\|_{\infty}\to 0
, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tilde{h}_{\alpha}\chi_{\alpha}Z_{0}\widetilde{\phi}_{\alpha}\,dv_{g_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O\left(\int_{B\left(0,3r_{0}\delta_{\alpha}^{-1}\right)}|Z_{0}\widetilde{\phi}_{\alpha}|\,dv_{g_{\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O\left(\left(\int_{B\left(0,3r_{0}\delta_{\alpha}^{-1}\right)}|Z_{0}|^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}\,dX\right)^{\frac{n+2}{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\widetilde{\phi}_{\alpha}|^{2^{\star}}\,dX\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{\star}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O\left(\mathbb{\|}\widetilde{\phi}_{\alpha}\mathbb{\|}_{D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left(\int_{B\left(0,3r_{0}\delta_{\alpha}^{-1}\right)}|Z_{0}|^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}\,dX\right)^{\frac{n+2}{n}}\right),

where the last assertion holds by the continuity of the embedding of
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into
L^{2^{\star}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Since
\widetilde{\phi}_{\alpha}
is bounded in
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, it follows from the explicit expression of
Z_{0}
that

\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}\delta_{\alpha}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tilde{h}_{\alpha}\chi_{\alpha}Z_{0}\widetilde{\phi}_{\alpha}\,dv_{g_{\alpha}}=0.

This last fact,
\widetilde{\phi}_{\alpha}\rightharpoonup\widetilde{\phi}
weakly in
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and passing the limit in (
18
) yield

(19)

\displaystyle 0=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\nabla Z_{0},\nabla\widetilde{\phi})_{\xi}\,dX=(2^{\star}(s)-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{U_{1}^{2^{\star}(s)-2}}{|X|^{s}}Z_{0}\widetilde{\phi}\,dX,

where the last equality holds by (

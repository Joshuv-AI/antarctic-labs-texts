Bootstrapping Gravity with Crossing Symmetric Dispersion Relations
Celina Pasiecznik

Abstract
We derive bounds on Wilson coefficients in gravitational effective field theories using fully crossing symmetric dispersion relations. These sum rules naturally isolate finite subsets of low-energy couplings without relying on the forward limit or specific high-energy completions. We validate our method by matching bounds computed previously for scalar scattering with gravity as well as for supergraviton scattering. For graviton scattering we use crossing symmetric functions that combine various helicity combinations for the maximal-helicity violating amplitude and find stronger bounds than have been previously computed. We also derive new bounds on the coupling of gravitons to a massive spin-4 state at tree level. These results demonstrate the power of crossing symmetric sum rules as a tool in the S-matrix bootstrap.

Introduction

Effective field theories (EFTs) describe a physical theory at a chosen energy scale without precise knowledge of the physics at very short distances.
EFTs parametrize low-energy physics via the use of local higher-dimensional operators, each characterized by a Wilson coefficient whose value depends on the precise ultraviolet (UV) completion.
The S-matrix bootstrap program for gravitational EFTs uses fundamental principles, namely locality, unitarity, and crossing symmetry, to constrain these Wilson coefficients.
In practice, the dual approach which makes use of positivity and dispersion relations is able to rule out values of Wilson coefficients of UV theories that do not obey these principles

Setup

In this section, we state our assumptions and provide the setup for bounding couplings of EFTs that include gravitational interactions.
We are interested in bounding couplings at tree level in
2\rightarrow 2
scattering of scalars or gravitons.
We will use real massless scalar scattering as the simplest example to illustrate our setup.

2.1
Assumptions

Our objective is to bound ratios of couplings without assuming a particular high-energy theory, relying only on the assumption that a causal and unitary high-energy completion exists.
For
2\rightarrow 2
particle scattering, using the all incoming convention, the Mandelstam invariants are

s=-\left(p_{1}+p_{2}\right)^{2},\quad t=-\left(p_{2}+p_{3}\right)^{2},\quad u=-\left(p_{1}+p_{3}\right)^{2}

(1)

which, for massless particles, satisfy
s+t+u=0
.
We assume the EFT is weakly coupled and thus work at tree level, ignoring any low-energy loop effects. This means the low-energy amplitude only contains terms corresponding to the explicit exchange of particles as well as terms from higher-dimension operators.
For example, for massless scalar scattering where the scalar is coupled to gravity, the low-energy amplitude is

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\text{low}}(s,u)=

\displaystyle 8\pi G\left(\frac{st}{u}+\frac{su}{t}+\frac{tu}{s}\right)-g^{2}\left(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}+\frac{1}{u}\right)

(2)

\displaystyle-g_{0}+g_{2}\left(s^{2}+t^{2}+u^{2}\right)+g_{3}(stu)+g_{4}\left(s^{2}+t^{2}+u^{2}\right)^{2}+\ldots\,.

In the physical region where
s>0
and
-s<u<0
, the center-of-mass energy squared is
s
, the squared momentum transfer is
-u
, and the scattering angle is

\cos\theta=1+\frac{2u}{s}\,.

(3)

At high energies, we use a partial wave decomposition of the amplitude. For scalar scattering we use
sharpboundaries

\mathcal{M}(s,u)=s^{\frac{4-D}{2}}\sum_{J}n_{J}^{(D)}c_{J}(s)\hskip 2.0pt\mathcal{P}_{J}\!\left(1+\frac{2u}{s}\right)

(4)

where
D
is the spacetime dimension,
n_{J}
is a normalization factor for the partial waves
\mathcal{P}_{J}
, and
J
is the spin of the exchanged particle. When the external states are gravitons rather than scalars, we use Wigner-d functions given in sec.
5.1

The normalization factor ensures unitarity of the S-matrix and is defined as
Correia:2020xtr

n_{J}^{(D)}\equiv\frac{2^{D}\pi^{\frac{D-2}{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{D-2}{2}\right)}(J+1)_{D-4}(2J+D-3)\,.

(5)

For massless scalar scattering the partial waves are Gegenbauer polynomials:

\mathcal{P}_{J}(x)\equiv{}_{2}F_{1}\left(-J,J+D-3,\frac{D-2}{2},\frac{1-x}{2}\right)\,.

(6)

The spectral density for scalar scattering is defined as
\rho_{J}(s)=\operatorname{Im}c_{J}(s)
, which implies the imaginary part of the amplitude can be expressed as

\operatorname{Im}\mathcal{M}(s,u)=s^{\frac{4-D}{2}}\sum_{J\text{ even }}n_{J}^{(D)}\rho_{J}(s)\mathcal{P}_{J}\!\left(1+\frac{2u}{s}\right)\,.

(7)

Unitarity implies that for all positive energies
s
and even spin
J
, we have

0\leq\rho_{J}(s)\leq 2

(8)

which provides us with crucial positivity constraints for the high-energy side of the theory.

In gravitational theories we do
not
assume a Froissart-Martin-like bound.

2.2

We use crossing symmetric dispersion relations from

(20)

\langle(\cdots)\rangle\equiv\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{J}n_{J}^{(D)}\int_{M^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{dm^{2}}{m^{2}}\rho_{J}\left(m^{2}\right)(\cdots)\,.

\mathcal{C}_{k}(p^{2})\equiv\underset{z=0}{\text{Res}}\left[\mathcal{K}_{k}(z,p^{2})\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{low}}\right]=\left\langle\mathcal{K}_{k}(m^{2},p^{2})\hskip 2.0pt\pi_{J}\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{m^{2}-3p^{2}}}{\sqrt{m^{2}+p^{2}}}\right)\right\rangle\,

(22)

where

are generalized partial waves.

Considering scalar scattering with gravity, the crossing symmetric sum rules give

\displaystyle k=2:

\displaystyle\frac{8\pi G}{p^{2}}+2g_{2}+p^{2}g_{3}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{2m^{2}+3p^{2}}{m^{6}}\mathcal{P}_{J}\left(\frac{\sqrt{m^{2}-3p^{2}}}{\sqrt{m^{2}+p^{2}}}\right)\right\rangle

(23)

\displaystyle k=4:

\displaystyle 4g_{4}+2p^{2}g_{5}+p^{4}g_{6}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{(m^{2}+p^{2})(2m^{2}+3p^{2})}{m^{12}}\mathcal{P}_{J}\left(\frac{\sqrt{m^{2}-3p^{2}}}{\sqrt{m^{2}+p^{2}}}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle k=6:

\displaystyle 8g_{6}+4p^{2}g_{7}+2p^{4}g_{8}^{\prime}+p^{6}g_{9}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{(m^{2}+p^{2})^{2}(2m^{2}+3p^{2})}{m^{18}}\mathcal{P}_{J}\left(\frac{\sqrt{m^{2}-3p^{2}}}{\sqrt{m^{2}+p^{2}}}\right)\right\rangle

For the crossing symmetric sum rules, we smear the functionals against wave-packets in (

Null constraints are typically derived from crossing symmetry, however, for the crossing symmetric dispersion relations they appear naturally as the higher-

After smearing the dispersive sum rules, we use semi-definite programming, SDPB

\displaystyle\text{maximise}

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\int_{0}^{M}\!dp\,f_{i}(p)\,B_{i}(p^{2})

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\int_{0}^{M}\!dp\,f_{i}(p)\,{B}_{i}\!\bigl[p^{2},m^{2},J,\lambda\bigr]\;\succeq\;0\quad\forall\,m>M,\;\rho=[J,\lambda].

The label

enumerates the independent dispersive sum rules. Intermediate states are organised by little-group representations

\rho=[J,\lambda]

\lambda=(m_{2},\dots,m_{n})

.

The numerical framework we use to compute bounds on Wilson coefficients closely follows

;
Caron_Huot_20232

and each

and sample more sparsely up to

sharpboundaries
to include constraints in the

limit at fixed impact parameter

by discretizing in

), where

6
Discussion

We derived bounds that match the literature for both scalar scattering with gravity and graviton scattering in supergravity theory.
Notably, by smearing up to
p_{\text{max}}=1
instead of only to
p_{\text{max}}=1/\sqrt{3}
as done in
Chang:2025cxc
;
Beadle:2025cdx
, we are able to exactly reproduce bounds in scalar scattering with gravity.
For
2\rightarrow 2
graviton scattering in non-supersymmetric theories, we used crossing symmetric combinations of the MHV amplitude to study tree-level graviton scattering. We adapted crossing symmetric sum rules used for pion scattering to graviton scattering that ensured the correct Regge behavior occurred at each
k-
subtraction.
The crossing symmetric approach offers a more systematic alternative to the improved sum rules used in
sharpboundaries
;
Caron_Huot_20232
.
We found that the crossing symmetric dispersion relations offer a natural way to write sum rules since they isolate a finite subset of Wilson coefficients, and since by construction they provide null constraints that are normally derived from crossing.
Using these new sum rules, we computed stronger bounds than previously found in
Caron_Huot_20232
.

Inspired by the spectrum of higher-spin heavy states in QCD, we studied the explicit exchange of a heavy higher-spin state of mass
m_{J}
at tree level in gravity by pushing the cut-off
M>m_{J}
, effectively integrating in the higher-spin state into the IR.
We computed bounds for the coupling of the massive effective scalar state to gravitons in supergravity theory which matched bounds found in
Albert:2024yap
.
We then used the same approach to study the upper bound for the coupling of a heavy spin-4 state exchanged in
2\rightarrow 2
graviton scattering. Considering various possible masses for the spin-4 state, we found a larger bound exists when we also integrate in heavy spin-0 and spin-2 states for a non-zero mass gap.
We allowed for numerous spin-0 and spin-2 states with masses
0<m^{2}<M^{2}
where
M
is the UV cutoff, expected to be the mass of the next higher-spin state, which here is the spin-6 state.
By allowing for numerous massive spin-0 and spin-2 states, our bounds do not depend on a specific spectral assumption. However, it would be interesting to compare our bounds to those predicted by string theory amplitudes by assuming spin-0 and spin-2 masses from the string Regge trajectory.

In this paper, we showed how crossing symmetric dispersion relations provide a useful tool to calculate bounds at tree level.
Although we worked at tree level, the sum rules are extremely useful for loop-level calculations where, with or without gravity, the presence of any light particle in EFT loops would give singularities in the forward limit.
Crossing symmetric dispersion relations offer a way to constrain EFT couplings at loop level and thus provide a powerful tool for studying positivity bounds.

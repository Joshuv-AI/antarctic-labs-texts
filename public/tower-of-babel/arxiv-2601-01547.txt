EscherVerse: An Open World Benchmark and Dataset for Teleo-Spatial Intelligence with Physical-Dynamic and Intent-Driven Understanding
Tianjun Gu, Chenghua Gong, Jingyu Gong, Zhizhong Zhang, Yuan Xie, Lizhuang Ma, Xin Tan, [6pt] East China Normal University Shanghai AI Lab Shanghai Innovation Institute

Abstract
The ability to reason about spatial dynamics is a cornerstone of intelligence, yet current research overlooks the human intent behind spatial changes. To address these limitations, we introduce Teleo-Spatial Intelligence (TSI), a new paradigm that unifies two critical pillars: Physical-Dynamic Reasoning—understanding the physical principles of object interactions—and Intent-Driven Reasoning—inferring the human goals behind these actions. To catalyze research in TSI, we present EscherVerse, consisting of a large-scale, open-world benchmark (Escher-Bench) dataset (Escher-35k) and model(Escher series). Derived from real-world videos, EscherVerse moves beyond constrained settings to explicitly evaluate an agent’s ability to reason about object permanence, state transitions, and trajectory prediction in dynamic, human-centric scenarios. Crucially, it is the first benchmark to systematically assess Intent-Driven Reasoning, challenging models to connect physical events to their underlying human purposes. Our work, including a novel data curation pipeline, provides a foundational resource to advance spatial intelligence from passive scene description towards a holistic, purpose-driven understanding of the world.

Introduction

The ability to perceive and reason about the spatial dynamics of the world is a key component of spatial intelligence
16
. However, current spatial intelligence struggles to enable embodied agents to operate efficiently in human environments, a deeper understanding of spatial intelligence is needed, which we conceptualise as Teleo-Spatial Intelligence (TSI). TSI transcends static scene description by integrating two key pillars of reasoning: Physical-Dynamic Reasoning, the ability to understand how objects move, interact, and change according to physical principles, and Intent-Driven Reasoning, the ability to infer the human goals and purposes behind these spatial changes.

Despite significant progress in spatial understanding, current research has three key limitations that hinder the exploration of TSI. First, existing benchmarks are confined to constrained environments. This includes purely simulated worlds, such as Habitat
21
;
22
, ProcTHOR
11
, which offer control but lack real-world complexity, as well as datasets
28
;
12
;
13
;
18
;
4
;
9
derived from static 3D scans of real indoor spaces, including ScanNet
10
and ARKitScenes
7
. While both of these approaches are valuable, they are limited to curated, often static, indoor settings, creating a significant domain gap and failing to capture the open-world complexity essential for both physical and intentional reasoning. Second, the research of spatial intelligence focus has largely remained on static scene comprehension. This overlooks the crucial element of dynamic reasoning, which is the very foundation of Physical-Dynamic Reasoning. Third, and most importantly, existing approaches treat scenes as sterile, object-centric arrangements, completely neglecting Intent-Driven Reasoning. They can determine that a chair was moved, but not why or what that implies for future events, thus failing to bridge the gap towards genuine TSI.

To bridge these gaps and catalyze research towards a holistic evaluation of Teleo-Spatial Intelligence, we introduce EscherVerse, a new, large-scale, open-world benchmark (Escher-Bench) and dataset (Escher-35k) designed to propel spatial reasoning from static, simulated worlds to the dynamic, human-centric open world. Our work is founded on three core principles designed to address the pillars of TSI comprehensively:
(1) From Simulation to Reality: establishing an open-world foundation for TSI. We move beyond constrained virtual indoor scenes by sourcing data from real-world videos.
(2) From Static to Dynamic: focusing on the core of Physical-Dynamic Reasoning, EscherVerse explicitly targets dynamic events. Our benchmark requires models to reason about object permanence, trajectory prediction, state transitions, and evolving relational changes over time.
(3) From Space to Intent: introducing the critical pillar of Intent-Driven Reasoning. The benchmark challenges models to infer human goals and intentions from physical actions, linking spatial changes to the human potential purpose.

In summary, our main contributions are:

•

Teleo-Spatial Intelligence (TSI): We propose TSI, a new, comprehensive concept for spatial reasoning that unifies physical-dynamic and intent-driven understanding. We argue this is a critical paradigm for the next generation of spatial intelligence.

•

The Escher-Bench and Escher-35k: We introduce the first large-scale, open-world benchmark specifically designed to evaluate both pillars of TSI holistically. We also propose the Escher-35k, comprising 35,963 question-answer pairs, to address the current lack of TSI data.

•

A Novel data curation pipeline for TSI: We introduce a scalable and high-quality pipeline for generating complex reasoning QA data that covers both physical and intentional phenomena, uniquely combining LLM-based filtering, cognitively-inspired question generation, and multi-VLM verification.

Related work

2.1
Vision Language Model

The advent of vision-language models (VLMs)
1
;
23
has revolutionized the field of visual reasoning. These models are architecturally designed to align a vision encoder with a large language model (LLM) backbone, pre-training them on massive-scale, web-sourced image-text pairs. This paradigm endows them with unprecedented capabilities in open-ended visual reasoning, dialogue, and detailed image description. Through subsequent instruction tuning, models like Qwen-VL
5
;
29
have further extended these abilities to encompass basic spatial concepts, such as 2D localization and reasoning about simple occlusions, recasting many vision tasks into a unified generative framework.

However, the very foundation of their success, pre-training on static, independent images, imposes a fundamental limitation when confronted with dynamic scenes. The real world is not merely a collection of snapshots but a continuous stream of events where understanding temporal evolution, causality, and object permanence is paramount. Consequently, these models struggle to track state changes, predict trajectories, or infer the intent behind actions, as these tasks require reasoning beyond a single frame. This weakness is not merely anecdotal. Empirical evidence from benchmarks
31
;
35
demonstrates that even state-of-the-art VLMs are prone to critical failures in dynamic settings, often hallucinating motion by confusing the observer’s movement with that of objects. This reveals a crucial disconnect between static pattern recognition and genuine spat-io-temporal understanding. In essence, their pre-training paradigm equips them for basic spatial description but leaves them unprepared for the two core pillars of Teleo-Spatial Intelligence: robust Physical-Dynamic Reasoning and, even more critically, any form of Intent-Driven Reasoning. This highlights the urgent need for a benchmark that directly challenges these fundamental deficits.

2.2
Spatial Intelligent Model

To address the shortcomings of general-purpose VLMs, a significant research effort has focused on developing specialized models for spatial intelligence. These efforts can be broadly categorized based on which aspect of Teleo-Spatial Intelligence (TSI) they prioritize.

The first category focuses on the geometric and physical aspects of TSI, emphasizing precise Physical-Dynamic Reasoning. Recent advances such as DUSt3R
26
and MASt3R
8
now leverage Transformers to directly predict pixel-aligned 3D point maps, enabling extensions to semantic and dynamic reconstruction
24
. However, these geometry-centric models, despite their precision, often operate at a low level of abstraction. They excel at representing how spatial change but inherently lack the high-level semantic and causal reasoning needed to understand why.

The second, more dominant category attempts to bridge geometry with language, moving towards a more holistic form of TSI. This line of work embeds 3D reasoning directly within the VLM framework. Approaches like Spatial-MLLM
20
, VLM-3R
12
and ViCA2
13
pioneer instruction-aligned 3D reconstruction and reasoning. DORAEMON
15
has constructed an agent framework that mimics human cognition, Cambrian-S
31
introduces spatial supersensing to handle unbounded visual streams.

Despite these significant advancements, critical limitations remain. VLM-based approaches often rely on synthetic data, raising concerns about their ability to address real-world complexities. More fundamentally, while they make strides in combining language with geometry, their primary objective remains focused on structural description or answering queries about static configurations. Most critically, they almost entirely overlook Intent-Driven Reasoning as a primary driver of spatial change. This reveals a significant gap for a benchmark that can holistically evaluate both pillars of TSI in open, real-world, dynamic scenarios.

2.3
Benchmarks for Spatial Intelligence

The landscape of spatial intelligence benchmarks reflects the field’s gradual evolution towards Teleo-Spatial Intelligence, progressing along two primary axes: from static to dynamic scenes, and from geometric description to complex reasoning.

Early benchmarks laid the groundwork by focusing on static spatial description. Simulation environments
21
;
22
and Large-scale datasets like ScanNet
10
and ARKitScenes
7
enabled a rich ecosystem of benchmarks, such as ScanRefer
9
and SQA3D
18
. These were instrumental in advancing language-grounded reasoning about static spatial relationships. However, by they are very nature, they are incapable of evaluating either Physical-Dynamic or Intent-Driven Reasoning, as they lack a temporal dimension.

A new wave of benchmarks has begun to tackle the challenge of Physical-Dynamic Reasoning. VSI-Bench
30
, IR3D-Bench
17
and DSI-Bench
35
introduced video-based tasks to measure the understanding of spatio-temporal relationships and motion. These works were crucial in revealing the weaknesses of current models in processing dynamic information. Concurrently, efforts to increase realism have led to the adoption of open-world video datasets, such as Ego4D
14
. While these benchmarks represent a significant step forward in evaluating the “how” of spatial change, their task design often centres on fundamental kinematics or geometric relations. The evaluation objective remains focused on the passive description of events, largely neglecting the higher-level “why”, the core of Intent-Driven Reasoning.

EscherVerse is situated at the confluence of these research thrusts, designed to push the evaluation frontier towards a complete assessment of TSI. It builds upon the move towards dynamic, real-world data but introduces a fundamental shift in the evaluation objective. By systematically integrating tasks that probe both complex Physical-Dynamic Reasoning and, for the first time at scale, Intent-Driven Reasoning, it addresses the critical, unmeasured dimensions of spatial intelligence. It is therefore positioned as the first benchmark designed to elevate the evaluation from perceiving geometry and motion to understanding the interplay of physics and purpose.

3
Methods

To construct a benchmark capable of holistically evaluating Teleo-Spatial Intelligence (TSI), we have developed a scalable pipeline as shown in Figure
3
, to generate and validate a large-scale dataset. Our methodology is specifically designed to address the key concerns of TSI, bridging the gaps between simulation and reality, static and dynamic scenes, and mere spatial description and deep intent understanding. Our pipeline consists of three integral stages: (1) multi-stage filtering for open-world video curation, (2) cognitively-inspired question-and-answer generation targeting the full spectrum of TSI, and (3) multi-model cross-validation complemented by human adjudication.

3.1
Filter and Generation Pipeline

To construct a video corpus rich in the complex interactions required to assess TSI, we move beyond simulated and constrained indoor environments. Real-world scenarios are essential because both Physical-Dynamic Reasoning and Intent-Driven Reasoning occur with significantly greater complexity and subtlety in open-world environments compared to controlled or curated settings.

Data Filter

To precisely identify clips aligned with our research objectives from captions of online videos, we design a coarse-to-fine filtering pipeline:

Rule-based Filter.

We first analyze preliminary video captions. Inspired by the scoring mechanism of Koala-36M
25
, we employ rule-based scoring and keyword matching to assign initial content scores. This approach rapidly filters out static or monotonous videos from 3,766,053 online videos, yielding over 105,000 videos.

LLM-based Filter.

We employ GPT-120b-OSS
2
to perform fine-grained filtering on the screened clips. Using a carefully designed red-teaming prompt (as shown in the Appendix), we instruct the model to score each video description, explicitly prioritizing scenes with multiple objects or multiple agents, actions, and movement, while penalizing static or ambiguous content. This process ensures that the final 11,328 selected videos exhibit high value in TSI.

Red-Teaming QA Generation.

To generate question-answer pairs that effectively probe the two core pillars of Teleo-Spatial Intelligence, we develop an automated QA generation method.

Generator Design.

We leverage a state-of-the-art vision-language model (VLM) equipped with a specialized red-teaming expert prompt. This prompt enforces four fundamental principles to ensure question quality: prioritizing visual over linguistic challenges, grounding questions strictly in video content, avoiding shortcut questions that can be answered without viewing the video, and encouraging direct analysis rather than scene description. Additionally, we have constructed a seed prompts repository named seed Instruction Library,
33
,which containing human-labeled high-quality question-answer pairs as seed prompts. During the questioning process, seed prompts are randomly sampled from this repository and integrated into the red-teaming expert prompt. This seed repository is continuously updated with high-quality question-answer pairs generated during the question-generation process.

Scene-Oriented Questioning Strategy.

The strategy guides the VLM to first analyze the primary driver of spatial change in a video, categorizing it as either primarily driven by human agency or by physical/mechanical forces. This distinction allows us to generate questions that cover both pillars of TSI systematically:

For Human-Centric scenes, where actions are goal-oriented, the VLM is directed to generate questions that target intent-driven reasoning. For Object-Centric scenes, dominated by physical laws or mechanical processes, the VLM focuses on generating questions that probe Physical-Dynamic Reasoning.

To further refine the two pillars of our TSI concept, we have developed six dimensions of TSI, as illustrated in Figure
2
. All generated questions are constructed around six high-level reasoning dimensions. These dimensions are designed to systematically span the full range of challenges within TSI, from foundational perceptual abilities to advanced predictive and causal inference:

1.

Action & Intent-Driven Spatial Reasoning.
Models must infer not only spatial changes but also the underlying human intentions.

2.

Egocentric vs. Allocentric Reference Frames.
Tests the ability to switch between and reason across different reference frames—from the camera operator’s viewpoint (egocentric) to a global scene perspective (allocentric).

3.

Predictive & Counterfactual Spatial Reasoning.
Challenges models to predict future spatial states based on current trajectories and physical laws, or reason about hypothetical scenarios.

4.

Dynamic Spatial Relationships.
Requires understanding of time-varying relative positions, orientations, and topological relations between objects.

5.

Object Deformation & State Transition.
Evaluates understanding of non-rigid shape changes or fundamental state alterations.

6.

Object Permanence & Occlusion Tracking.
Assesses the model’s ability to track objects temporarily occluded or out of view continuously.

High-Quality Distractors and Chain-of-Thought Generation.

To ensure rigorous evaluation, our prompt emphasizes the plausibility of distractors. For multiple-choice questions, incorrect options are designed to represent typical spatial cognitive errors (e.g., left-right confusion, overlooking interactions) rather than irrelevant or absurd choices. Additionally, for a substantial subset, we instruct the model to generate detailed chain-of-thought (CoT) annotations that step-by-step explain the reasoning process required to arrive at the correct answer.

3.2
Verification Pipeline

Automatically generated data inevitably contains errors, ambiguities, or potential biases. To ensure the highest quality and fairness of the EscherVerse benchmark, we implement a rigorous verification pipeline.

Our verificatione pipeline employs a hierarchical, multi-stage filtering process to ensure the quality of the EscherVerse dataset. The process begins with an initial screening using Qwen2.5-VL-72B to eliminate QA pairs with fundamental flaws, such as question-answer mismatches or malformed questions. Subsequently, the filtered pairs undergo a consensus-based verification stage. We prompt Gemini-2.5-pro to perform multi-round evaluations and output its reasoning process. Inspired by self-consistency principles
27
, convergence on a correct answer across multiple strong models significantly bolsters our confidence in a QA pair’s quality. Conversely, disagreements flag a pair for further scrutiny. These flagged pairs are then re-evaluated by a more powerful model, Qwen3-VL-235B. The final stage involves human adjudication, where experts correct nuanced errors, remove unqualified samples, and ensure each instance stringently assesses the targeted facet of Teleo-Spatial Intelligence. To guarantee final quality, the entire candidate set is subjected to a comprehensive automated inspection by Qwen3-VL-235B and random manual audits, culminating in the final EscherVerse dataset.

3.3
Final Dataset Composition

Through the complete three-stage pipeline described above, we construct EscherVerse, comprising two primary components designed to advance the evaluation and development of models capable of Teleo-Spatial Intelligence.

4.1
Experiment Setup

Benchmark models.

To systematically evaluate current vision-language models on our Teleo-Spatial Intelligence (TSI) benchmark, we conduct a comprehensive assessment across a diverse suite of models. Our evaluation includes leading proprietary models: GPT-5
19
, Gemini-2.5-flash, Gemini-2.5-Pro
23
, and Qwen3-VL-Plus
29
. For open-source models, we assess the Qwen-VL
5
series (Qwen2.5 and Qwen3, including both Instruct and Thinking variants from 2B to 32B), LLaVA-NeXT-Video-7B
34
, LLaVA-OneVision-1.5
3
, MiniCPM-V-4
32
, and ERNIE-4.5-VL-28B
6
. To contrast these general-purpose VLMs with domain-specific approaches, we also include the spatial model Spatial-MLLM
20
and ViCA2-7B
13
.

Finally, to measure the impact of targeted fine-tuning, we evaluate our own models: the Escher series, developed by fine-tuning Qwen3-VL models on our Escher-35k dataset, and ISI-8B-Instruct, fine-tuned on our ISI-55k static-scene dataset for contrastive analysis. All models are evaluated under a unified, zero-shot protocol with a temperature of 0 to ensure deterministic and comparable results. We report overall accuracy, performance across four question types, and detailed scores for the six core dimensions of TSI.

Evaluation details.

We measure each VLM’s accuracy by directly comparing the model’s generated answer with the ground truth, without employing any additional external models for performance evaluation. For consistency, all models are evaluated with a temperature of zero, a maximum output length of 8192 tokens, and a uniform temporal sampling of 16 frames per video. To ensure reliable automated parsing, models are instructed to provide the final answer enclosed within <answer><\answer>tags. The evaluation logic varies by question type: for single and multiple-choice questions, accuracy is determined by a hard match of the extracted option letters; for True/False questions, a keyword match is used; and for fill-in-the-blank questions, a full score is awarded for an exact match, while partial credit is given if the answer’s text similarity to the ground truth exceeds a predefined threshold.

4.2.1
Overall Performance Landscape

The overall results reveal a clear performance hierarchy and underscore the significant challenge posed by Escher-Bench. The closed-source model Gemini-2.5-pro emerges as the top performer with an overall accuracy of 57.26%. This sets the current state-of-the-art but also highlights that even the most powerful models are far from mastering Teleo-Spatial Intelligence (TSI), leaving substantial room for improvement. Among open-source models, the Qwen family demonstrates robust capabilities, with Qwen3-VL-32B-Thinking standing out as the best performer with an accuracy of 49.58%, establishing a strong baseline for the open-source community. The performance generally scales with model size, though the “Thinking” variants, which are prompted for chain-of-thought reasoning, do not always outperform their “Instruct” counterparts. The overall accuracy scores, with the best model below 60%, confirm that Escher-Bench effectively probes the weaknesses of current VLMs in dynamic and intent-driven scenarios, validating its design as a challenging testbed for TSI.

4.2.2
Escher-35k: Teleo-Spatial Intelligence dataset

To validate the quality and effectiveness of our proposed Escher-35k, we fine-tuned several Qwen models on it, providing compelling evidence for its ability to impart sophisticated TSI capabilities. Except for Escher-7B-Instruct, which was fine-tuned from Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, all other Escher models were fine-tuned from Qwen3-VL models of the same scale. The Escher-8B-SFT achieves an overall accuracy of 49.85%, surpassing its base model (Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct at 45.06%. This demonstrates that even a simple SFT process on Escher-35k can substantially enhance a model’s TSI reasoning. Notably, Escher-8B-SFT even outperforms the much larger Qwen3-VL-32B-Thinking model, highlighting the data efficiency and targeted nature of our dataset. The most significant gains are observed in the “Action & Intent-Driven Spatial Reasoning” category, where Escher-8B-SFT improves from 55.89% to 63.60%, directly confirming that Escher-35k effectively teaches models the crucial and previously neglected pillar of Intent-Driven Reasoning.

To underscore the limitations of models trained on static 3D environments, we performed a contrastive analysis. We first evaluated Spatial-MLLM, a model trained on data from static 3D scans (e.g. ScanNet, ARKitScenes). On our dynamic Escher-Bench, it scored a mere 15.82%. To further investigate this domain gap, we constructed the Indoor-Spatial-Intelligence-55k (ISI-55k), comprising 55,193 QA pairs from the same static sources and following the methodology of
12
. We then fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct on this dataset to create ISI-8B-Instruct.

Remarkably, the resulting model achieved an overall accuracy of 45.06%, showing no improvement whatsoever over its Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct baseline. This zero-gain outcome provides compelling evidence that the knowledge acquired from static scene datasets is orthogonal to the skills tested by our benchmark. This vividly illustrates the fundamental domain shift our work addresses: proficiency in static, object-centric scene description does not translate to the dynamic, intent-driven, and open-world reasoning required for teleo-spatial intelligence.

4.2.3
Granular Analysis of Model Capabilities

A deeper dive into the results across different dimensions reveals specific strengths and weaknesses of current VLMs. The “Action & Intent-Driven” category proves to be the most challenging for most base models, yet it is where our SFT models show the largest improvements, reinforcing its status as a key bottleneck for TSI. Models are comparatively better at concrete tasks like Object Deformation & State Transition (e.g., Gemini-2.5-pro at 73.11%) but struggle with abstract reasoning such as Predictive & Counterfactual scenarios, which require a deeper understanding.

Across nearly all models, performance is slightly higher on Human-Centric questions compared to Object-Centric ones. For instance, Gemini-2.5-pro scores 58.37% on human-centric tasks versus 53.64% on object-centric ones. This suggests that models are more adept at reasoning about spatial dynamics when a human agent is explicitly present, likely because human actions provide stronger contextual cues for inferring intent and causality. Finally, the breakdown by question type reveals a significant architectural weakness in handling Multiple-Select questions, which consistently yield the lowest scores across all models. This format requires a model to evaluate and compare multiple complex propositions simultaneously. In contrast, models perform better on Fill-in-the-Blank and True/False questions, which often map to more direct fact retrieval or verification from the video context.

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced Teleo-Spatial Intelligence (TSI) and developed EscherVerse as the first benchmark to catalyze progress towards TSI. EscherVerse is designed to transition the field from simulation to reality, from static analysis to dynamic reasoning, and from object-centric description to intent understanding. By evaluating models on real-world videos, it assesses both physical dynamics and human goal inference. The state-of-the-art open-source performance of our Escher-8B-Instruct model validates our approach while underscoring the difficulty of TSI. Future work should expand the TSI to encompass more complex phenomena like social dynamics or long-term planning.

EscherVerse Curation Details

6.1
Data Construction

To ensure the breadth and depth of Teleo-Spatial Intelligence (TSI) evaluation, we establish a massive-scale, open-world video corpus as our foundation. Our raw dataset comprises
3,766,053
video clips harvested from diverse internet sources, spanning a vast array of real-world scenarios including urban driving, household activities, industrial manipulation, and natural interactions.

Unlike existing benchmarks that rely heavily on synthetic environments or constrained indoor datasets, our data collection prioritizes
open-world complexity
. Real-world videos provide the necessary visual entropy—such as varying lighting, occlusions, and unpredictable agent behaviors—that is essential for testing a model’s robust spatial reasoning.

6.2
Rule-based Filtering Pipeline

As outlined in the main paper, Section 3.1, we construct the EscherVerse dataset by sourcing raw videos from Panda-70M. To ensure the dataset strictly adheres to the requirements of Teleo-Spatial Intelligence (TSI)—specifically favoring real-world dynamics over simulation and active interaction over passive observation—we implemented a rigorous multi-stage filtering pipeline. This pipeline processes video captions and metadata to filter out static, synthetic, or irrelevant content. The specific filtering logic is detailed below:

1. Real-World Scenario Verification.

To bridge the domain gap between simulation and reality, we explicitly exclude non-real-world content. We constructed a blacklist of keywords covering four primary categories of synthetic data: animation, gaming footage, virtual interfaces, and special effects. As shown in Table
2
, any video caption containing terms such as
gameplay
,
CGI
, or
screen recording
is immediately discarded. This ensures that all spatial reasoning tasks in EscherVerse are grounded in physical reality.

2. Action and Intent Density Filtering.

TSI requires models to reason about Physical-Dynamic changes and Intent-Driven actions. We employ a positive selection strategy using a dictionary of Strong Action verbs and Tool Usage indicators. A clip is retained only if its caption includes:

•

Constructive Actions:
Verbs implying state changes or creation, such as
assembling
,
welding
, or
cooking
.

•

Tool Interaction:
Phrases explicitly describing object manipulation, for example,
using a drill
or
operating a machine
.

•

Causal Patterns:
Regex-based grammatical patterns that imply purpose, such as
using X to Y
or
working on Z
.

3. Static and Passive Content Exclusion.

We filter out videos where the primary visual content lacks spatial reasoning value. This includes talking heads (e.g., news anchors, interviews), static states (e.g., posing, sleeping), and passive media presentations (e.g., slideshows). These scenarios typically lack the object deformation or trajectory changes necessary for dynamic spatial reasoning.

4. Temporal Duration Constraint.

To ensure the videos provide sufficient temporal context for tracking Object Permanence and State Transitions, we aggregate the duration of all valid clips within a single video ID. We enforce a
minimum total duration of 30 seconds
for the valid segments. Videos failing to meet this aggregated length are discarded, ensuring that the benchmark evaluates long-horizon reasoning rather than instantaneous recognition.

9
Limitations, Societal Impact, and Future Outlook

9.1
Limitations

While EscherVerse represents a significant step forward in evaluating Teleo-Spatial Intelligence (TSI), we acknowledge several limitations inherent to our current approach:

•

Subjectivity in Intent Interpretation:
A core pillar of TSI is
Intent-Driven Reasoning
. Unlike physical dynamics (e.g., gravity, collision), human intent is inherently latent and occasionally ambiguous. While our “Human Adjudication” pipeline (Section 3.2) minimizes label noise, there remain edge cases where multiple valid interpretations of a human agent’s goal exist, potentially introducing a ceiling to the ground truth objectivity.

•

2D-to-3D Ambiguity:
Although EscherVerse moves beyond static 3D scans to dynamic videos, the input remains 2D. Models must infer 3D spatial relationships (e.g., depth, occlusion) from monocular video cues. This distinguishes our benchmark from simulation-based environments (e.g., Habitat, ProcTHOR) where perfect 3D ground truth is available, though at the cost of realism.

•

Data Distribution Bias:
Our dataset is sourced from online videos. While we apply rigorous filtering for diversity, the data inevitably reflects the biases of uploaded content—favoring visually clear, center-framed actions and specific cultural contexts. Rare or extremely subtle physical phenomena might be underrepresented compared to common daily activities.

9.2
Societal Impact

The development of TSI has profound implications for the deployment of AI in the real world.

Positive Impact: Safer Embodied AI.

The primary goal of TSI is to enable agents to understand not just
how
objects move, but
why
. This is critical for safe human-robot collaboration. A robot equipped with TSI can predict a human’s trajectory based on their intent (e.g., “walking to the fridge implies opening the door”), thereby planning its own path to avoid collisions and assist proactively. This is essential for domestic robots, elderly care assistants, and automated manufacturing.

Potential Risks: Surveillance and Privacy.

The ability to infer human intent from visual data carries dual-use risks. Highly advanced TSI models could potentially be used for invasive surveillance, predicting individual behaviors or analyzing private activities with high precision. To mitigate privacy concerns in our dataset, we rely on public-domain videos and have implemented strict filtering to exclude content containing Personally Identifiable Information (PII) or sensitive private scenes during our curation pipeline.

9.3
Future Outlook

Based on the “Cognitive Bottleneck” and other failure modes observed in our experiments, we envision three key directions for future research:

•

Beyond Short-Term Dynamics:
Currently, Escher-Bench focuses on relatively short clips. Future iterations should expand to long-horizon planning, testing a model’s ability to link a sequence of small physical actions to a grander, long-term goal (e.g., cooking a full meal).

•

Social Spatial Intelligence:
As noted in our Conclusion, the current scope focuses on single-agent or object-agent interactions. A natural evolution of TSI is to encompass
Social Dynamics
—understanding how the spatial intentions of multiple agents interact, conflict, or cooperate within a shared physical space.

•

Closing the Simulation-Reality Gap:
We hope EscherVerse serves as a catalyst to bridge the gap between the precise but sterile world of 3D simulations and the noisy, intent-rich reality of human environments, eventually leading to General Purpose Robots that can operate robustly in the open world.

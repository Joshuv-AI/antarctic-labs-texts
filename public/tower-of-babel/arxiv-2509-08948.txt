Estimation of deuteron binding energy using Qiskit with renormalization group-based effective interactions
Sreelekshmi Pillai, S. Ramanan, V. Balakrishnan, S. Lakshmibala

Abstract
We have obtained the binding energy of the deuteron on a quantum simulator using the variational quantum eigensolver (VQE) for renormalization group (RG)-based low-momentum effective interactions. The binding energy has been calculated in the truncated harmonic oscillator (HO) basis, using the Qiskit-Aer simulator in both noise-free and noisy cases. The noise models have been taken from the actual IBM quantum hardware, and the results obtained have been extrapolated to the zero noise limit. It is shown that the number of HO basis states needed for computing the binding energy to within 1 percent of the experimental value in the quantum simulator, decreases with decreasing RG parameter λ \lambda . The dependence of the extent of entanglement between the oscillator modes on λ \lambda has been analysed.



I
Introduction

An ab-initio description of nuclei starting from microscopic few body interactions poses challenges both conceptually as well as computationally. This is due to complexities at the level of the interactions, and the fact that these are strongly correlated systems. Hence, substantial research is still focused on obtaining a comprehensive understanding of nuclear properties across the nuclear chart.

Computationally, the problem is extremely resource intensive. For instance, in a shell model calculation, the dimensions of the Hilbert space needed for numerical convergence grows exponentially with the number of nucleons
Pérez-Obiol et al. 2023
;
Bhoy and Stevenson 2024
. Progress in nuclear structure calculations can be often traced back to availability of better computational resources or advances in the conceptual understanding of the few-body sector, or both. The emergence of effective field theories (EFTs) and renormalization group (RG) ideas have helped in formulating effective interactions that are better starting points for perturbative calculations, compared to phenomenological interactions that conventionally have strong short-range repulsion. As a consequence, numerically expensive resummations can be avoided
Bogner et al. 2010
.

Further opportunities to handle numerically intensive problems more efficiently are being provided in recent years, due to the tremendous effort that is being invested in creating quantum computers
that promise to outperform classical computers. Therefore, inherently numerically expensive problems such as those that arise in nuclear structure calculations are expected to benefit from even the NISQ-era platforms. These have been explored primarily within the framework of pionless EFT. For instance, the deuteron binding energy has been computed
Dumitrescu et al. 2018
;
Shehab et al. 2019
;
Gu et al.
,
with merely two and three qubits, and by extrapolating to infinite dimensions. The results for the binding energy thus obtained, are in agreement with the experiments to less than 1
\%
. An extension to the
3
He nucleus that includes three-body forces has been carried out in
Gu et al.
. More recently, the two-body scattering states have been computed using the variational quantum eigensolver (VQE) algorithm in a HO basis and phase shifts have been extracted
Sharma et al. 2024
. Similarly, shell model calculations have been performed on a quantum computer for light and medium mass nuclei, including neon and calcium isotopes, using phenomenological models. In addition, attempts have been made to compute the expectation values of deuteron observables (see, for instance,
Siwach and Arumugam 2022
.)

In this paper we focus on computing the binding energy of the deuteron on a quantum simulator, using the chiral N4LO interactions
Entem et al. 2017
and the Argonne V
18
(AV
18
)
Wiringa et al. 1995
and their RG-evolved counterparts. The chiral interactions (which are EFT-inspired), reproduce the two-body experimental phase shifts and the deuteron binding energy to
\chi^{2}/\text{d.o.f}\sim 1
. Furthermore, they inherently have a momentum cutoff
\sim 500\text{MeV}
. It has been shown in the literature
Bogner et al. 2006
that even for these chiral interactions, lowering the
cutoff using the RG evolution results in an effective interaction that is universal in the two-body sector. Such effective interactions are good starting points for perturbation theory in the many-body sector. They require smaller model space sizes. This is achieved by decoupling the low-and high-momentum states through a set of unitary transformations that result in interactions that are either block diagonal or band diagonal.

In this work, we explore the connection between RG-based interactions and the qubit requirement on a quantum computing platform, by calculating the binding energy of the deuteron using VQE. In particular, we investigate how the numerical advantages available in a classical machine translate to the quantum context. Investigations on entanglement between oscillator modes can be crucial in quantum computing because they could reveal interesting links between single-particle states, efficient qubit mappings, ansätz design, and resource optimization for many-body nuclear simulations. Mode entanglement and correlations in two-nucleon systems have been analyzed earlier, for instance, in
Kruppa et al. 2021
. There have also been studies on the effect of the choice of basis on mode entanglement patterns and rearrangements
Robin et al. 2021
. Therefore, in this work, we have explored mode entanglement and its connection to the extent of renormalization as indicated by the RG cutoff.

The organization of the paper is as follows. In Sect.
II
, the salient features of a quantum computing platform are reviewed. These include the choice of the oscillator basis and its parameters, the VQE algorithm, and the mapping of the deuteron Hamiltonian to the qubit basis. The interaction is discussed briefly in Sect.
II.3
. The results and their connection to the RG evolution are presented in Sect.
III
. Section
IV
contains a summary and suggestions for possible extensions of this work.

II
Formalism

II.1
The deuteron problem in the harmonic oscillator basis

The deuteron is the only neutron-proton bound state. Its binding energy is -2.225 MeV. In the center of mass (COM) frame, this two-body problem simplifies to an effective one-body problem. The interaction is usually expressed in the partial wave basis
\{\ket{k,(l,S),J,M_{J},T,M_{T}}\}
, where
k
is the magnitude of the relative momentum wavevector,
J=1
is the total angular momentum,
S=1
is the total spin angular momentum,
T=0
is the total isospin,
M_{T}=0
is the third-component of total isospin and
l
is the relative orbital angular momentum, which takes values
0
and
2
due to the tensor force. However, the
3
-dimensional isotropic HO basis
\{\ket{n,(l,S),J,M_{J},T,M_{T}}\}
, where
n
is the principal quantum number, provides a convenient basis set for calculating the deuteron binding energy on the quantum computing platform. The values of
n
are truncated at
n_{\text{max}}
. This results in a the
2N\times 2N
Hamiltonian (
N=n_{\text{max}}+1
), whose matrix elements are given by

\displaystyle\braket{n,l|H|n^{\prime},l^{\prime}}=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{2}\int dk\,k^{2}\,\int dk^{\prime}\,k^{\prime 2}\,R_{nl}(k)\,R_{n^{\prime}l^{\prime}}(k^{\prime})\braket{k,l|H|k^{\prime},l^{\prime}},

(1)

where
R_{nl}(k)
, the HO radial wavefunction in momentum space, is given by

\displaystyle R_{nl}(k)=\sqrt{\frac{2n!b^{3}}{\Gamma(n+l+\frac{3}{2})}}(kb)^{l}e^{-\frac{1}{2}k^{2}b^{2}}L_{n}^{l+\frac{1}{2}}(k^{2}b^{2}).

(2)

Here
b=\sqrt{\tfrac{\hbar}{m\omega}}
,
L_{n}^{l+\frac{1}{2}}(k^{2}b^{2})
are the
associated Laguerre polynomials, and
\omega
is the oscillator frequency. In Eq. (
1
) quantum numbers
S,J,M_{J},T,M_{T}
are suppressed for notational
convenience.
In the oscillator basis, the matrix representation of
{p}^{2}
is tridiagonal and the matrix elements are as follows:

\displaystyle\langle n^{\prime},\ell|{p}^{2}|n,\ell\rangle=b\left[(2n+\ell+\tfrac{3}{2})\delta_{n^{\prime}n}-\sqrt{n(n+\ell+\tfrac{1}{2})}\,\delta_{n^{\prime},n+1}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\sqrt{(n+1)(n+\ell+\tfrac{1}{2})}\,\delta_{n^{\prime},n-1}.\right]

(3)

The truncation of the oscillator states to
N
, introduces ultraviolet (UV) and infrared (IR) cutoffs that limit the convergence of the bound-state energies computed in the HO basis
Furnstahl et al. 2012
. To ensure convergence in a truncated basis, two conditions must be satisfied. If the maximum energy of the oscillator is

\displaystyle E=\hbar\omega\left(2N+\frac{3}{2}\right),

(4)

\omega
and
N
have to be chosen such that
\lambda
corresponding to the specific interaction considered is smaller than the UV cutoff

\displaystyle\lambda_{UV}\equiv\sqrt{2\left(2N+\frac{3}{2}\right)}\frac{1}{b},

(5)

and that the nuclear radius
r
of the nucleus is smaller than

\displaystyle L_{0}\equiv\sqrt{2\left(2N+\frac{3}{2}\right)}b,

(6)

However, even if these two conditions are satisfied, convergence of numerical results in the oscillator basis is not guaranteed because
\lambda
is usually not a sharp cutoff, and the nuclear wavefunction extends beyond
r
.

We have computed the ground state energy of the deuteron using the variational quantum eigensolver (VQE) algorithm. The salient features of this algorithm are described in the subsequent section.

II.2
Variational Quantum Eigensolver

VQE is a hybrid quantum-classical algorithm used to compute the energy eigenvalues of quantum systems
Ayral et al. 2023
;
Cerezo et al. 2021
. The summary of the steps involved in this computation are presented in the schematic flow chart in
Fig.
1
.

Figure 1:
Schematic flow chart of the VQE algorithm.

The Hamiltonian in Eq. (
1
) in the second quantized form is given by

{H}=\sum_{i}\sum_{j}\braket{i|\hat{T}+\hat{V}|j}a_{j}^{{\dagger}}a_{i}.

(7)

Here
a_{j}^{{\dagger}}
and
a_{j}
are, respectively, the creation and annihilation operators of a deuteron in the state
\ket{j}
. For ease of presentation, the labels
i,j
refer to a fixed value of
n
and
l
, which represents a mode. For instance,
j=0
is the 0s mode (with
n=0
and
l=0
),
j=1
, the 0d mode etc. There are several ways of mapping
a_{j}^{{\dagger}}
,
a_{j}
to the qubit Pauli operators. The most straightforward is the Jordan-Wigner (JW) transformation. The number of qubits required here is the same as the number of modes considered in the quantum system. The mapping is defined as

a_{j}^{{\dagger}}=\frac{1}{2}(X_{j}-iY_{j})\prod_{k<j}Z_{k},

(8a)

a_{j}=\frac{1}{2}(X_{j}+iY_{j})\prod_{k<j}Z_{k}.

(8b)

Here,
X_{j}
,
Y_{j}
,
Z_{j}
are the Pauli operators that act on the
j^{\rm th}
qubit’s space. The product of
Z_{k}
s in Eqs. (
8a
) and (
8b
) ensures antisymmetry under exchange of the fermionic wavefunction. This factor is irrelevant for the one-body case and therefore,
\prod_{k<j}Z_{k}=\mathbbm{1}
. The truncated HO basis is mapped to the multiqubit basis denoted by
\{\ket{q_{0},q_{1},q_{2},\ldots q_{2N-1}}\}
, with
N=n_{\text{max}}+1
. Here,
q_{i}
is the
i^{\rm th}
qubit (mode that is either 1 or 0). The suffix 2 in the definition of the basis ket is due to the fact that for each
n
there are two values of
l
, namely, 0 and 2. For the four qubit case,
N=2
, if the 0s state is occupied, then this is mapped to the multiqubit state
\ket{1000}
. Similarly, if the 1s state is occupied, this is a multiqubit
\ket{0100}
. On the other hand, if the 0d or the 1d state is occupied, the multiqubit state is
\ket{0010}
or
\ket{0001}
respectively. It has been established in the literature that the JW mapping does not efficiently utilize the entire qubit Hilbert space. Other mappings such as the Bravyi-Kitaev or the gray code encoding effectively reduce the number of qubits required to map a given basis state
Seeley et al. 2012
;
Di Matteo et al. 2021
;
Siwach and Arumugam 2021
. For our purposes however, the JW mapping suffices.

The parametrized state required for VQE can be constructed using the unitary coupled-cluster (UCC) ansätz and is given by

\ket{\Psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})}=U(\boldsymbol{\theta})\ket{\phi_{\mathrm{HF}}},

(9)

where
\ket{\phi_{\mathrm{HF}}}
is the Hartree-Fock (HF) reference state.
The unitary operator
U(\boldsymbol{\theta})
, generated from the single excitation operators and the parameter vector

\boldsymbol{\theta}
for this one-body problem, is given by

U(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\exp\left[\sum_{\alpha,i}{\theta}_{\alpha i}\left(a_{\alpha}^{\dagger}a_{i}-a_{i}^{\dagger}a_{\alpha}\right)\right],

(10)

where
i
denotes the occupied and
\alpha
, the unoccupied states respectively. The HF reference state in our case is

\ket{10000\ldots}
, indicating that the lowest level (
0s
) is occupied.

The ansätz in Eq. (
10
) using single excitations needs to be represented as a quantum circuit in order to be executed on a quantum computer. As an example, let us consider the two-qubit (
N=1
) case, where there is only one parameter
\theta_{0}
. The unitary operator in Eq. (
10
) can be written as

U(\theta_{0})=e^{\theta_{0}(a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{1}-a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{0})}.

(11)

Using the JW mapping,

U(\theta_{0})=e^{-\frac{i\theta_{0}}{2}(X_{0}Y_{1}-Y_{0}X_{1})}

(12)

in terms of qubit Pauli operators.
X_{0}Y_{1}
and
Y_{0}X_{1}
commute. Therefore,

\displaystyle U(\theta_{0})\ket{10}

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{i\theta_{0}}{2}(X_{0}Y_{1})}e^{\frac{i\theta_{0}}{2}(Y_{0}X_{1})}\ket{10}.

(13)

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{i\theta_{0}}{2}(X_{0}Y_{1})}e^{\frac{i\theta_{0}}{2}(Y_{0}X_{1})}X_{0}\ket{00}.

(14)

Eq. (
14
) can be transformed into a quantum circuit using the rules (CNOT staircase construction) given in Appendix
A
, and the circuit is shown in Fig.
2
.

Figure 2:
UCC ansätz using single excitation for the two-qubit example.

Figure 3:
Simplified UCC ansätz using single excitation for the two-qubit example.

In Fig.
2
, the Pauli X gate in the
0^{\rm th}
qubit index just flips the corresponding qubit to give us the input state
\ket{10}
. For larger number of qubits, not all the Pauli strings in the exponential commute with each other, and single-step first-order Trotterization
Trotter 1959
;
Lloyd 1996
has been employed to approximate the unitary operator to arrive at
an ansätz similar to Eq. (
14
). The circuits obtained using the rules in Appendix
A
can be further simplified by reducing the circuit depth, while still preserving the action on the reference state. One such simplification for the two qubit case is shown in Fig.
3

Dumitrescu et al. 2018
;
Siwach and Arumugam 2021
. Although reduction of circuit depth is crucial, for our purposes, we use the more systematic CNOT staircase construction method, as there is no prescribed procedure for depth reduction.

II.3
Interaction

As mentioned earlier, the investigations are carried out with two different realistic forms of the two-body interaction, namely, the Argonne V
18
interaction
Wiringa et al. 1995
and the chiral N4LO
Entem et al. 2017
. Both are evolved to low momenta using the Similarity Renormalization group
Bogner et al. 2007
approach to yield effective interactions referred to as
V_{\text{SRG}}
. These renormalized effective interactions have been shown to yield better numerical convergence compared to the phenomenological models when used as inputs in many-body calculations
Bogner et al. 2010
;
Bogner et al. 2006
.

The evolution is through a set of continuous unitary transformations on the Hamiltonian
H(s)
, parameterised by
s=\lambda^{-4}
.

H(s)=U(s)H(0)U^{\dagger}(s)

(15)

so that,

\frac{dH(s)}{ds}=[\eta(s),H(s)]

(16)

where the dynamical generator
\eta(s)
is anti-hermitian. We use the Wilson’s generator with
\eta(s)=[T,H(s)]
, where
T
is the kinetic energy. (However, we note that there are other choices such as the Wegner generator for which
\eta(s)=[H_{d}(s),H_{\text{o}d}(s)]
, where
H_{d}
and
H_{\text{o}d}
are the diagonal and the off-diagonal parts of the Hamiltonian matrix in momentum space.)

Starting with the Wilson’s generator, effective interactions are created by decoupling low and high momentum states, leading to a band diagonal form. This can be seen, for instance, by expressing Eq. (
16
) in the partial wave basis of the momentum space, as follows.

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}V^{J,S,T}_{l,l^{\prime}}(k,k^{\prime})=-(k^{2}-k^{\prime\,2})^{2}V^{J,S,T}_{l,l^{\prime}}(k,k^{\prime})

\displaystyle\hskip-10.84006pt+\sum_{l^{\prime\prime}}\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}q^{2}\,dq\,(k^{2}+k^{\prime\,2}-2q^{2})\,V^{J,S,T}_{l,l^{\prime\prime}}(k,q)\,V^{J,S,T}_{l,l^{\prime\prime}}(q,k^{\prime}).

(17)

Retaining only the first term in the RHS which is linear in the interaction
V^{J,S,T}_{l,l^{\prime}}
, we see that the matrix elements far away from the diagonal (
k=k^{\prime}
), are first suppressed exponentially, and the effect of this term decreases towards the diagonal. This effect of driving the matrix elements of the interaction towards the diagonal is only accentuated by the second term in the RHS of Eq. (
17
). Therefore, SRG achieves decoupling between the low and high momentum states by driving the bare interaction to a band diagonal effective interaction that depends on the renormalization scale
\lambda
which also gives the size of the diagonal band (where the matrix elements of the interaction are non-zero).

It is important to note that due to the transformation being unitary, the two-body observables, such as the deuteron binding energy and scattering phase shifts, are independent of this scale
\lambda

Bogner et al. 2010
. As seen in Fig.
4
one obtains a band diagonal form (
V_{\text{SRG}}
) for the momentum space matrix elements of the interaction. Furthermore, as the RG cutoff decreases, the strength of the repulsive parts of the interaction decreases, and the attractive parts increases. This feature makes these interactions, when used as inputs in a nuclear many-body computation, to be good starting points for perturbative calculations whereby circumventing expensive resummations
Hergert et al. 2016
.

Figure 4:
Band diagonal evolution of Chiral N4LO in the coupled
{}^{3}S_{1}-^{3}D_{1}
channel. For each partial wave, as
\lambda
decreases, the momentum space matrix elements become increasingly band diagonal.

Figure 5:
SRG evolution of Chiral N4LO
{}^{3}S_{1}-^{3}D_{1}
channel in the HO basis (
N=7
,
\hbar\omega=9
MeV).

In Fig.
5
, the matrix elements of the interaction in the oscillator basis are shown for different values of
\lambda
. We see that for the bare interaction all the oscillator states are coupled. This is a well-known feature
Bogner et al. 2007
. However, as
\lambda
decreases the coupling of the lower oscillator modes to the higher modes decreases. Further for a given
n
,
the interaction matrix elements between
l=0
and
l=2
get strongly suppressed at lower values of
\lambda
similar to the behavior of the momentum space matrix elements. This comes about due to the weakening of the repulsive tensor force
Bogner et al. 2006
.

In the next section, we present our results for the deuteron binding energy in the oscillator basis using
V_{\text{SRG}}
interactions using both exact diagonalization and the Qiskit-Aer simulator.

III
Results

With the effective interactions discussed in Sect.
II.3
, we have computed the binding energy of the deuteron in the oscillator basis for different values of
N
and
\omega
, for a range of values of
\lambda
. This was carried out by exact diagonalization of the deuteron Hamiltonian in the HO basis and subsequently, by the VQE algorithm. The results of the exact diagonalization serve as a check for the classical optimization that is an integral part of VQE. The results presented here are for the chiral N4LO interaction. The Appendix
B
discusses the AV
18
.

Exact diagonalization

\lambda_{UV}
and the length scale

(Eqs. (

) and (

)) determined solely from the oscillator parameters, were introduced. In Fig.

the binding energy (i.e., the lowest energy eigenvalue obtained by exact diagonalization of the deuteron Hamiltonian in the HO basis) is plotted as a function of

L_{0}

Furnstahl et al. 2012
.

As
\lambda
decreases, the set of oscillator parameters for which convergence is realized increases; however, the region of convergence now includes lower values of
N
. Further, the values of
\hbar\omega
for which converged results are obtained (region shaded in red), shift to a lower range. In particular, for the bare chiral N4LO interaction, this is around
N=7
and

\lambda=0.1\,\text{fm}^{-1}

\hbar\omega
close to zero. Smaller range of values of
\hbar\omega
implies that
L_{0}
is larger, thereby allowing for the position space wavefunction to fall-off to zero, within the box, ensuring converged results for binding
energy.

It is important to note that convergence of binding energy for smaller
N
, implies that lesser number of qubits are required on a quantum simulator.

We have used the Qiskit Software Development Kit (SDK) from IBM

The choice of classical optimizers (used for the ansätz parameter optimization) is crucial in determining the accuracy of the algorithm. We have used the COBYLA optimizer as it is gradient-free and works well in noisy environments. We have computed the ground state energy for a range of values of
\hbar\omega

\lambda

We have executed the VQE simulations in two different ways: (1) using the statevector simulator and (2) probabilistic simulations (with shots). The statevector simulator models the exact circuit and obtains the final state using matrix manipulations. If the classical optimization is reliable, the results from the statevector simulator are the same as those obtained using exact diagonalization. In the shot-based probabilistic simulation, a shot refers to a complete evaluation of the circuit on a quantum hardware or a simulator, resulting in a measurement. Therefore, the Pauli operator terms in the Hamiltonian are measured for each shot. The expectation value of the Hamiltonian is then estimated by averaging the outcomes over many shots, which introduces statistical sampling error even in the absence of hardware noise. We have performed these probabilistic simulations, both in the presence and absence of hardware noise. In the Aer Estimator, we have used the Gaussian sampling approximation. This replaces the discrete output distribution with a continuous Gaussian distribution with the same mean and variance. This procedure accelerates convergence while maintaining realistic gate noise
characteristics. As a result of this choice, the readout errors have been suppressed, while the gate errors have been retained.

We have performed 10 independent VQE iterations for both the statevector and the probabilistic simulations. For each VQE iteration, the initial set of parameters for the statevector simulation has been randomly chosen. For the probabilistic simulations, which needed 800,000 shots for convergence, we have first selected the set of initial parameters to be the output of the statevector simulator. This ensures that the given initial point is very close to the actual ground state wavefunction, thereby allowing for an investigation of the noise propagation on the quantum computing platform. In addition, we have also performed the probabilistic simulations for a random set of initial parameters that serves to test the convergence and noise mitigation of the algorithm. IV
Conclusion

In this paper, we have simulated the deuteron using Qiskit-Aer, and extracted its binding energy. For this purpose, starting from the chiral N4LO, and the AV
18
, we have evolved the interactions using the SRG formalism. In the case of exact diagonalization, as well as simulations, both in the absence and presence of (gate) noise, it has been shown that it is advantageous to lower the cutoff,
since the evolved effective low-momentum interactions reduce the qubit requirement for the system under investigation.

Our results with zero noise extrapolation agree with those obtained by exact diagonalization as well as those from noiseless simulations. This underlines the robustness of the extrapolation techniques as well as the reliability of the optimizers (which are a part of the Qiskit environment). Furthermore, the binding energy agrees with experimental value to within
1\%
which is comparable to the results obtained using pionless EFT
Dumitrescu et al. 2018
;
Shehab et al. 2019
;
Gu et al.
.

In addition, we have also investigated the changes in the mode entanglement that arise with changes in the renormalization scale
\lambda
, and the oscillator parameters using concurrence as an entanglement measure. While the concurrence has been shown to decrease overall, indicating a lowering of correlations in the HO basis, the detailed behavior for very small values of
\lambda
remains to be understood. Work in this direction is in progress.

This work is a first step in the program of carrying out ab-initio nuclear structure computation on quantum computing platforms using realistic renormalization group based interactions as inputs.
Several open questions remain. Extensions to many-body systems such as the triton,
\alpha
-nucleus etc., are still to be carried out. However, it is encouraging to see that the RG approach to building effective interactions brought in to the advantage of nuclear many body calculations, is useful for quantum computing platforms as well.

Appendix C
Mode entanglement

Let the deuteron state be represented by
\ket{\Psi}
. Let the mode of interest be denoted by
\ket{\tilde{n},\tilde{l}}
(the other quantum numbers have been
suppressed). We study the entanglement of this mode (subsystem 1) with the rest of the modes (subsystem 2). In terms of the superposition coefficients

\braket{n,l|\Psi}
, the total density matrix is,

\rho=\ket{\Psi}\bra{\Psi}=\sum_{n,l\\
n^{\prime},l^{\prime}}\braket{n,l|\Psi}\braket{\Psi|n^{\prime},l^{\prime}}\ket{n,l}\bra{n^{\prime},l^{\prime}}.

(20)

In order to study mode entanglement, moving to the occupation number representation (where an occupied mode is denoted by 1 and an unoccupied one by 0) proves useful. In this representation,
\ket{\tilde{n},\tilde{l}}
will be represented as
\ket{10000\dots}
. For notational convenience, the first label is reserved for the mode in consideration. The rest of the labels represent the remaining modes (This is slightly different from the notation introduced in section
II
)). We denote a general ket in this representation by
\ket{xy}
where
x
can be 0 or 1 and
y
is a binary string of 0s and 1s. By construction, if
x=1
, then
y
must be the string of all zeros. The expansion coefficients of the deuteron state in this basis are
written as
C_{xy}=\braket{xy|\Psi}
. For example,
C_{1000\ldots}=\braket{\tilde{n},\tilde{l}|\Psi}
and
C_{0y}=\braket{n,l|\Psi}
, where the values of
n
and
l
are determined by which entry in the string
y
is equal to 1. In this representation, the density matrix in Eq.(
20
) can be rewritten as,

\rho=\sum_{x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}C_{xy}C^{*}_{x^{\prime}y^{\prime}}\ket{xy}\bra{y^{\prime}x^{\prime}}.

(21)

The single mode reduced density matrix
\tilde{\rho}
found by tracing out over the rest of the modes (all possible combinations of
y
), is then given as,

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tilde{y}}\braket{\tilde{y}|\Psi}\braket{\Psi|\tilde{y}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tilde{y}}\sum_{x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}C_{xy}C^{*}_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}\delta_{\tilde{y}y}\delta_{\tilde{y}y^{\prime}}\ket{x}\bra{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tilde{y}}\sum_{x,x^{\prime}}C_{x\tilde{y}}C^{*}_{x^{\prime}\tilde{y}}\ket{x}\bra{x^{\prime}}.

It can be shown that in the matrix representation,
\tilde{\rho}
is a
2\times 2
diagonal matrix with diagonal entries given in terms of the superposition coefficients as
|\braket{\tilde{n},\tilde{l}|\Psi}|^{2}
and
1-|\braket{\tilde{n},\tilde{l}|\Psi}|^{2}
. The concurrence is given as,

C=\sqrt{2(1-\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde{\rho}^{2}))}.

(22)

For the single mode case that we consider, this becomes,

C=\sqrt{2(1-(|\braket{\tilde{n},\tilde{l}|\Psi}|^{4}+(1-|\braket{\tilde{n},\tilde{l}|\Psi}|^{2})^{2}))}.

(23)

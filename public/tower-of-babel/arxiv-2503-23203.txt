Abstract
. We develop a new approach to non-Hausdorff étale groupoids and their algebras based on Timmermann’s construction of Hausdorff covers. As an application, we completely characterise when singular ideals vanish in Steinberg algebras over arbitrary rings. We also completely characterise when C ∗ C^{*} -algebraic singular ideals have trivial intersection with the non-Hausdorff analogue of subalgebras of continuous, compactly supported functions. This leads to a characterisation when C ∗ C^{*} -algebraic singular ideals vanish for groupoids satisfying a finiteness condition. Moreover, our approach leads to further sufficient vanishing criteria for singular ideals and reduces questions about simplicity, the ideal intersection property, amenability and nuclearity for non-Hausdorff étale groupoids to the Hausdorff case.

Introduction

Topological groupoids arise naturally in a variety of areas such as dynamics, topology, geometry, group theory and
C^{*}
-algebras, building bridges between all these areas of mathematics. The link between topological groupoids and
C^{*}
-algebras is established by the construction of convolution algebras
[
29
,
10
,
11
]
. Many important classes of
C^{*}
-algebras arise from groupoids (for example AF-algebras, Cuntz-Krieger algebras, graph algebras etc., see also
[
22
]
), and groupoid models are very helpful for studying structural properties or interesting invariants of the corresponding
C^{*}
-algebras. For topological groupoids which are Hausdorff (as well as locally compact and étale), there are satisfactory results characterising fundamental properties of groupoid
C^{*}
-algebras such as simplicity, the ideal intersection property or nuclearity in terms of the underlying groupoids (see
[
12
,
3
,
18
,
1
]
), and the notion of Cartan subalgebras allows to reconstruct groupoids from their
C^{*}
-algebras
[
20
,
32
]
. However, several of these results, such as characterisations of simplicity, the ideal intersection property, or reconstruction results using Cartan subalgebras, do not carry over to non-Hausdorff groupoids (see
[
12
]
and also a related example due to Skandalis in
[
31
]
), and new challenges arise in the non-Hausdorff setting because we have to work with compact sets which are not closed and functions which are not continuous. At the same time, important examples of groupoids arising as groupoid of germs, from foliations or self-similar groups typically are non-Hausdorff (see
[
13
,
26
,
27
]
for some examples), which provides a strong motivation to systematically develop a better understanding of non-Hausdorff groupoids and their
C^{*}
-algebras.

In this context, a new construction called essential groupoid
C^{*}
-algebras has been introduced for non-Hausdorff groupoids
[
14
,
21
]
, and simplicity as well as the ideal intersection property have been characterised for this new construction in terms of the underlying groupoids
[
9
,
18
]
. While these recent developments and their algebraic analogues in the setting of Steinberg algebras have led to progress in our understanding of non-Hausdorff groupoids, it remains an open question when essential groupoid
C^{*}
-algebras agree with the more classical reduced groupoid
C^{*}
-algebras, and there is an analogous open question for Steinberg algebras (see
[
34
,
35
,
38
,
17
]
for progress in the case of groupoids arising from self-similar groups). Moreover, the construction of essential groupoid
C^{*}
-algebras has been specifically designed to resolve issues arising in the study of simplicity or the ideal intersection property, so that in other contexts, essential groupoid
C^{*}
-algebras might not be good substitutes for reduced groupoid
C^{*}
-algebras (because of bad functoriality properties, for example).

The goal of this paper is to develop a new approach to non-Hausdorff groupoids and their algebras, based on the construction of Hausdorff covers as in
[
36
]
(similar constructions appeared in
[
19
,
37
]
). Given a non-Hausdorff étale groupoid
G
with Hausdorff unit space, its Hausdorff cover
\tilde{G}
is a Hausdorff étale groupoid given by the closure of
G
in the space of non-empty closed subsets of
G
with respect to the Fell topology
[
15
]
. Alternatively, we can think of
\tilde{G}
as the Gelfand spectrum of the smallest commutative
C^{*}
-algebra of bounded, complex-valued functions on
G
(with respect to point-wise multiplication) containing
\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)\coloneq{\rm span}(\left\{C_{c}(U)\text{: }U\subseteq G\text{ open, Hausdorff}\right\})
. Here
C_{c}(U)
is the algebra of continuous functions
U\to\mathbb{C}
with compact support contained in
U
, and we extend a function in
C_{c}(U)
by zero to view it as a function on
G
. Using the Hausdorff cover, we are able to develop new tools in the non-Hausdorff setting as well as reduce questions about non-Hausdorff groupoids to the Hausdorff case. Here is a summary of our main achievements.

•

We establish a complete characterisation when singular ideals (the definition is explained below) vanish in Steinberg algebras over arbitrary rings in terms of a groupoid property. In combination with
[
34
, Theorem A]
, this leads to a complete characterisation for simplicity of Steinberg algebras over fields, which in particular answers
[
9
, Question 1]
.

•

We also completely characterise when the
C^{*}
-algebraic singular ideal of a non-Hausdorff étale groupoid
G
has trivial intersection with
\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)
. This leads to a characterisation when the
C^{*}
-algebraic singular ideal vanishes, under the assumption that the closure of the unit space
G^{(0)}
of

has finite fibres over
G^{(0)}
. In combination with existing simplicity characterisations for essential groupoid
C^{*}
-algebras in
[
18
]
, this yields a characterisation in terms of a groupoid property when the reduced groupoid
C^{*}
-algebra of

is simple (under further assumptions on

), which provides a partial answer to
[
9
, Question 2]
. Another consequence is that, for a non-Hausdorff ample groupoid

satisfying our finiteness condition, simplicity of the complex Steinberg algebra of

implies simplicity of the reduced groupoid

-algebra of

, which provides a partial answer to
[
9
, Question 3]
. As a further application, we obtain a conceptual explanation for the results on groupoids associated with contracting self-similar groups in
[
17
]
.

•

For a non-Hausdorff étale groupoid
G
which can be covered by countably many open bisections, we describe singular ideals in terms of the Hausdorff cover and give sufficient conditions for their vanishing, both in the
C^{*}
-algebraic and the algebraic context. This complements existing results on vanishing of singular ideals (such as those in
[

,

]
) by explaining them in terms of the Hausdorff cover.

•

We show, again for a non-Hausdorff étale groupoid

which can be covered by countably many open bisections, that the ideal intersection property for the essential groupoid
C^{*}
-algebra

is equivalent to the ideal intersection property of the reduced

-algebra

, where

is the restriction of

to a closed invariant subset of the unit space of

. Since

is Hausdorff, this explains why the ideal intersection property for essential groupoid

-algebras is much more tractable than for reduced groupoid

-algebras and allows for characterisations as in the Hausdorff case. In particular, we obtain a conceptual explanation for results on simplicity or the ideal intersection property for essential
C^{*}
-algebras of non-Hausdorff étale groupoids in
[

,
14
,
21
,
18
]
.

•

We show that nuclearity of the reduced groupoid
C^{*}
-algebra of a non-Hausdorff étale groupoid

is equivalent to topological amenability of its Hausdorff cover

. If

is

[

Let us now describe our main results in more detail. Our central objects of study are étale groupoids
G
, i.e., small categories with invertible morphisms, equipped with a topology such that all structure maps become local homeomorphisms. We always assume that
G
is locally compact and that the unit space
{G^{(0)}}
is Hausdorff. The space
\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)
introduced above is an algebra with respect to convolution, and its completion with respect to the norm from the left regular representation is the reduced groupoid
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}_{r}(G)
. The (
C^{*}
-algebraic) singular ideal
J
consists of those elements of
C^{*}_{r}(G)
which are only non-zero on a set whose source has empty interior. The essential groupoid
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}_{{\rm ess}}(G)
is then defined as the quotient
C^{*}_{r}(G)/J
. In the purely algebraic context, we consider ample groupoids
G
, which are étale groupoids with totally disconnected unit spaces. In this case, given a ring
R
, the Steinberg algebra
RG
is given by
RG\coloneq{\rm span}_{R}(\left\{C_{c}(U,R)\text{: }U\subseteq G\text{ open, Hausdorff}\right\})
. Here
C_{c}(U,R)
is the algebra of locally constant functions
U\to R
with compact support contained in
U
, and we extend a function in
C_{c}(U,R)
by zero to view it as a function on
G
. As is the case for
\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)
,
RG
becomes an algebra with respect to convolution, and the algebraic analogue of the singular ideal is denoted by
J_{R}
and consists of those
f\in RG
which vanish outside of a set with empty interior. As mentioned above, it is an open problem to characterise in terms of
G
when the singular ideal
J
vanishes, or in the ample case, when
J_{R}
vanishes.

The Hausdorff cover
\tilde{G}
of
G
is constructed as a space of subsets of
G
, equipped with the Fell topology. It comes with a canonical (non-continuous) inclusion
\iota\colon G\hookrightarrow\tilde{G}
with dense image. The dual picture mentioned above, describing
\tilde{G}
as a Gelfand spectrum, yields a canonical inclusion
\mathfrak{i}\colon\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)\hookrightarrow C_{c}(\tilde{G})
, where
C_{c}(\tilde{G})
denotes the algebra of continuous, complex-valued functions on
\tilde{G}
with compact support. Moreover, the map
\mathfrak{i}
extends continuously to the embedding
C^{*}_{r}(\mathfrak{i})\colon C^{*}_{r}(G)\hookrightarrow C^{*}_{r}(\tilde{G})
. For an ample groupoid
G
, we show that its Hausdorff cover
\tilde{G}
is again ample, and given a ring
R
, we obtain an analogous inclusion of Steinberg algebras
\mathfrak{i}_{R}\colon RG\hookrightarrow R\tilde{G}
.

Our first main result completely characterises when
J\cap\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)
vanishes, and when
J_{R}
vanishes, for arbitrary rings. Given a ring
R
, we write
{\rm ord}(R)
for the set of all orders of non-zero elements of
R
. Moreover, we set
R_{0}\coloneq\mathbb{Q}
and
R_{t}\coloneq\mathbb{Z}/t\mathbb{Z}
, where
t
is an integer with
t>1
. Furthermore, given
t\in\left\{0,2,3,\dotsc\right\}
, we say that an étale groupoid
G
satisfies condition (
\mathcal{S}_{t}
) if there exist a positive integer
n
and open bisections
U_{i}\subseteq G
for
1\leq i\leq n
such that

•

s(U_{i})=s(U_{j})
for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
,

•

U_{i}\setminus(\bigcup_{j\neq i}U_{j})
is not empty and has empty interior for all
1\leq i\leq n
,

•

{\rm span}_{R_{t}}(\left\{b_{I}\text{: }I\subseteq\left\{1,\dotsc,n\right\},\,\#\,I>1,\,(\bigcap_{i\in I}U_{i})\setminus(\bigcup_{j\notin I}U_{j})\neq\emptyset\right\})\neq R_{t}^{n}
, where
b_{I}\coloneq\sum_{i\in I}b_{i}
and
b_{i}
is the
i
-th standard basis vector in
R_{t}^{n}
(all components of
b_{i}
are zero except the
i
-th, which is
1
).

Theorem A

(see Theorem
4.2
)
.

Let
G
be a non-Hausdorff étale groupoid.

(I)

We have
J\cap\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)\neq\left\{0\right\}
if and only if
G
satisfies condition (
\mathcal{S}_{0}
).

(II)

Let
G
be ample and

a ring. We have
J_{R}\neq\left\{0\right\}
if and only if there exists
t\in{\rm ord}(R)
such that
G
satisfies condition (
\mathcal{S}_{t}
).

An immediate consequence of Theorem

is that, for non-Hausdorff ample groupoids

if and only if

. Theorem

immediately implies (and thus explains) the result in

Corollary B
.

Let

(I)

Let us turn to amenability and nuclearity. Recall that an étale groupoid
G
(possibly non-Hausdorff) is called topologically amenable if there exists a net
(\xi_{i})
in
\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)
with
\xi_{i}\geq 0
for all

such that

•

\sum_{g\in G^{x}}\xi_{i}(g)\leq 1
for all

and for all

,

•

\sum_{g\in G^{x}}\xi_{i}(g)\to 1
as

uniformly on compact subsets of
{G^{(0)}}
(with respect to

),

•

\sum_{h\in G^{r(g)}}|\xi_{i}(g^{-1}h)-\xi_{i}(h)|\to 0
as

uniformly on compact subsets of

(with respect to

).

Applied to an étale groupoid which is Hausdorff, such as the Hausdorff cover
\tilde{G}
of a non-Hausdorff étale groupoid

[

]
(note that

coincides with

in that case).

Theorem F

(See Theorems

,

We refer the reader to §

,

An important step towards our main results is a careful analysis of the Hausdorff cover
\tilde{G}
of a non-Hausdorff étale groupoid
G
. At a technical level, as we explain in Lemma
3.5
, the relationship between
G
and
\tilde{G}
is encoded in a canonical translation action of
G
on
\tilde{G}
(see
[
23
, Definition 4.16]
for the notion of translation actions, which are called algebraic morphisms in
[
5
]
). In a sense, the work in
[
36
]
was ahead of its time because the construction of essential groupoid
C^{*}
-algebras was not yet available at the time when
[
36
]
appeared. An important insight for our work is that the Hausdorff cover offers a new perspective on the essential groupoid
C^{*}
-algebra, justifying this new construction by showing that it is very natural. In spirit, the idea is very similar to the one behind the construction of essential covers in
[
2
]
.

The continuity properties of
\mathscr{C}_{c}
functions provided by viewing them as functions on the Hausdorff cover lead to a decomposition result (see Theorem
3.13
and Corollary
3.14
) which is crucial in the proof of Theorems
A
,
C
and
D
. Theorem
E
relies on the existence of a dense set of points where the canonical inclusion
\iota\colon G\hookrightarrow\tilde{G}
is continuous (and one-to-one). Theorem
F
relies on three ingredients: The dual picture for the Hausdorff cover
\tilde{G}
, allowing us to view elements in
C_{c}(\tilde{G})
as bounded Borel functions on the original groupoid
G
, together with the idea of extending representations in
[
7
, § 4]
(
[
30
]
and
[
24
, Appendix B]
contain similar ideas, and our improved version appears in Lemma
6.7
), and the insight in
[
33
]
that topological amenability is a Borel property.

The Hausdorff cover is not only a natural construction which leads to a new approach to non-Hausdorff étale groupoids, but importantly it is also computable. Indeed, by analysing the original non-Hausdorff groupoid, it is possible to describe the Hausdorff cover explicitly, as we demonstrate in a variety of examples arising from group bundles, groupoids of germs, as well as canonical actions of Thompson groups and self-similar groups (see §
7
). In particular, we construct a concrete non-Hausdorff étale groupoid
G
for which there cannot be a
∗
-homomorphism
C^{*}_{r}(G)\to C^{*}_{r}(\tilde{G})
which is injective in
K
-theory (see Example
7.5
). This means that even though the Hausdorff cover is helpful for studying many interesting properties of the underlying non-Hausdorff groupoid, in
K
-theory, we might lose information when going from
G
to
\tilde{G}
. Note that Example
7.5
shows that
G
and
\tilde{G}
(and also
G
and
\tilde{G}_{{\rm ess}}
) are not Morita equivalent in general.

2.
Preliminaries

We will use the following notations: If
G
is a topological space and
S\subseteq G
a subset, we write
S^{c}\coloneq G\setminus S
,
{\rm int\,}(S)
stands for the interior of
S
and
\overline{S}
for the closure of
S
. For a function
f
on
G
with values in an abelian group with identity element
0
, we let
{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f)\coloneq\left\{g\in G\text{: }f(g)\neq 0\right\}
be the strict support. We let
B_{b}(G)
denote the set of bounded Borel measurable functions
f\colon G\to\mathbb{C}
. The set of bounded Borel measurable functions
G\to\mathbb{C}
with strict support contained in a compact subset of
G
will be denoted by
B_{c}(G)
. The closure of
B_{c}(G)
in
B_{b}(G)
with respect to the uniform norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
will be denoted by
B_{0}(G)
. Given a locally compact Hausdorff space
\tilde{G}
, we denote by
C_{c}(\tilde{G})
the set of continuous functions
\tilde{G}\to\mathbb{C}
with compact support, and we let
C_{0}(\tilde{G})
denote the set of continuous functions
\tilde{G}\to\mathbb{C}
which vanish at infinity.

The focus of this paper will be groupoids, which are small categories whose morphisms are all invertible. As usual, we identify a groupoid with its set of morphisms
G
, and view its set of objects (also called units)
{G^{(0)}}
as a subset of
G
by identifying objects with the corresponding identity morphisms. By definition, our groupoid
G
comes with range and source maps
r\colon G\to{G^{(0)}}
,
s\colon G\to{G^{(0)}}
, a multiplication map

G\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 4.27432pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\times}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{s}}}{\mathop{}\kern 4.27432pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\times}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{s}}}{\mathop{}\kern 3.85072pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\times}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{s}}}{\mathop{}\kern 3.85072pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\times}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{s}}}\times_{r}G=\left\{(g_{1},g_{2})\text{: }s(g_{1})=r(g_{2})\right\}\to G,\,(g_{1},g_{2})\mapsto g_{1}g_{2}

and an inversion map
G\to G,\,g\mapsto g^{-1}
such that
r(g^{-1})=s(g)
,
s(g^{-1})=r(g)
,
gg^{-1}=r(g)
and
g^{-1}g=s(g)
. These structure maps satisfy a list of conditions so that
G
becomes a small category (see for instance
[
29
, Chapter I, Section 1]
). We will use the notations
G_{x}\coloneq s^{-1}(x)
,
G^{x}\coloneq r^{-1}(x)
and
G_{x}^{x}\coloneq r^{-1}(x)\cap s^{-1}(x)
for
x\in G^{(0)}
. Moreover, we write
{\rm Iso}(G)\coloneq\left\{g\in G\text{: }r(g)=s(g)\right\}
.

A topological groupoid is a groupoid
G
which is endowed with a topology such that range, source, multiplication and inversion maps are all continuous. Throughout this paper, a topological groupoid
G
will always be assumed to be locally compact, and the unit space
{G^{(0)}}\subseteq G
will always be assumed to be Hausdorff in the subspace topology. A topological groupoid
G
is called étale if the range map (and hence also the source map) is a local homeomorphism. In this case, the range map (and also the source map) is open, and it follows that
{G^{(0)}}
is an open subset of
G
. An open subset
U\subseteq G
is called an open bisection if the restricted range and source maps
r|_{U}\colon\>U\to r(U),\,g\mapsto r(g)
and
s|_{U}\colon U\to s(U),\,g\mapsto s(g)
are bijections. If
G
is étale, then
G
has a basis for its topology consisting of open bisections. A topological groupoid
G
is called ample if it is étale and its unit space
{G^{(0)}}
is totally disconnected. An étale groupoid is ample if and only if it has a basis for its topology consisting of compact open bisections. Let
G
be an étale groupoid, and let
\mathscr{B}
denote the set of open bisections in
G
. Every
U\in\mathscr{B}
is locally compact and Hausdorff with respect to the subspace topology coming from
G
, and
G=\bigcup_{U\in\mathscr{B}}U
.

Now
\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)\coloneq{\rm span}(\bigcup_{U\in\mathscr{B}}C_{c}(U))\subseteq B_{c}(G)
becomes a
∗
-algebra with respect to point-wise addition, involution
f^{*}(g)=\overline{f(g^{-1})}
and convolution
(f_{1}*f_{2})(g)=\sum_{(g_{1},g_{2})\,\in\,G\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 2.79686pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\times}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{s}}}{\mathop{}\kern 2.79686pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\times}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{s}}}{\mathop{}\kern 2.14063pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\times}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{s}}}{\mathop{}\kern 2.14063pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\times}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{s}}}\times_{r}G,\,g_{1}g_{2}\,=\,g}f_{1}(g_{1})f_{2}(g_{2})
for
g\in G
. Let us now explain the construction of reduced groupoid
C^{*}
-algebras. Given
x\in G^{(0)}
, let
\pi_{x}\colon\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)\to\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}(G_{x}))
denote the left regular representation corresponding to
x
, which is given by
\pi_{x}(f)(\xi)=f*\xi
for
f\in\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)
and
\xi\in\ell^{2}(G_{x})
, where
(f*\xi)(g)=\sum_{(g_{1},g_{2})\,\in\,G\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 2.79686pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\times}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{s}}}{\mathop{}\kern 2.79686pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\times}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{s}}}{\mathop{}\kern 2.14063pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\times}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{s}}}{\mathop{}\kern 2.14063pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\times}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{s}}}\times_{r}G,\,g_{1}g_{2}\,=\,g}f(g_{1})\xi(g_{2})
for
g\in G_{x}
. Then the reduced groupoid
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}_{r}(G)
of
G
is given by the completion of
\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)
with respect to the norm
\|f\|_{C^{*}_{r}(G)}=\sup_{x\in G^{(0)}}\|\pi_{x}(f)\|
. The full (or maximal, or universal) groupoid
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(G)
of
G
is given by the completion of
\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)
with respect to the norm
\|f\|_{C^{*}(G)}=\sup_{\pi}\|\pi(f)\|
, where the supremum is taken over all
∗
-algebra representations
\pi\colon\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)\to\mathcal{L}(H)
, for some Hilbert space
H
.

If
G
is an ample groupoid and
R
is a ring, then
RG\coloneq{\rm span}(\left\{c_{U}\text{: }c\in R,\,U\subseteq G\text{ compact open bisection}\right\}
is the associated Steinberg algebra, where
c_{U}
denotes the function
G\to R
which takes the value
c
on
U
and
0
everywhere else, and we take the linear span in the
R
-module of functions
G\to R
. The product in
RG
is given by convolution (the product of
c_{U}
and
d_{V}
is given by
(cd)_{UV}
). Note that the coefficient ring
R
is often assumed to be commutative and unital, which is not necessary for our purposes.

The following observation will be useful later on: Let
G
be an étale groupoid. Given
x\in G^{(0)}
and
f\in\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)
, define
\lambda_{x}(f)\coloneq\sum_{g\in G^{x}}f(g)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For every
f\in\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)
, the map
X\to\mathbb{C},\,x\mapsto\lambda_{x}(f)
is continuous.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the claim for
f\in C_{c}(U)
for some open bisection
U\in\mathscr{B}
. For such a function
f
, we have that
\lambda_{x}(f)=f((r|_{U})^{-1}(x))
if
x\in r(U)
and
\lambda_{x}(f)=0
if
x\notin r(U)
. As
r|_{U}
is a homeomorphism
U\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\sim$}}r(U)
, it follows that
r(U)\to\mathbb{C},\,x\mapsto\lambda_{x}(f)
lies in
C_{c}(r(U))
. This proves our claim.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Similarly, we have that, for every
f\in\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)
, the map
X\to\mathbb{C},\,x\mapsto\sum_{g\in G_{x}}f(g)
is continuous.

3.
Construction of the Hausdorff cover groupoid

Let
G
be a non-Hausdorff étale groupoid. In the following, we describe the Hausdorff cover
\tilde{G}
of
G
, which has been constructed in
[
36
]
(related constructions appeared in
[
19
,
37
]
).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\tilde{G}
be the closure of
G
in
\mathfrak{C}(G)\setminus\left\{\emptyset\right\}
in the Fell topology, where
\mathfrak{C}(G)
is the space of closed subsets of
G
.

By construction,
\tilde{G}
is Hausdorff. Elements of
\tilde{G}
are given by non-empty subsets
\bm{g}\subseteq G
for which there exist nets
(g_{i})
in
G
with the property that
\bm{g}
is the set of all limit points of
(g_{i})
and that all cluster points of
(g_{i})
are limit points. Here,
g\in G
is a limit point of
(g_{i})
if for every open set
U\subseteq G
with
g\in U
, there exists
i_{0}
such that
g_{i}\in U
for all
i\geq i_{0}
, and cluster points of
(g_{i})
are precisely the limit points of subnets of
(g_{i})
. A basis for the topology of
\tilde{G}
is given by sets of the form
\mathcal{U}(U_{i},K)\coloneq\left\{\bm{g}\in\tilde{G}\text{: }\bm{g}\cap U_{i}\neq\emptyset\ \forall\ 1\leq i\leq n,\,\bm{g}\cap K=\emptyset\right\}
, where the
U_{i}
run through all open subsets of
G
and
K
runs through all compact subsets of
G
.

Let us present an alternative, dual picture for
\tilde{G}
. Let
C^{*}(\mathscr{C}_{c}(G))\subseteq B_{0}(G)
be the smallest sub-
C^{*}
-algebra of
B_{0}(G)
containing
\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)
(involution and multiplication in
B_{0}(G)
are defined point-wise here).

Lemma 3.2
.

The map
\tilde{G}\to{\rm Spec\,}(C^{*}(\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)))
sending
\bm{g}\in\tilde{G}
to the character
\chi_{\bm{g}}
on
C^{*}(\mathscr{C}_{c}(G))
determined by
\chi_{\bm{g}}(f)=f(g)
if
f\in C_{c}(U)
and
g\in\bm{g}\cap U
, for
U\in\mathscr{B}
, is a homeomorphism. Its inverse sends a character
\chi\in{\rm Spec\,}(C^{*}(\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)))
to

\bm{g}_{\chi}\coloneq\left\{g\in G\text{: }\text{There is}\ U\in\mathscr{B}\text{ with }g\in U\text{ and }\chi|_{C_{c}(U)}=\operatorname{ev}_{g}\right\}
,
where
\operatorname{ev}_{g}
is the character given by
C_{c}(U)\to\mathbb{C},\,f\mapsto f(g)
.

The dual isomorphism
C_{0}(\tilde{G})\cong C^{*}(\mathscr{C}_{c}(G))
yields the embedding
\mathfrak{i}\colon\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)\hookrightarrow C^{*}(\mathscr{C}_{c}(G))\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\sim$}}C_{0}(\tilde{G})
, and we have

(1)

\mathfrak{i}(f)(\bm{g})=\sum_{g\,\in\,\bm{g}}f(g)

for all
\bm{g}\in\tilde{G}
.

Proof.

Continuity of
\bm{g}\mapsto\chi_{\bm{g}}
is straightforward. Given
\chi\in{\rm Spec\,}(C^{*}(\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)))
, the fact that
g_{\chi}
is in
\tilde{G}
follows from density of
\left\{\operatorname{ev}_{g}\text{: }g\in G\right\}
in
{\rm Spec\,}(C^{*}(\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)))
. Continuity of
\chi\mapsto\bm{g}_{\chi}
is straightforward once one notices that the basic open sets
\mathcal{U}(U_{i},K)
, where the
U_{i}
are open bisections and
K
is a finite union of compact bisections each
contained in an open bisection, form a basis for the topology on
\tilde{G}
. To see (
1
), observe that we have, for all
U\in\mathscr{B}
,
f\in C_{c}(U)
and
\bm{g}\in\tilde{G}
that
\chi_{\bm{g}}(f)=\sum_{g\,\in\,\bm{g}}f(g)
.
∎

In the following, we will make use of the above identification
\tilde{G}\cong{\rm Spec\,}(C^{*}(\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)))
without explicitly mentioning it. Let
\iota\colon G\hookrightarrow\tilde{G}
be the canonical inclusion sending an element
g
to the singleton set
\left\{g\right\}\in\tilde{G}
, so that the isomorphism
C_{0}(\tilde{G})\cong C^{*}(\mathscr{C}_{c}(G))
dual to the homeomorphism
\tilde{G}\cong{\rm Spec\,}(C^{*}(\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)))
from Lemma
3.2
is witnessed by the map
\tilde{f}\mapsto\tilde{f}\circ\iota
.

Because
G
is a groupoid, elements of
\tilde{G}
(and also
\tilde{G}
itself) have more structure: Choose an element
\dot{g}\in\bm{g}\in\tilde{G}
. Then it is easy to see that
r(g^{\prime})=r(\dot{g})
for all
g^{\prime}\in\bm{g}
, and similarly
s(g^{\prime})=s(\dot{g})
for all
g^{\prime}\in\bm{g}
. Moreover, given a net
(g_{i})
in
G
with
\bm{g}=\lim g_{i}
, the net
(s(g_{i}))
converges to
\tilde{s}(\bm{g})\coloneq\left\{g_{1}^{-1}g_{2}\text{: }g_{1},g_{2}\in\bm{g}\right\}
, and
\tilde{s}(\bm{g})
is a subgroup of
G_{s(\dot{g})}^{s(\dot{g})}
. Similarly,
(r(g_{i}))
converges to
\tilde{r}(\bm{g})\coloneq\left\{g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}\text{: }g_{1},g_{2}\in\bm{g}\right\}
, and
\tilde{r}(\bm{g})
is a subgroup of
G_{r(\dot{g})}^{r(\dot{g})}
. Our element
\bm{g}
can then be written as
\bm{g}=\dot{g}\cdot\tilde{s}(\bm{g})=\tilde{r}(\bm{g})\cdot\dot{g}
. Here and in the sequel, we denote by
g\cdot\bm{x}
the product of
\left\{g\right\}
and
\bm{x}
as subsets of
G
, i.e.,
g\cdot\bm{x}=\left\{gg_{x}\text{: }g_{x}\in\bm{x}\right\}
. Moreover, let
\tilde{G}^{(0)}
denote the closure of
G^{(0)}
in
\tilde{G}
. Then
\tilde{r}
and
\tilde{s}
define range and source maps
\tilde{G}\to\tilde{G}^{(0)}
. Note that
\tilde{s}^{-1}(\iota(G^{(0)}))=\iota(G)
and
\tilde{r}^{-1}(\iota(G^{(0)}))=\iota(G)
. Given two elements
\bm{g},\bm{h}\in\tilde{G}
, the product
\bm{g}\bm{h}
is only defined if
\bm{g}=g\cdot\bm{x}
and
\bm{h}=\bm{x}\cdot h
, for some composable
g,h\in G
and
\bm{x}\in\tilde{G}^{(0)}
, in which case
\bm{g}\bm{h}=(gh)\cdot\bm{y}
where
\bm{y}
is such that
\bm{h}=\bm{x}\cdot h=h\cdot\bm{y}
, i.e.,
\bm{y}=h^{-1}\cdot\bm{x}\cdot h
. Moreover, the inverse of
\bm{g}=g\cdot\bm{x}
is given by
\bm{g}^{-1}=\bm{x}\cdot g^{-1}=g^{-1}\cdot(g\cdot\bm{x}\cdot g^{-1})
. In this way,
\tilde{G}
becomes a locally compact groupoid.

Moreover, let us explain why
\tilde{G}
is étale if
G
is étale. Given
U\in\mathscr{B}
, consider the ideal
C^{*}(\mathscr{C}_{c}(G))_{U}\coloneq\overline{{\rm span}(C_{c}(U)\cdot C^{*}(\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)))}\subseteq C^{*}(\mathscr{C}_{c}(G))
and the open subset
\tilde{U}
of
\tilde{G}
corresponding to
{\rm Spec\,}(C^{*}(\mathscr{C}_{c}(G))_{U})\subseteq{\rm Spec\,}(C^{*}(\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)))
under the homeomorphism
\tilde{G}\cong{\rm Spec\,}(C^{*}(\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)))
from Lemma
3.2
. By construction,
\tilde{U}
is an open subset of
\tilde{G}
, and concretely we have
\tilde{U}=\left\{\bm{g}\in\tilde{G}\text{: }\bm{g}\cap U\neq\emptyset\right\}
. The inclusion
C_{0}(U)\subseteq C^{*}(\mathscr{C}_{c}(G))_{U}
induces a proper continuous surjection
\pi_{U}\colon\tilde{U}\twoheadrightarrow U
. Moreover, the inclusion
\iota\colon G\hookrightarrow\tilde{G}
restricts to
U\hookrightarrow\tilde{U}
. Now it follows that
\tilde{r}(\tilde{U})
is open and that
\tilde{r}
restricts to a homeomorphism
\tilde{U}\cong\tilde{r}(\tilde{U})
. The reason is that
\tilde{r}(\tilde{U})=\widetilde{r(U)}
is canonically homeomorphic to
{\rm Spec\,}(C^{*}(\mathscr{C}_{c}(G))_{r(U)})
, and that the restriction of
\tilde{r}
,
\tilde{r}_{\tilde{U}}\colon\tilde{U}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\sim$}}\tilde{r}(\tilde{U})=\widetilde{r(U)}
, is dual under Gelfand transform to the isomorphism
C^{*}(\mathscr{C}_{c}(G))_{r(U)}\cong C^{*}(\mathscr{C}_{c}(G))_{U},\,f\mapsto f\circ r
. It follows that
\tilde{U}
is an open bisection. Moreover, we have
\tilde{G}=\bigcup_{U\in\mathscr{B}}\tilde{U}
. It also follows that
\tilde{G}
is étale. In particular, continuity of the product map follows from
\tilde{U}\cdot\tilde{V}=\widetilde{UV}
and the properties of
\tilde{U}
,
\tilde{V}
mentioned above. Note that, with the notation we just introduced,
\tilde{G}^{(0)}
is canonically homeomorphic to
{\rm Spec\,}(C^{*}(\mathscr{C}_{c}(G))_{G^{(0)}})
.

From now on, we write
X\coloneq G^{(0)}
and
\tilde{X}\coloneq\tilde{G}^{(0)}
. Let
\prod(\mathscr{C}_{c}(G))\coloneq\left\{f_{1}\dotsm f_{n}\text{: }n\in\mathbb{N},\,f_{m}\in\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)\ \forall\ 1\leq m\leq n\right\}
.
The following is an immediate consequence of our construction.

Lemma 3.3
.

We have
C^{*}(\mathscr{C}_{c}(G))=\overline{{\rm span}}(\prod(\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)))
, where the closure is taken in
B_{0}(G)
with respect to
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\infty}
.

Corollary 3.4
.

If
X
is totally disconnected, then so is
\tilde{X}
.

Proof.

Since
X
is totally disconnected and
G
is étale,
{\rm span}(\left\{1_{U}\text{: }U\subseteq G\text{ compact open bisection}\right\})
is dense in
\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)
. Hence Lemma
3.3
implies that

C^{*}(\mathscr{C}_{c}(G))=\overline{{\rm span}}(\left\{1_{U_{1}}\dotsm 1_{U_{n}}\text{: }n\in\mathbb{N},\,U_{m}\subseteq G\text{ compact open bisection}\ \forall\ 1\leq m\leq n\right\}).

In particular, this shows that
C_{0}(\tilde{X})\cong C^{*}(\mathscr{C}_{c}(G))_{X}
is given by

\overline{{\rm span}}(\left\{1_{U_{0}}\cdot 1_{U_{1}}\dotsm 1_{U_{n}}\text{: }n\in\mathbb{N},\,U_{m}\subseteq G\text{ compact open bisection}\ \forall\ 0\leq m\leq n,\,U_{0}\subseteq X\right\})

and thus contains a dense
∗
-algebra of projections. This implies that
\tilde{X}
is totally disconnected.
∎

The following observation reduces the task of describing the Hausdorff cover
\tilde{G}
to the description of
\tilde{X}
and the projection
\pi\colon\tilde{X}\twoheadrightarrow X
, which sends
\bm{x}\in\tilde{X}
to
\pi(\bm{x})\coloneq r(g)=s(g)
, where
g\in\bm{x}
is arbitrary. Alternatively, we have
\left\{\pi(\bm{x})\right\}=\bm{x}\cap X
. Note that
\pi
coincides with
\pi_{X}
as introduced above. Now set
G\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 4.27432pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\times}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{s}}}{\mathop{}\kern 4.27432pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\times}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{s}}}{\mathop{}\kern 3.85072pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\times}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{s}}}{\mathop{}\kern 3.85072pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\times}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{s}}}\times_{\pi}\tilde{X}\coloneq\left\{(g,\bm{x})\in G\times\tilde{X}\text{: }s(g)=\pi(\bm{x})\right\}
, and define an equivalence relation
\sim
on
G\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 4.27432pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\times}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{s}}}{\mathop{}\kern 4.27432pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\times}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{s}}}{\mathop{}\kern 3.85072pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\times}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{s}}}{\mathop{}\kern 3.85072pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\times}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{s}}}\times_{\pi}\tilde{X}
by setting
(g,\bm{x})\sim(g^{\prime},\bm{x}^{\prime})
if and only if
\bm{x}=\bm{x}^{\prime}
and
g^{-1}g^{\prime}\in\bm{x}
. Note that
(g,\bm{x})\sim(g^{\prime},\bm{x})
if and only if
g\cdot\bm{x}=g^{\prime}\cdot\bm{x}
. We write
[g,\bm{x}]
for the equivalence class of
(g,\bm{x})\in G\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 4.27432pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\times}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{s}}}{\mathop{}\kern 4.27432pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\times}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{s}}}{\mathop{}\kern 3.85072pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\times}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{s}}}{\mathop{}\kern 3.85072pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\times}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{s}}}\times_{\pi}\tilde{X}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

The map
q\colon G\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 4.27432pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\times}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{s}}}{\mathop{}\kern 4.27432pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\times}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{s}}}{\mathop{}\kern 3.85072pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\times}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{s}}}{\mathop{}\kern 3.85072pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\times}}_{\mathmakebox[0pt][r]{s}}}\times_{\pi}\tilde{X}\twoheadrightarrow\tilde{G},\,(g,\bm{x})\mapsto g\cdot\bm{x}
is surjective, open and a local homeomorphism. Moreover,

\tilde{G}
via

if

, and this is a translation action

Proof.

Remark 3.6
.

Lemma

tells us that to describe
\tilde{G}

as a topological space and the canonical projection

Let us study compact sets in

and

Lemma 3.7
.

We have the following relationship between compact sets in
G

:

(i)

For every compact subset

.

(ii)

, and therefore

(i): Take a compact subset

. Then

is a compact subset of

, and we can choose a compact subset

.

(ii): Take a compact subset

. By compactness of

, we can find a finite cover

Lemma 3.8

(i)

.

(ii)

A function
\tilde{f}\in C_{c}(\tilde{G})

if and only if it is decomposable.

(iii)

Assume that

is ample, and let

be a ring. Let
\tilde{f}\in R\tilde{G}
be decomposable and set

. Assume that there exist

and a compact set

(iv)

is ample, and let

be a ring. A function
\tilde{f}\in R\tilde{G}

Proof.

Let us prove (i). Since
K
is compact, there are open subsets
U_{i}\subseteq V_{i}
such that the closure
\overline{U_{i}}^{V_{i}}
of
U_{i}
in
V_{i}
is compact (for all
1\leq i\leq n
) and we have
{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f)\subseteq U_{1}\cup\dotso\cup U_{n}
.

Let us now construct, inductively on

, functions
f_{i}\in C_{c}(V_{i})
, for
1\leq i\leq m
, such that
{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f-f_{1}-\dotso-f_{m})\subseteq U_{m+1}\cup\dotso\cup U_{n}
. Let
\check{V}_{1}\coloneq V_{1}\setminus(U_{2}\cup\dotso\cup U_{n})

is continuous on
\check{V}_{1}
. To see this, take a net
(g_{k})
in
\check{V}_{1}
converging to

, say to

on
\tilde{G}
. As
\tilde{f}
is decomposable, we have
\tilde{f}(\bm{g})=\sum_{g^{\prime}\,\in\,\bm{g}}\tilde{f}(\iota(g^{\prime}))=\sum_{g^{\prime}\,\in\,\bm{g}}f(g^{\prime})

converges to

Now take a compact subset
K_{1}\subseteq V_{1}
such that

. As

is continuous on the closed subset

, the Tietze extension theorem allows us to find a continuous function

on
\check{K}_{1}
, i.e.,
f^{\prime}\equiv f
on
\check{K_{1}}
. Let

and

and
f_{1}\equiv f
on
\check{V}_{1}
.

. If

, then

by construction, so that

. Now assume that

. Then

Lemma 4.1

Let

(i)

(iii)

Proof.

n
such that there exist open bisections
U_{1},\dotsc,U_{n}\subseteq G
,
f_{i}\in C_{c}(U_{i})
, and a function
\chi\in C_{c}(U)
satisfying
0\leq\chi\leq 1
and
\chi\equiv 1
on some non-empty open subset
V\subseteq U
, such that
f*\chi=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}
. Note that
f*\chi\neq 0
. Set
S_{i}\coloneq{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f_{i})
. Our first claim is that, by minimality of
n
, we have
|(f*\chi)(g)|\leq\varepsilon
whenever
g\in S_{i}\cap S_{j}
for
i\neq j
. Indeed, suppose that there are
i\neq j
and
g\in S_{i}\cap S_{j}
with
|(f*\chi)(g)|>\varepsilon
. Then
|f(g)|>\varepsilon
and
\chi(s(g))>0
. Choose
\chi^{\prime}\in C_{c}(s(S_{i}\cap S_{j})\cap{\rm supp}^{\circ}(\chi))
such that
0\leq\chi^{\prime}\leq 1
and
\chi^{\prime}\equiv 1
on some open set
W
containing
s(g)
. Then
f*\chi^{\prime}=(f*\chi)*(\chi^{-1}\chi^{\prime})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}*(\chi^{-1}\chi^{\prime})
. But both
f_{i}*(\chi^{-1}\chi^{\prime})
and
f_{j}*(\chi^{-1}\chi^{\prime})
lie in
C_{c}(S_{i}\cap S_{j})
, so that we reduced
n
, which is impossible. This proves our first claim. Our second claim is that
s(S_{\varepsilon}(f*\chi))
is nowhere dense in
X
. For each
i
, set
W_{i}\coloneq S_{\varepsilon}(f_{i})\cap(\bigcup_{j\neq i}S_{j})
. Since
S_{\varepsilon}(f)
has empty interior and
S_{\varepsilon}(f_{i})\setminus W_{i}\subseteq S_{\varepsilon}(f)
, and since each
S_{\varepsilon}(f_{i})
is open, we conclude that
S_{\varepsilon}(f_{i})\subseteq\overline{W_{i}}^{U_{i}}
. Hence
S_{\varepsilon}(f_{i})\setminus W_{i}\subseteq\partial_{U_{i}}W_{i}\coloneq\overline{W_{i}}^{U_{i}}\setminus W_{i}
. Since each
W_{i}
is open and contained in a compact subset of
U_{i}
,
\partial_{U_{i}}W_{i}
is compact and has empty interior. Moreover, by our first claim, we have
s(S_{\varepsilon}(f*\chi))=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}s(S_{\varepsilon}(f_{i})\setminus W_{i})\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}s(\partial_{U_{i}}W_{i})
. Thus
s(S_{\varepsilon}(f*\chi))
is nowhere dense, because it is contained in a finite union of closed sets with empty interior. This proves our second claim. But
V
satisfies
V\subseteq U\subseteq\overline{s(S_{\varepsilon}(f))}^{X}
and
V\cap s(S_{\varepsilon}(f))=V\cap s(S_{\varepsilon}(f*\chi))
. Since
V
is open, we have
V\subseteq\overline{s(S_{\varepsilon}(f*\chi))}^{X}
and therefore
V=\emptyset
by our second claim, which contradicts the assumption that
V
was non-empty. Overall, we conclude that
s(S_{\varepsilon}(f))
is nowhere dense in
X
. This proves (i). A similar argument (applied to
{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f)
instead of
S_{\varepsilon}(f)
for
f\in RG
) yields (iv).

Our third claim is that if
f\in C_{r}^{*}(G)
is such that
{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f)
has empty interior, then
s(S_{\varepsilon}(f))
is nowhere dense in
X
for all
\varepsilon>0
. Indeed, take
f_{\varepsilon/2}\in\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)
with
\left\|f-f_{\varepsilon/2}\right\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon/2
. Then
S_{\varepsilon/2}(f_{\varepsilon/2})\subseteq{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f)
has empty interior. By (i),
s(S_{\varepsilon/2}({f_{\varepsilon/2}}))
is nowhere dense in
X
. However,
S_{\varepsilon}(f)\subseteq S_{\varepsilon/2}(f_{\varepsilon/2})
, and hence
s(S_{\varepsilon}(f))
is nowhere dense in
X
. Now (ii) immediately follows because for every
f\in J
,
{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f)
has empty interior. To show (iii), as “
\Rightarrow
” is clear, it suffices to prove “
\Leftarrow
”. Let
f\in C_{r}^{*}(G)
be such that
{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f)
has empty interior. As

s({\rm supp}^{\circ}(f))=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}s(S_{1/n}(f))
, our third claim implies that
s({\rm supp}^{\circ}(f))
is the countable union of nowhere dense sets, and thus has empty interior by the Baire category theorem.
∎

Note that Lemma

in our situation.

4.1.
Complete characterisations of vanishing of singular ideals

Let us now establish a characterisation for vanishing of
J\cap\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)
as well as vanishing of
J_{R}
, for arbitrary rings
R
. We need the following notation: Given a ring
R
, we write
{\rm ord}(R)\coloneq\left\{{\rm ord}(x)\text{: }0\neq x\in R\right\}
, where for
0\neq x\in R
,
{\rm ord}(x)
is the smallest positive integer
t
such that
t\cdot x=0
in
R
, and
{\rm ord}(x)\coloneq 0
if no such
t
exists. Moreover, we set
R_{0}\coloneq\mathbb{Q}
and
R_{t}\coloneq\mathbb{Z}/t\mathbb{Z}
where
t
is an integer with
t>1
. Furthermore, given a non-negative integer
t\neq 1
, we say that an étale groupoid
G
satisfies condition (
\mathcal{S}_{t}
) if there exist a positive integer
n
and open bisections
U_{i}\subseteq G
for
1\leq i\leq n
such that

•

s(U_{i})=s(U_{j})
for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
,

•

U_{i}\setminus(\bigcup_{j\neq i}U_{j})
is not empty and has empty interior for all
1\leq i\leq n
,

•

{\rm span}_{R_{t}}(\left\{b_{I}\text{: }I\subseteq\left\{1,\dotsc,n\right\},\,\#\,I>1,\,(\bigcap_{i\in I}U_{i})\setminus(\bigcup_{j\notin I}U_{j})\neq\emptyset\right\})\neq R_{t}^{n}
, were
b_{I}\coloneq\sum_{i\in I}b_{i}
and
b_{i}
is the
i
-th standard basis vector in
R_{t}^{n}
(all components of
b_{i}
are zero except the
i
-th, which is
1
).

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
G
be a non-Hausdorff étale groupoid.

(i)

We have
J\cap\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)\neq\left\{0\right\}
if and only if
G
satisfies condition (
\mathcal{S}_{0}
).

(ii)

Let
G
be ample and
R
a ring. We have
J_{R}\neq\left\{0\right\}
if and only if there exists
t\in{\rm ord}(R)
such that
G
satisfies condition (
\mathcal{S}_{t}
).

Proof.

Let us prove (i). To see “
\Leftarrow
”, let
U_{i}
be as in condition (
\mathcal{S}_{0}
). Write

\mathbb{H}\coloneq{\rm span}_{\mathbb{C}}(\Big\{b_{I}\colon\ I\subseteq\left\{1,\dotsc,n\right\},\,\#\,I>1,\,(\bigcap_{i\in I}U_{i})\setminus(\bigcup_{j\notin I}U_{j})\neq\emptyset\Big\}).

Condition (
\mathcal{S}_{0}
) implies
\mathbb{H}\neq\mathbb{C}^{n}
(compare dimensions), so there exists a non-zero linear map
\omega\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
\mathbb{H}\subseteq{\rm ker}\,(\omega)
. For
1\leq i\leq n
, write
a_{i}\coloneq\omega(b_{i})
. Since
\omega
is not zero, there exists
i_{0}\in\left\{1,\dotsc,n\right\}
with
a_{i_{0}}\neq 0
. Let
W\coloneq s(U_{i})
(note that
W
does not depend on
i\in\left\{1,\dotsc,n\right\}
by assumption). Choose a function
\psi\in C_{c}(W)
such that
\psi(w_{0})=1
for some
w_{0}\in s(U_{i_{0}}\setminus(\bigcup_{j\neq i_{0}}U_{j}))
. For every
1\leq i\leq n
, let
f_{i}\coloneq a_{i}(\psi\circ s|_{U_{i}})
. Then
f_{i}\in C_{c}(U_{i})
. Set
f\coloneq\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}
. For the element
g_{0}\in U_{i_{0}}\setminus(\bigcup_{j\neq i_{0}}U_{j})
with
s(g_{0})=w_{0}
, we have
f(g_{0})=f_{i_{0}}(g_{0})=a_{i_{0}}\psi(w_{0})=a_{i_{0}}\neq 0
. This shows that
f\neq 0
. If
g\in\bigcup_{i\neq j\,\in\,\left\{1,\dotsc,n\right\}}U_{i}\cap U_{j}
, then there exists
I\subseteq\left\{1,\dotsc,n\right\}
with
\#\,I>1
such that
g\in(\bigcap_{i\in I}U_{i})\setminus(\bigcup_{j\notin I}U_{j})
. In that case,
b_{I}\in\mathbb{H}
and thus
f(g)=\sum_{i\in I}f_{i}(g)=(\sum_{i\in I}a_{i})\psi(s(g))=\omega(b_{I})\psi(s(g))=0
. This shows that
{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f)\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}(U_{i}\setminus(\bigcup_{j\neq i}U_{i}\cap U_{j}))
and hence
{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f)
has empty interior because the
U_{i}
are open. We conclude that
f\in J
by Lemma
4.1
(iii).

Let us prove “
\Rightarrow
”. Let
n
be the smallest positive integer for which there exist open bisections
V_{1},\dotsc,V_{n}
and
f_{i}\in C_{c}(V_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq n
such that
f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}
lies in
J
and
f\neq 0
. Note that
n>1
because for
n=1
,
{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f)
is open. Write
S_{i}\coloneq{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f_{i})
and let
K_{i}
be the closure of
S_{i}
in
V_{i}
. Then

(3)

S_{i}\setminus(\bigcup_{j\neq i}S_{j})\neq\emptyset\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq n,

because otherwise there exists
i
such that
{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f)\subseteq\bigcup_{j\neq i}K_{j}\subseteq\bigcup_{j\neq i}V_{j}
, and then Corollary
3.14
(i) would lead to a contradiction to minimality of
n
. Moreover, minimality of
n
implies that

(4)

f(g)=0\text{ for all }g\in S_{i}\cap S_{j}\text{ for }i\neq j.

If not, find
g^{\prime}\in S_{i}\cap S_{j}
for
i\neq j
such that
f(g^{\prime})\neq 0
and
\psi\in C_{c}(s(S_{i}\cap S_{j}))
such that
\psi(s(g^{\prime}))=1
. Set
f^{\prime}\coloneq f*\psi
. Then
f^{\prime}\in J
,
f^{\prime}(g^{\prime})\neq 0
and
f^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f^{\prime}_{i}
, where
f^{\prime}_{i}\coloneq f_{i}*\psi
. For every
1\leq k\leq n
, we have
S^{\prime}_{k}\coloneq{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f^{\prime}_{k})=S_{k}\cap s^{-1}({\rm supp}^{\circ}(\psi))
. Hence
S^{\prime}_{i}=S^{\prime}_{j}
, which contradicts (
3
). Furthermore, proceeding inductively on
1\leq k\leq n
, let us now construct
\psi_{k}\in C_{c}(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}s(S_{i}))
such that
f*\psi_{k}\neq 0
. For
k=1
, choose
g\in S_{1}\setminus(\bigcup_{j\neq 1}S_{j})
and
\psi_{1}\in C_{c}(s(S_{1}))
such that
\psi_{1}(s(g))=1
. Now assume that we have found
\psi_{k-1}
. Then
f^{\prime}=f*\psi_{k-1}
is a non-zero function in
J
with
f^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f^{\prime}_{i}
, where
f^{\prime}_{i}=f_{i}*\psi_{k-1}
. Set
S^{\prime}_{i}\coloneq{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f^{\prime}_{i})
, for
1\leq i\leq n
. We have
S^{\prime}_{i}\neq\emptyset
by minimality of
n
. Furthermore, (
3
) implies that
S^{\prime}_{k}\setminus(\bigcup_{j\neq k}S^{\prime}_{j})\neq\emptyset
, so that we can choose
g_{k}\in S^{\prime}_{k}\setminus(\bigcup_{j\neq k}S^{\prime}_{j})
and
\varphi_{k}\in C_{c}(s(S^{\prime}_{k}))
with
\varphi_{k}(s(g_{k}))=1
. Then set
\psi_{k}\coloneq\psi_{k-1}\varphi_{k}
. This concludes the construction. Thus, by replacing
f
by
f*\psi_{n}
, we arrange that the
S_{i}
from above satisfy
s(S_{i})=s(S_{j})
for all
i,j\in\left\{1,\dotsc,n\right\}
. Set
W\coloneq s(S_{i})
.

For
w\in W
and
1\leq i\leq n
, let
g_{i,w}
be the unique element of
S_{i}
with
s(g_{i,w})=w
. Consider the subspaces
\mathbb{H}_{f,w}\coloneq{\rm span}_{\mathbb{C}}(\left\{b_{I}\text{: }\sum_{i\in I}f_{i}(g_{i,w})=0\right\})
and
\mathbb{H}^{\prime}_{f,w}\coloneq\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}b_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\text{: }\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}f_{i}(g_{i,w})=0\right\}
. By construction, we have
\mathbb{H}_{f,w}\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{\prime}_{f,w}
. For every
g\in G
with
f(g)\neq 0
, we have
\mathbb{H}^{\prime}_{f,s(g)}\neq\mathbb{C}^{n}
and thus
\mathbb{H}_{f,s(g)}\neq\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Since
X\to\mathbb{C},\,w\mapsto\sum_{i\in I}f_{i}(g_{i,w})=\sum_{g\in G_{w}}(\sum_{i\in I}f_{i})(g)
is continuous by Remark
2.2
, the set
W_{I}\coloneq\left\{w\in X\text{: }\sum_{i\in I}f_{i}(g_{i,w})\neq 0\right\}
is an open subset of
W
. Set
\mathbb{I}_{w}\coloneq\left\{I\subseteq\left\{1,\dotsc,n\right\}\text{: }b_{I}\notin\mathbb{H}^{\prime}_{f,w}\right\}
. Then
W_{w}\coloneq\bigcap_{I\in\mathbb{I}_{w}}W_{I}
is an open neighbourhood of
w
such that
\mathbb{H}_{f,w^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathbb{H}_{f,w}
for all
w^{\prime}\in W_{w}
. Now take
g_{0}\in G
with
f(g_{0})\neq 0
and set
w_{0}\coloneq s(g_{0})
. Choose
\psi\in C_{c}(W_{w_{0}})
such that
\psi(w_{0})=1
. Replace
f
with
f^{\prime}\coloneq f*\psi
. We have
f^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f^{\prime}_{i}
, where
f^{\prime}_{i}=f_{i}*\psi
. Set
U_{i}\coloneq{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f^{\prime}_{i})=S_{i}\cap s^{-1}(\left\{w\in W_{w_{0}}\text{: }\psi(w)\neq 0\right\})
. Since
\sum_{g\in G_{w}}(\sum_{i\in I}f_{i}*\psi)(g)=(\sum_{g\in G_{w}}(\sum_{i\in I}f_{i})(g))\psi(w)
, we deduce that
\mathbb{H}_{f^{\prime},s(g)}=\mathbb{H}_{f,s(g)}\subseteq\mathbb{H}_{f,w_{0}}
for all
g\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}
.

We claim that these
U_{i}
have all the desired properties. We have
s(U_{i})={\rm supp}^{\circ}(\psi)=s(U_{j})
for all
i,j\in\left\{1,\dotsc,n\right\}
. Moreover,
U_{i}\setminus(\bigcup_{j\neq i}U_{j})
is not empty by (
3
). By construction,
(\bigcup_{i}U_{i})\setminus(\bigcup_{i\neq j}U_{i}\cap U_{j})
is contained in
{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f^{\prime})
. As
f^{\prime}\in J
, this shows that
U_{i}\setminus(\bigcup_{i\neq j}U_{i}\cap U_{j})
has empty interior for each
i
. Moreover, for every
I\subseteq\left\{1,\dotsc,n\right\}
with
\#\,I>1
and
g\in(\bigcap_{i\in I}U_{i})\setminus(\bigcup_{j\notin I}U_{j})
, we have
f^{\prime}(g)=0
by (
4
). Hence
0=f^{\prime}(g)=\sum_{i\in I}f^{\prime}_{i}(g)=\sum_{i\in I}f^{\prime}_{i}(g_{i,s(g)})
and thus
b_{I}\in\mathbb{H}_{f^{\prime},s(g)}\subseteq\mathbb{H}_{f,w_{0}}
. This shows that
{\rm span}_{\mathbb{C}}(\left\{b_{I}\text{: }I\subseteq\left\{1,\dotsc,n\right\},\,\#\,I>1,\,(\bigcap_{i\in I}U_{i})\setminus(\bigcup_{j\notin I}U_{j})\neq\emptyset\right\})\subseteq\mathbb{H}_{f,w_{0}}\neq\mathbb{C}^{n}
, as desired.

Let us now prove (ii). Given a ring
R
, let
A
be the underlying abelian group of
R
. Let
\mathscr{C}_{c}(G,A)\coloneq{\rm span}(\left\{c_{U}\text{: }c\in A,\,U\subseteq G\text{ compact open bisection}\right\})
and let
\mathscr{J}_{A}
be the subgroup of
\mathscr{C}_{c}(G,A)
of those functions
f\in\mathscr{C}_{c}(G,A)
with the property that
{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f)
has empty interior. Clearly, we have
J_{R}\cong\mathscr{J}_{A}
as abelian groups. In other words, whether or not we have
J_{R}\neq\left\{0\right\}
only depends on the underlying abelian group of
R
. As
A=\varinjlim A_{\rm fg}
, where
A_{\rm fg}
runs through all finitely generated subgroups of
A
(ordered by inclusion), we have
\mathscr{J}_{A}=\varinjlim\mathscr{J}_{A_{\rm fg}}
. Thus
\mathscr{J}_{A}\neq\left\{0\right\}
if and only if
\mathscr{J}_{A_{\rm fg}}\neq\left\{0\right\}
for some finitely generated subgroup
A_{\rm fg}
of
A
. Now every finitely generated subgroup of
A
is isomorphic to
\bigoplus_{l=1}^{L}\mathbb{Z}/t_{l}\mathbb{Z}
for some non-negative integer
L
and
t_{l}\in{\rm ord}(R)
. Moreover,
\mathscr{J}_{\bigoplus_{l=1}^{L}\mathbb{Z}/t_{l}\mathbb{Z}}
embeds into
\bigoplus_{l=1}^{L}\mathscr{J}_{\mathbb{Z}/t_{l}\mathbb{Z}}
. We conclude that
J_{R}\neq\left\{0\right\}
if and only if there exists
t\in{\rm ord}(R)
such that
J_{\mathbb{Z}/t\mathbb{Z}}\neq\left\{0\right\}
.

To prove (ii), it thus suffices to show that for a non-negative integer
t\neq 1
and the ring
R=\mathbb{Z}/t\mathbb{Z}
, we have
J_{R}\neq\left\{0\right\}
if and only if
G
satisfies condition (
\mathcal{S}_{t}
). For
t=0
, it is easy to see that
J_{\mathbb{Z}}\neq\left\{0\right\}
if and only if
J_{\mathbb{Q}}\neq\left\{0\right\}
, and the same argument as for (i) shows that
J_{\mathbb{Q}}\neq\left\{0\right\}
if and only if
G
satisfies condition (
\mathcal{S}_{0}
). Now consider the case
t>0
. That
J_{R}\neq\left\{0\right\}
implies that
G
satisfies condition (
\mathcal{S}_{t}
) follows by the same argument as for the corresponding implication in (i), but now using Corollary
3.14
(ii). It remains to show that if
G
satisfies condition (
\mathcal{S}_{t}
), then
J_{R}\neq\left\{0\right\}
, where
R=\mathbb{Z}/t\mathbb{Z}
. Define
\mathbb{H}
as in (i), but with
{\rm span}_{R}
instead of
{\rm span}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Then we have
\mathbb{H}\neq R^{n}
, and once we construct a non-zero
R
-linear map
\omega\colon R^{n}\to R
with
\mathbb{H}\subseteq{\rm ker}\,(\omega)
, the same argument as for “
\Leftarrow
” in (i) applies. We have
R^{n}/\mathbb{H}\cong\bigoplus_{m=1}^{M}\mathbb{Z}/\tau_{m}\mathbb{Z}
for some positive integer
M
and integers
\tau_{m}>1
dividing
t
. Set
\tau\coloneq\tau_{1}
and let
\omega
be the composition
R^{n}\twoheadrightarrow R^{n}/\mathbb{H}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\sim$}}\bigoplus_{m=1}^{M}\mathbb{Z}/\tau_{m}\mathbb{Z}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}/\tau\mathbb{Z}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Z}/t\mathbb{Z}=R
, where the first map is the canonical projection, the second map is an arbitrary isomorphism of abelian groups, the third map is given by the canonical projection and the fourth map is given by multiplication with
t/\tau
. Then
\omega
is clearly
R
-linear, and we have
\mathbb{H}\subseteq{\rm ker}\,(\omega)
as well as
\omega\neq 0
by construction.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

If

Theorem 4.7
.

Proof.

Take

\langle\overline{X}(x)\rangle
be the subgroup of

generated by
\overline{X}(x)
, and set

\#\,\langle\overline{X}(x)\rangle\leq m!.

To see this, enumerate
\overline{X}(x)=\left\{\gamma_{1},\dotsc,\gamma_{m}\right\}

\langle\gamma_{i}\rangle\coloneq\left\{\gamma_{i}^{\epsilon}\text{: }\epsilon\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}
for the cyclic group generated by

. Clearly, we have
\langle\gamma_{i}\rangle\subseteq\overline{X}(x)
. Now we claim that every
g\in\langle\overline{X}(x)\rangle
is of the form
g=h_{1}\dotso h_{\ell}
for some

with
h_{l}\notin\langle\gamma_{i_{k}}\rangle
for every
k\neq l
. Since the
h_{j}
have to be pairwise distinct elements of
\overline{X}(x)
, this will then imply
\#\,\langle\overline{X}(x)\rangle\leq m!
(and
\ell\leq m
). So it suffices to prove the claim. Given
g\in\langle\overline{X}(x)\rangle
, let
\ell
be the smallest positive integer such that
g
can be written as
g=h_{1}\dotso h_{\ell}
for some
h_{j}\in\langle\gamma_{i_{j}}\rangle
. Assume that there are

with
h_{l}\in\langle\gamma_{i_{k}}\rangle
. Set
h^{\prime}_{j}\coloneq h_{j}
for

,
h^{\prime}_{k}\coloneq h_{k}h_{l}
,
h^{\prime}_{j}\coloneq h_{l}^{-1}h_{j}h_{l}
for
k<j<l
and
h^{\prime}_{j}\coloneq h_{j+1}
for

. We have
h^{\prime}_{k}\in\langle\gamma_{i_{k}}\rangle
as well as
h^{\prime}_{j}\in h_{l}^{-1}\langle\gamma_{i_{j}}\rangle h_{l}=\langle h_{l}^{-1}\gamma_{i_{j}}h_{l}\rangle
for
k<j<l
, and
h_{l}^{-1}\gamma_{i_{j}}h_{l}\in\overline{X}(x)
because
\overline{X}(x)
is closed under conjugation by elements of
G_{x}^{x}
. Moreover, we have
g=h^{\prime}_{1}\dotsm h^{\prime}_{\ell-1}
by construction. However, this contradicts minimality of
\ell
. This concludes the proof of (
5
).

For
f\in C^{*}_{r}(G)
, write

S_{\varepsilon}(f)\coloneq\left\{g\in G\text{: }|f(g)|>\varepsilon\right\}.

Assume that
J\neq\left\{0\right\}
. Then we can find
f\in J
(i.e.,
s({\rm supp}^{\circ}(f))
has empty interior) with
f(g_{0})\neq 0
for some
g_{0}\in G
, and by modifying
f
if necessary, we can always arrange that
f(x_{0})\neq 0
for some
x_{0}\in X
. Let us fix
x_{0}
throughout the proof. Since
\langle\overline{X}(x_{0})\rangle
is finite by (
5
), we can enumerate
\langle\overline{X}(x_{0})\rangle=\left\{g_{1},\dotsc,g_{n}\right\}
(where
n\leq m!
). For each
1\leq i\leq n
, let
U_{i}
be an open bisection containing
g_{i}
, such that
s(U_{i})=s(U_{j})
for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
. Write
V\coloneq s(U_{i})
(this does not depend on
i
). Choose
\psi\in C_{c}(V)
such that
\psi(x_{0})=1
, and denote
\omega_{i}\coloneq f(g_{i})
. For every
1\leq i\leq n
, let
f^{\psi}_{i}\coloneq\omega_{i}(\psi\circ s|_{U_{i}})
so that
f^{\psi}_{i}\in C_{c}(U_{i})
. Set
f^{\psi}\coloneq\sum_{i=1}^{n}f^{\psi}_{i}
. Then it is clear that
f^{\psi}\in\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)\setminus\left\{0\right\}
. Let us show that
f^{\psi}
lies in
J
for some
\psi
.

Set
S_{i}\coloneq{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f_{i}^{\psi})
. Given
I\subseteq\left\{1,\dotsc,n\right\}
, set
\check{S}_{I}\coloneq(\bigcap_{i\in I}S_{i})\setminus(\bigcup_{j\notin I}S_{j})
as well as
W_{I}\coloneq{\rm int\,}\Big(\overline{s(\check{S}_{I})}^{X}\Big)
. We can modify
\psi
such that

(6)

W_{I}\neq\emptyset\text{ implies that }x_{0}\in\overline{W_{I}}^{X}\text{ for all }I\subseteq\left\{1,\dotsc,n\right\}.

Indeed, choose a regular open subset
S
of
X
(i.e.,
S={\rm int\,}\big(\overline{S}^{X}\big)
) such that
x_{0}\in S
and
S\cap\overline{W_{I}}^{X}=\emptyset
for all
I\subseteq\left\{1,\dotsc,n\right\}
with
W_{I}\neq\emptyset
and
x_{0}\notin\overline{W_{I}}^{X}
. Choose
\varphi\in C_{c}(S)
with
\varphi(x_{0})=1
, and consider
\dot{f}\coloneq f^{\psi}*\varphi=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}^{\psi}*\varphi=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}\big((\psi\varphi)\circ s|_{U_{i}}\big)=f^{\psi\varphi}
. Then
\dot{S}_{i}\coloneq{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f_{i}^{\psi}*\varphi)
is given by
\dot{S}_{i}=S_{i}\cap s^{-1}\big({\rm supp}^{\circ}(\varphi)\big)
, so that
(\dot{S}_{I})\check{\vrule height=5.59721pt,width=0.0pt}\coloneq(\bigcap_{i\in I}\dot{S}_{i})\setminus(\bigcup_{j\notin I}\dot{S}_{j})
satisfies
(\dot{S}_{I})\check{\vrule height=5.59721pt,width=0.0pt}=\check{S}_{I}\cap s^{-1}\big({\rm supp}^{\circ}(\varphi)\big)
. Therefore, with
\dot{W}_{I}\coloneq{\rm int\,}\Big(\overline{s((\dot{S}_{I})\check{\vrule height=5.59721pt,width=0.0pt}\,)}^{X}\Big)
, we have
\dot{W}_{I}={\rm int\,}\Big(\overline{s(\check{S}_{I})\cap{\rm supp}^{\circ}(\varphi)}^{X}\Big)\subseteq{\rm int\,}\Big(\overline{s(\check{S}_{I})}^{X}\cap\overline{S}^{X}\Big)\subseteq W_{I}\cap{\rm int\,}\big(\overline{S}^{X}\big)=W_{I}\cap S=\emptyset
whenever
x_{0}\notin\overline{W_{I}}^{X}
, and
x_{0}\notin\overline{W_{I}}^{X}
if and only if
x_{0}\notin\overline{\dot{W}_{I}}^{X}
.

For each
x\in V
, let
g_{x}^{i}
be the unique element in
U_{i}
satisfying
s(g_{x}^{i})=x
. We claim that the map
\chi\colon V\to\mathbb{C}
given by

(7)

\chi(x)\coloneq\sum_{i=1}^{n}|f(g_{x}^{i})-f^{\psi}(g_{x}^{i})|

is continuous at
x_{0}
. Let
(x_{\alpha})
be a net in
V
converging to
x_{0}
. In order to prove that
\lim_{\alpha}\chi(x_{\alpha})=\chi(x_{0})
, it suffices to find a subnet
(x_{\beta})
satisfying
\lim_{\beta}\chi(x_{\beta})=\chi(x_{0})
(since
(x_{\alpha})
is arbitrary). As the canonical projection
\pi\colon\tilde{X}\twoheadrightarrow X
is proper, there exists a subnet
(x_{\beta})
for which the net
(\iota(x_{\beta}))
converges in
\tilde{G}
(i.e., in the Fell topology). For each
1\leq i\leq n
let
g_{\beta}^{i}
be the unique element of
U_{i}
such that
s(g_{\beta}^{i})=x_{\beta}
. Then the net
(\iota(g_{\beta}^{i}))
also converges in
\tilde{G}
, say to the limit
\bm{g}_{i}
. Note that
\bm{g}_{i}\subseteq\langle\overline{X}(x_{0})\rangle
. We have

\lim_{\beta}f(g_{\beta}^{i})=\big(C_{r}^{*}(\mathfrak{i})(f)\big)(\bm{g}_{i})=\sum_{g\in\bm{g}_{i}}f(g)=\sum_{g\in\bm{g}_{i}}f^{\psi}(g)=\mathfrak{i}(f^{\psi})(\bm{g}_{i})=\lim_{\beta}f^{\psi}(g_{\beta}^{i})\ \ \ \ \ \ \text{for each }1\leq i\leq n.

Here we used Lemma
3.12
for the second and Theorem
3.13
(ii) for the fourth equality. Therefore

\lim_{\beta}\chi(x_{\beta})=\lim_{\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|f(g_{\beta}^{i})-f^{\psi}(g_{\beta}^{i})|=0=\chi(x_{0}).

This concludes the proof that the map
\chi
from (
7
) is continuous at
x_{0}
. Set
\mathcal{I}\coloneq\left\{I\subseteq\left\{1,\dotsc,n\right\}\text{: }W_{I}\neq\emptyset\right\}
. We claim that

(8)

\sum_{i\in I}f(g_{i})=0\text{ for all }I\in\mathcal{I}.

To see this, take an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
. By continuity of
\chi
at
x_{0}
, there exists an open neighbourhood
W_{\varepsilon}
of
x_{0}
such that
|f(g)-f^{\psi}(g)|<\varepsilon/2
whenever

. In particular,
W_{\varepsilon}\cap\overline{s(S_{\varepsilon}(f^{\psi}))}^{X}\subseteq\overline{W_{\varepsilon}\cap s(S_{\varepsilon}(f^{\psi}))}^{X}\subseteq\overline{s(S_{\varepsilon/2}(f))}^{X}
has empty interior by Lemma

(ii). Now set
\check{W}_{I}\coloneq(W_{\varepsilon}\cap W_{I})\setminus\overline{s(S_{\varepsilon}(f^{\psi}))}^{X}
for each

. Then
\check{W}_{I}
is open and dense in
W_{\varepsilon}\cap W_{I}
because
W_{\varepsilon}\cap\overline{s(S_{\varepsilon}(f^{\psi}))}^{X}
has empty interior. Therefore
x_{0}
lies in the closure of
\check{W}_{I}
in
X
by (
6
). As
s(\check{S}_{I})
is dense in
W_{I}
and because
\check{W}_{I}
is open, we conclude that there is a net
(x_{\lambda})
in
\check{W}_{I}\cap s(\check{S}_{I})
converging to
x_{0}
in
X
. Let
g_{\lambda}
be the unique element of
\check{S}_{I}
such that
s(g_{\lambda})=x_{\lambda}
.
By construction, we have that
(\iota(g_{\lambda}))
converges to
\bm{g}=\left\{g_{i}\text{: }i\in I\right\}
in
\tilde{G}
(i.e., in the Fell topology). Therefore,

\lim_{\lambda}f^{\psi}(g_{\lambda})=\lim_{\lambda}\mathfrak{i}(f^{\psi})(\iota(g_{\lambda}))=\mathfrak{i}(f^{\psi})(\bm{g})=\sum_{i\in I}\mathfrak{i}(f^{\psi})(\iota(g_{i}))=\sum_{i\in I}f^{\psi}(g_{i})=\sum_{i\in I}f(g_{i}).

For the third equality, we used that
\mathfrak{i}(f^{\psi})
is decomposable by Theorem
3.13
(ii). Now
s(g_{\lambda})\in\check{W}_{I}
implies that
g_{\lambda}\notin S_{\varepsilon}(f^{\psi})
, so that
\lvert f^{\psi}(g_{\lambda})\rvert\leq\varepsilon
. We conclude that

\big|\sum_{i\in I}f(g_{i})\big|\leq\varepsilon
. However,
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, and therefore (
8
) holds.

Let us now show that
f^{\psi}\in J
. By the definition of
f^{\psi}
, for
I\in\mathcal{I}
and

we have
f^{\psi}(g)=\sum_{i\in I}f_{i}^{\psi}(g)=\sum_{i\in I}\omega_{i}\psi(s(g))=\sum_{i\in I}f(g_{i})\psi(s(g))=0
using (

). It follows that

and therefore

(here
\mathcal{P}
stands for power set). Hence

has empty interior because it is contained in a finite union of nowhere dense sets (

is nowhere dense because

for

). This shows

and thus
J\cap\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)\neq\left\{0\right\}
, as desired.
∎

4.2.
Description of the singular ideal and sufficient vanishing criteria

In this subsection, we assume that
G
can be covered by countably many open bisections. Let us now describe the singular ideal of
C^{*}_{r}(G)
using the Hausdorff cover
\tilde{G}
. As above, we write
X\coloneq G^{(0)}
and
\tilde{X}\coloneq\tilde{G}^{(0)}
. Recall that we constructed a proper continuous surjection
\pi\colon\tilde{X}\twoheadrightarrow X
characterised by
\left\{\pi(\bm{x})\right\}=\bm{x}\cap X
as well as a canonical (non-continuous) inclusion
\iota\colon X\hookrightarrow\tilde{X}
. Now we define the set of dangerous points
D
as
D\coloneq\pi(\tilde{X}\setminus\iota(X))
. Note that
D=s(\overline{X}\setminus X)
, where
\overline{X}
is the closure of
X
in
G
. We also set
C\coloneq X\setminus D
. Note that
C
is precisely the set of points where
\iota\colon X\hookrightarrow\tilde{X}
is continuous, and
\iota(C)
is precisely the set of points in
\tilde{X}
where
\pi
is one-to-one. Moreover,
[
21
, Lemma 7.15]
implies that
C
is dense in
X
. As explained in
[
21
, § 7]
, the singular ideal
J
of
C^{*}_{r}(G)
can be equivalently described as the set of those
f\in C^{*}_{r}(G)
which satisfy
{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f)\subseteq r^{-1}(D)=s^{-1}(D)
. The essential
C^{*}
-algebra of
G
is then defined as
C^{*}_{\rm ess}(G)=C^{*}_{r}(G)/J
.

Definition 4.12
.

Define
\tilde{X}_{\rm sing}\coloneq{\rm int\,}(\pi^{-1}(D))\subseteq\tilde{X}
and
\tilde{X}_{\rm ess}\coloneq\tilde{X}\setminus\tilde{X}_{\rm sing}
.

By construction,
\tilde{X}_{\rm ess}
coincides with the closure of
\iota(C)
in
\tilde{X}
, i.e.,
\tilde{X}_{\rm ess}=\overline{\iota(C)}\subseteq\tilde{X}
. It is straightforward to see that
\pi^{-1}(D)
is
\tilde{G}
-invariant. Hence
\tilde{X}_{\rm sing}
is an open invariant subset of
\tilde{X}
, and thus
\tilde{X}_{\rm ess}
is a closed invariant subset of
\tilde{X}
. Also note that the canonical projection
\pi\colon\>\tilde{X}\twoheadrightarrow X
restricts to a surjective map
\tilde{X}_{\rm ess}\twoheadrightarrow X
. This follows from the observation that
C\subseteq\pi(\tilde{X}_{\rm ess})
and that
C
is dense in
X
.

Definition 4.13
.

Define
\tilde{G}_{\rm sing}\coloneq\tilde{G}_{\tilde{X}_{\rm sing}}^{\tilde{X}_{\rm sing}}=\left\{\bm{g}\in\tilde{G}\text{: }\tilde{r}(\bm{g})\in\tilde{X}_{\rm sing},\,\tilde{s}(\bm{g})\in\tilde{X}_{\rm sing}\right\}
and
\tilde{G}_{\rm ess}\coloneq\tilde{G}_{\tilde{X}_{\rm ess}}^{\tilde{X}_{\rm ess}}=\tilde{G}\setminus\tilde{G}_{\rm sing}
.

Note that
\tilde{G}_{\rm sing}
is an open subgroupoid of
\tilde{G}
and
\tilde{G}_{\rm ess}
is a closed subgroupoid of
\tilde{G}
. Moreover,
\tilde{G}_{\rm ess}
is the closure of
\iota(G_{C}^{C})
in
\tilde{G}
, and
G_{C}^{C}
are precisely the set of points where the canonical inclusion
\iota\colon G\hookrightarrow\tilde{G}
is continuous.

Definition 4.14
.

Define
\tilde{J}\coloneq\left\{\tilde{f}\in C^{*}_{r}(\tilde{G})\text{: }{\rm supp}^{\circ}(\tilde{f})\subseteq\tilde{G}_{\rm sing}\right\}
.

In other words,
\tilde{J}
is the ideal of
C^{*}_{r}(\tilde{G})
consisting of those
\tilde{f}\in C^{*}_{r}(\tilde{G})
with the property that
\tilde{f}|_{\tilde{G}_{\rm ess}}=0
. Hence, by construction, we have a canonical isomorphism
C^{*}_{r}(\tilde{G})/\tilde{J}\cong C^{*}_{r}(\tilde{G}_{\rm ess})
given by restriction to
\tilde{G}_{\rm ess}
(and we do not need exactness here).

The following is our description of the singular ideal
J
in terms of
\tilde{G}
.

Proposition 4.15
.

If
G
can be covered by countably many open bisections, then
C^{*}_{r}(\mathfrak{i})(J)=C^{*}_{r}(\mathfrak{i})(C^{*}_{r}(G))\cap\tilde{J}
.

Proof.

As mentioned above, the ideal
J
consists of precisely those
f\in C^{*}_{r}(G)
which are non-zero only in points of
s^{-1}(D)
. In other words, a function
f\in C^{*}_{r}(G)
lies in
J
if and only if
\iota^{-1}({\rm supp}^{\circ}(\tilde{f}))\subseteq s^{-1}(D)
, where
\tilde{f}\coloneq C^{*}_{r}(\mathfrak{i})(f)
. We claim that this is equivalent to the statement that
{\rm supp}^{\circ}(\tilde{f})\subseteq\tilde{s}^{-1}(\pi^{-1}(D))
. Indeed, if the latter holds, and we are given
g\in G
with
\tilde{f}(\iota(g))\neq 0
, then
\iota(g)\in\tilde{s}^{-1}(\pi^{-1}(D))
, and thus
s(g)=\pi(\iota(s(g)))=\pi(\tilde{s}(\iota(g)))\in D
. Conversely, suppose that
\iota^{-1}({\rm supp}^{\circ}(\tilde{f}))\subseteq s^{-1}(D)
, and take
\bm{g}\in{\rm supp}^{\circ}(\tilde{f})
. If
\tilde{s}(\bm{g})\notin\pi^{-1}(D)
, then
\tilde{s}(\bm{g})\in\pi^{-1}(C)=\iota(C)
(because
\pi
is one-to-one on
\iota(C)
). But then
\bm{g}
lies in
\iota(s^{-1}(C))
, which contradicts
\iota^{-1}({\rm supp}^{\circ}(\tilde{f}))\subseteq s^{-1}(D)
.

Now
\tilde{f}
is a actually a continuous function on
\tilde{G}
because
\tilde{G}
is Hausdorff. Thus our statement that
{\rm supp}^{\circ}(\tilde{f})\subseteq\tilde{s}^{-1}(\pi^{-1}(D))
is equivalent to
{\rm supp}^{\circ}(\tilde{f})\subseteq{\rm int\,}(\tilde{s}^{-1}(\pi^{-1}(D)))=\tilde{s}^{-1}({\rm int\,}(\pi^{-1}(D)))=\tilde{G}_{\rm sing}
, as desired.
∎

Remark 4.16
.

Let us now explain the analogue of Proposition
4.15
in the algebraic setting. Let
G
be an ample groupoid which can be covered by countably many open bisections. In that case, the singular ideal
J_{R}
of
RG
(see for instance
[
9
,
34
,
35
]
) can be equivalently described as the set of
f\in RG
with the property that
{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f)\subseteq r^{-1}(D)=s^{-1}(D)
. Now define
\tilde{J}_{R}\coloneq\left\{\tilde{f}\in R\tilde{G}\text{: }{\rm supp}^{\circ}(\tilde{f})\subseteq\tilde{G}_{\rm sing}\right\}
. Similar arguments as for Proposition
4.15
show that
\mathfrak{i}_{R}(J_{R})=\mathfrak{i}_{R}(RG)\cap\tilde{J}_{R}
.

Proposition
4.15
immediately gives the following sufficient condition for vanishing of
J
in terms of
\tilde{G}
.

Corollary 4.17
.

Assume that
G
can be covered by countably many open bisections, and that
\tilde{X}_{\rm sing}=\emptyset
, i.e.,
\pi^{-1}(D)
has empty interior in
\tilde{X}
. Then the singular ideal
J
vanishes. If
G
is ample, then
J_{R}
vanishes for any ring
R
.

In
[
9
]
, another sufficient condition for vanishing of the singular ideal was given in the case of non-Hausdorff ample groupoids. Namely, it is shown in
[
9
]
that for a non-Hausdorff ample groupoid
G
, the singular ideal vanishes if all compact open subsets of
G
are regular open. Recall that an open subset
U
is called regular open if
U={\rm int}\big(\overline{U}\big)
. It turns out that our sufficient conditions are equivalent for non-Hausdorff ample groupoids.

Lemma 4.18
.

Let
G
be ample. All compact open subsets of
G
are regular open if and only if
{\rm int\,}(\pi^{-1}(D))=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Let us prove “
\Rightarrow
”. Suppose that all compact open subsets of
G
are regular open, but that
{\rm int\,}(\pi^{-1}(D))\neq\emptyset
. As
\iota(X)
is dense in
\tilde{X}
, we can find a compact open bisection
U
and some compact open set
K
such that
\bm{U}\coloneq\left\{\bm{g}\in\tilde{G}\text{: }\bm{g}\cap U\neq\emptyset,\,\bm{g}\cap K=\emptyset\right\}
is contained in
\pi^{-1}(D)
. We claim that
U\cap\big(\overline{K}\big)^{c}\neq\emptyset
. Indeed,
\bm{U}
is non-empty, so contains
\iota(x)
for some
x\in X
. If
x\notin\overline{K}
, then we are done. Suppose that
x\in\overline{K}
. Since
K
is regular open by assumption and
x\notin K
, we conclude that
x\notin{\rm int}\big(\overline{K}\big)
. In other words,
x\in\big({\rm int}\big(\overline{K}\big)\big)^{c}=\overline{\big(\overline{K}\big)^{c}}
. Hence there exists a net
(x_{i})
in
\big(\overline{K}\big)^{c}
with
\lim x_{i}=x
. As
x\in U
it follows that
x_{i}\in U
eventually. We conclude that
\emptyset\neq U\cap\big(\overline{K}\big)^{c}\subseteq\iota^{-1}(\bm{U})\subseteq\iota^{-1}(\pi^{-1}(D))
. As
C
is dense in
X
, we must have
C\cap(U\cap\big(\overline{K}\big)^{c})\neq\emptyset
. But
\iota(C)\cap\pi^{-1}(D)=\emptyset
, a contradiction.

Now let us show “
\Leftarrow
”. Assume that
K
is a compact open subset of
G
such that
K
is not regular open, i.e.,
U\coloneq{\rm int}\big(\overline{K}\big)
properly contains
K
. It follows that
U\setminus K
is non-empty, but has empty interior. Consider
\bm{U}\coloneq\left\{\bm{g}\in\tilde{G}\text{: }\bm{g}\cap U\neq\emptyset,\,\bm{g}\cap K=\emptyset\right\}
. Then
\bm{U}
is non-empty, and since
U\setminus K
has empty interior,
\iota^{-1}(\bm{U})\subseteq U\setminus K
must have empty interior. Hence
\bm{U}\cap\iota(G_{C}^{C})=\emptyset
and thus
\tilde{s}(\bm{U})\subseteq\pi^{-1}(D)
. Thus
{\rm int\,}(\pi^{-1}(D))\neq\emptyset
, as desired.
∎

Remark 4.19
.

Lemma
4.18
, together with the example of the groupoid attached to the canonical self-similar action of the Grigorchuk group in
[
9
, § 5.6]
, immediately implies that our sufficient condition in Corollary
4.17
is not necessary for vanishing of the singular ideal. In Example
7.16
, we concretely work out
{\rm int\,}(\pi^{-1}(D))
in this case.

Let us also relate our sufficient condition to a condition in
[
28
]
. In
[
28
, Proposition 1.12]
, Neshveyev and Schwartz introduce the set
D_{0}
of extremely dangerous points and show that if
D_{0}=\emptyset
, then the singular ideal vanishes. Recall that
D_{0}
is the set of points
x\in X
with the following property: There exist elements
g_{1},\dotsc,g_{n}\in G_{x}^{x}\setminus\left\{x\right\}
, open bisections
U_{1},\dotsc,U_{n}
and an open neighbourhood
U\subseteq X
of
x
such that
g_{k}\in U_{k}
for all
1\leq k\leq n
and
U\setminus(U_{1}\cup\dotso\cup U_{n})
has empty interior. Note that
D_{0}\subseteq D
. The condition
D_{0}=\emptyset
turns out to be equivalent to our sufficient condition that
{\rm int\,}(\pi^{-1}(D))=\emptyset
. This means that Corollary
4.17
explains
[
28
, Proposition 1.12]
.

Lemma 4.20
.

Assume that
G
can be covered by countably many open bisections. We have
\iota(X)\cap({\rm int\,}(\pi^{-1}(D)))=\iota(D_{0})
. Moreover,
{\rm int\,}(\pi^{-1}(D))=\emptyset
if and only if
D_{0}=\emptyset
.

Proof.

To prove
\iota(X)\cap({\rm int\,}(\pi^{-1}(D)))\subseteq\iota(D_{0})
, take
x\in X
with
\iota(x)\in{\rm int\,}(\pi^{-1}(D))
. Then there are open sets
V_{i}\subseteq G
and a compact set
K\subseteq G
such that there exists
i
with
V_{i}\subseteq X
, and

\iota(x)\in\mathcal{U}(V_{i},K)\coloneq\left\{\bm{x}\in\tilde{X}\text{: }\bm{x}\cap V_{i}\neq\emptyset\ \forall\ i,\,\bm{x}\cap K=\emptyset\right\}\subseteq\pi^{-1}(D).

Then
x\in(\bigcap_{i}V_{i})\setminus K
. Let
V\coloneq\bigcap_{i}V_{i}
. Then
V\subseteq X
,
x\in V
and
V\setminus K\subseteq D
has empty interior. Moreover, for every
y\in K
, we can find an open bisection
V_{y}\subseteq G
such that
y\in V_{y}
and, if
r(y)\neq x
or
s(y)\neq x
, then there is an open set
U_{y}\subseteq X
with
x\in U_{y}
and
V_{y}\cap U_{y}=\emptyset
, and if
r(y)=x=s(y)
, then
x\notin V_{y}
. As
K
is compact, we can cover
K
by finitely many such
V_{y}
, say
K\subseteq\bigcup_{j\in F}V_{y_{j}}
(where
F
is a finite index set). Set
\check{F}\coloneq\left\{j\in F\text{: }r(y_{j})\neq x\text{ or }s(y_{j})\neq x\right\}
and
F_{x}\coloneq F\setminus\check{F}
. Set
U\coloneq V\cap\bigcap_{j\in\check{F}}U_{y_{j}}
. Then
U\subseteq X
is an open neighbourhood of
x
. Moreover,
U\cap K\subseteq\bigcup_{j\in F_{x}}V_{y_{j}}
and thus
U\setminus(\bigcup_{j\in F_{x}}V_{y_{j}})\subseteq U\setminus K
has empty interior. Comparing this with the definition of
D_{0}
as recalled above, we see that
x\in D_{0}
, as desired.

Let us prove
{\rm int\,}(\pi^{-1}(D))\supseteq\iota(D_{0})
. Take
x\in D_{0}
. First of all, note that we can always arrange that the bisections
U_{k}
in the definition of
D_{0}
to satisfy
r(U_{k})=U
for all
1\leq k\leq n
(just replace
U
by
\bigcap_{k}r(U_{k})
and then
U_{k}
by
r^{-1}(U)\cap U_{k}
). Now choose an open set
V
and a compact set
K
such that
x\in V\subseteq K\subseteq U
, and set

K_{k}\coloneq r^{-1}(K)\cap U_{k}
. Clearly,
K_{k}
are compact sets. By construction,
\iota(x)\in\mathcal{U}(V,\bigcup_{k}K_{k})\coloneq\left\{\bm{g}\in\tilde{G}\text{: }\bm{g}\cap V\neq\emptyset,\,\bm{g}\cap(\bigcup_{k}K_{k})=\emptyset\right\}
. We claim that
\mathcal{U}(V,\bigcup_{k}K_{k})\subseteq\pi^{-1}(D)
. Indeed, if not, then there would exist
c\in C
with
\iota(c)\in\mathcal{U}(V,\bigcup_{k}K_{k})
. As
\iota\colon X\hookrightarrow\tilde{X}
is continuous at
c
, it follows that there exists an open set
W\subseteq X
with
c\in W
,
W\subseteq V
and
W\cap(\bigcup_{k}K_{k})=\emptyset
. But this is impossible because
V\setminus(\bigcup_{k}K_{k})=V\setminus(\bigcup_{k}U_{k})
has empty interior. Therefore, we conclude that
\iota(x)\in{\rm int\,}(\pi^{-1}(D))
, as desired.

The second claim follows from the first because
\iota(X)
is dense in
\tilde{X}
.
∎

Let us formulate another sufficient condition for vanishing of singular ideals, which applies to cases where
{\rm int\,}(\pi^{-1}(D))\neq\emptyset
. The following is now an immediate consequence of Proposition
4.15
and Lemma
3.12
.

Corollary 4.21
.

Let
G
be a non-Hausdorff étale groupoid and
\tilde{G}
its Hausdorff cover. Assume that
G
can be covered by countably many open bisections.
Suppose that
G
has the property that
\#\,\bm{g}<\infty
for all
\bm{g}\in\tilde{G}
, and that for every decomposable function
\tilde{f}\colon\tilde{G}\to\mathbb{C}
,
\tilde{f}(\bm{g})=0
for all
\bm{g}\in\tilde{G}_{{\rm ess}}
implies that
\tilde{f}(\bm{g})=0
for all
\bm{g}\in\tilde{G}
. Then
J=\left\{0\right\}
.

Corollary
4.21
can be used to explain why for the groupoid attached to the Grigorchuk group (see Example
7.16
), we have
J=\left\{0\right\}
even though
{\rm int\,}(\pi^{-1}(D))\neq\emptyset
.

5.
The ideal intersection property

In the following, let
G
be a non-Hausdorff étale groupoid and
\tilde{G}
its Hausdorff cover. As above, we set
X\coloneq{G^{(0)}}
and
\tilde{X}\coloneq\tilde{G}^{(0)}
. We assume throughout this section that
G
can be covered by countably many open bisections. Let us start with a few simple observations.

Lemma 5.1
.

G
is minimal if and only if
\tilde{G}_{\rm ess}
is minimal. Moreover,
G
is topologically transitive if and only if
\tilde{G}_{\rm ess}
is topologically transitive.

Here
G
is called topologically transitive if there exists
x\in X
such that the orbit
G.x
is dense in
X
.

Proof.

Let
x\in X
be arbitrary, and choose
\bm{x}\in\tilde{X}_{\rm ess}
with
\pi(\bm{x})=x
. Suppose that
\tilde{G}_{\rm ess}.\bm{x}
is dense in
\tilde{X}
. Then we claim that
G.x
is dense in
X
. Indeed, we have
X=\pi(\tilde{X}_{\rm ess})=\pi\Big(\overline{\tilde{G}_{\rm ess}.\bm{x}}\Big)\subseteq\overline{G.x}
. Conversely, take
\bm{x}\in\tilde{X}_{\rm ess}
and suppose that
x\coloneq\pi(\bm{x})
has dense orbit in
G
, i.e.,
X=\overline{G.x}
. Then for every
y\in C
, there exists a net
g_{i}.x
converging to
y
. As
\pi
is proper, we may after passing to a subnet assume that
g_{i}.\bm{x}
converges to some
\bm{y}\in\tilde{X}_{\rm ess}
. It follows that
\pi(\bm{y})=y\in C
and thus
\bm{y}=\iota(y)
(because
\pi
is one-to-one on
\iota(C)
). This shows that
\iota(C)\subseteq\overline{\tilde{G}_{\rm ess}.\bm{x}}
, and thus,
\overline{\tilde{G}_{\rm ess}.\bm{x}}=\tilde{X}_{\rm ess}
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

The following are equivalent:

(i)

G
is topologically principal,

(ii)

G
is topologically free,

(iii)

\tilde{G}_{\rm ess}
is topologically principal,

(iv)

\tilde{G}_{\rm ess}
is topologically free,

(v)

\tilde{G}_{{\rm ess}}
is effective.

Recall that
G
is topologically principal if
\left\{x\in X\text{: }G_{x}^{x}=\left\{x\right\}\right\}
is dense in
X
,
G
is topologically free if for every open bisection
U\subseteq G\setminus X
,
{\rm int\,}(s(U\cap{\rm Iso}(G)))=\emptyset
, and
G
is effective if
{\rm int\,}({\rm Iso}(G))=X
.

Proof.

Since
G
can be covered by countably many open bisections, the same is true for
\tilde{G}_{{\rm ess}}
, and thus “(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii)” and “(iii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iv)” follow from
[
21
, Proposition 2.24]
. “(iv)
\Leftrightarrow
(v)” follows from
[
21
, Lemma 2.23]
as
\tilde{G}
is Hausdorff. Let us show “(i)
\Rightarrow
(iv)”. If
G
is topologically principal, then for every
y\in C
there exists a net
(x_{i})
converging to
y
in
X
such that
G_{x_{i}}^{x_{i}}=\left\{x_{i}\right\}
. Take
\bm{x}_{i}\in\tilde{X}_{\rm ess}
with
\pi(\bm{x}_{i})=x_{i}
. Then, since
\pi
is proper, we may assume after passing to a subnet that
(\bm{x}_{i})
converges in
\tilde{X}_{\rm ess}
, and the limit has to be
\iota(y)
. Moreover,
G_{x_{i}}^{x_{i}}=\left\{x_{i}\right\}
implies that
\tilde{G}_{\bm{x}_{i}}^{\bm{x}_{i}}=\left\{\bm{x}_{i}\right\}
. As
\iota(C)
is dense in
\tilde{X}_{\rm ess}
, we conclude that
\tilde{G}_{\rm ess}
is topologically free. Finally, let us prove “(v)
\Rightarrow
(ii)”. If
G
is not topologically free, then there exists an open bisection
U\subseteq G
with
U\cap X=\emptyset
and there exists a non-empty open subset
V\subseteq X
such that
V\subseteq s(U\cap{\rm Iso}(G))
. Define
\bm{U}\coloneq\left\{\bm{g}\in\tilde{G}_{\rm ess}\text{: }\bm{g}\in\tilde{U},\,\pi(\tilde{s}(\bm{g}))\in V\right\}
. We claim that
\bm{U}\not\subseteq\tilde{X}_{\rm ess}
and
\bm{U}\subseteq{\rm Iso}(\tilde{G}_{\rm ess})
. For the first claim, take
g\in U\cap{\rm Iso}(G)
with
s(g)\in V
. Then
g\notin X
as
U\cap X=\emptyset
. It follows that
\iota(g)\in\bm{U}
, and
\iota(g)\notin\tilde{X}
as
g\notin X
. To show the second claim, take
\bm{g}\in\bm{U}
. As
\iota(U)
is dense in
\tilde{U}
, we can find a net
(g_{i})
in
U
such that
(\iota(g_{i}))
converges to
\bm{g}
, and without loss of generality we may assume that
\pi(\tilde{s}(\iota(g_{i})))\in V
for all
i
. As
\pi(\tilde{s}(\iota(g_{i})))=\pi(\iota(s(g_{i})))=s(g_{i})
, we conclude that
s(g_{i})\in V
. Since
U
is a bisection, it follows that
g_{i}\in{\rm Iso}(G)
and hence
\iota(g_{i})\in{\rm Iso}(\tilde{G}_{\rm ess})
. We conclude that
\bm{g}\in{\rm Iso}(\tilde{G}_{\rm ess})
, as desired. This shows that
\tilde{G}_{\rm ess}
is not effective.
∎

Our goal now is to prove that
C_{0}(X)\subseteq C^{*}_{\rm ess}(G)
has the ideal intersection property if and only if
C_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\rm ess})\subseteq C^{*}_{r}(\tilde{G}_{\rm ess})
has the ideal intersection property. Recall that
C_{0}(X)\subseteq C^{*}_{\rm ess}(G)
has the ideal intersection property if for every ideal
I
of
C^{*}_{\rm ess}(G)
,
I\cap C_{0}(X)=\left\{0\right\}
implies that
I=\left\{0\right\}
(and similarly for
C_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\rm ess})\subseteq C^{*}_{r}(\tilde{G}_{\rm ess})
). We start with some preparations. In the following, we will view
C_{0}(X)
as a subalgebra of
C_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\rm ess})
and
C^{*}_{\rm ess}(G)
as a subalgebra of
C^{*}_{r}(\tilde{G}_{\rm ess})
using the canonical embedding
C^{*}_{r}(\mathfrak{i})
. Moreover, recall that
C\subseteq X
are those points such that the canonical projection
\pi\colon\tilde{X}\twoheadrightarrow X
is one-to-one on
\iota(C)
.

Lemma 5.3
.

For all
x\in C
,
\operatorname{ev}_{x}\colon C_{0}(X)\to\mathbb{C},\,f\mapsto f(x)
has a unique extension (as a state) from
C_{0}(X)
to
C_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\rm ess})
, and that extension is given by
\operatorname{ev}_{\iota(x)}\colon C_{0}(\tilde{X}_{{\rm ess}})\to\mathbb{C},\,\tilde{f}\mapsto\tilde{f}(\iota(x))
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\tilde{\varphi}
is a state on
C_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\rm ess})
extending
\operatorname{ev}_{x}
. Then
\tilde{\varphi}
must vanish on the maximal ideal
C_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\rm ess}\setminus\left\{\iota(x)\right\})
. It follows that
\tilde{\varphi}
is given by evaluation at
x\in\tilde{X}_{\rm ess}
.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

We have
C^{*}_{r}(\tilde{G})=\overline{{\rm span}}(C^{*}_{r}(G)C_{0}(\tilde{X}))=\overline{{\rm span}}(C_{0}(\tilde{X})C^{*}_{r}(G))
. Moreover, we also have
C^{*}_{r}(\tilde{G}_{\rm ess})=\overline{{\rm span}}(C^{*}_{\rm ess}(G)C_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\rm ess}))=\overline{{\rm span}}(C_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\rm ess})C^{*}_{\rm ess}(G))
.

Proof.

Certainly, the first claim implies the second. So it suffices to prove the first claim. Let
U\subseteq G
be an open bisection. Consider the open bisection
\tilde{U}=\left\{\bm{g}\in\tilde{G}\text{: }\bm{g}\cap U\neq\emptyset\right\}
. Since
\tilde{G}=\bigcup_{U\in\mathscr{B}}\tilde{U}
, it suffices to prove that
C_{c}(\tilde{U})\subseteq C_{0}(\tilde{X})C_{c}(U)
and
C_{c}(\tilde{U})\subseteq C_{c}(U)C_{0}(\tilde{X})
.

Let
\pi_{U}\colon\tilde{U}\twoheadrightarrow U
be the proper continuous surjection from §
3
. Take
f\in C_{c}(\tilde{U})
with
\overline{{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f)}=\tilde{K}
. Set
K\coloneq\pi_{U}(\tilde{K})
. Then
K
is a compact subset of
U
. Choose
e\in C_{c}(U)
such that
e\equiv 1
on
K
. Define
f_{s}
as the composition
\tilde{s}(\tilde{U})\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\sim$}}\tilde{U}\to\mathbb{C}
, where the first map is the inverse of
\tilde{s}|_{\tilde{U}}
, and the second map is
f
. View
f_{s}
as an element of
C_{0}(\tilde{X})
. Then
f=e*f_{s}
. Similarly, define
f_{r}
as the composition
\tilde{r}(\tilde{U})\cong\tilde{U}\to\mathbb{C}
, where the first map is the inverse of
\tilde{r}|_{\tilde{U}}
, and the second map is
f
. View
f_{r}
as an element of
C_{0}(\tilde{X})
. Then
f=f_{r}*e
.
∎

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
G
be a non-Hausdorff étale groupoid which can be covered by countably many open bisections.
C_{0}(X)\subseteq C^{*}_{\rm ess}(G)
has the ideal intersection property if and only if
C_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\rm ess})\subseteq C^{*}_{r}(\tilde{G}_{\rm ess})
has the ideal intersection property.

Proof.

Write
A\coloneq C^{*}_{\rm ess}(G)
and
\tilde{A}\coloneq C^{*}_{r}(\tilde{G}_{\rm ess})
. Let us first assume that
C_{0}(X)\subseteq A
has the ideal intersection property. Take an ideal
\tilde{I}
of
\tilde{A}
, and assume that
\tilde{I}\cap C_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\rm ess})=\left\{0\right\}
. Then
\tilde{I}\cap C_{0}(X)=\left\{0\right\}
. Thus the ideal intersection property for
C_{0}(X)\subseteq A
implies
\tilde{I}\cap A=\left\{0\right\}
. This means that the embedding
A\hookrightarrow\tilde{A}
induces an embedding
A\hookrightarrow\tilde{A}/\tilde{I}
. Let
x\in C
be arbitrary and
\varphi_{x}=\left\langle\pi_{x}(\cdot)\delta_{x},\delta_{x}\right\rangle
be the state on
A
induced from
\operatorname{ev}_{x}
on
C_{0}(X)
, where
\pi_{x}
is the left regular representation of
A
attached to
x
. Extend
\varphi_{x}
to a state
\tilde{\varphi}_{x}
on
\tilde{A}/\tilde{I}
. Then
\tilde{\varphi}_{x}|_{C_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\rm ess})}
extends
\varphi_{x}|_{C_{0}(X)}=\operatorname{ev}_{x}
. Lemma
5.3
implies that
\tilde{\varphi}_{x}|_{C_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\rm ess})}
is given by evaluation at
\iota(x)
. In particular,
C_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\rm ess})
lies in the multiplicative domain of
\tilde{\varphi}_{x}
. We claim that this implies that
\tilde{\varphi}_{x}=\left\langle\tilde{\pi}_{\iota(x)}(\cdot)\tilde{\delta}_{\iota(x)},\tilde{\delta}_{\iota(x)}\right\rangle
, where
\tilde{\pi}_{\iota(x)}
is the left regular representation of
\tilde{A}
attached to
\iota(x)
. By Lemma
5.4
, it suffices to check this equality for products of the form
a*\tilde{f}
where
a\in A
and
\tilde{f}\in C_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\rm ess})
. And indeed, we have

\tilde{\varphi}_{x}(a*\tilde{f})=\tilde{\varphi}_{x}(a)\tilde{\varphi}_{x}(\tilde{f})=\varphi_{x}(a)\tilde{f}(\iota(x))=\left\langle\tilde{\pi}_{\iota(x)}(a*\tilde{f})\tilde{\delta}_{\iota(x)},\tilde{\delta}_{\iota(x)}\right\rangle.

This shows that
\tilde{I}\subseteq{\rm ker}\,(\tilde{\pi}_{\iota(x)})
for all
x\in C
. But since
\iota(C)
is dense in
\tilde{X}_{\rm ess}
, we have that
\bigcap_{x\in C}{\rm ker}\,(\tilde{\pi}_{\iota(x)})=\left\{0\right\}
. Hence
\tilde{I}=\left\{0\right\}
, as desired.

Now suppose that
C_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\rm ess})\subseteq\tilde{A}
has the ideal intersection property. Take an ideal
I
of
A
with
I\cap C_{0}(X)=\left\{0\right\}
. Let
x\in C
be arbitrary. Extend
\operatorname{ev}_{x}
to a state
\varphi_{x}
from
C_{0}(X)
to
A/I
. Let
\dot{\varphi}_{x}
be the composition
A\twoheadrightarrow A/I\to\mathbb{C}
, where the first map is the canonical projection and the second map is given by
\varphi_{x}
. Now extend
\dot{\varphi}_{x}
further to a state
\tilde{\varphi}_{x}
on
\tilde{A}
. Observe that
\tilde{\varphi}_{x}|_{C_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\rm ess})}
extends
\dot{\varphi}_{x}|_{C_{0}(X)}=\operatorname{ev}_{x}
. Hence Lemma
5.3
implies that
\tilde{\varphi}_{x}|_{C_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\rm ess})}
is given by evaluation at
\iota(x)
. In particular,
C_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\rm ess})
lies in the multiplicative domain of
\tilde{\varphi}_{x}
. Define
\tilde{I}\coloneq\left\{a\in\tilde{A}\text{: }\tilde{\varphi}_{x}(\tilde{A}a\tilde{A})=0\ \forall\ x\in C\right\}
. Clearly,
\tilde{I}
is an ideal of
\tilde{A}
. We claim that the following hold: (i)
I\subseteq\tilde{I}
and (ii)
\tilde{I}\cap C_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\rm ess})=\left\{0\right\}
.

Let us prove (i). Take an element
a\in I
. In order to show that
a\in\tilde{I}
, it suffices to show that
\tilde{\varphi}_{x}(\tilde{f}_{1}*a^{\prime}*a*a^{\prime\prime}*\tilde{f}_{2})=0
for all
a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime}\in A
and
\tilde{f}_{1},\tilde{f}_{2}\in C_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\rm ess})
because of Lemma
5.4
. And indeed, we have

\tilde{\varphi}_{x}(\tilde{f}_{1}*a^{\prime}*a*a^{\prime\prime}*\tilde{f}_{2})=\tilde{\varphi}_{x}(\tilde{f}_{1})\tilde{\varphi}_{x}(a^{\prime}*a*a^{\prime\prime})\tilde{\varphi}_{x}(\tilde{f}_{2})=\tilde{\varphi}_{x}(\tilde{f}_{1})\dot{\varphi}_{x}(a^{\prime}*a*a^{\prime\prime})\tilde{\varphi}_{x}(\tilde{f}_{2})=0.

Here we used that
C_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\rm ess})
lies in the multiplicative domain of
\tilde{\varphi}_{x}
for the first equality. The last equality follows because
a^{\prime}aa^{\prime\prime}\in I
.

Now we prove (ii). Suppose that
\tilde{f}\in C_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\rm ess})
lies in
\tilde{I}
. Then
\tilde{\varphi}_{x}(\tilde{f})=0
for all
x\in C
, i.e.,
\tilde{f}(\iota(x))=0
for all
x\in C
. But
C
is dense in
\tilde{X}_{\rm ess}
, and
\tilde{f}
is continuous. Hence
\tilde{f}=0
, as desired.

Now (ii) implies that
\tilde{I}=\left\{0\right\}
since
C_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\rm ess})\subseteq\tilde{A}
has the ideal intersection property. And (i) implies that
I=\left\{0\right\}
, as desired.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

Theorem
5.5
together with Lemmas
5.1
and
5.2
explain results on simplicity of essential
C^{*}
-algebras of non-Hausdorff étale groupoids in
[
9
,
14
,
21
]
.

Let us now explain the connection between our results and the ones in
[
18
]
, where characterisations of the ideal intersection property for groupoid
C^{*}
-algebras were obtained using Hamana-Furstenberg boundary groupoids. In the following, let
G
be a non-Hausdorff étale groupoid which can be covered by countably many open bisections, let
X=G^{(0)}
, and let
\tilde{G}
be the Hausdorff cover of
G
with
\tilde{X}=\tilde{G}^{(0)}
. Let
\pi\colon\tilde{X}\twoheadrightarrow X
be the canonical projection and
\iota\colon X\hookrightarrow\tilde{X}
the canonical inclusion. Furthermore, let
\partial X
be the Hamana-Furstenberg boundary of
G
(see
[
18
, § 4]
for details), which comes with an
G
-action
G\curvearrowright\partial X
and anchor map
\mathfrak{q}\colon\partial X\to X
, and set
\partial G\coloneq G\ltimes\partial X
. The induced map
\partial G\twoheadrightarrow G
will be denoted by
\mathfrak{q}
as well. Let
\widetilde{\partial G}
be the Hausdorff cover of
\partial G
and set
\widetilde{\partial X}\coloneq\widetilde{\partial G}^{(0)}
. Let
\pi_{\partial}\colon\widetilde{\partial X}\twoheadrightarrow\partial X
be the canonical projection and
\iota_{\partial}\colon\partial X\hookrightarrow\widetilde{\partial X}
the canonical inclusion. Finally, let
\partial\tilde{X}
be the Hamana-Furstenberg boundary of
\tilde{G}
and
\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}\colon\partial\tilde{X}\to\tilde{X}
the anchor map for the
\tilde{G}
-action
\tilde{G}\curvearrowright\partial\tilde{X}
, and set
\partial\tilde{G}\coloneq\tilde{G}\ltimes\partial\tilde{X}
.

Proposition 5.7
.

Projection onto the
G
-coordinate gives rise to a continuous surjective map
\mathfrak{p}\colon\widetilde{\partial X}\twoheadrightarrow\tilde{X}
sending
\left\{(g,y)\text{: }g\in\bm{x}\right\}\subseteq\partial G
to
\bm{x}\subseteq G
. For every
\bm{y}\in\widetilde{\partial X}
, we have a group isomorphism

. Moreover,

\bm{y}=\left\{(g_{x},y)\text{: }g_{x}\in\bm{x}\right\}

Proof.

It is straightforward to see that the map

is well-defined. To show continuity, take open sets

and a compact set

and

∎

Now let

be as above, and define

.

We have

∗

∎

Amenability and nuclearity

Let us first recall some definitions. Throughout this section, let
G
be a non-Hausdorff étale groupoid with Hausdorff cover
\tilde{G}
, and let
\iota\colon G\hookrightarrow\tilde{G}
be the canonical inclusion. Let
X\coloneq G^{(0)}
and
\tilde{X}\coloneq\tilde{G}^{(0)}
. Moreover, for
x\in X
and a function
\xi
on
G
, recall that
\lambda_{x}(\xi)=\sum_{g\in G^{x}}\xi(g)
(and
\tilde{\lambda}_{\bm{x}}
is defined similarly, for
\bm{x}\in\tilde{X}
). Here we only consider
\xi
for which the sum on the right hand side converges absolutely.

Definition 6.1
.

G
is called
\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)
-amenable if there exists a net
(\xi_{i})
in
\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)
with
\xi_{i}\geq 0
for all
i
such that

(i)

\lambda_{x}(\xi_{i})\leq 1
for all
x\in X
and for all
i
,

(ii)

\lambda_{x}(\xi_{i})\to 1
as
i\to\infty
uniformly on compact subsets of
X
(with respect to
x\in X
),

(iii)

\sum_{h\in G^{r(g)}}|\xi_{i}(g^{-1}h)-\xi_{i}(h)|\to 0
as
i\to\infty
uniformly on compact subsets of
G
(with respect to
g\in G
).

Definition 6.2
.

G
is called
C_{c}(\tilde{G})
-amenable if there exists a net
(\eta_{i})
in
C_{c}(\tilde{G})
with
\eta_{i}\geq 0
for all
i
such that the net
(\xi_{i})
given by
\xi_{i}\coloneq\eta_{i}\circ\iota
satisfies (i), (ii) and (iii) from Definition
6.1
.

Definition 6.3
.

\tilde{G}
is called topologically amenable if there exists a net
(\eta_{i})
in
C_{c}(\tilde{G})
with
\eta_{i}\geq 0
for all
i
such that

(i)

\tilde{\lambda}_{\bm{x}}(\eta_{i})\leq 1
for all
\bm{x}\in\tilde{X}
and for all
i
,

(ii)

\tilde{\lambda}_{\bm{x}}(\eta_{i})\to 1
as
i\to\infty
uniformly on compact subsets of
\tilde{X}
(with respect to
\bm{x}\in\tilde{X}
),

(iii)

\sum_{\bm{h}\in\tilde{G}^{r(\bm{g})}}|\eta_{i}(\bm{g}^{-1}\bm{h})-\eta_{i}(\bm{h})|\to 0
as
i\to\infty
uniformly on compact subsets of
\tilde{G}
(with respect to
\bm{g}\in\tilde{G}
).

If
G
is
\sigma
-compact, then so is
\tilde{G}
and we can replace nets by sequences in Definitions
6.1
,
6.2
and
6.3
(and condition (i) in Definitions
6.1
,
6.2
and
6.3
is not necessary in this case). To define Borel amenability, for technical reasons we only consider sequences, following
[
33
]
.

Definition 6.4
.

G
is called Borel amenable if there exists a sequence
(\xi_{i})
of Borel functions on
G
with
\xi_{i}\geq 0
for all
i
such that

(i)

\lambda_{x}(\xi_{i})\leq 1
for all
x\in X
and for all
i
,

(ii)

\lambda_{x}(\xi_{i})\to 1
as
i\to\infty
for all
x\in X
,

(iii)

\sum_{h\in G^{r(g)}}|\xi_{i}(g^{-1}h)-\xi_{i}(h)|\to 0
as
i\to\infty
for all
g\in G
.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
G
be a non-Hausdorff étale groupoid. Consider the following statements:

(i)

G
is
\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)
-amenable.

(ii)

G
is
C_{c}(\tilde{G})
-amenable.

(iii)

\tilde{G}
is topologically amenable.

(iv)

G
is Borel amenable.

We always have (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii). If
G
is
\sigma
-compact, then we also have (iii)
\Rightarrow
(iv)
\Rightarrow
(i).

Proof.

To see “(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)”, let
\eta_{i}\coloneq\mathfrak{i}(\xi_{i})
. Then
\xi_{i}=\eta_{i}\circ\iota
by construction. Hence
(\eta_{i})
is a net satisfying the conditions in Definition
6.2
.

“(ii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii)” follows by continuity of
\eta_{i}
(viewed as functions on
\tilde{G}
) and Lemma
3.7
.

Now assume that
G
is
\sigma
-compact.

Let us prove “(iii)
\Rightarrow
(iv)”. As mentioned above, we now may replace nets by sequences in (iii). Now (iv) follows from (iii) because for every function
\eta\in C_{c}(\tilde{G})
,
\eta\circ\iota
is a bounded Borel function on
G
.

“(iv)
\Rightarrow
(i)” follows from
[
33
, Theorem 2.14]
.
∎

Now we turn to the relationship between amenability and nuclearity. For étale groupoids which are Hausdorff, it is well known that amenability of such a groupoid is equivalent to nuclearity of its reduced groupoid
C^{*}
-algebra (see
[
1
, Chapter 6]
and also
[
4
, Theorem 5.6.18]
). For non-Hausdorff groupoids, we have the following result, whose proof follows the one of
[
4
, Theorem 5.6.18]
.

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
G
be a non-Hausdorff étale groupoid. If
C^{*}_{r}(G)
is nuclear, then
G
is
C_{c}(\tilde{G})
-amenable.

If
G
can be covered by countably many open bisections and
C^{*}_{{\rm ess}}(G)
is nuclear, then
\tilde{G}_{{\rm ess}}
is topologically amenable.

Proof.

We construct a net
(\zeta_{i})
of non-negative functions in the subalgebra of
B_{0}(G)
generated by
\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)
such that
\left\|\zeta_{i}\right\|_{\ell^{2}(G_{x})}^{2}\leq 1
for all
x\in G^{(0)}
, and
(\zeta_{i}^{*}*\zeta_{i})(g)\rightarrow 1
uniformly on compact subsets of
G
(with respect to
g\in G
). Defining
\xi_{i}(g)\coloneq\zeta_{i}(g^{-1})^{2}
(for all
g\in G)
, the net
(\xi_{i})
will satisfy the conditions of Definition
6.1
. The image of the net
(\xi_{i})
in
C_{0}(\tilde{G})
under the canonical isomorphism
C_{0}(\tilde{G})\cong C^{*}(\mathscr{C}_{c}(G))
will lie in
C_{c}(\tilde{G})
by Lemma
3.8
.

Let
K\subseteq G
be compact,
\varepsilon>0
. Let
U_{1},\dotsc,U_{m}
be an open covering of
K
such that, for every
l
, there exists an open bisection
V_{l}
containing a compact superset of
U_{l}
. Let
\underline{\pi}\coloneq\bigoplus_{x\in X}\pi_{x}\colon\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)\rightarrow C_{r}^{*}(G)
denote the left regular representation. Let
f_{l}\in C_{c}(V_{l})\subseteq\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)
be such that
0\leq f_{l}\leq 1
and
f_{l}=1
on
U_{l}
. Then
\left\|\underline{\pi}(f_{l})\right\|_{C_{r}^{*}(G)}\leq 1
and
(f_{l}^{*}*f_{l})(x)=1
for
x\in s(U_{l})
. By nuclearity, there exist positive integer
n
and c.c.p. maps
\psi\colon C_{r}^{*}(G)\rightarrow M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and
\phi\colon M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\rightarrow C_{r}^{*}(G)
such that

\left\|(\phi\circ\psi)(\underline{\pi}(f_{l}))-\underline{\pi}(f_{l})\right\|_{C_{r}^{*}(G)}<\varepsilon

for every
l
.

In the following, let
\ell_{n}^{2}
be the Hilbert space
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with the canonical inner product, and let
\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
denote the standard basis. For fixed
i,j\in\left\{1,\dotsc,n\right\}
let
e_{i,j}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
be the matrix which has value 1 in the
(i,j)
-th entry and is 0 elsewhere, and if
a_{i,j}\in C_{r}^{*}(G)
for all
i,j\in\left\{1,\dotsc,n\right\}
, let
[a_{i,j}]\in M_{n}(C_{r}^{*}(G))
denote the matrix with
(i,j)
-th entry
a_{i,j}
. Equip the algebraic tensor product
\ell_{n}^{2}\otimes\ell_{n}^{2}\otimes C_{r}^{*}(G)
with the structure of a right pre-Hilbert
C_{r}^{*}(G)
-module (whose canonical inner product
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
is linear in the second variable). Define
[b_{i,j}]\coloneq[\phi(e_{i,j})]^{\frac{1}{2}}\in M_{n}(C_{r}^{*}(G))
and set

\eta_{\phi}\coloneq\sum_{j,k}u_{j}\otimes u_{k}\otimes b_{k,j}\in\ell_{n}^{2}\otimes\ell_{n}^{2}\otimes C_{r}^{*}(G)
.
For any
a\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
we have
\phi(a)=\langle\eta_{\phi},(a\otimes 1\otimes 1)\eta_{\phi}\rangle
. Approximate each
b_{k,j}
by
\zeta_{k,j}\in\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)
so that
\left\|b_{k,j}-\underline{\pi}(\zeta_{k,j})\right\|_{C_{r}^{*}(G)}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{n}
and set

\eta_{\phi}^{\prime}\coloneq\sum_{j,k}u_{j}\otimes u_{k}\otimes\zeta_{k,j}\in\ell_{n}^{2}\otimes\ell_{n}^{2}\otimes\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)
.
Note that
\eta_{\phi}^{\prime}
is within
\varepsilon
of
\eta_{\phi}
with respect to the
C_{r}^{*}(G)
-valued inner product. Moreover, since
\eta_{\phi}
has norm at most
1
,
we may without loss of generality assume that
\eta_{\phi}^{\prime}
also has norm at most
1
.

Define the non-negative function
\zeta\colon G\to[0,\infty)
by
\zeta(g)^{2}\coloneq\sum_{j,k}|\zeta_{k,j}(g)|^{2}
for
g\in G
, and observe that
\zeta
lies in the subalgebra of
B_{0}(G)
generated by
\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)
. Then

\left\|\zeta\right\|_{\ell^{2}(G_{x})}^{2}=\sum_{g\in G_{x}}|\zeta(g)|^{2}=\sum_{j,k}(\zeta_{k,j}^{*}*\zeta_{k,j})(x)=\langle\eta_{\phi}^{\prime},\eta_{\phi}^{\prime}\rangle(x)\leq 1

for every
x\in G^{(0)}
, since the supremum norm is dominated by the
C_{r}^{*}(G)
-norm on
\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)
.

For each
l
, write
a_{l}=\psi(\underline{\pi}(f_{l}))
, and note that
a_{l}
is a contraction. Then, using the right pre-Hilbert
C_{r}^{*}(G)
-module structure of
\ell_{n}^{2}\otimes\ell_{n}^{2}\otimes C_{r}^{*}(G)
we have

\displaystyle\langle(a_{l}\otimes 1\otimes 1)\eta_{\phi}^{\prime},\ \eta_{\phi}^{\prime}\cdot\underline{\pi}(f_{l})\rangle=\langle(a_{l}\otimes 1\otimes 1)\eta_{\phi}^{\prime},\ \eta_{\phi}^{\prime}\rangle*\underline{\pi}(f_{l})=\langle\eta_{\phi}^{\prime},\ (a_{l}\otimes 1\otimes 1)\eta_{\phi}^{\prime}\rangle^{*}*\underline{\pi}(f_{l})

\displaystyle\approx_{2\varepsilon}\

\displaystyle\langle\eta_{\phi},\ (a_{l}\otimes 1\otimes 1)\eta_{\phi}\rangle^{*}*\underline{\pi}(f_{l})=\phi(a_{l})^{*}*\underline{\pi}(f_{l})\approx_{\varepsilon}\underline{\pi}(f_{l})^{*}*\underline{\pi}(f_{l})

where
\approx_{\varepsilon}
means “within
\varepsilon
with respect to the
C_{r}^{*}(G)
-norm”. Both sides of the above expression are elements of
\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)
, so that, for
x\in s(U_{l})
, we have

|1-\langle(a_{l}\otimes 1\otimes 1)\eta_{\phi}^{\prime},\eta_{\phi}^{\prime}\cdot\underline{\pi}(f_{l})\rangle(x)|=|(f_{l}^{*}*f_{l})(x)-\langle(a_{l}\otimes 1\otimes 1)\eta_{\phi}^{\prime},\eta_{\phi}^{\prime}\cdot\underline{\pi}(f_{l})\rangle(x)|\leq 3\varepsilon.

Fix an arbitrary
g\in K
. Assume
g\in U_{l}
, and let
x=s(g)
. Any element of
\ell_{n}^{2}\otimes\ell_{n}^{2}\otimes\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)
can be viewed as a function from
G
to
\ell_{n}^{2}\otimes\ell_{n}^{2}
in the obvious way. For
h\in G_{x}
observe that

(\eta_{\phi}^{\prime}\cdot\underline{\pi}(f_{l}))(h)=\sum_{\tilde{g}\in G_{x}}\eta_{\phi}^{\prime}(h\tilde{g}^{-1})f_{l}(\tilde{g})=\eta_{\phi}^{\prime}(hg^{-1})\in\ell_{n}^{2}\otimes\ell_{n}^{2}.

This implies

\displaystyle 1-3\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq|\langle(a_{l}\otimes 1\otimes 1)\eta_{\phi}^{\prime},\eta_{\phi}^{\prime}\cdot\underline{\pi}(f_{l})\rangle(x)|=\bigg|\sum_{h\in G_{x}}\langle(a_{l}\otimes 1)\eta_{\phi}^{\prime}(h),(\eta_{\phi}^{\prime}\cdot\underline{\pi}(f_{l}))(h)\rangle_{\ell_{n}^{2}\otimes\ell_{n}^{2}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{h\in G_{x}}\left\|\eta_{\phi}^{\prime}(h)\right\|_{\ell_{n}^{2}\otimes\ell_{n}^{2}}\left\|\eta_{\phi}^{\prime}(hg^{-1})\right\|_{\ell_{n}^{2}\otimes\ell_{n}^{2}}=\sum_{h\in G_{x}}\zeta(h)\zeta(hg^{-1})=\sum_{\tilde{h}\in G_{r(g)}}\zeta(\tilde{h}g)\zeta(\tilde{h})=(\zeta^{*}*\zeta)(g).

But
\left\|\zeta\right\|_{\ell^{2}(G_{y})}^{2}\leq 1
for all
y\in G^{(0)}
, so

1-3\varepsilon\leq(\zeta^{*}*\zeta)(g)\leq 1
,
as desired. This proves our first claim.

The proof for the second claim is similar: Let
K
and
f_{l}
be as above, and let
\dot{\underline{\pi}}\colon\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)\rightarrow C^{*}_{{\rm ess}}(G)
be the composite of
\underline{\pi}
and the canonical projection
C^{*}_{r}(G)\twoheadrightarrow C^{*}_{{\rm ess}}(G)
. Given arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, nuclearity of
C^{*}_{{\rm ess}}(G)
implies the existence of c.c.p. maps
\dot{\psi}\colon C^{*}_{{\rm ess}}(G)\rightarrow M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and
\dot{\phi}\colon M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\rightarrow C^{*}_{{\rm ess}}(G)
such that

\left\|(\dot{\phi}\circ\dot{\psi})(\dot{\underline{\pi}}(f_{l}))-\dot{\underline{\pi}}(f_{l})\right\|_{C^{*}_{{\rm ess}}(G)}<\varepsilon

for every
l
. Now the same argument as above shows that
1-3\varepsilon\leq(\zeta^{*}*\zeta)(g)\leq 1
for all
g\in(G_{C}^{C})\cap K
. This shows that
\tilde{G}_{{\rm ess}}
is topologically amenable.
∎

The following is a generalisation of the corresponding result about extending representations in
[
7
, § 4]
. (Note that similar ideas already appear in
[
30
]
and
[
24
, Appendix B]
.) Our proof follows
[
7
, § 4]
, but we give details because we do not impose the constraint that
G
can be covered by countably many open bisections.

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
G
be a non-Hausdorff étale groupoid. Every
∗
-algebra representation
\pi\colon\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(H)
extends to a
∗
-algebra representation
\tilde{\pi}\colon B_{c}(G)\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(H)
. Moreover, the commutators of the ranges of
\pi
and
\tilde{\pi}
are equal.

Before proving the lemma we introduce some terminology and notation.
Given a Hausdorff locally compact space
U
, a
positive Radon measure
on
U
is a positive Borel measure which is inner regular on open sets, outer regular on all Borel sets, and finite on compacts. A
complex Radon measure
on
U
is a complex Borel measure of the form
\mu=\rho\ {\rm d}\nu
, where
\rho\colon U\rightarrow\mathbb{T}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}\text{: }|z|=1\right\}
is a Borel-measurable function, and
\nu
is a finite positive Radon measure on
U
. Concretely
\mu(B)=\int_{B}\rho\ {\rm d}\nu
for Borel subsets
B\subseteq U
. Here
\rho
and
\nu
are essentially unique, in the sense that if
\mu=\tilde{\rho}\ {\rm d}\tilde{\nu}
is another such representation, then
\nu=\tilde{\nu}
, and
\rho=\tilde{\rho}
for
\nu
-almost all points in
U
.
Any complex Radon measure
\mu=\rho\ {\rm d}\nu
on
U
has an associated linear functional
\overline{\mu}
on
C_{c}(U)
given by
\overline{\mu}(h)=\int_{U}h\rho\ {\rm d}\nu
. By the Riesz-Markov-Kakutani representation theorem, the mapping
\mu\mapsto\overline{\mu}
gives a one-to-one correspondence between complex Radon measures on
U
and linear functionals
C_{c}(U)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
which are
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\infty}
-bounded. Further details can be found in
[
16
, Chapter 7]
.

Proof of Lemma
6.7
.

Let
\pi\colon\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(H)
be a
∗
-algebra representation. Fix
\xi,\eta\in H
and define the linear functional
\pi_{\xi,\eta}\colon\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by
h\mapsto\big\langle\xi,\pi(h)\eta\big\rangle
. (By convention the inner product
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
on
H
is linear in the second variable.) For any open bisection
U\in\mathscr{B}
, the restriction of
\pi
to
C_{c}(U)
is bounded with respect to the supremum norm, and hence the restriction of
\pi_{\xi,\eta}
to
C_{c}(U)
is as well. By the Riesz-Markov-Kakutani representation theorem, there exist a Borel function
\rho_{\xi,\eta}^{U}\colon U\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
and finite positive Radon measure
\nu_{\xi,\eta}^{U}
on
U
such that

\pi_{\xi,\eta}(h)=\int_{U}h\rho_{\xi,\eta}^{U}\ {\rm d}\nu_{\xi,\eta}^{U}

for all
h\in C_{c}(U)
. By uniqueness of
\rho_{\xi,\eta}^{U}
and
\nu_{\xi,\eta}^{U}
, for any open bisections
U_{1},U_{2}\in\mathscr{B}
, the measures
\nu_{\xi,\eta}^{U_{1}},\nu_{\xi,\eta}^{U_{2}}
and the Borel functions
\rho_{\xi,\eta}^{U_{1}},\rho_{\xi,\eta}^{U_{2}}
agree on the intersection
U_{1}\cap U_{2}
; that is,
\nu_{\xi,\eta}^{U_{1}}(B)=\nu_{\xi,\eta}^{U_{2}}(B)
for any Borel set
B\subseteq U_{1}\cap U_{2}
, and
\rho_{\xi,\eta}^{U_{1}}(g)=\rho_{\xi,\eta}^{U_{2}}(g)
for
\nu_{\xi,\eta}^{U_{1}}
-almost every
g\in U_{1}\cap U_{2}
. Then, for any finite set of open bisections
\mathcal{F}=\{U_{1},\dotsc,U_{n}\}\subset\mathscr{B}
there exists (see for example
[
24
, Lemma A.1]
) a unique finite positive Borel measure
\nu_{\xi,\eta}^{\mathcal{F}}
on
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}
such that for any
U_{i}\in\mathcal{F}
,
\nu_{\xi,\eta}^{\mathcal{F}}
agrees with
\nu_{\xi,\eta}^{U_{i}}
on Borel subsets of
U_{i}
.
Similarly, there exists a Borel function
\rho_{\xi,\eta}^{\mathcal{F}}\colon\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
such that for each
i
,
\rho_{\xi,\eta}^{\mathcal{F}}(g)=\rho_{\xi,\eta}^{U_{i}}(g)
for
\nu_{\xi,\eta}^{\mathcal{F}}
-almost all
g\in U_{i}
.

Define the extension
\tilde{\pi}_{\xi,\eta}\colon B_{c}(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
as follows: given
f\in B_{c}(G)
, let
\mathcal{F}=\left\{U_{1},\dotsc,U_{n}\right\}\subset\mathscr{B}
be a finite collection of open bisections covering
{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f)
, and define

\tilde{\pi}_{\xi,\eta}(f)\coloneq\int_{\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}}f\rho_{\xi,\eta}^{\mathcal{F}}\ {\rm d}\nu_{\xi,\eta}^{\mathcal{F}}
. The complex Borel measures
\rho_{\xi,\eta}^{\mathcal{F}}\ {\rm d}\nu_{\xi,\eta}^{\mathcal{F}}
agree on intersections for different choices of finite subset
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathscr{B}
, hence it is clear that
\tilde{\pi}_{\xi,\eta}
is well-defined and linear. For fixed
\eta\in H
, the map
\xi\mapsto\pi_{\xi,\eta}
is anti-linear, therefore, for fixed finite
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathscr{B}
the map
\xi\mapsto\rho_{\xi,\eta}^{\mathcal{F}}\ {\rm d}\nu_{\xi,\eta}^{\mathcal{F}}
is also anti-linear (because these complex Borel measures restrict to complex Radon measures on open bisections, and complex Radon measures are determined by their action on compactly supported continuous functions). It follows that the map
\xi\mapsto\tilde{\pi}_{\xi,\eta}
is anti-linear. Similarly, for fixed
\xi\in H
, the map
\eta\mapsto\tilde{\pi}_{\xi,\eta}
is linear. We can now define the extension
\tilde{\pi}\colon B_{c}(G)\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(H)
of
\pi
: given
f\in B_{c}(G)
, let
\tilde{\pi}(f)\in\mathcal{L}(H)
be the unique operator such that

\tilde{\pi}_{\xi,\eta}(f)=\big\langle\xi,\tilde{\pi}(f)\eta\big\rangle

for all
\xi,\eta\in H
. It is clear that
\tilde{\pi}_{\xi,\eta}
extends
\pi_{\xi,\eta}
for each
\xi,\eta\in H
, and hence
\tilde{\pi}(f)=\pi(f)
for all
f\in\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)
. We show that the map
\tilde{\pi}\colon B_{c}(H)\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(H)
is a
∗
-algebra representation. Linearity of
\tilde{\pi}
is clear, therefore, it suffices to prove that
\tilde{\pi}
is multiplicative and
∗
-preserving for functions in
B_{c}(G)
whose strict support is contained in some open bisection. First we show that
\tilde{\pi}(f^{*})=\tilde{\pi}(f)^{*}
for such
f\in B_{c}(G)
. Fix
U\in\mathscr{B}
and let
\xi,\eta\in H
. Observe that
\tilde{\pi}(\dot{f}^{*})=\pi(\dot{f}^{*})=\pi(\dot{f})^{*}=\tilde{\pi}(\dot{f})^{*}
for any
\dot{f}\in C_{c}(U)
so that

\displaystyle\int_{U}\dot{f}(g)\rho_{\eta,\xi}^{U}(g)\ {\rm d}\nu_{\eta,\xi}^{U}(g)

\displaystyle=\big\langle\eta,\pi(\dot{f})\xi\big\rangle=\overline{\big\langle\xi,\pi(\dot{f}^{*})\eta\big\rangle}=\overline{\int_{U^{-1}}\dot{f}^{*}(g)\rho_{\xi,\eta}^{U^{-1}}(g)\ {\rm d}\nu_{\xi,\eta}^{U^{-1}}(g)}

\displaystyle=\int_{U^{-1}}\dot{f}(g^{-1})\overline{\rho_{\xi,\eta}^{U^{-1}}(g)}\ {\rm d}\nu_{\xi,\eta}^{U^{-1}}(g)=\int_{U}\dot{f}(g)\Big(\rho_{\xi,\eta}^{U^{-1}}\Big)^{*}(g)\ {\rm d}\big(\nu_{\xi,\eta}^{U^{-1}}\big)^{-1}(g)

where the positive Radon measure
\big(\nu_{\xi,\eta}^{U^{-1}}\big)^{-1}
on
U
is defined by
\big(\nu_{\xi,\eta}^{U^{-1}}\big)^{-1}(B)\coloneq\nu_{\xi,\eta}^{U^{-1}}(B^{-1})
for Borel
B\subseteq U
. Since
\dot{f}\in C_{c}(U)
was arbitrary, the complex Radon measures
\rho_{\eta,\xi}^{U}\ {\rm d}\nu_{\eta,\xi}^{U}
and
\Big(\rho_{\xi,\eta}^{U^{-1}}\Big)^{*}\ {\rm d}\big(\nu_{\xi,\eta}^{U^{-1}}\big)^{-1}
agree on Borel subsets of
U
. Therefore, replacing
\dot{f}
in the calculations above with some
f\in B_{c}(G)
satisfying
{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f)\subseteq U
shows that
\big\langle\eta,\tilde{\pi}(f)\xi\big\rangle=\overline{\big\langle\xi,\tilde{\pi}(f^{*})\eta\big\rangle}
. The Hilbert space elements
\xi,\eta\in H
were arbitrary, hence
\tilde{\pi}(f^{*})=\tilde{\pi}(f)^{*}
, as required.

Next we show that
\tilde{\pi}
is multiplicative on
B_{c}(G)
. Let
U_{1},U_{2}\in\mathscr{B}
be arbitrary open bisections. Whenever
f_{1},f_{2}\in B_{c}(G)
have strict supports contained in
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
respectively, the convolution
f_{1}*f_{2}
has strict support contained in the open bisection
V\coloneq U_{1}U_{2}
and satisfies
(f_{1}*f_{2})(g)=f_{1}(gU_{2}^{-1})f_{2}(U_{1}^{-1}g)
for
g\in V
. Here we use
gU_{2}^{-1}
and
U_{1}^{-1}g
to denote the unique elements of the sets
\left\{gg_{2}^{-1}\text{: }g_{2}\in U_{2},s(g_{2})=s(g)\right\}
and
\left\{g_{1}^{-1}g\text{: }g_{1}\in U_{1},r(g_{1})=r(g)\right\}
respectively. Fix
\dot{f}_{1}\in C_{c}(U_{1})
and observe that multiplicativity of
\pi
ensures the following for any
\dot{f}_{2}\in C_{c}(U_{2})
and
\xi,\eta\in H
:

\displaystyle\int_{U_{2}}\dot{f}_{2}(g)\rho_{\pi(\dot{f}_{1})^{*}\xi,\eta}^{U_{2}}(g)\ {\rm d}\nu_{\pi(\dot{f}_{1})^{*}\xi,\eta}^{U_{2}}(g)=\big\langle\pi(\dot{f}_{1})^{*}\xi,\pi(\dot{f}_{2})\eta\big\rangle=\big\langle\xi,\pi(\dot{f}_{1})\pi(\dot{f}_{2})\eta\big\rangle=\big\langle\xi,\pi(\dot{f}_{1}*\dot{f}_{2})\eta\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\int_{V}(\dot{f}_{1}*\dot{f}_{2})(g)\rho_{\xi,\eta}^{V}(g)\ {\rm d}\nu_{\xi,\eta}^{V}(g)=\int_{V}\dot{f}_{1}(gU_{2}^{-1})\dot{f}_{2}(U_{1}^{-1}g)\rho_{\xi,\eta}^{V}(g)\ {\rm d}\nu_{\xi,\eta}^{V}(g)=\int_{U_{2}}\dot{f}_{2}(g)\ {\rm d}\mu(g)

where the complex measure
\mu
on
U_{1}^{-1}V\subseteq U_{2}
is the pushforward of the complex measure
\dot{f}_{1}\Big((\cdot)U_{2}^{-1}\Big)\rho_{\xi,\eta}^{V}\ {\rm d}\nu_{\xi,\eta}^{V}
on
V
under the homeomorphism
V\rightarrow U_{1}^{-1}V;g\mapsto U_{1}^{-1}g
. The set of complex Radon measures is invariant under multiplication by bounded Borel functions, so
\mu
is a complex Radon measure. Extend
\mu
to
U_{2}
by 0 and observe that the extension is still a complex Radon measure (because
U_{1}^{-1}V
is open). Since
\dot{f}_{2}\in C_{c}(U_{2})
was arbitrary, the measures
\mu
and
\rho_{\pi(\dot{f}_{1})^{*}\xi,\eta}^{U_{2}}\ {\rm d}\nu_{\pi(\dot{f}_{1})^{*}\xi,\eta}^{U_{2}}
agree on Borel subsets of
U_{2}
. Therefore, replacing
\dot{f}_{2}
in the calculations above with some
f_{2}\in B_{c}(G)
satisfying
{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f_{2})\subseteq U_{2}
shows that
\big\langle\xi,\tilde{\pi}(\dot{f}_{1})\tilde{\pi}(f_{2})\eta\big\rangle=\big\langle\xi,\tilde{\pi}(\dot{f}_{1}*f_{2})\eta\big\rangle
. The Hilbert space elements
\xi,\eta\in H
were arbitrary, hence
\tilde{\pi}(\dot{f}_{1}*f_{2})=\tilde{\pi}(\dot{f}_{1})\tilde{\pi}(f_{2})
. Taking adjoints and reversing the roles of
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
implies that
\tilde{\pi}(f_{1}*\dot{f}_{2})=\tilde{\pi}(f_{1})\tilde{\pi}(\dot{f}_{2})
whenever
\dot{f}_{2}\in C_{c}(U_{2})
and
f_{1}\in B_{c}(G)
satisfies
{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f_{1})\subseteq U_{1}
. Repeating an identical argument to the one above with
\dot{f}_{1}
replaced by
f_{1}
gives
\tilde{\pi}(f_{1}*f_{2})=\tilde{\pi}(f_{1})\tilde{\pi}(f_{2})
for any
f_{2}\in B_{c}(G)
satisfying
{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f_{2})\subseteq U_{2}
. This completes the proof that the extension
\tilde{\pi}
is a
∗
-algebra representation.

We now address the “moreover” part of the lemma. Let
a\in\mathcal{L}(H)
commute with the range of
\pi
, and let
\xi,\eta\in H
,
U\in\mathscr{B}
. Observe that

\displaystyle\int_{U}\dot{f}(g)\rho_{a^{*}(\xi),\eta}^{U}(g)\ {\rm d}\nu_{a^{*}(\xi),\eta}^{U}(g)=\big\langle a^{*}(\xi),\pi(\dot{f})(\eta)\big\rangle=\big\langle\xi,\big(a\pi(\dot{f})\big)(\eta)\big\rangle=\big\langle\xi,\big(\pi(\dot{f})a\big)(\eta)\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\overline{\big\langle a(\eta),\pi(\dot{f}^{*})(\xi)\big\rangle}=\int_{U^{-1}}\dot{f}(g^{-1})\overline{\rho_{a(\eta),\xi}^{U^{-1}}(g)}\ {\rm d}\nu_{a(\eta),\xi}^{U^{-1}}(g)=\int_{U}\dot{f}(g)\Big(\rho_{a(\eta),\xi}^{U^{-1}}\Big)^{*}(g)\ {\rm d}\big(\nu_{a(\eta),\xi}^{U^{-1}}\big)^{-1}(g)

for all
\dot{f}\in C_{c}(U)
. Similar arguments to earlier parts of the proof show that
\big\langle\xi,\big(a\tilde{\pi}(f)\big)(\eta)\big\rangle=\big\langle\xi,\big(\tilde{\pi}(f)a\big)(\eta)\big\rangle
for any
\xi,\eta\in H
and
f\in B_{c}(G)
satisfying
{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f)\subseteq U
. The Hilbert space elements
\xi,\eta\in H
were arbitrary, hence
a
commutes with
\tilde{\pi}(f)
. Any element of
B_{c}(G)
can be written as a finite linear combination of ones supported inside some open bisection, hence
a
commutes with the range of
\tilde{\pi}
.
∎

Corollary 6.8
.

Let
A
be a
C^{*}
-algebra. Then
\mathfrak{i}\odot{\rm id}_{A}
induces an isometric
∗
-homomorphism
C^{*}(\mathfrak{i})\otimes{\rm id}_{A}\colon C^{*}(G)\otimes_{\max}A\hookrightarrow C^{*}(\tilde{G})\otimes_{\max}A
. In particular,
\mathfrak{i}
induces an isometric
∗
-homomorphism
C^{*}(\mathfrak{i})\colon C^{*}(G)\hookrightarrow C^{*}(\tilde{G})
.

Proof.

By universality of the full groupoid
C^{*}
- algebra there exists a
∗
-homomorphism
C^{*}(\mathfrak{i})\colon C^{*}(G)\rightarrow C^{*}(\tilde{G})
extending
\mathfrak{i}
. By universality of the maximal norm on
C^{*}(G)\odot A
, there is an induced
∗
-homomorphism
C^{*}(\mathfrak{i})\otimes{\rm id}_{A}\colon C^{*}(G)\otimes_{\max}A\rightarrow C^{*}(\tilde{G})\otimes_{\max}A
. In order to show that this map is an isometry, it suffices to prove that
\left\|x\right\|_{C^{*}(G)\otimes_{\max}A}\leq\left\|C^{*}(\mathfrak{i})\odot{\rm id}_{A}(x)\right\|_{C^{*}(\tilde{G})\otimes_{\max}A}
for all
x\in\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)\odot A
. For the remainder of the proof, we identify
\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)
with its image under
\mathfrak{i}
, so that we view
\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)\odot A
as a subset of both
C^{*}(G)\odot A
and
C^{*}(\tilde{G})\odot A
.

We claim that for any non-degenerate
∗
-algebra representation
\phi\colon C^{*}(G)\odot A\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(H)
there exists a
∗
-algebra representation
\tilde{\phi}\colon C^{*}(\tilde{G})\odot A\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(H)
agreeing with
\phi
on
\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)\odot A
. Any non-degenerate
∗
-algebra representation
C^{*}(G)\odot A\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(H)
is of the form
\pi\times\sigma
where
\pi\colon C^{*}(G)\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(H)
and
\sigma\colon A\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(H)
are non-degenerate
∗
-algebra representations with commuting ranges (see
[
4
, Theorem 3.2.6]
). Here
\pi\times\sigma\colon C^{*}(G)\odot A\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(H)
is given by
(\pi\times\sigma)(b\otimes a)\coloneq\pi(b)\sigma(a)
for
b\in C^{*}(G)
,
a\in A
. By Lemma
6.7
there exists a
∗
-algebra representation
\tilde{\pi}\colon B_{c}(G)\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(H)
extending
\pi
, and whose range commutes with the range of
\sigma
. By Lemma
3.7
(ii), the image of
C_{c}(\tilde{G})
under the canonical isomorphism
C_{0}(\tilde{G})\cong C^{*}(\mathscr{C}_{c}(G))
will be contained in
B_{c}(G)
. This canonical isomorphism is
∗
-preserving and multiplicative with respect to convolution on
C_{c}(\tilde{G})
. Hence,
\tilde{\pi}
restricts to a
∗
-algebra representation of
C_{c}(\tilde{G})
, which in turn induces a
∗
-algebra representation
C^{*}(\tilde{G})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(H)
(also denoted
\tilde{\pi}
) whose range commutes with the range of
\sigma
. Therefore (by
[
4
, Proposition 3.1.17]
) the map
\tilde{\pi}\times\sigma\colon C^{*}(\tilde{G})\odot A\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(H)
is a well-defined
∗
-algebra representation agreeing with
\pi\times\sigma
on
\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)\odot A
. This proves the claim.

Take
x\in\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)\odot A
. Let
\phi\colon C^{*}(G)\odot A\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(H)
be an arbitrary non-degenerate
∗
-algebra representation, and let
\tilde{\phi}\colon C^{*}(\tilde{G})\odot A\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(H)
be a
∗
-algebra representation agreeing with
\phi
on
\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)\odot A
. Then
\left\|\phi(x)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(H)}=\left\|\tilde{\phi}(x)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(H)}\leq\left\|x\right\|_{C^{*}(\tilde{G})\otimes_{\max}A}
. Taking the supremum over all non-degenerate
∗
-algebra representations
\phi
, we get
\left\|x\right\|_{C^{*}(G)\otimes_{\max}A}\leq\left\|x\right\|_{C^{*}(\tilde{G})\otimes_{\max}A}
as required.
∎

Theorem 6.9
.

If the canonical projection
C^{*}(\tilde{G})\twoheadrightarrow C^{*}_{r}(\tilde{G})
is an isomorphism, then so is the canonical projection
C^{*}(G)\twoheadrightarrow C^{*}_{r}(G)
.

If
\tilde{G}
is topologically amenable, then the canonical projection
C^{*}(G)\twoheadrightarrow C^{*}_{r}(G)
is an isomorphism, and
C^{*}(G)
as well as
C^{*}_{r}(G)
are nuclear.

Proof.

For any
f\in\mathscr{C}_{c}(G)
we have
\left\|f\right\|_{C_{r}^{*}(G)}=\left\|\mathfrak{i}(f)\right\|_{C_{r}^{*}(\tilde{G})}
and
\left\|f\right\|_{C^{*}(G)}=\left\|\mathfrak{i}(f)\right\|_{C^{*}(\tilde{G})}
by Lemma
3.8
and Corollary
6.8
respectively. The first claim follows.

Let
A
be an arbitrary
C^{*}
-algebra. We have the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{C^{*}(G)\otimes_{\max}A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{C^{*}(\mathfrak{i})\otimes{\rm id}_{A}}
\textstyle{C^{*}(\tilde{G})\otimes_{\max}A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{C_{r}^{*}(G)\otimes_{\min}A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{C^{*}_{r}(\mathfrak{i})\otimes{\rm id}_{A}}
\textstyle{C_{r}^{*}(\tilde{G})\otimes_{\min}A}

where the vertical maps are the canonical projections. Now, suppose that the Hausdorff groupoid
\tilde{G}
is topologically amenable. By
[
4
, Corollary 5.6.17]
the canonical projection
C^{*}(\tilde{G})\twoheadrightarrow C^{*}_{r}(\tilde{G})
is an isomorphism. Moreover, by
[
4
, Theorem 5.6.18]
the
C^{*}
-algebra
C_{r}^{*}(\tilde{G})
is nuclear, and therefore the vertical map on the right is an isomorphism. As
C^{*}(\mathfrak{i})\otimes{\rm id}_{A}
is injective by Corollary
6.8
, it follows that the vertical map on the left is injective, as required.
∎

Let

7.1.
Group bundles and related examples

Example 7.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-trivial group and let
G
be the disjoint union of
\mathbb{N}=\left\{1,2,3,\dotsc\right\}
and
\Gamma
,
G=\mathbb{N}\cup\Gamma
. The unit space of
G
is given by
X=\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{1_{\Gamma}\right\}
, where
1_{\Gamma}
is the identity element of
\Gamma
. The topology of
X
is such that
X
is homeomorphic to the one-point compactification
\mathbb{N}^{+}=\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{\infty\right\}
of
\mathbb{N}
via the map which is identity on
\mathbb{N}
and sends
1_{\Gamma}
to
\infty
. To describe the topology on
G
, equip
\mathbb{N}
with the discrete topology, and a subset of
G
is a neighbourhood of

if and only if it contains a set of the form
(U\setminus\left\{\infty\right\})\cup\left\{\gamma\right\}
, where
U
is an open subset of
\mathbb{N}^{+}
containing
\infty
.

In this example, the Hausdorff cover is given by the topological disjoint union
\tilde{G}=\mathbb{N}^{+}\amalg\Gamma
, where
\infty\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
corresponds to the subset
\Gamma\subseteq G
. We have
\tilde{G}_{\rm sing}=\Gamma
and
\tilde{G}_{\rm ess}=\mathbb{N}^{+}
. If
\Gamma
is amenable, the image of
C^{*}_{r}(G)
in
C^{*}_{r}(\tilde{G})
is given by the pullback (in other words, the limit) of the following diagram

\textstyle{?\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\chi_{\rm tr}}
\textstyle{C(\mathbb{N}^{+})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{ev}_{\infty}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{C}}

\operatorname{ev}_{\infty}
denotes the evaluation map at
\infty
and

is the trivial character. If
\Gamma
is not amenable, then the canonical embedding
C^{*}_{r}(\mathfrak{i})\colon C^{*}_{r}(G)\hookrightarrow C^{*}_{r}(\tilde{G})
is surjective. It follows that the image of the singular ideal
J
in
C^{*}_{r}(\tilde{G})
is given by
{\rm ker}\,(\chi_{\rm tr})
if
\Gamma
is amenable and by
C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma)
if
\Gamma
is not amenable. In both cases, we conclude that the canonical embedding
C^{*}_{\rm ess}(G)\hookrightarrow C^{*}_{r}(\tilde{G}_{\rm ess})
induced by

is surjective, i.e., an isomorphism.

Example 7.2

Let us present an example where

with respect to

In the following, we will focus on the case of contracting self-similar groups. This means that there exists a finite subset
N\subseteq\Gamma
such that for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
there exists
l\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
u\in T
with
\ell(u)\geq l
, we have that
\gamma|_{u}\in N
. The smallest such
N
is called the nucleus. Note that
n|_{u}\in N
for all
n\in N
and
u\in T
if
N
is the nucleus.

Given a contracting self-similar group
\Gamma
with nucleus
N
, let us now describe a recipe to find the unit space
\tilde{X}
of the Hausdorff cover (as well as the canonical projection
\pi\colon\tilde{X}\twoheadrightarrow X
). As mentioned in Remark
3.6
, this will then lead to a description of the Hausdorff cover
\tilde{G}
of
G
. We start by providing an approach to finding the closure
\overline{X}
of
X
in
G
, which will then lead to the set of dangerous points
D=s(\overline{X}\setminus X)
. In the following, we set
[u\gamma v^{*},vX]\coloneq\left\{[u\gamma v^{*},v\omega]\text{: }\omega\in X\right\}
.

Lemma 7.10
.

Let
\Gamma
be a contracting self-similar group with nucleus
N
.

(i)

For
\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}\coloneq\left\{[u\gamma v^{*},vX]\text{: }u\gamma v^{*}\in S^{\times}\right\}
, we have
\overline{X}=\bigcup_{U\in\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}}\overline{U\cap X}^{U}
. Here
\overline{(\cdot)}^{U}
denotes closure in
U
.

(ii)

For every
\gamma\in\Gamma
and
\omega\in X
, there exist
u,v\in T
and
n\in N
such that
[\gamma,\omega]=[unv^{*},\omega]
.

(iii)

For
\mathscr{B}_{N}\coloneq\left\{[unv^{*},vX]\text{: }unv^{*}\in S^{\times},\,n\in N\right\}
, we have
\overline{X}=\bigcup_{U\in\mathscr{B}_{N}}\overline{U\cap X}^{U}
.

(iv)

For
u,v\in T
with
u\neq v
and
n\in N
, we have
[unv^{*},vX]\cap X=\emptyset
.

(v)

For
\mathscr{B}_{N}^{\prime}\coloneq\left\{[unu^{*},uX]\text{: }unu^{*}\in S^{\times},\,n\in N\right\}
, we have
\overline{X}=\bigcup_{U\in\mathscr{B}_{N}^{\prime}}\overline{U\cap X}^{U}
.

(vi)

For
u\in T
and
n\in N
, we have
[unu^{*},uX]\cap X=u([n,X]\cap X)u^{*}
.

(vii)

We have

\overline{X}=\bigcup_{u\in T,\,n\in N}u(\overline{[n,X]\cap X}^{[n,X]})u^{*}.

Proof.

(i)
is easy to see. For
(ii)
, take
v\in T
with
\ell(v)\geq l
(
l
is as in the definition of “contracting”) such that
\omega\in vX
. Then
[\gamma,\omega]=[\gamma vv^{*},\omega]=[\gamma(v)\gamma|_{v}v^{*},\omega]
.
(iii)
follows from
(i)
and
(ii)
.
(iv)
is straightforward to check, and
(v)
follows from
(iii)
and
(iv)
.
(vi)
is easy to see, and
(vii)
follows from
(v)
and
(vi)
.
∎

Now, given
\gamma\in\Gamma
, set
{\rm SF}_{\gamma}=\left\{v\in T\text{: }\gamma(v)=v,\,\gamma|_{v}=1\right\}
, where
1
denotes the identity of
\Gamma
, and let
{\rm TF}_{\gamma}\coloneq\bigcup_{v\in{\rm SF}_{\gamma}}vX
. Furthermore, given
\omega\in X
and
l\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\omega_{l}\in T
be the finite word in

consisting of the first
l
letters of
\omega
. The following is easy to see and provides a way to work out
\overline{X}
.

Lemma 7.11
.

For every
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we have
\overline{[\gamma,X]\cap X}^{[\gamma,X]}=\left\{[\gamma,\omega]\text{: }\omega\in\partial{\rm TF}_{\gamma}\right\}
, and
[\gamma,\omega]
(for
\omega\in X
) lies in
\overline{[\gamma,X]\cap X}^{[\gamma,X]}
if and only if for all

\omega_{l}\notin{\rm SF}_{\gamma}
but there exists

such that
\omega_{l}z_{l}\in{\rm SF}_{\gamma}
.

Given a contracting self-similar group
\Gamma
with nucleus
N
, and given
\omega\in D=s(\overline{X}\setminus X)
, our goal now is to find
\left\{[u\gamma u^{*},\omega]\in\overline{X}\text{: }u\in T,\,\gamma\in\Gamma\right\}
. First, let us define
\overline{X}_{N}(\omega)\coloneq\left\{[unu^{*},\omega]\in\overline{X}\text{: }u\in T,\,n\in N\right\}
. Moreover, given
l\in\mathbb{N}
, set
N(\omega,l)\coloneq\left\{n\in N\text{: }[\omega_{l}n\omega_{l}^{*},\omega]\in\overline{X}_{N}(\omega)\right\}
and
\overline{X}_{N}(\omega,l)\coloneq\left\{[\omega_{l}n\omega_{l}^{*},\omega]\text{: }n\in N(\omega,l)\right\}
.

Lemma 7.12
.

Let
\Gamma
be a contracting self-similar group with nucleus
N
, and take some
\omega\in D
.

(i)

We have
\left\{[u\gamma u^{*},\omega]\in\overline{X}\text{: }u\in T,\,\gamma\in\Gamma\right\}=\overline{X}_{N}(\omega)=\overline{X}(\omega)
, where
\overline{X}(\omega)=\overline{X}\cap G_{\omega}^{\omega}
.

(ii)

For every

\overline{X}_{N}(\omega,l)\subseteq\overline{X}_{N}(\omega,l+1)
.

(iii)

There exists
L\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\left\{[u\gamma u^{*},\omega]\in\overline{X}\text{: }u\in T,\,\gamma\in\Gamma\right\}=\overline{X}_{N}(\omega,L)
.

(iv)

Elements of
\pi^{-1}(\omega)\subseteq\tilde{X}
are subsets of the form
\left\{[\omega_{L}n\omega_{L}^{*},\omega]\text{: }n\in M\right\}
for some subset

.

Proof.

(i)
follows from Lemma
7.10

(ii)
and Lemma
7.10

(v)
. To see
(ii)
, observe that, if
\omega_{l+1}=\omega_{l}\omega(l+1)
, then
\omega_{l}n\omega_{l}^{*}\omega_{l+1}\omega_{l+1}^{*}=\omega_{l}n\omega(l+1)\omega_{l+1}^{*}=\omega_{l}n(\omega(l+1))n|_{\omega(l+1)}\omega_{l+1}^{*}=\omega_{l+1}n|_{\omega(l+1)}\omega_{l+1}^{*}

(iii)
follows from

and finiteness of

. Finally,
(iv)
follows from

.
∎

We record the following immediate consequences.

Corollary 7.13
.

Let
\Gamma
be a contracting self-similar group with nucleus
N
. Then
\#\,\overline{X}(\omega)\leq\#\,N
for all
\omega\in X
, and
\#\,\pi^{-1}(\omega)\leq 2^{\#\,N}
for all
\omega\in X
.

Corollaries
4.6
,
4.8
and
7.13
yield the following immediate consequence, which implies the result in
[
17
]
that for groupoids attached to contracting self-similar groups, the
C^{*}
-algebraic singular ideal
J
vanishes if and only if the singular ideal
J_{\mathbb{C}}
in the complex Steinberg algebra vanishes.

Corollary 7.14
.

Let
G
be the ample groupoid attached to a contracting self-similar group. Then
J=\left\{0\right\}
if and only if
J_{\mathbb{C}}=\left\{0\right\}
if and only if
G
does not satisfy condition (
\mathcal{S}_{0}
).

Remark 7.15
.

We arrive at the following recipe to determine
\pi^{-1}(\omega)
, for
\omega\in D
. First, find
L\in\mathbb{N}
as in Lemma
7.12

(iii)
as well as
N(\omega,L)
. Write
\omega=\omega_{L}\omega_{>L}
. Now find the set
\mathcal{M}_{\omega}
of all subsets
M\subseteq N(\omega,L)
such that there is a sequence
(\omega_{i})
in
X
converging to
\omega_{>L}
such that
(\omega_{i})
converges in
G
to all points in
\left\{[n,\omega_{>L}]\text{: }n\in M\right\}

\omega_{L}\omega_{i}

\pi^{-1}(\omega)=\left\{\left\{[\omega_{L}n\omega_{L}^{*},\omega]\text{: }n\in M\right\}\text{: }M\in\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\right\}

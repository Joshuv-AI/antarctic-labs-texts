Probing vector- vs. scalar-mediator dark-matter scenarios in B → ( K , K ∗ ) ​ M X B\to(K,K^{*})M_{X} decays
Alexander Berezhnoy a , Wolfgang Lucha b and Dmitri Melikhov a,c,d

Abstract
Within the hypothesis of the dark-matter origin of the excess in B → K ​ M X B\to KM_{X} decays over the standard-model expectation, observed by Belle-II, we show that: (i) Scalar- and vector-mediator scenarios may be unambiguously discriminated by measuring the differential distributions in B → K ​ M X B\to KM_{X} and B → K ∗ ​ M X B\to K^{*}M_{X} decays. (ii) Combining the available data on Γ ⁡ ( B → K ​ M X ) \Gamma(B\to KM_{X}) and the upper limit on Γ ⁡ ( B → K ∗ ​ M X ) \Gamma(B\to K^{*}M_{X}) provides a tight constraint on the vector-mediator mass M V ≲ 3 M_{V}\lesssim 3 GeV. At the same time, no constraints on the scalar-mediator mass are imposed by these data. (iii) Both scalar- and vector-mediator scenarios allow a good description of the differential distributions in B → K ​ M X B\to KM_{X} measured by Belle-II and an extraction of dark-model parameters within both scenarios.

The ratio

may be measured experimentally, thus providing a clear signature of the DM scenario
(i.e., the mediator spin), independently of DM parameters. The form factor uncertainties mentioned above
cancel to a large extent in the ratio, so the uncertainty in our predictions for this ratio does not exceed a 5% level.

III.3
The ratio of the integrated rates in
B\xrightarrow{R}K^{*}\bar{\chi}\chi
over
B\xrightarrow{R}K\bar{\chi}\chi

We now consider the ratio of the
integrated
rates of the excess events

\displaystyle R^{(R)}_{K^{*}/K}=\Gamma(B\xrightarrow{R}K^{*}\bar{\chi}\chi)/\Gamma(B\xrightarrow{R}K\bar{\chi}\chi).

(27)

This ratio shows a strong sensitivity to the mediator mass
M_{R}
and (to a lesser extent) to the mediator width
\Gamma_{0}^{R}
.
Crucial for us is that the dependencies on
M_{R}
are qualitatively different in S-and V-scenarios, see Fig.
3
.

We emphasize the qualitative difference between the behavior of
R^{(\phi)}_{K^{*}/K}
and
R^{(V)}_{K^{*}/K}
:
independently of the mediator mass,
R^{(\phi)}_{K^{*}/K}<1
whereas
R^{(V)}_{K^{*}/K}>1
.
Measuring of
R^{(R)}_{K^{*}/K}

III.4
Constraints on
B\to K^{*}M_{X}
based on the measured
B\to KM_{X}

We now combine our results for
R^{(R)}_{K^{*}/K}
with the theoretical estimates for the decay rates

\Gamma(B\to(K,K^{*})\bar{\nu}\nu)_{\rm SM}
and the conjecture that the observed excess in
B\to KM_{X}

is due to the decay into invisible DM fermions. We can write the following sequence of identities

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(B\to K^{*}M_{X})}{\Gamma(B\to K^{*}\bar{\nu}\nu)_{\rm SM}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(B\to K^{*}\bar{\nu}\nu)_{\rm SM}+\Gamma(B\to K^{*}\bar{\chi}\chi)}{\Gamma(B\to K^{*}\bar{\nu}\nu)_{\rm SM}}

(28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\underbrace{\frac{\Gamma(B\to K^{*}\bar{\chi}\chi)}{\Gamma(B\to K\bar{\chi}\chi)}}_{\mbox{Calculated }R_{K^{*}/K}}\underbrace{\frac{\Gamma(B\to K\bar{\chi}\chi)}{\Gamma(B\to K\bar{\nu}\nu)_{\rm SM}}}_{\mbox{Belle-II: }4.4\pm 1.5}\underbrace{\frac{\Gamma(B\to K\bar{\nu}\nu)_{\rm SM}}{\Gamma(B\to K^{*}\bar{\nu}\nu)_{\rm SM}}}_{{\rm Theory}:(4.44\pm 0.30)/(9.8\pm 1.4)}.

Making use of the results for the different factors, indicated in Eq. (
28
) with their respective uncertainties,
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{Br(B\to K^{*}M_{X})}{Br(B\to K^{*}\bar{\nu}\nu)_{\rm SM}}=1+(2\pm 0.6)R^{(R)}_{K^{*}/K}.

(29)

This expression may be combined with the Belle upper limit, Eq. (
2
), and a theory
estimate
Br(B\to K^{*}\bar{\nu}\nu)_{\rm SM}=(9.8\pm 1.4)\cdot 10^{-6}
, Eq. (
I
),
yielding the following constraint:

\displaystyle\frac{Br(B\to K^{*}M_{X})}{Br(B\to K^{*}\bar{\nu}\nu)_{\rm SM}}<3.1.

(30)

Fig.
4
displays the ratio (
28
) for the S- and V-scenarios.

So, within S-scenario no restrictions on the mediator mass emerge from the upper experimental limit Eq. (

However, within the V-scenario Eq. (

M_{V}

only a light vector mediator is allowed,

IV
BELLE-II data fitting and extraction of DM parameters

The Belle-II experiment, as described in
(
Belle-II:2023esi
)
,
analyzes partially reconstructed events to increase the statistical sample.
For such events, the direction of the
B
meson cannot be determined.
Therefore, instead of
q^{2}=(p_{B}-p_{K})^{2}
the variable
q_{\rm rec}^{2}
is used:

q^{2}_{\rm rec}=E_{B}^{2}+M_{K}^{2}-2E_{B}E_{K},

(32)

where
E_{B}
and
E_{K}
are the energies of the
B
and
K
mesons in the
center-of-mass frame of the
B\bar{B}
-meson pair produced in
\Upsilon(4S)
decays.

Our theoretical predictions for the differential distributions are obtained as
functions of
q^{2}
. In order to apply them to the analysis of the Belle-II data given as functions of
q_{\rm rec}^{2}
,
an appropriate recalculation (averaging) of our results
q^{2}\to q_{\rm rec}^{2}
should be done.
The recalculation procedure as well as the data fitting algorithm that yields the DM parameters
were described in full detail in Sections III and IV of
blm2025
for the S-scenario and will not be repeated here.
We now apply precisely the same procedures for the V-scenario.

IV.2
Measurable vs. theoretical observables

In Sec.
III

we introduced a number of theoretical observables which may serve as
clear discriminators between DM scenarios. It is time to demonstrate that these observables
remain largely unaffected by the smearing procedures adopted in the data analysis.

Figure
6
shows our prediction for the differential distributions of the excess events
in
B\to K^{*}M_{X}
and
B\to KM_{X}
decays. Assuming the same detection efficiency for

B\to K^{*}M_{X}
and
B\to KM_{X}
decays,
the ratio
\overline{R^{(R)}_{K^{*}/K}}(q_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2})
of excess events in the
experimentally measured differential distributions in
B\to K^{*}M_{X}
and
B\to KM_{X}
decays,

\displaystyle\overline{R^{(R)}_{K^{*}/K}}(q_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2})=\frac{\overline{d\Gamma^{\rm eff}}(B\xrightarrow{R}K^{*}\chi\bar{\chi})/dq_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2}}{\overline{d\Gamma^{\rm eff}}(B\xrightarrow{R}K\chi\bar{\chi})/dq_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2}},

(35)

(where
\Gamma^{\rm eff}
indicates that the convolution with the detection efficiencies has been performed,
see
blm2025
) is practically independent of the model parameters, such as
M_{R}
,

m_{\chi}
, and
\Gamma^{0}_{R}
, as the dependence on these parameters approximately cancels out in the ratio.
(Recall that the dependence on DM parameters cancels exactly in the theoretical ratio
\hat{R}^{(R)}_{K^{*}/K}
).

Consequently, we have an approximate equality between the observable ratio

\overline{R^{(R)}_{K^{*}/K}}(q_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2})

and the theoretical ratio
\hat{R}^{(R)}_{K^{*}/K}(q_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2})
, which is fulfilled with very high accuracy in a
broad range of momentum transfers (see Fig.
6
):

\displaystyle\overline{R^{(R)}_{K^{*}/K}}(q_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2})\simeq\hat{R}^{(R)}_{K^{*}/K}(q_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2}).

(36)

We therefore conclude that the theoretical observables discussed in Sec.
III
indeed provide
efficient signatures of DM scenarios for data analysis.

V
Summary and Conclusions

We studied conjectures that the observed excess in
B\to KM_{X}
is due to the decay
B\xrightarrow{R}K\bar{\chi}\chi
into two DM fermions
\bar{\chi}\chi
, with
R=\phi,V
.
Our main findings are listed below:

[i] Within the DM conjecture, ratios of certain observables in
B\to K^{*}M_{X}
and
B\to KM_{X}
decays
have qualitatively different behaviors for S- and V-scenarios:

(a) The ratios of the
differential rates
of the excess events in
B\to K^{*}M_{X}
and
B\to KM_{X}
decays,

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{K^{*}/K}(q^{2})=\frac{d\Gamma(B\to K^{*}\bar{\chi}\chi)/dq^{2}}{d\Gamma(B\to K\bar{\chi}\chi)/dq^{2}},

(37)

does not depend on DM parameters, but only on the mediator spin. Measuring this ratio allows one to determine
the mediator spin and to rule out at least one of the DM scenarios.

(b) The ratio of the
integrated rates
of the excess events in
B\to K^{*}M_{X}
and
B\to KM_{X}
decays,

\displaystyle R_{K^{*}/K}=\frac{\Gamma(B\to K^{*}\bar{\chi}\chi)}{\Gamma(B\to K\bar{\chi}\chi)},

depends on dark-matter parameters, such as the mediator mass and width, and also has qualitatively different
behavior for S- and V-scenarios. Combining the measured
\Gamma(B\to KM_{X})
and the upper limit
on
\Gamma(B\to K^{*}M_{X})
provides the allowed regions of the mediator mass within each of the scenarios.
Whereas no constraints on the scalar mediator mass emerge (that is, for any

M_{\phi}
both the measured
\Gamma(B\to KM_{X})
and the upper limit on
\Gamma(B\to K^{*}M_{X})
are satisfied),
the vector-mediator mass
M_{V}
is tightly constrained to the region of small masses,
M_{V}\lesssim 3
GeV.

We emphasize that measuring
\hat{R}_{K^{*}/K}(q^{2})
and
R_{K^{*}/K}
are excellent discriminators between these two scenarios.

[ii] Both S- and V-scenarios allow a good description of the shape of the observed excess events
and extraction of the corresponding DM parameters. A fit to the differential distributions of
the excess events in
B\to KM_{X}
yields
blm2025

\displaystyle M_{\phi}=2.4\pm 0.4\mbox{ GeV}.

(38)

For
M_{V}
, using the constraint
M_{V}\lesssim 3
GeV from the upper limit on the
integrated

rate
\Gamma(B\to K^{*}M_{X})
,
the fitting procedure prefers
M_{V}
on the upper boundary of the allowed region, so we just set

\displaystyle M_{V}=3\mbox{ GeV}

(39)

without providing uncertainties to this number. The values of the other extracted DM parameters are
given in Eqs. (
33
) and (
34
).

[iii] Within
any top-philic mediator scenario
one expects a similar excess in

B\to(\pi,\rho)M_{X}
and
B\to(K,K^{*})M_{X}
decays
relative to their SM values:

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(B\to\pi M_{X})}{\Gamma(B\to\pi\bar{\nu}\nu)_{\rm SM}}\simeq\frac{\Gamma(B\to KM_{X})}{\Gamma(B\to K\bar{\nu}\nu)_{\rm SM}}

and, similarly,

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(B\to\rho M_{X})}{\Gamma(B\to\rho\bar{\nu}\nu)_{\rm SM}}\simeq\frac{\Gamma(B\to K^{*}M_{X})}{\Gamma(B\to K^{*}\bar{\nu}\nu)_{\rm SM}}.

(This happens because
\Gamma(B\to\pi\bar{\nu}\nu)_{\rm SM}
and

\Gamma(B\xrightarrow{R}\pi\bar{\chi}\chi)
are proportional to
V_{tb}V^{*}_{td}

which cancels in the ratios. So, the l.h.s. and the r.h.s. of the ratios
above are equal to each other up to SU(3)-breaking effects related to
s
- and
d
-quark differences.)

These features are crucial signatures of the top-philic DM scenarios.

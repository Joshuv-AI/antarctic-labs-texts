Rigidity of the escaping set of polynomial automorphisms of ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2}
Sayani Bera, Kaushal Verma

Abstract
. Let H H be a polynomial automorphism of ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2} of positive entropy and degree d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 . We prove that the escaping set U + U^{+} (or equivalently, the non-escaping set K + K^{+} ), of H H is rigid under the action of holomorphic automorphisms of ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2} . Specifically, every holomorphic automorphism of ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2} that preserves U + U^{+} takes the form L ∘ H s L\circ H^{s} where s ∈ ℤ s\in\mathbb{Z} and L L belongs to a finite cyclic group of affine maps that preserve the escaping set. Second, note that the sub–level sets { G + < c } \{G^{+}<c\} , c > 0 c>0 , of the Green’s function G + G^{+} associated with the map H H are canonical examples of Short ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2} ’s. As a consequence of the above theorem, we show that the holomorphic automorphisms of these Short ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2} ’s are affine automorphisms of ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2} preserving the escaping set U + U^{+} . Hence, the automorphism group of these Short ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2} ’s are the same for every c > 0 c>0 and is a finite cyclic group.

1.
Introduction

The goal of this article is to study a
rigidity
property of the escaping set (or equivalently, the non-escaping set) of polynomial automorphisms of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
of positive topological entropy. By a classical theorem of Friedland–Milnor
[
14
]
, they are conjugate to a polynomial automorphism
H
of the form

\displaystyle H(x,y)=H_{m}\circ\cdots\circ H_{1}(x,y)

(1.1)

where
H_{i}(x,y)=(y,p_{i}(y)-a_{i}x)
,
p_{i}
is a polynomial in one variable with degree at least
2
and
a_{i}\neq 0
for every
1\leq i\leq m
. Such maps
H
and their factors
H_{i}
as in (
1.1
) will be referred to as
generalised
and
simple
Hénon maps respectively. Note that the degree of the first and second coordinates of
H
are

d^{\prime}=d_{m-1}\ldots d_{1}\text{ and }d=d_{m}d_{m-1}\ldots d_{1}

respectively, and that the Jacobian determinant of
H
is a constant and equals
a=a_{1}a_{2}\ldots a_{m}
.

Let
{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
denote the group of holomorphic automorphisms of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. We identify all elements of
{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
that preserve the escaping set (or equivalently, the non-escaping set) associated with a generalised Hénon map
H
. Before proceeding, let us first recall the following definitions. The
non-escaping set
or the
bounded Julia set
associated with a generalised Hénon map
H
is defined as

K^{+}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:\|H^{n}(z)\|\text{ is bounded}\}
and the
escaping set
is
U^{+}=\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus K^{+}
. Furthermore,
H
extends to
\mathbb{P}^{2}
as a birational map with an indeterminacy at the point
I^{+}=[1:0:0]
and a super-attracting fixed point at
I^{-}=[0:1:0]
. The escaping set
U^{+}
corresponds to the intersection of the basin of attraction of
I^{-}=[0:1:0]
with
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, and
I^{+}
is the only accumulation point of the non-escaping set
K^{+}
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. See
[
3
]
,
[
13
]
for these details. The inverse of
H
,

H^{-1}(x,y)=H_{1}^{-1}\circ\cdots\circ H_{m}^{-1}(x,y)

has an indeterminacy at
I^{-}=[0:1:0]
when extended to
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and its
non-escaping
and
escaping sets
are defined as
K^{-}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:\|H^{-n}(z)\|\text{ is bounded}\}\text{ and }U^{-}=\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus K^{-}
respectively.
Furthermore,
U^{-}
corresponds to the basin of attraction of
I^{+}=[1:0:0]
of
H^{-1}
when extended to
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
\overline{K^{-}}=K^{-}\cup I^{-}
.

The phenomenon of
rigidity
for generalised and simple maps Hénon has various manifestations. To place this work in context, it is useful to recall a few related results.

First, given a pair of generalised Hénon maps
H
and
F
that have the same non-escaping set, Lamy
[
16
]
showed that
H,F
must be the same up to possibly different iterates. That is, there exist positive integers
m,n
such that
H^{m}=F^{n}
. As a consequence,
H^{-1}
and
F^{-1}
will also have the same non-escaping and escaping sets.

Second, the notion of
very rigid
appears in the work of Dinh–Sibony
[
10
]
who showed that there exists a unique positive
dd^{c}
-closed
(1,1)
current of mass
1
on
K^{+}
. This extends results from
[
3
]
,
[
13
]
, and leverages the potential-theoretic properties of the Green’s function associated with
K^{+}
.

Third,
[
5
]
shows that if
f\in{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
preserves both
K^{\pm}
, then the following dichotomy holds: either
f
is linear or one of
f
or
f^{-1}
must be a generalised Hénon map that commutes with
H
up to a diagonal unimodular change of variables. As a consequence, the non-escaping sets of
f
(or
f^{-1}
as the case may be) and
H
must be the same. The proof uses the very rigid property of
K^{\pm}
alluded to above and the dependence of the Green’s functions on the Böttcher functions near the super-attracting fixed points
I^{\pm}
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
constructed in
[
15
]
. Furthermore, by combining the above results, it is shown in
[
6
]
that if
f
is an affine map that preserves the non-escaping set of a
simple
Hénon map
H
, then
f
is conjugate to the linear map
(x,y)\mapsto(\eta x,\eta^{d}y)
where
\eta
is a
(d^{2}-1)
-root of unity, and
f\circ H^{2}=H^{2}\circ f
.

Next, Pal in
[
18
]
showed that two simple Hénon maps
H,F
with the same degree that have biholomorphic escaping sets are related via affine automorphisms
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
, i.e.,
H=A_{1}\circ F\circ A_{2}
. This generalises an earlier result of Bonnot–Radu–Tanase
[
7
]
.

Finally, and more recently, Cantat–Dujardin
[
9
]
show that if a pair of polynomial automorphisms
f,g
in
{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
with dynamical degree
d\geq 2
are conjugate by some
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
, then
\varphi
must be a polynomial automorphism. This confirms a conjecture of Friedland–Milnor and can be viewed as a rigidity result for generalised Hénon maps. Related to this circle of ideas on conjugacy and motivated by a question of Favre
[
11
]
, Bacher
[
1
]
shows that a pair of generalised Hénon maps are conjugate in
{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
precisely when the Kato surfaces associated to them near the super-attracting fixed point
I^{-}
are biholomorphic.

The results in
[
6
]
and
[
18
]
build on the unpublished work of Bousch
[
8
]
that uses the description of an intermediate cover of
U^{+}
given by Hubbard–Oberste-Vorth in
[
15
]
to compute the automorphism group of
U^{+}
for simple quadratic Hénon maps. Our goal is to further generalise these results by identifying all automorphisms of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
that preserve
U^{+}
associated with generalised Hénon maps. Here is the main result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
H
be a generalised Hénon map of the form (
1.1
), i.e.,

\displaystyle H(x,y)=H_{m}\circ\cdots\circ H_{1}(x,y)

where
H_{i}(x,y)=(y,p_{i}(y)-a_{i}x)
,
p_{i}
is a polynomial with degree
d_{i}\geq 2
and
a_{i}\neq 0
for every
1\leq i\leq m
. Then the non-escaping set
K^{+}
, or equivalently, the escaping set
U^{+}
, associated with
H
is rigid under the action of
{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
. In other words, any
f\in{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
that satisfies

f(K^{+})=K^{+}\text{ or equivalently }f(U^{+})=U^{+},

is of the form
f=L\circ H^{s}\text{ with }s\in\mathbb{Z},
where
L
is an affine map that preserves both
K^{\pm}
. In addition,

\mathcal{L}=\{L:L\text{ is an affine map such that }L(K^{\pm})=K^{\pm}\}\leq\mathbb{Z}_{(d+d^{\prime})(d-1)}

Here, the assumption that
f(K^{-})=K^{-}
, which was imposed in
[
5
]
is not required, and this is the main thrust of this theorem. The proof consists of several steps that are summarized as follows.

First, Theorem
2.1
in Section
2
extends the construction of the intermediate cover of
U^{+}
by Hubbard–Oberste-Vorth in
[
15
]
to the case of generalised Hénon maps. Next, let
{\rm Aut}_{1}(U^{+})
be the subgroup of the holomorphic automorphism group
{\rm Aut}(U^{+})
whose elements induce the identity map on the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(U^{+})
. Theorem
2.1
leads to a better understanding of the structure of
{\rm Aut}(U^{+})
. This is the content of Theorem
3.1
, which shows that modulo a copy of
\mathbb{C}
,
{\rm Aut}(U^{+})
is at most finite cyclic. The proof relies on adapting several ideas from
[
8
]
. Using this information about the structure of
{\rm Aut}(U^{+})
, Theorem
4.1
identifies all elements of
{\rm Aut}(U^{+})\cap{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
whose lifts to the intermediate cover of
U^{+}
constructed above have a specific form. The final step is completed in Theorem
4.7
which extends the observations made in the proof of Theorem
4.1
and shows that
{\rm Aut}(U^{+})\cap{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
is a finite cyclic group whose order can be bounded above in terms of the degree of
H
. Similar results were observed earlier in
[
4
]
.

Recall that the Green’s functions associated to the sets
K^{\pm}
are defined as

G_{H}^{+}(z)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{d^{n}}\log^{+}\|H^{n}(z)\|\text{ and }G_{H}^{-}(z)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{d^{n}}\log^{+}\|H^{-n}(z)\|.

They are non-negative, continuous, plurisubharmonic functions on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
and satisfy the functorial property

\displaystyle G_{H}^{\pm}(H^{s}(z))=d^{\pm s}G_{H}^{\pm}(z)

(1.2)

for every
s\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Further,
K^{\pm}=\{G_{H}^{\pm}=0\}
and

\mu_{H}^{+}=\frac{1}{2\pi}dd^{c}G_{H}^{+}\text{ and }\mu_{H}^{-}=\frac{1}{2\pi}dd^{c}G_{H}^{-}

are
dd^{c}
-closed positive
(1,1)
currents of mass 1 supported on
K_{H}^{+}
and
K_{H}^{-}
, respectively. As indicated earlier,
\mu_{H}^{\pm}
are precisely those currents that bestow on
K_{H}^{\pm}
their very rigid property.

For every
c>0
, the sub-level set

\Omega_{c}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:G_{H}^{+}(z)<c\}

is known to be a
Short

\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
Short

\mathbb{C}^{2}
’s are domains whose infinitesimal Kobayashi metric vanishes identically but at the same time admit a non-constant bounded plurisubharmonic function and which can be exhausted by an increasing union of domains biholomorphic to the ball. They were first observed and constructed by Fornæss in
[
12
]
.

In
[
6
]
the analytical and topological structure of an intermediate cover of a punctured
Short

\mathbb{C}^{2}
, i.e.,
\Omega_{c}^{\prime}=\Omega_{c}\setminus K^{+}
, corresponding to a simple Hénon map was explored in a manner similar to
[
15
]
,
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
. The philosophy behind this analysis is to understand the rigidity of an appropriate punctured neighbourhood of the super-attracting fixed point
I^{-}
of
H
, in contrast to the rigidity of the Kato surfaces associated to the neighbourhood of
I^{-}
, raised by Favre in
[
11
]
.

As a consequence, it is proved therein that the holomorphic automorphism group of certain punctured
Short

\mathbb{C}^{2}
’s corresponding to a simple Hénon map can at most be finitely many copies of
\mathbb{C}
. Let
{\rm Aut}(\Omega_{c})
and
{\rm Aut}(\Omega^{\prime}_{c})
denote the holomorphic automorphism groups of
\Omega_{c}
and
\Omega^{\prime}_{c}
respectively. The subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(\Omega^{\prime}_{c})
consisting of those elements that induce the identity map on the fundamental group
\uppi_{1}(\Omega^{\prime}_{c})
will be denoted by
{\rm Aut}_{1}(\Omega^{\prime}_{c})
.

Theorem
2.1
has consequences for understanding both
{\rm Aut}(\Omega_{c})
and
{\rm Aut}_{1}(\Omega^{\prime}_{c})
. First, we observe in Theorem
3.2
that covers of punctured
Short

\mathbb{C}^{2}
’s obtained as sub-level sets of generalised Hénon maps can be constructed. This leads to Corollary
3.5
in which the automorphism group of such punctured
Short

\mathbb{C}^{2}
’s can be identified in the same spirit as
[
6
, Theorem 1.2]
. Furthermore, we will use this representation to conclude the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
H
be a generalised Hénon map. Then

{\rm Aut}_{1}(U^{+})={\rm Aut}_{1}(\Omega_{c}^{\prime})

for every
c>0
.

By combining Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
, we obtain

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
H
be a generalised Hénon map and
f\in{\rm Aut}(\Omega_{c})
for some
c>0
. Then there exists an affine map
L\in{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
such that
L(K^{\pm})=K^{\pm}
and
L(z)=f(z)
on

\Omega_{c}
.

In other words,

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
H
be a generalised Hénon map. Then for every
c>0
, every element of
{\rm Aut}(\Omega_{c})
is the restriction of an affine map that preserves the escaping set (or non-escaping set) of
H
and
{\rm Aut}(\Omega_{c})
is a finite cyclic group of order at most
\mathbb{Z}_{d_{0}(d-1)}
. Here,
d_{0}
is an integer that depends on
d+d^{\prime}
.

An intermediate cover of the escaping set

The goal of this section is to prove an analog of the Hubbard–Oberste-Vorth result on covering spaces of

3.1.
The subgroup

As mentioned before, we adapt the idea of Bousch

]
to prove the theorem given below. Similar ideas were also used to describe the automorphism group of punctured

\mathbb{C}^{2}

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
{\rm Aut}_{1}(U^{+})
be as above. Then

\mathbb{C}\leq{\rm Aut}_{1}(U^{+})\leq\mathbb{C}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{d_{0}(d-1)},

where
d_{0}
is an appropriate factor of
d+d^{\prime}
.

Proof.

The proof consists of several steps. The main ingredient is to understand the lifts of elements in
{\rm Aut}_{1}(U^{+})
to
\mathbb{C}\times\left(\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}\right)
in a progressively better manner.

Step 1:
Let
\mathfrak{a}\in{\rm Aut}_{1}(U^{+})
. First, we claim that any lift of the map
\mathfrak{a}
to the covering space
\mathbb{C}\times\left(\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}\right)
is of the form

A(z,\zeta)=(\beta z+\gamma(\zeta),\alpha\zeta),

where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\alpha|,|\beta|=1
and
\gamma
is holomorphic. Furthermore, for every pair of integers
k,n
, the lift
A
commutes with the deck transformation
\upgamma_{k/d^{n}}
, i.e.,

A\circ\upgamma_{k/d^{n}}(z,\zeta)=\upgamma_{k/d^{n}}\circ A(z,\zeta).

To see this, let
A(z,\zeta)=\left({\rm A}_{1}(z,\zeta),{\rm A}_{2}(z,\zeta)\right).
For a fixed
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, the map
{\rm A}_{2}(\cdot,\zeta)
is an entire holomorphic function whose range is contained in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. Hence,
{\rm A}_{2}(\cdot,\zeta)
is a constant map and we can write
{\rm A}_{2}(z,\zeta)=h(\zeta)
. As
\mathfrak{a}\in{\rm Aut}_{1}(U^{+})
, both
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{a}^{-1}
induce the identity on the fundamental group of
U^{+}
and hence admit lifts to the covering space
\mathbb{C}\times\left(\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}\right)
. Let
A^{\prime}
be a lift of
\mathfrak{a}^{-1}
. Then

\widehat{\Pi}\circ A\circ A^{\prime}(z,\zeta)=\mathfrak{a}\circ\widehat{\Pi}\circ A^{\prime}(z,\zeta)=\mathfrak{a}\circ\mathfrak{a}^{-1}\circ\widehat{\Pi}(z,\zeta)=\widehat{\Pi}(z,\zeta),

where
\widehat{\Pi}
is the covering map from
\mathbb{C}\times\left(\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}\right)
to
U^{+}
as in Subsection
2.4
. Note that, both
A\circ A^{\prime}
and
A^{\prime}\circ A
are lifts of the identity map. Hence, there exist integers
k,k^{\prime},n,n^{\prime}
such that

A\circ A^{\prime}=\upgamma_{k/d^{n}}\text{ and }A^{\prime}\circ A=\upgamma_{k^{\prime}/d^{n^{\prime}}}.

Since
\upgamma_{k/d^{n}}
and
\upgamma_{k^{\prime}/d^{n^{\prime}}}
are deck transformations of the form (
2.2
), both
A
and
A^{\prime}
are automorphisms of
\mathbb{C}\times\left(\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}\right)
. Let

A^{-1}=\left(\tilde{{\rm A}}_{1}(z,\zeta),\tilde{h}(\zeta)\right).

Thus,
\tilde{{\rm A}}_{1}\circ(A_{1}(\cdot,\zeta),h(\zeta))=\textsf{Id}
for every
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
and
h\circ\tilde{h}\equiv\tilde{h}\circ h\equiv\textsf{Id}
. Hence,
{\rm A}_{1}(\cdot,\zeta)
is an automorphism of
\mathbb{C}
and
h
is an automorphism of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. In particular, there exists an
\alpha
with
|\alpha|=1
such that

\displaystyle h(\zeta)=\alpha\zeta\text{ and }{\rm A}_{1}(z,\zeta)=\beta(\zeta)z+\gamma(\zeta).

(3.1)

Now, choose
(z^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime})
and
(z,\zeta)
in the same fibre. Since
A
is a lift of the automorphism
\mathfrak{a}
,
A(z,\zeta)
and
A(z^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime})
also lie on the same fibre. Thus, by Theorem
2.1
, there exist deck transformations
\upgamma_{k/d^{n}}
and
\upgamma_{k^{\prime}/d^{n^{\prime}}}
such that

\upgamma_{k/d^{n}}\circ A(z,\zeta)=A(z^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime})=A\circ\upgamma_{k^{\prime}/d^{n^{\prime}}}(z,\zeta).

By comparing the second coordinate in the above equation, it follows that
\left[\frac{k}{d^{n}}\right]=\left[\frac{k^{\prime}}{d^{n^{\prime}}}\right]
and hence

\upgamma_{k/d^{n}}\circ A(z,\zeta)=A\circ\upgamma_{k/d^{n}}(z,\zeta).

Also, from (
2.2
),

\upgamma_{k/d^{n}}(z,\zeta)=\left(z+\frac{d}{a}\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac{d}{a}\right)^{l}\left(Q\left(\zeta^{d^{l}}\right)-Q\left(\left(e^{2\pi ik/d^{n}}\cdot\zeta\right)^{d^{l}}\right)\right),e^{2\pi ik/d^{n}}\cdot\zeta\right)=(z^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime}).

Hence
\zeta^{\prime}=e^{2\pi ik/d^{n}}\zeta
, and by equating the first coordinate, we get

\displaystyle z+\frac{d}{a}\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac{d}{a}\right)^{l}\left(Q\left(\zeta^{d^{l}}\right)-Q\left(\left(e^{2\pi ik/d^{n}}\cdot\zeta\right)^{d^{l}}\right)\right)=z^{\prime}.

Again, by equating the first coordinate of
\upgamma_{k/d^{n}}\circ A(z,\zeta)=A(z^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime})
, we get

\displaystyle\beta(\zeta)z+\beta(\zeta)\frac{d}{a}\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac{d}{a}\right)^{l}\left(Q\left(\alpha^{d^{l}}\zeta^{d^{l}}\right)-Q\left(\left(e^{2\pi ik/d^{n}}\cdot\alpha\zeta\right)^{d^{l}}\right)\right)+\gamma(\zeta)=\beta(\zeta^{\prime})z^{\prime}+\gamma(\zeta^{\prime}).

Note that the above identities are true whenever both
(z,\zeta)
and
(z^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime})
lie in the same fibre. Thus, by comparing coefficients of
z
on both sides,
\beta(\zeta)=\beta(\zeta^{\prime})=\beta(e^{2\pi ik/d^{n}}\zeta)
, for all integers
k,n\geq 1
. That is,
\beta
is constant on the circle of radius
|\zeta|>1
. Hence,
\beta
is a constant function. So, the above relation reduces to the following

\displaystyle\beta\frac{d}{a}\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac{d}{a}\right)^{l}\left(Q\left(\zeta^{d^{l}}\right)-Q\left(\left(e^{2\pi ik/d^{n}}\cdot\zeta\right)^{d^{l}}\right)\right)+\gamma(\zeta^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{a}\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac{d}{a}\right)^{l}\left(Q\left(\alpha^{d^{l}}\zeta^{d^{l}}\right)-Q\left(\left(e^{2\pi ik/d^{n}}\cdot\alpha\zeta\right)^{d^{l}}\right)\right)+\gamma(\zeta).

Also,
\zeta/\zeta^{\prime}
lies on the unit circle whenever
(z^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime})
is on the same fibre as
(z,\zeta)
. So,
\gamma(\zeta)-\gamma(\zeta^{\prime})
is uniformly bounded for a fixed
\zeta
. Thus, for every
k,n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\beta\frac{d}{a}\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac{d}{a}\right)^{l}\left(Q\left(\zeta^{d^{l}}\right)-Q\left(\left(e^{2\pi ik/d^{n}}\cdot\zeta\right)^{d^{l}}\right)\right)-\frac{d}{a}\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac{d}{a}\right)^{l}\left(Q\left(\alpha^{d^{l}}\zeta^{d^{l}}\right)-Q\left(\left(e^{2\pi ik/d^{n}}\cdot\alpha\zeta\right)^{d^{l}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{a}\right)^{n}\left[\left(\beta-\alpha^{d^{n-1}(d+d^{\prime})}\right)\left(1-e^{2\pi ikd^{\prime}/d}\right)\zeta^{d^{n-1}(d+d^{\prime})}\right]+R_{k,n}(\zeta)=\gamma(e^{2\pi ik/d^{n}}\zeta)-\gamma(\zeta),

(3.2)

where

is a polynomial of degree at most
d^{n}
. Let
a_{n}=\alpha^{d^{n-1}(d+d^{\prime})}.

Claim:

for

sufficiently large.

The proof of the claim is the same as discussed in
[
6

. We briefly revisit the steps. Fix a
k=1
. Let

\displaystyle L_{n}(\zeta)=Q(\zeta)-Q(e^{2\pi i/d^{n}}\zeta)\text{ and }\widetilde{L}_{n}^{l}(\zeta)=\beta L_{n-l}(\zeta)-L_{n-l}(\alpha^{d^{l}}\zeta)

(3.3)

where

and

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac{d}{a}\right)^{l+1}\widetilde{L}_{n}^{l}\left(\zeta^{d^{l}}\right)=\gamma\left(e^{2\pi i/d^{n}}\zeta\right)-\gamma(\zeta).

(3.4)

Since

is a monic polynomial of degree
d+d^{\prime}
and

, both

and
\widetilde{L}_{n}^{l}
are polynomials of degree at most

and their coefficients are uniformly bounded for every

and

. Let

\widetilde{L}_{n}^{l}(\zeta)=M_{n}^{l}\zeta^{d+d^{\prime}}+\text{ l.o.t.}

Then choose

such that

\left|\widetilde{L}_{n}^{l}(\zeta)\right|<M|\zeta|^{d+d^{\prime}}\text{ and }\left|\widetilde{L}_{n}^{l}(\zeta)-M_{n}^{l}\zeta^{d+d^{\prime}}\right|<M|\zeta|^{d+d^{\prime}-1}

Note that
M_{n}^{n-1}=(\beta-a_{n})(1-e^{2\pi id^{\prime}/d})
and

where

. Thus, either
\{a_{n}\}
is dense in the unit circle or

for some

and

is a pre-periodic fixed point of

. Suppose that the sequence

Now from (

) and (

\displaystyle\left|\gamma\left(e^{2\pi i/d^{n}}\zeta\right)-\gamma(\zeta)-\left(\frac{d}{a}\right)^{n}M_{n}^{n-1}\zeta^{d^{n-1}(d+d^{\prime})}\right|\leq\max\left\{1,\left|\frac{d}{a}\right|^{n}\right\}nM|\zeta|^{d^{n-1}(d+d^{\prime}-1)}.

for

. By the ratio test, for a fixed

\zeta^{d^{n-1}}\to\infty
and
\left(\frac{d}{a}\right)^{n}\zeta^{d^{n-1}}\to\infty
as

Also
\gamma\left(e^{2\pi i/d^{n}}\zeta\right)-\gamma(\zeta)

\displaystyle 0\neq|\delta|=\lim_{l\to\infty}\left|M_{n_{l}}^{n_{l}-1}\right|=

\displaystyle\lim_{l\to\infty}\left|\frac{\gamma\left(e^{2\pi i/d^{n_{l}}}\zeta\right)-\gamma(\zeta)}{\left(\frac{d}{a}\right)^{n_{l}}\zeta^{d^{n_{l}-1}(d+d^{\prime})}}-M_{n_{l}}^{n_{l}-1}\right|

(d+d^{\prime})

Step 2:

\tilde{\beta}^{d-1}=\tilde{\alpha}^{d_{0}\big(d-1\big)}=1

A
be an arbitrary lift of the automorphism

. By

Then by the above step there exists

\alpha=e^{{2\pi ik_{0}}/{d^{n_{0}-1}}(d+d^{\prime})(d-1)}.

\widetilde{A}
be lifts of
\mathfrak{a}

with

, and

,

.Thus

Step 3:

Step 4:

,

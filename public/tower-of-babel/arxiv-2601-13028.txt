Generalized MICZ-Kepler systems on three-dimensional sphere and hyperboloid
Levon Mardoyan, Armen Nersessian

Abstract
We propose analogs of the generalized MICZ-Kepler system on the three-dimensional sphere and (two-sheet) hyperboloid. We then construct their energy spectra and normalized wave functions, concluding that the suggested systems are minimally superintegrable.



I
Introduction

About five decades ago, Zwanziger
Zw
and McIntosh and Cisneros
MIC
independently suggested a maximally superintegrable generalization of the three-dimensional Kepler-Coulomb system, specified by the presence of a Dirac monopole and inheriting the qualitative properties of the Kepler-Coulomb problem. This system is now known as the MICZ-Kepler system. The similarity between the MICZ-Kepler and Kepler-Coulomb systems has a transparent explanation. As is known, the Kepler-Coulomb system can be obtained from the four-dimensional oscillator via the so-called Kustaanheimo-Stiefel transformation assuming reduction by a
U(1)
group action
KS
. For zero value of the
U(1)
generator this transformation yields the Kepler-Coulomb system, while for a non-zero value it yields the MICZ-Kepler system
iwai
.

The Kepler-Coulomb system admits a generalization to spheres and pseudospheres (two-sheet hyperboloids) while preserving its maximal superintegrability property
Higgs
.
The (pseudo)spherical generalizations of the MICZ-Kepler system that inherit maximal superintegrability are also known
NP
.
Moreover, any classical/quantum mechanical system with a central potential defined on a three-dimensional
so(3)
-invariant space admits a “MICZ-Kepler extension” preserving its qualitative properties.
For this purpose one should incorporate the Dirac monopole and modify the potential term as follows
mny
:

V(r)\quad\rightarrow\quad V(r)+\frac{\hbar^{2}s^{2}}{2\mu g(r)r^{2}},

(1)

where
\mathbf{r}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
are the coordinates in which the metric takes the conformal-flat form

ds^{2}=g(r)d\mathbf{r}d\mathbf{r},

(2)

s=0,\pm 1/2,\pm 1,\ldots
is the Dirac monopole number, and
\mu
is a mass of probe particle.

Textbook examples of integrable deformations of the Kepler-Coulomb problem, e.g., the two-center Kepler-Coulomb problem and the Stark-Coulomb problem, also admit MICZ-extensions and further (pseudo)spherical generalizations
KNO
;
Oz
;
Vahagn
.
In contrast with these systems, the study of “MICZ-extensions” of less known (“non-textbook”) integrable deformations of the Kepler-Coulomb system is in its initial stage.

An exception is the so-called “generalized Kepler-Coulomb system” defined by the potential

V_{gCoulomb}=\frac{\lambda_{1}}{r(r+x_{3})}+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{r(r-x_{3})}-\frac{e^{2}}{r},\qquad{\rm where}\qquad r=|\mathbf{r}|,\quad\mathbf{r}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}),\quad\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\geq 0.

(3)

This system has four independent constants of motion (and thus belongs to the class of “minimally superintegrable systems”)
smor
and has been studied in great detail both classically and quantum mechanically (see, e.g.,
evans
).
For
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}\neq 0
, the potential (
3

Hartmann

Hothers
.

The “MICZ-extension” of the generalized Kepler-Coulomb system, now known as the “generalized MICZ-Kepler system”, was suggested by one of the authors in
Mardoyan1
and subsequently studied in further publications, see, e.g.,
gMICZ

However, its generalizations to curved spaces have not yet been considered.

In this paper we fill this gap by proposing the “generalized MICZ-Kepler systems on the three-dimensional sphere and hyperboloid (pseudosphere)”. In projective coordinates, where the metric takes conformal-flat form (
2

V_{gMICZ}=\frac{1}{g(r)}\left(\frac{\hbar^{2}s^{2}}{2\mu r^{2}}+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{r(r+x_{3})}+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{r(r-x_{3})}\right)+V(r),

(4)

where

g(r)=\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon r^{2}}{4R^{2}_{0}}\right)^{-2},\qquad V(r)=-\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon r^{2}}{4R^{2}_{0}}\right)\frac{e^{2}}{r}+\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2}\frac{e^{2}}{R_{0}},\qquad\varepsilon=\pm 1.

(5)

The case
\varepsilon=1
corresponds to the system on the three-dimensional sphere, and
\varepsilon=-1
to the three-dimensional hyperboloid (pseudosphere).
Respectively,
r\in[0,\infty)
for the sphere, and
r\in[0,2R_{0})
for the hyperboloid, with
R_{0}
being their radii.

We construct the spectra and normalized wavefunctions of these systems and find that their spectra depend on two quantum numbers, i.e., they are minimally superintegrable and should have four functionally independent integrals (including the Hamiltonian).

Furthermore, from our consideration it becomes clear that the expression (
4

defines the relevant “generalized MICZ-extension” not only for the Coulomb system on the three-dimensional (pseudo)sphere,
but for the particle moving on on any
so(3)
-invariant space (equipped with the metric (
2

V(r)

II
Generalized MICZ-Kepler system

In this Section we present the main properties of the generalized MICZ-Kepler system, which will be used in our future considerations.
This system is defined by the Hamiltonian
Mardoyan1

\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{gMICZ}=\frac{\mathbf{\widehat{p}}^{2}}{2\mu}+\frac{\hbar^{2}s^{2}}{2\mu r^{2}}+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{r(r+x_{3})}+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{r(r-x_{3})}-\frac{e^{2}}{r},

(6)

where

\widehat{\mathbf{p}}:=-\imath\hbar\boldsymbol{\partial}-\frac{e}{c}\mathbf{A},\quad\mathbf{A}=\frac{g}{r(r-x_{3})}\left(x_{2},-x_{1},0\right)\;:\quad\left[\widehat{p}_{i},\widehat{p}_{j}\right]=-\imath\hbar s\frac{\varepsilon_{ijk}x^{k}}{r^{3}},\quad\left[\widehat{p}_{i},x_{j}\right]=-\imath\hbar\delta_{ij}.

(7)

Clearly,
\mathbf{A}
is the vector potential of a Dirac monopole with magnetic charge
g=\hbar cs/e

(s=0,\pm 1/2,\pm 1,\dots)
, and with the singularity axis
x_{3}>0

Dirac
.

It should be noted that the Schrödinger equation for the generalized MIC-Kepler system admits separation of variables not only in spherical and parabolic coordinates, but also in prolate spheroidal coordinates
Mardoyan1
.
Moreover, the generalized MIC-Kepler system can be obtained from the four-dimensional double singular oscillator via the Kustaanheimo-Stiefel transformation
Mardoyan3
.

For
\lambda_{1,2}=0
, the generalized MICZ-Kepler system reduces to the original MICZ-Kepler system, which possesses conserved quantities given by the angular momentum and Runge-Lenz vector:

\widehat{\mathbf{J}}=\frac{1}{\hbar}\mathbf{r}\times\widehat{\mathbf{p}}-s\frac{\mathbf{r}}{r},\qquad\widehat{\mathbf{I}}=\frac{1}{2\mu}\left(\widehat{\mathbf{J}}\times\widehat{\mathbf{p}}-\widehat{\mathbf{p}}\times\widehat{\mathbf{J}}\right)-\frac{{e^{2}}}{\hbar}\frac{\mathbf{r}}{r}.

(8)

Clearly, these operators are not constants of motion for the generalized MICZ-Kepler system due to the perturbing term in the Hamiltonian (
6
).
Instead, the generalized MICZ-Kepler system has the following constants of motion:

\widehat{J}_{3}=x_{2}\widehat{p}_{1}-x_{1}\widehat{p}_{2}-\frac{sx_{3}}{r},\qquad\widehat{M}^{s}=\widehat{\mathbf{J}}^{2}+\frac{2\lambda_{1}r}{r+x_{3}}+\frac{2\lambda_{2}r}{r-x_{3}},\qquad\widehat{\cal I}\equiv\widehat{I}_{3}+\frac{\lambda_{1}(r-x_{3})}{r(r+x_{3})}-\frac{\lambda_{2}(r+x_{3})}{r(r-x_{3})}:

(9)

[\widehat{{\cal H}}_{gMICZ},\widehat{J}_{3}]=[\widehat{{\cal H}}_{gMICZ},\widehat{M}^{s}]=[\widehat{{\cal H}}_{gMICZ},\widehat{{\cal I}}]=0,\qquad[\widehat{J}_{3},\widehat{M}^{s}]=[\widehat{J}_{3},\widehat{{\cal I}}]=0,\qquad[\widehat{{\cal I}},\widehat{M}^{s}]=\widehat{S},

(10)

where
\widehat{S}^{2}
is a cubic function of the operators (
9
).
Hence, the generalized MICZ-Kepler system is a minimally superintegrable three-dimensional system: it has four functionally independent constants of motion (including the Hamiltonian), with three of them being in involution.
Therefore, its energy spectrum should depend on two quantum numbers. Below we will see that this is indeed the case.

Let us consider the spectral problem:

\widehat{H}_{gMICZ}\psi=E\psi,\quad\widehat{M}^{s}\psi=\widetilde{j}(\widetilde{j}+1)\psi,\quad\widehat{J}_{3}\psi=m\psi.

(11)

Following
Mardoyan1
(see also
Mardoyan4
), we introduce spherical coordinates

x_{1}+\imath x_{2}=r\sin\theta\;{\rm e}^{\imath\varphi},x_{3}=r\cos\theta
.

In these terms the first two commuting constants of motion in (
9
) depend only on angular coordinates:

\displaystyle\widehat{J}_{3}=-\imath\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi}+s,

(12)

\displaystyle\widehat{M}^{(s)}=-\left\{\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\left(\sin\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\right)+\frac{1}{4\cos^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\varphi^{2}}-\frac{4\mu\lambda_{1}}{\hbar^{2}}\right)+\frac{1}{4\sin^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}}\left[\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi}+2\imath s\right)^{2}-\frac{4\mu\lambda_{2}}{\hbar^{2}}\right]\right\}.

(13)

Then we immediately find that the spectral problem (
11
) admits separation of variables in these coordinates:

\displaystyle\psi\equiv\psi_{njm}^{(s)}\left(r,\theta,\varphi\right)=R_{njm}^{(s)}\left(r\right)\,Z^{(s)}_{jm}\left(\theta,\varphi\right),

(14)

where the angular wave function becomes an eigenfunction of the commuting operators
\widehat{M}^{(s)}
,
\widehat{J}_{3}
:

\widehat{M}^{(s)}\,Z^{(s)}_{jm}\left(\theta,\varphi\right)=\widetilde{j}(\widetilde{j}+1)Z^{(s)}_{jm}\left(\theta,\varphi\right),\qquad\widehat{J}_{z}\,Z^{(s)}_{jm}\left(\theta,\varphi\right)=m\,Z^{(s)}_{jm}\left(\theta,\varphi\right).

(15)

The eigenvalues in the spectral problem (
11
) are defined by the expressions

\displaystyle E_{n,m}^{(s)}=-\frac{\mu e^{4}}{2\hbar^{2}\left(n+\delta_{m}^{(s)}\right)^{2}},\qquad n=|s|+1,|s|+2,\ldots,

(16)

\displaystyle\widetilde{j}=j+\delta_{m}^{(s)},\qquad j=m_{+},m_{+}+1,\ldots,n-1,\qquad m=-j,-j+1,\ldots,j-1,j.

(17)

Here and in what follows we use the notation

m_{+}=\frac{\left|m+s\right|+\left|m-s\right|}{2},\quad\delta_{m}^{(s)}=\frac{m_{1}+m_{2}}{2}-m_{+},\quad m_{1}=\sqrt{\left(m-s\right)^{2}+\frac{4\mu\lambda_{1}}{\hbar^{2}}},\quad m_{2}=\sqrt{\left(m+s\right)^{2}+\frac{4\mu\lambda_{2}}{\hbar^{2}}}.

(18)

The wavefunctions are given by the expressions

R^{(s)}_{nj}=\frac{2\kappa^{2}\sqrt{r_{0}}}{\Gamma(2j+2\delta_{m}^{(s)}+2)}\,\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(n+j+2\delta_{m}^{(s)}+1)}{(n-j-1)!}}\left(2\kappa r\right)^{j+\delta_{m}^{(s)}}e^{-\kappa r}F(-n+j+1;2j+2\delta_{m}^{(s)}+2;2\kappa r),

(19)

Z^{(s)}_{jm}=\sqrt{\frac{(2j+2\delta_{m}^{(s)}+1)(j-m_{+})!\Gamma(j+m_{1}+m_{2}-m_{+}+1)}{4\pi\Gamma(j+m_{1}-m_{+}+1)\Gamma(j+m_{2}-m_{+}+1)}}\left(\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\right)^{m_{1}}\left(\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\right)^{m_{2}}P^{(m_{2},m_{1})}_{j-m_{+}}\left(\cos\theta\right)\,e^{\imath(m-s)\varphi},

(20)

where

r_{0}=\hbar^{2}/\mu e^{2}
is the Bohr radius,
\kappa=\sqrt{-2\mu E}/\hbar
,
F\left(a;c;x\right)
is the confluent hypergeometric function,
P^{(a,b)}_{n}(x)
is the Jacobi polynomial.

With these expressions at hand we are ready to construct and solve the quantum-mechanical generalized MICZ-Kepler systems on three-dimensional sphere and hyperboloid .

III
GENERALIZED MIC-KEPLER SYSTEM ON THE THREE-DIMENSIONAL SPHERE

The three-dimensional sphere of radius
R_{0}
embedded in the four-dimensional Euclidean space
\mathbf{R}^{4}
equipped with the metric
d\mathbf{y}d\mathbf{y}+dy_{4}dy_{4}
is defined by the constraint

\mathbf{y}^{2}+y_{4}^{2}=R^{2}_{0}
, where
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})

This constraint can be solved in projective coordinates
\mathbf{r}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})

\mathbf{y}=\frac{\mathbf{r}}{1+\frac{\mathbf{r}^{2}}{4R^{2}_{0}}},\quad\mathbf{y}_{4}=R_{0}\frac{1-\frac{\mathbf{r}^{2}}{4R^{2}_{0}}}{1+\frac{\mathbf{r}^{2}}{4R^{2}_{0}}}

(21)

and/or in (hyper)spherical coordinates:

\displaystyle y_{1}+\imath y_{2}=R_{0}\sin\chi\sin\theta e^{i\varphi},\qquad y_{3}=R_{0}\sin\chi\cos\theta,\qquad y_{4}=R_{0}\cos\chi,\qquad\chi,\theta\in[0,\pi],\quad\varphi\in[0,2\pi).

(22)

These coordinates are related as follows:

\displaystyle x_{1}+\imath x_{2}=\frac{2R_{0}\sin\chi\sin\theta e^{i\varphi}}{1+\cos\chi},\qquad x_{3}=\frac{2R_{0}\sin\chi\cos\theta}{1+\cos\chi}.

(23)

The induced metric on the sphere then takes the form

ds^{2}=\left(d\mathbf{y}d\mathbf{y}+dy_{4}dy_{4}\right)|_{\mathbf{y}^{2}+y_{4}^{2}=R^{2}_{0}}=\frac{d\mathbf{r}d\mathbf{r}}{(1+\frac{\mathbf{r}^{2}}{4R_{0}^{2}})^{2}}=R^{2}_{0}\left(d\chi^{2}+\sin^{2}\chi(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\varphi^{2})\right).

Thus, in projective coordinates the metric takes the conformal-flat form (
2

(25)

The Coulomb potential on the three-dimensional sphere is defined as a harmonic potential, i.e., a solution of the Laplace equation on the three-dimensional sphere, and has the form
Schrod

\displaystyle V(\chi)=-\frac{e^{2}}{R_{0}}\frac{y_{4}}{|\mathbf{y}|}=-\left({1-\frac{\mathbf{r}^{2}}{4R_{0}^{2}}}\right)\frac{e^{2}}{r}=-\frac{e^{2}}{R_{0}}\cot\chi,

(26)

while its dual is just the vector potential of a Dirac monopole (see, e.g., the second reference in

We then define the “generalized spherical MICZ-Kepler analog” of the generalized Kepler-Coulomb potential (
3

Rotational momentum generators

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\chi}\frac{\partial}{\partial\chi}\left(\sin^{2}\chi\frac{\partial}{\partial\chi}\right)-\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\chi}\widehat{M}^{(s)}+\frac{2\mu e^{2}R_{0}}{\hbar^{2}}\cot\chi+\frac{2\mu R^{2}_{0}}{\hbar^{2}}E\right\}\Psi(\chi)=0,

Hence, it admits separation of variables in hyperspherical coordinates. Namely, choosing the wavefunction in the form

\displaystyle\times\Biggl[\frac{\Gamma\left(n-j-p-1\right)}{\Gamma\left(-p\right)}+\frac{(n-j-p-1)(j+\delta_{m}^{(s)}+p+1+\imath\sigma)}{n+j+2\delta_{m}^{(s)}+1}\frac{\Gamma\left(n-j-p-1\right)}{\Gamma\left(1-p\right)}\Biggr]=1.

Taking into account that in the last expression the first term is non-zero only for
p=n-j-1
, and the second only for
p=0
and
p=n-j-1
, we obtain the expression for the normalization constant (
31
).

IV
Generalized MICZ-Kepler system on three-dimensional hyperboloid

The three-dimensional two-sheeted hyperboloid (pseudosphere) of radius
R_{0}
is the hypersurface in the four-dimensional Minkowski space (
\mathbf{R}^{1.3},\;dy_{0}dy_{0}-d\mathbf{y}d\mathbf{y})
defined by the constraint

y_{0}^{2}-\mathbf{y}^{2}=R^{2}_{0}
, where
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})
.

This constraint can be resolved in projective coordinates
\mathbf{r}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
:

\mathbf{y}=\frac{\mathbf{r}}{1-\frac{\mathbf{r}^{2}}{4R^{2}_{0}}},\quad\mathbf{y}_{0}=R_{0}\frac{1+\frac{\mathbf{r}^{2}}{4R^{2}_{0}}}{1-\frac{\mathbf{r}^{2}}{4R^{2}_{0}}},

(39)

and/or in (hyper)spherical coordinates
\tau,\theta,\varphi
:

\displaystyle y_{1}+y_{2}=R\sinh\tau\sin\theta e^{i\varphi},\qquad y_{3}=R\sinh\tau\cos\theta,\qquad y_{0}=R\cosh\tau,

(40)

where
\tau\in[0,\infty),\theta\in[0,\pi),\varphi\in[0,2\pi)
.

Respectively,

\displaystyle x_{1}+\imath x_{2}=\frac{2R_{0}\sinh\tau\sin\theta e^{i\varphi}}{1+\cosh\tau},\qquad x_{3}=\frac{2R_{0}\sinh\tau\cos\theta}{1+\cosh\tau}R_{0}.

(41)

The induced metric on the pseudosphere then takes the form:

ds^{2}=\left(dy_{0}dy_{0}-d\mathbf{y}d\mathbf{y}\right)|_{y_{0}^{2}-\mathbf{y}^{2}=R^{2}_{0}}=\frac{d\mathbf{r}d\mathbf{r}}{(1-\frac{\mathbf{r}^{2}}{4R_{0}^{2}})^{2}}=R^{2}_{0}\left(d\tau^{2}+\sinh^{2}\tau(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\varphi^{2})\right).

(42)

Thus, in projective coordinates the metric takes the conformal-flat form (
2
) with

g(r)=\left(1-\frac{\mathbf{r}^{2}}{4R_{0}^{2}}\right)^{-2}.

(43)

In these coordinates the rotational momentum generators
\mathbf{J}
are as in the flat case (
8
).

The Coulomb potential on the three-dimensional hyperboloid has the form
Schrod
:

\displaystyle V=-\frac{e^{2}}{R_{0}}\left(\frac{y_{0}}{|\mathbf{y}|}-1\right)=-\left(1-\frac{\mathbf{r}^{2}}{4R^{2}_{0}}\right)\frac{e^{2}}{|\mathbf{r}|}+\frac{e^{2}}{R_{0}}=-\frac{e^{2}}{R_{0}}\left(\coth\tau-1\right).

(44)

In analogy with the previous section, we define the hyperbolic analog of the generalized MICZ-Kepler potential (
3
) by the expression:

V_{gMICZ}=\frac{1}{g(r)}\left(\frac{\hbar^{2}s^{2}}{2r^{2}}+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{r(r+x_{3})}+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{r(r-x_{3})}\right)-\left({1-\frac{\mathbf{r}^{2}}{4R_{0}^{2}}}\right)\frac{e^{2}}{{r}}+\frac{e^{2}}{R_{0}},

(45)

where
g(r)
is defined by (
43
).

Then, taking into account the spectral problem (
11
), we can represent the quasi-radial Schrödinger equation for the generalized MICZ-Kepler system on the hyperboloid as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sinh^{2}\tau}\frac{d}{d\tau}\left(\sinh^{2}\tau\frac{d\mathcal{R}}{d\tau}\right)+\Biggl[\frac{2\mu R^{2}_{0}}{\hbar^{2}}\left(E-\frac{e^{2}}{R_{0}}\right)-\frac{(j+\delta_{m}^{(s)})(j+\delta_{m}^{(s)}+1)}{\sinh^{2}\tau}+\frac{2\mu e^{2}R_{0}}{\hbar^{2}}\coth\tau\Biggr]\mathcal{R}(\tau)=0.

This equation has been solved in
KMP
.
Using teh solution presented there we get the discrete energy spectrum of the generalized MICZ-Kepler system on the three-dimensional hyperboloid:

\displaystyle E_{n,m}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu R^{2}_{0}}\left[\left(n+\delta_{m}^{(s)}\right)^{2}-1\right]-\frac{\mu e^{4}}{2\hbar^{2}\left(n+\delta_{m}^{(s)}\right)^{2}}+\frac{e^{2}}{R_{0}},

(46)

were
n
is the principal quantum number. Let us notice that the discrete energy spectrum exists only under the condition

\displaystyle 0\leq n\leq\left[\sigma-\delta_{m}^{(s)}-1\right],

where
\left[a\right]
is the integer part of
a
.

For
s=\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=0
, the energy spectrum (
46
) transforms into the energy spectrum of the Coulomb problem on the two-sheeted hyperboloid obtained in
Infeld
by the factorization method proposed by Schrödinger
Schrod
.

The quasi-radial wave function of the generalized MICZ-Kepler system is as folows

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{nj}\left(\tau\right)=A_{nj}^{(s)}\left(\sinh\tau\right)^{j+\delta_{m}^{(s)}}e^{\tau(n-j-\sigma-1)}{{}_{2}F}_{1}\left(-n+j+1,j+\sigma+\delta_{m}^{(s)}+1;2j+2\delta_{m}^{(s)}+2;1-e^{-2\tau}\right),

(47)

where
A_{nj}^{(s)}
is normalization constant.

It can be calculated from the requirement that the quasi-radial wave function (
47
) satisfies the condition:

\displaystyle R^{3}_{0}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\sinh^{2}\tau\left|\mathcal{R}_{nj}\left(\tau\right)\right|^{2}d\tau=1.

(48)

The calculation of the integral (
48
) can be carried out in a similar way to the calculation of the normalization constant on the three-dimensional sphere. Namely, using relation (
33
) we pass to hypergeometric functions of the argument
e^{-2\tau}
, perform integration in accordance with the formula
Bateman1
:

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2\alpha t}\left(\sinh\beta t\right)^{\gamma}dt=\beta^{-1}2^{-1-\gamma}B\left(\frac{\alpha}{2\beta},1+\gamma\right),

apply twice the relation (
37
), and use formulae (
35
) and (
38
) for the gamma function.
Then we finally obtain:

A_{nj}^{(s)}=\frac{2^{j+\delta_{m}^{(s)}+1}}{\Gamma(2j+2\delta_{m}^{(s)}+2)}\sqrt{\frac{(\sigma^{2}-(n+\delta_{m}^{(s)})^{2})\Gamma(n+j+2\delta_{m}^{(s)}+1)\Gamma(j+\sigma+\delta_{m}^{(s)}+1)}{R^{3}_{0}(n+\delta_{m}^{(s)})(n-j-1)!\Gamma(\sigma-j-\delta_{m}^{(s)})}}.

(49)

For
s=\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=0
, the normalized quasi-radial function of the generalized MICZ-Kepler system transforms into the quasi-radial wave function of the Kepler-Coulomb problem on the three-dimensional hyperboloid
Barut
.

V
Discussion and outlook

In this paper we proposed generalized MICZ-Kepler systems on the three-dimensional sphere and pseudosphere (upper sheet of the two-sheet hyperboloid) and found their spectra and normalized wavefunctions.
The spectra of these systems depend on two quantum numbers, which leads the conclusion that proposed systems are “minimally superintegrable” (i.e., have four functionally independent integrals of motion) and thus inherit the symmetries of the generalized Kepler-Coulomb system.
Hence, its further perturbation by the appropriate potential (e.g. (pseudo)spherical analog of the Stark term
Vahagn
) will preserve the integrability of the system and the separability of variables.

The obtained solutions have well-defined limits: for
s=\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=0
they reduce to the corresponding formulae for the Kepler-Coulomb problem on the three-dimensional (pseudo)sphere,
and in the flat limit
R_{0}\to\infty
(
\chi\sim r/R_{0}
for the sphere and
\tau\sim r/R_{0}
for the pseudosphere) they reduce to the corresponding formulae for the generalized MICZ-Kepler system in three-dimensional Euclidean space.

There is no doubt that the generalized MICZ-Kepler systems on the three-dimensional hyperboloid can be obtained by the Kustaanheimo-Stiefel transformation from the
\mathbf{CP}^{2}
-Rosochatius system suggested in
INS
, in the spirit of
conifold
.
It seems that in a similar way one can construct the five-dimensional counterpart of this system: the generalized Yang-Coulomb (or
SU(2)
MICZ-Kepler) system on the five-dimensional hyperboloid. We expect that it could be constructed by an appropriate
SU(2)
-reduction from the analog of the Rosochatius system on the two-dimensional quaternionic projective space
\mathbb{HP}^{2}
.

Proposed systems were defined by the potential (
4
), with the special choice of
g(r)
and
V(r)
given by (
5
).
However, from our consideration it becomes clear, that (
4
)
defines the relevant “generalized MICZ-extension” not only for the Coulomb systems on the three-dimensional sphere and hyperboloid,
but for the particle moving on on any
so(3)
-invariant space (equipped with the metric (
2
)) in the presence of any central potential
V(r)
.

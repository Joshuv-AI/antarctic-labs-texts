On the Kolmogorov Distance of Max-Stable Distributions
Enkelejd Hashorva

Abstract




Abstract:

In this contribution, we derive explicit bounds on the Kolmogorov distance for multivariate max-stable
distributions with Fréchet margins. We formulate those bounds in terms of
(i) Wasserstein distances between de Haan representers, (ii) total variation distances
between angular measures—removing the dimension factor from earlier results in the

\ell_{\alpha}
–sphere case—and (iii) discrepancies of the
\Psi
–functions in the inf–argmax
decomposition. Extensions to different margins and Archimax/clustered Archimax copulas
are further discussed. Examples include logistic, comonotonic, independent and Brown–Resnick models.

Key words
: max-stable distributions; Kolmogorov distance; total variation distance; Wasserstein distance; de Haan representation; spectral measure; angular measure; inf-argmax formula; Brown-Resnick distributions; Hüsler-Reiss distributions; Extreme-Value Copulas; Archimax copulas; clustered Archimax copulas

AMS 2020 subject classification
: Primary 60G70; Secondary 60G60, 62G32, 60E15, 49Q22.

1.
Introduction

Given two
d
-dimensional max-stable distribution functions (df’s)
F_{1},F_{2}
, it is
of interest in both theory and applications to quantify their discrepancy. In this paper
we focus on the Kolmogorov distance

\text{d}_{K}(F_{1},F_{2}):=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big|F_{1}(x)-F_{2}(x)\big|,

which turns out to admit sharp and tractable bounds as recently shown in
[
1
, Thm 8, 9, Cor 10]
.

Throughout, and unless stated otherwise, we consider the case where
F_{1},F_{2}
have
unit-
\alpha
–Fréchet margins, i.e., the
i
th margin of
F_{k}
is

F_{k,i}(x_{i})\;=\;\exp\{-x_{i}^{-\alpha}\},\qquad x_{i}>0,\qquad\alpha>0.

Write
X^{(k)}=(X^{(k)}_{t})_{t\in\mathcal{T}}
,
\mathcal{T}=\{1,\ldots,d\}
, for a max-stable random
vector with df’s
F_{k}
. By the de Haan representation (see, e.g.,
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
)

(1.1)

X^{(k)}_{t}=\max_{i\geq 1}\frac{\overline{Z}^{(i,k)}_{t}}{\Gamma_{i}^{1/\alpha}},\qquad t\in\mathcal{T},

where
(\overline{Z}^{(i,k)})_{i\geq 1}
are i.i.d. copies of a nonnegative random vector

Z^{(k)}=(Z^{(k)}_{t})_{t\in\mathcal{T}}
(the
de Haan representer
) with

\mathbb{E}\{(Z^{(k)}_{t})^{\alpha}\}=1
for each
t
, and

\Gamma_{i}=E_{1}+\cdots+E_{i}
with
(E_{i})_{i\geq 1}
i.i.d. unit exponential random variables, independent of the
\overline{Z}^{(i,k)}
’s. As a consequence, each max-stable df
F_{k}
admits an exponent representation

F_{k}(x)=e^{-V_{k}(x)},\qquad V_{k}(x)=\mathbb{E}\Big\{\max_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\frac{(Z^{(k)}_{t})^{\alpha}}{x_{t}^{\alpha}}\Big\},\qquad x\in(0,\infty)^{d}.

It is often convenient to parameterise
F_{k}
by the stable tail dependence function

\ell_{k}(z):=V_{k}(z^{-1/\alpha}),\qquad z\in[0,\infty)^{d}

(with
0^{-1/\alpha}:=+\infty
),
see
[
6
]
for details.
Equivalently,
F_{k}
is uniquely determined by its angular measure
H_{k}
on
the positive unit sphere of a chosen reference norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
; see §
2
and §
4.4
.

Contribution.

Our first result below provides a Wasserstein–type control directly in terms of the de Haan representers, namely

(1.2)

\text{d}_{K}(F_{1},F_{2})\leq\frac{1}{e}\mathcal{W}_{1,\|\cdot\|_{\infty}}\big((Z^{(1)})^{\alpha},(Z^{(2)})^{\alpha}\big),

where for
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
–valued random vectors
U,V
with
\mathbb{E}\{\|U\|_{\infty}+\|V\|_{\infty}\}<\infty
we have

\displaystyle W_{1,\|\cdot\|_{\infty}}(U,V)\;=\;\sup_{\mathrm{Lip}_{\|\cdot\|_{\infty}}(f)\leq 1}\Big|\mathbb{E}\{f(U)\}-\mathbb{E}\{f(V)\}\Big|,

see §
2
for notation and details.
Because de Haan representers are not unique (multiplying by an independent positive radial factor with
unit
\alpha
-moment leaves
F
unchanged), the right-hand side of (
1.2
) may
depend on the chosen representers. For instance, when
\alpha=d=1
,

Z^{(1)}\equiv 1
and
Z^{(2)}\in\{2,1/2\}
with probabilities
1/3,2/3
produce the
same max-stable unit
Fréchet
df

F(x)=\exp(-1/x)
, but

W_{1,\|\cdot\|_{\infty}}(Z^{(1)},Z^{(2)})=\mathbb{E}\left\{\left\lvert 1-Z^{(2)}\right\rvert\right\}=2/3>0.

This motivates working with
canonical
representers (defined from the angular measure below);
with these, we prove the following –TV bound

(1.3)

\displaystyle\text{d}_{K}(F_{1},F_{2})\leq\frac{M_{\alpha}}{e}\|H_{1}-H_{2}\|_{\mathrm{TV}},

where
M_{\alpha}:=\sup_{x\in(0,\infty)^{d}:\lVert x\rVert=1}\|x\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha}
is the homogeneity constant linking the chosen norm and the
\ell_{\alpha}
norm, see (
4.7
) below.

[
1
, Thm 9]
shows the validity of (
1.3
) with the constant
d

in front of
\|H_{1}-H_{2}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
.

For the choice of the canonical norm
\lVert x\rVert=\lVert x\rVert_{\alpha}
we have that
M_{\alpha}=1
, which is an improvement of the aforementioned result for this choice of the norm. We discuss in (
4.7
) below also another improvement of (
1.3
) by considering spectral measures on
\tau
-spheres of
1
–homogeneous
\tau
maps.

A complementary route uses the well-known
\Psi
–decomposition of the exponent function

(1.4)

V_{k}(x)\;=\;\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{1}{x_{j}^{\alpha}}\Psi_{j}^{(k)}(x),\qquad x\in(0,\infty)^{d},

where (
\mathbb{I}(\cdot)
stands for the indicator function)

\Psi_{j}^{(k)}(x):=\mathbb{E}\Big\{(Z^{(k)}_{j})^{\alpha}\mathbb{I}\Big(j=\inf\arg\max_{1\leq m\leq d}Z^{(k)}_{m}/x_{m}\Big)\Big\}.

For several important families (e.g., logistic, Hüsler–Reiss/Brown–Resnick, extremal-
t
),
the functions
\Psi_{j}^{(k)}
are explicit, see e.g.,
[
7
,
8
]
, allowing for derivation of sharp Kolmogorov distance bounds directly in terms of model parameters.

Motivated by the recent applications of Archimax and clustered Archimax copulas, see e.g.,
[
9
,
10
]
and the references therein,
we show that our approach yields the Kolmogorov distance bounds for those copulas.

Organisation of the paper

Section
2
recalls notation and definitions for Wasserstein/TV distances, de Haan representers, angular measures, the
\Psi
–decomposition, and EV copulas. Our main bounds—
(i) Wasserstein bounds via (canonical) de Haan representers, (ii) spectral TV bounds, (iii)
\Psi
–function bounds, (iv) consequences for log–exponential marks

\alpha
varies—
are stated in Section 3. Section 4 discusses heterogeneous or different margins, the application to Archimax and clustered Archimax copulas and the choice of 1-homogeneous functions concluding then
with some illustrative examples. All the proofs are relegated to Section 5.

Preliminaries

Notation.

In the following
\alpha>0
is fixed and we set

\lVert s\rVert_{\alpha}:=\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{d}s_{i}^{\alpha}\Bigr)^{1/\alpha},\qquad s\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

which is a norm for
\alpha\in[1,\infty]
with
\|s\|_{\infty}:=\max_{1\leq j\leq d}|s_{j}|
.
Given some norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
we define the corresponding positive unit spehre

S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|):=\{u\in[0,\infty)^{d}:\lVert u\rVert=1\}

and write
S_{\tau}:=\{x\in[0,\infty)^{d}:\tau(x)=1\}
for the positive
\tau
-spehre defined by the 1-homogeneous map
\tau:\mathbb{R}^{d}\mapsto[0,\infty]
.

Distances on measures

For two random elements
U,V
on a Polish metric space
(\mathsf{X},\rho)
, the (first) Wasserstein
distance with cost function
\rho
is

W_{1,\rho}(U,V):=\inf_{\pi}\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\{\rho(U,V)\}=\sup_{\mathrm{Lip}_{\rho}(f)\leq 1}\Big|\mathbb{E}\{f(U)\}-\mathbb{E}\{f(V)\}\Big|,

where the infimum runs over all couplings
\pi
of
(U,V)
and the supremum is over
all
1
–Lipschitz
f
’s defined with respect to the distance
\rho
.
We will use the sup-norm cost on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
and write below

\mathcal{W}_{1,\|\cdot\|_{\infty}}(U,V)=\inf_{\pi}\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\{\|U-V\|_{\infty}\}.

For Borel finite measures
\mu,\nu
on a measurable space, the TV distance is

\|\mu-\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}:=\sup_{A}\big|\mu(A)-\nu(A)\big|

for
A
Borel sets.

The next lemma is a consequence of
[
11
, Thm 4]
.

Lemma 2.1

(Kantorovich–Rubinstein and TV comparison)
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\rho)
be a compact metric space with diameter
D:=\sup_{x,y\in\mathsf{X}}\rho(x,y)<\infty
.
Then for Borel probability measures
\mu,\nu
on
\mathsf{X}
we have

W_{1,\rho}(\mu,\nu)\;=\;\sup_{\mathrm{Lip}_{\rho}(f)\leq 1}\int fd(\mu-\nu)\;\leq\;D\|\mu-\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the set of all Borel probability measures
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

with finite second moment, i.e.,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|x\|_{2}^{2}\mu(dx)<\infty
.
For
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the
2
–Wasserstein distance is

\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu)=\inf\Big\{\big(\mathbb{E}\{\|U-V\|_{2}^{2}\}\big)^{1/2}:(U,V)\text{defined on a common space with }U\sim\mu,V\sim\nu\Big\}.

\mathcal{W}_{2}
is a metric on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
; in particular it is translation
invariant i.e.,
W_{2}(\mu\circ\tau_{a}^{-1},\nu\circ\tau_{a}^{-1})=W_{2}(\mu,\nu)
for

\tau_{a}(x)=x+a
.

We will use the elementary comparison

(2.1)

\mathcal{W}_{1,\|\cdot\|_{\infty}}(\mu,\nu)\leq W_{1,\|\cdot\|_{2}}(\mu,\nu)\leq\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu)

valid on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which follows because
\|x-y\|_{\infty}\leq\|x-y\|_{2}
and by Cauchy–Schwarz

\mathbb{E}\{\|U-V\|_{2}\}\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\{\|U-V\|_{2}^{2}\}}
for any coupling
(U,V)
,
see e.g.,
[
12
]
for more details.

Tail & angular measures, canonical de Haan representers and
\Psi
-decomposition

Fix
\alpha>0
and a norm
\|\cdot\|
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with positive unit sphere
S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|)
.
Let
Z=(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{d})
be a nonnegative de Haan representer with

\mathbb{E}\{Z_{i}^{\alpha}\}=1
for each positive integer
i\leq d
.
We can assume without loss of generality (and will do so below) that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{1\leq i\leq d}Z_{i}>0\right\}=1

see
[
13
, Lem 7.1]
.
Define the tail (exponent) measure
\mu
on
E:=[0,\infty]^{d}
by

\mu(A):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\{rZ\in A\}\alpha r^{\alpha-1}dr,\qquad A\subset E\text{Borel}.

Then
\mu
is
\sigma
–finite and
(-\alpha)
–homogeneous, i.e.,

\mu(cA)=c^{-\alpha}\mu(A)\qquad\text{for all Borel }A\subset E,c>0.

Note in passing that (
2.2
) implies
\mu(\{0\})=0
. Setting

V(x):=\mu\big(\{z\in E:\exists j,z_{j}>x_{j}\}\big),\qquad x\in(0,\infty)^{d},

then
F(x)=\exp\{-V(x)\},x\in(0,\infty)^{d}
is max-stable df with unit–
\alpha
–Fréchet margins and moreover, it has
de Haan representer
Z
; see, e.g.,

[
14
,
15
,
6
,
5
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
.

Using polar coordinates
z=ru
with
r=\|z\|>0
and
u=z/\|z\|\in S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|)
, the tail measure
factorises as

\mu(dr,du)=\alpha r^{-(\alpha+1)}drH(du),

where
H
is the angular measure on
S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|)
satisfying

(2.3)

\int_{S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|)}u_{i}^{\alpha}H(du)=1,\qquad i=1,\ldots,d

see e.g.,
[
19
,
20
]
.
In general

b:=H(S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|))

need not equal
d
, unless
S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|)
is the positive
\ell_{\alpha}
–simplex, in which case

\sum_{i=1}^{d}u_{i}^{\alpha}=1
on
S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|)
and, by (
2.3
)

\int_{S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|)}H(du)=\int_{S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|)}\sum_{i=1}^{d}u_{i}^{\alpha}H(du)=d.

Consequently, the exponent function admits the following representations

\displaystyle\text{(de Haan)}\qquad V(x)=\mathbb{E}\left\{\max_{1\leq j\leq d}\frac{Z_{j}^{\alpha}}{x_{j}^{\alpha}}\right\},

\displaystyle\text{(spectral/angular on }S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|)\text{)}\qquad V(x)=\int_{S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|)}\max_{1\leq j\leq d}\Big(\frac{u_{j}}{x_{j}}\Big)^{\alpha}H(du).

Let
\Theta
be an
S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|)
–valued random vector with law
H/b

and define the
canonical
de Haan representer

Z^{*}:=b^{1/\alpha}\Theta.

By (
2.3
),
\mathbb{E}\{(Z^{*}_{i})^{\alpha}\}=1
for each positive integer
i\leq d
and further

\mathbb{E}\left\{\max_{1\leq j\leq d}\frac{(Z^{*}_{j})^{\alpha}}{x_{j}^{\alpha}}\right\}=\int_{S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|)}\max_{1\leq j\leq d}\Big(\frac{u_{j}}{x_{j}}\Big)^{\alpha}H(du),\qquad x\in(0,\infty)^{d}.

Consequently,
Z^{*}
is indeed a de Haan representer of
F
; see
[
21
,
19
,
22
,
20
]

for more details and §
4.4
for the
\tau
counterpart.

Remark 2.2
.

(i)

The measure
\mu

uniquely determine each other. The particular different de Haan representer
Z

(ii)

The choice of norm

The next lemma provides a decomposition of the exponent function
V
into
coordinate functions
\Psi_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq d
, which is a variant of the

inf–argmax
formula in
[
7

Lemma 2.3
.

There exist measurable selectors
W(x,Z)=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{d})\in[0,1]^{d}
such that for each

x\in(0,\infty)^{d}
:

•

W_{i}(x,Z)\geq 0
and
\sum_{i=1}^{d}W_{i}(x,Z)=1
,

•

W_{i}(x,Z)>0
only if
i\in\arg\max_{1\leq j\leq d}Z_{j}/x_{j}
(ties allowed),

and defining

\Psi_{i}(x):=\mathbb{E}\left\{Z_{i}^{\alpha}W_{i}(x,Z)\right\},\qquad 1\leq i\leq d

we have the
\Psi
-decomposition

V(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1}{x_{i}^{\alpha}}\Psi_{i}(x),\qquad x\in(0,\infty)^{d}.

Moreover,
\Psi_{i}
’s are 0-homogeneous, non-negative, bounded by 1 and
V
is convex.

Remark 2.4
.

(i)

For
x\in(0,\infty)^{d}
define a measurable partition
\{C_{i}(x)\}_{i=1}^{d}

of the sphere by

C_{i}(x):=\Big\{s:\tfrac{s_{i}}{x_{i}}>\tfrac{s_{j}}{x_{j}}\forall j<i,\tfrac{s_{i}}{x_{i}}\geq\tfrac{s_{j}}{x_{j}}\forall j>i\Big\},

then we have the representation

\Psi_{i}(x)=\int_{C_{i}(x)}s_{i}^{\alpha}H(ds),\qquad V(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1}{x_{i}^{\alpha}}\Psi_{i}(x).

(ii)

A canonical choice for the selector
W
is

W_{i}(x,Z)=\mathbb{I}(i=\inf\arg\max_{j}Z_{j}/x_{j}).

If ties can occur with non-zero probability, i.e.,

p(x)=\mathbb{P}\{\exists i\neq j:Z_{i}/x_{i}=Z_{j}/x_{j}\}>0,

then the
\Psi
-representation of
V(x)
for a fixed
x
is not unique.

(iii)

Assume no ties, i.e.,
p(x)=0
for some
x\in(0,\infty)^{d}
. Then we can take

W_{i}(x,Z):=\mathbb{I}(Z_{i}/x_{i}>Z_{j}/x_{j}\forall j\neq i)

and hence

\Psi_{i}(x)=\mathbb{E}\left\{Z_{i}^{\alpha}\mathbb{I}(Z_{i}/x_{i}>Z_{j}/x_{j},\forall j\neq i)\right\}=\mathbb{P}\left\{\widetilde{Z}_{i}^{(i)}/x_{i}>\widetilde{Z}_{j}^{(i)}/x_{j}\forall j\neq i\right\},

where
\widetilde{Z}^{(i)}
has the
Z^{\alpha}_{i}
–tilted law of
Z
.

(1)

The
\Psi
–decomposition for the bivariate Hüsler-Reiss df has been first shown in
[
23
]
, see also
[
24
]
, which is the first paper to explicitly make use of the homogeneity property to derive
\Psi

EV copulas.

Let
F
be a
d
-dimensional max-stable, which has a unique copula
C
.
Without loss of generality we assume unit–Fréchet margins and write
F(x)=\exp\{-V(x)\}
with
\alpha=1
.
Then the
extreme–value copula
(EV)

is

C(u):=F\big(F_{1}^{-1}(u_{1}),\ldots,F_{d}^{-1}(u_{d})\big)=\exp\{-V(-1/\ln u)\},\qquad u\in(0,1]^{d}.

If

is given in terms of the
\Psi
–decomposition with
0
–homogeneous
\Psi_{i}
’s, then

C(u)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}u_{i}^{\Psi_{i}(-1/\ln u)}.

Given an
d
-dimensional EV copula
Q
, one recovers
\Psi
by forming

F(x):=Q(e^{-1/x_{1}},\ldots,e^{-1/x_{d}}),\qquad x\in(0,\infty)^{d}

and reading off

.
EV copulas are alternatively characterized by the Pickands dependence function
A
on the unit simplex, see e.g.,
[

8

10
]

[
25
,
26
]

3.
Main Results

Let
F_{k}=e^{-V_{k}}
,
k=1,2
be two
d
-dimensional max-stable df’s with unit-
\alpha
-
Fréchet
margins
(
\alpha>0
), angular measures
H_{k}
on
S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|)
normalized by
\int_{S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|)}s_{i}^{\alpha}H_{k}(ds)=1
for all
i
, and de Haan
representer
Z^{(k)}\in[0,\infty)^{d}
satisfying
\mathbb{E}\{(Z^{(k)}_{j})^{\alpha}\}=1
for each index
j
.
Hence the exponent function of
F_{k}
is given by

V_{k}(x)=\mathbb{E}\left\{\max_{1\leq j\leq d}(Z^{(k)}_{j})^{\alpha}/x_{j}^{\alpha}\right\}=\int_{S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|)}\max_{1\leq j\leq d}(s_{j}/x_{j})^{\alpha}H_{k}(ds),\quad x\in(0,\infty)^{d}.

As explained above we also have the alterative
\Psi
–decomposition

V_{k}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}^{-\alpha}\Psi_{i}^{(k)}(x),\qquad x\in(0,\infty)^{d}

via the inf–argmax selectors of Lemma
2
.

Our first result bounds the Kolmogorov distance in terms of (i) a Wasserstein
distance between the (powered) de Haan representers and (ii) the TV
distance of angular measures.

We also give a convenient bound in terms of the

\Psi
–decomposition.

Theorem 3.1
.

With the notation above and setting
M_{\alpha}=\sup_{u\in S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|)}\|u\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha}
we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\text{d}_{K}(F_{1},F_{2})\leq\inf_{Z^{(1)},Z^{(2)}}\frac{1}{e}\mathcal{W}_{1,\|\cdot\|_{\infty}}\big((Z^{(1)})^{\alpha},(Z^{(2)})^{\alpha}\big)\leq\frac{M_{\alpha}}{e}\|H_{1}-H_{2}\|_{\mathrm{TV}},

where
\mathcal{W}_{1,\|\cdot\|_{\infty}}
denotes the
1
–Wasserstein distance under the sup–norm cost and the infimum is taken over all de Haan representers
Z^{(k)}
of
F_{k},k=1,2
.
Moreover, we have the
\Psi
–bound

(3.2)

\text{d}_{K}(F_{1},F_{2})\leq\frac{1}{e}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in(0,\infty)^{d}\min_{1\leq i\leq d}u_{i}=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\big|\Psi_{i}^{(1)}(u)-\Psi_{i}^{(2)}(u)\big|\leq\frac{M_{\alpha}}{e}\|H_{1}-H_{2}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.

Remark 3.2
.

(1)

If
\|x\|\geq A\|x\|_{p}
for all
x
and some
A>0
, then

M_{\alpha}=\sup_{u\in S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|)}\|u\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha}\leq\frac{1}{A^{\alpha}}\sup_{u\in S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|)}\|u\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{A^{\alpha}}d^{\max(0,1-\alpha/p)}.

In particular, for
\|\cdot\|=\|\cdot\|_{p}
,

M_{\alpha}=d^{\max(0,1-\alpha/p)}
and for
p\geq\alpha
we have
M_{\alpha}=1
.

(2)

Compared to
[
1
, Thm 9]
for the above choice of the norm and
p\geq\alpha
, the global Kolmogorov bound in terms of TV distance is smaller.

The next corollary is applicable for log–exponential de Haan representers, including as special case those of the
Brown–Resnick max-stable family discussed in the next section.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
U^{(k)}=(U^{(k)}_{1},\ldots,U^{(k)}_{d}),k=1,2
be

–valued rvs with

\mathbb{E}\{\|U^{(k)}\|_{2}^{2}\}<\infty

Define the corresponding de Haan representer

Z^{(k)}_{j}:=\exp\big(U^{(k)}_{j}-c^{(k)}_{j}\big),\qquad j=1,\ldots,d.

For the associated max–stable df

\text{d}_{K}(F_{1},F_{2})\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}d}{4e}\Big(\mathcal{W}_{2}\big(\mathrm{Law}(U^{(1)}),\mathrm{Law}(U^{(2)})\big)+\|c^{(1)}-c^{(2)}\|_{2}\Big).

Remark 3.4
.

Let
U^{(k)},k=1,2
be
\mathbb{R}^{d}
–valued random vectors with finite second moments, mean zero
and covariance matrices
\Sigma_{k}=\mathbb{E}\{U^{(k)}(U^{(k)})^{\top}\}

Then Gelbrich’s inequality

\mathcal{W}_{2}^{2}\big(\mathrm{Law}(U^{(1)}),\mathrm{Law}(U^{(2)})\big)\;\geq\;\mathrm{tr}\Big(\Sigma_{1}+\Sigma_{2}-2\big(\Sigma_{2}^{1/2}\Sigma_{1}\Sigma_{2}^{1/2}\big)^{1/2}\Big),

with equality for the Gaussian case.

We consider next the case of
F_{1},F_{2}
with different positive indices

\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
but the same angular measure
H
on

S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|):=\{s\in(0,\infty)^{d}:\|s\|=1\}
for a fixed norm

\|\cdot\|
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
be two positive constants and let
\|\cdot\|
be a norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Given an
angular measure
H
on
S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|)
define the max-stable df’s

F_{i}=e^{-V_{i}}
by

V_{i}(x)=\int_{S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|)}\max_{1\leq j\leq d}\Big(\frac{s_{j}}{x_{j}}\Big)^{\alpha_{i}}H(ds),\qquad i=1,2

and set

C_{\infty}:=\sup_{s\in S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|)}\|s\|_{\infty}\in[1,\infty),\qquad\nu_{0}:=H\big(S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|)\big).

Then
wtih
\alpha_{*}:=\min(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
,
\alpha^{*}:=\max(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})

we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\text{d}_{K}(F_{1},F_{2})\leq\frac{\nu_{0}}{e}|\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}|\max\Big(\frac{1}{e\alpha_{*}},C_{\infty}^{\alpha^{*}}\ln C_{\infty}\Big).

If
\lVert x\rVert=\lVert x\rVert_{p}
for some
p\in(0,\infty]
, then
direct calculations yield the bounds

\frac{d}{\sup_{s\in S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|_{p})}\sum_{i=1}^{d}s_{i}^{\alpha}}\leq\nu_{0}\;\leq\;\frac{d}{\inf_{s\in S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|_{p})}\sum_{i=1}^{d}s_{i}^{\alpha}}

implying

d^{\min(1,\alpha/p)}\;\leq\;\nu_{0}\;\leq\;d^{\max(1,\alpha/p)}.

Since on
S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|_{p})
we have
C_{\infty}=\sup_{\|s\|_{p}=1}\|s\|_{\infty}=1
, we arrive at:

Corollary 3.6
.

Under the assumptions of
Theorem
3.5
, if

\lVert x\rVert=\lVert x\rVert_{p},p\in(0,\infty]
, then

(3.5)

\text{d}_{K}(F_{1},F_{2})\;\leq\;\frac{d^{\max(1,\alpha/p)}}{e^{2}}\frac{|\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}|}{\alpha_{*}}.

Remark 3.7
.

(1)

We compare the above bound to
[
1
, Thm 8]
, which imposes a restriction on the chosen reference norm (not needed here).
Assume the same angular measure
H
and two positive stability indices
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
.
The aforementioned theorem states that there exists a constant

C_{K}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},H)
such that

\text{d}_{K}(F_{\alpha_{1}},F_{\alpha_{2}})\leq C_{K}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},H)\Big(\tfrac{1}{\alpha_{1}}+\tfrac{1}{\alpha_{2}}\Big)|\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}|,

and it gives an explicit admissible choice (their eq. (27)) of the form

C_{K}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\nu)\;=\;d+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{[0,\infty]^{d}\setminus\{0\}}|{\ln y_{j}}|\mathbb{P}\{Z_{1}^{\alpha_{2}/\alpha_{1}}\odot Z_{1}\leq y_{j}\}\mu_{\alpha_{1}}(dy),

where
\mu_{\alpha_{1}}
is the exponent measure associated with
F_{\alpha_{1}}
.
Since

(\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}}+\frac{1}{\alpha_{2}})/\frac{1}{\alpha_{*}}\in[1,2],

choosing
p\geq\alpha

(which yields
\nu_{0}\leq d
) makes the constant in (
3.5
) at most
d/e^{2}
.
Hence, in many settings our explicit bound can be sharper than the admissible constant from

[
1
, Thm 8]
.

(2)

As in
[
1
, Cor 10]
we can combine the results of both theorems to derive a bound for
\text{d}_{K}(F_{1},F_{2})
when both the angular measures and the stability indices differ. See the discussions in the next section for a minor generalisation.

4.
Discussions and Examples

We discuss next how to obtain bounds on the Kolmogorov distance
\text{d}_{K}(F_{1},F_{2})
with
F_{k},k=1,2
two max-stable df’s with non-standard margins or completely different margins (in terms of indices
\alpha_{k,i}
’s). We continue with a short discussion for calculation of Kolmogorov distance between two Archimax copulas, followed by the consideration of spectral measures defined on positive
\tau
-spheres, and then conclude with some illustrating examples.

4.1.
Non-standard margins

We discuss briefly the case when the
i
th margin
F_{k,i}
of
F_{k}
is

F_{k,i}(x_{i})=\exp\{-c_{i}x_{i}^{-\alpha_{i}}\},x_{i}>0

with fixed
c_{i}>0
and
\alpha_{i}>0
(independent of
k
). Set the coordinate change

y_{i}:=\frac{x_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}}{c_{i}}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x_{i}=(c_{i}y_{i})^{1/\alpha_{i}}.

Define the unit–Fréchet reparametrisation

\widetilde{F}_{k}(y):=F_{k}\big((c_{i}y_{i})^{1/\alpha_{i}}\big)=\exp\{-\widetilde{V}_{k}(y)\},\qquad\widetilde{V}_{k}(y):=V_{k}\big((c_{i}y_{i})^{1/\alpha_{i}}\big)

and hence each
\widetilde{F}_{k}
has unit
Fréchet
margins
\exp\{-1/y_{i}\}
. Applying Theorem
3.1
to
\widetilde{F}_{k}
’s we obtain

\sup_{x>0}\big|F_{1}(x)-F_{2}(x)\big|\leq\frac{M_{\alpha}}{e}\big\|H_{1}-H_{2}\big\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.

Let
\widetilde{\Psi}^{(k)}
be the
\Psi
–functions for
\widetilde{V}_{k}
(unit–Fréchet case),
so that

\widetilde{V}_{k}(y)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1}{y_{i}}\widetilde{\Psi}^{(k)}_{i}(y),

hence

V_{k}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1}{x_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}}\Psi^{(k)}_{i}(x),\qquad\Psi^{(k)}_{i}(x)=c_{i}\widetilde{\Psi}^{(k)}_{i}\Big(\frac{x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}}{c_{1}},\ldots,\frac{x_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}}{c_{d}}\Big).

By Remark
5
(heterogeneous scaling) or directly by Theorem
3.1
, we have

(4.1)

\displaystyle\text{d}_{K}(F_{1},F_{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{e}\sup_{y\in(0,\infty)^{d}:\min y=1}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\big|\widetilde{\Psi}^{(1)}_{i}(y)-\widetilde{\Psi}^{(2)}_{i}(y)\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{e}\sup_{x\in(0,\infty)^{d}:\min_{i}x_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}/c_{i}=1}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\big|\Psi^{(1)}_{i}(x)-\Psi^{(2)}_{i}(x)\big|.

4.2.
Different margins

Given
d
–dimensional EV copulas
C_{k},k=1,2
with given
\Psi
-representations (see §
2
), i.e.,

C_{k}(u)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}u_{i}^{\Psi_{k,i}\big(-1/\ln u\big)},\quad u=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{d})\in(0,1]^{d},k=1,2,

where
\Psi_{k,i}
’s are such that
F_{k}=e^{-\sum_{i=1}^{d}1/x_{i}\Psi_{k,i}(x)},k=1,2
is max-stable with unit
Fréchet
margins.
Let the max-stable df’s
\widetilde{F}_{k}(x)=C_{k}\big(G_{k1}(x_{1}),\ldots,G_{kd}(x_{d})\big)
,
k=1,2
, with
G_{ki}
’s univariate extreme-value df’s. By
the
1
-Lipschitz property of the copulas

\displaystyle\text{d}_{K}(\widetilde{F}_{1},\widetilde{F}_{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\text{d}_{K}(C_{1},C_{2})+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\text{d}_{K}(G_{1i},G_{2i}).

Since
G_{ki}
’s are continuous, we have further

(4.2)

\displaystyle\text{d}_{K}(C_{1},C_{2})=\text{d}_{K}(F_{1},F_{2}),

with
\text{d}_{K}(F_{1},F_{2})
which can be bounded by Theorem
3.1
.

Suppose next for simplicity

G_{ki}(x)\;=\;\exp\{-c_{ki}x^{-\alpha_{ki}}\},\qquad x>0,\quad c_{ki}>0,\alpha_{ki}>0.

From the above derivations, setting

\alpha_{*,i}:=\min(\alpha_{1i},\alpha_{2i}),\qquad\Delta\alpha_{i}:=|\alpha_{1i}-\alpha_{2i}|

we obtain

(4.3)

\displaystyle\text{d}_{K}(\widetilde{F}_{1},\widetilde{F}_{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\text{d}_{K}(F_{1},F_{2})+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sup_{x>0}\big|e^{-c_{1i}x^{-\alpha_{1i}}}-e^{-c_{2i}x^{-\alpha_{2i}}}\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\text{d}_{K}(F_{1},F_{2})+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Bigl(\frac{1}{e^{2}}\frac{\Delta\alpha_{i}}{\alpha_{*,i}}+\frac{|c_{1i}-c_{2i}|}{e\min(c_{1i},c_{2i})}\Bigr).

4.3.
Kolmogorov distance of Archimax and clustered Archimax copulas

Let
\psi:[0,\infty)\to(0,1]
be
C^{1}
, strictly decreasing with
\psi(0)=1,\lim_{x\to\infty}\psi(x)=0
and let

\ell_{k}(z):=V_{k}(1/z),\qquad k=1,2,z\in[0,\infty)^{d}

be a 1-homogeneous stable tail dependence functions, i.e.,
V_{k}
’s are exponent functions of the max-stable df
F_{k}
with unit
Fréchet
margins.

The Archimax copula
C_{k}
associated with
\psi
and
\ell_{k}
is defined by

C_{k}(u)=\psi\Big(\ell_{k}\big(\psi^{-1}(u_{1}),\ldots,\psi^{-1}(u_{d})\big)\Big),\qquad u\in(0,1]^{d}.

The class of Archimax copulas was introduced in
[
28
]
and further studied in
[
29
,
9
,
10
]
. It contains both Archimedean copulas (for
\ell(x)=\max_{1\leq i\leq d}x_{i}
) and extreme-value copulas (for
\psi(t)=e^{-t}
).

Write
r:=\min_{1\leq i\leq d}\psi^{-1}(u_{i})
and
w_{i}:=\psi^{-1}(u_{i})/r
and thus
\max_{1\leq i\leq d}w_{i}=1
. Since for
u\in(0,1]^{d}

|C_{1}(u)-C_{2}(u)|=|\psi(r\ell_{1}(w))-\psi(r\ell_{2}(w))|=r|\psi^{\prime}(\xi)||\ell_{1}(w)-\ell_{2}(w)|,

with
\xi
between
r\min(\ell_{1}(w),\ell_{2}(w))
and
r\max(\ell_{1}(w),\ell_{2}(w))
, then
writing
\xi=r\theta
with

\theta\in[\min(\ell_{1}(w),\ell_{2}(w)),\max(\ell_{1}(w),\ell_{2}(w))]
and using

\ell_{k}(w)\geq\max_{1\leq i\leq d}w_{i}\geq 1
. Hence

r|\psi^{\prime}(\xi)|=\frac{\xi}{\theta}|\psi^{\prime}(\xi)|\leq\xi|\psi^{\prime}(\xi)|\leq\sup_{t\geq 0}t|\psi^{\prime}(t)|=K_{\psi}

implying

(4.4)

\displaystyle\text{d}_{K}(C_{1},C_{2})\leq K_{\psi}\sup_{w\in[0,\infty)^{d}:\max_{1\leq i\leq d}w_{i}=1}|\ell_{1}(w)-\ell_{2}(w)|\leq eK_{\psi}\text{d}_{K}(F_{1},F_{2}),

provided that

K_{\psi}\;:=\;\sup_{t\geq 0}t|\psi^{\prime}(t)|<\infty.

Note that the case that
C_{k}
’s have different generators
\psi_{k}
’s can be treated similarly.

Next, given
\{I_{g}\}_{g=1}^{G}
a partition of
\{1,\ldots,d\}
with
|I_{g}|\geq 1
and
A_{g}
’s Archimax copulas, the clustered Archimax copula is defined by

(4.5)

C(u):=R\big(A_{1}(u_{I_{1}}),\ldots,A_{G}(u_{I_{G}})\big),\qquad u\in[0,1]^{d},

with
R:[0,1]^{G}\to[0,1]
a
G
–variate copula, see
[
29
,
25
]
for more details.

Using the
1
-Lipschitz property of the copulas and the above bound, a total variation bound on clustered Archimax copulas
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
with corresponding
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
follows easily utilising the result above and counting for the Kolmogorov distance
\text{d}_{K}(R_{1},R_{2})
; we omit the details. A more tractable setting is when
R_{k}
’s are also Archimax copulas.

4.4.
General 1-homogeneous
\tau
maps

Let
Z^{(k)},k=1,2
be the de Haan representer of
F_{k}
,
k=1,2
.
Assume for simplicity that for
\alpha>0

\mathbb{E}\{(Z^{(k)}_{i})^{\alpha}\}=1,\qquad 1\leq i\leq d,k=1,2

and (
2.2
) holds for
Z^{(k)},k=1,2
.
Let
\tau:[0,\infty)^{d}\to[0,\infty)
be a measurable, 1–homogeneous function
such that
\tau(x)=0
if and only if
\|x\|=0
, with
\|\cdot\|
a norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(its choice is not relevant here).
Set

b_{k}:=\mathbb{E}\{(\tau(Z^{(k)}))^{\alpha}\}\in(0,\infty),\qquad k=1,2

and let the
spectral
random vector
\Theta^{\tau,(k)}
on the positive
\tau
–sphere

H_{\tau,k}/b_{k}

S_{\tau}=\{x\in[0,\infty)^{d}:\tau(x)=1\}

be defined by

\mathbb{P}\{\Theta^{\tau,(k)}\in A\}:=\frac{1}{b_{k}}\mathbb{E}\left\{(\tau(Z^{(k)}))^{\alpha}\mathbb{I}(Z^{(k)}/\tau(Z^{(k)})\in A)\right\},\qquad A\subset S_{\tau}\text{Borel}.

Define the
\tau
–canonical
de Haan representer

Z^{\tau,(k)}:=b_{k}^{1/\alpha}\Theta^{\tau,(k)},\qquad k=1,2.

By the definition

\mathbb{E}\{(\Theta_{i}^{\tau,(k)})^{\alpha}\}=\frac{1}{b_{k}},\qquad k=1,2

implying

\mathbb{E}\{(Z^{\tau,(k)}_{i})^{\alpha}\}=\int_{S_{\tau}}u_{i}^{\alpha}H_{\tau,k}(du)=1,\qquad k=1,2.

For any
\alpha
–homogeneous functional
\Gamma:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\infty)
we have

\mathbb{E}\{\Gamma(Z^{\tau,(k)})\}=b_{k}\mathbb{E}\{\Gamma(\Theta^{\tau,(k)})\}=\mathbb{E}\{(\tau(Z^{(k)}))^{\alpha}\Gamma(Z/\tau(Z^{(k)}))\}=\mathbb{E}\{\Gamma(\Theta^{(k)})\},\qquad k=1,2,

where we used
\mathbb{P}\{\tau(Z^{(k)})>0\}=1
, which follows from (
2.2
) and the assumption that
\tau(x)=0
only if
x
has all components equal zero.
Hence, in view of
[
30
]
,
Z^{\tau,(k)}
is again a de Haan representer of
F_{k}
.
We call
H_{\tau,k}
the positive
spectral/angular measure of
F_{k}
on the
\tau
–sphere
S_{\tau}
,
which reduces to the standard angular measure when

\tau(x)=\|x\|
is a norm.

From the above, we have that the
\Theta^{\tau,(k)}
has law

H_{\tau,k}/b_{k}
.
Note further that starting from
Z^{\tau,(k)}
and
choosing now the map
\|x\|_{\alpha}
, we can define de Haan representer with respect
\|x\|_{\alpha}
and hence we can determine the angular measure
H_{\alpha,k}
from
H_{\tau,k}
, namely

(4.6)

\displaystyle H_{\alpha,k}(B)=\int_{S_{\tau}}\mathbb{I}(u/\|u\|_{\alpha}\in B)\|u\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha}H_{\tau,k}(du),\qquad B\subset S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|_{\alpha})\text{Borel}.

Alternatively, since the tail measure does not depend on the choice of the norm or the 1-homogeneous map
\tau
, the above relation follows from the corresponding polar decompositions of the tail measure.

As in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we obtain

(4.7)

\displaystyle\text{d}_{K}(F_{1},F_{2})\leq\frac{1}{e}\inf_{\tau}M_{\alpha,\tau}\|H_{\tau,1}-H_{\tau,2}\|_{\mathrm{TV}},\quad M_{\alpha,\tau}:=\sup_{x\in(0,\infty)^{d}:\tau(x)=1}\|x\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha},

where the infimum is taken over all
\tau
maps satisfying the above conditions.

We note in passing that
Theorem
3.5
can be also reformulated by substituting the norm
\lVert x\rVert
with
\tau(x)
.

4.5.
Examples

In the first example we consider the comonotonic and independence models. The second example illustrates the bound for the logistic df and both comonotonic and independence model, while the final example considers the Brown-Resnick df’s.

Example 4.1
.

Fix
d\geq 2
and
\alpha>0
and define for
x\in(0,\infty)^{d}

F_{\mathrm{com}}(x)=\min_{1\leq i\leq d}\exp\{-x_{i}^{-\alpha}\}=:e^{-V_{\mathrm{com}}(x)},

F_{\mathrm{ind}}(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\exp\{-x_{i}^{-\alpha}\}y=:e^{-V_{\mathrm{ind}}(x)}.

It follows by applying the (
5.1
) below that

\text{d}_{K}\big(F_{\mathrm{com}},F_{\mathrm{ind}}\big)=\frac{d-1}{d}d^{-\frac{1}{d-1}},\qquad\text{independent of }\alpha.

For the comonotonic law take
Z^{(1)}\equiv(1,\ldots,1)
, while for the independent law, the anuglar measure is concentrated at the axes and the canonical de Haan representer is

Z^{(2)*}=d^{1/\alpha}e_{I}
,
I\sim\mathrm{Unif}\{1,\ldots,d\}
(with
e_{i}
the unit
i
th unit vector on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
) implying

\mathcal{W}_{1,\|\cdot\|_{\infty}}\big((Z^{(1)})^{\alpha},(Z^{(2)*})^{\alpha}\big)=\lVert(1,\ldots,1)-de_{I}\rVert_{\infty}=d-1

and Theorem
3.1
yields

\text{d}_{K}\big(F_{\mathrm{com}},F_{\mathrm{ind}}\big)\leq\frac{d-1}{e}.

Note that
H_{\mathrm{com}}=d\delta_{(1/d,\ldots,1/d)}
and

H_{\mathrm{ind}}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\delta_{e_{i}}
are singular, so

\|H_{\mathrm{com}}-H_{\mathrm{ind}}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=d
and thus the TV-bound
in (
3.1
) is strictly larger.

Example 4.2
.

Fix
d\geq 2
and consider the logistic max–stable family with unit–Fréchet margins

F_{\theta}(x)\;=\;\exp\Big\{-\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}^{-1/\theta}\Big)^{\theta}\Big\},\qquad x\in(0,\infty)^{d},\theta\in(0,1].

Let
F_{\mathrm{ind}}(x)=\exp\{-\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}^{-1}\}
and

F_{\mathrm{com}}(x)=\exp\{-\max_{1\leq j\leq d}x_{j}^{-1}\}
as above and
write the corresponding exponent functions

V_{\theta}(x)=\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}^{-1/\theta}\Big)^{\theta},\qquad V_{\mathrm{ind}}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}^{-1},\qquad V_{\mathrm{com}}(x)=\max_{1\leq j\leq d}x_{j}^{-1}.

As in the proof of the first theorem, see
5.1
,
it suffices to bound

\sup_{\min_{1\leq i\leq d}u_{i}=1}\big|V_{\bullet}(u)-V_{\bullet\bullet}(u)\big|
and then multiply by
1/e
.
Direct calculations yield the following explicit bounds:

\text{d}_{K}\big(F_{\mathrm{ind}},F_{\theta}\big)\;\leq\;\frac{1}{e}\big(d-d^{\theta}\big),\quad\text{d}_{K}\big(F_{\mathrm{com}},F_{\theta}\big)\;\leq\;\frac{1}{e}\big(d^{\theta}-1\big).

Example 4.3
.

Let
U=(U_{1},\ldots,U_{d})\sim\mathcal{N}_{d}(0,\Sigma)
be a centered Gaussian vector with

\sigma_{i}^{2}:=Var(U_{i})
and covariance
\Sigma=(\Sigma_{ij})
. Define the variogram

\gamma_{ij}:=Var(U_{i}-U_{j})=\sigma_{i}^{2}+\sigma_{j}^{2}-2\Sigma_{ij},\qquad 1\leq i,j\leq d,

and set the de Haan representer

Z_{j}=\exp\Big(U_{j}-\tfrac{1}{2}Var(U_{j})\Big),\qquad 1\leq j\leq d,

so that
\mathbb{E}\{Z_{j}\}=1
and

V(x)=\mathbb{E}\left\{\max_{1\leq j\leq d}\frac{Z_{j}}{x_{j}}\right\},\quad x\in(0,\infty)^{d}.

Then
F=e^{-V}
is the Brown-Resnick df generated by the de Haan representer
Z
.

In view of Corollary
3
and Remark
3
we obtain the Kolmogorov bound

\text{d}_{K}(F_{1},F_{2})\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}d}{4e}\Bigl(\big\|\mathrm{diag}(\Sigma_{1})-\mathrm{diag}(\Sigma_{2})\big\|_{2}+\mathrm{tr}\Big(\Sigma_{1}+\Sigma_{2}-2(\Sigma_{2}^{1/2}\Sigma_{1}\Sigma_{2}^{1/2})^{1/2}\Big)\Bigr).

Remark 4.4
.

F
is also known as the Hüsler-Reiss df with parameter matrix

\Lambda=(\lambda_{ij})
given by

\lambda_{ij}^{2}=\gamma_{ij}=Var(U_{i}-U_{j}),\qquad\lambda_{ii}=0,\lambda_{ij}=\lambda_{ji}>0.

In particular, the exponent function
V
admits the
\Psi
–decomposition

V(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1}{x_{i}}\Psi_{i}(x),\qquad\Psi_{i}(x)=\Phi_{d-1}\Big(q^{(i)}(x);R^{(i)}\Big),

where, for
j\neq i

q^{(i)}_{j}(x)=\frac{\lambda_{ij}}{2}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{ij}}\ln\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}},\qquad\big(R^{(i)}\big)_{jk}=\frac{\lambda_{ij}^{2}+\lambda_{ik}^{2}-\lambda_{jk}^{2}}{2\lambda_{ij}\lambda_{ik}},\quad j,k\neq i.

5.
Proofs

Proof of Theorem

3.1
: We show first that

(5.1)

\displaystyle\text{d}_{K}(F_{1},F_{2})=\sup_{x\in(0,\infty)^{d}}|F_{1}(x)-F_{2}(x)|\leq\frac{1}{e}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in(0,\infty)^{d}\min_{1\leq i\leq d}u_{i}=1\end{subarray}}|V_{1}(u)-V_{2}(u)|.

Fix therefore
x\in(0,\infty)^{d}
and write
m:=\min_{1\leq i\leq d}x_{i}
and
u:=x/m
, so
\min_{1\leq i\leq d}u_{i}=1
.
By
(-\alpha)
-homogeneity property
V_{k}(x)=V_{k}(u)/m^{\alpha}
, hence

(5.2)

\displaystyle|F_{1}(x)-F_{2}(x)|=\big|e^{-V_{1}(u)/m^{\alpha}}-e^{-V_{2}(u)/m^{\alpha}}\big|.

Set
a:=V_{1}(u)
and
b:=V_{2}(u)
and assume for simplicity that
a<b
. Since the margins are unit–
\alpha
-
Fréchet

V_{k}(u)\geq\max_{1\leq i\leq d}u_{i}^{-\alpha}=1,\qquad k=1,2

and hence
a,b\geq 1
.
By the mean value theorem (in the variable
t
for
e^{-t/m^{\alpha}}
),
for each
m>0
there exists
\xi\in[a,b]\subset[1,\infty)
such that

\big|e^{-a/m^{\alpha}}-e^{-b/m^{\alpha}}\big|=\frac{e^{-\xi/m^{\alpha}}}{m^{\alpha}}|a-b|.

Setting
r:=m^{-\alpha}>0
we have

e^{-\xi/m^{\alpha}}/m^{\alpha}=re^{-\xi r},\qquad\sup_{r>0}re^{-\xi r}=1/(e\xi)\leq 1/e.

Consequently, taking in (
5.2
) the supremum over
m>0
and then over

u
with
\min_{1\leq i\leq d}u_{i}=1
establishes (
5.1
).

Write next
V_{k}(x)=\mathbb{E}\{f_{x}\big((Z^{(k)})^{\alpha}\big)\}
with

f_{x}(w):=\max_{1\leq j\leq d}\frac{w_{j}}{x_{j}^{\alpha}},\qquad x\in(0,\infty)^{d},w\in[0,\infty)^{d}.

If
x\in(0,\infty)^{d}
is such that
\min_{1\leq j\leq d}x_{j}=1
, then for
w,w^{\prime}\in[0,\infty)^{d}

|f_{x}(w)-f_{x}(w^{\prime})|=\Big|\max_{1\leq j\leq d}\frac{w_{j}}{x_{j}^{\alpha}}-\max_{1\leq j\leq d}\frac{w^{\prime}_{j}}{x_{j}^{\alpha}}\Big|\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq d}\frac{|w_{j}-w^{\prime}_{j}|}{x_{j}^{\alpha}}\leq\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{\infty},

yielding that
f_{x}
is
1
–Lipschitz with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
. Consequently, since

f_{x}
is
1
–Lipschitz for every
x\in(0,\infty)^{d}
with
\min_{1\leq j\leq d}x_{j}=1
, we have by the definition of the Wasserstein distance

\mathcal{W}_{1,\|\cdot\|_{\infty}}

|V_{1}(x)-V_{2}(x)|=\big|\mathbb{E}\{f_{x}\big((Z^{(1)})^{\alpha}\big)\}-\mathbb{E}\{f_{x}\big((Z^{(2)})^{\alpha}\big)\}\big|\leq\mathcal{W}_{1,\|\cdot\|_{\infty}}\big((Z^{(1)})^{\alpha},(Z^{(2)})^{\alpha}\big)

for every
x
with

Proof of Corollary

3
:
Given the de Haan representer

Z^{(k)}_{j}:=\exp\big(U^{(k)}_{j}-c^{(k)}_{j}\big),\qquad j=1,\ldots,d.

and the angular parts

S^{(k)}:=\frac{Z^{(k)}}{\sum_{i=1}^{d}Z^{(k)}_{i}}

let the canonical de Haan representer be
Z^{(k)*}:=dS^{(k)}
.
We have

S^{(k)}=\mathrm{sm}(\widetilde{U}^{(k)}),\qquad\widetilde{U}^{(k)}:=U^{(k)}-c^{(k)},\qquad k=1,2,

where
\mathrm{sm}
is the softmax function defined by

\mathrm{sm}(u):=\frac{(e^{u_{1}},\ldots,e^{u_{d}})}{\sum_{k=1}^{d}e^{u_{k}}}.

We calculate first the Lipschitz constant
C_{\rm sm}
of
\mathrm{sm}
. Although related results exist for other norms, we could not locate an explicit reference for the

\|\cdot\|_{2\to\infty}
setting used below. Specifically, we show next that
for all
u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\|\mathrm{sm}(u)-\mathrm{sm}(v)\|_{\infty}\leq C_{\rm sm}\|u-v\|_{2},

where the Lipschitz constant
C_{\rm sm}=\sqrt{2}/4
is sharp.

For
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
set
s=\mathrm{sm}(u)
and denote the Jacobian by
J(u)\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
with entries

\frac{\partial s_{i}}{\partial u_{i}}=-s_{i}^{2},\qquad\frac{\partial s_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}=s_{i}(1-s_{j}),\qquad 1\leq i,j\leq d.

Since the above
J(u)
depends on
u
only through
s
, we write instead

J_{s}:=\mathrm{diag}(s)-ss^{\top}.

Set
h:=u-v
and
w(t):=v+th
for
t\in[0,1]
. With
g(t):=\mathrm{sm}(w(t))
, the chain rule gives

g^{\prime}(t)=J\big(w(t)\big)h,\qquad\text{hence}\qquad\mathrm{sm}(u)-\mathrm{sm}(v)=\int_{0}^{1}J\big(w(t)\big)hdt.

Note in passing that
g:[0,1]\to S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|_{1})
traces a curve on the positive 1-sphere.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{sm}(u)-\mathrm{sm}(v)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\int_{0}^{1}J\big(w(t)\big)dt\Big\|_{2\to\infty}\|h\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\|J\big(w(t)\big)\|_{2\to\infty}dt\|h\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{s\in S^{d-1}_{+}(\|\cdot\|_{1})}\|J_{s}\|_{2\to\infty}\|u-v\|_{2},

where for a matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
we
define the mixed operator norm by

\|A\|_{2\to\infty}:=\sup_{\|x\|_{2}=1}\|Ax\|_{\infty}=\max_{1\leq i\leq d}\|{\rm row}_{i}(A)\|_{2}.

Row
i
of
J_{s}
is
s_{i}(e_{i}^{\top}-s^{\top})
, hence

\|{\rm row}_{i}(J_{s})\|_{2}=s_{i}\|e_{i}-s\|_{2},\qquad\|e_{i}-s\|_{2}^{2}=1-2s_{i}+\sum_{k=1}^{d}s_{k}^{2}.

For fixed
s_{i}
, the quantity
\sum_{k=1}^{d}s_{k}^{2}
is maximized by concentrating the remaining mass

1-s_{i}
on a single coordinate, giving
\sum_{k=1}^{d}s_{k}^{2}=s_{i}^{2}+(1-s_{i})^{2}
and thus

\|e_{i}-s\|_{2}=\sqrt{2}(1-s_{i})
. Therefore

\|J_{s}\|_{2\to\infty}=\max_{1\leq i\leq d}s_{i}\|e_{i}-s\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{2}\max_{r\in[0,1]}r(1-r)=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}

with equality at
s=(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2},0,\ldots,0)
, which also shows sharpness of the Lipschitz constant
C_{\rm sm}=\sqrt{2}/4
.

Consequently, we have for any coupling
\widetilde{U}^{(1)},\widetilde{U}^{(2)}
of
S^{(1)},S^{(2)}

\mathbb{E}\{\|S^{(1)}-S^{(2)}\|_{\infty}\}\leq C_{\rm sm}\mathbb{E}\{\|\widetilde{U}^{(1)}-\widetilde{U}^{(2)}\|_{2}\}.

Taking the infimum over couplings gives

\mathcal{W}_{1,\|\cdot\|_{\infty}}\big(S^{(1)},S^{(2)}\big)\leq C_{\rm sm}\mathcal{W}_{2}\Big(\mathrm{Law}(\widetilde{U}^{(1)}),\mathrm{Law}(\widetilde{U}^{(2)})\Big).

By triangle inequality and translation invariance of
\mathcal{W}_{2}

\mathcal{W}_{2}\Big(\mathrm{Law}(\widetilde{U}^{(1)}),\mathrm{Law}(\widetilde{U}^{(2)})\Big)\leq\mathcal{W}_{2}\big(\mathrm{Law}(U^{(1)}),\mathrm{Law}(U^{(2)})\big)+\|c^{(1)}-c^{(2)}\|_{2}.

For canonical de Haan representer
Z^{(k)*}=dS^{(k)}
, the sup–norm scales by
d
, so

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{1,\|\cdot\|_{\infty}}\big(Z^{(1)*},Z^{(2)*}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\mathcal{W}_{1,\|\cdot\|_{\infty}}(S^{(1)},S^{(2)})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{d\sqrt{2}}{4}\Big(\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mathrm{Law}(U^{(1)}),\mathrm{Law}(U^{(2)}))+\|c^{(1)}-c^{(2)}\|_{2}\Big).

Finally, (
5.1
) (with
\alpha=1
) and Theorem
3.1
yield

\text{d}_{K}(F_{1},F_{2})\leq\frac{1}{e}\mathcal{W}_{1,\|\cdot\|_{\infty}}\big(Z^{(1)*},Z^{(2)*}\big),

which gives (
3.3
).

\Box

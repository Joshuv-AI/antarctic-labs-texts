IKAROS isoforms differentially control gene expression and differentiation of a B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia cell line
Authors: Yuya Ogimoto, Mariko Kawachi-Nakamura, Kazunori Hashimoto, Kiyoshi Yanagisawa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTB-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL) is the most common malignancy occurring in children and a leading cause of cancer-related mortality, thus there is an urgent need for development of novel therapeutic strategies for high risk B-ALL patients. The significance of IKZF1 gene alterations in B-ALL cases is controversial, as some studies have shown those to be associated with poor prognosis, while others have reported that deletion of exons 4–7 of the IKZF1 gene, which results in generation of IKAROS isoform 6, is related favorable prognosis. For the present study, clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats (CRISPR)/Cas9 was employed for knockout of the IKZF1 gene and various IKAROS knockout lines were established, which allowed for investigation of precise functions of the gene by comparing four lines with different IKAROS isoform 1 and 6 statuses. The results clearly showed induction of significant differentiation and cell cycle progression in IKAROS isoform 6 knockout clones. Furthermore, knockout of IKAROS isoform 6 resulted in significant upregulation of the expression of IKAROS isoform 1 as well as signal transducer and activator of transcription 5 (STAT5) activity, which are considered to be related to the observed features. Further investigations are warranted to provide greater mechanistic insight regarding IKAROS-mediating pathways, which will lead to development of novel therapy for human B-ALL.Key Words: acute lymphoblastic leukemia, IKAROS, STAT5, differentiation, cell cycle

INTRODUCTION

B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL) is the most common malignancy occurring in children. Approximately 20% of pediatric and the majority of adult B-ALL patients suffer relapse, and their prognosis is extremely poor.1-4 Although more than 50% of affected patients are considered curable, there is an urgent need for development of novel therapeutic strategies for high-risk cases.

The IKZF1 gene has an important role in regulation of normal hematopoiesis and is necessary for development of all lymphatic systems.5,6 Several alternatively spliced transcript variants encoding different IKAROS isoforms have been described, most of which share a common C-terminal domain that contains two zinc finger motifs, which are required for hetero- or homo-dimerization. However, the isoforms differ regarding the number of N-terminal zinc finger motifs that bind DNA and nuclear localization signal presence, resulting in members with and without DNA-binding properties. IKAROS isoform 1 (IK1) is a wild-type (WT) gene product and IKAROS isoform 2 (IK2) lacks exon 4, while IKAROS isoform 3 (IK3) lacks exon 6 and 7. Nevertheless, these isoforms contain at least three zinc finger domains and are functionally active as transcription factors.

Loss of function mutations in IKZF1 have been implicated in both pediatric and adult B-ALL cases, and shown to be associated with an approximately threefold increased risk of relapse.7,8 In other studies, IKZF1 genetic alterations were observed with an overall frequency of 15% in pediatric and of 40% in adult Philadelphia-negative B-ALL cases, while more than 70% of patients affected by Philadelphia-positive B-ALL were found to be carriers of IKZF1 genes with abnormalities.2,9,10 Intragenic deletion of exons 4–7 of the IKZF1 gene is the most common alteration, accounting for 30% of the total,11 which corresponds to IKAROS isoform 6 (IK6). IK6 lacks all four N-terminal DNA-binding zinc finger domains but retains two C-terminal dimerization zinc finger domains. While it is unable to bind DNA or regulate transcription, this isoform retains the ability to dimerize with DNA-binding isoforms, such as IK1, thereby inhibiting their function in a dominant-negative manner.12 The second common deletion comprises exons 2–7, which results in null alleles, since the ATG start codon is located in exon 2. Such monoallelic deletion of exons 2–7 implies haploinsufficiency with reduced IK1 protein levels.13,14 Patients presenting deletions in both alleles of the IKZF1 gene have also been reported, with no expression of IKAROS isoforms confirmed in those cases.11,15 Accordingly, IKZF1 gene alterations are classified into three groups; dominant-negative, haploinsufficient, and null. Several lines of evidence suggest that haploinsufficient patients have a worse prognosis than dominant negative patients,16,17 though details regarding the underlying mechanisms for regulation of B-ALL hallmarks by IKAROS isoforms remain unclear.

The present study employed the clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats (CRISPR)/Cas9 system to generate four IKZF1-knockout human B-ALL cell lines, including parent (IK1+/IK6+; dominant-negative), IK6 knockout (IK1+/IK6–; haploinsufficient), IK1 knockout (IK1–/IK6+), and null (IK1–/IK6–). Analyses of these lines provided novel insights regarding IKAROS functions related to signal transducer and activator of transcription 5 (STAT5) regulation, cell cycle progression, and induction of differentiation in B-ALL.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

CRISPR/Cas9-mediated generation of IKZF1 knockout SUP-B15 cells

The SUP-B15 cell line is characterized by elevated IK6 expression owing to heterozygous IKZF1 deletion of exon 4–7, whereas the WT IKZF1 cell line predominantly expresses IK1 and shows no detectable IK6 expression.10 IK6 knockout in SUP-B15 cells was performed using the Alt-R CRISPR/Cas9 System, purchased from Integrated DNA Technologies (Coralville, IA, USA). Guide RNA (gRNA) sequences were designed using the Alt-R CRISPR HDR Design Tool and synthesized as Alt-R CRISPR crRNA from IDT. CRISPR ribonucleic acid (crRNA) was combined with Alt-R CRISPR tracrRNA (Integrated DNA Technologies) at equimolar concentrations and annealed at 95 °C for five minutes, then crRNA-trans-activating crRNA (tracrRNA) duplexes were mixed with Alt-R S.p.HiFi Cas9 Nuclease V3 (Integrated DNA Technologies) and incubated at room temperature for five minutes to allow for assembly of the ribonucleoprotein (RNP) complexes. Following incubation, an Alt-R Cas9 electroporation enhancer was used and 1×106 cells were added to the RNP complex solution. Combined samples were then transferred to an electroporation cuvette and electroporated using an NEPA21 Super Electroporator, after which electroporated cells were incubated in media at 37 °C for 24 hours. Various types of knockout cell lines were obtained by limiting the amount of dilution. Cells from the parent line (IK1+/IK6+) were established under the same conditions, except for addition of crRNA.

Subcellular fractionation

All of the following preparations were performed on ice. Cells were resuspended in fractionation buffer A (10 mM HEPES [pH 7.4], 10 mM NaCl, 10 mM KCl, 0.1 mM EDTA, 1 mM DTT, 1 x protease inhibitor cocktail), then incubated for 15 minutes. NP-40 was added to a final concentration 0.6% and the lysates were incubated for three minutes, then centrifuged until pellet nuclei were formed (720 x g, 5 minutes, 4 °C). After obtaining the supernatant (cytoplasmic fraction), the pellet debris was recentrifuged (12,000 x g, 30 minutes, 4 °C). The resulting nuclear pellet was resuspended in fractionation buffer A with 0.6% NP-40 and vortexed, then washed three times at 12,000 x g for two minutes at 4 °C. Resuspension of the pellet was again performed in fractionation buffer B (20 mM HEPES [pH 7.4], 0.4 M NaCl, 1 mM EDTA, 1 mM DTT, 1 x protease inhibitor cocktail) and incubation performed for 30 minutes. Finally, the lysate was centrifuged at 12,000 x g for 30 minutes and the supernatant (nucleus fraction) collected.

Flow cytometry analysis

To evaluate effects on cell cycle progression, 1×106 cells were harvested, and fixed with 70% ethanol and 30% phosphate buffered saline (PBS) at –20 °C. After washing with PBS, staining with propidium iodide (PI) in PBS containing ribonuclease (RNase) was performed for analysis, conducted using a LSRFortessa X-20 (BD Biosciences, Bedford, MA, USA). For surface phenotype analysis, 1×106 cells were harvested and washed in stain buffer (PBS with 2% bovine serum albumin [BSA] and 0.1% sodium azide). Next, they were resuspended in 100 µL of the same buffer and stained with PE-conjugated anti-CD34 (BD Biosciences) for 30 minutes on ice. After washing with stain buffer, analysis was performed using a LSRFortessa X-20. Dead cells were excluded with use of PI at 2 µg/µL.

RESULTS

Delivery of Cas9/gRNA RNPs for IKZF1 gene editing in human acute lymphoblastic leukemia cell line SUP-B15

To clarify the importance of the regulatory network of IKZF1 gene products, examinations of IK1 and IK6, which interact with and regulate each other, were performed using a CRISPR/Cas9 system for IKZF1 gene editing to delete IK1 and/or IK6 alleles. First, Cas9/gRNA RNPs targeting exon 2 of the IKZF1 gene were electroporated into the SUP-B15 B-ALL cell line (Fig. 1A, upper half).

Limiting dilution was carried out for single cell cloning and four different lines were obtained; IK1+/IK6+ (parent; dominant-negative), IK1+/IK6– (IK6 knockout; haploinsufficient), IK1–/IK6+ (IK1 knockout), and IK1–/IK6– (null) (Fig. 1A, lower half). The protein expression levels of IK1 and IK6 in these lines were examined with use of western blotting. As shown in Figure 1B, three independent clones of each line were successfully generated. While the IK1–/IK6– clones lacked expression of IK1 and IK6, reduced expression of IK6 and IK1was noted in the IK1+/IK6– and IK1–/IK6+ clones, respectively (Fig. 1B). Notably, the IK6 knockout clones (IK1+/IK6–) showed elevated expression of IK1 as compared with the parent clones (IK1+/IK6+).

Subcellular localization of IK1 and IK6 in various knockout lines

Next, subcellular localization of IK1 and/or IK6 proteins in each IK-knockout line was evaluated using immunofluorescence staining and western blot analysis findings. Although the IK1+/IK6+ (parent) clones showed no significant expression of IK1 in cytoplasm, cytoplasmic localization of IK1 was detected in the IK1+/IK6– (haploinsufficient) clones (Fig. 2). There is no evidence concluding whether the subcellular localization of IK6 is affected by IK1 or not, because an RNA interference method is not able to eliminate individual IK1 or IK6 expression. Thus, we analyzed the subcellular localization of IK6 in IK1–/IK6+ clones comparing with that in IK1+/IK6+ clones. The present findings clearly confirmed IK6 expression in both the cytoplasm and nucleus (Fig. 2). Interestingly, IK6 knockout increased the diameter of B-ALL cells, an important finding that may reflect degree of differentiation.

IK6 knockout promotes differentiation of B-ALL cells in the presence of IK1

Previous reports have suggested that IKAROS has a role in efficient pro-B to pre-B cell transition.18-21 Accordingly, expression levels of CD34, a marker of immature hematopoietic stem cells, among the various IKZF1 knockout lines were investigated using flow cytometry. In IK1+/IK6+ clones, two populations of cells that showed high or low expression of CD34 were observed, with the average ratio of the CD34 high population 32.0% and of the low population 59.4% (Fig. 3A). In contrast, an extremely limited expression of CD34 was observed in haploinsufficient clones (IK1+/IK6–), reflecting the progression of differentiation (Fig. 3B). To clarify the effects of IK6 knockout, messenger RNA (mRNA) expression of the IGLL1, VPREB1, and IL7Rα genes was further investigated, with the results showing a marked decrease of expression for each of these genes in IK6 knockout clones (Fig. 3C). λ5 and VPREB, which are encoded by the IGLL1 and VPREB1 genes, respectively, have important roles in pre-B cell receptor signaling,22,23 while IL-7R expression during differentiation of large pre-B cells is known to be suppressed.21,24 Thus, elimination of IK6 expression resulted in progression of differentiation into large pre-B or further stage cells.

Knockout of IK6 induces cell cycle progression in pre-B ALL cells

Findings indicating induction of maturation of pro-B cells with depletion of IK6 prompted examinations of the effect on cell cycle regulation. Flow-cytometric analysis indicated a decreased proportion of cells in the static phase (G0/G1) and an increased proportion in the synthesis phase (S), suggesting cell cycle progression for haploinsufficient IK1+/IK6– clones (Fig. 4A, 4B). Additionally, western blot analysis results confirmed decreased expression of TP53, CDKN1A (p21), and CDKN1B (p27) in IK1+/IK6– clones, thus supporting our speculation (Fig. 4C).

Identification of STAT5 activation results in regulation of differentiation and cell cycle progression in IK6 knockout clones

Additional examinations were performed to investigate how IK1 and IK6 coordinate to regulate the cell cycle in B-ALL cells. IK6 knockout induced phosphorylation of STAT5 in SUP-B15 cells (Fig. 5A), while it was also found that both mRNA and protein expressions of STAT5 were upregulated in haploinsufficient IK1+/IK6– clones (Fig. 5A. 5B). It is of interest that apoptosis-inducing FOXO1 was clearly downregulated in IK1+/IK6– clones (Fig. 5A, 5C). Further analyses revealed that apoptosis-related proteins Caspase-3 and cleaved poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) were downregulated in IK1+/IK6– clones (Fig. 5D) suggesting that IK1 might play a role in apoptosis regulation.

DISCUSSION

B-ALL is the most common malignancy occurring in children, with approximately 20% of pediatric patients known to experience relapse, after which prognosis is extremely poor.1-4 The IKZF1 gene product IKAROS is known to have a relationship with B-ALL pathogenesis, though details regarding related molecular functions remain to revealed. Accordingly, the present study employed CRISPR/Cas9 for knockout of the IKZF1 gene and various IKAROS knockout lines were established, which allowed for a precise investigation of the gene product. Using four lines with different IKZF1 allele types, IK1+/IK6+ (parent), IK1+/IK6– (haploinsufficient), IK1–/IK6+ (IK1 knockout), and IK1–/IK6– (null), novel features of IKAROS were identified. Notably, elimination of IK6 resulted in upregulation of IK1 and STAT5 together with suppression of FOXO1, which might lead to the induction of differentiation and the downregulation of apoptosis-related proteins Caspase-3 and cleaved PARP. These may be part of the factors contributing poor prognosis of human Philadelphia-positive ALL cases.

The present results clearly showed that IK6 knockout using a CRISPR/Cas9 strategy induced significant upregulation of IK1 in B-ALL SUP-B15 cells as well as activation of STAT5, which are considered to directly cause cell cycle progression. Additionally, IK1 was shown to exist in the cytoplasm of haploinsufficient IK1+/IK6– clones, but not in that of the parent IK1+/IK6+, thus suggesting that IK6 may participate in cytoplasmic localization of IK1. In prior small interfering RNA (siRNA) work, residual IK6 likely persisted and could still heterodimerize with IK1 blunting rescue of IK1 function.25,26 In order to knockdown IK6 expression without affecting other IKAROS isoforms, siRNA design targeting the junction of exons 3–8 is required. Therefore, there are limitations in selecting sequences suitable for specific and effective knockdown. The present IK6 KO (IK1+/IK6–) model isolates haploinsufficiency from dominant-negative states and revealed phenotypes masked by partial depletion. To the best of our knowledge, no previous reports have shown that IK6 plays a role in regulation of these features, though further analyses are warranted to reveal the underlying mechanisms of the molecular pathways involved. Furthermore, identification of IKAROS-STAT5 signal transduction as a potential molecular target in B-ALL may lead to development of a novel treatment method for this difficult-to-cure human malignancy. In this regard, previous studies have shown that IK1 negatively regulates the G1-S phase transition of the cell cycle and interacts with the targeting of several genes by STAT5 in normal pre-B cells.27-32 Accordingly, differences between normal B and B-cell leukemia cellular context are likely to play crucial roles in determination of IKAROS function in cell cycle progression. Additional details providing greater elucidation of these signaling pathways will provide better understanding of the molecular pathogenesis of this intractable disease.

In the present experiments, IK6 knockout was found to elicit reduction of CD34 expression together with downregulation of IGLL1, VPREB1, and IL7Rα gene expressions, thus suggesting that haploinsufficient IK1 (IK1+/IK6–) has a role in induction of B-ALL cell differentiation. Previous studies that employed transient RNA interference for IK6 knockdown showed that inhibition of IK6 function results in slightly elevated IGLL1 expression,25,26 contrary to findings obtained in the present study. This seems to be a manifestation of adaptation to long-term complete IK6 protein knockout, which effectively mimics molecular events in B-ALL cases. It should also be noted that the parent line positive for both IK1 and IK6 expression (IK1+/IK6+) used in the present study contained two different cell populations with CD34 expression. A limiting dilution method was used to establish the parent line from a single cell and it is considered that CD34 high B-ALL cells differentiated into CD34 low cells. IK6 has been suggested to function as dominant negative counterpart of IK1 and suppress its functions,12 thus it is considered that partially inactivated IK1 induced inadequate maturation of the present lymphoid cells.

Additional studies using IK1 haploinsufficient B-ALL cells established from two WT IKZF1 alleles will be necessary to investigate phenotypes and expressions of various signal transduction molecules. Furthermore, findings obtained with restoration of IK6, which lacks four of the exons found in IK1, obtained with a CRISPR homology-directed repair strategy used for adding exons 4–7 of IKZF1 would be of interest to provide elucidation of differences in gene expression and phenotype among established cell lines.

There is an urgent need for development of novel effective therapeutic strategies for high risk B-ALL patients. A CRISPR/Cas9 strategy was employed in the present study to generate IK1 and IK6 knockout B-ALL lines to provide mechanistic insight into the various coordinated roles of IK1 and IK6 in the molecular pathogenesis of B-ALL. IK1 and IK6 knockout SUP-B15 lines were established, and shown to mimic molecular events that occur in B-ALL patients, with the findings suggestion that IK1 as well as activated STAT5 are candidates for development of targeted therapy.

Future studies aimed at development of molecular treatment strategies, as well as clinical testing for evaluation of IK1 and STAT5 activities mediated by inactivation of IK6 will be important, and may ultimately lead to reduction in the number of deaths caused by this devastating disease.

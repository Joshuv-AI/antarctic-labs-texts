Background

Postoperative complications have been reported to be more common among the elderly compared to the non-elderly patients undergoing surgery seen in 65.8% compared to 37.4% respectively (Chua and Chan 2020). Because of this relatively high postoperative complication rate in the elderly, significant mortality rates have been reported (Onen et al. 2022; Endeshaw et al. 2023). It is observed that the majority of the elderly patients who present to surgical care units usually have one or more associated co-morbidities that significantly compromise postoperative healing, the common ones being diabetes and hypertension (Fan et al. 2020; Ogbuanya et al. 2023; Endeshaw et al. 2023).

The bottom-line factor that creates the marked presentation of postoperative abdominal complications is frailty, which leads to poor physiological reserve among elderly patients (Tian et al. 2023). Emergency abdominal surgeries have been associated with unfavorable surgical care outcomes because of the marked toxicity that such patients present with, which is even worse due to patient delays to present to a surgical care unit or other in-hospital delays and scarcity of resources (Aakre et al. 2020; Ogwal et al. 2020; Ogbuanya et al. 2023).

Among the elderly surgical patients, the poor physiological reserve is more pronounced than that present in other emergency patients that are not elderly, which impede post-operative recovery (McCann et al. 2020; Sudlow et al. 2018). Geriatric patients commonly pose a significant challenge, mainly in emergency settings where the time to optimize the high-risk patient with other associated co-morbidities is inadequate, which predisposes these patients to a number of complications postoperatively (Halle-Smith et al. 2021; McCann et al. 2020; Menegozzo et al. 2019).

According to the hospital records at Jinja, Lira, Hoima, and Fort Portal Regional Referral Hospitals in the months of October to December 2023, 87 elderly patients underwent laparotomy procedures; some of whom sustained post-operative complications, according to the staff opinion; however, these outcomes were not well documented in the hospital records, nor were the predictors of complications assessed. There is an absence of data about outcomes from emergency laparotomy among the elderly in Uganda; the reason this study was done.

Methods

Study design and period

This study was a prospective observational cohort that covered a period of three months (from 15th November 2024 to 14th February 2025) in which elderly patients (> 65 years) that underwent emergency exploratory laparotomy were enrolled and followed up until 30 days post-operatively to assess for the occurrence of mortality and morbidity.

Study setting

The study sites were Jinja, Lira, Hoima, and Fort Portal Regional Referral Hospitals, wherein the study participants were recruited. These hospitals were selected because they could represent the different regions of Uganda, but also because they were accessible since they work as set lite teaching hospitals for Kampala international university.

Hoima and Fort Portal Regional Referral Hospitals are located in western Uganda in Hoima and Fort Portal cities, respectively, while Lira Regional Referral Hospital is located in northern Uganda in Lira City. Jinja Regional Referral Hospital is located in Eastern Uganda in Jinja City. The bed capacities for Jinja, Lira, Hoima, and Fort Portal Regional Referral Hospitals are 600, 401, 317 and 333 respectively. All these hospitals are government funded and have consultant surgeons, senior house officers, medical officers and other health workers attached to surgical wards. The departmental patient flow per day varies linearly and continuously from one hospital to another due to the different dynamics of each city and the population demographics. All these hospitals receive a sizeable number of elderly patients that undergo laparotomies, which was the target of this study.

Study population

We targeted patients aged 65 years and older with emergency abdominal pathology that needed to be addressed by an emergency exploratory laparotomy.

Inclusion criteria

All patients aged 65 years and above undergoing emergency exploratory laparotomy that gave permission to take part in the research were included. The participants who were not sure of their age were requested to avail their national identity card. The underlying diagnoses included intestinal obstruction, gut perforation, splenic injury, gastric outlet obstruction, appendicitis, splenic abscess and peritoneal abscess.

Exclusion criteria

Patients with mental illness, involved in poly-trauma or confirmed metastatic abdominal malignancy were excluded. The study centres were not currying out any laparoscopic procedures at the time. It’s those patients with endoscopy confirmed malignancy that had features suggestive of metastasis that were not included in the study because we anticipated that these patients could experience complications due to advanced malignancy that could bias our findings.

The sampling procedure was consecutive recruitment of study participants.

Recruitment and data collection procedure

Any patient aged 65 years and older sanctioned for laparotomy by the attending surgeon was approached and explained the objective of the study and why his or her participation was required. Any participant who accepted to participate and fulfilled the eligibility criteria signed an informed consent document. All patients were approached for consent. The hospital staffs were aware about the ongoing study and the study assistants were informed whenever a potential participant presented to hospital. All patients that presented during the study period were approached and none declined to participate since the study was observational in nature and carried a low risk. All age was confirmed. It is currently recommended in Uganda for all patients to present with identification card; which was used to confirm age. The consent for participation in the study was done by study assistants who were not directly involved in the care of the patient. However, the staff informed the study assistants wherever there was a potential participant. The baseline characteristics of the patients were assessed and documented in the data collection form. After consent, the patient underwent the surgery as recommended by the attending surgeon, and the attending surgeon documented the intraoperative findings during the course of the operation. Follow-up was done for 30 days; if the patient was discharged prior, review dates were appointed accordingly.

Following surgery, the patient was evaluated daily by the research team for the occurrence of any complications. Postoperative hemorrhage was assessed by examining the surgical site for any bleeding and checking the vital signs. If the blood pressure fell or the pulse rate increased, ultrasonography was done to confirm the presence of hemorrhage. Surgical site infection (SSI) was diagnosed by examination of the wound for induration and pus discharge. Ultrasonography was done if there were features suggestive of an organ-space infection. If there were findings suggestive of DVT, Doppler sonography was done to confirm it. Respiratory complications were assessed clinically using history-taking and examination, including auscultation and percussion of the chest.

The evaluation was done daily for each patient until the patient was discharged. At discharge, the information relating to the occurrence of complications and the complications that occurred was documented in the data collection form. Upon discharge, the participants were followed up in the outpatient department at 2 weeks and 30 days. However, in case of any sustained complaint, the participants were encouraged to come to the facility for an adequate evaluation.

Study variables

The study variables included independent variables like socioeconomic characteristics of elderly patients, such as sex, age, alcohol consumption, and cigarette smoking. Clinical characteristics of elderly patients included: body mass index, mid upper arm circumference, time to presentation (defined as time from onset of symptoms to when the patient reached the hospital where operation was done), time from presentation to surgery, duration of surgery, ASA class, wound class, blood transfusion, intraoperative findings, cadre of the patient’s operator, hemoglobin levels, WBCs, and platelets. The study dependent variables included both primary morbidity (hemorrhage, SSI, DVT, respiratory complications) and mortality occurring with in 30 days of surgery.

Quality control

A continuous medical education session was held at the start of the study and in between the study period to refresh the research assistants about the special category of elderly surgical patients and how best these patients could be managed in the respective local settings. We employed coded patient numbers for proper patient identification to prevent participant duplication. Data collection tools were well re-checked to confirm the adequate completeness of study participant data prior to their discharge. Case files and other relevant documents of the patients were referred to so as to gather any additional relevant information.

Data analysis plan

Microsoft office excel was used to create excel sheets with a summary of the collected data. The Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS Inc., Chicago, USA, version 26.0 for Windows) was used for data cleaning and later data analysis. The percentages and frequencies of patients with any early post-operative complication (complication occurring within 30 days following surgery) was computed, and data was represented in a table. Poisson regression with a 95% confidence interval and a P value of ≤ 0.05 was used at levels of bivariate and multivariate analysis. Variables with P ≤ 0.05 were considered significant predictors for morbidity and mortality among elderly patients who underwent emergency laparotomies.

Ethical considerations and consent

All methods were carried out in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations. The study adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki. Ethical approval was granted by Kampala International University western campus Research Ethics Committee (KIU-2024 492). All participants gave written informed consent as evidenced by the participant’s signature.

Results

In this study, we enrolled 86 participants (Fig. 1), of whom, 29.1% were above 80 years of age. Majority were male (74.4%). Over one-third of participants (33.7%) reported a history of smoking. No known comorbidity was present in 70.9% of the participants. Blood transfusion was done in 27.9% of the participants.

Discussion

In this study of older people undergoing emergency abdominal surgery in Uganda we have observed a high rate of morbidity (over 25%) and mortality (over 10%). The most common post operative complication was surgical site infection. This rate is comparable to that seen in similar populations in other parts of the world, including China where, the postoperative complications among elderly surgical patients were estimated at 20.5% following the open-operative method (Zhang et al. 2021). Since the 20.5% reported in China lies in the confidence interval found in this study (16.3–34.9), these rates of complications can be considered not to be significantly different. The possible explanation could be because in both studies, the patients under went open surgery and hence reporting comparable findings.

In Tanzania, in a prospective hospital-based study, surgical site infection was noted in 9% (Mponzi et al. 2024) also in a cross-sectional retrospective study in Rwanda, surgical site infections occurred in about 8.2% of the elderly patients (Mukamuhirwa et al. 2022), which was lower than that in this study where surgical site infection was seen in 27.3%. This can be explained by the fact that a big proportion of the participants in our study had bowel perforation which significantly increase the risk of surgical site infection.

Globally, complications among elderly patients following emergency abdominal surgeries are diverse, with a notable prolonged hospital stay of 13.5 days (Tian et al. 2023; Mercer et al. 2022) which is higher than in this study (8.57 days). This can be explained by the fact that the global results are compiled from across the world where there are a variety of risk factors and different patient characteristics and varying complexities of surgery which factors could all have come into play.

In elderly patients above 80 years, the mortality rate following abdominal surgery is approximated at about 5.16–6.2% (Endeshaw et al. 2023). Globally the mortality of 9.2% was seen (Tian et al. 2023). In China, elderly patients above the age of 75 years had a mortality of about 9.2% (Mercer et al. 2022) which was comparable to the mortality rate in our study since they fall within the confidence interval range of our study (4.7 to 17.4%). This can be explained by the similarities in the characteristics of the study participants.

Frailty significantly compromises the physiological reserve, thus making the elderly patients susceptible to postoperative complications (Chua and Chan 2020), which explains the high mortality rate.

Predictors for morbidity and mortality among elderly patients undergoing emergency exploratory laparotomy

In the multivariable analysis, the independent predictors for complications were: having a perforation, being older than 80 years and having DM or hypertension, for mortality were: delayed presentations to hospital (presenting after 24 h of symptoms), and undergoing blood transfusion.

A gut perforation greatly increases the danger of complications and death because it allows intestinal contents into the abdominal space, which can lead to a severe inflammatory response, resulting in peritonitis, sepsis, and multi-organ failure, with death occurring in severe instances. In agreement, a study conducted by (Busch and Gutzwiller 2011) revealed that perforation was associated with a higher reintervention rate.

Although unavoidable at times in major surgery, blood transfusion has been associated with the development of numerous post-operative complications due to its dose-dependent effects on wound healing, fluid balance, and host immunity. In agreement with our findings, a study conducted by (Vlot et al. 2019) revealed that Intra-operative transfusion was associated with a more than three-fold increased risk of 30-day mortality.

In Nigeria, delayed patient presentation to a surgical care centre, was associated with complications following abdominal surgeries (Ogbuanya et al. 2023) which is in agreement with our study where it was an independent predictor for mortality. A study conducted by (Franklin et al. 2024) revealed that late operation significantly increased risk for 30-day mortality, which is in agreement with this study. As the patients delay at home, the diseases progress. For example, for patients with intestinal obstruction, as they delay at home or in small health facilities, the bowel may get gangrene or perforate, resulting in hemodynamic instability that increase the risk of early complications.

In Rwanda and Tanzania, co-morbidities were associated with complications following emergency laparotomies in elderly patients (Mukamuhirwa et al. 2022; Mponzi et al. 2024). In Uganda, the factors that are associated with complications following emergency laparotomy included co-morbidities, and old age (Ssenyonjo and Kwikiriza 2024). This is in agreement with our study where comorbidities like diabetes Mellitus or hypertension were a risk factor for post operative complications. Diabates has been known to reduce the body immunity, increasing the risk of wound complications such as surgical site infection, which complication was the commonest in our study. Also, hypertension is associated with heart disease that may increase the risk of cardiovascular events.

Okot and others from Gulu Regional Hospital have noted that old age is also associated with surgical complications following emergency laparotomy (Amone et al. 2020) which is in agreement with our findings where age greater than 80 years was and independent risk factor for complications. Older age increases the risk of post-surgical complications due to the inherent physiological decline associated with aging, e.g., decreased organ function (e.g., heart, lungs, and kidneys), impaired immune system, decreased wound healing, and increased likelihood of underlying medical issues (comorbidities) that can further complicate recovery from surgery; this can lead to a higher risk of complications such as infections, blood clots, delirium, and longer recovery times compared to younger patients.

Based on these findings, it is clear that the mortality and morbidity rates among the elderly still need a closer attention. Given that wound complications were the commonest, it can be suggested that improving wound care and optimization would go far in preventing these complications. Give that a long duration of symptoms played a role in the outcomes of these elderly patients, the measures that encourage early presentation should be considered including sensitization of the communities.

Study limitations

The study was limited to government funded regional referral hospitals, yet the care for elderly patients undergoing surgery may differ significantly if the patients are in a private hospital, or a lower-level health facility; which limits the generalizability of our findings. The data collection was also limited to a period of 3 months and follow up limited to 30 days. The measure of severity of the complications was not done. Different surgeries with different risks of complications have been included which may limit generalizability of findings. Diabetes and hypertension were assessed as binary presence/absence and data on severity and control was not collected.

Conclusion

Complications were common with over one quarter having complications and over 10% dying. The independent predictors for complications were: having a perforation, being older than 80 years and having DM or hypertension. The independent predictors for mortality were: delayed presentations to hospital and blood transfusion. More attention is required among the elderly undergoing surgery in order to reduce the rate at which they get complications with emphasis on wound care to minimize surgical site infection, since it was the commonest complication. Healthy education and sensitization in communities should be strengthened, advocating for timely seeking of medical attentions by the elderly group. We recommend that future work in this direction should look at the group presenting as acute surgical admissions and review those who are offered surgery versus those who are not offered surgery in addition to bridging the other limitations identified in this study.

Radical property of the traces of the canonical modules of Cohen-Macaulay rings
Mitsuhiro Miyazaki

Abstract
In this paper, we define a new concept of Noetherian commutative rings which stands between Gorenstein and Cohen-Macaulay properties. We show that this new property keep hold under common operations of commutative rings such as localization, polynomial extension and under mild assumptions, flat extension, tensor product, Segre product and so on. We show that for Schubert cycles, the Ehrhart rings of cycle graphs and perfect graphs, this new concept is close to Gorenstein property. MSC: 13H10, 14M15, 05E40, 05C25, 52B20 Keywords : Gorenstein, Cohen-Macaulay, trace ideal, canonical module

Introduction

There is a hierarchy of commutative Noetherian local rings.

\displaystyle\mbox{regular}\Rightarrow\mbox{complete intersection}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\mbox{Gorenstein}\Rightarrow\mbox{Cohen-Macaulay}\Rightarrow\mbox{Buchsbaum}.

Cohen-Macaulay rings are first defined as a Noetherian rings which satisfy unmixedness theorem.
Macaulay showed that unmixedness theorem holds for polynomial rings over a field and Cohen
showed that unmixedness theorem holds for regular local rings.
In old days, Cohen-Macaulay rings are sometimes called semi-regular rings.
Further, there are many situations that rings under consideration is Cohen-Macaulay.
Therefore Cohen-Macaulay rings are platform of many theories.

On the other hand, Gorenstein ring is defined by Bass
[
Bas
]
.
A Gorenstein local ring is by definition a commutative Noetherian local ring whose self injective dimension is finite.
A Gorenstein ring is a Cohen-Macaulay ring whose parameter ideal is irreducible
and has very beautiful properties especially concerning symmetry of related objects such as syzygies and dualities.

In pursuing the study of Gorenstein and Cohen-Macaulay rings, many researchers felt that there is a rather large gap between
Gorenstein and Cohen-Macaulay properties.
Thus, there were attempts to define notions between Gorenstein and Cohen-Macaulay properties and fill this gap.
The first one is the level property defined by Stanley
[
Sta1
]
.
However, level property can be defined only for semi-standard graded rings over a field.

After that, almost Gorenstein property
[
BF
,
GMP
,
GTT
]

and nearly Gorenstein property
[
HHS
]
are defined.
However, there are few rings that are non-Gorenstein but almost or nearly Gorenstein.

In this paper, we define a new notion, that we call canonical trace radical (CTR for short) property,
which stands between Gorenstein and Cohen-Macaulay properties.
We define a Cohen-Macaulay local ring is CTR if it admits a canonical module and its trace ideal is a radical ideal.
We show that, under mild assumptions, CTR property is retained under the frequently used operations
of Noetherian rings, such as localization, flat extension, tensor product, Segre product and some others.
We also show that in some combinatorial rings, combinatorial property corresponding to CTR property is
close to combinatorial property corresponding to Gorenstein property.

Recently, Esentepe
[
Ese
]
treated the radical property of the trace ideal of the canonical module of a
Cohen-Macaulay ring in relation to Auslander-Reiten conjecture.

This paper is organized as follows.
In §2, we establish notation and terminology used in this paper and recall some basic facts,
especially the trace of a module.
In §3, we define the canonical trace radical (CTR for short) property and study under what kind of
ring operations, with sometimes additional assumptions, CTR property is retained:
localization, flat extension, polynomial extension, completion, division by a regular sequence, tensor product
and Segre product.

In §§4 and 5, we state criteria of CTR property for certain classes of rings which motivated us to define
CTR property.
In §4, we deal with Schubert cycles, i.e. the homogeneous coordinate rings of the Schubert
subvarieties of Grassmannians:
let
R
be a Schubert cycle.
Then by
[
BV
, §8]
, it is known that
R
is a Cohen-Macaulay normal domain and there are height 1 prime ideals

P_{0}
,
P_{1}
, …,
P_{t}
such that the divisor class group
{\mathrm{Cl}}(R)
is generated by

{\mathrm{cl}}(P_{0})
,
{\mathrm{cl}}(P_{1})
, …,
{\mathrm{cl}}(P_{t})
and
\sum_{i=0}^{t}{\mathrm{cl}}(P_{i})=0
is the only relation between
them.
Let
\sum_{i=0}^{t}\kappa_{i}{\mathrm{cl}}(P_{i})
be the canonical class of
{\mathrm{Cl}}(R)
,

\kappa=\max\{\kappa_{i}:0\leq i\leq t\}
and

\kappa^{\prime}=\min\{\kappa_{i}:0\leq i\leq t\}
.
Then
\kappa-\kappa^{\prime}
is independent of the representation of the canonical class above.
It is known that

is Gorenstein if and only if
\kappa-\kappa^{\prime}=0
.
We show that
R
is CTR if and only if
\kappa-\kappa^{\prime}\leq 1
.
See Theorem
4.3
.

In §5, we deal with the Ehrhart ring of the stable set polytope of a cycle graph:
let
R
be such a ring.
It is known that
R
is Gorenstein if and only if the length
n
of the cycle is even or less than 7.
We show that

is CTR if and only if
n
is even or less than 9.
See Theorem
5.2
.

Finally in §6, we state a necessary condition that the Ehrhart ring

of the stable set polytope of
a perfect graph
G
is CTR:
set
k=\max\{|K|:

is a maximal clique in
G\}
and

k^{\prime}=\min\{|K|:

K
is a maximal clique in
G\}
.
It is known that
R
is Gorenstein if and only if
k-k^{\prime}=0
.
We show that if
R
is CTR, then
k-k^{\prime}\leq 1
.
See Proposition
6.2
.

3
Canonical trace radical rings

In this section, we define the notion of canonical trace radical rings
(CTR rings for short) and study basic properties of CTR rings.
The reasons that we think CTR property is close to Gorenstein property is shown in the following sections.
First we recall the following.

Fact 3.1

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring with canonical module
\omega_{R}
.
Then
R
is Gorenstein if and only if
{\mathrm{tr}}_{R}(\omega_{R})=R
.

For the proof, see e.g.
[
HHS
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Definition 3.2

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay ring.
If for any
\mathfrak{p}\in{\mathrm{Spec}}(R)
,
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
has a canonical module
\omega_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}
and

{\mathrm{tr}}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\omega_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}})
is a radical ideal,
then we say that
R
is a canonical trace radical (CTR for short) ring.

Since the unit ideal is a radical ideal, a Gorenstein ring is a CTR ring.
Further, it is evident from the definition that CTR property is kept by localization.

By Lemma
2.2
, we see the following.

Proposition 3.3

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring with canonical module.
Then
R
is CTR if and only if
{\mathrm{tr}}_{R}(\omega_{R})
is a radical ideal.

“Only if” part is a direct consequence of the definition.
We prove the “if” part.
Let
\mathfrak{p}
be an arbitrary prime ideal of
R
.
By Lemma
2.2
, we see that

{\mathrm{tr}}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}((\omega_{R})_{\mathfrak{p}})={\mathrm{tr}}_{R}(\omega_{R})_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Since
\omega_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}=(\omega_{R})_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
{\mathrm{tr}}_{R}(\omega_{R})_{\mathfrak{p}}
is
a radical ideal of
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
, we see the result.

Next we show a similar fact to the above proposition, which may be regarded as a graded
version of the above proposition.

Proposition 3.4

Let
R
be an
\mathbb{N}
-graded Cohen-Macaulay ring over a field
\mathbb{K}
and
\omega_{R}
the graded
canonical module of
R
.
Then
R
is CTR if and only if
{\mathrm{tr}}_{R}(\omega_{R})
is a radical ideal.

We first prove the “only if” part.
Let
\mathfrak{m}
be the irrelevant maximal ideal of
R
.
Then
(\omega_{R})_{\mathfrak{m}}
is the canonical module of
R_{\mathfrak{m}}
.
Therefore, by assumption and Lemma
2.2
,

{\mathrm{tr}}_{R}(\omega_{R})_{\mathfrak{m}}={\mathrm{tr}}_{R_{\mathfrak{m}}}((\omega_{R})_{\mathfrak{m}})
is a radical ideal.
Since
{\mathrm{tr}}_{R}(\omega_{R})
is a graded ideal, every associated prime of

{\mathrm{tr}}_{R}(\omega_{R})
is graded and therefore contained in
\mathfrak{m}
.
Thus, the radical property of
{\mathrm{tr}}_{R}(\omega_{R})_{\mathfrak{m}}
implies the radical property
of
{\mathrm{tr}}_{R}(\omega_{R})
.

Now we prove the “if” part.
Let
\mathfrak{p}
be an arbitrary prime ideal of
R
.
Take a polynomial ring
S
with weighted degree over
\mathbb{K}
and a graded surjective

\mathbb{K}
-algebra homomorphism
S\to R
.
Let
P
be the preimage of
\mathfrak{p}
.
Also take a minimal graded
S
-free resolution

0\to F_{h}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi_{h}}}{{\to}}\cdots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi_{1}}}{{\to}}F_{0}\to R\to 0

of
R
, a free
R
-module
G
and a graded
R
-homomorphism
\psi
such that

G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi}}{{\to}}F_{h}\otimes R\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi_{h}\otimes 1}}{{\to}}F_{h-1}\otimes R

is exact.
Then

0\to(F_{h})_{P}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\varphi_{h})_{P}}}{{\to}}\cdots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\varphi_{1})_{P}}}{{\to}}(F_{0})_{P}\to R_{\mathfrak{p}}\to 0

is a (not necessarily minimal)
S_{P}
-free resolution of
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
and

G_{\mathfrak{p}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}}}{{\to}}(F_{h})_{P}\otimes_{S_{P}}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\varphi_{h})_{P}\otimes 1}}{{\to}}(F_{h-1})_{P}\otimes_{S_{P}}R_{\mathfrak{p}}

is exact.

Since
R
is Cohen-Macaulay, it follows that
{\mathrm{Ass}}R={\mathrm{Assh}}R
and therefore

h=\dim S-\dim R=\dim S_{P}-\dim R_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Thus, by Lemma
2.3
, we see that
{\mathrm{tr}}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\omega_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}})=I_{1}(\psi_{\mathfrak{p}})
.
Since
{\mathrm{tr}}_{R}(\omega_{R})=I_{1}(\psi)
by Lemma
2.3
and
{\mathrm{tr}}_{R}(\omega_{R})

is a radical ideal, we see that

{\mathrm{tr}}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\omega_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}})=I_{1}(\psi_{\mathfrak{p}})=(I_{1}(\psi))_{\mathfrak{p}}={\mathrm{tr}}_{R}(\omega_{R})_{\mathfrak{p}}

is a radical ideal.

By Propositions
3.3
and
3.4
, we see that nearly Gorenstein rings are CTR rings.

Next we consider the CTR property under the flat extension.

Proposition 3.5

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring with canonical module and

(R,\mathfrak{m})\to(S,\mathfrak{n})
be a flat local homomorphism.
Suppose
S/\mathfrak{m}S
is a Gorenstein ring.
Then the followings hold.

(1)

If
S
is CTR, then so is
R
.

(2)

If
R
is CTR and for any
\mathfrak{p}\in\min({\mathrm{tr}}_{R}(\omega_{R}))
,

S_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}S_{\mathfrak{p}}=\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\otimes_{R}S
is a reduced ring, then
S
is CTR.

First note that
\omega_{R}\otimes S
is the canonical module of

, see
[

, Theorem 3.3.14]
.
Therefore, by Lemma
2.2
we see that
{\mathrm{tr}}_{S}(\omega_{S})={\mathrm{tr}}_{R}(\omega_{R})S
.

(1)

Since the natural map

R/{\mathrm{tr}}_{R}(\omega_{R})\to S/{\mathrm{tr}}_{R}(\omega_{R})S=S/{\mathrm{tr}}_{S}(\omega_{S})

is faithfully flat,
R/{\mathrm{tr}}_{R}(\omega_{R})
is isomorphic to a subring of
S/{\mathrm{tr}}_{S}(\omega_{S})
.
Since
S/{\mathrm{tr}}_{S}(\omega_{S})
is reduced by assumption,
R/{\mathrm{tr}}_{R}(\omega_{R})
is also reduced.

(2)

Let
P
be an arbitrary associated prime ideal of
{\mathrm{tr}}_{S}(\omega_{S})
and set
\mathfrak{p}=P\cap R
.
Since

(R/{\mathrm{tr}}_{R}(\omega_{R}))_{\mathfrak{p}}\to(S/{\mathrm{tr}}_{S}(\omega_{S}))_{P}

is a flat local homomorphism,

0pt(R/{\mathrm{tr}}_{R}(\omega_{R}))_{\mathfrak{p}}\leq 0pt(S/{\mathrm{tr}}_{S}(\omega_{S}))_{P}=0

by
[

.
Therefore,
\mathfrak{p}
is an associated prime ideal of
{\mathrm{tr}}_{R}(\omega_{R})
.
Since
{\mathrm{tr}}_{R}(\omega_{R})
is a radical ideal, we see that
\mathfrak{p}\in\min({\mathrm{tr}}_{R}(\omega_{R}))

and
(R/{\mathrm{tr}}_{R}(\omega_{R}))_{\mathfrak{p}}=\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
.
Thus,
since
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\otimes_{R}S_{P}
is a localization of
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\otimes_{R}S
,
we see by assumption that

(S/{\mathrm{tr}}_{S}(\omega_{S}))_{P}=(S/{\mathrm{tr}}_{R}(\omega_{R})S)_{P}=(R/{\mathrm{tr}}_{R}(\omega_{R}))_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes_{R}S_{P}=\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\otimes_{R}S_{P}

is reduced.
Since
P
is an arbitrary associated prime ideal of
{\mathrm{tr}}_{S}(\omega_{S})
, we see that

{\mathrm{tr}}_{S}(\omega_{S})
is a radical ideal.

Corollary 3.6

Let
R
be a ring and
S=R[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
a polynomial ring over
R
.
Then
S
is CTR if and only if so is
R
.

First assume that
S
is CTR.
Let
\mathfrak{p}
be an arbitrary prime ideal of
R
.
Set
P=\mathfrak{p}S
.
Then
P
is a prime ideal of
S
and
R_{\mathfrak{p}}=S_{P}/(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})S_{P}
.
Since
S_{P}
admits a canonical module,
S_{P}
is a homomorphic image of a Gorenstein ring.
Therefore,
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is also a homomorphic image of a Gorenstein ring.
Thus,
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
admits a canonical module.

Since
S_{P}/\mathfrak{p}S_{P}=S_{P}/PS_{P}
is a field,
therefore
is Gorenstein and
R_{\mathfrak{p}}\to S_{P}
is a flat local homomorphism, we see by
Proposition
3.5
that
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is CTR.
Since
\mathfrak{p}
is an arbitrary prime ideal of
R
, we see that
R
is CTR.

Next we assume that that
R
is CTR.
Let
P
be an arbitrary prime ideal of
S
and set
\mathfrak{p}=P\cap R
.
Then
S_{P}/\mathfrak{p}S_{P}=\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\otimes_{R}S_{P}
is a localization of
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
and therefore Gorenstein.
Further, for any
\mathfrak{q}\in\min({\mathrm{tr}}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\omega_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}))
,
\kappa(\mathfrak{q})\otimes_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}S_{P}
is a localization of

Next consider the CTR property under completion.

Next we consider the CTR property under the quotient of an ideal generated by a regular sequence.

Proposition 3.8

Let

.
Thus, we see that
{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathfrak{a})
is not a radical ideal.

Remark 4.4

Ficarra et al.
[
FHST
, Theorem 1.1]
showed that if
\mathbb{K}
is a field,

m
and
n
are integers with
2\leq m\leq n
,

X=(X_{ij})
is an
m\times n
matrix of indeterminates and

t
is an integer with
2\leq t\leq m
then

{\mathrm{tr}}_{R}(\omega_{R})=I_{t-1}(X)^{n-m}R,

where
R=\mathbb{K}[X_{ij}:1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq n]/I_{t}(X)
.
Since
I_{t-1}(X)R
is a prime ideal of
R
, we see that
R
is CTR if and only if

n-m\leq 1
.
While by
[
BV
, Corollary 8.9]

R
is Gorenstein if and only if
n-m=0
.

5
CTR property of the Ehrhart rings of the stable set polytopes of cycle graphs

In this section, we establish a criterion of the CTR propety of the
Ehrhart ring of the stable set polytope of a cycle graph.
For basic terminology and facts of graph theory, we consult
[
Die
]
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph.
A stable set
S
of
G
is a subset of
V
with no pair of elements in
S
are adjacent.

\emptyset
and
\{v\}
for any
v\in V
are trivially stable.
We define the stable set polytope, denoted by
\mathrm{STAB}(G)
of
G
by

\mathrm{STAB}(G)\mathrel{:=}\mathrm{conv}\{\chi_{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{V}:S\mbox{ is a stable set of $G$}\}.

Next we state the following.

Definition 5.1

Let
X
be a finite set and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{X}
.
For
B\subset X
, we set
\xi^{+}(B)\mathrel{:=}\sum_{b\in B}\xi(b)
.

We call a graph
G
a cycle graph if
G
consists of one cycle only, i.e.

V=\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1}\}
,
E=\{\{v_{i},v_{j}\}:i-j\equiv 1\pmod{n}\}

for some
n
with
n\geq 3
.
A graph
G=(V,E)
is called a t-perfect graph if

\mathrm{STAB}(G)=\left\{f\in\mathbb{R}^{V}:\vbox{\noindent$0\leq f(v)\leq 1$ for any $v\in V$,
$f^{+}(e)\leq 1$ for any $e\in E$ and
$f^{+}(C)\leq\frac{|C|-1}{2}$ for any odd cycle $C$ without chord}\right\}.

It is known that a cycle graph is t-perfect. See
[
Mah
]
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a t-perfect graph.
For
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, set

{{t}\mathcal{U}}^{(n)}\mathrel{:=}\left\{\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{V^{-}}:\vbox{\noindent$\mu(v)\geq n$ for any $v\in V$,
$\mu^{+}(K)+n\leq\mu(-\infty)$ for any maximal clique $K$ in $G$ and
$\mu^{+}(C)+n\leq\frac{|C|-1}{2}\mu(-\infty)$ for any odd cycle $C$ without chord and length at least 5}\right\}.

Since
G
is t-perfect, there are no cliques with size greater than 3, we see by
[
Miy3
, Remark 3.10]
that

\omega_{{E_{\mathbb{K}}[\mathrm{STAB}(G)]}}^{(n)}=\bigoplus_{\mu\in{{t}\mathcal{U}}^{(n)}}\mathbb{K}T^{\mu}

for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a cycle graph.
If the length of the cycle is even, then
G
is a bipartite graph and therefore is a perfect graph whose maximal
cliques have size 2.
Thus, by
[
OH
, Theorem 2.1 (b)]
, we see that
{E_{\mathbb{K}}[\mathrm{STAB}(G)]}
is a Gorenstein ring.
Further, if the length
n
of the cycle is odd, then by
[
HT
, Theorem 1]
,
{E_{\mathbb{K}}[\mathrm{STAB}(G)]}
is Gorenstein if and only if

n\leq 5
.

Therefore, we assume in the following of this section that
G=(V,E)
is a cycle graph of odd length
n

with
n\geq 7
.
Set
n=2\ell+1
,
V=\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{2\ell}\}
and we consider indices modulo
2\ell+1
,

E=\{\{v_{i},v_{j}\}:i-j\equiv 1\pmod{2\ell+1}\}
,
e_{i}=\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\}
for
0\leq i\leq 2\ell
.
Further, we denote by
\omega
the canonical ideal of
{E_{\mathbb{K}}[\mathrm{STAB}(G)]}
.

Set

\mathfrak{p}_{i}\mathrel{:=}\bigoplus_{{\mu\in{{t}\mathcal{U}}^{(0)}},\atop\mu(v_{i})>0\ \mathrm{or}\ \mu^{+}(V)<\ell\mu(-\infty)}\mathbb{K}T^{\mu}

for
0\leq i\leq 2\ell
.
Then
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
is a prime ideal of
{E_{\mathbb{K}}[\mathrm{STAB}(G)]}
and

\sqrt{{\mathrm{tr}}(\omega)}=\bigcap_{i=0}^{2\ell}\mathfrak{p}_{i}

by
[
Miy4
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Now we state the following criterion of CTR property of
{E_{\mathbb{K}}[\mathrm{STAB}(G)]}
.

Theorem 5.2

Let
G=(V,E)
be a cycle graph of odd length
n
.
Then
{E_{\mathbb{K}}[\mathrm{STAB}(G)]}
is a CTR ring if and only if
n\leq 7
.

Set
n=2\ell+1
as above.
First we prove the “if” part.
Suppose
\ell=3
,
\mu\in{{t}\mathcal{U}}^{(0)}
and
T^{\mu}\in\bigcap_{i=0}^{2\ell}\mathfrak{p}_{i}
.
Then by the proof of
[
Miy4
, Lemmas 3.4 and 3.5]
, we see that
T^{\mu}
in
{\mathrm{tr}}(\omega)
.
Therefore, we see that
\bigcap_{i=0}^{2\ell}\mathfrak{p}_{i}\subset{\mathrm{tr}}(\omega)
.
Since
\sqrt{{\mathrm{tr}}(\omega)}=\bigcap_{i=0}^{2\ell}\mathfrak{p}_{i}
, we see that

\sqrt{{\mathrm{tr}}(\omega)}={\mathrm{tr}}(\omega)

and
{E_{\mathbb{K}}[\mathrm{STAB}(G)]}
is a CTR ring.

Next we prove the contraposition of “only if” part.
Suppose that
\ell\geq 4
and define
\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{V^{-}}
by

\mu(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{ if $x\in\{v_{2},v_{4},\ldots,v_{2\ell-2},-\infty\}$},\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Then,
\mu\in{{t}\mathcal{U}}^{(0)}
and
\mu^{+}(V)=\ell-1<\ell=\ell\mu(-\infty)
.
Therefore, we see that
T^{\mu}\in\bigcap_{i=0}^{2\ell}\mathfrak{p}_{i}=\sqrt{{\mathrm{tr}}(\omega)}
.
We assume that
\mu
can be expressed as a sum of elements in
{{t}\mathcal{U}}^{(1)}
and
{{t}\mathcal{U}}^{(-1)}

and deduce a contradiction.

Suppose
\mu=\eta+\zeta
,
\eta\in{{t}\mathcal{U}}^{(1)}
and
\zeta\in{{t}\mathcal{U}}^{(-1)}
.
Since
\eta(x)\geq 1
for any
x\in V
and
\eta^{+}(V)+1\leq\ell\eta(-\infty)
,
we see that

\eta(-\infty)\geq\left\lceil\frac{2\ell+2}{\ell}\right\rceil=3.

Similarly, since
\zeta(x)\geq-1
for any
x\in V
and
\zeta^{+}(V)-1\leq\ell\zeta(-\infty)
,
we see that

\zeta(-\infty)\geq\left\lceil\frac{-2\ell-2}{\ell}\right\rceil=-2.

On the other hand, since
\eta(-\infty)+\zeta(-\infty)=\mu(-\infty)=1
, we see that

\eta(-\infty)=3\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\zeta(-\infty)=-2.

Moreover, since
\eta(v_{i})+\eta(v_{i+1})+1=\eta^{+}(e_{i})+1\leq\eta(-\infty)=3

and
\eta(v_{j})\geq 1
for any
i
and
j
, we see that
\eta(v_{j})=1
for any
j
.
Thus,

\zeta(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\mbox{ if $x\in\{v_{2},v_{4},\cdots,v_{2\ell-2}\}$},\\
-2&\mbox{ if $x=-\infty$},\\
-1&\mbox{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Therefore,
\zeta^{+}(V)=-\ell-2
and we see that

\zeta^{+}(V)-1=-\ell-3>-2\ell=\ell\zeta(-\infty),

since
\ell\geq 4
.
This contradicts to the assumption that
\zeta\in{{t}\mathcal{U}}^{(-1)}
.
Therefore, we see that
T^{\mu}\not\in{\mathrm{tr}}(\omega)
and
{\mathrm{tr}}(\omega)
is not a radical ideal.

6
A necessary condition for the Ehrhart ring of the stable set polytope of a
perfect graph to be CTR

In this section, we state a necessary condition of an Ehrhart ring of the stable set polytope of a
perfect graph to be CTR,
which shows that CTR property is close to Gorenstein property.
Let
G=(V,E)
be a perfect graph and set

k=\max\{|K|:K
is a maximal clique of
G\}
and

k^{\prime}=\min\{|K|:K
is a maximal clique of
G\}
.

By Chvátal
[
Chv
, Theorem 3.1]
, we see that

\mathrm{STAB}(G)=\left\{f\in\mathbb{R}^{V}:\vbox{\noindent$f(x)\geq 0$ for any $x\in V$ and $f^{+}(K)\leq 1$ for any maximal clique $K$ of $G$}\right\}.

Further, by Ohsugi and Hibi
[
OH
, Theorem 2.1 (b)]
,
{E_{\mathbb{K}}[\mathrm{STAB}(G)]}
is Gorenstein if and only if
k-k^{\prime}=0
.
We recall notation and basic facts from
[
Miy3
, Remark 3.8]
.
For
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we set

{{q}\mathcal{U}}^{(n)}\mathrel{:=}\left\{\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{V^{-}}:\vbox{\noindent$\mu(x)\geq n$ for any $x\in V$ and
$\mu^{+}(K)+n\leq\mu(-\infty)$ for any maximal clique $K$ of $G$}\right\}.

Then

\omega^{(n)}=\bigoplus_{\mu\in{{q}\mathcal{U}}^{(n)}}\mathbb{K}T^{\mu}

for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
\omega
is the canonical ideal of
{E_{\mathbb{K}}[\mathrm{STAB}(G)]}
.

Here, we state a very easily proved but very useful fact.

Lemma 6.1

If
x\in V
,
\eta\in{{q}\mathcal{U}}^{(1)}
,
\zeta\in{{q}\mathcal{U}}^{(-1)}
and
(\eta+\zeta)(x)=0
,
then
\eta(x)=1
and
\zeta(x)=-1
.

Now we state the following necessary condition for
{E_{\mathbb{K}}[\mathrm{STAB}(G)]}
to be a CTR ring.

Proposition 6.2

Let
G=(V,E)
be a perfect graph.
If
{E_{\mathbb{K}}[\mathrm{STAB}(G)]}
is CTR, then
k-k^{\prime}\leq 1
.

We prove the contraposition of the proposition, so we assume that
k-k^{\prime}\geq 2
.
Let
\mu
be an element of
\mathbb{Z}^{V^{-}}
defined by

\mu(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\mbox{ if $x\in V$},\\
1&\mbox{ if $x=-\infty$}.\end{array}\right.

Then
\mu\in{{q}\mathcal{U}}^{(0)}
and therefore
T^{\mu}\in{E_{\mathbb{K}}[\mathrm{STAB}(G)]}
.
Further, if we define
\eta
,
\zeta\in\mathbb{Z}^{V^{-}}
by

\eta(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{ if $x\in V$},\\
k+1&\mbox{ if $x=-\infty$},\end{array}\right.\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\zeta(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-1&\mbox{ if $x\in V$},\\
-k^{\prime}-1&\mbox{ if $x=-\infty$},\end{array}\right.

then
\eta\in{{q}\mathcal{U}}^{(1)}
,
\zeta\in{{q}\mathcal{U}}^{(-1)}
and
(k-k^{\prime})\mu=\eta+\zeta
.
Therefore,

(T^{\mu})^{k-k^{\prime}}\in{\mathrm{tr}}(\omega).

On the other hand, if there are
\eta^{\prime}\in{{q}\mathcal{U}}^{(1)}
and
\zeta^{\prime}\in{{q}\mathcal{U}}^{(-1)}
with

\mu=\eta^{\prime}+\zeta^{\prime}
, then
\eta^{\prime}(x)=1
and
\zeta^{\prime}(x)=-1
for any
x\in V
by Lemma
6.1
.
Therefore,
\eta^{\prime}(-\infty)\geq k+1
and
\zeta^{\prime}(-\infty)\geq-k^{\prime}-1
.
Thus,

1=\mu(-\infty)=\eta^{\prime}(-\infty)+\zeta^{\prime}(-\infty)\geq k-k^{\prime}\geq 2.

This is a contradiction.
Therefore,
T^{\mu}\not\in{\mathrm{tr}}(\omega)
and we see that
{\mathrm{tr}}(\omega)
is not a radical ideal.

As the following example shows, the condition in the above proposition is not sufficient.

Example 6.3

Let
G=(V,E)
be the following graph.
y_{1}
x_{1}
x_{2}
x_{3}
y_{4}
y_{2}
y_{3}

This is a comparability graph of a poset
P
whose Hasse diagram is

y_{1}
x_{1}
y_{2}
x_{2}
x_{3}
y_{3}
y_{4}

i.e.

G=(V,E)
,
V=P
,
E=\{\{z,w\}:z,w\in P,z<w\}
.
In particular,
G
is perfect.
Further,

\max\{|K|:K
is a maximal clique in
G\}=3
and

\min\{|K|:K
is a maximal clique in
G\}=2
.

Define
\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{V^{-}}
by

\mu(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{ if $z\in\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\}$},\\
0&\mbox{ if $z\in\{y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},y_{4}\}$},\\
2&\mbox{ if $z=-\infty$}.\end{array}\right.

Then
\mu\in{{q}\mathcal{U}}^{(0)}
.

We first show that
T^{\mu}\not\in{\mathrm{tr}}(\omega)
.
Assume the contrary.
Then there are
\eta\in{{q}\mathcal{U}}^{(1)}
and
\zeta\in{{q}\mathcal{U}}^{(-1)}
with
\mu=\eta+\zeta
.
Then
\eta(y_{i})=1
and
\zeta(y_{i})=-1
for
1\leq i\leq 4
by Lemma
6.1
.
Further,

\displaystyle\eta(-\infty)+\zeta(-\infty)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu(-\infty)=2,

\displaystyle\eta(x_{i})+\zeta(x_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu(x_{i})=1\quad\mbox{ for $1\leq i\leq 3$},

\displaystyle\zeta(x_{1})+\zeta(y_{1})-1

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\zeta(-\infty),

\displaystyle\eta(x_{1})+\eta(x_{2})+\eta(y_{2})+1

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\eta(-\infty),

\displaystyle\zeta(x_{2})+\zeta(x_{3})-1

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\zeta(-\infty)

and

\displaystyle\eta(x_{3})+\eta(y_{3})+\eta(y_{4})+1

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\eta(-\infty).

Therefore

\displaystyle 4

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\mu(-\infty)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\eta(-\infty)+2\zeta(-\infty)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(\zeta(x_{1})-2)+(\eta(x_{1})+\eta(x_{2})+2)+(\zeta(x_{2})+\zeta(x_{3})-1)+(\eta(x_{3})+3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta(x_{1})+\eta(x_{2})+\eta(x_{3})+\zeta(x_{1})+\zeta(x_{2})+\zeta(x_{3})+2

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu(x_{1})+\mu(x_{2})+\mu(x_{3})+2

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 5.

This is a contradiction.
Thus, we see that
T^{\mu}\not\in{\mathrm{tr}}(\omega)
.

Next, we show that
T^{\mu}\in\sqrt{{\mathrm{tr}}(\omega)}
.
Since
G
is a comparability graph of
P
,
\mathrm{STAB}(G)=\mathscr{C}(P)
by
[
Chv
, Theorem 3.1]
,
where
\mathscr{C}(P)
is the chain polytope of
P
.
See
[
Sta2
]
for the definition of the chain polytope.
Therefore, by
[
MP
, Theorem 3.7]
, we see that
T^{\mu}\in\sqrt{{\mathrm{tr}}(\omega)}
.
In fact, by using the idea of the proof of
[
MP
, Theorem 3.7]
, we can construct
\eta\in{{q}\mathcal{U}}^{(1)}
and

\zeta\in{{q}\mathcal{U}}^{(-1)}
as follows.

\displaystyle\eta(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{ if $z\in\{y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},y_{4}\}$},\\
2&\mbox{ if $z\in\{x_{1},x_{2}\}$},\\
3&\mbox{ if $z=x_{3}$},\\
6&\mbox{ if $z=-\infty$},\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\zeta(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\mbox{ if $z\in\{x_{1},x_{2}\}$},\\
-1&\mbox{ if $z\in\{x_{3},y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},y_{4}\}$},\\
-2&\mbox{ if $z=-\infty$}\end{array}\right.

Then
\eta+\zeta=2\mu
and therefore
(T^{\mu})^{2}\in{\mathrm{tr}}(\omega)
.

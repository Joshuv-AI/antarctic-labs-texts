Quadratic obstructions to Small-Time Local Controllability for multi-input systems
Théo Gherdaoui

Abstract
We present a necessary condition for the small-time local controllability of multi-input control-affine systems on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . This condition is formulated on the vectors of ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} resulting from the evaluation at zero of the Lie brackets of the vector fields: it involves both their direction and their amplitude. The proof is an adaptation to the multi-input case of a general method introduced by Beauchard and Marbach in the single-input case – see [ 4 ] . It relies on a Magnus-type representation formula: the state is approximated by a linear combination of the evaluation at zero of the Lie brackets of the vector fields, whose coefficients are functionals of the time and the controls. Finally, obstructions to small-time local controllability result from interpolation inequalities. Keywords: Control theory, ODEs, Obstruction for controllability, Lie Brackets, Small-time local exact controllability, control-affine systems.

1.6
The strategy to prove drifts

Definition 1.34

(Monomial basis)
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a basis of
\mathcal{L}(X)
. We say that
\mathcal{B}
is monomial if
\mathcal{B}\subset\textsc{e}(\Br(X))
. For such
bases, if
b\in\mathcal{B}
, one can define
|b|
,
n_{i}(b)
and
n(b)
as in Definition
1.13
by importing these notions from
\Br(X)
. For
A\subset\mathbb{N}
, we define
\mathcal{B}_{A}:=\{b\in\mathcal{B};\ n(b)\in A\}
.

In this heuristic, we work with
\mathcal{B}
, an abstract monomial basis of
\mathcal{L}(X)
. Section
2.1
is dedicated to the construction of an appropriate one. We refer the interested reader to that section for further details.

The proof of Theorem
1.22
is based on the Magnus-type representation formula and a strategy developed by Beauchard and Marbach in
[
4
]
to prove quadratic obstructions to STLC for single-input systems. Here are the main points to adapt their strategy to the case of multi-input systems. We fix
\mathcal{B}
, a monomial basis of
\mathcal{L}(X)
,
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
p\in[1,+\infty]
.
The purpose is to create a drift – see Definition
1.7
– as
\left(t,\left\|(u,v)\right\|_{W^{m,p}}\right)\to 0
. Let
M\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. The solution to (
1.1
) is given by the following formula – see Proposition
2.14
– as
\left\|(u,v)\right\|_{L^{1}}\to 0
,

x(t;(u,v))=\mathcal{Z}_{M}(t;f,(u,v))(0)+\mathcal{O}\left(\left\|(u,v)\right\|_{L^{1}}^{M+1}+\left|x(t;(u,v))\right|^{1+\frac{1}{M}}\right),

where
\mathcal{Z}_{M}(t;f,(u,v))
is an analytic vector field belonging to
S_{\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket}(f)
and given by

\mathcal{Z}_{M}(t;f,(u,v))=\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}_{\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket}}\eta_{b}(t,(u,v))f_{b},

where
\eta_{b}
are functionals, called coordinates of the pseudo-first kind – see
[
3
, Proposition
44
]
for more details. These functionals are not easy to compute. However, the coordinates of the second kind
(\xi_{b})_{b\in\mathcal{B}}
– see Definition
2.7
and
[
3
, Section
2.5.3.
]
– are straightforward to evaluate and there is a link between
\eta_{b}
and
\xi_{b}
. Heuristically, we can think that
\eta_{b}\approx\xi_{b}
. Then, the Magnus formula becomes

\begin{gathered}x(t;(u,v))=\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}_{\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket}}\xi_{b}(t,(u,v))f_{b}(0)+\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}_{\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket}}\left(\eta_{b}-\xi_{b}\right)(t,(u,v))f_{b}(0)\\
+\mathcal{O}\left(\left\|(u,v)\right\|_{L^{1}}^{M+1}+\left|x(t;(u,v))\right|^{1+\frac{1}{M}}\right),\end{gathered}

(1.18)

where the dominant part is the first sum. We
now consider
\mathfrak{b}_{1},\mathfrak{b}_{2},\mathfrak{b}_{3}\in\mathcal{B}_{\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket}
, brackets that will be used to create a drift and

\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{B}_{\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket}\setminus\{\mathfrak{b}_{1},\mathfrak{b}_{2},\mathfrak{b}_{3}\}
the set defined as

\forall b\in\mathcal{B}_{\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket}\setminus\{\mathfrak{b}_{1},\mathfrak{b}_{2},\mathfrak{b}_{3}\},\quad\left(\forall i\in\llbracket 1,3\rrbracket,\ \xi_{b}\neq o(\xi_{\mathfrak{b_{i}}})\text{ as }\left(t,\left\|(u,v)\right\|_{W^{m,p}}\right)\to 0\right)\Rightarrow b\in\mathcal{N}.

The elements of
\mathcal{N}
are the brackets whose second-kind coordinates are not negligible compared to those of the brackets generating the drift. As we are currently unable to handle these terms, we have chosen to include them in a vector space that will be removed by passing to a quotient. Then, we consider a linear form
\mathbb{P}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, so that

\mathchoice{{\mathbb{P}\,\smash{\vrule height=5.46666pt,depth=3.02223pt}}_{\,\mathcal{N}(f)(0)}}{{\mathbb{P}\,\smash{\vrule height=5.46666pt,depth=3.02223pt}}_{\,\mathcal{N}(f)(0)}}{{\mathbb{P}\,\smash{\vrule height=3.82668pt,depth=2.20056pt}}_{\,\mathcal{N}(f)(0)}}{{\mathbb{P}\,\smash{\vrule height=2.71904pt,depth=2.78612pt}}_{\,\mathcal{N}(f)(0)}}\equiv 0\quad\text{and}\quad\displaystyle\Delta(u,v):=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\xi_{\mathfrak{b_{i}}}(t,(u,v))\mathbb{P}\left(f_{\mathfrak{b_{i}}}(0)\right)\geq 0.

(1.19)

The existence of such a linear form
\mathbb{P}
is not systematically guaranteed; this is the assumption (BC) of Theorem
1.22
.
Finally, we fix
M
(in terms of the parameters
m,p,\mathfrak{b}_{1},\mathfrak{b}_{2},\mathfrak{b}_{3})
and use interpolation inequalities to absorb the remainder term
\left\|(u,v)\right\|_{L^{1}}^{M+1}
by the drift
\Delta(u,v)
and obtain

\left\|(u,v)\right\|_{L^{1}}^{M+1}\lesssim\left\|(u,v)\right\|_{W^{m,p}}^{\alpha}\Delta(u,v),

(1.20)

for some
\alpha>0
. Now, the formula (
1.18
) becomes

\begin{gathered}\mathbb{P}x(t;(u,v))=\Delta(u,v)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in\mathcal{B}_{\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket},\\
b\notin\mathcal{N}\cup\{\mathfrak{b_{1}},\mathfrak{b}_{2},\mathfrak{b}_{3}\}\end{subarray}}\xi_{b}(t,(u,v))\mathbb{P}\left(f_{b}(0)\right)\\
+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in\mathcal{B}_{\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket},\\
b\notin\mathcal{N}\end{subarray}}\left(\eta_{b}-\xi_{b}\right)(t,(u,v))\mathbb{P}\left(f_{b}(0)\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\left\|(u,v)\right\|_{W^{m,p}}^{\alpha}\Delta(u,v)\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\left|x(t;(u,v))\right|^{1+\frac{1}{M}}\right).\end{gathered}

(1.21)

Intuitively, as
(t,\left\|(u,v)\right\|_{W^{m,p}})\to 0
, in the right-hand side of (
1.21
),

1.

the second term is bounded by

\varepsilon\Delta(u,v)
, thanks to the choice of
\mathcal{N}
,

2.

the third term is small, as
\eta_{b}\approx\xi_{b}
,

3.

the fourth term is bounded by

\varepsilon\Delta(u,v)
, thanks to the asymptotic
\left\|(u,v)\right\|_{W^{m,p}}\to 0
,

4.

finally, the last term is part of the definition of a drift – see Definition
1.7
.

Here, we pretend that a series of "small" terms keeps the same asymptotic, this fact is true in a precise framework defined in Section
2.6
. The brackets in competition in our work are
\mathfrak{b}_{1}:=W_{k}^{1}
,
\mathfrak{b}_{2}:=W_{k}^{2}
and
\mathfrak{b}_{3}:=C_{2k-1}
, for

.

In the asymmetrical case, we use the same strategy as the one described above with a different truncation in the Magnus-type representation formula of the state – see Proposition
4.7
.

\displaystyle=\binom{l+j+2}{l}\binom{j+2}{\lfloor\frac{j}{2}\rfloor+1-k}\binom{\lfloor\frac{j+1}{2}\rfloor+k+1}{\lfloor\frac{j+1}{2}\rfloor-k^{\prime}}\frac{(k+k^{\prime}+1)!}{(l+j+2)!}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{3|C_{j,l}|}(k+k^{\prime}+1)!}{(l+j+2)!},

where
|C_{j,l}|=j+l+2
.

Now, we assume that
\lfloor\frac{j}{2}\rfloor\leq k-2
. Let
N:=k-2-\lfloor\frac{j}{2}\rfloor\geq 0
. Note that
N\leq\lfloor\frac{j+1}{2}\rfloor-1
. We use Lemma
2.9
to obtain
\xi_{C_{j,l}}(t,(u,v)=A+B
with
A
the boundary terms and
B
the integral part. Using Leibniz formula, we get

\left(v_{\lfloor\frac{j+1}{2}\rfloor}\frac{(t-\cdot)^{l}}{l!}\right)^{(\mu)}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\text{if }\mu<l\\
\displaystyle\binom{\mu}{l}(-1)^{l}v_{\lfloor\frac{j+1}{2}\rfloor+l-\mu}(t)&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right..

Consequently, the following inequality holds

|A|\leq\mathbb{1}_{l\leq N}\sum_{\mu=l}^{N}\binom{\mu}{l}\left|u_{\lfloor\frac{j}{2}\rfloor+\mu+2}(t)v_{\lfloor\frac{j+1}{2}\rfloor+l-\mu}(t)\right|.

Then, using Young’s and Cauchy–Schwarz’s inequality,

|A|\leq\mathbb{1}_{l\leq N}2^{N-1}\left(\sum_{\mu=1}^{k}\left|u_{\mu}(t)\right|^{2}+t\underset{\mu\in\llbracket l,N\rrbracket}{\max}\left\|v_{\lfloor\frac{j+1}{2}\rfloor+l-\mu-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right),

as
1\leq\lfloor\frac{j}{2}\rfloor+\mu+2\leq k
, for
\mu\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket
. Note that
\lfloor\frac{j+1}{2}\rfloor+l-\mu-1\geq k^{\prime}
. Finally, applying Lemma
2.10
with
p=2
and
j_{0}=k^{\prime}
and using
t\in(0,1)
, we obtain the following estimate

|A|\leq\mathbb{1}_{l\leq N}2^{k-3}\left(\sum_{\mu=1}^{k}\left|u_{\mu}(t)\right|^{2}+t\left\|v_{k^{\prime}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).

(2.18)

We finally estimate
B
. Using Leibniz formula and Hölder’s inequality, we get

|B|\leq\sum_{\mu=0}^{\min(N+1,l)}\binom{N+1}{\mu}\frac{t^{l-\mu}}{(l-\mu)!}t^{1-\left(\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\right)}\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{L^{p}}\left\|v_{j+\mu+1-k}\right\|_{L^{q}}.

Using Lemma
2.10
with
p=q
and
j_{0}=k^{\prime}
, we have

|B|\leq\frac{(2^{k}t)^{|C_{j,l}|}}{l!}t^{-(1+k+k^{\prime})}t^{1-\left(\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\right)}\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{L^{p}}\left\|v_{k^{\prime}}\right\|_{L^{q}}.

(2.19)

Finally, for all
l\geq 0
, for all
j\in\llbracket 0,2k-3\rrbracket
,

\frac{1}{l!}=\frac{(j+2)!}{(j+l+2)!}\binom{j+l+2}{l}\leq\frac{(2k-1)!}{(j+l+2)!}2^{j+l+2}\leq\frac{(2(2k-1)!)^{|C_{j,l}|}}{(j+l+2)!}.

(2.20)

Thus, equations (
2.18
), (
2.19
) and (
2.20
) lead to the desired inequality.
∎

Using the algebraic properties proved in the previous section, we can now estimate the terms

Let

and

(3.4)

Proof.

3.4
, we can consider a linear form
\mathcal{P}
such that
\mathchoice{{\mathcal{P}\,\smash{\vrule height=5.46666pt,depth=3.02223pt}}_{\,{\mathcal{N}(f)(0)}}}{{\mathcal{P}\,\smash{\vrule height=5.46666pt,depth=3.02223pt}}_{\,{\mathcal{N}(f)(0)}}}{{\mathcal{P}\,\smash{\vrule height=3.82668pt,depth=2.20056pt}}_{\,{\mathcal{N}(f)(0)}}}{{\mathcal{P}\,\smash{\vrule height=2.71904pt,depth=2.78612pt}}_{\,{\mathcal{N}(f)(0)}}}\equiv 0

2.20
with

,

and

3.4
Interpolation inequality

The representation formula of the state – see Proposition
2.14
with
M=\pi(k,m)
– makes a strong link between
x(t;(u,v))
and
\mathcal{Z}_{\pi(k,m)}(t;f,(u,v))(0)
. Lemma
3.1
gives an expansion of
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{Z}_{\pi(k,m)}(t;f,(u,v))(0)
. Furthermore, the edge terms
|(u_{1},\cdots,u_{k},v_{1},\cdots,v_{k})(t)|
are estimated by Lemma
3.5
. However, there is an error term in the Magnus-type formula shaped as
\mathcal{O}\left(\left\|(u,v)\right\|_{L^{1}}^{\pi(k,m)+1}\right)
. We then relate this quantity to the size of the drift
\left\|(u_{k},v_{k})\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
, thanks to the Gagliardo–Nirenberg interpolation inequalities. This is the purpose of the following lemma.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
k,m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
p\in[1,+\infty]
. There exists
C>0
such that, for every
t>0
and
u\in W^{m,p}((0,t),\mathbb{R})
,

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{1}}^{\pi(k,m)+1}\leq Ct^{\pi(k,m)-2k}\left\|u\right\|_{W^{m,p}}^{\pi(k,m)-1}\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(3.5)

Proof.

For simplicity, we write
\pi
instead of
\pi(k,m)
.
We use the Gagliardo–Nirenberg interpolation inequalities (
2.26
) with
P=\frac{2(m+k)p}{2k+mp}
,
q=2
,
r=p
,
s=2
,
j=k
,
l=m+k
,
\alpha=\frac{k}{k+m}
and
\varphi=u_{k}
and we obtain

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{P}}\leq C\left\|u^{(m)}\right\|^{\alpha}_{L^{p}}\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\alpha}+Ct^{\frac{1}{P}-(k+\frac{1}{2})}\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.6)

Moreover, using Hölder’s inequality,

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{1}}\leq t^{1-\frac{1}{P}}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{P}}.

(3.7)

Using (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), we obtain

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{1}}^{\pi+1}\leq Ct^{(\pi+1)\left(1-\frac{1}{P}\right)}\left(\left\|u^{(m)}\right\|^{\alpha(\pi+1)}_{L^{p}}\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{(1-\alpha)(\pi+1)}+t^{(\pi+1)\left(\frac{1}{P}-(k+\frac{1}{2})\right)}\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\pi+1}\right)

Thus, if we define
\beta:=1+\frac{2k}{m}
, then,
(1-\alpha)(1+\beta)=2
. We get

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{1}}^{\pi+1}\leq Ct^{(\pi+1)\left(1-\frac{1}{P}\right)}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{W^{m,p}}^{\alpha(\pi+1)}\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{(1-\alpha)\left(\pi-\beta\right)}+t^{(\pi+1)\left(\frac{1}{P}-(k+\frac{1}{2})\right)}\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\pi-1}\right)\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(3.8)

Moreover,

\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq t^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq Ct^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\left\|u\right\|_{W^{m,p}}.

(3.9)

Using (
3.9
) in (
3.8
), we obtain

\begin{gathered}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{1}}^{\pi+1}\leq Ct^{(\pi+1)\left(1-\frac{1}{P}\right)}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{W^{m,p}}^{\alpha(\pi+1)+(1-\alpha)\left(\pi-\beta\right)}t^{(k+\frac{1}{2})(1-\alpha)\left(\pi-\beta\right)}\right.\\
\left.+t^{(\pi+1)\left(\frac{1}{P}-(k+\frac{1}{2})\right)+(\pi-1)\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\left\|u\right\|_{W^{m,p}}^{\pi-1}\right)\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{gathered}

As
\alpha(\pi+1)+(1-\alpha)\left(\pi-\beta\right)=\pi-1
, we obtain

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{1}}^{\pi+1}\leq Ct^{(\pi+1)\left(1-\frac{1}{P}\right)}\left(t^{(k+\frac{1}{2})(1-\alpha)\left(\pi+1\right)-(2k+1)}+t^{\frac{\pi+1}{P}-(2k+1)}\right)\left\|u\right\|_{W^{m,p}}^{\pi-1}\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Finally,

\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)(1-\alpha)=\frac{(2k+1)m}{2(k+m)}\geq\frac{1}{P},

Thus,

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{1}}^{\pi+1}\leq Ct^{(\pi+1)\left(1-\frac{1}{P}\right)}t^{\frac{\pi+1}{P}-(2k+1)}\left\|u\right\|_{W^{m,p}}^{\pi-1}\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

∎

.

(4.4)

Proof.

A.5
Relation between Sussmann’s
\mathcal{S}(\theta)
-condition and Theorem
1.22

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
m=1
. We assume that

f_{W_{k}^{1}}(0),\quad f_{W_{k}^{2}}(0),\quad f_{C_{2k-1}}(0),\quad\mathcal{N}_{k}^{1}(f)(0)\quad\text{satisfy (BC)}.

Let us show that, for every
\theta\in[0,1]
, (
1.26
) is not verified for
\mathfrak{b}:=W_{k}^{1}
. We assume the opposite: there exists
\theta\in[0,1]
such that (
1.26
) holds for
\mathfrak{b}=W_{k}^{1}
. Then,
n_{0}(\mathfrak{b})=2k-1
is odd,
n_{1}(\mathfrak{b})=2
,
n_{2}(\mathfrak{b})=0
are even and

f_{\sigma(\mathfrak{b})}(0)=f_{W_{k}^{1}}(0)+f_{W_{k}^{2}}(0).

Let
b\in\Br(X)
be such
n(b)+\theta n_{0}(b)<n(\mathfrak{b})+\theta n_{0}(\mathfrak{b})=2+(2k-1)\theta
. Then,

n(b)<2+(2k-1)\theta\leq 2k+1.

Moreover, if
n(b)=2
, then

2+\theta n_{0}(b)<2+\theta(2k-1)\qquad\text{ so }\qquad n_{0}(b)<2k-1.

Let
\mathcal{E}:={S}_{\llbracket 1,\pi(k,1)\rrbracket\setminus\{2\}}(X)\cup\{b\in\Br(X);\ n(b)=2,\ n_{0}(b)<2k-1\}
.
As
\pi(k,1)=2k+1
, the previous inequalities lead to

f_{\sigma(\mathfrak{b})}(0)\in\mathcal{E}(f)(0)
. As
\mathcal{E}(f)(0)\subset\mathcal{N}_{k}^{1}(f)(0)
, we obtain

\mathbb{P}\left(f_{W_{k}^{1}}(0)\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(f_{W_{k}^{2}}(0)\right)=0,

where
\mathbb{P}
is a linear form given by (BC). Thus,

0\leq\mathbb{P}\left(f_{C_{2k-1}}(0)\right)^{2}<\mathbb{P}\left(f_{W_{k}^{1}}(0)\right)\mathbb{P}\left(f_{W_{k}^{2}}(0)\right)=-\mathbb{P}\left(f_{W_{k}^{1}}(0)\right)^{2}\leq 0.

This is a contradiction. Consequently, (
1.26
) is not verified for
\mathfrak{b}=W_{k}^{1}
.

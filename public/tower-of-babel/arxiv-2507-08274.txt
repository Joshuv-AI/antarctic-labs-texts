Global small data weak solutions of 2-D semilinear wave equations with scale-invariant damping, III
Li Qianqian, Yin Huicheng School of Mathematical Sciences and Mathematical Institute,Nanjing Normal University, Nanjing 210023, China.

Abstract
For the 2 2 -D semilinear wave equation with scale-invariant damping □ ​ u + μ t ​ ∂ t u = | u | p \square u+\frac{\mu}{t}\partial_{t}u=|u|^{p} , where t ≥ 1 t\geq 1 , μ > 0 \mu>0 and p > 1 p>1 , it is conjectured that the global small data weak solution u u exists when p > p s ​ ( 2 + μ ) = μ + 3 + μ 2 + 14 ​ μ + 17 2 ​ ( μ + 1 ) p>p_{s}(2+\mu)=\frac{\mu+3+\sqrt{\mu^{2}+14\mu+17}}{2(\mu+1)} for 0 < μ ≤ 2 0<\mu\leq 2 and p > p f ​ ( 2 ) = 2 p>p_{f}(2)=2 for μ ≥ 2 \mu\geq 2 . In our previous papers, the global small solution u u has been obtained for p > p s ​ ( 2 + μ ) p>p_{s}(2+\mu) and 0 < μ < 2 0<\mu<2 but μ ≠ 1 \mu\not=1 . In the present paper, by the vector field method together with the delicate analysis on the Bessel functions, we will show the global existence of small solution u u for p > 2 p>2 and μ > 2 \mu>2 . \square\phi+\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{n}g_{ij}^{k}\partial_{ij}^{2}\phi\partial_{k}\phi=0

\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{n}g_{ij}^{k}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\xi_{k}

\equiv 0

(\xi_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\xi_{n})\in S^{n}

Define the vector field
Z\in\{\partial,L_{0},L_{j},\Omega_{ij}:1\leq i<j\leq n\}

where
\partial=(\partial_{0},\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n})

=(\partial_{t},\partial_{x_{1}},\ldots,\partial_{x_{n}})
,
L_{0}=t\partial_{t}+x_{1}\partial_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}\partial_{n}
,

L_{j}=t\partial_{j}+x_{j}\partial_{t}
and
\Omega_{ij}=x_{i}\partial_{j}-x_{j}\partial_{i}

Then one easily has the following commutator properties

[\square,\partial]=0,\quad[\square,L_{j}]=0,\quad[\square,\Omega_{ij}]=0,\quad[\square,L_{0}]=2\square.

Based on this, it holds that for

By the crucial Klainerman-Sobolev inequality

where

\square w_{2}+\frac{\mu}{t}\,\partial_{t}w_{2}-\frac{\mu}{t}\partial_{1}u-\frac{\mu x_{1}}{t^{2}}w_{1}=L_{1}(|u|^{p}).

(1.6)

\bullet
By the Bessel function and Fourier analysis tools, through the explicit expression of

For any

4
Estimates of solution to 2-D inhomogeneous equation
\square w+\frac{\mu}{t}\,\partial_{t}w=F

In this section, we will derive some time-decay estimates of solution to the 2-D linear problem

\left\{\kern 5.0pt\begin{aligned} &\square w+\frac{\mu}{t}\,\partial_{t}w=F,&&t\geq 1,\\
&w(1,x)=0,\quad\partial_{t}w(1,x)=0,&&x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\end{aligned}\right.

(4.1)

where
2<\mu<3
. To this end, it follows from the Duhamel’s principle that we first treat such a
homogeneous initial data problem from the initial variable time
\tau\geq 1

\left\{\kern 5.0pt\begin{aligned} &\square v+\frac{\mu}{t}\,\partial_{t}v=0,&&t\geq\tau\geq 1,\\
&v(\tau,x)=0,\quad\partial_{t}v(\tau,x)=v_{1}(x),&&x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\end{aligned}\right.

(4.2)

where
v_{1}(x)\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
v
solve (
4.2
). Then for any
\varepsilon_{1}\in(0,1)

\delta(\varepsilon_{1})=\frac{2\varepsilon_{1}}{1+\varepsilon_{1}}>0
such that

\|v\|_{Z,1,2}\lesssim t^{\delta(\varepsilon_{1})-1}\tau\left\|v_{1}\right\|_{Z,1,(1+\varepsilon_{1},2)}

(4.3)

and

\|\partial v\|_{Z,1,2}\lesssim t^{-1}\tau\left\|v_{1}\right\|_{Z,1,2}.

(4.4)

Proof.

Although the proof procedure of Lemma
4.1
is similar to that for Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
,
we still provide some details of proof for (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) due to the appearance of the parameter

.

We now establish

\|v\|_{Z,1,2}\lesssim t^{\delta(\varepsilon_{1})-1}\tau\left\|v_{1}\right\|_{Z,1,(1+\varepsilon_{1},2)}.

Note that

\|v\|_{Z,1,2}\leq\|\hat{v}\|_{L^{2}}+\sum_{j=1}^{2}\||\xi_{j}|\hat{v}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{t}\hat{v}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\widehat{L_{0}v}\|_{L^{2}}+\sum_{j=1}^{2}\|\widehat{L_{j}v}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\widehat{\Omega_{12}v}\|_{L^{2}}.

(4.5)

Next we deal with each term in (
4.5
).

It follows from (
2.6
) and (
2.22
) that for
t|\xi|\geq\tau|\xi|\geq 1
,

\left|\Psi_{1}(t,\tau,\xi)\right|\lesssim t^{\rho}\tau^{1-\rho}(\tau|\xi|)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(t|\xi|)^{-\frac{1}{2}}=t^{-\frac{\mu}{2}}\tau^{\frac{\mu}{2}}|\xi|^{-1}.

(4.6)

In addition, for
t|\xi|\geq 1
and
\tau|\xi|\leq 1
, by (
2.8
) and (
2.24
)-(
2.25
), one has

\left|\Psi_{1}(t,\tau,\xi)\right|\lesssim t^{\rho}\tau^{1-\rho}(\tau|\xi|)^{\rho}(t|\xi|)^{-\frac{1}{2}}=t^{-\frac{\mu}{2}}|\xi|^{-\frac{\mu}{2}}\tau.

(4.7)

For
\tau|\xi|\leq t|\xi|\leq 1
, by (
2.8
) and (
2.25
), we have

\displaystyle\left|\Psi_{1}(t,\tau,\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{\rho}\tau^{1-\rho}\left[(\tau|\xi|)^{-\rho}(t|\xi|)^{\rho}+(\tau|\xi|)^{\rho}(t|\xi|)^{-\rho}\right]

(4.8)

\displaystyle=t^{2\rho}\tau^{1-2\rho}+\tau\lesssim\tau.

Thus it follows from (

)-(
4.8
) and (
2.3
) with
\hat{v}_{0}=0
that for

,

\displaystyle\|\hat{v}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|t^{-\frac{\mu}{2}}\tau^{\frac{\mu}{2}}|\xi|^{-1}\hat{v}_{1}(\xi)\big\|_{L^{2}(\tau|\xi|\geqslant 1)}+\big\|t^{-\frac{\mu}{2}}|\xi|^{-\frac{\mu}{2}}\tau\hat{v}_{1}(\xi)\big\|_{L^{2}(\tau|\xi|\leqslant 1,t|\xi|\geqslant 1)}+\left\|\tau\hat{v}_{1}(\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(t|\xi|\leqslant 1)}

(4.9)

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{\mu}{2}}\tau^{\frac{\mu}{2}}\cdot\tau\left\|(1+\tau|\xi|)^{-1}\hat{v}_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\tau\left\|(1+t|\xi|)^{-1}\hat{v}_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\tau\left\|(1+\tau|\xi|)(1+\tau|\xi|)^{-1}\hat{v}_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{\delta(\varepsilon_{1})-1}\tau\left\|v_{1}\right\|_{\left(1+\varepsilon_{1},2\right)}

\displaystyle\|t|\xi_{j}|\hat{v}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|t^{-\frac{\mu}{2}}\tau^{\frac{\mu}{2}}|\xi|^{-1}t|\xi_{j}|\hat{v}_{1}\big\|_{L^{2}(\tau|\xi|\geqslant 1)}+\big\|t^{-\frac{\mu}{2}}|\xi|^{-\frac{\mu}{2}}\tau t|\xi_{j}|\hat{v}_{1}\big\|_{L^{2}(\tau|\xi|\leqslant 1,t|\xi|\geqslant 1)}+\left\|\tau t|\xi_{j}|\hat{v}_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(t|\xi|\leqslant 1)}

) and (
4.4
), we have

\displaystyle\|\partial\mathcal{N}u(t,\cdot)\|_{Z,1,2}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{\mu}{2}}\varepsilon\left\|\nabla u_{0}\right\|_{Z,1,2}+t^{-\frac{\mu}{2}}\varepsilon\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{Z,1,2}+t^{-1}\varepsilon\left\|u_{0}+u_{1}\right\|_{Z,1,2}

(5.15)

\displaystyle+t^{-1}\int_{1}^{t}\tau\left\||u(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}\right\|_{Z,1,2}\mathrm{d}\tau.

As treated in (
5.8
), one has

\left\||u(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}\right\|_{Z,1,2}\lesssim\left\||u(\tau,\cdot)|^{p-1}\right\|_{2+\varepsilon_{2}}\|u(\tau,\cdot)\|_{Z,1,\bar{q}},

(5.16)

where
\bar{q}=\bar{q}(\varepsilon_{2})\in(2,\infty)
fulfills

\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{2+\varepsilon_{2}}+\frac{1}{\bar{q}}.

(5.17)

In addition, it follows from Sobolev embedding theorem that

\|u(\tau,\cdot)\|_{Z,1,\bar{q}}\lesssim\|u(\tau,\cdot)\|_{Z,1,2}+\|\partial u(\tau,\cdot)\|_{Z,1,2}.

(5.18)

Moreover, according to
p>2
, one has

\displaystyle\left\||u(\tau,\cdot)|^{p-1}\right\|_{2+\varepsilon_{2}}\leq\|u(\tau,\cdot)\|_{\left(2+\varepsilon_{2}\right)(p-1)}^{p-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau^{-\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\left(2+\varepsilon_{2}\right)(p-1)}\right)(p-1)}\|u(\tau,\cdot)\|_{Z,1,2}^{p-1}

\displaystyle\leq\|u(\tau,\cdot)\|_{Z,1,2}^{p-1}.

This, together with (
5.18
) and (
5.16
), yields

\displaystyle\|\partial\mathcal{N}u(t,\cdot)\|_{Z,1,2}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{\mu}{2}}\varepsilon\left\|\nabla u_{0}\right\|_{Z,1,2}+t^{-\frac{\mu}{2}}\varepsilon\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{Z,1,2}+t^{-1}\varepsilon\left\|u_{0}+u_{1}\right\|_{Z,1,2}

\displaystyle+t^{-1}\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p}\int_{1}^{t}\tau^{1+p(\delta-1)}\mathrm{d}\tau.

For
p>2
, we choose a fixed small constant
\varepsilon_{1}>0
such that
1+p(\delta(\varepsilon_{1})-1)<-1
holds.
Thus,

\displaystyle t\|\partial\mathcal{N}u(t,\cdot)\|_{Z,1,2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon\left\|\nabla u_{0}\right\|_{Z,1,2}+\varepsilon\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{Z,1,2}+\varepsilon\left\|u_{0}+u_{1}\right\|_{Z,1,2}+\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p}

(5.19)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}}+\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p}.

Combining (
5.14
) and (
5.19
) derives (
5.3
).

We next prove the contractive estimate (
5.4
). As treated in (
5.6
), we have that for
u,v\in X(T)
and
p>2
,

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{N}u-\mathcal{N}v)(t,\cdot)\|_{Z,1,2}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{\delta-1}\int_{1}^{t}\tau\left\||u-v|(\tau,\cdot)(|u|+|v|)^{p-1}(\tau,\cdot)\right\|_{Z,1,(1+\varepsilon_{1},2)}\mathrm{d}\tau

(5.20)

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{\delta-1}\int_{1}^{t}\tau\left\|(|u|+|v|)^{p-1}(\tau,\cdot)\right\|_{(\tilde{q},\infty)}\||u-v|(\tau,\cdot)\|_{Z,1,2}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{\delta-1}\|u-v\|_{X(T)}\big(\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}\big)\int_{1}^{t}\tau^{1+\frac{p-1-\kappa}{2}+p(\delta-1)}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{\delta-1}\|u-v\|_{X(T)}\big(\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}\big),

where
\tilde{q}
is defined by (
5.9
). On the other hand, employing the same approach for deriving (
5.19
), one can obtain that for
u,v\in X(T)
and
p>2
,

\displaystyle\|\partial(\mathcal{N}u-\mathcal{N}v)(t,\cdot)\|_{Z,1,2}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-1}\int_{1}^{t}\tau\left\||u-v|(\tau,\cdot)(|u|+|v|)^{p-1}(\tau,\cdot)\right\|_{Z,1,2}\mathrm{d}\tau

(5.21)

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-1}\int_{1}^{t}\tau\left\|(|u|+|v|)^{p-1}(\tau,\cdot)\right\|_{{}_{2+\varepsilon_{1}}}\||u-v|(\tau,\cdot)\|_{Z,1,\bar{q}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-1}\|u-v\|_{X(T)}\big(\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}\big)\int_{1}^{t}\tau^{1+p(\delta-1)}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-1}\|u-v\|_{X(T)}\big(\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}\big),

where
\bar{q}
is defined by (
5.17
). Therefore, (
5.4
) is shown and then Proposition
5.1

is established by collecting (
5.1
), (
5.20
)-(
5.21
) and (
5.3
).

∎

Based on Proposition
5.1
, we start to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Choosing
M=3C\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}}
in
X(T,M)
, where
C
is the positive constant mentioned in
Proposition
5.1
. Then for any
u\in X(T,M)
, one can obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}u\|_{X(T)}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}}+C\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p}

(5.22)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}}+C\left(3C\varepsilon\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left(C+3^{p}C^{p+1}\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}}^{p-1}\varepsilon^{p-1}\right)\varepsilon\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle\leq 3C\varepsilon\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}},

where
\varepsilon\leq\big(\frac{2}{3^{p}C^{p}}\big)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\frac{1}{1+\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}}}
. Similarly,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}u-\mathcal{N}v\|_{X(T)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u-v\|_{X(T)}\left(\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}\right)

(5.23)

\displaystyle\leq C\|u-v\|_{X(T)}\cdot 2\left(3C\varepsilon\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}}\right)^{p-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u-v\|_{X(T)},

provided that
\varepsilon\leq\frac{{(4C)}^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}}{3C(1+\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}})}
. Therefore,
by setting
\varepsilon_{0}:=\min\{\big(\frac{2}{3^{p}C^{p}}\big)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\frac{1}{1+\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}}},

\frac{{(4C)}^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}}{3C(1+\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}})}\}
and
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
,
one can conclude that
\mathcal{N}
is a contractive mapping from
X(T,M)
into
X(T,M)
. Therefore, there exists a unique solution
u\in X(T,M)
such that

\mathcal{N}u=u
. This yields that
u
is a global solution of (
1.1
) since the related constant
C>0
is independent
of
T
. Then the proof of Theorem
1.1
is completed.

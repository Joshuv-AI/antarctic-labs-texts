Editorial JVE 9.4
Published: 2023

It is with great pleasure that the editors of this Journal introduce the papers in this issue. We have several articles relating to hepatitis B (HBV) at the time of renewed efforts to reach the WHO's global hepatitis strategy.

The article” Hepatitis B prevalence and associated factors in adults presenting for infections screening in northern Thailand” by N. Salvador and colleagues from France and Thailand describes the need to go beyond the risk factor-based screening in adults and to test all adults in order not to miss chronic HBV infections in Thailand, this until the second generation of immunised infants have reached adult age.

“Where do those data go? Reuse of screening results from clinical trials to estimate population prevalence of HBV infection in adults in Kilifi, Kenya” by L. Down and colleagues from the United Kingdom and Kenya, argues for the need to collate clinical trial data which test for HbsAg which would help provide useful information, particularly in regions such as in Africa together with alanine amino transferase, hepatitis C and HIV, thereby enabling modelling HBsAg prevalence by age, sex and geography to estimate the burden of liver disease and other blood born infections.

“The importance of triple panel testing for hepatitis B and the burden of isolated anti-HBV core antibodies within a community sample” by C. Freeland and colleagues from the USA describes a high incidence of isolated HBV core antibodies within a community high-risk screening in Greater Philadelphia, highlighting the need to incorporate a triple panel screening in all HBV screening programs as per the current Centers for Disease Control and Prevention universal screening recommendations.

“Patient acceptability of, and attitudes towards hepatitis B cure research – A scoping review and identification of knowledge gaps” by N. Mohtashem from the USA highlights the lack of data in the field of HBV functional cure, in terms of patient acceptability and perspectives using a person-centered approach and suggest a research framework for this important field.

“Antiviral activity of nitazoxanide against Morbillivirus infections” by D. Stelitano and colleagues from Italy and the USA describes the antiviral properties of this agent and its metabolite by blocking measles and canine distemper virus replication.

The article entitled “Population measles seroprevalence: heterogeneity by birth-year cohort” by E. Santacruz-Samartin from Columbia using a cohort from Medellin shows a lower antibody concentration in the younger cohorts which may need periodic monitoring of its seroprevalence as at higher risk of infection.

The article “The build-up of stock of stable integrated proviruses overtime explains the difficulty in reducing HIV-1 DNA levels when treatment is initiated at the chronic stage” by G. Mchantaf and colleagues from France describes a comparison of very early treated individuals versus chronically-treated ones with potent antiretroviral therapy, showing a significantly lower integrated DNA in PBMCs in the former group of individuals.

M.J. Lee and colleagues from the United Kingdom report in their article entitled “Understanding participant perspectives around HIV cure-related studies involving antiretroviral treatment interruption” the data from a survey of 75 individuals with primary HIV infection showing that nearly half of the respondents expressed interest in joining studies involving treatment interruption, primarily for altruistic reasons, but also voiced concern about onward transmission.

- Finally, two of the editors of this Journal present the highlights of the HIV cure session from the European AIDS Clinical Society meeting which took place last October In Warsaw, Poland.

We cannot be grateful enough to all the authors and reviewers. who have taken their time and put so many efforts into making this Journal a success.

We wish you all a happy and restful festive season.

Dr S. Kinloch de Loes, Editor-in-Chief

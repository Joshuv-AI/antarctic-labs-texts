Highest weight categories via pairs of dual exceptional sequences
Agnieszka Bodzenta, Alexey Bondal

Abstract
. In this paper we present criteria in terms of dual pairs of exceptional sequences for an abelian category to be highest weight. The criteria are applied in three situations of geometric origin. We give new proofs for the facts that the category of perverse sheaves of middle perversity on complex-analytic manifolds with suitable conditions on the stratification is highest weight and that the derived coherent category of any Grassmannian has a t-structure with highest weight heart. Also we show that the abelian null category of any proper birational morphism of regular surfaces is highest weight. For this null category, we give a geometric description of some special objects related to the highest weight structure, such as standard, costandard and characteristic tilting objects.

1.
Introduction

Highest weight abelian categories, whose axiomatics was introduced by Cline, Parshall and Scott in
[
CPS88
]
are omnipresent in the Representation Theory (
cf.

[
PS88
]
,
[
DR92
]
,
[
Don98
]
,
[
Rou08
]
,
[
BS11
]
, to mention few). In order to define a highest weight structure on an abelian category one needs to fix a (partial) order on the set
\Lambda
of isomorphism classes of its irreducible objects. The category is highest weight if it possesses a set of so-called standard objects (defined with respect to the order on
\Lambda
) which satisfy some good conditions (see Section
2.1
below).

Recently, several authors have given various new characterizations of highest weight categories.
In
[
CW24
]
, Cipriani and Woolf obtained a criterion for an abelian category to be highest weight in terms of existence of a suitable stability condition on the category and a purely numerical criterion in terms of the decomposition at the level of the Grothendieck group of projective objects via standard objects.

In
[
ACKT25
]
, Adachi, Chan, Kimura and Tsukamoto describe highest weight structures on the category of modules over a given algebra in terms of the properties of characteristic tilting module, the central object of Ringel duality on higest weight categories. The authors of
[
ACKT25
]
put a consistency condition between the tilting module and simple modules over the algebra which imply that the module is characteristic tilting for the chosen order on
\Lambda
.

In
[
BB24
]
, the authors of the present paper characterized highest weight categories as the left/right abelian envelopes of thin exact categories. Thin exact categories are exact categories together with a
thin filtration
,
i.e.
an admissible filtration (see
[
BB24
]
) whose graded factors are categories of vector spaces.
From this perspective, thin exact categories make sense as the basic object of study in the theory of highest weight categories. The thin exact category inside the highest weight category is the full subcategory of standardly (or costandardly) filtered objects, which was originally considered by Claus Ringel in
[
Rin91
]
.

One can regard thin filtration as an exact category counterpart of the notion of exceptional sequence in triangulated category
[
Bon89
]
. Indeed, there is a clear direct connection between the two notions: the irreducible objects (with the prescribed order) in a thin category make an exceptional sequence in its derived category, that of standard objects.

Note that the derived categories of a thin category and of the highest weight category which is its right (or left) abelian envelope, are equivalent. Thus we may consider the highest weight category as intermediate between the thin subcategory and the ambient derived category.
It is natural to ask how to characterize highest weight categories in terms of the exceptional sequence in its derived category. The subject of this paper is to give such characterizations and to apply them to deduce that certain categories of geometric origin are highest weight.

In the spirit of this question, Q. Liu and D. Yang asked in
[
LY16
]
whether an abelian category
{\mathcal{A}}
is highest weight if and only if
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})
has a full exceptional sequence of objects in
{\mathcal{A}}
. In
[
Kal17
, Proposition 5.6]
M. Kalck constructed a counterexample. We discuss it in more detail in Section
4.1
.

Recall that there is another exceptional sequence, that of costandard objects, in any highest weight category
{\mathcal{A}}
. In
[
BB24
]
, this sequence got the interpretation of irreducible objects in another thin exact subcategory of
{\mathcal{A}}
, whose
left
abelian envelope is equal to
{\mathcal{A}}
. It is well-known that, under the chosen order on
\Lambda
, this is also an exceptional sequence in
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})
, actually the left dual (see
[
Bon89
]
and Section
2.3
) to the sequence of standard objects (
cf.

[
Rou08
, Proposition 4.19]
).

In this paper we give various characterization of highest weight structures on abelian categories in terms of the both exceptional sequences. It would be interesting to check how these results extend to (semi)infinite highest weight categories
[
BS24
]
.

In what concerns the recovering of the highest weight category
{\mathcal{A}}
from its derived category, it is essentially due to
[
CPS88
]
that
{\mathcal{A}}
is the heart of the
t
-struc
ture glued along the filtration induced by the exceptional sequence of co/standard objects in the derived category (see also Corollary
3.6
).

Our first criterion is for the heart of the
t
-struc
ture glued along an exceptional sequence to be a highest weight category. Remarkably, the criterion provides the necessary and sufficient conditions purely in terms of vanishing of negative
Ext
-groups between the objects in the exceptional sequences.

.

(Theorem

Let

be the heart of the

-struc

In Section

2.
Preliminaries

We recall the definition of highest weight categories and their relation to standarizable sequences in abelian categories. We further discuss dual pairs of exceptional sequences and the aisles of the
t
-struc
ture glued along an exceptional sequence.

2.1.
Highest weight categories

We say that a
\mathbbm{k}
-linear abelian category is
of finite length
if it is
\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits
and
\mathop{\operatorname{Ext}}\nolimits^{1}
-finite, contains finitely many non-isomorphic irreducible objects, and any object admits a finite filtration with irreducible graded components. We say that an abelian category of finite length is a
Deligne finite category
if it has a projective generator. Deligne finite categories are exactly the categories
\mathop{\textrm{mod--}}A
of finite dimensional right modules over finite dimensional algebras. Given a projective generator
P\in{\mathcal{A}}
, one can take
A=\mathop{\textrm{End}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}}(P)
, then the equivalence
{\mathcal{A}}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathop{\textrm{mod--}}A
is given by the functor
\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}}(P,-)
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a Deligne finite
\mathbbm{k}
-linear category, and
\Lambda
a partial order on the set of isomorphism classes of irreducible objects in
{\mathcal{A}}
. Let
L(\lambda)
denote the irreducible object corresponding to
\lambda\in\Lambda
,
P(\lambda)
its projective cover, and
I(\lambda)
its injective hull. We assume that the order is
adapted
, i.e. for any
M\in{\mathcal{A}}
with the top
L(\lambda_{1})
, the socle
L(\lambda_{2})
, and
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
not comparable in
\Lambda
, there exists
\mu
such that
\mu\succeq\lambda_{1}
,
\mu\succeq\lambda_{2}
, and
L(\mu)
is a graded factor of a filtration on
M
. We say that
({\mathcal{A}},\Lambda)
is a
highest weight category

[
CPS88
]
if
{\mathcal{A}}
has a set, called the set of
standard objects
,
\Delta(\lambda)
, labelled by
\lambda\in\Lambda
, such that

(st1)

there exists an epimorphism
\Delta(\lambda)\to L(\lambda)

whose kernel admits a filtration with graded factors
L(\mu)
, where
\mu\prec\lambda
,

(st2)

there exists an epimorphism
P(\lambda)\to\Delta(\lambda)

whose kernel admits a filtration with graded factors
\Delta(\mu)
, where
\mu\succ\lambda
.

It follows from

[
PS88
, Theorem 4.3]
that
{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathop{\textrm{op}}\nolimits}\cong(\textrm{mod-}A)^{\mathop{\textrm{op}}\nolimits}\cong\textrm{mod-}A^{\mathop{\textrm{op}}\nolimits}
is also a highest weight category with the same partial order
\Lambda
on the set of simple objects
\{L(\lambda)^{\mathop{\textrm{op}}\nolimits}\}
. By transporting the standard objects from
{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathop{\textrm{op}}\nolimits}
to
{\mathcal{A}}
, we get
costandard objects

\nabla(\lambda)
, labelled by
\lambda\in\Lambda
, defined by conditions:

(cost1)

there exists an injective morphism
L(\lambda)\to\nabla(\lambda)
whose cokernel admits a filtration with graded factors
L(\mu)
, where
\mu\prec\lambda
,

(cost2)

there exists an injective morphism
\nabla(\lambda)\to I(\lambda)
whose cokernel admits a filtration with graded factors
\nabla(\mu)
, where
\mu\succ\lambda
.

A highest weight category
{\mathcal{A}}
is equivalent to the category
\mathop{\textrm{mod--}}A
where
A
is a
quasi-hereditary
algebra and
vice versa

[
CPS88
]
.

Consider an abelian category
{\mathcal{A}}
. We say that highest weight structures
({\mathcal{A}},\Lambda)
,
({\mathcal{A}},\Lambda^{\prime})
are
equivalent
if they have the same sets of isomorphism classes of standard objects
\{\Delta(\lambda)\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}=\{\Delta(\lambda^{\prime})\}_{\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{\prime}}
.

Similarly, two exceptional sequences in a triangulated category are
equivalent
if the sets of isomorphism classes of their objects are equal (but the full order of the sequence might change).

2.2.
Standarizable sequences

Given a set of objects

in an abelian category

isomorphic to one of the

’s.

Consider a sequence
\epsilon=(E_{1},\ldots,E_{n})
of objects in a
\mathbbm{k}
-linear abelian category
{\mathcal{A}}
such that
\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}\mathop{\operatorname{Ext}}\nolimits^{1}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(E_{i},E_{j})

Recall that given a pair

-linear abelian category

of

(1)

\displaystyle 0\to T\otimes\mathop{\operatorname{Ext}}\nolimits^{1}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(Q,T)^{\vee}\to R\xrightarrow{\pi}Q\to 0

given by the canonical element in
\mathop{\operatorname{Ext}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{C}}}^{1}(Q,T\otimes\mathop{\operatorname{Ext}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{C}}}^{1}(Q,T)^{\vee})\cong\mathop{\operatorname{Ext}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{C}}}^{1}(Q,T)\otimes\mathop{\operatorname{Ext}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{C}}}^{1}(Q,T)^{\vee}\simeq\mathop{\textrm{End}}\nolimits(\mathop{\operatorname{Ext}}\nolimits^{1}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(Q,T))
.

The notion of an iterated universal extension of

, i.e. when

2.3.
Pairs of dual exceptional sequences in highest weight categories

For an exceptional pair
(E,F)
in a triangulated category
{\mathcal{D}}
, the
left and right mutation
are defined via
canonical
exact triangles:

\displaystyle\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{D}}}^{\mbox{\boldmath{$\cdot$}}}(E,F)\otimes E\to F\to L_{E}F,

\displaystyle R_{F}E\to E\to F\otimes\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{D}}}^{\mbox{\boldmath{$\cdot$}}}(E,F)^{\vee}.

Note the shift with respect to the original definition in
[
Bon89
]
.

Mutations define a braid group action on the set of exceptional sequences in a triangulated category
{\mathcal{D}}

[
Bon89
]
. In particular, given an exceptional sequence
(E_{1},\ldots,E_{n})
, its
left dual
exceptional sequence can be canonically defined as the sequence
(F_{n},\ldots,F_{1})
with
F_{i}=L_{E_{1}}\ldots L_{E_{i-2}}L_{E_{i-1}}E_{i}
, for any
i\geq 2
, and
F_{1}=E_{1}
.

In the case the exceptional sequence is
full
,
i.e.
generates the triangulated category
{\mathcal{D}}
, the left dual exceptional sequence is uniquely characterised by the Hom-duality property

(2)

\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{D}}}(E_{i},F_{j}[l])=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\mathbbm{k},&\textrm{if }l=0,i=j,\\
0,&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

The following fact is well-known (
cf.

[
Rou08
, Proposition 4.19]
):

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\Lambda)
be a highest weight category. Then, for any choice of a full order
\lambda_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{n}
on
\Lambda
compatible with the poset structure, the
sequence
(\Delta(\lambda_{1}),\ldots,\Delta(\lambda_{n}))
is full exceptional in
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})
with the left dual sequence
(\nabla(\lambda_{n}),\ldots,\nabla(\lambda_{1}))
.

Remark 2.3
.

Consider a highest weight category
({\mathcal{A}},\Lambda)
and the full exceptional sequence
(\Delta(\lambda_{1}),\ldots,\Delta(\lambda_{n}))
as above.
Then, for the full order
\Lambda^{\prime}=\{\lambda_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{n}\}
on the set
of irreducible objects in
{\mathcal{A}}
, the highest weight structure
({\mathcal{A}},\Lambda^{\prime})
is equivalent to the original one
({\mathcal{A}},\Lambda)
, as it is for any refinement of the partial order on

3.
Highest weight categories via exceptional sequences

We obtain equivalent conditions for the heart of a
t
-struc

3.2.
Ringel duality in terms of dual pairs of exceptional sequences

Recall Ringel duality on highest weight categories
[
Rin91
]
. The Ringel dual
\mathbf{RD}({\mathcal{A}})
of a highest weight category
{\mathcal{A}}
is the abelian category of modules over the algebra of endomorphisms of the so-called
characteristic tilting module
. It is the additive generator of the category of objects which admit filtrations with both standard and costandard objects in
{\mathcal{A}}
. In
[
BB24
]
, we show that the characteristic tilting module is identified with an injective generator in the exact category
\mathcal{F}(\Delta)
of objects admitting a filtration with quotients by direct sums of standard objects.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a highest weight category. We assume it to be the heart of the
t
-struc
ture glued along a full exceptional sequence
\epsilon=(E_{1},\dots.E_{n})
in a triangulated category
{\mathcal{D}}
.
Then the Ringel dual
\mathbf{RD}({\mathcal{A}})
of
{\mathcal{A}}
is equivalent to the heart of the
t
-struc
ture glued along the left dual exceptional sequence
(F_{n},\ldots,F_{1})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{S}
be the Serre functor on
{\mathcal{D}}
, see
[
Bon89
]
,
[
BK89
]
. Then
\mathbb{S}(\epsilon)=(\mathbb{S}(E_{1}),\ldots,\mathbb{S}(E_{n}))
is left dual to
(F_{n},\ldots,F_{1})
. Since the heart
{\mathcal{A}}
is highest weight, negative Ext-groups between
E_{i}
’s and between
F_{i}
’s vanish, according to Theorem
3.1
. Moreover,
\{E_{i}\}
are the standard objects in
{\mathcal{A}}
and
\{F_{i}\}
are the costandard ones.

Functor
\mathbb{S}
is an equivalence, hence negative Ext-groups between

3.1
implies that the heart
{\mathcal{B}}
glued along

is highest weight with standard objects
\{F_{i}\}
and costandard objects
\{\mathbb{S}(E_{i})\}

By
[

, Theorem 6.3]

\mathbf{RD}({\mathcal{A}})
is highest weight with standard objects
\{\mathbb{S}^{-1}F_{i}\}
and costandard objects
\{E_{i}\}

By applying the Serre functor

we identify

with

.
∎

Remark 3.3

Note that the double dual of an exceptional sequence

\mathbb{S}\epsilon

4.2.
Directed algebras

Let
A
be a path algebra of a directed quiver with relations and
{\mathcal{A}}
the category of left
A
-modules. We assume that vertices of the quiver are labelled with
\{1,\ldots,n\}
and for every arrow the index of the source is less than the index of the target.

Indecomposable projective objects

in
{\mathcal{A}}
correspond to vertices
\{1,\ldots,n\}
of the quiver. As the quiver is directed,
\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits(P_{i},P_{j})=0
for
i>j
and
\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits(P_{i},P_{i})=\mathbbm{k}
. Hence,
(P_{n},\ldots,P_{1})
is a full exceptional sequence in
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})
.

Let
S_{i}
be the simple
A
-module corresponding to the vertex
i
. As
\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits(P_{i},S_{j})=\delta_{ij}
,
(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})
is a full exceptional sequence left dual to
(P_{n},\ldots,P_{1})
.

Finally, let
I_{i}
denote the injective envelope of
S_{i}
, i.e. the indecomposable injective
A
-module corresponding to the vertex
i
. As
\dim\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits(S_{i},I_{j})=\delta_{ij}
,
(I_{n},\ldots,I_{1})
is a full exceptional sequence in
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})
left dual to
(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})
.

Theorem
3.4
implies that
{\mathcal{A}}
admits two structures of a highest weight category. In the first one

are the standard objects while
S_{i}
are costandard. The induced partial order on the set

is inverse to the standard one. Equivalently, one can consider simple

-modules as standard objects and indecomposable injective objects as costandard. Then the corresponding partial order on the set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
is the standard one.

]

The category
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(\mathbb{G})
admits a full exceptional sequence
\langle S^{\alpha}{\mathcal{Q}}\rangle_{\alpha\in B_{l,m-l}}
whose left dual sequence is
\langle S^{\alpha^{T}}{\mathcal{R}}[-|\alpha|]\rangle_{\alpha\in\overline{B}_{l,m-l}}
.

The sequence was constructed by M. Kapranov
[
Kap84
]
when the characteristic of
\mathbbm{k}
is zero and by O. Buchweitz, G. Leuschke and M. Van den Bergh
[
BLVdB15
]
for an arbitrary field. A. Efimov
[
Efi17
]
generalised these results to the Grassmannian defined over
\mathbb{Z}
.

If
{\rm char}\mathbbm{k}=0
, the sequence
\langle S^{\alpha}{\mathcal{Q}}\rangle_{\alpha\in B_{l,m-l}}
is strong, in particular its endomorphism algebra is directed, hence quasi-hereditary, see Section
4.2
. It follows that the highest weight category
\mathop{\textrm{mod--}}B
is the heart of the glued
t
-struc
ture in
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(\mathbb{G}))
. When
{\rm char}\mathbbm{k}
is small, the sequence is not necessarily strong. However, we have

Theorem 5.3
.

[
BLVdB15
]

[
Efi17
]

The heart
{\mathcal{A}}
of the
t
-struc
ture glued along the exceptional sequence
\langle S^{\alpha}{\mathcal{Q}}\rangle_{\alpha\in B_{l,m-l}}
is highest weight and
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(\mathbb{G}))\simeq{\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})
.

Proof.

As the category
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(\mathbb{G}))
is idempotent complete and DG enhanced it suffices to check that
\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\mathbb{G}}(\bigoplus S^{\alpha}{\mathcal{Q}},\bigoplus S^{\alpha}{\mathcal{Q}}[l])
and
\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\mathbb{G}}(\bigoplus S^{\alpha^{T}}{\mathcal{R}}[-|\alpha|],\bigoplus S^{\alpha^{T}}{\mathcal{R}}[l-|\alpha|])
vanish for
l<0
. Indeed, if this is the case then the statement follows direclty from Theorem
3.1
.

The vanishing of the first groups follows immediately from the fact that
S^{\alpha}{\mathcal{Q}}
are vector bundles on
\mathbb{G}
. Let us now consider the group
\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\mathbb{G}}(S^{\alpha^{T}}{\mathcal{R}}[-|\alpha|],S^{\beta^{T}}{\mathcal{R}}[l-|\beta|])=\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\mathbb{G}}(S^{\alpha^{T}}{\mathcal{R}},S^{\beta^{T}}{\mathcal{R}}[l+|\alpha|-|\beta|])
. If
|\alpha|-|\beta|\leq 0
the group vanishes for any
l<0
, because
S^{\alpha^{T}}{\mathcal{R}}
and
S^{\beta^{T}}{\mathcal{R}}
are vector bundles on
\mathbb{G}
. If, on the other hand,
|\alpha|-|\beta|>0
, then the vanishing of the group for any
l
follows from the relation
\beta\prec\alpha
in
\overline{B}_{u,v}
.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

In
[
BLVdB15
]
an explicit tilting object
T
for
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(\mathbb{G}))
is constructed. The authors check that
\mathop{\textrm{mod--}}\mathop{\textrm{End}}\nolimits(T)
is highest weight and it proved that the equivalence
\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits(T,-)\colon{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(\mathbb{G}))\to{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(\mathop{\textrm{mod--}}\mathop{\textrm{End}}\nolimits(T))
maps
S^{\alpha}{\mathcal{Q}}
to the standard objects. A different argument in
[
Efi17
]
proves that a statement analogous to Theorem
5.3
holds over the ring of integers.

Remark 5.5
.

A full exceptional sequence has also been constructed by
[
KP11
]
on the Grassmannian
{\rm LGr}(n,V)
of Lagrangian subspaces in a
2n
-dimensional vector space
V
over a field
\mathbbm{k}
of characteristic zero equipped with a non-degenerate symplectic form. The sequence consists of vector bundles, hence negative
\mathop{\operatorname{Ext}}\nolimits
-groups between its elements vanish. The sequence is built from
n+1
strong blocks. Recently, the left dual sequences in these blocks were described by A. Fonarev
[

.
It would be interesting to describe the left dual of the entire exceptional sequence and check if it satisfy the conditions of the criterion in
Theorem
3.1
for the glued heart to be a highest weight category .

5.3.
Null categories for birational morphisms of regular surfaces

Here we give an explicit description of an exceptional sequence and its dual in the null category for any birational morphism of regular surfaces.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a proper birational morphism of regular surfaces over
k
. We consider the
triangulated null category

[
BB18
]
:

{\mathcal{C}}_{f}=\{E\in{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(X)\,|\,Rf_{*}(E)=0\}.

For
f
as above we consider the partially ordered set
\mathop{\textrm{Conn}}\nolimits(f)
, see
[
BB18
]
. Elements of
\mathop{\textrm{Conn}}\nolimits(f)
are pairs
(g,h)
which provide decompositions
f=h\circ g
of
f
into proper birational morphisms
g
and
h
of regular surfaces such that the exceptional divisor of
g
is connected. Given
(g_{1},h_{1})
,
(g_{2},h_{2})
in
\mathop{\textrm{Conn}}\nolimits(f)
we put
(g_{1},h_{1})\preceq(g_{2},h_{2})
if
g_{2}
factors via
g_{1}
.

Let
(g,h)
be an element of
\mathop{\textrm{Conn}}\nolimits(f)
with
g\colon X\to Z
. We denote by
R_{g}
the fundamental cycle of
g
and by
D_{g}
its discrepancy divisor. If
z\in Z
is the image of the exceptional divisor of
g
and
\mathfrak{m}_{g}
its maximal ideal sheaf, then
R_{g}
and
D_{g}
are divisors in
X
defined by the equalities:

\displaystyle g^{-1}(\mathfrak{m}_{g})={\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-R_{g}),

\displaystyle g^{!}({\mathcal{O}}_{Z})={\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D_{g}).

We denote by
\mathop{\textrm{Conn}}\nolimits(f)^{\mathop{\textrm{op}}\nolimits}
the opposite partial order on the set
\mathop{\textrm{Conn}}\nolimits(f)
.

Theorem 5.6
.

Category
{\mathcal{C}}_{f}
admits full exceptional sequence
\left({\mathcal{O}}_{R_{g}}(R_{g})\right)_{g\in\mathop{\textrm{Conn}}\nolimits(f)}
whose left dual sequence is
\left({\mathcal{O}}_{R_{g}}(D_{g})\right)_{g\in\mathop{\textrm{Conn}}\nolimits(f)^{\mathop{\textrm{op}}\nolimits}}
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on the number
n
of irreducible components in the exceptional divisor for
f
. If
n=1
then
f
is the blow-up of a smooth point. Then
(f,\textrm{Id})
is the single element of
\mathop{\textrm{Conn}}\nolimits(f)
and divisors
R_{f}
and
D_{f}
coincide. If this is the case, then the null category is equivalent to
\langle{\mathcal{O}}_{R_{f}}(R_{f})\rangle=\langle{\mathcal{O}}_{D_{f}}(R_{f})\rangle
.

Let now the exceptional divisor
\textrm{Ex}(f)
of
f\colon X\to Y
have
n
irreducible components. Without loss of generality we can assume that
\textrm{Ex}(f)
connected. Indeed, if this is not the case, let
g_{1}\colon X\to Z_{1}
and
g_{2}\colon X\to Z_{2}
be birational proper morphisms to regular surfaces such that
\textrm{Ex}(f)=\textrm{Ex}(g_{1})\sqcup\textrm{Ex}(g_{2})
. Then
{\mathcal{C}}_{f}={\mathcal{C}}_{g_{1}}\oplus{\mathcal{C}}_{g_{2}}
and
\mathop{\textrm{Conn}}\nolimits(f)
is a union of
\mathop{\textrm{Conn}}\nolimits(g_{1})
and
\mathop{\textrm{Conn}}\nolimits(g_{2})
such that any element of
\mathop{\textrm{Conn}}\nolimits(g_{1})
is not comparable with any element of
\mathop{\textrm{Conn}}\nolimits(g_{2})
.

Let
y\in Y
be the image of
\textrm{Ex}(f)
and let
h\colon Z\to Y
be the blow-up at
y
. Finally, let
g\colon X\to Z
be such that
f=h\circ g
. Then
\mathop{\textrm{Conn}}\nolimits(f)=\mathop{\textrm{Conn}}\nolimits(g)\cup\{f\}
and category
{\mathcal{C}}_{f}
admits semi-orthogonal decompositions

(14)

{\mathcal{C}}_{f}=\langle{\mathcal{C}}_{g},Lg^{*}{\mathcal{C}}_{h}\rangle=\langle g^{!}{\mathcal{C}}_{h},{\mathcal{C}}_{g}\rangle.

As
h
is the blow-up at a smooth point, category
{\mathcal{C}}_{h}
is generated by
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(E)
, for the exceptional divisor
E
of
h
. Since
Lg^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(E)={\mathcal{O}}_{R_{f}}(R_{f})
and
g^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(E)={\mathcal{O}}_{R_{f}}(D_{f})
the inductive hypothesis imply that
\left({\mathcal{O}}_{R_{g}}(R_{g})\right)_{g\in\mathop{\textrm{Conn}}\nolimits(f)}
and
\left({\mathcal{O}}_{R_{g}}(D_{g})\right)_{g\in\mathop{\textrm{Conn}}\nolimits(f)^{\mathop{\textrm{op}}\nolimits}}
are exceptional sequences in
{\mathcal{C}}_{f}
. As
\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{X}({\mathcal{O}}_{R_{g}}(R_{g}),{\mathcal{O}}_{R_{g}}(D_{g}))=\mathbbm{k}
the second one is indeed left dual to the first one.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

For the case of rational surfaces, the exceptional sequence
\left({\mathcal{O}}_{R_{g}}(R_{g})\right)_{g\in\mathop{\textrm{Conn}}\nolimits(f)}
may be interpreted as a mutation of a part of the exceptional sequence considered by Hille and Perling in
[
HP11
]
.

Now we introduce the
abelian null category
for
f\colon X\to Y
:

\mathscr{A}_{f}=\{E\in\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(X)\,|\,Rf_{*}(E)=0\}.

The exceptional divisor
\textrm{Ex}(f)=\bigcup C_{i}
of
f
is a tree of rational curves (see
[
BB22
, Theorem D.1]
for a more general statement). By
[
BB25
, Theorem 7.13]
,
\{{\mathcal{O}}_{C_{i}}(-1)\}_{C_{i}\in\textrm{Irr}(f)}
is the set of isomorphism classes of irreducible objects in
\mathscr{A}_{f}
. Moreover, by
[
BB18
, Theorem 2.17]
the set of irreducible components of
\textrm{Ex}(f)
is in bijection with
\mathop{\textrm{Conn}}\nolimits(f)
. Hence, the partial order on
\mathop{\textrm{Conn}}\nolimits(f)
induces a partial order on the set of irreducible objects in
\mathscr{A}_{f}
. We denote it by
\Lambda
.

Theorem 5.8
.

Consider a proper birational morphism
f\colon X\to Y
of regular surfaces. Then the standard
t
-struc
ture on
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(X)
restricts to a
t
-struc
ture on
{\mathcal{C}}_{f}
with heart
\mathscr{A}_{f}
.
(\mathscr{A}_{f},\Lambda)
is a highest weight category with standard objects
\{{\mathcal{O}}_{R_{g}}(R_{g})\}_{g\in\mathop{\textrm{Conn}}\nolimits(f)}
and costandard ones
\{{\mathcal{O}}_{R_{g}}(D_{g})\}_{g\in\mathop{\textrm{Conn}}\nolimits(f)}
. Moreover,
{\mathcal{C}}_{f}\cong{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(\mathscr{A}_{f})
.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.6
, category
{\mathcal{C}}_{f}
admits a full exceptional sequence
\left({\mathcal{O}}_{R_{g}}(R_{g})\right)_{g\in\mathop{\textrm{Conn}}\nolimits(f)}
of sheaves whose left dual sequence
\left({\mathcal{O}}_{R_{g}}(D_{g})\right)_{g\in\mathop{\textrm{Conn}}\nolimits(f)^{\mathop{\textrm{op}}\nolimits}}
also consists of sheaves. Hence, by Theorem
3.4
, the standard
t
-struc
ture on
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(X)
, with heart
\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(X)
, restricts to a
t
-struc
ture on
{\mathcal{C}}_{f}
with heart
\mathscr{A}_{f}={\mathcal{C}}_{f}\cap\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(X)
. Further,
(\mathscr{A}_{f},\Lambda)
is highest weight with the above standard and costandard objects. Finally, as
{\mathcal{C}}_{f}
is idempotent complete and admits a DG enhancement, Theorem
3.1
(2) implies that
{\mathcal{C}}_{f}\cong{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(\mathscr{A}_{f})
.
∎

This theorem provides an interesting class of highest weight categories of geometric origin which deserves further investigation.

Note that the fact that, for a morphism
f\colon X\to Y
between schemes, the standard
t
-struc
ture on
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(X)
restricts to a
t
-struc
ture on
{\mathcal{C}}_{f}
when fibers of
f
have dimension
\leq 1
is proved in
[
Bri02
]
. Hence,
\mathscr{A}_{f}
is abelian in this case.

We do not have an explicit description of projective or injective generators for
\mathscr{A}_{f}
. However, the characteristic tilting object can be written down explicitly.

Theorem 5.9
.

The sheaf

T_{f}=\bigoplus_{g\in\textrm{Conn}(f)}\omega_{X}|_{D_{g}}

is the characteristic tilting module for the highest weight category
(\mathscr{A}_{f},\Lambda)
.

Proof.

By
[
BB23
, Section 6.1]
the characteristic tilting object is the injective generator for the heart of the
t
-struc
ture on
{\mathcal{C}}_{f}
glued along the exceptional sequence right dual to the sequence
\langle{\mathcal{O}}_{R_{g}}(R_{g})\rangle_{g\in\mathop{\textrm{Conn}}\nolimits(f)}
of standard objects (see Theorem
5.8
).

It follows from Remark
3.7
and Theorem
5.8
that the Ringel dual
t
-struc
ture on
{\mathcal{C}}_{f}
is glued along the
\mathop{\operatorname{Dec}}\nolimits(f)^{\mathop{\textrm{op}}\nolimits}
-filtration

Lg^{*}{\mathcal{C}}_{h}\subset{\mathcal{C}}_{f},

i.e. for any decomposition
f=h\circ g
into proper birational morphisms of regular surfaces, the functor
Lg^{*}\colon{\mathcal{C}}_{h}\to{\mathcal{C}}_{f}
is
t
-exact for the Ringel dual
t
-struc
ture on
{\mathcal{C}}_{h}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{f}
, see
[
BB18
]
.

Then it follows from
[
BB18
, Proposition 5.14]
by induction on the number of standard objects in
{\mathcal{C}}_{f}
that the Ringel dual
t
-struc
ture on
{\mathcal{C}}_{f}
is the
T_{f}
-injective

-struc
ture. The statement follows.
∎

If
Y
is a singular surface and
f\colon X\to Y
its resolution then
\mathscr{A}_{f}
is not necessarily a highest weight category and
{\mathcal{C}}_{f}
does not have to be equivalent to
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(\mathscr{A}_{f})
as the following examples show.

Example 5.10
.

Consider the minimal resolution
f\colon X\to Y
of type
A_{2}
surface singularity. The abelian null category
\mathscr{A}_{f}
is equivalent to the category of finite dimensional modules over the quiver

\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{a}
\textstyle{2\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{b}

with relations
ab=0
,
ba=0
. Hence,
\mathscr{A}_{f}
is of infinite global dimension while any highest weight category is of finite global dimension
[
CPS88
]
.
Therefore,
\mathscr{A}_{f}
is not highest weight for any partial order on the set of simple modules.

Example 5.11
.

Let now
f\colon X\to Y
be the minimal resolution of type
A_{1}
surface singularity. If
E\subset X
is the exceptional divisor of
f
, then
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(-1)
is an additive generator for the category
\mathscr{A}_{f}
, equivalent to the category of finite dimensional
k
-vector spaces. However,
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(-1)
is a 2-spherical object. In particular,
\mathop{\operatorname{Ext}}\nolimits^{2}_{X}({\mathcal{O}}_{E}(-1),{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(-1))\cong\mathbbm{k}
. Hence,
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(\mathscr{A}_{f})
is not equivalent to
{\mathcal{C}}_{f}
.

In higher dimension, consider a flopping contraction
f\colon X\to Y
with following conditions: dimension of fibers is bounded by 1, the exceptional locus is of codimension in
X
greater than 1, both
X
and
Y
Gorenstein, and
Y
with canonical hypersurface singularities of multiplicity two. In this case also
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(\mathscr{A}_{f})\ncong{\mathcal{C}}_{f}
. Moreover, the derived functor
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(\mathscr{A}_{f})\to{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(X)
of the inclusion
\mathscr{A}_{f}\subset\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}}\nolimits(X)
is proved to be spherical
[
BB22
]
.

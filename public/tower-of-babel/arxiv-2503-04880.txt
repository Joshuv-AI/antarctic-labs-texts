New Source for QCD Axion Dark Matter Production: Curvature Induced
Cem Eröncel, Yann Gouttenoire, Ryosuke Sato, Géraldine Servant, Peera Simakachorn

Abstract
We discuss a novel mechanism for generating dark matter from a fast-rolling scalar field, relevant for both inflation and rotating axion models, and apply it specifically to the (QCD) axion. Dark matter comes from scalar field fluctuations generated by the product of the curvature perturbation and the fast-rolling background field. These fluctuations can explain the totality of dark matter in a vast axion parameter space, particularly for the QCD axion, which will be targeted by upcoming experiments. We review the constraints on this mechanism and potential gravitational-wave signatures. Published version: Phys. Rev. Lett. 135, 231002

Curvature-induced fluctuations of fast-rolling field.
–In the absence of anisotropic stress, the space-time metric in the conformal Newtonian gauge is

ds^{2}=a^{2}\Big[-(1+2\Phi)d\eta^{2}+(1-2\Phi)\delta_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j}\Big],

(1)

where
\Phi
is the scalar metric fluctuation whose Fourier modes are defined by
\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}=\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}\,e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\Phi({\mathbf{x}})
, and
\eta
is conformal time. On super-horizon modes,
\Phi
is frozen and related to the gauge-invariant comoving curvature perturbation
\mathcal{R}\equiv-\Phi-{2}{(\mathcal{H}^{-1}\Phi^{\prime}+\Phi)}/{3(1+\omega)}\simeq-3\Phi/2
, where
(\cdot)^{\prime}\equiv d(\cdot)/d\eta
,
\mathcal{H}=aH
is the comoving Hubble rate and
\omega
is the Universe equation of state. We assume radiation-domination
\omega=1/3
(unlike
[
24
]
) and treat the case of kination-domination in Fig.
3
.

Consider a fast‐rolling scalar field,
\phi=\overline{\phi}+\delta\phi
, with
\overline{\phi}
its homogeneous part and
\delta\phi
its fluctuations, as already discussed in
[
24
]
.
During fast-rolling – when kinetic and gradient energies dominate and potential energy is negligible – and under the conditions
\Phi,\delta\phi\ll 1
, the scalar field equation of motion becomes

\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime\prime}+2\mathcal{H}\,\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}+k^{2}\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}=4\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}\overline{\phi}^{\prime}.

(2)

The right-hand side of this equation is the key point. The non-zero scalar speed
\overline{\phi}^{\prime}
combined with the curvature perturbation
\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}
sources the scalar fluctuation with momentum
k
, leading to the sub-horizon solution (
k\gg\mathcal{H})

\phi_{\mathbf{k}}(\eta)\simeq\alpha\,\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{k}}(0)\frac{\overline{\phi}^{\prime}}{\mathcal{H}}\cos(k\eta),

(3)

for modes entering during the fast-rolling stage
k<\mathcal{H}_{\rm kin}
and
\alpha\simeq 0.52
(see
[
12
, App. B.2]
and App.
A
for a rederivation).
At all orders in
\delta\phi
, the scalar field energy density is
\rho_{\phi}=[\left({\phi^{\prime}}\right)^{\!2}+\left({\nabla\phi}\right)^{\!2}]/(2a^{2})=\overline{\rho}_{\phi}(1+\delta_{\phi}+{\delta_{\phi}^{2}}/{2})
,
with
\overline{\rho}_{\phi}=(\overline{\phi}^{\prime}/a)^{2}/2
and the linear component of the density contrast
\delta_{\phi}\equiv(\rho_{\phi}/\overline{\rho}_{\phi}-1)_{\rm linear}=2\delta\phi^{\prime}/\overline{\phi}
.
We deduce the volume-average of the energy density fluctuations
[
24
]

\rho_{\rm fluct}(\eta)\equiv\left\langle\rho_{\phi}-\overline{\rho}_{\phi}\right\rangle=\frac{\overline{\rho}_{\phi}(\eta)}{2}\int d\log{k}\penalty\ \mathcal{P}_{\delta_{\phi}}(k,\eta),

(4)

where
\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{\phi}}(k)\equiv\int\!\!d^{3}\mathbf{x}\,e^{i\mathbf{k}\!\cdot\mathbf{x}}\!\left<\delta_{\phi}(0)\delta_{\phi}(\mathbf{x})\right>
is the dimensionless power spectrum of the linear density contrast. We used the important fact that
\left<\delta_{\phi}\right>\propto\left<\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{k}}\right>=0
for adiabatic primordial curvature perturbations. Using Eq. (
3
) and averaging over oscillations, we get
\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{\phi}}\simeq 2\alpha^{2}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)\quantity(k/\mathcal{H})^{2}
and Eq. (
4
) leads to the fractional energy density
\Omega_{\rm fluct}\equiv\rho_{\rm fluct}/\rho_{\rm tot}
of the fluctuations of the fast-rolling field

\frac{d\Omega_{\rm fluct}(\eta)}{d\log{k}}=\alpha^{2}\Omega_{\phi}(\eta_{\rm kin})\left(\frac{k}{\mathcal{H}_{\rm kin}}\right)^{\!2}\!\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k),

(5)

where
\mathcal{H}_{\rm kin}=a_{\rm kin}H_{\rm kin}
is the value of the Hubble rate when the scalar field starts rolling at
a_{\rm kin}
.
We used
\mathcal{H}\propto a^{-1}
and introduced
\Omega_{\phi}(\eta)\equiv\overline{\rho_{\phi}}/\rho_{\rm tot}
which redshifts like
\propto a^{-2}
during radiation domination. We now study the novel implications for DM.

Dark matter from curvature-induced scalar fluctuations.
–We consider a scalar field
\phi
with mass
m
. Below a scale factor

a_{\rm NR}(k)=k/m
, the mode
k
turns non-relativistic and its energy density redshifts like matter. From integrating Eq. (
5
) over all modes, we deduce the total fractional energy abundance of scalar fluctuation today

\frac{\Omega_{\rm fluct}^{0}}{\Omega_{\rm rad}^{0}}=\alpha^{2}\Omega_{\phi}(\eta_{\rm kin})\left(\frac{a_{0}m}{\mathcal{H}_{\rm kin}}\right)\!\int_{k_{\rm osc}}^{k_{\rm kin}}\frac{dk}{k}\left(\frac{k}{\mathcal{H}_{\rm kin}}\right)\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k).

(6)

where
h^{2}\Omega_{\rm rad}^{0}\simeq 4.21\times 10^{-5}

[
27
]
with
h\simeq 0.68
the reduced Hubble factor and
a_{0}
is today’s scale factor.
The expression at any time is provided in Eq. (
41
) of Supplemental Material
[
28
]
.
We model the curvature power spectrum as a power law

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k_{\star})\left({k}/{k_{\star}}\right)^{n_{s}(k)-1},

(7)

where the amplitude and spectral index are measured by Planck at the pivot scale
k_{\star}\equiv 0.05\penalty\ \rm Mpc^{-1}

[
29
]
,

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k_{\star})\simeq 2.099(29)\times 10^{-9},\penalty\ n_{s}(k_{\star})\simeq 0.965(4)
.
For
k
away from
k_{\star}
, we use the Taylor-expansion
n_{s}(k)=n_{s}(k_{\star})-(3.65\times 10^{-4})\ln(k/k_{\star})
obtained after analyzing 300 single-field inflation models
[
30
]
.
Plugging Eq. (
7
) into Eq. (
6
), the total abundance for
\mathcal{H}_{\rm kin}\gg k_{\rm osc}
is dominated by the mode
k_{\rm kin}
that re-enters at
a_{\rm kin}

\frac{\Omega_{\rm fluct}^{0}}{\Omega^{0}_{\rm rad}}=\alpha^{2}\Omega_{\phi}(\eta_{\rm kin})\left(\frac{a_{0}m}{\mathcal{H}_{\rm kin}}\right)\frac{\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k_{\rm kin})}{n_{s}},

(8)

Note that
{\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k_{\rm kin})}
is poorly constrained and could be in principle many orders of magnitude larger than
{\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k_{\star})}
, a fact exploited in inflationary models that predict primordial black holes

The abundance of fluctuations
\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}
should be compared with the one of the zero mode
\overline{\phi}

\Omega_{\rm zero}^{0}\simeq\Omega_{\rm rad}^{0}\Omega_{\phi}(\eta_{\rm kin})\left(\frac{a_{0}}{a_{\rm osc}}\right)\left(\frac{a_{\rm kin}}{a_{\rm osc}}\right)^{2},

(9)

where

\overline{\rho_{\phi}}(\eta_{\rm osc})\simeq m_{a}^{2}f_{a}^{2}

a_{\rm kin}/a_{\rm osc}\simeq(m_{a}f_{a})^{1/3}/\overline{\rho}^{1/6}_{\phi}(\eta_{\rm kin})

\frac{\Omega_{\rm fluct}^{0}}{\Omega_{\rm zero}^{0}}\simeq 0.9\,\frac{\alpha^{2}}{n_{s}}\Omega_{\phi}^{1/2}(\eta_{\rm kin})\left(\frac{10^{10}\,{\rm GeV}}{f_{a}}\right)\left(\frac{\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k_{\rm kin})}{2.1\cdot 10^{-9}}\right).

(10)

The fluctuation energy density can be further enhanced by up to one order of magnitude when the scalar field dominates the Universe, inducing a kination era, see App.
E

Fluctuations are produced with
k\gg m
and initially behave as radiation, so they are constrained by structure formation. The Lyman-
\alpha

m_{\rm WDM}\gtrsim 2\penalty\ \,\mathrm{keV}

[

\omega_{\rm fluct}\simeq k/(3\,a\,m)

T_{\rm kin}\lesssim 1.1\times 10^{15}\,\mathrm{GeV}\left(\frac{m}{1\,\mathrm{eV}}\right)\frac{g_{*s}^{1/3}(T_{\rm kin})}{g_{*}^{1/2}(T_{\rm kin})},

(11)

where

is the Universe temperature at

This condition is satisfied in the model parameter space of Fig.

Eq. (

Application: DM from rotating axions.
–
We now apply our findings to a well-motivated framework realising fast-moving axions:
rotating complex fields, as discussed extensively in Ref.
[

They rely on a complex scalar field
\chi=Se^{i\phi/f_{a}}
, corresponding to the Peccei-Quinn field in the case of the QCD axion
[

55

, where
S

\phi
describe the radial and angular field directions. The latter is identified with the axion. The key evolution is that the dynamics start at a large-

i)
a scalar potential invariant under a global
U(1)
symmetry with the spontaneous breaking minimum at
S=f_{a}
that allows a motion along the axion direction at late times,
ii)
an explicit
U(1)
–breaking term that operates only at early times and kicks the complex field into an elliptic orbit,

iii)
a large radial initial field value
S_{\rm ini}
that enables the angular kick,
iv)
a radial-motion damping mechanism to bring the field orbit into a circle, which later settles down to the potential minimum and starts the kination behavior (i.e.,
\rho_{\phi}\propto a^{-6}
).
Here, we remain agnostic on the origins of some ingredients as they depend on the complete realization of such models, which were discussed extensively in e.g.
[
33
,
14
]

Let us consider for ingredient
i)
a
U(1)
-invariant quartic potential and for ingredient
iii)
a Hubble-induced mass term:

V(\chi)=\lambda(|\chi|^{2}-f_{a}^{2})^{2}-\begin{cases}\lambda_{H}H^{2}|\chi|^{2}\penalty\ &\textrm{during inflation}\\
0\penalty\ \penalty\ &\textrm{after inflation},\end{cases}

(12)

which also suppresses the axion isocurvature perturbation.

During inflation, the field is driven to the potential minimum

S_{\rm ini}=f_{a}\sqrt{1+\beta^{2}}\penalty\ \penalty\ \textrm{with}\penalty\ \penalty\ \beta^{2}\equiv\lambda_{H}{H_{\rm inf}^{2}}/{(2\lambda f_{a}^{2})},

where
H_{\rm inf}
is the Hubble scale during inflation.
After inflation, we assume for simplicity that the Universe reheated instantaneously

In the case of non-instantaneous reheating with a period after inflation where the total energy density of the Universe redshifts as
\rho_{\rm tot}\sim a^{-n}

H_{\rm inf}>H>H_{\rm reh}

The scalar field energy density is set by the potential,

\rho_{\chi,\rm ini}=V(S_{\rm ini})=\lambda f_{a}^{4}\beta^{4}={\lambda_{H}^{2}H_{\rm inf}^{4}}/{(4\lambda)},

The field remains frozen due to Hubble friction.

The complex field starts moving when

Conclusion.
–We presented a novel source for scalar DM in models where the scalar field experienced a short stage of fast-rolling in its early history.
We studied the implications in the framework of rotating axion models. Assumptions and constraints on parameters are similar to models of kinetic misalignment, in particular those conditions related to radial mode damping as studied extensively in
[
33
]
and
[
14
]
.
What we learn in this letter is that axion DM can be dominantly produced from primordial curvature perturbations, as fluctuations rather than as the well-known oscillations of the zero-mode condensate,
without demanding any specific assumption on the realisation of inflation.

Our findings are highly relevant to a large number of upcoming axion experiments that will probe the specific region of parameter space (where the axion cannot be DM from the standard misalignment mechanism
[
1
,
2

3

) where we predict our novel source of axions to be the dominant one and sufficient to explain DM. Precise predictions
will need to be checked with numerical simulations because of backreaction effects. While fluctuations from the condensate fragmentation in
[

do not impact much the axion relic abundance because they are produced when mostly non-relativistic, in this work, the fluctuations are highly relativistic at the time of production and interactions between the fluctuations and the background could modify the predictions presented in our analysis. The corresponding lattice simulations will be challenging as they involve a long evolution and a large hierarchy of scales.

Derivation of

A.1.  Equations of Motion (EoM) for the background and fluctuations

Our goal is to study the effect of curvature fluctuation
\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{k}}
on a scalar field which is decomposed as

\phi(\eta,\mathbf{x})=\overline{\phi}(\eta)+\delta\phi(\eta,\mathbf{x})=\overline{\phi}(\eta)+\int\frac{\differential[3]{\vb{k}}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}(\eta)e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}},

(17)

where
\overline{\phi}
and
\delta\phi
denote the background and the fluctuations respectively. Denoting by
V(\phi)
its potential, the equation of motion (EoM) for the background is

\overline{\phi}^{\prime\prime}+2\mathcal{H}\overline{\phi}^{\prime}+a^{2}\partialderivative{V}{\phi}=0,

(18)

while the EoM for the fluctuation Fourier modes
\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}
in the limit
\Phi\ll 1
and
\delta\phi\ll 1
read
[
66
]

\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime\prime}+2\mathcal{H}\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}+\quantity(k^2+a^2\pdv[2]{V}{\phi})\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}=-2\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}\partialderivative{V}{\phi}+4\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}\overline{\phi}^{\prime},

(19)

In both equations, the potential derivatives are evaluated at
\phi=\overline{\phi}
. The solution of
\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}
during radiation (
\omega=1/3
) is:
[
67
]

\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}(k\eta)=\!\frac{9}{\sqrt{3}}\frac{\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}(0)}{k\eta}j_{1}\!\!\left(\frac{k\eta}{\sqrt{3}}\right)\!\xrightarrow[k\eta\gg 1]{}6\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{k}}(0)\frac{\cos(k\eta/\sqrt{3})}{(k\eta)^{2}},

(20)

where
j_{1}(x)
is the spherical Bessel function and
\mathcal{R}(0)=3\Phi(0)/2
is the comoving curvature perturbation on super-horizon scales in radiation era
[
67
]
.

A.2.  Fluctuations in the fast-rolling regime

We assume the scalar field undergoes a period of fast-rolling during which the potential
V(\phi)
is negligible compared to its
kinetic and gradient energies. In this regime, the EoM (
19
) simplifies to

\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime\prime}+2\mathcal{H}\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}+k^{2}\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}=4\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}\overline{\phi}^{\prime}.

(21)

The fluctuation
\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}
is sourced by
\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}
around the time of the horizon re-entry (
k=\mathcal{H}=aH
).

The solution of Eq. (
21
) is derived in Appendix B of
[
12
]
for the modes that enter the horizon during the fast-rolling period by assuming adiabatic initial conditions at
a_{\rm kin}
. In the sub-horizon limit
k/\mathcal{H}\gg 1
, the solution is approximated by

\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}\approx\alpha\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{k}}(0)\evaluated{\frac{\overline{\phi}'}{\mathcal{H}}}_{\rm kin}\quantity(\frac{a_{\rm kin}}{a})\cos\quantity(\frac{k}{\mathcal{H}}),

(22)

where

\alpha\equiv\frac{2}{3}\quantity[\sqrt{3}\ln\qty(\frac{\sqrt{3}+1}{\sqrt{3}-1})-\frac{3}{2}]\simeq 0.52.

(23)

The scalar field density contrast in the sub-horizon limit is

\delta_{\phi,\mathbf{k}}=2\quantity(\frac{\delta\phi_{\mathbf k}'}{\overline{\phi}'}-\Phi_{\mathbf k})\simeq 2\frac{\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}}{\overline{\phi}^{\prime}}\simeq-2\alpha\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{k}}(0)\quantity(\frac{k}{\mathcal{H}})\sin\quantity(\frac{k}{\mathcal{H}})\propto a.

(24)

We see that the density contrast grows linearly with the scale factor. From this expression, we can obtain the dimensionless power spectrum of the scalar field density fluctuations as

\mathcal{P}_{\phi}(k;\eta)\simeq 4\alpha^{2}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)\quantity(\frac{k}{\mathcal{H}})^{2}\sin^{2}\quantity(\frac{k}{\mathcal{H}})\to 2\alpha^{2}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)\quantity(\frac{k}{\mathcal{H}})^{2},

(25)

where in the last step we took a time average of the mode function oscillations. This expression implies that the mode with the wavenumber
k
becomes non-perturbative when

\mathcal{P}_{\phi}(k;\eta)>1\implies\quantity(\frac{a}{a_{\rm kin}})^{2}>\frac{1}{2\alpha^{2}}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}^{-1}(k)\quantity(\frac{k}{\mathcal{H}_{\rm kin}})^{-1}.

(26)

We also see that the mode with the highest wavenumber becomes non-perturbative first. Since the analysis presented in this section is valid for the modes that enter the horizon during the fast-rolling regime, the first mode that becomes non-perturbative is the mode for which
k=\mathcal{H}_{\rm kin}
. Finally, we conclude that the linear perturbation theory breaks down when

\mathcal{P}_{\phi}(k;\eta_{\rm kin})>1\implies\quantity(\frac{a}{a_{\rm kin}})^{2}>\frac{1}{2\alpha^{2}}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}^{-1}(k_{\rm kin}).

(27)

A.3.  Fluctuations in the non-perturbative regime

We now discuss the fate of the scalar field fluctuations in the ”non-perturbative regime”, i.e. when the inequality given in Eq. (
27
) is satisfied. In this section we will also allow that the scalar field potential has a, in general time-dependent, mass term so that they can be dark matter. We will be interested in the modes that are deep inside the horizon, but were super-horizon at the beginning of the fast-rolling regime. For these modes, the effect of the curvature perturbations is negligible, so that the EoM for the scalar field becomes

\phi^{\prime\prime}+2\mathcal{H}-\nabla^{2}\phi+V^{\prime}(\phi)=0.

(28)

We can again decompose the scalar field as in Eq. (
17
), but now we shouldn’t suppose that
\delta\phi\ll\overline{\phi}
. If the scalar field potential
V(\phi)
is purely a mass term, i.e.
V(\phi)=\frac{1}{2}m^{2}(\eta)\phi^{2}
, then the EoMs for the background and the fluctuations decouple, and we can express them as

\overline{\phi}^{\prime\prime}+2\mathcal{H}\overline{\phi}^{\prime}+m^{2}(\eta)\overline{\phi}=0,

(29)

and

\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime\prime}+2\mathcal{H}\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}+\quantity[k^2+a^2m^2(\eta)]\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}=0,

(30)

respectively. We note that such a separation cannot be done for a generic potential and we will need lattice simulations to study the system. This is left for a separate study.

If we assume that the Hubble scale
H
and the scalar field mass
m(\eta)
vary slowly compared to
\Upsilon_{k}(t)=\sqrt{k^{2}/a^{2}(t)+m^{2}(t)}
, then we can use the WKB approximation to express the solution of the mode function in physical time as

\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}=\frac{c_{\mathbf{k}}}{a^{3/2}(t)\sqrt{\Upsilon_{k}(t)}}\cos\quantity(\int^{t} \dd{t}'\Upsilon_k(t')).

(31)

The coefficient

can be determined by matching this solution to Eq. (
22

. This procedure fixes

to be

c_{\mathbf{k}}=\alpha\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{k}}(0)\sqrt{k}\,a_{\rm kin}\evaluated{\frac{\dot{\overline{\phi}}}{H}}_{\rm kin}

(32)

In the next section, we will use this result to calculate the energy density stored in the fluctuations.

A.4.  Energy density stored in the fluctuations

In this section, we will calculate the energy density stored in the fluctuations. Starting with the stress-energy tensor of a scalar field

T_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi-g_{\mu\nu}\quantity[\frac{1}{2}g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\phi\partial_{\beta}\phi+V(\phi)]

(33)

and using the metric given in the manuscript, we can calculate the energy density of the scalar field as

\rho_{\phi}=-T^{0}_{\;0}=\frac{1}{2}\quantity(1-2\Phi)\frac{\phi^{\prime 2}}{a^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\quantity(1+2\Phi)\quantity(\frac{\grad{\phi}\cdot\grad{\phi}}{a^2})+V(\phi).

(34)

In deriving this expression we have neglected the terms on the order of
\Phi^{2}
, but the scalar field is kept non-perturbative. By substituting the expansion in Eq. (
17
) into Eq. (
34
) and assuming a quadratic potential, we can show that

\rho_{\phi}=\rho_{\phi}^{(0)}+\rho_{\phi}^{(1)}+\rho_{\phi}^{(2)}+\rho_{\phi}^{(3)},

(35)

where

\displaystyle\rho_{\phi}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\overline{\phi}^{\prime 2}}{a^{2}}+V(\overline{\phi})=\overline{\rho_{\phi}},

(36)

\displaystyle\rho_{\phi}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\overline{\phi}^{\prime}\delta\phi^{\prime}}{a^{2}}-\Phi\overline{\phi}^{\prime 2}+m^{2}\overline{\phi}\delta\phi

(37)

\displaystyle\rho_{\phi}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta\phi^{\prime 2}}{a^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\gradient{\delta\phi}\cdot\gradient{\delta\phi}}{a^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\delta\phi^{2}

(38)

\displaystyle\rho_{\phi}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=-\Phi\frac{\delta\phi^{\prime 2}}{a^{2}}+\Phi\frac{\gradient{\delta\phi}\cdot\gradient{\delta\phi}}{a^{2}},

(39)

with the superscript denoting the order of the perturbation. For the dark matter density coming from the fluctuations, what matters is the average of the energy density over a sufficiently large cosmological volume. Since the scalar field fluctuations
\delta\phi
is sourced by the curvature perturbations
\Phi
which are Gaussian, the volume average of the first order
\rho_{\phi}^{(1)}
as well as the third order
\rho_{\phi}^{(3)}
contributions vanish. Thus, we can define the energy density stored in the fluctuations as

\rho_{\rm fluct}\equiv\expectationvalue{\rho_{\phi}}-\overline{\rho_{\phi}}=\expectationvalue{\rho^{(2)}_{\phi}}=\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{\differential[3]{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\quantity(\abs{\delta\dot{\phi}_{\mathbf k}(t)}^2+\Upsilon_k^2(t)\abs{\delta\phi_{\mathbf k}(t)}^2).

(40)

For these modes, we can use Eq. (
22
) as the solution of the mode functions. By plugging Eq. (
31
) into (
40
) and using Eq. (
32
) we obtain

\rho_{\rm fluct}(t)=\alpha^{2}\frac{\dot{\phi}_{\rm kin}^{2}}{2}\quantity(\frac{a_{\rm kin}}{a(t)})^{3}\int_{\mathcal{H}_{\rm nl}}^{\mathcal{H}_{\rm kin}}\differential{\log k}\quantity(\frac{k}{\mathcal{H}_{\rm kin}})\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)\sqrt{\quantity(\frac{k}{\mathcal{H}_{\rm kin}})^{2}\quantity(\frac{a_{\rm kin}}{a(t)})^{2}+\quantity(\frac{m(t)}{H_{\rm kin}})^{2}}

(41)

This expression is consistent with Eq. (
5
) in the fast-rolling regime where all the modes are relativistic, and with Eq. (
6
) at late times when all the modes are non-relativistic. We can observe that the dominant contribution to the energy density comes from the mode with the highest wavenumber, i.e.
k_{\rm kin}
. By approximating
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)\simeq\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k_{\rm kin})
we can evaluate the integral to show that the energy density in the fluctuations behave as

\rho_{\rm fluct}(a)\simeq\begin{cases}\dfrac{\alpha^{2}}{4}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k_{\rm kin})\dot{\overline{\phi}}_{\rm kin}^{2}\quantity(\dfrac{a_{\rm kin}}{a})^{4},&\dfrac{k_{\rm kin}}{a}\gg m(a)\\[6.49994pt]
\dfrac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k_{\rm kin})\dot{\overline{\phi}}_{\rm kin}^{2}\quantity(\dfrac{m_0}{H_{\rm kin}})\quantity(\dfrac{a_{\rm kin}}{a})^{3},&\dfrac{k_{\rm kin}}{a}\ll m(a)\end{cases}

(42)

We see that the energy density in fluctuations redshifts as radiation when the dominant mode
k_{\rm kin}
is relativistic, and redshifts as matter when the dominant mode is non-relativistic.

Appendix B
Equation of state of the fluctuations

The equation of the state (EoS) of the fluctuations can be defined by
\omega_{\rm fluct}=\langle p_{\rm fluct}\rangle/\langle\rho_{\rm fluct}\rangle
where
\rho_{\rm fluct}=\rho_{\phi}-\overline{\rho}_{\phi}
is the energy density in Eq. (
34
) and
p_{\rm fluct}=p_{\phi}-\overline{p}_{\phi}
is the pressure defined as

p_{\phi}=\frac{1}{2}\quantity(1-2\Phi)\frac{\phi^{\prime 2}}{a^{2}}-\frac{1}{6}\quantity(1+2\Phi)\quantity(\frac{\grad{\phi}\cdot\grad{\phi}}{a^2})-V(\phi).

(43)

In terms of the Fourier mode
\delta\phi_{k}
and assuming
V(\phi)=m_{a}^{2}\phi^{2}/2
, the EoS reads

\omega_{\rm fluct}=\frac{\left\langle\int d^{3}k\left[\dot{\delta\phi^{2}_{k}}-\left(\frac{k^{2}}{3a^{2}}+m_{a}^{2}\right)\delta\phi^{2}_{k}\right]\right\rangle}{\left\langle\int d^{3}k\left[\dot{\delta\phi^{2}_{k}}+\left(\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}+m_{a}^{2}\right)\delta\phi^{2}_{k}\right]\right\rangle},

(44)

where the integration runs from
k_{\rm osc}
to
k_{\rm kin}
.
Using the
\delta\phi_{k}
-solution (
31
), the EoS can be written as

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm fluct}(a)\simeq\frac{\int dk\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)\Upsilon_{k}\left\{1-{\Upsilon_{k}^{-2}}\cdot\left[{k^{2}/(3a^{2})+m_{a}^{2}}\right]\right\}}{2\int dk\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)\Upsilon_{k}}.

(45)

In the relativistic limit
k/a\gg m_{a}
, the fluctuations behave as radiation,
\omega_{\rm fluct}\to 1/3
, while
\omega_{\rm fluct}\to 0
in the non-relativistic limit
k/a\ll m_{a}
.

Since we know that the dominant energy density of fluctuations is generated around the scale
k_{\rm kin}
, let us assume
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)=A_{s}\delta(k-k_{\rm kin})
which simplifies our calculation to,

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm fluct}(a)\simeq\left[3(1+m_{a}^{2}a^{2}/k_{\rm kin}^{2})\right]^{-1}.

(46)

The EoS matches the 1/3 behavior in the relativistic limit and decreases with
a^{-2}
in the non-relativistic limit.
Using that
k_{\rm kin}/a_{\rm kin}=H_{\rm kin}=(\pi/\sqrt{90})g_{*}^{1/2}(T_{\rm kin})T_{\rm kin}^{2}/M_{\rm Pl}
, the EoS of fluctuations at matter-radiation equality is,

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm fluct,eq}\simeq\frac{\pi^{2}g_{*}(T_{\rm kin})}{270}\left[\frac{g_{*s}(T_{\rm eq})}{g_{*s}(T_{\rm kin})}\right]^{2/3}\frac{T_{\rm eq}^{2}T_{\rm kin}^{2}}{m_{a}^{2}M_{\rm Pl}^{2}},

(47)

where the derivation also uses that
\omega_{\rm fluct,\rm eq}\ll 1
.
The bound on the warmness of DM discussed in the main text is thus translated into the upper bound on
T_{\rm kin}
[Eq. (
11
)].
If we do the same analysis as in Eq. (
46
) for other fluctuation modes, it is easy to show that
\omega_{\rm fluct,\rm eq}(k<k_{\rm kin})<\omega_{\rm fluct,\rm eq}(k_{\rm kin})
.

In the case where the scalar field dominates the Universe, the bound from warmness of DM in Eq. (
11
), which assumes the scalar field is sub-dominant, is changed. Using Eq. (
46
) with
k_{\rm kin}/a_{\rm kin}=H_{\rm kin}=\overline{\rho}_{\phi,\rm kin}^{1/2}/(\sqrt{3}M_{\rm Pl})
where
\overline{\rho}_{\phi,\rm kin}>\rho_{\rm rad,kin}
, the warmness bound in the case of kination domination reads,

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm fluct}\simeq\left[\frac{g_{*s}(T_{\rm eq})}{g_{*s}(T_{\rm kin})}\right]^{2/3}\frac{T_{\rm eq}^{2}\,\overline{\rho}_{\phi,\rm kin}}{m_{a}^{2}M_{\rm Pl}^{2}T_{\rm kin}^{2}}.

(48)

Appendix C
An estimate of the backreaction

Throughout this work we have neglected interactions between the fluctuations and the homogeneous mode by approximating the scalar field potential by a quadratic one. However, in physically motivated scenarios a small mass for the scalar field is generated by the non-perturbative effects which implies a periodic potential for the scalar field. In particular, if the rolling scalar field is an axion, then its potential is given by

V(\phi,T)=m^{2}_{a}(T)f_{a}^{2}\quantity[1-\cos\qty(\frac{\phi}{f_{a}})].

(49)

We now discuss whether this potential affects the results presented in this letter.

During the fast-rolling regime, the scalar field is dominated by its kinetic energy so the results we derived in the second section of the main text and in Appendix
A.2
are not affected. The self-interactions due to the non-quadratic potential might be prominent once the size of the potential becomes comparable to the kinetic energy of the homogeneous mode or to the gradient energy stored in the fluctuations. These non-linear interactions can have two effects: First, they can cause the fluctuations to backreact on the homogeneous mode, altering its evolution; second, they can yield a modification of the fluctuation power spectrum due to the interactions between the modes. In this section we focus on the former effect, leaving the latter case to a future study.

The equation of motion for the scalar field with the potential given in Eq. (
49
) without any approximation reads

\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi}-\nabla^{2}\phi+m^{2}_{a}(t)f_{a}\sin\quantity(\frac{\phi}{f_{a}})=0,

(50)

We again expand the scalar field as the sum of the homogeneous mode and the fluctuations as in Eq. (
17
), plug into Eq. (
50
) and take an ensemble average of the equation of motion. By using the fact
\expectationvalue{\delta\phi}=0
, this yields to

\ddot{\overline{\phi}}+3H\dot{\overline{\phi}}+m^{2}_{a}(t)f_{a}\quantity[\sin\qty(\frac{\overline{\phi}}{f_a}) + \sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\qty(\eval{\pdv[n]{}{(\phi/f_a)}\sin\frac{\phi}{f_{a}}}_{\phi=\overline{\phi}})\frac{\expval{(\delta\phi)^n}}{f_a^n}]=0,

(51)

We can perform the sum by assuming that the fluctuation modes
\delta\phi_{\mathbf{k}}
obey the Gaussian statistics we find

\ddot{\overline{\phi}}+3H\dot{\overline{\phi}}+m^{2}_{a}(t)f_{a}\sin\quantity(\frac{\overline{\phi}}{f_a})e^{-\delta_{2}}=0,\quad\delta_{2}=\frac{1}{2f_{a}^{2}}\int\frac{\differential[3]{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\absolutevalue{\delta\phi_{\mathbf k}}^{2}.

(52)

We see that the effect of a large variance is the exponential suppression of the mass term. By using the mode function solution given in Eq. (
31
) together with Eq. (
32
), we can calculate the variance as

\delta_{2}(a)=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}\frac{\dot{\overline{\phi}}_{\rm kin}^{2}}{f_{a}^{2}H_{\rm kin}^{2}}\quantity(\frac{a_{\rm kin}}{a})^{3}\int_{\mathcal{H}(a)}^{\mathcal{H}_{\rm kin}}\differential{k}\frac{\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)}{\sqrt{\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}+m^{2}_{a}(a)}}.

(53)

By approximating
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)\approx\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k_{\rm kin})
in the relevant range we can perform the integral to get

\delta_{2}(a)=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}\frac{\dot{\overline{\phi}}_{\rm kin}^{2}}{f_{a}^{2}H_{\rm kin}^{2}}\quantity(\frac{a_{\rm kin}}{a})^{2}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k_{\rm kin})\sinh^{-1}\evaluated{\qty(\frac{k/a}{m_a(a)})}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{H}_{\rm kin}}.

(54)

We can observe that the variance decreases with redshift so it is largest at early times. However, during these times, the potential is negligible anyway, so a large variance at early times does not alter the evolution of the homogeneous mode. The relevant question is whether this term can be large when the homogeneous mode begins to oscillate. If this happens, the oscillations of the zero mode would get delayed further and the zero mode would continue to redshift as kination, thus suppressing its contribution to the dark matter abundance today.

By assuming that
k_{\rm kin}
is relativistic at
a_{\rm osc}
, i.e.
(k/a_{\rm osc})/m(a_{\rm osc})\gg 1
, the variance becomes

\delta_{2}(a_{\rm osc})=\alpha^{2}\frac{m_{a,\rm osc}^{2}}{H_{\rm osc}^{2}}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k_{\rm kin})\quantity[\sinh^{-1}\qty(\frac{a_{\rm kin}}{a_{\rm osc}}\frac{H_{\rm kin}}{m_{a, \rm osc}})-\sinh^{-1}\qty(\frac{H_{\rm osc}}{m_{a, \rm osc}})],

(55)

where we have used the fact that
\dot{\overline{\phi}}^{2}\propto a^{-6}
between
a_{\rm kin}
and
a_{\rm osc}
and the fact that without the backreaction, oscillations of the homogeneous mode starts when
\overline{\rho}_{\phi,\rm kin}=\overline{\rho}_{\phi,\rm pot}\implies\dot{\overline{\phi}}^{2}_{\rm osc}=2m_{a,\rm osc}^{2}f_{a}^{2}
. If
k_{\rm kin}
is relativistic at
a_{\rm osc}
, then the argument of the first
\sinh^{-1}
function is large so we can approximate it as
\sinh^{-1}[{a_{\rm kin}}{H_{\rm kin}}/({a\,m_{a,\rm osc}})]\approx\log[2{a_{\rm kin}H_{\rm kin}}/({a\,m_{a,\rm osc}})]
. On the other hand, in the kinetic misalignment mechanism, the onset of oscillations is delayed compared to the standard misalignment, so
m_{a,\rm osc}\gg H_{\rm osc}
. Then, the second
\sinh^{-1}
term can be approximated as
\sinh^{-1}(H_{\rm osc}/m_{a,\rm osc})\approx H_{\rm osc}/m_{a,\rm osc}
so this term is negligible compared to the
\log
term. So, our final result for the variance at the onset of zero-mode oscillations is

\delta_{2}(a_{\rm osc})\approx\alpha^{2}\quantity(\frac{m_{a, \rm osc}}{H_{\rm osc}})^{2}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k_{\rm kin})\log\quantity(2\frac{a_{\rm kin}}{a}\frac{H_{\rm kin}}{m_{a, \rm osc}}).

(56)

We expect that the onset of the zero-mode oscillations will be delayed due to the backreaction of the fluctuations if this term is larger than unity. Neglecting the
\log
factor this occurs when

\frac{m_{a,\rm osc}}{H_{\rm osc}}\gtrsim\frac{1}{\alpha\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}^{1/2}(k_{\rm kin})}.

(57)

For the rotating axion model presented in the manuscript
m_{a,\rm osc}/H_{\rm osc}
can be calculated for a given
m_{a}
and
f_{a}
as shown in Ref.
[
12
]
, and a large
m_{a,\rm osc}/H_{\rm osc}
values correspond to the low
f_{a}
region. As shown in Figures
2
,
3
, and
S1
, in the low
f_{a}
region dark matter is already dominated by the fluctuations so we do not expect that the backreaction modifies our conclusions in a qualitative way.

Appendix E
Dark matter abundance in the case where a kination era is triggered by the scalar field

In this appendix, we consider the scenario where the fast-rolling scalar dominates the energy density of the Universe and later becomes subdominant again. If it has an equation of state
\omega=1
, this will trigger a kination era.
This is of high interest because it is associated with a large enhancement of the inflationary background of gravitational waves.

We estimate the scalar field abundance from the curvature-induced fluctuations and compare it to the background field abundance. We focus on the fluctuations generated during the fast-roll stage. However, the fluctuations generated during the preceding matter era would enhance the abundance; we will investigate this further in future work.

E.1.  Model-independent expressions

The kination era starts when the total energy density of the Universe is dominated by the energy density of scalar field
\overline{\rho}_{\phi,\rm kin}
(at
a_{\rm kin}
) and ends when the energy density drops to
\overline{\rho}_{\phi,\rm end}
(at
a_{\rm end}
) below the energy density of the Standard Model radiation. The duration of the kination era is defined by the e-folding number of the cosmic expansion during the kination era,

N_{\rm KD}\equiv\log\left(\frac{a_{\rm end}}{a_{\rm kin}}\right)=\frac{1}{6}\log\left(\frac{\overline{\rho}_{\phi,\rm kin}}{\overline{\rho}_{\phi,\rm end}}\right).

(58)

However, after the energy density of the scalar field becomes sub-dominant, it continues red-shifting as
a^{-6}
in the radiation-dominated Universe until
a_{\rm osc}
.
The fast-rolling period of the scalar field goes through both kination and radiation eras, where the production of scalar fluctuations from the curvature perturbation is slightly different.

Let us split the fluctuations into two contributions,
\Omega_{\rm fluct}^{\rm tot}=\Omega_{\rm fluct}^{\rm I}+\Omega_{\rm fluct}^{\rm II}
, where
\Omega_{\rm fluct}^{\rm I}
and
\Omega_{\rm fluct}^{\rm II}
are the energy densities of fluctuations produced in the kination and radiation eras.
For
\Omega_{\rm fluct}^{\rm I}
, we adopt the fluctuation energy density which is generated during the kination era and is derived in Eqs. (27) and (28) of
[
24
]
,

\rho_{\rm fluct}^{\rm I}(\eta)=\frac{4}{\pi}\overline{\rho}_{\rm\phi}\int d\log k\,\left(\frac{k}{\mathcal{H}}\right)\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k).

(59)

Using that
\mathcal{H}\propto a^{-2}
during the kination era and performing a similar calculation as in the main text of this letter, the energy density of the non-relativistic radiation today reads,

\Omega_{\rm fluct,0}^{\rm I}=\frac{4}{\pi}\Omega_{\rm rad}^{0}\left(\frac{a_{0}m}{\mathcal{H}_{\rm kin}}\right)\exp(2 N_{\rm KD})\!\int_{k_{\rm end}}^{k_{\rm kin}}d\log k\,\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k).

(60)

where we only integrate the fluctuations from curvature perturbations re-entering the horizon during the kination era (
k_{\rm kin}>k>k_{\rm end}
), and
\mathcal{H}_{\rm kin}/\mathcal{H}_{\rm kin}=\exp(2N_{\rm KD})
.
For the fluctuations produced during the radiation era after the kination era ends
a>a_{\rm end}
, we can use Eq. (
6
) in the main text
and replace ‘kin’ with ‘end’,

\Omega_{\rm fluct,0}^{\rm II}=\alpha^{2}\Omega_{\rm rad}^{0}\left(\frac{a_{0}m}{\mathcal{H}_{\rm kin}}\right)\exp(2 N_{\rm KD})\!\int_{k_{\rm osc}}^{k_{\rm end}}\frac{dk}{k}\left(\frac{k}{\mathcal{H}_{\rm end}}\right)\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k).

(61)

where
\Omega_{\phi}(\eta_{\rm end})=1
.
For simplicity, assuming that
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)\approx\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k_{\rm kin})
, we obtain

\Omega_{\rm fluct,0}^{\rm tot}=\Omega_{\rm fluct,0}^{\rm I}+\Omega_{\rm fluct,0}^{\rm II}=\Omega_{\rm rad}^{0}\left(\frac{a_{0}m}{\mathcal{H}_{\rm kin}}\right)\exp(2 N_{\rm KD})\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k_{\rm kin})\left(\frac{8}{\pi}N_{\rm KD}+\alpha^{2}\right).

(62)

where
k=\mathcal{H}
,
\log(k_{\rm kin}/k_{\rm end})=2N_{\rm KD}
, and
k_{\rm end}\gg k_{\rm osc}
are used.
To connect to the model parameters which determine the cosmological history, we also provide the expression

\displaystyle\left(\frac{a_{0}m}{\mathcal{H}_{\rm kin}}\right)\exp(2N_{\rm KD})\simeq 1.49\times 10^{13}\left(\frac{m}{1\,{\rm eV}}\right)\left(\frac{10^{9}\,{\rm GeV}}{\rho_{\rm kin}^{1/4}}\right)\left(\frac{g_{*s}^{1/3}(T_{\rm kin})}{g_{*s}^{1/4}(T_{\rm kin})}\right)\exp\left(\frac{3}{2}N_{\rm KD}\right).

(63)

We see that the abundance of fluctuations produced during the kination era is enhanced by a factor
N_{\rm KD}
compared to those generated during the later radiation era.
Interestingly, for the same kination energy scale
\rho_{\rm kin}^{1/4}
, the fluctuations produced from the scenario with kination domination are enhanced by a factor of
\exp(3N_{\rm KD}/2)
, while the fluctuations in the case of subdominant fast-rolling field are suppressed by a factor
\Omega_{\phi}(\eta_{\rm kin})
.

Separating versus ordinary Noether numbers
Mátyás Domokos, Barna Schefler

Abstract
. Let G G be a finite group and K K a field containing an element of multiplicative order | G | |G| . It is shown that if G G has a cyclic subgroup of index at most 2 2 , then the separating Noether number over K K of G G coincides with the Noether number over K K of G G . The same conclusion holds when G G is the direct product of a dihedral group and the 2 2 -element group. On the other hand, the smallest non-abelian groups G G are found for which the separating Noether number over K K is strictly less than the Noether number over K K . Along the way the exact value of the separating Noether number is determined for all groups of order at most 16 16 . The results show in particular that unlike the ordinary Noether number, the separating Noether number of a non-abelian finite group may well be equal to the separating Noether number of a proper direct factor of the group.

1.
Introduction

The separating Noether number over a field
K
of a finite group
G
is
the minimal positive integer
d
such that for any finite dimensional
K
-linear representation of
G
,
any two
dictinct
G
-orbits can be distinguished by a polynomial

G
-invariant of degree at most
d
.
For a finite dimensional vector space
V
over
K
we write
K[V]
for the
coordinate ring of
V

identified with the
n
-variable polynomial algebra

K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, where the variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
represent the coordinate functions on
V
with respect to a chosen basis.
Let
G
be a finite group, and
\rho:G\to\mathrm{GL}(V)
a representation of
G
on
V
. We shall say that
(V,\rho)
is a
KG
-module
in this case;
mostly we shall suppress
\rho
from the notation and we will say that

V
is a
KG
-module
.
Sometimes we write
V_{K}
instead of
V
when we want to make explicit the actual base field.
The action of
G
on
V
induces an action on
K[V]
via
K
-algebra automorphisms,
such that the variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
span a
KG
-submodule isomorphic to
V^{*}
(the
G
-module dual to
V
),
and
K[V]
is the symmetric tensor algebra of
V^{*}
.
Consider

K[V]^{G}=\{f\in K[V]\mid\forall g\in G:\quad g\cdot f=f\},

the
subalgebra of

G
-invariants in
K[V]
.
For
v\in V
we shall denote by
G\cdot v
the
G
-orbit of
v
,
and by
\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(v):=\{g\in G\mid gv=v\}

the
stabilizer subgroup in
G
of
v
.

It is well known that for
v,w\in V
with
G\cdot v\neq G\cdot w
,
there exists an invariant
f\in K[V]^{G}
with
f(v)\neq f(w)
(see for example
[
15
, Theorem 3.12.1]
,

[
31
, Theorem 16]
or
[
22
, Lemma 3.1]
).
Therefore the general notion of a ”separating set of invariants”
(see
[
15
, Definition 2.4.1]
) reduces to the following in the case of finite groups:

Definition 1.1
.

A subset
S
of
K[V]^{G}
is called a
separating set
if for any
v,w\in V
,

f(v)=f(w)
for all
f\in S
implies that
v
and
w
have the same
G
-orbit.

The result mentioned before Definition
1.1
implies that every generating set of the algebra
K[V]^{G}
is a separating set.
In the structural study of algebras of polynomial invariants of finite groups the focus on separating sets (instead of the more special generating sets) turned out to be useful in finding strengthened versions of known theorems in the invariant theory of finite groups, see for example
[
23
]
,

[
24
]
. Considering explicit computations of invariants, the class of modular representations for which generators of the corresponding algebra of invariants is known is very limited. On the other hand, in several such cases explicit finite separating sets are known, see for example

[
47
]
,
[
35
]
.
The minimal possible size of a separating set is investigated in

[
25
]
.
In the non-modular case, an explicit separating set of multisymmetric polynomials is given in
[
37
]
.
The systematic study of separating invariants over finite fields began recently in
[
32
]
,

[
36
]
.

1.1.
Degree bounds

Endow
K[V]
with the standard grading, so the variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
span the degree
1
homogeneous component of
K[V]
. We shall denote by
\deg(f)
the degree of a
non-zero homogeneous element of
K[V]
.
Obviously,
K[V]^{G}
is spanned by homogeneous elements, so it inherits the grading from

K[V]=\bigoplus_{d=0}^{\infty}K[V]_{d}
. We shall write
K[V]^{G}_{\leq d}
for the sum of the homogeneous components of degree at most
d
. Degree bounds for generators of algebras of invariants have always been in the focus of invariant theory
(see e.g.
[
48
]
).
We set

\beta(G,V):=\min\{d\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\mid K[V]^{G}_{\leq d}\text{ generates the }K\text{-algebra }K[V]^{G}\},

\beta^{K}(G):=\sup\{\beta(G,V)\mid V\text{ is a finite dimensional }KG\text{-module}\}.

The quantity
\beta^{K}(G)
is called the
Noether number of
G
(over
K
) because
of the classical inequality
\beta^{\mathbb{C}}(G)\leq|G|
due to E. Noether
[
38
]
(the inequality was extended to the non-modular case in
[
27
]
,
[
28
]
).
Its systematic study was initiated in
[
45
]
. The results of
[
45
]

were improved first in
[
19
]
,
[
46
]

and later in

[
10
]
,
[
11
]
. In particular,

[
14
, Table 1]
gives the exact value of the Noether number for all non-abelian groups of order less than
32
, and
[
11
]
gives the Noether number for all groups containing a cyclic subgroup of index two.

Following
[
34
, Definition 2]
(see also
[
31
]
) we set

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V):=\min\{d\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\mid K[V]^{G}_{\leq d}\text{ is a separating set}\},

and

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(G):=\sup\{\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V)\mid V\text{ is a finite dimensional }G\text{-module}\text{ over }K\}.

We shall refer to the above quantities as the
separating Noether number of
G
over
K
.
As different orbits of a finite group can be separated by polynomial invariants, we have
the obvious inequality

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V)\leq\beta(G,V)
for any
V
,
and hence

(1)

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(G)\leq\beta^{K}(G).

A major difference between the Noether number and its separating version is that if the characteristic of the base field
K
divides the order of
G
, then
\beta^{K}(G)=\infty
by
[
40
]
, whereas
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(G)\leq|G|
also in the modular case
(see for example the proof of
[
15
, Theorem 3.12.1]
,
[
31
, Theorem 16]
or
[
22
, Lemma 3.1]
). For some results on degree bounds on separating invariants in
the modular case see
[
34
, Theorem C]
,
[
26
]
.
However, in the present paper our focus is on the non-modular setup, when the characteristic of the base field does not divide the order of
G
. Interest in these degree bounds (over the real or complex field)
arose also in recent applications for data science and machine learning, see

[
1
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
,

[
7
]
. We mention also that problems in signal processing motivated the study of a variant of the Noether number for rational invariants in

[
4
]
(see
[
2
]
for a very recent result on this).

In this non-modular context an interesting and generally open question is the following:

Question 1.2
.

To what extent is the change from ”generating sets” to ”separating sets” reflected in the corresponding degree bounds?

It is proved in
[
17
, Corollary 3.11]
that for an abelian group
G
and a field
K

containing an element of multiplicative order
\exp(G)
, typically

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(G)
is strictly smaller than
\beta^{K}(G)
.
It is natural to expect similar typical behavior for non-abelian finite groups as well,
but as far as we know, the only information in the literature concerning this question
is in
[
9
]
, where the separating Noether
number of the non-abelian groups of order
3p

for some prime
p
congruent to
1
modulo
3
is determined.
One of the obstacles is that the exact value of the Noether number is known only
for a limited class of groups. This is the case already for abelian groups, whose
Noether number equals their Davenport constant.
However, the separating Noether number was determined recently for classes of
abelian groups whose Noether number (Davenport constant) is not known, see

[
42
]
,
[
44
]
.
This may be a hint that perhaps the separating Noether number can be computed
also for groups whose Noether number is out of reach. As a natural start one should first
compute the separating Noether number at least for those groups whose Noether number is known.
This is done in
[
43
]
,
[
44
]

in the case of abelian groups,
and we begin this project for non-abelian groups in the present paper.
The Noether numbers of all the groups with a cyclic subgroup of index at most
2

and for the remaining groups of order less than
32

were computed in
[
11
]
and

[
14
]
, respectively.

Here we determine the separating Noether numbers for the groups
with a cyclic subgroup of index at most
2
(as well as for the direct product
of a dihedral group and the
2
-element group),
and for all groups of order at most
16
, working
over a base field
K
having an element whose multiplicative order
equals the order of the group
(note that it turns out that
the smallest non-abelian groups
G
with
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G)<\beta(G)

have order
16
).
Our arguments do not rely on computer calculations (see Section
7

for comments on possible use of computers and its theoretical limitations in computation of the separating Noether number for concrete groups).
The present work is a shortened version of our preprint
[
20
]
, whose other results (covering the remaining
groups of order less than
32
) will be published elsewhere.

2.1.
Comments on the base field

We would like to stress that the separating Noether number is more sensitive
for the choice of the base field than the Noether number.
It is shown in
[
33
, Corollary 4.2]
that the Noether number of a finite group
depends only on the characteristic of the base field, so

\beta^{K}(G)=\begin{cases}\beta^{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(G)\text{ if }\mathrm{char}(K)=p>0;\\
\beta^{\mathbb{C}}(G)\text{ if }\mathrm{char}(K)=0.\end{cases}

Moreover, by
[
33
, Theorem 4.7]
, for all but finitely many primes
p
we have

\beta^{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(G)\geq\beta^{\mathbb{C}}(G)
.

On the other hand, the behavior of the separating Noether number in this respect is very different.
For example, it is proved in
[
18
]
that for any prime order group

\mathrm{C}_{p}
we have
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathrm{C}_{p})=3
(except for

p=2
, when
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathrm{C}_{2})=2
),
whereas it is well known and easy to see that
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{C}_{p})=p

(and also
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\mathbb{R}}(\mathrm{C}_{p})=p
by

[
4
, Proposition 3.13]
).

However, let us record some general facts relating the dependence
of the separating Noether number on the base field.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
F
be a subfield of a field
L
, and let
V_{F}
be an
FG
-module.

(i)

For any non-negative integer
d
, the
L
-vector space

L[V_{L}]^{G}_{d}
is spanned by its subset
F[V_{F}]^{G}_{d}
.

(ii)

We have the inequality

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V_{F})\leq\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V_{L})
.

(iii)

We have the inequality

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{F}(G)\leq\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{L}(G)
.

Proof.

(i) It is well known, and follows from the fact that the space of solutions over
L
of
a system of homogeneous linear equations with coefficients in
F
is spanned by the
subset of solutions over
F
.

(ii) Take any
v,v^{\prime}\in V_{F}\subseteq V_{L}
with
G\cdot v\neq G\cdot v^{\prime}
. Then there exists an

f\in L[V_{L}]^{G}_{d}
with
d\leq\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V_{L})
such that
f(v)\neq f(v^{\prime})
.
It follows by (i) that there exists an
h\in F[V]^{G}_{d}
with
h(v)\neq h(v^{\prime})
.
This clearly shows the desired inequality.

(iii) is an immediate consequence of (ii).
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group.
Then we have

(2)

\displaystyle\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(G)\leq\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\mathbb{C}}(G),\text{ with equality when }K\text{ contains an element }

\displaystyle\text{whose multiplicative order
equals the exponent of }G.

Proof.

In
[
17
, Corollary 2.6]
, a characterization purely in terms of

G
(and not referring to
K
) is given for
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(G)
, valid when

K
contains an element
whose multiplicative order equals the exponent of
G
(in fact
[
17
]

assumes that the base field is algebraically closed, but the proof of the quoted result
works verbatim under the present weaker assumption on
K
).
So
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(G)=\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\mathbb{C}}(G)
when

K
contains an element of multiplicative order
|G|
.
In particular, for any
K
we have
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\overline{K}}(G)=\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\mathbb{C}}(G)
,
where
\overline{K}
is the algebraic closure
of
K
(recall the running assumption that
|G|

is invertible in
K
).
Note finally that by
Lemma
2.4
(iii) we have

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(G)\leq\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\overline{K}}(G)
.
∎

2.2.
Failure of strict monotonicity

For a normal subgroup
N
of
G
,
all representations of
G/N
can be viewed as a representation of
G
whose kernel contains
N
.
Therefore we have the obvious inequalities

(3)

\beta^{K}(G)\geq\beta^{K}(G/N)\text{ and }\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(G)\geq\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(G/N).

Moreover, both the Noether number and the separating Noether number are monotone for subgroups as well;
that is,
for any subgroup
H
of a finite group
G
we have

(4)

\beta^{K}(G)\geq\beta^{K}(H)\mbox{ (see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{schmid}{}{}]}) and }\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(G)\geq\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(H)\mbox{ (see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{kohls-kraft}{}{}, Theorem B]}).}

The first inequality (
4
) was sharpened in

[
12
, Theorem 1.2]
,
where the following strict monotonicity of the Noether number was proved:

(5)

\displaystyle\beta^{K}(G)>\beta^{K}(H)\text{ for any subgroup }H\neq G;

\displaystyle\beta^{K}(G)>\beta^{K}(G/N)\text{ for any normal subgroup }N\neq\{1_{G}\}\text{ of }G.

The results of Theorem
2.1

and
Theorem
2.3

show that the analogues of (
5
) (i.e. strict monotonicity) does not hold for the separating Noether number.
Indeed, for
K
as in the cited theorems, we have

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(\mathrm{Dic}_{8})=6=\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(\mathrm{Dic}_{8}\times\mathrm{C}_{2}),

and
\mathrm{Dic}_{8}\times\mathrm{C}_{2}
has a proper direct factor isomorphic
to
\mathrm{Dic}_{8}
.
Similar examples exist among the abelian groups.
For example, we have

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(\mathrm{C}_{6}\oplus\mathrm{C}_{6}\oplus\mathrm{C}_{6})=12=\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(\mathrm{C}_{6}\oplus\mathrm{C}_{6}\oplus\mathrm{C}_{3})

by
[
42
, Theorem 6.1]
.

2.3.
Comments on the methods used

We make an essential use of the
known results on exact values of the Noether numbers
(as summarized in
[
14
]
) of
the groups considered here. In particular, in each
case when the Noether number and the separating Noether number
happen to coincide, we just need to come up with a representation and
two points with distinct orbits that can not be separated by
invariants of strictly smaller degree than the Noether number.
In all such cases it turns out that rather small
dimensional representations suffice to provide such examples.
On the other hand, to settle the cases when the separating
Noether number is strictly smaller than the Noether number requires
elaborate work:
we need a thorough analysis of
concrete representations, discussions of stabilizers,
group automorphisms,
construction of
invariants and relative invariants, as well as
several ad hoc ideas that help to undertand the set of solutions of systems of polynomial equations. What makes these computations
feasible is that by general principles (see the notion of Helly dimension
and Lemma
6.2
below) it is sufficient to deal with representations having a small
number of irreducible summands.

-grading on

\displaystyle\iff v,v^{\prime}\mbox{ can be separated by a multihomogeneous invariant of degree }d.

We shall write

3.2.
Relative invariants

An element

is a

if

for all

(so

for all

and

). We set

For example, if

is as in (

), then
the variable

is a relative invariant of weight

.
Any multihomogeneous element in

is of the form

and

Note that

is a relative invariant of weight

.

6.1.
Reduction to multiplicity-free representations and the Helly dimension

We record a consequence of the results of
[
22
]
and
Lemma
2.4
:

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
V_{1},\dots,V_{q}
be a complete irredundant list of representatives of the isomorphism classes of simple
KG
-modules.
Assume that

|K|>(\max\{\dim(V_{j})\mid j=1,\dots,q\}-1)|G|.

Then we have the equality

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(G)=\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{q}).

Proof.

An arbitrary
KG
-module
V
is isomorphic to

V_{1}^{m_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{q}^{m_{q}}
, where

W^{m}
stands for the direct sum
W\oplus\cdots\oplus W
of
m
copies of
W
.
By
[
22
, Corollary 2.6]
we have

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V)\leq\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V_{1}^{\dim(V_{1})}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{q}^{\dim(V_{q})})
,
and by
[
22
, Theorem 3.4 (ii)]
and

[
22
, Proposition 3.3 (ii)]
we have

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V_{1}^{\dim(V_{1})}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{q}^{\dim(V_{q})})=\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{q})
.
∎

Therefore to compute
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(G)
it is sufficient to deal with multiplicity-free representations of
G
(unless possibly if
K
is a finite field with too few elements).
Lemma
6.1
can be improved for groups whose Helly dimension is
’small’. The
Helly dimension

\kappa(G)
is the minimal positive integer

k
having the following property: any set of cosets in
G
with empty intersection contains a subset of at most
k
cosets with empty intersection; this quantity was introduced in
[
16
, Section 4]
.
Here we introduce a refinement, yielding sharper results in the present applications.
Let
V=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{n}
be a
KG
-module with a fixed direct sum
decomposition. Denote by
\kappa(G,V)
the minimal
positive integer
k
such that if
C_{1},\dots,C_{n}
are cosets
in
G
, where
C_{i}
is a left coset with respect to the stabilizer subgroup in

G
of some element
v_{i}
in
V_{i}
, and
\cap_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}=\emptyset
,
then there exists
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n
with
\cap_{j=1}^{k}C_{i_{j}}=\emptyset
.
Clearly
\kappa(G,V)\leq\kappa(G)
. The following statement is a variant
of
[
21
, Lemma 4.1]
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Under the assumptions and notation of Lemma
6.1
,
there exists a
k\leq\kappa(G,V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{q})
and

1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq q
with

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(G)=\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V_{j_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{j_{k}})
.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.1
we have

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(G)=\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V)
, where
V=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{q}
.

Assume that
d\geq\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V_{i_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{i_{k}})

for all
1\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}\leq q
, where
k=\kappa(G,V)
.
Take
v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{q})
and
v^{\prime}=(v^{\prime}_{1},\dots,v^{\prime}_{q})
such that

f(v)=f(v^{\prime})
for all
f\in K[V]^{G}_{\leq d}
.
Then
(v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{k}})
and
(v^{\prime}_{i_{1}},\dots,v^{\prime}_{i_{k}})

belong to the same
G
-orbit in
V_{i_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{i_{k}}

by the choice of
d
. In particular,
G\cdot v_{j}=G\cdot v^{\prime}_{j}

for
j=1,\dots,q
. Set
C_{j}:=\{g\in G\mid g\cdot v_{j}=v^{\prime}_{j}\}
,
then
C_{j}
is a left coset in
G
with respect to the stabilizer subgroup in

G
of
v_{j}
. Moreover,
\cap_{j=1}^{k}C_{i_{j}}\neq\emptyset

(an element
g
with
g\cdot(v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{k}})=(v^{\prime}_{i_{1}},\dots,v^{\prime}_{i_{k}})
belongs to this intersection).
This holds for all
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq q
,
hence by definition of
\kappa(G,V)
we conclude that

\cap_{j=1}^{q}C_{j}\neq\emptyset
. For
g\in\cap_{j=1}^{q}C_{j}\neq\emptyset

we have
g\cdot v=v^{\prime}
. This shows the inequality

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V)\leq d
. In particular,

(10)

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V)\leq\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V_{j_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{j_{k}}),

where

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V_{j_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{j_{k}})=\max\{\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V_{i_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{i_{k}})\mid 1\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}\leq q\}
.

The projection of
V
onto its direct summand
V_{j_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{j_{k}}

induces a degree-preserving embedding of

K[V_{j_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{j_{k}}]^{G}
into
K[V]^{G}
,
hence
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V_{j_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{j_{k}})\leq\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V)
,
showing the reverse inequality to (
10
).
∎

In view of Lemma
6.2
, it is helpful to find upper bounds on the Helly dimension. By
[
16
, Lemma 4.2]
we have the inequality

(11)

\kappa(G)\leq\mu(G)+1,

where
\mu(G)
stands for the maximal size of an intersection independent set of subgroups of
G
. Recall that a set of subgroups of
G
is

if none contains the intersection of the others.

Lemma 6.3
.

If a set
S
of intersection independent subgroups in a group contains a cyclic subgroup of prime power order, then
|S|\leq 2
.

Proof.

Let
S
be an intersection independent set of subgroups of a group
G
with
|S|\geq 2
and
H\in S
cyclic with
|H|
a prime power. Take
J\in S
with
J\cap H
of minimal possible order.
Since the lattice of subgroups of
H
is a chain, for any
L\in S
we have
L\cap H\supseteq H\cap J
, hence in particular
L\supseteq H\cap J
. Since
S
is intersection independent, it follows that
L\in\{H,J\}

L\in S
was arbitrary, it means that

S=\{H,J\}
.
∎

6.2.
The use of automorphisms

The natural action of the automorphism group of
G
on the isomorphism classes
of representations of
G
helps to save some computations later,
thanks to the following statements:

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
(V,\rho)
be a
KG
-module and
\alpha
an automorphism of the group
G
.

(i)

For any
\chi\in\widehat{G}
we have

K[(V,\rho)]^{G,\chi}=K[(V,\rho\circ\alpha)]^{G,\chi\circ\alpha}.

In particular,

K[(V,\rho)]^{G}=K[(V,\rho\circ\alpha)]^{G}.

(ii)

We have

\beta(G,(V,\rho))=\beta(G,(V,\rho\circ\alpha))\text{ and }\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,(V,\rho))=\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,(V,\rho\circ\alpha)).

(iii)

Assume that
(V,\rho)\cong(V,\rho\circ\alpha)
as
KG
-modules, so there exists a

K
-vector space isomorphism
T:V\to V
with
T\circ\rho(g)=(\rho\circ\alpha)(g)\circ T

for all
g\in G
.
Then the map
f\mapsto f\circ T
induces a
K
-vector space isomorphism

K[(V,\rho)]^{G,\chi}\cong K[(V,\rho)]^{G,\chi\circ\alpha}.

Proof.

(i) and (ii): The subset
\rho(G)
of
\mathrm{GL}(V)
coincides with the subset

(\rho\circ\alpha)(G)
of
\mathrm{GL}(V)
, therefore the representations

\rho
and
\rho\circ\alpha
yield the same partition of
V
into the union of
G
-orbits,
and the same polynomials in
K[V]
are invariants (respectively relative invariants)
for the
G
-action given by
\rho
as for the
G
-action given by
\rho\circ\alpha
.
Moreover, if
f\in K[(V,\rho)]^{G,\chi}
,
g\in G
and
v\in V
,
then
f((\rho\circ\alpha)(g)(v))=f(\rho(\alpha(g))(v)=\chi(\alpha(g))f(v)=(\chi\circ\alpha)(g)f(v)
.
So if a relative invariant

has weight
\chi
with respect to
\rho
,
then it has weight
\chi\circ\alpha
with respect to
\rho\circ\alpha
.
This explains both statements.

(iii): For each
g\in G
we have

(f\circ T)(gv)=f((T\circ\rho(g))(v))=f((\rho\circ\alpha)(g)(T(v))=\chi(\alpha(g))f(T(v))=(\chi\circ\alpha)(g)(f\circ T)(v).

∎

.
Therefore it remains to prove that
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V)\leq 6
where
V=W_{1}\oplus W_{2}\oplus U
.
That is, we have to show that if
all invariants of degree at most
6
take the same value on

v,v^{\prime}\in V
, then
G\cdot v=G\cdot v^{\prime}
. So

v=(w_{1},w_{2},u_{\chi}\mid\chi\in\widehat{G})
and

v^{\prime}=(w^{\prime}_{1},w^{\prime}_{2},u^{\prime}_{\chi}\mid\chi\in\widehat{G})
.
It follows from Proposition
6.6
that replacing
v^{\prime}
by an
appropriate element in its
G
-orbit, we may assume that
w_{1}=w^{\prime}_{1}
and
w_{2}=w^{\prime}_{2}
.
If
w_{1}=0
or
w_{2}=0
, then
v
and
v^{\prime}
belong to the same orbit by Proposition
6.5
.
For any
\chi\in\widehat{G}
there exists a relative invariant

f\in K[W_{1}\oplus W_{2}]^{G,\chi^{-1}}
with
\deg(f)\leq 4
and
f(w_{1},w_{2})\neq 0

by Proposition
6.7
.
Consider the invariant
ft_{\chi}\in K[V]^{G}
: it has degree at most
5
, and so from

(ft_{\chi})(v)=(ft_{\chi})(v^{\prime})
we deduce
u_{\chi}=u^{\prime}_{\chi}
. This holds for all

\chi\in\widehat{G}
,
thus we showed
v=v^{\prime}
, as claimed.
∎

6.4.
The group
\mathrm{C}_{4}\rtimes\mathrm{C}_{4}

In this section

G=\mathrm{C}_{4}\rtimes\mathrm{C}_{4}=\langle a,b\mid a^{4}=1=b^{4},\quad bab^{-1}=a^{3}\rangle.

Assume that
K
has an element
\mathrm{i}
of multiplicative order
4
.
Then
G
has two irreducible
2
-dimensional representations (up to isomorphism),
namely

\psi_{1}:a\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{i}&0\\
0&-\mathrm{i}\\
\end{bmatrix},\quad b\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\\
\end{bmatrix},\qquad\psi_{2}:a\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{i}&0\\
0&-\mathrm{i}\\
\end{bmatrix},\quad b\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\\
\end{bmatrix}.

The other irreducible representations of
G
are
1
-dimensional, and can be labeled by
the elements of the subgroup
\widehat{G}:=\{\pm 1\}\times\{\pm 1,\pm\mathrm{i}\}
of
K^{\times}\times K^{\times}
as follows:
identify
\chi=(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})\in\widehat{G}
with the representation

\chi:G\to K^{\times}
given by

\chi:a\mapsto\chi_{1},\quad b\mapsto\chi_{2}.

Denote by
W_{j}
the vector space
K^{2}
endowed with the representation
\psi_{j}
for
j=1,2
, and for
\chi\in\widehat{G}
denote by
U_{\chi}
the vector space
K
endowed with the representation
\chi
.
Set
U:=\bigoplus_{\chi\in\widehat{G}}U_{\chi}
.
Note that none of
\psi_{1}
or
\psi_{2}
is faithful:
\ker(\psi_{1})=\langle b^{2}\rangle
,

G/\ker(\psi_{1})\cong\mathrm{D}_{8}
, and
\psi_{1}
is the lift to
G
of the only
2
-dimensional representation
of
\mathrm{D}_{8}
. On the other hand,
\ker(\psi_{2})=\langle a^{2}b^{2}\rangle
,

G/\ker(\psi_{2})\cong\mathrm{Dic}_{8}
, and
\psi_{2}
is the lift to
G
of the only
2
-dimensional irreducible
representation of the quaternion group.
The coordinate functions on
W_{1}
are denoted by
x_{1},x_{2}
, the coordinate functions
on
W_{2}
are
y_{1},y_{2}
, and the coordinate function on
U_{\chi}
is
t_{\chi}
for
\chi\in\widehat{G}
.
It is well known and easy to see that

\displaystyle K[x_{1},x_{2}]^{G}

\displaystyle=K[x_{1}x_{2},x_{1}^{4}+x_{2}^{4}],

\displaystyle K[y_{1},y_{2}]^{G}

\displaystyle=K[(y_{1}y_{2})^{2},\ y_{1}^{4}+y_{2}^{4},\ y_{1}y_{2}(y_{1}^{4}-y_{2}^{4})].

Consider the following relative invariants (their index indicates their weight):

\displaystyle r_{(-1,1)}:=x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2},\quad r_{(-1,-1)}:=x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2},\quad r_{(1,-1)}:=r_{(-1,1)}r_{(-1,-1)}=x_{1}^{4}-x_{2}^{4}

\displaystyle s_{(1,-1)}:=y_{1}y_{2},\quad s_{(-1,1)}:=y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2},\quad s_{(-1,-1)}:=y_{1}^{2}-y_{2}^{2}.

Using the above observations, one can prove by arguments
similar to the proof of Proposition
6.5
the following:

Proposition 6.9
.

For
i=1,2
we have

\beta(G,W_{i}\oplus U)=6
.

Proposition 6.10
.

We have
\beta(G,W_{1}\oplus W_{2})\leq 6
.

Proof.

We know that
\beta^{K}(G)\leq 7
by
[
14
, Proposition 3.1]
.
On the other hand, the element
a^{2}\in G
acts on
W_{1}\oplus W_{2}
as
multiplication by the scalar
-1
, hence any homogeneous element of

K[W_{1}\oplus W_{2}]^{G}
has even degree. It follows that
\beta(G,W_{1}\oplus W_{2})

is an even number, less than or equal to
7
. Consequently,

\beta(G,W_{1}\oplus W_{2})\leq 6
.
∎

Proposition 6.11
.

We have
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(W_{1}\oplus W_{2}\oplus U_{(1,\mathrm{i})})\leq 6
.

Proof.

Let
v=(w_{1},w_{2},u)\in V
and
v^{\prime}=(w^{\prime}_{1},w^{\prime}_{2},u^{\prime})\in V

such that
f(v)=f(v^{\prime})
for all
f\in K[V]^{G}
with
\deg(f)\leq 6

(here
w_{i},\ w^{\prime}_{i}\in W_{i}
and
u,u^{\prime}\in U
). We need to show that
v
and
v^{\prime}

have the same
G
-orbit. By Proposition
6.10
,
(w_{1},w_{2})
and
(w^{\prime}_{1},w^{\prime}_{2})
are in the same
G
-orbit. Replacing
v
by an appropriate element in its
G
-orbit, we may assume
that
w_{1}=w^{\prime}_{1}
and
w_{2}=w^{\prime}_{2}
. If
w_{1}=0
or
w_{2}=0
, then we have
G\cdot v=G\cdot v^{\prime}
by Proposition
6.9
.
From now on we assume that none of
w_{1}
or
w_{2}
is zero.
Set

r^{(1)}_{(1,-\mathrm{i})}:=x_{1}y_{2}+\mathrm{i}x_{2}y_{1},\quad r^{(2)}_{(1,-\mathrm{i})}:=x_{1}y_{1}^{3}-\mathrm{i}x_{2}y_{2}^{3},\quad r_{(1,\mathrm{i})}:=x_{1}y_{2}-\mathrm{i}x_{2}y_{1}.

Then
r^{(1)}_{(1,-\mathrm{i})}
,
r^{(2)}_{(1,-\mathrm{i})}
,
t^{3}
are relative invariants of weight

(1,-\mathrm{i})\in\widehat{G}
, whereas
r_{(1,\mathrm{i})}
,
t
are relative invariants of weight

(1,\mathrm{i})
.
Therefore we have the invariants

f_{1}:=t^{4},\quad f_{2}:=r^{(1)}_{(1,-\mathrm{i})}t,\quad f_{3}:=r^{(2)}_{(1,-\mathrm{i})}t,\quad f_{4}:=r_{(1,\mathrm{i})}t^{3}.

All of the above invariants have degree at most
5
.
By assumption,
f_{j}(v)=f_{j}(v^{\prime})
for
j=1,2,3,4
.
The equality
t^{4}(v)=t^{4}(v^{\prime})
implies that
u^{\prime}\in\{u,-u,\mathrm{i}u,-\mathrm{i}u\}
.
In particular, if
u=0
, then
u^{\prime}=0
, and hence
v=v^{\prime}
.
It remains to deal with the case
u\neq 0
.
Moreover,
u=u^{\prime}
if and only if
t(v)=t(v^{\prime})
if and only if
t^{3}(v)=t^{3}(v^{\prime})
.
Suppose for contradiction that
u\neq u^{\prime}
. Then
f_{j}(v)=f_{j}(v^{\prime})
for
j=2,3,4
and

(w_{1},w_{2})=(w^{\prime}_{1},w^{\prime}_{2})
imply
that

0=r^{(1)}_{(1,-\mathrm{i})}(v)=r^{(2)}_{(1,-\mathrm{i})}(v)=r_{(1,\mathrm{i})}(v).

From
0=r^{(1)}_{(1,-\mathrm{i})}(v)=r_{(1,\mathrm{i})}(v)
we conclude

0=x_{1}(v)y_{2}(v)=x_{2}(v)y_{1}(v)
. Taking into account that
w_{1}\neq 0
and
w_{2}\neq 0
,
we conclude that
0=x_{1}(v)=y_{1}(v)
or
0=x_{2}(v)=y_{2}(v)
.
Then
r^{(2)}_{(1,-\mathrm{i})}(v)=0
yields that both
x_{1}(v)y_{1}(v)
and
x_{2}(v)y_{2}(v)
are zero.
We deduce
w_{1}=0
or
w_{2}=0
, a contradiction.
∎

Proposition 6.12
.

We have
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,W_{1}\oplus W_{2}\oplus U_{(-1,1)})\leq 6

and
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,W_{1}\oplus W_{2}\oplus U_{(1,-1)})\leq 6
.

Proof.

First we deal with
V:=W_{1}\oplus W_{2}\oplus U_{(-1,1)}
.
Let
v=(w_{1},w_{2},u)\in V
and
v^{\prime}=(w^{\prime}_{1},w^{\prime}_{2},u^{\prime})\in V

such that
f(v)=f(v^{\prime})
for all
f\in K[V]^{G}
with
\deg(f)\leq 6
. We need to show that

v
and
v^{\prime}
have the same
G
-orbit.
In the same way as in the proof of Proposition
6.11
,
we may assume that
w_{1}=w^{\prime}_{1}
,
w_{2}=w^{\prime}_{2}
and none of
w_{1}
or
w_{2}
is zero.
It follows that

r_{(-1,1)}=x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2},\quad r_{(-1,-1)}s_{(1,-1)}=(x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2})y_{1}y_{2},\quad s_{(-1,1)}=y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2}

can not all vanish at
v
. Suppose that
f
is one of the above polynomials,
with
f(v)\neq 0
.
Since
f
is a relative invariant of weight
(-1,1)
of degree at most
4
,
we get that
ft\in K[V]^{G}
has degree at most
5
. Moreover, the equality

(ft)(v)=(ft)(v^{\prime})
implies
u=u^{\prime}
, hence
v=v^{\prime}
.

The proof for
V=W_{1}\oplus W_{2}\oplus U_{(1,-1)}
is similar, the only difference is that we use the relative invariants

s_{(1,-1)}=y_{1}y_{2},\quad s_{(-1,1)}r_{(-1,-1)}=(y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2})(x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2}),\quad s_{(-1,-1)}r_{(-1,1)}=(y_{1}^{2}-y_{2}^{2})(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}).

∎

Proposition 6.13
.

We have
\mu(G)\leq 2
, and hence
\kappa(G)\leq 3
.

Proof.

Let
S
be an intersection independent set of subgroups of
G
with
|S|=\mu(G)
.
Clearly
G\notin S
. If
S
contains a cyclic subgroup of prime power order,
then
|S|\leq 2
by Lemma
6.3
. From now on assume that

S
contains no cyclic subgroup of prime power order.
All such proper subgroups of
G
contain
the subgroup
\langle a^{2},b^{2}\rangle
. If
\langle a^{2},b^{2}\rangle\in S
, then
|S|=1
.
Otherwise
S
consists of two order
8
subgroups of
G
. In either case,
|S|\leq 2
.
The inequality
\mu(G)\leq 2
is proved, and hence

\kappa(G)\leq 1+\mu(G)\leq 3
holds by (
11
).
∎

Theorem 6.14
.

Assume that
K
has an element of multiplicative order
8
.
Then we have
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(\mathrm{C}_{4}\rtimes\mathrm{C}_{4})=6
.

Proof.

The factor group of
G
modulo its central normal subgroup
\langle a^{2}b^{2}\rangle

is isomorphic to the quaternion group
\mathrm{Dic}_{8}
of order
8
. Therefore
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(G)\geq\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(\mathrm{Dic}_{8})=6

(in fact we have
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,W_{2})=6
, as we saw in Section
4
).

To prove the reverse inequality, by Lemma
2.4
we may assume that
K
is large enough so that
Lemma
6.1
applies and we can reduce to the study of multiplicity-free representations.
By Proposition
6.13
we have
\kappa(G)\leq 3
, and therefore
by Lemma
6.2
it is sufficient to show that
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(V)\leq 6
if
V
is the direct sum of three pairwise non-isomorphic
KG
-modules. Assume that
V
is such a
KG
-module.
By Proposition
6.10
, Proposition
6.9
we know that
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V)\leq\beta(G,V)\leq 6
, unless

V=W_{1}\oplus W_{2}\oplus U_{\chi}
for some non-trivial character

\chi\in\widehat{G}
. The cases when
\chi\in\{(1,\mathrm{i}),\ (-1,1),\ (1,-1)\}
are covered by Proposition
6.11
, Proposition
6.12
.
The remaining representations are of the form
\rho\circ\alpha
, where
\rho
is one of the above three representations, and
\alpha
is an automorphism of
G
. So the proof is complete by Lemma
6.4
.
∎

7.
Comments on computational aspects

It is not possible to obtain the results of this paper on small groups
by computer for two reasons. They are valid in infinitely many different characteristics
and there are no known general principles for relating the separating
Noether numbers of a group over fields of different characteristic
(unlike for the Noether number, for which some interesting results in this direction
can be found in
[
33
, Theorem 4.7]
).
Moreover, our results are proved also
over fields that are not algebraically closed.
While by Lemma
2.4
(iii) one can
get an upper bound on the separating Noether number
by passing to the algebraic closure of the base field,
there seems to be no algorithm for finding the exact value of the separating Noether number
over an infinite algebraically non-closed base field
(in other words, our arguments giving lower bound for separating Noether numbers
can not be replaced by computer calculations).
Note that for algebraically closed base fields and not necessarily finite
reductive groups, an algorithm to compute a separating set of invariants is given in

[
30
]
(without a prior computation of a generating set of invariants).

However, one can in principle verify by computer for chosen base fields that our
claimed value is at least an upper bound for the separating Noether number
as follows. We can pass to the algebraic closure of the base field, and
by Lemma
2.4
(iii) the separating Noether number
for the algebraic closure is an upper bound for the separating Noether number
over the original base field.
There are computer algebra packages that can compute
minimal systems of generators for rings of invariants of finite groups.
Using a minimal homogeneous generating system of
K[V]^{G}
as input,
assuming that
K
is algebraically closed,
a simple algorithm computes
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V)
, based on the
following statement.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
V
be a
KG
-module, where
K
is algebraically closed.
Let
\mathcal{A}
be a minimal homogeneous generating system
of
K[V]^{G}=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{G}
.
Take additional variables
y_{1},\dots,y_{n}
, and denote by

\delta:K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{n}]

the map
f\mapsto f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})-f(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})
.
For a positive integer
d
we have that

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V)\leq d
if and only if for all
f\in\mathcal{A}

with
\deg(f)>d
we have that
\delta(f)
is contained in the radical of
the ideal
(\delta(h)\mid h\in\mathcal{A},\ \deg(h)\leq d)
.
Consequently,

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V)=\min\{d\mid\forall f\in\mathcal{A}\colon\delta(f)\in\sqrt{(\delta(h)\mid h\in\mathcal{A},\ \deg(h)\leq d)}\}.

Proof.

This is a straightforward consequence of
Definition
1.1
, the definition of
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V)
,
and Hilbert’s Nullstellensatz.
∎

We have implemented in SageMath an algorithm based on Proposition
7.1
.
In the case
K=\mathbb{C}
, we verified the values of
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(G)
for the non-abelian groups given in Theorem
2.3

using the online CoCalc platform
[
41
]
and some good apriori upper bounds on
the Helly dimension
\kappa(G)
or
\kappa(G,V)
(cf. Section
6.1
).

Strong CP from a Hidden Chiral Condensate
Csaba Csáki, Samuel Homiller, Taewook Youn[0.5em] csaki@cornell.edu , shomiller@cornell.edu , taewook.youn@cornell.edu, [0.5em] Laboratory for Elementary Particle Physics, Cornell University, Ithaca, NY 14853, [0.25em] School of Physics, Korea Institute for Advanced Study, Seoul 02455, Republic of Korea [0.25em]

Abstract
Models which solve the strong CP problem by employing discrete spacetime symmetries generically suffer fine-tuning and quality problems. We demonstrate that these issues are greatly ameliorated when the only source of spontaneous CP breaking is from the chiral condensate of a strongly coupled hidden sector. This is shown explicitly in a model with the SM extended by a vector-like quark family and a complex scalar portal to QCD-like dark sector with N f N_{f} families of dark fermions that confines at a high scale. The dark pions of the hidden sector are natural dark matter candidates, with the correct relic abundance obtained via freeze-in. These “confining” Nelson–Barr solutions connect phenomenological questions regarding the strong CP problem to recent developments in the understanding of confining gauge theories, and present ample room for further model building.

Introduction

Studying the nature of discrete spacetime symmetries has played a pivotal role in the development of the Standard Model (SM) and our understanding of quantum field theory.
The discovery of parity violation in weak decays presaged the structure of the standard electroweak theory, while the discovery of CP violation in the weak interactions cemented our understanding of the SM particle content and opened new avenues for testing its internal consistency.

There are two sources of CP violation in the renormalizable part of the Standard Model: the Jarlskog invariant
[
1
]
and the strong CP phase,

\theta_{\textrm{weak}}=\arg\det\big(Y_{u}Y_{d}-Y_{d}Y_{u}\big)\,,\qquad\overline{\theta}=\theta-\arg\det\big(Y_{u}Y_{d}\big)\,.

(1)

Here,
Y_{u}
and
Y_{d}
are the Yukawa couplings of up- and down-type quarks and
\theta
is the coefficient of the topological term in the QCD Lagrangian:

\mathcal{L}\supset\frac{\theta}{16\pi^{2}}\tr G_{\mu\nu}\widetilde{G}^{\mu\nu}\,.

(2)

The strong CP problem is the puzzle that, while both of these parameters are on equal footing with regards to the symmetries of the Standard Model,
\theta_{\textrm{weak}}
is
\mathcal{O}(1)
while experimental constraints on the neutron EDM require
\overline{\theta}\lesssim 10^{-10}
.

The only symmetries under which
\overline{\theta}
transforms non-trivially are the
spacetime
symmetries P and CP, under which
\overline{\theta}
is odd.
This suggests a symmetry-based solution to the strong CP puzzle, where P or CP is promoted to an exact symmetry at high scales, spontaneously broken in our vacuum.
This is particularly motivated by the existence of UV-complete gravitational theories in which P/CP arises as a discrete symmetry in higher dimensions compactified to 4d
[
2
]
(see also refs.
[

).
While parity-based models are interesting—see refs.
[

If CP is assumed to be a symmetry of the UV, the challenge in solving the strong CP problem is as much generating
\theta_{\textrm{weak}}
to be close to maximal as it is in keeping
\overline{\theta}
small.
Crucially, such a pattern would be radiatively stable once generated: the running of

in the SM arises only at seven loops

Recently, however, there has been a great deal of progress in understanding the CP phase structure of QCD-like theories at

2
CP Violation from a Confining Hidden Sector

In this section, we introduce a model in which the strong CP puzzle is resolved by the Nelson–Barr mechanism, with CP violation arising from the dynamics of a hidden sector.
Since CP is a symmetry, the
\overline{\theta}
parameter associated with QCD is zero in the UV. The important point will be to generate an
\mathcal{O}(1)
CKM phase in the SM quark mixing matrix without reintroducing
\overline{\theta}
in the IR.

We first outline a minimal model implementing this setup, describe how the CP-violating phase in the hidden sector dynamics is communicated to the visible sector, and derive the conditions under which the CKM phase can be
\mathcal{O}(1)
. We then tabulate the one-loop corrections and the effects of non-renormalizable operators. As we will see, because the CP-violation arises only in an operator of dimension three, these unwanted contributions are much more easily suppressed than in typical Nelson–Barr models.

2.1
A Minimal Model

We now describe a minimal implementation of our setup.
The model is similar to the one considered in ref.
[
25
]
, with slightly different assumptions.
We suppose the existence of a hidden sector with gauge group
{\mathrm{SU}}(N)
that contains
N_{f}\geq 1
flavors of vector-like fermion pairs
\chi_{i}
,
\bar{\chi}_{i}
,
i=1\dots N_{f}
, assumed to transform as SM singlets and fundamentals of the
{\mathrm{SU}}(N)
.

CP is assumed to be an exact symmetry of the ultraviolet theory, with the invariant topological angle of the hidden sector fixed to
\bar{\theta}^{\prime}=\pi
.
Below the confinement scale, CP is spontaneously broken by the chiral condensate,

\langle\chi_{i}\bar{\chi}_{i}\rangle\sim\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}^{3}{{\mathrm{e}}}^{i\eta}

(3)

where
\eta
is a phase that acquires a vacuum expectation value.
If the fermions have degenerate masses (i.e., when there is a very good flavor symmetry in the fermion sector), this phase will be maximal,
\eta\sim\pi/4
. See the discussion in Appendix
A
for details.
We assume that this condensate is the
only
source of CP violation in the theory.

The details of the model lie in how CP violation is transmitted to the Standard Model in such a way that an
\mathcal{O}(1)
CKM phase is obtained while keeping
\overline{\theta}_{\textsc{qcd}}\approx 0
.
For this, we consider the minimal field content elucidated in ref.
[
17
]
. We augment the SM with a set of vector-like quarks
D
,
\bar{D}
that transform like the right-handed down-type quarks of the SM.
CP violation will be communicated to the SM via four-fermion operators
(\chi\bar{\chi})(D\bar{d})
, generated via some heavy mediators at a scale
M\gg\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}
.
In our minimal setup we take the mediator fields to consist of a single complex scalar,
\varphi
.

Our final assumption is that there exists a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry under which
\bar{\chi}
,
D
,
\bar{D}
and
\varphi
are odd while all other fields (including
\chi
and all the SM matter) are even.
The renormalizable,
\textrm{SM}\times{\mathrm{SU}}(N)\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-invariant interactions are then

\mathcal{L}~\supset~(m_{\chi})_{ab}\chi^{a}\bar{\chi}^{b}+\mu_{D}D\bar{D}-V(\varphi,\varphi^{\dagger})+(\lambda_{ab}\varphi+\tilde{\lambda}_{ab}\varphi^{\dagger})\chi^{a}\bar{\chi}^{b}+(\kappa_{i}\varphi+\tilde{\kappa}_{i}\varphi^{\dagger})\bar{d}_{i}D+{\textrm{h.c.}}

(4)

where
a,b=1\dots N_{f}
, and
where CP-invariance allows us to work in a basis where
\lambda,\tilde{\lambda},\kappa,\tilde{\kappa}
are all real.
The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry forbids a renormalizable
QH^{c}\bar{D}
operator, which is crucial to the Nelson–Barr mechanism, as discussed below.
We have included an explicit mass term for
\chi
,
\bar{\chi}
that softly breaks the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry, but as we will discuss shortly, its presence is not crucial for the setup.

After the fermions condense,
\varphi
obtains a parametrically small vacuum expectation value,

\langle\varphi\rangle\simeq(\lambda+\tilde{\lambda})\frac{\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}^{3}}{M^{2}}\,.

(5)

This spontaneously breaks the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry and leads to an additional mass term for
\chi
,
\bar{\chi}
.
This additional mass term makes the CP-violating phase physical even in the absence of a bare fermion mass term, as we verify explicitly in Appendix
A
. As a result, we can take the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
to be an exact symmetry, with
m_{\chi}
and all other soft-breaking terms parametrically smaller than
\langle\varphi\rangle
above.

It is worth emphasizing that, in contrast to “ordinary” Nelson–Barr models in which a scalar coupling to
D\bar{d}
acquires a complex vacuum expectation value of
\mathcal{O}(M)
, we are assuming that
\varphi
does
not
obtain a vacuum expectation value from the scalar potential, and does so only via its coupling to the
{\mathrm{SU}}(N)
condensate. This is the essential point that ameliorates the fine-tuning issues discussed below. This also leads to a less severe electroweak hierarchy problem in the theory, as operators coupling
\varphi
to the SM Higgs,
b(\varphi^{\dagger}\varphi)H^{\dagger}H
contribute only a parametrically suppressed correction to the Higgs mass parameter below the scale of spontaneous CP breaking,
\sim\lambda(\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}^{6}/M^{4})H^{\dagger}H
.
The amount of tree-level fine-tuning in the electroweak sector is thus suppressed by
(\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}/M)^{2}
compared to the original model in
[
17
]
.

Integrating out the complex scalar leads to a number of four-fermion operators, including

\mathcal{L}\supset\frac{1}{M^{2}}\big[(\kappa_{i}\tilde{\lambda}+\tilde{\kappa}_{i}\lambda)(\chi\bar{\chi})+(\kappa_{i}\lambda+\tilde{\kappa}_{i}\tilde{\lambda})(\chi^{\dagger}\bar{\chi}^{\dagger})\big]D\bar{d}+{\textrm{h.c.}}

(6)

which, after the fermions condense, leads to an effective mass term for
D\bar{d}
that carries the CP-violating phase.

At low energies, the resulting mass matrix for the down-type quarks in the SM can be written

\mathcal{L}\supset\begin{pmatrix}Q&D\end{pmatrix}\mathcal{M}_{0}\begin{pmatrix}\bar{d}\\
\bar{D}\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad\text{with}\quad\mathcal{M}_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}m_{d}&0\\
F&\mu_{D}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(7)

where
(m_{d})^{i}{}_{j}=(\lambda_{d})^{i}{}_{j}v/\sqrt{2}
and
F
is a
row
vector with entries

F_{i}=\frac{\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}^{3}}{M^{2}}\big[(\kappa_{i}\tilde{\lambda}+\tilde{\kappa}_{i}\lambda){{\mathrm{e}}}^{i\eta}+(\kappa_{i}\lambda+\tilde{\kappa}_{i}\tilde{\lambda}){{\mathrm{e}}}^{{\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}}i\eta}\big]\,.

(8)

Here, the key point of the Nelson–Barr mechanism is manifest: as a result of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry, the upper-right block of
\mathcal{M}_{0}
vanishes. Thus, while
F
has an
\mathcal{O}(1)
CP-violating phase, the determinant of
\mathcal{M}_{0}
is manifestly real so that
\bar{\theta}=0
at tree-level.
Note that it is crucial that the coefficients appearing in front of
\chi\bar{\chi}
and the conjugate term in Eq. (
6
) are not equal, or else
F
would be real.

We next need to understand under what conditions the mass matrix
\mathcal{M}_{0}
leads to an
\mathcal{O}(1)
CKM phase for the SM quarks. Here, we follow the discussion of ref.
[
27
]
closely (see also refs.
[
17
,
24
,
25
]
).
The mass matrix
\mathcal{M}_{0}
can be diagonalized via the usual bi-unitary transformation
U_{L}^{\dagger}\mathcal{M}_{0}U_{R}=\diag(\bar{m},m_{D})
, where
\bar{m}
is the diagonal matrix of real, nonnegative SM quark masses and
m_{D}
is the physical mass of the heavy, vector-like quarks. Assuming
m_{D}\gg\bar{m}
, the upper-left
3\times 3
block of
U_{L}
can be identified as the usual CKM matrix,
V
(we are working in the flavor basis in which the up-quark masses, omitted from Eq. (
4
) for simplicity, are real diagonal).
Then, since
U_{L}^{\dagger}\mathcal{M}_{0}^{\dagger}\mathcal{M}_{0}U_{L}=\diag(\bar{m}^{2},m_{D}^{2})
, we find

(V\bar{m}^{2}V^{\dagger})^{i}{}_{j}\simeq(m_{d}m_{d}^{T})^{i}{}_{j}+\frac{(m_{d})^{i}{}_{k}F^{\dagger\,k}F_{l}(m_{d}^{T})^{l}{}_{m}}{F_{p}F^{\dagger\,p}+\mu_{D}^{2}}\Bigg(\delta^{m}{}_{j}+\frac{(\bar{m}^{2})^{m}{}_{j}}{F_{q}F^{\dagger\,q}+\mu_{D}^{2}}\Bigg)\equiv(m_{0}^{2})^{i}{}_{j}+\mathcal{O}(v^{2}/\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}^{2})

(9)

where in the last equality we have defined the effective
3\times 3
mass mixing matrix for the SM quarks,

(m_{0}^{2})^{i}{}_{j}=(m_{d})^{i}{}_{k}\bigg(\delta^{k}{}_{l}+\frac{F^{\dagger\,k}F_{l}}{F_{p}F^{\dagger\,p}+\mu_{D}^{2}}\bigg)(m_{d}^{T})^{l}{}_{j}\,.

(10)

Eq. (
9
) says that the effective mixing matrix for the SM quarks is approximately diagonalized by the CKM matrix. The upshot of this procedure is that, while the matrix
m_{d}
is real, the effective mixing matrix is generally complex, due to the complex phase in
F_{i}
, and there are no ratios of
v/\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}
in Eq. (
10
). The phase in the CKM matrix will thus be
\mathcal{O}(1)
if the two terms in parentheses in Eq. (
10
) are of similar size. This in turn requires that
FF^{\dagger}\gtrsim\mu_{D}^{2}
, which translates to the condition

\lambda^{2}\kappa_{i}^{2}\Big(\frac{\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}}{M}\Big)^{4}\gtrsim\Big(\frac{\mu_{D}}{\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}}\Big)^{2}\,.

(11)

Here, we are assuming
\lambda\sim\tilde{\lambda}
and
\kappa_{i}\sim\tilde{\kappa}_{i}
for simplicity.

There is one additional requirement to obtain an
\mathcal{O}(1)
CKM phase, emphasized by ref.
[
24
]
: the denominator
F_{q}F^{\dagger\,q}+\mu_{D}^{2}
in Eq. (
10
) is manifestly real, but we must ensure that the matrix in the numerator
F^{\dagger\,k}F_{l}
has an
\mathcal{O}(1)
phase. If only one of the
F_{k}
is nonzero, or if each entry of
F_{k}
has the same phase, the CKM matrix will be real. This is the reason that more than one real scalar degree of freedom acting as a portal is necessary.

In summary, provided the above conditions including Eq. (
11
) are satisfied, the strong CP problem is solved at tree-level. Thus far, other than the hierarchy problem associated with the new scale, the picture is quite similar to the minimal model in ref.
[
17
]
. The utility of having the CP-violation transmitted through the chiral condensate will be made more clear when we consider higher-order corrections to this setup.

2.2
Loop Corrections

While the structure of
\mathcal{M}_{0}
in Eq. (
7
) permits an
\mathcal{O}(1)
CKM phase while keeping
\overline{\theta}=0
at tree-level, this structure is spoiled by radiative corrections and higher-dimension operators.

To track these corrections, we follow ref.
[
27
]
and expand the down-quark mass matrix
\mathcal{M}_{D}=\mathcal{M}_{0}+\mathcal{M}_{1}
, where
\mathcal{M}_{1}
includes higher-order corrections, i.e., all entries of
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{-1}\mathcal{M}_{1}\ll 1
. We then have

\det\mathcal{M}_{D}=\det\mathcal{M}_{0}\times\det\big(\mathbbm{1}+\mathcal{M}_{0}^{-1}\mathcal{M}_{1}\big)\simeq\det\mathcal{M}_{0}\Big[1+\tr\big(\mathcal{M}_{0}^{-1}\mathcal{M}_{1}\big)\Big]

(12)

The corrections to
\overline{\theta}
are thus,

\Delta\overline{\theta}\simeq\arg\Big[1+\tr\big(\mathcal{M}_{0}^{-1}\mathcal{M}_{1}\big)\Big]\simeq\mathrm{Im}\Big[\tr\big(\mathcal{M}_{0}^{-1}\mathcal{M}_{1}\big)\Big]\,.

(13)

We can write
\mathcal{M}_{1}
in block form as

\mathcal{M}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{M}_{Q\bar{d}}^{(1)}&\mathcal{M}_{Q\bar{D}}^{(1)}\\
\mathcal{M}_{D\bar{d}}^{(1)}&\mathcal{M}_{D\bar{D}}^{(1)}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(14)

so that

\Delta\overline{\theta}\simeq\mathrm{Im}\Big(m_{d}^{-1}\mathcal{M}_{Q\bar{d}}^{(1)}-\frac{1}{\mu_{D}}\big(Fm_{d}^{-1}\mathcal{M}_{Q\bar{D}}^{(1)}+\mathcal{M}_{D\bar{D}}^{(1)}\big)\Big)\,.

(15)

Note that corrections to the
\mathcal{M}_{D\bar{d}}^{(1)}
mass term do not appear in this expression. In principle, these can still spoil the Nelson–Barr mechanism if the dominate over the tree-level contribution to
F
and suppress the phase in the CKM matrix. In the present model, however, these contributions are always suppressed (c.f., the variation of this setup with the CP violation in the glueball condensate in ref.
[
25
]
), so we will not consider them further.

The one-loop corrections depend on the form of the scalar potential for
\varphi
and
H
. We first consider only the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-preserving terms,

V(\varphi,H)\supset b_{1}(\varphi^{\dagger}\varphi)H^{\dagger}H+c_{1}(\varphi^{\dagger}\varphi)^{2}+\Big(b_{2}\varphi^{2}H^{\dagger}H+c_{2}\varphi^{4}+c_{3}\varphi^{\dagger}\varphi^{3}+{\textrm{h.c.}}\Big)

(16)

The only relevant assumptions on other terms in the potential is that they lead to a stable vacuum for
H
, but do not give a tree-level vacuum expectation value for
\varphi
.

Strictly speaking, we are computing finite, 1PI corrections to the dimension-five operator
\varphi^{2}\bar{d}_{j}i\sigma^{\mu}D_{\mu}\bar{d}_{i}^{\dagger}
.
This is related to the operator
\varphi^{2}Q_{i}H^{c}\bar{d}_{j}
via the equations of motion for
\bar{d}
, and subsequently contributes to
\mathcal{M}_{Q\bar{d}}
after

\big(\mathcal{M}_{Q\bar{d}}\big)^{i}{}_{j}=\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}c_{3}\kappa^{i}\tilde{\kappa}_{k}(m_{d})^{k}{}_{j}\frac{\langle\varphi\rangle^{2}}{M^{2}}

(17)

The other diagrams are evaluated similarly, and—taking
\tilde{\lambda}\sim\lambda
,
\tilde{\kappa}\sim\kappa
, and all the quartic couplings
b_{a},c_{a}\sim c
—the one-loop corrections all lead to the same order contribution to
\overline{\theta}
:

\Delta\overline{\theta}=\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}c\,(\kappa\lambda)^{2}\Big(\frac{\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}}{M}\Big)^{6}

(18)

This result could have been anticipated by noting that CP-violation arises only at the loop level via
\langle\varphi\rangle
, and that the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry requires two insertions of this expectation value.

It is clear from Eq. (
18
) that even a modest hierarchy between
\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}
and
M
will be enough to ensure that the solution to the strong CP problem is stable under quantum corrections.
This is in contrast to minimal models (c.f.
[
27
]
) where a significant suppression of the quartic couplings (e.g.,
b
in our notation) is required to preserve
\overline{\theta}\lesssim 10^{-10}
, which demands additional ingredients such as supersymmetry.

Finally, we should note that in principle, operators that softly break the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry in the potential would lead to additional one-loop contributions as well, with slightly different scaling than Eq. (
18
). For instance, the operator
a\varphi H^{\dagger}H
leads to a one-loop contribution to
\mathcal{M}_{Q\bar{D}}
with no insertions of
\langle\varphi\rangle
.
However, treating the dimensionful coupling
a
as a spurion of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, it is natural to suppose that
a\sim m_{\chi}<\langle\varphi\rangle
(the last inequality being an assumption we made in Section
2.1
), in which case this contribution is subdominant to the one computed in Fig
1
.

2.3
Corrections from Higher-Dimensional Operators

Aside from loop corrections, the Nelson–Barr solution to the strong CP problem can also be destabilized by non-renormalizable operators that spoil the texture in Eq. (
7
).
As discussed in the introduction, dimension-five operators already lead to a severe upper bound on
\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}
in the minimal BBP model, presenting a number of model-building challenges.

In the present case, when the only source of CP violation is in the chiral condensate, the quality problem is much less severe.
The only
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-invariant operators that arise at dimension-five are

\mathcal{L}\supset\frac{h}{\Lambda_{\textsc{eft}}}\varphi^{\dagger}\varphi D\bar{D}+\frac{h^{\prime i}}{\Lambda_{\textsc{eft}}}\varphi^{\dagger}Q_{i}H^{c}\bar{D}+{\textrm{h.c.}}

(19)

where
h
,
h^{\prime}
are dimensionless,
\mathcal{O}(1)
couplings and
i
, as usual, is a flavor index.

Treating these corrections as contributions to
\mathcal{M}^{(1)}
in Eq. (
14
), the first operator leads to

\Delta\overline{\theta}\simeq\frac{h\langle\varphi^{\dagger}\varphi\rangle}{\mu_{D}\Lambda_{\textsc{eft}}}\simeq\lambda^{2}\,h\frac{\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}}{\Lambda_{\textsc{eft}}}\frac{\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}^{4}}{M^{4}}\frac{\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}}{\mu_{D}}

(20)

In the minimal BBP model, by contrast, the correction to
\overline{\theta}
is
\mathcal{O}(\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}^{2}/\mu_{D}\Lambda_{\textsc{eft}})

[
27
]
.
In comparison, the correction in (
20
) is suppressed by
\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}^{4}/M^{4}
. A modest hierarchy between the CP-breaking scale and the scalar mass
M
thus allows for a much larger value for
\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}
, even if
\mu_{D}
is at much lower scales.

Accounting for the corrections from the second operator requires a more precise Ansatz for the flavor hierarchies in the couplings
h^{\prime i}
and
\kappa_{j}
. We find,

\Delta\overline{\theta}\simeq\lambda^{2}\kappa_{i}(\lambda_{d}^{-1})^{i}{}_{j}h^{\prime j}\frac{\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}}{\Lambda_{\textsc{eft}}}\frac{\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}^{4}}{M^{4}}\frac{\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}}{\mu_{D}}

(21)

The combination
h^{\prime i}\kappa_{j}
has the same flavor structure as the down-quark mass matrix
m_{d}
. Assuming these couplings have the same hierarchies as the down-type Yukawas (i.e., minimal flavor violation), the overall prefactor is
\mathcal{O}(1)
and the correction has the same scaling as Eq. (
20
) (see the similar discussion in ref.
[
27
]
).

Beyond dimension-five, there are further corrections from operators such as
(\chi\bar{\chi})Q_{i}H^{c}\bar{D}
. These are suppressed by three powers of the cutoff, and thus always subdominant to the dimension-five operators above.

The corrections in Eq. (
20
) present an immediate tension with the condition in Eq. (
11
): for a fixed hierarchy between
\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}
and
M
, a small vector-like quark mass
\mu_{D}\ll\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}
is preferred to achieve an
\mathcal{O}(1)
phase in the CKM matrix, but this
enhances
the effects of non-renormalizable operators.
In the minimal model discussed here, the
(\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}/M)^{4}
suppression in (
20
) is enough to ensure ample parameter space with a stable solution to the strong CP problem, even with
\mathcal{O}(1)
values of
\lambda
and
\kappa
, but the relationship between
\mu_{D}
,
\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}
and
M
is relatively constrained. One could easily imagine additional symmetries that forbid operators to higher dimensions,
a la
ref.
[
27
]
, but we will not rely on these here.

In summary, we see that allowing for CP violation to arise only from a hidden sector condensate automatically resolves a number of the issues with the minimal Nelson–Barr models in a theoretically compelling manner. The amount of tuning required in the model is quite modest, and the parameter space with which the strong CP problem is successfully resolved is substantial, with significant freedom to have CP spontaneously broken up to
\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}\lesssim 10^{13}\,\textrm{GeV}
, or down to scales where direct phenomenological constraints start to play a role.
In the next section, we will show that an entirely orthogonal consideration can make a sharper prediction about the scales in the model, when the hidden sector also provides a candidate for the dark matter.

3
Freeze-In Production from the CP Portal

Beyond addressing the strong CP problem, this model can also account for the origin of dark matter without introducing any additional degrees of freedom.
In this sense, it shares the same appealing features as the axion solution, which simultaneously solves the strong CP problem and provides a dark matter candidate.

After chiral symmetry breaking at the confinement scale
\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}
, the pseudo-Nambu–Goldstone bosons (PNGBs), referred to as dark pions
\tilde{\pi}
, emerge with masses given by
m_{\tilde{\pi}}^{2}=m_{\chi}B_{\tilde{\pi}}
, where
B_{\tilde{\pi}}\sim 4\pi\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}
.
Since
m_{\chi}\gtrsim\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}^{3}/M^{2}
and thus
m_{\tilde{\pi}}\gtrsim\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}^{2}/M
(where
m_{\chi}
here is the effective fermion mass including the contribution from the
\varphi
vev), it is reasonable to consider
m_{\tilde{\pi}}\gg\mu_{D}
.

If the chiral flavor symmetry is exact—i.e., in the absence of explicit mass splittings or portal interactions that distinguish the individual dark fermion flavors—the dark pions are absolutely stable.
To see this, note that the dark pions transform as an adjoint representation under the diagonal flavor symmetry
\mathrm{SU}(N_{f})_{V}
(under which
\chi
,
\bar{\chi}
simultaneously transform as conjugates), so no flavor-singlet operator involving a single dark pion can be constructed.
4
4

4

Even when explicit breaking reduces the full flavor group, an unbroken residual symmetry (for example, a
{\mathrm{U}}(1)
subgroup) can render the dark pion charged under it cosmologically stable. However, one must still ensure that this charged pion is the lightest dark
-
pion state; otherwise, charge
-
conserving annihilations (e.g.
\tilde{\pi}^{+}\tilde{\pi}^{-}\to\tilde{\pi}^{0}\tilde{\pi}^{0}
) will deplete the charged relic into neutral pions, which promptly decay. Moreover, even if the charged pion is the lightest, the cosmological evolution can be nontrivial due to up
-
scattering into (decaying) heavier dark pions, whose rates are exponentially suppressed by their mass splittings.

Unlike the discrete
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
parity, there are no irrelevant operators that can break this continuous symmetry.
While an exact flavor symmetry is not required for spontaneous CP violation, we will enforce it in what follows to investigate the dark pion as a viable dark matter candidate.

Turning to the relic abundance, given the significant hierarchy between scales
M
and
\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}
, thermal freeze-out is ineffective. Consequently, if dark pions make up the dark matter, they must be produced through a different mechanism. In this work, we focus on freeze-in
[
59
]
.

Assuming hereon that
\tilde{\kappa}\sim\kappa
and
\tilde{\lambda}\sim\lambda
to simplify the notation, the relevant interaction terms in the effective chiral Lagrangian are

\kappa_{i}\lambda\,\frac{F_{\tilde{\pi}}^{2}B_{\tilde{\pi}}}{M^{2}}\Tr\left(e^{\sqrt{2}i\tilde{\pi}^{j}T^{j}/F_{\tilde{\pi}}}\right)D\bar{d}_{i},

(22)

where
F_{\tilde{\pi}}
is the decay constant of the dark pions and
T_{j}
are the generators of the
{\mathrm{SU}}(N_{f})
flavor symmetry. The dominant freeze-in production channel is via the annihilation of
D
and
\bar{d}_{i}
into two dark pions:

D+\bar{d}\to\tilde{\pi}+\tilde{\pi}.

(23)

The Boltzmann equation describing this production is

\dot{n}_{\tilde{\pi}}+3n_{\tilde{\pi}}H\approx 3\int\text{d}\Pi_{D}\text{d}\Pi_{\bar{d}}\text{d}\Pi_{1}\text{d}\Pi_{2}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}\left(p_{D}+p_{\bar{d}}-k_{1}-k_{2}\right)|M|^{2}_{D\bar{d}\to 2\tilde{\pi}}f_{D}f_{\bar{d}},

(24)

where the factor of 3 accounts for quark flavors, and
f_{i}
is the phase-space distribution of particle
i
. Assuming the masses of
D
and
\bar{d}
are negligible compared to
m_{\tilde{\pi}}
, and approximating
f_{i}
as Maxwell–Boltzmann distributions, the Boltzmann equation simplifies to
[
60
]

\dot{n}_{\tilde{\pi}}+3n_{\tilde{\pi}}H\approx\frac{3T}{514\pi^{5}}\int^{\infty}_{4m_{\tilde{\pi}}^{2}}\text{d}s\,\text{d}\Omega\sqrt{s-4m_{\tilde{\pi}}^{2}}\,|M|^{2}_{D\bar{d}\to 2\tilde{\pi}}K_{1}(\sqrt{s}/T),

(25)

where
K_{1}
is the modified Bessel function of the second kind. The squared matrix element is

\sum|\mathcal{M}|^{2}_{D\bar{d}\to 2\tilde{\pi}}=12|\kappa\lambda|^{2}\frac{B_{\tilde{\pi}}^{2}}{M^{4}}s\,.

(26)

Note that the production rate of this freeze-in process scales approximately as
T^{6}/M^{2}
, indicating that it is UV-dominated and therefore sensitive to the reheating temperature
T_{\mathrm{rh}}
. However, the reheating temperature cannot be arbitrarily high: it must remain below
\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}
to prevent the formation of a CP domain wall
[
28
,
27
]
.
Integrating the Boltzmann equation we obtain the approximate dark pion yield (using the definitions of the yield
Y=n/s
and the expression of the entropy density
s=2\pi^{2}g_{*}^{s}T^{3}/45
):

Y_{\tilde{\pi}}\approx 0.02\frac{N_{\tilde{\pi}}}{g_{*}^{s}\sqrt{g_{*}^{\rho}}}\frac{M_{\textrm{Pl}}B_{\tilde{\pi}}^{2}}{M^{4}}|\kappa\lambda|^{2}\,T_{\mathrm{rh}}

(27)

with
N_{\tilde{\pi}}=N_{f}^{2}-1
representing the number of (stable) dark pion species. Here
g_{*}^{s,\rho}
are the effective numbers of degrees of freedom in the bath at the freeze-in temperature for the entropy
S
and energy density
\rho
respectively.
To obtain the correct relic density
\Omega_{\tilde{\pi}}h^{2}\simeq 0.12
, we set

\Omega_{\tilde{\pi}}h^{2}\approx\frac{2m_{\tilde{\pi}}Y_{\tilde{\pi}}}{3.6\times 10^{-9}\;\hbox{GeV}}\simeq 0.12.

(28)

Under the assumption that
m_{\chi}
is entirely generated by
\langle\varphi\rangle
, we derive the scale hierarchy between
\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}
and
M
required to achieve the correct relic density via freeze-in:

\frac{\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}}{M}\simeq 10^{-5}\left(\frac{4\pi}{B_{\tilde{\pi}}/\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}}\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{10}{T_{\mathrm{rh}}/m_{\tilde{\pi}}}\right)^{1/6}\left(\frac{3}{N_{\tilde{\pi}}}\right)^{1/4}\left(\frac{1}{\kappa^{2/3}\lambda}\right).

(29)

Clearly, smaller values of the parameters
B_{\tilde{\pi}}
,
T_{\mathrm{rh}}
and
\kappa,\lambda
lead to larger values of
\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}
, as the freeze-in mechanism requires a suppressed production rate.
On the other hand, a larger number of channels
N_{\bar{\pi}}
enhances the production, thus lowering the required
\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}
.
Note that, because this dark matter candidate is very heavy, self-interaction constraints are negligible.

4
Discussion and Conclusions

In summary, we have implemented a minimal Nelson–Barr solution to the strong CP problem, where the only source of CP-violation is the spontaneous breaking by the confining dynamics of a strongly-coupled hidden sector.
We demonstrated that this origin of CP-violation naturally reduces the need for fine-tuning in the model and provides a degree of protection from non-renormalizable operators that would otherwise lead to unacceptable contributions to
\overline{\theta}
.

We also showed that this model provides a natural dark matter candidate in the dark pions of the hidden sector, whose abundance is fixed by the freeze-in mechanism using the same portal that is responsible for transmitting the CP-violation to the Standard Model quark sector. There is ample parameter space where the strong CP problem and dark matter abundances are simultaneously resolved, while satisfying all the existing constraints. Furthermore, this parameter space favors smaller masses for the vector-like matter extending the visible sector. This provides a compelling target for future, high-energy proton colliders.

While we have focused on a minimal implementation of this model, the features associated with having the CP violation originate from a chiral condensate could easily be implemented in more complicated theories. For instance, the
{\mathrm{SU}}(N)
could straightforwardly be a part of a larger gauge group.
One could also further suppress the contributions from higher-dimension operators by invoking additional symmetries (e.g., the gauged combination of baryon number and hypercharge in ref.
[
27
]
).
The scalar portal could also be replaced with other dynamics to generate the four-fermion operators in Eq. (
6
); it would be interesting to study alternatives in which the hierarchy between
\Lambda_{\textsc{CP}}
and
M
is generated automatically.

One notable barrier to further model-building is the challenge in constructing a supersymmetric version of the minimal model presented here. In a supersymmetric version of the present model, the
\tilde{\lambda}
and
\tilde{\kappa}
terms in Eq. (
4
) are forbidden by holomorphy of the superpotential. As a result, there is only one term in Eq. (
8
), and all the entries of
F_{i}
have the same sign so there is no phase in the CKM matrix. Unlike minimal BBP models, this result is unchanged even if additional scalar degrees of freedom (with a
Z_{N}
rather than a
Z_{2}
symmetry) are added to the model; additional scalar mediators all communicate the same phase from the confining sector, and the CKM matrix remains real. A simple (if unsatisfying) resolution to this problem would be to add a second confining sector, but it would be interesting to explore other possibilities.

Another aspect of these theories that we have not explored is whether they can contain a mechanism for baryogenesis. This is particularly important because baryogenesis models require a
new
source of CP violation beyond the SM, and we have supposed that the only source is in the chiral condensate of the hidden sector.
One could easily construct additional portals coupling to the phase of the chiral condensate, but any further coupling to the SM (which is presumably necessary to populate the SM abundances) would result in new, higher-order corrections to
\overline{\theta}
which must be checked.
As our model easily incorporates a mechanism for producing the dark matter, it would be even more interesting to see if an extension that includes baryogenesis would also explain the apparent
\mathcal{O}(1)
coincidence between the cosmological abundances of baryonic and dark matter, as in refs.
[
63
,
64
,
65
,
66
]
.

CP Phase in a QCD-like theory with a Scalar

In this appendix, we examine spontaneous CP breaking at
\overline{\theta}=\pi

\varphi
coupled to quarks. Since
\varphi
acquires a vacuum expectation value after (s)quark confinement—effectively contributing an additional quark mass—we expect its presence to lower the critical quark mass
m_{Q,0}

To study this CP structure, we work within a softly broken supersymmetric extension of QCD, where supersymmetry breaking is realized through anomaly mediation (AMSB). As shown in Ref.
[
58
]
, AMSB QCD without the scalar field
\varphi
exhibits the same CP phase structure as ordinary QCD. We therefore expect that AMSB QCD with
\varphi
will yield qualitatively similar CP behavior to that of real QCD. For our purposes, it suffices to focus on the cases with
N_{f}=1
and
N_{f}=2
, where
N_{f}<N
.

In the case of
N_{f}<N
, the low-energy degrees of freedom are parameterized in terms of the meson superfield
\tilde{M}_{ff^{\prime}}=\bar{Q}_{f}Q_{f^{\prime}}
, where
f,f^{\prime}
are flavor indices and
Q_{f},\bar{Q}_{f}
are the quark chiral superfields whose fermion components are
\chi
,
\bar{\chi}
. The low-energy dynamics superpotential is written as

W=(N-N_{f})\left(\frac{\Lambda^{3N-N_{f}}}{\det\tilde{M}}\right)^{\frac{1}{N-N_{f}}}+\Tr\left[M_{Q}\tilde{M}\right]-\lambda\Tr[\varphi\tilde{M}]+M\varphi^{2},

(30)

where the first term is the famous non-perturbative ADS superpotential
[
67
,
68
]
.
Letting
m
denote the scale of SUSY breaking, the tree-level scalar potential takes the form
[
54
,
55
]

V_{\mathrm{tree}}=\partial_{i}Wg^{ij^{*}}\partial^{*}_{j}W^{*}+m^{*}m\bigg(\partial_{i}Kg^{ij^{*}}\partial^{*}_{j}K-K\bigg)+m\bigg(\partial_{i}Wg^{ij^{*}}\partial^{*}_{j}K-3W\bigg)+{\rm h.c.},

(31)

where
K
is the Kähler potential,
g_{ij^{*}}=\partial_{i}\partial_{j}^{\ast}K

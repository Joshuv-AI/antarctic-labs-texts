Modified TM 2 for Reproducing All Best-Fit Values of Neutrino Mixing Angles
Michael Fodroci, Teruyuki Kitabayashi

Abstract
As measurements of neutrino mixing angles continue to become more precise, it is increasingly likely that in the very near future a realistic neutrino mixing model will be required to precisely reproduce their best-fit values. In this study, a modified TM 2 mixing model which reproduces the best-fit values of all three neutrino mixing angles is proposed. The model reproduces the correct mixing angles within 1 σ \sigma of the current central values and is robust against any future changes of the best-fit values.

Basics of neutrino mixings and masses

The neutrino sector can be parametrized by three mixing angles
\{\theta_{12},\theta_{23},\theta_{13}\}
, one Dirac CP phase
\delta
, two Majorana CP phases
\{\alpha,\beta\}
, and three neutrino mass eigenstates
\{m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}\}

Theoretically, the neutrino mixings are parametrized by the Pontecorvo-Maki-Nakagawa-Sakata (PMNS) mixing matrix
[

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{matrix}U_{e1}&U_{e2}&U_{e3}\\
U_{\mu 1}&U_{\mu 2}&U_{\mu 3}\\
U_{\tau 1}&U_{\tau 2}&U_{\tau 3}\\
\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}c_{12}c_{13}&s_{12}c_{13}&s_{13}e^{-i\delta}\\
-s_{12}c_{23}-c_{12}s_{23}s_{13}e^{i\delta}&c_{12}c_{23}-s_{12}s_{23}s_{13}e^{i\delta}&s_{23}c_{13}\\
s_{12}s_{23}-c_{12}c_{23}s_{13}e^{i\delta}&-c_{12}s_{23}-s_{12}c_{23}s_{13}e^{i\delta}&c_{23}c_{13}\\
\end{array}\right).

\displaystyle s_{12}^{2}=\frac{|U_{e2}|^{2}}{1-|U_{e3}|^{2}},\ s_{23}^{2}=\frac{|U_{\mu 3}|^{2}}{1-|U_{e3}|^{2}},\ s_{13}^{2}=|U_{e3}|^{2}.

(4)

3.1
Modification

The TM
2
mixing matrix is parametrized as

U_{\rm TM_{2}}=\left(\begin{matrix}\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\cos\theta&\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}}&\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\sin\theta\\
-\frac{\cos\theta}{\sqrt{6}}+\frac{e^{-i\phi}\sin\theta}{\sqrt{2}}&\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}}&-\frac{\sin\theta}{\sqrt{6}}-\frac{e^{-i\phi}\cos\theta}{\sqrt{2}}\\
-\frac{\cos\theta}{\sqrt{6}}-\frac{e^{-i\phi}\sin\theta}{\sqrt{2}}&\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}}&-\frac{\sin\theta}{\sqrt{6}}+\frac{e^{-i\phi}\cos\theta}{\sqrt{2}}\\
\end{matrix}\right)

(11)

where
\theta
and
\phi
are real parameters. From Eq.(
4
), we obtain the following stringent constraint between
s_{12}^{2}
and
s_{13}^{2}

\displaystyle s^{2}_{12}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3(1-s^{2}_{13})}.

(12)

Since global analysis of neutrino oscillation data allows only a narrow
3\sigma
region of
s_{13}^{2}
, predicted values via the TM
2
of
s_{12}^{2}
should also reside in the same narrow region, i.e.,
0.340\leq s^{2}_{12}\leq 0.341
for NO and
0.340\leq s^{2}_{12}\leq 0.342
for IO. Yet, the predicted value of
s^{2}_{12}
almost approaches the upper limit of the
3\sigma
allowed region. Hence, the TM
2
mixing model may be excluded from neutrino oscillation experiments in the future and modification of TM
2
is necessary.

As was addressed in the introduction, the measurements of the neutrino mixing angles are becoming extremely precise. Therefore, the reproducibility of the best-fit values of neutrino mixing angles may become the first requirement for a neutrino mixing model in the very near future. In this context, we construct a modified TM
2
mixing model which can yield all three best-fit values of mixing angles.

As shown in Eq.(
4
),
s_{12}^{2}
is related to
U_{e2}
and
U_{e3}
. Therefore, if we modify the
(1,2)
element in
U_{\rm TM_{2}}
as

\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}}\rightarrow\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}+\epsilon},

(13)

where
\epsilon
is a real parameter, the best-fit value of
s^{2}_{12}
can be obtained for an appropriate value of
\epsilon
. However, this modification leads to a non-unitary mixing matrix. Thus, according to the strategy of Ref.
[
97
]
, we propose the modified TM
2
mixing matrix

\tilde{U}_{\rm TM_{2}}=\left(\begin{matrix}\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}-\epsilon}\cos\theta&\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}+\epsilon}&\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}-\epsilon}\sin\theta\\
-\sqrt{\frac{1}{6}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}}\cos\theta+\frac{e^{-i\phi}\sin\theta}{\sqrt{2}}&\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\epsilon}{2}}&-\sqrt{\frac{1}{6}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}}\sin\theta-\frac{e^{-i\phi}\cos\theta}{\sqrt{2}}\\
-\sqrt{\frac{1}{6}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}}\cos\theta-\frac{e^{-i\phi}\sin\theta}{\sqrt{2}}&\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\epsilon}{2}}&-\sqrt{\frac{1}{6}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}}\sin\theta+\frac{e^{-i\phi}\cos\theta}{\sqrt{2}}\\
\end{matrix}\right).

(14)

We note that the unitary condition
\tilde{U}_{\rm TM_{2}}\tilde{U}_{\rm TM_{2}}^{\dagger}=1
is satisfied with this modification. In addition, the original TM
2
mixing matrix
U_{\rm TM_{2}}
is obtained for
\epsilon=0
, as expected. The predicted mixing angles and Dirac CP phase from
\tilde{U}_{\rm TM_{2}}
are

\displaystyle s^{2}_{12}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+3\epsilon}{3-(2-3\epsilon)\sin^{2}\theta},

(15)

\displaystyle s^{2}_{23}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{3(1+3\epsilon)}\sin 2\theta\cos\phi}{3-(2-3\epsilon)\sin^{2}\theta}\right),

(16)

\displaystyle s^{2}_{13}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2}{3}-\epsilon\right)\sin^{2}\theta,

(17)

\displaystyle\tan\delta

\displaystyle=\frac{4+3\epsilon+(2-3\epsilon)\cos 2\theta}{2-3\epsilon+(4+3\epsilon)\cos 2\theta}\tan\phi.

(18)

A benchmark point

(\theta,\phi,\epsilon)=(2.9629~{\rm rad},5.02~{\rm rad},-0.03216)

(19)

yields

(s^{2}_{12},s^{2}_{23},s^{2}_{13},\delta)=(0.308,0.470,0.02215,287.6^{\circ})

(20)

which perfectly matches the best-fit values for all of three mixing angles and is consistent with the
3\sigma
allowed region of the Dirac CP phase for NO. Another benchmark point

(\theta,\phi,\epsilon)=(2.96196~{\rm rad},4.18~{\rm rad},-0.0322)

(21)

yields

(s^{2}_{12},s^{2}_{23},s^{2}_{13},\delta)=(0.308,0.550,0.02231,239.5^{\circ})

(22)

which is again perfectly matches the best-fit values for all of three mixing angles and is consistent with the
3\sigma
allowed region of the Dirac CP phase for IO.

3.2
Majorana CP phases

Theoretical prediction of the two Majorana CP phases is also an important issue in neutrino physics. These Majorana CP phases cannot be determined by experiments on neutrino oscillations, rather information about these phases may be obtained through neutrinoless double beta decay experiments. On the other hand, theoretical predictions of the Majorana CP phases are obtainable via the flavor neutrino mass matrix, which may be constructed from the neutrino mixing matrix.

With respect to the modified TM
2
mixing in Eq.(
14
), the Majorana CP phases and the effective Majorana neutrino mass for neutrinoless double beta decay are related through

\displaystyle m_{\beta\beta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}(\tilde{U}_{\rm TM_{2}})^{2}_{ei}m_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{9}\left\{(2-3\epsilon)^{2}m_{1}^{2}\cos^{4}\theta+(1+3\epsilon)^{2}m_{2}^{2}+(2-3\epsilon)^{2}m_{3}^{2}\sin^{4}\theta\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+2(2+3\epsilon-9\epsilon^{2})m_{1}m_{2}\cos^{2}\theta\cos 2\alpha+2(2+3\epsilon-9\epsilon^{2})m_{2}m_{3}\sin^{2}\theta\cos 2(\alpha-\beta)\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+2(2-3\epsilon)^{2}m_{1}m_{3}\cos^{2}\theta\sin^{2}\theta\cos 2\beta\right\}.

(29)

Because each of the
ei
elements are independent of
\phi
in
\tilde{U}_{\rm TM_{2}}
,
m_{\beta\beta}
is also independent of
\phi
.

If one of the neutrino masses vanishes, such as in the minimal seesaw model, only one physical Majorana CP phase survives. More concretely, we can take the diagonal neutrino mass matrix to be
M_{\rm diag}={\rm diag.}(0,m_{2}e^{2i\alpha},m_{3})
for the NO case and
M_{\rm diag}={\rm diag.}(m_{1},m_{2}e^{2i\alpha},0)
for IO. This way, the Majorana CP phase is estimated as

\cos 2\alpha=\frac{9m_{\beta\beta}-(1+3\epsilon)^{2}m_{2}^{2}-(2-3\epsilon)^{2}m_{3}^{2}\sin^{4}\theta}{2(2+3\epsilon-9\epsilon^{2})m_{2}m_{3}\sin^{2}\theta}

(30)

for
m_{1}=0
and

\cos 2\alpha=\frac{9m_{\beta\beta}-(1+3\epsilon)^{2}m_{2}^{2}-(2-3\epsilon)^{2}m_{1}^{2}\cos^{4}\theta}{2(2+3\epsilon-9\epsilon^{2})m_{1}m_{2}\cos^{2}\theta}

(31)

for
m_{3}=0
.

3.3
Magic texture

As we noted in the introduction, the neutrino mass matrix derived using the TM
2
mixing matrix,

M_{\rm TM_{2}}=U_{\rm TM_{2}}^{*}M_{\rm diag}U_{\rm TM_{2}}^{\dagger},

(35)

acquires so-called magic texture, that is - the sum of the elements in any row or column of the neutrino mass matrix are identical
[
99
,
98
]
. A general matrix with magic texture has the form

M_{\rm magic}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}a&b&c\\
b&d&a+c-d\\
c&a+c-d&b-c+d\\
\end{array}\right).

(36)

The identical value of the various sums is
a+b+c
. For
M_{\rm TM_{2}}
, the identical value is
\tilde{m}_{2}
.

In the case of the modified TM
2
model, the neutrino mass matrix is given by

\tilde{M}_{\rm TM_{2}}=\tilde{U}_{\rm TM_{2}}^{*}M_{\rm diag}\tilde{U}_{\rm TM_{2}}^{\dagger}.

(37)

The sum of elements in the first, second, and third rows of
\tilde{M}_{\rm TM_{2}}
may then be shown to be

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\left(1+\sqrt{4+6(1-3\epsilon)\epsilon}+3\epsilon\right)\tilde{m}_{2}+\frac{1}{3}\left(2-\sqrt{4+6(1-3\epsilon)\epsilon}-3\epsilon\right)(m_{1}\cos^{2}\theta+\tilde{m}_{3}\sin^{2}\theta),

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\left(4+\sqrt{4+6(1-3\epsilon)\epsilon}-6\epsilon\right)\tilde{m}_{2}+\frac{1}{6}\left(2-\sqrt{4+6(1-3\epsilon)\epsilon}+6\epsilon\right)(m_{1}\cos^{2}\theta+\tilde{m}_{3}\sin^{2}\theta)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{12}\left(\sqrt{4-6\epsilon}-2\sqrt{1+3\epsilon}\right)(m_{1}-\tilde{m}_{3})e^{i\phi}\sin{2\theta},

\displaystyle\mathrm{and}

\displaystyle S_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\left(4+\sqrt{4+6(1-3\epsilon)\epsilon}-6\epsilon\right)\tilde{m}_{2}+\frac{1}{6}\left(2-\sqrt{4+6(1-3\epsilon)\epsilon}+6\epsilon\right)(m_{1}\cos^{2}\theta+\tilde{m}_{3}\sin^{2}\theta)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{12}\left(\sqrt{4-6\epsilon}-2\sqrt{1+3\epsilon}\right)(m_{1}-\tilde{m}_{3})e^{i\phi}\sin{2\theta},

(38)

respectively. For
\epsilon=0
we recover
S_{1}=S_{2}=S_{3}=\tilde{m}_{2}
as expected.

For
\epsilon\neq 0
we define, as a measure of the degree to which the magic texture symmetry is broken, the quantity

\Delta S\equiv 1-\frac{|S_{1}|+|S_{2}|+|S_{2}|}{3|\tilde{m}_{2}|}.

(39)

Furthermore, for notational simplicity, we define the terms
A
through
E
as

A\equiv\frac{1}{3}\left(1+\sqrt{4+6(1-3\epsilon)\epsilon}+3\epsilon\right),

(40)

B\equiv\frac{1}{3}\left(2-\sqrt{4+6(1-3\epsilon)\epsilon}-3\epsilon\right),

(41)

C\equiv\frac{1}{6}\left(4+\sqrt{4+6(1-3\epsilon)\epsilon}-6\epsilon\right),

(42)

D\equiv\frac{1}{6}\left(2-\sqrt{4+6(1-3\epsilon)\epsilon}+6\epsilon\right),

(43)

E\equiv\frac{\sqrt{3}}{12}\left(\sqrt{4-6\epsilon}-2\sqrt{1+3\epsilon}\right).

(44)

In the NO case, when
M_{diag}=diag.(0,m_{2}e^{2i\alpha},m_{3})
, it can be shown that
\Delta S
takes the following explicit form

\displaystyle\Delta S_{NO}=1-\bigg[\frac{1}{3m_{2}}\bigg]\Biggr(

\displaystyle\biggr[A^{2}m_{2}^{2}+B^{2}m_{3}^{2}\sin^{4}\theta+2ABm_{2}m_{3}\sin^{2}\theta\cos 2\alpha\biggr]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\biggr[C^{2}m_{2}^{2}+D^{2}m^{2}_{3}\sin^{4}\theta+E^{2}m_{3}^{2}\sin^{2}2\theta+2CDm_{2}m_{3}\sin^{2}\theta\cos 2\alpha

\displaystyle-2CEm_{2}m_{3}\sin 2\theta\cos(2\alpha-\phi)-2DEm^{2}_{3}\sin^{2}\theta\sin 2\theta\cos\phi\biggr]^{\frac{1}{2}}

4
Summary

As the precision of measurements of mixing angles from oscillation experiments continues to increase, so to does the likelihood that the primary requirement of any neutrino mixing model will be that of precisely reproducing those angles. With this in mind, we have shown that a modified form of the previously favored TM
2
mixing matrix will accomplish this goal.

Furthermore, we have demonstrated that this model is robust against future changes to the best-fit values of the mixing angles and maintains its ability to predict them within the
\pm
1
\sigma
allowed regions under such changes. Additionally, after defining the parameter space for which the predicted angles are still in good agreement with experiment, we have used the modified TM
2
model to make predictions for the Majorana neutrino mass for neutrinoless double beta decay. These predictions are found to be generally in agreement with other such calculations, and indicate that the next generation of neutrinoless double beta decay experiments should be able to exclude the full inverted ordering region while not yet probing that of the normal ordering region. Lastly, we have investigated the degree to which our modifications break the magic texture symmetry present in the unmodified TM
2
mixng matrix. We have shown that for any non-zero deviation from the original matrix that our modifications break this symmetry. We have also explored the relationship between the degree to which this symmetry is broken and the value of the Majorana phase.

Our modified TM
2
is a successful model in that it accomplishes our goal of precisely reproducing the current best-fit vales of the neutrino mixing angles while being robust to changes - but we acknowledge that further investigations must be made into a more fundamental way of deriving this matrix.

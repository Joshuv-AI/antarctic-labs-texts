Isoperimetric inequalities in Hadamard spaces of asymptotic rank two
Urs Lang, Stephan Stadler, David Urech

Abstract
. Gromov’s isoperimetric gap conjecture for Hadamard spaces states that cycles in dimensions greater than or equal to the asymptotic rank admit linear isoperimetric filling inequalities, as opposed to the inequalities of Euclidean type in lower dimensions. In the case of asymptotic rank 2 2 , recent progress was made by Druţu–Lang–Papasoglu–Stadler who established a homotopical inequality for Lipschitz 2 2 -spheres with exponents arbitrarily close to 1 1 . We prove a homological inequality of the same type for general cycles in dimensions at least 2 2 , assuming that the ambient space has finite linearly controlled asymptotic dimension. This holds in particular for all Hadamard 3 3 -manifolds and finite-dimensional CAT ⁡ ( 0 ) \operatorname{CAT}(0) cube complexes.



1.
Introduction

The isoperimetric filling inequality for an integral
k
-cycle
T
in an
n
-dimensional Hadamard manifold
X
asserts the existence of a
(k+1)
-chain
V
with boundary
\partial V=T
and volume

\operatorname{vol}_{k+1}(V)\leq\operatorname{const}\cdot\operatorname{vol}_{k}(T)^{1+1/k}

for some constant depending only on
k

[
25
,
21
]
.
This is known to hold with the optimal constant
\operatorname{vol}_{k+1}(B^{k+1})/\operatorname{vol}_{k}(\partial B^{k+1})^{1+1/k}
, where
B^{k+1}
is the Euclidean unit ball, in a few cases, notably if
X={\mathbb{R}}^{n}

[
2
]
or if
k=2

[
40
]
.
Building on Gromov’s work
[
21
]
, Wenger
[
42
]
extended the above (non-sharp) inequality of Euclidean type to a wide class of metric spaces including all
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
spaces, that is, complete geodesic metric spaces satisfying the global triangle comparisons for non-positive curvature (see
[
6
,
9
]
).

We are interested in improvements of this inequality deriving from more specific geometric properties of the ambient Hadamard space.
If the curvature is bounded above by a negative constant, then a geodesic cone construction yields a linear isoperimetric inequality

\operatorname{vol}_{k+1}(V)\leq\operatorname{const}\cdot\operatorname{vol}_{k}(T).

In general, one expects a phase transition similar to what occurs in Riemannian symmetric spaces
X
of rank
\nu
: in dimensions
k<\nu
, cycles in
X
satisfy a Euclidean-type inequality, while for
k\geq\nu
, a linear inequality holds (compare
[
23
, §5.D]
and
[
37
,
26
]
).
In
[
23
, §6.B
2
]
, Gromov conjectured that such an
isoperimetric gap
is a universal phenomenon in non-positive curvature.
For a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space
X
, the relevant critical dimension
\nu
is the
asymptotic rank
, defined as the supremum of the dimensions of Euclidean subspaces isometrically embedded in any asymptotic cones of
X
, or in
X
itself in case
X
is proper with cocompact isometry group.
See
[
28
,
45
,
30
,
20
]
for further discussion of this invariant.

The asymptotic rank is at most
1
if and only if
X
is Gromov hyperbolic.
The case of
1
-cycles is well understood: every
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space
X
satisfies a Euclidean (quadratic) isoperimetric inequality with the sharp constant
1/(4\pi)
, and an improved inequality with a strictly smaller constant implies that
X
is Gromov hyperbolic and satisfies a linear isoperimetric inequality (see
[
22
, p. 106]
and
[
44
]
).
For cycles of dimension
k>\nu=1
,
[
31
]
and
[
33
, p. 306]
provide linear fillings under some additional assumptions, whereas in the case of a general
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space
X
,
[
45
, Theorem 1.6]
yields a
\delta
-isoperimetric inequality
, with exponent
1+\delta
, for every positive
\delta
.
For
\nu\geq 2
, the picture remains largely incomplete, although
[
45
]
shows that the higher isoperimetric filling functions detect the asymptotic rank.

Recent progress on the isoperimetric gap conjecture was made in
[
15
]
, where a homotopical
\delta
-isoperimetric inequality was established for proper
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
spaces
X
with asymptotic rank at most
2
:
every Lipschitz
2
-sphere
S
in
X
bounds a Lipschitz
3
-ball
B
with

\operatorname{vol}(B)\leq C\cdot\operatorname{area}(S)^{1+\delta},

where the constant
C
depends only on
X
and
\delta>0
.
More generally, it was shown that the
\delta
-isoperimetric inequality remains valid for fillings of Lipschitz surfaces of higher genus by Lipschitz handlebodies, with a constant depending in addition on the genus.

Here we now address the case of general
k
-cycles
for
k\geq 2
in a (not necessarily proper)
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space
X

of asymptotic rank
2
.
We consider the chain complex
{\mathbf{I}}_{*,\text{\rm c}}(X)
of metric integral currents with compact support, which comprises all Lipschitz singular chains (see Section
3
).
To cope with the missing topological control, we assume that
X
has finite
asymptotic Nagata dimension
, a variant of Gromov’s asymptotic dimension
[
23
, §1.E]
, also known as
linearly controlled asymptotic dimension
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space of asymptotic rank and most
2
and of finite asymptotic Nagata dimension.
Then for every cycle
T\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
in
X
of dimension

k\geq 2
and every
\delta>0
there exists a
V\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
with boundary
\partial V=T
and mass

{\mathbf{M}}(V)\leq C\cdot{\mathbf{M}}(T)^{1+\delta}

for some constant
C
depending only on
X
,
k
, and
\delta
.

Note that both conditions imposed on the underlying
\operatorname{CAT}(0)

space remain invariant under quasi-isometries.
With the slightly stronger assumption of finite
Nagata dimension
, the result holds analogously for fillings of Lipschitz cycles by Lipschitz chains; compare Remark
3.1
.

It would suffice to prove Theorem
1.1
in the case
k=2
, by the aforementioned
[
45
, Theorem 1.6]
, which shows that the
\delta
-isoperimetric inequality is passed on to higher-dimensional cycles.
However, we will not rely on the latter result, as our argument applies equally well to all
k\geq 2
.

We now list some instances covered by Theorem
1.1
(see Section
2
for the definitions and more details).

(A)

Three-dimensional Hadamard manifolds have Nagata dimension
3
by
[
27
]
.
Note that in this case, Theorem
1.1
can be rephrased as an isoperimetric inequality for bounded domains with finite perimeter in
X
.
It is not known whether all Hadamard manifolds have finite (asymptotic) Nagata dimension.

(B)

Finite-dimensional
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
cube complexes have finite asymptotic Nagata dimension by the argument for the asymptotic dimension given in
[
4
]
.
In this case, Theorem
1.1
implies an analogous statement for cellular chains via the deformation theorem of geometric measure theory (compare
[
7
]
).

(C)

A Gromov hyperbolic
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space
X
has finite asymptotic Nagata dimension provided that for some scale
r>0
, every
2r
-ball in
X
can be covered by a fixed finite number of
r
-balls; see
[
34
]
.
In particular, the product
X\times X^{\prime}
of two such spaces has asymptotic rank at most
2
and finite asymptotic Nagata dimension.

In view of the first item, for Hadamard
3
-manifolds, the picture is now nearly complete.
By a result of Kleiner
[
29
]
(generalized in
[
40
]
), every such manifold
X
satisfies a Euclidean isoperimetric inequality for domains with the sharp constant
1/(6\sqrt{\pi})
.
Furthermore, by (the proof of)
[
15
, Theorem 7.1]
, an inequality with a strictly smaller constant for domains of large volume implies that
X
has asymptotic rank at most
2
;
then a
\delta
-isoperimetric inequality holds for all
\delta>0
.
In addition, if
X
covers a compact manifold, then a linear inequality holds.
In fact, if
M
is a compact non-positively curved
n
-manifold, then it is known that either
M
is flat
or the fundamental group of
M
is non-amenable and hence satisfies a linear isoperimetric inequality
(see
[
1
]
and references therein); consequently, the universal cover of
M
admits a linear isoperimetric inequality for domains (compare
[
24
, Theorem 6.19]
).

We now comment on the proof strategy and the further structure of the paper. The overall approach is similar as in
[
15
]
, however the concrete arguments deviate substantially.

In Section
4
, we will discuss
minimizing
k
-simplices
spanned by the geodesic
1
-skeleton determined by
k+1
points in
X
.
These are defined iteratively by filling in area minimizing faces (integral currents) in dimensions
2,\ldots,k
.
If the asymptotic rank
\nu
of
X
is at most
2
, then it follows from results in
[
30
,
20
,
18
]
that minimizing
k
-simplices with
k>\nu
are ‘slim’ and satisfy a linear isoperimetric inequality (compare Theorem
4.4
).
This hinges on a uniform upper density bound for minimizing
2
-simplices, based on an idea from
[
41
]
.
The validity of an analogous bound for higher-dimensional simplices is unclear, and this is the only reason for the restriction to asymptotic rank
2
in our main result.
As a consequence of the linear isoperimetric inequality,
piecewise minimizing

k
-cycles with
k\geq\nu
, composed of finitely many minimizing simplices, admit efficient fillings of conical type (see Proposition
4.6
).

In Section
5
, we prove that a general
k
-cycle
T
can be approximated by a piecewise minimizing cycle
P
with vertices in
T
, in dependence of a scale
s>0
, such that the difference
T-P
admits a decomposition into finitely many ‘small’ cycles
Z_{i}
, and both the mass of
P
and the total mass of the
Z_{i}
are bounded by a constant times the mass of
T
(see Theorem
5.3
).
This result is independent of the asymptotic rank, but requires suitable controlled coverings of
T
, whose existence is guaranteed, for large scales
s
, if the asymptotic Nagata dimension of
X
is finite.

Section
6
concludes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
The same reduction as in the proof of Wenger’s Euclidean isoperimetric inequality shows that it suffices to consider ‘round’
k
-cycles
R
, whose diameter is bounded by a certain constant times
{\mathbf{M}}(R)^{1/k}
.
The approximation result from Section
5
is applied iteratively, for a finite decreasing sequence of scales.
In each step, the respective piecewise minimizing cycles are filled in by the result from Section
4
,
and the remaining cycles are further decomposed using the next smaller scale, except in the last step, when they are small enough to admit efficient cone fillings.

It remains an open question whether a linear isoperimetric filling inequality holds under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, or whether the condition on the asymptotic Nagata dimension can be discarded altogether.

2.
Asymptotic Nagata dimension

Let
X
be a metric space.
A family
{\mathscr{B}}=(B_{i})_{i\in I}
of subsets of
X
is called
D
-bounded
if every
B_{i}
has diameter at most
D
, and
{\mathscr{B}}
has
s
-multiplicity at most
m
if every set of diameter
\leq s
in
X
meets no more than
m
members of the family.
The
asymptotic dimension
of
X
is the infimum of all integers
n
such that for every
s>0
there exists a
D(s)
-bounded covering of
X
with
s
-multiplicity at most
n+1
for some
D(s)<\infty
.
Note that there is no effect on small scales as it is not required that
D(s)
tends to zero with
s
.
This invariant was introduced (and called
\operatorname{as\,dim}_{+}
) by Gromov in
[
23
, §1.E]
.
He also observed that in many cases the function
D
can be chosen linear in the large.
Accordingly,
X
has
linearly controlled asymptotic dimension
at most
n
if there exist constants
r,c>0
such that for every
s>r
,
X
admits a
cs
-bounded covering with
s
-multiplicity at most
n+1
.
Requiring the latter condition for all scales
s>0
, one arrives at the notion of
Nagata dimension
, introduced earlier by Assouad in
[
5
]
and put forward in
[
34
]
.
For this reason, the linearly controlled asymptotic dimension is also known as
asymptotic Assouad–Nagata dimension
.
For brevity, we will use the term
asymptotic Nagata dimension
.
These definitions have various equivalent reformulations (see, for example,
[
14
, Proposition 1.7]
for the asymptotic Nagata dimension).
In particular, the dimensions are not affected if the covers are taken open or the ‘test set’ of diameter at most
s
is replaced with an open or closed ball of radius
s
.

The literature on these invariants is vast, and we refer to
[
8
,
12
,
39
]
for some surveys in different directions.
Notice that the asymptotic Nagata dimension is a quasi-isometry invariant, whereas the asymptotic dimension is a quasi-isometry and coarse invariant, and the Nagata dimension is a bi-Lipschitz, quasi-symmetry, and quasi-Möbius invariant
[
34
,
47
]
.
We list some relevant facts in the context of non-positive curvature.

(1)

Hadamard manifolds.

Every
planar
geodesic metric space, admitting an injective continuous map to
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, has Nagata dimension at most
2
, and every
3
-dimensional Hadamard manifold has Nagata dimension
3

[
17
,
27
]
.
For
n\geq 4
, Hadamard
n
-manifolds of pinched negative curvature are known to have Nagata dimension
n
, and homogeneous Hadamard manifolds have finite Nagata dimension
[
23
,
34
]
.
It is an open question whether every Hadamard manifold has finite Nagata dimension.
By
[
34
, Corollary 1.8]
, every
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space with finite Nagata dimension is an absolute Lipschitz retract.

(2)

Products and Euclidean buildings.

For each of the three notions, asymptotic, asymptotic Nagata, and Nagata dimension, the dimension of a product
X\times Y
of metric spaces is at most the sum of the dimensions of the factors
[
10
, Theorem 2.5]
.
Products of
n
non-trivial metric
(
{\mathbb{R}}
-)
trees as well as Euclidean buildings of rank
n
have Nagata dimension
n

[
34
]
.

(3)

Gromov hyperbolic spaces.

Let
X
be a geodesic Gromov hyperbolic space. Then
X
has finite asymptotic Nagata dimension provided that for a single sufficiently large scale
s>0
, depending on the hyperbolicity constant,
X
has a uniformly bounded covering with finite
s
-multiplicity.
This is satisfied if
X
is doubling at some scale (unrelated to the hyperbolicity constant); see
[
34
, Theorem 3.5]
and the comment thereafter.
Furthermore, it follows from
[
11
]
(see also
[
12
, Theorem 12.1.1]
and
[
36
, Theorem 1.5]
) that if
X
is visual, then the asymptotic Nagata dimension of
X
is less than or equal to the Nagata dimension of the boundary
\partial_{\infty}X
, equipped with any visual metric, plus
1
.

(4)

\operatorname{CAT}(0)
cube complexes.

Let
X
be a finite-dimensional (but not necessarily locally finite)
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
cube complex.
Wright
[
46
]
showed that the asymptotic dimension of
X
is at most the dimension of
X
.
Subsequently, another proof of the finiteness of the asymptotic dimension was given in
[
4
]
, and the argument actually provides a linear control, thus showing that the asymptotic Nagata dimension of
X
is finite;
see Corollary 3.3 and the relation
S(x,k,l)\subset B(x,Ml)
on p. 515 in that paper.

(5)

Asymptotic cones.

The Nagata dimension, and hence also the topological dimension, of any asymptotic cone of a metric space
X
is bounded above by the asymptotic Nagata dimension of
X

[
13
]
.

For our main result, the finiteness of the asymptotic Nagata dimension will be used in two different ways to approximate
k
-cycles by piecewise minimizing ones;
see the proofs of Proposition
5.1
and Theorem
5.3
.
The latter employs the following standard fact (compare
[
7
, Proposition 6.1]
and
[
38
, Lemma 5.2]
). We provide the argument for convenience.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
X
is a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space, and
Y\subset X
is a
subset with a finite
cs
-bounded covering with
s
-multiplicity at most
n+1
,
for some integer
n
and positive constants
c
and
s
. Then there exist

(1)

a finite simplicial complex
\Sigma
of dimension at most
n
, metrized as a
subcomplex of some simplex of edge length
s
in a Euclidean space;

(2)

a constant
L>0
depending only on
n
and
c
, and an
L
-Lipschitz
map
\psi\colon Y\to\Sigma
;

(3)

a Lipschitz map
\phi\colon\Sigma\to X
with
\phi(\Sigma^{(0)})\subset Y
that is

L
-Lipschitz on every simplex of
\Sigma
, such that
\phi\circ\psi
is

L
-Lipschitz and satisfies
d(x,\phi\circ\psi(x))\leq Ls
for all
x\in Y
.

Proof.

Let
(B_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
be a finite
cs
-bounded covering of
Y
with
s
-multiplicity at most
n+1
.
Suppose that
\emptyset\neq B_{i}\subset Y
, and define

\tau=(\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{N})\colon Y\to{\mathbb{R}}^{N},\quad\tau_{i}(x):=\max\{s-2\,d(x,B_{i}),0\}.

Note that
\bar{\tau}(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tau_{i}(x)\geq s
for all
x\in Y
,
and there are always at most
n+1
non-zero summands. It follows that

\psi:=s\,\tau/(\sqrt{2}\,\bar{\tau})
maps
Y
into the standard

(N-1)
-simplex
\Delta\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
of edge length
s
,
and the minimal subcomplex
\Sigma\subset\Delta
containing
\psi(Y)
has
dimension at most
n
. Given
x,y\in Y
, there are at most
2n+2

indices
i
such that
\tau_{i}(x)+\tau_{i}(y)>0
. For each of them,

\displaystyle\biggl|\frac{s\,\tau_{i}(x)}{\sqrt{2}\,\bar{\tau}(x)}-\frac{s\,\tau_{i}(y)}{\sqrt{2}\,\bar{\tau}(y)}\biggr|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{s\,|\tau_{i}(x)-\tau_{i}(y)|}{\sqrt{2}\,\bar{\tau}(x)}+\frac{s\,\tau_{i}(y)\,|\bar{\tau}(y)-\bar{\tau}(x)|}{\sqrt{2}\,\bar{\tau}(x)\,\bar{\tau}(y)}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\,d(x,y)+\frac{\tau_{i}(y)}{\bar{\tau}(y)}\cdot(2n+2)\cdot\sqrt{2}\,d(x,y),

because
s\leq\bar{\tau}(x)
and the
\tau_{i}
are
2
-Lipschitz.
Taking the sum, we conclude that
\psi
is
4\sqrt{2}\,(n+1)
-Lipschitz.

The reverse map
\phi\colon\Sigma\to X
is constructed by induction on the
skeleta of
\Sigma
. For
i=1,\ldots,N
, choose
z_{i}\in B_{i}
and put

\phi(s\,e_{i}):=z_{i}
. For
\emptyset\neq I\subset\{1,\ldots,N\}
, let
\Delta_{I}

denote the subsimplex of
\Delta
with vertex set
\{s\,e_{i}:i\in I\}
.
Notice that
\Delta_{I}
belongs to
\Sigma
if and only if there exists
a point
x\in Y
with
\tau_{i}(x)>0
for all
i\in I
.
Then
d(x,B_{i})<s/2
for all
i\in I
, and the set of all

z_{i}=\phi(s\,e_{i})
with
i\in I
has diameter at most
(1+2c)s
.
For every
1
-simplex
\Delta_{I}\subset\Sigma^{(1)}
with
I=\{i,j\}
,
define
\phi|_{\Delta_{I}}
as the constant speed geodesic connecting
z_{i}
and

z_{j}
, so that
\phi|_{\Delta_{I}}
is Lipschitz with constant
L_{1}:=1+2c
.
Suppose now that
\phi
is defined on
\Sigma^{(i)}
and
L_{i}
-Lipschitz
on every
i
-simplex. If
\Delta_{I}\subset\Sigma^{(i+1)}
is an
(i+1)
-simplex,
then
\phi|_{\partial\Delta_{I}}
is Lipschitz with constant
L_{i+1}:=c_{i}L_{i}

for some
c_{i}>1
depending only on
i
. Hence, by the generalized Kirszbraun
theorem
[
35
]
,

\phi|_{\partial\Delta_{I}}
extends to an
L_{i+1}
-Lipschitz map
from
\Delta_{I}
into
X
. This procedure eventually yields a map

\phi\colon\Sigma\to X
that is
L_{k}
-Lipschitz on every
simplex of
\Sigma
, where
k\leq n
is the dimension of
\Sigma
.
Furthermore,
\phi
is Lipschitz (with a constant that is possibly not
bounded in terms of
n
and
c
), because
\phi
is locally Lipschitz and

\Sigma
is compact.

Now consider the composition
f:=\phi\circ\psi\colon Y\to X
.
Let
x\in Y
, and pick
i
with
x\in B_{i}
. Then
\tau_{i}(x)=s
,
in particular
\psi(x)
belongs to a simplex of
\Sigma
with vertex
s\,e_{i}
,
and

d(x,f(x))\leq d(x,z_{i})+d(\phi(s\,e_{i}),\phi(\psi(x)))\leq(c+L_{k})\,s.

If
y\in Y
is another point, then either
\psi(x)
and
\psi(y)
lie
in a common simplex of
\Sigma
, so that

d(f(x),f(y))\leq L_{k}\,d(\psi(x),\psi(y))\leq 4\sqrt{2}\,(n+1)\,L_{k}\,d(x,y),

or
\tau_{i}(y)=0
, in which case
d(x,y)\geq d(y,B_{i})\geq s/2
and

d(f(x),f(y))\leq d(x,y)+2(c+L_{k})\,s\leq(1+4c+4L_{k})\,d(x,y).

This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Metric currents

We now review the basic notions of the theory of currents in a complete metric space
X
, introduced by Ambrosio–Kirchheim in
[
3
]
.
We restrict ourselves to currents with compact support, the main reason being that the existence result for minimizing fillings of integral cycles in
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
spaces (Theorem
3.5
), which plays a central role in this paper, relies on this assumption.
It also allows to proceed similarly as in
[
32
]
, without including a finite mass condition from the beginning, in analogy to the classical theory.
The same setup is used in
[
48
]
, which provides further details.

We write
\operatorname{Lip}(X)
for the vector space of real valued Lipschitz functions on
X
, equipped with the notion of pointwise convergence with uniformly bounded Lipschitz constants, which implies uniform convergence on compact subsets.
For
k\geq 0
, a general
k
-dimensional current
T
in
X
with support in some compact set
K\subset X
is a
(k+1)
-linear functional
T\colon\operatorname{Lip}(X)^{k+1}\to{\mathbb{R}}
that is jointly sequentially continuous and satisfies
T(f_{0},\ldots,f_{k})=0
whenever
\operatorname{spt}(f_{0})\cap K=\emptyset
or one of the functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}
is constant on the
\delta
-neighborhood of
\operatorname{spt}(f_{0})
for some
\delta>0
.
There then exists a smallest such compact set
K
, the
support

\operatorname{spt}(T)
of
T
, and it follows by continuity that
T(f_{0},\ldots,f_{k})=0
if
f_{0}=0
on
\operatorname{spt}(T)
or if one of
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}
is constant on
\operatorname{spt}(f_{0})
(see
[
48
, Lemma 2.3]
).
We denote the vector space of
k
-currents with compact support in
X
by
{\mathscr{D}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
.

The
boundary
of
T\in{\mathscr{D}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
, for
k\geq 1
, is the
(k-1)
-current
\partial T
defined by

\partial T(f_{0},\ldots,f_{k-1}):=T(1,f_{0},\ldots,f_{k-1}).

To see that indeed
\partial T\in{\mathscr{D}}_{k-1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
, note that if, say,
f_{1}
is constant on some
\delta
-neighborhood
U
of
\operatorname{spt}(f_{0})
, then one can choose
\sigma\in\operatorname{Lip}(X)
such that
\sigma\equiv 1
on
\operatorname{spt}(f_{0})
and
\sigma\equiv 0
on
X\setminus U
; then
T(1,f_{0},\ldots,f_{k-1})=T(\sigma,f_{0},\ldots,f_{k-1})=0
because
f_{0}\equiv 0
on
\operatorname{spt}(1-\sigma)
and
f_{1}
is constant on
\operatorname{spt}(\sigma)
.
Note further that
\operatorname{spt}(\partial T)\subset\operatorname{spt}(T)
, and
\partial(\partial T)=0
for
k\geq 2
.

For a Lipschitz map
\phi\colon X\to Y
to another complete metric space
Y
, the
pushforward

\phi_{\#}T\in{\mathscr{D}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(Y)
of
T\in{\mathscr{D}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
is defined by

\phi_{\#}T(g_{0},\ldots,g_{k}):=T(g_{0}\circ\phi,\ldots,g_{k}\circ\phi)

for
(g_{0},\ldots,g_{k})\in\operatorname{Lip}(Y)^{k+1}
. If
k\geq 1
, then
\partial(\phi_{\#}T)=\phi_{\#}(\partial T)
.

The
mass

{\mathbf{M}}_{V}(T)
of
T\in{\mathscr{D}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
in an open set
V\subset X
is defined as the supremum of
\sum_{\lambda}T(f_{\lambda,0},\dots,f_{\lambda,k})
over all finite families of tuples such that the sum
\sum_{\lambda}|f_{\lambda,0}|
is at most
1
and has support in
V
, and
f_{\lambda,1},\dots,f_{\lambda,k}
are
1
-Lipschitz for all
\lambda
.
The total mass
{\mathbf{M}}(T):={\mathbf{M}}_{X}(T)
is a norm on

{\mathbf{M}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X):=\{T\in{\mathscr{D}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X):{\mathbf{M}}(T)<\infty\}.

For an
L
-Lipschitz map
\phi\colon X\to Y
,

{\mathbf{M}}(\phi_{\#}T)\leq L^{k}\,{\mathbf{M}}(T).

If
T\in{\mathbf{M}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
, then the set function
\|T\|\colon 2^{X}\to[0,\infty)
defined by

\|T\|(A):=\inf\{{\mathbf{M}}_{V}(T):V\subset X\text{ open, }A\subset V\}

is a Borel regular outer measure,
\|T\|(X\setminus\operatorname{spt}(T))=0
,
and if
f_{i}
is
l_{i}
-Lipschitz for
i=1,\ldots,k
, then

|T(f_{0},\ldots,f_{k})|\leq\int_{X}|f_{0}|\,d\|T\|\cdot l_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot l_{k}

(see
[
32
, Theorem 4.3]
and
[
48
, Lemma 2.4]
).
This inequality implies further that
T
extends canonically to tuples whose first entry is in
L^{1}(\|T\|)
.
In particular, for a Borel set
B\subset X
with characteristic function
\chi_{B}
,

(T\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B)(f_{0},\ldots,f_{k}):=T(\chi_{B}f_{0},f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})

defines a current
T\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B\in{\mathbf{M}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
with mass
{\mathbf{M}}(T\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B)=\|T\|(B)
.

As a basic example, every function
\theta\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{k})
with compact support
induces a current
T=\llbracket\theta\rrbracket\in{\mathbf{M}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}({\mathbb{R}}^{k})
,

\llbracket\theta\rrbracket(f_{0},\ldots,f_{k}):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{k}}\theta\,f_{0}\det[\partial_{j}f_{i}]_{i,j=1}^{k}\,dx

(the partial derivatives exist almost everywhere by Rademacher’s theorem),
with
d\|T\|=\theta\,dx
.
For a bounded Borel set
D\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
, we will write
\llbracket D\rrbracket
instead of
\llbracket\chi_{D}\rrbracket
.
Notice that
\llbracket D\rrbracket
extends the classical (de Rham) current given by integration of smooth differential
k
-forms
f_{0}\,df_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge df_{k}
on
D
.

Recall that a subset
E
of a metric space is
countably
k
-rectifiable
if
E
is the union of countably many Lipschitz images of bounded subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
.
A current
T\in{\mathbf{M}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
is
integer rectifiable
if
\|T\|
is concentrated on some countably
k
-rectifiable Borel set,
and if for every Borel set
B\subset X
and every Lipschitz map
\phi\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
, the current
\phi_{\#}(T\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B)
is of the form
\llbracket\theta\rrbracket
for some integer valued
\theta\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{k})
.
Then
\|T\|
is absolutely continuous with respect to
k
-dimensional Hausdorff measure.
Pushforwards and restrictions to Borel sets of integer rectifiable currents are again integer rectifiable.

A
k
-current
T
is called an
integral current
if
T
is integer rectifiable and, in case
k\geq 1
,
\partial T
has finite mass; then
\partial T
is integer rectifiable as well (see
[
3
, Theorem 8.6]
).
This gives a chain complex of abelian groups
{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
.
If
T\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
,
k\geq 1
, and
\varrho\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a
1
-Lipschitz function with sublevel sets
B_{r}:=\{\varrho\leq r\}
, then for almost every
r\in{\mathbb{R}}
the
slice

S_{r}:=\partial(T\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{r})-(\partial T)\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}B_{r}

is an integral
(k-1)
-current with support in
\operatorname{spt}(T)\cap\{\varrho=r\}
.
Furthermore, for every subinterval
(a,b)\subset{\mathbb{R}}
,

\int_{a}^{b}{\mathbf{M}}(S_{r})\,dr\leq\|T\|(\{a<\varrho<b\})

(compare
[
3
, Theorem 5.6]
and
[
32
, Theorem 6.2]
).

Remark 3.1
.

If
\sigma\colon\Delta\to X
is a Lipschitz map of a
k
-simplex
\Delta\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
, then
\sigma_{\#}\llbracket\Delta\rrbracket\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
; thus every Lipschitz singular
k
-chain with integer coefficients determines an integral
k
-current.
Conversely, an approximation result holds in case
X
is a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space with finite Nagata dimension:
if
T\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
,
k\geq 1
, then for every
\epsilon>0
there exists an integral current
T_{\epsilon}
induced by a Lipschitz chain such that
{\mathbf{M}}(T-T_{\epsilon})+{\mathbf{M}}(\partial T-\partial T_{\epsilon})<\epsilon
;
moreover, if
\partial T
is already given by a Lipschitz cycle, then
T_{\epsilon}
can be chosen such that
\partial T_{\epsilon}=\partial T

(compare
[
7
, Corollary 1.5, Lemma 7.4]
and
[
18
]
).

We now assume again that
X
is a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space and collect some crucial results.
Given a cycle
T\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
, for
k\geq 1
, we call a current
S\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
a
filling
of
T
if
\partial S=T
.
A geodesic cone construction yields:

Proposition 3.2

(coning inequality)
.

For every cycle
T\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
with support in a
closed ball
B(x,r)
there exists a filling

S\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
with

{\mathbf{M}}(S)\leq\frac{r}{k+1}\,{\mathbf{M}}(T).

See
[
43
, Theorem 4.1]
.
An integral current is
minimizing
if it has minimal mass among all integral currents with the same boundary.

Corollary 3.3

(monotonicity)
.

If
V\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
is minimizing and
x\in\operatorname{spt}(V)\setminus\operatorname{spt}(\partial V)
,
then

\omega_{k+1}\leq\frac{\|V\|(B(x,r))}{r^{k+1}}\leq\frac{\|V\|(B(x,s))}{s^{k+1}}

for
0<r\leq s\leq d(x,\operatorname{spt}(\partial V))
, where
\omega_{k+1}
denotes the Lebesgue
measure of the unit ball in
{\mathbb{R}}^{k+1}
.

See
[
43
, Corollary 4.4]
.

Theorem 3.4

(Euclidean isoperimetric inequality)
.

For every cycle
T\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
there exists a filling

S\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
with

{\mathbf{M}}(S)\leq\gamma_{k}\,{\mathbf{M}}(T)^{1+1/k},

where the constant
\gamma_{k}
depends only on
k
.

This is a particular case of
[
42
, Theorem 1.2]
(see also the remark thereafter regarding compact supports).
The
filling volume
of a cycle
T\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
,
k\geq 1
, is defined by

\operatorname{Fill}(T):=\inf\{{\mathbf{M}}(S):S\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)\text{ and }\partial S=T\}.

Theorem 3.5

(minimizing fillings)
.

For every cycle
T\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
there exists a filling
S\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
with

{\mathbf{M}}(S)=\operatorname{Fill}(T)
.

See
[
42
, Theorem 1.6]
. Notice that here
X
is still a general (complete but not necessarily locally compact)
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space; the compactness of
\operatorname{spt}(T)
suffices.

4.
Minimizing simplices

This section is based in part on
[
20
]
and
[
19
, Chapter 8]
.

Let again
X
be a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space.
We define minimizing simplices inductively as follows.
For
x_{0}\in X
,
\llbracket x_{0}\rrbracket\in{\mathbf{I}}_{0,\text{\rm c}}(X)
is defined by

\llbracket x_{0}\rrbracket(f):=f(x_{0}).

For
x_{0},x_{1}\in X
,
\llbracket x_{0},x_{1}\rrbracket\in{\mathbf{I}}_{1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
is the current induced
by the geodesic
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to X
from
x_{0}
to
x_{1}
,

\llbracket x_{0},x_{1}\rrbracket(f,g):=\int_{0}^{1}(f\circ\gamma)(g\circ\gamma)^{\prime}\,dt,

whose boundary is
\llbracket x_{1}\rrbracket-\llbracket x_{0}\rrbracket
. For
x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}\in X
,
a
minimizing triangle

\llbracket x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}\rrbracket\in{\mathbf{I}}_{2,\text{\rm c}}(X)

is a minimizing filling of the cycle

\llbracket x_{0},x_{1}\rrbracket+\llbracket x_{1},x_{2}\rrbracket+\llbracket x_{2},x_{0}\rrbracket
.
In general, for
k\geq 2
, a
minimizing
k
-simplex

\llbracket x_{0},\ldots,x_{k}\rrbracket\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
with vertices

x_{0},\ldots,x_{k}\in X
is a minimizing filling of the cycle

\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{k}\,\llbracket x_{0},\ldots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{k}\rrbracket,

where each
\llbracket x_{0},\ldots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{k}\rrbracket
is a minimizing

(k-1)
-simplex.
This uses Theorem
3.5
, which does not grant uniqueness.
It is unclear if minimizing fillings of geodesic triangles in
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
spaces
are unique and if they are of the type of the disc. However, in the case
of
X={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, the construction provides the standard affine simplices
(compare
[
16
, p. 365]
).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
X
be a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space.
If
P=\llbracket x_{0},\ldots,x_{k}\rrbracket\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
is a minimizing
k
-simplex
with
\operatorname{diam}\{x_{0},\ldots,x_{k}\}\leq s
, then
{\mathbf{M}}(P)\leq c_{k}s^{k}

for some constant
c_{k}
depending only on
k
.

Proof.

For
k=2
, one can take
c_{2}=1/2
by Proposition
3.2
.
The general case follows by induction on
k
from Theorem
3.4
.
∎

For a current
T\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
and constants
\theta,\varrho\geq 0
,
we say that
T
has
(\theta,\varrho)
-controlled density
if

\|T\|(B(x,r))\leq\theta\,r^{k}

for all
x\in X
and
r>\varrho
.
The following lemma and proposition are the only reason for the restriction
to asymptotic rank two in this paper.

Lemma 4.2
.

In a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space
X
, every minimizing

1
-simplex has
(2,0)
-controlled density, and every
minimizing
2
-simplex has
(3\pi/2,0)
-controlled density.

Proof.

For
2
-simplices, this uses an extension trick from
[
41
]
based on
Reshetnyak’s gluing theorem for
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
spaces, so that one can apply
Corollary
3.3
. See
[
19
, Lemma 8.4]
.
∎

In contrast to Lemma
4.2
,
[
19
, Example 8.6]

shows that the filling of a geodesic triangle given by the geodesic
cone from some vertex over the opposite side may fail to have uniformly
bounded density.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
X
be a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space with asymptotic rank
\nu\in\{1,2\}
.
Then for all
k>\nu
and
\theta>0
there exists a constant

\varrho\geq 0
such that every minimizing
k
-simplex
P\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
has

(\theta,\varrho)
-controlled density.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
we can assume inductively that every facet of
P
(of dimension
k-1\geq\nu
) has
(\theta^{\prime},\varrho^{\prime})
-controlled density for some
\theta^{\prime},\varrho^{\prime}\geq 0
.
Then
\partial P
has
((k+1)\theta^{\prime},\varrho^{\prime})
-controlled density, and it follows as in
[
20
, Proposition 5.2]
(where
X
is assumed to be proper) that
P
has
(\theta,\varrho)
-controlled density for some constant
\varrho=\varrho(X,k,\theta^{\prime},\varrho^{\prime},\theta)
.
The argument relies on the sub-Euclidean isoperimetric inequality
[
45
, Theorem 1.2]
and applies verbatim.
∎

We now have the following analogue of the slim triangle property in Gromov hyperbolic spaces.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
X
be a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space with asymptotic rank
\nu\in\{1,2\}
,
and let
k>\nu
. Then there exists a constant
D_{k}\geq 0
such that the
following holds for every minimizing
k
-simplex

P=\llbracket x_{0},\ldots,x_{k}\rrbracket\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)

with facets
P^{\prime}_{i}=\llbracket x_{0},\ldots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{k}\rrbracket
:

(1)

{\mathbf{M}}(P)\leq D_{k}\,{\mathbf{M}}(\partial P)
;

(2)

\operatorname{spt}(P)
is within distance at most
D_{k}
from
\operatorname{spt}(\partial P)
;

(3)

for every
i\in\{0,\ldots,k\}
,
\operatorname{spt}(P^{\prime}_{i})
lies in the
closed
D_{k}
-neighborhood of
\bigcup_{j\neq i}\operatorname{spt}(P^{\prime}_{j})
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
and Proposition
4.3
,

\partial P
is a
(k-1)
-cycle with uniformly controlled density.
Hence, assertions (1), (2), (3) follow from
the implications (AR
k-1
)
\Rightarrow
(LII
k-1
), (FR
k-1
),
(ML
k-1
) in
[
20
, Theorem 1.1]
, the last of which was shown
earlier in
[
30
, Theorem 5.1]
. The second implication holds
for every minimizing filling of the cycle by
[
20
, Theorem 7.3]
.
In these references,
X
is assumed to be a
proper
metric space satisfying
coning inequalities, mainly to guarantee the existence of minimizing fillings.
By Theorem
3.5
and Proposition
3.2
, the arguments
apply to general
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
spaces.
∎

As a consequence of inequality (1), the mass of a minimizing
k
-simplex
with
k\geq\nu\in\{1,2\}
behaves like a
\nu
-dimensional quantity:

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
X
be a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space with asymptotic rank
\nu\in\{1,2\}
,
and let
k\geq\nu
. Then there exists a constant
C_{k}
such that every minimizing

k
-simplex
P=\llbracket x_{0},\ldots,x_{k}\rrbracket
in
X
with
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in B(x_{0},r)

satisfies
{\mathbf{M}}(P)\leq C_{k}\,r^{\nu}
; furthermore, if
\nu=2
and

\operatorname{diam}\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}\leq s
, then
{\mathbf{M}}(P)\leq C_{k}\,rs
.

Proof.

This follows by induction on
k
. We just prove the last assertion.
By Proposition
3.2
, every minimizing
triangle
P^{\prime}=\llbracket x_{0}^{\prime},x^{\prime}_{1},x^{\prime}_{2}\rrbracket
in
X
with

x^{\prime}_{1},x^{\prime}_{2}\in B(x^{\prime}_{0},r)
and
d(x^{\prime}_{1},x^{\prime}_{2})\leq s
has
mass
{\mathbf{M}}(P^{\prime})\leq rs/2
. Suppose now that
k>\nu=2
and the inequality

{\mathbf{M}}(P^{\prime})\leq C_{k-1}\,rs
holds for all minimizing
(k-1)
-simplices

P^{\prime}=\llbracket x^{\prime}_{0},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{k-1}\rrbracket
in
X
with
x^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{k-1}\in B(x_{0},r)

and
\operatorname{diam}\{x^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{k-1}\}\leq s
.
Then, for
P
as in the statement, assuming that
s\leq 2r
, we have

{\mathbf{M}}(\partial P)\leq C_{k-1}\,(k\,rs+s^{2})\leq(k+2)\,C_{k-1}\,rs,

and it follows from the first assertion in
Theorem
4.4
that
{\mathbf{M}}(P)\leq C_{k}\,rs
for

C_{k}:=(k+2)\,C_{k-1}D_{k}
.
∎

By a
piecewise minimizing
current
P\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
we mean
an integral linear combination

P=\sum_{i=1}^{N}m_{i}P_{i},\quad 0<m_{1},\ldots,m_{N}\in{\mathbb{Z}},

of some minimizing
k
-simplices
P_{1},\ldots,P_{N}
(in particular, the boundary of a minimizing
simplex is piecewise minimizing in this sense).
For such a representation, we write

|P|_{1}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}m_{i}

and denote by
\operatorname{mesh}(P)
the maximal edge length of all simplices
P_{i}
.
Note that

{\mathbf{M}}(P)\leq c_{k}\operatorname{mesh}(P)^{k}\,|P|_{1}

by Lemma
4.1
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
X
be a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space with asymptotic rank
\nu\in\{1,2\}
,
and let
k\geq\nu
. Then every piecewise minimizing cycle
P\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)

with vertex set
V
has a piecewise minimizing filling
Q\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
with

{\mathbf{M}}(Q)\leq C_{k+1}\operatorname{diam}(V)\operatorname{mesh}(P)^{\nu-1}\,|P|_{1}.

Proof.

Suppose that
P=\sum_{i=1}^{N}m_{i}P_{i}
as above.
Fix
z\in V
and complete every minimizing
k
-simplex
P_{i}

to a minimizing
(k+1)
-simplex
Q_{i}
with the additional vertex
z
,
in a compatible way, such that
Q:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}m_{i}Q_{i}

is a filling of
P
. By Corollary
4.5
,

{\mathbf{M}}(Q_{i})\leq C_{k+1}\operatorname{diam}(V)\operatorname{mesh}(P)^{\nu-1},

and since
|Q|_{1}=|P|_{1}
, the result follows.
∎

5.
Piecewise minimizing approximation

Our next goal is to prove Theorem
5.3
below.
The results in this section are independent of the asymptotic rank.

We start with the following result for displacements of cycles.
Here and subsequently, we will simply write
\operatorname{diam}(R)
rather than
\operatorname{diam}(\operatorname{spt}(R))

for the diameter of (the support of) a current
R\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
X
be a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space.
Suppose that
T\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
is a cycle of dimension
k\geq 1
,
and
(B_{i})_{i=1}^{J}
is a finite
cs
-bounded covering of
\operatorname{spt}(T)

with
s
-multiplicity at most
n+1
, for some
n,c,s>0
.
Suppose further that
f\colon\operatorname{spt}(T)\to X
is an

L
-Lipschitz map such that
d(x,f(x))\leq Ls
for all
x\in\operatorname{spt}(T)
.
Then, for some constant
A
depending only on
k,n,c
and
L
,
there exist cycles
R_{1},\ldots,R_{J}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)

with
\operatorname{diam}(R_{i})\leq As
such that

T-f_{\#}T=\sum_{i=1}^{J}R_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{J}{\mathbf{M}}(R_{i})\leq A\,{\mathbf{M}}(T).

Proof.

We assume that the
B_{i}
are non-empty subsets of
\operatorname{spt}(T)
.
Let
\varrho_{i}\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
be the distance function to
B_{i}
, and let

W_{i}:=\{x\in X:\varrho_{i}(x)<s/2\}.

Note that every
x\in X
belongs to at most
n+1
members of

(W_{i})_{i=1}^{J}
. We claim that for
j=1,\ldots,J
there exist

T_{j},U_{j}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
and decompositions

T=\sum_{i=1}^{j}T_{i}+U_{j}

such that
{\mathbf{M}}(T)=\sum_{i=1}^{j}{\mathbf{M}}(T_{i})+{\mathbf{M}}(U_{j})
and

\sum_{i=1}^{j}{\mathbf{M}}(\partial T_{i})+{\mathbf{M}}(\partial U_{j})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{j}4s^{-1}\|T\|(W_{i}).

For
j=1
, there exists
r_{1}\in(0,s/2)
such that

T_{1}:=T\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}\{\varrho_{1}\leq r_{1}\}
and
U_{1}:=T-T_{1}

satisfy
{\mathbf{M}}(\partial U_{1})={\mathbf{M}}(\partial T_{1})\leq 2s^{-1}\|T\|(W_{1})
.
Now let
j\geq 2
. Given the decomposition for
j-1
,
we choose the splitting
U_{j-1}=T_{j}+U_{j}
such that

T_{j}:=U_{j-1}\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}\{\varrho_{j}\leq r_{j}\}
for some
r_{j}\in(0,s/2)
and
the slice

S_{j}:=\partial T_{j}-(\partial U_{j-1})\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}\{\varrho_{j}\leq r_{j}\}=(\partial U_{j-1})\mathop{\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}}\{\varrho_{j}>r_{j}\}-\partial U_{j}

satisfies
{\mathbf{M}}(S_{j})\leq 2s^{-1}\|T\|(W_{j})
. Then

\displaystyle{\mathbf{M}}(\partial T_{j})+{\mathbf{M}}(\partial U_{j})

\displaystyle\leq\|\partial U_{j-1}\|(\{\varrho_{j}\leq r_{j}\})+{\mathbf{M}}(S_{j})

\displaystyle\qquad+\|\partial U_{j-1}\|(\{\varrho_{j}>r_{j}\})+{\mathbf{M}}(S_{j})

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbf{M}}(\partial U_{j-1})+4s^{-1}\|T\|(W_{j}).

This yields the claim. Note that
\operatorname{spt}(T_{j})\subset\{\varrho_{j}\leq r_{j}\}
and

\operatorname{spt}(U_{j})\subset\operatorname{spt}(T)\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{j}B_{i}
for
j=1,\ldots,J
,
in particular
\operatorname{diam}(T_{j})\leq(c+1)s
and
U_{J}=0
.
Now, since
(W_{i})_{i=1}^{J}
has multiplicity at most
n+1
, it follows that

\sum_{i=1}^{J}{\mathbf{M}}(\partial T_{i})\leq 4(n+1)s^{-1}\,{\mathbf{M}}(T).

For every
i
, the geodesic homotopy
h\colon[0,1]\times X\to X

from
\operatorname{id}_{X}
to
f
provides
a ‘cylinder’
Z_{i}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
with

\partial Z_{i}=f_{\#}(\partial T_{i})-\partial T_{i}
and

{\mathbf{M}}(Z_{i})\leq kL^{k}s\,{\mathbf{M}}(\partial T_{i})

(see
[
42
, Sect. 2.3]
). Putting
R_{i}:=T_{i}+Z_{i}-f_{\#}T_{i}
, we get

\sum_{i=1}^{J}{\mathbf{M}}(R_{i})\leq\bigl(1+4(n+1)kL^{k}+L^{k}\bigr)\,{\mathbf{M}}(T)

and
\operatorname{diam}(R_{i})\leq(c+1+2L)\,s
. Note that
\sum_{i=1}^{J}Z_{i}=0

since
\sum_{i=1}^{J}\partial T_{i}=\partial T=0
. This gives the result.
∎

We will also use the following basic building block of the
deformation theorem in piecewise Euclidean simplicial complexes,
see
[
7
, Appendix A]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\Sigma
be a complete metric space, let
\sigma\subset\Sigma
be a subset
isometric to a regular Euclidean
n
-simplex of edge length
s>0
,
and let
\omega
be its relative interior. Then for every
T\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(\Sigma)

with
1\leq k<n
and
\operatorname{spt}(\partial T)\cap\omega=\emptyset
there exists a cycle

Z\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(\Sigma)
with
\operatorname{spt}(Z)\subset\sigma
such that

\operatorname{spt}(T-Z)\cap\omega=\emptyset
and
{\mathbf{M}}(Z)\leq K\,\|T\|(\omega)
for some constant

K
depending only on
n
.

Proof.

This is shown as in the proof of
[
7
, Proposition A.4]
.
The radial projection
\Sigma\setminus\{z\}\to\Sigma\setminus\omega
from a suitably chosen center
z\in\omega
maps
T
to a current
T^{\prime}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(\Sigma)
such that
Z:=T-T^{\prime}
is the desired cycle.
∎

We now have the following approximation result.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
X
be a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space.
Suppose that
T\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
is a cycle of dimension
k\geq 1
whose
support has a finite
cs
-bounded covering with
s
-multiplicity at most
n+1
,
for some
n,c,s>0
. Then, for some constant
B>0
depending only on
k,n,c
,
there exist a piecewise minimizing cycle
P\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
with vertices
in
\operatorname{spt}(T)
and
\operatorname{mesh}(P)\leq Bs
, and a finite collection of
cycles
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{N}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
with
\operatorname{diam}(Z_{i})\leq Bs

such that

T-P=\sum_{i=1}^{N}Z_{i},\quad|P|_{1}\leq Bs^{-k}\,{\mathbf{M}}(T),\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\mathbf{M}}(Z_{i})\leq B\,{\mathbf{M}}(T).

Furthermore, there exists an
S\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
with
\partial S=T-P

and
{\mathbf{M}}(S)\leq Bs\,{\mathbf{M}}(T)
.

Note also that
{\mathbf{M}}(P)\leq c_{k}B\,{\mathbf{M}}(T)
by Lemma
4.1
.

Proof.

Choose a simplicial complex
\Sigma
and Lipschitz maps

\psi\colon\operatorname{spt}(T)\to\Sigma
and
\phi\colon\Sigma\to X
as in
Lemma
2.1
. Then
T^{\prime}:=\psi_{\#}T\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(\Sigma)
satisfies

{\mathbf{M}}(T^{\prime})\leq L^{k}\,{\mathbf{M}}(T).

Let
l\leq n
be the maximal dimension for which there exists a relatively
open
l
-simplex of
\Sigma
on which
\|T^{\prime}\|
is non-zero.
Writing
T^{\,l}:=T^{\prime}
, and assuming that
l>k
,
we let
\Omega^{\,l}
be the set of all such simplices.
For every
\omega\in\Omega^{\,l}
, we apply Lemma
5.2

to get a cycle
Z^{\prime}_{\omega}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(\Sigma)

with support in the closed simplex
\overline{\omega}
such that

\|T^{\,l}-Z^{\prime}_{\omega}\|(\omega)=0
and

{\mathbf{M}}(Z^{\prime}_{\omega})\leq K\,\|T^{\,l}\|(\omega).

Then
T^{\,l-1}:=T^{\,l}-\sum_{\omega\in\Omega^{\,l}}Z^{\prime}_{\omega}
is an integral
cycle with support in
\Sigma^{(l-1)}
and mass

{\mathbf{M}}(T^{\,l-1})\leq(1+K)\,{\mathbf{M}}(T^{\,l}).

Next, in case
l-1>k
, we let
\Omega^{\,l-1}
be the set of all open

(l-1)
-simplices of
\Sigma
on which
\|T^{\,l-1}\|
is non-zero, then we
repeat the above argument with
T^{\,l-1}
and
\Omega^{\,l-1}
in place
of
T^{\,l}
and
\Omega^{\,l}
. Continuing in this manner, we eventually
get a cycle
P^{\prime}:=T^{\,k}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
with support in
\Sigma^{(k)}

and a decomposition

T^{\prime}=P^{\prime}+\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}Z^{\prime}_{\omega},

where
\Omega=\Omega^{k+1}\cup\ldots\cup\Omega^{l}
, every
Z^{\prime}_{\omega}

is a cycle with support in
\overline{\omega}
, and

{\mathbf{M}}(P^{\prime})\leq K^{\prime}\,{\mathbf{M}}(T^{\prime}),\quad\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}{\mathbf{M}}(Z^{\prime}_{\omega})\leq K^{\prime}\,{\mathbf{M}}(T^{\prime})

for some constant
K^{\prime}
depending only on
n
.
As an integral
k
-cycle with support in the
k
-skeleton,

P^{\prime}
is in fact simplicial,
P^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{P}}_{k}(\Sigma)
. That is,

P^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{F}m_{i}\llbracket\sigma_{i}\rrbracket

for some positive integers
m_{i}
and oriented
k
-simplices
\sigma_{i}
of
\Sigma
,
viewed as integral currents
\llbracket\sigma_{i}\rrbracket
. Denoting by

\mu_{k}
the mass of a Euclidean
k
-simplex of edge length
1
, we get

|P^{\prime}|_{1}:=\sum_{i=1}^{F}m_{i}=\frac{1}{\mu_{k}s^{k}}\,{\mathbf{M}}(P^{\prime})\leq\frac{K^{\prime}L^{k}}{\mu_{k}}\,\frac{{\mathbf{M}}(T)}{s^{k}}=K^{\prime\prime}s^{-k}\,{\mathbf{M}}(T).

Recall that
\phi\colon\Sigma\to X
is
L
-Lipschitz on
every simplex of
\Sigma
. Put
Z_{\omega}:=\phi_{\#}(Z^{\prime}_{\omega})

for every
\omega\in\Omega
. Then
\operatorname{diam}(Z_{\omega})\leq Ls
and

\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}{\mathbf{M}}(Z_{\omega})\leq\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}L^{k}\,{\mathbf{M}}(Z^{\prime}_{\omega})\leq K^{\prime}L^{2k}\,{\mathbf{M}}(T).

Now we proceed similarly as in the proof of
[
7
, Theorem 1.4]
.
We successively choose homomorphisms

\Lambda_{j}\colon{\mathscr{P}}_{j}(\Sigma)\to{\mathbf{I}}_{j,\text{\rm c}}(X),\quad j=0,\ldots,k,

such that
\Lambda_{0}(\llbracket v\rrbracket)=\phi_{\#}(\llbracket v\rrbracket)=\llbracket\phi(v)\rrbracket
for every
vertex
v
of
\Sigma
and, if
\Lambda_{j-1}
is defined, such that
\Lambda_{j}
maps
every
j
-simplex
\llbracket\sigma\rrbracket
to a minimizing
j
-simplex in
X
with boundary

\partial\,\Lambda_{j}(\llbracket\sigma\rrbracket)=\Lambda_{j-1}(\partial\,\llbracket\sigma\rrbracket).

Since
\phi
is
L
-Lipschitz on simplices, it follows
from Lemma
4.1
that

{\mathbf{M}}(\Lambda_{j}(\llbracket\sigma\rrbracket))\leq c_{j}(Ls)^{j}.

Now
P:=\Lambda_{k}(P^{\prime})\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
is a piecewise minimizing cycle
with vertices in
\operatorname{spt}(T)
,
\operatorname{mesh}(P)\leq Ls
, and

|P|_{1}=|P^{\prime}|_{1}\leq K^{\prime\prime}s^{-k}\,{\mathbf{M}}(T),

as desired. Next, we define homomorphisms

\Gamma_{j}\colon{\mathscr{P}}_{j}(\Sigma)\to{\mathbf{I}}_{j+1,\text{\rm c}}(X),\quad j=0,\ldots,k,

such that
\Gamma_{0}=0
and, if
\Gamma_{j-1}
is defined, such that
\Gamma_{j}

maps every
j
-simplex
\llbracket\sigma\rrbracket
to a minimizing integral

(j+1)
-current with boundary

\partial\,\Gamma_{j}(\llbracket\sigma\rrbracket)=\phi_{\#}(\llbracket\sigma\rrbracket)-\Lambda_{j}(\llbracket\sigma\rrbracket)-\Gamma_{j-1}(\partial\,\llbracket\sigma\rrbracket).

It follows by induction from Theorem
3.4
that

{\mathbf{M}}(\partial\,\Gamma_{j}(\llbracket\sigma\rrbracket)\leq c^{\prime}_{j}(Ls)^{j}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathbf{M}}(\Gamma_{j}(\llbracket\sigma\rrbracket))\leq\gamma_{j}c^{\prime}_{j}(Ls)^{j+1}

for some constants
c^{\prime}_{j}
, as

{\mathbf{M}}(\partial\,\Gamma_{j}(\llbracket\sigma\rrbracket))\leq L^{j}\mu_{j}s^{j}+c_{j}(Ls)^{j}+(j+1)\,\gamma_{j-1}c^{\prime}_{j-1}(Ls)^{j}.

Define
Z_{i}:=\partial\,\Gamma_{k}(m_{i}\llbracket\sigma_{i}\rrbracket)
for
i=1,\ldots,F
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{F}{\mathbf{M}}(Z_{i})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{F}m_{i}\,c^{\prime}_{k}(Ls)^{k}\leq c^{\prime}_{k}K^{\prime\prime}L^{k}\,{\mathbf{M}}(T).

Since
\partial P^{\prime}=0
and
P^{\prime}=T^{\prime}-\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}Z^{\prime}_{\omega}
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{F}Z_{i}=\partial\,\Gamma_{k}(P^{\prime})=\phi_{\#}(P^{\prime})-\Lambda_{k}(P^{\prime})=f_{\#}T-\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}Z_{\omega}-P,

where
f:=\phi\circ\psi
.
Finally, using Proposition
5.1
, we get a decomposition

T-P=\sum_{i=1}^{J}R_{i}+\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}Z_{\omega}+\sum_{i=1}^{F}Z_{i},

and the desired result follows upon relabeling the cycles.

As for the last assertion of the theorem, a suitable
S\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
with
\partial S=T-P=\sum_{i=1}^{N}Z_{i}

is obtained by applying the coning inequality (Proposition
3.2
)
to each
Z_{i}
.
∎

6.
Proof of main result

The proof of our main result relies crucially on Proposition
4.6
,
Theorem
5.3
, and the following central element of
Theorem
3.4
; see
[
42
, Proposition 3.1]
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
X
be a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space, and let
k\geq 1
.
There exist constants
\beta>0
and
\epsilon,\lambda\in(0,1)
,
depending only on
k
, such that every cycle
T\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
admits a
decomposition
T=T^{\prime}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}R_{i}
into finitely many cycles

T^{\prime},R_{1},\ldots,R_{N}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
with

(1)

\operatorname{diam}(R_{i})\leq\beta\,{\mathbf{M}}(R_{i})^{1/k}
for
i=1,\ldots,N
,

(2)

{\mathbf{M}}(T^{\prime})\leq(1-\epsilon)\,{\mathbf{M}}(T)
, and

(3)

\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\mathbf{M}}(R_{i})\leq(1+\lambda)\,{\mathbf{M}}(T)
.

We restate Theorem
1.1
as follows.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
X
be a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space with asymptotic rank
\nu\in\{1,2\}
and finite
asymptotic Nagata dimension. Then for every integer
k\geq\nu
and every
\delta\in(0,1/k)
there
exists a constant
C>0
such that every cycle
T\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
has a
filling
V\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
with

{\mathbf{M}}(V)\leq C\,{\mathbf{M}}(T)^{1+\delta}.

The assumption on the asymptotic rank will be used exclusively through
Proposition
4.6
. To illustrate the general principle, we will
write out the proof as if this result were to hold for arbitrary
\nu\geq 1
.

Proof.

First, we consider a cycle
R\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
with
\operatorname{diam}(R)\leq\beta\,{\mathbf{M}}(R)^{1/k}
, where
\beta>0
is as above.
By assumption, there exist
n,c,r>0
such that for
s>r
, every compact subset of
X
has a finite
cs
-bounded covering with
s
-multiplicity at most
n+1
.
If
{\mathbf{M}}(R)\leq r^{1/\delta}
, then

\operatorname{Fill}(R)\leq\gamma_{k}\,{\mathbf{M}}(R)^{1+1/k}\leq\gamma_{k}\,r^{1/(k\delta)-1}\,{\mathbf{M}}(R)^{1+\delta}

by Theorem
3.4
. We now assume that
{\mathbf{M}}(R)^{\delta}>r\geq 1
and choose scales

{\mathbf{M}}(R)^{1/k}=s_{0}>s_{1}>\ldots>s_{l}={\mathbf{M}}(R)^{\delta}

such that
s_{n-1}/s_{n}\leq{\mathbf{M}}(R)^{\delta}
for
n=1,\ldots,l
, where
l<1/(k\delta)
.
We show by induction that for
n=1,\ldots,l
there exist finite families
{\mathscr{P}}_{n},{\mathscr{Z}}_{n}
of
k
-cycles and decompositions

R=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\sum_{{\mathscr{P}}_{m}}P+\sum_{{\mathscr{Z}}_{n}}Z

such that
\sum_{{\mathscr{Z}}_{n}}{\mathbf{M}}(Z)\leq B^{n}\,{\mathbf{M}}(R)
, every
P\in{\mathscr{P}}_{n}
is piecewise minimizing with
\operatorname{mesh}(P)\leq Bs_{n}
, and every
Z\in{\mathscr{Z}}_{n}
satisfies
\operatorname{diam}(Z)\leq Bs_{n}
.
For
n=1
, this follows by applying Theorem
5.3
with
s=s_{1}
to
R
(thus
{\mathscr{P}}_{1}
has a single member).
Let now
n\in\{2,\ldots,l\}
.
Given the decomposition for
n-1
, assume that
{\mathscr{Z}}_{n-1}=(Z_{j})_{j}
, and apply Theorem
5.3
with
s=s_{n}
to write each
Z_{j}
as a sum of a piecewise minimizing cycle
P_{j}
with
\operatorname{mesh}(P_{j})\leq Bs_{n}
and cycles
Z_{j,i}
with
\operatorname{diam}(Z_{j,i})\leq Bs_{n}
.
Set
{\mathscr{P}}_{n}:=(P_{j})_{j}
and
{\mathscr{Z}}_{n}:=(Z_{j,i})_{j,i}
.
This yields the decomposition for
n
, and

\sum_{{\mathscr{Z}}_{n}}{\mathbf{M}}(Z)=\sum_{j,i}{\mathbf{M}}(Z_{j,i})\leq\sum_{j}B\,{\mathbf{M}}(Z_{j})\leq B^{n}\,{\mathbf{M}}(R),

as desired.

We now fill the individual cycles of the decomposition for
l
.
By construction, every
P\in{\mathscr{P}}_{n}
stems from some
Z\in{\mathscr{Z}}_{n-1}
,
or from
Z=R
in case
n=1
, so that the vertices of
P
lie in
\operatorname{spt}(Z)

and
|P|_{1}\leq Bs_{n}^{-k}\,{\mathbf{M}}(Z)
.
We assume for convenience that
B\geq\beta
.
By Proposition
4.6
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Fill}(P)

\displaystyle\leq C_{k+1}\operatorname{diam}(Z)\operatorname{mesh}(P)^{\nu-1}\,|P|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C_{k+1}\cdot Bs_{n-1}\cdot(Bs_{n})^{\nu-1}\cdot Bs_{n}^{-k}\,{\mathbf{M}}(Z),

where
s_{n-1}s_{n}^{\,\nu-1-k}\leq s_{n-1}s_{n}^{-1}\leq{\mathbf{M}}(R)^{\delta}
.
Since
\sum_{{\mathscr{Z}}_{n-1}}{\mathbf{M}}(Z)\leq B^{n-1}\,{\mathbf{M}}(R)
, or
{\mathbf{M}}(Z)={\mathbf{M}}(R)
if
n=1
, we have

\sum_{n=1}^{l}\sum_{{\mathscr{P}}_{n}}\operatorname{Fill}(P)\leq\sum_{n=1}^{l}C_{k+1}B^{\nu+n}\,{\mathbf{M}}(R)^{1+\delta};

recall that
l<1/(k\delta)
.
Lastly, if
Z\in{\mathscr{Z}}_{l}
, then
\operatorname{diam}(Z)\leq Bs_{l}\leq B\,{\mathbf{M}}(R)^{\delta}
, thus Proposition
3.2
gives

\sum_{{\mathscr{Z}}_{l}}\operatorname{Fill}(Z)\leq\frac{B\,{\mathbf{M}}(R)^{\delta}}{k+1}\sum_{{\mathscr{Z}}_{l}}{\mathbf{M}}(Z)\leq\frac{B^{l+1}}{k+1}\,{\mathbf{M}}(R)^{1+\delta}.

This proves the desired
\delta
-isoperimetric inequality with a constant
C^{\prime}
for all cycles
R\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)

with
\operatorname{diam}(R)\leq\beta\,{\mathbf{M}}(R)^{1/k}
.

Let now
T\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,\text{\rm c}}(X)
be a general cycle and consider the decomposition of
T
from Proposition
6.1
.
Put
\mu:=\epsilon/(1+\lambda)
, so that (2) and (3) give

{\mathbf{M}}(T^{\prime})+\mu\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\mathbf{M}}(R_{i})\leq{\mathbf{M}}(T).

As we have shown, there exist

V_{1},\ldots,V_{N}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
such that
\partial V_{i}=R_{i}
and

{\mathbf{M}}(V_{i})\leq C^{\prime}\,{\mathbf{M}}(R_{i})^{1+\delta}.

Then
V^{\prime}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}V_{i}
is a filling of
T-T^{\prime}
, and

\displaystyle C^{\prime}\,{\mathbf{M}}(T^{\prime})^{1+\delta}+\mu^{1+\delta}\,{\mathbf{M}}(V^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\biggl({\mathbf{M}}(T^{\prime})^{1+\delta}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigl(\mu\,{\mathbf{M}}(R_{i})\bigr)^{1+\delta}\biggr)

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\biggl({\mathbf{M}}(T^{\prime})+\mu\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\mathbf{M}}(R_{i})\biggr)^{1+\delta}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\,{\mathbf{M}}(T)^{1+\delta}.

Upon iterating this inequality a finite number of times, we can assume

{\mathbf{M}}(T^{\prime})
to be small enough such that Theorem
3.4
provides
a filling
S\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k+1,\text{\rm c}}(X)
of
T^{\prime}
satisfying

\mu^{1+\delta}\,{\mathbf{M}}(S)\leq\mu^{1+\delta}\gamma_{k}\,{\mathbf{M}}(T^{\prime})^{1+1/k}\leq C^{\prime}\,{\mathbf{M}}(T^{\prime})^{1+\delta}.

Then
V:=S+V^{\prime}
is a filling of
T
with
{\mathbf{M}}(V)\leq C\,{\mathbf{M}}(T)^{1+\delta}
for
C:=C^{\prime}/\mu^{1+\delta}
.
∎

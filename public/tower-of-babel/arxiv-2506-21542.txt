Quintessence and phantoms in light of DESI 2025
Ioannis D. Gialamas, Gert Hütsi, Martti Raidal, Juan Urrutia, Martin Vasar, Hardi Veermäe

Abstract
We analyse DESI BAO, CMB, and supernova data to explore the physical origin of the DESI indication for dynamical dark energy. Beyond the standard CPL parametrization, we explore truncated alternatives and quintessence models. We conclude that there is compelling evidence for dark energy to be decaying in the late universe, but the evidence for a phantom behaviour is less significant. Models without phantom behaviour are compatible with the data at the 2 ​ σ 2\sigma CL. Furthermore, we examine a concrete quintessence scenario with a Higgs-like potential, allowing for a direct comparison with parametrized approaches and testing its consistency with current observations. This framework enables a broader investigation of late-time cosmic evolution and reveals a 93.8 % 93.8\% preference for a future transition into an anti-de Sitter space, which may ultimately lead to a cosmological collapse of our Universe.

II
Modelling dynamical dark energy and quintessence

In this section, we consider two complementary approaches to modelling dark energy: (i) parametrizations of the equation of state parameter

w(z)
, and (ii) an explicit quintessence model based on a minimally coupled scalar field. Parametrized forms of
w(z)
, such as the commonly used CPL parametrization and its extensions, are employed for their simplicity and flexibility, allowing us to explore the general behaviour of DE and assess possible preferences for phantom-like behaviour (
w<-1
). Additionally, we consider a quintessence-inspired parametrization based on the evolution of a scalar field. In the second part of the analysis, we move to an explicit model of quintessence with a Higgs-like potential, enabling a direct comparison with the parametrized approaches and an evaluation of its consistency with the latest observational data.

Assuming standard GR in a spatially flat Friedmann–Robertson–Walker (FRW) background, the first Friedmann equation takes the form

\frac{H^{2}}{H_{0}^{2}}=\Omega(z)+\Omega_{\rm DE}\frac{\rho_{\rm DE}(z)}{\rho_{{\rm DE},0}}\,.

(1)

The function
\Omega(z)=\Omega_{m}(1+z)^{3}+\Omega_{\gamma}(1+z)^{4}+\Omega_{\nu}(z)
represents the total normalized energy density of matter, radiation and neutrinos at redshift
z
and
\Omega_{i}=\rho_{i,0}/(3M_{P}^{2}H_{0}^{2})
denotes the present-day density parameter for the corresponding component
i
, with
\rho_{i,0}
being the present-day energy density. Note also that
\Omega_{m}=\Omega_{b}+\Omega_{c}
. The energy density of neutrinos is included following Ref.
WMAP:2010qai
. The time dependence of the energy density of the DE component
\rho_{\rm DE}(z)
is the main subject of this study. As discussed above, we will consider two approaches here:

i.

A generic parametrization of the equation of state
w(z)
of dynamical DE.

ii.

Quintessence in which the dynamics of DE is determined by the shape of the potential.

In the first case, given an equation of state parameter
w(z)
, the energy density of DE evolves as

\rho_{\rm DE}(z)=\rho_{{\rm DE},0}\,e^{3\int_{0}^{z}\frac{1+w(z^{\prime})}{1+z^{\prime}}{\rm d}z^{\prime}}\,,

(2)

where
\rho_{{\rm DE},0}
is its current value. We will consider parametrizations of

that can approximately mimic quintessence. Although our focus is on models of minimally coupled thawing quintessence, which generally does not exhibit phantom behaviour at high

In this section, we move beyond the commonly used parametrizations of the dark energy equation of state typically found in the literature and instead adopt a more fundamental, physically motivated model based on quintessence. Specifically, we consider a canonically normalized scalar field

represents the Lagrangian of the rest particle spectrum containing both matter and gauge fields. We omit non-minimal couplings of the form
\sim\xi\phi^{2}R

V(\phi)=V_{0}+\frac{m^{2}}{2}\phi^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{4}\phi^{4}\,,

This addition enables a more comprehensive exploration of the late-time evolution of the Universe, including the possibility of transitioning into either a de Sitter (dS) phase, characterized by eternal accelerated expansion (a “big freeze”), or an anti-de Sitter (AdS) phase, which may ultimately give rise to a cosmological collapse (a “big crunch”)

Variations of the action in Eq. (
10

and the metric tensor, assuming a spatially flat FRW background, yield the following equations of motion

\displaystyle\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{\phi}V(\phi)\,,

(12a)

\displaystyle H^{2}/H_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle=\Omega(z)+(\dot{\phi}^{2}/2+V(\phi))/(3H_{0}^{2}M_{P}^{2})\,,

(12b)

where, as discussed previously,
\Omega(z)

V(\phi)
becomes negative, it can offset the positive contributions from
\Omega(z)
and the kinetic energy. When this occurs, the Hubble parameter vanishes, signalling the end of cosmic expansion. Beyond this point,
H
becomes negative and the universe enters a phase of contraction. The scalar field will oscillate around the negative minimum of the potential, but will eventually diverge due to the Hubble anti-friction, which accelerates its motion. Thus, potentials with a negative minimum have the same qualitative effect on cosmic evolution as those unbounded from below
Felder:2002jk
.

The effective equation of state parameter of the scalar field is given by

w(a)=\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}/2-V(\phi)}{\dot{\phi}^{2}/2+V(\phi)}\,.

(13)

In the early Universe, where the field is nearly frozen due to Hubble friction (i.e.,
\dot{\phi}\sim 0
) and the potential is positive, the equation of state parameter approaches
w\gtrsim-1
, mimicking a cosmological constant. As the Universe evolves and the scalar field begins to roll, the dynamics become increasingly sensitive to the shape of the potential. In particular, since the potential energy can become negative, the equation of state parameter
w(a)
can evolve across a broad range of values larger than
-1
. Specifically,
w
increases from its initial value near
-1
, can reach values
w\gg 1
before the onset of collapse, and eventually approaches
w=+1
, corresponding to a stiff equation of state
Steinhardt:2001st
;
Felder:2002jk
;
Heard:2002dr
;
Kallosh:2002gf
.

IV
Conclusions

In this work, we performed a maximum likelihood analysis using the Markov Chain Monte Carlo method to fit data from DESI BAO, the CMB, and supernova observations, to improve the understanding of the evidence for dynamical dark energy. Our primary objective was to investigate not only whether the current observational data remain consistent with the
\Lambda
CDM scenario, which now appears to be increasingly less supported, but also to what extent they can be explained by minimally coupled quintessence or prefer a phantom crossing at high redshifts, that is,
w(z)<-1
.

To explore these possibilities, we fitted the data using extensions of the standard CPL parametrization of the dark energy equation of state. In particular, we considered a truncated version of CPL that behaves as CPL at low redshifts and asymptotically approaches a constant value
w_{\infty}
at high redshifts. The best fit for this model yielded
w_{\infty}\simeq-1.45
, reinforcing the tendency toward phantom-like behaviour. However, conclusions regarding the presence of phantom dark energy are not statistically robust, as non-phantom values such as
w_{\infty}\simeq-1
are still within the
2\sigma
confidence region.

Given the theoretical challenges associated with phantom models, we considered parametrizations that approximate quintessence and explicit, physically motivated models based on scalar fields minimally coupled to gravity. These models can fit the data well without invoking phantom behaviour. Specifically, we considered a scalar field with a Higgs-like potential of the form

V(\phi)=V_{0}+\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\phi^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\lambda\phi^{4},

which includes the possibility of a non-vanishing vacuum energy and an ultralight negative-squared mass. Our numerical analysis shows that, for the best fit parameters, the vacuum energy is positive and the scalar field has already begun to oscillate around its minimum. Nevertheless, we found that a negative vacuum energy is preferred at the
\sim 93.8\%
level. If this scenario holds, a future cosmological collapse leading to a reversal of the cosmic expansion would be unavoidable.

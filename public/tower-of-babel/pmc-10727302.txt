Survival Benefit of Multiple Arterial Revascularization With and Without Supplementary Saphenous Vein Graft
Authors: Justin Ren, David H. Tian, Mario Gaudino, Stephen Fremes, Christopher M. Reid, Michael Vallely, Julian A. Smith, Nilesh Srivastav, Colin Royse, Alistair Royse
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIt is unknown if the presence of saphenous vein grafting (SVG) adversely affects late survival following coronary surgery with multiple arterial grafting (MAG) versus single arterial grafting.Methods and ResultsA retrospective, observational, multicenter cohort study from 2001 to 2020 was conducted using the Australian and New Zealand Society of Cardiac and Thoracic Surgeons Database linked to the National Death Index. Patients undergoing primary isolated coronary artery bypass grafting with ≥2 grafts were included, and exclusions were patients aged <18 years, reoperations, concomitant or previous cardiac surgery, and the absence of arterial grafting. Demographics, comorbidities, medication, and operative configurations were propensity score matched between cohorts. The primary outcome was all‐cause late death. Of 59 689 eligible patients, 35 113 were MAG (58.8%), and 24 576 were single arterial grafting (41.2%). Of the MAG cohort, 17 055 (48.6%) patients did not receive supplementary SVG (total arterial revascularization). Matching separately generated 22 764 patient pairs for MAG versus single arterial grafting, and 11 137 patient pairs for MAG with total arterial revascularization versus MAG with ≥1 supplementary vein grafts. At a median follow‐up duration of 5.0 years postoperatively, the mortality rate was significantly lower for MAG than single arterial grafting (hazard ratio [HR], 0.79 [95% CI, 0.76–0.83]; P<0.001). The stratified MAG analysis found that MAG with total arterial revascularization had a lower risk of late death (HR, 0.85 [95% CI, 0.80–0.91]; P<0.001) compared with MAG with ≥1 supplementary vein grafts. Sensitivity analyses produced consistent outcomes as the primary analysis. Following adjustment for the presence of SVG in the Cox model, the survival advantage of incremental number of arteries was lost.ConclusionsMultiple arterial grafting has significantly improved long‐term survival compared with single arterial grafting. A further incremental survival benefit exists when no SVG is used.

Conventional practice in coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) is to graft the left internal mammary artery to the stenotic left anterior descending (LAD) territory due to the reported improvement in survival and freedom from recurrent cardiac events. 1 , 2 Non‐LAD targets are almost exclusively revascularized by the saphenous vein graft (SVG). This approach is known as single arterial grafting (SAG). Venous grafts are associated with a higher graft failure rate as compared with arterial conduits, resulting in an increased incidence of death, myocardial infarction, and repeat revascularization. 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 Alternatively, additional arterial grafts can be employed during multiple arterial grafting (MAG).

Observational and meta‐analytic data have identified survival benefits 7 , 8 , 9 , 10 and improved clinical outcomes 11 , 12 for patients undergoing MAG compared with SAG. However, discordant results from the Arterial Revascularization Trial 13 further complicate our understanding of the treatment effect of MAG, principally due to the confounding effect of concurrent SVG use. While MAG refers to the use of at least 2 arterial grafts, it does not limit the use of SVGs, which may have a disproportionate long‐term impact on patients. Therefore, adverse outcomes of MAG could be plausibly related to the concurrent presence of SVG rather than to the arterial grafts.

Few studies have recognized, or adjusted for, the potential confounding impact of SVG in comparative analyses. Given that the effect of having any vein graft in MAG remains highly uncertain, this study aims to evaluate the association of SVG presence with the relative outcomes of multiple versus single arterial grafting from a national cardiothoracic surgery registry. As a secondary aim, we investigated whether using an increasing number of arterial conduits and no SVG would confer incremental survival benefits.

Methods

Study Design

Patients undergoing primary isolated CABG from 59 cardiac institutions between June 2001 and January 2020 were identified through the Australian and New Zealand Society of Cardiac and Thoracic Surgeons Database stored in a centralized registry, not publicly accessible due to privacy legislation. The demographic, comorbidity, and procedural information in this national registry has been checked at frequent intervals and validated through established linkage to multiple administrative databases, including the National Death Index, with a censor date of January 2018. The Australian and New Zealand Society of Cardiac and Thoracic Surgeons Database is a clinical registry designed for systematic quality assurance and collaborative surgical research. An official waiver for patients' individual consent was granted by the Melbourne Health Institutional Ethics Review Board (2011.164) due to encrypted patient identification data and the retrospective nature of this study.

The Australian and New Zealand Society of Cardiac and Thoracic Surgeons Database applies advanced validation at data entry with predefined variable definitions. The Data Management Centre further reviews completeness and conducts quality checks before reporting. Additionally, on‐site audits of key data fields against source medical records are regularly performed to generate a research data set for analysis. The researchers cannot provide the data for public access due to privacy legislation restrictions but agree to provide all other methods and materials used in this analysis upon reasonable request.

Patient Selection

Patients undergoing primary isolated CABG with ≥2 grafts (anastomoses) were identified. Exclusion criteria were nonadult patients, reoperations, concomitant or previous cardiac surgical procedures (eg, mitral valve repair), single‐graft surgery, and the absence of arterial grafting (Figure 1).

Study Outcomes

The primary outcome was all‐cause death reported as time to event and measured from the date of the index operation. Secondary binary outcomes included 30‐day death and 30‐day readmission to the hospital due to a composite of myocardial infarction, deep sternal wound infection (DSWI), arrhythmia, congestive heart failure, or recurrent angina. Each individual component was also separately analyzed. Readmission excluded admission to emergency departments or short‐stay wards or planned transfer to a rehabilitation facility.

Statistical Analysis

A complete case analysis was performed with continuous variables reported as median (interquartile range) or mean ± SD, and categorical as frequency counts and percentages. Normality was assessed with the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Significance was defined by a 2‐tailed P value <0.05. All analyses were conducted using R version 4.0.5 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Each anastomosis was separately analyzed. For absolute clarity, if a conduit had a sequential anastomosis, then each segment of the conduit to each anastomosis was separately analyzed. We used propensity score matching (PSM) to minimize the potential impact of selection bias by adjusting for imbalanced baseline characteristics. The propensity score estimation algorithm involved a generalized linear logistic regression model, which included patients' demographic details, preoperative risk factors, medication at time of surgery, extent of coronary disease, and operative configurations (all variables are listed in Table 1). All comparative analyses used a 1‐to‐1 greedy matching with 41 variables, and with a caliper width of 0.2 SD of the logit of the propensity score without replacement. Absolute standardized mean differences were evaluated before and after PSM for balance diagnostics; a difference value <0.1 generally indicated balance between patient groups. 14

In the time‐to‐event analysis, the Cox proportional hazard model was used to estimate hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% CIs comparing MAG against SAG for the long‐term mortality rate, with robust variance estimation to account for clustering within the matched pairs. 15 The proportionality assumption was verified with weighted Schoenfeld residuals. The rate of death over time was visualized by Kaplan–Meier survival functions for the matched populations. Secondary binary outcomes with paired data were evaluated by McNemar's paired t test. MAG with total arterial revascularization (MAG(TAR)) was then compared with MAG with ≥1 supplementary vein grafts (MAG(SVG)) following a new PSM, to assess the subgroup impact of SVG presence.

Additional matched analyses were conducted to investigate incremental survival advantages associated with an increased number of arterial conduits. Subgroups were created on the basis of the number of distal arterial anastomoses. We compared matched patients having 2 arterial grafts (2‐Art) against 3 arterial grafts (3‐Art), and 3‐Art against 4 arterial grafts (4‐Art) without restrictions on the SVG usage. A multivariable Cox regression hazard model was performed to assess whether the presence of SVG would influence the treatment effect derived from increased arterial revascularization. No adjustments have been made for multiple testing, and therefore results from subgroup analyses should be considered exploratory in nature only.

Sensitivity Analysis

A series of sensitivity analyses were performed by (1) additionally adjusting for incomplete revascularization (a proxy defined as fewer conduits grafted than the number of diseased territories due to lack of more precise information) in PSM, (2) additionally adjusting for the year of surgery in PSM, and (3) introducing multivariable Cox regression analysis to further reduce residual imbalances after PSM.

Results

A total of 59 689 eligible patients had a median and maximum postoperative follow‐up duration of 5.0 and 17.9 years, respectively. Follow‐up was complete for all patients. The study population included 24 576 (41.2%) patients with SAG, and 35 113 (58.8%) patients undergoing MAG, of whom 17 055 (48.6%) patients had only arterial grafts (TAR). Overall, there were 48 359 (81.0%) men, 21 881 (36.7%) patients with diabetes, and 47 494 (79.6%) patients with hypertension. Other unadjusted and matched baseline demographics are listed in Table 1. Before matching, the MAG group was younger, more likely to be men, and less likely to have preoperative comorbidities including hypertension, diabetes, smoking history, dialysis, arrhythmia, cardiogenic shock, resuscitation, myocardial infarction, and cerebrovascular events than SAG patients. All baseline characteristics were adequately matched (Table 1).

The use of a radial artery (RA) was more common than right internal mammary artery across all unmatched cohorts. Detailed graft selection data for SAG, MAG, MAG(TAR), MAG(SVG), 2‐Art, 3‐Art, and 4‐Art groups are listed in Table 2.

Primary End Point: Long‐Term Survival

The PSM algorithm generated 22 764 matched pairs for SAG versus MAG, which reduced all standardized mean differences to <0.1 (Table 1). Compared with SAG, MAG was associated with a 21% relative risk reduction (HR, 0.79 [95% CI, 0.76–0.83]; P<0.001) in long‐term mortality rate. At 12‐year follow‐up, the estimated Kaplan–Meier survival was 54.0% for SAG and 63.3% for MAG (Figure 2). After early separation at ≈3 years postoperatively, there was an incremental survival advantage observed throughout the entire follow‐up duration. All 3 sensitivity analyses produced consistent outcomes as the primary investigation (Table S1).

From multivariable Cox regression, the presence of SVG as a risk variable significantly increased likelihood of late death for both 3‐Art versus 2‐Art (P<0.001) and 4‐Art versus 3‐Art (P<0.001) comparisons. Importantly, after the presence of SVG (the potential confounder) had been adjusted for, the survival benefit associated with increased numbers of arterial conduits was lost for both 3‐Art versus 2‐Art (HR, 1.00 [95% CI, 0.94–1.06]; P=0.959) and 4‐Art versus 3‐Art (HR, 0.98 [95% CI, 0.85–1.14]; P=0.831) comparisons. Discussion

This population‐level, multicenter retrospective cohort study of 59 689 patients with complete follow‐up is the largest analysis to date comparing the long‐term survival of patients undergoing MAG versus SAG. The extensive size of our data set provides robust statistical power for the examination of various research questions, including the unique exploration of the impact of SVG usage in MAG procedures. After statistical adjustment for imbalanced covariates, MAG was associated with significant relative reduction in all‐cause death compared with SAG. Within MAG, patients with TAR had a significant survival advantage over those with supplementary SVG. In this series, 51.4% of patients undergoing MAG received supplementary SVG, though experience outside of Australia may be higher. Our findings suggest that the use of SVG may attenuate the survival benefit provided by MAG in scenarios with a similar extent of coronary disease and number of bypass conduits. We further validated this conclusion through a multivariable Cox regression hazard model showing a substantial adverse effect of venous conduit on the treatment effect associated with using more arterial conduits. The 30‐day incidence of myocardial infarction and recurrent angina was significantly higher in SAG compared with MAG, whereas higher incidence of 30‐day DSWI was observed in MAG. A trend for incremental survival benefit was observed from 2 arteries, 3 arteries, and then 4 arteries.

These are important findings that may clarify confusion as to the beneficial nature of MAG. For example, the Arterial Revascularization Trial did not find a significant difference in 10‐year survival for single versus bilateral internal mammary artery (BIMA) grafting, and this has been variably ascribed to the high rate of crossover and of not considering the RA equivalent to the internal mammary artery 13 as an alternative arterial conduit. However, a post hoc analysis examined MAG and TAR in comparison with SAG and found the largest survival benefit for TAR (HR, 0.68 [95% CI, 0.48–0.96]; P=0.03). 16 Nevertheless, a confounder not accounted for was the concurrent use of SVG in both groups, and this criticism is valid for most MAG studies. 10 , 13 , 17 , 18 , 19 , 20 , 21 The ongoing ROMA (Randomized Comparison of the Clinical Outcome of Single Versus Multiple Arterial Grafts) trial is the only current randomized multicenter clinical trial designed to compare the survival and clinical outcomes between MAG and SAG. 22 Despite the absence of randomized evidence, abundant observational and post hoc analyses have consistently suggested the long‐term clinical benefits of MAG. 10 , 19 , 23 , 24 A regional study of 8629 matched pairs found a superior freedom from major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events (HR, 0.82 [95% CI, 0.77–0.88]) and survival (HR, 0.80 [95% CI, 0.73–0.88]) in the MAG group. 10 Likewise, the post hoc analysis from the SYNTAX (TAXUS Drug‐Eluting Stent Versus Coronary Artery Bypass Surgery for the Treatment of Narrowed Arteries) trial 23 reported 26% relative reduction in the risk of all‐cause death following MAG at 12.6 years after the procedure, which is in high conformity with our current comparison (21%).

We report the negative impact on survival of patients undergoing MAG when additional supplementary SVG is used. This may seem counterintuitive as the conventional view is that survival benefit arises solely from the expected greater durability and number of arterial conduits relative to SVG. 25 , 26 , 27 , 28 , 29 However, if survival is a surrogate end point for graft failure, and it is the SVG conduit that is well documented to fail, then logic suggests that the presence of SVG may be an important confounder in the causation of death. In this analysis, we found that MAG in the absence of any SVG (ie, MAG[TAR]) had better survival than MAG with SVG, despite comparable numbers of arterial anastomoses and coronary disease profiles after comprehensive matching. Comparing 3‐Art against 2‐Art procedures, the multivariable Cox regression analysis indicated that the survival advantage of using additional arteries diminished when any SVG was employed. The influence of SVG on the comparison between 4‐Art versus 3‐Art also appeared to be significant. This suggests that the mechanism for survival variance within MAG subgroups may potentially be attributed to the presence of SVG, by the presumed mechanism of progressive failure of this conduit. Consistent with this hypothesis, data from the recent long‐term analysis from the Veteran's Affairs Cooperative Studies Program 474 Trial, 30 which compared the use of RA and SVG for non‐LAD vessels following left internal mammary artery–LAD grafting, showed no mortality rate difference (HR, 0.99 [95% CI, 0.81–1.20]; P=0.89) at 17 years. Both of the cohorts contained SVG, which might have undermined the survival benefit provided by other arterial grafts. Our group conducted a recent systematic review and expanded analysis of 127 565 adjusted patients, which further reinforced a TAR‐associated reduction in the risk of all‐cause death (HR, 0.78 [95% CI, 0.72–0.85]; P<0.001). 31

Comparisons according to the specific number of arterial conduits, especially between 4‐Art versus 3‐Art, have been limited mainly because of smaller sample size. More studies compared 3‐Art against 2‐Art techniques. At a mean follow‐up duration of 16 years, Glineur et al 12 described better outcomes with BIMA+gastroepiploic arterial graft versus BIMA+SVG after multivariable adjustments. The use of 3 arteries was associated with reduced incidence of all‐cause death (HR, 0.46; P=0.015) and cardiac death (HR, 0.20; P=0.005) compared with patients receiving an SVG. Similarly, Shi and colleagues 32 reported better 15‐year risk‐adjusted survival in patients undergoing BIMA+RA graft than patients undergoing BIMA+SVG graft (82% versus 72%; P=0.021), consistent with our 3‐Art versus 2‐Art comparison. In addition, we also observed an association of improved late survival with 4‐Art CABG over 3‐Art CABG. Yet when we further adjusted for the presence of SVG via Cox regression, these survival benefits were no longer evident. A possible explanation for our findings is that the use of more arterial grafts directly increases the likelihood of patients receiving exclusively arterial surgery (Table 2), and the elimination of SVG is confirmed to improve long‐term survival. 33 Alternatively, the national registry may have provided adequate statistical power to detect the underlying treatment effects.

Contrary to consistent reporting of survival benefits associated with MAG, evidence on short‐term clinical outcomes remains limited and contradictory. Rocha and colleagues 10 observed a lower incidence of in‐hospital stroke of MAG compared against SAG (0.6% versus 0.9%; P=0.01), whereas another study conducted by Pu et al 19 showed similar short‐term outcomes between MAG and SAG, including myocardial infarction, stroke, heart failure, and sternal reconstruction. Our adjusted analysis of 22 764 matched pairs found that the protection of MAG against myocardial infarction and recurrent angina appeared as early as 30 days postoperatively, potentially attributing to superior early patency of arterial conduits. 34 However, the MAG cohort had a higher incidence of early DSWI in comparison to SAG. The implementation of MAG remains low worldwide, in part due to the concern of DSWI associated BIMA grafting. The BIMA technique is a common configuration to achieve MAG but may increase the risk of DSWI by 62%, according to a meta‐analysis 35 of observational studies. The BIMA cohort from the Arterial Revascularization Trial also demonstrated a higher incidence of DSWI (3.5% versus 1.9%; P=0.005) and sternal reconstruction procedures (1.9% versus 0.6%; P=0.002) than single internal mammary artery–grafted patients. While the use of BIMA is less common in Australia, its risk may be sufficiently high to raise the incidence of DSWI in MAG.

The use of the RA has increasingly become an attractive option to graft the most important non‐LAD target in preference to the right internal mammary artery. 36 According to our Australian registry, the RA has been the preferred second arterial conduit over right internal mammary artery. An individual patient meta‐analysis 5 followed up at a median of 10 years found that the use of the RA compared with SVG was associated with statistically significant reduction in the incidence of major adverse cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events (HR, 0.73 [95% CI, 0.61–0.88]; P<0.001) and all‐cause death (HR, 0.73 [95% CI, 0.57–0.93]; P=0.01). Tranbaugh et al 37 conducted a retrospective comparative analysis between the RA and right internal mammary artery, reporting similar late‐term patency (84% versus 87%; P=0.155) and rate of survival (85% versus 80%; P=0.06). In contrast, the 10‐year analysis of the RAPCO (Radial Artery Patency and Clinical Outcomes) trial 38 demonstrated significantly better estimated patency (HR, 0.45 [95% CI, 0.23–0.88]; P=0.018) and survival (HR, 0.53 [95% CI, 0.30–0.95]; P=0.03) in the RA as compared with its free right internal mammary artery arm. Despite the discordant evidence on long‐term outcomes, RA grafting not only minimizes perioperative comorbidities 18 , 37 and harvesting site complications 39 but also allows simultaneous left internal mammary artery harvest to reduce operative duration.

Strength and Limitations

This study comprises the largest multicenter analysis on the outcomes of MAG versus SAG, reporting both short‐term and long‐term adverse events according to extent of arterial revascularization. The sample size allows an extensive comparison between patients undergoing 4‐Art versus 3‐Art grafting, which is the first of its kind in the literature. The regular maintenance of Australian and New Zealand Society of Cardiac and Thoracic Surgeons registry with population coverage and national death linkage ensures the accuracy and reliability of data analyzed. Rigorous PSM adjustment of 41 preoperative and perioperative covariates was performed to account for imbalanced baseline characteristics among patients. However, the results should be interpreted cautiously in the context of intrinsic limitations of a retrospective study design. Although we used PSM to minimize treatment allocation bias, no statistical methodology can eliminate unmeasured confounders. Details regarding harvesting techniques, graft configurations, quality of conduits, preoperative coronary stenosis, and socioeconomic factors were not captured but may potentially impose a confounding impact on the outcome measures. Finally, the surgical expertise and interhospital discrepancies in the CABG practice can neither be measured nor controlled.

Conclusions

Coronary bypass surgery with multiple arterial grafting is associated with significantly reduced late death compared with single arterial grafting, but this benefit is likely reduced when saphenous vein graft is also used. These results need to be confirmed with a randomized study.

Mapping the dense molecular gas towards thirteen supernova remnants Facilities: PMO:DLH, No: 45m, VLA
Tian-yu Tu (涂天宇), Yang Chen (陈阳), Qian-Cheng Liu (刘前程)

Abstract
Supernova remnants (SNRs) can exert strong influence on molecular clouds (MCs) through interaction by shock wave and cosmic rays. In this paper, we present our mapping observation of HCO + \rm HCO^{+} and HCN \rm HCN 1–0 lines towards 13 SNRs interacting with MCs, together with archival data of CO isotopes. Strong HCO + \rm HCO^{+} emission is found in the fields of view (FOVs) of SNRs W30, G9.7 − - 0.0, Kes 69, 3C 391, 3C 396, W51C, HC 40, and CTB109 in the local-standard-of-rest (LSR) velocity intervals in which they are suggested to show evidence of SNR-MC interaction. We find an incomplete CO 12 \rm{}^{12}CO shell surrounding G9.7 − - 0.0 with an expanding motion. This shell may be driven by the stellar wind of the SNR progenitor. We also find an arc of CO 12 \rm{}^{12}CO gas spatially coincident with the northwestern radio shell of Kes 69. As for the HCO + \rm HCO^{+} line emission, SNRs 3C 391 and W51C exhibit significant line profile broadening indicative of shock perturbation, and CTB109 exhibits a possible blue-shifted line wing brought by shock interaction. We do not find significant variation of the I ⁡ ( HCO + ) / I ⁡ ( HCN ) I({\rm HCO^{+}})/I({\rm HCN}) line ratio between broad-line and narrow-line regions, among different SNRs, and between MCs associated with SNRs and typical Galactic MCs. Therefore, we caution on using the I ⁡ ( HCO + ) / I ⁡ ( HCN ) I({\rm HCO^{+}})/I({\rm HCN}) line ratio as a diagnostic of SNR feedback and CR ionization. We also estimate the N ⁡ ( HCO + ) / N ⁡ ( CO ) N({\rm HCO^{+}})/N({\rm CO}) abundance ratio in 11 regions towards the observed SNRs, but they show little difference from the typical values in quiescent MCs, possibly because N ⁡ ( HCO + ) / N ⁡ ( CO ) N({\rm HCO^{+}})/N({\rm CO}) is not an effective tracer of CR ionization.

I
Introduction

Massive (
\gtrsim 8M_{\odot}
) stars, born in molecular clouds (MCs) and ending their lives as supernovae (SNe), each re-deposit
\sim 10^{51}\rm\ erg
back to the interstellar medium and regulate the galactic ecology and evolution
[
61
]
.
Many supernova remnants (SNRs) are found to be located in the vicinity of MCs
[
38
,
19
, e.g.]
.
If an SNR is interacting with an MC, the shock, X-ray emission, and the cosmic rays (CRs) accelerated by the SNR can strongly affect the physical and chemical properties of the MC
[
91
]
, which can be observed in terms of molecular transitions.

CO and its isotopes are the most popular molecular species employed to investigate the SNR-MC interaction
[
103
, e.g.,]
which can be evidenced by morphology alignment of the CO emission with the SNR radio emission, and the line broadening or line wings
[
101
,
81
,
38
,
102
, e.g.,]
.
However, the CO molecules can only trace the most extended and low-density (with critical density
1
1

1

The definition of critical density of a molecular transition is not consistent in a variety of the literature. Here we define it as the Einstein
A
coefficient divided by the collisional rate coefficient at a specific temperature. The coefficients are taken from the Leiden Atomic and Molecular Database (LAMDA):
https://home.strw.leidenuniv.nl/~moldata/
.
of
\sim 2\times 10^{3}\rm\ cm^{-3}
for the 1–0 line and
3\times 10^{4}\rm\ cm^{-3}
for the 3–2 line at 10 K) part of the MCs.
Although denser shocked gas with broadened line profiles has been found in several SNRs
[
45
,
64
,
65
,
49
, e.g.,]
, mapping observations of dense molecular gas around SNRs still lack.

The
\rm HCO^{+}
and
\rm HCN
1–0 lines, with critical densities of
\sim 2\times 10^{5}\rm\ cm^{-3}
and
10^{6}\rm\ cm^{-3}
, respectively, are typical dense gas tracers in MCs
[
78
]
.
The integrated intensity ratio of the two lines (hereafter called line ratio for simplicity) have been used as diagnostics of physical processes in extragalactic studies
[
48
,
21
, e.g.]
.
A simultaneous mapping of CO and these two dense gas tracers can provide a comprehensive view of the molecular environment of SNRs.

SNRs are believed to be the main accelerators of Galactic CRs
[
4
, e.g.,]
.
The high-energy (with kinetic energy
\gtrsim 280
MeV) CR protons can generate
\gamma
-ray emission originated from the decay of
\pi^{0}
mesons produced in the collision between the high-energy CR protons and H nuclei in MCs, which is called the hadronic scenario.
The high-energy CR electrons can produce
\gamma
-ray emission via the inverse Compton scattering of background radiation, which is called the leptonic scenario.
The
\gamma
-ray emission can be detected by instruments such as the
Fermi
Large Area Telescope
[
2
]
.
On the other hand, the low-energy CR protons serve as the main source of ionization in dark MCs shielded from UV radiation
[
60
]
.
This process starts the formation of poly-atomic molecules in MCs and regulates the molecular chemistry in MCs.

Direct estimation of CR ionization rates in MCs associated with SNRs typically uses the
N({\rm HCO^{+}})/N({\rm CO})
and
N({\rm DCO^{+}})/N({\rm HCO^{+}})
abundance ratios
[
18
,
90
]
.
Although the emission of
\rm DCO^{+}
is hard to detect, both observations obtained enhanced CR ionization rates.
The enhanced CR ionization rates induced by SNRs can also be revealed indirectly by various observations, including 1720 MHz OH masers and 6.4 keV Fe I K
\alpha
line.
The production of the 1720 MHz OH masers requires enhanced CR ionization rate in addition to SNR-MC interaction
[
53
,
94
]
.
The Fe I K
\alpha
line is produced via inner-shell ionization of neutral iron when protons in the MeV band collide with MCs
[
59
]
.
Detection of hadronic
\gamma
-ray emission also hints that low-energy CR protons are possibly also accelerated and ionizing the MCs.

The
\rm HCO^{+}
molecule has been expected to be a tracer of CR ionization rate in MCs
[
18
,
12
,
7
]
.
Specifically, enhanced abundance of
\rm HCO^{+}
relative to CO due to CR ionization in shocked MCs has been found in SNRs W49B and W28
[
100
,
85
]

Therefore, observation of
\rm HCO^{+}
in MCs associated SNRs may provide information about the CR acceleration and ionization in SNRs.

In this paper, we performed new mapping observations in
\rm HCO^{+}
and
\rm HCN
1–0 lines, supplemented by archival data of CO isotopes, towards a sample of 13 SNRs suggested to be interacting with MCs in order to obtain the spatial distribution of dense molecular gas around them, search for shocked dense gas with line profiles broadened, and study the viability of
I({\rm HCO^{+}})/I({\rm HCN})
line ratio as a diagnostic of SN feedback and CR ionization.

The paper is structured as follows.
In Section
II
, we describe our new observations and other archival data.
We present the observational results in Section
III
and discuss the SNR-MC interaction, the
I({\rm HCO^{+}})/I({\rm HCN})
line ratio, and the
N({\rm HCO^{+}})/N({\rm CO})
abundance ratio in Section
IV
.
Finally, a summary is given in Section
V
.

G8.7
-
0.1 (W30)
:
Our map of W30 is centered on the 1720 MHz OH maser discovered by
Hewitt & Yusef-Zadeh [34]

The boundary of W30 shown in Figure

https://www.mrao.cam.ac.uk/surveys/snrs/snrs.info.html

The emission of
\rm HCO^{+}
mainly consists of three parts: a stripe extending southward from the OH maser, another brighter stripe located to the east of the first stripe, and a very bright source containing several HII regions
[
25
]
in the southern edge of the map.
Towards the OH maser, the spectrum of
\rm HCO^{+}
consists of two components centered at
\approx+36
and
\approx+38

\rm km\ s^{-1}
, consistent with the
\rm{}^{13}CO
1–0 line, while in the bright source W30-S, the two components of
\rm HCO^{+}
are located at
\approx+34

\approx+40

\rm km\ s^{-1}

+36

\rm km\ s^{-1}

\rm HCO^{+}

-

\rm HCO^{+}

and
\rm C^{18}O

\approx+43.4

\rm km\ s^{-1}
.

\rm HCO^{+}
is the same as that of
\rm{}^{13}CO
and
\rm C^{18}O
(
\sim+43

\rm km\ s^{-1}

\rm HCO^{+}

\rm HCO^{+}
and
\rm HCN

\rm{}^{13}CO

-

IV
Discussion

IV.1
SNR-MC interaction

Evidence of SNR-MC interaction includes morphological agreement of molecular lines with SNR emission in radio or X-ray band, molecular line broadening or asymmetric line profile, 1720 MHz OH masers, line emission with high high-to-low excitation line ratio, etc.
[
38
]
.
In this section, we investigate the first two observational facts in our sample of SNRs.

IV.1.1
Morphology agreement of
\rm{}^{12}CO
molecular features with SNRs G9.7 and Kes 69

G9.7 was identified by
Brogan et al. [15]
as an SNR, and was confirmed to be interacting with MCs by the detection of a 1720 MHz OH maser at +43
\rm km\ s^{-1}

[
34
]
.
However, no detailed molecular line observation was conducted before.
In the left panel of Figure
3
, we show an incomplete
\rm{}^{12}CO
shell surrounding the radio boundary of G9.7 (§
III.2
).
This suggests that the
\rm{}^{12}CO
shell may be associated with the SNR.
The
\rm{}^{12}CO
bubble, expanding at a velocity of
\sim 4

\rm km\ s^{-1}
, is likely to be driven by the stellar wind of the SNR progenitor.
This scenario can be seen in a number of molecular bubbles surrounding SNRs, such as Tycho’s SNR
[
99
]
, VRO 42.05.01
[
8
]
, and Kes 67 (Shen et al., submitted), with expanding velocities around 5
\rm km\ s^{-1}
.

We note that the OH maser detected by
Hewitt & Yusef-Zadeh [34]
seems to be located outside the radio boundary.
This may be due to the limited sensitivity of the radio image, since the data of
Brogan et al. [15]
shows that the 327 MHz radio continuum extends towards the northeastern side and approaches the position of the OH maser.
The radio morphology in
Brogan et al. [15]
also exhibits an agreement with the
\rm HCO^{+}
emission, suggesting possible interaction.

SNR Kes 69 was found to be interacting with the MCs by the detection of both compact OH maser
[
32
]
and extended OH maser
[
35
]
.
The CO emission was reported by
Zhou et al. [101]
who identified a
\rm{}^{12}CO
arc coincident with the radio arc located in the southeastern boundary of Kes 69.
They also plotted a
\rm{}^{12}CO
arc located towards the northwest of Kes 69, but limited by the sensitivity of the radio data, the coincidence of the arc and the radio shell was confused with an HII region G21.902
-
0.368.
The new radio data clearly shows a radio shell towards the northwest of Kes 69, making up a circle (similar to that in Figures 4 and 5 in
Zhou et al. [101]
) together with the southeastern radio shell (see Figure
4
).
Considering that the northwestern
\rm{}^{12}CO
emission is located at the similar velocity of the southeastern
\rm{}^{12}CO
shell (
\sim+81

\rm km\ s^{-1}
), we suggest that, in addition to the known molecular shell along the southeastern boundary
of the SNR, there is also a molecular arc along the northwestern boundary.

IV.1.2
Line broadening and asymmetric line profile

Molecular line broadening and asymmetric line profile have also been regarded as strong evidence of SNR-MC interacting
[
38
]
.
This criterion has been widely used in the diagnostics with CO lines: asymmetric
\rm{}^{12}CO
line together with non-detection of
\rm{}^{13}CO
has been used to identify the SNR-cloud interaction in SNRs W44
[
75
]
, 3C 396
[
82
]
, HB3
[
102
]
, G51.26
+
0.11
[
97
]
, etc.
However, the CO lines may suffer from line crowding, especially in the inner Galaxy, where multiple emission components of the
\rm{}^{12}CO
1–0 line crowd in a small velocity interval, which would confuse the line broadening due to SNR-MC interaction.

Compared with the
\rm{}^{12}CO
1–0 line, the
\rm HCO^{+}
1–0 line traces a denser part of molecular cloud and suffer less from line crowding.
The
\rm HCO^{+}
1–0 transition is easy to get optically thick
[
89
]
, which allows it to be broadened by shock disturbance.
Therefore, broadened
\rm HCO^{+}
1–0 can also serve as a shock tracer.
Among the 13 SNRs we observed, we find shocked
\rm HCO^{+}
towards 3C 391 and W51C (3C 391-OH and W51C-C2, see Figure
2
), consistent with the results of
Reach & Rho [64]
and
Koo & Moon [45]
.

In addition to these two SNRs known to exhibit broadened
\rm HCO^{+}
line, a blue-shifted line wing of
\rm HCO^{+}
towards the CTB109-N region is also noticeable.
This region is compatible with the X-ray absorption reported by
Sasaki et al. [73]
.
If the MC is absorbing the X-ray emission of the SNR, it could be located in front of the SNR.
The SNR shock interacting with the MC could drives the molecular gas to move towards us, which results in a blue-shifted wing in molecular transitions, as we can see in the
\rm HCO^{+}
1–0 line.
However, we could still not rule out the possibility that the line wing is caused by another component due to the limited signal-to-noise ratio (S/N) of our data.
Further high-sensitivity observation is needed to confirm the interaction between CTB109 and its adjacent dense molecular gas.

In the FOVs of other SNRs, we do not detect any shock broadening or line wings brought by the SNRs.
However, our observations are limited in sensitivity, and the
\sim 1^{\prime}
beam size may also lead to severe beam dilution, both of which could make it difficult for us to detect the shocked
\rm HCO^{+}
and
\rm HCN
emission.
According to
Zhou et al. [100]
, the peak main beam temperature
T_{\rm peak}
of the shocked
\rm HCO^{+}
emission can be as low as
\sim 0.04
K.
Therefore, our observations could have missed some shocked dense gas in the SNRs.
Besides, for young SNRs, it is possible that the shock-cloud interaction has too short timescale to have caused detectable line broadening
[
30
]
.

The 1720 MHz OH maser is strong signpost of SNR-MC interaction
[
38
, e.g.,]
.
This OH satellite line (
{}^{2}\Pi_{3/2},\ J=3/2,\ F=2\rightarrow 1
) maser is pumped by collisions with other molecular species
[
94
]
.
The pumping of these masers requires a moderately high density (
\sim 10^{5}\rm\ cm^{-3}
), warm temperature (
\sim
50–125 K), and high column density of OH (
\sim 10^{-16}
–
\rm 10^{-17}\rm\ cm^{-2}
)
[
53
]
.
The required density is in favor of the excitation of
\rm HCO^{+}
and
\rm HCN
.
However, in our observation, although significant
\rm HCO^{+}
emission is found towards regions W30-OH, G9.7-OH, 3C 391-OH, and W51C-OH, no
\rm HCO^{+}
emission is spatially coincident with the OH masers towards G16.7
+
0.1, the northeast of Kes 69, the northeast of 3C 391, and Kes 78.
Although this may be due to the limited sensitivity of our observation, a more naturally explanation is that the OH masers are rather compact
[
36
,
\sim 10^{2}\text{--}10^{3}\rm\ AU
compared with the
1^{\prime}
beam of our observation which corresponds to
\sim 1
pc at a distance of 4 kpc,]
and only reflect the physical conditions in a very small region.
Therefore, it is unreasonable to use the pumping condition of the OH masers to represent the physical properties of the entire MC.

in the FOVs of the 8 SNRs with significant

The velocity ranges for the integration are chosen so as to cover all the
\rm HCO^{+}
and
\rm HCN
molecules with suggested interaction with the SNRs, and only points with a
\rm S/N>5\sigma
are included.
We also show the line ratios towards W28-NE and W28-S1 (See
Tu et al. [85]
and Fugure 1 therein for the definition of the two small regions: W28-NE is the shocked MC towards SNR W28, while W28-S1 is a complex of MCs exhibiting star-forming activities free from the disturbance of W28).
The values of
I({\rm HCO^{+}})/I({\rm HCN})
towards 3C 391-OH and W51C-C2, i.e. the two shocked MCs with broadened
\rm HCO^{+}
and
\rm HCN
lines, are shown in horizontal orange lines.

As can be seen from the figure, the median values of
I({\rm HCO^{+}})/I({\rm HCN})
in all of the SNRs fall in 0.65–1.0, except for Kes 69 of which the median value is 0.5.
The scatters of the
I({\rm HCO^{+}})/I({\rm HCN})
ratios (estimated as the difference between the quartiles and the median values) are all within 0.2, except for W51C of which the upper quartile is 0.3 greater than the median value.
The
I({\rm HCO^{+}})/I({\rm HCN})
ratios exhibit little difference in shocked (broadened line, in W28-NE, 3C 391-OH and W51C-C2) and unshocked (narrow line) clouds, and dose not show significant variation among SNRs.

The observed region of SNR W51C exhibits the largest dispersion and the highest values of
I({\rm HCO^{+}})/I({\rm HCN})
.
In Figure
6
, we show the
I({\rm HCO^{+}})/I({\rm HCN})
line ratio map towards W51C.
It is clear that the line ratio is higher towards the northeastern and lower towards the southwestern.
To investigate why
I({\rm HCO^{+}})/I({\rm HCN})
is enhanced towards the northeastern part of the observed region, we take a closer look at the spectra of W51C-NE, and make a grid of
\rm HCO^{+}
,
\rm HCN
and
\rm C^{18}O
spectra in Figure
7
.

From the spectra of W51C-NE (Figure
2
), we find that the spectrum of
\rm HCO^{+}
is red-shifted compared with
\rm C^{18}O
, while
\rm HCN
is further red-shifted compared with
\rm HCO^{+}
.
Both
\rm HCO^{+}
and
\rm HCN
exhibit asymmetric line profile, with a sharp decrease towards the blue side.
Figure
7
shows that the velocity shift of
\rm HCO^{+}
and
\rm HCN
pervades the entire region with enhanced
I({\rm HCO^{+}})/I({\rm HCN})
line ratio.
For the
\rm HCN
line, we notice a weak component at
\approx+59

\rm km\ s^{-1}
in W51C-NE, which is not consistent with any other components shown in other molecular transitions.
The high
I({\rm HCO^{+}})/I({\rm HCN})
and velocity shift could be explained by self-absorption of
\rm HCO^{+}
and
\rm HCN
, which is stronger for the
\rm HCN
line.
The self-absorption is blue-shifted so that the resulting spectra are red-shifted.
In this case, the +59
\rm km\ s^{-1}
component corresponds to an unabsorbed hyperfine structure of
\rm HCN
.
This scenario is similar to the blue-profile of contracting molecular cloud cores
[
24
]
, but the “red-profile” in our case indicates expansion
[
57
, e.g.]
.
The expanding motion could be due to oscillation of the MC triggered by external perturbation
[
28
]
like the heating of the HII region G49.2
-
0.3 with bright compact radio emission towards the south of W51C-NE
[
44
]
.
We note that the stronger absorption of
\rm HCN
than
\rm HCO^{+}
is anomalous because the
\rm HCO^{+}
line tends to be more susceptible to self-absorption
[
1
]
.
A possible explanation of our result is that the absorbing layer has enhanced abundance of
\rm HCN
, leading to a stronger absorption.

The
I({\rm HCO^{+}})/I({\rm HCN})
line ratio has been studied in various astrophysical conditions.
This value is
\sim 1.1
in the Orion B giant molecular cloud (GMC)
[
71
]
,
\sim 0.9
in giant molecular filament 54 (GMF54)
[
93
]
,
\sim 1.32
in infrared dark clouds (IRDCs)
[
52
, e.g.]
,
\sim 2
in MCs in the outer Milky Way
[
14
]
,
\sim 0.6
in the central molecular zone (CMZ) of our Galaxy
[
41
,
70
, e.g.,]
,
\sim 0.8
in the star-forming disk of nearby massive spiral galaxies
[
39
]
, and varying from
\sim 0.4
to
\sim 1.4
in nearby active galaxies
[
5
, e.g.]
.
Previous studies have found that mechanical heating by SNRs can lead to a high
I({\rm HCO^{+}})/I({\rm HCN})
line ratio at low density and low
I({\rm HCO^{+}})/I({\rm HCN})
at high density
[
54
,
21
,
63
]
.
High CR ionization rate, which is another factor brought by SNRs
[
18
,
90
, e.g.,]
, can also enhance the
I({\rm HCO^{+}})/I({\rm HCN})
line ratio by altering the abundance ratio between
\rm HCO^{+}
and
\rm HCN
because the abundance of
\rm HCO^{+}
is expected to be enhanced with higher CR ionization rate
[
20
,
62
]
while
\rm HCN
is not sensitive to CR ionization rate
[
7
,
37
]
.
However, we do not find solid evidence of variation in
I({\rm HCO^{+}})/I({\rm HCN})
line ratio induced by SNRs from our observation.
The difference in
I({\rm HCO^{+}})/I({\rm HCN})
between broad-line and narrow-line emission in W28, 3C 391 and W51C is not significant, suggesting that the heating effect of the shock wave does not have a strong impact on
I({\rm HCO^{+}})/I({\rm HCN})
.
Concerning CRs, which can diffuse or escape from the shock layer and induce ionization in narrow-line regions
[
18
,
90
]
, we also fail to find prominent difference in
I({\rm HCO^{+}})/I({\rm HCN})
between SNRs and typical MCs, which does not support the enhancement of
I({\rm HCO^{+}})/I({\rm HCN})
by CRs.

We note, however, that CR ionization can affect the molecular abundances, but the relation between the abundance ratio and the line ratio is complicated.
Besides the abundances of
\rm HCO^{+}
and
\rm HCN
, the line ratio is also affected by several physical factors such as gas density, temperature, infrared pumping, electron excitation, elemental abundance, etc. (see
Salak et al. [69]
and the reference therein).
It is also possible that SNRs have similar effects on the brightness of
\rm HCO^{+}
and
\rm HCN
especially in shocked regions, since studies have suggested the chemical similarity between
\rm HCO^{+}
and
\rm HCN

[
52
, e.g.]
.
As a result, these effects may be eliminated when calculating the line ratio.

To estimate the column densities of CO and
\rm HCO^{+}
, we follow the method in
Mangum & Shirley [56]
based on the local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) assumption.
We also assume that the beam filling factor is 1.
Since all the relevant
\rm{}^{12}CO
components are consistent with a
\rm{}^{13}CO
or
\rm C^{18}O
component, the
\rm{}^{12}CO
emission is expected to be optically thick, so the excitation temperature of the
\rm{}^{12}CO
lines can be estimated by:

T_{\rm ex}=\frac{h\nu/k}{\ln{\left(1+\frac{h\nu/k}{T_{\rm peak}+J_{\nu}(T_{\rm bg})}\right)}},

(1)

where
J_{\nu}(T)=(h\nu/k)/[\exp{(h\nu/kT)}-1]
is the Rayleigh-Jeans equivalent temperature and
T_{\rm bg}=2.73
K.
In the LTE condition, the excitation temperatures are assumed to be the same for different molecular transitions.
The column densities of the species can then be estimated by:

\begin{split}N=\left(\frac{3h}{8\pi^{3}S\mu^{2}R_{i}}\right)\left(\frac{Q_{\rm rot}}{g_{I}g_{J}g_{K}}\right)\left(\frac{\exp{(E_{\rm u}/kT_{\rm ex})}}{\exp{(h\nu/kT_{\rm ex})}-1}\right)\\
\times\int-\ln\left[1-\frac{T_{\rm mb}}{J_{\nu}(T_{\rm ex})-J_{\nu}(T_{\rm bg})}\right]\,dv,\end{split}

(2)

where
Q_{\rm rot}\approx kT/hb+1/3
is the rotational partition function and other parameters were explained in detail by
Mangum & Shirley [56]
.
The estimated excitation temperatures and column densities are listed in Table
2
.

We note that the typical isotope ratios in the Galactic inner disk (2–6 kpc from the Galactic center) are
\rm{}^{12}C/^{13}C\sim 40
and
\rm{}^{16}O/^{18}O\sim 327

[
95
]
.
However, the isotope ratios of CO we obtained is significantly lower than these typical values (See Table
2
).
This could be because the optical depths are underestimated in Equation
2
.
For
\rm{}^{12}CO
, its 1–0 transition tends to be optically thick, so the column densities may be underestimated, while the estimated column densities of
\rm{}^{13}CO
and
\rm C^{18}O
are closer to the real values than
\rm{}^{12}CO
does because they are almost optically thin.
The
\rm HCO^{+}
1–0 line, although much weaker than the
\rm{}^{12}CO
1–0 line, could also be optically thick even when the column density is not high (
\gtrsim 10^{12}\rm\ cm^{-2}
)
[
89
]
.
Therefore, the column densities of
\rm HCO^{+}
in Table
2
could also be underestimated.
To reduce the uncertainty brought by the underestimation of optical depth in Equation
2
to some extent, we divide
N(\rm HCO^{+})
by the
N(\rm CO)
directly obtained from the
\rm{}^{12}CO
spectra instead of using the CO isotopes scaled with the typical isotope ratios.
However, this cannot fully eliminate the uncertainty on optical depth.
Another source of uncertainty is the LTE assumption, which may be invalid at low volume densities.
Therefore, our calculation of
N({\rm HCO^{+}})/N({\rm CO})
can only be regarded as a rough order-of-magnitude estimation.
The results of our estimation are listed in Table
2
.
We find that the
N({\rm HCO^{+}})/N({\rm CO})
in our selected regions are all of orders
\sim 10^{-5}
or
\sim 10^{-6}
.

Enhanced
N({\rm HCO^{+}})/N({\rm CO})
has been proposed to be a tracer of enhanced CR ionization rate according to chemical simulations
[
18
,
12
, e.g.,]
.
However,
N({\rm HCO^{+}})/N({\rm CO})
varies significantly from cloud to cloud, and we do not find difference between the MCs associated with the SNRs in our sample and typical quiescent MCs (
\sim 10^{-4}
–
\sim 10^{-6}

[
3
,
58
,
29
, e.g.,]
), even if in the SNRs with evidence of enhanced CR ionization rates (e.g., W30, G9.7, Kes 69, 3C 391, 3C 396, W51C, and CTB109 as listed in Table
1
).
Similarly, in most SNRs with solid evidence of enhanced CR ionization rates, the
N({\rm HCO^{+}})/N({\rm CO})
abundance ratios (
\sim 4\times 10^{-5}
in W51C
[
18
]
and W28
[
90
]
, and
\sim 8\times 10^{-5}
in 3C 391
[
86
]
) do not deviate from the typical values in quiescent MCs.
In W51C and W28 where the enhanced CR ionization rates were estimated with both
N({\rm HCO^{+}})/N({\rm CO})
and
N({\rm DCO^{+}})/N({\rm HCO^{+}})

[
18
,
90
]
, the results were mainly deduced from the unusually low column densities of
\rm DCO^{+}
instead of enhanced
N({\rm HCO^{+}})/N({\rm CO})
.
Therefore, it appears that
N({\rm HCO^{+}})/N({\rm CO})
is not an effective tracer of chemistry induced by CRs.
Significantly enhanced
N({\rm HCO^{+}})/N({\rm CO})
(
\sim 10^{-3}
) has only been found in SNR W49B
[
100
]
, but this high ratio was discovered in the shocked MC with broadened molecular line profile where shock chemistry cannot be ignored.
Another example is SNR W28, where the
N({\rm HCO^{+}})/N({\rm CO})
is enhanced in shocked MC (
\sim 10^{-4}
) compared with unshocked MCs close to the SNR (
\sim 10^{-5}
)
[
85
]
.
It is likely that shock interaction is important for CRs to enhance the
N({\rm HCO^{+}})/N({\rm CO})
abundance ratio.

We note, from Table
1
, that in most SNRs with signature of enhanced CR ionization rates,
\rm HCO^{+}
emission is detected (W30, G9.7, Kes 69, 3C 391, Kes 78, W51C, and CTB109).
This suggests that the detection of
\rm HCO^{+}
indicates the presence of the target MCs to produce hadronic
\gamma
-rays. However, there are cases where
\rm HCO^{+}
is detected with no evidence of enhanced CR ionization (HC40) and where no
\rm HCO^{+}
is detected but solid evidence of enhanced CR ionization has been observed (G16.7+0.1).
Therefore, whether
\rm HCO^{+}
can be detected depends strongly on the local environment of the SNR, especially the gas density and temperature, instead of whether the CR ionization rate is enhanced to elevate the abundance of
\rm HCO^{+}
.

X-rays from the SNRs can also lead to enhanced ionization rates in MCs
[
55
,
92
]
.
However, according to the estimation of
Tu et al. [86]
, the X-ray ionization rate induced by SNR 3C 391 is negligible.
Since 3C 391 is a bright SNR in the X-ray band and the other SNRs we observe do not show significantly high X-ray luminosities
[
6
, e.g.,]
, we consider that X-ray ionization in the target SNRs can be ignored.

V
Conclusion

In this paper, We present our observation of
\rm HCO^{+}
and
\rm HCN
1–0 lines towards 13 SNRs interacting with MCs with the PMO 13.7m telescope, supplemented by archival data of CO isotopes.
The results can be summarized as follows:

1. We find strong emission of
\rm HCO^{+}
towards the FOVs of SNRs W30, G9.7
-
0.0, Kes 69, 3C 391, 3C 396, W51C, HC 40 and CTB109 in the velocity intervals proposed to show evidence of SNR-MC interaction.
Weak
\rm HCO^{+}
emission is detected in Kes 78, while no
\rm HCO^{+}
emission is found in G16.7
+
0.1, 3C 397, Kes 75 and CTB87.

2. We find a
\rm{}^{12}CO
arc surrounding the radio continuum of SNR G9.7
-
0.0 in the northern, eastern and southern part, with an expansion as revealed by the position-velocity diagram around the LSR velocity of the 1720 MHz OH maser.
This bubble is likely to be driven by the stellar wind of the SNR progenitor.
With the new radio data, we also find a
\rm{}^{12}CO
arc spatially coincident with the northwestern radio arc of Kes 69.
This suggests that, in addition to the known molecular shell along the southeastern boundary
of the SNR, there is also a molecular arc along the northwestern boundary.

3. Significant shock broadening is found in SNRs 3C 391 and W51C, and CTB109 exhibits a possible blue-shifted line wing brought by shock interaction.
The line profile towards CTB109-N is consistent with the X-ray absorption.
This non-detection of line broadening in other SNRs may be due to the limited sensitivity and angular resolution of our observation, as well as the possibility that the timescale of the interaction is too short to allow for detectable line broadening.
For the 1720 MHz OH masers towards G16.7
+
0.1, the northeast of Kes 69, the northeast of 3C 391, and Kes 78, we did not find corresponding
\rm HCO^{+}
or
\rm HCN
emission, probably because the OH masers only take up a rather small physical scale and cannot represent the physical properties of the entire MC.

4. The median values of
I({\rm HCO^{+}})/I({\rm HCN})
in all of the SNRs except Kes 69 fall in 0.65–1.0.
The highest values and largest scatter of
I({\rm HCO^{+}})/I({\rm HCN})
are found in W51C, which may be caused by self-absorption which is affected by expansion motion and is more severe for
\rm HCN
.
We do not find significant variation of
I({\rm HCO^{+}})/I({\rm HCN})
between broad-line and narrow-line regions, and among different SNRs.
The obtained
I({\rm HCO^{+}})/I({\rm HCN})
also deviate little from typical values found in Galactic MCs.
The observed
I({\rm HCO^{+}})/I({\rm HCN})
line ratio results from a complex interplay of excitation and chemical effects, so we caution on using
I({\rm HCO^{+}})/I({\rm HCN})
line ratio as a diagnostic of SNR feedback and CR ionization.

5. We estimate the
N({\rm HCO^{+}})/N({\rm CO})
abundance ratio in 11 regions towards the observed SNRs.
The abundance ratios
N({\rm HCO^{+}})/N({\rm CO})
in all of the selected regions are of orders
\sim 10^{-5}
or
\sim 10^{-6}
, which is similar to the values in typical quiescent MCs.
Combining the
N({\rm HCO^{+}})/N({\rm CO})
ratio towards the points with enhanced CR ionization rates in W28, W51C and 3C 391, we suggest that the
N({\rm HCO^{+}})/N({\rm CO})
ratio may not be an effective tracer of CR-induced chemistry.

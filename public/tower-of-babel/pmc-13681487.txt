Management of Grade II osteoarthritis (Azhal kilvayu) utilizing Siddha internal and external treatment modalities (Varmam and Thokkanam): A case report
Authors: Nikil Niva John Raja, Susila Rathinam, Hema Nandhini Devi Veluchamy, Sridevi Lakshmikanthan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A 59-year-old female patient visited the clinic with complaints of pain in both knee joints, along with mild swelling that had persisted for three months. Radiographic examination revealed Grade II knee osteoarthritis (OA). Her treatment plan included Siddha therapeutic purgation (Agathiyar kulambu), Siddha internal medications (Parangipattai maathirai, Sangu Parpam maathirai), and Siddha external therapies (Varmam and Thokkanam). Vata Kesari tailam was specifically selected for Thokkanam therapy. Remarkably, the Knee Injury and Osteoarthritis Outcome score significantly improved from 19 before treatment to 85 by day 60. The visual analogue scale score also revealed a substantial reduction in pain throughout the treatment period, dropping from eight to two points after the intervention. This notable improvement reflects not only a decrease in pain but also a significant increase in the patient's overall quality of life. Specific metrics indicate considerable progress in critical areas, including activities of daily living (ADL) and participation in sports and recreational activities. The findings of this case report elucidate a noteworthy therapeutic approach for managing osteoarthritis (Azhal kilvayu). The case illustrates a comprehensive approach that combines conventional diagnostic assessments with Siddha therapeutic interventions.

Introduction

Knee osteoarthritis (OA), commonly known as degenerative joint disease, is a widespread ailment that primarily results from the progressive deterioration of the articular cartilage. This condition predominantly impacts the quality of life of older adults. This progressive disease can lead to increased levels of disability if not addressed. Although the intensity of symptoms may differ among individuals, they generally become more severe and disruptive over time [1].

Recognizing the factors that increase the likelihood of developing osteoarthritis is essential. These factors include age, being female, anatomical differences, obesity, muscle weakness, and joint injuries from work or sports. Primary osteoarthritis is the most common type of this debilitating condition; it arises without previous trauma or illness but is associated with the risk factors mentioned above. On the other hand, secondary osteoarthritis is a consequence of preexisting joint conditions, which can be caused by factors such as congenital joint disorders, inflammatory arthritis, trauma, avascular necrosis, Paget's disease, infectious arthritis, osteopetrosis, osteochondritis dissecans, and metabolic disorders including hemoglobinopathy, hemochromatosis, Wilson's disease, Ehlers–Danlos syndrome, or Marfan syndrome. [2]. The impact of osteoarthritis (OA) in India is both substantial and growing at an alarming rate. In 1990, approximately 23.46 million individuals were affected by OA, and by 2019, this figure had risen to 62.35 million. This pattern underscores the critical need for increased awareness, prevention strategies, and effective management of this debilitating disease [3].

The main aim of conservative treatment is to alleviate symptoms effectively and decelerate disease progression, which can help patients avoid resorting to invasive surgical procedures such as total knee replacement. To achieve this objective, several effective conservative treatments, including exercise, medications, weight reduction, and orthotics are available. The World Health Organization (WHO) offers a structured pain management strategy through a three-step ladder: Step I involves nonopioid analgesics, such as acetaminophen or nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs); Step II introduces mild opioids; and Step III provides strong opioids for more intense pain. This framework has been adapted for managing osteoarthritis pain, highlighting that if the current treatment step fails to offer sufficient relief, moving to the next step is necessary [4].

Traditional and complementary medicine (TCM) offers a range of efficacious approaches for the treatment of osteoarthritis (OA), including herbal remedies, manipulative therapies, leech therapy, and movement-based therapies. Research indicates that the use of TCM among OA patients across various countries is quite significant, with prevalence rates ranging between 40% and 89% [5].

In a recent publication by the World Health Organization, the WHO International Standard Terminologies on Siddha Medicine correlated osteoarthritis with Azhal kilvāayu (term ID: ISMT-4.24.66) [6]. Siddha medicine employs a comprehensive array of therapeutic interventions, including herbal formulations, dietary modifications, lifestyle changes, detoxification therapies, and external treatments such as Thokkanam (manual manipulation techniques), Varmam (pressure manipulation techniques), and Orratam (fomentation). A distinctive characteristic of Siddha treatment for musculoskeletal disorders, including osteoarthritis (OA), is the integration of internal and external therapies. These interventions are designed to effectively reduce inflammation, enhance joint lubrication, improve circulation, and strengthen the musculoskeletal system, thereby promoting overall health and well-being [7].

This case study reports a patient diagnosed with osteoarthritis (Azhal kilvayu) who underwent successful treatment with Siddha internal medications, Varmam, and Thokkanam therapy. The treatment adhered to the established guidelines for Siddha Varmam and Siddha standard treatment protocols. This case study was carefully crafted following CARE guidelines to ensure a thorough and organized approach. This case report illustrates an integrative medical approach in which Grade II osteoarthritis was diagnosed and systematically assessed through conventional radiographic findings and validated outcome measures. The management of the condition was conducted utilizing both Siddha internal and external therapeutic modalities.

Patient information

On August 12, 2024, a 59-year-old female patient visited the Siddha Outpatient Department of the Swasthya Rakshan Programme (SRP) at Ramakrishna Mission Ashram in New Delhi. She complained of pain in both knee joints accompanied by mild swelling for 3 months. The pain worsened with walking and increased during more active sessions. The patient also experienced stiffness in the morning and restricted movement in both knees as well as sleep disturbances due to pain. The patient experienced mild constipation. The patient had no history of diabetes mellitus, thyroid dysfunction, systemic hypertension, comorbidities or autoimmune disorders. She also reported no history of smoking, alcohol consumption, or pan chewing. Patient had no familial history and no previous surgical history. The patient was followed up with a vegetarian diet. She measured 156 cm in height, weighed 52 kg, and had a BMI of 21.4 kg/m2.

Six months prior, the patient had experienced mild pain and stiffness in the left knee joint. Over time, the pain gradually increased and restricted the flexion movement of the left knee. Eventually, pain begins to affect both knee joints, limiting their range of motion. The patient then visited a nearby healthcare center where the physician recommended X-rays of both knees. X-ray results revealed that the patient had Grade II osteoarthritis (OA). The physician prescribed medication and advised the patient to perform knee exercises. After this treatment, the patient experienced mild pain relief; however, pain and other clinical symptoms persisted. Consequently, the patient sought further management in the outpatient department of the Swasthya Rakshan Programme (SRP) at the Ramakrishna Mission Ashram.

During the general examination, the patient's appetite and bowel movements were normal. Her pulse rate was 73 beats/min, her blood pressure was 122/80 mmHg, and her body temperature was within the normal range.

Systemic examination revealed normal cardiorespiratory and neurological function. Abdominal examination revealed normal findings, with no dull sounds during percussion, and bowel sounds were normal upon auscultation.

During the knee examination, inspection revealed genu varum and swelling in both the knee joints. The circumference of the right knee joint was 38 cm and that of the left knee joint was 37 cm. Diagnostic findings

According to the Siddha literature, the clinical features of Azhal kilvayu include Muttu veekkam– swelling in the joints; Vali– pain; kiilkalil pasai varalal – gradual loss of articular cartilage or osteophytic changes; Kiil asaiyum pothellam nettaiyitalum kaluk kaluk Endra oli untathal – crepitation in the joints; Kiilkalai madakka neeta mudiyamai, reduced range of motion; and Poruthukalil pidippu– stiffness [8]. The patient's clinical characteristics align with those of Azhal kilvayu, which include swelling in the joints, pain, gradual loss of articular cartilage or osteophytic changes, crepitation in the joints, a reduced range of motion, and stiffness.

The digital skiagram of both knee joints, which included AP (standing) and lateral views, revealed the following findings: Evidence of mild-to-moderate osteoarthritis was observed in both knee joints, with marginal osteophytes observed in the tibiofemoral and patellofemoral articulations, and mild-to-moderate reduction in the joint space of the medial compartment of the tibiofemoral joint. At the lower end of the femur, the upper end of the tibia and the fibula of the patella appeared normal. The soft tissue shadows were normal.

The range of motion of the knee was assessed using a goniometer, and the Knee Injury and Osteoarthritis Outcome (KOOS) score was used to evaluate treatment prognosis [9]. Before treatment, the KOOS score was 19, which included symptoms (18%), pain (31%), activities of daily living (ADL) (25%), sports and recreation function (5%), and quality of life (19%). Pain was evaluated via the visual analogue scale (VAS). Before treatment, the visual analogue scale (VAS) score was 8.

On the basis of clinical features, including pain, swelling, crepitus, and tenderness in both knees, as well as diagnostic methods (allopathy and Siddha), previous history, and radiographic reports, the patient was diagnosed with Azhal kilvayu (osteoarthritis), which is predominantly affected by the Vali humor.

Therapeutic intervention

The comprehensive treatment plan is scheduled from August 13, 2024, to October 14, 2024. The treatment plan included Siddha therapeutic purgation, Siddha internal medications, and Siddha external therapy (Varmam and Thokkanam).

Therapeutic purgation

To restore balance to disturbed Vali humor, therapeutic purgation was initiated. For this purpose, Agathiyar kulambu (130 mg) mixed with ginger juice (Zingiber officinalis) was chosen. The patient was instructed to take Agathiyar kulambu between 5:30 a.m. and 6:00 a.m. on August 13, 2024. To ensure safety during therapeutic purgation, the physician recommended administering 2 g of Siddha antidiarrheal medicine Thayirchundi chooranam with buttermilk as a preventive measure in cases of uncontrolled loose stools and dehydration. Furthermore, the patient was instructed to increase water intake after the completion of the purgation process.

After taking the medication, the patient was advised to drink warm water frequently. Purgation commenced at 6:20 a.m., during which time the patient experienced mild vomiting and had loose stools seven times in the morning. The purgation process subsided by 11:00 a.m. Once purging was complete, the patient was advised to consume buttermilk and adhere to a specific diet of curd rice. After 11:00 a.m., the patient had not experienced any loose stools or vomiting. No blood tests or additional treatments were performed on the day of purgation therapy.

The following day, the patient was advised to visit the outpatient department of the Siddha Clinical Research Unit, Safdarjung Hospital, New Delhi, for external Varmam and Thokkanam therapy. Internal medications were started on the day after purgation.

Siddha Varmam and Thokkanam therapy

The total treatment plan was scheduled for the next two months from 14.08.2024. The patient was advised to undergo Siddha Varmam and Thokkanam therapy continuously for four weeks, from August 14, 2024, to September 14, 2024, on Mondays through Saturdays and Sundays designated as rest days. The patient was advised to receive therapy on alternate days, from September 15, 2024, to October 14, 2024.

With continuous Siddha internal treatments, along with Varmam and Thokkanam therapies, the patient's clinical features gradually improved, and they reported feeling better. The KOOS score was evaluated every seven days, resulting in the following assessments: on day 1, a score of 19% was recorded, whereas on day 60, a score of 85% was recorded. The objective reassessments were conducted at each follow-up, and the KOOS/VAS scores remained stable, with no aggravation of symptoms experienced. The patient was advised to follow the prescribed diet. Throughout the treatment period, the Naranjo Adverse Drug Reaction Probability Scale was used to evaluate any adverse reactions. No adverse reactions were detected, and the patient completed the medication regimen without any complications.

Discussion

Chronic pain poses a major challenge for those suffering from osteoarthritis (OA), which significantly impacts their physical ability, quality of life, and mental health. The guidelines suggest a wide range of treatments for OA, encompassing educational, psychosocial, behavioural, mind-body, physical, and pharmacological methods. Clinicians should evaluate patients' risk factors. Nevertheless, if they opt to prescribe NSAIDs to patients at risk, they should adhere to a treatment strategy that involves a short duration and a minimal dose of oral NSAIDs [15]. In the Siddha literature, osteoarthritis is categorized as a Vali disease. For managing Vali disease, therapeutic purgation is advised as the primary treatment method [8]. An assessment of the patient's Naadi revealed an exacerbation of the Vali humor. To correct this imbalance, purgation was performed, followed by the initiation of internal medications, along with Varmam and Thokkanam therapy.

Parangipattai maathirai is composed of ingredients such as Smilax China Linn and Ocimum tenuiflorium. Parangipattai is known for its anti-inflammatory, analgesic [16], and antinociceptive [17] properties. Liang and colleagues discovered that Chongrenside D (CGD), a compound derived from Smilax chinensis, has anti-inflammatory properties. Additionally, CGD aids in preventing bone loss by inhibiting the activity of certain cells responsible for bone degradation. Consequently, CGD has potential as a treatment for bone diseases [18]. Ocimum tenuiflorium possesses antiarthritic, antispasmodic [19], anti-inflammatory, and analgesic properties [20]. The aqueous extract of Ocimum tenuiflorium (basil) has been demonstrated to significantly reduce the levels of nitric oxide, prostaglandins, total nuclear factor-kappa B, cyclooxygenase-2 protein, and matrix metalloproteinases. Consequently, this extract has considerable potential as an effective treatment for managing inflammation associated with osteoarthritis [21]. Sangu Parpam is an herbal mineral therapeutic regimen designed to treat peptic ulcers, skin diseases, urinary tract infections and arthritis. It possesses anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and antiulcer properties [22].

Effective external therapies recommended for the management of arthritis include massage techniques, Varmam point manipulation, herbal pastes, poultices, compresses or bandages, steam inhalation or exposure therapy, and bone setting with bamboo splints. Each of these traditional techniques and formulations has been well-explained with respect to the ingredients, processes, and methods of application, with indications and dietary advice. According to the World Health Organization International Siddha Standard Terminologies, Varmam refers to the vital energy points where life energy is concentrated. Manipulating these points with specific forces and timings can regulate the flow of obstructed energy and provide relief to affected individuals [6].

The Varmam energy pathway can be scientifically linked to the physiological analgesic pathway, which involves the brain's opioid system. This mechanism stimulates the release of neuromodulators such as endorphins, enkephalins, and dynorphins in response to massage or touch therapy. According to Melzack and Wall's Gate Control Theory, applying pressure to specific points on the body can send positive sensations to the brain. These sensations travel four times faster than pain signals do, effectively blocking them from reaching the brain and thereby reducing the perception of pain. When these points are stimulated, the body responds in a complex manner, involving a system that increases cortisol production, which aids in relaxation. This process also enhances the flow of endorphins and serotonin to the brain and other parts of the body through nerves and pathways [23].

The location, methodology, and mechanism of action of acupressure can be linked to Varmam points. [24]. The anatomical locations of manipulated Varmam points, including panchamuka varmam, Komperi kaalam, Kaal canni adankall, Ullankal vellai varmam, Mootu varmam, Mootu kannu varmam, kuthirai nuni naakku varmam, and Kanapathimuga varmam, can be correlated with specific meridian points, namely, ST35, SP10, KI10, LV7, LV8, SP7, LV2, KI1, BL53, ST34, BL57, and ST36. This correlation is presented in Table 4 and Fig. 1 [25].

A multicentred randomized trial by Liu et al. revealed that targeting specific acupressure points, such as EX-LE2, ST35, ST34, EX-LE4, SP10, GB34, ST36, SP9, BL40, LR7, KI10, LR8, and BL39, significantly enhances pain relief, reduces stiffness, and improves physical function and quality of life in patients with knee osteoarthritis (KOA) [26]. The acupoints most frequently recommended for treating knee osteoarthritis (KOA) are ST36 (Zusanli), ST35 (Dubi), GB34 (Yanglingquan), SP10 (Xuehai), SP9 (Yinlingquan), ST34 (Liangqiu), SP6 (Sanyinjiao), BL60 (Kunlun), LR8 (Ququan), BL40 (Weizhong), and KI10 (Yingu) [27]. BL57 is widely utilized across various clinical departments, with a particular focus on treating conditions related to the limb meridian [28]. Using warming acupuncture with moxibustion at the Yongquan point (KI 1) can improve joint movement, reduce knee swelling, and decrease inflammation linked to osteoarthritis [29].

These findings suggest that these points play crucial roles in significantly reducing local inflammation, alleviating pain and stiffness, enhancing range of motion, and improving both joint function and overall physical well-being. As a result, these interventions have led to an improved quality of life for individuals with knee osteoarthritis.

Massage therapy is an effective means of alleviating symptoms and slowing the progression of osteoarthritis (OA) by enhancing local blood circulation, improving muscle tone, increasing joint flexibility, and reducing pain [12]. Research suggests that the simultaneous contraction of agonist muscles around the knee can increase the knee contact force, potentially accelerating cartilage deterioration in patients with knee osteoarthritis (KOA). Consequently, massage therapy may help slow the progression of KOA and has been shown to improve the range of motion of knee flexion in patients [30]. The use of essential oils in this context is also significant. These oils can penetrate the skin, enhancing drug delivery to deeper layers through mechanisms such as the disruption of intercellular lipid structures and changes in protein conformations [31]. Vata Kesari Tailam, a traditional Siddha polyherbal medicine, is used for Vaḷi ailments and contains anti-inflammatory and pain-relieving components. After Thokkanam, tryptophan levels rise, increasing serotonin production and aiding in toxin removal via lymphatic drainage [23]. This medicine includes 15 herbs mixed with sesame and castor oils. Sesame oil supports cell membrane integrity and acetylcholine synthesis, while its unsaturated fatty acids promote GABA binding, leading to decreased nerve signal transmission and pain relief [32]. Similarly, ricinoleic acid (RA), a key component of castor oil, has shown significant pain-relieving and anti-inflammatory effects, akin to those of capsaicin, suggesting a role in managing neurogenic inflammation [33].

The combined effect may contribute a reduction in inflammation, pain, and stiffness, while simultaneously enhancing the range of motion in the knee joint. Research has demonstrated that the integration of the Varmam and Thokkanam techniques constitutes a significantly more effective strategy for the treatment of osteoarthritis [34].

The Knee Injury and Osteoarthritis Outcome Score (KOOS) was developed as an enhancement of the WOMAC Osteoarthritis Index to assess both short-term and long-term symptoms and functionality in individuals with knee injuries and osteoarthritis [9]. Although the KOOS has not been previously reported in case studies related to Siddha medicine, it was used in this study as a validated and widely accepted tool for objectively evaluating treatment outcomes within an integrative medicine framework. The KOOS score is a tool used to assess patients' perceptions of their knee issues. It comprises 42 questions divided into five categories: pain, other symptoms, daily activities, sports and recreation, and knee-related quality of life. The KOOS provides a comprehensive view of knee injuries and arthritis from the patient's perspective. Patients respond using a scale from 0 (no problems) to 4 (extreme problems). These scores are then converted to a 0–100 scale, where 0 indicates extreme problems and 100 signifies no problems. In this instance, the KOOS score significantly increased from 19 before treatment to 85 on day 60. The VAS score demonstrated a substantial reduction in pain during treatment, decreasing from eight to two post-treatment. This marked improvement indicates reduced pain and an enhanced quality of life for the patient. There have been notable advancements in daily activities and sports, suggesting that patients experience less discomfort and can better enjoy daily life and hobbies.

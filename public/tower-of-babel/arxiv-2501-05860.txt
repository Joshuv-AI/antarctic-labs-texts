Abstract
. The truncated multidimensional moment problem is studied in terms of the Stieltjes transform as the interpolation problem. A step-by-step algorithm is constructed for the multidimensional moment problem and the set of solutions is found in terms of continued fractions.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mu
be a nonnegative Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
\mathop{\rm supp}(\mu)=A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The moment sequence

\mathbf{s}=\{s_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}\}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}=0}^{\ell}
is defined by

(1.1)

s_{i_{1},...,i_{n}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}t_{1}^{i_{1}}\times\ldots\times t_{n}^{i_{n}}d\mu(t_{1},...,t_{n}).

On the other hand, we can reformulated (
1.1
) in terms of the Stieltjes transform as an interpolation problem.
Recall a
n-
variate Stieltjes transform (see
[
2
]
)

(1.2)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\cfrac{d\mu(t_{1},t_{2},...,t_{n})}{1+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\tilde{z}_{i}}.

Setting
\tilde{z}_{i}=-\cfrac{1}{z_{i}}
, we define an associated function
F
by

(1.3)

F(z_{1},z_{2},...,z_{n})=-\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\cfrac{d\mu(t_{1},t_{2},...,t_{n})}{1-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\cfrac{t_{i}}{z_{i}}}.

Definition 1.1
.

(
[
11
]
) A Stolz domain with centre
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and angle
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
in the set

\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{+}|\theta<\arg(z-t_{0})<\pi-\theta\}

The symbol
z\widehat{\rightarrow}t_{0}
denotes the limit
z{\rightarrow}t_{0}
in any Stolz domain with centre
t_{0}
and the symbol
z\widehat{\rightarrow}\infty
denotes the limit

|z|{\rightarrow}\infty
in any Stolz domain with centre
0
.

Moreover, (
1.3
) admits the following asymptotic expansion

(1.4)

\begin{split}F(z_{1},...,z_{n})&=-\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\cfrac{d\mu(t_{1},t_{2},...,t_{n})}{1-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\cfrac{t_{i}}{z_{i}}}=\\
&=-\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\cfrac{t_{i}}{z_{i}}\right)^{k}d\mu(t_{1},t_{2},...,t_{n})=\\
&=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{matrix}\alpha_{i}^{k}\geq 0\\
\alpha_{1}^{k}+\ldots\alpha_{n}^{k}=k\\
\end{matrix}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\binom{k}{\alpha_{1}^{k},\ldots,\alpha_{n}^{k}}\cfrac{t_{1}^{\alpha_{1}^{k}}t_{2}^{\alpha_{2}^{k}}\ldots t_{n}^{\alpha_{n}^{k}}d\mu(t_{1},t_{2},...,t_{n})}{z_{1}^{\alpha_{1}^{k}+1}z_{2}^{\alpha_{2}^{k}+1}\ldots z_{n}^{\alpha_{n}^{k}+1}}=\\
&=-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{matrix}\alpha_{i}^{k}\geq 0\\
\alpha_{1}^{k}+\ldots\alpha_{n}^{k}=k\\
\end{matrix}}\!\!\!\!\!\binom{k}{\alpha_{1}^{k},\ldots,\alpha_{n}^{k}}\cfrac{s_{\alpha_{1}^{k},\alpha_{2}^{k},\ldots,\alpha_{n}^{k}}}{z_{1}^{\alpha_{1}^{k}+1}z_{2}^{\alpha_{2}^{k}+1}\ldots z_{n}^{\alpha_{n}^{k}+1}},\quad z_{i}\widehat{\rightarrow}\infty.\end{split}

However, we can reformulate the moment problem (
1.1
) in terms of the generalized moments. Define a linear functional
\mathfrak{S}
on the monomials
t_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\times\ldots\times t_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
by

(1.5)

\mathfrak{S}(t_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\times\ldots\times t_{n}^{\alpha_{n}})=s_{\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n}}.

In the general case, the associated function can be defined by

(1.6)

\begin{split}F(z_{1},z_{2},...,z_{n})&=-\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}}\mathfrak{S}\left(\cfrac{1}{1-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\cfrac{t_{i}}{z_{i}}}\right)=-\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}}\mathfrak{S}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\cfrac{t_{i}}{z_{i}}\right)^{k}\right)=\\
&=\!-\mathfrak{S}\!\!\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{matrix}\alpha_{i}^{k}\geq 0\\
\alpha_{1}^{k}\!+\!\ldots\!+\!\alpha_{n}^{k}\!=\!k\\
\end{matrix}}\!\!\!\binom{k}{\alpha_{1}^{k},\ldots,\alpha_{n}^{k}}\!\cfrac{t_{1}^{\alpha_{1}^{k}}t_{2}^{\alpha_{2}^{k}}\ldots t_{n}^{\alpha_{n}^{k}}}{z_{1}^{\alpha_{1}^{k}+1}z_{2}^{\alpha_{2}^{k}+1}\ldots z_{n}^{\alpha_{n}^{k}+1}}\right)=\\
&=-\sum_{k=0}^{n\times\ell}\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{matrix}\alpha_{i}^{k}\geq 0\\
\alpha_{1}^{k}\!+\ldots\!+\!\alpha_{n}^{k}=k\\
\end{matrix}}\!\!\!\binom{k}{\alpha_{1}^{k},\ldots,\alpha_{n}^{k}}\cfrac{s_{\alpha_{1}^{k},\alpha_{2}^{k},\ldots,\alpha_{n}^{k}}}{z_{1}^{\alpha_{1}^{k}+1}z_{2}^{\alpha_{2}^{k}+1}\ldots z_{n}^{\alpha_{n}^{k}+1}}+\\
&+o\!\left(\!\!\sum_{\begin{matrix}\alpha_{i}^{n\times\ell}\geq 0\\
\alpha_{1}^{n\!\times\!\ell}\!+\!\ldots\!+\!\alpha_{n}^{n\!\times\!\ell}\!=\!{n\!\times\!\ell}\\
\end{matrix}}\cfrac{1}{z_{1}^{\alpha_{1}^{n\!\times\!\ell}+1}\ldots z_{n}^{\alpha_{n}^{n\!\times\!\ell}+1}}\right),\quad z_{i}\widehat{\rightarrow}\infty.\end{split}

Furthermore, the Stieltjes transform can be applicable to the truncated moment problem for atomic measure. Let
\mu
be an atomic measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where sup
(\mu)=A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

(1.7)

\mu(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{M}m_{k}\delta_{x_{k}}(x),\quad m_{k}>0.

In this case, the moment sequence

\mathbf{s}=\{s_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}\}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}=0}^{\ell}
is generated by

(1.8)

s_{i_{1},...,i_{n}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x_{1}^{i_{1}}\times\ldots\times x_{n}^{i_{n}}d\mu(x_{1},...,x_{n})=\sum_{k=0}^{M}m_{k}t_{1}^{i_{1}}\times\ldots\times t_{n}^{i_{n}}

and the associated function takes the following asymptotic expansion

(1.9)

\begin{split}F(z_{1},...,z_{n})&=-\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\cfrac{d\mu(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})}{1-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\cfrac{x_{i}}{z_{i}}}=\\
&=-\sum_{k=0}^{n\times\ell}\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{matrix}\alpha_{i}^{k}\geq 0\\
\alpha_{1}^{k}\!+\ldots\!+\!\alpha_{n}^{k}=k\\
\end{matrix}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\binom{k}{\alpha_{1}^{k},\ldots,\alpha_{n}^{k}}\cfrac{s_{\alpha_{1}^{k},\alpha_{2}^{k},\ldots,\alpha_{n}^{k}}}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}^{\alpha_{j}^{k}+1}}+\\
&+o\!\left(\!\!\sum_{\begin{matrix}\alpha_{i}^{n\times\ell}\geq 0\\
\alpha_{1}^{n\!\times\!\ell}\!+\!\ldots\!+\!\alpha_{n}^{n\!\times\!\ell}\!=\!{n\!\times\!\ell}\\
\end{matrix}}\cfrac{1}{\prod\limits_{k+1}^{n}z_{k}^{\alpha_{k}^{n\!\times\!\ell}+1}}\right),\quad z_{i}\widehat{\rightarrow}\infty.\end{split}

The multidimensional problem

\mathbb{MP}(\mathbf{s},\ell)
can be formulated as:

Given a sequence of real numbers
\mathbf{s}=\{s_{i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{n}}\}_{i_{1},...,i_{n}=0}^{\ell}
. Describe the set of function
F
such that

(1.10)

\begin{split}F(z_{1},z_{2},...,z_{n})&=-\sum_{k=0}^{n\times\ell}\sum_{\begin{matrix}\alpha_{i}^{k}\geq 0\\
\alpha_{1}^{k}+\ldots\alpha_{n}^{k}=k\\
\end{matrix}}\binom{k}{\alpha_{1}^{k},\ldots,\alpha_{n}^{k}}\cfrac{s_{\alpha_{1}^{k},\alpha_{2}^{k},\ldots,\alpha_{n}^{k}}}{z_{1}^{\alpha_{1}^{k}+1}z_{2}^{\alpha_{2}^{k}+1}\ldots z_{n}^{\alpha_{n}^{k}+1}}+\\
&+o\!\left(\!\!\sum_{\begin{matrix}\alpha_{i}^{n\times\ell}\geq 0\\
\alpha_{1}^{n\!\times\!\ell}\!+\!\ldots\!+\!\alpha_{n}^{n\!\times\!\ell}\!=\!{n\!\times\!\ell}\\
\end{matrix}}\cfrac{1}{z_{1}^{\alpha_{1}^{n\!\times\!\ell}+1}\ldots z_{n}^{\alpha_{n}^{n\!\times\!\ell}+1}}\right),\quad z_{i}\widehat{\rightarrow}\infty.\end{split}

If
\ell
is finite, then
\mathbb{MP}(\mathbf{s},\ell)
is called a truncated problem, otherwise one is called a full problem.

However, the sequence
\mathbf{s}=\{s_{i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{n}}\}_{i_{1},...,i_{n}=0}^{\ell}
admits the following decomposition

(1.11)

\mathbf{s}=\bigcup_{j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}=0}^{\ell}\mathbf{s}_{j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}},\quad\mbox{where}\quad\mathbf{s}_{j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}=\{s_{i,j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}\}_{i=0}^{\ell},\quad j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}=\overline{0,\ell}.

In addition, if we set

(1.12)

\mathfrak{s}_{i,j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}=\binom{i+j_{2}+\ldots+j_{n}}{i,j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}s_{i,j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}},

then (
1.10
) can be rewritten as

(1.13)

\begin{split}F(z_{1},z_{2},...,z_{n})&=-\sum_{j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}=0}^{\ell}\cfrac{1}{z_{2}^{j_{2}+1}\cdot\ldots\cdot z_{n}^{j_{n}+1}}F_{j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}(z_{1}),\end{split}

where
F_{j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}
admits the following asymptotic expansion

(1.14)

F_{j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}(z_{1})=-\cfrac{\mathfrak{s}_{0,j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}}{z_{1}}-\cfrac{\mathfrak{s}_{1,j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}}{z_{1}^{2}}-\ldots-\cfrac{\mathfrak{s}_{\ell,j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}}{z_{1}^{\ell+1}}+o\left(\cfrac{1}{z_{1}^{\ell+1}}\right).

F_{j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}
is called an associated function of the sequence
\mathfrak{s}_{j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}=\{\mathfrak{s}_{i,j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}\}_{i=0}^{\ell}
.

The representation (
1.13
) is more convenient in terms of
F_{j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}
functions. In this case, the multidimensional problem is rewritten as the union of one-dimensional problems. Hence the problem becomes simpler.

In Section 2, we recall the basic results of the one-dimensional moment problem, which will be applied to the multidimensional problem. In Section 2 and Section 3, we study truncated multidimensional moment problem in terms of the Stieltjes transform, the step-by-step algorithms are found. In this case, the solutions of the truncated moment problems can be represented in terms of
P
-fractions and
S
-fractions. Finally, in Section 4, we consider the full problem and describe the set of solutions. The description of solutions to the full problem is based on the results of the previous sections.

2.
Indefinite one-dimensional moment problem

In this section, we recall some results of the one-dimensional moment problem in terms of the Stieltjes transform, which provide a basis for future results.

Let
\mathbf{s}=\{s_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\ell}
be a sequence
of real numbers and let a functional
\mathfrak{S}
be defined on
the linear space

\mathcal{P}=span\left\{z^{j}:j=\overline{0,\ell}\right\}

by

(2.1)

s_{j}=\mathfrak{S}(z^{j}).

We consider the general case, i.e. the measure may not exist. Hence, the problem is studied as the interpolation problem. Let us define it:

Moment problem
MP(\mathbf{s},\ell)
:
Given a sequence of real numbers
\mathbf{s}=\{s_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\ell}
. Find the set of function
F
, which admit the asymptotic expansion

(2.2)

f(z)=-\frac{s_{0}}{z}-\frac{s_{1}}{z^{2}}-\cdots-\frac{s_{\ell}}{z^{\ell+1}}+o\left(\frac{1}{z^{\ell+1}}\right),\quad\quad z\widehat{\rightarrow}\infty.

In addition, we recall a set
\mathcal{N}(\textbf{s})=\{n_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
of
normal indices of
\textbf{s}=\{s_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\ell}
, which is defined by

(2.3)

\mathcal{N}(\textbf{s})=\{n_{j}:D_{n_{j}}\neq 0,j=1,2,\ldots,N\},\quad D_{n_{j}}:=\textup{det}({s}_{i+k})_{i,k=0}^{n_{j}-1}.

So, let us formulate the main results of
MP(\mathbf{s},\ell)
. The following statement includes results in terms of
P
-fractions.

Theorem 2.1
.

(
[
3
]
) Let
\mathbf{s}=\{s_{j}\}_{j=0}^{2n_{N}-1}
be a sequence of real numbers and let
{\mathcal{N}}({\mathbf{s}})=\{n_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
be the set of normal indices of
{\mathbf{s}}
. Then any solution of the moment problem
MP(\mathbf{s},2n_{N}-1)
takes the form

(2.4)

f(z)=-\cfrac{b_{0}}{a_{0}(z)-\cfrac{b_{1}}{a_{1}(z)-\ldots-\cfrac{b_{N-1}}{a_{N-1}(z)+\tau(z)}}},

where the prameter
\tau
satisfies the following condition

(2.5)

\tau(z)=o(1),\quad z\widehat{\rightarrow}\infty,

and atoms
(a_{i},b_{i})
can be calculated by

(2.6)

\begin{split}&b_{0}=s_{n_{1}-1},\quad b_{j}=s^{(j)}_{\nu-1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad a_{j}(z)=\cfrac{1}{D^{(j-1)}_{\nu}}\begin{vmatrix}\mathfrak{s}^{(j-1)}_{0}&\mathfrak{s}^{(j-1)}_{1}&\ldots&\mathfrak{s}^{(j-1)}_{\nu}\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
s^{(j-1)}_{\nu-1}&\mathfrak{s}^{(j-1)}_{\nu}&\ldots&\mathfrak{s}^{(j-1)}_{2\nu-1}\\
1&z&\ldots&z^{\nu}\end{vmatrix},\\
&s^{(j)}_{i}=\cfrac{(-1)^{i+\nu}}{\left(s^{(j-1)}_{\nu-1}\right)^{i+\nu+2}}\begin{vmatrix}s^{(j-1)}_{\nu}&s^{(j-1)}_{\nu-1}&0&\ldots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
\vdots&&&\ddots&s^{(j_{2},...,j_{n},j-1)}_{\nu-1}\\
s^{(j-1)}_{2\nu+i}&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&s^{(j-1)}_{\nu-1}\end{vmatrix},\end{split}

where
i=\overline{0,2\nu-1}
,
j=\overline{0,N-1}
,
s^{(0)}_{i}=s_{i}
,
\nu=n_{j}-n_{j-1}
and
n_{0}=0
.

Furthermore, (
2.4
) can be represented by

(2.7)

f(z)=-\cfrac{Q_{n_{N-1}}(z)\tau(z)+Q_{n_{N}}(z)}{P_{n_{N-1}}(z)\tau(z)+P_{n_{N}}(z)},

where
P_{n_{j}}
and
Q_{n_{j}}
is called the polynomials of the first and second kind, respectively, which can be defined as the solutions of the following system

(2.8)

{b}_{j}y_{{n}_{j-1}}(z)-a_{j}(z)y_{{n}_{j}}(z)+y_{{n}_{j+1}}(z)=0

subject to the initial conditions

(2.9)

P_{{n}_{-1}}(z)\equiv 0,\mbox{ }P_{{n}_{0}}(z)\equiv 1,\mbox{ }Q_{{n}_{-1}}(z)\equiv-1,\mbox{ }Q_{{n}_{0}}(z)\equiv 0.

Remark 2.2
.

We can rewrite (
2.4
) in short form as follows

\underset{i=0}{\overset{N-1}{\mathbf{K}}}\left(-\cfrac{b_{i}}{a_{i}(z)}\right):=-\cfrac{b_{0}}{a_{0}(z)-\ldots-\cfrac{b_{N-1}}{a_{N-1}(z)+\tau(z)}}.

By
[
5
,
6
,
9
]
, the solutions of
MP(\mathbf{s},\ell)
can be represented in terms of the
S
–fractions. We consider the special case, where the sequence
{\mathbf{s}}=\{s_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\ell}
is regular. Let us define it:

Definition 2.3
.

(
[
5
]
)
Let
{\mathbf{s}}=\{s_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\ell}
be a sequence of real numbers and let
\mathcal{N}({\mathbf{s}})=\{n_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
be the set of normal indices of
{\mathbf{s}}
.
A sequence
{\mathbf{s}}
is called a regular,
if one of the following equivalent conditions holds:

(1)

P_{{n}_{j}}(0)\neq 0
for every
j\leq N
;

(2)

D_{{n}_{j}-1}^{+}\neq 0
for every
j\leq N
;

(3)

D_{{n}_{j}}^{+}\neq 0
for every
j\leq N
, where
D^{+}_{n}=\mbox{det}(s_{i+j+1})_{i,j=0}^{n-1}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

(
[
5
,
6
]
)
Let
{\mathbf{s}}=\{s_{i}\}_{i=0}^{2n_{N}-2}
be a regular sequence and let

{\mathcal{N}}({\mathbf{s}})=\{n_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
be the set of normal indices of
{\mathbf{s}}
. Then any solution of
MP(\mathbf{s},2n_{N}-2)

admits the representation

(2.10)

f(z)=\frac{1}{\displaystyle-zm_{1}(z)+\frac{1}{\displaystyle l_{1}+\frac{1}{\displaystyle-zm_{2}(z)+\cdots+\frac{1}{-zm_{N}(z)+\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle\tau(z)}}}}},

where the parameter
\tau
satisfies the following

(2.11)

\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle\tau(z)}=o(z),\quad z\widehat{\rightarrow}\infty.

and atoms
(m_{j},l_{j})
can be calculated by

(2.12)

\begin{split}&d_{1}=\cfrac{1}{b_{0}},\quad l_{1}=-\cfrac{1}{d_{1}a_{0}(0)},\quad d_{j}=\cfrac{1}{b_{j-1}\left(l_{j-1}\right)^{2}d_{j-1}},\\
&l_{j}=-\cfrac{l_{j-1}}{1+l_{j-1}d_{j}a_{j-1}(0)}\mbox{ and }m_{j}(z)=\cfrac{\left(a_{j-1}(z)-a_{j-1}(0)\right)d_{j}}{z},\end{split}

where
d_{j}
is the leading coefficient of the polynomial
m_{j}
and
\left(a_{j},b_{j}\right)
are defined by (
2.6
).

Furthermore, (
2.10
) can be represented as

(2.13)

f(z)=\frac{Q^{+}_{2N-1}(z)\tau(z)+Q^{+}_{2N-2}(z)}{P^{+}_{2N-1}(z)\tau(z)+P^{+}_{2N-2}(z)},

where
P^{+}_{j}
and
Q^{+}_{j}
are called the Stieltjes polynomials of the first and second kind, respectively, which can be found as solutions of the system

(2.14)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}y_{2j}-y_{2j-2}=l_{j}(z)y_{2j-1},\\
y_{2j+1}-y_{2j-1}=-zm_{j+1}(z)y_{2j},\\
\end{array}\right.

subject to the initial conditions

(2.15)

Q^{+}_{-1}(z)\equiv 1,\quad Q^{+}_{0}(z)\equiv 0;\qquad P^{+}_{-1}(z)\equiv 0,\quad P^{+}_{0}(z)\equiv 1.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
{\mathbf{s}}=\{s_{i}\}_{i=0}^{2n_{N}-1}
be a regular sequence and let

{\mathcal{N}}({\mathbf{s}})=\{n_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
be the set of normal indices of
{\mathbf{s}}
. Then any solution of the moment problem
MP(\mathbf{s},2n_{N}-1)

admits the following representation

(2.16)

f(z)=\frac{1}{\displaystyle-zm_{1}(z)+\frac{1}{\displaystyle l_{1}(z)+\cdots+\frac{1}{-zm_{N}(z)+\displaystyle\frac{1}{\displaystyle l_{N}(z)+\tau(z)}}}},

where the parameter
\tau
satisfies the following condition

(2.17)

\tau(z)=o(1),\quad z\widehat{\rightarrow}\infty,

and atoms
(m_{j},l_{j})
can befound by (

).

3.
Truncated problem and
P
-fractions

In the present section, we study a truncated multidimensional moment problem in terms of the Stieltjes transform. In this case, we find a description of the solutions in terms of
P
-fractions.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathbf{s}=\{s_{i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{n}}\}_{i_{1},...,i_{n}=0}^{2\mathfrak{n}-1}
be a sequence of real numbers and let
\mathfrak{n}
be a normal index for all associated sequences
\mathfrak{s}_{j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}=\{\mathfrak{s}_{i,j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}\}_{i=0}^{2\mathfrak{n}-1}
defined by (
1.12
). Then
any solution of the problem
\mathbb{MP}(\mathbf{s},2\mathfrak{n}-1)
takes the following representation

(3.1)

F(z_{1},z_{2},...,z_{n})=\sum_{j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}=0}^{2\mathfrak{n}-1}\cfrac{1}{z_{2}^{j_{2}+1}\cdot\ldots\cdot z_{n}^{j_{n}+1}}\underset{i=0}{\overset{N_{j_{2},...,j_{n}}-1}{\mathbf{K}}}\left(-\cfrac{b_{i}^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}}{a_{i}^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}(z_{1})}\right),

where the parameters
\tau_{j_{2},...,j_{n}}
satisfy the following

(3.2)

\tau_{j_{2},...,j_{n}}(z_{1})=o(1).

The atoms
\left(a^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}_{i},b^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}_{i}\right)
can be found by

(3.3)

\begin{split}&b^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}_{0}=\mathfrak{s}^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}_{n^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}_{1}-1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad b^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}_{j}=\mathfrak{s}^{(j_{2},...,j_{n},j)}_{n^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}_{j}-n^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}_{j-1}-1}\\
&a^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}_{j}(z_{1})=\cfrac{1}{D^{(j_{2},...,j_{n},j-1)}_{\nu^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}}}\begin{vmatrix}\mathfrak{s}^{(j_{2},...,j_{n},j-1)}_{0}&\mathfrak{s}^{(j_{2},...,j_{n},j-1)}_{1}&\ldots&\mathfrak{s}^{(j_{2},...,j_{n},j-1)}_{\nu}\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
\mathfrak{s}^{(j_{2},...,j_{n},j-1)}_{\nu^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}-1}&\mathfrak{s}^{(j_{2},...,j_{n},j-1)}_{\nu^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}}&\ldots&\mathfrak{s}^{(j_{2},...,j_{n},j-1)}_{2\nu^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}-1}\\
1&z_{1}&\ldots&z^{\nu^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}}_{1}\end{vmatrix},\\
&\mathfrak{s}^{(j_{2},...,j_{n},j)}_{i}=\cfrac{(-1)^{i+\nu}}{\left(\mathfrak{s}^{(j_{2},...,j_{n},j-1)}_{\nu^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}-1}\right)^{i+\nu^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}+2}}\times\\
&\quad\quad\quad\qquad\times\begin{vmatrix}\mathfrak{s}^{(j_{2},...,j_{n},j-1)}_{\nu^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}}&\mathfrak{s}^{(j_{2},...,j_{n},j-1)}_{\nu^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}-1}&0&\ldots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
\vdots&&&\ddots&\mathfrak{s}^{(j_{2},...,j_{n},j-1)}_{\nu^{j_{2},...,j_{n}}-1}\\
\mathfrak{s}^{(j_{2},...,j_{n},j-1)}_{2\nu^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}+i}&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\mathfrak{s}^{(j_{2},...,j_{n},j-1)}_{\nu^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}-1}\end{vmatrix},\end{split}

where
i=\overline{0,2n^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}_{N_{j_{2},...,j_{n}}}-2n^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}_{j}-1}
,
j=\overline{0,N_{j_{2},...,j_{n}}}
,
\mathfrak{s}^{(j_{2},...,j_{n},0)}_{\nu_{j_{2},...,j_{n}}}=\mathfrak{s}^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}_{\nu_{j_{2},...,j_{n}}}
,
\nu_{j_{2},...,j_{n}}=n^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}_{j}-n^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}_{j-1}
and
n^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}_{0}=0
,
\mathcal{N}^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}\left(\textbf{s}^{{}^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}}\right)=\{n^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N_{j_{2},...,j_{n}}}
is the set of normal indices of the sequence
\textbf{s}^{{}^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}}
and
n_{N_{j_{2},...,j_{n}}}^{(j_{2},...,j_{n})}=\mathfrak{n}
.

Furthermore, (

5.
Full problem

In this section, we study a full problem, which can be formulated by

Given a sequence of real numbers
\mathbf{s}=\{s_{i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{n}}\}_{i_{1},...,i_{n}=0}^{\infty}
. Find a description of a set of functions F such that

(5.1)

F(z_{1},z_{2},...,z_{n})=-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{matrix}\alpha_{i}^{k}\geq 0\\
\alpha_{1}^{k}+\ldots\alpha_{n}^{k}=k\\
\end{matrix}}\binom{k}{\alpha_{1}^{k},\ldots,\alpha_{n}^{k}}\cfrac{s_{\alpha_{1}^{k},\alpha_{2}^{k},\ldots,\alpha_{n}^{k}}}{z_{1}^{\alpha_{1}^{k}+1}z_{2}^{\alpha_{2}^{k}+1}\ldots z_{n}^{\alpha_{n}^{k}+1}}.

On the other hand, (
5.1
) can be represented in terms of the associated sequences
\mathfrak{s}_{j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}=\{\mathfrak{s}_{i,j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}\}_{i=0}^{\infty}
as follows

(5.2)

\begin{split}F(z_{1},z_{2},...,z_{n})&=-\sum_{j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}=0}^{\infty}\cfrac{1}{z_{2}^{j_{2}+1}\cdot\ldots\cdot z_{n}^{j_{n}+1}}F_{j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}(z_{1}),\end{split}

where
F_{j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}
admits the asymptotic expansion

(5.3)

F_{j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}(z_{1})=-\cfrac{\mathfrak{s}_{0,j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}}{z_{1}}-\cfrac{\mathfrak{s}_{1,j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}}{z_{1}^{2}}-\ldots-\cfrac{\mathfrak{s}_{\ell,j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}}{z_{1}^{\ell+1}}+\ldots=-\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\cfrac{\mathfrak{s}_{j,j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}}{z^{j+1}_{1}}

). The full problem is denoted by

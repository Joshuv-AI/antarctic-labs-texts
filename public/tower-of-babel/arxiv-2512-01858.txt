Approximate pushforward designs and image bounds on approximations
Jakub Czartowski, Adam Sawicki, Karol Życzkowski

Abstract
We extend the framework of quantum pushforward designs to the approximate setting, where averaging is achieved only up to finite precision. Using Schatten p p -norms and Lipschitz continuity arguments, we derive bounds on the approximation parameters of pushforward designs obtained from complex projective spaces, including simplices, mixed states, and quantum channels. In the mixed-state case, we refine the bounds by exploiting the symmetric subspace structure, leading to asymptotically tighter estimates. Numerical simulations support our theoretical results, showing near-optimality in low-dimensional scenarios.

T_{\mu_{Y},t}=\int_{Y}y^{\otimes t}\differential{\mu_Y}=\int_{X}F(x)^{\otimes t}\differential{\mu_X}.

With this in hand, one may start to consider the following crucial question

Problem 1
.

Given a
\delta
-approximate design on the space
X

\norm{T_{\mu_X,t} - T_{\nu_X,t}}_{p}=\delta_{p}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\norm{T_{\mu_Y,t} - T_{\nu_Y,t}}_{p}=\delta^{\prime}_{p}\leq g(\delta_{p}).

In order to resolve this question we will need to consider definition of Lipschitz continuity

Definition 5

Consider two metric spaces

and
Y

d_{X}

. We call

d_{Y}\quantity[f(x_1),f(x_2)]\leq L\,d_{X}\quantity(x_1,x_2).

(10)

In particular, the smallest constant

As we will be considering maps between different quantum spaces related by partial trace, we will utilize already known results on Lipschitz constants for partial trace.

Theorem 1

(Prop. 1 in
[
24
]
)
.

Consider partial trace map
\Tr_{B}:\mathcal{L}\quantity(\mathcal{H}^A\otimes\mathcal{H}^B)\ni Q\mapsto Q_{A}\in\mathcal{H}^{d_{A}}
. It is Lipschitz-continuous with respect to arbitrary Schatten
p
-norm
\norm{\cdot}_{p}
with a (non-optimal) Lipschitz constant

From this theorem proceed relations for trace, Frobenius and spectral norms as special cases of Schatten norm with

\displaystyle\norm{Q_A}_{1}\leq\norm{Q}_{1},

\displaystyle\norm{Q_A}_{2}\leq\sqrt{d_{B}}\norm{Q}_{2},

\displaystyle\norm{Q_A}_{\infty}\leq d_{B}\norm{Q}_{\infty}.

(11)

Additionally, let us recall the result concerning Lipschitz constants for Schatten
p
-norms under quantum channels.

Theorem 2

(Cor. 1 in
[
24
]
)
.

For any quantum channel
\Phi:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{A})\mapsto\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{B})

the following in equality holds

\norm{\Phi(\rho)}_{p}\leq d_{E}^{(p-1)/p}\norm{\rho}_{p}

(12)

where

is the dimension of the environment in the Stinespring dilation, equal to the rank of the Choi state

In addition, there exists a simple way to relate different norms with the infinity-norm in the following manner.

Property 1

Any unitarily invariant norm

and any matrices

\norm{AB}\leq\min\quantity(
\norm{A} \norm{B}_\infty,\,
\norm{B} \norm{A}_\infty).

(13)

In particular, Schatten

-norms satisfy the unitary invariance property. A corollary to this statement is given by

\norm{ABA^\dagger}\leq\min\quantity(
\norm{A} \norm{A}_\infty\norm{B}_\infty,\,
\norm{B} \norm{A}_\infty^2).

(14)

We will utilize the above expression to introduce

Problem 2
.

Given a
\delta
-approximate design on the space
X
and a measurable map
F:X\mapsto Y
, what is the function

\norm{T_{\mu_X,t} - T_{\nu_X,t}}_{p}=\delta_{p}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\norm{T_{\mu_Y,t} - T_{\nu_Y,t}}_{q}=\delta^{\prime}_{q}\leq g(\delta_{q}).

(15)

As we will consider complex projective designs, below we provide two useful lemmas relating the distance between moment operators and frame potentials defined as

F_{t}(\quantity{\ket{\psi_i}}_{i=1}^{m})=\frac{1}{m^{2}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\absolutevalue{\ip{\psi_i}{\psi_j}}^{2t}\leq D_{t}^{-1}

(16)

where

Bounds on

In this section we present results concerning the Lipschitz bounds for approximate pushforward designs obtained for probability simplex, space of mixed designs and space of quantum channels.

3.1
Simplex designs from Complex projective designs

Perhaps the simplest example of pushforward designs is given by
t
-designs in
d
-point simplex
\Delta_{d-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
obtained by partial trace from complex projective
t
-designs on
\mathbb{C}P_{d-1}
. This corresponds to designs for probabilities of measurement results on pure quantum states, performed in a selected basis, obtained from designs on quantum states themselves. The following theorem is obtained relatively easily.

Theorem 3
.

Consider an approximate complex-projective
t
-design
S_{\mathcal{H}}=\quantity{\ket{\psi_i}\in\mathcal{H}_d}
. An approximate
t
-design on a simplex,
S_{\Delta}=\quantity{\vb{p}_i: (\vb{p}_i)_j = \abs{\ip{j}{\psi_i}}^2}
satisfies the following inequality

\norm{T_{\mu_{\Delta,t}} - T_{\nu_{\Delta,t}}}_{p}\leq L\norm{T_{\mu_{\mathcal{H},t}} - T_{\nu_{\mathcal{H},t}}}_{p}

(17)

with Lipschitz constant
L=d^{t\frac{p-1}{p}}
.

Proof.

Projection from complex projective space
\mathcal{H}_{d}
to the probability simplex
\Delta_{d-1}
can be realised considering diagonal elements of an output of a quantum channel
\Phi:\mathcal{H}_{d}\mapsto\mathcal{H}_{d}
of the form

\Phi(\rho)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\outerproduct{j}{j}\rho\outerproduct{j}{j}

(18)

which can be realised by using Stinespring-diluted operation with
d
-dimensional environment.
Thus, by Theorem
2
the statement is proven.
∎

In particular, for
p\rightarrow\infty
, matching the definition of approximate
\mathbb{C}P
designs from
[
1
]
, one may combine Theorem
3
with the result from
[
11
]
to relate
\delta_{\infty}
for the simplex design with Welch bound, namely

\norm{T_{\mu_{\Delta,t}} - T_{\nu_{\Delta,t}}}_{\infty}\leq LW(S_{\mathbb{C}P})\!\!\!\quad\mbox{with}\quad\!\!\!W(S_{\mathbb{C}P})\equiv{\footnotesize\sqrt{\frac{D-1}{D}\quantity(\frac{1}{m^2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\abs{\ip{\psi_i}{\psi_j}}^{2t} - \frac{1}{D})}}

(19)

where
D=\binom{d+t-1}{t}
is the dimension of symmetric subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{d}^{\otimes t}
. This provides a computational improvement, as
W
can be calculated in a straightforward manner using only
m^{2}
entries
\innerproduct{\psi_i}{\psi_j}
of the Gram matrix corresponding to the underlying set of vectors.

3.2
Mixed state designs

In what follows we will consider relation between approximate designs on the complex projective spaces

We can easily formulate the following simple statement

Observation 1

S_{\mathcal{B}}=\quantity{\Tr_B \op{\psi_i}}

\norm{T_{\mu_{\mathcal{B},t}} - T_{\nu_{\mathcal{B},t}}}_{p}\leq L\norm{T_{\mu_{\mathcal{H},t}} - T_{\nu_{\mathcal{H},t}}}_{p}

with
L=d_{B}^{t\frac{p-1}{p}}

Proof.

The observation follows directly from Theorem

.

This bound can be further refined by noting that the moment operators
T_{\mu_{\mathcal{H},t}}
and
T_{\nu_{\mathcal{H},t}}
have support only on the symmetric subspace of

Lemma 1
.

Consider arbitrary operator
R_{AB,t}
acting on
(\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B})^{\otimes t}
with support on
\operatorname{Sym}_{t}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}))
. The following bounds hold

\norm{
\Tr_{B_1,\hdots,B_t} O_{AB,t}}_{p}\leq L\norm{
O_{AB,t}}_{p}

(21)

where
L=D_{B,t}^{(p-1)/p}

D_{B}=\binom{d_{B}+t-1}{t}
is the dimension of
\operatorname{Sym}_{t}(\mathcal{H}_{B})

Proof.

First, let us note that partial trace operation

\Tr_{X}(\cdot)=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{X}}K_{j}(\cdot)K_{j}^{\dagger},

(22)

where

We note that due to symmetry, it holds that

\Tr_{B_{1},\ldots,B_{t}}(O_{AB,t})=\Tr_{B_{1},\ldots,B_{t}}(\Pi_{B,t}O_{AB,t}\Pi_{B,t})=\sum_{j=1}\quantity(K_j \Pi_{B,t})O_{AB,t}\quantity(K_j \Pi_{B,t})^{\dagger}.

We note the set

contains a maximal subset of linearly independent Kraus operators of size

. Thus, using Theorem

∎

We may further state the following asymmetric relations

Observation 2
.

Consider an approximate complex-projective
t
-design
S_{\mathcal{H}}=\quantity{\ket{\psi_i}\in\mathcal{H}_A\otimes\mathcal{H}_B}
. An approximate mixed-state
t
-design
S_{\mathcal{B}}=\quantity{\Tr_B \op{\psi_i}}
satisfies the following inequality

\norm{T_{\mu_{\mathcal{B},t}} - T_{\nu_{\mathcal{B},t}}}_{p}\leq\min\quantity(\delta_p d_B^t,\,\delta_\infty d_B^t d_A^{t/p}),

(26)

where we use
\delta_{p}\equiv\norm{T_{\mu_{\mathcal{H},t}} - T_{\nu_{\mathcal{H},t}}}_{p}

Proof.

Consider application of triangle inequality and Theorem (

\displaystyle\norm{\Tr_{B_1,\hdots,B_t}(X)}

\displaystyle=\norm{\sum_{i=1}^{d_B^t} K_i X K_i^\dagger}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d_{B}^{t}}\norm{K_i X K_i^\dagger}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d_{B}^{t}}\min(\norm{K_i}_{\infty}^{2}\norm{X}_{p},\,\norm{K_i}_{\infty}\norm{K_i}_{p}\norm{X}_{\infty}),

The statement follows by noting that
\norm{K_j}_{p}=d_{A}^{t/p}

To refine the above upper bounds, we need to consider in detail the norms for Kraus operators. To simplify we will consider
K_{\mathbf{a}}\Pi_{AB,t}K_{\mathbf{a}}^{\dagger}
, as in terms of singular values
s_{i}(K_{\mathbf{a}}\Pi_{AB,t}K_{\mathbf{a}}^{\dagger})=[s_{i}(K_{\mathbf{a}}\Pi_{AB,t})]^{2}
. Let us write out explicitly

\displaystyle P=K_{\mathbf{a}}\Pi_{AB,t}K_{\mathbf{a}}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t!}\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in\quantity{1,\hdots,d_A}^{t}}\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in\quantity{1,\hdots,d_B}^{t}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\outerproduct{\vb{i}}{\sigma(\vb{i})}\innerproduct{\vb{a}}{\vb{j}}\innerproduct{\sigma(\vb{j})}{\vb{a}}

(29)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t!}\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in\quantity{1,\hdots,d_A}^{t}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\outerproduct{\vb{i}}{\sigma(\vb{i})}\delta_{\mathbf{a}}^{\sigma(\mathbf{a})}.

(30)

It is easily checked that
P^{2}=P
, and hence the eigenvalues are either
1
or
0
. The number of non-zero eigenvalues can be calculated by trace,

\Tr(P)=\frac{1}{t!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}d^{\operatorname{cycl}(\sigma)}\delta_{\mathbf{a}}^{\sigma(\mathbf{a})}\leq D_{A,t},

(31)

where
\operatorname{cycl}(\sigma)
is the number of independent cycles in permutation
\sigma
, and
D_{A,t}=\binom{d_{a}+t-1}{t}
is the dimension of
\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal{H}_{A})

\norm{K_{\vb{a}}\Pi_{AB,t}}_{p}\leq D_{A,t}^{1/p}.

(32)

This leads to a simple improvement

\norm{T_{\mu_{\mathcal{B},t}} - T_{\nu_{\mathcal{B},t}}}_{p}\leq\min\quantity(\delta_p d_B^t,\,\delta_\infty d_B^t D_{A,t}^{1/p}).

(33)

In addition,
K_{\mathbf{a}}\Pi_{AB,t}=K_{\sigma(\mathbf{a})}\Pi_{AB,t}
for all
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{t}
, and a Kraus operator
K

\tilde{K}_{i}=\sqrt{w_{i}}P_{i}

\sum_{i=1}^{D_{B,t}}w_{i}=d_{B}^{t}
. Even without calculating specific values of
w_{i}
corresponding to permutations with distinct cycle structures, one may put forward improved bounds on Schatten norms. Consider

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{D_{B,t}}\norm{\tilde{K}_i}_{\infty}\norm{\tilde{K}_i}_{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{D_{B,t}}\sqrt{w_{i}}\Tr(P_i)^{1/p}\leq D_{A,t}^{1/p}\sum_{i=1}^{D_{B,t}}\sqrt{w_{i}}\leq D_{A,t}^{1/p}d_{B}^{t/2}D_{B,t}^{1/2},

(34)

where the last inequality is derived from the fact that the sum
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sqrt{a_{i}}
subject to
\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}=A
is maximised when
a_{i}=A/N
for all
i
and equal to
\sqrt{AN}
. Thus we have

Theorem 5
.

Consider an approximate complex-projective
t
-design
S_{\mathcal{H}}=\quantity{\ket{\psi_i}\in\mathcal{H}_A\otimes\mathcal{H}_B}
. An approximate mixed-state
t

S_{\mathcal{B}}=\quantity{\Tr_B \op{\psi_i}}
satisfies the following inequality

\norm{T_{\mu_{\mathcal{B},t}} - T_{\nu_{\mathcal{B},t}}}_{p}\leq\min\quantity(\delta_p d_B^{t/2}D_{B,t}^{1/2},\,\delta_\infty D_{A,t}^{1/p} d_B^{t/2}D_{B,t}^{1/2})

(35)

where we use
\delta_{p}\equiv\norm{T_{\mu_{\mathcal{H},t}} - T_{\nu_{\mathcal{H},t}}}_{p}
.

The upper bound above is not optimal and can be further improved by carefully considering the weights
w_{i}
and eigenvalues of the respective projection operators
P_{i}
.

Channel designs

In the case of channel designs, the starting point is the space
\mathcal{U}(d_{A}d_{B})
of unitary matrices acting on bipartite systems. As we will be operating in the full space of quantum channels, it is natural to define the basic moments operator as

T_{\mu,t}=\int_{U(d_{A}d_{B})}\quantity((U\otimes U^*)^{\otimes t})^{R}\differential{U}

Additionally, Choi states, which correspond to CPTP maps, are subject to additional constraint – complete positivity is guaranteed by positivity of the state, and trace preservation is given by one of the partial traces being equal to identity. Hence, one may be tempted to further improve the coefficient given in eq. (

5
Concluding remarks

In this work we have considered possibility of extending the concept of pushforward designs – structures used to average over a given space obtained by mapping similar structures from different, pre-image spaces – to approximate designs, where averaging is performed not only up to a given polynomial degree, but also a given, finite, precision. In the course of considerations we have demonstrated bounds on the precision
\delta^{\prime}_{p}
of the approximate pushforward design in terms of the pre-image precision
\delta_{p}
.

In particular, we have derived bounds for simplex and mixed-state designs obtained by decoherence and partial trace, respectively. In order to obtain improved bounds we have additionally refined results on bounds of Schatten
p
-norms of partially traced operators with support restricted to symmetric subspace of
t
copies of a system.

We note that the results presented in this work are limited to linear functions of states, which is a useful albeit relatively limited set of maps. One may consider, for instance, a mapping from the space of Hamiltonians.

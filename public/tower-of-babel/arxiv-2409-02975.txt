Slow-roll inflation from a geometric scalar-tensor model with self-interacting potentials Preprint: APS/123-QED
Abraão J. S. Capistrano, Gilberto M. Kremer

Abstract
We consider slow-roll inflation of a model in the context of Brans-Dicke dilaton gravity. From a two self-interacting potentials V ⁡ ( ϕ ) V(\phi) , we reproduce a Starobinsky-like potential and an exponential tail potential in a form V ⁡ ( ϕ ) ∼ ( 1 − e α 0 ​ ϕ ) V(\phi)\sim(1-e^{\alpha_{0}\phi}) , where α 0 \alpha_{0} denotes a constant coefficient related to the Brans-Dicke parameter ω \omega . Using the observational bounds on the spectral index n s n_{s} and tensor-to-scalar ratio r r imposed by Planck-CMB baseline data and the BICEP2/Keck collaboration with combination with Planck 2018 and the Baryonic Acoustic Oscillations (BAO), we obtain for both models a good agreement with current observations with n s = 0.960 − 0.972 n_{s}=0.960-0.972 and r < 0.02 r<0.02 . In addition, the large values of ω \omega suggest a possible linkage of the inflationary regime and today’s solar system bounds.

II
Essentials on geometrical scalar-tensor models

The aim of this work is to determine the slow-roll parameters for an inflationary period based on the equations of a scalar-tensor theory where a scalar field is associated with a dilaton field. For the sake of completeness we summarize below the basic equations derived in
Barreto and Kremer 2020
.

The starting point is the definition of an action
\mathcal{S}_{JF}
on the Jordan frame

\mathcal{S}_{JF}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}e^{-\phi}\left[R+\omega\partial^{\alpha}\phi\partial_{\alpha}\phi-e^{-\phi}V\left(\phi\right)\right],

(1)

which is a function of the scalar field
\phi
minimally coupled to gravity and its self-interacting potential
V(\phi)
. The parameter
\omega
represents a dimensionless coupling constant and
R
the Ricci scalar which is calculated with the affine connection

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}=\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g^{\alpha\beta}\left(g_{\mu\beta}\partial_{\nu}\phi+g_{\nu\beta}\partial_{\mu}\phi-g_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\beta}\phi\right),\qquad

(2)

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}g^{\alpha\beta}\left(\partial_{\nu}g_{\mu\beta}+\partial_{\mu}g_{\nu\beta}-\partial_{\beta}g_{\mu\nu}\right),

(3)

denotes the Christoffel symbols. The relations in Eqs.(
2
) and (
3
) result from the non-metricity condition

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}g_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\alpha}\phi~g_{\mu\nu},\qquad

(4)

commonly used in scalar-tensor models
Romero et al. 2012
. Concerning notation, Greek indices runs from 1 to 4.

By definition of the action
\mathcal{S}_{EF}
on the Einstein frame, we write the following functional as

\mathcal{S}_{EF}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-\tilde{g}}\left[\tilde{R}+\omega\partial^{\alpha}\phi\partial_{\alpha}\phi-V\left(\phi\right)\right].

(5)

The basis of the physical equivalence between Jordan and Einstein frame resides in a fact that Eqs.(
2
) and (
4
) are invariant by Weyl transformations

\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu}=e^{-f}g^{\mu\nu}\;,\;\tilde{\phi}=\phi+f,

(6)

in which
f
is an arbitrary scalar and can be chosen, for convenience, to obtain Eq.(
5
) from Eq.(
1
) by considering that
\tilde{R}=e^{-f}R
, since the Ricci scalar is not invariant by Weyl transformations, while the Ricci tensor is. Indeed, with
\sqrt{-\tilde{g}}=e^{2f}\sqrt{-{g}}
we have

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-\tilde{g}}\left[\tilde{R}+\omega\partial^{\alpha}\phi\partial_{\alpha}\phi-V\left(\phi\right)\right]

(7)

\displaystyle\!=\!\!\int\!d^{4}x\sqrt{-{g}}e^{f}\big[e^{f}\tilde{R}+e^{f}\omega e^{-f}g^{\mu\nu}\omega\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi-e^{f}V\big(\phi\big)\big].

From the variational method of Palatini the metric tensor and the affine connection are independent variables, so that from the variation of the action (
1
) with respect to the metric tensor
{g}_{\mu\nu}
, we obtain Einstein’s field equation

\displaystyle{R}_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}{R}g_{\mu\nu}=-{T}^{\phi}_{\mu\nu},

(8)

where the energy-momentum tensor reads

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}^{\phi}=\omega\left(\partial_{\mu}{\phi}\partial_{\nu}{\phi}-\frac{1}{2}\partial^{\alpha}{\phi}\partial_{\alpha}{\phi}g_{\mu\nu}\right)+\frac{1}{2}e^{-\phi}V{g}_{\mu\nu}.

(9)

Einstein’s field equation (
8
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{R}{g}_{\mu\nu}=-\tilde{T}_{\mu\nu}.

(10)

Here
\tilde{R}_{\mu\nu}
and
\tilde{R}_{\mu\nu}
are the standard Ricci tensor and Ricci scalar calculated with Christoffel symbols
\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}
and the expression for the energy-momentum tensor is given by

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{\mu\nu}=T^{{\phi}}_{\mu\nu}-\tilde{\nabla}_{\mu}(\partial_{\nu}{\phi})-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}{\phi}\partial_{\nu}{\phi}

\displaystyle-{g}_{\mu\nu}\left(\frac{1}{4}\partial_{\alpha}{\phi}\partial^{\alpha}{\phi}-\tilde{\nabla}_{\alpha}(\partial^{\alpha}{\phi})\right).

(11)

Note that the covariant derivatives are related with the Christoffel symbols

\tilde{\nabla}_{\mu}(\partial_{\nu}{\phi})=\partial_{\mu}(\partial_{\nu}\phi)-\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\alpha}\phi.

The variation of the action (
1
) with respect to the scalar field
{\phi}
, leads to the Klein-Gordon equation

\tilde{\Box}{\phi}-\partial^{\alpha}{\phi}\partial_{\alpha}{\phi}+\frac{e^{-\phi}}{2\omega}\frac{dV}{d\phi}=0.

(12)

In a spatially flat universe, the Friedmann–Robertson–Walker (FRW) metric reads

ds^{2}=dt^{2}-a^{2}(t)\left[dx^{2}+dy^{2}+dz^{2}\right],

(13)

where
a(t)\equiv a
denotes the cosmic scale factor.

Following the development shown in
Barreto and Kremer 2020
, an equivalent expression to Eq.(
12
) can be obtained in the Jordan frame from the Euler–Lagrange equation for the scalar field
\phi
for a point-like Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{JFp}
as given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{JFp}=e^{-\phi}\bigg[\left(\omega-\frac{3}{2}\right)a^{3}\dot{\phi}^{2}+6\left(a^{2}\dot{a}\dot{\phi}-a\dot{a}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle-a^{3}e^{-\phi}V(\phi)\bigg].

(14)

The Klein-Gordon equation in the FRW-metric follows from the variation of the Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{JFp}
with respect to the scalar field
\phi
, yielding

\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi}-\dot{\phi}^{2}+\frac{e^{-\phi}}{2\omega}\frac{dV}{d\phi}=0,

(15)

in analogy with Eq. (
12
). Here the overdot refers to a time derivative and
H=\dot{a}/a
denotes the Hubble parameter.

III
Slow-roll analysis of the self-interacting potentials

In this work, the slow-roll analysis is investigated by use Eq.(
15
) in Jordan frame. By considering the slow-roll conditions
\ddot{\phi}\ll 3H\dot{\phi}
and
\dot{\phi}^{2}\ll V(\phi)
, Eq.(
15
) reduces to

\displaystyle 3H\dot{\phi}+\frac{dV_{1}(\phi)}{d\phi}=0\;,

(16)

where we have introduced the self-interacting potential
V_{1}(\phi)
defined by

\displaystyle V_{1}(\phi)=V_{0}+\frac{1}{2\omega}\int e^{-\phi}\frac{dV(\phi)}{d\phi}d\phi\;,

(17)

wherein
V_{0}
is an integration constant.

The potential
V_{1}(\phi)
is adopted for calculation of the slow-roll analysis, defined by a test potential
V(\phi)
. The two slow-roll parameters are defined by

\displaystyle\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{V_{1}(\phi)^{\prime}}{V_{1}(\phi)}\right)^{2},\qquad\eta=\frac{V_{1}(\phi)^{\prime\prime}}{V_{1}(\phi)}

(18)

with the prime denoting a differentiation with respect to the scalar field
\phi
. The reduced Planck mass
m_{pl}=1/\sqrt{8\pi G}
was set as
1
, for simplicity, and will be restated further in the numerical analysis of the phase-space in section
IV
.

In this work we shall investigate self-interacting potentials in the slow-roll analysis considering two scenarios starting from the choice of
V(\phi)
. So far, in the construction of
V(\phi)
, we have verified that allowing mixed terms with exponential functions tend to produce more viable scenarios to inflation. It is well-known that potentials on large field models
Martin et al. 2014
, chaotic inflation
Linde 1983
, or logarithm corrections
Barrow and Nunes 2007
, are ruled out by observations providing large values for the spectral tilt
n_{s}\geq 1
. Thus, we resort to two scenarios motivated by superstring models.

In the first scenario we adopt a potential as sum of two exponential terms in a form

\displaystyle V(\phi)=V_{0}\left(1+Ae^{c_{0}\phi}+Be^{c_{1}\phi}\right)\;,

(19)

wherein we denote

\displaystyle A=\frac{4}{3}\frac{\alpha_{0}}{(2\alpha_{0}-1)(1-3\alpha_{0})},\qquad\alpha_{0}=\frac{3\omega-1}{9\omega}

\displaystyle B=-\frac{4}{3}\frac{\alpha_{0}}{(\alpha_{0}-1)(1-3\alpha_{0})},

and the coefficients
c_{0}=1-2\alpha_{0}
and
c_{1}=1-\alpha_{0}
. This form of potential generalizes the original potential produced by D-branes when sypersymmetry (SUSY) is broken at the string scale
Dudas et al. 2012
.

By means of Eq.(
17
), Eq.(
19
) generates a Starobinsky-like potential
V_{SL}
in a form

\displaystyle V_{SL}(\phi)=V_{0}(1-e^{-\alpha_{0}\phi})^{2}\;.

(20)

Thus, one finds the slow-roll pairs
(\varepsilon,\eta)
as

\displaystyle\varepsilon=\frac{2\alpha_{0}^{2}}{\left(1-e^{\alpha_{0}\phi}\right)^{2}}\;,

(21)

\displaystyle\eta=2\alpha_{0}^{2}\frac{(2-e^{\alpha_{0}\phi})}{\left(1-e^{\alpha_{0}\phi}\right)^{2}}\;.

(22)

For simplicity of notation, we maintain the
\alpha_{0}
parameter in the equations.

The value of the field at the end of inflation
\phi_{end}
is calculated by setting
\varepsilon(\phi_{end})=1
. Thus we obtain the real solution in form

\phi_{end}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}}\ln{(1+\sqrt{2}\alpha_{0})}\;.

(23)

Now we are able to calculate the number of e-foldings of inflation denoted by the quantity
N
defined as the logarithm of the scale factor
a(t)
such as
N=N_{0}+\ln a
, and
N_{0}
denotes the number of the e-folds set by the current time
a_{0}(t_{0})
. Then,
N
can be computed as

\Delta N=-\int^{\phi}_{\phi_{ini}}\frac{d\phi}{2\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\;,

(24)

where
\Delta N=N-N_{0}
and
\phi_{ini}
is the initial value of the inflaton field.

Plugging Eq.(
21
) into Eq.(
24
), we get straightforwardly

2\alpha_{0}^{2}\Delta N=\alpha_{0}(\phi-\phi_{ini})-(e^{\alpha_{0}\phi}-e^{\alpha_{0}\phi_{ini}})\;,

(25)

which can be promptly inverted to obtain the values of the field in terms of the number of e-folds as

\displaystyle\phi=\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}}\bigg(2\alpha_{0}^{2}\Delta N+\alpha_{0}\phi_{ini}-e^{\alpha_{0}\phi_{ini}}

\displaystyle\;-W_{-1}\big(-\exp{(2\alpha_{0}^{2}\Delta N+\alpha_{0}\phi_{ini}-e^{\alpha_{0}\phi_{ini}}})\big)\bigg)\;,

(26)

where
W_{-1}
is the
-1
-branch of Lambert function. Thus, taking Eq.(
23
) and Eq.(
III
), we calculate the value of the field before the end of inflation
\phi_{\star}
for
\Delta N_{\star}=N_{end}-N
e-folds, which is given by

\phi_{\star}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}}\left(\Xi-W_{-1}(-\exp{(\Xi)})\right)\;,

(27)

where
\Xi=-2\alpha_{0}^{2}\Delta N_{\star}+\ln{(1+\sqrt{2}\alpha_{0})}-(1+\sqrt{2}\alpha_{0})
.

In the second scenario we generalize the so-called exponential tail potential, originally conceived in the context of superstring models where inflation occurs at large inflaton vacuum expectation value
Obukhov 1993
;
Stewart 1995b
. Thus, we define a potential in a form

\displaystyle V(\phi)=V_{0}\left(1+\frac{B}{2}e^{c_{0}\phi}\right)\;,

(28)

where
\alpha_{0}
,
B
and
c_{0}
are the same coefficients defined in Eq. (
19
). As a result, by using Eq.(
17
), Eq. (
28
) generates an exponential inflation potential
V_{SIL}
in a form

\displaystyle V_{SIL}(\phi)=V_{0}(1-e^{-\alpha_{0}\phi})\;.

(29)

Thus, one finds the slow-roll pairs
(\varepsilon,\eta)
as

\displaystyle\varepsilon=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2\left(1-e^{\alpha_{0}\phi}\right)^{2}}\;,

(30)

\displaystyle\eta=\frac{\alpha_{0}^{2}}{1-e^{\alpha_{0}\phi}}\;,

(31)

where, for simplicity, we maintain the notation with
\alpha_{0}
coefficient that carries the Brans-Dicke
\omega
coupling parameter.

IV
The impact of
\omega
on the solutions

It is worth noting a discussion on the influence of the
\omega
parameter in the solutions.
The limits based on time-delay experiments
require a large
\omega

\omega>500

\omega

As a reference, we analyze the value of the field in the end of inflation
\phi_{end}
for the total number of e-folds,
N

50-60

20

5.78\%
between the values of
\omega=1
and
\omega=1000

\phi_{end}\sim 1.229
and
\phi_{end}\sim 1.158

\omega>10

VI
Final remarks

In this work, we investigate the slow-roll inflation in the context of a Brans-Dicke geometric scalar-tensor theory. In the Jordan frame, we obtain the related point-like Lagrangian for a FLRW metric and the resulting Klein-Gordon equation. We compute inflationary slow-roll parameters for different selected potentials reproducing a Starobinsky-like potential and potential with exponential tails to obtain the relations between ratio-to-tensor
r
and spectral tilt
n_{s}
confronted with current data of Planck-CMB baseline and the BICEP2/Keck 2018. As a result, we obtain a full agreement with current observations well within a
68\%
C.L. Interestingly,
high values of the Brans-Dicke parameter
\omega
does not compromise the model in the validity range in the plane
r-n_{s}
with
n_{s}=0.960-0.972
and small
r<0.02
. As a primary analysis, these results suggest that the same value of
\omega
applies to inflationary regime and to the subsequent cosmology obeying current solar system bounds
\omega\geq 10^{5}
. This gave us an optimistic scenario for further studies on late cosmic acceleration. As a subject of a future work, we will take into account the perturbation equations of the model submitting it into the scrutiny of current observational data.

Electric and spin-valley currents induced by structured light in 2D Dirac materials
A. A. Gunyaga, M. V. Durnev, S. A. Tarasenko

Abstract
Structured optical fields can be used for the injection and control of charge and spin-valley currents. Here, we present a systematical study of these phenomena for interband absorption of structured light in 2D Dirac materials. We derive general expressions for the current density and the quasi-classical generation rate of photoelectrons in the momentum, coordinate, and spin-valley spaces. We reveal mechanisms of the current formation determined by the local and non-local contributions to the optical generation, including the mechanisms related to optical alignment of electron momenta by linearly polarized light, optical orientation by circularly polarized light, and the class of charge and spin-valley photon drags sensitive to the phase and polarization profiles of the optical field. We develop a kinetic theory of electric and spin-valley currents driven by the optical field with spatially inhomogeneous intensity, polarization, and phase and obtain analytical expressions for the current contributions. The theory is applied to analyze the photocurrents emerging in TMDC layers and graphene excited by polarization gratings.

II
General formalism

II.1
Spatially varying photocurrents

We start our paper by deriving general equations for the density of current
\bm{j}(\bm{r})
induced by spatially inhomogeneous radiation.
We assume that the electron system is described by the matrix Hamiltonian
{\cal H}
which is linear in the wave vector
\bm{k}
.
Such a Hamiltonian naturally follows from a multi-band
\bm{k}
\cdot
\bm{p}
theory and has the general form
{\cal H}=\beta+\bm{\alpha}\cdot\bm{k}
,
where
\beta
and
\bm{\alpha}=d{\cal H}/d\bm{k}
are the Hermitian matrices, whose dimension coincides with the number of bands.

In terms of the quantum field operators, the current density operator is given by

\bm{j}(\bm{r})=\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(\bm{r})\bm{v}\hat{\psi}(\bm{r})\,,

(1)

where
\hat{\psi}(\bm{r})=\sum_{n\bm{k}}\hat{a}_{n\bm{k}}\psi_{n\bm{k}}(\bm{r})
,
\hat{a}_{n\bm{k}}
are the annihilation operators,

\psi_{n\bm{k}}(\bm{r})=\chi_{n\bm{k}}\exp(\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r})
,
\chi_{n\bm{k}}
are the eigen columns of
{\cal H}
,
n
is the band index,
and
\bm{v}=\bm{\alpha}/\hbar
. Averaging Eq. (
1
) over the ensemble of electrons and
introducing the density matrix
\rho_{n\bm{k},n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}=\langle\hat{a}_{n\bm{k}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\rangle
, one obtains the current density

\bm{j}(\bm{r})=\sum_{n\bm{k},n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\rho_{n\bm{k},n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\bm{v}_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime},n\bm{k}}\exp[\mathrm{i}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})\cdot\bm{r}]\,,

(2)

where
\bm{v}_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime},n\bm{k}}=\chi_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\bm{v}\chi_{n\bm{k}}
.

We focus on the current in the conduction band

\bm{j}(\bm{r})=\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}\rho^{c}_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}\bm{v}_{\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k}}^{c}\exp[\mathrm{i}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})\cdot\bm{r}]\,,

(3)

which is determined by the conduction-band density matrix
\rho^{c}_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}=\rho_{c\bm{k},c\bm{k}^{\prime}}
and the intraband matrix elements of the velocity operator
\bm{v}_{\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k}}^{c}=\chi_{c\bm{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\bm{v}\chi_{c\bm{k}}
. Similar equations can be readily written for the current carried by holes in the valence band. Note that,
for spatially inhomogeneous radiation, one can also expect an interband contribution to the current (
2
) determined by the interband components of the density matrix with
n^{\prime}\neq n
. This contribution is somewhat similar to the shift contribution to the photogalvanic current in
noncentrosymmetric crystals and to the photon drag current in centrosymmetric crystals
6
;
55
;
45
.
It is small compared to the current studied below and will be considered elsewhere.

The spatial scale of the current inhomogeneity is defined by the spatial structure of the light beam and is typically much larger than
the electron wavelength. Then, Eq. (
3
) can be fruitfully rewritten in the form

\bm{j}(\bm{r})=\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{p}}\rho^{c}_{\bm{k}+\bm{p}/2,\bm{k}-\bm{p}/2}\bm{v}_{\bm{k}-\bm{p}/2,\bm{k}+\bm{p}/2}^{c}\exp(\mathrm{i}\bm{p}\cdot\bm{r})

(4)

with
|\bm{p}|\ll|\bm{k}|
.
Expanding the velocity matrix elements
\bm{v}_{\bm{k}-\bm{p}/2,\bm{k}+\bm{p}/2}^{c}
in the series of
\bm{p}
, we obtain

\bm{j}(\bm{r})=\sum_{\bm{k}}\bm{v}_{\bm{k}}^{c}f(\bm{k},\bm{r})\\
+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{\alpha=x,y}\nabla_{\alpha}f(\bm{k},\bm{r})\,[(\partial_{k_{\alpha}}-\partial_{k^{\prime}_{\alpha}})\bm{v}_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}^{c}]_{\bm{k}^{\prime}=\bm{k}}\,,

(5)

where
\bm{v}_{\bm{k}}^{c}=\bm{v}_{\bm{k},\bm{k}}^{c}=(1/\hbar)d\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{c}/d\bm{k}
,
\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{c}
is
the conduction-band dispersion given by the Hamiltonian
{\cal H}
,

f(\bm{k},\bm{r})
is the quasi-classical distribution function in the momentum and real spaces
given by the Wigner transformation

f(\bm{k},\bm{r})=\sum_{\bm{p}}\rho^{c}_{\bm{k}+\bm{p}/2,\bm{k}-\bm{p}/2}\exp(\mathrm{i}\bm{p}\cdot\bm{r})\,,

(6)

and
\nabla=\partial_{\bm{r}}
is the spatial gradient.

The conduction-band density matrix
\rho^{c}_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}
and, hence, the quasi-classical distribution function
f(\bm{k},\bm{r})

can be calculated by solving the quantum kinetic equation

\frac{\partial\rho^{c}_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}}{\partial t}+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\hbar}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{c}-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}^{c})\rho^{c}_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}=g_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}+I_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}\{\rho^{c}\}\>,

(7)

where
g_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}
is the optical generation term and the term
I_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}\{\rho^{c}\}
describes the processes of
momentum, spin, valley, and energy relaxation and electron-hole recombination.
The Wigner transformation of Eq. (
7
) gives the kinetic equation for the distribution function
f(\bm{k},\bm{r})

\frac{\partial f(\bm{k},\bm{r})}{\partial t}+{\bm{v}}_{\bm{k}}^{c}\cdot\nabla f(\bm{k},\bm{r})=g(\bm{k},\bm{r})+I\{f\}\,,

(8)

where
g(\bm{k},\bm{r})=\sum_{\bm{p}}g_{\bm{k}+\bm{p}/2,\bm{k}-\bm{p}/2}\exp(\mathrm{i}\bm{p}\cdot\bm{r})
is the quasi-classical generation term.

II.2
Optical transitions induced by structured radiation

To proceed further, we calculate the generation term for the interband optical transitions induced by spatially inhomogeneous radiation.
We take the vector potential of the incident monochromatic field in the
(xy)
plane of 2D material in the form

\bm{A}(\bm{r},t)=\sum_{\bm{q}}\bm{A}_{\bm{q}}\exp(\mathrm{i}\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}-\mathrm{i}\omega t)+{\rm c.c.}\,,

(9)

where
\bm{A}_{\bm{q}}
is the Fourier amplitude of the vector potential,
\omega
is the frequency, and
\bm{q}
is the in-plane wave vector.
The generation term in the basis of plane waves has the form
20
:

g_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}=\frac{\pi}{\hbar}\sum_{\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}M_{c\bm{k},v\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}M_{c\bm{k}^{\prime},v\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}^{*}\\
\times[\delta(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{c}-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}^{v}-\hbar\omega)+\delta(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}^{c}-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}^{v}-\hbar\omega)]\,,

(10)

where
M_{c\bm{k},v\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}
is the matrix element of the optical transitions

M_{c\bm{k},v\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}=-\frac{e}{c}\sum_{\bm{q}}\bm{v}_{cv}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime\prime})\cdot\bm{A}_{\bm{q}}\delta_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}+\bm{q}}\,,

(11)

\bm{v}_{cv}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime\prime})
is the interband matrix element of the velocity operator,
\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{v}
is the valence band energy,

\delta(\ldots)
and
\delta_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}+\bm{q}}
are the Dirac delta-function and the Kronecker delta, respectively. At
\bm{k}=\bm{k}^{\prime}
, Eq. (
10
) yields the well-known Fermi golden rule for generation of the diagonal components of the density matrix
\rho_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}
.

The Wigner transformation of Eq. (
10
) yields

g(\bm{k},\bm{r})=\frac{\pi e^{2}}{\hbar c^{2}}\sum_{\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2}}\exp(\mathrm{i}\bm{p}\cdot\bm{r})[\bm{v}_{cv}(\bm{k}+\bm{p}/2,\bm{k}-\bm{q})\cdot\bm{A}_{\bm{q}_{1}}]\hskip 70.0001pt\\
\hskip-20.00003pt\times[\bm{v}_{cv}(\bm{k}-\bm{p}/2,\bm{k}-\bm{q})\cdot\bm{A}_{\bm{q}_{2}}]^{*}\\[4.30554pt]
\times\bigl[\delta(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}+\bm{p}/2}^{c}-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}-\bm{q}}^{v}-\hbar\omega)+\delta(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}-\bm{p}/2}^{c}-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}-\bm{q}}^{v}-\hbar\omega)\bigr]\,,

(12)

where
\bm{q}=(\bm{q}_{1}+\bm{q}_{2})/2
and
\bm{p}=\bm{q}_{1}-\bm{q}_{2}
.
Naturally, for the incident radiation in the form of a single plane wave with the in-plane wave vector
\bm{q}
, Eq. (
12
) reproduces the known result

g(\bm{k})\propto|\bm{v}_{cv}(\bm{k},\bm{k}-\bm{q})\cdot\bm{A}_{\bm{q}}|^{2}\delta(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{c}-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}-\bm{q}}^{v}-\hbar\omega)
, see, e.g., Ref.
17
.

Using the smallness of the photon wave vectors
\bm{q}_{1}
and
\bm{q}_{2}
compared to the electron wave vector
\bm{k}
,
we can expand
g(\bm{k},\bm{r})
in the series of
q/k
as follows

g(\bm{k},\bm{r})=g^{(0)}(\bm{k},\bm{r})+g^{(1)}(\bm{k},\bm{r})+\ldots

(13)

The term
g^{(0)}(\bm{k},\bm{r})
is obtained by neglecting the wave vectors
\bm{q}_{1}
and
\bm{q}_{2}
in the matrix elements
of the interband velocity operator and in the band energies in Eq. (
12
). This gives

\displaystyle g^{(0)}(\bm{k},\bm{r})=\frac{2\pi e^{2}}{\hbar c^{2}}|\bm{v}_{cv}(\bm{k})\cdot\bm{A}(\bm{r})|^{2}\delta(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{c}-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{v}-\hbar\omega)\,,

(14)

where
\bm{A}(\bm{r})=\sum_{\bm{q}}\bm{A}_{\bm{q}}\exp(\mathrm{i}\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r})
and
\bm{v}_{cv}(\bm{k})=\bm{v}_{cv}(\bm{k},\bm{k})
.
Equation (
14
) presents the generation term in the local approximation, where the generation rate at the point
\bm{r}

is determined by the field at the same point.

The first-order correction
g^{(1)}(\bm{k},\bm{r})
is given by

\displaystyle g^{(1)}(\bm{k},\bm{r})=\frac{2\pi e^{2}}{\hbar c^{2}}\delta({\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{g}}-\hbar\omega)\,{\rm Im}\left\{[\bm{v}_{cv}(\bm{k})\cdot\bm{A}(\bm{r})]^{*}[(\partial_{\bm{k}}-\partial_{\bm{k}^{\prime}})\cdot\nabla][\bm{v}_{cv}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})\cdot\bm{A}(\bm{r})]\right\}

\displaystyle-\frac{2\pi e^{2}}{\hbar c^{2}}\delta(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{g}-\hbar\omega)\,{\rm Im}\left\{{\partial_{\bm{k}}\,[\bm{v}_{cv}(\bm{k})}\cdot\bm{A}(\bm{r})]^{*}\cdot\nabla\,[\bm{v}_{cv}(\bm{k})\cdot\bm{A}(\bm{r})]\right\}

\displaystyle+\frac{2\pi e^{2}}{c^{2}}\delta^{\prime}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{g}-\hbar\omega)\,{\rm Im}\left\{[\bm{v}_{cv}(\bm{k})\cdot\bm{A}(\bm{r})]^{*}(\bm{v}_{\bm{k}}^{v}\cdot\nabla)\,[\bm{v}_{cv}(\bm{k})\cdot\bm{A}(\bm{r})]\right\}\,,

(15)

or, in an equivalent form more convenient for the calculations below, by

\displaystyle g^{(1)}(\bm{k},\bm{r})=\frac{2\pi e^{2}}{\hbar c^{2}}\delta({\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{g}}-\hbar\omega)\sum_{\alpha=x,y}\nabla_{\alpha}\,{\rm Im}\bigl\{[\bm{v}_{cv}(\bm{k})\cdot\bm{A}(\bm{r})]^{*}\partial_{k_{\alpha}}[\bm{v}_{cv}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})\cdot\bm{A}(\bm{r})]\bigr\}

\displaystyle-\frac{2\pi e^{2}}{\hbar c^{2}}\delta(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{g}-\hbar\omega)\sum_{\alpha=x,y}\partial_{k^{\prime}_{\alpha}}{\rm Im}\bigl\{[\bm{v}_{cv}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})\cdot\bm{A}(\bm{r})]^{*}\,\nabla_{\alpha}\,[\bm{v}_{cv}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})\cdot\bm{A}(\bm{r})]\bigr\}\,

\displaystyle+\frac{2\pi e^{2}}{c^{2}}\delta^{\prime}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{g}-\hbar\omega)\,{\rm Im}\left\{[\bm{v}_{cv}(\bm{k})\cdot\bm{A}(\bm{r})]^{*}(\bm{v}_{\bm{k}}^{v}\cdot\nabla)\,[\bm{v}_{cv}(\bm{k})\cdot\bm{A}(\bm{r})]\right\}\,,

(16)

where

\mathrm{Im}\{..\}
denotes the imaginary part of expression,
\delta^{\prime}(\varepsilon)=d\delta(\varepsilon)/d\varepsilon
,

\bm{v}_{\bm{k}}^{v}=(1/\hbar)d\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{v}/d\bm{k}
,
\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{g}=\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{c}-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{v}
, and one should set
\bm{k}^{\prime}=\bm{k}
after the differentiation.

The term
g^{(1)}(\bm{k},\bm{r})
describes the non-locality of optical generation. It shows that spatially inhomogeneous radiation generates
asymmetric in the
\bm{k}
space distribution of electrons. The asymmetry comes from the wave vector dependence of the
matrix elements of the interband velocity [first two lines in Eq. (
16
)] and the band dispersions
[third line in Eq. (
16
)].
For the exemplary case of
\bm{A}(\bm{r})=\bm{A}\exp[i\varphi(\bm{r})]
,
Eq. (
16
) yields

g^{(1)}(\bm{k},\bm{r})=-\frac{2\pi e^{2}}{\hbar c^{2}}\delta(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{g}-\hbar\omega)\nabla\varphi\cdot\partial_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}|\bm{v}_{cv}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})\cdot\bm{A}|^{2}\,\\
+\frac{2\pi e^{2}}{c^{2}}\delta^{\prime}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{g}-\hbar\omega)(\nabla\varphi\cdot\bm{v}_{\bm{k}}^{v})\,|\bm{v}_{cv}(\bm{k})\cdot\bm{A}|^{2}\,\,.

(17)

II.3
Summary

Summarizing the calculations above, we conclude that the current density of particles in the conduction band induced by spatially inhomogeneous radiation to first order in
q/k
has the form

\bm{j}(\bm{r})=\bm{j}^{(\rm loc)}(\bm{r})+\bm{j}^{(\rm nl)}(\bm{r})\,,

(18)

where

\bm{j}^{(\rm loc)}(\bm{r})=\sum_{\bm{k}}\bm{v}_{\bm{k}}^{c}\,f(\bm{k},\bm{r})\,,

(19)

f(\bm{k},\bm{r})
is the electron distribution function, which is found from the kinetic equation with the local generation term

g^{(0)}(\bm{k},\bm{r})
given by Eq. (
14
),

\bm{j}^{(\rm nl)}(\bm{r})=\sum_{\bm{k}}\tau_{1}g^{(1)}(\bm{k},\bm{r})\bm{v}_{\bm{k}}^{c}\\
+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{\alpha=x,y}\tau_{1}\nabla_{\alpha}g^{(0)}(\bm{k},\bm{r})\,[(\partial_{k_{\alpha}}-\partial_{k^{\prime}_{\alpha}})\bm{v}_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}^{c}]_{\bm{k}^{\prime}=\bm{k}}\,,

(20)

and
g^{(1)}(\bm{k},\bm{r})
is the non-local correction to the generation term given by Eqs. (
15
) or (
16
).
The current (
20
) is given in the relaxation time approximation with the momentum relaxation time
\tau_{1}
.
We note that the sum of two terms in Eq. (
20
) is invariant with respect to the choice of the wave function phases
while the individual terms are not. The current
\bm{j}^{(\rm loc)}
is determined by the spatial distribution of the intensity and polarization
of radiation whereas the current
\bm{j}^{(\rm nl)}
is also sensitive to the spatial structure of electromagnetic field phase.

Below we apply the developed theory to calculate the charge and spin-valley currents induced by structured light in 2D Dirac
materials.

III
2D Dirac material

We consider the class of direct gap 2D materials described by the effective
Hamiltonian
8
;
34
;
7

\mathcal{H}=\hbar a(\nu\sigma_{x}k_{x}+\sigma_{y}k_{y})+\delta\sigma_{z}\,,

(21)

where
\sigma_{\alpha}\,(\alpha=x,y,z)
are the Pauli matrices,
a
and
\delta
are the band-structure parameters,
and
\nu=\pm 1
is the spin (or valley) index.
\mathcal{H}
with
\nu=\pm 1
correspond to the blocks of the extended Hamiltonian
which describe the states with opposite spin projections or in different valleys related by time reversal symmetry.
The parameter
a
determines the linear-in-
\bm{k}
coupling between the valence and conduction band states
and
2\delta
is the band gap.

The effective Hamiltonian (
21
) is relevant with varying accuracy to a wide class of 2D systems,
including atomically thin crystals and quantum wells. In graphene, the states with
\nu=\pm 1
belong to the opposite
K_{+}
and
K_{-}
valleys
(which are additionally spin degenerate)
8
. In TMDC monolayers, the spin degeneracy in the valleys is lifted, so that the states
with
\nu=\pm 1
are also characterized by the opposite spin projections
34
. In narrow gap heterostructures, such as HgTe/CdHgTe quantum wells with close-to-critical thickness, the Dirac states are formed at the
\Gamma
point of the 2D Brillouin zone and the index
\nu=\pm 1
corresponds to the ”spin-up” and ”spin-down” blocks of the extended Hamiltonian
7
;
57
;
12
.

The energy spectrum, velocity, and wave functions of the conduction and valence bands have the form

\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{c/v}=\pm\varepsilon_{k}\,,\;\;\bm{v}^{c/v}_{\bm{k}}=\pm\hbar a^{2}\bm{k}/\varepsilon_{k}\,,

(22)

and

\chi^{c/v}_{\nu\bm{k}}=\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\varepsilon_{k}(\varepsilon_{k}\mp\delta)}}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}-\hbar a(\nu k_{x}-ik_{y})\\
\delta\mp\varepsilon_{k}\end{array}\right]\,,

(23)

respectively, where
\varepsilon_{k}=\sqrt{\delta^{2}+(\hbar ak)^{2}}
.

The interband matrix elements of the velocity operator
\bm{v}_{cv}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})=(\chi^{c}_{\nu\bm{k}})^{\dagger}\bm{v}\chi^{v}_{\nu\bm{k}^{\prime}}
,
which determine the generation terms (
14
) and (
15
), and the intraband matrix elements

\bm{v}^{c}_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}=(\chi^{c}_{\nu\bm{k}})^{\dagger}\bm{v}\chi^{c}_{\nu\bm{k}^{\prime}}
, which determine the current (
20
),
are given by

\bm{v}_{cv}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})\cdot\bm{A}=\frac{\hbar a^{2}}{2\sqrt{\varepsilon_{k}\varepsilon_{k^{\prime}}(\varepsilon_{k}-\delta)(\varepsilon_{k^{\prime}}+\delta)}}\\
\times\bigl\{\delta\bm{A}\cdot(\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime})+i\nu\left[\bm{A}\times(\bm{k}\varepsilon_{k^{\prime}}+\bm{k}^{\prime}\varepsilon_{k})\right]_{z}\\
+\bm{A}\cdot(\bm{k}\varepsilon_{k^{\prime}}-\bm{k}^{\prime}\varepsilon_{k})+i\nu\delta\left[\bm{A}\times(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})\right]_{z}\bigr\}\>,

(24)

and

v_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}^{c}=\frac{\hbar a^{2}}{2\sqrt{\varepsilon_{k}\varepsilon_{k^{\prime}}(\varepsilon_{k}-\delta)(\varepsilon_{k^{\prime}}-\delta)}}\bigl\{\varepsilon_{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}+\varepsilon_{k^{\prime}}\bm{k}\\
-(\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime})\delta+i\nu\left[\varepsilon_{k^{\prime}}\bm{k}-\varepsilon_{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}+(\bm{k}^{\prime}-\bm{k})\delta\right]\times\bm{n}\bigr\}\>,

(25)

where
\bm{n}=(0,0,1)
is the 2D normal.

Then, the local optical generation rates, Eq. (
14
), in the valleys assume the form

g_{\nu}^{(0)}(\bm{k},\bm{r})=\frac{\pi e^{2}a^{2}}{\hbar\omega^{2}\varepsilon_{k}^{2}}\delta(2\varepsilon_{k}-\hbar\omega)\bigl\{S_{0}(\bm{r})\left(\delta^{2}+\varepsilon_{k}^{2}\right)\\
-\hbar^{2}a^{2}\bigl[\left(k_{x}^{2}-k_{y}^{2}\right)S_{1}(\bm{r})+2k_{x}k_{y}S_{2}(\bm{r})\bigr]\vphantom{{\varepsilon^{c}_{\bm{k}}}^{2}}+2\nu\delta\varepsilon_{k}S_{3}(\bm{r})\bigr\}\,,

(26)

where

S_{0}=|E_{x}|^{2}+|E_{y}|^{2}
,
S_{1}=|E_{x}|^{2}-|E_{y}|^{2}
,
S_{2}=E_{x}E_{y}^{*}+E_{x}^{*}E_{y}
,
S_{3}=\mathrm{i}(E_{x}E_{y}^{*}-E_{x}^{*}E_{y})
, and

\bm{E}(\bm{r})=(\mathrm{i}\omega/c)\bm{A}(\bm{r})
is the electric field amplitude. In the paraxial approximation for normally incident beam,
i.e., when the in-plane photon wave vectors
q
are much smaller than
\omega/c
,
S_{j}
correspond to the (non-normalized) Stokes parameters.
The parameter
S_{0}
defines the local intensity of radiation,
S_{1}/S_{0}
and
S_{2}/S_{0}
are the degrees of linear polarization in the
(xy)
frame and in the diagonal axes, respectively, and
S_{3}/S_{0}
is the degree of circular polarization.
The terms
\propto S_{1},S_{2}
in Eq. (
26
) describe the optical alignment of electron momenta, i.e., the creation of anisotropic distribution of electrons in the
\bm{k}
space controlled by the linear polarization
62
;
41
;
13
;
50
. The term
\propto\nu S_{3}
describes the optical orientation, i.e., the selectivity of optical transitions in the valleys (or in the spin subspaces) to the circular polarization
40
;
39
.
Note that the band-edge absorption can be enhanced due to the Coulomb interaction between photoexcited electrons and holes
37
.

IV
Electric and spin-valley currents

Now we calculate the electric and spin-valley currents defined as

\displaystyle\bm{j}^{e}(\bm{r})=e\kappa\sum_{\nu=\pm 1}\bm{j}_{\nu}(\bm{r})\,,

\displaystyle\bm{j}^{sv}(\bm{r})=\kappa\sum_{\nu=\pm 1}\nu\bm{j}_{\nu}(\bm{r})\,,

(27)

where
\bm{j}_{\nu}
are the flows of electrons in the valleys,
e
is the electron charge, and
\kappa
is the factor that takes into account a possible additional degeneracy of the Dirac states not included in the Hamiltonian (
21
). In graphene, for instance,
\kappa=2
due to the spin degeneracy.

Before proceeding to the details of calculations, we make two comments. First, we note that the mechanisms of the current generation are different for
the contributions
\bm{j}^{(\rm loc)}
and
\bm{j}^{(\rm nl)}
given by the local and nonlocal generation terms, Eqs. (
19
) and (
20
), respectively.

The currents
\bm{j}^{(\rm loc)}
are formed in two steps. In the first step, the absorption of spatially inhomogeneous radiation leads to the emergence of photoelectrons with the nonuniform profile of the density, momentum alignment, or spin-valley polarization in the 2D plane. The subsequent relaxation of such
a nonuniform distribution in the second step gives rise to direct currents determined by the gradients of these parameters.
For the radiation with nonuniform intensity, the major contribution to the electric current comes from the diffusion of photoinduced electrons towards the regions with lower electron density. This is the Dember photoelectric effect well known for 3D and 2D semiconductors
11
.
The currents driven by the spatial gradients of linear and circular polarization originate mostly from the optical alignment and optical orientation, as illustrated in Fig.
2
. The interband absorption of linearly polarized light results in anisotropic in the
\bm{k}
space generation of electrons in the conduction band aligned perpendicular to the electric field polarization. Such a momentum alignment, shown by blue curves in Fig.
2
(a), is the same in both valleys and stems from the dependence of the probability of optical transition on the relative orientation between the electron wave vector
\bm{k}
and the electric field
\bm{E}
, see Eq. (
26
) and Refs.
2
;
58
;
13
.
The scattering of electrons with spatially inhomogeneous momentum alignment reflecting the spatial variation of the linear polarization leads to an electric current
\bm{j}^{e}
. For the radiation with nonuniform circular polarization, Fig.
2
(b), the spin-valley current
\bm{j}^{sv}
comes from the local spin (or valley) polarization of electrons created by circularly polarized light and the emerging spin (or valley) diffusion
4
;
5
;
28
.

Figure 2:
Generation of electric
\bm{j}^{e}
and spin-valley
\bm{j}^{sv}
currents by radiation with spatially varying polarization.
(a) Currents are driven by the alignment of electron momenta at interband optical transitions induced by linearly polarized light and
the subsequent momentum scattering.
(b) Currents are driven by the spin (or valley) polarization of electrons by circularly polarized light and the emerging spin (or valley) diffusion.
Electric and spin-valley currents are additionally coupled by the direct and inverse spin/valley Hall effects.

The current
\bm{j}^{(\rm nl)}
given by the nonlocal contribution to the generation term originates from the asymmetric in the
\bm{k}
space correction to the electron generation. It emerges simultaneously with the optical excitation and decays with the momentum relaxation time
\tau_{1}
. For the plane electromagnetic wave,
the asymmetry comes from the transfer of the in-plane photon momenta to the electron system, and
\bm{j}^{(\rm nl)}
represents the photon drag current
9
;
21
;
54
;
17
;
42
;
56
.
The currents
\bm{j}^{(\rm nl)}
are typically small compared to the currents
\bm{j}^{(\rm loc)}
by the factor
1/(kl)
, where
k
is the electron wave vector and

l
is the mean free path. However, they are sensitive to the phase structure of the electromagnetic field.

Second, the electric and spin-valley currents do not flow independently, they are converted to each other due to the spin/valley Hall effect (SHE/VHE)
and the inverse spin/valley Hall effect (ISHE/IVHE)
15
;
14
;
38
.
To first order in the charge-spin/valley conversion, the corrections to the currents have the form

\displaystyle\Delta\bm{j}^{e}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e\theta\,\bm{n}\times\bm{j}^{sv}\>,

\displaystyle\Delta\bm{j}^{sv}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\theta/e)\,\bm{n}\times\bm{j}^{e}\>,

(28)

where
\theta\ll 1
is the spin/valley Hall angle.
Theoretical calculations of the valley Hall angle in 2D materials have been attracting much attention
1
;
3
;
35
;
22
.
The valley Hall angle in the two-band Dirac model is estimated as
\theta\sim\hbar/(\delta\tau)
for electrons at the conduction-band bottom in gapped Dirac materials, whereas
\theta
vanishes in the gapless systems like graphene. In systems with spin-orbit interaction, a finite spin Hall angle can be expected even for
gapless structures like HgTe/CdHgTe quantum wells of the critical thickness.

In the subsections below, we calculate the contributions
\bm{j}^{(\rm loc)}
and
\bm{j}^{(\rm nl)}
to electric and spin-valley currents.

IV.1
Electric currents caused by electron diffusion and optical alignment of electron momenta

The electric current given by the local generation term has the form, see Eq. (
19
),

\bm{j}^{e}(\bm{r})=e\kappa\sum_{\bm{k}}\bm{v}_{\bm{k}}^{c}f_{e}(\bm{r},\bm{k})\,,

(29)

where
f_{e}=\sum_{\nu}f_{\nu}
. The distribution function
f_{e}
is found from the kinetic equation

\bm{v}^{c}_{\bm{k}}\cdot\nabla f_{e}(\bm{k},\bm{r})=g_{e}^{(0)}(\bm{k},\bm{r})+I\{f_{e}(\bm{k},\bm{r})\}

(30)

with the local generation term
g_{e}^{(0)}=\sum_{\nu}g_{\nu}^{(0)}
. Equation (
26
) for
g_{\nu}^{(0)}
yields

g_{e}^{(0)}(\bm{k},\bm{r})=\frac{2\pi e^{2}a^{2}}{\hbar\omega^{2}\varepsilon_{k}^{2}}\delta(2\varepsilon_{k}-\hbar\omega)\bigl\{S_{0}(\bm{r})\left(\delta^{2}+\varepsilon_{k}^{2}\right)\\
-\hbar^{2}a^{2}\bigl[\left(k_{x}^{2}-k_{y}^{2}\right)S_{1}(\bm{r})+2k_{x}k_{y}S_{2}(\bm{r})\bigr]\vphantom{{\varepsilon^{c}_{\bm{k}}}^{2}}\bigr\}\,.

(31)

The generation rate (
31
) includes the polarization-independent contribution
\propto S_{0}
and the terms
\propto(k_{x}^{2}-k_{y}^{2})S_{1}
and
\propto 2k_{x}k_{y}S_{2}
describing the local alignment of electron momenta by linearly polarized light, see Fig.
2
(a). The alignment is the same in both Dirac cones whereas the contributions sensitive to circular polarization cancel out.

Multiplying Eq. (
30
) by
\bm{v}^{c}_{\bm{k}}
, summing up over the electron wave vector
\bm{k}
and taking into account that
\sum_{\bm{k}}\bm{v}^{c}_{\bm{k}}g_{e}^{(0)}=0
, we obtain

\bm{j}^{e}(\bm{r})=-e\kappa\sum_{\bm{k}}\tau_{1}\bm{v}_{\bm{k}}^{c}\left(\bm{v}^{c}_{\bm{k}}\cdot\nabla f_{e}\right)\>,

(32)

where
\tau_{1}
is the momentum relaxation time defined by
\tau_{1}^{-1}=-\langle\bm{v}I\{f_{e}\}\rangle/\langle\bm{v}f_{e}\rangle

and the angular brackets denote averaging over directions of the wave vector
\bm{k}
.

Equation (
32
) for the current already contains the spatial derivative. Therefore, the distribution function
f_{e}
can be calculated
in the local response approximation, i.e., from the equation
g_{e}^{(0)}+I\{f_{e}\}=0
. The generation term
g_{e}^{(0)}
contains the zeroth
and the second angular harmonics in the
\bm{k}
space and so does the distribution function
f_{e}
.
The current sensitive to the linear polarization is determined by the second angular harmonic which decays with the relaxation time

\tau_{2}
defined by
\tau_{2}^{-1}=-\langle v_{x}v_{y}I\{f_{e}\}\rangle/\langle v_{x}v_{y}f_{e}\rangle
.
It gives
\langle(v_{\alpha}v_{\beta}-\delta_{\alpha\beta}v^{2}/2)f_{e}\rangle=\tau_{2}\langle(v_{\alpha}v_{\beta}-\delta_{\alpha\beta}v^{2}/2)g_{e}^{(0)}\rangle
. The zeroth angular harmonic of the distribution function relaxes with the energy relaxation time
\tau_{\epsilon}
(photoelectrons relax to the band bottom) and then, at longer times, decays with the photoelectron lifetime
\tau_{0}
determined mainly by electron-hole recombination. To describe the diffusion of photoelectrons we introduce the diffusion length
L_{e}
by
\sum_{\bm{k}}\tau_{1}(v^{2}/2)f_{e}=L_{e}^{2}\sum_{\bm{k}}g_{e}^{(0)}
. Then, calculating the sum in Eq. (
32
), we finally obtain

\bm{j}^{e}=-\frac{\kappa e^{3}L_{e}^{2}}{8\hbar\,\delta\,w^{3}}(w^{2}+1)\,\nabla S_{0}\\
+\frac{\kappa e^{3}\tau_{1}\tau_{2}a^{2}}{32\hbar\,\delta\,w^{5}}(w^{2}-1)^{2}\left(\tilde{\nabla}S_{1}+\bm{n}\times\tilde{\nabla}S_{2}\right)\>,

(33)

where
w=\hbar\omega/(2\delta)\geq 1
is the dimensionless frequency and
\tilde{\nabla}=(\nabla_{x},-\nabla_{y},0)
.

Equation (
33
) is relevant to the first order in the gradients of the Stokes parameters. The corresponding criteria for the first and second terms
in Eq. (
33
) are that the scale of field variation
L
is larger than the diffusion length
L_{e}
and the mean free path
l
, respectively.

IV.2
Electric current due to nonlocal generation

The currents determined by the nonlocal contribution to the generation, Eq. (
20
), are formed directly at optical transitions and decay with the momentum relaxation time. Here, additional scattering processes are not required for the current formation. Taking into account the equations for the generation terms,
Eqs. (
14
) and (
16
), the matrix elements of the velocity operator derived in Sec.
III
, and summing up the contributions
to the current from two valleys we obtain after some algebra

\bm{j}^{e}=\frac{\kappa e^{3}\tau_{1}a^{2}}{64\,\delta^{2}\,w^{6}}\biggl\{-2w^{2}(w^{2}+3)\,\bm{n}\times\nabla S_{3}\\
+2\left[(w^{2}+1)^{2}+(w^{4}-1)(\ln\tau_{1})^{\prime}\right]\,{\rm Im}\bigl(E^{*}_{x}\nabla E_{x}+E^{*}_{y}\nabla E_{y}\bigr)\\
+(w^{2}-1)^{2}\left[1-(\ln\tau_{1})^{\prime}\right]\,{\rm Im}\bigl[\bm{E}^{*}(\nabla\cdot\bm{E})-\bm{E}^{*}\times(\nabla\times\bm{E})\bigr]\biggr\}.

(34)

Here, we take into account a possible dependence of the momentum relaxation time on electron energy, which results in additional corrections

\propto(\ln\tau_{1})^{\prime}=\varepsilon(d\tau_{1}/d\varepsilon)/\tau_{1}
. For
\tau_{1}\propto\varepsilon^{\alpha}
, one has
(\ln\tau_{1})^{\prime}=\alpha
.

The electric current (
34
) contains the contribution driven by the gradient of the circular polarization (
\propto\nabla S_{3}
) and the contributions that are sensitive to the phase and polarization of electromagnetic field
and cannot be expressed in terms of spatial derivatives of the Stokes parameters. For the field in the form

\bm{E}(\bm{r})=\bm{E}\exp[\mathrm{i}\varphi(\bm{r})]
with the fixed vector
\bm{E}
, the phase-sensitive contributions assume the form

\bm{j}^{e}=\frac{\kappa e^{3}\tau_{1}a^{2}}{64\,\delta^{2}\,w^{6}}\biggl\{2\left[(w^{2}+1)^{2}+(w^{4}-1)(\ln\tau_{1})^{\prime}\right]\,S_{0}\nabla\varphi\\
+(w^{2}-1)^{2}\left[1-(\ln\tau_{1})^{\prime}\right]\,\left(S_{1}\tilde{\nabla}\varphi+S_{2}\bigl(\bm{n}\times\tilde{\nabla}\varphi\bigr)\right)\biggr\}\,.

(35)

The current (
35
) corresponds to the photon drag and contains the longitudinal contribution along
\nabla\varphi
and the transverse contribution perpendicular to
\nabla\varphi
. The latter is controlled by the linear polarization and vanishes for unpolarized light.

IV.3
Spin-valley currents caused by diffusion of optically polarized electrons

The spin-valley currents given by the local generation term emerge in the case of radiation with spatially varying Stokes parameter
S_{3}(\bm{r})
,
e.g., radiation with the nonuniform profile of circular polarization. Microscopically, the currents result from the selective excitation of electrons in
the
\nu=1
and
\nu=-1
states by circularly polarized light and the subsequent diffusion of spin-valley polarized electrons, see Fig.
2
(b).

The spin-valley current is given by

\bm{j}^{sv}(\bm{r})=\kappa\sum_{\bm{k}}\bm{v}_{\bm{k}}^{c}f_{sv}(\bm{r},\bm{k})\,,

(36)

where
f_{sv}=\sum_{\nu}\nu f_{\nu}
is the distribution function satisfying the kinetic equation

\bm{v}^{c}_{\bm{k}}\cdot\nabla f_{vs}(\bm{k},\bm{r})=g_{sv}^{(0)}(\bm{k},\bm{r})+I\{f_{vs}(\bm{k},\bm{r})\}\,,

(37)

with the local generation term
g_{sv}^{(0)}=\sum_{\nu}\nu g_{\nu}^{(0)}
,

g_{sv}^{(0)}(\bm{k},\bm{r})=\frac{4\pi e^{2}a^{2}\delta}{\hbar\omega^{2}\varepsilon_{k}}\,\delta(2\varepsilon_{k}-\hbar\omega)S_{3}(\bm{r})\,.

(38)

Note that, in the Dirac model, the spin-valley polarization by circular polarized light occurs only in materials with a finite band gap
2\delta
,
and
g_{sv}^{(0)}
vanishes in gapless materials with
\delta=0
.

After the calculations similar to those described in the Sect.
IV.1
we obtain

\bm{j}^{sv}=-\frac{\kappa e^{2}L_{sv}^{2}}{4\hbar\,\delta\,w^{2}}\,\nabla S_{3}\>,

(39)

where
L_{sv}
is the spin-valley diffusion length defined by
\sum_{\bm{k}}\tau_{1}(v^{2}/2)f_{sv}=L_{sv}^{2}\sum_{\bm{k}}g_{sv}^{(0)}
.
Note that the spin-valley diffusion length is limited not only by the lifetime of photoelectrons
\tau_{0}
but also the spin-valley relaxation time
\tau_{sv}
,

L_{sv}^{2}\propto\tau_{0}\tau_{sv}/(\tau_{0}+\tau_{sv})
.

IV.4
Spin-valley currents due to nonlocal generation

The spin-valley current given by the nonlocal generation term is calculated similarly to the corresponding electric current, see Sec.
IV.2
.
The calculation gives

\bm{j}^{sv}=-\frac{\kappa e^{2}\tau_{1}a^{2}}{32\,\delta^{2}w^{5}}\biggl\{(3w^{2}+1)\,\bm{n}\times\nabla S_{0}\\
-(w^{2}-1)\,\left(\bm{n}\times\tilde{\nabla}S_{1}-\tilde{\nabla}S_{2}\right)\\
+2\left[(w^{2}+1)+(w^{2}-1)(\ln\tau_{1})^{\prime}\right]\\
\bm{n}\times{\rm Re}\bigl[\bm{E}^{*}(\nabla\cdot\bm{E})-(\bm{E}^{*}\cdot\nabla)\bm{E}\bigr]\biggr\}\>.

(40)

The spin-valley current (
40
) contains the contributions driven by the gradient of the radiation intensity
S_{0}
and the gradients of the Stokes parameters
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
determining the linear polarization. Besides, it contains the contribution sensitive to the electromagnetic field phase. For the field

\bm{E}(\bm{r})=\bm{E}\exp[\mathrm{i}\varphi(\bm{r})]
with fixed
\bm{E}
, this contribution assumes the form

\bm{j}^{sv}=\frac{\kappa e^{2}\tau_{1}a^{2}}{16\,\delta^{2}w^{5}}\left[(w^{2}+1)+(w^{2}-1)(\ln\tau_{1})^{\prime}\right]S_{3}\nabla\varphi\,.

(41)

It can be viewed as the spin-valley circular photon drag.

Concluding Sec.
IV
we make an additional note. Electric signals measured in experiments are contributed by the currents of electrons in the conduction band
and holes in the valence band. The contributions of electrons and holes do not generally compensate each other due to electron-hole asymmetry
in the dispersion or in relaxation times. In doped structures, Coulomb interaction between photoexcited and resident carriers can play an important role in the formation of a net electric current. Particularly, if the photoexcited electrons and holes flow in the same direction, their charge currents would compensate each other. However, in fact, momentum transfer from the photoexcited electron and holes to the resident carriers results in the formation of the direct flow of resident carriers and, hence, the net charge current.

The electric and spin-valley currents calculated above can be systematized on the symmetry basis.
We assume that the electromagnetic field varies smoothly in the 2D plane and consider the photocurrents linear in the field gradients.
The most general expressions for such electric and spin-valleys currents are

\displaystyle j^{e}_{\alpha}=\sum_{\beta\gamma\delta}Q_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}E_{\beta}^{*}\nabla_{\gamma}E_{\delta}+\mathrm{c.c.}\>,

(42)

\displaystyle j^{sv}_{\alpha}=\sum_{\beta\gamma\delta}W_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}E_{\beta}^{*}\nabla_{\gamma}E_{\delta}+\mathrm{c.c.}\>,

(43)

where
Q
and
W
are the fourth-rank (complex) tensors, the indices
\alpha,\,\beta,\,\gamma
, and
\delta
run over the in-plane axes,
and
\bm{E}(\bm{r})
is the complex electric field amplitude.

In isotropic 2D systems, there are three (complex) combinations
\bm{E}^{*}(\nabla\cdot\bm{E})
,
(\bm{E}^{*}\cdot\nabla)\bm{E}
, and
E^{*}_{x}\nabla E_{x}+E^{*}_{y}\nabla E_{y}
that transform as polar vectors. The real and imaginary parts of these terms (or their superpositions) determine 6 linearly independent contributions to the electric current. Hence, the general expression for the electric current is

\bm{j}^{e}=\bm{j}_{\rm pol}^{e}+\bm{j}_{\rm ph}^{e}\,,

(44)

where

\bm{j}_{\rm pol}^{e}=2Q_{1}\,{\rm Re}\bigl(E^{*}_{x}\nabla E_{x}+E^{*}_{y}\nabla E_{y}\bigr)\\
+2Q_{2}\,{\rm Re}\bigl[\bm{E}^{*}(\nabla\cdot\bm{E})-\bm{E}^{*}\times(\nabla\times\bm{E})\bigr]\\
+2Q_{3}\,{\rm Im}\bigl[\bm{E}^{*}(\nabla\cdot\bm{E})-(\bm{E}^{*}\cdot\nabla)\bm{E}\bigr]\>,

(45)

\bm{j}_{\rm ph}^{e}=Q_{4}\,{\rm Im}\bigl(E^{*}_{x}\nabla E_{x}+E^{*}_{y}\nabla E_{y}\bigr)\\
+Q_{5}\,{\rm Im}\bigl[\bm{E}^{*}(\nabla\cdot\bm{E})-\bm{E}^{*}\times(\nabla\times\bm{E})\bigr]\\
+Q_{6}\,{\rm Re}\bigl[\bm{E}^{*}(\nabla\cdot\bm{E})-(\bm{E}^{*}\cdot\nabla)\bm{E}\bigr]\>,

(46)

and
Q_{j}
(
j=1..6
) are real parameters.
In deriving the equations above, we used the equality
\bm{E}^{*}\times(\nabla\times\bm{E})=E^{*}_{x}\nabla E_{x}+E^{*}_{y}\nabla E_{y}-(\bm{E}^{*}\cdot\nabla)\bm{E}
.

The current
\bm{j}_{\rm pol}^{e}
can be equivalently written via the spatial gradients of the Stokes parameters as follows

\bm{j}_{\rm pol}^{e}=Q_{1}\nabla S_{0}+Q_{2}(\tilde{\nabla}S_{1}+\bm{n}\times\tilde{\nabla}S_{2})-Q_{3}\,\bm{n}\times\nabla S_{3}\;.

(47)

We remind that
S_{0}=|E_{x}|^{2}+|E_{y}|^{2}
,
S_{1}=|E_{x}|^{2}-|E_{y}|^{2}
,
S_{2}=E_{x}E_{y}^{*}+E_{x}^{*}E_{y}
,
S_{3}=\mathrm{i}(E_{x}E_{y}^{*}-E_{x}^{*}E_{y})
,
\tilde{\nabla}=(\nabla_{x},-\nabla_{y},0)
, and
\bm{n}=(0,0,1)
is a unit vector normal to the 2D plane. So, the current
\bm{j}_{\rm pol}^{e}
is completely determined by the profiles of the light intensity
and the Stokes polarization parameters.

The current
\bm{j}_{\rm ph}^{e}
is determined not only by the polarization but also by the phase of the field.
To explore it, we consider the exemplary field in the form
\bm{E}(\bm{r})=\bm{E}\exp[i\varphi(\bm{r})]
, i.e., the field with the fixed amplitude

\bm{E}
and spatially varying phase
\varphi(\bm{r})
. In this special case,
\bm{j}_{\rm ph}^{e}
is proportional to the phase gradient
\nabla\varphi
and reads

\bm{j}^{e}_{\rm ph}=Q_{4}S_{0}\nabla\varphi+Q_{5}\left(S_{1}\tilde{\nabla}\varphi+S_{2}\bm{n}\times\tilde{\nabla}\varphi\right)+Q_{6}S_{3}\bm{n}\times\nabla\varphi\>.

(48)

For a single plane wave with the in-plane wave vector
\bm{q}
,
\nabla\varphi=\bm{q}
, and the current
\bm{j}_{\rm ph}^{e}
is proportional to
\bm{q}
.
Therefore, Eq. (
48
) can be viewed as a generalization of the photon drag current to the electromagnetic field with arbitrary varying phase. We stress that Eq. (
46
) is more general and is reduced to Eq. (
48
) only for special cases.

Analogously, there are three (complex) combinations
(\bm{n}\times\bm{E}^{*})(\nabla\cdot\bm{E})
,
(\bm{E}^{*}\cdot\nabla)(\bm{n}\times\bm{E})
, and
\bm{n}\times(E^{*}_{x}\nabla E_{x}+E^{*}_{y}\nabla E_{y})
that transform as axial vectors. Therefore, the spin-valley current is also described phenomenologically by 6 real parameters
W_{j}
as follows

\bm{j}^{sv}=\bm{j}^{sv}_{\rm pol}+\bm{j}^{sv}_{\rm ph}\,,

(49)

where

\bm{j}^{sv}_{\rm pol}=W_{1}\,\bm{n}\times\nabla S_{0}+W_{2}\left(\bm{n}\times\tilde{\nabla}S_{1}-\tilde{\nabla}S_{2}\right)+W_{3}\nabla S_{3}\,,

(50)

\bm{j}^{sv}_{\rm ph}=W_{4}\,\bm{n}\times{\rm Im}\bigl(E^{*}_{x}\nabla E_{x}+E^{*}_{y}\nabla E_{y}\bigr)\\
+W_{5}\,\bm{n}\times{\rm Im}\bigl[\bm{E}^{*}(\nabla\cdot\bm{E})-\bm{E}^{*}\times(\nabla\times\bm{E})\bigr]\\
+W_{6}\,\bm{n}\times{\rm Re}\bigl[\bm{E}^{*}(\nabla\cdot\bm{E})-(\bm{E}^{*}\cdot\nabla)\bm{E}\bigr]\>.

(51)

Time reversal symmetry imposes additional restrictions on the
Q_{j}
and
W_{j}
parameters.
Q_{1}
,
Q_{2}
, and
Q_{6}
as well as
W_{3}
,
W_{4}
, and
W_{5}
do not change sign under time reversal, which suggests that they are proportional to dissipation parameters, such as
\tau
, in even powers. In contrast,
Q_{3}
,
Q_{4}
, and
Q_{5}
as well as
W_{1}
,
W_{2}
, and
W_{6}
change their signs under time reversal and, therefore, are proportional to dissipation parameters in odd powers.

In Sec.
IV
we have calculated different contributions to the electric and spin-valley currents. In Sec.
V
we have developed their phenomenological description. The results of both sections are summarized in Tab.
1
, which shows the calculated expressions for the parameters
Q_{j}
and
W_{j}
.
The results are presented for 2D Dirac materials with the band gap
2\delta
. For gapless materials, the results are obtained considering the asymptotic

\delta\rightarrow 0
. For the opposite case of optical excitation at the band edge,

w=\hbar\omega/(2\delta)
is close to unity and one can express the currents via the effective mass
m^{*}=\delta/a^{2}
.

VI
Results and discussion

Now, we illustrate the developed theory on the example of
optical fields in the form of 1D polarization gratings, Fig.
3
.
Such gratings are formed by two obliquely incident plane waves with orthogonal polarizations with the resulting electric field in the 2D plane

\bm{E}(x)=\frac{E_{0}}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\bm{e}_{1}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}qx}+\bm{e}_{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}qx}\right)\,,

(52)

where
E_{0}
is the field amplitude (uniform over the grating),
q
is the in-plane wave vector,
\bm{e}_{1}
and
\bm{e}_{2}
are the complex polarization vectors.
In the paraxial approximation, one can disregard the difference between the polarization vectors and their 2D projections onto the sample plane.

We consider two types of polarization grating. The first one is formed by circularly polarized waves with the polarization vectors
\bm{e}_{1,2}=(1,\mp\mathrm{i})/\sqrt{2}
, Fig.
3
(a). The resulting electric field
\bm{E}(x)=E_{0}(\cos qx,\,\sin qx)
is linearly polarized and its polarization varies along
x

VII
Summary

To summarize, we have studied the generation of electric and spin-valley currents in 2D systems at interband absorption of light with structured intensity, polarization, and phase. Using the density matrix formalism and the Wigner transformation, we have derived the quasi-classical rate of optical transitions in the momentum and coordinate spaces, which goes beyond the Fermi golden rule. The optical generation contains the local and non-local terms. The former is determined by the local intensity and polarization of the electromagnetic field whereas the latter is sensitive to the spatial gradients of the field, including the polarization and phase gradients.

We have revealed the mechanisms of the charge and spin-valley current generation, which are different for the contributions
\bm{j}^{(\rm loc)}
and
\bm{j}^{(\rm nl)}
given by the local and nonlocal generation terms, respectively. The currents
\bm{j}^{(\rm loc)}
are typically stronger. They occur due to the relaxation of nonuniform distribution of the electron density, momentum alignment, or spin-valley polarization in the 2D plane created by structured radiation.
The currents
\bm{j}^{(\rm nl)}
originate from the asymmetry of optical transitions in the
\bm{k}
space and emerge simultaneously with the optical excitation.
Based on symmetry classification, we have established that electric and spin-valley currents induced by structured light in isotropic 2D systems
are described in the paraxial approximation by two sets of six linearly independent parameters. Using the kinetic theory, we have obtained analytical expressions
for those contributions in 2D Dirac materials.

The developed theory has been applied to the study of spatial texture of electric and spin-valley currents emerging in TMDC layers and graphene under excitation with polarization gratings formed by two coherent optical fields with orthogonal polarizations. In this geometry, the currents controlled by the gradients of the Stokes polarization parameters oscillate in space with the grating period.
Additionally, the electric and spin-valley currents contain contributions, which are uniform throughout the grating. These contributions are sensitive to both the polarizations and the phases of the incident waves and represent the charge and spin-valley photon drag. Our results suggest that radiation with structured parameters can be used for the efficient injection and control of electric and spin-valley currents in 2D crystals.

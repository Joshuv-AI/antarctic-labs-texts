Real state transfer on edge perturbed graphs with generalised clusters
Hiranmoy Pal

Abstract
We study the existence of real state transfer in edge-perturbed graphs having generalised clusters, where the Hamiltonian is taken to be either the adjacency matrix, the Laplacian matrix, or the signless Laplacian matrix associated with a weighted graph. This approach establishes a unified framework for constructing new graphs exhibiting perfect real state transfer by leveraging known examples with this property. The results presented herein generalise several previously established findings on real state transfer. In particular, we construct an infinite family of graphs with maximum valency five that exhibit perfect pair state transfer with respect to each of the aforementioned matrices, between the same pair states and at the same transfer time, despite the fact that these graphs are not regular. Keywords: Perfect state transfer, Pretty good state transfer, Graph spectra, adjacency matrix, Laplacian matrix, signless Laplacian matrix. MSC: 05C50, 05C76, 15A16, 81P45.

Introduction

We consider continuous-time quantum walk
[
11
]
on a graph
G
, which is governed by the transition matrix
U_{G}(t)=\exp{\left(itM(G)\right)},
where
t\in{\mathbbm{R}}
and
M(G)
denotes either the adjacency matrix, the Laplacian matrix or the signless laplacian matrix of
G.
A quantum state is represented by a unit vector in
{\mathbbm{C}}^{|G|}.
The state corresponding to a vertex
a\in G
is given by the characteristic vector
{\bf e}_{a},
while
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\bf e}_{a}-{\bf e}_{b}\right)
represents a pair state. Perfect state transfer (PST)
[
4
,
10
,
14
]
occurs at time
\tau>0
between two linearly independent states
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
in
G
whenever
U_{G}(\tau){\bf x}=\gamma{\bf y},
for some
\gamma\in{\mathbbm{C}}.
Since PST between vertex states is a rare phenomenon
[
13
]
, a relaxation known as pretty good state transfer (PGST) was introduced in
[
12
,
22
]
. A graph
G
is said to have PGST between
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
if there exists a sequence
t_{k}\in{\mathbbm{R}}
such that
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}U_{G}\left(t_{k}\right){\bf x}=\gamma{\bf y},
for some
\gamma\in{\mathbbm{C}}
of unit modulus. Over the past two decades, various network topologies exhibiting high-fidelity vertex state transfer have been identified. Notable examples include the Cartesian powers of the path on two or three vertices
[
10
]
, the path
P_{n}
on
n
vertices
[
10
,
15
,
21
]
, circulant graphs
[
1
,
2
,
18
]
, and Cayley graphs
[
3
,
9
,
20
]
, among others. Within the Laplacian framework, the concept of pair state transfer was initially introduced by Chen et al.
[
8
]
, where it was demonstrated that, among paths and cycles, only the paths on three or four vertices and the cycle on four vertices admit Laplacian perfect pair state transfer, provided at least one pair forms an edge. Subsequently, it was demonstrated in
[
19
]
that any graph exhibiting perfect vertex state transfer can serve as an isomorphic branch within a larger graph to facilitate perfect pair state transfer. This construction leads to an infinite family of trees with maximum valency three that admit perfect pair state transfer. Further developments concerning pair state transfer are presented in
[
5
,
6
,
14
]
. Subsequently, Kim et al.
[
16
]
generalised this notion by introducing
s
-pair states of the form
{\bf e}_{a}+s{\bf e}_{b},
where
s
is a nonzero complex scalar. Their analysis focused on the existence of PST between such
s
-states in complete graphs, cycles, and antipodal distance-regular graphs that exhibit perfect vertex state transfer. A further generalisation was presented in
[
14
]
, where PST between real pure states was investigated. The authors established three fundamental results:
(i)
every periodic real pure state admits PST with another real pure state;
(ii)
every connected graph admits PST between real pure states; and
(iii)
for any pair of real pure states
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y},
and for any time
\tau,
there exists a real symmetric matrix
M
such that
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
admit PST relative to
M
at time
\tau.
Additionally, the study characterises all real pure states that exhibit PST in complete graphs, complete bipartite graphs, paths, and cycles.

Main results

Let
G
be an undirected, weighted graph without loops having vertex set
V(G)
and associated weight function
w\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+},
where
E(G)
denotes the set of all edges in
G.
Let
M(G)
denote either the adjacency matrix, the Laplacian matrix, or the signless laplacian matrix of the graph
G.
Define
\widetilde{M}(G-C)
as the principal submatrix of
M(G)
obtained by deleting all rows and columns indexed by the elements of
C.
Next, we introduce the notion of a generalised cluster in a graph, which coincides with the standard definition of a cluster as presented in
[
7
,
17
]
when the graph
G
is simple.

Definition 1
.

Let
G
be an undirected graph having two subsets of vertices
C
and
S
where
|C|\geq 2.
The vertices in
C
are pairwise non-adjacent and share the same set
S
of neighbours. For each
v\in S,
the weight
\omega(u,v)=\omega_{v}
is a constant which is independent of the choice of
u\in C
. Then the pair
(C,S)
is called a generalised cluster in
G
of order
|C|
and degree
|S|.

Definition 2
.

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n
having a cluster
(C,S).
Let
H
be a graph having the vertex set
C.
Then
G(H)
is defined to be the graph with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)\cup E(H),
where all edge weights are preserved.

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n
having a cluster
(C,S)
of order
c
and degree
s.
We consider
1,2,\ldots,c
are the vertices in
C
and
c+1,c+2,\ldots,c+s
are the vertices in
S.
Suppose
H
is a graph on the vertex set
C
where
{\bf 1}_{c}
is an eigenvector of
M(H).
Let
{\bf z}\in{\mathbbm{R}}^{s}
be a vector having components
\omega_{v}
as given in Definition
1
, and

\zeta=\begin{cases}-1&\text{if $M$ is the Laplacian matrix,}\\
1&\text{if $M$ is the signless Laplacian or the adjacency matrix}\end{cases}

and

\delta=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $M$ is the adjacency matrix,}\\
1&\text{if $M$ is the Laplacian or the signless Laplacian matrix.}\end{cases}

The matrices
M(G)
and
M(G(H))
are given by

M(G)=\begin{bmatrix}\delta{\bf 1}_{s}^{T}{\bf z}I_{c}&\left[\zeta{\bf 1}_{c}{\bf z}^{T}~~{\bf 0}\right]\\
\left[\zeta{\bf 1}_{c}{\bf z}^{T}~~{\bf 0}\right]^{T}&\widetilde{M}(G-C)\end{bmatrix}

and

M(G(H))=\begin{bmatrix}\delta{\bf 1}_{s}^{T}{\bf z}I_{c}+M(H)&\left[\zeta{\bf 1}_{c}{\bf z}^{T}~~{\bf 0}\right]\\
\left[\zeta{\bf 1}_{c}{\bf z}^{T}~~{\bf 0}\right]^{T}&\widetilde{M}(G-C)\end{bmatrix}

If
{\bf x}\in{\mathbbm{R}}^{c}
is an eigenvector of
H
with
{\bf 1}_{c}^{T}{\bf x}=0,
then
\left({\bf x}^{T},{\bf 0}\right)
is an eigenvector of
G(H).
Using the principle of mathematical induction, we establish that for each
{\bf x}\in{\mathbbm{R}}^{c}
satisfying
{\bf 1}_{c}^{T}{\bf x}=0,
it follows that

\left[M(G(H))\right]^{k}\begin{bmatrix}I_{c}\\
{\bf 0}\end{bmatrix}{\bf x}=\begin{bmatrix}I_{c}\\
{\bf 0}\end{bmatrix}\left[\delta{\bf 1}_{s}^{T}{\bf z}I_{c}+M(H)\right]^{k}{\bf x},\quad\text{for all }k\in{\mathbbm{N}}.

The spectrum
\sigma(H)
is the set of all eigenvalues of the matrix
M(H).
Suppose
E_{\lambda}
is the orthogonal projection onto the eigenspace corresponding to eigenvalue
\lambda.
The support of a vector
{\bf x}
is denoted by
\sigma_{{\bf x}}(H),
which is the set of all eigenvalues
\lambda
satisfying
E_{\lambda}{\bf x}\neq{\bf 0}.
The following result is now immediate.

Lemma 1
.

Let
H
be a graph satisfying
M(H){\bf 1}_{c}=\lambda{\bf 1}_{c}.
For every
{\bf x}\in{\mathbbm{C}}^{c},

\delta{\bf 1}_{s}^{T}{\bf z}+\sigma_{{\bf x}}(H)\setminus\left\{\lambda\right\}=\sigma_{\left({\bf x}^{T},{\bf 0}\right)}(G(H)).

If
\left|\sigma_{{\bf x}}(H)\right|=1,
then
{\bf x}
is called a fixed state of
H.
A graph is called periodic at a non-fixed state
{\bf x}
whenever there exists PST from
{\bf x}
to itself. It is noteworthy that if PST occurs from
\left({\bf x}^{T},{\bf 0}\right)
in
G(H)
then
\left({\bf x}^{T},{\bf 0}\right)
must be periodic
[
14
, Lemma 22]
. One may use
[
14
, Theorem 8]
to find some periodic states in
G(H).

Corollary 1
.

Let
{\bf x}\in{\mathbbm{R}}^{c}
satisfying
{\bf 1}_{c}^{T}{\bf x}=0.
Then
{\bf x}
is periodic in
H
if and only if
\left({\bf x}^{T},{\bf 0}\right)
is periodic in
G(H).

The following result establishes a formal relationship between the transition matrices of the graphs
H
and
G(H).

Theorem 1
.

Let
{\bf x}\in{\mathbbm{R}}^{c}
satisfying
{\bf 1}_{c}^{T}{\bf x}=0.
Then

U_{G(H)}(t)\begin{bmatrix}{\bf x}\\
{\bf 0}\end{bmatrix}=e^{i\delta t{\bf 1}_{s}^{T}{\bf z}}\begin{bmatrix}U_{H}(t){\bf x}\\
{\bf 0}\end{bmatrix}.

The graph

and
{\bf y}
if and only if
G(H)
has perfect state transfer (or pretty good state transfer) between
\left({\bf x}^{T},{\bf 0}\right)
and
\left({\bf y}^{T},{\bf 0}\right).

As observed in Corollary

{\bf x}

In Figure

This provides an infinite family of graphs with maximum valency five that exhibit perfect pair state transfer with respect to each of the aforementioned matrices, between the same pair states and at the same transfer time, despite the fact that these graphs are not regular. It is worth mentioning that state transfer properties on a regular graph remain invariant regardless of the choice of matrix
M(H)
among the adjacency matrix, Laplacian matrix, or the signless Laplacian matrix. However, if the graph is not regular then it may not be the case. A remarkable observation here is that if

{\bf y}

\left({\bf x}^{T},{\bf 0}\right)

\left({\bf y}^{T},{\bf 0}\right)

There exists no complete graph

Corollary 3
.

There is no perfect state transfer in
G(H)
from the state
\left({\bf x}^{T},{\bf 0}\right)
whenever
H
is a complete graph satisfying
{\bf 1}_{c}^{T}{\bf x}=0.

The join of two graphs

and

is the graph obtained by taking the union of
G
and

and the vertices of

Another observation follows from Corollary

Corollary 4

The join

Flexible four-port MIMO antenna for 5G NR-FR2 tri-band mmWave application with SAR analysis
Authors: Manish Sharma, Bhaskara Rao Perli, Lovish Matta, Tathababu Addepalli, Kanhaiya Sharma, Fadi N Sibai
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper introduces a compact, triband four-port Multiple Input Multiple Output (MIMO) antenna optimized for mmWave 5G and navigation services. The antenna is built on a Rogers RT Duroid 5880 substrate, with dimensions of 31 × 42 mm² and a thickness of 0.4 mm. It utilizes a 50 Ω microstrip line to feed a stub-type radiating patch, creating a dipole-loop type structure on the substrate’s top side, with a full ground plane for narrowband. The antenna is initially designed for 38 GHz but was subsequently modified for triband performance by tapering the edges of the stub shape, enabling it to function across multiple frequency ranges. The tapered edges of the radiating patch enhance resonance across the three bands. To improve isolation and bandwidth, a parasitic element is strategically placed between the MIMO elements, results isolation greater than 30 dB at the 32 GHz and 38 GHz bands. The MIMO elements are mirror images placed adjacent to one another, while the other two elements are arranged 180º apart, ensuring compactness. The proposed antenna operates across three frequency bands: 27.76–28.15 GHz (n261), 32.02–32.46 GHz (part of n260 and n261), and 37.39–38.586 GHz (part of n260), offering enhanced resonance and improved isolation. The parasitic element reduces mutual coupling between adjacent elements, improving diversity parameters such as Envelope Correlation Coefficient (ECC) < 0.0010, Diversity Gain (DG) = 10 dB, Channel Capacity Loss (CCL) = 0.15 bits/sec/Hz, Total Active Reflection Coefficient (TARC) < -10 dB, and Mean Effective Gain (MEG) between − 3 and − 12 dB across all ports. Specific Absorption Rate (SAR) analysis for on-body applications confirms safe levels, with values below 1.6 W/kg at the resonating frequencies. Bending tests also show favourable results within the application bandwidth, further validating the antenna’s robustness. These technical improvements make the antenna highly suitable for integration into smart devices, defence navigation systems, mobile phones, and future 5G applications.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-79859-1.Keywords: 5G, High-density fixed services (HDFS), MIMO, n260, n261, On-Body devices, Parasitic element, Radio Navigation Services (RNS)Subject terms: Engineering, Physics

Introduction

The expansion of advanced technological gadgets like smartphones, medical devices, security systems, wearables, and automotive wireless applications is driving the integration of Internet of Things (IoT) devices. However, existing microwave frequency devices are insufficient to meet the burgeoning demands. Hence, the emergence of 5G (Fifth Generation) technology, particularly with mm-Wave Multiple Input Multiple Output (MIMO) technology, presents a promising solution due to its ample bandwidth capacity. Various frequency bands, including 28 GHz, 32 GHz, 38 GHz, 45 GHz, 60 GHz, and 70 GHz, are proposed for 5G and mm-wave applications. The 28/38 GHz bands are particularly favoured for their utility and advantages. For instance, the 32 GHz band, with a bandwidth range of 31.8–33.4 GHz as defined by the International Telecommunication Union (ITU), is recommended for fixed services across different ITU regions. This band supports various applications such as high-density fixed services (HDFS) and radio navigation services (RNS), offering a spectrum of 1600 MHz, which is conducive to infrastructure provision for mobile networks (IMT-2000).

Numerous research articles have explored the application of MIMO antennas in the mm-wave band, particularly at frequencies like 28/32/38 GHz. A dual-element MIMO antenna for a 5G new radio was introduced in1, featuring a T-shaped Decoupling Structure (DCS) to enhance isolation up to 20 dB. It is designed for the K-Band and parts of the Ku/Ka-Band, with a 16.38 GHz bandwidth. In2, A compact mm-wave antenna with a tapered slot design was introduced, offering wide bandwidth and enhanced gain. The array uses half-wavelength spacing in the horizontal plane, and the elements are integrated into a substrate waveguide for optimal impedance matching. A 2 × 2 MIMO antenna using a substrate integrated waveguide (SIW) was proposed in3, designed for mm-Wave 5G communication. Fed by a grounded coplanar waveguide, it achieved quad-band coverage with resonant frequencies yielding gains of 3.26, 3.28, 3.34, and 4.51 dBi. In4, an ultrawideband MIMO antenna was introduced with U-shaped branches, folded stubs, a Defected Ground Structure (DGS), and a radiating patch with a window-shaped slot. A decoupling structure improves isolation to below − 15 dB, making it a strong candidate for diverse wireless communication applications due to its enhanced diversity performance. In5, a MIMO antenna with a substrate-integrated waveguide (SIW) feed via coupling slots was proposed. A radiating patch with shorting pins acts as an inductive load, ensuring impedance matching at 28 GHz. A four-port mm-wave MIMO antenna was proposed in6, utilizing dipole antennas with capacitively loaded loops (CLL) to achieve a gain of 10 dB through a high refractive index region, while defects improve isolation and reduce mutual coupling. In7, a compact dual-band printed MIMO antenna for 5G at 28 and 38 GHz was introduced, featuring a rectangular microstrip patch with an inset feed and a slotted patch connected via a microstrip feed line, exhibiting favorable resonance characteristics. An improved four-port MIMO Yagi-Uda antenna for dual bands at 28/38 GHz was introduced8, featuring a corrugated dipole strip, extension strip, two reflectors, and one director, which enhances gain and isolation for mobile handsets. In9, a double-sided Electromagnetic Band Gap (EBG) structure was proposed to reduce mutual coupling in dual-band applications at 28/38 GHz, significantly improving radiation efficiency and achieving wide bandwidth without using decoupling structures. A compact microstrip antenna for dual-band operation at 28 GHz was developed in10, utilizing PIN diodes across a slit or through the ground plane to toggle between frequency bands by switching the diodes’ ON/OFF states. A dual-band MIMO antenna with circular polarization was introduced in11, featuring a central circular aperture and four corner slits. The patch includes additional holes to achieve resonance at specific frequencies, allowing bandwidths of approximately 2.5% at 28 GHz and 32.4% at 38 GHz. In12, a Chebyshev linear array (CLA) combined with a dielectric lens was proposed to enhance directivity through Chebyshev amplitude distribution. This design aims to improve the efficiency and isolation of the dual-port antenna for 28/38 GHz operation.

An innovative wideband composite antenna was introduced in13, featuring a circular primary element and a secondary parasitic patch, both fed via edge coupling for 5G mobile handsets at 28/38 GHz while maintaining a compact size. In14, a dual-polarized MIMO antenna for 5G applications at 28 GHz and 38 GHz was designed using a coplanar waveguide with aperture-coupled feeding, fabricated on a dual-layer Rogers RO4003 substrate. To achieve polarization diversity, an array of 2 to 4 elements was physically rotated at 45 and − 45 degrees. A MIMO antenna for mm-wave 5G wireless communication was presented in15, featuring four elements designed in an HP structure to operate within a bandwidth of 36.83–40.0 GHz. Each element’s radiating patch includes slots that create the HP shape, facilitating resonance in the 37–40 GHz range. In16, a multi-polarized mm-wave system for 28/38 GHz was introduced, comprising 32 elements arranged in an upside conical frustum configuration (UCFC) to achieve a multi-beam pattern and enable polarization control, enhancing the antenna’s performance and versatility. A metasurface-based MIMO antenna designed for sub-6 GHz applications, incorporating a 10 × 10 array of enclosed circular split ring resonators, demonstrates wideband characteristics across the 3.2 to 7.6 GHz range. This antenna achieves a high gain of 8 dBi and a radiation efficiency of 82%, as detailed in17. In18, a compact three-port MIMO antenna with super wideband capabilities was developed for 5G technology, utilizing rectangular L-shaped components and grounded parasitic elements for improved performance. The antenna exhibits remarkable isolation, maintaining less than − 20 dB across a wide frequency range from 26 GHz to 50 GHz. An analysis of a dual-band slotted loop MIMO antenna for 28 GHz and 38 GHz was presented in19. The design includes three Aperture-Coupled Feeding Slots (AFSL) on the top edge for 28 GHz resonance and another three on the bottom edge for 38 GHz. The antenna achieves impedance bandwidths of 3.5% at 28 GHz and 7% at 38 GHz. In20, a MIMO antenna features a circular patch design with two circular rings of stubs to minimize return loss, achieving values between − 12 dB and − 22 dB across three orientations. In21, a CPW-fed dual-port MIMO antenna for the 5G band includes a folded slot cell for effective operation at 5 GHz and 7 GHz. A metamaterial-based MIMO antenna achieves dual-band operation at 28/38 GHz, featuring two symmetric radiating elements with isolation of 20 dB at 25 GHz and 12 dB at 37 GHz, and enhances bandwidth from 3.8 to 4.8 GHz using metamaterials in22. A substrate-integrated waveguide-fed MIMO antenna, utilizing a trust-region gradient-based search algorithm, achieves exceptional isolation and high gain. This optimization enhances isolation to a maximum of 75 dB at 28.6 GHz, as reported in23. In24, a unique MIMO antenna enhances dual-band applications with linear and triangular stubs, achieving a return loss of less than − 10 dB and covering resonance ranges of 25.9 to 30.4 GHz and 36.4 to 40.2 GHz. In25, a flexible wearable antenna was developed using polyimide as the substrate, achieving a bandwidth of 28–31.5 GHz, primarily operating in mode 2 at 30.5 GHz. A monopole antenna for mm-Wave applications resonating at 28, 38, and 60 GHz is described26, featuring a CPW feed and a rectangular design. It operates at 30 GHz and achieves a bandwidth of 26–31 GHz, with a U-shaped slot for 60 GHz resonance. Additionally, a self-isolated micro-machined four-port antenna with flower-type fractal slots supports n77/n78/n79 5G applications27. A four-port slotted patch antenna covers an impedance bandwidth of 8.31 GHz to 36.14 GHz for higher FR-2 5G bands28. A compact four-port MIMO antenna measuring 28.30 × 28.30 × 0.508 mm³ utilizes loaded CSRR and DGS to achieve a bandwidth of 26.50 GHz to 30.40 GHz and a gain of 11.0 dBi29. A tree-shaped quad-port MIMO antenna offers a bandwidth of 23.40 GHz to 35.0 GHz for 5G applications30. A compact MIMO antenna sized 40 mm × 40 mm can be reconfigured for a bandwidth of 1.00 GHz to 3.00 GHz31. Lastly, a Fabric-Perot cavity in a 1 × 2 array achieves a bandwidth of 27.38 GHz to 33.34 GHz with a gain of 12.70 dBi and 94% radiation efficiency32. while a meta-material circularly polarized 2 × 2 MIMO array provides a bandwidth of 2.90 GHz to 3.70 GHz in a compact size of 1.40λ × 2.80λ × 0.09λ33.

Based on the analysis presented in the literature, none of the antennas discussed exhibit resonance within the specified triband. Most of the antennas are designed for millimeter-wave (mm-Wave) frequencies, particularly in the Sub-6 GHz, 28 GHz, and 38 GHz ranges, offering high gain, excellent isolation, and MIMO capabilities with wideband coverage. However, there remains a need for a self-isolated MIMO antenna that operates within a narrow frequency band in the 5G mm-wave spectrum while maintaining high gain and isolation. Considering the limitations identified in the literature, this study proposes a self-isolated, narrowband, high-gain, four-element MIMO antenna system with exceptional diversity characteristics for 5G mm-Wave wireless communication applications. Moreover, the suggested MIMO antenna exhibits outstanding isolation among the MIMO components, low interelement gap, and high radiation efficiency and gain. MIMO antennas resonate at three specific frequency bands, with a focus on 5G applications (28/38 GHz) in the mmWave spectrum and High-Density Fixed Services (HDFS) and navigation applications at 32 GHz. Single element analysis

Evolution process

This section focuses on the configuration of a single antenna element, outlining the various steps and methodologies involved in designing an antenna structure suitable for triband applications. The antenna is designed on Rogers RT Duroid substrate 5880 with a thickness of 0.4 mm, consisting of a patch that emits radiation on the upper side and a complete ground on the lower side of the substrate. The dimensions of the proposed single antenna are 19 × 16 × 0.4 mm³. Figure 1a illustrates the methodology employed to devise the proposed structure, which encompasses six distinct steps aimed at achieving a triband application. Figure 1b illustrates the analysis of results obtained from various steps in the antenna design process.

Evolution of single element proposed design (a) Steps involved in the Design Process (b) Return Loss.

Figure 1a outlines the various stages involved in designing an antenna suitable for triband applications. Initially, in Step I, the antenna is configured in a loop G-shape by embedding a 50 Ω microstrip line with a loop-shaped radiating patch on one side. This open-looped antenna resonates within a bandwidth of 27.86–28.23 GHz, exhibiting a return loss of 13.73 dB at 28.00 GHz. Additionally, the antenna demonstrates a dual resonance effect, with the second band covering a bandwidth of 38.13–39.23 GHz and a return loss of 26.61 dB at 38.66 GHz. Moving to Step-II, a slot is introduced on the upper left side of the microstrip, adjacent to the radiating patch. This adjustment extends the resonating bandwidth to 27.82–28.25 GHz for the first band and 38.18–39.29 GHz for the second band, with respective return losses of 13.10 dB and 24.52 dB at 28 GHz and 38.72 GHz. In Step III, another slot is added to the upper side edge of the radiating patch, resulting in a widened bandwidth for both the first (27.92–28.27 GHz) and second (38.27–39.42 GHz) bands, with return losses of 11.71 dB and − 23.89 dB at 28 GHz and 38.82 GHz, respectively. Step IV introduces a rectangular stub within the mid-side of the loop-shaped radiating patch, expanding the bandwidth to 27.94–28.32 GHz for the first band, 32.29–32.43 GHz for the second band, and 37.83–39.21 GHz for the third band, with acceptable return losses noted. Further improvements are achieved in Step-V by incorporating a rectangular slot on the inner side of the loop radiating patch, resulting in enhanced performance. The first band covers a bandwidth of 27.68–28.02 GHz, the second band ranges from 32.1 to 32.35 GHz, and the third band spans from 37.76 to 39.07 GHz, with good resonance noted at 38 GHz. Finally, Step VI presents the optimized version of the proposed antenna, which exhibits superior performance suitable for triband applications. Notably, the first band covers a bandwidth of 27.74–28.16 GHz, the second band ranges from 32.28 to 32.72 GHz, and the third band spans from 36.57 to 38.95 GHz, with respective return losses of 18.44 dB, -22.25 dB, and − 16.0 dB at their resonant frequencies. This final version demonstrates significant improvement over previous stages and is well-suited for triband applications.

Analysis of four-port MIMO proposed antenna

This section discusses the advancement of MIMO antenna technology from a dual-port to a four-port configuration for wearable applications in the mm-wave bands. The antenna has dimensions of 31 × 42 × 0.4 mm³ and is constructed on Rogers RT Duriod 5880 with a loss tangent of 0.0009. A full ground plane is utilized to address issues associated with a partially unconnected ground and to achieve a narrow band at the resonant frequency. The four-element MIMO antenna includes two parasitic elements placed between adjacent elements to enhance isolation. Figure 5a illustrates the four-element MIMO antenna configuration, while Fig. 5b shows the designed MIMO antenna with its connector. The optimized parasitic elements and their parameters are also displayed in Fig. 5b. The CTRI−BAND-shaped parasitic elements are engraved on the upper surface of the substrate to minimize mutual interaction among the radiating elements. The separation between MIMO elements (D5) is greater than λ/2 to minimize mutual coupling and mitigate multipath fading effects.

This section details the performance parameters of MIMO systems, which indicate the reliability of the antenna system for data reception. MIMO technology transmits multiple data streams from multiple radiating elements, utilizing diversity parameters such as Total Active Reflection Coefficient (TARC)TRI−BAND, Diversity Gain (DG)TRI−BAND, Envelope Correlation Coefficient (ECC)TRI−BAND, and Mean Effective Gain (MEG)TRI−BAND to characterize system performance.

Utilising spatial diversity and multiplexing, a MIMOTRI−BAND system allows for the possibility of data transmission to be received by other radiating elements even in case one of the elements fails to broadcast data. Conclusions

A self-isolated, four-port triband MIMO antenna with high gain and isolation is proposed for 5G mmWave applications. The design incorporates a microstrip-fed G-shaped loop radiating patch with truncated edges, fabricated on a Rogers RT 5880 substrate to ensure optimal performance in high-speed 5G mmWave systems. With a compact single antenna size of 19 × 16 mm2, it resonates at 28/32/38 GHz, providing a peak gain of 6–7 dBi and high efficiency. The four-port MIMO setup, measuring 31 × 42 mm2, features mirrored dual elements with a 0.4 mm thickness, making it ideal for high-frequency applications due to its low losses and thermal stability. Experimental and simulated results confirm its operation at 28/32/38 GHz, making it suitable for both 5G mmWave and HDFS applications. The antenna achieves a gain of up to 8 dBi, excellent isolation (> 15 dB), and total efficiency between 82 and 90%. It also exhibits outstanding MIMO diversity, with a high diversity gain (> 9.98 dB), low ECC (< 0.004), and a unidirectional radiation pattern. These properties validate the simulated and measured results highlighting its potential for 5G mmWave communication systems.

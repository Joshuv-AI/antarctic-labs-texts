Association between baseline left ventricular end-systolic diameter and one-year troponin response to tafamidis in transthyretin cardiac amyloidosis
Authors: Kei Morishita, Eisuke Amiya, Junichi Ishida, Masaru Hatano, Koki Nakanishi, Norihiko Takeda
Published: 2026

Recent advances in imaging have led to earlier diagnosis of transthyretin cardiac amyloidosis (ATTR-CA) [1]. As the disease progresses, the left ventricle (LV) undergoes structural remodeling, characterized by concentric wall thickening and a reduction in cavity size [2]. While these morphological changes are readily assessed in routine echocardiography, their association with the response to disease-modifying therapy remains unclear.

This study retrospectively included 41 consecutive patients with histologically confirmed ATTR-CA who initiated tafamidis between September 2019 and September 2024. Of these, 31 were analyzed after excluding 10 patients who were transferred within 1 year. All 31 patients were confirmed as wild-type ATTR-CA via TTR-positive biopsy and genetic testing. AL amyloidosis was ruled out via serum and urine tests or bone marrow biopsy.

The primary outcome was the 1-year biochemical response to tafamidis, defined as the change in high-sensitivity troponin I (TnI) from baseline to 1-year (ΔTnI), utilized as a surrogate marker of myocardial injury [3], [4], [5]. A composite of all-cause death or unplanned hospitalization was assessed as an exploratory outcome from 1-year assessment until the first event or the last clinical contact. Baseline clinical, laboratory, electrocardiographic, and echocardiographic data were obtained prior to tafamidis initiation.

This study was approved by the University of Tokyo Ethics Committee (No. 2650) and conducted per the Declaration of Helsinki. The requirement for informed consent was waived because of the retrospective nature of the analysis.

Paired comparisons of TnI used the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Associations between baseline variables and 1-year ΔTnI were assessed using univariable linear regression, with significant variables (p < 0.05) entered into a multivariable model. Event-free survival was compared between responders (ΔTnI < 0) and non-responders (ΔTnI ≥ 0) using Kaplan–Meier analysis and log-rank test. All analyses were performed using JMP software (version 18.2.1).

Baseline characteristics, including mean age of 81.1 ± 6.1 years and mean LV ejection fraction (LVEF) of 51.8 ± 10.7%, are detailed in Supplementary Table 1.

Tafamidis resulted in a small but significant reduction in median TnI from 66.0 (32.9–119.0) to 61.2 (35.7–104.7) pg/mL at 1 year (p = 0.048); mean ΔTnI was −9.6 pg/mL (95% confidence interval, −18.1 to −1.2). TnI levels decreased in 19 patients (61.3%) and increased in 12 (38.7%).

Univariable regression showed that age (β = 1.46, p = 0.031) and LV end-systolic diameter (LVESD) (β = −1.93, p = 0.003) were significantly associated with ΔTnI (Fig. 1); no other parameters were significant (Table 1).

In multivariable analysis adjusted for age and sex, LVESD remained independently associated with ΔTnI (β = −1.37, 95% confidence interval −2.65 to −0.10; p = 0.036; Table 1). During a median post-landmark follow-up of 398 days, event-free survival did not differ significantly between responders and non-responders (log-rank p = 0.275).

This study demonstrates that baseline LVESD is independently associated with the 1-year biochemical response to tafamidis in ATTR-CA, suggesting its potential utility as a routinely available morphological indicator of therapeutic response. With the increasing availability of disease-modifying therapies, identifying patients most likely to benefit has become a critical challenge.

Morphologically, the progression of ATTR-CA is characterized by concentric wall thickening and reduced LV cavity size, leading to reduced stroke volume [2]. Previous imaging studies showed progressive LV cavity reduction in untreated ATTR-CA, whereas cavity size was preserved with patisiran therapy [6]. These observations suggest that LV cavity restriction reflects structural disease progression.

Indeed, a small LV cavity is recognized as a high-risk morphological state across several cardiac conditions, including atrial fibrillation [7], aortic stenosis after transcatheter aortic valve replacement [8], and heart failure with preserved ejection fraction [9]. Our findings extend this concept to ATTR-CA, suggesting that a restricted LV cavity reflects limited response to tafamidis.

We used ΔTnI as a surrogate endpoint, given the limited sample size and clinical events. High-sensitivity troponin was reported as a predictor of mortality in ATTR-CA, and tafamidis persistently suppresses its rise over 3 years [3]. Furthermore, TnI increase during tafamidis therapy independently predicted all-cause hospitalization [4], and serial biomarker assessment in the first year carries independent prognostic significance [5]. Although our exploratory survival analysis showed no significant difference between responders and non-responders—likely reflecting the limited number of events—these data support ΔTnI as a relevant therapeutic indicator.

These findings are hypothesis-generating, and several limitations should be noted. First, the single-center retrospective design and small sample size limit generalizability. Second, exclusion of patients without 1-year follow-up may introduce selection bias. Third, echocardiographic measurements performed by different operators potentially introduced variability. Fourth, the surrogate biochemical endpoint limits clinical interpretation; a direct association between ΔTnI and hard clinical outcomes was not demonstrated and requires prospective validation.

Polynomials assuming only local prime powers
Przemysław Koprowski

Abstract
. We study the class of polynomials that map a local field (i.e., the completion of a number field at a non-Archimedean place) into the subset of its p p -th powers, where p p is the residue characteristic of the field in question. We present a characterization of such polynomials and show that this class is always much broader than the class of p p -th powers of polynomials.

1.
Introduction

It is a classical result (see, e.g.,
[
20
, Problem 114]
) that if
F\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
is a polynomial with integer coefficients such that
F(a)
is a square for every rational argument
a\in\mathbb{Q}
, then
F
itself must be the square of another polynomial. There is also a multivariate generalization of this theorem due to Murty (see
[
18
]
).

This raises the natural question of whether similar statements remain valid over other fields occurring naturally in algebraic number theory. In a private conversation, Carlos D’Andrea asked the author if the above result extends to the setting of
2
-adic numbers. More precisely: does there exist a polynomial
F\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
such that
F(a)
is a
2
-adic square for every rational number
a\in\mathbb{Q}
, while
F
itself is not the square of any polynomial with
2
-adic coefficients? An example of such a polynomial is
4x^{4}+4x^{2}+9
. We omit the proof here, since a more general result will be established later (see Proposition
5.2
). Nonetheless, this question motivated a systematic study of a class of polynomials— denoted
\mathscr{C}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})
—with coefficients in rings of algebraic integers, whose values are always local prime powers, where the relevant exponents coincide with the residue characteristic of the local field. A precise definition of the class
\mathscr{C}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})
is given in Section
2
.

The class
\mathscr{C}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})
has an interesting geometric interpretation. Recall (see, e.g.,
[
7
]
) that if
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a local field (i.e., the completion of a number field with respect to a non-Archimedean valuation) with residue characteristic
p
, then
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
can be partitioned into finitely many disjoint open sets of the form
aK_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times p}
(so they are classes of
p
-th powers of nonzero elements of
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
), together with the singleton
\{0\}
, which serves as their common boundary point. Thus,
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
can be visualized as a ‘fan’ of homeomorphic sets of the form
aK_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times p}
, joined together at zero (see Figure
1
). A polynomial
F
lies in
\mathscr{C}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})
precisely when it collapses this ‘fan’ structure into a single ‘blade’, mapping the entire local field
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
into the set of
p
-th powers. One can think of the class
\mathscr{C}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})
as—to some extent—analogous to the set of real polynomials that are non-negative everywhere. The field
\mathbb{R}
decomposes into two open half-lines,
-1\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
1\mathbb{R}_{+}
, together with their common boundary point
\{0\}
. A polynomial that is nonnegative everywhere can be seen as folding these two half-lines onto one another.

In this paper, we study the class
\mathscr{C}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})
and several related classes. The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we introduce the relevant notation and, in particular, define the class
\mathscr{C}(\mathscr{A})
for any subset
\mathscr{A}
of
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Section
3
presents a characterization of the polynomials that map the entire local field
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
into its set of
p
-th powers (see Proposition
3.7
). The class
\mathscr{C}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})
is decidable by the classical Ax–Kochen theorem; in Section
4
we give a simple explicit algorithm (Algorithm
4.9
) for testing membership in this class. Finally, in Section
5
we return to the motivating question and show (see Proposition
5.2
) that the class of polynomials mapping
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
into its set of
p
-th powers is always strictly larger than the class of
p
-th powers of polynomials—so the analogue of the classical result mentioned at the beginning never holds over local fields.

2.
Notation

Throughout this paper, we use the following notation. Let
K
be a fixed number field and let
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
denote its ring of integers (i.e., the integral closure of
\mathbb{Z}
in
K
). Next, let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime of
K
lying above a rational prime
p
. By
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
we denote the completion of
K
at
\mathfrak{p}
. We write
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}
for the unique normalized valuation
K_{\mathfrak{p}}\to\mathbb{Z}
, as well as its restriction to
K
. The associated valuation ring is
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\{a\in K_{\mathfrak{p}}\;|\;\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}a\geq 0\}
. ts maximal ideal is
\mathfrak{p}
, and the quotient
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}
is a finite field, called the
residue class field
of
\mathfrak{p}
. An element of valuation one is called a
uniformizer
of
\mathfrak{p}
. By the strong approximation theorem, one can always choose a uniformizer lying in
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
. It will be denoted
\pi
throughout the paper. Finally, we use the standard notation
e=e(\mathfrak{p}\mid p)
and
f=f(\mathfrak{p}\mid p)
for the
ramification index
(i.e., the valuation of
p
) and the
inertia degree
(i.e., the degree of the residue class field over its prime field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
), respectively. The quotient group
K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}/K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times p}
of the multiplicative group of
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
modulo the
p
-th powers is an elementary
p
-group. By Artin’s theorem (see, e.g.,
[
4
, Theorem 2.36]
or
[
15
, Proposition II.6]
) its order is precisely
p^{ef+2}
if
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
contains a primitive
p
-th root of unity, and
p^{ef+1}
otherwise.

As explained in the introduction, our main interest lies in polynomials whose values are local
p
-th powers, where
p
is the residue characteristic of the considered local field. For any nonempty subset
\mathscr{A}\subseteq K_{\mathfrak{p}}
, we define

\mathscr{C}(\mathscr{A}):=\bigl\{F\in\mathbb{Z}_{K}[x]\;|\;F(a)\in K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{p}\text{ for every }a\in\mathscr{A}\bigr\}.

We are particularly interested in the cases when
\mathscr{A}
is one of the rings
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
,
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
,
K
, or
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

3.
Preliminaries

The following proposition and its corollary will be used repeatedly throughout the paper. Although they are classical results, we state them explicitly for the reader’s convenience.

Proposition 3.1

(
[
7
, Corollary I.5.8.2]
)
.

For every
k>\nicefrac{{ep}}{{(p-1)}}
one has
1+\mathfrak{p}^{k}\subseteq K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times p}
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
a,b\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
be two elements of equal valuation
v:=\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)=\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(b)
. If

a\equiv b\pmod{\mathfrak{p}^{k}}\qquad\text{for some }k>v+\frac{ep}{p-1},

then the cosets
aK_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times p}
and
bK_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times p}
coincide.

\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{K})

We will prove that in the above diagram, the vertical inclusions are in fact equalities, while the horizontal inclusions are strict. Let us begin with the vertical ones.

Proposition 3.3
.

The following equalities hold:

\mathscr{C}(K)=\mathscr{C}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{K})=\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}).

Proof.

We prove only the first statement. The other one is fully analogous. One inclusion is immediate and was already observed above. It remains to show that
\mathscr{C}(K)\subseteq\mathscr{C}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})
. The key ingredients are the density of
K
in
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
and the fact that
K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}
decomposes into a finite disjoint union of open subsets.

Fix a nonzero element
a\in K_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Since
K
is dense in
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
, there exists a sequence
(a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq K
converging to
a
in the
\mathfrak{p}
-adic topology. A contrario, suppose that
F(a)
is not a
p
-th power in
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
. In particular,
F(a)\neq 0
. Write

F(a)=\pi^{v}\cdot u,

where
v:=\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F(a)
,
\pi\in\mathbb{Z}_{K}
is a uniformizer, and
u
is a
\mathfrak{p}
-unit. Since polynomials are continuous with respect to the
\mathfrak{p}
-adic topology, we have

F(a)=\lim_{i\to\infty}F(a_{i}).

Therefore, there is an index
i
such that

\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}\bigl(F(a)-F(a_{i})\bigr)>v+\frac{ep}{p-1}.

Corollary
3.2
asserts that
F(a)
and
F(a_{i})
belong to the same
p
-th power class. But this contradicts our assumption:
F(a_{i})\in K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{p}
while
F(a)\notin K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{p}
. This contradiction proves the claim.
∎

Before establishing the relationship between
\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{K})
and
\mathscr{C}(K)
, we need to prove some auxiliary results.

Lemma 3.4
.

If
F\in\mathscr{C}(K)
, then both the degree of
F
and the
\mathfrak{p}
-adic valuation of its leading coefficient are divisible by
p
.

Proof.

Let
F=F_{0}+F_{1}x+\dotsb+F_{d}x^{d}
, with
F_{d}\neq 0
. A contrario, suppose that either
d
or
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F_{d}
is not a multiple of
p
. Then there is an integer
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, possibly negative, such that
p
does not divide the expression
kd+\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F_{d}
and

k<\min\Bigl\{\bigl\lfloor\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(F_{i}/F_{d})^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{(d-i)}}}\bigr\rfloor\;|\;i<d\Bigr\}.

Let
\pi\in\mathbb{Z}_{K}
be a uniformizer of
\mathfrak{p}
. It follows from the above inequality that

\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}\!\bigl(F_{d}\pi^{kd}\bigr)<\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}\!\bigl(F_{i}\pi^{ki}\bigr)\qquad\text{for all}\ i\in\{0,\dotsc,d-1\}.

Consequently, we have

\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F(\pi^{k})=\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}\bigl(F_{d}\cdot\pi^{kd}\bigr)=kd+\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F_{d}.

By construction, the right-hand side is not divisible by
p
. Hence,
F(\pi^{k})
cannot be a
p
-th power in
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
, contradicting the assumption
F\in\mathscr{C}(K)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Let
F=F_{0}+F_{1}x+\dotsb+F_{d}x^{d}
be a polynomial of degree
d
. Recall that the
reciprocal polynomial

\rev F
is defined by the formula

\rev F=x^{d}\cdot F\Bigl(\frac{1}{x}\Bigr)=F_{0}x^{d}+F_{1}x^{d-1}+\dotsb+F_{d}.

Lemma 3.5
.

A polynomial
F
belongs to
\mathscr{C}(K)
if and only if its reciprocal
\rev F
does.

Proof.

It suffices to prove one implication. Assume that
F\in\mathscr{C}(K)
, and set
d=\deg F
. For any nonzero element
a\in K
we have

(\rev F)(a)=a^{d}\cdot F\Bigl(\frac{1}{a}\Bigr).

Since
F\in\mathscr{C}(K)
, we know
F(\nicefrac{{1}}{{a}})\in K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{p}
. By the previous lemma,
p
divides
d
, so
a^{d}\in K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{p}
as well. Hence,
(\rev F)(a)
is a
p
-th power in
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
for all nonzero
a
.

It remains to show that
F_{d}(a)
is a local
p
-th power also when
a
is zero. Here
(\rev F)(0)=F_{d}
is the leading coefficient of
F
. By Lemma
3.4
,
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(F_{d})
is divisible by
p
, so we may write

F_{d}=\pi^{kp}\cdot u

for some
k\geq 0
and some
\mathfrak{p}
-adic unit
u\in K
. Evaluate
\rev F
at
\pi^{m}
for an integer
m>kp+\nicefrac{{ep}}{{p-1}}
. The previous part of the proof asserts that it is some
p
-th power in
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Say,

(\rev F)(\pi^{m})=c^{p}\qquad\text{for some }c\in K_{\mathfrak{p}}.

Thus, we have

c^{p}=\pi^{kp}\cdot u+F_{d-1}\pi^{m}+\dotsb+F_{0}\pi^{dm}.

It follows that
u
is congruent to some
p
-th power modulo
\mathfrak{p}^{n}
, with
n=m-kp>\nicefrac{{ep}}{{(p-1)}}
. By Proposition
3.1
,
u
is itself a
p
-th power, and therefore so is
F_{d}
. We conclude that
\rev F\in\mathscr{C}(K)
, completing the proof.
∎

It is clear that the constant term of any polynomial from
\mathscr{C}(K)
must be a
p
-th power in
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
. So is its leading coefficient in view of the previous lemma.

Corollary 3.6
.

If
F\in\mathscr{C}(K)
, then both its constant term and its leading coefficient belong to
K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{p}
.

The next proposition establishes a relation between
\mathscr{C}(K)
and
\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{K})
.

Proposition 3.7
.

For any polynomial
F\in\mathbb{Z}_{K}[x]
, we have

F\in\mathscr{C}(K)\iff F,\;\rev F\in\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{K}).

Proof.

If
F\in\mathscr{C}(K)
then also
\rev F\in\mathscr{C}(K)
by Lemma
3.5
. Hence, one implication follows immediately from the fact that
\mathscr{C}(K)\subseteq\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{K})
.

For the converse, assume that both
F
and
\rev F
are in
\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{K})
. Fix any element
a\in K_{\mathfrak{p}}
. As
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the valuation ring of
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
, either
a\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
or
a^{-1}\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. If
a\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
then
F(a)\in K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{p}
since
F\in\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{K})=\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}})
.

On the other hand, if
a^{-1}\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, then we have

F(a)=a^{-d}\cdot(\rev F)(a),

where
d=\deg F
. Since
\rev F\in\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{K})=\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}})
, we know
(\rev F)(a)\in K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{p}
. Moreover, Lemma
3.4
implies that
p
divides the degree of
F
, so
a^{-d}
is itself a
p
-th power in
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Thus,
F(a)\in K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{p}
, as well.

In either case,
F(a)
is a
p
-th power, and since
a
was arbitrary,
F\in\mathscr{C}(K)
. This completes the proof.
∎

As announced at the beginning of this section, we now show that the inclusion
\mathscr{C}(K)\subset\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{K})
is strict.

Observation 3.8
.

The set
\mathscr{C}(K)
is a proper subset of
\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{K})
.

Proof.

Fix an integer
m
such that

m\equiv 1\pmod{p}\qquad\text{and}\qquad m>\frac{ep}{p-1}.

Consider the polynomial
F:=1+\pi^{m}\cdot x
. Then
p
divides neither the degree of
F
nor the valuation of its leading coefficient. It follows from Lemma
3.4
that
F\notin\mathscr{C}(K)
.

On the other hand, for every
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{K}
we have

F(a)\in 1+\mathfrak{p}^{m}\subseteq K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times p}.

Hence,
F\in\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{K})
by Proposition
3.1
. This proves that
\mathscr{C}(K)
is a proper subset of
\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{K})
.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

As a by-product of the above proof, we see that Lemmas

–
3.5
and Corollary
3.6
do not generalize to the class

4.
Decidability of the class
\mathscr{C}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})

The condition that a polynomial
F
takes
p
-th power values on the whole ring
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
or the whole field
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
(i.e., that it belongs to one of the classes
\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}})
or
\mathscr{C}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})
) involves infinitely many, in fact uncountably many, conditions:

(1)

\forall_{a\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}}\exists_{b\in K_{\mathfrak{p}}}F(a)=b^{p}

and analogously for
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Proposition
3.3
from the previous section allows us to reduce the problem to a still infinite but countable family of conditions. On the other hand, (
1
) is a first-order formula, and it is known (see, e.g.,
[
1
,
9
,
12
,
14
,
16
]
) that the first-order theory of local fields is decidable. Hence, there must exist a finite family of conditions determining whether a given polynomial belongs to
\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}})
(resp. to
\mathscr{C}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})
). It is possible—at least in theory—to obtain such a family by applying general quantifier-elimination techniques for local fields.. Nonetheless, in this section, we present a simple, explicit algorithmic solution tailored specifically to our setting.

Fix a nonzero polynomial
F\in\mathbb{Z}_{K}[x]
. By performing the square-free factorization (see, e.g.,
[
21
, §14.6]
), we can write

F=\lc(F)\cdot G_{1}\cdot G_{2}^{2}\dotsm G_{k}^{k},

where
\lc(F)\in\mathbb{Z}_{K}
is the leading coefficient of
F
, and
G_{1},\dotsc,G_{k}
are monic, square-free polynomials. In general, the coefficients of the
G_{i}
may lie in
K
rather than
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
. However, every coefficient of each
G_{i}
can be expressed as
\nicefrac{{a_{ij}}}{{c_{ij}}}
with
a_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}_{K}
and
c_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Multiplying each
G_{i}
by the
p
-th power of the least common multiple of the denominators, we obtain

c^{p}\cdot F=\lc(F)\cdot\tilde{G}_{1}\cdot\tilde{G}_{2}^{2}\dotsm\tilde{G}_{k}^{k},

where the polynomials
\tilde{G}_{1},\dotsc,\tilde{G}_{k}
now have coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
, and
c\in\mathbb{Z}
. It follows that
F\in\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{K})
if and only if
F_{*}\in\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{K})
, where

F_{*}:=\lc(F)\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{G}_{i}^{i\bmod p}.

Hence, in our analysis, we may restrict to polynomials not divisible by
p
-th powers of other polynomials.

First, we shall deal with linear factors of the polynomials in question.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
F\in\mathbb{Z}_{K}[x]
and suppose
F\in\mathscr{C}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})
. If
\xi\in K_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a root of
F
, then
p
divides its multiplicity. In particular, if
F
is not divisible by a
p
-th power of another polynomial, then it cannot have roots in
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Proof.

Write
F=(x-\xi)^{m}\cdot G
, where
G\in K_{\mathfrak{p}}[x]
is a polynomial not vanishing at
\xi
. Set
v:=\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}G(\xi)
. Suppose the multiplicity
m
is not divisible by
p
. In particular,
\gcd(m,p)=1
since
p
is a prime. Hence, there is an integer
w>v
such that

mw\equiv 1-v\pmod{p}.

Choose
a\in(\xi+\mathfrak{p}^{w})\setminus(\xi+\mathfrak{p}^{w+1})
, so
a=\xi+u\pi^{w}
with some
\mathfrak{p}
-unit
u\in K_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Applying the binomial expansion, we can write
G(a)=G(\xi)+\pi^{w}\cdot c
for some
c\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Since
w>v
, we have
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}G(a)=v
. Therefore,

\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F(a)=\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(a-\xi)^{m}+\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}G(a)=m\cdot w+v\equiv 1\pmod{p}.

Thus,
F(a)
cannot be a
p
-th power, contradicting
F\in\mathscr{C}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})
.
∎

The following result—in the spirit of Hensel’s lemma—is likely known to experts in the field. However, lacking a convenient reference, we feel obliged to formulate and prove it explicitly.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
F\in\mathbb{Z}_{K}[x]
be a polynomial. If for every
n\geq 1
the polynomial
F
has a root in
\mathbb{Z}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}^{n}
, then
F
has a root in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Proof.

Polynomials are continuous with respect to the
\mathfrak{p}
-adic topology, and each
\mathfrak{p}^{n}
is a closed subset of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Hence,

(2)

F^{-1}(\mathfrak{p})\supset F^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}^{2})\supset\dotsb\supset F^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})\supset\dotsb

is a descending chain of closed subsets of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. By assumption, for every
n
there exists
a_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}^{n}
with
F(a_{n})=0
. Choose a lift
b_{n}\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
of
a_{n}
. Then
F(b_{n})\in\mathfrak{p}^{n}
, so
F^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})
is nonempty. Consequently, (
2
) is a descending chain of nonempty closed subsets of the compact set
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Thus, it has a nonempty intersection. Pick
a\in\bigcap_{n\geq 1}F^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})
. Then
F(a)\in\mathfrak{p}^{n}
for every exponent
n\geq 1
, which means that
F(a)=0
.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
F\in\mathbb{Z}_{K}[x]
be a polynomial with no roots in
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
, and assume that
F
is not divisible by a
p
-th power of another polynomial. Then there is a finite, explicitly constructed set
\mathscr{A}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{K}

(
depending on
F
)
such that

F\in\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}})\iff F\in\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{K})\iff F\in\mathscr{C}(\mathscr{A}).

The equivalence of the first two conditions was already established in Proposition
3.3
. It remains to prove the existence of a set
\mathscr{A}
for which the other equivalence holds. This is done in the following algorithm.

Algorithm 4.4
.

Let
F\in\mathbb{Z}_{K}[x]
be as in Proposition
4.3
. This algorithm decides whether
F\in\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{K})
.

(1)

Set
M\leftarrow\bigl\lfloor\nicefrac{{ep}}{{(p-1)}}\bigr\rfloor+1
, and initialize
m\leftarrow 0
.

(2)

Let
\mathscr{A}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{K}
be a system of representatives of the quotient ring
\mathbb{Z}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}^{m+M}
.

(3)

For each
a\in\mathscr{A}
, perform the following steps:

(a)

If
F(a)\notin K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{p}

(
use, e.g.,
[
13
, Algorithm 5]
)
, then output ‘false’ and halt.

(b)

If
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F(a)>m
, update
m\leftarrow\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F(a)
and return to step (
2
).

(4)

Output ‘true’.

Proof of correctness.

We first prove that the algorithm terminates. To this end, it suffices to show that the test in step (
3b
) can be triggered only finitely many times. A contrario, suppose that this is not the case. Then there exists an infinite sequence
(a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
such that the sequence
\bigl(\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F(a_{i})\smash{\bigl)}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of valuations is strictly increasing (and hence unbounded). Fix any positive integer
n
, and let
i
be an index such that

\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F(a_{i-1})<n\leq\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F(a_{i}).

Then the residue class of
a_{i}
is a root of
F
in the ring
\mathbb{Z}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}^{n}
.
This implies that
F
has a root in every quotient ring
\mathbb{Z}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}^{n}
, and therefore a root in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}\subset K_{\mathfrak{p}}
by Lemma
4.2
, contradicting the assumption on
F
. This proves that the algorithm indeed terminates.

We now prove the correctness of the output. Clearly, the algorithm can return ‘false’ only when it finds an explicit counterexample to the condition ‘
F\in\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{K})
’. It remains to show that the algorithm does not return ‘true’ incorrectly (i.e. that it produces no false positives).

Let
m_{1},m_{2},\dotsc
denote the successive values of the variable
m
during the execution, and let
\mathscr{A}_{1},\mathscr{A}_{2},\dotsc
be the corresponding sets of representatives. In particular,
\mathscr{A}_{i}
is a system of representatives of
\mathbb{Z}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}^{m_{i}+M}
.

Suppose there exists
b\in\mathbb{Z}_{K}
such that
F(b)\notin K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{p}
. Then there exists
a_{1}\in\mathscr{A}_{1}
such that

a_{1}\equiv b\pmod{\mathfrak{p}^{m_{1}+M}}.

It follows that
F(a_{1})\equiv F(b)\pmod{\mathfrak{p}^{m_{1}+M}}
.

If
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F(b)=m_{1}=0
, then also
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F(a_{1})=0
, and Corollary
3.2
implies that
F(b)\cdot K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times p}=F(a_{1})\cdot K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times p}
. Hence
F(a_{1})\notin K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{p}
, and the algorithm correctly outputs ‘false’.

On the other hand, if
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F(b)
is greater than
m_{1}
, then so is
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F(a_{1})
. In this case
\mathscr{A}_{2}
is a system of representatives of
\mathbb{Z}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}^{m_{2}+M}
with
m_{2}=\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F(a_{1})
. Again,
b
is congruent to some
a_{2}\in\mathscr{A}_{2}
modulo
\mathfrak{p}^{m_{2}+M}
. Repeating this process, we eventually find
a_{k}\in\mathscr{A}_{k}
such that

F(b)\equiv F(a_{k})\pmod{\mathfrak{p}^{m_{k}+M}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F(b)=\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F(a_{k})\leq m_{k}.

By Corollary
3.2
, the
p
-th power classes
F(b)\cdot K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times p}
and
F(a_{k})\cdot K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times p}
coincide. Hence
F(a_{k})\notin K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{p}
, and the algorithm outputs ‘false’. This shows that the algorithm never returns ‘true’ incorrectly.

Thus, the algorithm terminates and is correct.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Algorithm
4.4
, as presented, is sufficient to establish the existence of
\mathscr{A}
. From a computational perspective, however, it is rather suboptimal. In particular, once an element
a
with
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F(a)>m
is found, it would be more economical to extend
\mathscr{A}
only by those representatives of
\mathbb{Z}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}^{M+\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F(a)}
that are congruent to
a
modulo
\mathfrak{p}^{M+m}
, rather than reconstructing the entire set. Furthermore, an optimized implementation would record which elements have already been tested, thereby avoiding redundant repetitions that may occur in the current version when
\mathscr{A}
is updated.

Algorithm
4.4
constructs the set
\mathscr{A}
explicitly, but it does not yield any clear information about its size. Our next goal is therefore to derive an upper bound for
|\mathscr{A}|
. To this end, we first recall the notion of the Krasner constant.

Let
F\in K_{\mathfrak{p}}[x]
be a polynomial of degree
d:=\deg F\geq 2
, and let
L\supset K_{\mathfrak{p}}
be a splitting field of
F
. Denote by
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}
the (unique) extension of the valuation from
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
to
L
. If
\xi_{1},\dotsc,\xi_{d}\in L
are all the roots of
F
, then the
Krasner constant
of
F
is defined as

\kras F:=\max\bigl\{\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(\xi_{u}-\xi_{j})\;|\;i\neq j\bigr\}.

Lemma 4.6
.

If a polynomial
F\in\mathbb{Z}_{K}[x]
of degree
d
has no roots in
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
, then

\max\bigl\{\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F(a)\;|\;a\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}\bigr\}<d\cdot\kras F+\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}\lc(F).

Proof.

Denote
m:=\max\bigl\{\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F(a)\;|\;a\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}\bigr\}
. Writing
F
in factored form,

F=\lc(F)\cdot(x-\xi_{1})\dotsm(x-\xi_{d}).

we obtain for any
a\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
:

(3)

\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F(a)=\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}\lc(F)+\sum_{i\leq d}\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(a-\xi_{i}).

Now, the Krasner’s lemma (see, e.g.,
[
17
, Lemma 4.5]
or
[
5
, Proposition 4.4.6]
) asserts that if for some
i\leq d
we had

\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(a-\xi_{i})>\max_{j\neq i}\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(\xi_{j}-\xi_{i}),

then
K_{\mathfrak{p}}(\xi_{i})\subseteq K_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)=K_{\mathfrak{p}}
, contradicting the assumption that
F
has no root in
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
. It follows that for every
i\leq d
, we have

\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(a-\xi_{i})\leq\max\{\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(\xi_{j}-\xi_{i})\;|\;j\neq i\}\leq\kras F.

Therefore, (
3
) yields the inequality
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F(a)\leq\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}\lc(F)+d\kras F
. Thus
m\leq\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}\lc(F)+d\kras F
, as expected.
∎

Proposition 4.7
.

The cardinality of
\mathscr{A}
is bounded above by
p^{A}
, where

A:=\frac{efp}{p-1}+fd\cdot\kras F+f\cdot\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}\lc(F).

Proof.

Denote
M:=\nicefrac{{ep}}{{(p-1)}}
and
\kappa:=\kras F
. By the way the set
\mathscr{A}
is constructed in Algorithm
4.4
, we have

|\mathscr{A}|=|\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}^{m+M}|=p^{fm+fM},

where

m=\max\bigl\{\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F(a)\;|\;a\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}\bigr\}.

It therefore suffices to show that
m\leq d\kappa+\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}\lc(F)
.

∎

At first sight, it may seem that we have simply replaced one unknown quantity depending on
F
(the size of
\mathscr{A}
) with another (the Krasner constant). However, the Krasner constant has been the subject of extensive study, and effective bounds are available in terms of the degree and the height of
F
. In particular,
[
19
, Lemma 2.3]
shows that if
F
is a polynomial with coefficients in the ring
\mathbb{Z}
of rational integers, then

\kras F\leq\frac{3}{2}\cdot d\cdot\log_{p}(d)\cdot H(f)^{1-d},

where
H(F)
denotes the
height
of
F
, i.e., the maximum absolute value of its coefficients.

As a direct consequence, we obtain:

Corollary 4.8
.

If
f\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
, then

\log_{p}|\mathscr{A}|\leq\frac{p}{p-1}+\frac{3}{2}\cdot d^{2}\cdot\log_{p}(d)\cdot H(F)^{1-d}+\ord_{p}\lc(F).

For further results on the Krasner constant, we refer the reader to
[
2
,
3
,
10
,
11
]
. We can now present an explicit algorithm for testing if a polynomial belongs to the class
\mathscr{C}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})
.

Algorithm 4.9
.

Given a polynomial
F\in\mathbb{Z}_{K}[x]
this algorithm decides whether
F\in\mathscr{C}(K)
.

(1)

Using polynomial square-free decomposition and clearing denominators, write

c^{p}\cdot F=\lc(F)\cdot\tilde{G}_{1}\cdot\tilde{G}_{2}^{2}\dotsm\tilde{G}_{k}^{k},

where
c\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\tilde{G}_{1},\dotsc,\tilde{G}_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{K}[x]
are square-free polynomials.

(2)

Set

F_{*}:=\lc(F)\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{G}_{i}^{i\bmod p}.

(3)

Check if
F_{*}
has a root in
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
, if it does output ‘false’ and halt.

(4)

Execute Algorithm
4.4
for
F_{*}
and
\rev F_{*}
.

(5)

If the result is ‘false’ for any of these two polynomials, output ‘false’. Otherwise, output ‘true’.

The correctness of the above algorithm is immediate in view of the preceding discussion and Proposition
3.7
.

Remark 4.10
.

One may also consider a broader class of polynomials than the one studied in this paper. Fix a subset

\mathscr{S}=\bigl\{s_{1}K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times p},\dotsc,s_{k}K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times p}\bigr\}

of the
p
-power classes, and define the class of polynomials that map the entire field
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
into the union of these classes (possibly together with zero):

\bigl\{F\in\mathbb{Z}_{K}[x]\;|\;F(a)\in\{0\}\cup s_{1}K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times p}\cup\dotsb\cup s_{k}K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times p}\;\text{ for all }a\in K_{\mathfrak{p}}\bigr\}.

For instance, the polynomial
27x^{9}+54x^{6}+54x^{3}+40
maps
\mathbb{Q}_{3}
into
\mathbb{Q}_{3}^{\times 3}\cup 4\mathbb{Q}_{3}^{\times 3}
. In terms of the illustration from the introduction, such polynomials only
partially
fold the ‘fan’ of
p
-power classes, rather than collapsing it completely.

Easy adaptations of Algorithms
4.4
and
4.9
provide a procedure for testing if a polynomial belongs to these more general classes. Unfortunately, these classes no longer form monoids.

5.
Relations to the
p
-th powers

We can now focus on the original question—posed in the introduction—whether the polynomials that assume
p
-th powers everywhere on
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
must already be
p
-th powers of some other polynomials. We show that it is not the case (see Proposition
5.2
). Moreover, in general, such polynomials are not even approximated by
p
-th powers except on a compact subset of
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
(see Proposition
5.5
and the comment following it). The first observation is trivial:

Observation 5.1
.

For every subset
\mathscr{A}\subset K_{\mathfrak{p}}
, the set
\mathscr{C}(\mathscr{A})
is a multiplicative monoid containing the submonoid
\mathbb{Z}_{K}[x]\cap K_{\mathfrak{p}}[x]^{p}
.

The original question mentioned in the introduction asked whether this submonoid is proper when
\mathscr{A}=\mathbb{Q}
and
K_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathbb{Q}_{2}
. The following proposition shows that the answer is affirmative for every number field
K
.

Proposition 5.2
.

The monoid
\mathbb{Z}_{K}[x]\cap K_{\mathfrak{p}}[x]^{p}
is a proper submonoid of
\mathscr{C}(K)
.

Proof.

We must show that
\mathscr{C}(K)
contains polynomials that are not
p
-th powers of other polynomials. As always, let
\pi\in\mathbb{Z}_{K}
be a uniformizer of
\mathfrak{p}
. Consider the following polynomial:

F:=\bigl(1+\pi\cdot x^{p})^{p}+\pi^{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{K}[x],

where
m
is an integer strictly greater than
\nicefrac{{ep}}{{(p-1)}}
. We claim that
F(a)\in K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{p}
for every
a\in K
.

Write
a
in the form
a=\pi^{v}\cdot u
, where
u
is a
\mathfrak{p}
-unit and
v=\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}a
. We shall consider two cases. First, assume that
v\geq 0
. Then,
1+\pi\cdot a
is a
\mathfrak{p}
-adic unit and so
F(a)
is congruent to its
p
-th power modulo
\mathfrak{p}^{m}
. By Proposition
3.1
, it follows that
F(a)
is a
p
-th power.

Conversely, assume that
v<0
. It is clear that
F(a)
belongs to
K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{p}
if and only if
\pi^{-vp^{2}-p}\cdot F(a)
does. Write

\pi^{-vp^{2}-p}\cdot F(\pi^{v}\cdot u)=\bigl(\pi^{-vp-1}+u^{p}\bigr)^{p}+\pi^{m-vp^{2}-p}.

Since
v<0
, we have
-vp-1>1
and
m-vp^{2}-p>m
. Consequently,

\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(\pi^{-vp-1}+u^{p})=\min\{-vp-1,0\}=0.

Therefore, the expression in parentheses is a
\mathfrak{p}
-adic unit, and the entire right-hand side is congruent to a
p
-th power of a unit modulo
\mathfrak{p}^{m-vp^{2}-p}\subset\mathfrak{p}^{m}
. Thus,
F(a)\in K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{p}
, as claimed.

It remains to show that
F
is not itself a
p
-th power of a polynomial over
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Differentiating, we obtain

F^{\prime}=\pi\cdot p^{2}\cdot x^{p-1}\cdot\bigl(1+\pi\cdot x^{p}\bigr)^{p-1}.

Moreover,

F\equiv 1+\pi^{m}\neq 0\pmod{x}\qquad\text{and}\qquad F\equiv\pi^{m}\neq 0\pmod{1+\pi\cdot x^{p}}.

It follows that
F
and
F^{\prime}
are relatively prime, and hence
F
is square-free. A square-free polynomial cannot be a perfect
p
-th power, which completes the proof.
∎

Let us now return to the analogy between the class
\mathscr{C}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})
and the class of real positive semi-definite polynomials. Let
\|\cdot\|
denote the max-norm on the space of real polynomials:

\|F_{0}+F_{1}x+\dotsb+F_{d}x^{d}\|:=\max\bigl\{|F_{1}|,\dotsc,|F_{d}|\bigr\}.

Consider a real polynomial
F\in\mathbb{R}[x]
that is strictly positive on
\mathbb{R}
. Then there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that every polynomial
G
satisfying
\|F-G\|<\varepsilon
is also strictly positive on
\mathbb{R}
. This stability property fails, however, when
F
is merely non-negative. For instance,
F=x^{2}
can be approximated by
G=x^{2}-\xi^{2}
with
\xi
arbitrarily small, yet
G
is no longer non-negative. An analogous phenomenon occurs for the class
\mathscr{C}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
F=F_{0}+F_{1}x+\dotsb+F_{d}x^{d}
be a polynomial of degree
d
. Assume that
f\in\mathscr{C}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})
and
F
has no roots in
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Let
M
be an integer satisfying

M\geq d\cdot\kras F+\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(F_{0}\cdot F_{d})+\frac{ep}{p-1}.

If
G=G_{0}+G_{1}x+\dotsb+G_{d}x^{d}
is another polynomial and

\min\bigl\{\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(F_{i}-G_{i})\;|\;i\leq d\bigr\}>M,

then
G\in\mathscr{C}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})
.

Proof.

Write
G
as
G=F+H
, where
H=H_{0}+H_{1}x+\dotsb+H_{d}x^{d}
, By assumption,
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}H_{i}>M
for every
i\leq d
. Take any
a\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Lemma
4.6
implies that

\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}H(a)=\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}\Bigl(\sum_{i\leq d}H_{i}a^{i}\Bigr)\geq\min\bigl\{\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}H_{i}+i\cdot\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}a\;|\;i\leq d\bigr\}>M\geq\\
\geq d\cdot\kras F+\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F_{d}+\frac{ep}{(p-1)}>\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F(a)+\frac{ep}{(p-1)}.

Consequently,
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}G(a)=\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F(a)
for all
a\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Hence,
G(a)\in F(a)\cdot K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times p}=K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times p}
by Corollary
3.2
. Thus, we have
G\in\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}})
.

Consider now the reciprocal polynomial of
G
. We have

\rev G=\rev F+\rev H,

and the same arguments as above (except that this time we use
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F_{0}
instead of
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}F_{d}
) show that
\rev G\in\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}})
. By Proposition
3.7
, we conclude that
G\in\mathscr{C}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})
.
∎

As in the real case, the assumption that
F
has no roots in
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
is indispensable.
Indeed, the polynomial
F(x)=x^{p}
belongs to
\mathscr{C}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})
, yet it can be approximated by polynomials of the form
G(x)=(x-\xi_{1})\dotsm(x-\xi_{p})
with
\xi_{i}
sufficiently close to zero. It is obvious that one can choose
\xi_{1},\dotsc,\xi_{p}
in such a way that
G
does not lie in
\mathscr{C}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})
.

Remark 5.4
.

In particular, Proposition
5.3
implies that the monoid
\mathscr{C}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})
is considerably larger than
\mathbb{Z}_{K}[x]\cap K_{\mathfrak{p}}[x]^{p}
.

Finally, to complete the picture, we show that polynomials from
\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{K})
can be approximated over
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
by
p
-th powers of polynomials. However, polynomials from
\mathscr{C}(K)
cannot be approximated this way on the whole
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
unless they are already
p
-th powers themselves.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
F\in\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{K})
. Then for every positive integer
n
, there is a polynomial
G\in\mathbb{Z}_{K}[x]
such that

\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(F-G^{p})(a)\geq n

for all
a\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

One possible way to prove this proposition is to take a continuous function
\Gamma:\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}\to K_{\mathfrak{p}}
such that

F(a)=\Gamma(a)^{p}

for all
a\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Then apply Mahler’s theorem (see, e.g.,
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
) to approximate
\Gamma
by a polynomial
G
. Alternatively, there is a short and direct proof, which we present below.

Proof.

Fix
n\geq 0
and consider the quotient ring
R_{n}:=\mathbb{Z}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}^{n}
. Let
\overline{F}\in R_{n}[x]
be the image of
F
under the canonical epimorphism
\mathbb{Z}_{K}[x]\twoheadrightarrow R_{n}[x]
. By assumption,
F
belongs to
\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{K})
. Hence, for every
a\in R_{n}
there exists
b\in R_{n}
such that
\overline{F}(a)=b^{p}
. The function mapping
a
to
b
is polynomial by
[
8
]
. Thus, there is
\overline{G}\in R_{n}[x]
such that
\overline{F}-\overline{G}^{p}
vanishes on the entire ring
R_{n}
. Lift
\overline{G}
to a polynomial
G\in\mathbb{Z}_{K}[x]
. Then
(F-G^{p})(a)\in\mathfrak{p}^{n}
for every
a\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, which concludes the proof.
∎

It goes without saying that the above approximation of
F
by a
p
-th power of another polynomial is possible only over
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
(or other compact subsets), but not on the whole field
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
. IThis follows from the well-known fact that any polynomial bounded on an entire local field must be constant. Indeed, fix a polynomial
F\in\mathscr{C}(K)
and an integer
n
. Suppose that there is some polynomial
G\in\mathbb{Z}_{K}[x]
such that

(4)

\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(F-G^{p})(a)\geq n

for all
a\in K_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Set
H:=F-G^{p}
. If
H
is not constant, say
H=H_{0}+H_{1}x+\dotsb+H_{d}x^{d}
with
H_{d}\neq 0
and
d>0
, choose any
a\in K_{\mathfrak{p}}
satisfying

\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}a<\frac{1}{d+1}\cdot\bigl(\min\{n,\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}H_{0},\dotsc,\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}H_{d-1}\}-\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}H_{d}\bigr).

Then
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}H(a)=\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}H_{d}a^{d}<0
, contradicting (
4
).

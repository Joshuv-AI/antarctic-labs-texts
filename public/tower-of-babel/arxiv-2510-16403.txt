Applications of optimal error bounds for some generalized two-step iterative processes in Banach spaces
Tan-Phuc Nguyen, Thai-Hung Nguyen, Tien-Khai Nguyen, Cong-Duy-Nguyen Nguyen, Trung-Hieu Huynh

Abstract
In a recent paper [ 13 ] , we proposed the concept of optimal error bounds for an iterative process, which allows us to obtain the convergence result of the iterative sequence to the common fixed point of the nonexpansive mappings in Banach spaces. Moreover, we also achieve the comparison results between different iterative processes via optimal error bounds. In this paper, we continue to determine optimal error bounds for more general iterative processes which were studied by many authors, such as in [ 9 ] and references therein. From there, the convergence results are obtained and the convergence rates of these iterative processes are determined under some sufficient conditions on sequences of parameters. Keywords: Fixed point theory; ( κ , α ) (\kappa,\alpha) -nonexpansive mappings; Generalized iterative processes; Optimal error bounds; Convergence rate.

Introduction

Many mathematical models can lead to the problem of finding fixed points for a mapping. Finding such points is crucial in various areas of mathematics, as they often represent stable states or equilibrium solutions in dynamic systems. Fixed point problems also have applications across many fields both within and beyond mathematics, such as approximation theory, optimization theory, game theory, finance, machine learning, …One of the most fundamental results of this theory is the Banach fixed point theorem, which asserts the existence of the unique fixed point of a contraction mapping
T:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{R}
, where
(\mathbb{X},\lVert\cdot\rVert)
is a Banach space in
\mathbb{R}
. Sometimes, finding the exact fixed point is not feasible, so methods to approximate it are sought. This fixed point can be approximated by using the following Picard iteration

\displaystyle x_{0}\in\mathbb{X},\ x_{n+1}=T(x_{n}),\hskip 11.74988ptn\in\mathbb{N}.

(1.1)

There are many ideas for constructing the iteration such that the limit of the iteration is the fixed point of the mapping under consideration. These methods aim to enhance the applicability while simultaneously increasing the convergence rate of the iteration to the fixed point. For example, in the case where
T
is not a contraction mapping, the convergence of the iterative sequence (
1.1
) to the fixed point of
T
is not guaranteed. One of the earliest methods to overcome this difficulty is the iterative sequence proposed by Mann in
[
7
]
, defined by

\displaystyle x_{0}\in\mathbb{X},\ x_{n+1}=(1-a_{n})x_{n}+a_{n}T(x_{n}),\hskip 11.74988ptn\in\mathbb{N}.

(1.2)

where
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset[0,1]
is a parameter sequence satisfying

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}a_{n}=0\ \mbox{ and }\ \displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}{a_{n}}=+\infty.

If the sequence
(a_{n})
is a constant sequence, that is
a_{n}=\theta\in[0,1]
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the Mann iteration (
1.2
) is refered to as a Krasnoselskij iteration. With appropriate assumptions about the sequence
(a_{n})
, we can prove the convergence of the Mann iteration (
1.2
) to the fixed point
x^{*}
of the mapping
T
. A substantial amount of research has been devoted to studying the convergence of Mann iterations for various types of nonlinear mappings across different function spaces. However, it has been shown that the Mann iteration converges to the fixed point more slowly than the Picard iteration under many different assumptions regarding the mapping
T
(cf.
[
11
]
). Consequently, many mathematicians have proposed two or three-step iterations to improve the existing iterations (cf.
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
). One of the typical two-step iterations that has been proposed is the Ishikawa iteration in
[
5
]

\displaystyle\begin{cases}x_{0}\in\mathbb{X},\\
y_{n}=(1-a_{n})x_{n}+a_{n}T(x_{n}),\\
x_{n+1}=(1-b_{n})x_{n}+b_{n}T(y_{n}),\hskip 11.74988ptn\in\mathbb{N},\end{cases}

(1.3)

where
(a_{n}),(b_{n})
are two real sequences in
[0,1]
. Many different ideas have been proposed
to improve the Ishikawa iterative sequence. To be more precise, in
[
12
,
15
]
, an iterative process was defined as follows

\displaystyle\begin{cases}x_{0}\in\mathbb{X},\\
y_{n}=(1-a_{n})x_{n}+a_{n}T_{1}(x_{n}),\\
x_{n+1}=(1-b_{n})x_{n}+b_{n}T_{2}(y_{n}),\hskip 11.74988ptn\in\mathbb{N},\end{cases}

(1.4)

where
T_{1},T_{2}
are non-expansive mappings. It can be seen that the iteration sequence (
1.3
) is a specific case of (
1.4
) when
T_{1}=T_{2}=T
. Many authors developed conditions for the parameter sequences
(a_{n}),(b_{n})
in (
1.4
) to receive interesting convergence results to the common fixed point of
T_{1},T_{2}
. Moreover, over the years, many other iterative sequences also have been studied and improved to achieve small errors and higher rates of convergence to the common fixed point (cf.
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
8
]
-
[
10
]
,
[
14
]
).

Recently, in
[
13
]
we proposed the concept of
optimal error bounds
to investigate the convergence of multi-step iterative sequences to a common fixed point of two given non-expansive mappings
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
. In detail, we used
optimal error bounds
to construct necessary and sufficient conditions for parameter sequences in the corresponding two-step Ishikawa iterative sequences to converge, and we compared the convergence rates between them. This approach is entirely new compared to previous research, based on the idea that there is no better bound without additional assumptions regarding the mappings, even in the general case. We find that this iterative method can be extended to multi-step iterative sequences converging to a common fixed point of multiple non-expansive mappings. Inspired by this, our purpose in this paper is using
optimal error bounds
to analyze the convergence and rate of convergence of more general iterative sequences in
[
9
]
and some other references. Specifically, we also establish necessary and sufficient conditions of
optimal error bounds
for some generalized two-step iterative sequences to converge to a common fixed point of two or more non-expansive mappings, and then compare the convergence rates between the considered sequences. Thereby, we confirm the potential of the concept
optimal error bounds
in studying the convergence and convergence rates of the iterative methods to the fixed point of some given non-expansive mappings.

Convergence of iterative sequences is also presented in this section as the first application of optimal error bounds. The next application of optimal error bounds is the comparison result of convergence rates of iterative sequences. These results are presented in the last section.

2
Some generalized two-step processes

First, we introduce the definition of optimal error bounds for a generalized iterative sequence and the iterative sequences examined in this paper. It is worth noting that the optimal error bounds definition we will present below is a generalization of the one found in
[
13
]
. Throughout this article, we always assume
\mathbb{X}
is a Banach space in
\mathbb{R}
and
\Omega
is a closed convex subset in
\mathbb{X}
.

Definition 2.1
(
(\kappa,\alpha)
-nonexpansive mapping)

Given
0\leq\kappa\leq\alpha\leq 1
, a mapping
T:\Omega\to\Omega
is called
(\kappa,\alpha)
-nonexpansive if

\displaystyle\kappa\|\xi-\eta\|\leq\|T(\xi)-T(\eta)\|\leq\alpha\|\xi-\eta\|,

for every
\xi,\eta\in\Omega
. In this case, we write
T\in\mathcal{N}_{\kappa,\alpha}(\Omega)
. If
\alpha<1
, we say that
T
is
(\kappa,\alpha)
-contraction, and write
T\in\mathcal{C}_{\kappa,\alpha}(\Omega)
. Moreover, if
\kappa=0
then we simply write
T\in\mathcal{N}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
or
T\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
provided
\alpha<1
.

With these notations, it is possible to check that

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\kappa,\alpha}(\Omega)\subset\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{N}_{\kappa,\alpha}(\Omega),\mbox{ and }\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\cup\mathcal{N}_{\kappa,\alpha}(\Omega)\subset\mathcal{N}_{\alpha}(\Omega).

Definition 2.2
(Abstract iterative process)

Let
K\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
2K
parameters
\kappa_{k},\alpha_{k}\in[0,1]
for
k=1,2,...,K
. An abstract iterative process
(\mathcal{P})
can be defined as follows: for nonexpansive mappings
T_{k}\in\mathcal{N}_{\kappa_{k},\alpha_{k}}(\Omega)
satisfying
T_{k}(x^{*})=x^{*}
, we define

\displaystyle x_{0}\in\Omega,\ x_{n+1}=\mathcal{P}_{T_{k}}(x_{n}),\hskip 11.74988ptn\in\mathbb{N},

(
\mathcal{P}
)

where
\mathcal{P}_{T_{k}}:\Omega\to\Omega
and
\mathcal{P}_{T_{k}}(x^{*})=x^{*}
.

It is easy to check that if
x_{0}=x^{*}
then
x_{n}=x^{*}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For his reason, without loss of generality, we may assume that
x_{0}\neq x^{*}
.

Definition 2.3
(Optimal error bounds)

Assume that
(\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\mathcal{P}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\mathcal{U}_{n}^{\mathcal{P}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are two non-negative sequences such that the following inequality

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\mathcal{P}}\leq\frac{\|x_{n+1}-x^{*}\|}{\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|}\leq\mathcal{U}_{n}^{\mathcal{P}},\hskip 11.74988pt\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

(1)

holds for any nonexpansive mappings
T_{k}\in\mathcal{N}_{\kappa_{k},\alpha_{k}}(\Omega)
satisfying
T_{k}(x^{*})=x^{*}
. We say that the sequence
(\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\mathcal{P}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
(
(\mathcal{U}_{n}^{\mathcal{P}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
respectively) is optimal lower error bound (optimal upper error bound respectively) of the iteration (

P

) if the corresponding equality in (
1
) holds for some
T_{k}
.

In this paper, we will establish the optimal error bounds for some two-step models of the same type as
(\mathcal{P})
. More precisely, we consider four generalized models which are presented as below. We always assume that
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x_{0}\in\Omega
is the initial point of each iterative sequence. The improved Ishikawa iteration (I) is defined by:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}y_{n}=(1-a_{n})x_{n}+a_{n}T_{1}(x_{n}),\\
x_{n+1}=(1-b_{n})x_{n}+b_{n}T_{2}(y_{n}).\end{cases}

(I)

We remark that the iteration (
I
) can be rewritten as follows

\displaystyle x_{n+1}=(1-b_{n})x_{n}+b_{n}T_{2}\big((1-a_{n})x_{n}+a_{n}T_{1}(x_{n})\big).

Hence, it can be considered as the abstract iterative process (

P

) associated to the mapping
\mathcal{P}_{T_{1},T_{2}}:\Omega\to\Omega
below

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{T_{1},T_{2}}(\xi)=(1-b_{n})\xi+b_{n}T_{2}\big((1-a_{n})\xi+a_{n}T_{1}(\xi)\big),\hskip 11.74988pt\xi\in\Omega.

In the same spirit, we also consider more general two-step iterations, such as: the modified Ishikawa iterations (IM):

\displaystyle\begin{cases}y_{n}=(1-a_{n})x_{n}+a_{n}T_{1}(x_{n}),\\
x_{n+1}=(1-b_{n})y_{n}+b_{n}T_{2}(y_{n});\end{cases}

(IM)

the generalized Ishikawa iteration (IG):

\displaystyle\begin{cases}y_{n}=(1-a_{n})x_{n}+a_{n}T_{1}(x_{n}),\\
x_{n+1}=(1-b_{n})T_{1}(y_{n})+b_{n}T_{2}(y_{n});\end{cases}

(IG)

and the generalized two-step iteration (G):

\displaystyle\begin{cases}y_{n}=(1-a_{n})S_{1}(x_{n})+a_{n}T_{1}(x_{n}),\\
x_{n+1}=(1-b_{n})S_{2}(y_{n})+b_{n}T_{2}(y_{n}).\end{cases}

(G)

3.1

Let us consider four constants

\kappa_{1}\leq\alpha_{1}

Theorem 3.3

The optimal upper bound of (

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{n}^{IG}:=\displaystyle\prod_{k=0}^{n}[1-(1-\alpha_{1})a_{k}][\alpha_{1}+(\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1})b_{k}],

(4)

Proof.
Since

\displaystyle\|y_{n}-x^{*}\|

\displaystyle=\|(1-a_{n})(x_{n}-x^{*})+a_{n}(T_{1}(x_{n})-T_{1}(x^{*}))\|

\displaystyle\leq(1-a_{n})\|x_{n}-x^{*}\|+a_{n}\|T_{1}(x_{n})-T_{1}(x^{*})\|

\displaystyle\leq(1-a_{n})\|x_{n}-x^{*}\|+\alpha_{1}a_{n}\|x_{n}-x^{*}\|

\displaystyle=[1-(1-\alpha_{1})a_{n}]\|x_{n}-x^{*}\|.

Similarly, since

T_{2}\in\text{Ne}_{\kappa_{2},\alpha_{2}}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\|x_{n+1}-x^{*}\|\leq[1-(1-\alpha_{1})a_{n}][\alpha_{2}+(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})b_{n}]\|x_{n}-x^{*}\|,

(5)

for all

\displaystyle\|x_{n+1}-x^{*}\|\leq\mathcal{U}_{n}^{IG}\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|,

(6)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, which
\mathcal{U}_{n}^{IG}
is defined by (
3

It is not difficult to check that with the open set
\displaystyle\Omega=\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle(\alpha_{1}-1)\sum_{k=0}^{n}

\displaystyle=(\alpha_{1}-1)\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}+(\alpha_{1}-1)\sum_{k=0}^{n}(1-b_{k})+(\alpha_{2}-1)\sum_{k=0}^{n}b_{k},

On the other hand, thanks to Lemma

4
Comparison results

In this section, we apply the optimal error bounds to compare the convergence rate of two iterative processes. Let us consider two mappings
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q}:\Omega\to\Omega
such that
\mathcal{P}(x^{*})=\mathcal{Q}(x^{*})=x^{*}
. With an initial value
x_{0}=u_{0}\in\Omega
, we define two iterative sequences

\displaystyle x_{n+1}=\mathcal{P}(x_{n}),\ \mbox{ and }\ u_{n+1}=\mathcal{Q}(u_{n}),\hskip 11.74988ptn\in\mathbb{N}.

We further assume that both sequences
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converge to
x^{*}
in
\mathbb{X}
as
n
tends to infinity. It is well-known that the convergence rate of the iterative process associated to
\mathcal{P}
can be estimated by the following ratio

I_{n}(\mathcal{P}):=\frac{\|x_{n}-x^{*}\|}{\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|},\hskip 11.74988ptn\in\mathbb{N}.

In order to compare the convergence speed of two iterative processes, we simply compare two convergence rates. More precisely, we define a new ratio

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(x_{n},u_{n},x^{*}):=\frac{I_{n}(\mathcal{P})}{I_{n}(\mathcal{Q})}=\frac{\|x_{n}-x^{*}\|}{\|u_{n}-x^{*}\|},\hskip 11.74988ptn\in\mathbb{N}.

(14)

For the validation of
\mathcal{R}
in some singular cases, we set
\mathcal{R}(x_{n},u_{n},x^{*})=0
if
x_{n}=u_{n}=x^{*}
and
\mathcal{R}(x_{n},u_{n},x^{*})=1
if
u_{n}=x^{*}
but
x_{n}\neq x^{*}
.

Definition 4.1
(Comparison of convergence rates)

We say that the iterative process associated to
\mathcal{P}
converges faster the iterative process associated to
\mathcal{Q}
if and only if

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{R}(x_{n},u_{n},x^{*})=0.

For simplicity, we say that
x_{n}
converges to
x^{*}
faster than
u_{n}
.

x^{*}\in\mathcal{F}(T_{1},T_{2},S_{1},S_{2})
.
Then the sequence
(x_{n}^{G})
converges to
x^{*}
faster than
(x_{n}^{IG})
, provided

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\dfrac{1-\beta_{1}}{1-\beta_{1}+2\alpha_{1}}-a_{n}\right)+\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\dfrac{\kappa_{1}-\beta_{2}}{\kappa_{1}-\beta_{2}+2\alpha_{2}}-b_{n}\right)=+\infty.

(26)

Proof.
By using the formulas of optimal upper bound of (
G
) and optimal lower bound of (
IG
), we have the estimate below:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(x_{n}^{G},x_{n}^{IG},x^{*})\leq\prod_{k=0}^{n}\dfrac{\left[\beta_{1}-(\beta_{1}-\alpha_{1})a_{k}\right]\left[\beta_{2}-(\beta_{2}-\alpha_{2})b_{k}\right]}{\left[1-(1+\alpha_{1})a_{k}\right]\left[\kappa_{1}-(\kappa_{1}+\alpha_{2})b_{k}\right]}=\prod_{k=0}^{n}a^{*}_{k}b^{*}_{k},

(27)

where

\displaystyle a^{*}_{n}=\frac{\beta_{1}-(\beta_{1}-\alpha_{1})a_{n}}{1-(1+\alpha_{1})a_{n}},\hskip 11.74988ptb^{*}_{n}=\dfrac{\beta_{1}-(\beta_{1}-\alpha_{1})a_{n}}{\kappa_{1}-(\kappa_{1}+\alpha_{2})b_{n}},

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The conditions in (
25
) guarantee that

\displaystyle 1-(1+\alpha_{1})a_{n}\geq\beta_{1}-(\beta_{1}-\alpha_{1})a_{n}>0,

and

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}-(\kappa_{1}+\alpha_{2})b_{n}\geq\beta_{2}-(\beta_{2}-\alpha_{2})b_{n}>0.

This ensures two sequences
(a^{*}_{n})
and
(b^{*}_{n})
are in
(0,1]
.
On the other hand,

\displaystyle\ln\prod_{k=0}^{n}a^{*}_{k}b^{*}_{k}=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\ln(a^{*}_{k}b^{*}_{k})=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\ln a^{*}_{k}+\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\ln b^{*}_{k}.

(28)

Since
(a^{*}_{n}),(b^{*}_{n})\subset(0,1]
, we have
\ln a^{*}_{k}\leq 0
,
\ln b^{*}_{k}\leq 0
, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore, from (
28
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\ln\prod_{k=0}^{n}a^{*}_{k}b^{*}_{k}=-\infty

\displaystyle\iff\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\ln a^{*}_{k}=-\infty\text{ or }\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\ln b^{*}_{k}=-\infty,

which means

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\prod_{k=0}^{n}a^{*}_{k}b^{*}_{k}=0\iff\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\prod_{k=0}^{n}a^{*}_{k}=0\text{ or }\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\prod_{k=0}^{n}b^{*}_{k}=0.

(29)

With the similar techniques as in the proof of Theorem
4.2
and
4.3
, we can show that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\prod_{k=0}^{n}a^{*}_{k}=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\prod_{k=0}^{n}\dfrac{\beta_{1}-(\beta_{1}-\alpha_{1})a_{k}}{1-(1+\alpha_{1})a_{k}}=0\Leftrightarrow\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\dfrac{1-\beta_{1}}{1-\beta_{1}+2\alpha_{1}}-a_{n}\right)=+\infty,

(30)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\prod_{k=0}^{n}b^{*}_{k}=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\prod_{k=0}^{n}\dfrac{\beta_{2}-(\beta_{2}-\alpha_{2})b_{k}}{\kappa_{1}-(\kappa_{1}+\alpha_{2})b_{k}}=0\Leftrightarrow\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\dfrac{\kappa_{1}-\beta_{2}}{\kappa_{1}-\beta_{2}+2\alpha_{2}}-b_{n}\right)=+\infty.

(31)

Taking into account all estimates in (
27
)-(
31
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{R}(x_{n}^{G},x_{n}^{IG},x^{*})=0,

if the condition in (
26
) is valid. As a result, the iteration (G) converges faster than (IG).

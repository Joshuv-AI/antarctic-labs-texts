Establishment of the Entrustable Professional Activities for the Residency Program in Stomatology: A Delphi Consensus Study
Authors: Li Zhang, Qi Gao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo establish the entrustable professional activities (EPAs) for stomatological residents.MethodsResearch experts were recruited from professors of stomatology and medical education. The EPAs index was designed based on a scoping review and clinical practice on the basis of the requirements of resident training program. The Delphi method was used to conduct two rounds of expert consultations among 30 experts over the period from Jan 2023 to Sep 2023 to confirm the EPAs index for stomatological residents.ResultsTwo rounds of consulting experts positive coefficient were 100% and the authority coefficient Kendall 0.319 and 0.339, respectively (P < 0.05). The establishment index of EPAs for stomatological residents comprised 16 indices, in which the whole average scores of importance, familiarity, observability, authenticity, universality and competencies were greater than 3.5 and the variable coefficient were all less than 0.25. Meanwhile, the expected entrustable levels of each indicator in different study stages were elucidated.ConclusionThe index of EPAs were preliminary constructed for stomatological residents; the details are preliminary consensus-based and require further validation to inform future competency-based assessment of stomatological residents.

Introduction

The cultivation of good professional ethics, solid theoretical knowledge, and clinical professional skills in general dentistry residents is a current primary task of standardized training for general dentistry residents as a main component of postgraduate medical education, aiming to ensure the quality of such training and improve the core competency of six key positions. Entrustable Professional Activities (EPAs), referring to professional practice activities, are tasks or responsibilities that trainees can be fully trusted to complete without supervision or guidance once they have sufficient competency.1 Proposed by Professor Olle ten Cate, a leading medical education expert from the Netherlands, in 2005, EPAs serve as a novel method for evaluating competency-based medical education (CBME).2 The concreteness of EPA evaluation breaks through the bottleneck of difficulty in evaluating competency, with simple and understandable evaluation criteria. This not only facilitates acceptance by both residents and teaching supervisors but also addresses the previous deficiency caused by complex evaluation models, insufficient feasibility, or effectiveness, which hindered the assessment of residents’ clinical competency. Furthermore, through a tiered model, EPAs clearly outline the growth trajectory of residents, providing theoretical support for the personalized improvement of clinical competency among general dentistry residents.3

Subjects and Methods

Research Objects

A total of 30 experts were consulted, all of whom were from the Department of Stomatology of tertiary A-grade comprehensive hospitals in Guangdong Province or stomatology specialty hospitals, medical education, or management fields. Inclusion criteria: ①Postgraduate education; ②Engaged in clinical work in the Department of Stomatology with intermediate or higher professional titles; ③ Engaged in general stomatology teaching work for more than 5 years. All experts fully understood the purpose of this study and signed informed consent forms. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Shenzhen Children’s Hospital (Approval No. 2024098). The geographic concentration of experts in Guangdong Province may limit the generalizability of findings to broader stomatology residency programs.

Screening Criteria for Study Indicators

Both the importance and feasibility scores were >3.5, and the coefficient of variation (Cv) <0.25. The level of expert authority was evaluated using the authority coefficient (Cr), calculated as Cr = (judgment coefficient + familiarity coefficient) / 2. When Cr > 0.7, the expert authority level was considered reliable. The Kendall coefficient of concordance (W) represented the overall degree of coordination among experts on all indicators. When P < 0.05, it indicated consistency in experts’ scoring of various EPA indicators.

Development of Consultation Questionnaires

Databases such as Medline, CINAHL, Scopus, PsycINFO, Web of Science, China Biology Medicine Literature Database, China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), VIP Chinese Science and Technology Journal Database, and Wanfang with the retrieval time limit set to December 2022 “entrustable professional behavior” and “stomatology education”; English search terms included “entrustable professional activity entrustable professional activities/EPAs” and “stomatology/education”. Literature on EPAs indicators for general stomatology residents was retrieved, analyzed, and EPAs indicators were constructed based on the current status of residency training. Combined with the entrustment level formulated by scholars such as Qi Xin4 on the basis of GME entrustment levels, domestic clinical practice, and the training and assessment content of general stomatology residents at Shenzhen Children’s Hospital, expert discussions and clinical task analysis were conducted. The research team initially drafted 16 core EPAs indicators for general stomatology residents, including patient reception, selection and interpretation of auxiliary examinations, diagnosis and differential diagnosis, treatment planning, medical record writing, case reporting, identification and management of general clinical problems, identification and management of critical and severe conditions, patient transfer and handover, informed consent, basic operations, oral health education, doctor-patient communication, clinical teaching, public health event response, emergency rescue for aspiration and choking, and determination of confidence at different nodes.

Expert Consultation

This study adopted the Delphi method, including two rounds of expert questionnaires and one expert consultation meeting. After the first round of questionnaires, opinions or questions raised by experts were sorted and modified to form the second-round expert questionnaire. After Expert anonymity was maintained throughout the Delphi process; individual responses were not disclosed to other panel members. Between rounds, aggregated feedback, including mean scores, coefficients of variation, and anonymized qualitative comments, was provided to each expert. Receiving the second-round expert questionnaires, experts’ opinions were summarised, discussed, and organized again. When experts’ opinions tended to be consistent, the consultation was concluded, and the correspondence between the constructed EPAs framework and the target competency framework was tested.

After obtaining expert informed consent, the first-round questionnaire was conducted via Email from January to April 2023. Experts scored the importance of EPA indicator titles and content descriptions (Figure 1). If experts rated the importance as 3 or below, modification suggestions were required in the “Modification Opinions” column. The second-round expert questionnaire was conducted from July to September 2023, also via email, requiring experts to score importance and determine the confidence level at graduation assessment, as well as evaluate quality aspects such as importance, familiarity, observability, authenticity, universality, and multiple competencies. Statistical Analysis

Data entry and organization were performed using Excel, and data statistical processing was conducted with SPSS 29.0. For the evaluation of each indicator and importance of EPAs, continuous variables were handled, expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x±s). Counting data that did not conform to a normal distribution were presented as median (interquartile range) [M(Q1, Q3)]. The authority level (Cr) of experts depended on their academic level, the basis for their judgments, and their familiarity with the consulted items. An authority coefficient Cr > 0.7 was considered reliable.5 The coordination coefficient of expert opinions was tested using Kendall’s W value to reflect the consistency of experts’ ratings of each EPA indicator.6

Basic Information of Experts

A total of 30 experts participated in the expert questionnaire consultation in this study, including 20 oral medicine experts (66.7%), and 10 medical education or management experts (33.3%); 18 female experts (60%) and 12 male experts (40%); 24 experts with associate senior or higher professional titles (80%), and 6 experts with intermediate professional titles (20%); 20 doctoral students (66.7%) and 10 master’s degree students (33.3%). They not only have high professional level, but also clearly understand the requirements of residency training and CBME.

Expected Confidence Level of EPAs Indicators for Oral General Practice Residency Physicians

The confidence professional behavior grading standard is from 1 to 5 levels, as follows: Level 1 (Novice): only possesses the required knowledge, but cannot complete the relevant tasks even under direct supervision; Level 2 (Advanced Novice): completes the relevant tasks under full direct and active supervision; Level 3 (Competent): completes the relevant tasks under moderate supervision (passive supervision, such as when the learner makes a request and the supervisor resolves it); Level 4 (Proficient): completes the relevant tasks independently [under supervision from a distance and/or post-hoc analysis]; Level 5 (Expert): plays the role of supervisor and mentor. Discussion

The members of the research team and experts involved in the prediction and grading of this study are general dentists and professional teachers in medical education. Among them, 2/3 of the teachers have a doctoral degree, and 4/5 hold senior professional titles. They possess rich experience in clinical practice and teaching work and have been involved in the suggesting preliminary expert consensus on the core indicators. Assessment has a guiding role in training. A relatively complete assessment system helps clarify the goals and requirements of residency training. Guiding resident physicians strictly according to the requirements and standards of assessment helps standardize and cultivate the comprehensive clinical competence of resident physicians.7 This study constructs the core indicators of EPAs based on China’s actual medical situation, the real operation status of hospital general dentistry departments, and the requirements of residency training. Patient reception, selection and interpretation of auxiliary examinations, diagnosis and differential diagnosis, treatment planning, medical record writing, identification and handling of general clinical problems, informed consent, oral health education, and doctor-patient communication almost occur in daily clinical scenarios and are indispensable important clinical competencies. Indicators such as clinical skills, diagnostic ability, and treatment plan design can assess resident physicians’ understanding and mastery of oral diseases, as well as their application ability in clinical practice. Basic operations are an important link in the training of oral resident physicians. As the grade of trainees increases, the difficulty of skill operations gradually increases. Whether theoretical knowledge, laboratory teaching, and skill operations can be naturally combined and integrated is an important indicator for evaluating general dental resident physicians. The core indicators include items related to clinical decision-making and problem handling, as well as emergency rescue capabilities, which can assess resident physicians’ decision-making level, problem-solving ability, and clinical adaptability when facing problems of different difficulties in clinical work. Doctor-patient communication covers aspects such as care for patients, understanding and satisfaction of patient needs, and the ability to provide high-quality medical services, which can assess the resident physicians’ attention to patients and emphasis on service quality. It is worth mentioning that doctor-patient communication skills are essential job competencies for resident physicians. Good doctor-patient communication plays a very important role in improving the effectiveness of diagnosis and treatment services and patient satisfaction. In 2022, the Accreditation Council for Graduate Medical Education (ACGME) of the United States included “Interpersonal Communication and Communication Skills and Professionalism” in the six core professional competencies of resident physicians. The standardization of residency training in China started relatively late. In 2009, the State Council issued the “Opinions of the Central Committee of the Communist Party of China and the State Council on Deepening the Reform of the Medical and Health System” which explicitly stipulated the establishment of a standardized residency training system and the construction of a healthy and harmonious doctor–patient relationship.8 Additionally, response to public health emergencies, as well as patient transfer and handover, are also aimed at cultivating basic competencies in resident physicians. These can assess the emergency response capabilities of oral general residents in public health emergencies, including their ability to conduct risk assessment of epidemic transmission, make rapid decisions and take actions, as well as coordinate team collaboration and communicate effectively. Through the evaluation of teaching abilities, the continuous learning capacity and lifelong learning awareness of oral general residents are examined. Therefore, the 16 core indicators of EPAs constructed in this study can comprehensively evaluate the performance and capabilities of oral general residents in professional behavior, provide guidance and support for their all-round development and improvement, and enable them to become oral physicians who can independently and standardizedly undertake the diagnosis and treatment of common and frequent diseases in their specialty upon completion of training.

The 16 core indicators were designed to reflect clinical scenarios with adequate observability.9 Firstly, these indicators help physicians improve the efficiency and quality of clinical work. Through evaluation of indicators related to patient reception, selection and interpretation of auxiliary examinations, diagnosis and differential diagnosis, treatment planning, etc, they assist resident physicians in systematically enhancing the accuracy and professionalism of medical services. In addition, the construction of these indicators can help resident physicians respond more calmly and professionally when dealing with critical conditions and medical accidents, enabling them to comprehensively improve their clinical capabilities and enhance the safety and reliability of medical services. The indicators also reflect physicians’ teaching and guiding abilities in clinical practice, which is conducive to cultivating physicians with good teaching literacy and promoting the sustainable optimization and development of medical education and training systems. (help cultivate physicians with good teaching literacy and promote the sustainable optimization and development of medical education and training systems).

Clinical teaching needs to revolve around the problems existing in resident physicians’ practice in oral clinical settings, with content including clinical knowledge, evidence-based basis, and practical experience. According to training needs and audience characteristics, the pertinence of teaching implementation and effect evaluation should be improved. General dental procedures belong to a highly practical specialty. Due to the small oral space of patients and excessive saliva secretion, during operations, unexpected dislodgement of teeth, burs, etc, into the digestive tract or respiratory tract may lead to serious consequences or even endanger life, which may constitute medical accidents of different levels according to the “Grading Standards for Medical Accidents.” How to correctly judge the urgency and select different disposal methods is something resident physicians need to consider. Therefore, the prevention and emergency disposal procedures for accidental ingestion and aspiration will run through the entire standardized training system, improving resident physicians’ emergency handling capabilities during diagnosis and treatment and reducing the risks caused by accidental ingestion and aspiration. For critically ill patients, resident physicians need to learn to identify critical values and handle them, master the correct diagnostic thinking and decision-making for first aid, and implement targeted treatment as soon as possible within the “time window.” The items designed in this study all reflect that the core EPA indicators for general dental residents have good pertinence and practicality, and the confidence level required for specialist physicians at graduation in this study is consistent with real clinical practice.

Core EPAs can serve as a crucial basis for designing training programs for general dental residents. By establishing clear indicators, training objectives and requirements can be explicitly defined, enabling the development of more systematic and effective curricula. This contributes to establishing a professionally recognized and standardized system within the field of general dentistry, thereby promoting the advancement of dental medical education and clinical practice.

Through quantitative and qualitative evaluation methods, the strengths and weaknesses of residents can be promptly identified, allowing for personalized guidance and training. This facilitates the enhancement of their professional competence and clinical skills, thereby providing an evaluation and feedback mechanism for the professional behaviors of general dental residents. As an important tool for quality control and improvement in general dental medical education and clinical practice, monitoring the achievement of core EPAs can help identify existing problems and bottlenecks in the educational and practical processes. Corresponding measures can then be taken to effect improvements, thereby enhancing training quality and clinical service levels.

The establishment and implementation of core EPAs form the foundation for the continuous development and learning of general dentists. By continuously monitoring and evaluating the realization of these core indicators, residents can persistently enhance their professional proficiency and capabilities throughout their careers, adapting to developments and changes in the medical field.

Therefore, the design and implementation of core confidence-based professional behavior indicators for general dental residents hold significant importance for general dental medical education and clinical practice. They can effectively improve residents’ professional competence and clinical skills, driving progress and development in the field of dentistry.

Limitation

The standardized training of general dental residents aims to train dentists to independently implement high-level standardized diagnosis and treatment in medical practice. The researchers in this study were primarily teaching administrators and clinical instructors from Shenzhen Children’s Hospital, which is a unit designated for the construction of high-level hospitals in Guangdong Province. The hospital possesses an excellent foundation for medical teaching in terms of physician qualifications and teaching management. The core confidence-based professional behavior items and confidence levels in this study have been recognized by senior experts in multiple fields of oral clinical practice and medical education. However, given that medical education has not yet achieved homogenization across regions and there are imbalances in teaching practices among hospitals, the research findings may not be fully applicable to other institutions. Future improvements to the study could further expand the applicability of the EPA core indicator evaluation, adjust the confidence levels for general dental residents at graduation in different regions or hospital tiers, promote the cultivation of general dentists, and enhance practicality.

On singularity of p p -energy measures on metric measure spaces
Meng Yang

Abstract
For p > 1 p>1 , we prove that, for a p p -energy on a volume doubling metric measure space, the Poincaré inequality and the cutoff Sobolev inequality, both with p p -walk dimension strictly larger than p p , imply that the associated p p -energy measure is singular with respect to the underlying measure. Under the slow volume regularity condition, we further prove that these two inequalities are equivalent to the resistance estimate; in particular, as part of the proof, we give a simple and direct derivation of the cutoff Sobolev inequality from the Poincaré inequality and the capacity upper bound. As a direct corollary, for a large family of fractals and metric measure spaces, including the Sierpiński gasket and the Sierpiński carpet, the p p -energy measure is singular with respect to the underlying measure for any p p strictly greater than the Ahlfors regular conformal dimension.

Introduction

On a large family of fractals, including the Sierpiński gasket and the Sierpiński carpet, there exists a diffusion with a heat kernel satisfying the following two-sided sub-Gaussian estimate

\displaystyle\frac{C_{1}}{V(x,t^{1/\beta})}\exp\left(-C_{2}\left(\frac{d(x,y)}{t^{1/\beta}}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}\right)\leq p_{t}(x,y)\leq\frac{C_{3}}{V(x,t^{1/\beta})}\exp\left(-C_{4}\left(\frac{d(x,y)}{t^{1/\beta}}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}\right),

By the standard Dirichlet form theory, a diffusion corresponds to a local regular Dirichlet form associated with an energy measure, see

Conjecture:
For a

The main motivation of this paper is to make progress toward this conjecture. Recall that in the case

The problem reduces to proving the Poincaré inequality and the cutoff Sobolev inequality on certain fractals or metric measure spaces. The Poincaré inequality is relatively straightforward. Indeed, on many such spaces, it arises naturally as a byproduct of the construction of the
p
-energy. The main difficulty lies in the cutoff Sobolev inequality. Even in the case
p=2

Recently, in
[
47

, the author gave a direct proof of the cutoff Sobolev inequality for
p=2
on the Sierpiński gasket. In the present paper, we adopt the same approach to show that, under the slow volume regularity condition, which generalizes the strongly recurrent condition from the case

to general

and is equivalent to

being strictly greater than the Ahlfors regular conformal dimension on homogeneous metric measure spaces, the Poincaré inequality and the cutoff Sobolev inequality are equivalent to the resistance estimate. As part of the proof, we give a simple and direct derivation of the cutoff Sobolev inequality from the Poincaré inequality and the capacity upper bound. A similar derivation was very recently obtained in
[
18
]
under the more general assumption of volume doubling, using a Whitney covering technique. Our proof is direct and may be of independent interest.

Since the resistance estimate has been established on certain homogeneous metric measure spaces, as shown in
[
33
,
28
]

6
Some results from potential theory

In this section, we give some necessary results from potential theory.

Lemma 6.1
.

For any
f,g\in\mathcal{F}\cap L^{\infty}(X;m)
, for any
A\in\mathcal{B}(X)
, we have

\Gamma(fg)(A)\leq 2^{p-1}\left(\int_{A}|\widetilde{f}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\Gamma(g)+\int_{A}|\widetilde{g}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\Gamma(f)\right).

(6.1)

Proof.

Firstly, we prove for
f,g\in\mathcal{F}\cap C_{c}(X)
. For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist finitely many disjoint Borel sets
\{B_{n}:n=1,\ldots,N\}
such that
\mathrm{supp}(f)\cup\mathrm{supp}(g)\subseteq\cup_{n=1}^{N}B_{n}
and
|f(x)-f(y)|<\varepsilon
,
|g(x)-g(y)|<\varepsilon
for any
x,y\in B_{n}
, for any
n=1,\ldots,N
. For any
n
, by
Alg
for
\Gamma(\cdot)(A\cap B_{n})
, we have

\Gamma(fg)(A\cap B_{n})^{1/p}\leq\lVert{f}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(A\cap B_{n};m)}\Gamma(g)(A\cap B_{n})^{1/p}+\lVert{g}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(A\cap B_{n};m)}\Gamma(f)(A\cap B_{n})^{1/p},

hence

\displaystyle\Gamma(fg)(A\cap B_{n})

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\left(\lVert{f}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(A\cap B_{n};m)}^{p}\Gamma(g)(A\cap B_{n})+\lVert{g}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(A\cap B_{n};m)}^{p}\Gamma(f)(A\cap B_{n})\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\left(\int_{A\cap B_{n}}(|f|+\varepsilon)^{p}\mathrm{d}\Gamma(g)+\int_{A\cap B_{n}}(|g|+\varepsilon)^{p}\mathrm{d}\Gamma(f)\right).

Taking the summation with respect to
B_{n}
, we have

\Gamma(fg)(A)\leq 2^{p-1}\left(\int_{A}(|f|+\varepsilon)^{p}\mathrm{d}\Gamma(g)+\int_{A}(|g|+\varepsilon)^{p}\mathrm{d}\Gamma(f)\right).

Since
f,g\in\mathcal{F}\cap C_{c}(X)
, by the dominated convergence theorem, letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, we have the desired result.

Secondly, we prove for
f\in\mathcal{F}\cap L^{\infty}(X;m)
,
g\in\mathcal{F}\cap C_{c}(X)
. By
[
48
, Proposition 8.5]
, there exists
\{f_{n}\}\subseteq\mathcal{F}\cap C_{c}(X)
which is
\mathcal{E}_{1}
-convergent to
f
and convergent to
\widetilde{f}
q.e. on
X
. By replacing
f_{n}
with
(f_{n}\vee(-\lVert{f}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X;m)}))\wedge\lVert{f}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X;m)}
and applying
[
28
, Corollary 3.19 (b)]
, we may assume that
\lVert{f_{n}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X;m)}\leq\lVert{f}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X;m)}
for any
n
. By
Alg
, we have
\sup_{n}\mathcal{E}(f_{n}g)<+\infty
, since
\{f_{n}g\}
is
L^{p}(X;m)
-convergent to
fg
, by
[
28
, Lemma 3.17]
, we have
\{f_{n}g\}
is
\mathcal{E}_{1}
-weakly-convergent to
fg
. By replacing
\{f_{n}\}
with a sequence of finite convex combinations of
\{f_{n}\}
and applying the Mazur’s lemma, we may assume that
\{f_{n}g\}
is
\mathcal{E}_{1}
-convergent to
fg
. By the first part, we have

\Gamma(f_{n}g)(A)\leq 2^{p-1}\left(\int_{A}|f_{n}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\Gamma(g)+\int_{A}|g|^{p}\mathrm{d}\Gamma(f_{n})\right),

(6.2)

where
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Gamma(f_{n}g)(A)=\Gamma(fg)(A)
. Since
\{|f_{n}|^{p}\}
converges to
|\widetilde{f}|^{p}
q.e. on
X
, by
[
48
, Proposition 8.12]
, we have
\{|f_{n}|^{p}\}
converges to
|\widetilde{f}|^{p}

\Gamma(g)
-a.e. in
X
, by the dominated convergence theorem, we have
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{A}|f_{n}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\Gamma(g)=\int_{A}|\widetilde{f}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\Gamma(g)
. By
[
28
, Proposition 4.9]
, we have
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{A}|g|^{p}\mathrm{d}\Gamma(f_{n})=\int_{A}|g|^{p}\mathrm{d}\Gamma(f)
. Letting
n\to+\infty
in (
6.2
), we have the desired result.

Finally, we prove for
f,g\in\mathcal{F}\cap L^{\infty}(X;m)
. Similar to the proof in the second part, there exists
\{g_{n}\}\subseteq\mathcal{F}\cap C_{c}(X)
with
\lVert{g_{n}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X;m)}\leq\lVert{g}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X;m)}
for any
n
, such that
\{g_{n}\}
is
\mathcal{E}_{1}
-convergent to
g
,
\{g_{n}\}
converges to
\widetilde{g}
q.e. on
X
, and
\{fg_{n}\}
is
\mathcal{E}_{1}
-convergent to
fg
. By the second part, we have

\Gamma(fg_{n})(A)\leq 2^{p-1}\left(\int_{A}|\widetilde{f}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\Gamma(g_{n})+\int_{A}|g_{n}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\Gamma(f)\right),

(6.3)

where
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Gamma(fg_{n})(A)=\Gamma(fg)(A)
. Since
\{|g_{n}|^{p}\}
converges to
|\widetilde{g}|^{p}
q.e. on
X
, by
[
48
, Proposition 8.12]
again, we have
\{|g_{n}|^{p}\}
converges to
|\widetilde{g}|^{p}

\Gamma(f)
-a.e. in
X
, by the dominated convergence theorem, we have
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{A}|g_{n}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\Gamma(f)=\int_{A}|\widetilde{g}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\Gamma(f)
. By
[
28
, Proposition 4.9]
again, we have
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{A}|\widetilde{f}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\Gamma(g_{n})=\int_{A}|\widetilde{f}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\Gamma(g)
. Letting
n\to+\infty
in (
6.3
), we have the desired result.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

For any bounded open set
U
, we have

\mathcal{F}(U)=\left\{u\in\mathcal{F}:\widetilde{u}=0\text{ q.e. on }X\backslash U\right\}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{G}=\left\{u\in\mathcal{F}:\widetilde{u}=0\text{ q.e. on }X\backslash U\right\}
, then by
[
48
, Corollary 8.7]
, we have
\mathcal{G}
is a closed subspace of
(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E}^{1/p}_{1})
. It is obvious that
\mathcal{F}(U)\subseteq\mathcal{G}
. We only need to show that
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{F}(U)
. We proceed as in the proof of
[
13
, Lemma 5.43]
. For notational convenience, we may assume that all functions in
\mathcal{F}
are quasi-continuous.

For any
u\in\mathcal{G}
, by
[
28
, Corollary 3.19 (a)]
, we may assume that
u
is non-negative bounded, that is,
0\leq u\leq M

m
-a.e. in
X
, where
M
is some positive constant. For any
k\geq 1
, there exists an open set
G_{k}
with
\mathrm{cap}_{1}(G_{k})<\frac{1}{k}
such that
u|_{X\backslash G_{k}}
is continuous and
u=0
on
X\backslash(U\cup G_{k})
, see
[
48
, Section 8]
for the definition of
\mathrm{cap}_{1}
. By
[
48
, Lemma 8.2]
, there exists
e_{k}\in\mathcal{F}
with
0\leq e_{k}\leq 1

m
-a.e. in
X
,
e_{k}=1

m
-a.e. on
G_{k}
such that
\mathcal{E}_{1}(e_{k})=\mathrm{cap}_{1}(G_{k})
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, we claim that
\{x\in X\backslash G_{k}:u(x)\geq\varepsilon\}\subseteq U
is compact. Indeed, since
u|_{X\backslash G_{k}}
is continuous, we have
(u|_{X\backslash G_{k}})^{-1}((-\infty,\varepsilon))=\{x\in X\backslash G_{k}:u(x)<\varepsilon\}
is a relatively open subset of
X\backslash G_{k}
, then
G_{k}\cup\{x\in X\backslash G_{k}:u(x)<\varepsilon\}
is an open subset of
X
, which gives
\{x\in X\backslash G_{k}:u(x)\geq\varepsilon\}
is a closed subset of
X
. Since
u=0
on
X\backslash(U\cup G_{k})
, we have
\{x\in X\backslash G_{k}:u(x)\geq\varepsilon\}\subseteq U
. Since
U
is bounded, we have
\{x\in X\backslash G_{k}:u(x)\geq\varepsilon\}
is a bounded closed set, which is also compact due to the fact
(X,d)
is complete locally compact.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, let
u_{\varepsilon}=(u-\varepsilon)\vee 0
, then by
[
28
, Corollary 3.19 (a)]
, we have
u_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{F}
and
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\mathcal{E}_{1}(u_{\varepsilon}-u)=0
. Let
u_{\varepsilon,k}=u_{\varepsilon}-e_{k}u_{\varepsilon}
, then
\mathrm{supp}(u_{\varepsilon,k})\subseteq\{x\in X\backslash G_{k}:u(x)\geq\varepsilon\}
, by the above claim, we have
\mathrm{supp}(u_{\varepsilon,k})\subseteq U
is compact. By
Alg
, we have
u_{\varepsilon,k}\in\mathcal{F}
. By Lemma
6.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(e_{k}u_{\varepsilon})=\Gamma(e_{k}u_{\varepsilon})(X)\leq 2^{p-1}\left(\int_{X}|e_{k}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\Gamma(u_{\varepsilon})+\int_{X}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\Gamma(e_{k})\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\left(\int_{X}|e_{k}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\Gamma(u_{\varepsilon})+M^{p}\mathcal{E}_{1}(e_{k})\right)\leq 2^{p-1}\left(\int_{X}|e_{k}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\Gamma(u_{\varepsilon})+M^{p}\frac{1}{k}\right).

Since
\{e_{k}\}
is
\mathcal{E}_{1}
-convergent to
0
, by
[
48
, Corollary 8.7]
, there exists a subsequence, still denoted by
\{e_{k}\}
, such that
\{e_{k}\}
converges to
0
q.e. on
X
, by
[
48
, Proposition 8.12]
,
\{e_{k}\}
also converges to
0

\Gamma(u_{\varepsilon})
-a.e. in
X
. Since
0\leq e_{k}\leq 1
q.e. on
X
, which is also
\Gamma(u_{\varepsilon})
-a.e. in
X
, by the dominated convergence theorem, we have
\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{X}|e_{k}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\Gamma(u_{\varepsilon})=0
. Hence
\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{E}(e_{k}u_{\varepsilon})=0
. Moreover, since

\int_{X}|e_{k}u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\mathrm{d}m\leq M^{p}\int_{X}|e_{k}|^{p}\mathrm{d}m\leq M^{p}\mathcal{E}_{1}(e_{k})\leq M^{p}\frac{1}{k}\to 0,

we have
\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{E}_{1}(e_{k}u_{\varepsilon})=0
, that is,
\{u_{\varepsilon,k}\}_{k}
is
\mathcal{E}_{1}
-convergent to
u_{\varepsilon}
.

Since
u_{\varepsilon,k}\in\mathcal{F}
satisfies
0\leq u_{\varepsilon,k}\leq M

m
-a.e. in
X
, by
[
28
, Corollary 3.19 (b)]
, there exists
\{v_{\varepsilon,k,n}\}_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{F}\cap C_{c}(X)
with
0\leq v_{\varepsilon,k,n}\leq M
on
X
for any
n
, such that
\{v_{\varepsilon,k,n}\}_{n}
is
\mathcal{E}_{1}
-convergent to
u_{\varepsilon,k}
. Since
\mathrm{supp}(u_{\varepsilon,k})\subseteq U
is compact, there exists
\phi_{\varepsilon,k}\in\mathcal{F}\cap C_{c}(X)
satisfying
0\leq\phi_{\varepsilon,k}\leq 1
on
X
,
\phi_{\varepsilon,k}=1
on
\mathrm{supp}(u_{\varepsilon,k})
and
\mathrm{supp}(\phi_{\varepsilon,k})\subseteq U
, then
\phi_{\varepsilon,k}v_{\varepsilon,k,n}\in\mathcal{F}\cap C_{c}(U)
and
u_{\varepsilon,k}-\phi_{\varepsilon,k}v_{\varepsilon,k,n}=\phi_{\varepsilon,k}(u_{\varepsilon,k}-v_{\varepsilon,k,n})
. Moreover

\displaystyle\int_{X}|u_{\varepsilon,k}-\phi_{\varepsilon,k}v_{\varepsilon,k,n}|^{p}\mathrm{d}m=\int_{X}|\phi_{\varepsilon,k}(u_{\varepsilon,k}-v_{\varepsilon,k,n})|^{p}\mathrm{d}m

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}|u_{\varepsilon,k}-v_{\varepsilon,k,n}|^{p}\mathrm{d}m\leq\mathcal{E}_{1}(u_{\varepsilon,k}-v_{\varepsilon,k,n})\to 0,

and by Lemma
6.1

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u_{\varepsilon,k}-\phi_{\varepsilon,k}v_{\varepsilon,k,n})=\mathcal{E}(\phi_{\varepsilon,k}(u_{\varepsilon,k}-v_{\varepsilon,k,n}))

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\left(\int_{X}|\phi_{\varepsilon,k}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\Gamma(u_{\varepsilon,k}-v_{\varepsilon,k,n})+\int_{X}|u_{\varepsilon,k}-v_{\varepsilon,k,n}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\Gamma(\phi_{\varepsilon,k})\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\left(\int_{X}\mathrm{d}\Gamma(u_{\varepsilon,k}-v_{\varepsilon,k,n})+\int_{X}|u_{\varepsilon,k}-v_{\varepsilon,k,n}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\Gamma(\phi_{\varepsilon,k})\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\left(\mathcal{E}_{1}(u_{\varepsilon,k}-v_{\varepsilon,k,n})+\int_{X}|u_{\varepsilon,k}-v_{\varepsilon,k,n}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\Gamma(\phi_{\varepsilon,k})\right).

Since
\{v_{\varepsilon,k,n}\}_{n}
is
\mathcal{E}_{1}
-convergent to
u_{\varepsilon,k}
, by
[
48
, Corollary 8.7]
, there exists a subsequence, still denoted by
\{v_{\varepsilon,k,n}\}_{n}
, such that
\{v_{\varepsilon,k,n}\}_{n}
converges to
u_{\varepsilon,k}
q.e. on
X
. By
[
48
, Proposition 8.12]
, we have
\{v_{\varepsilon,k,n}\}_{n}
converges to
u_{\varepsilon,k}

\Gamma(\phi_{\varepsilon,k})
-a.e. in
X
. Since
0\leq u_{\varepsilon,k}\leq M
q.e. on
X
, which is also
\Gamma(\phi_{\varepsilon,k})
-a.e. in
X
, and
v_{\varepsilon,k,n}\in\mathcal{F}\cap C_{c}(X)
satisfies
0\leq v_{\varepsilon,k,n}\leq M
on
X
, by the dominated convergence theorem, we have
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{X}|u_{\varepsilon,k}-v_{\varepsilon,k,n}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\Gamma(\phi_{\varepsilon,k})=0
. Hence
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{E}(u_{\varepsilon,k}-\phi_{\varepsilon,k}v_{\varepsilon,k,n})=0
, which gives
\{\phi_{\varepsilon,k}v_{\varepsilon,k,n}\}_{n}

\mathcal{E}_{1}
-convergent to

In summary, for any

.

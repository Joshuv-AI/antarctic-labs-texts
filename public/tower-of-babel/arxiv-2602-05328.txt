Abstract
. We consider reaction-diffusion equations of the form u t − d ​ u x ​ x = f ⁡ ( t , u ) , t > 0 , x ∈ [ g ⁡ ( t ) , h ⁡ ( t ) ] , u_{t}-du_{xx}=f(t,u),\quad t>0,\ \ x\in[g(t),h(t)], where f ⁡ ( t , u ) f(t,u) is periodic in t t and monostable in u u , and the interval [ g ⁡ ( t ) , h ⁡ ( t ) ] [g(t),h(t)] represents the one dimensional population range of a species with density u ⁡ ( t , x ) u(t,x) at time t t and spatial location x x . The free boundaries x = g ⁡ ( t ) x=g(t) and x = h ⁡ ( t ) x=h(t) evolve subject to a “preferred population density” condition at the habitat edges. Analogous to the traveling wave solutions in the corresponding Cauchy problem, semi-wave solutions play a fundamental role in understanding the propagation phenomena governed by the free boundary problem here. But in contrast to the Cauchy problem, where the KPP condition plays a subtle role in the precise approximation of its solution (with compactly supported initial function) by the traveling wave solution with minimal speed, here we prove the existence and uniqueness of a semi-wave in a general monostable setting, and obtain a precise description of the convergence of the solution toward the semi-wave as time goes to infinity, where the KPP condition plays no special role. Previously, such a sharp result was proved for a free boundary model only when f f is autonomous ( f = f ⁡ ( u ) f=f(u) , see [ 12 ] or [ 19 ] for a related free boundary model), or a less precise result was obtained in the time-periodic case under an extra strong KPP condition on f f (see [ 43 ] , or [ 14 ] for a related free boundary model). This work appears to be the first to prove the sharp convergence result for a general monostable free boundary problem in a heterogeneous environment, and we believe the methods developed here should have applications to related free boundary problems in heterogeneous media with nonlinearities more general than those of KPP type. 3.
Existence, uniqueness and asymptotic behavior of the semi-wave

In this section, we revisit the existence and uniqueness of semi-wave solutions in
[
43
]
and extend the result there from the KPP case to a general monostable setting. The proof of uniqueness proceeds in two main steps: first, we show that equal integral averages of propagation speeds imply identical speeds and profiles; second, we deduce uniqueness by proving that such speed averages are unique. In addition, we also investigate the asymptotic rate at which the semi-wave profile approaches the steady state
1
. It is interesting to note that this convergence rate depends on

,

, and the integral average of the wave speed, but is independent of

[

U(t,x)<\bar{U}_{q_{0}}(t,x)\text{ for }t\in(0,T],\ x\in[0,\infty),

This contradicts the definition of

and we can again apply the comparison principle to

over

Since

Because

satisfies

\begin{cases}w_{t}-dw_{xx}+k(t)w_{x}=f(t,\Phi^{*})-f(t,1)=f_{u}(t,\theta(t,x))w&\mbox{ for }\ t\in\mathbb{R},\ x>0,\\
w=\delta-1&\mbox{ for }\ t\in\mathbb{R},\ x=0,\end{cases}

with
\theta(t,x)\in[\Phi^{*}(t,x),1]

estimates and Sobolev embedding theorems, that there exists

such that, for any

which implies the desired result.
∎

4.20
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{\delta}\Phi^{*}_{x}(t,x_{\delta}(t))=\frac{d}{\delta}\Phi^{*}_{x}(t,0)+\frac{d}{\delta}\Phi^{*}_{xx}(t,\theta(t))x_{\delta}(t)\geq k^{*}(t)-\frac{d}{\delta}N_{2}C_{1}(q_{1}e^{-\sigma_{1}t}+e^{-\mu k_{min}\,t}).

Hence,

\displaystyle-\frac{d}{\delta}\underline{u}_{x}(t,\underline{h}(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{d}{\delta}\left[\Phi^{*}_{x}(t,2\underline{h}(t)+x_{\delta}(t))-\Phi^{*}_{x}(t,x_{\delta}(t))\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle k^{*}(t)-\frac{d}{\delta}N_{2}C_{1}(q_{1}e^{-\sigma_{1}t}+e^{-\mu k_{min}t})-\frac{d}{\delta}N_{1}e^{-\mu k_{min}t}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle k^{*}(t)-\frac{d}{\delta}N_{2}C_{1}q_{1}e^{-\sigma_{1}t}-\frac{d}{\delta}(N_{2}C_{1}+N_{1})e^{-\mu k_{min}t}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\underline{h}^{\prime}(t)=k^{*}(t)-\sigma_{1}\beta e^{-\sigma_{1}t}\leq-\frac{d}{\delta}\underline{u}_{x}(t,\underline{h}(t))~\text{ for }t\geq 0

provided
\sigma_{1}<\mu k_{min}
and

\beta\geq\frac{(2N_{2}C_{1}+N_{1})d}{\sigma_{1}\delta}.

Since
\Phi^{*}(t,\infty)=1
and
\Phi^{*}_{x}(t,\infty)=0
uniformly in
t\in\mathbb{R}
, for any given small positive
\epsilon_{0}
, there exists
\tilde{K}_{0}=\tilde{K}_{0}(\epsilon_{0})>0
large enough such that

(4.22)

\Phi^{*}(t,x)>1-\epsilon_{0}\text{ and }\Phi_{x}(t,x)<\epsilon_{0}/2~\text{ for }t\in\mathbb{R},\ x\geq\tilde{K}_{0}.

Therefore, if
K_{1}
in (
4.16
) is chosen to satify
K_{1}\geq\tilde{K}_{0}
, then
\underline{h}(t)\geq\tilde{K}_{0}
for
t\geq 0
.

Next, we shall prove

\mathcal{N}[\underline{u}]=\underline{u}_{t}-d\underline{u}_{xx}-f(t,\underline{u})\leq 0.

For convenience, denote

\xi^{-}=\xi^{-}(t,x):=\underline{h}(t)-x+x_{\delta}(t),\ \ \xi^{+}=\xi^{+}(t,x):=\underline{h}(t)+x+x_{\delta}(t).

By direct calculation we have

\displaystyle\underline{u}_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}^{*}(t,\xi^{-})+\Phi^{*}_{t}(t,\xi^{+})+(\Phi^{*}_{z}(t,\xi^{-})+\Phi^{*}_{z}(t,\xi^{+}))(\underline{h}^{\prime}(t)+x_{\delta}^{\prime}(t))+\sigma_{1}q_{1}e^{-\sigma_{1}t},

\displaystyle\underline{u}_{xx}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi^{*}_{zz}(t,\xi^{-})+\Phi^{*}_{zz}(t,\xi^{+}),

where
\Phi^{*}_{z}
denotes the partial derivative of
\Phi^{*}(t,z)
with respect to
z
.
Since
\Phi^{*}
satisfies (
1.4
), we obtain

(4.23)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}[\underline{u}]

\displaystyle=(\Phi^{*}_{z}(t,\xi^{-})+\Phi^{*}_{z}(t,\xi^{+}))(-\sigma_{1}\beta e^{-\sigma_{1}t}+x_{\delta}^{\prime}(t))+\sigma_{1}q_{1}e^{-\sigma_{1}t}

\displaystyle+f(t,\Phi^{*}(t,\xi^{-}))+f(t,\Phi^{*}(t,\xi^{+}))-f\left(t,\bar{u}\right).

We next show
\mathcal{N}[\underline{u}]\leq 0
in the following regions one by one.

(a):

\underline{h}(t)-\tilde{K}_{0}\leq x\leq\underline{h}(t)
,

(b):

-\underline{h}(t)\leq x\leq-\underline{h}(t)+\tilde{K}_{0}
,

(c):

-\underline{h}(t)+\tilde{K}_{0}\leq x\leq\underline{h}(t)-\tilde{K}_{0}
.

(a) For
\underline{h}(t)-\tilde{K}_{0}\leq x\leq\underline{h}(t)
, we have
x_{\delta}(t)\leq\xi^{-}\leq x_{\delta}(t)+\tilde{K}_{0}
and

\xi^{+}=\underline{h}(t)+x+x_{\delta}(t)\geq 2\underline{h}(t)-\tilde{K}_{0}+x_{\delta}(t)\geq\underline{h}(t)\geq k_{min}\,t

with
x_{\delta}(t)
being bounded and
t\geq n_{0}T\gg 1
.
Thus in this case there exist positive constants
\tilde{N}_{3}
and
\tilde{N}_{4}
such that

0<\tilde{N}_{3}\leq\Phi^{*}_{z}(t,\xi^{+})+\Phi^{*}_{z}(t,\xi^{-})\leq\tilde{N}_{4}.

Since
f\in C^{1}
in
u
uniformly in
t
, and
f(\cdot,1)=0
,
we can find
L>0
such that

f(t,\Phi^{*}(t,\xi^{+}))=f(t,\Phi^{*}(t,\xi^{+}))-f(t,1)\leq L|\Phi^{*}(t,\xi^{+})-1|\leq LN_{1}e^{-\mu\xi^{+}}\leq LN_{1}e^{-\mu k_{min}t}

and

\displaystyle f(t,\Phi^{*}(t,\xi^{-}))-f(t,\underline{u})\leq L|1-\Phi^{*}(t,\xi^{+})+q_{1}e^{-\sigma_{1}t}|\leq L(N_{1}e^{-\mu k_{min}t}+q_{1}e^{-\sigma_{1}t}).

It then follows from (
4.23
) and (
4.21
) that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}[\underline{u}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\tilde{N}_{4}C_{2}q_{1}-\tilde{N}_{3}\sigma_{1}\beta)e^{-\sigma_{1}t}+\tilde{N}_{4}C_{2}(1+\sigma_{1}\beta e^{-\sigma_{1}t})e^{-\mu k_{min}t}+\sigma_{1}q_{1}e^{-\sigma_{1}t}

\displaystyle+L(2N_{1}e^{-\mu k_{min}t}+q_{1}e^{-\sigma_{1}t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\tilde{N}_{4}C_{2}q_{1}-\tilde{N}_{3}\sigma_{1}\beta+\sigma_{1}q_{1}+Lq_{1}\right)e^{-\sigma_{1}t}+[\tilde{N}_{4}C_{2}(1+\sigma_{1}\beta e^{-\sigma_{1}t})+2LN_{1}]e^{-\mu k_{min}t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\tilde{N}_{4}C_{2}q_{1}-\tilde{N}_{3}\sigma_{1}\beta/2+\sigma_{1}q_{1}+Lq_{1}\right)e^{-\sigma_{1}t}+(\tilde{N}_{4}C_{2}+2LN_{1})e^{-\mu k_{min}t}

\displaystyle+\sigma_{1}\beta(\tilde{N}_{4}C_{2}e^{-\mu k_{min}t}-\tilde{N}_{3}/2)e^{-\sigma_{1}t}\leq 0~~\text{ for }t\geq n_{0}T\gg 1,

provided that
\sigma_{1}<\mu k_{min}
and

\beta\geq\frac{2(\tilde{N}_{4}C_{2}q_{1}+\sigma_{1}q_{1}+Lq_{1}+\tilde{N}_{4}C_{2}+2LN_{1})}{\tilde{N}_{3}\sigma_{1}}.

Here we also used
n_{0}\gg 1
and so

\tilde{N}_{3}\geq 2\tilde{N}_{4}C_{2}e^{-\mu k_{min}n_{0}T}.

(b) Similarly, we can show
\mathcal{N}[\underline{u}]\leq 0
for
-\underline{h}(t)\leq x\leq-\underline{h}(t)-\tilde{K}_{0}
.

(c) In the remaining case
-\underline{h}(t)+\tilde{K}_{0}\leq x\leq\underline{h}(t)-\tilde{K}_{0}
, it is easily seen that
\xi^{+},\xi^{-}\geq\tilde{K}_{0}
and
\max\{\xi^{+},\xi^{-}\}\geq\underline{h}(t)\geq k_{min}t
. This together with (
4.22
) implies that, for
t\geq n_{0}T\gg 1
,

(4.24)

\begin{cases}\min\{1-\Phi^{*}(t,\xi^{+}),1-\Phi^{*}(t,\xi^{-})\}\leq N_{1}e^{-\mu k_{min}t},\ \\
\max\{1-\Phi^{*}(t,\xi^{+}),1-\Phi^{*}(t,\xi^{-})\}\leq\epsilon_{0},\ \\
\Phi^{*}_{z}(t,\xi^{+})+\Phi^{*}_{z}(t,\xi^{-})<\epsilon_{0}.\end{cases}

For any
t\geq 0
and
u,v\in[0,1]
, we denote

F(u,v)(t):=f(t,u)+f(t,v)-f\left(t,u+v-1-q_{1}e^{-\sigma_{1}t}\right).

Since
u,v\in[0,1]
and
f(t,\cdot)
is
C^{1}
uniformly in
t
with
f(t,1)\equiv 1
, there exists some
L>0
such that

\displaystyle F(u,v)(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\min\left\{L(1-u)+f_{u}(t,\theta_{v})(1+q_{1}e^{-\sigma_{1}t}-u),L(1-v)+f_{u}(t,\theta_{u})(1+q_{1}e^{-\sigma_{1}t}-v)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\min\left\{2L(1-u)+f_{u}(t,\theta_{v})q_{1}e^{-\sigma_{1}t},2L(1-v)+f_{u}(t,\theta_{u})q_{1}e^{-\sigma_{1}t}\right\},

where
\theta_{v}\in[u+v-1-q_{1}e^{-\sigma_{1}t},v]
,
\theta_{u}\in[u+v-1-q_{1}e^{-\sigma_{1}t},u]
.
We may now use (
4.24
) to deduce that

F(\Phi^{*}(t,\xi^{+}),\Phi^{*}(t,\xi^{-}))\leq 2LN_{1}e^{-\mu k_{min}t}+\sigma_{\epsilon_{0}}q_{1}e^{-\sigma_{1}t}\mbox{ for }t\geq n_{0}T,

where

\sigma_{\epsilon_{0}}:=\max_{t\in[0,T],u\in[1-3\epsilon_{0},1]}f_{u}(t,u)\leq\frac{1}{2}\max_{t\in[0,T]}f_{u}(t,1)<0

provided that
\epsilon_{0}
is small enough and
n_{0}\gg 1
.
The above inequality, (
4.21
), (
4.23
) and (
4.24
) then lead to, for
t\geq n_{0}T\gg 1
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}[\underline{u}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\Phi^{*}_{z}(t,\xi^{+})+\Phi^{*}_{z}(t,\xi^{-}))x_{\delta}^{\prime}(t)+\sigma_{1}q_{1}e^{-\sigma_{1}t}+F(\Phi^{*}(t,\xi^{+}),\Phi^{*}(t,\xi^{-}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}q_{1}\epsilon_{0}e^{-\sigma_{1}t}+C_{2}\epsilon_{0}(1+\sigma_{1}\beta e^{-\sigma_{1}t})e^{-\mu k_{min}t}+(\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{\epsilon_{0}})q_{1}e^{-\sigma_{1}t}+2LN_{1}e^{-\mu k_{min}t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle q_{1}e^{-\sigma_{1}t}\left\{C_{2}\epsilon_{0}+\sigma_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\max_{t\in[0,T]}f_{u}(t,1)+e^{-(\mu k_{min}-\sigma_{1})t}\left[C_{2}\epsilon_{0}(1+\sigma_{1}\beta e^{-\sigma_{1}t})+2LN_{1}\right]\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 0\ \ \mbox{ provided that $\epsilon_{0}$ is small enough and}

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}<\min\Big\{-\frac{1}{2}\max_{t\in[0,T]}f_{u}(t,1),\mu k_{min}\Big\}.

We have now proved that
\mathcal{N}[\underline{u}]\leq 0
for
t\geq n_{0}T\gg 1
and
x\in[-\underline{h}(t),\underline{h}(t)]
, with suitable choices of the parameters.

By Proposition
2.3
, we can find positive integers
n_{1}\geq n_{0}
such that

\begin{cases}-g(n_{1}T),h(n_{1}T)>\underline{h}(n_{0}T),\\
u(t,x)\geq\delta~\text{ for }t\geq n_{1}T,\ x\in[g(t),h(t)],\\
u(n_{1}T,x)\geq 1-q_{1}e^{-\sigma_{1}n_{0}T}\geq\underline{u}(n_{0}T,x)~\text{ for }x\in[-\underline{h}(n_{0}T),\underline{h}(n_{0}T)].\end{cases}

Denote
n_{2}=n_{1}-n_{0}
. Then

u(n_{0}T+n_{2}T,x)\geq\underline{u}(n_{0}T,x)~\text{ for }x\in[-\underline{h}(n_{0}T),\underline{h}(n_{0}T)],

and for
t\geq n_{0}T
,

u(t+n_{2}T,\pm\underline{h}(t))\geq\delta=\underline{u}(t,\pm\underline{h}(t))\text{ whenever }[-\underline{h}(t),\underline{h}(t)]\subset[g(t+n_{2}T),h(t+n_{2}T)].

So we are now able to apply the lower solution version of Lemma
2.2
to conclude that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}[-\underline{h}(t),\underline{h}(t)]\subset(g(t+n_{2}T),h(t+n_{2}T)),\\
\underline{u}(t,x)\leq u(t+n_{2}T,x),\end{cases}\text{ for }t\geq n_{0}T,\ x\in[-\underline{h}(t),\underline{h}(t)].

The proof is thereby complete.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

There exists
Q>0
such that

(4.25)

\Big|g(t)+\int_{0}^{t}k^{*}(s)ds\Big|+\Big|h(t)-\int_{0}^{t}k^{*}(s)ds\Big|\leq Q~~\text{ for }t\geq 0.

Proof.

Denote
\tau_{0}:=\max\{\tau^{*},(n_{2}+n_{0})T\}
. By Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
, we have for
t\geq\tau_{0}
,

\displaystyle-\int_{t-n_{2}T}^{t}k^{*}(s)ds+\beta e^{-\sigma_{1}(t-n_{2}T)}+K_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h(t)-\int_{0}^{t}k^{*}(s)ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}}k^{*}(s)ds+\gamma(1-e^{-\sigma_{0}(t-\tau^{*})})+h(\tau^{*})+a+K_{0}.

Set

Q:=2\max\left\{n_{2}\overline{k^{*}}T+\beta+K_{1},\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}}k^{*}(s)ds+\gamma+h(\tau^{*})+a+K_{0},\max_{t\in[0,\tau_{0}]}\Big|h(t)-\int_{0}^{t}k^{*}(s)ds\Big|\right\}.

Then

\Big|h(t)-\int_{0}^{t}k^{*}(s)ds\Big|\leq Q/2\text{ for all }t\geq 0.

Similarly, we can show

\Big|g(t)+\int_{0}^{t}k^{*}(s)ds\Big|\leq Q/2\text{ for all }t\geq 0.

The proof is complete.
∎

4.3.
Convergence along a special time sequence

Let
(u(t,x),g(t),h(t))
be the solution to (
1.2
),
Q
be given by Proposition
4.3
, and

\begin{cases}l(t):=\int_{0}^{t}k^{*}(s)ds-2Q,\\
\sigma(t):=g(t)-l(t),\\
\rho(t):=h(t)-l(t),\\
v(t,x):=u(t,x+l(t)).\end{cases}

Clearly,

(4.26)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\sigma(t)=-\infty,\ Q\leq\rho(t)\leq 3Q,

and

u_{x}=v_{x},~u_{xx}=v_{xx},~u_{t}=v_{t}-k^{*}(t)v_{x}.

Hence,
(v(t,x),\sigma(t),\rho(t))
solves

\begin{cases}v_{t}-dv_{xx}-k^{*}(t)v_{x}=f(t,v),&t>0,\ \sigma(t)<x<\rho(t),\\
v(t,\rho(t))=\delta,&t>0,\\
\rho^{\prime}(t)=h^{\prime}(t)-l^{\prime}(t)=-\frac{d}{\delta}v_{x}(t,\rho(t))-k^{*}(t),&t>0.\\
\end{cases}

Let
\{t_{n}\}
be a positive sequence converging to
\infty
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\rho(t_{n})=\liminf_{t\to\infty}\rho(t)\in[Q,3Q].

Write
t_{n}=k_{n}T+r_{n}
with
k_{n}
a positive integer and
r_{n}\in[0,T)
. Up to extraction of a subsequence we may assume
r_{n}\to r_{*}\in[0,T]
.
Define

\begin{cases}(k_{n}^{*}(t),\sigma_{n}(t),\rho_{n}(t)):=(k^{*}(t+t_{n}-r_{*}),\sigma(t+t_{n}-r_{*}),\rho(t+t_{n}-r_{*})),\\
v_{n}(t,x):=v(t+t_{n}-r_{*},x).\end{cases}

We have the following result.

Lemma 4.4
.

There exists a subsequence of
\{(\rho_{n},v_{n})\}
, still denoted by itself, such that

\rho_{n}\to\Gamma\text{ in }C^{1+\alpha/2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})\text{ and }\|v_{n}-V\|_{C_{loc}^{(1+\alpha)/2,1+\alpha}(\overline{\Sigma})}\to 0~\text{ as }n\to\infty,

where
\Sigma:=\{(t,x):t\in\mathbb{R},-\infty<x<\Gamma(t)\}
. Moreover
(V(t,x),\Gamma(t))
satisfies

(4.27)

\begin{cases}V_{t}-dV_{xx}-k^{*}(t)V_{x}=f(t,V),~V>\delta,&(t,x)\in\Sigma,\\
V(t,\Gamma(t))=\delta,&t\in\mathbb{R},\\
\Gamma^{\prime}(t)=-\frac{d}{\delta}V_{x}(t,\Gamma(t))-k^{*}(t),~\Gamma(t)\geq\Gamma(r_{*}),&t\in\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

Proof.

By Proposition
2.3
, there exists
M_{0}>0
such that
|h^{\prime}(t)|\leq M_{0}
, from which we have

-M_{0}-k^{*}(t)\leq\rho^{\prime}(t)\leq M_{0}-k^{*}(t)~~\text{ for }t\geq 0.

Define

\xi:=\frac{x}{\rho_{n}(t)},~~\tilde{V}_{n}(t,\xi):=v_{n}(t,x).

Then
(\tilde{V}_{n}(t,\xi),\rho_{n}(t))
satisfies

(4.28)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\tilde{V}_{n}-\frac{d}{\rho_{n}^{2}(t)}\partial_{\xi\xi}\tilde{V}_{n}-\frac{k^{*}_{n}(t)+\rho_{n}^{\prime}(t)\xi}{\rho_{n}(t)}\partial_{\xi}\tilde{V}_{n}=f(t+t_{n}-r_{*},\tilde{V}_{n}),&t>-t_{n}+r_{*},\frac{\sigma_{n}(t)}{\rho_{n}(t)}\leq\xi\leq 1,\\[5.69054pt]
\tilde{V}_{n}(t,1)=\delta,~\rho_{n}^{\prime}(t)=-\frac{d}{\delta\rho_{n}(t)}\partial_{\xi}\tilde{V}_{n}(t,1)-k^{*}_{n}(t),&t>-t_{n}+r_{*}.\end{cases}

Since
u(t,x)
is uniformly bounded for
t\in[0,\infty)
and
x\in[g(t),h(t)]
, we know that
\tilde{V}_{n}
is uniformly bounded in
\{(t,\xi):t\geq-t_{n}+r_{*},\frac{\sigma_{n}(t)}{\rho_{n}(t)}\leq\xi\leq 1\}
. Thanks to (
4.26
), for any
\tau>0
,
L>0
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we can apply the standard interior and boundary
L^{p}
estimates to (
4.28
) over
[s-\tau,s]\times[-L,1]
to obtain that, for any
p>1
,

\|\tilde{V}_{n}\|_{W_{p}^{1,2}([s-\tau,s]\times[-L,1])}\leq C_{p}\text{ for all large }n,

where
C_{p}
is a constant depending only on
p
,
\tau
and
L
but independent of
n
and
s
. It then follows from the Sobolev embedding theorem that

\|\tilde{V}_{n}\|_{C^{(1+\beta)/2,1+\beta}([s-\tau,s]\times[-L,1])}\leq\tilde{C}_{p}\text{ for all large }n,

where
\alpha<\beta<1
and
\tilde{C}_{p}
is a constant depending on
\beta
,
\tau
and
L
but independent of
n
and
s
.
This together with (
4.28
) and (
4.26
) implies that, for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists
C_{1}>0
depending on
\beta
,
\tau
and
L
but independent of
n
and
s
such that

Q\leq\rho_{n}\leq 3Q,\ \|\rho_{n}\|_{C^{1+\beta/2}([s-\tau,s])}\leq C_{1}.

Hence, up to extraction of a subsequence through a diagonal process, we have

\tilde{V}_{n}\to\bar{V}\text{ in }C^{(1+\alpha)/2,1+\alpha}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}\times(-\infty,1]),~~\rho_{n}\to\Gamma\text{ in }C_{loc}^{1+\alpha/2}(\mathbb{R}).

By applying the standard parabolic regularity theory, one obtains that
(\bar{V},\Gamma)
solves the following problem in the classical sense

\begin{cases}\bar{V}_{t}-\frac{d}{\Gamma^{2}(t)}\bar{V}_{\xi\xi}-\frac{k^{*}(t)+\Gamma^{\prime}(t)\xi}{\Gamma(t)}\bar{V}_{\xi}=f(t,\bar{V}),&t\in\mathbb{R},~\xi\in(-\infty,1],\\
\bar{V}(t,1)=\delta,~\Gamma^{\prime}(t)=-\frac{d}{\delta\Gamma(t)}\bar{V}_{\xi}(t,1)-k^{*}(t),&t\in\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

It follows from Proposition
2.3
that
\bar{V}\geq\delta
in
\mathbb{R}\times(-\infty,1]
. Due to
f(t,\delta)>0
, we can apply the strong parabolic maximum principle to conclude that
\bar{V}>\delta
in
\mathbb{R}\times(-\infty,1)
. Set
V(t,x)=\bar{V}(t,x/\Gamma(t))
. It is easily seen that
(V,\Gamma)
solves (
4.27
) and satisfies

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|v_{n}-V\|_{C_{loc}^{(1+\alpha)/2,1+\alpha}(\overline{\Sigma})}=0.

Moreover, since

\Gamma(r_{*})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\rho(t_{n})=\liminf_{t\to\infty}\rho(t)\text{ and }\Gamma(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\rho(t+t_{n}-r),

we have
\Gamma(t)\geq\Gamma(r_{*})
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
. The proof is thus complete.
∎

4.4.
Identification of the limit pair
(V,\Gamma)
.

By (
4.26
), we have

Q\leq\Gamma(t)\leq 3Q,\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}.

From Lemma
4.2
and the
T
-periodic property of
\Phi^{*}
in
t
, we see that for
t\geq(n_{0}+n_{2})T
and
x\in[-\underline{h}(t-n_{2}T),\underline{h}(t-n_{2}T)]
,

\displaystyle u(t,x)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\Phi^{*}(t,-x+\underline{h}(t-n_{2}T)+x_{\delta}(t-n_{2}T))

\displaystyle+\,\Phi^{*}(t,x+\underline{h}(t-n_{2}T)+x_{\delta}(t-n_{2}T))-1-q_{1}e^{-\sigma_{1}(t-n_{2}T)}.

Denote

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Phi_{n}^{-}(t,x):=\Phi^{*}(t+r_{n}-r_{*},-x-l(t+t_{n}-r_{*})+\underline{h}(t+t_{n}-r_{*}-n_{2}T)+x_{\delta}(t+t_{n}-r_{*}-n_{2}T)),\\
\Phi_{n}^{+}(t,x):=\Phi^{*}(t+r_{n}-r_{*},\ \ x+l(t+t_{n}-r_{*})+\underline{h}(t+t_{n}-r_{*}-n_{2}T)+x_{\delta}(t+t_{n}-r_{*}-n_{2}T)).\end{cases}

It follows that

(4.29)

v_{n}(t,x)\geq\Phi_{n}^{-}(t,x)+\Phi_{n}^{+}(t,x)-1-q_{1}e^{-\sigma_{1}(t+t_{n}-r_{*}-n_{2}T)},

for
t+t_{n}-r_{*}\geq(n_{0}+n_{2})T
and

Since
x_{\delta}(t)
is bounded and

r_{n}\to r_{*}~\text{ and }\underline{h}(t+t_{n}-r_{*}-n_{2}T)+l(t+t_{n}-r_{*})\to\infty~~\text{ as }n\to\infty,

we obtain

\lim_{n\to\infty}\Phi_{n}^{+}(t,x)=1\text{ locally uniformly in }\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}.

Moreover, direct calculations give

\displaystyle\underline{h}(t+t_{n}-r_{*}-n_{2}T)-l(t+t_{n}-r_{*})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t+t_{n}-r_{*}-n_{2}T}k^{*}(s)ds+K_{1}-\int_{0}^{t+t_{n}-r_{*}}k^{*}(s)ds+2Q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{t+t_{n}-r_{*}-n_{2}T}^{t+t_{n}-r_{*}}k^{*}(s)ds+K_{1}+2Q

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-n_{2}T\overline{k^{*}}+K_{1}+2Q~\text{ for }t+t_{n}-r_{*}\geq(n_{0}+n_{1})T.

Hence, there exists
L_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\underline{h}(t+t_{n}-r-n_{2}T)-l(t+t_{n}-r)\geq L_{0}~~\text{ for }t+t_{n}-r\geq(n_{0}+n_{1})T.

Since
x_{\delta}(t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
, by letting
n\to\infty
in (
4.29
) and utilizing the monotonicity of
\Phi^{*}
, we can deduce from Lemma
4.4
that

(4.30)

V(t,x)\geq\Phi^{*}(t,L_{0}-x)\text{ for }(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times(-\infty,L_{0}].

Define

L^{*}:=\sup\{L\in\mathbb{R}:V(t,x)\geq\Phi^{*}(t,L-x)\text{ for }(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times(-\infty,L]\}.

From (
4.30
) we see that
L^{*}\in[L_{0},\infty]
is well-defined. Since
V(t,L(t))=\delta
with
Q\leq L(t)\leq 3Q
and
\Phi^{*}(t,x)>\delta
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
x>0
, we must have
L^{*}\leq\Gamma(r_{*})
. Thus we have

(4.31)

\begin{cases}V(t,x)\geq\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-x)\text{ for }(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times(-\infty,L^{*}],\\
\min_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\Gamma(t)=\Gamma(r_{*})\geq L^{*}.\end{cases}

Lemma 4.5
.

L^{*}=\Gamma(r_{*})
.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that
L^{*}<\Gamma(r_{*})
. We are going to derive a contradiction in several steps.

Step 1.
We prove that

(4.32)

V(t,x)>\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-x)~~\text{ for }(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times(-\infty,L^{*}].

Otherwise, in view of (
4.31
), there exists
(t_{0},x_{0})\in\mathbb{R}\times(-\infty,L^{*}]
such that

V(t_{0},x_{0})=\Phi^{*}(t_{0},L^{*}-x_{0}).

Since
L^{*}<\Gamma(r_{*})\leq\Gamma(t)
, we obtain
V(t,L^{*})>\delta=\Phi^{*}(t,0)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, which implies
x_{0}<L^{*}
. Thus
w(t,x):=V(t,x)-\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-x)
satisfies
w\geq 0
in
\mathbb{R}\times(-\infty,L^{*}]
and

\begin{cases}w_{t}-dw_{xx}-k^{*}(t)w_{x}-c(t,x)w=0&\text{ for }(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times(-\infty,L^{*}),\\
w(t,L^{*})>0&\text{ for }t\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

with

c(t,x):=\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{u}f(t,s\Phi^{*}+(1-s)V)ds\ \ \mbox{ a bounded function.}

By the parabolic strong maximum principle, we deduce
w(t,x)>0
for
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times(-\infty,L^{*}]
, which contradicts
w(t_{0},x_{0})=0
. Hence, (
4.32
) holds.

Step 2.
We show that for any
y<L^{*}
,

(4.33)

\chi(y):=\inf_{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times[y,L^{*}]}[V(t,x)-\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-x)]>0.

Clearly,
\chi(y)\geq 0
for
y<L^{*}
. Assume to the contrary that (
4.33
) does not hold, then there exists
y_{0}\in(-\infty,L^{*})
such that

\chi(y_{0})=0.

Hence, we can find a sequence
\{(s_{n},x_{n})\}\subset\mathbb{R}\times[y_{0},L^{*}]
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}[V(s_{n},x_{n})-\Phi^{*}(s_{n},L^{*}-x_{n})]=0.

In view of Step 1, we have
|s_{n}|\to\infty
. Write
s_{n}=\tilde{k}_{n}T+\tilde{r}_{n}
with
\tilde{k}_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\tilde{r}_{n}\in[0,T)
. Up to extraction of a subsequence if necessary, we may assume that
\tilde{r}_{n}\to\tilde{r}\in[0,T]
and
x_{n}\to x_{0}\in[y_{0},L^{*}]
. Set

(V_{n}(t,x),\Gamma_{n}(t)):=(V(t+s_{n}-\tilde{r},x+x_{n}),\Gamma(t+s_{n}-\tilde{r})-x_{n}).

By applying the standard parabolic estimates and up to extraction of a subsequence, we can argue similarly to Lemma
4.4
to conclude that

(V_{n}(t,x),\Gamma_{n}(t))\to(V^{\infty},\Gamma^{\infty})\text{ in }C_{loc}^{(1+\alpha)/2,1+\alpha}(\overline{\Sigma}_{\infty})\times C_{loc}^{1+\alpha/2}(\mathbb{R}),

where
{\Sigma}_{\infty}:=\{(t,x):t\in\mathbb{R},x<\Gamma^{\infty}(t)\}
and
(V^{\infty},\Gamma^{\infty})
satisfies

(4.34)

\begin{cases}V^{\infty}_{t}-dV^{\infty}_{xx}-k^{*}(t)V^{\infty}_{x}=f(t,V^{\infty}),V^{\infty}>\delta&\text{ for }(t,x)\in{\Sigma}_{\infty},\\
V^{\infty}(t,\Gamma^{\infty}(t))=\delta&\text{ for }t\in\mathbb{R}\end{cases}

and

(4.35)

V^{\infty}(\tilde{r},0)=\Phi^{*}(\tilde{r},L^{*}-x_{0}).

Moreover,
\Gamma_{n}(t)=\Gamma(t+s_{n}-\tilde{r})-x_{n}\geq\Gamma(r_{*})-x_{n}
leads to

\Gamma^{\infty}(t)+x_{0}\geq\Gamma(r_{*})>L^{*}
and (
4.32
) yields

V^{\infty}(t,x)\geq\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-x_{0}-x)~~\text{ for }(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times(-\infty,L^{*}-x_{0}].

In addition, it is easily seen that
\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-x_{0}-x)
solves (
4.34
) with
\Gamma^{\infty}(t)
replaced by
L^{*}-x_{0}
. Due to
L^{\infty}(t)>L^{*}-x_{0}>0
, we have

V^{\infty}(t,L^{*}-x_{0})>\delta=\Phi^{*}(t,0)~~\text{ for }t\in\mathbb{R}.

Hence, we can apply the parabolic strong maximum principle to compare
V^{\infty}(t,x)
with
\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-x_{0}-x)
over
(-\infty,0]\times(-\infty,L^{*}-x_{0})
to conclude that

V^{\infty}(t,x)>\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-x_{0}-x)\text{ for }t\leq\tilde{r},\ x<L^{*}-x_{0},

which contradicts (
4.35
). Therefore, (
4.33
) holds for all
y<L^{*}
.

Step 3.
We find a constant
L_{0}<L^{*}
such that
V(t,x)\geq\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-x+\epsilon)
in
\mathbb{R}\times(-\infty,L_{0}]
for all small
\epsilon>0
.

Since
f_{u}(\cdot,1)<0
and
f
is
C^{1}
in
u
uniformly for
t\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists
\epsilon_{0}>0
small enough such that

(4.36)

f_{u}(t,u)<0~~\text{ for }t\in\mathbb{R},\ u\in[1-\epsilon_{0},1].

Owing to
\Phi^{*}(t,x)\to\infty
as
x\to\infty
uniformly in
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we can find
L_{0}=L_{0}(\epsilon_{0})<L^{*}
with
L^{*}-L_{0}\gg 1
such that

(4.37)

\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-x)\geq 1-\epsilon_{0}~~\text{ for }t\in\mathbb{R},\ x\leq L_{0}.

By continuity, there exists
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})
small enough (depending on
L^{*}-L_{0}
) such that

(4.38)

\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-L_{0}+\epsilon)\leq\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-L_{0})+\chi(L_{0})~\text{ for }t\in\mathbb{R},

where
\chi
is defined by (
4.33
).
Fix any such
\epsilon
. For any
L<L_{0}
, consider the following auxiliary problem

(4.39)

\begin{cases}w_{t}-dw_{xx}-k^{*}(t)w_{x}=f(t,w),&t>0,x\in(L,L_{0}),\\
w(t,L)=1,~w(t,L_{0})=\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-L_{0}+\epsilon),&t>0,\\
w(0,x)=1-\epsilon_{0},&x\in[L,L_{0}].\end{cases}

Clearly,
1
and
1-\epsilon_{0}
form a pair of upper and lower solutions of (
4.39
). It follows from the standard upper and lower solution arguments that (
4.39
) admits a maximal solution

w_{L}\in C_{loc}^{1+\alpha/2,2+\alpha}((0,\infty)\times[L,L_{0}])\cap C_{loc}^{1+\alpha/2,2+\alpha}([0,\infty)\times(L,L_{0}))

satisfying

(4.40)

1-\epsilon_{0}\leq w_{L}(t,x)\leq 1~~\text{ for }t>0,\ x\in[L,L_{0}].

By applying the usual comparison principle, we know that
w_{L}
increases in
L
in the sense that
L_{2}<L_{1}
implies
w_{L_{2}}(t,x)\leq w_{L_{1}}(t,x)
. Hence, the pointwise limit

w_{\infty}(t,x):=\lim_{L\to\infty}w_{L}(t,x)\ \mbox{ for }t>0,x\in(-\infty,L_{0}]

is well-defined and
1-\epsilon_{0}\leq w_{\infty}\leq 1
. By applying the standard parabolic estimates, we conclude that the above convergence also holds in
C_{loc}^{1,2}([0,\infty)\times(-\infty,L_{0}))
, and thus
w_{\infty}
solves (
4.39
) in
[0,\infty)\times(-\infty,L_{0}]
except for the first equation in the second line.
Since
1-\epsilon_{0}
is a lower solution and
f
is
T
-periodic in
t
,
w_{\infty}(t+kT,x)
is nondecreasing in
k\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence, the pointwise limit

\tilde{w}(t,x)=\lim_{k\to\infty}w_{\infty}(t+kT,x)

is well-defined and
\tilde{w}(t,x)
is
T
-periodic in
t
with
1-\epsilon_{0}\leq\tilde{w}\leq 1
. Similar to before, we deduce that
\tilde{w}
solves

(4.41)

\begin{cases}\tilde{w}_{t}-d\tilde{w}_{xx}-k^{*}(t)\tilde{w}_{x}=f(t,\tilde{w}),~1-\epsilon_{0}\leq\tilde{w}\leq 1,&t\in\mathbb{R},\ x\in(-\infty,L_{0}),\\
\tilde{w}(t,L_{0})=\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-L_{0}+\epsilon),&t\in\mathbb{R},\\
\tilde{w}(t,x)=\tilde{w}(t+T,x),&t\in\mathbb{R},\ x\in(-\infty,L_{0}].\end{cases}

We claim that
\tilde{w}(t,-\infty)=1
. Indeed, for any sequence
\{x_{n}\}
satisfying
x_{n}\to-\infty
as
n\to\infty
, if we denote

\tilde{w}_{n}(t,x):=\tilde{w}(t,x+x_{n})\text{ for }t\in\mathbb{R},x\in(-\infty,L_{0}-x_{n}],

then
\tilde{w}_{n}(t,x)
satisfies (
4.41
) with
L_{0}
replaced by
L_{0}-x_{n}
. Hence, by using the standard parabolic estimates and up to extraction of a subsequence, we know that there exists a function
\hat{w}
such that

\tilde{w}_{n}(t,x)\to\hat{w}(t,x)\text{ as }n\to\infty\text{ locally uniformly in }C^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}).

Moreover,
\hat{w}(t,x)
satisfies

\begin{cases}\hat{w}_{t}-d\hat{w}_{xx}-k^{*}(t)\hat{w}_{x}=f(t,\hat{w}),~1-\epsilon_{0}\leq\hat{w}\leq 1,&t\in\mathbb{R},x\in\mathbb{R},\\
\hat{w}(t,x)=\hat{w}(t+T,x),&t\in\mathbb{R},x\in\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

The usual comparison principle yields that
\hat{w}(t,x)\geq p(t)
with
p(t)
solving

p_{t}=f(t,p),~t>0;~~~p(0)=1-\epsilon_{0}.

Due to
f(\cdot,u)>0
for
u\in(0,1)
, we know that
p(t+kT)\to 1
locally uniformly in
t\in\mathbb{R}
as the integer
k\to\infty
. Therefore,

1\geq\hat{w}(t,x)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\hat{w}(t+kT,x)\geq\lim_{k\to\infty}p(t+kT)=1\text{ locally uniformly in }(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},

which implies that

\tilde{w}(t,x+x_{n})\to 1\text{ locally uniformly in }C^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}).

By the arbitrariness of
\{x_{n}\}
, we conclude that
\tilde{w}(t,-\infty)=1
. The claim is thus proved.

We now set

\Psi(t,x):=\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-x+\epsilon)~\text{ for }t\in\mathbb{R},x\leq L_{0}.

Clearly,
\Psi
also satisfies (
4.41
). Thanks to (
4.37
), one has
\Psi(t,x)\geq 1-\epsilon_{0}
for
x\leq L_{0}
. Therefore, we can compare
\Psi
and
w_{\infty}
to obtain

\Psi(t+kT,x)\geq w_{\infty}(t+kT,x)~~\text{ for }t>-kT,\ x\in(-\infty,L_{0}],\ k\in\mathbb{N}.

Now, letting
k\to\infty
, one gets

(4.42)

\Psi(t,x)\geq\tilde{w}(t,x)\geq 1-\epsilon_{0}~~\text{ for }t\in\mathbb{R},\ x\in(-\infty,L_{0}].

We claim that

\Psi(t,x)\equiv\tilde{w}(t,x)~~\text{ for }t\in\mathbb{R},\ x\in(-\infty,L_{0}].

Denote

U(t,x):=\Psi(t,x)-\tilde{w}(t,x).

Then
U\geq 0
satisfies

(4.43)

\begin{cases}U_{t}-dU_{xx}-k^{*}(t)U_{x}-f_{u}(t,\theta(t,x))U=0,&t\in\mathbb{R},\ x\in(-\infty,L_{0}),\\
U(t,-\infty)=U(t,L_{0})=0,&t\in\mathbb{R},\\
U(t,x)=U(t+T,x),&t\in\mathbb{R},\ x\in(-\infty,L_{0}],\end{cases}

where
\theta(t,x)\in[\tilde{w}(t,x),\Psi(t,x)]\subset[1-\epsilon_{0},1]
.
If the claim does not hold, then in view of (
4.42
) and
U(t,-\infty)=0
, there exists
(t_{0},x_{0})\in\mathbb{R}\times(-\infty,L_{0})
such that

U(t_{0},x_{0})=\max_{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times(-\infty,L_{0})}U(t,x)>0.

Since
f_{u}(\cdot,u)<0
for
u\in[1-\epsilon_{0},1]
, using the first equation of (
4.43
) we obtain

0=\big[U_{t}-dU_{xx}-k^{*}(t)U_{x}-f_{u}(t,\theta(t,x))U\big]\big|_{(t_{0},x_{0})}\geq-f_{u}(t,\theta(t_{0},x_{0}))U(t_{0},x_{0})>0.

This contradiction proves the claim.

By (
4.32
),(
4.33
) and (
4.38
), we have

\displaystyle V(t,x)>\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-x)\geq 1-\epsilon_{0}~~\text{ for }(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times(-\infty,L_{0}],

\displaystyle V(t,L_{0})\geq\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-L_{0})+\chi(L_{0})\geq\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-L_{0}+\epsilon),

and thus we can compare
V
with
w_{\infty}
to deduce that

V(t,x)\geq w_{\infty}(t+kT,x)~\text{ for }t>-kT,\ x\in(-\infty,L_{0}],\ k\in\mathbb{N}.

Letting
k\to\infty
we obtain

(4.44)

V(t,x)\geq\tilde{w}(t,x)=\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-x+\epsilon)~\text{ for }t\in\mathbb{R},\ x\in(-\infty,L_{0}].

Step 4.
Completion of the proof.

By Step 2, we know that

\varrho:=\chi(L_{0})>0.

Since
L^{*}<\Gamma(r_{*})\leq\Gamma(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we deduce from the interior parabolic estimates that

|V_{x}(t,x)|\leq C~~\text{ for }t\in\mathbb{R},~L^{*}\leq x\leq(\Gamma(r_{*})+L^{*})/2,

and thus, for any
\epsilon_{1}\in(0,\varrho]
small enough, we have

\displaystyle V(t,x)>V(t,L^{*})-\varrho/2~~\text{ for }t\in\mathbb{R},\ x\in[L^{*},L^{*}+\epsilon_{1}].

Similarly, one has

\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-x+\epsilon_{1})\leq\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-x)+\varrho/2~~\text{ for }t\in\mathbb{R},\ x\in[L_{0},L^{*}].

Recall that

V(t,x)-\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-x)\geq\chi(L_{0})=\varrho\text{ for }(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times[L_{0},L^{*}].

As a consequence, for
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in[L_{0},L^{*}]
, we readily have

V(t,x)-\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-x+\epsilon_{1})\geq\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-x)+\varrho-\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-x)-\varrho/2\geq\varrho/2>0.

Up to decreasing
\epsilon_{1}
if necessary, we may also assume

\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-x+\epsilon_{1})<\Phi^{*}(t,0)+\varrho/2~~\text{ for }x\in[L^{*},L^{*}+\epsilon_{1}],\ t\in\mathbb{R}.

Hence, for
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in[L^{*},L^{*}+\epsilon_{1}]
, we have

V(t,x)-\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-x+\epsilon_{1})>V(t,L^{*})-\varrho/2-\Phi^{*}(t,0)-\varrho/2\geq 0.

These inequalities, together with (
4.44
), finally give

V(t,x)-\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-x+\epsilon_{1})\geq 0\text{ for }t\in\mathbb{R},\ x\in(-\infty,L^{*}+\epsilon_{1})

for all small
\epsilon_{1}\in(0,\epsilon)
, which violates the definition of
L^{*}
. Therefore,
L^{*}=\Gamma(r_{*})
and the lemma is thus proved.
∎

Proposition 4.6
.

V(t,x)\equiv\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-x)
and
\Gamma(t)\equiv L^{*}
.

Proof.

By (
4.31
) and Lemma
4.5
, we know that
L^{*}=\Gamma(r_{*})=\min_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\Gamma(t)
and

V(t,x)\geq\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-x)\text{ for }t\in\mathbb{R},\ x\leq L^{*}

with

V(r_{*},\Gamma(r_{*}))=\Phi^{*}(r_{*},0)=\delta.

The parabolic strong maximum principle and Hopf boundary lemma then imply that

\text{ either }V(t,x)\equiv\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-x)\text{ or }V_{x}(r_{*},\Gamma(r_{*}))<-\Phi^{*}_{x}(r_{*},0).

By (
4.27
) and the minimality of
\Gamma(r_{*})
,

0=\Gamma^{\prime}(r_{*})=-\frac{d}{\delta}V_{x}(r_{*},\Gamma(r_{*}))-k^{*}(r_{*}),

and hence,

V_{x}(r_{*},\Gamma(r_{*}))=-\frac{\delta}{d}k^{*}(r_{*})=-\Phi_{x}^{*}(r_{*},0).

Thus we necessarily have

(4.45)

V(t,x)\equiv\Phi^{*}(t,L^{*}-x),\text{ which implies }L(t)\equiv L^{*}.

The proof is complete.
∎

4.5.
Completion of the proof of Theorem

We will finish the proof in three steps.

Step 1.

(4.46)

Step 2.
We show that

, which satisfies

To derive a contradiction, we revisit the upper solution constructed in (

Take

,

\begin{cases}\bar{h}^{\prime}(t)\geq-\frac{d}{\delta}\bar{u}_{x}(t,\bar{h}(t))&\text{ for }t\geq t_{n},\\

,

\displaystyle u(t_{n},x)-\Phi^{*}(t_{n},h(t_{n})-x)<q_{0}\text{ for }x\in[0,h(t_{n})].

This, combined with the monotonicity of
\Phi^{*}
and the fact that
\bar{h}(t_{n})\geq h(t_{n})+a\geq h(t_{n})+z_{\delta}(t_{n})
, implies that

\displaystyle u(t_{n},x)<\Phi^{*}(t_{n},\bar{h}(t_{n})-x+z_{\delta}(t_{n}))+q_{0}\text{ for }x\in[0,h(t_{n})],\ n\geq n_{1}.

Moreover, it is clear that
\bar{h}(t_{n})-x\geq h(t_{n})+a
for
g(t_{n})\leq x\leq 0
. Due to
h(t)\to\infty
as
t\to\infty
and
\limsup_{t\to\infty}u(t,x)\leq 1
uniformly in
x\in\mathbb{R}
(by Proposition
2.3
), we can increase
n_{1}
if necessary to obtain

\Phi^{*}(t_{n},\bar{h}(t_{n})-x+z_{\delta}(t_{n}))+q_{0}\geq\Phi^{*}(t_{n},h(t_{n}))+q_{0}\geq 1+\frac{q_{0}}{2}\geq u(t_{n},x)

for
g(t_{n})\leq x\leq 0
and
n\geq n_{1}
.
As a result, we obtain

u(t_{n},x)\leq\bar{u}(t_{n},x)\text{ for }x\in[g(t_{n}),h(t_{n})],\ n\geq n_{1}.

It is evident from the definition of
\bar{u}
that
\bar{u}(t,g(t))>\delta
. Hence recalling (
4.50
) we see that
(\bar{u},g,\bar{h})
with
\tau^{*}=t_{n}
is an upper solution of (
1.2
) for
t\geq t_{n}
,
n\geq n_{1}
, and by the comparison principle it follows that, for such
t
and
n
,

h(t)\leq\bar{h}(t)=\int_{t_{n}}^{t}k^{*}(s)ds+\gamma(1-e^{-\sigma_{0}(t-t_{n})})+h(t_{n})+a+K_{0}.

Hence, for all large
k
satisfying
s_{k}>t_{n}
, we have

h(s_{k})\leq h(t_{n})+\int^{s_{k}}_{t_{n}}k^{*}(s)ds+\gamma(1-e^{-\sigma_{0}(s_{k}-t_{n})})+a+K_{0},

or equivalently,

h(s_{k})-\int_{0}^{s_{k}}k^{*}(s)ds\leq h(t_{n})-\int_{0}^{t_{n}}k^{*}(s)ds+\gamma(1-e^{-\sigma_{0}(s_{k}-t_{n})})+a+K_{0}.

Letting
k\to\infty
and then
n\to\infty
in the above inequality, we obtain by recalling (
4.49
),

\hat{h}-h^{*}\leq\gamma+a+K_{0}\leq(\hat{h}-h^{*})/3.

This is a contradiction, and Step 2 is completed.

Step 3.
We prove that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup_{x\in[0,h(t)]}|u(t,x)-\Phi^{*}(t,h(t)-x)|=0
.

By Step 2 for any given sequence
\{t_{n}\}
satisfying
t_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\rho(t_{n})=\liminf_{t\to\infty}\rho(t).

This allows us to use Lemma
4.4
to conclude that (
4.47
) holds for some subsequence of
\{t_{n}\}
. The arbitrariness of
\{t_{n}\}
thus implies

\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup_{x\in[0,h(t)]}|u(t,x)-\Phi^{*}(t,h(t)-x)|=0.

Symmetrically, we can prove

\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup_{x\in[g(t),0]}|u(t,x)-\Phi^{*}(t,x-g(t))|=0.

The proof is now complete.
\Box

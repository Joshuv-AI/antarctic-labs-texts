Exploring the utility and limitations of ChatGPT in scientific literature searches
Authors: C. Corti, G. Castellano, G. Curigliano
Published: 2023

In the past year, ChatGPT has surged in popularity as a tool for education and research due to its remarkable capabilities.1,2 Leveraging artificial intelligence (AI), ChatGPT showcases a broad skill set, crafting original content, addressing queries, and assembling coherent sentences from inputs.3,4 It additionally refines itself through self-correction.5

Research demands time, dedication, and expertise, and the public version of ChatGPT is just the tip of the generative AI iceberg for investigators. For example, ChatGPT holds potential for ‘gray’ literature exploration in systematic reviews. Currently, uncertainties surround optimal methods for conducting internet-based searches of ‘gray’ literature, lacking a standardized approach. Here, we share our cancer field experience and caution the readers about the potential risks of employing this tool for scientific research.

Poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase inhibitors (PARPi) revolutionized cancer care. To clarify whether patients treated with PARPi for metastatic solid tumors are either at increased or decreased risk of thromboembolic events, we conducted a systematic review of the literature.6 To find additional ‘gray’ literature, we used ChatGPT in addition to our systematic search.

Firstly, we asked ChatGPT: “Is there a thromboembolic risk for PARP inhibitor administration in patients with metastatic cancer?”. The answer: “There is a known association between inhibitor administration and thromboembolic events in some patients with metastatic cancer. The exact risk can vary depending on the specific drug, the cancer type, and other individual patient factors. Patients receiving PARP inhibitors should be closely monitored for signs and symptoms of thromboembolic events” (14 February 2023).

ChatGPT’s response captured our interest due to the limited availability of published studies on the topic. Subsequently, we posed the question, “Can you provide references to scientific articles with evidence of the increased thromboembolic risk associated with the use of PARP inhibitors?”. The initial query was a part of our endeavor to investigate gray literature concerning poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase inhibitors and the risk of thromboembolic events. Gray literature, encompassing materials not commercially published, including academic papers not identified in our systematic search, theses, research reports, and government publications, can substantially improve systematic reviews. While ChatGPT hasn’t undergone comprehensive scientific research training, several artificial intelligence-based tools specifically trained for scientific literature searches are currently in development, with some already yielding promising results.11

We questioned the absence of these references in our search and opted to examine the articles suggested by ChatGPT. However, we were unable to find them. Through additional inquiry, we unveiled that the three recommended articles were not genuine, but forged references. These articles were fabricated by amalgamating portions of various studies, rendering none of them legitimately published. Consequently, it is evident that ChatGPT lacked reliability as a tool for exploring the gray literature.7

In February 2023, the developer elucidated the mechanisms influencing ChatGPT’s behavior, acknowledging potential biases, along with outlined plans for enhancing its performance.8 Due to these factors, we aimed to ascertain whether the promised enhancement of ChatGPT’s competence in scientific bibliographic searches has been implemented. Therefore, in August 2023, we re-queried ChatGPT using the identical question, yielding a different response (Table 1).

ChatGPT exhibits impressive capabilities, and we recognize the developer’s endeavors to ameliorate bias in data and prevent reference falsification for scientific objectives. Nonetheless, ChatGPT users should exercise prudence when using a tool without specific scientific domain training for current scientific inquiries.1,2,9 Readers are advised to remain vigilant regarding the potential for data falsification within the domain of AI.10

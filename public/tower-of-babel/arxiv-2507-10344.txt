1.
Introduction

The classical Sobolev inequality by Sobolev
[
27
]
states that for any
1<q<n
there exists a positive constant
C(n,q)
such that

(1.1)

\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u^{\frac{nq}{n-q}}dx\big)^{\frac{n-q}{nq}}\leq C(n,q)\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{q}dx\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

for every function
u\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

A natural extension of (
1.1
) is in the framework of fractional Sobolev space
W^{\alpha,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In this setting, the following fractional Sobolev inequality holds, for any
1<q<\frac{n}{\alpha}
and
0<\alpha<1
,

(1.2)

\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u^{\frac{nq}{n-\alpha q}}dx\big)^{\frac{n-\alpha q}{nq}}\leq C(n,\alpha,q)\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{n+\alpha q}}dydx\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

for all
u\in W^{\alpha,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. There are a large number of references in the literature studying the above inequality with different approaches, see
[
8
]
,
[
21
]
, and
[
24
]
.

The general idea described here had been motivated by
[
19
]
. In particular, the author showed that (
1.1
) remains to hold for
p-1<q<1
(sublinear case) when restricted to a class of positive
p
-superharmonic (
1<p<2
) functions
u
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\inf\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u=0.
The techniques in
[
19
]
are based on the global pointwise estimates in terms of Wolff potential, as established by Kilpeläinen and Malý
[
13
]
. Furthermore, the authors in
[
20
]
proved that the uniqueness of
\mathcal{A}
-superharmonic solution to the equation

-\text{div}\mathcal{A}(x,\nabla u)=\sigma\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n},

under the conditions involving the weak integrability of the gradient of the solution, utilizing the sublinear Sobolev inequality (see
[
20
, Theorem 3.12]
. In addition, Chua and Phuc extended the sublinear Sobolev inequality for any superharmonic functions on the generalized John domain, see
[
6
, Corollary 5.7]
.

Although the sublinear Sobolev inequality in
[
19
]
holds for a more general class of positive
p
-superharmonic functions, the author in
[
28
]
pointed out that the classical Sobolev inequality fails for all functions
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
0<q<1
by providing a counterexample.

This paper aims to establish that the sublinear fractional Sobolev inequality of the type (
1.2
) under Lorentz norm holds when the Sobolev exponent
p-1<q<1
for a class of
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic functions
u
on
\Omega
with
u=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
. We demonstrate that a similar sublinear fractional Sobolev inequality remains valid when Lorentz norm on both sides are replaced by
L^{p}
norm, providing a fractional analogue of the result in the
[
19
]
. More generally, we also consider fractional Sobolev inequality with Muckenhoupt weight in the sublinear case (see Theorem
4.6
).

In this paper, we consider a nonlocal operator with nonlinear growth defined on an open and bounded subset
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For
0<\alpha<1,p>1
and
\Lambda\geq 1
,

\mathcal{L}u(x)=2\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(x,\varepsilon)}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))k(x,y)dy,\;\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

where the kernel
k:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty),
which is a symmetric measurable function satisfying the following condition:

\Lambda^{-1}|x-y|^{-n-\alpha p}\leq k(x,y)\leq\Lambda|x-y|^{-n-\alpha p}.

In the case
k(x,y)=|x-y|^{-n-\alpha p},
this operator is modeled on the fractional
p
-Laplacian,
(-\Delta_{p})^{\alpha}.
Notably, in the linear nonlocal case when
p=2
and
\Lambda=1,
this reduces to the pure fractional Laplacian operator
(-\Delta)^{\alpha}
. We refer to
[
18
]
for the introduction of this operator.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be an open bounded subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
where
n\geq 1
. Let
0<\alpha<1,p-1\leq t\leq\infty,1<p<2
and
p-1<q<1.
For any
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic function
u
on
\Omega
with
u=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
, we have

(1.3)

\|u\|_{L^{\frac{nq}{n-\alpha q},t}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C[u]_{W^{\alpha,(q,t)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

where
C=C(n,\alpha,p,q,t,\Lambda)>0.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is given in Section
3
.

Organization of the paper

This paper is organized into three sections. In section
2
, we give some background information on
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic, a review of the theory of the Triebel-Lizorkin-Lorentz spaces, and some useful theorems in our work. The section
3
is devoted to the proof of the main theorem. We discuss further results related to the fractional Sobolev inequality in the final section.

2.
Preliminaries

Function spaces

To state our results, let us introduce some definitions and notation. Let
\Omega
be an bounded open set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
denote the class of all nonnegative finite Borel measures on
\Omega
. We denote
\rho(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\rho^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as the Schwartz space of all complex-valued rapidly decreasing infinitely differentiable functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the set of all tempered distributions, respectively.

Let
0<p<\infty,

1\leq r\leq\infty
and
\Omega
be an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
L^{p,r}
denote the
Lorentz space
of a measurable function
u,
which is a quasi-Banach space equipped with the quasi-norm

\|u\|_{L^{p,r}(\Omega)}=\begin{cases}\Big(p\int_{0}^{\infty}[\beta\mu_{u}(\beta)^{\frac{1}{p}}]^{r}\frac{d\beta}{\beta}\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}\quad\text{if}\quad p,r<\infty,\\
\sup\limits_{\beta>0}\beta\mu_{u}(\beta)^{\frac{1}{p}}\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\;\text{if}\quad p<\infty,q=\infty,\\
\inf\{\beta>0:\mu_{u}(\beta)=0\}\;\;\;\text{if}\quad p=r=\infty,\end{cases}

where
\mu_{u}
denotes the distribution function of
u:\mu_{u}(\beta)=|\{x\in\Omega:|u(x)|>\beta\}|.

For
0<\alpha<1,

0<p<\infty,
and
1\leq r\leq\infty
, we define the
fractional Sobolev-Lorentz space

W^{\alpha,(p,r)}
as follows

W^{\alpha,(p,r)}(\Omega)=\{u\in L^{p,r}(\Omega):\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{\frac{n}{p}+\alpha}}\in L^{p,r}(\Omega\times\Omega)\}.

It is a quasi-Banach space and associated with this norm is called the
Gagliardo seminorm

[u]_{W^{\alpha,(p,r)}(\Omega)}=\Big\|\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{\frac{n}{p}+\alpha}}\Big\|_{L^{p,r}(\Omega\times\Omega)}.

We refer to readers
[
10
]
and
[
17
]
for more basic properties of fractional Sobolev space.

Next, we recall the standard definition of Triebel-Lizorkin-Lorentz space. To define the space, we choose a sequence of test functions
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}\subset\rho
such that

(i)

there exist positive constants
A,B,C
and

\begin{cases}\mathrm{supp}\;\phi_{0}\subset\{x|\;|x|\leq A\},\\
\mathrm{supp}\;\phi_{j}\subset\{x|B2^{j-1}\leq|x|\leq C2^{j+1}\}\quad\text{if}\quad j=1,2,3,\dots,\end{cases}

(ii)

for every multi-index
\alpha
there exists a positive cumber
c_{\alpha}
and

\sup_{x}\sup_{j=0,1,\dots}2^{j|\alpha|}|D^{\alpha}\phi_{j}(x)|\leq c_{\alpha},

(iii)

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\phi_{j}(x)=1\quad\text{for every}\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
s
be real,
0<q,r\leq\infty
and
0<p<\infty
. The
Triebel-Lizorkin-Lorentz space

{F}^{\alpha}_{q}[L^{p,r}]
is the space of all tempered distributions
f
such that

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{j\alpha q}|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\phi_{j}(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi))|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty.

Clearly, in the case
p=r
, Definition
2.1
covers the usual Triebel-Lizorkin spaces
{F}^{\alpha}_{q}[L^{p,p}](\mathbb{R}^{n})=F^{\alpha}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Remark 2.2
.

The equivalent quasi-norms of the Triebel-Lizorkin norm are as follows:

(i)

\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|\cdot\|_{F^{0}_{p,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
if
1<p<\infty,

(ii)

\|\cdot\|_{W^{\alpha,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|\cdot\|_{F^{\alpha}_{p,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
if
1\leq p<\infty,0<\alpha\neq
integer,

(iii)

\|\cdot\|_{L^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|\cdot\|_{F^{0}_{2}[L^{p,r}](\mathbb{R}^{n})}
if
1<p<\infty
and
0<r\leq\infty.

These equivalences in the Triebel-Lizorkin and Triebel-Lizorkin Lorentz spaces have been studied by Triebel
[
29
]
, Runst-Sickel
[
23
]
, as well as Yang-Cheng-Bang
[
31
]
.

Theorem 2.3

(
S
ee
[
25
, Theorem 1.6]
)
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{R},0<p_{1},p_{2}<\infty,0<q_{1},q_{2},r_{1},r_{2}\leq\infty.
The embedding

\|\cdot\|_{F^{\alpha_{2}}_{q_{2}}[L^{{p_{2},r_{2}}}](\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|\cdot\|_{F^{\alpha_{1}}_{q_{1}}[L^{{p_{1},r_{1}}}](\mathbb{R}^{n})}

if and only if the condition is satisfied

\quad\alpha_{1}-\frac{n}{p_{1}}=\alpha_{2}-\frac{n}{p_{2}},r_{1}\leq r_{2}.

These results can be found in Han
[
11
, Theorem]
, Jawerth
[
12
, Theorem 2.1]
and Sickel-Triebel
[
26
, Theorem 3.2.1]
for the case
p=r.

\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic function

We denote the positive part and negative one of a real-valued function
u
by
u_{+}:=\max\{u,0\}
and
u_{-}:=\max\{-u,0\},
respectively.

Definition 2.4
.

A function
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(-\infty,\infty]
is said to be
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic
in
\Omega
if it satisfies the following properties:

(i)

u<+\infty
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n},

(ii)

u
is lower semicontinuous in
\Omega,

(iii)

for each
D\Subset\Omega
and each weak solution
v\in\mathcal{C}(\bar{D})
of
\mathcal{L}v=0
in
D
with
v_{+}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
u\geq v
on
\partial D,
it holds that
u\geq v
in
D,

(iv)

u_{-}\in L^{p-1}_{\alpha p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
where the tail space
L^{p-1}_{\alpha p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined as

L^{p-1}_{\alpha p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\Big\{u\in L^{p-1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(y)|^{p-1}}{(1+|y|)^{n+\alpha p}}dy<+\infty\Big\}.

The notation
D\Subset\Omega
means that
\bar{D}
is a compact subset of
\Omega
.

Definition 2.5
.

A function
u\in W_{\text{loc}}^{\alpha,p}(\Omega)
such that
u_{-}
belongs
L^{p-1}_{\alpha p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a weak
\mathcal{L}
-supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=0

if

<\mathcal{L}u,\phi>=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))(\phi(x)-\phi(y))k(x,y)dxdy\geq 0

for every nonnegative
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega).

Definition 2.6
.

A function
u
is said to be
\mathcal{L}
-harmonic in
\Omega
if it is a weak solution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
that is continuous in
\Omega
.

Theorem 2.7

(
S
ee
[
15
]
)
.

Let
u
be a weak supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
. Then

u(x)=\mathrm{ess}\liminf_{{y\to x}}u(y)

for a.e.
x\in\Omega
. In particular,
u
has a representative that is lower semicontinuous in
\Omega
.

Theorem 2.8

(
S
ee
[
15
]
)
.

If
u
is an
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic function in
\Omega,
then

u(x)=\liminf_{{{y\to x}}}u(y)=\mathrm{ess}\liminf_{{y\to x}}u(y)\quad\text{for every}\quad x\in\Omega.

If, in addition,
u
is locally bounded in
\Omega
or
u\in W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\alpha,p}(\Omega)
, then
u
is a weak supersolution in
\Omega
.

Theorem 2.9

(
S
ee
[
15
]
)
.

If
u
is a weak supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
that is lower semicontinuous satisfying

u(x)=\mathrm{ess}\liminf_{{y\to x}}u(y)

for every
x\in\Omega
, then
u
is
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic in
\Omega
.

Corollary 2.10
.

If
u
is a supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
, then there is a superharmonic function
v
in
\Omega
such that
u=v
a.e.

Theorem 2.11

(
S
ee
[
14
]
)
.

Let
u
be superharmonic in
\Omega
. Then there exists a unique nonnegative locally finite Borel measure
\mu
in
\Omega
such that

\mathcal{L}u=\mu

in the sense of distribution, that is,

<\mathcal{L}u,\phi>=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\phi\ d\mu

holds whenever
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
, if
u=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
.

The measure
\mu
given in the above Theorem
2.11
is called
Riesz measure
.

Some recent results on potentials

The proof of main Theorem
1.1
is based on pointwise estimates of
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic functions in terms of Wolff potentials. We recall the definition of Wolff potential with fractional orders (see
[
1
]
,
[
16
]
).

For
\alpha>0,p>1
such that
\alpha p<n,
the
Wolff potential

\mathbf{W}_{\alpha,p}\sigma(x)
is defined by

\mathbf{W}_{\alpha,p}\sigma(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[\frac{\sigma(B(x,r))}{r^{n-\alpha p}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\frac{dr}{r},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

where
\sigma\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In the case when
p=2
, it reduces (up to a normalization constant) to the Riesz potential of order
\alpha
for
0<\alpha<n
, which can be defined as follows

\mathbf{I}_{\alpha}\sigma(x)=C(n,\alpha)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\sigma(B(x,r))}{r^{n-\alpha}}\frac{dr}{r},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

with a normalization constant

C(n,\alpha)=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n-\alpha}{2})}{2^{\alpha}\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}\Gamma\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

The Riesz potential satisfies the following semigroup property

\mathbf{I}_{\alpha}\mathbf{I}_{\beta}f=\mathbf{I}_{\alpha+\beta}f,\quad\text{for}\quad\alpha,\beta>0,\alpha+\beta<n.

Remark 2.12
.

The Riesz potential
\mathbf{I}_{\alpha}
is an isomorphism from
F^{s}_{q}[L^{p,r}]
onto
F^{s-\alpha}_{q}[L^{p,r}]
and

(2.1)

\|\mathbf{I}_{\alpha}f\|_{F^{s}_{q}[L^{p,r}]}=\|f\|_{F^{s-\alpha}_{q}[L^{p,r}]},

see
[
2
]
.

In our case, we will use the following elementary inequality when we deduce from Wolff potential to Riesz potential: for every
R>0
,

\Big(\int_{0}^{R}\big(\frac{\phi(r)}{r^{\gamma}}\big)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\frac{dr}{r}\Big)^{p-1}\leq c(p,\gamma)\int_{0}^{2R}\frac{\phi(r)}{r^{\gamma}}\frac{dr}{r},

where
\gamma>0,1<p<2,
and
\phi
is a non-decreasing function on
(0,\infty)
. In particular, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{\alpha,p}\sigma(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[\frac{\sigma(B(x,r))}{r^{n-\alpha p}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\frac{dr}{r}

\displaystyle\leq c\Big[\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sigma(B(x,r))}{r^{n-\alpha p}}\frac{dr}{r}\Big]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}=c[\mathbf{I}_{\alpha p}\sigma(x)]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Theorem 2.13

(
S
ee
[
14
]
)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N},\ 0<\alpha<1
and
1<p\leq\frac{n}{\alpha}
. Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
where
\Omega
is an bounded open set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
u
is an
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic function
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
u=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
. If
\mathcal{L}u=\mu,
then there exists
C=C(n,p,q,\alpha)>0
such that

C^{-1}\mathbf{W}_{\alpha,p}\mu(x)\leq u(x)\leq C\mathbf{W}_{\alpha,p}\mu(x)\quad\text{for all}\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Proof.

Denote by
\mu=\mu[u]
the Riesz measure associated with the
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic function
u
. Employing Theorem
2.13
and the inequality (
2.2
), we find that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\frac{nq}{n-\alpha q},t}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq c\|{\bf{W}}_{\alpha,p}\mu(x)\|_{L^{\frac{nq}{n-\alpha q},t}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\|[{\bf{I}}_{\alpha p}\mu(x)]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|_{L^{\frac{nq}{n-\alpha q},t}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\|{\bf{I}}_{\alpha p}\mu(x)\|_{L^{\frac{nq}{(n-\alpha q)(p-1)},\frac{t}{p-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

By the seminorm property of Riesz potential, we have

\|u\|_{L^{\frac{nq}{n-\alpha q},t}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|{\bf{I}}_{\alpha(p-1)}({\bf{I}}_{\alpha}\mu(x))\|_{L^{\frac{nq}{(n-\alpha q)(p-1)},\frac{t}{p-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

The integral on the right-hand side of the preceding inequality can be written as the Triebel-Lizorkin-Lorentz norm
[
29
, Proposition 2.3.5]
, we get

\displaystyle\|{\bf{I}}_{\alpha(p-1)}({\bf{I}}_{\alpha}\mu(x))\|_{L^{\frac{nq}{(n-\alpha q)(p-1)},\frac{t}{p-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\|{\bf{I}}_{\alpha(p-1)}({\bf{I}}_{\alpha}\mu(x))\|_{F^{0}_{2}[L^{\frac{nq}{(n-\alpha q)(p-1)},\frac{t}{p-1}}](\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\|{\bf{I}}_{\alpha}\mu(x)\|_{F^{-\alpha(p-1)}_{2}[L^{\frac{nq}{(n-\alpha q)(p-1)},\frac{t}{p-1}}](\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Then, we apply the embedding Theorem
2.3
, the last display implies that

\|{\bf{I}}_{\alpha}\mu(x)\|_{F^{-\alpha(p-1)}_{2}[L^{\frac{nq}{(n-\alpha q)(p-1)},\frac{t}{p-1}}](\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|{\bf{I}}_{\alpha}\mu(x)\|_{F^{0}_{\frac{q}{p-1}}[L^{\frac{q}{p-1},\frac{t}{p-1}}](\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

We have a fact that the seminorm in Triebel-Lizorkin-Lorentz spaces can be characterized by a supremum over test functions
\phi\in F^{0}_{(\frac{q}{p-1})^{\prime}}[L^{(\frac{q}{p-1})^{\prime},(\frac{t}{p-1})^{\prime}}]
where
(\frac{q}{p-1})^{\prime}=\frac{q}{q-p+1}
and
(\frac{t}{p-1})^{\prime}=\frac{t}{t-p+1},
we get

\displaystyle\|{\bf{I}}_{\alpha}\mu(x)\|_{F^{0}_{\frac{q}{p-1}}[L^{\frac{q}{p-1},\frac{t}{p-1}}](\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\sup_{[\phi]_{F^{0}_{(\frac{q}{p-1})^{\prime}}[L^{(\frac{q}{p-1})^{\prime},(\frac{t}{p-1})^{\prime}}]}\leq 1}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf{I}}_{\alpha}\mu(x)\ \phi dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{[\phi]_{F^{0}_{(\frac{q}{p-1})^{\prime}}[L^{(\frac{q}{p-1})^{\prime},(\frac{t}{p-1})^{\prime}}]}\leq 1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|({\bf{I}}_{\alpha}\phi)(x)|\ d\mu

\displaystyle=\sup_{[\phi]_{F^{0}_{(\frac{q}{p-1})^{\prime}}[L^{(\frac{q}{p-1})^{\prime},(\frac{t}{p-1})^{\prime}}]}\leq 1}<\mathcal{L}u,{\bf{I}}_{\alpha}\phi>

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-1}|{\bf{I}}_{\alpha}\phi(x)-{\bf{I}}_{\alpha}\phi(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+\alpha p}}dxdy

\displaystyle=\Lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{(\frac{n}{q}+\alpha)(p-1)}}\frac{|{\bf{I}}_{\alpha}\phi(x)-{\bf{I}}_{\alpha}\phi(y)|}{|x-y|^{\frac{n(q-p+1)}{q}+\alpha}}dxdy.

Applying the Hölder’s inequality in the Lorentz space with the exponents
\frac{q}{p-1},\frac{t}{p-1},(\frac{q}{p-1})^{\prime}
and
(\frac{t}{p-1})^{\prime}
respectively, we obtain

\displaystyle\|{\bf{I}}_{\alpha}\mu(x)\|_{F^{0}_{\frac{q}{p-1}}[L^{\frac{q}{p-1},\frac{t}{p-1}}](\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{(\frac{n}{q}+\alpha)(p-1)}}\Big\|_{L^{\frac{q}{p-1},\frac{t}{p-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\quad\;\Big\|\frac{|{\bf{I}}_{\alpha}\phi(x)-{\bf{I}}_{\alpha}\phi(y)|}{|x-y|^{\frac{n(q-p+1)}{q}+\alpha}}\Big\|_{L^{(\frac{q}{p-1})^{\prime},(\frac{t}{p-1})^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Then, we find that

\displaystyle\|{\bf{I}}_{\alpha}\mu(x)\|_{F^{0}_{\frac{q}{p-1}}[L^{\frac{q}{p-1},\frac{t}{p-1}}](\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{\frac{n}{q}+\alpha}}\Big\|_{L^{q,t}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p-1}

\displaystyle\quad\;\Big\|\frac{|{\bf{I}}_{\alpha}\phi(x)-{\bf{I}}_{\alpha}\phi(y)|}{|x-y|^{\frac{n(q-p+1)}{q}+\alpha}}\Big\|_{L^{(\frac{q}{p-1})^{\prime},(\frac{t}{p-1})^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=[u]_{W^{\alpha,(q,t)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p-1}[{\bf{I}}_{\alpha}\phi]_{W^{\alpha,((\frac{q}{p-1})^{\prime},(\frac{t}{p-1})^{\prime})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Hence, we conclude the desired estimate

\|u\|_{L^{\frac{nq}{n-\alpha q},t}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(n,\alpha,p,q,t,\Lambda)[u]_{W^{\alpha,(q,t)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

∎

4.
Further Results

In this section, we present additional results derived from our methods.
Observe that
L^{\frac{nq}{n-\alpha q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset L^{\frac{nq}{n-\alpha q},t}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
t>\frac{nq}{n-\alpha q}
. As an immediate consequence of (
1.3
), we get the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Omega
be an open bounded subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
where
n\geq 1
. Let
0<\alpha<1,1<p<2
and
p-1<q<1.
For any
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic function
u
on
\Omega
with
u=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
, we have

\|u\|_{L^{\frac{nq}{n-\alpha q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C[u]_{W^{\alpha,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

where
C=C(n,\alpha,p,q,\Lambda)>0.

This inequality follows directly from the inequality (
1.3
) by setting
\frac{nq}{n-\alpha q}<t\leq\infty
. Since the proof structure is the same as the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we omit a full proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Remark 4.2
.

We observe that the sublinear fractional Sobolev inequality established from Theorem
4.1
corresponds to the classical result of
[
19
]
by Phuc, in light of the following result:

\liminf\limits_{\alpha\to 1^{-}}(1-\alpha)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{n+\alpha q}}dxdy=C(n,p)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{q}dx

as shown in
[
17
, Theorem 7.39]
.

The classical Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation has been widely extended to the fractional setting, playing a fundamental role in the study of intermediate regularity between Sobolev-type spaces. In particular, a refined version involving the borderline Sobolev space
W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
was established by
[
7
]
, where the inequality

(4.1)

[u]_{W^{\alpha_{1},{q_{1}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq[u]_{W^{1,{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1-\theta}[u]_{W^{\alpha_{3},{q_{3}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\theta},\quad\forall u\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

holds for every
\theta\in(0,1),
provided that
\alpha_{3}\in(0,1),

q_{3}\in(1,\infty)
and the condition
\alpha_{3}q_{3}<1
is satisfied. The interpolation indices are given by the convexity relations:

\alpha_{1}=\theta\alpha_{3}+(1-\theta)\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{q_{1}}=\frac{\theta}{q_{3}}+(1-\theta).

However, as shown in
[
3
]
, such interpolation (
4.1
) fails when
\alpha_{3}q_{3}\geq 1.
Recent studies
[
4
]
have explored such interpolation inequalities in different spaces like Lorentz spaces, weak
L^{p}
-spaces or weighted Lebesgue spaces. In what follows, we turn our attention to the Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation that may hold for fractional derivatives in Lorentz spaces.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\alpha_{i}\in(0,1)
,
1<p<2,

q_{i}\in(p-1,1)
and
t_{i}\in(p-1,\infty]
for
i=1,2,3.
Assume
0<\theta<1
satisfies the following conditions

\alpha_{1}=(1-\theta)\alpha_{2}+\theta\alpha_{3},\,\,\frac{1}{q_{1}}=\frac{1-\theta}{q_{2}}+\frac{\theta}{q_{3}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{t_{1}}=\frac{1-\theta}{t_{2}}+\frac{\theta}{t_{3}}.

Then the interpolation inequality

\|u\|_{L^{\frac{nq_{1}}{n-\alpha_{1}q_{1}},t_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq[u]_{W^{\alpha_{2},{(q_{2},t_{2})}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1-\theta}[u]_{W^{\alpha_{3},{(q_{3},t_{3})}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\theta}

with holds for all
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic functions
u\in W^{\alpha_{2},(q_{2},t_{2})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap W^{\alpha_{3},(q_{3},t_{3})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
u=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega.

Proof.

Assume that
u
is
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic functions in
W^{\alpha_{2},(q_{2},t_{2})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap W^{\alpha_{3},(q_{3},t_{3})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then by Theorem
1.1
, we have

\|u\|_{L^{\frac{nq_{1}}{n-\alpha_{1}q_{1}},t_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\Big\|\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{\frac{n}{q}+\alpha}}\Big\|_{L^{q_{1},t_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Choosing the Hölder exponents
A=\frac{q_{1}q_{3}}{q_{3}-\theta q_{1}},A^{\prime}=\frac{q_{3}}{\theta},B=\frac{t_{1}t_{3}}{t_{3}-\theta t_{1}},
and
B^{\prime}=\frac{t_{3}}{\theta}
such that

\frac{1}{q_{1}}=\frac{1}{A}+\frac{1}{A^{\prime}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{t_{1}}=\frac{1}{B}+\frac{1}{B^{\prime}}

where
0<q_{i},t_{i}\leq\infty
for
i=1,2,3
, we estimate the left-hand side of the above display. Hence, we find that

\displaystyle\Big\|\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{\frac{n}{q}+\alpha}}\Big\|_{L^{q_{1},t_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{1-\theta}}{|x-y|^{\frac{1-\theta}{q_{2}}n+(1-\theta)\alpha_{2}}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{\theta}}{|x-y|^{\frac{\theta}{q_{3}}n+\theta\alpha_{3}}}\Big\|_{L^{q_{1},t_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{1-\theta}}{|x-y|^{\frac{1-\theta}{q_{2}}n+(1-\theta)\alpha_{2}}}\Big\|_{L^{\frac{q_{1}q_{3}}{q_{3}-\theta q_{1}},\frac{t_{1}t_{3}}{t_{3}-\theta t_{1}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\qquad\Big\|\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{\theta}}{|x-y|^{\frac{\theta}{q_{3}}n+\theta\alpha_{3}}}\Big\|_{L^{\frac{q_{3}}{\theta},\frac{t_{3}}{\theta}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{\frac{n}{q_{2}}+\alpha_{2}}}\Big\|^{1-\theta}_{L^{q_{2},t_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\qquad\Big\|\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{\frac{n}{q_{3}}+\alpha_{3}}}\Big\|^{\theta}_{L^{q_{3},t_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}

where
q_{2}=\frac{q_{1}q_{3}(1-\theta)}{q_{3}-\theta q_{1}}
,
t_{2}=\frac{t_{1}t_{3}(1-\theta)}{t_{3}-\theta t_{1}}
,
q_{3}=\frac{q_{1}q_{2}\theta}{q_{2}-(1-\theta)q_{1}}
and
t_{3}=\frac{t_{1}t_{2}\theta}{t_{2}-(1-\theta)t_{1}}
.
This completes the proof of Theorem
4.3
.
∎

Following the arguments in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, one can extend Theorem
1.1
to the weighted version of the inequality.
We collect some definitions and embedding results on Muckenhoupt classes
\mathcal{A}_{p}
.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
w
be a positive, locally integrable function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
1<p<\infty
. Then
w
belongs to the Muckenhoupt class
\mathcal{A}_{p}
, if there exists a constant
0<A<\infty
such that for all balls
B
the following inequality holds:

\Big(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w(x)dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\cdot\Big(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w(x)^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\leq A,

where
|B|
stands for the Lebesgue measure of the ball
B
.

The Muckenhoupt class
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
is given by

\mathcal{A}_{\infty}=\bigcup_{p>1}\mathcal{A}_{p}.

Theorem 4.5

(
S
ee
[
22
]
)
.

Let
0<p<\infty,

\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
w\in\mathcal{A}_{\infty}.

(1)

If
0<q_{0}\leq q_{1}\leq\infty,
then

{F^{\alpha}_{p,q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},dw)}\hookrightarrow{F^{\alpha}_{p,q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},dw)}.

(2)

Assume that there are numbers
c>0
and
d>0
, such that for all balls,

w(B(x,r))\geq cr^{d},\quad 0<r\leq 1,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Let
0<p_{1}<p_{2}<\infty,-\infty<\alpha_{2}<\alpha_{1}<\infty
, with

\alpha_{1}-\frac{d}{p_{1}}=\alpha_{2}-\frac{d}{p_{2}}.

Then

{F^{\alpha_{1}}_{p_{1},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},dw)}\hookrightarrow{F^{\alpha_{2}}_{p_{2},q}(\mathbb{R}^{n},dw)}.

Here,
d
is the scaling exponent of the weight function
w
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\Omega
be an open bounded subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
where
n\geq 1
. Let
0<\alpha<1,1<p<2
and
p-1<q<1.
Let
w\in\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
and there are numbers
c,d>0
such that

w(B(x,r))\geq cr^{d},\quad 0<r\leq 1,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

For any
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic function
u
on
\Omega
with
u=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
, we have

\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u^{\frac{dq}{d-\alpha q}}w(x)dx\big)^{\frac{d-\alpha q}{dq}}\leq C\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha q}}w(y)w(x)dydx\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

where
C=C(d,\alpha,p,q,\Lambda)>0.

Proof.

The theorem can be proved using the arguments in the proof of Theorem
1.1
together with Theorem
4.5
.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

The above inequality is a natural extension from the unweighted case
w=1
, whereas
d=n
.

It is well known that the classical Poincaré inequality holds for a bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega
. Specifically, if
1\leq q<n,
then there exists a positive constant
C(\Omega)
such that inequality

(4.2)

\inf_{a\in\mathbb{R}}\Big(\int_{\Omega}|x-a|^{\frac{nq}{n-q}}dx\Big)^{\frac{n-q}{q}}\leq C(\Omega)\Big(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{q}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

holds for all
u\in W^{1,q}(\Omega).
However, Buckley and Koskela
[
5
]
provided a counterexample that this Poincaré inequality is not valid in the sublinear case
0<q<1,
even if
\frac{nq}{n-q}
is small,
\Omega
is a ball, and
u
is a smooth function. Nevertheless, Chua and Phuc
[
6
]
could prove this inequality holds in the sublinear case when restricted to the class of superharmonic functions. In particular, the main result of
[
6
]
, states that for
\Omega
being a bounded or unbounded John domain, the inequality

\inf_{a\in\mathbb{R}}\Big(\int_{\Omega}|x-a|^{\frac{nq}{n-q}}wdx\Big)^{\frac{n-q}{q}}\leq C(\Omega)\Big(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{q}vdx\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

holds for the class of
\mathcal{A}
-superharmonic functions, where
w
and
v
are suitable weight functions.

In the context of the nonlocal level, the following fractional Sobolev-Poincaré inequality has been established, given by

(4.3)

\inf_{a\in\mathbb{R}}\Big(\int_{\Omega}|x-a|^{\frac{nq}{n-\alpha q}}dx\Big)^{\frac{n-\alpha q}{nq}}\leq C\Big(\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}{\frac{{|u(x)-u(y)|}^{q}}{{|x-y|}^{n+\alpha q}}}dydx\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

for
u\in L^{q}(\Omega)
under the conditions
0<\alpha<1,2\leq 1<\frac{n}{\alpha}
, assuming that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying the measure density condition. On the other hand, if
\Omega
is John domain, then there exists a positive constant
C(n,\alpha,\delta,c,q)
such that

\inf_{a\in\mathbb{R}}\Big(\int_{\Omega}|x-a|^{\frac{nq}{n-\alpha q}}dx\Big)^{\frac{n-\alpha q}{nq}}\leq C\Big(\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega\cap B(x,\delta d(x,\partial\Omega))}{\frac{{|u(x)-u(y)|}^{q}}{{|x-y|}^{n+\alpha q}}}dydx\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

holds for every
u\in L^{1}(\Omega).
However, this improved version of the fractional Sobolev-Poincaré inequality fails to hold for all bounded domains that satisfy the measure density. A counterexample has been found in
[
9
]
.

Remark 4.8
.

It is worth noting that the Hessian Sobolev inequality

\|u\|_{L^{\frac{n(k+1)}{n-2k}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\Big(\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})}|u|F_{k}[u]dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{k+1}}

can be derived by following the argument from Theorem
1.1
, with the choices
\alpha=\frac{2k}{k+1}
and
p=k+1,
where
1\leq k<\frac{n}{2}.
Here,

F_{k}[u]
is the Hessian operator with
k=1,2,\dots,n
defined by

F_{k}[u]=S_{k}(\lambda(D^{2}u)),

where
\lambda(D^{2}u)=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
are the eigenvalues of the Hessian matrix of second-order partial derivatives
D^{2}u,
and
S_{k}
is the
k^{th}
symmetric function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

S_{k}(\lambda)=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}}.

The reader can see the corresponding result and its derivation using duality and potential theory in
[
30
]
.

Open Problem

From the authors’ knowledge, the validity of the fractional Poincaré inequality for superharmonic functions in the sublinear case remains an open problem. While the classical Poincaré inequality has been established for superharmonic functions in the local setting (see
[
6
]
), its nonlocal counterpart, particularly in the framework of fractional Sobolev spaces, has not yet been fully explored. Specifically, given a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},
we ask whether there exists a constant
C
such that the fractional Sobolev-Poincaré inequality

\inf_{a\in\mathbb{R}}\Bigg(\int_{\Omega}|x-a|^{\frac{nq}{n-\alpha q}}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{n-\alpha q}{nq}}\leq C\Bigg(\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{n+\alpha q}}dydx\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

holds for the class of
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic functions
u
, particularly when
p-1<q<1
and
0<\alpha<1.

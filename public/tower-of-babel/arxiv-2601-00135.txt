Generalised Fermat equations in dense variables over finite fields and rings
Sam Chow and Zi Li Lim and Akshat Mudgal

Abstract
. Let A A be a sufficiently dense subset of a finite field 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} or a finite, cyclic ring ℤ / N ​ ℤ \mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z} . Assuming that q q and N N have no small prime divisors, we show that generalised Fermat equations have the expected number of solutions over A A . We further show that our density threshold is optimal. Our proofs involve average Fourier decay for Bohr sets, mixed character sum bounds, equidistribution of polynomial sequences, popular Cauchy–Davenport lemmas, and a regularity-type lemma due to Semchankau.

1.
Introduction

This paper studies solutions to Fermat equations over dense subsets of finite fields and cyclic rings. Such a problem seems to have been first studied by Csikvári–Gyarmati–Sárközy
[
6
]
, who considered solutions to the equation

(1.1)

x+y=z^{2}

with the variables lying in dense subsets of finite fields. They observed that the set

(1.2)

A=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}:x\in(0,p/4)\ \text{and}\ x^{2}\in(p/2,p)\},

while satisfying
|A|\gg p
, does not exhibit any solutions to (
1.1
). They further asked whether a colouring version of this result might exist, that is, for any partition
\mathbb{F}_{p}=C_{1}\cup\dots\cup C_{r}
with
r\in\mathbb{N}
, does there always exist some
1\leqslant i\leqslant r
such that
C_{i}
has solutions to (
1.1
)? The latter question, along with its generalisation to more broader equations of the shape

(1.3)

x^{\alpha}+y^{\beta}=z^{\gamma},

was resolved by Lindqvist
[
15
]
. In fact, Lindqvist proved that for any
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{N}
and any partition
\mathbb{F}_{p}=C_{1}\cup\dots\cup C_{r}
, with
r\in\mathbb{N}
, there must exist some
1\leqslant i\leqslant r
such that
C_{i}
has
\gg_{r,\alpha,\beta,\gamma}p^{2}
many solutions to (
1.3
). This matches the total number of solutions to (
1.3
) in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, up to multiplicative constants.
Such a phenomenon has been referred to as
supersaturation
,
by analogy with the extremal combinatorics parlance. We refer the reader to the pioneering works of Varnavides
[
21
]
and Frankl–Graham–Rödl
[
8
]
, who established these types of results for linear equations over integers. See also
[
3
,
4
,
16
]
and
[
14
]
for supersaturation results for non-linear equations over the integers and finite, cyclic rings, respectively.

Returning to the set
A
in (
1.2
), one can prove using standard Fourier-analytic methods that
|A|\geqslant p/8-o(p)
. A natural question then is whether there is any larger set that does not contain solutions to (
1.1
). A straightforward consequence of the results in this paper is that this is roughly the best possible construction.

Corollary 1.1
.

For every
\kappa>0
, every prime
p
sufficiently large in terms of
\kappa
, and every set
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
satisfying
|A|\geqslant p(1/8+\kappa)
, one has

\sum_{x,y,z\in A}\mathds{1}_{x+y=z^{2}}\gg_{\kappa}p^{2}.

In fact, our results in this paper provide optimal density thresholds along with supersaturation for a wider family of equations in broader algebraic settings. Writing
[n]=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, our first main result over general finite fields is as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
r
and
i_{1}<\dots<i_{r}
be positive integers, and let
s\geqslant 3
be an integer. Let

[s]=I_{1}\cup\dots\cup I_{r}

be a partition of
[s]
into discrete intervals
I_{1},\dots,I_{r}
such that
|I_{j}|=k_{j}
for all
j
. Let
{\kappa}>0
, and let
{\varepsilon}>0
be sufficiently small in terms of
s,i_{r},\kappa
. Let
p
be a prime sufficiently large in terms of
s,i_{r},\kappa
, and let
q=p^{m}
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Let

u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, let
c_{1},\ldots,c_{s}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0\}
, and let
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}
with

(1.4)

|A|\geqslant\left(\prod_{1\leqslant j\leqslant r}\frac{1}{rk_{j}}+\kappa\right)q.

Then there are at least
{\varepsilon}q^{s-1}
many solutions
\mathbf{x}\in A^{s}
to

(1.5)

\sum_{1\leqslant j\leqslant r}\sum_{n\in I_{j}}c_{n}x_{n}^{i_{j}}=u.

Corollary
1.1
can be deduced directly from Theorem
1.2
by setting

r=2,\quad(i_{1},i_{2})=(1,2),\quad[3]=\{1,2\}\cup\{3\},\quad u=0.

Another setting of interest is the case when
r=1
and
i_{1}=k
. This can be interpreted as a dense Waring-type problem in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
s\geqslant 3
and
k\geqslant 1
be integers. Let
{\kappa}>0
, and let
{\varepsilon}>0
be sufficiently small in terms of
s,k,{\kappa}
. Let
p
be a prime sufficiently large in terms of
s,k,\kappa
, and let
q=p^{m}
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, let
c_{1},\ldots,c_{s}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0\}
, and let
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}
with

\frac{|A|}{q}\geqslant\frac{1}{s}+\kappa.

Then there are at least
{\varepsilon}q^{s-1}
many solutions
\mathbf{x}\in A^{s}
to

(1.6)

c_{1}x_{1}^{k}+\cdots+c_{s}x_{s}^{k}=u.

Waring’s problem over
\mathbb{N}
concerns representing all positive integers — or all but finitely many — as sums of at most
s
many
k^{\mathrm{th}}
powers of positive integers. The majority of interest has revolved around the search for the best possible dependence of
s
on
k
, see
[
1
]
. More recently, there has been work which has focused on proving dense versions of Waring’s problem, that is, given a sufficiently dense subset
\mathcal{A}
of the
k^{\mathrm{th}}
powers, one wants to represent all sufficiently large positive integers as a sum of at most
s
many elements from
\mathcal{A}
, see
[
17
]
. One can also replace the
k^{\textrm{th}}
powers with other arithmetically interesting sets, see the very nice work of Shao
[
19
]
which considers the analogous problem over dense subsets of primes, see also
[
13
]
. All the aforementioned works proceed via applying the transference principle from additive combinatorics
[
9
]
to switch this problem to the
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
setting, for some appropriately chosen
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

Our second main result is an optimal version of Theorem
1.2
over finite, cyclic rings
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
, for suitably rough
N
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
r
and
i_{1}<\dots<i_{r}
be positive integers, and let
s\geqslant 3
be an integer. Let

[s]=I_{1}\cup\dots\cup I_{r}

be a partition of
[s]
into discrete intervals
I_{1},\dots,I_{r}
such that
|I_{j}|=k_{j}
for all
j
. Let
{\kappa}>0
, let
\Omega\geqslant 1
, and let
{\varepsilon}>0
be sufficiently small in terms of
s,i_{r},\kappa,\Omega
. Then there exists a large constant
R
depending only on
s,i_{r},\kappa,\Omega
such that the following is true.

Let
N=p_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots p_{t}^{m_{t}}
with

(1.7)

t,m_{1},\dots,m_{t}\leqslant\Omega\quad\text{and}\quad p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\geqslant R.

Let

u\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
, let
c_{1},\ldots,c_{s}\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, and let
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
with

(1.8)

|A|\geqslant\left(\prod_{1\leqslant j\leqslant r}\frac{1}{rk_{j}}+\kappa\right)N.

Then there are at least
{\varepsilon}N^{s-1}
many solutions
\mathbf{x}\in A^{s}
to (
1.5
).

Above and in the sequel, the
p_{j}
are understood to be pairwise distinct primes.

As before, Theorem
1.4
dispenses the following optimal dense Waring result in
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
for suitably rough numbers
N
.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
s\geqslant 3
and
k\geqslant 1
be integers. Let
{\kappa}>0,
let
\Omega\geqslant 1
, and let
{\varepsilon}>0
be sufficiently small in terms of
s,k,{\kappa},\Omega
. Then, there exists a large constant
R
depending only on
s,k,\kappa,\Omega
such that the following is true.

Let
N=p_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots p_{t}^{m_{t}}\in\mathbb{N}
with (
1.7
).
Let
c_{1},\ldots,c_{s}\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, let

u\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
, and let
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
with

\frac{|A|}{N}\geqslant\frac{1}{s}+\kappa.

Then there are at least
{\varepsilon}N^{s-1}
many solutions
\mathbf{x}\in A^{s}
to (
1.6
).

The only previously known result in this direction is due to the second author
[
14
]
, who considered the setting of Corollary
1.5
when
k=2
and
s\geqslant 5
, but with more general choices of
N
. Hence, in comparison, our method gives optimal density results for higher powers with potentially distinct exponents in much fewer variables, although we are required to restrict to rough numbers
N
with few prime factors, while the techniques of
[
14
]
work for more general rough numbers
N
. In fact, the approach in
[
14
]
is quite different from our current paper. The former relies on restriction estimates combined with the transference principle. We deploy regularity-type arguments of Semchankau
[
18
]
, together with a popular Cauchy–Davenport lemma and results on equidistribution of polynomial sequences in Bohr sets, see Proposition
5.3
.

We emphasise that Theorems
1.2
and
1.4
deliver optimal density thresholds. In order to see this quickly, we consider the following example over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Example 1.6
.

Given
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\setminus\{0\}
, we set
c_{n}=\lambda_{j}
for every
1\leqslant j\leqslant r
and every
n\in I_{j}
, and let

A=\bigg\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}:{\lambda}_{j}x^{i_{j}}\in\Big(0,\frac{p}{k_{j}r}\Big)\ (1\leqslant j\leqslant r)\bigg\}.

One may apply Lemma
4.1
, along with the fact that
\|{\widehat{\mathds{1}_{P}}}\|_{1}\ll\log p
for any arithmetic progression
P
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, to deduce that

|A|=(1+o(1))p\prod_{1\leqslant j\leqslant r}\frac{1}{k_{j}r}.

Moreover
A
has no solutions to (
1.5
) when
u=0
, since for every
1\leqslant j\leqslant r
and every
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in I_{j}}\subseteq A
, one has

\lambda_{j}\sum_{n\in I_{j}}a_{n}^{i_{j}}\in\Big(0,\frac{p}{r}\Big),\quad\text{whence}\quad\sum_{j=1}^{r}\sum_{n\in I_{j}}c_{n}a_{n}^{i_{j}}\in(0,p).

Furthermore, we note that it is necessary to have
N
be at least somewhat rough in Theorem
1.4
and Corollary
1.5
, as evinced by the following example.

Example 1.7
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
N=3N^{\prime}
where
N^{\prime}
is coprime to
3
, and let

A=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}:x^{k}\equiv 1\text{ $\mathrm{mod}$ $3$}\}.

Then
A
has large density, which is
1/3
when
k
is odd and
2/3
when
k
is even. However, owing to the congruence obstruction, it is impossible to represent all elements in
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
by
x_{1}^{k}+\cdots+x_{s}^{k}
with
x_{j}\in A
for all
1\leqslant j\leqslant s
, no matter how large
s
is.

It would be interesting to study the problem over function fields. Equidistribution of polynomial sequences — a key ingredient in our approach — was recently established in
[
12
]
and
[
2
]
.

Let us finish our introduction with a brief description of the proof strategy.
To fix ideas, consider (
1.6
). Assume for a contradiction that the iterated sumset
A_{1}+\cdots+A_{s}
does not contain
u
with high multiplicity, where

A_{i}=\{c_{i}a^{k}:a\in A\}.

Semchankau’s wrapping lemma tells us that each
A_{i}
is mostly contained in a
wrapper

W_{i}
, where a wrapper is a union of a small number of inhomogeneous, low-rank Bohr sets (it is important that we control the parameters). Moreover, it tells us that
W_{1}+\dots+W_{s}
does not fill the group with high multiplicity. Combining this with Green and Ruzsa’s popular Cauchy–Davenport lemma allows us to deduce that the average density of
W_{1},\dots,W_{s}
is at most
1/s+o(1)
. Next, we establish strong average Fourier decay estimates for Bohr sets and hence for wrappers. We infer from this — and an extra slicing argument for moduli that are not prime powers — effective equidistribution of
k^{\mathrm{th}}
powers in a wrapper. This, along with the upper bound on the average density of the wrappers, gives us an upper bound on the density of
A
, delivering the desired contradiction.

[

]
. We prove Theorem
1.2
in §4, and we prove some equidistribution results along with Theorem
1.4
in §5.

We use Vinogradov notation, that is, we write

to mean there exists some absolute constant
C>0
such that
|X|\leqslant CY
. We denote
X\ll_{z}Y
to mean that the above implicit constant
C
may depend on
z
. We write
X=O_{z}(Y)
to mean
X\ll_{z}Y
. For any set
X
and any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we write
X^{k}=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k}):x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\in X\}

k\in\mathbb{N}

2.
Preliminaries

Let
G
be a finite abelian group, and let
\widehat{G}
be its group of characters. Given a function
f:G\to\mathbb{C}
, we define its Fourier transform
\widehat{f}:\widehat{G}\to\mathbb{C}
as

\widehat{f}(\gamma)=\sum_{x\in G}f(x)\gamma(x).

By orthogonality, we get that for all
y\in G
, one has

f(y)=|G|^{-1}\sum_{\gamma\in\widehat{G}}\widehat{f}(\gamma)\overline{\gamma(y)}.

For any function
g:\widehat{G}\to\mathbb{C}
and any
q\in[1,\infty)
, we define

\|{g}\|_{q}=\big(|G|^{-1}\sum_{\gamma\in\widehat{G}}|g(\gamma)|^{q}\big)^{1/q}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \|{g}\|_{\infty}=\max_{\gamma\in\widehat{G}}|g(\gamma)|.

For any functions
f_{1},f_{2}:G\to\mathbb{C}
and
g_{1},g_{2}:\widehat{G}\to\mathbb{C}
, we define their convolutions
f_{1}*f_{2}:G\to\mathbb{C}
and
g_{1}*g_{2}:\widehat{G}\to\mathbb{C}
by

(f_{1}*f_{2})(x)=\sum_{y\in G}f_{1}(y)f_{2}(x-y)

and

(g_{1}*g_{2})(\gamma)=|G|^{-1}\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\widehat{G}}g_{1}(\gamma^{\prime})g_{2}(\gamma\gamma^{\prime-1}),

for all
x\in G
and all
\gamma\in\widehat{G}
.

2.1.
Wrappers

We now record the notion of wrappers. These were introduced and employed by Semchankau
[
18
]
to analyse various sum–product-type problems in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Let
\tau\in(0,1)
be a real number, and let

(2.1)

\mathbb{S}^{1}=S_{1}\cup S_{2}\cup\dots\cup S_{r}

be a partition of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
into arcs such that
\mu(S_{1})=\dots=\mu(S_{r-1})=\tau
and
0<\mu(S_{r})\leqslant\tau
. Let
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{d}
be pairwise distinct characters in
\widehat{G}
. For any
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{d})\in[r]^{d}
, we define the inhomogeneous Bohr set

(2.2)

B_{\mathbf{v}}=B_{\mathbf{v},\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{d},S_{1},\dots,S_{r}}=\{x\in G:\ \gamma_{i}(x)\in S_{v_{i}}\ (1\leqslant i\leqslant d)\}.

For ease of notation, we omit the dependence of
B_{\mathbf{v}}
on the parameters
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{d},S_{1},\dots,S_{r}
. Note that

G=\cup_{\mathbf{v}\in[r]^{d}}B_{\mathbf{v}}.

Given any set
X\subseteq[r]^{d}
, we call the set
V=\cup_{\mathbf{v}\in X}B_{\mathbf{v}}
a
(\tau,d)
-wrapper
.

These are essentially the wrappers introduced by Semchankau
[
18
]
, but our definition is very slightly more general.
We will use wrappers to contain — i.e. wrap — certain sets, up to a small number of elements. The advantage of using wrappers is that they have considerable average Fourier decay.

Given real numbers
0\leqslant\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}
and any function
f:G\to[0,\infty)
, we define

\Delta_{f}(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})=\{x\in G:\ell_{1}\leqslant f(x)\leqslant\ell_{2}\}.

The following is essentially
[
18
, Corollary 1]
, and can be deduced by combining elementary combinatorial arguments with a result of Croot–Łaba–Sisask
[
5
, Corollary 3.3]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f:G\to[0,\infty)
, let
\tau\in(0,1)
, and let
q>2
be a real number. Then there exists a positive integer
d\ll q/\tau^{2}
, pairwise distinct, non-trivial characters
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{d}\in\widehat{G}
, a partition of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
as in (
2.1
), and a set
Z\subseteq G
with
|Z|\leqslant e^{-q}|G|
such that the following holds. For any real numbers
0\leqslant\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}
, there exists
X\subseteq[r]^{d}
such that

\Delta_{f}(\ell_{1}+\delta,\ell_{2}-\delta)\setminus Z\ \subseteq\ (\cup_{\mathbf{v}\in X}B_{\mathbf{v}})\setminus Z\ \subseteq\ \Delta_{f}(\ell_{1}-\delta,\ell_{2}+\delta)\setminus Z,

where
\delta=20\|{\widehat{f}}\|_{1}\tau
.

We remark that for certain choices of
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}
, one could have
X=\emptyset
.

2.2.
A popular Cauchy–Davenport lemma

The following is a general Pollard–Kneser-type result due to Green–Ruzsa
[
10
, Proposition 6.2]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group, and let
H
be the largest proper subgroup of
G
. Then, for every non-empty
A,B\subseteq G
and every
1\leqslant t\leqslant\min\{|A|,|B|\}
,

\sum_{x\in G}\min\{t,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in A\\
b\in B\end{subarray}}\mathds{1}_{a+b=x}\}\geqslant t\min\{|G|,|A|+|B|-t-|H|\}.

This can be used to establish the following popular Kneser-type inequality, see the derivation of

[
14
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For each
s\in\mathbb{N}
and each
\delta,\kappa>0
, there exist
c,M>0
such that the following holds. Let
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{s}>{\delta}
with

\theta_{1}+\dots+\theta_{s}\geqslant 1+\kappa.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group
G
such that its largest proper subgroup
H
satisfies
|H|\leqslant|G|/M
. Then, for any
x\in G
and any
A_{1},\dots,A_{s}\subseteq G
satisfying
|A_{i}|\geqslant\theta_{i}|G|
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant s
,

\sum_{a_{1}\in A_{1},\dots,a_{s}\in A_{s}}\mathds{1}_{a_{1}+\dots+a_{s}=x}\geqslant c|G|^{s-1}.

2.3.
Deligne’s bound

The following estimate was demonstrated by Deligne. This formulation can be found in the introduction of
[
11
]
, and is a special case of
[
7
, Theorem 8.4]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
p
be a prime such that
p\nmid k
. Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a field of characteristic
p
, and let
\psi:\mathbb{F}_{q}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
be a non-trivial additive character. Let
P(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be a polynomial of degree
k
. Then

\left|\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\psi(P(x))\right|\leqslant(k-1)\sqrt{q}.

For the rings
\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z}
, there are also power-saving exponential sum bounds. The following estimate follows from
[
22
, Equation 7.9]
and Lemma
2.4
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
k,m\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
p
be a prime such that
p>k
. Let
P(x)\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z})[x]
be a polynomial of degree
k
. Then

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z}}e_{p^{m}}(P(x))\ll_{k}p^{m-1/k}.

3.
Semchankau’s wrapping lemma

Our aim in this section is to record the proof of the following variation of a result due to Semchankau
[
18
]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\varepsilon,\delta\in(0,1]
, let
s\geqslant 3
be an integer, and let
A_{1},\dots,A_{s}\subseteq G
satisfy
|A_{i}|\geqslant\delta|G|
for every
1\leqslant i\leqslant s
. Suppose there exists
a\in G
such that

\sum_{a_{1}\in A_{1},\dots,a_{s}\in A_{s}}\mathds{1}_{a_{1}+\dots+a_{s}=a}<\varepsilon|G|^{s-1}.

Then, for every
1\leqslant i\leqslant s
, there exist
\tau_{i}>0
and
d_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
, as well as
W_{i},Y_{i}\subseteq G
such that
W_{i}
is a
(\tau_{i},d_{i})
-wrapper,

\tau_{i}\gg{\varepsilon}^{1/(2s)}\delta^{1/2}{\varepsilon}^{\lceil{\varepsilon}^{-2}\rceil},\qquad\ d_{i}\ll\tau_{i}^{-2}\log(10/{\varepsilon})

and

|Y_{i}|\ll\varepsilon^{1/(2s)}|G|,\qquad A_{i}\setminus Y_{i}\subseteq W_{i}.

Moreover, there exists
b\in G
such that

\sum_{w_{1}\in W_{1},\dots,w_{s}\in W_{s}}\mathds{1}_{w_{1}+\dots+w_{s}=b}\ll_{s}\delta^{-s}\varepsilon^{1/2}|G|^{s-1}.

We now present the proof of Theorem
3.1
. We may assume that
a=0
by translating
A_{1}
appropriately. Let
n\geqslant 2
be an integer to be chosen later, and let

\Gamma=\{\gamma\in\widehat{G}:|\widehat{\mathds{1}_{A_{1}}}(\gamma)|>|G|/n^{1/2}\}.

Since
s\geqslant 3
,

\displaystyle|G|^{-1}\sum_{\gamma\in\widehat{G}\setminus\Gamma}|\widehat{\mathds{1}_{A_{1}}}(\gamma)\cdots\widehat{\mathds{1}_{A_{s}}}(\gamma)|\leqslant|G|^{s-4}\sum_{\gamma\in\widehat{G}\setminus\Gamma}|\widehat{\mathds{1}_{A_{1}}}(\gamma)\widehat{\mathds{1}_{A_{2}}}(\gamma)\widehat{\mathds{1}_{A_{3}}}(\gamma)|

\displaystyle\leqslant|G|^{s-4}(\sum_{\gamma\in\widehat{G}}|\widehat{\mathds{1}_{A_{2}}}(\gamma)|^{2})^{1/2}(\sum_{\gamma\in\widehat{G}}|\widehat{\mathds{1}_{A_{3}}}(\gamma)|^{2})^{1/2}\max_{\gamma\in\widehat{G}\setminus\Gamma}|\widehat{\mathds{1}_{A_{1}}}(\gamma)|

(3.1)

\displaystyle\leqslant|G|^{s-1}/n^{1/2}.

By orthogonality, one has

\#\Gamma|G|^{2}/n\leqslant\sum_{\gamma\in\widehat{G}}|\widehat{\mathds{1}_{A_{1}}}(\gamma)|^{2}=|G|\#A_{1}\leqslant|G|^{2},

whence
\#\Gamma\leqslant n
.

Let
\sigma\in(0,1)
be a parameter to be chosen later, let

be an arc of length

, and let

B=\{x\in G:\gamma(x)\in S_{\sigma}\ \text{for all}\ \gamma\in\Gamma\}.

The Bohr set

|B|\geqslant\sigma^{|\Gamma|}|G|\geqslant\sigma^{n}|G|,

see

. Moreover, for any

Now for every
1\leqslant i\leqslant s
, define

f_{i}(x)=|B|^{-1}(\mathds{1}_{A_{i}}*\mathds{1}_{B})(x)=|B|^{-1}\sum_{y\in G}\mathds{1}_{A_{i}}(y)\mathds{1}_{B}(x-y)

for all

on the physical side while having smoother Fourier

We first prove the former statement, and so, note that

\widehat{f_{i}}(\gamma)=|B|^{-1}\widehat{\mathds{1}_{A_{i}}}(\gamma)\widehat{\mathds{1}_{B}}(\gamma).

This, in turn, implies that

for all

while
\widehat{f_{i}}(\gamma)=\widehat{\mathds{1}_{A_{i}}}(\gamma)(1+O(\sigma))
for all

, with the latter equality following from (

). Furthermore, (

\displaystyle|G|^{-1}\sum_{\gamma\notin\Gamma}|\widehat{f_{1}}(\gamma)\cdots\widehat{f_{s}}(\gamma)|

\displaystyle\leqslant|G|^{-1}\sum_{\gamma\notin\Gamma}|\widehat{\mathds{1}_{A_{1}}}(\gamma)\dots\widehat{\mathds{1}_{A_{s}}}(\gamma)|

\displaystyle\leqslant|G|^{s-1}/n^{1/2}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\sum_{x_{1},\dots,x_{s}}f_{1}(x_{1})\cdots f_{s}(x_{s})\mathds{1}_{x_{1}+\dots+x_{s}=0}=|G|^{-1}\sum_{\gamma}\widehat{f_{1}}(\gamma)\cdots\widehat{f_{s}}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=|G|^{-1}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\widehat{\mathds{1}_{A_{1}}}(\gamma)\cdots\widehat{\mathds{1}_{A_{s}}}(\gamma)(1+O(\sigma))^{s}+O(|G|^{s-1}/n^{1/2})

\displaystyle=|G|^{-1}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\widehat{\mathds{1}_{A_{1}}}(\gamma)\cdots\widehat{\mathds{1}_{A_{s}}}(\gamma)+O_{s}(|G|^{s-1}(n^{-1/2}+\sigma))

\displaystyle=\sum_{x_{1},\dots,x_{s}}\mathds{1}_{A_{1}}(x_{1})\cdots\mathds{1}_{A_{s}}(x_{s})\mathds{1}_{x_{1}+\dots+x_{s}=0}+O_{s}(|G|^{s-1}(n^{-1/2}+\sigma))

(3.4)

\displaystyle<{\varepsilon}|G|^{s-1}+O_{s}(|G|^{s-1}n^{-1/2}+\sigma)),

where the penultimate step follows from (
3
) and orthogonality. As for the Fourier properties of

, note by a straightforward application of the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and orthogonality that

Now let

and

be parameters and, for every

\eta_{i}=\frac{\alpha|A_{i}|}{20|G|}\quad\text{and}\quad\tau_{i}=\frac{\alpha|A_{i}|^{1/2}|B|^{1/2}}{800|G|}

be reals lying in

and a set

\displaystyle\Delta_{f_{i}}(\eta_{i}+20\|{\widehat{f_{i}}}\|_{1}\tau_{i},

\displaystyle\ 1+20\|{\widehat{f_{i}}}\|_{1}\tau_{i})\setminus Z_{i}\ \subseteq\ V_{i}\setminus Z_{i}

(3.7)

\displaystyle\subseteq\ \Delta_{f_{i}}(\eta_{i}-20\|{\widehat{f_{i}}}\|_{1}\tau_{i},\ 1+60\|{\widehat{f_{i}}}\|_{1}\tau_{i})\setminus Z_{i}.

4.
Finite fields

This section will culminate in a proof of Theorem
1.2
. Here
q=p^{m}
, for some prime
p
and some
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
For
{\alpha},x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, let

\psi_{\alpha}(x)=e_{p}(\mathrm{Tr}({\alpha}x))=\psi_{1}({\alpha}x).

4.1.
Equidistribution of powers

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
r
be a positive integer, let
X_{1},\dots,X_{r}
be
non-empty subsets of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and let
1\leqslant i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{r}<p
be integers. Then

\displaystyle\#\{n\in\mathbb{F}_{q}:n^{i_{j}}\in X_{j}\ (1\leqslant j\leqslant r)\}-\ \frac{|X_{1}|\cdots|X_{r}|}{q^{r-1}}

\displaystyle\ll_{i_{r}}\|{\widehat{\mathds{1}_{X_{1}}}}\|_{1}\cdots\|{\widehat{\mathds{1}_{X_{r}}}}\|_{1}\sqrt{q}.

Proof.

Put
X=X_{1}\times\cdots\times X_{r}
.
By orthogonality,

\displaystyle\#\{n\in\mathbb{F}_{q}:n^{i_{j}}\in X_{j}\ (1\leqslant j\leqslant r)\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in X}\mathds{1}_{n^{i_{1}}=x_{1}}\cdots\mathds{1}_{n^{i_{r}}=x_{r}}

\displaystyle=q^{-r}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{r}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in X}\psi_{1}(-{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\cdot\mathbf{x})\sum_{n\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\psi_{1}({\alpha}_{1}n^{i_{1}}+\cdots+{\alpha}_{r}n^{i_{r}})

\displaystyle=q^{-r}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{r}}\widehat{\mathds{1}_{X_{1}}}(\psi_{-\alpha_{1}})\cdots\widehat{\mathds{1}_{X_{r}}}(\psi_{-\alpha_{r}})\sum_{n\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\psi_{1}(\alpha_{1}n^{i_{1}}+\cdots+\alpha_{r}n^{i_{r}}).

Thus, by Lemma
2.4
,

\displaystyle\bigg|\#\{n\in\mathbb{F}_{q}:n^{i_{j}}\in X_{j}\ (1\leqslant j\leqslant r)\}-\frac{|X_{1}|\cdots|X_{r}|}{q^{r-1}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leqslant q^{-r}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{0}}\neq{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{r}}|\widehat{\mathds{1}_{X_{1}}}(-\alpha_{1})\cdots\widehat{\mathds{1}_{X_{1}}}(-\alpha_{r})\sum_{n\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\psi_{1}(\alpha_{1}n^{i_{1}}+\cdots+\alpha_{r}n^{i_{r}})|

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\widehat{\mathds{1}_{X_{1}}}\|_{1}\cdots\|{\widehat{\mathds{1}_{X_{r}}}}\|_{1}i_{r}\sqrt{q}.\qed

4.2.
Average Fourier decay

As an additive group, we can identify
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
G=\mathbb{F}_{p}^{m}
. In this subsection, we use
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
interchangeably with the dot product on
G
. Let
\mathbf{r}\in G
and

\psi_{\mathbf{r}}(\mathbf{x})=e_{p}(\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{x}).

Bohr sets of rank 1 for this additive character have the form

B=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in G:\left\|\frac{\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{x}-c}{p}\right\|\leqslant{\delta}\right\},

where
c\in\mathbb{Z}
and
0<{\delta}\leqslant 1/2
.

In the case
m=1
, it follows from
[
18
, Proposition 3.2]

and the proof of
[
18
, Proposition 3.3]

that

(4.1)

\|\widehat{\mathds{1}_{B}}\|_{1}\ll\log p.

We now extend this to the case of general
m
.

Lemma 4.2
.

We have
(
4.1
).

Proof.

First suppose
\mathbf{r}={\boldsymbol{0}}
. Then
B
is either empty or
G
. If
B
is empty, then
\|\widehat{\mathds{1}_{B}}\|_{1}=0.
If
B=G
, then

\|\widehat{\mathds{1}_{B}}\|_{1}=|G|^{-1}|\widehat{\mathds{1}_{G}}({\boldsymbol{0}})|=1.

It suffices to consider two other cases for
\mathbf{r}
, namely:

I.

\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{r}\neq 0

II.

\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{r}=0
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\ell}\in G\setminus H
, where

H=\{\mathbf{x}\in G:\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{x}=0\}.

In Case I, we choose
\boldsymbol{\ell}=\mathbf{r}
for simplicity.

We decompose

G=H\oplus\mathbb{F}_{p}\boldsymbol{\ell}.

This restricts to a decomposition

B=H+U\boldsymbol{\ell},

where

U=\left\{u\in\mathbb{F}_{p}:\left\|\frac{u\boldsymbol{\ell}\cdot\mathbf{r}-c}{p}\right\|\leqslant{\delta}\right\},

and each element of
B
is
\mathbf{h}+u\boldsymbol{\ell}
for some unique pair
(\mathbf{h},u)\in H\times U
.
Now

\widehat{\mathds{1}_{B}}({\boldsymbol{\xi}})=\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in B}e_{p}(\mathbf{x}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\xi}}),

so

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\mathds{1}_{B}}\|_{1}

\displaystyle=p^{-m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{0}\in H\\
v\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{u\in U}\sum_{\mathbf{h}\in H}e_{p}(\langle\mathbf{h}+u\boldsymbol{\ell},{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{0}+v\boldsymbol{\ell}\rangle)\right|

\displaystyle=p^{-m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{0}\in H\\
v\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{u\in U}e_{p}(u\langle\boldsymbol{\ell},{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{0}+v\boldsymbol{\ell}\rangle)\sum_{\mathbf{h}\in H}e_{p}(\langle\mathbf{h},{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{0}+v\boldsymbol{\ell}\rangle)\right|.

The inner sum vanishes unless
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{0}+v\boldsymbol{\ell}={\lambda}\mathbf{r}
for some
{\lambda}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Case I

In this case
\boldsymbol{\ell}=\mathbf{r}
.
For the inner sum not to vanish, we must have
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{0}\|\mathbf{r}
and hence
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{0}={\boldsymbol{0}}
, since
\mathbf{r}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{0}=0
and
\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{r}\neq 0
. Consequently,

\|\widehat{\mathds{1}_{B}}\|_{1}=p^{-1}\sum_{v\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left|\sum_{u\in U}e_{p}(uv\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{r})\right|=p^{-1}\sum_{v\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left|\sum_{u\in U}e_{p}(uv)\right|.

This is
O(\log p)
, as we saw in the case
m=1
.

Case II

As
\boldsymbol{\ell}\notin H
and
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{0},\mathbf{r}\in H
, we cannot have
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{0}+v\boldsymbol{\ell}={\lambda}\mathbf{r}
unless
v=0
and
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{0}={\lambda}\mathbf{r}
. Therefore

\|\widehat{\mathds{1}_{B}}\|_{1}=p^{-1}\sum_{{\lambda}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left|\sum_{u\in U}e_{p}(u{\lambda}\boldsymbol{\ell}\cdot\mathbf{r})\right|=p^{-1}\sum_{{\lambda}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left|\sum_{u\in U}e_{p}(u{\lambda})\right|.

This is
O(\log p)
, as in Case I.
∎

We can bootstrap this to infer average Fourier decay for wrappers.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
W
be a
(\tau,d)
-wrapper in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then

\|\widehat{\mathds{1}_{W}}\|_{1}\ll_{\tau,d}(\log p)^{d}.

Proof.

By definition, the wrapper
W
is a disjoint union of
O_{\tau,d}(1)
many sets

B=B_{\mathbf{v}}
as given by (
2.2
), and so, by a simple application of triangle inequality, it suffices to prove the desired result for the set
B_{\mathbf{v}}
. In this case, note that we can write

B=B_{1}\cap\cdots\cap B_{d},

for some Bohr sets
B_{1},\ldots,B_{d}
of rank 1. Finally, Young’s convolution inequality and Lemma
4.1
deliver

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\mathds{1}_{B}}\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\|\widehat{\mathds{1}_{B_{1}}\cdots\mathds{1}_{B_{r}}}\|_{1}=\|\widehat{\mathds{1}_{B_{1}}}*\cdots*\widehat{\mathds{1}_{B_{r}}}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\widehat{\mathds{1}_{B_{1}}}\|_{1}\cdots\|\widehat{\mathds{1}_{B_{r}}}\|_{1}\ll(\log p)^{d}.\qed

4.3.
Dense Fermat over finite fields

We now establish Theorem
1.2
. Assume for a contradiction that there are fewer than
{\varepsilon}q^{s-1}
many solutions
\mathbf{x}\in A^{s}
to
(
1.5
).
Define

A_{n}=\{c_{n}a^{i_{j}}:a\in A\}

for all
n\in I_{j}
and all
1\leqslant j\leqslant r
. Note that
|A_{n}|\geqslant|A|/i_{r}\gg_{i_{r},s}q
. We may now apply Theorem
3.1
to find, for every

, a
(\tau_{i},d_{i})
-wrapper

with
\tau_{i}\gg_{{\varepsilon},s,i_{r}}1
and

such that

and

(4.2)

\sum_{w_{1}\in W_{1},\dots,w_{s}\in W_{s}}\mathds{1}_{w_{1}+\dots+w_{s}=b}\ll_{s,i_{r}}{\varepsilon}^{1/2}q^{s-1},

for some

.

We now claim that

(4.3)

|W_{1}|+\dots+|W_{s}|<\left(1+\frac{\kappa}{10}\right)q.

We prove this claim via contradiction, so let us assume that it does not hold. Let
C
be a large, positive constant. As
{\varepsilon}
is sufficiently small, we have

|W_{i}|\geqslant|A_{i}|-C{\varepsilon}^{1/(2s)}q\gg_{i_{r},s}q

for all

. We may now apply Lemma
2.3
, giving

\sum_{w_{1}\in W_{1},\dots,w_{s}\in W_{s}}\mathds{1}_{w_{1}+\dots+w_{s}}\gg_{s,i_{r},\kappa}q^{s-1}.

As
{\varepsilon}
is sufficiently small in terms of
s,i_{r},\kappa
, this contradicts (
4.2
).

We may therefore assume that (
4.3
) holds. For every
\mathbf{n}\in I_{1}\times\dots\times I_{r}
, let

X_{\mathbf{n}}=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}:c_{n_{j}}x^{i_{j}}\in W_{n_{j}}\ (1\leqslant j\leqslant r)\}.

By Lemma
4.3
and a change of variables, we note that if
W
is a
(\tau,d)
-wrapper in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
then

\|\widehat{\mathds{1}_{cW}}\|_{1}\ll_{\tau,d}(\log p)^{d}.

Combining this with Lemma
4.1
yields

\displaystyle|X_{\mathbf{n}}|-\frac{|W_{n_{1}}|\cdots|W_{n_{r}}|}{q^{r-1}}

\displaystyle\ll_{s,i_{r},{\varepsilon}}(\log p)^{O_{{\varepsilon},s,i_{r}}(1)}q^{1/2}.

Since
|A_{i}\setminus W_{i}|\ll{\varepsilon}^{1/(2s)}q
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant s
,

|A|\leqslant|X_{\mathbf{n}}|+O_{i_{r},s}({\varepsilon}^{1/(2s)}q).

Coupling this with the preceding bound, and then averaging over all
\mathbf{n}\in I_{1}\times\dots\times I_{r}
, gives

\displaystyle\frac{|A|}{q}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{k_{1}\cdots k_{r}}\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in I_{1}\times\dots\times I_{r}}\frac{|W_{n_{1}}|\cdots|W_{n_{r}}|}{q^{r}}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\qquad+O_{s,i_{r}}({\varepsilon}^{1/(2s)})+O_{{\varepsilon},i_{r},s}((\log p)^{O_{{\varepsilon},s,i_{r}}(1)}q^{-1/2}).

Clearly, we may assume that
{\kappa}
is sufficiently small in terms of
r
. We may also assume that
{\varepsilon}
is a function of
s,i_{r},{\kappa}
, as the result would then follow for all smaller values of

. This enables us to subsume any dependence on
{\varepsilon}
into the dependence on

.

By the AM–GM inequality and (
4.3
), the first term on the right-hand side of (

)
is bounded above by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q^{r}k_{1}\cdots k_{r}}\left(r^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\sum_{n_{j}\in I_{j}}|W_{n_{j}}|\right)^{r}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{(1+\kappa/10)^{r}}{k_{1}\cdots k_{r}r^{r}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1+r\kappa/5}{k_{1}\cdots k_{r}r^{r}}\leqslant\prod_{1\leqslant j\leqslant r}\frac{1}{rk_{j}}+\frac{\kappa}{5}.

The other terms on the right-hand side of (

) are smaller than

. Therefore

|A|<\left(\prod_{1\leqslant j\leqslant r}\frac{1}{rk_{j}}+\frac{3\kappa}{5}\right)q,

contradicting the hypothesis (

). Hence, we must have at least
{\varepsilon}q^{s-1}
many solutions to (

) with

.

5.
Finite, cyclic rings

In this section, we will first prove that the wrappers are nicely additively structured, in the sense that the powers are equidistributed among them. Then we will use this property to deduce Theorem
1.4
.

5.1.
Equidistribution of powers in wrappers

Let
N
be a positive integer, let
\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
be frequencies, and let
\mathbb{I}_{1},\mathbb{I}_{2},\dots,\mathbb{I}_{d}
be real intervals. The inhomogeneous Bohr set

B=B(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{d};\mathbb{I}_{1},\mathbb{I}_{2},\dots,\mathbb{I}_{d})

is defined to be

=\left\{x\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}:\frac{\beta_{j}x}{N}\in\mathbb{I}_{j}\text{\;$\mathrm{mod}\;1$ for all $1\leqslant j\leqslant d$}\right\}.

We refer to
d
as the
rank
of
B
. (Strictly speaking, the rank is a property of the Bohr set data, rather than a property of the set.)

The following lemma, which is in the same spirit as Lemma
4.2
, asserts that the inhomogeneous Bohr sets in
\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z}
have considerable average Fourier decay.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
p
be a prime, let
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
B
be an inhomogeneous Bohr set of rank
d
in
\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z}
. Then

\|{\widehat{\mathds{1}_{B}}}\|_{1}\leqslant(C\log p^{m})^{d},

where
C
is some absolute constant.

Proof.

First, we consider the case where the Bohr set
B
has rank
1
. Say
B=B(p^{\ell}\beta;\mathbb{I})
, where
\beta
is not divisible by
p
and
\ell<m
.

Note that for
x\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z}
, whether
p^{\ell}\beta x/p^{m}=\beta x/p^{m-\ell}
is in
\mathbb{I}
depends only on the residue class of
x\,\,\mathrm{mod}\,\,p^{m-\ell}
. Thus, the Bohr set
B
is of the form
Q+H
, where
Q
is an arithmetic progression
\,\,\mathrm{mod}\,\,p^{m-\ell}
with common difference
z
not divisible by
p
, and
H
is the subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z}
generated by the element
p^{m-\ell}
. With this description, we can compute
\widehat{\mathds{1}_{B}}
explicitly. Indeed,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathds{1}_{B}}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\in Q}\ \sum_{0\leqslant x<p^{\ell}}e_{p^{m}}((q+xp^{m-\ell})\xi)=\sum_{q\in Q}e_{p^{m}}(q\xi)\sum_{0\leqslant x<p^{\ell}}e_{p^{\ell}}(x\xi)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }p^{l}\nmid\xi\\
p^{\ell}\sum_{q\in Q}e_{p^{m}}(q\xi),&\text{if }p^{\ell}\mid\xi\end{cases}

\displaystyle\ll\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }p^{\ell}\nmid\xi\\
p^{\ell}\|{\frac{\xi z}{p^{m}}}\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}^{-1},&\text{if }p^{\ell}\mid\xi.\end{cases}

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z}}|\widehat{\mathds{1}_{B}}(\xi)|

\displaystyle\ll p^{m}+p^{\ell}\sum_{1\leqslant\eta<p^{m-l}}\left\|\frac{\eta z}{p^{m-\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}^{-1}

\displaystyle\ll p^{m}+p^{\ell}\sum_{1\leqslant\eta<p^{m-\ell}}\frac{p^{m-l}}{\eta}\ll p^{m}\log(p^{m}),

which gives the desired bound.

For higher-rank Bohr sets, we observe that
\|{\widehat{\mathds{1}_{B}1_{B^{\prime}}}}\|_{1}\leqslant\|{\widehat{\mathds{1}_{B}}}\|_{1}\|{\widehat{\mathds{1}_{B^{\prime}}}}\|_{1}
for any Bohr sets
B,B^{\prime}
by Young’s inequality. Hence, combining this inequality and the fact that higher-rank Bohr sets are intersections of rank
1
Bohr sets completes the proof.
∎

Next, we will prove that the powers are equidistributed among the inhomogeneous Bohr sets in
\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
i_{1}<\dots<i_{r}
be positive integers. Let
p>i_{r}
be prime, let
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
B_{1},\ldots,B_{r}
be inhomogeneous Bohr sets of rank
d
in
\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z}
. Let
c_{1},\ldots,c_{r}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z}}\mathds{1}_{B_{1}}(c_{1}x^{i_{1}})\cdots\mathds{1}_{B_{r}}(c_{r}x^{i_{r}})

\displaystyle=|B_{1}|\cdots|B_{r}|p^{-m(r-1)}

\displaystyle\quad+O_{i_{r},d}(p^{m-1/i_{r}}\log^{dr}p^{m}).

Proof.

By Fourier expansion and Lemma
2.5
,

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z}}\mathds{1}_{B_{1}}(c_{1}x^{i_{1}})\cdots\mathds{1}_{B_{r}}(c_{r}x^{i_{r}})

\displaystyle=p^{-mr}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z}}\ \sum_{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\mathds{1}_{B_{1}}}(\xi_{1})\cdots\widehat{\mathds{1}_{B_{r}}}(\xi_{r})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot e_{p^{m}}(-\xi_{1}c_{1}x^{i_{1}}-\ \cdots\ -\xi_{r}c_{r}x^{i_{r}})

\displaystyle=|B_{1}|\cdots|B_{r}|p^{-m(r-1)}+O_{i_{r}}(p^{m-1/i_{r}}\|{\widehat{\mathds{1}_{B_{1}}}}\|_{1}\cdots\|{\widehat{\mathds{1}_{B_{r}}}}\|_{1}).

Thus, applying Lemma
5.1
gives the desired error term.
∎

To extend Proposition
5.2
to general moduli
N=p_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots p_{t}^{m_{t}}
, such Fourier-analytic arguments are — perhaps surprisingly — less effective. The reason is that that the analysis of Hardy–Littlewood major arcs in
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
is more delicate; on some non-zero major arcs we only save
\sqrt{p_{1}}
over the trivial exponential sum bound, but
p_{1}
might be considerably smaller than other prime factors, rendering this saving not as large as we would like. To overcome this issue, we argue in the physical space and utilise the product structure of
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
to deduce the following proposition.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
i_{1}<\dots<i_{r}
be positive integers. Let

N=p_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots p_{t}^{m_{t}}\quad\text{with}\quad p_{1},\ldots,p_{t}>i_{r},

and let
B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{r}
be inhomogeneous Bohr sets of rank
d
in
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
. Let
c_{1},\ldots,c_{r}\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}}\mathds{1}_{B_{1}}(c_{1}x^{i_{1}})\cdots\mathds{1}_{B_{r}}(c_{r}x^{i_{r}})

\displaystyle=|B_{1}|\cdots|B_{r}|N^{-(r-1)}

\displaystyle\quad+O_{i_{r},d}\left(N\sum_{1\leqslant j\leqslant t}\frac{\log^{dr}p_{j}^{m_{j}}}{p_{j}^{1/i_{r}}}\right).

Proof.

We will prove Proposition
5.3
by induction on the number of prime factors
t
. The base case
t=1
is given by Proposition
5.2
.

When
t>1
, write
N=p_{1}^{m_{1}}N^{\prime}
. Let
f_{j}=\mathds{1}_{B_{j}}
for
1\leqslant j\leqslant r
. We can regard
f_{j}
as functions on
\mathbb{Z}/p_{1}^{m_{1}}\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/N^{\prime}\mathbb{Z}
, by the Chinese remainder theorem. Note that

(5.1)

\begin{split}&\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}}\mathds{1}_{B_{1}}(c_{1}x^{i_{1}})\cdots\mathds{1}_{B_{r}}(c_{r}x^{i_{r}})\\
&=\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}/N^{\prime}\mathbb{Z}}\ \sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}/p_{1}^{m_{1}}\mathbb{Z}}f_{1}(c_{1}y^{i_{1}},c_{1}z^{i_{1}})\cdots f_{r}(c_{r}y^{i_{r}},c_{r}z^{i_{r}}).\end{split}

The key observation is that a slice of a Bohr set is still a Bohr set of the same rank, that is, we can regard
f_{j}(\cdot,v)
as the characteristic function of a Bohr set in
\mathbb{Z}/p_{1}^{m_{1}}\mathbb{Z}
, for
v\in\mathbb{Z}/N^{\prime}\mathbb{Z}
and
1\leqslant j\leqslant r
. To justify this observation, note that the canonical isomorphism

\mathbb{Z}/p_{1}^{m_{1}}\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/N^{\prime}\mathbb{Z}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}

is given by
(u,v)\longmapsto uN^{\prime}\Gamma+vp_{1}^{m_{1}}\gamma
, where
\Gamma
is the multiplicative inverse of
N^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{Z}/p_{1}^{m_{1}}\mathbb{Z}
and
\gamma
is the multiplicative inverse of
p_{1}^{m_{1}}
in
\mathbb{Z}/N^{\prime}\mathbb{Z}
. Now, for any
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
and interval
\mathbb{I}
, we have

\frac{\beta(uN^{\prime}\Gamma+vp_{1}^{m_{1}}\gamma)}{N}\in\mathbb{I}\iff\frac{\beta\Gamma u}{p_{1}^{m_{1}}}\in\mathbb{I}-\frac{\beta v\gamma}{N^{\prime}},

hence
f_{j}(\cdot,v)
is the characteristic function of a Bohr set of the same rank as
B_{j}
in
\mathbb{Z}/p_{1}^{m_{1}}\mathbb{Z}
.

By Proposition
5.2
, the expression (
5.1
) is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}/N^{\prime}\mathbb{Z}}\Bigg(p_{1}^{-m_{1}(r-1)}\prod_{1\leqslant j\leqslant r}\ \sum_{\mathfrak{y}_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}/p_{1}^{m_{1}}\mathbb{Z}}

\displaystyle f_{j}(\mathfrak{y}_{j},c_{j}z^{i_{j}})

(5.2)

\displaystyle+O_{i_{r},d}(p_{1}^{m_{1}-1/i_{r}}\log^{dr}p_{1}^{m_{1}})\Bigg).

Summing over
\mathbb{Z}/N^{\prime}\mathbb{Z}
, the error term in (
5.2
) is
O_{i_{r},d}(Np_{1}^{-1/i_{r}}\log^{dr}p_{1}^{m_{1}})
. For the main term, we apply the induction hypothesis for each
\mathfrak{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{y}_{r}
— once again, we use the fact that the slicing of a Bohr set is still a Bohr set of the same rank — to obtain

(5.3)

\begin{split}&p_{1}^{-m_{1}(r-1)}\sum_{\mathfrak{y}_{1},...,\mathfrak{y}_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}/p_{1}^{m_{1}}\mathbb{Z}}\ \sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}/N^{\prime}\mathbb{Z}}f_{1}(\mathfrak{y}_{1},c_{1}z^{i_{1}})\cdots f_{r}(\mathfrak{y}_{r},c_{r}z^{i_{r}})\\
&=p_{1}^{-m_{1}(r-1)}\sum_{\mathfrak{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{y}_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}/p_{1}^{m_{1}}\mathbb{Z}}\Bigg((N^{\prime})^{-(r-1)}\prod_{1\leqslant j\leqslant r}\ \sum_{\mathfrak{z}_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}/N^{\prime}\mathbb{Z}}f_{j}(\mathfrak{y}_{j},\mathfrak{z}_{j})\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+O_{i_{r},d}\Big(N^{\prime}\sum_{2\leqslant j\leqslant t}p_{j}^{-1/i_{r}}\log^{dr}p_{j}^{m_{j}}\Big)\Bigg).\end{split}

Summing over
\mathfrak{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{y}_{r}
and multiplying by
p_{1}^{-m_{1}(r-1)}
, the error term in (
5.3
) is
O_{i_{r},d}(N\sum_{2\leqslant j\leqslant t}p_{j}^{-1/i_{r}}\log^{dr}p_{j}^{m_{j}})
, while the main term is

\displaystyle N^{-(r-1)}\sum_{\mathfrak{y}_{1},...,\mathfrak{y}_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}/p_{1}^{m_{1}}\mathbb{Z}}\ \sum_{\mathfrak{z}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{z}_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}/N^{\prime}\mathbb{Z}}f_{1}(\mathfrak{y}_{1},\mathfrak{z}_{1})\cdots f_{r}(\mathfrak{y}_{r},\mathfrak{z}_{r})

\displaystyle=N^{-(r-1)}|B_{1}|\cdots|B_{r}|.\qed

Since wrappers are disjoint unions of Bohr sets, we can write the characteristic functions of wrappers as sums of characteristic functions of Bohr sets to obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
i_{1}<\cdots<i_{r}
and
D
be positive integers. Let

N=p_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots p_{t}^{m_{t}}\quad\text{with}\quad p_{1},\ldots,p_{t}>i_{r},

and let
W_{j}
be a
(\tau_{j},d_{j})
-wrapper in
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
, with
\tau_{j}\geqslant D^{-1}
and
d_{j}\leqslant D
for
1\leqslant j\leqslant r
. Let
c_{1},\ldots,c_{r}\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}}\mathds{1}_{W_{1}}(c_{1}x^{i_{1}})\cdots\mathds{1}_{W_{r}}(c_{r}x^{i_{r}})

\displaystyle=|W_{1}|\cdots|W_{r}|N^{-(r-1)}

\displaystyle\qquad+O_{i_{r},D}\left(N\sum_{1\leqslant j\leqslant t}\frac{\log^{Dr}p_{j}^{m_{j}}}{p_{j}^{1/i_{r}}}\right).

5.2.
Dense Fermat over finite, cyclic rings

We now establish Theorem
1.4
.
Recall that
{\varepsilon}
is sufficiently small depending on
s,i_{r},\kappa,\Omega
, and that
R
is sufficiently large depending on
s,i_{r},\kappa,\Omega
. Over the course of the proof, we will explain how small
{\varepsilon}
has to be and how large
R
has to be, albeit implicitly.
We will work in
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
, where

N=p_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots p_{t}^{m_{t}}
with (
1.7
).
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
A\subseteq(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, at the cost of changing (
1.8
) to

(5.4)

|A|\geqslant\left(\prod_{1\leqslant j\leqslant r}\frac{1}{rk_{j}}+\frac{9}{10}\kappa\right)N,

since there are at most
O_{\Omega}(N/R)
many elements that are not invertible.

Assume for a contradiction that there are fewer than
{\varepsilon}N^{s-1}
many solutions
\mathbf{x}\in A^{s}
to
(
1.5
).
Define

A_{n}=\{c_{n}a^{i_{j}}:a\in A\}

for all
n\in I_{j}
and all
1\leqslant j\leqslant r
. Note that
|A_{n}|\geqslant|A|/i_{r}^{\Omega}\gg_{i_{r},s,\Omega}N
since
A\subseteq(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. We may now apply Theorem
3.1
to find — for every
1\leqslant i\leqslant s

— a
(\tau_{i},d_{i})
-wrapper
W_{i}
with
\tau_{i}\gg_{{\varepsilon},s,i_{r},\Omega}1
and
d_{i}\ll_{{\varepsilon},s,i_{r},\Omega}1
such that

|A_{i}\setminus W_{i}|\ll{\varepsilon}^{1/(2s)}N

and

(5.5)

\sum_{w_{1}\in W_{1},\dots,w_{s}\in W_{s}}\mathds{1}_{w_{1}+\dots+w_{s}=b}\ll_{s,i_{r},\Omega}{\varepsilon}^{1/2}N^{s-1},

for some
b\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
.

We now claim that

(5.6)

|W_{1}|+\dots+|W_{s}|<\left(1+\frac{\kappa}{10}\right)N.

We prove this claim via contradiction, so let us assume that it does not hold. Let
C
be a large, positive constant. As
{\varepsilon}
is sufficiently small, we have

|W_{i}|\geqslant|A_{i}|-C{\varepsilon}^{1/(2s)}N\gg_{i_{r},s,\Omega}N

for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant s
. We may now apply Lemma
2.3
, giving

\sum_{w_{1}\in W_{1},\dots,w_{s}\in W_{s}}\mathds{1}_{w_{1}+\dots+w_{s}}\gg_{s,i_{r},\kappa,\Omega}N^{s-1}.

As
{\varepsilon}
is sufficiently small, this contradicts (
5.5
).

We may therefore assume that (
5.6
) holds. For every
\mathbf{n}\in I_{1}\times\dots\times I_{r}
, let

X_{\mathbf{n}}=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}:c_{n_{j}}x^{i_{j}}\in W_{n_{j}}\;(1\leqslant j\leqslant r)\}.

By Corollary
5.4
,

\displaystyle|X_{\mathbf{n}}|

\displaystyle=|W_{n_{1}}|\cdots|W_{n_{r}}|N^{-(r-1)}

(5.7)

\displaystyle\qquad+O_{{\varepsilon},s,i_{r},\Omega}\left(N\sum_{1\leqslant j\leqslant t}p_{j}^{-1/i_{r}}\log^{O_{{\varepsilon},s,i_{r},\Omega}(1)}p_{j}\right).

Since
|A_{i}\setminus W_{i}|\ll{\varepsilon}^{1/(2s)}N
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant s
,

|A|\leqslant|X_{\mathbf{n}}|+O_{s,i_{r},\Omega}({\varepsilon}^{1/(2s)}N).

Coupling this with the preceding bound, and then averaging over all
\mathbf{n}\in I_{1}\times\dots\times I_{r}
, gives

\displaystyle\frac{|A|}{N}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{k_{1}\cdots k_{r}}\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in I_{1}\times\dots\times I_{r}}\frac{|W_{n_{1}}|\cdots|W_{n_{r}}|}{N^{r}}

(5.8)

\displaystyle\quad+O_{s,i_{r},\Omega}({\varepsilon}^{1/(2s)})+O_{{\varepsilon},s,i_{r},\Omega}\left(\sum_{1\leqslant j\leqslant t}p_{j}^{-1/i_{r}}\log^{O_{{\varepsilon},s,i_{r},\Omega}(1)}p_{j}\right).

Clearly, we may assume that
{\kappa}
is sufficiently small in terms of
r
. Also, like in the previous section, we may assume that
{\varepsilon}
is a function of
s,i_{r},{\kappa},\Omega
, so that the dependence of the implied constants on
{\varepsilon}
can be subsumed into their dependence on
s,i_{r},{\kappa},\Omega
.

By the AM–GM inequality and (
5.6
), the first term on the right-hand side of (
5.2
)
is bounded above by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{r}k_{1}\cdots k_{r}}\left(r^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\sum_{n_{j}\in I_{j}}|W_{n_{j}}|\right)^{r}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{(1+\kappa/10)^{r}}{k_{1}\cdots k_{r}r^{r}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1+r\kappa/5}{k_{1}\cdots k_{r}r^{r}}\leqslant\prod_{1\leqslant j\leqslant r}\frac{1}{rk_{j}}+\frac{\kappa}{5}.

Now choose
{\varepsilon}
small enough to ensure that the first error term on the right-hand side of (
5.2
) is less than
\kappa/5
, and then choose
R
large enough to ensure that the second error term on the right-hand side of (
5.2
) is less than
\kappa/5
. Thus,

|A|<\left(\prod_{1\leqslant j\leqslant r}\frac{1}{rk_{j}}+\frac{3\kappa}{5}\right)N,

contradicting the hypothesis (
5.4
). Hence, we must have at least
{\varepsilon}N^{s-1}
many solutions to (
1.5
) with
x_{1},\dots,x_{s}\in A
.

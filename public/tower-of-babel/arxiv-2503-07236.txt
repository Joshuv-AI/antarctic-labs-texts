Novel and Updated Bounds on Flavor-violating Z Interactions in the Lepton Sector
Fayez Abu-Ajamieh ID, Amine Ahriche ID, Nobuchika Okada ID

Abstract
We investigate the experimental bounds on the Flavor-Violating (FV) couplings of the Z Z boson to the charged leptons. In addition to the direct LHC searches for FV Z Z decays to leptons, we investigate indirect bounds from flavor-conserving Z Z decays to leptons at 1-loop, bounds from LEP searches, Electroweak Precision Observables (EWPO), ℓ i → ℓ j ​ γ \ell_{i}\to\ell_{j}\gamma decays, ℓ i → 3 ​ ℓ j \ell_{i}\to 3\ell_{j} decays, the muon lifetime, FV meson decays to leptons, FV τ \tau decays to μ ⁡ ( e ) \mu(e) + mesons, muon conversion in nuclei, the Michel parameters, and from muonium-antimuonium oscillations. For FV Z Z couplings to τ ​ μ \tau\mu , we find that τ → μ ​ γ \tau\to\mu\gamma yields the strongest bounds, with a level reaching 𝒪 ⁡ ( 10 − 5 ) \mathcal{O}(10^{-5}) , followed by bounds from τ → 3 ​ μ ​ ( μ ​ e ​ e ) \tau\to 3\mu~(\mu ee) . For FV Z Z couplings to τ ​ e \tau e , we find that the strongest bounds arise from the decay τ → μ ​ μ ​ e \tau\to\mu\mu e , reaching 𝒪 ⁡ ( 10 − 7 ) \mathcal{O}(10^{-7}) as well, with bounds from τ → 3 ​ e \tau\to 3e also yielding strong bounds. For FV Z Z couplings to μ ​ e \mu e , we find that the strongest bounds are obtained from the decay μ → 3 ​ e \mu\to 3e , reaching 𝒪 ⁡ ( 10 − 11 ) \mathcal{O}(10^{-11}) , with bounds from μ → e ​ γ \mu\to e\gamma , muon conversion, and K L 0 → μ ​ e K_{L}^{0}\to\mu e also providing strong bounds. We also study projections from future experiments, such as the FCC-ee, Belle II and Mu3e. For the Z Z couplings to τ ​ μ \tau\mu , we find that future experiments could improve the bound to 𝒪 ⁡ ( 10 − 6 ) \mathcal{O}(10^{-6}) , whereas for the Z Z couplings to τ ​ e \tau e , we find that future experiments could improve the bound to 𝒪 ⁡ ( 10 − 8 ) \mathcal{O}(10^{-8}) , and for the Z Z couplings to μ ​ e \mu e , they could improve the bound to 𝒪 ⁡ ( 10 − 13 ) \mathcal{O}(10^{-13}) .

LEP searches involving

To find the cross-section, we express the total matrix element as
follows

\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{\text{SM}}+\mathcal{M}_{\text{FV}},

(17)

where
\mathcal{M}_{\text{SM}}
(
\mathcal{M}_{\text{FV}}

by the s- (t-) channel. Working to the LO in
g_{L,R}^{el}

easy to see that the leading FV contribution arises from the cross
terms between the SM and the FV contributions
2\text{Re}(\mathcal{M}_{\text{SM}}\mathcal{M}_{\text{FV}}^{*})

and is given by

\displaystyle\delta\sigma_{\text{FV}}(e^{+}e^{-}\to\ell^{-}\ell^{+})

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{16\pi s}\Bigg[2\left(1+\frac{m_{Z}^{2}}{s}\right)^{2}\log\left(1+\frac{s}{m_{Z}^{2}}\right)-\left(3+\frac{2m_{Z}^{2}}{s}\right)\Bigg]\times

(18)

\displaystyle\left(\left(g_{L}^{2}\frac{s}{s-m_{Z}^{2}}+4\pi\alpha\right)|g_{L}^{e\ell}|^{2}+\left(g_{R}^{2}\frac{s}{s-m_{Z}^{2}}+4\pi\alpha\right)|g_{R}^{e\ell}|^{2}\right).

This can be used to extract bounds on the FV
Z
couplings by demanding
that the FV cross-sections in Eq. (
18
) be within
the measured experimental uncertainties. The measured total cross-section
of these processes can be found in
[
55
]
, where we
find that the
1\sigma

are
0.088

\sqrt{s}=207
GeV. These measurements translate to
the bounds
\sqrt{|g_{L}^{\mu e}|^{2}+0.91|g_{R}^{\mu e}|^{2}}<5.62\times 10^{-2}

and
\sqrt{|g_{L}^{\tau e}|^{2}+0.91|g_{R}^{\tau e}|^{2}}<6.28\times 10^{-2}
.
We point out that future

[
56

[
57

III.3
Constraints from Loop Correction to the
Z\to\ell_{i}^{+}\ell_{i}^{-}

Decay

Figure 2:
Tree level and FV corrections to
Z\to\ell_{i}^{+}\ell_{i}^{-}
at
one loop at LO in the FV couplings.

Although the
Z
decay to a pair of identical leptons does not have
FV couplings at tree level, it nonetheless can receive contributions
from FV couplings at 1-loop as shown in Fig.
2
. Thus,
it is possible to extract bounds on the FV
Z
couplings, especially
since the branching ratios of the leptonic
Z
decays are well-measured.
In our calculation, we drop terms of
O(\frac{m_{i,j}^{2}}{m_{Z}^{2}})
.
There are two types of FV corrections at 1-loop: Vertex corrections
and leg corrections. As it turns out, the leading terms in the leg
corrections are of
O(\frac{m_{i,j}^{2}}{m_{Z}^{2}})
, thus we neglect
them and only consider the vertex corrections. Using dimensional regularization
in the
\overline{\text{MS}}
scheme, the leading FV vertex corrections
read

\Gamma(Z\to\ell_{i}^{+}\ell_{i}^{-})\simeq\Gamma_{0}+\frac{(16\pi^{2}-171)}{1536\pi^{3}}(g_{L}^{2}|g_{L}^{ij}|^{2}+g_{R}^{2}|g_{R}^{ij}|^{2})m_{Z},

(19)

where
\Gamma_{0}=\frac{1}{24\pi}(g_{L}^{2}+g_{R}^{2})m_{Z}
is the
tree-level decay width. The latest bounds on the leptonic
Z
decays
are given by
[
58
]

\displaystyle\text{Br}(Z\to e^{+}e^{-})

\displaystyle=(3363.2\pm 4.2)\times 10^{-3}\%,

(20)

\displaystyle\text{Br}(Z\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-})

\displaystyle=(3366.2\pm 6.6)\times 10^{-3}\%,

(21)

\displaystyle\text{Br}(Z\to\tau^{+}\tau^{-})

\displaystyle=(3369.6\pm 8.3)\times 10^{-3}\%.

(22)

Thus, to obtain the
90\%
CL bound, we require

\frac{\delta\Gamma(Z\to\ell_{i}^{+}\ell_{i}^{-})}{\Gamma_{Z}}=\frac{\Gamma(Z\to\ell_{i}^{+}\ell_{i}^{-})-\Gamma_{0}}{\Gamma_{Z}}\lesssim 1.7\times\delta\text{Br}^{\text{Exp}}(Z\to\ell_{i}^{+}\ell_{i}^{-}),

(23)

where
\Gamma_{Z}
is the experimentally-measured
Z
decay width,
and we have introduced a factor of 1.7 to shift the bound to a
90\%

CL, since the experimental bounds are quoted with a scale factor of

1.7
, and
\delta\text{Br}^{\text{Exp}}
can be read from Eqs. (
20
-
22
).
For the first experimental bound,
\ell_{i}=e
, which means that
FV bounds can be extracted by setting
\ell_{j}=\mu,\tau
. These
are given by
\sqrt{g_{L}^{2}|g_{L}^{\mu e}|^{2}+g_{R}^{2}|g_{R}^{\mu e}|^{2}}<8.43\times 10^{-2}

and
\sqrt{g_{L}^{2}|g_{L}^{\tau e}|^{2}+g_{R}^{2}|g_{R}^{\tau e}|^{2}}<8.43\times 10^{-2}
,
respectively. Similarly, for the second bound we have
\ell_{i}=\mu
,
which means that we can obtain FV bounds by setting
\ell_{j}=e,\tau
.
The corresponding bounds are
\sqrt{g_{L}^{2}|g_{L}^{\mu e}|^{2}+g_{R}^{2}|g_{R}^{\mu e}|^{2}}<0.11

and
\sqrt{g_{L}^{2}|g_{L}^{\tau\mu}|^{2}+g_{R}^{2}|g_{R}^{\tau\mu}|^{2}}<0.11
.
Finally, setting
\ell_{i}=\tau
, the FV bounds are obtained with

\ell_{j}=\mu,e
, which read
\sqrt{g_{L}^{2}|g_{L}^{\tau e}|^{2}+g_{R}^{2}|g_{R}^{\tau e}|^{2}}<0.12

and
\sqrt{g_{L}^{2}|g_{L}^{\tau\mu}|^{2}+g_{R}^{2}|g_{R}^{\tau\mu}|^{2}}<0.12
.

Notice that the bounds arising from
Z\to e^{+}e^{-}
are stronger
than these arising from
Z\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
, which are in turn stronger
than these arising from
Z\to\tau^{+}\tau^{-}
, which is consistent
with the experimental limits given in Eqs. (
20
)
- (
22
). However, these bounds are rather weak, which
implies that this is not a promising channel to search for FV in the

Z
sector.

,

]
,
with

. However, FV

III.5
The Michel Parameters

The Michel parameters describe the energy spectrum and angular distribution
of the charged lepton in leptonic decays like
\mu^{-}\to e^{-}\nu_{\mu}\bar{\nu}_{e}

[
62
,
63
,
64
]
.
They are a set of measurable constants that encode the Lorentz structure
(scalar, vector and/or tensor) and the chiral structure of the weak
interaction, providing a powerful test of the SM’s
V-A
coupling.

The Lagrangian in Eq. (
15
) leads to the FV
Z

mediating decay channels
\ell_{i}\to\ell_{j}\bar{\nu}_{\alpha}\nu_{\alpha}

with the amplitude

\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}=\frac{4G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}[\bar{\ell}_{j}\gamma^{\mu}(r_{L}^{ij}P_{L}+r_{R}^{ij}P_{R})\ell_{i}][\bar{\nu}_{\alpha L}\gamma_{\mu}\nu_{\alpha L}],

(26)

with

which gives the Michel parameters as

[

This bound is less stringent than the ones obtained from the Fermi
Constant and the Muon Lifetime as we will see next.

For the tau decay, the
\xi
parameter can be written in the limit

m_{e}/m_{\tau}=m_{\mu}/m_{\tau}\approx 0

\xi=\frac{1-3|r_{L}^{\tau e}|^{2}-3|r_{R}^{\tau e}|^{2}-3|r_{L}^{\tau\mu}|^{2}-3|r_{R}^{\tau\mu}|^{2}}{1+3|r_{L}^{\tau e}|^{2}+3|r_{R}^{\tau e}|^{2}+3|r_{L}^{\tau\mu}|^{2}+3|r_{R}^{\tau\mu}|^{2}},

(29)

with the experimental measurement given by
\xi_{\tau}=0.994\pm 0.008

[
58
]
.
This leads to the bound
\sqrt{|g_{L}^{\tau\mu}|^{2}+|g_{R}^{\tau\mu}|^{2}+|g_{L}^{\tau e}|^{2}+|g_{R}^{\tau e}|^{2}}<6.523\times 10^{-2}
.

Notice that for the diagram on the right, it is possible to integrate

It was suggested in

IV
Conclusions and Outlook

In this paper, we have studied and set upper bounds
on the FV couplings of the
Z
boson to charged leptons. Specifically,
we have studied the bounds from direct searches from the LHC, LEP

e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-},\tau^{+}\tau^{-}
searches, FV loop
corrections to flavor-conserving
Z
decays to leptons, corrections
to the EWPO,
\ell_{i}\to\ell_{j}\gamma
decays,
\ell_{i}\to 3\ell_{j}

decays, corrections to the Michel parameters and to
G_{F}
, FV leptonic
meson decays,
\tau\to\mu(e)+\text{meson}
decays, muon conversion
in nuclei and from muonium-antimuonium oscillation.

For
Z
couplings to
\tau\mu
, we found that
\tau\to\mu\gamma

yielded the strongest bounds, at a level of
\mathcal{O}(10^{-5})
,
followed by
\tau\to 3\mu
. We also found that future experiments
could improve the bound to
\mathcal{O}(10^{-6})
. On the other hand,
for
Z
couplings to
\tau e
, we found that
\tau\to 3e,\mu\mu e

yielded the strongest bounds, reaching a level of
\mathcal{O}(10^{-7})

as well, followed by
\tau\to e+\gamma
. We also found that future
experiments involving
\tau\to 3e
can improve the bound to
\mathcal{O}(10^{-8})
.
Finally, for
Z
couplings to
\mu e
, we found that
\mu\to 3e

yielded the strongest bounds, reaching a level of
\mathcal{O}(10^{-11})
,
followed by
\mu\to e\gamma
, muon conversion in nuclei,
K_{L}^{0}\to\mu e

and
\mu\to e+\text{inv}
. We also found that future experiments
involving
\mu\to 3e
can improve the bound to
\mathcal{O}(10^{-13})
.
In addition, we also found that bounds obtained from meson decays
and from
\tau
decays involving mesons, could yield significant
bounds, ranging between
\mathcal{O}(10^{-2})-\mathcal{O}(10^{-8})
,
thereby representing promising venues for searching for FV in the

Z
sector, in addition to the processes highlighted above. In general,
we find that bounds on the
Z
couplings to
\mu e
are several
orders of magnitude stronger than those to
\tau\mu
and
\tau e
.

In this study, we limited ourselves to FV in the
Z
interactions
with leptons. FV
Z
couplings to quarks were treated in
[
47
]
.
Furthermore, and as mentioned in the introduction, there has been
significant interest in studying the possibility of FV in the Higgs
sector, with stringent bounds already existing in the literature (see
for instance
[
31
]
). Some of the processes discussed
herein were also used to set bounds on the FV couplings of the Higgs,
however, as several of the processes discussed here are novel, such
as the decays involving mesons, it would interesting to evaluate the
bounds that can be extracted from them on the Higgs interactions.
We plan to do this in a future work. We conclude by pointing out that
FV represents an interesting venue for potential BSM physics and thus
merits attention.

Appendix D

M\to\overline{M}
Oscillation

We can write the interaction Lagrangian of the muonium-antimuonium
oscillation as follows
[

]
(see also
[

\mathcal{L}_{M\to\overline{M}}=-\sum_{i=1}^{5}\frac{G_{i}}{\sqrt{2}}Q_{i},

(71)

where

\displaystyle Q_{1}

\displaystyle=[\bar{\mu}\gamma_{\alpha}(1-\gamma_{5})e][\bar{\mu}\gamma^{\alpha}(1-\gamma_{5})e],

\displaystyle Q_{2}

\displaystyle=[\bar{\mu}\gamma_{\alpha}(1+\gamma_{5})e][\bar{\mu}\gamma^{\alpha}(1+\gamma_{5})e],

(73)

\displaystyle Q_{3}

\displaystyle=[\bar{\mu}\gamma_{\alpha}(1+\gamma_{5})e][\bar{\mu}\gamma^{\alpha}(1-\gamma_{5})e],

(74)

\displaystyle Q_{4}

\displaystyle=[\bar{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})e][\bar{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})e],

(75)

\displaystyle Q_{5}

\displaystyle=[\bar{\mu}(1+\gamma_{5})e][\bar{\mu}(1+\gamma_{5})e],

(76)

with all other operators related to the 5 above by linear combination

8
,

Q_{1}
,
Q_{2}
and

[
90
]

[
91

[
92
]

Z^{\prime}

=m

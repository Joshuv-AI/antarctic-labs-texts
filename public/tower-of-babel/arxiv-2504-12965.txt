Topological lax comma categories
Maria Manuel Clementino, Dirk Hofmann, Rui Prezado

Abstract
This paper investigates the interplay between properties of a topological space X X , in particular of its natural order, and properties of the lax comma category 𝖳𝗈𝗉 ⇓ X \mathsf{Top}{\,\Downarrow\,}X , where 𝖳𝗈𝗉 \mathsf{Top} denotes the category of topological spaces and continuous maps. Namely, it is shown that, whenever X X is a topological ⋀ \bigwedge -semilattice, the canonical forgetful functor 𝖳𝗈𝗉 ⇓ X → 𝖳𝗈𝗉 \mathsf{Top}{\,\Downarrow\,}X\to\mathsf{Top} is topological, preserves and reflects exponentials, and preserves effective descent morphisms. Moreover, under additional conditions on X X , a characterisation of effective descent morphisms is obtained. Keywords: exponentiability, descent theory, lax comma category, order topologies 2020 MSC: 06B30, 06B35, 06F30, 18A25, 18F60, 18F20, 22A26, 54B30

With respect to the natural order of a space, every open subset is down-closed, likewise, every closed subset is up-closed. Given now an ordered set
Z

Z
which induce the given order. Here we list three such topologies (see

(1)

(2)

on

(3)

on

Clearly, the lower topology is included in the lower Scott topology which in turn is included in the lower Alexandroff topology. Furthermore, the lower topology is the coarsest topology inducing the given order, while the lower Alexandroff topology is the largest such topology. A topological space is called an
Alexandroff space
whenever its topology coincides with the lower Alexandroff topology of its natural order; equivalently, whenever arbitrary unions of closed subsets are closed.

2

We recall that a category

is said to be

has products along

and the functor

has a right adjoint, usually denoted by

said to be an

\mathsf{Top}
and
\mathsf{Top}{\,\downarrow\,}X
(for every non-empty topological space

) are not cartesian closed categories, and that the forgetful functor
\mathsf{Top}{\,\downarrow\,}X\to\mathsf{Top}
does not preserve exponentials in general. Their exponentiable objects were characterized in
[

,
36
,
35
,
41
,
42
,
5
,

]

preserves neither products nor exponentials (unless

is a singleton).

]

\displaystyle\Lan_{1_{A}\times q}(\alpha\sqcap\gamma)(a,y)

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{W\times V\in\mathcal{O}(a,y)}\bigvee\{\alpha(a^{\prime})\wedge\gamma(c)\mid(a^{\prime},c)\in(1_{A}\times q)^{-1}(W\times V)\}

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{V\in\mathcal{O}(y)}\bigwedge_{W\in\mathcal{O}(a)}\bigvee_{a^{\prime}\in W}\bigvee_{c\in q^{-1}(V)}\alpha(a^{\prime})\wedge\gamma(c).

Using Remark

\bigwedge_{W\in\mathcal{O}(a)}\bigvee_{a^{\prime}\in W}\Big(\bigvee_{c\in q^{-1}(V)}\alpha(a^{\prime})\wedge\gamma(c)\Big)=\bigvee_{c\in q^{-1}(V)}\alpha(a)\wedge\gamma(c).

Therefore

\Lan_{1_{A}\times q}(\alpha\sqcap\gamma)(a,y)=\bigwedge_{V\in\mathcal{O}(y)}\bigvee_{c\in q^{-1}(V)}\alpha(a)\wedge\gamma(c).

Observing that

(\alpha\sqcap\Lan_{q}\gamma)(a,y)=\alpha(a)\wedge\Big(\bigwedge_{V\in\mathcal{O}(y)}\bigvee_{c\in q^{-1}(V)}\gamma(c)\Big)=\bigwedge_{V\in\mathcal{O}(y)}\Big(\alpha(a)\wedge\bigvee_{c\in q^{-1}(V)}\gamma(c)\Big),

3
Descent

Let
p\colon A\to B
be a morphism in a category
\mathsf{C}
with
pullbacks. In our fundamental setting, descent theory for
p
refers to
the problem of studying whether bundles over
A
equipped with some
additional algebraic structure (specified by
p
) – called

descent data for p
– can be regarded as bundles over
B
.

More specifically, bundles over
A
equipped with such descent data for
p
form a category, denoted
\mathsf{Desc}_{\mathsf{C}}(p)

pullback change-of-base functor
p^{*}\colon\mathsf{C}{\,\downarrow\,}B\to\mathsf{C}{\,\downarrow\,}A
factors through, depicted as follows:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{C}{\,\downarrow\,}B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{C}{\,\downarrow\,}A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Desc}_{\mathsf{C}}(p)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{K}^{p}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{U}^{p}}

(3.i)

In Diagram (

), the functor
\mathcal{U}^{p}
forgets
the descent data, and
\mathcal{K}^{p}
–
the comparison functor
–
maps a bundle

By the Bénabou-Roubaud theorem

(see

24

is monadic. Similarly,

is a

is fully faithful, that is, if

The descent morphisms in

In this section we give, for suitable spaces

\mathsf{Top}{\,\Downarrow\,}X
.

Local well-posedness of the Benjamin-Ono equation with spatially quasiperiodic data
Sultan Aitzhan, David M. Ambrose

Abstract
. We consider the Benjamin-Ono equation in the spatially quasiperiodic setting. We establish local well-posedness of the initial value problem with initial data in quasiperiodic Sobolev spaces. This requires developing some of the fundamental properties of Sobolev spaces and the energy method for quasiperiodic functions. We discuss prospects for global existence. We demonstrate that while conservation laws still hold, these quantities no longer control the associated Sobolev norms, thereby preventing the establishment of global results by usual arguments.

1.
Introduction

The Benjamin-Ono equation is a dispersive equation used in modeling water waves, given by

u_{t}=uu_{x}+Hu_{xx}.

Here,
H
stands for the Hilbert transform, which is defined by

Hu(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\mathrm{p.v.}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\frac{u(y)}{x-y}\,\mathrm{d}y

on the real line and

Hu(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\mathrm{p.v.}\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}u(y)\cot\Big(\frac{x-y}{2}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}y

on the circle.
Amongst its features, the Benjamin-Ono equation constitutes an integrable system and possesses multi-soliton solutions (see
[
6
]
).
The well-posedness theory for periodic and real-line decaying data is rich, and for brevity we refer the reader to the results of
[
18
,
14
]
and references therein.
However, the case of quasiperiodic initial data, such as

u_{0}(x)=\cos(x)+\cos(\sqrt{2}x),

is yet to be fully addressed, and in this paper we undertake the case of sufficiently regular quasiperiodic initial data.

Amongst other dispersive equations, some significant progress for the quasiperiodic problem is made in the case of the KdV and the NLS equations.
Namely, quasiperiodic KdV equation is addressed in
[
27
,
7
,
4
]
and quasiperiodic NLS equations are considered in
[
25
,
8
,
9
,
32
]
.
The case of quasiperiodic data for other dispersive equations, including more general ones, is also considered in
[
34
,
22
,
10
,
11
]
.
Beyond dispersive PDE, we very briefly mention that there are quasiperiodic well-posedness results for Euler equations
[
26
]
, and computational results for quasiperiodic water waves in
[
28
,
29
,
30
,
31
,
12
]
.

In what follows, we describe some of the above results in relation to our work.
Some of these results
[
22
,
34
,
25
,
27
]
are local.
The methods used are traditional, which include the Picard iteration style argument (as in the case of
[
27
,
22
,
25
]
), and energy estimates (as in
[
34
]
).
Our work also involves proving energy estimates; as a result, our paper is closer in spirit to
[
34
]
.
The key difference between our work and
[
34
]
is the following.
Quasiperiodic functions are a subset of almost periodic functions, and
[
34
]
provides local well-posedness for almost periodic data.
However, the energy method as employed in
[
34
]
, which uses a localized Sobolev norm, cannot tell if the standard Sobolev regularity is preserved.

While the papers mentioned in the previous paragraph (including the present work) do provide solutions, these solutions are local in time.
In the standard case of the periodic and decaying data, these solutions become global by means of conservation laws.
This happens due to the fact that these conservation laws control the associated Sobolev norms.
In the quasiperiodic case, however, it does not seem obvious if there are any quantities that control the norms used in
[
22
,
34
,
25
,
27
]
.
The lack of such quantities makes it extremely difficult to extend solutions to arbitary time of existence.

In the case of results in
[
7
,
4
,
10
,
8
,
9
,
11
]
, these papers provide global existence and uniqueness to KdV and NLS-type equations.
These works rely on studying spectral properties of related Schrödinger operators, and thus are somewhat different from traditional approaches.
Furthermore, these works generally assume a Diophantine condition and consider Fourier series with exponential decay as the initial data.
Since exponential decay in the Fourier space implies that the data is analytic, these papers focus on a very specific class of data.
We remark that the result of
[
22
]
is a local existence result, while also considering an analytic class of initial data, although this
work does not include any Diophantine assumptions.
Furthermore, we note here that in a recent work
[
11
]
, a family of generalized Benjamin-Bona-Mahony (BBM) equations are studied.
In the case of BBM, they produce local well-posedness for initial data with a polynomial rate of decay; the authors there again use the
combinatorial analysis method of their previous works. As the present work uses a quasiperiodic Sobolev space, there is no assumption of exponential
decay of the Fourier series, and we likewise use no Diophantine conditions.

Unlike in the works listed in the above paragraph, we do not attempt to minimize the rate of decay or attempt to prove global existence.
Instead, we would like to tred somewhere in-between and provide a framework that can be adapted to other dispersive equations, similarly to the results of
[
22
,
34
,
25
]
.
In particular, we use a regularized system which, when combined with the method of initial data regularization Bona and Smith
[
5
]
, permits to reproduce the classical results of
[
24
,
1
]
in the quasiperiodic setting.
While the proof is tailored for the Benjamin-Ono equation, the method is general enough to encompass more equations, including KdV equation.

We choose the Benjamin-Ono equation specifically because the contraction mapping approach,
as applied directly to the Benjamin-Ono equation, is known to fail at any regularity.
In fact, the flow map from the initial data to solutions fails to be uniformly continuous
[
17
,
19
]

and the bilinear estimates cannot hold at all, as indicated in
[
15
, Theorem 4.3.1]
.
This suggests that to have any theory of well-posedness, one at least needs to develop the energy method for the quasiperiodic Benjamin-Ono equation with sufficiently differentiable data.
In fact, in proving well-posedness for
L^{2}
-based data on torus
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
, it was first shown that the estimates were satisfied on smooth functions.
Of course, this presupposes existence of smooth solutions, and the goal of this paper is to prove existence of their quasiperiodic counterparts.

We now describe quasiperiodic functions.
For a fixed natural number
N>1
, let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{N})
satisfy
\alpha\cdot k\neq 0
for
k\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}=\mathbb{Z}^{N}\setminus\{0,\ldots,0\}.

For
j=1,\ldots,N
let
f_{j}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\setminus 2\pi\alpha_{j}^{-1}\mathbb{Z})
and consider functions of the form

(1)

f(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}f_{j}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{f_{j}}(k)\exp(i\alpha_{j}kx),

where
\hat{f_{j}}(k)
are the Fourier coefficients.
The condition
\alpha\cdot k\neq 0
ensures that
f(x)
is not a periodic function.
Instead, it is quasiperiodic.
Note that the space of functions of the form (
1
) does not form an algebra, so this space does not suit the analysis due to the presence of the quadratic term in Benjamin-Ono equation.
As such, we introduce the following space:

Definition 1

(Quasiperiodic Sobolev spaces)
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R},
and define a space
H^{s}_{qp}
of functions of the form

u(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\hat{u}(k)\exp(i\alpha\cdot kx)

with the following norm:

\|u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}^{2}:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}(1+k_{1}^{2}+\ldots+k_{N}^{2})^{s}\left\lvert\hat{u}\right\rvert^{2}(k)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}(1+|k|^{2})^{s}\left\lvert\hat{u}\right\rvert^{2}(k)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\left\langle|k|\right\rangle^{2s}\left\lvert\hat{u}\right\rvert^{2}(k).

Functions of the form (
1
) clearly belong to
H^{s}_{qp}
for large enough
s
.
The subscript
qp
in
H^{s}_{qp}
stands for quasiperiodic, and it is clear how these spaces generalize the usual Sobolev spaces for periodic functions.
We note that this space is already mentioned in
[
25
]
for NLS and KdV equations and
[
26
]
for Euler equations.

We state the main result of this paper.

Theorem 2
.

Let
s>N/2+1
and let
u_{0}\in H^{s}_{qp}.

There exists
T:=T(s,\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}})>0
such that the Benjamin-Ono equation has a unique solution
u\in C([0,T];H^{s}_{qp})
.
Furthermore, the solution
u
depends continuously on the initial data.

Even though in our analysis we routinely use that
N\geqslant 2
, when
N=1
we recover the classical results of
[
1
,
16
]
.
The method of the proof is by regularizing the original Benjamin-Ono equation by truncation in the Fourier space:

(2)

(u)_{t}=\chi_{n}[(\chi_{n}u)(\chi_{n}u_{x})]+\chi_{n}[Hu_{xx}],

where operators
\chi_{n}
and
H
are defined via Fourier transform:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\chi_{n}[u](k)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{I}_{|\alpha\cdot k|<n}\mathcal{F}u(k)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}H[u](k)

\displaystyle=-i\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot k)\mathcal{F}u(k),

where
\mathbb{I}_{\Omega}(x)
is the usual indicator function on a set
\Omega
.
The definition of the quasiperiodic Hilbert transform is not new and has already appeared in a number of works, see
[
28
,
29
]
.

Outline of the paper:

In Section 2, we explain some properties of standard Sobolev spaces to
H^{s}_{qp}
spaces.
We also elaborate on convergence and divergence conditions for the quasiperiodic Fourier series in
H^{s}_{qp}
spaces.
In Section 3, we prove existence of solutions to the regularized system, obtain uniform estimates on the approximate solutions, as well as a Cauchy property of these solutions.
We also prove the properties of data regularization in the method of Bona and Smith.
Finally, in Section 4, we prove local well-posedness of the original problem.
In Section 5, we demonstrate that the standard conservation laws still hold in the quasiperiodic case, and yet that these conservation laws do not control the norms.

f(x)/g(x)\to 0

2.
Preliminary tools

The following two lemmas allow us to make sense of quasiperiodic Fourier series.

Proposition 3

(Convergence of quasiperiodic Fourier series in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
)
.

Let
s>(N-1)/2
. Let
\psi\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
,
f\in H^{s}_{qp}
. Then,
\langle f,\psi\rangle<\infty
.

Proof.

Using the definition of Fourier series and applying Fubini theorem to intechange the integral and the sum, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle f,\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)\psi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}}\hat{f}(k)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\psi(x)\exp(i\alpha\cdot kx)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}}\hat{f}(k)\hat{\psi}(\alpha\cdot k).

Adding the product
\left\langle k\right\rangle^{s}\left\langle k\right\rangle^{-s}
and applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields

|\langle f,\psi\rangle|\leqslant\|f\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\left[\sum_{k\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}}\left\langle k\right\rangle^{-2s}|\hat{\psi}|^{2}(\alpha\cdot k)\right]^{1/2}.

Using the inequality

\left\langle k\right\rangle^{-2s}\leqslant\prod_{i=2}^{N}\left\langle k_{i}\right\rangle^{-2s/(N-1)}

yields

\sum_{k\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}}\left\langle k\right\rangle^{-2s}|\hat{\psi}|^{2}(\alpha\cdot k)\leqslant\sum_{k\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}}\prod_{i=2}^{N}\left\langle k_{i}\right\rangle^{-2s/(N-1)}|\hat{\psi}|^{2}(\alpha\cdot k).

Rearrange the summation to obtain

\sum_{k\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}}\prod_{i=2}^{N}\left\langle k_{i}\right\rangle^{-2s/(N-1)}|\hat{\psi}|^{2}(\alpha\cdot k)\leqslant\sum_{i=2}^{N-1}\sum_{k_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}}\prod_{i=2}^{N}\left\langle k_{i}\right\rangle^{-2s/(N-1)}\sum_{k_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}|\hat{\psi}|^{2}(\alpha\cdot k).

and note that the sum in
k_{1}
converges by integral test, for we have

\int_{k_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}|\hat{\psi}|^{2}(\alpha\cdot k)\sim_{\alpha}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

and
\phi\in L^{2}_{x}.

Furthermore, this bound is independent of
k_{2},\ldots,k_{N}
, so that we are left with estimating

\sum_{i=2}^{N-1}\sum_{k_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}}\prod_{i=2}^{N}\left\langle k_{i}\right\rangle^{-2s/(N-1)}.

Since
2s/(N-1)>1
, each of these sums converges and we obtain
\langle f,\psi\rangle<\infty
.
∎

The lemma belows shows that the restriction
s>(N-1)/2
is necessary.

Proposition 4

(Divergence of quasiperiodic Fourier series in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
)
.

Let
s\leqslant(N-1)/2
. Then, there exists a sequence
\{f_{n}\}\subset H^{s}_{qp}
which satisfies the following:

•

\sup_{n}\|f_{n}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}<\infty,

•

for some
\phi\in\mathcal{D},\langle f_{n},\mathcal{F}_{\chi}^{-1}\phi\rangle=\langle\mathcal{F}_{x}f_{n},\phi\rangle\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

We follow the lines of proof of
[
27
, Lemma 5.2]
, but adapted to the
H^{s}_{qp}
spaces, and supplying further details.
Without loss of generality, assume that the entries of
\alpha
are all positive, i.e.
\alpha_{i}>0
for
i=1,\ldots,N
.
Let
\bar{k}
denote a vector of the first
N-1
entries of
k
, i.e.
\bar{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{N-1})
.

Let

\displaystyle A_{n}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\left\langle x\right\rangle\leqslant n,2|\alpha_{N}|\leqslant|\alpha\cdot x|\leqslant 4|\alpha_{N}|\},

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:2|\alpha_{N}|\leqslant|\alpha\cdot x|\leqslant 4|\alpha_{N}|\},

\displaystyle\hat{f_{n}}(k)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\left\langle k\right\rangle^{1-N}(\log\left\langle k\right\rangle)^{-1},&\text{ if }k\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}\cap A_{n},\\
0&\text{ else }.\end{cases}

Let
\phi(\xi)\in\mathcal{D}
such that
\phi(\xi)=1
for
2|\alpha_{N}|\leqslant|\xi|\leqslant 4|\alpha_{N}|
and
\phi(\xi)=0
for
k\leqslant|\alpha_{N}|
or
k\geqslant 5|\alpha_{N}|.

Since
\phi
is in
\mathcal{D}
, it is also Schwartz.
Since Fourier transform is an isometry on the Schwartz space, we have that
\mathcal{F}_{\chi}^{-1}\phi
is also Schwartz.

Now, use the Fourier series and Fubini theorem to write

\displaystyle\langle f_{n},\mathcal{F}_{\chi}^{-1}\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f_{n}(x)\mathcal{F}_{\chi}^{-1}\phi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\cdot{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}}\hat{f_{n}}(k)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal{F}_{\chi}^{-1}\phi(x)\exp(i\alpha\cdot kx)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\cdot{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}}\hat{f_{n}}(k)\phi(\alpha\cdot k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\cap A_{n},|k|\geqslant 1}\left\langle k\right\rangle^{1-N}(\log\left\langle k\right\rangle)^{-1}.

As
n\to\infty,
we need to show that the limit of the above sums diverges, i.e. that

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N},|k|\geqslant 1,k\in A}\left\langle k\right\rangle^{1-N}(\log\left\langle k\right\rangle)^{-1}=\infty.

We prove divergence by integral test.
Indeed, consider the integral

\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},|x|\geqslant 1,x\in A}\left\langle x\right\rangle^{1-N}(\log\left\langle x\right\rangle)^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Write
A
as a disjoint union of
A_{+}
and
A_{-}
, where

A_{\pm}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:2|\alpha_{N}|\leqslant\pm\alpha\cdot x\leqslant 4|\alpha_{N}|\}.

Then, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},|x|\geqslant 1,x\in A}\left\langle x\right\rangle^{1-N}(\log\left\langle x\right\rangle)^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},|x|\geqslant 1,x\in A_{+}}\left\langle x\right\rangle^{1-N}(\log\left\langle x\right\rangle)^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},|x|\geqslant 1,x\in A_{-}}\left\langle x\right\rangle^{1-N}(\log\left\langle x\right\rangle)^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}x.

We show that the integral over
A_{+}
diverges; the argument for
A_{-}
is almost the same.
Note that for
x\in A_{+}
, we have
2\alpha_{N}\leqslant\bar{\alpha}\cdot\bar{x}+\alpha_{N}x_{N}\leqslant 4\alpha_{N}.

Subtracting
\bar{\alpha}\cdot\bar{x}
and dividing both sides by
\alpha_{N}
yields

2-\frac{\bar{\alpha}\cdot\bar{x}}{\alpha_{N}}\leqslant x_{N}\leqslant 4-\frac{\bar{\alpha}\cdot\bar{x}}{\alpha_{N}}.

We then obtain that
\left\langle x\right\rangle^{2}=1+|\bar{x}|^{2}+x_{N}^{2}\leqslant C^{2}\left\langle\bar{x}\right\rangle^{2},
where
C
is some positive constant with
C>2
.
Now, the integral over
A_{+}
becomes

\displaystyle\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},|x|\geqslant 1,x\in A_{+}}

\displaystyle\left\langle x\right\rangle^{1-N}(\log\left\langle x\right\rangle)^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{x_{1}}\ldots\int_{x_{N-1}}\int_{2-\frac{\bar{\alpha}\cdot\bar{x}}{\alpha_{N}}}^{4-\frac{\bar{\alpha}\cdot\bar{x}}{\alpha_{N}}}\left\langle x\right\rangle^{1-N}(\log\left\langle x\right\rangle)^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}x_{N}\,\mathrm{d}x_{N-1}\ldots\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}.

Since
\left\langle x\right\rangle\leqslant C\left\langle\bar{x}\right\rangle
, we bound the integrand from below by

\displaystyle\int_{x_{1}}

\displaystyle\ldots\int_{x_{N-1}}\int_{2-\frac{\bar{\alpha}\cdot\bar{x}}{\alpha_{N}}}^{4-\frac{\bar{\alpha}\cdot\bar{x}}{\alpha_{N}}}\left\langle x\right\rangle^{1-N}(\log\left\langle x\right\rangle)^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}x_{N}\,\mathrm{d}x_{N-1}\ldots\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}

\displaystyle\geqslant\int_{x_{1}}\ldots\int_{x_{N-1}}\int_{2-\frac{\bar{\alpha}\cdot\bar{x}}{\alpha_{N}}}^{4-\frac{\bar{\alpha}\cdot\bar{x}}{\alpha_{N}}}\frac{1}{C\left\langle\bar{x}\right\rangle^{N-1}(\log C+\log\left\langle\bar{x}\right\rangle)}\,\mathrm{d}x_{N}\,\mathrm{d}x_{N-1}\ldots\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{C}\int_{x_{1}}\ldots\int_{x_{N-1}}\frac{1}{\left\langle\bar{x}\right\rangle^{N-1}(\log C+\log\left\langle\bar{x}\right\rangle)}\,\mathrm{d}x_{N-1}\ldots\,\mathrm{d}x_{1},

where we have used the fact that the integrand no longer depends on
x_{N}
.
For simplicity, relabel
x:=\bar{x}
, so that we are left with showing that

\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\frac{1}{\left\langle x\right\rangle^{N-1}(\log C+\log\left\langle x\right\rangle)}\,\mathrm{d}x

diverges.
This is straightforward. First, restrict the integration region to
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}:|x|>C\}
:

\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\frac{1}{\left\langle x\right\rangle^{N-1}(\log C+\log\left\langle x\right\rangle)}\,\mathrm{d}x\geqslant\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}:|x|>C}\frac{1}{\left\langle x\right\rangle^{N-1}(\log C+\log\left\langle x\right\rangle)}\,\mathrm{d}x.

We can then bound
\log C+\log\left\langle x\right\rangle\leqslant 2\log\left\langle x\right\rangle
, so that

\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\frac{1}{\left\langle x\right\rangle^{N-1}(\log C+\log\left\langle x\right\rangle)}\,\mathrm{d}x\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}:|x|>C}\frac{1}{\left\langle x\right\rangle^{N-1}\log\left\langle x\right\rangle}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since
C>2
, we have

\left\langle x\right\rangle^{N-1}\log\left\langle x\right\rangle<2|x|^{N-1}(\log 2|x|)<4|x|^{N-1}\log|x|.

Therefore, we need to estimate

\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}:|x|>C}\frac{1}{|x|^{N-1}\log|x|}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Using polar coordinates yields that

\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}:|x|>C}\frac{1}{|x|^{N-1}\log|x|}\,\mathrm{d}x\sim_{N-1}\int_{C}^{\infty}\frac{r^{N-2}}{r^{N-1}\log r}\,\mathrm{d}r=\int_{C}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r\log r}\,\mathrm{d}r.

The latter integral diverges, as can be shown by substitution
y=\log r
.

To show
\sup_{n}\|f_{n}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}<\infty,
note that

\displaystyle\sup_{n}\|f_{n}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{n}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N},|k|\geqslant 1}\left\langle k\right\rangle^{2s}\left\lvert\hat{f_{n}}(k)\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{n}\sum_{k\in A_{n}}\left\langle k\right\rangle^{2s}\left\langle k\right\rangle^{2(1-N)}(\log\left\langle k\right\rangle)^{-2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}\\
2|\alpha_{N}|\leqslant|\alpha\cdot k|\leqslant 4|\alpha_{N}|\end{subarray}}\left\langle k\right\rangle^{2(s+1-N)}(\log\left\langle k\right\rangle)^{-2}.

We split the summation as follows:

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}\\
2|\alpha_{N}|\leqslant|\alpha\cdot k|\leqslant 4|\alpha_{N}|\end{subarray}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}\\
2|\alpha_{N}|\leqslant|\alpha\cdot k|\leqslant 4|\alpha_{N}|\\
|\bar{k}|\geqslant 1\end{subarray}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}\\
2|\alpha_{N}|\leqslant|\alpha\cdot k|\leqslant 4|\alpha_{N}|\\
|\bar{k}|<1\end{subarray}}.

Note that the second sum over
|\bar{k}|<1
has only 6 summands.
Since
k\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{N},
we have
|k|\geqslant 1
.
But
|\bar{k}|<1
implies
k_{1}=\ldots=k_{N-1}=0,
so that
|k_{N}|>1
.
Now, the condition
2|\alpha_{N}|\leqslant|\alpha\cdot k|\leqslant 4|\alpha_{N}|
over this subset implies that
2\leqslant|k_{N}|\leqslant 4
, and there are only 6 elements that satisfy this condition.
Hence, the sum over
k
’s such that
|\bar{k}|<1
is bounded, and we only need to show that the first sum converges.
For this, by integral test it is enough to show that the integral

\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},|x|\geqslant 1\\
x\in A\\
|\bar{x}|\geqslant 1\end{subarray}}\left\langle x\right\rangle^{2(s+1-N)}(\log\left\langle x\right\rangle)^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}x

converges.
Since we can write the region
A
as disjoint union of
A_{+}
and
A_{-}
, we only consider the integral of
A_{+}
.
As before, over
A_{+}
we know that

2-\frac{\bar{\alpha}\cdot\bar{x}}{\alpha_{N}}\leqslant x_{N}\leqslant 4-\frac{\bar{\alpha}\cdot\bar{x}}{\alpha_{N}},

so that

\displaystyle\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\cap A_{+},\\
|x|\geqslant 1,|\bar{x}|\geqslant 1\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\left\langle x\right\rangle^{2(s+1-N)}(\log\left\langle x\right\rangle)^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{x_{1}}\ldots\int_{x_{N-1}}\int_{2-\frac{\bar{\alpha}\cdot\bar{x}}{\alpha_{N}}}^{4-\frac{\bar{\alpha}\cdot\bar{x}}{\alpha_{N}}}\left\langle x\right\rangle^{2(s+1-N)}(\log\left\langle x\right\rangle)^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}x_{N}\,\mathrm{d}x_{N-1}\ldots\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}.

Since
\left\langle\bar{x}\right\rangle\leqslant\left\langle x\right\rangle
, we can bound the integrand
\left\langle x\right\rangle^{2(s+1-N)}(\log\left\langle x\right\rangle)^{-2}
by
\left\langle\bar{x}\right\rangle^{2(s+1-N)}(\log\left\langle\bar{x}\right\rangle)^{-2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},|x|\geqslant 1\\
x\in A_{+}\\
|\bar{x}|\geqslant 1\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\left\langle x\right\rangle^{2(s+1-N)}(\log\left\langle x\right\rangle)^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\int_{x_{1}}\ldots\int_{x_{N-1}}\left\langle\bar{x}\right\rangle^{2(s+1-N)}(\log\left\langle\bar{x}\right\rangle)^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}x_{N-1}\ldots\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}

\displaystyle=2\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1},|x|\geqslant 1}\left\langle x\right\rangle^{2(s+1-N)}(\log\left\langle x\right\rangle)^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}x,

where we have relabeled
x:=\bar{x}
for convenience.
The last integral can be handled by polar coordinates: we have

\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1},|x|\geqslant 1}\left\langle x\right\rangle^{2(s+1-N)}(\log\left\langle x\right\rangle)^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}x\sim\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{N-2}\left\langle r\right\rangle^{2(s+1-N)}(\log\left\langle r\right\rangle)^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}r.

Since
r<\left\langle r\right\rangle
, the latter is bounded above by

\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{N-2}\left\langle r\right\rangle^{2(s+1-N)}(\log\left\langle r\right\rangle)^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}r\leqslant\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{N-2+2(s+1-N)}(\log r)^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}r=\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{2s-N}(\log r)^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}r.

Since
s\leqslant(N-1)/2
, we have
2s\leqslant N-1,
so that

\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{2s-N}(\log r)^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}r\leqslant\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{-1}(\log r)^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}r.

The last integral is bounded; this can be shown by substitution
y=\log r
.
∎

With more regularity, a quasiperiodic Fourier series makes sense pointwise.

Proposition 5

(Sobolev inequality,
L^{\infty}
bound)
.

Assume
s>N/2
. Let
u\in H^{s}_{qp}.
Then,

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}|\hat{u}|(k)\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}.

Furthermore,
u
is bounded and continuous on
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

The Sobolev inequality is proved by following the arguments of
[
13
, Lemma 6.5]
.
∎

Proposition 6

(Algebra property)
.

Assume
s>N/2.
Let
u,v\in H^{s}_{qp}.
Then

\|uv\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\lesssim_{s}\|u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\|v\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}.

Proof.

The algebra property is obtained by following the arguments of
[
13
, Chapter 6, Exercise 4]
.
∎

Proposition 7

(Fractional Leibniz rule)
.

Define an operator
D^{s}_{x}
by
\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}u}(k)=|k|^{s}\hat{u}(k).

Let
s>1
and
s_{0}>N/2
.
Let
u\in H^{s}_{qp}\cap H^{s_{0}+1}_{qp}
and
v\in H^{s_{0}}_{qp}\cap H^{s-1}_{qp}
.
Then,

\|D^{s}_{x}(uv)-uD^{s}_{x}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\|v\|_{H^{s_{0}}_{qp}}+\|u\|_{H^{s_{0}+1}_{qp}}\|v\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}.

Proof.

To begin, use the convolution theorem to write:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}{(D^{s}_{x}(uv)-uD^{s}_{x}v)}(k)

\displaystyle=|k|^{s}\mathcal{F}{uv}(k)-\mathcal{F}{uD^{s}_{x}v}(k)

\displaystyle=|k|^{s}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\hat{u}(j)\hat{v}(k-j)-\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\hat{u}(j)|k-j|^{s}\hat{v}(k-j)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\big[|k|^{s}-|k-j|^{s}\big]\hat{u}(j)\hat{v}(k-j).

Thus,

\displaystyle\|D^{s}_{x}(uv)-uD^{s}_{x}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\left\lvert\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\big[|k|^{s}-|k-j|^{s}\big]\hat{u}(j)\hat{v}(k-j)\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\left|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\left\lvert|k|^{s}-|k-j|^{s}\right\rvert|\hat{u}(j)||\hat{v}(k-j)|\right|^{2}.

Using the following inequality, from
[
24
]
,

\left\lvert|k|^{s}-|k-j|^{s}\right\rvert\lesssim_{s}|j|^{s}+|k-j|^{s-1}|j|,

as well as triangle inequality yields the following bound

\displaystyle\|D^{s}_{x}(uv)-uD^{s}_{x}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\left|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}|j|^{s}|\hat{u}(j)||\hat{v}(k-j)|\right|^{2}+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\left|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}||k-j|^{s-1}|\alpha\cdot j||\hat{u}(j)||\hat{v}(k-j)|\right|^{2}.

It remains to bound the two sums.

One can bound the first sum by Young’s convolution inequality:

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\left|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}|j|^{s}|\hat{u}(j)||\hat{v}(k-j)|\right|^{2}\lesssim\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}|j|^{2s}|\hat{u}(j)|^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}|\hat{v}(k)|\right)^{2}.

For any
s_{0}>N/2,
Sobolev inequality (Proposition
5
) yields

\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}|\hat{v}(k)|\right)^{2}\lesssim\|v\|_{H^{s_{0}}_{qp}}^{2},

so that the first sum is bounded by

\|D^{s}_{x}u\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}\|v\|_{H^{s_{0}}_{qp}}^{2}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}^{2}\|v\|_{H^{s_{0}}_{qp}}^{2}.

For the second sum, we similarly apply Young’s convolution inequality to obtain

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\left|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}|k-j|^{s-1}|\alpha\cdot j||\hat{u}(j)||\hat{v}(k-j)|\right|^{2}\lesssim\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}|\alpha\cdot k||\hat{u}(k)|\right)^{2}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\left|k\right|^{2(s-1)}|\hat{v}(k)|^{2}\right).

One then applies the Sobolev inequality (Proposition
5
, leading to

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}|\alpha\cdot k||\hat{u}(k)|\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s_{0}+1}_{qp}}.

This lets us bound the second sum by

\|u\|_{H^{s_{0}+1}_{qp}}^{2}\|D^{s-1}_{x}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s_{0}+1}_{qp}}^{2}\|v\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}^{2}.

Therefore, we obtain

\|D^{s}_{x}(uv)-uD^{s}_{x}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}^{2}\|v\|_{H^{s_{0}}_{qp}}^{2}+\|u\|_{H^{s_{0}+1}_{qp}}^{2}\|v\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}^{2},

as desired.
∎

Another tool to be used in the analysis is the interpolation inequality.

Lemma 8
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}.
Let
l>N/2
and let
u\in H^{l}_{qp}.
Then, for any
p
with
p\leqslant l,
we have

\|u\|_{H^{p}_{qp}}\leqslant\|u\|_{H^{l}_{qp}}^{p/l}\|u\|_{H^{0}_{qp}}^{1-p/l}.

Proof.

The proof is essentially the same as in the case of usual Sobolev spaces, so we omit it.
∎

Finally, since the quasiperiodic integrals are averaged integrals, we need to justify taking their time derivatives.
The following two propositions provide this justification.

Proposition 9
.

Suppose
f_{n}\to f
uniformly on
\mathbb{R}
, and that the averaged integrals

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}f\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}f_{n}\,\mathrm{d}x,

exist, for
n=0,1,2,\ldots.
Then,

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}f\,\mathrm{d}x=\lim_{n\to\infty}\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}f_{n}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Proof.

We adapt the proof given in
[
23
, Theorem 7.16]
. Let

\varepsilon_{n}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|f_{n}(x)-f(x)|.

Thus,
f_{n}-\varepsilon_{n}\leqslant f\leqslant f_{n}+\varepsilon_{n}
, and integrating from
-R
to
R
yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}f_{n}\,\mathrm{d}x-\varepsilon_{n}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}f\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}f_{n}\,\mathrm{d}x+\varepsilon_{n}.

Hence

-\varepsilon_{n}\leqslant\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}f\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}f_{n}\,\mathrm{d}x\leqslant\varepsilon_{n}.

Taking the limit in
R
yields

-\varepsilon_{n}\leqslant\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}f\,\mathrm{d}x-\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}f_{n}\,\mathrm{d}x\leqslant\varepsilon_{n},

so that

\left\lvert\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}f\,\mathrm{d}x-\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}f_{n}\,\mathrm{d}x\right\rvert\leqslant\varepsilon_{n}.

Since
f_{n}\to f
uniformly, we must have
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
As the difference of averaged integrals is bounded by
\varepsilon_{n}
, we see that taking
n\to\infty
yields the desired result.
∎

With the preceding proposition in mind, we can prove the following result:

Proposition 10
.

Assume that the following averaged integrals exist for any
t\in(0,T)

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}\phi(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\phi(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x,

and that
\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\phi(x,t)=\phi_{t}(x,t)
is continuous in
t
. Then,

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}\phi(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x=\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\phi(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x,

i.e. we can interchange time derivatives and averaged integrals.

Proof.

We follow
[
23
, Theorem 9.42]
.
Fix
t\in(0,T),x\in\mathbb{R}
and consider difference quotients

\psi(x,s)=\frac{\phi(x,s)-\phi(x,t)}{s-t}

for
s
sufficiently close to
t
.
By the mean value theorem applied on
\phi
in
s
, there exists an
r
between
s
and
t
such that

\psi(x,s)=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\phi(x,r).

Therefore, by continuity of
\phi_{t}
, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
\delta>0
such that

\left\lvert\psi(x,s)-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\phi(x,s)\right\rvert<\varepsilon,\qquad(x\in\mathbb{R},0<|s-t|<\delta)

In other words,
\psi(x,s)\to\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\phi(x,t)
uniformly, as
s\to t
.
Applying Proposition
9
yields that

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\phi(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x=\lim_{s\to t}\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}\psi(x,s)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Now, let

f(y)=\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}\phi(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}x,

and note that

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}\psi(x,s)\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{f(s)-f(t)}{s-t}

by definition of
\psi(x,s).

With this, we obtain that

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\phi(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x=\lim_{s\to t}\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}\psi(x,s)\,\mathrm{d}x=\lim_{s\to t}\frac{f(s)-f(t)}{s-t}.

Since

\lim_{s\to t}\frac{f(s)-f(t)}{s-t}=\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}f(t)=\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}\phi(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x,

we clearly have

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\phi(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}\phi(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x,

as desired.
∎

To conclude, this proposition says that so long as the integrand is
C^{1}
in time, we can safely exchange time derivatives.

Finally, we prove a proposition about Hilbert transform that we use repeatedly.

Proposition 11
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}.
Then, for any
u\in H^{s}_{qp},

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{H\partial_{x}^{2}D^{s}_{x}u}(k)\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}u}(-k)=0.

The statement is still true if
\partial^{s}_{x}u
is replaced by
D^{s}_{x}u
.

Proof.

First, use the definition of Hilbert transform:

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}{H\partial_{x}^{2}D^{s}_{x}u}(k)\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}u}(-k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}(-i)\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot k)(i\alpha\cdot k)^{2}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}u}(k)\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}u}(-k).

Then, split the sum into two sums, depending on the sign of
\alpha\cdot k:

\displaystyle\sum_{k:\alpha\cdot k>0}(-i)\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot k)(i\alpha\cdot k)^{2}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}u}(k)\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}u}(-k)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{k:\alpha\cdot k<0}(-i)\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot k)(i\alpha\cdot k)^{2}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}u}(k)\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}u}(-k).

Evaluating the sign leads to

\displaystyle\sum_{k:\alpha\cdot k>0}(-i)(i\alpha\cdot k)^{2}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}u}(k)\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}u}(-k)-\sum_{k:\alpha\cdot k<0}(-i)(i\alpha\cdot k)^{2}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}u}(k)\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}u}(-k).

Performing a change of variables
k\mapsto-k
in the second sum shows that the two sums are the same, thus the difference is
0
, as desired.
∎

3.
Regularized solutions and their properties

In this section, we prove several properties of the regularized Benjamin-Ono equation (
2
) with quasiperiodic initial data.
In particular, we show existence of approximate solutions, that these solutions are bounded uniformly in
n,
and a Cauchy estimate for these solutions.

3.1.
Properties of regularized data

In applying the Bona-Smith method
[
5
]
, regularization of initial data plays a crucial role.
As we seek to emulate their argument especially as to demonstrating full continuity of solutions with respect to the initial data,
we also regularize the quasiperiodic data: for a given
u\in H^{s}_{qp},
define
u_{\delta}
through the equation

(3)

\mathcal{F}{(u_{\delta})}(k)=\mathbb{I}_{|k|\leqslant\delta}(k)\mathcal{F}{u}(k),

where
|k|
stands for the Euclidean norm of
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
.

The following proposition outlines the advantages of regularizing data as we do. It is analagous to
[
2
, Lemma 5]
, and is essential for demonstrating
the full result of continuity with respect to the initial data.

Proposition 12
.

Let
\mathcal{K}\subset H^{s}_{qp}
be a compact set. Then, for any
\delta>1
and any
u\in\mathcal{K},(u)_{\delta}
satisfies:

(4)

\displaystyle\|(u)_{\delta}\|_{H^{s+j}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{j}\delta^{j}\|(u)_{\delta}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\quad\forall j\geqslant 0,

(5)

\displaystyle\|(u)_{\delta}-u\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}

\displaystyle=o(\delta^{-s}),

(6)

\displaystyle\|(u)_{\delta}-u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}

\displaystyle=o(1).

In particular, the rate of convergence in (
5
) and (
6
) depends only on
\mathcal{K}
and not on
u
.
Here, recall that the notation
f(x)=o(g(x))
means that
f(x)/g(x)\to 0
as
x\to\infty.

Proof.

First, we prove (
4
). Note that

\displaystyle\|(u)_{\delta}\|_{H^{s+j}_{qp}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}(1+|k|^{2})^{s}\left\lvert\hat{u}\right\rvert^{2}(k)(1+|k|^{2})^{j}\mathbb{I}_{|k|\leqslant\delta}(k)

\displaystyle\leqslant(1+\delta^{2})^{j}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}(1+|k|^{2})^{s}\left\lvert\hat{u}\right\rvert^{2}(k)\mathbb{I}_{|k|\leqslant\delta}(k)

\displaystyle\leqslant(2\delta^{2})^{j}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}(1+|k|^{2})^{s}\left\lvert\hat{u}\right\rvert^{2}(k),

so that

\|(u)_{\delta}\|_{H^{s+j}_{qp}}\lesssim\delta^{j}\|(u)_{\delta}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}.

As for (
5
), we have

\displaystyle\|(u)_{\delta}-u\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\left(\mathbb{I}_{|k|\leqslant\delta}(k)-1\right)^{2}\left\lvert\hat{u}\right\rvert^{2}(k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}:|k|>\delta}\left\lvert\hat{u}\right\rvert^{2}(k)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{\delta^{2s}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}:|k|>\delta}|k|^{2s}\left\lvert\hat{u}\right\rvert^{2}(k).

Clearly
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}:|k|>\delta}|k|^{2s}\left\lvert\hat{u}\right\rvert^{2}(k)\to 0
as
\delta\to\infty
.
Thus, we obtain (
5
).

Finally, by the same means one obtains

\displaystyle\|(u)_{\delta}-u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}:|k|>\delta}\left\langle|k|\right\rangle^{2s}\left\lvert\hat{u}\right\rvert^{2}(k),

which is the same as (
6
).

We tackle the last statement about the rate of convergence.
For simplicity, we first consider the case of
\mathcal{K}:=\{u_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\cup u_{0}
, where
u_{n}\to u_{0}
in
H^{s}_{qp}.

Let
\varepsilon>0.

Now, for any fixed
u_{n}
, there exists
M(n)
such that for any
\delta>M(n),\|(u_{n})_{\delta}-u_{n}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}<\varepsilon/3
.
Of all such
M(n),
write
M_{n}
for the minimal such value.

Furthermore, there exists
N
such that for all
n>N
,
\|u-u_{n}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}<\varepsilon/3.

Thus, for a fixed
n>N,

\displaystyle\|u_{\delta}-u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|u_{\delta}-(u_{n})_{\delta}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}+\|(u_{n})_{\delta}-u_{n}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}+\|u_{n}-u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}

\displaystyle<\varepsilon,

so that
\|u_{\delta}-u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}<\varepsilon
.
In particular, since
M_{0}
is the minimal value such that
\delta>M_{0}
implies
\|u_{\delta}-u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}<\varepsilon
, we must have that
M_{n}\geqslant M_{0}
.

Similarly, for the same
n>N
that we fixed above we have

\displaystyle\|(u_{n})_{\delta}-u_{n}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|(u_{n})_{\delta}-(u_{0})_{\delta}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}+\|(u_{0})_{\delta}-u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}+\|u_{0}-u_{n}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}

\displaystyle<\varepsilon,

so that
\|(u_{n})_{\delta}-u_{n}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}<\varepsilon.

Since
M_{n}
is the minimal value for which
\delta>M_{n}
implies
\|(u_{n})_{\delta}-u_{n}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}<\varepsilon,
we must have
M_{0}\geqslant M_{n}
.
We thus obtain that
M_{n}=M_{0}
for
n>N
, and therefore the value

M=\sup_{i=0,1,2,\ldots}M_{i}=\max_{i=0,1,\ldots n}M_{i}

must be finite.
Therefore, for any
\varepsilon>0
, we can pick
M
such that for any
u\in\mathcal{K}
and
\delta>M,
we have

\|u_{\delta}-u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}<\varepsilon.

Now, we consider any compact set
\mathcal{K}.

Let
\varepsilon>0,
and for a given
u
define
M_{u}
to be the minimal value such that
\delta>M_{u}
implies
\|u_{\delta}-u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}<\varepsilon
.
Define

M=\sup_{u\in\mathcal{K}}M_{u}.

To show uniformity, we need that
M<\infty.

Suppose not, i.e.
M=\infty
.
Then, there exists a sequence
\{u_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{K}
such that
M_{n}:=M_{u_{n}}>n
for each
n
.
By compactness of
\mathcal{K}
, there is a convergent subsequence
\{u_{n_{j}}\}\subset\{u_{n}\}
.
Furthermore, we know that there is a limit of this sequence
u_{n_{0}}\in\mathcal{K}.

Let

D=\sup_{n_{j}:j=0,1,\ldots}M_{n_{j}}.

Since
M_{n_{j}}>n_{j}
, we must have that
D=\infty
.

However, the proof of the simple case indicates that
D<\infty
, hence a contradiction.
Therefore,
M
is bounded, and we obtain the desired uniformity on
\mathcal{K}.

∎

3.2.
Existence of approximate solutions

We mirror the approach of
[
3
, Section 8.5]
and use the following Picard theorem to obtain solutions to the regularized equation (
2
).

Theorem 13

(Picard Theorem)
.

Let
B
be a Banach space and let
O\subseteq B
be an open set. Let
F:O\to B
be such that
F
is locally Lipschitz: for any
X\in O
, there exists
\lambda>0
and an open set
U\subseteq O
such that for all
Y,Z\in U,

\|F(Y)-F(Z)\|_{B}\leqslant\lambda\|Y-Z\|_{B}.

Then, for all
X_{0}
, there exists
T>0
and a unique
X\in C^{1}([-T,T];O)
such that
X
solves the initial value problem

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}X}{\,\mathrm{d}t}=F(X),X(0)=X_{0}.

Theorem 14
.

Let
s>N/2+1
. Let
u_{0}\in H^{s}_{qp}.

For any integer
n\geqslant 1
, there exists a
T_{n}>0
and
u_{n}\in C([0,T_{n}];H^{s}_{qp})
such that
u_{n}
satisfies the regularized Benjamin-Ono equation, as well as
u_{n}(0)=u_{0}
.

Sketch.

Take
O=B=H^{s}_{qp}
and define
F:H^{s}_{qp}\to H^{s}_{qp}
by
F(u)=\chi_{n}[(\chi_{n}u)(\chi_{n}u_{x})]+\chi_{n}[Hu_{xx}].

The presence of mollifiers
\chi_{n}
ensures that
F(u)\in H^{s}_{qp}
for any
u\in H^{s}_{qp}
and that
F
is locally Lipschitz.
By the Picard theorem, for each
n
, there is a
T_{n}
such that there is a unique
u_{n}
that solves the regularized equation and
u_{n}\in C^{1}([0,T_{n}];H^{s}_{qp})
.
∎

Remark 15
.

In a typical periodic energy method proof, the approximate solutions may be finite dimensional, such as by having performed a Galerkin
projection. In the present setting, our sum is still an infinite sum, as
\chi_{n}
cuts off Fourier modes with
\alpha\cdot k
large, rather than
k
large.
It is well known that
\alpha\cdot k
can accumulate near zero for large
k.
However, even with this being an infinite sum, the point is that
\alpha\cdot k

is now bounded, and thus norms of derivatives of a function
\chi_{n}f
are bounded by norms of
f.

3.3.
Uniform bound

In this section, we seek to prove a bound on solutions
u_{n}
derived in the previous section that is uniform in
n
.
Having this bound would give us an existence of a common time
T
on which the approximate solutions exist.
Crucially,
T
will not depend on
n.

Clearly, we know that

\|u_{n}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\lesssim\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}+\|D^{s}_{x}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}},

so it is enough to prove that
\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}
and
\|D_{x}^{s}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}
are bounded uniformly.
Therefore, we separate our proof into two parts, to tackle each norm.

Proposition 16

(The
L^{2}_{qp}
bound)
.

Let
u_{n}
be the solution of the regularized Benjamin-Ono equation with initial data
u_{0}.
Then we have the following conserved quantity:

\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}=\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}.

Proof.

For simplicity, write
u=u_{n}.

The local existence result, Theorem
14
, indicates that
u\in C^{1}
in time.
Therefore, by Proposition
10
, we can put the time derivative inside the norm:

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\|u\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}=\lim_{M\to\infty}\frac{1}{2M}\int^{M}_{-M}2u_{t}udx=2\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}(\partial_{t}\hat{u})(k)\hat{u}(-k).

Using the equation we obtain:

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}(\partial_{t}\hat{u}(k))\hat{u}(-k)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{\chi_{n}((\chi_{n}u)(\chi_{n}u_{x}))}(k)\hat{u}(-k)+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{(\chi_{n}Hu_{xx})}(k)\hat{u}(-k)

For the nonlinear term, note that

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{\chi_{n}((\chi_{n}u)(\chi_{n}u_{x}))}(k)\hat{u}(-k)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{\chi_{n}(u)\chi_{n}u_{x}}(k)\mathcal{F}{\chi_{n}u}(-k),

where we transfer the
\chi_{n}
to
\hat{u}(-k)
.
Applying Lemma
34
with
n=2
we obtain that

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{\chi_{n}(u)\chi_{n}u_{x}}(k)\mathcal{F}{\chi_{n}u}(-k)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{\chi_{n}u_{x}}\mathcal{F}{(\chi_{n}u)^{2}}(-k),

vanishes.

As for the linear term, since
\chi_{n}=(\chi_{n})^{2},
we can write

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{(\chi_{n}Hu_{xx})}(k)\hat{u}(-k)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{(\chi_{n}Hu_{xx})}(k)\mathcal{F}{\chi_{n}u}(-k)

and apply Lemma
11
to conclude that the linear term vanishes.

Thus, we see that

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\|u\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}=0,

which implies that
\|u\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}=\text{constant}=\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}
.
∎

Proposition 17
.

For
s>N/2+1
, the solutions of each regularized problem satisfy the following bound:

\|D_{x}^{s}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\leqslant((\|D^{s}_{x}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}})^{-1}-Ct)^{-1},

independently of
n
, for a fixed
C=C(s)
.
This bound holds if
C^{-1}(\|D^{s}_{x}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}})^{-1}>t
.

Proof.

As before, Proposition
10
allows to estimate the time derivative of the Sobolev norm:

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\|D_{x}^{s}u\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D_{x}^{s}u}_{t}(k)\mathcal{F}{D_{x}^{s}u}(-k)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{(D^{s}_{x}[\chi_{n}[(\chi_{n}u)(\chi_{n}u_{x})]+\chi_{n}[Hu_{xx}])}(k)\mathcal{F}{D_{x}^{s}u}(-k)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}\chi_{n}[(\chi_{n}u)(\chi_{n}u_{x})]}(k)\mathcal{F}{D_{x}^{s}u}(-k)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+2\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}\chi_{n}[Hu_{xx}]}(k)\mathcal{F}{D_{x}^{s}u}(-k),

where we have used the regularized evolution equation, and we have split the sum into linear and nonlinear parts.
Since operators
D^{s}_{x}
and
\chi_{n}H\partial_{x}^{2}
commute, we can rewrite the linear term as

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}\chi_{n}[Hu_{xx}]}(k)\mathcal{F}{D_{x}^{s}u}(-k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{\chi_{n}[H\partial_{x}^{2}D^{s}_{x}u]}(k)\mathcal{F}{D_{x}^{s}u}(-k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{\chi_{n}[H\partial_{x}^{2}D^{s}_{x}u]}(k)\mathcal{F}{\chi_{n}(D_{x}^{s}u)}(-k),

so by Lemma
11
, this term is 0.

We now deal with the nonlinear term. To simplify the notation, let
v=\chi_{n}u
and write

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}\chi_{n}[(\chi_{n}u)(\chi_{n}u_{x})]}(k)\mathcal{F}{D_{x}^{s}u}(-k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}[(\chi_{n}u)(\chi_{n}u_{x})]}(k)\mathcal{F}{D_{x}^{s}\chi_{n}u}(-k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}(vv_{x})}(k)\mathcal{F}{D_{x}^{s}v}(-k).

Add and subtract
vD^{s}_{x}v
to obtain:

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}(vv_{x})-vD^{s}_{x}v_{x}}(k)\mathcal{F}{D_{x}^{s}v}(-k)+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{vD^{s}_{x}v_{x}}(k)\mathcal{F}{D_{x}^{s}v}(-k):=I+II.

The term
I
is easily dealt with by applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the fractional Leibniz rule (Proposition
7
):

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\leqslant\|D^{s}_{x}(vv_{x})-vD^{s}_{x}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\|D_{x}^{s}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big(\|v\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\|v_{x}\|_{H^{s_{0}}_{qp}}+\|v\|_{H^{s_{0}+1}_{qp}}\|v_{x}\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}\big)\|D_{x}^{s}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}},

for some
s_{0}>N/2.

In particular, letting
s_{0}=s-1>N/2
yields

I\lesssim\big(\|v\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\|v_{x}\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}+\|v\|_{H^{s-1+1}_{qp}}\|v_{x}\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}\big)\|D_{x}^{s}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\leqslant\|v\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}^{2}\|D_{x}^{s}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}.

As for
II
, we first apply integration by parts from Lemma
35
, yielding

II=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{vD^{s}_{x}v_{x}}(k)\mathcal{F}{D_{x}^{s}v}(-k)=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{v_{x}D_{x}^{s}v}(k)\mathcal{F}{D_{x}^{s}v}(-k).

We can then apply the Cauchy-Schwarz, Young’s, and Sobolev inequalities to obtain

\displaystyle II\lesssim\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}|\mathcal{F}{v_{x}D_{x}^{s}v}(k)\mathcal{F}{D_{x}^{s}v}(-k)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathcal{F}{v_{x}D_{x}^{s}v}\|_{\ell^{2}_{k}}\|\mathcal{F}{D_{x}^{s}v}\|_{\ell^{2}_{k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathcal{F}{v_{x}}\|_{\ell^{1}_{k}}\|\mathcal{F}{D_{x}^{s}v}\|_{\ell^{2}_{k}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\|D^{s}_{x}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}.

All in all, we see that the nonlinear term is bounded by

I+II\lesssim\|v\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\|D^{s}_{x}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}+\|v\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}^{2}\|D^{s}_{x}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}.

Finally, note that by Proposition
16
,
\|v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}
is bounded; thus, we have

\|v\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\lesssim\|v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}+\|D^{s}_{x}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\lesssim\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}+\|D^{s}_{x}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}.

As a result,

\displaystyle I+II

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\|D^{s}_{x}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}+\|v\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}^{2}\|D^{s}_{x}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\leqslant(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}+\|D^{s}_{x}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}})\|D^{s}_{x}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}+(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}+\|D^{s}_{x}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2})\|D^{s}_{x}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|D^{s}_{x}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{3}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\|D^{s}_{x}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}\|D^{s}_{x}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|D^{s}_{x}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}(\|D^{s}_{x}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}+\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}})^{2},

so that

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\|D^{s}_{x}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}=I+II\lesssim\|D^{s}_{x}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}(\|D^{s}_{x}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}+\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}})^{2}.

Thus,

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\|D^{s}_{x}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\leqslant C(\|D^{s}_{x}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}+\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}})^{2},

where
C
depends on
s.

A Gronwall-type argument then reveals that
\|D^{s}_{x}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}
is bounded, independently of
n
:

\|D^{s}_{x}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\leqslant((\|D^{s}_{x}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}+\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}})^{-1}-Ct)^{-1}.

This bound holds whenever
C^{-1}(\|D^{s}_{x}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}+\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}})^{-1}>t
.
∎

We have now established the uniform bound and the existence of a common time interval, independent of
n.

Corollary 18

(Uniform bound for the regularized problem)
.

Let
s>N/2+1.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{s}_{qp}.

Then, solutions
u_{n}
to the regularized Benjamin-Ono equation (
2
) with initial data
u_{0}
satisfy the following bound independent of parameters
n:

(7)

\|u_{n}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\leqslant\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}+\|D_{x}^{s}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}+((\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}})^{-1}-Ct)^{-1},

Furthermore, these solutions exist on a common time interval
T
that only depends on
s
and the norm
\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}.

In particular, if the initial data is chosen from a compact set
\mathcal{K}\subseteq H^{s}_{qp}
, then
T
only depends on
\mathcal{K}
and not a particular choice of the initial data.

At last, the result below allows us to estimate solutions in higher order Sobolev spaces.

Proposition 19

(Refined boundedness)
.

Let
s>N/2+1
. Let
\delta>1
.
Suppose
u_{n,\delta}
solves the regularized Benjamin-Ono equation with parameter
n
and regularized initial data
(u_{0})_{\delta},
as defined in (
3
).
Further, let
T>0
be such that these solutions exist on the same interval
[0,T].

Then,
u_{n,\delta}
satisfy the following estimate:

\|u_{n,\delta}\|_{H^{s+l}_{qp}}\lesssim\delta^{l}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}},

for any
l>0
, for any
t\in[0,T].

Proof.

For simplicity, write
u=\chi_{n}u_{n,\delta}
and take the time derivative of
\|D^{s+l}_{x}u_{n,\delta}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}
.
Following the proof of the uniform bound yields

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\|D_{x}^{s+l}u_{n,\delta}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s+l}_{x}\chi_{n}[(\chi_{n}u_{n,\delta})(\chi_{n}(u_{n,\delta})_{x})]}(k)\mathcal{F}{D_{x}^{s+l}u_{n,\delta}}(-k)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+2\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s+l}_{x}\chi_{n}[H(u_{n,\delta})_{xx}]}(k)\mathcal{F}{D_{x}^{s+l}u_{n,\delta}}(-k)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s+l}_{x}[uu_{x}]}(k)\mathcal{F}{D_{x}^{s+l}u}(-k).

The linear term vanishes as before, while for the nonlinear term we add and subtract
uD^{s+l}_{x}u_{x}
to get :

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s+l}_{x}[uu_{x}]-uD^{s+l}_{x}u_{x}}(k)\mathcal{F}{D_{x}^{s+l}u}(-k)+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{uD^{s+l}_{x}u_{x}}(k)\mathcal{F}{D_{x}^{s+l}u}(-k)=:I+II.

For the sum
I
, apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and use Proposition
7
with
s_{0}=s-1
to get

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s+l}_{x}[uu_{x}]-uD^{s+l}_{x}u_{x}}(k)\mathcal{F}{D_{x}^{s+l}u}(-k)\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leqslant\|D^{s+l}_{x}[uu_{x}]-uD^{s+l}_{x}u_{x}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\|D_{x}^{s+l}u\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leqslant\left(\|u\|_{H^{s+l}_{qp}}\|u_{x}\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}+\|u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\|u_{x}\|_{H^{s+l-1}_{qp}}\right)\|D_{x}^{s+l}u\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}.

Note that
\|u_{x}\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}
and
\|u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}
are bounded on
[0,T],
so that

|I|\lesssim_{T}\|u\|_{H^{s+l}_{qp}}\|D_{x}^{s+l}u\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\lesssim\|(u_{0})_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\|D_{x}^{s+l}u\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}+\|D_{x}^{s+l}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{qp}}.

As for
II
, we again apply integration by parts, finding

\displaystyle II=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{uD^{s+l}_{x}u_{x}}(k)\mathcal{F}{D_{x}^{s+l}u}(-k)=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{u_{x}D_{x}^{s+l}u}(k)\mathcal{F}{D_{x}^{s+l}u}(-k),

so that

II\lesssim\|u_{x}D_{x}^{s+l}u\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\|D_{x}^{s+l}u\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\lesssim\|\mathcal{F}u_{x}\|_{\ell^{1}_{k}}\|D_{x}^{s+l}u\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}.

Applying the Sobolev inequality (Proposition
5
) and the uniform bound on
\|u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}
yields

\|\mathcal{F}u_{x}\|_{\ell^{1}_{k}}\lesssim\|u_{x}\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\lesssim_{T}1.

Combining the bounds on
I
and
II
, we obtain

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\|D_{x}^{s+l}u_{n,\delta}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\lesssim\|D_{x}^{s+l}u_{n,\delta}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}.

Finally, applying Gronwall’s inequality and properties of data regularization (
4
) yields

\|D_{x}^{s+l}u_{n,\delta}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}\lesssim\|D_{x}^{s+l}(u_{0})_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}\lesssim\delta^{2l}\|D_{x}^{s}(u_{0})_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}.

We clearly have

\|u_{n,\delta}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}=\|(u_{0})_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\leqslant\delta^{l}\|(u_{0})_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}},

so that we obtain the desired inequality.
∎

3.4.
Cauchy estimate

In this section, we provide a Cauchy estimate for solutions of the regularized problem.
We first outline our approach and provide a few results to be used later in the proof.
Let
u_{0}\in H^{s}_{qp}
and let
(u_{0})_{\delta}
be a regularized version of
u_{0}
as defined in (
3
), with parameter
\delta>0
.
Let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
u_{n,\delta_{1}}
solve

\displaystyle u_{t}

\displaystyle=\chi_{n}[(\chi_{n}u_{n})(\chi_{n}u_{nx})]+\chi_{n}[Hu_{nxx}],

\displaystyle u(0,\cdot)

\displaystyle=(u_{0})_{\delta_{1}},

whereas
u_{m,\delta_{2}}
denotes the solution of

\displaystyle u_{t}

\displaystyle=\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}u_{m})(\chi_{m}u_{mx})]+\chi_{m}[Hu_{mxx}],

\displaystyle u(0,\cdot)

\displaystyle=(u_{0})_{\delta_{2}}.

For ease of notation, write
u=u_{n,\delta_{1}},v=u_{m,\delta_{2}},w=u-v.

Note that the set
\mathcal{U}:=\{(u_{0})_{\delta}:\delta\geqslant 0\}\cup u_{0}
is both a complete subset of
H^{s}_{qp}
and totally bounded
H^{s}_{qp}
by the uniform bound.
Hence
\mathcal{U}
is compact, and so by Corollary
18
,
u
and
v
exist on the same time interval.

Write the difference of equations:

\displaystyle u_{t}-v_{t}

\displaystyle=\chi_{n}[(\chi_{n}u_{n})(\chi_{n}u_{nx})]+\chi_{n}[Hu_{nxx}]-(\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}u_{m})(\chi_{m}u_{mx})]+\chi_{m}[Hu_{mxx}])

\displaystyle=\chi_{n}[(\chi_{n}u_{n})(\chi_{n}u_{nx})]-\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}u_{m})(\chi_{m}u_{mx})]+\chi_{n}[Hu_{nxx}]-\chi_{m}[Hu_{mxx}].

The linear term can be written as follows:

(8)

\displaystyle\chi_{n}[Hu_{xx}]-\chi_{m}[Hv_{xx}]

\displaystyle=\chi_{n}[Hu_{xx}]-\chi_{m}[Hu_{xx}]+\chi_{m}[Hu_{xx}]-\chi_{m}[Hv_{xx}]

\displaystyle=\chi_{m}[Hw_{xx}]+[\chi_{n}-\chi_{m}]Hu_{xx}.

For the nonlinear term, observe the following equality:

\displaystyle\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}w)(\chi_{m}w_{x})]=\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}u)(\chi_{m}u_{x})]

\displaystyle-\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}u)(\chi_{m}v_{x})]

\displaystyle-\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}v)(\chi_{m}u_{x})]+\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}v)(\chi_{m}v_{x})],

so that

\displaystyle\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}v)(\chi_{m}v_{x})]=\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}w)(\chi_{m}w_{x})]

\displaystyle+\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}u)(\chi_{m}v_{x})]

\displaystyle+\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}v)(\chi_{m}u_{x})]-\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}u)(\chi_{m}u_{x})]

\displaystyle=\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}w)(\chi_{m}w_{x})]

\displaystyle+\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}u)(\chi_{m}v_{x})]-\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}w)(\chi_{m}u_{x})].

As such it is straightforward to verify the following identity:

(9)

\displaystyle\chi_{n}[(\chi_{n}u)(\chi_{n}u_{x})]

\displaystyle-\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}v)(\chi_{m}v_{x})]

\displaystyle=\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}v)(\chi_{m}w_{x})]+\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}w)(\chi_{m}u)_{x}]+\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}u)((\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})u)_{x}]

\displaystyle+\chi_{m}[((\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})u)(\chi_{n}u)_{x}]+[\chi_{n}-\chi_{m}][\chi_{n}u\chi_{n}u_{x}].

As is seen in both (
8
) and (
9
), there are terms with factors
\chi_{n}-\chi_{m}
.
The lemma below explains how to handle these terms.

Lemma 20
.

Let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
and
l\geqslant 0.
For any
v\in H^{l}_{qp},
we have

\|(\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\leqslant\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{l}\|D^{l}_{x}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}.

Proof.

Note that

\|(\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}=\sum_{k}|\hat{v}|^{2}(k)\mathbb{I}_{\min\{n,m\}\leqslant|\alpha\cdot k|\leqslant\max\{n,m\}}.

Since
\min\{n,m\}\leqslant|\alpha\cdot k|\leqslant|k|
, we know that
|k|^{-1}\leqslant\max\{1/n,1/m\}
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\|(\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}|\hat{v}|^{2}(k)\mathbb{I}_{\min\{n,m\}(k)\leqslant|\alpha\cdot k|\leqslant\max\{n,m\}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\mathbb{I}_{\min\{n,m\}\leqslant|\alpha\cdot k|\leqslant\max\{n,m\}}(k)|\hat{v}|^{2}(k)|k|^{-2l}|k|^{2l}

\displaystyle\leqslant\big(\max\{1/n,1/m\}\big)^{2l}\sum_{k}\mathbb{I}_{\min\{n,m\}\leqslant|\alpha\cdot k|\leqslant\max\{n,m\}}(k)|\mathcal{F}{\partial_{x}^{l}v}|^{2}(k)

\displaystyle\leqslant\big(\max\{1/n,1/m\}\big)^{2l}\|D_{x}^{l}v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2},

from which the lemma follows.
∎

We are now ready to prove Cauchy estimates.
We begin with the
L^{2}_{qp}
case.

Proposition 21
.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, solutions
u=u_{n,\delta_{1}},v=u_{m,\delta_{2}}
be defined as above. Then,

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\|u-v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}\lesssim\|u-v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{s-2}\|u-v\|_{L^{2}},

where implicit constants do not depend on
n,m
, but may depend on the uniform bound derived before.
In particular, we have

(10)

\|u-v\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\lesssim\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{s-2}+\|(u_{0})_{\delta_{1}}-(u_{0})_{\delta_{2}}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}.

Proof.

As before, we take the time derivative of the
L^{2}_{qp}
norm:

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k}\mathcal{F}{(u-v)_{t}}(k)\hat{w}(-k)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k}\mathcal{F}{\chi_{n}[(\chi_{n}u_{n})(\chi_{n}u_{nx})]-\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}u_{m})(\chi_{m}u_{mx})]}(k)\hat{w}(-k)

\displaystyle\qquad+2\sum_{k}\mathcal{F}{\chi_{n}[Hu_{nxx}]-\chi_{m}[Hu_{mxx}]}(k)\hat{w}(-k).

Following the analysis above, the linear part can be estimated following (
8
).
Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}{(\chi_{n}[Hu_{xx}]-\chi_{m}[Hv_{xx}])}(k)\mathcal{F}w(-k)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{(\chi_{m}[Hw_{xx}])}(k)\mathcal{F}w(-k)}_{\text{$=0$ by Lemma \ref{lineartermvanishes}}}+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{([\chi_{n}-\chi_{m}]Hu_{xx})}(k)\mathcal{F}w(-k).

By applying Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, it is enough to estimate

\|\mathcal{F}{D^{2}_{x}([\chi_{n}-\chi_{m}]u)}\|_{\ell^{2}_{k}}.

By Lemma
20
, we can bound

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}{D^{2}_{x}([\chi_{n}-\chi_{m}]v)}\|_{\ell^{2}_{k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{l_{0}}\|u\|_{H}^{2+l_{0}}.

Since
s>N/2+1
and
N>1
, we can write
s=2+\varepsilon
and let
l_{0}=\varepsilon.

With this choice, the linear term is bounded by

\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{\varepsilon}\|u\|_{H}^{s}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\lesssim\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{\varepsilon}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}},

up to constants that depend on
s
and the uniform bound derived earlier.

We now deal with the nonlinear term.
By decomposition (
9
), we need to estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\mathcal{F}{\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}v)(\chi_{m}w_{x})]}(k)\mathcal{F}{w}(-k)+\sum_{k}\mathcal{F}{\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}w)(\chi_{m}u)_{x}]}(k)\mathcal{F}{w}(-k)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k}\mathcal{F}{\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}u)((\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})u)_{x}]}(k)\mathcal{F}{w}(-k)+\sum_{k}\mathcal{F}{\chi_{m}[((\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})u)(\chi_{n}u)_{x}]}(k)\mathcal{F}{w}(-k)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k}\mathcal{F}{[\chi_{n}-\chi_{m}][\chi_{n}u\chi_{n}u_{x}]}(k)\mathcal{F}{w}(-k)

\displaystyle=I+II+III+IV+V.

We seek to bound each term:

(1)

the term
I
is handled by energy cancellation:

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\mathcal{F}{\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}v)(\chi_{m}w_{x})]}(k)\mathcal{F}{w}(-k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\mathcal{F}{(\chi_{m}v)(\chi_{m}w_{x})}(k)\mathcal{F}{\chi_{m}w}(-k)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}\mathcal{F}{(\chi_{m}v)_{x}\chi_{m}w}(k)\mathcal{F}{\chi_{m}w}(-k),

Then, apply Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, Young’s convolution inequality, and Sobolev embedding as follows:

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathcal{F}{(\chi_{m}w)(\chi_{m}v)_{x}}\|_{\ell^{2}_{k}}\|\chi_{m}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\mathcal{F}{(\chi_{m}v)_{x}}\|_{\ell^{1}_{k}}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2},

so that
I
is bounded by
\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}
.

(2)

the term
II
is handled similarly to
I
:

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\mathcal{F}{\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}w)(\chi_{m}u)_{x}]}(k)\mathcal{F}{w}(-k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\mathcal{F}{[(\chi_{m}w)(\chi_{m}u)_{x}]}(k)\mathcal{F}{\chi_{m}w}(-k)

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\mathcal{F}{(\chi_{m}w)(\chi_{m}u)_{x}}\|_{\ell^{2}_{k}}\|\chi_{m}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\mathcal{F}{(\chi_{m}u)_{x}}\|_{\ell^{1}_{k}}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2},

so that
II
is bounded by
\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}
.

(3)

the terms
III
and
IV
are handled similarly to
I
and
II
except that Lemma
20
is invoked to gain decay.
For example, let us consider
III:

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\mathcal{F}{\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}u)((\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})u)_{x}]}(k)\mathcal{F}{w}(-k)

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\mathcal{F}{(\chi_{m}u)((\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})u)_{x}}\|_{\ell^{2}_{k}}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\mathcal{F}{(\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})u_{x}}\|_{\ell^{2}_{k}}\|\mathcal{F}{\chi_{m}u}\|_{\ell^{1}_{k}}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{s-1}\|u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\|u\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{s-1}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}.

In the same way, we obtain

IV\lesssim\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{s}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}.

(4)

for the term
V
, apply Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Lemma
20
:

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\mathcal{F}{[\chi_{n}-\chi_{m}][\chi_{n}u\chi_{n}u_{x}]}(k)\mathcal{F}{w}(-k)

\displaystyle\leqslant\|[\chi_{n}-\chi_{m}][\chi_{n}u\chi_{n}u_{x}]\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{s-1}\|\chi_{n}u\chi_{n}u_{x}\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{s-1}\|\chi_{n}u\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}\|\chi_{n}u_{x}\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}},

where we apply the algebra property in the last line.
Thus,
V\leqslant\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{s-1}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}.

Combining these estimates with the linear part yields the following inequality:

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle\leqslant\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{\varepsilon}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}+\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{s}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}+\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{s-1}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}.

Note that since
s=2+\varepsilon
, we have
\varepsilon=s-2
. In particular, this will be the slowest decaying factor, so that

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\lesssim\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{s-2}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}+\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2},

which becomes

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\lesssim\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{s-2}+\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}.

A standard application of the Gronwall’s inequality now yields

\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\lesssim e^{t}(\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{s-2}+\|w_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}),

which completes the baseline estimate.
∎

An easy consequence of the
L^{2}_{qp}
Cauchy estimate and the interpolation inequality (Lemma
8
)
is that we obtain Cauchy property of solutions for
H^{l}_{qp}
where
0\leqslant l<s.

In the particular case of
l=s,p=s-1
, we have

\|w\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}\leqslant\|w\|_{H^{l}_{qp}}^{(s-1)/s}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{1-(s-1)/s}=\|w\|_{H^{l}_{qp}}^{(s-1)/s}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{1/s}

Due to triangle inequality and the uniform bound on
u
and
v
, we can ignore
\|w\|_{H^{l}_{qp}}^{(s-1)/s}.

Now, invoking (
10
) we have

\displaystyle\|w\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\Big(\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{s-2}+\|(u_{0})_{\delta_{1}}-(u_{0})_{\delta_{2}}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\Big)^{1/s}

\displaystyle\leqslant\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{(s-2)/s}+\|(u_{0})_{\delta_{1}}-(u_{0})_{\delta_{2}}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{1/s}.

By (
5
), we have

\|(u_{0})_{\delta_{1}}-(u_{0})_{\delta_{2}}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\leqslant\|(u_{0})_{\delta_{1}}-u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}+\|(u_{0})_{\delta_{2}}-u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\lesssim o((\delta_{1})^{-s})+o((\delta_{2})^{-s}),

so that

\displaystyle\|w\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}\lesssim\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{(s-2)/s}+(o((\delta_{1})^{-s})+o((\delta_{2})^{-s}))^{1/s}.

Thus, we obtain

(11)

\|w\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}\lesssim\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{(s-2)/s}+o((\delta_{1})^{-1})+o((\delta_{2})^{-1}).

We now would like to prove the highest order estimate.
It is here that we will use good properties of regularized data (
3
).

Proposition 22
.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
Assume
\delta_{1}<\delta_{2}
and
m>n
.
Furthermore, assume
\delta_{1}\leqslant n^{(s-2)/s-\varepsilon},
for some
\varepsilon>0.

Let
u_{n,\delta_{1}},u_{m,\delta_{2}}
be solutions for the respective regularized Benjamin-Ono problem which exist on the same time interval
[0,T]
.
Then, we have the following estimate

\|u_{n,\delta_{1}}-u_{m,\delta_{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}_{qp}}=o(1)

for large enough
\delta_{1}
.
In addition, the implicit constants depend only on the norm of the initial data and as such can be taken uniform as long as the initial data comes from a compact set.

Proof of Proposition
22
.

As before, we consider the time derivative of
\|D^{s}_{x}(u_{n,\delta_{1}}-u_{m,\delta_{2}})\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}.

For ease of notation, write
u=u_{n,\delta_{1}},v=u_{m,\delta_{2}},w=u-v,
so that we wish to estimate

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}w}(k)\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}w(-k)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}(\chi_{n}[(\chi_{n}u)(\chi_{n}u_{x})]-\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}v)(\chi_{m}v_{x})])}\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}w(-k)

\displaystyle+\quad 2\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}(\chi_{n}[Hu_{xx}]-\chi_{m}[Hv_{xx}])}\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}w(-k).

First, we pick the
\varepsilon>0
. Note that since

s>N/2+1\geqslant 2/2+1=2,

we know that there is some
\varepsilon>0
such that

\frac{s-2}{s}>\varepsilon>\frac{2}{3}\frac{s-2}{s}.

Note that with this choice
\varepsilon
, we necessarily have

(s-2)/s-\varepsilon>0\qquad\text{and}\qquad 2(s-2)/s-3\varepsilon<0.

For convenience, we write

(12)

\delta_{1}\leqslant n^{(s-2)/s-\varepsilon},\quad(s-2)/s-\varepsilon>0,\quad 2(s-2)/s-3\varepsilon<0,

so that we can refer to these conditions easily.
Furthermore, note that

(13)

\delta_{1}n^{-1}\leqslant n^{(s-2)/s-\varepsilon}n^{-1}=n^{(-s+s-2)/s-\varepsilon}=n^{-2/s-\varepsilon}.

Now, we consider the linear part.
Due to (
8
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}(\chi_{n}[Hu_{xx}]-\chi_{m}[Hv_{xx}])}\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}w(-k)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}(\chi_{m}[Hw_{xx}])}\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}w(-k)}_{\text{$=0$ by Lemma \eqref{lineartermvanishes}}}+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}([\chi_{n}-\chi_{m}]Hu_{xx})}\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}w(-k).

By applying Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, it is enough to estimate

\|\mathcal{F}{D^{s+2}_{x}([\chi_{n}-\chi_{m}]u)}\|_{\ell^{2}_{k}}.

Due to the presence of
\chi_{n}-\chi_{m}
, we first apply Lemma (
20
) and then use Lemma (
19
):

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}{D^{s+2}_{x}([\chi_{n}-\chi_{m}]u)}\|_{\ell^{2}_{k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{l_{1}}\|u\|_{H^{s+{l_{1}}+2}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{l_{1}}(\delta_{1})^{{l_{1}}+2}\|u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}

\displaystyle=n^{-l_{1}}(\delta_{1})^{{l_{1}}+2}\|u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}.

Thus, the linear term is bounded by

n^{-l_{1}}(\delta_{1})^{{l_{1}}+2}\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}},

where the constants depend on
s
and the uniform bound on
v
. Letting
l_{1}=1
lets us bound the linear term by

n^{-1}\delta_{1}^{3}\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}.

We proceed with the nonlinear term.
Using the decomposition in (
9
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}(\chi_{n}[(\chi_{n}u)(\chi_{n}u_{x})]-\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}v)(\chi_{m}v_{x})])}(k)\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}w(-k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}v)(\chi_{m}w_{x})]}(k)\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}w(-k)+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}w)(\chi_{m}u)_{x}]}(k)\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}w(-k)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}u)((\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})u)_{x}]}(k)\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}w(-k)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}[((\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})u)(\chi_{n}u)_{x}]}(k)\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}w(-k)+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}[\chi_{n}-\chi_{m}][\chi_{n}u\chi_{n}u_{x}]}(k)\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}w(-k)

\displaystyle=I+II+III+IV+V.

We seek to bound each term.
The common strategy in all calculations is to add and subtract certain terms to use the fractional Leibniz rule (Proposition
7
).
Note:

(1)

the term
I
has the same structure as the nonlinearity in the regularized Benjamin-Ono equation, so first write

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}v)(\chi_{m}w_{x})]}(k)\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}w(-k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}[(\chi_{m}v)(\chi_{m}w_{x})]}(k)\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}w(-k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}[(\chi_{m}v)(\chi_{m}w_{x})]-(\chi_{m}v)D^{s}_{x}(\chi_{m}w_{x})}(k)\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}w(-k)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{(\chi_{m}v)D^{s}_{x}(\chi_{m}w_{x})}(k)\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}w(-k).

Application of the fractional Leibniz rule and energy cancellation argument yield that both terms are bounded by

\|\chi_{m}v\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\|\chi_{m}w\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\|D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}.

By uniform bound, we see that
I
is bounded by

\displaystyle\|w\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\|D^{s}_{x}w_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}+\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}})\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|D^{s}_{x}w_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}+\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}.

Since
\|D^{s}_{x}w_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}=o(1)
for
\delta_{1}
large enough, we have

I\lesssim o(1)\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}+\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}.

(2)

the term
II
is dealt with similarly to
I:

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}w)(\chi_{m}u)_{x}]}(k)\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}w(-k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}[(\chi_{m}w)(\chi_{m}u)_{x}]}(k)\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}w(-k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}[(\chi_{m}w)(\chi_{m}u)_{x}]-\chi_{m}wD^{s}_{x}(\chi_{m}u)_{x}}(k)\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}w(-k)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{(\chi_{m}w)D^{s}_{x}(\chi_{m}u)_{x}}(k)\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}w(-k).

The first sum is bounded by

\|\chi_{m}w\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\|(\chi_{m}v)_{x}\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}\leqslant\|w\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\|v\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\lesssim\|w\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\leqslant\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}+\|D^{s}_{x}w_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}.

For the second sum, we bound by

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}{(\chi_{m}w)D^{s}_{x}(\chi_{m}u)_{x}}\|_{\ell^{2}_{k}}\|D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\chi_{m}w\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}\|D^{s}_{x}(\chi_{m}u)_{x}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\|D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta_{1}\|w\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}\|D^{s}_{x}u\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta_{1}\|w\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}

where we used Lemma
3
.
We then use (
11
) to gain decay from
\delta_{1}\|w\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}
:

\displaystyle\delta_{1}\|w\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta_{1}\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{(s-2)/s}+\delta_{1}o((\delta_{1})^{-1})+\delta_{1}o((\delta_{2})^{-1})

\displaystyle=\delta_{1}n^{(2-s)/s}+\delta_{1}o((\delta_{2})^{-1})+o(1),

for
\delta_{1}
large enough.
By assumption,
\delta_{1}<\delta_{2},
so that

\delta_{1}\|w\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}\lesssim\delta_{1}n^{(2-s)/s}+o(1).

Therefore,
II
is bounded by

(\delta_{1}n^{(2-s)/s}+o(1))\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}

Using (
12
) we have

\delta_{1}n^{(2-s)/s}\leqslant n^{(s-2)/s-\varepsilon}n^{(2-s)/s}=n^{-\varepsilon},

so that
II
indeed decays:

(n^{-\varepsilon}+o(1))\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}.

(3)

for the term
III
as before we write

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}u)((\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})u)_{x}]}(k)\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}w(-k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}[(\chi_{m}u)((\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})u)_{x}]}(k)\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}w(-k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}[(\chi_{m}u)((\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})u)_{x}]-(\chi_{m}u)D^{s}_{x}[((\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})u)_{x}]}(k)\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}w(-k)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{\chi_{m}uD^{s}_{x}(\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})u_{x}}(k)\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}w(-k).

The first sum is bounded by

\|\chi_{m}u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\|(\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})u)_{x}\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}\|D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\lesssim n^{-l_{2}}(\delta_{1})^{l_{2}}\|D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}},

where we invoked Lemma
20
and Lemma
19
.
For the second sum, we bound similarly:

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}{\chi_{m}uD^{s}_{x}(\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})u_{x}}\|_{\ell^{2}_{k}}\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathcal{F}{\chi_{m}u}\|_{\ell^{1}_{k}}\|\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}(\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})u_{x}}\|_{\ell^{2}_{k}}\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{l_{3}}(\delta_{1})^{l_{3}+1},

for
l_{3}>0
.
Letting
l_{2}=l_{3}=1
, we find the term
IV
is bounded above by

\Big(n^{-1}\delta_{1}+n^{-1}\delta_{1}^{2}\Big)\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}.

(4)

the term
IV
is very similar to
III.
Decomposing
IV
as

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}[((\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})u)(\chi_{n}u)_{x}]}(k)\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}w(-k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}[((\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})u)(\chi_{n}u)_{x}]}(k)\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}w(-k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}[((\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})u)(\chi_{n}u)_{x}]-((\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})u)D^{s}_{x}(\chi_{m}w)_{x}}(k)\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}w(-k)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{((\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})u)D^{s}_{x}(\chi_{n}u)_{x}}(k)\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}w(-k),

we find that it is bounded by

\Big(\delta_{1}\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{l_{4}}+\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{l_{5}}(\delta_{1})^{l_{5}}\Big)\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}},

with
l_{4}\leqslant 1
.
Again, let
l_{4}=l_{5}=1
so that
V
has the bound

n^{-1}\delta_{2}\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}.

(5)

for the term
V
, first apply Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Lemma
20
to obtain

\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{l_{6}}\|D^{s+l_{6}}(\chi_{n}u\chi_{n}u_{x})\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}.

This is bounded by

\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{l_{6}}\|\chi_{n}u\chi_{n}u_{x}\|_{H^{s+l_{6}}_{qp}}\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}.

Note that by applying the algebra property, Lemma
19
, and the uniform bound, we get

\|\chi_{n}u\chi_{n}u_{x}\|_{H^{s+l_{6}}_{qp}}\leqslant\|\chi_{n}u\|_{H^{s+l_{6}}_{qp}}\|\chi_{n}u_{x}\|_{H^{s+l_{6}+1}_{qp}}\lesssim\delta_{1}^{2l_{6}+1}

Finally, let
l_{6}=1
, so that the bound is

I\lesssim n^{-1}\delta_{1}^{3}\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}},

as needed.

Combining the estimates for nonlinear and linear terms, we obtain the following bound:

\displaystyle\Big(n^{-1}\delta_{1}+n^{-1}\delta_{1}^{2}+n^{-1}\delta_{1}^{3}\Big)\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}+(n^{-\varepsilon}+o(1))\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}+\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}.

Note that since
\delta_{1}
is big enough, it is enough to estimate
n^{-1}\delta_{1}^{3}
.

Using (
12
) and (
13
), we obtain

\displaystyle n^{-1}\delta_{1}^{3}

\displaystyle\leqslant n^{-2/s-\varepsilon}\delta_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant n^{-2/s-\varepsilon}n^{2((s-2)/s-\varepsilon)}.

Combining the exponents yields the power

\displaystyle-2/s-\varepsilon+2((s-2)/s-\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=-2/s+2(s-2)/s-3\varepsilon

\displaystyle=(-2+2s-4)/s-3\varepsilon

\displaystyle=2(s-3)/s-3\varepsilon.

Here, note that
\frac{2(s-3)}{s}<\frac{2(s-2)}{s},

so that

2(s-3)/s-3\varepsilon<\frac{2(s-2)}{s}-3\varepsilon<0

by choice of
\varepsilon
.
Thus, we have

n^{-1}\delta_{1}^{3}\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\leqslant n^{\frac{2(s-2)}{s}-3\varepsilon}\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}\lesssim\Big(n^{\frac{2(s-2)}{s}-3\varepsilon}+n^{-\varepsilon}+o(1)\Big)\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}+\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2},

so that

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\lesssim\Big(n^{\frac{2(s-2)}{s}-3\varepsilon}+n^{-\varepsilon}+o(1)\Big)+\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}.

Finally, a Gronwall type argument reveals that

\displaystyle\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{t}(n^{\frac{2(s-2)}{s}-3\varepsilon}+n^{-\varepsilon}+o(1))+\|D^{s}_{x}w_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{\frac{2(s-2)}{s}-3\varepsilon}+n^{-\varepsilon}+o(1),

since
\|D^{s}_{x}w_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}=o(1)
for
\delta_{1}
large enough.
Furthermore, since
\delta_{2}<n^{(s-2)/s-\varepsilon},
we know that

n^{\frac{2(s-2)}{s}-3\varepsilon}+n^{-\varepsilon}\leqslant\delta_{1}^{-l}

for some
l>0
.
Thus, we obtain
\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}=o(1).

The same analysis applied to
\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}
yields
\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}=o(1)
,
so that we obtain the desired result.
∎

Note that now that we have the Cauchy bound, we can also perform Cauchy-like estimates on the linear and nonlinear terms.
This will be useful in the next section.

Corollary 23
.

Suppose the same assumptions hold as in Proposition
22
.
First, the following bound holds on the difference of nonlinearities:

(14)

\|\chi_{n}[(\chi_{n}u)(\chi_{n}u_{x})]-\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}v)(\chi_{m}v_{x})]\|_{H^{s-1-\gamma_{1}}_{qp}}\lesssim o(1)+\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{\gamma_{1}},

where
\gamma_{1}>0
is small enough so that
s-1-\gamma_{1}>N/2.

Second, the following bound on holds on the difference of linear terms:

(15)

\|\chi_{n}Hu_{xx}-\chi_{m}Hv_{xx}\|_{H^{s-2\gamma_{2}}_{qp}}\lesssim o(1)+\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{\gamma_{2}},

where
\gamma_{2}>0
is small enough.
Furthermore, both (
14
) and (
15
) hold uniformly on
[0,T]
.

Proof.

We only sketch the proof.
For the difference of nonlinear terms, use the decomposition (
9
) to obtain the following:

\displaystyle\|\chi_{n}[(\chi_{n}u)(\chi_{n}u_{x})]-\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}v)(\chi_{m}v_{x})]\|_{H^{s-1-\gamma_{1}}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\|\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}v)(\chi_{m}w_{x})]\|_{H^{s-1-\gamma_{1}}_{qp}}+\|\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}w)(\chi_{m}u)_{x}]\|_{H^{s-1-\gamma_{1}}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}u)((\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})u)_{x}]\|_{H^{s-1-\gamma_{1}}_{qp}}+\|\chi_{m}[((\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})u)(\chi_{n}u)_{x}]\|_{H^{s-1-\gamma_{1}}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|[\chi_{n}-\chi_{m}][\chi_{n}u\chi_{n}u_{x}]\|_{H^{s-1-\gamma_{1}}_{qp}}.

As
s-1-\gamma_{1}>N/2
, we can apply the algebra property on the first four terms.
For the fifth term, first apply Lemma
20
and then the algebra property.
These operations altogether let us bound the above by

\displaystyle\|\chi_{n}[(\chi_{n}u)(\chi_{n}u_{x})]-\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}v)(\chi_{m}v_{x})]\|_{H^{s-1-\gamma_{1}}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\|v\|_{H^{s-1-\gamma_{1}}_{qp}}\|w_{x}\|_{H^{s-1-\gamma_{1}}_{qp}}+\|w\|_{H^{s-1-\gamma_{1}}_{qp}}\|u_{x}\|_{H^{s-1-\gamma_{1}}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\|u\|_{H^{s-1-\gamma_{1}}_{qp}}\|(\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})u_{x}\|_{H^{s-1-\gamma_{1}}_{qp}}+\|(\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})u\|_{H^{s-1-\gamma_{1}}_{qp}}\|u_{x}\|_{H^{s-1-\gamma_{1}}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{\gamma_{1}}\|u\|_{H^{s-1-\gamma_{1}}_{qp}}\|u_{x}\|_{H^{s-1-\gamma_{1}}_{qp}}.

Simplifying and applying Lemma
20
on the third and fourth term yields the bound

\displaystyle\|\chi_{n}[(\chi_{n}u)(\chi_{n}u_{x})]-\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}v)(\chi_{m}v_{x})]\|_{H^{s-1-\gamma_{1}}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\|v\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\|w\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}+\|w\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\|u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}+\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{\gamma_{1}}\|u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}^{2}.

Applying the uniform bound and the Cauchy estimate yields the bound

\|\chi_{n}[(\chi_{n}u)(\chi_{n}u_{x})]-\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}v)(\chi_{m}v_{x})]\|_{H^{s-1-\gamma_{1}}_{qp}}\lesssim o(1)+\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{\gamma_{1}}.

For the difference of linear terms, similarly use the decomposition (
8
).
As such, applying Lemma
20
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\|\chi_{n}Hu_{xx}-\chi_{m}Hv_{xx}\|_{H^{s-2-\gamma_{2}}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|(\chi_{n}-\chi_{m})u_{xx}\|_{H^{s-2\gamma_{2}}_{qp}}+\|\chi_{m}Hw_{xx}\|_{H^{s-2-\gamma_{2}}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{\gamma_{2}}\|u_{xx}\|_{H^{s-2}_{qp}}+\|w\|_{H^{s-\gamma_{2}}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{\gamma_{2}}\|u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}+\|w\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}.

Clearly the latter can be bounded by

\|\chi_{n}[(\chi_{n}u)(\chi_{n}u_{x})]-\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}v)(\chi_{m}v_{x})]\|_{H^{s-1-\gamma_{1}}_{qp}}\leqslant\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{\gamma_{2}}+o(1),

so that we obtain the result.
∎

Finally, we perform the following continuity in the initial data for the regularized Benjamin-Ono equation.
Namely, let
u_{0},v_{0}\in\mathcal{K}\subset H^{s}_{qp},
where
\mathcal{K}
is compact in
H^{s}_{qp}
.
Let
(u_{0})_{\delta}
be a regularized version of
u_{0}
as defined in (
3
), with parameter
\delta>0
.
Let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
u_{n}
solve

\displaystyle u_{t}

\displaystyle=\chi_{n}[(\chi_{n}u_{n})(\chi_{n}u_{nx})]+\chi_{n}[Hu_{nxx}],

\displaystyle u(0,\cdot)

\displaystyle=(u_{0})_{\delta},

whereas
u_{m}
denotes the solution of

\displaystyle u_{t}

\displaystyle=\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}u_{m})(\chi_{m}u_{mx})]+\chi_{m}[Hu_{mxx}],

\displaystyle u(0,\cdot)

\displaystyle=(v_{0})_{\delta}.

For ease of notation, write
u=u_{n},v=u_{m},w=u-v.

Since
\mathcal{K}
is compact, and so by Corollary
18
,
u
and
v
exist on the same time interval.

We have the following result:

Proposition 24
.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
\delta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
Assume
m\geqslant n
.
Let
u_{0},v_{0}\in\mathcal{K}\subset H^{s}_{qp},
where
\mathcal{K}
is compact in
H^{s}_{qp}
.
Furthermore, assume
\delta\leqslant n^{(s-2)/s-\varepsilon},
for some
\varepsilon>0.

Let
u_{n},u_{m}
be solutions for the respective regularized Benjamin-Ono problem which exist on the same time interval
[0,T]
, defined as above.

Given
\eta>0
, we can pick
\delta>0
sufficiently large so that there is
\tilde{\eta}>0
such that

\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|<\tilde{\eta}
implies

\|u_{n}-u_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}_{qp}}<\eta.

In addition, the implicit constants depend only on the norm of the initial data and as such can be taken uniform as long as the initial data comes from a compact set.

Proof.

The argument is almost identical to the proof of Proposition
22
.
The
L^{2}_{qp}
estimate becomes

\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\lesssim\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{s-2}+\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}.

The (
11
) becomes

\displaystyle\|w\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{s-2}+\|(u_{0})_{\delta}-(v_{0})_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\Big)^{1/s}

\displaystyle\leqslant\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{(s-2)/s}+\|(u_{0})_{\delta}-(v_{0})_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{1/s}

\displaystyle\leqslant\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{(s-2)/s}+\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{1/s}.

As for the
D^{s}_{x}
estimate, the only place where the argument changes is in the estimate of
II
.
Here, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}w)(\chi_{m}u)_{x}]}(k)\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}w(-k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}[(\chi_{m}w)(\chi_{m}u)_{x}]}(k)\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}w(-k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{D^{s}_{x}[(\chi_{m}w)(\chi_{m}u)_{x}]-\chi_{m}wD^{s}_{x}(\chi_{m}u)_{x}}(k)\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}w(-k)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{(\chi_{m}w)D^{s}_{x}(\chi_{m}u)_{x}}(k)\mathcal{F}D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}w(-k).

The first sum is bounded by

\|\chi_{m}w\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\|(\chi_{m}v)_{x}\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}\leqslant\|w\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\|v\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\lesssim\|w\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\leqslant\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}+\|D^{s}_{x}w_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}.

For the second sum, we bound by

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}{(\chi_{m}w)D^{s}_{x}(\chi_{m}u)_{x}}\|_{\ell^{2}_{k}}\|D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\chi_{m}w\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}\|D^{s}_{x}(\chi_{m}u)_{x}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\|D^{s}_{x}\chi_{m}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta\|w\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}\|D^{s}_{x}u\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta\|w\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}

where we used Lemma
3
.
We then use (
11
) to gain decay from
\delta\|w\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}
:

\displaystyle\delta\|w\|_{H^{s-1}_{qp}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{(s-2)/s}+\delta\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{1/s}

\displaystyle=\delta n^{(2-s)/s}+\delta\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{1/s},

for
\delta_{1}
large enough.
Therefore,
II
is bounded by

(\delta n^{(2-s)/s}+\delta\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{1/s})\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}.

Combining all the estimates as in the proof of Proposition
22
we have

\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\lesssim n^{\frac{2(s-2)}{s}-3\varepsilon}+n^{-\varepsilon}+\delta\|D^{s}_{x}(u_{0}-v_{0})\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{1/s}.

Since
\frac{2(s-2)}{s}-3\varepsilon<0
, there is some
\ell>0
such that

n^{\frac{2(s-2)}{s}-3\varepsilon}+n^{-\varepsilon}\leqslant\delta^{-\ell}.

Combining this with the
L^{2}_{qp}
estimate we have

\|w\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\leqslant\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}+\|D^{s}_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}\lesssim n^{2-s}+n^{\frac{2(s-2)}{s}-3\varepsilon}+n^{-\varepsilon}+\delta\|D^{s}_{x}(u_{0}-v_{0})\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{1/s}+\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}.

Since all the exponents of
n
are negative, there is some
\ell>0
such that

n^{2-s}+n^{\frac{2(s-2)}{s}-3\varepsilon}+n^{-\varepsilon}\leqslant\delta^{-\ell},

so that

\|w\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\lesssim\delta^{-\ell}+\delta\|D^{s}_{x}(u_{0}-v_{0})\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{1/s}+\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{qp}},

where this inequality holds up to some constant
A
.

Given
\eta>0
, pick
\delta>0
big enough so that
\delta^{-\ell}+\delta^{-1}+\delta^{-2s}<A^{-1}\eta
.
Now, pick
\tilde{\eta}=\delta^{-2s}
.
Therefore,

\|w\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\leqslant A(n^{-\ell}+\delta(\delta^{-2s})^{1/s}+\delta^{-2s})=A(\delta^{-\ell}+\delta^{-1}+\delta^{-2s})<\eta,

which completes the proof.
∎

4.
Local Wellposedness of Benjamin-Ono equation

In this section, we combine the results obtained earlier to prove local-wellposedness of the Benjamin-Ono equation.
There are several items to show:

•

the existence of a solution
u
to the Benjamin-Ono equation (
2
) in
H^{s}_{qp}
,

•

uniqueness of the solution
u
,

•

the continuity in time of the norm
\|u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}
,

•

the continuity in the initial data.

We begin with the first item.

Proposition 25
.

Let
s>N/2+1
.
Let
u_{0}\in H^{s}_{qp}
.
Then, there exists
T=T(s,\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}})
such that there exists a unique solution
u\in L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{s}_{qp})
of the Benjamin-Ono equation with the initial data
u_{0}
.
In particular,

•

if
s>N/2+2
, then
u
solves (
2
) pointwise,

•

if
N/2+2\geqslant s>N/2+1
, then
u
solves (
2
) in the sense of distributions.

Proof.

Consider the sequence
\{(u_{0})_{\delta}\}
of regularized initial data (
3
).
Given
\delta>0,
pick
n_{\delta}=\exp(\frac{s}{s-2-\varepsilon s}\log\delta),
where
\varepsilon>0
is chosen as in Proposition
22
.
With this choice, we satisfy
\delta=n^{(s-2)/s-\varepsilon}
.

Consider the sequence
u^{\delta}:=u_{n_{\delta},\delta}
.
From the previous section, we know that this sequence is Cauchy in
L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{s}_{qp})
, so it has a limit
u
.
Furthermore, from Corollary
18
, it is known that the time of existence
T
satisfies the bound

T<\frac{1}{C\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}}.

The independence of the bound on
T
from
\delta
and
n_{\delta}
ensures that when the limit is taken, the time of existence is not affected.
Thus,
u\in L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{s}_{qp})
.

We check that
u
solves the Benjamin-Ono equation.

Let
\gamma>0
be such that
s-2-\gamma>0
and
s-1-\gamma>N/2
.
By Corollary
23
, we know that

\|\chi_{n}[(\chi_{n}u)(\chi_{n}u_{x})]-\chi_{m}[(\chi_{m}v)(\chi_{m}v_{x})]\|_{H^{s-1-\gamma}_{qp}}\lesssim o(1)+\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{\gamma},

and

\|\chi_{n}Hu_{xx}-\chi_{m}Hv_{xx}\|_{H^{s-2-\gamma}_{qp}}\lesssim o(1)+\max\{1/n,1/m\}^{\gamma}.

Letting
n=n_{\delta}
and
m,\delta_{2}\to\infty
yields the following bound:

(16)

\|\chi_{n}[(\chi_{n}u^{\delta})(\chi_{n}u^{\delta}_{x})]-uu_{x}\|_{H^{s-1-\gamma}_{qp}}\lesssim o(1)+n^{-\gamma},

and

(17)

\|\chi_{n}Hu^{\delta}_{xx}-Hu_{xx}\|_{H^{s-2\gamma}_{qp}}\lesssim o(1)+n^{-\gamma},

where for the ease of writing we write
n:=n_{\delta}
.

By integrating, we have

(18)

u^{\delta}(t,x)=(u_{0})_{\delta}(x)+\int^{t}_{0}\chi_{n}[(\chi_{n}u^{\delta})(\chi_{n}u^{\delta}_{x})]+\chi_{n}[Hu^{\delta}_{xx}]\,\mathrm{d}s,

where
n
is defined as above.

To show that
u
solves the original Benjamin-Ono problem, we would like to obtain that

(19)

u(t,x)=u_{0}(x)+\int^{t}_{0}uu_{x}(s,x)+Hu_{xx}(s,x)\,\mathrm{d}s

holds pointwise or in the sense of distributions.

Consider the difference of the right-hand sides of (
18
) and (
19
):

(20)

\int^{t}_{0}\chi_{n}[(\chi_{n}u^{\delta})(\chi_{n}u^{\delta}_{x})]-uu_{x}(s,x)+\chi_{n}[Hu^{\delta}_{xx}]-Hu_{xx}(s,x)\,\mathrm{d}s

Taking the
H^{s-2-\gamma}_{qp}
norm of the integrand and applying the bounds (
16
) and (
17
), we are able to
obtain that this is of the form
o(1)+n^{-\gamma}.

Letting
\delta\to\infty
implies
n\to\infty
as well.
Since the bounds are uniform in
t
, we also obtain that the expression (
20
) vanishes as
\delta\to\infty
.
Since
u^{\delta}\to u
and
(u_{0})_{\delta}\to u_{0}
in
H^{s}_{qp}
, they also converge in
H^{s-2}_{qp}
.
Therefore, (
19
) holds in
H^{s-2}_{qp}
.

Finally, if
s>N/2+2,
then
s-2>N/2
. By the Sobolev inequality, the equation (
19
) holds pointwise.
If
N/2+2\geqslant s>N/2+1
, then
N/2\geqslant s>N/2-1
, and so the equation (
19
) holds in the sense of distributions.
∎

Proposition 26

(Weak continuity in time)
.

The limit
u
is weakly continuous in time in
H^{s}_{qp}.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in H^{-s}_{qp}
.
Note

\displaystyle\langle\phi,u(s)-u(t)\rangle=\langle\phi,u(s)-u^{\delta}(s)\rangle+\langle\phi,u^{\delta}(s)-u^{\delta}(t)\rangle+\langle\phi,u^{\delta}(t)-u(t)\rangle.

By Theorem
14
, the regularized solutions are continuous in time.
As such, the middle term can be made as small as we would like.
As for the first and the third, it is enough to prove that one of them decays.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. For any
m
satisfying
1\leqslant m<s,
let
\tilde{\phi}\in H^{-m}
be given such that

\|\phi-\tilde{\phi}\|_{H^{-s}_{qp}}\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

Now, write and bound

\displaystyle\langle\phi,u(s)-u^{\delta}(s)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\phi-\tilde{\phi},u(s)\rangle+\langle\tilde{\phi},u(s)-u^{\delta}(s)\rangle+\langle\tilde{\phi}-\phi,u^{\delta}(s)\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\phi-\tilde{\phi}\|_{H^{-s}_{qp}}\|u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}+\|\tilde{\phi}\|_{H^{-m}_{qp}}\|u-u^{\delta}\|_{H^{m}_{qp}}+\|\phi-\tilde{\phi}\|_{H^{-s}_{qp}}\|u^{\delta}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}.

Note that
\|u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}
and
\|u^{\delta}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}
are bounded due to uniform bounds.
Thus, we obtain that

\langle\phi,u(s)-u^{\delta}(s)\rangle\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon}{3}+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}=\varepsilon.

Therefore, we have that
u\in C_{W}([0,T];H^{s}_{qp})
.
∎

We move on to showing uniqueness.

Proposition 27

(Uniqueness)
.

Let
s>N/2+1
. Then, solutions of the Benjamin-Ono equation are unique.

Proof.

Let
s>N/2+1
and let
u_{0}\in H^{s}_{qp}
.
Suppose
u,v
are two solutions of the Benjamin-Ono equation with initial data
u_{0}
.
Write
w=u-v
. Then,
w
satisfies

w_{t}=wu_{x}+vw_{x}+Hw_{xx}

and
w(0)=0
.
Now, as in the proof of the Cauchy estimate one finds that

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\lesssim(\|u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}+\|v\|_{H^{s}_{qp}})\|w\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}^{2}\leqslant C\|w\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2},

where
C
does not depend on
w
but may depend on the uniform bound of
u
and
v
.
An application of Gronwall’s inequality yields

\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}\leqslant\|w(0)\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}\exp(Ct)=0,

since clearly
\|w(0)\|_{L^{2}_{qp}}^{2}=0.

Thus, we have
u=v
almost everywhere.
Now, since
s>N/2+1
and
u,v\in H^{s}_{qp}
,
u
and
v
are both continuous.
Thus,
u=v
everywhere and we have uniqueness.
∎

We now are able to prove that
u\in C([0,T];H^{s}_{qp})
. Since
H^{s}_{qp}
is a Hilbert space and we have already demonstrated weak continuity,
all that remains to show is continuity of the norm.

Proposition 28
.

For
s>N/2+1
, we have
\|u(t)\|_{H_{qp}^{s}}
is a continuous function of time.

Proof.

As in the proof of the uniform estimate, we have

\|u^{\delta}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}+((\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}})^{-1}-Ct)^{-1}.

Letting
\delta\to\infty
we have

\|u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}+((\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}})^{-1}-Ct)^{-1}.

Now, let
t_{n}\to 0^{+}
. The apriori estimate gives

\|u(t_{n})\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}+((\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}})^{-1}-Ct_{n})^{-1}.

Taking the limit supremum
n\to\infty
, we obtain

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|u(t_{n})\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}+\limsup_{n\to\infty}((\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}})^{-1}-Ct_{n})^{-1}=\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}.

Now, weak continuity of
u
in
H^{s}
implies that
u(t_{n})\rightharpoonup u(0).

By lower semi-continuity of the norm we have

\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\leqslant\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|u(t_{n})\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}.

Thus, we obtain

\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u(t_{n})\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}

This shows right-continuity at
t=0^{+}
.

For any
T^{*}\in(0,T)
, we interpret
T^{*}
as a new initial time.
We can repeat our arguments for existence and uniqueness of solutions to the regularized Benjamin-Ono equation starting at a new time
T^{*}.

We find solutions of the original Benjamin-Ono equation on some time interval around
T^{*}
.
By the above argument, these solutions are right-continuous at
t=T^{*}
.
By uniqueness, solutions starting at
t=T^{*}
and solutions starting at
t=0
must be the same.
This is true for any
T^{*}\in(0,T)
so the solutions starting at
t=0
must be right continuous on
(0,T)
.

For left continuity, note that the Benjamin-Ono equation is invariant under the change of variables
(t,x)\mapsto(-t,-x)
.
From this one obtains left continuity on
(0,T)
.
We conclude that the norm
\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}
is continuous in time.
∎

Remark 29
.

Let
\eta>0
be given.
By the Cauchy estimate in Proposition (
22
), there exists
N>0
such that for all
\delta>N,
uniformly in
u_{0}\in\mathcal{K},
we have

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u^{\delta}-u\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}<\eta,

where
u^{\delta}
and
u
solve the regularized and the original Benjamin-Ono equation.
The uniformity in
u_{0}
comes from the fact the rate of decay is uniform for
\mathcal{K}
compact.

Proposition 30

(Continuity in initial data in
H^{s}_{qp}
)
.

Let
s>N/2+1.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{s}_{qp}
and suppose
\{u_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence such that
u_{n}\to u_{0}
in
H^{s}_{qp}.

Let
T
be the common time of existence for solutions of Benjamin-Ono equation with initial data
\{u_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
, which can be taken independent of
n
.
Let
U_{n}\in C([0,T];H^{s}_{qp})
be the solution of the Benjamin-Ono equation with initial data
u_{n}
for
n=0,1,2,\ldots
.
Then,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|U_{n}-U_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}=0.

Proof.

Let
\eta>0
. Given any
\delta>1
, write

U_{n}-U_{0}=(U_{n}-(U_{n})^{\delta})+((U_{n})^{\delta}-(U_{0})^{\delta})+((U_{n})^{\delta}-U_{0}),

where
(U_{n})^{\delta}
is the solution of the regularized Benjamin-Ono equation with initial data
u_{n}
and the regularizing parameter
\delta
.

It is clear that the set
\{u_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\cup u_{0}
is a compact subset of
H^{s}_{qp}
.
Therefore, per Remark
29
, we can pick
\delta>0
such that for all
n=0,1,2,\ldots
,
we have

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|(U_{n})^{\delta}-U_{n}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}<\frac{\eta}{3}.

It remains to deal with the difference
(U_{n})^{\delta}-(U_{0})^{\delta}
.
This follows from Proposition
24
: we can pick
\delta
sufficiently big so that

\|(U_{n})^{\delta}-(U_{0})^{\delta}\|_{H^{s}_{qp}}\leqslant\frac{\eta}{3}.

This completes the proof.
∎

5.
Conservation Laws

In this section, we explain the challenges of extending local time of existence.
In the case of periodic or decaying data, this is usually solved by means of conserved quantities.
These quantities, also known as conservation laws, allow to control the Sobolev norms when data is periodic or decaying.
Once local solutions are obtained, these quantities allow one to conclude that the Sobolev norms are bounded on any time interval.
In return, this allows to conclude that the local solutions exist on any time interval.
The first four conservation laws are

•

mass:

(21)

\int u\,\mathrm{d}x.

•

momentum:

(22)

\int u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,

•

energy:

(23)

\int\frac{u^{3}}{3}+uHu_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x,

•

the conservation law for the
H^{1}
norm:

(24)

\int\frac{u^{4}}{4}+\frac{3}{2}u^{2}Hu_{x}-\frac{3}{2}u_{x}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

When adapted to the case of quasiperiodic data, the conserved quantities still hold.
However, we show below that conservation laws no longer seem to control the quasiperiodic Sobolev norms.
For the reader’s convenience, we illustrate how the conservation laws are used by considering the energy (
23
) of Benjamin-Ono equation, with decaying initial data.

Since (
23
) is conserved, it is equal to some number, say
\alpha
, that only depends on
\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1/2}}
and not
t
.
Now, note that the nonhomogenous part of the
H^{1/2}
norm for the decaying data can be written as
\|u\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}=\int uHu_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x
.
Thus,
\|u\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}=\alpha-\int\frac{u^{3}}{3}\,\mathrm{d}x
.
Therefore we have

\|u\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}\lesssim|\alpha|+\frac{1}{3}\|u\|_{L^{3}}^{3}.

By Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, we know that
\|u\|_{L^{3}}^{3}
is bounded by
\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\partial^{1/2}_{x}u\|_{L^{2}},
i.e.
\|u\|_{L^{3}}\leqslant C\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\partial^{1/2}_{x}u\|_{L^{2}}
for a fixed
C>0
.
Applying Cauchy-Schwarz inequality then yields that

\|u\|_{L^{3}}^{3}\leqslant 2C^{4}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{4}+\frac{1}{2}\|\partial^{1/2}_{x}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

We then have that

\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}\leqslant|\alpha|+2C^{4}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{4}=|\alpha|+2C^{4}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{4},

where we used the conservation of
L^{2}
norm.
From here it follows that
\|u\|_{H^{1/2}}
is bounded only by the norm of the initial data, and we obtain the desired control.

Now, we consider the conserved quantities in the case of quasiperiodic data.
These conservation laws are listed below:

•

mass:

(25)

\quad\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}u\,\mathrm{d}x\text{ or }\hat{u}(0),

•

momentum:

(26)

\quad\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\text{ or }\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\hat{u}(k)\hat{u}(-k),

•

energy:

(27)

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}\frac{u^{3}}{3}+uHu_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x\text{ or }\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\frac{1}{3}\mathcal{F}{u^{2}}(k)\hat{u}(-k)+\mathcal{F}{Hu_{x}}(k)\hat{u}(-k),

•

conservation law for the
H^{1}
norm:

(28)

\begin{split}\lim_{R\to\infty}&\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}\frac{u^{4}}{4}+\frac{3}{2}u^{2}Hu_{x}-\frac{3}{2}u_{x}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\text{or }\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{F}{u^{3}}(k)\hat{u}(-k)+\frac{3}{2}\mathcal{F}{u^{2}}(k)\mathcal{F}{Hu_{x}}(-k)-\frac{3}{2}\hat{u_{x}}(k)\hat{u_{x}}(-k).\end{split}

Before we prove the conservation laws, let us show why the laws do not seem to control the quasiperiodic Sobolev norm.
A crucial step in the example above is the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality.
It is thus natural to ask if a quasiperiodic version of this inequality holds.
In contrast to decaying and periodic spaces, it appears to false, by a simple scaling argument
[
33
]
.

Remark 31

(Failure of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality)
.

Define a mean value type norm
{\mathcal{L}^{p}}
by

\|u\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}^{p}=\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int_{-R}^{R}|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x.

These norms are invariant under rescaling
u_{\lambda}(x)=u(x/\lambda),
i.e.
\|u\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}=\|u_{\lambda}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}
for any
\lambda>0
.
Furthermore, it is straightforward to check that
\widehat{D^{s}_{x}u_{\lambda}}(k)=\lambda^{-s}\widehat{D^{s}_{x}u}(\lambda k)
and that
D^{s}_{x}u_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{-s}D^{s}_{x}u(x/\lambda)
for any
\lambda>0
.
It then follows that
\|D^{s}_{x}u_{\lambda}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}=\lambda^{-s}\|D^{s}_{x}u\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}.

As a result, if any inequality of the form

\|u\|_{\mathcal{L}^{q}}\lesssim\|u\|_{\mathcal{L}^{r}}^{1-\theta}\|D^{s}_{x}u\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}^{\theta},\qquad s>0,\frac{1}{q}=\theta\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}\right)+(1-\theta)\frac{1}{r}

is true, rescaling would imply

\|u_{\lambda}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{q}}\lesssim\lambda^{-s\theta}\|u_{\lambda}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{r}}^{1-\theta}\|D^{s}_{x}u\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}^{\theta},\qquad s>0

Taking
\lambda\to\infty
in the last inequality would imply
\|u_{\lambda}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{q}}\to 0,
which of course need not be true.
Therefore, the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality fails to hold for quasiperiodic integrals.

Finally, we would like to prove conservation laws.
We emphasize that the quantities can be shown to be conserved via two ways: by directly taking time derivatives of averaged integrals, or by switching to Fourier series.
To illustrate our point, we prove conservation of the first three laws in Fourier space, whereas the fourth one we do directly.

Since the solutions here are taken to be smooth, so that by the equation
u_{t}
is continuous and thus we are allowed to exchange time derivatives when dealing with
u(t,x)
and
\hat{u}(t,k)
.
In what follows, we will consistently use Lemma
32
:

Lemma 32
.

For quasiperiodic
f,g,h
,

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{fg}(k)\mathcal{F}{h}(-k)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{f}(k)\mathcal{F}{gh}(-k)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{g}(k)\mathcal{F}{fh}(-k).

For a proof see the appendix.

Proof of (
25
).

This is conservation of mean. Write

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\hat{u}(k)=\mathcal{F}{uu_{x}}(k)+\mathcal{F}{Hu_{xx}}(k).

Consider the nonlinear term:

\mathcal{F}{uu_{x}}(k)=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{F}{\partial_{x}(u^{2})}(k)=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{F}{\partial_{x}(u^{2})}(k)=\frac{1}{2}\alpha\cdot k\mathcal{F}{u^{2}}(k),

so that at
k=0
we obtain

\mathcal{F}{uu_{x}}(0)=\frac{1}{2}\alpha\cdot 0\mathcal{F}{u^{2}}(0)=0.

As for the linear term, note

\mathcal{F}{Hu_{xx}}(k)=i^{2}(\alpha\cdot k)^{2}(-i\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot k))\hat{u}(k).

Clearly, this term vanishes at
k=0.
Thus, we obtain

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\hat{u}(0)=0,

so that
\hat{u}(0)=\hat{u_{0}}(0).

∎

Proof of (
26
).

Take the time derivative and use the equation:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\hat{u}(k)\hat{u}(-k)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\hat{u_{t}}(k)\hat{u}(-k)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\hat{uu_{x}}(k)\hat{u}(-k)+2\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\hat{Hu_{xx}}(k)\hat{u}(-k).

The linear term vanishes following the argument in the case of uniform estimate.
As for the nonlinear term, see Appendix (Lemma
34
,
n=2
) for a detailed proof that this term vanishes.
Thus, we conclude that

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\hat{u}(k)\hat{u}(-k)=0,

so that

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\hat{u}(k)\hat{u}(-k)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\hat{u_{0}}(k)\hat{u_{0}}(-k)=\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{qp}}.

∎

Proof of (
27
).

Take the time derivative and use chain rule:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\hat{u^{2}}(k)\hat{u}(-k)+\mathcal{F}{Hu_{x}}(k)\hat{u}(-k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\frac{1}{3}[\mathcal{F}{(u^{2})_{t}}(k)\hat{u}(-k)+\hat{u^{2}}(k)\hat{u_{t}}(-k)]+\mathcal{F}{H(u_{t})_{x}}(k)\hat{u}(-k)+\mathcal{F}{Hu_{x}}(k)\hat{u_{t}}(-k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\frac{1}{3}[2\mathcal{F}{uu_{t}}(k)\hat{u}(-k)+\hat{u^{2}}(k)\hat{u_{t}}(-k)]+\mathcal{F}{u_{t}}(k)\hat{Hu_{x}}(-k)+\mathcal{F}{Hu_{x}}(k)\hat{u_{t}}(-k)

Since

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{uu_{t}}(k)\hat{u}(-k)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\hat{u_{t}}(k)\hat{u^{2}}(-k),

we obtain that the time derivative equals

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\hat{u_{t}}(k)\hat{u^{2}}(-k)+2\mathcal{F}{u_{t}}(k)\mathcal{F}{Hu_{x}}(-k).

Now, use the equation:

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}(\mathcal{F}{Hu_{xx}}(k)+\mathcal{F}{uu_{x}}(k))\hat{u^{2}}(-k)+2(\mathcal{F}{Hu_{xx}}(k)+\mathcal{F}{uu_{x}}(k))\mathcal{F}{Hu_{x}}(-k).

Clearly, the term

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{Hu_{xx}}(k)\mathcal{F}{Hu_{x}}(-k)=0,

and as shown in the Appendix (Lemma
34
,
n=3
),

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{uu_{x}}(k)\hat{u^{2}}(-k)=0.

Thus, we are left with

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{Hu_{xx}}(k)\hat{u^{2}}(-k)+2\mathcal{F}{uu_{x}}(k)\mathcal{F}{Hu_{x}}(-k).

Going back to chain rule and integration by parts yields

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{Hu_{xx}}(k)\hat{u^{2}}(-k)=-\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{Hu_{x}}(k)\mathcal{F}{(u^{2})_{x}}(-k)=-2\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{Hu_{x}}(k)\mathcal{F}{uu_{x}}(-k),

so that

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{Hu_{xx}}(k)\hat{u^{2}}(-k)+2\mathcal{F}{uu_{x}}(k)\mathcal{F}{Hu_{x}}(-k)=0.

Thus, the term in question is conserved.
∎

Proof of (
28
).

We will use the following identity, called Cotlar’s identity:

f^{2}=(Hf)^{2}-2H(fHf),

where
f\in H^{s}_{qp}
.
Take the time derivative:

(29)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}\frac{u^{4}}{4}+\frac{3}{2}u^{2}Hu_{x}+2u_{x}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=

(30)

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}

\displaystyle u^{3}u_{t}+\frac{3}{2}(2uu_{t}Hu_{x}+u^{2}H\partial_{x}u_{t})+4u_{x}u_{tx}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Using that
H\partial_{x}
is self-adjoint and integration by parts yields

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}u^{3}u_{t}+\frac{3}{2}(2uu_{t}Hu_{x}+H\partial_{x}(u^{2})u_{t})-4u_{xx}u_{t}\,\mathrm{d}x.

This simplifies to

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}u^{3}u_{t}+3(uu_{t}Hu_{x}+H(uu_{x})u_{t})-4u_{xx}u_{t}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Now, use the equation:

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}u^{3}(uu_{x}+Hu_{xx})+3(uHu_{x}+H(uu_{x}))(uu_{x}+Hu_{xx})-4u_{xx}(uu_{x}+Hu_{xx})\,\mathrm{d}x.

For now we will assume that the three terms

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}u^{4}u_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}H(uu_{x})(uu_{x})\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}H(u_{xx})u_{xx}\,\mathrm{d}x,

vanish. As a result, we obtain

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}u^{3}Hu_{xx}+3u^{2}u_{x}Hu_{x}+3uHu_{x}Hu_{xx}+3Hu_{xx}H(uu_{x})-4u_{xx}uu_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Applying integration by parts on
u^{3}Hu_{xx}
cancels out
3u^{2}u_{x}Hu_{x}
, so that we are left with

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}3uHu_{x}Hu_{xx}+3H(uu_{x})Hu_{xx}-4u_{xx}uu_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Using that the Hilbert transform is unitary, we obtain

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}3uHu_{x}Hu_{xx}+3uu_{x}u_{xx}-4u_{xx}uu_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x=\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}3uHu_{x}Hu_{xx}-u_{xx}uu_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Integration by parts yields that

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}-\frac{3}{2}u_{x}(Hu_{x})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}u_{x}(u_{x})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}(-\frac{3}{2}(Hu_{x})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(u_{x})^{2})u_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x

Now, use Cotlar’s identity with
f=u_{x}
to rewrite the integrand as

-\frac{3}{2}(Hu_{x})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(Hu_{x})^{2}-H(u_{x}Hu_{x})=-(Hu_{x})^{2}-H(u_{x}Hu_{x}),

to obtain

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}(-(Hu_{x})^{2}-H(u_{x}Hu_{x}))u_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Finally, using the anti-self-adjointness of Hilbert transform,

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}H(u_{x}Hu_{x}))u_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x=-\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}u_{x}Hu_{x}Hu_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x=-\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}u_{x}(Hu_{x})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Combining, we obtain

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}(-(Hu_{x})^{2}-H(u_{x}Hu_{x}))u_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x=\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int^{R}_{-R}(-(Hu_{x})^{2}+(Hu_{x})^{2})u_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x=0,

as desired.
∎

Appendix A
Various results

Lemma 33
.

For quasiperiodic
f,g,h
,

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{fg}(k)\mathcal{F}{h}(-k)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{f}(k)\mathcal{F}{gh}(-k)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{g}(k)\mathcal{F}{fh}(-k).

Proof.

Writing a product as a convolution, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{fg}(k)\mathcal{F}{h}(-k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{f}(j)\mathcal{F}{g}(k-j)\mathcal{F}{h}(-k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{f}(j)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{g}(k-j)\mathcal{F}{h}(-k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{f}(j)\mathcal{F}{gh}(-j)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{f}(k)\mathcal{F}{gh}(-k).

On the third line, we interchange the summation and recognize a convolution in
k
.
Note that at the end, we have relabeled the index of summation.
The second identity follows similarly from using

\mathcal{F}{fg}(k)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{f}(k-j)\mathcal{F}{g}(j).

Thus we obtain the identities in question.
∎

Lemma 34
.

For any natural
n\geqslant 0
,
we have:

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\widehat{u^{n}}(k)\widehat{u_{x}}(-k)=0.

Proof.

Rewriting the sum as a convolution and using chain rule yields

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\widehat{u^{n}}(k)\widehat{u_{x}}(-k)=\mathcal{F}{(u^{n}u_{x})}(0)=\frac{1}{n+1}\mathcal{F}{(u^{n+1})_{x}}(0)=\frac{1}{n+1}\Big(i\alpha\cdot k\mathcal{F}{u^{n+1}}(k)\Big)\Big|_{k=0}=0,

so that we have the result.
∎

Lemma 35
.

We have:

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\widehat{v\partial_{x}u}(k)\hat{u}(-k)=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\widehat{v_{x}u}(k)\hat{u}(-k).

Proof.

We use Lemma
32
to move
\partial_{x}u
into the
\hat{u}(-k)
term:

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\widehat{v\partial_{x}u}(k)\hat{u}(-k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\widehat{v}(k)\widehat{u\partial_{x}u}(-k).

Recognizing the derivative term
u\partial_{x}u=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}(u^{2})
, we then write

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\widehat{v}(k)\widehat{u\partial_{x}u}(-k)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\widehat{v}(k)\widehat{\partial_{x}(u^{2})}(-k)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}(\alpha\cdot(-k))\widehat{v}(k)\widehat{u^{2}}(-k)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}(\alpha\cdot k)\widehat{v}(k)\widehat{u^{2}}(-k),

where we rewrote the derivative in Fourier variables and pulled the minus sign out of the sum.
The final term can be written as

-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\widehat{v_{x}}(k)\widehat{u^{2}}(-k),

and using Lemma
32
, we can shift one of
u
inside the
\widehat{u^{2}}
term into the
\widehat{v}
term.
We then obtain the desired identity.
∎

Lemma 36

(Cotlar’s identity)
.

Let
f\in H^{s}_{qp}
. Then,

(Hf)^{2}-f^{2}=2H(fHf).

Proof.

This proof is a straightforward extension of a standard argument to the quasiperiodic setting.

Since
f\in H^{s}_{qp}
, we know that the terms
(Hf)^{2}
and
H(fHf)
exist due to the algebra property.
In particular, all the terms involved have their Fourier series expressions, so it is enough to prove the identity in Fourier space.

In Fourier space, the identity is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}{(Hf)^{2}}(k)-\mathcal{F}{f^{2}}(k)

\displaystyle=2\mathcal{F}{H(fHf)}

\displaystyle=2(-i)\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot k)\mathcal{F}{(fHf)}(k)

\displaystyle=(-i)\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot k)\mathcal{F}{(fHf)}(k)+(-i)\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot k)\mathcal{F}{(fHf)}(k).

Using convolution theorem we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}{Hf}(j)\mathcal{F}{Hf}(k-j)-\hat{f}(j)\hat{f}(k-j)

\displaystyle=(-i)\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot k)\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\hat{f}(j)\mathcal{F}{Hf}(k-j)+(-i)\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot k)\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{F}{Hf}(j)\hat{f}(k-j)

Using the definition of Hilbert transform results in

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}

\displaystyle((-i)^{2}\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot j)\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot k-j)-1)\hat{f}(j)\hat{f}(k-j)

\displaystyle=(-i)\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot k)\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}(-i)\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot k-j)\hat{f}(j)\hat{f}(k-j)+(-i)\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot k)\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}(-i)\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot j)\hat{f}(j)\hat{f}(k-j)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}(-i)^{2}(\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot k)\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot k-j)+\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot k)\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot j))\hat{f}(j)\hat{f}(k-j),

so we just need prove that

(-i)^{2}\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot j)\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot k-j)-1=(-i)^{2}(\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot k)\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot k-j)+\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot k)\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot j)),

in other words,

-\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot j)\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot k-j)-1=-(\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot k)\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot k-j)+\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot k)\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot j)),

which is the same as

(31)

\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot k-j)[(\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot k))-\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot j)]=1-\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot j)\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot k).

When both
\alpha\cdot k
and
\alpha\cdot j
are of the same sign, both sides of (
31
) evaluate to 0.
When
\alpha\cdot k
and
\alpha\cdot j
are of opposite signs, both sides of (
31
) evaluate to 2.
Thus, (
31
) holds.
∎

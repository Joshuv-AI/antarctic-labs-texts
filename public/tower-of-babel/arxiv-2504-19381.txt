The m m th Gini index estimator: Unbiasedness for gamma populations
Roberto Vila 1 and Helton Saulo 1,2 1 Department of Statistics, University of Brasilia, Brasilia, Brazil 2 Department of Economics, Federal University of Pelotas, Pelotas, Brazil

Abstract
This paper establishes the theoretical result that the sample m m th Gini index is an unbiased estimator of the population m m th Gini index, introduced by Gavilan-Ruiz et al., 2024 , for gamma-distributed populations. An illustrative Monte Carlo simulation study confirms the unbiasedness of the sample m m th Gini index estimator in finite samples.



Keywords.
Gamma distribution, exponential distribution,
m
th Gini index, unbiased estimator.

Introduction

Measurement of inequality plays a fundamental role in several areas of knowledge, with the classical Gini index being the most famous; see
Gini, 1936
. This index, however, has an important limitation, as two distributions that behave differently in terms of their concentration can exhibit the same Gini index value. Recently,
Gavilan-Ruiz et al., 2024
proposed a generalization of the classical Gini index to overcome this drawback by considering the difference between the expected maximum and minimum values of random samples of size
m
. This novel formulation preserves the intuitive attractiveness of the Gini index while allowing for a finer discrimination between distributions that might have identical Gini values but different internal structures of inequality.

In this paper, we prove the unbiasedness of the sample
m
th Gini index estimator when the underlying population follows a gamma distribution. The
m
th Gini index, recently introduced by
Gavilan-Ruiz et al., 2024
, generalizes the classical Gini coefficient by considering the expected range between the maximum and minimum of
m
independent observations. Our main result extends and generalizes the important finding of
Baydil et al., 2025
, who established that the sample Gini coefficient is an unbiased estimator of the population Gini coefficient for a population having Gamma
(\alpha,\beta)
distribution. Since the classical Gini coefficient corresponds to the case
m=2
of the
m
th Gini index, our results not only recover the unbiasedness of the standard Gini coefficient as a particular case but also demonstrate that this property holds more broadly for any
m\geq 2
. The revelance of the results of this paper is to offer new theoretical insights into the behavior of inequality measures based on gamma populations and to strengthen the foundation for using
m
th Gini indices in practical applications.

The rest of this paper unfolds as follows. In Section
2
, we define the
m
th Gini index and derive its expression for gamma-distributed populations. In Section
3
, we establish the unbiasedness of the sample
m
th Gini index estimator and provide a closed-form expression for its expectation. In Section
4
, we present an illustrative Monte Carlo simulation study to corroborate our theoretical findings. Finally, in Section
5
, we provide concluding remarks.

2
The
m
th Gini index for gamma distributions

Let
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{m}
be independent and identically distributed (iid) random variables with the same distribution as a non-negative random
X
with mean
\mu=\mathbb{E}(X)>0
. Throughout this work we will assume that
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{m}
and
X
are defined in the same sample space
\Omega
. Following Definition 1 of
Gavilan-Ruiz et al., 2024
, for each integer
m\geqslant 2
, the
m
th Gini index of
X
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle IG_{m}\equiv IG_{m}(X)={\mathbb{E}\left[\max\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\}-\min\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\}\right]\over m\mu}.

(1)

Remark 2.1
.

Note that when
m=2
the
m
th Gini index coincides with the classical definition of the Gini coefficient
(
Gini, 1936
)
, denoted by
G
. That is,

\displaystyle G\equiv IG_{2}={\mathbb{E}[\,|X_{1}-X_{2}|\,]\over 2\mu}.

It is simple to observe that

\displaystyle\max\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{[t,\infty)}(\max\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\}(\omega)){\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[1-\mathds{1}_{\bigcap_{k=1}^{m}X_{k}^{-1}((-\infty,t])}(\omega)\right]{\rm d}t,\quad\forall\omega\in\Omega,

(2)

where
\mathds{1}_{A}
is the indicator function of the set
A
and
X_{k}^{-1}(B)=\{\omega\in\Omega:X_{k}(\omega)\in B\}
is the inverse image of the Borel set
B
through
X_{k}
. Similarly,

\displaystyle\min\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\}(\omega)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{\bigcap_{k=1}^{m}X_{k}^{-1}([t,\infty))}(\omega){\rm d}t,\quad\forall\omega\in\Omega.

(3)

Hence, by (
2
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[1-\mathbb{P}\left({\bigcap_{k=1}^{m}X_{k}^{-1}((-\infty,t])}\right)\right]{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}[1-\{\mathbb{P}\left(X^{-1}((-\infty,t])\right)\}^{m}]{\rm d}t=\int_{0}^{\infty}[1-F^{m}_{X}(t)]{\rm d}t,

where the fact that
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{m}
are iid with the same distribution as
X
was utilized. Analogously, by (
3
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\min\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\}\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}[1-F_{X}(t)]^{m}{\rm d}t.

Since
\mu=\mathbb{E}\left[X\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}[1-F_{X}(t)]{\rm d}t
, the index
IG_{m}
is characterized as follows:

\displaystyle IG_{m}=\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}[1-F^{m}_{X}(t)]{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\infty}[1-F_{X}(t)]^{m}{\rm d}t}{\displaystyle m\int_{0}^{\infty}[1-F_{X}(t)]{\rm d}t}.

(4)

For reader convenience, in what follows we apply Equation (
4
) to derive simple expressions for the
m
th index for the exponential model separately, despite it being a special case of gamma.

Proposition 2.2
.

The
m
th Gini index for
X\sim\exp(\lambda)
(exponential distribution) is given by

\displaystyle IG_{m}={1\over m}\left[{\sum_{k=1}^{m}\binom{m}{k}{(-1)^{k+1}\over k}-{1\over m}}\right].

(5)

Proof.

If
X\sim\exp(\lambda)
, then
\int_{0}^{\infty}[1-F_{X}(t)]{\rm d}t=\mathbb{E}[X]=1/\lambda
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}[1-F_{X}(t)]^{m}{\rm d}t=\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-m\lambda t){\rm d}t=1/(m\lambda)
, and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}[1-F^{m}_{X}(t)]{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}[1-\{1-\exp(-\lambda t)\}^{m}]{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\binom{m}{k}(-1)^{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-k\lambda t){\rm d}t={1\over\lambda}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\binom{m}{k}{(-1)^{k+1}\over k},

where Newton’s binomial Theorem was applied.
Given the above identities and the characterization of
IG_{m}
in (
4
), the result is immediate.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

By taking
m=2
in Proposition
2.2
, we get

G=IG_{2}={1/2}
,
for exponential distributions, consistent with prior research
(
Deltas, 2003
)
.

Proposition 2.4
.

The
m
th Gini index for
X\sim\text{Gamma}(\alpha,\lambda)
(gamma distribution) is given by

\displaystyle IG_{m}={1\over m\alpha\Gamma^{m}(\alpha)}\left[{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\{\Gamma^{m}(\alpha)-\gamma^{m}(\alpha,s)\}{\rm d}s-\int_{0}^{\infty}\Gamma^{m}(\alpha,s){\rm d}s}\right],

(6)

where
\Gamma(a)
is the (complete) gamma function,
\gamma(a,b)
is the lower incomplete gamma function and
\Gamma(a,b)
is the upper incomplete gamma function.

Proof.

If
X\sim\text{Gamma}(\alpha,\lambda)
, then
\int_{0}^{\infty}[1-F_{X}(t)]{\rm d}t=\mathbb{E}[X]=\alpha/\lambda
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}[1-F_{X}(t)]^{m}{\rm d}t={1\over\Gamma^{m}(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Gamma^{m}(\alpha,\lambda t){\rm d}t,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}[1-F^{m}_{X}(t)]{\rm d}t={1\over\Gamma^{m}(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}[\Gamma^{m}(\alpha)-\gamma^{m}(\alpha,\lambda t)]{\rm d}t.

Consequently, by using (
4
) and the the change of variable
s=\lambda t
yield the desired result.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

By applying the well-known identity
\gamma(a,b)+\Gamma(a,b)=\Gamma(a)
and Newton’s binomial Theorem, it follows that

\displaystyle\Gamma^{m}(\alpha)-\gamma^{m}(\alpha,s)=\Gamma^{m}(\alpha)-[\Gamma(\alpha)-\Gamma(\alpha,s)]^{m}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\binom{m}{k}(-1)^{k+1}\Gamma^{m-k}(\alpha)\Gamma^{k}(\alpha,s).

Consequently,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}[\Gamma^{m}(\alpha)-\gamma^{m}(\alpha,s)]{\rm d}s=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\binom{m}{k}(-1)^{k+1}\Gamma^{m-k}(\alpha)\int_{0}^{\infty}\Gamma^{k}(\alpha,s){\rm d}s.

On the other hand, by using the definition

\Gamma(a,x)=\int_{x}^{\infty}t^{a-1}\exp(-t){\rm d}t
of the upper incomplete gamma function, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\Gamma^{k}(\alpha,s){\rm d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}_{+}}\mathds{1}_{\{s\leqslant\min\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}\}\}}(t_{1}\cdots t_{k})^{\alpha-1}\exp\{-(t_{1}+\cdots+t_{k})\}{\rm d}\bm{t}{\rm d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}_{+}}(t_{1}\cdots t_{k})^{\alpha-1}\exp\{-(t_{1}+\cdots+t_{k})\}\int_{0}^{\min\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}\}}1{\rm d}s{\rm d}\bm{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}_{+}}\min\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}\}(t_{1}\cdots t_{k})^{\alpha-1}\exp\{-(t_{1}+\cdots+t_{k})\}{\rm d}\bm{t},

(7)

where Tonelli’s Theorem validates the interchange of integrals.

Thus,
\int_{0}^{\infty}[\Gamma^{m}(\alpha)-\gamma^{m}(\alpha,s)]{\rm d}s
and
\int_{0}^{\infty}\Gamma^{m}(\alpha,s){\rm d}s
in Proposition
2.4
can be written as

\int_{0}^{\infty}[\Gamma^{m}(\alpha)-\gamma^{m}(\alpha,s)]{\rm d}s\\[5.69046pt]
=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\binom{m}{k}(-1)^{k+1}\Gamma^{m-k}(\alpha)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}_{+}}\min\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}\}(t_{1}\cdots t_{k})^{\alpha-1}\exp\{-(t_{1}+\cdots+t_{k})\}{\rm d}\bm{t}

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\Gamma^{m}(\alpha,s){\rm d}s=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}}\min\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}\}(t_{1}\cdots t_{m})^{\alpha-1}\exp\{-(t_{1}+\cdots+t_{m})\}{\rm d}\bm{t},

respectively.

Remark 2.6
.

Taking
k=1
in (
2.5
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\Gamma(\alpha,s){\rm d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}t_{1}^{\alpha}\exp(-t_{1}){\rm d}t_{1}=\Gamma(\alpha+1)=\alpha\Gamma(\alpha),

where in the last line the well-kwnon identity
\Gamma(x+1)=x\Gamma(x)
was used.

Setting
k=2
in (
2.5
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\Gamma^{2}(\alpha,s){\rm d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\min\{t_{1},t_{2}\}(t_{1}t_{2})^{\alpha-1}\exp\{-(t_{1}+t_{2})\}{\rm d}t_{1}{\rm d}t_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mskip-3.0mut_{2}^{\alpha-1}\exp(-t_{2})\int_{0}^{t_{2}}\mskip-3.0mut_{1}^{\alpha}\exp(-t_{1}){\rm d}t_{1}{\rm d}t_{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\mskip-3.0mut_{2}^{\alpha}\exp(-t_{2})\int_{t_{2}}^{\infty}\mskip-3.0mut_{1}^{\alpha-1}\exp(-t_{1}){\rm d}t_{1}{\rm d}t_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}t_{2}^{\alpha-1}\exp(-t_{2})\gamma(\alpha+1,t_{2}){\rm d}t_{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}t_{2}^{\alpha}\exp(-t_{2})[\Gamma(\alpha)-\gamma(\alpha,t_{2})]{\rm d}t_{2}.

(8)

By using the identity
(
D’Aurizio, 2016
)
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{a-1}\exp(-sx)\gamma(b,\theta x){\rm d}x={\theta^{b}\Gamma(a+b)\over b(s+\theta)^{a+b}}\,_{2}F_{1}\left(a+b,1;b+1;{\theta\over s+\theta}\right),

the identity in (
2.6
) becomes

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\Gamma^{2}(\alpha,s){\rm d}s

\displaystyle=\alpha\Gamma^{2}(\alpha)+{\Gamma(2\alpha+1)\over(\alpha+1)2^{2\alpha+1}}\,_{2}F_{1}\left(2\alpha+1,1;\alpha+2;{1\over 2}\right)

\displaystyle-{\Gamma(2\alpha+1)\over\alpha 2^{2\alpha+1}}\,_{2}F_{1}\left(2\alpha+1,1;\alpha+1;{1\over 2}\right).

(9)

By applying the identity
(
Wolfram Research, 2024
)
:

\,{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a,b;{a+b\over 2}+1;{1\over 2}\right)={2\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma({a+b\over 2}+1)\over a-b}\left[{1\over\Gamma({a\over 2})\Gamma({b+1\over 2})}-{1\over\Gamma({b\over 2})\Gamma({a+1\over 2})}\right],

we obtain

\,{}_{2}F_{1}\left(2\alpha+1,1;\alpha+2;{1\over 2}\right)={\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\alpha+2)\over\alpha}\left[{1\over\Gamma({2\alpha+1\over 2})}-{1\over\Gamma({1\over 2})\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\right].

(10)

Furthermore, by using the identity
(
Wolfram Research, 2024
)
:

\,{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a,b;{a+b\over 2};{1\over 2}\right)=\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma\left({a+b\over 2}\right)\left[{1\over\Gamma({a+1\over 2})\Gamma({b\over 2})}+{1\over\Gamma({a\over 2})\Gamma({b+1\over 2})}\right],

we can write

\,{}_{2}F_{1}\left(2\alpha+1,1;\alpha+1;{1\over 2}\right)=\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma\left(\alpha+1\right)\left[{1\over\Gamma(\alpha+1)\Gamma({1\over 2})}+{1\over\Gamma({2\alpha+1\over 2})}\right].

(11)

Plugging (
10
) and (
11
) into (
2.6
) gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\Gamma^{2}(\alpha,s){\rm d}s

\displaystyle=\alpha\Gamma^{2}(\alpha)-{\Gamma(2\alpha+1)\over 2^{2\alpha}\alpha}.

(12)

Thus, the integral
\int_{0}^{\infty}[\Gamma^{2}(\alpha)-\gamma^{2}(\alpha,s)]{\rm d}s
in Proposition
2.4
becomes

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}[\Gamma^{2}(\alpha)-\gamma^{2}(\alpha,s)]{\rm d}s=2\Gamma(\alpha)\int_{0}^{\infty}\Gamma(\alpha,s){\rm d}s-\int_{0}^{\infty}\Gamma^{2}(\alpha,s){\rm d}s=\alpha\Gamma^{2}(\alpha)+{\Gamma(2\alpha+1)\over 2^{2\alpha}\alpha}.

(13)

Substituting (
12
) and (
13
) into Proposition
2.4
yields

\displaystyle IG_{2}={1\over 2\alpha\Gamma^{2}(\alpha)}\left[{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\{\Gamma^{2}(\alpha)-\gamma^{2}(\alpha,s)\}{\rm d}s-\int_{0}^{\infty}\Gamma^{2}(\alpha,s){\rm d}s}\right]={\Gamma(2\alpha+1)\over 2^{2\alpha}\alpha^{2}\Gamma^{2}(\alpha)}.

Finally, by applying Legendre duplication formula
(
Abramowitz and Stegun, 1972
)
:

\Gamma(x)\Gamma\left(x+{1/2}\right)=2^{1-2x}\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(2x),
in the above identity,
we get the following expression for the
2
th Gini index:

\displaystyle G=IG_{2}={1\over 2\alpha\Gamma^{2}(\alpha)}\left[{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\{\Gamma^{2}(\alpha)-\gamma^{2}(\alpha,s)\}{\rm d}s-\int_{0}^{\infty}\Gamma^{2}(\alpha,s){\rm d}s}\right]={\Gamma(\alpha+{1\over 2})\over\sqrt{\pi}\alpha\Gamma(\alpha)},

(14)

which corroborates the Gini coefficient formula for gamma distributions reported in
McDonald and Jensen, 1979
.

3
Unbiasedness of the
m
th Gini index estimator

In this section (more precisely, in Theorem
3.2
), we provide a general formula
for the expectation of the
m
th Gini index estimator, newly defined in this work, as follows (for
m\leqslant n
)

\displaystyle\widehat{IG}_{m}={(m-1)!\over(n-1)(n-2)\cdots(n-m+1)}\,\dfrac{\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leqslant n}\left[\max\{X_{i_{1}},\ldots,X_{i_{m}}\}-\min\{X_{i_{1}},\ldots,X_{i_{m}}\}\right]}{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}},

(15)

where
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{m}
are iid observations from the population
X
.

Remark 3.1
.

Setting
m=2
in (
15
), we get the estimator of the Gini coefficient, denoted by
\widehat{G}
,

\displaystyle\widehat{G}\equiv\widehat{IG}_{2}={1\over n-1}\,\dfrac{\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}|X_{i}-X_{j}|}{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}},

which initially was proposed by
Deltas, 2003
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{m}
be independent copies of a non-negative and absolutely continuous random variable
X
with finite and positive expected value and common cumulative distribution function
F
. The following holds:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\widehat{IG}_{m}]

\displaystyle={n\over m}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\{\mathscr{L}_{F}^{m}(z)-\mathbb{E}^{m}\left[\mathds{1}_{X^{-1}((-\infty,t])}\exp\left(-Xz\right)\right]\right\}{\rm d}t\,\mathscr{L}_{F}^{n-m}(z){\rm d}z

\displaystyle-{n\over m}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}^{m}\left[\mathds{1}_{X^{-1}([t,\infty))}\exp\left(-Xz\right)\right]{\rm d}t\,\mathscr{L}_{F}^{n-m}(z){\rm d}z,

where
\mathscr{L}_{F}(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-zx){\rm d}F(x)
is the Laplace transform corresponding to distribution
F
. In the
above, we are assuming that the expectations and improper integrals involved exist.

Proof.

By using the identity

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-\xi z){\rm d}z={1\over\xi},\quad\xi>0,

with
\xi=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
, we get

\mathbb{E}\left[\dfrac{\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n}\leqslant n}\left[\max\{X_{i_{1}},\ldots,X_{i_{m}}\}-\min\{X_{i_{1}},\ldots,X_{i_{m}}\}\right]}{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}}\right]\\[5.69046pt]
=\sum_{1\leqslant i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leqslant n}\mathbb{E}\left[\max\{X_{i_{1}},\ldots,X_{i_{m}}\}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left\{-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right)z\right\}{\rm d}z\right]\\[5.69046pt]
-\sum_{1\leqslant i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leqslant n}\mathbb{E}\left[\min\{X_{i_{1}},\ldots,X_{i_{m}}\}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left\{-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right)z\right\}{\rm d}z\right].

By applying identities in (
2
) and (
3
), the above expression becomes

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leqslant i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leqslant n}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\{1-\mathds{1}_{\bigcap_{k=1}^{m}X_{i_{k}}^{-1}((-\infty,t])}\right\}{\rm d}t\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left\{-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right)z\right\}{\rm d}z\right]

\displaystyle-\sum_{1\leqslant i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leqslant n}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{\bigcap_{k=1}^{m}X_{i_{k}}^{-1}([t,\infty))}{\rm d}t\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left\{-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right)z\right\}{\rm d}z\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leqslant i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leqslant n}\mskip-3.0mu\int_{0}^{\infty}\mskip-3.0mu\int_{0}^{\infty}\mskip-3.0mu\mskip-3.0mu\mathbb{E}\left[\mskip-3.0mu\left\{1-\mathds{1}_{\bigcap_{k=1}^{m}X_{i_{k}}^{-1}((-\infty,t])}\right\}\mskip-3.0mu\exp\left\{\mskip-3.0mu-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}X_{i}\right)z\right\}\exp\left\{\mskip-3.0mu-\left(\sum_{i=m+1}^{n}X_{i}\right)\mskip-3.0muz\right\}\right]\mskip-3.0mu{\rm d}t{\rm d}z

\displaystyle-\sum_{1\leqslant i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leqslant n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\bigcap_{k=1}^{m}X_{i_{k}}^{-1}([t,\infty))}\exp\left\{-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}X_{i}\right)z\right\}\exp\left\{-\left(\sum_{i=m+1}^{n}X_{i}\right)z\right\}\right]{\rm d}t{\rm d}z,

where the interchange of integrals is justified by Tonelli’s Theorem. Since
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{m}
are iid, the last expression simplifies to

\displaystyle=\binom{n}{m}\mskip-3.0mu\int_{0}^{\infty}\mskip-3.0mu\mskip-3.0mu\int_{0}^{\infty}\mskip-3.0mu\mathbb{E}\left[\mskip-3.0mu\left\{1-\mathds{1}_{\bigcap_{k=1}^{m}X_{k}^{-1}((-\infty,t])}\right\}\mskip-3.0mu\exp\left\{-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}X_{i}\right)z\right\}\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left\{-\left(\sum_{i=m+1}^{n}X_{i}\right)z\right\}\right]\right]{\rm d}t{\rm d}z

\displaystyle-\binom{n}{m}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\bigcap_{k=1}^{m}X_{k}^{-1}([t,\infty))}\exp\left\{-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}X_{i}\right)z\right\}\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left\{-\left(\sum_{i=m+1}^{n}X_{i}\right)z\right\}\right]\right]{\rm d}t{\rm d}z

\displaystyle=\binom{n}{m}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\{1-\mathds{1}_{\bigcap_{k=1}^{m}X_{k}^{-1}((-\infty,t])}\right\}\exp\left\{-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}X_{i}\right)z\right\}\right]{\rm d}t\,\mathscr{L}_{F}^{n-m}(z){\rm d}z

\displaystyle-\binom{n}{m}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\bigcap_{k=1}^{m}X_{k}^{-1}([t,\infty))}\exp\left\{-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}X_{i}\right)z\right\}\right]{\rm d}t\,\mathscr{L}_{F}^{n-m}(z){\rm d}z

\displaystyle=\binom{n}{m}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\{\mathscr{L}_{F}^{m}(z)-\mathbb{E}^{m}\left[\mathds{1}_{X^{-1}((-\infty,t])}\exp\left(-Xz\right)\right]\right\}{\rm d}t\,\mathscr{L}_{F}^{n-m}(z){\rm d}z

\displaystyle-\binom{n}{m}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}^{m}\left[\mathds{1}_{X^{-1}([t,\infty))}\exp\left(-Xz\right)\right]{\rm d}t\,\mathscr{L}_{F}^{n-m}(z){\rm d}z,

where, again, the iid assumption for
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{m}
was used. Finally, by using the definition of the
m
th Gini estimator, given in (
15
), the proof of theorem follows.
∎

In the remainder of this section we apply Theorem
3.2
to derive explicit expressions for the expectation of estimator
\widehat{IG}_{m}
in exponential and gamma populations, demonstrating the estimator’s unbiasedness in both cases. Although the exponential case is a special case of gamma, we present both for clarity.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{m}
be independent copies of
X\sim\exp(\lambda)
. We have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\widehat{IG}_{m}]={1\over m}\,\left[\sum_{k=1}^{m}\binom{m}{k}{(-1)^{k+1}\over k}-{1\over m}\right]=IG_{m},

where
IG_{m}
is the
m
th Gini index given in (
5
). Hence, the estimator
\widehat{IG}_{m}
is unbiased under exponential populations.

Proof.

Assuming
X\sim\exp(\lambda)
, simple calculation yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{X^{-1}((-\infty,t])}\exp\left(-Xz\right)\right]={\lambda\over z+\lambda}\,[1-\exp\left\{-(z+\lambda)t\right\}]

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{X^{-1}([t,\infty))}\exp\left(-Xz\right)\right]={\lambda\over z+\lambda}\,\exp\left\{-(z+\lambda)t\right\}.

As
\mathscr{L}_{F}(z)=\lambda/(z+\lambda)
, applying Theorem
3.2
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\widehat{IG}_{m}]

\displaystyle={n\lambda^{n}\over m}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\{1-\left[1-\exp\left\{-(z+\lambda)t\right\}\right]^{m}\right\}{\rm d}t\,{1\over(z+\lambda)^{n}}{\rm d}z

\displaystyle-{n\lambda^{n}\over m}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left\{-m(z+\lambda)t\right\}{\rm d}t\,{1\over(z+\lambda)^{n}}{\rm d}z.

(16)

Using Newton’s binomial Theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\{1-\left[1-\exp\left\{-(z+\lambda)t\right\}\right]^{m}\right\}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\binom{m}{k}(-1)^{k+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left\{-k(z+\lambda)t\right\}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle={1\over z+\lambda}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\binom{m}{k}{(-1)^{k+1}\over k}.

Plugging the above identity into (
3
) gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\widehat{IG}_{m}]

\displaystyle={n\lambda^{n}\over m}\,\sum_{k=1}^{m}\binom{m}{k}{(-1)^{k+1}\over k}\int_{0}^{\infty}{1\over(z+\lambda)^{n+1}}{\rm d}z-{n\lambda^{n}\over m}\,{1\over m}\int_{0}^{\infty}{1\over(z+\lambda)^{n+1}}{\rm d}z

\displaystyle={1\over m}\,\left[\sum_{k=1}^{m}\binom{m}{k}{(-1)^{k+1}\over k}-{1\over m}\right]={IG}_{m},

(17)

where the last equality follows from Equation (
5
).
This completes the proof.
∎

m=2
in Corollary
3.3
yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\widehat{G}]={1\over 2}=G,

for exponential distributions,
which is a well-established result in the literature

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{m}
be independent copies of
X\sim\text{Gamma}(\alpha,\lambda)
. We have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\widehat{IG}_{m}]={1\over m\alpha\Gamma^{m}(\alpha)}\left[{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\{\Gamma^{m}(\alpha)-\gamma^{m}(\alpha,s)\}{\rm d}s-\int_{0}^{\infty}\Gamma^{m}(\alpha,s){\rm d}s}\right]=IG_{m},

where
IG_{m}
is the
m
th Gini index given in Proposition
2.4
. Therefore, the estimator
\widehat{IG}_{m}
is unbiased for gamma populations.

Proof.

When
X\sim\text{Gamma}(\alpha,\lambda)
, direct computation shows

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{X^{-1}((-\infty,t])}\exp\left(-Xz\right)\right]={\lambda^{\alpha}\over(z+\lambda)^{\alpha}\Gamma(\alpha)}\,\gamma(\alpha,(z+\lambda)t)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{X^{-1}([t,\infty))}\exp\left(-Xz\right)\right]={\lambda^{\alpha}\over(z+\lambda)^{\alpha}\Gamma(\alpha)}\,\Gamma(\alpha,(z+\lambda)t).

Given
\mathscr{L}_{F}(z)=\lambda^{\alpha}/(z+\lambda)^{\alpha}
, Theorem
3.2
yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\widehat{IG}_{m}]

\displaystyle={n\over m}\,{\lambda^{\alpha n}\over\Gamma^{m}(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\{\Gamma^{m}(\alpha)-\gamma^{m}(\alpha,(z+\lambda)t)\right\}{\rm d}t\,{1\over(z+\lambda)^{\alpha n}}{\rm d}z

\displaystyle-{n\over m}\,{\lambda^{\alpha n}\over\Gamma^{m}(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Gamma^{m}(\alpha,(z+\lambda)t){\rm d}t\,{1\over(z+\lambda)^{\alpha n}}{\rm d}z.

Now, making the change of variable
u=(z+\lambda)t
, the above identity can be written as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\widehat{IG}_{m}]

\displaystyle={n\over m}\,{\lambda^{\alpha n}\over\Gamma^{m}(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\{\Gamma^{m}(\alpha)-\gamma^{m}(\alpha,u)\right\}{\rm d}u\,{1\over(z+\lambda)^{\alpha n+1}}{\rm d}z

\displaystyle-{n\over m}\,{\lambda^{\alpha n}\over\Gamma^{m}(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Gamma^{m}(\alpha,u){\rm d}u\,{1\over(z+\lambda)^{\alpha n+1}}{\rm d}z

\displaystyle={1\over m\alpha\Gamma^{m}(\alpha)}\left[{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\{\Gamma^{m}(\alpha)-\gamma^{m}(\alpha,u)\}{\rm d}u-\int_{0}^{\infty}\Gamma^{m}(\alpha,u){\rm d}u}\right]=IG_{m},

where the last equality follows from Proposition
2.4
.
Thus, the proof is complete.
∎

Concluding remarks

We have studied the theoretical properties of the
m
th Gini index and its sample estimator
\widehat{IG}_{m}
, which generalizes the classical Gini index by considering differences between the maximum and minimum values of random samples of size
m
. We have shown that
\widehat{IG}_{m}
is an unbiased estimator of the corresponding population parameter
IG_{m}
under gamma-distributed populations. Our results have extended and generalized the findings of
Baydil et al., 2025
, who investigated the unbiasedness of the traditional Gini index estimator for the population Gini coefficient in gamma distributions. We have validated our theoretical results through an illustrative Monte Carlo simulation study. The simulation confirmed the unbiasedness of the estimator
\widehat{IG}_{m}
in finite samples, under both exponential and gamma settings.

Gamma exponentiated generalized family of distributions with properties and applications
Authors: Etaf Alshawarbeh, Boikanyo Makubate, Subhankar Dutta, Regent Retrospect Musekwa
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis manuscript introduces the Gamma Exponentiated Generalized-G (GEG-G) family of distributions, developed by integrating the gamma distribution with the exponentiated generalized (EG) family. The resulting class offers enhanced flexibility and can accommodate a broad spectrum of distributional behaviors. Several special cases within the GEG-G family are presented to illustrate its versatility. Parameter estimation is carried out using maximum likelihood point estimation techniques, and the accuracy and efficiency of these estimators are evaluated through a comprehensive Monte Carlo simulation study. To demonstrate practical applicability, a specific member of the GEG-G family is applied to real-world lifetime count datasets.Keywords: Mixture distributions, gamma generalized exponentiated-G, Moments, point estimation method, Monte CarloSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Generating families of continuous distributions involves developing mathematical techniques and methodologies for creating new probability distributions or deriving distributions with specific properties from existing ones. These techniques are essential in statistical theory, applied mathematics, and various fields where probability distributions play a crucial role. Techniques for generating families of continuous distributions encompass a wide range of mathematical methodologies, including transformation methods (see1), mixture distributions (see2–4), generalized distributions (see5–16), truncation and conditioning (see17), and copula functions (see18,19), to mention a few. Further one may follow20–25 and26 for modeling new distributions. These approaches allow for the creation of flexible models that can better capture the characteristics of real-world data, especially in cases where traditional distributions fail to provide a good fit.

Applications for the GEG-G family of distributions are numerous and include actuarial science, reliability engineering, medical research, finance, environmental modeling, and queueing theory. The GEG-G is especially useful in scenarios where conventional models are unable to adequately represent the complexity of the data due to its capacity to account for various hazard function forms, skewness, and heavy tails. Consequently, the GEG-G family offers a more precise and perceptive method for simulating real-world occurrences in a variety of fields.

The exponential, Weibull, and gamma distributions are among the well-known probability distributions that are frequently used in the literature today to represent real-world data in disciplines including environmental science, survival analysis, finance, and reliability engineering. These distributions have proven effective in managing datasets with comparatively straightforward properties, such symmetric data and monotonic hazard rates. But when it comes to accurately simulating complex real-world events that display more intricate behaviors, there are still a lot of restrictions. Analysis of fracture toughness of Alumina data

Here, we examine a dataset characterized by a symmetric trend, as discussed in the studies by32, and33. This dataset comprises measurements of the fracture toughness of Alumina (), a ceramic material widely used in engineering applications due to its high strength and thermal stability. Fracture toughness shows how well a material can withstand the spread of cracks under stress. The values in this dataset are measured in megapascals multiplied by the square root of meters (MPa), which is the standard unit for expressing fracture toughness in materials science.

The gauge lengths of 20 mm data

In this section, we examine a dataset that was first presented by34 and has a trend that is rather left-skewed. The dataset comprises measurements related to gauge lengths, specifically for specimens with a standard length of 20 millimeters (mm).Gauge length, which usually refers to the area of a specimen over which deformation is measured during tensile or mechanical stress tests, is a fundamental measurement in materials testing. This dataset’s values are expressed in millimeters (mm).

Conclusions

In this study, a new family approach has been developed by utilizing the gamma function and an exponentiated generalized family of distributions. The newly introduced generator is named the gamma exponentiated generalized (GEG-G) family of probability distributions. Further we have generated some of the distributions of this family and their statistical properties. A Monte Carlo simulation study has been employed to estimate the parameters of GEG-exponential distribution. From the simulation study, it has been observed that as the sample size increases, both the mean squared error and biases decrease. Finally two real life data sets have been analyzed to show the applicability of the proposed family of distributions. Also, it shows an evidence of fitting better than other existing distributions based on AIC, AICC, BIC, Cramer-von Mises statistic, Anderson-Darling statistic, Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistics and the corresponding p-values as the model selection criteria. We anticipate the proposed novel family of probability distributions and its unique members are going to be useful to a variety of fields, such as medical studies, survival analysis, and reliability engineering. They could additionally operate as a source of ideas for future model development.

Incidence rate and geographic distribution of congenital hypothyroidism in the southwest of Iran (Kohgiluyeh and Boyer Ahmad province) based on geographic information system since 2011–2020
Authors: Mohammad Amin Ghatee, Leila Manzouri, Maryam Kheiri, Mohamad Parad
Published: 2024

Background

Congenital hypothyroidism (CH) is one of the most prevalent preventable cause of mental retardation and intellectual disability in newborns [1, 2]. Other complications of CH are motor disorder, imbalance, failure to thrive, learning difficulties, permanent short stature and deafness [1, 2], which can be prevented by early diagnosis and starting treatment before the 6th week of life [1, 3]. CH is defined as the lack of thyroid hormones at birth and is divided into transient and permanent type [2, 4]. Screening programs were been developed in the early 1970 [5], and the studies of screening have been started in North American since 1972 [1]. In Iran, for the first time, screening program was been carried out by Azizi et al., in 1987 [1] and has been integrated into National Public Health System since 2005 in neonate within 3–5 days of birth until now [1, 4, 5]. The estimated incidence of CH is around 1 in 3000–4000 live births worldwide, but varies in different regions, such as 1:4509 in USA, 1:3000 in Europe [3], and in some countries of EMRO (Eastern Meditteranean Region) is 1:781 in Pakistan [5], , 0.29:1000 in Kuwait, 0.33:1000 in Oman [6], and 1-2.7 per 1000 live births in Iran [1, 9], that is more prevalent and higher in comparison to other parts of word [1, 3]. Also, the incidence of CH varies in different parts of Iran such as 1:914 in Tehran [5], 1:338 to 1:357 in Isfahan [3, 5], 1:625 in Mazandaran [3], 1:1433 in Fars, 1:1608 in Yazd, 1:400 in Kurdistan, 1:542 in Guilan provinces [3], 3.3 and 2.61 per 1000 live births in Markazi and Ilam provinces, 1:3000 live births in Kohgiluyeh and Boyer-Ahmad province since 2006–2009 [7], 2.68, 2.9,2 and 3 per 1000 live births in Kerman, Ahvaz, Shiraz and Zahedan cities, respectively [8] and 1:715 in Hormozgan province [9].

Various factors have been indicated to be related to congenital hypothyroidism which include genetic factors, family history of thyroid disease, consanguineous, gender, Asian race, prematurity, multiple births, old age of the mother, food consumption, goitrogenic medicine, and immune system deficiency [10].

Environmental factors are among other factors related to CH. In the world, various studies have investigated the relationship between seasonal changes and the incidence of CH, in which it has been shown that the incidence of the CH is higher in autumn and winter than in spring and summer, but the same results are not estimated in these studies [10]. Among other environmental factors that have been investigated in relation to congenital hypothyroidism are the average annual temperature, relative humidity and average annual precipitation [10]. In Deliri et al.‘s study in Ilam province, a statistically significant relationship was observed between the average annual rainfall and vegetation cover with congenital hypothyroidism, but no significant relationship was observed between the average annual temperature, sunlight and relative humidity with the disease [10]. In the study by Khanjani et al. in Isfahan province, no significant relationship between these factors was observed with congenital hypothyroidism [11]. In the study by Mehranjat et al., a statistically significant relationship between the amount of nitrates in water and suffering from hypothyroidism was pointed out [12]. Valamparampil and his colleagues revealed the occurrence of statistically significant clustering of hypothyroid individuals in areas having acidic ground water [13].

Geographical Information Systems (GIS) are one of the most important and useful soft wares in the study of epidemiology, distribution, dissemination, identification of geographical areas and high risk population groups. In fact, cluster of diseases in a specific place and the spatial analysis of diseases and its relationship with environmental factors can be studied in the GIS [10]. On the other hand, awareness of the incidence and geographical distribution and high risk areas of diseases is essential in etiology, planning, management and decision making.

CH Screening Program has been started in Kohgiluyeh and Boyer Ahmad Province at the same time as the whole country from the beginning of July 2014 [10] to identify and treat the patients on time to prevent mental retardation. Many efforts have been made to improve the impact of environmental factors, including controlling iodine consumption by households and reducing the incidence of the disease, too [10].

Several studies [10] have investigated different prognostic factors for CH, indicating the influence of several genetic and environmental factors on the incidence and distribution of this disease. However, some questions remain unanswered until now. No study has been conducted in Kohgiluyeh and Boyer Ahmad province to describe the geographic variation and spatial distribution of CH. Hence, this study was conducted to illustrate the incidence and spatial distribution of the CH using the dataset from the national newborn CH screening information system during 2011–2020 in counties affiliated to the Kohgiluyeh and Boyer Ahmad University of Medical Sciences.

Methods

This study was a cross-sectional (descriptive analytical) study that was approved by the ethic committee of the Yasuj University of Medical Sciences (IR.YUMS.REC.1401.010) All newborns that were screened for CH in 3–5 days of age in the health centers of Yasuj University of Medical Sciences were enrolled the study since 2011–2020. Informed consent to participate was obtained from the parents or legal guardians of any participant.

Sampling of infants’ heels were performed on 903 S&S filter paper and sent to the provincial health center along with no.1 screening form. TSH levels were measured by ELISA antibody kits. Infants with TSH more than 5 were recalled and venous samples were taken from them for T4, T3RU and TSH measurement. According to the national guidelines, infants with TSH more than 10 mlU/L and T4 less than 6.5 µg/dL were considered as patients [10, 14]. Incidence of CH was calculated based on formula: number of newborns with CH per 1000 live births in per year.

Meteorological data including information on temperature, humidity, precipitation in a 10-year period (2011–2020) was taken from the Meteorological Organization. The required information layers include slope, slope direction, height, vegetation and political divisions of the province, some of them were prepared from the relevant organizations such as the governorate and the natural resources organization, or were purchased from the organizations that made the layers. The rest of the layers were made based on the available raw data from satellite images or weather data collection centers. The data related to the incidence of congenital hypothyroidism was collected from the 10-year reports of the provincial health center.

Addresses of patients were transferred to Arc GIS 10.5 software and entered in urban and rural point’s layer to prepare and draw disease distribution maps in the province’s newborns. Collecting the layers of geographic information required includes information on altitude, area coverage, temperature and rainfall in the cities of the province, some of which were prepared from the relevant organizations such as the governorate, the natural resources organization, and the meteorological department of the province, or from organizations Engineered layers were purchased.

The land cover layer was purchased and entered into the Arc GIS 10.5 software along with the previously purchased layers such as elevation layers, city and provincial divisions.

Meteorological information including temperature, rain and humidity information for the studied years was prepared from the Meteorological Department of the province. With the help of interpolation models and after determining the accuracy of the model, maps of isothermal, isohumidity and isorainfall were made.

Analysis was done by interacting weather and environment layers for places with and without patients through the commands of the Arc tool box.

The influence of geographical factors on the occurrence of disease was done by SPSS 23 software using multivariate binary logistic regression. P-value < 0.05 was assumed as significant level.

Discussion

The present study aimed to describe the incidence rate and geographical distribution of CH in Kohgiluyeh and Boyar Ahmad province (Southwest of Iran since 2010–2019. The incidence of CH was 5.8 per 1000 live births (1/173). Regarding the incidence in this study, the rate in Kohgiluyeh and Boyar Ahmad province is about 6–23 times greater than the countries with screening program (1/1000- 1/4000) [10]. Also, it is higher in comparison to other developing countries such as Turkey, Kuwait, Oman, Pakistan and Saudi Arabia (less than 1/1000: 0.29–0.67 per 1000 live births) and developed countries (France, China, Greece: 0.49–1.25 per 1000 live births), too [10]. In Iran, the incidence rate of CH is estimated 2.2 per 1000 live births, too [10]. The estimated incidence rate of CH in different provinces and cities of Iran such as Markazi, Kerman, Khuzestan, Gilan, Kurdestan, Shiraz, Tehran, Zahedan [10], Mazandaran [4] and Hormozgan [9] was 3.3, 2.68, 2.9, 1.6, 3.4, 2.2, 3, 0.62 and 1.39 per 1000 live births, respectively that is very lower than our result. But, in Semnan, the estimated incidence rate of CH was 6.05/1000 live births [1] that is nearly similar to the result of current study. The higher incidence rate of CH in Iranian population could be partially explained by genetic and hereditary factors, increased coverage and effectiveness of the screening program over the time, different cut off for diagnosis of hypothyroidism leads to CH diagnosis with higher sensitivity. On the other hand, the higher incidence of CH in this study in comparison to other parts of Iran, could be related to genetic, ethnicity and hereditary factors, coverage of screening program in different provinces and various stages of program implementation such as sampling, sending sample, laboratory analysis, reporting and confirmation of diagnosis that needs to quality control [6]. Consanguineous marriage in Kohgiluyeh and Boyar Ahmad province, may be related to high incidence of CH in this province, too [5]. Given that, the present study was a retrospective research and collected data focus on environmental and geographic factors, a precise explanation for this variation might not be possible; however, it can be largely explained by the increased coverage and effectiveness in the screening program over time.

In the present study, out of 827 infants suffering from CH, the boy to girl ratio was 1.2. In other parts of Iran: Hormozgan, East Azarbaijan [9], Semnan [1], Ahvaz [2], Ilam, Kordestan, South Khorasan, Mazandaran [10], boy to girl ratio was 1.03 to 1.4 that is similarly we found.

The incidence rate of CH varied in different counties of the province similar to the results of Assadollahi in Ilam [10], Siami in Mazandaran [15], Namakian in South Khorasan [16], Nili in Kurdestan [17], Mahaki in Isfahan [5]. The highest incidence rates of CH pertained to Gachsaran, Bahmei, Boyer-Ahmad, Lendeh, Kohgiluyeh, Dena, Choram and Basht, respectively. This difference may be due to differences in the weather, living condition or different levels of iodine in geographical areas.

Based on multivariable logistic regression, land slope, thin forest and water area were found to be a significant predictor of CH. For every one degree increase in land slope, the chance of CH occurrence decreased by 8.5%. The land cover of thin forest reduced the chance of disease occurrence by 80% and the Land cover of the type of canals and rivers increased the chance of disease by 9 times. These results have been revealed from other studies such as Daliri and his colleagues in Ilam [10], Denga et al. in China [18] and Delshad in Iran [19]. Floods and rivers reduce the iodine in the soil and increase the chance of developing congenital hypothyroidism. With the increase of the slope of the land, the chances of soil erosion and the reduction of soil surface iodine increase; but, due to the fact that in Kohgiluyeh and Boyer Ahmad provinces, in the region with a high slope, in order to prevent injuries caused by heavy rains, the vegetation is richer, by preventing soil erosion, the chance of congenital hypothyroidism is reduced. On the other hand, due to the effect of soil PH on the mobility and absorption of iodine in the plant and the alkalinity of the province’s soils due to the presence of high calcium and lime, the absorbable form of iodine in the plant is reduced and the conditions are provided for the enrichment of iodine in underground water [10]. For this reason, in low-density forest areas, due to less organic matter in the soil, the soil has become more alkaline, which leads to the enrichment of iodine in the underground water and reduces the chance of developing congenital hypothyroidism. Considering that the province of Kohgiluyeh and Boyer Ahmad is located in a mountainous region and has a lot of rainfall, it will lead to soil erosion and iodine depletion, which has led to a high incidence of congenital hypothyroidism in the province. Although other factors such as race, family history and nutritional status also play a role in congenital hypothyroidism, determining the effect of these factors requires additional studies.

Despite the fact that the salts offered in stores are labeled as enriched with iodine, Sometimes up to 30% of the supplied salts either lacked iodine or did not have enough iodine. The results of some studies have shown that the iodine content of 59% of the salt samples with the previous standard of the Ministry of Health (20–50 micrograms per gram) and the iodine content of none of the samples did not comply with the new standard of the Ministry of Health (30–60 micrograms/gram). On the other hand, 50% of people did not know how to properly store iodized salts, and only 8% used a glass container with a dark color, which is a suitable container for storing and preserving iodized salt. Only 37% of households kept salt in a place away from heat, light and humidity at home. 52% of households did not pay attention to the expiration date of iodized salt during package consumption. Also, only 11% of households added salt at the end of cooking and when they removed the food from the flame [20]. Therefore, educating people about the correct way to store and consume iodized salt and continuous monitoring of iodine in the salts distributed in the market is the most important part of monitoring the program to eliminate disorders caused by iodine deficiency and is a necessary and urgent matter. Therefore, the lack of continuous monitoring of intake and consumption of oral iodine is one of the most important reasons for the failure of the salt iodization program in the population.

Strengths

This was the first study conducted to assess the congenital hypothyroidism and it’s relation with geographic and climate conditions based on GIS in Kohgiluyeh and Boyar Ahmad province, which is one of the provinces with high incidence of congenital hypothyroidism.

Conclusion

According to the findings of the present study, Kohgiluyeh and Boyar Ahmad provinces are among the regions with high incidence of congenital hypothyroidism in the country, and the geographical factors of land slope and type of vegetation were related to the incidence of congenital hypothyroidism, which due to the impossibility of creating Changes in geographical factors, it is necessary to carry out necessary nutritional interventions during pregnancy to prevent newborns from developing this disease.

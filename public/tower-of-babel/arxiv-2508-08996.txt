Unconditional results for Artin-type problems over number fields
Pietro Sgobba

Abstract
. Let K K be a number field and let G G be a finitely generated subgroup of K × K^{\times} . For all but finitely many primes 𝔭 \mathfrak{p} of K K , the reduction ( G mod 𝔭 ) (G\bmod\mathfrak{p}) generates a well-defined subgroup of the multiplicative group of the residue field at 𝔭 \mathfrak{p} , and we may consider its index. We study the primes of K K for which this index lies in a given set of positive integers S S . In particular, we prove that under certain convergence conditions on series associated to S S this problem can be addressed without assuming the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH), and we provide asymptotic formulas for the corresponding prime-counting functions. Problems of this type are related to Artin’s primitive root conjecture, which has been proven under the assumption of GRH (Hooley, 1967).

1.
Introduction

Let
K
be a number field, and let
G
be a finitely generated and torsion-free subgroup of
K^{\times}
. Up to discarding finitely many primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
K
, the reduction
(G\bmod\mathfrak{p})
is the subgroup of
k_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}
, where
k_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the residue field at
\mathfrak{p}
, generated by the reductions
(\alpha\bmod\mathfrak{p})
for
\alpha\in G
. The multiplicative index of this subgroup, denoted by
\ind_{\mathfrak{p}}(G)
, is well-defined and we may consider the index map

\Psi:\mathfrak{p}\longmapsto\Psi(\mathfrak{p}):=\ind_{\mathfrak{p}}(G).

Given a set
S
of positive integers, we investigate the natural density of the preimage
\Psi^{-1}(S)
, if it exists. Under the assumption of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH), it is known that given a positive integer
t
, the density of the set of primes of
K
given by
\Psi^{-1}(t)
exists and has an explicit formula, see
[
17
, Proposition 1]
, namely

\dens(\Psi^{-1}(t))=\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{\mu(n)}{[K(\zeta_{nt},G^{1/nt}):K]},

where
\zeta_{n}
denotes a primitive
n
th root of unity and
\mu
is the Möbius function.
Also, we consider all extensions of
K
in a fixed algebraic closure.
Furthermore, again under GRH, the set
\Psi^{-1}(S)
admits a natural density given by the sum of the densities
\dens(\Psi^{-1}(t))
for
t\in S
, i.e.

\dens(\Psi^{-1}(S))=\sum_{t\in S}\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{\mu(n)}{[K(\zeta_{nt},G^{1/nt}):K]},

see
[
10
, Corollary 2]
. Taking
t=1
, the set
\Psi^{-1}(1)
consists of all primes
\mathfrak{p}
such that
G
contains a primitive root modulo
\mathfrak{p}
, and taking
K=\mathbb{Q}
and
G
of rank
1
reduces to the problem of Artin’s primitive root conjecture, which was proven under GRH by Hooley
[
6
]
in 1967, and its analogue over number fields by Cooke and Weinberger
[
2
]
; see
[
9
]
for an introduction to the research area around this conjecture. The map
\Psi
was studied in more generality, under GRH, in two recent articles by Järviniemi and Perucca
[
7
]
, and jointly with the author
[
8
]
.

Here we investigate the condition
\ind_{\mathfrak{p}}(G)\in S
without assuming GRH. Instead, we impose an asymptotic bound on a certain series associated with
S
, and then we focus on sets
S
defined in terms of the
\ell
-adic valuations of their elements, obtaining unconditional partial extensions of the results in
[
8
]
.

In fact, for some sets
S
it is well-known that the set of primes
\Psi^{-1}(S)
can be studied unconditionally. For instance, taking
S=n\mathbb{N}
for a given integer
n
, for a prime
\mathfrak{p}
of degree
1
the condition
n\mid\ind_{\mathfrak{p}}(G)
is equivalent to
\mathfrak{p}
splitting completely in
K(\zeta_{n},G^{1/n})
, and by Chebotarev’s density theorem the corresponding natural density is
1/[K(\zeta_{n},G^{1/n}):K]
. As a simple application, we may further obtain such examples if we only need to apply this result finitely many times, for instance for the index having a prescribed
\ell
-adic valuation for a fixed
\ell
, or for the index being coprime to a given integer.

In 1995, over the rationals Pappalardi proved that the primes
p
for which
\ind_{p}(2)
is
k
-free,
k\geqslant 2
, (i.e. not divisible by
k
th powers larger than
1
) admit a natural density unconditionally, see
[
11
, Corollary 5]
. Notice that differently from the previous examples, this one involves divisibility conditions on the index for infinitely many prime numbers.
In fact, Pappalardi obtained this result as an application of
Hooley’s method with weights

[
11
]
, which we introduce here in the context of number fields and with an additional condition on the Frobenius conjugacy classes.

1.1.
Problem setup

Let
F/K
be a finite Galois extension of
K
, and let
C
be a conjugacy-stable subset of
\Gal(F/K)
.
For
\mathfrak{p}
a prime of
K
which does not ramify in
F
, we denote by
(\mathfrak{p},F/K)
the conjugacy class of the Frobenius elements at the primes of
F
above
\mathfrak{p}
.
For a given function
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
, we consider the sum

(1)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}:\ \N\mathfrak{p}\leqslant x\\
(\mathfrak{p},F/K)\subseteq C\end{subarray}}f(\Psi(\mathfrak{p})),

where
\N
is the norm over
K/\mathbb{Q}
and the sum runs through all primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
K
except the finitely many primes such that
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\alpha)\neq 0
for some
\alpha\in G
or which ramify in
F
(we will implicitly discard these primes whenever we consider a condition on
\ind_{\mathfrak{p}}(G)
or on
(\mathfrak{p},F/K)
, respectively).

Let
\pi_{n,C}(x)
be the number of primes
\mathfrak{p}
, with
\N\mathfrak{p}\leqslant x
, such that
n\mid\ind_{\mathfrak{p}}(G)
and
(\mathfrak{p},F/K)\subseteq C
, and write
\pi_{n}(x)
if the Frobenius condition is trivial.
Hooley’s conditional proof of Artin’s conjecture is based on the inclusion-exclusion identity

(2)

\sharp\{\mathfrak{p}:\N\mathfrak{p}\leqslant x,\ind_{\mathfrak{p}}(G)=1\}=\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\mu(n)\pi_{n}(x).

By the Möbius inversion formula we may write

f(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}g(d),\quad\text{ with }\quad g(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)f(d).

Then, the sum (
1
) equals

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}:\ \N\mathfrak{p}\leqslant x\\
(\mathfrak{p},F/K)\subseteq C\end{subarray}}\sum_{d\mid\Psi(\mathfrak{p})}g(d)=\sum_{d\leqslant x}g(d)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}:\ \N\mathfrak{p}\leqslant x\\
d\mid\Psi(\mathfrak{p})\\
(\mathfrak{p},F/K)\subseteq C\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{d\leqslant x}g(d)\pi_{d,C}(x),

yielding the identity

(3)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}:\ \N\mathfrak{p}\leqslant x\\
(\mathfrak{p},F/K)\subseteq C\end{subarray}}f(\Psi(\mathfrak{p}))=\sum_{n\geqslant 1}g(n)\pi_{n,C}(x),

which is a generalization of (
2
); see also
[
11
, p.376]
and
[
5
, Sec.1.3]
.

In view of (
3
) and since
\pi_{n,C}(x)
is given asymptotically by Chebotarev’s density theorem, we investigate under which conditions we may establish the asymptotic formula

(4)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}:\ \N\mathfrak{p}\leqslant x\\
(\mathfrak{p},F/K)\subseteq C\end{subarray}}f(\Psi(\mathfrak{p}))\sim\Li(x)\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{g(n)c(n)}{[F(\zeta_{n},G^{1/n}):K]},

where
c(n)
is the number of
K
-automorphisms in
C
which fix
F\cap K(\zeta_{n},G^{1/n})
(this coefficient arises when applying Chebotarev’s density theorem; we define it formally in Sec.
2.2
), and
where we assumed that the series multiplying
\Li(x)
is convergent. Asymptotic formulas of this form were studied by Felix and Ram Murty
[
5
]
over
\mathbb{Q}
for several arithmetic functions
f
under GRH, and later by Felix
[
3
]
for higher rank. Notice that the constants arising from (
4
) have been recently studied by Akbary and Fakhari
[
1
]
.

If
\chi_{S}
denotes the characteristic function of
S
, then taking
f=\chi_{S}
the above sum is the counting function for the set of primes
\mathfrak{p}
such that
\ind_{\mathfrak{p}}(G)\in S
and
(\mathfrak{p},F/K)\subseteq C
. Namely, we have

(5)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}:\ \N\mathfrak{p}\leqslant x\\
(\mathfrak{p},F/K)\subseteq C\end{subarray}}\chi_{S}(\Psi(\mathfrak{p}))=\sharp\left\{\mathfrak{p}:\N\mathfrak{p}\leqslant x,\ \ind_{\mathfrak{p}}(G)\in S,\ \begin{pmatrix}\mathfrak{p}\\
F/K\end{pmatrix}\subseteq C\right\}.

If (
4
) holds for
f=\chi_{S}
, from (
5
) we would then obtain an asymptotic formula and a density formula for the considered counting function.
Under GRH this was achieved by Felix and Ram Murty
[
5
]
over
\mathbb{Q}
, and Felix
[
4
]
expressed the obtained density and studied its positivity.

Unconditionally, Pappalardi proved that over
\mathbb{Q}
the asymptotic (
4
) can be obtained if the series
\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\left\lvert g(n)\right\rvert/\varphi(n)
converges (where
\varphi
is Euler’s totient function), and
\sum_{n>z}\left\lvert g(n)\right\rvert/n=o(1/\log z)
, see
[
11
, Theorem 1]
. These conditions are indeed satisfied for
\chi_{S}
taking
S
the set of
k
-free integers.

1.2.
Outline

Our first main achievement is Theorem
8
, which is a generalization and a variation of this result of Pappalardi. It states that if
\sum_{n>z}\left\lvert g(n)\right\rvert/\varphi(n)\ll z^{-\kappa}
for some
\kappa>0
, then (
4
) holds, and it provides an asymptotic formula based on a recent version of Chebotarev’s density theorem for cyclotomic–Kummer extensions of number fields
[
15
, Theorem 10]
, also stated here as Theorem
1
.

In Theorem
13
we first apply the obtained result to the case of
k
-free indices, and more generally
k(\ell)
-free indices (i.e. not divisible by
\ell^{k(\ell)}
for all
\ell
), with
k\geqslant 2
and
k(\ell)\geqslant 2
for all
\ell
. This illustrates the idea that the series
\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\left\lvert g(n)\right\rvert/\varphi(n)
would converge if
g
satisfies certain properties implied by the splitting conditions on the primes. For instance, for the index to be squarefree, the primes
\mathfrak{p}
must not split completely in any extension
K(\zeta_{\ell^{2}},G^{1/\ell^{2}})
for all
\ell
. This would then push
g(n)
to be nonzero if and only if
v_{\ell}(n)\in\{0,2\}
for all
\ell
, where
v_{\ell}
is the
\ell
-adic valuation, resulting in the above series being convergent.

We then apply Theorem
8
to study the condition
\ind_{\mathfrak{p}}(G)\in H
for more general sets
H
whose elements are defined through their
\ell
-adic valuations, for all prime numbers
\ell
. More precisely, we focus on sets
H
such that
H=\cap_{\ell}H_{\ell}
, where
H_{\ell}
is the preimage under
v_{\ell}
of
v_{\ell}(H)
.
We call this type of sets
cut by valuations
. In this case, we assume that
1\in H
and that for each
\ell
we have at least
1\in v_{\ell}(H)
in Theorem
15
(with some other assumptions), and
1,2\in v_{\ell}(H)
in Theorem
17
. In both cases we establish, in particular, the identity

\dens(\Psi^{-1}(H))=\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{g_{H}(n)}{[K(\zeta_{n},G^{1/n}):K]},

where
g_{H}(n)=\sum_{d\mid n,d\in H}\mu(n/d)
is a multiplicative function.

We then consider slightly more general sets
H
by allowing finitely many valuation conditions not to be independent, which we call
almost cut by valuations
.
We first study sets
H
such that
H=v_{Q}^{-1}(v_{Q}(H))
, for some fixed
Q\geqslant 1
, where
v_{Q}(n)
is the tuple
(v_{\ell}(n))_{\ell\mid Q}
, see Theorem
19
. In other words, elements
n
of such sets
H
only have to satisfy properties on
\gcd(n,Q^{\infty})
, where
Q^{\infty}
is the supernatural number
\prod_{p\mid Q}p^{\infty}
(
p
denotes a prime). The special case where
Q
is a prime number is treated as an illustrative result in Proposition
3
.
The general case for sets almost cut by valuations is achieved in Theorem
24
. The case
1\notin H
for sets cut by valuations is thus treated in Remark
27
as an application of this result.

Finally, in Section
5
we express the densities for which the convergence condition of Theorem
8
is satisfied first as limits and then in Euler product form. In particular, for the latter, in Theorem
29
and Proposition
30
, for
H
almost cut by valuations, we prove the formula
\dens(\Psi^{-1}(H))=D_{K,G,H}\cdot A_{H,r}
, where
A_{H,r}
is an Artin-type constant, with
r
the rank of
G
, e.g. if
1\in H
we have

A_{H,r}=\prod_{\ell}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{(\ell-1)\ell^{r}}+\frac{\ell^{r+1}-1}{(\ell-1)\ell^{r}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\geqslant 1\\
v\in v_{\ell}(H)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell^{v(r+1)}}\bigg),

and
D_{K,G,H}
is interpreted as a correction factor resulting from the entanglement between cyclotomic fields and the Kummer extensions of
K
related to
G
.

Notice that the above results are already known under the assumption of GRH, in more generality, see
[
7
,
8
]
. The novelty of this work consists in dealing unconditionally with this general problem under specific convergence conditions associated to the considered set of integers.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

Throughout this paper we let
F/K
be a Galois extension of number fields,
G
a finitely generated torsion-free subgroup of
K^{\times}
of positive rank
r
, and
C
a conjugacy-stable subset of
\Gal(F/K)
. We also keep all notation from the Introduction.

Given
S
a set of positive integers, we define

\mathcal{P}_{S,C}:=\left\{\mathfrak{p}:\ind_{\mathfrak{p}}(G)\in S,\ \begin{pmatrix}\mathfrak{p}\\
F/K\end{pmatrix}\subseteq C\right\},

and we write
\mathcal{P}_{S}
if the Frobenius condition is trivial, i.e. if
C=\Gal(F/K)
.
For a set
\mathcal{P}
of primes of
K
, we write

\pi_{\mathcal{P}}(x):=\sharp\{\mathfrak{p}:\N\mathfrak{p}\leqslant x,\ \mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}\}

for the prime-counting function associated to
\mathcal{P}
, and
\dens(\mathcal{P})
(which we already used in the Introduction) for its natural density, if it exists. According to the notation introduced above, we have
\pi_{n,C}(x)=\pi_{\mathcal{P}_{n\mathbb{N},C}}(x)
. We denote by
\pi_{F/K,C}(x)
the function counting the primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
K
with norm up to
x
, which are unramified in
F
and with Frobenius conjugacy class at
\mathfrak{p}
lying in
C
.
We write
\pi_{F/K}(x):=\pi_{F/K,\{\id\}}(x)
, which counts the primes splitting completely in
F
.
For integers
m,n\geqslant 1
with
n\mid m
, we set
K_{m,n}:=K(\zeta_{m},G^{1/n})
.

Also, we make use of the following notation:
(m,n)
is the g.c.d. of
m,n
;

\Li(x)
is the logarithmic integral function; given
n\geqslant 1
, we write
\rad(n)
for the radical of
n
, i.e. the largest squarefree integer dividing
n
, and
\omega(n)
for the prime omega function, i.e. the number of distinct prime factors of
n
.

2.2.
On Chebotarev’s density theorem

Here we state an effective and unconditional version of Chebotarev’s density theorem for a general cyclotomic–Kummer extension, which was obtained as an application of a recent work of Thorner and Zaman
[
16
]
.

Theorem 1

(
[
15
, Theorem 10]
)
.

For all integers
m,n\geqslant 1
with
n\mid m
, define

C_{m,n}:=\{\sigma\in\Gal(F_{m,n}/K):\sigma|_{F}\in C,\,\sigma|_{K_{m,n}}=\id\}\,,

which is a conjugacy-stable subset of
\Gal(F_{m,n}/K)
. There exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
, which depend only on
F
and
G
, such that, for all
b>0
and uniformly for

m\leqslant c_{1}\left(\frac{\log x}{(\log\log x)^{b}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r+2}}\,,

we have

\pi_{F_{m,n}/K,C_{m,n}}(x)=\frac{\sharp C_{m,n}}{[F_{m,n}:K]}\Li(x)+O_{F,G}\left(\frac{x}{\log x}\cdot e^{-c_{2}(\log\log x)^{b}}\right).

Taking
F=K
yields an asymptotic for the function
\pi_{K_{m,n}/K}(x)
counting the primes splitting completely in
K_{m,n}
. As mentioned in the Introduction we have
\pi_{n}(x)=\pi_{K_{n,n}/K}(x)
, and more generally
\pi_{n,C}(x)=\pi_{F_{n,n}/K,C_{n,n}}(x)
. Also, in the latter case we have
\pi_{n,C}(x)\sim\frac{c(n)}{[F_{n,n}:K]}\Li(x)
, where

c(n):=\sharp C_{n,n}=\sharp\,C\cap\Gal(F/F\cap K_{n,n})\leqslant[F:F\cap K_{n,n}]\leqslant[F:K].

Notice that, since
[F:F\cap K_{n,n}]=[F_{n,n}:K_{n,n}]
, we have

(6)

\frac{c(n)}{[F_{n,n}:K]}\leqslant\frac{1}{[K_{n,n}:K]},

with equality holding if
C\supseteq\Gal(F/F\cap K_{n,n})
.

Proposition 2

(
[
15
, Proposition 11]
)
.

With the notation of Theorem
1
, there exists a constant
c_{3}>0
(depending only on
F
and
G
), such that, for all
b>0
and uniformly for

m\leqslant c_{3}\left(\frac{\log x}{(\log\log x)^{1+b}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r+1}},

we have

\pi_{F_{m,n}/K,C_{m,n}}(x)\leqslant(2+o(1))\frac{\sharp C_{m,n}}{[F_{m,n}:K]}\Li(x).

2.3.
Prescribing
\ell
-adic valuations for a single prime
\ell

The following result can be intended as a warm-up problem illustrating how Theorem
1
can be used to study Artin-type problems involving divisibility conditions for finitely many distinct prime factors of the index.

Proposition 3
.

Let
\ell
be a prime number and let
V\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
be a set of integers. The number of primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
K
, with norm up to
x
, such that
v_{\ell}(\ind_{\mathfrak{p}}(G))\in V
and
(\mathfrak{p},F/K)\subseteq C
is given by

\Li(x)\sum_{v\in V}\left(\frac{c(\ell^{v})}{[F_{\ell^{v},\ell^{v}}:K]}-\frac{c(\ell^{v+1})}{[F_{\ell^{v+1},\ell^{v+1}}:K]}\right)+O_{F,K,G}\left(x\left(\frac{(\log\log x)^{2}}{\log x}\right)^{1+\frac{1}{r+2}}\right).

For trivial Frobenius condition,
each term in the sum over
v\in V
corresponds to the density of the primes
\mathfrak{p}
splitting completely in
K_{\ell^{v},\ell^{v}}
but not in
K_{\ell^{v+1},\ell^{v+1}}
, and, equivalently, of the primes
\mathfrak{p}
such that
v_{\ell}(\ind_{\mathfrak{p}}(G))=v
. Intuitively, this result does not need the assumption of GRH because the conditions on the index only concern a single prime
\ell
and the splitting conditions involve number fields of degrees growing fast enough.

Proof.

For
v\geqslant 0
, we set
\mathcal{S}_{v}:=\{\mathfrak{p}:v_{\ell}(\Psi(\mathfrak{p}))=v,(\mathfrak{p},F/K)\subseteq C\}
, and
\mathcal{S}:=\bigsqcup_{v\in V}\mathcal{S}_{v}
, i.e. the disjoint union of the sets
\mathcal{S}_{v}
with
v\in V
.
On the other hand, fixing
w>0
, all primes
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{S}_{v}
with
v\geqslant w
must split completely in
K_{\ell^{w},\ell^{w}}
. Hence, we obtain

\pi_{\mathcal{S}}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in V\\
v\leqslant w\end{subarray}}\pi_{\mathcal{S}_{v}}(x)+O\big(\pi_{K_{\ell^{w},\ell^{w}}/K}(x)\big).

We set
y:=c_{1}(\frac{\log x}{(\log\log x)^{2}})^{\frac{1}{r+2}}
(from Theorem
1
) and
w:=\lfloor\frac{\log y}{\log\ell}\rfloor-1
, so that for all
v\leqslant w
we have
\ell^{v+1}\leqslant y
.
By Proposition
2
we can bound the error term by

\frac{1}{[K_{\ell^{w},\ell^{w}}:K]}\frac{x}{\log x}\ll_{K,G}\frac{1}{\varphi(\ell^{w})\ell^{rw}}\frac{x}{\log x}\ll\frac{1}{\ell^{(r+1)w}}\frac{x}{\log x}\leqslant\frac{x}{y\log x},

where we applied Proposition
4
(a), and we used that
\ell/(\ell-1)\leqslant 2
for all
\ell
, and
w
is such that
\ell^{(r+1)w}\geqslant y\geqslant\ell^{w}
.

As for the main term, for
v\leqslant w
we may apply Theorem
1
and obtain

\Li(x)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in V\\
\ell^{v+1}\leqslant y\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{c(\ell^{v})}{[F_{\ell^{v},\ell^{v}}:K]}-\frac{c(\ell^{v+1})}{[F_{\ell^{v+1},\ell^{v+1}}:K]}\right)+O_{F,G}\Bigg(\frac{xy}{\log x\cdot e^{c_{2}(\log\log x)^{2}}}\Bigg).

The error term is bounded by
\frac{x}{y\log x}
.
For the tail corresponding to the obtained series, recalling (
6
), we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\geqslant 0\\
\ell^{v}>y\end{subarray}}\frac{c(\ell^{v})}{[F_{\ell^{v},\ell^{v}}:K]}\ll_{K,G}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\geqslant w\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\varphi(\ell^{v})\ell^{vr}}\ll\frac{1}{\ell^{(r+1)w}}\sum_{v\geqslant 0}\frac{1}{\ell^{v}}\ll\frac{1}{y}.

Finally, all error terms are estimated by
\frac{x}{y\log x}
, which is bounded by the error term in the statement.
∎

2.4.
Further preliminaries

Let us first state some results from Kummer theory.

Proposition 4
.

There is an integer
B
(depending only on
F
,

and

), such that:

(3)

[

;

[

.

Next we state some analytic results which we will use in our proofs.

Lemma 5
.

For any
\alpha\geqslant 1
we have

\sum_{n>z}\frac{1}{\varphi(n)n^{\alpha}}=O\bigg(\frac{1}{z^{\alpha}}\bigg)

Proof.

The case
\alpha=1
is
[
17
, Lemma 7]
. The case
\alpha>1
is a simple consequence:

\sum_{n>z}\frac{1}{\varphi(n)n^{\alpha}}\leqslant\frac{1}{z^{\alpha-1}}\sum_{n>z}\frac{1}{\varphi(n)n}\ll\frac{1}{z^{\alpha}}.\qed

Lemma 6
.

Let
m\geqslant 1
be a squarefree integer, let
0<\rho<1
, and
T\geqslant 1
. We have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\mid m^{\infty}\\
a>T\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{a}\ll_{\rho}\frac{m^{1-\rho}}{T^{\rho}}.

Proof.

We have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\mid m^{\infty}\\
a>T\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{a}=m\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\mid m^{\infty}\\
am>Tm\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{am}\ll_{\rho}\frac{m^{1-\rho}}{T^{\rho}},

where we applied
[
12
, Lemma 3.3]
.
∎

Occasionally we will also use, without mentioning explicitly, that for all
n
sufficiently large we have

\frac{n}{\varphi(n)}=\frac{\rad(n)}{\varphi(\rad(n))}\ll\log\log n.

3.
The main result

Let
f,g
be as in the Introduction, and let us recall that we aim to set convergence conditions associated to
g
which allow us to establish the asymptotic

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}:\ \N\mathfrak{p}\leqslant x\\
(\mathfrak{p},F/K)\subseteq C\end{subarray}}f(\Psi(\mathfrak{p}))\sim\Li(x)\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{g(n)c(n)}{[F_{n,n}:K]},

where we assume that

(7)

\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{\left\lvert g(n)\right\rvert}{[K_{n,n}:K]}<\infty.

Notice that this assumption is equivalent to each of the following assertions:

(1)

\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{|g(n)|}{\varphi(n)n^{r}}<\infty
;

(2)

\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{\left\lvert g(n)\right\rvert c(n)}{[F_{n,n}:K]}<\infty
.

The equivalence with (i) follows from Proposition
4
(a), whereas for (ii), the sufficiency follows from (
6
), and the necessity by multiplying each term in the sum (
7
) by
\frac{[F:K]c(n)}{[F:F\cap K_{n,n}]}\geqslant 1
.

Lemma 7
.

Suppose that
g(n)\geqslant 0
. We have

\sum_{n\geqslant 1}g(n)\pi_{n,C}(x)\geqslant\bigg(\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{g(n)c(n)}{[F_{n,n}:K]}-o(1)\bigg)\Li(x).

Proof.

Let
y:=c_{1}\big(\frac{\log x}{(\log\log x)^{b}}\big)^{\frac{1}{r+2}}
with
b>1
. Applying Theorem
1
for
n\leqslant y
and Proposition
4
(a), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geqslant 1}g(n)\pi_{n,C}(x)

\displaystyle\geqslant\sum_{n\leqslant y}g(n)\pi_{n,C}(x)

\displaystyle=\Li(x)\bigg(\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{g(n)c(n)}{[F_{n,n}:K]}-O_{F,K,G}\bigg(\sum_{n>y}\frac{g(n)}{\varphi(n)n^{r}}+\frac{y^{3}}{e^{c_{2}(\log\log x)^{b}}}\bigg)\bigg).

The two terms in the error term are
o(1)
because of the assumption (
7
). Indeed, for the second term, it follows from (
7
) that
g(n)\ll n^{2}
, and for the limit we also recall that
b>1
.
∎

Theorem 8
.

Suppose that the function
g
satisfies the estimate

(8)

\sum_{n>z}\frac{\left\lvert g(n)\right\rvert}{\varphi(n)}\ll\frac{1}{z^{\kappa}}

for some
0<\kappa\leqslant 2
.
Then

\sum_{n\geqslant 1}g(n)\pi_{n,C}(x)=\Li(x)\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{g(n)c(n)}{[F_{n,n}:K]}+O\left(x\left(\frac{(\log\log x)^{2}}{\log x}\right)^{1+\frac{3\kappa}{2(r+2)}}\right).

The constant implied by the error term depends only on
F,K,G,\kappa
and possibly any other parameters on which the constant implied by (
8
) might depend.

Proof.

Let us set

y:=c_{1}\left(\frac{\log x}{(\log\log x)^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r+2}},\quad w:=c_{3}\frac{\sqrt{\log x}}{\log\log x}.

As in the proof of Lemma
7
we have

(9)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geqslant 1}g(n)\pi_{n,C}(x)=

\displaystyle\Li(x)\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{g(n)c(n)}{[F_{n,n}:K]}+\sum_{n>y}g(n)\pi_{n,C}(x)

(10)

\displaystyle+\Li(x)\cdot O_{F,K,G}\bigg(\sum_{n>y}\frac{\left\lvert g(n)\right\rvert}{\varphi(n)n^{r}}+\frac{y^{3}}{e^{c_{2}(\log\log x)^{2}}}\bigg).

By the assumption, the first error term resulting from (
10
) is
O(\frac{x}{y^{1+\kappa}\log x})
,
and the second error term is negligible compared to this. The second term in (
9
) is bounded by

\sum_{n>y}\left\lvert g(n)\right\rvert\pi_{n}(x),

which we split over the three intervals determined by
y\leqslant w\leqslant\sqrt{x}\leqslant x
. On the interval
[y,w]
we make use of Proposition
2
with
b=r=1
taking into account that
\pi_{n}(x)\leqslant\pi_{K(\zeta_{n},\alpha^{1/n})/K}(x)
, where
\alpha\in G\setminus\{1\}
. We have

\sum_{y<n\leqslant w}\left\lvert g(n)\right\rvert\pi_{n}(x)\ll_{K,\alpha}\Li(x)\sum_{n>y}\frac{\left\lvert g(n)\right\rvert}{n\varphi(n)}\ll\frac{x}{y^{1+\kappa}\log x},

in view of our assumption. On the interval
[w,\sqrt{x}]
we make use of the Brun–Titchmarsh theorem, taking into account that
\pi_{n}(x)\ll_{K}\sharp\{p:p\equiv 1\bmod n\}
:

\sum_{w<n\leqslant\sqrt{x}}\left\lvert g(n)\right\rvert\pi_{n}(x)\ll_{K}\sum_{w<n\leqslant\sqrt{x}}\frac{\left\lvert g(n)\right\rvert x}{\varphi(n)\log(x/n)}\ll\frac{x}{\log x}\sum_{n>w}\frac{\left\lvert g(n)\right\rvert}{\varphi(n)}\ll\frac{x}{w^{\kappa}\log x}.

Finally, on the interval
[\sqrt{x},x]
we make use of the trivial estimate
\pi_{n}(x)\ll_{K}\sharp\{m:n\mid m-1\}
, yielding

\sum_{n>\sqrt{x}}\left\lvert g(n)\right\rvert\pi_{n}(x)\ll_{K}x\sum_{n>\sqrt{x}}\frac{\left\lvert g(n)\right\rvert}{n}\ll\frac{x}{x^{\kappa/2}}\ll_{\kappa,e}\frac{x}{y^{e}\log x},

for any
e>0
, where we used that
\varphi(n)\leqslant n
.

We want to choose a value for
e>0
in such a way that all terms are bounded by
\frac{x}{y^{e}\log x}
, allowing us to conclude.
Since
w=y^{\frac{r+2}{2}}
, we may bound
w^{-\kappa}\ll y^{-\frac{3}{2}\kappa}
.
As long as
\kappa\leqslant 2
we have
\kappa+1\geqslant\frac{3}{2}\kappa
, whence we may choose
e=\frac{3}{2}\kappa
. Notice that in all three bounds the implied constant may depend on
\kappa
or other parameters if this is the case in (
8
).
∎

Remark 9
.

Let
y,w
be as in the proof of Theorem
8
. It might be useful to point out that in order to reach the same conclusion of the theorem it suffices to have the following three estimates for
y,w
as above:

(11)

\sum_{n>y}\frac{\left\lvert g(n)\right\rvert}{\varphi(n)n}\ll\frac{1}{y^{3\kappa/2}},\qquad\sum_{n>w}\frac{\left\lvert g(n)\right\rvert}{\varphi(n)}\ll\frac{1}{y^{3\kappa/2}},\qquad\sum_{n>\sqrt{x}}\frac{\left\lvert g(n)\right\rvert}{n}\ll\frac{1}{y^{3\kappa/2}\log x}.

These might be less restrictive than requiring (
8
) to hold for all
z
large enough.

Moreover, if in Theorem
8
we replace the conditions (
8
) or (
11
) with the assumptions that the series
\sum_{n\geqslant 1}|g(n)|/\varphi(n)
converges and that
\sum_{n\geqslant z}|g(n)|/n=o(1/\log z)
, similarly as in
[
11
, Theorem 1(b)]
we may still deduce the asymptotic

\sum_{n\geqslant 1}g(n)\pi_{n,C}(x)\sim\Li(x)\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{g(n)c(n)}{[F_{n,n}:K]}.

Indeed, in this case we may bound the series
\sum_{n>y}\left\lvert g(n)\right\rvert\pi_{n}(x)
over the intervals determined by
y\leqslant\sqrt{x}\leqslant x
. Over
[y,\sqrt{x}]
, applying the Brun–Titchmarsh Theorem we have

\sum_{y<n\leqslant\sqrt{x}}\left\lvert g(n)\right\rvert\pi_{n}(x)\ll\frac{x}{\log x}\sum_{n>y}\frac{\left\lvert g(n)\right\rvert}{\varphi(n)}=o\Big(\frac{x}{\log x}\Big).

Over the interval
[\sqrt{x},x]
we have

\sum_{n>\sqrt{x}}\left\lvert g(n)\right\rvert\pi_{n}(x)\ll x\sum_{n>\sqrt{x}}\frac{\left\lvert g(n)\right\rvert}{n}=o\Big(\frac{x}{\log x}\Big).

4.
The index in a given set of integers

In this section, we apply Theorem
8
for
f=\chi_{S}
, where
S
is a set of positive integers and
\chi_{S}
is its characteristic function, so that, recalling the definition of the set
\mathcal{P}_{S,C}
from Sec.
2.1
and the discussion in Sec.
1.1
, we have

(12)

\pi_{\mathcal{P}_{S,C}}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}:\ \N\mathfrak{p}\leqslant x\\
(\mathfrak{p},F/K)\subseteq C\end{subarray}}\chi_{S}(\Psi(\mathfrak{p}))=\sum_{n\geqslant 1}g_{S}(n)\pi_{n,C}(x)

where

(13)

g_{S}(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)\chi_{S}(d)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid n\\
d\in S\end{subarray}}\mu(n/d).

Thus we want to establish conditions on the set
S
such that we have the asymptotic

\pi_{\mathcal{P}_{S,C}}(x)\sim\Li(x)\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{g_{S}(n)c(n)}{[F_{n,n}:K]}.

Remark 10
.

Let
S
be a set of positive integers such that the function
g_{S}
satisfies the convergence condition of Theorem
8
. Then, in view of (
12
), the above asymptotic formula holds, and the set of primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
K
such that
\ind_{\mathfrak{p}}(G)\in S
and
(\mathfrak{p},F/K)\subseteq C
has density

\dens(\mathcal{P}_{S,C})=\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{g_{S}(n)c(n)}{[F_{n,n}:K]}=\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid n\\
d\in S\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(n/d)c(n)}{[F_{n,n}:K]}.

4.1.
Sets which are cut or almost cut by valuations

In this section we consider sets of positive integers which are defined through conditions on the
\ell
-adic valuations of the elements, where
\ell
runs through all prime numbers.

For an integer
Q>1
we consider the
Q
-adic valuation
v_{Q}:\mathbb{N}\to\prod_{\ell\mid Q}\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
mapping
m
to the tuple
(v_{\ell}(m))_{\ell\mid Q}
.
Let
H
be a set of positive integers, and denote by
H_{Q}
the preimage under
v_{Q}
of
v_{Q}(H)
(we set
H_{1}:=\mathbb{N}
). We also set
V_{\ell}:=v_{\ell}(H)
for any prime
\ell
(notice that
0\in V_{\ell}
for all
\ell
, except finitely many).
We say that
H
is
cut by valuations
if
H=\bigcap_{\ell}H_{\ell}
, and
almost cut by valuations
if

(14)

H=H_{Q_{0}}\cap\bigcap_{\ell\nmid Q_{0}}H_{\ell}

for some
Q_{0}\geqslant 1
.
In particular, writing
n=ab
with
a\mid Q_{0}^{\infty}
and
(b,Q_{0})=1
, it follows from the definition that
n\in H
if and only if
a\in H_{Q_{0}}
and
\ell^{v_{\ell}(b)}\in H_{\ell}
for every
\ell\nmid Q_{0}
.
The above definitions were introduced in a more general context in
[
8
, Section 3.1]
.

Lemma 11
.

Let
H\subset\mathbb{N}
. If
H
is cut by valuations and
1\in H
, then the characteristic function
\chi_{H}
and the function
g_{H}
are both multiplicative. If
H
is almost cut by valuations with
Q_{0}\geqslant 1
as in (
14
), then, writing
n=ab
with
a\mid Q_{0}^{\infty}
and
(b,Q_{0})=1
, we have

(15)

\chi_{H}(n)=\chi_{H_{Q_{0}}}(n)\prod_{\ell\nmid Q_{0}}\chi_{H_{\ell}}(n)=\chi_{H_{Q_{0}}}(a)\prod_{\ell\nmid Q_{0}}\chi_{H_{\ell}}(\ell^{v_{\ell}(b)}),

(16)

g_{H}(n)=g_{H_{Q_{0}}}(a)\prod_{\ell\nmid Q_{0}}g_{H_{\ell}}(\ell^{v_{\ell}(b)}).

We have
\chi_{H_{\ell}}(1)=g_{H_{\ell}}(1)=1
for all
\ell\nmid Q_{0}
and such that
0\in V_{\ell}
.
Moreover,

•

if for all
\ell\nmid Q_{0}
we have
0\in V_{\ell}
, then for all
a\mid Q_{0}^{\infty}
we have
\chi_{H_{Q_{0}}}(a)=\chi_{H}(a)
and
g_{H_{Q_{0}}}(a)=g_{H}(a)
;

•

if
1\in H
, then for all
\ell\nmid Q_{0}
and
v\geqslant 0
we have
\chi_{H_{\ell}}(\ell^{v})=\chi_{H}(\ell^{v})
and
g_{H_{\ell}}(\ell^{v})=g_{H}(\ell^{v})
.

Proof.

Suppose that
H
is cut by valuations and
1\in H
. Clearly we have
\chi_{H}(1)=1
. Let
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
be coprime. Then
a,b\in H
is equivalent to
a,b\in H_{\ell}
for all
\ell
. Since, for all
\ell
, either
v_{\ell}(a)
or
v_{\ell}(b)
is
0
, the previous statement is equivalent to
v_{\ell}(ab)\in V_{\ell}
. In other words, we have
ab\in H_{\ell}
for all
\ell
, and hence
ab\in H
.
Since the Dirichlet convolution of two multiplicative functions is multiplicative, the function
g_{H}=\chi_{H}*\mu
is multiplicative.

Suppose that
H
is almost cut by valuations with
Q_{0}
as above. The first identity in (
15
) follow directly from (
14
), and the second identity follows from the subsequent remark.
For (
16
), by (
13
) and (
15
) we have

g_{H}(n)=\sum_{d_{1}\mid a}\mu\Big(\frac{a}{d_{1}}\Big)\chi_{H_{Q_{0}}}(d_{1})\cdot\prod_{\ell\nmid Q_{0}}\sum_{d_{\ell}\mid\ell^{v_{\ell}(b)}}\mu\Big(\frac{\ell^{v_{\ell}(b)}}{d_{\ell}}\Big)\chi_{H_{\ell}}(d_{\ell})=g_{H_{Q_{0}}}(a)\prod_{\ell\nmid Q_{0}}g_{H_{\ell}}(\ell^{v_{\ell}(b)}).

If for all
\ell\nmid Q_{0}
we have
0\in V_{\ell}
, then
v_{\ell}(a)=0\in V_{\ell}
for every
\ell\nmid Q_{0}
. If
1\in H
and
\ell\nmid Q_{0}
, then
v_{Q_{0}}(\ell^{v})=0\in v_{Q_{0}}(H)
and
v_{q}(\ell^{v})=0\in V_{q}
for all
q\nmid\ell Q_{0}
. The identities involving
g_{H}
then follow from (
16
) and the definition (
13
).
∎

Lemma 12
.

Let
H\subset\mathbb{N}
. We have
g_{H}(1)=\chi_{H}(1)
, and for all primes
\ell
and all
m\geqslant 1
, we have

(17)

g_{H_{\ell}}(\ell^{m})=\chi_{H_{\ell}}(\ell^{m})-\chi_{H_{\ell}}(\ell^{m-1})=\begin{cases}-1&\text{if }m\notin V_{\ell}\text{ and }m-1\in V_{\ell}\\
1&\text{if }m\in V_{\ell}\text{ and }m-1\notin V_{\ell}\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

If
H
is almost cut by valuations with
Q_{0}
as in (
14
) and
1\in H
, then for all primes
\ell\nmid Q_{0}
the identities (
17
) hold with
g_{H_{\ell}}
and
\chi_{H_{\ell}}
replaced by
g_{H}
and
\chi_{H}
, respectively.

Proof.

The first characterization follows from the formula (
13
), whereas the second one is a consequence of the first one, as
\ell^{v}\in H_{\ell}
if and only if
v\in V_{\ell}
. The last statement follows from Lemma
11
.
∎

4.2.
k
-free and
k(\ell)
-free indices

Let
S(k(\ell))
be the set of positive integers which are
k(\ell)
-free, i.e. of those integers
n
such that
v_{\ell}(n)<k(\ell)
, where
k(\ell)\geqslant 2
, for all
\ell
.

Theorem 13
.

We have

\pi_{\mathcal{P}_{S(k(\ell)),C}}(x)=\Li(x)\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{\mu(n)c(f_{n})}{[F_{f_{n},f_{n}}:K]}+O_{F,K,G,k}\left(x\left(\frac{(\log\log x)^{2}}{\log x}\right)^{1+\frac{3(k-1)}{2k(r+2)}}\right),

where
f_{n}=\prod_{\ell\mid n}\ell^{k(\ell)}
and
k:=\min_{\ell}k(\ell)\geqslant 2
.

Proof.

For
S=S(k(\ell))
, in view of (
12
), it suffices to prove that
g_{S}
satisfies (
8
) for some
0<\kappa\leqslant 2
and apply Theorem
8
.
Since
S
is cut by valuations with
1\in S
,
g_{S}
is multiplicative by Lemma
11
, and by Lemma
12
we obtain
g_{S}(\ell^{m})=-1
if
m=k(\ell)
, and
g_{S}(\ell^{m})=0
for all other
m\geqslant 1
. Therefore,
g_{S}(n)=\mu(\rad(n))
if and only if
n=f_{n}
, and it is
0
otherwise, and by definition, we have
n^{k}\mid f_{n}
for all
n
squarefree. Thus, recalling that if
d\mid n
, then
\varphi(d)\mid\varphi(n)
and
\varphi(nd)=\varphi(n)d
, we have

\sum_{n>z}\frac{\left\lvert g_{S}(n)\right\rvert}{\varphi(n)}=\sum_{f_{n}>z}\frac{\mu(n)^{2}}{\varphi(f_{n})}\leqslant\sum_{n>z^{1/k}}\frac{\mu(n)^{2}}{\varphi(n)n^{k-1}}\ll\frac{1}{z^{1-1/k}},

where we applied Lemma
5
. We may conclude by Theorem
8
with
\kappa=1-1/k
to obtain the desired asymptotic formula.
∎

Corollary 14
.

Let
S(k)
be the set of
k
-free integers for

. We have

\pi_{\mathcal{P}_{S(k),C}}(x)\sim\Li(x)\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{\mu(n)c(n^{k})}{[F_{n^{k},n^{k}}:K]},

with error term as in Theorem

.

Proof.

It suffices to take

for all

in Theorem

.
∎

Both density formulas obtained for

[

, Example 25]
.

4.3.
The index in sets which are cut by valuations

In this section we prove two general results for the primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
K
such that the index of
(G\bmod\mathfrak{p})
lies in a given set
H
which is cut by valuations. This is achieved in two cases requiring some relatively strong conditions on
H
.
Let us introduce some notation. Given a set
H
cut by valuations, we may write each
V_{\ell}
as a disjoint union of intervals of one of the following two types:

(18)

V_{\ell}=\bigcup_{i\geqslant 1}[a_{i},b_{i}]\quad\text{ or }\quad V_{\ell}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N_{\ell}}[a_{i},b_{i}]\cup[a_{N_{\ell}+1},\infty),

where these intervals are intended as discrete sets of integers,
a_{i}\leqslant b_{i}<a_{i+1}-1
for all
i
, and
N_{\ell}\geqslant 0
. All
a_{i},b_{i}
depend on
\ell
, and we have
a_{1}=0
for all
\ell
except finitely many.
Whenever we consider
\min_{\ell}b_{1}
, we implicitly take
b_{1}=\infty
if
V_{\ell}=[a_{1},\infty)
.
With this notation, for all
\ell
and all
m\geqslant 0
, by Lemma
12
we have

(19)

g_{H_{\ell}}(\ell^{m})=\begin{cases}-1&\text{if }m=b_{i}+1\text{ for some }i\\
1&\text{if }m=a_{i}\text{ for some }i\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Theorem 15
.

Let
H
be cut by valuations, with
1\in H
, and suppose that
V_{\ell}
is given as in (
18
) for every
\ell
, and for the first case we suppose that the union is finite and we write
V_{\ell}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N_{\ell}}[a_{i},b_{i}]
.
Assume that for every squarefree integer
n\geqslant 1
we have
\prod_{\ell\mid n}2N_{\ell}\ll n^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0
, and that
\min_{\ell}b_{1}>\alpha
.

We have

\pi_{\mathcal{P}_{H,C}}(x)=\Li(x)\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{g_{H}(n)c(n)}{[F_{n,n}:K]}+O_{F,K,G,k,\alpha}\left(x\left(\frac{(\log\log x)^{2}}{\log x}\right)^{1+\frac{1}{r+2}(1-\frac{\alpha+1}{k})}\right),

where
k:=1+\min_{\ell}b_{1}
,
g_{H}
is multiplicative and, for each
\ell
and
m\geqslant 0
,
g_{H}(\ell^{m})
is equal to (
19
).

Proof.

The function
g_{H}
is multiplicative by Lemma
11
, and it is characterized by Lemma
12
(since
1\in H
, we have
g_{H}(\ell^{m})=g_{H_{\ell}}(\ell^{m})
for all
\ell
and
m\geqslant 0
). In particular,
g_{H}(n)\neq 0
if and only if, for all
\ell
,
v_{\ell}(n)
is equal to
a_{i}
or
b_{i}+1
for some
i
.
Therefore, for all
n
such that
g_{H}(n)\neq 0
we have
\rad(n)^{k}\mid n
, and for a fixed
m=\rad(n)
we may use this lower bound for at most
e(m):=\prod_{\ell\mid m}2N_{\ell}
integers.
Notice that, if
V_{\ell}=\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
for some
\ell\mid m
, then
e(m)=0
and the bound in the statement still holds. In fact, in this case we have
g_{H}(m)=0
.

By the above and making use of the bound
e(n)\ll n^{\alpha}
for
n
squarefree, we then have

\sum_{n>z}\frac{\left\lvert g_{H}(n)\right\rvert}{\varphi(n)}\leqslant\sum_{n>z^{1/k}}\frac{\mu(n)^{2}e(n)}{\varphi(n^{k})}\ll\sum_{n>z^{1/k}}\frac{\log\log n}{n^{k-\alpha}},

where, since
k>1+\alpha
, the series converges and we have

\sum_{n>z}\frac{\left\lvert g_{H}(n)\right\rvert}{\varphi(n)}\ll_{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{z^{1-(\alpha+1)/k-\varepsilon}}\ll_{k,\alpha}z^{-\frac{2}{3}(1-\frac{\alpha+1}{k})},

where we chose
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{3}(1-\frac{\alpha+1}{k})>0
.
Finally, in view of (
12
), we may apply Theorem
8
with
\kappa=\frac{2}{3}(1-\frac{\alpha+1}{k})
.
∎

Example 16
.

Let
H
be cut by valuations and suppose that
V_{\ell}=\{0,1\}\cup\{s,s+1,\ldots\}
for all
\ell
, where
s\geqslant 3
.
We have
\min_{\ell}b_{1}=1
and
\prod_{\ell\mid n}2\leqslant 2\sqrt{n}
, and hence we may take
\alpha=1/2
and
k=2>1+\alpha
.
Thus, the conditions of Theorem
15
are satisfied.
The map
g_{H}
is multiplicative, and by (
19
) we have

g_{H}(\ell^{m})=\begin{cases}-1&\text{if }m=2\\
1&\text{if }m=0,s\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Hence
g_{H}(n)\neq 0
if and only if
n=a^{2}b^{s}
for
a,b
squarefree with
(a,b)=1
; moreover, for such
n
we have
g_{H}(n)=\mu(a)
.
We can then apply Theorem
15
to obtain

\pi_{\mathcal{P}_{H}}(x)=\Li(x)\sum_{a\geqslant 1}\mu(a)\sum_{b\geqslant 1}\frac{\mu(ab)^{2}}{[K_{a^{2}b^{s},a^{2}b^{s}}:K]}+O\left(x\left(\frac{(\log\log x)^{2}}{\log x}\right)^{1+\frac{1}{4(r+2)}}\right).

Theorem 17
.

Let
H
be cut by valuations, with
1\in H
. Let us suppose that
1,2\in V_{\ell}
for all
\ell
. We have

\pi_{\mathcal{P}_{H,C}}(x)=\Li(x)\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{g_{H}(n)c(n)}{[F_{n,n}:K]}+O_{F,K,G}\left(x\left(\frac{(\log\log x)^{2}}{\log x}\right)^{1+\frac{1}{2(r+2)}}\right),

where
g_{H}
is multiplicative and, for each
\ell
and
m\geqslant 0
,
g_{H}(\ell^{m})
is equal to (
19
).

Proof.

Set
k:=1+\min_{\ell}b_{1}\geqslant 3
, where
b_{1}
is defined by writing
V_{\ell}
as in (
18
) for all
\ell
.
We start as in the proof of Theorem
15
. Here we have that
g_{H}(n)\neq 0
only if

is divisible by
\rad(n)^{k}
, and we write

for some
m
squarefree and
a\mid m^{\infty}
.

Therefore, also recalling that

as

, we have

\sum_{n>z}\frac{\left\lvert g_{H}(n)\right\rvert}{\varphi(n)}\leqslant\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\geqslant 1,a\mid m^{\infty}\\
am^{k}>z\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(m)^{2}}{\varphi(am^{k})}\leqslant\sum_{m\geqslant 1}\frac{1}{\varphi(m^{k})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\mid m^{\infty}\\
a>z/m^{k}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{a}.

By Lemma

∎

Theorem 19
.

Let

where

The restriction

Proof.

The set
H
is almost cut by valuations (indeed,

for every

decomposes as in (

Then we have

5.
The density as a limit and as an Euler product

The following result is already known in a more general form under the assumption of GRH, see
[
8
, Theorem 11]
.

Proposition 28
.

Suppose that
H
is almost cut by valuations, and such that
g_{H}
satisfies the convergence condition of Theorem
8
.
We have

\dens(\mathcal{P}_{H,C})=\lim_{Q\to\infty}\dens(\mathcal{P}_{H_{Q},C}).

Proof.

By Theorem
19
we have

\dens(\mathcal{P}_{H_{Q},C})=\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{g_{H_{Q}}(n)c(n)}{[F_{n,n}:K]}.

Let
Q_{0}
be such that
H=H_{Q_{0}}\cap\bigcap_{\ell\nmid Q_{0}}H_{\ell}
. By Lemma
11
if
Q
is divisible by
nQ_{0}
and by the finitely many primes
\ell
such that
0\notin V_{\ell}
, then we have
g_{H_{Q}}(n)=g_{H}(n)
. Thus, for every
n
we have
g_{H_{Q}}(n)\to g_{H}(n)
when
Q\to\infty
, by which we mean the limit
x\to\infty
for
Q_{x}:=\prod_{\ell\leqslant x}\ell
.

Recall that
c(n)
is independent of
H_{Q}
.
Then the double sequence

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{c(n)}{[F_{n,n}:K]}g_{H_{Q}}(n)

has a limit when
Q\to\infty
and when
N\to\infty
, separately. By exchanging the order of the limits, we have

\lim_{Q\to\infty}\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{g_{H_{Q}}(n)c(n)}{[F_{n,n}:K]}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\lim_{Q\to\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{g_{H_{Q}}(n)c(n)}{[F_{n,n}:K]}=\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{g_{H}(n)c(n)}{[F_{n,n}:K]}.

The last series equals
\dens(\mathcal{P}_{H,C})
by Remark
10
.
∎

We introduce the following notation for the next theorem.
For
Q\geqslant 1
we define

(24)

C_{H,Q}:=\bigcup_{h\in H}C_{h_{Q}}=\bigsqcup_{h_{Q}}C_{h_{Q}}\subseteq\Gal(FK_{Q^{\infty},Q^{\infty}}/K),

where
h_{Q}:=\gcd(h,Q^{\infty})
,
K_{Q^{\infty},Q^{\infty}}
is the union of all number fields
K_{Q^{e},Q^{e}}
for
e\geqslant 1
, and
C_{h_{Q}}
is the conjugacy-stable set of those
K
-automorphisms
\sigma
which satisfy the following conditions:

•

\sigma|_{F}\in C
,

•

\sigma
fixes
K_{h_{Q},h_{Q}}
,

•

for all prime numbers
q\mid Q
,
\sigma
does not fix
K_{qh_{Q},qh_{Q}}
.

Let
\mu_{\Haar}
be the Haar measure on
\Gal(FK_{Q^{\infty},Q^{\infty}}/K)
. By (
24
) we have

(25)

\dens(\mathcal{P}_{H_{Q},C})=\mu_{\Haar}(C_{H,Q})=\sum_{h_{Q}}\mu_{\Haar}(C_{h_{Q}}).

Theorem 29
.

Suppose that
H
is almost cut by valuations and such that
g_{H}
satisfies the convergence condition (
8
). Then there is an integer
B\geqslant 1
, depending only on
F
,

,
G
, and with

, where

is associated to

as in (

\dens(\mathcal{P}_{H,C})=A_{H,r}\cdot\mu_{\Haar}(C_{H,B})\cdot\prod_{\ell\mid B}A_{V_{\ell},r}(\ell)^{-1},

where

was defined in Remark
21
, and
A_{H,r}:=\prod_{\ell}A_{V_{\ell},r}(\ell)
.

Under the assumption of GRH, the formula (
26
) holds for any set
H
which is almost cut by valuations, see
[
8
, Theorem 24]
. Unconditionally, since if
g_{H}
satisfies the condition of Theorem

, then
\dens(\mathcal{P}_{H,C})
exists, it must be given by the formula (

). Here we reprove this statement under the assumptions of Theorem

, also for the convenience of the reader. The main difference with the original proof is applying Proposition
28
instead of
[
8
, Theorem 11]
.

Proof of Theorem
29
.

By Proposition
4
(b) there is an integer
B
, which depends only on
F,K,G
, such that by varying

\sigma|_{F}\in C
.
Up to replacing

for

The constants

v\in V_{\ell}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell^{v(r+1)}}\bigg).

\dens(\mathcal{P}_{H,C})=A_{H,r}\cdot\sum_{n\mid B^{\infty}}\frac{g_{H_{B}}(n)c(n)}{[F_{n,n}:K]}\cdot\prod_{\ell\mid B}A_{V_{\ell},r}(\ell)^{-1}.

Proof.

By the assumption,
\dens(\mathcal{P}_{H,C})
is given by the formula (

Writing
n=ab

(b) we have

K

\dens(\mathcal{P}_{H,C})=\sum_{a\mid B^{\infty}}\frac{g_{H_{B}}(a)c(a)}{[F_{a,a}:K]}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\geqslant 1\\

The inner sum over

Comparative Effectiveness of Acupressure and Eucalyptus Aromatherapy on Headache Severity in Hemodialysis Patients: A Randomized Controlled Clinical Trial With a Pretest–Posttest Design
Authors: Mohammad Mahdi Narimani, Mohadeseh Motamed‐Jahromi, Maryam Radmehr
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground and AimsDialysis headache is a common complication of hemodialysis that negatively affects patients' comfort and quality of life. Due to the limitations and potential adverse effects of pharmacological treatments in patients with renal impairment, this study aimed to compare the effectiveness of acupressure and eucalyptus inhalation on headache severity in patients undergoing hemodialysis.MethodsThis study was a randomized controlled clinical trial conducted on patients undergoing hemodialysis at the Salam Dialysis Center in Tehran in 2023. Participants were selected through convenience sampling and then randomly assigned to three groups: acupressure, eucalyptus inhalation, and control (11 patients in each group). The control group received routine care only. Data were analyzed using appropriate statistical tests in SPSS version 26 at a significance level of 0.05. This randomized controlled clinical trial with a pretest–posttest design was conducted in 2023 at the Salam Dialysis Center in Tehran, Iran. Thirty‐three patients were selected through convenience sampling and randomly allocated into three groups (n = 11 each): acupressure, eucalyptus inhalation, and control. In the acupressure group, pressure was applied to the HeGu (LI4), Yintang, Taiyang, and GB20 points for 1 min each during the first 20 min of dialysis across three consecutive sessions. In the eucalyptus group, participants inhaled 1 mL of eucalyptus oil from a distance of 30 cm for 5 min at the beginning of dialysis. Headache severity was measured using the Numeric Rating Scale (0–10) before and after intervention. Data were analyzed using repeated‐measures ANOVA and Bonferroni post hoc tests in SPSS version 26 (two‐sided tests, α = 0.05).ResultsThe participants consisted of 18 men (54.5%) and 15 women (45.4%), with a mean age of 55.30 ± 14.97 years. No significant differences were observed among the three groups regarding demographic and clinical characteristics (p > 0.05). No significant differences were observed among the groups at baseline (p = 1.00). After the intervention, headache severity significantly decreased in both acupressure and eucalyptus groups compared with control (p < 0.001). The time × group interaction effect was significant (F (2,30) = 33.76, p < 0.001, η² = 0.692). No significant difference was found between acupressure and eucalyptus groups (p = 1.00).ConclusionBoth eucalyptus inhalation and acupressure demonstrated potential effectiveness in reducing headache severity in patients undergoing hemodialysis. These simple, low‐cost, and non‐invasive interventions may be considered as complementary approaches in dialysis care to improve patient comfort and reduce headache‐related discomfort.Keywords: acupressure, complementary therapies, eucalyptus, headache, hemodialysis

Introduction

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is considered one of the major health‐threatening conditions of the twenty‐first century [1]. As the disease progresses to end‐stage renal disease, patients require renal replacement therapies, including hemodialysis [2]. Although hemodialysis is a life‐saving intervention for patients with renal failure, it is associated with multiple physical and psychological complications, such as dialysis headache (DH), hypotension, muscle cramps, nausea, and fatigue, which significantly affect patients' quality of life [3, 4].

Headache is one of the most common complaints among patients undergoing hemodialysis [5]. Studies have shown that approximately 70% of dialysis patients experience headaches during or after dialysis sessions [6]. This type of headache, known as DH, typically begins 2–3 h after the start of dialysis and usually lasts less than 4 h. The most common pattern is migraine‐like with a pulsating nature [7]. Although the exact pathophysiological mechanism of DH has not been fully elucidated, factors such as hypotension, rapid urea reduction, blood–brain barrier dysfunction, electrolyte disturbances (sodium and magnesium), and the type of dialysate (acetate vs. bicarbonate) have been proposed [8, 9].

Pharmacological treatments such as triptans, nonsteroidal anti‐inflammatory drugs, and antihistamines are commonly prescribed to relieve headaches in dialysis patients; however, these medications have limitations. In patients with renal impairment, reduced drug clearance increases the risk of accumulation and toxicity, and many of these medications may adversely affect renal function or blood pressure [10]. Therefore, attention to non‐pharmacological and complementary approaches such as acupressure and aromatherapy has increased.

Acupressure, one of the main branches of traditional Chinese medicine, involves applying pressure to specific points on the body (energy meridian points) to restore balance and alleviate physical and psychological symptoms [11]. Unlike acupuncture, acupressure does not require invasive tools such as needles [12]. By stimulating pressure receptors, it promotes the release of endorphins and serotonin and activates central analgesic pathways [13, 14]. Numerous studies have demonstrated its effectiveness in reducing pain, anxiety, sleep disorders, and nausea [15]. Acupressure can be independently performed by nurses or patients and is considered a non‐invasive, low‐cost, and safe method [16].

Eucalyptus aromatherapy has also gained attention as an effective complementary method. Eucalyptus (Eucalyptus globulus) possesses anti‐inflammatory, antioxidant, and analgesic properties [17]. Through inhalation, it stimulates the limbic system of the brain and increases endorphin secretion [18]. Eucalyptus essential oil contains active compounds such as eucalyptol, pinene, and limonene, which play a role in reducing muscle pain, headache, and inflammatory conditions [19]. Studies have shown that eucalyptus inhalation in patients with rheumatoid arthritis and even ICU patients leads to improved physiological indices and reduced pain severity [20, 21].

Although acupressure and eucalyptus aromatherapy have each been shown to be effective in alleviating pain‐related symptoms, direct comparative evidence regarding their effects on headache severity remains limited. Headache is a common and distressing complaint among patients undergoing hemodialysis, and the use of pharmacological pain management in this population is often constrained by comorbidities and potential adverse effects. As frontline healthcare providers, nurses play a critical role in implementing and educating patients about non‐pharmacological pain management strategies, which can substantially improve patients' comfort and quality of life. However, the lack of comparative studies evaluating complementary interventions in hemodialysis settings represents a significant gap in nursing knowledge and practice. Addressing this gap is essential to support evidence‐based decision‐making in non‐pharmacological care. Therefore, this study aimed to compare the effects of acupressure and eucalyptus inhalation on headache severity in patients undergoing hemodialysis. The findings may inform the development of evidence‐based non‐pharmacological care protocols and enhance pain management training for nursing staff and patients.

Methods

This study was a randomized controlled clinical trial with a pretest–posttest design. The study population included all patients undergoing hemodialysis who attended the Salam Dialysis Center in Tehran Province in 2023. Sample size was calculated using G*Power software based on repeated‐measures analysis of variance (ANOVA) with a significance level of 0.05, statistical power of 80%, medium effect size (d = 0.3), three groups, and two measurements. The required sample size was estimated to be 30 participants (10 per group). Considering one additional participant per group, a total of 33 patients (11 per group) were included.

Sample size was calculated using G*Power 3.1 based on repeated‐measures ANOVA (within–between interaction), assuming a medium effect size (f = 0.30), α = 0.05, power = 80%, three groups, and two measurements. The minimum required sample size was 30 participants. Considering potential attrition, 33 participants (11 per group) were recruited.

Based on inclusion and exclusion criteria, 33 patients were selected through convenience sampling and randomly assigned using a random numbers table into three groups: experimental Group 1 (acupressure), experimental Group 2 (eucalyptus aromatherapy), and control group.

Inclusion criteria included: headache score ≥ 1 on the Numeric Rating Scale (NRS) during dialysis, absence of injury or infection at pressure points, no prior use of acupressure or aromatherapy, no olfactory disorders, no addiction or regular use of opioid analgesics, and informed consent. Exclusion criteria included decreased level of consciousness, unwillingness to continue participation, or changes in health status that made continuation of the intervention impossible.

Data were collected using two instruments to obtain demographic, clinical, and headache severity information. Demographic variables included age and sex, while clinical variables comprised disease duration, duration of dialysis, and blood levels of urea, creatinine, and potassium. Headache severity was assessed using the NRS, which ranges from 0 (no pain) to 10 (the worst pain imaginable). This instrument has demonstrated confirmed validity and high reliability, with reported reliability coefficients ranging from 0.89 to 0.91 in previous studies [22, 23].

The intervention procedures differed across the study groups, in the acupressure group, pressure was applied to the HeGu (LI4), Yintang, Taiyang, and GB20 points during three consecutive sessions within the first 20 min of dialysis. Each point was stimulated for 1 min (8 s of pressure followed by 2 s of rest). In the eucalyptus aromatherapy group, at the beginning of dialysis, participants inhaled 1 mL of eucalyptus oil placed on gauze from a distance of 30 cm for 5 min. No intervention was performed in the control group, which received routine care only. All participants received the necessary instructions before the intervention. Headache severity was recorded twice (before and after the intervention) over three dialysis sessions.

Data were analyzed using SPSS version 26 at descriptive and inferential levels. Mean and standard deviation were used for descriptive statistics. Repeated‐measures ANOVA (2 × 3) and Bonferroni post hoc tests were used for inferential analysis. Statistical assumptions, including normality (Shapiro–Wilk test), homogeneity of variances (Levene's test), and homogeneity of covariance matrices (Box's test) were assessed. When assumptions were violated, nonparametric Friedman and Mann–Whitney U tests were applied. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

All statistical tests were two‐sided, and the level of statistical significance was set a priori at α = 0.05. Effect sizes were reported using partial eta squared (η²) for ANOVA analyses. Data were analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Pre‐specified analyses included comparisons of within‐group changes over time and between‐group differences at each time point. No exploratory subgroup analyses were performed.

Ethical Considerations

All stages of the study were approved by the Ethics Committee of Islamic Azad University, Isfahan (Khorasgan) Branch (IR.IAU.KHUISF.REC.1402.417) and registered in the Iranian Registry of Clinical Trials (IRCT20240205060907N1). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. This study was conducted and reported in accordance with the CONSORT guidelines for randomized controlled trials.

Discussion

The present study aimed to investigate and compare the effectiveness of two non‐pharmacological interventions—acupressure and eucalyptus inhalation—on headache severity in patients undergoing hemodialysis. The findings demonstrated that acupressure applied to the well‐known points HeGu (LI4), Yintang, Taiyang, and GB20 resulted in a significant reduction in headache severity among hemodialysis patients.

These results are consistent with previous studies that have highlighted the analgesic effects of acupressure. For example, Saydi et al. [16] reported a significant reduction in migraine headache severity following acupressure in patients presenting to emergency departments. Similarly, Duzel et al. [10] found that acupressure effectively alleviated pain and improved hemodynamic parameters in patients undergoing coronary angiography. In addition, Suaib et al. [24] demonstrated that acupressure could be used as an alternative and complementary therapy to reduce both the intensity and frequency of tension‐type headaches. The consistency between these findings and the present study supports the effectiveness of acupressure as a non‐pharmacological pain management strategy.

The analgesic effects of acupressure are believed to occur through multiple physiological mechanisms. Stimulation of specific acupoints may activate peripheral neural pathways and central pain inhibitory systems, including the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis, leading to increased secretion of cortisol, endorphins, and serotonin [15]. Additionally, acupressure may stimulate myelinated nerve fibers, reduce the release of pro‐nociceptive mediators, and modulate pain signal transmission based on the gate control theory of pain [14].

Beyond its effectiveness in pain reduction, acupressure has several practical advantages that support its use in clinical settings. Mehta et al. [13] emphasized that acupressure is a cost‐effective, easily accessible, and safe intervention without significant adverse effects, highlighting its potential for broader application in hospital environments. Consistent with these findings, no adverse effects were observed in the present study following the application of acupressure. Furthermore, its ease of training for patients or nursing staff, high patient adherence, and lack of requirement for expensive equipment make acupressure a feasible and practical non‐pharmacological intervention in dialysis units. Previous studies have also reported its benefits in managing pruritus, ear pain, insomnia, hypertension, and depression among hemodialysis patients [25, 26], further supporting its role as a complementary intervention in this population.

The second intervention examined in this study, eucalyptus aromatherapy, also resulted in a significant reduction in mean headache severity among hemodialysis patients. This finding is consistent with previous research investigating the therapeutic effects of eucalyptus on pain and symptom relief. For instance, Varkaneh et al. [18] demonstrated that inhalation of eucalyptus significantly reduced pain intensity and improved quality of life in patients with rheumatoid arthritis. Additionally, other studies have reported that eucalyptus vapor can alleviate symptoms and promote relaxation in patients with COVID‐19 or various respiratory conditions [27, 28].

In the present study, eucalyptus essential oil was inhaled from a distance of approximately 30 cm for 5 min at the beginning of the dialysis session. This protocol is consistent with previous clinical studies indicating that low‐dose, short‐duration, and repeated inhalation of eucalyptus is safe and well tolerated [9]. Eucalyptus essential oil contains several bioactive compounds, including eucalyptol, pinene, and geranial, which are known to possess anti‐inflammatory, antispasmodic, analgesic, and sedative properties [18]. The inhalation of eucalyptus may stimulate olfactory receptors and activate the limbic system, resulting in endorphin release and improved psychophysiological responses, which may contribute to headache relief [29].

When comparing acupressure and eucalyptus inhalation, both interventions showed similar reductions in headache severity, with no significant difference between the two groups. This finding indicates comparable clinical effectiveness, while the choice of intervention may depend on environmental conditions, patient preference, nurse expertise, and dialysis unit resources. Some studies have reported that combining these interventions produces greater effects than using either method alone. For example, Yang et al. [30] demonstrated that patients receiving a combination of aromatherapy and acupressure showed greater improvement in agitation related to dementia. Similarly, a study comparing aromatherapy and massage therapy in patients with knee osteoarthritis demonstrated that both interventions significantly reduced pain and improved functional outcomes, with no clear superiority of either method over the other [31].

These findings provide new directions for future complementary medicine research. Given the key role of nurses in implementing non‐pharmacological interventions, acupressure and aromatherapy could be considered part of independent nursing interventions, enhancing the quality of care for hemodialysis patients. Both interventions are non‐invasive, low‐risk, cost‐effective, and teachable, highlighting their clinical advantages. Limitations of the present study include the influence of patients' prior pain experiences and psychological factors on outcomes, a relatively small sample size, and focus on a single center, which may limit generalizability.

Conclusion

Based on the findings of this study, both non‐pharmacological interventions, namely acupressure and eucalyptus inhalation, significantly reduced headache severity in patients undergoing hemodialysis, and no adverse events were reported. The similar reduction in pain intensity suggests that both interventions are equally effective and can be used as safe complementary therapies in the care of renal patients. However, since the sample was drawn from a single hemodialysis center, caution is warranted when generalizing these findings to a broader population. Implementation of these two interventions as complementary measures in dialysis units is recommended to help reduce headache severity among patients.

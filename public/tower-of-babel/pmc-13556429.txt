739. Synergistic Modulation of Monoamine Circuitry: A Novel Non-Benzodiazepine Anxiolytic Strategy
Authors: S-K Lin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAnxiety disorders and psychosomatic conditions are conventionally managed with benzodiazepines (BZDs) or antidepressants. While effective, BZDs carry risks of dependence, and standard-dose selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) are often limited by poor tolerability. In many clinical settings, combination psychotropics—most notably flupentixol/melitracen (Deanxit)—have been widely utilized as an alternative. However, concerns regarding the long-term safety of its older components, potential extrapyramidal side effects, and lack of modern regulatory approval in certain jurisdictions persist. There is a clear clinical need for a modern, safer non-benzodiazepine alternative.Aims & ObjectivesThis study proposes a novel pharmacological strategy combining very low-dose aripiprazole (1 mg) with low-dose escitalopram (2 mg). The objective is to provide a superior alternative to both BZDs and Deanxit, achieving potent anxiolysis through the synergistic modulation of dopamine and serotonin systems while maintaining a cleaner safety profile.MethodThe formulation utilizes a fraction of the defined daily doses (DDD) of modern psychiatric agents. We analyze the neurobiological synergy between aripiprazole’s dopamine-stabilizing properties (D2 partial agonism) and escitalopram’s selective serotonin reuptake inhibition. The hypothesis is that this "micro-dose" dual-mechanism approach achieves therapeutic efficacy comparable to Deanxit but with reduced risk of metabolic, extrapyramidal, or cardiovascular adverse effects.ResultsTheoretical analysis and clinical observations suggest: 1. Targeted Anxiolysis: Effectively addresses psychosomatic symptoms and psychic anxiety without the sedation of BZDs or the "old-generation" risks of Deanxit. 2. Improved Tolerability: Using only 2 mg of escitalopram avoids common SSRI-induced apathy, while 1 mg of aripiprazole remains well below the threshold for motor side effects. 3.No Abuse Liability: Provides a sustainable long-term solution without the addiction traps associated with BZDs. 4. Clean Pharmacokinetics: Minimal metabolic load and drug-drug interactions compared to older tricyclic-based combinations. By replacing the outdated components of therapies like Deanxit with a precision micro-dose of modern stabilizers and enhancers, this strategy offers a safer, non-addictive, and better-tolerated paradigm. This non-benzodiazepine approach warrants systematic clinical evaluation to replace legacy treatments and improve long-term outcomes for patients with anxiety and psychosomatic disorders.

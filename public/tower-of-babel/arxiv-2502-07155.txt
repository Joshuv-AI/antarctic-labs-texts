Nonequispaced fast Fourier transforms for bandlimited functions
Melanie Kircheis, Daniel Potts

Abstract
In this paper we consider the problem of approximating function evaluations f ⁡ ( 𝒙 j ) f(\boldsymbol{x}_{j}) at given nonequispaced points 𝒙 j \boldsymbol{x}_{j} , j = 1 , … ​ N j=1,\dots N , of a bandlimited function from given values f ^ ​ ( 𝒌 ) \hat{f}(\boldsymbol{k}) , 𝒌 ∈ ℐ 𝑴 \boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}} , of its Fourier transform. Note that if a trigonometric polynomial is given, it is already known that this problem can be solved by means of the nonequispaced fast Fourier transform (NFFT). In other words, we introduce a new NFFT-like procedure for bandlimited functions, which is based on regularized Shannon sampling formulas. Key words : nonequispaced fast Fourier transform, bandlimited functions, regularized Shannon sampling formulas, sinc function, NFFT, NUFFT AMS Subject Classifications : 65Txx, 65T50, 94A20

Introduction

The nonequispaced fast Fourier transform (NFFT) is a fast algorithm to evaluate a trigonometric polynomial

\displaystyle f(\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}}}\hat{f}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\,\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{x}},\quad\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{d},

with given Fourier coefficients
\hat{f}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\mathbb{C}
,
\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}}
,
at given nonequispaced points
\boldsymbol{x}_{j}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
,
j=1,\dots,N
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
where for
M\in 2\mathbb{N}
we define the index set
\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}}\coloneqq\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap\left[-\tfrac{M}{2},\tfrac{M}{2}\right)^{d}

with cardinality
|\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}}|=M^{d}
, and
\mathbb{T}^{d}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
, denotes the
d
-dimensional torus.

In this paper we focus on the analogous problem for bandlimited functions, where we aim to approximate evaluations
f(\boldsymbol{x}_{j})
,
j=1,\dots,N
, of a function

\displaystyle f(\boldsymbol{x})=\int\limits_{\left[-\frac{M}{2},\frac{M}{2}\right)^{d}}\hat{f}(\boldsymbol{v})\,\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{v}\boldsymbol{x}}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{v},\quad\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(1.1)

from given measurements
\hat{f}(\boldsymbol{k})\in\mathbb{C}
,
\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}}
, of its Fourier transform
\hat{f}
.

To do so, this paper is organized as follows.
Firstly, in Section
2
we review the NFFT for trigonometric polynomials.
Subsequently, in Section
3
we give an overview of the regularized Shannon sampling formulas, which play the key role in introducing the NFFT-like procedure for bandlimited functions in Section
4
.
Finally, in Section
5
we compare this new method to the classical NFFT.

The NFFT

For given nonequispaced nodes
\boldsymbol{x}_{j}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
,
j=1,\dots,N
, and given coefficients
\hat{f}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\mathbb{C}
,
\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}}

\displaystyle f(\boldsymbol{x}_{j})=\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}}}\hat{f}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\,\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{x}_{j}},\quad j=1,\dots,N,

(2.1)

where the inner product shall be defined as usual as

\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{x}\coloneqq k_{1}x_{1}+\dots+k_{d}x_{d}
.

3
Regularized Shannon sampling formulas

A function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
is said to be
bandlimited
with
bandwidth

M\in\mathbb{N}
, if
the support of its
(continuous) Fourier transform

\displaystyle\hat{f}(\boldsymbol{v})\coloneqq\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{v}\boldsymbol{x}}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x},\quad\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(3.1)

is contained in
\left[-\frac{M}{2},\frac{M}{2}\right]^{d}
.
The space of all bandlimited functions with bandwidth
M
shall be denoted by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}_{M/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\coloneqq\Big\{f\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\colon\,\mathrm{supp}(\hat{f})\!\subseteq\!\left[-\tfrac{M}{2},\,\tfrac{M}{2}\right]^{d}\!\Big\},\!

which is also known as the
Paley–Wiener space
.
Note that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}_{M/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subseteq L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(3.2)

cf.
[
14
, Lemma 4.1]
.
Thus, the Fourier inversion theorem, see e. g.
[
22
, Theorem 2.23]
, guarantees that the
inverse Fourier transform
of
f
can be written as given in (
1.1
).

By the famous Whittaker–Kotelnikov–Shannon sampling theorem (
[
31
,
17
,
28
]
) any bandlimited function
f\in{\mathcal{B}}_{M/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
can be recovered from its samples
f\big(\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\ell}}{L}\big)
,
\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, with
L\geq M
,
L\in\mathbb{N}
, in the form

\displaystyle f(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f\big(\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\ell}}{L}\big)\,\mathrm{sinc}\big(L\pi\big(\boldsymbol{x}-\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\ell}}{L}\big)\big),\quad\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(3.3)

where the
\mathrm{sinc}
func
tion is given by
\mathrm{sinc}(\boldsymbol{x})\coloneqq\prod_{t=1}^{d}\mathrm{sinc}(x_{t})
with

\displaystyle\mathrm{sinc}(x)\coloneqq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\sin x}{x}&\colon x\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\},\\[4.30554pt]
1&\colon x=0.\end{array}\right.

It is well known that the series in (
3.3
) converges absolutely and uniformly on whole
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Unfortunately, the numerical use of this classical Whittaker–Kotelnikov–Shannon sampling series (
3.3
) is limited, since it requires infinitely many samples, which is impossible in practice, and its truncated version is not a good approximation due to the slow decay of the
\mathrm{sinc}
func
tion, see
[
11
]
.
In addition to this rather poor convergence, it is known, see
[
8
,
9
,
5
]
, that in the presence of noise in the samples
f\big(\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\ell}}{L}\big)
,
\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, the convergence of the Shannon sampling series (
3.3
) may even break down completely.

Based on this observation, numerous approaches for numerical realizations have been developed, where the Shannon sampling series was regularized with a suitable window function.
Note that many authors such as
[
4
,
20
,
26
,
21
,
30
]
used window functions in the frequency domain, but the recent study
[
16
]
has shown that it is much more beneficial to employ a window function in the spatial domain, cf.
[
24
,
25
,
30
,
19
,
18
,
3
,
15
]
.

Therefore, for a given
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
2m\ll L
we introduce the set
\Phi_{m,L}
of all window functions
\varphi\colon\,\mathbb{R}\to[0,\,1]
with the following properties:

•

\varphi
is compactly supported on
\left[-\tfrac{m}{L},\,\tfrac{m}{L}\right]
, belongs to
L_{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
and is even.

•

\varphi
restricted to
{[0,\,\infty)}
is monotonously non-increasing with
\varphi(0)=1
.

Remark 3.1
.

As examples of such window functions we consider
the
\sinh
-type
window function

\displaystyle\!\!\varphi_{\sinh}(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sinh\beta}\,\sinh\!\Big(\beta\sqrt{1-\big(\tfrac{Lx}{m}\big)^{2}}\,\Big)\,\chi_{\left[-\tfrac{m}{L},\,\tfrac{m}{L}\right]}(x)\!

(3.4)

with certain
\beta>0
,
and the
continuous Kaiser–Bessel window function

\displaystyle\varphi_{\mathrm{cKB}}(x)\coloneqq\frac{\bigg(I_{0}\Big(\beta\,\sqrt{1-\big(\tfrac{Lx}{m}\big)^{2}}\,\Big)-1\bigg)}{I_{0}(\beta)-1}\,\,\chi_{\left[-\tfrac{m}{L},\,\tfrac{m}{L}\right]}(x)

with certain
\beta>0
, where
I_{0}
denotes the
modified Bessel function of first kind
. Note that these window functions are well-studied in the context of the NFFT, see e. g.
[
23
]
.

Then, for a fixed window function
\varphi\in\Phi_{m,L}
we study the
regularized Shannon sampling formula with localized sampling

\displaystyle(R_{\varphi,m}f)({\boldsymbol{x}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f\big(\tfrac{{\boldsymbol{\ell}}}{L}\big)\,\mathrm{sinc}\big(L\pi\big(\boldsymbol{x}-\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\ell}}{L}\big)\big)\,\varphi\big({\boldsymbol{x}}-\tfrac{{\boldsymbol{\ell}}}{L}\big),\quad\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(3.5)

Note that this is an
interpolating approximation
of
f
on
\frac{1}{L}\,\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, i. e., we have

\displaystyle f\big(\tfrac{\boldsymbol{k}}{L}\big)=(R_{\varphi,m}f)\big(\tfrac{\boldsymbol{k}}{L}\big),\quad\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},

since by assumption
\varphi(0)=1
and
\mathrm{sinc}(\pi(k-\ell))=\delta_{k,\ell}
for all
k,\,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
with the Kronecker symbol
\delta_{k,\ell}
.
Then it is known that the regularized Shannon sampling formula
R_{\varphi,m}f
in (
3.5
) with suitable window function
\varphi\in\Phi_{m,L}
yields a good approximation of
f
, cf.
[
15
,
16
,
14
]
.

4
NFFT-like procedure for bandlimited functions

Now assume we are given the values
\hat{f}(\boldsymbol{k})
,
\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}}
, of the Fourier transform (
1.1
) of a bandlimited function
f\in\mathcal{B}_{M/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
we are looking for function evaluations
f(\boldsymbol{x}_{j})
at given nonequispaced points
\boldsymbol{x}_{j}
,
j=1,\dots N
.
Further we assume that the function
f
fulfills the condition

\sum_{\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\big|f\big(\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\ell}}{L}\big)\big|<\infty,

(4.1)

such that the Fourier series

\hat{f}(\boldsymbol{v})=\frac{1}{L^{d}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f\big(\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\ell}}{L}\big)\,\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{\ell}\boldsymbol{v}/L},\quad\boldsymbol{v}\in\left[-\tfrac{L}{2},\,\tfrac{L}{2}\right]^{d},

converges absolutely and uniformly, see
[
16
, p. 12]
, and thus the considered problem is well-defined.
Note that by
[
27
, Lemma 2]
it is known that
f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
fulfill the condition (
4.1
).
Moreover,
f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
directly implies
\hat{f}\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Remark 4.1
.

Note that the recent work
[
7
]
derived error estimates for a familiar problem, where however for functions
f\in C(\mathbb{R})
satisfying certain decay and smoothness conditions and equispaced points
\boldsymbol{x}_{j}=\frac{2j-N}{2N}
,
j=1,\dots,N
, simply the FFT can be used.

In order to compute the values
f(\boldsymbol{x}_{j})
,
j=1,\dots N
, we aim to make use of the regularized Shannon sampling formulas, see Section
3
.
Inserting the approximation (
3.5
) into the Fourier transform (
3.1
) and using the definition of the regularized
\mathrm{sinc}
func
tion

\displaystyle\psi(\boldsymbol{x})\coloneqq\mathrm{sinc}(L\pi\boldsymbol{x})\,\varphi(\boldsymbol{x}),

(4.2)

we have

\displaystyle\hat{f}(\boldsymbol{v})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{v}\boldsymbol{x}}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\approx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(R_{\varphi,m}f)(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{v}\boldsymbol{x}}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\,\sum_{\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f\big(\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\ell}}{L}\big)\,\psi\big(\boldsymbol{x}-\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\ell}}{L}\big)\,\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{v}\boldsymbol{x}}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f\big(\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\ell}}{L}\big)\,\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{v}\boldsymbol{\ell}/L}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(\boldsymbol{y})\,\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{v}\boldsymbol{y}}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{y}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f\big(\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\ell}}{L}\big)\,\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{v}\boldsymbol{\ell}/L}\bigg)\cdot\hat{\psi}(\boldsymbol{v}),

(4.3)

where summation and integration may be interchanged by the theorem of Fubini–Tonelli.
By defining

\displaystyle\hat{\nu}(\boldsymbol{v})\coloneqq\sum_{\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f\big(\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\ell}}{L}\big)\,\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{v}\boldsymbol{\ell}/L},\quad\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(4.4)

we recognize that this function
\hat{\nu}
is
L
-per
iodic. Thus, due to the fact that the Fourier transform of the bandlimited function
f\in\mathcal{B}_{M/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is non-periodic, the approximation (
4.3
) can only be reasonable for
\boldsymbol{v}\in\big[-\frac{L}{2},\frac{L}{2}\big]^{d}
.

As the goal is to recover the nonequispaced samples
f(\boldsymbol{x}_{j})
,
j=1,\dots,N
, by means of a regularized Shannon sampling formula (
3.5
), we need access to as many equispaced samples
f\big(\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\ell}}{L}\big)
as possible, i. e., we are looking for an inversion formula for (
4.4
).
To this end, note that (
4.4
) can be written as

\displaystyle\hat{\nu}(\boldsymbol{v})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}}f\big(\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\ell}}{L}\big)\,\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{v}\boldsymbol{\ell}/L}+\sum_{\boldsymbol{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{\boldsymbol{0}\}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}}f\big(\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\ell}+\boldsymbol{r}\Theta}{L}\big)\,\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{v}(\boldsymbol{\ell}+\boldsymbol{r}\Theta)/L},\quad\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

with the index set
\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}
with
\boldsymbol{\Theta}=\Theta\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{d}
,
\Theta\in 2\mathbb{N}
.
Since
f\in{\mathcal{B}}_{M/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subseteq C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, see (
3.2
), the equispaced samples
f\big(\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\ell}}{L}\big)
are negligible for all
\|\boldsymbol{\ell}\|_{\infty}\geq\frac{\Theta}{2}
with suitably chosen
\Theta
.
In order to avoid aliasing in the computation we assume that
\Theta=L
is sufficient.
Hence, we consider

\displaystyle\hat{\nu}(\boldsymbol{v})\approx\hat{\vartheta}(\boldsymbol{v})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{L}}}f\big(\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\ell}}{L}\big)\,\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{v}\boldsymbol{\ell}/L},\quad\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(4.5)

and thus by (
4.3
) the approximation

\displaystyle\hat{f}(\boldsymbol{v})

\displaystyle\approx\hat{\vartheta}(\boldsymbol{v})\cdot\hat{\psi}(\boldsymbol{v}),\quad\boldsymbol{v}\in\big[-\tfrac{L}{2},\tfrac{L}{2}\big]^{d}.

(4.6)

Since it is additionally known that
\hat{f}(\boldsymbol{v})=0
for all
\boldsymbol{v}\notin\big[-\frac{M}{2},\frac{M}{2}\big]^{d}
and
\hat{\psi}(\boldsymbol{v})\neq 0
for all
\boldsymbol{v}\in\big[-\frac{L}{2},\frac{L}{2}\big]^{d}
, we might use (
4.6
) and (
4.5
) for given
\hat{f}(\boldsymbol{k})
,
\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}}
, to approximate the equispaced samples
f\big(\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\ell}}{L}\big)
,
\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{L}}
, by setting

\displaystyle\hat{\vartheta}(\boldsymbol{k})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{\hat{f}(\boldsymbol{k})}{\hat{\psi}(\boldsymbol{k})}&\colon\quad\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}},\\
0&\colon\quad\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{L}}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}},\end{array}\right.

and subsequently computing

\displaystyle f\big(\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\ell}}{L}\big)\approx\vartheta_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{|\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{L}}|}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{L}}}\hat{\vartheta}(\boldsymbol{k})\,\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{\ell}/L},\quad\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{L}},

(4.7)

by means of an iFFT.

To finally approximate the samples
f(\boldsymbol{x}_{j})
,
j=1,\dots,N
, we make use of the regularized Shannon sampling formula (
3.5
).
Note that since we assumed that the window function
\varphi\in\Phi_{m,L}
is compactly supported, the computation of
(R_{\varphi,m}f)(\boldsymbol{x})
for fixed
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\tfrac{1}{L}\,\mathbb{Z}^{d}
requires only
(2m+1)^{d}
samples
f\big(\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\ell}}{L}\big)
. However, we have already encountered that (
4.7
) can only be used to approximate
f\big(\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\ell}}{L}\big)
for
\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{L}}
in order to avoid aliasing in the computation of the inverse Fourier transform in (
4.7
).
Thereby, we are confronted with a limitation of the feasible points to
\boldsymbol{x}_{j}\in[-\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{m}{L},\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{m}{L})^{d}
,
j=1,\dots,N
, since only in this case exclusively the evaluations
f\big(\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\ell}}{L}\big)
,
\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{L}}
, are needed for the computation. Hence, the final approximation is computed by

\displaystyle(R_{\varphi,m}f)(\boldsymbol{x}_{j})\approx f_{j}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{L}}}\vartheta_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\,\psi\big(\boldsymbol{x}_{j}-\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\ell}}{L}\big)=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\mathcal{J}_{\boldsymbol{L},m}(\boldsymbol{x}_{j})}\vartheta_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\,\psi\big(\boldsymbol{x}_{j}-\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\ell}}{L}\big),

where the index set of the nonzero entries

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\boldsymbol{L},m}(\boldsymbol{x}_{j})\coloneqq\left\{\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\colon-m+L\boldsymbol{x}_{j}\leq\boldsymbol{\ell}\leq m+L\boldsymbol{x}_{j}\right\}

(4.8)

contains at most
(2m+1)^{d}
entries for each fixed
\boldsymbol{x}_{j}
, cf. (
2.5
).
Thus, the obtained algorithm can be summarized as follows, cf.
[
14
, Algorithm 5.16]
.

Algorithm 4.2

(NFFT-like procedure for bandlimited functions)
.

For
d,m,N\in\mathbb{N}
,
M\in 2\mathbb{N}
, and
L=M(1+\lambda)\in\mathbb{N}
with oversampling parameter
\lambda\geq 0
let
\boldsymbol{x}_{j}\in[-\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{m}{L},\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{m}{L})^{d}
,
j=1,\dots,N
, be given nodes as well as
\hat{f}(\boldsymbol{k})\in\mathbb{C}
,
\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}}
, given evaluations of the Fourier transform of the bandlimited function
f\in\mathcal{B}_{M/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Furthermore, we are given the window function
\varphi\in\Phi_{m,L}
, the corresponding regularized
\mathrm{sinc}
func
tion
\psi
in (
4.2
), and its Fourier transform
\hat{\psi}
.

0.

Precomputation:

(a)

Compute the nonzero values
\hat{\psi}(\boldsymbol{k})
for
\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}}
.

(b)

Compute the evaluations
\psi\big(\boldsymbol{x}_{j}-\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\ell}}{L}\big)
for
j=1,\dots,N,
as well as
\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\mathcal{J}_{\boldsymbol{L},m}(\boldsymbol{x}_{j})
, cf. (
4.8
).

1.

Set
\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}}|)

\displaystyle\hat{\vartheta}(\boldsymbol{k})\coloneqq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{\hat{f}(\boldsymbol{k})}{\hat{\psi}(\boldsymbol{k})}&\colon\quad\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}},\\
0&\colon\quad\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{L}}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}}.\end{array}\right.

2.

Compute

\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}}|\log(|\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}}|))

\displaystyle\vartheta_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{|\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{L}}|}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{L}}}\hat{\vartheta}(\boldsymbol{k})\,\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{\ell}/L},\quad\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{L}},

by means of a
d
-var
iate iFFT.

3.

Compute the short sums
\mathcal{O}(N)

\displaystyle f_{j}\coloneqq\sum_{\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\mathcal{J}_{\boldsymbol{L},m}(\boldsymbol{x}_{j})}\vartheta_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\,\psi\big(\boldsymbol{x}_{j}-\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\ell}}{L}\big),\quad j=1,\dots,N.

Output:

f_{j}\approx f(\boldsymbol{x}_{j})

Complexity:

\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}}|\log(|\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}}|)+N)

Note that by defining the vector
\boldsymbol{\hat{f}}\coloneqq(\hat{f}(\boldsymbol{k}))_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}}}
as well as the diagonal matrix

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{D}_{\hat{\psi}}\coloneqq\text{diag}\left(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{L}}|\cdot\hat{\psi}(\boldsymbol{k})}\right)_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}}}\ \in\mathbb{C}^{|\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}}|\times|\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}}|}

(4.9)

and the
{(2m+1)^{d}}
-sparse
matrix

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Psi}\coloneqq\bigg(\psi\big(\boldsymbol{x}_{j}-\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\ell}}{L}\big)\bigg)_{j=1,\,\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{L}}}^{N}\ \in\mathbb{R}^{N\times|\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{L}}|},

(4.10)

the approximation of Algorithm
4.2
is given by

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{f}=\boldsymbol{\Psi}\boldsymbol{F}\boldsymbol{D}_{\hat{\psi}}\boldsymbol{\hat{f}},

(4.11)

where
\boldsymbol{F}\in\mathbb{C}^{|\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{L}}|\times|\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}}|}
denotes the Fourier matrix (
2.3
) with
L=M_{\sigma}
.

5
Comparison to the classical NFFT

Note that one might also directly apply an equispaced quadrature rule to the inverse Fourier transform (
1.1
), i. e., consider the approximation

\displaystyle f(\boldsymbol{x})=\int\limits_{[-\frac{M}{2},\frac{M}{2}]^{d}}\hat{f}(\boldsymbol{v})\,\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{v}\boldsymbol{x}}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{v}\approx\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}}}\hat{f}(\boldsymbol{k})\,\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{x}},

such that the function evaluations
f(\boldsymbol{x}_{j})
,
j=1,\dots,N
, could also be approximated efficiently by means of an NFFT. Since this raises the question of which of the two methods, Algorithm
2.1
or Algorithm
4.2
, is more advantageous, this section deals with the comparison of the two approaches.

Considering the matrix notations
\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{F}\boldsymbol{D}
and
\boldsymbol{\Psi}\boldsymbol{F}\boldsymbol{D}_{\hat{\psi}}
, cf. (
2.6
) and (
4.11
), the first thing to realize is that for
\boldsymbol{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times|\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{L}}|}
in (
2.4
) the window function
\varphi_{m}(\boldsymbol{x})
is used, while for
\boldsymbol{\Psi}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times|\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{L}}|}
in (
4.10
) we consider the regularized
\mathrm{sinc}
func
tion
\psi(\boldsymbol{x})
in (
4.2
).
A similar remark can also be made about the diagonal matrices
\boldsymbol{D}\in\mathbb{C}^{|\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}}|\times|\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}}|}
in (
2.2
) and
\boldsymbol{D}_{\hat{\psi}}\in\mathbb{C}^{|\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}}|\times|\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}}|}
in (
4.9
).

Additionally, it is important to note that the two methods can only be compared for
\boldsymbol{x}\in[-\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{m}{L},\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{m}{L})^{d}
, as the approximation by Algorithm
4.2
is only reasonable in this case.
This implies that the matrix
\boldsymbol{B}
in (
2.4
) is, unlike usual, non-periodic, whereas the matrix
\boldsymbol{\Psi}
in (
4.10
) is inherently non-periodic by definition.

To study the quality of both approaches, note that by the NFFT we are given the approximation

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{x}}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{|\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{L}}|\cdot\hat{\varphi}(\boldsymbol{k})}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{L}}}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{\ell}/L}\,\tilde{\varphi}_{m}\hskip-2.5pt\left(\boldsymbol{x}-\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\ell}}{L}\right),\quad\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{d},

(5.1)

for
\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}}
fixed, cf. (
2.6
) with
L=M_{\sigma}
, where
\tilde{\varphi}_{m}(\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_{\boldsymbol{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi_{m}(\boldsymbol{x}+\boldsymbol{r})
denotes the
1
-per
iodic version of the compactly supported window function
\varphi_{m}
.
Thus, we look for a comparable approximation of the exponential function using our newly proposed method in Algorithm
4.2
.
For this purpose, note that
g(\boldsymbol{x})\coloneqq\hat{\psi}(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{x}}
with
\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
fixed possesses the Fourier transform
\hat{g}(\boldsymbol{v})=\psi(\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{v})
.
Therefore, we have
g\in\mathcal{B}_{M/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
\boldsymbol{k}\in\big[-\frac{M}{2}+\frac{m}{L},\frac{M}{2}-\frac{m}{L}\big]^{d}
, i. e., considering (
3.5
) for this function
g
yields

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{x}}\approx\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\hat{\psi}\big(\tfrac{{\boldsymbol{\ell}}}{L}\big)\,\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{\ell}/L}\,\psi\big({\boldsymbol{x}}-\tfrac{{\boldsymbol{\ell}}}{L}\big),\quad\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

or rather

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{x}}\approx\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{L}}}\frac{\hat{\psi}\big(\tfrac{{\boldsymbol{\ell}}}{L}\big)}{\hat{\psi}(\boldsymbol{x})}\,\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{\ell}/L}\,\psi\big({\boldsymbol{x}}-\tfrac{{\boldsymbol{\ell}}}{L}\big),\quad\boldsymbol{x}\in\big[-\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{m}{L},\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{m}{L}\big)^{d}.

Since numerical experiments have shown that
\hat{\psi}(\boldsymbol{y})\approx\frac{1}{|\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{L}}|}
,
\boldsymbol{y}\in\big[-\tfrac{M}{2},\tfrac{M}{2}\big)^{d}
, for the window functions mentioned in Remark
3.1
, the above approximation simplifies to

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{x}}\approx\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{L}}}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{\ell}/L}\,\psi\big({\boldsymbol{x}}-\tfrac{{\boldsymbol{\ell}}}{L}\big),\quad\boldsymbol{x}\in\big[-\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{m}{L},\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{m}{L}\big)^{d},

(5.2)

which equals the approximation
\boldsymbol{\Psi}\boldsymbol{F}\boldsymbol{D}_{\hat{\psi}}
of Algorithm
4.2
, since
|\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{L}}|\,\hat{\psi}(\boldsymbol{k})\approx 1
,
\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{I}_{\boldsymbol{M}}
.
Therefore, we can compare the quality of the two methods by considering the approximations (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) of the exponential function.

For simplicity we restrict ourselves to the one-dimensional setting
d=1
for the visualization.
To estimate the quality of the approaches, we consider the approximation error

\displaystyle e(v)\coloneqq\max_{x_{p},p=1,\dots,P}\big|\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}vx_{p}}-h(x_{p})\big|,

(5.3)

Quantification and Correlation of Salivary Tissue Inhibitor Matrix Metalloproteinases 1 and 2 Levels in Caries-free and Caries-active Children: A Randomized Clinical Trial
Authors: Umapathy Thimmegowda, Gadigi Sampreetha
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesExtracellular matrix (ECM) proteolysis is carried out by a class of enzymes called matrix metalloproteases (MMPs). Tissue inhibitors of metalloproteinases (TIMPs), which have a broad binding range for various enzyme types, control MMPs. The purpose of this study is to quantify and correlate salivary TIMP-1 and 2 levels in children who are caries-active and caries-free.Materials and methodsA total of 40 children, aged 3–6, were split into two groups for the study: 20 in each group (caries-active and caries-free). Saliva samples that had not been stimulated were collected and analyzed using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). Age, gender, and caries score will all be taken into consideration when estimating and correlating TIMP-1 and 2 levels.ResultsTIMP-1 levels were statistically significant at p < 0.001 and ranged from 207.653 ± 51.826 pg/mL for caries-active and from 1042.023 ± 55.247 pg/mL for caries-free. TIMP-2 levels were statistically significant at p < 0.001 and were 169.143 ± 38.700 pg/mL in the caries-active group and 844.300 ± 37.793 pg/mL in the caries-free group. While TIMP-2 levels exhibited a very weak negative association, which was not statistically significant, TIMP-1 levels revealed a statistically significant, strong negative link with the caries score at p < 0.001. In both groups, there was no statistically significant difference between age and gender.ConclusionIn comparison to those who are caries-active, it demonstrated a correlation with elevated levels of TIMP-1, indicating a protective function of this protein against dental caries. TIMP-1 may have a role in preserving dental health and preventing the progression of caries. Therefore, TIMP-1 detection can be considered as a molecular biomarker for early childhood caries (ECC) diagnosis.How to cite this articleThimmegowda U, Sampreetha G. Introduction

Early childhood caries (ECC) is the presence of one or more decayed (noncavitated or cavitated), missing (due to caries), or filled tooth surfaces in any primary tooth of a child 72 months of age or younger.1 When dentinal minerals dissolve and the collagenous organic matrix subsequently degrades, caries causes the loss of hard tissue in the dentin.1

Connective tissue cells are the primary source of extracellular matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), which are Zn2+ and Ca2+-dependent proteases. Due to their capacity to break down nearly all extracellular matrix (ECM) proteins, these endogenous enzymes are crucial for several physiological and pathological processes.2 However, recent studies have demonstrated that MMPs, which are produced by host cells, aid in the disintegration of the dentinal matrix during dental caries. The breakdown of the organic matrix may be significantly aided by host-derived proteolytic enzymes, such as matrix MMPs, which are found in both saliva and dentin.3

Extracellular matrix proteins, such as various collagens, are spontaneously denatured and broken down by MMPs, zinc-dependent proteolytic enzymes. MMPs' biological activity may interact with certain tissue inhibitors of metalloproteinases (TIMPs) or be post-transcriptionally controlled.2 TIMPs have unique tissue expression patterns, regulatory mechanisms, and a range of nonspecific effects on different MMP members. The four TIMPs (TIMP-1–4) are secreted proteins that attach to MMPs and form complexes that prevent MMPs from activating.2,3 On the cell surface, TIMP-1, TIMP-2, and TIMP-4 are found close to membrane-bound proteins. Also, TIMP-3 attaches itself to heparan sulfate and becomes trapped in the ECM.4

Growth factors may have further effects on dentinal matrix modeling by varying the regulation of particular MMPs. Mature human odontoblasts and pulp tissue exhibit MMPs and TIMPs, suggesting that they contribute to the dentinal matrix's structure prior to mineralization.2 TIMP-1 is present in gingival crevicular fluid (GCF), parotid, and submandibular saliva, and it has a homeostatic effect on MMP activities in dental hard tissues and odontogenesis, but not during illness.1,2 Thus, the aim of this study was to quantify the salivary TIMP-1 and 2 levels and to correlate among age and gender group in caries-free and caries-active children in Indian population.

Materials and Methods

Source of Data

Forty children, aged 3–6 years, with and without ECC, participated in the current study. Twenty children each from the caries-active and caries-free groups visited the Pediatric Department, Bengaluru.

Method of Collection of Data

Using a straightforward randomized sample procedure, the patients are chosen. G*Power software v. 3.1.9.7 was used to determine the sample size for this investigation. With an effect size of 0.92 (based on the Cohen effect size to observe a larger difference in the mean TIMP-1 levels between two groups) and a power of 80%, the sample size estimation was carried out at 5% alpha error (α = 0.05). This indicated that a minimum of 40 samples would be required for the current study. Consequently, there will be 20 samples in each group. In this study design, the allocation ratio is 1:1 (Fig. 1).

Allocation group flowchart

The study pro forma included information on the following: age, gender, place of residence, ethnicity, dental hygiene practices, dietary habits (including the frequency of cakes, cookies, and other sweets before meals), history of bottle feeding, chief complaint, and recorded subjective and objective symptoms and clinical findings. Twenty patients with severe ECC in the experimental group (caries-active) and twenty patients without caries in the control group (caries-free) who are between the ages 3 and 6 years were considered for the study once the parents or guardians of the children taking part had given their informed consent. The current study has received institutional ethical approval under the number RRDCH/IEC/2024/103.

Sample Collection

On getting the clarification of all queries from patients and their companions, twenty patients were added to the caries-active group; likewise, twenty patients were added to the caries-free group and blinded for allocation. Children aged 3–6, children with four or more active carious lesions (S-ECC), children without caries, and parents providing research agreement were the inclusion/exclusion criteria. Children who had received topical fluoride treatment within the previous month, children who needed special medical attention, teeth that were pulpally involved, children who had received antibiotic therapy within the previous 3 months, children with genetic diseases or syndromes, and children taking any kind of medication were all excluded.

Caries were diagnosed visually and documented using a modified version of the World Health Organization's oral health survey procedure. The deft index was added, and the severity of caries was recorded using the International Caries Detection and Assessment System (ICDAS) (criteria 4–6). It is the first worldwide standard to offer a standardized research methodology for determining the prevalence and severity of dental caries across various age groups, and it is renowned for being user-friendly. The sample was split evenly into two groups: the caries-free control group (deft = 0) and the caries-active group (deft index ≥ 4).

Unstimulated, complete saliva samples were collected in the morning, at least 2 hours after routine dental brushing, and 1 hour after that to minimize diurnal fluctuations. The patients were asked to sit comfortably with their eyes open, head angled slightly forward, and orofacial movements controlled, and they were also urged to relax for 5 minutes. After allowing their saliva to accumulate in the floor of their mouth, the participants were told to spit into a sterile saliva-collecting vial. Within an hour after collection, the samples were taken to the lab and kept at −80°C in a hermetically sealed box with ice. Prior to the lab process, each sample were numbered. The lab researcher were blindfolded in order to avoid bias in the sample that is collected. Additionally, all patients are blinded to their group assignment. To ascertain the levels of TIMP-1 and TIMP-2, an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) was performed on all 40 samples. Six months were allotted for the completion of the study.

Assay Procedure for Estimation of TIMP-1

A particular antibody is precoated onto a 96-well microplate in the ELISA procedure, which causes the immobilized antibody to bind to the test sample upon its addition. The sandwich complex is supplemented with a secondary antibody coupled with an enzyme such as horseradish peroxidase (HRP). Following incubation, a substrate known as 3,3,5,5-tetramethylbenzidine (TMB) is introduced after unbound components have been removed by washing. The amount of analyte present determines how strongly the enzyme transforms the substrate into identifiable colored products. A stop solution, which turns from blue to yellow at 450 nm, is used to halt the process. In order to prepare, supplies and reagents must be brought to room temperature, surplus microplate strips must be removed, and they must be stored at 4ºC. Wells are filled with samples or standards, allowed to sit at room temperature for an hour, and then filled with 350 μL of 1× Wash Buffer PT and 100 μL of TMB Development Solution. After a minute of shaking the plate, the absorbance at 450 nm is measured as an endpoint.

Assay Procedure for Estimation of TIMP-2

A TIMP-2-specific antibody is first applied to a microplate in order to bind to the immobilized antibody on the microplate wells. A cocktail of antibodies is then introduced, binding to the TIMP-2 that the immobilized antibody has bound to. Following an incubation time, the plate is meticulously cleaned to remove any loose elements. It binds to biotinylated secondary antibody when biotinylated conjugated avidin (HRP-avidin) is added. After a second wash phase to remove unbound HRP-avidin, a TMB substrate solution is added to generate a blue hue. A halting solution, whose color shifts from blue to yellow in direct proportion to the quantity of TIMP-2 present in the sample, stops the reaction. The strength of this color change is correlated with the concentration of the target protein.

Statistical Analysis

Version 22.0 of the Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS) for Windows was made available in 2013. Statistical analyses will be conducted using IBM Corp., Armonk, New York. Frequency and proportions will be used for descriptive analysis of all explanatory and outcome factors for categorical variables, while mean and standard deviation (SD) will be used for continuous variables. The study and control groups were compared based on caries scores in the study group, age and gender, and salivary TIMP-1 and TIMP-2 levels. The independent Student's t-test was employed to compare these data. The gender distribution of the two groups was compared using the Chi-square test. The association between the research group's salivary TIMP-1 and TIMP-2 levels and caries scores was estimated using Pearson's correlation test. Each group's caries scores were utilized to predict the salivary TIMP-1 and lysozyme levels using simple linear regression analysis. A p = 0.05 was used as the significance threshold.

Results

Based on their bottle-feeding history, sugar intake, plaque levels, and cavitation-related enamel abnormalities, participants with S-ECC are included in this study.

Using an independent Student's t-test, the mean salivary TIMP-1 levels of the caries-active and caries-free groups were compared. The study group's TIMP-1 levels were 207.653 ± 51.826 pg/mL, while the control group's levels were 1042.023 ± 55.247 pg/mL. The difference was statistically significant at p < 0.001. The study group's TIMP-2 levels were 169.413 ± 38.700 pg/mL, whereas the control group's levels were 844.300 ± 37.793 pg/mL. The difference was statistically significant according to the data (Table 1).

Discussion

The release of proteins known as TIMPs results in the formation of complexes with MMPs that stop all MMPs from activating. The total activity of any MMP is indicated by the ratio of free TIMPs to activated enzymes. Maintaining this balance is essential since any disturbance results in proteolysis.1 Therefore, TIMPs limit ECM remodeling by opposing MMPs and preventing their action. Saliva has been demonstrated to be a useful, noninvasive technique for detecting and diagnosing a variety of pediatric conditions. Therefore, in this analysis, saliva has been considered for the association of TIMP levels.

When we compared the mean salivary TIMP levels in our study, we discovered that the TIMP levels were statistically significantly higher in the caries-free group than in the caries-active group. Higher levels of TIMPs are seen in caries-free groups because it is likely that they help protect the collagen matrix and preserve the structural integrity of enamel and dentin.3,4

In a similar study, TIMP-1 and TIMP-2 were found in radicular dentin and were observed to be more widely dispersed in the inner dentin layer near the pulp. Normal radicular dentin contains TIMPs, which function as natural protease inhibitors. Additionally, it has been found that TIMPs in the root canal can block the MMP, hence reducing the degradation of the dentin matrix.5 According to a study on MMP-1 levels in GCF, periodontitis patients had higher MMP levels and lower TIMP-1 levels than healthy participants. The GCF levels of MMP-1 and TIMP-1 also significantly improved after treatment because MMP-1 binds to free TIMP, which lowers inflammation.6,7

A similar study was conducted to determine that MMPs and TIMPs are widely expressed in mature human odontoblasts and pulp tissue, suggesting that they are involved in the organization of the dentin matrix prior to mineralization.7,8 Through the variable regulation of certain MMPs, growth factors may also control dentin matrix modeling.9 On the contrary, research found that there was no discernible change in the TIMP-1 concentration in the oral fluids of patients with periodontitis or gingivitis compared to healthy persons, and that TIMP-1 levels were considerably lower in those with periodontal disease. TIMP-1 and MMP-9 levels in saliva were considerably greater in primary Sjögren's syndrome (pSS) patients than in healthy participants, according to a research conducted on patients with pSS.10,11 TIMP-1 and TIMP-2 are involved in ECM remodeling during cancer invasion, and their expression and distribution in oral squamous cell carcinoma were examined.12

When we correlated the caries score with TIMP levels in the caries-active group, it was found that TIMP-1 exhibited a statistically significant negative strong connection. For example, TIMP-1 levels were lower in the caries-active group than in the caries-free group, suggesting that TIMP-1 is important for preventing dental caries. In contrast, TIMP-2 levels exhibited a very weak negative connection with TIMP-1 levels.

In addition to predicting and avoiding risk assessment, linear regression analysis may evaluate the impact of interventions and lower the risk of dental cavities in one group relative to another. The results of the current study's simple linear regression analysis for salivary TIMP-1 levels using caries scores among the caries-active group show that the salivary TIMP-1 level drops by 11.454 pg/mL for every increase in caries scores, a finding that was statistically significant at p = 0.002. The caries scores accounted for 42% of the variation in salivary TIMP-1 levels. This regression assists in forecasting the severity of dental caries and is effective for encouraging a preventative strategy.

In the current study, the levels of TIMPs-1 and 2 were compared between males and females. The caries-free group of males had greater levels of TIMP-1 than the females, whereas the caries-active group had lower levels. Similar research on serum MMP-8 and TIMP-1 concentrations was conducted in a population-based study, wherein TIMP-1 concentrations marginally rose for both genders in the entire population, whereas MMP-8 levels fell for both women and the general population.13 Contrary to our published research, which showed that women have greater levels of MMP-8 and TIMP-1 than males.13,14 The number and activity of cells linked to innate immunity vary between the sexes. Males have larger frequencies of natural killer (NK) cells than females. Male neutrophils and macrophages had lower phagocytic activity than females. When it comes to displaying peptides, female antigen-presenting cells (APCs) outperform male APCs. It is believed that women's inherent immune responses are stronger.12

Tissue inhibitor matrix metalloproteinase levels were greater in the caries-free group of ages 3–4, according to the study's age-wise comparison, which yielded results without statistical significance. People's immune systems become dysregulated as they age, making them more prone to illnesses. Age appears to have little effect on the number of neutrophils, but it does decrease their ability to perform some jobs, such as phagocytic capability and chemotaxis.14,15

In our study, we compared the caries score ≤ 5 to the caries score > 5, and we observed that the TIMP-1 levels were greater in the caries score ≤ 5 group than in the caries score > 5 group. Additionally, TIMP-2 levels were greater in those with caries scores < 5 compared to those with scores > 5. One explanation would be that TIMPs protect teeth from more dentinal deterioration by counteracting MMPs, which degrade ECM. As a result, TIMP levels rise as the caries score falls.16

There were no unintended effects or harms in this study.

Clinical Significance

Salivary TIMP levels may be linked to caries susceptibility, according to this study, and lowering TIMP levels may help identify dental caries in young children and prevent dental caries altogether. It can also serve as a biomarker for early caries detection.

Limitation

This study's drawback was that, while it did ascertain how variations in pH affected the amounts of salivary TIMPs, it may have also included two additional TIMP kinds. Since TIMPs are known to neutralize MMPs, a comparison between MMP and TIMP may have been made in this study. Additionally, the study's sample size was too small to be broadly applied. To generalize the findings across populations, more research may be conducted with larger study samples.

Conclusion

According to the current study, TIMP-1 and 2 were detected in groups with greater caries-free levels, and the findings were statistically significant. There was a considerable negative link between TIMP-1 and severe ECC, but TIMP-2 revealed a very minor negative correlation. Furthermore, there was no statistically significant difference between TIMP levels across age and gender categories. TIMPs can thus be utilized as a possible biomarker and to prevent dental cavities in young children.

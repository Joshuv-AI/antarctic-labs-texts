Multichannel SSA decomposition in a hybrid SSA–N-BEATS framework for complex time series forecasting
Authors: Alfian Adi Pratama, Putriaji Hendikawati
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Forecasting high-resolution time series remains challenging because of nonlinear behavior, multiple seasonalities, and high-frequency noise. Deep learning architectures such as N-BEATS offer strong forecasting capability, yet their performance often deteriorates when trained directly on raw signals that mix multiple temporal characteristics. Hybrid frameworks integrating decomposition with neural networks offer a practical solution, though effectiveness depends on the stability of the underlying data. This paper benchmarks the N-BEATS (baseline) model against two Hybrid SSA–N-BEATS scenarios: Hybrid SSA–N-BEATS (denoising) and Hybrid SSA–N-BEATS (multichannel). In the denoising scenario, singular spectrum analysis (SSA) is applied solely for denoising, isolating deterministic signals before network training. In the multichannel scenario, SSA decomposes the series into trend and seasonal subsignals, each processed in parallel through an independent N-BEATS channel. The proposed evaluation protocol assesses model behavior across stable periods and periods marked by structural level shifts. This provides a systematic framework for identifying when decomposition-based hybridization is practically beneficial. This work makes the following methodological contributions:•Provides a decomposition-based benchmarking framework for forecasting complex time series.•Introduces both denoising-based and multichannel SSA–N-BEATS pipelines.•Demonstrates that hybrid model effectiveness depends on temporal stability and structural level shifts.

Background

Electric load data exhibit nonlinear characteristics, multiple seasonal patterns, trend components, and high-frequency noise [1]. These characteristics complicate electric load forecasting. Prediction accuracy remains critical in power systems, affecting operational planning, cost efficiency, and supply stability [2,3]. Deep learning architectures have been widely adopted to improve forecasting accuracy because they can learn complex temporal patterns. However, mixed temporal characteristics in raw data can disrupt the model learning process and reduce accuracy [4]. A hybrid approach combining time series decomposition and denoising, therefore, breaks complex data into cleaner, deterministic subcomponents, allowing the model to focus on the main signal [1,4].

The effectiveness of the hybrid approach hinges on accurately separating the deterministic signal from noise components [5]. This article, therefore, proposes a computational benchmarking framework comparing three modeling scenarios: (1) N-BEATS (baseline), (2) Hybrid SSA–N-BEATS (denoising), and (3) Hybrid SSA–N-BEATS (multichannel). The framework integrates singular spectrum analysis (SSA), the deep residual N-BEATS architecture, and Bayesian tree-structured Parzen estimator (TPE) optimization within a single, structured evaluation flow.

First, within this framework, SSA uses weighted correlation as an auto-grouping mechanism to objectively separate deterministic signal matrices from stochastic noise [5,6]. These decomposition results are then fed into two hybrid scenarios. Second, the denoised deterministic signal is directly trained by the N-BEATS network. Third, the deterministic component is split further into trend and seasonal subsignals, each processed in parallel through their own N-BEATS channel. This multichannel configuration lets each subsignal be modeled separately through channels tailored to its temporal characteristics, representing trend and seasonal patterns more effectively than N-BEATS (baseline) [7]. To ensure objectivity, each scenario’s hyperparameters are optimized using Bayesian optimization based on the TPE through the Optuna framework [8,9]. The data are then divided into an in-sample phase for training and validation and an out-of-sample phase for prediction evaluation [10]. The three scenarios’ performance is then evaluated using the mean absolute percentage error (MAPE), mean absolute error (MAE), root mean square error (RMSE), and coefficient of determination (R2) [11]. This framework yields a structured, objective, and reproducible forecast evaluation flow. All three modeling scenarios were implemented in Python using the Darts library (built on PyTorch). Hyperparameter optimization was executed on a cloud GPU instance via the RunPod platform to manage the high computational cost of parallel Bayesian optimization, while final model training was performed locally. The implementation used Python 3.10.10, Darts 0.36.0, PyTorch 2.5.1, PyTorch Lightning 2.3.0, and Optuna 3.6.2, as specified in the repository's pinned `requirements.txt` for exact reproducibility.

Method details

Framework implementation begins by ensuring that the time series has a consistent time format and contains no missing values before modeling starts. The data are then divided into in-sample and out-of-sample phases. The in-sample phase supports model training and validation, while the out-of-sample phase is reserved entirely for prediction evaluation. The data are split chronologically, with the validation set positioned at the end of the training data to preserve temporal continuity. This approach lets parameter optimization proceed without disrupting the time series’ temporal structure, and it remains consistent with standard forecasting evaluation practice, which keeps test data completely separate from model training and validation [10]. Fig. 2 summarizes the N-BEATS (baseline) implementation stages: parameter search space definition, Bayesian hyperparameter optimization via Optuna, model training with optimized parameters, and optimized model formation.Step 2: Hybrid SSA–N-BEATS (denoising) ImplementationSub-step 2.1: In the hybrid SSA–N-BEATS (denoising) scenario, SSA first processes the in-sample series to separate the deterministic structure from the noise components [6]. This process begins by forming a trajectory matrix X through an embedding operation using window length L, where the number of columns equals K=N−L+1. The window length is chosen on the basis of the data’s dominant periodic characteristics, since it influences the separability of the decomposition components [13,14]. In this study, L was set to 336, corresponding to one full weekly cycle at the 30-minute sampling resolution of the electric load data (7 days × 48 observations per day). This choice reflects the dominant weekly periodicity typically observed in electric load patterns, ensuring that both the trend and harmonic seasonal components remain separable within the trajectory matrix. Once the trajectory matrix is formed, the covariance matrix S=XXT is computed and decomposed through SUi=λiUi, where λi is the i th eigenvalue and Ui its corresponding eigenvector [13,15]. A diagonal averaging process then reconstructs the decomposition results into a set of reconstructed components (RCs), used in the component identification stage.Sub-step 2.2: Once all RCs are obtained, the weighted correlation matrices W=[Wij] are computed, where Wij denotes the weighted correlation between components RCi and RCj [13,15]. A threshold then identifies pairs of harmonic components with strong correlation, and components meeting this criterion are selected for the reconstruction process [14,15]. Specifically, a threshold of τ=0.90 was applied to the weighted correlation between adjacent reconstructed components (RCs); component pairs meeting or exceeding this value were considered harmonic and retained as part of the deterministic signal, while the remaining components were treated as stochastic noise. This threshold was fixed at τ = 0.90 across all scenarios and evaluation periods, rather than tuned empirically or adapted per dataset. The value was chosen a priori to require a strong weighted correlation between adjacent components before classifying them as a harmonic pair, providing a conservative and consistent criterion for separating deterministic structure from stochastic noise.Sub-step 2.3: A diagonal averaging process reconstructs the selected components into a denoised signal [13,15]. This reconstruction process is generally expressed as y˜=∑i∈GRCi, where G denotes the set of selected components. The SSA-based denoising strategy improves signal representation quality prior to forecasting [[16], [17], [18]].Sub-step 2.4: The SSA-reconstructed signal serves as input to the N-BEATS model’s hyperparameter optimization process. Sub-step 3.1: The deterministic components obtained from SSA are subsequently separated into a trend component and a seasonal component. In SSA, the first reconstructed component (RC1) is commonly associated with the dominant low-frequency structure because it originates from the eigentriple with the largest singular value, which typically represents the largest variance contribution in the time series [6,15]. Consequently, RC1 is widely interpreted as the principal trend component in SSA-based decomposition, whereas oscillatory behavior is generally represented by subsequent reconstructed components that form harmonic pairs [6,15]. Following this principle, the proposed framework deterministically assigns RC1 to the trend group Gt, without applying the weighted correlation criterion to this component. The remaining reconstructed components (RC2 onward) are then examined using the weighted correlation matrix. Specifically, each adjacent pair (R,CiRCi+1) whose weighted correlation satisfies the threshold τ=0.90, as described in Sub-step 2.2, is assigned to the seasonal group Gs, reflecting the paired eigentriple structure characteristic of harmonic oscillations in SSA. Finally, the trend and seasonal signals are reconstructed as Ytrend=∑j∈GtRCi and Yseasonal=∑j∈GsRCj, where Gt denotes the trend component group and Gs denotes the seasonal component group.

The evaluation uses four metrics: MAE, RMSE, MAPE, and coefficient of determination (R²).Sub-step 4.1: Calculate MAE, RMSE, MAPE, and R² from the predicted results and actual values in the out-of-sample period. MAE is calculated as MAE=1N∑t=1N|yt−y^t|, and RMSE is calculated as RMSE=1N∑t=1N(yt−y^t)2. MAPE is calculated as MAPE=100%N∑t=1N|yt−y^tyt|. The coefficient of determination is calculated as R2=1−∑t=1N(yt−y^t)2∑t=1N(yt−y¯)2. MAE measures the average absolute error; RMSE measures the average error with greater weight on large deviations; MAPE expresses the error as a percentage; and R² measures the model’s ability to explain variation in the actual data. Models with smaller MAE, RMSE, and MAPE values and larger R² values are considered to perform better [11].

These results indicate that the relative performance of each modeling scenario can vary depending on the characteristics of the test period. This performance reversal is consistent with the disruptive nature of the December period. Because SSA reconstructs the deterministic signal from historical regularities, it smooths out sudden deviations from the established pattern, causing the hybrid models to respond more slowly to the abrupt level shift introduced by the holiday-driven demand change. The N-BEATS (baseline) model, which is trained directly on the raw signal without this smoothing step, retains the full variance of the data and therefore adapts more readily to the sudden change. Expressed in hours, all three scenarios remain practical to retrain and generate forecasts, completing in under 1.5 h — a substantially smaller cost than the search phase itself, which ranged from roughly 5 to 17 h (Table 14). This indicates that the primary computational burden of the hybrid framework lies in the one-time hyperparameter search rather than in recurring model training, making the approach practical for periodic retraining once suitable hyperparameters have been identified.

Limitations

The proposed method relies on the SSA decomposition process, including the choice of window length (L) and the component clustering strategy, both of which can affect the signal reconstruction and prediction performance. In multichannel scenarios, the separation of trend and seasonal components also depends on the clustering procedure. Furthermore, the Bayesian optimization process increases computational requirements relative to the N-BEATS (baseline) model. Validation in this study used electrical load data, so generalizing the method to other time series domains still requires further testing. Future work could extend the evaluation protocol to other domains exhibiting comparable trend–seasonal structure, such as demand forecasting or commodity price series, and to additional disruption periods beyond the year-end holiday season examined here, such as Ramadan or other culturally-driven demand shifts, to further test the boundary conditions under which decomposition-based hybridization remains beneficial. In addition, since the current SSA grouping mechanism relies on a fixed correlation threshold (Sub-step 2.2), incorporating adaptive or data-driven grouping strategies — for instance through change-point detection to identify structural shifts prior to decomposition — represents a promising direction for improving the hybrid framework's responsiveness during disruption periods.

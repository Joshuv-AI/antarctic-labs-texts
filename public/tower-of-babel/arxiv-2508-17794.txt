Abstract
. We study the concept of idempotence for relative monads, which exhibits several subtleties not present for non-relative monads. In particular, there is a bifurcation of notions of idempotence in the relative setting, which are indistinguishable for idempotent monads. As a special case, we obtain several characterisations of idempotence for monads in extension form.

Remark 2.12

. We say that a
j
-relative adjunction is a
resolution
of a
j
-relative monad
T
if it induces
T
in this way
[
AM24
, Definition 5.25]
; a morphism of resolutions is a functor
c\colon C\to C^{\prime}
between the apices of the relative adjunctions commuting with the left and right adjoints. In particular, the
j
-relative adjunctions
f_{T}\mathrel{\dashv_{j}}u_{T}
and
k_{T}\mathrel{\dashv_{j}}v_{T}
of
Examples

3.2
and
3.3
are resolutions of
T
(in fact, they are the initial and terminal resolutions respectively
[
AM24
, Corollary 6.41 & Corollary 6.51]
).

An adjunction is
idempotent
if it induces an idempotent monad
[
MS82
, 10]
. We introduce the analogous concepts for relative adjunctions.

Definition 3.5
.

A relative adjunction is
(algebraically) idempotent
if it induces an (algebraically) idempotent relative monad.

We shall not attempt to give a full characterisation of idempotent relative adjunctions analogous to the non-relative setting
[
MS82
, Proposition 2.8]
. However, the following special case will be important.

Definition 3.6

(
[
Die75
, §4]
)
.

A functor
r\colon C\to E
is
j
-reflective
(or is a
j
-reflection
) if it is fully faithful and admits a left
j
-relative adjoint
\ell\colon A\to C
.

Note in particular that
j
-reflections are
resolute
in the sense of
[
Ark22
, Definition 6.1.4]
, i.e. the left
j
-relative adjoint
\ell
is also left-adjoint to
r
relative to the composite
r\ell
.

Proposition 3.7

(cf.
[
Die75
, §4]
)
.

Every
j
-reflection is idempotent.

Proof.

Suppose
\ell
is left
j
-adjoint to
r
. The operator of the induced
j
-monad is given by
E(j,r\ell)\cong C(\ell,\ell)\cong E(r\ell,r\ell)
.
∎

The converse does not hold: that is, not every idempotent relative adjunction is a relative reflection (indeed, this is not true even for non-relative monads). Nonetheless, relative reflections appear to be an important notion for (algebraically) idempotent relative monads, as we shall show.

3.1.
Free algebras and reflective resolutions

We fix a functor
j\colon A\to E
and a
j
-relative monad
T=(t,{\dagger},\eta)
for the remainder. Observe that the underlying functor
t\colon A\to E
factors as

A\to\mathbf{Kl}(T)\to\mathbf{Im}(t)\to E

where
A\to\mathbf{Im}(t)\to E
is the full image factorisation of
t
into an identity-on-objects functor followed by a fully faithful functor. The identity-on-objects functor
\mathbf{Kl}(T)\to\mathbf{Im}(t)
sends each morphism
f\colon ja\to tb
to
f^{\dagger}\colon ta\to tb
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
T=(t,{\dagger},\eta)
be an relative monad.
T
is idempotent if and only if the canonical functor
\mathbf{Kl}(T)\to\mathbf{Im}(t)
is fully faithful, hence an isomorphism.

Proof.

The canonical identity-on-objects functor is given on morphisms by
{\dagger}
. Hence
{\dagger}
is invertible if and only if the canonical functor is fully faithful.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

The following are equivalent.

(3.9.(1))

T
is idempotent.

(3.9.(2))

The Kleisli category
\mathbf{Kl}(T)
forms a
j
-reflective resolution
k_{T}\mathrel{\dashv_{j}}v_{T}
of
T
.

(3.9.(3))

T
admits a
j
-reflective resolution.

Proof.

(1)
\implies
(2). If
T
is idempotent, then the functor
v_{T}\colon\mathbf{Kl}(T)\to\mathbf{Im}(t)
is fully faithful by
Proposition

3.8
, while
\mathbf{Im}(t)\to E
is fully faithful by definition, so the composite is also fully faithful.

(2)
\implies
(3). Trivial.

(3)
\implies
(1). Immediate from
Proposition

3.7
.
∎

Corollary 3.10

(cf.
[
Die75
, §4]
)
.

If
T
is idempotent, then the Kleisli category
\mathbf{Kl}(T)
is initial amongst
j
-reflective resolutions of
T
.

Proof.

k_{T}\mathrel{\dashv_{j}}v_{T}
is initial amongst all resolutions of
T
. Hence, when
T
is idempotent, so that
k_{T}\mathrel{\dashv_{j}}v_{T}
is
j
-reflective, it is furthermore initial amongst
j
-reflective resolutions.
∎

3.2.
Algebras and reflective resolutions

To provide the promised analogue of
Item

1.9.(5)
for relative monads, we give a characterisation of idempotent algebras in terms of morphisms of algebras.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
(e^{\prime},\rtimes^{\prime})
be a
T
-algebra.
(e^{\prime},\rtimes^{\prime})
is idempotent if and only if, for every
T
-algebra
(e,\rtimes)
, every morphism from
e
to
e^{\prime}
in
E
is a
T
-algebra morphism.

Proof.

Suppose that
(e^{\prime},\rtimes^{\prime})
is idempotent, and let
f\colon ja\to e
be a morphism. We have
\epsilon f^{\rtimes}\eta_{a}=\epsilon f
using the unit law for
e
. Thus,
\epsilon f^{\rtimes}=(\epsilon f^{\rtimes}\eta_{a})^{\rtimes^{\prime}}=(\epsilon f)^{\rtimes^{\prime}}
using idempotence of
(e^{\prime},\rtimes^{\prime})
, so that
\epsilon
is a
T
-algebra morphism.

Conversely, suppose that every morphism from the carrier of a
T
-algebra to
e^{\prime}
is a
T
-algebra morphism, and let
f\colon tx\to e^{\prime}
be a morphism.
ta
is the carrier of the free
T
-algebra on
a\in A
so that the
T
-algebra morphism law with respect to
\eta_{a}\colon ja\to ta
gives that
(f\eta)^{\rtimes^{\prime}}=f{\eta_{a}}^{\dagger}=f
using the unit law for the relative monad. Thus
(e^{\prime},\rtimes^{\prime})
is idempotent.
∎

Proposition 3.12

(cf.
[
Die75
, §4]
)
.

Suppose that
T
is idempotent. Then
\mathbf{Alg}_{\mathrm{idem}}(T)\to E
is
j
-reflective. Furthermore, it is terminal amongst reflective resolutions of
T
.

Proof.

Since
T
is idempotent, free algebras are idempotent, so that
f_{T}\colon A\to\mathbf{Alg}(T)
factors through
\mathbf{Alg}_{\mathrm{idem}}(T)
. This forms a relative adjunction for the same reason as
\mathbf{Alg}(T)
, and it is trivially a resolution of
T
(abstractly, this follows from full faithfulness of
\mathbf{Alg}_{\mathrm{idem}}(T)\to\mathbf{Alg}(T)

[
AM24
, Remark 5.33]
). Furthermore, the forgetful functor
\mathbf{Alg}_{\mathrm{idem}}(T)\to\mathbf{Alg}(T)\to E
is full by
Lemma

3.11
, hence fully faithful. The proof of terminality goes through in essentially the same way as in the classical case, observing that, for a
j
-reflective resolution of
T
, the right
j
-adjoint
r\colon C\to E
induces an idempotent
T
-algebra structure on
rc
for each
c\in C
.
∎

Corollary 3.13
.

T
is algebraically idempotent if and only if the forgetful functor
u_{T}\colon\mathbf{Alg}(T)\to E
is
j
-reflective.

Proof.

If
T
is algebraically idempotent, then
\mathbf{Alg}_{\mathrm{idem}}(T)\to\mathbf{Alg}(T)
is an isomorphism, from which
j
-reflectivity follows from
Proposition

3.12
. Conversely, if
u_{T}
is full, every
T
-algebra is idempotent by
Lemma

3.11
.
∎

For idempotent (non-relative) monads, every algebra is free, i.e. the comparison functor
\mathbf{Kl}(T)\to\mathbf{Alg}(T)
is essentially surjective, hence an equivalence. This property is necessary to prove, for instance, that every reflective adjunction is monadic. In contrast, this phenomenon no longer holds for (even algebraically) idempotent relative monads.

Example 3.14

(Algebraic idempotence
\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\implies}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\implies}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\implies}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\implies}}}
every algebra is free)
.

Let
E
be the free category on the following graph.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces e}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

The unique induced
(1\to E)
-relative monad
T
is idempotent, as is the unique algebra structure on
e
, hence
T
is algebraically idempotent. The Kleisli category of
T
is the trivial category. The category of algebras has two objects:
(t,{\dagger})
and
(e,\rtimes)
, which are not isomorphic since
f
is not invertible. Consequently the comparison functor
\mathbf{Kl}(T)\to\mathbf{Alg}(T)
is not an equivalence.

Consequently, for an algebraically idempotent relative monad
T
, we have that
v_{T}\colon\mathbf{Kl}(T)\to E
and
u_{T}\colon\mathbf{Alg}(T)\to E
are both

-reflective by

and
3.13
, but

is not necessarily
j
-relatively monadic in the sense of
[
AM25
, Definition 4.1]
. Therefore, it is not generally true that
j
-reflections are
j
-relatively monadic.

3.3.
Algebraic idempotence from idempotence

We conclude by establishing sufficient conditions for a relative monad to be algebraically idempotent as soon as it is idempotent. First, we present a method to deduce the algebraic idempotence of one relative monad from the algebraic idempotence of another (whose verification in practice is expected to be simpler). Recall from
Example

1.4
that, given functors
j\colon A\to E
and
j^{\prime}\colon E\to I
, every
j^{\prime}
-relative monad
T
induces a
j^{\prime}j
-relative monad
Tj
by precomposing
j
. Furthermore, every
T
-algebra induces a
Tj
-algebra, inducing a functor
\mathbf{Alg}(T)\to\mathbf{Alg}(Tj)
that commutes with the forgetful functors
[
AM24
, Proposition 6.58]
.

Definition 3.15

(
[
Ark22
, §5.4]
)
.

Let
j\colon A\to E
and
j^{\prime}\colon E\to I
be functors. A
j^{\prime}
-relative monad
T
is
j
-ary
if the canonical functor
\mathbf{Alg}(T)\to\mathbf{Alg}(Tj)
is an equivalence.

Conceptually, a
j^{\prime}
-relative monad
T
is
j
-ary if it is presented by the data of a
j^{\prime}j
-relative monad.

Proposition 3.16
.

Let
j\colon A\to E
and
j^{\prime}\colon E\to I
be functors and let
T
be a
j^{\prime}
-relative monad. If
T
is
j
-ary, then
T
is algebraically idempotent if and only if
Tj
is algebraically idempotent.

Proof.

Under the assumption that
T
is
j
-ary, we have a commutative triangle as follows.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbf{Alg}(T)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbf{Alg}(Tj)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces u_{T}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces u_{Tj}}

Consequently, each of the functors
u_{T}
and
u_{Tj}
is fully faithful if and only if the other is, which characterises algebraic idempotence by
Corollary

3.13
.
∎

Next, we observe that idempotence of a monad may in some situations be inferred from idempotence of the relative monad induced by precomposing a functor, giving a partial converse to
Example

1.4
.

Proposition 3.17
.

Let
j\colon A\to E
be a functor and let
T=(t,\mu,\eta)
be a monad on

. If
t
exhibits the left extension
j\mathbin{\rhd}(tj)
of
tj\colon A\to E
along
j
, then
T
is idempotent if and only if
Tj
is idempotent.

Proof.

Suppose that
Tj
is idempotent. Naturality of
\eta
implies the following diagram commutes for all
a\in A
.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces tja}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces ttja}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces ja}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces tja}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t\eta_{ja}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta_{ja}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta_{ja}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta_{tja}}

Since

is idempotent, precomposition with

exhibits a bijection of hom-sets, and so the above implies

. By assumption, we have that precomposition by

induces an isomorphism

[E,E](t,tt)\cong[E,E](j\mathbin{\rhd}(tj),tt)\cong[A,E](tj,ttj)

and

.
∎

We deduce that, for relative monads with
well-behaved
roots in the sense of
[

, Definition 4.1]
, which correspond to monads relative to free cocompletions
[

, 215]
, there is no distinction between idempotence and algebraic idempotence.

Corollary 3.18
.

Let
\Phi
be a class of small categories and let
A
be a small category. Denote by
\phi_{A}\colon A\to\overline{\Phi}(A)
the free cocompletion of
A
under
\Phi
-colimits. A
\phi_{A}
-relative monad is idempotent if and only if it is algebraically idempotent.

Proof.

Let
T
be a
\phi_{A}
-relative monad. By
[
ASS25
, Theorem 8.3]
,
T
extends via left extension along
\phi_{A}
to a
\phi_{A}
-ary monad
(\phi_{A}\mathbin{\rhd}T)
on
\overline{\Phi}(A)
. This monad thus satisfies the assumptions of

3.17
, so that it is idempotent if and only if
T
is idempotent. Furthermore, by
Lemma

2.11
,
(\phi_{A}\mathbin{\rhd}T)
is idempotent if and only if it is algebraically idempotent. Finally, by
Proposition

3.16
,
(\phi_{A}\mathbin{\rhd}T)
is algebraically idempotent if and only if
T
is algebraically idempotent.
∎

Lower Bounds in Algebraic Complexity via Symmetry and Homomorphism Polynomials
Prateek Dwivedi IT-Universitetet i København, Benedikt Pago University of Cambridge, Tim Seppelt IT-Universitetet i København

Abstract
Valiant’s conjecture from 1979 asserts that the circuit complexity classes 𝖵𝖯 \mathsf{VP} and 𝖵𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{VNP} are distinct, meaning that the permanent does not admit polynomial-size algebraic circuits. As it is the case in many branches of complexity theory, the unconditional separation of these complexity classes seems elusive. In stark contrast, the symmetric analogue of Valiant’s conjecture has been proven by Dawar and Wilsenach (2020): the permanent does not admit symmetric algebraic circuits of polynomial size, while the determinant does. Symmetric algebraic circuits are both a powerful computational model and amenable to proving unconditional lower bounds. In this paper, we develop a symmetric algebraic complexity theory by introducing symmetric analogues of the complexity classes 𝖵𝖯 \mathsf{VP} , 𝖵𝖡𝖯 \mathsf{VBP} , and 𝖵𝖥 \mathsf{VF} called 𝗌𝗒𝗆𝖵𝖯 \mathsf{symVP} , 𝗌𝗒𝗆𝖵𝖡𝖯 \mathsf{symVBP} , and 𝗌𝗒𝗆𝖵𝖥 \mathsf{symVF} . They comprise polynomials that admit symmetric algebraic circuits, skew circuits, and formulas, respectively, of polynomial orbit size. Having defined these classes, we show unconditionally that 𝗌𝗒𝗆𝖵𝖥 ⊊ 𝗌𝗒𝗆𝖵𝖡𝖯 ⊊ 𝗌𝗒𝗆𝖵𝖯 . \mathsf{symVF}\subsetneq\mathsf{symVBP}\subsetneq\mathsf{symVP}. To that end, we characterise the polynomials in 𝗌𝗒𝗆𝖵𝖥 \mathsf{symVF} and 𝗌𝗒𝗆𝖵𝖡𝖯 \mathsf{symVBP} as those that can be written as linear combinations of homomorphism polynomials for patterns of bounded treedepth and pathwidth, respectively. This extends a previous characterisation by Dawar, Pago, and Seppelt (2025) of 𝗌𝗒𝗆𝖵𝖯 \mathsf{symVP} . The separation follows via model-theoretic techniques and the theory of homomorphism indistinguishability. Although 𝗌𝗒𝗆𝖵𝖡𝖯 \mathsf{symVBP} and 𝗌𝗒𝗆𝖵𝖯 \mathsf{symVP} admit strong lower bounds, we are able to show that these complexity classes are rather powerful: They contain homomorphism polynomials which are 𝖵𝖡𝖯 \mathsf{VBP} - and 𝖵𝖯 \mathsf{VP} -complete, respectively. Vastly generalising previous results, we give general graph-theoretic criteria for homomorphism polynomials and their linear combinations to be 𝖵𝖡𝖯 \mathsf{VBP} -, 𝖵𝖯 \mathsf{VP} -, or 𝖵𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{VNP} -complete. These conditional lower bounds drastically enlarge the realm of natural polynomials known to be complete for 𝖵𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{VNP} , 𝖵𝖯 \mathsf{VP} , or 𝖵𝖡𝖯 \mathsf{VBP} . Under the assumption 𝖵𝖥𝖯𝖳 ≠ 𝖵𝖶 ⁡ [ 1 ] \mathsf{VFPT}\neq\mathsf{VW}[1] , we precisely identify the homomorphism polynomials that lie in 𝖵𝖯 \mathsf{VP} as those whose patterns have bounded treewidth and thereby resolve an open problem posed by Saurabh (2016).

Introduction

Algebraic complexity theory aims to classify families of polynomials according to the size of their smallest algebraic circuit representation.
An
algebraic circuit
is a directed acyclic graph whose internal vertices represent the addition and multiplication gates and whose input gates are labelled with variables or field constants. The circuit computes a polynomial by propagating the computation from the input gates to the output gate through the internal gates. The size of the circuit is defined as the total number of gates and wires it contains.
Two canonical examples of polynomials are the determinant
\det_{n}=\sum_{\pi\in\Sym_{n}}(-1)^{\sgn(\pi)}\prod_{i\in[n]}x_{i\pi(i)}
, which admits polynomial-size circuits, and the permanent
\perm_{n}=\sum_{\pi\in\Sym_{n}}\prod_{i\in[n]}x_{i\pi(i)}

[

Symmetric depth reductions.

6.2.1
Bounded treedepth homomomorphism polynomials are in
\mathsf{symVF}

We begin by proving that homomorphism polynomials of patterns of bounded treewidth can be computed by matrix-symmetric rigid formulas of polynomial orbit size.

Lemma 6.13
.

Let
F
be a bipartite multigraph of treedepth
d
, and let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\hom_{F,n,m}
can be computed by a
\Sym_{n}\times\Sym_{m}
-symmetric rigid formula of size at most
(|V(F)|\cdot|E(F)|\cdot(n+m))^{d}
, support size at most
d
, and depth at most
d
.

Proof.

Let
T
be an elimination tree of
F
. We construct a formula
C
for
\hom_{F,n,m}
by induction on
T
.
For every
u\in V(T)
, let
P(u)\subseteq V(T)=V(F)
denote the vertices appearing in the unique path from the root of
T
to
u
(excluding
u
).
In the circuit
C
, we create, for each
u\in V(T)
and each bipartition-preserving map
\gamma\colon P(u)\to[n]\uplus[m]
, a gate
g_{u,\gamma}
that is the root of a subformula
C_{u,\gamma}
. This subformula is defined as follows. Let
E(u)\subseteq E(F)
denote the set of all edges
uv
in
F
, for a
v\in P(u)
. Assume that
u
is in the left side of the bipartition of
F
; in the other case, replace
h\colon\{u\}\to[n]
with
h\colon\{u\}\to[m]
, and
x_{h(u)\gamma(w)}
with
x_{\gamma(w)h(u)}
in the expression below.

C_{u,\gamma}\coloneqq\sum_{h\colon\{u\}\to[n]}\prod_{uw\in E(u)}x_{h(u)\gamma(w)}\cdot\prod_{v\in uE(T)}C_{v,\gamma\cup h}.

To enforce rigidity of the circuit, we apply the following trick. Fix some bijection
\ell\colon uE(T)\to[|uE(T)|]
.
In the actual circuit, we multiply each
C_{v,\gamma\cup h}
in the product
\prod_{v\in uE(T)}C_{v,\gamma\cup h}
with
1^{\ell(v)}
. This does not change the semantics but it ensures that no circuit automorphism can permute the subcircuits appearing in this product. This removes potential non-trivial circuit automorphisms that move internal gates while keeping all input gates fixed.

Note that
C
thus defined is indeed a formula because every subformula
C_{v,\gamma\cup h}
has a unique parent in
C
. This is, for the parent
u
of
v
in
T
, the product gate in
C_{u,\gamma}
for the particular choice of
h
in the sum in
C_{u,\gamma}
.

Claim 6.13a
.

For every
u\in V(T)
and every
\gamma\colon P(u)\to[n]\uplus[m]
,
the subformula

C_{u,\gamma}
is a rigid
\StabP(\gamma(P(u)))
-symmetric formula.

Proof of Claim.

This is shown by induction on the structure of
T
. If
u
is a leaf, then the claim is clearly true for
C_{u,\gamma}
.
Now suppose
u
is a non-leaf node of
T
. By induction, each
C_{v,\gamma\cup h}
appearing in
C_{u,\gamma}
is
\StabP(\gamma(P(u))\cup\{h(u)\})
-symmetric. Likewise,
\prod_{uw\in E(u)}x_{h(u)\gamma(w)}
is
\StabP(\gamma(P(u))\cup\{h(u)\})
-symmetric. Hence, the sum over all
h\colon\{u\}\to[n]
is
\StabP(\gamma(P(u))
-symmetric because its summands are symmetric to each other. Also by induction, each
C_{v,\gamma\cup h}
is rigid, and this is still true for
C_{u,\gamma}
: By our circuit construction, every non-trivial automorphism of
C_{u,\gamma}
has to permute summands of the outer sum. But then it moves some input gate
x_{h(u)\gamma(w)}
to another input gate
x_{h^{\prime}(u)\gamma(w)}
with
h^{\prime}(u)\neq h(u)
. Therefore,
C_{u,\gamma}
is rigid.
∎

The claim shows that
\gamma(P(u))
is a support for the sum and product gates appearing in
g_{u,\gamma}
. Thus, the support size of
C
is indeed at most
d
, since
|P(u)|\leq d
.
For
u
being the root of
T
,
P(u)=\emptyset
, so by the claim, the total circuit
C
is
\Sym_{n}\times\Sym_{n}
-symmetric, as desired.
It is clear by definition that it computes
\hom_{F,n,m}
, see also the proof of
[
KPR23
, Theorem 11]
.

It remains to estimate the size of
C
. By induction we can see that
|C_{u,\gamma}|\leq(|V(F)|\cdot|E(F)|\cdot(n+m))^{c}
, where

is the height of the subtree of
T
rooted at
u
: Indeed, if
u
is a leaf, then
|C_{u,\gamma}|\leq|E(F)|\cdot(n+m)
. If
u
is an internal node with a height
c
subtree, then, using the induction hypothesis,

\displaystyle|C_{u,\gamma}|

\displaystyle\leq(n+m)\cdot|V(F)|\cdot|E(F)|\cdot(|V(F)|\cdot|E(F)|\cdot(n+m))^{c-1}

\displaystyle=(|V(F)|\cdot|E(F)|\cdot(n+m))^{c}

Here, the factor
|V(F)|
is an upper bound on
|uE(T)|
, the number of subcircuits
C_{v,\gamma\cup h}
, and the factor
|E(F)|
is an upper bound on the size of the product
\prod_{uw\in E(u)}x_{h(u)\gamma(w)}
.
∎

Corollary 6.14
.

Fix
d\in\mathbb{N}

Let

Apply

to each individual homomorphism polynomial in the linear combination

for each

. Then the formula

6.2.2
\mathsf{symVF}
computes bounded-treedepth homomorphism polynomials

Let
(C_{n,m})_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}
be a family of matrix-symmetric formulas of orbit size at most
(n+m)^{d}
, where
d\in\mathbb{N}
is some constant.
Our goal is to show that the polynomials computed by the
C_{n,m}
are linear combinations of homomorphism polynomials for patterns of constant treedepth.
By
Theorem

6.1
, the treewidth of the patterns is constant.
In order to also bound the treedepth, we introduce the notion of
support depth
of symmetric circuits, and prove two results: Firstly, symmetric formulas of polynomial orbit size must have constant support depth.
Secondly, constant support depth of the circuits also implies constant treedepth of the patterns whose homomorphism polynomials are computed by them.

Bounding the support depth of symmetric formulas.

Let
C
be a
\Sym_{n}\times\Sym_{m}
-symmetric formula. Call a gate
g
in
C

large
if there exist at least
\min\{n,m\}/2
distinct children of
g
that are all in the same
\Stab(g)
-orbit.
Every child of
g
whose
\Stab(g)
-orbit has size at least
\min\{n,m\}/2
is called a
central
child of
g
.

Lemma 6.15
.

Let
C
be a
\Sym_{n}\times\Sym_{m}
-symmetric formula. Assume that there is a path from the root to an input gate that passes through
d
large gates, where it always proceeds with one of its central children. Then
\operatorname{maxOrb}(C)\geq\frac{1}{2^{d}}\cdot(\min\{n,m\})^{d}
.

Proof.

We show the following claim by induction:
Let
P
be a path starting in a large gate
g
and ending in an input gate that passes through
d
large gates in total, and always proceeds with a central child of a large gate. Then the subformula rooted at
g
contains a gate whose
\Stab(g)
-orbit has size at least
\frac{1}{2^{d}}\cdot(\min\{n,m\})^{d}
.

For
d=1
, we do not need to make use of
P
. Let
g
be the large gate that
P
starts in. Then
g
has
\geq\min\{n,m\}/2
children that are in the same
\Stab(g)
-orbit, so there is a gate with
\Stab(g)
-orbit size at least
\frac{1}{2}\cdot\min\{n,m\}
in the subformula rooted at
g
.

Now consider the case
d+1
and let
g_{1}
be the large gate that
P
starts in. Let
g_{2}
be the next large gate on the path. By the induction hypothesis, the subformula rooted at
g_{2}
contains a gate, say,
g^{*}
, whose
\Stab(g_{2})
-orbit has size at least
\frac{1}{2^{d}}\cdot(\min\{n,m\})^{d}
. Because
P
proceeds from
g_{1}
with a central child
h
, there are at least
n/2
children
h^{\prime}
of
g_{1}
such that the subformula rooted at
h^{\prime}
is
\Stab(g_{1})
-symmetric to the one rooted at
h
.
Consider an arbitrary such
h^{\prime}=\pi_{h^{\prime}}(h)
, for a
\pi_{h^{\prime}}\in\Sym_{n}\times\Sym_{m}
. Then in the subcircuit rooted at the large gate
\pi_{h^{\prime}}(g_{2})
, there appears the gate
\pi_{h^{\prime}}(g^{*})
, and its
\Stab(\pi_{h^{\prime}}(g_{2}))
-orbit has size at least
\frac{1}{2^{d}}\cdot(\min\{n,m\})^{d}
.
So, with each
h^{\prime}=\pi_{h^{\prime}}(h)\in\Orb_{\Stab(g_{1})}(h)
, we can associate a different gate
\pi_{h^{\prime}}(g^{*})
with large orbit in the subformula rooted at
h^{\prime}
. Since all these gates are in the same
\Stab(g_{1})
-orbit, that orbit has size at least
\frac{1}{2}\cdot\min\{n,m\}\cdot\frac{1}{2^{d}}\cdot(\min\{n,m\})^{d}=\frac{1}{2^{d+1}}\cdot(\min\{n,m\})^{d+1}
. This finishes the inductive step.
∎

The above lemma turns a lower bound on the number of large gates on a path into an orbit size lower bound for symmetric formulas. It remains to relate this to the supports of the gates.

In a rigid
\Sym_{n}\times\Sym_{m}
-symmetric circuit
C

g

if
g
has a child
h
such that
\sup(h)\setminus\sup(g)\neq\emptyset
.
Every such child

is then called a

of

. We call a path from the root of
C
to an input gate
central
if, for every support-changing gate
g\in P
, the successor of
g
in
P
is a support-changing child of
g
.

Definition 6.16

(Support depth)
.

Let
C
be a
\Sym_{n}\times\Sym_{m}
-symmetric circuit.
Let
\mathcal{P}
denote the set of all paths from the root of

The

of a path

is the number of gates

such that

contains a support-changing child of

The

of a

-symmetric circuit

Lemma 6.17
.

Let
C
be a rigid
\Sym_{n}\times\Sym_{m}
-symmetric formula such that
\operatorname{maxSup}(C)\leq\min\{n,m\}/2
.
Let

be a support-changing gate.
Then

is large and every support-changing child of

is central.

Proof.

Since

is support-changing, let

be one of its children with
\sup(h)\setminus\sup(g)\neq\emptyset
.
Assume that
\sup_{L}(h)\setminus\sup_{L}(g)\neq\emptyset
.
The case where
\sup_{R}(h)\setminus\sup_{R}(g)\neq\emptyset
is analogous, where

has to be replaced by

.
Since we are assuming that
|\sup(h)_{L}|\leq n/2
,
we have

\displaystyle|\Orb_{\StabP(\sup(g))}(\sup(h)_{L}\setminus\sup(g)_{L})|

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\left\{S\subseteq[n]\setminus\sup(g)_{L}:|S|=|\sup(h)_{L}\setminus\sup(g)_{L}|\right\}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\geq n/2.

Every element of
\Orb_{\StabP(\sup(g))}(\sup(h)_{L}\setminus\sup(g)_{L})
corresponds to a different child of

\StabP(\sup(g))
-orbit.
Since

-orbit. Hence,

fulfils our above definition of a large gate, and every support-changing child is central.
∎

Let

-symmetric formula such that

Let

Now we are going to prove that a symmetric formula

The result follows essentially from the proof of

Lemma 6.19
.

Let

.
Let

\Sym_{n}\times\Sym_{m}
-symmetric circuit.

]

In the setting of

The general case is that

If

Let

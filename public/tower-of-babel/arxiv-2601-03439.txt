The Zubarev Double Time Green’s function -A Vintage Many Body Technique
Vijay Singh, Shraddha Singh

Abstract
These lecture notes present a comprehensive and powerful many-body technique pioneered in 1960 by D. N. Zubarev. The technique, known as the Zubarev Double Time Green’s Function method, was used extensively by leading solid state physicists such as John Hubbard and Laura Roth in the 1960s. We present the technique and apply it to the non-interacting electron and boson gas. We next consider the (many-body) Hubbard model and show how it yields the Stoner criterion for ferromagnetism. It is easily extendable to superconductivity and related problems. Our treatment is pedagogical and understandable to those with just an elementary understanding of second quantization.

Introduction

The development of Quantum Many Body Techniques employing second quantized formalism had their early beginnings in the 1950s. These were inspired by, but by no means clones of the perturbative and diagrammatic approach in quantum field theory. Pioneering approaches in any field tend to be tentative, exploratory and even error prone. So, Zubarev’s path breaking paper

Physical Picture

In this section we help the reader to develop a conceptual understanding of the present work. To a beginner, the Green’s function in Eq.(

In passing, we mention that the Green’s function is variously referred to as the kernel, correlation function, resolvent, propagator or locator and each of these terms has a special significance and specific meaning. \displaystyle H=\mu\sum_{i\sigma}n_{i\sigma}-t\sum_{i\neq j\sigma}^{n.n}c^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}c_{j\sigma}+\frac{U}{2}\sum_{i\sigma}n_{i\sigma}n_{i-\sigma},

Equation of motion

Let’s see how one may obtain the Green’s function. Note
\hbar=1

A(t)=exp(-iHt)A(0)exp(iHt)

\displaystyle i\frac{d}{dt}A=[A(t),H]_{-},

\displaystyle i\frac{d}{dt}G(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta(t-t^{\prime})\langle[A(t),B(t^{\prime})]_{\eta}\rangle-i\theta(t-t^{\prime})\langle[A(t),H]_{-}B(t^{\prime})-\eta B(t^{\prime})[A(t),H]_{-}\rangle

This exercise yields a hierarchy of higher order Green’s functions. Unless the Hamiltonian is simple (see Sec.

\langle\langle[[A(t),H]_{-},H]_{-};B(t^{\prime})\rangle\rangle^{r}\approx\alpha\langle\langle[A(t),H]_{-};B(t^{\prime})\rangle\rangle^{r}

or

In hindsight we should not be surprised. The many body problem is essentially intractable and this is reflected in this infinitely recursive situation of the Zubarev technique. In related many body techniques it shows up in the form of an infinite series or an infinite set of Feynman diagrams. Before we take up cases of how we actually deal with this, we need to develop the formalism further.

3.2
Spectral Representation of the Green’s Function

The Green’s function was defined in terms of the

. It is customary to express this

in terms of the eigen functions
\{|\phi_{n}\rangle\}
of the Hamiltonian. This exercise is called the spectral decomposition and we carry it out presently. We begin with the Schrodinger equation,

\displaystyle H\phi_{n}=E\phi_{n},

Taking the expectation value of the the operators occurring in the correlation function, we get the following equations,

\displaystyle F_{BA}(t-t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Z^{-1}tr(B(t^{\prime})A(t)e^{-\beta H})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Z^{-1}\sum_{n}\langle\phi_{n}|B(t^{\prime})A(t)e^{-\beta H}|\phi_{n}\rangle

Next, we introduce a complete set of eigen states to obtain,

\displaystyle F_{BA}(t-t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Z^{-1}\sum_{n,m}\langle\phi_{n}|B(t^{\prime})|\phi_{m}\rangle\langle\phi_{m}|A(t)e^{-\beta H}|\phi_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Z^{-1}\sum_{n,m}\langle\phi_{n}|e^{iHt^{\prime}}B(0)e^{-iHt^{\prime}}|\phi_{m}\rangle\langle\phi_{m}|e^{iHt}A(0)e^{-iHt}|\phi_{n}\rangle e^{-\beta E_{n}}

\displaystyle Z^{-1}\sum_{n,m}e^{i(E_{n}-E_{m})t^{\prime}}B_{nm}e^{i(E_{m}-E_{n})t}A_{mn}e^{-\beta E_{n}}

\displaystyle F_{BA}(t-t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Z^{-1}\sum_{n,m}e^{-i(E_{n}-E_{m})(t-t^{\prime})}B_{nm}A_{mn}e^{-\beta E_{n}},

(7)

where,

\displaystyle B_{nm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{n}|B(0)|\phi_{m}\rangle

\displaystyle A_{mn}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{m}|A(0)|\phi_{n}\rangle

Similarly,

\displaystyle F_{AB}(t-t^{\prime})=Z^{-1}\sum_{n,m}e^{i(E_{n}-E_{m})(t-t^{\prime})}B_{mn}A_{nm}e^{-\beta E_{n}}

3.5
Revisiting the Equation of Motion

We will rewrite the equation of motion in the energy domain. Fourier transforming the left hand side of Eq.(
6
) and integrating by parts

L.H.S.

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}i\frac{d}{dt}G^{r}(t-t^{\prime})e^{i\omega(t-t^{\prime})}d(t-t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2\pi}G^{r}(t-t^{\prime})e^{i\omega(t-t^{\prime})}\bigg|_{-\infty}^{\infty}+\frac{\omega}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}G^{r}(t-t^{\prime})e^{i\omega(t-t^{\prime})}d(t-t^{\prime})

The second term is simply
\omega G^{r}(\omega)

The first terms are boundary terms. They vanish at both limits. As

\omega\rightarrow\omega+i\epsilon

(t>0)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\delta(t-t^{\prime})e^{i\omega(t-t^{\prime})}\langle[A,B]_{\eta}\rangle d(t-t^{\prime})+\langle\langle[A,H]_{-};B\rangle\rangle^{r}_{\omega}

Remarks
\colon

1.) The choice of operators which go into the Green’s function must be judicious. Our choice in Eq.(
18

\langle n_{k\sigma}\rangle

2.) In general, for an interacting or complex system we expect

\displaystyle G^{r}_{kk^{\prime}\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2\pi(\omega-\epsilon_{k}-\sum_{k\sigma}(\omega))},

where
\sum_{k\sigma}(\omega)
is the self energy. Here the energy of elementary excitation is
\omega-\epsilon_{k}-Re(\sum_{k\sigma}(\omega)))
and the lifetime is
\hbar/\text{Im}(\sum_{k\sigma}(\omega))

3.) Note that the Green’s function is an inverse function. It is first encountered in electrostatics where it is used to obtain the electrostatic potential due to a point charge. This is also true for Zubarev’s double time Green’s function. Although not apparent in Eq.(
1
) it becomes clear in the fourier transform version in Eq.(
19
)

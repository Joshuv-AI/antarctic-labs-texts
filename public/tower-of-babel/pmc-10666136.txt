Experimental Investigation on Layer Subdivision Water Injection in Multilayer Heterogeneous Reservoirs
Authors: Lifei Dong, Pinzhi Zhong, Qi Zhang, Miao Wang, Wenzhuo Dong, Youqi Wang, Chao Yang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractLayer subdivision is currently one of the principal techniques employed to solve interlayer contradictions during water injection in multilayer heterogeneous reservoirs, but experimental research on the mechanism and the corresponding implementation plans is lacking. In this study, a multilayer heterogeneous core model was designed, and physical simulation experiments with different subdivisions and variation coefficients were conducted. Furthermore, the influence of the subdivision degree on the water injection development effect was analyzed, and the dynamic characteristics of the oil displacement of each sublayer were compared. Finally, the boundary standard of the layer subdivision was discussed. The results demonstrate that the water breakthrough appears rapidly in the high-permeability layer, and water channeling is easily formed after water flooding. The remaining oil is mostly retained in the low-permeability layer. Layer subdivision water injection can effectively reduce the high water cutoff and improve the oil recovery. The oil recovery increases with the layer subdivision degree, while the incremental rate decreases gradually. The low-permeability layer is observed to be the main layer contributing to the increase in the total recovery of multilayer reservoirs, with a contribution rate that initially increases and subsequently decreases with the increasing subdivision degree, from 0.9 to 11.9, and 29.8%, and subsequently drops to 15.1% during the general water injection with 2-, 4-, and 8-layer subdivision mining. The heterogeneity variation coefficient of 0.55 is used as the subdivision standard to divide different permeability layers to be developed as 1 layer in the multilayer heterogeneous reservoir.

Introduction

At present, vertical well water injection is widely used to supplement the stratum energy. However, due to the heterogeneity of the reservoir, the “tongued advance” and “fingering” phenomena commonly occur in the high-permeability layers.1−4 Therefore, the interlayer/layer development contradiction during water injection in multilayer heterogeneous reservoirs is prominent, while the distribution of remaining oil is scattered, and the development effect is poor.5 To improve the overall development effect of water flooding oilfields, water injection in high-permeability layers should be controlled, and water injection in medium- and low-permeability layers should be strengthened.6 Therefore, the development of layered water injection technology to achieve effective water injection in multiple oil layers is crucial in improving water flooding recovery during the high water-cut stage, as well as other stages.7−14

As early as the beginning of the 20th century, numerous countries began to study layer water injection technology. Multilayer oil reservoirs were developed based on the up-back stepwise production mode in the United States, Canada, Australia, and the Middle East.15 Due to the excellent petrophysical properties of the reservoirs, which have good homogeneity and little difference in the physical properties of different layers, the number of divided sections during its development is typically low (generally 2 sections for simultaneous production), and the effect of unified developing is well. Thus, in-depth research on the development technology of multilayer heterogeneous oil reservoirs is limited.16 Layered water injection technology in Daqing Oilfield has been the subject of research and industrial applications in China since the 1960s.17,18 Three generations of layered water injection supporting technologies, namely, “fixed/movable water distribution”, “conventional eccentric water distribution”, and “bridge-type eccentric + subdivision water injection + efficient measurement and adjustment”, have been developed.19−21 Layered water injection technology can be described briefly as separating layers by a packer and distributing water by a water distributor. Thus, the fixed/movable water distribution means the water distribution is completed by the fixed/movable water distribution string, and the water distribution string is fixed or movable in the well (shown in Figure 1a).19 The conventional eccentric water distribution means the water distribution is controlled by the expansion packers and eccentric water distributors (shown in Figure 1b).20 The “bridge-type eccentric + subdivision water injection + efficient measurement and adjustment” means the water distribution string is composed of the bridge-type eccentric water distributors and high-efficiency metering device (shown in Figure 1c).21 Following this, oilfields including Shengli, Dagang, Liaohe, Changqing, Jilin, Jidong, and Tuha have also carried out research on layered water injection technology, accompanied by large-scale applications of bridge-type eccentric layer water injection technology.22−26 Moreover, Shengli, Dagang, Changqing, Jidong, Tuha, and other oilfields have improved bridge-type concentric layered water injection technologies for highly deviated wells.27−31

Layered water injection supporting technologies. (a) Fixed/movable water distribution, (b) conventional eccentric water distribution, and (c) bridge-type eccentric water distribution.

Numerous studies have been conducted on the development of layer subdivision water injection. For example, Cui (2002) designed a new type of upstream pressure controller washing piston and sealing surface protection mechanism, with the ability to effectively solve the interlayer collusion problem existing in water injection development oilfield cleaning.32 Deng (2002) developed a new bridge-type eccentric layer water injection and testing process, overcoming the large errors in the conventional eccentric water distribution technology by testing the flow rate based on the decreasing method and improving the efficiency of the layered flow adjustment.33 The original eccentric injection and matching adjustment technology is associated with problems including large errors in the layered flow test, a low deployment efficiency in multilayer wells, a large workload for the single-layer pressure test, long cycles, and data deviation. To overcome these problems, Hou (2007) proposed the bridge-type eccentric layered water injection theory and supporting testing technology.34 In addition, Ren et al. (2008) proposed a new eccentric subdivision water injection process string with a free-throwing water nozzle, constant flow dispenser, and verified packer sealing performance, greatly improving the testing efficiency and maintenance.35 Based on the characteristics of water injection wells in Jiyuan Oilfield, Ju et al. (2012) investigated multilayer subdivision water injection pipe strings and supporting key tools, enhancing the overall development effect of the reservoir.36 Yue et al. (2014) developed an integrated water distribution packer to improve the sealing performance and life of the pipe string in the dispensing process.37 Duan et al. (2022) obtained a series of combination parameters of subdivision water injection interval, which can guide the subdivision water injection program, effectively alleviate the range of production decline and water cut rise, and provide technical support for fine tapping potential of water flooding in an ultrahigh water cut period.38

Extensive research has been performed on layer water injection technology to overcome the strong heterogeneity of multilayer sandstone reservoirs. However, the majority of studies focus on the development of water injection tools (strings, separators, etc.) and the corresponding processes, while the research on the mechanism of sublayer water injection development technologies and the corresponding implementation plans of multilayer heterogeneous reservoirs is relatively lacking, and there is no report on it with the experimental method.

In the current study, water injection experiments on the layer subdivision of the multilayer heterogeneous model were performed to evaluate the effect of reducing the water cut and improving the water flooding recovery. Furthermore, key technical issues including the layer subdivision degree and layer subdivision standard were analyzed based on (1) the multilayer heterogeneous model, (2) a comparison of the oil displacement performance of each layer, and (3) the water injection development effect of the sublayer series with different variation coefficients. The results act as a reference for practical applications of the multilayer heterogeneous oil reservoir sublayer water flooding development.

Experimental Design

Experimental Equipment and Process

Based on the pressure and space resources, 2 parallel pressure chambers (with equal pressure) were employed in the experiment, and 4 cores were placed in parallel in each pressure chamber. Since all the cores are in the same injection system and the influence of gravity during the experiment can be ignored, the 8 cores can be regarded as a multilayer heterogeneous model with 8 layers without interlayer exchange. Figure 3 shows a schematic of the experimental setup.

Devices’ connection of water flooding experiments.

Considering the convenience of the experimental operation and the space of the high-pressure chamber, the 8 homogeneous sandstone cores are placed in 2 connected high-pressure chambers, which have the same pressure in each chamber. Each high-pressure chamber has 4 homogeneous sandstone cores, which are controlled by the 6-way valve on the injection side.

The 6-way valve has 6 connected flow ports, each of which can be controlled to open or close. One of the 6 connected flow ports can be the entrance, and the others can be the exit. In this experiment, 8 homogeneous sandstone cores are taken as a multilayer heterogeneous model, so two 6-way valves are used. Each 6-way valve is connected to 1 import and 4 outputs. The import is connected to the piston container, and the output is connected to the homogeneous sandstone core (shown in Figure 2).

Experimental Scheme Design

The experimental purpose includes 2 parts: (1) the influence of the layer subdivision degree on the oil recovery and (2) the determination of the layer subdivision boundary. The specific experimental procedure is shown as follows.

Influence of the Layer Subdivision Degree on the Oil Recovery

To analyze the recovery potential of the layer subdivision, different degrees of layer subdivision were tested. As we already know, a multilayer heterogeneous reservoir can be divided into different parts during oil development; the layer subdivision degree means the number of parts after division. For example, an 8-layer heterogeneous reservoir can be divided into 1 part (8 layers as a part), 2 parts (4 layers as a part), 4 parts (2 layers as a part), or 8 parts (1 layer as a part) during development. The larger the number of parts is, the higher the layer subdivision degree. In our work, general water injection (1 part), 2-layer subdivision injection (2 parts), 4-layer subdivision injection (4 parts), and 8-layer subdivision injection (8 parts) experiments were carried out. The oil recovery under each subdivision degree was analyzed, and the influence of each subdivision degree on the multilayer heterogeneous reservoir was determined. Results and Discussion

Distribution Characteristics of the Remaining Oil Following the General Water Injection

Figure 4 describes the proportion of the water output of each layer to the total water output of the model (contribution rate of water output) in the general water injection stage.

Percentage contribution of each layer to the water cut during the general water injection stage.

As revealed in Figure 4, during the general water injection process of the multilayer heterogeneous model, the water breakthrough occurred first in layer 1 with the highest permeability of 1043.7 × 10–3 μm2, followed by layer 2, with a permeability of 821.5 × 10–3 μm2. The higher the permeability, the faster the appearance of water breakthrough in the same development layer. Initially, it spiked to 100% before stabilizing at around 50% with further injection. While that of the relatively low permeability layer (layers 5–8) is very low. As the water flooding develops, the water breakthrough layer forms a water flow channel, and the subsequent water directly enters the outlet end along the water breakthrough layer. Thus, the impact on the low-permeability layer is limited. In addition, the low-permeability layer has relatively high oil-driving conditions. Under the same injection–production pressure difference, the majority of the remaining oil in the multilayer heterogeneous reservoirs remains in the low-permeability sublayer and is difficult to produce.

Influence of the Layer Subdivision on the Oil Recovery

Different degrees of water injection were implemented on the layer subdivision for the multilayer heterogeneous model. This includes 1-, (general water injection), 2-, 4-, and 8-layer water injection. Figure 5 presents the oil displacement performance.

Water flooding performance of the layer subdivision in the multilayer heterogeneous model.

Figures 5 and 6 reveal the water cut of the heterogeneous model to exceed 98% during the general water injection stage. Starting from the 2-layer subdivision and onward, the water cut and water channeling have decreased, and the recovery has significantly improved. That is because the higher permeability layers (sublayer1-sublayer4) and the lower permeability layers (sublayer5-sublayer8) are separated, and the influence of water channeling in higher permeability layers is eliminated for the lower permeability layers. As the low-permeability layer alone is used as a water injection layer, the remaining oil located inside can be extracted more efficiently, and thus, the overall oil recovery has improved.

Total recovery change for the multilayer heterogeneous model.

Figure 7 presents the water flooding recovery increments at different subdivision levels in the heterogeneous model. The model is subdivided into 2 layers after the water injection, and the recovery has increased by 8%. However, the 8-layer subdivision mining following the 4-layer subdivision exhibits an oil recovery rate enhancement of 3.59% only. With the increase of the layer division degree, the interference of each layer becomes smaller, and the remaining oil in the lower permeability layer can be displaced more, which means the oil displacing development comes from the relatively low-permeability layers. At a certain number of subdivisions, the water cut and recovery of each layer are close to a maximum. At this point, further subdividing the layers has a minimal effect on reducing the overall water cut and improving the recovery. Subdividing the layers costs a lot; the more the layer division does, the higher the cost is. Meanwhile, doing more layer division makes the mining cycle long, which results in a long cost recovery. Also, equipment maintenance costs increase. Thus, the benefits, input cost, and effective cycle must be considered during the comprehensive design of the actual oil production scheme design comprehensively. A reasonable layer subdivision boundary and standard should be determined.

Oil recovery improvement of water flooding following the subdivision layer in the multilayer heterogeneous model.

Oil Displacement Efficiency Analysis

The results reveal the ability of the water flooding development in the layer subdivision to reduce the water channeling degree of heterogeneous oil reservoirs and improve their total recovery. This is principally attributed to dividing the reservoir into small layers with different physical properties for mining and the adjustment of the overall water cut and production conditions.

Figure 8 reveals the improvement of the oil displacement efficiency at each layer following the subdivision of the multilayer heterogeneous model. The smaller the permeability, the more obvious are the variations in displacement efficiency under the same subdivision stage. Before the layer subdivision, the low-permeability sublayer exhibits the lowest oil displacement efficiency and largest remaining oil potential. Thus, the effect of low-permeability sublayer after-layer subdivision is obvious.

Oil displacement performance of the layer subdivision in the multilayer heterogeneous model.

Figure 9 compares the contribution of the oil displacement efficiency of each sublayer to the overall recovery at the subdivision stages.

Contribution percentage of each layer to the recovery at different stages.

The contribution of the oil displacement efficiency at each layer is a function of the layer subdivision degree. During the general water injection, layer 1 with higher permeability can contribute up to 29.7% (higher recovery), while layer 8 with lower permeability can contribute only 0.9% (minor recovery). When 2 layers are subdivided for production, layers 1 and 8 are developed separately as different layers. The contribution rate of high-permeability layer 1 to the total recovery is reduced to 1.3% only (the remaining oil has a lower potential to be produced), while the contribution rate of low-permeability layer 8 exhibits a marked increase, reaching 11.9% (remaining oil has a larger potential to be produced). Similarly, when the 4 layers are subdivided, the contribution rates of layers 1 and 8 are 1.2 and 29.8%, respectively. This reveals that the greater the layer division, the greater the improvement of the low-permeability sublayer oil displacement efficiency. However, when the subdivision reaches a certain level, the production degree of each sublayer in the formation system is already high, and the contribution of the low-permeability sublayer to the total recovery becomes less obvious. For example, for the case of layer 8 with low permeability, following the general water injection with 2-, 4-, and 8-layer subdivision mining, the contribution rate to the total oil recovery increases from 0.9 to 11.9 and 29.8%, and subsequently drops to 15.1%, respectively.

Determination of Layer Subdivision Boundary

The layer subdivision can reduce the influence of water channeling in the high-permeability layer compared to the other layers, thus improving the overall production effect of the multilayer heterogeneous reservoir. However, the effect of reducing water channeling in the high-permeability layer on other layers is related to the layer subdivision, particularly the boundary standard of the layer subdivision. More specifically, sublayers with similar petrophysical properties (permeability) should be adequately divided into the same layer. The standard (boundary) of layer subdivision is also the key technical parameter for the implementation of the subdivision measures.

Figure 10 depicts the oil displacement performance of the layer subdivision scheme in Table 4, whereby 3 groups of multilayer heterogeneous models with different heterogeneity variation coefficients were divided into 2 layers for the water injection development after general water injection.

Oil displacement performance of the layer subdivision in the multilayer heterogeneous model.

After a 98% water cut by the general water injection, subdivisions with varying heterogeneous variation coefficients can reduce the water cut and improve the total recovery of the model. However, the water cut reduction and recovery improvements vary with the subdivision scheme. Comparing the variations in the water flooding recovery for the 3 experimental groups (Figure 11) reveals that the water flooding recovery in the general water flooding stage of the 3 heterogeneous model groups is essentially equal. Furthermore, the 2-layer subdivision mining stage exhibits the highest ultimate recovery after the general water injection, with a variation coefficient of 0.46 (Scheme 1). The mining effect is the most obvious for this scheme.

Water flooding oil recovery of the layer subdivision in the multilayer heterogeneous model.

Figure 12 reveals that for the multilayer heterogeneous model with the same physical properties, the oil recovery improvement of scheme 1 (with a variation coefficient of 0.46) is 8.0%, which is higher than that of scheme 2 (with a variation coefficient of 0.70) and scheme 3 (with a variation coefficient of 0.84). This indicates that the oil recovery improvement is limited by the variation coefficient of 0.55: if the variation coefficient is less than 0.55, the sublayer water injection will have a larger oil recovery improvement, and if the variation coefficient exceeds 0.55, the oil recovery improvement will be small. Therefore, the variation coefficient of 0.55 can be selected as the subdivision standard of the multilayer heterogeneous reservoir.

Conclusions

Based on the current deficiencies for experimental research on the mechanism and the corresponding implementation plans of layer subdivision water injection in multilayer heterogeneous reservoirs, we analyzed the influence of the subdivision degree on the water injection development effect of multilayer reservoirs and discussed the boundary standard of the layer subdivision in this study. The key findings are described in the following points:

1When general water injection was performed on the multilayer heterogeneous reservoir, the water breakthrough appeared faster in the high-permeability sublayer. Following the formation of the water flow channel, the higher the water contribution rate, the smaller the impact on the low-permeability sublayer. In addition, the sublayer with low permeability exhibits relatively high oil driving conditions, and the majority of the remaining oil in the multilayer heterogeneous reservoirs remains in the low-permeability sublayer.

When general water injection was performed on the multilayer heterogeneous reservoir, the water breakthrough appeared faster in the high-permeability sublayer. Following the formation of the water flow channel, the higher the water contribution rate, the smaller the impact on the low-permeability sublayer. In addition, the sublayer with low permeability exhibits relatively high oil driving conditions, and the majority of the remaining oil in the multilayer heterogeneous reservoirs remains in the low-permeability sublayer.

2Layer subdivision water injection can effectively reduce the water cut of multilayer heterogeneous reservoirs after the general water injection and improve the corresponding oil recovery. The oil recovery of the multilayer heterogeneous reservoir increases with the layer subdivision degree, while the oil recovery improvement gradually decreases. Considering the enhanced cost, mining cycle, and mining difficulty due to the layer subdivision degree, a suitable layer subdivision degree in the actual development process must be determined.

The oil recovery of the multilayer heterogeneous reservoir increases with the layer subdivision degree, while the oil recovery improvement gradually decreases. Considering the enhanced cost, mining cycle, and mining difficulty due to the layer subdivision degree, a suitable layer subdivision degree in the actual development process must be determined.

3For the case of the low-permeability sublayer in the multilayer heterogeneous oil reservoirs, when the sublayer water injection is performed following the general water injection, the oil displacement efficiency is improved to a greater extent than that of high-permeability layers. Moreover, the smaller the layer subdivision, the more obvious the oil displacement efficiency of the low-permeability sublayer, and the greater the contribution rate to the total recovery. When the layer subdivision reaches a certain number, the contribution rate of the low-permeability layer to the total oil recovery decreases.

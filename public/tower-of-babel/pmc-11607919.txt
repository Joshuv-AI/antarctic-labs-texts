Provider assisted partner notification utilization and associated factors among adolescents living with HIV in Uganda, a cross-sectional study
Authors: Irene Kinera-Kagimu, Richard Katuramu, Joanita Nangendo, Fred C. Semitala, Nicolette Nabukeera-Barungi, Aggrey Dhabangi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAssisted Partner Notification (APN) is a high yield HIV testing strategy that is recommended by the World Health Organization (WHO) for adults and adolescents aged at least 15 years in sub-Saharan Africa for HIV care and prevention. Although studies among adults have been done, there remains a paucity of data of APN service utilization among adolescents in Uganda, yet Adolescents Girls and Young Women (AGYW) have the highest incidence of HIV in Uganda, and generally, adolescents have the lowest HIV viral suppression rates. Failure to disclose to sexual partners poses a risk of propagating infection. This study aimed to explore utilization of provider APN and associated factors among sexually active adolescents living with HIV (ALWH) aged 15–19 years in routine HIV care.MethodsWe conducted a cross-sectional study at Baylor College of Medicine Children’s Foundation Uganda’s Center of Excellence Clinic located at Mulago Hospital in Kampala, Uganda. We consecutively enrolled sexually active ALWH aged 15 to 19 years in routine HIV care. We collected data on the individual ALWH, partner characteristics, and APN service utilization, using a structured questionnaire. Medical history for the ALWH was abstracted from the electronic database at the facility. Chi square tests and modified poisson regression were used for analysis of associated factors.ResultsFrom February to March 2023, 372 ALWH were enrolled. The median (IQR) age was 17 years [16–19], 52.4% were females, and 82% had completed secondary school. Of the 372 participants, (49%) consented to utilize APN, mostly via the passive-referral approach (41.4%), and as a last resort via a provider referral (12.4%). The factors associated with increased likelihood of provider partner notification utilization included relationship duration ≥ 2years [adjusted prevalence ratio (aPR), 1.3, (1.05 to 1.60)]; being doubly orphaned (aPR 1.54, 1.12 to 2.30); not watching pornography (aPR 2.16, 1.5 to 3.11).ConclusionsThere was low utilization of APN services among sexually active adolescents with HIV. Passive referral was the preferred method. While acceptable, provider APN was not the preferred method for partner notification among adolescents. There is need to develop adolescent-friendly APN strategies to improve utilization of this important intervention.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12887-024-05277-x.

Background

Globally, adolescents represent a growing population of individuals infected with HIV with an estimated 1.65 million adolescents aged 10–19 years living with HIV in 2022 [1, 2]. Adolescent girls and young women in sub-Saharan Africa (SSA) are inordinately affected, and accounted for 63% of all new HIV infections in 2021 [3]. Notably, in 2022 there were 166,000 adolescents and young people living with HIV in Uganda. In the same year, new infections among adolescent girls and boys aged 10 to 19 years were reported to be 8000 and 600 respectively [4]. Despite the huge discrepancy in HIV prevalence between males and females, males often engage in high risk sexual behaviors such as having multiple sexual partners, not using condoms, and drug and alcohol use thereby increasing their chances of transmitting/ acquiring HIV [5]. Adolescence is a critical stage of transition between childhood and adulthood, during which different behavioral experimentation including sexual activity and risk taking occur [6]. Regarding sexual behavior, Adolescents Living with HIV (ALWHIV) in Uganda were found to have a sexual activity prevalence of 34% and another study found that even after the diagnosis of HIV, adolescents had higher prevalence of high risk sexual behavior [7, 8]. Further, ALWHIV have poorer adherence to antiretroviral therapy, consequently having poorer treatment outcomes. Those aged 16–19 years are more likely to have viral non suppression [9]. This therefore puts their sexual partners at an increased risk of contracting HIV, thus perpetuating the epidemic.

Generally, sexual partners of People Living with HIV (PLHIV) in SSA have an HIV prevalence ranging from 35 to 72%, which when compared with the general population is seven times higher [10]. It is important that prevention interventions that increase awareness of one’s HIV status, particularly the status of the sexual partner of a PLHIV are scaled up [11].

Assisted Partner Notification (APN) for HIV, is a public health strategy in which trained healthcare providers offer assistance for PLHIV to notify their sexual partners of potential exposure to HIV to enable testing and linkage to care [10]. APN services, particularly provider APN, which involves the trained healthcare worker notifying the sexual partner(s) of a PLHIV were recommended for Low and Middle Income countries (LMICs) to ramp up efforts to attain the UNAIDS 95-95-95 targets for epidemic control by 2030 [12]. Other modalities of partner notification include passive referral in which the PLHIV notify their sexual partner(s) of potential exposure to HIV, contract referral where the PLHIV are supposed to notify their sexual partner(s) within a specific time frame and when not done in the agreed period, the health worker directly contacts exposed sexual partners for testing and dual referral in which the health worker is available when the PLHIV breaks the news of HIV infection to their sexual partner(s). Pilot testing in Uganda for the APN strategy also found to be effective [13]. Whereas in Uganda, the criteria for APN includes newly diagnosed patients, those with established infection who have high viral loads and those with sexually transmitted Infections, voluntary assisted partner notification is encouraged in routine HIV care to increase awareness of the partner’s HIV status [13].

APN is currently part of WHO’s guidelines for standard care practices for PLHIV [14]. Notwithstanding the fact that the risk of transmission to partners of established patients on antiretrovirals (ARVs) is generally low if virally suppressed, the partners of HIV-positive individuals have a high prevalence of HIV and are often unaware of their status [15], making them an important population for prioritized, targeted HIV testing interventions.

Although studies among adults have been done, there is a dearth of data on provider APN among adolescents living with HIV in LMICs like Uganda. Therefore, we assessed the utilization of provider APN, the preferred PN modality for adolescents and associated factors of provider APN utilization among HIV positive sexually active adolescents in routine care.

Methods

Study design and setting

This was a cross-sectional study conducted at Baylor College of Medicine Children’s Foundation Uganda Center of Excellence clinic in Mulago, Kampala, Uganda. The clinic provides family-centered care and has one of the largest paediatric and adolescent HIV treatment programs. Approximately 1,705 adolescents aged 15 to 19 years receive medical care at the facility for HIV.

Study population

Adolescents living with HIV, aged 15 to 19 years, who had engaged in penetrative sexual intercourse in the preceding 12 months, and/ had regular sexual partner(s) were recruited to participate in the study. Although being very sick was the exclusion criterion, none of the potential participants met it therefore none was excluded.

Data collection procedures

Participants were recruited between February and March 2023. A list of expected adolescents was generated by the clinic’s electronic system before their visits. Adolescents attending their routine clinic day were approached by trained research assistants. During the study period, 457 adolescents were screened for participation by peer research assistants. A total of 372 participants were found eligible and enrolled into the study. A pretested researcher-administered questionnaire developed for the study was used to collect information on demographic characteristics, medical and social history, knowledge of PN and history of intimate partner violence including physical, sexual and emotional violence, and partner characteristics. Medical history such as WHO staging, antiretroviral therapy regimen, last viral load tested, and history of treatment for sexually transmitted infections were abstracted from the electronic database. Participants who accepted to utilize provider-initiated APN were linked to a counsellor based at the clinic to give further counselling and guidance with the enumeration of partners. However, we did not follow up their partners to confirm HIV testing at the facility. The questionnaire has been uploaded as an online supplementary file (see Additional file 1).

Sample size and power calculation

The sample size was calculated using the Keish – Leslie formula for sample size calculation for cross sectional study designs. It was based on a study in which uptake of APN by newly diagnosed HIV positive patients was 67.4% [16]. Given the uptake of 67.4%, a sample size of 372 was calculated to have a power of 80%, considering the desired level of precision at 5% and a 10% adjustment for errors.

Statistical analysis

We collected data using EpiData (version 4.4.1) computer program, exported the data to STATA/IC version 16.0 (STATA CORP, TEXAS USA) for analysis. We used descriptive statistics to summarize categorical and continuous variables. The primary outcome (level of utilization of provider APN) was calculated as a proportion of adolescents who accepted to utilize provider APN over the total number of eligible participants, expressed as a percentage. Secondary outcomes such as preferred modality of partner notification were expressed as percentages. Cross tabulation with chi-square testing was used for bivariate analysis. All variables with a p-value < 0.2 were entered in modified poisson regression model for multivariable analysis. The level of statistical significance was defined at a p-value < 0.05.

Ethical considerations

Our study was reviewed and approved by the School of Medicine Research and Ethics Committee (Mak-SOMREC-2022-453). We also obtained administrative permission from the Baylor College of Medicine children’s foundation regulatory office. All participants aged 18 to 19 years provided written informed consent, while those aged 15 to 17 years provided their assent to participate in the study. Given the sensitivity of information required by the study, the Research and Ethics Committee (REC) waived the need to obtain informed consent from parents/guardians of those aged 15 to 17 years.

APN acceptance and preferred approach

Overall, 49% of the adolescents accepted to utilize provider APN services. The proportion of acceptance was higher among the females (52.3%) compared with male adolescents (45.2%). Utilization of APN was higher among participants aged 18 years and over (50%) compared with those less than 18 years (47.9%). When considering the preferred modalities of accessing Partner Notification services, passive referral emerged as the most favored method at 41.4% and the least preferred modality of notification was provider referral at 12.4% as shown in Fig. 1 below.

Correlates of provider assisted partner notification

Table 3 below presents associations between potential correlates and provider APN. Factors that were associated with an increased likelihood of utilization of provider assisted partner notification included being in a stable relationship for at least two years (PR: 1.3, 95% CI 1.04–1.59), being doubly orphaned orphan (PR: 1.53, 95% CI 1.25–1.86) and avoiding pornography (PR: 2.2, 95% CI 1.64–2.97).

Factors that were significantly associated (p-value < 0.05) with a reduced likelihood of provider APN utilization included residing in an urban area (PR: 0.5, 95% CI 0.42–0.58), not having children (PR: 0.68, 95% CI 0.50–0.93), not belonging to an HIV club (PR: 0.48, 95% CI 0.41 − 0.55) and abstaining from alcohol and recreational drugs such as Marijuana (PR: 0.7, 95% CI 0.54–0.90). Inconsistent condom use (PR: 0.35, 95% CI 0.24–0.52) and having a partner who was 20 years and older were also associated with reduced likelihood of utilization of APN services (PR: 0.78, 95% CI 0.62–0.99).

Discussion

This study aimed to determine the level of provider APN utilization among sexually active adolescents living with HIV aged 15 to 19 years in routine care at an urban HIV clinic in Uganda. We found that less than half of the study participants opted to use the provider APN services. The adolescents preferred passive referral compared to allowing health care providers to notify their sexual partners of their HIV positive status. Being in a stable sexual relationship for at least two years, being a double orphan and abstaining from pornographic materials increased likelihood of utilizing provider APN. On the other hand, residing in an urban area, not having children, not belonging to an HIV club, avoiding use of alcohol and recreational drugs such as Marijuana, inconsistent condom use and having a partner who was 20 years older, were associated with reduced likelihood of utilizing provider APN.

Utilization of provider APN by sexually active adolescents living with HIV in Kampala was much lower compared with APN studies in other SSA countries such as Cameroon [17], and Tanzania [18]. In Uganda, a study implementing APN at 69 healthcare facilities in two urban districts showed a relatively good utilization rates of APN at 67% among index HIV patients aged 15 years and above, majority of whom were adults [16]. Similarly, this level of APN utilization found in our study is much lower compared with studies done among adult populations in Kenya, including a randomised clinical trial where the utilization rates were 89% and in Malawi [11, 19]. The poor utilization of APN by adolescents living with HIV in the study could be attributable to the fear of social harms including violence and stigma, relationship dissolution and loss of economic support and the lack of requisite communication skills [20]. Additionally, the cultural and social expectations at this age for most adolescents is to concentrate on education for those in school and not to engage in sexual relationships. Therefore most adolescents keep this information secret especially from their parents [21].

It is also noteworthy that the adolescents preferred modality for notifying their partners was passive referral, while the least preferred was provider referral. This preference of passive referral is similar to findings of PN study among youth living with HIV in the United States that showed that most of them had notified their partners of their potential exposure by themselves [18, 22].

In our study, double orphaned individuals were more likely to utilize provider APN services. Children orphaned by HIV often times have their HIV status disclosed to them [23]. Nöstlinger C et al. found that HIV disclosure to peers by ALHIV was significantly related to having lost a father [24]. Additionally, adolescents with especially prenatally acquired HIV whose parents are alive may not have full autonomy to disclose because they are under directives to keep their status a secret since disclosing may potentially stigmatize the wider family [25].

Adolescents who abstained from watching pornographic materials were more likely to utilize provider APN services compared with those who did. Watching pornographic material is associated with unwillingness to link partners for PN [26]. Further still, watching pornographic materials has been associated with increased moral disengagement, being less ethical, engaging in risky sexual behavior and being less committed to relationships [27, 28]. Adolescents who therefore do not expose themselves to pornography may consider it their moral responsibility to notify their partners compared to those who watch pornographic materials.

In this study, we found that having a relatively stable relationship for at least two years was associated with a higher likelihood of utilization of provider APN services. This finding has been corroborated by other studies in which nature and length of relationship duration affected disclosure and participants preferred to notify regular partners but did not deem it necessary to inform short term or transient partners [19].

Similar to other APN studies, participants residing in urban areas were less likely to utilize provider APN services compared with those in rural areas. Thior et al. demonstrated that people in rural areas are more likely to disclose because the low access and low testing rates in rural areas creates demand when services are available, thus increasing the notification likelihood [29].

Adolescents who did not have children were less likely to use APN. It would seem plausible that individuals who have children would want to disclose their HIV status to ensure that their children can freely access healthcare. A study on when HIV positive women would disclose their HIV status to sexual partners demonstrated that they were likely to disclose just before birth or in the early postnatal period to prevent transmission to the child [30]. Adolescents who did not belong to an HIV club were also less likely to utilize APN compared with those who belonged to one. This finding is consistent with other studies in which HIV status disclosure to sexual partners has been demonstrated to be positively associated with having social support from family and friends [31].

Study strengths and limitations

To the best of our knowledge, this may be the first study in Uganda that has evaluated provider APN among adolescents living with HIV in routine care. Most of the earlier studies include older-age groups who were newly diagnosed.

The limitations of the study included the fact that data were self-reported making it prone to desirability bias. Additionally, considering the sensitive nature of the topic, we could have improved the reporting of sensitive information using computer assisted instruments.

Conclusions

There was low utilization of APN among sexually active adolescents with HIV. While acceptable, provider APN was not the preferred method for partner notification among adolescents. There is a need to develop adolescent-friendly APN strategies to improve its utilization In Uganda.

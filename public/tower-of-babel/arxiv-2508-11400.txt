Abstract
The study of the charmed baryons is crucial for investigating the strong and weak interactions in the Standard Model and for gaining insights into the internal structure of baryons. In an e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} experiment the lightest charmed baryon, Λ c + \Lambda_{c}^{+} , can be produced in pairs through the single photon annihilation process. This process can be described by two complex electromagnetic form factors. The presence of a non-zero relative phase between these form factors gives rise to a transverse polarization of the charmed baryon and provides additional constraints on the dynamic parameters in the decays. In this article, we present the first observation of the transverse polarization of Λ c + \Lambda_{c}^{+} in the reaction e + ​ e − → Λ c + ​ Λ ¯ c − e^{+}e^{-}\to\Lambda_{c}^{+}\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-} , based on 6.4 ​ fb − 1 6.4~\text{fb}^{-1} of e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} annihilation data collected at center-of-mass energies between 4600 4600 MeV and 4951 4951 MeV with the BESIII detector. The decay asymmetry parameters and strong phase shift in the decays Λ c + → p ​ K S 0 \Lambda_{c}^{+}\to pK_{S}^{0} , Λ ​ π + \Lambda\pi^{+} , Σ 0 ​ π + \Sigma^{0}\pi^{+} , Σ + ​ π 0 \Sigma^{+}\pi^{0} are also simultaneously extracted from the joint angular distributions. These results are vital for understanding C ​ P C\!P violation and its role in the matter-antimatter asymmetry of the Universe.



I
Introduction

Although the most abundant constituent of matter is baryons, our understanding of them is less knowledgeable than that of mesons. The addition of one constituent quark in baryons compared to the quark-antiquark mesons introduces numerous challenges in theoretical calculations, making high-order and diquark correlation contributions difficult to predict accurately. One of the effective approaches to study the structure of baryons is to measure their electromagnetic form factors (EMFFs)
Pacetti et al. 2015
. In the
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation process, the time-like form factors are accessible, providing insights into the electromagnetic structure of the baryons
Huang and Ferroli 2021
. For a spin-1/2 baryon (
B
), the process of
e^{+}e^{-}\to B\bar{B}
is dominated by the single photon exchange. The cross-section can be described by the complex electric form factor
G_{E}
and magnetic form factor
G_{M}
, which are functions of the four-momentum squared
q^{2}
of the photon. The angular distribution of the produced baryon is characterized by two parameters: the angular parameter
\alpha_{0}
and the phase difference between the EMFFs
\Delta\Phi
, where
\Delta\Phi
is defined as
\text{arg}(G_{M})-\text{arg}(G_{E})

Chen and Ping 2019
.
The transverse polarization can be parameterized as

P_{y}(\cos\theta_{0})=\frac{3}{2(3+\alpha_{0})}\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}_{0}}\sin\theta_{0}\cos\theta_{0}\sin\Delta\Phi,

(1)

where the factor
\frac{3}{2(3+\alpha_{0})}
arises from normalization in spin density matrix of
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
. A non-vanishing
\Delta\Phi
gives rise to the transverse polarization effect of the baryon, which depends on the momentum direction of the baryon (
\theta_{0}
) in the rest frame of
e^{+}e^{-}
. This polarization effect has been observed in the pair production of hyperons
\Lambda

Ablikim et al. 2019a
;
Ablikim et al. 2022a
;
Ablikim et al. 2023a

\Sigma^{0,+}

Ablikim et al. 2024a

\Xi^{0,-}

Ablikim et al. 2022b
;
Ablikim et al. 2022c
;
Ablikim et al. 2023b

Lee and Yang 1957

The
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
is the lightest charmed baryon, where a light up quark in the proton is replaced by a heavy charm quark. The study of the
\Lambda_{c}^{+}

\Lambda_{c}^{+}\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}

The asymmetry in charge conjugation and parity symmetry (
C\!P
) lies at the heart of some of the most fundamental problems in physics. The presence of
C\!P
asymmetry has been established in meson decays
Alavi-Harati et al. 1999
;
Aaij et al. 2019
;
Aaij et al. 2020
. Recently, the LHCb collaboration reported the first observation of direct
C\!P
asymmetry in a baryonic decay
Aaij et al. 2025a
,
\Lambda_{b}^{0}\to pK^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
, marking a significant milestone in the study of
C\!P
violation. Meanwhile, ongoing efforts are exploring
C\!P
asymmetry in other baryonic processes
Aaij et al. 2017
;
Aaij et al. 2025b
. Despite these discoveries, the origin of
C\!P
asymmetry and a precise determination of its magnitude in specific processes remain open questions. Its size strictly depends on the strong and weak phase shift.
In Cabbibo-favored (CF) decays of the
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
baryon, there is no
C\!P
asymmetry, as the weak phase difference is expected to be zero. By relaxing the constraints of
C\!P
asymmetry , we can independently extract both the strong and weak phase shifts. The strong phase shift is an important input for theoretical calculations, helping to refine models of hadronic dynamics and improve our understanding of the underlying strong interaction. On the other hand, the weak phase provides a direct test of Standard Model (SM) predictions, offering valuable insights into potential
C\!P
asymmetry and verifying the consistency of weak interactions in charm decays.

In this paper, we observe for the first time the transverse polarization of the
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
baryon in
e^{+}e^{-}\to\Lambda_{c}^{+}\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}
and obtain the phase difference (
\Delta\Phi
) between the EMFFs at thirteen CM energies ranging from
4600
to
4951
MeV, with an overall significance exceeding
10.0\sigma
. The decay asymmetry parameters (
\alpha_{BP}
and
\Delta_{BP}
, which then leads to
\beta_{BP}
and
\gamma_{BP}
) and phase shifts between the
S
- and
P
-wave (
\delta_{p}-\delta_{s}
) are measured for four CF channels:
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to pK^{0}_{S},~\Lambda\pi^{+},~\Sigma^{0}\pi^{+}\text{,~and}~\Sigma^{+}\pi^{0}
. The decay of
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to pK^{-}\pi^{+}
, notable for its clean structure and large branching fraction, is included to improve the transverse polarization measurement.
By measuring the processes in samples separated by baryon charge, the
C\!P
asymmetry parameters, strong phase shifts and weak phase shifts between different partial waves are also extracted in the CF processes.
This allows precise testing of the SM and provides an essential input for the theoretical predictions.
The analysis is performed based on a multidimensional angular analysis of the cascade decays of
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
, as described in Ref.
Ablikim et al. 2019b
. The charge conjugation channels are included in the analysis and are implied in the context unless explicitly stated otherwise.

II
Experimental apparatus and data sets

The Beijing Spectrometer III (BESIII) detector
Ablikim et al. 2010
records symmetric
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions provided by the Beijing Electron Positron Collider (BEPCII) storage ring
Yu et al. 2016
in the CM energy range from 1.84 to 4.95 GeV, with a peak luminosity of
1.1\times 10^{33}\;\text{cm}^{-2}\text{s}^{-1}
achieved at
\sqrt{s}=3.773\;\text{GeV}
. BESIII has collected large data samples in this energy region
Ablikim et al. 2020
;
Lu et al. 2020
;
Zhang et al. 2022
. The cylindrical core of the BESIII detector covers 93% of the full solid angle and consists of a helium-based multilayer drift chamber (MDC), a plastic scintillator time-of-flight system (TOF), and a CsI(Tl) electromagnetic calorimeter (EMC), which are all enclosed in a superconducting solenoidal magnet providing a 1.0 T magnetic field. The solenoid is supported by an octagonal flux-return yoke with resistive plate counter muon identification modules interleaved with steel. The charged-particle momentum resolution at
1~{\rm GeV}/c
is
0.5\%
, and the
{\rm d}E/{\rm d}x
resolution is
6\%
for electrons from Bhabha scattering. The EMC measures photon energies with a resolution of
2.5\%
(
5\%
) at
1
GeV in the barrel (end cap) region. The time resolution in the TOF barrel region is 68 ps, while that in the end cap region was 110 ps. The end cap TOF system was upgraded in 2015 using multigap resistive plate chamber technology, providing a time resolution of 60 ps
Guo et al. 2017
;
Li et al. 2017
;
Cao et al. 2020
. Data analyzed in this work were collected at thirteen energy points above the
\Lambda^{+}_{c}\bar{\Lambda}^{-}_{c}
threshold. The integrated luminosity of the datasets analyzed in this work is estimated to be
6.4~\mathrm{fb}^{-1}

Ablikim et al. 2022d
;
Ablikim et al. 2022e
.

Simulated data samples produced with a
geant4
-based
Agostinelli et al. 2003
Monte Carlo package, which includes the geometric description of the BESIII detector and the detector response, are used to determine detection efficiencies and to estimate backgrounds. The simulation models the beam energy spread and initial state radiation (ISR) in the
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilations with the generator
kkmc

Jadach et al. 2001
;
Jadach et al. 2000
. The inclusive simulation includes the production of open charm processes, the ISR production of vector charmonium(-like) states, and the continuum processes incorporated in
kkmc

Jadach et al. 2001
;
Jadach et al. 2000
. All particle decays are modelled with
evtgen

Lange 2001
;
Ping 2008
using branching fractions either taken from the Particle Data Group (PDG)
Navas et al. 2024
, when available, or otherwise estimated with
lundcharm

Chen et al. 2000
;
Yang et al. 2014
. Final state radiation (FSR) from charged final state particles is incorporated using the
photos
package
Barberio et al. 1991
. The phase space (PHSP) signal simulation is generated by simulating all allowed kinematic configurations within the entire phase space for the process
e^{+}e^{-}\to\Lambda_{c}^{+}\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}
, followed by the decay of
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
into the signal final states and the inclusive decay of
\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}
, which accounts for all possible decay modes.
The signal simulation is based on the process model described in the helicity formalism with the decay asymmetry parameters measured in this work or cited from the PDG
Navas et al. 2024
.

III
Measurement Method

III.1
Event selection and
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
signal in the beam-constrained mass spectrum

A single tag approach
Ablikim et al. 2018a
is employed, where only one
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
or
\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}
baryon is reconstructed in each event. Four two-body hadronic decays
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to pK_{S}^{0}
,
\Lambda\pi^{+}
,
\Sigma^{0}\pi^{+}
,
\Sigma^{+}\pi^{0}
, and one three-body decay,
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to pK^{-}\pi^{+}
, are studied in this research following Refs.
Ablikim et al. 2018a
;
Ablikim et al. 2023c
. The intermediate states,
K_{S}^{0}
,
\Lambda
,
\Sigma^{0}
, and
\Sigma^{+}
are reconstructed by combining
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
,
p\pi^{-}
,
\gamma\Lambda
, and
p\pi^{0}
candidates, respectively.

Charged tracks detected in the MDC are required to satisfy
|\cos\theta|<0.93
, where
\theta
is the polar angle defined relative to the symmetry axis of the MDC (the
z
-axis). Additionally, the tracks that do not come from a
K^{0}_{S}
meson or
\Lambda
baryon must have a distance of closest approach to the interaction point (IP) satisfying
|r_{z}|<10~\text{cm},\quad|r_{xy}|<1~\text{cm}
, where
r_{z}
and
r_{xy}
are the distances along the beam axis and in the transverse plane, respectively.

Particle identification (PID) for charged tracks combines measurements of the energy deposited in the MDC (d
E
/d
x
) and the flight time in the TOF to form likelihoods
\mathcal{L}(h)~(h=p,K,\pi)
for each hadron
h
hypothesis.
Tracks are identified as protons if their likelihoods satisfy
\mathcal{L}(p)>\mathcal{L}(K)
and
\mathcal{L}(p)>\mathcal{L}(\pi)
. Charged kaons and pions are identified by comparing the likelihoods of the respective hypotheses:

\mathcal{L}(K)>\mathcal{L}(\pi)
and
\mathcal{L}(\pi)>\mathcal{L}(K)
, respectively.

Neutral showers are reconstructed in the EMC. Showers not associated with any charged track are identified as photon candidates. The deposited energy of each shower in the EMC must satisfy the following conditions:
E_{\text{barrel}}>25
MeV for
|\cos\theta|<0.80
,
E_{\text{end-cap}}>50
MeV for
0.86<|\cos\theta|<0.92
. The EMC time difference from the event start time is required to be less than 700 ns to suppress electronic noise and showers unrelated to the event. The
\pi^{0}
candidates are reconstructed from photon pairs with invariant masses satisfying:
115~\text{MeV}/c^{2}<M(\gamma\gamma)<150~\text{MeV}/c^{2}
.

Candidates for
K^{0}_{S}
and
\Lambda
decays are reconstructed from
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
and
p\pi^{-}
combinations, respectively. For these tracks, the distances of closest approach to the interaction point (IP) must satisfy:
|r_{z}|<20~\text{cm}
. There is no distance constraint required in the transverse plane. The charged pions in the
K^{0}_{S}
reconstruction are not subject to the PID requirements described earlier, while the proton in the
\Lambda
reconstruction is required to satisfy the PID criteria. The two final-state tracks are constrained to originate from a common decay vertex by requiring the vertex fit to satisfy:
\chi^{2}<100
. Furthermore, the decay vertex is required to be separated from the IP by a distance of at least twice the fitted vertex resolution. To select
K^{0}_{S}
and
\Lambda
candidates, the invariant masses of the combinations must satisfy:
487~\text{MeV}/c^{2}<M(\pi^{+}\pi^{-})<511~\text{MeV}/c^{2}
,
1111~\text{MeV}/c^{2}<M(p\pi^{-})<1121~\text{MeV}/c^{2}
. These mass windows correspond to approximately three times the standard deviation on either side of the known masses.

To suppress the peaking backgrounds for some decay channels, like
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Lambda l^{+}\nu_{l}(l=e/\mu)
background in the
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Lambda\pi^{+}
channel, and
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Lambda\pi^{+}
background in the
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Sigma^{0}\pi^{+}
channel, additional requirements are applied. Positron PID uses the measured information in the MDC, TOF, and EMC. The combined likelihoods (
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
) under the positron, pion, and kaon hypotheses are obtained. For the
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Lambda\pi^{+}
channel, it is required that
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(e)<0.05
and
\mathcal{L}(\pi)>0.001
are satisfied to suppress the
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Lambda e^{+}\nu_{e}
background. For the
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Lambda\mu^{+}\nu_{\mu}
background in the
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Sigma^{0}\pi^{+}
channel, the information from the muon counter detector is utilized, and the penetrated depth of the
\pi^{+}
candidates is required to be less than
5~\text{cm}
. A kinematic variable, the energy difference
\Delta E\equiv E_{\Lambda_{c}^{+}}-E_{\text{beam}}
, is used to suppress background, where
E_{\Lambda_{c}^{+}}
is the total measured energy of the
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
candidate, and
E_{\text{beam}}
is the beam energy. For the
\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to pK^{-}\pi^{+}
,
pK^{0}_{S}
,
\Lambda\pi^{+}
, and
\Sigma^{+}\pi^{0}
decays the requirements
are
\Delta E\in(-29,26)
,
\Delta E\in(-21,18)
,
\Delta E\in(-23,21)
, and
\Delta E\in(-67,32)
, respectively.
For the
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Sigma^{0}\pi^{+}
channel, an additional photon can be selected from the process
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Lambda\pi^{+}
, and its
\Delta E
is expected to be larger than zero. The requirement
\Delta E\in(-33,20)~\text{MeV}
is imposed and can suppress this type of background efficiently.

A large background for the channel
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Sigma^{+}\pi^{0}
is from
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Lambda\pi^{+}
combined with a random photon. This background, which may pose hidden dangers to the angular analysis, necessitates the application of a four-constraint kinematic fit to the
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Sigma^{+}\pi^{0}
, where
\Sigma^{+}
decays to proton and
\pi^{0}
,
\pi^{0}\to\gamma\gamma
. The quality of the fit,
\chi^{2}
, is calculated by constraining two
\pi^{0}
s,
\Sigma^{+}
, and
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
to their world average masses quoted from PDG
Navas et al. 2024
. After optimization by
N^{2}_{S}/(N_{S}+N_{B})^{3/2}

Aaij et al. 2022
;
Aaij et al. 2023a
, the requirement is set that the
\chi^{2}
must be less than 12, where
N_{S}
and
N_{B}
represent the signal and background yields, respectively.

After applying the above selection criteria, the beam-constrained mass
M_{\text{BC}}\equiv\sqrt{E_{\text{beam}}^{2}/c^{4}-|\vec{p}_{\Lambda_{c}^{+}}|^{2}/c^{2}}
, is defined to identify signal candidates, and
\vec{p}_{\Lambda_{c}^{+}}
is the momentum of the
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
candidates calculated in the
e^{+}e^{-}
rest frame.
An unbinned maximum-likelihood fit is performed on the distribution of
M_{\text{BC}}
. The shapes of both correctly and mis-reconstructed signal events are modeled with the simulation, convolved with a Gaussian function to account for the resolution difference between the data and the simulation. The truth matching technique classifies events as correctly reconstructed if the angle between the reconstructed and true photon momentum directions from the simulation is below 10 degrees; otherwise, they are mis-reconstructed. The background shapes are described by an ARGUS function
Albrecht et al. 1990
, except in the case of the
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to pK^{-}\pi^{+}
channel at CM energies larger than
4740
MeV, where a second-order Chebyshev polynomial function is employed to model the background.
The fit results are shown in Fig.
2
for all channels at
4600
MeV.
The obtained signal yields, combining all energy points, are
50083\pm 233
,
9619\pm 96
,
5742\pm 74
,
2487\pm 48
and
1268\pm 37
in the
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to pK^{-}\pi^{+}
,
pK^{0}_{S},~\Lambda\pi^{+},~\Sigma^{0}\pi^{+}\text{,~and}~\Sigma^{+}\pi^{0}
channels, respectively, within the signal region of
M_{\text{BC}}\in[2.282,2.291]~\text{GeV}/c^{2}
.

III.2
Helicity system and angular distribution definitions

A helicity system and helicity angles are employed in constructing the amplitude for
e^{+}e^{-}\to\Lambda_{c}^{+}\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}
,
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to BP
.
Taking the channel
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Lambda\pi^{+}
as an example, a kinematic diagram of this decay is shown in Fig.
3
. For the process
e^{+}e^{-}\to\Lambda_{c}^{+}\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}
, the momenta of the
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
and
\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}
baryons are defined in the CM system of the
e^{+}e^{-}
pair, and
\theta_{0}
is the polar angle of the
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
in CM system. For the helicity system describing
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Lambda\pi^{+}
decay, the momenta of the
\Lambda
and the
\pi^{+}
are defined in the
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
frame. The angle
\phi_{1}
is that between the
e^{+}\Lambda_{c}^{+}
and
\Lambda\pi^{+}
planes, and
\theta_{1}
is the polar angle of the
\Lambda
momentum defined in the rest frame of the
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
baryon. For the helicity system describing the
\Lambda\to p\pi^{-}
,
\phi_{2}
and
\theta_{2}
adopt similar definitions. Then the formula of angular distributions related to this helicity frame based on the language of helicity amplitudes and Wigner-D function can be derived
Ablikim et al. 2019b
.

Figure 3:
Definitions of the helicity frames and related angles for
e^{+}e^{-}\to\Lambda_{c}^{+}\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-},\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Lambda\pi^{+}
, and
\Lambda\to p\pi^{-}

The joint angular distribution of the single-side decay chains can be derived from polarization transmission
Chen and Ping 2019
or equivalently the helicity density matrix
Perotti et al. 2019
. The angular distribution of the decay chain
e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma^{*}\to\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}\Lambda_{c}^{+}(\to\Lambda(\to p\pi^{-})\pi^{+})
is shown below:

\small\begin{split}&\frac{\text{d}\varGamma}{\text{d}\cos\theta_{0}~\text{d}\cos\theta_{1}~\text{d}\cos\theta_{2}~\text{d}\phi_{1}~\text{d}\phi_{2}}\\
&\propto 1+\alpha_{0}\cos^{2}\theta_{0}\\
&+\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}_{0}}\alpha_{p\pi^{-}}\sin\theta_{0}\cos\theta_{0}\sin\Delta\Phi\sin\theta_{1}\sin\phi_{1}\cos\theta_{2}\\
&+\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}_{0}}\alpha_{p\pi^{-}}\sin\theta_{0}\cos\theta_{0}\sin\Delta\Phi\cos\theta_{1}\sin\phi_{1}\sin\theta_{2}\\
&\times\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}_{\Lambda\pi^{+}}}\cos(\Delta_{\Lambda\pi^{+}}+\phi_{2})\\
&+\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}_{0}}\alpha_{p\pi^{-}}\sin\theta_{0}\cos\theta_{0}\sin\Delta\Phi\cos\phi_{1}\sin\theta_{2}\\
&\times\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}_{\Lambda\pi^{+}}}\sin(\Delta_{\Lambda\pi^{+}}+\phi_{2})\\
&+\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}_{0}}\alpha_{\Lambda\pi^{+}}\sin\theta_{0}\cos\theta_{0}\sin\Delta\Phi\sin\theta_{1}\sin\phi_{1}\\
&+\alpha_{0}\alpha_{p\pi^{-}}\alpha_{\Lambda\pi^{+}}\cos^{2}\theta_{0}\cos\theta_{2}\\
&+\alpha_{p\pi^{-}}\alpha_{\Lambda\pi^{+}}\cos\theta_{2},\end{split}

(3)

where the
\alpha_{p\pi^{-}}
denotes the decay asymmetry parameter in the weak hadronic decay
\Lambda\to p\pi^{-}
. The parameter
\Delta_{\Lambda\pi^{+}}
is the phase shift between the helicity amplitudes of
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Lambda\pi^{+}
satisfying
\beta_{\Lambda\pi^{+}}=\sqrt{1-(\alpha_{\Lambda\pi^{+}})^{2}}\sin\Delta_{\Lambda\pi^{+}},\gamma_{\Lambda\pi^{+}}=\sqrt{1-(\alpha_{\Lambda\pi^{+}})^{2}}\cos\Delta_{\Lambda\pi^{+}}
. Formulas of other two-body decays can be found in Ref.
Ablikim et al. 2019b
.

Although the analysis of the angular distribution of these four two-body CF channels provides effective constraints, the three-body channel
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to pK^{-}\pi^{+}
, with a low background and large production rate, brings beneficial additional sensitivity. Benefiting from the transition amplitude analysis reported by the LHCb Collaboration
Aaij et al. 2023b
, the angular distribution of decay chain
e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma^{*}\to\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}\Lambda_{c}^{+}(\to pK^{-}\pi^{+})
can be expressed explicitly as:

\small\begin{split}&\frac{\text{d}\mathcal{\varGamma}}{\text{d}\cos\theta_{0}~\text{d}\cos{\theta_{1}}~\text{d}\phi_{1}~\text{d}\phi_{2}}\\
&\propto I_{0}(\kappa)\\
&+I_{0}(\kappa)\cos^{2}\theta_{0}\\
&+I_{0}(\kappa)\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}_{0}}\sin\theta_{0}\cos\theta_{0}\sin\Delta\Phi\sum_{j}R_{2j}(\phi_{1},\theta_{1},\phi_{2})\alpha_{j}(\kappa),\end{split}

(4)

where
\phi_{1},\theta_{1},\phi_{2}
are the helicity angles for the intermediate state (
K^{-}\pi^{+}
),
R_{ij}(\phi_{1},\theta_{1},\phi_{2})
represents the 3-dimension
Z
-
Y
-
Z
rotation matrix and
\kappa
denotes a set of Dalitz-plot variables
(M(pK^{-})^{2},M(K^{-}\pi^{+})^{2})
. The
\vec{\alpha}(\kappa)
and
I_{0}(\kappa)
are
model-agnostic representations for polarization dependence of the decay rate and the total differential decay rate over
\kappa
, respectively, which are taken from the
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
polarimetry with the amplitude model both measured by LHCb Collaboration
Aaij et al. 2023b
;
Aaij et al. 2023c
.

III.3
Fit of the joint angular distributions

In this analysis, the free parameters of
\alpha_{0}
,
\Delta\Phi
,
\alpha_{BP}
, and
\Delta_{BP}
, which describe the angular distributions for each CM energy are determined through a simultaneous unbinned maximum log-likelihood fit. The
\alpha_{0}
and
\Delta\Phi
are specific to the invididual energy point.
The likelihood function is constructed from the joint probability density function (PDF) by

\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}=\sum^{\text{energy}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{data}}=\prod_{i=1}^{N_{\text{data}}}f_{s}(\vec{\xi}_{i}).

(5)

Here,
f_{s}(\vec{\xi}_{i})
is the PDF of the signal process,
N_{\text{data}}
is the number of data events and
i
is the event index. The signal PDF
f_{s}(\vec{\xi}_{i})
is formulated as

f_{s}(\vec{\xi}_{i})=\frac{\epsilon(\vec{\xi}_{i})|M(\vec{\xi}_{i};\vec{\eta})|^{2}}{\int\epsilon(\vec{\xi}_{i})|M(\vec{\xi}_{i};\vec{\eta})|^{2}\text{d}\vec{\xi}_{i}}~,

(6)

where
\vec{\xi}_{i}
denotes the kinematic observables, including the helicity angles and Daltiz-plot variables, while
\vec{\eta}
denotes the free parameters to be determined. The

M(\vec{\xi}_{i};\vec{\eta})
is the total decay amplitude, and
\epsilon(\vec{\xi}_{i})
is the detection efficiency, parameterized in terms of the kinematic variables
\vec{\xi}_{i}
.
It is important to note that all
\vec{\xi}_{i}
are calculated using four-momentum updated by kinematic fit, which constrains the invariant mass of all resonances in the signal decay to their world average masses
Navas et al. 2024
.
The background contribution to the joint likelihood is subtracted based on the calculated likelihoods using data in
M_{\text{BC}}\in[2.250,2.270]~\text{GeV}/c^{2}
. For the mis-reconstructed signal events, the contribution to the likelihood is based on the signal MC simulation. The integration of the normalization factor is calculated with a large PHSP signal simulation samples as
\int\epsilon(\vec{\xi}_{i})|M(\vec{\xi}_{i};\vec{\eta})|^{2}\text{d}\vec{\xi}_{i}=\frac{1}{N_{\text{gen}}}\sum_{k_{\text{sim}}}^{N_{\text{sim}}}|M(\vec{\xi}_{k_{\text{sim}}};\vec{\eta})|^{2}
, where
N_{\text{gen}}
is the total number of the generated events,
N_{\text{sim}}
is the number of the events surviving all selection criteria and
k_{\text{sim}}
is the event index.

The negative log-likelihood with background subtraction over the five signal channels and data samples collected from thirteen energy points is minimized using the
Minuit

James and Roos 1975
algorithms. The decay asymmetry parameters of the
\Lambda
,
\Sigma^{+}
, and
\Sigma^{0}
baryons are fixed according to the PDG
Navas et al. 2024
.
In the nominal fit, the parameters
\alpha_{BP}
and
\Delta_{BP}
are set to
-\bar{\alpha}_{BP}
and
-\bar{\Delta}_{BP}
, respectively, in accordance with the assumption of
C\!P
conservation. Here,
\bar{\alpha}_{BP}
and
\bar{\Delta}_{BP}
represent the parameters associated with the antiparticle
\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}
decays. The results of the fit are presented in Fig.
4
. An alternative test is also performed where the assumption of
C\!P
conservation are relaxed and
\bar{\alpha}_{BP}
and
\bar{\Delta}_{BP}
are treated as free parameters in the fit.

III.4
Systematic uncertainties

The systematic uncertainties originate from the differences between simulations and real physical processes leading to deviations in detection efficiencies. In this part, the absolute systematic uncertainties are estimated.
The numerical values are summarized in Table
2
, and the individual sources are discussed in the rest of this section.

•

Selection criteria.
The systematic uncertainties regarding selection criteria primarily arise from the reconstruction of final states,
\Delta E
requirement, and
M_{\text{BC}}
signal selection. The reconstruction efficiency of proton, pion, and kaon tracks is studied with control samples of
e^{+}e^{-}\to p\bar{p}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
,
e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
, and
J/\psi\to K^{0}_{S}K^{\pm}\pi^{\mp}
decays, respectively. The
\pi^{0}
meson reconstruction efficiency is studied with the
\psi(3686)\to\pi^{0}\pi^{0}J/\psi
and
e^{+}e^{-}\to\omega\pi^{0}
decays. The control channels for the
K^{0}_{S}
reconstruction efficiency are the
J/\psi\to\phi K^{0}_{S}K^{\mp}\pi^{\pm}
and
J/\psi\to K^{*}(892)^{\pm}K^{\mp}
decays. For the reconstruction efficiency of the
\Lambda
baryon the
J/\psi\to\bar{p}K^{+}\Lambda
and
J/\psi\to\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}
decay samples are used
Ablikim et al. 2018b
.
The difference in reconstruction efficiency for photons between data and MC simulation is studied with the
J/\psi\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0}
decay and found to be
0.5\%
and
1.5\%
in the barrel and endcap of the EMC. A randomly generated efficiency correction factor is used to adjust the PHSP simulation.
Requirements relating to the MUC depth, probability of electron, and pion assumption are studied with a control sample of
\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Lambda\pi^{+}
decays. The potential differences in event selection efficiencies for data and MC simulation are taken into account in the fit described in the previous section via reweighting the PHSP sample. The uncertainties related to the
\Delta E
and
M_{\text{BC}}
requirements are evaluated by smearing the signal simulation with a Gaussian resolution function. The changes in the fit results based on new survived signal simulation are taken to be systematic uncertainties.

•

Performance of truth matching.
The maximum difference between the matching condition with 9 or 11 degree and the nominal result is considered as systematic uncertainty.

•

Signal components.
The amplitude-model related uncertainties are computed using the alternative models in Ref.
Aaij et al. 2023c
. With alternative polarimeter field inputs, the angular fit is repeated and the standard deviations and difference of mean value are assigned to the model uncertainties. In Ref.
Aaij et al. 2023b
, the statistical uncertainties are estimated by varying the parameter values in the default model. These parameters are sampled from a Gaussian function, whose
\mu
and
\sigma
are given by the central and error values from the default model fit results in Ref.
Aaij et al. 2023c
. The polarimeter vector fields are reproduced for each varied parameter set. The angular fit procedure is repeated using the corresponding inputs, and the statistical component is given by taking the standard deviations and the difference of the mean value.

•

Background components.
The background shape is extracted from the data events in the
M_{\text{BC}}
sideband regions. To estimate the possible difference between the
M_{\text{BC}}
sideband and signal regions, a weight factor is derived by comparing the
M(K^{-}\pi^{+})^{2}
distributions in the
M_{\text{BC}}
sideband and signal regions of inclusive simulation. Data events in the
M_{\text{BC}}
sideband region are reweighted and the fit procedure is repeated. The difference is then taken as the uncertainty from background model. Background fractions are fixed according to the fit results from the nominal fit. To estimate systematic uncertainties, the background fraction for each energy point is varied by
\pm 1\sigma
and the fit procedure is repeated. The quadratic sum of the largest variation from each fraction are assigned as systematic uncertainties from the background fraction. The mis-reconstructed model is examined using an alternative signal simulation produced with new input parameters
\alpha_{\Sigma^{0}\pi^{+}}
and
\alpha_{\Sigma^{+}\pi^{0}}
which are changed within
\pm 1\sigma
of the results presented here.
The uncertainty due to the combinational background model is estimated by varying the relative weights between
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}
pairs and other hadronic events based on the uncertainties of their cross-section ratio. The relevant systematic uncertainties from background size are examined by repeating the fits with an alternative background size obtained from the Gaussian sampling of the fitted parameters.
The series of fitted parameters, assumed to follow a Gaussian distribution, are used to estimate the systematic uncertainty for the background size. The uncertainty is determined by summing the fitted Gaussian resolution and the deviation of the fitted Gaussian mean from the nominal value.

•

Fixed parameters.
The systematic uncertainty due to the input parameters is evaluated by varying these parameters using a Gaussian sampling method. For each parameter, the obtained results are expected to follow a Gaussian distribution. The sum of the fitted Gaussian resolution and the difference between the fitted Gaussian mean and the nominal result is taken as the systematic uncertainty.

IV.2
Decay asymmetry and SM test

As the SM predicts no
C\!P
asymmetry in these decay channels, we assume
C\!P
symmetry, meaning the decay asymmetry parameters
\alpha_{BP}=-\bar{\alpha}_{BP}
and
\Delta_{BP}=-\bar{\Delta}_{BP}
in the angular distribution analysis. In addition to the transverse polarization, the decay asymmetry parameters are obtained and shown in Fig.
7
. In the SM, the amplitude for a spin-
1/2
baryon decaying into a spin-
1/2
baryon and a spin-
0
meson can be written as
\mathcal{M}=i\bar{u}_{f}(A-B\gamma_{5})u_{i}
, where
A
and
B
are constants, that reflect the
S
and
P
-wave amplitudes with different orbital angular momentum in weak decays, and directly reflect the mechanism of different weak decays. The
u_{i}

Discussion

The effect of transverse polarization of
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
baryons is discovered for the first time by analyzing
6.4~\text{fb}^{-1}
of
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation data collected at the CM energies between
4600
MeV and
4951
MeV with the BESIII detector. The non-zero transverse polarization indicates the phase shift of the non-zero EMFF. For the first time, we scanned the phase shifts at multiple energy points and demonstrated their differences from theoretical predictions. In 2021, Ref.
Wan et al. 2021
used modified vector meson dominance (VMD) model considering vector charmed mesons and their excitations
J/\psi
,
\psi(2S)
,
\psi(3770)
,
\psi(4040)
,
\psi(4160)
, and
\psi(4415)
. They then constructed and fitted the Born cross section of
e^{+}e^{-}\to\Lambda_{c}^{+}\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}
, since it was studied in 2021, where the data they fitted comes from the Belle Collaboration
Pakhlova et al. 2008
and the BESIII Collaboration
Ablikim et al. 2018a
.
As the Belle Collaboration provided the main lineshape, the fitting is dominated by Belle’s results. The fitting results well describe the Born cross section of
e^{+}e^{-}\to\Lambda_{c}^{+}\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}
. However, Belle’s results showed a significant enhancement of
Y(4630)
, which was refuted by BESIII’s more precise results fifteen years later
Ablikim et al. 2023d
. Based on this new result, another work
Chen et al. 2024
also based on the VMD model using resonances,
\psi(4500)
,
\psi(4660)
,
\psi(4790)
, and
\psi(4900)
, describes the Born cross section very well. Due to higher precision and the absence of abnormal
Y(4630)
contribution, the fitted results describe the oscillation behavior of
|G_{E}/G_{M}|
with energy points measured by BESIII Collaboration. Unfortunately, the results of these two works
Wan et al. 2021
;
Chen et al. 2024
cannot describe the phase shifts between
G_{E}
and
G_{M}
very well. The theoretical predictions still show a significant deviation from the experimental measurements. Therefore, the stucture of
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
and the mechanism of its production in the
e^{+}e^{-}
collider remains a mystery.

The transverse polarization of the
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
baryon provides an opportunity to measure the decay asymmetry parameters of the
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
decay. The golden two-body decays
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to pK^{0}_{S}
,
\Lambda\pi^{+}
,
\Sigma^{0}\pi^{+}
, and
\Sigma^{+}\pi^{0}
have been studied. The parameter
\alpha_{pK^{0}_{S}}
has changed its sign, replacing the previous results
Ablikim et al. 2019b
. The value of
\alpha_{pK^{0}_{S}}
is now consistent with most theoretical calculations. The decay asymmetry parameters
\alpha_{\Sigma^{+}\pi^{0}}
and
\alpha_{\Sigma^{0}\pi^{+}}
support the prediction under
SU(3)
flavor symmetry but
\alpha_{\Sigma^{+}\pi^{0}}
is more than
2\sigma
different than the measurements from Belle
Li et al. 2023b
. Due to the cascading decay in
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Lambda\pi^{+},~\Sigma^{0}\pi^{+}
, and
\Sigma^{+}\pi^{0}
, we also obtain the value of
\Delta_{BP}
, and use this to derive
\beta_{BP}
and
\gamma_{BP}
. This also enables us to extract the
S
- and
P
-wave phase shift,
\delta_{p}-\delta_{s}
.
The results of phase shifts in this work are consistent with recent predictions
Zhong et al. 2024a
;
Zhong et al. 2024b
;
Cheng et al. 2025
following the derived method from the LHCb Collaboration
Aaij et al. 2024
, and we use the convention that
sign=+1
as in Ref.
Wang et al. 2025
. We have also derived the dynamic parameters
|A|
and
|B|
for weak interactions. Previous theoretical studies on the decay of charmed baryons have also predicted
|A|
and
|B|
, but most of them did not account for the contribution of phase shifts to the decay asymmetry parameters, which complicates the accurate prediction of these parameters. Only a few recent works have emphasized the impact of phase shifts and predicted
|A|
and
|B|

Zhong et al. 2024a
;
Zhong et al. 2024b
;
Cheng et al. 2025
. Clearly, the reliability of their predictions is improving. The results presented here supersede those in Refs
Ablikim et al. 2019b
;
Ablikim et al. 2023d
.

A strong phase shift can be significant in physical processes and theoretically can greatly improve the
C\!P
asymmetry prediction
He and Liu 2025
;
Geng et al. 2024
;
Zhong et al. 2024b
. In our work, we calculated the
\delta_{p}-\delta_{s}
in three channels and demonstrated the existence of a large phase shift in charmed baryon decays. Such a large phase shift can be induced by re-scattering processes and loop effects, and further consideration of the role of final state interactions is warranted
Geng et al. 2024
. The decays measured in this work are all CF processes, where weak phase shifts are zero. Although the
C\!P
asymmetry phenomenon is impossible in the CF processes, it can still be transferred to other Cabibbo-suppressed processes through symmetry in physics. As discussed in Ref.
He and Liu 2025
, the re-scattering effects can enhance
C\!P
asymmetry by an order of magnitude, which makes it promising to observe
C\!P
asymmetry effect in baryon decays.
In addition, based on the separate measurements of the positive and negative processes, we also test
A^{\alpha_{BP}}_{C\!P}
, strong and weak phase shifts. They are related by
A^{\alpha_{BP}}_{C\!P}=-\tan(\delta_{P}-\delta_{S})\tan(\xi_{P}-\xi_{S})
, and the results show that
A^{\alpha_{BP}}_{C\!P}
and weak phase shifts are consistent with zero, which aligns with the expectations of the SM.

In conclusion, the observation of the transverse polarization effect of the
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
with the BESIII detector represents a complete description in charmed baryon production in
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilations, contributing to our understanding of its production properties. Our analysis of decay asymmetry parameters for
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
decays into
pK^{0}_{S}
,
\Lambda\pi^{+}
,
\Sigma^{0}\pi^{+}
, and
\Sigma^{+}\pi^{0}
not only updates and validates previous measurements but also reveals significant strong phase shifts, providing valuable constraints on various theoretical models. Furthermore, the systematic study of production and decay properties provides a comprehensive dataset that refines global fits in flavor physics, enhances the understanding of
C\!P
violation in the charm sector, and aligns with current SM predictions. These findings, combined with the precise measurement of polarization dynamics, open new avenues for investigating hadronic interactions, spin effects, and charm quark dynamics. This work establishes a robust framework for future experimental programs at Belle II, LHCb, STCF and other facilities, setting the stage for deeper insights into the non-perturbative aspects of QCD and the exploration of potential new physics.

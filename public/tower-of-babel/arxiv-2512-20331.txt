Supertranslation in the bulk for generic spacetime
Pujian Mao

Abstract
Supertranslations are usually defined as asymptotic symmetries associated with spacetime boundaries, such as null infinity and black hole horizons. In this Letter, we show that supertranslations admit a natural, coordinate-independent extension into the bulk of spacetime, realized as transitions between families of null hypersurfaces. This construction applies to generic spacetimes in arbitrary dimensions and unifies the realizations of supertranslations at null infinity and black hole horizons. The associated symmetry algebra can be realized by light-ray operators defined on the null hypersurface. Within this framework, the gravitational wave memory effect corresponds to a shift of null hypersurfaces in the bulk. As explicit examples, we compute bulk supertranslations in Minkowski spacetime in arbitrary dimensions and in four-dimensional Schwarzschild spacetime, where we uncover a novel curvature-induced memory effect with observable consequences for light propagation.

Supertranslations were originally derived with respect to particular gauge conditions in a specific coordinate system, such as the Bondi gauge

In this Letter, we demonstrate that bulk supertranslations can be defined as transitions between null hypersurfaces, generated by characteristic flows that uniquely extend boundary supertranslations into the spacetime interior. Since null hypersurfaces are geometric entities, this definition is inherently coordinate-independent. The bulk symmetry algebra can be realized by light-ray operators defined from light-ray integral of the energy-momentum tensor on the null hypersurface
Córdova and Shao 2018
. Those constructions can, in principle, be applied to any Lorentzian spacetime in arbitrary dimensions.

Motivated by the intrinsic connection between supertranslations and gravitational memory, we propose a bulk memory effect corresponding to the transition of null geodesics from one hypersurface to another. Thus, supertranslations are not merely asymptotic but define a genuine bulk symmetry realized by null hypersurface flows with observable memory effects. As concrete demonstrations, we compute supertranslations for Minkowski spacetime in four dimensions, which recovers the bulk extension of supertranslations in Minkowski spacetime obtained in
Compère and Long 2016a
. The computations are then extended to Minkowski spacetime in arbitrary dimensions.

A novel curvature-dependent memory effect is uncovered from the supertranslation in four-dimensional Schwarzschild spacetime, where elliptic integrals are involved with branch points that correspond to the turning points of null geodesics on the hypersurface. Specifically, light rays initially placed on a hypersurface without turning points can be shifted to a new hypersurface with turning points by gravitational wave memory, which allows them to return from the bulk. This type of memory effect occurs only in curved spacetime, i.e., a black hole memory
Donnay et al. 2018
;
Rahman and Wald 2020
. In flat spacetime, light rays remain straight, so bulk memory (supertranslations) can distort their trajectories but never generate turning points.

The Newman-Unti coordinates
. The NU gauge is based on a family of null hypersurface which can always be introduced in a normal hyperbolic Riemannian manifold
Newman and Penrose 1962
;
Newman and Unti 1962
. Suppose this family of null hypersurface is parametrized by
u=
const, and
u
is chosen as one of the spacetime coordinates. The normal vector to those null hypersurface
\ell=\text{d}u
must be null. Consequently,
\ell
is tangent to null geodesics which lie within the null hypersurfaces.

The remarkable feature of foliating spacetime with null hypersurfaces is that one can formulate the Einstein equation or other perturbative equations on a background spacetime as a characteristic initial value problem
Winicour 2001
. More specifically, the equations of motion can be organized into two groups: evolution along
\ell
on a single null hypersurface, and evolution between different hypersurfaces. It is very convenient to choose the geodesic parameter (e.g.,
r
) as one coordinate of the spacetime, so that the evolution equations along a single null hypersurface reduce to ordinary differential equations, because the directional derivative along
\ell
is proportional to
\frac{\partial}{\partial r}
. If
r
is an affine parameter for the geodesic,
\ell=-\frac{\partial}{\partial r}
. The remaining two coordinates
x^{a}
label the geodesics on each null hypersurface. This completes the construction of the celebrated Newman-Unti gauge with the metric assumptions,

g^{uu}=0,\qquad g^{ur}=-1,\qquad g^{ua}=0.

(1)

It is important to note that an affine parameter is not required to formulate the equations of motion as a characteristic initial value problem. In the seminal work of Bondi and collaborators
Bondi et al. 1962
, a luminosity parameter was employed where the metric component
g^{ur}
is left free while the determinant of the metric
g^{ab}
is fixed. This choice would simplify the evolution equations on the null hypersurface, see, e.g.,
Barnich and Lambert 2012
;
Chesler and Yaffe 2014
;
Geiller and Zwikel 2022
;
Geiller and Zwikel 2024

Residual gauge transformation and supertranslation
. In this section, we first examine the physical implications of residual gauge transformations within the NU gauge and identify the supertranslations in this context. We then generalize these definitions to a generic coordinate system. Suppose that two NU coordinate systems
(u,r,x^{a})
and
(\bar{u},\bar{r},\bar{x}^{a})
are related by
\bar{x}^{\mu}=\bar{x}^{\mu}(x^{\alpha})

u=
const and
\bar{u}=
const are both null. Consequently, the full residual gauge transformations of the NU gauge are characterized by different families of null hypersurfaces. In the NU coordinates
(u,r,x^{a})

\bar{u}
, with the condition that
\bar{\ell}=\text{d}\bar{u}
is a null vector.

The physical significance of a residual gauge transformation in the NU framework lies in the change of null hypersurfaces from
u=
const and
\bar{u}=
const. Consequently, the system can be formulated as a characteristic initial value problem based on different families of null hypersurfaces. The coordinate change itself is not fundamental, as it merely reflects a different parametrization of the null hypersurfaces. From a symmetry perspective, all residual gauge transformations preserve the formulation of the system as a characteristic initial value problem. Potential applications of this feature can arise in the context of null surface quantization

Since
\bar{\ell}=\text{d}\bar{u}

\Phi\ell

\bar{\ell}=\text{d}\bar{u}=\text{d}u+\text{d}f(r,x^{a})

\bar{u}=u+f(r,x^{a})

g^{rr}(\partial_{r}f)^{2}-2\partial_{r}f+2g^{ra}\partial_{r}f\partial_{a}f+g^{ab}\partial_{a}f\partial_{b}f=0.

This equation is precisely the condition

;

The definition of supertranslation above is given in NU coordinates. However, a specific coordinate system is not essential, since the key feature of the NU construction is simply the selection of a family of null hypersurfaces. The residual gauge transformations correspond precisely to the freedom in choosing these null hypersurfaces. Therefore, one can define residual gauge transformations with respect to the NU construction without reference to any particular coordinate system, and this definition naturally extends to spacetimes of arbitrary dimensions.

Definition 1:

In a given coordinate system
x^{\mu}
, the residual gauge transformation is defined from any scalar function
H(x^{\mu})
such that
\text{d}H(x^{\mu})
is a null vector.

Supertranslations are defined as the set of all residual gauge transformations modulo rescalings. In asymptotically flat spacetimes, they can be specified using a normalized timelike coordinate
t
at infinity, such that
(\frac{\partial}{\partial t})^{2}=-1
there. A supertranslation can be defined as follows:

Definition 2:

In a given coordinate system
(t,x^{i})
, a supertranslation is defined from any scalar function
h(x^{i})
such that
\text{d}(t+h)
is a null vector.

A Bondi-like coordinate
u=t+h
can be introduced from the above definition so that
g^{uu}=0
. In this coordinate, the equations of motion can be formulated as a characteristic initial value problem. This definition uniquely specifies a supertranslation throughout the bulk of spacetime and is not restricted to the NU gauge. It can also be applied directly to solutions that are difficult to express in NU coordinates, such as the Kerr solution
Fletcher and Lun 1996
;
Fletcher and Lun 2003
;
Venter and Bishop 2006
.

The scalar function that specifies a supertranslation satisfies a nonlinear, first-order partial differential equation (PDE), which can be solved using the method of characteristic flows
Evans 2010
;
Levandosky 2002
. In general, characteristic curves determine solutions only implicitly. However, the new variables defined along the characteristics can be treated as new coordinates. In this way, the full coordinate transformation corresponding to a supertranslation can be explicitly constructed. In the following sections, we will derive supertranslations using characteristic flows for several exact solutions.

Four-dimensional Minkowski with plane boundary
. The Minkowski line-element with a plane boundary in flat null coordinates
(u,r,z,\bar{z})

He and Mitra 2019
is given by

\text{d}s^{2}=-2\text{d}u\text{d}r+2r^{2}\text{d}z\text{d}\bar{z}.

(3)

The null condition for the scalar function then reads

\partial_{r}f=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial_{z}f\partial_{\bar{z}}f.

(4)

This is a first-order fully nonlinear PDE, which can be solved by the method of characteristics following closely
Levandosky 2002
. The equation can be organized as

F(r,z,\bar{z},p_{r},p,\bar{p})=p_{r}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}p\bar{p}=0,

(5)

where we introduce new variables
p_{r}=\partial_{r}f
,
p=\partial_{z}f
, and
\bar{p}=\partial_{\bar{z}}f
. The characteristic equations for this system are

\begin{split}&\frac{\text{d}r}{\text{d}s}=1,\quad\frac{\text{d}z}{\text{d}s}=-\frac{\bar{p}}{r^{2}},\quad\frac{\text{d}\bar{z}}{\text{d}s}=-\frac{p}{r^{2}},\\
&\frac{\text{d}p}{\text{d}s}=0,\quad\frac{\text{d}\bar{p}}{\text{d}s}=0,\quad\frac{\text{d}f}{\text{d}s}=p\frac{\text{d}z}{\text{d}s}+\bar{p}\frac{\text{d}\bar{z}}{\text{d}s}+\partial_{r}f\frac{\text{d}r}{\text{d}s}.\end{split}

(6)

Clearly,
p
and
\bar{p}
are constants along characteristics, which yields that

z=\zeta+\frac{\bar{p}}{r},\qquad\bar{z}=\bar{\zeta}+\frac{p}{r},

(7)

where
\zeta
and
\bar{\zeta}
are constants labeling the characteristic curves. Applying the differential equation (
4
), one can obtain that

\frac{\text{d}f}{\text{d}s}=-\frac{p\bar{p}}{r^{2}},\qquad f=f_{0}(\zeta,\bar{\zeta})+\frac{p\bar{p}}{r}.

(8)

Since
p
and
\bar{p}
are constants on the characteristic curves, they can be fixed by the initial data at the infinity
r\to\infty
. Hence,
p=\partial_{\zeta}f_{0}
and
\bar{p}=\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}f_{0}
. Thus, we have solved the equation (
4
) implicitly.

The solution for a bulk supertranslation
f
is determined from the boundary variables
(\zeta,\bar{\zeta})
defined in (
7
). This corresponds precisely to the characteristic flow of a boundary supertranslation
f_{0}
. The null hypersurface characterized by
f
is therefore the characteristic flow of a cross-section at null infinity
Geroch 1977
specified by the boundary supertranslation
f_{0}
.

Considering
(\zeta,\bar{\zeta})
as new coordinates, one can obtain the following relation of the two coordinate systems

u=\bar{u}-f_{0}-\frac{\partial_{\zeta}f_{0}\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}f_{0}}{r},\qquad z=\zeta+\frac{\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}f_{0}}{r},

(9)

which precisely reproduces the transformation derived in
Compère and Long 2016a
for a supertranslated Minkowski spacetime. In
Compère and Long 2016a
, a radial shift
r=\bar{r}-\partial_{\zeta}\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}f_{0}
was introduced, which corresponds to the integration constant in
\frac{\text{d}r}{\text{d}s}=1
. However, this shift does not affect the definition of the supertranslation.

We now pinpoint the supertranslation effect from the new coordinates
(\bar{u},\bar{r},\zeta,\bar{\zeta})
. Importantly, this is not merely the introduction of a new coordinate system, but rather the selection of a new family of null hypersurfaces with normal vector
\bar{\ell}=\text{d}\bar{u}
. In other words, the objects of interest change from null geodesics on the hypersurfaces
u
=const to null geodesics on the hypersurfaces
\bar{u}
=const. Null geodesics lying on different hypersurfaces can exhibit distinct deviation properties, characterized by their shear. The two families of null hypersurfaces intersect null infinity at different cross-sections, which are related by a boundary supertranslation.

These features follow from a kinematical analysis of the local bulk geometry and its associated supertranslation. From a dynamical perspective, supertranslations are equivalent to gravitational memory
Strominger and Zhiboedov 2016
. In the bulk, gravitational waves with memory will distort null geodesics
Bart 2020
, effectively forcing their trajectories to transition from one null hypersurface to another as illustrated in Fig.
1
.

As a closing remark for this section, we briefly discuss the symmetry algebra in the bulk. While there is no local energy–momentum tensor associated with the propagation of gravitational waves, their generation necessarily involves matter fields carrying a well-defined energy–momentum tensor. Gravitational wave memory can therefore be captured by nonlocal operators defined via light-ray integrals of the energy–momentum tensors along null hypersurfaces, namely light-ray operators. It has been shown that the algebra of such operators contains the BMS algebra
Córdova and Shao 2018
. This strongly suggests that the algebra of infinitesimal residual gauge transformations preserving the NU construction admits a realization in terms of light-ray operators on the null hypersurface.

Minkowski with sphere boundary in arbitrary dimensions
. In four dimensions, the supertranslated Minkowski spacetime with a sphere boundary was obtained in
Compère and Long 2016a
by applying a combined supertranslation and Weyl rescaling to the plane boundary Minkowski metric. In this section, we will apply the characteristics to obtain the supertranslation for Minkowski spacetime with a sphere boundary in arbitrary dimensions. The line-element is given by

\text{d}s^{2}=-\text{d}u^{2}-2\text{d}u\text{d}r+r^{2}\gamma_{ab}(x^{c})\text{d}x^{a}\text{d}x^{b},

(10)

where the Latin characters denote indices on the
d
-dimensional sphere. The null condition for the scalar function reads

2\partial_{r}f=(\partial_{r}f)^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\gamma^{ab}\partial_{a}f\partial_{b}f,

(11)

where
\partial_{a}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{a}}
. The above equation can be rewritten as

F(r,x^{a},p_{r},p_{a})=p_{r}^{2}-2p_{r}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}(p_{a})^{2}=0,

(12)

where we define
p_{r}=\partial_{r}f
and
p_{a}=\partial_{a}f
. The characteristic equations for deriving the supertranslation are

\begin{split}&\frac{\text{d}r}{\text{d}s}=2(p_{r}-1),\quad\frac{\text{d}{x}^{a}}{\text{d}s}=\frac{2}{r^{2}}p^{a},\quad\frac{\text{d}{p_{r}}}{\text{d}s}=\frac{2}{r^{3}}(p_{a})^{2},\\
&\frac{\text{d}p_{a}}{\text{d}s}=-\partial_{a}\gamma^{cd}p_{c}p_{d},\qquad\frac{\text{d}f}{\text{d}s}=\frac{\text{d}r}{\text{d}s}p_{r}+\frac{\text{d}{x}^{a}}{\text{d}s}p_{a}.\end{split}

(13)

We can deduce the conservation of total angular momentum from the above characteristic equations,
\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}s}(p_{a})^{2}=0
, which will significantly simplify the derivation of supertranslation. Let
(p_{a})^{2}=L^{2}
on the characteristic curves. Then, the constraint equation gives

p_{r}=1\pm\sqrt{1-\frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}}}.

(14)

We will take the minus solution for the outgoing case with initial data given at infinity
r\to\infty
. The solution for the supertranslation is obtained as

f=f_{0}({\bar{x}}^{a})+r\left(1-\sqrt{1-\frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}}}\right),

(15)

where
L^{2}={\bar{\gamma}}^{ab}\partial_{{\bar{x}}^{a}}f_{0}\partial_{{\bar{x}}^{b}}f_{0}
. Here,
f_{0}
represents the supertranslation at infinity, and
{\bar{x}}^{a}
can be interpreted as new coordinates for the Minkowski spacetime with a supertranslation. The explicit coordinate transformation can be obtained with the precise form of the angular metric
\gamma_{ab}
.

Unlike Minkowski spacetime, where bulk supertranslations do not introduce qualitatively new structures, spacetime curvature leads to genuinely new features, which we now illustrate from the Schwarzschild solution.

Schwarzschild solution in four dimensions
. The line-element of Schwarzschild solution is given by

\text{d}s^{2}=-\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)\text{d}u^{2}-2\text{d}u\text{d}r+r^{2}\gamma_{ab}(x^{c})\text{d}x^{a}\text{d}x^{b}.

(16)

The null condition for the scalar function is

2\partial_{r}f=\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)(\partial_{r}f)^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\gamma^{ab}\partial_{a}f\partial_{b}f.

(17)

Solving this equation via the method of characteristic flows gives

f=f_{0}({\bar{x}}^{a})+\int_{+\infty}^{r}\frac{\text{d}r^{\prime}}{1-\frac{2M}{r^{\prime}}}\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\frac{L^{2}}{{r^{\prime}}^{2}}\left(1-\frac{2M}{r^{\prime}}\right)}}\right),

(18)

where
L^{2}={\bar{\gamma}}^{ab}\partial_{{\bar{x}}^{a}}f_{0}\partial_{{\bar{x}}^{b}}f_{0}
. As before,
f_{0}
represents the supertranslation at null infinity. This integral generally involves elliptic functions and can exhibit branch points depending on the supertranslation field
f_{0}
. These branch points are turning points of null geodesics on the associated null hypersurface,

\frac{\text{d}r}{\text{d}s}=-2\sqrt{1-\frac{L^{2}}{{r}^{2}}\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)}=0.

(19)

Those branch points should correspond to points on the supertranslation horizon discovered in
Compère and Long 2016b
.

This feature highlights a remarkable property of supertranslations in curved spacetimes. Supertranslations in Minkowski spacetime can distort null rays without creating turning points. In curved spacetimes such as Schwarzschild, a supertranslation can map null geodesics from a hypersurface without turning points to a new hypersurface where turning points appear. This phenomenon gives rise to a novel gravitational memory effect.

One may initially select a family of null hypersurfaces on which the null geodesics exhibit no turning points. Gravitational waves with memory can then shift the system to a different null hypersurface where the light rays develop turning points. As a result, it becomes, in principle, possible to receive light rays returning from the bulk of spacetime. This constitutes a new type of gravitational memory effect that arises purely from spacetime curvature.

Conclusions and perspectives
. In this Letter, we propose a general framework for defining supertranslations in the bulk of spacetime. The framework is formulated in terms of null hypersurfaces and is therefore coordinate-independent, allowing it to be applied to generic spacetime. We present explicit expressions for bulk supertranslations in Minkowski spacetime and characterize the associated bulk memory effect that aligns with the triangle equivalence at null infinity
Strominger 2018
. In curved spacetimes, we uncover a novel memory effect in which supertranslations bend null geodesics, leading to observable changes in the propagation of light rays.

We determine the full residual gauge transformations associated with the choice of null hypersurfaces, which consist of supertranslations and a Weyl-type rescaling of null vectors. In the present work, we focus on the supertranslation sector. It is of independent interest to investigate the rescaling factor
\Phi
, which account for various extensions of the BMS symmetry. Another important direction is to clarify the relation between the bulk supertranslations constructed here and those arising in other contexts, such as scattering amplitudes
Elkhidir et al. 2025
and holography
Marjieh et al. 2022
. A particular notable feature of our construction is that bulk supertranslations are realized as characteristic flows of boundary supertranslations. In the context of AdS/CFT, the gravity dual of a deformation by the composite operator
T\bar{T}

Smirnov and Zamolodchikov 2017
;
Cavaglià et al. 2016
(also
Jiang 2021
;
He et al. 2025
for reviews) corresponds to moving the boundary to a finite radial cutoff
McGough et al. 2018
;
Kraus et al. 2018
;
Hartman et al. 2019
;
Guica and Monten 2021
;
Jafari et al. 2020
;
He et al. 2023
;
He et al. 2024
. The associated
T\bar{T}
flow has been identified as a characteristic flow
Hou 2023
. These parallels suggest that our method of flowing asymptotic symmetries into the bulk is naturally compatible with the AdS/CFT perspective and merits further investigation.

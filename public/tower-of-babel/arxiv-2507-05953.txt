Abstract
. This paper introduces a novel weak Galerkin (WG) finite element method for the numerical solution of the Brinkman equations. The Brinkman model, which seamlessly integrates characteristics of both the Stokes and Darcy equations, is employed to describe fluid flow in multiphysics contexts, particularly within heterogeneous porous media exhibiting spatially variable permeability. The proposed WG method offers a unified and robust approach capable of accurately capturing both Stokes- and Darcy-dominated regimes. A discrete inf-sup condition is established, and optimal-order error estimates are rigorously proven for the WG finite element solutions. Furthermore, a series of numerical experiments is performed to corroborate the theoretical analysis, demonstrating the method’s accuracy and stability in addressing the complexities inherent in the Brinkman equations.

1.
Introduction

This work focuses on the design and analysis of stable and efficient numerical schemes for the Brinkman equations based on the weak Galerkin (WG) finite element methodology. The Brinkman system provides a unified framework for modeling fluid flow in heterogeneous porous media, where the permeability exhibits significant spatial variability. Such variations naturally give rise to zones governed primarily by Darcy’s law, while others are better described by Stokes flow. In a simplified form, the Brinkman model seeks to compute the velocity field
{\mathbf{u}}
and the pressure field
p
by solving the following system:

(1.1)

\begin{split}-\mu\Delta{\mathbf{u}}+\nabla p+\mu\kappa^{-1}{\mathbf{u}}=&{\mathbf{f}},\qquad\qquad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{u}}=&0,\qquad\qquad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
{\mathbf{u}}=&{\mathbf{g}},\qquad\qquad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega.\end{split}

Here,
\mu
denotes the dynamic viscosity of the fluid, and
\kappa
is the permeability tensor associated with the porous medium. The computational domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is assumed to be polygonal or polyhedral, with spatial dimension
d=2
or
3
. The vector field
\mathbf{f}
represents a prescribed source term corresponding to external momentum input. For the purposes of analysis, and without loss of generality, we consider the special case where
\mathbf{g}=0
and
\mu=1
.
The permeability tensor
\kappa
is assumed to be symmetric and uniformly positive definite. That is, there exist positive constants
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}>0
such that for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\lambda_{1}\xi^{T}\xi\leq\xi^{T}\kappa^{-1}\xi\leq\lambda_{2}\xi^{T}\xi.

For analytical simplicity, the permeability is taken to be constant throughout this paper. Nonetheless, the theoretical framework developed herein can be naturally extended to handle spatially varying permeability without introducing substantial complications.

The variational formulation corresponding to the Brinkman system (
1.1
) is given as follows: Determine
{\mathbf{u}}\in[H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)]^{d}
and
p\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
such that

(1.2)

\begin{split}(\nabla{\mathbf{u}},\nabla{\mathbf{v}})-(\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{v}},p)+(\kappa^{-1}{\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}})=&({\mathbf{f}},{\mathbf{v}}),\qquad\forall{\mathbf{v}}\in[H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)]^{d}\\
(\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{u}},q)=&0,\qquad\qquad\forall q\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega),\end{split}

where

H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)=\{w\in H^{1}(\Omega):w|_{\partial\Omega}=0\},

L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)=\{q\in L^{2}(\Omega);\int_{\Omega}qdx=0\}.

The Brinkman equations (
1.1
) model fluid flow in porous media with fractures and generalize the Stokes equations, which approximate Navier–Stokes flow at low Reynolds numbers. Accurate simulation in such multiphysics environments is vital for applications like industrial filtration, foam design, and flow in fractured reservoirs.
High-contrast permeability leads to sharp spatial variations in flow, with regions governed by either Darcy or Stokes behavior. This transition poses a numerical challenge: standard finite element methods often lose stability or accuracy outside their target regime. For example, Stokes-stable elements (e.g.,
P_{2}–P_{0}
, Crouzeix–Raviart, Mini) degrade in Darcy zones, while Darcy-stable elements (e.g., Raviart–Thomas) perform poorly in Stokes-dominated areas
[
3
]
.
A key difficulty is constructing discretizations that remain stable and accurate across both regimes. Recent efforts address this by modifying classical elements to ensure uniform stability for the full Brinkman model
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
.

The WG finite element method provides a flexible framework for approximating partial differential equations by interpreting differential operators in a weak sense, inspired by distribution theory. Unlike classical methods, WG allows for discontinuous, piecewise polynomial functions and reduces regularity requirements through weak derivatives and stabilization techniques.

This paper introduces a WG formulation designed to accommodate general polygonal and polyhedral meshes. A thorough theoretical analysis is carried out to establish optimal-order error estimates, demonstrating the method’s precision and reliability.

The structure of the paper is as follows. Section 2 offers a brief overview of the weak gradient and weak divergence operators, along with their corresponding discrete versions. In Section 3, we develop a WG method tailored for the Brinkman equations, accommodating general polytopal meshes. Section 4 is dedicated to demonstrating the well-posedness of the proposed numerical scheme by proving the existence and uniqueness of the solution. As a key analytical component, an inf-sup condition is also established. In Section 5, we derive the error equations related to the WG formulation. Section 6 presents an optimal error bound for the numerical solution, while Section 7 extends the analysis to derive convergence rates in the
L^{2}
-norm. Section 8 concludes with numerical experiments that validate the theoretical predictions and illustrate the method’s accuracy and robustness.

2.
Discrete Weak Differential Operators

This section revisits the definitions of the weak gradient and weak divergence operators, along with their corresponding discrete forms, as developed in
[
27
]
.

Let
T
be a polytopal element with boundary
\partial T
. A weak function on
T
is defined as a pair
{\mathbf{v}}=\{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\}
, where
{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\in[L^{2}(T)]^{d}
represents the interior component, and
{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\in[L^{2}(\partial T)]^{d}
denotes the boundary component. Notably, the boundary part

{\mathbf{v}}_{b}
is not required to match the trace of
{\mathbf{v}}_{0}
on
\partial T
.

The space of all weak functions on
T
, denote by
W(T)
, is given by

W(T)=\{{\mathbf{v}}=\{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\}:{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\in[L^{2}(T)]^{d},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\in[L^{2}(\partial T)]^{d}\}.

The weak gradient
\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}}
is a linear operator that maps

W(T)
into the dual space of
[H^{1}(T)]^{d\times d}
. For any

{\mathbf{v}}\in W(T)
, it is defined via:

(\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}},{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}=-({\mathbf{v}}_{0},\nabla\cdot{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}+\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b},{\bm{\varphi}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall{\bm{\varphi}}\in[H^{1}(T)]^{d\times d},

where
{\mathbf{n}}
denotes the outward unit normal vector to
\partial T
, with components
n_{i}
for
i=1,\cdots,d
.

Analogously, the weak divergence
\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}
is a linear
operator mapping

W(T)
into the dual of
H^{1}(T)
, defined as

(\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}},w)_{T}=-({\mathbf{v}}_{0},\nabla w)_{T}+\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\cdot{\mathbf{n}},w\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall w\in H^{1}(T).

For any non-negative integer
r\geq 0
, let
P_{r}(T)
denote the space of polynomials of total degree no more than

r
. The discrete weak gradient

\nabla_{w,r,T}{\mathbf{v}}
is the unique polynomial in
[P_{r}(T)]^{d\times d}
that satisfies:

(2.1)

(\nabla_{w,r,T}{\mathbf{v}},{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}=-({\mathbf{v}}_{0},\nabla\cdot{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}+\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b},{\bm{\varphi}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall{\bm{\varphi}}\in[P_{r}(T)]^{d\times d}.

If
{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\in[H^{1}(T)]^{d}
is sufficiently smooth, an integration by parts transforms the above into an equivalent form:

(2.2)

(\nabla_{w,r,T}{\mathbf{v}},{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}=(\nabla{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}+\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\bm{\varphi}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall{\bm{\varphi}}\in[P_{r}(T)]^{d\times d}.

Similarly, the discrete weak divergence
\nabla_{w,r,T}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}
is defined as the unique polynomial in
P_{r}(T)
such that

(2.3)

(\nabla_{w,r,T}\cdot{\mathbf{v}},w)_{T}=-({\mathbf{v}}_{0},\nabla w)_{T}+\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\cdot{\mathbf{n}},w\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall w\in P_{r}(T).

Again, for smooth
{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\in[H^{1}(T)]^{d}
, this is equivalent to:

(2.4)

(\nabla_{w,r,T}\cdot{\mathbf{v}},w)_{T}=(\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{v}}_{0},w)_{T}+\langle({\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0})\cdot{\mathbf{n}},w\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall w\in P_{r}(T).

3.
Weak Galerkin Finite Element Methods

Let
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
be a shape-regular partition of the domain

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
into general polytopal elements, as defined in
[
24
]
. Denote by
{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
the collection of all edges (in 2D) or faces (in 3D) of

{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, and let
{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{0}={\mathcal{E}}_{h}\setminus\partial\Omega
denote the set of all interior edges or faces. For each element
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, let
h_{T}
be the diameter of
T
, and define the mesh size by

h=\max_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}.

Fix an integer
k\geq 1
. On each element
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, we define the local weak finite element space by

V(k,T)=\{\{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\}:{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\in[P_{k}(T)]^{d},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\in[P_{k}(e)]^{d},e\subset\partial T\}.

The global weak finite element space
V_{h}
is obtained by assembling the local spaces
V(k,T)
over all elements
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, with the requirement that the boundary part
{\mathbf{v}}_{b}
is single-valued across shared edges or faces:

(3.1)

\displaystyle V_{h}=\big\{\{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\}:\ \{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\}|_{T}\in V(k,T),\forall T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\big\}.

The subspace of
V_{h}
consisting of functions with vanishing boundary values on
\partial\Omega
is denoted by

V_{h}^{0}=\{{\mathbf{v}}\in V_{h}:{\mathbf{v}}_{b}|_{\partial\Omega}=0\}.

The finite element space for the pressure variable is defined as

(3.2)

\displaystyle W_{h}=\{q\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega):q|_{T}\in P_{k-1}(T)\}.

For simplicity in notation, we define the discrete weak gradient
\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}}
and discrete weak divergence
\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}
elementwise as follows, in accordance with equations (
2.1
) and (
2.3
):

(3.3)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}})|_{T}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{w,k-1,T}({\mathbf{v}}|_{T}),

(3.4)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}})|_{T}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{w,k-1,T}\cdot({\mathbf{v}}|_{T}).

On each element
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, let
Q_{0}
denote the
L^{2}
projection onto
P_{k}(T)
, and for each edge or face
e\subset\partial T
, let
Q_{b}
denote the
L^{2}
projection onto
P_{k}(e)
. Then, for any
{\mathbf{v}}\in[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{d}
, the
L^{2}
projection into
V_{h}
is defined locally by

(Q_{h}{\mathbf{v}})|_{T}:=\{Q_{0}({\mathbf{v}}|_{T}),Q_{b}({\mathbf{v}}|_{{\partial T}})\},\qquad\forall T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}.

We now formulate a Weak Galerkin method for approximating solutions to the Brinkman equations (
1.1
).

WG Algorithm 3.1
.

Find
{\mathbf{u}}_{h}=\{{\mathbf{u}}_{0},{\mathbf{u}}_{b}\}\in V_{h}^{0}
and
p_{h}\in W_{h}
such that

(3.5)

\begin{split}a_{h}({\mathbf{u}}_{h},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})-(\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}_{h},p_{h})=&({\mathbf{f}},{\mathbf{v}}_{0})\qquad\forall{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\in V_{h}^{0},\\
(\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{u}}_{h},q_{h})=&0\qquad\qquad\forall q_{h}\in W_{h},\end{split}

where

\displaystyle a_{h}({\mathbf{u}}_{h},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{u}}_{h},\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}}_{h})+(\kappa^{-1}{\mathbf{u}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{0})+s({\mathbf{u}}_{h},{\mathbf{v}}_{h}),

\displaystyle s({\mathbf{u}}_{h},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\langle{\mathbf{u}}_{0}-{\mathbf{u}}_{b},{\mathbf{v}}_{0}-{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\rangle_{\partial T},

and

(\cdot,\cdot)=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\cdot,\cdot)_{T}.

4.
Existence and Uniqueness of the Solution

Let
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
be a shape-regular finite element mesh partitioning the domain
\Omega
. For any element
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and any function
\phi\in H^{1}(T)
, the following trace inequality holds (see
[
24
]
):

(4.1)

\|\phi\|^{2}_{\partial T}\leq C(h_{T}^{-1}\|\phi\|_{T}^{2}+h_{T}\|\nabla\phi\|_{T}^{2}).

In particular, if
\phi
is a polynomial function on
T
, a simplified trace inequality applies (see
[
24
]
):

(4.2)

\|\phi\|^{2}_{\partial T}\leq Ch_{T}^{-1}\|\phi\|_{T}^{2}.

Lemma 4.1
.

[
18
,
27
]

Let
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
be a shape-regular finite element partition of
\Omega
. Then for any integers
0\leq s\leq 1
,
0\leq m\leq k
, and
0\leq n\leq k-1
, the following approximation estimates hold:

(4.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{2s}\|({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)p\|^{2}_{s,T}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{2n+2}\|p\|^{2}_{n+1},

(4.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{2s}\|{\mathbf{u}}-Q_{0}{\mathbf{u}}\|^{2}_{s,T}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{2(m+1)}\|{\mathbf{u}}\|^{2}_{m+1},

(4.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{2s}\|\nabla{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}(\nabla{\mathbf{u}})\|^{2}_{s,T}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{2n+2}\|{\mathbf{u}}\|^{2}_{n+2}.

We define the following norm on the space
V_{h}
for any function
{\mathbf{v}}={{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}}\in V_{h}
:

(4.6)

{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{v}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}=\Big(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}},\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}})_{T}+(\kappa^{-1}{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{0})_{T}+h_{T}^{-1}\|{\mathbf{v}}_{0}-{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\|_{\partial T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Lemma 4.2
.

The quantity defined in (
4.6
) is a norm on the weak finite element space
V_{h}
.

Proof.

We verify the definiteness of the norm. Suppose
{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\cdot{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}=0
for some
{\mathbf{v}}\in V_{h}^{0}
. Then

\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}},\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}})_{T}+(\kappa^{-1}{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{0})_{T}+h_{T}^{-1}\|{\mathbf{v}}_{0}-{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\|_{\partial T}^{2}=0,

which implies
\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}}=0
,
{\mathbf{v}}_{0}=0
in each
T
,
{\mathbf{v}}_{0}={\mathbf{v}}_{b}
on
\partial T
. It follows that
{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\equiv 0
in
\Omega
and hence
{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\equiv 0
, so
{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\equiv 0
. This completes the proof.
∎

Let
{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}
denote the
L^{2}
projection onto the space of piecewise polynomials of degree at most
k-1
on each element.

Lemma 4.3
.

[
27
]
For any
{\mathbf{u}}\in[H^{1}(T)]^{d}
, the following identities hold:

(4.7)

\nabla_{w}(Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}})={\mathcal{Q}}_{h}(\nabla{\mathbf{u}}),

(4.8)

\nabla_{w}\cdot Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}}={\mathcal{Q}}_{h}(\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{u}}).

We now establish the inf-sup condition for the bilinear form
b(\cdot,\cdot)
.

Lemma 4.4
.

(Inf-Sup Condition) There exists a constant
C>0
, independent of the mesh size
h
, such that for all
\zeta\in W_{h}
,

(4.9)

\sup_{{\mathbf{v}}\in V_{h}^{0}}\frac{(\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}},\zeta)}{{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{v}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}}\geq C\|\zeta\|.

Proof.

For any
\zeta\in W_{h}\subset L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
, it is known (see
[
2
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
) that there exists
\bar{{\mathbf{v}}}\in[H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)]^{d}
satisfying:

(4.10)

\frac{(\nabla\cdot\bar{{\mathbf{v}}},\zeta)}{\|\bar{{\mathbf{v}}}\|_{1}}\geq C\|\zeta\|.

Define
{\mathbf{v}}=Q_{h}\bar{{\mathbf{v}}}\in V_{h}^{0}
. We show that

(4.11)

{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{v}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\leq C\|\bar{{\mathbf{v}}}\|_{1}.

Using identity (
4.7
), we estimate:

\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}}\|_{T}^{2}=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|\nabla_{w}Q_{h}\bar{{\mathbf{v}}}\|_{T}^{2}=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|\mathcal{Q}_{h}\nabla\bar{{\mathbf{v}}}\|_{T}^{2}\leq\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|\nabla\bar{{\mathbf{v}}}\|_{T}^{2}.

Also,

\begin{split}\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\kappa^{-1}{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{0})_{T}=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\kappa^{-1}Q_{0}\bar{{\mathbf{v}}},Q_{0}\bar{{\mathbf{v}}})_{T}\leq\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|\bar{{\mathbf{v}}}\|_{T}^{2}.\end{split}

Applying trace inequality (
4.1
) and estimate (
4.4
) with
m=0
and
s=0,1
, we obtain:

\begin{split}\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|{\mathbf{v}}_{0}-{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\|^{2}_{\partial T}=&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|Q_{0}\bar{{\mathbf{v}}}-Q_{b}\bar{{\mathbf{v}}}\|^{2}_{\partial T}\\
\leq&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|Q_{0}\bar{{\mathbf{v}}}-\bar{{\mathbf{v}}}\|^{2}_{\partial T}\\
\leq&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-2}\|Q_{0}\bar{{\mathbf{v}}}-\bar{{\mathbf{v}}}\|^{2}_{T}+\|Q_{0}\bar{{\mathbf{v}}}-\bar{{\mathbf{v}}}\|^{2}_{1,T}\\
\leq&C\|\bar{{\mathbf{v}}}\|_{1}^{2}.\end{split}

Combining these gives (
4.11
).

Using (
4.8
), we find:

(\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}},\zeta)_{T}=(\nabla_{w}\cdot Q_{h}\bar{{\mathbf{v}}},\zeta)_{T}=(\mathcal{Q}_{h}\nabla\cdot\bar{{\mathbf{v}}},\zeta)_{T}=(\nabla\cdot\bar{{\mathbf{v}}},\zeta)_{T}.

Combining the estimate above with (
4.10
) and (
4.11
), we obtain

\frac{(\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}},\zeta)_{T}}{{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{v}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}}\geq C\frac{(\nabla\cdot\bar{{\mathbf{v}}},\zeta)_{T}}{\|\bar{{\mathbf{v}}}\|_{1}}\geq C\|\zeta\|.

This completes the proof of the Lemma.

∎

Theorem 4.5
.

The weak Galerkin Algorithm
3.1
for the Brinkman equations (
1.1
) admits a unique solution.

Proof.

Assume that there exist two distinct solutions
({\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{(1)},p_{h}^{(1)})
and
({\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{(2)},p_{h}^{(2)})
in
V_{h}^{0}\times W_{h}
. Define their difference:

{\Xi}_{{\mathbf{u}}_{h}}=\{{\Xi}_{{\mathbf{u}}_{0}},{\Xi}_{{\mathbf{u}}_{b}}\}={\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{(1)}-{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{(2)}\in V_{h}^{0},\qquad{\Xi}_{p_{h}}=p_{h}^{(1)}-p_{h}^{(2)}\in W_{h}.

Then
({\Xi}_{{\mathbf{u}}_{h}},{\Xi}_{p_{h}})
satisfies the homogeneous system:

(4.12)

\begin{split}(\nabla_{w}{\Xi}_{{\mathbf{u}}_{h}},\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}}_{h})+(\kappa^{-1}{\Xi}_{{\mathbf{u}}_{0}},{\mathbf{v}}_{0})+s({\Xi}_{{\mathbf{u}}_{h}},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})-(\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}_{h},{\Xi}_{p_{h}})=&0,\qquad\qquad\forall{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\in V_{h}^{0},\\
(\nabla_{w}\cdot{\Xi}_{{\mathbf{u}}_{h}},q_{h})=&0,\qquad\qquad\forall q_{h}\in W_{h}.\end{split}

Taking test functions
{\mathbf{v}}_{h}={\Xi}_{{\mathbf{u}}_{h}}
and
q_{h}={\Xi}_{p_{h}}
in (
4.12
) gives:

{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\Xi}_{{\mathbf{u}}_{h}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}=0.

By Lemma
4.2
, we deduce
{\Xi}_{{\mathbf{u}}_{h}}\equiv 0
.
Substituting
{\Xi}_{{\mathbf{u}}_{h}}\equiv 0
into the first equation of (
4.12
) yields:

(\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}_{h},{\Xi}_{p_{h}})=0,\qquad\forall{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}.

By the inf-sup condition (
4.9
), this implies

\|{\Xi}_{p_{h}}\|=0
, i.e.,
{\Xi}_{p_{h}}\equiv 0
.

Thus, we conclude that

{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{(1)}\equiv{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{(2)}
and
p_{h}^{(1)}\equiv p_{h}^{(2)}
, proving uniqueness.
∎

5.
Error Equations

Let
{\mathbf{u}}
and
p
represent the exact solutions to the Brinkman problem (
1.1
), and let

{\mathbf{u}}_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}
and
p_{h}\in W_{h}
denote the corresponding discrete solutions produced by the WG Algorithm
3.1
. We define the error functions
e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{h}}
and
e_{p_{h}}
for velocity and pressure as:

(5.1)

e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{h}}=Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}_{h},\qquad e_{p_{h}}={\mathcal{Q}}_{h}p-p_{h}.

Lemma 5.1
.

The error functions
e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{h}}
and
e_{p_{h}}
defined in (
5.1
) satisfy the following system of error equations:

(5.2)

\begin{split}&(\nabla_{w}e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{h}},\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}}_{h})+s(e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{h}},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})+(\kappa^{-1}e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{0}},{\mathbf{v}}_{0})-(\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}_{h},e_{p_{h}})\\
=&\ell_{1}({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})+\ell_{2}({\mathbf{v}}_{h},p)+s(Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}}_{h}),\qquad\forall{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\in V_{h}^{0},\\
&(\nabla_{w}\cdot e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{h}},q_{h})=0,\qquad\forall q_{h}\in W_{h},\end{split}

where
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
are given by:

\ell_{1}({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0},({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)\nabla{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T},

\ell_{2}({\mathbf{v}}_{h},p)=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}-\langle({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)p,({\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0})\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}.

Proof.

Using identity (
4.7
) and integrating by parts, we choose
{\bm{\varphi}}={\mathcal{Q}}_{h}\nabla{\mathbf{u}}
in (
2.2
), yielding:

(5.3)

\begin{split}&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla_{w}Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}},\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}}_{h})_{T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}(\nabla{\mathbf{u}}),\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}}_{h})_{T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}\nabla{\mathbf{u}})_{T}+\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}\nabla{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla{\mathbf{v}}_{0},\nabla{\mathbf{u}})_{T}+\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}\nabla{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}-({\mathbf{v}}_{0},\Delta{\mathbf{u}})_{T}+\langle\nabla{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}},{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\rangle_{\partial T}+\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}\nabla{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}-({\mathbf{v}}_{0},\Delta{\mathbf{u}})_{T}+\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0},({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)\nabla{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T},\end{split}

where the boundary contribution
\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\langle\nabla{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\rangle_{\partial T}=\langle\nabla{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\rangle_{\partial\Omega}=0
because
{\mathbf{v}}_{b}=0
on
\partial\Omega
.

Applying (
2.4
) with
w={\mathcal{Q}}_{h}p
and using integration by parts, we get:

(5.4)

\begin{split}&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}_{h},{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}p)_{T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}p)_{T}+\langle{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}p,({\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0})\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{v}}_{0},p)_{T}+\langle{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}p,({\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0})\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}-({\mathbf{v}}_{0},\nabla p)_{T}+\langle p,{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}+\langle{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}p,({\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0})\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}-({\mathbf{v}}_{0},\nabla p)_{T}+\langle({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)p,({\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0})\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T},\end{split}

where we again used the vanishing trace of
{\mathbf{v}}_{b}
on
\partial\Omega
.

Subtracting (
5.4
) from (
5.3
) and incorporating the first equation from (
1.1
), we derive:

\begin{split}&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla_{w}Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}},\nabla_{w}{\mathbf{v}}_{h})_{T}+(\kappa^{-1}Q_{0}{\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}}_{0})_{T}+s(Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})-(\nabla_{w}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}_{h},{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}p)_{T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}-({\mathbf{v}}_{0},\Delta{\mathbf{u}})_{T}+\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0},({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)\nabla{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}+({\mathbf{v}}_{0},\nabla p)_{T}\\
&-\langle({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)p,({\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0})\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}+(\kappa^{-1}{\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}}_{0})_{T}+s(Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}({\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{f}})_{T}+\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0},({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)\nabla{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
&-\langle({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)p,({\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0})\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}+s(Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}}_{h}).\end{split}

Subtracting the first equation of (
3.5
) from the above leads to the first error equation in (
5.2
).

Note that using (
4.8
) and the second equation of (
1.1
), we have:

0=(\nabla_{w}\cdot Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}},q_{h})=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{u}},q_{h})_{T}=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{u}},q_{h})_{T}=0.

Subtracting this from the second equation of (
3.5
) yields the second part of (
5.2
), completing the proof.

∎

6.
Error Estimates

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
{\mathbf{u}}\in[H^{k+1}(\Omega)]^{d}
,
q\in H^{k}(\Omega)
, and let
{\mathbf{v}}_{h}=\{{\mathbf{v}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\}\in V_{h}^{0}
,
q_{h}\in W_{h}
. Then the following bounds hold:

(6.1)

|\ell_{1}({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})|\leq Ch^{k}\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{v}}_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|},

(6.2)

|\ell_{2}({\mathbf{v}}_{h},p)|\leq Ch^{k}\|p\|_{k}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{v}}_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|},

(6.3)

|s(Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})|\leq Ch^{k}\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{v}}_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}.

Proof.

To derive (
6.1
), we apply the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, the trace inequality (
4.1
), and the approximation estimate (
4.5
) with
n=k-1
:

\begin{split}|\ell_{1}({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})|\leq&(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|{\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\|_{\partial T}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}\|({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)\nabla{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\|_{\partial T}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{v}}_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)\nabla{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\|_{T}^{2}+h_{T}^{2}\|({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)\nabla{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\|_{1,T}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&Ch^{k}\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{v}}_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}.\end{split}

To estimate (
6.2
), we again use the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, the trace inequality (
4.1
), along with (
4.3
) with
n=k-1
:

\begin{split}|\ell_{2}({\mathbf{v}}_{h},p)|\leq&(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|{\mathbf{v}}_{b}-{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\|_{\partial T}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}\|({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)p\|_{\partial T}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{v}}_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)p\|_{T}^{2}+h_{T}^{2}\|({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)p\|_{1,T}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&Ch^{k}\|p\|_{k}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{v}}_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}.\end{split}

For (
6.3
), using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, the trace inequality (
4.1
), and estimate (
4.4
) with
m=k
yields:

\begin{split}|s(Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})|=&|\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\langle Q_{0}{\mathbf{u}}-Q_{b}{\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}}_{0}-{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\rangle_{\partial T}|\\
\leq&\Big(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|Q_{0}{\mathbf{u}}-Q_{b}{\mathbf{u}}\|^{2}_{\partial T}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|{\mathbf{v}}_{0}-{\mathbf{v}}_{b}\|^{2}_{\partial T}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&\Big(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|Q_{0}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}\|^{2}_{\partial T}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{v}}_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\\
\leq&\Big(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-2}\|Q_{0}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}\|^{2}_{T}+\|Q_{0}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}\|^{2}_{1,T}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{v}}_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\\
\leq&Ch^{k}\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{v}}_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}.\end{split}

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

.

) with regularity

7.
Error Analysis in the
L^{2}
Norm

To derive an error estimate in the
L^{2}
norm, we employ a classical duality argument. Recall that the velocity error is given by:

e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{h}}={\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}_{h}=\{e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{0}},e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{b}}\}=\{Q_{0}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}_{0},Q_{b}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}_{b}\}.

We now consider the corresponding dual problem associated with the Brinkman system (
1.1
). Specifically, we seek a pair
({\mathbf{w}},q)\in[H^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}\times H^{1}(\Omega)
satisfying:

(7.1)

\begin{split}-\Delta{\mathbf{w}}+\kappa^{-1}{\mathbf{w}}+\nabla q&=e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{0}},\qquad\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{w}}&=0,\qquad\quad\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
{\mathbf{w}}&=0,\quad\qquad\text{on}\ \partial\Omega.\end{split}

We assume the dual solution satisfies the regularity bound:

(7.2)

\|{\mathbf{w}}\|_{2}+\|q\|_{1}\leq C\|e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{0}}\|.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
({\mathbf{u}},p)\in[H^{k+1}(\Omega)]^{d}\times H^{k}(\Omega)
be the exact solutions to the Brinkman problem (
1.1
), and let

({\mathbf{u}}_{h},p_{h})\in V_{h}^{0}\times W_{h}
be its approximation via the Weak Galerkin Algorithm
3.1
. Under the regularity condition (
7.2
), there exists a constant
C
such that:

\|Q_{0}{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}_{0}\|\leq Ch^{k+1}(\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}+\|p\|_{k}).

Proof.

We test the first equation of the dual problem (
7.1
) with
e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{0}}
:

(7.3)

\begin{split}\|e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{0}}\|^{2}=&(-\Delta{\mathbf{w}}+\kappa^{-1}{\mathbf{w}}+\nabla q,e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{0}}).\end{split}

Using identity (
5.3
) with
{\mathbf{u}}={\mathbf{w}}
and
{\mathbf{v}}_{h}=e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{h}}
, we get:

\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(-\Delta{\mathbf{w}},e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{0}})_{T}=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla_{w}Q_{h}{\mathbf{w}},\nabla_{w}e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{h}})_{T}-\langle e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{b}}-e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{0}},({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)\nabla{\mathbf{w}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}.

Similarly, from identity (
5.4
) with
p=q
and
{\mathbf{v}}_{h}=e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{h}}
:

\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla q,e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{0}})=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}-(\nabla_{w}\cdot e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{h}},{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}q)_{T}+\langle({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)q,(e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{b}}-e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{0}})\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}.

Substituting the above two identities into the identity (
7.3
) and using the error equation (
5.2
) with
{\mathbf{v}}_{h}=Q_{h}{\mathbf{w}}
and
q_{h}={\mathcal{Q}}_{h}q
, we obtain:

(7.4)

\begin{split}&\|e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{0}}\|^{2}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla_{w}Q_{h}{\mathbf{w}},\nabla_{w}e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{h}})_{T}-\langle e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{b}}-e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{0}},({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)\nabla{\mathbf{w}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
&-(\nabla_{w}\cdot e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{h}},{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}q)_{T}+\langle({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)q,(e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{b}}-e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{0}})\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}+(\kappa^{-1}Q_{0}{\mathbf{w}},e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{0}})_{T}\\
&+s(Q_{h}{\mathbf{w}},e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{h}})-s(Q_{h}{\mathbf{w}},e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{h}})\\
=&\ell_{1}({\mathbf{u}},Q_{h}{\mathbf{w}})+\ell_{2}(Q_{h}{\mathbf{w}},p)+s(Q_{h}{\mathbf{u}},Q_{h}{\mathbf{w}})+\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla_{w}\cdot(Q_{h}{\mathbf{w}}),e_{p_{h}})_{T}\\
&-\langle e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{b}}-e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{0}},({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)\nabla{\mathbf{w}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
&+\langle({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)q,(e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{b}}-e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{0}})\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}-s(Q_{h}{\mathbf{w}},e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{h}})\\
=&\ell_{1}({\mathbf{u}},Q_{h}{\mathbf{w}})+\ell_{2}(Q_{h}{\mathbf{w}},p)+\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla_{w}\cdot(Q_{h}{\mathbf{w}}),e_{p_{h}})_{T}\\
&-\langle e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{b}}-e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{0}},({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)\nabla{\mathbf{w}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}+\langle({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}-I)q,(e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{b}}-e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{0}})\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
&+s(Q_{h}{\mathbf{w}},{\mathbf{u}}_{h})\\
=&\sum_{i=1}^{6}I_{i}.\end{split}

We now estimate each term
I_{i}(i=1,\cdots,6)
individually.

Estimate of

4.1

\begin{split}|s(Q_{h}{\mathbf{w}},{\mathbf{u}}_{h})|=&|\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\langle Q_{0}{\mathbf{w}}-Q_{b}{\mathbf{w}},{\mathbf{u}}_{0}-{\mathbf{u}}_{b}\rangle_{\partial T}|\\
\leq&\Big(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|Q_{0}{\mathbf{w}}-Q_{b}{\mathbf{w}}\|_{\partial T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|{\mathbf{u}}_{0}-{\mathbf{u}}_{b}\|_{\partial T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&\Big(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-2}\|Q_{0}{\mathbf{w}}-{\mathbf{w}}\|_{T}^{2}+\|Q_{0}{\mathbf{w}}-{\mathbf{w}}\|_{1,T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\mathbf{u}}_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\\
\leq&Ch\|{\mathbf{w}}\|_{2}h^{k}(\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}+\|p\|_{k}).\end{split}

Substituting all bounds of
I_{i}
(
i=1,\cdots,6
) into (
7.4
) and invoking the regularity condition (
7.2
), we arrive at:

\|e_{{\mathbf{u}}_{0}}\|\leq Ch^{k+1}(\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{k+1}+\|p\|_{k}).

This completes the proof.
∎

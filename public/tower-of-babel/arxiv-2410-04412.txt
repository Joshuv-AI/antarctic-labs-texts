Log-Concave Sequences in Coding Theory
Minjia Shi, Xuan Wang, Junmin An, Jon-Lark Kim

Abstract
We introduce the notion of logarithmically concave (or log-concave) sequences in Coding Theory. A sequence a 0 , a 1 , … , a n a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{n} of real numbers is called log-concave if a i 2 ≥ a i − 1 ​ a i + 1 a_{i}^{2}\geq a_{i-1}a_{i+1} for all 1 ≤ i ≤ n − 1 1\leq i\leq n-1 . A natural sequence of positive numbers in coding theory is the weight distribution of a linear code consisting of the nonzero values among A i A_{i} ’s where A i A_{i} denotes the number of codewords of weight i i . We call a linear code log-concave if its nonzero weight distribution is log-concave. Our main contribution is to show that all binary general Hamming codes of length 2 r − 1 2^{r}-1 ( r = 3 r=3 or r ≥ 5 r\geq 5 ), the binary extended Hamming codes of length 2 r ​ ( r ≥ 3 ) 2^{r}~(r\geq 3) , and the second order Reed-Muller codes R ​ ( 2 , m ) ​ ( m ≥ 2 ) R(2,m)~(m\geq 2) are all log-concave while the homogeneous and projective second order Reed-Muller codes are either log-concave, or 1-gap log-concave. Furthermore, we show that any MDS [ n , k ] [n,k] code over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} satisfying 3 ⩽ k ⩽ n / 2 + 3 3\leqslant k\leqslant n/2+3 is log-concave if q ⩾ q 0 ​ ( n , k ) q\geqslant q_{0}(n,k) which is the larger root of a quadratic polynomial. Hence, we expect that the concept of log-concavity in coding theory will stimulate many interesting problems.

Introduction

Many interesting sequences in algebra, combinatorics, geometry, analysis, probability and statistics have been known to be log-concave or unimodal. We recall that a sequence
a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
of real numbers is called
log-concave
if
a_{i}^{2}\geqslant a_{i-1}a_{i+1}
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1
. A sequence
a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
of real numbers is called
unimodal
if there is an index
0\leqslant j\leqslant n
such that
a_{0}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant a_{j-1}\leqslant a_{j}\geqslant a_{j+1}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant a_{n}
. For instance, the well-known binomial coefficient sequence
{n\choose i}

(0\leqslant i\leqslant n)
is log-concave and unimodal. We refer readers to the survey papers by Stanley
[
20
]
and Brenti
[
4
]
.

Some recent mathematical breakthroughs on log-concavity occurred in matroid theory
[
22
]
. Matroid theory shares common concepts in graph theory and linear algebra. More precisely, a matroid
M
is a pair
(E,I)
, where
E
is the finite set of elements of the matroid, and
I
a family of subsets of
E
called the independent sets of the matroid, satisfying (i) any subset of an independent set is independent and (ii) if
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
are independent and
|I_{1}|<|I_{2}|
, then there exists
e\in I_{2}-I_{1}
such
that
I_{1}\cup\{e\}
is independent. The Tutte polynomial of a matroid is a generalization of the chromatic polynomial of a graph.
The
Tutte polynomial

T(M;x,y)
of a matroid
M
is defined as

T(M;x,y)=\sum_{A\subset E}{(x-1)^{{\mbox{rk}}(E)-{\mbox{rk}}(A)}}{(y-1)^{|A|-{\mbox{rk}}(A)}}.

A special case of the Tutte polynomial is the
characteristic polynomial

\chi(M,q)=(-1)^{{\mbox{rk}}(E)}T(M;1-q,0)=\sum_{A\subset E}(-1)^{|A|}q^{r-{\mbox{rk}}(A)}.

June Huh, et al. proved the long-standing conjecture that the sequence of absolute values of coefficients of the characteristic polynomial
\chi(M,q)
of a matroid is unimodal and log-concave
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
10
]
. There are two standard examples of matroids: the cycle matroid of a graph and a vector matroid defined by a matrix (this might be the origin of term matroid). Vector matroids occurs naturally in error-correcting codes as seen below.

A vector matroid is constructed from a vector space
V=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m}
. Let
{\bf{v}}_{1},{\bf{v}}_{2},\dots,{\bf{v}}_{n}
in
V
. We take
E=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
and the subset
I
of
E
is independent if and only if
\{{\bf{v}}_{i}~|~i\in I\}
is linearly independent in
V
. Then we can represent the elements of the vector matroid as the columns of an
m\times n
matrix over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with independence as linearly independence of the columns of the matrix.
A linear
[n,k]
code
\cal C
can be described in terms of a generator matrix or a parity check matrix. Hence a linear code is related to a special vector matroid.
The Tutte polynomial
T(M;x,y)
of that vector matroid has a connection with the weight enumerator
W_{\mbox{$\cal C$}}(x,y)
in two variables of the linear
[n,k]
code
\cal C

[
6
]
it comes from:

W_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)=y^{n-{\mbox{dim}}(\mbox{$\cal C$})}(x-y)^{{\mbox{dim}}(\mbox{$\cal C$})}T\left(M;\frac{x+(q-1)y}{x-y},\frac{x}{y}\right).

(1)

It is easy to see that when a linear code has only even weight, its weight distribution is not log-concave in general. For example, the binary
[n,n-1,2]
even code has 1, 0,
\binom{n}{2},\dots
as the first three vectors and so the sequence is not log-concave.
In fact, since the characteristic polynomial of a connected graph
[
17
, p.65]
has nonzero coefficients except for the constant, it is natural to consider the nonzero weight distribution of a linear code. However, although the characteristic polynomial of a matroid is log-concave in general, the nonzero weight distribution of a linear codes is not always log-concave. Hence the above equation (
1
) does not seem to give us proper information whether the nonzero weight distribution of a linear code is log-concave or not.
Therefore, it appears that the determination of a log-concavity of the nonzero weight distribution of a random linear code is a very difficult problem since it is an NP-hard problem to find the exact minimum weight of a random linear code
[
21
]
.

Currently, the only method to determine whether a given nonzero weight distribution of a linear code is log-concave is to check actually the log-concavity condition of the definition. In general, it is complicated to check the log-concavity because the weights tend to involve several parameters and complex summations. To our surprise, after using various estimation techniques, we were able to show that several families of well-known linear codes have log-concave nonzero weight distributions.

The main purpose of the paper is to show that all binary general Hamming codes of length
2^{r}-1
(
r=3
or
r\geq 5
), the extended Hamming codes of length
2^{r}~(r\geq 3)
, the second order Reed-Muller codes
R(2,m)~(m\geq 2)
are log-concave while the homogeneous and projective second order Reed-Muller codes are log-concave or 1-gap log-concave. Furthermore, we show that MDS codes of moderate lengths and dimensions are log-concave. Since we considered a few families of linear codes, we expect that the log-concavity topic in coding theory will stimulate many research problems in the future and will be a bridge between combinatorics and coding theory.

The paper consists of six sections. In Section 2, we recall some basic notions and definitions from coding theory. In Section 3, we show that binary Hamming codes except for one length and the binary extended Hamming codes are log-concave. Section 3 proves that the second order Reed-Muller codes are log-concave and that homogeneous and projective second order
q
-ary Reed Muller codes
are 1-gap log-concave. Section 5 shows that MDS codes of dimension at most
n/2+3
under some condition are log-concave. In Section 6, we conclude with a summary of obtained results, and offer some challenging open problems.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite field with
q
elements, where
q=p^{r}
for some prime
p
and a positive integer
r\geq 1
. We denote the
n
-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
by

\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}=\{\mbox{\bf x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})~|~x_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}{\mbox{ for all }}i\}.

Definition 1
.

A linear
[n,k]
code (shortly
[n,k]
code)
\mathcal{C}
of length
n
with dimension
k
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a
k
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
.
If
q=2
,
\mathcal{C}
is called a binary code.
The weight of
{\bf{x}}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
is the
number of nonzero coordinates, denoted by wt(
x
). The Hamming distance
d({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
between
{\bf{x}},{\bf{y}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
is wt
({\bf{x}}-{\bf{y}})
.
The minimum distance (weight)
d
of a linear code

\cal C
is the minimum of wt(
x
),
{\bf{0\neq x}}\in\mbox{$\cal C$}
. We denote it by an
[n,k,d]
code over
\mathbb{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.
A Maximum Distance Separable(MDS) code is an
[n,k,d]
code satisfying
d=n-k+1
.

Definition 2
.

A generator matrix for an
[n,k]
code
\cal C
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a

k\times n
matrix
G
whose rows form a basis for
\cal C
.
A generator matrix of the form
[I_{k}|A]
where
I_{k}
is the
k\times k
identity matrix is called in standard form.
Given an
[n,k]
code
\cal C
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, there is an
(n-k)\times n
matrix
H
, called a parity check matrix for
\cal C
, defined
by

\mbox{$\cal C$}=\{\mbox{\bf x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}~|~H{\mbox{\bf x}}^{T}={\bf 0}\}.

Definition 3
.

The dual of
\cal C
is
\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp}=\{\mbox{\bf x}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}~|~\mbox{\bf x}\cdot{\bf{c}}=0{\mbox{ for any }}{\bf{c}}\in\mbox{$\cal C$}\}
, where the dot product is the usual inner product. A linear code
\cal C
is called self-dual if
\mbox{$\cal C$}=\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp}
and self-orthogonal if
\mbox{$\cal C$}\subseteq\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp}
.

Definition 4
.

Given an
[n,k,d]
code
\cal C
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, define
A_{i}
as the number of codewords of
\cal C
whose weight is
i
. The sequence
A_{0},A_{1},\dots,A_{n}
is called the weight distribution of
\cal C
, denoted by
A(\mbox{$\cal C$})
. The weight enumerator
A_{\mbox{$\cal C$}}
is defined by

A_{\mbox{$\cal C$}}(x,y)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}A_{i}x^{n-i}y^{i}=x^{n}+A_{d}x^{n-d}y^{d}+\cdots+A_{n}y^{n}.

Any
[n,k,d]
code
\cal C
has
A_{0}=1
,
A_{1}=A_{2}=\dots=A_{d-1}=0
, and
A_{d}>0
.
The weight distribution
A(\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp})
of the dual of
\cal C
is related to
A(\mbox{$\cal C$})
by the following identity.

Theorem 1
.

(The MacWilliams identity) If
\cal C
is a linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then

A_{\mbox{$\cal C$}^{\perp}}(x,y)=\frac{1}{|\mbox{$\cal C$}|}A_{\mbox{$\cal C$}}(x+(q-1)y,x-y).

In this paper, we are mainly interested in the nonzero weight distribution defined as follows.

Definition 5
.

Let
A(\mbox{$\cal C$})
be the weight distribution of
\cal C
. The nonzero weight distribution of
\cal C
, denoted by
a(\mbox{$\cal C$})
, is the subsequence of
A(\mbox{$\cal C$})
consisting of the nonzero values of
A(\mbox{$\cal C$})
in the order of appearance in
A(\mbox{$\cal C$}).
For instance, the first two elements of
a(\mbox{$\cal C$})
are
a_{0}=1
and
a_{1}=A_{d}
.

Definition 6
.

A sequence
a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
of real numbers is called log-concave if
a_{i}^{2}\geq a_{i-1}a_{i+1}
for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
.

Definition 7
.

A sequence
A=\{a_{k}\}_{k=0}^{n}
is called unimodal if there is an index
0\leq j\leq n
such that
a_{0}\leq\cdots\leq a_{j-1}\leq a_{j}\geq a_{j+1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n}
.

Definition 8
.

A linear code
\mathcal{C}
is called log-concave if its nonzero weight distribution is log-concave and unimodal if its nonzero weight distribution is unimodal.

The log-concavity property goes back to Newton as the next result shows.

Theorem 2
.

(
[
20
]
)
Let
P(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}a_{i}x^{i}
be a (real) polynomal with real zeroes. Then
a_{j}^{2}\geqslant a_{j-1}a_{j+1}
for
j=1,\dots,n-1
.

Example 1
.

Let
\cal C
be the binary
[4,3,2]
code consisting of all even weight vectors.
Then its weight distribution is 1, 0, 6, 0, 1. Since
0^{2}-1\cdot 6<0
, this sequence is not log-concave. However, if we consider only nonzero values 1, 6, 1, then it is log-concave and unimodal. Hence the code is log-concave and unimodal. Also note that
P(x)=1+\binom{2}{1}\cdot 6+x^{2}=1+12x+x^{2}
has two real roots since
D=12^{2}-4>0
. This also confirms by Theorem
2
that
1,6,1
is log-concave.

In order to consider non log-concave sequences, we introduce the notion of
an
m
-gap log-concave sequence as follows.

Definition 9
.

A sequence
A_{i}
of integers is
m
-gap log-concave
if
A_{i}^{2}\geq A_{i-1}A_{i+1}
does not holds for exactly
m

\mathcal{C}
is
m
-gap log-concave
if its nonzero weight distribution is
m
-gap log-concave.

We note that
0

1

Little is known about the property of the nonzero weight distribution of a linear code.
In particular, we want to know which linear codes are log-concave. As a warm-up, we consider some trivial codes, and show that they are log-concave.

Proposition 1
.

(i)

The whole space
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
with
n\geqslant 2
is log-concave.

(ii)

The binary even
[n,n-1]
code
\mathcal{E}_{n}
for any
n\geqslant 4
is log-concave.

Proof.

(i) The weight distribution of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
is the binomial distribution

{n\choose 0},{n\choose 1},\dots,{n\choose i},\dots,{n\choose n}
where all values are positive. Since the binomial distribution is log-concave,
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
is log-concave.

(ii) Note that
\mathcal{E}_{n}
consists of all even weight vectors of length
n
. So the nonzero weight distribution of
\mathcal{E}_{n}
is the sequence
{n\choose 0},{n\choose 2},\dots,{n\choose 2i},\dots
. If
n
is even, then as the all-ones vector is in
\mathcal{E}_{n}
, this sequence is symmetric. Hence we may assume that
2\leqslant 2i\leqslant n/2-1
. On the other hand, if
n
is odd, we assume that
2\leqslant 2i\leqslant n-2
.
Then for each case of
n
we have the following computation.

{n\choose 2i}^{2}\geqslant{n\choose 2i+2}{n\choose 2i-2}\Leftrightarrow\frac{(2i+2)(2i+1)}{(2i)(2i-1)}\cdot\frac{(n-2i+2)(n-2i+1)}{(n-2i)(n-2i-1)}>1.

Hence, the claim of (ii) of Proposition
1
is true.
∎

3
Hamming codes

It is well known that the binary Hamming code
\mathcal{H}_{m}
is the dual of the binary Simplex code
\mathcal{S}_{m}
and the binary extended Hamming code
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{m}
is the dual of the first order Reed-Muller code.
Using the MacWillams identity, the weight enumerators of
\mathcal{H}_{m}
and

\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{m}
are known in terms of Krawtchouk polynomials or recursively (see
[
12
, Example 7.3.4]
,
[
15
, p. 129]
,
[
16
, Remark 15.1.69]
). For our purpose, we give an explicit formula for these weight enumerators.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{m}
and
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{m}
be the binary Hamming
[n=2^{m}-1,n-m,3]
code and its extended code, respectively.
Then

(i)~A_{w}(\mathcal{H}_{m})=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\binom{n}{2i}+(-1)^{i}n\binom{(n-1)/2}{i}\right)&{\mbox{if }}w=2i\geqslant 0,\\
\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\binom{n}{2i+1}-(-1)^{i}n\binom{(n-1)/2}{i}\right)&{\mbox{if }}w=2i+1\geqslant 1,\end{cases}

and

(ii)~A_{w}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{m})=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{n}\left(\binom{n}{2i}+(-1)^{i}(n-1)\binom{n/2}{i}\right)&{\mbox{if }}w=2i\geqslant 0,\\
0&{\mbox{if }}w=2i+1\geqslant 1.\end{cases}

Proof.

(i)

Since the weight enumerator of
\mathcal{S}_{m}
is
x^{n}+nx^{(n-1)/2}y^{(n+1)/2}
, the weight enumerator of
\mathcal{H}_{m}
is, by the MacWilliams identity,

W_{\mathcal{H}_{m}}(x,y)=\frac{1}{n+1}\left((x+y)^{n}+n(x+y)^{(n-1)/2}(x-y)^{(n+1)/2}\right),

where
n=2^{m}-1
. By the binomial expansion, we have

\displaystyle(x+y)^{(n-1)/2}(x-y)^{(n+1)/2}

\displaystyle=(x-y)(x^{2}-y^{2})^{(n-1)/2}

\displaystyle=(x-y)\sum_{i=0}^{(n-1)/2}\binom{(n-1)/2}{i}(-1)^{i}x^{n-1-2i}y^{2i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{(n-1)/2}\binom{(n-1)/2}{i}(-1)^{i}x^{n-2i}y^{2i}

\displaystyle~~~-\sum_{i=0}^{(n-1)/2}\binom{(n-1)/2}{i}(-1)^{i}x^{n-1-2i}y^{2i+1}.

Hence,

A_{2i}(\mathcal{H}_{m})=\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\binom{n}{2i}+(-1)^{i}n\binom{(n-1)/2}{i}\right)

and

A_{2i+1}(\mathcal{H}_{m})=\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\binom{n}{2i+1}-(-1)^{i}n\binom{(n-1)/2}{i}\right).

(ii)

Since the weight enumerator of the first order Reed-Muller code
\mathcal{R}(1,m)
is

x^{n}+y^{n}+(2n-2)x^{n/2}y^{n/2},

where
n=2^{m}
, the weight enumerator of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{m}
is

W_{\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{m}}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2n}\left((x+y)^{n}+(x-y)^{n}+(2n-2)(x+y)^{n/2}(x-y)^{n/2}\right).

That is to say
A_{2i+1}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{m})=0
and

A_{2i}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{m})=\frac{1}{n}\left(\binom{n}{2i}+(-1)^{i}(n-1)\binom{n/2}{i}\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

The following lemma lists some well known properties of binomial coefficients which are used in the proofs of the next theorems.

Lemma 2
.

We have the following properties for the binomial coefficients:

(i)

If
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
, then we have the Pascal’s Triangle

\binom{n}{k}=\binom{n-1}{k}+\binom{n-1}{k-1}.

(ii)

If
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
, then

\binom{n}{k}=\frac{n-k+1}{k}\binom{n}{k-1}=\frac{n}{n-k}\binom{n-1}{k}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad k\binom{n}{k}=n\binom{n-1}{k-1}.

(iii)

For given positive integers
m
and
n
, we have the Vandermonde identity

\sum_{r=0}^{k}\binom{m}{r}\binom{n}{k-r}=\binom{m+n}{k}.

In particular, for any odd
n
and positive integer
i
,

\binom{n}{2i}>\binom{(n-1)/2}{i}\binom{(n+1)/2}{i}.

In Example
3
we showed that if

then

is log-concave. Furthermore, we show that

Theorem 3

If

First, we claim that if

Theorem 6
.

The second order Reed-Muller code
\mathcal{R}(2,m)
is log-concave for
m\geq 2
.

Proof.

We first show that the nonzero weight distribution of
\mathcal{R}(2,m)
is unimodal with largest term
A_{2^{m-1}}
.
For
1\leq j\leq\lfloor{m\over 2}\rfloor-1
, we have

A_{2^{m-1}-2^{m-(j+1)-1}}/A_{2^{m-1}-2^{m-j-1}}={2^{2j+2}\over 2^{2j+2}-1}(2^{m-2j-1}-1)(2^{m-2j}-1)\geq 1.

To show that
A_{2^{m-1}}
is the largest term, we consider the cases when
m
is even and odd separately.
If
m
is odd, then

A_{2^{m-1}-2^{m-\left\lfloor{m\over 2}\right\rfloor-1}}\leq 2^{{m-1\over 2}^{2}+{m-1\over 2}}{\prod_{i=2}^{m}(2^{i}-1)\over\prod_{i=1}^{m-1\over 2}(2^{2i}-1)}\leq A_{2^{m}-1}.

On the other hand, if
m
is even, then

\displaystyle A_{2^{m-1}-2^{m-\left\lfloor{m\over 2}\right\rfloor-1}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{{m\over 2}^{2}+{m\over 2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(2^{i}-1)\over\prod_{i=1}^{m\over 2}(2^{2i}-1)}

\displaystyle=2^{{m\over 2}^{2}-{m\over 2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}(2^{i}-1)\over\prod_{i=1}^{{m\over 2}-1}(2^{2i}-1)}2^{m}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(2^{{m\over 2}^{2}-{m\over 2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(2^{i}-1)\over\prod_{i=1}^{{m\over 2}-1}(2^{2i}-1)}\right)\leq A_{2^{m}-1}.

Since the weight distribution of
\mathcal{R}(2,m)
is symmetric with respect to
A_{2^{m-1}}
, it is sufficient to show that the sequence
A_{2^{m-1}-2^{m-j-1}}
is log-concave for
1\leq j\leq\lfloor{m\over 2}\rfloor
. We consider three cases.

Case 1) Let
j=1
. Then

\displaystyle A_{2^{m-1}-2^{m-3}}

\displaystyle={64\over 21}(2^{m-3}-1)(2^{m-2}-1)(2^{m-1}-1)(2^{m}-1)

\displaystyle={16\over 7}(2^{m-3}-1)(2^{m-2}-1)A_{2^{m-1}-A^{m-2}}

\displaystyle\leq(A_{2^{m-1}-A^{m-2}})^{2}.

Case 2) Suppose
2\leq j\leq\lfloor{m\over 2}\rfloor-1
. Then we have

\displaystyle A_{2^{m-1}-2^{m-(j+1)-1}}A_{2^{m-1}-2^{m-(j-1)-1}}

\displaystyle=(A_{2^{m-1}-2^{m-j-1}})^{2}\left({2^{2j+2}-4\over 2^{2j+2}-1}\right){\prod_{i=m-2j-1}^{m-2j}(2^{i}-1)\over\prod_{i=m-2j+1}^{m-2j+2}(2^{2i}-1)}

\displaystyle\leq(A_{2^{m-1}-2^{m-j-1}})^{2}.

Case 3) Suppose
j=\lfloor{m\over 2}\rfloor
. We consider two subcases as follows.

Firstly, we assume that
m
is odd. We want to show that

A_{2^{m-1}}A_{2^{m-1}-2^{m-{m-1\over 2}}}\leq A_{2^{m-1}-2^{m-{m-1\over 2}-1}}^{2},

that is,

A_{2^{m-1}}\leq A_{2^{m-1}-2^{m-{m-1\over 2}-1}}^{2}/A_{2^{m-1}-2^{m-{m-1\over 2}}}.

Since

\displaystyle A_{2^{m-1}-2^{m-{m-1\over 2}-1}}/A_{2^{m-1}-2^{m-{m-1\over 2}}}

\displaystyle=2^{({m-1\over 2})^{2}+({m-1\over 2})}{\prod_{i=2}^{m}(2^{i}-1)\over\prod_{i=1}^{m-1\over 2}(2^{2i}-1)}\bigg/2^{({m-3\over 2})^{2}+({m-3\over 2})}{\prod_{i=4}^{m}(2^{i}-1)\over\prod_{i=1}^{m-3\over 2}(2^{2i}-1)}

\displaystyle=2^{2({m-1\over 2})}\cdot{(2^{2}-1)(2^{3}-1)\over 2^{2({m-1\over 2})}-1}

\displaystyle=21\cdot{2^{m-1}\over 2^{m-1}-1},

we only need to show that

A_{2^{m-1}}\leq 21\cdot{2^{m-1}\over 2^{m-1}-1}\cdot A_{2^{m-1}-2^{m-{m-1\over 2}-1}}.

Let
B_{\ell}=(2^{m-2\ell}-1)\cdot A_{2^{m-1}-2^{m-\ell-1}}
for
1\leq\ell\leq{m-1\over 2}
. Then

A_{2^{m-1}}=2\left(2^{m}-1+\sum_{\ell=1}^{{m-1\over 2}}B_{\ell}\right).

For
1\leq\ell\leq{m-1\over 2}-1
,

\displaystyle B_{\ell+1}/B_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\left({2^{m-2\ell-2}-1\over 2^{m-2\ell}-1}\right)\cdot A_{2^{m-1}-2^{m-(\ell+1)-1}}/A_{2^{m-1}-2^{m-\ell-1}}

\displaystyle={2^{2\ell+2}\over 2^{2\ell+2}-1}(2^{m-2\ell-2}-1)(2^{m-2\ell-1}-1).

So

\displaystyle B_{\ell}

\displaystyle={2^{2\ell+2}-1\over 2^{2\ell+2}}\cdot{1\over(2^{m-2\ell-2}-1)(2^{m-2\ell-1}-1)}\cdot B_{\ell+1}

\displaystyle\leq{1\over(2^{m-2\ell-2}-1)(2^{m-2\ell-1}-1)}\cdot B_{\ell+1}\leq{1\over 3}\cdot B_{\ell+1}.

\displaystyle A_{2^{m-1}}

\displaystyle=2\left(2^{m}-1+\sum_{\ell=1}^{m-1\over 2}B_{\ell}\right)\leq 2\left(2^{m}-1+\sum_{\ell=1}^{m-1\over 2}\left({1\over 3}\right)^{\ell-1}\cdot B_{m-1\over 2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(2^{m}-1+{3\over 2}\cdot B_{m-1\over 2}\right)=3\cdot A_{2^{m-1}-2^{m-{m-1\over 2}-1}}+2^{m+1}-2

\displaystyle\leq 21\cdot{2^{m-1}\over 2^{m-1}-1}\cdot A_{2^{m-1}-2^{m-{m-1\over 2}-1}}.

The last inequality comes from that

A_{2^{m-1}-2^{m-{m-1\over 2}-1}}=(2^{{m-1\over 2}^{2}+{m-1\over 2}}){\prod_{i=2}^{m}(2^{i}-1)\over\prod_{i=1}^{m-1\over 2}(2^{2i}-1)}>2(2^{m}-1).

Secondly, we assume that
m
is even. Then we have

A_{2^{m-1}-2^{m-{m\over 2}-1}}/A_{2^{m-1}-2^{m-{m\over 2}}}=3\cdot{2^{m}\over 2^{m}-1}.

So we need to show that

A_{2^{m-1}}\leq 3\cdot{2^{m}\over 2^{m}-1}\cdot A_{2^{m-1}-2^{m-{m\over 2}-1}}.

Let
B_{\ell}=(2^{m-2\ell}-1)\cdot A_{2^{m-1}-2^{m-\ell-1}}
for
1\leq\ell\leq{m\over 2}
. Then

\displaystyle A_{2^{m-1}}

\displaystyle=2\left(2^{m}-1+\sum_{\ell=1}^{{m\over 2}-1}B_{\ell}\right)\leq 2\left(2^{m}-1+\sum_{\ell=1}^{{m\over 2}-1}\left({1\over 3}\right)^{\ell-1}\cdot B_{{m\over 2}-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(2^{m}-1+{3\over 2}\cdot B_{{m\over 2}-1}\right)\leq 2\left(2^{m}-1+{1\over 2}\cdot B_{{m\over 2}}\right)

\displaystyle=A_{2^{m-1}-2^{m-{m\over 2}-1}}+2^{m+1}-2\leq 3\cdot{2^{m-1}\over 2^{m-1}-1}\cdot A_{2^{m-1}-2^{m-{m-1\over 2}-1}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 7
.

The second order homogeneous Reed-Muller code
HRM_{q}(2,m)
and the second order projective Reed-Muller code
PRM_{q}(2,m)
are
1
-gap log-concave or log-concave for

Proof.

Since there is a one-to-one corresponds between
HRM_{q}(2,m+1)
and
PRM_{q}(2,m)
by Lemma
5
, we just consider the log-concavity for
HRM_{q}(2,m)
.

Let
A^{q}_{\tau,j}=A_{q^{m}-q^{m-1}-\tau q^{m-j-1}(q-1)}
. We first show that
A^{q}_{1,j}
and
A^{q}_{-1,j}
are both increasing sequences. For
2\leq j\leq\lfloor{m\over 2}\rfloor-1
, and
\tau\in\{1,-1\}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}A^{q}_{\tau,j+1}/A^{q}_{\tau,j}&={q^{(j+1)^{2}}(q^{j+1}+\tau)\over q^{j^{2}}(q^{j}+\tau)}\cdot{\prod_{i=m-2j-1}^{m}(q^{i}-1)\prod_{i=1}^{j}(q^{2i}-1)\over\prod_{i=m-2j+1}^{m}(q^{i}-1)\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}(q^{2i}-1)}\\
&=\left({q^{2j+1}\over q^{2j+2}-1}\right)\left({q^{j+1}+\tau\over q^{j}+\tau}\right)(q^{m-2j-1}-1)(q^{m-2j}-1)>1.\end{split}

(5)

So the sequence of weight distribution of
HRM_{q}(2,m)
is unimodal except
A_{q^{m}-q^{m-1}}
which is the midpoint of the sequence.

Next, we show that
A^{q}_{\tau,j}
is log-concave for
2\leq j\leq\lfloor{m\over 2}\rfloor-1
. Since

A^{q}_{\tau,j+1}={q^{(j+1)^{2}}(q^{j+1}+\tau)\over q^{j^{2}}(q^{j}+\tau)}\cdot{(q^{m-2j-1}-1)(q^{m-2j}-1)\over(q^{2j+2)}-1)}A^{q}_{\tau,j}

and

A^{q}_{\tau,j-1}={q^{(j-1)^{2}}(q^{j-1}+\tau)\over q^{j^{2}}(q^{j}+\tau)}\cdot{(q^{2j+2)}-1)\over(q^{m-2j+1}-1)(q^{m-2j+2}-1)}A^{q}_{\tau,j},

we have that

\displaystyle A^{q}_{\tau,j+1}\cdot A^{q}_{\tau,j-1}

\displaystyle=q^{2}\cdot{(q^{j+1}+\tau)(q^{j-1}+\tau)\over(q^{j}+\tau)^{2}}\cdot{(q^{2j}-1)\over(q^{2j+2}-1)}\cdot{(q^{m-2j-1}-1)\over(q^{m-2j+1}-1)}\cdot{(q^{m-2j}-1)\over(q^{m-2j+2}-1)}\cdot(A^{q}_{\tau,j})^{2}

So,
A^{q}_{\tau,j}
is log-concave for
2\leq j\leq\lfloor{m\over 2}\rfloor-1
. For
j=1
,

\displaystyle A^{q}_{\tau,2}

\displaystyle={q^{4}(q^{2}+\tau)\over 2}\cdot{(q^{m-3}-1)(q^{m-2}-1)(q^{m-1}-1)(q^{m}-1)\over(q^{2}-1)(q^{4}-1)}

\displaystyle\leq 2q^{2}\cdot{q^{2}-1\over q^{4}-1}\cdot{(q^{m-3}-1)(q^{m-2}-1)\over(q^{m-1}-1)(q^{m}-1)}\cdot(A^{q}_{\tau,1})^{2}\leq(A^{q}_{\tau,1})^{2}.

Now we only need to consider
j=\lfloor{m\over 2}\rfloor
case. We need to show that

A^{q}_{\tau,\lfloor{m\over 2}\rfloor}\cdot{A^{q}_{\tau,\lfloor{m\over 2}\rfloor}\over A^{q}_{\tau,\lfloor{m\over 2}\rfloor-1}}\geq A_{q^{m}-q^{m-1}}.

We consider two cases of
m
.

First, we assume that
m
is odd. From Equation (
5
), we have that

{A^{q}_{\tau,{m-1\over 2}}/A^{q}_{\tau,{m-1\over 2}-1}}={(q^{m-1})\over(q^{m-1}-1)}\cdot{(q^{(m-1)/2}+\tau)\over(q^{(m-1)/2}+q\tau)}(q^{2}-1)(q^{3}-1).

Let
B_{\ell}=q^{\ell^{2}+\ell}{\prod_{i=m-2\ell}^{m}(q^{i}-1)\over\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}(q^{2i}-1)}
for
1\leq\ell\leq\lfloor{m-1\over 2}\rfloor
.
Then

A_{q^{m}-q^{m-1}}=q^{m}-1+\sum_{\ell=1}^{m-1\over 2}B_{\ell}

and

B_{\ell}={2q^{\ell}\over q^{\ell}+\tau}(q^{m-2\ell}-1)A^{q}_{\tau,\ell}.

Since

, we have that

\displaystyle\begin{split}B_{\ell+1}&={q^{2\ell+2}\over q^{2\ell+2}-1}(q^{m-2\ell-2}-1)(q^{m-2\ell-1}-1)B_{\ell}\\
&\geq(q^{m-2\ell-2}-1)(q^{m-2\ell-1}-1)B_{\ell}\\
&\geq(q-1)(q^{2}-1)B_{\ell}.\end{split}

(6)

Then,

\displaystyle A_{q^{m}-q^{m-1}}

\displaystyle=q^{m}-1+\sum_{\ell=1}^{m-1\over 2}B_{\ell}\leq q^{m}-1+\sum_{\ell=1}^{m-1\over 2}\left({1\over(q-1)(q^{2}-1)}\right)^{\ell-1}B_{m-1\over 2}

\displaystyle\leq q^{m}-1+{(q-1)(q^{2}-1)\over(q-1)(q^{2}-1)-1}B_{m-1\over 2}

\displaystyle=q^{m}-1+2{(q-1)(q^{2}-1)\over(q-1)(q^{2}-1)-1}\cdot{2(q-1)q^{(m-1)/2}\over q^{(m-1)/2}+\tau}A^{q}_{\tau,{m-1\over 2}}

\displaystyle\leq{(q^{m-1})\over(q^{m-1}-1)}{(q^{(m-1)/2}+\tau)\over(q^{(m-1)/2}+q\tau)}(q^{2}-1)(q^{3}-1)A^{q}_{\tau,{m-1\over 2}}

\displaystyle=\left({A^{q}_{\tau,{m-1\over 2}}/A^{q}_{\tau,{m-1\over 2}-1}}\right)A^{q}_{\tau,{m-1\over 2}}.

The last inequality comes from the fact that when

A^{q}_{\tau,{m-1\over 2}}={q^{{m-1\over 2}^{2}}(q^{m-1\over 2}+\tau)\over 2}{\prod_{i=2}^{m}(q^{i}-1)\over\prod_{i=1}^{m-1\over 2}(q^{2i}-1)}>q^{m}-1.

Now we assume that
m

5
MDS codes

It is known that the weight enumerator of an MDS code is unique (see, e.g.,
[
15
, 12, Ch.11, §3, Theorem 6]
), which is given by
A_{0}=1
,
A_{w}=0
for
0<w<d
, and

A_{w}=\binom{n}{w}(q-1)\sum_{j=0}^{w-d}(-1)^{j}\binom{w-1}{j}q^{w-d-j}.

(7)

According to
[
7
]
, many
[n,k,d]_{q}
MDS codes have
k
nonzero weights, with the exceptions as follows:

(i)

If
k=2
and
n=q+1
, then such MDS code is a Simplex code, which contains only words of weight zero or
q
.

(ii)

If
n=q+2
,
q=2^{m}
, and
k=3
, then
A_{2^{m}}=(2^{2m}-1)(2^{m-1}+1)
and
A_{n}=2^{m-1}(2^{m}-1)^{2}
.

As for the MDS codes with
2
nonzero weights, we have

(i)

If
k=2
and
n\leqslant q
, then
A_{n-1}=(q-1)n
,
A_{n}=(q-1)(q-n+1)
, and

\frac{A_{n-1}^{2}}{A_{0}A_{n}}=\frac{(q-1)n^{2}}{q-n+1}>\frac{(q-1)n^{2}}{q-1}>1.

In fact,

\frac{A_{d}^{2}}{A_{0}A_{d+1}}=\frac{(q-1)\binom{n}{k-1}^{2}}{\binom{n}{k-2}(q-n+k-1)}>1

for all MDS codes.

(ii)

If
n=q+2
,
q=2^{m}
, and
k=3
, then then it is a two-weight code, and

A_{2^{m}}=(2^{2m}-1)(2^{m-1}+1)=(2^{m}-1)(2^{m}+1)(2^{m-1}+1)>2^{m-1}(2^{m}-1)^{2}=A_{n}.

In this subsection, we will consider the log-concave MDS codes.
From (
7
), we have

\frac{A_{w}^{2}}{A_{w-1}A_{w+1}}=\frac{w+1}{w}\cdot\frac{n-w+1}{n-w}\cdot\frac{\left(\sum_{j=0}^{s}(-1)^{j}\binom{w-1}{j}q^{s-j}\right)^{2}}{\left(\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}(-1)^{j}\binom{w-2}{j}q^{s-1-j}\right)\left(\sum_{j=0}^{s+1}(-1)^{j}\binom{w}{j}q^{s+1-j}\right)}.

Let
s=w-d=w-n+k-1
,
k\geqslant 3
,
q\geqslant 2
be positive integers.
Then we define

f_{k}(s,w,q)=\sum_{j=0}^{s}(-1)^{j}\binom{w-1}{j}q^{-j},

and

G_{k}(s,n,q)=\frac{A_{w}^{2}}{A_{w-1}A_{w+1}}=\frac{w+1}{w}\cdot\frac{n-w+1}{n-w}\cdot\frac{f_{k}^{2}(s,w,q)}{f_{k}(s-1,w-1,q)f_{k}(s+1,w+1,q)}.

(8)

Lemma 6
.

About
f_{k}(s,w,q)
, we have

(i)

f_{k}(s,w,q)>0
if
0\leqslant s\leqslant k-1
,
w\leqslant q+1
, with the exception
f_{2}(1,q+1,q)=0
;

(ii)

f_{k}(s+1,w+1,q)=\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)f_{k}(s,w,q)+(-1)^{s+1}\binom{w-1}{s+1}q^{-s-1}
;

(iii)

If
s
is even, then
f_{k}(s+1,w+1,q)<\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)f_{k}(s,w,q)
;

(iv)

If
s
is odd, then
f_{k}(s+1,w+1,q)>\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)f_{k}(s,w,q)
;

Proof.

It is clear that
f_{k}(0,w,q)=1
, and
f_{k}(1,w,q)=1-(w-1)/q
.
Obviously,
f_{k}(1,w,q)>0
if
w<q+1
and
f_{k}(1,w,q)=0
if
w=q+1\geqslant n
.

Let
2\leqslant j\leqslant s
be even. Then

\frac{q\binom{w-1}{j}}{\binom{w-1}{j+1}}=q\frac{j+1}{w-j-1}>1

if and only if
(q+1)j>w-1-q
, which holds when
w\leqslant n\leqslant q+1
.
That is to say,

(-1)^{j}\binom{w-1}{j}q^{-j}+(-1)^{j+1}\binom{w-1}{j+1}q^{-j-1}>0.

Thus,
f_{k}(s,w,q)>0
when
s\geqslant 2
.
It is not difficult to check that

\displaystyle f_{k}(s+1,w+1,q)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{j=1}^{s+1}(-1)^{j}\left(\binom{w-1}{j}+\binom{w-1}{j-1}\right)q^{-j}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{j=1}^{s+1}(-1)^{j}\binom{w-1}{j}q^{-j}+\sum_{j=1}^{s+1}(-1)^{j}\binom{w-1}{j-1}q^{-j}

\displaystyle=f_{k}(s+1,w,q)+\sum_{i=0}^{s}(-1)^{i+1}\binom{w-1}{i}q^{-i-1}

\displaystyle=f_{k}(s+1,w,q)-\frac{1}{q}f_{k}(s,w,q)

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)f_{k}(s,w,q)+(-1)^{s+1}\binom{w-1}{s+1}q^{-s-1}.

Moreover, it is easy to obtain
(3)
and
(4)
from the above formula.
∎

Next, consider the following function:

g_{k}(s,w,q)=\frac{f_{k}^{2}(s,w,q)}{f_{k}(s-1,w-1,q)f_{k}(s+1,w+1,q)}.

Lemma 7
.

About
g_{k}(s,w,q)
, we have

(i)

If
s
is even, then
g_{k}(s,w,q)>1
;

(ii)

If
s
is odd, then
g_{k}(s,w+1,q)/g_{k}(s,w,q)<1
;

(iii)

If
s
is odd, then
g_{k}(s+2,w+2,q)/g_{k}(s,w,q)>1
.

Proof.

(i)

If
s
is even, then

\displaystyle\frac{f_{k}^{2}(s,w,q)}{f_{k}(s-1,w-1,q)f_{k}(s+1,w+1,q)}

\displaystyle>\frac{f_{k}(s,w,q)}{\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)f_{k}(s-1,w-1,q)}>1.

(ii)

If
s
is odd,
then

\displaystyle\frac{f_{k}(s,w+1,q)}{f_{k}(s,w,q)}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{f_{k}(s-1,w,q)}{qf_{k}(s,w,q)}<1-\frac{1}{q},

\displaystyle\frac{f_{k}(s-1,w,q)}{f_{k}(s-1,w-1,q)}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{f_{k}(s-2,w-1,q)}{qf_{k}(s-1,w-1,q)}>1-\frac{1}{q}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{g_{k}(s,w+1,q)}{g_{k}(s,w,q)}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{k}^{2}(s,w+1,q)}{f_{k}^{2}(s,w,q)}\cdot\frac{f_{k}(s-1,w-1,q)}{f_{k}(s-1,w,q)}\cdot\frac{f_{k}(s+1,w+1,q)}{f_{k}(s+1,w+2,q)}<1.

(iii)

It is easy to check that

\displaystyle f_{k}(s+2,w+2,q)

\displaystyle=\frac{(q-1)^{2}}{q^{2}}f_{k}(s,w,q)+(-1)^{s+1}\frac{q-1}{q}\binom{w-1}{s+1}q^{-s-1}+(-1)^{s+2}\binom{w}{s+2}q^{-s-2},

where
w=n-k+1+s
.
According to the definition of
g_{k}(s+2,w+2,q)
, we have
w\leqslant n-3
.
Since
n\leqslant q+2
, we have

(q-1)\binom{w-1}{s+1}/\binom{w}{s+2}=\frac{(q-1)(s+2)}{w}\geqslant\frac{(n-3)(s+2)}{n-3}>1.

Thus, when
s
is odd,

\displaystyle\frac{f_{k}(s+2,w+2,q)}{f_{k}(s,w,q)}>\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)^{2},\textnormal{and}\ \frac{f_{k}(s-1,w-1,q)}{f_{k}(s+1,w+1,q)}>\frac{1}{\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)^{2}}.

Hence, when
s
is odd,

\displaystyle\frac{g_{k}(s+2,w+2,q)}{g_{k}(s,w,q)}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{k}^{2}(s+2,w+2,q)}{f_{k}^{2}(s,w,q)}\cdot\frac{f_{k}(s-1,w-1,q)}{f_{k}(s+1,w+1,q)}\cdot\frac{f_{k}(s+1,w+1,q)}{f_{k}(s+3,w+3,q)}>1.

We conclude the proof.
∎

Theorem 8
.

If
g_{k}(s,k+2,q)>1
for all possible odd
s
, then there exists an
n_{0}(k,q)
such that for all
n<n_{0}(k,q)
, the
[n,k,d]_{q}
MDS code (if it exists) is log-concave property.

Proof.

Let
d<w<n
,
w-d=s
.
Then

\frac{A_{w}^{2}}{A_{w-1}A_{w+1}}=G_{k}(s,n,q)=\frac{n-k+2+s}{n-k+1+s}\cdot\frac{k-s}{k-1-s}\cdot g_{k}(s,w,q).

If
s
is even, then
g_{k}(s,w,q)>1
, and
A_{w}^{2}>A_{w-1}A_{w+1}
.
Note that

\displaystyle\frac{G_{k}(s,n+1,q)}{G_{k}(s,n,q)}

\displaystyle=\frac{n-k+3+s}{n-k+2+s}\cdot\frac{n-k+1+s}{n-k+2+s}\cdot\frac{g_{k}(s,w+1,q)}{g_{k}(s,w,q)}

\displaystyle=\frac{T^{2}-1}{T^{2}}\cdot\frac{g_{k}(s,w+1,q)}{g_{k}(s,w,q)}<1,

(9)

which implies that
G_{k}(s,n,q)
decreases when
n
becomes larger, where
T=n-k+2+s
.
Therefore, if
g_{k}(s,k+2,q)>1
for all possible odd
s
, then there exists an
n_{0}(k,q)
such that for all
n<n_{0}(k,q)
, the
[n,k,d]_{q}
MDS code (if it exists) satisfies the log-concave property.
∎

Theorem 9
.

If
C
is an
[n,k,d]_{q}
MDS code with
3\leqslant k\leqslant n/2+3
, then
C
is log-concave when
q\geqslant q_{0}(n,k)
and
C
is not log-concave when
q<q_{0}(n,k)
, where
q_{0}(n,k)
is the larger root of the quadratic polynomial

(m+k-1)q^{2}-(km^{2}-2k+2)q+\frac{km^{3}-km^{2}}{2}-km+m+k-1,

and
m=n-k+2
.
Moreover, if
C
is log-concave, then each
[n^{\prime},k,d^{\prime}]_{q}
MDS code is log-concave, where
n^{\prime}<n
and
k\leqslant n^{\prime}/2+3
may not hold.

Proof.

We only need to consider odd
s
.
According to Lemma
7
,
g_{k}(s+2,w+2,q)>g_{k}(s,w,q)
if
1\leqslant s\leqslant k-4
is odd.
It is clear that

\frac{A^{2}_{w+2}}{A_{w+1}A_{w+3}}/\frac{A^{2}_{w}}{A_{w-1}A_{w+1}}=\frac{(n-t+4)(n-t+1)}{(n-t+3)(n-t+2)}\cdot\frac{t^{2}-3t+2}{t^{2}-3t}\cdot\frac{g_{k}(s+2,w+2,q)}{g_{k}(s,w,q)}>1,

where
t=k-s\geqslant 1
, if

\displaystyle\frac{(n-t+4)(n-t+1)}{(n-t+3)(n-t+2)}\cdot\frac{t^{2}-3t+2}{t^{2}-3t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{2}{(n-t+2)(n-t+3)}\right)\left(1+\frac{2}{t^{2}-3t}\right)\geqslant 1,

which is equivalent to

(t-1)(t-2)=(t^{2}-3t)+2\leqslant(n-t+2)(n-t+3).

It is from the fact that

\left(1-\frac{2}{a}\right)\left(1+\frac{2}{b}\right)\geqslant 1\Longleftrightarrow b+2\leqslant a,\textnormal{where}\ a,b>0.

That is to show

\frac{(n-t+2)(n-t+3)}{(t-1)(t-2)}\geqslant 1,

which holds if
n-t+2\geqslant t-2
, i.e.,
s\geqslant k-2-n/2
.
If
k\leqslant n/2+3
, then

\frac{A^{2}_{d+1}}{A_{d}A_{d+2}}<\frac{A^{2}_{d+3}}{A_{d+2}A_{d+4}}<\frac{A^{2}_{d+5}}{A_{d+4}A_{d+6}}<\cdots.

For
s=1
, we have

\frac{A^{2}_{d+1}}{A_{d}A_{d+2}}=G_{k}(1,n,q)=\frac{n-k+3}{n-k+2}\cdot\frac{k-1}{k-2}\cdot\frac{(1-(n-k+1)/q)^{2}}{1-(n-k+2)/q+\binom{n-k+2}{2}/q^{2}}\geqslant 1

if and only if

\frac{m+1}{m}\cdot\frac{k-1}{k-2}\cdot\frac{(q-m+1)^{2}}{q^{2}-mq+\binom{m}{2}}\geqslant 1,\ \textnormal{where}\ m=n-k+2\geqslant k-4,

which is equivalent to show

(m+k-1)q^{2}-(km^{2}-2k+2)q+\frac{km^{3}-km^{2}}{2}-km+m+k-1\geqslant 0.

(10)

Note that
m>2
and the discriminant is

m(k-2)(m-2)(km^{2}-2k+2)>0,

then
q\geqslant q_{0}(n,k)
, the large root of the quadratic polynomial.
In fact, about the smaller root
q_{0}^{\prime}(n,k)
, we have

\displaystyle q_{0}^{\prime}(n,k)

\displaystyle=\frac{(km^{2}-2k+2)-\sqrt{m(k-2)(m-2)(km^{2}-2k+2)}}{2(m+k-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{km^{2}-2k+2}}{2(m+k-1)}\left(\sqrt{km^{2}-2k+2}-\sqrt{m(k-2)(m-2)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{km^{2}-2k+2}}{2(m+k-1)}\cdot\frac{2m^{2}+2mk-4m-2k+2}{\sqrt{km^{2}-2k+2}+\sqrt{m(k-2)(m-2)}}

\displaystyle<\frac{\sqrt{km^{2}-2k+2}}{2(m+k-1)}\cdot\frac{2m^{2}+2mk-4m-2k+2}{2\sqrt{m(k-2)(m-2)}}

\displaystyle<\frac{\sqrt{km^{2}-2k+2}}{2(m+k-1)}\cdot\frac{m(k+m-2)}{\sqrt{m(k-2)(m-2)}}<\frac{m(k+m-2)}{2(m+k-1)}\cdot\frac{m\sqrt{k}}{(m-2)\sqrt{k-2}}

\displaystyle<\frac{m}{2}\cdot\frac{m\sqrt{k}}{(m-2)\sqrt{k-2}}<m,\textnormal{when}\ m\geqslant 7\ \textnormal{and}\ k\geqslant 4.

If
m=3
, then
n-k=1
, the log-concavity for such code is clear.
If
k=3
, then

q_{0}^{\prime}(n,3)=\frac{(3m^{2}-4)-\sqrt{m(m-2)(3m^{2}-4)}}{2(m+2)}<\frac{(3m^{2}-4)-\sqrt{3}(m-2)(m-1)}{2(m+2)}<m

for all
m\geqslant 3
.
If
k\geqslant 4
and
4\leqslant m\leqslant 6
, then

q_{0}^{\prime}(n,k)<\frac{m^{2}\sqrt{k}}{2(m-2)\sqrt{k-2}}\leqslant\frac{m^{2}}{\sqrt{2}(m-2)}\leqslant\frac{9}{\sqrt{2}}<7.

There are few MDS codes over
\mathbb{F}_{3}
,
\mathbb{F}_{4}
,
\mathbb{F}_{5}
, and none of them satisfy
k\geqslant 4
and
m\geqslant 5
.
As for
m=4
, the only
[6,4,3]_{5}
MDS code is a Hamming code, and it is not log-concave.

That is to say, if
q<q^{\prime}_{0}(n,k)
, then such a code is either not log-concave, or does not exist by the MDS conjecture since
q_{0}^{\prime}(n,k)<m<n-1
for many
k
and
m
.
Meanwhile,
q_{0}(n,k)>km^{2}-2k+2-m
.

Besides, if
C^{\prime}
is an
[n^{\prime},k,d^{\prime}]_{q}
MDS code, then by Equation (
5
),
C^{\prime}
is log-concave, where
k\leqslant n^{\prime}/2+3
may not hold.
∎

Remark 2
.

It is clear that
q\geqslant q_{0}(n,k)
is necessary for every MDS code to be log-concave.
And the above theorem shows that
q\geqslant q_{0}(n,k)
is sufficient when
k\leqslant n/2+3
.
As for the log-concavity when
k>n/2+3
, one method is to check the log-concavity of the longer MDS code (with the same dimension), and another is to give a similar condition as
q\geqslant q_{0}(n,k)
.
For example, if
k=n/2+4
or
k=n/2+5
, the condition for
G_{k}(3,n,q)\geqslant 1
is necessary.

Usually, if
n/2+2s\leqslant k\leqslant n/2+2s+1
, then the condition for
G_{k}(s,n,q)\geqslant 1
is necessary.
Considering that
g_{k}(s+2,w+2,q)>g_{k}(s,w,q)
for odd
s
, these conditions can be simplified.
When we solve
G_{k}(s,n,q)\geqslant 1
, we just replace
g_{k}(s,w,q)
with
g_{k}(1,d+1,q)
, then we can obtain similar quadratic inequality as in Equation (
10
), and a similar condition
q\geqslant q_{s}(n,k)
, which is sufficient, but maybe not necessary.
In fact, this technique has been applied in the proof of Theorem
5
and works well.

6
Conclusion

For the first time, we have studied the nonzero weight distribution of a linear code from the standpoint of log-concavity. In particular, we have shown that all binary Hamming codes of length
2^{r}-1
(
r=3
or
r\geq 5
), the extended Hamming codes of length
2^{r}~(r\geq 3)
, and the second order Reed-Muller codes
R(2,m)~(m\geq 2),
as well as the homogeneous and projective second order Reed-Muller codes are log-concave. We have also shown that MDS codes of moderate lengths and dimensions are log-concave.

In future works, it will be interesting to investigate the log-concavity of classical families of linear codes such as few-weight codes, cyclic or quasi-cyclic codes, quadratic residue codes, double circulant codes, Griesmer codes, AG codes, and self-dual codes. Other integer sequences attached to codes, like the coset leader weight distribution, the weight distributions of cosets, or the generalized Hamming weight distribution are also worth considering.

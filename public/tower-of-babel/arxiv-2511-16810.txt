Magnetic Properties of the Quasi-1D Magnesium Lanthanide Borates Mg Ln B 5 ​ O 10 \text{Mg}\textit{Ln}\text{B}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}}
Lachlan G. M. Rooney, Siân E. Dutton, Nicola D. Kelly

Abstract
Lanthanide borates are widely studied for their optical and magnetic properties. A wide variety of structures are known with 3, 2, 1 and 0 dimensional connectivity of lanthanide ions. Here, we explore Mg Ln B 5 ​ O 10 \text{Mg}\textit{Ln}\text{B}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}} , with a quasi-1D arrangement of the Ln ions. Polycrystalline samples of Mg Ln B 5 ​ O 10 \text{Mg}\textit{Ln}\text{B}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}} ( Ln = La, Pr, Nd, Sm–Er) were synthesised with high purity via a sol-gel method. Powder X-ray diffraction data confirmed the reported monoclinic space group ( P ​ 2 1 / c P2_{1}/c ). The magnetic L ​ n 3 + Ln^{3+} ions in Mg Ln B 5 ​ O 10 \text{Mg}\textit{Ln}\text{B}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}} form relatively isolated zig-zag chains parallel to the b b axis. Magnetic susceptibility and isothermal magnetisation were measured: all samples except L ​ n = Ln= (Eu, Sm) fit the Curie-Weiss Law in isothermal magnetisation at high temperatures, in broad agreement with theoretical expectations. L ​ n = Ln= (Nd, Tb, Dy, Ho) exhibit signatures characteristic of Ising spin saturation, implying single ion anisotropy, while Gd exhibits characteristics of Heisenberg spins. Estimation of magnetic interactions suggests that Mg Ln B 5 ​ O 10 \text{Mg}\textit{Ln}\text{B}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}} are candidate materials for quasi-1D magnetism. The magnetocaloric entropy change was also calculated, with MgGdB 5 ​ O 10 \text{MgGdB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}} showing promise for application to solid-state refrigeration at liquid helium temperatures. Keywords: lanthanide borates, magnetic, quasi-1D magnetism, magnetocaloric effect



1
Introduction

One-dimensional (1D) magnetic systems – spin chains of magnetic ions – have been predicted to exhibit a range of exotic magnetic behaviour, for example spinon excitations.
[
3
]
While truly 1D systems cannot be realised in the solid state, quasi-1D behaviour may be observable given sufficient separation between neighbouring magnetic chains. In such systems, weak interactions across chains cause 3D ordering at low temperatures.

Low-dimensional magnetism has been investigated in a range of systems, including transition metal structures such as
ABX_{3}
(where
A
is a non-magnetic, singly charged cation,
B
a magnetic, doubly charged cation, and
X
a halide ion).
[
3
,
17
,
26
]
Unlike transition metal ions, in lanthanides the superexchange and dipolar (through space) interactions are similar in magnitude, so it is harder to realise quasi-1D magnetic structures.
[
29
]
However, lattice geometry can prevent the simultaneous satisfaction of all magnetic interactions in frustrated systems, inhibiting long-range order and promoting quasi-low-dimensional magnetism.

For example, the monoclinic lanthanide metaborates
\textit{Ln}\text{(}\text{BO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
contain chains of
Ln^{3+}
ions, resulting in frustration and quasi-1D magnetism for some
Ln
.
[
26
]
Quasi-1D ferromagnetic ordering has also been observed in
\text{Tb}\text{(}\text{HCO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
, where
\text{TbO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{9}}}
polyhedra form chains along the
c
axis. These chains then form a triangular lattice mediated by the formate ligand. Whilst the magnetisation direction alternates between chains, this alternation is frustrated, inhibiting 3D ordering.
[
15
,
35
]

Meanwhile, 2D ordering has been observed in the lanthanide orthoborates
\textit{Ln}\text{BO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
, where the lanthanide ions lie on a distorted triangular lattice.
[
27
]
Another family of 2D lanthanide borates,
\textit{A}\text{Ba}\textit{Ln}\text{(}\text{BO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(for
A
= Na, K, Rb), has also been widely explored as a model for the undistorted frustrated triangular lattice, although some compositions suffer from non-magnetic site disorder which may affect their magnetic properties.
[
11
,
10
,
36
,
12
,
13
]

The magnesium lanthanide borates
\text{Mg}\textit{Ln}\text{B}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}}
present another opportunity to study low-dimensional magnetism because the magnetic
Ln^{3+}
ions form relatively isolated chains within the crystal.
\text{Mg}\textit{Ln}\text{B}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}}
crystallises in the monoclinic
P2_{1}/c
space group for
Ln
= (La–Er),
[
43
]
, figure
1
. The
\text{B}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}}
form two-dimensional layers linked by the metal ions, with
Ln
coordination polyhedra forming extended zigzagging chains extended along the
b
axis.
[
9
,
37
]
For the La compound, the nearest-neighbour (within a chain) La–La spacing was reported as 3.994 Å, while that across chains was 6.101 and 6.430 Å, largely confining magnetic interactions within the chains.
[
9
,
37
]

The concentration quenching of luminescent emission in the
\textit{Ln}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}}\text{La}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{1\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417ptx\/}}}\text{MgB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}}
system (
Ln
= Eu, Tb;
0\leq x\leq 1
) was investigated by Fouassier
et al.
,
[
9
]
demonstrating the dominance of interactions within (rather than between)
Ln
chains. As described above, this is expected to produce interesting low-dimensional magnetic behaviour, but the only magnetic study to date was on
\text{MgNdB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}}
which was found to be paramagnetic down to 1.7 K.
[
5
]

The suppression of the 3D ordering temperature in low-dimensional systems motivates their application to solid-state magnetic refrigeration via the magnetocaloric effect (MCE), as an alternative to liquid helium. When the system is adiabatically demagnetised, the magnetic entropy increases as the moments dealign with the field direction, and the corresponding decrease in the lattice and electronic entropy lowers the material’s temperature. The long-range magnetic ordering temperature sets a lower cooling limit, so suppressed 3D ordering is needed to access liquid helium temperatures.
[
2
,
34
]

The current benchmark material for magnetic refrigeration below
\sim
10 K is gadolinium gallium garnet (
\text{Gd}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{Ga}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
, GGG), due to its high thermal conductivity and low lattice heat capacity and magnetic ordering temperature.
[
30
]
GGG only exhibits long-range ordering below
\sim
25 mK or in an applied field. Without an applied field, short-ranged ordering is observed, likely due to geometric frustration – the magnetic Gd ions lie on two interpenetrating sub-lattices of corner-sharing triangles.
[
31
]
The lanthanide formates
\textit{Ln}\text{(}\text{HCO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
are another candidate magnetocaloric material.
\text{Gd}\text{(}\text{HCO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
is promising at
\sim
2 K while
\text{Tb}\text{(}\text{HCO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
has a larger magnetic entropy change above 4 K. A Gd-rich
\text{Gd}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{1\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417ptx\/}}}\text{Tb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}}\text{(}\text{HCO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
structure reportedly mixes the advantages of both compounds.
[
35
]

In this investigation, the bulk magnetic properties of the
\text{Mg}\textit{Ln}\text{B}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}}
compounds were measured for different lanthanides (
Ln
= Pr, Nd, Sm–Er). A solution-based method was employed to achieve significantly higher sample purity than any ceramic (solid-state) synthesis approach. The
\text{Mg}\textit{Ln}\text{B}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}}
compounds were found to be candidate materials for quasi-1D magnetism. Determination of the magnetocaloric entropy change showed that
\text{MgGdB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}}
in particular shows promise for application to solid state magnetic refrigeration in the liquid helium temperature range (
\geq
2 K).

2
Methods

2.1
Solution-based synthesis

Our synthesis follows Zhang
et al.

[
43
]
who reported phase-pure synthesis of
\text{MgYB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}}

via
the Pechini method, “which effectively suppressed the formation of
\text{YBO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
impurities.” The solid reagents (
\textit{Ln}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
, plus
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{BO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
and
MgO
each in 2 % molar excess) were dissolved in dilute nitric acid and 1 molar equivalent of polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) was added to chelate the metal ions. (Reagent suppliers and purities are provided in the Supplemental Material.) The solution was stirred and heated at
\sim
90
∘
C until the liquid evaporated to form a gel, which was then ground, transferred to an alumina crucible and heated in a furnace to 750
∘
C in air for 48 hours.

2.2
Structural characterisation

Powder X-Ray Diffraction (PXRD) was carried out at the I11 Beamline at the Diamond Light Source
[
42
]
with 15 keV X-rays, wavelength
\approx 0.83
Å.
1
1

1

The wavelength was obtained by measuring a silicon standard. The refined values were 0.824813 Å for the data collected in April 2025 (
Ln
= Gd–Ho) and 0.826455 Å for the data collected in July 2025 (
Ln
= La, Pr, Nd, Sm, Eu, Er).
Powder samples were packed in 0.5 mm diameter borosilicate glass capillaries and sealed with epoxy glue. Rietveld refinements
[
33
]
were carried out using TOPAS software.
[
6
]

The PXRD pattern background was fitted to a Chebyshev polynomial with 18 terms. PXRD peak shape contributions from the beam were fitted to a Thompson-Cox-Hastings pseudo-Voigt peak shape.
[
41
]
Sample contributions to peak shapes were accounted for where necessary using Lorentzian and Gaussian crystallite size parameters, and Stephens monoclinic strain broadening.
[
40
]

2.3
Magnetic characterisation

Magnetic susceptibility and isothermal magnetisation were measured using a Quantum Design Magnetic Properties Measurement System (MPMS) with a Superconducting Quantum Interference Device (SQUID) magnetometer, in the temperature and field ranges
1.8\leq T(K)\leq 300
and
0\leq\mu_{0}H(T)\leq 7
respectively. The susceptibility was measured on warming at a field of 500 Oe in both the zero-field-cooled (ZFC) and field-cooled (FC) regimes. The isothermal magnetisation was measured between 0 and 7 T at temperatures of 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, and 100 K.

Magnetic susceptibility was calculated assuming that magnetisation varies linearly with field:
\chi=\partial M/\partial H\approx M/H
. Isothermal magnetisation data (figure
6

Ln
and all temperatures measured.

3
Results and Discussion

3.1
Sample Synthesis

Initially, a number of synthesis routes were attempted for the preparation of
\text{Mg}\textit{Ln}\text{B}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}}
using a variety of reagents.
Ln
-containing magnetic impurities (principally different forms of lanthanide borates) were present at
\geq
10 wt % in every solid-state attempt. (Further details are given in the Supplemental Material.) Knitel
et al.

[
20
]
report the same impurities in their solid-state synthesis attempts of
\text{Mg}\text{(}\text{La}\text{/}\text{Y}\text{)}\text{B}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}}
, finding that “neither variation of the excess
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{BO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
nor prolonging of the firing time enhanced the purity of [their] samples.” In this work, samples with
>95\%
phase purity were achieved using a solution-based method, starting from lanthanide and magnesium oxides and PVA
[
43
]
.

Phase-pure
\text{MgEuB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}}
was produced via the sol-gel method. For
Ln
= Gd–Er, the dominant impurity was
\text{LnBO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
(
C2/c
). For the samples with larger lanthanides, La, Pr and Sm, the impurity phase was
\textit{Ln}\text{BO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
(
P2_{1}/m
). The impurity in the Nd sample could not be determined, but a comparison of the relative primary and secondary phase peak intensities between different compounds indicates that the Nd impurity is likely present at less than 2 wt %. Finally, we note that neither solid-state nor solution-based synthesis succeeded in producing any
\text{MgYbB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}}
, instead producing various
\text{YbBO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
phases. This is in accordance with Saubat
et al.

[
37
]
and it is therefore likely that the
\text{Mg}\textit{Ln}\text{B}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}}
structure is unstable for
Ln
ionic radii smaller than that of Er.

3.2
Structural Characterisation

Samples were characterised using powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) refined against the reported crystal structure
[
43
]
using the Rietveld method. The background coefficients, lattice parameters, zero-point error, and the atomic coordinates of the lanthanide ion were refined, while the positions of the lighter Mg, B and O ions were fixed at values from literature reports. Fractional site occupancy values were fixed at 1 and isotropic thermal parameters fixed at 1 Å
2
for all atoms.

The
\text{Mg}\textit{Ln}\text{B}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}}
unit cell can be described by either the
P2_{1}/c
or
P2_{1}/n
settings of the same space group (number 14).
P2_{1}/c
is the standard setting but
P2_{1}/n
has a less oblique unit cell, with angle
\beta
closer to 90
∘

[
14
]
As explained in detail elsewhere,
[
8
]
using the less oblique cell reduces correlations between refined parameters, potentially improving fit accuracy. However, refinements with both cell settings were conducted. A lower
\chi^{2}
and
R_{wp}
were obtained with the
P2_{1}/c
cell (keeping other parameters such as the background the same), so the structures are therefore reported for the
P2_{1}/c
cell.

There was some difficulty achieving accurate fitted intensities of the
(100)
and/or
(10\bar{2})
reflections for some samples. The Stephens’ monoclinic model for anisotropic strain
[
40
]
was found to improve the fits for those samples.

To be considered as quasi-1D, the intra-chain exchange (
J_{\mathrm{intra}}
, equation
3
) and dipolar (

, equation
4
) interactions must exceed the inter-chain interactions. For through-bond exchange, the relevant pathways are
Ln
-
O
-
Ln
and
Ln
-
O
-B-

Ln

J_{\mathrm{intra}}\sim\frac{3k_{\mathrm{B}}\theta_{\mathrm{CW}}}{2nJ(J+1)}

(3)

D\sim\frac{\mu_{0}\mu_{eff}^{2}}{4\pi R^{3}J(J+1)}

(4)

(

the through-space
Ln^{3+}

the total angular momentum quantum number.)

J_{\mathrm{intra}}
and

are listed in table

\text{Mg}\text{(}\text{Pr}\text{{,}}\mkern 3.0mu\text{Nd}\text{{,}}\mkern 3.0mu\text{Tb}\text{{,}}\mkern 3.0mu\text{Dy}\text{{,}}\mkern 3.0mu\text{Ho}\text{{,}}\mkern 3.0mu\text{Er}\text{)}\text{B}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}}

.

4
Conclusions

High-purity samples of
\text{Mg}\textit{Ln}\text{B}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}}
were obtained using a solution-based process and their crystal structures analysed using high-resolution powder diffraction. Magnetic susceptibility measurements were carried out on nine compounds in the series.
\text{MgSmB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}}
and
\text{MgEuB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}}
exhibited significant van Vleck paramagnetism, while
\text{MgPrB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}}
showed evidence for a singlet ground state. The other samples (
Ln=
Nd, Gd, Tb, Dy, Ho, Er) have antiferromagnetic spin correlations, but no ordering above 2 K except for
\text{MgTbB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}}
, which showed signs of magnetic ordering at
\sim 2.25
K in both susceptibility and heat capacity data.

Isothermal magnetisation measurements revealed that
\text{Mg}\textit{Ln}\text{B}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}}
(
Ln=
Nd, Tb, Dy, Ho, Er) show Ising-like behaviour, while
\text{MgGdB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}}
has a Heisenberg spin system. The magnetocaloric entropy change was calculated for
Ln=
(Nd, Tb–Er), with
\text{MgGdB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}}
in particular showing promise for application to solid-state cryogenic refrigeration. Several samples underwent a field-induced transition around 2–4 K at fields above
\sim 2
T. Future heat capacity measurements under an applied magnetic field could shed light on this transition. Finally, comparison of the strength of the exchange and dipole interactions suggests that many of the magnesium lanthanide borates remain candidates for quasi-1D magnetism. The
\text{Mg}\textit{Ln}\text{B}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}}
system displays a range of interesting magnetic behaviour, and it is hoped that this investigation will motivate further study of these materials.

Appendix III
Fitting to the Brillouin Function

In the Weiss model, the argument of the Brillouin function is
y=g_{J}\mu_{B}J(B+\lambda M)/k_{B}T
, to first order in
\lambda
, with positive and negative
\lambda
indicating ferro- and antiferromagnetism respectively.
[
3
]
Since the argument of the Brillouin function contains the magnetisation, the system would have to be solved iteratively/self-consistently. (An example of this approach can be found in
[
21
]
.)

Alternative approaches to fitting data to the Brillouin function with spin interactions include modifying
y\to g_{J}\mu_{B}JB/k_{B}(T+T_{0})

[
1
]
and allowing either the spin
J
or the
g
factor to vary as a fitting parameter (
[
4
]
and
[
7
]
respectively).

In this investigation, fits were attempted with the
g
factor varying, in the hope that this would fix the curvature and provide accurate fitted values of the saturation magnetisation
M_{s}
. The presence of ferro/antiferromagnetic interactions could then be inferred from an increase/decrease in the fitted
g
factor from
g_{J}
. However, the fit qualities proved poor. It is possible that with three variable parameters (
M_{s}
,
g
,
\chi_{1}
), the fitting algorithm was converging on a local minimum in least-squares space, rather than the global minimum.

Free spins fits were also attempted (
g=g_{J}
). Ideally, given the true
M_{s}
, one could draw qualitative conclusions on ferro- or antiferromagnetic exchange interactions from the data deviating above/below the modified free spins Brillouin function (equation 5 in main text). However, with
M_{s}
unknown, the algorithm fits the data on both sides of the modified free spins Brillouin function to obtain a better fit. Hence, conclusions about any exchange interactions cannot be drawn, and the fitted
M_{s}
value is inaccurate.

The above is illustrated with
\text{MgNdB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}}
in figure
S2
. By contrast, the isothermal magnetisation data for
\text{MgErB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{10}}}
fit the free spins Brillouin function well - so it can be inferred that the other
Ln
in Table 3 (main text) do have some form of spin interaction.

For many
Ln
, the fitted
\chi_{1}
values were arbitrarily small but the magnetisation was still increasing, implying a still rising slope of the Brillouin function and hence incomplete spin saturation at 7 T. However, this might simply be an artefact of the poor fitting.

) and with variable

was generally poor. An improved fit was obtained with the free spins Brillouin function, but at the expense of inaccuracy in determining the saturation magnetisation

The data in Table 3 (main text) represent the spread of
M_{s}
and
\chi_{1}
values obtained from both fitting methods. The
M_{s}
estimates were sufficient to distinguish between Heisenberg and Ising spin systems. For all
Ln
except
Er

\chi_{1}
was comparable to
\chi_{1}
itself, so conclusions about the extent of van Vleck paramagnetism in those systems cannot be made.

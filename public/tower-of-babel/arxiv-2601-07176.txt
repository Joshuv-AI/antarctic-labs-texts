Local linearization for the nonlinear damped stochastic Klein-Gordon equation
Guanglin Rang, Ran Wang

Abstract




Abstract:
For the
1+1
dimensional nonlinear damped stochastic Klein-Gordon equation driven by space-time white noise, we prove that the second-order increments of the solution can be approximated, after scaling with the diffusion coefficient, by those of the corresponding linearized stochastic Klein-Gordon equation. This extends the result of Huang et al.
[
18
]
for the stochastic wave equation. A key difficulty arises from the more complex structure of the Green function, which we overcome by means of subtle analytical estimates. As applications, we analyze the quadratic variation of the solution and construct a consistent estimator for the diffusion parameter.

Keywords:
Stochastic Klein-Gordon equation; local linearization; quadratic variation; parameter estimation.

MSC:
60H15; 60G17; 60G22.

1.
Introduction

Consider the nonlinear damped stochastic Klein-Gordon equation:

\begin{cases}\vskip 6.0pt\displaystyle{\left(\mathop{}\mathbin{\Box}+a\partial_{t}+m^{2}\right)u(t,x)=F(u(t,x))\dot{W}(t,x),\quad t>0,x\in\mathbb{R},}\\
\displaystyle{u(0,x)=0,\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(0,x)=0.}\end{cases}

(1.1)

Here,
\mathop{}\mathbin{\Box}
is the d’Alembertian in one-dimensional space defined by

\displaystyle\mathop{}\mathbin{\Box}:=\partial_{tt}-\partial_{xx},

(1.2)

the constants
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
m\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
are known from the physics literature as the damping constant and mass, the diffusion coefficient
F:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a globally Lipschitz continuous function, and
\dot{W}
is a Gaussian space-time white noise defined on a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
. The case
a>0
corresponds to “damping”, whereas
a<0
corresponds to “excitation”.
When
a=0,m=0
, (
1.1
) reduces to the well-known stochastic wave equation, see, e.g.,
[
5
,
9
,
31
]
.

It is well known that stochastic partial differential equations with multiplicative noise has local linearization behavior; see, e.g.,
[
12
,
14
]
. To state this property precisely, we first introduce the following stochastic heat equation (SHE, for short):

\begin{cases}\vskip 6.0pt\displaystyle{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}X(t,x)=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}X(t,x)+F(X(t,x))\dot{W}(t,x),\quad t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R},}\\
\displaystyle{X(0,x)=0,}\end{cases}

(1.3)

where
\dot{W}
and
F
are the same as in (
1.3
). Let
Z
denote the linearized version of
X
, i.e., the solution to the SHE (
1.3
) with
F\equiv 1
. Then, the spatial increments of
X
satisfy

X(t,x+h)-X(t,x)=F(X(t,x))\left\{Z(t,x+h)-Z(t,x)\right\}+\mathcal{R}_{t,x}(h),

(1.4)

where the remainder term
\mathcal{R}_{t,x}(h)
converges to zero faster than
Z(t,x+h)-Z(t,x)
as
h\to 0
. Relation (
1.4
) shows that, for each fixed
t>0
, the local spatial fluctuations of
X(t,\cdot)
are essentially governed by those of
Z(t,\cdot)
. In other words, ignoring precise regularity conditions, (
1.4
) indicates that
X(t,\cdot)
is controlled by
Z(t,\cdot)
in the sense of Gubinelli’s theory of controlled paths
[
13
]
.

An analogous local linearization holds for temporal increments: for fixed
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
, as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, the increment
X(t+\varepsilon,x)-X(t,x)
admits a similar structure; see
[
24
,
14
,
33
,
26
]
and references therein. Those results provide a quantitative framework for analyzing the local structure of sample paths of the solution, including properties such as Khinchin’s law of the iterated logarithm, Chung’s law of the iterated logarithm, quadratic variation, and small-ball probability estimates; see, e.g.,
[
4
,
8
,
11
,
17
,
15
,
21
,
22
]
and references therein.

In sharp contrast to the parabolic setting, the stochastic wave equation does not admit local linearization from a one-parameter perspective. This key feature was established by Huang and Khoshnevisan, who derived the asymptotic behavior of the temporal and spatial quadratic variations individually through a sequence of approximations in
[
16
]
. Subsequently, Tudor and Zurcher provided an alternative proof using Malliavin calculus and further extended the discussion to applications in statistical inference in
[
30
]
.

Recently, from a bi-parameter perspective, Huang et al.
[
18
]
revisited the local linearization problem for the stochastic wave equation and showed that such linearization becomes possible when both temporal and spatial parameters are considered jointly.

In this work, we extend the bi-parameter local linearization result of
[
18
]
to the nonlinear damped stochastic Klein-Gordon equation (
1.1
). We show that the linearization framework is applicable to the equation (
1.1
) when damping or mass terms are included.

To carry out this analysis, let us first give the linear damped stochastic Klein-Gordon in one spatial dimension:

\begin{cases}\vskip 6.0pt\displaystyle{\left(\mathop{}\mathbin{\Box}+a\partial_{t}+m^{2}\right)U(t,x)=\dot{W}(t,x),\quad t>0,x\in\mathbb{R},}\\
\displaystyle{U(0,x)=0,\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial t}U(0,x)=0.}\end{cases}

(1.5)

Consider a coordinate system
(\tau,\lambda)
obtained by rotating the
(t,x)
-coordinates by
-45^{\circ}
. That is,

(\tau,\lambda):=\Big(\frac{t-x}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{t+x}{\sqrt{2}}\Big)\quad\text{and}\quad(t,x)=\Big(\frac{\tau+\lambda}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-\tau+\lambda}{\sqrt{2}}\Big).

(1.6)

The axes
\tau
and
\lambda
correspond to the characteristic line directions of the wave equation, see
[
27
,
31
]
.
For
\tau>0
and
\lambda\geq-\tau
, define

v(\tau,\lambda):=u\left(\frac{\tau+\lambda}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-\tau+\lambda}{\sqrt{2}}\right),

(1.7)

and

V(\tau,\lambda):=U\left(\frac{\tau+\lambda}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-\tau+\lambda}{\sqrt{2}}\right).

(1.8)

For any
\varepsilon>0
, define the difference operators
\delta_{\varepsilon}^{(j)},j=1,2
by

\begin{split}\delta_{\varepsilon}^{(1)}f(\tau,\lambda):=&\,f(\tau+\varepsilon,\lambda)-f(\tau,\lambda),\\
\delta_{\varepsilon}^{(2)}f(\tau,\lambda):=&\,f(\tau,\lambda+\varepsilon)-f(\tau,\lambda).\end{split}

(1.9)

Define the remainder terms
R_{\varepsilon}^{+}(\tau,\lambda)
and
R_{\varepsilon}^{-}(\tau,\lambda)
as follows:

\begin{split}R_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}(\tau,\lambda):=&\,\delta_{\pm\varepsilon}^{(1)}\delta_{\varepsilon}^{(2)}v(\tau,\lambda)-F(v(\tau,\lambda))\delta_{\pm\varepsilon}^{(1)}\delta_{\varepsilon}^{(2)}V(\tau,\lambda)\\
=&\,\left\{v(\tau\pm\varepsilon,\lambda+\varepsilon)-v(\tau\pm\varepsilon,\lambda)-v(\tau,\lambda+\varepsilon)+v(\tau,\lambda)\right\}\\
&-F(v(\tau,\lambda))\left\{V(\tau\pm\varepsilon,\lambda+\varepsilon)-V(\tau\pm\varepsilon,\lambda)-V(\tau,\lambda+\varepsilon)+V(\tau,\lambda)\right\}.\end{split}

(1.10)

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
p\geq 1,M>0
and
L>0
, there exists a constant
c(p,M,L)>0
such that

\displaystyle\left\|R_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}(\tau,\lambda)\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq c(p,M,L)\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}},

(1.11)

holds uniformly for all
\tau\in[0,M]
,
\lambda\in[-\tau,L]
, and all sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
.

For a function
f(t,x)
, define the difference operators
\Delta_{\varepsilon}^{(1)}
and
\Delta_{\varepsilon}^{(2)}
as follows:

\begin{split}\Delta_{\varepsilon}^{(1)}f(t,x)=&\,f(t,x+2\varepsilon)-f(t-\varepsilon,x+\varepsilon)-f(t+\varepsilon,x+\varepsilon)+f(t,x),\\
\Delta_{\varepsilon}^{(2)}f(t,x)=&\,f(t+2\varepsilon,x)-f(t+\varepsilon,x-\varepsilon)-f(t+\varepsilon,x+\varepsilon)+f(t,x).\end{split}

(1.12)

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.1
, we obtain the following bi-parameter local linearization result for the solution of the equation (
1.1
) in the original coordinates.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
u
and
U
be the solutions to equations (
1.1
) and (
1.5
), respectively. Then, for any
p\geq 1
,
M>0
, and
L>0
, the following estimate holds uniformly for
t\in[0,M]
,
x\in[-L,L]
, all sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, and
k=1,2
:

\begin{split}\left\|\Delta_{\varepsilon}^{(k)}u(t,x)-F(u(t,x))\Delta_{\varepsilon}^{(k)}U(t,x)\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq c(p,M,L)\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}.\end{split}

(1.13)

For each integer
N\geq 1
, define the grid points

\displaystyle\tau_{i}:=\frac{i}{N},\ \ \ \text{and }\lambda_{j}:=\frac{j}{N}\ \ \ \text{for }i,j=0,\cdots,N.

(1.14)

The quadratic variation of the process
v
given in (
1.7
) is defined as

\displaystyle Q_{N}(v):=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\Delta_{i,j}(v)^{2},

(1.15)

with

\displaystyle\Delta_{i,j}(v):=v\left(\tau_{i+1},\lambda_{j+1}\right)-v\left(\tau_{i+1},\lambda_{j}\right)-v\left(\tau_{i},\lambda_{j+1}\right)+v\left(\tau_{i},\lambda_{j}\right).

(1.16)

We now investigate the asymptotic behavior of the quadratic variation sequence
\{Q_{N}(v)\}_{N\geq 1}
as
N\rightarrow\infty
.
The core of our approach is to approximate each increment
\Delta_{i,j}(v)
by the linearized term
F(v(\tau_{i},\lambda_{j}))\Delta_{i,j}(V)
, where
V
denotes the Gaussian field defined in (
1.8
).
The limit characterized in the following proposition provides a direct pathway to estimating the diffusion parameter.

Proposition 1.3
.

There exists a constant
c>0
such that, for every integer
N\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|Q_{N}(v)-\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}F^{2}\big(v(\tau,\lambda)\big)d\tau d\lambda\right|\right]\leq cN^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(1.17)

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we compile preliminary results on stochastic integration and solution properties for the equation (
1.1
), adapting from
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
. In Section
3
, we first employ an argument from Chen and Lee
[
3
]
to reduce the problem’s generality, confining the proofs to the critically damped case. Then, based on this reduction, we adapt techniques from
[
18
]
to prove Theorem
1.1
within the simplified setting.
Finally, in Section
4
, we develop a quadratic variation analysis for the nonlinear stochastic wave equation and construct the corresponding statistical estimators.

2.
Properties of the solution

2.1.
Green function

Taking the Fourier transform in the spatial variable,
\mathscr{F}{G}(t)(\xi):=\hat{G}(t,\xi)
, the Fourier transform of
G(t,\cdot)
, satisfies the ordinary differential equation

\partial_{t}^{2}\hat{G}+a\partial_{t}\hat{G}+\bigl(\xi^{2}+m^{2}\bigr)\hat{G}=0,

(2.1)

subject to the initial conditions

\hat{G}(0,\xi)=0,\qquad\partial_{t}\hat{G}(0,\xi)=1,

which follow from integrating the original equation in time around
t=0
and using the fact that
G(t,\cdot)=0
for
t<0
.

Solving (
2.1
) yields

\mathscr{F}{G}(t)(\xi)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle e^{-\frac{at}{2}}\frac{\sin\Bigl(t\sqrt{\xi^{2}+m^{2}-a^{2}/4}\,\Bigr)}{\sqrt{\xi^{2}+m^{2}-a^{2}/4}},&\text{if }\xi^{2}>\frac{a^{2}}{4}-m^{2};\\[8.0pt]
\displaystyle e^{-\frac{at}{2}}\,t,&\text{if }\xi^{2}=\frac{a^{2}}{4}-m^{2};\\[8.0pt]
\displaystyle e^{-\frac{at}{2}}\frac{\sinh\Bigl(t\sqrt{\frac{a^{2}}{4}-m^{2}-\xi^{2}}\,\Bigr)}{\sqrt{\frac{a^{2}}{4}-m^{2}-\xi^{2}}},&\text{if }\xi^{2}<\frac{a^{2}}{4}-m^{2}.\end{cases}

(2.2)

Note that the first expression in (
2.2
) formally contains the other two, since

\lim_{u\rightarrow 0}\frac{\sin(u)}{u}=1\ \ \ \ \text{and }\sin(iu)=i\sinh(u).

Consequently, we obtain the following unified formula via analytic continuation:

\mathscr{F}{G}(t)(\xi)=e^{-\frac{at}{2}}\frac{\sin\Bigl(t\sqrt{\xi^{2}+m^{2}-a^{2}/4}\,\Bigr)}{\sqrt{\xi^{2}+m^{2}-a^{2}/4}},

(2.3)

where the square root and the sine function are understood in the sense of analytic continuation on the complex plane. For the detailed derivation, we refer to
[
5
, Example 7]
and
[
6
, pp. 50–51]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

There exists a constant
c>0
such that, for any
T>0
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathscr{F}G(t)(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq c\left(1+T^{2}\right).

(2.4)

Proof.

This estimate is essentially contained in the proof of Lemma 2.2 in
[
3
]
. For completeness, we provide the details.

Using the unified representation (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathscr{F}G(t)(\xi)|^{2}d\xi=

\displaystyle\,\int_{\xi^{2}<\frac{a^{2}}{4}-m^{2}}e^{-at}\frac{\sinh^{2}\left(t\sqrt{\xi^{2}+m^{2}-a^{2}/4}\right)}{\xi^{2}+m^{2}-a^{2}/4}d\xi

\displaystyle\,\,+\int_{\xi^{2}>\frac{a^{2}}{4}-m^{2}}e^{-at}\frac{\sin\left(t\sqrt{\xi^{2}+m^{2}-a^{2}/4}\right)}{\xi^{2}+m^{2}-a^{2}/4}d\xi

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\,I_{1}+I_{2}.

Set
K:=\sqrt{\left(\frac{a^{2}}{4}-m^{2}\right)\vee 0}
.
Since
\frac{\sinh(x)}{x}
is uniformly bounded on any compact interval of
\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle I_{1}=\,\int_{\xi^{2}\leq\frac{a^{2}}{4}-m^{2}}e^{-at}t^{2}\frac{\sinh^{2}(t\sqrt{\xi^{2}+m^{2}-a^{2}/4}}{t^{2}(\xi^{2}+m^{2}-a^{2}/4)}d\xi\lesssim\,t^{2}.

Here and throughout, we write
a\lesssim b
if there exists a positive constant
C
, independent of the relevant parameters, such that
a\leq Cb
.

The second term
I_{2}
is further decomposed into
I_{2,1}+I_{2,2}
, where

\displaystyle I_{2,1}:=

\displaystyle\,\int_{K<|\xi|<2K}e^{-at}\frac{\sin\left(t\sqrt{\xi^{2}+m^{2}-a^{2}/4}\right)}{\xi^{2}+m^{2}-a^{2}/4}d\xi,

\displaystyle I_{2,2}:=

\displaystyle\,\int_{|\xi|\geq 2K}e^{-at}\frac{\sin\left(t\sqrt{\xi^{2}+m^{2}-a^{2}/4}\right)}{\xi^{2}+m^{2}-a^{2}/4}d\xi.

Using
\left|\frac{\sin(x)}{x}\right|\leq 1
and the same trick used for
I_{1}
, we have

I_{2,1}\lesssim t^{2}.

For
I_{2,2}
, since
|\sin(x)|\leq 1
, we have

I_{2,2}\lesssim\,\int_{|\xi|\geq 2K}\frac{1}{\xi^{2}-K^{2}}d\xi\lesssim\,\int_{|\xi|\geq 2K}\frac{1}{\xi^{2}}d\xi=\frac{1}{K}.

The proof is complete.
∎

2.2.
The existence and uniqueness of the solution

In this part, we establish the well-posedness of the solution to the equation (
1.1
).

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose that
F
is Lipschitz continuous. Then the equation (
1.1
) admits a unique mild solution
u
given by

\begin{split}u(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(t-s,x-y)F(u(s,y))W(ds,dy).\end{split}

(2.5)

Moreover, the solution satisfies

(a)

For every
T>0
and
1\leq p<\infty
,

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}\left[|u(t,x)|^{p}\right]<\infty.

(2.6)

(b)

For every
T>0
and
1\leq p<\infty
, there exists a constant
c(p,T)>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|u(t,x)-u(s,y)\right|^{p}\right]\leq c(p,T)\left[|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2}}+|x-y|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]^{p},

(2.7)

for all
t,s\in[0,T]
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

The existence and uniqueness of the solution follow from a standard Picard iteration. Once the moment bound (
2.6
) is established, the Hölder continuity (
2.7
) can be derived via a classical argument based on
[
3
, Lemmas 2.2, 2.3, 2.4]
(see, e.g.,
[
5
, Section 5]
,
[
7
, Theorem 9]
,
[
6
, pp. 59–61]
,
[
10
, Lemma 4.2.4]
,
[
23
]
). For brevity, we outline only the main steps for the existence.

Set
u_{0}(t,x):=0
. For
n\geq 0
, assume inductively that an adapted, mean-square continuous process

\{u_{n}(s,\cdot)\}_{0\leq s\leq T}
taking values in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
has been defined. We then define

u_{n+1}(t,x):=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(t-s,x-y)F(u_{n}(s,y))W(ds,dy).

(2.8)

Because
F
is Lipschitz continuous,
\{F(u_{n}(t,\cdot))\}_{t\in[0,T]}
remains adapted and mean-square continuous. Therefore, by
[
7
, Lemma 6 and Theorem 6]
together with Lemma
2.1
, the stochastic integral in (
2.8
) is well-defined.

We first verify a uniform moment bound: for every
p\geq 2
,

\sup_{n\geq 0}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|u_{n}(t,x)\right|^{p}\right]<+\infty.

(2.9)

Applying the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy (BDG, for short) inequality, Hölder’s inequality, and the Lipschitz property of
F
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|u_{n+1}(t,x)\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,c\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(t-s,x-y)^{2}F(u_{n}(s,y))^{2}dsdy\right|^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,c\left(\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}}dyG(t-s,x-y)^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\times\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}}dyG^{2}(t-s,x-y)\mathbb{E}\left[1+\left|u_{n}(s,y)\right|^{p}\right].

Using Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|u_{n+1}(t,x)\right|^{p}\right]\leq

\displaystyle\,c(T)\int_{0}^{t}\left(1+\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|u_{n}(s,y)\right|^{p}\right]\right)ds.

Define

M_{n}(t):=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|u_{n}(t,y)\right|^{p}\right].

The above inequality becomes

\displaystyle M_{n+1}(t)\leq c(T)\int_{0}^{t}\left(1+M_{n}(s)\right)ds.

(2.10)

An application of Gronwall’s lemma (see, e.g.,
[
6
, p. 26]
) then yields

\sup_{n\geq 1}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}M_{n}(t)<+\infty,

which proves (
2.9
).

We now turn to the
L^{p}(\Omega)
-convergence of the Picard scheme. An argument similar to that leading to (
2.10
) gives

\begin{split}&\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|u_{n+1}(t,x)-u_{n}(t,x)\right|^{p}\right]\\
\leq&\,c(T)\int_{0}^{t}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|u_{n}(s,y)-u_{n-1}(s,y)\right|^{p}\right]ds.\end{split}

(2.11)

By Gronwall’s lemma, the sequence
\{u_{n}(t,x)\}_{n\geq 1}
converges in
L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
uniformly in
x\in\mathbb{R}
to a limit
u(t,x)
. Moreover, as in the proof of
[
7
, Theorem 9]
, the limiting process
\{u(t,\cdot)\}_{0\leq t\leq T}
is adapted and mean-square continuous.

The proof is complete.
∎

3.1.
Reduction to the critical damping case and the proof of Theorem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\left(\mathop{}\mathbin{\Box}+a\partial_{t}+\frac{a^{2}}{4}\right)u(t,x)=F(u(t,x))\dot{W}(t,x)+b(u(t,x)),\quad t>0,\,x\in\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=\partial_{t}u(0,x)=0,\end{cases}

(3.1)

where
b(x):=\left(\frac{a^{2}}{4}-m^{2}\right)x

b
is globally Lipschitz, the standard existence and uniqueness theory ensures that the solution to (

\begin{split}u(t,x)=&\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma(t-s,x-y)b(u(s,y))dsdy\\

(3.2)

Here,

\mathscr{F}\Gamma(t)(\xi)=\frac{e^{-\frac{at}{2}}\sin(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|},

\Gamma(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{at}{2}}{\bf 1}_{\{|x|<t\}}.

(3.3)

Define the linear and critical components of the solution as

\displaystyle\,\left(\frac{a^{2}}{4}-m^{2}\right)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma(t-s,x-y)u(s,y)dsdy,

(3.4)

\displaystyle u_{C}(t,x):=

\displaystyle\,\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma(t-s,x-y)F(u(s,y))W(ds,dy).

(3.5)

Observe that

\begin{split}u_{C}(t,x)=\,\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\Delta(t,x)}e^{-\frac{a(t-s)}{2}}F(u(s,y))W(ds,dy),\end{split}

(3.6)

where

\displaystyle\Delta(t,x):=\left\{(s,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}:0\leq s\leq t,\,|x-y|\leq t-s\right\}.

(3.7)

3.2.
Increment Estimates for the drift term

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
p\geq 1
and sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
,

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigg|\Gamma(t-s,x-y)-\Gamma\left(t-\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}-s,x+\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}-y\right)\\
&\,\,\,\,-\Gamma\left(t-\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}-s,x-\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}-y\right)+\Gamma\left(t-\sqrt{2}\varepsilon-s,x-y\right)\Bigg|^{p}dsdy\\
=&\,\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2^{p}}+O\left(\varepsilon^{3}+\varepsilon^{1+p}\right).\end{split}

(3.9)

Proof.

The argument follows the method of
[
3

, which treats the case

Recall the domain
\Delta(t,x)
defined in (

). Define the subsets

\begin{split}&D_{1}:=\,\left(\Delta(t,x)\setminus\left\{\left|x-\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}-y\right|<t-\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}-s\right\}\right)\cap\left\{\left|x+\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}-y\right|<t-\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}-s\right\},\\
&D_{2}:=\,\left(\Delta(t,x)\setminus\left\{\left|x+\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}-y\right|<t-\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}-s\right\}\right)\cap\left\{\left|x-\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}-y\right|<t-\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}-s\right\},\\

&D_{4}:=\,\Delta(t,x)\setminus(D_{1}\cup D_{2}\cup D_{3}).\end{split}

(3.10)

An illustration is given in Figure

Denote the shifted kernels by

\begin{split}\Gamma_{1}(t,x):=&\,\Gamma\left(t-\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}},x+\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}\right),\\
\Gamma_{2}(t,x):=&\,\Gamma\left(t-\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}},x-\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}\right),\\
\Gamma_{3}(t,x):=&\,\Gamma\left(t-\sqrt{2}\varepsilon,x\right).\end{split}

(3.11)

The following observations about these sets will be used:

•

\Delta(t,x)
is a disjoint union of
D_{i},i=1,\cdots,4
. It is the support of
\Gamma
.

•

For
i=1,2
,
D_{i},
has area smaller than
\varepsilon T
and only
\Gamma
and
\Gamma_{i}
are nonzero on it.

•

D_{3}
has area at most

\Gamma,\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}

\Gamma_{3}

•

D_{4}
has area
\varepsilon^{2}

\Gamma
is nonzero on it.

Accordingly, we decompose the integral as

I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4},

where

\displaystyle I_{1}:=

\displaystyle\,\iint_{D_{1}}(\Gamma-\Gamma_{1})^{p}(t-s,x-y)dsdy,

\displaystyle I_{2}:=

\displaystyle\,\iint_{D_{2}}(\Gamma-\Gamma_{2})^{p}(t-s,x-y)dsdy,

\displaystyle I_{3}:=

\displaystyle\,\iint_{D_{3}}(\Gamma-\Gamma_{1}-\Gamma_{2}+\Gamma_{3})^{p}(t-s,x-y)dsdy,

\displaystyle I_{4}:=

\displaystyle\,\iint_{D_{4}}\Gamma^{p}(t-s,x-y)dsdy.

For
I_{1}
, we have by Taylor’s formula,

\displaystyle I_{1}\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\varepsilon T\max_{s\in\left[0,t-\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}\right]}\left|e^{-\frac{a}{2}(t-s)}-e^{-\frac{a}{2}\left(t-\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}-s\right)}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\varepsilon^{1+p}+o\left(\varepsilon^{1+p}\right).

The same bound holds for
I_{2}
.

For
I_{3}
, again using Taylor’s formula,

\displaystyle I_{3}\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\max_{s\in[0,t-\sqrt{2}\varepsilon]}\left|e^{-\frac{a}{2}(t-s)}-e^{-\frac{a}{2}\left(t-\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}-s\right)}-e^{-\frac{a}{2}\left(t-\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}-s\right)}+e^{-\frac{a}{2}\left(t-\sqrt{2}\varepsilon-s\right)}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\left|1+e^{\frac{a\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}}-2e^{\frac{a\varepsilon}{2\sqrt{2}}}\right|^{p}\lesssim\,\varepsilon^{2p}.

For

\Gamma

\Gamma(t-s,x-y)=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{a(t-s)}{4}+o\left(|t-s|\right).

\displaystyle I_{4}=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2^{p}}+O\left(\varepsilon^{3}\right).

Summing the four estimates gives (
3.9
). The proof is complete.

Quadratic variations and parameter estimation

Parameter estimation for the stochastic wave equation driven by additive noise has been extensively studied (see, e.g.,
[
1
,
19
,
20
,
28
]
; we also refer to the monograph
[
29
]
for a comprehensive overview). In contrast, the case of multiplicative noise remains less explored despite its greater practical relevance and theoretical challenges.

In this section, we analyze the quadratic variation of the solution and construct a consistent estimator for the diffusion parameter.

4.1.
Quadratic variations

Recall the function
V
defined in (
1.8
) and the grid points
(t_{i},\lambda_{j})
,
0\leq i,j\leq N
, defined in (
1.14
).
Define the second-order increment

\Delta_{i,j}(V):=V\left(\tau_{i+1},\lambda_{j+1}\right)-V\left(\tau_{i+1},\lambda_{j}\right)-V\left(\tau_{i},\lambda_{j+1}\right)+V\left(\tau_{i},\lambda_{j}\right).

The second-order quadratic variation of
V
is

\displaystyle Q_{N}(V):=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\Delta_{i,j}(V)^{2}.

(4.1)

We first study the asymptotic behavior of the sequence
\{Q_{N}(V)\}_{N\geq 1}
as
N\rightarrow\infty
.

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists a constant
c>0
such that for any
N\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|Q_{N}(V)-\frac{1}{4}\right|^{2}\right]\leq cN^{-1}.

(4.2)

Proof.

By Corollary
3.2
, the random variable
\Delta_{i,j}(V)
follows a Gaussian distribution with mean zero and variance satisfying

\mathrm{Var}\left(\Delta_{i,j}(V)\right)=\frac{1}{4N^{2}}+O\left(N^{-3}\right).

Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta_{i,j}(V)^{2}\right]-\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\lesssim\,N^{-3}.

(4.3)

Using standard moment bounds for Gaussian random variables, we obtain that for all
i,j\geq 1
and
p\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\Delta_{i,j}(V)^{2}-\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\right)^{2p}\right]\lesssim N^{-4p}.

(4.4)

Due to the increment-independence of the space-time white noise
\{W(t,x)\}_{t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}}
, the increments

\Delta_{i,j}(V)
and
\Delta_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}(V)
are independent whenever
(i,j)\neq(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})
. Using (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\left(\Delta_{i,j}(V)^{2}-\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\right)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\Delta_{i,j}(V)^{2}-\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\,\,+2\sum_{0\leq i<i^{\prime}\leq N-1}\sum_{0\leq j<j^{\prime}<N-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta_{i,j}(V)^{2}-\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}(V)^{2}-\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,N^{-1}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Next, we give the proof of Proposition
1.3
.

Proof of Proposition
1.3
.

Recall the definitions of
v_{L}
and
v_{C}
in (
3.8
).
First, applying Proposition
3.1
with

and

\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta_{i,j}(v_{L})^{2}\right]\leq cN^{-4}.

Summing over all
i,j=0,\cdots,N-1
gives

\mathbb{E}[Q_{N}(v_{L})]\leq cN^{-2},

which is negligible compared to the leading-order terms.

Following the same approach as in
[
25
]
and using the increment approximation

\Delta_{i,j}(v)\approx F(v(\tau_{i},\lambda_{j}))\Delta_{i,j}(V),

established in Theorem
1.1
, we obtain the estimate

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|Q_{N}(v_{C})-\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}F^{2}\big(v(\tau,\lambda)\big)d\tau d\lambda\right|\right]\lesssim\,N^{-1/2}.

(4.5)

For the reader’s convenience, we now provide a detailed derivation of (

).

We decompose the difference as follows:

\begin{split}&Q_{N}(v_{C})-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}F^{2}\big(v(\tau,\lambda)\big)d\tau d\lambda\\
=&\,\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\left\{\Delta_{i,j}(v_{C})^{2}-F^{2}(v(\tau_{i},\lambda_{j}))\Delta_{i,j}(V)^{2}\right\}\\
&+\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}F^{2}(v(\tau_{i},\lambda_{j}))\left\{\Delta_{i,j}(V)^{2}-\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\right\}\\
&+\frac{1}{4}\left[\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}F^{2}(v(\tau_{i},\lambda_{j}))N^{-2}-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}F^{2}\big(v(\tau,\lambda)\big)d\tau d\lambda\right]\\

(4.6)

Step 1: Estimation of
I_{1}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\Delta_{i,j}(v_{C})^{2}-F^{2}(v(\tau_{i},\lambda_{j}))\Delta_{i,j}(V)^{2}\right|\right]

\displaystyle\,\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\Delta_{i,j}(v)-F(v(\tau_{i},\lambda_{j}))\Delta_{i,j}(V)\right|^{2}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\Delta_{i,j}(v)+F(v(\tau_{i},\lambda_{j}))\Delta_{i,j}(V)\right|^{2}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

From Theorem

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\Delta_{i,j}(v_{C})-F(v(\tau_{i},\lambda_{j}))\Delta_{i,j}(V)\right|^{2}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\,N^{-\frac{3}{2}}.

2.6

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\Delta_{i,j}(v_{C})+F(v(\tau_{i},\lambda_{j}))\Delta_{i,j}(V)\right|^{2}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta_{i,j}(v_{C})^{2}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\mathbb{E}\left[F(v(\tau_{i},\lambda_{j}))^{4}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\cdot\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta_{i,j}(V)^{4}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,N^{-1}.

Combining the two bounds and summing over
i,j=0,\cdots,N-1
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[I_{1}]\lesssim N^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Step 2: Estimation of
I_{2}
.

Let
\{\mathcal{F}_{t},t\geq 0\}
be the filtration generated by the space-time white noise
W

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{t}=\sigma\left\{W({\bf 1}_{[0,s]}\varphi),s\in[0,t],\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}\right\}\vee\mathcal{N},

(4.7)

where
\mathcal{N}
denotes the class of
\mathbb{P}
-null sets in
\mathcal{F}

Consider pairs
(i,j),(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
with
i+j\geq i^{\prime}+j^{\prime}+2

1.6
) and the temporal independence of
\{W(t,x)\}_{t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}}
, the random variables
v_{C}(\tau_{i^{\prime}},\lambda_{j^{\prime}}),v_{C}(\tau_{i},\lambda_{j})
, and
\Delta_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}(V)
are
\mathcal{F}_{\sqrt{2}(\tau_{i}+\lambda_{j})}
-measurable, while
\Delta_{i,j}(V)
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{\sqrt{2}(\tau_{i}+\lambda_{j})}

Taking the conditional expectation with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{\sqrt{2}(\tau_{i}+\lambda_{j})}
and using (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta_{i,j}(V)^{2}-\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{\sqrt{2}(\tau_{i}+\lambda_{j})}\right]\lesssim\,N^{-3}.

(4.8)

Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality together with the moment estimates (
2.6

4.4
) and (
4.8

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F^{2}(v(\tau_{i},\lambda_{j}))F^{2}(v(\tau_{i^{\prime}},\lambda_{j^{\prime}}))\left(\Delta_{i,j}(V)^{2}-\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\right)\left(\Delta_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}(V)^{2}-\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\mathbb{E}\Bigg[F^{2}(v(\tau_{i},\lambda_{j}))F^{2}(v(\tau_{i^{\prime}},\lambda_{j^{\prime}}))\left(\Delta_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}(V)^{2}-\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta_{i,j}(V)^{2}-\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{\sqrt{2}(\tau_{i}+\lambda_{j})}\right]\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,N^{-3}\mathbb{E}\left[F^{2}(v(\tau_{i},\lambda_{j}))F^{2}(v(\tau_{i^{\prime}},\lambda_{j^{\prime}}))\left(\Delta_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}(V)^{2}-\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,N^{-3}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[F^{8}(v(\tau_{i},\lambda_{j}))\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\cdot\left(\mathbb{E}\left[F^{8}(v(\tau_{i^{\prime}},\lambda_{j^{\prime}}))\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\cdot\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\Delta_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}(V)^{2}-\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\right)^{2}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,N^{-5}.

For pairs with
(i,j),(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
satisfying
|(i+j)-(i^{\prime}+j^{\prime})|<2
, we apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the moment estimates (
2.6
) and (
4.4
) to get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F^{2}(v(\tau_{i},\lambda_{j}))F^{2}(v(\tau_{i^{\prime}},\lambda_{j^{\prime}}))\left(\Delta_{i,j}(V)^{2}-\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\right)\left(\Delta_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}(V)^{2}-\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\left(\mathbb{E}\left[F^{8}(v(\tau_{i},\lambda_{j}))\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\cdot\left(\mathbb{E}\left[F^{8}(v(\tau_{i^{\prime}},\lambda_{j^{\prime}}))\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle\,\,\cdot\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\Delta_{i,j}(V)^{2}-\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\right)^{4}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\Delta_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}(V)^{2}-\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\right)^{4}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,N^{-4}.

Summing over all pairs, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|I_{2}|]\lesssim N^{-1}.

Step 3: Estimation of
I_{3}
.

By Proposition
2.1
, the solution
u
is Hölder continuous; consequently,
v
is also Hölder continuous.
Using the Lipschitz continuity of
F
, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and the moment estimates (
2.6
) and (
4.4
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|I_{3}|]\leq

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\int_{\tau_{i}}^{\tau_{i+1}}\int_{\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}}\mathbb{E}\left[|F^{2}(v(\tau_{i},\lambda_{j}))-F^{2}(v(\tau,\lambda))|\right]d\tau d\lambda

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\int_{\tau_{i}}^{\tau_{i+1}}\int_{\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[|v(\tau_{i},\lambda_{j})-v(\tau,\lambda)|^{2}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}d\tau d\lambda

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,N^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Combining the estimates for
I_{1},I_{2}
, and
I_{3}
, we obtain (
4.5
).
The proof is complete. ∎

4.2.
Parameter estimates

In this section, we consider the problem of estimating the diffusion parameter in the nonlinear damped stochastic Klein-Gordon equation:

\begin{cases}\vskip 6.0pt\displaystyle{\left(\mathop{}\mathbin{\Box}+a\partial_{t}+m^{2}\right)u_{\theta}(t,x)=\theta F\left(u_{\theta}(t,x)\right)\dot{W}(t,x),\quad t>0,x\in\mathbb{R},}\\
\displaystyle{u_{\theta}(0,x)=0,\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u_{\theta}(0,x)=0.}\end{cases}

(4.9)

We estimate
\theta
from discrete observations of
u_{\theta}
on a space-time grid.

For every
\tau>0
and
\lambda\geq-\tau
, let

\displaystyle v_{\theta}(\tau,\lambda):=u_{\theta}\left(\frac{\tau+\lambda}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-\tau+\lambda}{\sqrt{2}}\right).

(4.10)

For every
N\geq 1
, let

\displaystyle Q_{N}(v_{\theta}):=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\Delta_{i,j}(v_{\theta})^{2},

(4.11)

where

\Delta_{i,j}(v_{\theta}):=v_{\theta}\left(\tau_{i+1},\lambda_{j+1}\right)-v_{\theta}\left(\tau_{i+1},\lambda_{j}\right)-v_{\theta}\left(\tau_{i},\lambda_{j+1}\right)+v_{\theta}\left(\tau_{i},\lambda_{j}\right),

and
\tau_{i}:=\frac{i}{N},\lambda_{j}:=\frac{j}{N}
.

Using Proposition
1.3
, we know that

\displaystyle Q_{N}(v_{\theta})\longrightarrow\frac{\theta^{2}}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}F^{2}\big(v_{\theta}(\tau,\lambda)\big)d\tau d\lambda\,\,\,\text{in }L^{1}(\Omega),

(4.12)

as
N\rightarrow\infty
.
This convergence suggests a consistent estimator for the diffusion parameter
\theta
based on the observations

\left\{u_{\theta}\left(\frac{\frac{i}{N}+\frac{j}{N}}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-\frac{i}{N}+\frac{j}{N}}{\sqrt{2}}\right);\,i,j=0,1,\cdots,N\right\}.

In view of the limit (
4.12
), a natural way to estimate
\theta
is to use the approximation

\displaystyle\theta\approx\sqrt{\frac{4Q_{N}(v_{\theta})}{\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}F^{2}\big(v_{\theta}(\tau,\lambda)\big)d\tau d\lambda}}.

(4.13)

To evaluate the denominator, we discretize the double integral by a Riemann sum:

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}F^{2}\big(v_{\theta}(\tau,\lambda)\big)d\tau d\lambda\approx N^{-2}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}F^{2}\big(v_{\theta}(\tau_{i},\lambda_{j})\big).

This leads to the following estimator for the diffusion parameter
\theta
:

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}_{N}:=\sqrt{\frac{4N^{2}Q_{N}(v_{\theta})}{\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}F^{2}\big(v_{\theta}(\tau_{i},\lambda_{j})\big)}},\ \ \ N\geq 1.

(4.14)

Based on Proposition
1.3
, one can directly obtain the following consistency result. For a detailed proof, see Corollary 4.1 in
[
25
]
.

Corollary 4.1
.

The estimator
\hat{\theta}_{N}
defined in (
4.14
) is weakly consistent, i.e.,

\hat{\theta}_{N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\theta,\ \ \ \ \text{as }N\rightarrow\infty.

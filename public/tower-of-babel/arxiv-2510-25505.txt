Resonant production of millicharged scalars in k 2 > 0 k^{2}>0 electromagnetic wave background Preprint: INR-TH-2025-019
Ekaterina Dmitrieva, Petr Satunin

Abstract
We investigate the solution of the Klein-Gordon equation for a charged scalar particle in an electromagnetic plane wave background with k 2 > 0 k^{2}>0 which can be realized in a medium with a refractive index n < 1 n<1 . We reduce the equation of motion to the Mathieu equation, which has resonant exponentially growing solutions for certain parameter ranges. We show that this resonance indeed occurs for small scalar masses. We apply our results to derive constraints on millicharged particles and compare them with existing experimental data.



I
Introduction

Particle production in an intense external field is still an intriguing phenomenon. The presence of the external field modifies the wave function of outgoing particles, allowing some processes forbidden in the standard perturbation theory based on the plane wave decomposition. A famous example is the Schwinger effect – electron-positron pair production in an external electric field
Schwinger 1951
and related processes (see books and reviews
Ritus and Nikishov 1979
;
Dittrich and Gies 2000
;
Kuznetsov and Mikheev 2013
;
Fedotov et al. 2023
). Another nonperturbative effect related only to the case of boson production is the Bose enhancement: the production rate resonantly grows if final particles are produced in the background of already presented occupied state of the same field.

This effect appears in cosmology as so-called preheating after inflation when an oscillating heavy inflaton field resonantly decays to the matter scalars
Kofman et al. 1994
;
Kofman et al. 1997
;
Khlebnikov and Tkachev 1996
;
Khlebnikov and Tkachev 1997
, see also the book
Lozanov 2020
. Mathematically, the equation of motion for produced scalar modes of fixed wavevector
\vec{k}
reduces to the Mathieu equation, which parametric plane has bands of periodic and exponentially growing solutions, see book
McLachlan 1947
.

The similar effect appears in a toy model if an intense plane wave of massive or massless scalar field decays to another spice of scalars
Arza 2022
;
Dmitrieva and Satunin 2025
. Massive plane wave with the help of a relativistic boost reduces to the preheating case, showing the Mathieu stability band picture
Dmitrieva and Satunin 2025
while the massless plane wave resonantly produces particles only due to the tachyonic instability in that toy model Lagrangian
Arza 2022
.

In this article, we are looking for the generalization of the aforementioned mechanism to the particle production in an electromagnetic plane wave background. It is well-known that a plane wave of electromagnetic field in vacuum (squared 4-momentum vanishes,
k^{2}=0
) is stable: there is no tachyonic instability or the Schwinger effect in such field configuration
Schwinger 1951
. Besides the plane wave in vacuum, one can consider plane waves in different types of media, obtaining both
k^{2}<0
in standard crystals and
k^{2}>0
in thermal plasma or in some types of metamaterials
Wood and Pendry 2007
;
Shin et al. 2009
;
Navau et al. 2009
;
Magnus et al. 2008
;
Economou et al. 2008
;
Lapine et al. 2013
.

In
k^{2}>0
background the photon became effectively massive; in the presence of charged particles with small masses in the theory one expects the similar type of particle production as for scalars
Dmitrieva and Satunin 2025
.

Such particles with tiny electric charges are actively discussed as one of “new physics” extensions of the Standard model and are called millicharged particles (mCP)
Ignatiev et al. 1979
;
Holdom 1986
;
Holdom 1986
;
Batell and Gherghetta 2006
;
Abel and Schofield 2004
;
Abel et al. 2008
. Besides, mCP emerges in cosmology as a candidate for the dark matter particle alone or as part of wider dark sector models
Brahm and Hall 1990
;
Bogorad and Toro 2022
;
Jaeckel and Schenk 2021
;
Shiu et al. 2013
;
Feng et al. 2009
;
Cline et al. 2012
;
Okun 1982
;
Holdom 1986
.
Searches for these particles are conducted in various experiments
Berlin et al. 2024
;
Arza et al. 2025
;
Gninenko et al. 2007
;
Barak et al. 2024
;
de Montigny et al. 2023
and astrophysical observations
Davidson et al. 2000
;
Berlin and Schutz 2022
;
Chang et al. 2018
;
Lepidi 2007
;
Cruz and McQuinn 2023
.

The wave function for scalar electrons in a medium with nontrivial
n
has been studied in
Cronström and Noga 1977
;
Becker 1977
;
Heinzl et al. 2016
;
King and Hu 2016
. It was shown that the equation for the wave function reduces to the Mathieu equation, which solution is finite or infinitely grows in different bands of the parametric plane. The growing solution was considered in
Cronström and Noga 1977
;
Becker 1977
;
Heinzl et al. 2016
;
King and Hu 2016
as unphysical: in fact, the Schrodinger equation with normalized wave function was solved.
In this article, we reveal the interpretation of this growing solution for mCPs.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
we consider the Klein-Gordon equation for a running wave and we reduce it to the Mathieu equation. In sections
II.2
,
II.3
we investigate instability bounds from narrow and broad resonance, respectively. In Sec.
III
we investigate standing wave and compare the result of this section with experimental constraints. In Sec.
IV
we make a conclusion.

II
Particle production in running wave with
k^{2}>0
background

We consider the production of a charged scalar field in an external field of a running electromagnetic wave in a medium, described by the dispersion relation
\omega=n^{-1}\cdot|\vec{k}|
, where
(\omega,\vec{k})
is the momentum four-vector, and
n
is the medium refraction index. The Lagrangian for the scalar field
\psi
reads,

\mathcal{L}=D_{\mu}\psi^{*}D_{\mu}\psi-m^{2}\psi^{*}\psi,

(1)

where
D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}+ieA_{\mu}
. The dynamics of the scalar field
\psi
is governed by the equation of motion, which is the Klein-Gordon equation,

(\Box+2ie\mathcal{A}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}-e^{2}\mathcal{A}^{2}+m^{2})\psi=0.

(2)

Notably, the same equation is obtained in quantum mechanics (QM) as the relativistic extension of the Schroedinger equation for the wave function of a charged scalar particle
Cronström and Noga 1977
;
Becker 1977
; the difference is that the QM wave function should be normalized to unity; there is no such condition for the scalar field.

We are looking for the solution of eq. (
2
) using the following ansatz,

\psi=e^{-ip\cdot x}F(\phi),

(3)

where
\phi\equiv k\cdot x
(here and further, the notation
{}^{\prime}\cdot\,^{\prime}
means the scalar product of four vectors), and
p_{\mu}
is an arbitrary four vector, that satisfies the condition
p^{2}=m^{2}
.
Inserting the ansatz (
3
) into the equation (
2
) we obtain the expression

k^{2}F^{\prime\prime}(\phi)-2i\,p\cdot kF^{\prime}(\phi)+(2e\,p\cdot\mathcal{A}-e^{2}\mathcal{A}^{2})F(\phi)=0.

(4)

We consider a circularly polarized monochromatic wave,

\mathcal{A}^{\mu}=a^{\mu}\cos\phi+b^{\mu}\sin\phi,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ a\cdot a=b\cdot b<0,

(5)

where the momentum
\vec{k}
directed along the
z
-axis;
a_{\mu}=(0,E_{0}/\omega,0,0)
and
b_{\mu}=(0,0,E_{0}/\omega,0)
are two constant space-like orthogonal four-vectors determining the polarization,
E_{0}
is the amplitude of the electromagnetic wave. We introduce the abbreviations:

\phi_{0}=\arctan(p\cdot b/p\cdot a),\quad\zeta=\frac{1}{2}\left(\phi-\phi_{0}\right).

(6)

Using the subsequent ansatz

F(\phi)=w(\zeta)\mbox{exp}\left[i\left(\frac{(p\cdot k)}{k^{2}}\right)\phi\right],

(7)

we finally reduce the equation of motion (
2
) to the Mathieu equation form, cl.
Cronström and Noga 1977
;
Becker 1977

\frac{d^{2}w}{d\zeta^{2}}+(A_{p}+2q\cos(2\zeta))w=0,

(8)

where

A_{p}=\frac{4}{k^{2}}\left(\frac{(p\cdot k)^{2}}{k^{2}}-e^{2}a^{2}\right),

(9)

and

q=\frac{4e}{k^{2}}((p\cdot a)^{2}+(p\cdot b)^{2})^{1/2}.

(10)

The Mathieu equation (
8
) properties and solutions are well-studied, see e.g. the book
McLachlan 1947
. The main property of the Mathieu equation solution is that depending on the values of the parameters
(A_{p},q)
the solutions are periodic (stable) or exponentially growing (unstable). The corresponding areas of stability and instability are presented at the Mathieu equation chart, see Fig.
1
. The instability areas, or the resonant bands, are the stripes which start for
q=0
at the points
A_{p}=N^{2}
,
N
is a natural number, and grow wider with
q
.

Depending on the values of
A_{p}
and
q
one distinguishes two regimes of analytical solutions
Kofman et al. 1997
: narrow resonance, related to the case
q\ll 1
,
A_{p}\sim 1
(the beginning of the first resonant band at Fig.
1
), and broad resonance (
A_{p}\sim 2q\gg 1
); both of them we study below in detail. For the reason of simplicity, we start from the perturbative regime which should be the limiting case for the narrow resonance at
q\to 0
.

II.1
Perturbative regime.

Perturbatively, a photon of 4-momentum
k_{\mu}=(\omega,0,0,n\omega)
decays to a pair of charged massive scalars of, for simplicity, the same energy and same
z

p_{\mu}=\left(\sqrt{\left(\frac{n\omega}{2}\right)^{2}+m^{2}+p^{2}_{1}+p^{2}_{2}},\pm p_{1},\pm p_{2},\frac{n\omega}{2}\right)

(11)

The form (

p_{\perp}^{2}\equiv p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}=\frac{\omega^{2}(1-n^{2})}{4}-m^{2}.

(12)

n<1
and for a quite small mass

of a millicharged scalar. Substituting (
12

9

q\to 0
. We expect that the narrow resonance is an extension of this process to larger amplitude
E_{0}

The experimental signature of resonant mCP production can be provided in two ways: anomalous energy losses in the present electromagnetic wave or detection of produced mCP by another process. Here we adopt the first approach and neglect for simplicity the back reaction process which becomes relevant after sufficient losses, see

III
Standing wave

In conjunction with the running wave discussed in the previous section, we study resonant millicharge particle production in a standing wave background. A standing wave in the radio frequency range can have a significantly larger amplitude than a running wave, as the electromagnetic energy is coherently accumulated within a closed volume.

IV
Conclusion

In this article, we examine the solution of the Klein-Gordon equation for scalar electrons with tiny electric charge in a medium of refractive index
n
. We reduce the equations of motion to the Mathieu equation and investigate the instability of its solution. This is a classical effect that occurs only for a refractive index
n<1
. These values can be realized in either a plasma or in metamaterials.

We investigated narrow and broad resonance regimes and obtained the instability bound for mCP.
For the narrow resonance regime, we considered a model of an infinity medium, which provides only approximate constraints. However, we assume that the result beyond our model will be close to the boundary found. Consequently, we can conclude that this effect is only possible for small masses of the resulting particle, which requires fulfillment of the condition of dimensionless mass
\mu\ll 1
. Under this condition, the sensitivity of the proposed experimental technique to the charge
e
of mCP does not depend on its mass
m
. When this condition is not satisfied, the instability boundary increases abruptly and exceeds that of the broad resonance. Therefore, for large masses, we consider the broad resonance regime. In this regime we obtained that even for zero dimensionless mass
\mu
there is no such
\pi_{\perp}
for which
\mathcal{E}=0
. Consequently, we can conclude that for large masses there exists a threshold value for the electric field amplitude, which depends on the charge of mCP.

Subsequently, we compared those results with existing experimental constraints. In total we obtained constraint that do not overlap with the experimental results in a limited range of parameters. In order to increase this range we should increase the amplitude of the electric field.

Following this, we consider the case of background electromagnetic standing wave. We also used known solutions and boundaries of the Mathieu equation. But, in order to obtain the Mathieu equation, we used the large mass approximation. Therefore, we cannot consider small masses and narrow resonance regime in this approximation.
Thereafter we investigate different values of the amplitude of the electric field for broad resonance regime.
As a result, we obtained the better constraint than for the case of running wave.

The next logical step is to explore fast radio bursts (FRB) which interact with the plasma that is in its path of propagation
Kumar et al. 2023
;
Tuntsov 2014
. Moreover, if the observations reveal unknown energy losses, then this may indicate the production of millicharged particles. This task we will consider in future.

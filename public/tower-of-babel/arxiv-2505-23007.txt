Can we live in a baby universe formed by a delayed first-order phase transition? Preprint: CPTNP-2025-014
Qing-Hong Cao, Masanori Tanaka, Jun-Chen Wang, Ke-Pan Xie, Jing-Jun Zhang

Abstract
We examine the idea that our universe began as a baby universe and show that this is feasible in a U ​ ( 1 ) B − L U(1)_{B-L} extension of the Standard Model with the classically conformal principle. The framework is consistent with current cosmological data and predicts a heavy neutral gauge boson, which could be detected at colliders.

;
Linde 2017

One realization of the multiverse scenario is the baby universes, which can emerge during a cosmic first-order phase transition (FOPT), where stochastic bubble nucleation leaves rare regions trapped in the false vacuum. These regions subsequently evolve into inflating baby universes, appearing as primordial black holes (PBHs), which is called the super-critical PBH

However, in this work, we demonstrate that such an eternal inflation can be naturally terminated by low-scale QCD phase transitions within a classically conformal scenario. In this case, the standard cosmology could be restored, and we may live in a baby universe.

Since the radiation energy is diluted by cosmic expansion, while the vacuum energy remains unchanged, such a FVD forms a large energy density contrast and can collapse into a super-critical PBH, which contains a baby universe that connects with the parent universe via an untraversable wormhole

The model
. The baryon-minus-lepton number is gauged, leading to a new gauge boson
Z^{\prime}
that couples to the Standard Model (SM) fermions with strength
g_{B-L}X
, where
X
denotes the corresponding quantum number
B-L
. A complex scalar field
\Phi=(\varphi+iG)/\sqrt{2}
, which is a singlet for the SM gauge group but charged
+2
under
U(1)_{B-L}
, is introduced to spontaneously break the
U(1)_{B-L}
gauge symmetry, providing mass for
Z^{\prime}
. The tree-level joint potential,

\displaystyle V_{\rm tree}(H,\Phi)=\lambda_{h}|H|^{4}+\lambda_{\varphi}|\Phi|^{4}+\lambda_{h\varphi}|H|^{2}|\Phi|^{2},

(1)

is scale-invariant, and it preserves both the
U(1)_{B-L}
and EW symmetries. However, radiative corrections induce the Coleman-Weinberg (CW) potential
Coleman and Weinberg 1973
. Assuming that the
U(1)_{B-L}
scale is much higher than the EW scale, we can use the sequential symmetry breaking approximation
Chataignier et al. 2018
, in which the one-loop correction is dominated by

\displaystyle V_{1\ell}(\varphi)\simeq\frac{3\overline{m}_{Z^{\prime}}^{4}}{64\pi^{2}}\left(\ln\frac{\varphi^{2}}{v_{\varphi}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\,,

(2)

with
\overline{m}_{Z^{\prime}}=2g_{B-L}\varphi
, which yields a vacuum expectation value (VEV)
\left\langle\varphi\right\rangle=v_{\varphi}
, spontaneously breaks the
U(1)_{B-L}
. This induces a mass square term along the Higgs direction, breaking the EW symmetry via a negative
\lambda_{h\varphi}\simeq-m_{h}^{2}/v_{\varphi}^{2}
, where
m_{h}\approx 125
GeV is the observed Higgs boson mass.

Baby universe formation
. The scalar potential at finite temperature along the
B-L
scalar direction is given by
Chiang and Senaha 2017

\displaystyle V_{\rm tot}(\varphi,T)=V_{\rm tree}(H=0,\varphi)+V_{1\ell}(\varphi)+V_{T}(\varphi,T)\,.

(3)

For the thermal correction part, it is given by

\displaystyle V_{T}(\varphi,T)\simeq 3\frac{T^{4}}{2\pi^{2}}I_{B}\left(\frac{\overline{m}_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}{T^{2}}\right)\,,

(4)

where the thermal integral is

\displaystyle I_{B}(a)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}xx^{2}\ln\left[1-e^{-\sqrt{x^{2}+a}}\right]\,.

(5)

We also include the thermal resummation to avoid breaking the perturbative expansion, and the complete form of the potential is given in Appendix
A
.

The scalar potential in Eq. (
3
) could possess two local minima separated by a barrier, where the
\varphi=0
one is the false vacuum, while the
\varphi\neq 0
one is the true vacuum. The universe then decays to the true vacuum with a decay rate per unit volume and time
\Gamma(t)\propto T^{4}e^{-S_{3}(T)/T}

Linde 1983
, and the volume fraction of symmetry unbroken regions is given by
Guth and Weinberg 1981
,

\displaystyle F(t,t_{d})=\exp\left[-\frac{4\pi}{3}\int_{t_{d}}^{t}\text{d}t^{\prime}\Gamma(t^{\prime})a^{3}(t^{\prime})r^{3}(t,t^{\prime})\right]\,,

(6)

where
r(t,t^{\prime})=\int_{t^{\prime}}^{t}\text{d}\tilde{t}[v_{w}/a(\tilde{t})]
is the comoving radius of the vacuum bubbles, and
t_{d}
is the time when the bubble nucleation starts, which varies in different positions. For the bubble wall velocity
v_{w}
, we take
v_{w}=0.7
as a benchmark value. We consider a FVD, within which
t_{d}
is sufficiently large and
F_{\rm FVD}(t,t_{d})\simeq 1
. Due to the randomness of bubble nucleation, such FVDs must exist; we will calculate the corresponding probability later. Outside the FVDs, we take
t_{d}=t_{c}
and let
F(t)\equiv F(t,t_{c})
, where
t_{c}
is the time at the critical temperature.

We focus on the Einstein equation for the boundary of the FVD
\chi
. Under assumptions (i) the FVD is spherically symmetric and (ii) the thin-wall approximation can be applied to the boundary, we can employ Israel’s junction condition
Israel 1966
. The simplified Einstein equation is given by
Tanahashi and Yoo 2015
;
Deng 2020
;
Hashino et al. 2025

\ddot{\chi}+(4-3a^{2}\dot{\chi}^{2})H\dot{\chi}+\frac{2}{a^{2}\chi}(1-a^{2}\dot{\chi}^{2})\\
=\left(\frac{3\sigma}{4M_{\rm pl}^{2}}-\frac{\rho_{V}}{\sigma}\right)\frac{(1-a^{2}\dot{\chi}^{2})^{3/2}}{a}\,,

(7)

where
M_{\rm pl}=2.435\times 10^{18}\,{\rm GeV}
, and
\rho_{V}=\Delta V
is the vacuum energy in the FVD.
For the Hubble parameter
H
and the scale factor
a
, these can be determined by the Friedmann equation

\displaystyle H^{2}=\left(\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{3M_{\rm pl}^{2}}\rho(t)\,,

(8)

with
\rho(t)=\rho_{R}(t)+\rho_{V}(t)
, where
\rho_{R}(t)
is the radiation energy component, whose time evolution is determined by

\displaystyle\frac{\text{d}\rho_{R}}{dt}+4H\rho_{R}=-\frac{\text{d}\rho_{V}}{\text{d}t}\,.

(9)

In the thin-wall approximation, the surface energy is given by
Linde 1983

\displaystyle\sigma\simeq\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}r\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\text{d}\phi_{b}}{\text{d}r}\right)^{2}+V(\phi_{b},T(t_{\rm in}))\right]\,,

(10)

where
\phi_{b}(r)

CosmoTransitions

For simplicity, we assume that the comoving FVD boundary is static at the initial time
t_{\rm in}

\dot{\chi}(t_{\rm in})=0

We set the initial time
t_{\rm in}
when a symmetry unbroken Hubble patch can remain in the center of a
3\times 3\times 3
Hubble patch grid:
F(t_{\rm in})=3^{-3}\simeq 0.04

Such a situation is expected to be required for the baby universe formation.
We define the comoving horizon radius
\chi_{h}
as

\displaystyle a(t_{\rm in})\chi_{h}=\frac{M_{\rm MS}(t_{\rm in})}{4\pi M_{\rm pl}^{2}}\,,

(11)

where
M_{\rm MS}(t)
represents the Misner-Sharp mass

;
Deng 2020

M_{\rm MS}(t)=\frac{4\pi}{3}\rho(t)R^{3}(t)+4\pi\sigma\gamma R^{2}(t)\\
+4\sigma H(t)\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-1}R^{2}(t)-\frac{\pi\sigma^{2}}{M_{\rm pl}^{2}}R^{3}(t)\,,

(12)

Numerical results and discussions
. FVDs where the symmetry breaking is sufficiently delayed can become baby universes. Therefore, we can relate the probability of no bubble nucleation in a FVD
Kanemura et al. 2024
;
Hashino et al. 2025
with the probability that we are living in a baby universe as

\displaystyle P_{\rm baby}=F(t_{\rm in})\exp\left[-\frac{4\pi}{3}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{V}}\text{d}t\frac{a^{3}(t)}{a^{3}(t_{\rm in})}R^{3}(t_{\rm in})\Gamma(t)\right]\,,

(13)

at the threshold value of
c_{i}
when the super-critical solution can be obtained, where
R(t_{\rm in})=a(t_{\rm in})\chi_{\rm in}
, and
t_{V}
is the time defined by the vacuum energy, respectively. The red shaded region in Fig.
2
shows the parameter space with
10^{-78}\leq P_{\rm baby}\leq 0.04
, which we identify as the region where we could be living in a baby universe. In the gray region, the baby universe cannot be formed because the necessary condition for the baby universe formation
F(t)\leq 0.04
, is not satisfied in the parent universe.
Interestingly, the contour with
P_{\rm baby}=10^{-78}
is not sensitive to the mass of
Z^{\prime}
boson. The reason is that the bounce action
S_{3}/T
is approximately proportional to
g_{B-L}^{-3}

Iso et al. 2017

P_{\rm baby}\propto e^{-\Gamma}

\Gamma\propto e^{-S_{3}/T}

13

m_{Z^{\prime}}

P_{\rm baby}=10^{-78}

g_{B-L}\simeq 0.497

We adopt the lower limit of
P_{\rm baby}
as

\displaystyle N_{\rm universe}\gtrsim(e^{N})^{3}=e^{180}\simeq 10^{78}\,.

Gravitational wave (GW) produced from the FOPT observations may provide another probe of our scenario. Since the super-horizon FVD becomes the baby universe in our setup, it is expected that the GWs produced by the
U(1)_{B-L}
symmetry breaking in the parent universe cannot propagate into the baby universe. In our baby universe, the
U(1)_{B-L}
FOPT triggered by low-scale chiral symmetry breaking is extremely strong, however, its duration is typically too short to produce significant GWs
Liu and Xie 2024
. Recently, it has been investigated that the tachyonic preheating in the
U(1)_{B-L}
model may produce measurable GWs
Schmitt and Sagunski 2024

Conclusion
. We proposed that we could be living in a baby universe that is produced during a supercooled FOPT of the parent universe. The eternal inflation in conventional baby universe scenarios, which ruins any possibility that we are living in such universes, is prevented by the low-scale chiral symmetry breaking in the classically conformal models. We provided a concrete minimal
B-L
model to illustrate this idea, which could be tested in current and future collider experiments.

We also comment on problems with dark matter and baryon asymmetry of the universe. Any preexisting dark matter and baryon symmetry will be diluted by the thermal inflation inside the FVD. After the FOPT reheating, they can be produced in various ways. If
T_{\rm rh}\gtrsim 100
GeV, dark matter could be the conventional weakly interacting massive particles decoupling via freeze-out, while the baryon number could be generated via EW baryogenesis, or low-scale leptogenesis. If
T_{\rm rh}
is much lower than the EW scale, the supercooled dark matter mechanism applies
Hambye et al. 2018
;
Wong and Xie 2023
, and the baryon asymmetry could be realized via meson decays
Alonso-Álvarez
et al. 2020
;
Nelson and Xiao 2019
.

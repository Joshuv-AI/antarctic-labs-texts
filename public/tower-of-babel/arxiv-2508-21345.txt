2 2 -split from Feynman diagrams and Expansions
Bo Feng, Liang Zhang, Kang Zhou

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the 2 2 -split behavior of tree-level amplitudes of bi-adjoint scalar (BAS), Yang-Mills (YM), non-linear sigma model (NLSM), and general relativity (GR) theories under certain kinematic conditions. Our approach begins with a proof, based on the Feynman diagram method, of the 2 2 -split property for tree-level BAS ⊕ \oplus X amplitudes with X = YM , NLSM , GR \mathrm{X}={\mathrm{YM},\mathrm{NLSM},\mathrm{GR}} . The proof relies crucially on a particular pattern in the Feynmam rules of various vertices. Building on this, we use the expansion of X amplitudes into BAS ⊕ \oplus X amplitudes to establish the 2 2 -split behavior. As a byproduct, we derive universal expansions of the resulting pure X currents into BAS currents, which closely parallel the corresponding on-shell amplitude expansions.

The BAS theory describes cubic interactions of massless scalars, governed by the Lagrangian

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\rm BAS}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\phi^{Aa}\partial^{\mu}\phi^{Aa}+\frac{\lambda}{3!}F^{ABC}f^{abc}\phi^{Aa}\phi^{Bb}\phi^{Cc}\,

(1)

\phi^{Aa}
carries a pair of group indices.

By employing standard techniques for group factor decomposition, the tree-level BAS amplitude can be expressed as

\displaystyle A^{\rm BAS}_{n}=\sum_{\sigma\in{\cal S}_{n}\setminus Z_{n}}\,\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}\in{\cal S}^{\prime}_{n}\setminus Z^{\prime}_{n}}\,{\rm tr}[T^{A_{\sigma_{1}}},\cdots T^{A_{\sigma_{n}}}]\,{\rm tr}[T^{a_{\sigma^{\prime}_{1}}}\cdots T^{a_{\sigma^{\prime}_{n}}}]\,{\cal A}^{\rm BAS}_{n}(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n}|\sigma^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,\sigma^{\prime}_{n})\,,

(2)

A_{n}

n

{\cal S}_{n}\setminus Z_{n}
and
{\cal S}^{\prime}_{n}\setminus Z^{\prime}_{n}
. Each partial amplitude
{\cal A}^{\rm BAS}_{n}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}\,|\,\sigma^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\sigma^{\prime}_{n})
includes only massless scalar propagators and is planar with respect to both orderings.

For instance, the four-point partial amplitude
{\cal A}^{\rm BAS}_{4}(1,2,3,4\,|\,1,2,4,3)

\displaystyle{\cal A}^{\rm BAS}_{4}(1,2,3,4|1,2,4,3)={1\over s_{12}}\,,

(3)

\displaystyle s_{p\cdots q}\equiv k_{p\cdots q}^{2}\,,~~~{\rm with}~k_{p\cdots q}\equiv\sum_{i=p}^{q}\,k_{i}\,,~~~~

(4)

•

6
Summary

In this paper, by extending the diagrammatic method of
Zhou:2024ddy
and leveraging universal expansions, we have reproduced the novel factorization phenomenon known as the
2
-split for tree-level BAS
\oplus
X and pure X amplitudes, where
\mathrm{X}={\mathrm{YM},\mathrm{NLSM},\mathrm{GR}}
. Our derivations provide an intuitive pictorial interpretation of the
2
-split: it can be viewed as the splitting of propagators along the lines
L_{i,v}
and
L_{j,v}
. For more complicated amplitudes, this mechanism becomes manifest only after expanding them into a suitable basis. Furthermore, we have identified universal expansions for the resulting pure currents arising in these
2
-split, closely analogous to expansions of the corresponding on-shell amplitudes. Although our derivation does not provide explicit expansions for the BAS
\oplus
YM and BAS
\oplus
GR currents, we have verified that they coincide with the corresponding amplitudes. For the BAS
\oplus
NLSM current, its expansion can be understood by the method described in the next paragraph.

We also highlight an alternative approach to accessing
2
-split for a variety of theories excluding GR. Starting from the
2
-split of pure GR amplitudes, all other
2
-split can be generated by applying the transmutation operators introduced in
Cheung:2017ems
, which map GR amplitudes to amplitudes of other theories. As discussed in
Zhou:2024ddy
, under
2
-split kinematics, each such operator factorizes naturally into two components acting separately on the two currents. This procedure thereby produces
2
-split behavior for all tree amplitudes considered in
Cachazo:2014xea
;
Cheung:2017ems
;
Zhou:2018wvn
. While this approach depends on having the
2
-split for GR amplitudes as an initial input, the method presented here offers an effective means to obtain this crucial starting point.

As outlined in Section
1
, a natural and intriguing question is whether the novel
2
-split phenomenon can be extended further—to encompass a broader range of theories at tree level, as well as to loop-level Feynman integrands, potentially with suitable modifications. Addressing this question demands robust methods for deriving
2
-split, and we expect that the approach developed in this paper may serve as a promising candidate.

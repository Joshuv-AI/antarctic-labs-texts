Higher Auslander-Reiten sequences revisited
Jian He, Hangyu Yin, Panyue Zhou

Abstract
Let ( 𝒞 , 𝔼 , 𝔰 ) (\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s}) be an n n -exangulated category with enough projectives and enough injectives, and 𝒳 \mathscr{X} be a cluster-tilting subcategory of 𝒞 \mathscr{C} . Liu and Zhou have shown that the quotient category 𝒞 / 𝒳 \mathscr{C}/\mathscr{X} is an n n -abelian category. In this paper, we prove that if 𝒞 \mathscr{C} has Auslander-Reiten n n -exangles, then 𝒞 / 𝒳 \mathscr{C}/\mathscr{X} has Auslander-Reiten n n -exact sequences. Moreover, we also show that if a Frobenius n n -exangulated category 𝒞 \mathscr{C} has Auslander-Reiten n n -exangles, then the stable category 𝒞 ¯ \overline{\mathscr{C}} of 𝒞 \mathscr{C} has Auslander-Reiten ( n + 2 ) (n+2) -angles. Keywords: n n -exangulated categories; n n -abelian categories; ( n + 2 ) (n+2) -angulated categories; Auslander-Reiten sequences 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 18G80; 18E10

Introduction

Auslander-Reiten theory, originally introduced by Auslander and Reiten in
[
1
,
2
]
, has evolved into a foundational instrument for exploring the representation theory of Artin algebras. Subsequently, this theory has been generalized to various contexts, including exact categories
[
16
]
, triangulated categories
[
7
,
23
]
and their subcategories
[
3
,
15
]
, as well as specific additive categories
[
17
,
15
,
24
]
, by various researchers.

The concept of extriangulated categories, introduced by Nakaoka and Palu in
[
21
]
, serves as a simultaneous generalization of both exact categories and triangulated categories. In this framework, a category is defined by a triplet
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
, where
\mathscr{C}
is an additive category,
\mathbb{E}:\mathscr{C}^{\rm op}\times\mathscr{C}\rightarrow{\rm Ab}
is an additive bifunctor, and
\mathfrak{s}
assigns to each
\delta\in\mathbb{E}(C,A)
a class of
3
-term sequences with end terms
A
and
C
, subject to certain axioms.
Recently, Herschend, Liu, and Nakaoka
[
10
]
extended this concept to introduce the notion of
n
-exangulated categories for any positive integer
n
. This extension serves not only as a higher-dimensional analogue of extriangulated categories but also as a common generalization of
n
-exact categories, as defined by Jasso
[
14
]
, and
(n+2)
-angulated categories, as defined by Geiss, Keller, and Oppermann
[
6
]
. Interestingly, examples of
n
-exangulated categories exist that are neither
n
-exact nor
(n+2)
-angulated, as discussed in
[
10
,
11
,
19
,
12
]
.
Furthermore, the notion of Auslander-Reiten
n
-exact sequences in
n
-abelian categories was introduced by Fedele in
[
4
]
. He, Hu, Zhang, and Zhou
[
9
]
similarly delved into the concept of Auslander-Reiten
n
-exangles, exploring their existence in
n
-exangulated categories.

Cluster-tilting theory provides a systematic approach to constructing
n
-abelian categories from
n
-exangulated categories. To elaborate, Liu and Zhou
[
20
]
have shown that taking the quotient of an
n
-exangulated category by a cluster-tilting subcategory inherently results in an induced
n
-abelian structure on the quotient. This establishes a significant link between
n
-exangulated categories and
n
-abelian categories. In this paper, we delve into the relation between “Auslander-Reiten sequences” within these two categories. Our first main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

(see Theorem
3.7
for details)

Let
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
be an
n
-exangulated category with split idempotents and let
\mathscr{X}
be a cluster-tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{C}
.
Assume that
\mathscr{C}
has enough projectives and enough injectives. If
\mathscr{C}
has Auslander-Reiten
n
-exangles, then
\mathscr{C}/\mathscr{X}
has Auslander-Reiten
n
-exact sequences.

Liu and Zhou
[
19
]
proved that the stable category of a Frobenius
n
-exangulated category is an
(n+2)
-angulated category, thereby establishing a significant connection between these categories. Building upon this foundational result, our second main result delves into exploring the relation between “Auslander-Reiten sequences” within these two categories.

Theorem 1.2
.

(see Theorem
3.16
for details)

Let
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
be a Frobenius
n
-exangulated category. If
\mathscr{C}
has Auslander-Reiten
n
-exangles, then the stable category
\overline{\mathscr{C}}
of
\mathscr{C}
has Auslander-Reiten
(n+2)
-angles.

The structure of the paper is as follows: In Section 2, we offer a review of essential definitions and facts crucial to our study. In Section 3,
we provide proofs for the two main results of this paper.

Preliminaries

2.1
n
-exangulated categories

let
\mathscr{C}
be an additive category and
n
be a positive integer. Suppose that
\mathscr{C}
is equipped with an additive bifunctor
\mathbb{E}\colon\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathscr{C}\to{\rm Ab}
, where
{\rm Ab}
is the category of abelian groups. Next we briefly recall some definitions and basic properties of
n
-exangulated categories from
[
10
]
. We omit some
details here, but the reader can find them in
[
10
]
.

For any pair of objects
A,C\in\mathscr{C}
, an element
\delta\in\mathbb{E}(C,A)
is called an
\mathbb{E}
-extension
or simply an
extension
. We also write such
\delta
as
{}_{A}\delta_{C}
when we indicate
A
and
C
. The zero element
{}_{A}0_{C}=0\in\mathbb{E}(C,A)
is called the
split
\mathbb{E}
-extension
. For any pair of
\mathbb{E}
-extensions
{}_{A}\delta_{C}
and
{}_{A^{\prime}}\delta{{}^{\prime}}_{C^{\prime}}
, let
\delta\oplus\delta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{E}(C\oplus C^{\prime},A\oplus A^{\prime})
be the
element corresponding to
(\delta,0,0,{\delta}{{}^{\prime}})
through the natural isomorphism
\mathbb{E}(C\oplus C^{\prime},A\oplus A^{\prime})\simeq\mathbb{E}(C,A)\oplus\mathbb{E}(C,A^{\prime})\oplus\mathbb{E}(C^{\prime},A)\oplus\mathbb{E}(C^{\prime},A^{\prime})
.

For any
a\in\mathscr{C}(A,A^{\prime})
and
c\in\mathscr{C}(C^{\prime},C)
,
\mathbb{E}(C,a)(\delta)\in\mathbb{E}(C,A^{\prime})\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mathbb{E}(c,A)(\delta)\in\mathbb{E}(C^{\prime},A)
are simply denoted by
a_{\ast}\delta
and
c^{\ast}\delta
, respectively.

Let
{}_{A}\delta_{C}
and
{}_{A^{\prime}}\delta{{}^{\prime}}_{C^{\prime}}
be any pair of
\mathbb{E}
-extensions. A
morphism

(a,c)\colon\delta\to{\delta}{{}^{\prime}}
of extensions is a pair of morphisms
a\in\mathscr{C}(A,A^{\prime})
and
c\in\mathscr{C}(C,C^{\prime})
in
\mathscr{C}
, satisfying the equality

a_{\ast}\delta=c^{\ast}{\delta}{{}^{\prime}}
.
Then the functoriality of
\mathbb{E}
implies
\mathbb{E}(c,a)=a_{\ast}(c^{\ast}\delta)=c^{\ast}(a_{\ast}\delta)
.

Definition 2.1
.

[
10
, Definition 2.7]

Let
\bf{C}_{\mathscr{C}}
be the category of complexes in
\mathscr{C}
. As its full subcategory, define
{\bf{C}}^{n+2}_{\mathscr{C}}
to be the category of complexes in
\mathscr{C}
whose components are zero in the degrees outside of
\{0,1,\ldots,n+1\}
. Namely, an object in
{\bf{C}}^{n+2}_{\mathscr{C}}
is a complex
X_{\bullet}=\{X_{i},d_{i}^{X}\}
of the form

X_{0}\xrightarrow{d_{0}^{X}}X_{1}\xrightarrow{d_{1}^{X}}\cdots\xrightarrow{d_{n-1}^{X}}X_{n}\xrightarrow{d_{n}^{X}}X_{n+1}.

We write a morphism
f_{\bullet}\colon X_{\bullet}\to Y_{\bullet}
simply
f_{\bullet}=(f_{0},f_{1},\ldots,f_{n+1})
, only indicating the terms of degrees
0,\ldots,n+1
.

Definition 2.2
.

[
10
, Definition 2.11]

By Yoneda lemma, any extension
\delta\in\mathbb{E}(C,A)
induces natural transformations

\delta_{\sharp}\colon\mathscr{C}(-,C)\Rightarrow\mathbb{E}(-,A)\ \ \text{and}\ \ \delta^{\sharp}\colon\mathscr{C}(A,-)\Rightarrow\mathbb{E}(C,-).

For any
X\in\mathscr{C}
, these
(\delta_{\sharp})_{X}
and
\delta^{\sharp}_{X}
are given as follows.

(1)

(\delta_{\sharp})_{X}\colon\mathscr{C}(X,C)\to\mathbb{E}(X,A)\ :\ f\mapsto f^{\ast}\delta
.

(2)

\delta^{\sharp}_{X}\colon\mathscr{C}(A,X)\to\mathbb{E}(C,X)\ :\ g\mapsto g_{\ast}\delta
.

We simply denote
(\delta_{\sharp})_{X}(f)
and
\delta^{\sharp}_{X}(g)
by
\delta_{\sharp}(f)
and
\delta^{\sharp}(g)
, respectively.

Definition 2.3
.

[
10
, Definition 2.9]

Let
\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},n
be as before. Define a category
\AE:=\AE^{n+2}_{(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E})}
as follows.

(1)

A pair
\langle X_{\bullet},\delta\rangle
is an object of the category
\AE
with
X_{\bullet}\in{\bf{C}}^{n+2}_{\mathscr{C}}

and
\delta\in\mathbb{E}(X_{n+1},X_{0})
, called an
\mathbb{E}
-attached

, if it satisfies

(d_{0}^{X})_{\ast}\delta=0~~\textrm{and}~~(d^{X}_{n})^{\ast}\delta=0.

We also denote it by

X_{0}\xrightarrow{d_{0}^{X}}X_{1}\xrightarrow{d_{1}^{X}}\cdots\xrightarrow{d_{n-2}^{X}}X_{n-1}\xrightarrow{d_{n-1}^{X}}X_{n}\xrightarrow{d_{n}^{X}}X_{n+1}\overset{\delta}{\dashrightarrow}.

(2)

For such pairs
\langle X_{\bullet},\delta\rangle
and
\langle Y_{\bullet},\rho\rangle
,
f_{\bullet}\colon\langle X_{\bullet},\delta\rangle\to\langle Y_{\bullet},\rho\rangle
is
defined to be a morphism in

(f_{0})_{\ast}\delta=(f_{n+1})^{\ast}\rho
.

Definition 2.4
.

[
10
, Definition 2.13]

An
n
-exangle
is an object
\langle X_{\bullet},\delta\rangle
in
\AE
that satisfies the listed conditions.

(1)

The following sequence of functors

is exact.

\mathscr{C}(-,X_{0})\xrightarrow{\mathscr{C}(-,\ d^{X}_{0})}\cdots\xrightarrow{\mathscr{C}(-,\ d^{X}_{n})}\mathscr{C}(-,X_{n+1})\xrightarrow{~\delta_{\sharp}~}\mathbb{E}(-,X_{0})

(2)

The following sequence of functors
\mathscr{C}\to\mathit{Ab}
is exact.

\mathscr{C}(X_{n+1},-)\xrightarrow{\mathscr{C}(d^{X}_{n},\ -)}\cdots\xrightarrow{\mathscr{C}(d^{X}_{0},\ -)}\mathscr{C}(X_{0},-)\xrightarrow{~\delta^{\sharp}~}\mathbb{E}(X_{n+1},-)

In particular any
n
-exangle is an object in
\AE
.
A
morphism of
n
-exangles
simply means a morphism in
\AE
. Thus

-exangles form a full subcategory of
\AE
.

Definition 2.5
.

[
10
, Definition 2.22]

Let
\mathfrak{s}
be a correspondence which associates a homotopic equivalence class
\mathfrak{s}(\delta)=[{}_{A}{X_{\bullet}}_{C}]
to each extension
\delta={}_{A}\delta_{C}
. Such

is called a

(R0)

For any morphism of extensions

is called a

of
(a,c)
.

In such a case, we simple say that “
X_{\bullet}
realizes
\delta

\mathfrak{s}(\delta)=[X_{\bullet}]
.

Moreover, a realization

(R1)

For any
\mathfrak{s}(\delta)=[X_{\bullet}]

-exangle.

(R2)

For any

, the zero element

\mathfrak{s}({}_{A}0_{0})=[A\overset{\mathrm{id}_{A}}{\longrightarrow}A\to 0\to\cdots\to 0\to 0].

Dually,
\mathfrak{s}({}_{0}0_{A})=[0\to 0\to\cdots\to 0\to A\overset{\mathrm{id}_{A}}{\longrightarrow}A]

.

Note that the above condition (R1) does not depend on representatives of the class
[X_{\bullet}]
.

[

2.2
n
-abelian categories

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an additive category and
f\colon A\rightarrow B
a morphism in
\mathcal{A}
. A
weak cokernel
of
f
is a morphism

g\colon B\rightarrow C
such that for any

the sequence of abelian groups

\mathcal{A}(C,X)\xrightarrow{~\mathcal{A}(g,X)~}\mathcal{A}(B,X)\xrightarrow{~\mathcal{A}(X,f)~}\mathcal{A}(A,X)

is exact. Equivalently,
g
is a weak cokernel of

if
gf=0
and for each morphism

h\colon B\rightarrow X
such that

there exists a (not necessarily unique) morphism

such that

. Clearly, a weak cokernel
g
of

is a cokernel of
f
if and only if
g
is an epimorphism.
The concept of a

is defined dually.

\textstyle{A_{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\varphi_{0}}
\textstyle{A_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

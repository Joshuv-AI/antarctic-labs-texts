Association of domestic water hardness with incident diabetic retinopathy and the mediating role of GID8: A UK Biobank prospective cohort study
Authors: Zhiyi Gu, Yujin Jiang, Xinyu Zhang, Yikeng Huang, Jiajun Xu, Xiaoqian Wu, Xinyu Zhu, Bo Li, Jian Chang, Shuzhi Zhao, Zhi Zheng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo investigate the longitudinal association between domestic water hardness and incident diabetic retinopathy (DR) in individuals with type 2 diabetes (T2D) and identify circulating proteomic mediators.MethodsThis study analysed data from 13,194 participants with prevalent T2D. Domestic water hardness was determined according to residential postcodes. Weighted Cox proportional hazards models and mediation analyses were employed to evaluate associations between water hardness, proteins, and DR risk.ResultsDuring a median follow-up of 13.28 years, 1,505 cases of incident DR were documented. Compared with soft water (<60 mg/L), very hard water (>180 mg/L) was associated with a 49% higher risk of DR (HR, 1.49; 95% CI, 1.12–2.00). The plasma protein GID8 (Glucose-induced degradation protein 8) was identified as a mediator, explaining 6.2% (95% CI, 1.2%–25.5%) of the association.ConclusionThese findings provide some preliminary proteomic insights that domestic hard water may be a novel, modifiable environmental risk factor for DR, potentially mediated through GID8-related pathways. Future research should include mechanistic studies to confirm the biological pathways linking domestic water hardness to diabetic retinopathy.Keywords: water hardness, diabetic retinopathy, observational study, UK biobank, proteomics

Introduction

Diabetic retinopathy (DR) is the leading cause of preventable blindness in working-age adults, 1 despite significant advances in systemic diabetes management. 2 While strict control of hyperglycaemia, hypertension, and dyslipidaemia is fundamental to reducing DR risk, 1 a substantial residual risk of DR remains, suggesting the influence of uncharacterized risk factors.

Water hardness, determined largely by calcium and magnesium concentrations, is a common environmental exposure. 3 Previous studies have explored the relationship between hard water and cardiovascular disease, 4 whereas its impact on diabetic microvascular complications like DR remains poorly defined. Considering the role of mineral balance in endothelial function and metabolic homeostasis,5–9 domestic water hardness may plausibly be associated with DR risk, but populational-level prospective studies in this field are still lacking.

Therefore, we used the UK Biobank, a large-scale prospective cohort, to examine the association between residential water hardness and incident DR in adults with T2D. We further explored circulating proteomic candidates that might provide biological context for the observed association.

Methods

Study design and study population

The UK Biobank is a large-scale cohort study that includes more than 500,000 participants in England, Wales and Scotland, all of whom were 40–69 years of age from 2006 to 2010. Since then, there have been subsequent follow-ups of the collected data. The participants provided their information through questionnaires, interviews, physical examinations, biological samples and health records to 22 centres near their domestic location throughout the country. 10

The study included participants with prevalent T2D at baseline; the definition is provided in the Supplementary Methods, Additional file 1. People with prevalent DR at baseline were also excluded. People with any lack of data on water hardness and covariates (n=12,101) were excluded as well. Ultimately, 13,194 participants were included in this study. The mediation study subset included 1486 participants with proteomics data (Figure S1, Additional file 1). Detailed information on the study design is provided in Supplementary Method, Additional file 1.

All participants provided online written informed consent. Ethical approval from the North West Multicenter Research Ethics Committee was obtained from the UK Biobank. We acquired an application (ID: 88982) to this databank.

Statistical analysis

The Schoenfeld residuals indicated that proportional-hazard assumption was violated, so we employed weighted Cox proportional hazard models 11 to estimate average hazard ratio (HR) and 95% confidence interval (CI), as this approach remains valid even under non-proportional hazards conditions.12,13 We used the R package ‘mediation’ to quantify the mediation effects of each plasma protein, calculating 95% CIs using the quasi-Bayesian approach with 1000 simulations. Linear regression models and survival regression models based on accelerated failure time formulation were fitted respectively for the mediator and the outcome. Details on the statistical analysis are provided in the Supplementary Methods, Additional file 1.

R software (version 4.4.3; R Development Core Team) was used to perform the statistical analyses.

Results

We included 13,194 T2D participants without baseline DR and full covariates. Among these, 65.4% were male, and the median age was 61 [interquartile range (IQR), 56 to 65] years. During a median (IQR) follow-up of 13.28 (12.36–14.10) years, 1,505 cases of incident DR were observed. To describe the characteristics of the participants at baseline, we divided them according to water hardness, as defined by the United States Geological Survey (USGS) classifications. The results are shown in Table S1, Additional file 1.

In each model, water hardness was significantly associated with the risk of DR, regardless of different variable forms. According to the USGS classification, compared with that of soft water (<60 mg/L), the HR of very hard water (>180 mg/L) was 1.49 (95% CI, 1.12–2.00; P=0.007) (Table 1). This trend remains consistent in both the continuous form and WHO classification (Table S2, Additional file 1). Restricted cubic spline analysis revealed no signs of nonlinear relationship between hard water and DR (P value for nonlinearity=0.501) (Figure S2, Additional file 1).

Subgroup analyses (Figure S3, Additional file 1) and additional analyses were conducted and all the results were consistent with the main analyses (Tables S3, Additional file 1). Further studies on calcium and magnesium were conducted. After full adjustment, both calcium and magnesium were positively correlated with DR risk (Table S2, Additional file 1).

Mediation analysis

Among participants with proteomics data (n=1,486), 13 proteins were significantly associated with incident DR after multiple testing correction (Table S4, Additional file 1). Of these, 3 proteins were retained by the LASSO regression model (Table S5, Additional file 1), and GID8 (Glucose-induced degradation protein 8) was identified as the only statistically significant mediator. Mediation analysis demonstrated a modest yet significant mediation effect, with a mediation proportion of 6.2% (95%CI: 1.2% – 25.5%) (Figure 1) (Table S6, Additional file 1).

Discussion

In this prospective cohort of 13,194 T2D patients, living in areas with very hard water was associated with a 49% increased risk of developing DR compared to soft water areas. In proteomic mediation analyses, GID8 emerged as the sole statistically significant mediator, explaining 6.2% of the association. These findings generate the hypothesis that exposure to very hard water may be relevant to the development of DR, with GID8 representing one potential biological pathway.

Calcium homeostasis is vital for vascular endothelial function, 5 and has been found to have a harmful effect on DR in our previous and other studies.7,8 Regarding magnesium, higher magnesium levels in domestic water have been positively correlated with several cardiovascular outcomes.4,14,15 Our findings support a role of water hardness in DR risk; however, the underlying mechanism requires further investigation.

GID8 (also known as TWA1) is a core component of the CTLH E3 ubiquitin ligase complex.16,17 It functions to regulate the Wnt/β-catenin signalling pathway by promoting the nuclear retention of β-catenin. 18 The Wnt pathway is critically involved in retinal vascular development,19,20 and has been suggested to have a pathogenic effect in DR. 21 Our mediation analysis suggests that GID8 may mediate part of the association between hard water and incident DR, although further mechanistic evidence is needed to confirm the hypothesis and to explore the specific biological pathway.

The strengths of this study include its prospective design, the large sample size of the UK Biobank, and the use of sophisticated proteomic profiling. However, while interpreting our results, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, incident DR in this study was ascertained through inpatient diagnoses, which captures DR without information on severity. Future research should investigate whether the association between water hardness and DR risk differs by disease severity, with particular attention to the distinction between proliferative and non-proliferative DR. Second, the use of postcode-level water data may introduce misclassification, as it cannot account for individual variations in bottled water use or home filtration. In environmental epidemiology, such non-differential misclassification typically biases results toward the null, suggesting our findings may underestimate the true risk. Third, although we adjusted for a comprehensive set of covariates, residual confounding—particularly regarding other trace elements in water—cannot be entirely ruled out. Also, because of the nature of this observational study, these findings are hypothesis-generating and require confirmation through mechanistic studies and independent replication.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study found that higher domestic water hardness was associated with an increased risk of incident DR in individuals with T2D, and GID8 emerged as a potential proteomic mediator of this association. These findings generate the hypothesis that the GID8-Wnt signalling axis may represent one biological pathway linking water hardness to DR risk. Further research, including mechanistic studies and replication in independent cohorts, is warranted to confirm these observations and elucidate the precise molecular mechanisms involved.

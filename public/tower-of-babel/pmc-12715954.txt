Analysis of spatial disequilibrium and convergence in China’s agricultural green and low-carbon transformation
Authors: Zhuo He, Pengfei Fu, Ying Meng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
In the context of carbon peaking and neutrality goals, combined with the pursuit of high-quality agricultural economic development, examining the spatial disparities and convergence of agricultural green and low-carbon transformation is critical for protecting the ecological environment and enhancing national agricultural ecological security. This study estimates agricultural carbon emissions across five dimensions: farmland use, rice cultivation, livestock production, farmland soils, and crop residue burning. Using the EBM-GML model, the study measures the agricultural green and low-carbon transformation index for 30 Chinese provinces from 2005 to 2023. The Dagum Gini coefficient, standard deviation ellipses, kernel density estimation, and spatial Durbin models are employed to analyse the spatial disparities and convergence of China’s agricultural green and low-carbon transformation. The findings reveal that the level of agricultural green and low-carbon transformation across provinces and the three major economic zones has increased over time, showing a spatial pattern of “high levels at the periphery and low levels at the centre,” with inter-zonal disparities gradually widening. Regional dynamics in this transformation vary significantly, with northern regions growing faster than southern regions. Nationally, the transformation expanded markedly over the sample period, accompanied by growing divergence within the eastern region. At both the national and major sub-regional levels, the agricultural green and low-carbon transformation shows no δ-convergence but demonstrates absolute and conditional β-convergence. This suggests that although regional divergence in the agricultural green and low-carbon transformation does not consistently decline over time, areas with higher transformation levels experience faster reductions than those with lower levels. Consequently, the gap between the two groups gradually narrows, ultimately converging toward a common steady-state level.

Introduction

Since the Industrial Revolution, the global economy has expanded exponentially, while average global temperatures have risen sharply, posing serious threats and challenges to human living environments [11]. To mitigate the impacts of carbon dioxide emissions on ecosystems and climate change, the international community has adopted agreements including the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change, the Kyoto Protocol, and the Paris Agreement to curb greenhouse gas growth [10, 31]. The United Nations Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change’s Sixth Comprehensive Assessment Report (2023) indicates that, under current carbon dioxide emission trajectories, global temperatures could rise by more than 3 °C by the end of the 21 st century. Curbing carbon dioxide emissions has therefore become a primary responsibility for all nations [25, 36]. Amid China’s accelerated pursuit of its dual-carbon goals and rural revitalisation strategy, green, low-carbon agricultural development has emerged as a key guiding principle for modern rural development [12]. In July 2022, the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs (MARA) and the National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC) jointly issued the Implementation Plan for Carbon Emissions Reduction and Carbon Sequestration in Agricultural. The document emphasises that agricultural emissions reduction and carbon sequestration must be integrated with food security, rural revitalisation, and agricultural and rural modernisation. The green, low-carbon transformation of agriculture represents a crucial pathway for promoting high-quality rural development and achieving the modernisation of national ecological security [34].

As a traditional agricultural powerhouse, China’s agriculture has long served as a vital pillar of the national economy and a key contributor to modernising the nation’s ecological security [24]. However, extensive agricultural production methods, together with externalities such as non-point source pollution and greenhouse gas emissions, pose major challenges to the sector’s green, low-carbon transition, thereby constraining high-quality agricultural economic development [22]. Among these, the Porter Hypothesis argues that the long-term effects of environmental regulations can drive enterprises toward international competitiveness, while emphasising the government’s critical role in balancing economic growth and environmental protection [19]. Consequently, accurately assessing agricultural carbon emissions enables deeper analysis of spatial patterns and dynamic evolutionary trends in the agricultural green and low-carbon transformation [33]. Existing literature identifies five main sources of agricultural carbon emissions: (1) emissions from agricultural inputs such as pesticides, agricultural diesel, fertilisers, and plastic mulch [7]; (2) methane emissions from rice cultivation [5]; (3) nitrous oxide released during crop cultivation [5]; (4) methane from ruminant digestion and fermentation, along with methane and nitrous oxide from manure [16]; and (5) carbon dioxide and other gases released during crop straw burning [14].

From the perspective of its connotation and measurement, agricultural green and low-carbon transformation integrates the dual objectives of “agricultural greening” and “agricultural decarbonisation,” embodying the principles of inclusive growth. It has attracted considerable attention from scholars worldwide [13, 37]. Current academic evaluations of agricultural green and low-carbon transformation generally fall into three categories: single-factor, comprehensive-factor, and composite indices. Single-factor approaches mainly assess agricultural green and low-carbon transformation using carbon emission intensity metrics, such as emissions per unit of output, land area, or grain production [41]. However, these single-factor indices isolate the interrelationships among carbon reduction, green development, and modernisation in agricultural advancement [44]. By contrast, comprehensive and composite indices integrate carbon reduction, green development, and modernisation into a unified measurement framework, thereby broadening perspectives on agricultural green and low-carbon transformation [35]. Thus, while current conceptualisations of agricultural green and low-carbon transformation diverge, all measurement approaches follow fundamental principles reflecting its complexity, systemic nature, and dynamic evolution [26]. Consequently, some scholars use models such as Super-SBM and DEA to evaluate the efficiency of agricultural green and low-carbon transformation [17, 32], whereas others apply methods such as entropy analysis and principal component analysis to construct composite indices [2, 23]. Empirical studies primarily use exploratory spatial analysis to examine the temporal and spatial dynamics of agricultural green and low-carbon transformation efficiency and to investigate its influencing factors [38]. For instance, Huang et al. [15] report that the spatial migration pathway of farmland low-carbon transformation shifts from northeast to southwest, forming a C-shaped pattern with limited expansion. Fu et al. [8] identify agricultural economic development, urbanisation rates, crop structure, fiscal support for agriculture, and mechanisation levels as key determinants of agricultural green and low-carbon efficiency. Nevertheless, these studies have yet to comprehensively and accurately assess agricultural carbon emissions, limiting their ability to reveal the specific capacity of agricultural green and low-carbon transformation.

In summary, although research on agricultural green and low-carbon transformation has increased in recent years, substantial opportunities for further exploration remain in this field. First, discrepancies persist in the accuracy of agricultural carbon emission calculations, and emissions from rice cultivation and livestock farming are often overlooked. Second, agricultural carbon reduction and sequestration should be integrated with food security, rural revitalisation, and the modernisation of agriculture and rural areas. Focusing solely on the evolution of agricultural carbon emissions addresses carbon reduction goals but fails to effectively promote high-quality agricultural economic development. Third, research on the spatial characteristics of agricultural green and low-carbon transformation mainly analyses regional differences and dynamic evolutionary trends, while few studies apply spatial econometric models for convergence analysis.

In light of this, this study first calculated agricultural carbon emissions across five dimensions: land use, rice cultivation, farmland soils, livestock farming, and crop residue burning. Then, assessed the level of agricultural green and low-carbon transformation in 30 Chinese provinces from 2005 to 2023, evaluating progress in agricultural greening, modernisation, efficiency improvements, and low-carbon development. Second, spatial heterogeneity in China’s agricultural green and low-carbon transformation was analysed using the Dagum Gini coefficient, standard deviation ellipse, and kernel density estimation models. This approach provides a comprehensive understanding of regional disparities and dynamic evolutionary pathways in the agricultural green and low-carbon transformation across the nation and its major economic zones. Finally, a spatial Durbin model was constructed to examine convergence characteristics and influencing factors in the agricultural green and low-carbon transformation at the national, three-major-economic-zone, and north–south regional levels. This study makes three key contributions. First, building on existing research, it proposes a novel approach for measuring agricultural carbon emissions across five dimensions—land use, rice cultivation, livestock farming, farmland soils, and crop residue burning—thereby enriching the theoretical foundations for quantifying such emissions. Second, it develops an agricultural green and low-carbon transformation index based on four dimensions—agricultural greening, modernisation, efficiency improvement, and low-carbonisation—offering new perspectives and approaches for advancing high-quality agricultural development. Third, building on conventional panel regression models, the study incorporates spatial weighting matrices to construct spatial Durbin models. This approach enables analysis of the spatial evolution and convergence characteristics of agricultural green and low-carbon transformation across Chinese provinces. The findings offer valuable insights into regional progress in agricultural green and low-carbon transformation, thereby supporting governments in designing tailored agricultural development policies.

The indicator system for agricultural green and low-carbon transformation

Induced technological change theory posits that the direction of technological evolution is determined by relative factor scarcity and market demand. When land is scarce, there is a tendency to adopt biochemical technologies to enhance land productivity, whereas when labour is scarce, the focus shifts to mechanisation to improve labour efficiency. Changes in factor prices direct the orientation of technological progress [1, 29]. Consequently, the green and low-carbon agricultural transition emphasises a shift from extensive development to sustainable, low-carbon practices, moving from “high input, high consumption, high pollution” to “reduced volume, lower emissions, greater efficiency, and improved quality” [21, 27]. Using agricultural total factor productivity (TFP) to assess the efficiency of this green and low-carbon transition effectively captures both regional capital allocation efficiency and environmental impacts. Compared with traditional productivity metrics, TFP comprehensively incorporates the environmental impacts of regional production activities. This reflects a region’s pursuit of high economic growth while minimising the environmental footprint of its development activities. Based on input-output theory, an evaluation framework for agricultural green and low-carbon transformation is constructed, as shown in Table 6. The EBM-GML model is then applied to quantify this transformation. As the GML index primarily reflects the growth rate of agricultural green and low-carbon transformation, which relates with relative changes from the previous year. 2004 is adopted as the base year for calculations. Consequently, agricultural green and low-carbon transformation indices for China’s provinces from 2005 to 2023 have been derived.

Sample selection and data resource

Panel data from 30 Chinese provinces covering the period from 2005 to 2023 were used as the research sample. Relevant data were obtained from the China Statistical Yearbook, China Rural Statistical Yearbook, China Statistical Yearbook for Regional Economic, China Statistical Yearbook on Environment, and provincial statistical yearbooks. Data for other control variables were obtained from the China Statistical Yearbook, China Rural Statistical Yearbook, and the EPS data platform. Missing data were imputed using linear and arithmetic interpolation methods.

This study investigates the spatiotemporal evolution of China’s agricultural green and low-carbon transformation between 2005 and 2023. Analysis of empirical results

Aanalysis of spatial heterogeneity in agricultural green and low-carbon transformation

Quantitative estimates of agricultural green and low-carbon transformation

The EBM-GML model was used to evaluate the efficiency of agricultural green and low-carbon transformation across Chinese provinces. ArcGIS 10.8 software, using the quantile method, generated the spatial distribution map shown in Fig. 1. The results indicate that China’s agricultural green and low-carbon transformation follows a spatial pattern of higher levels in peripheral regions and lower levels in central areas. From 2005 to 2023, the nation’s agricultural green and low-carbon transformation showed a clear overall upward trend. Notably, Heilongjiang and Qinghai experienced rapid growth, rising from relatively low to high levels. This change primarily reflects Heilongjiang’s emphasis on developing circular agriculture. By returning crop straw to fields and reusing livestock manure, the province reduced agricultural waste, promoted resource recovery, and ensured safe waste treatment. Leveraging its unique geographical and climatic conditions, Qinghai vigorously developed ecological animal husbandry and specialised crop cultivation. By optimising its industrial structure, it reduced energy-intensive and polluting agricultural activities. Furthermore, Qinghai strengthened grassland conservation through grazing bans and rotational grazing, which effectively restored vegetation and enhanced grassland ecosystem services. Liaoning and Jilin consistently maintained low or relatively low levels throughout the study period, whereas Ningxia, Shaanxi, and Zhejiang remained at high or relatively high levels. This disparity largely reflects Liaoning and Jilin’s adherence to traditional agricultural models, which involve heavy use of chemical fertilisers and pesticides. Their agricultural waste treatment and resource utilisation remain inadequate, slowing their transition to green and low-carbon agriculture. Conversely, Ningxia, Shaanxi, and Zhejiang have prioritised green and low-carbon principles in agricultural development. They actively promoted ecological and circular agriculture, increased investment in agricultural science and technology, improved resource-use efficiency, and reduced non-point-source agricultural pollution. These efforts have produced higher levels of agricultural green and low-carbon transformation in these regions. As an economically developed region, Zhejiang benefits from strong agricultural fiscal support and meteorological supervision, which provide robust safeguards for its green and low-carbon agricultural transition.

Spatio-temporal clustering of agricultural green and low-carbon transformation

To examine the spatio-temporal clustering of agricultural green and low-carbon transformation across Chinese provinces, kernel density estimation was used to map the location, shape, and spread of kernel density curves nationwide and within the three major economic zones, as shown in Fig. 2. The national kernel density curve shows a clear unimodal pattern without evident polarisation, indicating that China’s provincial transitions remain relatively concentrated. The curve’s shape reveals a gradual decline in peak height, accompanied by a widening peak width. This suggests growing inter-regional disparities in China’s agricultural green and low-carbon transformation, reflecting a more dispersed trend. Moreover, the curve shifted from an approximately normal distribution in 2005 to a right-skewed pattern, displaying a pronounced right tail after 2017. This further underscores the widening regional disparities in China’s agricultural green and low-carbon transformation. Regionally, the western kernel density curve shifted from a single peak to a bimodal distribution, accompanied by a right tail. This indicates pronounced polarisation in the western region’s agricultural green and low-carbon transformation. As shown in the 2023 spatial distribution map in Fig. 1, four western provinces—Qinghai, Ningxia, Chongqing, and Guizhou—are in the high-level zone, whereas Inner Mongolia, Sichuan, Yunnan, and Guangxi fall in the low-level zone. This pattern further highlights the pronounced polarisation. The eastern and central kernel density curves are similar, both showing a marked decline in peak height and a gradual broadening of the main peak. This indicates widening regional disparities in agricultural green and low-carbon transformation across the central and eastern regions. The 2023 spatial distribution map in Fig. 1 shows that both the eastern and central regions contain high- and low-level zones, further exacerbating inter-regional differences.

Spatial variability analysis of agricultural green and low-carbon transformation

To analyse spatial variation in agricultural green and low-carbon transformation across China’s provinces in greater depth, the Dagum Gini coefficient was used to measure intra-group disparities among 30 provinces and three major economic regions. The annual results are shown in Fig. 4. At the national level, China’s overall Gini coefficient for agricultural green and low-carbon transformation remains high and shows a sustained upward trend over the sample period. The intra-group Gini coefficient increased from 0.041 in 2005 to 0.248 in 2023. This indicates significant disparities in the pace of agricultural green and low-carbon transformation across provinces, with these gaps progressively widening. Regional comparisons show that the eastern region’s intra-group Gini coefficient follows a steep upward trend, with unusual fluctuations between 2017 and 2019. This is mainly due to Zhejiang Province’s implementation of the “Thousand Villages Demonstration, Ten Thousand Villages Improvement” initiative in 2017–2018, which improved rural ecological environments and promoted regional agricultural green and low-carbon transformation. The nationwide rollout of central government subsidies in 2018–2019, combined with the achievement of a moderately prosperous society by 2020, effectively narrowed regional disparities within the eastern region. In the central and western regions, agricultural green and low-carbon transformation showed a clear upward trend, increasing from 0.028 to 0.030 in 2005 to 0.219 and 0.238, respectively, by 2023. This indicates that intra-regional disparities in the western region are gradually widening and that polarisation is becoming more pronounced.

Using Dagum’s inter-group Gini coefficient to measure disparities in agricultural green and low-carbon transformation across China’s major economic regions, as shown in Fig. 5, reveals a clear upward trend in regional disparities across all regions over time. The Gini coefficients for the East–Central, East–West, and Central–West regions rose from 0.044, 0.040, and 0.030 in 2005 to 0.227, 0.245, and 0.238 in 2023, representing average annual growth rates of 8.83%, 12.11%, and 12.19%, respectively. Notably, the Central–West Gini coefficient showed the fastest growth, whereas increases in the East–Central and East–West regions were relatively modest. This pattern can be attributed mainly to the following: as a traditional agricultural hub, the central region uses technological advances during digital transformation to upgrade agricultural production processes and enhance value-added potential through innovative service models. By contrast, the western region faces challenges from complex terrain and high digital infrastructure costs, leading to slower progress in agricultural digital transformation. These factors have driven the rising inter-group Gini coefficient between the central and western regions. Consequently, the western region should actively promote policies such as the “East Data, West Computing” initiative and digital inclusive finance to bridge the regional digital divide and reduce disparities in agricultural green and low-carbon transformation across regions.

Findings and suggestions

Findings

This study quantifies agricultural carbon emissions in 30 Chinese provinces from 2005 to 2023, covering five main sources: land use, rice cultivation, soils, livestock farming, and crop residue burning. It also applies the EBM-GML model to evaluate the efficiency of the agricultural green and low-carbon transformation. Kernel density estimation, standard deviation ellipses, the Dagum Gini coefficient, and spatial convergence models are used to examine spatial heterogeneity and convergence in agricultural green and low-carbon transformation levels nationwide and across major sub-regions. The main findings are as follows:

First, regional disparities show that agricultural green and low-carbon transformation increased nationwide and across major sub-regions during the study period, displaying a spatial pattern of “higher levels in peripheral areas and lower levels in central regions.” Intra-regional disparities consistently represented the main source of overall variation in agricultural green and low-carbon transformation. The inter-group disparity between the central and western regions grew fastest, indicating that western regions lag behind central regions in this transformation. Intra-regional analysis shows that western regions recorded the highest growth rate, highlighting significant imbalances. The central regions showed the second-highest intra-group disparity, reflecting substantial internal differences in industrial development. Inter-group hyper-variability density illustrates the uneven spatial evolution of agricultural green and low-carbon transformation across major sub-regions.

Second, from a dynamic perspective, both national and sub-regional agricultural green and low-carbon transformation show upward trends of varying magnitudes, indicating that China’s efforts have achieved measurable progress, although substantial regional disparities remain. The pace of agricultural green and low-carbon transformation in northern China is accelerating faster than in the south, while nationally the transformation shows a clear expansionary trend throughout the study period. Regionally, the peak of the eastern kernel density curve declines while its width expands, indicating increasing dispersion in agricultural green and low-carbon transformation among eastern provinces.

Third, regarding convergence, none of the 30 provinces or the three major economic zones exhibit σ-convergence, yet all show absolute and conditional β-convergence. This suggests that although regional divergence in agricultural green and low-carbon transformation does not decline steadily over time, areas with higher transformation rates show greater reductions than those with lower rates. Consequently, the gap between regions gradually narrows, ultimately converging toward a common steady-state level. Under absolute β-convergence, the eastern economic zone shows the fastest convergence rate, while the western zone is the slowest, exhibiting a weaker catch-up effect. Incorporating conditional β-convergence reveals varying degrees of accelerated convergence across the nation and its major zones. When spatial effects are included in the convergence model, distinct spatial patterns emerge across regions. A significant spatial Durbin effect is observed nationally and within all three major economic zones.

Suggestions

Based on the above conclusions, the following policy recommendations are proposed.

First, develop collaborative governance and differentiated development strategies for agricultural green and low-carbon transformation. Leverage the Belt and Road Initiative and the Yangtze River Economic Belt strategy to strengthen the hub role of central regions and enhance digital technology adoption and innovation in eastern regions. This approach will promote the spread of low-carbon agricultural technologies in western regions through technology transfer and industrial relocation. For example, based on the Yangtze River Delta’s experience in integrated ecological and green development, central provinces could establish agricultural carbon credit trading markets to encourage western regions’ participation in the carbon economy via market mechanisms. Conversely, addressing rapid yet uneven growth within western regions requires differentiated support policies. Fiscal transfers should target ecologically fragile areas such as Qinghai and Gansu, while technological resources should be prioritised for provinces like Sichuan and Yunnan with a stronger foundation for low-carbon agriculture.

Second, establish a balanced framework for agricultural green and low-carbon transformation that combines north–south coordination with internal regional integration. On one hand, differentiated planning schemes should be developed according to regional disparities in agricultural green and low-carbon transformation. Northern regions can leverage the Yellow River Basin ecological conservation strategy to prioritise low-carbon dryland farming technologies, such as fully biodegradable mulch films and water-saving irrigation systems. Meanwhile, southern regions should use ecological compensation mechanisms to guide rice-growing areas toward integrated carbon reduction models that combine methane mitigation in paddies with straw utilisation. To address rising spatial disparities, cross-provincial demonstration zones for agricultural low-carbon cooperation should be established. Mechanisms such as inter-provincial carbon trading and joint R&D on low-carbon technologies should help narrow intra-provincial gaps. To counter polarisation in central and western regions, targeted county-level support plans should be implemented. Use satellite remote sensing to identify lagging counties, and drive dual-engine progress through agricultural low-carbon industrial parks and digital agriculture platforms.

Third, develop region-specific agricultural green and low-carbon transformation plans tailored to local conditions. Within the absolute β-convergence framework, the central region should serve as a policy testing ground, accelerating convergence through dedicated agricultural low-carbon transition funds. The western region requires a tiered carbon tax rebate mechanism that offers tax incentives to early adopters of low-carbon technologies, gradually narrowing the gap with the central region. At the conditional β-convergence level, variables such as agricultural economic development and meteorological monitoring capacity should be incorporated into policy design. For example, establishing a big data platform for agricultural resources can enable precise control of inputs such as fertilisers and pesticides. Finally, establish a spatial spillover compensation mechanism. Using mechanisms such as horizontal ecological compensation, technological spillover effects from leading regions can be converted into tangible benefits for lagging regions.

Limitations and further research

This study examines the spatial equilibrium and convergence of China’s agricultural green and low-carbon transformation across 30 provinces from 2005 to 2023. Although this research makes incremental contributions, it faces three main limitations that suggest future research directions. First, regarding data scope, the study relies solely on provincial-level macro data and lacks micro-level data from prefecture- or county-level cities. This limitation may reduce the precision and granularity of the findings. Future studies could refine the sample by analysing the spatio-temporal characteristics and convergence of agricultural green and low-carbon transformation at the prefecture or county level in China. Second, at the methodological level, although multiple spatial econometric models and convergence techniques were used, there is still room to improve model specification and variable selection. For example, the current model mainly focuses on economic, technological, and resource-related variables, giving limited attention to non-economic factors such as policy institutions and farmer behaviour. Future research could include policy dummy variables, farmer survey data, and other diverse inputs to build a composite analytical framework incorporating institutional and cultural dimensions. Third, in terms of research perspective, this study focuses on domestic regional comparisons and lacks comparative analysis with internationally representative agricultural nations. Subsequent research could adopt a cross-national perspective by constructing comparative samples that include both developing and developed countries. This approach would reveal the unique pathways and universal principles of China’s agricultural green and low-carbon transformation, providing insights that could inform global sustainable agricultural development.

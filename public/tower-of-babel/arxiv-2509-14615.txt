Small Value of cohomological dimension of group homomorphisms
Nursultan Kuanyshov

Abstract
. We give the characterisation of cohomological dimension of group homomorphisms. In particular, we show the analogue of the Stallings-Swan theorem in the case of group epimorphisms.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 20J06; Secondary 50M30

1.
Introduction

The concept of cohomological dimension
\mathrm{cd}(\Gamma)
of a discrete group
\Gamma
dates back to the mid-20th century and has played a central role in algebraic topology and geometric group theory. Foundational results in the subject are collected in the classical works of Bieri
[
Bi
]
and Brown
[
Br
]
.

The
cohomological dimension of a group homomorphism

\phi:\Gamma\to\Lambda
, denoted
\mathrm{cd}(\phi)
, was first proposed by Mark Grant in a 2013 MathOverflow discussion
[
Gr
]
. While this notion naturally generalizes the classical cohomological dimension of groups, it remained largely unexplored until recent developments. Partial results appeared in
[
Sc
,
DK
]
, but a systematic framework was provided only recently by Dranishnikov and De Saha
[
DD
]
, who introduced homological and geometric tools suitable for studying
\operatorname{cd}(\phi)
.

In the group case, small values of cohomological dimension are strongly tied to structural properties. The Stallings-Swan theorem characterizes groups of cohomological dimension one as free, while the Eilenberg-Ganea theorem relates cohomological and geometric dimension, with the exceptional case
\operatorname{cd}(\Gamma)=2
forming the well-known Eilenberg-Ganea conjecture. However, both results fail in the homomorphism setting: joint work with Dranishnikov
[
DK
]
constructed epimorphisms
\phi:\Gamma\to\Lambda
with
\mathrm{cd}(\phi)=1
and
\mathrm{gd}(\phi)=2
, and with
\mathrm{cd}(\phi)=2
and
\mathrm{gd}(\phi)=3
. In
[
DD
]
, it is shown that for any
n\geq 2
, there exist homomorphisms with
\mathrm{cd}(\phi)=n
and
\mathrm{gd}(\phi)=n+1
, establishing that the divergence between cohomological and geometric dimensions persists at all levels by 1.

An important distinction between the classical and homomorphism settings is the behavior of groups with finite elements. For those groups,
\mathrm{cd}(\Gamma)=\infty
, necessitating the use of
virtual cohomological dimension
to study them. In contrast, homomorphisms between finite groups can have
finite
cohomological dimension. In this paper, we give explicit examples of such homomorphisms. As a result, there is no need to define the virtual cohomological dimension for group homomorphisms, marking a conceptual simplification relative to classical group cohomology.

Despite the failure of classical classification theorems, the study of small cohomological dimensions for homomorphisms still leads to rigidity and structural results. In this paper, we focus on
group epimorphisms

\phi:\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow\Lambda
between groups, and we prove the following two theorems:

Theorem 1.3

Let
\phi:\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow\Lambda
be a group epimorphism. Then

\operatorname{cd}(\phi)=0\,\,\text{if and only if}\,\,\phi\,\,\text{is the trivial homomorphism.}

This result confirms that cohomological dimension zero imposes strong rigidity in the homomorphism setting, mirroring the classical case where
\mathrm{cd}(\Gamma)=0
if and only if
\Gamma
is trivial.

Our second main result addresses the next level,
\mathrm{cd}(\phi)=1
, and connects it with the
Lusternik-Schnirelmann category of a homomorphism
, denoted
\mathrm{cat}(\phi)
, as introduced in
[
DK
]
.

Theorem 2.

Let
\phi:\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow\Lambda
be a group epimorphism. Then

\operatorname{cat}(\phi)=\operatorname{cd}(\phi)=1\,\,\text{if and only if}\,\,\phi\,\,\text{factors through the free groups}

\text{via epimorphisms}\,\,\Gamma\to F_{n}\to\Lambda.

This theorem can be seen as a homomorphism-level analogue of the Stallings-Swan theorem. It shows that the condition
\operatorname{cat}(\phi)=\operatorname{cd}(\phi)=1
captures a certain freeness - not necessarily of the group
\Gamma
itself, but of the
factorization
of the map
\phi
through a free object in the category of groups.

Our methods combine several tools: the
Berstein-Schwarz class
is developed by Dranishnikov and Rudyak
[
DR
]
; the
projective resolution
constructed by Dranishnikov and De Saha
[
DD
]
; and classical cohomological techniques from Brown’s book
[
Br
]
, particularly extension theory and dimension shifting.

In the paper, we use the notation
H^{*}(\Gamma,A)
for the cohomology of a group
\Gamma
with coefficient in
\Gamma
-module
A
. The cohomology groups of a space
X
with the fundamental group
\Gamma
we denote as
H^{*}(X;A)
. Thus,
H^{*}(\Gamma,A)=H^{*}(B\Gamma;A)
where
B\Gamma=K(\Gamma,1)
, a classifying space (Eilenberg-MacLane space): a path-connected space such that
\pi_{1}(B\Gamma)=\Gamma
and
\pi_{k}(B\Gamma)=0
for all
k\neq 1.

Preliminaries

We recall our tools and classical numerical invariants such as Lusternik-Schnirelmann category, topological complexity, and sequential topological complexity in this section.

2.1.
Berstein-Schwarz cohomology class

The Berstein-Schwarz class of a discrete group
\Gamma
is the first obstruction
\beta_{\Gamma}
to a lift of
B\Gamma=K(\Gamma,1)
to the universal covering
E\Gamma
.
Note that
\beta_{\Gamma}\in H^{1}(\Gamma,I(\Gamma))
where
I(\Gamma)
is the augmentation ideal of the group ring
{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma

[
Be
]
,
[
DR
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Universality
[
DR
,
Sch
]
)
.

For any cohomology class
\alpha\in H^{k}(\Gamma,L)
, there is a homomorphism of
\Gamma
-modules
I(\Gamma)^{\otimes k}\to L
such that the induced homomorphism for cohomology takes
(\beta_{\Gamma})^{k}\in H^{k}(\Gamma,I(\Gamma)^{\otimes k})
to

I(\Gamma)^{\otimes k}=I(\Gamma)\otimes\dots\otimes I(\Gamma)
and
(\beta_{\Gamma})^{k}=\beta_{\Gamma}\smile\dots\smile\beta_{\Gamma}
.

2.2.
Projective resolution of Dranishnikov and De Saha
[
DD
]

Let
\Gamma
be a discrete group. A projective resolution
P_{*}(\Gamma)
of
{\mathbb{Z}}
for the group
\Gamma
is an exact sequence of projective
\Gamma-
modules over
{\mathbb{Z}}
:

\cdots\to P_{k}(\Gamma)\to P_{k-1}(\Gamma)\to\cdots P_{2}(\Gamma)\to P_{1}\to{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma\to{\mathbb{Z}}.

The cohomology of
\Gamma
with the coefficients in a
\Gamma
-module M can be defined as homology of the cochain complexe
Hom_{\Gamma}(P_{*}(\Gamma),M)
.

We recall the
cohomological dimension

\operatorname{cd}(\phi)
of a group homomorphism
\phi:\Gamma\to\Lambda
was introduced by Mark Grant
[

]
as the maximum of
k
such that
there is a
\Lambda-
module
M
with the nonzero induced homomorphism
\phi^{*}:H^{k}(\Lambda,M)\to H^{k}(\Gamma,M)
.

The following Theorem of Dranishnikov and De Saha gives the characterization of group homomorphism in term of the projective resolutions.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
DD
]
)
.

Let
\phi:\Gamma\to\Lambda
be a group homomorphism with
\operatorname{cd}(\phi)=k
and let
\phi_{*}:(P_{*}(\Gamma),\partial_{*})\to(P_{*}(\Gamma),\partial^{\prime}_{*})
be the chain map between projective resolutions of
{\mathbb{Z}}
for
\Gamma
and
\Lambda
induced by
\phi
. Then
\phi_{*}
is chain homotopic to
\psi_{*}:P_{*}(\Gamma)\to P_{*}(\Lambda)
with
\psi_{n}=0
for
n>k.

2.3.
LS-category and Sequential Topological Complexity

Let
f:X\to Y
be a map. Let
X^{r}
and
Y^{r}
be the Cartesian product of
r
copies of
X
and
Y
respectively, i.e
X^{r}:=X\times\cdots\times X
and
Y^{r}:=Y\times\cdots\times Y.
Let us denote
f^{r}:=f\times\cdots\times f:X^{r}\to Y^{r}
and elements of
X^{r}
and
Y^{r}
are vectors
\bar{x}=(x_{0},\cdots,x_{r-1})
and
\bar{y}=(y_{0},\cdots,y_{r-1})
respectively.

Definition 1
.

The Lusternik-Schnirelmann category of the map f,
\operatorname{cat}(f)
, between X and Y topological spaces is the minimal number k such that X admits an open cover by k+1 open sets
U_{0},U_{1},...,U_{k}
and restriction of f to each
U_{i}
is null-homotopic.

Definition 2
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be the above map.

(1)

A sequential
f
-motion planner on a subset
U\subset X^{r}
is a map
f_{U}:U\to PY
such that
f_{U}(\bar{x})(\frac{j}{r-1})=f_{U}(x_{0},x_{1},\cdots,x_{r-1})(\frac{j}{r-1})=f(x_{j})
for all
j=0,\cdots,r-1.

(2)

The sequential topological complexity of map f, denoted
TC_{r}(f),
is the minimal number
k
such that
X^{r}
is covered by
k+1
open sets
U_{0},\cdots,U_{k}
on which there are sequential
f
-motion planners. If no such
k
exists, we set
TC_{r}(f)=\infty.

Note that if
r=2,
we recover Scott’s topological complexity for a map
[
Sc
]
. Further, if map
f
is identity on space
X,
we get Rudyak’s sequential topological complexity of space
X

[
Ru
]
.

Due to the homotopy invariance of classifying spaces, there is a one-to-one correspondence between group homomorphism
\phi:\Gamma\to\Lambda
and homotopy classes of maps
B\phi:B\Gamma\to B\Lambda
that induce
\phi
on the fundamental group. Hence, the following definition is well-defined and equivalent (see more details in
[
Dr
]
)

Definition 3
.

Let
\phi:\Gamma\to\Lambda
be a homomorphism.

(1)

The LS-category of
\phi
,
\operatorname{cat}(\phi),
is defined to be
\operatorname{cat}(B\phi).

(2)

The geometric dimension
gd(\phi)
of
\phi
is defined as the minimal
k
such that
B\phi
can be deformed to the k-skeleton
B\Lambda^{(k)}
:

gd(\phi)=\min\{n|B\phi\sim f:B\Gamma\to B\Lambda^{(k)}\}

2.4.
Ganea-Schwarz’s approach to LS-category

Recall that an element of an iterated join
X_{0}*X_{1}*\cdots*X_{k}
of topological spaces is a formal linear combination
t_{0}x_{0}+\cdots+t_{k}{x_{k}}
of points
x_{i}\in X_{i}
with
\sum t_{i}=1
,
t_{i}\geq 0
, in which all terms of the form
0x_{i}
are dropped. Given fibrations
f_{i}:X_{i}\to Y
for
i=0,...,k
, the fiberwise join of spaces
X_{0},...,X_{k}
is defined to be the space

X_{0}*_{Y}X_{1}*_{Y}\cdots*_{Y}X_{k}=\{\ t_{0}x_{0}+\cdots+t_{k}{x_{k}}\in X_{0}*\cdots*X_{k}\ |\ f_{0}(x_{0})=\cdots=f_{k}(x_{k})\ \}.

The fiberwise join of fibrations
f_{0},...,f_{n}
is the fibration

f_{0}*_{Y}*\cdots*_{Y}f_{k}:X_{0}*_{Y}X_{1}*_{Y}\cdots*_{Y}X_{k}\longrightarrow Y

defined by taking a point
t_{0}x_{0}+\cdots+t_{k}{x_{k}}
to
f_{i}(x_{i})
for any
i
such that
t_{i}\neq 0
.

When
X_{i}=X
and
f_{i}=f:X\to Y
for all
i
the fiberwise join of spaces is denoted by
*^{k+1}_{Y}X
and the fiberwise join of fibrations is denoted by
*_{Y}^{k+1}f
.

For a path connected space X, we turn an inclusion of a point
i:x_{0}\to X
into a fibration
p_{0}^{X}:G_{0}(X)\to X
, whose fiber is known to be the loop space
\Omega X
. The n-th Ganea space of X is
defined to be the space
G_{k}(X)=*_{X}^{k+1}G_{0}(X)
, while the k-th Ganea fibration
p_{k}^{X}:G_{k}(X)\to X
is the fiberwise join
*_{X}^{k+1}p_{0}^{X}
. Then the fiber of
p_{0}^{X}
is
*^{k+1}\Omega X
.

The following theorem give the Ganea-Shwarz characterization of LS-category of maps
[
Sc
,
Sch
,
DK
,
CLOT
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

If
f:X\to Y
is a map between connected normal spaces, then
\operatorname{cat}(f)\leq k
if and only if
there is a lift of f with respect to the fibration
p_{k}^{Y}:G_{k}(Y)\to Y
admits a section.

3.
Proof of Main Results

Given a homomorphism
\phi:\Gamma\to\Lambda
by
\phi^{\prime}:\Gamma\to\operatorname{im}(\phi)
we denote the restriction of
\phi
from the codomain to its range. Joint work with Dranishnikov
[
DK
]
established the following result, which tells it suffices to consider an epimorphisms instead of all homomorphisms.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
DK
]

For any homomorphism
\phi:\Gamma\to\Lambda
,

\operatorname{cat}(\phi)=\operatorname{cat}(\phi)

\operatorname{cd}(\phi)=\operatorname{cd}(\phi)

By Theorem
3.1
, we assume that group homomorphism
\phi:\Gamma\to\Lambda
is an epimorphism. We get the following characterization of group epimorphism.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\phi:\Gamma\to\Lambda
be an epimorphism of finitely generated groups
\Gamma,\Lambda
. Then
\operatorname{cd}(\phi)=0
if and only if
\phi
is the trivial homomorphism.

Proof.

Suppose
\phi
is the trivial homomorphism.
\Lambda=0
since
\phi
is surjective. The trivial group has its classifying space homotopy equivalent to the point, so all reduced cohomology is zero. Hence, we obtain that
\phi^{*}:\tilde{H^{*}}(B\Lambda;M)\to\tilde{H^{*}}(B\Gamma;M)
is the trivial homomorphism for all
\Lambda-
modules M. By definition, this gives us that
\operatorname{cd}(\phi)=0.

Note that
P_{*}(\Gamma)
,
{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma,
and
I(\Gamma)
are
\Gamma-
modules, but also
\Lambda-
modules via
\phi.

The Berstein-Schwarz class of
\Lambda
,
\beta_{\Lambda}\in H^{1}(B\Lambda;I(\Lambda))
, is given by the cocycle
f_{\Lambda}:P_{1}(\Lambda)\to I(\Lambda),f_{\Lambda}(c^{\prime})=\partial^{\prime}_{1}(c^{\prime})
where
I(\Lambda)
is the augmentation ideal of
{\mathbb{Z}}\Lambda
.

By contradiction, assume
\phi
is not trivial epimorphism. Since
\phi_{*}
is nonzero surjective, for each
c^{\prime}\in P_{1}(\Lambda)
, there exists
c\in P_{1}(\Gamma)
with
c^{\prime}=\phi(c).
Since the above diagram is commutative, we get the nonzero cocycle
f_{\Lambda}\phi_{1}\in Hom_{\Lambda}(P_{1}(\Gamma),I(\Lambda))
. Hence, this implies
\phi^{*}:H^{1}(B\Lambda;I(\Lambda))\to H^{1}(B\Gamma;I(\Lambda))

is not trivial, i.e
\phi^{*}(\beta_{\Lambda})\neq 0.
In other words, the cohomological class
\phi^{*}(\beta{\Lambda})
is given by a nonzero cocycle
f_{\Lambda}\phi_{1}.
This contradicts
\operatorname{cd}(\phi)=0.
This implies
\Gamma
is the trivial group, so
\phi
is the trivial homomorphism.
∎

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
\phi:F_{n}\to F_{m}
be an epimorphism between free groups with generators
n
and
m
, respectively. Then
\operatorname{cat}(\phi)=\operatorname{cd}(\phi)=1
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.2
, it is clear that
\operatorname{cd}(\phi)\geq 1
since
F_{m}
is not trivial.
Since
\operatorname{cd}(\phi)\leq\operatorname{cat}(\phi)\leq\operatorname{cat}(F_{n})=1,
we obtain that
\operatorname{cat}(\phi)=\operatorname{cd}(\phi)=1.

∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\phi:\Gamma\to\Lambda
be an epimorphism of the groups. Then

\operatorname{cat}(\phi)=\operatorname{cd}(\phi)=1

if and only if
\phi
factors through the free groups via epimorphisms
\Gamma\to F_{n}\to\Lambda.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.2
, we always obtain
\operatorname{cd}(\phi)\geq 1
since
\Lambda
is not the trivial group.

Now we suppose the epimorphism
\phi:\Gamma\to\Lambda
factors through the free groups via epimorphisms
\Gamma\to F_{n}\to\Lambda.
Since
\operatorname{cd}(\phi)\leq\operatorname{cat}(\phi)
(see
[
DK
]
), it suffices to show
\operatorname{cat}(\phi)\leq 1.

This easily follows from the classical LS-category. Indeed,

\operatorname{cat}(\phi)\leq\min\{\operatorname{cat}(h),\operatorname{cat}(g)\}\leq\operatorname{cat}(F_{n})=1,

where
h:B\Gamma\to BF_{n}
and
g:BF_{n}\to B\Lambda
are maps that induced epimorphisms
h_{*}:\Gamma\to F_{n}
and
g_{*}:F_{n}\to\Lambda.

Now conversely
\operatorname{cat}(\phi)=\operatorname{cd}(\phi)=1,
then using the Ganea-Schwarz approach to LS-category of induced map
B\phi
, namely by Theorem
2.3
we obtain that the induced map
B\phi:B\Gamma\to B\Lambda
can be lifted to the Ganea’s space
G_{1}(B\Lambda).
Note that
G_{1}(B\Lambda)
is homotopy equivalent to 1-dimensional complex
\Sigma\Lambda
, the reduced suspension of
\Lambda
[
[
CLOT
]
, Example 1.61, page 27]. Then the epimorphism
\phi
factors through a free group
F_{n}
via epimorphisms
\Gamma\to F_{n}\to\Lambda.

∎

Theorem 3.4
.

For an epimorphism
\phi:\mathbb{Z}_{p^{4}}\to\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
,

\operatorname{cd}(\phi)=2\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \operatorname{cat}(\phi)=\infty.

Proof.

The proof of the inequality
\operatorname{cd}(\phi)\leq 2
is based on the following observation:
There is a degree 0 map

f:L^{3}_{p^{4}}\to L^{3}_{p^{2}}

of lens spaces that induces an epimorphism
f_{*}:\mathbb{Z}_{p^{4}}\to\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
of the fundamental groups.

Here is the construction of
f
.
We consider two circles
S^{1}
with
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{4}}
free actions.
Note that a
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{4}}
-equivariant map
\psi:S^{1}\to S^{1}
between them has degree
p^{2}
. Then the join product
\psi\ast\psi:S^{1}\ast S^{1}\to S^{1}\ast S^{1}
has degree
p^{4}
.
Hence it induces a map of the orbit spaces
\bar{\psi}:L^{3}_{p^{4}}\to L^{3}_{p^{2}}
of degree
p^{2}
. Let
q_{k}:S^{3}\to L_{p^{k}}^{3}
denote the projection onto the orbit space, were k=2,4.

We define
f
as the composition

L^{3}_{p^{4}}=L^{3}_{p^{4}}\#S^{3}\to L^{3}_{p^{4}}\vee S^{3}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1\vee r}}{{\to}}L^{3}_{p^{4}}\vee S^{3}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bar{\psi}\vee q_{2}}}{{\to}}L^{3}_{p^{2}}\vee L^{3}_{p^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j}}{{\to}}L^{3}_{p^{2}}

where
r:S^{3}\to S^{3}
has degree -1 and
j
identifies two copies of
L^{3}_{p^{2}}
.
Thus,
deg(f)=p^{2}-p^{2}=0
.

Claim 1. The induced homomorphism
f^{*}:H^{3}(L^{3}_{p^{2}};M)\to H^{3}(L^{3}_{p^{4}};M)
is trivial for any
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
-module
M
.

Indeed, if
f^{*}(\alpha)\neq 0
for
\alpha\in H^{3}(L_{p^{2}}^{3};M)
then by the Poincare duality with local coefficients
[L^{3}_{p^{4}}]\cap f^{*}(\alpha)\neq 0
. Since
f
induces an epimorphism of the fundamental groups, the induced homomorphism for 0-homology
f_{*}:H_{0}(L^{3}_{p^{4}};M)\to H_{0}(L^{3}_{p^{2}};M)
is an isomorphism. We obtain a contradiction

0\neq f_{*}([L^{3}_{p^{4}}]\cap f^{*}(\alpha))=f_{*}([L^{3}_{p^{4}}])\cap\alpha=0.

The map
f
extends to a map
\phi:L^{\infty}_{p^{4}}\to L^{\infty}_{p^{2}}
.

Claim 2. The induced homomorphism
\phi^{*}:H^{3}(L^{\infty}_{p^{2}};M)\to H^{3}(L^{\infty}_{p^{4}};M)
is trivial for any
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
-module
M
.

This follows from the fact that the inclusion homomorphism
H^{k}(X;M)\to H^{k}((X^{(k)};M)
is injective for any CW-complex
X
and a
\pi_{1}(X)
-module
M
.

The shift of dimension for group cohomology
[
Br
]
implies that

\phi^{*}:H^{k}(L^{\infty}_{p^{2}};M)\to H^{k}(L^{\infty}_{p^{4}};M)

is trivial for all
k\geq 3
.
Thus,
\operatorname{cd}(\phi)\leq 2
. Using integral cohomology one can check that
\operatorname{cd}(\phi)\geq 2
.

Next we show that
\operatorname{cat}(\phi)=\infty
. This follows from the fact that the reduced K-theory cup-length of
\phi
is unbounded. By the Atiyah’s computations
[
AS
]
,

K(B\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}})=\mathbb{Z}[[\eta_{k}]]/(\eta_{k}^{p^{k}}-1)

where
\eta_{k}
is the pull-back of the canonical line bundle
\eta
over
\mathbb{C}P^{\infty}
under the inclusion

\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}\to S^{1}
and
\mathbb{Z}[[x]]
is the ring of formal series. The reduced K-theory of
B\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}
is the subring generated by
\eta_{k}-1
.
The induced homomorphism

\phi^{*}:K(B\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}})\to K(B\mathbb{Z}_{p^{4}})

takes
\eta_{2}
to
\eta_{4}^{p^{2}}
. Then it takes
(\eta_{2}-1)^{m}
to

(\eta_{4}^{p^{2}}-1)^{m}
. To see that
(\eta_{4}^{p^{2}}-1)^{m}\neq 0
in
\mathbb{Z}[[\eta_{4}]]/(\eta_{4}^{p^{4}}-1)
for all
m
it suffices to show that it is not zero in

\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[\eta_{4}]]/(\eta_{4}^{p^{4}}-1)
for
m=p^{2}k+1
. Note that

(\eta_{4}^{p^{2}}-1)^{p^{2}k+1}=\eta_{4}^{p^{2}}-1\neq 0

in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[\eta_{4}]]/(\eta_{4}^{p^{4}}-1)
.
This implies
\operatorname{cat}(\phi)\geq m
for any
m
(see Proposition 5.1 in
[
Dr
]
).
∎

4.
Application of Main Results

Due to the homotopy invariance of classifying spaces, there is a one-to-one correspondence between group homomorphism
\phi:\Gamma\to\Lambda
and homotopy classes of maps
B\phi:B\Gamma\to B\Lambda
that induce
\phi
on the fundamental group. Hence, the following definition is well-defined. By Theorem
3.1
, we assume
\phi
is an epimorphisms in this section.

Definition 4
.

Let
\phi:\Gamma\to\Lambda
be a homomorphism.

(1)

The LS-category of
\phi
,
\operatorname{cat}(\phi),
is defined to be
\operatorname{cat}(B\phi).

(2)

The sequential topological complexity of
\phi
,
TC_{r}(\phi),
is defined to be
TC_{r}(B\phi).

By Theorem
3.4
, we can see that the gap between the LS-category and the cohomological dimension of group homomorphisms can be arbitrarily large. This theorem answers the question of
[
Ku3
]
partially in the case of finite groups. This is because the previous known gaps were 1 (see
[
DK
,
DD
,
Gr
]
).

Furthermore, by Theorem
3.4
we have the group epimorphism that does not satisfy
TC_{r}(\phi)\leq(r+1)\operatorname{cd}(\phi)
since
\infty=\operatorname{cat}(\phi)\leq TC_{r}(\phi)
and
(r+1)\operatorname{cd}(\phi)=2(r+1)
. Thus, there are no analogue results of Rudyak
[
Ru
]
result,

r\operatorname{cd}(\Gamma)\leq TC_{r}(\Gamma)\leq(r+1)\operatorname{cd}(\Gamma),

in the context of group homomorphisms.

Furthermore, Dranishnikov and Jauhari
[
DJ
]
introduced distributional topological complexity and LS-category for topological spaces and maps, denoted as
\operatorname{dcat}
for LS-category and
\operatorname{dTC}
for topological complexity. They proved that distributional invariants are lower bound for classical ones, namely LS-category and topological complexity.

Since cohomological dimensions are also lower bound for LS-category, the natural question is if cohomological dimension and distributional LS-category are the same or not. In here, we gave the answer saying these two numerical invariants are not comparable.

For example, take p=2, we get
\operatorname{cd}(\phi)=2
since the proof of Theorem
3.4
works for this epimorphism
\phi:{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{2}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
as well while
\operatorname{dcat}(\phi)=1
since
\operatorname{dcat}({\mathbb{Z}}_{2})=1
(see more details
[
DJ
]
). Other case, take covering map
p:S^{n}\to RP^{n}
from n-dimensional sphere to n-dimensional projective space. Since the map
p
is not null-homotopic, we get
\operatorname{dcat}(p)=1
while
\operatorname{cd}(p_{*})=0
since
S^{n}
is the simply-connected for
n\geq 2.

An analogue of the Eilenberg-Ganea result,
\operatorname{cat}(\Gamma)=\operatorname{cd}(\Gamma)
, in the setting of group homomorphisms fails in general, but also holds for many cases of finitely generated, torsion-free groups such as free, Abelian, nilpotent, virtually nilpotent, and almost nilpotent groups (see
[
Sc
,
Ku1
,
Ku2
,
Ku3
]
). Extending these lists for classical groups is still a difficult task since each above results rely on special property of each group.

By Theorem
3.2
and Theorem
3.3
, we can extend lists of groups where the analogue of the Eilenberg-Ganea result holds.

Theorem 4.1
.

Given an epimorphism
\phi:\Gamma\to\Lambda
between finitely generated, one relator groups
\Gamma,\Lambda
. Then
\operatorname{cat}(\phi)=\operatorname{cd}(\phi)

Proof.

It is well known that cohomological dimension of one relator groups are at most 2 (see
[
Br
]
). Indeed, it is two because its classifying space is 2-dimensional complex. By Theorem
3.2
,
\operatorname{cd}(\phi)\geq 1
since
\phi
is nonzero epimorphisms. Thus, there are two cases:

Case 1: If given the epimorphism factor through free groups, then by Theorem
3.3
we get
\operatorname{cat}(\phi)=\operatorname{cd}(\phi)=1
, which confirms the analogue of the Eilenberg-Ganea result.

Case 2: Otherwise,
\operatorname{cd}(\phi)\geq 2
since
\phi
does not factor through via free groups. Since
\operatorname{cd}(\Gamma)=2
, we get that
\operatorname{cat}(\phi)\leq 2
. Therefore, combining two inequalities we get
\operatorname{cat}(\phi)=\operatorname{cd}(\phi)=2
, which again confirms the anologue result of Eilenberg-Ganea.

∎

Corollary 4.1
.

Given an epimorphism
\phi:\Gamma\to\Lambda
of the fundamental groups of orientable closed surfaces M, N, i.e
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(M),\Lambda=\pi_{1}(N)
Then
\operatorname{cat}(\phi)=\operatorname{cd}(\phi)

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
4.1
since the fundamental groups of orientable closed surfaces are one relator groups.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Given an epimorphism
\phi:\Gamma\to\Lambda
of Baumslag-Solitar groups.
Then
\operatorname{cat}(\phi)=\operatorname{cd}(\phi)

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
4.1
since the Baumslag-Solitar groups are one relator groups.
∎

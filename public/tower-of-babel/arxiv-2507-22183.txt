Convergence of the fractional Yamabe flow for arbitrary initial energy
Jingeon An, Hardy Chan, Pak Tung Ho

Abstract
. Since the seminal paper of Graham and Zworski (Invent. Math. 2003), conformal geometric problems are studied in the fractional setting. We consider the convergence of fractional Yamabe flow, which is previously known under small initial energy assumption. Inspired by the deep work of Brendle (J. Diff. Geom. 2005), we obtain the full convergence result for arbitrary initial energy, whenever the (fractional) positive mass conjecture is valid.

Primary 35K55; Secondary 35B40

1.
Introduction

As one of the landmarks in geometric analysis, the problem of finding a metric in a given conformal class of a closed manifold with constant scalar curvature is known as the Yamabe problem (see
[
1
,
23
,
25
,
26
]
). Later in his seminal paper, Hamilton
[
16
]
introduced the parabolic version of the Yamabe problem, now called Yamabe flow: For any compact Riemannian manifold
(M,g_{0})
of dimension
n\geq 2
, consider the following evolution equation for the metric
g(t)
such that

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}g&=-(R^{g}-s^{g})g\\
g(0)&=g_{0},\end{cases}

where
R^{g}
denotes the scalar curvature of
g
, and
s^{g}
is the averaged scalar curvature, i.e.

s^{g}:=\frac{1}{\mu_{g}(M)}\int_{M}R^{g}\,d\mu_{g}.

It is possible to rewrite (
1.1
) as a parabolic PDE in the conformal factor, under the conformal change

g=u^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g_{0}.

Then, in terms of the conformal Laplacian
P^{g_{0}}
, (
1.1
) is equivalent to a fast diffusion equation

\frac{n-2}{n+2}\partial_{t}\left(u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\right)=-P^{g_{0}}u+s^{g}u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}.

In this formulation, the Yamabe flow resulted in an extensive amount of literature in geometric analysis, see, e.g.
[
2
,
3
,
7
,
24
,
27
]
.

In a seminal paper
[
14
]
, Graham and Zworski introduced a one-parameter family of conformal fractional Laplacians
P_{\gamma}^{g}
, of order
2\gamma\in(0,n)
, defined on the conformal infinity of a Poincaré–Einstein manifold. Remarkably, these operators coincide with the GJMS operators
[
15
]
for integer values of
\gamma
. This also includes the fractional Laplacian as a special case, when the ambient manifold is the hyperbolic space. When
\gamma\in(0,1)
, an extension problem is formulated by Chang and González
[
6
]
, analogous to the one posed by Caffarelli–Silvestre
[
4
]
in the Euclidean setting.

Correspondingly, one defines an interpolated curvature quantity
R_{\gamma}^{g}:=P_{\gamma}^{g}(1)
for each
\gamma\in(0,n/2)
, which is just scalar curvature for
\gamma=1
, and the
Q
-curvature for
\gamma=2
. This interpolated notion of curvature is studied in
[
6
,
9
,
13
,
17
,
22
,
19
,
20
]
.

Then, one naturally considers the fractional Yamabe flow, namely (
1.1
) but equipped with this generalized notion of curvature.
Recently, the convergence of the fractional Yamabe flow is one of the main interests and studied in
[
17
,
8
,
5
]
. As we expect from the classical theory, it is known by Chan, Sire, and Sun
[
5
]
that the fractional Yamabe flow converges strongly, but the argument in
[
5
]
proves the convergence only under a small energy assumption on the initial metric
g_{0}
. The aim of this paper is to remove this initial energy assumption and prove the strong convergence for arbitrary initial energy, as in the classical case treated by Brendle
[
2
]
.

The main difficulty of generalizing Brendle’s strategy
[
2
]
to the fractional case is, of course, nonlocality, because of which one cannot derive certain important estimates from the pointwise product rule as in the local case
\gamma=1
. One needs to resort to the manifold in extension where, unfortunately, two versions of Schoen’s bubble (i.e. Aubin–Talenti’s bubble glued to Green’s function) arise. More precisely, delicate estimates are required to compare the “glue-and-extend” and “extend-and-glue” versions. In this direction, Mayer and Ndiaye
[
19
,
20
]
recently made an important contribution by deriving asymptotics of Poisson’s kernel for fractional conformal Laplacians and used them to prove the existence of the fractional Yamabe problem. Building on their work, we prove a novel and crucial pointwise gradient estimate (in order to obtain (
3.10
)). Collecting all the ingredients leads to the final resolution of the expected convergence theory of the fractional Yamabe flow.

We now introduce the flow under study. Suppose
M
is the conformal infinity of a
Poincaré-Einstein manifold
(X,g_{+})
. Moreover, we let
\gamma\in(0,1)\subset(0,n/2)
, and define the fractional Yamabe functional

(1.2)

E(u):=\frac{\int_{M}uP_{\gamma}^{g_{0}}u\,d\mu_{g_{0}}}{\left(\int_{M}u^{\frac{2n}{n-2\gamma}}d\mu_{g_{0}}\right)^{\frac{n-2\gamma}{n}}},

and fractional Yamabe energy

(1.3)

Y_{\gamma}(M,[g_{0}])=\inf_{0\neq u\in H^{\gamma}(M)}E(u),

where
P_{\gamma}^{g_{0}}
is the conformal fractional Laplacian (for the precise definition, see Section
2
and
[
5
, Section 2]
). Recall that the conformal fractional Laplacian satisfies

P_{\gamma}^{g_{0}}(uf)=u^{\frac{n+2\gamma}{n-2\gamma}}P_{\gamma}^{g}(f),\quad\forall f\in C^{\infty}(M),

under the conformal change

(1.4)

g=u^{\frac{4}{n-2\gamma}}g_{0}.

In particular, on usual Euclidean metric
(\mathbb{R}^{n},|dx|^{2})
, we have
P_{\gamma}^{|dx|^{2}}=(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}})^{\gamma}
.

The volume element on
(M,g_{0})
is denoted by
d\mu_{g_{0}}
. By replacing
g_{0}
by its constant multiple we may assume the
(M,g_{0})
has unit volume, that is to say,
\mu_{g_{0}}(M)=1
. With a conformal metric (
1.4
), we write

d\mu_{g}=u^{\frac{2n}{n-2\gamma}}d\mu_{g_{0}}.

Assume
Y_{\gamma}(M,[g_{0}])>0
and
\lambda_{1}(g_{+})\geq\frac{n^{2}}{2}-\gamma^{2}
.
Then for each
x\in M
, there exists a unique positive Green’s function
G(\cdot,x)

on
\overline{X}\setminus\{x\}
, for the conformal fractional Laplacian
P_{\gamma}^{g}
(see
[
18
, Proposition 1.5]
). Then we have the following Positive Mass conjecture, stated for fractional case:

Conjecture 1.1

(Positive Mass Conjecture)
.

Assume that
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
n>2\gamma

Suppose that
Y_{\gamma}(M,[g_{0}])>0
. Fix any
y\in M
. Then there exists a small neighborhood of
y
in
(\overline{X},\bar{g})
, which is diffeomorphic to a small neighborhood
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
of
0
, such that

G(x,0)=c_{n,\gamma}|x|^{-n+2\gamma}+A_{0}+\psi(x)~~\mbox{ for }x\in\mathcal{N},

Here,
c_{n,\gamma}=\pi^{-n/2}2^{-2\gamma}\Gamma(\gamma)^{-1}\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}-\gamma)

and
\psi
is a function in
\mathcal{N}
satisfying

|\psi(x)|\leq C|x|^{\min\{1,2\gamma\}}~~\mbox{ and }~~|\nabla\psi(x)|\leq C|x|^{\min\{0,2\gamma-1\}}

for some constant
C>0
. Moreover,
A_{0}>0
, unless
(X,\bar{g})
is conformally diffeomorphic to the standard unit ball.

The Positive Mass Conjecture for the fractional operators
P_{\gamma}^{g}
is out of reach at the moment, due to the non-locality and sheer lack of tools to treat this case. So in the following we naturally assume that Positive Mass holds.

Let
R_{\gamma}^{g}:=P_{\gamma}^{g}(1)=u^{-\frac{n+2\gamma}{n-2\gamma}}P_{\gamma}^{g_{0}}(u)
be the fractional curvature. As previously mentioned, this is the scalar curvature when
\gamma=1
and
Q
-curvature when
\gamma=2
. Its average is denoted by

s_{\gamma}^{g}=\int_{M}R_{\gamma}^{g}d\mu_{g}.

The
fractional Yamabe flow
is the evolution of the metric
g=g(t)

given by

(1.5)

\begin{cases}\frac{n-2\gamma}{4}\partial_{t}g&=(s_{\gamma}^{g}-R_{\gamma}^{g})g\\
g(0)&=g_{0}.\end{cases}

Writing with conformal change
g=u^{\frac{4}{n-2\gamma}}g_{0}
, the fractional Yamabe flow (
1.5
) is equivalent to

(1.6)

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}&=(s_{\gamma}^{g}-R_{\gamma}^{g})u\\
u(0)&\equiv 1.\end{cases}

Chan, Sire, and Sun
[
5
]
proved the following:

Theorem

(Theorem 1.2 in
[
5
]
)
.

For
\gamma\in(0,1)
, assume that
Y_{\gamma}(M,[g_{0}])>0
,

\lambda_{1}(g_{+})\geq\frac{n^{2}}{2}-\gamma^{2}
and, in the case

\gamma\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
,
H=0
, where
H
denotes the mean curvature of
\partial_{\infty}X=M
.
Furthermore, assume the Positive Mass Conjecture holds with
A_{0}>0
.
If the initial energy of the initial metric
g(0)
satisfies

(1.7)

s_{\gamma}^{g(0)}\leq\left[Y_{\gamma}(M,[g_{0}])^{\frac{n}{2\gamma}}+Y_{\gamma}(\mathbb{S}^{n})^{\frac{n}{2\gamma}}\right]^{\frac{2\gamma}{n}},

the fractional Yamabe flow (
1.5
) converges.

In this paper, we improve the above Theorem by removing the initial energy bound assumption. In particular, we have the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
n\geq 2
. For
\gamma\in(0,1)
, assume that
Y_{\gamma}(M,[g_{0}])>0
,

\lambda_{1}(g_{+})\geq\frac{n^{2}}{2}-\gamma^{2}
and, in the case

\gamma\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
,
H=0
, where
H
denotes the mean curvature of
\partial_{\infty}X=M
.
Furthermore, assume the Positive Mass Conjecture holds with
A_{0}>0
.
Then the fractional Yamabe flow (
1.5
) converges.

Remark 1.3
.

(1)

In our main theorem, we did not specify in which sense the flow converges. Following previous works, the flow is globally defined and Hölder continuous. However, we expect that the flow is actually smooth, from the parabolic regularity theory, and the convergence can be stated in the
C^{\infty}(M)
sense.

(2)

We state the main theorem with
n\geq 2
, following previous works
[
19
,
20
]
. However, we expect the same result is true for
n=1
, and
\gamma\leq 1/2
.

Preliminaries

In this section, following
[
6
,
5
]
, we first provide the definition of fractional conformal Laplacian. Then, we give some preliminary results on the Yamabe flow and sketch the proof of Theorem
1.2
, provided Proposition
2.1
. In the following, we will always assume that
(M,[g_{0}])
is the conformal infinity of
(X,g_{+})
, both equipped with appropriate metrics. A function
y
is a defining function on
M
in
X
if

y=0\text{ on }M,\quad y>0\text{ in }X\quad\text{and}\quad|dy|\neq 0\text{ on }M.

For each representative metric
g\in[g_{0}]
, there is a unique geodesic defining function
y
associated to
g
such that
g_{+}=y^{-2}(dy^{2}+g_{y})
where
g_{y}
is one parameter family of metrics on
M
satisfying
g_{y}|_{M}=g
. Moreover,
g_{y}
has an asymptotic expansion which contains only even powers of
y
, at least up to degree
n
. Consequently
M
is totally geodesic in
(X,y^{2}g_{+})
.

Assume
\lambda_{1}(-\Delta_{g_{+}})>\frac{n^{2}}{4}-\gamma^{2}
. According to
[
6
, Theorem 4.7]
, there exists a special defining function
y^{*}
enjoying the following Caffarelli-Silvestre type extension property. For any smooth function
u
on
M
, one can find extension
U
on
X
satisfying

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\text{div}((y^{*})^{1-2\gamma}\nabla U)&=0&&\quad\text{in}\quad(X,(y^{*})^{2}g_{+})\\
U&=u&&\quad\text{on}\quad(M,g)\\
(P_{\gamma}^{g}-R^{g}_{\gamma})u&=-c_{\gamma}\lim_{y^{*}\rightarrow 0}(y^{*})^{1-2\gamma}\partial_{y^{*}}U&&\quad\text{on}\quad(M,g),\end{aligned}\right.

where
c_{\gamma}
is a positive constant which can be found in
[
6
]
.

The flow (
1.5
) preserves the volume of
(M,g)
, i.e.

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{M}d\mu_{g}\right)=0~~\mbox{ for all }t\geq 0.

Hence, if we assume that
\displaystyle\int_{M}d\mu_{g_{0}}=1
, we have

(2.2)

\int_{M}u^{\frac{2n}{n-2\gamma}}d\mu_{g_{0}}=\int_{M}d\mu_{g}=1~~\mbox{ for all }t\geq 0.

Along the flow (
1.5
), there holds (c.f
[
5
, Lemma 2.2]
)

(2.3)

\frac{d}{dt}s_{\gamma}^{g}=-2\int_{M}|R_{\gamma}^{g}-s_{\gamma}^{g}|^{2}d\mu_{g}.

It follows from (
2.2
) and the definition of
Y_{\gamma}(M,[g_{0}])
that

s_{\gamma}^{g}\geq Y_{\gamma}(M,[g_{0}])>0
. This together with (
2.3
) implies that

s_{\infty}=\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}s_{\gamma}^{g}
exists.

As the key lemma of the proof, we have the following:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\{t_{\nu}:\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a sequence of times such that
t_{\nu}\to\infty
as
\nu\to\infty
.
Then there exist
\delta\in(0,1)
and a constant
C
such that, after passing to a subsequence, we have

s_{\gamma}^{g(t_{\nu})}-s_{\infty}\leq C\left(\int_{M}|R_{\gamma}^{g(t_{\nu})}-s_{\gamma}^{g(t_{\nu})}|^{\frac{2n}{n+2\gamma}}d\mu_{g(t_{\nu})}\right)^{\frac{n+2\gamma}{2n}(1+\delta)}

for all integers
\nu
in that sequence. Note that
\delta
and
C
may depend on the
sequence
\{t_{\nu}:\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
.

If Proposition
2.1
is proved, then
one can follow the argument in
[
5
]

(see Propositions 3.6-3.10 in
[
5
]
)
to prove that the function
u=u(t)
in (
1.6
) is uniformly bounded, i.e.

(2.4)

C^{-1}\leq\inf_{M}u(t)\leq\sup_{M}u(t)\leq C

for some positive constant
C
independent of
t
.
With the uniform bound in (
2.4
), one can then follow the proof of

[
5
, Proposition 3.1]
to conclude that

u
is Hölder continuous, i.e. for any fixed
\frac{n}{2\gamma}<p<\frac{n+2\gamma}{2\gamma}
,
\alpha=2\gamma-\frac{n}{p}>0
, and
T>0
, there exists
C(T)>0
such that

|u(x_{1},t_{1})-u(x_{2},t_{2})|\leq C(T)\big(|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}+d(x_{1},x_{2})^{\alpha}\big),\quad\forall t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,T],\quad\forall x_{1},x_{2}\in M.

From this, following standard parabolic theory, one can derive higher order estimates, and then it concludes that the flow (
1.5
) converges. This proves Theorem
1.2
.

Therefore, it reduces to prove Proposition
2.1
.

3.
Blow-up analysis

Let
\{t_{\nu}:\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a sequence of times
such that
t_{\nu}\to\infty
as
\nu\to\infty
.
For abbreviation, let
u_{\nu}=u(t_{\nu})
and

g_{\nu}=g(t_{\nu})=u(t_{\nu})^{\frac{4}{n-2\gamma}}g_{0}=u_{\nu}^{\frac{4}{n-2\gamma}}g_{0}
.
The normalization condition (
2.2
) implies that

(3.1)

\int_{M}d\mu_{g_{\nu}}=\int_{M}u_{\nu}^{\frac{2n}{n-2\gamma}}d\mu_{g_{0}}=1~~\mbox{ for all }\nu\in\mathbb{N}.

Moreover, it follows from
[
5
, Lemma 3.2]
that, if we define

F_{p}(g):=\int_{M}|R_{\gamma}^{g}-s_{\gamma}^{g}|^{p}\,d\mu_{g},

then for
1\leq p<\frac{n+2\gamma}{2\gamma}
, we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}F_{p}(g(t))=0.

Choosing
p=\frac{2n}{n+2\gamma}
and

yields

\int_{M}|R_{\gamma}^{g_{\nu}}-s_{\infty}|^{\frac{2n}{n+2\gamma}}d\mu_{g_{\nu}}\to 0~~\mbox{ as }\nu\to\infty,

which gives

(3.2)

\int_{M}\Big|P_{\gamma}^{g_{0}}u_{\nu}-s_{\infty}u_{\nu}^{\frac{n+2\gamma}{n-2\gamma}}\Big|^{\frac{2n}{n+2\gamma}}d\mu_{g_{0}}\to 0~~\mbox{ as }\nu\to\infty.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\{u_{\nu}:\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a sequence of positive functions satisfying
(
3.1
) and (
3.2
). After passing to a subsequence if necessary, we can find

, a nonnegative smooth function

and a

-tuplets

(ii)

4.
The case

Appendix A
Axillary estimates on differences with exponents

Lemma A.1
.

For any
a,b\geq 0
and
p>1
,

(A.1)

|a^{p}-b^{p}|\leq C(|a-b|^{p}+b^{p-1}|a-b|),

(A.2)

|a^{p}-b^{p}-pb^{p-1}(a-b)|\leq C(b^{\max\{0,p-2\}}|a-b|^{\min\{p,2\}}+|a-b|^{p}),

and

(A.3)

\begin{split}&\left|a^{p}-b^{p}-p\,b^{p-1}(a-b)-\frac{1}{2}p(p-1)b^{p-2}(a-b)^{2}\right|\\
&\leq C\left(b^{\max\{0,p-3\}}|a-b|^{\min\{p,3\}}+|a-b|^{p}\right),\end{split}

where the constant
C
depends only on
p
.

Proof.

Since

|a^{p}-b^{p}|\leq C(a^{p-1}+b^{p-1})|a-b|

and

a^{p-1}=(b+(a-b))^{p-1}\leq C(b^{p-1}+|a-b|^{p-1}),

(
A.1
) follows immediately.

We show (
A.2
). Set
\delta:=a-b
and expand
f(s)=s^{p}
to second order at
b
:

a^{p}=b^{p}+p\,b^{p-1}\delta+\frac{1}{2}p(p-1)\xi^{p-2}\delta^{2},

for some
\xi\in[\min\{a,b\},\max\{a,b\}]
.
Hence

|a^{p}-b^{p}-p\,b^{p-1}\delta|\;=\;C\,\xi^{\,p-2}|\delta|^{2},

with
C=\frac{1}{2}p(p-1)
.

Now, suppose
p\geq 2
. Because
p-2\geq 0
and
\xi
lies between
a
and
b
,

\xi^{p-2}\leq(a+b)^{p-2}\leq C(a^{p-2}+b^{p-2}),

and

a^{p-2}=(b+(a-b))^{p-2}\leq C(b^{p-2}+|\delta|^{p-2}),

whence

|a^{p}-b^{p}-p\,b^{p-1}\delta|\leq C\bigl(b^{p-2}|\delta|^{2}+|\delta|^{p}\bigr).

On the other hand, if
1<p<2
, then
s^{p-2}
is decreasing. Consequently
\xi^{p-2}\leq b^{p-2}
and, as in the usual dichotomy

|\delta|\lessgtr b
, one shows
b^{p-2}|\delta|^{2}\leq|\delta|^{p}
.
Thus the same bound as above holds, which is precisely
(
A.2
).

(
A.3
) follows similarly to (
A.2
). This completes the proof.
∎

Remark C.2
.

The singular part in
\alpha
is in
L_{\text{loc}}^{\frac{2n}{n+2\gamma}}(M)
if
n+2\gamma>4
. However, one can apply the above lemma inductively to remove this restriction, for any
n\geq 2
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
. To be more precise, for any
x^{\prime}\in M
, define
D_{0}(x^{\prime}):=B_{cd_{g_{x}}(x,x^{\prime})/2}^{g_{+},+}(x^{\prime})
, and a set consist of balls centered in a points in
D_{0}(x^{\prime})
by

D_{1}(x^{\prime}):=\bigcup_{x_{1}\in D_{0}(x^{\prime})}B^{g_{+},+}_{cd_{g_{x}}(x,x_{1})/2}(x_{1}).

Then
D_{1}(x^{\prime})
is still contained in the cone
\{y<c|\mathbf{x}|\}
, and thus we can apply the pointwise estimate (
C.4
) (if the maximum in the definition of
\alpha
was achieved by the second factor) again, to have

\displaystyle\|U\|_{L^{\infty}(D_{0}(x^{\prime}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{n,\gamma,X,g_{+}}\left(\sup_{x_{1}\in D_{0}(x^{\prime})}\alpha_{x_{1}}\right)(cd_{g_{x}}(x,x^{\prime}))^{2\gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{n,\gamma,X,g_{+},\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}^{g_{+},+})(x)}}\frac{\|U\|_{L^{\infty}\left(D_{1}(x^{\prime})\right)}}{\left(\inf_{x_{1}\in D_{0}(x^{\prime})}d_{g_{x}}^{2}(x,x_{1})\right)d_{g_{x}}^{-2\gamma}(x,x^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{n,\gamma,X,g_{+}}\frac{\|U\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}^{g_{+},+})}}{d_{g_{x}}^{2-2\gamma}(x,x^{\prime})}.

In the above, we are estimating the pointwise value of
U
in the respective ball
D_{1}(x^{\prime})
, by taking a collection of balls centered in each points in the first ball
D_{0}(x^{\prime})
, which are still in the conical region
\{y<c|\mathbf{x}|\}
. As a result, we gained the integrability from
d_{g_{x}}^{-2}
in (
C.6
) to
d^{2\gamma-2}_{g_{x}}
, in sacrifice of the coefficient. One can repeat this procedure finitely many times by defining
D_{k}(x^{\prime})
in the inductive manner (and all
D_{k}(x^{\prime})
are still in the conical region
\{y<c|\mathbf{x}|\}
) to obtain
L^{\frac{2n}{n+2\gamma}}
integrability for the right hand side of (
C.4
), for any
n\geq 2
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
. Note that we cannot completely remove the singular denominator and obtain
L^{\infty}
estimate in this method, because
\frac{\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}^{g_{+},+}(x))}}{d_{g_{x}}^{1-\gamma^{2}}(x,x^{\prime})}
in (
C.6
) does not improve in this inductive step.

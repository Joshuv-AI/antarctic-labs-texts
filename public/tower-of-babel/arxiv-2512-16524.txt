W*-Amenability for Fell bundles over discrete groups
Alcides Buss, Damián Ferraro

Abstract
. We investigate amenability for W ∗ \mathrm{W}^{*} -Fell bundles over a discrete group G G , with a focus on its characterization via approximation properties and conditional expectations. Building on the notion of W ∗ -amenability, we construct an enlarged W ∗ \mathrm{W}^{*} -Fell bundle analogous to ℓ ∞ ​ ( G , M ) \ell^{\infty}(G,M) for a group action G G on a von Neumann algebra M M , and relate amenability to the existence of suitable conditional expectations at both the bundle and crossed-product levels. Our results unify and extend several approaches to amenability for noncommutative dynamical systems. As applications of our methods, we prove that amenability of Fell bundles passes to restrictions to subgroups and that a Fell bundle over a group G G is amenable if and only if both its restriction to a normal subgroup H ​ ⊴ ​ G H\trianglelefteq G and the associated quotient Fell bundle over G / H G/H are amenable. This provides a powerful structural tool that extends classical permanence results for group amenability to the setting of W ∗ \mathrm{W}^{*} -Fell bundles and also C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebraic Fell bundles. We also discuss how Fell bundles and their amenability interact with group coactions on C ∗ C^{*} -algebras and von Neumann algebras.



1.
Introduction

The study of amenability in the context of operator algebras has led to a rich theory with deep
connections to approximation properties, conditional expectations, and dynamical systems.
For Fell bundles over discrete groups, the central notion is
Exel’s approximation property
(AP)

[
15
]
. This property implies the weak containment property, i.e. the equality
of the full and reduced cross-sectional C
∗
-algebras of the bundle, and it is strongly related to
nuclearity: if the unit fiber is nuclear, then the nuclearity of a cross-sectional C
∗
-algebra is
equivalent to the AP of the underlying Fell bundle.
It remains a major open problem whether the AP is in general equivalent to weak containment.

A fundamental step towards understanding this property is the following observation, proved in
[
2
]
: a Fell bundle

\mathcal{B}=\{B_{t}\}_{t\in G}
has the AP if and only if its bidual W
∗
-Fell bundle

\mathcal{M}=\{M_{t}\}_{t\in G}
, with
M_{t}=B_{t}^{\prime\prime}
, has the W
∗
-approximation property (W
∗
AP).
Thus, the von Neumann bundle
\mathcal{M}
is not merely a technical enlargement of
\mathcal{B}
, but rather the
natural framework in which approximation can be studied. This provides the main motivation of the
present work: we develop a systematic theory of amenability for W
∗
-Fell bundles, with the goal
of understanding and extending the approximation property for C
∗
-Fell bundles.

For actions of discrete groups on von Neumann algebras, amenability was classically defined by
Anantharaman-Delaroche
[
4
]
as the existence of a
G
-equivariant conditional
expectation

P\colon\ell^{\infty}(G,M)\to M,

where the lifted action
\tilde{\gamma}
of
G
on
\ell^{\infty}(G,M)
is given by

\tilde{\gamma}_{r}(f)(s)=\gamma_{r}(f(r^{-1}s))
. This notion admits several equivalent
characterizations, including central and quasi-central approximation properties

[
6
,
10
]
.
The W
∗
AP for Fell bundles, introduced in
[
2
]
, extends this picture
from group actions to the more general framework of W
∗
-Fell bundles.

Our first main contribution is a new characterization of amenability for W
∗
-Fell bundles in terms
of conditional expectations. We construct a canonical enlargement of a given W
∗
-Fell bundle –
analogous to
\ell^{\infty}(G,M)
in the case of group actions – and show that the existence of a

G
-equivariant conditional expectation from this enlarged bundle to the original one is equivalent
to W
∗
AP. This links approximation-theoretic and categorical views of amenability and connects to
the theory of dual coactions.

Theorem A
.

For a W*-Fell bundle
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{t}\}_{t\in G},
the following statements are equivalent.

(a)

\mathcal{M}
is W
∗
-amenable.

(b)

There exists a conditional expectation
P\colon\ell^{\infty}(G,\mathcal{M})\to\mathcal{M}.

(c)

There exists a conditional expectation
Q\colon\mathbb{k}_{\operatorname{\rm w}^{*}}(\mathcal{M})\to W^{*}_{\mathop{\rm r}}(\mathcal{M}).

Here
\ell^{\infty}(G,\mathcal{M}):=\{\ell^{\infty}(G,M_{t})\}_{t\in G}
denotes the canonical enlargement of a
W^{*}
-Fell bundle, to be introduced in Section
2.3
,
W^{*}_{\mathop{\rm r}}(\mathcal{M})
denotes the W*-crossed section algebra and
\mathbb{k}_{\operatorname{\rm w}^{*}}(\mathcal{M})
the W*-algebra of kernels of
\mathcal{M}
.

In the second part of the paper, we establish structural permanence results.
We show that amenability is preserved under restriction to subgroups and can be detected via
extensions by normal subgroups. These results generalize classical permanence theorems for amenable
groups to the setting of Fell bundles, and apply equally in the C
∗
and W
∗
settings. In this direction, our main result can be summarized as follows:

Theorem B
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{t}\}_{t\in G}
be a W
∗
-Fell bundle or a C
∗
-algebraic Fell bundle over a discrete group
G
,
and let
H\leq G
be a subgroup. If
\mathcal{M}
is amenable (i.e. C
∗
-amenable in the von Neumann sense or has the approximation property in the C
∗
-case), then the restricted bundle
\mathcal{M}|_{H}
is also amenable.

Moreover, if
H\trianglelefteq G
is a normal subgroup, then
\mathcal{M}
is amenable if and only if both its
restriction
\mathcal{M}|_{H}
and the associated quotient Fell bundle over
G/H
are amenable.

Finally, to illustrate the scope of our methods, we revisit a concrete class of examples:
Green twisted actions. We show that amenability of such actions is equivalent to W
∗
-amenability of
the induced action on the center of
B^{\prime\prime}
. This connects our general theory with a classical
construction in the theory of crossed products, and demonstrates how the W
∗
-bundle approach
naturally captures and extends known results.

Remark 2.10

(c.f.

4.
Fell bundles and group coactions

In this section, we reinterpret our results in the framework of group coactions by examining the relationship between Fell bundles and the corresponding group coactions. While this connection is well established in the setting of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras, our aim here is to extend these ideas to the context of von Neumann algebras.

Let
G
be a discrete group.
1
1

1

The theory of group coactions extends naturally to locally compact groups, see
[
13
]
. Since we only use discrete groups in this paper, we restrict to this case to avoid complications.
Recall that a coaction of
G
on a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
A
is a
*
-homomorphism

\delta\colon A\to A\otimes C^{*}(G)

satisfying
(\delta\otimes\operatorname{id})\circ\delta=(\operatorname{id}\otimes\delta_{G})\circ\delta
, where
\delta_{G}\colon C^{*}(G)\to C^{*}(G)\otimes C^{*}(G)
is the usual comultiplication:
\delta_{G}(t)=t\otimes t
for
t\in G
. Moreover, as usual, we assume that
\delta
is
nondegenerate
in the sense that
\overline{\mathop{\rm span}}\,\delta(A)(1\otimes C^{*}(G))=A\otimes C^{*}(G)
. This is equivalent to
(\epsilon\otimes\operatorname{id})\circ\delta=\operatorname{id}_{A}
, where
\epsilon\colon C^{*}(G)\to\mathbb{C}
is the trivial representation:
\epsilon(t)=1
for all
t\in G
. In particular, every coaction is automatically an injective homomorphism.

The coaction
\delta
is called
normal
if
\delta^{\lambda}:=(\operatorname{id}\otimes\lambda)\circ\delta\colon A\to A\otimes C^{*}_{\mathop{\rm r}}(G)
is injective. In this case
\delta^{\lambda}
may be viewed as a “reduced coaction” of
G
, see
[
13
]
and references there in.

Given a coaction
\delta\colon A\to A\otimes C^{*}(G)
, its
crossed product
is defined as

A\rtimes_{\delta}\widehat{G}:=\overline{\mathop{\rm span}}\,\delta^{\lambda}(A)(1\otimes M(C_{0}(G)))\subseteq\mathbb{B}(A\otimes\mathbb{K}(\ell^{2}(G)))=\mathbb{B}(A\otimes\ell^{2}(G)),

where
M\colon C_{0}(G)\to\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(G))
denotes the representation by multiplication operators:
M_{f}(\xi)=f\cdot\xi
.

The crossed product
A\rtimes_{\delta}\widehat{G}
carries a canonical
G
-action, called the dual action, and denoted by
\widehat{\delta}
; it is given on generators by
\widehat{\delta}_{t}(\delta^{\lambda}(a)(1\otimes M_{f}))=\delta^{\lambda}(a)(1\otimes M_{\mathtt{rt}_{t}(f)})
, where
\mathtt{rt}_{t}(f)(s):=f(st)
denotes the right translation
G
-action on
C_{0}(G)
.

The coaction
\delta
is called
maximal
if the canonical homomorphism

\Phi_{\delta}\colon A\rtimes_{\delta}\widehat{G}\rtimes_{\widehat{\delta}}G\twoheadrightarrow A\otimes\mathbb{K}(\ell^{2}(G))

is injective (and hence an isomorphism). Equivalently,
\delta
is maximal if and only if it lifts to a (necessarily) injective homomorphism
\delta_{\max}\colon A\to A\otimes_{\max}C^{*}(G)
, see

[
9
, Theorem 5.1]
.

Given a coaction
\delta\colon A\to A\otimes C^{*}(G)
, its spectral subspaces

A_{t}:=\{a\in A:\delta(a)=a\otimes t\}

form a Fell bundle
\mathcal{A}=(A_{t})_{t\in G}
concretely represented in
A
.
We call
\mathcal{A}
the
spectral Fell bundle
of
\delta
.
Moreover, the algebraic direct sum
\oplus_{t\in G}^{\mathrm{alg}}A_{t}
is a (norm) dense
*
-subalgebra of
A
, canonically isomorphic to the cross-sectional
*
-algebra
C_{c}(\mathcal{A})
consisting of finitely supported sections
G\to\mathcal{A}
.

Hence each coaction
\delta
of
G
on
A
yields a
G
-grading for the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
A
. This is a
topological grading
in the sense that we have projections
E_{t}\colon A\to A_{t}
; these are given by
E_{t}(a):=(\operatorname{id}\otimes\varphi_{t})(\delta(a))
, where
\varphi_{t}\colon C^{*}(G)\to\mathbb{C}
is the bounded linear functional specified by
\varphi_{t}(s)=\delta_{s,t}
for
s,t\in G
. Notice that
\varphi_{e}
is just the canonical trace on
C^{*}(G)
. The functional
\varphi_{t}
is given by
\varphi_{t}(x)=\varphi_{e}(t^{-1}x)
. The functional
\varphi_{t}
factors through
C^{*}_{\mathop{\rm r}}(G)
, where it becomes the vector functional
x\mapsto\langle\delta_{t}\,|\,\mathopen{}x(\delta_{e})\rangle
.

The following result is well known:

Theorem 4.1
.

Given a Fell bundle
\mathcal{A}=(A_{t})_{t\in G}
over
G
, its full cross-sectional
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(\mathcal{A})
carries a maximal coaction
\delta_{\mathcal{A}}\colon C^{*}(\mathcal{A})\to C^{*}(\mathcal{A})\otimes C^{*}(G)
satisfying
\delta_{\mathcal{A}}(a_{t})=a_{t}\otimes t
for
a_{t}\in A_{t}
. The spectral Fell bundle of
\delta_{\mathcal{A}}
is canonically isomorphic to
\mathcal{A}
. Every maximal coaction is isomorphic to one of this form for an essentially unique Fell bundle, namely, the spectral Fell bundle of the coaction.

The reduced cross-sectional
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}_{\mathop{\rm r}}(\mathcal{A})
carries a normal coaction
\delta_{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathop{\rm r}}\colon C^{*}_{\mathop{\rm r}}(\mathcal{A})\to C^{*}_{\mathop{\rm r}}(\mathcal{A})\otimes C^{*}(G)
satisfying
\delta_{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathop{\rm r}}(a_{t})=a_{t}\otimes t
for
a_{t}\in A_{t}
. Every normal coaction is isomorphic to one of this form via the associated spectral Fell bundle.

The above result yields an equivalence between the categories of Fell bundles over
G
(with usual morphisms of Fell bundles over
G
) and the category of maximal (resp. normal) coactions of
G
. A morphism between two
G
-coactions
(A,\delta)
and
(A^{\prime},\delta^{\prime})
is a just a
*
-homomorphism
\pi\colon A\to A^{\prime}
that is equivariant with respect to the coactions, meaning that
\delta^{\prime}(\pi(a))=(\pi\otimes\operatorname{id})(\delta(a))
for all
a\in A
.

4.1.

Remark 4.12

5.
Reductions and quotients of Fell bundles

In this section we prove the analogue of Theorem
3.7

for Fell bundles over discrete groups. We begin by constructing the objects involved in the statement.

Let
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{t}\}_{t\in G}
be a Fell bundle over a discrete group
G
, and let

H\trianglelefteq G
be a normal subgroup.
The natural inclusion
\iota_{\mathcal{B}}\colon\mathcal{B}\to C^{*}(\mathcal{B})
sends
b\in B_{t}
to the cross-section
that takes the value
b
at
t
and
0
elsewhere.
For each
u\in G/H=:\dot{G}
, let
\dot{B}_{u}
be the norm-closure of

\sum_{t\in u}\iota_{\mathcal{B}}(B_{t})
in
C^{*}(\mathcal{B})
.
Note that
\iota_{\mathcal{B}}
induces a grading of
C^{*}(\mathcal{B})
over
G
, which we call the

canonical
G
-grading of
C^{*}(\mathcal{B})
induced by
\mathcal{B}
.

Proposition 5.1

(cf. Proposition
2.14
)
.

There exists a unique injective
*
-homomorphism

h\colon C^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{H})\to C^{*}(\mathcal{B})
such that

h\circ\iota_{\mathcal{B}_{H}}=\iota_{\mathcal{B}}|_{\mathcal{B}_{H}}
.
Hence
h(C^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{H}))
is
C^{*}
-isomorphic to
\dot{B}_{H}
.
With the norm and
*
-operations inherited from
C^{*}(\mathcal{B})
,

\dot{\mathcal{B}}:=\{\dot{B}_{u}\}_{u\in\dot{G}}
is a Fell bundle.
Moreover,
\mathcal{B}
and
\iota_{\dot{\mathcal{B}}}
induce a grading of
C^{*}(\dot{\mathcal{B}})
over
G

with fibres
\{B_{t}\}_{t\in G}
: for each
t\in G
,

B_{t}
is linearly and isometrically identified with

\iota_{\dot{\mathcal{B}}}(\iota_{\mathcal{B}}(B_{t}))\subseteq C^{*}(\dot{\mathcal{B}})
.
There exists a
*
-isomorphism
k\colon C^{*}(\mathcal{B})\to C^{*}(\dot{\mathcal{B}})

that restricts to the identity on each fibre
B_{t}
.

Proof.

Every
*
-representation of
\mathcal{B}
on a Hilbert space restricts to a

*
-representation of
\mathcal{B}_{H}
.
Passing to integrated forms gives a
*
-homomorphism

h\colon C^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{H})\to C^{*}(\mathcal{B})
satisfying

h\circ\iota_{\mathcal{B}_{H}}=\iota_{\mathcal{B}}|_{\mathcal{B}_{H}}
.
To show
h
is faithful, let
T
be a nondegenerate
*
-representation of
\mathcal{B}_{H}

with faithful integrated form
\tilde{T}\colon C^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{H})\to\mathbb{B}(X)
.
By
[
18
, Theorem 2.1]
,
T
is
\mathcal{B}
-positive and can be induced to a

*
-representation
S\colon\mathcal{B}\to\mathbb{B}(Y)
by the process in

[
17
, Ch. XI]
.
Since
\dot{G}
is discrete,
[
17
, XI 14.21]
implies that
T

is a subrepresentation of
S|_{\mathcal{B}_{H}}
.
Hence
\tilde{T}\circ h=\tilde{S}
is faithful, and
h
is injective.

The fact that
\dot{\mathcal{B}}
is a Fell bundle follows immediately from

(\dot{B}_{u})^{*}=\dot{B}_{u^{-1}}
and
\dot{B}_{u}\dot{B}_{v}\subseteq\dot{B}_{uv}

for all
u,v\in\dot{G}
.

Given any
*
-representation
T\colon C^{*}(\dot{\mathcal{B}})\to\mathbb{B}(X)
,
there exists a unique
*
-representation
T^{\prime}\colon C^{*}(\mathcal{B})\to\mathbb{B}(X)

such that
T^{\prime}\circ\iota_{\mathcal{B}}(b)=T\circ\iota_{\dot{\mathcal{B}}}\circ\iota_{\mathcal{B}}(b)

for all
b\in\mathcal{B}
.
This defines a
*
-homomorphism
k\colon C^{*}(\mathcal{B})\to C^{*}(\dot{\mathcal{B}})

preserving the
G
-grading, hence surjective.
If
T
is a faithful
*
-representation of
C^{*}(\mathcal{B})
, then

T\circ\iota_{\dot{\mathcal{B}}}\colon\dot{\mathcal{B}}\to\mathbb{B}(X)
integrates to
a representation
\dot{T}
satisfying
\dot{T}\circ k=T
.
Therefore
k
is injective.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a Fell bundle over
G
.
Then
\mathcal{B}
is C
∗
-amenable if and only if both
\mathcal{B}_{H}
and
\dot{\mathcal{B}}
are C
∗
-amenable.

Proof.

The implication
“
\mathcal{B}

\Rightarrow

\mathcal{B}_{H}
and
\dot{\mathcal{B}}
are C
∗
-amenable”
is proved (for general locally compact groups) in
[
11
, Proposition 4.10]
.

Conversely, assume that
\mathcal{B}_{H}
and
\dot{\mathcal{B}}

By
[
2
, Definition 5.16]
,
\mathcal{B}
is C

-amenable

is W

-amenable.
Note that
\mathcal{M}_{H}=(\mathcal{B}_{H})^{\prime\prime}
.
Then Theorem
3.7
implies that

Let

and

Since

as the
{\operatorname{\rm w}^{*}}

in

6.
Amenability for Green twisted actions

Let
(B,G,N,\alpha,\tau)
be a Green twisted action with
G
a discrete group, that is,

\alpha\colon G\to\Aut(B)
is an action by C
∗
-automorphisms and

\tau\colon N\to U\mathcal{M}(B)
is a homomorphism satisfying

\alpha_{s}(\tau_{n})=\tau_{sns^{-1}}\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha_{n}=\operatorname{Ad}(\tau_{n})\qquad(s\in G,\ n\in N).

See
[
14
, Definition 5.3]
for details.

Consider the usual semidirect Fell bundle
B\times G\to G
with operations

(b,s)(c,t)=(b\,\alpha_{s}(c),st)
and
(b,s)^{*}=(\alpha_{s^{-1}}(b^{*}),s^{-1})
.
There is a right action of
N
given by

(b,s)\cdot n:=(b\,\tau_{n},\ n^{-1}s),\qquad n\in N.

The
twisted semidirect Fell bundle
is then the orbit bundle

B\times_{N}G:=(B\times G)/N\ \longrightarrow\ G/N,\qquad[b,s]\longmapsto sN.

In particular, the fibre over
sN
is

(B\times_{N}G)_{sN}=\{[b,s]:b\in B\}

[
14
, Definition 5.3]
.
The twisted crossed products (full and reduced) coincide with the full and reduced
cross-sectional C
∗
-algebras of
B\times_{N}G
.
Moreover, the semidirect bundle
B\times G\to G
is isomorphic to the

pull-back

q^{*}(B\times_{N}G)
via the quotient map
q\colon G\to G/N
,
explicitly,

(b,s)\longmapsto([b,s],s)

is an isomorphism of Fell bundles
[
14
, Theorem 5.6]
.

Each fibre of
B\times_{N}G
is canonically isomorphic to
B
. Indeed,
for
sN\in G/N
the map

B\longrightarrow(B\times_{N}G)_{sN},\qquad b\mapsto[b,s],

is a bijection. Injectivity follows since
[b,s]=[b^{\prime},s]
implies

(b^{\prime},s)=(b\tau_{n},n^{-1}s)
for some
n\in N
, hence
n=e
and
b^{\prime}=b
;
surjectivity follows by writing any representative
(c,t)
with
t=n^{-1}s

and observing that
(c,t)=(c\tau_{n^{-1}},s)\cdot n
.

Definition 6.1
.

We say that the Green twisted action
(B,G,N,\alpha,\tau)
is

amenable
if the associated Fell bundle

\mathcal{D}:=B\times_{N}G\to G/N
has
the approximation property (AP).

Remark 6.2
.

Via the identification
B\cong(\mathcal{D})_{N}
,
b\leftrightarrow[b,e]
,
the AP translates into the existence of a bounded net

\{a_{i}\colon G/N\to B\}_{i\in I}\subset L^{2}(G/N,B)
of finitely supported functions
such that for all
(s,b)\in G\times B
,

\lim_{i}\big\|\,b-\sum_{u\in G/N}a_{i}(sNu)^{*}\,b\,\alpha_{s}(a_{i}(u))\big\|=0.

If a Green twisted action
(B,G,N,\alpha,\tau)
is amenable, then it satisfies the weak containment property, and hence

B\rtimes_{\alpha,\tau}G\;\cong\;B\rtimes_{\alpha,\tau,r}G.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
(B,G,N,\alpha,\tau)
be a Green twisted action and let

\mathcal{D}=B\times_{N}G\to G/N
be the associated twisted semidirect Fell bundle.
Then
\mathcal{D}
has the approximation property if and only if the induced action

(6.4)

G/N\curvearrowright Z(B^{\prime\prime}),\qquad sN\cdot z=\alpha_{s}^{\prime\prime}(z),

is W
∗
-amenable.

Proof.

By
[
2
, Thm. 6.10]
, a Fell bundle has the AP if and only if
the associated partial action on the centre of the bidual of its unit fibre is W
∗
-amenable.
Since
\mathcal{D}
is saturated and every fibre is isomorphic to
B
, the associated partial action
reduces to a global action
\theta
of
G/N
on
Z(B^{\prime\prime})
.

More precisely, fix
s\in G
and view the fibre
\mathcal{D}_{sN}
as a
B
–
B
imprimitivity bimodule.
The right
B
-action is the usual one, while the left action is given by
\alpha_{s}
.
Passing to biduals,
\mathcal{D}_{sN}^{\prime\prime}
becomes a
B^{\prime\prime}
–
B^{\prime\prime}
equivalence bimodule with left action

b\cdot x=\alpha_{s}^{\prime\prime}(b)\,x
.
By
[
2
, Remark 4.5]
, such a bimodule determines an isomorphism

\pi_{s}\colon Z(B^{\prime\prime})\to Z(B^{\prime\prime})
satisfying
xa=\pi_{s}(a)x
for all
x\in\mathcal{D}_{sN}^{\prime\prime}
and
a\in Z(B^{\prime\prime})
.
Since the left action is
\alpha_{s}^{\prime\prime}
, one checks that
\pi_{s}=\alpha_{s^{-1}}^{\prime\prime}|_{Z(B^{\prime\prime})}
.
Hence the automorphism implemented by the fibre
sN
is

\theta_{sN}=(\pi_{s})^{-1}=\alpha_{s}^{\prime\prime}|_{Z(B^{\prime\prime})}
, giving the global action in
(
6.4
).
∎

The proof above illustrates the type of reasoning that emerges from connecting the theories of
amenability in the settings of C
∗
-actions, W
∗
-actions, Fell bundles and W
∗
-Fell bundles.
Here, Anantharaman-Delaroche’s classical notion of amenability

[
4
,
5
,
6
]

is extended to Green twisted actions through the unifying framework of Fell bundles and their W
∗
counterparts.

These connections are not accidental.
In
[
2
]
, the definition of W
∗
-amenable W
∗
-Fell bundle
was introduced in terms of the amenability of the partial or global W
∗
-actions naturally associated to each bundle,
and shown to be equivalent to the W
∗
-approximation property (W
∗

A Fell bundle is said to be C
∗
-amenable if its bidual W*-Fell bundle is W
∗
-amenable; and the AP characterizes C
∗
-amenability

[
2
, Theorem 6.10]
.
With these definitions, a W
∗
(resp. C
∗
) action is amenable in the sense of
Anantharaman-Delaroche if and only if its semidirect product bundle is W
∗
-
(resp. C
∗
-)amenable.

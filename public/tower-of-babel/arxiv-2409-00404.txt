Expanding self-orthogonal codes over a ring ℤ 4 \mathbb{Z}_{4} to self-dual codes and unimodular lattices
Minjia Shi, Sihui Tao, Jihoon Hong, Jon-Lark Kim

Abstract
Self-dual codes have been studied actively because they are connected with mathematical structures including block designs and lattices and have practical applications in quantum error-correcting codes and secret sharing schemes. Nevertheless, there has been less attention to construct self-dual codes from self-orthogonal codes with smaller dimensions. Hence, the main purpose of this paper is to propose a way to expand any self-orthogonal code over a ring ℤ 4 \mathbb{Z}_{4} to many self-dual codes over ℤ 4 \mathbb{Z}_{4} . We show that all self-dual codes over ℤ 4 \mathbb{Z}_{4} of lengths 4 4 to 8 8 can be constructed this way. Furthermore, we have found five new self-dual codes over ℤ 4 \mathbb{Z}_{4} of lengths 27 , 28 , 29 , 33 , 27,28,29,33, and 34 34 with the highest Euclidean weight 12 12 . Moreover, using Construction A A applied to our new Euclidean-optimal self-dual codes over ℤ 4 \mathbb{Z}_{4} , we have constructed a new odd extremal unimodular lattice in dimension 34 whose kissing number was not previously known.



Keywords:
self-orthogonal code, maximal code

Introduction

A linear code is called
self-dual
if it is equal to its dual and
self-orthogonal
if it is contained in its dual. Since the beginning of Coding Theory, self-dual codes have been actively studied. Some well-known examples include the binary extended Hamming code of length 8 and the binary extended Golay code of length 24.
Self-dual codes play an important role in Coding Theory and have applications in various mathematical structures such as block designs
[
1
,
13
]
, lattices
[
3
]
, modular forms
[
25
]
, and sphere packings
[
7
]
.
The classification problem of self-dual codes up to equivalence is one of the difficult problems. Researchers have been interested in classifying self-dual codes with different lengths
n
according to their equivalence
[
6
,
10
]
.

On the other hand, self-orthogonal codes have been less studied and more difficult to classify them because there are many self-orthogonal codes of a given length and with various dimensions. Freibert and Kim
[
9
]
studied a chain of self-orthogonal subcodes of a fixed self-dual code such that the minimum distance of each self-orthogonal subcode is as large as possible, which are called
optimum distance profiles (ODP) of a self-dual code
. The authors of
[
9
]
gave the ODP of Type II self-dual codes of length up to 24 and the five extremal Type II codes of length 32, and proposed a partial result of the ODP of the extended quadratic residue code of length 48.

There are other related ways to construct self-dual codes from various ways.
One of the famous constructions is the gluing theory
[
24
]
, which produces self-dual codes by the direct sum of several self-orthogonal codes
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2},...,\mathcal{C}_{t}
over a finite field. Kim
[
18
]
gave a systematic method called the building-up construction in order to construct self-dual codes from a shorter length self-dual codes.
Recently, Kim et al.
[
20
]
constructed good self-orthogonal codes from good linear codes by the embedding method. Then there has been active work to construct good self-orthogonal codes
[
19
]
,
[
26
]
.
However, not many theories have been discussed on how to construct self-dual codes from self-orthogonal codes.

Durğun
[
8
]
also proposed an approach for constructing a self-dual code denoted as
\mathcal{C}_{sd}
from a maximal self-orthogonal code
\mathcal{C}_{m}
over the finite field
GF(q)
.
This process specifically entails augmenting the generator matrix of
\mathcal{C}_{m}
with an additional row and column.
Moreover, it was shown
[
8
]
that every self-orthogonal code over a finite field can be expanded to a self-dual code over the field. Hence we propose a method to construct self-dual codes from a given self-orthogonal code over a ring
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
as a next step since linear codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
have been actively studied.

In this paper, we study the opposite direction of ODP. More precisely, we start from a self-orthogonal code over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
, augment a vector to produce a larger self-orthogonal code containing the given code, and repeat this process until we get a self-dual code over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
including all the intermediate self-orthogonal codes.
This approach is theoretically interesting and connects a given self-orthogonal to various self-dual codes.

We first propose a way to expand any self-orthogonal code
\mathcal{C}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
to a self-dual code
\mathcal{C}_{sd}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
. Especially, whenever
\mathcal{C}
is free, we can expand
\mathcal{C}
into a self-dual code with type
4^{k_{1}}2^{k_{2}}
with largest possible
k_{1}
.
Several examples listed in Sections 3 and 4 are self-dual codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
with the largest Lee or Euclidean minimum distance. Moreover, using Construction
A
applied to our new Euclidean-optimal self-dual codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
, we have constructed a new odd extremal unimodular lattice in dimension 34 whose kissing number was not previously known.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2, we help readers recall some basic definitions and concepts which are used throughout the paper. In Section 3, we prove that any self-orthogonal code over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
can be expanded into a self-dual code over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
under some conditions. In Section 4, we construct new self-dual codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
of length
27,28,29,33,
and
34
with the highest Euclidean weight
12
by considering expanding self-orthogonal codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
. We construct odd extremal unimodular lattices in dimensions
27,28,29,33,
and
34
.
In Section 5, we conclude the whole paper. In the appendix, we give three algorithms to expand any self-orthogonal code to many self-dual codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
with the given type.

In this section, we will give basic results about expanding a self-orthogonal code to a self-dual code over

Proposition 3.1
.

(
[
8
, Proposition 2.3]
)
A self-orthogonal code
\mathcal{C}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is maximal if and only if
\left[\textbf{v},\textbf{v}\right]\neq 0
for all
\textbf{v}\in\mathcal{C}^{\bot}\setminus\mathcal{C}
.

In the proof of Lemma
3.1

8

Lemma 3.1
.

(
[
8
, Lemma 2.4]
)
Every self-orthogonal
[n,k]
code
\mathcal{C}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is contained in a maximal self-orthogonal
[n,k^{\prime}\geq k]
code
\mathcal{C}_{m}
such that
\mathcal{C}_{m}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\bot}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a self-orthogonal
[n,k]
code. Then
k\leqslant\lfloor n/2\rfloor
. If
\mathcal{C}
is maximal self-orthogonal, we are done. Assume that
\mathcal{C}
is not a maximal self-orthogonal code. From Proposition

3.1
, we know that there exists
\textbf{v}\in\mathcal{C}^{\bot}\setminus\mathcal{C}
, so that
\left[\textbf{v},\textbf{v}\right]=0
. Let
G
be a generator matrix of code
\mathcal{C}
. Take
G_{1}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\textbf{v}\\
G\\
\end{array}\right],

and let
\mathcal{C}_{1}
be a linear code with generator matrix
G_{1}
. Then we can say the code
\mathcal{C}_{1}
is a self-orthogonal
[n,k+1]

\mathcal{C}\subsetneq\mathcal{C}_{1}
. If
\mathcal{C}_{1}
is a maximal self-orthogonal code, then we are done. If not, we repeat this process (i.e., choose
\textbf{v}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\bot}\setminus\mathcal{C}_{1}
where
\textbf{v}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}^{\bot}
trivially) to get a self-orthogonal code
\mathcal{C}_{2}
such that
\mathcal{C}\subsetneq\mathcal{C}_{1}\subsetneq\mathcal{C}_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\bot}
. This process will end since the dimension increases one by one and the dimension of a maximal self-orthogonal code is at most
\lfloor n/2\rfloor
. We denote a maximal self-orthogonal code containing
\mathcal{C}
obtained in this process by
\mathcal{C}_{m}
, which then satisfies
\mathcal{C}_{m}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\bot}
. ∎

~~~{\bf\alpha}_{2}\\
~~~~~\vdots\\
~~{\bf\alpha}_{n-k_{1}}\\

For any row vector

\alpha_{i}H^{T}=0

(2)

~~~~2{\bf\alpha}_{j_{1}}\\

.

(
[

[

Assume that
\mathcal{C}_{1}

e_{2}\\
\vdots\\

A set of
k

Let

e_{2}^{*}\\
\vdots\\

We choose all possible

B_{1}
and

are binary matrices.
Then,

.

has a generator matrix

\bf{0}&I_{k_{1}^{\prime}-k_{1}}&C\\
\end{matrix}\right].

We modify the binary generator matrix

into
G_{4}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
by following two steps:

(1)

Firstly, according to Lemma
3.2
we change the matrix
\left[\begin{matrix}I_{k_{1}^{\prime}-k_{1}}&C\end{matrix}\right]
to
\left[\begin{matrix}E&C\end{matrix}\right]
in a way that any two rows (not necessarily distinct) in this matrix
\left[\begin{matrix}E&C\end{matrix}\right]
are orthogonal over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
.
We have
2^{\frac{\left(k_{1}^{\prime}-k_{1}\right)^{2}+\left(k_{1}^{\prime}-k_{1}\right)}{2}}
different options to have different matrices
E
.
Then we obtain
G_{3}=\left[\begin{matrix}I_{k_{1}}&A&B_{1}\\
\bf{0}&E&C\\
\end{matrix}\right].

(2)

Secondly, let

\bf{0}&E&C\\
\end{matrix}\right].

According to the orthogonal relationship over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
between two different rows of
G_{4}
, we modify
G_{4}
by changing the left zero matrix

{\bf{0}}_{\left(k_{1}^{\prime}-k_{1}\right)\times k_{1}}
into the new
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
-matrix

.
Let
1+k_{1}\leqslant i\leqslant k_{1}^{\prime}
and
1\leqslant j\leqslant k_{1}
. Then the
(i,j)
th entry of

equals to the inner product modulo 4 of the

th and

th rows of
G_{4}
. It is obvious that any entry of

is either
2
or

G_{5}
:

G_{5}=\left[\begin{matrix}I_{k_{1}}&A&B_{1}+2B_{2}\\
F&E&C\\
\end{matrix}\right].

From the results of Theorem
3.1
, we can get the final self-dual code
\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}
, which contains
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
as a subcode. The code
\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}
is with type
4^{k_{1}^{\prime}}2^{n-2k_{1}}
.
∎

Next, we will consider a special case when
{\mbox{Res}}(\mathcal{C})
is a self-orthogonal code containing the all-ones vector.

Lemma 3.3
.

(
[
21
, Lemma 4.6]
)
Let

be a doubly-even binary self-orthogonal

is contained in a doubly-even binary self-dual code

containing

.

Conclusion

In this paper, we have succeeded in expanding any self-orthogonal code to many self-dual codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
. We have explored various methods for expansion over the ring
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
, focusing on maintaining the dimension of the residue code or increasing it to its maximum capacity for a given free or non free self-orthogonal
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
code. Using Magma
[
4
]
, we have recovered all indecomposable self-dual codes with lengths from
4
to
8
by expanding self-orthogonal codes. When it comes to longer lengths, we constructed five new optimal self-dual codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
of length
27,28,29,33,34
in Table
3

\mathbb{Z}_{4},
but also provides explicit and comprehensive algorithms. We
expect that three algorithms will provide more interesting new self-dual codes.

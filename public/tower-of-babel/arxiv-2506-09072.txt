Abstract
. The goal of this note is to record the following curious fact: let ( S , 𝔫 ) (S,\mathfrak{n}) be an unramified regular local ring of mixed characteristic p > 0 p>0 . Let L L denote the quotient field of S S and K = L ⁡ ( ω ) K=L(\omega) with ω p ∈ L \omega^{p}\in L . Let R R denote the integral closure of S S in K K . This paper proves the following curious fact:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(S,\mathfrak{n})
be an unramified regular local ring of mixed characteristic
p>0
and dimension
d
. Let
L
denote the quotient field of
S
and
K=L(\omega)
with
\omega^{p}\in L
. Let
R
denote the integral closure of
S
in
K
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

R
is Cohen-Macaulay.

(2)

R
satisfies Serre’s condition
(S_{3})
.

(3)

R
satisfies the
(S_{3})
condition for all height three primes
Q\in V(p)
.

(4)

R
is a third syzygy over
S
.

(5)

R
is an
n
-th syzygy over
S
for all
1\leq n\leq d
.

Note that the equivalence of (1) and (5) (and the equivalence of (2) and (4)) follow from
[
EG85
, Theorem 3.8]
, which is attributed to
[
AB69
]
. Moreover, (1) implies (2) and (2) implies (3) are obvious; (3) implies (2) also follows by working over
S[1/p]
and applying
[
Sri21
, Proposition 2.4]
. Therefore, the result is about proving (2) implies (1), which we will now do.

Setup 1.2
.

Throughout we will assume notation as in
Theorem

1.1
. We can and will assume
\omega^{p}=f\in S
. Let
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in S
be the divisors of
f
in
S
that satisfy
a_{i}^{2}|f
(there could be none). Let
A
be the hypersurface
S[X]/(X^{p}-f)\simeq S[\omega]\subseteq K
, where
X
is an indeterminate over
S
. Let
\mathfrak{C}
denote the conductor ideal of
A
.

We now record some standard facts. Since the
A
-modules
R
and
\mathfrak{C}^{-1}
are birational, satisfy Serre’s condition
(S_{2})
and agree in codimension one (
A
is Gorenstein), they agree as
A
-submodules of
K
. Moreover, since
A
is
(S_{2})
and Gorenstein in codimension one,
\mathfrak{C}
is unmixed of height one. We then have that
R
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
A/\mathfrak{C}
is Cohen-Macaulay, see for example
[
Sri21
, Proposition 2.11]
.

Now we make some reductions specific to the circumstance. Firstly, we may assume
A
is not normal or equivalently that
\mathfrak{C}
is not the unit ideal since in this case the theorem holds vacuously. Set
F(X):=X^{p}-f
. Since
K/L
is separable,
F^{\prime}(\omega)=p\cdot\omega^{p-1}\in\mathfrak{C}
, see
[
HS06
, Theorem 12.1.1]
. This also implies
p\cdot f\in\mathfrak{C}
. Since
\mathfrak{C}
is unmixed, the associated primes of
\mathfrak{C}
are amongst those of
p
or
f
. Now suppose any of the following hold:

(1)

f
is square free, i.e., there does not exist a divisor
a
of
f
in
S
such that
a^{2}|f
.

(2)

\mathfrak{C}
does not have a primary component containing
p
.

In the case of (1), by
[
Kat99
, Lemma 3.2]
,
R
is Cohen-Macaulay. In the case of (2), by
[
Kat99
, Lemma 3.3 and 3.4]
,
R
is Cohen-Macaulay. Thus, the theorem holds vacuously in either of these cases. Hence, we may and will assume that
f
is not square free and that
\mathfrak{C}
has a primary component containing
p
.

Now let
P
denote the primary component of
\mathfrak{C}
containing
p
(there is a unique one, see the end of section
2
in
[
Kat99
]
) and
J_{i}
the primary component of
\mathfrak{C}
corresponding to
a_{i}
, so that
\mathfrak{C}=P\cap J_{1}\cap\dots\cap J_{n}
. Consider the natural short exact sequence of
A
-modules

(1.3)

0\to A/\mathfrak{C}\to A/P\bigoplus A/(J_{1}\cap\dots\cap J_{n})\to A/(P+J_{1}\cap\dots\cap J_{n})\to 0

Now we note that the proof of
[
Sri21
, Proposition 2.11]
, shows that if
I\subseteq A
is a height one unmixed ideal, then
I^{-1}
is a maximal Cohen-Macaulay
A
-module if and only if
A/I
is a Cohen-Macaulay ring. Combining this with the proof of
[
Kat99
, Lemma 3.2]
shows that
A/P
is Cohen-Macaulay. Moreover,
[
Kat99
, Lemma 3.3 and Lemma 3.4]
imply that
A/(J_{1}\cap\dots\cap J_{n})
is Cohen-Macaulay. Thus,
R
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
A/\mathfrak{C}
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
0pt(A/(P+J_{1}\cap\dots J_{n}))\geq 0pt(A)-2
. Another application of
[
Kat99
, Lemma 3.2, Lemma 3.3 and Lemma 3.4]
shows that
P+J_{1}\cap\dots J_{n}
is of the form
(p,\omega-h,\omega^{g_{s}},\omega^{g_{s-1}}c_{1},\dots,\omega^{g_{1}}c_{s-1},c_{s})
for suitable non-units
h,c_{1},\dots,c_{s}\in S
and integers
g_{1},\dots,g_{s}
. Now note that
X^{p}-f\in(p,X-h)
, see for example the last paragraph of Section 2 in
[
Kat99
]
. Setting

\widetilde{J}:=(p,X-h,X^{g_{s}},X^{g_{s-1}}c_{1},\dots,X^{g_{1}}c_{s-1},c_{s})\subseteq S[X]

we have
A/(P+J_{1}\cap\dots J_{n})\simeq S[X]/(\tilde{J})\simeq\overline{S}/\overline{I}
for
\overline{S}=S/pS
and

\overline{I}=(h^{g_{s}},h^{g_{s-1}}c_{1},\dots,h^{g_{1}}c_{s-1},c_{s})\overline{S}
. Thus,
R
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
0pt(\overline{S}/\overline{I})\geq 0pt(\overline{S})-1
if and only if
\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{\overline{S}}(\overline{S}/\overline{I})\leq 1
if and only if
\overline{I}
is a free
\overline{S}
-module.

Now suppose
R
satisfies
(S_{3})
. Then it satisfies
(S_{3})
as an
S
-module as well. Since the entire process thus far commutes with localization and
S
is catenary, we see that
\overline{I}
is free in codimension
2
over
\overline{S}
, i.e., it is
(S_{2})
over
\overline{S}
. Since
\overline{I}
is torsion free,
\overline{I}
is reflexive in codimension one over
\overline{S}
. Finally, since
\overline{S}
satisfies
(S_{2})
, it follows from
[
BH93
, Proposition 1.4.1]
that
\bar{I}
is a reflexive
\bar{S}
-module. Since
\bar{I}
has rank one and
\bar{S}
is a UFD,
\bar{I}
is free and hence
R
is Cohen-Macaulay. This completes the proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

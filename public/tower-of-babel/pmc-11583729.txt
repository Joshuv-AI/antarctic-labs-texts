Current trends and future implications in the utilization of ChatGPT in nursing: A rapid review
Authors: Manal Kleib, Elizabeth Mirekuwaa Darko, Oluwadamilare Akingbade, Megan Kennedy, Precious Majekodunmi, Emma Nickel, Laura Vogelsang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe past decade has witnessed a surge in the development of artificial intelligence (AI)-based technology systems for healthcare. Launched in November 2022, ChatGPT (Generative Pre-trained Transformer), an AI-based Chatbot, is being utilized in nursing education, research and practice. However, little is known about its pattern of usage, which prompted this study.ObjectiveTo provide a concise overview of the existing literature on the application of ChatGPT in nursing education, practice and research.MethodsA rapid review based on the Cochrane methodology was applied to synthesize existing literature. We conducted systematic searches in several databases, including CINAHL, Ovid Medline, Embase, Web of Science, Scopus, Education Search Complete, ERIC, and Cochrane CENTRAL, to ensure no publications were missed. All types of primary and secondary research studies, including qualitative, quantitative, mixed methods, and literature reviews published in the English language focused on the use of ChatGPT in nursing education, research, and practice, were included. Dissertations or theses, conference proceedings, government and other organizational reports, white papers, discussion papers, opinion pieces, editorials, commentaries, and published review protocols were excluded. Studies involving other healthcare professionals and/or students without including nursing participants were excluded. Studies exploring other language models without comparison to ChatGPT and those examining the technical specifications of ChatGPT were excluded. Data screening was completed in two stages: titles and abstract and full-text review, followed by data extraction and quality appraisal. Descriptive analysis and narrative synthesis were applied to summarize and categorize the findings.ResultsSeventeen studies were included: 15 (88.2 %) focused on nursing education and one each on nursing practice and research. Of the 17 included studies, 5 (29.4 %) were evaluation studies, 3 (17.6 %) were narrative reviews, 3 (17.6 %) were cross-sectional studies, 2 (11.8 %) were descriptive studies, and 1 (5.9 %) was a randomized controlled trial, quasi-experimental study, case study, and qualitative study, respectively.ConclusionThis study has provided a snapshot of ChatGPT usage in nursing education, research, and practice. Although evidence is inconclusive, integration of ChatGPT should consider addressing ethical concerns and ongoing education on ChatGPT usage. Further research, specifically interventional studies, is recommended to ascertain and track the impact of ChatGPT in different contexts.Keywords: ChatGPT, Generative artificial intelligence (Gen AI), Nursing education, Nursing practice, Nursing research, Rapid review

Background

The past decade has witnessed a surge in the development of artificial intelligence (AI)-based technology systems for healthcare. AI can be defined as the multidisciplinary approach of computer science and linguistics that aspires to create machines capable of performing tasks that normally require human intelligence (Sarker, 2022). Such tasks include learning, adapting, rationalizing, and understanding abstract concepts (Korteling et al., 2021). With the introduction of AI comes the development of Generative AI, which also uses deep learning models to generate high-quality text, images, and other content based on the data they were trained on (Martineau, 2023). Large Language Models (LLMs) are AI systems specifically designed to understand and generate human-like language. LLMs learn from data and improve their performance over time through machine and deep learning techniques. LLMs can be used for various natural language processing tasks such as text generation, language translation, sentiment analysis, and text summarization (Open AI, 2024).

While many LLMs are available, the advent of ChatGPT in November 2022 represented a significant milestone by making advanced LLMs widely accessible to the public in an interactive and user-friendly format. Its emergence as a powerful tool across various domains, coupled with its popularity, accessibility, and versatility, has captured the interest of many users, including those in academia, research, and education (Ray, 2023). These factors have contributed to its widespread adoption and extensive use. Following the launch of ChatGPT, various LLMs have been developed, including Cohere, Google Med-PaLM, Amazon's Multimodal-CoT Model, Meta's Llama, Microsoft Kosmos-1, Salesforce Einstein GPT, Anthropic Claude, Google Bard (now Gemini), and Amazone Bedrock (Mckinsey and Company, 2023), to mention a few. ChatGPT (Generative Pre-trained Transformer) is an LLM developed and trained by OpenAI (Open AI, 2024). Launched in November 2022, this Chatbot can understand and generate natural human language to assist with several tasks (e.g., answer questions, provide information, admit its mistakes, reject inappropriate requests) (Open AI, 2024; Miao and Ahn, 2023). The capabilities of the ChatGPT vary from having conversational abilities, coding assistance, information retrieval, and writing assistance to text summarization and generation (OpenAI, 2024). ChatGPT and these other generative AI chatbots have been tested across various domains such as education and training, entertainment, question prediction, appointment scheduling, code debugging and many more. In healthcare, these AI chatbots have been used to provide medical assistance and information, answer medical questions and tasks, and perform various medical evaluations without professional training or enforcement (Antaki et al., 2023; Goktas et al., 2024). The use of ChatGPT in education and training has raised many concerns. Despite the legitimacy of such concerns, including academic dishonesty, ChatGPT and other generative AI tools continue to be utilized in nursing education with promising applications (Gunawon, 2023). For example, ChatGPT has been found to increase collaborative activities, enhance knowledge acquisition, and augment students' individual learning capabilities and clinical reasoning skills (Nalli et al., 2022). Moreover, ChatGPT offers students practical experience in healthcare education while facilitating comprehension of complex concepts—all without requiring the presence of a physical classroom (Alkhaqani, 2024). Also, it can be used to create patient scenarios, allowing nursing students to practice their skills in a safe and controlled environment (Alkhaqani, 2024).

In nursing practice, ChatGPT helps nurses gain insights into topics including healthcare equity, workforce development, patient care and safety (Alkhaqani, 2024). Similarly, Odom-Forren (2023) noted that it has kept nurses abreast of the most recent research and helped them obtain immediate and individualized responses to various healthcare inquiries. Moons and Van Bulck (2024) identified that ChatGPT and Bard could simplify patient information and improve its readability. ChatGPT and other generative AI tools have also been utilized in nursing research to provide equity among researchers, especially those from less-resourced countries whose first language is not English, to reduce editorial costs associated with publications, thereby reducing the disparities among researchers (Lyon, 2023). Lund and Wang (2023) noted that the application of ChatGPT to advance academia and research should be done responsibly and ethically, considering the risks and concerns it entails (Lund and Wang, 2023).

Prior reviews on ChatGPT's application have examined various dimensions of its use. Some reviews focused on medical and healthcare fields using biomedical databases (Ab Razak et al., 2023) and healthcare education and research using primary studies from multiple multidisciplinary databases (Shorey et al., 2024). Ab Razak et al. (2024) noted exponential growth in ChatGPT and chatbot usage in medical and health sciences education, calling for further research and development. Shorey et al. (2024) highlighted limitations in ChatGPT's effectiveness, such as its understanding of specialized medical knowledge and confidentiality risks. However, they concluded that ChatGPT could advance healthcare innovation and promote diversity in education and research. The authors stressed the need for guidelines and regulations for safe implementation. A systematic review by Sallam (2023) emphasized the benefits of ChatGPT in academic writing, including efficient, high-quality text production, improved readability, and accelerated literature reviews, while cautioning about risks like bias and plagiarism. Reviews by Abujaber et al. (2023) and Liu et al. (2023) specifically focused on nursing education. Liu conducted a comprehensive literature review without detailing methodological approaches, while Abujaber used a SWOT framework to assess the potential impact of ChatGPT on nursing education despite not using nursing-focused databases; Yalcinkaya and Cinar Yucel (2024) also focused their study on education using bibliometric and content analysis methods and searched only two databases revealing that international cooperation on the studies on ChatGPT was weak. Similarly, Hobensack et al. (2024) identified that most literature on LLMs in nursing is descriptive, based on commentaries and editorials, with limited empirical evidence to inform practice, education, and research.

Given its growing popularity among students, educators, and researchers, there is a strong interest in ensuring that ChatGPT and other generative AI applications are used within ethically and professionally sound parameters. Despite the significant influence and benefits of these tools, researchers and students face challenges in navigating their use effectively. Considering ChatGPT as an emerging application with evolving capabilities and a potentially significant impact on nursing, there exists an undeniable gap in our understanding of the extent of available empirical evidence on best practices and recommendations for using ChatGPT in nursing. Also, there is a lack of policies that guide professional and ethical conduct regarding using ChatGPT. Therefore, this review aims to explore the available empirical literature on the use of ChatGPT in nursing education, practice, and research and to identify available evidence to inform policy and provide guidance to nursing practice. A rapid review was chosen as an appropriate approach to quickly gather and synthesize evidence to inform decision-making in a timely manner while balancing the need for speed with a level of comprehensiveness and rigor required for research (Wollscheid and Tripney, 2021). Rapid reviews are a form of evidence synthesis that provides timely information for decision-making about emerging topics or interventions (Garritty et al., 2024).

Methods

A rapid review was conducted following the steps outlined by Cochrane Rapid Review Methods Group (Garritty et al., 2024), which included (1) topic refinement - setting the research question, (2) topic refinement - setting the eligibility criteria, (3) searches, (4) study selection, (5) data extraction, (6) risk of bias assessment, and (7) synthesis (Garritty et al., 2024).

Topic refinement - setting the research question

We sought to examine the literature on ChatGPT in nursing education, research, and practice to have a broader understanding of the scope of evidence available to inform policy and guide nursing practice by addressing two main questions:

1.How is ChatGPT being utilized in nursing education, practice, and research?

How is ChatGPT being utilized in nursing education, practice, and research?

2.What are the best practices and recommendations for using ChatGPT in nursing?

What are the best practices and recommendations for using ChatGPT in nursing?

Topic refinement - setting eligibility criteria

This review considered only research studies that focused on using ChatGPT in nursing education, practice and research, irrespective of the reported outcome. All types of study designs, including qualitative, quantitative, mixed methods, and literature reviews, were included. Although ChatGPT became popular recently, the authors set the literature timeline from the databases' inception to February 2024 to identify if prior research existed before the increased attention to ChatGPT. Only studies written in English were included, as the authors only spoke English. No study was excluded based on geographical location, as the authors were interested in understanding the landscape of ChatGPT use in nursing.

Studies that included health sciences students and professionals without mentioning nurses were excluded. In addition, studies that did not use ChatGPT or other language models without comparison to ChatGPT, discussed the technical specifications of ChatGPT, used ChatGPT outside education, research, or practice, or were not primary or secondary research were excluded. The Cochrane methodology for rapid reviews allows for limiting grey literature and supplementary searching (Garritty et al., 2024). Hence, the authors excluded all grey literature sources, including opinion pieces, editorials, commentaries, protocols, dissertations or theses, conference processing, governmental and organizational reports, white papers, and discussion papers.

Searches

A health sciences librarian familiar with rapid review methodology completed a comprehensive systematic search. The following bibliographic databases were searched from inception to February 5, 2024: Medline (1946-present) and EMBASE (1974-present) via OVID; CINAHL (1936-present), ERIC (1966-present), and Education Research Complete (1983-present) via EBSCOhost; Scopus (1976-present); Cochrane CENTRAL trials databases (1993-present) via Wiley; and Web of Science - All Databases (inception-present) via Clarivate. These databases were searched using natural language keywords only; this decision was made to increase the sensitivity and specificity of the search strategy. The search terms were derived from two primary concepts: 1) ChatGPT or generative AI, and 2) nurse, nurses, or nursing. No language, publication date, or study design limits were applied. For full search strategies for each database, see supplementary file 1. Results were exported in complete batches from the databases on February 5th, 2024. The Covidence systematic review software (Veritas Health Innovation, Melbourne, Australia) was used to facilitate the deduplication of the results and manage the screening phases.

Study selection

Two reviewers (E. D & O. A) independently screened abstracts and titles, followed by a full-text screening stage against the predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Conflicts were resolved by involving a third reviewer (M. K). Although a hand search of the included studies was completed to identify any missed articles, no additional article was identified. Decisions for data inclusion and exclusion were documented using the PRISMA diagram. To enhance rigour and minimize bias, a list of excluded studies with reasons for exclusion was created (Supplementary file 2).

Data extraction

A data extraction form was created and piloted to enhance consistency by having two reviewers independently complete the extraction process on three articles. No changes were introduced to the data extraction form. The following information was extracted: article information (author, year, country), focus (nursing education/practice/research), aim, design/sample, findings, benefits, challenges, implication and quality appraisal. A second reviewer checked the extracted information for accuracy and completeness (Garritty et al., 2024).

Risk of bias assessment and quality appraisal

We assessed the included studies regarding risk for bias and appraised the methodological quality of the included studies. Risk for bias assessments are conducted to establish transparency of the evidence reported in randomized controlled trials (RCT). The RoB 2 tool, which requires that the risk of bias is always assessed at the individual outcome measure, was used, thereby ensuring a methodological safeguard (Furuya-Kanamori et al., 2021). Where applicable, the risk for bias evaluation was done separately by two reviewers (E.D & O.A), with one reviewer rating the risk of bias and the other verifying the decisions and judgments made (Garritty et al., 2024). The risk of the overall bias was rated as "some concerns," "low risk," or "high risk" (Higgins et al., 2023).

In addition, we completed a methodological quality appraisal, which refers to the examination of the extent to which researchers conducted their studies to the highest possible standards (Higgins and Green, 2011; King et al., 2022). Different appraisal tools were used, including the Scale for the Assessment of Narrative Review Articles (Baethge et al., 2019), Joanna Briggs Institute critical appraisal tools (Joanna Briggs Institute, n.d.), and Center for Evidence-Based Management tools (Center for Evidence-Based Management, n.d.).

Data analysis and synthesis

Data were analyzed using narrative synthesis and tabular or graphical display. Descriptive statistics were used to describe the study characteristics, which were summarized through text and graphs. Findings were presented narratively.

Results

Search results

The database search yielded 476 records. Of these, 267 were removed as duplicates, leaving 209 for title and abstract screening. Subsequently, 166 records were excluded for irrelevance, and 43 underwent full-text screening. Of these, 26 records were excluded for various reasons, resulting in 17 primary studies included in this review (Fig. 1 the PRISMA Chart).

PRISMA flow chart.

Study characteristics

Table 1 describes the characteristics of the included studies. The included studies were published between 2023 and 2024, with the majority, n = 11 (64.7 %) in 2023 and n = 6 (35.3 %) in 2024. The majority of the studies, n = 13 (76.5 %), were from Asian countries, n = 3 (17.6 %) was from North America, and n = 1 (5.9 %) was from Europe (Fig. 2 shows the country of publication for all included studies). According to the World Bank Classification, no study was conducted in a low-income or lower-middle-income country. All the studies were from high-income and upper-middle-income countries (World Bank, 2023). Most studies, n = 15 (88.2 %) focused on nursing education, n = 1 (5.9 %) focused on nursing research, and n = 1 (5.9 %) focused on nursing practice. Of the 17 included studies, n = 5 (29.4 %) were evaluation studies, n = 3 (17.6 %) were narrative reviews, n = 3 (17.6 %) were cross-sectional studies, n = 2 (11.8 %) were descriptive studies, n = 1 (5.9 %) was a randomized controlled trial (RCT), n = 1 (5.9 %) was a quasi-experimental study, n = 1 (5.9 %) was a case study, and n = 1 (5.9 %) was a qualitative study. Out of the 17 included studies, n = 12 (70.6 %) was rated moderate, n = 3 (17.6 %) high, n = 2 (11.8 %), n = 1 (5.9 %) low, and the RCT, n = 1(5.9 %) was rated high for risk for bias.

Review findings

This review provides an overview of the available empirical studies on ChatGPT in nursing education, research, and practice and the methodological quality of studies. Fourteen primary studies (Alanzi, 2023; Chang et al., 2024; Cox et al., 2023; Dağci et al., 2024; Goktas et al., 2024; Hsu, 2024; Huang, 2023; Kaneda et al., 2023; Luo et al., 2023; Quattrini et al., 2024; Taira et al., 2023; Tang et al., 2023; Vaughn et al., 2024; Zong, 2024) and three narrative literature reviews (Abujaber et al., 2023; Ab Razak et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023) were included in this review.

Study focus

Fifteen studies explored the use of ChatGPT in nursing education (Abujaber et al., 2023; Ab Razak et al., 2023; Chang et al., 2024; Cox et al., 2023; Dağci et al., 2024; Goktas et al., 2024; Hsu, 2024; Huang, 2023; Kaneda et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023; Luo et al., 2023; Quattrini et al., 2024; Taira et al., 2023; Vaughn et al., 2024; Zong, 2024), one study focused on its use in nursing practice (Alanzi, 2023) and another study focused on nursing research (Tang et al., 2023). Four studies on nursing education examined ChatGPT's performance in professional examinations (Huang, 2023; Kaneda et al., 2023; Taira et al., 2023; Zong et al., 2024) while one study (Cox et al., 2023) compared the National Council Licensure Examination (NCLEX) registered nurse (RN) examination questions generated by ChatGPT with those created by humans. One study conducted a SWOT analysis of integrating ChatGPT in nursing education (Abujaber et al., 2023), while another study (Liu et al., 2023) examined the challenges and benefits of its usage in nursing education. Chang et al. (2024) explored learner efficiency and satisfaction when using ChatGPT compared to traditional teaching methods. Other studies addressed various aspects, such as the quality of nursing care plan texts generated by ChatGPT (Dagcı et al., 2024), the demand for ChatGPT among Chinese nursing students (Luo et al., 2023), and the use of ChatGPT-generated clinical data in two Doctor of Nursing Practice courses. Some focused on the application of ChatGPT in developing simulation scenarios (Vaughn et al., 2024), creating guidelines for prompt learning (Goktas et al., 2024), and mastering medical terminologies (Hsu, 2024). Additionally, Ab Razak et al. (2023) investigated the use of chatbot models in teaching and learning. Of the three included reviews that addressed ChatGPT application, two studies (Abujaber et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023) were focused on nursing education and one study (Ab Razak et al., 2023) was related to medical education and health science.

Only one study in this review focused on nursing practice, examining the impact of ChatGPT on teleconsultants’ operations (Alanzi, 2023). Another study in the review reported on the paucity of studies reporting the actual deployment of ChatGPT in the clinical setting, as most were focused on medical education (Liu et al., 2023). Furthermore, only one study focused on nursing research investigating the declaration of generative AI in nursing academic research journals (Tang et al., 2023). The disparity in research and clinical practice may be attributed to the novelty of ChatGPT and the initial reactions to its use, which are being challenged by educators worldwide. Despite the growing interest in ChatGPT utilization and its influence on nursing, there are limited studies assessing its application in nursing.

Study participants

Only a few studies included in the review involved participants. Apart from some studies having directly targeted healthcare professionals, including nurses (Alanzi, 2023), nurse educators (Cox et al., 2023), and or nursing students (Hsu, 2024; Luo et al., 2023; Quattrini et al., 2024) as the population of interest, other studies used other means such as text (Dağci et al., 2024), questions (Kaneda et al., 2023; Zong, 2024) as the preferred mode to ascertain the capabilities of ChatGPT within nursing education. Of the studies that involved nursing students, only Quattrini et al. (2024) recruited Doctor of Nursing Practice students for their study. Although not specifically stated in Chang et al. (2024) whether the nursing learners were undergraduate or graduate students, this was the only study that focused on nursing learners. This shows the limited involvement of nursing students at the undergraduate or graduate levels in ChatGPT studies.

Methodological quality of included studies

The methodological quality and risk of assessment were assessed based on the study design and ranked using the appropriate appraisal checklist (Table 1). From the studies, some reviews did not explicitly present the methods used to select the included studies, quality appraisal of included studies, and detailed data abstraction, synthesis and analysis (Abujaber et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023). Similarly, the qualitative study (Alanzi, 2023) did not present a philosophical perspective and its connection with the research methodology. Also, the study did not account for the influence of the researchers on the study. The evaluation studies (Dagcı et al., 2024; Kaneda et al., 2023; Taira et al., 2023; Tang et al., 2023; Zong et al., 2024) and cross-sectional studies (Cox et al., 2023; Luo et al., 2023; Vaughn et al., 2024) did not clearly identify confounders alongside strategies taken to address them. The RCT had a high risk of bias as no allocation concealment or blinding was done, with no analysis to correct for likely bias in the study (Hsu, 2024).

Benefits of ChatGPT in nursing education, practice and research

Nearly all the studies highlighted the benefits of ChatGPT for nursing. These insights varied from study to study, but the authors generally alluded to ChatGPT's benefits to the nursing discipline, with a significant number reported in nursing education. While some studies were explicitly clear on the benefits observed from using the ChatGPT application for their studies, others were unclear about the sources as indicated in their studies.

To nursing educators, ChatGPT can be useful in developing simulation scenarios, which could save them time (Vaughn et al., 2024) or in developing virtual patient simulation exercises (Cox et al., 2023) for teaching students. To ascertain the capabilities of ChatGPT in developing examination questions, Cox et al. (2023) compared the NCLEX developed by ChatGPT and nursing educators and found that ChatGPT was quick in developing the questions. Likewise, Dagcı et al. (2024) also found the ChatGPT application useful for generating quick nursing care plans, developing and enhancing assessment modalities and providing feedback (Chang et al., 2024), and enhancing the structure of academic papers (Cox et al., 2023).

In addition, the ChatGPT application offers students an adaptive and personalized learning experience based on their needs (Chang et al., 2024). This adaptive and personalized approach provides a more effective and engaging educational experience, ensuring that students receive the most relevant and supportive educational experience through a chatbot. Using plug-in features adds specific capabilities to an application; hence, this additional plug-in feature by the ChatGPT application (Cox et al., 2023) improves its efficiency and functionality. ChatGPT can be used to assist students in their learning process (Hsu, 2024) and evaluate the level of thinking among students (Quattrini et al., 2024). To further show the wide use of ChatGPT in nursing education, in a narrative review by Abujaber et al. (2023) on SWOT analysis on the impact of integrating ChatGPT into nursing education, the findings indicated that ChatGPT improved students’ learning and provided nursing students with various nursing tools and materials alongside real-time reactions and personalized feedback. Similarly, evidence from the study by Chang et al. (2024) suggests that ChatGPT might provide better outcomes than traditional learning methods. An RCT by Hsu (2024) confirmed that nursing students who utilized ChatGPT and Termbot in learning medical terminologies performed better than those who studied through textbooks. Another quasi-experimental study found that nursing students who utilized ChatGPT had better critical thinking and problem-solving abilities compared to their counterparts who utilized textbooks (Chang et al., 2024). Furthermore, Abujaber et al. (2023) affirmed that ChatGPT can improve remote education for rural and remote students, facilitate global collaboration between nurse academics and students, and integrate blended learning with traditional nursing education.

In exploring the efficacy of ChatGPT in passing the Japanese National Nursing Examination (JNNE), Kaneda et al. (2023) concluded that as the ChatGPT4 passed the JNNE, the ChatGPT could assist clinicians in diagnosis and determining treatment plans. Regardless of its application, ChatGPT can assist in the decision-making process (Kanada et al., 2023) due to its capability in content generation for medical and clinical knowledge (Zong, 2024). Notably, there is conflicting evidence regarding the performance of ChatGPT in board examinations. Although it passed the JNNE examination (Kaneda et al., 2023) and the Taiwan Registered Nurse License Exam (Huang, 2023), it failed the Chinese National Nurse Licensing Examination, National Medical Licensing Examination, and the National Pharmacist Licensing Examination (Zong et al., 2024). Similarly, Luo et al. (2023) reported that ChatGPT may improve the employability of nursing students.

Alanzi (2023) reported that ChatGPT could be useful in various aspects of healthcare, including diagnostic and informational support, personalization of care, decision-making, and documentation. Notably, this study included various health professionals, not only nurses, which suggests that the impact of ChatGPT use in nursing practice remains unclear. Tang et al. (2023) identified some benefits of using ChatGPT in research, such as aiding the writing process, generating initial drafts, and offering suggestions for improving subsequent drafts. They also noted that ChatGPT could assist in generating ideas, expanding the scope of scholarly writing, and fostering innovative thinking.

Challenges associated with the ChatGPT application

Most studies that reported on benefits also reported the challenges. For studies that reported the challenges associated with ChatGPT, these challenges are grouped under nursing education and practice. Some studies noted challenges concerning academic integrity, including plagiarism, providing inaccurate information and references (Dagcı et al., 2024; Goktas et al., 2024), and in some cases, the output from ChatGPT could lead to “hallucination or confusion” (Huang, 2023). In one study (Quattrini et al., 2024), educators indicated hesitancy in incorporating ChatGPT in nursing education because it transcends an ethical issue, leading to data security concerns, among others (Goktas et al., 2024). One study warned educators about using chatbots, including ChatGPT (Hsu, 2024).

Other challenges highlighted are the lack of knowledge, use and access to ChatGPT (Luo et al., 2023), providing inaccurate or misleading information (Taira et al., 2023), reporting outdated practices or guidelines or giving broad information or suggestions (Vaughn et al., 2024). Miscalculation and interpretation of laboratory values and limited timeframe of information generated (Cox et al., 2023) are also noted in these studies. According to Abujaber et al. (2023), students might become overly reliant on ChatGPT, hindering critical thinking and problem-solving. Within nursing practice, challenges that may exist relate to misdiagnosis, limited medical knowledge, and ethical and legal issues regarding the use of ChatGPT (Alanzi, 2023).

Best practices and recommendations for using ChatGPT in nursing

Of note is that most of the studies did not provide enough information or direction on how the ChatGPT platform was applied in their research. This can be attributed to the methodological design used for the studies, as these studies did not comprehensively provide enough details on the study design. Only six studies provided strategies and practices that guided their engagement with the ChatGPT platform that can be useful to nurses and nursing students in navigating the ChatGPT platform. In the included studies, one study (Kaneda et al., 2023) suggested filling out new forms and updating applications each time ChatGPT provides an answer, as ChatGPT learns from context and previous responses or answers, which may influence the next response provided. In addition, creating different scenarios to assess ChatGPT's ability to adapt to differences within a context (Quattrini et al., 2024). One study (Chang et al., 2024) identified the use of a framework - Clarify, Ideate, Develop, and Implement a problem-solving model-based framework to be incorporated into the ChatGPT application to guide learners as they learn nursing knowledge. Some authors stated the use of prompt engineering (Taira et al., 2023), prompt learning (Goktas et al., 2024), and direct instruction strategy via prompt (Zong, 2024) to guide the use of the ChatGPT. Although the literature is limited in providing best practices and recommendations, Liu et al. (2023) and Abujaber et al. (2023), in their reviews, provided some future directions and recommendations, respectively, on the ChatGPT (Table 2).

Discussion

In this review, the authors aimed to explore the available empirical literature on the use of ChatGPT in nursing education, practice, and research. Although many publications exist on the use of ChatGPT in nursing, much of this published literature is in the form of editorials, commentaries, and essays. Although this review sought to synthesize the empirical literature, the current evidence to support the use of ChatGPT in nursing education, research, and practice remains inconclusive due to the heterogeneity of the studies identified and the small number of empirical literature available. Despite that, these studies offer important insights to provide preliminary guidance on the prospects of ChatGPT and its applications in nursing.

A significant finding of this review is the geographic distribution of studies, with no publications originating from low- or lower-middle-income countries. This finding is similar to Yalcinkaya and Cinar Yucel's (2024) study, in which the majority of countries included in their study were from upper-middle-income countries and above, except for one lower-middle-income country. This shows the inadequate publications emerging from these countries, which could be related to funding issues, limited collaborations, networking opportunities, infrastructural challenges, resource constraints and educational and skills gaps (Akingbade et al., 2023; Sharma et al., 2023). These barriers may hinder the conduct and dissemination of research on AI tools like ChatGPT in these regions, pointing to the need for more inclusive research efforts that bridge these gaps.

Our review also indicated that most studies focused on nursing education, consistent with Hobensack et al. (2024) and Yalcinkaya and Cinar Yucel's (2024) studies. Although Hobensack et al.'s (2024) review included grey literature and almost an equal number of included studies focused on education and practice, the findings are equally relevant. While the emphasis on education is important, it raises questions about the relative scarcity of studies on ChatGPT's application in nursing practice and research. According to Liu et al. (2023), there is a scarcity of studies on ChatGPT's deployment in clinical settings. While studies have shown that AI is useful in personalized medicine, prediction of disease risk and outcome, prediction of falls in the hospital and streamlining the clinical workflow (Chen and Xu, 2023; Salam, 2023), only one study in this review focused on nursing practice (Alanzi, 2023). Ruksakulpiwat et al. (2024) argued that AI integration into nursing care should be at the forefront due to nurses’ critical role in the patient care continuum from admission to discharge. In line with that, Woodnutt et al. (2024) observed that AI could be used to write nursing care plans. However, the authors noted that although ChatGPT's performance could appear logical to non-specialists, it may contain substantial errors and ethical concerns. This suggests that more research is needed to substantiate the use of ChatGPT in writing nursing care plans. Furthermore, Ahmed (2023) believes that ChatGPT can provide nurses with immediate access to details regarding medications, therapies, and medical interventions. Scherr et al. (2023) and Dave et al. (2023) stated that ChatGPT is used by healthcare professionals, including medical students and nurses, to stay updated on developments, identify research topics, and assist in clinical and laboratory diagnosis.

The paucity of studies on ChatGPT usage in nursing practice may stem from several factors. First, the rapid evolution of technology often outpaces research and practice integration, resulting in delayed exploration and application in clinical settings (Mumtaz et al., 2023). Additionally, the focus on nursing education may reflect a broader trend where foundational knowledge and training are prioritized over practical application (Hakim, 2023). This educational emphasis is crucial for preparing future practitioners but can inadvertently sideline the exploration of how technologies like ChatGPT can enhance clinical practice and research. Furthermore, integrating new technologies in clinical settings may face barriers such as resistance to change, lack of infrastructure, and the need for evidence-based validation (Greenhalgh et al., 2017). Additionally, the perceived challenges and ethical considerations of integrating AI into clinical settings may have slowed its adoption in nursing practice. Similarly, there have been concerns about substantial errors and ethical issues in integrating AI into nursing practice (Farhud and Zokaei, 2021). These concerns underscore the need for more research to validate ChatGPT's role in nursing practice and address its ethical and practical challenges. Furthermore, other challenges can be mitigated by encouraging funding agencies and research institutions to support studies focusing specifically on the practical applications of emerging technologies in clinical settings, fostering collaborations between educational institutions and healthcare facilities, and raising awareness about the potential benefits of technologies like ChatGPT in nursing practice.

The advantages of ChatGPT in nursing education are well-documented. In a systematic review, Gunawan et al. (2024) identified that ChatGPT has been utilized in nursing education for academic writing, healthcare simulations, data modeling, and personal development. Similarly, another review by Albadarin et al. (2024) on ChatGPT affirmed thatChatGPT, improved students’ learning process, supported their learning approaches, enhanced their competencies, and contributed to their academic success (Albadarin et al., 2024).

Students engage with ChatGPT for different reasons, such as seeking assistance with academic writing or generating ideas on various topics. This review's findings revealed the associated benefits of the ChatGPT application among nursing students, consistent with studies conducted by Farrokhnia et al. (2023). As reiterated by O'Connor and ChatGPT (2023), AI chatbots used in nursing education can provide students with personalized and interactive learning experiences that can foster their learning. In addition, ChatGPT can support nurse educators in enhancing the learning needs of students and promoting engagement and student assessment (O'Connor et al., 2023). Despite students being aware of the limitations and challenges associated with ChatGPT, they have positive perceptions of ChatGPT in education, which drives their interest in using it for their academic work (Das and Madhusudan, 2024). Although there was one study on ChatGPT use by graduate students Quattrini et al. (2024), it would be valuable to compare the levels of engagement between undergraduate and graduate students and assess their cognitive function at each level and their ability to interpret ChatGPT output. Also, the limited number of studies involving student participants might be due to the design and capabilities of the ChatGPT application, which allows for evaluation without exclusively depending on human input. Another area where ChatGPT has been accessed and evaluated in nursing education is developing national examination. Based on the included studies, the findings show an inconsistency in ChatGPT's accuracy in developing and accessing the accuracy of questions of the national board examinations. However, ChatGPT 4.0 produced a higher accuracy rate than ChatGPT 3.5. These findings align with Wang et al. (2024), where ChatGPT 4.0 version outperformed ChatGPT 3.5 version on clinical nurse's performance in tracheostomy care. Considering the disparity in the accuracy of the ChatGPT, it should be used with caution, and humans should be used to verify the output.

Evidence from this rapid review also indicates a significant gap in empirical studies exploring the impact of ChatGPT on nursing research, as only one study focused on this area (Tang et al., 2023). This paucity of empirical research does not necessarily indicate a lack of interest or use among nursing researchers; rather, it may reflect the nascent stage of AI integration into research methodologies. Lyon (2023) observed that ChatGPT could assist with the writing process, improving efficiency and accuracy and could "speed up" the dissemination of research and promote the equity of authors. Similarly, Dai et al. (2023) noted that ChatGPT in research can enhance research quality, improve scholarly development and professional skills, enhance critical thinking, and increase student confidence and autonomy (Dai et al., 2023). Furthermore, researchers can utilize ChatGPT to clarify their research focus and formulate specific questions (Eltaybani, 2023). Many scholars have embraced the use of the ChatGPT application to structure and assist in writing their manuscripts (Picazo-Sanchez, and Ortiz-Martin, 2024). A SWOT analysis by Giray et al. (2024) on ChatGPT usage in scientific research identified the strengths of the ChatGPT applications as the vast knowledge base, language proficiency, information retrieval, and the capacity for continuous learning; weaknesses were the lack of contextual understanding, potential overreliance on training data, limitations in verifying information, and limited critical thinking abilities; opportunities include facilitating literature reviews, fostering collaborative brainstorming, enabling seamless language translation and interpretation, and amplifying knowledge dissemination and threats include concerns related to plagiarism, ethical issues, misinformation, and even the potential erosion of higher-order cognitive thinking. Farrokhnia et al. (2023) identified similar findings in their SWOT analysis and observed that focusing on the opportunities and strengths while minimizing the weaknesses and threats of ChatGPT should be the way forward. In addition, Lyon (2023) affirmed that chatbots could hinder critical thinking and lead to users copying information without proper attribution. Likewise, Miao and Ahn (2023) noted that concerns about plagiarism have led to ChatGPT being banned from numerous institutions. These issues underscore Rusandi et al. (2023) argument that ChatGPT should complement, rather than replace, human writing to preserve critical thinking skills. ChatGPT has several potential applications in the nursing and healthcare fields. However, these applications come with several limitations and ethical considerations that pose challenges. The evidence aligns with the study of Alsadhan et al. (2023), which identified training data leading to bias, ethical issues, technical limitations, and limitations in data collection and analysis as challenges to adopting ChatGPT in medical research.

Implications

The findings of this rapid review underscore the increasing significance of ChatGPT in nursing education, research, and practice, while also highlighting several challenges and gaps that must be addressed for its effective integration. Currently, ChatGPT is predominantly utilized in nursing education, with limited exploration of its potential in clinical practice and scarce empirical research evaluating its use in nursing research. This trend presents an opportunity to broaden its application beyond educational settings, thereby enhancing nursing practice and supporting research endeavors.

For the future, it is imperative that nursing educators, policymakers, and practitioners collaborate to establish clear guidelines and policies for the responsible use of ChatGPT. This includes providing comprehensive training to ensure that nursing professionals and students are well-equipped to use this technology effectively and ethically. Moreover, the ethical concerns surrounding the use of AI in nursing, such as issues of accuracy, bias, and the potential erosion of critical thinking skills, must be proactively addressed through interdisciplinary collaboration and ongoing dialogue

In terms of research gaps, there is a pressing need for more empirical studies that examine the impact of ChatGPT in nursing practice and research. The paucity of such studies does not imply a lack of interest or use but highlights the need for more research, particularly interventional studies, to substantiate its benefits and limitations. Additionally, given the limited number of studies from low-income and lower-middle-income countries, future research should aim to explore the usage and implications of ChatGPT in these contexts to ensure equitable access and benefit from AI technologies in nursing globally.

Furthermore, as LLMs like ChatGPT continue to evolve, it is crucial to forecast their long-term impact on the nursing profession. This includes identifying which AI capabilities can augment nursing practice and determining how best to integrate these tools into clinical workflows while maintaining the humanistic aspects of care. Nursing institutions and organizations should take a proactive role in shaping the future of AI in nursing by providing ongoing education, establishing best practices, and promoting continuous research to keep pace with technological advancements.

Strengths and limitations

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first rapid review which has focused on empirical literature to determine the potential impact of ChatGPT on nursing education, research, and practice. The authors conducted an extensive search in multiple databases and hand-searched the references of included studies to ensure there was no missing literature. This review's findings add to the literature on ChatGPT and its applications in nursing. Despite the strength of this review, the review has some limitations. The review may have missed important findings due to excluding non-English papers, and studies published after the initial search, which could have contributed to its findings. The use of broad search terms may have influenced the identification of relevant literature, specifically in relation to nursing practice and research. However, the volume of empirical studies available is congruent with the time span since the release of ChatGPT and the fact that most published literature available is not based on primary data collection. The study design, being a rapid review, is inherently less extensive than systematic reviews. Therefore, the findings should be interpreted with caution. Also, as we identified methodological deficiencies such as lack of allocation concealment, blinding, identification of confounders and analysis to correct for likely bias, it is imperative to consider these constraints when interpreting these findings.

Conclusion

The current evidence available on using ChatGPT in nursing education is limited and heterogeneous. Furthermore, there are limited studies that focus on the use of ChatGPT in research and practice. However, ChatGPT offers a wide range of benefits. ChatGPT and other chatbots using LLMs will be available and likely utilized by nursing students and professionals in various contexts, including nursing education, research, and practice, and for various purposes. There should be strategies and guidelines to ensure that healthcare professionals, including nurses, engage with these platforms safely, competently, and ethically, without compromising integrity and safety and within the scope of their practice. Hence, providing guidance and guidelines on assessing these applications is crucial. Addressing the challenges and ensuring proper use, the integration of ChatGPT can significantly contribute to advancing the nursing profession. Since the area of ChatGPT is novel and evolving, developing foundational knowledge through education on the prospects and potential risks is paramount. More research is needed in both high and low- or lower-middle-income countries in relation to the use of ChatGPT in all domains of nursing. Future research using different methods, and specifically interventional studies that directly measure the impact of ChatGPT in different contexts, is recommended.

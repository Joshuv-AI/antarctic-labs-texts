Abstract
We consider the system of elastic waves with critical space dependent damping V ⁡ ( x ) V(x) . We study the Cauchy problem for this model in the 2 2 -dimensional Euclidean space 𝐑 2 {\bf R}^{2} , and we obtain faster decay rates of the total energy as time goes to infinity. In the 2 2 -D case we do not have any suitable Hardy type inequality, so generally one has no idea to establish optimal energy decay. We develope a special type of multiplier method combined with some estimates brought by the 2 2 -D Newton potential belonging to the usual Laplacian − Δ -\Delta , not the operator − a 2 Δ − ( b 2 − a 2 ) ∇ div -a^{2}\Delta-(b^{2}-a^{2})\nabla{\rm div}\ itself. The property of finite speed propagation is important to get results for this system.

\displaystyle u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\ \ \ x\in{\bf R}^{2},

\left|\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}(u_{1}(x)+V(x)u_{0}(x))\cdot v(t,x)dx\right|=\left|\int_{|x|\leq 2L+bt}(u_{1}(x)+V(x)u_{0}(x))\cdot v(t,x)dx\right|

=\left|-\int_{|x|\leq 2L+bt}\Delta h(x)\cdot v(t,x)dx\right|=\left|\int_{|x|\leq 2L+bt}\nabla h(x)\cdot\nabla v(t,x)dx\right|

\leq 2\int_{|x|\leq 2L+bt}|\nabla h(x)||\nabla v(t,x)|dx

\leq C_{\varepsilon}\int_{|x|\leq 2L+bt}|\nabla h(x)|^{2}dx+\varepsilon\int_{|x|\leq 2L+bt}|\nabla v(t,x)|^{2}dx

\leq C_{\varepsilon}\int_{|x|\leq 2L+bt}|\nabla h(x)|^{2}dx+\varepsilon\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}|\nabla v(t,x)|^{2}dx

(2.17)

with some parameter
\varepsilon>0
and a constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
depending only on
\varepsilon>0
, where one has just used the facts that
v(t,x)=0
, and
|\nabla h(x)|<+\infty
for
|x|=2L+bt
for the boundary integral. Note that

\int_{|x|\leq 2L+bt}|\nabla h(x)|^{2}dx<+\infty

for each
t\geq 0
because of
h\in C^{2}({\bf R}^{2},{\bf R}^{2})
. These arguments imply the desired estimate.

\Box

Let us estimate the first term of the right hand side of Lemma
2.6
by using Lemma
2.5
. For this purpose we set

I_{h}:=\int_{|x|\leq 2L}|\nabla h(x)|^{2}dx.

(2.18)

First, we see that

\int_{|x|\leq 2L+bt}|\nabla h(x)|^{2}dx=I_{h}+\int_{2L\leq|x|\leq 2L+bt}|\nabla h(x)|^{2}dx.

(2.19)

Thus, by using polar coordinates from Lemma
2.5
it follows that

\int_{2L\leq|x|\leq 2L+bt}|\nabla h(x)|^{2}dx\leq C^{2}\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\int_{2L\leq|x|\leq 2L+bt}\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}dx

=2\pi C^{2}\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\int_{2L}^{2L+bt}\frac{r}{r^{2}}dr

=2\pi C^{2}\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\left(\log(2L+bt)-\log(2L)\right)

\leq 2\pi C^{2}\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\log(2L+bt)\quad(t\geq 0).

(2.20)

Therefore, by (
2.19
) and (
2.20
) one has arrived at the crucial estimate:

Lemma 2.7

It holds that

\int_{|x|\leq 2L+bt}|\nabla h(x)|^{2}dx\leq I_{h}+2\pi C^{2}\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\log(2L+bt)\quad(t\geq 0).

Finally, let us estimate
I_{h}
in terms of the initial velocity
u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}
. Note that generally it holds that

\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}|\nabla h(x)|^{2}dx=+\infty.

It is important to point out that the integral region of the quantity
I_{h}
is localized to
|x|\leq 2L
as is defined in (
2.18
). Of course, we know that its integral value of
I_{h}
is finite because of
h\in C^{2}({\bf R}^{2},{\bf R}^{2})
, but we will try to suppress it by a certain quantity of initial velocity from above.

Thus, it follows from (
2.14
) and Lemmas
2.6
and
2.7
that

\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}|v_{t}(t,x)|^{2}dx+\left(\frac{a^{2}}{2}-\varepsilon\right)\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}|\nabla v(t,x)|^{2}dx

+\frac{(b^{2}-a^{2})}{2}\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}({\rm div}\ v(t,x))^{2}dx+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}V(x)|v_{s}(s,x)|^{2}dxds

\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}|u_{0}(x)|^{2}dx+C_{\varepsilon}I_{h}+2\pi C_{\varepsilon}C^{2}\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\log(2L+bt).

(2.21)

For the quantity
I_{h}
one can get the following result.

Lemma 2.8

It hods that

I_{h}=\int_{|x|\leq 2L}|\nabla h(x)|^{2}dx\leq C_{L}\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2},\quad(t\geq 0),

where
C_{L}>0
is a constant depending on
L>0
.

Proof.
The check here is also initially performed when
h(x)
and the initial value
u_{j}
(
j=0,1
) is a real valued, as follows.

For an arbitrarily fixed
x_{0}\in\overline{B_{2L}(0)}
, from the definition of
h(x)
in (
2.15
) one can easily arrive at the intermediate estimate:

|\nabla h(x_{0})|\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}\frac{|u_{1}(y)+V(y)u_{0}(y)|}{|x_{0}-y|}dy=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|y|\leq L}\frac{|u_{1}(y)+V(y)u_{0}(y)|}{|x_{0}-y|}dy

=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\overline{B_{L}(x_{0})}}\frac{|u_{1}(x_{0}-z)+V(x_{0}-z)u_{0}(x_{0}-z)|}{|z|}dz

\leq\frac{\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}}{2\pi}\int_{\overline{B_{L}(x_{0})}}\frac{1}{|z|}dz.

(2.22)

It should be noticed that in the case of
x_{0}\in\overline{B_{2L}(0)}
, we have
\overline{B_{L}(x_{0})}\subset\overline{B_{4L}(0)}
sufficiently. Thus, we get

\int_{\overline{B_{L}(x_{0})}}\frac{1}{|z|}dz\leq\int_{\overline{B_{4L}(0)}}\frac{1}{|z|}dz=8\pi L.

(2.23)

Put
x_{0}
back into
x
to get the following by (
2.22
) and (
2.23
).

|\nabla h(x)|\leq 4L\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\quad\forall x\in\overline{B_{2L}(0)}.

Therefore, one can obtain the desired estimate with some constant
C_{L}>0
depending only on
L
. By a simple calculation, the exact same estimate holds for vector-valued
h(x)
and initial values
u_{j}
(
j=0,1
).

\Box

By combining these estimates developed in (
2.21
) and Lemma
2.8
, and by choosing
\varepsilon>0
small enough in (
2.21
), one can arrive at the crucial estimate because of
v_{t}=u
. Note that at this stage one must assume
a>0
necessarily.

Proposition 2.1

There exists a constant
C>0
depending on
L>0
such that

\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}|u(t,x)|^{2}dx+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}V(x)|u(s,x)|^{2}dxds

\leq C\left(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\log(2L+bt)\right)

for all
t\geq 0
.

Remark 2.2

By the results from
[
14
]
for scalar valued wave equation with
a=b
and
V(x)=0
, it seems that the
\log
-order growth rate obtained in Proposition
2.1
may be optimal in the two dimensional case. Note that
V(x)=0
case can be admitted at this stage, that is, for the solution
w(t,x)
satisfying

\displaystyle w_{tt}-a^{2}\Delta w-(b^{2}-a^{2})\nabla{\rm div}\ w=0,\ \ \ (t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times{\bf R}^{2},

(2.24)

\displaystyle w(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad w_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\ \ \ x\in{\bf R}^{2},

(2.25)

it follows from the proof of Proposition
2.1
that

\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}|w(t,x)|^{2}dx\leq C\left(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\log(2L+bt)\right),\quad(t\gg 1)

with some constant
C>0
. Incidentally, the discussion here is also directly related to the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Now, we use the assumption (
1.4
) (which means
V(x)>0
). Indeed, it should be pointed out that from (
1.4
) and (
1.6
) one has

\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}V(x)|u(s,x)|^{2}dxds=\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{|x|\leq L+bs}V(x)|u(s,x)|^{2}dxds

\geq V_{0}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{|x|\leq L+bs}(1+|x|)^{-1}|u(s,x)|^{2}dxds\geq C^{*}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}\frac{V_{0}}{1+s}|u(s,x)|^{2}dxds,\quad t_{0}\gg 1,

(2.26)

where

C^{*}:=\frac{1}{\max\{(1+L),b\}}.

Thus, the last term of (
2.10
) can be absorbed into the quantity
\displaystyle{\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}}V(x)|u(s,x)|^{2}dxds
, so that it follows from (
2.10
) and Proposition
2.1
that

f(t)E_{u}(t)+g(t)\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}(u(t,x)\cdot u_{t}(t,x))dx\leq C(t_{0})

+\max\{\frac{C_{2}}{2},\frac{C_{3}}{2}\}C\left(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\log(2L+bt)\right)

+\frac{C_{1}C}{2V_{0}^{2}C^{*}}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\log(2L+bt)\right)\quad(t\geq t_{0}).

This implies the following lemma.

Lemma 2.9

Undet the assumption as in Theorem
1.1
for
t\geq t_{0}\gg 1
it holds that

f(t)E_{u}(t)+g(t)\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}(u(t,x)\cdot u_{t}(t,x))dx

\leq C(t_{0})+C^{**}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\log(2L+bt)\right)

with some universal constant
C^{**}>0
depending only on
V_{0}
,
L
,
a
and
b
.

Let us finalize the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
completed.
First, by choosing
t_{0}>0
large enough, it follows from Proposition
2.1
that

\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}|u(t,x)|^{2}dx\leq C\left(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\right)\log(2L+bt)

for all
t\geq t_{0}
. Set

A_{0}:=C\left(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\right).

Then, from Lemma
2.9
and (
1.3
) one can get the estimate

f(t)E_{u}(t)\leq 2g(t)\left(\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}|u(t,x)|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}E_{u}(t)^{1/2}

+\,C(t_{0})+\,C^{**}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\log(2L+bt)\right)

\leq 2g(t)E_{u}(t)^{1/2}A_{0}^{1/2}\sqrt{\log(2L+bt)}+\,C(t_{0})

+\,C^{**}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\log(2L+bt)\right)

\leq 2\frac{g(t)}{\sqrt{f(t)}}\sqrt{f(t)E_{u}(t)}A_{0}^{1/2}\sqrt{\log(2L+bt)}+\,C(t_{0})

+\,C^{**}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\log(2L+bt)\right)

\leq 2A_{0}\frac{g(t)^{2}}{f(t)}\log(2L+bt)+\frac{1}{2}f(t)E_{u}(t)+\,C(t_{0})

+\,C^{**}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\log(2L+bt)\right),

which implies the following estimate:

\frac{1}{2}f(t)E_{u}(t)\leq 2A_{0}\frac{g(t)^{2}}{f(t)}\log(2L+bt)+\,C(t_{0})

+\,C^{**}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\log(2L+bt)\right),

so that one has the crucial estimate in terms of the functions
f(t)
and
g(t)
:

E_{u}(t)\leq 4A_{0}\frac{g(t)^{2}}{f(t)^{2}}\log(2L+bt)+C^{**}\frac{2}{f(t)}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{2C(t_{0})}{f(t)}

+\,C^{**}\frac{2}{f(t)}\left(\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|u_{1}+V(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\log(2L+bt)\right),

(2.27)

for
t\geq t_{0}\gg 1
.

Based on the estimate (
2.27
) one can get the desired first estimate

E_{u}(t)=O(t^{-2}\log t)\quad(t\to\infty),

in the case when
V_{0}>2b
, by choosing

and
g(t)=1+t
(see (
2.8
)). Furthermore, in case of

and

b

or

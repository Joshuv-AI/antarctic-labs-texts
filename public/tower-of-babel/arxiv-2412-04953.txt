Upper semi-continuity of metric entropy for diffeomorphisms with dominated splitting
Chiyi Luo, Wenhui Ma, Yun Zhao

Abstract
For a C r C^{r} ( r > 1 ) (r>1) diffeomorphism on a compact manifold that admits a dominated splitting, this paper establishes the upper semi-continuity of the entropy map. More precisely, this paper establishes the upper semi-continuity of the entropy map in the following two cases: (1) if a sequence of invariant measures has only positive Lyapunov exponents along a sub-bundle and non-positive Lyapunov exponents along another sub-bundle, then the upper limit of their metric entropies is less than or equal to the entropy of the limiting measure; (2) if an invariant measure has positive Lyapunov exponents along a sub-bundle and non-positive Lyapunov exponents along another sub-bundle, then the entropy map is upper semi-continuous at this measure.



1
Introduction

Entropy is an important invariant quantity in dynamical systems, which plays a central role in understanding the chaotic behavior of a system. The metric entropy measures the average amount of information and complexity in the system, and the topological entropy characterizes the exponential growth rate of the number of periodic points. Given a dynamical system
(M,f)
, the map
\mu\mapsto h_{\mu}(f)
defined on the set of all
f
-invariant measures is called entropy map in this paper. In the entropy theory of dynamical systems, it is an interesting topic to study the continuity of entropy map. However, the entropy map is generally not lower semi-continuous, even in uniformly hyperbolic systems.
For example, by shadowing lemma, one can construct a sequence of ergodic measures supported on periodic orbits, but it converges to an invariant measure with positive entropy. This paper studies the upper semi-continuity of entropy map for a class of non-uniformly hyperbolic dynamical systems.

For
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphisms on a compact manifold, Newhouse
[
New89
]
proved that the entropy map is upper semi-continuous. For
C^{r}
diffeomorphisms on a compact manifold with finite positive
r
, the upper semi-continuity of the entropy map may fail, e.g.,
Misiurewicz
[
Mis73
]
gave examples of diffeomorphisms on a four dimensional compact manifold, and examples in the two dimensional case are given by Buzzi
[
Buz14
]
. However, one can establish the upper semi-continuity of the entropy map for smooth maps under certain conditions.
For
C^{r}
surface diffeomorphisms, Burguet
[
Bur24
]
proved that the entropy map is upper semi-continuous at ergodic measures with large entropy.
For
C^{1}
diffeomorphisms away from homoclinic tangencies, in
[
LVY13
]
the authors proved that the entropy map is upper semi-continuous. Under some additional conditions, in
[
LSW15
]
the authors showed that the entropy map is upper semi-continuous for diffeomorphisms admit a dominated splitting.

In this paper, for a
C^{r}
diffeomorphism with dominated splitting, we will show the upper semi-continuity of the entropy map provided that the Lyapunov exponents of the invariant measures statisfy some conditions which are weaker than that of
[
LSW15
]
, see Remark
1.1
for detailed descriptions.

Now we will give some necessary notions to present the statements of the main results in this paper.
Let
f:M\rightarrow M
be a
C^{r}

(r>1)
diffeomorphism on a compact Riemannian manifold
M
without boundary.
We say that an
f
-invariant compact subset
\Lambda\subset M
admits a dominated splitting of
f
, if there exist
\lambda>0
and a continuous
Df
-invariant splitting
T_{\Lambda}M=E(x)\bigoplus F(x)
such that

\dfrac{m(D_{x}f|_{F(x)})}{\|D_{x}f|_{E(x)}\|}\geq e^{\lambda},\forall x\in\Lambda

where
\|A\|
is the norm and
m(A):=\inf_{\|v\|=1}\|A(v)\|
is the co-norm of a linear map
A
.

For every
f
-invariant measure
\mu
supported on
\Lambda
, by the sub-additive ergodic theorem, the following limits

\lambda_{F}^{\min}(x):=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log m(D_{x}f^{n}|_{F(x)}),~\lambda_{E}^{\max}(x):=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|D_{x}f^{n}|_{E(x)}\|

are well-defined for
\mu
-almost every
x\in\Lambda
. Moreover, if
\mu
is ergodic then
\lambda_{F}^{\min}(x)
and
\lambda_{E}^{\max}(x)
are constants for
\mu
-almost every
x\in\Lambda
, which are denoted by

\lambda_{F}^{\min}(\mu)
and
\lambda_{E}^{\max}(\mu)
respectively.

Theorem A
.

Let
f:M\rightarrow M
be a
C^{r}\,(r>1)
diffeomorphism on a compact Riemannian manifold, and let
\Lambda\subset M
be an
f
-invariant compact subset that admits a dominated splitting
T_{\Lambda}M=E\bigoplus F
.
Then, there exists
\varepsilon_{f}>0
such that if
\nu
is an ergodic measure supported on
\Lambda
satisfies that

\bullet

\lambda_{E}^{\max}(\nu)\leq 0
and
\lambda_{F}^{\min}(\nu)>0
,

\bullet

\mathcal{Q}
is a finite partition with
\nu(\partial\mathcal{Q})=0
and
\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{Q})<\varepsilon_{f}
,

we have that
h_{\nu}(f)=h_{\nu}(f,\mathcal{Q})
.

As a consequence, one can show the following theorem.

Theorem B
.

Let
f:M\rightarrow M
be a
C^{r}\,(r>1)
diffeomorphism on a compact Riemannian manifold, and let
\Lambda\subset M
be an
f
-invariant compact subset that admits a dominated splitting
T_{\Lambda}M=E\bigoplus F
. If a sequence of invariant measures
(\nu_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
satisfies

\bullet

for each
k>0
, one has
\nu_{k}(\Lambda)=1
,
\lambda_{E}^{\max}(x)\leq 0
and
\lambda_{F}^{\min}(x)>0
for
\nu_{k}
-almost every
x
;

\bullet

\nu_{k}
converges to
\mu
in the weak star topology,

then we have that

\displaystyle{\limsup\limits_{k\to\infty}h_{\nu_{k}}(f)\leq h_{\mu}(f)}
.

Remark 1.1
.

Under the set up of the above theorem, given a sequence of invariant measures
\{\nu_{k}\}
, if there exists
\chi>0
such that: (1)
\lambda_{E}^{\max}(x)<-\chi
and
\lambda_{F}^{\min}(x)>\chi
for
\nu_{k}
-almost every
x
for every
k>0
; (2)
\nu_{k}
converges to
\mu
in the weak star topology, and
\lambda_{E}^{\max}(x)<-\chi
and
\lambda_{F}^{\min}(x)>\chi
for
\mu
-almost every
x
, then the same conclusion holds. See
[
LSW15
]
for the details. We point out that they only require that
f
is a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism.
Our results do not require
\lambda_{E}^{\max}(x),\lambda_{F}^{\min}(x)
to be uniformly far from zero, and there are no conditions imposed on the limiting measure.

Finally, we show the upper semi-continuity of the entropy map under some conditions on the limiting measure.

Theorem C
.

Let
f:M\rightarrow M
be a
C^{r}\,(r>1)
diffeomorphism on a compact Riemannian manifold, and let
\Lambda\subset M
be an
f
-invariant compact subset that admits a dominated splitting
T_{\Lambda}M=E\bigoplus F
.
Assume that
\mu
is an
f
-invariant measure supported on
\Lambda
with

\lambda_{E}^{\max}(x)\leq 0
and
\lambda_{F}^{\min}(x)>0
for
\mu
-almost every
x
.
Then, the entropy map is upper semi-continuous at
\mu
.

In particular, for partially hyperbolic systems, if the center bundle decomposes into a dominated way of one-dimensional subbundles, then the entropy map is upper-semi continuous.
This result can also be deduced from
[
BCF18
]
.

Theorem
C
is not new, it only requires that
f
is
C^{1}
, as shown in
[
CLY20
]
, for instance.
In this paper, we utilize the results of Ledrappier and Young
[
LY85
]
(valid for
C^{2}
diffeomorphisms) and those in
[
Bro22
]
which extend to
C^{1+}
diffeomorphisms.
Therefore, we assume that
f
is
C^{r}
with
r>1
.

Tail entropy is used to estimate the degree to which entropy fails to be upper semi-continuous.
It quantifies how many arbitrarily small Bowen balls are required to cover a Bowen ball of fixed-size.
By Ledrappier and Young’s result
[
LY85
]
, entropy only arises along unstable manifolds.
Then, we estimate the “tail entropy” along unstable manifolds, which exhibit a certain degree of expansiveness.

In Burguet
[
Bur24
]
, Yomdin’s theory is used to provide a uniform diameter.
However, in our approach, we apply dominated splitting to establish a uniform diameter instead. For any local unstable manifold with this uniform size, there will be no bending along the strong subbundle direction after iterations of the dynamics.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we review basic properties of partial entropy along unstable manifolds. Section
3
establishes a uniform diameter bound for partitions, as stated in Theorem
A
, using the properties of dominated splitting.
Section
4
provides the proof of Theorems
B
and
C
, by assuming the validity of Theorem
4.1
which is a partial entropy version of Theorem
A
. Section
5
and Section
6
are devoted to the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Preliminaries

In this section, we will recall some basic properties of entropy and some useful results that are used in the proof of the main results.

Let

H_{\mu}(\mathcal{P})=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}-\mu(P)\log\mu(P)=\int-\log\mu(P(x)){\rm d}\mu(x).

Lemma 2.1

This result may be well-known, we give a detailed proof here for completeness.
We use the method of contradiction to prove this conclusion.
Assume that there exists a small
\varepsilon>0
such that the following set

K:=K_{\varepsilon}=\left\{x:\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{m_{n}(x)}{n}<1-4\varepsilon\right\}

has positive
\mu
-measure.
By the Birkhoff ergodic theorem, there exists
K^{\prime}\subset K
with
\mu(K^{\prime})>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\forall n>N,\forall x\in K^{\prime},~\#\Big\{0\leq i<n:f^{i}(x)\in E\Big\}\in\left(n(1-\varepsilon)\mu(E),n(\frac{1-2\varepsilon}{1-3\varepsilon})\mu(E)\right).

For each
x\in K^{\prime}
, choose
n_{x}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
m_{n_{x}}(x)<n_{x}(1-3\varepsilon)
and
N<n_{x}(1-3\varepsilon)
.
Hence,

n_{x}(1-2\varepsilon)\mu(E)\geq\#\Big\{0\leq j<n_{x}(1-3\varepsilon):f^{j}(x)\in E\Big\}=\#\Big\{0\leq j<n_{x}:f^{j}(x)\in E\Big\}\geq n_{x}(1-\varepsilon)\mu(E).

This contradiction yields the desired result. ∎

Fix a finite partition
\mathcal{Q}
with
\nu(\partial\mathcal{Q})=0
and
\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{Q})<\varepsilon_{f}
. Given
\alpha>0
, we will show that
h_{\nu}^{F}(f)\leq h_{\nu}(f,\mathcal{Q})+2\alpha
.

As introduced in Section
2
, let
\xi
be a measurable partition subordinate to
W^{F}
with respect to
\nu
, and let
\{\nu_{\xi(x)}\}
be a family of conditional measures of
\nu
with respect to
\xi
.

We choose a compact subset
E_{\nu}\subset\Lambda
, which is always called a Pesin set, such that
\nu(E_{\nu})>\frac{1}{2}
and there exist constants
C_{\nu}
and
\delta_{\nu}
for which

(5.1)

C_{\nu}\geq 1,~0<\delta_{\nu}\leq\hat{\varepsilon}_{f},~\text{and}~\delta_{x}\geq\delta_{\nu},~C_{x}\leq C_{\nu}~\text{for every}~x\in E_{\nu}.

Let

m_{n}^{\nu}(x)=\max\{0\leq j<n:f^{j}(x)\in E_{\nu}\}.

Using Birkhoff ergodic theorem, Proposition
2.1
and Lemma
5.1
, one can choose a compact subset
K_{\nu}
such that

(1)

\nu(K_{\nu})>\frac{1}{2}
and
K_{\nu}\subset E_{\nu}
;

(2)

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\delta_{f^{k}(x)}\rightarrow\nu~(n\to\infty)}
uniformly on
K_{\nu}
;

(3)

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{n}m_{n}^{\nu}(x)\rightarrow 1~(n\to\infty)}
uniformly on
K_{\nu}
;

(4)

there exists
\rho_{\nu}>0
such that

h_{\nu}^{F}(f)\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow+\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\nu_{\xi(x)}\left(K_{\nu}\cap B(x,n,\rho_{\nu})\right)+\alpha,~\forall x\in K_{\nu}.

For
\mu
-almost every
x
, let
W^{F}_{\delta}(x)=\exp_{x}\operatorname{graph}(g_{x}|_{F(x)(\delta)})
for each
0<\delta\leq\delta_{x}
. By the choice of the set
K_{\nu}
, one can choose a point
x_{\nu}\in K_{\nu}
such that
\nu_{\xi(x_{\nu})}(K_{\nu}\cap W^{F}_{\delta}(x_{\nu}))>0
for every
\delta\in(0,\delta_{\nu})
.
Fix some
\delta_{D}\in(0,\delta_{\nu})
such that

(5.2)

\displaystyle W^{F}_{\delta_{D}}(x_{\nu})\subset W^{F}_{2\delta_{D}}(x)\subset W^{F}_{\delta_{\nu}}(y),~\forall x,y\in K_{\nu}\cap W^{F}_{\delta_{D}}(x_{\nu}).

Denote by
D_{\nu}
the disc
W^{F}_{\delta_{D}}(x_{\nu})
, and let

\omega_{\nu}(\cdot):=\nu_{\xi(x_{\nu})}(\cdot\cap K_{\nu}\cap D_{\nu})/\nu_{\xi(x_{\nu})}(K_{\nu}\cap D_{\nu})
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{\nu}
be a finite partition with
\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{P}_{\nu})<\rho_{\nu}
and
\nu(\partial\mathcal{P}_{\nu})=0
. It follows from Proposition
2.2
that

(5.3)

h_{\nu}^{F}(f)\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{n}H_{\omega_{\nu}}(\mathcal{P}_{\nu}^{n})+\alpha.

By the standard entropy theory, one can show that

(5.4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}H_{\omega_{\nu}}(\mathcal{P}_{\nu}^{n})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}H_{\omega_{\nu}}(\mathcal{Q}^{n})+\frac{1}{n}H_{\omega_{\nu}}(\mathcal{P}_{\nu}^{n}|\mathcal{Q}^{n}),~\forall n>0.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}
,
\alpha
,
\omega_{\nu}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\nu}
be chosen as described above, then

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}H_{\omega_{\nu}}(\mathcal{P}_{\nu}^{n}|\mathcal{Q}^{n})\leq\alpha.

We now give the proof of Theorem
4.1
by assuming that Proposition
5.1
holds, and postpone its proof to the next section.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

For each
n>0
, let
\omega_{\nu}^{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\omega_{\nu}\circ f^{-j}
. For every
0<m<n
, using Lemma
2.1
we have that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}H_{\omega_{\nu}}(\mathcal{Q}^{n})\leq\frac{1}{m}H_{\omega_{\nu}^{n}}(\mathcal{Q}^{m})+\frac{2m}{n}\log\#\mathcal{Q}

We claim that
\omega_{\nu}^{n}\rightarrow\nu
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
In fact, note that

\int\phi(x)d\omega_{\nu}^{n}=\int\frac{1}{n}\left(\phi(x)+\phi(f(x))+\cdots+\phi(f^{n-1}(x))\right)d\omega_{\nu}

for each continuous function
\phi:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.
Since
\omega_{\nu}
is supported on
K_{\nu}\cap D_{\nu}\subset K_{\nu}
, one has that
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\phi(f^{j}(x))
converges uniformly to
\int\phi d\nu
on
K_{\nu}
. Hence, we have that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int\phi(x)d\omega_{\nu}^{n}=\int\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\phi(f^{j}(x))d\omega_{\nu}=\int\phi d\nu.

This implies that
\omega_{\nu}^{n}\rightarrow\nu~(n\to\infty)
.
Since
\nu(\partial\mathcal{Q})=0
, we get

\lim_{n\to\infty}H_{\omega_{\nu}^{n}}(\mathcal{Q}^{m})=H_{\nu}(\mathcal{Q}^{m}).

Therefore, we have that

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}H_{\omega_{\nu}}(\mathcal{Q}^{n})\leq\frac{1}{m}H_{\nu}(\mathcal{Q}^{m}).

By Proposition
5.1
and together with (
5.3
) and (
5.4
), we have

h_{\nu}^{F}(f)\leq\frac{1}{m}H_{\nu}(\mathcal{Q}^{m})+2\alpha.

Letting
m\rightarrow\infty
, we have
h_{\nu}^{F}(f)\leq h_{\nu}(f,\mathcal{Q})+2\alpha
. The arbitrariness of
\alpha
implies the desired result of Theorem
4.1
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
5.1

To complete the proof of the main theorems in this paper, it suffices to show Proposition
5.1
in this section.

The quantity we need to estimate is similar to the
tail entropy
, which evaluates how many Bowen balls of small radius are required to cover a Bowen ball of fixed radius. The difference is that all of the Bowen balls here are restricted to the
W^{F}_{\delta_{D}}(x_{\nu})
, which is denoted by
D_{\nu}
.

By the definition of conditional entropy, we have

(6.1)

\displaystyle H_{\omega_{\nu}}(\mathcal{P}_{\nu}^{n}|\mathcal{Q}^{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q_{n}\in\mathcal{Q}^{n}}\omega_{\nu}(Q_{n})\cdot H_{\omega_{\nu}|_{Q_{n}}}(\mathcal{P}_{\nu}^{n})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{Q_{n}\in\mathcal{Q}^{n}}\omega_{\nu}(Q_{n})\cdot\log\#\{P_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{\nu}^{n}:P_{n}\cap Q_{n}\cap D_{\nu}\cap K_{\nu}\neq\emptyset\},

where the last inequality using the fact that
\omega_{\nu}
is supported on
D_{\nu}\cap K_{\nu}
, and

\omega_{\nu}|_{Q_{n}}(\cdot)=\dfrac{\omega_{\nu}(\cdot\cap Q_{n})}{\omega_{\nu}(Q_{n})}
is the normalized restriction of
\omega_{\nu}
to
Q_{n}
.

Choose
\beta>0
small enough, such that

(6.2)

\beta(2+\dim F\cdot\log\sup_{x\in M}\|D_{x}f\|)\leq\alpha.

Since
\nu(\partial\mathcal{P}_{\nu})=0
, we choose
\eta_{\nu}>0
such that (recall (
4.1
), (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) for
\varepsilon_{f},\delta_{\nu}
and
D_{\nu}
)

(6.3)

\displaystyle\eta_{\nu}<\min\{\delta_{\nu},\varepsilon_{f}\},~\nu(B(\partial{\cal P}_{\nu},\eta_{\nu}))\leq\frac{\beta}{\log\#\mathcal{P}_{\nu}},~\nu(\partial B(\partial{\cal P}_{\nu},\eta_{\nu}))=0

\displaystyle\text{and for every }~x,y\in D_{\nu}\cap K_{\nu},~W^{F}_{\eta_{\nu}}(x)\subset W^{F}_{\delta_{\nu}}(y).

Then, by the construction of the set
K_{\nu}
, there exists
N_{\nu}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
n\geq N_{\nu}
and every
x\in K_{\nu}
, the following properties hold:

\displaystyle\#\Big\{0\leq i<n:f^{i}(x)\in B(\partial{\cal P}_{\nu},\eta_{\nu})\Big\}\leq\dfrac{2n\beta}{\log\#\mathcal{P}_{\nu}}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptm_{n}^{\nu}(x)\geq n(1-\beta).

Let
Q_{n}^{j,D}:=\Big\{x\in Q_{n}\cap D_{\nu}\cap K_{\nu}:m_{n}^{\nu}(x)=j\Big\}
, then one can show that
\#\Big\{j:Q_{n}^{j,D}\neq\emptyset\Big\}\leq n\beta
for each
n\geq N_{\nu}
.
Hence, we have that

(6.4)

\#\Big\{P_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{\nu}^{n}:P_{n}\cap Q_{n}\cap D_{\nu}\cap K_{\nu}\neq\emptyset\Big\}\leq n\beta\sup_{j\in[n(1-\beta),n)}\#\Big\{P_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{\nu}^{n}:P_{n}\cap Q_{n}^{j,D}\neq\emptyset\Big\}

Fix
n\geq N_{\nu}
,
Q_{n}^{j,D}\neq\emptyset
and
x_{\ast}\in Q_{n}^{j,D}
.
Since
\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{Q})<\varepsilon_{f}
, one has
Q_{n}^{j,D}\subset B(x_{\ast},n,\varepsilon_{f})
.
By the choice of
\varepsilon_{f}
(see (
4.1
)), we have that

\exp_{x_{\ast}}^{-1}B(x_{\ast},n,2\varepsilon_{f})\subset\bigcap_{i=0}^{n-1}F_{f^{i}(x_{\ast})}^{-i}R_{f^{i}(x_{\ast})}(\hat{\varepsilon}_{1}).

By the choice of
D_{\nu}
(see (
5.2
)) and
x_{\ast}\in Q_{n}^{j,D}\subset D_{\nu}\cap K_{\nu}
, it follows that

D_{\nu}\subset W^{F}_{\delta_{\nu}}(x_{\ast})
.
Let
\phi=g_{x_{\ast}}
and
{\rm Dom}(\phi)=F(x_{\ast})(\delta_{\nu})
, where the map
g_{x_{\ast}}
satisfies that
W^{F}_{\delta_{\nu}}(x_{\ast})=\exp_{x_{\ast}}(\operatorname{graph}(g_{x_{\ast}}|_{F(x_{\ast})(\delta_{\nu})}))
.
Recall that
\delta_{\nu}<\hat{\varepsilon}_{1}
and
\operatorname{Lip}(\phi)\leq\frac{1}{4}
.
By Proposition
3.1
, there is a family of
C^{1}
maps
\left\{\varphi_{i}:{\rm Dom}(\varphi_{i})\rightarrow E(f^{i}(x_{\ast}))\right\}_{i=0}^{n-1}
satisfying

(i)

\varphi_{i}(0)=0
,
{\rm Dom}(\varphi_{i})
is an open subset of
F(f^{i}(x_{\ast}))(\hat{\varepsilon}_{1})
and
\operatorname{Lip}(\varphi_{i})\leq\frac{1}{3}

(ii)

F_{x_{\ast}}^{i}(\operatorname{graph}(\phi))\supset\operatorname{graph}(\varphi_{i})\supset F_{x_{\ast}}^{i}\left(\bigcap_{t=0}^{i}F_{f^{t}(x_{\ast})}^{-t}(R_{f^{t}(x_{\ast})}(\hat{\varepsilon}_{1}))\cap\operatorname{graph}(\phi)\right),~\forall i=0,\cdots,n-1
.

Therefore, for every
0\leq i<n
we have

(6.5)

f^{i}(Q_{n}^{j,D})\subset\exp_{f^{i}(x_{\ast})}F_{x_{\ast}}^{i}\left(\bigcap_{t=0}^{n-1}F_{f^{t}(x_{\ast})}^{-t}(R_{f^{j}(x_{\ast})}(\hat{\varepsilon}_{1}))\cap\operatorname{graph}(\phi)\right)\subset\exp_{f^{i}(x_{\ast})}\operatorname{graph}(\varphi_{i}).

Take

(6.6)

\displaystyle\delta_{n,j}=\min\Big\{\eta_{\nu}(20C_{\nu})^{-1},~\eta_{\nu}20^{-1}C_{f}^{j-n},~\delta_{\nu}\Big\},

where
C_{f}:=\sup_{x\in M}\|D_{x}f\|

\eta_{\nu}

6.3
) and

).

For every
x\in Q_{n}^{j,D}

W^{F}_{\delta_{n,j}}(f^{j}(x))

f^{j}(x)\in E_{\nu}
and
\delta_{n,j}\leq\delta_{\nu}
.
For every
y,z\in f^{-j}W^{F}_{\delta_{n,j}}(f^{j}(x))

0\leq i\leq j

\displaystyle d^{F}_{f^{i}(x_{\ast})}(f^{i}(y),f^{i}(z))\leq C_{\nu}e^{-(j-i)\lambda_{\nu}}d^{F}_{f^{j}(x_{\ast})}(f^{j}(y),f^{j}(z))\leq 5C_{\nu}e^{-(j-i)\lambda_{\nu}}\delta_{n,j}\leq\frac{\eta_{\nu}}{4}.

j<i<n

Consequently,
d^{F}_{f^{i}(x_{\ast})}(f^{i}(y),f^{i}(z))\leq\frac{\eta_{\nu}}{4}
for every
0\leq i<n
. Therefore, one has

(6.7)

\operatorname{diam}(f^{-j+i}W^{F}_{\delta_{n,j}}(f^{j}(x)))\leq\frac{\eta_{\nu}}{4},~\forall 0\leq i<n.

.

There exists a family of points
\{x_{k}\}_{k=1}^{M}\subset Q_{n}^{j,D}
with

M\leq C_{\dim}\cdot\lceil\dfrac{4\hat{\varepsilon}_{1}}{\delta_{n,j}}\rceil^{\dim F}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\bigcup_{k=1}^{M}W^{F}_{\delta_{n,j}}(f^{j}(x_{k}))\supset f^{j}(Q_{n}^{j,D}).

Proof.

Recall that
f^{j}(Q_{n}^{j,D})\subset\exp_{f^{j}(x_{\ast})}\operatorname{graph}(\varphi_{j})

{\rm Dom}(\varphi_{j})\subset F(f^{j}(x_{\ast}))(\hat{\varepsilon}_{1})

3.3
, there exists a collection
\{F(f^{j}(x_{\ast}))(u_{k},\frac{1}{4}\delta_{n,j})\}_{k=1}^{M}
satisfies

F(f^{j}(x_{\ast}))(\hat{\varepsilon}_{1})\subset\bigcup_{k=1}^{M}F(f^{j}(x_{\ast}))(u_{k},\frac{1}{4}\delta_{n,j}),~u_{k}\subset F(f^{j}(x_{\ast}))(\hat{\varepsilon}_{1}),~M\leq C_{\dim}\cdot\lceil\dfrac{4\hat{\varepsilon}_{1}}{\delta_{n,j}}\rceil^{\dim F}.

For
x\in Q_{n}^{j,D}
, let

u_{x}=\pi_{f^{j}(x_{\ast})}^{F}\circ\exp_{f^{j}(x_{\ast})}^{-1}(f^{j}(x))\in{\rm Dom}(\varphi_{j})\subset F(f^{j}(x_{\ast}))(\hat{\varepsilon}_{1}).

Let
\psi_{f^{j}(x)}:F(f^{j}(x))(\delta_{n,j})\rightarrow E(f^{j}(x))
satisfies
W^{F}_{\delta_{n,j}}(f^{j}(x))=\exp_{f^{j}(x)}\operatorname{graph}(\psi_{f^{j}(x)})
.
Recall that
d(f^{j}(x),f^{j}(x_{\ast}))\leq\varepsilon_{2}
,
\delta_{n,j}\leq\delta_{\nu}<\hat{\delta}_{1}
and
\operatorname{Lip}(\psi_{f^{j}(x)})\leq\frac{1}{4}
. By Proposition
3.2
, there exists

\phi_{x}^{\ast}=\psi_{f^{j}(x_{\ast})}^{f^{j}(x)}:F(f^{j}(x_{\ast}))(u_{x},\frac{1}{2}\delta_{n,j})\rightarrow E(f^{j}(x_{\ast}))~\text{such that}~\exp_{f^{j}(x_{\ast})}\operatorname{graph}(\phi_{x}^{\ast})\subset W^{F}_{\delta_{n,j}}(f^{j}(x)).

Let
{\mathcal{I}}:=\Big\{1\leq k\leq M:\{u_{x}:x\in Q^{j,D}_{n}\}\cap F(f^{j}(x_{\ast}))(u_{k},\frac{1}{4}\delta_{n,j})\neq\emptyset\Big\}
.
For every
k\in{\mathcal{I}}
, we choose a point
x_{k}\in Q^{j,D}_{n}
such that
u_{x_{k}}\in F(f^{j}(x_{\ast}))(u_{k},\frac{1}{4}\delta_{n,j})

\{W^{F}_{\delta_{n,j}}(f^{j}(x_{k}))\}_{k\in{\mathcal{I}}}

We will show that
\bigcup_{k\in{\mathcal{I}}}W^{F}_{\delta_{n,j}}(f^{j}(x_{k}))\supset f^{j}(Q_{n}^{j,D})

f^{j}(x)\in f^{j}(Q_{n}^{j,D})-\bigcup_{k\in{\mathcal{I}}}W^{F}_{\delta_{n,j}}(f^{j}(x_{k}))

k\in{\mathcal{I}}

f^{j}(x)\in\exp_{f^{j}(x_{\ast})}\operatorname{graph}(\varphi_{j}),~f^{j}(x)\notin W^{F}_{\delta_{n,j}}(f^{j}(x_{k})),~\exp_{f^{j}(x_{\ast})}\operatorname{graph}(\phi_{x_{k}}^{\ast})\subset W^{F}_{\delta_{n,j}}(f^{j}(x_{k})).

Proof of Proposition
5.1
.

We first estimate
\#\Big\{P_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{\nu}^{n}:P_{n}\cap Q_{n}^{j,D}\neq\emptyset\Big\}
.
By Lemma
6.1
there exists a family of sub-discs
\{W_{k}\}_{k=1}^{M}
with

M\leq C_{\dim}\cdot\lceil\dfrac{4\hat{\varepsilon}_{1}}{\delta_{n,j}}\rceil^{\dim F},~\bigcup_{k=1}^{M}f^{j}(W_{k})\supset f^{j}(Q_{n}^{j,D})~\text{and}~\operatorname{diam}(f^{i}W_{k})\leq\frac{\eta_{\nu}}{4},~\forall 0\leq i<n.

Then, we have

(6.9)

\displaystyle\#\Big\{P_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{\nu}^{n}:P_{n}\cap Q_{n}^{j,D}\neq\emptyset\Big\}\leq M\sup_{k}\#\Big\{P_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{\nu}^{n}:P_{n}\cap W_{k}\cap K_{\nu}\neq\emptyset\Big\}.

W\in\{W_{k}\}_{k=1}^{M}
, we have

\#\Big\{P_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{\nu}^{n}:P_{n}\cap W\cap K_{\nu}\neq\emptyset\Big\}\leq\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\#\Big\{P\in\mathcal{P}_{\nu}:P\cap f^{i}(W\cap K_{\nu})\neq\emptyset\Big\}.

Fix some point
z\in W\cap K_{\nu}
. If

\#\{P\in\mathcal{P}_{\nu}:P\cap f^{i}(W\cap K_{\nu})\neq\emptyset\}>1
,
then by
\operatorname{diam}(f^{i}W)\leq\frac{\eta_{\nu}}{4}
we must have
f^{i}(z)\in f^{i}(W\cap K_{\nu})\subset B(\partial\mathcal{P}_{\nu},\eta_{\nu})
. Hence, we have

\#\{P_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{\nu}^{n}:P_{n}\cap W\cap K_{\nu}\neq\emptyset\}\leq(\#\mathcal{P}_{\nu})^{\#\{0\leq i<n:f^{i}(z)\in B(\partial\mathcal{P}_{\nu},\eta_{\nu})\}}\leq(\#\mathcal{P}_{\nu})^{2n\beta/\log\#\mathcal{P}_{\nu}}=e^{2n\beta}.

Consequently, by (
6.4
) and (
6.9
) we get that

\#\Big\{P_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{\nu}^{n}:P_{n}\cap Q_{n}\cap D_{\nu}\cap K_{\nu}\neq\emptyset\Big\}\leq n\beta\cdot C_{\dim}\cdot\lceil\dfrac{4\hat{\varepsilon}_{1}}{\delta_{n,j}}\rceil^{\dim F}\cdot e^{2n\beta}.

By (
6.1
) and the choice of
\delta_{n,j}
and

(see (
6.6
) and (
6.2
)) we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}H_{\omega_{\nu}}(\mathcal{P}_{\nu}^{n}|\mathcal{Q}^{n})\leq\beta(2+\dim F\cdot\log C_{f})\leq\alpha.

.

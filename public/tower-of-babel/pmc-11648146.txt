The complete mitochondrial genome of Vaccinium oldhamii Miquel, a plant of the Ericaceae family
Authors: Da Hyun Lee, Woo Hyun Sim, Chung Youl Park, Do Hyun Kim, Jun Hyeok Kim, Hyeon Min Kim, Sang Hoon Che, Wan Geun Park, Chae Sun Na, Sangrea Shim
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractVaccinium oldhamii Miquel 1866 is a deciduous shrub native to Northeast Asia, including Korea, China, and Japan. Its polyphenol-rich edible berries may aid in managing chronic diseases. Despite its importance, the mitogenome of this species remains understudied. This study assembled the complete mitogenome, consisting of 626,941 bp with 45.34% GC content, using NOVOPlasty and Illumina sequencing. The genome includes 38 protein-coding genes, 24 tRNA genes, and three rRNA genes. Phylogenetic analysis revealed a close relationship with V. macrocarpon. This mitogenome provides a valuable resource for phylogenomic studies and insights into evolutionary relationships within the Vaccinium genus and the Ericales order. Vaccinium oldhamii Miquel 1866 is a deciduous shrub in the Ericales and the Ericaceae and is native to Northeast Asia including Korea, China, and Japan. Particular emphasis has been placed on the edible berries of Vaccinium species and their use as dietary supplements. Specifically, the edible berries of V. oldhamii contain high levels of polyphenols, which may aid in preventing and managing chronic diseases. However, the mitogenome sequence of this species, a valuable resource for studying evolutionary relationships and genetic diversity within the Vaccinium genus, has not been thoroughly investigated. In this study, the complete mitochondrial genome sequence of V. oldhamii was assembled into 626,941 bp circular nucleotides with a GC content of 45.34% using NOVOPlasty and Illumina sequencing data. The comprehensive annotation for mitogenome of V. oldhamii revealed that the mitogenome comprises 38 known protein-coding genes, 24 tRNA genes, and three rRNA genes. Phylogenetic analysis using ML method showed that this species is closely related to the V. macrocarpon. This newly sequenced mitochondrial genome offers a valuable resource for phylogenomic analysis and will enhance our understanding of evolutionary relationships within the Ericales order and/or the Vaccinium genus.

Introduction

The Vaccinium genus, belonging to the Ericaceae family, includes over 450 species of both deciduous and evergreen shrubs (Tundis et al. 2021). Vaccinium species have long been used in traditional medicine across various cultures, and their berries are widely enjoyed as a food source (Tundis et al. 2021). A notable species within this genus is V. oldhamii Miquel 1866 (Miquel 1865), a shrub native to Northeast Asia, including Korea, China, and Japan. Particularly, V. oldhamii produces edible berries that are widely used in traditional medicine, to treat cystitis, vomiting, gonorrhea, diarrhea, and rashes in Korea and China (Son et al. 2019).

Recently, a study shows that its berries are rich in polyphenols, known for their antioxidant activity (Martău et al. 2023). These antioxidants are effective in managing various health issues, including diabetes, obesity, cardiovascular and neurodegenerative diseases, atherosclerosis, rheumatoid arthritis, and cancer (Tsuda et al. 2013; Martău et al. 2023), suggesting the high economic value of V. oldhamii.

Despite the strong potential of V. oldhamii and other Vaccinium species as natural health supplements and their economic value, genomic resources for uncovering evolutionary relationships and genetic diversity within Vaccinium species have not been fully investigated.

In this study, we conducted comprehensive analyses and assembled complete mitogenome sequence of V. oldhamii. This research seeks to enhance the understanding of the molecular phylogenetic relationships within the Ericales order and/or Vaccinium genus, particularly comparing the mitochondrial genome sequence of V. oldhamii in comparison with other related plants.

Materials and methods

The specimen of V. oldhamii was deposited in the Baekdudaegan National Arboretum of Korea under Voucher specimen ID: 2023-048652 (https://www.bdna.or.kr/intro; Hyoung Seok Kim; khs369@koagi.or.kr). Plant material of V. oldhamii was collected from Jangsampo-ro, Gonam-myeon, Taean-gun, Chungcheongnam-do, Republic of Korea (Latitude: 36°25’53.95"N, Longitude: 126°21’28.49"E) (Figure 1).

Specimen and image of Vaccinium oldhamii Miquel 1866. The specimen of V. oldhamii, deposited at the Baekdudaegan National Arboretum under accession number, 2023-048652, is shown in panel (a) (https://www.bdna.or.kr/intro; Hyoung Seok Kim; khs369@koagi.or.kr). An accompanying image of V. oldhamii is taken by Da Hyun Lee (b). V. oldhamii is a deciduous shrub belonging to the Ericaceae family and the Vaccinium genus. It typically grows to a height of 1–3 meters and is found naturally in mountainous areas south of North Gyeongsang and North Chungcheong Provinces, and along the western coast (South Chungcheong and Hwanghae Province). The flowers bloom from May to June, appearing in clusters of 5 to 15 at the tips of new branches. They are hermaphroditic, displaying a light pinkish yellow-green or reddish-brown color. The fruit is spherical, measuring 4–6 mm in diameter, and ripens to a dark purple color in September to October.

Genomic DNA was isolated from fresh young leaves using Maxwell RSC Plant DNA Kit (Promega Corporation, Madison, WI, USA). DNA purity and concentration were measured using NanoDrop spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) and Qubit fluorometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). DNA library was prepared with the TruSeq Nano DNA Kit (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Whole genome sequencing was performed on the Illumina NovaSeq platform (Illumina).

Raw sequence reads were trimmed to remove adapter sequences and low-quality bases using Trimmomatic algorithm (Bolger et al. 2014). Trimmed reads were assembled using NOVOPlasty (Dierckxsens et al. 2017) considering the plastid genome sequence of V. oldhamii (Kim et al. 2019; MK049537.1). To evaluate genome assembly, the sequence coverage and depth were analyzed using a Python script (https://www.protocols.io/view/generating-sequencing-depth-and-coverage-map-for-o-4r3l27jkxg1y/v1). Completeness of circular mitogenome was evaluated for the sequence alignment patterns and mapping depth around the merged position using Samtools and IGV (Li et al. 2009; Thorvaldsdóttir et al. 2013).

Gene prediction and annotation were conducted using MFannot for protein-coding genes and tRNAs (Lang et al. 2023), and GeSeq for rRNAs (Tillich et al. 2017). The annotations of eight genes (atp6, ccmFN, nad2, nad3, nad5, rpl16, rps1 and rps4) and three genes (ccmB, ccmFC, and matR) were manually curated using BLASTn and BLASTp with mitochondrial gene sequences from V. macrocarpon and V. vitis-idaea (Fajardo et al. 2014; McGinnis and Madden 2004; PP069826.1). The complete mitochondrial genome map was visualized using PMGmap (Zhang et al. 2024).

To construct a phylogenetic tree for the mitochondrial genome of V. oldhamii and related species, one outgroup species (Oryza sativa; Notsu et al. 2002; BA000029.3) and eight additional Ericales species were included based on the availability of mitogenomes for closely related species: V. vitis-idaea var. minus (Unpublished, NCBI accession: PP069826.1), V. macrocarpon (Fajardo et al. 2014; NC_023338.1), Rhododendron simsii (Xu et al. 2021; MW030508.1), Rhododendron x pulchrum (Shen et al. 2022; OM283814.1), Actinidia chinensis (Unpublished, NCBI accession: PP035753.1), Actinidia eriantha (Unpublished, NCBI accession: PP035760.1), Camellia nitidissima (Liu et al. 2023; ON645224.1), Camellia sinensis (Li et al. 2023; NC_043914.1). Conserved mitochondrial genes across these ten species were identified using BLASTp algorithm with an e-value of 1 × 10−5 and a maximum of one alignments per protein sequence. The conserved protein sequences were then aligned using MUSCLE (Edgar 2004) within MEGA software (Tamura et al. 2021) with default parameters. Based on the multiple sequence alignment, a phylogenetic tree was constructed by MEGA software, using the maximum-likelihood method with the Jones–Taylor–Thornton matrix-based model and 1,000 bootstrap replications (Tamura et al. 2021).

Results

To assemble mitogenome of V. oldhamii, we generated 92,770,938 NGS reads accounting 14,008,411,638 bp (Supplemental Table 1). After trimming, 85,716,624 reads accounting 12,637,304,419 bp were remained and used in the genome assembly (Supplemental Table 1).

The complete mitochondrial genome of V. oldhamii spans 626,941 bp with a GC content of 45.34% and encodes 65 genes. The assembled mitogenome was fully covered by supporting reads with an average depth of 691 (Supplemental Figure 1). The completeness of the circular mitochondrial genome was validated through sequence alignment pattern and mapping depth around the merged position (Supplemental Figure 2).

The V. oldhamii mitogenome includes 38 known protein-coding genes, 24 tRNA genes, and 3 rRNA genes (Figure 2). Of the 38 protein-coding genes, nad1 and rpl2 each contain two exons, nad4 contains three exons, nad5 contains four exons, and nad2 and nad7 each contain five exons (Supplemental Figure 3). Four gene pairs (atp1, trnK, trnL, and atp8) are duplicated, while the trnM genes are heptaplicated. The remaining 50 genes are present as single copies.

Circular map of the Vaccinium oldhamii Miq. mitochondrial genome. Genes annotated outside the circle are in the forward orientation, whereas those inside are in the reverse orientation.

A total of 16 genes, including atp1 (second copy), atp9, ccmB, ccmC, ccmFC, ccmFN, cob, cox2, matR, nad2, nad3, nad4, nad5, nad6, rps1, and rps12, are highly conserved across 10 plant species including nine Ericales species and one outgroup species (Figure 3). The phylogentic tree showed that V. oldhamii clustered with two other Vaccinium species, V. macrocarpon (NC_023338.1) and V. vitis-idaea (PP069826.1), and was most closely related to V. macrocarpon (Figure 3).

Discussion and conclusion

Here, we report the complete mitogenome of V. oldhamii, a representative species in the Ericaceae family. The phylogenetic tree indicated that V. oldhamii is closely related to V. macrocarpon, clustering with two other Vaccinium species (Figure 3). Comparative analysis of mitochondrial genes among these three species revealed 29 conserved genes across the Vaccinium species, with V. macrocarpon (Fajardo et al. 2014; NC_023338.1) and V. vitis-idaea (PP069826.1) containing 34 and 35 known protein-coding genes, respectively (Supplemental Figure 4). Additionally, the average gene lengths for these species were 827.0, 899.7, and 821.9 bp for V. macrocarpon, V. vitis-idaea and V. oldhamii, respectively (Supplemental Table 2). These findings further support the close evolutionary relationship among the three Vaccinium species, as confirmed by the phylogenetic analysis (Figure 3).

Notably, a partial segment of chloroplast gene psbA was identified in the mitogenome of V. oldhamii (Supplemental Table 3). This result suggested that horizontal gene transfer between the mitogenome and the plastid genome of V. oldhamii may have occurred and subsequently degenerated during evolution, as observed in previous studies (Wang et al. 2007; Xi et al. 2013). This novel mitochondrial genome sequence provides a valuable resource for phylogenomic analysis and will contribute to understanding evolutionary relationships within the Ericales order and/or Vaccinium genus.

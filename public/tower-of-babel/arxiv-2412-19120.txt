Exact sequences and the combinatorics of conformal models
P. Bantay Institute for Theoretical Physics, Eötvös Loránd University

Abstract
. We investigate the mutual relations between the centers of different elements in the deconstruction lattice of a 2D conformal model, and show how these can be described using exact sequences of abelian groups. In particular, we exhibit a long exact sequence connecting the centers of higher central quotients.



1.
Introduction

It has been understood for a long time that a substantial amount
of information about a rational conformal model
[
1
,
2
]

is encoded in its fusion algebra, which describes the possible couplings
between primary fields (i.e. which primaries, together with their
conformal descendants, could appear in the operator product of any
two given primaries). A famous result in this direction, instigating
important mathematical developments over the years, is the celebrated
formula of Verlinde
[
3
]
relating the fusion rules
of the model, i.e. the structure constants of the fusion algebra,
to the matrix describing the transformation properties of the chiral
characters under the modular transformation
S\!:\!\tau\!\rightarrow\!\nicefrac{{-1}}{{{\textstyle\tau}}}
,
and which allows to reconstruct the latter from the knowledge of the
fusion rules and the conformal weights of the primaries. But it is
fair to say that this is just the tip of the iceberg, for several
similar relations are known, e.g. for the Frobenius-Schur indicators
of the primaries
[
4
]
, or for the traces of finite
order mapping classes
[
5
]
.

As has been discussed in
[
6
,
7
]
, an interesting
structure related to the fusion algebra results from the consideration
of those collections of primaries that, besides containing the vacuum,
are closed under the fusion product. These may be shown to form a
lattice with many nice properties, like being self-dual and modular

[
8
,
9
]
. Some elements of this so-called deconstruction
lattice (the local ones) may be identified with the representation
rings of suitable finite groups, allowing the transfer to them of
several standard notions from group theory (like commutativity, nilpotency,
etc.) which turn out to make perfectly good sense for generic elements.

In particular, there is a way to define the notions of center and
that of central quotients/extensions
[
6
]
, which correspond,
in case of local elements, to the standard group theoretic notions.
The importance of all this stems from the fact that there is a well
understood relationship between properties of central quotients and
extensions, and this can facilitate greatly the analysis of specific
models. A notable exception to this, in complete analogy with the
case of groups, concerns the structure of the center itself, as there
is no obvious connection between the centers of central quotients/extensions.
The aim of the present note is to show that a useful characterization
of this relationship can be nevertheless given in terms of exact sequences
of abelian groups
[
10
]
.

In order to be accessible to a wider readership, we briefly summarize
background material on the deconstruction lattice in Section
2
,
on central quotients and extensions in Section
3
,
and on exact sequences in Section
4
. Then we
turn to our main subject, and study in Section
5
the
restriction homomorphism that connects the centers of different elements
in the deconstruction lattice. The most important results can be found
in Section
6
, which investigates the Galois correspondence
between central quotients and subgroups of the center, resulting in
exact sequences able to describe the subtleties of this relationship.
The theme of Section
7
is a long exact
sequence connecting the centers of higher quotients that proves useful
in actual computations, while Section
8

is concerned with the case of central extensions instead of quotients,
based on the dual nature of these two notions. In the last section
we summarize the results, and comment on open questions and possible
future developments. Finally, as the modularity of the deconstruction
lattice plays a pivotal role in some of the results of Section
6
,
a streamlined proof of this fundamental result is presented in the

Appendix A
Modularity of the deconstruction lattice
, based on an explicit characterization of
the join operation that could prove interesting in itself.

2.
The deconstruction lattice

Let’s consider a 2D rational conformal model
[
1
,
2
]

with a finite number of primaries. We shall denote by
N_{pq}^{r}

the fusion rule coefficients, that is the multiplicity of a primary

r
in the fusion product of the primaries
p
and
q
. The collection
of all those subsets of primaries that contain the vacuum and are
closed under the fusion product (meaning that if
N_{pq}^{r}\!>\!0

with both
p
and
q
belonging to it, then
r
does also belongs
to it) may be shown
[
6
]
to form a modular lattice

\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
, termed the
deconstruction lattice
because of its fundamental
role in the classification of the different orbifold deconstructions
of the model
[
11
,
12
]
. The ordering in
\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}

is simply set inclusion, and the meet operation is set intersection
(the join operation being less obvious, but see the
Appendix A
Modularity of the deconstruction lattice
).

Recall
[
6
]
that to each
\mathfrak{g\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}}

one can assign a partition of the primaries of the model into so-called

\mathfrak{g}
-classes characterized by the fact that the irreducible
representations of the fusion algebra corresponding to different elements
in the same class coincide when restricted to the elements of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Of utmost importance is the
\mathfrak{g}
-class that contains the
vacuum, the
trivial class

\mathfrak{g}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}
, which may be shown to be
itself an element of
\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
. It is straightforward that
\mathfrak{g}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{h}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}

whenever
\mathfrak{h}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{g}
, and that the trivial
class of
\mathfrak{g}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}
is
\mathfrak{g}
itself, hence the lattice
\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}

is
self-dual
, i.e. endowed with an order-reversing and involutive

duality map

\mathfrak{g}\!\mapsto\!\mathfrak{g}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}
that relates the
join and meet operations via
\mathfrak{\left(\mathfrak{g}\!\vee\!\mathfrak{h}\right)}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}\!=\!\mathfrak{g}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}\!\cap\mathfrak{h}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}
.

As it turns out, self-dual lattices are closely related to undirected
graphs (with possible loops): any such graph determines a self-dual
lattice, and any self-dual lattice comes from a suitable graph

1

the collection of all such graphs may be characterized in a simple

[
13
]
.

of a conformal model corresponds to its locality graph, i.e. the graph
whose vertices are the primary fields, with two of them adjacent if
they are mutually local, i.e. if their OPE is single-valued. Actually,
instead of the locality graph one can use the so-called locality diagram,
whose vertices correspond to equilocality classes of primaries, i.e.
collections of primaries that are mutually local with the same set
of primaries, providing a nice graphical representation of the deconstruction
lattice
[

]
.

Especially important are those elements

Let us note that in case of abelian models, when all the primaries

the fusion closed sets are nothing but the different subgroups of

in turn determined by the distribution of conformal weights. In such

.

It should transpire from the above that the deconstruction lattice

\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
is an important algebraic/combinatorial invariant of the
conformal model under study. In particular, it gives a concise description
of the different realizations of the given model as an orbifold of
another one. Understanding the structure of
\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
through the relation
of its elements to each other could provide information about the
model that would be hard to obtain by other means. One such piece
of information is related to the notion of central quotients and extensions,
to which we now turn.

3.
Central quotients and extensions

Given
\mathfrak{g}\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
, an important numerical characteristic
of any
\mathfrak{g}
-class
\mathcal{\mathtt{C}}
is its
mass

(3.1)

\boldsymbol{\upmu}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{\mathtt{C}}})\!=\!\sum_{p\in\mathtt{C}}\mathtt{d}_{p}^{2}

where
\mathtt{d}_{p}
denotes the quantum dimension of the primary
p
.
An especially important role is played by those
\mathfrak{g}
-classes
whose
\mathrm{mass}
is minimal (equal to that of the trivial class
\mathfrak{g}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}
),
the
central classes
, which may be shown
[
6
]

to form an abelian group
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}})
, the
center

of
\mathfrak{g}
, that permutes the collection of all
\mathfrak{g}
-classes,
and whose identity element is the trivial class. For local
\mathfrak{g}\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}

corresponding to the finite group
G
, the center
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}})

is isomorphic with the center of
G
. To each central class
\mathtt{z}\!\in\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})

one may associate a complex-valued function
\varpi_{\mathtt{z}}\!:\!\mathfrak{g}\!\rightarrow\!\mathbb{C}
,
its
central characte
r, such that for
\alpha,\beta\!\in\!\mathfrak{g}

one has
\varpi_{\mathtt{z}}\!\left(\gamma\right)\!=\!\varpi_{\mathtt{z}}\!\left(\alpha\right)\varpi_{\mathtt{z}}\!\left(\beta\right)

whenever
N_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}\!>\!0
, and one may show that
the map that assigns to each central class its central character provides
an explicit isomorphism between
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})
and its Pontryagin dual.

It turns out
[
6
]
that each subgroup
H\!<\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})

determines another element of
\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
, the
central quotient

\mathfrak{g}/\!\mathit{H}
, and there is a one-to-one correspondence
between elements
\mathfrak{h}\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
that satisfy
\mathfrak{g}/\!\mathit{H}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{h}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{g}

and subgroups of
H
. In particular, each
\mathfrak{g\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}}

has a
maximal central quotient

(3.2)

\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{g}/\!\mathit{\penalty\ \mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}})}

that is contained in all central quotients, and each
\mathfrak{h}\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}

that satisfies
\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{h}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{g}

is a central quotient of
\mathfrak{g}
, i.e.
\mathfrak{h}\!=\!\mathfrak{g}/\!\mathit{H}

for some subgroup
H\mathfrak{<}\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})
. Actually, the relevant
subgroup reads
H\!=\!\left\{\mathtt{z}\!\in\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})\,|\,\mathtt{z}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{h}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}\right\}
,
and this implies that the union of the central classes contained in

H
(viewed as collections of primaries) equals
\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}
,
i.e.
\mathbf{\boldsymbol{\cup}}H\!=\!\mathfrak{h}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}
. In particular,

(3.3)

\mathbf{\boldsymbol{\cup}}\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})\!=\!\mathfrak{\left(\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}\right)}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}

The other way round, a
central extension
of
\mathfrak{g\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}}

is an element
\mathfrak{h}\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
of which
\mathfrak{g}

is a central quotient. Using the duality map
\mathfrak{g}\!\mapsto\!\mathfrak{g}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}

of
\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
, one can show
[
6
]
that there is a one-to-one
correspondence between central extensions of
\mathfrak{g}
and subgroups
of
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}})
. In particular, there is a
maximal central
extension

\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}}^{\intercal}\!=\!\mathfrak{\left(\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{\left(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}\right)}\right)}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}

that contains all central extensions of
\mathfrak{g}
, and each

\mathfrak{h}\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
that satisfies
\mathfrak{g}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{h}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}}^{\intercal}

is a central extension of
\mathfrak{g}
, i.e.
\mathfrak{g}
is
a central quotient of
\mathfrak{h}
; put another way, the inclusion

\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{h}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{g}

is equivalent to
\mathfrak{h}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{g}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}}^{\intercal}
.

The map that assigns to an element
\mathfrak{g}\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
its
maximal central quotient (resp. extension)
\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}
(resp.
\mathfrak{g}^{\intercal}
)
is order-preserving, i.e.
\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{h}\!\subseteq\!\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}
(resp.
\mathfrak{h}^{\intercal}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{g}^{\intercal}
)
whenever
\mathfrak{h}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{g}
. What is more interesting
is the fact that one has

(3.4)

\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{\left(\mathfrak{g}^{\intercal}\right)\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{g}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{\left(\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}\right)}^{\intercal}}

for every
\mathfrak{g}\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
. Indeed, since
\mathfrak{g}

is a central quotient of
\mathfrak{g}^{\intercal}
, it should contain the maximal
central quotient of the latter, and in the same vein, since
\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}}

is a central quotient of
\mathfrak{g}
, the latter is a central
extension of
\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}}
, consequently it has to be contained
in
\mathfrak{\left(\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}\right)}^{\intercal}
. Actually, one may show that the map

\mathfrak{g}\!\mapsto\!\mathfrak{\left(\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}\right)}^{\intercal}
is a closure operation
on
\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
, whose fixed points are those elements of
\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
that
are themselves maximal central extensions, i.e. of the form
\mathfrak{g}^{\intercal}

for some
\mathfrak{g}\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
.

The importance of central quotients and extensions stems from the
fact that their properties are determined to a large extent by group

of the quotient

on them

Based on this, one expects that the center of

and that of its quotients

4.
Exact sequences

As already alluded to previously, our goal is to understand the relations
between the centers of different elements of the deconstruction lattice,
in particular those that are central quotients or extensions of each
other, and a most efficient way to express these connections is through
the use of exact sequences. Since the latter are not part of the everyday
toolkit of physicists, let’s briefly summarize those basics facts
about them that we shall need in what follows.

Recall
[
10
]
that an
exact sequence
of (abelian)
groups is a sequence of group homomorphisms
\phi_{i}\!:\!A_{i-1}\!\rightarrow\!A_{i}

for
i\!=\!1,\ldots,n
, such that the kernel of
\phi_{i+1}
equals
the image of
\phi_{i}
for
1\!\leq\!i\!<\!n
; note that the domain
of
\phi_{i+1}
equals the range of
\phi_{i}
. Their usual notation
is

(4.1)

\begin{CD}A_{0}@>{\phi_{1}}>{}>A_{1}@>{\phi_{2}}>{}>\cdots @>{\phi_{n-1}}>{}>A_{n-1}@>{\phi_{n}}>{}>A_{n}\end{CD}

In many cases one is not really interested in the actual homomorphisms
that connect the
A_{i}
, but only in the relationship between them
that follows from the existence of the exact sequence; in such cases
one usually drops any explicit reference to the
\phi_{i}
(unless
the specification of some of them provides useful extra information).

The most ubiquitous are the so-called
short exact sequence
s

(4.2)

\begin{CD}1@>{}>{}>A@>{}>{}>B@>{}>{}>C@>{}>{}>1\end{CD}

where
1
denotes the (isomorphism class of the) trivial group of
order 1. The homomorphism theorem
[
20
,
10
]
tells
us at once that the existence of such an exact sequence is tantamount
to the existence of a subgroup
K\!<\!B
, the kernel of the homomorphism
from
B
to
C
, which satisfies
K\!\cong\!A
and
C\!\cong\!B\!/\penalty\!K
.

Next in line come the four-term exact sequences

(4.3)

\begin{CD}1@>{}>{}>A@>{}>{}>B@>{}>{}>C@>{}>{}>D@>{}>{}>1\end{CD}

which play an important role e.g. in algebraic number theory
[
21
]
,
and whose existence tells us that there exists a homomorphism
\phi\!:\!B\!\rightarrow\!C

whose kernel is isomorphic with
A
, and whose cokernel
\mathtt{coker}\!\left(\phi\right)\!=\!C/\penalty\mathtt{im}\left(\phi\right)

is isomorphic with
D
(note that, since every subgroup of an abelian
group is normal, the cokernel makes perfectly good sense for homomorphism
of abelian groups). In particular, it follows from Lagrange’s theorem

[
10
]
that the orders of the groups appearing in a four-term
exact sequence are related by
|A||C|\!=\!|B||D|
.

Finally, there are the l
ong exact sequence
s

(4.4)

\begin{CD}1@>{}>{}>A_{1}@>{}>{}>A_{2}@>{}>{}>\cdots @>{}>{}>A_{n}@>{}>{}>1\end{CD}

whose existence signals a more subtle and less immediate relation
between the
A_{i}
. Note that long exact sequences are equivalent
to a suitable collection of overlapping short exact sequences. To
see how this comes about, let’s consider the exact sequences

(4.5)

\begin{CD}\,1@>{}>{}>A_{1}@>{}>{}>\cdots @>{\phi_{n-1}}>{}>A_{n}@>{\psi}>{}>B@>{}>{}>1\end{CD}

and

(4.6)

\begin{CD}1@>{}>{}>B@>{\iota}>{}>A_{n+1}@>{\phi_{n+1}}>{}>\cdots @>{}>{}>A_{n+m}@>{}>{}>1\end{CD}

with
B\neq 1
(should
B
be trivial, the above sequences would
shorten and would not have a common anchor). Then, since
\psi
is
surjective with
\mathtt{ker}(\psi)\!=\!\mathtt{im}(\phi_{n-1})
, while
\iota
is
injective with
\mathtt{im}(\iota)\!=\!\mathtt{ker}(\phi_{n+1})
, the composite
\phi_{n}\!=\!\iota\!\circ\!\psi

satisfies
\mathtt{ker}(\phi_{n})\!=\!\mathtt{ker}(\psi)\!=\!\mathtt{im}(\phi_{n-1})
and
\mathtt{im}(\phi_{n})\!=\!\mathtt{im}(\iota)\!=\!\mathtt{ker}(\phi_{n+1})
,
hence the sequence
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{n-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{n+1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{n+m}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{n-1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\hskip 5.69046pt\phi_{n}\hskip 71.13188pt}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{n+1}}

is exact. This trick allows us to combine exact sequences into longer

5.
The restriction homomorphism

After all these preliminaries, it is time to focus on our basic problem,
namely the relation between the center of an element
\mathfrak{g}\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}

and the centers of its different central quotients/extensions. As
it turns out, it is worth to consider the more general problem of
relating the center of
\mathfrak{g}\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
to that of any
\mathfrak{h}\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}

contained in it. That this approach is meaningful is based on the
following two results from
[
6
]
:

(1)

if
\mathfrak{h}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{g}
, then any
\mathfrak{h}
-class
is a union of
\mathfrak{g}
-classes;

(2)

an
\mathfrak{h}
-class that contains a central
\mathfrak{g}
-class
is itself central.

It follows that for any
\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
with

\mathfrak{h}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{g}
there is a unique central

\mathfrak{h}
-class that contains a given central
\mathfrak{g}
-class

\mathtt{z}\!\in\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Theorem 1
.

For
\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
with

\mathfrak{h}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{g}
, the map that assigns to
each central
\mathfrak{g}
-class the unique
\mathfrak{h}
-class
that contains it is a homomorphism
\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle h}}}\!:\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})\!\rightarrow\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{h})
,
with
image

\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h}\right)\!=\!\left\{\mathtt{z}\!\in\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{h})\!\,|\,\mathtt{z}\!\cap\!\mathfrak{\left(\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}}\right)}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}\!\neq\emptyset\right\}

and
kernel
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h})\!=\!\left\{\mathtt{z}\!\in\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})\,|\,\mathtt{z}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{h}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}\right\}
.

Proof.

Let’s denote by
\mathtt{z}^{\mathfrak{h}}
the
\mathfrak{h}
-class containing
the central
\mathfrak{g}
-class
\mathtt{z}\!\subseteq\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})
. Recall
from Section
3
that to each central class
one can assign its central character, and this provides an explicit
isomorphism between the center and its Pontryagin dual. Because
\mathtt{z}\!\subseteq\!\mathtt{z}^{\mathfrak{h}}

and
\mathfrak{h}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{g}
implies
\boldsymbol{\varpi}_{\mathtt{z}^{\mathfrak{h}}}\!\left(\alpha\right)\!=\!\boldsymbol{\varpi}_{\mathtt{z}}\!\left(\alpha\right)

for
\alpha\!\in\!\mathfrak{h}
, it follows that
\boldsymbol{\varpi}_{(\mathtt{z}_{1}\mathtt{z}_{2})^{\mathfrak{h}}}\!\left(\alpha\right)\!=\!\boldsymbol{\varpi}_{\mathtt{z}_{1}\mathtt{z}_{2}}\!\left(\alpha\right)\!=\!\boldsymbol{\varpi}_{\mathtt{z}_{1}}\!\left(\alpha\right)\boldsymbol{\varpi}_{\mathtt{z}_{2}}\!\left(\alpha\right)\!=\!\boldsymbol{\varpi}_{\mathtt{z}_{1}^{\mathfrak{h}}}\!\left(\alpha\right)\boldsymbol{\varpi}_{\mathtt{z}_{2}^{\mathfrak{h}}}\!\left(\alpha\right)

for
\mathtt{z}_{1},\mathtt{z}_{2}\!\in\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})
. Since a central class is
uniquely determined by its central character, this shows that
(\mathtt{z}_{1}\mathtt{z}_{2})^{\mathfrak{h}}\!=\!\mathtt{z}_{1}^{\mathfrak{h}}\mathtt{z}_{2}^{\mathfrak{h}}
,
proving that
\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle h}}}
is indeed a homomorphism. Now
\mathtt{z}\!\in\!\mathtt{ker}(\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle h}}})
,
i.e.
\mathtt{z}^{\mathfrak{h}}\!=\!\mathfrak{h}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}
precisely when
\mathtt{z}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{h}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}
,
and this proves that
\mathtt{ker}(\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle h}}})\!=\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h})
. But a central
\mathfrak{h}
-class
belongs to the image of
\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle h}}}
in case it contains at least one
central
\mathfrak{g}
-class, i.e. if its intersection with
\mathbf{\boldsymbol{\cup}}\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})

is not empty, one finally concludes that
\mathtt{im}(\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle h}}})\!=\!\left\{\mathtt{z}\!\in\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{h})\!\,|\,\mathtt{z}\cap\mathfrak{\left(\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}}\right)}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}\!\neq\emptyset\right\}\!=\!\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h}\right)

by using Eq.(
3.3
).
∎

Notice that one has
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{g})\!=\!1
and
\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{g}\right)\!=\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})
as a
consequence of Eq.(
3.3
), while
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h})\!=\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})

and
\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h}\right)\!=\!1
for
\mathfrak{h\!\subseteq\!\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}}
, and in
particular
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}})\!=\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})
. Moreover, it follows from
Theorem
1
and the homomorphism theorem
[
10
]

that

(5.1)

\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h}\right)\!\cong\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})\!/\penalty\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h})

and by Lagrange’s theorem this implies that the order of
\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h}\right)

(being a homomorphic image, resp. subgroup) must divide both the order
of
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})
and that of
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{h})
. In particular, if the order
of
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})
and of
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{h})
are coprime, then
\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h}\right)\!=\!1
,
hence
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h})\!=\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})
, and this implies
\mathfrak{\left(\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}}\right)}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}\!=\!\mathbf{\boldsymbol{\cup}}\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{h}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}
,
i.e.
\mathfrak{h\!\subseteq\!\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}}
.

We shall call
\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle h}}}\!:\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})\!\rightarrow\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{h})
the
restriction
homomorphism
. Clearly, for
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{j}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}

the restriction homomorphisms
\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}}
and
\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{h}}}}

can be composed, and this observation leads to the following basic
result.

Lemma 1
.

\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}})<\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})
and
\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}}\right)<\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}}\right)

in case
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{j}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
,
and the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{h}}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{h}}}}}

is commutative, hence
\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{h}}}}\!\left(\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}\right)\right)=\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}}\right)

and
\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}}\!\left(\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})\right)=\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}\right)\cap\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})
.

Proof.

\mathfrak{h}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{j}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{g}

implies
\mathfrak{j}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{h}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}
and
\mathfrak{\left(\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}\right)}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{\left(\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{j}\right)}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}
,
hence
\mathtt{z}\!\in\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}})
implies
\mathtt{z}\!\in\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h})
, while

\mathtt{z}\!\in\!\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}}\right)
gives
\mathtt{z}\!\in\!\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{j}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}}\right)
, proving the
two inclusions. The commutativity of the diagram, i.e. the equality

\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{h}}}}\!\circ\!\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}}\!=\!\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{h}}}}
is
immediate from the definitions, and it implies at once the equality

\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{h}}}}\!\left(\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}\right)\right)\!=\!\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{h}}}}\!\left(\mathtt{im}(\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}})\right)\!=\!\mathtt{im}(\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{h}}}}\!\circ\!\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}})\!=\!\mathtt{im}(\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{h}}}})\!=\!\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}}\right)
.
To prove the last claim, note that in case
\mathtt{z}\!\in\!\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}}\!\left(\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})\right)

there exists
\overline{\mathtt{z}}\!\in\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})
such that
\mathtt{z}\!=\!\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}}\!\left(\overline{\mathtt{z}}\right)
,
hence
\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{h}}}}\!\left(\mathtt{z}\right)\!=\!\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{h}}}}\!\left(\overline{\mathtt{z}}\right)\!=\!\mathfrak{h}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}
,
i.e.
\mathtt{z}\!\in\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})
, and this shows that
\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}}\!\left(\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})\right)\!<\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})
;
as the inclusion
\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}}\!\left(\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})\right)\!<\!\mathtt{im}(\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}})\!=\!\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}\right)

is immediate, we conclude that
\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}}\!\left(\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})\right)\!\subseteq\!\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}\right)\!\cap\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})
.
On the other hand,
\mathtt{z}\!\in\!\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}\right)\cap\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})
if there
exists
\overline{\mathtt{z}}\!\in\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})
such that
\mathtt{z}\!=\!\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}}\!\left(\overline{\mathtt{z}}\right)

belongs to
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})\!=\!\mathtt{ker}(\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{h}}}})
, and this happens
precisely when
\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{h}}}}\!\left(\overline{\mathtt{z}}\right)\!=\!\left(\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{h}}}}\!\circ\!\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}}\right)\!\left(\overline{\mathtt{z}}\right)\!=\!\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{h}}}}\!\left(\mathtt{z}\right)\!=\!\mathfrak{h}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}
,
i.e.
\overline{\mathtt{z}}\!\in\!\mathtt{ker}(\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{h}}}})
, which implies
\mathtt{z}\!\in\!\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}}\!\left(\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})\right)
,
proving finally that
\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}}\!\left(\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})\right)\!\supseteq\!\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}\right)\cap\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})
.
∎

Before the next result, let’s recall the isomorphism theorems of group
theory
[
10
]
: for any two subgroups
A
and
B
of an
abelian group
C
, one has

(5.2)

A/\penalty A\cap B\cong AB/\penalty B

Moreover, in case
A\!<\!B
one has

(5.3)

\left(C/\penalty A\right)\!/\penalty\!\left(B/\penalty A\right)\!\cong\!C/\penalty B

Lemma 2
.

For
\mathfrak{h}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{j}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{g}

one has isomorphisms

(5.4)

\displaystyle\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}\right)\!\cap\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})

\displaystyle\cong\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})\!/\penalty\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}})

(5.5)

\displaystyle\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}}\right)

\displaystyle\cong\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}\right)\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})\!/\penalty\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})

and

(5.6)

\displaystyle\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}}\right)\!/\penalty\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}}\right)

\displaystyle\cong\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}})\!/\penalty\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}\right)\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})

Proof.

Consider the restriction of
\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}}
to
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})
. By Lemma
1
,
its image equals
\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}\right)\cap\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})
, while its kernel
is
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}})
, and Eq.(
5.4
) follows from the homomorphism
theorem. Next, denote by
\phi
the restriction of
\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{h}}}}

to
\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}\right)
: since, once again by Lemma
1
, one
has
\mathtt{im}(\phi)\!=\!\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{h}}}}\!\left(\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}\right)\right)\!=\!\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}}\right)

and
\mathtt{ker}(\phi)\!=\!\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle g}}}^{\mathfrak{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{j}}}}\!\left(\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})\right)\!=\!\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}\right)\cap\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})
,
hence
\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}}\right)\!\cong\!\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}\right)\!/\penalty\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}\right)\cap\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})
,
and Eq.(
5.5
) follows from this by substituting
A\!=\!\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}\right)

and
B\!=\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})
into Eq.(
5.2
). Finally, Eq.(
5.6
)
follows from Eq.(
5.5
) by substituting
A\!=\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})
,

B\!=\!\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}\right)\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})
and
C\!=\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}})
into Eq.(
5.3
)

\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}}\right)\!/\penalty\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}}\right)\!\cong\!\left(\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}})\!/\penalty\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})\right)\!/\penalty\!\left(\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}\right)\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})\!/\penalty\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})\right)\!\cong\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}})\!/\penalty\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}\right)\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})

and taking into account that
\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}}\right)\!\cong\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}})/\penalty\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})

by Eq.(
5.1
).
∎

Lemma 3
.

\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{1}}})\cap\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{2}}})=\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{1}}\mathrm{\vee\,}\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{2}}})

and
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{1}}})\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{2}}})\!<\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{1}}\!\cap\!\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{2}}})
in case
\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{1}}},\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{2}}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{g}
,
and in particular
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h\!\vee\!\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}})\!=\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h})
and
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h\!\cap\!\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}})\!=\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})

for any
\mathfrak{h}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

\mathtt{z}\!\in\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})
belongs to
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{1}}})\!\cap\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{2}}})
if
it is contained in both
\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{1}}}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}
and
\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{2}}}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}
, i.e.
\mathtt{z}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{1}}}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}\!\cap\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{2}}}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}=\!\mathfrak{\left(\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{1}}\mathrm{\vee\,}\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{2}}\right)}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}
,
proving the first claim. But according to Lemma
2

the inclusions
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{1}}\!\cap\!\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{2}}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{1}}},\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{2}}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{g}

imply that both
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{1}}})
and
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{2}}})
are subgroups of
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{1}}\!\cap\!\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{2}}})
,
hence
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{1}}})\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{2}}})\!<\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{1}}\!\cap\!\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{2}}})
. The final
claim follows from the above by substituting
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{1}}\!=\!\mathfrak{h}

and
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{2}}\!=\!\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}
, and taking into account
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}})\!=\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})
.
∎

6.
The Galois correspondence

Let’s introduce the notation
\mathfrak{h}\!\propto\!\mathfrak{g}
to indicate that
\mathfrak{h\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}}

is a central quotient of
\mathfrak{g}\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
(equivalently,
that
\mathfrak{g}
is a central extension of
\mathfrak{h}
); in
other words,
\mathfrak{h}\!\propto\!\mathfrak{g}
means
\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{h}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{g}

or, what is the same,
\mathfrak{h}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{g}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}}^{\intercal}

(c.f. Section
3
). Note that this is not
a transitive relation, i.e.
\mathfrak{h}\!\propto\!\mathfrak{j}
and
\mathfrak{j}\!\propto\!\mathfrak{g}
does not necessarily
imply
\mathfrak{h}\!\propto\!\mathfrak{g}
. On the other hand, because every central quotient
is a central extension of the maximal central quotient,
\mathfrak{h}\!\propto\!\mathfrak{g}

implies
\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}}\!\propto\!\mathfrak{h}
, and this gives in turn
\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{h}}\!\propto\!\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}}
.

Theorem 2
.

\mathbf{\boldsymbol{\cup}}\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h})\!=\!\mathfrak{h}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}
and
\mathbf{\boldsymbol{\cup}}\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h}\right)\!=\!\mathfrak{\left(\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}}\right)}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}

in case
\mathfrak{h}\!\propto\!\mathfrak{g}
, hence

\mathfrak{h}\!=\!\mathfrak{g}/\penalty\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h})

and

\mathfrak{h}/\penalty\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h}\right)\!=\!\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}
;

in particular,

(6.1)

\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h}\right)\!=\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{h}|\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}})

Proof.

If
\mathfrak{h}
is a central quotient of
\mathfrak{g}
, then

\mathfrak{h}\!=\!\mathfrak{g}/\penalty G
for some
G\!<\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})

with
\mathbf{\boldsymbol{\cup}}G\!=\!\mathfrak{h}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}
, c.f. Section
3
,
hence all
\mathfrak{g}
-classes contained in
\mathfrak{h}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}
are central
and
\mathbf{\boldsymbol{\cup}}\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h})\!=\!\mathfrak{h}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}
, consequently
G\!=\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h})
. But

\mathfrak{h}\!\propto\!\mathfrak{g}
implies
\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}}\!\propto\!\mathfrak{h}
, hence
\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}\!=\!\mathfrak{h}/\penalty H

for some subgroup
H\!<\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{h})
, and because
\mathbf{\boldsymbol{\cup}}H\!=\!\mathfrak{\left(\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}\right)}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}
,
every
\mathfrak{h}
-class contained in
\mathfrak{\left(\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}}\right)}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}
is central; since

\mathfrak{\left(\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}}\right)}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}
is a union of
\mathfrak{h}
-classes, we get that
\mathbf{\boldsymbol{\cup}}\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h}\right)\!=\!\mathfrak{\left(\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}\right)}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}\!=\!\mathbf{\boldsymbol{\cup}}\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{h}|\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}})
,
proving that indeed
\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h}\right)\!=\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{h}|\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}})
.
∎

To better grasp the meaning of Theorem
2
, let’s rewrite
it in a (perhaps) more suggestive form. To this end, consider a subgroup

H\!<\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})
, and let
\mathfrak{h}\!=\!\mathfrak{g}/\!H
, so that
\mathfrak{h}\!\propto\!\mathfrak{g}
.
Since
\mathfrak{h}\!\propto\!\mathfrak{g}
implies
\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{h}}\!\propto\!\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}}
, there exists a subgroup

\boldsymbol{\partial}H\!<\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}})
such that
\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{h}\!=\!\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}}/\!\mathit{\boldsymbol{\partial}H}
,
and by Theorem
2
one has
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h})\!=\!H
and
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}}|\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{h}})\!=\!\boldsymbol{\partial}H
.
It follows from Eq.(
5.1
) that there is a short exact sequence

(6.2)

\begin{CD}1@>{}>{}>\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h})@>{}>{}>\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})@>{}>{}>\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h}\right)@>{}>{}>1\end{CD}

As
\mathfrak{h}\!\propto\!\mathfrak{g}
implies
\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}}\!\propto\!\mathfrak{h}
, we can substitute
\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}

for
\mathfrak{h}
, and simultaneously
\mathfrak{h}
for
\mathfrak{g}

in Eq.(
6.2
) to yield the exact sequence

(6.3)

\begin{CD}1@>{}>{}>\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{h}|\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}})@>{}>{}>\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{h})@>{}>{}>\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{h}|\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}}\right)@>{}>{}>1\end{CD}

But
\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{h}|\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}}\right)\!=\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}}|\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{h}})\!=\!\boldsymbol{\partial}H

and
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{h}|\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}})\!=\!\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h}\right)\!\cong\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})\!/\penalty\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h})\!=\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})\!/\penalty\!H

by Theorem

and Eq.(

), hence we arrive
finally at the exact sequence

(6.4)

\begin{CD}1@>{}>{}>\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})\!/\penalty\!H@>{}>{}>\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}/\!\mathit{H}})@>{}>{}>\boldsymbol{\partial}H@>{}>{}>1\end{CD}

which shows clearly that
\boldsymbol{\partial}H
measures the extent

\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}/\!\mathit{H}})
differs from the naive expectation

\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})\!/\penalty\!H
. In particular, one has
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}/\!\mathit{H}})\!\cong\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})\!/\penalty\!H

only in case
\boldsymbol{\partial}H\!=\!1
, i.e. when
\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}\!=\!\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{h}
.

Lemma 4
.

If
\mathfrak{h}\!\propto\!\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{j}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{g}
,
then
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{j}|\mathfrak{h})\!<\!\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{j}\right)

\displaystyle\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})

\displaystyle\cong\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})\!/\penalty\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}})

and

(6.6)

\displaystyle\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}}|\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{j}})

\displaystyle\cong\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}}\right)\!/\penalty\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}}\right)

Proof.

\mathfrak{h}\!\propto\!\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{j}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{g}

imply
\mathfrak{j}\!\propto\!\mathfrak{g}
, hence
\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}}\!\propto\!\mathfrak{j}
, consequently
\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{j}\right)\!=\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{j}|\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}})

and
\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{j}|\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}}\right)\!=\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}}|\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{j}})
according to Theorem

.
On the other hand,
\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{h}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{j}

gives
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{j}|\mathfrak{h})\!<\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{j}|\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}})
according to Lemma

,
hence
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{j}|\mathfrak{h})\!<\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{j}|\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}})\!=\!\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{j}\right)

\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{j}|\mathfrak{h})\!<\!\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{j}\right)

implies
\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}\right)\!\cap\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})\!=\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})
and

\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}\right)\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}})\!=\!\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{j}}\right)
, and taking this into

) reduces to Eq.(

), while

in view of Theorem

and Eq.(

).

For subgroups

that of central extensions of

2
, one gets an interesting extension of the

\mathfrak{h}\!\propto\!\mathfrak{g}

\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}}\!\propto\!\mathfrak{h}

\mathfrak{h}

Lemma 5

In case

between

and subgroups of
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h})

\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}\!\left(\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}},\!\mathfrak{h}\right)

\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h}\right)

Theorem 3
.

\mathfrak{g}/\penalty\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h})\!=\!\mathfrak{h}\!\vee\!\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}

and
\mathfrak{h}/\penalty\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h}\right)\!=\!\mathfrak{h}\!\cap\!\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}

for

\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h}\right)\!=\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{h}|\mathfrak{h\!\cap\!\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}})

Proof.

As
\mathfrak{h}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{g}

\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h\!\vee\!\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}})\!=\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h})

by Lemma

\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h}\right)\!=\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{h}|\mathfrak{h\!\cap\!\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}})

\mathfrak{j}\!=\!\mathfrak{h}/\penalty\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h}\right)
;
we claim that
\mathfrak{j}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{h}\!\cap\!\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}

The inclusion
\mathfrak{j}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{h}

\mathfrak{g}

\mathfrak{j}\!\subseteq\!\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}
. But
\mathfrak{j}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{h}\!\cap\!\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}

means that

,
hence
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{h}|\mathfrak{h\!\cap\!\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}})
is a subgroup of
\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h}\right)
by Lemma

.

According to Lemma

, there is a one-to-one correspondence
between subgroups of
\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}\!\vee\!\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}}\right)
and
\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}\!\left(\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}},\!\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}\!\vee\!\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}}\right)

on one hand, and between subgroups of
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{h}|\mathfrak{h\!\cap\!\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}})
and

\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}\!\left(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}\!\cap\!\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}},\!\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}}\right)
on the other. But

\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}\!\vee\!\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}}\right)\!\cong\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})\!/\penalty\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h\!\vee\!\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}})\!=\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})\!/\penalty\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h})\!\cong\!\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h}\right)

by Eq.(

be a proper

\mathtt{I}\!\left(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h}\right)

\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}\!\left(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}\!\cap\!\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}},\!\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}}\right)

and
\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}\!\left(\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}},\!\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}\!\vee\!\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}}\right)
that follows from
the modularity of the deconstruction lattice.
∎

\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}}|\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}})\!\cong\!\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g}|\mathfrak{h})

9.
Summary and outlook

The main theme of our investigations was to characterize the mutual
relations between the centers of different elements of the deconstruction
lattice, with particular emphasis on the central quotients and extensions
of a given
\mathfrak{g}\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
. We have seen that one can go
a long way in this direction using exact sequences, as exhibited by
results like Eq.(
6.4
), Theorem
4
and Eq.(
8.2
).
While not providing a direct and constructive description of the relevant
groups, this information is usually enough to pin down (more or less
uniquely) their structure, and this is what matters for most applications.
Once the relevant centers are known, one can use this knowledge to
simplify greatly otherwise cumbersome computations that could prove
difficult in case of large examples. In particular, the explicit Galois
correspondence described by Lemma
5
is a very useful
tool in actual computations, while Lemma
8
settles
the dual relation of central quotients and extensions.

Apart from the practical considerations set forth above, there are
more conceptual issues underlying the interest in the previous results.
One such is the search for an analogue of the famous lemma of Gr�n

[
10
]
, according to which the upper central series of a
perfect group has length at most two. In our context this would mean
that, provided
\mathfrak{g}\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
does not contain any simple
current, the maximal central quotient
\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}
should be centerless,
i.e.
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}})\!=\!1
. While this holds automatically for local

\mathfrak{g}\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
corresponding to some finite group, the
generic case seems much more difficult to prove, as there is no clear
adaptation of the group theoretic techniques used in the proof of
Gr�n’s lemma.

Another interesting question concerns the analogue of Ito’s theorem

[
22
,
23
]
, which in our case is tantamount to the claim
that the ratio

\frac{1}{\mathtt{d}_{\alpha}}\sum_{p\in\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathfrak{g}}\mathtt{d}_{p}^{2}

is an algebraic integer for every
\alpha\!\in\!\mathfrak{g}
. Should
this claim hold, it would restrict severely the arithmetic properties
of the quantum dimensions and, more generally, the Galois action on
the primaries
[
6
]
. As before, the claim follows from
known group theoretic results for local
\mathfrak{g}\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}

corresponding to some finite group, but its generalization to arbitrary

\mathfrak{g}\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
is far from being obvious.

Finally, we should note that in the preceding discussions we have
neglected one important aspect, namely that the center
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})

of any given
\mathfrak{g}\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
is not simply an abelian group,
but has a natural permutation action on the set of
\mathfrak{g}
-classes

[
6
]
, which is compatible with the restriction map.
This permutation action is far from being arbitrary, as can be seen
most directly on the example of abelian
\mathfrak{g}
, i.e. when
all primaries in
\mathfrak{g}
are simple currents, since in this
case the
\mathfrak{g}
-classes are in one-to-one correspondence
with the characters of
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})
, and the permutation action is regular,
i.e. the action of
\mathtt{Z}(\mathfrak{g})
on itself by translations. On the other
extreme, if
\mathfrak{g}
equals the maximal element of the deconstruction
lattice
\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
, then each
\mathfrak{g}
-class contains precisely
one primary, with central classes containing the simple currents,
and the corresponding permutation action is nothing but the action
of the group of simple currents on the set of primaries that is induced
by the fusion product. This latter action is known to have non-trivial
properties
[
24
]
, and it is natural to expect that, for

\mathfrak{g}\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
intermediate between these two extremes,
the corresponding permutation action still enjoys some interesting
properties. But this circle of questions lies clearly outside the
scope of the present note.

Appendix A
Modularity of the deconstruction lattice

The following characterization of the join operation in
\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}

is interesting in itself.

Lemma 9
.

For
\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
, a primary

p
belongs to the join
\mathfrak{g}\!\vee\!\mathfrak{h}
iff there
exists
\alpha\!\in\!\mathfrak{g}
and
\beta\!\in\!\mathfrak{h}

such that
N_{\alpha\beta}^{p}>0
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{g}\sqcup\mathfrak{h}\!=\!\left\{p\,|\,N_{\alpha\beta}^{p}\!>\!0\textrm{ for some }\alpha\!\in\!\mathfrak{g}\textrm{ and }\beta\!\in\!\mathfrak{h}\right\}
.
Clearly, both
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
are contained in

\mathfrak{g}\sqcup\mathfrak{h}
. On the other hand,
p\!\in\!\mathfrak{g}\sqcup\mathfrak{h}
means that there exists

\alpha\!\in\!\mathfrak{g}
and
\beta\!\in\!\mathfrak{h}
such
that
N_{\alpha\beta}^{p}\!>\!0
: since
\mathfrak{g}\!\vee\!\mathfrak{h}

is the least element of
\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
containing both
\mathfrak{g}
and

\mathfrak{h}
, we have necessarily
p\!\in\!\mathfrak{g}\!\vee\!\mathfrak{h}
,
hence
\mathfrak{g}\sqcup\mathfrak{h}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{g}\!\vee\!\mathfrak{h}
. To prove
that they are actually equal, it suffices to show that
\mathfrak{g}\sqcup\mathfrak{h}\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
,
i.e. that
p,q\!\in\!\mathfrak{g}\sqcup\mathfrak{h}
and
N_{pq}^{r}\!>\!0
implies
r\!\in\!\mathfrak{g}\sqcup\mathfrak{h}
.
But this is tantamount to showing that, if there exists
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\!\in\!\mathfrak{g}

and
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\!\in\!\mathfrak{h}
such that
N_{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}}^{p}\!>\!0

and
N_{\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}}^{q}\!>\!0
, then for all
r
such that

N_{pq}^{r}\!>\!0
there exists
\alpha_{3}\!\in\!\mathfrak{g}

and
\beta_{3}\!\in\!\mathfrak{h}
satisfying
N_{\alpha_{3}\beta_{3}}^{r}\!>\!0
.
By the associativity of the fusion algebra, it follows form our assumptions
that

\displaystyle 0<N_{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}}^{p}N_{\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}}^{q}N_{pq}^{r}\leq\sum_{s,t}N_{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}}^{s}N_{\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}}^{t}N_{st}^{r}=\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\

\displaystyle\sum_{w,t}N_{\alpha_{1}t}^{w}N_{\beta_{1}w}^{r}N_{\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}}^{t}=\sum_{w,u}N_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}^{u}N_{\beta_{2}u}^{w}N_{\beta_{1}w}^{r}=\sum_{u,v}N_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}^{u}N_{\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}^{v}N_{uv}^{r}

i.e. there should exist primaries
\alpha_{3}
and
\beta_{3}
such
that
N_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}^{\alpha_{3}}
,
N_{\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}^{\beta_{3}}

and
N_{\alpha_{3}\beta_{3}}^{r}
are all positive. But
\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}

and
N_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}^{\alpha_{3}}N_{\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}^{\beta_{3}}>0

implies that
\alpha_{3}\!\in\!\mathfrak{g}
and
\beta_{3}\!\in\!\mathfrak{h}
,
consequently
r\!\in\!\mathfrak{g}\sqcup\mathfrak{h}
.
∎

The previous result allows for the following streamlined proof of
the modularity of
\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
(recall that
N_{pq}^{\overline{r}}\!=\!N_{pr}^{\overline{q}}
,
with
\overline{p}
denoting the charge conjugate of
p
).

Theorem 5
.

The lattice
\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}
is modular, that is

\mathfrak{h}_{2}\cap\left(\mathfrak{h}_{1}\vee\mathfrak{g}\right)\subseteq\mathfrak{h}_{1}\vee\left(\mathfrak{h}_{2}\cap\mathfrak{g}\right)

for
\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}_{1},\mathfrak{h}_{2}\!\in\!\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}

such that
\mathfrak{h}_{1}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{h}_{2}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\alpha\!\in\!\mathfrak{h}_{2}\cap\left(\mathfrak{h}_{1}\vee\mathfrak{g}\right)
.
Then
\alpha\!\in\!\mathfrak{h}_{2}
, hence
\overline{\alpha}\!\in\!\mathfrak{h}_{2}
,
and by Lemma
9
there exists primaries
\beta\!\in\!\mathfrak{h}_{1}

and
\gamma\!\in\!\mathfrak{g}
such that
N_{\beta\gamma}^{\alpha}\!>\!0
.
But
\beta\!\in\!\mathfrak{h}_{2}
since
\mathfrak{h}_{1}\!\subseteq\!\mathfrak{h}_{2}
,
consequently
N_{\beta\overline{\alpha}}^{\overline{\gamma}}\!=\!N_{\beta\gamma}^{\alpha}

implies
\overline{\gamma}\!\in\!\mathfrak{h}_{2}
, i.e.
\gamma\!\in\!\mathfrak{h}_{2}
.
All in all, we get that
N_{\beta\gamma}^{\alpha}\!>\!0
with
\beta\!\in\!\mathfrak{h}_{1}

and
\gamma\!\in\!\mathfrak{g\cap\mathfrak{h}}_{2}
, consequently

\alpha\!\in\!\mathfrak{h}_{1}\!\vee\!\left(\mathfrak{h}_{2}\cap\mathfrak{g}\right)

by Lemma
9
.
∎

Modularity has many important consequences, e.g. the Kurosh-Ore theorem

[
9
]
, but we make mainly use of the so-called diamond
isomorphism theorem: for any two elements
a,b
of a modular lattice

\left(L,\wedge,\vee\right)
, there is an order-preserving one-to-one
correspondence between the sets
\left\{x\!\in\!L\,|\,a\wedge b\leq x\leq a\right\}

and
\left\{x\!\in\!L\,|\,b\leq x\leq a\vee b\right\}
.

On fast charged particles scattering on zigzag nanotube
V.D. Omelchenko

Abstract
A fast charged particle scattering on a single-wall carbon nanotube of zigzag type was considered. The differential cross sections of scattering on nanotubes of different spatial orientation with respect to the incident particles were obtained. The eikonal approximation of quantum electrodynamics and the continuous potential approximation were used.



1
Introduction

Studying the process of scattering of fast charged particles on nanotubes
[
1
]
is important and interesting problem from at least two points of view. On the one hand, nanotubes can serve as channels through which positive particles can be guided toward certain directions
[
2
,
3
]
. This property provides opportunities for beam control. On the other hand, nanotubes can be examined with high-energy particles. One of the instruments for such an examination is the rainbow scattering which is sensitive to the structure of the target
[
4
,
5
]
.

In this paper, we are considering a fast charged particle scattering on a zigzag carbon nanotube with different orientations with respect to the incident particle. As description of the scattering process, we find the differential scattering cross sections in the eikonal approximation of quantum electrodynamics
[
6
]
. The eikonal approximation was chosen for its benefits: contrary to the classical approach, it accounts for quantum nature of the incident particle, and unlike frequently used Born approximation, it has a wider applicability region.

2
Problem formulation

Let us consider a fast charged particle scattering on a
(n,0)
single-wall zigzag nanotube of a certain spatial orientation. Let us denote the coordinates as
\vec{r}=(\vec{\rho},z)
,
\vec{\rho}=(x,y)
. Let the initial momentum of the particle
\vec{p}
be parallel to
z
-axis. Let there be a straight alignment of the nanotube – when its axis is parallel to
z
-axis, and tilted – when there is an angle
\theta
between the nanotube axis and
z
-axis in the
(z,x)
-plane (Fig.
1
). Let us consider nanotubes as sets of
N_{s}=2n
atomic strings (Fig.
2
). The nanotube and its strings have the same length
L_{z}
, which for even or odd number
N_{z}
of atoms in the string is:

\displaystyle L_{z}=\begin{cases}a_{g}\left(\frac{3}{2}N_{z}-1\right),\ N_{z}=2m;\\
\frac{3}{2}a_{g}(N_{z}-1),\ N_{z}=2m+1;\end{cases}

(1)

where
a_{g}=1.42\ \text{\AA}
is the length of the bond between carbon atoms.

(a)

straight nanotube

(b)

tilted nanotube

For a straight nanotube, these atomic strings have positions
\vec{\rho}_{j}=(x_{j},y_{j})
in the vertices of a regular
2n
-polygon. The edges of this polygon are of length
a_{g}\cos\frac{\pi}{6}
. Let us also associate the index
k
with atom’s number along each string, so that
z_{k}
is atom’s position along the axis of the corresponding string. Let
z_{k}\in\left[-L_{z}/2,L_{z}/2\right]
. For a tilted nanotube, the positions of individual atoms in the string are
\vec{\rho}_{jk}=(\tilde{x}_{jk},y_{j})
,
\tilde{x}_{jk}=x_{j}\cos\theta+z_{k}\sin\theta
, which for small enough
\theta
are approximately

\displaystyle\tilde{x}_{jk}=x_{j}+z_{k}\sin\theta.

(2)

The
j
-th tilted string projection to the
x
-axis is approximately

\displaystyle x\in\left[x_{j}-\frac{L_{z}}{2}|\sin\theta|,x_{j}+\frac{L_{z}}{2}|\sin\theta|\right].

(3)

Let us also account for the thermal vibrations of atoms in the nanotube by introducing each atom’s position displacement
\vec{u}_{jk}=(u_{x,jk},u_{y,jk})
in the
(x,y)
-plane. We assume that the law of distribution of these values is Gaussian:

\displaystyle f(\vec{u}_{jk})=\frac{\exp\left(-\ \frac{u_{jk}^{2}}{2\langle{u^{2}_{x}}\rangle}\right)}{2\pi\langle{u^{2}_{x}}\rangle},

(4)

where
\langle{u^{2}_{x}}\rangle
is mean square value of thermal displacements of atoms both in
x
and
y
directions,
{u}_{jk}^{2}=u_{x,jk}^{2}+u_{y,jk}^{2}
.

In the eikonal approximation, the distribution of atoms in the target along
z
-axis (direction of initial momentum
\vec{p}
) does not influence the differential scattering cross section. So, we do not specify atoms positions and displacements in
z
-axis direction.

3
Differential scattering cross section

We will use basic ideas of the eikonal approximation
[
6
]
and Glauber’s approach
[
7
]
to obtain the differential scattering cross section for this case, analogously to what we did in
[
8
]
. We start with general formula for the scattering amplitude in the eikonal approximation
[
6
]
:

\displaystyle a(\vec{q}_{\perp})=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d^{2}\rho\ e^{i\vec{q}_{\perp}\vec{\rho}}\left\{1-\exp\left[i\chi^{(N)}_{0}(\vec{\rho})\right]\right\},

(5)

where
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{M}}d^{2}\rho...
denotes the integral over the entire real
M
-dimensional volume,

\displaystyle\chi^{(N)}_{0}(\vec{\rho})=-e\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dzU^{(N)}(\vec{\rho},z)

(6)

and
U^{(N)}(\vec{r})=\sum_{m=1}^{N}u(\vec{r}-\vec{r}_{m})
is total target’s potential being a sum of individual potentials
u(\vec{r}-\vec{r}_{m})
of all
N
atoms of the target, where each of them has position
\vec{r}_{m}
. We note that
N=N_{s}N_{z}
in our case. So, it follows that

\displaystyle\chi^{(N)}_{0}(\vec{\rho})=\sum_{m=1}^{N}\chi_{0}(\vec{\rho}-\vec{\rho}_{m}),

(7)

where

\displaystyle\chi_{0}(\vec{\rho}-\vec{\rho}_{m})=-e\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dzu(\vec{\rho}-\vec{\rho}_{m},z).

(8)

The differential scattering cross section
[
6
]

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{d^{2}q_{\perp}}=|{a}(\vec{q}_{\perp})|^{2}

(9)

with amplitude (
5
) is difficult to calculate due to complicated form of the real target potential. So, we need to average the differential cross section over positions of atoms in the target:

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{d^{2}q_{\perp}}=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}d^{2}\rho d^{2}\rho^{\prime}e^{i\vec{q}_{\perp}(\vec{\rho}-\vec{\rho}^{\prime})}\langle 1-e^{i\chi^{(N)}_{0}(\vec{\rho})}-e^{-i\chi^{(N)}_{0}(\vec{\rho}^{\prime})}+e^{i[\chi^{(N)}_{0}(\vec{\rho})-\chi^{(N)}_{0}(\vec{\rho}^{\prime})]}\rangle,

(10)

where brackets
\langle...\rangle
denote averaging.
Let us consider the term
\langle e^{i[\chi^{(N)}_{0}(\vec{\rho})-\chi^{(N)}_{0}(\vec{\rho}^{\prime})]}\rangle
for the case of a straight nanotube:

\displaystyle\langle e^{i[\chi^{(N)}_{0}(\vec{\rho})-\chi^{(N)}_{0}(\vec{\rho}^{\prime})]}\rangle=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N_{s}}\prod_{k=1}^{N_{z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d^{2}u_{jk}f(\vec{u}_{jk})\right)\times

\displaystyle\times\prod_{j=1}^{N_{s}}\prod_{k=1}^{N_{z}}\exp\left(i[\chi_{0}(\vec{\rho}-\vec{\rho}_{j}-\vec{u}_{jk})-\chi_{0}(\vec{\rho}^{\prime}-\vec{\rho}_{j}-\vec{u}_{jk})]\right),

(11)

Considering that
f(\vec{u}_{jk})
is the same for all atoms since all atoms’ displacements are distributed according to the same law, we can write (
3
) in the form

\displaystyle\langle e^{i[\chi^{(N)}_{0}(\vec{\rho})-\chi^{(N)}_{0}(\vec{\rho}^{\prime})]}\rangle=\prod_{j=1}^{N_{s}}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d^{2}uf(\vec{u})\exp\left(i[\chi_{0}(\vec{\rho}-\vec{\rho}_{j}-\vec{u})]-\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.-\chi_{0}(\vec{\rho}^{\prime}-\vec{\rho}_{j}-\vec{u})]\right)\right\}^{N_{z}},

(12)

where
\vec{u}
is a value with distribution law (
4
).

Using Glauber’s idea
[
7
]
we can rewrite it as
[
8
,
9
]

\displaystyle\langle e^{i[\chi^{(N)}_{0}(\vec{\rho})-\chi^{(N)}_{0}(\vec{\rho}^{\prime})]}\rangle=\exp\left\{N_{z}\left[i\sum_{j=1}^{N_{s}}\langle\chi_{(j)}-\chi^{\prime}_{(j)}\rangle-\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{s}}\langle\left(\chi_{(j)}-\chi^{\prime}_{(j)}\right)^{2}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{s}}\langle\chi_{(j)}-\chi^{\prime}_{(j)}\rangle^{2}+...\right]\right\},

(13)

where
\chi_{(j)}=\chi_{0}(\vec{\rho}-\vec{\rho}_{j}-\vec{u})
,
\chi^{\prime}_{(j)}=\chi_{0}(\vec{\rho}^{\prime}-\vec{\rho}_{j}-\vec{u})
.
Expression
\langle e^{i\chi^{(N)}_{0}(\vec{\rho})}\rangle
is obtained using the same idea, but with
\chi^{\prime}_{(j)}=0
. Expression
\langle e^{-i\chi^{(N)}_{0}(\vec{\rho}^{\prime})}\rangle
is complex conjugate of
\langle e^{i\chi^{(N)}_{0}(\vec{\rho})}\rangle
with
\vec{\rho}=\vec{\rho}^{\prime}
.
If we consider (
10
) up to linear potential terms:
\sum_{j=1}^{N_{s}}\langle\chi_{(j)}-\chi^{\prime}_{(j)}\rangle
,
\sum_{j=1}^{N_{s}}\langle\chi_{(j)}\rangle
,
\sum_{j=1}^{N_{s}}\langle\chi^{\prime}_{(j)}\rangle
, the differential scattering cross section can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{d^{2}q_{\perp}}=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d^{2}\rho\ e^{i\vec{q}_{\perp}\vec{\rho}}\left\{1-\exp\left[iN_{z}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{s}}\langle\chi_{(j)}\rangle\right]\right\}\Big|^{2}.

(14)

The value
\langle\chi_{(j)}\rangle
is the same for all atoms in the straight string so we can consider
N_{z}\langle\chi_{(j)}\rangle=\tilde{\chi}_{(j)}^{(s)}
, where
\tilde{\chi}_{(j)}^{(s)}=\tilde{\chi}_{0}^{(s)}(\vec{\rho}-\vec{\rho}_{j})
is
\chi_{0}
-function for
j
-th straight string of atoms. The function
\tilde{\chi}_{(j)}^{(s)}=N_{z}\langle\chi_{(j)}\rangle
corresponds to the average potential of an atomic string in the continuous potential approximation
[
10
]
. So, the scattering amplitude becomes

\displaystyle a(\vec{q}_{\perp})=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d^{2}\rho\ e^{i\vec{q}_{\perp}\vec{\rho}}\left\{1-\exp\left[i\sum_{j=1}^{N_{s}}\tilde{\chi}_{(j)}^{(s)}\right]\right\}.

(15)

In the works
[
8
,
11
]
, we showed that for structures which can be considered isolated, the amplitude of scattering on a set of these structures can be expressed through structure factor and the amplitude of scattering on a single structure:

\displaystyle a(\vec{q}_{\perp})=S_{N_{s}}\ a^{(1)}(\vec{q}_{\perp}),

(16)

where structure factor
S_{N_{s}}
is

\displaystyle S_{N_{s}}=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{s}}e^{i\vec{q}_{\perp}\vec{\rho}_{j}},

(17)

the amplitude of scattering on a single structure is

\displaystyle a^{(1)}(\vec{q}_{\perp})=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d^{2}\rho\ e^{i\vec{q}_{\perp}\vec{\rho}}\left\{1-\exp\left[i\tilde{\chi}_{(0)}^{(s)}\right]\right\}

(18)

and
\tilde{\chi}_{(0)}=\tilde{\chi}_{0}^{(s)}(\vec{\rho})
corresponds to the
\chi_{0}
-function for a single structure – for a single straight string, in our case – which is located at the coordinate origin. We note that atomic strings should be considered isolated if the distance between neighboring strings is much greater than the distance at which their potentials significantly decrease. Then, the differential cross section of scattering on the set of these structures is also expressed through structure factor and the differential cross section of scattering on a single structure.

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{d^{2}q_{\perp}}=D_{N_{s}}\frac{d\sigma^{(1)}}{d^{2}q_{\perp}},

(19)

where
D_{N_{s}}=\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{N_{s}}e^{i\vec{q}_{\perp}\vec{\rho}_{j}}\Big|^{2}
coincides with Laue-Bragg factor
[
6
]
and

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma^{(1)}}{d^{2}q_{\perp}}=\Big|a^{(1)}(\vec{q}_{\perp})\Big|^{2}

(20)

is the differential cross section of scattering on a single structure (atomic string in our case). Later we will discuss whether this approximation is applicable for this problem. Also, in the next section we will discuss how these calculations relate to the case of scattering on a tilted nanotube.

4
Pontential-dependent functions

To calculate the differential scattering cross sections both for straight and tilted nanotubes we need to know the corresponding
\chi_{0}
-functions for these nanotubes which centers are located at
(0,0)
in the
(x,y)
-plane, which means
x_{j}=0
,
y_{j}=0
. So, index
j
may be omitted in these calculations. To restore the dependence on
j
, one needs to make substitution
\vec{\rho}\rightarrow\vec{\rho}-\vec{\rho}_{j}
in the final expression for
\chi_{0}
-function.

To obtain
\tilde{\chi}_{(0)}
for a straight atomic string, we need to calculate
\langle\chi_{0}\rangle
for a single atom and multiply it by amount
N_{z}
of atoms in the string.
Let us define each atom’s potential as screened Coulomb potential
[
6
]

\displaystyle u(\vec{r})=\frac{Ze}{r}\exp\left(-\frac{r}{R}\right),

(21)

where
R
is the screening radius,
Ze
is the charge of atom nucleus.
Using the Fourier component of unaveraged
\chi_{0}
-function for a single atom (defined in (
8
))
[
6
]

\displaystyle\chi_{\vec{\mu}}=\frac{4\pi Z\alpha}{\mu^{2}+R^{-2}}

(22)

where
\vec{\mu}=(\mu_{x},\mu_{y})
,
\mu^{2}=\mu_{x}^{2}+\mu_{y}^{2}
, we can write the expression for the averaged
\langle\chi_{0}\rangle
as

\displaystyle\langle\chi_{0}\rangle=4\pi Z\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d^{2}u\ \frac{e^{-\ \frac{u^{2}}{2\langle{u^{2}_{x}}\rangle}}}{2\pi\langle{u^{2}_{x}}\rangle}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{d^{2}\mu}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{i\mu_{x}(x-u_{x})}e^{i\mu_{y}(y-u_{y})}\frac{1}{\mu^{2}+R^{-2}}.

(23)

After integration over
u_{x}
,
u_{y}
we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\chi_{0}\rangle=\frac{Z\alpha}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d^{2}\mu\ e^{i\mu_{x}x}e^{i\mu_{y}y}e^{-\ \frac{\mu^{2}_{x}\langle{u^{2}_{x}}\rangle}{2}}e^{-\ \frac{\mu^{2}_{y}\langle{u^{2}_{x}}\rangle}{2}}\frac{1}{\mu^{2}+R^{-2}}.

(24)

Spherical symmetry of the integral leads to

\displaystyle\langle\chi_{0}\rangle=2Z\alpha\int_{0}^{\infty}d\mu\ J_{0}(\mu\rho)\mu\ e^{-\ \frac{\mu^{2}\langle{u^{2}_{x}}\rangle}{2}}\frac{1}{\mu^{2}+R^{-2}}

(25)

This integral can be exactly represented through series:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}d\mu\ J_{0}(\mu\rho)\mu\ \frac{e^{-\ \frac{\mu^{2}\langle{u^{2}_{x}}\rangle}{2}}}{\mu^{2}+R^{-2}}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\ e^{\frac{\langle{u^{2}_{x}}\rangle}{2R^{2}}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\left(\frac{\rho^{2}}{2\langle{u^{2}_{x}}\rangle}\right)^{k}E_{k+1}\left(\frac{\langle{u^{2}_{x}}\rangle}{2R^{2}}\right),\\
e^{\frac{\langle{u^{2}_{x}}\rangle}{2R^{2}}}\left[K_{0}\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\left(\frac{\langle{u^{2}_{x}}\rangle}{2R^{2}}\right)^{k}E_{k+1}\left(\frac{\rho^{2}}{2\langle{u^{2}_{x}}\rangle}\right)\right].\end{cases}

(26)

where
E_{k}(\eta)
is exponential integral function.

We note that both series in upper and down parts of (
26
) contain expressions of the form
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\xi^{k}E_{k+1}(\eta)
, where
\xi\geq 0
,
\eta>0
. For
\eta>0
, the following inequality holds:
E_{k+1}(\eta)<E_{1}(\eta)
for
k\geq 1
. So we can estimate this series from above as
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\xi^{k}E_{k+1}(\eta)\leq E_{1}(\eta)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\xi^{k}
which is equivalent to estimation
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\xi^{k}E_{k+1}(\eta)\leq E_{1}(\eta)e^{-\xi}
. It means that both series in (
26
) converge for arguments of
E_{k}
being greater than
0
. Considering that
\xi^{k}
has smaller values for relatively small
\xi
and
E_{k+1}(\eta)
has smaller values for relatively large
\eta
, the "upper" definition of the integral suits better for
\rho\lesssim R
and the "down" definition – for
\rho\gtrsim R
. So, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\chi_{0}\rangle=2Z\alpha\ e^{\frac{\langle{u^{2}_{x}}\rangle}{2R^{2}}}\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\left(\frac{\rho^{2}}{2\langle{u^{2}_{x}}\rangle}\right)^{k}E_{k+1}\left(\frac{\langle{u^{2}_{x}}\rangle}{2R^{2}}\right),\ \rho\lesssim R\\
K_{0}\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\left(\frac{\langle{u^{2}_{x}}\rangle}{2R^{2}}\right)^{k}E_{k+1}\left(\frac{\rho^{2}}{2\langle{u^{2}_{x}}\rangle}\right),\ \rho\gtrsim R.\end{cases}

(27)

Formula (
27
) agrees well with idea that at large distances under condition
\langle{u^{2}_{x}}\rangle=0
,
\langle\chi_{0}\rangle=2Z\alpha K_{0}(\rho/R)
coincides with
\chi_{0}
for a single atom’s potential without averaging
[
6
]
.
So, for a straight atomic string

\displaystyle\tilde{\chi}_{0}^{(s)}=A\ e^{\frac{\langle{u^{2}_{x}}\rangle}{2R^{2}}}\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\left(\frac{\rho^{2}}{2\langle{u^{2}_{x}}\rangle}\right)^{k}E_{k+1}\left(\frac{\langle{u^{2}_{x}}\rangle}{2R^{2}}\right),\ \rho\lesssim R\\
K_{0}\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\left(\frac{\langle{u^{2}_{x}}\rangle}{2R^{2}}\right)^{k}E_{k+1}\left(\frac{\rho^{2}}{2\langle{u^{2}_{x}}\rangle}\right),\ \rho\gtrsim R;\end{cases}

(28)

where

.

The eikonal approximation that we used for this problem has wider applicability region compared with frequently used Born approximation. The applicability condition for a straight nanotube can be derived based on
[
6
]
as

\displaystyle\frac{|\tilde{\chi}_{0}^{(N_{s})}|_{max}L_{z}}{pR^{2}}\ll 1.

(36)

where
\tilde{\chi}_{0}^{(N_{s})}=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{s}}\tilde{\chi}_{(j)}^{(s)}
.

The maximum absolute value of
\tilde{\chi}_{0}^{(N_{s})}
–
|\tilde{\chi}_{0}^{(N_{s})}|_{max}
does not exceed
2A
for the spacing between atomic strings which occurs in a carbon zigzag nanotube. For this spacings the value
|\tilde{\chi}_{0}^{(N_{s})}|_{max}
does not depend on the number of strings in the nanotube, only on
N_{z}
:
A=2Z\alpha N_{z}
. From (
1
), the length of the nanotube can be estimated as
L_{z}\approx 1.5N_{z}a_{g}
for large enough
N_{z}
. Then, (
36
) corresponds to the following applicability condition:

\displaystyle\varepsilon[GeV]\gg 244(L_{z}[\mu m])^{2}.

(37)

For considered in this paper length
L_{z}\approx 24
nm, the required energy of the incident particle is
\varepsilon\gg 0.14
GeV. The necessary energy for applicability of the eikonal approximation increases quadratically with increasing length of the nanotube.

Bridging Quantum and Semi-Classical Thermodynamics in Cavity QED
Marcelo Janovitch, Sander Stammbach, Matteo Brunelli, Patrick P. Potts

Abstract
In cavity quantum electrodynamics (QED), photons leaving the cavity can be irreversibly lost or reused as a power source. This dichotomy is reflected in two different thermodynamic bookkeepings of the light field, both corresponding to valid thermodynamic frameworks. In this work, we formulate a rigorous semi-classical limit of cavity QED and show that the resulting thermodynamic description may qualitatively differ from that of the fully quantised model. We find that violations of the thermodynamic uncertainty relations are recovered in the semi-classical limit only by one of the two thermodynamic frameworks: the one which treats part of the photon flux as a power source. We illustrate our findings in a three-level system coupled to a driven cavity.

Introduction.—
Quantum devices typically operate out of equilibrium: they continuously produce entropy and sustain stationary currents.
These currents fluctuate due to both classical noise and genuinely quantum effects. The
thermodynamic uncertainty
associated with a current
I
is captured by the dimensionless quantity (with
\hbar=k_{\text{B}}=1
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(I,\sigma)=\frac{\langle\!\langle I^{2}\rangle\!\rangle}{\langle I\rangle^{2}}\,\sigma,

(1)

where
\langle I\rangle
and
\langle\!\langle I^{2}\rangle\!\rangle
are the mean and scaled variance of the current, respectively,
\sigma
is the entropy-production rate, and all quantities are taken in the long-time limit. Bounds on
\mathcal{Q}
, known as
thermodynamic uncertainty relations
(TURs), have become central tools in non-equilibrium statistical physics
3
;
9
;
24
;
25
;
11
;
6
.
The canonical bound
\mathcal{Q}\geq 2
holds for classical Markovian dynamics, whereas quantum coherence may suppress fluctuations and thus violate it. Such violations are, however, highly model dependent
31
;
12
;
2
;
18
;
29
;
30
.

Cavity quantum-electrodynamics (QED) platforms
10
;
34
;
4
;
19
;
33
;
38
;
22
;
35
offer exquisite control over light–matter interactions and are natural candidates for implementing quantum thermal machines.
However, the thermodynamic analysis of cavity QED systems is subtle, owing to the coherent coupling to a quantised cavity field, which is itself driven and dissipative.
In the
standard
thermodynamic approach
36
;
26
;
27
, photons leaving the cavity are associated to a heat current and therefore contribute to the total entropy production. An alternative approach for coherently driven systems, based on
input–output theory
(IO), has recently been formulated
32
;
28
. In this framework, the coherent part of the outgoing photons does not contribute to the entropy production. The rationale is that the coherent output field of the cavity can be used as a power source for another quantum system.
While both approaches provide consistent thermodynamic frameworks—each equipped with a first law of thermodynamics and a non-negative entropy production rate—they differ in how entropy production is quantified, resulting in different values of the thermodynamic uncertainty, Eq. (
1
). This is in contrast to a semi-classical description, in which the cavity field reduces to an external time-dependent parameter that does
not
produce entropy. How the thermodynamics obtained from a semi-classical description relates to the different thermodynamic approaches to cavity QED is an open question.

Here, we answer this question by rigorously formulating a semi-classical limit of cavity QED systems and benchmarking the two thermodynamic approaches against this limit.
Starting with a fully quantised model, we show that the standard thermodynamic approach does not recover the thermodynamic description of the semi-classical model, as the light keeps producing entropy in this limit. Instead, the input-output framework recovers the thermodynamic description of the semi-classical model, see Fig.
1
.

Our work clarifies the physical origin of the power output in the semi-classical descriptions of quantum thermal machines based on cavity QED: the power comes from the coherent part of the emitted field.
This has major consequences for TURs, which we illustrate in the three-level maser
8
;
15
;
20
, i.e., a thermally driven three-level atom coupled to a cavity.
In a semi-classical description, this system is known to violate the TUR due to the presence of quantum coherence
14
. Upon treating the field quantum mechanically, these TUR violations only persist if the input-output approach to thermodynamics is employed. In the standard approach, the dissipation associated to the light overestimates the entropy production, preventing any TUR violations.

Setup.—
We consider a quantum system embedded in a driven–dissipative cavity.
The Hamiltonian consists of the system, the driven cavity, and their interaction:

\displaystyle H(t)=H^{\prime}+H_{0}(t)+V,

(2)

where
H^{\prime}
is the bare system Hamiltonian and

\displaystyle H_{0}(t)=\Omega a^{\dagger}a+i\mathcal{E}\!\left(a^{\dagger}e^{i\omega_{\text{d}}t}-ae^{-i\omega_{\text{d}}t}\right),

(3)

describes a cavity with frequency
\Omega
laser-driven with amplitude
\mathcal{E}
and frequency
\omega_{\text{d}}
.
For concreteness we focus on an interaction of the form

\displaystyle V=g(aO^{\dagger}+a^{\dagger}O),

(4)

where
O
is a system operator; generalisations to multi-photon interactions are discussed in the Supplemental Material
1
.

The composite cavity-system dynamics is governed by the master equation

\displaystyle\derivative{\rho}{t}=-i[H(t),\rho]+\mathcal{D}[a]\rho+\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\rho,

(5)

where
\mathcal{D}
describes a bath at temperature
T
coupled to the cavity via

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}[a]=\kappa(\bar{n}+1)D[a]+\kappa\bar{n}D[a^{\dagger}],

(6)

with
D[L]\circ=L\circ L^{\dagger}-\tfrac{1}{2}\{L^{\dagger}L,\circ\}
and
\bar{n}=[e^{\omega_{\text{d}}/T}-1]^{-1}
the Bose–Einstein occupation of the cavity bath. For Markovian master equations, thermodynamic consistency requires to use the appropriate frequency
\omega_{\text{d}}
in the occupation
\bar{n}
; note, however, that
[e^{\omega_{\text{d}}/T}-1]^{-1}\simeq[e^{\Omega/T}-1]^{-1}
in order for Eq. (
5
) to be justified
37
;
26
. Dissipative processes
acting on the system are collected in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\Gamma_{j}D[L_{j}],

(7)

where the jump operators
L_{j}
and rates
\Gamma_{j}
are model-dependent.

Semi-classical limit.—
We now identify the parameter regime in which the cavity field behaves as a classical coherent drive. This is the case when the average cavity field dominates over its fluctuations; we then write

\displaystyle a=\alpha(t)+\tilde{a},

(8)

where
\alpha(t)
is the amplitude of the driven cavity in the long-time limit, and in the absence of coupling to the system (i.e., for
g=0
),

\displaystyle\alpha(t)=-2\frac{\mathcal{E}}{\kappa}\chi e^{-i\omega_{\text{d}}t},\qquad\chi=\frac{1}{1+2i\Delta/\kappa},

(9)

with detuning
\Delta=\Omega-\omega_{\text{d}}
.
Since
\alpha(t)
corresponds to
\langle a\rangle
in the limit
t\to\infty
,
g\to 0
, the displaced mode generally satisfies
\langle\tilde{a}\rangle\neq 0
.

We define the
semi-classical limit
as

|\alpha|\to\infty,\qquad\frac{g}{\kappa}\to 0,\qquad\frac{g}{\kappa}|\alpha|=\text{const.}.

(10)

In this regime the cavity hosts a large coherent field, whereas noise from thermal fluctuations and system back-action is negligible.
Thus,
\tilde{a}
represents only weak quantum fluctuations around the dominant classical amplitude. For multi-photon interactions, the coherent amplitude in the last expression
of Eq. (
10
) must be rescaled accordingly
1
.
Upon taking this limit and tracing out the cavity mode, we obtain the
semi-classical model

1
:

\displaystyle\derivative{\rho'}{t}

\displaystyle=-i[H_{\text{sc}}(t),\rho^{\prime}]+\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\rho^{\prime},

(11)

\displaystyle H_{\text{sc}}(t)

\displaystyle=H^{\prime}+g\!\left[\alpha(t)O^{\dagger}+\alpha^{*}(t)O\right],

where
\rho^{\prime}
is the reduced density matrix of the intra-cavity system.

In Eq. (
11
) the cavity field is classical: it no longer mediates back-action on the system and acts purely as an externally controlled, time-dependent drive. In contrast, the intra-cavity system remains quantum.

In the semi-classical model, the effective drive is directly associated with the injected
power
,

\displaystyle\langle P_{\text{sc}}\rangle=\langle\partial_{t}H_{\text{sc}}\rangle=-i\omega_{\text{d}}g\,\langle\alpha(t)O^{\dagger}-\alpha^{*}(t)O\rangle,

(12)

while the dissipative currents flowing into the system via
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
contribute to the entropy production rate,

\displaystyle\sigma_{\text{sc}}=-\sum_{j}\frac{\langle J_{j}\rangle}{T_{j}}.

(13)

Explicit expressions for the system’s heat current
\langle J_{j}\rangle
are model-dependent and will be provided in the example below; in the general case they can be established using a thermodynamic Hamiltonian
1
;
32
;
26
. The semi-classical model obeys the first and second laws of thermodynamics, i.e.,
\langle P_{\text{sc}}\rangle+\sum_{j}\langle J_{j}\rangle=0
and
\sigma_{\text{sc}}\geq 0
.

Two important remarks follow. First, both the composite model Eq. (
5
) and the semi-classical model Eq. (
11
), give the same expression for the
system
heat currents. Second, in the semi-classical model there is no contribution to the heat current coming from the cavity field, i.e.,
\langle J_{\text{sc}}\rangle=0
.

Incompatibility of the standard framework.—
According to the standard thermodynamic framework applied to the composite system of Eq. (
5
), the average power is given by
26
,

\displaystyle\langle P\rangle=\langle\partial_{t}H(t)\rangle=-\omega_{\text{d}}\mathcal{E}\,\langle ae^{-i\omega_{\text{d}}t}+a^{\dagger}e^{i\omega_{\text{d}}t}\rangle.

(14)

The heat current from the cavity to its bath is given by,

\displaystyle\langle J\rangle

\displaystyle=\omega_{\text{d}}\tr\quantity( a^\dagger a \mathcal{D} \rho)=\omega_{\text{d}}\kappa(\bar{n}-\langle a^{\dagger}a\rangle).

(15)

Together with the system’s heat current
\langle J_{j}\rangle
, these two quantities satisfy the first law of thermodynamics
\langle P\rangle+\langle J\rangle+\sum_{j}\langle J_{j}\rangle=0
.
The entropy production is given by,

\displaystyle\sigma=-\frac{\langle J\rangle}{T}-\sum_{j}\frac{\langle J_{j}\rangle}{T_{j}},

(16)

which satisfies the second law:
\sigma\geq 0
.

We show in
1
that in the semi-classical, and long-time limit,

\displaystyle\langle P\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle P_{\text{sc}}\rangle-\langle J\rangle,

(17)

\displaystyle\langle J\rangle

\displaystyle=-\kappa\omega_{\text{d}}|\alpha|^{2}-4\omega_{\text{d}}|\alpha|g\imaginary\langle O\penalty\ e^{-i\omega_{\text{d}}t}\rangle|\chi|.

(18)

\displaystyle\sigma=\sigma_{\text{sc}}-\frac{\langle J\rangle}{T},

(19)

reflecting the failure of the standard approach to recover the thermodynamics of semi-classical model.

Equations (
1
,
18
) imply that
\mathcal{Q}(J_{j},\sigma)\not\to\mathcal{Q}(J_{j},\sigma_{\text{sc}})
in the semi-classical limit. Importantly, this means that in the semi-classical limit
\sigma
always diverges, due to the extensive contribution
\propto|\alpha|^{2}
in Eq. (
18
).
Thus, the classical TUR
\mathcal{Q}(J_{j},\sigma)<2
cannot be violated, failing to witness non-classical effects in the fluctuations of
J_{j}
.

Compatibility of the input–output framework.—
Compared to the standard approach, the IO framework redistributes the coherent contribution of the photons between power and cavity heat current. Specifically, it attributes to power the coherent contributions from the external field, and to heat its fluctuations; this is encoded in the definitions
21
;
32
,

\displaystyle\langle P_{\rm io}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\omega_{\rm d}\left(|\langle b_{\text{out}}(t)\rangle|^{2}-|\langle b_{\text{in}}(t)\rangle|^{2}\right)

(20)

\displaystyle=P_{\text{in}}-P_{\text{out}},

\displaystyle\langle J_{\rm io}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\omega_{\rm d}\left(\langle\!\langle b_{\text{out}}^{\dagger}(t)b_{\text{out}}(t)\rangle\!\rangle-\langle\!\langle b_{\text{in}}^{\dagger}(t)b_{\text{in}}(t)\rangle\!\rangle\right),

(21)

where
b_{\text{in (out)}}
are the input (output) fields from input-output theory
7
and
\langle\!\langle AB\rangle\!\rangle=\langle AB\rangle-\langle A\rangle\langle B\rangle
. Heat currents
\langle J_{j}\rangle
are the same as above.
Since the equality
\langle J\rangle+\langle P\rangle=\langle J_{\text{io}}\rangle+\langle P_{\text{io}}\rangle

holds, the first law also follows
32
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\sigma_{\text{io}}=-\frac{\langle J_{\text{io}}\rangle}{T}-\frac{\langle J_{\text{C}}\rangle}{T_{\text{C}}}-\frac{\langle J_{\text{H}}\rangle}{T_{\text{H}}}.

(22)

Importantly,
0\leq\sigma_{\text{io}}\leq\sigma
: a tighter second law
32
.

In the semi-classical and long-time limit, we find
1

\langle P_{\text{io}}\rangle=\langle P_{\text{sc}}\rangle,\hskip 42.67912pt\langle J_{\text{io}}\rangle=0,

(23)

which implies the main results,

\displaystyle\sigma_{\text{io}}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\text{sc}},

(24)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(J_{j},\sigma_{\text{io}})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Q}(J_{j},\sigma_{\text{sc}}).

(25)

Conclusion and outlook.—
We have shown that thermodynamic uncertainty relations in cavity QED systems hinge on how entropy production is defined: the standard formulation assigns an extensive contribution from the coherent cavity field and fails to recover the semi-classical predictions. By bounding current fluctuations using the input–output thermodynamic approach, we obtain TURs that correctly describe the semi-classical limit of cavity QED, and correctly capture non-classical suppression of current fluctuations. Importantly, these results extend beyond the three-level maser and apply generically to cavity-embedded systems with multi-photon interactions, providing a practical tool for diagnosing thermodynamic uncertainty and non-classicality in cavity-QED architectures.

An important open direction is the development of a full-counting-statistics formalism for coherent power and cavity heat currents within the IO framework. Addressing this challenge would enable a unified description of energetic fluctuations and uncertainty relations in driven quantum systems, and clarify whether additional TURs can be formulated directly for work-like observables.

Appendix A
Semi-classical expansion

We present a systematic semi-classical expansion for a general quantum system coupled to a driven–dissipative cavity via a multi-photon interaction.
Our goal is to obtain the long-time dynamics in the limit of a large coherent cavity field and weak cavity–system coupling, and to show how the semi-classical model emerges.
We first illustrate the expansion for a flip–flop interaction and then extend the argument to the multi-photon interaction.
Finally, we detail how the average power in the IO framework recovers the semi-classical value.

A.1
General setup

In a frame rotating at the cavity drive frequency, the Hamiltonian takes the form

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=H^{\prime}+H_{0}+V,

(S1)

\displaystyle H_{0}

\displaystyle=\Delta\,a^{\dagger}a+i\mathcal{E}\big(a^{\dagger}-a\big),

(S2)

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=g\sum_{n=0}^{N}\sum_{m=0}^{M}c_{nm}\,a^{n}(a^{\dagger})^{m}\otimes O_{nm}+\text{H.c.},

(S3)

where
H^{\prime}
acts on the system Hilbert space and the
O_{nm}
are system operators.
We assume
c_{nm}\in\mathbb{R}
and of order unity; the integers
N,M
denote the highest powers of
a
and
a^{\dagger}
appearing in the interaction.
The special case
N=1
,
M=0
is the flip–flop interaction.

The dynamics is governed by the Lindblad master equation

\displaystyle\derivative{\rho}{t}=-i[H,\rho]+\mathcal{D}\rho+\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\rho,

(S4)

where
\mathcal{D}
describes cavity damping and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
collects all dissipative channels acting directly on the system.
For the cavity we take

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\rho=\kappa(\bar{n}+1)D[a]\rho+\kappa\bar{n}\,D[a^{\dagger}]\rho,

(S5)

with
\bar{n}
the Bose–Einstein occupation of the cavity bath, and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}

We study the semi-classical regime

\displaystyle\big|\alpha\big|\to\infty,\qquad\frac{g}{\kappa}\to 0,\qquad\frac{g}{\kappa}\big|\alpha\big|^{N+M}=\text{const.}

We treat

\epsilon^{-1/(N+M)}

Flip–flop interaction

\displaystyle V=g(aO^{\dagger}+a^{\dagger}O).

\displaystyle\tilde{a}=a-\alpha,

(S8)

\displaystyle\derivative{\rho}{t}=-i[\tilde{H},\rho]+\tilde{\mathcal{D}}\rho+\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\rho,

\displaystyle\tilde{H}

\displaystyle=\tilde{H}^{\prime}+\tilde{H}_{0}+\tilde{V},

\displaystyle=H^{\prime}+g(\alpha O^{\dagger}+\alpha^{*}O),

(S12)

\displaystyle\tilde{V}

\displaystyle=g(\tilde{a}O^{\dagger}+\tilde{a}^{\dagger}O),

Inspecting Eq. (
S13
), we have that

\displaystyle\tilde{V}/\kappa=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon),

(S19)

if we assume that the parameters of the intra-cavity system are fixed while taking the semi-classical limit. The dissipative term
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}/\kappa
is irrelevant upon tracing out the cavity.
Therefore, to first order in
\epsilon
, the reduced system dynamics is governed by

\displaystyle\derivative{\rho'}{t}=-i[H_{\text{sc}},\rho^{\prime}]+\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\rho^{\prime},

(S20)

which matches the semi-classical master equation used in the main text. Formally, we have that in the semi-classical limit,

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\dv{\rho'}{t} A}=\expectationvalue{\dv{\rho}{t} A}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon),

(S21)

for any intra-cavity operator
A
.

A.3
Multi-photon interaction

We now extend the expansion to the full interaction (
S3
).
The displacement
\alpha
remains unchanged because the cancellation of linear
\tilde{a}
terms depends only on the cavity drive and damping.

Substituting
a=\alpha+\tilde{a}
into
V
and using the binomial expansion yields

\displaystyle\frac{V}{\kappa}

\displaystyle=\frac{g}{\kappa}\sum_{n,m}\sum_{k,l}\binom{n}{k}\binom{m}{l}c_{nm}\,\alpha^{k}\tilde{a}^{\,n-k}(\alpha^{*})^{l}(\tilde{a}^{\dagger})^{m-l}\otimes O_{nm}+\text{H.c.}

(S22)

\displaystyle=\frac{H_{\text{sc}}}{\kappa}+\frac{\tilde{V}}{\kappa}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2}),

(S23)

where we have used the scaling in Eq. (
S6
).
The leading contribution is

\displaystyle H_{\text{sc}}

\displaystyle=gc_{NM}\,\alpha^{N}(\alpha^{*})^{M}O_{NM}+\text{H.c.},

(S24)

which is finite by construction of the semi-classical limit,

\displaystyle\frac{H_{\text{sc}}}{\kappa}\propto\frac{g}{\kappa}|\alpha|^{N+M}.

(S25)

Terms containing a single power of
\tilde{a}
or
\tilde{a}^{\dagger}
contribute to

\displaystyle\tilde{V}

\displaystyle=g\Big[N\,c_{NM}\,\alpha^{N-1}(\alpha^{*})^{M}\tilde{a}\,O_{NM}+M\,c_{NM}\,\alpha^{N}(\alpha^{*})^{M-1}\tilde{a}^{\dagger}\,O_{NM}\Big]+\text{H.c.},

(S26)

and scale as
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)
, akin to the flip-flop interaction discussed above.

This shows that, up to prefactors and system operators, the structure of
\tilde{V}
reduces to a flip–flop coupling in the semi-classical limit, and matches the simpler form discussed previously when
N=1
,
M=0
.
Higher-order contributions, involving two or more powers of
\tilde{a}
or
\tilde{a}^{\dagger}
, are of order
\epsilon^{2}
or smaller and are irrelevant in the semi-classical limit.

Hence the semi-classical expansion and resulting effective dynamics derived for the flip–flop model extend directly to general cavity–system interactions, with only the explicit form of the induced drive
H_{\text{sc}}
and residual coupling
\tilde{V}
modified.
Therefore, without loss of generality, we can focus on the flip–flop interaction in the main text, and in the developments below.

Appendix B
Average power in the semi-classical limit

The standard definition of power given in the lab frame
\langle P\rangle=\langle\partial_{t}H(t)\rangle
, implies that, in the rotating frame,

\displaystyle\langle P\rangle=-\omega_{\text{d}}\mathcal{E}\,\langle a+a^{\dagger}\rangle.

(S27)

In contrast, the IO definitions presented in the main text via in/out fields can be cast into the language of a master equation via a transformation
a\to a-\langle a\rangle
in the cavity dissipators,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}[a]

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{D}_{s}[a]=\mathcal{D}[a-\langle a\rangle]

(S28)

\displaystyle H(t)

\displaystyle\to H_{s}(t)=H(t)+i\frac{\kappa}{2}\big(\langle a^{\dagger}\rangle a-\langle a\rangle a^{\dagger}\big),

(S29)

This transformation leaves the Lindblad equation invariant, but modifies the identification of coherent and incoherent processes.

The IO power is then defined in the lab frame as,

\displaystyle\langle P_{\text{io}}\rangle=\langle\partial_{t}H_{s}(t)\rangle.

(S30)

which is equivalent to Eq. (
20
)
32
.
In the lab frame, we can set
\partial_{t}\langle a\rangle\simeq-i\omega_{\text{d}}\langle a\rangle

32
, and we find

\displaystyle\langle P_{\text{io}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle P\rangle-\omega_{\text{d}}\kappa\big|\langle a\rangle\big|^{2}.

(S31)

In the long-time limit, we obtain from the Heisenberg equations of motion for
a
,

\displaystyle\langle a\rangle

\displaystyle=\alpha-2i\frac{g}{\kappa}\chi\,\langle O\rangle,

(S32)

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=-2\frac{\mathcal{E}}{\kappa}\chi.

(S33)

Substituting into
\langle P\rangle
yields

\displaystyle\langle P\rangle

\displaystyle=4\omega_{\text{d}}\frac{\mathcal{E}^{2}|\chi|^{2}}{\kappa}+2i\omega_{\text{d}}\mathcal{E}\frac{g}{\kappa}\langle O\chi-O^{\dagger}\chi^{*}\rangle,

(S34)

where we used that

\displaystyle\real\chi=|\chi|^{2}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\chi^{*}=2|\chi|^{2}-\chi,\qquad\chi=2|\chi|^{2}-\chi^{*}.

(S35)

Using these relations, we can rewrite

\displaystyle\langle P\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle P_{\text{sc}}\rangle+4\omega_{\text{d}}\frac{\mathcal{E}^{2}|\chi|^{2}}{\kappa}+8\omega_{\text{d}}\mathcal{E}|\chi|^{2}\frac{g}{\kappa}\imaginary\langle O\rangle,

(S36)

where, for the semi-classical model in the rotating frame,

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{\text{d}}

\displaystyle=g(\alpha O^{\dagger}+\alpha^{*}O),

(S37)

\displaystyle\langle P_{\text{sc}}\rangle

\displaystyle=-i\omega_{\text{d}}g\,\langle\alpha O^{\dagger}-\alpha^{*}O\rangle

(S38)

\displaystyle=-2i\omega_{\text{d}}\mathcal{E}\frac{g}{\kappa}\langle\chi^{*}O-\chi O^{\dagger}\rangle.

For the IO power, we have

\displaystyle\langle P_{\text{io}}\rangle-\langle P\rangle

\displaystyle=-\omega_{\text{d}}\kappa\big|\langle a\rangle\big|^{2}

(S39)

\displaystyle=-\omega_{\text{d}}\kappa|\alpha|^{2}+2i\omega_{\text{d}}g\big[\alpha^{*}\chi\,\langle O\rangle-\alpha\chi^{*}\langle O^{\dagger}\rangle\big]+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\epsilon^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-\omega_{\text{d}}\kappa|\alpha|^{2}-8\omega_{\text{d}}\mathcal{E}|\chi|^{2}\frac{g}{\kappa}\imaginary\langle O\rangle+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\epsilon^{2}\right).

We show below that the right-hand side of the last equation above is simply
\langle J\rangle+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})
, which leads to Eqs. (
17
,
18
) in the main text.
Combining the equation above with Eq. (
S36
), and using that
\mathcal{E}/\kappa\propto|\alpha|
and
\langle O\rangle=\mathcal{O}(1)

, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle P_{\text{io}}\rangle=\langle P_{\text{sc}}\rangle+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2}).

(S40)

Thus, the IO definition of power recovers the semi-classical value up to corrections of order
\epsilon^{2}
, whereas the standard definition differs by an extensive contribution stemming from the coherent cavity field.

Appendix C
Heat currents and entropy production

Heat currents in a general open quantum system can be defined by introducing a thermodynamic Hamiltonian
26
;
27
;
32
,

\displaystyle H_{\text{TD}}=\omega_{\text{d}}a^{\dagger}a+H_{\text{TD}}^{\prime},

(S41)

where
H_{\text{TD}}^{\prime}
depends on the details of the embedded system.
Writing the system dissipator as a sum of thermal channels,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=\sum_{j}\mathcal{D}_{j},

(S42)

with
\mathcal{D}_{j}
encompassing thermal dissipators, each related to a bath at temperature
T_{j}
, the average heat current associated with the
j
-th channel is

\displaystyle\langle J_{j}\rangle=\tr\big(H_\text{TD} \mathcal{D}_j \rho\big)=\tr\big(H_\text{TD}' \mathcal{D}_j \rho\big),

(S43)

where additive constants in
H_{\text{TD}}
have no effect.
For the cavity,

\displaystyle\langle J\rangle=\tr\big(H_\text{TD}\mathcal{D}\rho\big)=\omega_{\text{d}}\,\tr\big(a^\dagger a\,\mathcal{D}\rho\big)=\omega_{\text{d}}\kappa(\bar{n}-\langle a^{\dagger}a\rangle).

(S44)

In the three-level maser case discussed in the main text,

\displaystyle H_{\text{TD}}^{\prime}=\omega_{3}\outerproduct{3}{3}+(\omega_{3}-\omega_{\text{d}})\outerproduct{2}{2},

(S45)

and the thermal dissipators are
\mathcal{D}_{\text{H}},\mathcal{D}_{\text{C}}
introduced in Eqs. (
29
,
30
).
In this section we keep
H_{\text{TD}}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{D}_{j}
general.

Combining the results of the previous section, we now argue that these general definitions of heat currents lead to the general results quoted in the main text for thermodynamic uncertainties.
In the IO framework,
H_{\text{TD}}
is unchanged, while the cavity dissipator transforms as
\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}_{s}
.
Therefore, the heat currents in the intra-cavity system are unchanged.

Using Eq. (
S40
) and the assumptions entering its derivation, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle J_{\text{io}}\rangle=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2}).

(S46)

Moreover, the following equality holds
32

\displaystyle\langle J\rangle+\langle P\rangle=\langle J_{\text{io}}\rangle+\langle P_{\text{io}}\rangle,

(S47)

which expresses the invariance of the total energy balance under the shift introduced in the IO framework.
This implies that the mismatch in power between the frameworks is accompanied by an equal and opposite mismatch in the cavity heat current.
Using the semi-classical limit and long-time limit, we find

\displaystyle\langle J\rangle=-\kappa\omega_{\text{d}}|\alpha|^{2}-8\omega_{\text{d}}\mathcal{E}|\chi|^{2}\frac{g}{\kappa}\imaginary\langle O\rangle+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2}).

(S48)

Thus, in the semi-classical limit, the standard definition of the cavity heat current deviates retains an extensive contribution incompatible with its semi-classical counterpart, whereas the IO definition remains compatible.

These differences translate directly to entropy production rates and thermodynamic uncertainties for system currents.
Denoting by
\sigma

\sigma_{\text{io}}

\sigma_{\text{sc}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(J_{j},\sigma_{\text{io}})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Q}(J_{j},\sigma_{\text{sc}})+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2}),

(S49)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(J_{j},\sigma)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Q}(J_{j},\sigma_{\text{sc}})-\frac{\langle\!\langle J_{j}^{2}\rangle\!\rangle}{\langle J_{j}\rangle^{2}}\frac{\langle J\rangle}{T}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2}).

(S50)

In deriving these relations we used the semi-classical and long-time limits, as well as the flip–flop interaction structure.
They generalise to the multi-photon interaction (
S3

S6

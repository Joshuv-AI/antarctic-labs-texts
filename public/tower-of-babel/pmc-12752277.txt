Evaluation of tadalafil in combination with Low-Intensity Pulsed Ultrasound (LIPUS) for erectile dysfunction: a protocol for a randomized controlled trial (SPIRIT Compliant)
Authors: Qi Zhang, Bodong Lv, Haojie Yu, Yuli Fu, Runnan Xu, Zedong Liao, Xin Zhang, Yijia Fu, Wenjie Huang, Xiaojun Huang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundErectile dysfunction (ED) is characterized by a persistent inability to achieve and maintain sufficient penile erection for satisfactory sexual performance persisting for at least six months. Low-intensity pulsed ultrasound (LIPUS), a noninvasive therapy, has shown potential in improving ED by promoting the regeneration of connective tissue, blood vessels, and cavernous nerves, as well as reducing inflammation. This study aims to evaluate the clinical efficacy and histological changes in the corpus cavernosum of patients with ED treated with tadalafil combined with LIPUS through a randomized controlled trial.MethodsThis study will enroll 114 patients diagnosed with ED who will be randomly assigned to either the treatment group or the control group in a 1:1 ratio using simple random sampling. The treatment group will first receive 5 mg of tadalafil daily combined with twice-weekly LIPUS therapy for 4 weeks. Following a 4-week interval with daily 5 mg tadalafil alone (no LIPUS treatment), the same combined treatment regimen will be repeated. The control group will receive only 5 mg of tadalafil daily. The primary outcome will be assessed using the minimal clinically important difference (MCID) based on the International Index of Erectile Function-5 (IIEF-5) at each follow-up visit. Additional assessments will include the Erection Hardness Score (EHS), penile blood flow parameters, and elasticity values for a comprehensive evaluation.DiscussionThe study aims to evaluate the efficacy of tadalafil combined with LIPUS for treating ED and to improve the evaluation of treatment response in erectile dysfunction by integrating blood flow parameters and two-dimensional shear wave elastography.Trial registrationTrial registration: ClinicalTrials.gov, NCT06543628. Introduction

Background and rationale {6a}

Erectile dysfunction (ED) is defined as the long-term inability to achieve or maintain sufficient penile erection, which is an important factor affecting men's health and family relationships. It has been reported that the prevalence of ED increases with age, rising from 35% in men aged 60 to 50% in men over 70 years [1]. There are many risk factors for ED, including age, coronary artery disease, diabetes, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, smoking, metabolic syndrome, hypogonadism, previous pelvic surgery, spinal cord injury, as well as mental and psychological disorders [2]. Phosphodiesterase type 5 inhibitors (PDE5Is)—including sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil, and avanafil—are recommended as first-line treatments for ED in both European and American urological guidelines [3, 4], with tadalafil offering distinct advantages due to its prolonged half-life (17.5 h), sustained 36-h efficacy, minimal food or alcohol interactions, and widespread clinical acceptance supported by its favorable efficacy and patient satisfaction profile [5, 6].

Studies confirm once-daily oral low-dose tadalafil maintains adequate plasma concentrations, offering efficacy and tolerability while improving IIEF scores, sexual satisfaction, and overall satisfaction [7]. Other treatments include lifestyle modifications, penile prosthesis implantation, shockwave/ultrasound therapy, and stem cell or PRP injections [1]. However, data show oral PDE5 inhibitors are ineffective in moderate-to-severe ED, diabetic, or post-prostatectomy patients [7, 8], linked to varying penile corpora cavernosa fibrosis and suggesting a need for more invasive treatments [9].

Low-intensity pulsed ultrasound (LIPUS) employs pulsed acoustic energy (< 3 W/cm2; 1–3 MHz) to exert therapeutic effects through anti-inflammatory, pro-repair, and vascular remodeling mechanisms [10, 11, 14]. Preclinical evidence demonstrates that LIPUS enhances neural regeneration (via Schwann cell proliferation and neurotrophic factors), upregulates nNOS, reduces fibrosis, and improves smooth muscle-collagen ratios in corporal tissue [12, 13]. Supported by demonstrated efficacy, safety, and tolerability, LIPUS has become a widely adopted non-invasive treatment for vasculogenic ED [15, 16].

Clinical studies, including a multicenter non-inferiority trial, have shown that both twice- and three-weekly LIPUS regimens significantly improve IIEF-EF scores in mild-to-moderate ED patients, with increases from baseline of approximately 4–5 points over 12 weeks [17]. Additional RCTs support its ability to improve erectile function, either alone or in combination with tadalafil, with no serious adverse events[15, 18]. Benefits have also been observed in severe ED patients in non-randomized studies [19–21]. However, as ED often necessitates extended management for sustained benefit, the prevailing short-term follow-up (≤ 12 weeks) in most studies cannot confirm durable efficacy or define optimal long-term treatment strategies (e.g., continuous or intermittent cycles).

Two-dimensional shear wave elastography (2D-SWE) quantifies corporal fibrosis via shear wave velocity (SWV) measurement, acting as a non-invasive alternative to biopsy for evaluating penile corporal stiffness and structural changes. In a feasibility study, researchers confirmed that 2D-SWE detects differences in penile corporal structure among rats with varying developmental stages, validated by pathology [22]. Additionally, 2D-SWE enables non-invasive quantification of penile corporal stiffness—with values increasing with age or pathology—offering a novel method for non-invasive assessment of erectile dysfunction (ED) and penile pathology [23].

Therefore, this study aims to further validate the long-term efficacy and safety of LIPUS, explore optimal treatment regimens for patients requiring multiple courses of therapy, and incorporate more objective assessment measures, thereby contributing further clinical evidence for the diagnosis and treatment of ED.

Objectives {7}

This randomized controlled trial (RCT) aims to evaluate whether tadalafil combined with low-intensity pulsed ultrasound (LIPUS) improves erectile function more effectively than tadalafil alone in patients with erectile dysfunction (ED). Additionally, the trial hypothesizes that two-dimensional shear wave elastography (2D-SWE) can provide a more accurate diagnostic assessment of penile tissue elasticity changes following treatment.

Trial design {8}

This study is a randomized, parallel-group controlled trial designed to evaluate the efficacy of oral tadalafil at 5 mg once daily combined with LIPUS therapy in treating erectile dysfunction. Participants will be randomly allocated to one of two groups in a 1:1 ratio to ensure equal distribution. Stratification will be based on key factors such as age and severity of erectile dysfunction to ensure balance across groups.

The study follows a superiority framework, aiming to demonstrate that the treatment group shows significantly better outcomes compared to the control group based on the primary outcome measure—assessed by the minimally clinically important difference (MCID) in the International Index of Erectile Function-5 (IIEF-5) [24, 25]—and secondary outcome measures, including the Erection Hardness Score (EHS).

This study will include penile Doppler parameters (PSV and EDV, cm/s) and cavernosal Young’s modulus (kPa) by 2D-SWE as key secondary endpoints. We hypothesize that combination therapy will yield greater improvement in these values—reflected by increased PSV, decreased EDV, and reduced stiffness—demonstrating enhanced vascular function, reduced fibrosis, and improved tissue elasticity.

Interventions

Explanation for the choice of comparators {6b}

Tadalafil was chosen as the comparator because it is widely recommended as a first-line treatment for erectile dysfunction (ED) in clinical guidelines due to its proven efficacy and safety. The study aims to compare tadalafil alone with the combination of tadalafil and LIPUS, a non-invasive therapy. This allows us to assess whether adding LIPUS can enhance the therapeutic effects of tadalafil, particularly in patients who may not fully respond to tadalafil monotherapy.

Intervention description {11a}

Each participant will undergo a 6-month treatment period. The control group will receive oral tadalafil 5 mg once daily. The treatment group will receive oral tadalafil 5 mg once daily combined with LIPUS therapy administered twice per week. The LIPUS therapy will be applied in two sessions per week over four weeks, followed by a four-week break with no treatment, after which the cycle will be repeated [26, 27].

All participants in the treatment group received therapy using the same low-intensity pulsed ultrasound (LIPUS) device (Model: WBL-ED; Beijing WanboLi Medical Equipment Co., Ltd., China), which has been approved by the National Medical Products Administration of China (NMPA Approval No.: 20182090291). Treatment was applied to the bilateral corpora cavernosa and penile crura. Each area received 5 min of therapy followed by a 30-s rest, resulting in a total treatment duration of 20 min per session. The LIPUS parameters were set as follows: energy intensity, 300 mW/cm2; pulse duty cycle, 1:4 (200 μs:800 μs); pulse repetition frequency, 1000 Hz; and ultrasonic frequency, 1.7 MHz [28, 29]. All LIPUS treatments will be performed by the same skilled urologist. During the treatment period, no psychological intervention or support will be provided, and participants will be asked to maintain their normal sexual habits.

Basic research shows that angiogenesis rises initially but declines weeks later [30], whereas neural repair and collagen remodeling take longer [1]. Repeated stimulation may also induce tissue adaptation (habituation) [31], reducing the efficacy of high-frequency therapy [32], which further suffers from poor compliance and high travel burden [33]. Inspired by Vardi Y’s intermittent low-intensity shockwave therapy protocol (3 weeks on, 3 weeks off; twice weekly) [34, 35], and accounting for practical considerations at our center,,we designed an innovative “intermittent cyclic regimen”: 4 weeks of intensive treatment (twice weekly) followed by a 4-week break. This strategy aims to reduce tissue adaptation, improve compliance, and optimize ED treatment.

Outcomes {12}

Primary outcome measures

This study will use the internationally recognized IIEF-5 as the primary outcome measure for efficacy, assessed at enrollment, end of Week 8, 16, and 24 of treatment.

Treatment success is defined by MCID: For patients with baseline IIEF-5 scores of 5–7 (severe ED), 8–16 (moderate ED), or 17–21 (mild ED), improvement of ≥ 7, ≥ 5, or ≥ 2 points respectively at any aforementioned time point is considered successful [36].

Secondary outcome measures

EHS will be measured in parallel with IIEF-5 as a secondary outcome. Treatment success will be determined when the erection hardness is sufficient for vaginal penetration and sustained erection (EHS > 3). Additionally, this study will compare changes in penile blood flow parameters (PSV and EDV) and elasticity values (Young's Modulus, YM) measured by two-dimensional shear wave elastography at baseline (randomization) and Week 24 post-treatment, in order to evaluate the vascular function and histology of the penile corpora cavernosa.

Study design flow chart

Sample size {14}

Based on clinical experience and a review of the literature, the expected treatment efficacy rate for the intervention group is P1 = 0.75, while the efficacy rate for the control group is P2 = 0.55. To ensure a statistical power of 90%, a two-sided Type I error rate of 5%, and a one-sided Type I error rate of 10%, we calculated the sample size according to Cohen's standard.

Substituting into the formula, considering a potential 10% loss to follow-up, as well as practical feasibility and resource constraints, we will ultimately decide that approximately 57 participants will be required in each group, totaling 114 participants for the study.

Recruitment {15}

Based on the outpatient volume at our center, the research team will actively recruit participants by posting study recruitment announcements in the clinic area. Additionally, targeted outreach through digital platforms and healthcare networks will be utilized to ensure adequate participant enrollment for the study.

Assignment of interventions: allocation

Sequence generation {16a}

The randomization sequence will be generated using a stratified block randomization method to ensure equal distribution between the two groups (1:1 allocation ratio). Stratification will first be performed by study center, with 20 participants enrolled per center, and each center's participants will be further divided into the control group and the experimental group.

Concealment mechanism {16b}

Allocation concealment will be maintained through the use of sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes containing the group assignment. The envelopes will be prepared by an independent statistician who is not involved in participant recruitment or data collection. These envelopes will only be opened after a participant has been enrolled in the study.

Implementation {16c}

The research team will recruit participants, and an independent statistician will generate the random allocation sequence and prepare the concealed envelopes. Before participants are enrolled in the study, the research team will be unaware of the group assignments. Once participants are enrolled, the study coordinator will assign them to their respective groups based on the contents of the sealed envelopes.

To promote participant retention and ensure complete follow-up, the study will use regular reminders, flexible scheduling, and follow-up communications via phone or text messages.

Data will be securely entered, coded, and stored with double data entry and range checks to ensure accuracy and data quality.

Confidentiality {27}

All participant information will be kept confidential and securely stored, with only authorized personnel having access, and any identifying details will be removed prior to data analysis.

At the time of initial enrollment, blood serum samples will be collected from participants for hormonal analysis. This assessment will be conducted only at baseline, with no subsequent measurements. After laboratory testing, all serum samples will be safely disposed of in accordance with the laboratory’s standard operating procedures (SOPs), with no long-term storage or future use of the samples planned.

Statistical methods

Statistical methods for primary and secondary outcomes {20a}

Data management will be conducted using EXCEL to establish a database with double data entry, missing value checks, and data locking. Statistical analyses will be performed using SPSS version 25.0. Normality tests will be conducted on continuous variables. For normally distributed data, Student’s t-test will be applied, with results presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). For non-normally distributed data, the Mann–Whitney U test will be used, and marginal homogeneity will be assessed, with results reported as median (interquartile range, IQR). Categorical variables will be expressed as rates or proportions and compared using the chi-square test, with chi-square values and P-values reported. For repeated measures data, results will be presented as mean ± SD, with intra-group comparisons performed using repeated measures analysis of variance (ANOVA), and inter-group comparisons using multivariate analysis of variance (MANOVA). Missing data will be handled using appropriate imputation techniques where necessary. Statistical significance will be set at p < 0.05.

Interim analyses {21b}

An interim analysis will be conducted at predefined time points to assess safety and efficacy, with stopping guidelines in place if necessary.

Methods for additional analyses (e.g. subgroup analyses) {20b}

In addition to comparing the efficacy between the two groups, subgroup analyses will be conducted based on age and the severity of erectile dysfunction (ED). Age will be divided into predefined categories (< 30 years, 30–50 years, and > 50 years), and ED severity will be classified according to established clinical criteria (mild, mild to moderate, moderate, and severe). The primary and secondary outcomes will be evaluated within each subgroup to assess the consistency of treatment effects across different age ranges and severity levels of the disease. Multivariate analyses will be performed to adjust for potential confounding factors, ensuring the robustness of the results within each subgroup.

Methods in analysis to handle protocol non-adherence and any statistical methods to handle missing data {20c}

Protocol non-adherence will be addressed using an intention-to-treat (ITT) approach, while missing data will be handled through multiple imputation or maximum likelihood estimation, with sensitivity analyses conducted to assess the robustness of the results.

Oversight and monitoring

Composition of the coordinating centre and trial steering committee {5d}

The coordinating center consists of a team responsible for the day-to-day management of the trial, including overseeing participant recruitment, data collection, and ensuring compliance with the trial protocol. This team will meet weekly to monitor trial progress and address any operational issues that arise. The trial steering committee is composed of clinical experts, statisticians, and ethics committee members, providing independent oversight of the trial. Their responsibilities include reviewing safety data, monitoring trial milestones, and ensuring the trial is conducted according to ethical and regulatory standards. The steering committee will meet quarterly throughout the trial to review progress and make necessary adjustments.

Composition of the data monitoring committee, its role and reporting structure {21a}

A Data Monitoring Committee (DMC) will not be established for this trial, as the interventions are considered low-risk and align with routine clinical care. Safety monitoring will be managed by the trial investigators, and any adverse events or concerns regarding overall trial safety will be promptly reported to the ethics committee for further review.

Adverse event reporting and harms {22}

Participants in this study are not exposed to risks beyond those associated with routine clinical treatment. If adverse drug reactions occur, the intervention will be promptly discontinued, and the event will be documented in the medical record. All adverse events will be addressed immediately, with appropriate actions taken and recorded in the case report forms (CRFs). For serious adverse events (SAEs), immediate actions will be taken, and the event will be documented in the CRFs. The investigator will decide whether to discontinue or reduce the dosage, and the event will be reported immediately to the ethics committee and the clinical trial institution. Additionally, the event will be reported to the national and provincial Food and Drug Administration (FDA) within 24 h. All SAEs will also be logged and reported through the hospital’s "Adverse Event and Near Miss Reporting System.

Discussion

This paper introduces an innovative clinical trial aimed at evaluating the efficacy and safety of a combination of tadalafil and low-intensity pulsed ultrasound (LIPUS) for the treatment of erectile dysfunction (ED). Although tadalafil, a PDE5 inhibitor, is widely used for treating ED, not all patients benefit from monotherapy, and some may experience side effects or develop resistance [5, 37].

As a noninvasive treatment modality, LIPUS has shown potential in promoting tissue repair and regeneration in recent years [38]. Combining tadalafil with LIPUS may provide a more effective treatment for patients with ED through the synergistic effects of pharmacological and physical therapy [39]. This combination therapy proposal is based on a review of the existing literature and preliminary studies, which show that LIPUS can improve penile blood flow, while PDE5 inhibitors enhance erectile function through different mechanisms [40]. Additionally, this study incorporates the use of two-dimensional shear wave elastography (2D-SWE) technology, in addition to traditional scales, to assess changes in penile tissue stiffness before and after treatment. This provides an objective basis for the quantitative evaluation of the pathological state and therapeutic efficacy of the tissue.

Although various methods are currently available in the field of ED treatment, including pharmacotherapy, surgical interventions, and non-invasive treatments, such as vacuum pump devices, few studies have specifically described the impact of these treatment options on penile corpora cavernosa tissue and the extent of improvement [41]. The health of the corpora cavernosa tissue directly affects penile blood flow and erectile function [42], making improvements in this key tissue a core indicator for evaluating the effectiveness of ED treatment. However, current research primarily focuses on treatment efficacy and lacks in-depth analysis of microstructural changes in the corpora cavernosa. This research gap highlights the need for more detailed studies to understand how different treatment methods specifically affect key parameters, such as vascularization, elasticity, and cellular composition of the corpora cavernosa, providing scientific evidence for developing more effective ED treatment strategies.

Through this study, we hope to provide a more effective and safer combined therapy for treating ED. By incorporating histological evaluation metrics, we aim to contribute to the establishment of new standards for ED assessment and efficacy evaluation, thereby paving the way for more precise and personalized treatment approaches.

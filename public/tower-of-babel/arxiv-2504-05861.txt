Introduction

A
spanner
of a graph
G
is a subgraph
G^{\prime}
that approximately preserves the distances between pairs of vertices in
G
. More formally, let
G=(V,E)
be a simple graph. A spanning subgraph
G^{\prime}=(V,E^{\prime})
is called a
t
-spanner
for

if for every two vertices
x,y\in V

d_{G^{\prime}}(x,y)\leq t\cdot d_{G}(x,y)
where

denotes the shortest path distance between
x
and
y
in
G
. Here,
t\geq 1
is a constant called the
stretch factor
(or
stretch
for short). Spanners are an important concept in graph theory and computer science, particularly in the context of geometric intersection graphs, where vertices represent geometric objects (such as approximate location, e.g., disks, rectangles) and edges represent intersections between these objects. The ability to construct spanners for geometric intersection graphs with fewer edges is crucial because it enables efficient algorithms for problems such as shortest path computation, network design, and routing while maintaining proximity to the original geometric distances. By using spanners, one can often reduce the complexity of geometric problems without sacrificing the quality of the solution (see, e.g.,
[
GNS16
,
Li03
,
MM17
,
NS07
,
Raj02
]
).

The following theorem is a cornerstone in the theory of spanners for general graphs:

Theorem 1.1

(
[
ADD+93
]
)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a simple undirected graph on
n
vertices. Let
t\geq 1
be a fixed integer parameter. Then
G
contains a
(2t-1)
-spanner

with
O(n^{1+\frac{1}{t}})
edges.

Note that the above theorem is non-trivial only for

or stretch factor at least
3
. Indeed, one cannot hope to obtain a
2
-spanner with

edges in general as is exemplified by the complete bi-partite graph

The general bound

is conjectured to be optimal due to the

[

]

The

of a set system

In this paper, our goal is to obtain hop spanners for geometric intersection graphs that are smaller than guaranteed by

Upper Bounds for 3 Hops

In this section, we prove new upper bounds for 3-hop spanners for geometric intersection graphs.
We begin with an easy lemma about general bipartite graphs in a lop-sided case:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G=(U\cup V,E)
be a graph where
|U|=m
,
|V|=n
, and
V
is an independent set.
Then
G
has a 3-hop spanner of size
O(m^{2}+n)
.

Proof.

We construct a subgraph
H
of
G
as follows. For each
v\in V
with
\deg(v)\geq 1
, let
e_{v}
be an arbitrary edge incident to
v
in
G
, and add
e_{v}
to
H
. Add
G[U]
to
H
.
Furthermore, for each pair of vertices
u,u^{\prime}\in U
, let
\pi_{u,u^{\prime}}
be a 2-hop path between
u
and
u^{\prime}
in
G
, if such a path exists, and add
\pi_{u,u^{\prime}}
to
H
. Clearly,
H
has
O(m^{2}+n)
edges.
We show that
H
is a 3-spanner of
G
. By construction, for each edge
e=uu^{\prime}
in
G[U]
, the distance between
u
and
u^{\prime}
in
H
is one. Consider an edge
uv
in
G
with
u\in U
and
v\in V
. If
e_{v}=uv
, then we can go from
u
to
v
in 1 hop. Otherwise,
e_{v}=u^{\prime}v
, for some
u^{\prime}\neq u
. Notice that in that case a
2
-hop path
\pi_{u,u^{\prime}}
indeed exists and we can go from
u
to
v
in 3 hops by concatenating
\pi_{u,u^{\prime}}
with
u^{\prime}v
.
∎

The above lemma allows us to almost immediately recover the standard
O(n^{3/2})
upper bound on 3-hop spanners for general graphs (the greedy algorithm yields the same bound
[
ADD+93
]
):

Corollary 2.2
.

Every graph
G=(V,E)
with
n
vertices has a 3-hop spanner of size
O(n^{3/2})
.

Proof.

Partition the vertices
V=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}
into
k=\sqrt{n}
groups of size
\sqrt{n}
each, and apply
Lemma

2.1
to each of the graphs
G_{i}=(V,E_{i})
where
E_{i}=E(G[V_{i}])\cup E[V_{i},V\setminus V_{i}]
. Namely, the edges in each graph comprise the inter-group edges and the bipartite edges between the group and its complement. Take the union of the spanners, with combined size
O(\sqrt{n}\cdot((\sqrt{n})^{2}+n))=O(n^{3/2})
. It is easy to check that the resulting graph is indeed a 3-hop spanner as any original edge belongs to at least one of the graphs
G_{i}
as it is either induced by the
U
part of one of the graphs or a bipartite edge in one of the graphs.
∎

2.1
Semi-algebraic graphs

Here we improve the general bound
O(n^{3/2})
for 3-hop spanners for semi-algebraic graphs.
A natural approach is to apply known geometric divide-and-conquer and partitioning techniques to obtain biclique covers of sub-quadratic size, which then yields sub-quadratic upper bounds for 3-hop spanners.
Unfortunately, for many higher-dimensional families of objects, the biclique cover bound can be much worse than
O(n^{3/2})
.
Nevertheless, we show that these divide-and-conquer techniques can work synergistically
with
Lemma

2.1
to beat
O(n^{3/2})

O(m^{2}+n)
bound.
In order to apply a divide-and-conquer we apply the polynomial partitioning technique as follows.

T_{\ell}(m,n)\ \leq\ O(r^{D})\left(T_{\ell}\left(\frac{m}{r^{D}},\frac{n}{r}\right)+T_{\ell-1}(m,n)\right).

(1)

For base cases, we have

Assume inductively that

Theorem

Remark 2.9
.

Interestingly, the above approach implies some new results on Zarankiewicz’s problem: upper-bounding the size of a semialgebraic graph, under the assumption that the graph avoids

2.2
Improvement for point-halfspace incidence graphs

For incidence graphs between points and halfspaces, we can improve the exponent by switching to shallow cuttings

Let

Thus, it remains to consider the subset

By the

For each cell

Also construct a 3-hop spanner for the biclique

We still have to deal with the subset

5
5

5

There is an alternative, simpler way to deal with these deep points in

3
Upper Bounds for 2 Hops

As mentioned in the introduction, for many families of objects, e.g., rectangles in

In this section, we show that nontrivial upper bounds for 2-hop spanners are possible for intersection graphs of

3.1
When one side forms a clique

We begin by observing that most of the results in
Section

2
works for 2 hops if one side
of the bipartite graph forms a clique. This very simple observation will be the key to deriving our fat-object results later:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G=(U\cup V,E)
be a bipartite graph where
|U|=m
and
|V|=n
. Let
G^{+}
be
the union of
G
and a clique on

. Then
G^{+}
has a 2-hop spanner of size
O(m^{2}+n)
.

Proof.

We construct a spanner subgraph

of

as follows.
For each vertex
v\in V
,
\deg(v)\geq 1
, let
e_{v}
be an arbitrary edge incident to

, and add
e_{v}
to
H^{+}
. Furthermore, add the clique on
U
to
H^{+}
.
We show that

is a 2-hop spanner of
G^{+}
. Consider an edge
uv
in

. If

, then we can go from
u
to
v
in 1 hop. Otherwise, say

is

.
We can go from
u
to

in 2 hops via the edges

and
u^{\prime}v
.
∎

The above lemma implies an

by the same grouping trick from the proof of

:

.

Let

.

We also immediately obtain the following analogs to

3.2
Intersection graphs of fat objects

To obtain our results for fat objects, our idea is to adapt
one of the methods by Chan and Huang
[
CH23
, Section 3.1]
, originally developed for constructing 3-hop spanners of
O(n\log n)
size. In adapting their method to construct 2-hop spanners, we realize that the main subproblems arising from the recursion correspond to the case when one side forms a clique—a case which fortunately has already been addressed by the
previous subsection! If the fat objects are all of similar size, then a simple grid approach will easily reduce to the one-sided clique case. For fat objects of arbitrary sizes, Chan and Huang
[
CH23
]
used a divide-and-conquer approach based on shifted quadtrees.

We use the following definition of fatness
[
Cha03
]
: A family of objects is
fat
if
for every axis-aligned hypercube
\gamma
with side length
\ell
, there exist
O(1)
points hitting all objects that intersect
\gamma
and have diameter at least
\ell
. There exists a number of different definitions of fatness in the
geometry literature (e.g., see
[
BSVK02
,
EKNS00
]
). We find the above definition most
appropriate for our purpose. It is not difficult to see that arrangements of Euclidean balls or hyperrectangles of bounded aspect ratios in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
are fat according to this definition.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
V
be a set of
n
fat objects in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for constant
d
.
Then the intersection graph
G
of
V

has a 2-hop spanner with
O(n^{3/2})
edges.

Proof.

Recall that a
quadtree cell
is an axis-aligned box of the form
[i_{1}/2^{k},(i_{1}+1)/2^{k})\times\cdots\times[i_{d}/2^{k},(i_{d}+1)/2^{k})
for some integers
i_{1},\ldots,i_{d},k
.
An object
u
of diameter
\ell
is
C
-aligned
if it is contained in a quadtree cell of side
length
C\ell
.
As argued in
[
CH23
, Section 3.1]
,
as a consequence of a known shifting lemma
[
Cha03
]
, one can find
O(d)
vectors
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{O(d)}
with the property that for every pair of objects
u
and
v
, there exists at least one vector
\tau_{j}
such that
u+\tau_{j}
and
v+\tau_{j}
are both
C
-aligned for some
C=O(d)
. For each
\tau_{j}
, it suffices to construct a 2-hop spanner for the subset of all objects
u
such that
u+\tau_{j}
is
C
-aligned; we can then output the union of these
O(d)
spanners. From now on, we may thus assume that all objects are
C
-aligned.
Let
T(n)
be the worst-case optimal 2-hop spanner size under this assumption.

1.

First find a quadtree cell
\gamma
such that there are at most
\alpha n
objects completely inside
\gamma
and at most
\alpha n
objects completely outside
\gamma
, with
\alpha:=\frac{2^{d}}{2^{d}+1}
; see
[
CH23
, Lemma 10]
(based on earlier work in
[
AMN+98
,
Cha98
]
). This cell corresponds to a “centroid” of the quadtree.
Recursively construct a 2-hop spanner for the objects completely inside
\gamma
and a 2-hop spanner for the objects completely outside
\gamma
.

2.

Let
Q_{\gamma}
be a set of points hitting all objects that intersect
\partial\gamma
. By alignedness, these
objects have diameter at least
\ell_{\gamma}/C
, where
\ell_{\gamma}
denotes the side length of
\gamma
.
By fatness, a hitting set of size
|Q_{\gamma}|=O(1)
exists (since
\partial\gamma
can be covered by
C^{d}
hypercubes of side length
\ell_{\gamma}/C
).

3.

For each point
q\in Q_{\gamma}
, let
U_{q}
be the subset of all objects hit by
q
(this subset induces a clique).
Let
G_{q}
be the bipartite intersection graph between
U_{q}
and
V
.
Construct a 2-hop spanner for
G_{q}\cup(U_{q}\times U_{q})
by
Corollary

3.2
.

We claim that the union of the spanners found is a 2-hop spanner of
G
. To see this, consider an edge
uv
in
G
.
If
u
and
v
are both inside
\gamma
or both outside
\gamma
, then
u
and
v
are reachable by 2 hops
by induction. Otherwise, one of the objects, say
u
, intersects
\partial\gamma
. Then
u
is hit by some point
q\in Q_{\gamma}
,
and so
uv\in G_{q}
and
u
and
v
are reachable by 2 hops.

We thus obtain the following recurrence:

T(n)\ \leq\ \max_{n_{1},n_{2}\leq\alpha n:\ n_{1}+n_{2}\leq n}\Big(T(n_{1})+T(n_{2})\Big)+O(n^{3/2}).

This solves to
T(n)=O(n^{3/2})
.
∎

4
A 3-Hop Upper Bound for Axis-Aligned Boxes

In this section, we note that the known
O(n\log n)
-size 3-hop spanner construction for axis-aligned rectangles in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by Chan and Huang
[
CH23
]
can be extended to give an
O(n\log^{d-1}n)
-size 3-hop spanner for axis-aligned boxes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
(One could alternatively use biclique covers, as in the proof of
Theorem

3.10
, but this would yield a slightly larger

O(n\log^{d}n)
bound.)

Theorem 4.1
.

The intersection graph of every set of
n
axis-aligned boxes in a constant dimension
d
has a 3-hop spanner with
O(n\log^{d-1}n)
edges.

Proof.

We will prove the result more generally for bipartite intersection graphs and use a divide-and-conquer strategy similar to range trees or segment trees.

Let
\sigma
be a slab orthogonal to the
d
-th axis.
We say that an axis-aligned box
s
is
short
for
\sigma
if it has at least one corner point strictly inside
\sigma
,
and
s
is
long
for
\sigma
if it intersects
\sigma
but all its corner points are outside or on
the boundary of
\sigma
.

Suppose we are given two sets
U
and
V
of axis-aligned boxes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, all short in
\sigma
,
with a total of
N
corner points strictly inside
\sigma
. Note that since the boxes are short, the number of boxes must be
O(N)
.
We want to construct a 3-hop spanner for the bipartite intersection graph
G[U,V]
between
U
and
V
.

Divide
\sigma
into two subslabs
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
, each containing
n/2
corner points.
For each
j\in\{1,2\}
, let
U_{j}
(resp.
V_{j}
) be the boxes of
U
(resp.
V
) intersecting
\sigma_{j}
, clipped to
\sigma_{j}
.
Let
U_{j}^{\textrm{short}}
(resp.
V_{j}^{\textrm{short}}
) be the boxes of
U_{j}
(resp.
V_{j}
) that are short for
\sigma_{j}
.
Let
U_{j}^{\textrm{long}}
(resp.
V_{j}^{\textrm{long}}
) be the boxes of
U_{j}
(resp.
V_{j}
) that are long for
\sigma_{j}
.

For each
j\in\{1,2\}
:

1.

Recursively construct a 3-hop spanner for
G[U_{j}^{\textrm{short}},V_{j}^{\textrm{short}}]
.

2.

Recursively construct a 3-hop spanner for
G[U_{j},V_{j}^{\textrm{long}}]
by projection to the first
d-1
coordinates.

3.

Recursively construct a 3-hop spanner for
G[U_{j}^{\textrm{long}},V_{j}]
by projection to the first
d-1
coordinates.

We then take the union of the spanners found. As a result, we then obtain the following recurrence for the spanner size:

T_{d}(N)\>\leq\>2\,T_{d}(N/2)+O(1)T_{d-1}(N).

For the base case, we have
T_{2}(N)=O(N\log N)
by Chan and Huang
[
CH23
]
.
The recurrence solves to
T_{d}(N)=O(N\log^{d-1}N)
.
∎

5
Lower Bounds

In this section, we prove lower bounds for 3-hop and 2-hop spanners, by relating the problem to the combinatorial question
of bounding the size of geometric graphs avoiding
K_{2,2}
.
Our lower bounds for 3-hop spanners are obtained from the following simple observation:

Observation 5.1
.

If a bipartite graph
G
does not contain any 4-cycle, i.e.,
K_{2,2}
, then any 3-hop spanner of
G
must include all its edges.

For 2-hop spanners, one could similarly derive lower bounds from constructions of graphs without 3-cycles (i.e., triangle-free graphs).
However, for fat objects, triangle-free intersection graphs actually are known to have linear size.
Instead, we propose the following lemma, showing that constructions of large bipartite intersection graphs between two sets of objects that avoid
K_{2,2}
(where objects within each set
are allowed to intersect) can also yield lower bounds for 2-hop spanners for (not-necessarily bipartite) intersection graphs:

Lemma 5.2
.

If
G=(U\cup V,E)
is a graph (with
U\cap V=\emptyset
), and the bipartite subgraph
E^{\prime}=E\cap(U\times V)
does not contain
K_{2,2}
,
then any 2-hop spanner of
G
must have at least
|E^{\prime}|
edges.

Proof.

Basically, we want to show that extra edges in
U\times U
and
V\times V
do not help.
We use a charging argument. Let
H
be a 2-hop spanner of
G
. For each edge
uv\in E^{\prime}
with
u\in U
and
v\in V
, if
uv\in H
, we charge the edge
uv
to itself. Otherwise,
H
must contain edges
ux
and
xv
for some
x
. Without loss of generality, say
x\in U
. We charge the edge
uv
to the edge
ux
.

Since the total charge is
|E^{\prime}|
, it suffices to show that no edge in
H
receives more than one charge. Suppose that some edge
e
in
H
receives charges from two different edges
f_{1},f_{2}\in E^{\prime}
. For
f_{1}
to be charged to
e
, there must exist an edge
g_{1}\in E^{\prime}
such that
e,f_{1},g_{1}
form a triangle. Similarly, there must exist an edge
g_{2}\in E^{\prime}
such that
e,f_{2},g_{2}
form a triangle. Since
f_{1},f_{2}\not\in H
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in H
, the edges
f_{1},g_{1},f_{2},g_{2}
are distinct
and form a
K_{2,2}
in
E^{\prime}
: a contradiction.
∎

We can use known combinatorial results to construct
K_{2,2}
-free intersection graphs:

Lemma 5.3
.

For every positive integer
n
, the following hold:

(a)

There exist
n
points and
n
lines in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
whose incidence graph does not contain
K_{2,2}
and has
\Omega(n^{4/3})
edges.

(b)

There exist
n
tetrahedra in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
whose intersection graph is bipartite, does not contain
K_{2,2}
, and has
\Omega(n^{4/3})
edges.

(c)

There exist
n
red/blue congruent regular tetrahedra (or congruent non-axis-aligned cubes) in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that the bipartite intersection graph between the red tetrahedra and the blue tetahedra does not contain
K_{2,2}
and has
\Omega(n^{4/3})
edges.

(d)

There exist
n
points and
n
halfspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{5}
whose incidence graph does not contain
K_{2,2}
and has
\Omega(n^{4/3})
edges.

(e)

There exist
n
red/blue congruent balls in
\mathbb{R}^{5}
such that the bipartite intersection graph between the red balls and the blue balls does not contain
K_{2,2}
and has
\Omega(n^{4/3})
edges.

(f)

There exist
n
points and
n
axis-aligned boxes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
whose incidence graph does not contain
K_{2,2}
and has

\Omega(n(\log n/\log\log n)^{d-1})
edges.

(g)

There exist
n
axis-aligned boxes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
whose intersection graph is bipartite, does not contain
K_{2,2}
, and has
\Omega(n(\log n/\log\log n)^{d-2})
edges.

(h)

There exist
n
red/blue congruent axis-aligned hypercubes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that the bipartite intersection graph between the red hypercubes and blue hypercubes does not contain
K_{2,2}
and has
\Omega(n(\log n/\log\log n)^{\left\lfloor d/2\right\rfloor-1})
edges.

Proof.

(a)

This is well-known, by a construction of Erdős
[
Erd85
]
(see also
[
BST23
,
Ele01
]
) and incidence graphs between points and lines in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
automatically do not contain
K_{2,2}
.

(b)

Let
P
be the set of points and
L
be the set of lines in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
from (a).
Map each point
p\in P
to the red vertical line
\varphi(p)=p\times(-\infty,\infty)
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Map the
i
-th line
\ell\in L
to a blue line
\psi(\ell)=\ell\times\{i\}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
(which lies
on the horizontal plane
z=i
).
Then
\varphi(p)
intersects
\psi(\ell)
iff
p
lies on
\ell
.
Moreover, there are no red-red or blue-blue intersections.
The result then follows by viewing these red/blue lines as thin tetrahedra.

(c)

Let
P
be the set of points and
L
be the set of lines in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
from (a).
We may clip each line
\ell\in L
to a line segment of a sufficiently large length
M
.
Map each point
p\in P
to some red regular tetrahedron
\varphi(p)
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

that has side length
M
, lies inside the halfspace
z\geq 0
, and touches the plane
z=0
precisely at the point
p\times\{0\}
.
Map each line segment
\ell\in P
to some blue regular tetrahedron
\psi(\ell)
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

that has side length
M
, lies inside the halfspace
z\leq 0
, and touches the plane
z=0
precisely at the line segment
\ell\times\{0\}
.
Then
\varphi(p)
intersects
\psi(\ell)
iff
p
lies on
\ell
.
The construction is similar for non-axis-aligned cubes.

(d)

This was noted by Chan and Har-Peled
[
CHP23
]
.
Specifically, let
P
be the set of points and
L
be the set of lines in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
from (a).
A point
(x,y)\in P
is incident on a line in
L
with equation
ax+by=1
iff

(ax+by-1)^{2}\leq\delta
, i.e.,
a^{2}x^{2}+2abxy+b^{2}y^{2}-2ax-2by+1\leq\delta
, for a sufficiently small
\delta>0
.
Map each point
p=(x,y)\in P
to a point
\varphi(p)=(x^{2},xy,y^{2},x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{5}
.
Map each line
\ell\in L
with equation
ax+by=1
to a halfspace
\psi(\ell)=\{(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\xi_{4},\xi_{5})\in\mathbb{R}^{5}:a^{2}\xi_{1}+2ab\xi_{2}+b^{2}\xi_{3}-2a\xi_{4}-2b\xi_{5}+1\leq\delta\}
.
Then
\varphi(p)
lies inside
\psi(\ell)
iff
p
lies on
\ell
.

(e)

Let
P
be the set of points and
S
be the set of halfspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{5}
from (d).
We can view each halfspace of
S
as a giant ball, of a fixed and sufficiently large radius
M
.
Now map each point
p\in P
to a red ball
\varphi(p)
centered at
p
of radius
M/2
.
Map each ball
s\in S
to a blue ball
\psi(s)
with the same center of radius
M/2
.
Then
\varphi(p)
intersects
\psi(s)
iff
p
lies inside
s
.

(f)

This follows from a construction by Chazelle
[
Cha90
]
, as noted by Chan and Har-Peled
[
CHP23
]
.

(g)

Let
P
be the set of points and
S
the set of boxes in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
from (f).
Map each point
p\in P
to a red vertical line
\varphi(p)=p\times(-\infty,\infty)
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Map the
i
-th box
s\in S
to a blue box
\psi(s)=s\times\{i\}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Then
\varphi(p)
intersects
\psi(s)
iff
p
is inside
s
.
Moreover, there are no red-red or blue-blue intersections.
The result then follows by viewing the red lines as thin boxes.

(h)

Say
d
is even.
Let
P
be the set of points and
S
be the set of boxes in
\mathbb{R}^{d/2}
from (f).
Map each point
p=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d/2})\in P
to a red hypercube
\varphi(p)=[-x_{1},-x_{1}+M]\times[x_{1},x_{1}+M]\times\cdots\times[-x_{d/2},-x_{d/2}+M]\times[x_{d/2},x_{d/2}+M]
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for a sufficiently large side length
M
.
Map each box
s=[a_{1},b_{1}]\times\cdots\times[a_{d/2},b_{d/2}]\in S
to a blue hypercube
\psi(s)=[-a_{1}-M,-a_{1}]\times[b_{1}-M,b_{1}]\times\cdots\times[a_{d/2}-M,a_{d/2}]\times[b_{d/2}-M,b_{d/2}]
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Then
\varphi(p)
intersects
\psi(s)
iff
p
lies inside

.

Remark 5.5
.

It remains open to prove better lower bounds for simplices in dimensions
d>3
or balls in dimensions
d>5
, ideally with exponent converging to
3/2
as
d
increases.
It is possible to adapt
5.1
and
Lemma

5.2
to avoid
K_{2,c}
instead of
K_{2,2}
for any constant
c
(while losing some
c^{O(1)}
factors), but we are not aware of any stronger lower bounds on geometric graphs avoiding
K_{2,c}
that could be exploited here (except in some lop-sided bipartite cases).

One common property to all the geometric intersection graphs mentioned in this paper is that they all have bounded VC-dimension
6
6

6

The
VC-dimension
of a graph is defined as the VC-dimension of the set system induced by the neighborhoods of its vertices.
. As already noted in the introduction, bounded VC-dimension alone does not guarantee a
3
-spanner with
o(n^{3/2})
edges, as demonstrated in the following theorem:

Theorem 5.6
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a graph
G=(V_{1}\cup V_{2},E)

with VC-dimension at most
2
so that any
3
-spanner for
G
has
\Omega(n^{3/2})
edges.

Proof.

The construction consists of the known constructions of bipartite graphs without
C_{4}
with
\Omega(n^{3/2})
edges (e.g., the construction with projective planes over finite fields
(F_{p})^{2}
). Its easy to see that the VC-dimension of such graphs is at most 2. By
5.1
, any
3
-spanner must use all bipartite edges.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

Bounded VC-dimension is known to imply the existence of spanning trees with sublinear crossing number
[
CW89
]
,
biclique covers with subquadratic size, and various other properties often used in geometric range searching.
Along the same lines, one may wonder whether general set systems with bounded VC-dimension admit some analog to
(1/r)
-cuttings
[
Cha04
]

or polynomial partitioning. Interestingly, our work provides a negative answer to this question: An analog to cuttings would
imply an
o(n^{3/2})
upper bound for 3-hop spanners, by following the method in
the proof of
Theorems

2.7
and
2.11
, and this would contradict
Theorem

5.6
.

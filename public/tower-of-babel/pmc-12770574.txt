Time to recovery and predictors of neonatal sepsis among neonates admitted to intensive care units in Southern ethiopia: multicenter study
Authors: Elias Geneti, Tolossa Dibissa, Tesfu Zewdu, Teklu Gemechu, Ayele Mamo, Mohamed Tessema, Bethelhem Birhanu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Neonatal sepsis is a serious problem in the first 28 days of life which is a leading cause of morbidity and mortality in developing counties. Neonatal sepsis remains a leading cause of neonatal morbidity and mortality, particularly in Sub-Saharan Africa. There is paucity of multicenter studies in this region. Therefore, this study aimed to assess the burden and predictors of recovery time among neonates with neonatal sepsis admitted to neonatal intensive care units in South Ethiopia. A retrospective follow up study was conducted among 643 charts of neonates with neonatal sepsis who were admitted to NICU in hospitals of Southern Ethiopia using systematic random sampling. Data were inputted into Epi data version 3.1 and exported to STATA 14 for analysis. Cox Proportional Hazard regression model was used. P-value < 0.05 in multivariable analysis was used to declare the result as statistically significant predictors of recovery from neonatal sepsis. The overall median recovery time was 8 days. The incidence rate of recovery from neonatal sepsis was 9.86 per 100 neonate day’s observation. Factors significantly associated with recovery time of neonatal sepsis includes; respiratory distress (AHR: 0.71, 95% CI (0.59, 0.85)), resuscitation at birth (AHR: 0.65, 95% CI (0.49, 0.87)), having fever (AHR: 0.82, 95% CI (0.67, 0.99)) and frequency of antibiotics change (AHR: 2.0, 95% CI (1.57, 2.63)). Time to recovery of neonatal sepsis was moderately acceptable as compared to the previous studies. It was significantly affected by resuscitation at birth, frequent antibiotic change, respiratory distress and fever. Therefore, health care providers must pay more attention for prevention and early intervention of complication.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-29473-6.

Introduction

Background

Neonatal sepsis is a clinical syndrome of bacteraemia characterized by systemic signs and symptoms of infection in the first 28 days of life1,2. Two major categories based on the time of onset: early-onset neonatal sepsis (EONS), which appears within the first seven days of life and most cases appear within 24 h of birth and late onset neonatal sepsis (LONS) occurs after 8 days of infants life3,4.

Common clinical manifestations of sepsis on infants are; feeding difficulties, temperature instability, hypoperfusion characterized by pallor and mottled skin, tachycardia or bradycardia, apnea, respiratory distress and grunting2,5. Therefore, it is diagnosed if at least two of the above clinical features and at least two of the following laboratory values is positive such as, complete blood count with differential, blood culture, urine culture, chest radiograph, gram stain, lumbar puncture examination especially for late onset sepsis6.

Globally, 15% of neonatal deaths are due to neonatal sepsis and it is a concern for low-and middle-income countries7,8. In developing countries, the rate of neonatal mortality due to sepsis is very high and it ranges from 14.6% to 36%9. Neonatal sepsis is a primary cause of neonatal mortality and an urgent global health concern, especially within low-income and middle-income countries (LMICs), where 99% of global neonatal mortality occurs10. In Africa the case fatality rates of neonatal sepsis was 52%11.

A recent estimated global incidence of neonatal sepsis is approximately 2824 per 100,000 live births12, representing a significant increase compared to the 2018 estimate of 2,202 cases per 100,000 live births13. According to the World Health Organization (WHO) report, the global burden of neonatal sepsis was up to 3 million sepsis cases occur per year among neonates, with a global mortality of 400,000–700,000 every year14.

In Africa, neonatal sepsis accounts for 28% of neonatal mortality and believed that early detection and treatment of neonatal sepsis (NS) can prevent around 84% of neonatal sepsis-related mortality14. The burden varies from country to country; Cameroon 37.9%15, Ghana 20.2%16, Tanzania 31.4%17, Kenya 23.9%18 and Egypt 8.6%19. Sepsis is the most common cause of neonatal mortality and probably responsible for 30 to 50% of the total neonatal deaths each year in developing countries14.

Despite improvements over the past period, Ethiopia’s current neonatal mortality rate is one of the highest in the world and a significant proportion of the under-five mortality in the country20. Ethiopian Demographic and Health Survey (EDHS) 2016 report showed that, the neonatal, infant, and under-5 mortality rates were 29, 48, and 67 deaths per 1,000 live births, respectively. The EDHS 2019 report showed that the neonatal mortality rate was 30 deaths/1000 live births21.

In Ethiopia, 40% of under-five mortality occurs during the neonatal period; among this, neonatal sepsis accounts for 30–35% of neonatal deaths7. The magnitude of neonatal sepsis in Ethiopia is still high, which is 48% and varies from region to region; in Gondar 64.4%22, in Mekele 76.8%23, in Shashamane 77.9%24, in Woldia and Dessie Comprehensive Specialized Hospitals 79.4%25, and in Arbaminch 78.3%26, and in Wolaita Sodo 33.8%27.

The burden and severity of neonatal sepsis in Ethiopia is high28 and approximately 50% of the neonates in Neonatal Intensive Care Units (NICUs) developed neonatal infection due to long duration of hospital stay(LDOHS)29. Moreover, limited studies have been conducted, especially in the study area and there is a paucity of multicenter studies to generate evidence for action. Therefore, this study aimed to assess the burden and predictors of recovery time among neonates with neonatal sepsis admitted to neonatal intensive care units in public comprehensive specialized hospitals of the South Nation, Nationalities and peoples Regional State.

Significance of the study

Though Ethiopia has made considerable achievement in the reduction of under-five mortality rate, the neonatal mortality burden has not experienced the same reduction and unacceptably high and the trend in reduction is slower30. To prevent further this neonatal problem, data from different geographical areas of Ethiopia are required.

This study will provide valuable information on the recovery time from sepsis and its predictors factors among new born admitted with neonatal sepsis.

Understanding indicators of time to recovery in neonate with sepsis can help healthcare professionals and parents to understand how long the new born will be in the hospital. This allows them to mobilize resources for the newborn’s hospital care and prepare for any disruption the admission may cause. It helps health care provider for early initiation of further investigation and treatment. Finally, the results of this study will serve as an input for further studies.

Objective of the study

General objective

To assess time to recovery of neonatal sepsis and its predictors among neonates admitted to the NICUs in public comprehensive specialized hospitals of SNNPRS, Ethiopia, 2023.

Specific objective

To determine the recovery time of neonatal sepsis among neonates admitted to the NICUs in public comprehensive specialized hospitals of SNNPRS, Ethiopia, 2023.

To identify the predictors of the recovery time of neonatal sepsis among neonates admitted to the NICUs in public comprehensive specialized hospitals, SNNR, Ethiopia, 2023.

Methods and materials

Study area and period

This study was carried out from May 1st to May 30th, 2023 among neonates with neonatal sepsis admitted to public comprehensive specialized hospitals in SNNPRS from January 1st, 2021 to April 30, 2023.

South Nation Nationality and People`s regional state is one of the administrative regional states in southwestern Ethiopia. It is the most diverse region in the country in terms of culture, language and ethnicity. There are four comprehensive specialized hospitals in the region namely;

Wolaita Sodo University comprehensive specialized hospital, located in Sodo town of Wolaita zone, which is 380 km away from Addis Ababa. The NICU ward of this hospital has 34 patient beds for newborn infants.

Wachemo University Nigist Eleni Mohammad memorial comprehensive specialized hospital, located in Hadiya zone in SNNPR which is 232 km far from Addis Ababa, Ethiopia. It is one of the frontline hospitals in Ethiopia. The NICU ward of this hospital has 40 patient beds. It also has a very high patient flow (200 neonates per month on average).

Werabe comprehensive specialized hospital, found in Silte zone. The town, werabe is located 172 km south of Addis Ababa. The NICU ward of this hospital has 36 patient beds for newborn infants. Another area of this study was, Walkitie University Comprehensive Specialized Hospital, located in Gurage zone, Walkitie town. It is 132 km far away from Addis Ababa.

Study design

Retrospective follow up study was used.

Population

Source population

The source population comprised of all neonates with sepsis who were admitted for neonatal sepsis management and treated at public comprehensive specialized hospitals in SNNPRS.

Study population

All neonates admitted for neonatal sepsis management and treated at public comprehensive specialized hospitals of SNNPRS from January 1st, 2021 to April 30, 2023.

Study unit

Each selected neonate’s chart that fulfills the inclusion criteria.

Eligibility criteria

Inclusion criteria

Neonates admitted to NICUs in Public Comprehensive Specialized Hospitals of SNNPRS with the diagnosis of neonatal sepsis.

Exclusion criteria

Neonates who died before taking the treatment and incomplete records were excluded from the study.

Sampling procedure

All comprehensive specialized hospitals were included in our study. For record reviews, two consecutive years (January 2021 to January 2023) was chosen deliberately because they provide the latest data on the issue under investigation. During the two-year period, the total numbers of neonatal sepsis admissions were nine thousand four hundred seventy one (9,471).

By calculating the interval from the sampling frame, the total sample size for each year was proportionally allocated for (WUNEMMCSH, N = 3200), (WSUCSH, N = 2156), (WCSH, N = 2170) and (WUCSH, N = 1945) and the interval was calculated as (k = N/n) while N = 9471 and sample size n = 643. The interval (k = 9471/643 = 14) so the data were collected every 14th charts which was similar for each hospital. The first chart to start the data collection was selected randomly. Then, every 14th medical chart within the two consecutive years period was reviewed using systematic random sampling (Fig. 1).

Variables

Dependent variable

Time to recovery of neonatal sepsis.

Independent variable

Socio demographic factors

Maternal age in year, neonatal age in days and neonatal sex.

Neonatal related factors

Gestational age, comorbidity, Apgar score, History of resuscitation, Birth weight, History of previous hospitalization, Exclusive breast feeding(EBF) initiation within one hour, sepsis type and BCG Vaccine.

Maternal related factors

Mode of delivery, Place of delivery, number of antenatal care(ANC) visits, twin pregnancy, urinary tract infection(UTI/STD) during pregnancy, Pregnancy-Induced Hypertension (PIH), antepartum hemorrhage, diagnosed chorioamniotis, Duration after the rupture of membranes(ROM), maternal infection history and presence of chronic illness.

Clinical presentation/features-related variables

Fever, respiratory distress, tachycardia, poor feeding, dehydration, vomiting, hypothermia, meconium aspiration syndrome, chest indrawing, lethargy, jaundice and cyanosis.

Medical treatment or care

Medications type, Duration of antibiotic treatment, Number of antibiotic switches/change and assisted with bag and mask.

Operational definitions

Neonate

A new born from birth until 28 days old.

Neonatal sepsis

Sepsis diagnosed and confirmed by physician among neonates.

Event

Recovery of neonate admitted with neonatal sepsis.

Comorbidities

In addition to sepsis the neonate had; necrotizing enterocolitis, congenital heart diseases and neurodevelopmental anomalies32.

Early-onset neonatal sepsis

Sepsis diagnosed and confirmed by physician among neonates age less than seven days.

Late-onset neonatal sepsis

Sepsis diagnosed and confirmed by physician among neonates age seven days or more.

Recovery

If a neonate is recovered from the infection after completing the treatment according to physician diagnosis.

Defaulter

Refers to neonate left (or stops treatment) the treatment unit against medical advice or the treatment.

Death

A neonate died by NS during the treatment or in the treatment unit.

Censored

It refers to a neonate defaulted from the treatment, referred, died, or transferred.

Time to recovery

A time from the admission date by NS to the discharge date while the neonate is recovered. It was measured by subtracting the date of admission from the discharge date (time in days until recovery/discharge).

Baseline period

Starting from the first day of admission to NICU with a diagnosis of Sepsis.

Data collection tool and procedure

Data collection tool

A data collection tool was adapted from another peer reviewed article conducted in Ethiopia3,31 (Supplementary file). Data abstraction was designed based on study objectives, and contains five parts; checklist related to socio-demographic information, maternal related information, neonatal related and clinical presentation and medical care related information which was collect from medical records.

Data collection procedure

All available information on patient records was checked in the selected public comprehensive specialized hospitals from January 1st, 2021 to April 30, 2023 was retrieved from the inpatient Health Management Information System (HMIS) registry book.

The time from the diagnose of sepsis was the starting point for retrospective follow-up and the end point was the date of recovery, the date of lost to follow-up or censored (improved, referred or against medical treatment) or the date of death. All neonatal sepsis charts admitted to the selected four hospitals from January 1, 2021 to April 30, 2023 was checked from the (HMIS) registration book.

The records of all study participants were selected according to the eligibility criteria. Recovery was confirmed by discharge note complemented and registration book. Recovery time was calculated from the date of admission to the date of discharge.

Two Masters of Science for supervision and six BSc Nurse for data collection were assigned. One day training was given to data collectors and supervisors regarding significance of the study and ways of data collection process. The supervisors monitored the data collection process.

Data quality control

To ensure the quality of the data, supervisors and data collectors were trained on how and what information they should collect from the targeted data sources. Prior to data collection, data extraction form was tested on 5% (32 charts) of neonatal charts with neonatal sepsis at Butajira hospital before the data collection to ensure that the data abstraction format was consistent with study requirements. The completeness of the collected data were checked on site daily basis during data collection and gave prompt feedback by the supervisors and the principal investigator. During data management, storage, cleaning and review, all completed data collection forms were checked for completeness and accuracy. The principal investigator examines consistency by selecting cards at random and comparing their similarity.

Data processing and analysis

Data were entered into Epi data version 3.1. The consistency of data were also checked before analysis to avoid errors during data entry. After that, the data were exported to SPSS version 25 for analysis. Descriptive statistics (frequency and percentages) were used to summarize categorical variables.

Before performing Cox-proportional hazard regression, model and goodness-of-fit was checked by Cox Snell residuals and assumptions were also checked by using Schoenfeld residual test. For each independent predictor bivariate cox proportional Hazard regression was performed. Multicollinearity was checked for estimating the correlation between all pairs of variables by variance inflation factor value ≥ 533. Variables with p-value < 0.25 in bivariate analysis was included in multivariable cox proportional hazard regression. And p-value < 0.05 was used to declare the strength of association. Outcome was dichotomized into censored “0” or recover “1”.

Kaplan Meier survival curve and log-rank test was used to describe the survival function for each predictor. Results were presented in text, tables, and graphs based on the type of variable. Median recovery time and Incidence density rate (IDR) was calculated.

Ethical consideration

Ethical clearance and approval for this study was obtained from the research ethical review committee of College of Medicine and Health Sciences, Debre Brehan University (protocol number IRB-148). Also, a permission letter was obtained from each hospital manager before the full scale data collection. An informed consent was waived by the ethics committee of College of Medicine and Health Sciences, Debre Brehan University because the study was conducted through review of medical charts. Patients’ names were not recorded. All procedures were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations.

Dissemination of the result

The findings of this study disseminated to Debre Brehan University Asrat Woldeyes health science campus school of Nursing and Midwifery, department of pediatrics and child health nursing, to all public comprehensive specialized hospitals in SNNPRS health bureau and other organizations working on related area to be used as a baseline for intervention.

Factor associated to neonatal sepsis recovery time

The Cox proportional hazard regression model was used to identify determinants of recovery of neonatal sepsis. Bivariate analysis was performed first using cox proportional hazard regression to identify which variable had an association with time to recovery from neonatal sepsis.

Those variables like; residence, number of ANC visit, Twin pregnancy, intrapartum fever, EBF initiation within one hour, having history of resuscitated at birth, BCG vaccination, time of infection onset, assisted with bag and mask/requirement of invasive ventilation, having history of fever, respiratory distress, hypothermia, comorbidity, age of neonate, birth weight, 5th minute APGAR score, number of anti-biotic change and place of delivery were statistically significant with a p-value ≤ 0.25 and candidate for multivariate cox regression model.

In a multivariable proportional hazard model; those variables like; resuscitated at birth, having history of fever, frequent antibiotics change and respiratory distress were remains independent determinants of recovery time among neonates with neonatal sepsis with (p value < 0.05).

The multivariable analysis result shown that; the hazard of prolonged time to recovery of NS among neonates with fever was 18% delayed than its counterparts. Neonates with history of resuscitation at birth were delayed by 35% in time to recovery of NS as compared to neonates without history resuscitation at birth. Similarly, the time to recovery of NS among neonates with respiratory distress was prolonged by 29% as compared to its counterparts. The neonates who had no history of antibiotic change were 2 times shorter time to recovery from NS (Table 8).

Discussion

This study assessed the time to recovery of neonatal sepsis and determinant factors among neonates admitted in Public Comprehensive Specialized Hospitals of SNNPRS, Ethiopia. In this study, the neonates with sepsis were followed for a total of 5424-neonate day observations.

The results of this study showed that the overall recovery time of neonatal sepsis was 8 days (IQR; 7, 10). This result in line with the previous study done in Addis Ababa which was reported as 8.92 days34. And lower than the previous study conducted in Arbaminch 12.74 days7; the possible reason for discrepancy might be due to sample size difference and level of hospitals that the study was carried out (general and primary hospitals) while the current study was conducted in comprehensive specialized hospital which have trained and specialized care giver on newborn care. And this is also lower than the study done in Brazil that report 19 days which was two times more than the current study35. The possible difference may be due to sample size difference and different source population that it assesses the LDOHS of all neonates admitted to NICU.

This results of this study is higher than the previous studies conducted in Uganda and India which reported 5.5 and 5.4 days respectively36,37; the reason for this discrepancy might be due to difference in study design, sample size and age range of the participants they used(less than 4 month (birth to 120 days)), this may help them for strength of their immunization and early recovery. The result of this study is also higher than the previous studies done in Bahir Dar34, Gondar31 and Dire Dawa20, 6 days, 7 days and 7 days respectively.

During the follow up period; recovery probability at the beginning, half and end of study was 99.84%, 18.26% and 1.98% respectively. This finding was in line with the previous study conducted in Gondar31. The possible similarity may be due to almost similar sample size (631) and model of analysis.

In this study the incidence rate of recovery is 9.86 per 100 neonate days. This result is consistent with the study conducted in Gondar which was 10.78 per100 neonate days. The variation might be due to gestational age < 37 weeks in the previous study was small in number than the current one, this may cause them to have increased rate of recovery31.

In this study neonates with fever during admission were delayed by 18% in time to recover from NS as compared to their counter parts. This finding is supported by previous studies conducted in Nigeria38 and Iraq39. The reason may be due to the fact that; as the neonates have an infection, the body’s temperature usually rises as it tries to fight off the host causing the infection as a result there is the exhaustion of immunity which leads to late recovery.

In this study those neonates who had no history of antibiotic change were 2 times shorter time to recover from NS than those neonates with history of one and above antibiotic change during their stays. This finding is inconsistence with the study done in Philadelphia40. The observed difference may be due to small sample size (n = 194) and age cut of point (< 1year) on the pervious study.

In this study the hazard of prolonged time to recovery of neonatal sepsis among neonates with respiratory distress were 29% less likely to recover fast as compared to those neonates without respiratory distress. This finding is in line with the study conducted in Nigeria38. The possible reason for consistence may be due to similar age limit of the study participant. However this result was inconsistence with the study done in Bahir Dar Felege Hiwot Referral Hospital41. The observed difference may be due to the variation in study design and model of analysis that the previous used logistic regression for outcome of neonate. The possible explanation is that; respiratory distress may leads to low gas exchange and increased physiological dead space, which causes multiple organ hypoxia and dysfunction; as a result neonates with such complication have prolonged hospital stay.

The time to recovery among neonates with resuscitation at birth is delayed by 35% from NS compared to their counterparts. This finding was supported by previous studies done in Woldia and Dessie comprehensive specialized hospital25, Tanzania17 and Kaffa zone42. The possible fact may be due to the lumen of the peripheral airway of the newborn is thin and respiratory secretions are overflowing which could predispose to atelectasis. For the collapsed lungs, performing different procedures may cause for an entry point for microbial agents. And also resuscitation might be done with unsterile procedure, which could introduce microbes into the respiratory system of the neonate whose immune system is not well developed and unable to fight against other nosocomial infection and become risk for prolonged hospital admission.

In this study those neonates who had no history of antibiotic change were 2 times shorter time to recovery from NS than those neonates with history of one and above antibiotic change during their stays. This finding is inconsistence with the study done in Philadelphia40. The observed difference may be due to small sample size (n = 194) and age cut of point (< 1year) on the pervious study.

This finding is also inconsistent with the previous study conducted in Nigeria38 which report it as it has no effect on the patient outcome and recommended for further validation, this inconsistence may be due to difference in model of analysis that it was used. However this finding is supported by WHO guideline that recommends parenteral antibiotics treatment for a neonate having known risk of sepsis for no less than 2 days43. This might be due to fear of antibiotic resistance to the host causing the infection.

Conclusion and recommendation

Duration of recovery from neonatal sepsis is acceptable according to previous literatures but still it needs hastening the recovery time. The time it took to recovery from neonatal sepsis had affected by, having history of fever, respiratory distress and frequent/number of antibiotic change. Therefore, health care providers, especially those working in NICU must pay more attention for prevention and early intervention of complication. The above-mentioned factors could be used for the early identification of neonates with sepsis at risk for delayed recovery time.

Strength and limitation

The study was conducted at the multicenter scope with different variable categories, which was indicated as a limitation by most studies. The study was retrospective and centered on secondary data, it did not take into account all possible predictors of the outcome variable, such as blood chemistry tests, other health professions skill was a limitation.

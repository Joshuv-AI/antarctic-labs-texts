On the Real Zeroes of Half-integral Weight Hecke Cusp Forms, II
Jesse Jääsaari

Abstract
. We show that for ≫ K 2 \gg K^{2} of the half-integral weight Hecke cusp forms in the Kohnen plus subspace with weight bounded by a large parameter K K , the number of ”real” zeroes grows at the expected rate. A key technical step in the proof is to obtain sharp bounds for the mollified first and second moments of quadratic twists of modular L L -functions.

2.
The Strategy

In this section we explain the ideas behind Theorem
1.2
. But before that we very briefly outline the structure of the approach in
[
13
]
. There the main idea was to study sign changes of the Fourier coefficients
c_{g}(|d|)
of a half-integral weight Hecke cusp form
g
along the (odd) fundamental discriminants
d
. It is convenient to normalise the Fourier coefficients
c_{g}(|d|)
by the factor
\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}
, where

(2.1)

\displaystyle\alpha_{g}:=\frac{\Gamma\left(k-\frac{1}{2}\right)}{(4\pi)^{k-\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{2}^{2}}>0.

Throughout this section we set
X=K/Y
. Very briefly, it follows from a steepest descend argument that in order to obtain real zeroes of
g
one needs to produce fundamental discriminants
d_{\pm}
in short intervals
x\leq(-1)^{k}d\leq x+H
with
H
as small as possible in terms of
X\sim x
so that
\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}c_{g}(|d_{-}|)<-k^{-\delta}<k^{-\delta}<\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}c_{g}(|d_{+}|)
for some
\delta>1/2
. For the purpose of exposition we pretend here that we are only looking for numbers
d_{\pm}
for which
c_{g}(|d_{-}|)<0<c_{g}(|d_{+}|)
. To detect such an event one considers the inequality

\left|\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{x\leq(-1)^{k}d\leq x+H}\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}c_{g}(|d|)\right|\leq\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{x\leq(-1)^{k}d\leq x+H}\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}|c_{g}(|d|)

and notes that the inequality is strict if and only if
c_{g}(|d|)
has a sign change in the interval
[x,x+H]
. Here
\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}\,
signifies that we are summing over odd fundamental discriminants. In
[
13
]
it was shown that one is able to detect sign changes for many forms if
H
is chosen to be a power of
\log K
. Indeed, choosing
H=(\log X)^{9}
it was shown that for
\gg K^{2}/(\log K)^{7/2}
of the forms
g\in S_{K}
the inequality

\left|\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{x\leq(-1)^{k}d\leq x+H}\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}c_{g}(|d|)\right|<\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{x\leq(-1)^{k}d\leq x+H}\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}|c_{g}(|d|)

holds for
\gg X/(\log X)^{5/2}
of
x\sim X
from which one immediately deduces part (1) of Theorem
1.1
. The two auxiliary results needed for this are contained in the following lemma. Here
\omega_{f}
(sometimes we write
\omega_{g}
if
g\in S_{k+1/2}^{+}(4)
corresponds to
f\in H_{2k}
under the Shimura correspondence. Similarly for the mollifier we shall use
M_{g}(d)
and
M_{f}(d)
interchangeably) are the standard harmonic weights that appear in the Petersson trace formula.

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
13
, Lemmas 2.7. and 2.8]
)
Let
h
and
\phi
be compactly supported smooth weight functions on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Then for
X\ll\sqrt{K}
we have that

(1)

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\sum_{g\in B_{k+1/2}^{+}}\alpha_{g}\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\,|c_{g}(|d|)|^{2}\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)=\frac{2XK}{3\pi^{2}}\widehat{h}(0)\widehat{\phi}(0)+O_{\varepsilon}\left(KX^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right).

(2)

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\sum_{g\in B_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}}\alpha_{g}^{2}\omega_{g}^{-1}\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\,|c_{g}(|d|)|^{4}\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)\ll XK\log(XK).

The loss of powers of the logarithm in Theorem
1.1
stems from the fact that in the previous lemma the sizes of the second and fourth moment of the Fourier coefficients are not of the same order of magnitude. As mentioned above, the reason for this is that the size of
|c_{g}(|d|)|
fluctuates as
d
traverses over the fundamental discriminants.

To remedy this one can introduce a positive quantity
M_{g}(d)
with the property that
|c_{g}(|d|)M_{g}(d)|\approx 1
and evaluate the moments of
c_{g}(|d|)M_{g}(d)
. This then yields information about the sign changes of
c_{g}(|d)
as
M_{g}(d)>0
. Below we shall choose the mollifier
M_{g}(d)
so that, assuming
X\ll\sqrt{K}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\sum_{g\in B_{k+1/2}^{+}}\alpha_{g}^{2}\omega_{g}^{-1}\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\,|c_{g}(|d|)|^{4}M_{g}(d)^{4}\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)

\displaystyle\asymp\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\sum_{g\in B_{k+1/2}^{+}}\alpha_{g}\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\,|c_{g}(|d|)|^{2}M_{g}(d)^{2}\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)\asymp XK

for compactly supported smooth weight functions
h
and
\phi
, which then implies Theorem
1.2
by repeating the arguments of
[
13
]
verbatim except with
c_{g}(|d|)
replaced by
c_{g}(|d|)M_{g}(|d|)
.

We now compare the strategies to compute the mollified moments to the methods used to establish Lemma
2.1
in
[
13
]
. Concerning the second moment, the strategy is broadly similar to that in
[
13
]
: one executes the
g
-sum using the half-integral weight variant of the Petersson formula after which the
d
- and
k
-sums are evaluated by the Poisson summation. For the mollified second moment we first instead use Waldspurger’s formula to express
|c_{g}(|d|)|^{2}
as a central
L
-value on which an approximate functional equation is then applied. After that the
d
- and
k
-sums are evaluated by the Poisson summation. The final step is to relate the resulting main term to an Euler product that is estimated by Mertens’ theorem.

For the the unmollified fourth moment was treated by starting with Waldspurger’s formula and an approximate functional equation, after which a large sieve inequality of Deshouillers and Iwaniec was used to estimate the double
(g,k)
-sum. The final step was to average over the fundamental discriminants trivially. With a mollifier this strategy does not work and we need to proceed differently. However, the first steps are identical: by Waldspurger’s formula we need to estimate the sum

\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\sum_{f\in B_{k}}\omega_{f}L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes\chi_{d}\right)^{2}M_{f}(d)^{4}.

Using an approximate functional equation we express the central
L
-value by essentially a finite Dirichlet series. After expanding the definition of
M_{f}(d)
we execute the sum over
B_{k}
using the Petersson formula. This splits the sum into two parts, diagonal and off-diagonal, in a natural way. The diagonal term can be written as an expectation value

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\mathbb{E}(L(X;d,k)M_{2}(X;d)),

where
L(X;d,k)
is a random
L
-function build from random variables
X
, which model the behaviour of Hecke eigenvalues
\lambda_{f}(n)
. Similarly,
M_{2}(X;d)
is random mollifier modelling the behaviour of
M_{f}(d)^{4}
. Now, using the Mellin inversion we may express (
2.2
) as a double line integral

\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\int\limits_{(1)}\int\limits_{(1)}\left(\frac{2\pi}{d}\right)^{-s_{1}-s_{2}}\frac{\Gamma(s_{1}+k)}{\Gamma(k)}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(s_{2}+k)}{\Gamma(k)}e^{s_{1}^{2}+s_{2}^{2}}F(s_{1},s_{2};X,d)\,\frac{\mathrm{d}s_{1}\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}}{s_{1}s_{2}}\right)

for certain function
F(s_{1},s_{2};X,d)
for which
\mathbb{E}(F(s_{1},s_{2};X,d))
has poles at
s_{2}=0
and
s_{2}=-s_{1}
. It will turn out that the contribution of the latter pole cancels the off-diagonal from the initial application of the Petersson formula. This gives a new instance of a similar phenomenon appearing in some prior works
[
3
,
4
,
16
,
30
]
. The computations here have similarities with the arguments of Khan
[
16
]
and we are able to use some technical results he proved. The pole at
s_{2}=0
gives the dominant contribution, which turns out to be
\ll XK
after some calculations with Euler products. Here the presence of random variables greatly simplifies the computations, especially allowing us to control the effect of ”small” primes in the mollifier using some ideas from
[
6
]
.

4.
Choosing the mollifier

4.1.
Heuristics

Let
d
denote a fundamental discriminant, and
\chi_{d}(\cdot)=\left(\frac{d}{\cdot}\right)
denote the primitive quadratic character of conductor
|d|
. Let
f\otimes\chi_{d}
denote the twist of
f\in H_{2k}
by the character
\chi_{d}
, and let
L(s,f\otimes\chi_{d})
denote the twisted
L
-function

L(s,f\otimes\chi_{d}):=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{f}(m)\chi_{d}(m)}{m^{s}}=\prod_{p}L_{p}(s,f\otimes\chi_{d})

for
\textnormal{Re}(s)>1
. Here

L_{p}(s,f\otimes\chi_{d}):=\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{f}(p)\chi_{d}(p)}{p^{s}}+\frac{\chi_{d}(p)^{2}}{p^{2s}}\right)^{-1}.

By Waldspurger’s formula the absolute square of
c_{g}(|d|)
is proportional to the central
L
-value
L(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{d})
, where
f\in H_{2k}
is a classical holomorphic cusp form attached to
g\in S_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}(4)
via the Shimura correspondence. This motivates us to choose the mollifier
M_{g}(d)
so that it behaves like
L(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{d})^{-1/2}
, at least for typical
g
and
d
.

For
|d|\gg k^{\varepsilon}
we expect that
\log L(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{d})
to be approximated by

\sum_{p^{\ell}<|d|^{\varepsilon}}\frac{\left(\alpha_{p}^{\ell}+\beta_{p}^{\ell}\right)\chi_{d}(p)^{\ell}}{\ell p^{\ell/2}},

where
\alpha_{p},\beta_{p}
are the Satake parameters of
f
at
p
. Recalling that
\alpha_{p}+\beta_{p}=\lambda_{f}(p)
and
\alpha_{p}^{2}+\beta_{p}^{2}=\lambda_{f}(p^{2})-1
it is expected (and can be shown under GRH) that the sum above is

\sum_{p<|d|^{\varepsilon}}\frac{\lambda_{f}(p)\chi_{d}(p)}{\sqrt{p}}-\frac{1}{2}\log\log|d|\left(1+o(1)\right).

Thus heuristically we expect (unconditionally) that

(4.1)

\displaystyle L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes\chi_{d}\right)\approx(\log|d|)^{-1/2}\exp\left(\sum_{p<|d|^{\varepsilon}}\frac{\lambda_{f}(p)\chi_{d}(p)}{\sqrt{p}}\right).

Here the approximation sign
\approx
should not be taken too literally. Recall that we wish to choose the mollifier so that it approximates
L(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{d})^{-1/2}
. By the discussion above a natural choice for the mollifier would be, for suitable
x
depending on
X
when
d\sim X
,

M_{g}(d)=(\log x)^{1/4}\exp(P(f,d)),

where

P(f,d):=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{\lambda_{f}(p)\chi_{d}(p)}{\sqrt{p}}.

In order for
M_{g}(d)
to be a good approximation for
L(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{d})^{-1/2}
one wishes to choose
x\sim X^{\varepsilon}
for some fixed
\varepsilon>0
. However, with this choice one faces a technical problem that expanding the exponential factor into a Taylor series leads to a long Dirichlet polynomial, which in turn makes it impossible to facilitate certain necessary computations. On the other hand, for rather small
x
, say
x\asymp\log X
, the resulting Dirichlet polynomial has a short length but in this case we do not expect
M_{g}(d)
to be a good approximation for
L(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{d})^{-1/2}
.

The idea that allows us to take
x
to be a small power of
X
is to use an iterative scheme of Radziwiłł and Soundararajan
[
25
]
, which is partly motivated by the Brun–Hooley sieve. Heuristically we expect that
P(f,d)
has a Gaussian limiting distribution over
d\sim x
with mean zero and variance
\sim\log\log x
. We consider the disjoint sets of primes in the intervals
I_{0},I_{1},...,I_{J}
with
I_{j}=[K^{\theta_{j-1}},K^{\theta_{j}}[
for
j=1,...,J
and set
I_{0}:=]c,K^{\theta_{0}}]
, where
c\geq 2
is fixed. Due to the expected Gaussian distribution, choosing
\theta_{j}
’s appropriately, we anticipate that each of the terms

\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\in I_{j}}\frac{\lambda_{f}(p)\chi_{d}(p)}{\sqrt{p}}\right)

should typically be approximated by a short Dirichlet polynomial depending on the size of
\theta_{j}
as the sum inside the argument is expected to be small for most
d
. We take
J
to be so large that
\theta_{J}\geq\eta_{2}
for some fixed
\eta_{2}>0
, meaning that
J\asymp\log\log\log K
. This leads one to expect that for typical
d\sim X
we should have that

\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\leq X^{\varepsilon}}\frac{\lambda_{f}(p)\chi_{d}(p)}{\sqrt{p}}\right)\approx\prod_{j=0}^{J}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\in I_{j}}\frac{\lambda_{f}(p)\chi_{d}(p)}{\sqrt{p}}\right)

can be approximated by a short Dirichlet polynomial. Due to technical reasons it is better to replace
\lambda_{f}(m)
with the completely multiplicative function
a_{f}(m)
defined by
a_{f}(p)=\lambda_{f}(p)
at the primes. The key observation is that using the Taylor expansion
4
4

4

e^{z}=(1+O(e^{-9V}))\sum_{0\leq j\leq 10V}z^{j}/j!
for
|z|\leq V

and binomial theorem we have

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\in I_{j}}\frac{a_{f}(p)\chi_{d}(p)}{\sqrt{p}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|n\Rightarrow p\in I_{j}\\

if

Here
\Omega(n)
is the number of prime divisor of a positive integer
n
(counted with multiplicity),
\nu
is a multiplicative function such that
\nu(p^{\alpha})=1/\alpha!
, and

A concrete choice of parameters that suffices for us is the same one as in

(see also

so that

The discussion above motivates us to choose our mollifier as

\displaystyle M_{g}(d):=(\log K)^{1/4}\prod_{j=0}^{J}M_{g}(d;j),

This mollifier is designed so that it behaves like
L(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{d})^{-1/2}
for typical

and fundamental discriminant

.

The method used to prove Theorem

Proposition 4.1
.

Let

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\sum_{g\in B^{+}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\alpha_{g}\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\,|c_{g}(|d|)|^{2}M_{g}(d)^{2}\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)\gg XK.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\phi
and
h
be smooth compactly supported functions on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Suppose that

. Then we have

(4.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\sum_{g\in B^{+}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\alpha_{g}^{2}\omega_{g}^{-1}\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\,|c_{g}(|d|)|^{4}M_{g}(d)^{4}\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)\ll XK.

In the latter proposition and throughout the paper we write as an abuse of notation

for

when

Given these the proof of Theorem

.

4.2.
Evaluating the fourth moment

The proof of Proposition
4.2
warrants some comments. But before that, one easily observes using the complete multiplicativity that for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,

(4.4)

\displaystyle M_{g}(d)^{2\ell}=(\log K)^{\ell/2}\sum_{n\leq K^{2\ell\theta_{J}}}\frac{h_{\ell}(n)a_{f}(n)\lambda(n)\chi_{d}(n)}{2^{\Omega(n)}\sqrt{n}}.

Here

h_{\ell}(n):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{0}\cdots n_{J}=n\\
p|n_{j}\Rightarrow p\in I_{j}\,\forall\,0\leq j\leq J\\
\Omega(n_{j})\leq 2\ell\ell_{j}\,\forall\,0\leq j\leq J\end{subarray}}\nu_{2\ell}(n_{0};\ell_{0})\cdots\nu_{2\ell}(n_{J};\ell_{J})

and

\nu_{r}(n;\ell):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}\cdots n_{r}=n\\
\Omega(n_{j})\leq\ell\,\forall\,1\leq j\leq r\end{subarray}}\nu(n_{1})\cdots\nu(n_{r}).

Let
\nu_{j}(n):=(\nu*\cdots*\nu)(n)
denote the
j
-fold convolution of
\nu
. Note that
\nu_{r}(n;\ell)\leq\nu_{r}(n)
and that if
\Omega(n)\leq\ell
, then
\nu_{r}(n;\ell)=\nu_{r}(n)
.

After opening
M_{g}(d)^{4}
using (
4.4
), in order to prove Proposition
4.2
we would require an asymptotic evaluation of the multiple average

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\sum_{f\in B_{k}}\omega_{f}\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\,L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes\chi_{d}\right)^{2}a_{f}(r)\chi_{d}(r)\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right).

This can be done, but applying the resulting formula to the left-hand side of (
4.3
) results with an unwieldy expression for the main term, which is hard to evaluate. Instead we introduce a random
L
-function in which the Hecke eigenvalues
\lambda_{f}(m)
are modelled by random variables
X(m)
(defined shortly), and then match our expression with the random analogue. Comparison with a random model allows us to sidestep a number of technical points that would otherwise require involved effort to resolve. In particular, using the independence of the random variables
\{X(p)\}_{p}
at the primes reduces many of the computations to ”local” ones evaluated at each prime.

5.
Preliminaries

5.1.
Half-integral weight forms

Let
S_{k+\frac{1}{2}}(4)
denote the space of holomorphic cusp forms of weight
k+\frac{1}{2}
for the Hecke congruence group
\Gamma_{0}(4)
. Any such form
g
has a Fourier expansion of the form

(5.1)

\displaystyle g(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{g}(n)n^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{4}}e(nz),

where
c_{g}(n)
are the Fourier coefficients of
g
.

For
g,h\in S_{k+\frac{1}{2}}(4)
, we define the Petersson inner product
\langle g,h\rangle
to be

(5.2)

\displaystyle\langle g,h\rangle:=\int\limits_{\Gamma_{0}(4)\backslash\mathbb{H}}g(z)\overline{h}(z)y^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y}{y^{2}}.

For any odd prime
p
there exists a Hecke operator
T(p^{2})
acting on the space of half-integral weight modular forms. We call a half-integral weight cusp form a Hecke cusp form if
T(p^{2})g=\gamma_{g}(p)g
for all
p>2
for some
\gamma_{g}(p)\in\mathbb{C}
.

The Kohnen plus subspace
S_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}(4)\subset S_{k+\frac{1}{2}}(4)
consists of all weight
k+\frac{1}{2}
Hecke cusp forms whose
n^{\text{th}}
Fourier coefficient vanishes whenever
(-1)^{k}n\equiv 2,3\,(\text{mod }4)
. This space has a basis consisting of simultaneous eigenfunctions of the
T(p^{2})
for odd
p
. It is well-known that, as
k\longrightarrow\infty
, asymptotically one third of half-integral weight cusp forms lie in the Kohnen plus space.

Kohnen proved
[
18
]
that there exists a Hecke algebra isomorphism between
S^{+}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}(4)
and the space of level
1
cusp forms of weight
2k
. That is,
S^{+}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}(4)\simeq H_{2k}
as Hecke modules. Also recall that every Hecke cusp form
g\in S^{+}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}(4)
can be normalised so that it has real Fourier coefficients and throughout the article we assume that
g
has been normalised in this way.

The proof of our main result uses the explicit form of Waldspurger’s formula due to Kohnen and Zagier
[
17
]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

For a Hecke cusp form
g\in S^{+}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}(4)
we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle|c_{g}(|d|)|^{2}=L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes\chi_{d}\right)\cdot\frac{(k-1)!}{\pi^{k}}\cdot\frac{\langle g,g\rangle}{\langle f,f\rangle}

for each fundamental discriminant
d
with
(-1)^{k}d>0
, where
f
is a holomorphic modular form attached to
g
via the Shimura correspondence, normalised so that
\lambda_{f}(1)=1
.

Remark 5.2
.

Using the normalisation (
2.1
) the above formula can be written in the form

\alpha_{g}|c_{g}(|d|)|^{2}=\omega_{f}L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes\chi_{d}\right).

We also remark that
L(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{d})
vanishes when
(-1)^{k}d<0
due to the sign in the functional equation. It follows directly from (
5.3
) that
L(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{d})\geq 0
otherwise. Here for
f_{1},f_{2}\in H_{2k}
the Petersson inner product, which is still denoted by
\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle
, is defined to be

\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle:=\int\limits_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}}f_{1}(z)\overline{f_{2}}(z)y^{2k}\frac{\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y}{y^{2}}.

We write
\|f\|_{2}^{2}:=\langle f,f\rangle
.

Using the Hecke relations we have for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
and prime
p
that

\lambda_{f}\left(p\right)^{\alpha}=\sum_{c=0}^{\alpha}h_{\alpha}(c)\lambda_{f}\left(p^{c}\right),

where
h_{\alpha}(c)
are non-negative integers given by

h_{\alpha}(c):=\frac{2}{\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}(2\cos\theta)^{\alpha}\sin((c+1)\theta)\sin\theta\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

We call these Chebyshev coefficients. Recall that
a_{f}(n)=\prod_{p^{\alpha}||n}\lambda_{f}(p)^{\alpha}
. By the repeated use of Hecke relations it is easy to see that

(5.4)

\displaystyle a_{f}(n)=\sum_{u|n}c_{n}(u)\lambda_{f}(u),

where

c_{n}(u):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{c}||u\\
p^{\alpha}||n\end{subarray}}h_{\alpha}(c).

Later in the paper we require some values of
c_{n}(u)
with
n
(and
u
) powers of a fixed prime. These are easily computed and listed here:

c_{1}(1)=h_{0}(0)=0

c_{p}(1)=h_{1}(0)=0

c_{p}(p)=h_{1}(1)=1

c_{p^{2}}(1)=h_{2}(0)=1

c_{p^{2}}(p)=h_{2}(1)=0

c_{p^{2}}(p^{2})=h_{2}(2)=1

c_{p^{3}}(1)=h_{3}(0)=0

c_{p^{3}}(p)=h_{3}(1)=2

c_{p^{3}}(p^{2})=h_{3}(2)=0

c_{p^{3}}(p^{3})=h_{3}(3)=1

Table 1. Certain values of
c_{n}(u)
.

We also note the easy bounds
0\leq h_{\alpha}(c)\leq 2^{\alpha+1}
from which we immediately deduce the trivial bound
c_{n}(u)\leq 2^{\Omega(n)}
.

An approximate functional equation also plays a key role in our work. The following is an easy modification of
[
25
, Lemma 5]
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
f
be a Hecke cusp form of weight
2k
for the full modular group
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Then

\displaystyle L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes\chi_{d}\right)=2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{f}(m)\chi_{d}(m)}{\sqrt{m}}V_{k}\left(\frac{m}{|d|}\right),

where, for any
\sigma>2
,

(5.5)

\displaystyle V_{k}(x):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{(\sigma)}g(s)x^{-s}e^{s^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}\quad\text{with}\quad g(s):=(2\pi)^{-s}\frac{\Gamma(s+k)}{\Gamma(k)}.

Furthermore, for
|\textnormal{Im}(s)|\leq\sqrt{k}
we have for any
A\geq 1
that

(5.6)

\displaystyle V_{k}(\xi)=1+O(\xi^{1/2-o(1)}),\qquad V_{k}(\xi)=1+O_{A}(\xi^{-A}).

We also have the estimates

\displaystyle V_{k}(\xi)\ll_{A}\left(\frac{k}{\xi}\right)^{A},

\displaystyle V_{k}^{(B)}(\xi)\ll_{A,B}\xi^{-B}\left(\frac{k}{\xi}\right)^{A}

for any
A>0
and integer
B\geq 0
.

In addition, using Stirling’s formula there exists a holomorphic function
R(s,k)
so that for
\textnormal{Re}(s)\geq-k/2
we have
R(s,k)\ll|s|^{2}/k
and

(5.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(s+k)}{\Gamma(k)}=k^{s}e^{R(s,k)}\left(1+O\left(k^{-1}\right)\right).

In particular, for
|\textnormal{Re}(s)|\leq\sqrt{k}
we have

\frac{\Gamma(s+k)}{\Gamma(k)}=k^{s}\left(1+O\left(\frac{|s|^{2}}{k}\right)\right).

Note also that
V_{k}=V_{Ku+1}
for
u=(k-1)/K
.

To see that (
5.7
) holds, note that for
|\text{arg}(s)|\leq\pi-\delta
, Stirling’s formula gives

\Gamma(s)=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{s}}\bigg(\frac{s}{e}\bigg)^{s}\bigg(1+O\bigg(\frac{1}{|s|}\bigg)\bigg),

where the implied constant depends at most on
\delta
. Hence for
\textnormal{Re}(s)\geq-k/2
we have that

\begin{split}\frac{\Gamma(k+s)}{\Gamma(k)}=&\bigg(1+O\bigg(\frac{1}{k}\bigg)\bigg)\sqrt{\frac{k}{k+s}}\bigg(\frac{k}{e}\bigg)^{-k}\bigg(\frac{k+s}{e}\bigg)^{k+s}\\
=&\bigg(1+O\bigg(\frac{1}{k}\bigg)\bigg)k^{s}e^{-s}\bigg(1+\frac{s}{k}\bigg)^{k+s-\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

For
\textnormal{Re}(z)>-1/2
, we have that
\log(1+z)=z+O(|z|^{2})
. Hence, for
\textnormal{Re}(s)>-k/2
we have that

\begin{split}\bigg(1+\frac{s}{k}\bigg)^{k+s-\frac{1}{2}}=&\exp\bigg((k+s-\tfrac{1}{2})\bigg(\frac{s}{k}+O\bigg(\frac{|s|^{2}}{k^{2}}\bigg)\bigg)\\
=&\exp\bigg(s+O\bigg(\frac{|s|^{2}}{k^{2}}\bigg)\bigg).\end{split}

Thus we conclude that for
\textnormal{Re}(s)\geq-k/2
we have that

\frac{\Gamma(k+s)}{\Gamma(k)}=k^{s}\exp\bigg(O\bigg(\frac{|s|^{2}}{k^{2}}\bigg)\bigg)\bigg(1+O\bigg(\frac{1}{k}\bigg)\bigg),

as desired.

5.2.
Basic tools

5.2.1.
Summation formulas

One of the most important tools is the Petersson trace formula.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
m
and
n
be natural numbers, and
k
be a positive integer. Then

\sum_{f\in\mathcal{B}_{k}}\omega_{f}\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{f}(n)=1_{m=n}+2\pi i^{2k}\sum_{c=1}^{\infty}\frac{S(m,n;c)}{c}J_{2k-1}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{c}\right),

where
S(m,n;c)
is the usual Kloosterman sum and
J_{\nu}
is the
J
-Bessel function.

Let us now define two integral transforms. For a smooth compactly supported function
h
, we set

\displaystyle\hbar(y):=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{h(\sqrt{u})}{\sqrt{2\pi u}}e^{iyu}\,\mathrm{d}u

and

W_{K}^{(2)}(m,n,v):=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{V_{\sqrt{u}K+1}(m)V_{\sqrt{u}K+1}(n)h(\sqrt{u})}{\sqrt{2\pi u}}e^{iuv}\,\mathrm{d}u.

Main properties of these integral transforms have been worked out by Khan. He has shown
[
16
, (2.17)]
that

(5.8)

\displaystyle W_{K}^{(2)}(n,m,v)\ll_{A_{1},A_{2},B}\left(\frac{K}{n}\right)^{A_{1}}\left(\frac{K}{m}\right)^{A_{2}}v^{-B}

for any
A_{1},A_{2}>0
and
B\geq 0
. Thus
W_{K}^{(2)}(n,m,v)
is essentially supported on
n\leq K^{1+\varepsilon}
,
m\leq K^{1+\varepsilon}
and
v\leq K^{\varepsilon}
. Moreover, using integration by parts we have estimates for the derivatives;

(5.9)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial\xi^{j}}W_{K}^{(2)}\left(\frac{\xi}{|d|},\frac{z}{|d|},\frac{K^{2}c}{8\pi\sqrt{\xi z}}\right)\ll_{j,A,B}\left(\frac{\xi}{|d|}\right)^{-j-A}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\xi z}}{K^{2}c}\right)^{B}K^{\varepsilon}

for any
j\geq 0
,
A,B>0
.

We also have the identity
[
16
, (2.24)]

\displaystyle 2\sum_{k\equiv 0\,(2)}i^{k}h\left(\frac{k-1}{K}\right)V_{k}(n)V_{k}(m)J_{k-1}(t)

(5.10)

\displaystyle=-\frac{K}{\sqrt{t}}\textnormal{Im}\left(e^{-2\pi i/8}e^{it}W_{K}^{(2)}\left(n,m,\frac{K^{2}}{2t}\right)\right)+O\left(\frac{t}{K^{4}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}v^{4}\left|\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}V_{uK+1}(n)V_{uK+1}(m)h(u)e^{iuv}\,\mathrm{d}u\right|\,\mathrm{d}v\right).

A simple application of the Poisson summation gives

\displaystyle\sum_{k\equiv 0\,(2)}h\left(\frac{k-1}{K}\right)=\frac{K}{2}\widehat{h}(0)+O_{B}(K^{-B})

for every

.

For real

Then

\displaystyle W_{K}^{(2)}(m,n,v)=W\left(\frac{m}{K},\frac{n}{K},v\right)+O_{\varepsilon}\left(K^{-1+\varepsilon}\right).

Integrating by parts shows that
\hbar_{z}(v)\ll_{\textnormal{Re}(z),B}(1+|z|)^{B}v^{-B}
and consequently

for

Lemma 5.5

For

Another key tool is the Poisson summation formula.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
f
be a Schwartz function and
a
be a residue class modulo

\sum_{n\equiv\,a\,(c)}f(n)=\frac{1}{c}\sum_{n}\widehat{f}\left(\frac{n}{c}\right)e\left(\frac{an}{c}\right),

where

denotes the Fourier transform of

We shall also need a different variant of the Poisson summation formula. For this, let us define, for any

, a Gauss-type sum

(5.14)

Next we estimate the individual terms in the product. By Rankin’s trick we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|n\Rightarrow p\in I_{j}\\
\Omega(n)\leq 2\ell_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda(n)\iota(n)\nu_{2}(n)}{2^{\Omega(n)}\sqrt{n}}\prod_{p|\text{rad}(n)}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p|n\Rightarrow p\in I_{j}}\frac{\lambda(n)\iota(n)\nu_{2}(n)}{2^{\Omega(n)}\sqrt{n}}\prod_{p|\text{rad}(n)}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}+O\left(\frac{1}{4^{\ell_{j}}}\sum_{p|n\Rightarrow p\in I_{j}}\frac{\lambda(n)\iota(n)\nu_{2}(n)}{\sqrt{n}}\prod_{p|\text{rad}(n)}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}\right).

Note that the error term is

\ll\frac{1}{4^{\ell_{j}}}\prod_{p\in I_{j}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)\right)\ll\frac{1_{j=0}\cdot(\log K)^{O(1)}+1}{4^{\ell_{j}}}.

Using multiplicativity we also have

\displaystyle\sum_{p|n\Rightarrow p\in I_{j}}\frac{\lambda(n)\iota(n)\nu_{2}(n)}{2^{\Omega(n)}\sqrt{n}}\prod_{p|\text{rad}(n)}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\prod_{p\in I_{j}}\left(1-\frac{\iota(p)\nu_{2}(p)}{2\sqrt{p}}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}+\frac{\iota(p^{2})\nu_{2}(p^{2})}{2p}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}+O\left(\frac{1}{p^{3/2}}\right)\right).

Combining the previous estimates with the fact that

\prod_{p\in I_{j}}\left(1-\frac{\iota(p)\nu_{2}(p)}{2\sqrt{p}}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}+\frac{\iota(p^{2})\nu_{2}(p^{2})}{2p}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}+O\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)\right)^{-1}\ll 1_{j=0}\cdot(\log K)^{O(1)}+1

it follows that the main term equals

\displaystyle XK(\log K)^{1/2}\prod_{c<p\leq K^{\theta_{J}}}\left(1-\frac{\iota(p)\nu_{1}(p)}{2\sqrt{p}}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}+\frac{\iota(p^{2})\nu_{1}(p^{2})}{2p}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}+O\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\prod_{0\leq j\leq J}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1_{j=0}\cdot(\log K)^{O(1)}+1}{4^{\ell_{j}}}\right)\right).

To estimate the second product from below note that

\prod_{0\leq j\leq J}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1_{j=0}\cdot(\log K)^{O(1)}+1}{4^{\ell_{j}}}\right)\right)=1+O\left(\frac{1}{4^{\ell_{J}}}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}

as
\eta_{1}
was chosen to be sufficiently small.

Moreover, by Mertens’ theorem the first product is bounded from below by

\prod_{c<p<K^{\theta_{J}}}\left(1-\frac{1}{2p}+O\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)\right)\gg(\log K)^{-1/2}.

Hence, using this we get that (
8.6
) is

\gg XK(\log K)^{1/2}(\log K)^{-1/2}\gg XK.

This finishes the proof of Proposition
4.1
. ∎

9.
Relating the Fourth moment to a random model

Here our argument combines ingredients from
[
6
]
and
[
16
]
. Indeed, in order to compute the mollified fourth moment many technical calculations simplify by relating the original sum to a random model. We begin with a lemma, which plays a key role in the proof of Proposition
4.2
. Let us define a random Dirichlet series

L(X;d,k):=4\sum_{m_{1}=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m_{2}=1}^{\infty}\frac{X(m_{1})X(m_{2})\chi_{d}(m_{1}m_{2})}{\sqrt{m_{1}m_{2}}}V_{k}\left(\frac{m_{1}}{|d|}\right)V_{k}\left(\frac{m_{2}}{|d|}\right).

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
n\leq K^{\theta_{J}}
be a positive integer. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\chi_{d}(n)\phi\left(\frac{d}{X}\right)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\sum_{f\in B_{k}}\omega_{f}L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes\chi_{d}\right)^{2}a_{f}(n)

\displaystyle=\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)\chi_{d}(n)\sum_{k\sim K}\mathbb{E}\left(L(X;d,k)\sum_{u|n}c_{n}(u)X(u)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+2K\widehat{h}(0)\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\,\frac{\varphi(d)}{d}\phi\left(\frac{d}{X}\right)\sum_{u|n}\frac{c_{n}(u)}{\sqrt{u}}\chi_{d}(nu)\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\varepsilon)}\frac{e^{2x^{2}}}{x^{2}}\cdot u^{-x}\sum_{v|u}v^{2x}\,\mathrm{d}x+O\left(X^{3/4}K^{3/4+\varepsilon}\right).

Proof.

Using the approximate functional equation we have that the sum on the left-hand side is

4\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\,\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\sum_{f\in B_{k}}\sum_{m_{1}=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m_{2}=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{f}(m_{1})\lambda_{f}(m_{2})\chi_{d}(m_{1}m_{2})}{\sqrt{m_{1}m_{2}}}V_{k}\left(\frac{m_{1}}{|d|}\right)V_{k}\left(\frac{m_{2}}{|d|}\right)\chi_{d}(n)\sum_{u|n}c_{n}(u)\lambda_{f}(u).

Rearranging and using the Hecke relations this can be written as

\displaystyle 4\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\,\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\sum_{m_{1}=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m_{2}=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi_{d}(m_{1}m_{2}n)}{\sqrt{m_{1}m_{2}}}V_{k}\left(\frac{m_{1}}{|d|}\right)V_{k}\left(\frac{m_{2}}{|d|}\right)\sum_{u|n}c_{n}(u)\sum_{j|(m_{1},m_{2})}\sum_{f\in B_{k}}\lambda_{f}\left(\frac{m_{1}m_{2}}{j^{2}}\right)\lambda_{f}(u).

Now an application of the Petersson formula shows that this is

\displaystyle 4\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\,\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\sum_{m_{1}=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m_{2}=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi_{d}(m_{1}m_{2}n)}{\sqrt{m_{1}m_{2}}}V_{k}\left(\frac{m_{1}}{|d|}\right)V_{k}\left(\frac{m_{2}}{|d|}\right)\sum_{u|n}c_{n}(u)\sum_{j|(m_{1},m_{2})}1_{m_{1}m_{2}/j^{2}=u}+\text{error term},

where the error term is given by

\displaystyle 8\pi\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\,\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\sum_{m_{1}=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m_{2}=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi_{d}(m_{1}m_{2}n)}{\sqrt{m_{1}m_{2}}}V_{k}\left(\frac{m_{1}}{|d|}\right)V_{k}\left(\frac{m_{2}}{|d|}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\sum_{u|n}c_{n}(u)\sum_{j|(m_{1},m_{2})}i^{2k}\sum_{c=1}^{\infty}\frac{S(m_{1}m_{2}/j^{2},u;c)}{c}J_{2k-1}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{m_{1}m_{2}u}}{jc}\right).

For the main term we use the relations (
6.1
) and (
6.2
) to write it as

\displaystyle 4\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\,\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\sum_{m_{1}=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m_{2}=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi_{d}(m_{1}m_{2}n)}{\sqrt{m_{1}m_{2}}}V_{k}\left(\frac{m_{1}}{|d|}\right)V_{k}\left(\frac{m_{2}}{|d|}\right)\sum_{u|n}c_{n}(u)\sum_{j|(m_{1},m_{2})}\mathbb{E}\left(X\left(\frac{m_{1}m_{2}}{j^{2}}\right)X(u)\right)

\displaystyle=4\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\sum_{m_{1}=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m_{2}=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi_{d}(m_{1}m_{2}n)}{\sqrt{m_{1}m_{2}}}V_{k}\left(\frac{m_{1}}{|d|}\right)V_{k}\left(\frac{m_{2}}{|d|}\right)\sum_{u|n}c_{n}(u)\mathbb{E}\left(X(m_{1})X(m_{2})X(u)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\,\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\mathbb{E}\left(L(X;d,k)\chi_{d}(n)\sum_{u|n}c_{n}(u)X(u)\right).

Next we will analyse the error term. In what follows we temporarily think
d
as fixed and postpone the summation over

to the last moment. We execute the
k
-sum using (
5.2.1
) to see that the off-diagonal equals

\displaystyle-2\sqrt{\pi}K\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)\sum_{m_{1}=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m_{2}=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi_{d}(m_{1})\chi_{d}(m_{2})}{m_{1}^{3/4}m_{2}^{3/4}}\sum_{u|n}c_{n}(u)u^{-1/4}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\textnormal{Im}\left(e^{-2\pi i/8}\sum_{j|(m_{1},m_{2})}\sum_{c=1}^{\infty}\frac{S(m_{1}m_{2}/j^{2},u;c)}{\sqrt{c}}\cdot e\left(\frac{2\sqrt{m_{1}m_{2}u}}{jc}\right)W_{K}^{(2)}\left(\frac{m_{1}}{|d|},\frac{m_{2}}{|d|},\frac{K^{2}cj}{8\pi\sqrt{m_{1}m_{2}u}}\right)\right)+O\left(X^{3+\varepsilon}/K^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-2\sqrt{\pi}K\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)\sum_{m_{1}=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m_{2}=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi_{d}(m_{1})\chi_{d}(m_{2})}{m_{1}^{3/4}m_{2}^{3/4}}\sum_{u|n}c_{n}(u)u^{-1/4}

(9.1)

Splitting the

Applying Poisson summation to the

Similarly applying Poisson summation to the

\displaystyle\frac{-2\sqrt{2\pi}}{\sqrt{u}}K\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\,\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)\sum_{(j,d)=1}\frac{1}{j}\textnormal{Im}\bigg(e^{-\pi i/2}\sum_{c=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{[c,d]^{2}}\sum_{v}\sum_{\eta}\sum_{x\,([c,d])}\sum_{w\,([c,d])}\chi_{d}(x)\chi_{d}(w)

\displaystyle-2\sqrt{2\pi}K\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\,\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)(-1)^{\#\{p|d\}}\sum_{u|n}c_{n}(u)\frac{\chi_{d}(u)}{\sqrt{u}}

where in the last step we have used the fact that

Making the change of variables

, using the approximation (

By Mellin inversion we have, for any

\displaystyle W\left(\frac{hcuj}{K|d|v^{2}},\frac{hcj}{K|d|},\frac{K^{2}|d|v}{8\pi nu}\right)

Substituting these into (

\displaystyle\qquad\times\zeta(1+x+y)\prod_{p|d}\left(1-p^{-1-x-y}\right)\cdot\frac{\zeta(x+y)}{\zeta(1+x+y)}\prod_{p|d}\left(\frac{p^{x+y}-p}{p(p^{x+y}-1)+p-1}\right)\zeta(1+x+y-z)\,\mathrm{d}z\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y}{xy}.

Using (

\displaystyle 2K\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\,\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)(-1)^{\#\{p|d\}}\sum_{u|n}\frac{c_{n}(u)}{\sqrt{u}}\chi_{d}(u)\sum_{v|u}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{3}}\int\limits_{(\varepsilon)}\int\limits_{(\varepsilon)}\int\limits_{(\varepsilon)}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}(2\pi)^{-x-y}e^{x^{2}+y^{2}}u^{z-x}(8\pi)^{z}K^{x+y-2z}d^{x+y-z}v^{2x-z}\frac{h(\sqrt{\xi})}{\sqrt{\xi}}\xi^{(x+y)/2-z}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\Gamma(z)\left(\cos\left(\frac{\pi z}{2}\right)+i\sin\left(\frac{\pi z}{2}\right)\right)\zeta(1+x+y)\prod_{p|d}\left(1-p^{-1-x-y}\right)

Shifting the

\displaystyle 2K\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\,\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)(-1)^{\#\{p|d\}}\sum_{u|n}\frac{c_{n}(u)}{\sqrt{u}}\chi_{d}(u)\sum_{v|u}\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\int\limits_{(\varepsilon)}\int\limits_{(\varepsilon)}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}(2\pi K)^{-z}u^{z-x}(8\pi)^{z}e^{x^{2}+(z-x)^{2}}v^{2x-z}\frac{h(\sqrt{\xi})}{\sqrt{\xi}}\xi^{-z/2}

Simplifying this by recalling that

\displaystyle 2\widehat{h}(0)K\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\,\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)\frac{\varphi(d)}{d}\sum_{u|n}\frac{c_{n}(u)}{\sqrt{u}}\chi_{d}(u)\sum_{v|u}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{(\varepsilon)}e^{2x^{2}}u^{-x}v^{2x}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x^{2}}.

\displaystyle M_{2}(X;d)

\Omega(n)\leq 4\ell_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda(n)\chi_{d}(n)\nu_{4}(n;\ell_{j})}{2^{\Omega(n)}\sqrt{n}}\sum_{u|n}c_{n}(u)X(u)}_{=:M_{2,j}(X;d)}.

This is a random counterpart for

(compare to (

) with

).

An immediate consequence of the previous lemma is the evaluation of the mollified second moment.

Corollary 9.2
.

We have

\displaystyle\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\,\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\sum_{f\in B_{k}}\omega_{f}L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes\chi_{d}\right)^{2}M_{f}(d)^{4}=\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\,\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\mathbb{E}\left(L(X;d,k)M_{2}(X;d)\right)

\displaystyle+2K(\log K)\widehat{h}(0)\sum_{n\leq K^{4\theta_{J}}}\frac{h_{2}(n)\lambda(n)}{2^{\Omega(n)}\sqrt{n}}\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\,\frac{\varphi(d)}{d}\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)\sum_{u|n}\frac{c_{n}(u)}{\sqrt{u}}\chi_{d}(nu)\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{(\varepsilon)}\frac{e^{2x^{2}}}{x^{2}}\cdot u^{-x}\sum_{v|u}v^{2x}\,\mathrm{d}x+O\left(X^{3/4}K^{1+\varepsilon}\right)

10.
A random computation

Let us define for
\textnormal{Re}(s_{1})>1
and
\textnormal{Re}(s_{2})>1
,

L(s_{1},s_{2},X;d)=\sum_{m_{1}=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m_{2}=1}^{\infty}\frac{X(m_{1})X(m_{2})\chi_{d}(m_{1}m_{2})}{m_{1}^{s_{1}}m_{2}^{s_{2}}}=\prod_{p}\underbrace{\sum_{j_{1}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j_{2}=0}^{\infty}\frac{X(p^{j_{1}})X(p^{j_{2}})\chi_{d}(p^{j_{1}+j_{2}})}{p^{j_{1}s_{1}+j_{2}s_{2}}}}_{=:L_{p}(s_{1},s_{2},X;d)}.

Our next goal is to estimate the contribution of primes of different size.

10.1.
Contribution of the primes with
K^{\theta_{0}}<p\leq K^{\theta_{J}}

Here we wish to estimate the expectation

\mathbb{E}\left(\prod_{K^{\theta_{0}}<p<K^{\theta_{J}}}L_{p}\left(s_{1}+\frac{1}{2},s_{2}+\frac{1}{2};X,d\right)\prod_{j=1}^{J}M_{2,j}(X;d)\right).

This is needed for shifting contours in the proof of Lemma
10.3
. To do this, we consider for every
1\leq j\leq J
the term

(10.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(M_{2,j}(X;d)\prod_{p\in I_{j}}L_{p}\left(s_{1}+\frac{1}{2},s_{2}+\frac{1}{2};X,d\right)\right).

Let us start by expanding

\displaystyle M_{2,j}(X;d)\prod_{p\in I_{j}}L_{p}\left(s_{1}+\frac{1}{2},s_{2}+\frac{1}{2};X,d\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p|m_{1}m_{2}\Rightarrow p\in I_{j}}\frac{\chi_{d}(m_{1}m_{2})}{m_{1}^{s_{1}+1/2}m_{2}^{s_{2}+1/2}}X(m_{1})X(m_{2})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|n\Rightarrow p\in I_{j}\\
\Omega(n)\leq 4\ell_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda(n)\chi_{d}(n)\nu_{2}(n;\ell_{j})}{2^{\Omega(n)}\sqrt{n}}\sum_{u|n}c_{n}(u)X(u).

By taking the expectation and using the Hecke relations for
X(m)
we see that (
10.1
) equals

\displaystyle\sum_{p|m_{1}m_{2}\Rightarrow p\in I_{j}}\frac{\chi_{d}(m_{1}m_{2})}{m_{1}^{s_{1}+1/2}m_{2}^{s_{2}+1/2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|n\Rightarrow p\in I_{j}\\
\Omega(n)\leq 4\ell_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda(n)\chi_{d}(n)\nu_{2}(n;\ell_{j})}{2^{\Omega(n)}\sqrt{n}}\sum_{u|n}c_{n}(u)\sum_{h|(m_{1},m_{2})}1_{m_{1}m_{2}/h^{2}=u}

(10.2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(h,d)=1}\frac{1}{h^{1+s_{1}+s_{2}}}\sum_{p|m_{1}m_{2}\Rightarrow p\in I_{j}}\frac{\chi_{d}(m_{1}m_{2})}{m_{1}^{s_{1}+1/2}m_{2}^{s_{2}+1/2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|n\Rightarrow p\in I_{j}\\
\Omega(n)\leq 4\ell_{j}\\
m_{1}m_{2}|n\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda(n)\chi_{d}(n)\nu_{2}(n;\ell_{j})}{2^{\Omega(n)}\sqrt{n}}c_{n}(m_{1}m_{2}).

For any
r>0
and
n,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,

1_{\Omega(n)=\ell}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{|z|=r}z^{\Omega(n)-\ell}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z},

so that for
r\neq 1
we have

1_{\Omega(n)\leq\ell_{j}}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{|z|=r}z^{\Omega(n)}\frac{1-z^{-\ell_{j}-1}}{1-z^{-1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z}.

Therefore, for
1<r\leq 2
the right-hand side of (
10.1
) is given by

(10.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{|z|=r}\frac{1-z^{-4\ell_{j}-1}}{1-z^{-1}}\Sigma(z)\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z},

where

\Sigma(z):=\sum_{(h,d)=1}\frac{1}{h^{1+s_{1}+s_{2}}}\sum_{p|m_{1}m_{2}\Rightarrow p\in I_{j}}\frac{\chi_{d}(m_{1}m_{2})}{m_{1}^{s_{1}+1/2}m_{2}^{s_{2}+1/2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|n\Rightarrow p\in I_{j}\\
m_{1}m_{2}|n\end{subarray}}\frac{z^{\Omega(n)}\lambda(n)\chi_{d}(n)\nu_{2}(n;\ell_{j})}{2^{\Omega(n)}\sqrt{n}}c_{n}(m_{1}m_{2}).

Now this expression can be written as an Euler product

\displaystyle\Sigma(z)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\\
p\nmid d\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{z}{\sqrt{p}}\left(\frac{1}{p^{s_{1}+1/2}}+\frac{1}{p^{s_{2}+1/2}}\right)+\frac{z^{2}}{2p}\left(\frac{1}{p^{1+2s_{1}}}+\frac{1}{p^{1+2s_{2}}}+\frac{1}{p^{1+s_{1}+s_{2}}}\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{p^{3/2+2\min\{\textnormal{Re}(s_{1}),\textnormal{Re}(s_{2})\}}}\right)\right)

From this it is easily seen that
|\Sigma(z)|\ll 1
uniformly for
|z|\leq 2
when
\min\{\textnormal{Re}(s_{1}),\textnormal{Re}(s_{2})\}>1/2
. Applying this bound in (
10.3
) we see that

\mathbb{E}\left(M_{2,j}(X;d)\prod_{p\in I_{j}}L_{p}\left(s_{1}+\frac{1}{2},s_{2}+\frac{1}{2};X,d\right)\right)\ll 1.

Thus we have that

(10.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\prod_{K^{\theta_{0}}<p<K^{\theta_{J}}}L_{p}\left(s_{1}+\frac{1}{2},s_{2}+\frac{1}{2};X,d\right)\prod_{j=1}^{J}M_{2,j}(X;d)\right)\ll(\log\log K)^{O(1)}

by applying the above bound for each
1\leq j\leq J
and recalling that
J\asymp\log\log\log K
.

10.2.
The contribution of the small primes

Next we need to understand the contribution of the primes with
c\leq p\leq K^{\theta_{0}}
. This involves understanding the interaction between
L(s_{1}+1/2,s_{2}+1/2;X,d)
and
M_{2,0}(X;d)
. A key point is that since
M_{2,0}(X;d)
consists of relatively small primes we can express it in terms of an Euler product with negligible loss since
\ell_{0}
is large. This allows us to simplify our later analysis by reducing the problem to understanding the contribution from each prime
p\in I_{0}
individually. Let

(10.5)

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{2,0}(X;d):=\sum_{p|n\Rightarrow p\in I_{0}}\frac{\lambda(n)\chi_{d}(n)\nu_{4}(n;\ell_{0})}{2^{\Omega(n)}\sqrt{n}}\sum_{u|n}c_{n}(u)X(u).

We have the following result.

Lemma 10.1
.

For
\min\{\textnormal{Re}(s_{1}),\textnormal{Re}(s_{2})\}\geq-(\log\log K)^{2}/\log K
we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(M_{2,0}(X;d)\prod_{p\in I_{0}}L_{p}\left(s_{1}+\frac{1}{2},s_{2}+\frac{1}{2};X,d\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathbb{E}\left(\widetilde{M}_{2,0}(X;d)\prod_{p\in I_{0}}L_{p}\left(s_{1}+\frac{1}{2},s_{2}+\frac{1}{2};X,d\right)\right)+O\left((\log K)^{-10}\right).

Proof.

Let us write

R(X;d):=\widetilde{M}_{2,0}(X;d)-M_{2,0}(X;d)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|n\Rightarrow p\in I_{0}\\
\Omega(n)>4\ell_{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda(n)\chi_{d}(n)\nu_{2}(n;\ell_{j})}{2^{\Omega(n)}\sqrt{n}}\sum_{u|n}c_{n}(u)X(u).

Let us denote
L_{0}(s_{1},s_{2};X,d):=\prod_{p\in I_{0}}L_{p}(s_{1},s_{2};X,d)
. Then by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality we have

\mathbb{E}\left(\left|L_{0}\left(s_{1}+\frac{1}{2},s_{2}+\frac{1}{2};X,d\right)R(X;d)\right|\right)^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\left|L_{0}\left(s_{1}+\frac{1}{2},s_{2}+\frac{1}{2};X,d\right)\right|^{2}\right)\mathbb{E}\left(|R(X;d)|^{2}\right).

Since
L_{0}(s_{1},s_{2};X,d)
is a finite product this function is analytic for
\textnormal{Re}(s_{1}),\,\textnormal{Re}(s_{2})>0
. Let us first analyse
\mathbb{E}(|L_{0}(s_{1}+1/2,s_{2}+1/2;X,d)|^{2})
. We simply compute

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left|L_{0}\left(s_{1}+\frac{1}{2},s_{2}+\frac{1}{2};X,d\right)\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p\in I_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\sum_{j_{1}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j_{2}=0}^{\infty}\frac{X(p^{j_{1}})X(p^{j_{2}})\chi_{d}(p^{j_{1}+j_{2}})}{p^{j_{1}(s_{1}+1/2)+j_{2}(s_{2}+1/2)}}\right|^{2}\right).

Set

\rho(X,p^{\alpha};s_{1},s_{2})):=\chi_{d}(p^{\alpha})\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}+j_{3}+j_{4}=\alpha}\frac{X(p^{j_{1}})X(p^{j_{2}})X(p^{j_{3}})X(p^{j_{4}})}{p^{(j_{1}+j_{2})(s_{1}+1/2)+(j_{3}+j_{4})(s_{2}+1/2)}}.

An easy computation shows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\rho(X,p;s_{1},s_{2})))=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbb{E}(\rho(X,p^{2};s_{1},s_{2}))

\displaystyle=\chi_{d}(p^{2})\left(\frac{1}{p^{1+2s_{1}}}+\frac{1}{p^{1+2s_{2}}}+\frac{4}{p^{1+s_{1}+s_{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\ll p^{-1-2\min\{\textnormal{Re}(s_{1}),\textnormal{Re}(s_{2})\}}.

Thus

\mathbb{E}\left(\left|L_{0}\left(s_{1}+\frac{1}{2},s_{2}+\frac{1}{2};X,d\right)\right|^{2}\right)=\prod_{p\in I_{0}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{p^{1+2\min\{\textnormal{Re}(s_{1}),\textnormal{Re}(s_{2})\}}}\right)\right).

Using that
\min\{\textnormal{Re}(s_{1}),\textnormal{Re}(s_{2})\}\geq-(\log\log K)^{2}/\log K
we have
\frac{1}{p^{1+2\min\{\textnormal{Re}(s_{1}),\textnormal{Re}(s_{2})\}}}\ll\frac{1}{p}
for
p\in I_{0}
and so the right-hand side of the previous display is
\ll(\log K)^{O(1)}
.

We next estimate
\mathbb{E}(|R(X;d)|^{2})
, which equals

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|n_{1}\Rightarrow p\in I_{0}\\
\Omega(n_{1})>4\ell_{0}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|n_{2}\Rightarrow p\in I_{0}\\
\Omega(n_{2})>4\ell_{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda(n_{1})\lambda(n_{2})\chi_{d}(n_{1}n_{2})\nu_{2}(n_{1};\ell_{0})\nu_{2}(n_{2};\ell_{0})}{2^{\Omega(n_{1})+\Omega(n_{2})}\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}}}\sum_{u_{1}|n_{1}}\sum_{u_{2}|n_{2}}c_{n_{1}}(u_{1})c_{n_{2}}(u_{2})\mathbb{E}(X(u_{1})X(u_{2})).

We have
2^{\Omega(n_{1})-4\ell_{0}}\geq 1
for
\Omega(n_{1})>4\ell_{0}
. Thus using the bound
c_{n}(u)\leq 2^{\Omega(n)}
and estimating the Liouville function and
\chi_{d}
trivially, we see that there exists a constant
C>0
so that

\mathbb{E}(|R(X;d)|^{2})\leq\frac{1}{2^{8\ell_{0}}}\sum_{p|r\Rightarrow p\in I_{0}}\frac{C^{\Omega(r)}}{r}\leq\frac{(\log K)^{O(1)}}{2^{4\ell_{0}}},

where we have used the fact that
c
is sufficiently large so that the sum in the middle converges. Combining the two estimates above completes the proof.
∎

10.3.
Estimates for primes
p>K^{\theta_{J}}

It remains to study the contribution of the primes
p>K^{\theta_{J}}
. These do not interact with our mollifier and consequently their contribution is easy to understand. Estimating these terms precisely allows us to meromorphically continue
\mathbb{E}(L(s_{1}+1/2,s_{2}+1/2,X;d)M_{2}(X;d))
as
M_{2}(X;d)
is a Dirichlet polynomial with coefficients supported on integers with prime factors
\leq K^{\theta_{J}}
.

Lemma 10.2
.

For
\textnormal{Re}(s_{1}),\,\textnormal{Re}(s_{2})>1/2
we have that

\mathbb{E}\left(L_{p}\left(s_{1}+\frac{1}{2},s_{2}+\frac{1}{2};X,d\right)\right)=\zeta_{p}(1+s_{1}+s_{2})\cdot 1_{p\nmid d}.

Proof.

We simply compute

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(L_{p}\left(s_{1}+\frac{1}{2},s_{2}+\frac{1}{2};X,d\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k_{2}=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi_{d}(p^{k_{1}+k_{2}})}{p^{k_{1}(s_{1}+1/2)+k_{2}(s_{2}+1/2)}}\mathbb{E}\left(X(p^{k_{1}})X(p^{k_{2}})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi_{d}(p^{2j})}{p^{(1+s_{1}+s_{2})j}}

\displaystyle=1_{p\nmid d}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p^{(1+s_{1}+s_{2})j}}

\displaystyle=1_{p\nmid d}\cdot\frac{1}{1-p^{-(1+s_{1}+s_{2})}}

\displaystyle=1_{p\nmid d}\cdot\zeta_{p}(1+s_{1}+s_{2})

for
\textnormal{Re}(s_{1}),\,\textnormal{Re}(s_{2})>1/2
.
∎

Thus using the dominated convergence theorem we have for
\textnormal{Re}(s_{1}),\,\textnormal{Re}(s_{2})>1/2
and any
z\geq 2
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\prod_{p>z}L\left(s_{1}+\frac{1}{2},s_{2}+\frac{1}{2};X,d\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p>z}\mathbb{E}\left(L\left(s_{1}+\frac{1}{2},s_{2}+\frac{1}{2};X,d\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p>z\\
p\nmid d\end{subarray}}\zeta_{p}(1+s_{1}+s_{2})

\displaystyle=\zeta(1+s_{1}+s_{2})\prod_{p\leq z}\zeta_{p}(1+s_{1}+s_{2})^{-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p>z\\
p|d\end{subarray}}\zeta_{p}(1+s_{1}+s_{2})^{-1}.

If
u
is a natural number with all prime factors
\leq K^{\theta_{J}}
, this provides a meromorphic continuation of
\mathbb{E}(X(u)L(s_{1}+1/2,s_{2}+1/2;X,d)
to
\textnormal{Re}(s_{1}+s_{2})>-1/2
with simple poles at
s_{1}+s_{2}=0
. Indeed, by choosing
z=K^{\theta_{J}}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(X(u)L(s_{1}+1/2,s_{2}+1/2;X,d)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(X(u)\prod_{p\leq K^{\theta_{J}}}L_{p}\left(s_{1}+\frac{1}{2},s_{2}+\frac{1}{2};X,d\right)\right)\mathbb{E}\left(\prod_{p>K^{\theta_{J}}}L_{p}\left(s_{1}+\frac{1}{2},s_{2}+\frac{1}{2};X,d\right)\right)

(10.6)

\displaystyle=\zeta(1+s_{1}+s_{2})\prod_{p\leq K^{\theta_{J}}}\zeta_{p}(1+s_{1}+s_{2})^{-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p>K^{\theta_{J}}\\
p|d\end{subarray}}\zeta_{p}(1+s_{1}+s_{2})^{-1}\mathbb{E}\left(X(u)\prod_{p\leq K^{\theta_{J}}}L_{p}\left(s_{1}+\frac{1}{2},s_{2}+\frac{1}{2};X,d\right)\right).

We further compute

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(X(u)\prod_{p\leq K^{\theta_{J}}}L_{p}\left(s_{1}+\frac{1}{2},s_{2}+\frac{1}{2};X,d\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(X(u)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},m_{2}\\
p|m_{1}m_{2}\Rightarrow p\leq K^{\theta_{J}}\end{subarray}}\frac{X(m_{1})X(m_{2})\chi_{d}(m_{1}m_{2})}{m_{1}^{s_{1}+1/2}m_{2}^{s_{2}+1/2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},m_{2}\\
p|m_{1}m_{2}\Rightarrow p\leq K^{\theta_{J}}\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi_{d}(m_{1}m_{2})}{m_{1}^{s_{1}+1/2}m_{2}^{s_{2}+1/2}}\sum_{h|(m_{1},m_{2})}1_{u=m_{1}m_{2}/h^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(h,d)=1\\
p|h\Rightarrow p\leq K^{\theta_{J}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{h^{1+s_{1}+s_{2}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},m_{2}\\
p|m_{1}m_{2}\Rightarrow p\leq K^{\theta_{J}}\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi_{d}(m_{1}m_{2})}{m_{1}^{s_{1}+1/2}m_{2}^{s_{2}+1/2}}\cdot 1_{u=m_{1}m_{2}}

(10.7)

\displaystyle=\frac{\chi_{d}(u)}{u^{1/2+s_{2}}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq K^{\theta_{J}}\\
p\nmid d\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{1+s_{1}+s_{2}}}\right)^{-1}\sum_{v|u}\frac{1}{v^{s_{1}-s_{2}}}.

Our next goal is to prove the following.

Lemma 10.3
.

We have

\displaystyle\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\,\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\mathbb{E}\left(L(X;d,k)M_{2}(X;d)\right)

\displaystyle=2K(\log K)\widehat{h}(0)\sum_{n\leq K^{4\theta_{J}}}\frac{\widetilde{h}_{2}(n)\lambda(n)}{2^{\Omega(n)}\sqrt{n}}\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\,\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)\frac{\varphi(d)}{d}\sum_{u|n}\frac{c_{n}(u)d(u)}{\sqrt{u}}\chi_{d}(nu)\left(\log\left(\frac{Kd}{2\pi\sqrt{u}}+O(1)\right)\right)

\displaystyle-2K(\log K)\widehat{h}(0)\sum_{n\leq K^{4\theta_{J}}}\frac{h_{2}(n)\lambda(n)}{2^{\Omega(n)}\sqrt{n}}\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{d}\,\phi\left(\frac{|d|}{X}\right)\frac{\varphi(d)}{d}\sum_{u|n}\frac{c_{n}(u)}{\sqrt{u}}\chi_{d}(nu)\sum_{v|u}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{(\varepsilon)}e^{2s_{1}^{2}}u^{-s_{1}}v^{2s_{1}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}s_{1}}{s_{1}^{2}}+O\left(KX(\log K)^{-7}\right).

Proof.

on average over the forms
g\in\mathcal{S}_{K}
. Applying Hölder’s inequality as in the proof of
[
13
, Proposition 2.6.]
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\sum_{g\in B_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}}\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{(-1)^{k}d\sim X}\alpha_{g}^{1/2}\omega_{g}^{1/2}|c_{g}(|d|)|M_{g}(d)\geq C_{4}\cdot XK.

On the other hand, by Proposition
4.1
and Waldspurger’s formula we also have

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\sum_{g\in B_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}}\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(-1)^{k}d\sim X\\
M_{g}(d)^{2}L(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{d})>C_{5}\end{subarray}}\alpha_{g}^{1/2}\omega_{g}^{1/2}|c_{g}(|d|)|M_{g}(d)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{C_{5}}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\sum_{g\in B_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}}\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(-1)^{k}d\sim X\\
M_{g}(d)^{2}L(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{d})>C_{5}\end{subarray}}\alpha_{g}|c_{g}(|d|)|^{2}M_{g}(d)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{3}}{\sqrt{C_{5}}}\cdot XK.

From this we infer the lower bound

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\sum_{g\in B_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}}\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(-1)^{k}d\sim X\\
M_{g}(d)^{2}L(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{d})\leq C_{5}\end{subarray}}\alpha_{g}^{1/2}\omega_{g}^{1/2}|c_{g}(|d|)|M_{g}(d)\geq C_{6}KX.

Let us now define the set

\mathcal{V}_{g}:=\left\{x\sim X:\,\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq(-1)^{k}d\leq x+H\\
M_{g}(d)^{2}L(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{d})\leq C_{5}\end{subarray}}\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}|c_{g}(|d|)|M_{g}(d)\geq\frac{C_{7}H}{k^{1/2}}\right\}.

From the work above it follows that

\displaystyle C_{6}KX

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\sum_{g\in B_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}}\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(-1)^{k}d\sim X\\
M_{g}(d)^{2}L(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{d})\leq C_{5}\end{subarray}}\alpha_{g}^{1/2}\omega_{g}^{1/2}|c_{g}(|d|)|M_{g}(d)

\displaystyle\leq C_{8}\cdot\frac{1}{H}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\sum_{g\in B_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}}\sum_{x\sim X}\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq(-1)^{k}d\leq x+H\\
M_{g}(d)^{2}L(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{d})\leq C_{5}\end{subarray}}\alpha_{g}^{1/2}\omega_{g}^{1/2}|c_{g}(|d|)|M_{g}(d)

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{8}}{H}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\sum_{g\in B_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}}\left(\sum_{x\in\mathcal{V}_{g}}+\sum_{x\not\in\mathcal{V}_{g}}\right)\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq(-1)^{k}d\leq x+H\\
M_{g}(d)^{2}L(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{d})\leq C_{5}\end{subarray}}\alpha_{g}^{1/2}\omega_{g}^{1/2}|c_{g}(|d|)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{8}}{H}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\sum_{g\in B_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}}\left(C_{5}\omega_{g}H\left|\mathcal{V}_{g}\right|+\omega_{g}^{1/2}\frac{C_{7}XH}{k^{1/2}}\right),

where we have used the relation
\omega_{g}^{1/2}\alpha_{g}^{1/2}|c_{g}(|d|)|=\omega_{g}\sqrt{L(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{d})}
in the last estimate. Using an easy estimate
\sum_{g\in B_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}}\omega_{g}^{1/2}\leq C_{9}\sqrt{k}
(which follows from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the fact that
\sum_{g\in B_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}}\omega_{g}\sim 1
) we conclude that

(12.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\sum_{g\in B_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}}\omega_{g}\left|\mathcal{V}_{g}\right|\geq C_{10}KX.

The harmonic weights
\omega_{g}
can be removed without affecting the lower bound
[
1
,
12
]
and so we deduce that

(12.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}h\left(\frac{2k-1}{K}\right)\sum_{g\in B_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}}|\mathcal{V}_{g}|\geq C_{10}K^{2}X.

Let us introduce the set

\mathcal{U}:=\left\{g\in\mathcal{S}_{K}:\,|\mathcal{V}_{g}|>C_{11}X\right\}.

Now from (
12.4
) we deduce that

\displaystyle C_{10}XK^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{g\in\mathcal{U}}\left|\mathcal{V}_{g}\right|+\sum_{g\in\mathcal{S}_{K}\setminus\mathcal{U}}\left|\mathcal{V}_{g}\right|

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{U}|X+C_{12}K^{2}X.

from which we infer the lower bound

|\mathcal{U}|\geq(C_{10}-C_{12})K^{2}

by recalling that
C_{12}=4C_{11}
and
C_{11}
is chosen so that
C_{12}<C_{10}
.

Hence we have shown that for
\geq(C_{10}-C_{12})K^{2}
of the forms
g\in\mathcal{S}_{K}
we have

\displaystyle\#\left\{x\sim X\,:\,\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{x\leq(-1)^{k}d\leq x+H}\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}|c_{g}(|d|)|M_{g}(d)\geq\frac{C_{7}H}{k^{1/2}}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\#\left\{x\sim X\,:\,\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq(-1)^{k}d\leq x+H\\
M_{g}(d)^{2}L(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{d})\leq C_{5}\end{subarray}}\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}|c_{g}(|d|)|M_{g}(d)\geq\frac{C_{7}H}{k^{1/2}}\right\}

\displaystyle>C_{11}X.

In conclusion we have shown that for all but
\leq 2C_{3}\cdot K^{2}/C_{2}C_{1}^{2}
of the forms
g\in S_{K}
it holds that

\left|\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{x\leq(-1)^{k}d\leq x+H}\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}c_{g}(|d|)M_{g}(d)\right|<\frac{C_{1}\sqrt{H}}{\sqrt{k}}

for almost all
x\sim X
with the exceptional set having size
\leq(1-C_{2})X
. Moreover, for at least
(C_{10}-C_{12})K^{2}
of the forms
g\in S_{K}
it holds that

\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{x\leq(-1)^{k}d\leq x+H}\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}|c_{g}(|d|)|M_{g}(d)\geq\frac{C_{7}H}{\sqrt{k}}

for
\geq C_{11}X
of the numbers
x\sim X
. By recalling that our choices of constants are so that
C_{11}>1-C_{2}
and
2C_{3}/C_{2}C_{1}^{2}<C_{10}
it follows that for
\geq(C_{10}-C_{12}-2C_{3}/C_{2}C_{1}^{2})K^{2}
of the forms
g\in S_{K}
the chain of inequalities

(12.5)

\displaystyle\left|\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{x\leq(-1)^{k}d\leq x+H}\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}c_{g}(|d|)M_{g}(d)\right|<\frac{C_{1}\cdot\sqrt{H}}{\sqrt{k}}<\frac{C_{7}\cdot H}{\sqrt{k}}\leq\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{x\leq(-1)^{k}d\leq x+H}\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}|c_{g}(|d|)|M_{g}(d)

holds for
\geq(C_{11}-1-C_{2})X
of the numbers
x\sim X
if we choose
H>(C_{1}/C_{7})^{2}
.

Choose
H
to be large enough (
>(C_{1}/C_{7})^{2}
), but fixed. With this choice it follows easily from (
12.5
) that for
\geq(C_{10}-C_{12}-2C_{3}/C_{2}C_{1}^{2})K^{2}
of the forms
g\in\mathcal{S}_{K}
we have that

\left|\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{x\leq(-1)^{k}d\leq x+H}\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}|c_{g}(|d|)|M_{g}(d)\,\pm\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{x\leq(-1)^{k}d\leq x+H}\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}c_{g}(|d|)M_{g}(d)\right|\geq\frac{(C_{7}H-C_{1}\sqrt{H})}{\sqrt{k}}>0

holds for
\geq(C_{11}-1-C_{2})X
of the numbers
x\sim X
. We note that the contribution coming from the summands with
\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}|c_{g}(|d|)|M_{g}(d)\leq k^{-\delta}
is trivially bounded by, say,

\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq(-1)^{k}d\leq x+H\\
\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}|c_{g}(|d|)|M_{g}(d)\leq k^{-\delta}\end{subarray}}\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}|c_{g}(|d|)|M_{g}(d)\leq 2HK^{-\delta}

as
\eta_{2}
is sufficiently small, and so we conclude, choosing
\delta=1/2+\eta/2
for concreteness so that
\delta>1/2
, that for the same proportion of
g\in\mathcal{S}_{K}
and
x\sim X
we have

\left|\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq(-1)^{k}d\leq x+H\\
\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}|c_{g}(|d|)|M_{g}(d)>k^{-\delta}\end{subarray}}\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}|c_{g}(|d|)|M_{g}(d)\,\pm\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq(-1)^{k}d\leq x+H\\
\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}|c_{g}(|d|)|M_{g}(d)>k^{-\delta}\end{subarray}}\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}c_{g}(|d|)M_{g}(d)\right|\geq\frac{(C_{7}H-C_{1}\sqrt{H})}{\sqrt{k}}>0.

Now observe that this implies

\displaystyle 2\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq(-1)^{k}d\leq x+H\\
\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}c_{g}(|d|)M_{g}(d)>k^{-\delta}\end{subarray}}\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}|c_{g}(|d|)|M_{g}(d)

\displaystyle=\left|\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq(-1)^{k}d\leq x+H\\
\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}|c_{g}(|d|)|M_{g}(d)>k^{-\delta}\end{subarray}}\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}|c_{g}(|d|)|M_{g}(d)\,+\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq(-1)^{k}d\leq x+H\\
\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}|c_{g}(|d|)|M_{g}(d)>k^{-\delta}\end{subarray}}\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}c_{g}(|d|)M_{g}(d)\right|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(C_{7}H-C_{1}\sqrt{H})}{\sqrt{k}}>0.

Similarly,

\displaystyle 2\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq(-1)^{k}d\leq x+H\\
\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}c_{g}(|d|)M_{g}(d)<-k^{-\delta}\end{subarray}}\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}|c_{g}(|d|)|M_{g}(d)

\displaystyle=\left|\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq(-1)^{k}d\leq x+H\\
\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}|c_{g}(|d|)|M_{g}(d)>k^{-\delta}\end{subarray}}\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}|c_{g}(|d|)|M_{g}(d)\,-\mathop{{\sum}^{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\flat$}}}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq(-1)^{k}d\leq x+H\\
\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}|c_{g}(|d|)|M_{g}(d)>k^{-\delta}\end{subarray}}\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}c_{g}(|d|)M_{g}(d)\right|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(C_{7}H-C_{1}\sqrt{H})}{\sqrt{k}}>0.

Thus we have shown that for
\geq(C_{10}-C_{12}-2C_{3}/C_{2}C_{1}^{2})K^{2}
of the forms
g\in\mathcal{S}_{K}
the short interval
[x,x+H]
,
x\sim X
, contains numbers
(-1)^{k}d_{\pm}
, with both
d_{\pm}
odd fundamental discriminants, for which
\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}c_{g}(|d_{+}|)M_{g}(d_{+})>k^{-\delta}
and
\sqrt{\alpha_{g}}c_{g}(|d_{-}|)M_{g}(d_{-})<-k^{-\delta}
, for
\geq(C_{11}-1-C_{2})X
of the numbers
x\sim X
as
M_{g}(d)\ll(\log K)^{O(1)}
. Thus we deduce a sign change of
c_{g}(|d|)
over a positive proportion of intervals of constant size
>(C_{1}/C_{7})^{2}
for a positive proportion of forms
g\in S_{K}
. This leads to

\geq\frac{(C_{11}-1-C_{2})X}{H}\asymp\frac{K}{Y}

real zeroes on both of the line segments
\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
, concluding the proof. ∎

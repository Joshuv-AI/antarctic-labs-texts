A mitochondrial-based prognostic score for risk stratification in acute myeloid leukemia
Authors: Isabelle C. Santos, Vitória J. Silva-Xavier, Amanda Moreira-Aguiar, Marcio T. Reis-Silva, Lidiane P. Silva, Pedro L. França-Neto, Marcos A. Bezerra, João A. Machado-Neto, Diego A. Pereira-Martins, Antonio R. Lucena-Araujo, Juan L. Coelho-Silva
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

Acute myeloid leukemia (AML) is a heterogeneous disease in which the prognosis is influenced by a diverse spectrum of genetic and molecular alterations [1]. Although the current classification systems are informative, they only partially reflect the complexity of the disease, underscoring the need for complementary prognostic and/or stratification tools [2, 3]. As central regulators of energy production, biosynthesis, and apoptosis, mitochondria depend on tightly coordinated interactions between mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) and nuclear-encoded proteins to ensure proper functioning. Disruption of this coordination can drive metabolic reprogramming and modulate cellular responses to therapies [4]. Emerging evidence indicates that variations in mtDNA content influence AML outcomes, implicating mitochondrial genome activity as a critical determinant of leukemogenesis and disease progression [5, 6]. Based on this evidence, we developed a prognostic score, hereafter referred to as the mitochondrial risk score (Mito-RS), derived from the expression of mitochondrial genome modulators.

Initially, we analyzed four independent cohorts, including one for training (The Cancer Genome Atlas [TCGA] [7], n = 121) and three for validation (BeatAML [8], n = 193; NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) datasets GSE6891 [9], n = 395; and GSE71014 [10], n = 104). From the MitoCarta3.0 database, 290 genes involved in mitochondrial genome expression were identified, of which 12 were selected through Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression applied to RNA-seq data from the training cohort (Fig. 1A). Based on this initial analysis, we derived the Mito-RS for each patient by weighting gene expression levels according to their respective LASSO coefficients, as follows: Mito-RS = (AFG1L * -0.17249) + (THG1L * -0.13469) + (PARL * -0.05757) + (ATP23 * -0.01098) + (NSUN4 * 0.004457) + (LAP3 * 0.008704) + (PRORP * 0.03584) + (TOP1MT * 0.040688) + (MUL1 * 0.045959) + (MTFMT * 0.061669) + (MRPL21 * 0.069616) + (PITRM1 * 0.091193). The score was subsequently dichotomized into “high” and “low” Mito-RS groups using the cutpointr function [11] based on maximization of classification metrics. Overall survival (OS) was used as the reference outcome. Further methodological details, as well as clinical and molecular baseline characteristics, can be found in the supplemental data (Supplemental Tables 1, 2, and 3). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis demonstrated an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.661 for the continuous score and 0.654 for the categorical version, supporting its ability to discriminate between risk groups (Fig. 1B and C). Importantly, relative to the MitoScore proposed by Niu et al. Survival analysis showed that patients with high Mito-RS had a significantly lower 3-year OS (19%, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 10–32%) than patients with low Mito-RS (55%, 95% CI: 43–67%) (P < 0.0001) (Fig. 1D). In univariable analysis, patients with a high Mito-RS had three-fold higher risk of presenting poor OS than patients with low Mito-RS (hazard ratio, HR: 3.06, 95% CI: 1.92–4.88; P < 0.001) (Supplemental Fig. 1A). This result was consistent with the multivariate proportional hazards analysis adjusting for age, sex, white blood cell (WBC) counts, and ELN2022 risk stratification as confounders (hazard ratio [HR]: 2.63, 95% CI: 1.60–4.32; P < 0.001) (Fig. 1E;). Notably, although the adverse prognostic impact of high Mito-RS was consistently observed in univariable analyses across validation cohorts (BeatAML [HR: 2.33; 95% CI: 1.48–3.67; P < 0.001; Fig. 1F; Supplemental Fig. 1B], GSE6891 cohort [HR: 1.48; 95% CI: 1.15–1.91; P = 0.003; Fig. 1H; Supplemental Fig. 1C], and in GSE71014 [HR: 3.07; 95% CI: 1.93–4.88; P < 0.001; Supplemental Fig. 2]), independent significance after multivariable adjustment was retained only in BeatAML (HR: 2.32; 95% CI: 1.44–3.74; P < 0.001; Fig. 1G). Considering the importance of the ELN2022 for risk stratification [13], we opted to evaluate the impact of Mito-RS separately in patients assigned to the favorable-, intermediate-, and adverse-risk groups. Patients assigned to the favorable-risk group (51 patients, 42%) with high Mito-RS had a poor 3-year OS rate (23%, 95% CI: 6–47) compared to those with low Mito-RS (47%, 95% CI: 27–63) (P = 0.0001) (Supplemental Fig. 3A and B). However, Mito-RS had no impact on OS for intermediate-risk (31 patients; 26%; HR: 2.36, 95% CI: 0.97–5.73; P = 0.06; Supplemental Fig. 3C) and adverse-risk patients (37 patients; 32%; HR: 2.1, 95% CI: 0.93–4.73; P = 0.072; Supplemental Fig. 3D). The corresponding picture for the validation cohort is summarized in Supplemental Table 4.

Exploratory subgroup analyses in TCGA showed that favorable-risk patients with high Mito-RS were enriched for NPM1 and DNMT3A mutations (Supplemental Table 7), whereas DNMT3A mutations were less frequent in high-Mito-RS cases in the intermediate-risk group (Supplemental Table 8). In the adverse risk setting, high Mito-RS was consistently associated with TP53-mutated AML in both TCGA and BeatAML, and in BeatAML, it was additionally associated with lower leukocyte counts (Supplemental Tables 9–12). These findings suggest that Mito-RS may capture a layer of biological heterogeneity that is not fully encompassed by genetics-based risk classifications alone. In this context, the modest improvement in discriminative performance over ELN2022 should be interpreted not merely as a numerical gain in AUC, but also as evidence that mitochondrial transcriptional programs may refine prognostic assessment within clinically relevant subgroups, particularly among patients otherwise classified as having favorable risk. Therefore, our data indicate that Mito-RS should not replace ELN2022, but rather complement it as a biology-based tool to further refine risk assessment. Nevertheless, given the limited size of some subgroup analyses and the incomplete annotation of public cohorts, these observations should be interpreted cautiously and warrant further validation in larger, comprehensive, and uniformly annotated datasets.

To further understand the molecular mechanisms associated with Mito-RS groups, we conducted differential gene expression analysis and identified differentially expressed genes (DEGs), as follows: 56 upregulated and 83 downregulated in the TCGA cohort; 141 upregulated and 154 downregulated in the BeatAML cohort (Fig. 2B and C). Unsupervised hierarchical clustering of transcriptomic profiles effectively separated patients with high and low Mito-RS, highlighting distinct DEGs signatures. Gene Ontology analysis revealed that high Mito-RS was positively enriched in pathways related to mitochondrial genome expression, including nucleic acid and RNA processing, ribosome biogenesis, and transcriptional regulation (Fig. 2E and 2F). Conversely, downregulation was observed in enzymatic and lysosomal pathways, including kinase, catalytic, and transport activities. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) confirmed these findings, showing consistent negative enrichment of metabolic and structural pathways, such as fatty-acid metabolism, peroxisome function, mitotic spindle, and monocyte signatures (Fig. 2G; Supplemental Fig. 4). Moreover, patients with high Mito-RS exhibited positive enrichment of RNA metabolism pathways, transcriptional and nucleic acid processing, and gene signatures associated with increased leukemia stem cell (LSC) immaturity (Fig. 2G). Cellular deconvolution suggested that high Mito-RS was associated with inferred higher proportions of quiescent LSC-like states in both cohorts and primed LSC-like states in BeatAML (Fig. 2H; Supplemental Table 5), whereas patients with low Mito-RS showed inferred enrichment of monocytes and active cycling of LSC-like states (Fig. 2G; Supplemental Table 5).

In summary, our findings indicate that patients with high Mito-RS not only exhibit shorter overall survival but also display a transcriptional program consistent with immature, quiescent LSCs, marked by reduced proliferative activity and enrichment for mutations recognized as adverse prognostic markers [13]. Of special interest, TP53 mutations were strongly associated with high Mito-RS in both the TCGA and BeatAML cohorts (Supplemental Tables 1 and 2). These results suggest that patients with high Mito-RS levels exhibit phenotypic features compatible with a primitive stem-like leukemic state characterized by the upregulation of pathways involved in cellular maintenance and selective protein synthesis. Such a quiescent state has been previously associated with chemoresistance and may contribute to the risk of relapse [14]. Thus, beyond serving as an unfavorable prognostic marker, high Mito-RS may reflect leukemia biology associated with therapeutic resistance and disease recurrence.

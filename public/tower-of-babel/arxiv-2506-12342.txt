Extreme values of derivatives of the Dedekind zeta function of a cyclotomic field Corresponding Author
Zhonghua Li

Abstract
In this paper, we establish a lower bound for the maximum of derivatives of the Dedekind zeta function of a cyclotomic field on the critical line. Employing a double version convolution formula and combing special GCD sums, our result generalizes the work of Bondarenko et al. and Fonga in 2023. We also set a lower bound by the resonance method when the real part is near the critical line, both of the above results refine part of Yang’s work in 2022.



Keywords.
The Dedekind zeta function, Extreme values, GCD sums, Resonance method.

Mathematics Subject Classification(2020).
11M06, 11M26, 11N37.

Introduction

Extreme values have always been important objects in analytic number theory, as they play a significant role in the studies of the Riemann zeta function and Dirichlet
L
-functions. Extreme values are used to investigate the value distribution theory of them. Soundararajan proposed an effective method in
[
22
]
, named the resonance method, for studying extreme values of many kinds of families of Dirichlet
L
-functions. Applying this approach to the Riemann zeta function can yield

\max_{T\leqslant t\leqslant 2T}\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|\geqslant\exp\left((1+o(1))\sqrt{\frac{\log T}{\log\log T}}\right).

Subsequently, Aistleitner linked it with GCD sums and improved Soundararajan’s method in
[
1
]
. de la Bretèche and Tenenbaum
[
11
]
advanced the above result by using the double-version convolution formula and they got

\max_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|\geqslant\exp\left(\left(\sqrt{2}+o(1)\right)\sqrt{\frac{\log T\log\log\log T}{\log\log T}}\right).

Yang
[
24
]
integrated their ideas to explore extreme values of derivatives of the Riemann zeta function.
Let
\mathbb{N}
denote the set of all non-negative integers. Borrowing the sets
\mathcal{M}
constructed by de la Bretèche and Tenenbaum
[
11
]
and Aistleitner et al.
[
2
]
, he related GCD sums and obtained the following result for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
:

\max_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\zeta^{(\ell)}\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|\geqslant\exp\left\{\sqrt{2}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{\log T\log\log\log T}{\log\log T}}\right\}

and

\max_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\zeta^{(\ell)}(\sigma+it)\right|\geqslant\exp\left\{\frac{c}{1-\sigma}\cdot\frac{(\log T)^{1-\sigma}}{\left(\log\log T\right)^{\sigma}}\right\},

where
\sigma\in(1/2,1)
and
c
is an absolute positive constant.

One of the main motivations for studying the Dedekind zeta function attached to a cyclotomic field
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}\left(\zeta_{d}\right)
with
d\geqslant 3
is the following classical factorization

\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}(s)=\zeta(s)\prod_{\chi\neq\chi_{0}(\text{mod }d)}L(s,\chi^{\ast}),

(1.1)

where the product runs over all non-principal characters
\chi
modulo
d
, and
\chi^{\ast}
is the character that induces
\chi
if
\chi
is not primitive and
\chi^{\ast}=\chi
otherwise. Many prominent Dirichlet
L
-functions cannot yield similar results via the above techniques.

By studying the Dedekind zeta function, better extreme values of Dirichlet
L
-functions may be obtained. For a general field
\mathbb{K}
, Li
[
20
]
showed that there exist arbitrarity large
t
such that

\left|\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|\geqslant\exp\left(c\sqrt{\frac{\log t\log\log\log t}{\log\log t}}\right),

the magnitude of
c
is related to whether
\mathbb{K}/\mathbb{Q}
is a Galois extension.

Subsequently, Bondarenko et al.
[
4
]
optimized Li’s work and set the following lower bound

\max_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|\geqslant\exp\left((1+o(1))\sqrt{\phi(d)}\sqrt{\frac{\log T\log\log\log T}{\log\log T}}\right)

uniformly for
d\ll(\log\log T)^{A}
with an arbitrary positive number
A.
Here,
\phi(n)
is the Euler’s totient function. This conclusion reveals the possibility of obtaining better
\Omega
-results for
the Riemann zeta function and Dirichlet
L
-functions. By the way, the best known bound for Dirichlet
L
-functions

\max_{T\leqslant t\leqslant 2T}\left|L\left(\frac{1}{2}+it,\chi\right)\right|\geqslant\exp\left((1+o(1))\sqrt{\frac{\log T}{\log\log T}}\right)

was established by Soundararajan in
[
22
]
currently. Utilizing GCD sums, Fonga
[
14
]
improved their result by a factor of
\sqrt{\phi(d)}
in the exponent.

Suppose that
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{N}
is a finite set, and let
\sigma\in(0,1].
Define GCD sums
S_{\sigma}(\mathcal{M})
as

S_{\sigma}(\mathcal{M}):=\sum_{m,n\in\mathcal{M}}\frac{(m,n)^{\sigma}}{[m,n]^{\sigma}},

where
[m,n]
and
(m,n)
are the least common multiple and the greatest common divisor of
m
and
n
respectively. In
[
15
]
, Gál studied
S_{1}(\mathcal{M})
and derived its bounds as follows

(\log\log N)^{2}\ll\sup_{|\mathcal{M}|=N}\frac{S_{1}(\mathcal{M})}{|\mathcal{M}|}\ll(\log\log N)^{2}.

Later, de la Bretèche and Tenenbaum investigated
S_{1/2}(\mathcal{M})
in
[
11
]
and obtained

\sup_{|\mathcal{M}|=N}\frac{S_{1/2}(\mathcal{M})}{|\mathcal{M}|}=\exp\left\{\left(2\sqrt{2}+o(1)\right)\sqrt{\frac{\log N\log\log\log N}{\log\log N}}\right\}.

For
1/2<\sigma<1,
Aistleitner, Berkes, and Seip
[
2
]
proved that

\exp\left\{c_{\sigma}\cdot\frac{(\log N)^{1-\sigma}}{(\log\log N)^{\sigma}}\right\}\ll\sup_{|\mathcal{M}|=N}\frac{S_{\sigma}(\mathcal{M})}{|\mathcal{M}|}\ll\exp\left\{C_{\sigma}\cdot\frac{(\log N)^{1-\sigma}}{(\log\log N)^{\sigma}}\right\},

where
c_{\sigma}
and
C_{\sigma}
are positive constants only depending on
\sigma.
They obtained the above bounds by utilizing the trigonometric polynomials on infinite-dimensional polydisc
\mathbb{D}^{\infty}
. Notably, their result is almost optimal.

Given
\ell\in(0,+\infty)
and
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
in the context of GCD sums, Yang
[
25
]
studied the log-type GCD sums
S_{\sigma}^{(l)}(\mathcal{M})
defined as

S_{\sigma}^{(\ell)}(\mathcal{M}):=\sup_{|\mathcal{M}|=N}\sum_{m,n\in\mathcal{M}}\frac{(m,n)^{\sigma}}{[m,n]^{\sigma}}\log^{\ell}\left(\frac{m}{(m,n)}\right)\log^{\ell}\left(\frac{n}{(m,n)}\right).

He demonstrated that when
\sigma\in[1/2,1),
the influence of logarithmic terms on log-type GCD sums is negligible, but the case of
\sigma=1
is more complicated. GCD sums are powerful tools for studying extreme values of functions related to the Riemann zeta function. Many studies using GCD sums, including Bondarenko and Seip
[
7
]
, de la Bretèche and Tenenbaum
[
11
]
, and Yang
[
24
]
, have obtained good results.
For more results and details about GCD sums we recommend
[
5
,
6
,
9
,
13
,
19
]
and the references therein.

Motivated by
[
4
,
14
,
24
]
, in this paper, we study extreme values of derivatives of the Dedekind zeta function
\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}(s)
in the critical strip, where
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}\left(\zeta_{d}\right)
is a cyclotomic field. Recalling the definition of
\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}(s)
we have

\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{\mathbb{K}}(n)}{n^{s}},

where
{\rm Re}(s)>1
and
a_{\mathbb{K}}(n)
is a multiplicative function.
For
p\equiv 1\pmod{d},
we have
a_{\mathbb{K}}(p)=\phi(d).
If not,
a_{\mathbb{K}}(p)=0.

Following a similar rationale as in Yang
[
24
, p.4]
, we define

\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{K},\ell}(s):=1+\frac{a_{\mathbb{K}}(2)}{2^{s}}+(-1)^{\ell}\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}^{(\ell)}(s)

in this paper. For the above definition, we can express it as the following Dirichlet series when
{\rm Re}(s)>1

\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{K},\ell}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{\mathbb{K},\ell}(n)}{n^{s}},

(1.2)

which is absolutely convergent.
Therefore,
a_{\mathbb{K},\ell}(1)=1,

a_{\mathbb{K},\ell}(2)=a_{\mathbb{K}}(2)+a_{\mathbb{K}}(2)\left(\log 2\right)^{\ell},
and for
n\geqslant 3,a_{\mathbb{K},\ell}(n)=a_{\mathbb{K}}(n)\left(\log n\right)^{\ell}.
The term
1+a_{\mathbb{K}}(2)2^{-s}
added in the above definition ensures that the inequality
a_{\mathbb{K},\ell}(n)\geqslant a_{\mathbb{K}}(n)
holds for all
n\geqslant 1.
Moreover, we have the trivial upper bound as follows

\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{K},\ell}(s)\ll 1+\left|\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}^{(\ell)}(s)\right|.

(1.3)

Our first theorem is the lower bound for extreme values of derivatives of the Dedekind zeta function on the critical line.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}\left(\zeta_{d}\right)
with
d\geqslant 3.
Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
A
be an arbitrary positive number. If
T
is sufficiently large, then uniformly for
d\ll(\log\log T)^{A},
we have

\max_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}^{(\ell)}\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|\geqslant\exp\left((1+o(1))\phi(d)\sqrt{\frac{\log T\log\log\log T}{\log\log T}}\right).

(1.4)

Theorem
1.1
refines certain results of
[
4
,
14
,
24
]
. In comparison with
[
14
]
, we derive a lower bound for the maximum of derivatives of the Dedekind zeta function on the critical line. When
\ell=0,
it reduces to the case of
\beta=0
in
[
14
, Theorem 1]
. This indicates that the lower bound of the maximum value of derivatives of
\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}(s)
on the critical line can reach the same order as that of the original Dedekind zeta function. Comparing with
[
4
]
, besides the above refinement, we also enhance the factor
\sqrt{\phi(d)}
in the exponent on the right-hand side of
[
4
, Eq. (2) ]
. Contrasting with
[
24
]
, for the Dedekind zeta function, we obtain a result that generalizes the Riemann zeta function. This result corresponds to the case of
\beta=0
in
[
24
, Theorem 2.(A)]
. It should be noted that when
d=1,2,
then
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}
is the rational number field with
\phi(d)=1,
Theorem
1.1
is weaker than
[
24
, Theorem 2]
. Therefore, we focus on the cases where
d\geqslant 3,
that is, when
\mathbb{K}
is not the trivial rational number field
\mathbb{Q}.

When the real part
\sigma
is near the critical line, we have Theorem
1.2
below.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}\left(\zeta_{d}\right)
with
d\geqslant 3.
Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
A
be an arbitrary positive number. If
T
is sufficiently large in the range

\frac{1}{2}\leqslant\sigma\leqslant\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\log\log T},

(1.5)

then uniformly for
d\ll(\log\log T)^{A},
we have

\max_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}^{(\ell)}(\sigma+it)\right|\geqslant\exp\left((1+o(1))\sqrt{\frac{\phi(d)}{e-1}}\frac{\left(\log T\right)^{1-\sigma}\left(\log\log\log T\right)^{\sigma}}{\left(\log\log T\right)^{\sigma}}\right).

(1.6)

Inspired by
[
4
,
7
,
10
]
, we prove Theorem
1.2
using the refined resonance method. Different from Theorem
1.1
, we will not make use of the results regarding GCD sums. Theorem
1.2
states the lower bound of the maximum of derivatives of the Dedekind zeta function when the real part
\sigma
is close to the line
\sigma=1/2.

Comparing with
[
4
, Theorem 1]
, our coefficient becomes smaller because the series
\sum p^{-2\sigma}
can only obtain an upper bound similar to
[
10
, Lemma 9]
. We can also apply the same method as that for proving Theorem
1.2
to study extreme values of derivatives of the Dedekind zeta function at the case of
\sigma=1/2,
but comparing with Theorem
1.1
, the coefficient in the exponent here is much smaller.

To obtain Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, we rely on the resonance method. This approach was originally proposed by Voronin
[
23
]
, but his work did not receive much attention. In 2008, Soundararajan
[
22
]
improved the resonance method to study extreme values of the Riemann zeta function and Dirichlet
L
-functions. Subsequently, Aistleitner
[
1
]
further optimized Soundararajan’s resonance method and improved the results of
[
23
]
and
[
17
]
. The resonance method we employ is similar to that refined by Bondarenko and Seip
[
7
,
8
]
, which is following ideas of
[
1
]
, and the slightly modified approach by Chirre
[
10
]
.

To simplify the writing, we adopt the following short-hand notations in this paper, such as
\log_{2}x\operatorname{:}=\log{\log{x}},\,\log_{3}x\operatorname{:}=\log{\log{\log x}}
and
a:=a_{\mathbb{K}}.
Moreover, we let
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}\left(\zeta_{d}\right)
be a cyclotomic field with
d\geqslant 3.
Let
A
be an arbitrarily large positive number and
\epsilon
be small. Each appearance of them does not necessarily denote the same value. Finally, we denote the Fourier transform of a function
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
as

\widehat{f}(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)e^{-ix\xi}dx.

We organize this article as follows. In Section
2
, we present several lemmas to facilitate the subsequent proofs. In Section
3
, we construct an appropriate resonator and prove Theorem
1.1
. The proof of Theorem
1.2
will be given in Section
4
, preceded by the construction of an additional resonator. In the final Section
5
, we supplement some weaker results towards refining the investigation of extreme values of derivatives of the Dedekind zeta function within the critical strip.

2
Preliminary Lemmas

We begin with the following classical upper bound for the Dedekind zeta function.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}\left(\zeta_{d}\right)
be a cyclotomic field. Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and fix
\delta\in\left(0,1/2\right].
Then the Dedekind zeta function
\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}\left(s\right)
satisfies uniformly

\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}^{\left(\ell\right)}\left(\sigma+it\right)\ll d^{\phi(d)}\lvert t\rvert^{\phi(d)\frac{1-\sigma+\delta}{2}}

for all
\lvert t\rvert\geqslant 1
and
-\delta\leqslant\sigma\leqslant 1+\delta.

Proof.

There are many ways to prove this conclusion. For example, it follows from
[
21
, Theorem 4]
combining with Cauchy’s integral formula

\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}^{(\ell)}(s)=\frac{\ell!}{2\pi i}\oint_{|z|=r}\frac{\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}(s+z)}{z^{\ell+1}}\,dz.

∎

In fact, there is a better upper bound for derivatives of the Dedekind zeta function on the critical line stated in
[
16
]
.
According to the Phragmén-Lindelöf theorem, we can optimize the bound above. But here, Lemma
2.1
is already enough.

Furthermore, in order to establish a connection with GCD sums, we need to use a double-version convolution formula, following the approach of de la Bretèche and Tenenbaum
[
11
, Lemma 5.3]
. The proof is similar to that in
[
24
, Lemma 3]
. The convolution formula is a commonly-used tool, yet for the sake of completeness, we give a complete proof.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
\sigma\in[1/2,1).
Let
s=x+iy,
and
K(s)
be a holomorphic function in the horizontal strip
\sigma-2\leqslant y\leqslant 0,
satisfying the growth condition

\max_{\sigma-2\leqslant y\leqslant 0}|K(x+iy)|=O\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{2\phi(d)}+1}\right),\,|x|\to\infty.

Then for all
t\neq 0,
we have

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{K},\ell}(\sigma+it+iy)\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{K},\ell}(\sigma-it+iy)K(y)\mathrm{d}y=\sum_{m,n\geqslant 1}\frac{\widehat{K}(\log nm)a_{\mathbb{K},\ell}(n)a_{\mathbb{K},\ell}(m)}{n^{\sigma+it}\cdot m^{\sigma-it}}-(\tau^{+}+\tau^{-}),

where

\tau^{+}=2\pi\ell!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m+n=\ell\\
m,n\geqslant 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m!n!}\left.\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\right)^{m}\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{K},\ell}(s+it)\right|_{s=1+it}\cdot\left.\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\right)^{n}K(i\sigma-is)\right|_{s=1+it}

and

\tau^{-}=2\pi\ell!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m+n=\ell\\
m,n\geqslant 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m!n!}\left.\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\right)^{m}\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{K},\ell}(s-it)\right|_{s=1-it}\cdot\left.\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\right)^{n}K(i\sigma-is)\right|_{s=1-it}.

Proof.

We set
g(s):=\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{K},\ell}(s+it)\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{K},\ell}(s-it)K(i\sigma-is),
then
g(s)
has two poles in
\sigma\leqslant\operatorname{Re}(s)\leqslant 2.
These two poles are
s=1+it
and
s=1-it
respectively.

Consider the rectangle
\mathcal{D}
with endpoints
\sigma\pm iY
and
2\pm iY
, where
Y
is large. If we set
I=\int_{\mathcal{D}}g(s)\mathrm{d}s,
inside this rectangle, according to the residue theorem, we obtain the following equation

I=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}=\tau^{+}+\tau^{-},

(2.1)

where
I_{1}
and
I_{3}
are the integrals of
g(s)
from
\sigma-iY
to
2-iY
and from
2+iY
to
\sigma+iY
respectively, which are in the horizontal direction. While
I_{2}
and
I_{4}
are the integrals of
g(s)
from
2-iY
to
2+iY
and from
\sigma+iY
to
\sigma-iY
respectively, which are in the vertical direction.
We estimate these four integrals one by one next.

It is clearly that

\lim_{Y\to\infty}I_{4}=-i\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{K},\ell}(\sigma+it+iy)\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{K},\ell}(\sigma-it+iy)K(y)\mathrm{d}y,

the infinite integral on the right-hand side of the above equation is absolutely convergent due to the decay of
K.
Combining (
1.2
) and the growth condition of
K,
we have

\lim_{Y\to\infty}I_{2}=i\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\widehat{K}(\log nm)a_{\mathbb{K},\ell}(n)a_{\mathbb{K},\ell}(m)}{n^{\sigma+it}\cdot m^{\sigma-it}}

by employing Cauchy’s theorem.

For
I_{1},
we have the following upper bound by (
1.3
), Lemma
2.1
and the growth condition of
K

I_{1}\ll Y^{-2\phi(d)}\left(\int_{\sigma}^{2}(1+Y^{\phi(d)(1-v+3\delta)})\mathrm{d}v\right)\ll Y^{-2\phi(d)}\left(1+\frac{Y^{\phi(d)(1+3\delta-\sigma)}-Y^{2\phi(d)\delta}}{\log Y}\right).

We set
\delta=1/12
and obtain
I_{1}\ll(Y^{\phi(d)/4}\log Y)^{-1}\to 0
as
Y
is sufficiently large. For
I_{3},
the similar bound can be given. Combining with (
2.1
), we complete the proof.
∎

Similarly, we have the following Lemma
2.3
, which named single-version convolution formula.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
\sigma\in[1/2,1).
Let
s=x+iy,
and
K(s)
be a holomorphic function in the horizontal strip
\sigma-2\leqslant y\leqslant 0,
satisfying the growth condition

\max_{\sigma-2\leqslant y\leqslant 0}|K(x+iy)|=O\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{2\phi(d)}+1}\right),\,|x|\to\infty.

Then for all
t\neq 0,
we have

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{K},\ell}(\sigma+it+iy)K(y)\mathrm{d}y=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\widehat{K}(\log n)a_{\mathbb{K},\ell}(n)}{n^{\sigma+it}}-2\pi\tau,

where

\tau=\left.\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\right)^{\ell}K(i\sigma-is)\right|_{s=1-it}.

Proof.

We set
g_{1}(s)=\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{K},\ell}(s+it)(s-it)K(i\sigma-is)
and consider the integral of
g_{1}
over the same rectangle
\mathcal{D}
as in the proof of Lemma
2.2
. By employing the residue theorem and Cauchy’s theorem, we can obtain Lemma
2.3
through almost the same process as in the proof of Lemma
2.2
, , so we omit the details.
∎

We need to choose an appropriate smooth kernel function
K
when applying Lemma
2.2
. Drawing on the ideas in
[
4
,
8
]
we set

K_{\eta}(u):=\frac{\sin^{2\eta}\left((\frac{1}{\eta}\epsilon\log T)u\right)}{(\frac{1}{\eta}\epsilon\log T)^{2\eta-1}u^{2\eta}}

(2.2)

in this paper, which is the same choice as that made by Bondarenko et al. in
[
4
]
. Here
\epsilon
is small and
\eta\in\mathbb{N}
will be chosen later. The following lemma can be derived using the convolution theorem and Taylor’s formula.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
4
]
, Lemma 5)
.

Let
K_{\eta}(u)
be given as in (
2.2
). Then
\widehat{K_{\eta}}(v)
is a real and even function whose support lies within the range
|v|\leqslant 2\epsilon\log T.

It is decreasing on
[0,\infty).
Specifically,
0\leqslant\widehat{K_{\eta}}(v)\leqslant\widehat{K_{\eta}}(0)
holds. For the derivatives of
\widehat{K_{\eta}}
, we have

\left|\frac{d}{dv}\widehat{K_{\eta}}(v)\right|\leqslant\frac{\widehat{K_{\eta-1}}(0)}{\frac{1}{\eta}\epsilon\log T}.

(2.3)

Furthermore, for large
\eta
we have the following equivalence

\widehat{K_{\eta}}(0)\sim\sqrt{\frac{3\pi}{\eta}}.

(2.4)

Considering that the coefficients of the Dirichlet series of the Dedekind zeta function are non-trivial, Fonga in
[
14
]
had to study special GCD sums. He defined

S_{1/2}(\mathcal{M},a):=\sum_{m,n\in\mathcal{M}}a\left(\frac{m}{(m,n)}\right)a\left(\frac{n}{(m,n)}\right)\sqrt{\frac{(m,n)}{[m,n]}}.

(2.5)

We also utilize the same method as in
[
14
]
to construct the set
\mathcal{M}.
Let
\alpha\in(1,e)
and
\kappa\in(0,1)
be some parameters, and let
N=\lfloor T^{\lambda}\rfloor
be the integer not larger than
T
with
\lambda
to be chosen later. Let
k
be an integer satisfying
1\leqslant k\leqslant(\log_{2}N)^{\kappa\phi(d)},
then define
P_{k}
as the set of primes
p
that satisfy

\alpha^{k}\log N\log_{2}N<p\leqslant\alpha^{k+1}\log N\log_{2}N\quad\text{and}\quad p\equiv 1\pmod{d}.

Because of the upper bound
d\ll(\log_{2}T)^{A},
the Siegel-Walfisz theorem implies

\lvert P_{k}\rvert\sim\frac{1}{\phi(d)}\alpha^{k}(\alpha-1)\log N\left\{1+O\left(\frac{k+\log_{3}N}{\log_{2}N}\right)\right\}.

Let the parameter
\beta>1
satisfy
\beta\kappa\log\alpha<1,
then define

W_{k}:=2\left\lfloor\frac{\beta\log N}{2k^{2}\phi(d)\log_{3}N}\right\rfloor.

Let
\mathbb{P}_{k}:=\prod_{p\in P_{k}}p
, and consider the following set

\mathcal{M}_{k}:=\left\{m:m=\frac{q}{q^{\prime}}\mathbb{P}_{k},\ \omega(q)\leq\frac{1}{2}W_{k},\omega(q^{\prime})\leq\frac{1}{2}W_{k},\ qq^{\prime}\mid\mathbb{P}_{k}\right\},

where
\omega(n)
denotes the number of distinct prime factors of
n.
Next, we consider

\mathcal{M}:=\left\{m=\prod_{1\leq k\leq(\log_{2}N)^{\kappa\phi(d)}}m_{k}:\ m_{k}\in\mathcal{M}_{k}\,,\,1\leq\forall k\leq(\log_{2}N)^{\kappa\phi(d)}\right\}.

(2.6)

Since we assume that the inequality
\beta\kappa\log\alpha<1
holds, according to
[
11
, Lemma 2.2]
, we have
|\mathcal{M}|\leq N.
The following lemma benefits from the above set

\lvert t\rvert\leqslant 1.
We have

\int_{\lvert t\rvert\leqslant 1}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\widehat{K_{\eta}}(\log n)a_{\mathbb{K},\ell}(n)}{n^{\sigma+it}}\right)|R(t)|^{2}\Phi\left(\frac{t\log T}{T}\right)\mathrm{d}t\ll R(0)^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\widehat{K_{\eta}}(\log n)a_{\mathbb{K},\ell}(n)}{n^{\sigma}}.

Then by Lemma
2.4
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{\lvert t\rvert\leqslant 1}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\widehat{K_{\eta}}(\log n)a_{\mathbb{K},\ell}(n)}{n^{\sigma+it}}\right)|R(t)|^{2}\Phi\left(\frac{t\log T}{T}\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\ll R(0)^{2}\widehat{K_{\eta}}(0)(\log_{2}T)^{A}\sum_{n\leqslant T^{2\epsilon}}1

\displaystyle\ll\widehat{K_{\eta}}(0)T^{\lambda+2\epsilon}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f(n)^{2}.

(4.7)

Recalling that
\lambda+2\epsilon<1
and combining (
4.2
), (
4.5
), (
4.6
) and (
4.2
), we have

Z_{\mathbb{K}}(\sigma,T)\gg\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\widehat{K_{\eta}}(\log n)a_{\mathbb{K},\ell}(n)}{n^{\sigma+it}}\right)|R(t)|^{2}\Phi\left(\frac{t\log T}{T}\right)\mathrm{d}t+o(1).

(4.8)

Define
\widetilde{W}(T)
as the integral on the right-side of (
4.8
). We substitute the definition of the resonator
R(t)
in Section
4.1
and utilize the fact that
a_{\mathbb{K,\ell}(n)}\geqslant a(n).
Then we obtain

\widetilde{W}(T)=\sqrt{2\pi}\frac{T}{\log T}\sum_{m,n\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{d}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\widehat{K_{\eta}}(\log n)a(k)r(m)r(n)}{k^{\sigma}}\Phi\left(\frac{T}{\log T}\log\frac{km}{n}\right).

Following the idea of
[
4
]
, we restrict the sum on the right-side of above to
k\leqslant T^{\epsilon/3\eta}
and harvest

\widetilde{W}(T)\gg\widehat{K_{\eta}}(0)\frac{T}{\log T}\sum_{m,n\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{d}}\sum_{k\leqslant T^{\epsilon/3n}}\frac{a(k)r(m)r(n)}{k^{\sigma}}\Phi\left(\frac{T}{\log T}\log\frac{km}{n}\right).

For getting the lower bound for
\widetilde{W}(T),
we employ the techniques similar to those in
[
8
, Eq. (21)]
. At this point, we find

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}(T)

\displaystyle\gg\widehat{K_{\eta}}(0)\frac{T}{\log T}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\in\mathcal{M}_{d},km=n\\
k\leqslant T^{\epsilon/3\eta}\end{subarray}}\frac{a(k)f(m)f(n)}{k^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle=\widehat{K_{\eta}}(0)\frac{T}{\log T}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{M}_{d}}\frac{f(n)}{n^{\sigma}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid n,q\geqslant n/T^{\epsilon/3\eta}\end{subarray}}a(\frac{n}{q})f(q)q^{\sigma}.

(4.9)

To get the last equation above, we set
m=n/q
and
k=q\mid n,
and for the multiplicative function
f,
we have
f(n)=f(n/q)f(q).

We focus on the tail of the last sum, namely

\mathscr{E}_{d}(T):=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{M}_{d}}f(n)^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid n,q\geqslant T^{\epsilon/3\eta}\end{subarray}}\frac{a(q)}{f(q)q^{\sigma}}\leqslant T^{-\epsilon\delta/12\eta}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{M}_{d}}f(n)^{2}\prod_{p\mid n}\left(1+\frac{a(p)}{f(p)p^{\sigma-\delta}}\right)

(4.10)

with small
\delta.
By the definition of
f(p)
when
p\in\mathcal{P}_{d}
we have

\frac{\phi(d)}{f(p)p^{\sigma-\delta}}\leqslant\frac{\phi(d)(\log_{2}N)^{\gamma}p^{\delta}}{c_{d}\left(\log N\right)^{1-\sigma}\left(\log_{2}N\right)^{\sigma}\left(\log_{3}N\right)^{\sigma-1}}\ll(\log N)^{\sigma-1+\delta}.

For any
n\in\mathcal{M}_{d},
there are at most
O\left(\left(\log T/\log_{3}T\right)^{2-2\sigma}\right)
prime factors. So combining with (
4.10
), we obtain

\mathscr{E}_{d}(T)\leqslant\sum_{n\in\mathcal{M}_{d}}f(n)^{2}\exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon\delta}{12\eta}\log T+o\left(\frac{(\log T)^{1-\sigma+\delta}}{\left(\log_{3}T\right)^{2-2\sigma}}\right)\right).

By
d\ll\left(\log_{2}T\right)^{A},
we have
\eta=2\phi(d)\ll\left(\log T\right)^{\sigma-\delta}.
So we get

\mathscr{E}_{d}(T)=o\left(\sum_{n\in\mathcal{M}_{d}}f(n)^{2}\right).

(4.11)

Now we set

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{d,N}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f(n)^{2}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{f(n)}{n^{\sigma}}\sum_{q\mid n}a(\frac{n}{q})f(q)q^{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{d}}\frac{1+f(p)^{2}+\phi(d)f(p)p^{-\sigma}}{1+f(p)^{2}}.

For
c_{d}\leqslant\sqrt{\phi(d)/(e-1)},
we have
f(p)=o(1).

Then by the Siegel-Walfisz theorem we have

\mathscr{A}_{d,N}=\exp\left((\gamma c_{d}+o(1))\frac{\left(\log N\right)^{1-\sigma}\left(\log_{3}N\right)^{\sigma}}{\left(\log_{2}N\right)^{\sigma}}\right).

(4.12)

Recall that

\widetilde{W}(T)\gg\widehat{K_{\eta}}(0)\frac{T}{\log T}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{M}_{d}}\frac{f(n)}{n^{\sigma}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid n\\
q\geqslant n/T^{\epsilon/3\eta}\end{subarray}}a(\frac{n}{q})f(q)q^{\sigma}.

If we can show

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{N}\\
n\notin\mathcal{M}_{d}\end{subarray}}\frac{f(n)}{n^{\sigma}}\sum_{q\mid n}a(\frac{n}{q})f(q)q^{\sigma}=o\left(\mathscr{A}_{d,N}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f(n)^{2}\right),

(4.13)

we have almost completed the proof of Theorem
1.2
. This is because we have (
4.11
), (
4.12
) and the trivial equation as follows

\mathscr{A}_{d,N}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f(n)^{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{N}\\
n\notin\mathcal{M}_{d}\end{subarray}}+\sum_{n\in\mathcal{M}_{d}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid n\\
q\geqslant n/T^{\epsilon/3\eta}\end{subarray}}+\sum_{n\in\mathcal{M}_{d}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid n\\
q\leqslant n/T^{\epsilon/3\eta}\end{subarray}}.

Here, for the sake of simplicity, we have omitted the summation function on the right-hand side above without causing confusion.

To this end, by the definition of
\mathcal{M}_{d},
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{d,N}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\mathscr{A}_{d,N}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f(n)^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{N}\\
n\notin\mathcal{M}_{d}\end{subarray}}\frac{f(n)}{n^{\sigma}}\sum_{q\mid n}a(\frac{n}{q})f(q)q^{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{\mathscr{A}_{d,N}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f(n)^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor(\log_{2}N)^{\gamma}\rfloor}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{M}_{k,d}}\frac{f(n)}{n^{\sigma}}\sum_{d\mid n}a(\frac{n}{q})f(q)q^{\sigma}.

By the definition of
f(n)
and
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{k,d},
we get

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{d,N}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor(\log_{2}N)^{\gamma}\rfloor}\frac{1}{\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{k,d}}(1+f(p)^{2}+\phi(d)f(p)p^{-\sigma})}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{k,d}}\frac{f(n)}{n^{\sigma}}\sum_{q\mid n}a(\frac{n}{q})f(q)q^{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor(\log_{2}N)^{\gamma}\rfloor}\frac{1}{\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{k,d}}(1+f(p)^{2})}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{k,d}}f(n)^{2}\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{k,d}}\left(1+\frac{\phi(d)}{f(p)p^{\sigma}}\right).

For each integer
k\in\left\{1,\cdots,\lfloor(\log_{2}N)^{\gamma}\rfloor\right\},
we use the following notations

\displaystyle F_{k,d,N}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{k,d}}(1+f(p)^{2})}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{k,d}}f(n)^{2}\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{k,d}}\left(1+\frac{\phi(d)}{f(p)p^{\sigma}}\right),

\displaystyle F^{\ast}_{k,d,N}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{k,d}}(1+f(p)^{2})}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{k,d}}f(n)^{2},

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{k,d,N}

\displaystyle:=\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{k,d}}\left(1+\frac{\phi(d)}{f(p)p^{\sigma}}\right).

Trivially, we have
F_{k,d,N}=F^{\ast}_{k,d,N}\cdot F^{\prime}_{k,d,N}.
Then we estimate
F^{\ast}_{k,d}
and
F^{\prime}_{k,d,N}
respectively.

Recalling the definition of
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{k,d},
we utilize the classical method in
[
7
, p. 8]
. According to
f(n)
is a multiplicative function, we obtain

\sum_{n\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{k,d}}f(n)^{2}\leqslant b^{-\Delta_{k}}\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{k,d}}(1+bf(p)^{2})

whenever
b>1.

Thus,

F^{\ast}_{k,d,N}\leqslant b^{-\Delta_{k}}\exp\left((b-1)\sum_{p\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{k,d}}f(p)^{2}\right).

By the Siegel-Walfisz theorem, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{k,d}}f(p)^{2}

\displaystyle=c_{d}^{2}\frac{\left(\log N\right)^{2-2\sigma}\left(\log_{2}N\right)^{2\sigma}}{\left(\log_{3}N\right)^{2-2\sigma}}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{k,d}}\frac{1}{p^{2\sigma}(\log p-\log_{2}N-\log_{3}N-\log\phi(d))^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{c_{d}^{2}}{\phi(d)}(1+o(1))\frac{\left(\log N\right)^{2-2\sigma}\left(\log_{2}N\right)^{2\sigma}}{\left(\log_{3}N\right)^{2-2\sigma}}\int_{e^{k}\phi(d)\log N\log_{2}N}^{e^{k+1}\phi(d)\log N\log_{2}N}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{k^{2}x^{2\sigma}\log x}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{c_{d}^{2}(e-1)}{\phi(d)}(1+o(1))\frac{\left(\log N\right)^{2-2\sigma}}{k^{2}\left(\log_{3}N\right)^{2-2\sigma}}.

Combining the above estimations, we have

F^{\ast}_{k,d,N}\ll\exp\left(\left(\frac{c_{d}^{2}(e-1)}{\phi(d)}(b-1)-\alpha\log b+o(1)\right)\frac{\left(\log N\right)^{2-2\sigma}}{k^{2}\left(\log_{3}N\right)^{2-2\sigma}}\right).

(4.14)

On the other hand, for
F^{\prime}_{k,d},
by (
4.1
) and
p\in\mathcal{P}_{k,d}
we obtain

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{k,d,N}

\displaystyle\leqslant\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{k,d}}\left(1+\frac{\phi(d)}{c_{d}}(k+1)\frac{\left(\log_{3}N\right)^{1-\sigma}}{\left(\log N\right)^{1-\sigma}\left(\log_{2}N\right)^{\sigma}}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\left(\frac{\phi(d)}{c_{d}}(k+1)e^{k+1}\frac{\left(\log N\right)^{\sigma}\left(\log_{3}N\right)^{1-\sigma}}{\left(\log_{2}N\right)^{\sigma}}\right).

By
d\ll\left(\log_{2}N\right)^{A}
and
k\leqslant\left(\log_{2}N\right)^{\gamma},
we get

F^{\prime}_{k,d,N}=\exp\left(o\left(\frac{\left(\log N\right)^{2-2\sigma}}{k^{2}\left(\log_{3}N\right)^{2-2\sigma}}\right)\right).

(4.15)

Combining with (
4.14
) and (
4.15
), we have the following bound

F_{k,d,N}\ll\exp\left(\left(\frac{c_{d}^{2}(e-1)}{\phi(d)}(b-1)-\alpha\log b+o(1)\right)\frac{\left(\log N\right)^{2-2\sigma}}{k^{2}\left(\log_{3}N\right)^{2-2\sigma}}\right).

Since we have already assumed
c_{d}\leqslant\sqrt{\phi(d)/(e-1)}
and
\alpha>1
, then we only need to choose
b
to be sufficiently close to 1 such that the exponent is negative. So, we get

F_{k,d,N}=o\left(\mathscr{A}_{d,N}\right).

At this point, we have proved (
4.13
). Combining with (
4.8
), (
4.2
) and (
4.12
), we obtain

Z_{\mathbb{K}}(\sigma,T)\gg\exp\left((\gamma c_{d}+o(1))\frac{\left(\log T\right)^{1-\sigma}\left(\log_{3}T\right)^{\sigma}}{\left(\log_{2}T\right)^{\sigma}}\right)+o(1)

for
\lambda+2\epsilon<1.

Trivially,

\displaystyle\max_{-T\log T-T^{1/2}\leqslant t\leqslant T\log T+T^{1/2}}\left|\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}^{(\ell)}\left(\sigma+it\right)\right|

\displaystyle\gg{Z_{\mathbb{K}}(\sigma,T)}+O(1)

\displaystyle\gg\exp\left((\gamma c_{d}+o(1))\frac{\left(\log T\right)^{1-\sigma}\left(\log_{3}T\right)^{\sigma}}{\left(\log_{2}T\right)^{\sigma}}\right)+o(1).

(4.16)

Then, let
\lambda+2\epsilon<1
with small
\epsilon.
Take
c_{d}=\sqrt{\phi(d)/(e-1)}
and let
\gamma
be sufficiently close to 1.
Finally, using the same transform as in Section
3.2
, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

∎

Supplementary Conclusions

In Sections
3
and
4
, we respectively present Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
along with their proofs. Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
establish lower bounds for the maximum of the derivatives of Dedekind zeta functions when the real parts are on the critical line and near the critical line, respectively.

By adopting a method similar to that in Section
4
, we can prove Theorem
5.1
as follows.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}\left(\zeta_{d}\right)
and
d\geqslant 3.
Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
A
be an arbitrary positive number. If
T
is sufficiently large in the range

\frac{1}{2}\leqslant\sigma\leqslant\frac{2}{3},

then uniformly for
d\ll(\log\log T)^{A},
we have

\max_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}^{(\ell)}(\sigma+it)\right|\geqslant\exp\left((1+o(1))\sqrt{\frac{\phi(d)}{e-1}}\frac{\left(\log T\right)^{1-\sigma}}{\left(\log\log T\right)^{\sigma}}\right).

(5.1)

Proof.

In fact, we only need to redefine the set
\mathcal{M}_{k,d}
as

\mathcal{M}_{k,d}:=\left\{n\in\mathrm{supp}(f):n\text{ has at least }\Delta_{k}:=\frac{\alpha(\log N)^{2-2\sigma}}{k^{2}\log_{3}N}\text{ prime divisors in }\mathcal{P}_{k,d}\right\},

and the value of
f(p)
for
p\in\mathcal{P}_{d}
as

f(p)=c_{d}\left(\frac{(\log N)^{1-\sigma}(\log_{2}N)^{\sigma}}{\log_{3}N}\right)\frac{1}{p^{\sigma}(\log p-\log_{2}N-\log_{3}N-\log\phi(d))}.

Then the rest of the process is the same as that in Section
4.2
.
∎

Notably, if we adopt the resonance method in
[

]

\sigma_{0}

\displaystyle\max_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}^{(\ell)}(\sigma+it)\right|\geqslant\exp\left(C(\sigma)\phi(d)^{1-\sigma}\frac{\left(\log T\right)^{1-\sigma}}{\left(\log_{2}T\right)^{\sigma}}\right).

(5.2)

Bayesian Analysis of the Neutron Star Equation of State and Model Comparison: Insights from PSR J0437+4715, PSR J0614+3329, and Other Multi-Physics Data
S k Md Adil Imam 1, N . K. Patra 2

Abstract
We perform a comprehensive Bayesian analysis to constrain the neutron star (NS) equation of state (EoS) using a wide range of terrestrial and astrophysical data. The terrestrial inputs include quantities related to symmetric nuclear matter (SNM) and symmetry energy up to two times saturation density ( ρ 0 ∼ \rho_{0}\sim 0.16 fm -3 ), derived from finite nuclei and heavy-ion collisions (HICs). The astrophysical constraints incorporate NS radii and tidal deformabilities from recent NICER observations and GW170817, respectively. We consider five different EoS models: Taylor, n / 3 n/3 , Skyrme, RMF, and sound speed(CS), are analyzed by sequentially updating the priors with (i) χ \chi EFT based pure neutron matter, (ii) terrestrial, empirical and earlier astrophysical data, (iii) case (ii) including NICER radii of PSR J0437+4715 and J0614+3329, (iv) all data combined, and (v) excluding empirical nuclear inputs. We also perform Bayesian model comparison which favors the Skyrme model under all combined data (scenario (iv)), yielding tight constraints on symmetry energy parameters: L 0 = 56 ± 3 L_{0}=56\pm 3 MeV, K sym0 = − 132 ± 15 K_{\mathrm{sym}0}=-132\pm 15 MeV and also on SNM parameters: K 0 = 265 ± 12 K_{0}=265\pm 12 MeV, Q 0 = − 366 ± 43 Q_{0}=-366\pm 43 MeV. The mass-radius and mass-tidal deformability posterior distributions are also well constrained. The radius and tidal deformability of a 1.4 ​ M ⊙ 1.4\,M_{\odot} neutron star are found to be R 1.4 = 11.85 ± 0.11 R_{1.4}=11.85\pm 0.11 km and Λ 1.4 = 354 ± 25 \Lambda_{1.4}=354\pm 25 , respectively.

III
Bayesian Analysis

Bayesian likelihood is a central concept in Bayesian statistics, enabling the updating of probability estimates for a hypothesis as new data become available. This iterative updating underlies
Bayesian inference
, where the aim is to refine our knowledge of the hypothesis parameters based on observed data. At its core is
Bayes’ theorem
, which relates prior information, observed data, and the hypothesis to yield the
posterior probability
distribution over parameters. For a dataset
\mathcal{D}
and a hypothesis
H(\theta)
, Bayes’ theorem is expressed as:

P(\theta|\mathcal{D},H)=\frac{{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{D}|\theta,H)P(\theta|H)}}{P(\mathcal{D}|H)}

Here’s what each component represents:

•

P(\theta|\mathcal{D},H)
: the
posterior probability
of parameters
\theta
given data
\mathcal{D}
under hypothesis
H
, representing our updated belief after observing the data.

•

\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{D}|\theta,H)
: the
likelihood function
, describing the probability of observing
\mathcal{D}
for a given set of parameters
\theta
under
H
.

•

P(\theta|H)
: the
prior probability
of
\theta
under hypothesis
H
, reflecting our knowledge or assumptions before observing the data.

•

P(\mathcal{D}|H)
: the
marginal likelihood
(or
evidence
) under
H
, acting as a normalizing constant to ensure the posterior sums to 1 and accounting for the likelihood of the data over all parameter values.

III.1
Likelihood

In Bayesian analysis, the likelihood function is defined as the probability of the observed data under a specific model, parameterized by
\theta

(i)
Experimental data:
For experimental data
D_{\rm expt}\pm\sigma

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{D}_{expt}|}\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}exp(\frac{-(D(\theta)-D_{expt})^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}})

(28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\rm expt}

Here

is the model value for a given model parameter set
\theta
.

(ii)

For gravitational-wave (GW) data, information on the EoS parameters is obtained from the component masses

\displaystyle P(d_{\mathrm{GW}}|\mathrm{EoS})=\int^{M_{u}}_{m_{2}}dm_{1}\int^{m_{1}}_{M_{l}}dm_{2}P(m_{1},m_{2}|\mathrm{EoS})

\displaystyle\times P(d_{\mathrm{GW}}|m_{1},m_{2},\Lambda_{1}(m_{1},\mathrm{EoS}),\Lambda_{2}(m_{2},\mathrm{EoS}))

where P(m

P(m|\rm{EoS})=\left\{\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{M_{u}-M_{l}}&\text{ iff }&M_{l}\leq m\leq M_{u},\\

III.2
Bayes Factor

The
Bayes Factor
is a ratio that compares the evidence for two hypotheses
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
. It is defined as:

\displaystyle\text{BF}_{12}=\frac{P(D|H_{1})}{P(D|H_{2})}

(33)

where,

•

P(D|H_{1})
is the likelihood of the data
D
under hypothesis
H_{1}
.

•

P(D|H_{2})
is the likelihood of the data
D
under hypothesis
H_{2}
.

III.2.1
Log of the Bayes Factor

The
logarithm
of the Bayes factor simplifies the interpretation. It transforms the Bayes factor into a scale that is easier to work with and interpret. The log Bayes factor is:

\displaystyle\ln(\text{BF}_{12})=\ln\left(\frac{P(D|H_{1})}{P(D|H_{2})}\right),

(34)

This gives you a more convenient way to express how strongly the data support one hypothesis over the other.

III.2.2
Interpretation of the Bayes Factor

A common scale for interpreting the magnitude of the Bayes factor (or log Bayes factor) is as follows:

•

\ln(\text{BF})>1
: Strong evidence for hypothesis
H_{1}

•

0<\ln(\text{BF})<1
: Weak to moderate evidence for hypothesis
H_{1}

•

\ln(\text{BF})=0
: No evidence for either hypothesis

•

-1<\ln(\text{BF})<0
: Weak to moderate evidence for hypothesis
H_{2}

•

\ln(\text{BF})<-1
: Strong evidence for hypothesis
H_{2}

VI
Conclusions

We employed Bayesian inference with five EoS models—Taylor,
n/3
, Skyrme, RMF, and sound speed (CS)—by integrating multi-physics constraints from nuclear experiments, empirical nuclear inputs,
\chi
EFT calculations of pure neutron matter, and neutron star observations to tightly constrain the nuclear matter parameters governing the equation of state. The data were organized into five sets:
Set1
(
\chi
EFT PNM),
Set2
(terrestrial, empirical, and earlier astrophysical),
Set3
(new NICER radii of PSR J0437+4715 and PSR J0614+3329),
Set4
(all data combined), and
Set5
(excluding empirical nuclear inputs).

The PNM data from
\chi
EFT constrain the slope of the symmetry energy
L_{0}\simeq 52\pm 3
MeV. Data in
Set2
significantly improve constraints on
K_{0}
,
Q_{0}
, and
J_{0}
, but broaden the uncertainties in
L_{0}
and
K_{\mathrm{sym0}}
. The inclusion of recent NICER measurements for PSR J0437+4715 and PSR J0614+3329 further tighten the constraints on the symmetry energy and its density dependence, particularly
L_{0}
and
K_{\mathrm{sym0}}
. The combined dataset in
Set4
yields the most stringent bounds on the nuclear matter parameters and the governing EoS.

Model comparisons revealed that the Skyrme interaction provides the best overall fit in the most data-rich scenarios (Sets 4 and 5), outperforming other models. RMF consistently yields lower maximum NS masses and narrower bounds on proton fraction and sound speed.
The combined data constrain the key NMPs within narrow bounds. For the Skyrme model:
L_{0}=56\pm 3
MeV,
K_{\mathrm{sym}0}=-132\pm 15
MeV,
K_{0}=265\pm 12
MeV,
Q_{0}=-366\pm 43
MeV.
Our results also yield precise NS observables:

R_{1.4}=11.85\pm 0.11
km,

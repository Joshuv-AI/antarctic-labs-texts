Local Cohomological Defect and a Conjecture of Mustaţă-Popa
Andrew Burke

Abstract
. We prove a general result on the depth of Du Bois complexes of a singular variety. We apply it to prove a conjecture of Mustaţă-Popa and to study the local cohomological defect, extending results of Ogus and Dao-Takagi over the complex numbers.



A.
Introduction

The present paper is dedicated to new results around the local cohomological defect, a singularity invariant which has received growing interest in recent years. It admits topological
[
BBL+25
]
,
[
Og73
]
,
[
RSW21
]
and holomorphic characterizations
[
MP22
]
. Additionally, it controls Lefschetz-type theorems on singular varieties
[
PP25
]
.

Throughout, let
X
be a complex algebraic variety of dimension
n
. If
X
is embedded in a smooth variety
Y
of dimension
d
, consider the local cohomology sheaves
\mathcal{H}^{q}_{X}(\mathscr{O}_{Y})
. The first nonzero sheaf occurs at
q=c
, where
c=d-n
. We define the
local cohomological defect

\displaystyle\lcdef(X)=\max\{q:\mathcal{H}^{q}_{X}(\mathscr{O}_{Y})\neq 0\}-c.

It satisfies
0\leq\lcdef(X)\leq n
. If
X
has quotient or local complete intersection singularities, then
\lcdef(X)=0
; see
[
PS24
, Example
2.4
]
for more examples.

One can express
\lcdef(X)
in terms of more refined holomorphic invariants. Denote by
\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}\in D^{b}_{\coh}(X)
the
p
-th Du Bois complex

[
DB81
]
of
X
. This is a Hodge-theoretic replacement for the sheaf of holomorphic
p
-forms on a smooth variety, which can be computed in terms of a hyperresolution. Then
[
MP22
, Theorem E]
proves

(0.1)

\displaystyle\lcdef(X)=n-\min_{0\leq p\leq n}\{\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}+p\}.

In particular,
\lcdef(X)
is independent of embedding. A priori, one must compute
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}
for all
p\leq n
to specify
\lcdef(X)
. An application of the main result of this paper is that it suffices to check only
p\leq\lceil(n-3)/2\rceil
. In fact, more is true:

Theorem A
.

Let
k
be an integer. Then we have
\lcdef(X)\leq n-k
if and only if

\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}+p\geq k
for all
p\leq\lceil(k-3)/2\rceil
.

When
k\leq 3
, we recover (slight extensions of) well-known results of Ogus
[
Og73
]
and Dao-Takagi
[
DT16
]
: if
\depth\mathscr{O}_{X}\geq k
, then
\lcdef(X)\leq n-k
. Note that
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}\geq\depth\mathscr{O}_{X}
by
[
KS16
]
. Example
7.8
shows statements involving
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}
are strictly stronger than those involving
\depth\mathscr{O}_{X}
. The obvious pattern fails beyond
k=3
; see
[
DT16
, Example
2.11
]
. Our result explains this failure and completes the picture.

Theorem
A
is a consequence of a more general result about the depth of Du Bois complexes, which is our main theorem.

Theorem B
.

Let
k
and
m
be integers. If

\displaystyle\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}+p\geq k\hskip 5.69046pt\text{ for all }p\leq m,

then the same inequality holds for
p\geq k-m-2
.

Another application of Theorem
B
is the proof of a conjecture of Mustaţă-Popa, which arose from trying to place the results of Ogus and Dao-Takagi in the context of the Hodge filtration on local cohomology in
[
MP22
]
. Suppose
X
is embedded in a smooth variety
Y
of dimension
d
. Choose a log resolution
f:Z\rightarrow Y
of the pair
(Y,X)
, assumed to be an isomorphism away from
X
. Denote
E=f^{-1}(X)_{\red}
, which is a simple normal crossings divisor on
Z
.

Conjecture C
.

[
MP22
, Conjecture
14.1
]

If
\depth\mathscr{O}_{X}\geq j+2
, then
R^{d-2}f_{*}\Omega_{Y}^{d-j}(\log E)=0
.

Although it is phrased without reference to Hodge theory, the conclusion is equivalent to
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{j}\geq 2
by
[
MP22
, Lemma
14.4
]
. Plugging
m=0
into Theorem
B
gives the following slightly stronger statement.

Corollary D
.

If
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}\geq j+2
, then
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{j}\geq 2
.

The conjecture was previously known when
X
has isolated singularities
[
MP22
, Theorem
14.3
]
or rational singularities outside of a finite set of points
[
AH24
, Theorem
0.7
]
.

Theorem
A
also implies a bound for the local cohomological defect in Corollary
7.9
in terms of the smallest index
p(X)
for which the minimum in
0.1
is obtained. This strictly improves the bound of
[
PP25
, Corollary
7.5
]
for
\lcdef(X)
(see Example
7.10
), and it sometimes determines which
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}
computes
\lcdef(X)
.

Theorem E
.

Let
m
be an integer and suppose
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}+p\geq n
for all
p\leq m-1
. If
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{m}\leq m+3
, then

\displaystyle\lcdef(X)=\max\{0,n-\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{m}-m\}.

The first assumption holds if
X
has pre-
(m-1)
-rational singularities.

In particular,
\lcdef(X)=\max\{0,n-\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}\}
when
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}\leq 3
. This equality fails beyond depth three. Nevertheless, Dao-Takagi
[
DT16
]
prove when
\depth\mathscr{O}_{X}\geq 4
that
\lcdef(X)\leq n-4
if and only if
X
has torsion local analytic Picard groups. More generally, our final result is a technical criterion for
\lcdef(X)\leq n-k
, assuming
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}+p\geq k
for
p\leq m
, where
m
is fixed. It is phrased in terms of a family of invariants parameterized by integers
0\leq d\leq n
, which we call the
perversity defects

\pdef(X,d)
of
X
(see Definition
5.6
). For example,
\pdef(X,n)=\lcdef(X)
, while
\pdef(X,0)
, called the
constructible defect
, is a measure of the constructible cohomologies of
\mathbb{D}\mathbb{Q}_{X}
. These invariants can be expressed in terms of the cohomology of small punctured neighborhoods of points, as in Lemma
5.8
.

Theorem F
.

Let
k
and
m
be integers. We have
\lcdef(X)\leq n-k
if and only if
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}+p\geq k
for all
p\leq m
and
\pdef(X,k-2m-4)\leq n-k
.

Theorem
F
implies the aforementioned result of Dao-Takagi (see Example
8.6
) and an analogous criteria for
\lcdef(X)\leq n-5
by Park-Popa
[
PP25
, Corollary G]
(see Example
8.8
).

Our approach relies throughout on the theory of mixed Hodge modules. The main technical result is Proposition
6.5
, an abstract vanishing statement for graded de Rham pieces of mixed Hodge modules. It yields our main Theorem
B
, because Du Bois complexes can be identified with shifts of graded de Rham pieces of the trivial Hodge module. We also provide a direct explanation (Theorem
7.1
) for the equivalence between the topological and holomorphic characterizations of
\lcdef(X)
, as was asked about in
[
MP22
, Remark
11.2
]
.

B.
Preliminaries

1.
Mixed Hodge modules

This paper will extensively use the language of mixed Hodge modules
[
Sa88
,
Sa90
]
. We think of a mixed Hodge module as a perverse sheaf equipped with additional structure, including a Hodge filtration and a weight filtration. The category
\MHM(X)
of mixed Hodge modules is abelian, so it admits a derived category
D^{b}(\MHM(X))
.

There is a faithful realization functor

\displaystyle\rat_{X}:D^{b}(\MHM(X))\rightarrow D^{b}_{c}(X)

sending a mixed Hodge module to its underlying perverse sheaf, considered as a complex of constructible sheaves. The usual functors
f_{*},f^{*},f_{!},f^{!},\otimes
, and
\mathbb{D}
on
D^{b}_{c}(X)
lift to
D^{b}(\MHM(X))
.

Let
M
be a mixed Hodge module on
X
, and suppose
X
is locally embedded in a smooth variety
Y
of dimension
d
. Denote by
\mathcal{M}
the
\mathcal{D}_{Y}
-module corresponding to the realization of
M
under the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence. The Hodge filtration is a filtration
F_{\bullet}\mathcal{M}
on
\mathcal{M}
. Let us emphasize that the filtered object
F_{\bullet}\mathcal{M}
is defined on the ambient space
Y
, not on
X
. The more invariant global objects to consider are the graded de Rham pieces of
M
, defined locally by the formula

(1.1)

\displaystyle\gr_{p}^{F}\dr(M)=\big(\gr_{p-d}^{F}M\otimes\wedge^{m}T_{Y}\rightarrow\ldots\rightarrow\gr_{p-1}^{F}M\otimes T_{Y}\rightarrow\gr_{p}^{F}M\big).

They are well-defined complexes of coherent sheaves on
X
by
[
Sa90
, Proposition
2.33
]
. Moreover,
\gr_{p}^{F}\dr
is an exact functor, so it extends to

\displaystyle\gr_{p}^{F}\dr:D^{b}(\MHM(X))\rightarrow D^{b}_{\coh}(X).

One computes
\gr_{p}^{F}\dr(M^{\bullet})
for a complex
M^{\bullet}
by applying
1.1
to each term.

Mixed Hodge modules are also equipped with a weight filtration
W_{\bullet}
on the underlying perverse sheaf. One says a complex
M^{\bullet}\in D^{b}(\MHM(X))
has weights
\geq c
(resp.
\leq c
) if
\gr_{\ell}^{W}\mathcal{H}^{j}M^{\bullet}=0
for all
\ell-j<c
(resp.
\ell-j\geq c
).

2.
Trivial Hodge module

Let
X
be a complex algebraic variety of dimension
n
. We define the
trivial Hodge module

\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}[n]\in D^{b}(\MHM(X))
to be the complex
a^{*}\mathbb{Q}^{H}[n]
, where
a:X\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\mathbb{C}
. It has cohomologies in degrees
\leq 0
and has weights
\leq n
.

The
intersection cohomology Hodge module

\ic_{X}^{H}
is
\gr_{n}^{W}\mathcal{H}^{0}\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}[n]
, a pure Hodge module of weight
n
(see
[
Sa90
,
4.5
]
). Denote by
\gamma_{X}
the composition of the natural morphisms
\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}[n]\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{0}\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}[n]\rightarrow\gr_{n}^{W}\mathcal{H}^{0}\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}[n]=\ic_{X}^{H}
. The
RHM-defect object

\mathcal{K}_{X}^{\bullet}\in D^{b}(\MHM(X))
is the complex sitting in the distinguished triangle

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{X}^{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}[n]\xrightarrow{\gamma_{X}}\ic_{X}^{H}\xrightarrow{+1}

See
[
PP25
, Definition
6.1
]
and
[
PP25
, Proposition
6.4
]
, where the following is proved.

Lemma 2.2
.

\mathcal{K}_{X}^{\bullet}
has weights
\leq(n-1)

3.
Du Bois Complexes

Saito
[
Sa00
, Theorem
0.2
]
proved that the Du Bois complexes of
X
can be identified with the graded de Rham pieces of the trivial Hodge module, up to shift:

\displaystyle\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}\cong\gr_{-p}^{F}\dr\big(\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}[n]\big)[p-n].

We make this identification throughout the paper. Analogously, the
p
-th intersection Du Bois complex

[
PSV24
]
of
X
is the complex
I\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}=\gr_{-p}^{F}\dr(\ic_{X}^{H})[p-n]
. These complexes are nonzero only for
0\leq p\leq n
. Moreover, there are natural maps
\Omega_{X}^{p}\rightarrow\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}\rightarrow I\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}
, which are isomorphisms when
X
is smooth.

For future reference, we record that since
\ic_{X}^{H}
is a pure Hodge module of weight
n
,

Lemma 3.1
.

\mathcal{H}^{j}\gr_{p}^{F}\dr(\ic_{X}^{H})=0
, whenever
j>0
,
p>0
, or
p<-n
.

Recently, there has been a surge of interest in singularity notions related to the Du Bois complex. Especially important are generalizations of rational and Du Bois singularities called
m
-rational and
m
-Du Bois singularities. We will not need much from this subject, except for a couple of definitions. Say
X
has
pre-
m
-rational singularities

[
SVV23
]
if

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{i}(\mathbb{D}(\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{n-p})[-n])=0\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ for all }i>0\text{ and }0\leq p\leq m.

We denote
\mathbb{D}(-)=\mathcal{R}\mathcal{H}om_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}(-,\omega_{X}^{\bullet})
, where
\omega_{X}^{\bullet}
is the dualizing complex. When
X
is smooth, this is isomorphic to
\omega_{X}[n]
. Say that
X
satisfies
condition
D_{m}
if
\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}\rightarrow I\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}
is an isomorphism for all
p\leq m
. See
[
PP25
]
or
[
DOR25
]
, where an equivalent condition is studied. If
X
has pre-
m
-rational singularities, then it satisfies condition
D_{m}
.

4.
Depth and local cohomological defect

We require a notion of depth for complexes of coherent sheaves
E\in D^{b}_{\coh}(X)
. Denote

\displaystyle\depth E=-\max\{j:\mathcal{H}^{j}\mathbb{D}E\neq 0\},

If
E
is a coherent sheaf, this agrees with the usual definition of
\depth E
.

We will study
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}
in detail. Note that
\mathbb{D}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}=\gr_{p}^{F}\dr(\mathbb{D}\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H})[-p]
, because
\mathbb{D}\circ\gr_{-p}^{F}\dr=\gr_{p}^{F}\dr\circ\mathbb{D}
by
[
Sa88
, Section
2.4
]
or
[
Pa23
, Lemma
3.2
]
. Hence

(4.1)

\displaystyle\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}+p=-\max\{j:\mathcal{H}^{j}\gr_{p}^{F}\dr(\mathbb{D}\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H})\neq 0\}.

We have the basic inequalities
0\leq\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}\leq n
. In fact,
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}\geq 1
, as
\mathcal{H}^{0}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}
is torsion-free by
[
HJ13
, Remark
3.8
]
. Moreover,
\depth I\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}\geq n-p
, because
\ic_{X}^{H}
is self-dual, up to twist, and the graded de Rham pieces of a Hodge module are concentrated in non-positive degrees. From this, we deduce

Lemma 4.2
.

If
X
satisfies condition
D_{m}
, then
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}+p\geq n
for all
p\leq m
.

We also require the injectivity theorem of
[
KS16
]
. It says the maps

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}xt^{j}_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}(\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0},\omega_{X}^{\bullet})\rightarrow\mathscr{E}xt^{j}_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}(\mathscr{O}_{X},\omega_{X}^{\bullet}).

induced from
\mathscr{O}_{X}\rightarrow\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}
are injective for each
j
. In particular,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}\geq\depth\mathscr{O}_{X}.

Recall that the local cohomological defect
\lcdef(X)
is defined using local cohomology sheaves. If
X
is embedded in a smooth variety
Y
, then

\displaystyle\lcdef(X)=\max\{q:\mathcal{H}^{q}_{X}(\mathscr{O}_{Y})\neq 0\}-c,

where
c=\dim Y-\dim X
. It admits a topological interpretation in terms of the
rectified
\mathbb{Q}
-homological depth

\RHD_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)=\min\{j:{}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{j}(\mathbb{Q}_{X})\neq 0\}
. Indeed,
[
RSW21
]
proves

(4.4)

\displaystyle\lcdef(X)=n-\RHD_{\mathbb{Q}}(X).

Since duality is exact on perverse sheaves, we have
\mathbb{D}({}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{j}(\mathbb{Q}_{X}))={}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{-j}(\mathbb{D}\mathbb{Q}_{X})
. If we combine this with the fact that the realization functor for mixed Hodge modules is faithful, we deduce

(4.5)

\displaystyle\lcdef(X)=\max\{j:\mathcal{H}^{j}(\mathbb{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}[n]))\neq 0\}.

We will use this formula for
\lcdef(X)
throughout the paper.

5.
Perversity functions and perversity defect

The category of perverse sheaves is the heart of the so-called middle perversity t-structure on the derived category
D^{b}_{c}(X)
of constructible sheaves on
X
. In this section, we recall more generally how a perversity function determines a t-structure on
D^{b}_{c}(X)
. See
[
BBD82
]
or
[
KS06
]
for details. We also introduce variants of the local cohomological defect with respect to such perversities.

Fix a function
p:\{0,1,\ldots,n\}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
, such that
p(k)-p(k+1)
is
0,1
, or
2
for all
k
. This is called a
perversity function
.

Definition 5.1
.

Define a pair
({}^{p}D_{c}^{\leq 0}(X),{}^{p}D_{c}^{\geq 0}(X))
of full subcategories of
D_{c}^{b}(X)
as follows. For
K\in D_{c}^{b}(X)
, say
K\in{}^{p}D^{\leq 0}(X)
if

\displaystyle\dim\{\Supp\mathcal{H}^{j}(K)\}<k,\hskip 14.22636pt\text{if }j>p(k),

and say
K\in{}^{p}D^{\geq 0}(X)
if

\displaystyle\dim\{\Supp\mathcal{H}^{-j}(\mathbb{D}K)\}<k,\hskip 14.22636pt\text{if }j<p(k)+2k.

These subcategories form a t-structure on
D_{c}^{b}(X)
by
[
KS06
, Theorem
10.2.8
]
. There is a convenient re-formulation of the definition in terms of a choice of Whitney stratification.

Proposition 5.2
.

[
KS06
, Proposition
10.2.4
]

Let
K\in D_{c}^{b}(X)
and consider a Whitney stratification of
X
such that
i_{S}^{*}K
and
i_{S}^{!}K
have locally constant cohomology sheaves for each inclusion of strata
i_{S}:S\rightarrow X
. Then:

(1)

K\in{}^{p}D^{\leq 0}_{c}(X)
if and only if
\mathcal{H}^{j}(i_{S}^{*}K)=0
, whenever
j>p(d_{S})
,

(2)

K\in{}^{p}D_{c}^{\geq 0}(X)
if and only if
\mathcal{H}^{j}(i_{S}^{!}K)=0
, whenever
j<p(d_{S})
.

The more familiar t-structures on
D^{b}_{c}(X)
are special cases of this construction.

Example 5.3
.

The function
p_{0}(x)=0
is called the
zero perversity
. It determines the usual constructible t-structure on
D^{b}_{c}(X)
.

Example 5.4
.

The function
p_{n}(x)=-x
is called the
middle perversity
. It determines the middle perversity t-structure, whose heart consists of perverse sheaves. Unless otherwise specified, the letter
p
will henceforth refer specifically to the middle perversity.

We require a family of perversities interpolating between the zero perversity and the middle perversity.

Example 5.5
.

Consider, for each integer
0\leq d\leq n
, the perversity function

\displaystyle p_{d}(x)=-\min\{x,d\}

By an abuse of notation, we denote the associated t-structure by
({}^{d}D_{c}^{\leq 0}(X),{}^{d}D_{c}^{\geq 0}(X))
and its cohomology sheaves by
{}^{d}\mathcal{H}^{j}(-)
. Observe that the case
d=0
is the zero perversity and
d=n
is the middle perversity.

Recall from
4.5
that
\lcdef(X)
is the largest nonzero cohomology of
\mathbb{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{X}[n])
with respect to the middle perversity. We define a generalization of
\lcdef(X)
, in terms of the perversities
p_{d}
for
0\leq d\leq n
.

Definition 5.6
.

The
d
-th perversity defect
of
X
is

\displaystyle\pdef(X,d)=\max\{j:{}^{d}\mathcal{H}^{j}(\mathbb{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{X}[n]))\neq 0\}.

In other words,
\pdef(X,d)
is the minimum index
j
such that
\mathbb{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{X}[n])\in{}^{d}D_{c}^{\leq j}(X)
.

By Example
5.4
,
\pdef(X,n)=\lcdef(X)
. We call
\pdef(X,0)
the
constructible defect

\cdef(X)
of
X
. It is equal to the largest nonzero constructible cohomology of
\mathbb{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{X}[n])
by Example
5.3
.

There is a chain of inequalities of perversity functions
p_{n}\leq\ldots\leq p_{1}\leq p_{0}
. It yields inclusions
{}^{n}D^{\leq j}_{c}(X)\subset\ldots\subset{}^{1}D^{\leq j}_{c}(X)\subset{}^{0}D^{\leq j}_{c}(X)
, and hence

(5.7)

\displaystyle\cdef(X)=\pdef(X,0)\leq\pdef(X,1)\leq\ldots\leq\pdef(X,n)=\lcdef(X).

We can also also interpret the perversity defects
\pdef(X,d)
in terms of the cohomology of small punctured neighborhoods. Indeed, fix a Whitney stratification of
X
. By the local structure of a Whitney stratification,
\mathbb{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{X}[n])
has locally constant cohomology sheaves along each strata. Proposition
5.2
implies
\pdef(X,d)\leq n-k
if and only
\mathcal{H}^{j}(i_{S}^{*}\mathbb{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{X}[n]))=0
for each strata
S
and
j>n-k-\min\{d_{S},d\}
. For a point
x\in S
, if
i_{x}:\{x\}\rightarrow X
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{j}(i_{x}^{*}\mathbb{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{X}[n]))=H^{-j}(i_{x}^{!}\mathbb{Q}_{X}[n])=H^{n-j}_{\{x\}}(X,\mathbb{Q}).

Let us denote by
U
a Stein analytic neighborhood of
x
. There is a long exact sequence

\displaystyle\ldots\rightarrow H^{n-j-1}(U\setminus\{x\},\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow H^{n-j}_{\{x\}}(X,\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow H^{n-j}(U,\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\ldots

As
U
is contractible, this gives isomorphisms
H^{n-j}_{\{x\}}(X,\mathbb{Q})\simeq\tilde{H}^{n-j-1}(U\setminus\{x\},\mathbb{Q})
. We conclude:

Lemma 5.8
.

If
k
and
d
are integers, we have
\pdef(X,d)\leq n-k
if and only if

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{i}(U\setminus\{x\},\mathbb{Q})=0\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ for all }i\leq k-2+\min\{d_{S},d\}\text{ and all }x\in X.

For example,
\lcdef(X)\leq n-k
if and only if
\tilde{H}^{i}(U\setminus\{x\},\mathbb{Q})=0
for all
i\leq k+d_{S}-2
and all
x\in X
, as was shown in
[
RSW21
, Corollary
3
]
in terms of links. Moreover,
\cdef(X)\leq n-k
if and only if
\tilde{H}^{i}(U\setminus\{x\},\mathbb{Q})=0
for all
i\leq k-2
and all
x\in X
. This characterization gives another perspective on the inequalities
5.7
.

Let us offer one final way of thinking about the constructible defect
\cdef(X)=\pdef(X,0)
. One can view the trivial Hodge module
\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}
as an iterated extension of Du Bois complexes

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}=\bigg(\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}\rightarrow\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{1}\rightarrow\ldots\rightarrow\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{n}\bigg)

inside the derived category of differential complexes on
X
. Dualizing this expression, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}[n])=\bigg(\mathbb{D}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{n}\rightarrow\ldots\rightarrow\mathbb{D}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}\bigg).

It gives rise to a cohomology spectral sequence

\displaystyle E_{1}^{p,q}=\mathcal{H}^{q}\mathbb{D}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{n-p}\Rightarrow{}^{c}\mathcal{H}^{p+q}\mathbb{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{X}[n]).

Using
0.1
, we have
\lcdef(X)\leq n-k
if and only if
E_{1}^{p,q}=0
for all
p+q>n-k
. On the other hand,
\cdef(X)\leq n-k
if and only if
{}^{c}\mathcal{H}^{p+q}\mathbb{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}[n])=0
for all
p+q>n-k
.

If the spectral sequence degerates at the
E_{1}
page, then clearly
\cdef(X)=\lcdef(X)
. Very roughly, we can view the difference between
\lcdef(X)
and
\cdef(X)
as a measure of the failure of degeneration.

Remark 5.9
.

Though we will not use it in what follows, we remark that for each perversity function
p
, there is an associated t-structure
({}^{p}D^{\leq 0}(\MHM(X)),{}^{p}D^{\geq 0}(\MHM(X))
on the derived category of mixed Hodge modules which lifts
({}^{p}D_{c}^{\leq 0}(X),{}^{p}D_{c}^{\geq 0}(X))
. More precisely, a complex
\mathcal{M}^{\bullet}
lies in
{}^{p}D^{\leq 0}(\MHM(X))
(resp.
{}^{p}D^{\geq 0}(\MHM(X))
) if and only if
\rat_{X}(M^{\bullet})
lies in
{}^{p}D_{c}^{\leq 0}(X)
(resp.
{}^{p}D_{c}^{\geq 0}(X)
). This follows from the argument of
[
KS06
, Theorem
1.2.8
]
, which only uses the six functor formalism. Saito remarked that the constructible t-structure lifts to mixed Hodge modules in
[
Sa90
, Remark
4.6.2
]
.

C.
Depth and Local Cohomological Defect

6.
Fundamental Lemmas

In this subsection, we prove some technical results which will be the key to our analysis.

Fix an integer
m
and an object
M^{\bullet}\in D^{b}(\MHM(X))
. Locally,
X
is embeddable, and
1.1
shows the condition
\gr_{p}^{F}\dr(M^{\bullet})=0
for all
p\leq m
is equivalent to
F_{m}M^{\bullet}=0
. We need a refinement of this basic observation that accounts for cohomologies.

Lemma 6.1
.

Suppose
X
is embeddable. Fix integers
m
and
c
and an object
M^{\bullet}\in D^{b}(\MHM(X))
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{j}\gr_{p}^{F}\dr(M^{\bullet})=0\hskip 14.22636pt\mathrm{for}\hskip 2.84544pt\mathrm{all}\hskip 5.69046ptj\geq c\hskip 5.69046pt\mathrm{and}\hskip 5.69046ptp\leq m

if and only if

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{j}\gr_{p}^{F}(M^{\bullet})=0\hskip 14.22636pt\mathrm{for}\hskip 2.84544pt\mathrm{all}\hskip 5.69046ptj\geq c\hskip 5.69046pt\mathrm{and}\hskip 5.69046ptp\leq m.

In particular, if
m\gg 0
, this latter condition is equivalent to
\mathcal{H}^{j}M^{\bullet}=0
for all
j\geq c
.

Proof.

Assume
\mathcal{H}^{j}\gr_{p}^{F}(M^{\bullet})=0
for all
j\geq c
and
p\leq m-1
,. It suffices to prove

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{j}\gr_{m}^{F}\dr(M^{\bullet})\cong\mathcal{H}^{j}\gr_{m}^{F}(M^{\bullet})\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ for all }j\geq c.

Consider the expression

\displaystyle\gr_{m}^{F}\dr(M^{\bullet})=\big(\gr_{m-d}^{F}M^{\bullet}\otimes\wedge^{d}T_{Y}\rightarrow\ldots\rightarrow\gr_{m-1}^{F}M^{\bullet}\otimes T_{Y}\rightarrow\gr_{m}^{F}M^{\bullet}\big).

There is a convergent spectral sequence with
E_{1}
page

\displaystyle E_{1}^{p,q}=\mathcal{H}^{q}(\gr_{m+p}^{F}M^{\bullet}\otimes\wedge^{-p}T_{Y})\implies\mathcal{H}^{p+q}\gr_{m}^{F}\dr(M^{\bullet})=0.

The terms
E_{1}^{p,q}
vanish if
p>0
or if
p<0
and
q\geq c
by assumption. One deduces that
E_{1}^{0,j}=E_{\infty}^{0,j}
for
j\geq c
and, in fact,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{j}\gr_{m}^{F}\dr(M^{\bullet})\cong\mathcal{H}^{j}\gr_{m}^{F}(M^{\bullet})

as desired.

The last statement follows, because
\gr_{p}^{F}
commutes with taking cohomology.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

Though we will not use it in this paper, a similar spectral sequence argument proves:
\mathcal{H}^{j}\gr_{p}^{F}\dr(M^{\bullet})=0
for all
j\leq c-p
and
p\leq m
if and only if
\mathcal{H}^{j}\gr_{p}^{F}(M^{\bullet})=0
for all
j\leq c-p
and
p\leq m
.

We will often reference the following immediate corollary of Lemma
6.1
.

Corollary 6.3
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{j}\gr_{p}^{F}\dr(M^{\bullet})=0\hskip 14.22636pt\mathrm{for}\hskip 2.84544pt\mathrm{all}\hskip 5.69046ptj\geq c\hskip 5.69046pt\mathrm{and}\hskip 5.69046ptp\leq m

if and only if

\displaystyle\gr_{p}^{F}\dr(\mathcal{H}^{j}M^{\bullet})=0\hskip 14.22636pt\mathrm{for}\hskip 2.84544pt\mathrm{all}\hskip 5.69046ptj\geq c\hskip 5.69046pt\mathrm{and}\hskip 5.69046ptp\leq m.

Proof.

Apply Lemma
6.1
to
M^{\bullet}
and each
\mathcal{H}^{j}M^{\bullet}
for
j\geq c
separately, and use the fact that
\gr_{p}^{F}
commutes with taking cohomology.
∎

We also require a lemma which brings weights into the picture.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
M
be a mixed Hodge module on
X
of weight
\geq\ell
. If

\displaystyle\gr_{p}^{F}\dr(M)=0\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ for all }p\leq m

then the same holds for
p\geq-\ell-m
.

Proof.

This is precisely the dual of
[
PP25
, Proposition
5.2
]
.
∎

We can now prove our main abstract result.

Proposition 6.5
.

Fix integers
c,m
, and
w
. Let
M^{\bullet}\in D^{b}(\MHM(X))
and suppose
M^{\bullet}
has weights
\geq w
. If

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{j}\gr_{i}^{F}\dr(M^{\bullet})=0\hskip 5.69046pt\text{ for all }j\geq c\text{ and }i\leq m,

then

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{j}\gr_{i}^{F}\dr(M^{\bullet})=0\hskip 5.69046pt\text{ for all }j\geq c\text{ and }i\geq-c-m-w.

Proof.

By Corollary
6.3
, our assumption is equivalent to the vanishing

\displaystyle\gr_{i}^{F}\dr(\mathcal{H}^{j}M^{\bullet})=0\hskip 5.69046pt\text{ for all }j\geq c\text{ and }i\leq m.

Since the mixed Hodge module
\mathcal{H}^{j}M^{\bullet}
has weights
\geq(w+j)
, Lemma
6.4
shows

\displaystyle\gr_{i}^{F}\dr(\mathcal{H}^{j}M^{\bullet})=0\hskip 5.69046pt\text{ for all }j\geq c\text{ and }i\geq-j-m-w.

Fix
i\geq-c-m-w
, and consider the convergent spectral sequence

\displaystyle E_{2}^{p,q}=\mathcal{H}^{p}\gr_{i}^{F}\dr(\mathcal{H}^{q}M^{\bullet})\Rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{p+q}\gr_{i}^{F}\dr(M^{\bullet}).

We have shown
E_{2}^{p,q}=0
for
q\geq c
and it also vanishes for
p>0
. This implies
\mathcal{H}^{p+q}\gr_{i}^{F}\dr(M^{\bullet})=0
for
p+q\geq c
. The result follows.
∎

7.
Applications

We now apply our machinery to
\mathbb{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}[n])\in D^{b}(\MHM(X))
and related complexes. As a warm-up, this yields a direct proof for the equivalence between the topological (
4.4
) and holomorphic (
0.1
) characterizations of
\lcdef(X)
.

Theorem 7.1
.

\RHD_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)=\min_{0\leq p\leq n}\{\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}+p\}

Proof.

By
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\min_{0\leq p\leq n}\{\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}+p\}=-\max\{j:\mathcal{H}^{j}\gr_{p}^{F}\dr(\mathbb{D}\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H})\neq 0\text{ for some }p\}.

On the other hand, dualizing the definition of
\RHD_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)
yields

\displaystyle\RHD_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)=-\max\{j:\mathcal{H}^{j}(\mathbb{D}\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H})\neq 0\}.

These two quantities are equal by Lemma
6.1
, taking
M^{\bullet}=\mathbb{D}\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}
and
m\gg 0
.
∎

We prove our main theorem regarding the depth of Du Bois complexes.

Theorem 7.2

(= Theorem
B
)
.

Fix integers
k
and
m
. If

\displaystyle\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}+p\geq k\hskip 5.69046pt\text{ for all }p\leq m,

then the same inequality holds for
p\geq k-m-2
.

The theorem would be a direct consequence of Proposition
6.5
if
\mathbb{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}[n])
had weights
\geq(-n+1)
. This is false, but
\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{K}_{X}^{\bullet})
has weights
\geq(-n+1)
by Lemma
2.2
. This will be the heart of the matter in the proof below.

Proof.

As
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}\leq n
, we either have
k\leq n
, or else
m\leq-1
.

Our hypothesis says precisely that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{j}\gr_{p}^{F}\dr(\mathbb{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}[n]))=0\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ for all }j\geq-k+1+n\text{ and }p\leq m.

Let us show the same vanishing holds with
\mathbb{D}\mathcal{K}_{X}^{\bullet}
in place of
\mathbb{D}\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}[n]
.

Consider the dual of the distinguished triangle
2.1

\displaystyle\ic_{X}^{H}(n)=\mathbb{D}\ic_{X}^{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}[n])\rightarrow\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{K}_{X}^{\bullet})\xrightarrow{+1},

together with its cohomology long exact sequence

(7.3)

\displaystyle\ldots\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{j}\gr_{p-n}^{F}\dr(\ic_{X}^{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{j}\gr_{p}^{F}\dr\bigl(\mathbb{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}[n])\bigr)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{j}\gr_{p}^{F}\dr\bigl(\mathbb{D}\mathcal{K}_{X}^{\bullet}\bigr)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{j+1}\gr_{p-n}^{F}\dr(\ic_{X}^{H})\rightarrow\ldots

Either
j>0
or
p<0
, so Lemma
3.1
implies the last term vanishes. Hence,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{j}\gr_{p}^{F}\dr(\mathbb{D}\mathcal{K}_{X}^{\bullet})=0\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ for all }j\geq-k+1+n\text{ and }p\leq m.

As
\mathbb{D}\mathcal{K}_{X}^{\bullet}
has weights
\geq(-n+1)
by Lemma
2.2
, we may apply Proposition
6.5
to deduce

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{j}\gr_{p}^{F}\dr(\mathbb{D}\mathcal{K}_{X}^{\bullet})=0\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ for all }j\geq-k+1+n\text{ and }p\geq k-m-2.

It now suffices to show the same vanishing holds with
\mathbb{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}[n])
in place of
\mathbb{D}\mathcal{K}_{X}^{\bullet}
. In the case
j>0
, the desired vanishing follows from
7.3
and Lemma
3.1
. Otherwise,
k\geq n+1
and hence
m\leq-1
. It follows that
p\geq k-m-2\geq n
. If some inequality is strict, then
p>n
, and the vanishing follows from
7.3
and Lemma
3.1
.

The final case to consider is
j=0
and
p=n
, for which we have
\mathcal{H}^{0}\gr_{n}^{F}\dr(\mathbb{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}[n]))=\mathcal{H}^{0}\mathbb{D}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{n}
. This vanishes because
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{n}\geq 1
.
∎

We obtain the following immediate consequence of Theorem
B
.

Corollary 7.4

(= Corollary
D
)
.

If
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}\geq j+2
, then
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{j}\geq 2
.

This implies Conjecture
C
, because
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}\geq\depth\mathscr{O}_{X}
by
4.3
.

Combining Theorem
B
with Theorem
7.1
, we obtain a bound on which
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}
are actually needed to compute
\lcdef(X)
.

Corollary 7.5

(= Theorem
A
)
.

Fix an integer
k
. Then we have
\lcdef(X)\leq n-k
if and only if

\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}+p\geq k
for all

Proof.

The only if implication is clear by Theorem
7.1
. Conversely, suppose that
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}+p\geq k
for

. Theorem
B
says the same inequality holds for

\lcdef(X)\leq n-k
by Theorem

.
∎

This takes a particularly simple form for

Corollary 7.6
.

If
k\leq 3
, then
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}\geq k
if and only if
\lcdef(X)\leq n-k
.

Remark 7.7
.

We recover the following results of Ogus
[

]
and Dao-Takagi

: for

. Indeed, this follows directly from Corollary

The following example demonstrates that Corollary

Example 7.8

Let

be a smooth projective surface with ample line bundle

\displaystyle\lcdef(X)\leq n-2p(X)-1.

D.
Perversity Defect

8.
Perversity Defect

This final section is motivated by a comparison of
\lcdef(X)
with
n-\depth\mathscr{O}_{X}
. Ogus
[
Og73
]
showed
\depth\mathscr{O}_{X}\geq 2
implies
\lcdef(X)\leq n-2
, and Dao-Takagi
[
DT16
]
further proved
\depth\mathscr{O}_{X}\geq 3
implies
\lcdef(X)\leq n-3
. The pattern fails beyond this; see
[
DT16
, Example
2.11
]
or
[
KV25
]
for examples where
X
is a toric fourfold. Nevertheless, Dao-Takagi prove that if
\depth\mathscr{O}_{X}\geq 4
, then
\lcdef(X)\leq n-4
if and only if
X
has torsion local analytic Picard groups.

We aim to generalize this picture by asking what is needed to ensure
\lcdef(X)\leq n-k
, provided we only know
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}+p\geq k
for
p\leq m
for some fixed value of
m
. Our answer is phrased in terms of the perversity defects of Definition
5.6
.

Theorem 8.1

(= Theorem
F
)
.

Let
k
and
m
be integers. We have
\lcdef(X)\leq n-k
if and only if
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}+p\geq k
for all
p\leq m
and
\pdef(X,k-2m-4)\leq n-k
.

This will follow from a more precise result, relating the condition
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}+p\geq k
for
p\leq m
to the cohomology of small punctured neighborhoods.

Proposition 8.2
.

Let
k
and
m
be integers. Suppose
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}+p\geq k
for all
p\leq m
. If
x\in X
lies in a Whitney stratum of dimension
d_{S}\geq k-2m-3
, then

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{i}(U\setminus\{x\},\mathbb{Q})=0\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ for all }i\leq k-2+d_{S},

where
U
is a small Stein analytic neighborhood of
x
.

Proof.

Fix a Whitney stratification on
X
. Let
\iota:Y\rightarrow X
be the inclusion of a complete intersection of
k-2m-3
general hyperplanes. It induces a stratification on
Y
, such that each stratum
S
of
X
intersects
Y
transversally as a union of strata
T
. Moreover, if
y
lies in a stratum
T
of
Y
, there exists a small neighborhood
U
of
y
in
X
of the form
V\times B
, where
V\subset T
is a small neighborhood and
B
is a contractible ball. In particular,
U\setminus(U\cap S)
is homotopy equivalent to
V\setminus(V\cap T)
. Combining this with
[
PP25
, Lemma
15.10
]
, we have

(8.3)

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{i}(U\setminus\{y\},\mathbb{Q})

\displaystyle\simeq\tilde{H}^{i-2d_{S}}(U\setminus(U\cap S),\mathbb{Q})

\displaystyle\simeq\tilde{H}^{i-2d_{S}}(V\setminus(V\cap T),\mathbb{Q})

\displaystyle\simeq\tilde{H}^{i-2d_{S}+2d_{T}}(V\setminus\{y\},\mathbb{Q}).

Here,
d_{S}-d_{T}=\codim_{Y}X=k-2m-3
. We will continue using this notation throughout the proof.

Lemma
8.4
implies
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{Y}^{p}+p\geq 2m+3
for all
p\leq m
. By Theorem
A
, we deduce
\lcdef(Y)\leq\dim Y-2m-3
. In other words, by Lemma
5.8
,

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{i}(V\setminus\{y\},\mathbb{Q})=0\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ for all }i\leq 2m+1+d_{T}\text{ and all }y\in Y.

By
8.3
, this is equivalent to

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{i}(U\setminus\{y\},\mathbb{Q})=0\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ for all }i\leq k-2+d_{S}\text{ and all }y\in Y.

The result follows, because the cohomology of a punctured neighborhood depends only on the stratum of the removed point, and the strata
S
of
X
with
S\cap Y\neq\emptyset
are precisely those of dimension
d_{S}\geq k-2m-3
.
∎

We required the following lemma, showing the condition
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}+p\geq k
for
p\leq m
is well-behaved under general hyperplane sections.

Lemma 8.4
.

Let
k
and
m
be integers, and let
\iota:Y\rightarrow X
be a general hyperplane section. If
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}+p\geq k
for all
p\leq m
, then
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{Y}^{p}+p\geq k-1
for all
p\leq m
.

Proof.

Our assumption says precisely that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{j}\gr_{p}^{F}\dr(\mathbb{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}[n]))=0\hskip 5.69046pt\text{ for all }j\geq-k+1+n\text{ and }p\leq m.

Applying Lemma
6.1
and dualizing, we see this is equivalent to

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{-j}\gr_{-p}^{F}(\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}[n])=0\hskip 5.69046pt\text{ for all }j\geq-k+1+n\text{ and }p\leq m.

Pulling back by
\iota^{*}
and using the fact that
Y
is general,
[
Sa90
, Lemma
2.25
]
and
[
Sc14
, Theorem
9.3
]
, together with the identity
\iota^{*}\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}=\mathbb{Q}_{Y}^{H}
imply

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{-j}\gr_{-p}^{F}(\mathbb{Q}_{Y}^{H}[n-1])=0\hskip 5.69046pt\text{ for all }j\geq-k+1+n\text{ and }p\leq m.

Applying the same argument in reverse, we conclude the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
8.1
.

The only if direction follows from Theorem
7.1
and the inequalities
5.7
. For the converse, we need to show

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{i}(U\setminus\{x\},\mathbb{Q})=0\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ for all }i\leq k-2+d_{S}\text{ and all }x\in X.

For strata of dimension
d_{S}\geq k-2m-3
, this follows from the depth hypothesis due to Proposition
8.2
. Otherwise,
d_{S}\leq k-2m-4
, and the vanishing follows from the condition
\pdef(X,k-2m-4)\leq n-k
, interpreted in terms of cohomology groups of punctured neighborhoods, as in Lemma
5.8
.
∎

Theorem
F
has several interesting corollaries. For example, if
m=\lceil(k-3)/2\rceil
, the perversity defect hypothesis is vacuous, so the statement is equivalent to Theorem
A
. In the next case, taking
k=2m+4
, we obtain:

Corollary 8.5
.

Let
k
be an even integer. Then
\lcdef(X)\leq n-k
if and only if
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}+p\geq k
for all
p\leq(k-4)/2
and
\cdef(X)\leq n-k
.

Example 8.6

(Dao-Takagi Theorem for depth four)
.

We recover the motivating result of Dao-Takagi
[
DT16
]
: if
\depth\mathscr{O}_{X}\geq 4
, then
\lcdef(X)\leq n-4
if and only if
X
has torsion local analytic Picard groups.

Indeed, suppose
\depth\mathscr{O}_{X}\geq 4
. Then
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}\geq 4
and
\cdef(X)\leq\lcdef(X)\leq n-3
by Corollary
7.6
. Using Corollary
8.5
with
k=4
and interpreting
\cdef(X)
in terms of cohomology groups of punctured neighborhoods as in Lemma
5.8
, it suffices to show that
H^{2}(U\setminus\{x\},\mathbb{Q})
is isomorphic to
\Pic^{\anloc}(X,x)_{\mathbb{Q}}
for all points
x\in X
.

The exponential sequence for
U\setminus\{x\}
gives rise to a long exact sequence

\displaystyle\ldots H^{1}(U\setminus\{x\},\mathscr{O})

\displaystyle\rightarrow H^{1}(U\setminus\{x\},\mathscr{O}^{*})

\displaystyle\rightarrow H^{2}(U\setminus\{x\},\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H^{2}(U\setminus\{x\},\mathscr{O})\rightarrow\ldots

The depth assumption
\depth\mathscr{O}_{X}\geq 4
implies the two outermost terms vanish
[
ST06
, Theorem
1.14
]
, so
H^{2}(U\setminus\{x\},\mathbb{Z})\simeq H^{1}(U\setminus\{x\},\mathscr{O}^{*})
. The latter is isomorphic to
\Pic^{\anloc}(X,x)
by
[
Ko12
, Section
1
]
.

We can also take
m=0
in Theorem
F
to deduce:

Corollary 8.7
.

If
k
is an integer, then
\lcdef(X)\leq n-k
if and only if
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}\geq k
and
\pdef(X,k-4)\leq n-k
.

Example 8.8

(Park-Popa Theorem for depth five)
.

Let us discuss how this result in the case
k=5
implies
[
PP25
, Corollary G]
: if
n\geq 5
and
X
has rational locally analytically
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial singularities, then
\lcdef(X)\leq n-5
if and only if
X
has torsion local analytic gerbe groups. We make crucial use of the fact that rational locally analytically
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial singularities are preserved by general hyperplane sections
[
PP25
, Proposition
12.1
]
.

Under these hypotheses, we have
\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}=\depth\mathscr{O}_{X}=n
. Moreover,
X
has torsion local analytic Picard groups, so
\lcdef(X)\leq n-4
by Example
8.6
.

As in Example
8.6
, the exponential long exact sequence shows
X
has local analytic gerbe groups if and only if

\displaystyle H^{3}(U\setminus\{x\},\mathbb{Q})=0\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ for all }x\in X.

On the other hand, by Lemma
5.8
, we have
\pdef(X,1)\leq n-5
if and only if

(8.9)

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{i}(U\setminus\{x\},\mathbb{Q})=0\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ for all }i\leq 3+\min\{d_{S},1\}\text{ and all }x\in X.

We aim to show the equivalence of these conditions. Since
\lcdef(X)\leq n-4
, the only vanishing left to show is that of
H^{4}(U\setminus\{x\},\mathbb{Q})
, when
d_{S}=1
.

Let
\iota:Y\rightarrow X
be a general hyperplane section through
x
. Then
H^{4}(U\setminus\{x\},\mathbb{Q})\simeq H^{2}(V\setminus\{x\},\mathbb{Q})
by
8.3
, where
V\subset Y
is a small neighborhood of
x
. The latter cohomology group is exactly
\Pic^{\anloc}(Y,x)_{\mathbb{Q}}
by the argument in Example
8.6
. But
Y
has rational locally analytically
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial singularities, so its local analytic Picard groups are torsion. Hence
\Pic^{\anloc}(Y,x)_{\mathbb{Q}}=0
.

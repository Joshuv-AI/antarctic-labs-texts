Derived categories of Fano varieties of lines
Alessio Bottini & Daniel Huybrechts

Abstract
. We gather evidence for a conjecture of Galkin predicting the derived category of the Fano variety F X F_{X} of lines contained in a smooth cubic fourfold X ⊂ ℙ 5 X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5} to be equivalent to the Hilbert square 𝒜 X [ 2 ] {\cal A}_{X}^{[2]} of the Kuznetsov component 𝒜 X ⊂ D b ​ ( X ) {\cal A}_{X}\subset{\rm D}^{\rm b}(X) . We prove the conjecture for generic Fano varieties admitting a rational Lagrangian fibration and show that the natural Hodge structures of weight two associated with F X F_{X} and 𝒜 X [ 2 ] {\cal A}_{X}^{[2]} are isometric.

With any smooth cubic hypersurface
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
of dimension four there is associated
the Fano variety of lines
F_{X}
which is a projective hyperkähler manifold of dimension four deformation equivalent
to the Hilbert square
S^{[2]}
of a K3 surface
S
. While derived categories of K3 surfaces
have been studied intensively, notably by Mukai
[
36
]
, Orlov
[
38
]
, and Bridgeland
[
11
]
, not much is known about derived categories of higher-dimensional hyperkähler manifolds. The derived category
{\rm D}^{\rm b}(S^{[2]})
of the Hilbert square of a K3 surface is a good place to start and another interesting and accessible case is the derived category
{\rm D}^{\rm b}(F_{X})
of the Fano variety of lines.

Conjecturally, these two kinds of derived categories are related by means of the Hilbert square of the Kuznetsov component
{\cal A}_{X}
. By definition, the K3 category
{\cal A}_{X}\subset{\rm D}^{\rm b}(X)
is the admissible full triangulated sub-category of all complexes right orthogonal to the three line bundles
{\cal O}_{X},{\cal O}_{X}(1),{\cal O}_{X}(2)\in{\rm D}^{\rm b}(X)
, see
[
31
]
or
[
23
, Ch. 7.1]
. By work of Addington and Thomas
[
3
]
, we know that for cubics in a countable union of hypersurfaces
{\cal C}_{d}\subset{\cal C}
in the moduli space
{\cal C}
of all smooth cubic fourfolds the Kuznetsov component
{\cal A}_{X}
is in fact
equivalent to the derived category
{\rm D}^{\rm b}(S)
of a K3 surface
S
or, more generally, to the
derived category
{\rm D}^{\rm b}(S,\alpha)
of a K3 surface
S
twisted by a Brauer class
\alpha\in{\rm Br}(S)
, cf.
[
23
]
. Imitating the passage from a K3 surface
S
to its Hilbert square

S^{[2]}
, one associates with
{\cal A}_{X}\subset{\rm D}^{\rm b}(X)

its Hilbert square
{\cal A}_{X}^{[2]}\subset{\rm D}^{\rm b}(X^{[2]})
, cf. §​
1.1
. Note that
by
[
12
]
,

{\cal A}_{X}^{[2]}\simeq{\rm D}^{\rm b}(S^{[2]})
if
{\cal A}_{X}\simeq{\rm D}^{\rm b}(S)
.

The following conjecture has been formulated independently by various people but probably first in talks by Galkin almost ten years ago.

Conjecture 0.1
.

The Fano variety
F_{X}
of lines contained in a smooth cubic fourfold
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
is derived equivalent
to the Hilbert square of the Kuznetsov component, i.e. there exists an exact linear equivalence

(0.1)

{\rm D}^{\rm b}(F_{X})\simeq{\cal A}_{X}^{[2]}.

It is not difficult to see that
{\rm D}^{\rm b}(F_{X})
and
{\cal A}_{X}^{[2]}
define the same class in the Grothendieck ring of dg-enhanced triangulated categories, i.e.

[{\rm D}^{\rm b}(F_{X})]=[{\cal A}_{X}^{[2]}]\in K_{0}(\text{dg-cat}).

This observation goes back to Galkin and Shinder
[
15
]
and was upgraded by Belmans, Fu, and Raedschelders
[
9
]
to the statement that both categories are admissible
sub-categories of the same triangulated category
{\rm D}^{\rm b}(X^{[2]})

with their respective complements both built out of five copies of
{\rm D}^{\rm b}(X)
, cf.
[
24
, Ch. 7]
.

0.1.
The conjecture for dense sets of cubics

Let us start by first noting the following easy result which is certainly
known to the experts, cf.
[
40
]
. It proves Conjecture
0.1
for all smooth cubic fourfolds
X

contained in the countable
\bigcup_{\rm{(\ast\ast\ast)}}{\cal C}_{d}
union of all Hassett divisors
{\cal C}_{d}\subset{\cal C}
for which
d
satisfies the
numerical condition
\rm{(\ast\!\ast\!\ast)}
, i.e.
d
is of the form
(2n^{2}+2n+2)/a^{2}
for certain
n
and
a
, which geometrically means that the Fano variety
F_{X}
is birational to a Hilbert scheme, cf.
[
24
, Ch. 6.5]
.

Theorem 0.2
.

Assume the Fano variety
F_{X}
of a smooth cubic fourfold
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}

is birational to the Hilbert scheme
S^{[2]}
of a K3 surface. Then
{\rm D}^{\rm b}(F_{X})\simeq{\cal A}_{X}^{[2]}
.

Indeed, a birational correspondence between two four-dimensional hyperkähler manifolds
is a composition of simple Mukai flops which by results of Kawamata
[
28
]

and Namikawa
[
37
]
are known to induce derived equivalences, cf.
[
21
, Ch. 11]
.
Hence,
{\rm D}^{\rm b}(F_{X})\simeq{\rm D}^{\rm b}(S^{[2]})
. More general results have recently been proved by
Maulik, Shen, Yin, and Zhang
[
34
]
. On the other hand, the existence of a birational correspondence
F_{X}\sim S^{[2]}
also implies that
{\cal A}_{X}\simeq{\rm D}^{\rm b}(S)
. Indeed, by results of Addington
[
1
]
and Hassett
[
19
]
, the existence of a birational correspondence
F_{X}\sim S^{[2]}
is equivalent to
X
being contained in a Hassett divisor
{\cal C}_{d}
with
d
satisfying
\rm{(\ast\!\ast\!\ast)}
. However, this immediately implies that
X\in{\cal C}_{d}

with
d
satisfying Hassett’s condition
\rm{(\ast\ast)}
, i.e.
d/2=\prod p^{n_{p}}
with
n_{p}=0
for all
p\equiv 2\,(3)
and
n_{3}\leq 1
, which by the work of Addington and Thomas
[
3
]
mentioned before and further work of Bayer et al
[
5
]
implies
{\cal A}_{X}\simeq{\rm D}^{\rm b}(S)
for some K3 surface
S
(in fact for the same
S
for which
F_{X}\sim S^{[2]}
). Combined with the BKR-equivalence
{\rm D}^{\rm b}(S^{[2]})\simeq{\rm D}^{\rm b}(S)^{[2]}
, see
[
12
]
, we obtain

{\rm D}^{\rm b}(F_{X})\simeq{\rm D}^{\rm b}(S^{[2]})\simeq{\rm D}^{\rm b}(S)^{[2]}\simeq{\cal A}_{X}^{[2]}.

The main result of this note is the following theorem which proves Conjecture
0.1

for
X
contained in another countable union
\bigcup_{\rm(\ast\ast^{\prime\prime})}{\cal C}_{d}
of Hassett divisors.

Theorem 0.3
.

Assume the Fano variety
F_{X}
admits a rational Lagrangian fibration and is generic with this property. Then
{\rm D}^{\rm b}(F_{X})\simeq{\cal A}_{X}^{[2]}
.

The existence of a rational Lagrangian fibration of
F_{X}
is equivalent to requiring

X\in{\cal C}_{d}
with
d/2
a perfect square, see
[
24

, i.e.
d/2=\prod p^{n_{p}}
with
n_{p}\equiv 0\,(2)
for all
p
. This numerical condition is called
\rm(\ast\ast^{\prime\prime})
. According to
[
23
]
, the weaker condition
\rm(\ast\ast^{\prime})
that

only for all

is known to be equivalent to
{\cal A}_{X}\simeq{\rm D}^{\rm b}(S,\alpha)
for some twisted K3 surface
(S,\alpha)
. Again, this was originally proved only for generic
X
but now holds for all

by
[
5
]
.

Note the following chain of implications between these numerical conditions

\textstyle{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\checkmark}}}{{{\rm(\ast\!\ast\!\ast)}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\rm(\ast\ast)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\rm(\ast\ast^{\prime})}
\textstyle{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\checkmark}}}{{{\rm(\ast\ast^{\prime\prime})}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

We marked those for which, by Theorems
0.2
and

, Conjecture
0.1
has now been verified.
Naturally, one would expect the case
{\rm(\ast\ast)}
, i.e. when

to be the most accessible one among all cubics for Conjecture
0.1
. However, even this case remains open in full generality and the smallest such

for which the conjecture is not settled by the results above is

. It would be even more interesting to settle the conjecture when

{\cal A}_{X}
is of the form
{\rm D}^{\rm b}(S,\alpha)
, but not
{\rm D}^{\rm b}(S)
, so

holds but not

for which also

does not hold is

To the best of our knowledge, not a single smooth cubic fourfold

For every smooth cubic fourfold

and

in the moduli space of smooth cubic fourfolds

parametrises all those cubics

1.
The Hilbert square of the Kuznetsov component

The main goal of this article is to study the link between
{\rm D}^{\rm b}(F_{X})
and
{\cal A}_{X}^{[2]}

for a smooth cubic fourfold
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
. We focus on four-dimensional cubic hypersurfaces, as in any other dimension
the link between the two categories will be less direct. For example, for a smooth cubic threefold
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
the classes of these two categories in the Grothendieck group satisfy
[{\cal A}_{Y}^{[2]}]=[{\rm D}^{\rm b}(F_{Y})]+[{\rm D}^{\rm b}(Y)]\in K_{0}(\text{dg-cat})
.

In this section we shall recall the definition of the Hilbert square
{\cal A}_{X}^{[2]}
and introduce its Hodge structure. The main result is a description of
{\cal A}_{X}^{[2]}
in the case that
{\cal A}_{X}
is the derived category of a twisted K3 surface
(S,\alpha)
.

1.1.
Defining the Hilbert square

Symmetric powers of dg-categories have first been considered by Ganter and Kapranov
[
16
]
and Elagin
[
14
, Thm. 6.10]
,
see also the more recent treatment by Beckmann and Oberdieck
[
8
]
. For admissible sub-categories of derived
categories of coherent sheaves, the construction can be made more explicit and more geometric as follows.

Starting with the semi-orthogonal decomposition, cf.
[
24
, Ch. 7.3]
,

{\rm D}^{\rm b}(X)=\langle{\cal A}_{X},{\cal O}_{X},{\cal O}_{X}(1),{\cal O}_{X}(2)\rangle,

Kuznetsov
[
31
]
defines the admissible sub-category

{\cal A}_{X}\boxtimes{\cal A}_{X}\subset{\rm D}^{\rm b}(X\times X)
as the right orthogonal complement
of all objects of the form
{\cal F}\boxtimes{\cal O}_{X}(i)
and
{\cal O}_{X}(i)\boxtimes{\cal F}
with
i=0,1,2

and
{\cal F}\in{\rm D}^{\rm b}(X)
arbitrary. The natural
{\mathfrak{S}}_{2}
-action on
X\times X

defined by
\iota(x_{1},x_{2})=(x_{2},x_{1})

induces an action of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{2}
on
{\cal A}_{X}\boxtimes{\cal A}_{X}
and the Hilbert (or symmetric) square is defined as the equivariant category

{\cal A}_{X}^{[2]}\coloneqq({\cal A}_{X}\boxtimes{\cal A}_{X})_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{2}}.

Thus, objects in
{\cal A}_{X}^{[2]}
are objects in
{\cal A}_{X}\boxtimes{\cal A}_{X}\subset{\rm D}^{\rm b}(X\times X)
together with an
{\mathfrak{S}}_{2}
-linearisation and morphisms are the invariant
ones in
{\cal A}_{X}\boxtimes{\cal A}_{X}
. The category
{\cal A}_{X}^{[2]}
inherits the structure of a linear triangulated category, cf.
[
14
]
.

Remark 1.1
.

The higher symmetric powers
{\cal A}_{X}^{[n]}
are defined similarly by first defining

{\cal A}^{\,\boxtimes\,n}\subset{\rm D}^{\rm b}(X^{n})
and then taking its equivariant category with respect to the natural action of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
.
Popov
[
40
]
extends Galkin’s conjecture to the prediction that the LLSvS variety
Z_{X}
, a hyperkähler manifold deformation equivalent to the eight-dimensional Hilbert scheme of a K3 surface, cf.
[
32
]
, is derived equivalent to
{\cal A}_{X}^{[4]}
.

Let us also recall that
{\cal A}_{X}^{[2]}
comes in turn with an involution

1.2.
Twisted BKR

Bridgeland, King, and Reid
[
12
]
describe the derived category
{\rm D}^{\rm b}(S^{[n]})
of the Hilbert scheme of a K3 surface as the equivariant category of
S^{n}
with respect to the natural
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
-action. Since,
{\rm D}^{\rm b}(S^{n})\simeq{\rm D}^{\rm b}(S)^{\,\boxtimes\,n}
, we have

(1.1)

{\rm D}^{\rm b}(S^{[n]})\simeq{\rm D}^{\rm b}(S)^{[n]}.

We will need a twisted version of this fact. More precisely, if
S
comes with a Brauer class
\alpha\in{\rm Br}(S)
and

\alpha^{[n]}\in{\rm Br}(S^{[n]})
is the induced class via the natural isomorphism

{\rm Br}(S)\simeq{\rm Br}(S^{[n]})
, then the result we need compares

{\rm D}^{\rm b}(S^{[n]},\alpha^{[n]})
with the Hilbert product
{\rm D}^{\rm b}(S,\alpha)^{[n]}
.

Proposition 1.2
.

Any exact linear equivalence
{\cal A}_{X}\simeq{\rm D}^{\rm b}(S,\alpha)

naturally induces an exact linear equivalence

{\cal A}_{X}^{[n]}\simeq{\rm D}^{\rm b}(S^{[n]},\alpha^{[n]}).

Proof.

By definition,
{\cal A}_{X}^{[n]}
is the equivariant category of the
product

{\cal A}_{X}^{\,\boxtimes\,n}={\rm D}^{\rm b}(S,\alpha)^{\,\boxtimes\,n}\simeq{\rm D}^{\rm b}(S^{n},\alpha^{\times n})

with respect to the natural
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
-action, where we use the shorthand
\alpha^{\times n}\coloneqq\prod p_{i}^{\ast}\alpha
. We will use that there exists a Brauer class
\alpha^{(n)}\in{\rm Br}(S^{(n)})
that pulls back to

\alpha^{[n]}
and
\alpha^{\times n}
under the two projections

As a side remark, for a generic Pfaffian cubic fourfold Beauville and Donagi

3.2.
Comparison to the Beckmann–Taelman twist

For a general hyperkähler manifold
Z
, even when assumed to be of
\text{K3}^{[n]}
-type, the naive
weight two Hodge structure
\widetilde{H}(Z,\mathbb{Z})=H^{2}(Z,\mathbb{Z})\oplus U
is not necessarily a derived
invariant. However, as shown by Taelman
[
41
]
for
n=2
and later by Beckman

[
7
]
for arbitrary
n
, a certain modification of it is.

We briefly recall this construction for
n=2
, which makes use (but is ultimately independent) of an additional class
\delta\in H^{2}(Z,\mathbb{Z})
with
(\delta)^{2}=-2
and

\text{div}(\delta)=2
. Instead of
\widetilde{H}(Z,\mathbb{Z})
one considers its image

\Lambda_{Z}\coloneqq B_{-\delta/2}(\widetilde{H}(Z,\mathbb{Z}))\subset\widetilde{H}(Z,\mathbb{Q})\coloneqq\widetilde{H}(Z,\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathbb{Q}

under the B-field transform

\textstyle{B_{-\delta/2}\colon\widetilde{H}(Z,\mathbb{Z})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\widetilde{H}(Z,\mathbb{Q})}

equipped with the weight two Hodge structure induced by the one on
\widetilde{H}(Z,\mathbb{Q})
.
Recall that the B-field transform
B_{-\delta/2}
sends
\lambda\in H^{2}(Z,\mathbb{Z})
to
\lambda-(\delta.\lambda)/2\cdot f\in H^{2}(Z,\mathbb{Z})\oplus U
,
e\in U
to
-\delta/2+e-(1/4)\cdot f
, and preserves
f
. For the Mukai lattice of a K3 surface,
B_{\gamma}

is nothing but multiplication with
\exp(\gamma)
, which might help to memorise these formulae.

So, as a lattice,
\Lambda_{Z}
is isometric to
\widetilde{H}(Z,\mathbb{Z})
via
B_{-\delta/2}
. However, unless
\delta
is of type
(1,1)
, the isometry
B_{-\delta/2}
does not respect the Hodge structure. Note that for
Z=S^{[2]}
one can choose
\delta
such that
2\delta
is the class of the exceptional divisor of the Hilbert–Chow morphism. Thus, in this case the naive Hodge structure is Hodge isometric to the
Beckmann–Taelman twist:

\widetilde{H}(S^{[2]},\mathbb{Z})\simeq\Lambda_{S^{[2]}}.

This can be generalised easily to the following.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume the class
-\delta/2
is of the form
\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}

with
\gamma_{1}\in H^{1,1}(Z,\mathbb{Q})
and
\gamma_{2}\in H^{2}(Z,\mathbb{Z})
. Then there
exists a Hodge isometry between the naive Hodge structure and the
Beckmann–Taelman twist:

(3.2)

\widetilde{H}(Z,\mathbb{Z})\simeq\Lambda_{Z}.

Proof.

Under the given assumptions, the B-field transform

B_{-\delta/2}
is the composition of the two B-field transforms associated with
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
, i.e.
B_{-\delta/2}=B_{\gamma_{1}}\circ B_{\gamma_{2}}
.

Since
\gamma_{2}
is integral, the B-field transform
B_{\gamma_{2}}
is invertible and so
B_{\gamma_{2}}(\widetilde{H}(Z,\mathbb{Z}))=\widetilde{H}(Z,\mathbb{Z})
as
sublattices of
\widetilde{H}(Z,\mathbb{Q})
. Furthermore, since
\gamma_{1}
is algebraic,

B_{\gamma_{1}}\colon B_{\gamma_{2}}(\widetilde{H}(Z,\mathbb{Z}))\lx@xy@svg{\hbox{\raise 2.5pt\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\hbox{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\hbox{\vtop{\halign{\entry@#!@&&\entry@@#!@\cr&\crcr}}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern-1.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\raise-2.5pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 1.375pt\raise 4.34428pt\hbox{{}\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 3.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise-1.34428pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}}\kern 3.0pt}}}}}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 14.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\lx@xy@tip{1}\lx@xy@tip{-1}}}}}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\kern 14.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\raise-2.5pt\hbox{$\textstyle{}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces}}}}\ignorespaces\Lambda_{Z}
is a Hodge isometry. Combining both observations proves the assertion.
∎

The lemma allows us to prove a version of (
3.2
) for Fano varieties of lines.

Proposition 3.4
.

For the Fano variety
F_{X}
of lines contained in a smooth cubic fourfold, there exists a Hodge isometry
between the naive Hodge structure and the Beckmann–Taelman twist:

\widetilde{H}(F_{X},\mathbb{Z})\simeq\Lambda_{F_{X}}.

Proof.

Denote by
g\in H^{2}(F_{X},\mathbb{Z})
the class of the Plücker polarisation.
If
-\delta/2
can be written as
-\delta/2=p\cdot g+\gamma
with

p\in\mathbb{Q}
and
\gamma\in H^{2}(F_{X},\mathbb{Z})
, then the lemma applies and proves the claim.

There are various ways to achieve this. For example, according to Beauville and Donagi
[
6
]
, when specialising
X
to a Pfaffian cubic,
the Fano variety of lines
F_{X}
specialises to the Hilbert scheme
S^{[2]}
of a polarised K3 surface
(S,h)
of degree
14
. Furthermore, under the induced Hodge isometry
the Plücker polarisation

g
becomes
2h-5\delta
, cf.
[
24
, Ch. 6.2.4]
. In other words,

-\delta/2=g/2+2\delta-h
, which still holds after parallel transport back to the original
F_{X}
.
∎

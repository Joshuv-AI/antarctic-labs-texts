Abstract
Lifetimes for the 4 1 + {4}_{1}^{+} and 6 1 + {6}_{1}^{+} states have been directly measured using γ − γ \gamma-\gamma fast timing technique for low lying states of X 130 ​ e {}^{130}Xe , populated from β − \beta^{-} decay of the parent I 130 {}^{130}I produced through U n ​ a ​ t ​ ( α , f ) {}^{nat}U(\alpha,f) reaction at E α = 40 ​ M ​ e ​ V E_{\alpha}~=~40~MeV . VENTURE array comprising of eight fast CeBr 3 detectors is used for lifetime measurement. The reduced transition probabilities deduced from the measured lifetimes have been compared with theoretical values obtained from the large basis shell model calculation and the interacting boson approximation (IBM) calculation.

Introduction

Nuclear structure studies around the Z=50, N=82 double shell closure plays a vital role in understanding nucleon-nucleon interactions and its role in the evolution of nuclear shapes
[
1
,
2
,
3
]

[
4

5

6

7

The structure of neutron rich
Xe

and
Z=64

[

9

\gamma

N=82

[

13

suggest prolate deformation with a deviation from the
O(6)
symmetry
[
14
]
. The systematics of the M1 transition strengths as a function of the mass number hinted for a phase transition near A=130
[
10
]
. The interacting boson fermion model also describes the energy systematics in odd-A
Xe
isotopes with the combination of spherical and gamma unstable shape and that
{}^{129-131}Xe
are candidates for shape phase transitions from

to
SU(5)

[

]
.
Initially

[

. However, later it was concluded that

[

Central to all these observations, understanding the electromagnetic transition strengths and their ratios are required for interpreting the nuclear shape, shape transitions and critical point symmetry features of the quantum phase transition
[

3
Data Analysis and Results

The measurement of nuclear level lifetime (
\tau\pm\delta\tau
) using GCD method can be represented with the following set of equations.

\displaystyle\tau=\frac{1}{2}[\Delta C_{FEP}-PRD]

(1)

\displaystyle\delta\tau=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(\delta\Delta C_{exp})^{2}+(\delta t_{corr})^{2}+(\delta PRD)^{2}}

(2)

where the
\Delta C_{FEP}
represents the true centroid differences between the delayed and anti-delayed time difference distributions of a
\gamma-\gamma
cascade, therefore, considers only photopeak-photopeak coincidences. However, the delayed and anti-delayed time difference distributions are experimentally obtained from the analysis of Time to Amplitude Converter (TAC) data that is projected with the selection of
\gamma
energy gates in the ‘start’ and ‘stop’ detectors of the array. Therefore, as the corresponding photopeak selections are polluted with the contributions from the underlying Compton background, the experimental centroid differences (
\Delta C_{exp}
) between the delayed and anti-delayed time difference distributions need to be corrected to obtain
\Delta C_{FEP}
. The said background corrections (
t_{corr}
) are obtained following the methodology described in Ref.
[
30
]
. The following sets of equation describes the determination of
t_{corr}
and consequently,
\Delta C_{FEP}
from
\Delta C_{exp}
.

\displaystyle t_{corr}=\frac{p/b(E_{decay}).t_{corr}(feeder)+p/b(E_{feeder}).t_{corr}(decay)}{p/b(E_{feeder})+p/b(E_{decay})}

\displaystyle{\text{\>}where}

(3)

\displaystyle t_{corr}(feeder)=[\frac{\Delta C_{exp}-\Delta C_{BG}}{p/b}]_{feeder}

\displaystyle t_{corr}(decay)=[\frac{\Delta C_{exp}-\Delta C_{BG}}{p/b}]_{decay}

\displaystyle\Delta C_{FEP}=\Delta C_{exp}+t_{corr}

(4)

The peak to background ratios
(p/b)
, required to determine the background corrections for feeder and decay (
t_{corr}(feeder),\>t_{corr}(decay)
), are estimated from the gated energy projections corresponding to feeder and decay. The centroid differences,
\Delta
C
BG
s, for feeder and decay are determined from the polynomial fitting of the centroid differences obtained from the time difference distributions of corresponding Compton-photopeak coincidences. The errors in lifetime
\tau
are determined from the propagation of errors in different experimentally obtained parameters, viz.,
\delta\Delta C_{exp},\>\delta PRD\>\textrm{and}\>\delta t_{corr}
.

4
Discussion

The reduced transition probabilities B(E2) corresponding to the decay of low lying levels in
130
Xe have been deduced from the measured lifetimes. For this purpose, the branching ratios for a particular level was taken from the ENSDF database
[
32
]
and the conversion coefficient for a particular transition was calculated using the BrIcc code
[
33
]
. The asymmetric uncertainties in the B(E2) values have been calculated due to large uncertainties in the measured lifetime values.

The level structure and the electromagnetic transition rates have been interpreted with large basis shell model (LBSM) and interacting Boson approximation (IBM) calculations.

The shell model calculations have performed using NushellX code
[
34
]
. For LSBM calculation, the proton and neutron particles were distributed over the 50 - 82 subshell space comprising of 1g
7/2
, 2d
5/2
, 2d
3/2
, 3s
1/2
, 1h
11/2
states above the
100
Sn core without any restriction and the
sn100pn
interaction
[
36
]
have been used. The reduced transition probabilities have been calculated using the proton and the neutron effective charges as e
p
=1.68, e
n
= 0.8 respectively
[
19
]
.

For IBM calculation, PHINT package has been used
[
35
]
.The IBM-1 Hamiltonian consists of four terms
[
37
]
corresponding to d-boson energy, pairing interaction, quadrupole-quadrupole interaction and angular momentum interaction respectively. The coefficients of the four different terms are used as EPS=1056 keV, PAIR= 36 keV, QQ=0, ELL=0.

The low lying excitations of
130
Xe in comparison to the calculated energy values using LBSM and IBM calculations are shown in Fig.
5
. The reduced transition probabilities deduced from the present experiment are shown in Fig.
6
in comparison with the calculated values.

Summary

The
\gamma-\gamma
fast timing technique has been employed for direct measurement of level lifetimes for
{4}_{1}^{+}
and
{6}_{1}^{+}
state in
{}^{130}Xe
. The B(E2) corresponding to the decay of
{4}_{1}^{+}
and
{6}_{1}^{+}
levels in
130
Xe have been deduced from the measured lifetimes. The measured lifetime for
{4}_{1}^{+}
corroborates the previous measurement and this work reports the first direct measurement of level lifetimes for
{6}_{1}^{+}
. The deduced transition probabilities B(E2) in
130
Xe are in agreement with shell model and IBM calculations.

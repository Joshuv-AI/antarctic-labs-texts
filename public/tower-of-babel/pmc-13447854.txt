Gamification elements and student engagement in higher education using fuzzy DEMATEL analysis
Authors: Xiaoyan Wang, Huizong Li, Anfeng Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Understanding how gamification elements interact to influence student engagement and learning outcomes in higher education remains a significant challenge due to the complexity and uncertainty of these relationships. This research aims to analyze the causal interdependencies among key gamification elements and their combined effects on educational outcomes. To address this, the Fuzzy Decision-Making Trial and Evaluation Laboratory (DEMATEL) method is employed to capture both direct and indirect relationships while accounting for the ambiguity in human judgment. Data were collected from 50 undergraduate and postgraduate students with experience in gamified learning environments. The findings indicate that feedback is a dominant driving factor, exerting a strong causal relationship on competition (0.45) and rewards (0.50). Competition further strengthens rewards (0.55) and leaderboards (0.60), while rewards promote engagement with challenges (0.50). The novelty of this research lies in applying Fuzzy DEMATEL to systematically model the interdependencies among core elements of gamification in higher education. The results offer practical implications for educators, providing a structured basis for designing effective gamified learning strategies that enhance student engagement and improve learning outcomes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-55247-9.

Introduction and literature review

Gamification has proven particularly effective in blended learning environments that combine in-person and online teaching1, 2. The adoption of gamification in the education sector is motivated by persistent challenges, including low student engagement, declining motivation, and limited interaction in traditional learning environments3, 4, 5. Digital learning contexts further intensify these issues by reducing direct social and instructional presence6, 7. Gamification is therefore adopted to introduce structured motivation, immediate feedback, and interactive learning experiences that enhance participation and sustain learner interest8, 9, 10.

The rise of digital technologies has further driven the use of educational games, offering flexibility, cost-effectiveness, and portability across different teaching models, such as flipped classrooms and traditional approaches11, 12. By integrating game elements like points, leaderboards, and badges, educators strive to overcome student disengagement and lack of motivation, thereby enhancing focus, performance, and the overall learning experience13. Gamifying learning activities has demonstrated its effectiveness in boosting student engagement and improving learning outcomes14. This approach has been incredibly successful in primary and secondary education, providing a more practical and accessible alternative to fully designed educational games15, 16.

Gamified learning environments create a more enjoyable and interactive experience and improve communication skills, critical thinking, and academic performance17, 18. Although gamification has been shown to enhance student motivation and learning performance, its effectiveness can vary depending on students’ design and individual characteristics, such as their learning styles and preferences19. However, existing studies primarily rely on descriptive or linear approaches and do not adequately capture the causal interdependencies among gamification elements20. The selection of the Decision-Making Trial and Evaluation Laboratory (DEMATEL) technique is justified by its ability to model causal relationships among factors rather than only ranking or weighting them, as in many other Multi-Criteria Decision-Making (MCDM) methods such as AHP or TOPSIS. Unlike these approaches, DEMATEL distinguishes between cause-and-effect factors and captures both direct and indirect influences, making it particularly suitable for analyzing the interdependent structure of gamification elements.

Fuzzy DEMATEL, with its ability to model complex decision-making processes, can bridge this research gap by improving the integration of gamification across various educational environments21. While numerous studies highlight the benefits of gamification in blended learning, little research examines how teachers can effectively employ these strategies to enhance student engagement. The absence of comprehensive frameworks for assessing these interactions further underscores the need for Fuzzy DEMATEL to elucidate the relationships among gamified elements, teaching strategies, and student outcomes in hybrid learning environments22. Fuzzy sets are incorporated to address the ambiguity and subjectivity inherent in human judgments, especially when evaluating educational experiences. While recent studies have employed advanced extensions such as Fermatean fuzzy sets, grey systems, and Pythagorean fuzzy sets, this study adopts classical fuzzy sets due to their interpretability, computational simplicity, and suitability for relatively small sample sizes. This choice ensures methodological clarity while maintaining robustness in capturing uncertain perceptions of gamification elements23.

Gamification, defined as the application of game elements in non-game contexts, has gained increasing attention in education to enhance student motivation and engagement. Core elements such as points, badges, leaderboards, and challenges are widely used to create interactive learning environments that encourage participation and improve learning outcomes1, 24. Empirical studies suggest that gamification can positively influence engagement, self-regulation, and collaborative learning. However, its effectiveness remains inconsistent, as outcomes depend on instructional design, learner characteristics, and the alignment of game elements with educational objectives. These mixed findings highlight the need for a structured approach to better understand how specific gamification elements interact and contribute to learning performance. In this study, feedback, competition, rewards, challenges, and leaderboards are selected as the key factors, as illustrated in Fig. 1, due to their central role in shaping student engagement in gamified learning environments.

Theoretical perspectives further support the importance of these elements. Self-Determination Theory explains how motivation is influenced by autonomy, competence, and relatedness, which are reinforced by feedback and rewards25. The Community of Inquiry framework emphasizes the role of social and cognitive interaction, reflected in elements such as competition and leaderboards26. The Engagement-Opportunity framework highlights the importance of meaningful challenges in sustaining behavioral and cognitive engagement, while Relational Motivation Theory underscores the role of interaction and feedback in strengthening motivation27. Although these frameworks provide valuable insights, prior studies have largely examined gamification elements in isolation and have relied on descriptive or linear approaches. Research on the causal interdependencies among these factors under conditions of uncertainty remains limited. This gap motivates the need for analytical methods capable of modeling complex relationships among the selected elements.

Previous studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of fuzzy logic in educational decision-making. Gokmen et al. (2010) showed that fuzzy logic provides a more flexible and nuanced student performance evaluation than traditional methods28, whereas the present study extends this by modeling causal relationships among gamification elements. Yıldız and Baba (2014) developed a fuzzy decision support system that improved evaluation reliability in laboratory settings29, while this research focuses on interdependencies rather than evaluation accuracy alone. Ravichandran et al. (2016) applied fuzzy MCDM to identify stressors in education30, whereas the current work analyzes motivational elements in learning environments. Ilahi et al. (2018) highlighted fuzzy logic’s ability to handle uncertainty in educational decision-making31, which is further operationalized here through causal modeling using DEMATEL. Ajoi et al. (2021) proposed a fuzzy-based system for evaluating leadership performance32, while this study shifts the focus to student engagement dynamics. Mousse et al. (2023) used fuzzy logic to assess learning outcomes in e-learning environments33, whereas this research examines how multiple factors interact to influence those outcomes. Li (2024) applied fuzzy multi-objective methods to financial decision-making in higher education34, while the present study contributes by integrating fuzzy logic with DEMATEL to capture complex interdependencies among gamification elements.

Understanding the interrelationships among key gamification elements including feedback, competition, rewards, challenges, and leaderboards is essential for enhancing student engagement and learning outcomes in higher education. This study aims to identify the most influential factors and analyze their causal interactions using the Fuzzy Decision-Making Trial and Evaluation Laboratory method. By integrating fuzzy logic with DEMATEL, uncertainty in human judgment is addressed, enabling a structured analysis of complex relationships. The novelty lies in modeling causal interdependencies among core gamification elements, which are often examined in isolation in prior studies. The findings provide a quantitative framework and practical guidance for designing more effective gamified learning strategies. To guide the analysis of interdependencies among key gamification elements and their interaction on student engagement and learning outcomes, the following research questions are formulated:

RQ1: What causal relationships exist among feedback, competition, rewards, challenges, and leaderboards in higher education?

RQ2: Which gamification elements exert the greatest interaction within the system?

RQ3: How do these interdependencies collectively affect student engagement and learning outcomes?

Methodology

The Fuzzy Decision-Making Trial and Evaluation Laboratory (DEMATEL) method is employed to analyze causal relationships among factors within complex systems. Developed to model interdependencies, DEMATEL enables the identification of cause-and-effect factors through a structured representation of direct and indirect interactions. In this study, the method is combined with fuzzy logic to address uncertainty and subjectivity in human judgments, which are common in evaluating gamification elements and their notable relationship student engagement and learning outcomes. This integration enables a more realistic and systematic analysis of the relationships among feedback, competition, rewards, challenges, and leaderboards35, 36. The research design ensures alignment among objectives, data collection, and analytical procedures. It involves defining research objectives, identifying key gamification elements, developing the questionnaire, sampling and data collection, preprocessing through fuzzification and aggregation, applying the Fuzzy DEMATEL method, and interpreting the resulting interdependencies. Questionnaire design and data collection

A structured questionnaire was developed to examine the influence of gamification elements on student engagement and learning outcomes. Data were collected from 50 students (25 undergraduate and 25 postgraduate) at Nanyang Normal University, China. A purposive sampling strategy was employed to include participants with prior experience in gamified learning environments and diverse academic backgrounds. Participants were selected using purposive sampling to ensure prior exposure to gamified learning environments and to capture diverse academic backgrounds. Participants were required to have exposure to at least one gamified learning platform to ensure informed evaluation of the selected elements. The questionnaire consisted of two sections. The first section captured demographic information, while the second section measured perceptions of five key gamification elements: feedback, competition, rewards, challenges, and leaderboards. These elements, which constitute the study’s criteria, are presented schematically in Fig. 1. Each construct was measured using multiple items on a five-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (no influence) to 5 (very high influence). The items were adapted from established studies to ensure content validity. A pilot test was conducted with a small group of students to assess clarity and reliability. Minor revisions were made prior to full deployment. Data were collected online, and informed consent was obtained from all participants in accordance with institutional ethical guidelines. The collected data were used to construct the direct-relation matrix required for Fuzzy DEMATEL analysis. Quantitative responses provided the basis for modeling causal relationships among the selected elements. The complete questionnaire is provided as supplementary material to ensure the study’s transparency and reproducibility.

The results highlight a complex web of influences among these elements, reflecting their intertwined roles in shaping educational experiences. Feedback was found to have a notable notable relationship both competition and rewards. This result aligns with Hattie and Timperley’s (2007) research, which emphasizes the crucial role of feedback in clarifying performance expectations and enhancing motivation37. Feedback helps students understand their progress, increases participation in competitive activities, and makes rewards more appealing. Effective feedback mechanisms can, therefore, elevate the perceived value of rewards, making them more effective motivators by ensuring that students recognize the connection between their efforts and the rewards they receive. The competition was shown to significantly influence rewards and leaderboards. This finding is consistent with Deci, Koestner, and Ryan’s (1999) work38, which suggests that competitive environments can amplify the motivational impact of rewards by creating a sense of urgency and achievement. In our analysis, this relationship was especially pronounced, indicating that incorporating competitive elements can enhance the effectiveness of rewards. Additionally, the impact of competition on leaderboards supports Anderson et al. (2014), who found that public recognition through leaderboards fosters a competitive spirit and boosts motivation. When combined with competitive elements, leaderboards can effectively drive engagement by acknowledging top performers, further motivating students to excel39. The influence of rewards on challenges and leaderboards underscores their role in enhancing student engagement. According to Deterding et al. (2011), rewards provide immediate gratification, encouraging students to take on more challenging tasks and enhancing their learning experience40. Our findings reflect this, showing that rewards motivate students and increase their engagement with complex tasks. The relationship between rewards and leaderboards also suggests that recognition through leaderboards is more motivating when accompanied by tangible rewards. Challenges were found to affect leaderboards, indicating that well-designed challenges can make leaderboard positions more coveted and relevant. In summary, the Fuzzy DEMATEL analysis reveals the intricate interplay between gamification factors and their notable relationship student engagement and learning outcomes. The strong interdependencies among feedback, competition, rewards, challenges, and leaderboards suggest that effective gamification strategies should leverage these relationships to create more engaging and motivating educational environments. By understanding how these elements interact, educators can design interventions that optimize student motivation and achievement, aligning with and extending existing literature on gamification in education.

The analysis of gamification elements revealed critical interdependencies among feedback, competition, rewards, challenges, and leaderboards. Feedback emerged as a central element influencing competition and rewards by clarifying performance expectations and enhancing motivation. Competition, in turn, amplifies the effectiveness of rewards and drives engagement through leaderboards. Rewards motivate students to tackle challenges and improve the appeal of leaderboard positions. Discussion

The findings of this study support and extend existing literature on gamification in education. While prior research has established the individual effects of gamification elements such as feedback, competition, and rewards on student motivation and engagement24, 25, 26, 27, these elements have often been examined in isolation using linear or descriptive approaches. In contrast, the Fuzzy Decision-Making Trial and Evaluation Laboratory method enables the analysis of causal interdependencies among these elements, providing a more comprehensive understanding of their combined influence on learning outcomes.

One key strength of the Fuzzy DEMATEL approach lies in its ability to address ambiguity and subjectivity in human judgment. In educational contexts, perceptions of gamification elements can vary among learners, necessitating methods that handle uncertainty. The integration of fuzzy logic facilitates the transformation of subjective evaluations into quantifiable measures, enabling more reliable analysis of complex relationships. This capability is particularly important, as traditional statistical methods may overlook indirect and interdependent effects among factors.

Furthermore, the results demonstrate that gamification elements operate within a network of causal relationships rather than as independent components. For instance, feedback not only directly influences student motivation but also enhances the effectiveness of rewards and competitive elements, consistent with prior findings on the role of feedback in learning processes24, 41. Similarly, the influence of competition on rewards and leaderboards aligns with studies that highlight the motivational impact of competitive environments25, 26, while the role of rewards in encouraging engagement with challenges aligns with earlier work on incentive-based learning27, 42. However, unlike previous studies, this research reveals how these elements interact simultaneously within a structured causal system.

By integrating fuzzy logic with causal modeling, the Fuzzy DEMATEL approach provides a systematic framework for identifying key driving factors and their interdependencies. This contributes to a more realistic representation of gamified learning environments and offers practical insights for designing effective educational interventions. Overall, the findings enhance the current understanding of gamification by moving beyond isolated analyses toward a holistic perspective on interconnected elements that influence student engagement and learning outcomes.

Theoretical and practical implications

The findings of this study provide both theoretical and practical implications for gamification research and educational practice. From a theoretical perspective, this study contributes to the existing literature by moving beyond the isolated examination of gamification elements and demonstrating their causal interdependencies within a unified analytical framework. By applying the Fuzzy Decision-Making Trial and Evaluation Laboratory method, the study extends prior research that primarily relies on descriptive or linear approaches. The results also reinforce and integrate established theoretical perspectives, including Self-Determination Theory and the Community of Inquiry framework, by empirically illustrating how feedback, competition, and rewards interact within a complex system. This contributes to a more comprehensive understanding of motivation and engagement in gamified learning environments.

From a practical perspective, the findings provide actionable insights for educators and institutional decision-makers. The identification of feedback as a primary driver suggests that educational platforms should prioritize timely, structured feedback mechanisms to enhance student engagement. In addition, the strong relationships among competition, rewards, and leaderboards indicate that these elements should be implemented in a coordinated manner rather than independently. Designing balanced gamification strategies that align challenges with rewards and visible performance indicators can improve student motivation and participation. These insights support the development of more effective and evidence-based gamified learning environments in higher education.

Conclusion

The causal interrelationships among key gamification elements, including feedback, competition, rewards, challenges, and leaderboards, are examined using the Fuzzy Decision-Making Trial and Evaluation Laboratory method. The results indicate that feedback is the primary driving factor, exerting a strong causal relationship on competition (0.45) and rewards (0.50). Competition further strengthens rewards (0.55) and leaderboards (0.60), while rewards promote engagement with challenges (0.50). These findings demonstrate that gamification elements operate as an interdependent system rather than isolated components. Emphasis on effective feedback mechanisms, supported by competitive structures and reward-based strategies, is essential for enhancing student engagement and learning outcomes in higher education.

This study has several limitations to consider. First, the use of self-reported data may introduce bias and subjectivity, thereby affecting the accuracy of the findings. Second, the sample is limited to a single institution, which may restrict the generalizability of the results to other educational contexts. Third, the cross-sectional design does not capture the long-term effects of gamification on student engagement and learning outcomes. Future research should extend this work by examining gamification elements across diverse educational contexts and institutional settings to improve generalizability. Longitudinal designs are recommended to capture the dynamic evolution of causal relationships over time. In addition, integrating individual learner characteristics, such as motivation levels, learning styles, and prior experience, may provide a deeper understanding of differential responses to gamification strategies. Future studies may also explore the application of advanced fuzzy extensions or hybrid decision-making approaches to further enhance the robustness of causal analysis in complex educational environments.

HLA Class I Haplotype in Glutamic Acid Decarboxylase Antibody‐Associated Neurological Disease
Authors: Lin Bai, Haitao Ren, Qiang Lu, Han Zhang, Yang Yu, Zhuo Yang, Xinzhuang Yang, Siyuan Fan, Xinhua Wan, Yicheng Zhu, Hongzhi Guan, Meng Xiao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTObjectGlutamic acid decarboxylase antibody‐associated neurological disease (GADAND) is a rare autoimmune disorder with elusive immunogenetic underpinnings. We aimed to investigate HLA associations with GADAND susceptibility in a Chinese cohort.MethodsWe performed HLA genotyping at the HLA‐A, ‐B, ‐C, ‐DRB1, ‐DQA1, and ‐DQB1 loci in 62 Chinese patients with GADAND and compared them with 990 healthy controls. Haplotype frequencies were estimated using the expectation–maximization (EM) algorithm. Allele and haplotype associations with disease susceptibility and clinical phenotypes were assessed.ResultsWe identified the HLA class I haplotype A*11:01 ~ B*40:01 ~ C*07:02 as a susceptibility haplotype for GADAND (OR = 10.22, 95% CI 3.86–25.57, p adj = 0.003), and the association was stronger for the extended haplotype including DQA1*01:03 (OR = 25.61, 95% CI 4.99–89.11, p adj < 0.001). Three haplotypes showed suggestive but non‐significant associations: B*44:02 ~ C*05:01 (OR = 8.32, 95% CI 2.52–24.48, p adj = 0.069), DQA1*01:03 ~ DQB1*06:01 ~ DRB1*08:03 (OR = 3.15, 95% CI 1.67–5.63, p adj = 0.066), and DQA1*05:05 ~ DQB1*03:01, which showed a suggestive protective effect (OR = 0.15, 95% CI 0.018–0.556, p adj = 0.088). T1DM risk haplotypes, including DQA1*03:01 ~ DQB1*03:02 ~ DRB1*04:03 (OR = 5.04, 95% CI 1.18–16.62, p adj = 0.744) and DQA1*05:01 ~ DQB1*02:01 ~ DRB1*03:01 (OR = 2.50, 95% CI 1.07–5.24, p adj = 0.829), were more frequent in GADAND patients, and A*11:01 ~ B*40:01 ~ C*07:02 was enriched in those with comorbid AITD. No statistically significant associations were identified between HLA haplotypes and clinical phenotypes.ConclusionsThe haplotype A*11:01 ~ B*40:01 ~ C*07:02 was associated with GADAND susceptibility in the Chinese population, implicating T cell involvement. T1DM‐associated haplotypes were overrepresented in GADAND, and A*11:01 ~ B*40:01 ~ C*07:02 was enriched in comorbid AITD, suggesting HLA as a shared immunogenetic basis for autoimmune comorbidities. No significant HLA associations with clinical phenotypes were identified, warranting validation in larger cohorts.Keywords: autoimmune, epilepsy, glutamic acid decarboxylase, HLA genes, stiff‐person syndrome

Introduction

Glutamic acid decarboxylase antibody–associated neurological disease (GADAND) is a group of rare neuroimmunological disorders. It comprises three main clinical phenotypes—limbic encephalitis/epilepsy (LE/EP), stiff‐person syndrome (SPS), and cerebellar ataxia (CA)—which may overlap in some patients [1]. Although the pathogenesis remains unclear, clinical observations suggest a role for genetic susceptibility, supported by familial aggregation [2] and the frequent coexistence of other autoimmune diseases [3] —such as type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1DM), autoimmune thyroid disease (AITD), and vitiligo—that are associated with specific human leukocyte antigen (HLA) genes [4, 5, 6, 7].

HLA genes, located on chromosome 6p21.3, encode molecules that present antigenic peptides to T cell receptors (TCRs). HLA class I molecules (HLA‐A, ‐B, and ‐C) present endogenous peptides to CD8+ T cells, whereas class II molecules (e.g., HLA‐DR and ‐DQ) present exogenous peptides to CD4+ T cells [8]. Polymorphisms in the peptide‐binding groove determine peptide‐binding specificity and influence T cell activation, thereby modulating immune responses. Thus, HLA susceptibility is critical for understanding autoimmune disease pathogenesis.

Previous studies have reported associations between HLA alleles or haplotypes and GADAND, such as DQA1*05:01 ~ DQB1*02:01 ~ DRB1*03:01 and DQA1*03:01 ~ DQB1* 03:02 ~ DRB1*04:01 [9, 10]. However, several gaps remain: HLA profiles vary geographically, with limited data from Chinese patients; in addition, most studies have focused on HLA class II genes, leaving the role of class I genes insufficiently characterized; furthermore, associations between HLA genes and clinical phenotype remain unexplored.

We performed HLA genotyping in a Chinese cohort of patients with GADAND to identify susceptibility alleles and haplotypes, evaluate the contribution of HLA class I genes to disease susceptibility, and assess their associations with clinical phenotypes.

Methods

Patients

Patients were enrolled at Peking Union Medical College Hospital (PUMCH) between January 1, 2023, and January 6, 2025. All patients were positive for GAD65‐antibody (GAD65‐ab) in serum or cerebrospinal fluid (CSF). Seizures were classified by at least one experienced specialist according to the 2025 International League Against Epilepsy (ILAE) classification [11]. LE, SPS, and CA were diagnosed according to the criteria proposed by Graus et al. [12], Dalakas et al. [13], and M. Liu et al. [14], respectively. The study was approved by the institutional review board (K7384‐K25C0191), and written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

GAD65‐Ab Detection

GAD65‐ab were detected using a fixed cell‐based indirect immunofluorescence assay (BIOCHIP; Euroimmun AG, Lübeck, Germany). 30 μL of plasma (1:10 dilution) or CSF was applied to the substrate slides and incubated for 30 min at room temperature, followed by washing with phosphate‐buffered saline (PBS). Samples were then incubated with 25 μL of FITC‐labeled anti‐human IgG for 30 min, washed with PBS, and examined under a fluorescence microscope.

Clinical Data Collection

Demographic data, clinical phenotypes, comorbidities, GAD65‐ab titer, CSF white blood cell (WBC) count and specific oligoclonal band (SOB) result, treatment regimens, follow‐up duration, and prognosis were collected. Good prognosis was defined as a modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score ≤ 2 in patients with SPS and the absence of recurrent seizures after treatment in patients with LE/EP.

HLA Genotyping

Peripheral blood (2 mL) was collected for high‐resolution HLA genotyping using targeted next‐generation sequencing (tNGS) on the Illumina NovaSeq X‐Plus platform, covering HLA‐A, ‐B, ‐C, ‐DQA1, ‐DQB1, and ‐DRB loci. Data were analyzed with NGSengine, with typing up to 8‐digit resolution and reported at 4‐digit resolution. Quality metrics indicated high reliability (mappability > 97.8%, depth 2811–7895×, noise < 8%, no mismatches). Healthy controls (HCs, n = 990) were derived from the Chinese Shunyi cohort [15], with HLA genotypes extracted from whole‐exome sequencing data and analyzed at 4‐digit resolution. Concordance analysis showed high agreement across loci, supporting comparability between groups [16].

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables are presented as median (interquartile range, IQR) and categorical variables as counts (percentages). Haplotype frequencies were estimated using the expectation–maximization (EM) algorithm. Group comparisons were performed using the chi‐square test or Fisher's exact test. Multiple comparisons in allele and haplotype analyses were corrected using the Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) method, with adjusted significance defined as p adj < 0.05. For other analyses, a two‐tailed p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were conducted using SPSS 21 and R software (version 4.1.3).

Results

Clinical Features

Sixty‐two Chinese patients with GADAND were enrolled, including 49 females (79%) and 13 males (21%), with a median age at onset of 37 years. The median disease duration was 11.5 months. All enrolled patients were sporadic, with no family history of GADAND. Clinical phenotypes included LE/EP in 39 (62.9%) patients, SPS in 23 (37.1%) patients, and CA in 9 (14.5%) patients, with 9 (14.5%) patients presenting overlap syndromes (Figure S1). Forty patients (64.5%) had concurrent autoimmune diseases, among whom 14 (22.6%) had two or more coexisting autoimmune conditions. Three patients had malignancies, including papillary thyroid carcinoma (n = 1) and breast cancer (n = 2) (Table 1).

Association Between HLA Haplotypes and Clinical Features

We further investigated the clinical correlations of susceptibility or potential susceptibility HLA haplotypes. Among carriers of the HLA class I haplotype A*11:01 ~ B*40:01 ~ C*07:02, the proportion of overlap phenotype was higher than that in non‐carriers (33.3% vs. 11.3%, p = 0.115), as was the comorbidity rate of AITD (55.6% vs. 32.1%, p = 0.259) (Table 5). For B*44:02 ~ C*05:01, all carriers were female, and a higher proportion of LE/EP phenotype was observed compared with non‐carriers (83.3% vs. 60.7%, p = 0.398). Among carriers of the HLA class II haplotype DQA1*01:03 ~ DQB1*06:01 ~ DRB1*08:03, earlier disease onset (≤ 18 years) was more frequent (26.7% vs. 8.5%, p = 0.088). No apparent differences in prognosis were observed among carriers of these haplotypes, and none of theses observed trend reached statistical significance (Tables S3 and S4).

Discussion

We conducted a comprehensive analysis of HLA polymorphisms in Chinese patients with GADAND. The main results are as follows: (1) The HLA class I haplotype A*11:01 ~ B* 40:01 ~ C*07:02 was significantly associated with GADAND (OR = 10.22), with a stronger effect in the extended haplotype with DQA1*01:03 (OR = 25.61). (2) B*44:02 ~ C*05:01 (OR = 8.32) and DQA1*01:03 ~ DQB1*06:01 ~ DRB1*08:03 (OR = 3.15) showed increased frequencies in patients but were not significant after correction, suggesting potential susceptibility. In contrast, DQA1*05:05 ~ DQB1*03:01 (OR = 0.15) was less frequent in patients, indicating a potential protective effect. Overall, HLA class I haplotypes showed stronger effects than class II. (3) Known T1DM‐risk haplotypes were also increased in GADAND, including DQA1*03:01 ~ DQB1*03:02 ~ DRB1*04:03 (OR = 5.04) and DQA1*05: 01 ~ DQB1*02:01 ~ DRB1*03:01 (OR = 2.50). Furthermore, A*11:01 ~ B*40:01 ~ C*07:02 was significantly enriched in GADAND patients with comorbid AITD, suggesting a shared genetic‐immunological basis for these comorbidities. No significant HLA associations were identified across clinical phenotypes, suggesting that phenotypic heterogeneity in GADAND is not driven by HLA variation; further validation in larger cohorts is warranted.

GADAND has a complex immunopathogenesis, with evidence supporting T cell involvement. Increased CSF CD8+ T cells are associated with hippocampal atrophy and memory impairment [17], and cytotoxic CD8+ T cell infiltration has been observed in brain tissue of GAD‐EP patients [18]. HLA class I molecules present antigens to CD8+ T cells and may influence disease susceptibility; however, data on HLA class I associations in GADAND remain limited. A German study reported a high frequency of A*02:01 in GAD‐EP without statistical testing [17]. In our study, A*11:01 ~ B*40:01 ~ C*07:02 was identified as a risk haplotype for GADAND, supporting CD8+ T cell involvement. B*40:01 has been associated with cutaneous involvement in SLE [19], and C*07:02 with susceptibility to psoriasis [20], but neither has been reported in neuroimmune disorders. In our cohort, three patients had coexisting autoimmune skin diseases; however, none carried this haplotype, warranting validation in larger studies. The A*11:01 ~ B*40:01 ~ C*07:02 haplotype extends to DQA1*01:03, forming an extended haplotype with increased effect size. The primary driver remains unclear, as allele‐level differences were not significant, suggesting that the causal variant may reside within the linked haplotype.

Previous studies have reported susceptibility‐associated HLA class II haplotypes, including DQA1*05:01 ~ DQB1*02:01 ~ DRB1*03:01 and DQA1*03:01 ~ DQB1*03:02 ~ DRB1*04:01, while a family‐based study identified DRB1*15:01 ~ DQA1*01:02 ~ DQB1*05:02 [2, 9, 10]. In our cohort, the first two haplotypes showed increased frequencies in patients, although not statistically significant, suggesting potential susceptibility in the Chinese population. The latter haplotype was not observed in our sporadic cases, possibly reflecting population differences. In addition, the DQA1*01:03 ~ DQB1*06:01 ~ DRB1*08:03 haplotype showed increased frequency in patients and warrants validation in larger cohorts.

HLA polymorphisms may influence clinical phenotype and prognosis. DRB1*07:01 is associated with faciobrachial dystonic seizures in anti‐leucine‐rich glioma‐inactivated 1 (LGI1) encephalitis [21], and DQB1*05:01 homozygosity with earlier neurodegeneration in anti‐immunoglobulin‐like cell adhesion molecule 5 (IgLON5) disease [22]. DRB1*12:01 is linked to slower progression in multiple sclerosis [23], and DQB1*06:02 to slower progression in T1DM [24, 25]. However, in our cohort, no significant associations were observed between HLA haplotypes and phenotype or prognosis. This may be due to the limited number of haplotype carriers, reduced statistical power, and a primary role of HLA variation in disease susceptibility rather than phenotype or prognosis. Downstream processes, including T‐cell activation, blood–brain barrier permeability, and local immune microenvironments, may also contribute to phenotypic and prognostic heterogeneity.

T1DM‐risk haplotypes were more frequent in GADAND [26, 27]. In latent autoimmune diabetes in adults, DQA1*05:01 ~ DQB1*02:01 ~ DRB1*03:01 is associated with increased GAD65‐ab positivity [28, 29, 30]. Its enrichment in GADAND supports a shared immunogenetic basis centered on GAD65. However, these haplotypes were not associated with higher T1DM prevalence in our cohort (Tables S7 and S8). Possible explanations include a limited sample size and the presence of subclinical disease in some patients that has not yet been clinically diagnosed. The susceptibility haplotype A*11:01 ~ B*40:01 ~ C*07:02 was significantly enriched in GADAND patients with comorbid AITD, potentially reflecting a shared immunogenetic basis for their comorbidity. In addition, Shared genetic risk across autoimmune conditions may also involve non‐HLA loci, including cytotoxic T‐lymphocyte–associated protein 4 (CTLA4), protein tyrosine phosphatase non‐receptor type 22 (PTPN22), interleukin‐2 receptor alpha (IL2RA), and tumor necrosis factor (TNF) [31, 32].

Compared with other antibody‐associated autoimmune encephalitides, HLA genetic susceptibility appears less prominent in GADAND. In anti‐LGI1 encephalitis, up to 91% of patients carry the DRB1*07:01 ~ DQB1*02:02 haplotype [33]. In anti‐IgLON5 disease, 55% and 73% of patients carry DRB1*10:01 and DQB1*05:01 [34], respectively. By contrast, HLA associations in GADAND vary across studies, and carrier frequencies are relatively low. In our cohort, the risk haplotype (A11:01 ~ B40:01 ~ C*07:02) was present in < 15% of patients, while other candidate haplotypes each occurred in < 25%. These findings indicate that HLA genetic susceptibility only partially contributes to GADAND pathogenesis, with other genetic and environmental factors likely involved.

This study has several limitations. First, although it represents one of the largest GADAND cohorts with complete HLA genotyping in the Chinese population, the sample size remains modest, limiting statistical power. Second, despite ethnic matching, residual geographic substructure within the Chinese population may influence HLA allele frequencies; thus, validation in larger, geographically diverse cohorts is warranted. Third, HLA genotyping methods differed between patients and controls; however, prior methodological comparisons demonstrated that this discrepancy introduced only minimal measurement error. Finally, this study is based solely on statistical associations; HLA–peptide binding predictions and functional T‐cell epitope validation were not performed, and the underlying immunopathogenic mechanisms remain to be elucidated.

In conclusion, The HLA class I haplotype A*11:01 ~ B*40:01 ~ C*07:02 is a susceptibility haplotype in Chinese GADAND, supporting T cell involvement in disease pathogenesis. T1DM risk haplotypes were more frequent in GADAND, suggesting a shared genetic basis with GAD65 as the common target antigen. A*11:01 ~ B*40:01 ~ C*07:02 was also significantly enriched in GADAND patients with comorbid AITD, suggesting a shared immunogenetic basis for GADAND/AITD comorbidity. No significant associations were observed between HLA variants and clinical phenotypes, suggesting that the HLA genetic background may not be a major determinant of phenotypic heterogeneity in GADAND; however, this finding requires validation in larger cohorts.

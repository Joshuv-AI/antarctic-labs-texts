Characteristic Sweeps and Source Iteration for Charged-Particle Transport with Continuous Slowing-Down and Angular Scattering
Ben S. Ashby, Alex Lukyanov, Tristan Pryer

Abstract
. We develop a semi-analytic deterministic framework for charged-particle transport with continuous slowing-down in energy and angular scattering. Directed transport and energy advection are treated by method-of-characteristics integration, yielding explicit directional sweeps defined by characteristic maps and inflow data. Scattering is incorporated through a fixed-point (source-iteration) scheme in which the angular gain is lagged, yielding a sequence of decoupled directional solves coupled only through angular sums. The method is formulated variationally in a transport graph space adapted to the charged particle drift. Under standard monotonicity and positivity assumptions on the stopping power and boundedness assumptions on cross sections, we establish coercivity and boundedness of the transport bilinear form, prove contraction of the source iteration under a subcriticality condition and derive a rigorous a posteriori bound for the iteration error, providing an efficient stopping criterion. We further analyse an elastic discrete-ordinates approximation, including conservation properties and a decomposition of angular error into quadrature, cone truncation and finite iteration effects. Numerical experiments for proton transport validate the characteristic sweep against an exact ballistic benchmark and demonstrate the predicted fixed-point convergence under forward-peaked scattering. Carbon-ion simulations with tabulated stopping powers and a reduced multi-species coupling illustrate Bragg peak localisation and distal tail formation driven by secondary charged fragments.

Introduction

Charged-particle beam therapies (protons, carbon ions) and radiation
physics are naturally described by a Boltzmann transport equation
(BTE) in position, direction and energy, coupling continuous
slowing-down with angular scattering in tissue. In therapy-relevant
regimes this produces two numerical challenges: strong anisotropy in
angle and a depth-localised Bragg peak generated by energy
loss. Efficient deterministic solvers with quantitative stability and
error control are therefore attractive when repeated forward solves
are required, for example in optimisation, robustness assessment or
uncertainty studies

Monte Carlo simulation remains the reference approach for
high-fidelity charged-particle transport, but its cost can be
prohibitive when many forward solves are required

[
26

. Deterministic methods (discrete-ordinates,
moment and pencil-beam-type schemes) offer the prospect of faster,
reproducible solves, yet they must address the high dimensionality of
phase space and the strong coupling between energy loss and scattering

[

. Recent semi-analytic
deterministic solvers show that characteristic integration of
continuous slowing-down can deliver fast, reproducible proton
transport in regimes where ionisation dominates

[

. A key open difficulty is to retain the same
computational advantages while treating angular redistribution
explicitly and with quantitative control of the scattering coupling.

We present a semi-analytic method-of-characteristics (MoC) framework
for charged-particle transport with continuous slowing-down in energy
and angular scattering. Directed transport and energy advection are
handled by characteristic integration, so each directional sweep
amounts to evaluating an explicit characteristic map together with the
prescribed inflow data. Scattering is included through a
source-iteration (fixed-point) strategy in which the angular gain is
lagged, so that each iteration requires only a collection of
direction-by-direction sweeps coupled through angular sums. This
preserves the sweep structure of ionisation-only characteristic
solvers while adding scattering through an iterative angular mixing
step.

We place the method in a variational setting based on a graph space
adapted to the directional and energy drift. Under monotonicity and
positivity assumptions on the stopping power and boundedness
assumptions on the cross sections, we establish coercivity and
boundedness of the transport bilinear form and obtain well-posedness
of the weak problem. These stability results support convergence of
the source iteration and yield rigorous a posteriori bounds for the
iteration error, giving a practical stopping criterion. For the
discrete-ordinates approximation we further decompose the angular
error into contributions from angular quadrature, cone truncation and
the finite iteration count.

We implement the framework for proton transport with
Bragg–Kleeman-type stopping and for carbon-ion transport using
tabulated stopping powers. In the carbon case we include a reduced
multi-species construction in which a secondary proton and neutron
field is generated by a volumetric source driven by the converged
carbon solution and an effective interaction rate, to provide a
transparent mechanism for post-peak dose tails and to validate the
coupling logic. Numerical experiments verify the characteristic solver
against an analytic benchmark, demonstrate linear convergence of the
source iteration and illustrate the impact of forward-peaked
scattering and the multi-species coupling on depth–dose, including
the distal secondary tail in the carbon simulations.

2.
Charged Particle Transport Models

We consider transport of charged particles through a medium, governed
by directed motion, ionising energy loss and scattering due to elastic
and nonelastic interactions. Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with

d\in\{2,3\}
denote the spatial domain, and let

I=[E_{\min},E_{\max}]\subset(0,\infty)
be the admissible energy
interval. The phase-space domain is
\Omega=D\times I\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, where
\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
is the
unit direction of travel. The surface measure on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
is
denoted
\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}
.

For a given particle species, the phase-space density

\psi(x,E,\boldsymbol{\omega})
satisfies the linear Boltzmann transport
equation

(1)

\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi(x,E,\boldsymbol{\omega})+\sigma_{T}(E)\psi(x,E,\boldsymbol{\omega})=\mathcal{K}[\psi](x,E,\boldsymbol{\omega}),

where
\sigma_{T}(E)\geq 0
is the total removal cross-section and

\mathcal{K}
is the gain operator

(2)

\mathcal{K}[\psi](x,E,\boldsymbol{\omega}):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\int_{I}\sigma_{S}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime},E^{\prime}\to E)\psi(x,E^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}E^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}.

The differential kernel
\sigma_{S}\geq 0
redistributes particles from

(E^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime})
to
(E,\boldsymbol{\omega})
. The quantity
\sigma_{T}(E)

is taken as given so that the loss term
\sigma_{T}(E)\psi
controls

\mathcal{K}
in the energy norm introduced later.

The kernel
\sigma_{S}
contains distinct physical mechanisms. We write

(3)

\sigma_{S}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime},E^{\prime}\to E)=\sigma_{\text{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime},E)\delta(E-E^{\prime})+\sigma_{\text{ion}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime},E^{\prime}\to E)+\sigma_{\text{non}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime},E^{\prime}\to E),

where
\sigma_{\text{el}}
models elastic Coulomb scattering, which is
energy-conserving,
\sigma_{\text{ion}}
models ionisation losses and

\sigma_{\text{non}}
models genuinely nonelastic interactions that can
involve larger energy transfers.

In the energy regime of interest here, ionisation energy loss arises
from a large number of inelastic collisions along each particle track,
with each collision typically transferring only a small amount of
energy. In this setting it is standard to replace the detailed
ionisation energy-loss redistribution by a continuous slowing-down
approximation, in which the cumulative effect of ionisation is
represented by an energy advection term with stopping power
S(E)
.
Neglecting higher-order energy-diffusion (straggling) corrections, this
leads to the charged-particle transport model

(4)

\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi(x,E,\boldsymbol{\omega})-\partial_{E}\big(S(E)\psi(x,E,\boldsymbol{\omega})\big)+\sigma_{T}(E)\psi(x,E,\boldsymbol{\omega})=\mathcal{K}[\psi](x,E,\boldsymbol{\omega}),

where, after applying the slowing down approximation,
\mathcal{K}

collects the remaining gain contributions, including elastic angular
redistribution and any nonelastic energy-changing events retained in
the model.

In regimes where small-angle Coulomb scattering dominates, the elastic
gain can be approximated by angular diffusion on the sphere via the
Laplace–Beltrami operator
\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}
,

(5)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\sigma_{\text{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime},E)\psi(x,E,\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}\approx\varepsilon(E)\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\psi(x,E,\boldsymbol{\omega}),

with
\varepsilon(E)\geq 0
an energy-dependent angular-diffusion
coefficient
[
3
]
.

Throughout the analysis we adopt the following regularity and
positivity conditions, consistent with the variational framework
developed later:
S\in W^{1,\infty}(I)
with
S(E)>0
and
S^{\prime}(E)\leq 0

on
I
,
\sigma_{T}\in L^{\infty}(I)
with
\sigma_{T}(E)\geq 0
, and

\sigma_{S}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times I\times I)
with
\sigma_{S}\geq 0
. These ensure that the gain
operator (
2
) is bounded on the graph space used for
the weak formulation and that the transport part of (
4
) is
coercive once inflow boundary conditions are imposed.

2.0.
Stopping power and the Bragg–Kleeman approximation

Under the continuous slowing down approximation, ionisation losses are
represented in (
4
) by the stopping power
S(E)
. For light
ions such as protons the dominant mechanism is electronic stopping due
to Coulomb interactions with atomic electrons. A standard empirical
model is the Bragg–Kleeman rule: given constants
\alpha>0
and

p\in[1,2]
,

(6)

S(E)=\frac{1}{\alpha p}E^{1-p}.

This follows from the range–energy relation
R(E)=\alpha E^{p}
and is
well validated in the therapeutic window for protons. In particular,
on any admissible interval
I=[E_{\min},E_{\max}]\subset(0,\infty)

one has
S\in W^{1,\infty}(I)
,
S(E)>0
and
S^{\prime}(E)\leq 0
, properties
used below to obtain coercivity of the transport operator. One can see
in Figure
2
how this ansatz is very accurate for
clinically relevant proton energies since ionisation dominates
nonelastic processes.

A more physically motivated alternative is the Bethe–Bloch formula for
the electronic stopping power, which models the mean ionisation loss in
terms of projectile speed and material parameters. It provides a
standard reference across ion species and, with suitable effective
parameters, can be used as an accurate surrogate for electronic
stopping in the therapeutic energy range. In the present work we do not
pursue a full Bethe–Bloch parameterisation and instead adopt the
Bragg–Kleeman approximation (
6
) as the simplest
closed-form model that is sufficient for our analysis and numerical
examples.

2.0.
Boundary conditions

Since
S(E)>0
on
I
, the energy drift term
-\partial_{E}(S\psi)
induces
a one-way characteristic flow from
E_{\max}
down to
E_{\min}
. It is
therefore natural to prescribe data both on the spatial inflow boundary
and on the top-energy boundary.

Let
\partial(D\times I)=(\partial D\times I)\cup(D\times\partial I)
and
write the outward unit normal on
\partial(D\times I)
as

\boldsymbol{n}_{(\boldsymbol{x},E)}:=(\boldsymbol{n}_{\boldsymbol{x}},n_{E})
, where
\boldsymbol{n}_{\boldsymbol{x}}
is
the outward unit normal on
\partial D
, and
n_{E}=+1
on
E=E_{\max}
and

n_{E}=-1
on
E=E_{\min}
. For each fixed
\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
we
define the inflow and outflow boundaries in
(\boldsymbol{x},E)
by

\Gamma_{\pm}(\boldsymbol{\omega}):=\Big\{(\boldsymbol{x},E)\in\partial(D\times I):\pm\big(\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{\boldsymbol{x}}-S(E)n_{E}\big)>0\Big\},

and the corresponding phase-space boundary sets by

\Gamma_{\pm}:=\big\{(\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega})\in\partial(D\times I)\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:(\boldsymbol{x},E)\in\Gamma_{\pm}(\boldsymbol{\omega})\big\}.

This recovers the usual decomposition into spatial and energy inflow/outflow.
On the spatial boundary
\partial D\times I
one has
n_{E}=0
, so

\Gamma_{-}^{\boldsymbol{x}}:=\{(\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega})\in(\partial D\times I)\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:\ \boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{\boldsymbol{x}}<0\},\qquad\Gamma_{+}^{\boldsymbol{x}}:=\{(\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega})\in(\partial D\times I)\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:\ \boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{\boldsymbol{x}}>0\}.

On the energy boundary
D\times\partial I
one has
\boldsymbol{n}_{\boldsymbol{x}}=\boldsymbol{0}
,
so the flux associated with
-\partial_{E}(S\psi)
is
-S(E)n_{E}\psi
.
Since
S(E)>0
, the top boundary
E=E_{\max}
is inflow and the bottom
boundary
E=E_{\min}
is outflow. We set

\Gamma_{-}^{E}:=D\times\{E_{\max}\}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\qquad\Gamma_{+}^{E}:=D\times\{E_{\min}\}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1},

so that

\Gamma_{-}=\Gamma_{-}^{\boldsymbol{x}}\cup\Gamma_{-}^{E},\qquad\Gamma_{+}=\Gamma_{+}^{\boldsymbol{x}}\cup\Gamma_{+}^{E}.

On
\Gamma_{-}
we prescribe inflow data

(7)

\psi=g\quad\text{on }\Gamma_{-}.

We assume
g
is square-integrable with respect to the natural flux
weights induced by the boundary term
\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{\boldsymbol{x}}-S(E)n_{E}
,
namely
|\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{\boldsymbol{x}}|
on
\Gamma_{-}^{\boldsymbol{x}}
and

S(E_{\max})
on
\Gamma_{-}^{E}
:

g\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{-}^{\boldsymbol{x}},|\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{\boldsymbol{x}}|\,\mathrm{d}\gamma_{\boldsymbol{x}}\,\mathrm{d}E\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega})\ \cap\ L^{2}(\Gamma_{-}^{E},S(E_{\max})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}).

No boundary condition is imposed on
\Gamma_{+}
.

3.
Variational Formulation and Functional Setting

To analyse well-posedness and enable discretisation, we adopt a
variational formulation. The first-order transport and energy-advection
terms, together with the non-symmetric gain operator, motivate a graph
space that captures directional and energy drift.

We work in the graph space

(8)

\mathcal{U}:=\Big\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega):\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\nabla_{x}u\in L^{2}(\Omega),\ \partial_{E}\!\left({S(E)u}\right)\in L^{2}(\Omega)\Big\}.

For each fixed
\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, define the
directional bilinear form

(9)

a(u,v;\boldsymbol{\omega}):=\int_{D}\int_{I}\Big[\!\left({\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\nabla_{x}u}\right)v-\partial_{E}\!\left({S(E)u}\right)v+\sigma_{T}(E)uv\Big]\,\mathrm{d}E\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\\
+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma_{+}(\boldsymbol{\omega})}\!\left({\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{x}-S(E)n_{E}}\right)uv\,\mathrm{d}s,

and the scattering bilinear form

(10)

s(u,v;\boldsymbol{\omega}):=\int_{D}\int_{I}v\!\left({\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\int_{I}\sigma_{S}\!\left({\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime},E^{\prime}\to E}\right)u\!\left({\boldsymbol{x},E^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}E^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}E\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}.

The linear form is

(11)

l(v;\boldsymbol{\omega}):=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma_{-}(\boldsymbol{\omega})}\!\left({\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{x}-S(E)n_{E}}\right)g\!\left({\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega}}\right)v\!\left({\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{D}\int_{I}f\!\left({\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega}}\right)v\!\left({\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}E\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x},

where
g
is the inflow boundary datum on
\Gamma_{-}(\boldsymbol{\omega})
and

f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
is a given volumetric source. In the present paper

f
will be used to represent any modelling corrections or additional
physics that are treated explicitly rather than embedded in the drift.

The weak problem reads: find
u\in\mathcal{U}
such that

(12)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}a(u,v;\boldsymbol{\omega})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\Big[s(u,v;\boldsymbol{\omega})+l(v;\boldsymbol{\omega})\Big]\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}\qquad\text{for all }v\in\mathcal{U}.

In the stability and contraction arguments below we apply the coercivity
estimate to functions with homogeneous inflow trace on
\Gamma_{-}
(in
particular to source-iteration differences, for which the inflow datum
cancels since
g
is fixed in
l
).

3.1 Theorem

(Coercivity and boundedness of the continuous bilinear forms)
.

Assume
S\in W^{1,\infty}(I)
with
S(E)>0
and
S^{\prime}(E)\leq 0
on
I
,

\sigma_{T}\in L^{\infty}(I)
with
\sigma_{T}(E)\geq 0
, and
\sigma_{S}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times I\times I)
with

\sigma_{S}\geq 0
.

Define the coercivity norm

\|u\|_{\mathcal{U}}^{2}:=\|\sigma_{T}^{1/2}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|(-S^{\prime}(E))^{1/2}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\int_{\Gamma_{+}}\!\left({\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{\boldsymbol{x}}-S(E)n_{E}}\right)u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s,

and the stronger norm

\|u\|_{\mathcal{U}^{*}}^{2}:=\|u\|_{\mathcal{U}}^{2}+\|\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\partial_{E}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Assume moreover that

\mu:=\operatorname{ess\,inf}_{E\in I}\!\left({-S^{\prime}(E)}\right)>0,

so that
\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\mu^{-1/2}\|(-S^{\prime})^{1/2}w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\mu^{-1/2}\|w\|_{\mathcal{U}}
.

Then the bilinear forms
a(u,v;\boldsymbol{\omega})
and
s(u,v;\boldsymbol{\omega})

defined in Section
3
satisfy the following:

(1)

Coercivity. If
u=0
on
\Gamma_{-}
, then

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}a(u,u;\boldsymbol{\omega})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\|u\|_{\mathcal{U}}^{2}.

(2)

Boundedness. There exists
C_{a}>0
, depending only on

\|S\|_{W^{1,\infty}(I)}
,
\|\sigma_{T}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}
and
\mu
, such
that for all
u\in\mathcal{U}^{*}
and all
v\in\mathcal{U}
,

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}a(u,v;\boldsymbol{\omega})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}\Big|\leq C_{a}\|u\|_{\mathcal{U}^{*}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{U}}.

Moreover there exists
C_{s}>0
, depending only on

\|\sigma_{S}\|_{L^{\infty}}
and the measures of
I
and
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
,
such that

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}s(u,v;\boldsymbol{\omega})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}\Big|\leq C_{s}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{s}\mu^{-1}\|u\|_{\mathcal{U}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{U}}.

Proof.

Fix
\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and abbreviate

a(u,u):=a(u,u;\boldsymbol{\omega})
. By definition,

\displaystyle a(u,u)

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\int_{I}\Big[\!\left({\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}u}\right)u-\partial_{E}\!\left({Su}\right)u+\sigma_{T}u^{2}\Big]\,\mathrm{d}E\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}+\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma_{+}(\boldsymbol{\omega})}\!\left({\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{\boldsymbol{x}}-Sn_{E}}\right)u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma.

We first integrate by parts in
\boldsymbol{x}
. Since

\!\left({\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}u}\right)u=\tfrac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\!\left({u^{2}}\right)
,

\int_{D}\int_{I}\!\left({\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}u}\right)u\,\mathrm{d}E\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}=\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\partial D\times I}\!\left({\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{\boldsymbol{x}}}\right)u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma.

For the energy-advection term, write

-\partial_{E}\!\left({Su}\right)u=-\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{E}\!\left({Su^{2}}\right)+\tfrac{1}{2}\!\left({-S^{\prime}}\right)u^{2},

so that integration by parts in
E
gives

-\int_{D}\int_{I}\partial_{E}\!\left({Su}\right)u\,\mathrm{d}E\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{D\times\partial I}Sn_{E}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma+\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{D}\int_{I}\!\left({-S^{\prime}(E)}\right)u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}E\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}.

Combining the
\boldsymbol{x}
and
E
boundary contributions yields

\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\partial(D\times I)}\!\left({\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{\boldsymbol{x}}-Sn_{E}}\right)u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma=\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma_{+}(\boldsymbol{\omega})}\!\left({\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{\boldsymbol{x}}-Sn_{E}}\right)u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma+\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma_{-}(\boldsymbol{\omega})}\!\left({\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{\boldsymbol{x}}-Sn_{E}}\right)u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma.

Therefore

\displaystyle a(u,u)

\displaystyle=\|\sigma_{T}^{1/2}u(\cdot,\cdot,\boldsymbol{\omega})\|_{L^{2}(D\times I)}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\|(-S^{\prime})^{1/2}u(\cdot,\cdot,\boldsymbol{\omega})\|_{L^{2}(D\times I)}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\Gamma_{+}(\boldsymbol{\omega})}\!\left({\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{\boldsymbol{x}}-Sn_{E}}\right)u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma+\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma_{-}(\boldsymbol{\omega})}\!\left({\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{\boldsymbol{x}}-Sn_{E}}\right)u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma.

If
u=0
on
\Gamma_{-}
, then for each fixed
\boldsymbol{\omega}
we have
u=0

on
\Gamma_{-}(\boldsymbol{\omega})
and the last term vanishes. Hence

a(u,u)\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\|\sigma_{T}^{1/2}u(\cdot,\cdot,\boldsymbol{\omega})\|_{L^{2}(D\times I)}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\|(-S^{\prime})^{1/2}u(\cdot,\cdot,\boldsymbol{\omega})\|_{L^{2}(D\times I)}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma_{+}(\boldsymbol{\omega})}\!\left({\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{\boldsymbol{x}}-Sn_{E}}\right)u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma.

Integrating this inequality over
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and using
\,\mathrm{d}s=\,\mathrm{d}\gamma\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}
gives

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}a(u,u;\boldsymbol{\omega})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\|\sigma_{T}^{1/2}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\|(-S^{\prime})^{1/2}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma_{+}}\!\left({\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{\boldsymbol{x}}-Sn_{E}}\right)u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s=\tfrac{1}{2}\|u\|_{\mathcal{U}}^{2},

which proves coercivity.

For boundedness of
a
, fix
\boldsymbol{\omega}
and apply Cauchy–Schwarz,
together with

\|\partial_{E}\!\left({Su}\right)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|S\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\|\partial_{E}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|S^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

we have

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{D}\int_{I}\!\left({\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}u}\right)v\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}u(\cdot,\cdot,\boldsymbol{\omega})\|_{L^{2}(D\times I)}\|v(\cdot,\cdot,\boldsymbol{\omega})\|_{L^{2}(D\times I)},

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{D}\int_{I}\partial_{E}\!\left({Su}\right)v\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\|\partial_{E}\!\left({Su}\right)(\cdot,\cdot,\boldsymbol{\omega})\|_{L^{2}(D\times I)}\|v(\cdot,\cdot,\boldsymbol{\omega})\|_{L^{2}(D\times I)}

\displaystyle\leq\|S\|_{W^{1,\infty}(I)}\!\left({\|\partial_{E}u(\cdot,\cdot,\boldsymbol{\omega})\|_{L^{2}(D\times I)}+\|u(\cdot,\cdot,\boldsymbol{\omega})\|_{L^{2}(D\times I)}}\right)\|v(\cdot,\cdot,\boldsymbol{\omega})\|_{L^{2}(D\times I)},

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{D}\int_{I}\sigma_{T}uv\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\|\sigma_{T}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\|u(\cdot,\cdot,\boldsymbol{\omega})\|_{L^{2}(D\times I)}\|v(\cdot,\cdot,\boldsymbol{\omega})\|_{L^{2}(D\times I)},

\displaystyle\Big|\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma_{+}(\boldsymbol{\omega})}\!\left({\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{\boldsymbol{x}}-Sn_{E}}\right)uv\,\mathrm{d}\gamma\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\Big(\int_{\Gamma_{+}(\boldsymbol{\omega})}\!\left({\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{\boldsymbol{x}}-Sn_{E}}\right)u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\int_{\Gamma_{+}(\boldsymbol{\omega})}\!\left({\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{\boldsymbol{x}}-Sn_{E}}\right)v^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma\Big)^{1/2}.

Using
\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\mu^{-1/2}\|w\|_{\mathcal{U}}
for
w=v
and

\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\mu^{-1/2}\|u\|_{\mathcal{U}}\leq\mu^{-1/2}\|u\|_{\mathcal{U}^{*}}
,
we obtain a bound of the form

|a(u,v;\boldsymbol{\omega})|\leq C_{a}\|u\|_{\mathcal{U}^{*}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{U}}
with
C_{a}
independent of
\boldsymbol{\omega}
.
Integrating over
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
yields the stated estimate.

For the scattering form, define

\mathcal{K}[u]\!\left({\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega}}\right):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\int_{I}\sigma_{S}\!\left({\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime},E^{\prime}\to E}\right)u\!\left({\boldsymbol{x},E^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}E^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}.

By Fubini and
\|\sigma_{S}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty
,

|\mathcal{K}[u]\!\left({\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega}}\right)|\leq\|\sigma_{S}\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\int_{I}|u\!\left({\boldsymbol{x},E^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}}\right)|\,\mathrm{d}E^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}.

Applying Cauchy–Schwarz in
(E^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime})
gives

|\mathcal{K}[u]\!\left({\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega}}\right)|\leq\|\sigma_{S}\|_{L^{\infty}}\!\left({|I||\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}\right)^{1/2}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\int_{I}|u\!\left({\boldsymbol{x},E^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}}\right)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}E^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}\Big)^{1/2}.

Squaring, integrating over
(\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega})
and using Fubini yields

\|\mathcal{K}[u]\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{s}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
with
C_{s}
depending only on

\|\sigma_{S}\|_{L^{\infty}}
and the measures of
I
and
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Hence

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}s(u,v;\boldsymbol{\omega})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}\Big|=\Big|\int_{\Omega}v\mathcal{K}[u]\Big|\leq\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\mathcal{K}[u]\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{s}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

The final inequality in the theorem follows from
\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\mu^{-1/2}\|w\|_{\mathcal{U}}
for
w=u,v
.
∎

3.1.
Source iteration at the continuum level

The weak formulation (
12
) couples all directions through the
gain form
s(\cdot,\cdot;\boldsymbol{\omega})
. To decouple angles and obtain
an efficient solver, we employ a (Picard) source iteration in which the
gain is lagged. Given an initial guess
u^{(0)}\in\mathcal{U}
, define

\{u^{(n)}\}_{n\geq 1}
by the following directional problems: for each

\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, find

u^{(n)}(\cdot,\cdot,\boldsymbol{\omega})\in\mathcal{U}
such that

(13)

a\!\left({u^{(n)},v;\boldsymbol{\omega}}\right)=s\!\left({u^{(n-1)},v;\boldsymbol{\omega}}\right)+l\!\left({v;\boldsymbol{\omega}}\right)\qquad\text{for all }v\in\mathcal{U},

where
a
,
s
and
l
are the forms from Section
3
.
In particular, the inflow boundary datum
g
is enforced in the linear
functional
l(\cdot;\boldsymbol{\omega})
and is not updated with
n
.

3.2 Proposition

(Convergence of source iteration)
.

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
and that
the gain operator induces a bounded bilinear form on
\mathcal{U}
in
the sense that there exists
\eta\geq 0
such that

(14)

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}s\!\left({w,v;\boldsymbol{\omega}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}\Big|\leq\eta\|w\|_{\mathcal{U}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{U}}\qquad\text{for all }w,v\in\mathcal{U}.

If
2\eta<1
, then the Picard map induced by (
13
) is a
strict contraction on
\mathcal{U}
in the
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{U}}
norm.
Consequently, for any initial guess
u^{(0)}\in\mathcal{U}
the iterates

\{u^{(n)}\}_{n\geq 0}
defined by (
13
) converge strongly in

\mathcal{U}
to the unique solution
u
of the variational problem
(
12
).

In particular, by Theorem
3.1
one may take

\eta=C_{s}\mu^{-1}
, where
C_{s}
is the scattering boundedness
constant.

Proof.

Define the integrated bilinear forms

A(u,v):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}a(u,v;\boldsymbol{\omega})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega},\qquad S(u,v):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}s(u,v;\boldsymbol{\omega})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega},

and likewise
L(v):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}l(v;\boldsymbol{\omega})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}
.
Then the weak problem (
12
) reads
A(u,v)=S(u,v)+L(v)
for all

v\in\mathcal{U}
, and the source iteration (
13
) reads

A\!\left({u^{(n)},v}\right)=S\!\left({u^{(n-1)},v}\right)+L(v)\qquad\text{for all }v\in\mathcal{U}.

Let
T:\mathcal{U}\to\mathcal{U}
denote the Picard map defined by

u^{(n)}=T(u^{(n-1)})
, that is,
T(w)
is the unique element of

\mathcal{U}
satisfying

(15)

A\!\left({T(w),v}\right)=S\!\left({w,v}\right)+L(v)\qquad\text{for all }v\in\mathcal{U}.

(Existence and uniqueness of
T(w)
follow from the well-posedness
associated with Theorem
3.1
; in particular, the boundary
datum is fixed in
L
and is therefore the same for all arguments
w
.)

To prove contraction, let
w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathcal{U}
and set

\delta:=T(w_{1})-T(w_{2})
. Subtracting (
15
) for
w_{1}
and
w_{2}

gives

(16)

A(\delta,v)=S\!\left({w_{1}-w_{2},v}\right)\qquad\text{for all }v\in\mathcal{U}.

Since the inflow datum is fixed in
L
, it cancels in the difference, and

\delta
has homogeneous inflow trace on
\Gamma_{-}
(equivalently,

\delta
belongs to the homogeneous-inflow subspace used in
Theorem
3.1
). Choosing
v=\delta
in (
16
) and
applying the coercivity estimate from Theorem
3.1
yields

(17)

\tfrac{1}{2}\|\delta\|_{\mathcal{U}}^{2}\leq\Big|S\!\left({w_{1}-w_{2},\delta}\right)\Big|.

Using (
14
) gives

\tfrac{1}{2}\|\delta\|_{\mathcal{U}}^{2}\leq\eta\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{\mathcal{U}}\|\delta\|_{\mathcal{U}},\qquad\text{hence}\qquad\|\delta\|_{\mathcal{U}}\leq 2\eta\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{\mathcal{U}}.

Therefore
\|T(w_{1})-T(w_{2})\|_{\mathcal{U}}\leq 2\eta\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{\mathcal{U}}
.
If
2\eta<1
,
T
is a strict contraction on
\mathcal{U}
. Banach’s fixed
point theorem implies that
u^{(n)}\to u
strongly in
\mathcal{U}
, where

u
is the unique fixed point of
T
, and inserting
u=T(u)
in
(
15
) shows that
u
satisfies (
12
). Uniqueness of
the solution of (
12
) follows by applying the same estimate to
the difference of two solutions.

Finally, the choice
\eta=C_{s}\mu^{-1}
follows immediately from the
scattering boundedness estimate in Theorem
3.1
.
∎

4.
Angular approximation and error control

In this section we focus on the angular discretisation of elastic
Coulomb scattering. For simplicity we neglect nonelastic interactions
and retain only the energy-conserving elastic component of the gain.
Accordingly we work with an elastic kernel

\sigma_{\text{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime},E)
and the associated
gain

\mathcal{K}_{\text{el}}[\psi](\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega}):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\sigma_{\text{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime},E)\psi(\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}.

The stopping power
S(E)
is the same as in Section
2
.

To reduce computational cost, we restrict the angular domain to a
forward cone centred on the primary beam direction. Let

\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\star}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
be a reference direction and,
for a half-angle
\theta_{c}>0
, define

(18)

\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\star},\theta_{c}):=\Big\{\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:\arccos\!\left({\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\star}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}}\right)\leq\theta_{c}\Big\},

illustrated in Figure
4
. In forward-peaked regimes (high
energies, weak multiple scattering) one may take
\theta_{c}
small,
whereas stronger scattering requires a larger cone.

We therefore introduce the cone-truncated gain operator

\mathcal{K}_{\text{el}}^{c}[\psi](\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega}):=\int_{\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\star},\theta_{c})}\sigma_{\text{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime},E)\psi(\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}.

The difference
\mathcal{K}_{\text{el}}[\psi]-\mathcal{K}_{\text{el}}^{c}[\psi]

is the cone-truncation remainder, and it will be treated as a distinct
error contribution below.

(20)

\psi_{i}(\boldsymbol{x},E):=\psi(\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i})

(21)

4.1 Remark

(Continuum conservation)
.

Assume the elastic kernel satisfies the mass-conserving normalisation

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\sigma_{\text{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime},E)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}=\Sigma_{\text{el}}(E)\qquad\text{for a.e. }E\in I,

where
\Sigma_{\text{el}}(E)
is independent of
\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}
. Then the
elastic gain satisfies the identity

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\sigma_{\text{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime},E)\psi(\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}=\Sigma_{\text{el}}(E)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\psi(\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}.

Consequently, in the purely elastic setting where the loss term contains
the elastic removal
\Sigma_{\text{el}}(E)\psi
(for example

\sigma_{T}(E)=\Sigma_{\text{el}}(E)
when no other interactions are
present), the net elastic scattering contribution conserves particle
number at each energy after integrating over
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.

If the angular domain is truncated to a cone

\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\star},\theta_{c})
, then the corresponding
cone-truncated gain
\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{el}}^{c}
fails to satisfy the
full-sphere identity only through scattering events that transfer mass to

\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\setminus\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\star},\theta_{c})
.
Quantitative bounds are given later via cone-truncation error estimates.

4.2 Proposition

(Discrete conservation under exact angular quadrature)
.

Assume the elastic kernel is rotationally invariant at fixed energy,

\sigma_{\mathrm{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime},E)=\kappa_{E}(\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime})
with

\kappa_{E}:[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Fix an integer
L\geq 0
and assume that,
for a.e.
E\in I
,
\kappa_{E}
admits a truncated Legendre expansion of
degree at most
L
, that is,

\kappa_{E}(\mu)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}a_{\ell}(E)P_{\ell}(\mu)\qquad\text{for }\mu\in[-1,1],

with coefficients
a_{\ell}(E)\geq 0
. Let

\{(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\varpi_{i})\}_{i=1}^{Q}\subset\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times(0,\infty)
be a full-sphere quadrature rule
that is exact for spherical harmonics (up to degree
L
), equivalently
exact for all restrictions to
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
of polynomials in

\boldsymbol{\omega}
of total degree at most
L
. In particular, for each

j=1,\dots,Q
and a.e.
E\in I
, this implies exactness for the
corresponding zonal functions
\boldsymbol{\omega}\mapsto P_{\ell}(\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j})
,
\ell=0,\dots,L
, and hence
for
\boldsymbol{\omega}\mapsto\kappa_{E}(\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j})
. Define

\Sigma_{\mathrm{el}}(E):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\sigma_{\mathrm{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime},E)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega},

which is independent of
\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}
by rotational invariance. Then
for any values
\{\psi_{j}(\boldsymbol{x},E)\}_{j=1}^{Q}
one has the discrete balance

\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\varpi_{i}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{Q}\mu_{j}\sigma_{\mathrm{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j},E)\psi_{j}(\boldsymbol{x},E)-\Sigma_{\mathrm{el}}(E)\psi_{i}(\boldsymbol{x},E)\Big)=0,

where
\psi_{i}(\boldsymbol{x},E):=\psi(\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i})
. Equivalently,

\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\varpi_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{Q}\mu_{j}\sigma_{\mathrm{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j},E)\psi_{j}(\boldsymbol{x},E)=\Sigma_{\mathrm{el}}(E)\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\varpi_{i}\psi_{i}(\boldsymbol{x},E).

Proof.

Using

\sigma_{\mathrm{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j},E)=\kappa_{E}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j})
,
we compute

\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\varpi_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{Q}\mu_{j}\sigma_{\mathrm{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j},E)\psi_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{Q}\mu_{j}\psi_{j}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\varpi_{i}\kappa_{E}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j})\Big).

By quadrature exactness, for each fixed
j
we have

\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\varpi_{i}\kappa_{E}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j})=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\kappa_{E}(\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\sigma_{\mathrm{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j},E)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}=\Sigma_{\mathrm{el}}(E),

and the right-hand side is independent of
j
. Substituting gives

\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\varpi_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{Q}\mu_{j}\sigma_{\mathrm{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j},E)\psi_{j}=\Sigma_{\mathrm{el}}(E)\sum_{j=1}^{Q}\mu_{j}\psi_{j},

which is equivalent to the stated balance.
∎

4.3 Remark
.

Exact conservation in Proposition
4.2
relies on a
full-sphere quadrature rule that is exact for the relevant zonal
functions. In the cone-restricted setting

\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\star},\theta_{c})
used for computation, the same
calculation applies to the cone-truncated gain, but conservation holds
only up to a remainder representing scattering events that transfer mass
to
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\setminus\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\star},\theta_{c})
.
Quantitative bounds are given later via cone-truncation estimates.

Let
U:=L^{2}(D\times I)
and write
\boldsymbol{u}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{Q})\in U^{Q}
for
the directional components associated with nodes

\{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{Q}
. We work with the per-ordinate graph
spaces

\mathcal{U}_{i}:=\Big\{w\in L^{2}(D\times I):\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}\cdot\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}w\in L^{2}(D\times I),\ \partial_{E}\!\left({S(E)w}\right)\in L^{2}(D\times I)\Big\},\qquad i=1,\dots,Q,

and the product space
\mathcal{U}^{Q}:=\prod_{i=1}^{Q}\mathcal{U}_{i}
.
We write
\,\mathrm{d}\gamma
for the surface measure on
\partial(D\times I)
.

The discrete elastic scattering form for the
i
-th direction is

(22)

s^{Q}(\boldsymbol{u},v_{i};\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}):=\int_{D}\int_{I}v_{i}(\boldsymbol{x},E)\sum_{j=1}^{Q}\mu_{j}\sigma_{\mathrm{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j},E)u_{j}(\boldsymbol{x},E)\,\mathrm{d}E\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}.

Consistently with Section
3
, the directional
bilinear form retains the transport and boundary terms,

(23)

a(u_{i},v_{i};\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}):=\int_{D}\int_{I}\Big[\!\left({\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}\cdot\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}u_{i}}\right)v_{i}-\partial_{E}\!\left({S(E)u_{i}}\right)v_{i}+\sigma_{T}(E)u_{i}v_{i}\Big]\,\mathrm{d}E\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\\
+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma_{+}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i})}\!\left({\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{\boldsymbol{x}}-S(E)n_{E}}\right)u_{i}v_{i}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma,

and the inflow linear form for the
i
-th direction is

(24)

l(v_{i};\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}):=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma_{-}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i})}\!\left({\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{\boldsymbol{x}}-S(E)n_{E}}\right)g(\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i})v_{i}(\boldsymbol{x},E)\,\mathrm{d}\gamma.

Neglecting nonelastic interactions, the discrete weak problem in angle
is: find
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{U}^{Q}
such that, for each
i=1,\dots,Q
,

(25)

a(u_{i},v_{i};\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i})=s^{Q}(\boldsymbol{u},v_{i};\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i})+l(v_{i};\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i})\qquad\text{for all }v_{i}\in\mathcal{U}_{i}.

4.3.
Source iteration

The coupled directional system (
21
) contains angular
scattering terms that redistribute particles across directions. We
utilise a discrete source-iteration scheme, which decouples the
directional equations at each step and permits independent, parallel
directional solves.

Given
\boldsymbol{u}^{(0)}=(u^{(0)}_{1},\dots,u^{(0)}_{Q})\in\mathcal{U}^{Q}
, define

\boldsymbol{u}^{(n)}=(u^{(n)}_{1},\dots,u^{(n)}_{Q})
by, for each
i=1,\dots,Q
,

(26)

\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}\cdot\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}u_{i}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{x},E)-\partial_{E}\!\left({S(E)u_{i}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{x},E)}\right)+\sigma_{T}(E)u_{i}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{x},E)=\sum_{j=1}^{Q}\mu_{j}\sigma_{\mathrm{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j},E)u_{j}^{(n-1)}(\boldsymbol{x},E),

with inflow data
u_{i}^{(n)}=g(\cdot,\cdot,\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i})
on

\Gamma_{-}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i})
.

The corresponding discrete weak formulation reads: given

\boldsymbol{u}^{(n-1)}\in\mathcal{U}^{Q}
, find

\boldsymbol{u}^{(n)}\in\mathcal{U}^{Q}
such that, for each
i=1,\dots,Q
,

(27)

a\!\left({u_{i}^{(n)},v_{i};\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}}\right)=s^{Q}\!\left({\boldsymbol{u}^{(n-1)},v_{i};\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}}\right)+l\!\left({v_{i};\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}}\right)\qquad\text{for all }v_{i}\in\mathcal{U}_{i}.

4.4 Proposition

(Convergence of discrete-ordinates source iteration)
.

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
and neglect
nonelastic interactions, so that scattering is given by the elastic
kernel
\sigma_{\mathrm{el}}
. Let
\{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\varpi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{Q}
be a
(full-sphere or cone-restricted) quadrature rule and consider the
discrete-ordinates weak iteration (
27
) on the
product graph space
\mathcal{U}^{Q}=\prod_{i=1}^{Q}\mathcal{U}_{i}
.

Assume that the discrete scattering form satisfies the boundedness
estimate: there exists
\eta_{Q}\geq 0
such that

(28)

\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\varpi_{i}s^{Q}\!\left({\boldsymbol{w},v_{i};\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}}\right)\Big|\leq\eta_{Q}\|\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\mathcal{U}^{Q}}\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\mathcal{U}^{Q}}\qquad\text{for all }\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{U}^{Q},

where
\|\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\mathcal{U}^{Q}}^{2}:=\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\varpi_{i}\|w_{i}\|_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}^{2}

and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}
is the coercivity norm induced by

a(\cdot,\cdot;\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i})
.

If
2\eta_{Q}<1
, then the discrete source iteration (
27
)
is a strict contraction on
\mathcal{U}^{Q}
in the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{U}^{Q}}
.
Consequently, for any initial guess
\boldsymbol{u}^{(0)}\in\mathcal{U}^{Q}
the iterates

\boldsymbol{u}^{(n)}
converge strongly in
\mathcal{U}^{Q}
to the unique solution

\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{U}^{Q}
of the discrete weak problem (
25
).

Proof.

The argument is identical to the continuum proof of
Proposition
3.2
, with the integrated forms over

\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
replaced by the quadrature sums

\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\varpi_{i}(\cdot)
and with the space
\mathcal{U}
replaced by
the product graph space
\mathcal{U}^{Q}
. In particular, taking differences
between successive iterates eliminates the fixed inflow datum, so the
error has homogeneous inflow trace on each
\Gamma_{-}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i})
.
Testing the discrete error equation with the discrete error, applying
coercivity of the transport part (the discrete analogue of
Theorem
3.1
with
\boldsymbol{\omega}
fixed to
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}
),
and then using (
28
) yields the contraction factor

2\eta_{Q}
.
∎

5.
Error control for the angular approximation

This section quantifies the error introduced by restricting and
discretising angle and by terminating the source iteration. Throughout we
work with the elastic model of Section
4
, that is,
(
4
) with the gain replaced by the elastic operator.

5.0.
Setting and error decomposition

Let
\mathcal{A}
denote either
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
or the cone

\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\star},\theta_{c})
from (
18
). Define the

\mathcal{A}
-restricted elastic gain

\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{el}}^{\mathcal{A}}[\psi](\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega}):=\int_{\mathcal{A}}\sigma_{\mathrm{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime},E)\psi(\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}.

Let
\psi^{\mathcal{A}}
denote the corresponding continuum solution of
(
4
) with
\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{el}}
replaced by

\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{el}}^{\mathcal{A}}
. In particular,

\psi^{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}=\psi
.

Fix an angular domain
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
(either

\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
or a cone) and an angular rule

\{(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j},w_{j})\}_{j=1}^{Q}\subset\mathcal{A}\times(0,\infty)
.
Let
\psi^{\mathcal{A}}
denote the continuum solution of the elastic
transport problem in which the gain integral is restricted to

\mathcal{A}
. Let
\{\psi_{i}^{Q}\}_{i=1}^{Q}
denote the exact solution of
the coupled
Q
-direction discrete-ordinates system (
21
) posed
on
\mathcal{A}
with the same angular rule, and let

\{\psi_{i}^{Q,n}\}_{i=1}^{Q}
be the
n
th source-iteration iterate.

Then, for each node
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}
,

(29)

\psi(\cdot,\cdot,\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i})-\psi_{i}^{Q,n}=\big(\psi(\cdot,\cdot,\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i})-\psi^{\mathcal{A}}(\cdot,\cdot,\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i})\big)+\big(\psi^{\mathcal{A}}(\cdot,\cdot,\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i})-\psi_{i}^{Q}\big)+\big(\psi_{i}^{Q}-\psi_{i}^{Q,n}\big).

The first term is a model-truncation contribution, it vanishes when

\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
(since then
\psi^{\mathcal{A}}=\psi
), and
when
\mathcal{A}
is a cone it measures the effect of omitting
scattering into directions outside
\mathcal{A}
. The second term is the
discrete-ordinates (quadrature/collocation) contribution for the

\mathcal{A}
-restricted model, it compares the continuum solution

\psi^{\mathcal{A}}
sampled at
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}
with the exact coupled DOM
solution on the same ordinates. The third term is the source-iteration
contribution for solving the coupled DOM system.

To transfer gain consistency bounds into solution bounds we use the
directional coercivity norm induced by the transport form. For each
direction
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}
define

\|w\|_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}^{2}:=\|\sigma_{T}^{1/2}w\|_{L^{2}(D\times I)}^{2}+\|(-S^{\prime})^{1/2}w\|_{L^{2}(D\times I)}^{2}+\int_{\Gamma_{+}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i})}\!\left({\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{\boldsymbol{x}}-S(E)n_{E}}\right)w^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma,

and the weighted product norm

\|\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\mathcal{U}^{Q}}^{2}:=\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\varpi_{i}\|w_{i}\|_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}^{2}.

5.1 Lemma

(Directional stability)
.

Fix
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}
and consider two right-hand sides

F,\widetilde{F}\in\mathcal{U}_{i}^{\prime}
and two solutions

u,\widetilde{u}\in\mathcal{U}_{i}
with the same inflow data such that

a(u,v;\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i})=F(v),\qquad a(\widetilde{u},v;\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i})=\widetilde{F}(v)\qquad\text{for all }v\in\mathcal{U}_{i}.

Then

\|u-\widetilde{u}\|_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}\leq 2\|F-\widetilde{F}\|_{\mathcal{U}_{i}^{\prime}}.

Proof.

Subtract the two identities and test with
v=u-\widetilde{u}
. The error
has homogeneous inflow trace, so Theorem
3.1
gives

\tfrac{1}{2}\|u-\widetilde{u}\|_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}^{2}\leq|(F-\widetilde{F})(u-\widetilde{u})|\leq\|F-\widetilde{F}\|_{\mathcal{U}_{i}^{\prime}}\|u-\widetilde{u}\|_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}
.
∎

5.1.
Quadrature consistency on
\mathcal{A}

For an integrand
f:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}
set

I_{\mathcal{A}}(f):=\int_{\mathcal{A}}f(\boldsymbol{\omega})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega},\qquad Q_{\mathcal{A}}(f):=\sum_{j=1}^{Q}\mu_{j}f(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j}).

Define the angular fill distance

h_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}:=\sup_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\mathcal{A}}\min_{1\leq j\leq Q}\arccos\!\left({\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j}}\right).

If the rule is exact for spherical polynomials on
\mathcal{A}
up to
total degree
m
and
f\in W^{m+1,1}(\mathcal{A})
, then there exists

C=C(m,d)
such that

(30)

|I_{\mathcal{A}}(f)-Q_{\mathcal{A}}(f)|\leq Ch_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}^{m+1}\sum_{|\alpha|=m+1}\|D_{\tan}^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{A})}.

Applied pointwise in
(\boldsymbol{x},E)
to the elastic gain integrand

f(\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime})=\sigma_{\mathrm{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime},E)\psi^{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime})
, (
30
) bounds the
gain consistency defect

\!\left({\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{el}}^{\mathcal{A}}[\psi^{\mathcal{A}}]}\right)(\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i})-\sum_{j=1}^{Q}\mu_{j}\sigma_{\mathrm{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j},E)\psi^{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j}).

Using Lemma
5.1
yields the corresponding bound
on
\psi_{i}^{\mathcal{A}}-\psi_{i}^{Q}
.

5.2 Proposition

(Quadrature-induced angular discretisation error)
.

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
. Assume moreover
that the angular rule on
\mathcal{A}
is exact for spherical polynomials
up to degree
m
and that for each
i
the gain integrand

\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}\mapsto\sigma_{\mathrm{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime},E)\psi^{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime})
belongs to
W^{m+1,1}(\mathcal{A})

for a.e.
(\boldsymbol{x},E)\in D\times I
, with tangential derivatives
integrable in
(\boldsymbol{x},E)
. Then there exists
C_{\mathrm{ang}}=C(m,d)>0

such that for each
i=1,\dots,Q
,

\|\psi_{i}^{\mathcal{A}}-\psi_{i}^{Q}\|_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}\leq C_{\mathrm{ang}}h_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}^{m+1}\Xi_{m+1}^{(i)}(\psi^{\mathcal{A}},\sigma_{\mathrm{el}}),

where

\Xi_{m+1}^{(i)}(\psi^{\mathcal{A}},\sigma_{\mathrm{el}}):=\sum_{|\alpha|=m+1}\big\|D_{\tan}^{\alpha}\!\big(\sigma_{\mathrm{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\cdot,E)\psi^{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{x},E,\cdot)\big)\big\|_{L^{1}\big(D\times I;L^{1}(\mathcal{A})\big)}.

5.2.
Cone truncation

If
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
this contribution vanishes. Otherwise

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\star},\theta_{c})
and the truncated gain
omits scattering from
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\setminus\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\star},\theta_{c})
.
For any
f:\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\to\mathbb{R}
,

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}f(\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}-\int_{\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\star},\theta_{c})}f(\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\setminus\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\star},\theta_{c})}f(\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}.

A crude estimate is

(31)

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\setminus\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\star},\theta_{c})}f(\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}\Big|\leq\sup_{\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\setminus\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\star},\theta_{c})}|f(\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime})|\big|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\setminus\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\star},\theta_{c})\big|.

Moreover, for forward-peaked kernels a more informative measure is the
cone tail

e_{\mathrm{cone}}(E):=\sup_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\star},\theta_{c})}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\setminus\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\star},\theta_{c})}\sigma_{\mathrm{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime},E)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}.

This quantity measures the missing elastic gain at energy
E
. It enters
(
29
) through the truncation term

\psi(\cdot,\cdot,\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i})-\psi^{\mathcal{A}}(\cdot,\cdot,\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i})
.

5.2.
Source-iteration error and stopping

By Proposition
4.4
, the discrete source iteration is a
contraction in
\mathcal{U}^{Q}
provided the discrete scattering operator
has bound
\eta_{Q}
in
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{U}^{Q}}
satisfying
2\eta_{Q}<1
.
With
\rho:=2\eta_{Q}\in(0,1)
and
\boldsymbol{u}^{(\infty)}
denoting the exact
solution of (
25
),

(32)

\|\boldsymbol{u}^{(n)}-\boldsymbol{u}^{(\infty)}\|_{\mathcal{U}^{Q}}\leq\rho^{n}\|\boldsymbol{u}^{(0)}-\boldsymbol{u}^{(\infty)}\|_{\mathcal{U}^{Q}},

and the contraction property yields the a posteriori estimate

(33)

\|\boldsymbol{u}^{(n)}-\boldsymbol{u}^{(\infty)}\|_{\mathcal{U}^{Q}}\leq\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}\|\boldsymbol{u}^{(n)}-\boldsymbol{u}^{(n-1)}\|_{\mathcal{U}^{Q}}.

Hence, for a prescribed tolerance
\varepsilon>0
, it suffices to stop
when

\|\boldsymbol{u}^{(n)}-\boldsymbol{u}^{(n-1)}\|_{\mathcal{U}^{Q}}\leq\frac{1-\rho}{\rho}\varepsilon.

(Angular discretisation, truncation and iteration error)

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem

5.4 Corollary

(Separable tolerance allocation)
.

In the setting of Theorem

, a sufficient
condition for

\|(\psi-\psi_{i}^{\mathcal{A}})(\cdot,\cdot,\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i})\|_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}\leq\tfrac{\varepsilon}{3},\qquad C_{\mathrm{ang}}h_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}^{m+1}\Xi_{m+1}^{(i)}(\psi^{\mathcal{A}},\sigma_{\mathrm{el}})\leq\tfrac{\varepsilon}{3},\qquad\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}\|\boldsymbol{u}^{(n)}-\boldsymbol{u}^{(n-1)}\|_{\mathcal{U}^{Q}}\leq\tfrac{\varepsilon}{3C_{\mathrm{stab}}}.

5.5 Example

(Henyey–Greenstein on

6.
Numerical Experiments: Proton Transport

We now present numerical experiments to verify correctness of the
discretisation, assess convergence of the source iteration scheme and
investigate behaviour across representative forward-peaked scattering
regimes. All simulations are conducted using a uniform Cartesian mesh
and a piecewise constant angular discretisation unless stated otherwise.

6.0.
Implementation of the numerical algorithm

We solve the angularly discrete transport system by source iteration,
using the same discrete-ordinates quadrature framework as in
Section
4
. In particular, we work with quadrature nodes

\{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{Q}
and weights
\{\mu_{j}\}_{j=1}^{Q}
on the
chosen angular domain (full sphere or cone). The angularly discrete
unknowns are the directional values

\Psi_{j}(\boldsymbol{x},E):=\psi(\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j}),\qquad j=1,\dots,Q.

In these experiments we restrict to elastic Coulomb scattering and
therefore use the elastic kernel
\sigma_{\mathrm{el}}
from
Section
4
. The discrete elastic gain at node

\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}
is approximated by the midpoint quadrature rule,

(35)

\int_{\mathcal{A}}\sigma_{\mathrm{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime},E)\psi(\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}\approx\sum_{j=1}^{Q}\mu_{j}\bar{\sigma}_{\mathrm{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j},E)\Psi_{j}(\boldsymbol{x},E),

where
\bar{\sigma}_{\mathrm{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j},E)
is a
quadrature-consistent approximation of the kernel evaluated at the node

\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j}
(in the simplest case,

\bar{\sigma}_{\mathrm{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j},E)=\sigma_{\mathrm{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j},E)
).

To ensure discrete conservation of total fluence (gain–loss balance), we
enforce a discrete analogue of the identity in
Proposition
4.2
. Writing

\Sigma_{\mathrm{el}}(E):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\sigma_{\mathrm{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime},E)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega},

(which is independent of
\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}
under rotational invariance),
we impose the weighted row-sum condition

(36)

\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\varpi_{i}\bar{\sigma}_{\mathrm{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j},E)=\Sigma_{\mathrm{el}}(E),\qquad j=1,\dots,Q.

Equivalently, for any angular vector
\{\Psi_{j}(\boldsymbol{x},E)\}_{j=1}^{Q}
one has

\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\varpi_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{Q}\mu_{j}\bar{\sigma}_{\mathrm{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j},E)\Psi_{j}(\boldsymbol{x},E)=\Sigma_{\mathrm{el}}(E)\sum_{j=1}^{Q}\mu_{j}\Psi_{j}(\boldsymbol{x},E),

so the discrete elastic gain balances the corresponding loss in the
weighted angular sum.

6.1 Remark

(Relation to a finite-volume interpretation in angle)
.

Although we implement scattering via quadrature nodes and weights, the
formula (
35
) can be read as a finite-volume
exchange between angular control volumes: the weights
\mu_{j}
play the
role of angular “cell measures”, while

\bar{\sigma}_{\mathrm{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j},E)
acts as an
energy-dependent transfer rate from direction
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j}
to

\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}
. Condition (
36
)
then corresponds to conservation of total fluence in the angular
finite-volume balance.

When the angular domain is restricted to a forward cone, some fluence
is necessarily lost at the boundary of the cone. In regimes where the
scattering kernel is strongly forward-peaked (as is typical in proton
and ion transport), this loss is negligible in practice.

The angularly discrete system seeks functions
\Psi_{i}:D\times I\to\mathbb{R}

for
i=1,\dots,Q
satisfying

(37)

\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}\cdot\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\Psi_{i}(\boldsymbol{x},E)-\partial_{E}\!\left({S(E)\Psi_{i}(\boldsymbol{x},E)}\right)+\sigma_{T}(E)\Psi_{i}(\boldsymbol{x},E)=\sum_{j=1}^{Q}\mu_{j}\bar{\sigma}_{\mathrm{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j},E)\Psi_{j}(\boldsymbol{x},E),

which is the discrete-ordinates model (
21
) with the elastic
scattering sum realised via the quadrature-consistent coefficients

\bar{\sigma}_{\mathrm{el}}
. We solve (
37
) by the source
iteration method introduced in Section
4.3
. The
algorithmic structure is summarised in Algorithm
1
.

6.1.
Dose functional and discrete dose notation

The transport solver produces an approximation of the phase-space
density
\psi(\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega})
. In the numerical experiments we
primarily report dose-like outputs obtained by integrating
\psi
against
an energy-deposition weight. Concretely, for a given stopping power

S(E)
we define the (unnormalised) dose functional

(38)

\mathscr{D}(\boldsymbol{x}):=\int_{I}\int_{\mathcal{A}}S(E)\psi(\boldsymbol{x},E,\boldsymbol{\omega})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}\,\mathrm{d}E,

where
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
in the full angular model and

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\star},\theta_{c})
when a cone is used.
(Any constant factors converting energy deposition to physical dose in
Gy are absorbed into the overall normalisation in these tests.)

For the angularly discrete model with quadrature nodes

\{(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\varpi_{i})\}_{i=1}^{Q}\subset\mathcal{A}\times(0,\infty)
, let

\Psi_{i}(\boldsymbol{x},E)
denote the computed directional solution at

\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}
. The corresponding discrete dose is defined by replacing
the angular integral in (
38
) by quadrature and evaluating
the remaining energy integral numerically on the chosen energy grid:

(39)

\mathscr{D}^{Q}(\boldsymbol{x}):=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{E}}w_{k}\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\varpi_{i}S(E_{k})\Psi_{i}(\boldsymbol{x},E_{k}),

where
\{E_{k},w_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N_{E}}
are the energy nodes and weights used in
the implementation. When a reference solution is available analytically,
we compute the corresponding reference dose
\mathscr{D}
from (
38
)
using the same energy quadrature to isolate the spatial discretisation
error.

model this by the distributional kernel

(40)

\sigma_{\mathrm{el}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime},E)=\Sigma_{\mathrm{el}}(E)\delta(\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}),

Experiment 3: Simulations with a Henyey–Greenstein cross section

To model anisotropic (forward-peaked) angular scattering we use the
Henyey–Greenstein phase function on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
,

(43)

p_{\gamma}(\theta)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{1-\gamma^{2}}{1+\gamma^{2}-2\gamma\operatorname{cos}\left(\theta\right)},\qquad\theta\in[-\pi,\pi),

which satisfies the normalisation
\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}p_{\gamma}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta=1
.
For
\gamma\in(0,1)
the distribution is strongly forward-peaked, whereas

\gamma<0
corresponds to backscattering.

In these experiments we work in
d=2
, so directions are parametrised by
an angle
\theta
via
\boldsymbol{\omega}(\theta)=(\operatorname{cos}\left(\theta\right),\operatorname{sin}\left(\theta\right))
.
We take an energy-independent, rotationally invariant elastic kernel of
the form

\sigma_{\mathrm{el}}\!\big(\boldsymbol{\omega}(\theta),\boldsymbol{\omega}(\theta^{\prime})\big)=\Sigma_{\mathrm{el}}p_{\gamma}(\theta-\theta^{\prime}),

so that the corresponding gain is the circular convolution

\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{el}}[\psi](\boldsymbol{x},E,\theta)=\Sigma_{\mathrm{el}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}p_{\gamma}(\theta-\theta^{\prime})\psi(\boldsymbol{x},E,\theta^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}.

This kernel is isotropic about the current direction in the sense that it
depends only on the relative turning angle
\theta-\theta^{\prime}
.

Figure
8
shows
p_{\gamma}
for
\gamma=0.9
together
with its piecewise-constant approximation on the angular bins used in
the computation. Concretely, if
\{B_{j}\}_{j=1}^{Q}
is a partition of

[-\pi,\pi)
with bin measures
\mu_{j}:=|B_{j}|
and midpoints

\theta_{j}\in B_{j}
, then the bar height in bin
B_{j}
is the bin average

\bar{p}_{j}:=\mu_{j}^{-1}\int_{B_{j}}p_{\gamma}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta
, so that

p_{\gamma}\approx\sum_{j=1}^{Q}\bar{p}_{j}\mathbf{1}_{B_{j}}
and

\sum_{j=1}^{Q}\mu_{j}\bar{p}_{j}=1
.

7.
Numerical Experiments: Carbon Ion Transport

Unlike protons, carbon ions undergo nuclear interactions and
fragmentation in water, producing a broad spectrum of secondary
particles. The goal of this section is a demonstration of a
multi-species workflow built on the same characteristic sweeping
infrastructure as the proton solver. A scattered carbon primary field
is computed first, then secondary protons and neutrons are generated
from the converged carbon solution through prescribed volumetric
sources and transported in reduced models. Our emphasis is
out-of-field exposure, so we focus on neutrons (long-range transport
and relevance to protection quantities) and include secondary protons
as a simple charged-fragment proxy.

7.0.
Experiment 6: Out-of-field secondaries in carbon transport (protons and neutrons)

Carbon primaries are transported with continuous slowing down driven by
a tabulated stopping power. Let
S_{C}(E)
denote the linear stopping
power (MeV/cm) for carbon ions in water expressed in terms of the total
ion energy
E
(MeV). As in the proton experiments, we work with an
angularly discrete representation and compute directional carbon fluence

\Psi_{i}^{C}:D\times I\to\mathbb{R}
,
i=1,\dots,Q
, from

(45)

\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}\cdot\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\Psi^{C}_{i}(\boldsymbol{x},E)-\partial_{E}\!\left({S_{C}(E)\Psi^{C}_{i}(\boldsymbol{x},E)}\right)+\sigma_{T}^{C}(E)\Psi^{C}_{i}(\boldsymbol{x},E)=\sum_{j=1}^{Q}\mu_{j}\bar{\sigma}_{\mathrm{el}}^{C}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j})\Psi^{C}_{j}(\boldsymbol{x},E),

supplemented by a forward inflow carbon beam (supported only in the
central angular node, with the remaining inflow bins set to zero). The
right-hand side is treated by source iteration, with each iteration step
consisting of
Q
independent method-of-characteristics sweeps. The
characteristic map required by the sweep is defined using a fitted
Bragg–Kleeman range law
R_{\mathrm{BK}}(E)=\alpha_{C}E^{p_{C}}
, while all
dose postprocessing uses the tabulated
S_{C}(E)
.

From the converged carbon solution we form the scalar carbon fluence and
dose

(46)

\Phi^{C}(\boldsymbol{x},E):=\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\varpi_{i}\Psi^{C}_{i}(\boldsymbol{x},E),\qquad D_{C}^{Q}(\boldsymbol{x}):=\int_{I}S_{C}(E)\Phi^{C}(\boldsymbol{x},E)\,\mathrm{d}E.

To drive secondaries we introduce an effective macroscopic nuclear
interaction rate
\Sigma_{\mathrm{nuc}}(E)
and define the interaction
proxy

(47)

\mathcal{I}^{C}(\boldsymbol{x}):=\int_{I}\Sigma_{\mathrm{nuc}}(E)\Phi^{C}(\boldsymbol{x},E)\,\mathrm{d}E,

which concentrates secondary production in regions of high primary
fluence and high interaction probability. Secondary protons and neutrons
are then generated by volumetric sources that factorise into a spatial
production rate
\mathcal{I}^{C}(\boldsymbol{x})
, an emitted-energy density and a
simple angular distribution. Concretely, we take

(48)

Q_{i}^{P}(\boldsymbol{x},E_{P})=Y_{P}\mathcal{I}^{C}(\boldsymbol{x})w_{P}(E_{P})\frac{1}{Q},\qquad Q_{i}^{N}(\boldsymbol{x},E_{N})=Y_{N}\mathcal{I}^{C}(\boldsymbol{x})w_{N}(E_{N})\frac{1}{Q},

where
Y_{P}
and
Y_{N}
are yield parameters, while
w_{P}
and
w_{N}
are
prescribed energy densities. The uniform
1/Q
factor corresponds to
an isotropic redistribution over the discrete ordinates; this can be
replaced by a forward-peaked emission model or by a data-driven
angular distribution without changing the solver structure.

Given the sources, secondary protons are transported with ionisation
loss only,

(49)

\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}\cdot\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\Psi^{P}_{i}(\boldsymbol{x},E_{P})-\partial_{E_{P}}\!\left({S_{P}(E_{P})\Psi^{P}_{i}(\boldsymbol{x},E_{P})}\right)=Q_{i}^{P}(\boldsymbol{x},E_{P}),

with no inflow contribution, while neutrons are propagated in a reduced
streaming–removal model with no energy loss term,

(50)

\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}\cdot\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\Psi^{N}_{i}(\boldsymbol{x},E_{N})+\sigma_{T}^{N}(E_{N})\Psi^{N}_{i}(\boldsymbol{x},E_{N})=Q_{i}^{N}(\boldsymbol{x},E_{N}),

again with no inflow contribution. The associated secondary doses are
computed from the scalar secondary fluences

\Phi^{P}=\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\varpi_{i}\Psi_{i}^{P}
and
\Phi^{N}=\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\varpi_{i}\Psi_{i}^{N}

as

(51)

D_{P}(\boldsymbol{x}):=\int_{I_{P}}S_{P}(E_{P})\Phi^{P}(\boldsymbol{x},E_{P})\,\mathrm{d}E_{P},\qquad D_{N}(\boldsymbol{x}):=\int_{I_{N}}\kappa_{N}(E_{N})\Phi^{N}(\boldsymbol{x},E_{N})\,\mathrm{d}E_{N},

where
\kappa_{N}
is a prescribed kerma coefficient. The total dose is

D(\boldsymbol{x}):=D_{C}^{Q}(\boldsymbol{x})+D_{P}(\boldsymbol{x})+D_{N}(\boldsymbol{x})
.

7.1 Remark

(Scope of secondaries and follow-on modelling)
.

Carbon–water nuclear interactions generate many secondary
species. The present experiment is intentionally reduced. It is
motivated by out-of-field endpoints, we retain neutrons as the
principal long-range component and include secondary protons as a
simple charged-fragment proxy. The source construction
(
48
) is designed to be replaced by
data-driven yields and emitted spectra and extended to additional
fragment species and protection quantities without changing the
transport solvers or postprocessing. This is developed further in
the follow-on study
[
5
]
.

Some stability results for the fractional differential equations with two delays
Pragati Dutta, Sachin Bhalekar

Abstract
This paper investigates the stability properties of a nonlinear fractional differential equation with two discrete delays and a delay-dependent coefficient. Such equations arise in various biological and control systems where temporal delays influence feedback mechanisms. Two cases are analyzed: one where the first delay is zero, and the second acts through an exponential coefficient, and another where the first delay is fixed. We derive delay-independent stability conditions using linearization, characteristic equations, and bifurcation theory, along with complete theoretical proofs. The results are validated through numerical simulations and stability diagrams.

6
Conclusion

This paper investigated the stability of a fractional-order delay differential equation with a delay-dependent coefficient and two discrete delays. By linearizing the system and analyzing the characteristic equation, we derived both delay-independent and delay-dependent stability conditions. We studied two cases:
\tau_{1}=0
and
\tau_{1}=1
in detail. The numerical simulations and stability diagrams supported the theoretical findings.

The results show that the interaction between fractional order, feedback strength, and delay-dependent coefficients has a major influence on system stability. In particular, the presence of such coefficients can cause stability switches and transitions. These insights are useful for modeling biological and control systems where feedback and memory effects are important.

Future studies may extend this work to a deeper bifurcation analysis, nonlinear models with multiple delays, and stabilization methods for unstable states.

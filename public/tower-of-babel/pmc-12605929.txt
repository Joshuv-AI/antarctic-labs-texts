Molecular mechanisms and genetic predisposition to osteoarthritis and osteosarcoma through Mendelian inheritance patterns
Authors: Hao Fei, Wen Huang, Yueyuan Chen, Shuo Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOsteoarthritis and osteosarcoma are important orthopedic diseases whose genetic susceptibility and molecular mechanisms require further investigation. This study aims to systematically analyze the genetic variation characteristics and cellular molecular networks of these two diseases through Mendelian genetic approaches.MethodsGenome-wide association study (GWAS) was employed to identify genetic susceptibility loci for osteoarthritis, and protein-protein interaction networks were constructed to analyze key gene functions. Single-cell RNA sequencing was performed on osteosarcoma tissues, with t-SNE dimensionality reduction analysis to identify cell subpopulations and intercellular communication network analysis.ResultsOsteoarthritis GWAS analysis revealed multiple significant peaks across 22 chromosomes, identifying genetic variant loci associated with disease susceptibility. Protein-protein interaction networks revealed functional associations among key regulatory genes including TP53, PIK3R3, AKT1, and BRCA1. Mitochondrial ribosomal protein genes of the MRPL family showed coordinated high expression in specific cell subsets, suggesting the important role of mitochondrial function in tumorigenesis. The findings provide important insights into understanding the genetic basis and molecular mechanisms of orthopedic diseases, laying a theoretical foundation for precision medicine and targeted therapeutic strategy development.Keywords: Osteoarthritis, Osteosarcoma, Mendelian genetics, Genome-wide association study, Single-cell transcriptomics.

Introduction

Osteoarthritis (OA) and osteosarcoma (OS), as two major diseases in the orthopedic field, represent the most common degenerative joint disease and the most important primary bone malignancy, respectively. Osteoarthritis affects over 300 million people worldwide, and with the accelerating process of population aging, its incidence shows a significant upward trend, imposing a heavy burden on the social healthcare system [1, 2]. Although osteosarcoma has a relatively low incidence rate, it primarily affects children and adolescents, exhibits high malignancy, and has a poor prognosis, with a five-year survival rate of only 60–70%, seriously threatening patients’ lives and health. Despite significant differences in clinical manifestations and pathogenesis between these two diseases, both involve pathological changes in bone tissue and demonstrate distinct genetic susceptibility characteristics. Our comparative approach is scientifically justified by several key considerations: First, both diseases represent significant burdens in orthopedic medicine, affecting bone and cartilage tissues through distinct yet potentially overlapping molecular pathways. Second, recent evidence suggests shared molecular mechanisms in bone metabolism, inflammatory responses, and matrix remodeling between degenerative and neoplastic bone diseases. Third, the application of Mendelian genetic principles provides a unified theoretical framework for understanding genetic susceptibility across different musculoskeletal disorders, enabling identification of common regulatory networks and potential therapeutic targets [3–5].

Traditional disease genetics research has primarily been based on family studies and candidate gene approaches, but these methods have limitations in identifying genetic factors of complex diseases. Mendelian genetic principles provide an important theoretical framework for understanding the genetic basis of diseases. By analyzing causal relationships between genetic variants and disease phenotypes, they can more accurately reveal genetic susceptibility mechanisms of diseases [6, 7]. In recent years, the rapid development of genome-wide association studies (GWAS) has provided powerful technical tools for large-scale identification of disease-associated genetic variants. GWAS can systematically detect associations between single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) and diseases across the entire genome, enabling unbiased discovery of novel disease susceptibility genes and laying the foundation for precision medicine research.

However, traditional GWAS analysis primarily focuses on statistical associations of genetic variants, lacking deep insights into complex molecular mechanisms and intercellular interactions during disease development and progression. With the revolutionary advancement of single-cell RNA sequencing technology, researchers can comprehensively analyze cellular heterogeneity and gene expression patterns at single-cell resolution, providing unprecedented opportunities for understanding disease molecular mechanisms. Single-cell transcriptomics not only enables identification of rare cell subpopulations but also reveals dynamic communication networks between cells, thus providing deep insights into complex cellular interactions within disease microenvironments [8–10].

Although genetic research on osteoarthritis and osteosarcoma has made certain progress, many knowledge gaps remain. First, existing osteoarthritis GWAS studies have mainly focused on European populations, with limited understanding of genetic susceptibility characteristics in other populations. Second, single-cell transcriptomic studies of osteosarcoma are relatively scarce, and the cellular heterogeneity and molecular characteristics of its tumor microenvironment have not been comprehensively characterized. Finally, there is a lack of comprehensive studies combining genetic susceptibility analysis with cellular molecular mechanism research, limiting deep understanding of disease pathogenesis. This study will not only help deepen understanding of the genetic basis of orthopedic diseases but also provide important scientific support for translational medicine research and clinical applications.

Methods

Study design and ethics approval

This study employed a multi-omics integrative analysis design, combining genome-wide association study (GWAS), protein-protein interaction network analysis, and single-cell transcriptomics to systematically investigate the genetic susceptibility of osteoarthritis and molecular mechanisms of osteosarcoma [9, 11]. All datasets utilized in this study were obtained from publicly available databases, and thus did not involve direct interactions with human subjects or collection of biological samples. Consequently, specific ethical approval or informed consent was not required. Nevertheless, data use complied strictly with the ethical guidelines and terms of use outlined by each respective database.The study strictly adhered to the relevant provisions of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Genome-wide association analysis of osteoarthritis

The osteoarthritis GWAS analysis was conducted using the FinnGen R11 dataset, specifically the “finngen_R11_PRIM_KNEEARTHROSIS” cohort, which represents one of the largest and most well-characterized European ancestry cohorts for osteoarthritis research. This dataset comprises 24,955 knee osteoarthritis cases and 164,682 controls from the Finnish population, totaling 189,637 individuals. The FinnGen project combines genotype data from Finnish biobanks and digital health record data from Finnish health registries, providing exceptional phenotype accuracy and population homogeneity.

Diagnostic criteria followed the osteoarthritis classification standards established by the American College of Rheumatology (ACR), with comprehensive assessment combining imaging examinations and clinical symptoms. Exclusion criteria included: autoimmune diseases such as rheumatoid arthritis and systemic lupus erythematosus, severe hepatic and renal dysfunction, and recent use of immunosuppressive therapy. Genome-wide genotyping was performed using Illumina and Affymetrix high-density SNP arrays covering approximately 8.5 million single nucleotide polymorphisms on autosomes after imputation using the Finnish reference panel. Quality control criteria included: (1) Individual level: genotyping success rate > 95%, exclusion of gender-inconsistent individuals, random exclusion of one individual from closely related pairs (IBD > 0.1875); (2) SNP level: genotyping success rate > 95%, minor allele frequency (MAF) > 0.01, Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium test P-value > 1 × 10^-6. After quality control, 8,547,321 SNPs and 189,637 individuals were included in the final analysis.

Logistic regression models were selected for case-control association analysis due to their appropriateness for binary phenotypes and robust performance in controlling population stratification. We compared this approach with alternative methods including linear mixed models and found logistic regression provided optimal balance of computational efficiency and statistical power for our sample size. Age, gender, body mass index (BMI), and the first 10 principal components from principal component analysis were included as covariates to control for population stratification effects. The genome-wide significance threshold was set at P < 5 × 10^-8, and the suggestive significance threshold was set at P < 1 × 10^-5. Manhattan plots were used to display GWAS results, with the x-axis representing genomic positions across 22 autosomes and the y-axis representing statistical significance of SNPs (-log10 P-values) [12, 13]. To establish causal relationships between genetic variants and osteoarthritis risk, we implemented two-sample Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis. Instrumental variables (IVs) were selected using genome-wide significant SNPs (P < 5 × 10⁻⁸) followed by linkage disequilibrium clumping (r² < 0.001, window = 10,000 kb) to ensure independence. We validated the three core MR assumptions: (1) Relevance assumption through F-statistics (F >10) and partial R² calculations; (2) Independence assumption by testing associations between instruments and confounders (age, sex, BMI, smoking); (3) Exclusion restriction assumption using MR-Egger regression, weighted median, and MR-PRESSO methods to detect horizontal pleiotropy. Sensitivity analyses included leave-one-out analysis and heterogeneity testing using Cochran’s Q statistic.

Construction and analysis of protein-protein interaction networks

Based on GWAS analysis results, genetic loci significantly associated with osteoarthritis were screened, and candidate genes were determined through functional annotation and positional information. Additionally, known osteoarthritis-related genes were supplemented through literature mining and database searches to construct a core gene set. Protein-protein interaction information was obtained from multiple high-quality databases including STRING, BioGRID, HPRD, REACTOME (for pathway-specific interactions), KEGG (for metabolic pathway networks), IntAct (for experimentally verified interactions), and DisGeNET (for disease-gene associations). A confidence threshold was set above 0.4, with additional confidence scoring system weighting interactions based on experimental evidence quality and database consensus. Information from multiple databases was integrated, removing duplicate and low-quality interaction data to construct a high-confidence protein-protein interaction network. Cytoscape software (version 3.9.0) was used for network visualization and topology analysis. We compared different centrality measures including degree centrality, betweenness centrality, and closeness centrality, selecting degree centrality as the primary metric based on its robust performance in identifying hub genes. Basic topological parameters of the network were calculated, including node degree, betweenness centrality, closeness centrality, and clustering coefficient. Community detection algorithms were implemented to identify functional modules, with pathway enrichment analysis performed using hypergeometric tests to understand biological significance of network clusters. Key nodes (hub genes) and functional modules in the network were identified, and functional association patterns between genes were analyzed.

Single-cell RNA sequencing analysis of osteosarcoma

The single-cell RNA sequencing data were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database, specifically datasets GSM5362559 and GSM5578183_L31, containing approximately 15,000 and 12,000 single cells respectively from osteosarcoma tissue samples. The 10x Genomics Chromium platform was used for single-cell capture and barcode labeling, capturing approximately 10,000 single cells per sample. Sequencing was performed on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform with a sequencing depth of approximately 50,000 reads per cell. Cell Ranger software was used for raw data processing, including sequence alignment, barcode identification, and gene expression quantification [14–16]. Quality control criteria were optimized for osteosarcoma tissue characteristics: (1) Number of genes detected per cell between 500 and 5000 (adjusted from initial 200–6000 based on tissue-specific analysis); (2) Mitochondrial gene expression ratio < 15% (optimized for tumor tissue); (3) Each gene expressed in at least 3 cells. Sensitivity analyses demonstrated robustness of clustering results across different parameter settings. The Seurat package was used for data normalization and batch effect correction. We compared different normalization methods (SCTransform vs. LogNormalize) and selected SCTransform based on superior performance in reducing technical variation. Principal component analysis (PCA) was used for dimensionality reduction, with the first 30 principal components selected for subsequent analysis. UMAP (n_neighbors = 15, min_dist = 0.3, n_components = 2) and t-SNE (perplexity = 30, learning rate = 200, max iterations = 1000) algorithms were used for nonlinear dimensionality reduction visualization with fully documented parameters for reproducibility. Cell clustering was performed based on shared nearest neighbor (SNN) graphs with resolution parameter set to 0.5. Each cell cluster was annotated through differential expression gene analysis and known marker gene expression patterns, validated using multiple automated annotation tools including SingleR, CellTypist, and manual annotation based on canonical markers for cross-validation. To understand cellular dynamics and developmental trajectories, we implemented pseudotime trajectory analysis using Monocle3 to reconstruct differentiation pathways and identify key transition states. Velocity analysis was performed using velocyto.py and scVelo to infer directional cell state transitions. Differential abundance testing compared cell composition between different conditions using statistical methods appropriate for compositional data.

Intercellular communication network analysis

The CellChat package was used to analyze intercellular communication networks based on ligand-receptor pair information from the CellChatDB database. Communication probabilities between each pair of cell types were calculated to identify significant intercellular interactions. Expression threshold was set at 10%, meaning ligand or receptor genes had to be expressed in at least 10% of cells to be included in the analysis. Activity of different signaling pathways in various cell types was analyzed comprehensively, including growth factor signaling, cytokine signaling, extracellular matrix signaling, and immune checkpoint pathways. Sending and receiving intensities of signaling pathways were calculated to identify key signal-sending and signal-receiving cells. Intercellular communication network diagrams were drawn with enhanced visualization parameters: node size representing cell type importance and line thickness representing communication intensity, with optimized color schemes ensuring sufficient contrast between cell types. Sankey diagrams were used to display ligand-receptor interaction patterns in specific signaling pathways, clearly showing signal transmission pathways from sender to receiver cells.

Functional enrichment analysis

GO functional enrichment analysis was performed on differentially expressed genes, including three levels: Biological Process (BP), Cellular Component (CC), and Molecular Function (MF). Hypergeometric tests with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction (FDR < 0.05) were consistently applied across all enrichment analyses to maintain statistical rigor. Terms having P-value < 0.05 and false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05 were considered significantly enriched. Metabolic pathway and signaling pathway enrichment analysis was performed based on the KEGG database to identify key biological pathways related to diseases. Bubble plots with enhanced visualization were used to display enrichment results, with bubble size representing the number of enriched genes, color intensity representing significance level using scientifically appropriate color scales (viridis palette), and improved contrast ratios for publication quality [17, 18].

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R language (version 4.2.0). Statistical methodology was carefully selected and justified: logistic regression for GWAS analysis due to binary phenotype appropriateness, Wilcoxon rank-sum tests with Bonferroni correction for single-cell differential expression analysis, and hypergeometric tests for pathway enrichment. Power analyses were conducted for GWAS studies calculating power to detect effect sizes of 1.1–1.5 odds ratios at genome-wide significance levels. For single-cell analysis, power estimation used simulation-based approaches to ensure adequate statistical power for biological conclusions. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range), and categorical variables were expressed as frequency (percentage). Group comparisons were performed using Student’s t-test, Mann-Whitney U test, or chi-square test as appropriate. Multiple comparison correction was standardized using the Benjamini-Hochberg method across all analyses to control false discovery rate (FDR < 0.05). P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were two-sided tests.

The SNPs exceeding the genome-wide significance threshold suggest potential osteoarthritis susceptibility gene regions that warrant further investigation.

The protein-protein interaction network (Fig. 1B) illustrates the functional relationships among 127 key genes identified from the GWAS analysis, integrated from multiple databases with enhanced confidence scoring. This network encompasses several critical functional genes including TP53, a tumor suppressor gene crucial for cell cycle regulation; PIK3R3 and AKT1, which participate in the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway regulating cell survival and proliferation; BRCA1, involved in DNA repair mechanisms; EP300, functioning as a transcriptional co-activator; and MAPK3, a mitogen-activated protein kinase participating in cellular signal transduction. Functional enrichment analysis and protein-protein interaction network construction of differentially expressed genes in osteosarcoma

This study systematically analyzed osteosarcoma-related genes through bioinformatics approaches. Pathway enrichment analysis (Fig. 2A) with enhanced statistical rigor (FDR < 0.05, n = 2,847 differentially expressed genes) revealed that differentially expressed genes in osteosarcoma were primarily enriched in immune-related pathways, with allograft rejection showing the highest enrichment significance (P = 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁸, FDR = 3.4 × 10⁻¹⁶), followed by intestinal immune network for IgA production and type I diabetes mellitus pathways. These findings suggest that the immune system plays a crucial role in osteosarcoma development and progression, with specific emphasis on adaptive immune responses and autoimmune-related pathways potentially contributing to tumor immunosurveillance mechanisms. Additionally, viral infection-related pathways including influenza A, human T-cell leukemia virus 1 infection, and Epstein-Barr virus infection also demonstrated significant enrichment, indicating potential associations between viral infections and osteosarcoma pathogenesis, possibly through oncoviral mechanisms or immune system perturbations.

The protein-protein interaction network (Fig. 2C) further illustrated complex interaction patterns among osteosarcoma-related proteins, with a highly connected protein cluster forming at the network center, suggesting these proteins may collectively participate in key biological processes underlying osteosarcoma.

Single-cell transcriptomic analysis of osteosarcoma reveals cellular heterogeneity and gene expression signatures

Figure 3A presents a single-cell landscape of osteosarcoma tissue (n = 27,143 cells after quality control) based on t-SNE dimensionality reduction analysis with optimized parameters, successfully identifying 11 distinct cell types. These cell populations form clear clustering patterns in two-dimensional space, indicating significant transcriptomic differences between different cell types. The major cell types include tumor-associated osteoblasts (18.3% of total cells), chondrocytes (12.7%), and osteoclasts (8.4%), as well as immune cells in the tumor microenvironment such as macrophages (15.2%), T cells (11.8%), and B cells (7.3%). Notably, we identified nervous system-related cells including neurons (3.2%), oligodendrocytes (2.8%), astrocytes (4.1%), and microglia (6.7%), challenging traditional views of osteosarcoma composition. Stromal components included synovial fibroblasts (5.9%) and pericytes (3.6%). Figure 3B provides a detailed visualization of specific marker gene expression patterns across different cell types through a dot plot. The size of dots represents the percentage of cells expressing each gene, while color intensity reflects the average expression level. This visualization method clearly reveals the molecular characteristics of each cell type, with high-confidence marker genes showing specificity indices > 0.8 and expression in > 70% of cells within respective clusters. By comparing gene expression profiles across different cell types, we can gain deep insights into the cellular compositional complexity within osteosarcoma tissue and the functional properties of various cell subpopulations, laying an important foundation for subsequent tumor pathogenesis research and therapeutic target discovery.

Discussion

This study, based on Mendelian genetic principles, systematically explored the genetic susceptibility mechanisms of osteoarthritis and osteosarcoma, providing new perspectives for understanding the pathogenesis of these two important orthopedic diseases. Mendelian genetics emphasizes the causal relationship between genetic variants and phenotypes, and this theoretical framework has important guiding significance in complex disease research. Our Mendelian randomization analysis successfully demonstrated causal relationships between genetic variants and osteoarthritis risk, with instrumental variables explaining 2.8% of phenotypic variance and showing no evidence of horizontal pleiotropy, thus strengthening the causal inference beyond traditional association studies. Traditional association studies often only reveal statistical associations without establishing causal relationships, while analytical methods based on Mendelian genetics can better infer the direct impact of genetic variants on disease risk, reducing interference from environmental factors and reverse causation.

Osteoarthritis, as a multifactorial complex disease, involves the interaction of multiple factors including genetics, environment, age, and gender [2, 19]. Our comprehensive phenotype stratification revealed important heterogeneity in genetic effects: sex-stratified analysis showed stronger genetic effects in females (OR = 1.31) compared to males (OR = 1.18), potentially reflecting hormonal influences on cartilage metabolism. The polygenic risk scores demonstrated clinical utility with area under the curve (AUC) of 0.67 for osteoarthritis prediction, suggesting potential applications in risk stratification and early intervention strategies. The multiple significant peaks identified through GWAS analysis in this study not only validated known osteoarthritis susceptibility genes but also discovered new potential pathogenic loci. These findings strengthen the important role of genetic factors in osteoarthritis pathogenesis, with genetic factors estimated to explain 40–60% of osteoarthritis susceptibility. Particularly noteworthy is that the genetic variants we identified are mainly enriched in gene regions related to cartilage metabolism, inflammatory response, and extracellular matrix remodeling, which is highly consistent with the pathophysiological mechanisms of osteoarthritis.

Single-cell transcriptomic analysis of osteosarcoma revealed unprecedented complexity of the tumor microenvironment, successfully identifying 11 different cell types, which significantly expanded our understanding of osteosarcoma cellular composition. Our analysis of 27,143 single cells with optimized quality control parameters and multiple validation approaches provides high-confidence cell type identification and reveals the dynamic nature of the tumor microenvironment through pseudotime analysis. Traditional bulk RNA sequencing methods often mask cellular heterogeneity within tumor tissues due to averaging effects of mixed cells, while the application of single-cell technology enables us to precisely analyze molecular characteristics of each cell type at single-cell resolution.

Particularly striking is our discovery of nervous system-related cells in osteosarcoma tissue, including neurons (3.2%), oligodendrocytes (2.8%), astrocytes (4.1%), and microglia (6.7%). This finding challenges the traditional view that osteosarcoma is mainly composed of mesenchymal cells and provides quantitative evidence for neural involvement in bone tumors. Functional analysis revealed that these neural cells express genes associated with neurotransmitter signaling (GABA, glutamate pathways) and neural development (NEUROD1, SOX2), suggesting active neural remodeling within the tumor microenvironment. The interaction between the nervous system and bone tissue has received increasing attention in recent years, with neural regulation considered an important factor affecting bone metabolism and tumor progression [20–22]. Our findings provide important clues for further studying the mechanisms of neuro-bone interactions in osteosarcoma.

The coordinated high expression pattern of mitochondrial ribosomal protein gene MRPL family in specific cell subsets revealed the critical role of mitochondrial function in osteosarcoma development and progression. Our analysis showed strong co-expression (r > 0.75) of MRPL20, MRPL21, MRPL22, and MRPL18, particularly in proliferating osteoblasts and tumor-associated macrophages. Recent literature demonstrates that MRPL proteins are essential for mitochondrial ribosome assembly and protein synthesis, with their upregulation reflecting metabolic reprogramming toward oxidative phosphorylation in tumor cells. This metabolic shift may represent a therapeutic vulnerability, as targeting mitochondrial function through OXPHOS inhibitors could selectively affect tumor cells with high MRPL expression. Mitochondria, as the powerhouse of cells, have functional abnormalities closely related to metabolic reprogramming in various tumors. Abnormal expression of MRPL genes may affect mitochondrial protein synthesis, thereby influencing cellular energy metabolism and proliferative capacity. This discovery not only deepens our understanding of osteosarcoma metabolic characteristics but also provides theoretical basis for therapeutic strategies targeting mitochondrial function.

The protein-protein interaction network constructed in this study successfully integrated isolated genetic information from multiple databases with enhanced confidence scoring, revealing functional associations and regulatory hierarchies among 127 osteoarthritis-related genes. Network topology analysis identified five major functional modules with significant pathway enrichment, providing systematic insights into disease mechanisms beyond individual gene effects. Key genes at the network center, such as TP53, PIK3R3, AKT1, and BRCA1, not only play important roles in osteoarthritis pathogenesis but are also associated with various other diseases, reflecting shared molecular mechanisms between diseases. TP53, as the “guardian of the genome,” plays a central role in maintaining cellular homeostasis and DNA repair. In osteoarthritis, TP53 abnormalities may lead to excessive apoptosis and matrix degradation of chondrocytes, accelerating the degeneration process of articular cartilage. The PI3K/AKT signaling pathway is a key pathway regulating cell survival, proliferation, and metabolism, and its abnormal activation is closely related to abnormal proliferation and inflammatory responses of chondrocytes [23, 24]. Although BRCA1 is mainly known for its role in breast cancer, recent studies have found that it also plays an important role in bone metabolism and joint health. The advantage of this network analysis approach lies in its ability to identify functional modules and regulatory centers of disease-related genes, providing a holistic perspective for understanding systematic mechanisms of complex diseases. By analyzing network topological features, we can identify “hub genes” that play key roles in disease development and progression, which are often important candidates for drug target discovery.

Intercellular communication network analysis revealed complex cellular interaction patterns in the osteosarcoma microenvironment, with 1,247 significant ligand-receptor interactions quantified across 11 cell types. Our analysis identified NK cells as the most active signal-sending cells (outgoing score = 0.34) and B cells as the primary signal-receiving cells (incoming score = 0.41), suggesting complementary roles in tumor immunosurveillance. NK cells, as important components of the innate immune system, play key roles in anti-tumor immunity, but our study found that NK cells have extensive communication connections with multiple cell types, suggesting their functional complexity. The role of B cells in the tumor microenvironment has been controversial, with our analysis revealing dual functionality: B cells showed both pro-inflammatory signaling through cytokine pathways and potential tumor-promoting signals through growth factor pathways. The complex interaction pattern with 312 total interactions may explain the dual function of B cells in tumor immunity, requiring further investigation into specific B cell subsets and their functional states.

Particularly noteworthy is that the ligand-receptor interaction patterns we discovered provide molecular-level insights into signal transduction mechanisms in the tumor microenvironment. Key pathways identified include MIF signaling (promoting tumor cell survival), VEGF signaling (enhancing angiogenesis), and immune checkpoint pathways (regulating T cell activation). These findings suggest potential therapeutic targets: MIF inhibitors could disrupt tumor cell survival signals, anti-VEGF therapy could limit tumor angiogenesis, and immune checkpoint modulators could enhance anti-tumor immunity. These ligand-receptor pairs not only regulate cell survival and proliferation but also influence tumor invasion and metastatic capacity. Targeting key ligand-receptor interactions may provide new strategies for osteosarcoma treatment.

Limitations

This study has several important limitations that must be acknowledged: Technical limitations include single-cell capture efficiency biases potentially affecting rare cell type detection, dissociation protocol artifacts that may alter cell states, and batch effects despite correction efforts. The sample size for single-cell analysis (n = 2 samples) is relatively limited, which may not capture the full spectrum of tumor heterogeneity across different patients and disease stages. Computational limitations include assumptions underlying dimensionality reduction algorithms (t-SNE and UMAP may create artificial clustering patterns), potential clustering resolution biases affecting cell type identification, and inherent limitations in protein-protein interaction databases including coverage bias toward well-studied proteins and experimental condition dependencies that may not reflect in vivo states. Biological interpretation limitations include the static nature of single-cell snapshots versus dynamic biological processes, potential artifacts from tissue processing and cell isolation procedures, and limitations in inferring causal relationships from observational data despite MR analysis. The restriction to knee osteoarthritis may limit generalizability to other joint locations.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study systematically revealed the genetic susceptibility and molecular mechanisms of osteoarthritis and osteosarcoma through integrating multi-omics approaches, demonstrating causal genetic effects in osteoarthritis through Mendelian randomization, revealing unprecedented cellular heterogeneity in osteosarcoma including novel neural components, and identifying specific therapeutic targets through comprehensive network analysis.

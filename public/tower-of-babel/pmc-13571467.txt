Every cancer patient counts: strengthening European population-based cancer registries to improve cancer control
Authors: Gijs Geleijnse, Claudine Backes, Maria Dolores Chirlaque, Matthijs Sloep, Esteve Rodon Navarro, Mario Šekerija, Liesbet van Eycken, Giske Ursin
Published: 2026

Population-based cancer registries (PBCRs) systematically and continuously collect data on all cancer cases within defined populations following international standards to ensure harmonization, data quality and complete coverage. They operate on regional or national level and inform us of the burden of cancer in terms of incidence, survival and prevalence. Together with mortality, these three indicators are considered the essential elements of population-based cancer control.1

Through the European Cancer Information System (ECIS), the PBCRs in the European Network of Cancer Registries (ENCR) reveal cancer inequalities in and between geographies in Europe, they show where progress is made and where attention is required. With cancer spending expected to rise 59% by 2050,2 PBCRs are uniquely positioned to support the effective allocation of resources and effective implementation and evaluation of cancer prevention, early detection and quality of care.

Many European countries have national legislation on cancer registration. However, in practice PBCRs face persisting challenges in the collection of timely, relevant and high-quality population-based cancer data.3 Despite investments in digitalization in healthcare and the preparation of the European Health Data Space (EHDS), the infrastructure of PBCRs is frail: coverage of Europe is still suboptimal, timeliness of data publication is varying and PBCRs mention resource limitations as barrier to invest in innovation.4,5 European regulation on PBCR-based statistics is lacking. This contrasts with regulation on (cancer) mortality statistics,6 which are delivered by national statistics bureaus and Eurostat, which ensure timely and comparable collection of cause of death data on Europe.

The 192 European PBCRs follow the EU Commission’s Joint Research Centre (JRC)/ENCR guidelines for registration, but there is a large variation in their ability to collect timely, high-quality data and record elements such as stage at diagnosis and treatments.7,8 The Joint Action CancerWatch (2025–2028), with participation of 92 organisations from 29 countries, aims to improve the timeliness and quality of the PBCR data feeding into ECIS. With incomplete geographic coverage and limited availability of timely indicators on ECIS, the project addresses a pressing issue.

The European Court of Auditors has called for a monitoring framework for the European Commission’s Beating Cancer Plan.9 Although the ECIS was established with the purpose of monitoring the cancer burden in Europe, the current limitations in PBCR data infrastructure undermine ECIS’ ability to fulfil this monitoring role. Stronger coordination is required to provide Europe with more timely and high-quality cancer burden indicators. The following policy options on EU-level, member state level and PBCR-level can strengthen Europe’s ability to monitor its cancer control plans.

On the EU level, a formal recognition of PBCRs as core components of public health systems will support their mandate. Such a formal endorsement dedicated to PBCRs would be in line with the council recommendation for screening programs.10 The European Commission should publish criteria for data quality and timeliness for PBCR data on the ECIS, such that quality and progress of cancer registration in member states can be monitored and managed. Following the European Health Data Space Regulation, a data usage fee should be provided for PBCRs who prioritize delivery of data and statistics to ECIS. Moreover, PBCRs should be an integrated element of cancer screening programs and comprehensive cancer centers. Future EU project proposals on these topics should therefore require the involvement and adequate resourcing of PBCRs for planning, monitoring and evaluation.

On the member state level, a designated organization should be mandated with the responsibility to deliver national PBCR data to the ECIS. This organization should be the link between EU bodies and regional PBCRs and should be integrated in the design and monitoring of the national Cancer Mission Hubs. Besides adequate sustainable funding and resourcing for the PBCRs, national innovation funds allocated to artificial intelligence and digital sovereignty should support innovations in PBCRs.

The PBCRs should obtain a formal mandate from their government as a core component of public health and cancer control. They should allocate resources to increase efficiency of data collection and publication.

Policy action is required to strengthen PBCRs for effective, equitable cancer control. Timely, rich and high-quality population-based cancer data are essential for robust monitoring and evidence-informed European, national and local cancer policies. We need to count every cancer patient because every patient counts.

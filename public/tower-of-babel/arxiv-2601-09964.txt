Probabilistic heterogeneous Stirling numbers and Bell polynomials
Taekyun Kim, Dae San Kim

Abstract
. Let Y Y be a random variable satisfying specific moment conditions. 1.
Introduction

Let
Y
be a random variable satisfying the moment conditions

(1)

E\big[|Y|^{n}\big]<\infty,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}\cup{0},\quad\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|t|^{n}E[|Y|^{n}]}{n!}=0,\quad|t|<r,

for some
r>0
, where
E
denotes the mathematical expectation. Condition (
1
) ensures the existence of the moment generating function of
Y
, defined as

E\Big[e^{tY}\Big]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}E\big[Y^{n}\big],\quad(\text{see }[3,23,24]).

Furthermore, let
(Y_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) copies of
Y
, and let
S_{k}
denote their partial sums:

S_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}Y_{j},\ (k\geq 1),\quad\text{with }S_{0}=0.

Recently, Kim and Kim [18] introduced the heterogeneous Stirling numbers of the second kind,
H_{\lambda}(n,k)
, which generalize both the classical Stirling numbers of the second kind,
{n\brace k}
(as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
), and the Lah numbers,
L(n,k)
(as
\lambda\rightarrow 1
). They also investigated the heterogeneous Bell polynomials,
H_{n,\lambda}(x)
, which similarly generalize the Bell polynomials,
\phi_{n}(x)
, and the Lah-Bell polynomials,
LB_{n}(x)
. Special functions and polynomials serve as essential tools across mathematics, physics, and engineering. In recent years, significant research has focused on the degenerate versions and probabilistic extensions of these families. Degenerate polynomials are of interest not only for their number-theoretic and combinatorial properties but also for their applications in quantum mechanics and differential equations [6,14,17,29]. Concurrently, probabilistic extensions—including probabilistic Bell, Fubini, and Stirling polynomials—have emerged as a highly active area of study [13,15,16,19-21,24,26,27].

This paper introduces and explores the probabilistic heterogeneous Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with
Y
, denoted by
H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
. These numbers generalize the probabilistic Stirling numbers of the second kind,
{n\brace k}_{Y}
, and the probabilistic Lah numbers,
L^{Y}(n,k)
. Additionally, we investigate the probabilistic heterogeneous Bell polynomials associated with
Y
,
H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)
, which unify the probabilistic Bell polynomials,
\phi_{n}^{Y}(x)
, and the probabilistic Lah-Bell polynomials,
LB_{n}^{Y}(x)
.

The paper is organized as follows: Section 1 reviews the foundational definitions of Stirling, Lah, Bell, and Lah-Bell numbers and polynomials, alongside their probabilistic counterparts. It also recalls degenerate exponentials, heterogeneous Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with
Y
, heterogeneous Bell polynomials associated with
Y
and the partial Bell polynomials.
Section 2 presents our main results. We derive explicit expressions for
H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
(Theorems 2.1, 2.2) and
L^{Y}(n,k)
(Theorems 2.3, 2.4). For the polynomials
H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)
, we provide explicit formulas (Theorems 2.5, 2.9, 2.11), a Dobiński-like formula (Theorem 2.6), recurrence relations (Theorems 2.7, 2.8), a binomial identity (Theorem 2.10), and derivative properties (Theorem 2.14). We also establish connections between
H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
and partial Bell polynomials (Theorems 2.12, 2.13, 2.15). Finally, we provide applications for specific distributions: for a Poisson random variable, we relate the expectation of
\langle S_{k}\rangle_{n,\lambda}
to a specific value of

H_{n,\lambda}(x)
(Theorem 2.16) and get a sum representation for
H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)
involving the Bell polynomials (Theorem 2.17); for a Bernoulli random variable, we obtain explicit expressions for
H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
and
H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
(Theorem 2.18) and a sum representation involving the binomial coefficients for the expectation of
\langle S_{k}\rangle_{n,\lambda}
(Theorem 2.20). General references for this study include [2,4,5,7,8,9,11,22,23,25].

The Stirling numbers of the second kind
{n\brace k},\ (n,k\geq 0)
, are defined by

(2)

\frac{1}{k!}\big(e^{t}-1\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n\brace k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [9,22]).

The Lah numbers
L(n,k),\ (n,k\geq 0)
, are given by

(3)

\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\frac{1}{1-t}-1\bigg)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}L(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0).

Thus, by (
2
) and (
3
), we get

(4)

{n\brace k}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}j^{n},\quad(n\geq k),

and

L(n,k)=\frac{n!}{k!}\binom{n-1}{k-1},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [9,10,12,22]).

The probabilistic Stirling numbers of the second kind
{n\brace k}_{Y}
and probabilistic Lah numbers
L^{Y}(n,k)
are given by

(5)

{n\brace k}_{Y}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(-1)^{k-l}E\big[S_{l}^{n}\big],\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [3,20]),

and

(6)

L^{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(-1)^{k-l}E\Big[\langle S_{l}\rangle_{n}\Big],\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [10]),

where
\langle x\rangle_{0}=1,\ \langle x\rangle_{n}=x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1),\ (n\geq 1)
.

The Bell polynomials
\phi_{n}(x),\ (n\geq 0)
, are defined by

(7)

e^{x(e^{t}-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1,9,22]),

and Lah-Bell polynomials are given by

(8)

e^{x\big(\frac{1}{1-t}-1\big)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{LB}_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [10,12]).

From (
7
) and (
8
), we have Dobinski-like formulas:

(9)

\mathrm{LB}_{n}(x)=e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\langle k\rangle_{n}}{k!}x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [10,12]),

and

(10)

\phi_{n}(x)=e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{k^{n}}{k!}x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [17,20-22,24]).

Recently, probabilistic Bell polynomials
\phi_{n}^{Y}(x)
,
(n\geq 0)
, and probabilistic Lah-Bell polynomials
\mathrm{LB}_{n}^{Y}(x),\ (n\geq 0)
, are given by

(11)

\phi_{n}^{Y}(x)=e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{E[S_{k}^{n}]}{k!}x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [20,24]),

and

(12)

\mathrm{LB}_{n}^{Y}(x)=e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{E[\langle S_{k}\rangle_{n}]}{k!}x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [10,12]).

For any integer
k\geq 0
, the partial Bell polynomials are defined by

(13)

\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}x_{m}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\bigg)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}B_{n,k}\big(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n-k+1}\big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [9,20,24]),

where

\displaystyle B_{n,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n-k+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{1}+\cdots+l_{n-k+1}=k\\
l_{1}+2l_{2}+\cdots+(n-k+1)l_{n-k+1}=n\end{subarray}}\frac{n!}{l_{1}!l_{2}!\cdots l_{n-k+1}!}\bigg(\frac{x_{1}}{1!}\bigg)^{l_{1}}\bigg(\frac{x_{2}}{2!}\bigg)^{l_{2}}\cdots\bigg(\frac{x_{n-k+1}}{(n-k+1)!}\bigg)^{l_{n-k+1}},

with
l_{1},l_{2},\dots,l_{n-k+1}
nonnegative integers.
The complete Bell polynomials are given by

(14)

\exp\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x_{i}\frac{t^{i}}{i!}\bigg)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}\big(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}\big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [20,24]).

Thus, by (
13
) and (
14
), we get

B_{n}(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})=\sum_{k=0}^{n}B_{n,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n-k+1}),\ (n\geq 0).

For any nonzero
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the degenerate exponentials are defined by

(15)

e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\langle x\rangle_{k,\lambda}}{k!}t^{k},\quad e_{\lambda}(t)=e_{\lambda}^{1}(t),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [13-18]),

where

(x)_{0,\lambda}=1,\quad(x)_{n,\lambda}=x(x-\lambda)(x-2\lambda)\cdots\big(x-(n-1)\lambda\big),\ (n\geq 1).

For any integer
n\geq 0
, the heterogeneous Stirling numbers of the second kind
H_{\lambda}(n,k),\ (n,k\geq 0)
, are defined by

(16)

\langle x\rangle_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}H_{\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [18]),

where
\langle x\rangle_{n,\lambda}=(-1)^{n}(-x)_{n,\lambda},\quad(x)_{n}=(-1)^{n}\langle-x\rangle_{n}
.

From (
15
) and (
16
), we note that

(17)

\frac{1}{k!}\Big(e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-t)-1\Big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}H_{\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [18]).

Thus, by (
17
), we get

(18)

H_{\lambda}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}\langle j\rangle_{n,\lambda},\quad(n\geq k),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [18]).

From (
4
), (
6
) and (
18
), we note that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}H_{\lambda}(n,k)={n\brace k}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 1}H_{\lambda}(n,k)=L(n,k).

The heterogeneous Bell polynomials,
H_{n,\lambda}(x),\ (n\geq 0)
, are defined by

(19)

e^{x(e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-t)-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [18]).

From (
19
), we note that

H_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}H_{\lambda}(n,k),\ (n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [18]),

and

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}H_{n,\lambda}(x)=\phi_{n}(x),\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 1}H_{n,\lambda}(x)=\mathrm{LB}_{n}(x).

When
x=1,\ H_{n,\lambda}=H_{n,\lambda}(1)
are called the heterogeneous numbers.

2.
Probabilistic heterogeneous Stirling numbers of the second kind and Bell polynomials

Now, we consider a probabilistic extension of the heterogeneous Stirling numbers of the second kind,
H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k),\ (n,k\geq 0)
, which are called the
probabilistic heterogeneous Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with
Y
and defined by

(20)

\frac{1}{k!}\Big(E\Big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)\Big]-1\Big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0).

From (
20
), we have

(21)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(E\Big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)\Big]-1\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}\Big(E\Big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)\Big]\Big)^{j}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}E\Big[e_{\lambda}^{-(Y_{1}+\cdots+Y_{j})}(-t)\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}E\Big[e_{\lambda}^{-S}(-t)\Big]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}E\big[\langle S_{j}\rangle_{n,\lambda}\big]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, by (
21
), we get

(22)

\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}E\big[\langle S_{j}\rangle_{n,\lambda}\big]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}0,&\textrm{if $0\leq n<k$,}\\
H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k),&\textrm{if $n\geq k$.}\end{array}\right.

Therefore, by (
22
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

For
n\geq k\geq 0
, we have

(23)

H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}E\big[\langle S_{j}\rangle_{n,\lambda}\big].

In (
23
), we have

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 1}H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}E\big[\langle S_{j}\rangle_{n}\big]=L^{Y}(n,k),

and

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}E\big[S_{j}^{n}\big]={n\brace k}_{Y}.

From (
20
), we note that

(24)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(E\Big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)\Big]-1\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(E\Big[e^{-\frac{Y}{\lambda}\log(1-\lambda t)}\Big]-1\Big)^{k}=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}{l\brace k}_{Y}\frac{1}{l!}\bigg(-\frac{1}{\lambda}\log(1-\lambda t)\bigg)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}{l\brace k}_{Y}\lambda^{-l}\frac{1}{l!}\bigg(\log\bigg(\frac{1}{1-\lambda t}\bigg)\bigg)^{l}=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}{l\brace k}_{Y}\lambda^{-l}\sum_{n=l}^{\infty}{n\brack l}\lambda^{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}{l\brace k}_{Y}{n\brack l}\lambda^{n-l}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where
{n\brack k},\ (n,k\geq 0)
, are the unsigned Stirling numbers of the first kind defined by

\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\log\bigg(\frac{1}{1-t}\bigg)\bigg)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n\brack k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [18]).

Therefore, by (
24
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

For
n\geq k\geq 0
, we have

H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\sum_{l=k}^{n}{l\brace k}_{Y}{n\brack l}\lambda^{n-l}.

From (
6
), we note that

(25)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}L^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(E\bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{1-t}\bigg)^{Y}\bigg]-1\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(E\Big[e^{-Y\log(1-t)}\Big]-1\Big)^{k}=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}{l\brace k}_{Y}\frac{1}{l!}\big(-\log(1-t)\big)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}{l\brace k}_{Y}\sum_{n=l}^{\infty}{n\brack l}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}{l\brace k}_{Y}{n\brack l}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
25
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3
.

For
n\geq k\geq 0
, we have

L^{Y}(n,k)=\sum_{l=k}^{n}{l\brace k}_{Y}{n\brack l}.

Let
\log_{\lambda}t
be the compositional inverse function of
e_{\lambda}(t)
. Then we have

\log_{\lambda}(1+t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\big((1+t)^{\lambda}-1\big),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [14,16]).

Note that

\log_{\lambda}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)\big)=e_{\lambda}(\log_{\lambda}(t))=t.

From (
20
), we have

(26)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}L^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(E\bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{1-t}\bigg)^{Y}\bigg]-1\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(E\Big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1-t)\big)\Big]-1\bigg)^{k}=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}H_{\lambda}^{Y}(l,k)\frac{1}{l!}\big(-\log_{\lambda}(1-t)\big)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}H_{\lambda}^{Y}(l,k)\sum_{n=l}^{\infty}{n\brack l}_{\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k){n\brack l}_{\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where
{n\brack k}_{\lambda}
are the degenerate unsigned Stirling numbers of the first kind defined by

\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\log_{\lambda}\bigg(\frac{1}{1-t}\bigg)\bigg)^{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(-\log_{\lambda}(1-t)\Big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n\brack k}_{\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

for
k\geq 0
, (see [14,15,16,18]).

Therefore, by (
26
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.4
.

For
n\geq k\geq 0
, we have

L^{Y}(n,k)=\sum_{l=k}^{n}H_{\lambda}^{Y}(l,k){n\brack l}_{\lambda}.

Now, we define the
probabilistic heterogeneous Bell polynomials associated with
Y
by

(27)

e^{x\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)]-1\big)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

When
x=1,\ H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}=H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(1),\ (n\geq 0)
, are called the
probabilistic heterogeneous Bell numbers associated with
Y
.

By (
27
), we get

(28)

\displaystyle e^{x\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)]-1\big)}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}\Big(E\Big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)\Big]-1\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{k}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
27
) and (
28
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.5
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)x^{k}.

From (
27
), we note that

(29)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=e^{-x}e^{xE\big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)\big]}

\displaystyle=e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}\Big(E\Big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)\Big]\Big)^{k}=e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}E\Big[e_{\lambda}^{-S_{k}}(-t)\Big]

\displaystyle=e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E\Big[\langle S_{k}\rangle_{n,\lambda}\Big]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{E[\langle S_{k}\rangle_{n,\lambda}]}{k!}x^{k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
29
), we obtain the following Dobinski-like theorem.

Theorem 2.6
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{E\big[\langle S_{k}\rangle_{n,\lambda}\big]}{k!}x^{k}.

Note that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 1}H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{E\big[\langle S_{k}\rangle_{n}\big]}{k!}x^{k}=\mathrm{LB}_{n}^{Y}(x),

and

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{E\big[S_{k}^{n}\big]}{k!}x^{k}=\phi_{n}^{Y}(x).

By (
27
), we get

(30)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n+1,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{d}{dt}e^{x\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)]-1\big)}

\displaystyle=xE\Big[Ye_{\lambda}^{-(Y+\lambda)}(-t)\Big]e^{x\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)]-1\big)}

\displaystyle=x\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{k+1,\lambda}\big]\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}H_{m,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{k+1,\lambda}\big]H_{n-k,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
30
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.7
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

H_{n+1,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=x\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{k+1,\lambda}\big]H_{n-k,\lambda}^{Y}(x).

Now, we observe that

(31)

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}H_{n+m,\lambda}^{Y}(t)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\frac{y^{m}}{m!}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{m}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(t)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\frac{y^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{y^{m}}{m!}\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{m}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(t)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=e^{y\frac{d}{dx}}e^{t\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-x)]-1\big)}.

On the other hand, by binomial theorem, we get

(32)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{m}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(t)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\frac{y^{m}}{m!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(t)}{n!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{m}x^{n}\frac{y^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(t)}{n!}(x+y)^{n}=e^{t\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-x-y)]-1\big)}.

By (
31
) and (
32
), we get

(33)

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}H_{n+m,\lambda}^{Y}(t)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\frac{y^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=e^{y\frac{d}{dx}}e^{t\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-x)]-1\big)}

\displaystyle=e^{t\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-x-y)]-1\big)}.

From (
15
), we note that

(34)

\displaystyle E\Big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-x-y)\Big]

\displaystyle=E\bigg[(1-\lambda x)^{-\frac{Y}{\lambda}}\bigg(1-\frac{\lambda Y}{1-\lambda x}\bigg)^{-\frac{Y}{\lambda}}\bigg]

\displaystyle=E\bigg[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-x)e_{\lambda}^{-Y}\bigg(\frac{-y}{1-\lambda x}\bigg)\bigg].

By (
33
) and (
34
), we get

(35)

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}H_{n+m,\lambda}^{Y}(t)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\frac{y^{m}}{m!}=e^{t\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-x-y)]-1\big)}

\displaystyle=e^{t\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-x)]-1\big)}e^{t\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-x)]\big(e_{\lambda}^{-Y}\big(\frac{-y}{1-\lambda x}\big)-1\big)\big]\big)}

\displaystyle=e^{t\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-x)]-1\big)}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\bigg(E\bigg[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-x)\bigg(e_{\lambda}^{-Y}\bigg(\frac{-y}{1-\lambda x}\bigg)-1\bigg)\bigg]\bigg)^{j}.

Note that

(36)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\bigg(E\bigg[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-x)\bigg(e_{\lambda}^{-Y}\bigg(\frac{-y}{1-\lambda x}\bigg)-1\bigg)\bigg]\bigg)^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}E\bigg[e_{\lambda}^{-(Y_{1}+\cdots Y_{j})}(-x)\bigg(e_{\lambda}^{-Y_{1}}\bigg(\frac{-y}{1-\lambda x}\bigg)-1\bigg)\cdots\bigg(e_{\lambda}^{-Y_{j}}\bigg(\frac{-y}{1-\lambda x}\bigg)-1\bigg)\bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\sum_{n=j}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{j}=n}\binom{n}{l_{1},\dots,l_{j}}E\Big[e_{\lambda}^{-S_{j}-n\lambda}(-x)\langle Y_{1}\rangle_{l_{1},\lambda}\cdots\langle Y_{j}\rangle_{l_{j},\lambda}\Big]\bigg)\frac{y^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\sum_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{j}=n}\binom{n}{l_{1},\dots,l_{j}}E\bigg[e_{\lambda}^{-S_{j}-n\lambda}(-x)\prod_{i=1}^{j}\langle Y_{i}\rangle_{l_{i},\lambda}\bigg]\bigg)\frac{y^{n}}{n!}.

From (
35
) and (
36
), we have

(37)

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}H_{n+m,\lambda}^{Y}(t)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\frac{y^{m}}{m!}=e^{t\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-x)]-1\big)}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\bigg(E\bigg[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-x)\bigg(e_{\lambda}^{-Y}\bigg(\frac{-y}{1-\lambda x}\bigg)-1\bigg)\bigg]\bigg)^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\sum_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{j}=n}\binom{n}{l_{1},\dots,l_{j}}\frac{y^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}E\bigg[\langle S_{j}+ny\rangle_{l,\lambda}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\langle Y_{i}\rangle_{l_{i},\lambda}\bigg]\frac{x^{l}}{l!}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}H_{k,\lambda}^{Y}(t)\frac{x^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\sum_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{j}=n}\binom{n}{l_{1},\dots,l_{j}}E\bigg[\langle S_{j}+n\lambda\rangle_{m-k,\lambda}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\langle Y_{i}\rangle_{l_{i},\lambda}\bigg]\bigg)H_{k,\lambda}^{Y}(t)\frac{y^{n}}{n!}\frac{x^{m}}{m!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
37
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.8
.

For
m,n\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle H_{n+m,\lambda}^{Y}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{j}=n}\binom{n}{l_{1},\dots,l_{j}}E\bigg[\langle S_{j}+n\lambda\rangle_{m-k,\lambda}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\langle Y_{i}\rangle_{l_{i},\lambda}\bigg]H_{k,\lambda}^{Y}(t).

Taking
\lambda\rightarrow 0
and
\lambda\rightarrow 1
respectively gives

\phi_{n+m}^{Y}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\sum_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{j}=n}\binom{n}{l_{1},\dots,l_{j}}E\bigg[S_{j}^{m-k}\prod_{i=1}^{j}Y_{i}^{l_{i}}\bigg]\phi_{k}^{Y}(t),

and

\mathrm{LB}_{n+m}^{Y}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\sum_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{j}=n}\binom{n}{l_{1},\dots,l_{j}}E\bigg[\langle S_{j}+n\rangle_{m-k}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\langle Y_{i}\rangle_{l_{i}}\bigg]\mathrm{LB}_{k}^{Y}(t).

From (
27
), we have

(38)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=e^{x\big(E\big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)\big]-1\big)}

\displaystyle=\exp\bigg(x\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{j,\lambda}\big]\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\bigg)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(x\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{j,\lambda}\big]\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}B_{n,k}\Big(xE\big[\langle Y\rangle_{1,\lambda}\big],xE\big[\langle Y\rangle_{2,\lambda}\big],\dots,xE\big[\langle Y\rangle_{n-k+1,\lambda}\big]\Big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
38
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.9
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}B_{n,k}\Big(E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{1,\lambda}\big],E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{2,\lambda}\big],\dots,E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{n-k+1,\lambda}\big]\Big).

Note that

(39)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x+y)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=e^{(x+y)(E[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)]-1)}

\displaystyle e^{x(E[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)]-1)}e^{y(E[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)]-1)}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}H_{k,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}H_{l,\lambda}^{Y}(y)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}H_{k,\lambda}^{Y}(x)H_{n-k,\lambda}^{Y}(y)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
39
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.10
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x+y)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}H_{k,\lambda}^{Y}(x)H_{n-k,\lambda}^{Y}(y).

By (
13
) and (
27
), we get

(40)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=e^{x(E[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)]-1)}

\displaystyle=\Big(e^{E[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)]-1}-1+1\Big)^{x}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{x}{k}\Big(e^{E[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)]-1}-1\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{x}{k}k!\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}B_{n,k}\Big(H_{1,\lambda}^{Y},H_{2,\lambda}^{Y},\dots,H_{n-k+1,\lambda}^{Y}\Big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{x}{k}k!B_{n,k}\Big(H_{1,\lambda}^{Y},H_{2,\lambda}^{Y},\dots,H_{n-k+1,\lambda}^{Y}\Big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
40
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.11
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{x}{k}k!B_{n,k}\Big(H_{1,\lambda}^{Y},H_{2,\lambda}^{Y},\dots,H_{n-k+1,\lambda}^{Y}\Big).

From (
27
), we have

(41)

te^{x(E[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)]-1)}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}jH_{j-1,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{j}}{j!}.

By (
41
), we get

(42)

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}jH_{j-1,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\bigg)^{k}=t^{k}e^{kx(E[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)]-1)}=t^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}k^{j}x^{j}\frac{1}{j!}\Big(E\big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)\big]-1\Big)^{j}

\displaystyle=t^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}k^{j}x^{j}\sum_{n=j}^{\infty}H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,j)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n}k^{j}x^{j}H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,j)\frac{t^{n+k}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}k!\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}k^{j}x^{j}H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n-k,j)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

From (
42
), we have

(43)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}k^{j}x^{j}H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n-k,j)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}jH_{j-1,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}B_{n,k}\Big(H_{0,\lambda}^{Y}(x),2H_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(x),3H_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(x),\dots,(n-k+1)H_{n-k,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\Big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
43
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.12
.

For
n\geq k\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}k^{j}x^{j}H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n-k,j)

\displaystyle=B_{n,k}\Big(H_{0,\lambda}^{Y}(x),2H_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(x),3H_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(x),\dots,(n-k+1)H_{n-k,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\Big).

Now, we observe that

(44)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}B_{n,k}\Big(H_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(x),H_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(x),\dots,H_{n-k+1,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\Big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}H_{j,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\bigg)^{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(e^{x(E[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)]-1)}-1\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}{j\brace k}x^{j}\Big(E\big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)\big]-1\Big)^{j}=\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}{j\brace k}x^{j}\sum_{n=j}^{\infty}H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,j)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{j=k}^{n}{j\brace k}H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,j)x^{j}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
44
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.13
.

For
n\geq k\geq 0
, we have

B_{n,k}\Big(H_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(x),H_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(x),\dots,H_{n-k+1,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\Big)=\sum_{j=k}^{n}{j\brace k}H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,j)x^{j}.

By (
27
), we get

(45)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{k}H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{k}e^{x(E[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)]-1)}

\displaystyle=\Big(E\big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)\big]-1\Big)^{k}e^{x(E[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)]-1)}=k!\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}H_{\lambda}^{Y}(j,k)\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}H_{i,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{i}}{i!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}k!\sum_{i=0}^{n-k}\binom{n}{i}H_{i,\lambda}^{Y}(x)H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n-i,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

In particular, for
k=1
, then

(46)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{d}{dx}H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\Big(E\big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)\big]-1\Big)e^{x(E[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)]-1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{l,\lambda}\big]\frac{t^{l}}{l!}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}H_{j,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{j}}{j!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{j}E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{n-j,\lambda}\big]H_{j,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
45
) and (
46
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.14
.

For
n\geq k\geq 1
, we have

\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{k}H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=k!\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}\binom{n}{j}H_{j,\lambda}^{Y}(x)H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n-j,k),

and

\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{j}E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{n-j,\lambda}\big]H_{j,\lambda}^{Y}(x).

From (
20
), we have

(47)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(E\big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)\big]-1\Big)^{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{j,\lambda}\big]\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}B_{n,k}\Big(E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{1,\lambda}\big],E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{2,\lambda}\big],\dots,E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{n-k+1,\lambda}\big]\Big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
47
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.15
.

For
n\geq k\geq 0
, we have

H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=B_{n,k}\Big(E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{1,\lambda}\big],E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{2,\lambda}\big],\dots,E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{n-k+1,\lambda}\big]\Big).

Let
Y
be the Poisson random variables with parameter
\alpha(>0)
(see [23,28]). Then we have

(48)

E\big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)\big]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e_{\lambda}^{-n}(-t)\frac{\alpha^{n}e^{-\alpha}}{n!}=e^{-\alpha}e^{\alpha e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-t)}=e^{\alpha(e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-t)-1)}.

From (
48
), we have

(49)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E\big[\langle S_{k}\rangle_{n,\lambda}\big]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=E\big[e_{\lambda}^{-S_{k}}(-t)\big]

\displaystyle=E\Big[e_{\lambda}^{-(Y_{1}+\cdots+Y_{k})}(-t)\Big]=E\Big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y_{1}}(-t)\Big]E\Big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y_{2}}(-t)\Big]\cdots E\Big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y_{k}}(-t)\Big]

\displaystyle=e^{\alpha k(e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-t)-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n,\lambda}(k\alpha)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
49
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.16
.

Let
Y
be the Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha(>0)
. Then we have

\displaystyle E\big[\langle S_{k}\rangle_{n,\lambda}\big]=E\big[\langle Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\cdots+Y_{n}\rangle_{n,\lambda}\big]=H_{n,\lambda}(k\alpha),\ (n\geq 0).

Let
Y
be the Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha(>0)
. Then we have

(50)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=e^{x\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)]-1\big)}

\displaystyle=e^{x(e^{\alpha(e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-t)-1)}-1)}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\phi_{k}(x)\frac{\alpha^{k}}{k!}\Big(e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-t)-1\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\phi_{k}(x)\alpha^{k}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}H_{\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\phi_{k}(x)H_{\lambda}(n,k)\alpha^{k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
50
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.17
.

Let
Y
be the Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha(>0)
. For
n\geq 0
, we have

H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\phi_{k}(x)H_{\lambda}(n,k)\alpha^{k}.

Let
Y
be the Bernoulli random variable with probability of success
p
(see [23]). Then, for
n\geq 1
, we have

(51)

E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{n,\lambda}\big]=\sum_{i=0}^{1}\langle i\rangle_{n,\lambda}p(i)=\langle 1\rangle_{n,\lambda}p.

From (
20
) and (
51
), we note that

(52)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(E\big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)\big]-1\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{j,\lambda}\big]\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\bigg)^{k}=\frac{p^{k}}{k!}\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\langle 1\rangle_{j,\lambda}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=p^{k}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}B_{n,k}\big(\langle 1\rangle_{1,\lambda},\langle 1\rangle_{2,\lambda},\dots,\langle 1\rangle_{n-k+1,\lambda}\big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, by (
52
) and Theorem 2.15 with
Y=1
, we get

(53)

H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=p^{k}B_{n,k}\big(\langle 1\rangle_{1,\lambda},\langle 1\rangle_{2,\lambda},\dots,\langle 1\rangle_{n-k+1,\lambda}\big)=p^{k}H_{\lambda}(n,k).

From (
53
) and Theorem 2.5 with
Y=1
, we have

(54)

H_{n,\lambda}(xp)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(px)^{k}H_{\lambda}(n,k)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x).

Therefore, by (
54
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.18
.

Let
Y
be the Bernoulli random variable with probability of success
p
. For
n\geq 0
, we have

H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=H_{n,\lambda}(xp),\quad H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=p^{k}H_{\lambda}(n,k).

We observe that, for
n\geq 1
, we have

(55)

\displaystyle\langle 1\rangle_{n,\lambda}+\langle 2\rangle_{n,\lambda}+\cdots+\langle k\rangle_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\langle j\rangle_{n,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{l=1}^{n}H_{\lambda}(n,l)(j)_{l}=\sum_{l=1}^{n}H_{\lambda}(n,l)l!\sum_{j=1}^{k}\binom{j}{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{n}H_{\lambda}(n,l)l!\sum_{j=1}^{k}\bigg(\binom{j+1}{l+1}-\binom{j}{l+1}\bigg)=\sum_{l=1}^{n}H_{\lambda}(n,l)l!\binom{k+1}{l+1}.

Thus, by (
55
), we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 2.19
.

For
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\langle 1\rangle_{n,\lambda}+\langle 2\rangle_{n,\lambda}+\cdots+\langle k\rangle_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{l=1}^{n}H_{\lambda}(n,l)l!\binom{k+1}{l+1}.

Let
Y
be the Bernoulli random variable with probability of success
p
. Then we have

(56)

\displaystyle E\Big[e_{\lambda}^{-(Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\cdots+Y_{k})}\Big]

\displaystyle=E\big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y_{1}}(-t)\big]E\big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y_{2}}(-t)\big]\cdots E\big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y_{k}}(-t)\big]

\displaystyle=\Big(p\big(e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-t)-1\big)+1\Big)^{k},

since

E\big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(-t)\big]=(1-p)e_{\lambda}^{0}(-t)+pe_{\lambda}^{-1}(-t)=p\big(e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-t)-1\big)+1.

By (
56
), we get

(57)

\displaystyle E\Big[e_{\lambda}^{-(Y_{1}+\cdots+Y_{k})}(-t)\Big]=\big(p(e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-t)-1)+1\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}p^{j}\big(e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-t)-1\big)^{j}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}p^{j}j!\sum_{n=j}^{\infty}H_{\lambda}(n,j)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{k}{j}p^{j}j!H_{\lambda}(n,j)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

On the other hand, by (
17
), we get

(58)

\displaystyle E\Big[e_{\lambda}^{-(Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\cdots+Y_{k})}(-t)\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E\big[\langle Y_{1}+\cdots+Y_{k}\rangle_{n,\lambda}\big]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E\big[\langle S_{k}\rangle_{n,\lambda}\big]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
57
) and (
58
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.20
.

Let
Y
be the Bernoulli random variable with probability of success
p
. For
n\geq 0
, we have

E\big[\langle S_{k}\rangle_{n,\lambda}\big]=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{k}{j}p^{j}j!H_{\lambda}(n,j).

3.
Conclusion

In this study, we have successfully defined and analyzed the probabilistic heterogeneous Stirling numbers
H_{\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
and the probabilistic heterogeneous Bell polynomials
H_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)
. By leveraging the properties of the random variable
Y
and its associated partial sums
S_{k}
, we established a diverse array of results, including:

\indent\bullet
Explicit Expressions: Theorems for both the numbers and polynomials.

\indent\bullet
Operational Identities: A Dobiński-like formula and various recurrence relations.

\indent\bullet
Distributions: Specific applications involving Poisson and Bernoulli random variables.

These results demonstrate that the probabilistic heterogeneous framework provides a powerful tool for unifying diverse combinatorial identities. Future research may further explore the applications of these polynomials in the context of quantum mechanics or more complex stochastic processes.

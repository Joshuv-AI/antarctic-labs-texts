Level of Knowledge, Attitude, and Belief on Yoga and its Association with Practice among Nursing Officers of a Tertiary Care Hospital in Delhi
Authors: Suprakash Mandal, Puneet Misra, Pritam Halder, Gautam Sharma, Man Singh Jat, Rajesh Sagar, Shashi Kant, S. N. Dwivedi, Ramakrishnan Lakshmy, Kiran Goswami
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Introduction:Yoga is beneficial for mental, physical stress and noncommunicable diseases (NCDs). Due to long and hectic working hours, nursing officers experience stress and NCDs. Awareness of yoga helps adopt yoga practice and healthy lifestyle. Our objective was to assess the knowledge, attitude, and beliefs regarding yoga and examine their association with its practice among nursing officers.Methods:This was a cross-sectional analysis of baseline data of an intervention study on in-service nursing officers employed at workplace for at least 1 year. Those who were under treatment for any psychiatric disorder and any condition preventing practice yoga were excluded. After taking informed written consent, a pretested, semi-structured questionnaire was administered. Data were extracted in Microsoft Excel and analyzed by statistical software STATA V12.Results:A total of 105 participants completed the questionnaire, 78% were female, and the mean age was 33 years. Majority (79.0%) of them were aware of yoga as a way of healthy life and beneficial practice. A proportion of ever practiced participants were 53.3% and currently practicing were 12.4%. The level of knowledge, attitude, and belief score was positively correlated among themselves. The belief score had a significant association with current practice of yoga (odds ratio of 1.9, 95% confidence interval: 1.4–2.7, P < 0.001).Conclusion:Knowledge, attitude, and belief were higher among most of the participants though the level of practice was low. The score of knowledge, attitude, and belief was higher among those who had a history of either current or past practice. Belief score was significantly associated with current yoga practice. A larger study is recommended at workplace setting with further exploration.

Introduction

In the challenging scenario of healthcare delivery system, the well-being of nursing officers stands as a cornerstone for the provision of quality patient care. As frontline caregivers, nursing officers are often confronted with high-stress environments that can have significant implications for their physical and mental health.[1] Nursing officers, by virtue of their demanding roles, often face high levels of stress which can lead to physical and mental health issues.[2] The chronic conditions such as hypertension, musculoskeletal disorders, and anxiety disorders are prevalent among them due to the nature of their profession.[3] The quality of life is often compromised due to the high workplace stress, leading to altered caregiving behavior.[4] The burden of stress among these professionals is variable based on the study setting and area. In India, one study revealed that 87.6% of nurses were experiencing stress, with 2.1% experiencing severe stress.[5] Another study in a tertiary care center reported as high as 50.8% self-reported stress, anxiety (74%), and depression (70.8%).[6] The prevalence of stress-related disorders and chronic health conditions among nursing officers is a critical concern in the healthcare sector. Long working hours, emotionally charged situations, and physically demanding tasks contribute to their vulnerability. Research has indicated that a significant proportion of nursing professionals experience symptoms of stress, burnout, and related chronic health issues.[3]

Yoga is a recognized mind–body practice that helps in reducing the overall stress, anxiety, and common mental health-related condition.[78] Apart from this, it can stabilize the hypothalamo–pituitary–adrenal axis to ensure optimal functioning of hormonal and metabolic system in our body. In addressing the different noncommunicable diseases, yoga has been observed to play an adjuvant role.[891011] Apart from that, regular yoga practice increases the coping ability to withstand stressful situation in day-to-day life, thus increasing the resilience.[12] Those people who regularly practice yoga also tend to follow a disciplined and healthy lifestyle which decreases the overall risk of any chronic disease and improves the quality of life as well as the professional efficiency.[1314] Several interventional studies in Indian healthcare settings showed a promising effect among the nursing professionals to alleviate several health problems such as chronic low back pain, insomnia, stress, anxiety, and burnout as well as improving quality of life.[1516171819] Therefore, it is evident that yoga practice by the nursing professionals has a positive effect on health. However, systematic review and meta-analysis reported that the average drop-out in yoga-related research ranged from 10% to 14%, highest among female participants.[20] However, in routine life setting, the evidence on level of sustainable practice and drop-out rate are lacking. Apart from that in India, from several studies, a significant gap was found between level of knowledge, perception of yoga, and its practice in day-to-day life.[21] This gap varies by sociodemographic factors such as region, age, sex, occupation, and education. Therefore, recognizing the potential benefits of yoga in alleviating stress, promoting holistic well-being, and mitigating chronic health conditions, it becomes imperative to assess their knowledge, attitude, and practice toward this ancient discipline.[8] Apart from that, healthy and resilient workforce is essential in providing the quality patient care in healthcare settings.[22]

Understanding the determinants of yoga practice among healthcare providers can inform the design of workplace-based wellness interventions to alleviate stress, manage chronic conditions, and enhance overall well-being. Additionally, with the prevalent stress-related disorders in this profession, integrating yoga may lead to improved professional efficacy, ultimately benefiting both nursing officers, and the quality of patient care they deliver. This research seeks to bridge the gap between conventional healthcare practices and holistic well-being, advocating for a more balanced and resilient nursing workforce. In India, evidence is scarce in this area, especially among the nursing staff of a tertiary care hospital. Thus, our objective was to determine the level of knowledge, attitude, belief, and practice of yoga among nursing officers of a tertiary care hospital in Delhi.

Methods

Study design and participants

This was a cross-sectional analysis of the baseline data of the participants of an interventional study done at a tertiary care medical college in Delhi. The investigator assessed eligibility criteria based on the self-reported information. All the in-service nursing staff working at the institute for at least 1 year were approached for inclusion. Among the willing nursing officers, those who were already under pharmacological treatment for any psychiatric disorder at the time of enrollment, having service left for less than a year at the institute (from the date of enrollment), and any clinical condition that affected the ability to practice yoga were excluded. The study protocol was approved by the Institute Ethics Committee (Reference No.: IECPG-543/20.12.2017, RT-57/31.01.2018) on January 31, 2018. Informed written consent was taken from the participants.

Study variables

The primary outcome variables were the level of knowledge, attitude, and practice of yoga. A pretested, self-developed, structured questionnaire having six items was used to measure knowledge where correct answer had one mark each with a maximum total mark of 6. The attitude toward yoga was measured by a pretested self-developed self-administered Likert-type 10-item questionnaire having a total score from 10 to 60. The belief was measured by an 11 item Likert-type questionnaire reproduced from the previous study having a total score from 11 to 77.[23] The practice of yoga was captured by a self-developed self-administered semi-structured questionnaire asking the time of starting the yoga, duration of yoga practice, current practice status, etc., The past practice was defined as at least once a week practice 1 year ago, and the current practice was considered who reported at least one session in a week during the enrollment. Apart from these, basic sociodemographic details and workplace details were captured through a self-developed self-administered closed-ended questionnaire.

Statistical methods

Data were extracted in Microsoft Excel (Microsoft Corporation, headquartered in Redmond, Washington, USA) and analyzed by statistical software STATA V12 (StataCorp LLC, headquartered in College Station, Texas, USA). The descriptive analysis was reported by frequency and proportions for the categorical variables and by mean, standard deviation (SD), and median for the quantitative variables. The level of knowledge, attitude, and belief score was compared with the help of an unpaired t-test for the normally distributed or Man–Whitney U-test for the skewed distribution based on the Shapiro–Wilk test. Apart from this, the scores were tested for correlation among themselves, followed by bivariable linear regression to find the association. The level of statistical significance was considered 5%.

Results

Out of the willing 114 staff nurses from different departments in the institute, 110 participants were included in this study as per the eligibility criteria. Out of the 110 participants, 5 participants did not complete the interview question related to principal outcome leading to final sample size of 105 [Figure 1].

Study flow diagram

Of the total participants, three-fourth (74.2%) of the participants were female. The mean (SD) age was 33.8 ± 7.1 years. Overall, 72.4% of participants were married, and 2.8% were a divorcee.

The majority (80.9%) of the participants had a graduation degree in educational qualification, while the rest 20% had postgraduate degree. Almost half (50.5%) of the participants were doing duty in indoor (inpatient department), 7.6% in outdoor (outpatient department), 7.6% in critical care (intensive care unit), 1.8% in operation theater, and 29.5% in other areas. The mean ± SD duration of total job life was 11.2 ± 7.7 years and in the current institute 8.7 ± 7.3 years. The mean ± SD duty hours per week was 42 ± 5.3, while the number of night shift duty was 5 ± 0.6 days in a month [Table 1].

The knowledge score was not statistically different, though in the category of ever practiced, it was higher [Figure 2a]. Discussion

This was a cross-sectional analysis of the data of an interventional study. With this analysis, we aimed to assess and compare the level of knowledge, attitude, belief, and practice of yoga among nursing officers of a tertiary care hospital in Delhi. More than two-thirds of the participants showed a good level of knowledge on yoga. A similar finding was found on patients of the cardiology and neurology department of a tertiary care hospital conducted by Sharma et al.[24] The study reported that 90% of patients were aware about yoga, whereas 22% practiced it.

Another study done by Ankamreddy S et al. among medical students reported the similar finding to know about the benefits of including depression and sleep disturbances as well as lifestyle diseases such as obesity, hypertension, diabetes, and sleep disorders.[25] Apart from that, the currently practicing participants were 9% of male and 22% of female students which was similar to our finding being 12% overall.

A positive attitude toward yoga was seen among the nursing officers. The attitude score was also higher among those having a history of practice as well as among currently practicing participants. Positive attitudes and a willingness to take part in studies using yoga as a therapeutic intervention have been noted in the past, provided that the practice is administered under the supervision of qualified medical specialists.[26]

Similar to attitude, the belief score had the same pattern. The score was also significantly higher among those either practices in the past or currently practicing compared to those not practiced or practicing. This can be explained by that those who are naturally interested to yoga tend to enquire more as well as to believe positively in yoga. According to a national survey, 92.6% of respondents believed yoga changed their way of life.[21] In our study, around half (53.3%) of the participants had practiced yoga ever in then lifetime, of which 46.7% of participants practiced for <1 month whereas no one reported practice beyond 12 months. Overall, only 12.4% of participants were practicing yoga currently. Yoga practice among the participants from various small studies varied from 12.8% to 22.0%.[579222328] Only 11.8% of respondents to a recent countrywide survey reported practicing yoga, indicating that there is a similar gap between knowledge and practice across the nation.[21]

The score of knowledge, attitude, and belief had a positive correlation in our study. This finding is consistent with the finding from a previous study by Panneerselvam et al. and Dayal and Singh where all three domains were one study done among college students in Tamil Nadu and Lucknow city.[2729] The current practices were positively associated with the belief score, significantly implying that a higher level of belief influenced the practice in real life. The nonsignificant finding of attitude and knowledge score may be due to the low sample size.

Our study, being one of the very few studies, explored the knowledge, attitude, belief, and practice of yoga among nursing officers and quantified their level and interrelationship. Participants were from a tertiary care hospital and recruited from different sections of the hospital, which ensured the generalizability as different sections or wards had different types of work pattern load and work culture. The study also attempted the influence of each of the domains with the current practice of yoga. However, our study had a smaller sample size and hospital based. Apart from that, the participant self-reported the information which might have issue of social-desirability bias. Being a cross-sectional study, temporality and causality could not be established. The presence of social desirability bias and recall bias could not be ignored completely.

Conclusion

The knowledge, attitude, and belief level toward yoga was higher among the nursing officers throughout the various departments of the hospital. Although half of the participants had a history of practice in the past, a small proportion of them were practicing. The level of knowledge, attitude, and belief was also significantly higher among those who practiced in the past and currently practicing than not involved in practice. However, contrasting results were documented in view of practicing yoga. The belief level had a positive influence on the practice of yoga. A further large-scale study in a community-based setting is recommended to confirm the finding, and further exploration is recommended.

Abstract
. A result of Hoskins and Steinerberger [ Int. Math. Res. Not. , (13):9784–9809, 2022] states that repeatedly differentiating a random polynomials with independent and identically distributed mean zero and variance one roots will result, after an appropriate rescaling, in a Hermite polynomial. We use the theory of finite free probability to extend this result in two natural directions: (1) We prove central limit theorems for the fluctuations around these deterministic limits for the polynomials and their roots. (2) We consider a generalized version of the Hoskins and Steinerberger result by removing the finite second moment assumption from the roots. In this case the Hermite polynomials are replaced by a random Appell sequence conveniently described through finite free probability and an infinitely divisible distribution. We use finite free cumulants to provide compact proofs of our main results with little prerequisite knowledge of free probability required.



1.
Introduction

Our goal is to extend existing results on the convergence of roots of derivatives of random polynomials from the finite free probability perspective. We aim to apply the theory of
finite free cumulants
, first developed by
[
3
]
to linearize the

,

showing that the roots of

,
6
,
9
,

,

,

. We will consider the situation when

on polynomials defined by

which dilates the roots by

Proposition 1.1

(Hoskins and Steinerberger

]
)

Let

be as in (

) and assume the measure

, and moments of all orders. If

\tilde{p}_{k,N}(x)=\mathcal{D}_{\sqrt{N}}\partial_{k|N}p_{N}\left(x+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}X_{j}\right),

then, for any fixed

(1.5)

pointwise, almost surely, where

\He_{k}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\rfloor}\frac{k!(-1)^{j}}{j!(k-2j)!}\frac{x^{k-2j}}{2^{j}}.

In
[

Our main contribution is to extend this convergence of random polynomials with independent roots in two directions:

(1)

First, we prove central limit theorems (CLTs) for the polynomial

(2)

Second, we prove a generalized version of Proposition

depending only on the infinitely divisible distribution. This result applies to random polynomials of the form (

2.6

In both results we use the finite free cumulants as an efficient way of tracking how fluctuations of the original iid roots propagate through repeated differentiation.

1.1.
Notation and a short introduction to finite free probability

Finite free probability arose from Marcus, Spielman, and Srivastava’s celebrated work
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
on interlacing families. As our goal is to demonstrate how finite free cumulants provide an efficient and natural way of computing limits of the form (
1.5
), we will only provide basic definitions required for the proofs. We encourage the interested reader to see
[
21
,
18
,
3
]
for a more thorough introduction.

We will use
\Rightarrow
to denote convergence in distribution and
\mathcal{N}(a,\sigma^{2})
to denote the Gaussian distribution with mean (mean vector)
a
and variance (covariance matrix)
\sigma^{2}
. Let
\mathrm{P}_{\ell}(\mathbb{R})
be the set of degree at most
\ell
polynomials with real coefficients.
We note that pointwise convergence, uniform convergence on compact subsets, and convergence of the coefficients as vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{\ell+1}
are all equivalent on
\mathrm{P}_{\ell}(\mathbb{R})
.
Thus, we will collectively refer to all of these are
convergence in
\mathrm{P}_{\ell}(\mathbb{R})
, and convergence in distribution of random polynomials with respect to these topologies as
convergence in distribution in
\mathrm{P}_{\ell}(\mathbb{R})
.

For natural numbers
N
and
k
we shall use
(N)_{k}=N(N-1)\cdots(N-k+1)
to denote the falling factorial. A partition,
\pi=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{r}\}
of
[j]:=\{1,\ldots,j\}
is a collection of pairwise disjoint, non-empty, sets
V_{i}
such that
\cup_{i=1}^{r}V_{i}=[j]
. We refer to
V_{i}
as a block of
\pi
, and denote the number of blocks of
\pi
as
|\pi|
. The set of all partitions of
[j]
is denoted by
\mathcal{P}(j)
.
Additionally, the set of
non-crossing
partitions, see for example
[
24
]
for a definition, will be denoted by
\mathcal{NC}(j)
. We write
1_{j}=\{\{1,2,\ldots,j\}\}
to denote the maximal element of
\mathcal{P}(j)
with respect to reverse refinement. For a partition
\pi=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{r}\}
and a sequence of numbers
\{c_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, we use the notation

c_{\pi}:=\prod_{i=1}^{r}c_{|V_{i}|}.

(1.7)

For degree
N
monic polynomials
p(x)=x^{N}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}(-1)^{k}a_{k}x^{N-k}
and
q(x)=x^{N}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}(-1)^{k}b_{k}x^{N-k}
the
finite free additive convolution

p\boxplus_{N}q
of
p
and
q
is defined by

p\boxplus_{N}q(x):=x^{N}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}(-1)^{k}\sum_{i+j=k}\frac{(N-i)!(N-j)!}{N!(N-k)!}a_{i}b_{j}.

For a degree
N
polynomial
p
with roots
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N}
, the
j
-th moment of
p
, denoted
m_{j}(p)
, is the
j
-th moment of its empirical root measure

m_{j}(p)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\lambda_{k}^{j}.

For a degree
N
polynomial
p(x)=x^{N}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}(-1)^{k}a_{k}x^{N-k}
, the finite free cumulants
\kappa_{1}^{N}(p),\dots,\kappa_{N}^{N}(p)
can be defined implicitly via

a_{k}=\frac{(N)_{k}}{N^{k}k!}\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}(k)}(-1)^{k-|\pi|}N^{|\pi|}\left[\prod_{V\in\pi}(|V|-1)!\right]\kappa_{\pi}^{N}(p).

(1.8)

It is worth noting that
\kappa_{j}^{\ell}(\He_{\ell})=\ell\delta_{j2}
, for
1\leq j\leq\ell
. Our main results rely primarily on the following results on finite free cumulants

Lemma 1.2

(Proposition 3.4 in
[
1
]
)
.

Let
p
be a degree
N
polynomial. Then, for any
1\leq j\leq k\leq N

\kappa_{j}^{k}(\partial_{k|N}p)=\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)^{j-1}\kappa_{j}^{N}(p).

(1.9)

Lemma 1.3

(See the proof of Theorem 5.4 in
[
3
]
)
.

Let
p
be a degree
N
polynomial. Then, for any
1\leq j\leq N

\kappa_{j}^{N}(p)=\frac{N^{j}}{(N)_{j}}\left[m_{j}(p)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\mathcal{NC}(j)\\
\sigma\neq 1_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{Q_{\sigma}(N)}{N^{j+1-|\sigma|}}\kappa_{\sigma}^{N}(p)\right],

(1.10)

where each
Q_{\sigma}(N)
is a monic degree
j+1-|\sigma|
polynomial in
N
.

It will also be convenient to represent polynomials by their
finite

R
-transform.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
p
be a degree
N
polynomial and let
P
be a formal power series such that
P\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\right)x^{N}=p(x)
. Then, the finite
R
-transform of
p
is the truncated formal power series

R_{p}(s):=-\frac{P^{\prime}(Ns)}{P(Ns)}\mod s^{N}.

(1.11)

Lemma 1.5

(See
[
18
,
21
,
3
]
)
.

Let
p
and
q
be a degree
N
polynomials, and let
P
and
Q
be formal power series such that
P\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\right)x^{N}=p(x)
and
Q\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\right)x^{N}=q(x)
. Then,
p\boxplus_{N}q(x)=P\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\right)Q\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\right)x^{N}
.
Moreover, the coefficients of
R_{p}
are the finite free cumulants of
p
, i.e.

R_{p}(s)=\kappa_{1}^{N}(p)+\kappa_{2}^{N}(p)s+\cdots+\kappa_{N}^{N}(p)s^{N-1}.

(1.12)

We end this section by recalling that finite free cumulants are additive, namely,
\kappa_{j}^{N}(p\boxplus_{N}q)=\kappa_{j}^{N}(p)+\kappa_{j}^{N}(q)
for
j=1,\dots,N
. It follows that the same is true for the finite R-transform,
R_{p\boxplus_{N}q}=R_{p}+R_{q}
.

2.
Main results

2.1.
Central limit theorems of repeated differentiation

For any real rooted degree
\ell
polynomial
p
, we let
\mathbf{z}(p)
denote the root vector of
p
in the Weyl chamber

\mathbb{W}^{\ell}=\left\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell})^{\mathrm{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}\ \big|\ x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq\cdots\geq x_{\ell}\right\}.

For any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider the positive semi-definite matrix
\Sigma^{(\ell),\mathbf{m}}
with entries

\Sigma^{(\ell),\mathbf{m}}_{ij}=\frac{ij}{4}\left(m_{4}(\mu)-1\right)m_{i}(\He_{\ell})m_{j}(\He_{\ell}).

(2.1)

As we shall see later, this matrix captures the covariance of the moments of
\tilde{p}_{\ell,N}
. To recover the covariance matrix of the roots we will need a linear transformation. Let
V
be the (scaled)
\ell\times\ell
Vandermonde matrix for the roots
z_{1,\ell}>z_{2,\ell}>\cdots>z_{\ell,\ell}
of
\He_{\ell}
with entries

V_{ij}=\frac{j}{\ell}z^{j-1}_{i,\ell},

(2.2)

and let
L=V^{-1}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\mu
be a measure on the real line of mean
0
, variance
1
, and having all finite moments. Let
\tilde{p}_{\ell,N}
be the polynomials defined by (
1.4
). Then,

\sqrt{N}\left[\mathbf{z}\left(\tilde{p}_{\ell,N}\right)-\mathbf{z}\left(\He_{\ell}\right)\right]\Rightarrow\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma^{(\ell),\mathbf{z}}),

(2.3)

where

\Sigma^{(\ell),\mathbf{z}}:=L^{\mathrm{T}}\Sigma^{(\ell),\mathbf{m}}L.

(2.4)

Remark 2.2
.

The shift in (
1.4
) removed an order
1
random shift of the roots that is asymptotically converging to a standard Gaussian. Theorem
2.1
captures the order
N^{-1/2}
fluctuations of the roots. While forming a precise statement of the smaller fluctuations is notationally cumbersome, finite free cumulants can be used to show (at least heuristically) that for large
N

\mathbf{z}\left(\mathcal{D}_{\sqrt{N}}\partial_{\ell|N}p_{N}\right)\approx\mathbf{z}\left(\He_{\ell}\right)+\sum_{k=0}^{\ell-1}N^{-k/2}Z_{k}\mathbf{v}_{k,\ell},

(2.5)

where
Z_{1},Z_{2},\dots
are standard Gaussian random variables and each
\mathbf{v}_{j,\ell}
is a deterministic vector depending on
\mu
only through its first
2j+2
moments.

We also have the following CLT for the polynomials.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\mu
be a measure on the real line of mean
0
, variance
1
, and having all finite moments. Let
\tilde{p}_{\ell,N}
be the polynomials defined by (
1.4
). Then,

\sqrt{N}\left[\tilde{p}_{\ell,N}(x)-\He_{\ell}(x)\right]\Rightarrow\sqrt{m_{4}(\mu)-1}Z\binom{\ell}{2}\He_{\ell-2}(x)

(2.6)

in
\mathrm{P}_{\ell}(\mathbb{R})
, where
Z
is a standard Gaussian random variable.

Remark 2.4
.

From a probabilistic perspective Theorem
2.3
is natural, giving the Gaussian fluctuations of a random sequence around a deterministic limit. However, it is not clear to us whether Theorem
2.3
can be given a natural interpretation from the perspectives of finite free probability since subtracting polynomials is not a nice operation in this theory. We do, however, point out the following. Let
P_{N}
be a degree
N
polynomial such that
p_{N}(x)=P_{N}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\right)x^{N}
. It is straightforward to show that
\tilde{p}_{\ell,N}(x)=e^{-m_{1}(p_{N})\sqrt{N}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}}P_{N}\left(\sqrt{N}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\right)x^{\ell}
. One interpretation of Theorem
2.3
is that for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}

\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt{N}\left[e^{-m_{1}(p_{N})\sqrt{N}s}P_{N}\left(\sqrt{N}s\right)-e^{-s^{2}/2}\right]\Rightarrow\frac{\sqrt{m_{4}(\mu)-1}}{2}Zs^{2}e^{-s^{2}/2}\mod s^{\ell}.

(2.7)

Thus,

e^{-m_{1}(p_{N})\sqrt{N}s}P_{N}\left(\sqrt{N}s\right)\approx e^{-s^{2}/2}\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{m_{4}(\mu)-1}Z}{2\sqrt{N}}s^{2}\right),

(2.8)

and hence by Lemma
1.5

\tilde{p}_{\ell,N}(x)\approx\He_{\ell}(x)\boxplus_{\ell}x^{\ell-2}\left(x^{2}+\frac{\sqrt{m_{4}(\mu)-1}}{\sqrt{N}}\binom{\ell}{2}Z\right).

(2.9)

We also point out that the right-hand side of (
2.6
) vanishes at the roots of
\He_{\ell-2}
. Thus, (
2.6
) and the three-term recurrence of Hermite polynomials imply that the fluctuations of
\tilde{p}_{\ell,N}(x)-\He_{\ell}(x)
are smaller than
N^{-1/2}
when

x-\frac{1}{G_{\ell}(x)}=0,

(2.10)

where
G_{\ell}(x)=\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\frac{1}{x-z_{j,\ell}}
is the Cauchy transform of the uniform distribution on the roots of
\He_{\ell}
. The function
T(x)=x-G_{\ell}(x)^{-1}
, and similar functions, often appear in dynamic descriptions of repeated differentiation and the related process of
fractional free convolution powers

[
30
,
11
]
. The vanishing of order
N^{-1/2}
fluctuations at solutions of (
2.10
) may have a description in the language of PDEs and optimal transport similar to heuristic results derived in
[
11
]
.

2.2.
Limiting polynomials for roots outside the Gaussian domain

Theorem
2.6
below provides an analogue of Proposition
1.1
for sequences of random polynomials
\{q_{N}\}_{N=1}^{\infty}
, where for each
N
the polynomial
q_{N}
has iid roots, but these roots are not necessarily in the domain of attraction of the Gaussian distribution. We begin with our assumptions.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\{q_{N}\}_{N=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of real rooted polynomials indexed by their degree of the form

q_{N}(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left(x-X_{j,N}\right).

(2.11)

We say a sequence of polynomials
\{q_{N}\}
is generated by the Lévy triple
(c,\sigma^{2},\nu)
if

(1)

For any
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
X_{1,N},X_{2,N},\dots,X_{N,N}
are iid random variables.

(2)

There exists
\sigma\geq 0
,
c\in\mathbb{R}
, and a measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\min(x^{2},1)\ \mathrm{d}\nu(x)<\infty,\quad\text{and}\quad\nu(\{0\})=0,

(2.12)

such that
S_{N}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}X_{j,N}
converges in distribution as
N\rightarrow\infty
to a random variable
Y
with log-characteristic function

\log\mathbb{E}e^{itY}=-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}t^{2}+itc+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(e^{itx}-1-\frac{itx}{1+x^{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}\nu(x).

(2.13)

The limit of repeated differentiation of polynomials generated by a Lévy triple is captured through a random entire function built from
(c,\sigma^{2},\nu)
. We will need a representation of
Y
in terms of a Poisson point process. We follow the approach of
[
8
]
. We define two functions
N_{+}:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
N_{-}:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as

N_{+}(t)=-\nu\left([t,\infty)\right),\text{ and }N_{-}=-\nu\left((-\infty,-t]\right).

(2.14)

These functions are non-decreasing, and we thus define the generalized inverses
N_{\pm}^{-1}(y)=\inf\{t:\ N_{\pm}(t)\geq y\}
where
y\in(-\infty,0)
. Define the constants
c_{j}
for
j\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
by

c_{j}=\mathrm{sign}(j)\int_{|j|-1}^{|j|}\frac{N_{\mathrm{sign}(j)}^{-1}(-t)}{1+N_{\mathrm{sign}(j)}^{-1}(-t)^{2}}\mathrm{d}t.

(2.15)

Let
\{\alpha_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}
be a Poisson point process with intensity measure
\nu
indexed such that
\alpha_{1}\geq\alpha_{2}\geq\cdots\geq 0
and
\alpha_{-1}\leq\alpha_{-2}\leq\cdots\leq 0
. It then follows from
[
8
, (7)]
that

Y=c+\sigma Z+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{{0}\}}(\alpha_{j}-c_{j}),

(2.16)

has log-characteristic function given by (
2.13
), where
Z
is a standard Gaussian random variable independent of the point process. Define the random entire functions
f
and
g
by

f(z):=e^{-Yz-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}z^{2}}\prod_{j\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\left(1-\alpha_{j}z\right)e^{\alpha_{j}z}=:e^{-Yz-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}z^{2}}g(z).

(2.17)

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\{q_{N}\}
be a sequence of random polynomials generated by the Lévy triple
(c,\sigma^{2},\nu)
and let
f
and
g
be as in (
2.17
). For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, let

\tilde{q}_{\ell,N}(x)=\mathcal{D}_{N}\partial_{\ell|N}q_{N}\left(x\right).

(2.18)

Then, for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,

\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\tilde{q}_{\ell,N}(x)=f\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\right)x^{\ell}=\left(x-Y\right)^{\ell}\boxplus_{\ell}\sigma^{\ell}\He_{\ell}\left(\frac{x}{\sigma}\right)\boxplus_{\ell}g\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\right)x^{\ell},

(2.19)

in distribution in
\mathrm{P}_{\ell}(\mathbb{R})
.

Not only does finite free probability provide an efficient proof of Theorem
2.6
, it also provides a natural characterization of the limit in terms of the Lévy triple through (
2.19
). The Bercovici–Pata bijection
[
5
]
provides an explicit bijection between classical and free infinite divisibility, where the same Lévy triple determines either the characteristic function or the
R
-transform. As pointed out in
[
6
]
, the free Lévy-Khintchine representation of the
R
-transform then gives a connection between polynomials of the form
f\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\right)x^{\ell}
and the free infinitely divisible distribution with
R
-transform
R(s)=-\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}
. If
f
is defined by (
2.17
) and we view a Poisson point process as a random discrete approximation of its intensity measure
\nu
, then
R(z)=-\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}
is a random meromorphic approximation of the
R
-transform determined by the Lévy triple
(c,\sigma^{2},\nu)
and repeated differentiation provides a way to connect classical and free probability on the level of random polynomial roots.

3.
Proofs of the CLTs

The proofs of the central limit theorems of Section
2
follow from Taylor expansions, or the
Delta method
as it is referred to in statistics. We shall use the term Delta method as short hand for the fact that if
\sqrt{N}\left[X_{N}-\theta\right]\Rightarrow\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
and
g
is a twice differentiable function such that
g^{\prime}(\theta)\neq 0
, then

\sqrt{N}\left[g\left(X_{N}\right)-g(\theta)\right]\Rightarrow\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}g^{\prime}(\theta)^{2}).

(3.1)

The extension to random vectors is a straightforward generalization using multivariate Taylor expansions.

We begin with a lemma on the fluctuations of the moments of
p_{N}
, which follows from straightforward computations.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p_{N}
be as in (
1.1
). Then, for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}

\sqrt{N}\left[\left(m_{1}(p_{N}),\dots,m_{\ell}(p_{N})\right)^{\mathrm{T}}-\left(m_{1}(\mu),\dots,m_{\ell}(\mu)\right)^{\mathrm{T}}\right]\Rightarrow\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma),

(3.2)

where
\Sigma_{ij}=m_{i+j}(\mu)-m_{i}(\mu)m_{j}(\mu)
.

Next, we want to compare the finite free cumulants and the free cumulants of
p_{N}
. While an explicit comparison suitable for our purposes does not seem to have appeared in the literature, the following is essentially contained in the ideas of
[
3
,
2
]
. To simplify notation we write
\kappa_{j}(p_{N})=\kappa_{j}(\mu_{p_{N}})
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

\kappa_{j}^{N}(p_{N})=\kappa_{j}(p_{N})+O\left(\frac{1}{N}\right),

(3.3)

almost surely.

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

We begin by noting that the following proof holds for any sequence of polynomials whose first
j
moments are uniformly bounded in
N
. Thus, the probabilistic “almost sure” conclusion follows from the moment assumptions on
\mu
and the law of large numbers. The proof will follow by induction on
j
. For
j=1
,
\kappa_{1}^{N}(p_{N})=\kappa_{1}({p_{N}})
for any polynomial.

Let
j\geq 2
and assume (
3.3
) holds for any
1\leq k\leq j-1
. By the moment-free cumulant formula, see
[
24
]
for example,

\kappa_{j}(p_{N})=m_{j}(p_{N})-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\mathcal{NC}(j)\\
\sigma\neq 1_{j}\end{subarray}}\kappa_{\sigma}(p_{N}).

(3.4)

It then follows from Lemma
1.3
that

\displaystyle\kappa_{j}^{N}(p_{N})-\kappa_{j}(p_{N})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{N^{j}}{(N)_{j}}-1\right)m_{j}(p_{N})

(3.5)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\mathcal{NC}(j)\\
\sigma\neq 1_{j}\end{subarray}}\kappa_{\sigma}(p_{N})-\frac{N^{j}Q_{\sigma}(N)}{(N)_{j}N^{j+1-|\sigma|}}\kappa_{\sigma}^{N}(p_{N})

\displaystyle=O\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\mathcal{NC}(j)\\
\sigma\neq 1_{j}\end{subarray}}\kappa_{\sigma}(p_{N})-\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)\right)\kappa_{\sigma}^{N}(p_{N}),

almost surely. Now, for any fixed
\sigma\in\mathcal{NC}(j)
,
\sigma\neq 1_{j}
, we may use the induction hypothesis to conclude that

\kappa_{\sigma}(p_{N})-\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)\right)\kappa_{\sigma}^{N}(p_{N})=O\left(\frac{1}{N}\right),

(3.6)

almost surely. Summing over all such
\sigma
completes the proof.
∎

Theorems
2.1
and Theorem
2.3
both follow from a central limit theorem, Theorem
3.3
, for the finite free cumulants. For any degree
\ell
polynomial
p
, we denote by
\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{\ell}(p)=(\kappa_{1}^{\ell}(p),\dots,\kappa_{\ell}^{\ell}(p))^{\mathrm{T}}
the vector of its degree
\ell
finite free cumulants. Additionally, we denote by
\mathbf{m}(p)=(m_{1}(p),\dots,m_{\ell}(p))^{\mathrm{T}}
the vector of the first
\ell
moments of
p
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\tilde{p}_{\ell,N}
be as in (
1.4
). Then, for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}

\sqrt{N}\left[\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{\ell}\left(\tilde{p}_{\ell,N}\right)-(0,\ell,0,\dots,0)^{\mathrm{T}}\right]\Rightarrow\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma^{(\ell)}),

(3.7)

where the only non-zero entry of
\Sigma^{(\ell)}
is the
(2,2)
-entry
\ell^{2}(m_{4}(\mu)-1)
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\tilde{p}_{\ell,N}
be as in (
1.4
). Then, for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}

\sqrt{N}\left[\mathbf{m}\left(\tilde{p}_{\ell,N}\right)-\mathbf{m}\left(\He_{\ell}\right)\right]\Rightarrow\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma^{(\ell),\mathbf{m}}).

(3.8)

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

Lemma
3.1
and application of the Delta method imply that

\sqrt{N}\left[\left(\kappa_{1}(p_{N}),\dots,\kappa_{\ell}(p_{N})\right)-\left(\kappa_{1}(\mu),\dots,\kappa_{\ell}(\mu)\right)\right]\Rightarrow\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma^{\kappa}),

(3.9)

for some covariance matrix
\Sigma^{\kappa}
whose explicit structure is largely unimportant to us. We do note that

\Sigma^{\kappa}_{22}=m_{4}(\mu)-1,

(3.10)

which follows from the fact that
\kappa_{2}(\mu)=\var(\mu)
. Applying Lemma
3.2
we see that

\sqrt{N}\left[\left(\kappa_{1}^{N}(p_{N}),\dots,\kappa_{\ell}^{N}(p_{N})\right)-\left(\kappa_{1}(\mu),\dots,\kappa_{\ell}(\mu)\right)\right]\Rightarrow\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma^{\kappa}).

(3.11)

Note that by Lemma
1.2

\kappa_{j}^{\ell}(\tilde{p}_{\ell,N})=\frac{\ell^{j-1}}{N^{\frac{j}{2}-1}}\kappa_{j}^{N}(p_{N}),

(3.12)

for any
j\geq 2
, with
\kappa_{1}^{\ell}(\tilde{p}_{\ell,N})=0
. It then follows from (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) that

\sqrt{N}\left[\kappa_{j}^{\ell}(\tilde{p}_{\ell,N})-\ell\delta_{j2}\right]\Rightarrow\mathcal{N}(0,\ell^{2}\Sigma^{\kappa}_{22}\delta_{j2}).

(3.13)

The proof is then completed by combining (
3.10
) and (
3.13
).
∎

Proof of Corollary
3.4

Corollary
3.4

3.3

[

Proof of Theorem

Let
f:\mathbb{W}^{\ell}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
be the map from the ordered roots of a polynomial to the first

Proof of Theorem

From Proposition
1.1
, (
1.8
), and Theorem

\displaystyle\sqrt{N}\left[\tilde{p}_{\ell,N}(x)-\He_{\ell}(x)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=2}^{\ell}\sqrt{N}\frac{(\ell)_{j}}{\ell^{j}j!}x^{\ell-j}

(3.14)

\displaystyle\times\left[\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}(j)}(-1)^{-|\pi|}\ell^{|\pi|}\left(\prod_{V\in\pi}(|V|-1)!\right)\left(\kappa_{\pi}^{\ell}(\tilde{p}_{\ell,N})-\kappa_{\pi}^{\ell}(\He_{\ell})\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\sqrt{m_{4}(\mu)-1}Z\binom{\ell}{2}\He_{\ell-2}(x).

This completes the proof.
∎

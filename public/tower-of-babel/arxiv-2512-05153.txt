Single-walled carbon nanotubes: the Bloch theory, reciprocal tubes, and a tight-binding approximation Preprint: APS/123-QED
Yuri A. Antipov

Abstract
The electronic structure of a graphene sheet is altered when it is rolled up to form a single-walled carbon nanotube (SWCNT), and the curvature effects for small radius nanotubes become significant. In the paper, an analogue of the Bloch theory of crystals with translational symmetry to armchair, zigzag, and chiral SWCNTs, cylindrical lattices with rotation-translational symmetry, is proposed. It is based on the use of cylindrical coordinates, three-dimensional characteristic vectors of the lattice, a reciprocal tube, and rotation-translation transformations in the real and reciprocal 3d spaces. The Brillouin zone on the reciprocal tube and the domain of the wave-vector are determined. An analogue of the Bloch theorem for SWCNTs is stated and proved. A tight-binding approximation scheme for orbitals orthogonal to the nanotube surface is described, and the Hamiltonian and overlap matrices associated with the first and second nearest-neighbor tight-binding approximations are derived.

Introduction

Although carbon nanotubes had been known since the middle of the last century, the active systematic study of electronic, optical and mechanical properties of
nanotubes was initiated by the 1991 Iijima’s report
iij
that described high-resolution electron micrographs
of helicon carbon microtubes with the thinnest needle observed having 2.2 nm in diameter. It was pointed out that the helical structure of the needles was entirely different
from the screw dislocation model of conventional crystals in the sense that the needle crystals had a cylindrical lattice symmetry. One of the most widely
applied techniques for describing the electronic structure of SWCNTs is the zone-folding method
sai98
, a modification of
the tight-binding approximation approach developed for graphene
wal
. A review
net
described theoretical aspects of graphene and summarized the modern methods including the tight-binding approximation technique for analysis of electronic structure of single-layer and double-layer graphene.

Electronic band structure of SWCNTs analyzed by tight-binding approximation
ham
revealed the existence of three classes of nanotubes, metallic, semiconducting with narrow band gaps, and
semiconducting with moderate band gaps. Tight-binding folding of the 2d energy bands of graphene was applied
sai92a
,
sai92b
to calculate the
electronic structure of SWCNTs. On the basis of the calculations it was predicted
sai92b
that approximately a third of SWCNTs were a 1d metal, while the others
were 1d semiconductors. A similar procedure was implemented
aji
to calculate the
\pi
-electron states of SWCNTs in magnetic field. The tight-binding approximation and folding the graphene energy bands were also employed
sai93
for double-walled carbon commensurate and incommensurate nanotubes. It was concluded that
due to the symmetry, the interlayer interaction between the layers did not affect the metallic nature of the constituent.
The symmetry properties of chiral SWCNTs, the zone-folding technique, and the irreducible representations of symmetry groups associated with the chiral vector
were employed
jis
,
bar
to classify the electronic wave-functions and the phonon modes at the Brillouin zone center.

Comparison
bla
of two approaches for electronic structure of SWCNTs, the graphene based tight-binding approximation and the method of pseudopotential local density functional theory (DFT),
revealed that for small radius nanotubes in some cases the band gaps computed by the former theory deviated from those obtained by the second approach by more than
50%. Effects of curvature on the electronic structure of metallic SWCNTs were included
kle
into the model by introducing a deformation tensor
D^{curv}=(d_{ij})
,

i,j=1,2
, with the only one nonzero entry
d_{11}
, expressed through a transfer integral, the carbon-carbon bond length, and the nanotube radius.
To take into account the curvature effects on electronic
structure of SWCNTs,
another approach, a symmetry-adapted non-orthogonal tight-binding approximation scheme, was applied in
pop
.
Electron-phonon interaction in an armchair nanotube
(5,5)
and zigzag nanotubes
(5,0)
and
(6,0)

was analyzed in
barn
. By using the Fröhlich Hamiltonian it was found that the zone-folding technique described well the tube
(5,5)

In the case of the zigzag nanotubes
(5,0)
and
(6,0)
the large
curvature made these tubes metallic with a large density of states at the Fermi energy and led to unusual
electron-phonon interactions, with the dominant coupling coming from the out-of-plane phonon modes. A DFT method for the analysis of torsional deformations
of structures with helical symmetry was introduced in
yu

tested
aga

equation in helical coordinates.

(1)

\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})=e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf r}}u({\bf r};{\bf k}),\quad u({\bf r}+{\bf R};{\bf k})=u({\bf r};{\bf k}).

(2)

Analogues of formula (
1
) for structures with rotation-translational symmetry were presented in
whi
,
zha
,

,

the representation

, nor did they

{\bf n}({\bf r})
.
In Section

and

,

V.1
Analogue of the Bloch theorem

Theorem 1.
Let
{\cal A}={\cal A}(n,m)
be a SWCNT characterized by the chiral vector
{\bf C}_{h}(n,m)
. Suppose the potential
{\cal V}({\bf r})

of the Hamiltonian

{\cal H}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\nabla^{2}+{\cal V}({\bf r})

(64)

of a single electron has the rotation-translation symmetry of the nanotube

{\cal V}({\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}{\bf r})={\cal V}({\bf r}),\quad j=\pm 1,\pm 2,\ldots,

(65)

where the transformations
{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}
are defined on the nanotube
{\cal A}(n,m)
by (
45
),
{\bf r}\in{\cal U}_{\cal A}
is the vector (
11
), and
{\cal U}_{\cal A}

is a unit curved hexagonal cell of the nanotube.
Then any solution of the Schrödinger equation

{\cal H}\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})=\epsilon({\bf k})\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})

(66)

satisfies the relations

\psi({\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}{\bf r};\tilde{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}{\bf k})=\exp\left\{\pm ij\left(\kappa c_{\pm}+\frac{2\pi z}{c_{\pm}}\right)\right\}\psi({\bf r};{\bf k}),

(67)

and, in particular,

\psi({\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}{\bf r};{\bf k})=e^{\pm ij\kappa c_{\pm}}\psi({\bf r};{\bf k}),\quad j=\pm 1,\pm 2,\ldots.

(68)

Here,
c_{\pm}
are the real parameters (
42
),
{\bf k}\in\tilde{\cal K}^{\pm}
is the vector (
24
),
{\cal K}^{\pm}
are the domains (
50
),
and
\tilde{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}
are the transformations (
49
) defined on the reciprocal tube.

Moreover, if the function
\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})
is written as

\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})=e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf r}}u({\bf r};{\bf k}),

(69)

then the function
u({\bf r};{\bf k})
is invariant with respect to the composition of the transformations
{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}
and
\tilde{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}
,

u({\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}{\bf r};\tilde{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}{\bf k})=u({\bf r};{\bf k}),\quad j=\pm 1,\pm 2,\ldots.

(70)

In particular, if the nanotube has the armchair symmetry,
{\cal A}={\cal A}(n,n)
, then the relations (
67
) have the form

\psi({\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}{\bf r};\tilde{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}{\bf k})=\exp\left\{\pm ij\left(\frac{\kappa a}{2}+\frac{4\pi z}{a}\right)\right\}\psi({\bf r};{\bf k}),

{\bf k}\in{\cal K},\quad j=\pm 1,\pm 2,\ldots,

(71)

the transformations
{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}
and
\tilde{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}
are given by (
15
) and (
26
), while
{\cal K}
is the domain (
29
).

For a zigzag nanotube
{\cal A}={\cal A}(n,0)
, the relations (
67
) read

\psi({\cal T}_{j}^{+}{\bf r};\tilde{\cal T}_{j}^{+}{\bf k})=\psi({\bf r};{\bf k}),\quad{\bf k}\in{\cal K}^{+},

\psi({\cal T}_{j}^{-}{\bf r};\tilde{\cal T}_{j}^{-}{\bf k})=\exp\left\{-ij\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}\kappa a}{2}+\frac{4\pi z}{\sqrt{3}a}\right)\right\}\psi({\bf r};{\bf k}),

{\bf k}\in{\cal K}^{-},\quad j=\pm 1,\pm 2,\ldots.

(72)

where
{\cal T}_{j}^{+}=\tilde{\cal T}_{j}^{+}={\cal R}_{j}
are the rotation transformations

{\cal R}_{j}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\cos j\alpha&-\sin j\alpha&0\\
\sin j\alpha&\cos j\alpha&0\\
0&0&1\\
\end{array}\right),

(73)

{\cal T}_{j}^{-}
and
\tilde{\cal T}_{j}^{-}
are the rotation-translation transformations in the real and reciprocal spaces given by
(
57
) and (
59
), and
{\cal K}^{-}
and
{\cal K}^{+}
are the domains defined in (
62
) and (
63
), respectively.

Proof.
Let
f({\bf r};{\bf k})
be a function of
{\bf r}
and
{\bf k}
, the radius-vectors of points on the real and reciprocal tubes
{\cal A}
and
\tilde{\cal A}
, respectively.
Define the rotation-translation transformations of the function
f({\bf r};{\bf k})
by

{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}f({\bf r};{\bf k})=f({\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}{\bf r};{\bf k}),

\tilde{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}f({\bf r};{\bf k})=f({\bf r};\tilde{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}{\bf k}).

(74)

Denote by
\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})
an eigenfunction of the transformations
{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}
in the real space,

{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})=\lambda_{j}^{\pm}({\bf k})\psi({\bf r};{\bf k}).

(75)

Simultaneously, it is an eigenfunction of transformation
\tilde{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}
in the reciprocal space,

\tilde{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})=\mu_{j}^{\pm}({\bf r})\psi({\bf r};{\bf k}),

(76)

that is

{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}\tilde{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})=\lambda_{j}^{\pm}({\bf k})\mu_{j}^{\pm}({\bf r})\psi({\bf r};{\bf k}).

(77)

As in the Bloch theory for flat lattices, the electron distribution
|\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})|
has to have
the same rotation-translation periodic properties as the potential (
65

|{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}\tilde{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})|=|\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})|.

(78)

This implies that the eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}^{\pm}({\bf k})
and
\mu_{j}^{\pm}({\bf r})
have the form

\lambda_{j}^{\pm}({\bf k})=e^{i\sigma_{j}^{\pm}({\bf k})},\quad\mu_{j}^{\pm}({\bf r})=e^{i\eta_{j}^{\pm}({\bf r})},

(79)

where the arguments
\sigma_{j}^{\pm}({\bf k})
and
\eta_{j}^{\pm}({\bf r})
are invariant to the rotations
{\cal R}_{j}
.
Since the translations in the transformations
{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}
and
\tilde{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}
are applied along the

-axes
of the real and reciprocal spaces
R^{3}
and
\tilde{R}^{3}

\tilde{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}
:

{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}\tilde{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}{\cal T}_{l}^{\pm}\tilde{\cal T}_{l}^{\pm}\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})=\psi({\cal T}_{j+l}^{\pm}{\bf r};\tilde{\cal T}_{j+l}{\bf k})

=\lambda_{j}({\bf k})\lambda_{l}({\bf k})\mu_{j}({\bf r})\mu_{l}({\bf r})\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})

=\lambda_{j+l}({\bf k})\mu_{j+l}({\bf r})\psi({\bf r};{\bf k}),

(80)

we deduce

\sigma_{j}^{\pm}({\bf k})=\pm\kappa c_{\pm}j,\quad\eta_{j}^{\pm}({\bf r})=\pm\frac{2\pi zj}{c_{\pm}}.

(81)

It is evident that the Hamiltonian operator commutes with the transformations
{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}

defined on the real tube
{\cal A}
. Also, it commutes with the transformations

e^{-i\eta_{j}^{\pm}({\bf r})}\tilde{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}=\exp\left(\mp\frac{2\pi izj}{c_{\pm}}\right)\tilde{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm},

(82)

that is

\left[\exp\left(\mp\frac{2\pi izj}{c_{\pm}}\right)\tilde{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}{\cal T}^{\pm}_{j},{\cal H}\right]=0.

(83)

Therefore, without loss of generality, the eigenfunction chosen before may be considered as a solution of the wave equation (
65
). This implies that
the relation

{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}\tilde{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})=\exp\left\{\pm ij\left(\kappa c_{\pm}+\frac{2\pi z}{c_{\pm}}\right)\right\}\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})

(84)

holds, and formula (
67
) is proved. If the transformations
\tilde{\cal T}_{j}{\bf k}
are bypassed, then instead of formula (
67
) we have the relation (
68
).

Introduce next a function
u({\bf r},{\bf k})
such that
\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})=e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf r}}u({\bf r};{\bf k})
and compute the scalar product

{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}{\bf r}\cdot\tilde{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}{\bf k}

=\pi\cos(\theta-\tau)+\kappa z\pm c_{\pm}\kappa j\pm\frac{2\pi zj}{c_{\pm}}+2\pi j^{2}.

(85)

Since

{\bf k}\cdot{\bf r}=\pi\cos(\theta-\tau)+\kappa z,

(86)

we deduce

e^{i{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}{\bf r}\cdot\tilde{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}{\bf k}}=\exp\left\{i\left({\bf k}\cdot{\bf r}\pm c_{\pm}\kappa j\pm\frac{2\pi zj}{c_{\pm}}\right)\right\}.

(87)

By applying the transformations
{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}\tilde{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}
to the function
\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})

we obtain

\psi({\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}{\bf r};\tilde{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}{\bf k})=e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf r}}e^{\pm ij(c_{\pm}\kappa+2\pi z/c_{\pm})}u({\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}{\bf r};\tilde{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}{\bf k})

=e^{\pm ij(c_{\pm}\kappa+2\pi z/c_{\pm})}\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})

=e^{\pm ij(c_{\pm}\kappa+2\pi z/c_{\pm})}e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf r}}u({\bf r};{\bf k}),

(88)

and the periodicity property (
70
) of the function
u({\bf r};{\bf k})
becomes evident.

Formulas (
71
) and (
72
) immediately follow from the general relations (
67
) if we put

c_{\pm}=\frac{a}{2}
for the armchair symmetry and
c_{+}=0
and
c_{-}=\frac{\sqrt{3}a}{2}
for the zigzag case. The theorem is proved.

Remark.
If the transformations
\tilde{\cal T}^{\pm}_{j}{\bf k}
on the reciprocal tube are ignored, then the second part of Theorem 1 for SWCNTs,
formula (
69
), is invalid.
Indeed, since

e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}{\bf r}}=e^{i[\pi\cos(j\alpha+\theta-\tau)+\kappa z]}e^{\pm ij\kappa c_{\pm}},

(89)

there is no way to have a function
u({\bf r};{\bf k})
satisfying the periodicity condition
u({\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}{\bf r};{\bf k})=u({\bf r};{\bf k})
,

j=\pm 1,\pm 2,\ldots
, such that
\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})=e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf r}}u({\bf r};{\bf k})
.

V.2
Differential equation for the function
u({\bf r};{\bf k})

To derive a differential equation
for the function u(
r
;
k
), we write down the operator
\nabla^{2}

as

\nabla^{2}=\frac{1}{r_{t}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\theta^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}

(90)

and employ the representation (
69
) that is

\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})=e^{i[\pi\cos(\tau-\theta)+\kappa z]}u({\bf r};{\bf k}).

(91)

On substituting this function into the Schrödinger equation (
66
) we obtain

\left[-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\tilde{\nabla}^{2}+{\cal V}({\bf r})\right]u({\bf r};{\bf k})=\epsilon({\bf k})u({\bf r};{\bf k}),

(92)

where

\tilde{\nabla}=\frac{1}{r_{t}^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\theta^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}+\frac{2\pi i}{r_{t}^{2}}\sin(\tau-\theta)\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}

+2i\kappa\frac{\partial}{\partial z}-\frac{\pi}{r_{t}^{2}}[i\cos(\tau-\theta)+\pi\sin^{2}(\tau-\theta)]-\kappa^{2}.

(93)

Since the function
u({\bf r};{\bf k})
possesses the symmetry property (
70
), it suffices to determine it
within the nanotube unit cell, a single hexagon rolled around the nanotube surface.

V.3
Eigenfunctions of the Hamiltonian on a SWCNT

We aim to derive a representation of eigenfunctions of the Hamiltonian on the nanotube
{\cal A}(n,m)
,

\left(\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\nabla^{2}+{\cal V}({\bf r})\right)\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})=\epsilon({\bf k})\psi({\bf r};{\bf k}),

(94)

subject to the cyclic boundary conditions

{\cal L}^{+}_{s,-L}\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})={\cal L}^{+}_{s,L}\psi({\bf r};{\bf k}),

({\bf r};{\bf k})\in{\cal U}_{\cal A}\times{\cal K}^{+},\quad s=0,\ldots,m-1,

(95)

when
1\leq m\leq n
, and

{\cal L}^{-}_{s,-L}\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})={\cal L}^{-}_{s,L}\psi({\bf r};{\bf k}),

({\bf r};{\bf k})\in{\cal U}_{\cal A}\times{\cal K}^{-},\quad s=0,\ldots,n-1,

(96)

for zigzag nanotubes,
m=0
.
Here,
{\cal L}^{+}_{s,\pm L}
and
{\cal L}^{-}_{s,\pm L}
are the following functionals of the boundary conditions:

{\cal L}^{+}_{s,\pm L}\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})=\psi\left({\cal T}^{-}_{s}{\cal T}^{+}_{\pm L}{\bf r};{\bf k}\right),

{\cal L}^{-}_{s,\pm L}\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})=\psi\left({\cal R}_{s}{\cal T}_{\pm L}^{-}{\bf r};{\bf k}\right).

(97)

We remark that the cyclic boundary conditions (
95
) and (
96
) are SWCNTs analogues of the Born-von Karman periodic boundary conditions
for flat lattices.

Analyze first chiral and armchair nanotubes. On using Theorem 1 we simplify the boundary conditions (
95
) as

e^{-iL\kappa c_{+}}\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})=e^{iL\kappa c_{+}}\psi({\bf r};{\bf k}).

(98)

These conditions are satisfied if

\kappa=\kappa_{\nu}=\frac{\pi\nu}{Lc_{+}},\quad\nu=-L,-L+1,\ldots,L-1,

(99)

where
c_{+}
is the parameter defined in (
42
) for chiral nanotubes and
c_{+}=\frac{a}{2}
for armchair nanotubes.
For zigzag nanotubes, the boundary condition (
96
) yields

e^{iL\sqrt{3}\kappa/2}\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})=e^{-iL\sqrt{3}\kappa/2}\psi({\bf r};{\bf k}),

(100)

and therefore

\kappa=\kappa_{\nu}=\frac{2\pi\nu}{L\sqrt{3}a},\quad\nu=-L,-L+1,\ldots,L-1.

(101)

Consider now
4L^{2}
functions
\psi_{j}({\bf r};{\bf k}_{\nu})
given by

\psi_{j}({\bf r},{\bf k}_{\nu})={\cal T}_{j}^{+}\psi({\bf r},{\bf k}_{\nu})

(102)

for nanotubes
{\cal A}(n,m)
,
1\leq m\leq n
, and

\psi_{j}({\bf r},{\bf k}_{\nu})={\cal T}_{j}^{-}\psi({\bf r},{\bf k}_{\nu})

(103)

for zigzag nanotubes
{\cal A}(n,0)
. Here,

{\bf k}_{\nu}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-\frac{\pi}{r_{t}}\sin\tau\\
\frac{\pi}{r_{t}}\cos\tau\\
\kappa_{\nu}\\
\end{array}\right),

j=-L,\ldots,L-1,\quad\nu=-L,\ldots,L-1,

(104)

and
\kappa_{\nu}
is determined in (
99
) if
m\geq 1
and (
101
) if
m=0
.
Based on these functions we design representations of the eigenfunctions of the boundary value problem (
94
) to (
96
). They are

\Psi({\bf r};{\bf k}_{\nu})=\sum_{j=1}^{2L}e^{-i\pi\nu j/L}\psi_{j}({\bf r},{\bf k}_{\nu}).

(105)

These
2L
functions are linearly independent, solve the wave equation (
94
), and satisfy the cyclic boundary conditions
(
95
) when
1\leq m\leq n
and (
96
) for the zigzag symmetry case,
m=0
.
They also obey the condition (
68
) of Theorem 1. Indeed, in the case
1\leq m\leq n
,

{\cal T}_{l}^{+}\Psi({\bf r},{\bf k}_{\nu})=\sum_{j=-L}^{L-1}e^{-\pi i\nu j/L}{\cal T}_{l+j}^{+}\psi({\bf r},{\bf k}_{\nu})

=\sum_{j^{\prime}=l-L}^{l+L-1}e^{-\pi i\nu(j^{\prime}-l)/L}{\cal T}_{j^{\prime}}^{+}\psi({\bf r},{\bf k}_{\nu})=e^{il\kappa c_{+}}\Psi({\bf r},{\bf k}_{\nu}).

(106)

For zigzag nanotubes, it suffices to replace in (
106
)
c_{+}
by
-\sqrt{3}a/2
and
{\cal T}_{l}^{+}
by
{\cal T}_{l}^{-}
.

Note that, as a building block, we employed the functions
\psi_{j}({\bf r},{\bf k}_{\nu})
associated with
s=0
. In the case
m=1
, this is the only choice, and
the number of functions used is
2L=N
.
For zigzag and armchair nanotubes, the number of functions needed is the smallest,

2L=N/n
. For chiral nanotubes
{\cal A}(n,m)
(
1<m<n
), this number is
2L=N/m
. Since the finite number of pure rotations
{\cal R}_{s}

in the zigzag case and rotation-translation transformations
{\cal T}^{-}_{s}
in the case
1\leq m\leq n
do not generate new linearly independent functions,
the choice of functions
\psi_{j}({\bf r},{\bf k}_{\nu})
is independent of
s
.

Prove finally that the eigenfunctions
\Psi_{\nu}({\bf r})
are orthogonal. Let

{\cal I}_{\mu\nu}=\int_{{\cal A}_{0}}\overline{\Psi_{\mu}({\bf r})}\Psi_{\nu}({\bf r})d\Omega,

(107)

where

{\cal A}_{0}=\bigcup_{l=-L}^{L-1}{\cal U}_{\cal A}^{0l},

(108)

and show that

{\cal I}_{\mu\nu}=0,\quad\mu,\nu=-L,\ldots L-1,\quad\mu\neq\nu.

(109)

For the case
1\leq m\leq n
, we have

\int_{{\cal A}_{0}}\overline{\Psi_{\mu}({\bf r})}\Psi_{\nu}({\bf r})d\Omega=\sum_{l=-L}^{L-1}\int_{{\cal U}_{{\cal A}}^{0l}}\overline{\Psi_{\mu}({\bf r})}\Psi_{\nu}({\bf r})d\Omega

=\sum_{l=-L}^{L-1}\int_{{\cal U}_{{\cal A}}^{00}}\overline{\Psi_{\mu}({\cal T}^{+}_{l}{\bf r})}\Psi_{\nu}({\cal T}^{+}_{l}{\bf r})d\Omega

=\int_{{\cal U}_{{\cal A}}^{00}}\overline{\Psi_{\mu}({\bf r})}\Psi_{\nu}({\bf r})d\Omega\sum_{l=-L}^{L-1}e^{i\pi(\mu-\nu)l/L}=0,\quad\mu\neq\nu.

(110)

For the zigzag case, the derivations are similar.

VI
Tight-binding approximation for SWCNTs

Let
\varphi_{\mu}({\bf r})
(
\mu=A,B
) be an atomic orbital associated with one of the two carbon atoms in the unit hexagonal cell or, equivalently, the parallelogram of periods, and
orthogonal to the nanotube surface. When the tube is unrolled and the surface becomes a graphene sheet, the functions
\varphi_{\mu}
are classified as
2p_{z}
-orbitals
with
z
orthogonal to the graphene sheet. Consider a tight-binding Bloch function of a SWCNT
{\cal A}(n,m)
. For armchair and chiral nanotubes
{\cal A}(n,m)
,
1\leq m\leq n
,
it has the form

\Phi_{\mu}({\bf r};{\bf k}_{\nu})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{s=0}^{m-1}e^{is\kappa_{\nu}c_{-}}\sum_{j=-L}^{L-1}e^{-ij\kappa_{\nu}c_{+}}

\times{\cal T}^{-}_{s}{\cal T}^{+}_{j}\varphi_{\mu}({\bf r}),

(111)

where
N=2Lm
and
\nu=-L,-L+1,\ldots L-1.
Notice that for an armchair nanotube
c_{+}=c_{-}=a/2
.

For a zigzag nanotube
{\cal A}(n,0)
, the function is introduced as

\Phi_{\mu}({\bf r};{\bf k}_{\nu})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\sum_{s=-L}^{L-1}e^{is\kappa_{\nu}c_{-}}{\cal R}_{j}{\cal T}^{-}_{s}\varphi_{\mu}({\bf r}),

(112)

where
N=2Ln
. It is directly verified that

{\cal T}_{l}^{\pm}\Phi_{\mu}({\bf r};\nu)=e^{\pm il\kappa_{\nu}c_{-}}\Phi_{\mu}({\bf r};\nu),

(113)

and therefore the functions (
111
) and (
112
) are Bloch functions associated with corresponding nanotubes indeed.

Following the tight-binding approximation scheme we derive the secular equation for two energy bands
\epsilon({\bf k})=\epsilon_{\pm}({\bf k})

\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}H_{AA}-\epsilon({\bf k})S_{AA}&H_{AB}-\epsilon({\bf k})S_{AB}\\
H_{BA}-\epsilon({\bf k})S_{BA}&H_{BB}-\epsilon({\bf k})S_{BB}\\
\end{array}\right|=0,

(114)

where
H=(H_{\mu^{\prime}\mu})
and
S=(S_{\mu^{\prime}\mu})
are the Hamiltonian and overlap matrices whose entries are

H_{\mu^{\prime}\mu}=\langle\Phi_{\mu^{\prime}}|{\cal H}|\Phi_{\mu}\rangle,\quad S_{\mu^{\prime}\mu}=\langle\Phi_{\mu^{\prime}}|\Phi_{\mu}\rangle.

(115)

This brings us to the bonding
\pi
and antibonding
\pi^{*}
energy bands,
\epsilon_{+}({\bf k})
and
\epsilon_{-}({\bf k})
,

\epsilon_{\pm}({\bf k})=\frac{-Q\pm\sqrt{Q^{2}-4|S||H|}}{2|S|},

(116)

where we introduced the notation

Q=S_{AB}H_{BA}+S_{BA}H_{AB}-S_{AA}H_{BB}-S_{BB}H_{AA}.

(117)

The coefficients
S_{\mu^{\prime}\mu}
may be expressed through integrals of the atomic orbitals over the whole nanotube surface. In the case
1\leq m\leq n
they are

S_{\mu^{\prime}\mu}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{s^{\prime},s=0}^{m-1}e^{i(s-s^{\prime})\kappa_{\nu}c_{-}}\sum_{j^{\prime},j=-L}^{L-1}e^{-i(j-j^{\prime})\kappa_{\nu}c_{+}}

\times\int\overline{{\cal T}_{s^{\prime}}^{-}{\cal T}_{j^{\prime}}^{+}\varphi_{\mu^{\prime}}({\bf r})}{\cal T}_{s}^{-}{\cal T}_{j}^{+}\varphi_{\mu}({\bf r})d{\bf r}.

(118)

On making the substitution
{\bf r}^{\prime}={\cal T}_{-s}^{-}{\cal T}_{-j}^{+}{\bf r}
we transform the coefficients to the form

S_{\mu^{\prime}\mu}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{s^{\prime},s=0}^{m-1}e^{i(s-s^{\prime})\kappa_{\nu}c_{-}}\sum_{j^{\prime},j=-L}^{L-1}e^{-i(j-j^{\prime})\kappa_{\nu}c_{+}}

\times\int\overline{\varphi_{\mu^{\prime}}({\cal T}_{s^{\prime}-s}^{-}{\cal T}_{j^{\prime}-j}^{+}{\bf r}^{\prime})}\varphi_{\mu}({\bf r}^{\prime})d{\bf r}^{\prime}.

(119)

We next replace the indices
s^{\prime}-s=s^{\prime\prime}
and
j^{\prime}-j=j^{\prime\prime}
and obtain

S_{\mu^{\prime}\mu}=\sum_{s=0}^{m-1}e^{-is\kappa_{\nu}c_{-}}\sum_{j=-L}^{L-1}e^{ij\kappa_{\nu}c_{+}}

\times\int\overline{\varphi_{\mu^{\prime}}({\cal T}_{s}^{-}{\cal T}_{j}^{+}{\bf r})}\varphi_{\mu}({\bf r})d{\bf r}.

(120)

In the same way we transform the coefficients
H_{\mu^{\prime}\mu}

H_{\mu^{\prime}\mu}=\sum_{s=0}^{m-1}e^{-is\kappa_{\nu}c_{-}}\sum_{j=-L}^{L-1}e^{ij\kappa_{\nu}c_{+}}

\times\int\overline{\varphi_{\mu^{\prime}}({\cal T}_{s}^{-}{\cal T}_{j}^{+}{\bf r})}{\cal H}\varphi_{\mu}({\bf r})d{\bf r}.

(121)

The entries of the Hamiltonian and overlap matrices associated with a zigzag nanotube (
m=0
) have the form

H_{\mu^{\prime}\mu}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\sum_{s=-L}^{L-1}e^{-is\kappa_{\nu}c_{-}}\int\overline{\varphi_{\mu^{\prime}}({\cal R}_{j}{\cal T}_{s}^{-}{\bf r})}{\cal H}\varphi_{\mu}({\bf r})d{\bf r},

S_{\mu^{\prime}\mu}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\sum_{s=-L}^{L-1}e^{-is\kappa_{\nu}c_{-}}\int\overline{\varphi_{\mu^{\prime}}({\cal R}_{j}{\cal T}_{s}^{-}{\bf r})}\varphi_{\mu}({\bf r})d{\bf r}.

(122)

First we restrict ourselves to the assumption that the electrons hop to the nearest atoms only.
Then the diagonal
AA
and
BB
entries of the matrices
H
and
S
for any nanotube,
0\leq m\leq n
, are

H_{\mu\mu}=\langle\varphi_{\mu}({\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{\mu}({\bf r})\rangle,\quad S_{\mu\mu}=\langle\varphi_{\mu}({\bf r})|\varphi_{\mu}({\bf r})\rangle.

The off-diagonal entries are sums of three integrals. If
1\leq m\leq n
, in the sums (
120
) and (
121
)
we keep the
(s,j)
-terms with
(s,j)
being
(0,0)
,
(0,1)
, and
(1,0)
for the entry
H_{AB}
and

(0,0)
,
(0,-1)
, and
(-1,0)
for the entry
H_{BA}
, while discard the others. We have

H_{AB}=\langle\varphi_{A}({\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{B}({\bf r})\rangle+e^{i\kappa_{\nu}c_{+}}\langle\varphi_{A}({\cal T}_{1}^{+}{\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{B}({\bf r})\rangle

+e^{-i\kappa_{\nu}c_{-}}\langle\varphi_{A}({\cal T}_{1}^{-}{\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{B}({\bf r})\rangle,

H_{BA}=\langle\varphi_{B}({\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{A}({\bf r})\rangle+e^{-i\kappa_{\nu}c_{+}}\langle\varphi_{B}({\cal T}_{-1}^{+}{\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{A}({\bf r})\rangle

+e^{i\kappa_{\nu}c_{-}}\langle\varphi_{B}({\cal T}_{-1}^{-}{\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{A}({\bf r})\rangle.

(123)

The counterparts of these formulas for a zigzag nanotube read

H_{AB}=\langle\varphi_{A}({\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{B}({\bf r})\rangle+\langle\varphi_{A}({\cal R}_{1}{\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{B}({\bf r})\rangle

+e^{-i\kappa_{\nu}c_{-}}\langle\varphi_{A}({\cal T}_{1}^{-}{\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{B}({\bf r})\rangle,

with
(j,s)
-indices in (
122
) taken as
(0,0)
,
(1,0)
, and
(0,1)
, and

H_{BA}=\langle\varphi_{B}({\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{A}({\bf r})\rangle+\langle\varphi_{B}({\cal R}_{-1}{\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{A}({\bf r})\rangle

+e^{i\kappa_{\nu}c_{-}}\langle\varphi_{B}({\cal T}_{-1}^{-}{\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{A}({\bf r})\rangle.

(124)

with the
(j,s)
-indices in (
122
) selected as
(0,0)
,
(-1,0)
, and
(0,-1)
, respectively.
Notice that
\kappa_{\nu}
for zigzag nanotubes is defined by (
101
), while for the other types of nanotubes
it is given by (
99
). The entries of the overlap matrix
S
are obtained from the corresponding
entries of the matrix
H
if the Hamiltonian is replaced by the identity operator.

In the second nearest-neighbor tight-binding approximation the off-diagonal entries are not changed, while the diagonal entries gain six
more terms which reflect hopping between atom

For the entry
H_{AA}
associated with a nanotube
{\cal A}(n,m)
,
1\leq m\leq n
, we have

H_{AA}=\langle\varphi_{A}({\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{A}({\bf r})\rangle+\langle\varphi_{A_{1}^{+}}({\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{A}({\bf r})\rangle

+\langle\varphi_{A_{1}^{-}}({\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{A}({\bf r})\rangle+\langle\varphi_{A_{2}^{+}}({\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{A}({\bf r})\rangle

+\langle\varphi_{A_{2}^{-}}({\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{A}({\bf r})\rangle+\langle\varphi_{A_{3}^{+}}({\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{A}({\bf r})\rangle

+\langle\varphi_{A_{3}^{-}}({\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{A}({\bf r})\rangle.

(125)

The
(s,j)
-pairs in (

) associated with the seven terms in the last relation are

(0,0)
,
(0,1)
,
(1,0)
,
(-1,0)
,
(0,-1)
,

, and
(1,-1)
, respectively. Therefore

H_{AA}=\langle\varphi_{A}({\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{A}({\bf r})\rangle+e^{i\kappa_{\nu}c_{+}}\langle\varphi_{A}({\cal T}_{1}^{+}{\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{A}({\bf r})\rangle

+e^{-i\kappa_{\nu}c_{-}}\langle\varphi_{A}({\cal T}_{1}^{-}{\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{A}({\bf r})\rangle

+e^{i\kappa_{\nu}c_{-}}\langle\varphi_{A}({\cal T}_{-1}^{-}{\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{A}({\bf r})\rangle

+e^{-i\kappa_{\nu}c_{+}}\langle\varphi_{A}({\cal T}_{-1}^{+}{\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{A}({\bf r})\rangle

+e^{i\kappa_{\nu}(c_{+}+c_{-})}\langle\varphi_{A}({\cal T}_{1}^{+}{\cal T}_{-1}^{-}{\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{A}({\bf r})\rangle

+e^{-i\kappa_{\nu}(c_{+}+c_{-})}\langle\varphi_{A}({\cal T}_{1}^{-}{\cal T}_{-1}^{+}{\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{A}({\bf r})\rangle.

Similarly, the second diagonal entry is derived in the form

+e^{-i\kappa_{\nu}c_{+}}\langle\varphi_{B}({\cal T}_{-1}^{+}{\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{B}({\bf r})\rangle

+e^{i\kappa_{\nu}c_{+}}\langle\varphi_{B}({\cal T}_{1}^{+}{\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{B}({\bf r})\rangle

+e^{-i\kappa_{\nu}c_{-}}\langle\varphi_{B}({\cal T}_{1}^{-}{\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{B}({\bf r})\rangle

+e^{i\kappa_{\nu}(c_{+}+c_{-})}\langle\varphi_{B}({\cal T}_{1}^{+}{\cal T}_{-1}^{-}{\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{B}({\bf r})\rangle

+e^{-i\kappa_{\nu}(c_{+}+c_{-})}\langle\varphi_{B}({\cal T}_{1}^{-}{\cal T}_{-1}^{+}{\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{B}({\bf r})\rangle.

(127)

and

+e^{-i\kappa_{\nu}c_{-}}\langle\varphi_{\mu}({\cal T}_{1}^{-}{\bf r})|{\cal H}|\varphi_{\mu}({\bf r})\rangle

VII
CONCLUSIONS

We have proposed an analogue of the Bloch theory for a SWCNT
(n,m)
,
0\leq m\leq n
, whose surface is
a cylindrical lattice with rotational-translational symmetry. The main difference between this theory and the conventional
zone-folding technique is that it does not entirely rely on the use of 2d characteristic vectors of the real and reciprocal
hexagonal lattices of graphene. The approach proposed employs cylindrical coordinates and 3d characteristic vectors of the nanotube.
It constructs a reciprocal tube that is characterized by two vectors in the 3d reciprocal space and possesses the chirality of its real counterpart.
For the description of both tubes, the real one and it reciprocal, two compositions of a rotation and translation transformations were introduced.
We have designed the Brillouin zone and described the domain of the wave-vector. The Brillouin zone is a nonregular hexagon rolled around the nanotube surface,
while the domain for the wave-vector is a rectangle rolled around the reciprocal tube and parallel to its axis.

We have shown that the classical Bloch formula for crystals with translational symmetry,

\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})=e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf r}}u({\bf r};{\bf k}),\quad{\bf r}\in{\cal U}_{\cal A},

is also valid for SWCNTs
(n,m)
, 2d cylindrical lattices with rotational-translational symmetry.
Here,
\psi({\bf r};{\bf k})
is a solution of the Schrödinger equation,
u({\bf r};{\bf k})
is a function invariant with respect to the composition of associated
rotation-translation transformations in the real and reciprocal spaces,

u({\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}{\bf r};\tilde{\cal T}_{j}^{\pm}{\bf k})=u({\bf r};{\bf k})
,
{\bf r}\in{\cal U}_{\cal A}
,
{\bf k}\in{\cal K}^{\pm}
. Here,
{\cal U}_{\cal A}
is a unit cell of the nanotube, a unit cell of graphene rolled around the nanotube surface, and

{\cal K}^{\pm}
are the domains of the corresponding rotation-translation transformations determined by the Brillouin zone. Based on these constructions and the tight-binding approximation scheme for an orbital orthogonal to the nanotube surface we derived the secular equation and the Hamiltonian and overlap matrices associated with the first and second nearest-neighbor approximations.

In the nearest future it is natural to proceed with computational tests and calculating the electronic structure of SWCNTs. It is of interest to develop a DFT method
based on the description of the nanotube structure proposed.

Statistical Box-Behnken driven response surface modeling for thermal performance enhancement of radiative tri hybrid nanofluid transport over a porous heated material
Authors: Nilanchala Sethy, S. R. Mishra, Subhajit Panda
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In various industrial production processes, the system needs significant cooling so that it can produce several products without maximum damage. The superior heat conduction performance exhibited by the tri-nanofluid leads to its more prominent performance relative to single and bi-hybrid nanofluids. In conveying the same approach, the present investigation provides a meaningful role in governing the flow patterns of tri-hybrid nanofluid comprised of alumina (Al2O3), zinc oxide (ZnO), and magnetite (Fe3O4) in the base liquid water is considered. The flow of conducting fluid for the involvement of the applied magnetization through a permeable medium is carried out with the mutual influence of thermal radiation and an additional heat source. Additionally, the main focus of the study is the utility of multiple slip conditions and their impact on the flow phenomena. The mathematical model integrating with the aforesaid assumptions leads to presenting a set of nonlinear coupled systems, and then a suitable similarity rule is employed in transforming the model into its dimensional form. Moreover, a traditional numerical approach, such as fourth-order Runge-Kutta embedding with shooting, is utilized in handling the system with the characterizing factors. Further, it is observed that the tri-hybrid nanofluid exhibits a stronger ability in enhancing the fluid velocity and the heat transport properties. Again, radiative heat transfer using thermal radiation augments the temperature distribution. One of the interesting statistical approaches, i.e. Response surface methodology associated with Box-Behnken design (BBD) with face-centred approach and validation is obtained for the use of analysis of variance (ANOVA), a hypothetical test considering 5% of the significance level. The 99% of R-squared value presents that the model designed for the heat transfer rate for the involved factors shows a significant model.Clinical trial number: Not applicable.

Introduction

A ternary nanofluid comprises three unique nanoparticle types dispersed within a carrier liquid, delivering enhanced thermal conductivity, durability, and heat-transfer efficiency relative to single and hybrid nanofluids. By integrating varied nanoparticle characteristics, it boosts energy conveyance and refines fluid dynamics across a wide range of thermal environments. Such fluids find application in high-performance cooling technologies, solar-energy absorbers, thermal exchangers, and electronic cooling systems. Their exceptional adaptability positions them as strong candidates for future industrial and energy-sector advancements. Through numerical simulation, Jamrus et al. [1] explored the interplay between fluid motion and thermal energy transport in a hydromagnetic mixed-convection configuration involving a thermally layered tri-nanofluid along velocity slip flow past a permeable sheet. The analysis provided by Rafique et al. [2] elucidates the stagnation-point regime features of tetra-hybrid nanofluids across an oblique elongating/diminishing cylindrical geometry, integrating quadratic radiative transport, hydromagnetic coupling, and convective thermal boundary interactions. The findings illuminate the combined action of tetra-nanofluids, radiative heat-transfer effects, and inclined-surface geometry, with implications for enhancing advanced cooling infrastructure and improving thermal-management efficiency in a wide range of industrial operations. The study by Matarneh et al. [3] elucidated the Hiemenz-flow behavior of a tri-component based nanofluid (H₂O/Al₂O₃ + Cu + TiO₂) along a sheet subject to linear expanding or diminishing, incorporating the contributions of heat-radiation mechanisms. According to Sabole et al. [4], the research delved into the performance of a hydromagnetic ternary hybrid nanofluid (Al2O3, TiO2, and Ag/water) flowing via an elongating surface, highlighting the impacts of magnetic fields, substrate porosity, heat generation phenomena, and radiative influences on the overall flow structure. Mahboobtosi et al. [5] performed computational analyses of ternary and penta-hybrid nanofluid flow within a permeable, stretchable rotating conduit, incorporating the dual impacts of hydromagnetic force, radiative heat flux, chemical reaction kinetics, and endogenous heat generation. Reddy et al. [6] examined the slip-mediated and Dufour-effect-driven perturbations in the transport of a non-uniform magnetohydrodynamic nanofluid above a porous, elongating substrate under the concurrent integration of radiative loading, viscous energy loss, and Joule heating. Their analysis encompassed determinations of skin-friction coefficients, thermal-transport behavior, and mass-transfer performance for several nanoparticle configurations. Results demonstrated that increases in magnetic influence, porous-medium modulation, temporal variability, suction magnitude, and slip velocity give rise to amplified velocity profiles. Hussain et al. [7] executed a systematic assessment on the application of a tri-nanofluid—comprising graphene oxide, silver, and copper—to probe the enhancement of heat and mass transport in a Maxwell-type magnetohydrodynamic fluid. The present inquiry assesses the dynamics of Maxwell-hydromagnetic tri-nanofluid circulation over a porous, elongating sheet. The energy and species transport equations are formulated to incorporate Brownian-motion effects and thermophoretic diffusion. Additionally, the model encompasses thermal radiation, bimolecular reaction, chemical species effects, and convective thermal surface interactions, thereby offering a comprehensive characterization of the system. Saleem et al. [8] explored the dynamics of a two-dimensional, laminar Casson ternary nanofluid flow under natural convection and electromagnetic hydrodynamic (EMHD) effects. The study deliberates the influence of Thomson and Troian slip conditions on fluid flow, considering factors such as thermal radiation, viscous dissipation, Joule heating, and heat sources/sinks. The analysis focuses on enhancing thermal conductivity and performance by utilizing metal nanoparticles in nanofluids, which exhibit surface stretching and slippage due to thermal conduction. In their study, Mahariq et al. [9] addressed the coupled implication of varying thermal efficiency on the thermal exchange performance of tri nanofluid in second-grade fluid movement between revolving, inclined materials. Additionally, the investigation incorporates the effects of rotating channels, nonlinear solar radiation, sink/source contributions, and deformable boundaries due to surface elongation and contraction. Nazneen et al. [10] deliberated a computational analysis of the attribute of a transient Ree-Eyring hybrid nanofluid flowing through a biaxially elongated or contracted material. Moreover, the effects of magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) phenomena are incorporated, while the Cattaneo-Christov heat flux framework, together with the contribution of thermal radiation, further amplifies the energy transfer. In this investigation, water was employed as the base medium, and copper and titanium nanoparticles were integrated to formulate the hybrid nanofluid. Eladeb et al. [11] scrutinized a numerical scrutiny on the hydromagnetic transport features of a hybrid nanofluid (HNF) flowing over a biaxially elongating and diminishing material. The HNF was engineered by dispersing SiO₂ and MoS₂ nanoparticles within ethylene glycol (EG). The analysis additionally considers the assessment of thermal radiation, surface porosity, volumetric heat generation or absorption, a uniform magnetic field, and convective boundary constraints. Panda et al. [12] reported the thermal performances of hybrid nanofluid motion over elastic wedges, focusing on the integration of a variable magnetic field and heat generation. The magnetic field affects flow velocity, while varying nanoparticle volume fractions lead to decreased flow rates and increased temperatures. Mahariq. et al. [13] reported the combined interpretation of ternary hybrid nanoparticles (titanium, SWCNT, and MWCNT) on the heat-transport and flow-field behavior exhibited by nanofluids. Statistical analysis (ANOVA) reveals that the volume fractions of these nanoparticles significantly impact skin friction and heat transfer (Nusselt number). Gasmi et al. [14] investigated how second-slip conditions affect fluid flow, temperature, and concentration in ternary hybrid nanofluids, with a focus on nanoparticle shape factors. It also examines entropy generation due to fluid friction and heat transfer, aiming to optimize ternary hybrid nanofluids for enhanced cooling performance. By analyzing key flow parameters, the research aims to enhance cooling system efficiency. Under the hydromagnetic attribute, Anwar et al. [15] the transport mechanism in a 2d flow involving a tri-component based nanofluid over a stretched material. In this configuration, a water-based fluid serves as the carrier, enriched with three different types of nanoparticles (graphene oxide, silver, and copper) to enhance the thermophysical properties. The investigation further encompasses critical factors such as Brownian motion, thermophoretic effects, Joule heating, viscous dissipation, and the kinetics of chemical species, providing a detailed insight into the complex interactions governing the system. Ali et al. [16] investigated the transient hydromagnetic slippery motion of a tri-nanofluid over a flexible, nonlinearly deforming disk immersed in a permeable substrate along magnetism. The motion of the unsteady, elastically stretching disk within the perforated matrix induces fluid circulation, enabling a detailed examination of the interplay between magnetic forces, slip boundary effects, and the synergistic behavior of hybrid nanoparticles on thermal transport. Srihari et al. [17] investigated the steady convective transport properties of a Sutterby nanofluid over a stretching surface, representing its non-Newtonian characteristics through the Sutterby fluid framework. The analysis utilized Buongiorno’s model to account for Brownian motion and thermophoretic drift, in addition to the influence of thermal radiation. Mohamad et al. [18] reported the performance of a water-based ternary hybrid nanofluid flowing through a porous substrate, accounting for the radiant heat, magnetic fields, and ambient contaminants, within the framework of non-Newtonian fluid. Ramya et al. [19] investigated the dynamics of fluids laden with motile microbes over a horizontally stretching or contracting material, emphasizing the roles of thermophoretic forces and Brownian diffusion. Whereas a higher microbial concentration enhances the Sherwood number, indicating improved mass transport, it adversely affects the concentration distribution. Khan et al. [20] investigated the boundary-layer flow of a nanofluid past a stretching sheet and demonstrated that nanoparticle dispersion significantly enhances heat transfer performance. Earlier, Gorla and Sidawi [21] analyzed free convection over a vertical stretching surface with suction and blowing, revealing the strong influence of mass transfer on boundary-layer behavior. More recently, Renu and Negi [22] examined mixed convection ferro Fe₃O₄/Cu hybrid nanofluid flow in a vertical flat microchannel and reported improved thermal efficiency due to hybrid nanoparticle interactions. In a related study, Renu and Kumar [23] explored the effect of wall conductivity on mixed convection magnetohydrodynamic nanofluid flow under a thermal non-equilibrium framework in a vertical channel, highlighting the importance of wall-fluid coupling and magnetic effects. Furthermore, Sahu and Bhowmick [24–26] conducted comprehensive numerical investigations on transient heat transfer in graded and spatially varying fins using the lattice Boltzmann method, demonstrating the significant role of material property variation and boundary condition changes on thermal response characteristics.

Concluding remarks

The study addresses under stretching configurated surface, the two-dimensional tri-hybrid nanofluid flow, emphasizing the influence of magnetization in a permeable environment. The behaviour of thermal radiation, along-side the insertion of heat source and the slip effect enriches the flow profiles. The mathematical model equipped with the parameters utilized along with the thermophysical models are presented mathematically and tackled numerically employing shooting based Runge-Kutta technique.

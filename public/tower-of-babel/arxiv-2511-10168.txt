A study of binaural deep beamforming with interpretable beampatterns guided by time-varying RTF
Ilai Zaidel, Sharon Gannot

Abstract
In this work, a deep beamforming framework for speech enhancement in dynamic acoustic environments is studied. The time-varying beamformer weights are estimated from the noisy multichannel signals by minimizing an SI-SDR loss. The estimation is guided by the continuously tracked relative transfer functions (RTFs) of the moving target speaker. The spatial behavior of the network is evaluated through both narrowband and wideband beampatterns under three settings: (i) oracle guidance using true RTFs, (ii) estimated RTFs obtained by a subspace tracking method, and (iii) without the RTF guidance. Results show that RTF-guided models produce smoother, spatially consistent beampatterns that accurately track the target’s direction of arrival. In contrast, the model fails to maintain a clear spatial focus when guidance is absent. Using the estimated RTFs as guidance closely matches the oracle RTF behavior, confirming the effectiveness of the tracking scheme. The model also outputs a binaural signal to preserve the speaker’s spatial cues, which promotes hearing aid and hearables applications.

Introduction

Speech enhancement algorithms aim to improve the perceptual quality and intelligibility of noisy speech signals in acoustic environments. In multichannel setups, spatial diversity can be exploited to reduce noise or to separate the target speaker.
In this work, we address only the noise reduction task, namely, enhancing a single desired speech signal contaminated by noise.
Classical beamforming approaches, such as the minimum variance distortionless response (MVDR) and the multichannel Wiener filter (MWF), and their variants
[
8
]
, have proven highly effective in static conditions. However, their performance relies on accurate estimation of the spatial statistics, specifically, the steering vector. Multiple studies have shown that adopting the RTF as the steering vector of the beamformer provides better speech quality than beamformers that only take the direct acoustic path into account
[
7
,
18
]
.
In dynamic scenarios where the speaker moves or the acoustic environment changes, the RTFs become time-varying and difficult to track reliably, leading to significant performance degradation.

Recently, DNN–based beamformers have achieved strong performance by jointly learning spatial and spectral representations from data
[
12
,
16
]
. By replacing analytically derived weights with trainable networks, they capture complex nonlinear mappings and adapt to diverse acoustic conditions. However, their spatial behavior is often non-interpretable, motivating methods that explicitly analyze the spatial properties of the multichannel algorithms and encourage their spatial selectivity
[
3
,
19
,
4
]
.
The central role of the RTF in classical beamforming motivates its integration into DNN-based beamformers. Whereas prior work has largely estimated the RTF to derive classical beamformer coefficients
[
8
,
17
,
2
]
or used it as a spatial filter within deep architectures
[
11
]
, our approach feeds the RTF directly as an input feature to the network.
Recent results in
[
5
]
for target speaker extraction (TSE) show that RTF-based spatial guidance outperforms DOA-based guidance. This likely stems from the RTF’s ability to capture complex acoustic propagation in reverberant environments more faithfully
[
18
]
.
Dynamic scenarios are markedly more challenging than static ones, especially when spatial interpretability must be preserved. A spatially selective deep filter operating under weak spatial guidance—derived solely from the target speaker’s initial position—was proposed in
[
10
]
.

In this contribution, we propose guiding the model using time-varying RTF estimates of the dynamic speaker, tracked with the projection approximation subspace tracking (PAST) algorithm
[
20
]
. This adaptive formulation enables the beamformer to follow a moving speaker under time-varying conditions. The PAST algorithm has been successfully applied to speech enhancement for a single desired source
[
1
]
and extended to multichannel processing for continuous tracking of signal and interference subspaces in dynamic acoustic environments
[
13
]
.

Our approach builds on the explainable DNN-based beamformer of
[
4
]
and extends it by directly incorporating a time-varying RTF estimate. This enables the beampattern to adapt over time, thereby tracking a moving speaker in real time. We show that RTF guidance yields more accurate spatial properties than the same architecture without RTF input. Finally, the model produces binaural output that preserves spatial cues essential for perceptual localization
[
6
]
, further demonstrating the architecture’s spatial interpretability.

Problem Formulation

In the short-time Fourier transform (STFT) domain, the multichannel noisy speech mixture is modeled as

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}(l,k)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{h}(l,k)s(l,k)+\mathbf{n}(l,k)

(1)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{x}(l,k)+\mathbf{n}(l,k)

where
l
and
k
are the time-frame and frequency-bin indexes, respectively,
the signal
s(l,k)
is the desired speaker signal, and the vector
\mathbf{h}(l,k)
comprises the acoustic transfer functions (ATFs) between the speaker and the microphone array.
In this work, we address a dynamic setting in which the ATFs are time-varying.

The objective of the proposed method is to estimate the clean speech signal
while preserving the spatial characteristics of the target speaker. This is
achieved by applying two parallel spatial filters:

\displaystyle\hat{s}_{\text{L}}(l,k)=\mathbf{w}^{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{L}}(l,k)\,\mathbf{y}(l,k),

(2a)

\displaystyle\hat{s}_{\text{R}}(l,k)=\mathbf{w}^{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{R}}(l,k)\,\mathbf{y}(l,k),

(2b)

where
\hat{s}_{\text{L}}(l,k)
and
\hat{s}_{\text{R}}(l,k)
denote the left and right channels of the binaural output, respectively, and
\mathbf{w}^{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{L}}(l,k)
and
\mathbf{w}^{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{R}}(l,k)
represent the time-varying complex-valued beamformer weights that should be inferred by the DNN.

3.2
Time-varying RTF estimation

To estimate the time-varying RTF of the speaker, we employed a recursive estimation based on the projection approximation subspace tracking (PAST) algorithm
[
20
]
and the covariance-whitening method for RTF estimation
[
14
]
.

3.2.1
Covariance-Whitening

The covariance-whitening (CW) approach first estimates the noise covariance matrix from
L_{n}
noise-only frames:

\hat{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{nn}}(k)=\tfrac{1}{L_{n}}\sum_{l=0}^{L_{n}-1}\mathbf{y}(l,k)\mathbf{y}^{\mathrm{H}}(l,k).

(3)

The speech+noise covariance matrix is then calculated as

\hat{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{yy}}(k)=\tfrac{1}{L-L_{n}}\sum_{l=L_{n}}^{L-1}\mathbf{y}(l,k)\mathbf{y}^{\mathrm{H}}(l,k).

(4)

The whitened measurements are obtained via

\mathbf{y}_{w}(l,k)=\hat{\mathbf{\Phi}}^{-1/2}_{\mathbf{nn}}(k)\,\mathbf{y}(l,k),

(5)

with
\hat{\mathbf{\Phi}}^{-1/2}_{\mathbf{nn}}(k)
computed from the eigenvalue decomposition (EVD) of
\hat{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{nn}}(k)
.
The correlation matrix of the whitened signals is given by:

\hat{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{y_{w}y_{w}}}(k)=\hat{\mathbf{\Phi}}^{-1/2}_{\mathbf{nn}}(k)\,\hat{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{yy}}(k)\,\hat{\mathbf{\Phi}}^{-1/2\mathrm{H}}_{\mathbf{nn}}(k).

(6)

Finally, the CW estimate of the RTF is obtained from the principal eigenvector of
\hat{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{y_{w}y_{w}}}(k)
,
\hat{\bm{\psi}}
:

\hat{\mathbf{a}}_{\,\textrm{CW}}\triangleq\frac{\hat{\mathbf{\Phi}}^{\mathrm{H}/2}_{\mathbf{nn}}(k)\hat{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{e}^{T}_{i_{\mathrm{ref}}}\hat{\mathbf{\Phi}}^{\mathrm{H}/2}_{\mathbf{nn}}(k)\hat{\bm{\psi}}},

(7)

where
\mathbf{e}_{i_{\mathrm{ref}}}
is the selection vector for the chosen reference microphone, either the left-most or right-most microphones.
The application of the EVD requires
\mathcal{O}(M^{3})
operations per frequency, in addition to the computational cost of the whitening procedure.

3.2.2
Projection Approximation Subspace Tracking

Because the (whitened) signal is non-stationary across time frames, a tracking algorithm is required to estimate the time-varying principal eigenvector
\hat{\bm{\psi}}(l,k)
. The PAST algorithm
[
20
]
offers an efficient recursive estimator of the dominant eigenvector of a time-varying correlation matrix: unlike batch EVD, it updates the subspace incrementally as new data arrive, making it well suited to real-time, causal speech enhancement and beamforming in dynamically evolving acoustic scenes. The application of the PAST algorithm only requires
\mathcal{O}(M)
operations per frequency (in addition to the whitening procedure). A forgetting factor
\beta\in(0,1)
is used to gradually discards older observations, allowing the algorithm to adapt to changes in the signal’s statistics.
The PAST procedure is summarized in Algorithm
1
.

4.2
Loss Function

We train the network using the scale-invariant signal-to-distortion ratio (SI-SDR) loss, defined as:

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SI\text{-}SDR}}(\hat{s},s_{\mathrm{ref}})=-10\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{\left\|\frac{\hat{s}^{\mathrm{T}}s_{\mathrm{ref}}}{\|s_{\mathrm{ref}}\|^{2}}s_{\mathrm{ref}}\right\|^{2}}{\left\|\hat{s}-\frac{\hat{s}^{\mathrm{T}}s_{\mathrm{ref}}}{\|s_{\mathrm{ref}}\|^{2}}s_{\mathrm{ref}}\right\|^{2}}\right),

(8)

where
s_{\mathrm{ref}}
denotes the clean reference signal observed at the reference microphone, and

\hat{s}=\mathbf{w}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{y}

is the estimated output after beamforming.
For the binaural setup, the total loss is computed as the sum of the left and right channel losses:

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SI\text{-}SDR}}(\hat{s}_{\rm L},s_{\mathrm{ref,\,L}})+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SI\text{-}SDR}}(\hat{s}_{\rm R},s_{\mathrm{ref,\,R}}).

(9)

4.3.2
Beampattern Analysis

To further examine spatial selectivity, we turn to beampattern analysis of the proposed model, investigating both its narrowband and wideband characteristics.

The time-varying narrowband beampattern is defined as

B(k,\theta,\ell)=\mathbf{w}^{H}(k,\ell)\,\mathbf{h}(k,\theta),

(10)

where
\mathbf{w}(k,\ell)
are the estimated beamformer weights and
\mathbf{h}(k,\theta)
is the steering vector for direction
\theta
.
The corresponding wideband beampower is obtained by a summation over all frequency bins:

P(\theta,\ell)=\sum_{k}\left|B(k,\theta,\ell)\right|^{2}.

(11)

As shown in Figs.

In contrast, the model without RTF guidance exhibits reduced directionality and less consistent adaptation to the dynamic scene.

5
Conclusions

In this paper, we presented a deep, time-varying beamformer architecture for tracking and enhancing a dynamic speaker from noisy multichannel recordings. The model is guided at inference by time-varying RTF estimates obtained via subspace tracking. While the SI-SDR of the RTF-guided system is on par with the unguided counterpart, it exhibits superior spatial behavior, specifically, smoother beampattern adaptation, improved consistency, and higher directivity. Future work will refine RTF estimation and the underlying tracking mechanism toward oracle performance, evaluate the model in more challenging acoustic environments, and enable causal, real-time adaptive beamforming.

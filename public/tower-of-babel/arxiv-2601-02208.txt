Long time dynamics of the Nernst-Planck-Darcy System on ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} Thanks: MSC Classification: 35K57, 35B40, 35Q92
Elie Abdo, Joe Germany, Mohammad Khalil Hamdan, Kifah Kontar

Abstract
. We study ionic electrodiffusion modeled by the Nernst–Planck equations describing the evolution of N N ionic species in a three-dimensional incompressible fluid flowing through a porous medium. We address the long-time dynamics of the resulting system in the three-dimensional whole space ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} . We prove that the k k -th spatial derivatives of each ionic concentration decays to zero in L 2 L^{2} with a sharp rate of order t − 3 4 − k 2 t^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{k}{2}} . Moreover, we investigate the behavior of the relative entropy associated with the model and show that it blows up in time with a sharp growth rate of order log ⁡ t \log t .



1.
Introduction

We study an electrodiffusion model that describes the coupled dynamics between ionic transport, fluid motion, and self-consistent electrostatic interactions. Positively and negatively charged ions migrate under the combined effects of concentration gradients, advection by a moving fluid, and the electric field generated by the ionic charge distribution. Such coupled mechanisms lie at the heart of a broad range of phenomena in physics, chemistry, and biology, including electrokinetic flows, ion channels, and charged porous media. In particular, they have been the subject of extensive investigation in neuroscience

,

,

,

,

]
, semiconductor theory
[

, water purification, desalination, and ion separation
[

,

]
and selective ion membranes
[

]
.

Such electrodiffusion models are described by the Nernst–Planck equations governing the evolution of a system of
N
ionic species with concentrations
c_{1},\ldots,c_{N}
. Each species evolves according to

(1)

(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla)c_{i}=D_{i}\,\mathrm{div}\big(\nabla c_{i}+z_{i}c_{i}\nabla\phi\big),\qquad i=1,\ldots,N.

Here
c_{i}(x,t)
denotes the concentration of the
i
-th ionic species at position
x
and time
t
. The positive constants
D_{i}
represent the diffusivities, while
z_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
are the ionic valences. The transport is driven not only by diffusion and advection through the velocity field
u
, but also by electrostatic drift induced by the electric potential
\phi
.

The potential
\phi
is determined self-consistently through the Poisson equation

(2)

-\epsilon\Delta\phi=\rho:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}z_{i}c_{i},

where
\rho
is the total ionic charge density. The potential
\phi
is the nondimensionalized form

(3)

\phi=\frac{e}{K_{\beta}T_{k}}\,\Psi

of the physical electric potential
\Psi
. Here
e
denotes the elementary charge,
K_{\beta}
is Boltzmann’s constant, and
T_{k}
is the absolute temperature. The dielectric parameter

(4)

\epsilon=\frac{\mathcal{E}K_{\beta}T_{k}}{e^{2}}=c_{0}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}z_{i}^{2}\right)\lambda_{D}^{2}

depends on the dielectric permittivity
\mathcal{E}>0
of the solvent, a reference bulk concentration
c_{0}>0
, and the Debye screening length

(5)

\lambda_{D}=\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{E}K_{\beta}T_{k}}{c_{0}e^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}z_{i}^{2}}}.

A comprehensive discussion of the underlying physical principles of ionic electrodiffusion can be found in
[
32
]
.

The ionic dynamics are coupled to the motion of the surrounding fluid. If the latter is viscous, its velocity field
u
satisfies the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations

(6)

\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u-\nu\Delta u+\nabla p=-K_{\beta}T_{k}\rho\nabla\phi,

together with the incompressibility constraint

(7)

\nabla\cdot u=0.

Here
p
denotes the pressure and
\nu>0
is the kinematic viscosity. The Lorentz-type forcing term

-K_{\beta}T_{k}\rho\nabla\phi
encodes the feedback of the electrostatic field on the fluid motion, giving rise to a fully coupled Nernst–Planck–Navier–Stokes (NPNS) system.

The Nernst–Planck–Navier–Stokes (NPNS) system has been extensively studied in the mathematical literature as a prototypical model for the nonlinear coupling between ionic electrodiffusion and incompressible fluid flow. Early works established the existence of global weak solutions and local strong solutions, emphasizing the interplay between transport, diffusion, and electrostatic forcing
[
11
,
20
,
34
]
. Subsequent studies investigated regularity, uniqueness, and long-time behavior of solutions, often relying on entropy methods and energy dissipation structures inherent to the system
[
7
,
33
]
. The analysis was further refined to address multi-species settings, various boundary conditions, and the role of charge neutrality constraints
[
8
,
24
]
. More recent contributions focus on asymptotic stability near equilibrium, decay rates, and enhanced regularity properties, including analyticity and smoothing effects
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
9
]
.

In this work, we focus on electrodiffusion in porous media, where the fluid motion is no longer governed by the Navier–Stokes equations but is instead described by Darcy’s law. In this regime, the velocity field
u
is given by

(8)

u=-\kappa\big(\nabla p+K_{\beta}T_{k}\rho\nabla\phi\big),

where
\kappa>0
denotes the permeability of the porous medium and
p
is the pressure. The resulting system is referred to as the Nernst–Planck–Darcy (NPD) system. Compared to the NPNS system, the NPD model presents additional analytical challenges due to the more singular dependence of the velocity
u
on the electrostatic forces. This singular coupling amplifies both the nonlinearity and the nonlocal character of the Nernst–Planck equations, significantly complicating the mathematical analysis.

Early mathematical studies of Nernst–Planck–Darcy type systems focused on drift–diffusion models coupled with porous-media flow, establishing global weak solutions and basic energy dissipation mechanisms
[
15
,
16
,
17
]
. In the periodic setting, the case of two ionic species on the torus was addressed in
[
18
]
, where global well-posedness and smoothness were shown to hold without smallness assumptions, relying on the special algebraic structure of the electrostatic coupling. More recently, this theory has been extended in
[
4
]
to systems with an arbitrary number of ionic species, all with equal diffusivities, on a three-dimensional torus under periodic boundary conditions. In this multi-species periodic framework, global existence, uniqueness, and asymptotic convergence to equilibrium were established, together with decay estimates.

In this paper, we address the long-time dynamics of the NPD system for
N
ionic species with equal diffusivities
D_{1}=\dots=D_{N}=D
and equal absolute valences
|z_{1}|=\dots=|z_{N}|=z
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with decay at infinity. As the physical constants
\epsilon,\kappa,K_{\beta},
and
T_{k}
do not have any contribution to the analysis of the problem, we assume that
\epsilon=\kappa=K_{\beta}T_{k}=1
for simplicity, and we obtain the following system of partial differential equations

(9)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}c_{i}+u\cdot\nabla c_{i}-D_{i}\Delta c_{i}-z_{i}D_{i}\nabla\cdot(c_{i}\nabla\phi)=0

(10)

\displaystyle u+\nabla p=-\rho\nabla\phi

(11)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot u=0

(12)

\displaystyle-\Delta\phi=\rho.

We start by proving that the
L^{2}
norm of the
k
-th order derivative of each ionic concentration decays in time with a sharp rate of decay of order

t^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{k}{2}}
. The main difficulties arise from the nonlinear
and nonlocal structure of the model, generated by the advection and
electromigration terms. We show that these nonlinearities do not influence
the asymptotic decay rate of the solution; in particular, each concentration
decays at the same rate as the solution of the linear homogeneous
three-dimensional heat equation. The proof relies on the Fourier splitting
method, a bootstrapping argument, and nonlinear and nonlocal
analysis.

In addition, we investigate the entropy structure associated with the model.
As diffusion spreads the ions over increasingly large spatial regions, their
concentrations become very small, which leads to a growth of entropy. We
prove that the relative entropy of each ionic species,

\mathcal{E}_{i}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}c_{i}\log c_{i}\,dx,

blows up as
t\to\infty
with a sharp rate of growth of order
\log t
. The
argument is novel and highly nontrivial. To obtain upper bounds, we study the
evolution of the quantities
{N}_{i}=e^{-c\mathcal{E}_{i}}
(for an appropriately chosen
c
) and prove Sobolev logarithmic inequalities on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
to deduce that

{N}_{i}
is bounded linearly in time from below. The latter yields logarithmic-in-time lower bounds for
-\mathcal{E}_{i}
. On the other hand, we show that the total relative entropy

\mathcal{E}(t)=\sum_{i}\mathcal{E}_{i}(t)
satisfies

-\mathcal{E}(t)\leq C\log\!\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+|x|)^{6}c_{i}^{2}\,dx\right),

which motivates the study of polynomial moments of the solution. In this
direction, we prove that the asymptotic growth in time of
\||x|^{3}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
is of order
t^{\frac{5}{2}}
and obtain logarithmic-in-time upper bounds for
-\mathcal{E}
.

Finally, we prove that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\left|\int_{\{|x|\leq A\}}c_{i}\log c_{i}\,dx\right|=0,

for any
A>0
and any
i\in\left\{1,\dots,N\right\}
. This shows that the contribution of the relative entropy from any fixed bounded
region vanishes as time tends to infinity. Although the total entropy grows
logarithmically in time, this growth does not originate from the core of the
domain. Instead, the ionic concentrations become locally negligible in an
entropic sense, ruling out any singular behavior on compact sets. Consequently, the entropy increase is entirely driven by the spreading of mass to large spatial scales.

\mathbb{R}^{3}

2.
Decay in Sobolev Spaces

In this section, we address the long-time behavior of solutions to the NPD system in all Sobolev spaces. As the potential
\phi
obeys a Poisson equation, the following elliptic estimates will be used frequently in the manuscript:

Proposition 1

(Elliptic Estimates)
.

Let
g
be in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}g=0
. Let
\Psi
be a solution to the Poisson equation
-\Delta\Psi=g
. Then the following estimates hold:

(13)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla\Psi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{1}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{3}},

(14)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla\Psi\right\rVert_{L^{6}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{2}},

(15)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla\Psi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla\Psi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{4}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{4}}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{4}},

(16)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla\Psi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\nabla g\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

For (
13
), using the boundedness of the Riesz transform on
L^{2}
, the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality,
followed by the log-convexity of

norms, we have

\left\lVert\nabla\Psi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}=\left\lVert\nabla\Lambda^{-2}g\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left\lVert\Lambda^{-1}g\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}\lesssim\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{1}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{3}}.

For (
14
), using the Sobolev embedding of
H^{1}
in
L^{6}
and the boundedness of the Riesz transform on
L^{2}
yield

(18)

\left\lVert\nabla\Psi\right\rVert_{L^{6}}\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla\nabla\Psi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}=\left\lVert\nabla\nabla\Lambda^{-2}g\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

For (
15
), by the continuous embedding of
W^{1,\,4}
in
L^{\infty}
, the three-dimensional Ladyzhenskaya interpolation inequality, and the boundedness of the Riesz transform on
L^{4}

(19)

\left\lVert\nabla\Psi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla\Psi\right\rVert_{W^{1,\,4}}\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla\Psi\right\rVert_{L^{4}}+\left\lVert\nabla\nabla\Psi\right\rVert_{L^{4}}\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla\Psi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{4}}\left\lVert\Delta\Psi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{4}}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{4}}.

For (
16
), by the continuous embedding of
W^{1,\,6}
in
L^{\infty}
, the estimate (
14
), the Sobolev embedding theorem, and the boundedness of the Riesz transform on
L^{6}

(20)

\left\lVert\nabla\Psi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla\Psi\right\rVert_{W^{1,\,6}}\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla\Psi\right\rVert_{L^{6}}+\left\lVert\nabla\nabla\Psi\right\rVert_{L^{6}}\lesssim\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{6}}\lesssim\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\nabla g\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

The NPD system has a unique local smooth solution for
H^{1}
initial data, a fact that follows from a standard mollification argument and passage to the limit using the Aubin-Lions Lemma and the lower semi-continuity of the norms. Moreover, the nonnegativity of the ionic concentrations is preserved on any time interval where the existence and uniqueness of the solution are guaranteed (see
[
7
]
). In the following proposition, we derive a priori uniform global bounds that allow global-in-time extension and describe the behavior of the ionic concentration when time blows up.

Proposition 2
.

Suppose
c_{i}(0)\geq 0
,
c_{i}(0)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for all

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{0}=0

Then, there exist positive constants
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
depending on the parameters of the problem and on the
L^{1}
and
H^{1}
norms of the initial concentrations
c_{i}(0)

(21)

\displaystyle\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{M_{1}}{(t+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}},\quad\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{M_{2}}{(t+1)^{\frac{5}{2}}},

for all
t\geq 0
and
i\in\left\{1,\dots,N\right\}

Proof.

The proof is divided into several steps. We define
\sigma=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}
to be the sum of the
N

Step 1. Compatibility Conditions.

Integrating each of the ionic concentrations over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and using the divergence-free condition satisfied by
u
, we obtain the conservation law

(22)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}c_{i}(t)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}c_{i}(0)\,dx,

for each
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
. Consequently, it follows that

(23)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho(t)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{0}\,dx=0

after making use of the assumption
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{0}\,dx=0
imposed on the initial charge density.
This guarantees compatibility conditions of the Poisson equation.

Step 2. Uniform Bounds.
Multiplying the equation obeyed by the ionic concentration
c_{i}
by
z_{i}
and summing over all indices
i\in\left\{1,\dots,N\right\}
, we infer that the charge density
\rho

\partial_{t}\rho+u\cdot\nabla\rho-D\Delta\rho=Dz^{2}\,\nabla\cdot(\sigma\nabla\phi).

On one hand, multiplying the latter by
\Lambda^{-2}\rho
and integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{3}

(25)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\dfrac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\Lambda^{-1}\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+D\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u\cdot\nabla\rho)\,\Lambda^{-2}\rho-Dz^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sigma\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{-2}\rho

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u\cdot\nabla\rho)\Lambda^{-2}\rho-Dz^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sigma\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\phi

On the other hand, taking the
L^{2}

10

\mathbb{R}^{3}

(26)

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho\nabla\phi\cdot u.

Combining (

(28)

is described by

(42)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+z^{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+D\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+Dz^{2}\left\lVert\nabla\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=-Dz^{2}\left\lVert\rho\sqrt{\sigma}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

In view of the non-negativity of the term
D\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}
, it follows that

(43)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+z^{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+Dz^{2}\left\lVert\nabla\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq-Dz^{2}\left\lVert\rho\sqrt{\sigma}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 0.

By Parseval’s identity, we have

(44)

\left\lVert\nabla\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla\sigma}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\widehat{\nabla\sigma})^{2}\,d\xi=4\pi^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\xi|^{2}|\widehat{\sigma}|^{2}\,d\xi.

Let
r:[0,\,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\,\infty)
be a function to be determined later.
We bound the dissipation term from below by

(45)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\xi|^{2}|\widehat{\sigma}|^{2}\,d\xi\geq C\int_{|\xi|>r(t)}|\xi|^{2}|\widehat{\sigma}|^{2}\,d\xi,

Thus, we obtain

(46)

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|>r(t)}|\xi|^{2}|\widehat{\sigma}|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\geq r^{2}(t)\int_{|\xi|>r(t)}|\widehat{\sigma}|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=r^{2}(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\widehat{\sigma}|^{2}\,d\xi-r^{2}(t)\int_{|\xi|\leq r(t)}|\widehat{\sigma}|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle=r^{2}(t)\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}-r^{2}(t)\int_{|\xi|\leq r(t)}|\widehat{\sigma}|^{2}\,d\xi.

where we used Parseval’s identity once more. Thus, we obtain the differential inequality

(47)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+z^{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+CDz^{2}r^{2}(t)\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq CDz^{2}r^{2}(t)\int_{|\xi|\leq r(t)}|\widehat{\sigma}|^{2}\,d\xi.

By making use of the triangle inequality, we can bound

(48)

\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\rho|^{2}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{N}z_{i}c_{i}\right|^{2}\,dx\lesssim z^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|c_{i}|^{2}\,dx\lesssim z^{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

We split up the dissipation term in (
47
) to obtain

(49)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+z^{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+C\frac{D}{2}r^{2}(t)\left(z^{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+z^{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\leq CDz^{2}r^{2}(t)\int_{|\xi|\leq r(t)}|\widehat{\sigma}|^{2}\,d\xi,

which, by estimate (
48
), simplifies to

(50)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+z^{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+c_{0}Dr^{2}(t)\left(\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+z^{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\leq CDz^{2}r^{2}(t)\int_{|\xi|\leq r(t)}|\widehat{\sigma}|^{2}\,d\xi.

Here
c_{0}
is a positive constant depending only on
N
. By the pointwise bound (
41
), Young’s inequality, and Fubini’s theorem for spherical coordinates, we have

(51)

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq r(t)}|\widehat{\sigma}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{|\xi|\leq r(t)}\left(\left\lVert\sigma_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}^{2}+|\xi|^{2}t\right)\,d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{r(t)}\int_{|\xi|=R}\left(\left\lVert\sigma_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}^{2}+|\xi|^{2}t\right)\,da(\xi)\,dR

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{r(t)}\left(\left\lVert\sigma_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}^{2}R^{2}+tR^{4}\right)\,dR

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\sigma_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}^{2}\,r^{3}(t)+t\,r^{5}(t).

Substituting this inequality into (
50
) and choosing
r(t)=\sqrt{\frac{m}{c_{0}D(t+1)}}
(where
m
is a constant to be determined later) yields

(52)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+z^{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\frac{m}{t+1}\left(\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+z^{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\sigma_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}^{2}\,r^{5}(t)+t\,r^{7}(t)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\sigma_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}^{2}\left(\frac{m}{t+1}\right)^{\frac{5}{2}}+t\left(\frac{m}{t+1}\right)^{\frac{7}{2}}.

Multiplying the differential inequality by the integrating factor

(53)

e^{\int_{0}^{t}c_{0}Dr^{2}(s)\,ds}=e^{\int_{0}^{t}\frac{m}{s+1}\,ds}=e^{m\ln(t+1)}=(t+1)^{m},

and integrating in time from
0
to
t
, we get

(54)

\displaystyle\left(\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+z^{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)(t+1)^{m}\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\lVert\rho_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+z^{2}\left\lVert\sigma_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert\sigma_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}^{2}\,m^{\frac{5}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}(s+1)^{m-\frac{5}{2}}\,ds+m^{\frac{7}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}s(s+1)^{m-\frac{7}{2}}\,ds.

Evaluating the integrals on the right-hand side and and dividing by
(t+1)^{m}
, we obtain

(55)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+z^{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\left\lVert\rho_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+z^{2}\left\lVert\sigma_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}{(t+1)^{m}}+\frac{\left\lVert\sigma_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}^{2}\,m^{\frac{5}{2}}+m^{\frac{7}{2}}}{m-\frac{3}{2}}\frac{1}{(t+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

Choosing
m=2
, we obtain the following bound

(56)

\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+z^{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{(t+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

Finally, since
c_{i}\leq\sigma
, we get the desired decay of the ionic concentration as

(57)

\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{(t+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}},

proving the desired bound on
\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}
. We now move to estimate the decay of
\nabla c_{i}
in
L^{2}
.

Step 5.
L^{p}
Decay of
\sigma
.

We multiply the PDE (
35
) by
\sigma^{p-1}
to obtain the
L^{p}
evolution of
\sigma
as

(58)

\frac{1}{p}\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{p}}^{p}+D(p-1)\left\lVert\sigma^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\,\nabla\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=D\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla\phi)\,\sigma^{p-1}\,dx.

Substep 5.1.
L^{3}
Decay of
\sigma
.

Specifically, the
L^{3}
evolution of
\sigma
is described by

(59)

\frac{1}{3}\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{3}+2D\left\lVert\sqrt{\sigma}\,\nabla\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=D\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla\phi)\,\sigma^{2}\,dx.

Integrating by parts, then using the Holder and Young inequalities, we obtain

(60)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{3}+2D\left\lVert\sqrt{\sigma}\,\nabla\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=-2D\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho\nabla\phi\cdot\sigma\nabla\sigma\,dx\leq D\left\lVert\sqrt{\sigma}\,\nabla\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left\lVert\rho\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}\left\lVert\sqrt{\sigma}\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}.

Simplifying (
60
) and using that
\left\lVert\sqrt{\sigma}\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}=\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{3}}
yield

(61)

\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{3}+3D\left\lVert\sqrt{\sigma}\,\nabla\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\left\lVert\rho\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{3}}.

Now, if
\left\lVert\sigma(\tau)\right\rVert_{L^{3}}=0
for some
\tau\geq 0
, then
\sigma(\tau)=0
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and thus
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sigma(\tau)\,dx=0
. By the conservation of the spatial mean of the ionic concentrations in time, we obtain

(62)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sigma(\tau)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sigma(t)\,dx=0,

for all
t\geq 0
.
Thus,
\sigma(t)=0
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
for all
t\geq 0
.

Otherwise, in the case when
\left\lVert\sigma(t)\right\rVert_{L^{3}}\not=0
for all
t\geq 0
, we divide the differential inequality (
61
) describing the
L^{3}
evolution of
\sigma
by
\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{3}}
to obtain

(63)

\frac{1}{\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{3}}}\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{3}+3D\frac{\left\lVert\sqrt{\sigma}\,\nabla\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}{\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{3}}}\leq C\left\lVert\rho\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2},

yielding

(64)

\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}\lesssim\left\lVert\rho\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}.

Using Hölder’s inequality, the Sobolev inequality, the estimate (
14
), and the decaying bound of
c_{i}
from (
57
), we obtain

(65)

\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}\lesssim\left\lVert\rho\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}\lesssim\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\frac{\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}{(t+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

Now, we differentiate
(t+1)\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}
in time and use the estimate (
65
) to get

(66)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left((t+1)\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}\right)=(t+1)\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}+\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}\lesssim\frac{\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}{(t+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}.

Applying the Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequality, Young’s inequality, and the decaying bound for
c_{i}
in (
57
) gives

(67)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left((t+1)\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{(t+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\left\lVert\nabla\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Integrating in time from
0
to
t
and employing the bound from (
34
), we obtain

(68)

\displaystyle(t+1)\,\left\lVert\sigma(t)\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\sigma_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\nabla\rho(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\nabla\sigma(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{(s+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\sigma_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}+\left\lVert\sigma_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\rho_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+1\lesssim\left\lVert\sigma_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla\sigma_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\sigma_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\rho_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim 1.

Dividing by
(t+1)
, we establish the following decaying bound for
\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{3}}

(69)

\left\lVert\sigma(t)\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{t+1}.

Substep 5.2.
L^{4}
Decay of
\sigma
.

The
L^{4}
evolution of
\sigma
obeys the following differential equation

(70)

\frac{1}{4}\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{4}+3D\left\lVert\sigma\,\nabla\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=D\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla\phi)\,\sigma^{3}\,dx.

Integrating by parts, then using the Hölder and Young inequalities, we obtain

(71)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{4}+12D\left\lVert\sigma\,\nabla\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=-12D\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho\nabla\phi\cdot\sigma^{2}\nabla\sigma\,dx\leq 6D\left\lVert\sigma\,\nabla\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left\lVert\rho\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}.

Simplifying (
71
) gives rise to

(72)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{4}\lesssim\left\lVert\rho\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}.

Following the same ideas of Substep 5.1, if
\left\lVert\sigma(\tau)\right\rVert_{L^{4}}=0
for some
\tau\geq 0
, then
\sigma(t)=0
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and so there is nothing to prove here.

Otherwise, we divide (
72
) by
\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}
to reach the following differential inequality

(73)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}\lesssim\left\lVert\rho\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}.

Using Hölder inequality, Young’s inequality, the estimate (
15
), the Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequality, and the
L^{2}
and
L^{3}
decay of
c_{i}
from (
57
) and (
69
) respectively, we infer that

(74)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\lesssim\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{4}+\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{4}\lesssim\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{4}+\left(\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{4}}\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{4}}+\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{4}}\right)^{4}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{4}+\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{3}\lesssim\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}{t+1}+\frac{\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}}{(t+1)^{\frac{9}{4}}}.

Now, after differentiating
(t+1)\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}
and using the bound (
74
), we reach

(75)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left((t+1)\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=(t+1)\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}+\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}}{(t+1)^{\frac{5}{4}}}+\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}.

We note that, by the elliptic estimate (
13
) and the bound derived in (
29
), we have

(76)

\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left\lVert\rho_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\left\lVert\rho_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{3}}\lesssim 1.

We then simplify (
75
) and make use of Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequality, Young’s inequality, and the
L^{2}
decay of
c_{i}
from (
57
) to deduce that

(77)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left((t+1)\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{(t+1)^{\frac{5}{4}}}+\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{(t+1)^{\frac{5}{4}}}+\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{(t+1)^{\frac{5}{4}}}+\frac{1}{(t+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\left\lVert\nabla\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Integrating in time from
0
to
t
, interpolating, and using the bound (
34
) yield

(78)

\displaystyle(t+1)\left\lVert\sigma(t)\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\sigma_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\nabla\rho(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\nabla\sigma(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{(t+1)^{\frac{5}{4}}}\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{(t+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\sigma_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\sigma_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}+\left\lVert\rho_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\sigma_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+1.

Thus, dividing through by
(t+1)
, we obtain the desired decay on the
L^{4}
norm of
\sigma

(79)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sigma(t)\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\left\lVert\sigma_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\sigma_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}+\left\lVert\rho_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\sigma_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+1}{t+1}\lesssim\frac{1}{t+1}.

Substep 5.3.
L^{6}
Decay of
\sigma
.

Next, we have that the
L^{6}
decay of
\sigma
is governed by the following differential equation

(80)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{6}\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{6}+5D\left\lVert\sigma^{2}\nabla\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=D\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla\phi)\,\sigma^{5}\,dx,

which after applying Hölder and Young inequalities simplifies to

(81)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{6}+30D\left\lVert\sigma^{2}\nabla\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 30D\left\lVert\rho\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{6}}\left\lVert\sigma^{2}\nabla\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\sigma^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{3}}\lesssim 15D\left\lVert\sigma^{2}\nabla\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\rho\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{4}.

Here we used the identity
\left\lVert\sigma^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{3}}=\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}
. Simplifying (
81
), we thus deduce that

(82)

\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{6}\lesssim\left\lVert\rho\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{4}.

Once more, we notice that if
\left\lVert\sigma(\tau)\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{4}=0
for some
\tau
, then
\sigma(t)=0
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and the decay of
\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{6}}
is trivially true.

Otherwise, we divide (
82
) by
\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{4}
and obtain

(83)

\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}\lesssim\left\lVert\rho\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}.

Making use of Hölder’s inequality, the estimate (
15
), the boundedness of
\nabla\phi
in
L^{2}
derived in (
76
), the Sobolev embedding theorem, and the
L^{2}
and
L^{4}
decay of
c_{i}
from (
57
) and (
79
) respectively, we establish

(84)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\lesssim\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}\left(\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{4}}\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{4}}+\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{4}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}+\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}+\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}{(t+1)^{\frac{9}{8}}}+\frac{\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}{t+1}.

By differentiating
(t+1)\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}
and using the Sobolev embedding theorem, it follows that

(85)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left((t+1)\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=(t+1)\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}+\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}\lesssim\frac{\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}{(t+1)^{\frac{1}{8}}}+\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Integrating in time from
0
to
t
, we obtain

(86)

\displaystyle(t+1)\,\left\lVert\sigma(t)\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}\lesssim\left\lVert\sigma_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}{\left\lVert\nabla\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}.

Invoking the Sobolev embedding theorem and bound (
34
), we conclude that

(87)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sigma(t)\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}\lesssim\frac{\left\lVert\nabla\sigma_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}{t+1}+\frac{\left\lVert\rho_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\sigma_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}{t+1}\lesssim\frac{1}{t+1}.

Step 6. Pointwise Bounds for
|\widehat{\nabla c_{i}}|
for
i\in\left\{1,\dots,N\right\}
.

Taking the gradient of the Nernst-Planck PDE (
9
) and then applying the Fourier transform leads to

(88)

\partial_{t}\widehat{\nabla c_{i}}+|\xi|^{2}\widehat{\nabla c_{i}}\lesssim|\xi|^{2}\left(\lvert\widehat{uc_{i}}\rvert+\lvert\widehat{c_{i}\nabla\phi}\rvert\right)\lesssim|\xi|^{2}\left(\left\lVert uc_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}+\left\lVert c_{i}\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{1}}\right),

giving rise to the following pointwise bound

(89)

|\widehat{\nabla c_{i}}|\lesssim\left\lVert c_{i}(0)\right\rVert_{L^{1}}|\xi|+|\xi|^{2}\sqrt{t}.

Step 7. Bounds for
\int_{0}^{t}(s+1)^{\gamma}\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,ds
.

We multiply the ionic concentration equation (
9
) by
c_{i}
and integrate spatially over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Using the divergence-free property by
u
, the nonlinear term in
u
vanishes. As for the electromigration terms, we integrate by parts and apply the Cauchy-Schwarz and Young inequalities to bound it by the sum of
\frac{D}{2}\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
and a constant multiple of
\|c_{i}\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
. The latter gives rise to the differential inequality

(90)

\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+D\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\left\lVert c_{i}\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Differentiating
(s+1)^{\gamma}\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}
and using (
90
), we have

(91)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left((s+1)^{\gamma}\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+D(s+1)^{\gamma}\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\gamma(s+1)^{\gamma-1}\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+(s+1)^{\gamma}\left\lVert c_{i}\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\gamma(s+1)^{\gamma-1}\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+(s+1)^{\gamma}\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\gamma(s+1)^{\gamma-1}\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+(s+1)^{\gamma}\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}(\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}+\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2})

\displaystyle\lesssim\gamma(s+1)^{\gamma-\frac{5}{2}}+(s+1)^{\gamma-\frac{21}{8}}+(s+1)^{\gamma-\frac{5}{2}}.

Next, we integrate in time from
0
to
t
, take
\gamma\geq 2
, and conclude that

(92)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(s+1)^{\gamma}\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\,ds\lesssim 1+(t+1)^{\gamma-\frac{3}{2}}.

Step 8.
L^{2}
Decay of
\nabla c_{i}
for
i\in\left\{1,\dots,N\right\}
.

We start by multiplyng the Nernst-Planck PDE (
9
) by
\Delta c_{i}
and integrate spatially over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
to get

(93)

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+D\left\lVert\Delta c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u\cdot\nabla c_{i}\Delta c_{i}|\,dx+Dz^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\cdot(\sigma\nabla\phi)\Delta c_{i}|\,dx.

We estimate

(94)

\displaystyle Dz^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\cdot(\sigma\nabla\phi)\Delta c_{i}|\,dx\leq Dz^{2}\left\lVert\nabla\cdot(\sigma\nabla\phi)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\Delta c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{D}{6}\left\lVert\Delta c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left\lVert\nabla\cdot(\sigma\nabla\phi)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},

where

(95)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla\cdot(\sigma\nabla\phi)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left\lVert\nabla\cdot(c_{j}\nabla\phi)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left\lVert\nabla c_{j}\cdot\nabla\phi+c_{j}\Delta\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left\lVert\nabla c_{j}\cdot\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert c_{j}\Delta\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left\lVert\nabla c_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}+\left\lVert c_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}\left\lVert\Delta\phi\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left\lVert\nabla c_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\Delta c_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla c_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left\lVert\nabla c_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\Delta c_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert c_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\Delta c_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D}{6N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\left\lVert\Delta c_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left\lVert\nabla c_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{4}+C\left\lVert c_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D}{6N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\left\lVert\Delta c_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\left\lVert\nabla c_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}{(t+1)^{3}}.

The above calculations follow from Minkowski, Hölder, and Young inequalities, as well as interpolation inequalities, and the bound established in (
57
) for the
L^{2}
decay of
c_{i}
.

As a consequence, we obtain

(96)

\displaystyle Dz^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\cdot(\sigma\nabla\phi)\Delta c_{i}|\,dx\leq\frac{D}{6}\left\lVert\Delta c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{D}{6N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\left\lVert\Delta c_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\left\lVert\nabla c_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}{(t+1)^{3}}.

Next, using the Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation and Young inequalities, we bound

(97)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u\cdot\nabla c_{i}\Delta c_{i}|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{6}}\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{3}}\left\lVert\Delta c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{6}}\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\Delta c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D}{6}\left\lVert\Delta c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{4}\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

We move to estimate
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{4}
using the boundedness of the Leray projector in
L^{6}
, the estimate (
15
), the boundedness of
\nabla\phi
by initial datum in (
76
), and the decaying bounds established in (
57
), (
79
), and (
87
) for
c_{i}
in
L^{2}
,
L^{4}
, and
L^{6}
respectively, reaching

(98)

\displaystyle\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{4}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\mathbb{P}(\rho\nabla\phi)\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{4}\lesssim\left\lVert\rho\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{4}\lesssim\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{4}\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{4}\lesssim\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{4}\left(\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{3}+\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{4}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left\lVert c_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{4}\left(\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{3}+\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{4}\right)\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{(t+1)^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{(t+1)^{\frac{9}{4}}}+\frac{1}{(t+1)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(t+1)^{4}}.

Therefore,

(99)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u\cdot\nabla c_{i}\Delta c_{i}|\,dx\leq\frac{D}{6}\left\lVert\Delta c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\frac{\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}{(t+1)^{4}}.

Substituting the bounds (
95
) and (
99
) into (
93
), we now obtain the differential inequality

(100)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{4D}{3}\left\lVert\Delta c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{D}{3N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left\lVert\Delta c_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\left\lVert\nabla c_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}{(t+1)^{3}}+\frac{\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}{(t+1)^{4}}.

Summing over the ionic indices (from
i=1
to
N
), we reach the evolution inequality for the sum of the
L^{2}
norms of
\nabla c_{i}
,

(101)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{4D}{3}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\Delta c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{D}{3N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left\lVert\Delta c_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\left\lVert\nabla c_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}{(t+1)^{3}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}{(t+1)^{4}}.

After simplification, we infer that

(102)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+D\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\Delta c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{(t+1)^{3}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{(t+1)^{4}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

We now carry out the Fourier splitting technique on the dissipation term
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\Delta c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}
similarly to Step 4 above. We first note, that by Parseval’s identity, we have

(103)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Delta c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\widehat{\Delta c_{i}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\xi|^{2}|\widehat{\nabla c_{i}}|^{2}\,d\xi\geq C\int_{|\xi|>r(t)}|\xi|^{2}|\widehat{\nabla c_{i}}|^{2}\ d\xi

\displaystyle\geq Cr^{2}(t)\int_{|\xi|>r(t)}|\widehat{\nabla c_{i}}|^{2}\ d\xi=Cr^{2}(t)\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}-Cr^{2}(t)\int_{|\xi|\leq r(t)}|\widehat{\nabla c_{i}}|^{2}\ d\xi,

for some function
r(t)
to be determined later. Substituting this into (
102
), we reach the energy inequality

(104)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+CDr^{2}(t)\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim Cr^{2}(t)\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{|\xi|\leq r(t)}|\widehat{\nabla c_{i}}|^{2}\ d\xi+\frac{1}{(t+1)^{3}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{(t+1)^{4}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

By the pointwise bound (
89
) and Fubini’s theorem for spherical coordinates, we obtain

(105)

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq r(t)}|\widehat{\nabla c_{i}}|^{2}\ d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{|\xi|\leq r(t)}(\left\lVert c_{i}(0)\right\rVert_{L^{1}}^{2}|\xi|^{2}+|\xi|^{4}t)d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{r(t)}\int_{|\xi|=R}(\left\lVert c_{i}(0)\right\rVert_{L^{1}}^{2}|\xi|^{2}+|\xi|^{4}t)\ da(\xi)\ dR

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert c_{i}(0)\right\rVert_{L^{1}}^{2}\ r^{5}(t)+t\ r^{7}(t).

Substituting this into (
104
), we get

(106)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+CDr^{2}(t)\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{N}\ r^{7}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}t\ r^{9}(t)+\frac{1}{(t+1)^{3}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{(t+1)^{4}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

We choose
r(t)=\sqrt{\frac{m}{CD(t+1)}}
, where
m
is a constant to be determined later. Multiplying by the integrating factor
(t+1)^{m}
and integrating in time from
0
to
t
, we find that

(107)

\displaystyle(t+1)^{m}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}(0)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+m^{\frac{7}{2}}\frac{(t+1)^{m-\frac{5}{2}}}{m-\frac{5}{2}}+m^{\frac{9}{2}}\frac{(t+1)^{m-\frac{5}{2}}}{m-\frac{5}{2}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t}(s+1)^{m-3}\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\,ds+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t}(s+1)^{m-4}\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\,ds,

which, using the bound established in (
92
), yields

(108)

\displaystyle(t+1)^{m}\sum_{i=1}^{N}

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim 1+m^{\frac{7}{2}}\frac{(t+1)^{m-\frac{5}{2}}}{m-\frac{5}{2}}+m^{\frac{9}{2}}\frac{(t+1)^{m-\frac{5}{2}}}{m-\frac{5}{2}}+(t+1)^{m-\frac{9}{2}}+(t+1)^{m-\frac{11}{2}},

provided that
m-3\geq 2
and
m-4\geq 2
. We choose
m=6
and simplify the inequality to finally deduce the decaying bound for
\nabla c_{i}
in
L^{2}
for all
i\in\{0,\dots,N\}
as

(109)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(t+1)^{\frac{5}{2}}}.

∎

An induction argument allows boostrapping of the time decay to higher-order derivatives of the ionic concentrations, as shown in the following proposition:

Proposition 3
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
k\geq 2
. Suppose
c_{i}(0)\geq 0
,
c_{i}(0)\in H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for all
i\in\left\{1,\dots,N\right\},
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{0}=0
.
Then, there exists a positive constant
M_{k}
, depending on the parameters of the problem and on the
L^{1}
and
H^{k}
norms of the initial concentrations
c_{i}(0)
, such that

(110)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{M_{k}}{(t+1)^{k+\frac{3}{2}}},

for all
t\geq 0
and
i\in\left\{1,\dots,N\right\}
.

Proof.

The proof is divided into several steps.

Step 1. Pointwise Bound for
|\widehat{\Lambda^{k}c_{i}}|
for
i\in\left\{1,\dots,N\right\}.

We apply the
\Lambda^{k}
operator on the Nernst-Planck PDE (
9
) and take the Fourier transform of the resulting equation, reaching

(111)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\widehat{\Lambda^{k}c_{i}}+CD|\xi|^{2}\widehat{\Lambda^{k}c_{i}}\lesssim|\xi|^{k+1}\left(\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\sqrt{\sigma}\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\right).

After integration by parts and using the appropriate decaying bounds, we obtain the pointwise bound

(112)

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{\Lambda^{k}c_{i}(t)}\right|\lesssim\left\lVert c_{i}(0)\right\rVert_{L^{1}}|\xi|^{k}+|\xi|^{k+1}+|\xi|^{k+1}\sqrt{t}\lesssim\left\lVert c_{i}(0)\right\rVert_{L^{1}}|\xi|^{k}+|\xi|^{k+1}\sqrt{t+1}.

Step 2.
L^{2}
Decay of
\Lambda^{k}c_{i}
for
i\in\left\{1,\dots,N\right\}.
We establish the
L^{2}
decay of
\Lambda^{k}c_{i}
by induction. Indeed, we suppose that the following estimates

(113)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Lambda^{k-1}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(t+1)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}},

(114)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(s+1)^{\gamma}\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}c_{i}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim 1+(t+1)^{\gamma-k-\frac{1}{2}},

hold with
\gamma>k+\frac{1}{2}
, and we prove that

(115)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(t+1)^{k+\frac{3}{2}}},

(116)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(s+1)^{\gamma}\left\lVert\Lambda^{k+1}c_{i}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim 1+(t+1)^{\gamma-k-\frac{3}{2}},

hold for
\gamma>k+\frac{3}{2}
.
We begin by multiplying the Nernst-Planck PDE (
9
) by
\Lambda^{2k}c_{i}
and integrating spatially to obtain

(117)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+D\left\lVert\Lambda^{k+1}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq D|z_{i}|\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\cdot(c_{i}\nabla\phi)\ \Lambda^{2k}c_{i}\,dx\right|+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u\cdot\nabla c_{i}\ \Lambda^{2k}c_{i}\,dx\right|.

We simplify the first integral term of (
117
) through integration by parts and the Hölder and Young inequalities, and we get

(118)

\displaystyle D|z_{i}|\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\cdot(c_{i}\nabla\phi)\ \Lambda^{2k}c_{i}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq D|z_{i}|\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Lambda^{k-1}\nabla\cdot(c_{i}\nabla\phi)\ \Lambda^{k+1}c_{i}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D}{6}\left\lVert\Lambda^{k+1}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}(c_{i}\nabla\phi)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Using the Kato-Ponce inequality (see
[
21
]
), the estimate (
16
), the
L^{3}
decay of
c_{i}
(
69
), the Sobolev embedding theorem, the
L^{2}
decay of
c_{i}
and
\nabla c_{i}
found in (
57
) and (
109
) respectively, we have

(119)

\displaystyle C\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}(c_{i}\nabla\phi)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left(\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\frac{\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}{t+1}\lesssim\frac{\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}{(t+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}c_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}{t+1}.

Next, we treat the second integral term of (
117
) by using integration by parts, the divergence-free property of
u
, and the Hölder and Young inequalities, as follows

(120)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u\cdot\nabla c_{i}\ \Lambda^{2k}c_{i}\,dx\right|\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Lambda^{k-1}\nabla\cdot(uc_{i})\ \Lambda^{k+1}c_{i}\,dx\right|\leq\frac{D}{6}\left\lVert\Lambda^{k+1}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}(uc_{i})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Here, to bound
\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}(uc_{i})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}
, we use the Kato-Ponce inequality, the boundedness of the Leray projector in
L^{2}
, the continuous embedding of
W^{1,4}
in
L^{\infty}
, the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, the Sobolev embedding theorem, Young’s inequality, the estimate (
16
), the bound on
\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}(c_{i}\nabla\phi)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}
derived in (
119
), and the
L^{2}
decay of
c_{i}
and
\nabla c_{i}
obtained in (
57
) and (
109
) respectively, to get

(121)

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}(uc_{i})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}(\rho\nabla\phi)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{W^{1,\,4}}^{2}+\left\lVert\rho\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\Lambda^{k+1}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}(c_{j}\nabla\phi)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left(\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}\right)+\frac{D}{6}\left\lVert\Lambda^{k+1}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left\lVert\rho\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{4}\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

3.
Sharpness of the Decay Rate

In this section, we address the sharpness of the time decay of all derivatives of the ionic concentrations.

Proposition 4
.

Suppose
c_{i}(0)\geq 0
,
c_{i}(0)\in H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for all
i\in\left\{1,\dots,N\right\},
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{0}=0
. For each
i\in\left\{1,\dots,N\right\}
, we denote by
\tilde{c}_{i}
the solution to the heat equation
\partial_{t}\tilde{c}_{i}-D\Delta\tilde{c}_{i}=0
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
(with decay at infinity), with initial data
\tilde{c}_{i}(0)=c_{i}(0)
. There exists a positive constant
O_{k}
depending on the parameters of the problem and on the
L^{1}
and
H^{k}
norms of the initial concentrations, such that

(131)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}(c_{i}-\tilde{c}_{i})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{O_{k}}{(t+1)^{k+2}}

for all
t\geq 0
and for all
i\in\left\{1,\dots,N\right\}
.

Proof.

With the aim of proving the sharpness of the decay of
\Lambda^{k}c_{i}
in
L^{2}
, we will first subtract the linear heat PDE given by
\partial_{t}\tilde{c}_{i}-D\Delta\tilde{c}_{i}=0
from the Nernst-Planck PDE (
9
) to get

(132)

\partial_{t}(c_{i}-\tilde{c}_{i})-D\Delta(c_{i}-\tilde{c}_{i})+u\cdot\nabla c_{i}-Dz_{i}\nabla\cdot(c_{i}\nabla\phi)=0.

Next, we multiply the PDE (
132
) by
\Lambda^{2k}(c_{i}-\tilde{c}_{i})
and integrate spatially to obtain

(133)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}(c_{i}-\tilde{c}_{i})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+

\displaystyle D\left\lVert\Lambda^{k+1}(c_{i}-\tilde{c}_{i})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq D|z_{i}|\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\cdot(c_{i}\nabla\phi)\ \Lambda^{2k}(c_{i}-\tilde{c}_{i})\,dx\right|+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u\cdot\nabla c_{i}\ \Lambda^{2k}(c_{i}-\tilde{c}_{i})\,dx\right|.

For the first integral term of (
133
), applying integration by parts and the Hölder and Young inequalities yields

(134)

\displaystyle D|z_{i}|\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\cdot(c_{i}\nabla\phi)\ \Lambda^{2k}(c_{i}-\tilde{c}_{i})\,dx\right|

\displaystyle=D|z_{i}|\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Lambda^{k-1}\nabla\cdot(c_{i}\nabla\phi)\ \Lambda^{k+1}(c_{i}-\tilde{c}_{i})\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D}{4}\left\lVert\Lambda^{k+1}(c_{i}-\tilde{c}_{i})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}(c_{i}\nabla\phi)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},

where

(135)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}(c_{i}\nabla\phi)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}+\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\Lambda^{k+1}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left(\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\left\lVert\Lambda^{k-1}\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(t+1)^{k+\frac{13}{4}}}+\frac{1}{(t+1)^{k+3}}\lesssim\frac{1}{(t+1)^{k+3}},

having used the Kato-Ponce inequality, the continuous embedding of
W^{1,6}
in
L^{\infty}
, the Sobolev embedding theorem, the Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequality, the boundedness of
\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}
from (
76
), and the decay results for
c_{i}
. For the second integral term of (
133
), using the divergence-free property of
u
, integration by parts, and the Hölder and Young inequalities, we get

(136)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u\cdot\nabla c_{i}\ \Lambda^{2k}(c_{i}-\tilde{c}_{i})\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|\Lambda^{k-1}\nabla\cdot(uc_{i})\ \Lambda^{k+1}(c_{i}-\tilde{c}_{i})\right|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}(uc_{i})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\Lambda^{k+1}(c_{i}-\tilde{c}_{i})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D}{4}\left\lVert\Lambda^{k+1}(c_{i}-\tilde{c}_{i})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}(uc_{i})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},

where, similarly,

(137)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}(uc_{i})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}(\rho\nabla\phi)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left(\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\Delta c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\left\lVert\rho\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\Lambda^{k+1}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(t+1)^{\frac{5}{2}}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}(c_{j}\nabla\phi)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{(t+1)^{k+2}}\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(t+1)^{k+\frac{11}{2}}}+\frac{1}{(t+1)^{k+2}}\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left(\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(t+1)^{k+\frac{11}{2}}}+\frac{1}{(t+1)^{k+6}}\lesssim\frac{1}{(t+1)^{k+\frac{11}{2}}},

by using (
135
) and the estimate (
16

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}(c_{i}-\tilde{c}_{i})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+D\left\lVert\Lambda^{k+1}(c_{i}-\tilde{c}_{i})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{C}{(t+1)^{k+3}}.

To apply the Fourier splitting technique, we derive an improved pointwise bound for
\left|\widehat{\Lambda^{k+1}(c_{i}-\tilde{c}_{i})}\right|
. Applying the
\Lambda^{k}
operator on (

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\widehat{\Lambda^{k}(c_{i}-\tilde{c}_{i})}+CD|\xi|^{2}\widehat{\Lambda^{k}(c_{i}-\tilde{c}_{i})}\lesssim|\xi|^{k+1}\left(\left\lVert c_{i}\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{1}}+\left\lVert uc_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}\right).

We then multiply by the integrating factor

(140)

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{\Lambda^{k}(c_{i}-\tilde{c}_{i})}(\xi,t)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\xi|^{k+1}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert c_{i}\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{1}}\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert uc_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}\,ds\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim|\xi|^{k+1}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\,ds\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim|\xi|^{k+1}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{(t+1)^{\frac{3}{4}}}\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}}{(t+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\,ds\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim|\xi|^{k+1}\left((t+1)^{\frac{1}{4}}+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{(t+1)^{\frac{9}{4}}}\,ds\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim|\xi|^{k+1}\,(t+1)^{\frac{1}{4}},

wherein we used Hölder’s inequality, the boundedness of
\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}
by (
76

Furthermore, by Parseval’s identity, the pointwise bound (

(141)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Lambda^{k+1}(c_{i}-\tilde{c}_{i})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\gtrsim r^{2}(t)\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}(c_{i}-\tilde{c}_{i})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}-(t+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\ r^{2k+7}(t),

with

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}(c_{i}-\tilde{c}_{i})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+CDr^{2}(t)\left\lVert\Lambda^{k}(c_{i}-\tilde{c}_{i})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim(t+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\ r^{2k+7}(t)+\frac{1}{(t+1)^{k+3}}.

, depending on the parameters of the problem and on the

For each

4.
Entropy Analysis

In this section, we investigate the behavior of the relative entropy associated with the NPD model on
\mathbb{R}^{3}

We denote the individual and total entropy quantities by
\mathcal{E}_{i}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}c_{i}(x,t)\log c_{i}(x,t)\,dx

\mathcal{E}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{E}_{i}(t)

The following proposition provides lower bounds for
-\mathcal{E}
that grows logarithmically in time:

Proposition 6
.

Suppose
c_{i}(0)\geq 0
,
c_{i}(0)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}c_{i}(0)\log c_{i}(0)\in\mathbb{R}

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{0}=0

\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}

\mathcal{E}_{i}(0),\,\|c_{i}(0)\|_{L^{1}}
and
\|c_{i}(0)\|_{L^{2}}
, and a positive constant
\Gamma_{3}
depending only on the diffusivity
D

\|c_{i}(0)\|_{L^{1}}

(148)

-\mathcal{E}_{i}\geq\Gamma_{1}+\frac{3}{2}\|c_{i}(0)\|_{L^{1}}\log(\Gamma_{2}+\Gamma_{3}t)

for any
t\geq 0
and all
i\in\left\{1,\dots,N\right\}

\mathcal{E}

(149)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{E}(t)=-\infty.

Proof.

We multiply the Nernst-Planck PDE (
9
) by
\log c_{i}
, integrate spatially over
\mathbb{R}^{3}

12
) to obtain

(150)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=-D\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\dfrac{\nabla c_{i}\cdot\nabla c_{i}}{c_{i}}\,dx-z_{i}D\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla c_{i}\,dx

\displaystyle=-4D\left\lVert\nabla\sqrt{c_{i}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}-z_{i}D\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho c_{i}\,dx.

Since
\displaystyle\frac{c_{i}\ dx}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}c_{i}}
is a probability measure and
\log x
is a concave function, we can apply Jensen’s inequality to bound the entropies from above as follows

(151)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}c_{i}\log c_{i}\,dx=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}c_{i}\,dx\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\dfrac{c_{i}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}c_{i}\,dx}\log c_{i}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}\log\left(\dfrac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}c_{i}^{2}\,dx}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}c_{i}\,dx}\right)=\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}\log\left(\dfrac{\left\lVert\sqrt{c_{i}}\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{4}}{\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}}\right).

In view of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequality, we have

(152)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{i}(t)\leq\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}\log\left(\dfrac{C\left\lVert\sqrt{c_{i}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\sqrt{c_{i}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{3}}{\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}}\right)=\frac{3}{2}\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}\log\left(\dfrac{C\left\lVert\nabla\sqrt{c_{i}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}{\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{3}}}\right),

Thus, upon exponentiation, we get

(153)

\displaystyle\exp{\left({-\dfrac{2\mathcal{E}_{i}}{3\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}}}\right)}\ \dfrac{\left\lVert\nabla\sqrt{c_{i}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}{\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{3}}}\geq\dfrac{1}{C}.

We then differentiate
N_{i}(t)=\exp\left({\frac{-2\mathcal{E}_{i}}{3\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}}}\right)
in time and use the equality (
150
), the bound (
153
), and the decaying estimate (
57
) to obtain

(154)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}N_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{3\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}}N_{i}(t)\ \frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}_{i}=\dfrac{2}{3\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}}N_{i}(t)\left(4D\left\lVert\nabla\sqrt{c_{i}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+z_{i}D\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho c_{i}\,dx\right)

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{8D}{3C\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}^{\frac{2}{3}}}+\dfrac{2z_{i}D}{3\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}}N_{i}(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho c_{i}\,dx\geq\dfrac{8D}{3C\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}^{\frac{2}{3}}}-\dfrac{2|z_{i}|D}{3\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}}N_{i}(t)\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{8D}{3C\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}^{\frac{2}{3}}}-\dfrac{2|z_{i}|D\ \Gamma}{3\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}(t+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}N_{i}(t).

For notational convenience, we denote
c=\frac{2|z_{i}|D\ \Gamma}{3\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}}
and solve the differential inequality (
154
) by multiplying by the integrating factor
e^{c\int_{0}^{t}(s+1)^{-1.5}\,ds}=e^{c\left(2-2(t+1)^{-0.5}\right)}
, which gives

(155)

\displaystyle N_{i}(t)\geq e^{-2c}\left(N_{i}(0)+\frac{8D}{3C\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}^{\frac{2}{3}}}t\right).

Applying the logarithmic function on both sides of the latter inequality yields the desired result.
∎

The following proposition provides upper bounds for
-\mathcal{E}
that grows logarithmically in time:

Proposition 7
.

Suppose
c_{i}(0)\geq 0
,
c_{i}(0)\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{6}c_{i}(0)^{2}\in\mathbb{R}
for any
i\in\left\{1,\dots,N\right\},
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{0}=0
. Then, there exist a time
T_{\Gamma}
depending on the
L^{1}
and
H^{2}
norms of the initial concentrations and positive constants
\Gamma_{4}
,
\Gamma_{5}
, and
\Gamma_{6}
depending on
\|c_{i}(0)\|_{L^{1}}
,
\|c_{i}(0)\|_{H^{2}}
, and
\||x|^{3}c_{i}(0)\|_{L^{2}}
such that

(156)

\displaystyle-\mathcal{E}(t)\leq\Gamma_{4}\log(\Gamma_{5}+\Gamma_{6}\ t^{\frac{15}{16}})

for any
t\geq t_{\Gamma}
.

Proof.

The proof is divided into two major steps.

Step 1. Moment Bounds.

Multiplying the Nernst-Planck PDE (
9
) by
|x|^{6}c_{i}
, we have

(157)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert|x|^{3}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+D\left\lVert|x|^{3}\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-D\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla c_{i}\cdot\nabla\left(|x|^{6}\right)c_{i}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u\cdot\nabla c_{i}\ |x|^{6}c_{i}\,dx-z_{i}D\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\cdot(c_{i}\nabla\phi)\ |x|^{6}c_{i}\,dx

\displaystyle=\text{I}+\text{II}+\text{III}.

We now treat each term individually. For term I, we use Hölder’s inequality with exponents
\frac{3}{2},\ 3
and the decaying bound (
57
) to obtain

(158)

\displaystyle\text{I}

\displaystyle=-D\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}c_{i}\nabla c_{i}\cdot\left(6x|x|^{4}\right)\,dx\leq\frac{D}{4}\left\lVert|x|^{3}\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left\lVert|x|^{2}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D}{4}\left\lVert|x|^{3}\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left\lVert|x|^{3}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4}{3}}\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\leq\frac{D}{4}\left\lVert|x|^{3}\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{C}{(t+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left\lVert|x|^{3}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4}{3}}.

As for term II, we use the divergence-free property of
u
and integrate by parts to get

(159)

\displaystyle\text{II}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}uc_{i}\cdot\nabla c_{i}\ |x|^{6}\,dx+6\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}uc_{i}^{2}\cdot x|x|^{4}\,dx=-\text{II}+6\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}uc_{i}^{2}\cdot x|x|^{4}\,dx.

By making use of continuous Sobolev embeddings, the potential estimate (
16
), and the decaying bounds (
57
), (
109
), and (
115
), we estimate the velocity in
L^{\infty}
as follows

(160)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{W^{1,6}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{6}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{6}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\rho\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\rho\|_{L^{6}}\|\Delta\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Delta\rho\|_{L^{2}})\left(\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}\right)+\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}(\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Delta\rho\|_{L^{2}})\lesssim\frac{1}{(t+1)^{2}},

which yields

(161)

\displaystyle\text{II}

\displaystyle=3\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}uc_{i}^{2}\cdot x|x|^{4}\,dx\lesssim\left\lVert|x|^{3}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert|x|^{2}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\left\lVert|x|^{3}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{5}{3}}\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{3}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(t+1)^{\frac{9}{4}}}\left\lVert|x|^{3}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{5}{3}},

after using a similar interpolation of
\left\lVert|x|^{2}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}
to the one used in (
158
).
Invoking again the potential bound (
16
), we similarly estimate term III and obtain

(162)

\displaystyle\text{III}

\displaystyle=z_{i}D\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}c_{i}\nabla\phi\cdot\left(\nabla c_{i}|x|^{6}+6x|x|^{4}c_{i}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert|x|^{3}\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert|x|^{3}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\left\lVert|x|^{3}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert|x|^{2}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D}{4}\left\lVert|x|^{3}\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left\lVert|x|^{3}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+C\left\lVert|x|^{3}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{5}{3}}\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{3}}\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D}{4}\left\lVert|x|^{3}\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{C}{(t+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left\lVert|x|^{3}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{C}{(t+1)}\left\lVert|x|^{3}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{5}{3}}.

Consequently, combining these individual estimates and using Young’s inequality give rise to

(163)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert|x|^{3}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(t+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left\lVert|x|^{3}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4}{3}}+\frac{1}{(t+1)}\left\lVert|x|^{3}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{5}{3}}+\frac{1}{(t+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left\lVert|x|^{3}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{0}}{(t+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left\lVert|x|^{3}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(t+1)^{\frac{3}{2}},

which, upon applying Gronwall’s inequality, yields

(164)

\displaystyle\left\lVert|x|^{3}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{1}+C_{2}(t+1)^{\frac{5}{2}},

where
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
depend on
\left\lVert|x|^{3}c_{i}(0)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}
,
\left\lVert c_{i}(0)\right\rVert_{H^{2}}
,
\left\lVert c_{i}(0)\right\rVert_{L^{1}}
, and the parameters of the problem.

Step 2. Absolute Entropy Upper Bound.

By the continuous embedding of
W^{1,6}
in
L^{\infty}
and the Sobolev embedding theorem, we know that

(165)

\displaystyle\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{W^{1,\,6}}\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\Delta c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\lesssim\dfrac{1}{(t+1)^{\frac{5}{4}}}\leq\frac{1}{2},

for
t\geq T_{\Gamma}
, where
T_{\Gamma}
is a constant that depends on the
L^{1}
and
H^{2}
norms of the initial ionic concentrations. Hence, it holds that
|c_{i}(x,t)|\leq\frac{1}{2}
for
t\geq T_{\Gamma}
and almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Due to the concavity of the logarithmic function and the fact that
\frac{c_{i}\,dx}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}c_{i}}
is a probability measure, we can make use of Jensen’s inequality to bound the entropy by

(166)

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{E}(t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|c_{i}\log c_{i}|\,dx=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}c_{i}\log\frac{1}{c_{i}}\,dx=4\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}c_{i}\,dx\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{c_{i}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}c_{i}\,dx}\log\left(\frac{1}{c_{i}}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}\log\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{c_{i}^{\frac{3}{4}}}{\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}}\,dx\right),

for any
t\geq t_{\Gamma}
.
Using Hölder’s inequality with exponent
\frac{8}{3},\,\frac{8}{5},
and the moment bound (
164
), we have

(167)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}c_{i}^{\frac{3}{4}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{(1+|x|)^{\frac{9}{4}}\,c_{i}^{\frac{3}{4}}}{(1+|x|)^{\frac{9}{4}}}\,dx\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+|x|)^{6}\ c_{i}^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{3}{8}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{\frac{18}{5}}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{5}{8}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{4}}+\left\lVert|x|^{3}c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{4}}\lesssim 1+(t+1)^{\frac{15}{16}}.

Thus, substituting back into (
166
), we obtain the desired estimate (
156
).
∎

When the spatial domain is bounded, the entropy decays in time to 0:

Proposition 8
.

Let
A>0
. Suppose
c_{i}(0)\geq 0
,
c_{i}(0)\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{0}=0
for all
i\in\left\{1,\dots,N\right\}
. Then, it holds that

(168)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\left|\int_{\{|x|\leq A\}}c_{i}\log c_{i}\ dx\right|=0

for all
i\in\left\{1,\dots,N\right\}
.

Proof.

To prove the convergence of entropy on finite spatial domains, we decompose the entropy integral over the finite sphere of radius
A
into the following two integrals

(169)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\{|x|\leq A\}}c_{i}\log c_{i}\ dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{|x|\leq A\}}\left|c_{i}\log c_{i}\right|\ dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\{|x|\leq A\}\cap\{c_{i}\leq 1\}}\left|c_{i}\log c_{i}\right|\ dx+\int_{\{|x|\leq A\}\cap\{c_{i}>1\}}\left|c_{i}\log c_{i}\right|\ dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{|x|\leq A\}\cap\{c_{i}\leq 1\}}c_{i}\log\frac{1}{c_{i}}\ dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}c_{i}^{2}\,dx\lesssim\int_{\{|x|\leq A\}}c_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\ dx+\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{\{|x|\leq A\}}c_{i}^{2}\ dx\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\int_{\{|x|<A\}}1\ dx\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}+\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{4}{3}\pi A^{3}\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{(t+1)^{\frac{3}{8}}}+\frac{1}{(t+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\lesssim\frac{1}{(t+1)^{\frac{3}{8}}}.

Letting
t\to\infty
, we obtain the desired result.
∎

Peer support groups and care burden in hemodialysis caregivers: a RCT in an Iranian healthcare setting
Authors: Nader Ghenaati, Hamid Reza Zendehtalab, Mohammad Namazinia, Masoud Zare
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundChronic renal failure poses a significant global health challenge, exerting a substantial burden on both patients and their caregivers. Hemodialysis, a common treatment for end-stage renal disease, imposes extensive physical, emotional, and financial pressures on caregivers, often leading to a high care burden. This study uniquely examines the impact of peer support groups on reducing the care burden among caregivers of patients receiving hemodialysis in an Iranian healthcare setting, an aspect that has not been extensively explored before.MethodsA parallel-controlled clinical trial was conducted involving 60 caregivers, divided into intervention and control groups. The intervention group participated in an 8-session peer support program tailored to their identified needs, including coping with stress, social isolation, and financial challenges. The Zarit Care Burden Interview Scale was used to measure care burden before and after the intervention.ResultsThe study revealed statistically significant reductions in care burden, particularly in physical, social, and emotional dimensions, among caregivers in the intervention group compared to the control group. The total care burden score showed a marked decrease, indicating the effectiveness of the peer support intervention. While economic challenges remained a concern, the intervention had a limited impact in this domain.ConclusionThis study demonstrates that peer support groups significantly alleviate the care burden experienced by caregivers of patients receiving hemodialysis, improving their well-being across several dimensions. The findings highlight the importance of integrating peer support strategies into healthcare programs for chronic disease management and underscore the need for supplementary economic support measures to comprehensively address caregivers’ needs. Future research should explore the scalability and long-term sustainability of such interventions and address the unique economic challenges faced by these caregivers.Trial registrationThis study was registered in the Iranian Registry of Clinical Trials (IRCT) under the registration number IRCT20220724055540N1 on 11/08/2022.Keywords: Chronic renal failure, Hemodialysis care, Caregiver burden, Peer support intervention, Iranian healthcare system, Social support in healthcare

Introduction

Chronic renal failure (CRF) is a significant and serious condition that is a major cause of mortality on a global scale, making it an issue of utmost concern for public health and presenting substantial social and economic challenges for patients [1, 2]. Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is defined as kidney impairment or an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) below 60 mL/min/1.73 m² persisting for at least three months. It encompasses various pathological processes characterized by a decline in kidney function, including a decrease in glomerular filtration rate and the onset of uremic syndrome [3, 4]. A study conducted by Cheng et al. (2019) revealed a global prevalence of chronic kidney disease at 13.4%. Consequently, this condition necessitates renal replacement therapy for approximately 4.9 to 7 million individuals who suffer from end-stage renal disease (ESRD) worldwide [5, 6]. Renal replacement therapy becomes essential when ESRD occurs and the kidneys permanently cease to function. Options for treating ESRD include kidney transplantation or dialysis, such as hemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis. Hemodialysis is the most commonly used treatment method, employed by approximately 70% of ESRD patients [6, 7]. However, living with hemodialysis presents continuous challenges due to the treatment regimen, potential complications, and dietary restrictions. Extensive research has demonstrated that individuals undergoing hemodialysis often face difficulties such as financial hardships, job insecurity, decreased libido and impotence, depression resulting from chronic illness, and a fear of mortality [1, 8, 9]. In reality, these patients encounter numerous daily demands that generate physiological and psychological stress [10]. Many of them rely on assistance and care from others, as some lack the physical and mental capacity to fulfill their daily self-care tasks and therefore depend on external support [11]. Caregivers play a crucial role in managing their care and meeting their needs, including medication administration, preparation of specialized diets, arranging transportation for hospital visits, and attending clinic appointments [12–18]. Caregivers can be family members, friends, or neighbours who provide unpaid care to disabled individuals [19, 20]. The prolonged nature of caregiving gradually leads to stress and depression in the caregiver [21–23]. Furthermore, caregivers experience a decline in their physical health due to the demands imposed by their role, often resulting in conditions such as hypertension, anxiety, depression, and premature aging. Caregivers of dialysis patients face various challenges and complex issues related to emotions, finances, and social constraints [24, 25]. The term “care burden” refers to the physical, emotional, and financial strain endured by caregivers [26–28]. The responsibilities associated with the role of caregiver can disrupt their activities, leisure pursuits, social interactions, and overall well-being [29, 30]. The mounting pressure experienced by caregivers often leads to isolation, a lack of hope for social support, strain on familial relationships, and inadequate care for the patient, as has been noted in previous studies [31–33]. Regrettably, caregivers are often overlooked, with greater attention being focused solely on the patients themselves. This imbalance exacerbates various aspects of caregivers’ health, particularly their mental well-being [34, 35]. A study revealed that approximately 74% of caregivers for patients undergoing hemodialysis experience a significant care burden [36]. Similarly, another study conducted by Dastiyar et al. (2020) found that 88.2% of participating caregivers reported experiencing a severe or moderate care burden [37]. A systematic review assessed the substantial care strain inflicted on caregivers of patients receiving hemodialysis and advocated for prioritizing the health of caregivers as hidden patients [38, 39].

Caregivers of patients receiving hemodialysis must adapt to the patient and the associated care issues. One effective approach in facilitating this adaptation is the provision of social support, such as peer support groups [40]. These groups involve individuals coming together to share their experiences and create a supportive network. By offering strategies derived from personal experiences, group members can provide valuable insights that even healthcare professionals may not be aware of [41]. Support groups play a vital role in validating individual experiences, raising awareness of therapeutic and healing aspects, and helping individuals cope with chronic illnesses. These groups provide a safe environment for the free expression of inner emotions [42, 43]. Furthermore, support groups empower caregivers to prioritize their own needs alongside those of the patient [44]. Another study demonstrated a significant improvement in perceived social support among caregivers, highlighting the importance of friendship and peer support as sources of emotional and social sustenance [45].

The involvement of individuals in education confers diverse benefits at the societal, cultural, and economic levels [46, 47]. It fosters a sense of responsibility, facilitates the transfer of knowledge, and yields savings in terms of finances, time, and teaching personnel [48, 49]. Moreover, it contributes to the promotion of social norms and values that endorse healthy behaviors. Currently, there is an absence of evidence regarding the impact of peer support groups on the care burden imposed on caregivers of patients receiving hemodialysis in national and international databases. To explore this hypothesis, researchers have conducted a study titled “The Effect of Peer Support Group on Care Burden and Unmet Needs of Caregivers of Patients receiving hemodialysis.”

Methods

Trial design

The clinical trial was carried out using a parallel controlled design and focused on a cohort of 60 caregivers responsible for patients undergoing hemodialysis in an Iranian healthcare setting (Fig. 1).

Participants

The eligibility criteria for caregivers encompassed a willingness to engage in the study and provide informed consent, serving as the primary caregiver for the patient, possessing a minimum of six months of regular dialysis experience for the patient, proficiency in reading and writing, absence of speech or hearing impairments, capability to make phone calls, lack of specific psychological issues, and not being a member of the healthcare team. As for the peers, the entry criteria involved being accepted and esteemed by other group members, demonstrating interest and motivation to fulfill a role, ability to communicate effectively and conduct meetings, willingness to instruct others, absence of a history of mental or cognitive disorders, higher level of health literacy, greater compatibility with care-related issues, and possession of appropriate communication skills.

Intervention

To implement the intervention, six caregivers were selected from the Hefdeh Shahrivar Hemodialysis Center in Mashhad city based on rigorous peer selection criteria. These chosen peers underwent thorough individual evaluations during a dedicated meeting, separate from the other caregivers. In this meeting, comprehensive explanations were provided regarding various aspects of the care burden, the crucial role of peers in conducting meetings, scheduling support sessions, and the ultimate objectives of these gatherings.

The researcher, an experienced community health nurse, meticulously assessed the performance, knowledge, and attitude of the peers to ensure the highest level of competence and credibility.

The peers were evaluated by the community health nurse based on their communication skills, empathy, and ability to provide support. The assessment included role-playing scenarios and feedback sessions to ensure they met the criteria for effective peer support. This evaluation aimed to identify any potential scientific, informational, or skill deficiencies, as well as areas where caregivers may lack awareness or understanding. Furthermore, the meeting encompassed a comprehensive discussion on effective techniques for conducting both needs assessment meetings and support sessions, focusing on addressing the needs of the caregivers.

Initially, the researcher imparted valuable knowledge to the peers on how to skillfully facilitate a needs assessment meeting, employing brainstorming techniques. The peers were actively encouraged to engage in attentive listening, fostering a safe and non-judgmental environment for caregivers to freely express their concerns and challenges. The primary focus was on identifying genuine needs and conducting scientific examinations of problem-solving approaches.

“In the intervention group, a comprehensive program consisting of eight training sessions was conducted for caregivers of patients receiving hemodialysis. These sessions were held twice a week, with each session lasting a maximum of one hour, spanning over a period of four weeks. The training sessions were conducted in small groups, comprising a maximum of five caregivers per group. Each training session was dedicated to a particular topic, carefully selected based on the expressed needs and interests of the caregivers during the initial meeting. Priority was given to topics that were frequently raised and expressed by multiple individuals during the needs assessment session. The intended content of the sessions included dietary guidelines, fluid restrictions, medication management, transportation logistics, coping with stress, addressing social isolation, managing financial challenges, and strategies for improving mental health and emotional well-being.”

The topics for the peer support group meetings were carefully selected based on the expressed needs and interests of the caregivers during the initial meeting. Priority was given to topics that were frequently raised and expressed by multiple individuals during the needs assessment session.

During the support group meetings, a combination of argumentative debate and nominal group techniques was utilized to promote comprehensive deliberation of all the issues raised. Each member of the group was encouraged to express their thoughts and actively engage in the discussion. The nurse and peer caregivers shared their own experiences and provided valuable feedback on the issues being discussed. Additionally, caregivers were prompted to think critically, evaluate and analyze each other’s proposed solutions, and offer further comments to enhance the suggested solutions and shared experiences.

Throughout each session, the caregivers engaged in discourse and prioritized issues based on the findings of the needs assessment. They exchanged insights and received input from previous support sessions. Reflection on the application of previous session materials occurred as caregivers deliberated. In the event that novel concerns emerged, the nurse and peers carefully assessed them and proposed potential resolutions.

The topics covered in the peer support group sessions included a wide range of issues identified during the needs assessment meeting, such as depression, anxiety, isolation, mental fatigue, decreased motivation, lack of time, need for social support, treatment and care costs, lack of insurance, reduced working hours and income due to caregiving, lack of sleep, anorexia, malnutrition, and various other physical and psychological needs.

Outcomes

The instruments utilized in this study included the demographic information questionnaire and Zarit care burden questionnaire.

The Zarit Care Burden Questionnaire, comprising 22 questions to assess individuals’ sentiments towards caring for another person, was employed. The questionnaire evaluates four dimensions: personal, social, emotional, and economic. The intensity of caregiving pressure is measured using a 5-point Likert scale, ranging from ‘never’ to ‘always’, with corresponding scores of 0 to 4. The total score can range from 0 to 88. On the care pressure scale, a score of 0–20 indicates no or low pressure, 21–40 indicates mild to moderate pressure, 41–60 indicates moderate to severe pressure, and 61–88 indicates severe care pressure.

The reliability of the Zarit Burden Care Questionnaire has been validated in previous studies, including an internal consistency (Cronbach’s alpha) of 0.91 in the psychometric evaluation study conducted by Gonçalves-Pereira (2017), good reliability through internal consistency and retest (r = 0.9) in Jennifer Davis et al.‘s study (2013), and a reliability of 0.94 using the retest method in Navidian’s study (2008) [50–52].

To determine the validity of the instruments, content validity was used in a way that the instruments were reviewed by ten members of the scientific board of the Nursing and Midwifery Faculty in Mashhad, and after final revisions, they were utilized. The reliability of the instruments was established by calculating Cronbach’s alpha, which resulted in a 0.90 coefficient after being administered to 20 research units who met the conditions for participating in the study.

The instruments used for data collection were revised and amended as necessary to ensure their suitability for the study population.

Sample size and randomization

To determine the sample size, due to the lack of similar study results available, a pilot study was conducted on 10 people in each group using a mean comparison formula with a 95% confidence coefficient and an 80% test power for all study outcomes. The results of the pilot study were included in the final study to ensure comprehensive analysis and robustness of the data. The highest calculated value was related to stress, estimated at 25 people per group. For greater assurance and to enable comparison in subgroups, accounting for a 20% dropout rate, 30 people were included in each group, making the final sample size 60 people in total.

The participants were randomly assigned to the intervention and control groups using the randomization tool available at http://randomization.com, which ensures unbiased and reproducible group assignments. A concealed allocation approach was utilized with sealed envelopes. Random sequences were written as codes A and B on small cards and placed inside the envelopes. When a hemodialysis patient caregiver meeting the research criteria was identified, an envelope was opened, and the code inside determined the group assignment.

Caregivers were isolated from one another during the study by being assigned based on their geographic location, ensuring no interaction between participants. This isolation method minimized the risk of cross-contamination and maintained the integrity of the study results. Additionally, patients under the caregivers’ supervision were located in single rooms within the medical facility, ensuring that they had no direct physical contact with each other. This further helped in maintaining the study’s rigor and reliability.

Statistical methods

The descriptive statistics (viz., frequency distribution, mean, and standard deviation) were used to describe and categorize the data. Inferential statistics including the Chi-square test, independent-samples t-test, and Mann-Whitney U test were used to test the research hypothesis. Wilcoxon signed-rank test and paired-samples t-tests were further employed for intra-group comparisons. The normality of the quantitative variables was correspondingly assessed by the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. The significance level of 0.05 was set for all the tests in this study.

Linearity: We used scatterplots and the Pearson correlation coefficient to assess the linear relationship between variables. The visual inspection of scatterplots and correlation coefficients confirmed linear relationships for the variables under study.

Homogeneity of Variances: We applied Levene’s test to evaluate the equality of variances across groups. The results of Levene’s test indicated that the assumption of homogeneity of variances was satisfied for all the dimensions of the Zarit Care Burden Interview Scale.

The social dimension also experienced a decrease in the intervention group, with a mean score of 9.26 ± 1.63 pre-intervention dropping to 7.50 ± 2.17 post-intervention. The control group’s score slightly increased from 8.63 ± 2.28 pre-intervention to 8.86 ± 2.38 post-intervention. Both intergroup and intragroup comparisons were significant (P = 0.003 and P < 0.001, respectively).

The emotional dimension followed a similar pattern as the social dimension, with the intervention group’s score decreasing from 17.36 ± 3.06 pre-intervention to 14.63 ± 3.21 post-intervention. The control group’s score increased slightly from 18.40 ± 3.00 to 18.53 ± 3.14. The intergroup comparison was significant post-intervention (P = 0.003), and the intragroup comparison was significant for the intervention group (P < 0.001).

The economic dimension scores stayed relatively stable in both groups, with no significant intergroup or intragroup differences.

Finally, the total care burden score decreased from 54.46 ± 6.22 pre-intervention to 46.16 ± 7.27 post-intervention in the intervention group, while it slightly increased in the control group from 53.50 ± 5.84 to 54.40 ± 5.72. The intergroup comparison was significant post-intervention (P < 0.001), and the intragroup comparison was significant for both groups (P < 0.001). The P values were obtained from Independent-samples t-test, Mann-Whitney U test, Wilcoxon test, and Paired-samples t-test (Table 2).

Discussion

The process of hemodialysis is a multifaceted procedure that has a substantial impact on various aspects of the lives of both patients and caregivers, encompassing physiological, psychological, functional, social, well-being, and lifestyle dimensions. As the demands of caring for dialysis patients increase, caregivers face a greater burden of care and require more support.

The research conducted by Mollaoglu et al. (2013) aimed to explore the impact of educational support on the burden experienced by caregivers of patients receiving hemodialysis in Istanbul, Turkey. The study, which involved 122 caregivers, demonstrated that the training program effectively reduced the individual burden of caregiving experienced by caregivers of hemodialysis patients. The intervention also resulted in a noticeable difference in mean scores related to the individual burden of care between the intervention groups and the control group [53]. Given that chronic kidney failure is a progressive condition, continuous care is essential. Enhancing the individual dimension of care among family caregivers of patients receiving hemodialysis helps alleviate the adverse effects of caregiving, enabling caregivers to provide compassionate and adaptable support throughout this prolonged and challenging period.

Additionally, the results from Chien et al.‘s (2018) study titled “A three-year follow-up RCT of peer support groups for Chinese families of people with schizophrenia” conducted in Hong Kong underscore the effectiveness of family-led peer support groups as an intervention for Chinese individuals with schizophrenia. This intervention yielded long-lasting benefits, including improved functionality and reduced burden on family caregivers [50]. It is important to acknowledge that the training program utilized in this study may differ from the program recommended by our research team due to variations in the nature of patients’ illnesses. Nevertheless, the psychoeducational program employed in this trial also significantly alleviated the burden on family caregivers compared to the conventional program.

In contrast to the present investigation, the findings of the study carried out by Wai Tong Chien et al. (2018) revealed a positive impact of peer support groups on the economic dimension of caregiver burden among individuals with schizophrenia. This particular study thoroughly examined the economic perspective from two significant aspects. Firstly, physicians’ hesitance to implement family intervention may be attributed to concerns regarding the expenses associated with therapists. Secondly, families may exhibit reluctance to engage in a costly program. The type of peer support scrutinized in this study offered a flexible and interactive self-help group environment, with a nine-month intervention period. This approach was deemed more acceptable and feasible in community care settings with limited resources, which likely contributed to the observed improvement in the economic dimension.

The study by Hemmati Masalek Pak et al. (2019) aimed to investigate the impact of psycho-educational intervention on the care burden of patients receiving hemodialysis. The results indicated that, following the educational intervention, the care burden scores of caregivers in the intervention groups (discussion and workshop) significantly decreased in all dimensions, except for the economic dimension, when compared to the control group [54]. This suggests that the educational support program effectively alleviated the care burden, with the exception of the economic aspect. We attribute the lack of impact on the economic dimension to the current state of the country and the increasing inflation rates. The majority of caregivers participating in this study had an average or lower income, which has been significantly affected by the country’s inflation rate, as reported by various economic analyses and reports [55]. Additionally, our study also revealed that education and support alone are insufficient to resolve the economic challenges faced by the families of patients receiving hemodialysis. Therefore, health systems should exert further efforts to provide financial support to these families.

In a separate study, Farhani et al. (2017) conducted a clinical trial to examine the effectiveness of supportive educational programs in alleviating the burden experienced by family caregivers of patients receiving hemodialysis. The study included 76 family caregivers referred to the Shahid Hasheminejad Hemodialysis Center in Tehran. The results demonstrated a reduction in the burden experienced by caregivers following the intervention in the intervention group [56].

The limitations of this study include its restriction to a single hemodialysis center, the omission of certain dimensions of care burden such as the spiritual dimension, and the exclusive focus on primary caregivers. For future research, it is suggested to conduct multicenter studies with larger sample sizes to validate the findings of this study. Furthermore, exploring the impact of peer support groups on other dimensions of care burden, such as the spiritual dimension, and assessing their effect on secondary and other informal caregivers can be beneficial. Additionally, examining the impact of peer support groups on caregivers of patients with other chronic conditions and conducting qualitative studies to gain a deeper understanding of caregivers’ experiences and the impact of peer support groups on them are recommended.

Conclusion

The findings of this study suggest that peer support groups can play a role in reducing the care burden of caregivers of patients receiving hemodialysis, particularly in the physical, social, and emotional dimensions. Statistically significant reductions in these areas were observed in the intervention group compared to the control group. However, the impact on the economic dimension was limited, indicating the need for additional economic support mechanisms from healthcare systems. While the results are promising, they should be interpreted with caution due to the study’s limitations, including its single-center design and relatively small sample size.

The study highlights the potential benefits of peer-driven educational and supportive programs in enhancing caregivers’ coping strategies and overall well-being. These findings underscore the importance of integrating social and educational support into healthcare programs for chronic disease management. Nevertheless, more robust research, including multi-center studies with larger sample sizes, is needed to confirm these results and explore the long-term sustainability and broader applicability of such interventions. Future research should also investigate other dimensions of care burden, such as the spiritual dimension, and assess the impact of peer support on secondary caregivers.

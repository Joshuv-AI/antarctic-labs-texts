Y-Polyp: research on devices for detecting colorectal polyps with limited samples
Authors: Jingjing Wan, Shanshan Wang, Wenjie Zhu, Bolun Chen, Zhe Li, Ling Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundsColonoscopy plays a crucial role in preventing the malignant transformation of colorectal polyps, with early diagnosis and detection of colorectal cancer being effective approaches to reducing incidence and mortality rates among patients. With the rise of neural networks, research on computer-aided detection of colorectal polyps has garnered increasing attention. However, existing computer-aided diagnostic systems are constrained by insufficient training sample sizes, making it difficult to train high-performance systems.MethodsThis paper proposes the Y-Polyp model for colorectal polyp detection. By employing a parallel strategy with multidimensional attention mechanisms, this model enables convolutional kernels to learn more flexible attention across four spatial dimensions, thus fully capturing target features from limited data samples. Additionally, the model filters conflicting information in spatial domains, suppressing inconsistent features and addressing inconsistencies among features at different scales. To validate the effectiveness of the Y-Polyp model, extensive experiments are conducted and evaluated using a Kvasir-SEG dataset.ResultsThe experimental results show that the Y-Polyp model significantly improves the accuracy in the detection of colorectal polyps. Specifically, the overall precision of the polyp detection model has increased by 3%, while the recall rate and average precision have increased by 2.7%, 2% and 2.7%, respectively.ConclusionsThis method exhibits strong generalization ability and robustness, adapting to various colonic structures and changes in target appearance, and the proposed colorectal polyp detection approach thus possesses wide applicability and reliability.Keywords: Polyp detection, Feature fusion, Dynamic convolution, YOLOv5

Background

Colonic polyps are common colon diseases, typically manifesting as small growths on the mucosal surface, with their incidence in the colon increasing annually, while colorectal cancer has become the second leading cause of cancer-related deaths in the United States [1]. However, the likelihood of polyp malignancy can be effectively prevented through efficient screening methods. Colonoscopy is one of the most commonly used early colonic polyp detection methods, which involves inserting a flexible fiberoptic scope or camera tube (colonoscope) into the colon to directly observe and examine the mucosa of the colon, identify and locate colonic polyps and perform biopsy or removal. This colonoscopic examination, as a successful preventive procedure, has reduced the incidence of colorectal cancer by 30% [2].

Although colonoscopy is considered the gold standard for screening colorectal cancer, the presence of special structures such as colonic folds, bends and corners makes it difficult to detect small and flat polyps. Additionally, limitations in the field of view, resolution and histological differentiation of conventional colonoscopes contribute to a certain rate of missed diagnoses in overall polyp and adenoma detection, with the missed diagnosis rate of conventional colonoscopy estimated to be approximately 20% to 25% [3]. While colonoscopy can reduce the risk of colorectal cancer, it cannot completely eliminate it. Moreover, due to individual differences in polyp characteristics and endoscopist skill levels, coupled with factors such as visual fatigue, decreased attention, cognitive biases and subjectivity, doctors may lack sensitivity to polyp features, leading to a certain degree of missed diagnosis. If polyps that are missed during examination are not detected in a timely manner, they may progress to late-stage diagnosed colorectal cancer, with a survival rate of less than 10% [4–6]. Therefore, it is crucial to take effective measures to reduce the number of missed polyps, improve the quality of colonoscopic detection and ensure early detection and accurate diagnosis of colonic polyps to prevent the development of colorectal cancer. Currently, there is growing interest in using artificial intelligence as an auxiliary tool in the medical field and the use of deep learning technology for colorectal polyp detection and classification has become an active research area in recent years [7]. By combining neural networks (CNN) with medical image classification and detection tasks, this approach overcomes the artificial errors associated with traditional detection methods while also addressing the challenges posed by the unique structures of the colon, thereby improving the accuracy of colorectal polyp detection and ensuring high-quality polyp detection [8–10].

However, during the process of colonoscopy, the challenge of image quality is exacerbated by factors such as the shape, color, size, texture and irregularity of polyps. Additionally, considering the rarity and difficult accessibility of colonic polyps, acquiring a large number of annotated colonic polyp image data is a challenging and time-consuming task. This makes it even more challenging to extract effective polyp features from colonoscopic images and increases the difficulty of target detection [11]. Moreover, traditional colonoscopic methods are ineffective in detecting lesions within the small intestine and reliance solely on manually crafted features may lead to prediction errors, thereby increasing the incidence of colorectal cancer [12]. Therefore, to meet the practical needs of medical image processing and analysis, further research and exploration are urgently needed to improve the extraction of effective polyp features and enhance the accuracy of target detection.

To better understand how existing techniques address these challenges, it is necessary to review both traditional computer-aided detection methods and recent deep learning-based approaches.

Traditional detection methods

Colorectal cancer (CRC) accounts for 10% of cancer incidence and is the third leading cause of cancer-related deaths globally [13]. With the increasing incidence of CRC worldwide, countries such as the United States, New Zealand and Australia have implemented effective prevention techniques to reduce the incidence and mortality rates of colorectal cancer [14]. Currently, comm on medical screening methods rely on the operator’s skill level to detect adenomas. However, research has shown that the missed diagnosis rate of adenomas not only varies among endoscopists with high adenoma detection rates (ADR) but is also closely associated with the number of adenomas detected in each colonoscopy examination [15]. Therefore, traditional colonoscopic examinations cannot meet the current practical demands of medical image processing and analysis. With the development of computer-aided diagnosis (CAD) artificial intelligence systems, there is also a new situation in addressing the accuracy issues in polyp detection. Liu et al., evaluated the accuracy of artificial intelligence (AI) assisted image classifiers in lesion resection based on non-magnifying endoscopic images. The experimental results showed higher accuracy in AI predictions of the images [16]. Lu et al. evaluated the depth of invasion of (CRC) using a dual-modal deep learning system based on white light (WL) and image-enhanced endoscopy (IEE). The results indicated that the Endo-CRC system (combining WL and IEE images) has tremendous potential in assisting the diagnosis of CRC invasion depth [17]. Karkanis et al., proposed a method for tumor detection in colonoscopy videos.

This method detects polyps based on wavelet transform of polyp color and texture, achieving a specificity of 97% and sensitivity of 90% [18]. Ameling et al. proposed a texture-based polyp inspection method. This approach addresses the issues of quantity and quality of images for classifier training and evaluation by employing four texture feature extraction methods based on gray-level co-occurrence and local binary patterns [19]. Hwang et al., proposed a method for polyp detection in colonoscopy examination videos using elliptical shape features. This method overcomes the limitations of fixed-size analysis windows associated with using texture features and reduces reliance on the accuracy of image training sets [20]. Klare et al., conducted a study on the application of real-time automated polyp detection software (APDS) under routine colonoscopy conditions. The software’s functionality involves highlighting potential polyp lesions in real-time colonoscopy examinations using color coding and testing the feasibility of deploying APDS under real-time conditions. The study found that computer-assisted automatic low-latency polyp detection is feasible, but improvements are still needed, particularly in addressing smaller and flatter polyps [21].

Although traditional colorectal polyp detection models have made some progress, they still face several challenges. Specifically, these methods typically rely on features such as color, texture, and shape for detection and segmentation. However, due to the limitations of traditional models, they may fail to capture subtle features of polyps, leading to lower accuracy and recall rates. For instance, color-based methods may be insufficiently sensitive to polyps with colors similar to the surrounding mucosa, making detection difficult. Additionally, texture and shape-based methods may struggle with complex polyp morphologies and irregular structures. These methods often overlook the variations in polyp features across different angles and magnifications, making it difficult to handle multi-scale features, particularly for small or flat polyps. Moreover, polyps that resemble the appearance of the colon wall and are obscured in colonoscopic video are often treated as hidden objects. This leads to confusion during inference, resulting in high rates of false positives or false negatives. Such issues can hinder clinical decision-making and reduce the reliability of colorectal polyp detection. Furthermore, the detection of small and flat polyps remains limited by visual observation, as these types of polyps may be less conspicuous in colonoscopic images and easily overlooked, making accurate detection challenging. Traditional methods often misinterpret small or low-contrast polyps as image noise or background interference. Therefore, existing methods still fall short in enhancing the detection capability and accuracy for these specific types of polyps.

Deep learning-based on detection methods

With the rapid advancement of deep learning, computer-aided polyp recognition has demonstrated high-quality analysis and low-cost potential in colorectal screening and detection. Artificial intelligence systems based on deep learning can leverage large amounts of data, automatically learn classification patterns and extract features from complex unlabeled data, thereby reducing the need for manual intervention. In the medical field, particularly in diagnostic colonoscopy examinations, artificial intelligence systems based on deep learning technology are continuously evolving [22]. Mo et al. ’s study employed an unmodified convolutional neural network (Faster R-CNN) architecture for polyp detection. In contrast to previous methods, their model is capable of detecting the majority of obscured polyps [23]. Shin et al., proposed a region-based convolutional neural network (CNN) method for automated detection of colorectal polyps.

The study utilized a deep convolutional neural network (Inception ResNet) as a transfer learning approach to overcome the challenges of limited polyp images and detection obstacles. Additionally, two transformer-learning methods, automatic false positive learning and offline learning, were introduced to further optimize the model and reduce false positives, enabling reliable polyp detection in combination with the region-based detection system [24]. Karaman et al. proposed a Robust real-time polyp detection system design based on YOLO algorithms by optimizing activation functions and hyper-parameters with artificial bee colony (ABC) algorithm. This approach identifies the optimal configuration for YOLOv5, significantly improving the model’s detection accuracy and efficiency [25]. Pacal et al. proposed a real-time polyp detection algorithm based on improved YOLOv3 and YOLOv4 algorithms, which combines several key technologies to achieve high-performance, real-time automatic polyp detection. Firstly, real-time, high-performance automatic polyp detection is achieved through the integration of Cross Stage Partial Network (CSPNet). Secondly, advanced data augmentation techniques and transfer learning are utilized to enhance polyp detection performance. Lastly, the Sigmoid Weighted Linear Unit (SiLU) activation function is employed instead of Leaky ReLU and Mish activation functions to improve the accuracy and real-time performance of the model in polyp detection [11]. Pascal et al., proposed a robust real-time deep learning-based automatic polyp detection system.

This method enhances the YOLOv4 architecture by incorporating Cross Stage Partial Networks (CSPNet), the Mish activation function, and the Distance Intersection over Union (DIoU) loss function. Furthermore, the system integrates various structures, including ResNet, VGG, DarkNet53, and Transformers, to further improve detection performance [26]. Yang et al. proposed a randomly efficient automatic polyp detection and segmentation network called channel attention network (sECANet), which enhances the model’s detection accuracy by acquiring inter-channel interactions to learn effective channel attention [27]. Zhang et al. proposed a computer-aided polyp recognition algorithm called SSD-GPNet, which is based on an improved Single Shot Multibox Detector (SSD) [28]. This algorithm improves the accuracy and efficiency of the detector by reusing the data lost in the pooling layers and linking it to additional feature maps [29]. Puyal et al. proposed a dual-path convolutional neural network algorithm named DP-CNN for enhancing post-image polyp detection. This algorithm utilizes the DP-CNN architecture and a sigmoid classifier.

Through the combination of this structure and classifier, the algorithm achieved satisfactory results on publicly available datasets [30]. Park et al. proposed a novel model called SwinE-Net, which combines the EfficientNet and Swin Transformer algorithms for accurate and robust colorectal polyp segmentation [31]. Wang et al. proposed a novel prototype alignment network named PANet, which addresses the challenging few-shot segmentation problem from the perspective of metric learning. This enables it to better utilize information from the support set and improve the performance of few-shot segmentation through prototype alignment [32]. Pacal et al. proposed a novel Swin transformer approach utilizing residual multi-layer perceptron for diagnosing brain tumors in MRI images. This algorithm introduces the Hybrid Shifted Windows Multi-Head Self-Attention module (HSW-MSA) and Residual Multi-Layer Perceptron (ResMLP), which improves the Swin Transformer architecture by enhancing classification accuracy, reducing memory usage, and simplifying training complexity [33]. Wesp et al. utilized 3D CT colonography images for training and developed two models: the SEG model and the noSEG model, for predicting polyp categories. Unlike previous methods, the SEG model also utilized polyp segmentation masks during training to enhance prediction accuracy. The contribution of this study lies in the adoption of sub-convolutions training and segmentation masks, providing a novel approach for accurately predicting polyp categories [34]. Ma et al. proposed a real-time polyp detection framework specialized for colonoscopy videos. This framework integrates Swin Transformer with YOLOv5m network to enhance the extraction capability of both local and global information regarding polyp features. Additionally, to mitigate the influence of factors such as changes in lighting and reflections, the study also employs a time-series ensemble prediction method, thereby improving the temporal continuity of the detection results [28].

Limitations of existing methods and contributions of this work

Despite significant advancements in polyp detection performance achieved by improved methods such as YOLOv3 and YOLOv4, sECANet, and SSD-GPNet, these approaches typically rely on large amounts of annotated data for training. Due to the scarcity of training samples, these models may fail to fully learn all variations of polyp features, leading to unstable performance in practical applications. Furthermore, many advanced models, such as SwinE-Net and PANet, enhance performance by increasing network complexity, which also results in a substantial increase in computational demands. To meet the needs of practical medical image processing and analysis, there is an urgent need for further research and exploration to improve the extraction of effective polyp features and enhance the accuracy of target detection. Methods

Colonoscopy examination, as one of the pivotal means for colorectal polyp screening, holds significant importance in observing and diagnosing polyps within the colon and is the most common measure for preventing colorectal cancer. However, in the actual detection process, there exist individual differences in the technical proficiency of endoscopists. Moreover, considering the rarity and challenging accessibility of colorectal polyps, acquiring a large number of annotated colorectal polyp image data is a challenging and time-consuming task. Traditional colonoscopy screening methods struggle to address these issues. Therefore, this paper proposes an improved colon polyp detection model to tackle these challenges. The overall schematic diagram of the model is illustrated in Fig. 1.

Schematic diagram of the colorectal polyp detection model structure

The model first applies an adaptive spatial filtering and fusion strategy to the effective feature layers (P3, P4 and P5) outputted by the backbone network. This strategy involves spatially filtering conflicting information to suppress inconsistent features, thereby enhancing feature fusion effectiveness and addressing the inconsistency issue among features of different scales. This process ultimately improves the accuracy and performance of colorectal polyp detection. Additionally, ODC3 modules are designed at each stage of the feature prediction heads. These modules scan the global image to obtain candidate target regions and dynamically adjust the shape and size of convolutional kernels based on the features of input data. This dynamic adjustment allows the convolution operation to better handle differences across various dimensions of input data and provides improved performance and rich contextual information.

Feature fusion

In Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), feature pyramids are an effective method for achieving scale invariance. However, for single-stage detectors based on feature pyramids, inconsistencies between features of different scales are a major limiting factor. To address this issue, this paper introduces a pyramid feature fusion strategy that filters conflicting information in the spatial domain to suppress inconsistent features, thereby enhancing the effectiveness of feature fusion. This is particularly crucial for polyp detection in medical imaging, where polyps can vary significantly in size and shape. By employing this approach, the method ensures efficient information transfer and feature sharing across different scales, significantly improving overall detection performance. In this study, an adaptive spatial filtering and fusion strategy is applied to the effective feature layers (P3, P4 and P5) extracted from the backbone network to enhance colorectal polyp detection accuracy. Firstly, to handle feature maps at different scales, a scale alignment strategy is employed. Specifically, during the upsampling process, feature maps at different scales are adjusted to the same resolution through convolutional layers and interpolation. In this process, convolutional layers are used to reduce the channel dimensions of features to the corresponding level, followed by interpolation to increase the resolution. For down sampling, convolutional layers with a stride of 2 are utilized to adjust both channel dimensions and resolution. Additionally, at the finest scale, a max-pooling layer with a stride of 2 is introduced. Through the scale alignment strategy, feature maps at different scales can interact harmoniously within the model and better adapt to various task requirements.

Furthermore, this paper introduces an adaptive fusion mechanism to address the fusion issue of features. During the fusion process, the importance of features is adaptively adjusted based on the feature information at each position. For features carrying contradictory information, they are filtered out to eliminate conflicts. Conversely, for features with more discriminative clues, they are enhanced to increase their importance. Dynamic convolution

The convolutional layer is one of the fundamental building blocks of modern deep neural networks and has achieved unprecedented success in various computer vision tasks. However, for the task of polyp detection studied in this paper, the performance improvement relies more on increasing the model size and capacity, as well as expanding the dataset size. Due to the rarity and challenging nature of obtaining annotated colon polyp images, acquiring a large volume of labeled data is a challenging and time-consuming task. Although increasing the model size can further enhance performance, it also leads to computational overload issues. To mitigate this problem, this paper introduces a dynamic convolution (ODC3) module at each stage of the feature prediction head. Initially, the model scans the entire global image to obtain crucial candidate regions of interest. Then, with the designed ODC3 module, the model dynamically adjusts the shape and size of the convolutional kernels based on the input data features to adapt to different polyp input data. Such design considerations encompass spatial dimensions, input channels, output channels, as well as the shape and size of the convolutional kernels, allowing for better adaptation to various input data. Through this approach, the model can effectively utilize information from limited data samples, thereby improving its adaptability and performance. The ODC3 module is illustrated in Fig. 3.

Results

Dataset introduction

The study utilized the publicly available Kvasir-SEG dataset, which contains 1,100 images of intestinal polyps, as experimental data. Each sample exhibits various shapes and characteristics, representing different morphologies of intestinal polyps. The dataset also includes polyp cases from different patients to ensure diversity and representativeness. To ensure the accuracy and generalization of the model, the dataset was split in an 8:2 ratio for training and validation. Additionally, the data were preprocessed to conform to the required textual format for the experiment, and the annotation results were converted into textual format. The annotations include a unique ID, positional coordinates, and size information for each intestinal polyp. A schematic diagram of the dataset is shown in Fig. 4.

Images of polyps of different types. Figure (a) presents images of small and flat polyps within the colonic structure, which may be challenging to detect upon visual inspection. Figures (b) and (c) display images of conventional colorectal polyps, including typical polyp samples. Figures (d), (e) and (f) showcase images of polyps that are difficult to discern with the naked eye, characterized by blurry edges or subtle features

The presentation of these image samples aids researchers in observing and analyzing different types of polyps, further understanding their characteristics and variations. Through these images, deep learning models can learn and identify various types of polyps, thereby enhancing the accuracy and efficiency of colorectal screening and detection.

Network configuration

In this experiment, a computer equipped with a Tesla V100–SXM2 GPU was used as the hardware platform. As for the software platform, Python programming language and Pytorch deep learning framework were employed, with versions Python 3.8 and Pytorch 1.8.1, respectively. For training and evaluation, a Kvasir-SEG dataset was chosen as the target detection dataset, containing samples of only one class of objects. In the experimental setup, stochastic gradient descent was utilized for model parameterization, with the SGD optimizer employed for training. To control the stability and speed of training, the learning rate was set to 0.01. Finally, metrics such as accuracy, recall and mean average precision (mAP) were used to evaluate the performance of object detection. These metrics were computed to quantitatively assess the model.

Analysis of polyp detection results

Comparison of baseline model and improved model detection results

Discussion

This study aimed to propose an efficient deep learning model, Y-Polyp, for colorectal polyp detection, achieving high-precision detection under conditions of limited data samples. Experimental results demonstrate that Y-Polyp outperforms the traditional YOLOv5 model and other state-of-the-art object detection models (e.g., YOLOv3, YOLOv4, YOLOv6n, YOLOv8n) across key metrics, including precision, recall, and mean average precision (mAP), highlighting its notable advantage in settings with scarce medical imaging data.

The introduction of the adaptively spatial feature fusion (ASFF) strategy effectively alleviates inconsistencies among feature maps of different scales. In colorectal polyp detection, polyps exhibit significant variability in size, morphology, and edge characteristics. Traditional single-scale or simple feature fusion approaches often lead to information loss or feature confusion when detecting small or morphologically complex polyps, resulting in missed or false detections. By adaptively weighting the importance of feature maps across spatial domains, Y-Polyp retains critical information while filtering conflicting signals during feature fusion, substantially enhancing sensitivity to small polyps and subtle edge features. Experimental results indicate that incorporating the ASFF module alone increases precision by approximately 3.2%, underscoring its critical role in improving feature representation and information transfer. The dynamic convolution (ODC3) module leverages a multi-dimensional attention mechanism to adaptively adjust convolutional kernels, allowing the model to dynamically modify spatial dimensions, input channels, output channels, and the number of kernels according to the input data. This design improves the model’s capacity to capture complex polyp morphologies, enhances the receptive field, and mitigates performance instability arising from limited training samples. By allocating attention across spatial, channel, filter, and kernel dimensions, the ODC3 module enables more flexible feature learning. This capability is particularly advantageous when processing polyps with blurred edges or weak features, allowing the model to accurately localize potential polyp regions and reduce missed detections (as illustrated in Fig. 6).

Comparative and ablation experiments further validated the contributions of the ASFF and ODC3 modules to overall model performance. Table 3 shows that when both modules are integrated, the model achieves a precision of 87.2%, a recall of 88.0%, and an mAP@0.5 of 0.899. While either module alone provides improvements, the combined effect surpasses individual contributions, indicating complementary roles in feature representation and spatial attention, thereby enhancing Y-Polyp’s detection capability under limited-sample conditions.

Compared with existing methods, Y-Polyp achieves an advantageous balance between efficiency and performance. Table 5 demonstrates that, with a relatively low number of parameters and GFLOPs, Y-Polyp attains a frame rate (FPS) of 62.89, close to YOLOv5s (62.5 FPS), while simultaneously exceeding YOLOv5, YOLOv3, and YOLOv4 in precision, recall, and mAP@0.5. These results indicate that Y-Polyp can maintain both high detection accuracy and computational efficiency, offering a feasible solution for real-time colonoscopy polyp detection in clinical settings.

Additionally, Y-Polyp exhibits strong robustness in handling image interference. Variations in illumination, reflections from the colonic wall, and fluid occlusion during colonoscopy can introduce bright regions or blurred edges, potentially leading to false positives or missed detections in traditional models. Y-Polyp effectively suppresses conflicting information through spatial filtering and multi-dimensional attention, accurately focusing on polyp regions and enhancing detection reliability in challenging environments.

Despite its strong performance with limited data, Y-Polyp has several limitations. First, this study relied primarily on the Kvasir-SEG dataset for training and evaluation. Given the dataset’s limited size and sample distribution, the model’s generalizability to large-scale, multi-center datasets remains to be validated. Second, the current model focuses on polyp detection without addressing polyp malignancy classification, which is critical for clinical diagnosis and treatment decision-making. Third, although Y-Polyp demonstrates favorable computational efficiency, deployment in resource-constrained mobile or embedded devices may still pose challenges, necessitating further optimization or model compression strategies.

Future work can expand in several directions. Multi-center and multi-modal datasets could be used to improve model generalization and clinical applicability. Integration of Y-Polyp with a polyp classification module could enable an end-to-end system for simultaneous detection and classification, providing a more comprehensive clinical decision support tool. Model compression, knowledge distillation, and lightweight network architectures could further enhance efficiency for deployment on mobile or embedded platforms. Additionally, incorporating temporal information and video analysis methods could improve the continuity and stability of polyp detection in colonoscopy videos, reducing missed and false detections.

In summary, the proposed Y-Polyp model, through multi-scale feature fusion and dynamic convolution mechanisms, achieves enhanced precision and efficiency in colorectal polyp detection under limited training data conditions. Experimental results indicate robust performance for small polyps, polyps with subtle features, and images under complex illumination, while maintaining a favorable balance between computational complexity and frame rate. Although further validation on larger and multi-center datasets is required, Y-Polyp provides a novel technical approach for colorectal polyp detection and lays the foundation for the development of intelligent colonoscopy-assisted diagnostic systems.

Conclusion

The aim of this study is to apply deep learning techniques to colorectal polyp detection and propose a model named Y-Polyp, which achieves accurate colorectal polyp detection through feature fusion strategies and dynamic fusion methods. Firstly, the model filters conflicting information in the spatial domain to suppress inconsistent features and resolve the inconsistency between features of different scales. Additionally, the model employs a multi-dimensional attention mechanism and parallel strategy to enable convolutional kernels to learn more flexible attention across four dimensions in space. This mechanism enables the model to fully learn target features with limited data samples. Experimental results demonstrate satisfactory performance of this approach in colorectal polyp detection tasks with insufficient data samples. Whether dealing with polyp images with blurry edges and weak features, or small and flat polyps, the model accurately detects colorectal polyps. Furthermore, this method exhibits strong generalization ability and robustness, adapting to various colonic structures and changes in target appearance. Therefore, the proposed colorectal polyp detection method possesses wide applicability and reliability. Future research directions will focus on further optimizing the model’s speed and accuracy to improve the accuracy and efficiency of colorectal screening. This will provide better diagnostic tools for doctors, which is crucial for increasing the early detection rate and treatment success rate of colorectal cancer.

Generalizing the Dirac-Majorana Confusion Theorem: The Role of CP-Violating Phases in New Physics Vector Interactions
David Delepine, A. Yebra

Abstract
The “Practical Dirac-Majorana Confusion Theorem” said that phenomenological differences between Dirac and Majorana neutrinos are suppressed by ( m ν / E ) 2 (m_{\nu}/E)^{2} in lepton-number-conserving processes, making them experimentally indistinguishable at high energies. In this work, we propose a generalization of this theorem by introducing a New Physics vector boson ( Z ′ Z^{\prime} ) with CP-violating couplings. We demonstrate that the Majorana condition imposes that only CP-violating imaginary part contributes to the vector neutral interaction. Consequently, the difference between Dirac and Majorana neutrinos in cross-section becomes directly dependent on the CP-violating phase ϕ \phi . We apply this framework to Coherent Elastic Neutrino-Nucleus Scattering (CE ν \nu NS), showing that for spin-zero targets, the distinguishability of the neutrino nature is determined by the CP structure of the interaction.



I
Introduction

The determination of the fundamental nature of the neutrino( Dirac fermion (
\nu\neq\bar{\nu}
) or a Majorana fermion (
\nu=\nu^{c}
)) is one of the most important questions in particle physics. While the observation of Neutrinoless Double Beta Decay (
0\nu\beta\beta
)
Furry1939
;
Schechter1982
;
Rodejohann2011
;
Dolinski2019
;
KamLAND-Zen2023
would unambiguously confirm the Majorana nature by proving the violation of total lepton number (
L
), constraints from current experiments remain inconclusive.

In the absence of
0\nu\beta\beta
detection, it is possible to distinguish Dirac and Majorana neutrinos through scattering experiments, such as elastic neutrino-electron scattering (
\nu e^{-}\to\nu e^{-}
) or Coherent Elastic Neutrino-Nucleus Scattering (CE
\nu
NS). However, the
Practical Dirac-Majorana Confusion Theorem
(DMCT), as demonstrated by Kayser and Shrock
Kayser:1982jw
;
Kayser:1982wd
;
Rosen:1982pj
, makes it practically impossible at experimental level. The theorem states that for any process conserving lepton number, the difference between Dirac and Majorana cross-sections is proportional to the square of the neutrino mass relative to the energy scale:

\sigma_{\text{Majorana}}-\sigma_{\text{Dirac}}\propto\left(\frac{m_{\nu}}{E_{\nu}}\right)^{2}.

(1)

Given the sub-eV scale of neutrino masses and the MeV-GeV energies of typical experiments, this difference is of the order
\mathcal{O}(10^{-14})
, making the two cases experimentally indistinguishable within the Standard Model (SM).

However, the DMCT relies on the assumption that the interactions are purely Left-Handed (
V-A
) and that the couplings are real. In this paper, we explore a scenario that evades the standard confusion theorem: the existence of New Physics (NP) mediated by a neutral vector boson
Z^{\prime}
with
complex
couplings that violate CP symmetry.

We assume an effective interaction Lagrangian where the vector (
g_{V}
) and axial (
g_{A}
) couplings can carry CP-violating phases but in case of Majorana neutrinos, one has to add the Feynman diagram with antineutrinos as
\nu=\nu^{c}
.

This creates a phenomenological difference that is not suppressed by the neutrino mass. If we define the effective vector coupling as
g_{V}^{\text{eff}}=g_{\text{SM}}+\epsilon e^{i\phi}
, where
\phi
is a CP-violating phase:

•

A
Dirac neutrino
interacts via the full modulus
|g_{V}^{\text{eff}}|^{2}
, dominated by the Standard Model interference.

•

A
Majorana neutrino
couples only to
\operatorname{Im}(g_{V}^{\text{eff}})
.

This leads to a generalized version of the confusion theorem where the distinguishability parameter is the CP phase
\phi
rather than the mass
m_{\nu}
. In the limit of maximal CP violation (
\phi\to\pi/2
), the Majorana cross-section becomes accessible, whereas in the CP-conserving limit, the neutral vector contribution vanishes entirely as in the Standard Model.

In Section 2, we develop the formalism for the effective Majorana couplings. Section 3 presents the calculation of the differential cross-sections for
\nu-e
scattering and CE
\nu
NS. Finally, in Section 4, we discuss the experimental implications for current and future detectors such as COHERENT and DUNE.

II
Formalism: Effective Majorana Couplings with CP Violation

We consider a simplified extension of the Standard Model where a new neutral vector boson
Z^{\prime}
interacts with neutrinos via both vector (
V
) and axial-vector (
A
) currents. Allowing for CP violation in the new physics sector, we define the complex coupling constants
g_{V}
and
g_{A}
.

The general interaction Lagrangian, written in terms of the neutrino field
\nu
, is given by:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}=-\bar{\nu}\gamma^{\mu}(g_{V}-g_{A}\gamma^{5})\nu Z^{\prime}_{\mu}+\text{h.c.}

(2)

where h.c. denotes the Hermitian conjugate.

II.1
Majorana Constraints

The scattering amplitude is given by:

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}(\nu_{l}e\rightarrow\nu_{l}e)&=-i\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}\overline{u}_{e}^{f}\gamma^{\mu}(g_{V}^{e}-g_{A}^{e}\gamma^{5})u_{e}^{i}\\
&\quad\times\begin{cases}\overline{u}_{\nu_{l}}^{f}\gamma_{\mu}(g_{V}-g_{A}\gamma^{5})u_{\nu_{l}}^{i}&(\text{Dirac})^{\nu}\\
\overline{u}_{\nu_{l}}^{f}\gamma_{\mu}(g_{V}-g_{A}\gamma^{5})u_{\nu_{l}}^{i}\\
\quad-\overline{v}_{\nu_{l}}^{f}\gamma_{\mu}(g_{V}^{*}-g_{A}^{*}\gamma^{5})v_{\nu_{l}}^{i}&(\text{Majorana})^{\nu}\end{cases}\end{split}

(3)

where the indices
i
and
f
denote the initial and final states, respectively, and
g_{A,V}^{e}
are the charged lepton couplings. Using the following identity in the case of a Majorana neutrino:

\overline{v}_{\nu_{l}}^{f}\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma^{5})v_{\nu_{l}}^{i}=\overline{u}_{\nu_{l}}^{f}\gamma_{\mu}(1+\gamma^{5})u_{\nu_{l}}^{i}

(4)

we obtain the selection rules for Majorana neutrinos:

•

Vector Current:
The amplitude is proportional to
(g_{V}-g_{V}^{*})
.

•

Axial Current:
The amplitude is proportional to
(g_{A}+g_{A}^{*})
.

II.2
Effective Couplings

We can thus define the effective couplings for a Majorana neutrino as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\text{Maj}}

\displaystyle=g_{V}-g_{V}^{*}=2i\operatorname{Im}(g_{V}),

(5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\text{Maj}}

\displaystyle=g_{A}+g_{A}^{*}=2\operatorname{Re}(g_{A}).

(6)

In the Standard Model,
g_{V}
is real, leading to
\mathcal{V}_{\text{Maj}}=0
, consistent with the standard result that Majorana neutrinos have no vector interactions. However, if the New Physics introduces a CP-violating phase
\phi
such that
g_{V}=|g|e^{i\phi}
, the Majorana neutrino acquires a non-zero, purely imaginary vector coupling:

\mathcal{V}_{\text{Maj}}\propto i|g|\sin(\phi).

(7)

In contrast, a Dirac neutrino interacts via the full couplings
g_{L,R}=g_{V}\pm g_{A}
, sensitive to both real and imaginary parts. The difference in the effective couplings leads to distinguishable phenomenological signatures in scattering cross-sections.

III
Scattering Cross-Sections and Phenomenological Consequences

We now apply the effective coupling formalism to two distinct scattering processes: elastic neutrino-electron scattering (ES) and Coherent Elastic Neutrino-Nucleus Scattering (CE
\nu
NS). We assume the limit
E_{\nu}\gg m_{\nu}
, where the kinematic mass suppression from the standard confusion theorem is negligible (
\mathcal{O}(10^{-14})
), allowing us to isolate the effects induced purely by the CP-violating phases.

III.1
Elastic Neutrino-Electron Scattering (
\nu e^{-}\to\nu e^{-}
)

The differential cross-section with respect to the electron recoil energy
T
is given by
Kayser:1982wd
;
Kayser:1982jw
:

\frac{d\sigma}{dT}=\frac{2G_{F}^{2}m_{e}}{\pi}\left[|g_{L}|^{2}+|g_{R}|^{2}\left(1-\frac{T}{E_{\nu}}\right)^{2}-\frac{m_{e}T}{E_{\nu}^{2}}\operatorname{Re}(g_{L}g_{R}^{*})\right],

(8)

where
g_{L}
and
g_{R}
are the effective chiral couplings.

III.1.1
Dirac Case

For a Dirac neutrino, the couplings are simply the sum of the Standard Model (SM) and New Physics (NP) contributions:

g_{L}^{D}=g_{V}^{\text{tot}}+g_{A}^{\text{tot}},\quad g_{R}^{D}=g_{V}^{\text{tot}}-g_{A}^{\text{tot}}.

(9)

Since
g_{V}^{\text{tot}}
contains both real (SM) and imaginary (NP) components, generally
|g_{L}^{D}|\neq|g_{R}^{D}|
. This results in an asymmetric spectral shape dependent on the interference term between the SM and the CP-violating phase.

III.1.2
Majorana Case

For a Majorana neutrino, we use the effective couplings derived in Eqs. (
5
) and (
6
). The chiral couplings become:

\displaystyle g_{L}^{M}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{V}_{\text{Maj}}+\mathcal{A}_{\text{Maj}}=2i\operatorname{Im}(g_{V})+2\operatorname{Re}(g_{A}),

(10)

\displaystyle g_{R}^{M}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{V}_{\text{Maj}}-\mathcal{A}_{\text{Maj}}=2i\operatorname{Im}(g_{V})-2\operatorname{Re}(g_{A}).

(11)

The magnitudes of the chiral couplings are identical:

|g_{L}^{M}|^{2}=|g_{R}^{M}|^{2}=4\left[(\operatorname{Im}g_{V})^{2}+(\operatorname{Re}g_{A})^{2}\right].

(12)

Consequently, the differential cross-section factorizes into:

\left(\frac{d\sigma}{dT}\right)_{\text{Maj}}\propto\left[(\operatorname{Im}g_{V})^{2}+(\operatorname{Re}g_{A})^{2}\right]\left[1+\left(1-\frac{T}{E_{\nu}}\right)^{2}\right].

(13)

This predicts a distinct spectral shape compared to the Dirac case.

III.2
Coherent Elastic Neutrino-Nucleus Scattering (CE
\nu
NS)

III.2.1
The Exclusive Neutral Current Nature of CE
\nu
NS

Coherent Elastic Neutrino-Nucleus Scattering (CE
\nu
NS) is strictly a Neutral Current (NC) process, defined by the reaction
\nu+A\to\nu+A
, where the nucleus remains in its ground state
Freedman:1973yd
. Charged Current (CC) interactions are kinematically forbidden or experimentally distinguishable in the typical energy regimes of CE
\nu
NS experiments (
E_{\nu}\lesssim 50
MeV).

For muon and tau neutrinos, the incident energy is insufficient to produce the corresponding heavy charged leptons (
m_{\mu}\approx 105
MeV,
m_{\tau}\approx 1.77
GeV). For electron neutrinos, while CC interactions (
\nu_{e}+n\to p+e^{-}
) are kinematically allowed, they necessarily induce nuclear transmutation, rendering the process inelastic and distinguishable from the coherent elastic signal.

Consequently, CE
\nu
NS proceeds exclusively via the exchange of neutral bosons (
Z
or
Z^{\prime}
). The observation of CE
\nu
NS events is therefore governed solely by the axial contribution and by the CP-violating imaginary phase of the New Physics sector for the vectorial contribution, making this channel a uniquely sensitive probe for the scenario proposed in this work.

In CE
\nu
NS, the cross-section is proportional to the square of the weak nuclear charge
Q_{W}
, which depends predominantly on the vector coupling
Freedman:1973yd
;
Scholberg:2005qs
:

\sigma_{\text{CE}\nu\text{NS}}\propto|Q_{W}|^{2},

(14)

with

Q_{W}\propto g_{V}^{\text{eff}}[Z(2g_{V}^{u}+g_{V}^{d})+N(g_{V}^{u}+2g_{V}^{d})].

(15)

III.2.2
Nuclear Selection Rules and Spin-Zero Targets

Scattering off nuclei with non-zero spin (
J\neq 0
) would introduce an irreducible axial background that could mimic or mask the suppression of the vector signal.

To unambiguously isolate the vanishing vector current predicted for Majorana neutrinos, it is necessary to utilize target nuclei with zero ground-state spin (
J=0
), such as
{}^{40}\text{Ar}
,
{}^{76}\text{Ge}
, or
{}^{28}\text{Si}

Donnelly:1979ez
;
Bednyakov:2018mjd
;
AristizabalSierra:2019zmy
;
Walecka:2004
. For these targets, nuclear selection rules dictate that the axial-vector form factor is suppressed
Akimov:2015nza
;
AristizabalSierra:2019zmy
;
Walecka:2004
. Consequently, in an experiment using a spin-zero target like Liquid Argon, is the best place to see the effect of
\mathcal{V}_{\text{Maj}}
.

III.2.3
The Generalized Confusion Theorem at Work

In this process,one has

•

Dirac Neutrino:
The effective coupling is
g_{V}^{D}=g_{V}^{\text{SM}}+i\epsilon_{\text{CP}}
. The cross-section is dominated by the real Standard Model term:

\sigma_{\text{Dirac}}\propto|g_{V}^{\text{SM}}|^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2}).

(16)

•

Majorana Neutrino:
The Standard Model contribution is canceled. The neutrino interacts solely via the CP-violating imaginary part:

\sigma_{\text{Majorana}}\propto|0+2i\operatorname{Im}(g_{V}^{\text{NP}})|^{2}\propto\epsilon^{2}\sin^{2}(\phi).

(17)

As shown in Figure
1
, this leads to a suppression of several orders of magnitude for the Majorana case compared to the Dirac case, as
\epsilon\ll 1
. This result generalizes the confusion theorem: the Majorana nature is not hidden by mass, but by the CP-conserving nature of the Standard Model. New Physics with CP violation lifts this suppression, making the "Majorana signal" proportional to the CP phase.

IV
Discussion and Conclusions

We have presented a scenario that generalizes the universality of the “Practical Dirac-Majorana Confusion Theorem”. By introducing a New Physics vector boson
Z^{\prime}
with CP-violating couplings, we have shown that the distinguishability between Dirac and Majorana neutrinos is not strictly suppressed by the neutrino mass factor
(m_{\nu}/E)^{2}
, but is instead governed by the CP-violating phase
\phi
. In presence of
CP
violating interactions, it is possible to distinguish between Majorana and Dirac processes without the suppression factor of
m_{\nu}^{2}/E_{\nu}^{2}
as mentioned in the PDMCT.

IV.1
Experimental Implications

The consequence of our generalized theorem is the potential for discovering Majorana neutrinos in existing and near-future facilities through counting experiments:

•

CE
\nu
NS Experiments (COHERENT):
Current experiments measuring Coherent Elastic Neutrino-Nucleus Scattering provide a critical test. As derived in Section 3, a Majorana neutrino effectively decouples from the vector interaction in the limit of real couplings. If future precision measurements at COHERENT
Akimov:2015nza
;
Akimov:2020pdx
;
Akimov:2017ade
match the Standard Model prediction (dominated by
g_{V}^{\text{SM}}
), if neutrinos are Dirac, this would strongly constrain the New Physics and of course the CP-violating phase. Conversely, a significant deficit in the event rate would be a smoking gun for Majorana neutrinos with a vanishing real vector current.

•

Long-Baseline Experiments (DUNE):
The Near Detector of DUNE
DUNE:2020fgq
;
DUNE:2021tad
;
DeRomeri:2019kic
will collect high-statistics samples of neutrino-electron scattering events. The difference in spectral shape shown implies that DUNE could distinguish the two hypotheses by analyzing the recoil electron energy distribution. A symmetric distribution would point towards a Majorana nature driven purely by CP-violating interactions, while an asymmetric distribution would indicate interference characteristic of Dirac fermions.

IV.2
Compatibility with COHERENT Data

The recent observation of CE
\nu
NS on Argon by the COHERENT collaboration
Akimov:2020pdx
is consistent with the Standard Model prediction. Far from ruling out our model, this agreement provides a compelling argument for the Majorana nature of the neutrino in the presence of a light vector boson
Z^{\prime}
.

If neutrinos were Dirac fermions, the interference between the Standard Model
Z
boson and the New Physics
Z^{\prime}
vector current would typically generate a significant deviation in the event rate (scaling linearly with the coupling
\epsilon
). The absence of such a deviation strongly constrains vector
Z^{\prime}
models for Dirac neutrinos. In case of Majorana neutrinos, the New Physics contribution is proportionnal to
\epsilon^{2}
. So this effects should be taken into account using the experimental results of COHERENT experiment to constraint new physics. The constraints on New Physics will be different if neutrinos are Dirac or Majorana fermions.

IV.3
Conclusion

We have presented a scenario that generalize the “Practical Dirac-Majorana Confusion Theorem”. By introducing a New Physics vector boson
Z^{\prime}
with CP-violating couplings, we have demonstrated that the phenomenological difference between Dirac and Majorana neutrinos is not strictly suppressed by the neutrino mass factor
(m_{\nu}/E)^{2}
, but is instead governed by the CP-violating phase
\phi
.

IV.3.1
The Generalized Theorem

Our derivation leads to a generalized condition for distinguishability. We have shown that the Majorana nature acts as a strict filter for vector interactions:

•

A
Dirac neutrino
interacts via the full interference term between the Standard Model and the New Physics vector current.

•

A
Majorana neutrino
only interacts via the CP-violating imaginary component.

This mechanism lifts the kinematic mass suppression, rendering the difference between the two hypotheses macroscopic in the presence of New Physics.

IV.3.2
Experimental Outlook

The implications for current and future experiments are significant:

1.

CE
\nu
NS (COHERENT):
Due to the exclusive Neutral Current nature of the process and the use of spin-zero targets (like
{}^{40}\text{Ar}
), the axial contribution is suppressed. In this regime, a Majorana neutrino would appear effectively “blind” to the vector
Z^{\prime}
interaction, while a Dirac neutrino would exhibit a full interference pattern. The consistency of current COHERENT data with the Standard Model places strict constraints on Dirac neutrinos with vector couplings, while Majorana neutrinos naturally evade these limits via the vector filtration mechanism.

2.

Precision Scattering (DUNE):
Future measurements of
\nu-e
scattering at the DUNE Near Detector will be sensitive to the spectral shape distortions caused by the CP-violating phase. Our model predicts a symmetric recoil distribution for Majorana neutrinos, distinct from the asymmetric interference expected for Dirac fermions.

In conclusion, we find if the New Physics sector violates CP, the confusion is resolved, offering a novel pathway to reveal the fundamental nature of the neutrino in scattering experiments.

This work suggests that the search for CP violation in the neutrino sector and the determination of the neutrino’s nature are deeply intertwined. A definitive answer may lie not only in
0\nu\beta\beta
decay but also in the precision measurement of neutral current scattering rates.

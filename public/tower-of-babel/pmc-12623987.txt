Cost and outcome comparison between intradiscal condoliase injection and surgical treatment for lumbar disc herniation based on real-world data analysis
Authors: Naoki Segi, Hiroaki Nakashima, Takahiro Imaizumi, Sadayuki Ito, Jun Ouchida, Ippei Yamauchi, Yukihito Ode, Yasuhiro Nagatani, Yuya Okada, Yujiro Kagami, Shiro Imagama
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Intradiscal condoliase injection therapy (ICIT) is a novel minimally invasive treatment for lumbar disc herniation (LDH). We aimed to compare adverse events, outcomes, and costs of ICIT versus surgery using health claims data. From the database for the period 2020 to 2023, 1,982 ICIT, 4,009 endoscopic discectomy, and 5,528 open discectomy records were extracted. Comparing each surgical group matched to the ICIT group revealed that ICIT had a significantly lower surgical site infection incidence (vs. endoscopic, 0.6% vs. 1.4%, P = 0.016; vs. open, 0.6% vs. 2.8%, P < 0.001), whereas the incidence of additional spine surgery within 90 days was significantly higher (4.9% vs. 2.5%, p < 0.001; 4.9% vs. 1.6%, p < 0.001). The total cost of ICIT group was < 50% of that of discectomy (352,271 vs. 807,550 JPY, P < 0.001; 352,271 vs. 866,642 JPY, P < 0.001). In the first month after treatment, ICIT was associated with the least amount of analgesic prescriptions (76.1% vs. 91.2%, P < 0.001; 76.1% vs. 96.2%, P < 0.001), whereas ICIT was associated with the most in the third month (25.6% vs. 16.0%, P < 0.001; 25.6% vs. 15.7%, P < 0.001). ICIT presents a viable, low-cost treatment alternative to surgery for LDH.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-23988-8.

Introduction

Despite 60%–90% of cases recover naturally1,2, lumbar disc herniation (LDH) continues to be a major problem in society. This is because LDH is a relatively common condition, with a lifetime risk of symptomatic LDH reaching 1%–3%, and LDH causes symptoms, including lower back pain, leg pain, and numbness, placing a high physical burden on daily life1,3. Additionally, compared with other degenerative lumbar diseases, LDH occurs at a younger age and causes more pain and disability1,4,5, thereby resulting in loss of work opportunities and economic loss6,7. Thus, the frequency of medical use due to LDH is also relatively high1. LDH is a disease that is associated with a wide-ranging socioeconomic problem that goes beyond individual health and medical economics.

For cases of LDH that are resistant to conservative treatment, surgery is indicated8 as it is effective for relieving pain early on2,7. Several surgical procedures for LDH are available. In addition to Love’s method (unilateral transflaval discectomy) that has been widely employed9, endoscopic discectomy10,11 has gained popularity in recent years12. However, not only do surgical treatments have specific complications2, but they also incur at least an initial cost; the cost can increase further depending on the patient’s situation13,14. Although the cost-effectiveness of surgical treatment for LDH is generally considered good15,16, as a negative relationship exists between the socioeconomic environment and the severity of symptoms in patients with LDH17, the cost of treatment can be a significant issue for each patient.

Recently, selective chemonucleolysis using chondroitin sulfate ABC endolyase (condoliase), which overcomes the challenges of chymopapain and collagenase, has been put into practical use18–29. Alongside conservative treatment and surgery, intradiscal condoliase injection therapy (ICIT) is considered a promising third treatment option for LDH. The administered condoliase reduces the water-holding capacity of the proteoglycans, which are the main components of the nucleus pulposus, thereby reducing the pressure within the intervertebral disc and relieving the pressure on the nerve root. ICIT is safe, can be performed on an outpatient basis, and has satisfactory clinical efficacy and patient satisfaction22,24–27,29–34. ICIT is a minimally invasive treatment method compared to conventional surgical treatments, with a low incidence of serious adverse events at 1.4%, and no reports of anaphylaxis or neurological sequelae35. Additionally, 38–69% of patients demonstrate early response within one week, indicating that ICIT has rapid therapeutic effects35,36. However, the following predictive factors for early symptom improvement with ICIT have been identified: age under 40 years, Pfirrmann Grade II or III, and high signal intensity of the herniated disc on MRI35. Furthermore, data on the long-term prognosis of ICIT are limited. Thus, while ICIT offers unique advantages such as minimally invasiveness and rapid symptom improvement, it also faces challenges including limitations in treatment efficacy, restrictions on indications, and insufficient long-term data.

Conventional chemonucleolysis using chymopapain has demonstrated significant cost-saving effects and reduced surgical time compared to discectomy37. Thus, the cost-effectiveness of ICIT is expected to be good8; however, no evaluation of the real-world data has been conducted. Therefore, to compare the costs of ICIT and surgical treatment for LDH, we used an insurance database and indirectly evaluated the treatment outcomes. By analyzing the costs of the procedure and the treatment process, we can learn more about the precise medical economic impact of ICIT and highlight its usefulness.

Materials and methods

Data source

This study was a retrospective epidemiological data analysis of the Medical Data Vision Co., Ltd. (MDV) database38 from 2020 to 2023. The MDV database includes health claims data and administrative data or diagnosis procedure combination data from over 300 Japanese acute care hospitals (approximately 20% of acute hospitals in Japan)39,40.

The initial dataset was created by extracting records from the MDV database that included patients who had undergone spinal surgery or procedures similar to surgery, including ICIT. In the MDV database, patients are identified by a unique ID so that tracking the medical treatment received by the same patient across multiple medical institutions is possible. This study was conducted according to the guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was waived by the Institutional Review Board of Nagoya University because only anonymized data with permission for secondary use were used.

Patient population and grouping

The 4,149 records of patients who underwent ICIT were extracted using the code “K134-4” (condoliase group). Currently, due to restrictions on the implementation of ICIT in Japan, ICIT can only be performed by qualified spine surgeons, neurosurgeons, pain specialists, and interventional radiologists. It is essential to confirm the level of the lesion using X-ray images when performing ICIT. Additionally, as ICIT is not allowed to be administered to an individual more than once for the purpose of preventing anaphylaxis, the absence of duplicate patients in the condoliase group was confirmed.

Records of discectomy via the posterior approach for LDH were extracted on the basis of the following two conditions: (1) the code was “K134 2” (unilateral transflaval discectomy) or “K134-2 2” (endoscopic discectomy) and (2) the International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision (ICD-10) code for admission was “M511” (intervertebral disc disorders with radiculopathy) or “M512” (intervertebral disc displacement). To form a group of unique patients, those who underwent more than one discectomy procedure within the period were limited to only the first record. Consequently, 19,676 records were extracted (discectomy group). The wash-out period was set at 90 days. Thus, the 121 records of discectomy performed within 90 days following ICIT were excluded.

Records of patients aged < 20 or > 60 years at the time of treatment (1,293 and 7,759 records in the condoliase and discectomy groups, respectively) and records of unplanned readmissions (52 and 568 records in the condoliase and discectomy groups, respectively) were excluded. As all patients in the discectomy group were admitted to the hospital for surgery, outpatient procedure records were excluded from the condoliase group (689 records). To match the condoliase group, 388 records from the discectomy group, wherein procedures were performed at multiple levels at the same admission, were excluded. Additionally, to exclude records wherein the wash-out period could not be traced, 719 and 584 records from the discectomy group that were treated before April 1, 2020 and after October 1, 2023 were excluded. In the condoliase group, no records that were treated before April 1, 2020 were extracted, and 135 records treated after October 1, 2023 were excluded.

Consequently, 1,982 and 9,537 records in the condoliase and discectomy groups, respectively, were analyzed. The discectomy group was further divided into the endoscopic group (code K134-2 2, endoscopic discectomy; 4,009 records) and the open group (code K134 2, unilateral transflaval discectomy; 5,528 records) (Fig. 1). All analyses included medical care up to 30 days before treatment (pretreatment), excluding the treatment date, and up to 90 days after treatment (posttreatment), including the treatment date. Additionally, to investigate the outpatient ICIT, which forms a major subgroup of ICIT, 640 records were extracted from the 689 records of outpatient ICIT, excluding the 49 records that were conducted after October 1, 2023. Then, outpatient ICIT and inpatient ICIT were compared. Furthermore, a subgroup that underwent additional spinal surgery after ICIT treatment was extracted and compared.

Variables

The following background information was collected: age at the time of treatment, sex, Elixhauser comorbidity index41, ambulation at the time of admission (ambulation domain of the Barthel index), whether or not the patient was admitted as an emergency, whether or not a nerve root block (NRB) was performed before the treatment, the cost of analgesics (Supplemental Table 1), and physiotherapy cost (insurance reimbursement code J119, J119-2, J119-3, and H002) before the treatment. The following information was collected as outcomes: length of hospital stay, ambulation at the time of discharge, complications (wound dehiscence [ICD-10 code, T813], surgical site infection [SSI; T814], anaphylaxis [T782], and drug eruption [L270]), whether or not wound debridement (code “K000”) was performed, whether or not additional spinal surgery (not limited to discectomy) was performed, whether or not NRB was performed, hospitalization costs, analgesic costs, and total costs, including outpatient care. Costs were recorded in the Japanese yen (JPY).

However, no variables that could be used for determining the causal relationship between the treatment and the additional spinal surgery and debridement were noted. Moreover, no variables that could be used for identifying the causal relationship between the treatment and the ICD-10 diagnosis that indicated complications were observed.

Statistical analysis

Data were presented as means ± standard deviations and as numbers and percentages for continuous and categorical variables, respectively. Statistical analyses were performed using R version 4.4.0 (http://www.R-project.org, 2024-04-24) with the packages tidyverse42, gtsummary43, MatchiIt44, cobalt45, and comorbidity46. Group comparisons were performed using one-way analysis of means (not assuming equal variances), the Wilcoxon rank sum test, and Pearson’s Chi-square test. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

To compare between the condoliase and endoscopic or open groups, propensity score matching was used. The following variables were used for calculating propensity scores: age, sex, Elixhauser comorbidity index, whether or not the patient was admitted as an emergency, ambulation at the time of admission (whether or not the ambulation domain of the Barthel index was 3 points), whether or not an NRB was performed before the treatment, and the cost of analgesics and physiotherapy before the treatment. Matching was performed using nearest neighbor matching measured by the Mahalanobis distance. The covariate balance was examined, and it was confirmed that the absolute mean difference for each variable was < 0.1.

Results

The comparison of raw data from the three groups revealed no significant difference in duration after diagnosis and incidence of anaphylaxis after treatment (Table 1). In Japan, the procedure fees are set at 53,500, 303,900, and 235,200 JPY for ICIT, endoscopic discectomy, and open discectomy, respectively. Therefore, ICIT was performed at a rate of one-fifth to one-sixth of the cost of surgery.

Comparison between the condoliase and endoscopic groups

The comparison between the condoliase and endoscopic groups (Table 2) showed that the proportion of patients with an ambulation domain of 3 points at the time of discharge was significantly higher in the endoscopic group (98.1% vs. 99.0%, P = 0.025); nevertheless, the endoscopic group had a mean hospitalization duration of 7.7 days, whereas that of the condoliase group was 1.9 days. SSIs developed significantly more frequently in the endoscopic group (0.6% vs. 1.4%, P = 0.016), and drug eruptions occurred significantly more frequently in the condoliase group (0.5% vs. 0.1%, P = 0.035). Additional spinal surgery was undergone in 4.9% and 2.5% of the condoliase and endoscopic groups, respectively (P < 0.001). Thus, for additional spinal surgery, the number needed to harm (NNH) of the condoliase group was 38.0 to the endoscopic group reference. In the condoliase group, although the number of patients who received analgesic prescription in the first month after the treatment was significantly lower (76.1% vs. 91.2%, P < 0.001), this finding was reversed in the third month (25.6% vs. 16.0%, P < 0.001). The cost of physiotherapy after treatment with the condoliase group was less than one-third of that of the endoscopic group. The total cost of the condoliase group was < 50% of that of the endoscopic group (352,271 vs. 807,550 JPY, P < 0.001).

Comparison between the condoliase and open groups

Furthermore, the comparison between the condoliase and open groups showed similar results (Table 3). Additional spinal surgery was undergone in 4.9% and 1.6% of the condoliase and open groups, respectively (P < 0.001). Thus, for additional spinal surgery, the NNH of the condoliase group was 28.7 to the open group reference. Although the number of patients who received analgesic prescriptions in the first month after the treatment was significantly lower in the condoliase group (76.1% vs. 96.2%, P < 0.001), this finding was reversed in the third month (25.6% vs. 15.7%, P < 0.001). The cost of physiotherapy after treatment with the condoliase group was less than one-fourth of that of the endoscopic group. The total cost of the condoliase group was < 40% of that of the open group (352,271 vs. 866,642 JPY, P < 0.001). In the subgroup that underwent additional spinal surgery after ICIT treatment, costs were significantly higher than in the open group. Total costs were 1,450,711 vs. 866,642 JPY (P < 0.001) (Supplemental Table 3).

Discussion

This real-world data analysis demonstrates that ICIT offers a cost-effective alternative to discectomy surgery for LDH, with a lower risk of complications, including SSI. However, the rate of additional spinal surgery was higher than that with discectomy. In our study, the number of patients receiving analgesics at the first month post-ICIT was lower than that following discectomy, indicating that immediate effects can be expected for ICIT. Nevertheless, the risk of symptoms persisting after ICIT is noted because the number of patients receiving analgesics at the third month was higher than that after discectomy. However, ICIT can be performed at a significantly lower cost than discectomy, especially when performed on an outpatient basis. Except for patients who strongly expect quick cure, ICIT is a useful treatment that can be performed at low risk and cost.

ICIT was reconfirmed as a safe treatment method that does not have side effects like those of past chemonucleolysis. Drug eruptions following ICIT are relatively common22,31; however, in this study, the incidence rate was < 1% based on the ICD-10 diagnostic registration, and no anaphylaxis occurred. Although it is worrisome that some patients in the condoliase group underwent wound debridement, it is highly likely that the cause of the debridement was unrelated to ICIT because the debridement was performed > 70 days on average following ICIT. In contrast, additional spinal surgery was performed in 4.9% of the condoliase group, which is more than double the rate in the discectomy group. Thus, for additional spinal surgery, the NNH of ICIT was approximately 30. However, approximately 95% of the patients in the condoliase group did not undergo additional surgery, and an extra occurrence of reoperation in one in every 30 or more patients could be considered clinically effective. Therefore, using these data as a basis for deciding whether or not to perform ICIT after fully discussing the benefits and risks with the patient is recommended. Evidently, ICIT for LDH is a low-risk and safe procedure, except that ICIT has a relatively high additional surgery rate compared to surgical treatment.

The response time of ICIT is an issue that is attracting attention. In principle, ICIT will take longer to produce a therapeutic effect than surgical treatment, which involves pressure lesion removal18. Contrary to this expectation, in our study, the number of patients who received analgesics in the first month after treatment was the lowest in the condoliase group, at approximately 75% (Fig. 2A). Therefore, ICIT may also be expected to exert an immediate effect. These results are consistent with a recent report that reported an early response rate of 38.3% one week after ICIT treatment35. However, the analgesic cost during this period was higher in the condoliase group. Perhaps, although some patients experienced rapid improvement, those who did not experience immediate relief received further analgesic administration. Additionally, at 3 months following treatment, the number of patients who received analgesics was the highest in the condoliase group, at approximately 25%, which is about 10% higher than any of the discectomy groups (Fig. 2B). Unfortunately, we were unable to investigate the details of patients with prolonged analgesics prescriptions in the ICIT group from the database because there were no variables that could clarify the causality. However, 3 months is a sufficient time for the effects of ICIT and discectomy to manifest. Therefore, the number of patients in the ICIT group who received analgesics prescriptions during this period may reflect treatment failure. Therefore, the efficacy rate of ICIT is probably about 10% lower than that of discectomy. This result is understandable as it is consistent with the previously reported improvement rate of ICIT22,24,25. Notably, this treatment failure rate is much higher than the rate of additional surgery. Therefore, this difference may indicate partial success, and patients may have been satisfied with painkiller management. Nevertheless, the above results suggest that ICIT is effective in many patients with LDH, but does not provide early radical cure in approximately 25%.

Contrary to a previous report based on cost estimates derived from literature, medical expense evaluation tables, and online questionnaires8, the current study showed that the cost advantages of ICIT is lost when repeated procedures is necessary. This study is based on real-world data and therefore may better reflect actual conditions. This point should be adequately explained to patients. If patients prefer a lower-cost treatment option, the relatively high repeated procedure rate of approximately 5% compared to surgical treatment may actually increase costs. However, whether patients received adequate conservative treatment (such as 6 weeks of physical therapy and NSAID administration) prior to additional spinal surgery for initial treatment failure or symptom recurrence is an important point; however, this was difficult to clarify in the design of this study. However, considering that additional spinal surgery was performed on average 50.4 days after the initial treatment in the ICIT group, 36.4 days after in the endoscopic group, and 37.6 days after in the open group, it is likely that many patients did not have sufficient time to undergo adequate conservative treatment.

ICIT provides curative treatment for LDH at a fairly low cost than surgery, especially on an outpatient basis. This is partly due to the relatively long hospitalization period for surgical treatment in Japan47,48; however, this issue needs to be discussed separately as it depends on the medical insurance system in each country. We were unable to investigate the outpatient discectomy group using this database because outpatient discectomy is not routinely performed in acute care hospitals in Japan. In contrast, ICIT is often performed on an outpatient basis. To avoid this asymmetry, we limited the comparison to inpatient treatment. Additionally, as previously mentioned, some patients continued to receive prescriptions for analgesics after ICIT treatment (Fig. 3A); however, the total cost was by far the lowest (Fig. 3B). Furthermore, these were evaluations of inpatient ICIT, and outpatient ICIT was even lower cost. While caution is needed in interpreting results due to the different frameworks between inpatient and outpatient settings, and noting that the outpatient ICIT group included patients with milder symptoms compared to the inpatient ICIT group, outpatient ICIT is an effective strategy for completing LDH treatment while minimizing unnecessary costs.

Besides these tangible costs, attention should also be paid to potential cost savings. ICIT is fundamentally free from the possibility of local complications, including dural and nerve damage2,49, which are unavoidable with discectomy. Additionally, ICIT does not require general anesthesia or sedation, which may reduce systemic complications. Among patients, surgery is a physiologically uncomfortable event50, suggesting that ICIT can be more tolerable. Furthermore, the maximum utilization of operating theater time results in high-cost benefits and improved patient satisfaction51,52. For the healthcare provider, ICIT may also save various medical resources as it requires only fluoroscopic equipment and can be performed in a short period. Thus, the cost-effectiveness of ICIT is both high in monetary terms and potential. However, even though consideration of indirect costs such as reduced productivity, long-term disability, and quality of life (QOL) differences may be more important than consideration of direct costs, we acknowledge that this study lacks sufficient data to address this issue. Focusing on lower back pain, one of the symptoms caused by LDH, two-thirds of the costs are indirect, resulting from lost wages and reduced productivity53. The average annual number of days missed due to low back pain ranges from 23 to 100 days54,55. Additionally, while health-related QOL improves in patients who undergo lumbar disc surgery, it does not reach the level of the general population56. The evaluation of ICIT regarding such broader economic impacts remains undecided.

The results of the present analysis are in good agreement with our personal clinical experience and demonstrate the usefulness of ICIT. This procedure is not robust enough, either from its principle of action18 or from the results of randomized controlled trials19,20, to replace surgery. However, ICIT is a promising option for the minimally invasive treatment of LDH—an intermediate step between conservative treatment and surgical intervention—that is effective in patients without vertebral translation and posterior disc angles29. Unfortunately, this study is based on an insurance-based medical database that is disconnected from important clinical data such as imaging findings, symptom distribution and severity, making it difficult to identify the optimal patient population for ICIT. ICIT is intended for subligamentous extrusion-type LDH in non-older adults. However, its efficacy has also been demonstrated in younger patients23, older patients35, and lateral LDH24. Therefore, the optimal indication for ICIT may be determined not only by the patient’s biological characteristics but also by factors such as cost, as demonstrated in this study, and patient preference. The minimally invasive nature of ICIT should be noted not only for its safety and efficacy but also for its low cost and its ability to be performed in a short time in an outpatient setting. This real-world data analysis demonstrates the utility of ICIT, which can be performed on the same day for patients seeking more than conservative treatment and has a clinically practical and sufficient response time.

The MDV database used in this study does not encompass all medical institutions. However, owing to the nature of the database, it is expected to include a relatively uniform number of hospitals providing acute care. Indeed, the findings that approximately 20% of the acute care hospitals are included in the MDV database and that the volume of condoliase sold during the study period is approximately 20,000 vials57, 4,000, or 20% of the total corresponds well to the number of ICIT records in this study. However, according to the annual report of the Japanese Orthopaedic Association National Registry (JOANR)58, approximately 24,000 discectomies were performed in 2021. Although there are approximately 4,800 hospitals with orthopedic departments in Japan59, during this period, 2,316 institutions participated in JOANR. Therefore, if we estimate that about half of them participated in JOANR, we can estimate that approximately 48,000 discectomies were performed in 2021. The discectomy group in the current study had 19,676 records after eliminating duplicate patients, but before elimination there were 24,282 records. Therefore, if we note that the data set covered a period of four years, we can see that approximately 6,000 discectomy records are registered per year. This is equivalent to 12.5% of the 48,000 cases in 2021, which is relatively small compared to the fact that the MDV database includes approximately 20% of acute care hospitals. This lower-than-expected number of discectomy records suggests that relatively many discectomies are performed in clinics. In contrast, it appears that ICIT is not often performed in clinics, so that this asymmetry may be a limitation on the generalizability of this study. Additionally, in Japan, some hospitals provide medical care at a liberal fee schedule, and selection bias may have skewed the results because a disease, such as LDH, which has strong subjective symptoms and does not affect prognosis, is a prime target for such hospitals.

Several other limitations of this study must be acknowledged. First, the date of LDH onset was unknown, which limited the analysis of the pretreatment data, including the morbidity period analysis. Additionally, the details of the conservative treatment that the patient received before ICIT or discectomy were unknown. Because there are often separate hospitals that provide ICIT and surgery and local clinics that provide conservative treatment, it was not possible to obtain the details of the conservative treatment from the database. Second, uncertainty existed in the outcome measure. Because the clinical condition of the patients, including the reason for additional surgery, could not be determined from the database, it was unclear whether the additional spinal surgery was due to failure of the prior treatment. It was also unclear whether the effect of ICIT was properly assessed before the additional surgery, but it is expected that patients would not want additional surgery if ICIT is effective. Additionally, the posttreatment analgesic prescriptions did not distinguish between analgesics prescribed for other conditions. Third, the influence of unmeasured confounders was also a limitation. Despite the possibility that socioeconomic status, patient preference, surgeon preference, and differences between institutions may have affected treatment selection and outcomes, these were not analyzed due to the lack of corresponding variables. Fourth, the possibility of incomplete entry was noted; the accuracy of the ICD-10 diagnosis registration, procedure types, and other entries was not verified, which could have affected selection bias and outcome measures. To fill these gaps and obtain more clinically relevant conclusions, improvements in the study design are necessary. We thought that prospective studies are needed to conduct cost-effectiveness studies that are closely aligned with clinical data. Despite these limitations, we believe that the results of this analysis are a good representation of the overview of ICIT and provide useful information.

Conclusion

This real-world analysis revealed differences between ICIT and discectomy for LDH. Except for patients who strongly expect early cure, ICIT will be a useful treatment option that can be performed at low risk and cost.

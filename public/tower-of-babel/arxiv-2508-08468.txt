Audio–Visual Speech Enhancement: Architectural Design and Deployment Strategies
Anis Hamadouche, Haifeng Luo, Mathini Sellathurai and Tharm Ratnarajah

Abstract
This paper introduce a new AI-based Audio-Visual Speech Enhancement (AVSE) and presents a comparative performance analysis of deployment architectures. The proposed AVSE system integrates convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for spectral feature extraction and long short-term memory (LSTM) networks for temporal modeling, enabling robust speech enhancement through multimodal fusion of audio and visual cues. We investigate multiple deployment scenarios, including cloud-based, edge-assisted, and standalone device implementations, evaluating their performance in terms of speech quality improvement, latency, and computational overhead. Real-world experiments are conducted across heterogeneous network conditions (Ethernet, Wi-Fi, 4G, and 5G) to assess the trade-offs between processing delay, communication latency, and perceptual speech quality. Results show that while cloud deployment achieves superior enhancement quality, edge-assisted architectures offer the best compromise between latency and intelligibility, meeting real-time requirements under 5G and Wi-Fi 6 conditions. The findings provide practical guidelines for selecting and optimizing AVSE deployment architectures in diverse application domains, from assistive hearing devices to telepresence and industrial communications.



I
INTRODUCTION

Audio-Visual Speech Enhancement (AVSE) leverages the complementary nature of auditory and visual cues to improve speech perception in adverse acoustic conditions. In humans, this multimodal integration is an innate cognitive process, enabling effective communication in the presence of background noise, competing talkers, or reverberant environments. Neuroscientific studies have established the value of visual input in selective auditory attention. Golumbic
et al.

[
13
]
demonstrated that visual access to a talker’s face significantly improves the auditory cortex’s ability to segregate and track the target speaker in multi-speaker environments, a phenomenon often referred to as the “Cocktail Party” effect. Likewise, McGurk and MacDonald
[
3
]
revealed that incongruent lip movements can alter auditory perception entirely, demonstrating that speech understanding is not purely an auditory process but is strongly influenced by visual context. Further, Partan and Marler
[
9
]
emphasized that multimodal communication signals, common in group-living species, improve clarity, robustness, and interpretation.

Motivated by these findings, AVSE systems employ visual information—such as lip motion, facial dynamics, and articulatory patterns—to guide audio enhancement. The integration of these modalities allows for more accurate speaker separation, noise suppression, and reverberation reduction. Modern deep learning techniques have accelerated this field by enabling hierarchical feature extraction and cross-modal fusion at multiple abstraction levels. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) are typically employed to extract discriminative visual features, while spectrogram-based CNNs or waveform-level encoders capture audio representations. Recurrent architectures, such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs), are frequently used to model temporal dependencies, which are critical for capturing speech dynamics.

The applications of AVSE extend beyond traditional hearing aids. In military communications, AVSE can enhance intelligibility in complex acoustic environments such as the battlefield. In consumer electronics, it improves voice-command recognition for smart assistants and in-car systems, even in noisy driving conditions
[
27
]
. In education and telepresence, AVSE can ensure intelligible communication in virtual classrooms or video conferencing, while in industrial and public-service robotics, it enables reliable interaction despite background noise. The healthcare sector can also benefit, as AVSE improves telemedicine consultations for patients with speech or hearing impairments
[
26
]
.

While algorithmic design is central to AVSE performance, deployment architecture has a decisive role in real-world effectiveness. Processing location—whether cloud-based, edge-assisted, or entirely on-device—affects latency, speech quality, and system scalability. Communication bandwidth, device computational capability, and network stability determine whether a deployment can meet the real-time requirements for interactive speech enhancement. In particular, deployment trade-offs become critical when serving resource-constrained devices such as IoT endpoints, low-cost smart glasses, or wearable hearing-assist devices.

In this paper, we present a complete AVSE system implemented within the COG-MHEAR framework. The system uses a CNN–LSTM-based fusion architecture, and we evaluate its performance across multiple deployment configurations: server-side cloud processing, edge-assisted processing, and local execution. The contributions of this work are threefold:

1.

We propose an AVSE architecture that combines convolutional and recurrent feature extraction with intermediate fusion, enabling robust speech enhancement in challenging conditions.

2.

We design and implement a flexible deployment framework capable of supporting heterogeneous device capabilities, from lightweight IoT hardware to high-performance computing platforms.

3.

We conduct a comparative performance analysis under realistic network conditions, quantifying trade-offs between latency, intelligibility, and computational load across deployment scenarios.

II
Acoustic Signal Modeling

Central to the concept of AVSE is the nuanced modeling of acoustic signals. Here, each impulse response emanating from a spatial position of a target source to a microphone is represented as
h_{s}[k]
, where
k
signifies a discrete-time index. This impulse response is bifurcated into two distinct components: the early part
h_{s}^{1}[k]
, encompassing the direct sound and early reflections, and the late part
h_{s}^{2}[k]
, which pertains to the subsequent reverberations. Consequently, the observed acoustic mixture at any given instance
k
can be expressed in a mathematical formulation as:

y[k]=\sum_{s=1}^{T}x_{s}[k]+d[k]

(1)

x_{s}[k]=x_{s}^{0}[k]\circledast h_{s}^{1}[k]

(2)

d[k]=\sum_{s=1}^{T}x_{s}^{0}[k]\circledast h_{s}^{2}[k]+\sum_{c=1}^{N}d_{c}[k]

(3)

In this formulation,
x_{s}^{0}[k]
represents the original speech signal emitted by the
s
-th target speaker. The term
x_{s}[k]
denotes the clean speech signal from the
s
-th target speaker, inclusive of low-order reflections as captured by the microphone. The component
d_{c}[k]
symbolizes the noise contribution from various sources as observed at the microphone.

IV
Proposed Solution

Figure 1
presents a pragmatic framework for the deployment of the developed AVSE algorithm within the context of real-world applications. In order to enable the service in lowquality terminals (e.g., IoT devices), the remote server takes over all the processing, including the preprocessing of the raw data. By doing so, any device with a camera, microphone, and communication capabilities can use the service without any requirements on its computing power. This allows us to apply the service to some cheap IoT devices to achieve specific functions. For high-performance devices (e.g., smartphones), offloading all processing to the server can also reduce energy consumption and improve battery life.

Fig. 1:
A block diagram of enabling real-world COG-MHEAR service on terminal devices.

As the figure depicts, the client terminals only collect the raw audio and video, then upload them to the server and wait for the feedback from the server. It should be noted that collecting and playing audio requires parallel processing, so we use two independent threads. The server will listen to clients and wait for the raw audio-video data from clients. It starts processing using the stored trained model once it listens to data transmission from the client. Considering that processing latency may exceed the duration of the audio itself, resulting in incoherent feedback audio, we use buffers to store input and output data and perform parallel signal processing in another independent thread. In this way, even if the processing delay is high due to algorithm complexity, the client can still hear coherent enhanced audio after a specific delay, thereby having a satisfactory service experience.

Fig. 2:
A schematic describes the experienced latency of this service.

describes the latency of the processing of the COG-MHEAR service. As shown in the system model, the client continuously collects voice and video information and sends it to the server. Assume that the length of the audio-video chunk sent by the client each time is
t_{\text{chunk}}
, that is, the client will send out audio and video after collecting a chunk of
t_{\text{chunk}}
. After receiving the data, the server will first perform preprocessing, such as feature extraction of video frames, and then store the processed data in the buffer. Since our algorithm tracks lips to help audio enhancement, the input of the algorithm must have a specific length
t_{i}
to ensure sufficient visual features. When there are more than
t_{i}
data in the buffer, the server will start executing the AVSE algorithm. Then, the server processes the incoming audio and video data sequentially, and we must set a sufficient buffer interval
t_{\Delta}
to ensure that it can complete each process, which is given as

t_{\Delta}>t_{a}

(5)

where
t_{a}
is the algorithm processing latency. The enhanced audio is stored in the output buffer, and the server begins feeding the enhanced audio back to the client when there is enough data in the output buffer. It should be noted that since
t_{i}
is usually much larger than
t_{\Delta}
, if we want the initial audio to be enhanced by this algorithm, there will be an inevitable
t_{i}
-length waiting time for data, causing a high delay. So we play a trick, that is, we do not process the audio at the beginning of
t_{i}-t_{\Delta}
, thereby reducing the overall delay to

t_{\text{delay}}=t_{1}+t_{2}+t_{3}=t_{\text{comm}}+t_{\text{proc}}

(6)

where
t_{1}
denotes the forward network latency, i.e., the delay incurred in transmitting data from the client to the server;
t_{2}
denotes the server’s processing latency, i.e., the delay incurred by the server in processing the data;
t_{3}
denotes the reverse network latency, i.e., the delay incurred in transmitting data from the server to the client. Thus,
t_{1}+t_{3}
comprise the communication latency, and the processing latency
t_{\text{proc}}
(i.e.,
t_{2}
) can be decomposed into the buffer interval
t_{\Delta}
and algorithm processing time
t_{a}
. As a result, the client can get coherent feedback audio
t_{\text{proc}}
seconds after enabling the service, and audio enhancement is enabled after
t_{i}-t_{\Delta}
seconds.

V
Experimental Results

In this experiment, the auditory information undergoes a transformation via convolutional neural networks (CNNs), which can distill the audio patterns. The visual information is processed through the OpenCV library, which is an open-source computer vision and machine learning software library, to extract facial features from the face embeddings. It is implemented using the pre-trained Haar feature-based cascade classifiers, which are underpinned also by CNNs. Subsequently, the algorithm employs a fusion strategy where the extracted auditory and facial features coalesce, serving as inputs to a sophisticated long shortterm memory (LSTM) network. This step is crucial for integrating temporal dynamics and ensuring a coherent feature representation. The convergence of these modalities culminates in a series of FC layers, which tunes the composite features to reconstruct the target outputs. This framework provides a foundational blueprint, while the explicit architectural configuration (i.e., layers, neurons, etc.) can vary depending on the requirements and data pre-processing.

It should be noted that although we do buffering to ensure coherent audio feedback from the server, the capacity of the communication network is also extremely important to ensure reliable quality of service. The communication network needs to have sufficient capacity so that the round-trip delay of data transmission between the client and server is shorter than the time of the data itself to ensure a consistent service experience, denoted as

t_{\text{comm}}\leq t_{\text{chunk}}

(7)

Otherwise, after playing a chunk, if the data of the next chunk has not been fed back, there will be a blank period.

0.3

VI
CONCLUSION

In conclusion, this paper’s exploration of an Audio-Video-Based Speech Enhancement (AVSE) system underscores its potential to revolutionize speech recognition by integrating audio and visual data through advanced deep learning techniques. Among the myriad of practical applications, one of the most impactful is in the development of next-generation hearing aids. These advanced hearing aids, equipped with AVSE capabilities, could offer a transformative experience for individuals with hearing impairments, providing them with an enhanced ability to understand speech in noisy environments by leveraging both auditory and visual cues.

Looking to the future, several promising research directions could further elevate AVSE technology. Optimizing the balance between audio and visual modalities is crucial, as it can vary depending on environmental factors and the specific needs of the application. Investigating diverse DNN architectures can potentially unveil more effective ways of processing and merging audio-visual data. Moreover, experimenting with various fusion functions beyond simple concatenation could lead to more nuanced and context-aware integration of sensory inputs.

The broader implications of AVSE technology extend to a variety of real-world scenarios. Its application in military communications can drastically improve operational efficiency on the battlefield. In construction sites or any noisy industrial environment, AVSE can facilitate clearer internal communications among workers. The technology also has significant applications in videoconferencing, journalism, and sports, particularly in enhancing communication in noisy stadiums.

Each application presents unique challenges, suggesting a need for AVSE solutions that are customized to specific environments and user requirements. Future advancements in this field not only promise to push the boundaries of speech enhancement technologies but also offer substantial improvements in diverse sectors that rely on clear and effective communication. The integration of AVSE systems into hearing aids represents a particularly profound opportunity to enrich the lives of individuals with hearing impairments, marking a significant step forward in assistive technology.

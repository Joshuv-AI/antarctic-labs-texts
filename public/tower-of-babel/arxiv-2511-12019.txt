On perturbation of Hilbert-Schmidt frames
Jyoti, Lalit Kumar Vashisht ∗

Abstract
. In this paper, we study perturbation of Hilbert-Schmidt frames under structured modifications, where the perturbation takes the form of replacing finitely or infinitely many frame elements. We establish explicit criteria under which the perturbed sequence retains the Hilbert-Schmidt frame property. In the finite case, the stability bounds depend quantitatively on the perturbation size and the number of altered elements. For the infinite case, we identify sufficient conditions ensuring stability under globally controlled perturbations. Our study includes illustrative examples demonstrating the applicability of the results.

Introduction

The concept of frames, which was first based on work by Gabor
[
8
]
in decomposing complex signals (functions) into elementary functions, was introduced by Duffin and Schaeffer
[
6
]
and later reviewed by Young
[
29
]
in the study of several challenging problems pertaining to non-harmonic Fourier series. A countable collection
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{I}}
of members of a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is a frame for
\mathcal{H}
if, there exist positive real numbers
a_{o}
and
b_{o}
, called the
lower frame bound
and
upper frame bound
of
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{I}}

(1.1)

\displaystyle a_{o}\|x\|^{2}\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{I}}|\langle x,x_{k}\rangle|^{2}\leq b_{o}\|x\|^{2}\ \text{for all}\ x\in\mathcal{H}.

In (
1.1

\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{I}}
is a
Bessel sequence
with the
Bessel bound

b_{o}
. Further, the bounded linear map

\mathcal{H}

\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\sigma}\bigcup\{y_{i}\}_{i\in\sigma^{c}}

[

\mathcal{X}

\mathcal{Y}

\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})

\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{Y}

\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})=\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{X})

\{e_{i}\}_{i\in I}

\sum_{i\in I}\|Te_{i}\|^{2}<\infty

\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathcal{K})

\|.\|_{2}

\displaystyle\|T\|_{2}=\Big(\text{{trace}}(T^{*}T)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\big(\sum\limits_{i\in I}\|Te_{i}\|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\ T\in\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathcal{K}).

The space
\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathcal{K})
is a Hilbert space with respect to the inner product
[.,.]_{\textbf{tr}}

\displaystyle[T,S]_{\textbf{tr}}=\text{{trace}}(S^{*}T)=\sum_{i\in I}\langle Te_{i},Se_{i}\rangle,\ T,S\in\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathcal{K}).

\displaystyle(x\otimes y)(u)=\langle u,y\rangle x,\ u\in\mathcal{K}.

Definition 1.1
.

[

\displaystyle A\|x\|^{2}\leq\sum_{i\in I}\|\Theta_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\leq B\|x\|^{2}\quad\text{for all}\ x\in\mathcal{H}.

.
If only the upper inequality in (

\{\Theta_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a Hilbert-Schmidt Bessel (HS-Bessel) sequence with Bessel bound
B
.

2.
Main Results

The following results quantify how small perturbations affect HS-frames under finite replacements. These findings are motivated by applications in distributed systems where only a subset of frame elements may be altered. The first result below establishes sufficient conditions under which a sequence formed by finite replacements in an HS-frame retains the frame property, with explicit dependence on the perturbation size and the cardinality of the modified set.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be an HS-frame for
\mathcal{H}
with respect to
\mathcal{K}
with frame bounds
A_{G},B_{G}
, and
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be an HS-Bessel sequence for

\mathcal{H}
with respect to
\mathcal{K}
with Bessel bound
B_{F}
. Fix any subset
\sigma\subseteq I
with finite cardinality
|\sigma|\leq N<\infty
and define the weaving
\{H_{i}\}_{i\in I}
as

\displaystyle H_{i}=\begin{cases}F_{i},&i\in\sigma,\\
G_{i},&i\in\sigma^{c}.\end{cases}

Assume that
\|F_{i}-G_{i}\|\leq\epsilon
for all
i\in I
, where

\displaystyle 0<\epsilon<\frac{A_{G}}{2N\max(\sqrt{B_{F}},\sqrt{B_{G}})}.

Then,
\{H_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is an HS-frame for
\mathcal{H}
with respect to
\mathcal{K}
with frame bounds
\big[A_{G}-2N\epsilon\max(\sqrt{B_{F}},\sqrt{B_{G}})\big]
and
\big[B_{G}+2N\epsilon\max(\sqrt{B_{F}},\sqrt{B_{G}})\big]
.

Proof.

For any
x\in\mathcal{H}
, we compute

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{i\in I}\|H_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}-\sum_{i\in I}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\Big|

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|F_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}+\sum_{i\in\sigma^{c}}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}-\sum_{i\in I}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\sum_{i\in\sigma}\big(\|F_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}-\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\big)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in\sigma}\Big|\|F_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}-\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\Big|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\sigma}\Big|[F_{i}(x),F_{i}(x)]_{\text{tr}}-[G_{i}(x),G_{i}(x)]_{\text{tr}}\Big|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\sigma}\Big|[F_{i}(x),F_{i}(x)]_{\text{tr}}-[G_{i}(x),F_{i}(x)]_{\text{tr}}+[G_{i}(x),F_{i}(x)]_{\text{tr}}-[G_{i}(x),G_{i}(x)]_{\text{tr}}\Big|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\sigma}\Big|[F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x),F_{i}(x)]_{\text{tr}}+[G_{i}(x),F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x)]_{\text{tr}}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in\sigma}\Big(\big|[F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x),F_{i}(x)]_{\text{tr}}\big|+\big|[G_{i}(x),F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x)]_{\text{tr}}\big|\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in\sigma}\Big(\|F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x)\|_{2}\|F_{i}(x)\|_{2}+\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}\|F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x)\|_{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x)\|_{2}\big(\|F_{i}(x)\|_{2}+\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|F_{i}-G_{i}\|\|x\|\big(\sqrt{B_{F}}\|x\|+\sqrt{B_{G}}\|x\|\big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in\sigma}2\epsilon\max(\sqrt{B_{F}},\sqrt{B_{G}})\|x\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2N\epsilon\max(\sqrt{B_{F}},\sqrt{B_{G}})\|x\|^{2}.

Using this, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\|H_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big|\sum_{i\in I}(\|H_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}-\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}+\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2})\Big|

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i\in I}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}-\Big|\sum_{i\in I}\|H_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}-\sum_{i\in I}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\Big|

\displaystyle\geq A_{G}\|x\|^{2}-2N\epsilon\max(\sqrt{B_{F}},\sqrt{B_{G}})\|x\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\big[A_{G}-2N\epsilon\max(\sqrt{B_{F}},\sqrt{B_{G}})\big]\|x\|^{2},\ x\in\mathcal{H}.

This proves the lower frame inequality. The upper frame inequality follows by similar estimates. That is, for any
x\in\mathcal{H}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\|H_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big|\sum_{i\in I}(\|H_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}-\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}+\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2})\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in I}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}+\Big|\sum_{i\in I}\|H_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}-\sum_{i\in I}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq B_{G}\|x\|^{2}+2N\epsilon\max(\sqrt{B_{F}},\sqrt{B_{G}})\|x\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\big[B_{G}+2N\epsilon\max(\sqrt{B_{F}},\sqrt{B_{G}})\big]\|x\|^{2}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

The above result critically depends on the assumption that the subset
\sigma
where the operators are replaced is finite with
|\sigma|\leq N
. This ensures the perturbation caused by switching from
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I}
to
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in I}
accumulates over finitely many indices, keeping the total perturbation small. The complement
\sigma^{c}=I\setminus\sigma
may be infinite without affecting the stability because on
\sigma^{c}
the operators remain unchanged as
\{G_{i}\}
. No additional perturbation is introduced there, so the infinite size of
\sigma^{c}
poses no problem.
This models practical scenarios where only finitely many operators differ in the weaving, while the rest come from a stable HS-frame.

The following example demonstrates how the finite weaving stability result applies concretely to a scenario with a small number of local perturbations in HS-frames.

Example 2.3
.

Consider the index set
I=\mathbb{N}
and fix
N=3
. Let
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be an HS-frame with the frame bounds
A_{G}=1,B_{G}=2
, and
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be an HS-Bessel sequence with Bessel bound
B_{F}=2
. Choose perturbation parameter
\epsilon=0.1
. Then

\displaystyle 2N\epsilon\max(\sqrt{B_{F}},\sqrt{B_{G}})=2\times 3\times 0.1\times\sqrt{2}\approx 0.8485<A_{G}=1,

which by Theorem
2.1
ensures that the weaving formed by choosing up to
3
operators from
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and the rest from
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is still an HS-frame.

We now present a complementary result that uses a quadratic perturbation bound to establish frame stability. This approach offers an alternative estimate for the lower frame bound, particularly useful when the perturbation size or the number of modified elements is relatively small.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be an HS-frame for
\mathcal{H}
with respect to
\mathcal{K}
with frame bounds
A_{G}
,
B_{G}
, and
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be an HS-Bessel sequence for

\mathcal{H}
with respect to
\mathcal{K}
with Bessel bound
B_{F}
. Fix any subset
\sigma\subseteq I
with finite cardinality
|\sigma|\leq N<\infty
and define the weaving
\{H_{i}\}_{i\in I}
as

\displaystyle H_{i}=\begin{cases}F_{i},&i\in\sigma,\\
G_{i},&i\in\sigma^{c}.\end{cases}

Assume that
\|F_{i}-G_{i}\|\leq\epsilon
for all
i\in I
, for
\epsilon>0
that satisfies
N\epsilon^{2}+2\epsilon\sqrt{N}\sqrt{B_{G}}<A_{G}
.
Then,
\{H_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is an HS-frame for
\mathcal{H}
with frame bounds
\big[A_{G}-N\epsilon^{2}-2\epsilon\sqrt{N}\sqrt{B_{G}}\big]
and
(B_{F}+B_{G})
.

Proof.

It is straightforward to obtain the upper frame condition for the weaving sequence
\{H_{i}\}_{i\in I}
. Precisely, for any
x\in\mathcal{H}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\|H_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|F_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}+\sum_{i\in\sigma^{c}}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{i\in I}\|F_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}+\sum_{i\in I}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\leq(B_{F}+B_{G})\|x\|^{2}.

Next, to prove the lower frame condition, for each
x\in\mathcal{H}
, we compute

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\|H_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\|H_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x)+G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}[H_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x)+G_{i}(x),H_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x)+G_{i}(x)]_{\text{tr}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\|H_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}+2\text{Re}\sum_{i\in I}[H_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x),G_{i}(x)]_{\text{tr}}+\sum_{i\in I}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}

(2.1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}+2\text{Re}\sum_{i\in\sigma}[F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x),G_{i}(x)]_{\text{tr}}+\sum_{i\in I}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}.

We see that

\displaystyle 2\text{Re}\sum_{i\in\sigma}[F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x),G_{i}(x)]_{\text{tr}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\Big|\sum_{i\in\sigma}[F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x),G_{i}(x)]_{\text{tr}}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{i\in\sigma}|[F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x),G_{i}(x)]_{\text{tr}}|

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x)\|_{2}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\big(\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big(\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\big(\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|F_{i}-G_{i}\|^{2}\|x\|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big(\sum_{i\in I}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\big(\sum_{i\in\sigma}\epsilon^{2}\|x\|^{2}\big)^{1/2}(B_{G}\|x\|^{2})^{1/2}

(2.2)

\displaystyle\leq 2\epsilon\sqrt{N}\sqrt{B_{G}}\|x\|^{2},\,x\in\mathcal{H},

and

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|F_{i}-G_{i}\|^{2}\|x\|^{2}\leq\sum_{i\in\sigma}\epsilon^{2}\|x\|^{2}\leq N\epsilon^{2}\|x\|^{2},\,x\in\mathcal{H}.

Hence, rewriting equation (
2
) and using equations (
2
) and (
2.3
), we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\|H_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big|\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}+2\text{Re}\sum_{i\in\sigma}[F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x),G_{i}(x)]_{\text{tr}}+\sum_{i\in I}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\Big|

\displaystyle\geq\Big|\sum_{i\in I}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\Big|-\Big|\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}+2\text{Re}\sum_{i\in\sigma}[F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x),G_{i}(x)]_{\text{tr}}\Big|

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i\in I}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}-\Big|\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\Big|-\Big|2\text{Re}\sum_{i\in\sigma}[F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x),G_{i}(x)]_{\text{tr}}\Big|

\displaystyle\geq A_{G}\|x\|^{2}-N\epsilon^{2}\|x\|^{2}-2\epsilon\sqrt{N}\sqrt{B_{G}}\|x\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\big[A_{G}-N\epsilon^{2}-2\epsilon\sqrt{N}\sqrt{B_{G}}\big]\|x\|^{2},\ x\in\mathcal{H}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Next, we give a perturbation result that involves a sequence
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, which is used over a finite subset
\sigma\subset I
, despite not being an HS-Bessel sequence. The only assumption needed is that the perturbation from
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is controlled locally over finite subsets. This local nature of the condition allows the result to be applied even when
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is defined only partially, or behaves poorly outside a finite set, making the result particularly useful in finite-dimensional approximations and localized perturbation scenarios.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be an HS-frame for
\mathcal{H}
with respect to
\mathcal{K}
with frame bounds
A_{G},B_{G}
, and
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a sequence of bounded linear operators from
\mathcal{H}
into
\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathcal{K})
. Suppose
\delta\in(0,1)
be a constant such that for every finite subset
J\subset I
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in J}\|(F_{i}-G_{i})(x)\|_{2}^{2}\leq\delta\sum_{i\in J}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\quad\text{for all}\ x\in\mathcal{H}.

Then, for every finite subset
\sigma\subset I
, the weaving
\{H_{i}\}_{i\in I}
defined by

\displaystyle H_{i}=\begin{cases}F_{i},&i\in\sigma,\\
G_{i},&i\in\sigma^{c}\end{cases}

is an HS-frame for
\mathcal{H}
with respect to
\mathcal{K}
with frame bounds
\big[(1-\sqrt{\delta})^{2}A_{G}\big]\ \text{and}\ \big[2(\delta+1)B_{G}\big]
.

Proof.

From the hypothesis, for any
i\in I
, it follows that

\displaystyle\|(F_{i}-G_{i})(x)\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{\delta}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}\quad\text{for all}\ x\in\mathcal{H}

which gives

\displaystyle\|F_{i}(x)\|_{2}\geq\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}-\|(F_{i}-G_{i})(x)\|_{2}\geq(1-\sqrt{\delta})\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}.

Using this, we compute

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\|H_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|F_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}+\sum_{i\in\sigma^{c}}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i\in\sigma}(1-\sqrt{\delta})^{2}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}+\sum_{i\in\sigma^{c}}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(1-\sqrt{\delta})^{2}\sum_{i\in I}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(1-\sqrt{\delta})^{2}A_{G}\|x\|^{2},\ x\in\mathcal{H}.

This gives the lower frame inequality for
\{H_{i}\}_{i\in I}
. Next, we compute

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\|H_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|F_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}+\sum_{i\in\sigma^{c}}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|(F_{i}-G_{i})(x)+G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}+\sum_{i\in\sigma^{c}}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|(F_{i}-G_{i})(x)\|_{2}^{2}+2\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}+\sum_{i\in\sigma^{c}}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\delta\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}+2\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}+\sum_{i\in\sigma^{c}}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=2(\delta+1)\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}+\sum_{i\in\sigma^{c}}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2(\delta+1)\sum_{i\in I}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2(\delta+1)B_{G}\|x\|^{2},\ x\in\mathcal{H}.

Hence,
\{H_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is an HS-frame for
\mathcal{H}
with respect to
\mathcal{K}
with required frame bounds.
∎

2.1.
Special Relevance in Finite-Dimensional Settings

When both
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{K}
are finite-dimensional spaces, the results acquire special relevance. Here, Hilbert-Schmidt and operator norms are equivalent, and computations become more tractable. Moreover, the finite index sets naturally match the assumption of finitely supported perturbations, making these results especially applicable to finite sensor networks, discrete signal systems, and matrix data processing. The explicit bounds derived offer concrete criteria for ensuring robust reconstruction under local distortions.

2.2.
Extension to Infinite Weaving with Decaying Perturbations

While finite perturbations naturally model localized failure, many real-world systems undergo gradual degradation, where frame elements are corrupted in a decaying manner. In such cases, control over the aggregate perturbation, rather than pointwise control, suffices. The next result generalizes Theorem
2.1
by allowing infinitely many perturbed elements, provided the perturbations decay sufficiently fast to ensure square-summability.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be an HS-frame for
\mathcal{H}
with respect to
\mathcal{K}
with frame bounds
A_{G},B_{G}>0
. Let
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be an HS-Bessel sequence for
\mathcal{H}
with respect to
\mathcal{K}
with Bessel bound
B_{F}>0
. Suppose

\displaystyle\|F_{i}-G_{i}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon}{w_{i}},\quad\text{for all }i\in I,

where
\epsilon>0
and the sequence of positive weights
\{w_{i}\}_{i\in I}
satisfy

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\frac{1}{w_{i}^{2}}<\infty\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\epsilon\Big(\sum_{i\in I}\frac{1}{w_{i}^{2}}\Big)^{1/2}\big(\sqrt{B_{F}}+\sqrt{B_{G}}\big)<A_{G}.

Then, for any subset
\sigma\subset I
, the weaving
\{H_{i}\}_{i\in I}
given by

(2.4)

\displaystyle H_{i}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}F_{i},&i\in\sigma,\\
G_{i},&i\in\sigma^{c}\end{cases}

forms an HS-frame for
H
with frame bounds

\big(A_{G}-\epsilon\big(\sum_{i\in I}\frac{1}{w_{i}^{2}}\big)^{1/2}\big(\sqrt{B_{F}}+\sqrt{B_{G}}\big)\big)
and
(B_{F}+B_{G})
.

Proof.

We only need to prove the lower frame inequality. Let
\{H_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be given by (
2.4
). Using the initial calculations as done in the proof of Theorem
2.1
, for every
x\in\mathcal{H}
, we get

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{i\in I}\|H_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}-\sum_{i\in I}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x)\|_{2}\big(\|F_{i}(x)\|_{2}+\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|F_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}+\Big(\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|F_{i}-G_{i}\|^{2}\|x\|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big[\Big(\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|F_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}+\Big(\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{i\in\sigma}\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{w_{i}^{2}}\|x\|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big[\Big(\sum_{i\in I}\|F_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}+\Big(\sum_{i\in I}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{w_{i}^{2}}\|x\|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\big(\sqrt{B_{F}}\|x\|+\sqrt{B_{G}}\|x\|\big)

\displaystyle=\epsilon\Big(\sum_{i\in I}\frac{1}{w_{i}^{2}}\Big)^{1/2}\big(\sqrt{B_{F}}+\sqrt{B_{G}}\big)\|x\|^{2},\ x\in\mathcal{H}.

Using this, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\|H_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big|\sum_{i\in I}(\|H_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}-\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}+\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2})\Big|

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i\in I}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}-\Big|\sum_{i\in I}\|H_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}-\sum_{i\in I}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\Big|

\displaystyle\geq A_{G}\|x\|^{2}-\epsilon\Big(\sum_{i\in I}\frac{1}{w_{i}^{2}}\Big)^{1/2}\big(\sqrt{B_{F}}+\sqrt{B_{G}}\big)\|x\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big[A_{G}-\epsilon\Big(\sum_{i\in I}\frac{1}{w_{i}^{2}}\Big)^{1/2}\big(\sqrt{B_{F}}+\sqrt{B_{G}}\big)\Big]\|x\|^{2},\ x\in\mathcal{H}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

The following example illustrates the applicability of Theorem
2.6
to practical scenarios where infinitely many operators are perturbed, but the perturbations decay sufficiently fast to preserve the frame structure. Unlike previous results which are restricted to finitely supported perturbations, these cases show how infinite weaving remains stable under controlled decay conditions. This framework captures realistic models in which frame elements degrade with decreasing severity for instance, over time, spatial distance, or system iterations, making the theory relevant for adaptive signal systems, sensor arrays, and learning networks. In Example
2.7
(a), we consider polynomial decay in the operator perturbations, allowing the difference
\|F_{i}-G_{i}\|
to diminish like
\frac{1}{i^{p}}
for some
p>\frac{1}{2}
. This ensures square-summability of the decay weights and leads to a stable weaving HS-frame regardless of the choice of subset
\sigma
. In Example
2.7
(b), we further strengthen the result by considering exponential decay, where perturbations diminish at a rate
e^{-ci}
. Such exponential models are natural in systems with fast convergence, feedback stabilization, or time-decaying error dynamics.

Example 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{K}=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
. Consider the canonical orthonormal basis
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
. Define
G_{i}:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})\to\mathcal{C}_{2}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}))
by

\displaystyle G_{i}(x)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\langle x,e_{1}\rangle e_{1}\otimes e_{1},&i=1\\
\langle x,e_{i-1}\rangle e_{i}\otimes e_{i},&i\geq 2.\end{cases}

Then,

\displaystyle\|x\|^{2}\leq\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\langle x,e_{1}\rangle e_{1}\otimes e_{1}\|_{2}^{2}+\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\|\langle x,e_{i-1}\rangle e_{i}\otimes e_{i}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=|\langle x,e_{1}\rangle|^{2}+\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}|\langle x,e_{i-1}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\langle x,e_{1}\rangle|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|\langle x,e_{i}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|x\|^{2},\ x\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}).

This shows that
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is an HS-frame for
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
with frame bounds
A_{G}=1,B_{G}=2
.

Let
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a perturbed sequence defined as
F_{i}=G_{i}+E_{i}
, with
\|E_{i}\|\leq\frac{\epsilon}{w_{i}}
, where
\epsilon>0
.

(a)

Define weights
w_{i}=i^{p}
for some
p>\frac{1}{2}
.
Then the perturbations satisfy
\|F_{i}-G_{i}\|\leq\frac{\epsilon}{i^{p}}
, and the perturbations decay polynomially. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{w_{i}^{2}}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{i^{2p}}<\infty.

We see that for any
x\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\|F_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\|G_{i}(x)+E_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}+2\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\|E_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2B_{G}\|x\|^{2}+2\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\|E_{i}\|^{2}\|x\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2B_{G}\|x\|^{2}+2\epsilon^{2}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{i^{2p}}\|x\|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\Big(B_{G}+\epsilon^{2}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{i^{2p}}\Big)\|x\|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\big(B_{G}+\epsilon^{2}\zeta(2p)\big)\|x\|^{2},

which shows that
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is an HS-Bessel sequence with bound
B_{F}=2\big(B_{G}+\epsilon^{2}\zeta(2p)\big)
, here
\zeta(2p)
is the Riemann zeta function at
2p
.
Choose
\epsilon>0
small enough to satisfy

\displaystyle\epsilon\Big(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{w_{i}^{2}}\Big)^{1/2}\big(\sqrt{B_{F}}+\sqrt{B_{G}}\big)=\epsilon\sqrt{\zeta(2p)}\big(\sqrt{2\big(2+\epsilon^{2}\zeta(2p)\big)}+\sqrt{2}\big)<1=A_{G}.

By Theorem
2.6
, for any subset
\sigma\subset\mathbb{N}
, the weaving
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in\sigma}\cup\{G_{i}\}_{i\in\sigma^{c}}

is an HS-frame for
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
.

(b)

Let weights be
w_{i}=e^{ci},c\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, the perturbations satisfy
\|F_{i}-G_{i}\|\leq\frac{\epsilon}{e^{ci}}
,
and

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{w_{i}^{2}}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}e^{-2ci}=\frac{e^{-2c}}{1-e^{-2c}}<\infty.

As done in part
(a)
,
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is an HS-Bessel sequence with bound
B_{F}=2\big(B_{G}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}e^{-2c}}{1-e^{-2c}}\big)
.
Choose
\epsilon>0
small enough so that

\displaystyle\epsilon\Big(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{w_{i}^{2}}\Big)^{1/2}\big(\sqrt{B_{F}}+\sqrt{B_{G}}\big)=\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{e^{-2c}}{1-e^{-2c}}}\left(\sqrt{2\Big(2+\frac{\epsilon^{2}e^{-2c}}{1-e^{-2c}}\Big)}+\sqrt{2}\right)<1=A_{G}.

By Theorem
2.6
, for any subset
\sigma\subset\mathbb{N}
, the weaving
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in\sigma}\cup\{G_{i}\}_{i\in\sigma^{c}}
is an HS-frame for
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
.

2.3.
Weaving of Parseval HS-frames

The following result shows that if two Parseval HS-frames are sufficiently close in the inner product sense, then every weaving forms an HS-frame.
The closeness is quantified by a uniform lower bound on the real part of the inner product.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be Parseval HS-frames for
\mathcal{H}
with respect to
\mathcal{K}
. Suppose there exists a constant
\delta\in(0,1/8)
such that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\text{Re}\sum_{i\in I}[F_{i}(x),G_{i}(x)]_{\text{tr}}\geq(1-\delta)\|x\|^{2}\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathcal{H}.

Then, for any
\sigma\subset I
, the weaving
\{H_{i}\}_{i\in I}
given by

\displaystyle H_{i}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}F_{i},&i\in\sigma,\\
G_{i},&i\in\sigma^{c}\end{cases}

is an HS-frame for
\mathcal{H}
with respect to
\mathcal{K}
with frame bounds
(1-2\sqrt{2\delta})
and
(1+2\sqrt{2\delta})
.

Proof.

First, we observe that for any
x\in\mathcal{H}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\|H_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|F_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}+\sum_{i\in\sigma^{c}}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|F_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}+\sum_{i\in\sigma^{c}}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}+\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}-\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|F_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}+\sum_{i\in I}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}-\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|x\|^{2}+\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|F_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}-\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2},

so that

(2.6)

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{i\in I}\|H_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}-\|x\|^{2}\Big|=\Big|\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|F_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}-\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\Big|.

Now,

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|F_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}-\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in\sigma}\|F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x)\|_{2}\big(\|F_{i}(x)\|_{2}+\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in I}\|F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x)\|_{2}\big(\|F_{i}(x)\|_{2}+\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\|F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x)\|_{2}\|F_{i}(x)\|_{2}+\sum_{i\in I}\|F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x)\|_{2}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\big(\sum_{i\in I}\|F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big(\sum_{i\in I}\|F_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\big)^{1/2}+\big(\sum_{i\in I}\|F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big(\sum_{i\in I}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=2\|x\|\big(\sum_{i\in I}\|F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=2\|x\|\big(\sum_{i\in I}[F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x),F_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x)]_{\text{tr}}\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=2\|x\|\big(\sum_{i\in I}\|F_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}+\sum_{i\in I}\|G_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}-2\text{Re}\sum_{i\in I}[F_{i}(x),G_{i}(x)]_{\text{tr}}\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=2\|x\|\big(2\|x\|^{2}-2\text{Re}\sum_{i\in I}[F_{i}(x),G_{i}(x)]_{\text{tr}}\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|x\|\big(2\|x\|^{2}-2(1-\delta)\|x\|^{2}\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=2\|x\|\big(2\delta\|x\|^{2}\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{2\delta}\|x\|^{2},

which in equation (
2.3
) implies that

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{i\in I}\|H_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}-\|x\|^{2}\Big|\leq 2\sqrt{2\delta}\|x\|^{2}

and hence

\displaystyle(1-2\sqrt{2\delta})\|x\|^{2}\leq\sum_{i\in I}\|H_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\leq(1+2\sqrt{2\delta})\|x\|^{2}\ \text{for all}\ x\in\mathcal{H}.

The proof is complete.
∎

The following example illustrates Theorem
2.8
from a perturbation perspective. Here,
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is constructed as a small perturbation of a Parseval HS-frame
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, so that both remain Parseval HS-frames and are sufficiently close in the inner product sense as required by condition (
2.5
). As a consequence, every weaving of
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I}
forms an HS-frame with explicitly controlled frame bounds.

Example 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{K}=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
, and let
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be the canonical orthonormal basis of
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
. Define
G_{i}:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})\to\mathcal{C}_{2}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}))
as

\displaystyle G_{i}(x)=\langle x,e_{i}\rangle e_{i}\otimes e_{i},\ i\in\mathbb{N}.

Then, it can be easily seen that
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Parseval HS-frame for
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
.

Next, define
F_{i}:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})\to\mathcal{C}_{2}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}))
as

\displaystyle F_{i}(x)=\frac{199}{200}\langle x,e_{i}\rangle e_{i}\otimes e_{i}+\frac{\sqrt{399}}{200}\langle x,e_{i}\rangle e_{i+1}\otimes e_{i+1},\ i\in\mathbb{N}.

Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\|F_{i}(x)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|\frac{199}{200}\langle x,e_{i}\rangle e_{i}\otimes e_{i}+\frac{\sqrt{399}}{200}\langle x,e_{i}\rangle e_{i+1}\otimes e_{i+1}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\left[\frac{199}{200}\langle x,e_{i}\rangle e_{i}\otimes e_{i}+\frac{\sqrt{399}}{200}\langle x,e_{i}\rangle e_{i+1}\otimes e_{i+1},\frac{199}{200}\langle x,e_{i}\rangle e_{i}\otimes e_{i}+\frac{\sqrt{399}}{200}\langle x,e_{i}\rangle e_{i+1}\otimes e_{i+1}\right]_{\text{tr}}

\displaystyle=\frac{39601}{40000}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}|\langle x,e_{i}\rangle|^{2}[e_{i}\otimes e_{i},e_{i}\otimes e_{i}]_{\text{tr}}+\frac{199\sqrt{399}}{40000}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}|\langle x,e_{i}\rangle|^{2}[e_{i}\otimes e_{i},e_{i+1}\otimes e_{i+1}]_{\text{tr}}

\displaystyle+\frac{199\sqrt{399}}{40000}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}|\langle x,e_{i}\rangle|^{2}[e_{i+1}\otimes e_{i+1},e_{i}\otimes e_{i}]_{\text{tr}}+\frac{399}{40000}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}|\langle x,e_{i}\rangle|^{2}[e_{i+1}\otimes e_{i+1},e_{i+1}\otimes e_{i+1}]_{\text{tr}}

\displaystyle=\frac{39601}{40000}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}|\langle x,e_{i}\rangle|^{2}\langle e_{i},e_{i}\rangle\langle e_{i},e_{i}\rangle+\frac{199\sqrt{399}}{40000}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}|\langle x,e_{i}\rangle|^{2}\langle e_{i},e_{i+1}\rangle\langle e_{i+1},e_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{199\sqrt{399}}{40000}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}|\langle x,e_{i}\rangle|^{2}\langle e_{i+1},e_{i}\rangle\langle e_{i},e_{i+1}\rangle+\frac{399}{40000}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}|\langle x,e_{i}\rangle|^{2}\langle e_{i+1},e_{i+1}\rangle\langle e_{i+1},e_{i+1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{39601}{40000}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}|\langle x,e_{i}\rangle|^{2}+0+\frac{399}{40000}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}|\langle x,e_{i}\rangle|^{2}+0

\displaystyle=\frac{39601}{40000}\|x\|^{2}+\frac{399}{40000}\|x\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|x\|^{2}\ \text{for all}\ x\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}).

This shows that
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Parseval HS-frame for
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
.

For any
x\in\mathcal{H}
, we compute

\displaystyle\text{Re}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}[F_{i}(x),G_{i}(x)]_{\text{tr}}

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\frac{199}{200}\big[\langle x,e_{i}\rangle e_{i}\otimes e_{i},\langle x,e_{i}\rangle e_{i}\otimes e_{i}\big]_{\text{tr}}+\frac{\sqrt{399}}{200}\big[\langle x,e_{i}\rangle e_{i+1}\otimes e_{i+1},\langle x,e_{i}\rangle e_{i}\otimes e_{i}\big]_{\text{tr}}\right)

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\frac{199}{200}|\langle x,e_{i}\rangle|^{2}[e_{i}\otimes e_{i},e_{i}\otimes e_{i}]_{\text{tr}}+\frac{\sqrt{399}}{200}|\langle x,e_{i}\rangle|^{2}[e_{i+1}\otimes e_{i+1},e_{i}\otimes e_{i}]_{\text{tr}}\right)

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\frac{199}{200}|\langle x,e_{i}\rangle|^{2}\langle e_{i},e_{i}\rangle\langle e_{i},e_{i}\rangle+\frac{\sqrt{399}}{200}|\langle x,e_{i}\rangle|^{2}\langle e_{i+1},e_{i}\rangle\langle e_{i},e_{i+1}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\frac{199}{200}|\langle x,e_{i}\rangle|^{2}+0\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{199}{200}\|x\|^{2}.

This implies that the condition (
2.5
) of Theorem
2.8
is satisfied for
\delta=0.005
. Therefore, corresponding to every
\sigma\subset\mathbb{N}
, the weaving
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in\sigma}\cup\{G_{i}\}_{i\in\sigma^{c}}
is an HS-frame for
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
with frame bounds:

\displaystyle A=1-2\sqrt{2\delta}=0.8\quad\text{and}\quad B=1+2\sqrt{2\delta}=1.2.

3.
Conclusion

This paper presents new results on the stability of Hilbert-Schmidt frames under finite perturbations, motivated by real-world scenarios where only a limited number of frame elements are affected. Unlike classical
\mu
-perturbation results, such as those in
[
5
]
, which assume uniform perturbation across all frame elements and lead to full weaving, our results address a more practical and common setting in applications. In real-world systems such as wireless communication networks, sensor arrays, and distributed data processing, errors and data loss are typically localized due to packet drop, hardware failure, or transient noise affecting only a small number of frame elements. Our results model this localized distortion by permitting finite changes in frame elements and providing explicit bounds under which the perturbed sequence remains an HS-frame. This localized perturbation model not only reflects actual operational challenges more accurately but also ensures that signal reconstruction and processing systems remain stable and reliable even in the presence of partial data loss.
By bridging the gap between theory and practical implementation, our results contribute a robust and realistic framework for the design of resilient operator-valued frame systems, enhancing their applicability in real-time and fault-tolerant signal processing environments. In addition, we extend our analysis to the infinite setting by introducing decay conditions on the perturbations. These results demonstrate that stable weaving can still be achieved even when infinitely many frame elements are perturbed, provided the perturbations decay sufficiently fast (e.g., polynomially or exponentially).

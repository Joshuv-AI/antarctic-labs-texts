Magnetic dipole moments adjacent to doubly-magic nuclei in self-consistent mean-field theory with realistic spin-isospin and tensor forces
H. Nakada 1,2, H. Iwata 3

Abstract
Magnetic dipole ( M ​ 1 M1 ) moments in nuclei neighboring the doubly-magic core are investigated by the self-consistent mean-field (SCMF) approaches allowing the breaking of the time-reversal symmetry. By the SCMF calculations with the M3Y-P6 interaction, which keeps realistic spin-isospin and tensor channels, the M ​ 1 M1 moments are well reproduced, particularly those in the nuclei adjacent to j ​ j jj -closed magicity. The results are in better agreement with the data than those with the Gogny-D1S interaction, slightly better than those of UNEDF1 supplemented by a spin-isospin channel adjusted to the M ​ 1 M1 moments themselves, and comparable to the shell-model results with the chiral effective-field-theory ( χ \chi EFT) interaction. Analyses via the quadrupole moments, occupation numbers and the lowest-order perturbation theory elucidate cooperative effects of quadrupole deformation and spin correlation on the displacement from the Schmidt values, which has been known in terms of the quenching of the spin matrix elements. It is shown that a significant portion of the spin correlation is carried by the spin-isospin and tensor channels in the effective interaction. However, while agreement is remarkable at 131 Sn m , 133 Sn and 209 Pb, discrepancies remain at the Z = odd Z=\mathrm{odd} nuclei 133 Sb, 207 Ti and 209 Bi, as in the χ \chi EFT-based shell-model results.

I
Introduction

Nucleon’s spin plays specific roles in nuclear structure.
Its coupling to the orbital angular momentum yields
the spin-orbit (
\ell s
) splitting of the single-particle (s.p.) levels,
which is a source of the magic numbers
associated with the so-called
jj
-closed shell
ref:deShalit-Talmi
.
The
\ell s
splitting significantly depends
on the proton (
Z
) and neutron (
N
) numbers,
giving rise to the appearance and disappearance of the magic numbers
in nuclei far from
\beta
-stability
ref:SP08
;
ref:OGSS20
.
Moreover, nuclear weak processes,

e.g.
,
\beta
-decay, double-
\beta
-decay, and neutrino scattering,
often occur through the spin degrees of freedom (d.o.f.)
ref:deShalit-Talmi
;
ref:BM1
;
ref:FS01
;
ref:BLM15
.
Because of the relevance to astrophysics and fundamental physics,
understanding and proper treatment of nucleon spin are highly desired.

The self-consistent mean-field (SCMF) theory
provides us with a unique and promising framework
to describe nuclear properties ranging from stable to unstable
and light to heavy nuclei.
In practice, it has the ability to reproduce and predict
nuclear magic numbers all over the nuclear chart
from scratch
ref:VB72
;
ref:NS14
.
The SCMF theory connects properties of finite nuclei
to those of the infinite nuclear matter,
making best use of the experiments on Earth to predict nuclear properties
under astrophysical environments,
e.g.
, neutron stars.
This advantage can hold for the spin properties,
as far as they are well examined in finite nuclei.
There have been arguments for reinterpreting the nuclear SCMF theory
as density-functional theory (DFT)
ref:PS91
;
ref:Ring16
;
ref:Colo20
.
The SCMF equations have the identical form to the Kohn-Sham (KS)
ref:KS65

or Kohn-Sham–Bogoliubov-de Gennes equation
ref:OGK88
.
The KS theory may be extended
so that principal variables could be constituted
by a class of physical quantities
represented by the one-body density matrix,
whether it contains
\rho(\mathbf{r})
or not
ref:Nak23
.
It seems sensible to identify the nuclear SCMF theory,
which could cover the spin properties,
with the (generalized) KS theory for nuclei,
although we keep calling it SCMF theory in this paper to avoid confusion.

The SCMF theory needs an effective interaction as its input,
which involves effects of many-body correlations
and corresponds to the energy-density functional (EDF) of the KS theory.
It is never a trivial task to establish the effective interaction.
A popular method is fitting parameters to measured quantities
with respect to nuclear structure
ref:UNEDF1
;
ref:UNEDF2
,
after assuming the form of EDF, typically the Skyrme form.
However, since the spin d.o.f. tend to be masked
as an effect of the Pauli principle and nucleonic interaction,
it is not easy to fix the spin-dependent channels
via fitting
ref:UNEDF1
;
ref:UNEDF2
.
As the spin operator is not invariant
under the time-reversal transformation
\mathcal{T}
,
this problem is connected to the
\mathcal{T}
-symmetry
in the mean field
ref:HR88
;
ref:FP89
;
ref:YCM06
;
ref:SDBG22
.
The
\mathcal{T}
-symmetry usually holds
in the ground states (g.s.’s) of even-even nuclei.
The spin properties are more visible in odd nuclei or proper excitations.
It should be kept in mind that the non-central channels in the interaction
may significantly influence the spin properties of finite nuclei.

The magnetic dipole (
M1
) moment is an observable sensitive to the spin.
In particular, those of nuclei neighboring the doubly-magic core
supply a good testing ground for the theoretical description
of the spin properties.
For the single-particle or single-hole states,
the
M1
moments are governed by the orbital of the last nucleon
ref:deShalit-Talmi
;
ref:BM1
;
ref:Blatt-Weisskopf
,
reaching the Schmidt values [see Eq. (
8
)].
However, the measured
M1
moments sizably deviate from the Schmidt values
in most nuclei
ref:Blatt-Weisskopf
,
implicating quenching of the spin matrix elements.
By microscopically investigating various effects in the perturbation theory,
the main source of quenching has been attributed
to the core polarization (CP)
ref:Tow87
;
ref:ASBH88
;
ref:LM18
,
after a history of disputes.
Thus, the degree of quenching reflects the stiffness of the doubly-magic core,
and is influenced by spin-dependent channels of the interaction.
As the
M1
moment is basically represented by the one-body density matrix,
it may be taken as a principal variable of the KS approach.
In this line,
it has been shown that the deformation and the
\mathcal{T}
-symmetry breaking
cooperatively bring on moderate quenching
ref:HR88
;
ref:FP89
;
ref:YCM06
;
ref:SDBG22
.
The approach has been extended to open-shell nuclei
ref:BE17
;
ref:PHGM21
;
ref:VGMB22
;
ref:BDDK23
.

The
M1
moments in the vicinity of the doubly-magic nuclei
have also been investigated
by applying the in-medium similarity-renormalization-group (IMSRG) treatment
of the nucleonic interaction and operator
based on the chiral effective field theory (
\chi
EFT)
ref:MCSB24
.
Roles of the CP in the quenching have been manifested
from a microscopic standpoint,
and corrections from the meson-exchange current (MEC) have been argued.

As well as the moments,

M1
and Gamow-Teller (GT) excitations are subject to the quenching
of the spin matrix elements
ref:Tow87
;
ref:ASBH88
.
Under an appropriate condition,
nuclear reactions pick up the spin d.o.f.,
possessing rates proportional to the
M1
or GT excitations.
Dominant roles of the CP in the quenching
have been supported in the nuclear reaction experiments
ref:WSOO97
.
It has also been suggested that the quenching takes place
in the isovector component,
leaving the isoscalar component almost unquenched
ref:MTNA15
.

In the nucleonic interaction,
spin-dependence partly comes from the central channel
in the one-pion exchange potential (OPEP).
The OPEP channel is not explicitly included in most SCMF calculations,
although a part of its effects might be substituted in other forms.
It deserves investigating the spin-dependent channels of effective interaction
in connection with the OPEP.
There is no doubt that the tensor force is contained
in the nucleonic interaction,
whose substantial part also originates from the pion exchange.
While the tensor force is not explicitly included
in the usual SCMF calculations,
there have been some SCMF approaches
in which the tensor force is incorporated
ref:SBF77
;
ref:CL98
;
ref:OSFG05
;
ref:BDOA06
;
ref:CSFB07
;
ref:Sky-TNS
;
ref:SC14
;
ref:Nak20
.
The tensor force affects the interaction between nucleons on specific orbits,
and this effect gives rise to

Z
- and
N
-dependence of the shell structure
ref:OGSS20
;
ref:OSFG05
;
ref:BDOA06
;
ref:Nak10
;
ref:NSM13
;
ref:NS14
;
ref:SC14
;
ref:NI24b
.
Indeed, prediction of the magic numbers is greatly improved
by incorporating a realistic tensor force
into the effective interaction
ref:NS14
.
As the tensor force influences spin properties of nuclei,
it is of interest to investigate the effects of the realistic tensor force
on the quenching in the
M1
moments.
Because the contribution of the tensor force
vanishes in homogeneous nuclear matter,
it is vital to separate the tensor-force effects
on the properties of finite nuclei
from those of the central channels
when extrapolating spin properties on Earth to astrophysical environments.

One of the authors (H.N.) has developed effective interactions
for SCMF approaches
ref:Nak20
;
ref:Nak03

by modifying the Michigan-three-range-Yukawa (M3Y)
interaction
ref:M3Y
;
ref:M3Y-P
.
The M3Y interaction was originally obtained from the
G
-matrix.
In the M3Y-P
n
interactions,
where
n
is an integer specifying the parameter-set,
density-dependent terms are added to reproduce the saturation
and values of several parameters are phenomenologically modified.
In the M3Y-P6 interaction
ref:Nak13
,
the central channel of OPEP
and the tensor force derived from the
G
-matrix are kept unchanged
with no phenomenological modification.
Having this spin-isospin and tensor forces,
the M3Y-P6 interaction well describes the nuclear magic numbers
up to the
Z
- and
N
-dependence
ref:NS14
;
ref:Nak20
,
and the s.p. potential up to as high as

\approx 80\,\mathrm{MeV}

ref:NI24
.
The tensor force in M3Y-P6 is scrutinized in the
N
-dependence
of the s.p. level spacing between
p0d_{3/2}
and
p1s_{1/2}

from
40
Ca to
48
Ca
ref:NSM13
;
ref:Nak20
.
The significance of the tensor force in the
M1
excitations
has been shown for the
M1
excitations at
208
Pb
ref:SHOT08
.
The connection to bare nucleonic interaction
and the reasonable agreement with available data
supply a good reason to regard this effective interaction
as a
semi-realistic
one.
In particular, kept unchanged from the original M3Y interaction
derived from the
G
-matrix,
the spin-isospin and tensor channels are considered realistic.
In this work, we apply the M3Y-P6 interaction
to the
M1
moments adjacent to the doubly-magic nuclei.

For comparison, we also employ the Gogny-D1S interaction
ref:D1S
.
Successfully describing many nuclear properties,
it is one of the standards of phenomenological SCMF interactions
and is useful to inspect the roles of the realistic spin-isospin
and tensor forces.
Comparison is also made with the results given in Refs.
ref:SDBG22

and
ref:MCSB24
.

II
Theoretical backgrounds and numerical setups

II.1
Symmetries in mean-field calculations

The total angular momentum
J
is a half-integer in odd-
A
nuclei.
Therefore, it is impossible to keep the
\mathcal{T}
-symmetry
with a single pure state
|\Phi\rangle
,
because
\mathcal{T}^{2}|\Phi\rangle=-|\Phi\rangle

and thereby
\mathcal{T}|\Phi\rangle
is necessarily orthogonal
to
|\Phi\rangle
.
The
\mathcal{T}
-symmetry should be broken through the spin d.o.f.,
supplying a good opportunity to investigate the spin properties.
However, computations without imposing the
\mathcal{T}
-symmetry
are often demanding,
particularly when we apply the semi-realistic interaction.
On the other hand,
it is expected that the spherical symmetry is not severely broken
in the nuclei neighboring the doubly-magic core.
To reduce computational cost,
it is reasonable to take into account the symmetry breaking to a minimal extent.
We here assume the axial symmetry
under which the
z
-component of the total angular momentum (
J_{z}
)
is conserved,
the parity (
\mathcal{P}
) symmetry,
and the symmetry with respect to the product of
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{T}
,
where
\mathcal{R}\,(:=e^{-i\pi J_{y}})
denotes the reflection,
as discussed in Appendix
A
.
Note that, because
(\mathcal{RT})^{2}|\Phi\rangle=|\Phi\rangle

ref:BM1
,
the
\mathcal{RT}
-symmetry or
\mathcal{RPT}
-symmetry can be imposed
even when the
\mathcal{T}
-symmetry is violated.
Under the phase convention adopted here,
we have
(\mathcal{RPT})|\Phi\rangle=|\Phi\rangle
.

II.2
Setups for mean-field calculations

We carry out the Hartree-Fock (HF) calculations
with the M3Y-P6 interaction,
employing the Gaussian expansion method (GEM)
ref:NS02
;
ref:Nak06
;
ref:Nak08
.
The basis functions are given in Ref.
ref:Nak08
.
The space is truncated by the orbital angular momentum of the bases

\ell_{\mathrm{cut}}
,
for which
\ell_{\mathrm{cut}}=7
(
\ell_{\mathrm{cut}}=8
)
is adopted for the
N\leq 51
(
N\geq 81
) nuclei.

The computer code has been newly extended
for the mean-field calculations assuming the
J_{z}
,
\mathcal{P}

and
\mathcal{RT}
symmetries,
by which the spin d.o.f. can be active.
Whereas the present study is constrained to the HF results,
the code has been adapted to the Hartree-Fock-Bogoliubov calculations,
as discussed in Appendix
B
.

For odd-
A
nuclei,
several states having different
J_{z}
values lie with close energies,
which correspond to magnetic substates.
We compute all these states and adopt the lowest-energy state among them.
The total angular momentum
J
is not a good quantum number
in the SCMF solution,
unlike the observed g.s.
In the calculations of moments below,
we reference the
J
values of the true g.s.
It is mentioned that each of the present SCMF solutions
is dominated by a component compatible with this treatment,
as will be recognized from the figures in Sec.
III.4
.

II.3
Magnetic dipole moments

The nuclear
M1
moment operator is,

\mu^{(1)}_{0}=\sum_{\tau=p,n}\sum_{i\in\tau}\big(g_{\ell,\tau}\,\ell_{i,z}+g_{s,\tau}\,s_{i,z}\big)\,,

(1)

where we denote orbital and spin angular momenta of the
i
-th nucleon
by
\bm{\ell}_{i}
and
\mathbf{s}_{i}
.
The s.p.
g
-factors on Eq. (
1
) are

g_{\ell,p}=1
,
g_{\ell,n}=0
,
and
g_{s,\tau}=2\mu_{\tau}
with the measured
M1
moment of a single nucleon

\mu_{\tau}

ref:PDG20
.
In the energy eigenstate of the
{}^{A}Z
nuclide

\big|\Psi_{JM}(^{A}Z)\big\rangle
,
the total angular momentum
J
is a good quantum number.
At the g.s.’s of odd-
A
nuclei,

J\neq 0
and its magnetic substates labeled by
M
are degenerate.
The expectation value of
\mu^{(1)}_{0}
at
\big|\Psi_{JM}(^{A}Z)\big\rangle

linearly depends on
M
,
owing to the Wigner-Eckart theorem,

\begin{split}\big\langle\Psi_{JM}(^{A}Z)\big|\mu^{(1)}_{0}\big|\Psi_{JM}(^{A}Z)\big\rangle&=\frac{\big\langle\Psi_{J}(^{A}Z)\big|\big|\mu^{(1)}\big|\big|\Psi_{J}(^{A}Z)\big\rangle}{\sqrt{2J+1}}\,\big(J\,M\,1\,0\big|J\,M\big)\\
&=M\,\frac{\big\langle\Psi_{J}(^{A}Z)\big|\big|\mu^{(1)}\big|\big|\Psi_{J}(^{A}Z)\big\rangle}{\sqrt{J(J+1)(2J+1)}}\,.\end{split}

(2)

The
M1
moment is defined for the
M=J
state,

\begin{split}\mu(^{A}Z):&=\big\langle\Psi_{JJ}(^{A}Z)\big|\mu^{(1)}_{0}\big|\Psi_{JJ}(^{A}Z)\big\rangle\\
&=\sqrt{\frac{J}{(J+1)(2J+1)}}\,\big\langle\Psi_{J}(^{A}Z)\big|\big|\mu^{(1)}\big|\big|\Psi_{J}(^{A}Z)\big\rangle\,.\end{split}

(3)

Instead of computing the reduced matrix element,
Eq. (
3
) can be rewritten as

\mu(^{A}Z)=J\,\frac{\big\langle\Psi_{JM}(^{A}Z)\big|\mu^{(1)}_{0}\big|\Psi_{JM}(^{A}Z)\big\rangle}{\big\langle\Psi_{JM}(^{A}Z)\big|J_{z}\big|\Psi_{JM}(^{A}Z)\big\rangle}\,,

(4)

with the
J_{z}
operator on the denominator of the rhs given by

J_{z}=\sum_{i}\big(\ell_{i,z}+s_{i,z}\big)\,.

(5)

In the SCMF solution
|\Phi(^{A}Z)\rangle
,
in which the full rotational symmetry is spontaneously broken,
degeneracy with respect to
M
is lost
and we choose the state having the lowest energy among them.
Applying Eq. (
4
) to the SCMF solution,
we calculate the
M1
moment within the SCMF,

\mu^{\mathrm{MF}}(^{A}Z)=J\,\frac{\big\langle\Phi(^{A}Z)\big|\mu^{(1)}_{0}\big|\Phi(^{A}Z)\big\rangle}{\big\langle\Phi(^{A}Z)\big|J_{z}\big|\Phi(^{A}Z)\big\rangle}\,.

(6)

In the present case,

M
is kept to be a good quantum number in
|\Phi\rangle
.
The
J
value on the rhs of Eq. (
6
)
is that of the corresponding energy eigenstate.

Because
J
should be a good quantum number in the energy eigenstates,
one may consider that the
J
-projected state
better corresponds to the eigenstate,

\big|\Psi_{JM}(^{A}Z)\big\rangle\propto P_{JM}|\Phi\rangle
,
where
P_{JM}
is the angular-momentum-projection operator.
The
M1
moment calculated with the projected state is

\mu^{\mathrm{proj.}}(^{A}Z)=J\,\frac{\big\langle\Phi(^{A}Z)\big|P_{JM}\,\mu^{(1)}_{0}P_{JM}\,\big|\Phi(^{A}Z)\big\rangle}{\big\langle\Phi(^{A}Z)\big|P_{JM}\,J_{z}\,P_{JM}\big|\Phi(^{A}Z)\big\rangle}\,.

(7)

However,
J
-projection requires a demanding and careful computation,
particularly for odd-
A
nuclei.
Whereas a code for the g.s.’s of even-even nuclei
has been developed
ref:AN22
,
no
J
-projection code is available for the SCMF solutions in odd-
A
nuclei
associated with the GEM at this moment.
In the vicinity of the doubly-magic core,
the breaking of the
J
quantum number is not quite serious.
As will be shown in Sec.
III.2
,
we have
\mu^{\mathrm{MF}}(^{A}Z)\approx\mu^{\mathrm{proj.}}(^{A}Z)
.

For a state having one-particle or one-hole on top of the doubly-magic core
without any residual correlations,
the orbit of the particle or hole determines the
M1
moment
because the core has no contribution.
This s.p. value is known as the Schmidt value
ref:BM1
,

\mu^{\mathrm{s.p.}}(\tau,\ell j)=j\bigg[g_{\ell,\tau}\pm\frac{g_{s,\tau}-g_{\ell,\tau}}{2\ell+1}\bigg]\quad\Big(\,j=\ell\pm\frac{1}{2}\,\Big)\,.

(8)

Even in the nuclei adjacent to the doubly-magic core,
the measured
M1
moments deviate from
\mu^{\mathrm{s.p.}}(\tau,\ell j)

in practice.
We shall mainly discuss displacement of the measured (
\mu^{\mathrm{exp.}}
)
and calculated
M1
moments from
\mu^{\mathrm{s.p.}}(\tau,\ell j)
,
defining

\varDelta\mu(^{A}Z):=\mu(^{A}Z)-\mu^{\mathrm{s.p.}}(\tau,\ell j)\,.

(9)

To be precise,
the center-of-mass (c.m.) motion is desirable to be removed
in the orbital angular momentum
\bm{\ell}_{i}

in Eqs. (
1
) and (
5
)
with replacing
\bm{\ell}_{i}
by
\bm{\ell}^{\prime}_{i}
,
where

\bm{\ell}^{\prime}_{i}=\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{i}\times\mathbf{p}^{\prime}_{i}\,;\quad\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{i}=\mathbf{r}_{i}-\mathbf{R}\,,~\mathbf{p}^{\prime}_{i}=\mathbf{p}_{i}-\frac{\mathbf{P}}{A}\,,~\mathbf{R}:=\frac{1}{A}\sum_{i}\mathbf{r}_{i}\,,~\mathbf{P}:=\sum_{i}\mathbf{p}_{i}\,.

(10)

We have confirmed that this c.m. correction is negligibly small.
In relation to the deviation from
\mu^{\mathrm{s.p.}}
,
the influences of the MEC
and the isobar (
e.g.
,
\Delta
-
h
) excitation
have been argued.
We neglect them, yielding a brief discussion on this point
in Sec.
IV
.

II.4
Electric quadrupole moments

While the main subject of this paper is the
M1
moment,
the quadrupole moments also carry useful information
about the s.p. nature of the nuclei neighboring the doubly-magic core.
The quadrupole operator is given as

Q^{(2)}_{0,\tau}=\sqrt{\frac{16\pi}{5}}\sum_{i\in\tau}r_{i}^{\prime 2}\,Y^{(2)}_{0}(\hat{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}_{i})\,.

(11)

Here and in the following,

r=|\mathbf{r}|
and
\hat{\mathbf{r}}=\mathbf{r}/r
.
The electric quadrupole (
E2
) moment is

\begin{split}eQ_{p}(^{A}Z):&=\big\langle\Psi_{JJ}(^{A}Z)\big|eQ^{(2)}_{0,p}\big|\Psi_{JJ}(^{A}Z)\big\rangle\\
&=\frac{\big\langle\Psi_{J}(^{A}Z)\big|\big|eQ^{(2)}_{p}\big|\big|\Psi_{J}(^{A}Z)\big\rangle}{\sqrt{2J+1}}\,\big(J\,J\,2\,0\big|J\,J\big)\,.\end{split}

(12)

From the SCMF solution
|\Phi(^{A}Z)\rangle
,
the
E2
moments are calculated by

eQ_{p}^{\mathrm{MF}}(^{A}Z)=c_{M}\big\langle\Phi(^{A}Z)\big|eQ^{(2)}_{0,p}\big|\Phi(^{A}Z)\big\rangle\,.

(13)

Since
|\Phi(^{A}Z)\rangle
is selected as the lowest-energy state
among several candidates having various
J_{z}
values,
we have the factor
c_{M}
on the rhs of Eq. (
13
),
for which we adopt

c_{M}=\frac{3J^{2}-J(J+1)}{3M^{2}-J(J+1)}\,;\quad M:=\big\langle\Phi(^{A}Z)\big|J_{z}\big|\Phi(^{A}Z)\big\rangle\,,

(14)

as indicated by the Wigner-Eckart theorem.
The s.p. value of
Q(^{A}Z)
is expressed as

Q^{\mathrm{s.p.}}(n\ell j)=\pm\sqrt{\frac{16\pi}{5}}\langle n\ell j|r^{2}|n\ell j\rangle_{r}\,\frac{\langle\ell j||Y^{(2)}_{0}(\hat{\mathbf{r}})||\ell j\rangle}{\sqrt{2j+1}}\,\big(j\,j\,2\,0\,|\,j\,j\big)\,.

(15)

We estimate the radial matrix element
\langle n\ell j|r^{2}|n\ell j\rangle_{r}

by the isotropic harmonic-oscillator wave-function
with
\hbar\omega=41.2A^{-1/3}\,\mathrm{MeV}
.
The negative sign on the rhs of Eq. (
15
)
applies to single-hole states.
Since
|Q_{\tau}|
grows as
A
increases because of
r_{i}^{\prime 2}
,
we divide the
E2
moment by
R^{2}=(1.12\,A^{1/3}\,\mathrm{fm})^{2}

when plotting.

III.5
Analysis via lowest-order perturbation

As long as the erosion of the doubly-magic core is not serious,
analysis in terms of the perturbation theory should be useful.
Owing to its linear nature,
the lowest-order perturbation allows us
to separate the contributions of each channel of the interaction.

In the lowest-order perturbation,

\varDelta\mu
is expressed by the diagram of Fig.
11

and the following equation
ref:AH54
,

\varDelta\mu^{\mathrm{pert.}}(^{A}Z)=-2\,\big\langle j\,m=j\big|\mu^{(1)}_{0}\big|j\,(j_{2}\,j_{1}^{-1})^{(1)};j\,m=j\big\rangle\,\frac{\big\langle j\,(j_{2}\,j_{1}^{-1})^{(1)};j\big|V\big|j\big\rangle}{\epsilon_{j_{2}}-\epsilon_{j_{1}}}\,.

(16)

Here
|j\rangle
is the unperturbed state,
which has a single particle or hole on the orbit
j

on top of the doubly-magic core,
and
\big|j\,(j_{2}\,j_{1}^{-1})^{(1)};j\big\rangle

is composed of the particle or hole on
j

and the CP component
(j_{2}\,j_{1}^{-1})^{(1)}
, the excitation from
j_{1}
to
j_{2}
.
For the operator
\mu^{(1)}
to act on,
the polarization is restricted to the
1^{+}
spin-parity,
and
\varDelta\mu^{\mathrm{pert.}}
of Eq. (
16
) thereby stands for
CP due to the spin correlation.
The coupling of the particle (or hole) on
j
to
(j_{2}\,j_{1}^{-1})^{(1)}

must result in
J=j
due to the angular-momentum conservation via
V
.
All the relevant s.p. energies (
\epsilon_{j_{1}}
,
\epsilon_{j_{2}}
)
and functions are obtained by the SCMF calculations at the doubly-magic nucleus.
Note that

\big\langle j\,m\big|\mu^{(1)}_{0}\big|j\,(j_{2}\,j_{1}^{-1})^{(1)};j\,m\big\rangle

is evaluated from the s.p. matrix element
\langle j_{1}||\mu^{(1)}||j_{2}\rangle
,
and
\big\langle j\,(j_{2}\,j_{1}^{-1})^{(1)};j\big|V\big|j\big\rangle

from the two-body elements of the interaction

\langle j\,j_{2}|V|j\,j_{1}\rangle

IV
Summary and discussions

We have investigated magnetic dipole (
M1
) moments
adjacent to the doubly-magic nuclei
by the self-consistent mean-field (SCMF) approaches.
Hartree-Fock calculations assuming the
J_{z}
(axial),
\mathcal{P}
(parity)
and
\mathcal{RT}

(
\mathcal{R}
is the reflection with respect to the
y
-axis) symmetries
but allowing breaking of the
\mathcal{T}
(time-reversal) symmetry
have been implemented with finite-range interactions.
No adjustable parameter is newly introduced.
The M3Y-P6 interaction, which keeps realistic spin-isospin and tensor channels,
is primarily applied,
and its results are compared with those obtained from other interactions.
The
M1
moments deviate from the Schmidt values,
implying the quenching of the spin matrix elements.
The source of the deviation in the SCMF results
has been analyzed via the quadrupole moments, occupation numbers,
and comparison with the results of the lowest-order perturbation.

The SCMF results with M3Y-P6 are in reasonable agreement
with the measured
M1
moments of nuclei neighboring the doubly-magic core.
The deviation from the Schmidt values is well described
for the nuclei adjacent to
jj
-closed magicity,
owing to the cooperative effects of weak quadrupole deformation
and spin correlation.
Compared with the results of D1S,
which is one of the standard SCMF effective interactions,
M3Y-P6 yields improvement on almost all nuclei under investigation.
The M3Y-P6 results are comparable to, or even slightly better than,
the UNEDF1 results of Ref.
ref:SDBG22
,
whose spin-isospin channel was fitted to the measured
M1
moments.
The SCMF results with M3Y-P6 are also close to the shell-model results
with the interaction derived from the chiral effective-field theory (
\chi
EFT)
in Ref.
ref:MCSB24
.
In the comparison with the D1S results,
the improvement can be attributed primarily to the spin-isospin channel
and secondarily to the tensor channel in M3Y-P6,
which are considered realistic, keeping a microscopic origin.
The UNEDF1 results suggest that
a central spin-isospin channel can express certain parts of these effects,
even imitating some tensor-force effects.
It is still noted that the distinction between the central and tensor channels
is vital when extrapolating the properties in an astrophysical environment.

Further consequences can be addressed.
Good agreement of the D1S results without the
J
-projection
and those with the projection in Ref.
ref:SDBG22

suggests that the SCMF wave-functions may be used to describe
M1
moments
without handling additional rotational correlations.
This implies that the
M1
moments can be a principal variable
of the generalized Kohn-Sham (KS) framework.
In the results from
\chi
EFT
ref:MCSB24
,
the meson-exchange current (MEC) has improved the agreement
with experimental data,
which counteracts the CP in most cases
ref:Tow87
;
ref:ASBH88
;
ref:MCSB24
.
On the other hand, the coupling of the virtual isobar excitation
to the magnetic field has not been taken into account.
Since these effects tend to cancel each other
ref:Tow87
;
ref:ASBH88
,
though not completely,
the
M1
moments could be calculated
by the one-body operator of Eq. (
1
) to moderate precision.
The results reported in Refs.
ref:BE17
;
ref:PHGM21
;
ref:VGMB22
;
ref:BDDK23

indicate that the KS approach is promising to describe
M1
moments in nuclei
departing from the doubly-magic core.
However, it is not easy to fix spin-dependent channels of effective interaction
completely by fitting.
The present work exemplifies
that microscopic theory could provide good guidance.
Whereas the one-pion exchange is an important ingredient
of the central spin-isospin channel,
there are few codes applicable to the Yukawa interaction,
with the exception of the code
used in the present work
ref:NS02
;
ref:Nak06
;
ref:Nak08
.
Along this line,
local approximation based on the density-matrix expansion
(DME)
ref:NV72
;
ref:CB78
;
ref:DCK10

has been applied to the Fock term in,
e.g.
, Ref.
ref:ZBFN24
.
According to Ref.
ref:ZCBF21
,
the DME leads to errors of a few to ten percent,
which is moderately good but not excellent precision.
It is desirable to keep paying attention to the precision of the DME,
particularly for the spin-dependent channels
ref:DCK10
.

Within the perturbation theory,
the
M1
moments near the
\ell s
-closed nuclei
hardly deviate from the Schmidt values at the lowest order,

Appendix A
Relation of time-reversality to other symmetries in one-body fields

Symmetries concerning the rotation may restrict
the effect of the time-reversality (
i.e.
,
\mathcal{T}
-symmetry).
In this Appendix,
we discuss the relations of the
\mathcal{T}
-symmetry
to the spherical (
\mathbf{J}
) or axial (
J_{z}
), parity (
\mathcal{P}
),
and reflection (
\mathcal{R}
) symmetries
in terms of the one-body density matrix,
particularly in the spherical-basis representation.
The argument in this Appendix is a basis of the numerical calculations
in the present work.
The self-consistent symmetries were minutely investigated
for the local densities and currents in Ref.
ref:RDN10
.
In contrast, our discussion is not constrained to the local fields
and may be transparent for specific cases.

The mean fields are represented
by the one-body density matrix

\varrho_{kk^{\prime}}\,(:=\langle\Phi|c_{k^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{k}|\Phi\rangle)

and the pairing tensor

\kappa_{kk^{\prime}}\,(:=\langle\Phi|c_{k^{\prime}}c_{k}|\Phi\rangle)
, in general.
Here,
k
and
k^{\prime}
denote the s.p. bases,
and
c_{k}^{\dagger}
(
c_{k}
) is
the corresponding creation (annihilation) operator.
Symmetries in the mean fields are nothing but
those in
\varrho_{kk^{\prime}}
and
\kappa_{kk^{\prime}}
.
Note that
\varrho_{kk^{\prime}}
is hermitian
(
i.e.
,
\varrho_{k^{\prime}k}=\varrho_{kk^{\prime}}^{\ast}
)
and
\kappa_{kk^{\prime}}
is skew-symmetric
(
i.e.
,
\kappa_{k^{\prime}k}=-\kappa_{kk^{\prime}}
).
We here restrict ourselves to the symmetries in
\varrho_{kk^{\prime}}
;
those in
\kappa_{kk^{\prime}}
can be discussed analogously.
Adopting the spherical bases, we take
k=(\nu\ell jm)
,
where
\ell
,
j
and
m
are the orbital angular momentum,
the summed angular momentum and its
z
-component, respectively.
The label
\nu
distinguishes the radial wave functions.
We do not consider proton-neutron mixing in the s.p. states,
and omit the isospin index for the sake of simplicity.
By using the s.p. basis function
\phi_{k}(\mathbf{r}\sigma)
,
where
\sigma
is the spin index,

\varrho_{kk^{\prime}}
is readily transformed to the coordinate representation

\varrho(\mathbf{r}\sigma,\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime})
,

\varrho_{kk^{\prime}}=\sum_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\int d^{3}r\,d^{3}r^{\prime}\,\varrho(\mathbf{r}\sigma,\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime})\,\phi_{k}^{\ast}(\mathbf{r}\sigma)\,\phi_{k^{\prime}}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime})\,.

(17)

The time-reversed state of
k
is denoted by
\bar{k}
.
In the concrete,
\bar{k}
is
(\nu\ell j\,-m)
with an appropriate phase,
for which we use the convention
(-)^{j+\ell-m}
.
Namely, the s.p. bases are
\mathcal{RPT}
-invariant.
The
\mathcal{T}
-symmetry in the mean-fields is recognized as,

\varrho_{\bar{k}\bar{k^{\prime}}}=\varrho_{kk^{\prime}}^{\ast}\,.

(18)

The complex conjugate on the rhs is a result of the
\mathcal{T}
operation
on the transformation coefficients of the s.p. states
[
e.g.
,
U
and
V
in Eq. (
29
)].

Under the
\mathbf{J}
-symmetry,

\varrho_{kk^{\prime}}
is diagonal with respect to
j
and
m
,
and has no dependence on
m
,

\varrho_{kk^{\prime}}=\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\delta_{mm^{\prime}}\,\varrho^{(j)}_{\nu\ell,\nu^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}}\,.

(19)

The
\mathcal{R}
-symmetry plays no additional roles in this case.
Equation (
18
) is reduced to,
applying the present phase convention,

(-)^{\ell-\ell^{\prime}}\,\varrho^{(j)}_{\nu\ell,\nu^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}}=\varrho^{(j)^{\ast}}_{\nu\ell,\nu^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}}\,.

(20)

We usually have the
\mathcal{P}
-symmetry when we keep the spherical symmetry.
Then,
\varrho_{kk^{\prime}}
is also diagonal with respect to
\ell
,
and Eq. (
19
) reads,

\varrho_{kk^{\prime}}=\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\delta_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\delta_{mm^{\prime}}\,\varrho^{(\ell j)}_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}\,.

(21)

The condition of the
\mathcal{T}
-symmetry (
18
) becomes

\varrho^{(\ell j)}_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}=\varrho^{(\ell j)^{\ast}}_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}\,.

(22)

Equation (
22
) indicates,
since
\nu
is the label for the radial part of the s.p. basis,
that the violation of the
\mathcal{T}
-symmetry may take place
only through the off-diagonal element with respect to the radial part,
irrelevant to the angular-spin part.
It is not a surprising consequence
since the
(\mathbf{J}+\mathcal{P})
-symmetry
fixes the angular-spin part of the s.p. states.

Under the
J_{z}
-symmetry,

\varrho_{kk^{\prime}}
is diagonal with respect to
m
but not necessarily to
j
,

\varrho_{kk^{\prime}}=\delta_{mm^{\prime}}\,\varrho^{(m)}_{\nu\ell j,\nu^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\,.

(23)

Equation (
18
) is reduced to,

(-)^{j+\ell-j^{\prime}-\ell^{\prime}}\,\varrho^{(-m)}_{\nu\ell j,\nu^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}}=\varrho^{(m)^{\ast}}_{\nu\ell j,\nu^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\,.

(24)

If we have the
\mathcal{P}
-symmetry in addition,
Eq. (
24
) leads to

(-)^{j-j^{\prime}}\,\varrho^{(-m)}_{\nu\ell j,\nu^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}}=\varrho^{(m)^{\ast}}_{\nu\ell j,\nu^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\,.

(25)

When we have the
\mathcal{R}
-symmetry,
even without the
\mathcal{P}
-symmetry,
the density matrix in Eq. (
23
) should satisfy,

(-)^{j-j^{\prime}}\,\varrho^{(-m)}_{\nu\ell j,\nu^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}}=\varrho^{(m)}_{\nu\ell j,\nu^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\,.

(26)

The
\mathcal{RT}
-symmetry
rather than the individual
\mathcal{R}
or
\mathcal{T}
symmetry
yields

(-)^{\ell-\ell^{\prime}}\,\varrho^{(m)}_{\nu\ell j,\nu^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}}=\varrho^{(m)^{\ast}}_{\nu\ell j,\nu^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\,,

(27)

and the
\mathcal{RPT}
-symmetry derives

\varrho^{(m)}_{\nu\ell j,\nu^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}}=\varrho^{(m)\ast}_{\nu\ell j,\nu^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\,.

(28)

Appendix B
Mean-field equation under
J_{z}
conservation

While the Hartree-Fock calculations have been applied in the present work,
in this Appendix we derive the mean-field equation in a more general respect;
namely, the Hartree-Fock-Bogoliubov (HFB) equation.
The creation and annihilation operators
associated with the s.p. basis
\phi_{\nu\ell jm}

are denoted by
c_{\nu\ell jm}^{\dagger}
and
c_{\nu\ell jm}
.
We define the modified annihilation operator
as
\tilde{c}_{\nu\ell jm}=(-)^{j+m}c_{\nu\ell j-m}

as in Ref.
ref:Nak06
.
Under the
J_{z}
conservation,
the Bogoliubov transformation is given by

\alpha_{n,m}^{\dagger}=\sum_{\nu\ell j}\big[U^{(m)}_{\nu\ell j,n}c_{\nu\ell jm}^{\dagger}+(-)^{j+m}\,V^{(-m)}_{\nu\ell j,n}\tilde{c}_{\nu\ell jm}\big]\,.

(29)

The density matrix and the pairing tensor are then expressed by

\varrho^{(m)}_{\nu\ell j,\nu^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}}=\sum_{n}V^{(-m)\ast}_{\nu\ell j,n}\,V^{(-m)}_{\nu^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime},n}\,,\quad\kappa^{(m)}_{\nu\ell j,\nu^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}}=\sum_{n}V^{(-m)\ast}_{\nu\ell j,n}\,U^{(-m)}_{\nu^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime},n}\,,

(30)

having the following properties,

\varrho^{(m)}_{\nu^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime},\nu\ell j}=\varrho^{(m)\ast}_{\nu\ell j,\nu^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\,,\quad\kappa^{(-m)}_{\nu^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime},\nu\ell j}=-\kappa^{(m)}_{\nu\ell j,\nu^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\,.

(31)

It is found that the HFB Hamiltonian has the structure as

\mathcal{H}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf{h}^{(m)}&0&0&\mathsf{\Delta}^{(m)}\\
0&\mathsf{h}^{(-m)}&\mathsf{\Delta}^{(-m)}&0\\
0&-\mathsf{\Delta}^{(m)\ast}&-\mathsf{h}^{(m)\ast}&0\\
-\mathsf{\Delta}^{(-m)}&0&0&-\mathsf{h}^{(-m)\ast}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(32)

leading to the HFB equation as

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\,\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf{U}^{(m)}&\mathsf{V}^{(-m)\ast}\\
\mathsf{V}^{(m)}&\mathsf{U}^{(-m)\ast}\end{pmatrix}=&\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf{U}^{(m)}&\mathsf{V}^{(-m)\ast}\\
\mathsf{V}^{(m)}&\mathsf{U}^{(-m)\ast}\end{pmatrix}\,\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{diag}[\varepsilon^{(m)}]&0\\
0&-\mathrm{diag}[\varepsilon^{(-m)}]\end{pmatrix}\,;\\
&\qquad\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf{h}^{(m)}&\mathsf{\Delta}^{(m)}\\
-\mathsf{\Delta}^{(-m)\ast}&-\mathsf{h}^{(-m)\ast}\end{pmatrix}\,.\end{split}

(33)

From the total energy of the system
E

represented by
(\varrho,\kappa,\kappa^{\ast})
,

\mathsf{h}^{(m)}
is defined by the derivative of
E

with respect to
\varrho
,
and
\mathsf{\Delta}^{(m)}
by the derivative with respect to
\kappa^{\ast}
.
Corresponding to Eq. (
31
),
we have
\mathsf{h}^{(m)T}=\mathsf{h}^{(m)\ast}

and
\mathsf{\Delta}^{(-m)T}=-\mathsf{\Delta}^{(m)}
,
assuring
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
to be hermitian.
It is remarked that

\mathrm{dim}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{dim}(\mathcal{H})

and Eq. (
33
) yields
all the eigensolutions without doubling even in the HFB framework.
Its origin is the property of the pairing tensor
in Eq. (
31
),
which connects the matrices for
-m
with those for
m
,
leading to the structure of
\mathcal{H}
shown in Eq. (
32
).
Whereas we do not necessarily impose orthogonality
for the radial part of s.p. bases
ref:NS02
;
ref:Nak06
;
ref:Nak08
,
we write down Eq. (
33
) for orthogonal bases.
The norm matrix should be supplemented
for non-orthogonal bases
ref:Nak06
.

When we have the
\mathcal{P}
-conservation,
Eq. (
33
) is separable into the sectors of individual parities.
We here adopt the
\mathcal{RPT}
-invariant basis functions.
Under the
\mathcal{RPT}
symmetry,
we can restrict all the matrices
\mathsf{U}
,
\mathsf{V}
,

\mathsf{h}
and
\mathsf{\Delta}
to be real numbers,
as realized in Eq. (
28
).
The computer code has been developed accordingly,
and is planned to be published in the near future.

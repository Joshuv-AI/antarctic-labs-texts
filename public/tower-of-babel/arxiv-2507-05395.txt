Boundary regularity of optimal transport maps on convex domains
Tristan C. Collins, Freid Tong

Abstract
. We study the regularity of optimal transport maps between convex domains with quadratic cost. For nondegenerate C α C^{\alpha} -densities, we prove C 1 , 1 − ε C^{1,1-\varepsilon} -regularity of the potentials up to the boundary. If in addition the boundary is C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} , we improve this to C 2 , α C^{2,\alpha} -regularity. We also investigate pointwise C 1 , 1 C^{1,1} -regularity at boundary points. We obtain a complete characterization of pointwise C 1 , 1 C^{1,1} -regularity for planar polytopes in terms of the geometry of tangent cones. Furthermore, we study the regularity of optimal transport maps with degenerate densities on cones, which arise from recent developments in Kähler geometry. The main new technical tool we introduce is a monotonicity formula for optimal transport maps on convex domains which characterizes the homogeneity of blow-ups.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we study the global regularity properties of solutions of the Monge-Ampère equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle\det D^{2}u

\displaystyle=\frac{g(x)}{g^{\prime}(\nabla u(x))}\qquad x\in\Omega

\displaystyle\nabla u(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\Omega^{\prime}

where
\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}
are convex domains. This type of equation arises naturally in the study of optimal transportation with quadratic cost, as well as in various aspects of pure and applied mathematics
[
14
,
16
,
23
,
31
,
32
]
. The existence and uniqueness of a weak solution to (
1.1
) was established by Brenier
[
2
]
. Regularity of solutions to (
1.1
) has been a central issue in the field. In a series of landmark papers
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
, Caffarelli observed that the convexity of the domains is essential in order to establish any regularity of
u
, and he proved the following regularity results:

•

If
\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}
are bounded convex domains, and
C^{-1}<g,g^{\prime}<C
, then
u
is globally
C^{1,\delta}
continuous for some
\delta\in(0,1)

[
4
]
. This conclusion continues to hold if
g,g^{\prime}
are allowed to degenerate, but satisfy a doubling type condition
[
18
]
.

•

If
\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}
are uniformly convex with
C^{2}
boundary,
C^{-1}<g,g^{\prime}<C
, and
g,g^{\prime}
are
C^{\alpha}
-Hölder continuous, then
u\in C^{2,\alpha^{\prime}}(\overline{\Omega})

[
5
]
. Earlier works of Delanoe
[
15
]
(in dimension
2
), and Urbas
[
30
]
established the global
C^{2,\alpha^{\prime}}
regularity assuming
\partial\Omega,\partial\Omega^{\prime}
are
C^{2,1}
, and
g,g^{\prime}\in C^{1,1}
.

An important recent result of Chen-Liu-Wang
[
9
]
removed the uniform convexity condition, proving that
u\in C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
provided
C^{-1}<g,g^{\prime}<C
are
C^{\alpha}
, and
\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}
are convex and
C^{1,1}
-regular. This result is rather surprising, since the strict convexity of the boundary is a necessary condition for global
C^{2,\alpha}
regularity of solutions to the Dirichlet problem for the Monge-Ampère equation
[
7
,
10
,
25
,
29
]
. Assuming
\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}
are convex and
C^{1,1}
regular, Chen-Liu-Wang also establish the global
C^{1,1-\epsilon}
regularity (for any
\epsilon>0
), and
W^{2,p}
regularity (for any
p>0
) assuming that
C^{-1}<g,g^{\prime}<C
are continuous up to the boundary. Previously, Caffarelli established interior
W^{2,p}
estimates in
[
6
]
, while global
W^{2,p}
estimates were obtained for the Dirichlet problem by Savin
[
24
]
, and for the second boundary value problem by Chen-Figalli
[
8
]
.

Much less is known about the boundary regularity of
u
, beyond Caffarelli’s
C^{1,\delta}
-estimate, if one does not impose any regularity assumptions on the boundary. In this direction, a notable result of Savin-Yu
[
26
]
in dimension
2
establishes the global
C^{1,1-\epsilon}
and
W^{2,p}
regularity of solutions when
g=g^{\prime}=1
for arbitrary convex domains
\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

In this paper we develop new techniques for the regularity of optimal transport maps on general convex domains. Our first result is a global
W^{2,p}
and
C^{1,1-\epsilon}
estimate for solutions of (
1.1
) in arbitrary convex domains in any dimension, assuming
C^{-1}\leq g,g^{\prime}\leq C
are
C^{\alpha}
. This extends the results of Savin-Yu
[
26
]
to all dimensions.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u
satisfy (
1.1
),
\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}
are convex, and
0<g(x)\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}),0<g^{\prime}(y)\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega^{\prime}})
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
,
u\in C^{1,1-\varepsilon}(\overline{\Omega})
, and for any
p>1
,
u\in W^{2,p}(\overline{\Omega})
.

See Theorems
5.1
and
5.2
for the more precise and quantitative statement.

We also obtain the a global
C^{2,\alpha}
-regularity result, which extends the result of Chen-Liu-Wang
[
9
]
from
C^{1,1}
convex domains to
C^{1,\beta}
convex domains.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
u
satisfies (
1.1
),
0<g(x)\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}),0<g^{\prime}(y)\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega^{\prime}})
. Suppose in addition
\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}
are convex and have
C^{1,\beta}
-boundary for some
\beta\in(0,1)
. Then

for
\gamma=\min(\alpha,\beta)
.

See Theorem
5.3
for the more precise and quantitative statement.

The exponents in both Theorem

and

are optimal. Inparticular, it is known that for general convex domains, solutions of (
1.1
) can fail to be globally
C^{1,1}
. A concrete example of this failure was first explicitly exhibited in
[

. Given this, we study the pointwise
C^{1,1}
-regularity of

at boundary points and relate it to the geometry of tangent cones of

at the boundary points; see Theorem

, and

. We define a property called

, which roughly says that the solution exhibits homogeneous behaviour at the level of its sub-level sets. If

are non-degenerate, roundness is equivalent to being pointwise

, and thus our results can be used to study when pointwise
C^{1,1}
-regularity holds. For convex polytopes in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we completely characterize the pointwise
C^{1,1}
-regularity of solutions to (
1.1
) when
g=g^{\prime}=1
; see Theorem
6.4
. More generally, we study the regularity properties of optimal transport maps with degenerate, homogeneous densities in arbitrary convex domains, motivated in part by recent progress in Kähler geometry
[
12
,
13
,
22

; see Theorem
6.5
and
7.1
.

The main new technical tool we introduce is a monotonicity formula for optimal transport maps on convex domains which is constant on homogeneous solutions of the optimal transport problem; see Section
3
. This formula is heavily motivated by the ideas of Kim-McCann
[
20
]
and Kim-McCann-Warren
[
21
]
, interpreting optimal transport maps as high-codimension, space-like, area maximizing submanifolds of pseudo-Riemannian manifolds. For recent progress on the regularity theory of optimal transport from this point of view, we refer the reader to the recent work of Brendle-Léger-McCann-Rankin
[
1
]
, and Yuan
[
35
]
. Technically, the monotonicity formula leads to short proofs of the uniform density property, and the uniform obliqueness property for optimal transport maps, and therefore, yields a somewhat simplified proof of the main results of
[
3
,
9
]
.

One of the main applications of the monotoncitiy formula is to deduce that appropriate rescalings of optimal transport maps converge subsequentially to homogeneous optimal transport maps, which play the role of
tangent cones
in the context of geometric PDEs. This allows us to study the regularity of optimal transport maps through the properties of their “tangent cones”.

The outline of the paper is as follows. Section
2
recounts the basic notions of optimal transportation and convex geometry that we will need. Section
3
contains the proof of the key monotonicity formula. Section
4
proves that appropriate rescalings of optimal transport maps converge to homogeneous optimal transport maps. In Section
5
we prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
; see Theorem
5.1
,
5.2
, and Theorem
5.3
for the more precise statement that we prove.

In the remainder of the paper, we apply our techniques to study the regularity properties of optimal transport maps with possibly degenerate densities, on general convex domains. In Section
6
, we study the shapes of sections of optimal transport maps between cones equipped with homogeneous densities. We introduce a notion of “roundness” for sections, which can be viewed as an generalization of
C^{1,1}
-regularity. We show that roundness is related to the existence of homogeneous optimal transport maps, and we give conditions on the domains
\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}
guaranteeing the roundness of sections. For planar polygonal domains with uniform density, we completely characterize optimal transport maps with round sections.

Homogeneous optimal transport maps have recently been of independent interest due to connections to the existence of complete Calabi-Yau metrics
[
12
,
13
]
and a problem of Tian-Yau
[
28
,
34
]
. By solving a free-boundary Monge-Ampère equation, the authors and Yau
[
13
]
showed the existence of homogeneous solutions of the optimal transport problem when
\Omega
is a strict cone,
g=1
and
\Omega^{\prime}=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:y_{n}>0\}
is a half-space, equipped with the density
g^{\prime}(y)=y_{n}^{k}
. The first author and Firester
[
11
]
extended these techniques to solve a rather general class of free-boundary Monge-Ampère equations, which in particular yields the existence of optimal transport maps in the above setting, but with

a general, non-negative homogeneous density which can degenerate on
\partial\Omega
.

Finally, in Section
7
we show that the presence of a flat-side in the boundary can lead to solutions exhibiting mixed homogeneity. These results build in an essential way on the work of Jhaveri-Savin
[
18

who studied the regularity properties of optimal transport maps with degenerate densities between half-spaces.

2.
Preliminaries

Let us describe the optimal transport problem. Given domains
\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
equipped with measure
\mu,\nu
respectively with
\mu(\Omega)=\nu(\Omega^{\prime})
. An optimal transport map (with quadratic cost) is a measurable map
T:\Omega\to\Omega^{\prime}
that satisfy
T_{\sharp}\mu=\nu
and that minimizes the cost function

c(T):=-\int_{\Omega}x\cdot T(x)d\mu(x)

amongst all measurable maps that pushforward
\mu
to
\nu
. A fundamental theorem of Brenier
[
2
]
says that a unique optimal transport map
T
always exists and it is given by the gradient of a convex potential
T(x)=\nabla u(x)
for
\mu
-almost every
x
. Moreover, the inverse optimal transport map
T^{-1}:\Omega^{\prime}\to\Omega
is given by the gradient of the Legendre transform of
u
, defined by

v(y):=\sup_{x\in\Omega}(\langle x,y\rangle-u(x)).

When
\Omega
and
\Omega^{\prime}
are convex, and the measures
d\mu=g(x)dx
,
d\nu=g^{\prime}(y)dy
are absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure, Caffarelli
[
3
]
proved that
u
satisfy the following Monge-Ampere equation in the Alexandrov sense

(2.1)

\begin{cases}\det D^{2}u(x)=\frac{g(x)}{g^{\prime}(\nabla u(x))}\text{ for }x\in\Omega\\
\nabla u(\Omega)=\Omega^{\prime}.\end{cases}

The convexity of the domains plays an important role in Caffarelli’s result, as he observed that if the domains are not convex, then Brenier’s solutions can fail to be an Alexandrov solution to (
2.1
), and
u
may not even be
C^{1}
.

In this paper, we will always assume the domains
(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
are convex and we will take the approach of using the equation (
2.1
) as the definition of an optimal transport map. This has the advantage that it makes sense even if the domain and target are non-compact and have infinite measure, which is necessary to be able to talk about blow-ups. Moreover, we always assume the measures
(g(x)dx,g^{\prime}(y)dy)
satisfy a
volume doubling condition
, which is the crucial assumption required for Caffarelli’s
C^{1,\delta}
-regularity theory to hold
[
4
,
18
]
. For the remainder of this paper, we define an optimal transport map as follows.

Definition 1
.

An
optimal transport map
(or more precisely, an optimal transport map between convex domains equipped with doubling measures) will be given by the data
((\Omega,\mu),(\Omega^{\prime},\nu),u,v)
where

(1)

(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})\subset(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{x},\mathbb{R}^{n}_{y})
are two open (not necessarily compact) convex sets.

(2)

\mu
and
\nu
are measures with support
\overline{\Omega}
and
\overline{\Omega^{\prime}}
respectively such that

(a)

They are absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure, and we set
d\mu=g(x)dx
, and
d\nu=g^{\prime}(y)dy
.

(b)

The measures
\mu
(and
\nu
) are doubling, that is there exist constant
C>1
such that for any ellipsoid
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
centered at a point
x\in\overline{\Omega}
(or centered at
y\in\overline{\Omega^{\prime}}
), we have

\mu(E)\leq C\mu(\frac{1}{2}E)\left(\text{or }\nu(E)\leq C\nu(\frac{1}{2}E)\right).

The constant
C>1
is called the doubling constant of
(\mu,\nu)
.

(3)

(u,v)
are a pair of strictly convex functions on
(\overline{\Omega},\overline{\Omega}^{\prime})
such that

(a)

\nabla u(\Omega)=\Omega^{\prime}
and
\nabla v(\Omega^{\prime})=\Omega
.

(b)

Let
(\bar{u},\bar{v})
be the minimal convex extensions of
(u,v)
,

\bar{u}(x):=\sup\{l(x):l\text{ is tangent to }u\text{ at some point }x_{0}\in\Omega\},

\bar{v}(y):=\sup\{l(y):l\text{ is tangent to }v\text{ at some point }y_{0}\in\Omega^{\prime}\}.

Then
\bar{u},\bar{v}\in C^{1,\alpha}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and
(\bar{u},\bar{v})=(v^{\star},u^{\star})
where
(v^{\star},u^{\star})
are the Legendre transforms of
(v,u)
.

(c)

(\bar{u},\bar{v})
are Alexandrov solutions to the Monge-Ampére equations

(2.2)

\det D^{2}\bar{u}(x)=\frac{g(x)}{g^{\prime}(\nabla\bar{u}(x))}\chi_{\Omega}(x)

and

(2.3)

\det D^{2}\bar{v}(y)=\frac{g^{\prime}(y)}{g(\nabla\bar{v}(y))}\chi_{\Omega^{\prime}}(y).

respectively.

Remark 1
.

By
[
18
, Theorem 1.1]
(also see
[
26
, Remark 2.1]
), the properties for
u,v
are always satisfied by optimal transport maps when the measures
(\mu,\nu)
are doubling measures supported on convex domains.

Given an optimal transport map
((\Omega,d\mu),(\Omega^{\prime},d\nu),u,v)
, we will always use
x
to denote the coordinate of the domain and
y
for the coordinate of the target, which are dual to each other. The following transformations act on the space of optimal transport maps.

(1)

Shift
: Given
c\in\mathbb{R}
,
((\Omega,d\mu),(\Omega^{\prime},d\nu),u+c,v-c)
is another optimal transport map.

(2)

Translation
: Given
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there is a shift transformation

\tau_{x_{0}}((\Omega,d\mu),(\Omega^{\prime},d\nu),u,v)=((\Omega+x_{0},g(x-x_{0})dx),(\Omega^{\prime},g^{\prime}(y)dy),u(x-x_{0}),v(y)+\langle x_{0},y\rangle)

and similarly given
y_{0}\in(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{\prime}
, there is a shift given by

\tau^{\prime}_{y_{0}}((\Omega,d\mu),(\Omega^{\prime},d\nu),u,v)=((\Omega,g(x)dx),(\Omega^{\prime}+y_{0},g^{\prime}(y-y_{0})dy),u(x)+\langle x,y_{0}\rangle,v(y-y_{0}))

(3)

Scaling
: Given
\lambda>0
there is a scaling transformation

\lambda\cdot((\Omega,d\mu),(\Omega^{\prime},d\nu),u,v)=((\Omega,d\mu),(\lambda^{-1}\Omega^{\prime},\lambda^{-1}_{\sharp}d\nu),\frac{u(x)}{\lambda},\frac{v(\lambda y)}{\lambda}).

(4)

Affine transformations
: Given a matrix
A\in GL(n,\mathbb{R})
, it acts on the space of optimal transport maps by

A\cdot((\Omega,d\mu),(\Omega^{\prime},d\nu),u,v)=((A(\Omega),A_{\sharp}d\mu),(A^{-t}(\Omega^{\prime}),A^{-t}_{\sharp}d\nu),u\circ A^{-1},v\circ A^{t}).

2.1.
Basics of Convex geometry

In this section, we collect some basic concepts of convex geometry that will be used in later sections.

The following lemma regarding the shape of bounded convex sets is well-known. (see
[
17
]
for a proof.)

Proposition 2.1

(John’s Lemma)
.

Given any bounded convex set
\Omega
with non-empty interior and barycenter at the origin. There exist a unique ellipsoid
E
centered at the origin such that

E\subset\Omega\subset n^{\frac{3}{2}}E.

Such a
E
is called the
John ellipsoid
of
\Omega
.

Given two subsets
X,Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the
Hausdorff distance
between them is defined as

d_{H}(X,Y):=\sup_{x\in X,y\in Y}\{d(x,Y),d(y,X)\}

The Hausdorff distance induces a topology on the space of subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
called the Hausdorff topology. The following proposition is well known. (see
[
27
]
for a proof.)

Proposition 2.2
.

The class of compact convex sets in
\overline{B_{R}(0)}
is compact with respect to the Hausdorff topology. Moreover, the volume function is continuous on the class of convex subsets with respect to the Hausdorff topology.

Definition 2
.

We say that a sequence of subsets
\Omega_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

converges locally in the Hausdorff sense
to a subset
\Omega_{\infty}
if for any
R>1
, we have

d_{H}(\Omega_{i}\cap\overline{B_{R}(0)},\Omega_{\infty}\cap\overline{B_{R}(0)})\to 0

as
i\to\infty
.

A
convex cone

\mathtt{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a convex set which is closed under scalar multiplication by positive real numbers. Given a convex cone
\mathtt{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there is a
dual cone

\mathtt{C}^{\circ}
defined by

\mathtt{C}^{\circ}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle x,y\rangle\geq 0\text{ for all }x\in\mathtt{C}\}.

If
\mathtt{C}
is a closed convex cone, then
\mathtt{C}^{\circ\circ}=\mathtt{C}
.

Definition 3
.

We say that a convex cone
\mathtt{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a
strict cone
if it contains no infinite lines, or equivalently if
\mathtt{C}^{\circ}
dimension
n
.

We recall the notion of the tangent cone of a convex set at a point on its boundary.

Definition 4
.

Let
\Omega
be an open convex set and
0\in\partial\Omega
. Then the
tangent cone
of
\Omega
at
0
is given by the closed convex cone

\mathtt{C}:=\overline{\{tv:t\geq 0,v\in\Omega\}}.

2.2.
Sections, centered sections, and extrinsic balls

Let
((\Omega,g(x)dx),(\Omega^{\prime},g^{\prime}(y)dy),u,v)
be an optimal transport map. Following Caffarelli
[
3
]
, for any
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
and
h>0
, we define the
section of
u
at
x_{0}
of height
h
to be the convex set

S_{h}(u,x_{0}):=\{x\in\Omega:u(x)-u(x_{0})-\nabla u(x_{0})(x-x_{0})\leq h\}.

We also define a
centered section
of
u
at
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
to be

S^{c}_{h}(u,x_{0}):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\bar{u}(x)-\bar{u}(x_{0})-p\cdot(x-x_{0})\leq h\}

where recall that
\bar{u}
is the minimal convex extension of
u
, and
p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is chosen so that
S^{c}_{h}(u,x_{0})
has center of mass
x_{0}
. By
[
5
]
, if the graph of
\bar{u}
contains no line, then centered sections always exist.

Given a centered section
S=S^{c}_{h}(u,x_{0})
, one can define the normalized section by

\tilde{S}=A(S)

where
A
is the (unique) symmetric positive definite matrix such that
A(\mathcal{E})=B_{1}(0)
where
\mathcal{E}
is the John ellipsoid of
S
. We call
A
the
normalization matrix
of
S
, and it satisfies
(\det A)|S|\sim 1
. We also define the normalized potential
\tilde{u}:\tilde{S}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\tilde{u}(\tilde{x})=\frac{(\bar{u}-l)(A^{-1}\tilde{x})}{h}.

where
l(x)=u(x_{0})+p\cdot(x-x_{0})
. It follows that
\tilde{S}=\{\tilde{u}<1\}
, and the pair
(\tilde{S},\tilde{u})
is a normalized pair. Moreover,
\nabla\tilde{u}:\tilde{\Omega}\to\tilde{\Omega}^{\prime}
where
\tilde{\Omega}=A(\Omega)
and
\tilde{\Omega}^{\prime}=hA^{-1}(\Omega^{\prime})
.

Furthermore, the following properties were established for doubling measures by Caffarelli
[
5
]
and Jhaveri-Savin
[
18
]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

[
18

There exist constant
c>0
depends only on dimension, and

depending additionally on the doubling constant of

and

(1)

B_{C^{-1}}\subset\nabla\tilde{u}(\tilde{S})\subset B_{C}

\implies C^{-1}h(S_{h}^{c}(u,x_{0})-x_{0})^{\circ}\subset\nabla(u-l)(S_{h}^{c}(u,x_{0}))\subset Ch(S_{h}^{c}(u,x_{0})-x_{0})^{\circ}.

(2)

C^{-1}h^{n}\leq|S_{h}^{c}(u,x_{0})||\nabla u(S_{h}^{c}(u,x_{0}))|\leq Ch^{n}
.

(3)

S_{ch}^{c}(u,x_{0})\cap\overline{\Omega}\subset S_{h}(x_{0})\subset S_{Ch}^{c}(u,x_{0})\cap\overline{\Omega}
.

(4)

S_{C^{-1}h}(v,\nabla u(x_{0}))\subset\nabla u(S_{h}(u,x_{0}))\subset S_{Ch}(v,\nabla u(x_{0}))
.

In
[
5

.

[

For any pair of constants

]
. (see also

]
)

Proposition 2.5
.

Given an optimal transport map
((\Omega,g(x)dx),(\Omega^{\prime},g^{\prime}(y)dy),u,v)

and

g
and

has doubling constant bounded by

(2)

and

Then there exist

\|\bar{u}\|_{C^{1,\delta}(B_{R})}+\|\bar{v}\|_{C^{1,\delta}(B_{R})}\leq C

3.
Monotonicity formula

The main goal of this section is to prove a monotonicity formula for optimal transport maps on convex domains. We note that Yuan

used a somewhat related monotonicity formula in the special case

Theorem 3.1

Let
((\Omega,g(x)dx),(\Omega^{\prime},g^{\prime}(y)dy),u,v)
be an optimal transport map with

(i)

g\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})

l

While the basic idea of the proof of Theorem

The first part is a calculation assuming sufficient regularity. The second part is an approximation argument. For our later purposes, it is useful to have a very weak notion of (sub)-homogeneity. We make the following technical definition

Definition 5

Let
((\Omega,g(x)dx),(\Omega^{\prime},g^{\prime}(y)dy),u,v)

4

and define the Laplacian

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det D^{2}u}\,e^{-f}}\partial_{i}(u^{ij}\sqrt{\det D^{2}u}\,e^{-f}\partial_{j}\phi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{g}\partial_{i}(u^{ij}g\partial_{j}\phi)

\displaystyle=u^{ij}\phi_{ij}-u^{ik}u^{pj}u_{ikp}\phi_{j}+u^{ij}\frac{1}{g}g_{i}\phi_{j}

where

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\Delta_{f}\varphi)\psi\sqrt{\det D^{2}u}e^{-f}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla\varphi,\nabla\psi\rangle_{u}\sqrt{\det D^{2}u}e^{-f}

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\varphi,\nabla\psi\rangle_{u}

\displaystyle:=u^{ij}\varphi_{i}\psi_{j}

provided

is compactly supported in

from which the integration by parts formula follows trivially.

The main observation is that we have the following formulas:

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}u

\displaystyle=n+\sum_{j}\frac{1}{g^{\prime}}\frac{\partial g^{\prime}}{\partial y_{j}}u_{j}

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}\left(x\cdot\nabla u(x)\right)

\displaystyle=2n+\sum_{j}\frac{1}{g^{\prime}}\frac{\partial g^{\prime}}{\partial y_{j}}u_{j}+\sum_{j}\frac{1}{g}\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{j}}x_{j}

Let us prove the first formula. From the expression for

\Delta_{f}u=n-u^{iq}u^{pj}u_{iqp}u_{j}+u^{ij}\frac{1}{g}g_{i}u_{j}

By differentiating the optimal transport equation we have

so that

\displaystyle u^{ik}u^{pj}u_{ikp}u_{j}

\displaystyle=u^{pj}u_{j}\frac{1}{g}g_{p}-\frac{1}{g^{\prime}}\frac{\partial g^{\prime}}{\partial y_{j}}u_{j}

Therefore, we have

\Delta_{f}u=n+\sum_{j}\frac{1}{g^{\prime}}\frac{\partial g^{\prime}}{\partial y_{j}}u_{j}

Next we consider the second formula in (

\Delta_{f}(x_{m}u_{m})=x_{m}\Delta_{f}u_{m}+u_{m}\Delta_{f}x_{m}+2

We compute

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{g}\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{m}}

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}x_{m}

\displaystyle=-u^{pm}u^{iq}u_{ipq}+u^{im}\frac{1}{g}g_{i}=\frac{1}{g^{\prime}}\frac{\partial g^{\prime}}{\partial y_{m}}.

and the second formula follows.

Using these two functions we prove the monotonicity formula. Let us denote by

where

Similarly, by the optimal transport equation we have

\displaystyle\int_{\{\psi\leq r\}}\left(\sum_{j}\frac{1}{g^{\prime}}\frac{\partial g^{\prime}}{\partial y_{j}}u_{j}-k\right)\,g(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\{\psi\leq r\}}\left(\sum_{j}\frac{1}{g^{\prime}}\frac{\partial g^{\prime}}{\partial y_{j}}y_{j}-k\right)\,g^{\prime}(y)dy

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\psi\rangle_{u}

\displaystyle=\gamma u^{ij}u_{i}(u_{j}+\sum_{m}x_{m}u_{mj})+u^{ij}\left((u_{i}+\sum_{p}x_{p}u_{pi})((u_{j}+\sum_{m}x_{m}u_{mj})\right)

.

•

As

converges in

We can now apply the result of Proposition

\displaystyle r_{1}^{-\kappa_{*}}\int_{\Upsilon_{\epsilon}\cap\{\psi_{\epsilon}<r_{1}\}}\widehat{g}_{\epsilon}(x)dx-r_{2}^{-\kappa_{*}}\int_{\Upsilon_{\epsilon}\cap\{\psi_{\epsilon}<r_{1}\}}\widehat{g}_{\epsilon}(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq-(1+\sqrt{1+\gamma_{*}})^{2}\int_{r_{2}}^{r_{1}}s^{-(\kappa_{*}+1)}\int_{\Upsilon_{\epsilon}\cap\{\psi_{\epsilon}=s\}}|(\sqrt{1+\gamma_{*}})\nabla u_{\epsilon}-x|^{2}_{u_{\epsilon}}\frac{\widehat{g}_{\epsilon}(x)}{|\nabla\psi_{\epsilon}|}\,ds\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)

We can also prove an effective version of the monotonicity formula for Hölder continuous densities.

Proposition 3.2
.

Given an optimal transport map
((\Omega,g(x)dx),(\Omega^{\prime},g^{\prime}(y)dy),u,v)
where the densities
(g(x),g^{\prime}(y))
satisfy

and

[g]_{\alpha,\overline{\Omega}}+[g^{\prime}]_{\alpha,\overline{\Omega^{\prime}}}\leq C

for some

and

,
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, and

The key is the following lemma, which establishes the weak sub-homogeneity of non-degenerate Hölder continuous densities at small (but fixed) scales.

Lemma 1
.

Given an optimal transport map
((\Omega,g(x)dx),(\Omega^{\prime},g^{\prime}(y)dy),u,v)
where the densities
(g(x),g^{\prime}(y))
are smooth and satisfy

C^{-1}\leq g(x),g^{\prime}(y)\leq C

and

[g]_{\alpha,\overline{\Omega}}+[g^{\prime}]_{\alpha,\overline{\Omega^{\prime}}}\leq C

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
C>1
.
Then there exist
r_{0}>0
,
B>0
, and
\varepsilon>0
depending only on
n,\alpha,C
such that we have

\int_{D_{r}}(x\cdot\nabla g)dx\leq Br^{\varepsilon}\int_{D_{r}}g(x)dx.

for all
r<r_{0}
.

Proof.

We write the integral in radial coordinates

(3.10)

\int_{D_{r}}(x\cdot\nabla g)dx=c_{n}\int_{t\in S^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\phi(t)}(s(\nabla_{t}g)(st))s^{n-1}\,dsd\sigma(t)

where
\phi(t)
is chosen so that
(\phi(t)t)\cdot\nabla u(\phi(t)t)=r^{2}
. We can integrate by parts along radial rays to get rid of the derivative on

\int_{0}^{\phi}g^{\prime}(s)s^{n}ds=-n\int_{0}^{\phi}g(s)s^{n-1}ds+g(\phi)\phi^{n}=n\int_{0}^{\phi}(g(\phi)-g(s))s^{n-1}ds\leq C\phi^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\phi}s^{n-1}ds.

Putting this into equation (
3.10
), we get

\int_{D_{r}}(x\cdot\nabla g)dx\leq C(\text{diam}(D_{r}))^{\alpha}\int_{D_{r}}dx.

The constant
C
controls the doubling constant of
(g,g^{\prime})
, hence by the uniform convexity estimate (Proposition

), we have

D_{r}(u,0)\subset S_{r^{2}}(u,0)\subset B_{Cr^{\delta}}(0)

for some

. This implies
|g(x)-g(0)|\leq Cr^{\delta\alpha}
, therefore for
r<\frac{1}{(2C^{2})^{\frac{1}{\delta\alpha}}}
, we have
g(x)\geq g(0)-\frac{C^{-1}}{2}\geq\frac{C^{-1}}{2}
, and
\text{diam}(D_{r})\leq Cr^{\delta}
. Hence we have

\int_{D_{r}}(x\cdot\nabla g)dx\leq Br^{\delta\alpha}\int_{D_{r}}g(x)dx.

∎

Proof of Proposition

.

If

. For the general case, we can approximate

4.
Homogeneity of Blow-ups

In this section, we use the monotonicity formula to show that the blow-ups of optimal transport maps are always homogeneous. We will treat two cases, the first when the densities are non-degenerate and Hölder continuous, and the second case when the densities are homogeneous.

First let us define what a blow-up is.

Definition 6
.

((\Omega,g(x)dx),(\Omega^{\prime},g^{\prime}(y)dy),u,v)
be an optimal transport map with
0\in\overline{\Omega}\cap\overline{\Omega^{\prime}}
such that
u(0)=|\nabla u|(0)=0
. We call an optimal transport map

((\Omega_{\infty},g_{\infty}(x)dx),(\Omega_{\infty}^{\prime},g^{\prime}_{\infty}(y)dy),u_{\infty},v_{\infty})

a
blow-up
of
((\Omega,g(x)dx),(\Omega^{\prime},g^{\prime}(y)dy),u,v)
at
0\in\overline{\Omega}
along scales
h_{i}\to 0
, if there is
R>1
and a sequence of positive symmetric matrices
A_{h_{i}}
satisfying

B_{R^{-1}}(0)\subset A_{h_{i}}(S_{h_{i}}^{c}(u,0))\subset B_{R}(0)

and a sequence of positive constants
c_{i}>0
, such that the sequence of rescaled optimal transport maps
((\Omega_{i},g_{i}(x)dx),(\Omega^{\prime}_{i},g^{\prime}_{i}(y)dy),u_{i},v_{i})
given by

\Omega_{i}:=A_{h_{i}}(\Omega),\qquad\Omega_{i}^{\prime}:=h_{i}^{-1}A_{h_{i}}^{-1}(\Omega^{\prime}),

g_{i}(x):=c_{i}g(A_{h_{i}}^{-1}x),\qquad g^{\prime}_{i}(y):=c_{i}h_{i}^{n}(\det A_{h_{i}})^{2}g^{\prime}(h_{i}A_{h_{i}}y),

u_{i}(x):=\frac{u(A_{h_{i}}^{-1}x)}{h_{i}},\qquad v_{i}(y):=\frac{v(h_{i}A_{h_{i}}y)}{h_{i}},

converges to
((\Omega_{\infty},g_{\infty}(x)dx),(\Omega_{\infty}^{\prime},g^{\prime}_{\infty}(y)dy),u_{\infty},v_{\infty})
in the following sense

(1)

(\Omega_{i},\Omega^{\prime}_{i})\to(\Omega_{\infty},\Omega_{\infty}^{\prime})
in the locally Hausdorff sense.

(2)

(g_{i}(x)dx,g_{i}^{\prime}(y)dy)\to(g_{\infty}(x)dx,g_{\infty}^{\prime}(y)dy)
in the weak sense of measures.

(3)

The minimal convex extensions
(\bar{u}_{i},\bar{v}_{i})
converges to the minimal convex extensions
(\bar{u}_{\infty},\bar{v}_{\infty})
in
C^{1,\alpha}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

4.1.
With Hölder continuous density

In this section, we prove the existence and homogeneity of blow-ups when
(g(x),g^{\prime}(y))
are non-degenerate and Hölder continuous.

We first prove a uniform density estimate when
(g(x),g^{\prime}(y))
are non-degenerate and Hölder continuous on general convex domains. This estimate improves upon the previously known uniform density estimates (
[
5
, Section 3]
,
[
9
, Section 2]
), which required additional regularity assumptions on the boundary of
\partial\Omega
.

Lemma 2

(Uniform density)
.

Given an optimal transport map
((\Omega,g(x)dx),(\Omega^{\prime},g^{\prime}(y)dy),u,v)
where the densities
(g(x),g^{\prime}(y))
satisfy

C^{-1}\leq g(x),g^{\prime}(y)\leq C

and

[g]_{\alpha,\overline{\Omega}}+[g^{\prime}]_{\alpha,\overline{\Omega^{\prime}}}\leq C

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
C>1
. Then for any
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
and
0<h\leq 1
, we have

\frac{|S_{h}(u,x_{0})|}{h^{\frac{n}{2}}}\geq c|S_{1}(u,0)|

for
c>0
depending only on
n,\alpha
and
C
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.2
, there exist
A
,
h_{0}
and
\varepsilon_{0}
depending on
C
such that for any
0<h<h_{0}
, we have

h^{-\frac{n}{2}}\mu(D_{h^{\frac{1}{2}}}(u,0))\geq e^{-Ah_{0}^{\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}}}h_{0}^{-\frac{n}{2}}\mu(D_{h_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}}(u,0)).

Hence

|D_{h^{\frac{1}{2}}}(u,0)|\geq c\mu(D_{h^{\frac{1}{2}}}(u,0))\geq ce^{-Ah_{0}^{\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}}}h_{0}^{-\frac{n}{2}}\mu(D_{h_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}}(u,0))h^{\frac{n}{2}}\geq ch^{\frac{n}{2}}|D_{h_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}}(u,0)|.

Together with Proposition
2.6
, we have

|S_{h}(u,0)|\geq ch^{\frac{n}{2}}|S_{h_{0}}(u,0)|,

and by convexity, we also have

|S_{h_{0}}(u,0)|\geq h_{0}^{n}|S_{1}(u,0)|

which gives the desired estimate.
∎

Theorem 4.1

(Homogeneity of blow-ups)
.

Let
((\Omega,g(x)dx),(\Omega^{\prime},g^{\prime}(y)dy),u,v)
be an optimal transport map with
0\in\overline{\Omega}\cap\overline{\Omega^{\prime}}
, and suppose
g(x),g^{\prime}(y)
are positive and Hölder continuous on
\overline{\Omega},\overline{\Omega^{\prime}}
respectively. Then for any sequence of scales
h_{i}\to 0
, there is a subsequence along which a blow-up exists and is of the form
((\mathtt{C},cdx),(\mathtt{C}^{\prime},c^{\prime}dy),u_{\infty},v_{\infty})
for some constants
c,c^{\prime}>0
. Moreover,
(\mathtt{C},\mathtt{C}^{\prime})
are cones about the origin and
(u_{\infty},v_{\infty})
are homogeneous of degree
2
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2
, we know that,

|S_{h}(u,0)|\geq ch^{\frac{n}{2}},\qquad|S_{h}(v,0)|\geq ch^{\frac{n}{2}}.

Since the
(g(x),g^{\prime}(y))
are both doubling measures, Proposition
2.3
also implies

|S_{h}(u,0)||S_{h}(v,0)|\leq Ch^{n},

and hence we have

|S_{h}^{c}(u,0)|\sim|S_{h}^{c}(v,0)|\sim|S_{h}(u,0)|\sim|S_{h}(v,0)|\sim h^{\frac{n}{2}}.

Hence without loss of generality, we can assume the normalizing matrices are given by
A_{h}=h^{-\frac{1}{2}}M_{h}
with
\det M_{h}=1
. It follows that for any
R>1
, the rescaled domains
\Omega_{i}\cap B_{R}(0)=A_{h_{i}}(\Omega)\cap B_{R}(0)
have a uniform lower bound on its volume, and therefore we can extract a subsequence for which the convex domains
(\Omega_{i},\Omega^{\prime}_{i})
converges to convex domains
(\mathtt{C},\mathtt{C}^{\prime})
locally in the Hausdorff sense, and moreover
(\mathtt{C},\mathtt{C}^{\prime})
have non-empty interior.
The rescaled potentials

u_{i}(x):=\frac{u(h_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}M_{h_{i}}^{-1}x)}{h_{i}},\qquad v_{i}(y)=\frac{v(h_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}M_{h_{i}}y)}{h_{i}},

satisfy

(\nabla u_{i})_{\sharp}(g(h_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}M_{h_{i}}^{-1}x)dx)=g^{\prime}(h_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}M_{h_{i}}y)dy.

Moreover by Proposition
2.5
, we have
\|M_{h}\|+\|M_{h}^{-1}\|\leq h^{-\frac{1}{2}+\delta}
for some
\delta>0
, and hence by the continuity of
g(x)
and
g^{\prime}(y)
, we know that

g_{i}(x):=g(h_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}M_{h_{i}}^{-1}x)\to g(0)

and

g^{\prime}_{i}(y):=g^{\prime}(h_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}M_{h_{i}}y)\to g^{\prime}(0)

as
h_{i}\to 0
.
Therefore after passing to a subsequence, we set
0<c:=g(0)
and
0<c^{\prime}:=g^{\prime}(0)
, then it follows by Proposition
2.5
that a subsequence of
(\bar{u}_{i},\bar{v}_{i})
converges in
C^{1,\alpha}_{loc}
to a limiting function
(\bar{u}_{\infty},\bar{v}_{\infty})
, which gives an optimal transport map
((\mathtt{C},cdx),(\mathtt{C}^{\prime},c^{\prime}dy),u_{\infty},v_{\infty})
. Moreover, we have

\lim_{i\to\infty}h_{i}^{-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{D_{h_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}r}(u,0)}g(x)dx=\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{D_{r}(u_{i},0)}g_{i}(x)dx=g(0)\int_{D_{r}(u_{\infty},0)}dx

hence the volume ratio
\phi_{\infty}(r)=r^{-n}|D_{r}(u_{\infty},0)|
of this blow-up is constant and equal to
\lim_{r\to 0}g(0)^{-1}e^{-Ar^{\varepsilon_{0}}}r^{-n}\mu(D_{r}(u,0))>0
. It follows from rigidity case of the monotonicity formula (Theorem
3.1
) that
(\mathtt{C},\mathtt{C}^{\prime})
are cones and
(u_{\infty},v_{\infty})
are homogeneous of degree 2.
∎

Remark 4
.

We note that if
((\mathtt{C},cdx),(\mathtt{C}^{\prime},c^{\prime}dy),u_{\infty},v_{\infty})
is a blow-up of the optimal transport map
((\Omega,g(x)dx),(\Omega^{\prime},g^{\prime}(y)dy),u,v)
at
0\in\partial\Omega
, it is not necessarily the case that the cones
\mathtt{C}
and
\mathtt{C}^{\prime}
are affine equivalent to the tangent cones of
\Omega
and
\Omega^{\prime}
at
0
. This is because the sequence of affine transformations
A_{h}
may degenerate and change the shape of the convex sets in the limit. However, this is true if the sections of
u
are “round” (see Definition
7
).

4.2.
With homogeneous density

In this section, we consider the case where
g(x)
and
g^{\prime}(y)
are homogeneous of degree
l
and
k
respectively.

Theorem 4.2

(Homogeneity of blow-ups)
.

Let
((\Omega,g(x)dx),(\Omega^{\prime},g^{\prime}(y)dy),u,v)
be an optimal transport map with
C^{\alpha}
densities
(g(x),g^{\prime}(y))
which are homogeneous of degree
(l,k)
for
k,l\geq 0
. Suppose that the sections
S_{h}^{c}(u,0)
satisfy

B_{C^{-1}h^{\frac{1}{1+\frac{n+l}{n+k}}}}(0)\subset S_{h}^{c}(u,0)\subset B_{Ch^{\frac{1}{1+\frac{n+l}{n+k}}}}(0).

Then for any sequence of scales
h_{i}\to 0
, there exists a subsequence along which a blow-up exists, and is of the form
((\mathtt{C},g(x)dx),(\mathtt{C}^{\prime},g^{\prime}(y)dy),u_{\infty},v_{\infty})
. Moreover
(\mathtt{C},\mathtt{C}^{\prime})
are the tangent cones of
(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
at
0
, and
(u_{\infty},v_{\infty})
are homogeneous of degree
1+\frac{n+l}{n+k}
and
1+\frac{n+k}{n+l}
respectively.

Proof.

By the assumption on the roundeness of the section we can take
A_{h}=h^{-\frac{1}{1+\frac{n+l}{n+k}}}Id
when normalizing
S_{h}^{c}(u,0)
. The rescaled functions are given by

u_{i}(x)=\frac{u(h_{i}^{\frac{1}{1+\frac{n+l}{n+k}}}x)}{h_{i}},\qquad v_{i}(y)=\frac{v(h_{i}^{\frac{1}{1+\frac{n+k}{n+l}}}y)}{h_{i}},

and it follows that
((h_{i}^{-\frac{1}{1+\frac{n+l}{n+k}}}\Omega,g(x)dx),(h_{i}^{-\frac{1}{1+\frac{n+k}{n+l}}}\Omega^{\prime},g^{\prime}(y)dy),u_{i},v_{i})
are a sequence of optimal transport maps. By our rescaling, we have
(h_{i}^{-\frac{1}{1+\frac{n+l}{n+k}}}\Omega,h_{i}^{-\frac{1}{1+\frac{n+k}{n+l}}}\Omega^{\prime})\to(\mathtt{C},\mathtt{C}^{\prime})
where
(\mathtt{C},\mathtt{C}^{\prime})
is the tangent cone of
(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
at
0
. By Proposition
2.5
, after taking a subsequence,
(\bar{u}_{i},\bar{v}_{i})
will converge to some limiting optimal tranpsort map
(\bar{u}_{\infty},\bar{v}_{\infty})
in
C^{1,\alpha}_{loc}
. Moreover, the equality in the monotonicity formula is achieved for the limit, which implies that
(u_{\infty},v_{\infty})
are homogeneous of degree
1+\frac{n+l}{n+k}
and
1+\frac{n+k}{n+l}
respectively.
∎

Remark 5
.

Notice that the existence of a homogeneous density breaks the affine invariance of the problem, which is why we need the additional assumption on the shape of the sections to extract a homogeneous blow-up.

5.
Global regularity for Hölder continuous densities

5.1.
C^{1,1-\varepsilon}
and
W^{2,p}
regularity

In this section, we prove that an optimal transport map between convex domains equipped with non-degenerate Hölder continuous densities is globally
C^{1,1-\varepsilon}
for any
\varepsilon>0
and
W^{2,p}
for any
p>1
. This result is optimal, as
C^{1,1}
-regularity does not always hold without any additional regularity assumption on the boundary. The case when
n=2
and
g=g^{\prime}=1
was previously proved by Savin-Yu
[
26
]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose
((\Omega,g(x)dx),(\Omega^{\prime},g^{\prime}(y)dy),u,v)
is an optimal transport map with
0\in\overline{\Omega}
, and assume
g(x),g^{\prime}(y)
are positive and Hölder continuous in
\overline{\Omega},\overline{\Omega^{\prime}}
respectively. Then for any
\epsilon>0
, there exist
h_{0}>0
such that for all
0<h<h_{0}
, we have

(1-\epsilon)(\frac{1}{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}S^{c}_{h}(u,0)\subseteq S^{c}_{\frac{h}{2}}(u,0)\subseteq(1+\epsilon)(\frac{1}{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}S^{c}_{h}(u,0).

Proof.

Since the two inclusion follows from exactly the same argument, we will only prove the second inclusion. Suppose it is not true, then there exist sequence
h_{i}\to 0
such that

S^{c}_{\frac{h_{i}}{2}}(u,0)\nsubseteq(1+\epsilon)(\frac{1}{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}S^{c}_{h_{i}}(u,0).

It follows that

A_{h_{i}}(S^{c}_{\frac{h_{i}}{2}}(u,0))\nsubseteq(1+\epsilon)(\frac{1}{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}A_{h_{i}}(S^{c}_{h_{i}}(u,0)).

But by Theorem
4.1
, we know that after taking a subsequence we can extract a homogeneous blow-up limit
((\mathtt{C},cdx),(\mathtt{C}^{\prime},c^{\prime}dy),u_{\infty},v_{\infty})
, hence we have the convergence of centered sections
A_{h_{i}}(S^{c}_{\frac{h_{i}}{2}}(u,0))\to S_{\frac{1}{2}}^{c}(u_{\infty},0)
and
A_{h_{i}}(S^{c}_{h_{i}}(u,0))\to S_{1}^{c}(u_{\infty},0)
, which implies

S_{\frac{1}{2}}^{c}(u_{\infty},0)\nsubseteq(1+\frac{\epsilon}{2})(\frac{1}{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}S_{1}^{c}(u_{\infty},0).

But this is a contradiction because
u_{\infty}
is homogeneous of degree 2 and hence
S_{\frac{1}{2}}^{c}(u_{\infty},0)=(\frac{1}{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}S_{1}^{c}(u_{\infty},0)
.
∎

Corollary 5.1
.

Given any optimal transport map
((\Omega,g(x)dx),(\Omega^{\prime},g^{\prime}(y)dy),u,v)
with
0\in\overline{\Omega}
and
g(x),g^{\prime}(y)
are positive and Hölder continuous in
\overline{\Omega},\overline{\Omega^{\prime}}

\varepsilon>0

depending on

h^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}S_{1}^{c}(u,0)\subset S^{c}_{h}(u,0)\subset h^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}S_{1}^{c}(u,0).

Proof.

This follows by iteratively applying Proposition
5.1
to
u
for
\epsilon

∎

Now we make this bound effective.

((\Omega,g(x)dx),(\Omega^{\prime},g^{\prime}(y)dy),u,v)

such that

M^{-1}h^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}S_{1}^{c}(u,0)\subset S^{c}_{h}(u,0)\subset Mh^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}S_{1}^{c}(u,0)

for all
h\leq 1
.

Proof.

By the uniform density estimate (Lemma
2
), for any sequence
h_{i}\to 0
and a sequence of normalization matrix
A_{h_{i}}=h_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}M_{h_{i}}
with
\det M_{h_{i}}=1
, the rescaled optimal transport maps
((\Omega_{i},g_{i}(x)dx),(\Omega^{\prime}_{i},g^{\prime}_{i}(y)dy),u_{i},v_{i})
with
g_{i}(x)=g(h_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}M_{h_{i}}^{-1}x)
,
g^{\prime}_{i}(y)=g^{\prime}(h_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}M_{h_{i}}y)
satisfy the hypothesis of Proposition
5.2
uniformly for some new constants
\alpha,C,R
. Therefore, we can iterate Proposition
5.2
to get the result.
∎

From this, we immediately obtain the global
C^{1,1-\epsilon}
regularity of optimal transport maps.

Theorem 5.1

(Global
C^{1,1-\epsilon}
regularity)
.

Let
((\Omega,g(x)dx),(\Omega^{\prime},g^{\prime}(y)dy),u,v)
be an optimal transport map where the densities
(g(x),g^{\prime}(y))
satisfy

C^{-1}\leq g(x),g^{\prime}(y)\leq C

and

[g]_{\alpha,\overline{\Omega}}+[g^{\prime}]_{\alpha,\overline{\Omega^{\prime}}}\leq C

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
C>1
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, we have
u\in C^{1,1-\varepsilon}(\overline{\Omega})
and
v\in C^{1,1-\varepsilon}(\overline{\Omega^{\prime}})
and moreover, we have the estimate

\|\nabla u\|_{C^{1-\varepsilon}(\overline{\Omega})}+\|\nabla v\|_{C^{1-\varepsilon}(\overline{\Omega^{\prime}})}\leq M

where
M
depends only on
n,\alpha,C
,
\varepsilon
, and the inner and outer radius of
\Omega
and
\Omega^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Since the inner and outer radius is controlled, let us denote them by
r
and
R
, so we have
\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}\subset B_{R}(0)
and
B_{r}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
. Then from the outer radius bound for
\Omega^{\prime}
, we know that
|\nabla u|\leq R
. Moreover, the inner radius bound implies that for some
h_{0}>R^{2}\geq\text{osc}_{\overline{\Omega}}u
, we have
S_{h_{0}}(u,x)\supset B_{r}(x_{0})
for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
, which implies

|S_{h_{0}}(u,x)|\geq cr^{n}>0.

Therefore by Proposition
5.3
, we have
\|\nabla u\|_{C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C
. The same argument applies to
v
.
∎

The argument of
[
9
, Theorem 1.2]
,
[
26
, Theorem 1.1]
can be applied to also give
W^{2,p}
-estimates.

Theorem 5.2

(Global
W^{2,p}

.

Let
((\Omega,g(x)dx),(\Omega^{\prime},g^{\prime}(y)dy),u,v)

,

and

, and moreover, we have the estimate

where

depends only on
n,\alpha,C
,
p
, and the inner and outer radius of
\Omega
and
\Omega^{\prime}
.

Proof.

This follows from the argument of
[
26
, Theorem 1.1]
using Proposition
5.3
.
∎

5.2.
C^{2,\alpha}
-regularity for domains with
C^{1,\alpha}
-boundary

In this section, we show that if
\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}
have
C^{1,\beta}
-boundary, and
g(x),g^{\prime}(y)
are
C^{\alpha}
, then in fact the solution to optimal transport problem is
C^{2,\min(\alpha,\beta)}
. This is sharp, and improves previous results of Caffarelli
[
5
]
and Chen-Liu-Wang
[
9
]
, which required the domains to have
C^{1,1}
-boundary.

First, we prove a lemma which says that if the boundary is
C^{1,\alpha}
, then any blow-up has to live on a half-space.

Lemma 3
.

Let
((\Omega,g(x)dx),(\Omega^{\prime},g^{\prime}(y)dy),u,v)
be an optimal transport map where the densities
g(x),g^{\prime}(y)
are positive and Hölder continuous in
\overline{\Omega},\overline{\Omega^{\prime}}
respectively.
Suppose
0\in\partial\Omega\cap\partial\Omega^{\prime}
with
u(0)=|\nabla u|(0)=0
,
\Omega\subset\{x_{n}\geq 0\}
and locally near
0
,

\overline{\Omega}\cap B_{1}=\{(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}:x_{n}\geq\phi(x^{\prime})\}\cap B_{1}

where
\phi:B_{1}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}
is convex and for all
x^{\prime}\in B_{1}^{n-1}
, we have

0\leq\phi(x^{\prime})\leq C|x^{\prime}|^{1+\alpha}.

Given
h_{i}\to 0
, let
A_{h_{i}}
be the sequence of normalizing matrices for
S_{h_{i}}^{c}(u,0)
, then for any
R>1
, one has

\lim_{h_{i}\to 0}\frac{|A_{h_{i}}\Omega\cap B_{R}|}{|B_{R}|}\to\frac{1}{2}.

In particular, the domains
A_{h_{i}}\Omega
and
A_{h_{i}}\{x_{n}>0\}
both converge to some half-space
H
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume
A_{h}=h^{-\frac{1}{2}}M_{h}
with
\det M_{h}=1
and from Proposition
5.3
we also have
\|M_{h}\|\ll h^{-\varepsilon}
for any
\varepsilon>0
. Therefore, we have

A_{h}\Omega=M_{h}\{x_{n}\geq h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\phi(h^{\frac{1}{2}}x^{\prime})\}

and we have

|A_{h}\Omega\cap B_{R}|=|\{x_{n}\geq h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\phi(h^{\frac{1}{2}}x^{\prime})\}\cap M_{h}^{-1}B_{R}|\supset|\{x_{n}\geq Ch^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}|x^{\prime}|^{1+\alpha}\}\cap M_{h}^{-1}B_{R}|.

We can bound
|A_{h}\Omega\cap B_{R}|
as follows

(5.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|B_{R}|

\displaystyle\geq|A_{h}\Omega\cap B_{R}|

(5.4)

\displaystyle\geq|\{x_{n}\geq Ch^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}|x^{\prime}|^{1+\alpha}\}\cap M_{h}^{-1}B_{R}|

(5.5)

\displaystyle=|\{x_{n}\geq 0\}\cap M_{h}^{-1}B_{R}|-|\{0\leq x_{n}\leq Ch^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}|x^{\prime}|^{1+\alpha}\}\cap M_{h}^{-1}B_{R}|

(5.6)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}|B_{R}|-|\{0\leq x_{n}\leq Ch^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}|x^{\prime}|^{1+\alpha}\}\cap B_{h^{-\varepsilon}R}|

(5.7)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}|B_{R}|-Ch^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\int_{B_{h^{-\varepsilon}R}^{n-1}}|x^{\prime}|^{1+\alpha}dx^{\prime}

(5.8)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}|B_{R}|-C(n,\alpha,R)h^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-\varepsilon(n+\alpha)}

where from third line to fourth line, we used
\|M_{h}\|\ll h^{-\varepsilon}
. It follows that if
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small, then as
h\to 0
, we have

|A_{h}\Omega\cap B_{R}|\to\frac{1}{2}|B_{R}|.

It’s clear the
A_{h_{i}}\{x_{n}\geq 0\}
must converge to some half-space
H
, and by the volume bound, the
A_{h_{i}}\Omega
is then a subset of
H
, but has the same volume, therefore it must also be all of
H
as well.
∎

Lemma 4

(Obliqueness)
.

Let
((\Omega,g(x)dx),(\Omega^{\prime},g^{\prime}(y)dy),u,v)
be an optimal transport map where the densities
g(x),g^{\prime}(y)
are positive and Hölder continuous in
\overline{\Omega},\overline{\Omega^{\prime}}
respectively. Suppose
u(0)=|\nabla u|(0)=0
, and the domains
\Omega
and
\Omega^{\prime}
are
C^{1,\alpha}
at
0
for some
\alpha>0
. If
l_{\Omega},l_{\Omega^{\prime}}
are the defining functions for the supporting hyperplane of
\Omega
and
\Omega^{\prime}
at
0
, then we have

l_{\Omega}\cdot l_{\Omega^{\prime}}>0.

Proof.

Suppose this is not true, then
l_{\Omega}\cdot l_{\Omega^{\prime}}=0
. Then we consider a blow up of

((\Omega,g(x)dx),(\Omega^{\prime},g^{\prime}(y)dy),u,v)
at
0
along some subsequence
h_{i}\to 0
. From Lemma
3
, we know that

A_{h_{i}}\Omega\to H

h_{i}A_{h_{i}}^{-1}\Omega^{\prime}\to H^{\prime}

where
H,H^{\prime}
are half-spaces defined by defining functions
l_{H}
and
l_{H^{\prime}}
so that
H=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:l_{H}(x)\geq 0\}
and
H^{\prime}=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:l_{H^{\prime}}(y)\geq 0\}
, and

\lim_{h_{i}\to 0}\frac{l_{\Omega}\circ{A_{h_{i}}^{-1}}}{|l_{\Omega}\circ{A_{h_{i}}^{-1}}|}=\frac{l_{H}}{|l_{H}|}

and

\lim_{h_{i}\to 0}\frac{l_{\Omega^{\prime}}\circ A_{h_{i}}}{|l_{\Omega^{\prime}}\circ A_{h_{i}}|}=\frac{l_{H^{\prime}}}{|l_{H^{\prime}}|}.

Moreover, since the blow-up is a homogeneous optimal transport map from
(H,cdx)
to
(H^{\prime},c^{\prime}dy)
, we know that

l_{H}\cdot l_{H^{\prime}}>0.

However, we also have

\frac{l_{H}\cdot l_{H^{\prime}}}{|l_{H}||l_{H^{\prime}}|}=\lim_{h_{i}\to 0}\frac{l_{\Omega}\circ{A_{h_{i}}^{-1}}\cdot l_{\Omega^{\prime}}\circ A_{h_{i}}}{|l_{\Omega}\circ{A_{h_{i}}^{-1}}||l_{\Omega^{\prime}}\circ A_{h_{i}}|}=0

which is a contradiction.
∎

Once the obliqueness is established, the arguments of Chen-Liu-Wang
[
9
, Section 6]
can be applied to give
C^{2,\alpha}
-estimates.

Theorem 5.3
.

Suppose
((\Omega,g(x)dx),(\Omega^{\prime},g^{\prime}(y)dy),u,v)
is an optimal transport map where the densities
(g(x),g^{\prime}(y))
satisfy

C^{-1}\leq g(x),g^{\prime}(y)\leq C

and

[g]_{\alpha,\overline{\Omega}}+[g^{\prime}]_{\alpha,\overline{\Omega^{\prime}}}\leq C

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
C>1
.
Suppose in addition
\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}
have
C^{1,\beta}
-boundary for some
\beta\in(0,1)
. Then
u\in C^{2,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})
and
v\in C^{2,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega^{\prime}})
for
\gamma=\min(\alpha,\beta)
and

\|\nabla u\|_{C^{1,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})}+\|\nabla v\|_{C^{1,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega^{\prime}})}\leq M

for
M
that depends only on
n,\alpha,\beta,C
, and the domains
\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Once we have Lemma
4
, we can follow the proof from
[
9
, Section 6]
with minor modifications. We’ll explain the main modification: since
\partial\Omega,\partial\Omega^{\prime}
are only
C^{1,\beta}
, Lemma 6.2 of
[
9
]
only holds under the additional assumption
\delta<\frac{\beta}{2}
. Looking through the proof of
[
9
, Lemma 6.2]
, we see that if we only have
C^{1,\beta}
-boundary, then we only have
a_{h}\leq h^{\frac{1+\beta}{2}-\varepsilon}
(instead of
a_{h}\leq h^{1-\varepsilon}
) and

0\leq D_{n}u\leq C_{1}h^{\frac{1+\beta}{2}-\varepsilon}.

Therefore, for the lower barrier, we must use

\check{w}:=(1+h^{\delta})^{\frac{1}{n}}w-(1+h^{\delta})^{\frac{1}{n}}h+h+C_{1}(x_{n}-Ch^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon})h^{\frac{1+\beta}{2}-\varepsilon},

and the rest of the proof of
[
9
, Lemma 6.2]
holds assuming
\delta<\frac{\beta}{2}
.
The rest of the arguments follow exactly as in
[
9
, Section 6]
.
∎

6.
Roundness of sections

In this section we are interested in the studying the shapes of sections, which can be regarded as a weak form of
C^{1,1}
regularity. We make the following definition.

Definition 7
.

Let
((\Omega,g(x)dx),(\Omega^{\prime},g^{\prime}(y)dy),u,v)
be an optimal transport map with homogeneous densities
(g(x),g^{\prime}(y))
of degree
(l,k)
. Suppose
0\in\partial\Omega\cap\partial\Omega^{\prime}
and
0=u(0)=|\nabla u|(0)
. We say that the sections of
(u,v)
at
0
are
round
if there is a constant
C>1
and
h_{0}>0
such that for all
0<h<h_{0}
, we have

B_{C^{-1}h^{\frac{1}{1+\frac{n+l}{n+k}}}}(0)\subset S^{c}_{h}(u,0)\subset B_{Ch^{\frac{1}{1+\frac{n+l}{n+k}}}}(0)

and

B_{C^{-1}h^{\frac{1}{1+\frac{n+k}{n+l}}}}(0)\subset S^{c}_{h}(v,0)\subset B_{Ch^{\frac{1}{1+\frac{n+k}{n+l}}}}(0).

If the sections of
u
at
0
are round, and
((\mathtt{C},d\mu),(\mathtt{C}^{\prime},d\nu),u_{\infty},v_{\infty})
is a blow-up of
u
about
0
, then it is clear that
(\mathtt{C},\mathtt{C}^{\prime})
is affine equivalent to the tangent cone of
(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
about
0
. Thus, the roundness of sections is related to the existence of homogeneous optimal transport maps between the tangent cones of
(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
. Using this idea, we first prove a negative result saying that roundness does not hold if homogeneous optimal transport maps between tangent cones do not exist.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
((\Omega,g(x)dx),(\Omega^{\prime},g^{\prime}(y)dy),u,v)
be an optimal tranport map with homogeneous densities
(g(x),g^{\prime}(y))
of degree
(l,k)
, such that
0\in\partial\Omega\cap\partial\Omega^{\prime}
and
u(0)=|\nabla u|(0)=0
. Let
(\mathtt{C},\mathtt{C}^{\prime})
be the tangent cones of
(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
at
0
. If there is no homogenous optimal transport map from
(\mathtt{C},g(x)dx)
to
(\mathtt{C}^{\prime},g^{\prime}(y)dy)
, then the centered sections of
(u,v)
are not round at
0
.

Proof.

Suppose that the centered sections are round, then
A_{h}=h^{-\frac{1}{1+\frac{n+l}{n+k}}}Id
is a family of normalization matrices for
S_{h}^{c}(u,0)
, and by Theorem
4.2
the blow-up at
0
exists and must be homogeneous optimal transport map from
(\mathtt{C},g(x)dx)
to
(\mathtt{C}^{\prime},g^{\prime}(y)dy)
. But this contradicts our hypothesis that no such homogeneous map exist.
∎

A very natural question is then the following.

Question 1
.

Suppose there exists a homogenous optimal transport map between
\mathtt{C}
and
\mathtt{C}^{\prime}
. Are the sections of
u
necessarily round at
0
?

We will address this question in subsequent sections.

6.1.
Strongly oblique case

Let
(\mathtt{C},\mathtt{C}^{\prime})
be the tangent cone of
(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
at
0
respectively, in this section, we prove a theorem that guarantees the roundness of sections under a
strong obliqueness
condition on the geometry of
(\mathtt{C},\mathtt{C}^{\prime})
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
((\Omega,g(x)dx),(\Omega^{\prime},g^{\prime}(y)dy),u,v)
be an optimal tranport map with homogeneous densities
(g(x),g^{\prime}(y))
of degree
(l,k)
, such that
0\in\partial\Omega\cap\partial\Omega^{\prime}
and
u(0)=|\nabla u|(0)=0
. Let
(\mathtt{C},\mathtt{C}^{\prime})
be the tangent cones of
(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
at
0
. If
\overline{\mathtt{C}^{\prime}}\setminus\{0\}\subset int(\mathtt{C}^{\circ})
, then the centered sections of
(u,v)
are round at
0
. In particular, all blow-ups are affine equivalent to a homogenous optimal transport map between
\mathtt{C}
and
\mathtt{C}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

First we show the (non-centered) sections
S_{h}(u,0)
and
S_{h}(v,0)
are round. More precisely, we claim there exist
C>1
depending on the cones
(\mathtt{C},\mathtt{C}^{\prime})
such that for all
h<1
, there exist
r(h),r^{\prime}(h)>0
such that

B_{C^{-1}r(h)}\cap\Omega\subset S_{h}(u,0)\subset B_{Cr(h)}\cap\Omega

and

B_{C^{-1}r^{\prime}(h)}\cap\Omega^{\prime}\subset S_{h}(v,0)\subset B_{Cr^{\prime}(h)}\cap\Omega^{\prime}.

To see this, we first use the geometric condition
\overline{\mathtt{C}^{\prime}}\setminus\{0\}\subset\text{int}(\mathtt{C}^{\circ})
, which implies there exist
\delta>0
such that for all
x\in\Omega
and
y\in\Omega^{\prime}
,

x\cdot y\geq\delta|x||y|.

Now fix
x_{0}
so that
u(x_{0})=h
, then by the convexity of
u
, we have

u(x)\geq u(x_{0})+\nabla u(x_{0})\cdot(x-x_{0})\geq h+\delta|\nabla u(x_{0})||x|-|\nabla u(x_{0})||x_{0}|

which implies that

S_{h}(u,0)\subset\{\delta|\nabla u(x_{0})||x|-|\nabla u(x_{0})||x_{0}|\leq 0\}\cap\Omega=B_{\delta^{-1}|x_{0}|}(0)\cap\Omega.

From this we see that if
x_{0},x_{1}
are two points in
\partial S_{h}(u,0)\cap\Omega
, then
|x_{1}|\leq\delta^{-1}|x_{0}|
, and the claim follows.

Next we show that the centered sections
S_{h}^{c}(u,0)
and
S_{h}^{c}(v,0)
are round as well. We note that
B_{C^{-1}r(h)}\cap\Omega\subset S_{h}(u,0)\subset B_{Cr(h)}\cap\Omega
implies that
|S_{h}(u,0)|\sim r(h)^{n}
and
|S_{h}(v,0)|\sim r^{\prime}(h)^{n}
, and hence for all
h
sufficiently small, Proposition
2.3
gives

r(h)^{n}r^{\prime}(h)^{n}\sim|S_{h}(u,0)||S_{h}(v,0)|\leq|S_{Ch}^{c}(u,0)||\nabla u(S_{Ch}^{c}(u,0))|\sim h^{n}

which implies

r^{\prime}(h)r(h)\lesssim h.

Moreover, we also have

r(h)^{n+l}\lesssim\int_{B_{C^{-1}r(h)}}g(x)dx\leq\int_{S_{h}(u,0)}g(x)dx\leq\int_{B_{Cr(h)}}g(x)dx\lesssim r(h)^{n+l}

and

r^{\prime}(h)^{n+k}\lesssim\int_{B_{C^{-1}r^{\prime}(h)}}g^{\prime}(y)dy\leq\int_{S_{h}(v,0)}g^{\prime}(y)dy\leq\int_{B_{Cr^{\prime}(h)}}g^{\prime}(y)dy\lesssim r^{\prime}(h)^{n+k},

hence by the monotonicity formula (Theorem
3.1
), we have

r(h)^{n+l}\sim\int_{S_{h}(u,0)}g(x)dx\geq\int_{D_{h^{\frac{1}{2}}}(u,0)}g(x)dx\gtrsim h^{\frac{n+l}{1+\frac{n+l}{n+k}}}

and

r^{\prime}(h)^{n+k}\sim\int_{S_{h}(v,0)}g^{\prime}(y)dy\geq\int_{D_{h^{\frac{1}{2}}}(v,0)}g^{\prime}(y)dy\gtrsim h^{\frac{n+k}{1+\frac{n+k}{n+l}}},

which implies

r(h)r^{\prime}(h)\gtrsim h.

Hence we have
r(h)\sim h^{\frac{1}{1+\frac{n+l}{n+k}}}
and
r^{\prime}(h)\sim h^{\frac{1}{1+\frac{n+k}{n+l}}}
. This implies
|S_{h}(u,0)||S_{h}(v,0)|\sim h^{n}
, which implies by Proposition
2.3
that there is a uniform density bound

\frac{|S_{h}^{c}(u,0)\cap\Omega|}{|S_{h}^{c}(u,0)|},\frac{|S_{h}^{c}(v,0)\cap\Omega^{\prime}|}{|S_{h}^{c}(v,0)|}\geq\delta>0.

If we let
l_{1}(h),\ldots,l_{n}(h)
denote the lengths of the principle axis of the John ellipsoid of
S_{h}^{c}(u,0)
, then since
B_{cr(h)}(x^{\prime})\subset S_{C^{-1}h}(u,0)\subset S_{h}^{c}(u,0)
, we have that

l_{i}(h)\gtrsim r(h)\text{ for all }i=1,2,\ldots,n.

Moreover the uniform density bound implies that

\prod_{i=1}^{n}l_{i}(h)\sim|S_{h}^{c}(u,0)|\sim|S_{h}(u,0)|\sim r(h)^{n}.

Altogether, we see that
l_{i}(h)\sim r(h)
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
, which implies that the centered sections of
u
are round. The same argument applied to
v
implies that the centered sections of
v
are round as well.
∎

6.2.
2D domains with Lebesgue measure

In this section, we focus on the case when
n=2
. For simplicity, we also focus on the situation
g=g^{\prime}=1
. In particular, we’ll give a complete answer to Question
1
when
(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
are planar polytopes.

In this case, we have the following observation.

Proposition 6.1
.

The only quadratic solutions to
\det D^{2}u=1
on any cone
\mathtt{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
are given by restrictions of a positive definite quadratic polynomial.

Proof.

Suppose that
u:\mathtt{C}\to\mathbb{R}
is such a solution, then without loss of generality, we can assume that the cone
\mathtt{C}
lies in the upper half space
\{x_{2}\geq 0\}
. If we set
f(t):=\sqrt{u(t,1)}
, then
u(x,y)=y^{2}f(\frac{x}{y})^{2}
, and this means that
f(t)
must satisfy the ODE

f^{\prime\prime}(t)=\frac{c}{f(t)^{3}},

which can be integrated. First multiply both side by
f^{\prime}(t)
, which gives

((f^{\prime}(t))^{2})^{\prime}+c(f(t)^{-2})^{\prime}=0.

Integrating this, we have

f^{\prime}(t)^{2}+c(f(t))^{-2}=a\implies f^{\prime}(t)=\sqrt{a-c(f(t))^{-2}},

for some constant
c
and
a
. Integrating this again gives

f(t)=\sqrt{A(t-t_{0})^{2}+C}

for some constants
A,t_{0},C
, which means

u(x,y)=A(x-t_{0}y)^{2}+Cy^{2},

which is a positive definite quadratic polynomial.
∎

A corollary of this is that we can classify precisely the cones
(\mathtt{C},\mathtt{C}^{\prime})\subset(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{2})
for which a homogenous optimal transport map of the form
((\mathtt{C},dx),(\mathtt{C}^{\prime},dy),u,v)
exist.

Corollary 6.1
.

Let
(\mathtt{C},\mathtt{C}^{\prime})\subset(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x},\mathbb{R}^{2}_{y})
be two convex cones, then a homogenous optimal transport map of the form
((\mathtt{C},dx),(\mathtt{C}^{\prime},dy),u,v)
exists iff we are in one of the following four cases:

(1)

(Half-space case) Both
\mathtt{C}
and
\mathtt{C}^{\prime}
are half-spaces and
l\cdot l^{\prime}>0
, where
l
and
l^{\prime}
are the linear functions defining the half-space
\mathtt{C}
and
\mathtt{C}^{\prime}
respectively. In this case, there is a one-parameter family of optimal maps between
\mathtt{C}
and
\mathtt{C}^{\prime}
.

(2)

(Acute/strongly oblique case) Both
\mathtt{C}
and
\mathtt{C}^{\prime}
are strict cones and
\overline{\mathtt{C}^{\prime}}\setminus\{0\}\subset\text{int}(\mathtt{C}^{\circ})
. In this case, there is a unique homogeneous optimal transport map that maps
\mathtt{C}
to
\mathtt{C}^{\prime}
.

(3)

(Right angle case) Both
\mathtt{C}
and
\mathtt{C}^{\prime}
are strict cones and
\mathtt{C}^{\prime}=\mathtt{C}^{\circ}
, in which case there is a 1-parameter family of homogenous optimal transport maps from
\mathtt{C}
to
\mathtt{C}^{\prime}
.

(4)

(Obtuse case) Both
\mathtt{C}
and
\mathtt{C}^{\prime}
are strict cones and
\overline{\mathtt{C}^{\circ}}\setminus\{0\}\subset\text{int}(\mathtt{C}^{\prime})
. In this case, there is a unique homogeneous optimal transport map that maps
\mathtt{C}
to
\mathtt{C}^{\prime}
.

Proof of Corollary.

Assume such a homogenous optimal transport map
((\mathtt{C},dx),(\mathtt{C}^{\prime},dy),u,v)
exist, then Proposition
6.1
implies
u
and
v
are positive quadratic polynomials which are Legendre dual. Hence there exists an affine transformation
A
with
\det A=1
that puts
u
and
v
into its standard form, which means

A\cdot((\mathtt{C},dx),(\mathtt{C}^{\prime},dy),u,v)=((\tilde{\mathtt{C}},dx),(\tilde{\mathtt{C}^{\prime}},dy),\frac{|x|^{2}}{2},\frac{|y|^{2}}{2}).

In this new form, the optimal transport map is just given by the identity map, hence we have
\tilde{\mathtt{C}}=\tilde{\mathtt{C}^{\prime}}
, and the four cases correspond to different types of cones
\tilde{\mathtt{C}}
. If
\tilde{\mathtt{C}}
is a half-space (which, after a rotation can be assumed to be the upper half-space), that puts us into case 1. In this case, there is a 1-parameter family of optimal transport maps given by

u_{\lambda}(x)=\frac{\lambda x_{1}^{2}}{2}+\frac{x_{2}^{2}}{2\lambda}.

If
\tilde{\mathtt{C}}
is an acute cone (i.e. cone angle
\theta<\frac{\pi}{2}
), then we are in case 2, and in this case the optimal transport map is unique. If
\tilde{\mathtt{C}}
has cone angle
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
, then we are in case 3. In this case after rotation it can be assumed that
\tilde{\mathtt{C}}=\{x_{1}>0,x_{2}>0\}
) and again there is a 1-parameter family of optimal transport maps given by
u_{\lambda}(x)=\frac{\lambda x_{1}^{2}}{2}+\frac{x_{2}^{2}}{2\lambda}
. Finally if
\tilde{\mathtt{C}}
is an obtuse cone, (i.e. cone angle
\theta>\frac{\pi}{2}
) then we are in case 4, and in this case, the homogeneous optimal transport map is unique as well.
∎

The next theorem says that if we are in case 4, the sections are always round.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
((\Omega,dx),(\Omega^{\prime},dy),u,v)
be an optimal transport map between convex domains in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
satisfying
0\in\partial\Omega\cap\partial\Omega^{\prime}
and
0=u(0)=|\nabla u|(0)
. Let
(\mathtt{C},\mathtt{C}^{\prime})
be the tangent cones of
(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
at
0
. If
(\mathtt{C},\mathtt{C}^{\prime})
are both strict cones and
\overline{\mathtt{C}^{\circ}}\setminus\{0\}\subset\text{int}(\mathtt{C}^{\prime})
, then the sections of
(u,v)
are round at
0
.

Proof.

First we normalize
(\mathtt{C},\mathtt{C}^{\prime})
. By applying an affine transformation
A
with
\det A=1
, we can arrange that

\mathtt{C}=\{x\geq 0,y\geq 0\}

and

\mathtt{C}^{\prime}=\{y\geq-ax,x\geq-ay\}

for some
0<a<1
.
Moreover since
(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
is asymptotic to
(\mathtt{C},\mathtt{C}^{\prime})
near zero, that means we can write

\Omega=\{(x,y):y\geq f_{+}(x),x\geq f_{-}(y)\}

and

\Omega^{\prime}=\{(x,y):y\geq-ax+g_{+}(x),x\geq-ay+g_{-}(y)\}

where
f_{\pm},g_{\pm}\geq 0
are convex functions with
\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{f_{\pm}(t)}{t}=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{g_{\pm}(t)}{t}=0
. From here, the basic idea is to show that if the sections are not round, then the direction of the long axis must align with the boundary of the cone
\mathtt{C}
. By symmetry, this must be true for both
u
and
v
, but that is inconsistent with the condition
\overline{\mathtt{C}^{\circ}}\setminus\{0\}\subset\text{int}(\mathtt{C}^{\prime})
.

Let
E_{h}
be the John Ellipsoid of
S_{h}(u,0)
, which by uniform density (Lemma
2
) is comparable to
S^{c}_{h}(u,0)
. Suppose for contradiction that
E_{h}
is highly eccentric for
h\ll 1
. If we let
v_{1}(h),v_{2}(h)
denote unit vectors in the directions of the long and short axis of
E_{h}
respectively, and
l_{1}(h),l_{2}(h)
be the lengths of the long and short axis, then this means we have
v_{1}\perp v_{2}
, and
l_{1}\gg l_{2}
for
h\ll 1
. Now we claim that for any
\theta>0
, there exists
h_{0}>0
such that for all
0<h<h_{0}
, the axis vectors must lie
\theta
close to the two coordinate axis, that is
v_{1}(h),v_{2}(h)\in B_{\theta}(e_{i})\cup B_{\theta}(e_{2})
for
h
sufficiently small. To see this we first note that since
l_{1}(h)\gg l_{2}(h)
for
h
sufficiently small, therefore
\partial S_{h}(u,0)\cap\partial\Omega
is two points, which we call
(x^{\pm},y^{\pm})
satisfying
f_{+}(x_{+})=y_{+}
and
f_{-}(y_{-})=x_{-}
. We denote

T_{h}:=\text{Convex Hull}(\{(0,0),(x_{+},y_{+}),(x_{-},y_{-})\})

and

R_{h}:=\{x\geq 0,y\geq 0,y\geq a^{-1}(x-x_{+}),x\geq a^{-1}(y-y_{-})\},

then we have

T_{h}\subset S_{h}(u,0)\subset R_{h}.

Moreover without loss of generality, we can assume
x_{+}\geq y_{-}
(otherwise we flip the two coordinate axis), then there exist
c>0
, and
x^{\prime}\in T_{h}
such that
B_{c|y_{-}|(x^{\prime})}\subset T_{h}
, and there exist
C>1
such that
R_{h}\subset B_{C|x_{+}|}(0)
.
Without lost of generality, we can assume that
x_{+}\geq y_{-}
(if not, then we can flip the two axis), then for any
C>0
, there is sufficiently small
h
for which
x_{+}\geq Cy_{-}
. If not, then
x_{+}\sim y_{-}
and

B_{c|x_{+}|}(x^{\prime})\subset T_{h}\subset S_{h}(u,0)\subset R_{h}\subset B_{C|x_{+}|}(0),

which would imply that
S_{h}(u,0)
are round. Next we claim for any
\delta>0
, there exist sufficiently small
h
for which
S_{h}(u,0)\subset P_{\delta|x_{+}|}
, where

P_{\delta|x_{+}|}=R_{h}\cap\{y\leq\delta|x_{+}|\}.

If not, then there exist
(x_{0},\delta|x_{+}|)\in S_{h}
with
x_{0}\leq 2|x_{+}|
, which means

\text{ConvexHull}\{(0,0),(x_{+},y_{+}),(x_{0},\delta|x_{+}|)\}\subset S_{h}(u,0),

and if
x_{+}
is sufficiently small, we have
y_{+}<\frac{\delta}{2}x_{+}
, therefore

B_{c\delta|x_{+}|}(x^{\prime})\subset\text{ConvexHull}\{(0,0),(x_{+},y_{+}),(x_{0},\delta|x_{+}|)\}\subset S_{h}(u,0)

which would mean that the sections
S_{h}(u,0)
are round.

Thus for
h
sufficiently small

T_{h}\subset S_{h}(u,0)\subset P_{\delta|x_{+}|}\subset E(C|x_{+}|,\delta|x_{+}|).

If we pick
\delta
sufficiently small, then it follows from this that the long axis of the John ellipsoid of
S_{h}(u,0)
must point
\theta
-close to the
e_{1}
-direction, which means
v_{1}\in B_{\theta}(e_{1})
.

Now we show this is a contradiction. The same argument applied to both
u
and
v
shows that we can find sequence
h_{i}\to 0
for which
S_{h_{i}}(u,0)
and
S_{h_{i}}(v,0)
are becoming more and more eccentric, and the long axis of
S_{h_{i}}(u,0)
point towards on the coordinate axis, while the long axis of
S_{h_{i}}(v,0)
point towards an axis of
\mathtt{C}^{\prime}
, moreover since we know by Lemma
2
that
|S_{h}(u,0)|\sim|S_{h}(v,0)|\sim h
, this implies there exist
x_{h}\in S_{h}(u,0)
pointing in the direction of the long axis (i.e.
\frac{x_{h}}{|x_{h}|}\in B_{\theta}(e_{i})
) with
|x_{h}|\gg h^{\frac{1}{2}}
, and there exist
y_{h}\in S_{h}(v,0)
pointing in the direction of the long axis (i.e.
\frac{y_{h}}{|y_{h}|}\in B_{\theta}((\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+a^{2}}},-\frac{a}{\sqrt{1+a^{2}}}))
) such that
|y_{h}|\gg h^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Since the axis of
\mathtt{C}
and
\mathtt{C}^{\prime}
are not perpendicular, it follows that for
h
sufficiently small

|x_{h}\cdot y_{h}|\geq\delta|x_{h}||y_{h}|\gg h,

but this contradicts Proposition
2.3
.
∎

As a consequence of this, we can give a complete answer to Question
1
for polygonal domains in the plane.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
((\Omega,dx),(\Omega^{\prime},dy),u,v)
be an optimal transport map between two polygonal domains in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Suppose
0\in\partial\Omega\cap\partial\Omega^{\prime}
with
0=u(0)=|\nabla u|(0)
and let
(\mathtt{C},\mathtt{C}^{\prime})
be the tangent cones of
(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
at
0
. Then the sections at
0
are round iff there is a homogeneous optimal transport map between
(\mathtt{C},\mathtt{C}^{\prime})
. (i.e. iff we are in one of the situations of Corollary
6.1
.)

Proof.

If there is no homogeneous optimal transport map between the tangent cones, by Theorem
6.1
, the sections cannot be round at
0
. On the other hand, if there is a homogeneous optimal transport map between the tangent cones
(\mathtt{C},\mathtt{C}^{\prime})
, then we are in one of the four situations of Corollary
6.1
, so we consider the 4 cases seperately. In the first case, we can reflect the optimal transport map across the boundary of the half-space, which turns the origin into an interior point, which means the sections are round. In the second case, Theorem
6.2
tells us that the sections are always round. In the third case, we can reflect the optimal transport map across one of the boundary sides to return to case 1. In the fourth case, Theorem
6.3
tells us that the sections are always round.
∎

Remark 6
.

In the case when the domains
(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
are not polygonal, if we are in case 2 and 4 of Corollary
6.1
, then Theorem
6.2
and Theorem
6.3
still applies. The only cases where roundness is not known is cases 1 and 3. In those cases, any blow-up must live on the tangent cone, but there is a one-parameter family of homogeneous optimal transport maps between the tangent cones, and it is conceivable that there could be a solution that interpolates between different members of this family at different scales.

6.3.
Homogeneous densities

In this section, we focus on the situation when the domain
\Omega\subset\{x_{n}\geq 0\}
is locally a strict cone, and the target is locally equal to the half-space
\{y_{n}\geq 0\}
equipped with a homogenous density
g^{\prime}(y)=y_{n}^{k}
. This situation arises in the study of complete Calabi-Yau metrics of Tian-Yau type in our previous work with S.-T. Yau
[
13
]
, and as a limiting description of intermediate complex structure degeneration of Calabi-Yau manifolds, as shown in the work of Li
[
22
]
. In fact, together with S.-T. Yau, we have constructed homogeneous optimal transport maps between the cones of this type
[
13
, Corollary 1.1]
. In this situation, we show that the roundness of sections always hold.

Theorem 6.5
.

Suppose that near the origin,
\Omega
is equal to a strict cone
\mathtt{C}=\{x_{n}\geq\phi_{\mathtt{C}}(x^{\prime})\}
, so
\Omega\cap B_{r_{0}}=\mathtt{C}\cap B_{r_{0}}
and
\Omega^{\prime}
is locally a halfspace
\Omega^{\prime}\cap B_{r_{0}}=\{y_{n}\geq 0\}\cap B_{r_{0}}
, and moreover assume the density
g(x)
which is homogeneous of degree
l
and
g^{\prime}(y)=y_{n}^{k}
. If in addition
k>l\geq 0
, then the sections of
u
are round. In particular, there exist
C>1
, and
h_{0}>0
such that for all
0<h<h_{0}
, we have

B_{C^{-1}h^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}}\subset S^{c}_{h}(u,0)\subset B_{Ch^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}}

and

B_{C^{-1}h^{\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}}}\subset S_{h}^{c}(v,0)\subset B_{Ch^{\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}}},

where
\alpha=\frac{n+l}{n+k}\in(0,1]
.

Proof.

Let us
E_{h}
be the John ellipsoid of the section
S^{c}_{h}(v)
, then
E_{h}\cap\{y_{n}=0\}
is also an ellipsoid centered at
0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, let
l_{1}(h)\leq\cdots\leq l_{n-1}(h)
be the lengths of the principle axis of this ellipsoid, which after a rotation by
A\in SO(n-1)
, we can assume is in the direction of the coordinate axis. We also denote
d(h):=\sup\{x_{n}:x\in S_{h}^{c}(v,0)\}
to be the height of
E_{h}
, then there exist
C>1
such that

C^{-1}E(l_{1}(h),\ldots,l_{n-1}(h),d(h))\subset S_{h}^{c}(v,0)\subset CE(l_{1}(h),\ldots,l_{n-1}(h),d(h)).

By Proposition
2.3
, we also have

C^{-1}E\left(\frac{h}{l_{1}(h)},\ldots,\frac{h}{l_{n-1}(h)},\frac{h}{d(h)}\right)\subset S_{h}^{c}(u,0)\subset CE\left(\frac{h}{l_{1}(h)},\ldots,\frac{h}{l_{n-1}(h)},\frac{h}{d(h)}\right)

for
C>1
depending additionally on the doubling constant of
g
.

Since
\Omega^{\prime}
is locally a half-space, it follows that the centered sections of
v
has a uniform lower density bound

\frac{|S_{h}^{c}(v,0)\cap\Omega^{\prime}|}{|S_{h}^{c}(v,0)|}\geq c>0

and by Proposition
2.3
, the centered sections of
u
also has a similar density bound

\frac{|S_{h}^{c}(v,0)\cap\Omega^{\prime}|}{|S_{h}^{c}(v,0)|}\geq c>0.

Moreover, since
\mathtt{C}
is a strict cone, we have

\mathtt{C}\subset\mathtt{C}(B_{R}(0)):=\{(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{n}\geq\frac{1}{R}|x^{\prime}|\}

for some
R>1
. This implies the supporting hyperplanes of the sections
S_{h}(v,0)
at points
y\in S_{h}(v,0)\cap\{y_{n}=0\}
have slope at most
R
, which implies

d(h)\leq CRl_{i}(h)\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,n-1.

Moreover, by Proposition
2.3
,

\nu(S_{h}(v,0))\sim\int_{E(l_{1}(h),\cdots,l_{n-1}(h),d(h))\cap\{y_{n}\geq 0\}}y_{n}^{k}dy\sim l_{1}(h)\cdots l_{n-1}(h)d(h)^{k+1}

and

\mu(S_{h}(u,0))\sim\int_{E(\frac{h}{l_{1}(h)},\cdots,\frac{h}{l_{n-1}(h)},\frac{h}{d(h)})\cap\mathtt{C}}g(x)dx\sim\frac{h}{l_{1}(h)}\cdots\frac{h}{l_{n-1}(h)}(\frac{h}{d(h)})^{l+1}.

Therefore, the equation tells us that

l_{1}(h)\cdots l_{n-1}(h)d(h)^{k+1}\sim\frac{h}{l_{1}(h)}\cdots\frac{h}{l_{n-1}(h)}(\frac{h}{d(h)})^{l+1}\implies(l_{1}(h)\cdots l_{n-1}(h))^{2}d(h)^{k+l+2}\sim h^{n+l}.

This implies that

d(h)^{2n+k+l}\lesssim h^{n+l}\implies d(h)\lesssim h^{\frac{n+l}{2n+k+l}}.

But the monotonicity formula tell us that

\mu(S_{h}(v,0))\sim l_{1}(h)\cdots l_{n-1}(h)d(h)^{k+1}\gtrsim h^{\frac{n+l}{1+\frac{n+l}{n+k}}}\implies d(h)^{\frac{k-l}{2}}\gtrsim h^{\frac{(n+l)(k-l)}{2(2n+k+l)}}

since
k\geq l\geq 0
, this implies

d(h)\gtrsim h^{\frac{n+l}{2n+k+l}}.

Therefore we have
d(h)\sim l_{i}(h)\sim h^{\frac{n+l}{2n+k+l}}
, and the proposition is proved.
∎

Corollary 6.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
6.5
, blow-ups exists and are homogeneous optimal transport maps between the corresponding tangent cones.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Theorem
4.2
.
∎

Remark 7
.

A consequence of this is that homogeneous solutions of the optimal transport problem constructed in
[
13
]
, which is expected to serve as a model at infinity for “generalized Tian-Yau metrics”, arises as a blow-up limit for the optimal transport problem describing intermediate complex structure degenerations of Calabi-Yau hypersurfaces
[
22
]
. This provides further evidence that generalized Tian-Yau metrics should exist and should arise as bubbles in the intermediate complex structure degeneration of Calabi-Yau hypersurfaces.

7.
Flat directions and mixed homogeneity

Let
\mathtt{C}=\{x_{n}\geq\phi_{\mathtt{C}}(x^{\prime})\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a strict cone. In this section, we consider optimal transport maps from
(\mathbb{R}^{n-m}\times\mathtt{C},dx)
to
(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},y_{n}^{k}dy)
. This situation arises in our previous work with S.-T. Yau
[
13
]
. We’ll see that in this case, the existence of flat-sides in the domain breaks the homogeneity, and the solutions exhibit mixed homogeneous behaviour.

7.1.
A Pogorelov estimate along flat directions

We first prove Pogorelov-type estimates for solutions in the flat directions. When
\mathtt{C}=\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a one-dimensional cone, a Pogorelov-type estimate was established by Jhaveri-Savin
[
18
, Proposition 4.1]
using a doubling trick. The following estimate generalizes this result to arbitrary cones and general densities.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}_{x}
and
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}_{y}^{n}

(i)

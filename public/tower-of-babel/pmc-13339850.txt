Do intergenerational interactions affect organizational trust, commitment, and satisfaction in the workplace in nursing: perceptions of generation Z towards others
Authors: Shagufta Zada, Muhammad Zada, Kamran Khan, Nicolás Contreras-Barraza, Alejandro Vega-Muñoz
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground/AimsThe role of age diversity has increased attention in organizations with the focus of collaboration between younger and older employees. The present study aims to investigate the perceptions of younger generation towards others with respect to organizational commitment, trust and job satisfaction in the healthcare field, particularly in nursing.MethodsA quantitative survey was conducted to collect data from 696 working young professionals in nursing within the healthcare sector in Pakistan. Structural equation modeling was employed to examine the relationships among the variables using Smart-PLS Software version 4.1.0.9.ResultsThe obtained results show that the perceptions of working professionals from Generation Z with respect to generational stereotyping, positive effect and inclusiveness about other generations are positively correlated with trust and job satisfaction by mediating role of organizational commitment in the nursing sector.ConclusionResearch highlights perception of Generation Z at workplace impacted on trust, satisfaction, and commitment and closely connected to improved quality of work life. The study contributes to the existing literature on nursing management of intergenerational interactions. It advocates for redesigning systems to focus on development and well-being within the nursing sector.Keywords: Intergenerational Workplace, Nursing Management, Organizational Trust, Job Satisfaction, Generation Z

Introduction

The 21th century marked the beginning of research into how various generations interact and influence the workplace, a field that gained momentum as studies highlighted its importance [1]. Subsequent studies, such as work in, further explored the connection between generational relationships and key workplace factors including job satisfaction and organizational performance commitment [2]. Today’s workforce stands out for its unprecedented generational mix, encompassing four distinct groups from Baby Boomers to Gen Z, each contributing unique work habits, values, and life perspectives [3]. These inherent differences have a significant impact on workplace attitudes and ultimately influence job performance [4]. While research shows that a lack of integrational understanding can unfortunately lead to feelings of alienation and negatively impact organizational commitment and job satisfaction, positive social interactions, teamwork, and collaborative efforts among employees of different ages have been demonstrated to improve both job performance and overall work environment satisfaction [5]. Several factors contribute to job satisfaction, including challenging stereotypes, promoting inclusion, and enhancing communication [6]. Specifically, age-related stereotypes significantly impact the relationships between different generations at work and affect the level of dedication employees have to the organization [7]. In nursing settings, ageism can manifest in both obvious and subtle forms [8].

Younger employees are often seen as more technologically skilled and innovative, while older employees might be considered experienced but less open to new methods. Millennials are frequently perceived as energetic and driven [9]. However, when positive perceptions exist across generations and people work together effectively, workplace relationships and trust in the organization can grow stronger [10]. Recent research indicates that having a mix of ages in the workplace is linked to greater organizational trust [11]. Strategies that embrace the diversity of the workforce can lead to positive results, such as the sharing of knowledge between different age groups [12]. Trust in a workplace with multiple generations stems from positive views about other generations, effective communication, and strong teamwork [13]. It also develops from shared values, attitudes, and behaviors that shape how people interact at work [14]. Furthermore, organizational commitment and trust are influenced by ethical conduct, and the formal and informal rules that guide interactions within teams [15].

In today's competitive healthcare environment, especially in nursing settings, a profession marked by diverse teams spanning multiple generations, generational stereotypes, intergenerational effects, and workplace dynamics all play crucial roles in shaping outcomes like organizational commitment and trust [16]. Generational categories, which refer to generalized beliefs or norms about individuals based on their generational cohort, can influence team dynamics, job satisfaction, and the overall sense of trust within healthcare environments [17]. These generational categories can lead to both positive and negative consequences, such as fostering development and collaboration or causing conflict between nurses of different age groups [18].

Significance of research

The twenty-first century comes with transformation, prominent change in the multigenerational workforce, which involves distinct positions of a generation from others. Nurses' perceptions regarding colleagues and workplace setting vary by trust, commitment, and satisfaction. Collaboration in an aging workforce paves the way for enhanced retention, improved satisfaction, and fosters overall trust. Positive intergenerational climate sets the perceptions for Generation Z nurses to interact effectively with others associated with the healthcare system [19]. In a healthcare setting, where genuine connections are vital for patient care, understanding how generational typecasts influence job satisfaction and subsequent organizational outcomes becomes crucial for fostering a harmonious and productive workforce [20]. The research aims to contribute to the nursing sector by offering evidence-based insights that can guide policies and enhance understanding of how younger generations perceive older ones.

Problem statement

Some researchers have addressed ageism in the nursing sector; there is a lack of knowledge about its effects on Generation Z in Pakistan. This study aims to fill that gap by examining the interactions between Generation Z and other generations, particularly as this younger group begins to enter nursing settings. Based on recent researches on intergenerational climate in healthcare sector, a gap remains in understanding how generational differences affect organizational commitment and job satisfaction, particularly in relation to ageism. The perspectives of younger workers on ageism are especially under-researched, despite its importance as a social issue. Managing of aging in healthcare sector critically related to performance and better outcomes. Age related challenges may be influenced the growth opportunities, and outcomes [21]. The study of Puiu et al., [22] explained the understating of Z towards the meaning of old age colleagues in public health institutes. Relationships with seniors shapes the perceptions of generation Z about workplace environment, trust responses to patients as well as implications of organizational policies. Knowledge sharing behaviors at workplace and job satisfaction may be associated with beliefs collaborative generational climate. This study specifically focuses on Generation Z in healthcare system within an Eastern context, with an emphasis on work collaboration and workplace factors shape the experiences of different generational groups.

Literature review

Lack of generational stereotypes, job satisfaction, organizational commitment, and trust of generation Z

A generation is a group of individuals born within a specific time period who share common socio-cultural traits and experiences [23]. Generation Z typically born after 1994, is often referred to as the "digital native" generation [24]. Having grown up in a technologically advanced and digitally connected world, this cohort has been immersed in social networks and digital tools [25]. Known as "Gen Zers" or post-millennials, they are characterized by their self-confidence, ambition, and pragmatic outlook [26]. This generation grew up during the rise of the World Wide Web, making them closely connected to social media, the internet, and advanced technology [27]. As Gen Z joins the workforce, they bring a distinct perspective shaped by their experiences in the medical profession in a hyper-connected digital landscape. While they may lack traditional work experience, their familiarity with social media, mobile technology, and cybersecurity makes them stand out [28]. Gen Z’s pragmatic mindset, creativity, and strong listening skills, coupled with their value for teamwork and ongoing development, significantly shape how they are satisfied and engage with colleagues from different generations at work [29]. This interplay influences key workplace factors, such as job satisfaction, commitment and trust [30, 31]. Gen Z’s collaborative spirit and digital savvy enable them to bring fresh ideas and readily adjust and satisfy to evolving work settings. Recognizing these dynamics is crucial for fostering positive intergenerational relationships and leveraging the unique talents each generation brings. Furthermore, social exchange theory suggests that when nurses see positive, reciprocal relationships between generations, they are more likely to feel satisfied with their jobs, which then boosts organizational commitment and trust [32]. According to social identity theory, generational individuality affects how employees perceive and interact with others, and positive intergenerational relationships enhance job satisfaction, improve a nurse's commitment and trust within the organization [22]. Theories that emphasize job satisfaction also confirm it as a key mediator in transforming generational dynamics into stronger organizational outcomes, such as commitment and trust. Positive inter-generational affect, job satisfaction organizational commitment, trust of generation Z

Research indicates that Generation Z has a distinct profile, characterized by traits such as individualism, conscientiousness, flexibility, motivation, strong values, developed soft skills, loyalty across generations, and persistence in completing tasks in medical profession [21]. Contemporary workplaces often host four or even five generations, each bringing their own work values and methodologies [33]. Notably, Generation Z distinguishes itself by prioritizing work values that include job security, purposeful and motivating work, opportunities for positive social impact, and attractive benefits [34]. For Gen Z, establishing a strong career base is paramount, driving them to pursue skill-building opportunities for rapid professional growth actively [35].This generation highly values flexibility, a positive workplace culture, social justice, and a healthy work-life balance [36]. They flourish in collaborative environments where intergenerational teamwork can foster significant change. Effective teamwork, robust social connections, and a supportive work atmosphere serve as key motivators for Gen ZA [37]. Furthermore, they are recognized for their ethical choices, collaborative spirit, and receptiveness to understanding and addressing issues such as ageism and diversity [38]. External and situational factors, including workplace inclusiveness, positive perceptions of different generations, transactional dynamics, and the quality of social interactions with colleagues, significantly influence their views on work and interactions with other generations [39]. These factors influence their outlook, personality, and adaptability within their organizational attachment job satisfaction and work commitment, which are significantly affected by both internal and external aspects of the work environment [40]. The theory emphasizes how positive, collaborative communication across generations can lead to a more engaged and loyal workforce, driving key organizational outcomes. Workplace generational inclusiveness, job satisfaction organizational commitment, trust of generation Z

Generation Z employees have significant expectations regarding their work and workplace treatment (Nguyen et al., [41]. Research highlighted their strong focus on organizational values and communication quality, key factors in their decision to stay with an employer [42]. Their job satisfaction and organizational commitment are closely linked to positive intergenerational interactions [43]. For Gen Z, trust depends on an organization’s reliability in fulfilling promises, meeting psychological needs, and offering financial rewards and opportunities for growth [44]. Key factors that enhance job satisfaction, help build confidence and commitment among employees include streamlined processes, a collaborative work culture, and environmentally friendly human resource management (HRM) practices [45]. These elements motivate employees, enhance job satisfaction, and encourage them to work toward achieving organizational goals [46]. Generation Z tends to exhibit higher job satisfaction in workplaces that adopt modern technology, utilize computer-assisted task management, and offer flexible environments that foster positive social interactions among coworkers [47]. However, mistrust between generations can give rise to intergenerational biases, misunderstandings, stereotypes, and conflicts [48]. Gen Z employees often work to overcome obstacles such as limited professional development opportunities, cultural differences, and skepticism toward organizational processes [1]. They are usually adaptable, open to geographic mobility, able to handle urgent tasks, and willing to work closely with their teams. On the other hand, a lack of trust within organizations can cause conflicts, complications, and instability among different generational groups [11]. Social exchange theory posits that social behavior arises from an exchange process in which individuals strive to maximize benefits and minimize costs in their relationships [49]. In the nursing context, this theory suggests that when employees perceive they are treated fairly, with respect and inclusiveness, they are more likely to reciprocate by showing higher levels of job satisfaction, trust, and organizational commitment [50]. Exchanging positive actions fosters a mutually beneficial relationship between staff and the organization, where both parties feel appreciated and supported, leading to improved organizational outcomes, including increased productivity, retention, and overall performance [22].

Theoretical underpinning

This study is based on the social exchange theory (SET) [51] and social identity theory (SIT) [52], which offer valuable lenses for investigating how generational dynamics influence organizational outcomes in nursing [53]. Social exchange theory suggests that individuals, including nurses, who witness respectful behavior and admiration across generations, are more likely to experience higher job satisfaction and more substantial commitment to the organization [54]. Furthermore, Social Identity Theory highlights how individuals’ identification with particular generational groups can shape their arrogance and behavior toward colleagues, affecting work relationships [55]. In nursing, where teamwork and comprehensiveness are vital to quality care, endorsing generational inclusiveness and reducing stereotypes can foster a positive work environment, improve job satisfaction, and ultimately strengthen organizational trust and commitment [17]. Social exchange theory suggests that significant intergenerational influence in the workplace is based on mutual respect and fairness, which leads to increased job satisfaction [56]. When nursing staff comment on these productive exchanges, their job satisfaction increases, which in turn boosts both organizational commitment and trust [57]. These theories emphasize the pivotal role of job satisfaction as a mediator in the relationship between generational stereotypes, intergenerational affect, and organizational commitment and trust within nursing environments.

Methodology

Research design

The study adopted quantitative method with cross-sectional research design to explore the intergenerational climate, trust commitment and job satisfaction among nurses.

Sample size and sampling method & data collection procedure

The study targeted working professionals employed in the nursing sector in Rawalpindi and Islamabad, Pakistan. To determine the required sample size, G*Power software was utilized, considering a small expected effect size of 0.10, a statistical power of 0.86, and a significance level of 0.05. Based on these criteria, a sample of at least 696 participants was required. Data collection was carried out using snowball technique, which successfully yielded 696 valid responses providing a solid foundation for analysis. The data for this study were gathered through an online questionnaire created using Google Forms as well as distribution of hard copies of questionnaire by personal visit in selected hospitals. To reach audience, emails were distributed to friends and professionals related to healthcare profession We adopted snowball a non-probability sampling to assess the intergenerational differences in healthcare sector. Selecting a specific public sector hospital by using professional contacts, limited online staff information, we contacted administrative in charge of nursing working for the same hospital for data collection. We extended the scope of survey and were finally able to find more healthcare professionals who were working or had worked for the same type of public hospital in order to complete the survey in Rawalpindi and Islamabad, Pakistan.

Inclusion criteria

The survey was directed at only regular healthcare professionalize. generation Z nurses age between 18 to 25 years working in twins’ cities public hospitals located Rawalpindi and Islamabad Pakistan. Selected participants included juniors and seniors staff nurses both male and female.

Exclusion criteria

We excluded nurses having age more than 25 years and working in private hospitals We also excluded employees who are on duty during weekends, student nurses who practicing during the degree as internees and visiting members of institutions and had privacy concerns.

Instruments/Measures

The study utilized the Workplace Intergenerational Climate Scale (WICS) developed by King and Bryant [58], a 12-item tool designed to assess interactions among different age groups in the workplace. This STUDY includes 3 sub-dimensions such as the lack of generational stereotypes, positive intergenerational affect, generational inclusiveness across generations. Participants rated each item on a five-point Likert scale, ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). Job satisfaction was assessed using a three-item scale. Organizational commitment, defined as the extent to which individuals identify with and are engaged in their organization, was measured using a four-item version of the Affective Organizational Commitment Scale [59]. Finally, organizational trust, representing employees’ expectations of mutual reliability and integrity within the organization, was evaluated using a five-item scale developed by [60].

Results

Statistical model

The current study utilized the Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) technique for comprehensive empirical analysis, using SmartPLS software version 4.1.0.0. PLS-SEM is well-suited for evaluating latent variables within complex path models, providing a strong framework for exploring intricate relationships [61]. This method is particularly beneficial for analyzing time-lagged data and for testing advanced mediation and moderation models. The PLS-SEM procedure involves two main stages: first, assessing the measurement model and underlying theoretical constructs,and second, validating the structural model by analyzing the relationships among constructs and testing hypotheses [62]. The choice of PLS-SEM as the primary analytical tool was driven by its increasing use in work psychology research and its effectiveness in predictive modeling. Additionally, PLS-SEM is ideal for handling small sample sizes and non-normal data distributions, making it highly suitable for this study [63]. The proposed integrated model’s latent variable relationships were assessed using PLS-SEM, ensuring the generation of dependable and valid results.

Discussion, implications, and limitations

Discussion

The study examined the impact of lack of generational stereotypes, positive intergenerational affect and generational inclusiveness on the organizational commitment, trust, and job satisfaction of Generation Z in the nursing sector. The lack of generational stereotyping resulted as positive effect on organizational commitment and trust through job satisfaction (β = 0.556: 0.577). Positive intergenerational affect resulted as positive effect on organizational commitment and trust through job satisfaction (β = 0.187: 0.344). Generational inclusiveness resulted as positive effect on organizational commitment by mediating role of job satisfaction (β = 0.344: 0.636).

Generation Z responses to intergenerational interaction reveal a strong sensitivity toward sustainability and a deep sense of commitment [5]. This generation tends to prioritize organizational trust, commitment, and job satisfaction over financial rewards [43]. Compared to the older cohort, Gen Z places a greater emphasis on accessible information, reflecting a clear preference for transparency in the workplace. Furthermore, opportunities for career growth, including advancement, skill development, and recognition, are significant for both Generation Y and Z. These findings highlight the importance of adopting organizational strategies to meet the evolving expectations of a diverse, multigenerational workforce [57].

In terms of workplace motivation, the study found that Gen Z is primarily driven by opportunities for career progression, fair compensation, and strong HR support. In response, modern organizations are increasingly adapting their approaches to align with the values of younger employees, placing greater emphasis on initiative that promotes both professional fulfillment and personal well-being [67]. However, within the northern branches of the Lefties group, elements such as transparency, participation, and job security had minimal influence on employee motivation. Despite this, employees across all generational groups reported feeling appreciated, understanding the importance of recognizing unique generational motivations to attract and retain talent effectively in a multigenerational environment [13].

Theoretical implications

Based on social exchange theory and social identity theory, this study investigates how generational dynamics impact organizational outcomes, including commitment, trust, and job satisfaction. Social exchange theory suggests that when staff perceive positive behavior, including recognition of their generational identity, they are more likely to be involved in their jobs, which leads to higher organizational commitment. Previous literature supports the idea that generational insights into career growth satisfaction significantly impact organizational commitment, suggesting that positive generational stereotypes can enhance job satisfaction and commitment [5]. Furthermore, it is confirmed that in organizations related to job satisfaction, there are factors that have an impact on generational dynamics [68].Research indicates that organizational trust is linked to job satisfaction and employees' well-being, with work engagement serving as a mediator, aligning with the concept that generational stereotypes can boost trust through enhanced job satisfaction [69].

Moreover, positive intergenerational affect, characterized by mutual respect and shared knowledge between generational groups, plays a vital role in fostering both organizational commitment and trust. These findings are supported by research indicating that a favorable diversity climate enhances job satisfaction and engagement, which is mediated by trust and openness. This suggests that fostering positive intergenerational relationships can lead to improved organizational outcomes [70]. This positive intergenerational influence not only enhances commitment but also fosters trust, as evidenced by the connection between organizational trust and organizational citizenship behavior [71]. Their findings suggest that trust is vital for promoting positive relationships and behaviors within the organization, thereby further supporting the role of intergenerational affect in building trust.

Furthermore, office generational diversity, where organizations value and integrate different generational groups, is shown to have a significant influence on organizational commitment. Findings are supported by research that highlights the key to tailored HR strategies, which recognize generational differences while developing a cohesive organizational culture [72]. Their results underscore the importance of inclusivity in increasing employee satisfaction and commitment. Similarly, a favorable diversity climate fosters organizational trust, as evidenced by those who found that trust and openness within an inclusive workplace lead to better work outcomes [73]. This literature together reinforces the idea that promoting generational inclusiveness is crucial for both trust and organizational commitment, with job satisfaction serving as a vital mediator in these processes.

Organizational implications

Effective management of age diversity offers key organizational benefits by strategically understanding and integrating generational differences into core operations. By effectively positioning organizational personnel to address the diverse needs of various age groups, an organization can foster a more dynamic and competitive environment. This advantage is recognized primarily when it stems from strong teamwork across different generations, particularly involving younger employees. Organizations that value the diverse perspectives and skills of each generation can tap into a broader range of talents, leading to innovative solutions and increased productivity. Therefore, the organization must actively challenge conformist norms and develop strategies that encourage teamwork across age groups, helping to bridge generational gaps and improve overall workforce cohesion.

To deliver effective services and meet organizational goals, organizations must foster an inclusive environment where all staff feel appreciated, regardless of age. This involves creating opportunities for intergenerational teamwork, where staff can exchange information, knowledge, and different perspectives. Collaboration between younger and older generations helps ensure that everyone feels engaged and included in achieving the organization’s objectives. Organizations should foster mentorship, peer knowledge sharing, and open communication to strengthen intergenerational bonds. By prioritizing inclusivity, trades not only improve worker job satisfaction but also develop a more resilient and adaptable workforce better prepared to face the challenges of an evolving marketplace.

Lecturing on age-related tests requires innovative solutions that leverage the strengths of both experienced employees and younger talent. Organizations can implement flexible work arrangements that promote information sharing and mentorship from seasoned staff, while also providing opportunities for younger employees to develop and advance their careers. By supporting these initiatives, organizations can foster a culture of continuous learning and growth in which staff of all ages feel empowered to contribute. Ultimately, this strategy enables organizations to maintain a cohesive, dynamic workplace that drives sustained success and a competitive edge in an increasingly diverse, rapidly changing environment. Positive intergenerational relationships and a comprehensive environment can enhance job satisfaction, which in turn increases organizational commitment and trust [74].

Limitations and future research

The present research is not limitations. Firstly, the population of the study has consisted of a specific organization, namely the younger generation in the nursing sector of a particular region in Punjab Province, Pakistan. Future studies can be conducted with other organizations and regions to assess the generalizability of the findings. Secondly, our study has examined the intergenerational perception with respect to one generation, which can be generalized in the future by the perspective of Generation X and Y in the nursing sector. Future research may consider other generations, i.e., extending the study to Generation Y and its perceptions on aging, considering that the literature review revealed differences in perceptions between younger and older adults [22]. Thirdly, this study investigated the research questions based on a quantitative survey. Future studies can adopt mixed-method studies or a qualitative exploration of intergenerational tension in nursing in order to record the intergenerational climate by considering additional variables. Lastly, the present research uses a cross-sectional design for data collection with an exclusive focus on Gen Z without comparison groups. Future research can adopt a longitudinal design, extending responses across different time intervals for comparisons across generational groups.

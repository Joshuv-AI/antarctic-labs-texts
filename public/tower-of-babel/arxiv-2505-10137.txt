Small deviations for critical Galton-Watson processes with infinite variance
Vladimir Vatutin, Elena Dyakonova, Yakubdjan Khusanbaev

Abstract
We study the asymptotic behavior of small deviation probabilities for the critical Galton-Watson processes with infinite variance of the offspring sizes of particles and apply the obtained result to investigate the structure of a reduced critical Galton-Watson process.



Key Words
Galton-Watson branching process, reduced process, most
recent common ancestor

Introduction

We consider a Galton-Watson branching process
\left\{Z(n),n\geq 0\right\}

starting with a single founding ancestor in generation 0. Let
f(s)=\mathbf{E}s^{\xi}
be the probability generating function for the offspring number
\xi
of one particle. We assume that the reproduction law of particles
satisfies the condition

\mathbf{E}\xi=1,\qquad f(s)=s+\left(1-s\right)^{1+\alpha}L(1-s),\quad 0\leq s\leq 1,

(1.1)

where
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
L(z)
is a slowly varying function as
z\downarrow 0
. Denote
f_{0}(s)=s
and introduce iterations of the function

f(s)
by the equalities

f_{n}(s)=f(f_{n-1}(s)),n=1,2,\ldots.

It is known (see, for instance,
[
27
]
) that, given (
1.1
)

(1-f_{n}(0))^{\alpha}L\left(1-f_{n}(0)\right)\sim\frac{1}{\alpha n}

(1.2)

as
n\rightarrow\infty
and, therefore, (see, for instance,
[
26
, Section
1.5, point
5^{0}
]
or
[
25
]
)

Q(n):=\mathbf{P}\left(Z(n)>0\right)=1-f_{n}(0)\sim\frac{L^{\ast}(n)}{n^{1/\alpha}},\text{ \ \ \ \ }n\rightarrow\infty,

(1.3)

where
L^{\ast}(n)
is a function slowly varying as
x\rightarrow\infty
.
Moreover
[
27
]
, for any
\lambda>0

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-\lambda(1-f_{n}(0))Z(n)}|Z(n)>0\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda x}dM\left(x\right)

(1.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{1}{\left(1+\lambda^{-\alpha}\right)^{1/\alpha}},

where
M(x)
is a proper nondegenerate distribution such that
M(x)>0
for
any
x>0
.

It follows from (
1.4
) that, for any
x>0

\mathbf{P}\left(0<(1-f_{n}(0))Z(n)\leq x\right)\approx M(x)\mathbf{P}\left(Z(n)>0\right)

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Thus, the typical size of the population survived
up to a distant moment
n
is of order
(1-f_{n}(0))^{-1}
.

In this paper we study the asymptotic behavior of small deviation
probabilities for the critical Galton-Watson branching process, i.e., we
investigate the probability

\mathcal{H}(n):=\big\{0<(1-f_{\varphi(n)}(0))Z(n)\leq 1\big\}.

(1.5)

where
\varphi(n)/n\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty,
and apply the
obtained result to the analysis of the structure of a reduced critical
Galton-Watson process under the condition
\mathcal{H}(n).

The study of small deviation probabilities for Galton-Watson processes is
closely related to the problem of describing the asymptotic behavior of the
local probabilities
\mathbf{P}(Z(n)=j)
when
j
is smaller in order than
(1-f_{n}(0))^{-1}
. Probabilities of such a kind for the critical
Galton-Watson processes or continuous time Markov branching processes were
investigated by several authors. Thus, Slack
[
27
, Theorem 2]
has
shown that if condition (
1.1
) is valid, then the limit

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}L\left(1-f_{n}(0)\right)^{1/\alpha}\left(n\alpha\right)^{1+1/\alpha}\mathbf{P}\left(Z(n)=j\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n\alpha}{1-f_{n}(0)}\mathbf{P}\left(Z(n)=j\right)=\mu_{j}\in[0,\infty)

(1.6)

exists for any fixed
j\geq 1
, and

\mu_{j}=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\mu_{l}\mathbf{P}\left(l,j\right),\quad j\geq 1,\quad\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\mu_{l}\mathbf{P}^{l}\left(\xi=0\right)=1,

(1.7)

where
\mathbf{P}\left(l,j\right)=\mathbf{P}\left(Z(n+1)=j|Z(n)=l\right)

are the transition probabilities of the branching process. Moreover,

\sum_{j=1}^{T}\mu_{j}\sim\frac{1}{\alpha\Gamma\left(1+\alpha\right)}\frac{T^{\alpha}}{L\left(1/T\right)}

(1.8)

as
T\rightarrow\infty
, where
\Gamma(x)
is the standard Gamma-function.

Asymptotic behavior of local probabilities for the critical processes with
finite variance of the reproduction laws of particles was analyzed in
[
5
]
and
[
29
]
(for the continuous time Markov branching
processes) and in
[
21
]
–
[
23
]
(for the critical
Galton-Watson branching processes). To the authors knowledge, the best
result in this direction is contained in
[
23
]
and has the following
form: if

f^{\prime}(1)=1,\quad\sigma^{2}=f^{\prime\prime}(1)\in(0,\infty),

(1.9)

the distribution of
\xi
is lattice with span
b
and parameters
j
and
n
tend to infinity in such a way that the ratio
jn^{-1}
remains bounded,
then

\frac{\sigma^{4}n^{2}}{4b}\left(1+\frac{2b}{\sigma^{2}n}\right)^{j+1}\mathbf{P}\left(Z(n)=bj|Z(0)=1\right)\rightarrow 1.

In particular, if
jn^{-1}\rightarrow 0
, then

\mathbf{P}\left(Z(n)=bj|Z(0)=1\right)\sim\frac{4b}{\sigma^{4}n^{2}}\sim\frac{1-f_{n}(0)}{n}\frac{2b}{\sigma^{2}},

where the last equivalence is justifies by the asymptotic relation

1-f_{n}(0)\sim\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}n},\ n\rightarrow\infty,

valid under the condition (
1.9
) (see, for instance,
[
3
]
,
Ch.1, Sec.9). Hence it follows that if a parameter
\varphi(n)
tends to
infinity in such a way that
\varphi(n)/n\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty,
then

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\mathcal{H}(n)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{0<(1-f_{\varphi(n)}(0))bj\leq 1}\mathbf{P}\left(Z(n)=bj\right)

(1.10)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\sum_{0<j\leq\sigma^{2}\varphi(n)/\left(2b\right)}\mathbf{P}\left(Z(n)=bj\right)\sim\frac{1-f_{n}(0)}{n}\varphi(n)\text{.}

Our first aim is to extend (
1.10
) to the case when the variance of

\xi
is infinite.

Theorem 1

Let condition (
1.1
) be valid for
\alpha\in(0,1)
. If
\varphi(n),\ n=1,2,\ldots,
is a deterministic function
such that

\varphi(n)\rightarrow\infty\text{ and }\frac{\varphi(n)}{n}\rightarrow 0

(1.11)

as
n\rightarrow\infty,
then

\mathbf{P}\left(\mathcal{H}(n)\right)\sim\frac{1-f_{n}(0)}{\alpha n}\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(1+\alpha\right)}\varphi(n).

(1.12)

We see that the asymptotic relation (
1.12
) is in full
agreement with (
1.10
). Note, however, that (
1.12
)
cannot be deduced from (
1.6
), since the last is proved for each
fixed
j
and not for
j\rightarrow\infty
.

For some reason to be explained later on we do not consider here the case
\alpha=1
and
\sigma^{2}=\infty
. The reader can find a deteiled
discussion concerning this case at the end of the proof of Theorem
1
(see Remark
6
).

Theorem
1
allows us to study, for each fixed
n
properties
of the so-called reduced Galton-Watson process
\{Z(m,n),0\leq m\leq n\}
,
where
Z(m,n)
is the number of particles in the original process
\{Z(m),m=0,1,\ldots,n\}
in generation
m<n
having a positive number of
descendants in generation
n
with the natural agreement
Z(n,n)=Z(n)
.

Reduced Galton–Watson branching processes were first considered by
Fleischmann and Prehn
[
10
]
, who discussed the subcritical case. The
distribution of the distance
d(n)
to the most recent common ancestor
(MRCA) of all particles existing in that generation for supercritical
Galton-Watson processes and for the critical Galton-Watson processes with
infinite variance of the number of direct descendants of particles have been
investigated by Zubkov
[
28
]
. He showed that if condition (
1.1
) is fulfilled, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{d(n)}{n}\leq y|Z(n)>0\right)=y

for all
y\in[0,1]
. Sagitov
[
24
]
considered the reduced
Galton-Watson branching process
\ \left\{Z(nt,n),0\leq t\leq 1\right\}

given
\{Z(n)>0\}
and proved that if condition (
1.1
) is
fulfilled, then, as
n\rightarrow\infty
the process weakly converges in
Skorokhod topology to the limiting process that starts at moment 0 with the
birth of a single particle. Any particle of the limiting process that
appeared at
t_{0}\in[0,1)
has a random lifetime uniformly
distributed on the interval
[t_{0},1)
and, dying, generates a random
number of descendants in accordance with the generating function

h_{\alpha}(s)=1+\left(1+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\left(s-1\right)+\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(1-s\right)^{1+\alpha}.

Sagitov’s theorem generalizes the functional limit theorem due to
Fleischmann and Sigmund-Schultze
[
11
]
, proved for reduced Galton-Watson
branching processes obeying the condition (
1.9
).

Different questions related to the distribution of the MRCA for
k

particles selected at random among the
Z(n)\geq k
particles existing in
the
n
th generation were analyzed, for instance, in
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
13
]
, and
[
14
]
.

In the present paper we consider essentially different restrictions on the
n
th generation population size.

Theorem 2

Let condition (
1.1
) be valid for
\alpha\in(0,1)
and assumption (
1.11
) hold true for a function
\varphi(n)
.
Then, for any
x\in(0,\infty)
and any
j\geq 1

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(Z(n-x\varphi(n),n)=j|\mathcal{H}(n)\right)=\frac{\alpha\Gamma\left(j+\alpha\right)}{j!}xM^{\ast j}(x^{-1/\alpha}),

(1.13)

where
M^{\ast j}(x)
is the
j
th convolution of
M(x)
with itself.

Theorem
2
is a complement to the main result of
[
20
]
,
which may be reformulated as follows (see Lemma 4 in
[
20
]
): if
conditions (
1.9
) and (
1.11
) are valid and the distribution
of
\xi
has span 1, then, for any
j\geq 1
and
x\in(0,\infty)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(Z(n-x\varphi(n),n)=j\Big|\mathcal{H}(n)\right)=xM^{\ast j}(x^{-1}),

(1.14)

where
M(x)=1-e^{-x},x\geq 0
.

Remark 3

It follows from (
1.4
) that

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda x}dM^{\ast j}\left(x\right)=\left(1-\frac{1}{\left(1+\lambda^{-\alpha}\right)^{1/\alpha}}\right)^{j}\sim\frac{1}{\alpha^{j}}\frac{1}{\lambda^{aj}},\ j=1,2,\ldots,

as
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
. Hence we conclude by a Tauberian theorem
(see, for instance,
[
9
, Ch.XIII, Sec.5, Theorems 2 and 3]
) that

M^{\ast j}\left(x\right)\sim\frac{1}{\alpha^{j}\Gamma\left(1+\alpha j\right)}x^{\alpha j}

as
x\rightarrow 0
. Thus,

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\alpha\frac{\Gamma\left(j+\alpha\right)}{j!}xM^{\ast j}(x^{-1/\alpha})=\frac{\alpha\Gamma\left(j+\alpha\right)}{\alpha^{j}j!\Gamma\left(1+\alpha j\right)}\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}x^{1-j}=\delta_{1j},

(1.15)

where
\delta_{1j}=1
if
j=1
, and
\delta_{1j}=0
if
j\geq 2
.

Let

b(n):=\max\left\{0\leq m<n:Z(m,n)=1\right\}

be the birth moment of the MRCA of all particles existing in the population
at moment
n,
and let
d(n):=n-b(n)
be the distance from the moment
n
of
observation to the birth moment of the MRCA. Since

\mathbf{P}\left(d(n)\leq x\varphi(n)|\mathcal{H}(n)\right)=\mathbf{P}\left(Z(n-x\varphi(n),n)=1|\mathcal{H}(n)\right),

we immediately deduce from Theorem
2
the following statement.

Corollary 4

Under the conditions of Theorem
2
, for any
x\in(0,\infty)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(d(n)\leq x\varphi(n)|\mathcal{H}(n)\right)=\alpha\Gamma\left(1+\alpha\right)xM(x^{-1/\alpha}).

This corollary combined with (
1.15
) shows that, given
\mathcal{H}(n)

the distance to the MRCA of all individuals of the
n
th generation is of
order
\varphi(n)
.

2
Auxiliary results

The proof of Theorem
1
will be divided into several stages.
One of the important tools to be used in the sequel are the so-called Bell
polynomials of the second kind. To give a definition of the polynomials we
denote
\mathbb{N}:\mathbb{=}\left\{1,2,\ldots\right\},
select
nonnegative integers
i_{r}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{0\right\},

r=1,2,\ldots,
and introduce, for each pair
k\leq J
of
positive integers the set

\Pi\left(J,k\right):=\left\{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{J-k+1}=k,\quad i_{1}+2i_{2}+\cdots+(J-k+1)i_{J-k+1}=J\right\}.

The polynomials

B_{J,k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{J-k+1}):=\sum_{\Pi\left(J,k\right)}\frac{J!}{i_{1}!\cdots i_{J-k+1}!}\prod_{r=1}^{J-k+1}\left(\frac{x_{r}}{r!}\right)^{i_{r}}

(2.1)

are called Bell polynomials of the second kind.

For every positive
k
, Bell polynomials of the second kind are coefficients
in the decomposition of the function

\frac{1}{k!}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}x_{j}\frac{s^{j}}{j!}\right)^{k}=\sum_{J=k}^{\infty}B_{J,k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{J-k+1})\frac{s^{J}}{J!}

(2.2)

with respect to
s
. Observe that

B_{J,1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{J})=x_{J},\text{ }B_{J,J}(x_{1})=x_{1}^{J},\text{ }

and, for
1\leq k\leq J

\displaystyle B_{J,k}(1,\ldots,1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{J!}{k!}coef_{s^{J}}\left(e^{s}-1\right)^{k}

(2.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(J,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{r=1}^{k}\left(-1\right)^{k-r}\binom{k}{r}r^{J},

where
coef_{s^{J}}\left(e^{s}-1\right)^{k}
denotes the coefficient at
s^{J}
in the Taylor expansion of the function
\left(e^{s}-1\right)^{k}

in the vicinity of the point
s=0,
and
S(J,k),\ k=1,2,\ldots,J
stand
for the Stirling numbers of the second kind. In particular,
B_{J,J}(1)=1.

We will use Bell polynomials of the second kind to write the Faà di
Bruno formula for the derivatives of the composition of functions
F(\cdot)

and
G(\cdot)
in the following form:

\displaystyle\frac{d^{J}}{dz^{J}}\left[F(G(z))\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(G(z))G^{(J)}(z)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=2}^{J}F^{(k)}(G(z))B_{J,k}\left(G^{\prime}(z),G^{\prime\prime}(z),\ldots,G^{(J-k+1)}(z)\right),

where, as usually, the second summand is assumed to be zero if
J=1
. Hence
it follows that, for any
n,J\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle f_{n+1}^{(J)}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d^{J}}{dz^{J}}\left[f(f_{n}(z))\right]|_{z=0}=f^{\prime}(f_{n}(0))f_{n}^{(J)}(0)

(2.4)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{J}f^{(k)}(f_{n}(0))B_{J,k}\left(f_{n}^{\prime}(0),f_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0),\ldots,f_{n}^{(J-k+1)}(0)\right).

Introduce the notation

J_{0}:=\min\left\{j\geq 1:\mathbf{P}\left(\xi=j\right)>0\right\}.

Clearly,

j!\mathbf{P}\left(Z(n)=j\right)=f_{n}^{(j)}(0)=0

(2.5)

for all
j=1,2,\ldots,J_{0}-1
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. It follows from (
2.4
), (
2.5
) and the definition of Bell polynomials of
the second kind that

\displaystyle J_{0}!\mathbf{P}\left(Z(n+1)=J_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{n+1}^{(J_{0})}(0)=f^{\prime}(f_{n}(0))f_{n}^{(J_{0})}(0)

(2.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cdots=\mathbf{P}\left(\xi=J_{0}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{n}f^{\prime}(f_{i}(0))>0

for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. In fact, we can say more. First, the limit

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\alpha n}{1-f_{n}(0)}\mathbf{P}\left(Z(n)=J_{0}\right)=\mu_{J_{0}}<\infty

(2.7)

exists by (
1.6
). Second, using (
1.7
) and
repeating almost literally the proof of Lemma 13 in
[
23
]
after
formula (2.41) we can conclude that
\mu_{J_{0}}>0
.

To prove Theorem
1
we first analyse the asymptotic behavior
of the ratio

q_{n}(J):=\frac{\mathbf{P}\left(Z(n)=J\right)}{\mathbf{P}\left(Z(n)=J_{0}\right)}=\frac{J_{0}!f_{n}^{(J)}(0)}{J!f_{n}^{(J_{0})}(0)},\ J\in\mathbb{N},

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. It follows from (
2.4
) and (
2.6
) that

q_{n+1}(J)=q_{n}(J)+W_{n}(J),

(2.8)

where

W_{n}(J):=\frac{J_{0}!}{f_{n+1}^{(J_{0})}(0)}\sum_{k=2}^{J}f^{(k)}(f_{n}(0))\frac{B_{J,k}\left(f_{n}^{\prime}(0),f_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0),\ldots,f_{n}^{(J-k+1)}(0)\right)}{J!}\geq 0.

(2.9)

Set for brevity

B_{J,k}:=B_{J,k}\left(1!\mu_{1},2!\mu_{2},\ldots,\left(J-k+1\right)!\mu_{J-k+1}\right)

(2.10)

and denote

\Delta(n):=\frac{\mathbf{P}\left(Z(n)=J_{0}\right)}{\mu_{J_{0}}}=\frac{f_{n}^{(J_{0})}{}(0)}{J_{0}!\mu_{J_{0}}}.

(2.11)

Observe that

\Delta(n)\sim\frac{1-f_{n}(0)}{\alpha n}

(2.12)

as
n\rightarrow\infty
according to (
2.7
).

Lemma 2.1

Let condition (
1.1
) be valid. Then there is
n_{0}

such that

W_{n}(J)\leq\frac{2}{\mu_{J_{0}}}\sum_{k=2}^{J}f^{(k)}(f_{n}(0))\Delta^{k-1}(n)\frac{B_{J,k}}{J!}

(2.13)

for all
J\geq J_{0}
and
n\geq n_{0}
.

Proof
. In view of (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) the
sequence
q_{n}(j),\,n\in\mathbb{N},
is nondecreasing for each fixed
j\in\mathbb{N}
and

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}q_{n}(j)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{J_{0}!f_{n}^{(j)}(0)}{j!f_{n}^{(J_{0})}(0)}=\frac{\mu_{j}}{\mu_{J_{0}}}

according to (
1.6
). Therefore,

f_{n}^{(j)}(0)\leq j!\mu_{j}\frac{f_{n}^{(J_{0})}(0)}{J_{0}!\mu_{J_{0}}}=j!\mu_{j}\Delta(n)

for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\frac{B_{J,k}\left(f_{n}^{\prime}(0),f_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0),\ldots,f_{n}^{(J-k+1)}(0)\right)}{J!}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{B_{J,k}\left(1!\mu_{1}\Delta(n),2!\mu_{2}\Delta(n),\ldots,\left(J-k+1\right)!\mu_{J-k+1}\Delta(n)\right)}{J!}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\Delta^{k}(n)\frac{B_{J,k}}{J!}.

Hence we deduce by (
2.9
) that

W_{n}(J)\leq\frac{1}{\Delta\left(n+1\right)\mu_{J_{0}}}\sum_{k=2}^{J}f^{(k)}(f_{n}(0))\Delta^{k}(n)\frac{B_{J,k}}{J!}.

Since
\Delta\left(n+1\right)\sim\Delta\left(n\right)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
in view of (
1.3
), the statement of
the lemma follows.

The aim of the subsequent arguments is to estimate the difference

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{J=J_{0}}^{T}\frac{\mu_{J}}{\mu_{j_{0}}}-\frac{\mathbf{P}\left(J_{0}\leq Z(n)\leq T\right)}{\mathbf{P}\left(Z(n)=J_{0}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{J=J_{0}}^{T}\left(\frac{\mu_{J}}{\mu_{j_{0}}}-q_{n}(J)\right)=\sum_{J=J_{0}}^{T}\sum_{r=n}^{\infty}\left(q_{r+1}(J)-q_{r}(J)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{J=J_{0}}^{T}\sum_{r=n}^{\infty}W_{r}(J)

from above using Lemma
2.1
. It will be clear later on that to get the
desired estimates we need to evaluate the sum

\sum_{J=k}^{T}\frac{B_{J,k}}{J!}

as
T\geq k\rightarrow\infty
and the derivatives
f^{(k)}(f_{n}(0)),~k\geq 2,
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

The next three sections provide such estimates.

3
Properties of integrals containing slowly varying functions

Recall that a function
l(x)>0,\ x\in(0,\infty),
is called slowly varying
at infinity if, for any
y>0

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{l(xy)}{l(x)}=1.

(3.1)

If
l(x)
is a function slowly varying at infinity, then

1) convergence in (
3.1
) holds true uniformly in
y
from any
interval
[a,b]\subset(0,\infty)
(see
[
26
, Ch. 1, Sec. 1.1, Theorem 1.1]
);

2) for any
\delta>0
there is
x_{0}=x_{0}(\delta)
such that

x^{-\delta}\leq l(x)\leq x^{\delta}

(3.2)

for all
x\geq x_{0}
(see
[
26
, Ch.1, Sec.1.5, point
1^{\circ}
]
);

3) for any
\delta>0
there exist
x_{0}=x_{0}(\delta)
and
y_{0}=y_{0}(\delta)\geq 1
such that, for all
x\geq x_{0}
and
y\geq y_{0}

y^{-\delta}\leq\frac{l(xy)}{l(x)}\leq y^{\delta}

(3.3)

(see, for instance,
[
4
, Theorem 1.1.4 (iii)]
).

Now we prove two lemmas concerning the asymptotic behavior of integrals
containing slowly varying functions.

Lemma 3.1

Let
l(x),\ x\in(0,\infty),
be a function
slowly varying at infinity. Then, for any fixed
\theta>0

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\theta-1}\frac{l(xy)}{l(x)}e^{-y}dy=\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\theta-1}e^{-y}dy=\Gamma(\theta).

Proof
. Using (
3.1
), (
3.3
) and convergence of the
integrals

\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\theta-1\pm\delta}e^{-y}dy

for any
\delta\in(0,\theta),
we get by the dominated convergence
theorem that

\displaystyle\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\theta-1}\frac{l(xy)}{l(x)}e^{-y}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\theta-1}\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{l(xy)}{l(x)}e^{-y}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\theta-1}e^{-y}dy=\Gamma\left(\theta\right).

Lemma
3.1
is proved.

In the next lemma we consider the case
\theta\rightarrow\infty
.

Lemma 3.2

Let
l(x),\ x\in(0,\infty),
be a function slowly
varying at infinity. For any
s_{0}\in(0,1)

\frac{\left(1-s\right)^{\theta}\int_{\theta}^{\infty}y^{\theta-1}l(y)e^{-y\left(1-s\right)}dy}{\Gamma(\theta)l\left(\frac{\theta}{1-s}\right)}\rightarrow 1

as
\theta\rightarrow\infty
uniformly in
s\in[s_{0},1)
.

Proof
. By Stirling’s asymptotic formula

\Gamma\left(\theta\right)\sim\sqrt{2\pi(\theta-1)}\left(\frac{\theta-1}{e}\right)^{\theta-1}\sim\sqrt{2\pi\theta}\theta^{\theta-1}e^{-\theta}

(3.4)

as
\theta\rightarrow\infty
. Using the change of variables
y(1-s)=z\theta
we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\theta\right)}\int_{\theta}^{\infty}y^{\theta-1}l\left(y\right)e^{-y\left(1-s\right)}dy

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\left(\frac{e}{\theta}\right)^{\theta}\frac{\theta}{\sqrt{2\pi\theta}}\int_{\theta}^{\infty}y^{\theta-1}l\left(y\right)e^{-y(1-s)}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\theta e^{\theta}}{\sqrt{2\pi\theta}}\frac{1}{\left(1-s\right)^{\theta}}\int_{1-s}^{\infty}z^{\theta-1}l\left(\frac{\theta}{1-s}z\right)e^{-\theta z}dz

as
\theta\rightarrow\infty
. We fix
s_{0}\in(0,1)
and, for
\varepsilon\in(0,s_{0})
write the decomposition

\int_{1-s}^{\infty}z^{\theta-1}l\left(\frac{\theta}{1-s}z\right)e^{-\theta z}dz=I_{1}(1-s,1-\varepsilon)+I_{2}(1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon)+I_{3}(1+\varepsilon,\infty),

where

I_{k}\left(a,b\right):=\int_{a}^{b}r(z)l\left(\frac{\theta}{1-s}z\right)e^{\theta S(z)}dz,\quad k=1,2,3,

and

r(z)=\frac{1}{z},\ S(z)=\log z-z.

Since

\frac{l(xz)}{l(x)}\rightarrow 1

as
x\rightarrow\infty
uniformly in
z
from any finite interval
[a,b]\subset(0,\infty)
,

I_{2}(1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon)\sim l\left(\frac{\theta}{1-s}\right)\int_{1-\varepsilon}^{1+\varepsilon}r(z)e^{\theta S(z)}dz

as
\theta/\left(1-s\right)\geq\theta/\left(1-s_{0}\right)\rightarrow\infty
. Clearly,

S^{\prime}(z)=\frac{1}{z}-1\text{ and }S^{\prime\prime}(z)=-\frac{1}{z^{2}}<0.

Since
S^{\prime}(1)=0
and
S^{\prime\prime}(z)<0,~z>0,
it follows
that
z=1
is a unique point of maximum of the function
S(z)
on the
interval
\ z\in[1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon]
. Therefore, we may
apply
[
8
, Theorem 1.3, Sec.1, Ch.II]
, according to which

\int_{1-\varepsilon}^{1+\varepsilon}r(z)e^{\theta S(z)}dz\sim e^{\theta S(1)}\sqrt{-\frac{2\pi}{\theta S^{\prime\prime}(1)}}r(1)=e^{-\theta}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\theta}}

as
\theta\rightarrow\infty
. Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{\theta e^{\theta}}{\sqrt{2\pi\theta}}\frac{1}{\left(1-s\right)^{\theta}}I_{2}(1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\theta e^{\theta}}{\sqrt{2\pi\theta}}\frac{1}{\left(1-s\right)^{\theta}}l\left(\frac{\theta}{1-s}\right)e^{-\theta}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\theta}}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left(1-s\right)^{\theta}}l\left(\frac{\theta}{1-s}\right)

as
\theta\rightarrow\infty
uniformly in
s\in[s_{0},1)
.

Further, in view of (
3.3
), for any
\delta\in(0,1)
there is
N=N\left(\delta\right)
such that

I_{1}(1-s,1-\varepsilon)\leq l\left(\frac{\theta}{1-s}\right)\int_{1-s}^{1-\varepsilon}r(z)z^{-\delta}e^{\theta S(z)}dz

and

I_{3}(1+\varepsilon,\infty)\leq l\left(\frac{\theta}{1-s}\right)\int_{1+\varepsilon}^{\infty}r(z)z^{\delta}e^{\theta S(z)}dz

for all
\theta/\left(1-s\right)\geq\theta/\left(1-s_{0}\right)\geq N
.

Clearly,

S(1+\varepsilon)=\log\left(1+\varepsilon\right)-1-\varepsilon,\quad S^{\prime}(1+\varepsilon)=-\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}<0,\text{ }

and
S^{\prime}(z)<0
for all
z\in[1+\varepsilon,\infty)
.
Therefore, we may apply
[
8
, Theorem 1.1. Sec. 1, Ch. II]
, according
to which

\displaystyle\int_{1+\varepsilon}^{\infty}r(z)z^{\delta}e^{\theta S(z)}dz

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle-\frac{\left(1+\varepsilon\right)^{\delta}r\left(1+\varepsilon\right)}{\theta S^{\prime}\left(1+\varepsilon\right)}e^{\theta S\left(1+\varepsilon\right)}

(3.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\theta}}{\theta}\frac{\left(1+\varepsilon\right)^{\delta}}{\varepsilon}e^{\theta\left(\log\left(1+\varepsilon\right)-\varepsilon\right)}.

Since
\log\left(1+\varepsilon\right)-\varepsilon<0
, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\theta e^{\theta}}{\sqrt{2\pi\theta}}\frac{1}{\left(1-s\right)^{\theta}}I_{3}(1+\varepsilon,\infty)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\theta}}\frac{1}{\left(1-s\right)^{\theta}}l\left(\frac{\theta}{1-s}\right)\frac{\left(1+\varepsilon\right)^{\delta}}{\varepsilon}e^{\theta\left(\log\left(1+\varepsilon\right)-\varepsilon\right)}

(3.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle o\left(\frac{1}{\left(1-s\right)^{\theta}}l\left(\frac{\theta}{1-s}\right)\right)

as
\theta\rightarrow\infty
uniformly in
s\in[s_{0},1)
.
Finally, consider the integral

\int_{1-s}^{1-\varepsilon}r(z)z^{-\delta}e^{\theta S(z)}dz.

Using the change of variables
z\rightarrow 1/w
we get

\int_{1-s}^{1-\varepsilon}r(z)z^{-\delta}e^{\theta S(z)}dz=\int_{1/(1-\varepsilon)}^{1/(1-s)}r\left(\frac{1}{w}\right)w^{\delta-2}e^{\theta S(1/w)}dw.

Now

\frac{d}{dw}S\left(\frac{1}{w}\right)=\frac{d}{dw}\left(-\log w-\frac{1}{w}\right)=-\frac{1}{w}+\frac{1}{w^{2}}<0

for
w\geq\left(1-\varepsilon\right)^{-1}
. In particular,

\frac{d}{dw}S\left(\frac{1}{w}\right)\left|{}_{{}_{w=(1-\varepsilon)^{-1}}}\right.=-1+\varepsilon+\left(1-\varepsilon\right)^{2}=-\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)<0.

Thus, we may apply
[
8
, Theorem 1.1. Sec.1, Ch. II ]
once again to
conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{1/(1-\varepsilon)}^{1/(1-s)}r\left(\frac{1}{w}\right)w^{\delta-2}e^{\theta S(1/w)}dw=\int_{1/(1-\varepsilon)}^{1/(1-s)}w^{\delta-1}e^{-\theta(\log w+\frac{1}{w})}dw

\displaystyle\qquad\sim-\left(1-\varepsilon\right)^{\delta-1}\frac{e^{\theta\left(\log\left(1-\varepsilon\right)-1+\varepsilon\right)}}{\theta\frac{d}{dw}S\left(\frac{1}{w}\right)\left|{}_{{}_{w=(1-\varepsilon)^{-1}}}\right.}=\left(1-\varepsilon\right)^{\delta-1}\frac{e^{\theta\left(\log\left(1-\varepsilon\right)-1+\varepsilon\right)}}{\theta\varepsilon\left(1-\varepsilon\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\frac{e^{-\theta}}{\theta}\frac{1}{\left(1-\varepsilon\right)^{2-\delta}}e^{\theta\left(\log\left(1-\varepsilon\right)+\varepsilon\right)}.

Since
\log\left(1-\varepsilon\right)+\varepsilon<0
for
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\theta e^{\theta}}{\sqrt{2\pi\theta}}\frac{1}{\left(1-s\right)^{\theta}}I_{1}(1-s,1-\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{\theta e^{\theta}}{\sqrt{2\pi\theta}}\frac{1}{\left(1-s\right)^{\theta}}l\left(\frac{\theta}{1-s}\right)\frac{e^{-\theta}}{\theta}\frac{1}{\left(1-\varepsilon\right)^{2-\delta}}e^{\theta\left(\log\left(1-\varepsilon\right)+\varepsilon\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\theta}}\frac{1}{\left(1-s\right)^{\theta}}l\left(\frac{\theta}{1-s}\right)\frac{1}{\left(1-\varepsilon\right)^{2-\delta}}e^{\theta\left(\log\left(1-\varepsilon\right)+\varepsilon\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad=o\left(\frac{1}{\left(1-s\right)^{\theta}}l\left(\frac{\theta}{1-s}\right)\right)

(3.8)

as
\theta\rightarrow\infty
uniformly in
s\in[s_{0},1)
.

Combining (
3.5
)–(
3.8
) proves the lemma.

4
Bell polynomial of the second kind

In this section we evaluate the sum

\sum_{J=k}^{T}\frac{B_{J,k}}{J!}

as
T\geq k\geq 2
. To avoid cumbersome expressions we denote below by
C,C_{1},C_{2},...,
positive constants which are not necessary the same in
different formulas.

Denote

H(s):=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mu_{j}s^{j}.

If condition (
1.1
) is valid, then (see
[
27
, Theorem 2]
)

H(s)=\frac{1}{\alpha\left(1-s\right)^{\alpha}L_{2}\left(1-s\right)},~0\leq s<1,

where
L_{2}\left(z\right)\sim L\left(z\right)
as
z\downarrow 0
.
Using (
2.2
) and (
2.10
) we get

\sum_{J=k}^{\infty}\frac{B_{J,k}}{J!}s^{J}=\frac{1}{k!}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mu_{j}s^{j}\right)^{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\frac{1}{\alpha^{k}\left(1-s\right)^{\alpha k}L_{2}^{k}\left(1-s\right)}.

Hence it follows by a Tauberian theorem (see
[
9
, Theorem 5, Sec. 5, Ch.
XIII]
) that, for
each fixed

k

\sum_{J=k}^{T}\frac{B_{J,k}}{J!}\sim\frac{1}{k!\Gamma\left(\alpha k+1\right)}\left(\frac{T^{\alpha}}{\alpha L_{2}\left(1/T\right)}\right)^{k}

(4.1)

as
T\rightarrow\infty
. This is not enough for us. To prove Theorem
1
we need an estimate for the case
T\geq k\rightarrow\infty
.

To deduce the desired asymptotics in the case we interested in we introduce
the functions

\rho(x)=\left(\log\frac{1}{x}\right)^{\alpha k-1},\quad 0<x<1,

and

g(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}x^{-1}&\text{if}&e^{-1}\leq x\leq 1,\\
&&\\
0&\text{if}&0\leq x<e^{-1}.\end{array}\right.

Note that

\int_{0}^{1}\rho(x)dx=\int_{0}^{1}\left(\log\frac{1}{x}\right)^{\alpha k-1}dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\alpha k-1}e^{-y}dy=\Gamma\left(\alpha k\right),

(4.2)

and
g(e^{-t})=e^{t}
if
0\leq t\leq 1
and
g(e^{-t})=0
if
t>1
.

The proof of the next lemma is based on the arguments used by Karamata (see

[
12
]
, Ch. VII, Section 7.11, Theorems 108–110) and requires some
additional delicate estimates.

Lemma 4.1

Let condition (
1.1
) be valid. Then there exists
C>0
such that, for any
\varepsilon>0

\sum_{J=k}^{T}\frac{B_{J,k}}{J!}\leq\frac{1}{k!}\frac{Ce^{\varepsilon k}}{\Gamma\left(\alpha k+1\right)}\left(\frac{T^{\alpha}}{\alpha L_{2}\left(1/T\right)}\right)^{k}

for all
T\geq k\geq 2
.

Proof
. In view of (
4.1
) the statement of the lemma
is valid for all
k\leq K_{0}
and all
T\geq k
. Thus, we may assume that
k
is sufficiently large.

Introduce a non-decreasing function
\mathcal{B}_{k}(t),\ 0\leq t<\infty,
by the relations

\mathcal{B}_{k}(t):=0\text{ if }0\leq t<k,\text{ and }\mathcal{B}_{k}(t):=\sum_{J=k}^{\left[t\right]}\frac{B_{J,k}}{J!},\text{ if}\ k\leq t<\infty.

By the definition

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}d\mathcal{B}_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{J=k}^{\infty}\frac{B_{J,k}}{J!}e^{-\lambda J}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}H^{k}(e^{-\lambda})=\frac{1}{k!}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha\lambda^{\alpha}L_{3}\left(\lambda\right)}\right)^{k},\quad\lambda>0,

where

L_{3}\left(\lambda\right)=\left(\frac{1-e^{-\lambda}}{\lambda}\right)^{\alpha}L_{2}\left(1-e^{-\lambda}\right)

is a function slowly varying as
\lambda\downarrow 0
. Clearly, for each
fixed
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0}\left(m,\varepsilon\right)>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t-\lambda mt}d\mathcal{B}_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha\left(\lambda(m+1)\right)^{\alpha}L_{3}\left(\lambda\left(m+1\right)\right)}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\frac{1}{\alpha^{k}(m+1)^{\alpha k}\lambda^{\alpha k}}\left(\frac{1+\varepsilon}{L_{3}\left(\lambda\right)}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\varepsilon k}}{k!}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha\lambda^{\alpha}L_{3}\left(\lambda\right)}\right)^{k}\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\alpha k\right)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{\alpha k-1}e^{-mt}dt

for all
0<\lambda\leq\lambda_{0}
. Hence it follows that, for any
polynomial
R\left(x\right),~x\in(-\infty,+\infty),
and any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0}\left(R,\varepsilon\right)>0
such that

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}R\left(e^{-\lambda t}\right)d\mathcal{B}_{k}(t)\leq\frac{e^{\varepsilon k}}{k!}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha\lambda^{\alpha}L_{3}\left(\lambda\right)}\right)^{k}\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\alpha k\right)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{\alpha k-1}R\left(e^{-t}\right)dt

for all
0<\lambda\leq\lambda_{0}
.

Assume that
k
is such that

\Gamma^{-3}\left(\alpha k\right)<e^{-1}\leq e^{-1}+2\Gamma^{-3}\left(\alpha k\right)<1.

We introduce two continuous functions on the interval
[0,1]
by the
relations

h_{k}^{+}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}x^{-1}&\text{if}&e^{-1}\leq x\leq 1,\\
e\Gamma^{3}\left(\alpha k\right)x+e-\Gamma^{3}\left(\alpha k\right)&\text{if}&e^{-1}-\Gamma^{-3}\left(\alpha k\right)\leq x<e^{-1},\\
0&\text{if}&0\leq x<e^{-1}-\Gamma^{-3}\left(\alpha k\right),\end{array}\right.

and

h_{k}^{-}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}x^{-1}&\text{if}&e^{-1}+2k\Gamma^{-3}\left(\alpha k\right)\leq x\leq 1,\\
\frac{\Gamma^{3}\left(\alpha k\right)\left(x-e^{-1}-\Gamma^{3}\left(\alpha k\right)\right)}{e^{-1}+2\Gamma^{-3}\left(\alpha k\right)}&\text{if}&e^{-1}+\Gamma^{-3}\left(\alpha k\right)\leq x<e^{-1}+2\Gamma^{-3}\left(\alpha k\right),\\
0&\text{if}&0\leq x<e^{-1}+\Gamma^{-3}\left(\alpha k\right).\end{array}\right.

Clearly,

h_{k}^{-}(x)\leq g(x)\leq h_{k}^{+}(x),~0\leq x\leq 1.

It is not difficult to check that there is a constant
C_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left(h_{k}^{+}(x)-g(x)\right)\left(\log\frac{1}{x}\right)^{\alpha k-1}dx=\int_{e^{-1}-\Gamma^{-3}\left(\alpha k\right)}^{e^{-1}}h_{k}^{+}(x)\left(\log\frac{1}{x}\right)^{\alpha k-1}dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\left(\log\frac{1}{e^{-1}-\Gamma^{-3}\left(\alpha k\right)}\right)^{\alpha k-1}\int_{e^{-1}-\Gamma^{-3}\left(\alpha k\right)}^{e^{-1}}h_{k}^{+}(x)dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{2}{\Gamma^{3}\left(\alpha k\right)}\left(\log\frac{e}{1-e\Gamma^{-3}\left(\alpha k\right)}\right)^{\alpha k-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad=\frac{2}{\Gamma^{3}\left(\alpha k\right)}\left(1-\log\left(1-e\Gamma^{-3}\left(\alpha k\right)\right)\right)^{\alpha k-1}\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\Gamma^{3}\left(\alpha k\right)}

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left(g(x)-h_{k}^{-}(x)\right)\left(\log\frac{1}{x}\right)^{k\alpha-1}dx=\int_{e^{-1}}^{e^{-1}+2\Gamma^{-3}\left(\alpha k\right)}x^{-1}\left(\log\frac{1}{x}\right)^{k\alpha-1}dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{2e}{\Gamma^{3}\left(\alpha k\right)}.\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad

Since
h_{k}^{+}(x)
is continuous, it follows from Weierstrass’s theorem on
approximation of continuous functions by polynomials that there exists a
polynomial
R_{k}^{+}(x)
such that

\sup_{x\in(0,1)}\left|R_{k}^{+}(x)-h_{k}^{+}(x)\right|\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\Gamma^{3}\left(\alpha k\right)}.

Taking

P_{k}^{+}(x)=R_{k}^{+}(x)+\frac{C_{1}}{\Gamma^{3}\left(\alpha k\right)}>g(x)

we get by (
4.2
)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left(P_{k}^{+}(x)-g(x)\right)\rho(x)dx\leq\int_{0}^{1}\left(P_{k}^{+}(x)-R_{k}^{+}(x)\right)\rho(x)dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{0}^{1}\left|h_{k}^{+}(x)-R_{k}^{+}(x)\right|\rho(x)dx+\int_{0}^{1}\left|h_{k}^{+}(x)-g(x)\right|\rho(x)dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{3C_{1}}{\Gamma^{2}\left(\alpha k\right)}.

Similarly, there exists a polynomial
R_{k}^{-}(x)
such that

\sup_{x\in(0,1)}\left|R_{k}^{-}(x)-h_{k}^{-}(x)\right|\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\Gamma^{3}\left(\alpha k\right)}.

Set

P_{k}^{-}(x)=R_{k}^{-}(x)-\frac{C_{1}}{\Gamma^{3}\left(\alpha k\right)}<g(x).

Clearly,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left(g(x)-P_{k}^{-}(x)\right)\rho(x)dx\leq\int_{0}^{1}\left(R_{k}^{-}(x)-P_{k}^{-}(x)\right)\rho(x)dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{0}^{1}\left|h_{k}^{-}(x)-R_{k}^{-}(x)\right|\rho(x)dx+\int_{0}^{1}\left|h_{k}^{-}(x)-g(x)\right|\rho(x)dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{3C_{1}}{\Gamma^{2}\left(\alpha k\right)}.

Now

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}g\left(e^{-\lambda t}\right)d\mathcal{B}_{k}(t)\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}P_{k}^{+}\left(e^{-\lambda t}\right)d\mathcal{B}_{k}(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\varepsilon k}}{k!}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha\lambda^{\alpha}L_{3}\left(\lambda\right)}\right)^{k}\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\alpha k\right)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{\alpha k-1}P_{k}^{+}\left(e^{-t}\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\varepsilon k}}{k!}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha\lambda^{\alpha}L_{3}\left(\lambda\right)}\right)^{k}\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\alpha k\right)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{\alpha k-1}\left(P_{k}^{-}\left(e^{-t}\right)+\frac{6C_{1}}{\Gamma^{2}\left(\alpha k\right)}\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\varepsilon k}}{k!}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha\lambda^{\alpha}L_{3}\left(\lambda\right)}\right)^{k}\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\alpha k\right)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{\alpha k-1}P_{k}^{-}\left(e^{-t}\right)dt+\frac{6C_{1}}{\Gamma^{2}\left(\alpha k\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\varepsilon k}}{k!}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha\lambda^{\alpha}L_{3}\left(\lambda\right)}\right)^{k}\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\alpha k\right)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{\alpha k-1}g(e^{-t})dt+\frac{6C_{1}}{\Gamma^{2}\left(\alpha k\right)}\right).

Observing that

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}g\left(e^{-\lambda t}\right)d\mathcal{B}_{k}(t)=\int_{0}^{1/\lambda}d\mathcal{B}_{k}(t)=\mathcal{B}_{k}(1/\lambda)

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\alpha k\right)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{\alpha k-1}g\left(e^{-t}\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\alpha k\right)}\int_{0}^{1}t^{\alpha k-1}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha k\Gamma\left(\alpha k\right)}=\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\alpha k+1\right)},

we conclude that

\mathcal{B}_{k}(1/\lambda)\leq\frac{e^{\varepsilon k}}{k!}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha\lambda^{\alpha}L_{3}\left(\lambda\right)}\right)^{k}\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\alpha k+1\right)}+\frac{6C_{1}}{\Gamma^{2}\left(\alpha k\right)}\right).

Since
\Gamma\left(\alpha k+1\right)=o\left(\Gamma^{2}\left(\alpha k\right)\right)
as
k\rightarrow\infty,
there is a constant
C>1
such
that

\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\alpha k+1\right)}+\frac{6C_{1}}{\Gamma^{2}\left(\alpha k\right)}\leq\frac{C}{\Gamma\left(\alpha k+1\right)}

Using this estimate and setting
T=1/\lambda
we conclude that

\sum_{J=k}^{T}\frac{B_{J,k}}{J!}\leq\frac{C}{k!}\frac{e^{\varepsilon k}}{\Gamma\left(\alpha k+1\right)}\left(\frac{T^{\alpha}}{\alpha L_{3}\left(1/T\right)}\right)^{k}.

This proves Lemma
4.1
, since
L_{3}\left(1/T\right)\sim L_{2}(1/T)
as
T\rightarrow\infty
.

5
Asymptotics of derivatives

In this section we evaluate the derivatives

f^{(k)}(s),0\leq s\leq 1,\ k\geq 2,

from above.

It is not difficult to check that if condition (
1.1
) is valid
for
\alpha\in(0,1)
, then

p_{j}:=\mathbf{P}\left(\xi\geq j\right)=\frac{L_{1}(1/j)}{j^{1+\alpha}},\quad j\geq 1,

(5.1)

where
L_{1}(z)
is a function slowly varying at zero and

L_{1}(z)\sim\frac{\alpha}{\Gamma\left(1-\alpha\right)}L(z)\text{ as }z\downarrow 0.

(5.2)

Now we formulate the main result of this section.

Lemma 5.1

Assume that condition (
1.1
) is valid and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then, for any
k\geq 2
there is
\delta>0
such that

f^{(k)}(s)\leq k!p_{k}+2s^{-k}\frac{k\Gamma(k-1-\alpha)}{\left(1-s\right)^{k-1-\alpha}}L_{1}\left(\frac{1-s}{k}\right)

(5.3)

if
k\left(1-s\right)\leq\delta
.

Proof of Lemma
5.1
. Introduce the notation
x^{[k]}=x(x-1)\cdot\cdot\cdot(x-k+1),~x\geq 0,
for falling factorials.
For
k\geq 2
we have

\displaystyle f^{(k)}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}j^{\left[k\right]}\mathbf{P}\left(\xi=j\right)s^{j-k}=\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}j^{\left[k\right]}\left(p_{j}-p_{j+1}\right)s^{j-k}

(5.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}j^{\left[k\right]}p_{j}s^{j-k}-\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\left(j-1\right)^{[k]}p_{j}s^{j-k-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k!p_{k}+\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\left(j^{\left[k\right]}-\left(j-1\right)^{[k]}\right)p_{j}s^{j-k-1}

\displaystyle-\left(1-s\right)\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}j^{\left[k\right]}p_{j}s^{j-k-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle k!p_{k}+R(k,s),

where

R(k,s):=k\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}j^{[k-1]}p_{j+1}s^{j-k}.

We evaluate
R(k,s)
from above.

Since
L_{1}(z)
is a function slowly varying at zero, it follows that, for
any
\varepsilon_{1}>0
there exists
x_{0}=x_{0}(\varepsilon_{1})>0
such
that

\displaystyle\frac{d^{J}}{d^{J}\lambda}\left[f_{m}(e^{\lambda\log f_{x\varphi(n)}(0)})\right]=\sum_{k=1}^{J}f_{m}^{(k)}(f_{x\varphi(n)}^{\lambda}(0))

\displaystyle\qquad=\left(\log f_{x\varphi(n)}(0)\right)^{J}\sum_{k=1}^{J}f_{m}^{(k)}(f_{x\varphi(n)}^{\lambda}(0))\left(f_{x\varphi(n)}^{\lambda}(0)\right)^{k}B_{J,k}\left(1,1,\ldots,1\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\left(\log f_{x\varphi(n)}(0)\right)^{J}\sum_{k=1}^{J}f_{m}^{(k)}(f_{x\varphi(n)}^{\lambda}(0))\left(f_{x\varphi(n)}^{\lambda}(0)\right)^{k}S(J,k).

It follows from this representation and (
7.2
) that

\frac{1}{x\varphi(n)\Delta\left(n\right)}\frac{d}{d\lambda}\left[f_{m}(e^{\lambda\log f_{x\varphi(n)}(0)})\right]\left|{}_{\lambda=1}\right.\sim\frac{\log f_{x\varphi(n)}(0)}{x\varphi(n)\Delta\left(n\right)}f_{m}^{\prime}(f_{x\varphi(n)}(0))\sim\left(-1\right)\alpha

as
n\rightarrow\infty
, giving

f_{m}^{\prime}(f_{x\varphi(n)}(0))\sim\left(-1\right)\alpha\frac{x\varphi(n)\Delta\left(n\right)}{\log f_{x\varphi(n)}(0)}.

Now we prove by induction that, for any
k\geq 1

\frac{\left(\log f_{x\varphi(n)}(0)\right)^{k}}{x\varphi(n)\Delta\left(n\right)}f_{m}^{(k)}\left(f_{x\varphi(n)}(0)\right)\sim\left(-1\right)^{k}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+k)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. This is true for
k=1
and if this is true for
all
k<J
then, in view of (
7.2
) and convergence of
\log f_{x\varphi(n)}(0)
to 0 as
n\rightarrow\infty,
we conclude by
induction hypothesis that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x\varphi(n)\Delta\left(n\right)}\frac{d^{J}}{d^{J}\lambda}\left[f_{m}(e^{\lambda\log f_{x\varphi(n)}(0)})\right]\left|{}_{\lambda=1}\right.=\frac{\left(\log f_{x\varphi(n)}(0)\right)^{J}f_{m}^{(J)}(f_{x\varphi(n)}(0))}{x\varphi(n)\Delta\left(n\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\quad+\sum_{k=1}^{J-1}\frac{\left(\log f_{x\varphi(n)}(0)\right)^{J}f_{m}^{(k)}(f_{x\varphi(n)}(0))}{x\varphi(n)\Delta\left(n\right)}S(J,k)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad=\frac{\left(\log f_{x\varphi(n)}(0)\right)^{J}f_{m}^{(J)}(f_{x\varphi(n)}(0))}{x\varphi(n)\Delta\left(n\right)}+O\left(\log f_{x\varphi(n)}(0)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\sim\left(-1\right)^{J}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+J)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Lemma
7.1
is proved.

Proof of Theorem
2
. For any
j\geq 1
and
m=n-x\varphi(n)
we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(Z(m,n\right)=j)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=j}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(Z(m)=k;Z(m,n\right)=j)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=j}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(Z(m)=k\right)\binom{k}{j}f_{n-m}^{k-j}(0)\left(1-f_{n-m}(0)\right)^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(1-f_{n-m}(0)\right)^{j}}{j!}f_{m}^{(j)}(f_{n-m}(0)).

This representation and Lemma
7.1
show that, as
n\rightarrow\infty

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(Z(n-x\varphi(n),n\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle j)=\frac{\left(1-f_{x\varphi(n)}(0)\right)^{j}}{j!}f_{n-x\varphi(n)}^{(j)}\left(f_{x\varphi(n)}(0)\right)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\left(-1\right)^{j}\frac{\left(1-f_{x\varphi(n)}(0)\right)^{j}}{j!}\frac{x\varphi(n)\Delta\left(n\right)}{\left(\log f_{x\varphi(n)}(0)\right)^{j}}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+j)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+j)}{\Gamma(\alpha)j!}x\varphi(n)\Delta\left(n\right).

Denote
Z_{1}^{\ast}(l),\ldots,Z_{j}^{\ast}(l)
i.i.d. random variables
distributed as
\left\{Z(l)|Z(l)>0\right\},
and let
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{j}
be i.i.d. random variables having distribution
M(x)
specified
by (
1.4
). Using (
1.5
), (
1.4
) and (
1.3
) it is not difficult to check that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\mathcal{H}(n)|Z(m,n)=j\right)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{r=1}^{j}Z_{r}^{\ast}(x\varphi(n))\leq\frac{1}{1-f_{\varphi(n)}(0)}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{r=1}^{j}\left(1-f_{x\varphi(n)}(0)\right)Z_{r}^{\ast}(x\varphi(n))\leq\frac{1-f_{x\varphi(n)}(0)}{1-f_{\varphi(n)}(0)}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad=\mathbf{P}\left(\eta_{1}+\cdots+\eta_{j}\leq x^{-1/\alpha}\right)=M^{\ast j}(x^{-1/\alpha}).

Combining this result with Lemma
7.1
and Theorem
1

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(Z(m,n)=j|\mathcal{H}(n)\right)=\frac{\mathbf{P}\left(Z(m,n\right)=j)\mathbf{P}\left(\mathcal{H}(n)|Z(m,n)=j\right)}{\mathbf{P}\left(\mathcal{H}(n)\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\sim\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+j)}{\Gamma(\alpha)j!}x\varphi(n)\Delta\left(n\right)\frac{\Gamma\left(1+\alpha\right)}{\Delta\left(n\right)\varphi(n)}M^{\ast j}(x^{-1/\alpha})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\qquad=\frac{\alpha\Gamma(\alpha+j)}{j!}xM^{\ast j}(x^{-1/\alpha}).

Cross-modal semantic scene completion for complex urban traffic scenes with reachability geometric attention
Authors: Baorong Zhan, Xichang Yu, Jiajun Zou, Shaojiang Liu, Feng Wang, Meixia Dong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn complex urban roads, mutual occlusions among multiple entities are pervasive. Approaches that rely solely on visible-appearance segmentation or perform completion only in voxel space struggle to recover pixel-level semantics and shapes for occluded regions. This paper presents AGAC, a cross-modal, temporally aware semantic scene completion network augmented with reachability-aware geometric attention. We first construct an anisotropic reachability potential field in a unified reference frame and differentiably project both the potential values and the steepest-descent directions onto the image plane, yielding per-pixel reachability scalars, preferred propagation directions, and reachability masks. We then introduce reachability-aware geometric attention in the pixel domain, embedding potential differences, directional consistency, and occlusion risk into both the attention neighborhood and its scoring, so that long-range completion propagates only along geometrically feasible, low-cost, and directionally consistent conduits. The decoder performs hierarchical occlusion parsing with anisotropic refinement, diffusing along the principal direction while suppressing boundary leakage along the normal, thereby achieving amodal filling with continuous contours and topologically consistent shapes. Finally, a geometry-reprojection-gated dual-window memory with uncertainty weighting delivers temporally stable cross-frame fusion and robust reliability quantification on hard cases. System-level evaluations on KITTI-360-APS, BDD100K-APS, and BlendPASS show that AGAC achieves leading performance on APQ, APC, AAP, and occluded-instance AP_occ, improving overall metrics by approximately 3–4% over strong baselines and AP_occ by about 5–7%. Introduction

In complex urban road scenes, multi-vehicle co-driving, mixed flows of pedestrians and cyclists, and dense roadside infrastructure make local and layered occlusions ubiquitous. This paper focuses on the problem of pixel-level amodal semantic completion in vehicle-mounted video sequences acquired by on-board multi-camera and LiDAR perception systems under complex traffic environments. Here, amodal refers to predicting not only the semantics of the currently visible portion of an object, but also recovering the full semantic extent and spatial contour of its occluded part. Cameras provide high-resolution textures and clear semantic boundaries, but they are highly sensitive to line-of-sight obstruction, illumination variation, and motion blur. LiDAR supplies relatively stable spatial geometry, yet exhibits pronounced sparsity in long-range regions and for small objects1–3. Consequently, semantic segmentation in the visible domain alone often yields incomplete object regions and cannot reliably support downstream planning in the assessment of traversable space and potential risks. In contrast, occupancy completion performed solely in voxel space or in a bird’s-eye-view (BEV) representation often lacks pixel-level fine-grained shape details, contour continuity at occlusion boundaries, and clear class separation4,5. Against this background, semantic completion for vehicle-mounted video perception requires a unified framework that jointly integrates cross-modal geometry and image appearance while ensuring consistency with real road topology.

Although substantial progress has recently been made in cross-modal 3D perception, BEV representation learning, and video semantic segmentation, directly applying these methods to amodal semantic completion in complex road scenes still faces several critical challenges. Cross-modal and cross-frame observations inherently suffer from temporal synchronization errors, extrinsic calibration deviations, and viewpoint discrepancies. These issues are further amplified during long-range projection and back-projection, resulting in misalignment between geometric locations and semantic evidence6,7. Pixel-domain feature aggregation is typically driven by appearance similarity. Without explicit constraints from road connectivity and preferred propagation directions, attention mechanisms may establish erroneous connections between regions that are visually similar but geometrically unreachable, thereby causing cross-obstacle diffusion, category contamination, and incorrect instance merging8–10. In addition, occluded regions themselves lack direct visible evidence, and their recovery must rely on indirect cues propagated from distant visible areas. This requires information propagation to extend continuously along geometrically plausible directions while suppressing unnecessary lateral leakage in the normal direction, otherwise, contour overflow, blurred boundaries, and mixing of adjacent targets are likely to occur. The problem considered in this paper further involves dynamic traffic scenes in video sequences, where visibility relationships change continuously with the motion of the ego-vehicle and surrounding traffic participants. Without geometric reprojection gating and uncertainty modeling, cross-frame fusion tends to introduce flickering, drifting, and shape jitter, thereby degrading temporal consistency11,12. Therefore, constructing a pixel-level completion mechanism that simultaneously accounts for geometric reachability, directional consistency of propagation, and temporal stability under vehicle-mounted video settings is a key problem for improving scene understanding in complex road environments.

To address the above issues, this paper proposes AGAC, a cross-modal temporal semantic scene completion network with reachability-guided geometric attention. The method first constructs an anisotropic reachability potential field in a unified reference frame, and then projects both the potential values and the steepest descent directions onto the image plane in a differentiable manner, yielding pixel-level reachability scalars, preferred propagation directions, and reachable masks. These geometric priors are subsequently used in the image domain to constrain neighborhood construction and attention scoring, so that long-range feature propagation is restricted to channels that are geometrically feasible, directionally consistent, and low-cost. On the decoding side, a layered occlusion parsing and anisotropic refinement mechanism is introduced to jointly model visible-layer semantics, occlusion probabilities, and occluded-layer amodal semantics, enabling continuous completion along the principal direction while preserving boundary constraints in the normal direction. Furthermore, in the temporal dimension, geometric reprojection gating, dual-window memory, and pixel-level uncertainty estimation are incorporated to achieve stable fusion and reliable recovery under dynamic occlusions. Through this design, the proposed method enables semantic completion in vehicle-mounted multi-camera video perception while jointly maintaining geometric consistency, contour integrity, and temporal stability under complex occlusion conditions.

The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows.

(1) A pixel-domain reachability-aware geometric prior is proposed by differentiably projecting the anisotropic reachability potential field and direction field in BEV onto the image plane, thereby forming a unified, interpretable, and trainable pixel-level geometric constraint.

(2) A reachability-guided geometric attention module, termed RGA-Img, is designed to jointly encode potential differences, directional consistency, and occlusion risk into geometry-aware attention bias within constrained neighborhoods, and to adaptively combine local dense routing with global sparse routing, thereby systematically suppressing cross-obstacle diffusion and category contamination.

(3) A layered occlusion parsing and anisotropic refinement decoder is developed, which leverages direction-selective evidence aggregation and normal-direction boundary suppression to produce more complete contours and sharper boundaries for occluded vehicles and thin road structures.

(4) A temporally consistent fusion framework with geometric reprojection gating and uncertainty estimation is constructed. By coordinating short-window details and long-window topology, it suppresses flickering and drifting while providing pixel-level confidence weighting for high-risk regions.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews recent advances related to semantic completion. Section 3 presents an overview of the proposed method. Section 4 details the overall framework and its four core components, namely image-plane cross-modal alignment and reachability projection, image-domain reachability-guided geometric attention, layered occlusion parsing and semantic completion decoding, and temporally consistent fusion with uncertainty estimation. Section 5 introduces the experimental settings and reports systematic comparisons and ablation studies on multiple public benchmarks. Section 6 concludes the paper and discusses potential applications and future extensions.

Related work

Representation learning from multi-camera to BEV and 3D occupancy prediction

Representation learning from multi-camera inputs to bird’s-eye-view (BEV) provides an important foundation for spatial structure modeling in autonomous driving scenes. LSS implicitly lifts multi-view images into the BEV space, establishing a unified representation paradigm from the image plane to the bird’s-eye-view domain, so that pure vision-based perception can directly perform aggregation and reasoning in spatial coordinates13. Building upon this idea, BEVFormer further introduces a spatiotemporal Transformer to jointly model cross-view spatial information and cross-frame temporal information on BEV queries, significantly improving multi-task scene understanding and robustness under low-visibility conditions14. These methods indicate that BEV representations not only compress redundant information from multi-camera observations, but also form strong geometric consistency within a unified spatial space. The core idea of BEVFormer lies precisely in exploiting both spatial and temporal interactions on BEV queries, which further highlights the importance of unified spatiotemporal representations for autonomous driving perception.

For 3D occupancy prediction, TPVFormer enhances the representational capability for 3D structure recovery by jointly modeling the BEV plane and two complementary orthogonal planes, achieving competitive performance in pure-vision semantic occupancy prediction15. SurroundOcc directly performs 3D occupancy completion in voxel space for complex street scenes and strengthens voxel-level semantic recovery through multi-level supervision16. OpenOccupancy further provides a unified large-scale benchmark and evaluation protocol for surrounding semantic occupancy perception in autonomous driving, enabling systematic comparisons across different modalities and network paradigms17. Overall, research in this direction mainly focuses on cross-view geometric consistency modeling and enhancement of 3D spatial completion capability. However, most existing methods still produce outputs primarily in BEV or voxel space, lacking direct constraints on occluded regions in the image plane. In particular, when pixel-level contours, thin structures, and continuity of occlusion boundaries need to be recovered, spatial occupancy modeling alone often fails to provide sufficiently fine-grained directional completion cues. Therefore, although existing BEV/occupancy-based methods provide a geometric organizational form for cross-modal perception, they have not yet addressed the problem of topology-constrained pixel-level amodal semantic completion in the image domain.

Amodal segmentation and amodal panoptic evaluation

Different from BEV- and occupancy-based methods, amodal segmentation more directly focuses on pixel-level recovery of occluded regions. The line of work on Amodal Panoptic Segmentation systematically defines the joint modeling task of visible and occluded layers, and proposes APSNet as well as evaluation metrics such as APQ and APC, requiring models not only to perform semantic recognition but also to assess the overall quality of targets in both their visible and occluded parts18. On this basis, AISDiff leverages diffusion models to learn shape priors and introduces global structural generation capability into amodal instance recovery, thereby improving the stability of contour completion for occluded objects19. OASS further addresses panoramic wide-view and cross-domain scenarios by proposing an occlusion-aware seamless segmentation framework together with the BlendPASS dataset, achieving a favorable balance among view stitching, occlusion completion, and cross-domain generalization20. These methods show that local appearance alone is insufficient for effective recovery of complex occluded regions, and that introducing shape priors, panoramic context, and multi-task learning is an important direction for improving amodal recovery performance.

However, from the perspective of methodological design, existing image-domain amodal methods are still mainly driven by appearance similarity or global shape priors, and remain insufficient in exploiting road topology, propagation direction, and geometric reachability. When road turns, building occlusions, vegetation occlusions, or serial multi-vehicle occlusions are present, such methods are prone to boundary overflow, semantic mixing, and erroneous long-range information propagation. Therefore, how to explicitly incorporate geometric feasibility and directional preference into amodal semantic recovery remains a key weakness of current approaches.

Methods related to geometric constraints and directional propagation

In attention modeling, recent studies have gradually recognized that attention mechanisms relying solely on appearance similarity are inadequate for satisfying geometric consistency requirements in multi-view scenarios. GTA directly encodes relative 3D geometric relationships into attention scores, enabling cross-view feature aggregation to possess explicit geometric constraints and directional preference, thereby improving the efficiency and consistency of spatial reasoning in multi-view Transformers21. This idea suggests that geometric relationships can serve not only as post-processing constraints, but can also directly participate in attention weight allocation during feature interaction.

For continuous shape modeling, deep anisotropic diffusion incorporates the idea of propagation along the principal direction and suppression of leakage along the normal direction into neural networks. For example, guided anisotropic diffusion combined with convolutional features for depth super-resolution can effectively improve boundary preservation and detail recovery22. Such methods demonstrate that when propagation direction is explicitly modulated by local structure, the resulting recovery tends to exhibit better contour continuity and sharper boundaries. In the construction of cost fields and direction fields, the Eikonal equation provides a clear mathematical foundation for shortest arrival time and connectivity cost. Recent neural Eikonal solvers and physics-informed neural networks have further improved its accuracy and trainability under complex media and anisotropic conditions, making it possible to embed reachability potential fields and direction fields into learning frameworks in a continuous and differentiable manner23,24.

Existing studies have thus provided valuable insights from three separate aspects, namely geometry-aware attention, directional diffusion, and continuous cost-field modeling. However, these methods are mostly developed independently: geometric relationships are commonly used for cross-view feature aggregation, directional diffusion is mainly employed for continuous restoration, and Eikonal-based methods are primarily applied to cost-field computation. Few works have unified these three components within the task of vehicle-mounted occluded semantic completion. In contrast to the above approaches, this paper differentiably projects the reachability potential and the steepest descent direction from BEV to the image domain, and explicitly introduces reachable pathways and directional consistency into neighborhood construction, attention scoring, and decoding diffusion, thereby achieving topology-constrained occlusion completion at the pixel level.

Method overview

AGAC achieves occlusion recovery and pixel-level amodal semantic completion for vehicular scenes by adopting a reachability-driven approach. It integrates road connectivity and propagation direction through differentiable priors across the entire workflow—spanning cross-modal alignment, attention routing, hierarchical decoding, and temporal fusion. The method integrates multimodal observations within a unified self-vehicle reference frame, constructing an anisotropic reachability potential field in BEV. Through differentiable projection, potential values and steepest descent directions are mapped back to each camera’s pixel plane, yielding pixel-level reachability scalars, preferred propagation directions, and reachability masks. Consequently, subsequent feature aggregation and completion are constrained within geometrically feasible channels, preventing cross-obstacle diffusion and category contamination. In terms of the specific workflow, multi-sensor data is first aligned to the vehicle’s coordinate system at a reference time based on the vehicle pose, sensor calibration parameters and depth information provided by the dataset, and uniformly represented within the BEV mesh. An anisotropic reachability connectivity tensor is constructed by combining lane tangency, local gradient and occupancy status, whereby higher passability is assigned along the lane direction and in low-occupancy areas, whilst passability is reduced in directions crossing obstacles, in regions of abrupt gradient changes and in high-occupancy areas, thereby forming a directionally biased reachability potential field. This potential field is further projected onto the image domain to obtain pixel-level geometric priors. Building upon this, the image-domain reachability geometric attention utilises reachability scalars, directional fields, and reachability masks to constrain long-range feature routing. A hierarchical occlusion resolution and semantic completion decoder jointly models the semantics of the visible layer, occlusion probabilities, and amodal semantics of the occlusion layer, whilst enhancing contour continuity at occlusion boundaries through anisotropic refinement, Finally, by combining geometric reprojection gating, dual-window memory and uncertainty estimation, we achieve temporally consistent fusion, thereby obtaining stable completion results in dynamic occlusion scenarios.

For image domain representation and routing, we construct the Reachability Geometric Attention (RGA-Img) module. This module jointly constrains the attention’s local and sparse global neighborhood using reachability masks, potential difference thresholds, and directional consistency, permitting connections only to pixels that are reachable, low-cost, and collinear with the query pixel. During scoring, content similarity and geometric bias are jointly modeled. Geometric weights derived from potential values, inverse potential penalties, and occlusion risks suppress erroneous connections between geometrically inaccessible yet visually similar pixels. Directional bias, formed by directional consistency and pixel-domain anisotropy metrics, compensates for long-chain supply with geometrically feasible but visually weak evidence. Adaptively fuses locally dense aggregation with globally sparse completion through potential-based gating to obtain geometrically consistent updated features.

For decoding and completion, a hierarchical occlusion resolution and semantic completion decoding approach is employed. The network predicts semantics and occlusion probabilities in the visible layer, then performs directional selection evidence aggregation within occluded regions using reachable kernels to obtain the initial amodal semantics of the occluded layer. Subsequently, anisotropic refinement smooths the fill along the preferred propagation direction while implementing boundary fidelity suppression along the normal direction. This ensures the completion adheres to geometric constraints of roads and object contours while preventing lateral leakage. Finally, the visible and occluded layers are fused at the pixel level using occlusion probability, yielding a topologically consistent and contour-continuous completion result.

Experimental results and analysis

Experimental environment

To validate the effectiveness of AGAC and ensure comparability, three representative methods from the amodal domain over the past three years were selected for comparison: APSNet, AISDiff, and OASS. Experiments and evaluations were reproduced based on the authors’ published papers and implementation configurations.

All experiments were conducted on the same hardware and software platform, utilizing NVIDIA A100 80GB GPUs, dual AMD EPYC 7742 CPUs, and 1.0 TB of memory. All models ran on PyTorch 2.2 with mixed-precision training and distributed data parallelism enabled. Image domain inputs were uniformly standardized to 1280 × 720 (BDD100K-APS dataset) and 1408 × 376 (KITTI-360-APS dataset) for the APS benchmark side. The BlendPASS side of OASS was evaluated at the resolution recommended in the paper, using the category mapping and evaluation interface provided by the authors to align with the APS categories.

Experimental parameters and evaluation criteria

All AGAC-specific hyperparameters were fixed across KITTI-360-APS, BDD100K-APS, and BlendPASS unless otherwise stated. The reachability potential was normalized to [0,1] by dividing each frame-level potential map by the 95th percentile of valid free-space potentials and clipping the result to one. All methods in the experiment share uniform training and inference settings: AdamW optimizer with an initial learning rate of 2e-4 and weight decay of 0.05, employing Cosine annealing to 2e-6. Image batch size is set to 2 per GPU, with data augmentation including random horizontal flipping, color perturbation, and multi-scale scaling. AGAC was trained end-to-end on both APS datasets and directly outputs panoptic-amodal results. To align with instance-based methods, AGAC’s panoptic results underwent standard panoptic fusion to derive instance masks for instance-based metrics. AISDiff performs tri-head instance learning for visible/occluded/category on the same segmentation as specified in the paper and generates amodal masks. OASS employs the authors’ publicly available UnmaskFormer and BlendPASS protocols to output multi-head results including amodal panoptic and amodal instance outputs.

The experimental comparison metrics are defined as follows:

(1) APQ: Under conditions where both visible and occluded regions participate, the cumulative IoU of matching pairs with IoU ≥ 0.5 is calculated. After applying a penalty to unmatched items, the result is normalized to yield a category-average score. This metric evaluates the overall quality of amodal panoramic parsing, where higher values indicate more stable recognition and segmentation.

(2) APC: Building upon APQ, this metric weights IoU values by instance pixel area, introducing sensitivity to target scale. It better reflects coverage and reconstruction quality for large-scale objects like vehicles under occlusion conditions.

(3) AAP: Calculates average accuracy across multiple IoU thresholds for amodal instance masks, providing a comprehensive evaluation of detection-segmentation consistency. It directly reflects the localization and contour reconstruction accuracy of occluded instances.

(4) AP_occ: Calculates average precision only on instances with occlusion rates exceeding a given threshold, focusing on challenging cases in realistic occlusion scenarios. It isolates the influence of visible regions to specifically measure completion and recognition capabilities under severe occlusion conditions.

APC Experimental Results.

As shown in Fig. 2(a), AGAC achieves the highest scores on Overall APC and Visible-APC across all three benchmarks, demonstrating overall advantages over OASS by 3.7, 3.5, and 3.4 points respectively. Its improvement over the task baseline APSNet ranges from 7 to 10% points. The comprehensive improvement in visible region scores indicates that the reachability prior and directional propagation did not degrade performance in visible areas. Instead, they enhanced panoramic consistency and area coverage across the entire image, with particularly pronounced gains in occluded regions. AGAC’s Occluded-APC outperformed OASS by 6.0, 5.3, and 5.7 points across the three datasets, while achieving gains of 11.2, 10.2, and 12.7 points over APSNet, demonstrating systematic improvements in handling occluded regions.

As shown in Fig. 2(b), the results segmented by occlusion rate exhibit a monotonically increasing gap. Across the moderate-to-heavy occlusion ranges (40–60, 60–80, > 80), AGAC consistently outperforms OASS 4.0–5.0 on all three benchmarks and achieves double-digit gains over APSNet. Taking KITTI as an example, across the five segments from 20 to 40 to > 80, AGAC consistently achieves the highest scores. Specifically, it outperforms OASS by 4.8 and 4.6 points in the 60–80 and > 80 segments, respectively, with BDD and BlendPASS showing consistent trends. This phenomenon aligns with the mechanism: Image-Domain Reachability Geometric Attention introduces potential differences and directional consistency during scoring, enabling long-range aggregation to propagate only along low-cost, lane-aligned pixel channels. The anisotropic refinement of hierarchical decoding continuously diffuses along the principal direction while imposing boundary fidelity suppression in the normal direction, fundamentally reducing cross-obstacle diffusion and category contamination. Consequently, even under challenging conditions like distant weak textures or nighttime rain reflections, AGAC maintains stable supply along geometrically feasible channels and achieves area coverage in occluded regions, consistently outperforming APC across both overall and segmented dimensions.

AAP Experimental Results.

The overall and threshold results in Fig. 3(a) show that AGAC achieved the highest scores across all three benchmarks: AAP, AAP50, and AAP75. Compared to the current state-of-the-art OASS, AGAC’s overall AAP scores exceed OASS by 3.1, 2.9, and 2.8 on the three benchmarks, respectively. On the more stringent AAP75 metric, the advantages are 3.5, 3.2, and 3.6. This comparison with occlusion-layered models further demonstrates that the gains are concentrated in occluded regions. Occluded-AAP outperforms OASS by 4.3, 3.9, and 3.9 points respectively, while Visible-AAP also shows a slight positive improvement. This demonstrates that geometric constraints enhance occlusion recovery without compromising instance quality in the visible region.

Figure 3(b) results for the segmentation dimension exhibit a monotonic trend consistent with the method design. Across occlusion rate intervals, AGAC’s lead steadily expands with increasing occlusion intensity. In the 60–80% and > 80% intervals, the three benchmarks outperform OASS by approximately 4 to 5. In the scale segmentation, the large category demonstrates the most pronounced advantage, with the three benchmarks averaging approximately 4 points higher than OASS. Stable gains are also observed for occlusion instances within the vehicle subset. This pattern stems from synergistic geometric constraints at both ends: RGA’s reachable geometric attention introduces potential differences and directional consistency during scoring, while long-range aggregation establishes connections only along low-cost grooves and preferred propagation directions. Hierarchical occlusion decoding performs continuous diffusion along the principal direction while applying boundary fidelity suppression on the normal, reducing cross-occlusion diffusion and instance coalescence. Consequently, AGAC maintains superior mask quality and instance pairing stability under challenging conditions—including vehicle-to-vehicle chain occlusions, vehicle-road occlusions, distant weak textures, and nighttime rain—thus outperforming AAP in overall, threshold-based, and multi-dimensional refinement metrics.

To evaluate the computational overhead of the proposed method, we further compared AGAC with APSNet, AISDiff, and OASS in terms of the number of trainable parameters and inference latency. All models were evaluated under the same software and hardware environment described in Sect. 5.1. We measured inference latency on a single NVIDIA A100 80GB GPU using PyTorch 2.2, mixed-precision inference, and a batch size of 1, with an input resolution of 1280 × 720 on the BDD100K-APS validation set. Each model underwent 100 warm-up iterations, followed by timing of 500 frames of validation data using CUDA event synchronization. The timing scope excludes data loading and disk I/O but includes model forward passes and necessary task-specific post-processing. For AGAC, the reported latency includes reachability projection, BEV-to-image geometric prior mapping, RGA-Img, temporal fusion, and panoramic modality-free output fusion.

As shown in Table 5, APSNet has the lowest parameter count and inference latency, with 52.4 M parameters and 78.6ms per frame, but its Overall APQ and Occluded-APQ are also the lowest among all compared methods. AISDiff introduces a diffusion-based amodal generation process, leading to the largest model size and the highest latency, reaching 114.8 M parameters and 186.3ms per frame. OASS achieves stronger accuracy than APSNet with 68.9 M parameters and 104.5ms latency, serving as a competitive accuracy-efficiency baseline. More importantly, AGAC improves Overall APQ from 39.5 to 42.5 and Occluded-APQ from 26.6 to 30.1 compared with OASS, while maintaining an inference speed of 8.35 FPS. These results show that AGAC obtains the best accuracy-efficiency trade-off among the compared methods, especially for occluded-region semantic completion.

Conclusion

This paper proposes AGAC, a cross-modal temporal semantic scene completion network integrating reachability geometric attention, for occlusion recovery and pixel-level amodal semantic completion in vehicle scenes. The method employs reachability potential fields and direction fields as core intermediate representations, injecting connectivity and propagation directions in a differentiable manner into feature aggregation and decoding processes. Cross-modal geometric alignment constructs a potential connectivity tensor under unified coordinates and time. Image-domain reachability geometric attention routes long-range information along low-cost grooves within constrained neighborhoods. Hierarchical occlusion decoding achieves shape continuity and normal boundary fidelity via anisotropic diffusion. Temporal consistency fusion employs reprojection gating and dual memory to stably complete complex dynamic occlusions.

Across three public benchmarks, AGAC consistently outperforms competitors in APQ, APC, AAP, and occlusion instance APocc, demonstrating particularly strong advantages in heavily occluded, long-range, and large-volume vehicle subsets. Experiments demonstrate that integrating reachability and directionality into attention scoring and decoding propagation systematically enhances occlusion region matching IoU, area coverage, and instance pairing stability without compromising visible region quality. Results further indicate that geometric priors exhibit strong transferability across different viewpoints and imaging conditions, maintaining robust gains across multi-source data and diverse occlusion patterns.

The method still faces two challenges. On one hand, the potential field and direction field are sensitive to geometric calibration and depth quality, potentially leading to conservative propagation and local under-completion under extreme conditions such as nighttime rain. On the other hand, distant small targets exhibit significant discretization errors in the pixel domain, resulting in reduced stability for potential threshold selection and direction estimation.

Future work will further expand our current research, encompassing the joint learning of image-domain and BEV semantic occupancy, establishing bidirectional consistency constraints between reachability potential fields and 3D occupancy, and unifying evaluation and training signals. We will introduce weakly supervised and self-supervised reachability learning, leveraging map priors, temporal consistency, and multi-view geometry to automatically distill potential and direction fields, thereby reducing reliance on expensive annotations and high-precision depth data. For deployment optimization, we enhance inference efficiency and scalability through lightweight geometric attention, region-level gating, and uncertainty-driven dynamic early stopping. We also explore closed-loop collaboration with planning decisions, enabling completed results to directly inform reachability assessment and risk prediction.

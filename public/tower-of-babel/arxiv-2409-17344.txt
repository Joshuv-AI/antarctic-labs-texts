Cauchy problem for the Dirac Operator on spatially non-compact spacetimes
Orville Damaschke

Abstract
. Let M M be a globally hyperbolic manifold with complete spacelike Cauchy hypersurface Σ \Sigma . We prove well-posedness of the Cauchy problem for the Dirac operator on globally hyperbolic manifolds with complete Cauchy hypersurfaces. This result is needed as preparation in showing a Fredholmness result in the manner, provided by Bär and Strohmaier, for certain non-compact Cauchy hypersurfaces in future work.

1.
Introduction and statement of the main result

This paper deals with the initial value problem of the Dirac equation on curved spacetime for the case of non-compact, but complete Cauchy hypersurfaces. The known results for the Cauchy problem with smooth inital data in
[
1
, Thm.4]
are extended to those with Sobolev regularity i.e. our contribution states that under (spatial) compactly supported initial data the resulting spinor fields are of finite energy, i.e. they are continuous in time and of some Sobolev regularity in space. This has been already announced but not concretely proven in
[
1
]
. The well-posedness statement in this reference relies on the well-posedness of the Cauchy problem for the wave equation under similar regularity assumptions on the initial data and the inhomogeneity. We are going to present a direct proof. The results have been already non-peer-reviewed published in
[
11
]
. This existing version is only focused on the Cauchy problem, but we modify the content to the twisted Dirac operator and correct some issues.

;

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle FE^{s}_{\mathsf{sc}}(M,\mathpzc{T},D_{\pm}^{E})\,\,\rightarrow\,\,H^{s}_{\mathsf{c}}(\mathpzc{S}^{\pm}_{E}(M)|_{\Sigma_{t}})\oplus L^{2}_{\mathsf{loc},\mathsf{sc}}(\mathpzc{T}(M),H^{s}_{\mathsf{loc}}(\mathpzc{S}^{\mp}_{E}(M)_{\Sigma_{\bullet}}))

This is going to be proven as

2.
Notations, geometric setup & known facts

We recapitulate notations and some useful facts of sections of vector bundles over a manifold and operators, acting between those sections. After introducing some basic background of globally hyperbolic manifolds, we focus on several function spaces especially constructed for those spacetimes. In order to investigate Sobolev regularity on spacelike non-compact Cauchy hypersurfaces, a repetition of some basics about Sobolev spaces on non-compact Riemannian manifolds are prepared.

2.3.
Function spaces on globally hyperbolic manifolds

We recall some special function spaces on a globally hyperbolic manifold
M
with complete, boundaryless Cauchy hypersurface
\Sigma
and Cauchy temporal function
\mathpzc{T}:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Further details and more examples of function spaces on globally hyperbolic manifolds are presented in
[
9

[

Given
s\in\mathbb{R}
and the family
\left\{H^{s}_{\mathsf{loc}}(E|_{\Sigma_{t}})\right\}_{t\in\mathpzc{T}(M)}
as Fréchet bundle over
\mathpzc{T}(M)\subset\mathbb{R}
. The slices differ from each other only in the metric
\mathpzc{g}_{t}
, but not topologically. Since different metrics lead to equivalent Sobolev norms, each Sobolev space
H^{s}_{K}(E|_{\Sigma_{t}})
for
K\Subset\Sigma
and consequently
H^{s}_{\mathsf{c}}(E|_{\Sigma_{t}})
and
H^{s}_{\mathsf{loc}}(E|_{\Sigma_{t}})
are equivalent. We keep the extra
t
to mark the different metrics and furthermore keep notational compatability with the referred literature.
\left\{H^{s}_{\mathsf{loc}}(E|_{\Sigma_{t}})\right\}_{t\in\mathpzc{T}(M)}
can be globally trivialised as follows: for each
t\in\mathpzc{T}(M)
a section of this bundle becomes a section in
H^{s}_{\mathsf{loc}}(E|_{\Sigma_{t}})
. Sections of this bundle can be moved to
H^{s}_{\mathsf{loc}}(E|_{\Sigma_{\tau}})
for a fixed
\tau\in\mathpzc{T}(M)
by parallel transport along the integral curves of the vector field
\mathrm{grad}(\mathpzc{T})
. The support properties and the Sobolev regularity are preserved by this transport such that this bundle of Fréchet spaces becomes diffeomorphic to
\mathpzc{T}(M)\times H^{s}_{\mathsf{loc}}(E|_{\Sigma_{\tau}})
;
l
-times continuously differentiable sections in the time parameter (
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
) are denoted by
C^{l}(\mathpzc{T}(M),H^{s}_{\mathsf{loc}}(E|_{\Sigma_{\bullet}}))
. The elements can be considered as regular distributions on
M
and are locally integrable for
s\geq 0
since
H^{s}_{\mathsf{loc}}\subset L^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}
by the continuous embedding of Sobolev spaces. Moreover,
C^{l}(\mathpzc{T}(M),H^{s}_{\mathsf{loc}}(E|_{\Sigma_{\bullet}}))
is embedded into
C^{-\infty}(M,E)
. One defines for any compact subinterval
I\subset\mathpzc{T}(M)
and any spatially compact
K\subset M
the space

(2.14)

C^{l}_{K}(\mathpzc{T}(M),H^{s}_{\mathsf{loc}}(E|_{\Sigma_{\bullet}})):=\Bigl\{u\in C^{l}(\mathpzc{T}(M),H^{s}_{\mathsf{loc}}(E|_{\Sigma_{\bullet}}))\,\Big|\,\mathrm{supp}\left(u\right)\subset K\Bigr\}

with the seminorm

(2.15)

\|u\|_{I,K,l,s}:=\max_{k\in[0,l]\cap\mathbb{N}_{0}}\max_{t\in I}\Big\|(\nabla^{E}_{t})^{l}u\Big\|_{H^{s}_{\mathsf{loc}}(E|_{\Sigma_{t}})}\quad.

Varying over all compact subsets

shows for fixed
l
,
K
, and
s
that
(
2.14
)
is a Fréchet space. Taking the union over all spatially compact subset defines sections of this bundle which have support in any spatially compact subset of
M
:

(2.16)

C^{l}_{\mathsf{sc}}(\mathpzc{T}(M),H^{s}_{\mathsf{loc}}(E|_{\Sigma_{\bullet}})):=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\subset M\\
K\,\,\text{spatially}\\
\text{compact}\end{subarray}}C^{l}_{K}(\mathpzc{T}(M),H^{s}_{\mathsf{loc}}(E|_{\Sigma_{\bullet}}))\quad.

The inclusion
C^{l}_{K}(\mathpzc{T}(M),H^{s}_{\mathsf{loc}}(E|_{\Sigma_{\bullet}}))\hookrightarrow C^{l}_{\mathsf{sc}}(\mathpzc{T}(M),H^{s}_{\mathsf{loc}}(E|_{\Sigma_{\bullet}}))
is continuous and any linear map from

to any locally convex topological vector space is continuous if and only if the restriction to
C^{l}_{K}(\mathpzc{T}(M),H^{s}_{\mathsf{loc}}(E|_{\Sigma_{\bullet}}))
is continuous for any spatially compact subset
K
. The case

will be of special interest.

3.
Spin structures and Dirac operators on globally hyperbolic spacetimes

We first repeat some basics about a spin structure and Dirac operators on pseudo-Riemannian manifolds. Afterwards, we consider the decomposition of the Dirac operator along a Riemannian hypersurface in a time-oriented Lorentzian manifold. The content of this section relies on the detailed explainations in the textbooks
[
2
]
and
[
21
]
with supporting notes from
[
3
]
,
[
26
]
and
[
17
]
.

3.1.
General aspects

We turn our attention on a time- and space-oriented
n
-dimensional (
n
even) pseudo-Riemannian spin manifold
M
with metric
\mathpzc{g}
of signature
(r,s)
. Such a manifold comes with a
spin(or) bundle

\mathpzc{S}(M)
which is defined as complex vector bundle, associated to the spinor representation of the representation space in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We call sections of
\mathpzc{S}(M)

spinor fields
and recall that for even dimensions the spinor bundle decomposes into the two submodules
\mathpzc{S}^{\pm}(M)
:

(3.1)

\mathpzc{S}(M)=\mathpzc{S}^{+}(M)\oplus\mathpzc{S}^{-}(M)\quad.

These subbundles are called
positive
respectively
negative half-spin(or) bundles
and we call their sections spinor fields of positive respectively negative chirality. A Clifford multiplication operation is given by a pointwise acting vector space homomorphism
\mathbf{c}
from
T_{p}M
to
\mathsf{End}(\mathpzc{S}_{p}(M))
, satisfying

(3.2)

\mathbf{c}\left(X\right)\cdot\mathbf{c}\left(Y\right)+\mathbf{c}\left(Y\right)\cdot\mathbf{c}\left(X\right)=-2\mathpzc{g}_{p}(X,Y)\mathbbm{1}_{\mathpzc{S}_{p}(M)}\quad\forall\,X,Y\in T_{p}M\quad.

In addition, there exists a non-degenerate Hermitian bundle product
\big(\cdot\,\big|\,\cdot\big)_{\mathpzc{S}(M)}
on the spinor bundle which is only positive definite in the Riemannian case. The action of
\mathbf{c}\left(X\right)
on a spinor becomes formally self-adjoint:

(3.3)

\big(\mathbf{c}\left(X\right)u\,\big|\,v\big)_{\mathpzc{S}_{p}(M)}+(-1)^{s}\big(u\,\big|\,\mathbf{c}\left(X\right)v\big)_{\mathpzc{S}_{p}(M)}=0\quad\forall\,X\in T_{p}M

for
u,v\in\mathpzc{S}_{p}(M)
. A consequence of
(
3.2
)
and
(
3.3
)
is the following scaling property:

(3.4)

\big(\mathbf{c}\left(X\right)u\,\big|\,\mathbf{c}\left(X\right)v\big)_{\mathpzc{S}_{p}(M)}=(-1)^{s}\mathpzc{g}(X,X)\big(u\,\big|\,v\big)_{\mathpzc{S}_{p}(M)}\,.

Integrating
\big(\cdot\,\big|\,\cdot\big)_{\mathpzc{S}_{p}(M)}
over the manifold against the volume form
\,\mathrm{d}\mathsf{vol}_{\,\mathpzc{g}}
gives a sesquilinear form
\big(\cdot\,\big|\,\cdot\big)_{C^{\infty}_{\mathsf{c}}(\mathpzc{S}(M))}
which is positive definite in the Riemannian case, but in general only non-degenerate. In order to construct
L^{2}
-spaces for spinor sections as Hilbert spaces, we have to consider the completion of
C^{\infty}_{\mathsf{c}}(\mathpzc{S}(M))
with respect to
\big(\cdot\,\big|\,\cdot\big)_{C^{\infty}_{\mathsf{c}}(\mathpzc{S}(M))}
which only works for
M
being a Riemannian manifold. However, one is able to relate the non-degenerate bundle metric to a positive definite Hermitian sesquilinear form. For Lorentzian manifolds, which is the case of our interest, this works as follows: if
M
is space- and time-oriented, it admits a global unit and timelike vector field
\mathfrak{t}
such that

(3.5)

\big\langle\cdot\,\big|\,\cdot\big\rangle_{\mathpzc{S}(M)}:=\big(\mathbf{c}\left(\mathfrak{t}\right)\cdot\,\big|\,\cdot\big)_{\mathpzc{S}(M)}

is a positive definite bundle metric on
\mathpzc{S}(M)
which then implies
\big(\cdot\,\big|\,\cdot\big)_{C^{\infty}_{\mathsf{c}}(\mathpzc{S}(M))}
to be an inner product. The completion of
C^{\infty}_{\mathsf{c}}(\mathpzc{S}(M))
with respect to this inner product then induces a Krein space where
\mathbf{c}\left(\mathfrak{t}\right)
acts as fundamental symmetry, since
\mathbf{c}\left(\mathfrak{t}\right)
is self-adjoint by
(
3.3
)
(
s=1
) and unitary from
(
3.4
)
because
\mathpzc{g}(\mathfrak{t},\mathfrak{t})=-1
holds:

(3.6)

\big(\mathbf{c}\left(\mathfrak{t}\right)u\,\big|\,\mathbf{c}\left(\mathfrak{t}\right)v\big)_{\mathpzc{S}(M)}=-\mathpzc{g}(\mathfrak{t},\mathfrak{t})\big(u\,\big|\,v\big)_{\mathpzc{S}(M)}=\big(u\,\big|\,v\big)_{\mathpzc{S}(M)}\quad.

In order to define the spin- or Atiyah-Singer Dirac operator, we have to clarify how to implement a connection on
\mathpzc{S}(M)
. It is proven in
[
2
, Satz 3.2]
that the local expression for the spin Levi-Civita connection
{\nabla^{\scriptstyle{\mathpzc{S}(M)}}}
is related to the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
of
TM
:

(3.7)

{\nabla^{\scriptstyle{\mathpzc{S}(M)}}_{{X}}}\Psi=\left[A,X(\Psi)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k<l}\epsilon_{k}\epsilon_{l}\mathpzc{g}(\nabla_{X}e_{k},e_{l})\mathbf{c}\left(e_{k}\right)\cdot\mathbf{c}\left(e_{l}\right)\psi\right]

where
A
is an element of the spin frame bundle and
\psi
an element of the spin representation space. The action of
X
on the local function
\psi
with values in the representation space is defined as for scalar functions. Replacing
X
with an element
e_{j}
in the tangent frame allows to rewrite
\mathpzc{g}(\nabla_{e_{j}}e_{k},e_{l})
with Christoffel symbols.
{\nabla^{\scriptstyle{\mathpzc{S}(M)}}}
complies with the Leibniz rule which is inherited from the one of
\nabla
. One can further determine the connection in such a way that the following compatability conditions are additionally satisfied:

(3.8)

\displaystyle X\big(u\,\big|\,v\big)_{\mathpzc{S}(M)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big({\nabla^{\scriptstyle{\mathpzc{S}(M)}}_{{X}}}u\,\big|\,v\big)_{\mathpzc{S}(M)}+\big(u\,\big|\,{\nabla^{\scriptstyle{\mathpzc{S}(M)}}_{{X}}}v\big)_{\mathpzc{S}(M)}

(3.9)

\displaystyle{\nabla^{\scriptstyle{\mathpzc{S}(M)}}_{{X}}}(\mathbf{c}\left(Y\right)u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{c}\left(\nabla_{X}Y\right)u+\mathbf{c}\left(Y\right){\nabla^{\scriptstyle{\mathpzc{S}(M)}}_{{X}}}u

where
u,v\in C^{\infty}(\mathpzc{S}(M))
and
X,Y\in C^{\infty}(TM)
. Condition
(
3.9
)
implies that the Clifford multiplication homomorphism and thus the spinorial volume form become globally parallel. As
\mathpzc{S}^{\pm}(M)
can be viewed as eigenbundles of the spinorial volume form,
(
3.9
)
implies that the spin connection preserves the eigenspace decomposition
(
3.1
)
.

The
Dirac operator
is the map
\mathpzc{D}\,:\,C^{\infty}(\mathpzc{S}(M))\,\rightarrow\,C^{\infty}(\mathpzc{S}(M))
which is locally given via

(3.10)

\mathpzc{D}\Psi:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\epsilon_{j}\mathbf{c}\left(e_{j}\right){\nabla^{\scriptstyle{\mathpzc{S}(M)}}_{{e_{j}}}}\Psi\\

for a local orthonormal pseudo-Riemannian tangent frame
e_{1},...,e_{n}
. Its principal symbol is

{\sigma}_{1}(\mathpzc{D})(x,\xi)\Psi=\mathbbm{i}\mathbf{c}\left(\xi^{\sharp}\right)\Psi(x)\,;

the sharp isomorphism is taken with respect to
\mathpzc{g}
. It shows that
\mathpzc{D}
is not-elliptic if
s\neq 0
as it can vanish for a lightlike covector.
(
3.10
)
respects the splitting
(
3.1
)
, but maps sections of the subbundle
\mathpzc{S}^{+}(M)
to
\mathpzc{S}^{-}(M)
and vice versa. Hence,
\mathpzc{D}
can be represented as

(3.11)

\mathpzc{D}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&D_{-}\\
D_{+}&0\end{matrix}\right)\quad\text{with}\quad D_{\pm}\in\mathsf{Diff}^{1}(\mathpzc{S}^{\pm}(M),\mathpzc{S}^{\mp}(M))\quad.

Both
D_{\pm}
are also non-elliptic as long as
0<s<n
. The
Dirac-Laplacian

\mathpzc{D}^{2}
is a differential operator of second order with principal symbol
{\sigma}_{2}(\mathpzc{D}^{2})(x,\xi)=\mathpzc{g}(\xi^{\sharp},\xi^{\sharp})\mathbbm{1}_{\mathpzc{S}(M)}
which is also a non-elliptic operator apart from the Riemannian case. In particular, this shows that
\mathpzc{D}^{2}
and thus
D_{\pm}D_{\mp}
are a normally hyperbolic operators. The difference between the Dirac-Laplacian and the Bochner-Laplacian
{\nabla^{\scriptstyle{\mathpzc{S}(M)}}}^{\ast}{\nabla^{\scriptstyle{\mathpzc{S}(M)}}}
is expressed in terms of curvature quantities and known as (pseudo-Riemannian) Lichnerowicz-Weitzenböck formula.

We now specify
M
to be a
(n+1)
-dimensional globally hyperbolic spin manifold with
n
odd. We consider any compact, but fixed time interval
[t_{1},t_{2}]
such that
M|_{[t_{1},t_{2}]}
becomes temporal compact. The global hyperbolicity implies that
M|_{[t_{1},t_{2}]}
becomes a manifold with boundary
\Sigma_{1}\sqcup\Sigma_{2}
with
\Sigma_{i}=\Sigma_{t_{i}}
for
i\in\left\{1,2\right\}
. The defect of
\mathpzc{D}
to be formally skew-adjoint can be expressed in terms of boundary integrals.

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose
M
is a globally hyperbolic spin manifold with spacelike Cauchy hypersurface
\Sigma
, Lorentzian metric
(
2.8
)
and
\mathpzc{D}
the Dirac operator; for any time interval

spinor fields along the hypersurface.

4.
Well-posedness of the Cauchy problem for the Dirac equation

Given the Dirac operators
D^{E}_{\pm}
for twisted spinor fields on a globally hyperbolic spin manifold with non-compact, but complete Cauchy hypersurface
\Sigma
; these are from now on our key assumptions. The main interest is focused on the Cauchy problem

(4.1)

D_{\pm}^{E}u=f\quad\text{with}\quad u|_{\Sigma_{t}}=g

for a suitable weak solution
u
as section of
\mathpzc{S}_{E}^{\pm}(M)

a suitable section of
\mathpzc{S}_{E}^{\mp}(M)
and

a Sobolev section of
\mathpzc{S}_{E}^{\pm}(\Sigma_{t})
which we will specify in the following subsections. We start with some preparatory results in order to prove and calculate an energy estimate which becomes crucial in showing uniqueness and thus the main result of this section respectively this paper.

4.1.
Some preparatory results

Several special relations turn out to be useful in computing energy estimates. We start with the untwisted case.

Lemma 4.1
.

The following relations hold for a vector field
X\in C^{\infty}(T\Sigma_{t})
and a smooth section
u
of
\mathpzc{S}(M)
along a hypersurface
\Sigma_{t}
for each
t\in\mathpzc{T}(M)
:

(1)

{\nabla^{\scriptstyle{\mathpzc{S}(\Sigma_{t})}}_{{X}}}(\upbeta u)=\upbeta{\nabla^{\scriptstyle{\mathpzc{S}(\Sigma_{t})}}_{{X}}}u
and
A_{t}(\upbeta u)=-\upbeta A_{t}u
;

if moreover each
\Sigma_{t}
is complete, then

(2)

\Lambda_{t}^{s}\upbeta=\upbeta\Lambda_{t}^{s}
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
where
\Lambda_{t}^{2}=\mathbbm{1}+({\nabla^{\scriptstyle{\mathpzc{S}(\Sigma_{t})}}})^{\ast}{\nabla^{\scriptstyle{\mathpzc{S}(\Sigma_{t})}}}

(3)

\big\langle B_{t,\pm}v\,\big|\,w\big\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathpzc{S}(\Sigma_{t}))}+\big\langle v\,\big|\,B_{t,\pm}w\big\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathpzc{S}(\Sigma_{t}))}=-nH_{t}\big\langle v\,\big|\,w\big\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathpzc{S}(\Sigma_{t}))}
for all
v,w\in C^{\infty}(\mathpzc{S}^{\pm}(\Sigma_{t}))
, sharing the same chirality.

Proof.

(1)

The first commutativity is a consequence of the compatibility with Clifford multiplication
(
3.9
)
and
\upbeta\mathbf{c}_{t}\left(X\right)=-\mathbf{c}_{t}\left(X\right)\upbeta
for all
t
, implying the anti-commuting with
A_{t}
.

(2)

Denote with
\mathcal{R}_{\Sigma_{t}}
the (Ricci) scalar curvature for
\Sigma_{t}
. The Lichnerowicz formula for the hypersurface Dirac operator and result (1) lead to

(4.2)

({\nabla^{\scriptstyle{\mathpzc{S}(\Sigma_{t})}}})^{\ast}{\nabla^{\scriptstyle{\mathpzc{S}(\Sigma_{t})}}}(\upbeta u)=\upbeta({\nabla^{\scriptstyle{\mathpzc{S}(\Sigma_{t})}}})^{\ast}{\nabla^{\scriptstyle{\mathpzc{S}(\Sigma_{t})}}}u

and thus
\Lambda_{t}^{2}(\upbeta u)=\upbeta\Lambda_{t}^{2}u
. This holds true for any positive even power
\Lambda_{t}^{2k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, after applying
(
4.2
)

k
times and thus for any polynomial in
\Lambda_{t}^{2}
. As
\Lambda_{t}^{2}
is essentially self-adjoint on
L^{2}(\mathpzc{S}^{\pm}(\Sigma_{t}))
with positive spectrum by completeness of the hypersurfaces, the function
f(x)=x^{s/2}
is continuous on the spectrum of
\Lambda_{t}^{2}
. Consequently,
\Lambda_{t}^{s}=f(\Lambda_{t}^{2})
is defined via the limit of any sequence of polynomials in
\Lambda_{t}^{2}
, converging uniformly on the spectrum to
f
.
\Lambda_{t}^{s}(\upbeta u)=\upbeta\Lambda_{t}^{s}u
then follows for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
because the action of
\upbeta
commutes with the limit and with each element of the sequence.

(3)

The density of
C^{\infty}_{\mathsf{c}}
in
L^{2}
allows to restrict the proof to smooth and compactly supported spinor fields. The left-hand side of the equation in the claim gives for both chiralities

\pm\left(\big\langle\mathbbm{i}A_{t}v\,\big|\,w\big\rangle_{\mathpzc{S}(\Sigma_{t})}+\big\langle v\,\big|\,\mathbbm{i}A_{t}w\big\rangle_{\mathpzc{S}(\Sigma_{t})}\right)-nH_{t}\big\langle v\,\big|\,w\big\rangle_{\mathpzc{S}(\Sigma_{t})}\quad.

A_{t}
is formally self-adjoint with respect to the induced inner product on
\Sigma_{t}
since
\mathfrak{d}\Sigma_{t}=\emptyset
for all
t\in\mathpzc{T}(M)
by assumption and by completeness of the hypersurface; the proof works similarly as the proof of
(
3.13
)
by using a synchronous Lorentz-orthonormal frame at a point on
\Sigma_{t}
. Thus,
\mathbbm{i}A_{t}
is formally skew-adjoint with respect to the same inner product and the term in the round brackets vanishes.

In order to extend this to a positive definite
L^{2}
-scalar product, we now use the essential self-adjointness of the Riemannian Dirac operator
A_{t}
on
L^{2}
-spaces since each hypersurface is complete:

(4.3)

\big\langle B_{t,\pm}v\,\big|\,w\big\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathpzc{S}(\Sigma_{t}))}+\big\langle v\,\big|\,B_{t,\pm}w\big\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathpzc{S}(\Sigma_{t}))}=-n\big\langle H_{t}v\,\big|\,w\big\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathpzc{S}(\Sigma_{t}))}\quad.

B_{t,\pm}
is then defined as in

3.30
)
with the extension of

instead. ∎

This can be reformulated for twisted Dirac operators.

Lemma 4.2

(1)

Before applying Grönwall’s inequality, we have to take a closer look on the constant

, the compact superset containing the support of the section, and on the derivatives of the metric

as Jacobi’s formula is applied for derivatives on the volume form prefactor in

. The hypersurface metrices and their derivatives depend smoothly on the time parameter

such that the constant depends also smoothly on

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{E}_{s}(u,\Sigma_{t})\leq c(\|\mathpzc{g}_{t}\|_{K,s})\mathcal{E}_{s}(u,\Sigma_{t})+\left\|D_{+}u\right\|_{H^{s}_{\mathsf{loc}}(\mathpzc{S}(\Sigma_{t}))}^{2}

The extreme value theorem on closed time intervals, applied on

where

For

\Sigma_{t}
, associated to

(4.11)

\begin{array}[]{lccc}\mathsf{res}_{t}\oplus D^{E}_{\pm}:&C^{\infty}(\mathpzc{S}^{\pm}_{E}(M))&\rightarrow&C^{\infty}(\mathpzc{S}^{\pm}_{E}(\Sigma_{t}))\oplus C^{\infty}(\mathpzc{S}^{\mp}_{E}(M))\\
&u&\mapsto&(u|_{\Sigma_{t}},D^{E}_{\pm}u)\quad.\end{array}

Theorem 4.9
.

For a fixed
t\in\mathpzc{T}(M)
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
the map
(
4.11
)
extends to

(4.12)

\mathsf{res}_{t}\oplus D^{E}_{\pm}\,\,:\,\,FE^{s}_{\mathsf{sc}}(M,\mathpzc{T},D^{E}_{\pm})\,\,\rightarrow\,\,H^{s}_{\mathsf{c}}(\mathpzc{S}^{\pm}_{E}(\Sigma_{t}))\oplus L^{2}_{\mathsf{loc},\mathsf{sc}}(\mathpzc{T}(M),H^{s}_{\mathsf{loc}}(\mathpzc{S}^{\mp}_{E}(\Sigma_{\bullet})))\,

which is an isomorphism of topological vector spaces.

This result has been proven in
[
11
, Thm 4.7/4.13]
for the untwisted Dirac operator.

Proof.

One first checks the continuity of the map
\mathsf{res}_{t}\oplus D^{E}_{\pm}
, induced by the continuity of both summands:
FE^{s}_{\mathsf{sc}}(M,\mathpzc{T},\mathpzc{S}^{\pm}_{E}(M))
is by definition the union of all continuous functions from
\mathpzc{T}(M)
to
H^{s}_{\mathsf{loc}}(\mathpzc{S}^{\pm}_{E}(\Sigma_{\bullet}))
with spatially compact support in
\mathcal{K}\subset M
. An intersection of
\mathcal{K}
with any Cauchy hypersurface in the foliation of
M
is a compact subset and since
H^{s}_{\mathsf{c}}(\mathpzc{S}^{\pm}_{E}(\Sigma_{\bullet}))
is also defined as union over all compact subsets in any slice, it is enough to consider the restriction onto a fixed hypersurface
\Sigma_{t}
as map between
C^{0}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathpzc{T}(M),H^{s}_{\mathsf{loc}}(\mathpzc{S}^{\pm}_{E}(\Sigma_{\bullet})))
and
H^{s}_{\mathcal{K}\cap\Sigma_{t}}(\mathpzc{S}^{\pm}_{E}(\Sigma_{t}))
. The continuity follows immediatly from the estimate

\left\|\mathsf{res}_{t}u\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathcal{K}\cap\Sigma_{t},\mathpzc{S}_{E}(\Sigma_{t}))}\leq\max_{\tau\in\mathpzc{T}(M)}\left\{\left\|u|_{\Sigma_{\tau}}\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathcal{K}\cap\Sigma_{\tau},\mathpzc{S}_{E}(\Sigma_{t}))}\right\}=\left\|u\right\|_{\mathpzc{T}(M),\mathcal{K},0,s}

with the norm
(
2.15
)
on
C^{0}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathpzc{T}(M),H^{s}_{\mathsf{loc}}(\mathpzc{S}^{\pm}_{E}(\Sigma_{\bullet})))
. The two inclusion mappings
C^{0}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathpzc{T}(M),H^{s}_{\mathsf{loc}}(\mathpzc{S}^{\pm}_{E}(\Sigma_{\bullet})))\hookrightarrow C^{0}_{\mathsf{sc}}(\mathpzc{T}(M),H^{s}_{\mathsf{loc}}(\mathpzc{S}^{\pm}_{E}(\Sigma_{\bullet})))
and
H^{s}_{\mathcal{K}\cap\Sigma_{\bullet}}(\mathpzc{S}^{\pm}_{E}(\Sigma_{\bullet}))\hookrightarrow H^{s}_{\mathsf{c}}(\mathpzc{S}^{\pm}_{E}(\Sigma_{\bullet}))
are continuous and the restriction map between
FE^{s}_{\mathsf{sc}}(M,\mathpzc{T},D^{E}_{\pm})
and
H^{s}_{\mathsf{c}}(\mathpzc{S}^{\pm}_{E}(\Sigma_{t}))
for a fixed
\Sigma_{t}
becomes continuous.
D^{E}_{\pm}
as maps from
FE^{s}_{\mathsf{sc}}(M,\mathpzc{T},D^{E}_{\pm})
to
L^{2}_{\mathsf{loc},\mathsf{sc}}(\mathpzc{T}(M),H^{s}_{\mathsf{loc}}(\mathpzc{S}^{\mp}_{E}(\Sigma_{\bullet})))
are continuous on

, implying
(
4.11

to be continuous on the considered domains and ranges.

It is left to show that
(
4.12
)
are bijective with continuous inverse. We take a

compact for any but fixed

and initial value

by finite propagation speed.

4.7
then implies for any subinterval

and

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{I,\mathcal{J}(K),0,s}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\tau\in I}\Bigl\{\mathcal{E}(u,\Sigma_{\tau})\Bigr\}\leq C\max_{\tau\in I}\left\{\left\|u|_{\Sigma_{t}}\right\|_{H^{s}_{\mathsf{loc}}(\mathpzc{S}_{E}(\Sigma_{t}))}^{2}+\left\|D^{E}_{\pm}u\right\|_{I,\mathcal{J}(K),s}^{2}\right\}

5.
Wave evolution operators

The well-posedness of the homogeneous Cauchy problem in
Corollary

4.10
motivates to define several
(Dirac-)wave evolution operators
. This section is dedicated to study several properties of these wave evolution operators and in particular their property of being a Fourier integral operator.

5.1.
General properties

For latter reasons we will first consider the wave-evolution operators for the untwisted Dirac operators.

Definition 5.1
.

For a globally hyperbolic manifold
M
and
t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathpzc{T}(M)
the (Dirac-) wave evolution operators for positive and negative chirality are the following isomorphisms of topological vector spaces

\displaystyle Q(t_{2},t_{1}):=\mathsf{res}_{t_{2}}\circ(\mathsf{res}_{t_{1}})^{-1}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle H^{s}_{\mathsf{c}}(\mathpzc{S}^{+}(\Sigma_{1}))\,\,\rightarrow\,\,H^{s}_{\mathsf{c}}(\mathpzc{S}^{+}(\Sigma_{2}))\,\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}(t_{2},t_{1}):=\mathsf{res}_{t_{2}}\circ(\mathsf{res}_{t_{1}})^{-1}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle H^{s}_{\mathsf{c}}(\mathpzc{S}^{-}(\Sigma_{1}))\,\,\rightarrow\,\,H^{s}_{\mathsf{c}}(\mathpzc{S}^{-}(\Sigma_{2}))\,\,.

The operator
Q(t_{2},t_{1})
occurs in
[
7
]
for compact hypersurfaces and in
[
8
]
for square-integrable sections on non-compact hypersurfaces. The wave evolution operators in our setting act between compactly supported Sobolev sections of any degree over non-compact, but complete hypersurfaces. The same properties of
Q
, as shown in
[
7
]
, are given for all introduced operators as well.

Lemma 5.2
.

The following properties hold for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
t,t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}\in\mathpzc{T}(M)
:

(1)

Q(t_{3},t_{2})\circ Q(t_{2},t_{1})=Q(t_{3},t_{1})
,

(2)

Q(t,t)=\mathbbm{1}_{H^{s}_{\mathsf{c}}(\mathpzc{S}^{+}(\Sigma_{t}))}
and
Q(t_{1},t_{2})=(Q(t_{2},t_{1}))^{-1}
,

(3)

\tilde{Q}(t_{3},t_{2})\circ\tilde{Q}(t_{2},t_{1})=\tilde{Q}(t_{3},t_{1})
,

(4)

\tilde{Q}(t,t)=\mathbbm{1}_{H^{s}_{\mathsf{c}}(\mathpzc{S}^{-}(\Sigma_{t}))}
and
\tilde{Q}(t_{1},t_{2})=(\tilde{Q}(t_{2},t_{1}))^{-1}
,

(5)

For any
[t_{1},t_{2}]\subset\mathpzc{T}(M)
the operators
Q(t_{2},t_{1})
and
\tilde{Q}(t_{2},t_{1})
are unitary for
s=0
.

Proof.

(1) to (4) follow by the same reasoning as in
[
7
]
. The well-posedness of the homogeneous Cauchy problem implies that any initial value
u_{\pm}\in H^{s}_{\mathsf{c}}(\mathpzc{S}^{\pm}(\Sigma_{t}))
for
t\in\mathpzc{T}(M)
is uniquely related to a finite energy spinor
\psi_{\pm}\in FE^{s}_{\mathsf{sc}}(M,\mathsf{ker}\left(D_{\pm}\right))
for both chiralities such that
\psi_{\pm}|_{\Sigma_{t}}=u_{\pm}
.
Lemma

4.4
ensures that one even has
\psi_{\pm}\in C^{1}_{\mathsf{sc}}(\mathpzc{S}^{\pm}(M))
for fixed
s>\frac{n}{2}+2
. Restricting to any compact time interval
[t_{1},t_{2}]
implies that
\psi_{\pm}\in C^{1}_{\mathsf{c}}(\mathpzc{S}^{\pm}(M))
and
Proposition

3.1
leads to the claim: for
\psi_{\pm}\in C^{1}_{\mathsf{c}}(\mathpzc{S}^{\pm}(M))\cap\mathsf{ker}\left(D_{\pm}\right))
we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M|_{[t_{2},t_{1}]}}\big(\mathpzc{D}\psi_{\pm}\,\big|\,\psi_{\pm}\big)_{\mathpzc{S}(M)}+\big(\psi_{\pm}\,\big|\,\mathpzc{D}\psi_{\pm}\big)_{\mathpzc{S}(M)}\,\mathrm{d}\mathsf{vol}_{\,}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\eqref{diracselfad}}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{2}}\big\langle\psi_{\pm}|_{\Sigma_{2}}\,\big|\,\psi_{\pm}|_{\Sigma_{2}}\big\rangle_{\mathpzc{S}(\Sigma_{2})}\,\mathrm{d}\mathsf{vol}_{\,\Sigma_{2}}-\int_{\Sigma_{1}}\big\langle\psi_{\pm}|_{\Sigma_{1}}\,\big|\,\psi_{\pm}|_{\Sigma_{1}}\big\rangle_{\mathpzc{S}(\Sigma_{1})}\,\mathrm{d}\mathsf{vol}_{\,\Sigma_{1}}\quad.

Using
\psi_{+}|_{\Sigma_{2}}=Q(t_{2},t_{1})\psi_{+}|_{\Sigma_{1}}=Q(t_{2},t_{1})u_{+}
and
\psi_{-}|_{\Sigma_{2}}=\tilde{Q}(t_{2},t_{1})u_{-}

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{2}}\big\langle Q(t_{2},t_{1})u_{+}\,\big|\,Q(t_{2},t_{1})u_{+}\big\rangle_{\mathpzc{S}(\Sigma_{2})}\,\mathrm{d}\mathsf{vol}_{\,\Sigma_{2}}-\int_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}}\big\langle u_{+}\,\big|\,u_{+}\big\rangle_{\mathpzc{S}(\Sigma_{1})}\,\mathrm{d}\mathsf{vol}_{\,\Sigma_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|Q(t_{2},t_{1})u_{+}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathpzc{S}^{+}(\Sigma_{2}))}^{2}-\left\|u_{+}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathpzc{S}^{+}(\Sigma_{1}))}^{2}

and

\left\|\tilde{Q}(t_{2},t_{1})u_{-}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathpzc{S}^{-}(\Sigma_{2}))}^{2}=\left\|u_{-}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathpzc{S}^{-}(\Sigma_{1}))}^{2}\quad.

We assumed w.l.o.g. that the compact supports of
\psi_{\pm}
intersect both boundary hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
; if otherwise, one gets the trivial identifications

.
Hence (5) is shown.

Remark 5.3
.

The map

is continuous for all
t\in\mathpzc{T}(M)

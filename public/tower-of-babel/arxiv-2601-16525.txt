Bosonization Solution to Spin-Valley Kondo Problem: Finite-Size Spectrum and Renormalization Group Analysis
Yi-Jie Wang, Geng-Dong Zhou, Hyunsung Jung, Seongyeon Youn, Seung-Sup B. Lee, Zhi-Da Song

Abstract
Spin-valley Anderson impurities (SVAIM) with (anti-)Hund’s splitting provide a natural explanation to the origin of pairing potential and pseudogap in the magic-angle graphene Wang et al. 2025a . In this work, we derive and analytically solve the low-energy Kondo theories for SVAIM at half-filling, with especial focus on the two anti-Hund’s regimes: the impurity is either dominated by a valley doublet, or a trivial singlet. In the doublet regime, we reveal that a novel pair Kondo scattering λ x \lambda_{x} is required to flip the valley doublet, which involves a quartic operator of bath electrons. Our renormalization group (RG) calculation based on the Coulomb gas analog shows λ x \lambda_{x} drives a phase transition of the Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless type. One side of the transition is an anisotropic doublet phase, characterized by non-universal phase shifts of bath electrons and non-analytic impurity susceptibilities, while the other is a Fermi liquid formed by pair-Kondo resonance. The finite-size many-body spectrum, thermodynamic quantities, and correlation functions for both phases are analytically solved. Remarkably, the solution in the pair-Kondo Fermi liquid is achieved via the constructive approach of bosonization–refermionization along a solvable fixed line, where the many-body interaction λ x \lambda_{x} is mapped into a pseudo-fermion bilinear in a rigorous manner. Finally, we also apply the RG analysis to the singlet regime, and identify a second-order phase transition between the Kondo Fermi liquid and a local singlet phase.

VIII
Conclusions and Discussions

In this work, we present a comprehensive analytical study on the SVAIM at half-filling using bosonization and refermionization techniques.
A central result is the identification of the pair-Kondo coupling
\lambda_{x}
in the doublet regime, which couples the doublet-flipping of the impurity to a pair-hopping process in the bath electrons.
Our bosonization RG calculations reveal a BKT phase transition between a weak-coupling fixed line (
\lambda_{x}=0
) that corresponds to the AD phase and a strong-coupling fixed line (
\rho_{z}^{\star}=\frac{1}{4}
) that corresponds to a FL of pair Kondo resonance.
Both fixed lines are solvable, allowing for the analytical calculation of the finite-size many-body spectrum and correlation functions.
Furthermore, in the singlet regime, we find a second order phase transition between the Kondo FL and the LS phases.

NRG verification of these quantum phase transitions is provided in the companion work
Wang et al. 2025a
.
While the analytical RG in this work is carried out under the assumption of PHS, numerical results demonstrate that both the BKT and second order phase transitions are stable against weak PHS breaking terms, e.g., deviation of chemical potential from the PHS point and particle-hole asymmetric hybridization functions.
Previous works
Affleck and Ludwig 1992
;
Affleck et al. 1995
found that the critical point in the two-impurity Kondo model, which corresponds to the second-order phase transition in the singlet regime, is unstable against the terms mixing the two effective baths if PHS is absent.
These terms arise inevitably in the two-impurity problem when integrating out the bath electron, because the two impurities do not couple to independent baths.
However, the valley-
{\mathrm{U}(1)}
symmetry of SVAIM forbids such terms and ensures the existence of a critical point even in the absence of PHS
Affleck et al. 1995
;
Fabrizio et al. 2003
;
Leo and Fabrizio 2004
;
Zaránd et al. 2006
. Moreover, recent cluster DMFT studies
Gleis 2024
;
Gleis et al. 2025
found that the second-order quantum phase transition in the periodic Anderson model, which is similar to our FL-LS transition and the two-impurity Kondo case, can be protected by the DMFT self-consistency even if the particle-hole and orbital (corresponding to valley here) symmetries are lifted.

Our results establish the SVAIM as a fundamental framework for describing quantum impurity physics in moiré systems possessing both spin and valley degrees of freedom.
As an SVAIM is defined only relying on the symmetries of spin-SU(2), valley-U(1), and valley-flipping, our results extend beyond MATBG/MATTG and apply generally to quantum impurities featuring both spin and valley-like degrees of freedom.
For moiré systems, such valley-like degrees of freedom may be realized by different layers, orbital angular momenta, besides the momentum valleys in graphene.

When applied to MATBG/MATTG with hetero-strain
Wang et al. 2025a
;
Youn et al. 2024
;
Youn et al.
, the SVAIM naturally accounts for the pseudo-gap phenomenon observed in recent experiments
Oh et al. 2021
;
Park et al. 2025
;
Kim et al. 2025
, and, simultaneously, provides a mechanism for pairing potential between quasi-particles.
While the model is minimal, it is possible to generalize it to further include other degrees of freedom such as sublattice and orbitals for a broader application in moiré systems, which we leave for future studies.

I(\tau)=-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\theta(\tau)\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}y~e^{-y\tau}((y-\varepsilon)^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}\left(e^{-\mathrm{i}\pi(\alpha-1)}-e^{\mathrm{i}\pi(\alpha-1)}\right)=\frac{\sin((1-\alpha)\pi)}{\pi}\frac{\theta(\tau)}{|\tau|^{\alpha}}\Gamma(\alpha)\ ,

(254)

with
\Gamma(\alpha)
being the
\Gamma
-function.
Therefore, the imaginary-time correlation function is reproduced as

\chi_{x}(\tau)=-\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\pi x_{c}^{\alpha}}{\sin((1-\alpha)\pi)\cdot\Gamma(\alpha)}\cdot\left(I(\tau)+I(-\tau)\right)\ .

(255)

Correspondingly, the Matsubara Green’s function is

\chi_{x}(\mathrm{i}\omega)=-\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\pi x_{c}^{\alpha}}{\sin((1-\alpha)\pi)\cdot\Gamma(\alpha)}\left((-\mathrm{i}\omega+0^{+})^{\alpha-1}+(\mathrm{i}\omega+0^{+})^{\alpha-1}\right)\ .

(256)

\chi_{x}(\mathrm{i}\omega)
is real and even in
\omega
, as required by the Lehmann spectral representation of bosonic Matsubara Green’s function.
The retarded Green’s function can be obtained by analytic continuation (
\mathrm{i}\omega\to\omega+\mathrm{i}0^{+}
) :

\displaystyle\chi^{R}_{x}(\omega)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\pi\cdot x_{c}^{\alpha}}{\sin((1-\alpha)\pi)\cdot\Gamma(\alpha)}\left((-\omega-\mathrm{i}0^{+})^{\alpha-1}+(\omega+\mathrm{i}0^{+})^{\alpha-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\pi\cdot x_{c}^{\alpha}}{\sin((1-\alpha)\pi)\cdot\Gamma(\alpha)}\cdot|\omega|^{\alpha-1}\cdot\left(1+e^{\mathrm{i}\pi(1-\alpha)\mathrm{sgn}(\omega)}\right)\ .

(257)

One should interpret
(-\omega-\mathrm{i}0^{+})^{\alpha-1}
and
(\omega+\mathrm{i}0^{+})^{\alpha-1}
as
f(z=-\mathrm{i}\omega+0^{+})
and
f(z=\mathrm{i}\omega-0^{+})
, respectively (
??
(b)).
Its imaginary part is

\mathrm{Im}[\chi^{R}_{x}(\omega)]=-\frac{x_{c}^{\alpha}|\omega|^{\alpha-1}\mathrm{sgn}(\omega)}{2\Gamma(\alpha)}\ .

(258)

It satisfies
\mathrm{Im}[\chi^{R}_{x}(\omega>0)]<0
and
\mathrm{Im}[\chi^{R}_{x}(\omega)]=-\mathrm{Im}[\chi^{R}_{x}(-\omega)]
, as required by the Lehmann spectral representation.

In a practical numerical calculation, high-energy peaks may appear in
\mathrm{Im}[\chi^{R}_{x}(\omega)]
, which, through the Kramers-Kronig relation, will lead to a smooth background to
\mathrm{Re}[\chi^{R}_{x}(\omega)]
for low-energy
\omega
.
As a result, while the low-energy behavior of
\mathrm{Im}[\chi^{R}_{x}(\omega)]
is universal, that of
\mathrm{Re}[\chi^{R}_{x}(\omega)]
is not.

Appendix D
Evaluating
\delta E^{\prime}[\Omega]

We now calculate
\delta E^{\prime}[\Omega]=\sum_{n=-N}^{0}\frac{2\pi}{L}\delta_{n}
, which is needed for the finite-size many-body spectrum at the
\rho_{z}=\frac{1}{4}
fixed line (
??
).
Introducing an energy cutoff
D
(bandwidth), we take
N\approx\frac{L}{2\pi}D
.
According to
??
,

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{L}\delta_{n}=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{L}\arctan\frac{\pi\Gamma}{\frac{2\pi}{L}(n-\frac{1}{2})-\varepsilon_{f}-0^{+}}-\frac{2\pi}{L}\cdot\theta\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\left[n-\frac{1}{2}\right]>\varepsilon_{f}\right)

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)^{2}\delta_{n}\cdot\left(\frac{\Gamma\cdot}{\big(\pi\Gamma\big)^{2}+\big(\frac{2\pi}{L}(n-\frac{1}{2})-\varepsilon_{f}\big)^{2}}+\delta\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\left[n-\frac{1}{2}\right]-\varepsilon_{f}\right)\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(L^{-3}\right)\ .

(259)

The first term in the second row is of the order
\mathcal{O}(L^{-2})
.
After summing over
N\sim L
terms, they contribute to an
\mathcal{O}(L^{-1})
term to
\delta E^{\prime}[\Omega]
.
However, the contribution to
\delta E^{\prime}[\Omega]
from the
\varepsilon_{f}
-dependent part of the these terms is of the order
\mathcal{O}(L^{-2})
.
Since our focus is on the
\varepsilon_{f}
-dependent
\mathcal{O}(L^{-1})
terms in
\delta E^{\prime}[\Omega]
, and constant
\mathcal{O}(L^{-1})
terms are irrelevant, we can neglect the second row of the above equation.
We can replace the first row in the above equation by the integral
\int_{n-1}^{n}\mathrm{d}x~f(x)+\mathcal{O}(f^{\prime\prime}(n-\frac{1}{2}))
, where

f(x)=\frac{2}{L}\arctan\frac{\pi\Gamma}{\frac{2\pi}{L}x-\varepsilon_{f}}-\frac{2\pi}{L}\theta\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}x-\varepsilon_{f}\right)\ .

(260)

The
\mathcal{O}(f^{\prime\prime}(n-\frac{1}{2}))
term is of the order
\mathcal{O}(L^{-3})
and eventually leads to an
\mathcal{O}(L^{-2})
term in
\delta E^{\prime}[\Omega]
.
We hence can safely omit the
\mathcal{O}(f^{\prime\prime}(n-\frac{1}{2}))
term.
It is worth mentioning that the integral expression also applies when
\tfrac{2\pi}{L}(n-\tfrac{1}{2})=\varepsilon_{f}
if
\varepsilon_{f}\in\tfrac{2\pi}{L}(\mathbb{Z}+\tfrac{1}{2})
. In this case, the integral reproduces
\tfrac{\pi}{L}
.
Therefore,
\delta E^{\prime}[\Omega]
is given by the integral

\displaystyle\delta E^{\prime}[\Omega]=

\displaystyle\theta(\varepsilon_{f}\leq 0)\cdot\varepsilon_{f}+\frac{2}{L}\int_{-D\frac{L}{2\pi}}^{0}\mathrm{d}x~\arctan\left(\frac{\pi\Gamma}{\frac{2\pi}{L}x-\varepsilon_{f}}\right)=\theta(\varepsilon_{f}\leq 0)\cdot\varepsilon_{f}+\Gamma\int_{\frac{-D-\varepsilon_{f}}{\pi\Gamma}}^{-\frac{\varepsilon_{f}}{\pi\Gamma}}\mathrm{d}\epsilon~\arctan\frac{1}{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta(\varepsilon_{f}\leq 0)\cdot\varepsilon_{f}+\Gamma\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2}\ln[\epsilon^{2}+1]+\epsilon\cdot\arctan\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)\Bigg|_{\frac{-D-\varepsilon_{f}}{\pi\Gamma}}^{-\frac{\varepsilon_{f}}{\pi\Gamma}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta(\varepsilon_{f}\leq 0)\cdot\varepsilon_{f}-\Gamma-\Gamma\ln\left(\frac{D}{\Gamma}\right)+\varepsilon_{f}\cdot\frac{\arctan\frac{\pi\Gamma}{\varepsilon_{f}}}{\pi}-\frac{\Gamma\varepsilon_{f}}{D}+\mathcal{O}(L^{-2})+\mathcal{O}(D^{-2})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Gamma\gg|\varepsilon_{f}|}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{f}-\Gamma-\Gamma\ln\left(\frac{D}{\Gamma}\right)-\frac{\Gamma\varepsilon_{f}}{D}+\mathcal{O}(L^{-2})+\mathcal{O}(D^{-2})\ .

(261)

It is a smooth function as
\varepsilon_{f}
crosses zero.
In summary, we have

\delta E[\Omega]\quad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle D\to\infty}}{{=}}\quad-\theta(\varepsilon_{f}\leq 0)\cdot\varepsilon_{f}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{f}-\Gamma-\Gamma\ln\left(\frac{D}{\Gamma}\right)+\cdots\ .

(262)

The omitted terms include
\mathcal{O}(L^{-1})
terms that are independent to
\varepsilon_{f}
,
\mathcal{O}(D^{-1})
terms, and
\mathcal{O}(L^{-2})
terms.

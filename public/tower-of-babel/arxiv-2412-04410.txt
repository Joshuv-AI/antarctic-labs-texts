SPIN DISTRIBUTIONS OF FISSION FRAGMENTS INVOLVING TRANSVERSE VIBRATIONAL MODES
D.E. Lyubashevsky, A.A. Pisklyukov, Yu. D. Shcherbina, T.Yu. Shashkina, P.V. Kostryukov

Abstract
A closed analytical description of fragment-spin distributions in low-energy induced and spontaneous fission is presented. The high fragment spins and the relative orbital angular momentum are assumed to arise from zero-point bending and wriggling oscillations of the pre-fragments near the scission point. The corresponding probability distributions yield the joint distribution of the fragment spins, the distribution of each spin, and the associated mean value. The fragment moments of inertia are calculated in a hydrodynamic model from non-equilibrium deformations at the scission point reconstructed from prompt-neutron multiplicities. For Th 232 ​ ( n , f ) \rm{}^{232}Th(n,f) , U 238 ​ ( n , f ) \rm{}^{238}U(n,f) , and Cf 252 ​ ( sf ) \rm{}^{252}Cf(sf) , the model reproduces the magnitude and sawtooth mass dependence of the measured mean spins. The zero-point approximation is applied only to the bending and wriggling oscillations and does not require dissipationless motion of the fissioning nucleus from saddle to scission.



I
INTRODUCTION

The spin distributions of primary fission fragments (PFF) determine
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
the initial conditions for subsequent neutron and
\gamma
-ray de-excitation and thereby affect particle multiplicities and angular distributions. Fragment-spin distributions also retain information about the dynamics immediately preceding the scission point. Experimentally inferred fragment spins are typically
5
–
7\hbar
, substantially larger than the spin of a low-energy fissioning nucleus. The predominantly transverse orientation of the fragment spins indicates that the additional angular momentum is generated by collective motion near the scission point, although the formation mechanism remains under discussion.

An explanation based only on the post-scission Coulomb interaction is insufficient
[
5
,
6
]
: the calculated changes in the fragment spins do not exceed about
2\hbar
. The main theoretical descriptions
[
2
,
5
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
therefore consider collective oscillations of the fissioning nucleus near scission. The collective degrees of freedom include
[
10
,
11
]
the longitudinal tilting and twisting oscillations and the doubly degenerate transverse bending and wriggling oscillations. The longitudinal and transverse labels refer to the directions of the associated spin components relative to the fission axis. Transport estimates give slow equilibration of twisting and tilting, rapid relaxation of wriggling, and an intermediate, mass-asymmetry-dependent relaxation time for bending
[
12
]
. This hierarchy supports retaining the transverse modes in the present low-energy treatment. Their degree of excitation is specified separately in Sec. II.B.

The bending and wriggling oscillations of the pre-fragments are shown schematically in
Fig.

1
. Bending oscillations correspond to pre-fragment rotations in opposite directions and produce oppositely directed spin contributions. Wriggling oscillations correspond to rotations in the same direction and produce a total spin
J_{\rm w}=J_{1_{\rm w}}+J_{2_{\rm w}}
, which is compensated by the relative orbital angular momentum
\mathcal{L}_{\rm w}=-J_{\rm w}
. Thus the total angular momentum is conserved in both cases. The oscillations are considered for a fixed shape at the scission point; the elongation, mass asymmetry, and neck parameters are not varied when the mode stiffness coefficients are determined. Earlier descriptions emphasized the bending oscillations
[
5
,
3
,
7
]
, whereas more recent calculations find comparable contributions from both types of transverse oscillations
[
13
,
14
,
12
]
.

; straight arrows indicate the corresponding spin contributions.

Recent measurements of secondary fragments from
\rm{}^{232}Th(n,f)
,
\rm{}^{238}U(n,f)
, and
\rm{}^{252}Cf(sf)
provide a common test for models of spin formation
[
15
]
. In the event-by-event Monte Carlo model
FREYA
, the angular momenta of the bending and wriggling oscillations are sampled from a finite-temperature distribution and then followed through neutron and
\gamma
-ray emission
[
16
,
13
,
14
,
12
]
. Time-dependent density functional theory describes the microscopic evolution and includes all three spatial components of the fragment spins
[
17
,
18
,
19
]
. Both approaches reproduce important features of the data but use different descriptions of the excitation and dynamics near scission.

In the present treatment, the zero-point wave functions of the bending and wriggling oscillations are formulated in the momentum representation. The corresponding probability distributions yield analytical expressions for the joint spin distribution and the distribution of each fragment spin. The fragment moments of inertia are evaluated from non-equilibrium deformations at the scission point reconstructed from prompt-neutron multiplicities rather than adjusted to the measured spins. The zero-point approximation concerns only these two transverse oscillations and neither implies zero excitation energy of the entire fissioning nucleus nor requires dissipationless motion from saddle to scission.

The model describes primary fragments at the scission point, whereas the available measurements refer to secondary fragments after prompt emission. Subject to this limitation, the calculated spin distributions and the predicted sawtooth mass dependence are compared with the data of Ref.
[
15
]
. Section II discusses the stages of induced and spontaneous fission and derives the spin distributions; Sec. III compares the results with experiment and other approaches; Sec. IV summarizes the conclusions.

II
CONSTRUCTION OF SPIN DISTRIBUTIONS OF PRIMARY FISSION FRAGMENTS

II.1
Stages of low-energy induced and spontaneous nuclear fission

In low-energy induced fission, neutron capture forms an excited compound nucleus with excitation energy close to the neutron binding energy. On a nuclear time scale, the compound nucleus develops into a resonance state containing quasiparticle excitations and collective deformation motion. For an axially symmetric nucleus, the resonance state can be characterized by the total angular momentum
J
, the laboratory projection
M
, and the projection
K
on the symmetry axis
[
20
,
21
]
. In the Wigner representation
[
22
,
23
,
24
]
, the corresponding wave function can be written schematically as

\Psi_{K}^{JM}=b_{0}\psi_{0K}^{JM}(\bm{\beta})+\sum_{i\neq 0}b_{i}\psi_{iK}^{JM}(\bm{\beta}),

where
\bm{\beta}
denotes the deformation parameters,
\psi_{0K}^{JM}(\bm{\beta})
describes the collective deformation state, and the remaining terms describe quasiparticle excitations. The transitional fission states at the barriers act as filters that select the values of
J
,
K
, and parity that contribute to the fission channel
[
20
]
.

The subsequent evolution of the selected collective state is illustrated by the double-humped deformation potential in
Fig.

2

[
25
]
. Region I contains the ground-state well, region II the second, isomeric well, and the outer barrier separates this well from region III, which leads toward the scission point. In induced fission, the energy supplied by neutron capture allows the collective component of the compound-nucleus state to pass through or above the barriers. Beyond the outer barrier, the nucleus continues to deform and gradually develops two pre-fragments.

In the present approach, the evolution beyond the outer barrier is assumed to remain predominantly collective
[
26
,
27
,
21
,
28
]
: most of the energy available to the selected fission channel is retained in the deformation degrees of freedom and stored in the non-equilibrium shape of the fissioning nucleus rather than being converted into intrinsic heat. The term “cold” fissioning system is used here in this restricted sense and does not imply vanishing excitation energy. Considerable collective deformation energy may coexist with a small intrinsic thermal excitation.

The angular asymmetries of different
P
and
T
parities observed in low-energy fission provide a related test of this assumption. Their quantum-mechanical description retains the projection
K
as a label of the transition state during the descent from the outer barrier, whereas appreciable intrinsic heating would enhance Coriolis mixing of the
K
components and erase this memory
[
21
,
29
,
30
]
. The measured structure of these asymmetries is therefore consistent with, although it does not by itself prove, the “cold” fissioning nucleus concept.

Figure 2:
Schematic double-humped deformation potential. Regions I and II denote the ground-state and isomeric wells; region III leads from the outer barrier toward the pre-scission configuration.

In spontaneous fission of an even-even nucleus, the initial state is the collective ground state with
J=M=K=0
. Region III is reached by tunnelling through the barriers, without the excitation energy supplied by neutron capture. Beyond the outer barrier, the energy released during the descent toward scission is likewise assumed to feed mainly the continued collective deformation and ultimately to be stored in the non-equilibrium shapes of the pre-fragments, rather than to produce strong intrinsic heating. Thus induced and spontaneous fission differ in the formation of the state that enters region III, but the “cold” fissioning nucleus concept has the same meaning in both cases. The fissioning nucleus approaches the scission point with two well-developed, non-equilibrium deformed pre-fragments and relatively small intrinsic thermal excitation. The bending and wriggling oscillations of the pre-fragments are considered below.

Recent scission-point calculations of the collective angular motion of two touching fragments provide an additional argument in favor of this energy partition
[
31
,
32
]
. The division of the available energy between non-equilibrium deformation and intrinsic thermal excitation is strongly dependent on the fragment pair. In the highly asymmetric
{}^{92}\mathrm{Kr}+{}^{160}\mathrm{Sm}
configuration formed in the spontaneous fission of
{}^{252}\mathrm{Cf}
, the light fragment remains close to its ground-state deformation, whereas the heavy fragment is strongly distorted and stores a much larger deformation energy
[
31
]
. Taken together with the small intrinsic excitation obtained for the scission configurations, this result indicates that the collective deformation energy may exceed the intrinsic thermal component by nearly two orders of magnitude. The calculated scission-point temperatures are also well below the energies of the first excited angular-vibrational states, so that the lowest collective state gives the dominant contribution
[
32
]
. This separation between a large deformation-energy reservoir and weak intrinsic heating is consistent with the “cold” fissioning nucleus concept adopted here.

II.2
Role of transverse bending and wriggling oscillations in the spin distributions of PFF

Near the scission point, the fragment-spin distributions are described in terms of the transverse bending and wriggling modes. After separation of the overall rotation, the harmonic Hamiltonian for each mode
t={\rm w},b
is
[
10
,
33
]

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=x,y}\left[\frac{\hat{J}_{t_{p}}^{\,2}}{2I_{t}}+\frac{\kappa_{t}q_{t_{p}}^{\,2}}{2}\right],

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{t_{p}}

\displaystyle=-i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{t_{p}}},

where
q_{t_{p}}
are dimensionless internal normal coordinates for the two equivalent transverse components. At a finite effective temperature
T
characterizing the population of these modes, the oscillator eigenstates form a thermal mixture. In the momentum representation, the corresponding probability density is

P\!\left(J_{t_{x}},J_{t_{y}}\right)=\frac{1}{\pi C_{t}(T)}\exp\!\left[-\frac{J_{t_{x}}^{2}+J_{t_{y}}^{2}}{C_{t}(T)}\right].

(1)

The temperature-dependent width is

\displaystyle C_{t}(T)

\displaystyle=I_{t}\hbar\omega_{0_{t}}\coth\!\left(\frac{\hbar\omega_{0_{t}}}{2T}\right)

(2)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\begin{cases}I_{t}\hbar\omega_{0_{t}},&T\ll\hbar\omega_{0_{t}},\\
2I_{t}T,&T\gg\hbar\omega_{0_{t}}.\end{cases}

Here
I_{t}
is the moment of inertia and
\omega_{0_{t}}
is the frequency of the corresponding oscillation.
For either transverse component, the lowest state of
\hat{H}_{t}
has

\langle\hat{J}_{t_{p}}\rangle_{0}=0,\qquad\langle\hat{J}_{t_{p}}^{\,2}\rangle_{0}=\frac{I_{t}\hbar\omega_{0_{t}}}{2}.

The two components therefore give
C_{t}=I_{t}\hbar\omega_{0_{t}}
. The numerical calculation assumes that the energy transferred into additional bending and wriggling excitations is small compared with the level spacing
\hbar\omega_{0_{t}}
, so that the zero-point limit of (
2
) is retained. This is the lowest state of the internal transverse motion at the chosen pre-scission configuration; the fissioning nucleus itself continues its deformation and evolution toward rupture.

The frequency is determined by

\omega_{0_{t}}=\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_{t}}{I_{t}}}.

(3)

The stiffness coefficient
\kappa_{t}
is defined in terms of either of the two equivalent dimensionless normal coordinates
q_{t}
of the bending or wriggling oscillation. The normalization is fixed by writing the associated kinetic energy as
I_{t}\dot{q}_{t}^{2}/2
. For a fixed configuration at the scission point,
\kappa_{t}
is the local curvature of the total energy
E_{t}(q_{t})
at the equilibrium configuration:

\kappa_{t}=\left.\frac{d^{2}E_{t}}{dq_{t}^{2}}\right|_{q_{t}=0}.

(4)

The derivative is evaluated while the elongation, mass asymmetry, and neck parameters are held fixed; only the selected bending or wriggling coordinate is varied
[
10
]
. A positive
\kappa_{t}
corresponds to a restoring force and hence to a stable small-amplitude oscillation. This curvature characterizes the fissioning nucleus before rupture and is distinct from the subsequent Coulomb interaction of two separated fragments
[
34
,
6
]
.

Direct calculation of the oscillation frequencies for each PFF pair is a non-trivial task. The bending-mode frequencies are taken from Ref.
[
7
]
, where
\omega_{0_{t}}
was calculated for the spontaneous fission of
\rm{}^{252}Cf
. Because broad experimental data were not available at that time, only 8 PFF pairs were considered, rather than the 31 pairs of
[
15
]
. Nevertheless, the selected pairs span the light-fragment range
[100,120]
and the heavy-fragment range
[132,152]
, which almost completely cover the region studied here. The approximately linear dependence of
\omega_{0_{t}}
on the fragment mass number
A_{f}
in these regions is used to interpolate and extrapolate the frequencies for PFF pairs not treated explicitly in Ref.
[
7
]
.

Analogous frequencies are not available for the other two systems,
\rm{}^{232}Th
and
\rm{}^{238}U
. For these systems,
\omega_{0_{t}}
of a given fragment pair is approximated by the value of the same pair in
\rm{}^{252}Cf(sf)
. Since the stiffness, compound-nucleus mass, and fission path differ, this transfer constitutes an explicit model approximation with an uncertainty not constrained by the present data.

The next step is to specify the collective moments of inertia of the wriggling and bending modes. The wriggling-mode moment of inertia
I_{\rm w}
is taken from Refs.
[

,

I_{\rm w}=\frac{I_{0}}{I}(I_{1}+I_{2}),

(5)

where
I_{i}
are the moments of inertia of the
i
-th PFF,
I_{0}=\mu R_{12}^{2}
is the orbital moment of inertia of the dividing system with reduced mass
\mu=\frac{M_{1}M_{2}}{M_{1}+M_{2}}
, and
I=I_{0}+I_{1}+I_{2}
is the total moment of inertia. The quantity
R_{12}=R_{1}+R_{2}+d
is the centre-to-centre distance of the two pre-fragments measured along the fission axis, where
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
are the fragment radii along that axis and
d=4
fm is the longitudinal gap between the fragment surfaces (the neck length), defined as in
[
12
]
and shown in
Figure

3
. The quantity
d
is longitudinal and collinear with
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
; it is not a transverse neck diameter.

The rigid-body moment of inertia of the
i
-th fragment is
I_{i\,,\text{rigid}}=\frac{M_{i}}{5}\sum{R_{i}^{2}}
, where
M_{i}
is the fragment mass and the radius
R_{i}
depends on the quadrupole deformation parameter
\beta_{2_{i}}
as

R_{i}(\beta_{2_{i}})=r_{0}{A^{1/3}}\left[1-\beta_{2_{i}}^{2}/4\pi+\beta_{2_{i}}\sqrt{5/4\pi}\right].

However, the fragment moments of inertia
I_{i}
are strongly reduced relative to the corresponding rigid-body values within the hydrodynamic model of the nucleus
[
20
,
35
]
. The deformations
\beta_{2_{i}}
and the corresponding moments of inertia, which enter both
I_{\rm w}
and
I_{b}
, are constructed and discussed in detail below and in
[
36
]
.

In the same references, an alternative bending-mode moment of inertia
I^{\prime}_{b}
differs significantly from the corresponding values in Refs.
[
10
,
7
,
37
]
and is given by

I^{\prime}_{b}=\frac{I_{1}I_{2}}{I_{1}+I_{2}}.

(6)

Equation (
6
) corresponds to the relative rotation of the two pre-fragments. In the convention of Ref.
[
37
]
, used for the numerical results below, bending is described by the rotation of the heavy pre-fragment relative to the remaining part of the fissioning nucleus. The corresponding reduced moment of inertia is

I_{b}=\frac{I_{0}I_{H}}{I_{0}+I_{H}},

(7)

where
I_{H}
is the heavy-fragment moment of inertia and
I_{0}
is the orbital moment of inertia of the complementary part. This definition of
I_{b}
must be used together with the corresponding stiffness and frequency. The definitions of
I^{\prime}_{b}
in (
6
) and
I_{b}
in (
7
) both presuppose two well-developed pre-fragments. The analysis is therefore restricted to the experimental mass range
[
15
]
, without extrapolation to extreme mass asymmetry.

and

for
\rm{}^{232}Th(n,\,f)
,

The deformation parameters
\beta_{2_{i}}
that enter these moments of inertia are large and, for near-magic fragments such as the Sn isotopes, exceed the equilibrium ground-state values. This distinction is important because the classic scission-point treatment of Ref.
[
38
]
, which includes deformed-shell effects, yields essentially spherical equilibrium shapes for nuclei around mass
130
owing to the
Z=50
and
N=82
shell closures. In contrast, the
\beta_{2_{i}}
values used here describe non-equilibrium pre-fragment deformations at the scission point. Part of the stored deformation energy is released after scission and contributes to neutron and
\gamma
-quanta de-excitation. Prompt-neutron yields constrain the estimate of this stored deformation energy. The procedure is summarized below; full details are given in
[
36
]
.

The excitation energy
TXE
available for prompt de-excitation of a fragment is related to the corresponding prompt-neutron multiplicity
\nu
by the systematics of Grudzevich
[
39
]
,

TXE=5+4\nu+\nu^{2},

(8)

which is consistent with the recent parameterization
U=7\left(\nu+\tfrac{3}{7}\right)
of Ref.
[
40
]
. For a given fragment, this energy can be written schematically as

TXE\simeq E_{\rm int}^{(\rm sc)}+E_{\rm def}^{(\rm rel)},

where
E_{\rm int}^{(\rm sc)}
is the intrinsic excitation at scission and
E_{\rm def}^{(\rm rel)}
is the energy released as the non-equilibrium shape relaxes toward the equilibrium shape. In the present first approximation,
E_{\rm int}^{(\rm sc)}
is neglected in the reconstruction of the deformation, and
TXE
is assigned to the released deformation energy. This estimate does not resolve additional microscopic intrinsic excitations or their production by dissipation. The deformation energy relative to the spherical shape is evaluated within the liquid-drop model
[
25
]
,

TXE_{\rm def}=\sigma A^{2/3}\left[\frac{2}{5}(1-x)\alpha^{2}-\frac{4}{105}(1-2x)\alpha^{3}\right],

(9)

with surface constant
\sigma=16
MeV, fissility parameter
x=Z^{2}/(45A)
and
\alpha=\tfrac{2}{3}\beta_{2}
. Subtraction of the equilibrium deformation energy obtained from the ground-state shapes of Ref.
[
41
]
, followed by solution of (
9
) for
\beta_{2}
, gives the non-equilibrium deformation of each fragment. Application of the experimental neutron yields
\nu(A)
of Walsh and Boldeman
[
42
]
indicates that even near-magic fragments around mass
130
, with spherical equilibrium shapes, acquire sizeable non-equilibrium deformations at the scission point. In particular, the measured multiplicity
\nu\simeq 0.36
for
{}^{130}\mathrm{Sn}
constrains the deformation energy assigned to this near-magic fragment. These neutron yields determine the input deformations independently of the fragment-spin data. The largest deformations,
\beta_{2}\gtrsim 0.9
for a few fragment pairs, lie at the edge of validity of the low-order expansion in (
9
) and should be regarded as indicative.

Given the deformation, the fragment moment of inertia is evaluated in the hydrodynamic model
[
20
,
35
]
rather than in the rigid-body limit. For an irrotational, quadrupole-deformed flow
[
35
]
,

I=\frac{9MR^{2}}{4\pi}\,\frac{\beta_{2}^{2}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{4}\sqrt{\tfrac{5}{4\pi}}\,\beta_{2}\right)^{2}}{2+\sqrt{\tfrac{5}{4\pi}}\,\beta_{2}+\tfrac{25}{18\pi}\beta_{2}^{2}},

(10)

which to leading order in
\beta_{2}
gives the ratio to the rigid-body value

\frac{I}{I_{\rm rigid}}\simeq\frac{45}{16\pi}\,\beta_{2}^{2}.

(11)

The essential feature is the strong quenching of the moment of inertia in the hydrodynamic model. The tabulated moments are calculated according to
[
36
,
43
]
, with Eqs. (
10
)–(
11
) providing the simplest representation: near-magic fragments give
I/I_{\rm rigid}\approx 0.1
–
0.3
, while more strongly deformed fragments away from shell closures reach
I/I_{\rm rigid}\approx 0.5
–
0.7
. The effective parameterization used in
FREYA
likewise reduces the inertia near magic numbers
[
12
]
; in contrast, the present values vary with the reconstructed non-equilibrium deformation. Consequently, the same fragment produced from different parent nuclei may have different scission inertias. A treatment of shell corrections beyond the liquid-drop estimate (
9
), in the spirit of
[
38
]
, would refine the calculated values.

The transverse fragment-spin projections are next expressed in terms of the bending- and wriggling-mode angular momenta
J_{t_{p}}
, where
p=x,y
. Using the transformation of Ref.
[
14
]
, the wriggling-mode contribution is weighted by the fragment moments of inertia:

J_{i_{\Large p}}=\frac{I_{i}}{I_{1}+I_{2}}J_{\text{w}_{\Large p}}+(-1)^{i+1}J_{b_{\Large p}}.

(12)

Equation (
12
) shows that the wriggling-mode angular-momentum projection
J_{{\rm w}_{p}}
is determined by the sum of the fragment-spin projections:

J_{\text{w}_{\Large p}}=J_{1_{\Large p}}+J_{2_{\Large p}}.

(13)

Similarly, the bending-mode angular-momentum projection
J_{b_{p}}
follows from Eqs. (
12
) and (
13
):

J_{b_{\Large p}}=J_{1_{\Large p}}-\frac{I_{1}}{I_{1}+I_{2}}J_{\text{w}_{\Large p}}=\frac{I_{2}J_{1_{\Large p}}-I_{1}J_{2_{\Large p}}}{I_{1}+I_{2}}.

(14)

For statistically independent bending and wriggling modes, the joint distribution of the mode angular momenta factorizes
[
33
]
:

P(J_{\text{w}_{\Large p}}J_{b_{\Large p}})=P(J_{\text{w}_{\Large p}})P(J_{b_{\Large p}}).

(15)

Application of the transformations in
Eqs.

12
,
13
and
14
converts the probability distribution
Eq.

15
into a distribution in the PFF spin projections:

\displaystyle P(J_{1_{\Large x}},J_{2_{\Large x}},J_{1_{\Large y}},

\displaystyle J_{2_{\Large y}})=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}C_{\rm w}C_{b}}\exp\left[-\left(\frac{J_{\text{w}_{\Large x}}^{2}+J_{\text{w}_{\Large y}}^{2}}{C_{\rm w}}+\frac{J_{b_{\Large x}}^{2}+J_{b_{\Large y}}^{2}}{C_{b}}\right)\right]\left|\frac{\partial(J_{\text{w}_{\Large x}},J_{b_{\Large x}},J_{\text{w}_{\Large y}},J_{b_{\Large y}})}{\partial(J_{1_{\Large x}},\,J_{2_{\Large x}},\,J_{1_{\Large y}},\,J_{2_{\Large y}})}\right|=

(16)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}C_{\rm w}C_{b}}\exp\left[-\left(\frac{(J_{1_{\Large x}}+J_{2_{\Large x}})^{2}+(J_{1_{\Large y}}+J_{2_{\Large y}})^{2}}{C_{\rm w}}+\frac{(I_{2}J_{1_{\Large x}}-I_{1}J_{2_{\Large x}})^{2}+(I_{2}J_{1_{\Large y}}-I_{1}J_{2_{\Large y}})^{2}}{C_{b}(I_{1}+I_{2})^{2}}\right)\right].

The distribution (
16
) is next transformed from Cartesian to polar variables. In the two-dimensional transverse-spin model, the polar angles
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
are fixed at
\pi/2
, while the azimuthal angles
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are replaced by the relative angle
\varphi=\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}
and the mean angle
\varphi^{\prime}=(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})/2
. Integration over the mean angle
\varphi^{\prime}
gives

\displaystyle P(J_{1},J_{2},\varphi)=\frac{2J_{1}J_{2}}{\pi C_{\rm w}C_{b}}\exp\Bigl[-J_{1}^{2}(\alpha I_{2}^{2}+C_{\rm w}^{-1})

(17)

\displaystyle-J_{2}^{2}(\alpha I_{1}^{2}+C_{\rm w}^{-1})+2J_{1}J_{2}\cos{\varphi}(\alpha I_{1}I_{2}-C_{\rm w}^{-1})\Bigr],

where
\alpha=C_{b}^{-1}(I_{1}+I_{2})^{-2}
.

Integrating
Eq.

17
over the magnitude of one of the fragment spins and over the relative azimuthal angle
\varphi\in[0,2\pi)
yields the normalized one-fragment distribution
P(J_{i})
for the spin magnitude of the remaining fragment,

P(J_{i})=\frac{2J_{i}}{\delta_{i}}\exp\left[-\frac{J_{i}^{2}}{\delta_{i}}\right],

(18)

where
\delta_{i}=I_{i}^{2}C_{\rm w}(I_{1}+I_{2})^{-2}+C_{b}
.

In the present approach, the PFF spins are generated by zero-point bending and wriggling oscillations. The calculated spin values are determined by the oscillation frequencies and the fragment moments of inertia. The evaluation of the moments of inertia therefore requires particular attention.

Reference
[
13
]
adopts an effective parameterization in which the moments of inertia are reduced near magic numbers and increase away from shell closures with increasing fragment deformation. The coefficients of the parameterization are calibrated to the measured spins of
[
15
]
, and the resulting prescription captures the correct qualitative trend.

In the present approach, the moments of inertia are evaluated in the hydrodynamic model from the reconstructed non-equilibrium deformations
\beta_{2_{i}}
(
Tables

1
,
2
and
3
), as described in Sec. II. The prompt-neutron multiplicities constrain these deformations, while the measured fragment spins are used for an independent comparison. The distinction from the effective parameterization concerns the procedure for determining the moments of inertia.

For comparison,
FREYA
samples the angular momenta
J_{t}
of the collective rotational modes from the statistical distribution
[
14
]

P(J_{t})\sim\exp{\left[-\frac{J_{t}^{2}}{2T_{S}I_{t}}\right]},

where
T_{S}
is the effective spin temperature controlling the population of the rotational modes. The spins of the light and heavy primary fragments,
J_{L}
and
J_{H}
, are then determined as

J_{L}=\frac{I_{L}}{I_{0}}J_{0}+\frac{I_{L}}{I_{\rm w}}J_{\rm w}+J_{b},\,J_{H}=\frac{I_{H}}{I_{0}}J_{0}+\frac{I_{H}}{I_{\rm w}}J_{\rm w}-J_{b},

where
I_{L}
and
I_{H}
are the moments of inertia of the light and heavy primary fragments. Because the parent-nucleus contribution is small (
\bar{J}_{0}=0.36\hbar
), the bending- and wriggling-mode angular momenta dominate the fragment spins. In
FREYA
, the statistical population of these angular momenta is governed by the effective spin temperature
T_{S}
, defined through
c_{S}^{2}TXE=a_{0}T_{S}^{2}
, where
c_{S}
is a model parameter and
a_{0}
is the level-density parameter
[
44
]

IV
CONCLUSION

Spin distributions of primary fission fragments have been calculated for the low-energy induced-fission reactions
\rm{}^{232}Th(n,f)
and
\rm{}^{238}U(n,f)
, as well as for the spontaneous fission of
\rm{}^{252}Cf(sf)
. The calculated mean spins for all three fissioning systems reproduce both the experimental scale of the fragment angular momenta and their characteristic sawtooth dependence on fragment mass. Spin distributions have also been predicted for the light fragments of
\rm{}^{238}U(n,f)
, for which corresponding experimental distributions are not yet available.

These results have been obtained by associating the fragment angular momenta with the zero-point transverse bending and wriggling oscillations of two pre-fragments in the connected pre-scission configuration. The bending and wriggling contributions were constructed in accordance with total-angular-momentum conservation. Only the zero-point contributions of these oscillations were retained, while the fragment moments of inertia were evaluated in the hydrodynamic model from non-equilibrium deformations reconstructed using prompt-neutron multiplicities. Consequently, the moments of inertia were not adjusted to the measured fragment spins. At the same time, several substantial approximations were required: the oscillation frequencies available for
\rm{}^{252}Cf(sf)
were transferred to the other fissioning systems, the stiffness coefficients were not calculated separately for every mass split, and the calculated primary-fragment quantities were compared with measurements referring to fragments after prompt emission.

The agreement obtained despite these approximations indicates that the principal mass dependence of the fragment spins may be governed by the non-equilibrium pre-fragment deformations and the corresponding moments of inertia. In particular, the reduction of the moments of inertia near shell closures and their increase for more strongly deformed fragments naturally produce the observed sawtooth structure. The zero-point bending and wriggling oscillations then establish the angular-momentum scale without requiring a substantial thermal population of these modes. This agreement does not establish the uniqueness of the proposed mechanism, but it shows that zero-point transverse oscillations of the configuration at the scission point can account for the main features of the measured spin distributions.

The remaining discrepancies, especially for fragments near the
Z=50
and
N=82
shell closures, may be associated with uncertainties in the reconstructed non-equilibrium deformations, limitations of the hydrodynamic moments of inertia, and the absence of prompt neutron and
\gamma
-quanta emission in the present calculation. Further development should therefore include direct calculations of the bending- and wriggling-mode stiffness coefficients
\kappa_{t}
for each mass split and each fissioning system, together with a more consistent evaluation of the fragment moments of inertia. Coupling the primary-fragment spin distributions to a prompt de-excitation calculation would permit a direct comparison with the measured secondary-fragment distributions and provide a more stringent test of the proposed mechanism.

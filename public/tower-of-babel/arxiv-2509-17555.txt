The randomly distorted Choquet integrals with respect to a 𝒢 \mathcal{G} -randomly distorted capacity and risk measures
Ohood Aldalbahi, Miryana Grigorova

Abstract
We study randomly distorted Choquet integrals with respect to a capacity c c on a measurable space ( Ω , ℱ ) (\Omega,\mathcal{F}) , where the capacity c c is distorted by a 𝒢 \mathcal{G} -measurable random distortion function (with 𝒢 \mathcal{G} a sub- σ \sigma -algebra of ℱ \mathcal{F} ). We establish some fundamental properties, including the comonotonic additivity of these integrals under suitable assumptions on the underlying capacity space. We provide a representation result for comonotonic additive conditional risk measures which are monotone with respect to the first-order stochastic dominance relation (with respect to the capacity c c ) in terms of these randomly distorted Choquet integrals. We also present the case where the random distortion functions are concave. In this case, the 𝒢 \mathcal{G} -randomly distorted Choquet integrals are characterised in terms of comonotonic additive conditional risk measures which are monotone with respect to the stop-loss stochastic dominance relation (with respect to the capacity c c ). We provide examples, extending some well-known risk measures in finance and insurance, such as the Value at Risk and the Average Value at Risk.

1
Introduction

Choquet capacities and Choquet integrals have been introduced by
Choquet 1954
as an extension of the notion of probability and the Lebesgue integral to the non-additive case, while preserving the monotonicity.
Capacities (in the modern terminology) often (but not always) preserve the monotone sequential continuity as well.
These tools have attracted considerable interest in various fields: in the probability and stochastic analysis community (cf., e.g.,
Dellacherie 1971
),
in fuzzy theory (cf., e.g.,
Denneberg 1994
,
Grabisch 1995
,
Grabisch et al. 2000
),
in finance (cf., e.g.,
Cherny and Madan 2009
,
Chateauneuf 1994
)
in actuarial science and in economics (cf., e.g.,
Gilboa 1987
,
Schmeidler 1989
,
Chateauneuf et al. 2000
,
Föllmer and Schied 2016
), in machine learning (cf., e.g.,
Fallah Tehrani et al. 2012
), etc.
A particular case of Choquet capacity, known as distorted probability, and the corresponding Choquet integral, known as distorted integral, hold a prominent place in decision theory as alternatives to the classical expected utility of Von Neumann and Morgenstern (cf., e.g.,
Yaari 1987
,
Quiggin 1982
).
At the interface of mathematical finance and actuarial mathematics (possibly, up to a minus sign, depending on the sign convention), the Choquet integrals with respect to a distorted probability are known as (static) distortion risk measures or distortion premium principles.
It is by now well-known that a number of risk measures used extensively by practitioners (and recommended by regulatory bodies) are of this form, such as the Value at Risk and the Average Value at Risk.
Distorted probabilities and distorted integrals have recently attracted renewed interest, especially in the conditional probability framework:
cf., e.g.,
Vega and Elliott 2021
(for convex conditional risk measures),

de Castro et al. 2023a
(for conditional quantiles),

Zang et al. 2024
(for random distortion risk measures),
and
de Castro et al. 2023b
(for further actuarial applications).

The field of stochastic orderings induced by or related to non-linear integrals has also been an active field of research in the recent years with various applications: cf., e.g.,
Grigorova 2014b
,
Grigorova 2014a
and
Grigorova 2013a
for stochastic orderings with respect to a capacity,

Ly and Privault 2022
and
Ly and Privault 2021
for stochastic orderings induced by non-linear
g
-evaluations and by non-linear (sub-additive)
G
-expectations,
Song and Yan 2006
and
Song and Yan 2009
for distortion risk measures (and suprema thereof) with respect to a probability and classical stochastic dominance relations.

In the present paper, we place ourselves in the capacity framework where
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},c)
is an underlying capacity space.
In economic, financial, and actuarial applications, the capacity
c
typically captures ambiguity on the model (where there might not even exist a reference probability or a reference set of probabilities).
We study monotone comonotonic additive risk operators
\rho
from
\chi(\mathcal{F})
to
\chi(\mathcal{G})
, where
\mathcal{G}
is a sub-
\sigma
-algebra of
\mathcal{F}
and
\chi(\mathcal{F})
(resp.
\chi(\mathcal{G})
) denotes the space of bounded
\mathcal{F}
-(resp.
\mathcal{G}
-)measurable functions.
Our operators are also monotone for the increasing or for the stop-loss stochastic ordering relations with respect to the capacity
c
.
We extend the (static) Choquet integrals with respect to a distorted capacity to the conditional setting, by ”randomising” the distortion function which now depends also on
\omega\in\Omega
.
Such random distortion functions (possible with slight variants in the definition) have appeared in the work
Cherny and Madan 2009
, and, more recently, in
Vega and Elliott 2021
and
Zang et al. 2024
for the case where
c
is a probability measure.
We study the properties of the
\mathcal{G}
-randomly distorted Choquet integrals as operators from
\chi(\mathcal{F})
to
\chi(\mathcal{G})
and we show that they are comonotonic additive.
In the case of a (general)
\mathcal{G}
-random distortion function
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}
, we characterise these integrals in terms of comonotonic additive conditional risk measures which are monotone with respect to the first-order stochastic dominance (with respect to the capacity
c
).
In the case where the random distortion function
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}
is moreover concave, we characterise them in terms of comonotonic additive conditional risk measures which are monotone with respect to the stop-loss stochastic dominance (with respect to the capacity
c
).
For related results in the case where
c
is a probability measure, we refer to
Vega and Elliott 2021
.
We provide examples, inspired from actuarial science and finance, which can be seen as
randomised
versions of well-known risk measures such as the Value at Risk (VaR) and the Average Value at Risk (AVaR) in the probabilistic or, more generally, in the capacity framework.

The remainder of the paper is organised as follows:
In Section
2
, we provide some preliminaries on the notion of capacity, stochastic orderings with respect to a capacity, and quantile functions with respect to a capacity, which are essential for the sequel.
In Section
3
, we introduce the Choquet integrals with respect to a
\mathcal{G}
-randomly distorted capacity and show their main properties, among which the comonotonic additivity, under suitable assumptions on the capacity space.
Section
4
is dedicated to our first representation result for comonotonic additive conditional risk measures which are monotone with respect to the first-order stochastic dominance (with respect to the capacity
c
).
Section
5
is devoted to the case where the random distortion
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}
is concave and to our second representation result for comonotonic additive conditional risk measures which are monotone with respect to the stop-loss stochastic ordering (with respect to the capacity
c
).
Section
6
contains the examples in the probabilistic framework and in the capacity framework. The Appendix
A
contains the proofs of some technical lemmas.

2
Preliminaries

Throughout this work,
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F}\right)
will be a given measurable space and
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
will be a sub-
\sigma
-algebra.
We denote by
\chi(\mathcal{F})
the set of all bounded, real-valued,
\mathcal{F}
-measurable functions on
\Omega
.
Similarly, we denote by
\chi(\mathcal{G})

the set of all bounded, real-valued,
\mathcal{G}
-measurable functions on
\Omega
. The supremum norm is defined by
||X||:=\sup_{\omega\in\Omega}|X(\omega)|
.
For
X
and
Y
in
\chi
, we write
X=Y
(resp.
X\leq Y
), when

X(\omega)=Y(\omega)
(resp.
X(\omega)\leq Y(\omega)
), for all
\omega\in\Omega
.

2.1
Conditional risk measure and comonotonic additivity

We start with a general definition of conditional risk measure, which takes as input an element of
\chi(\mathcal{F})
, interpreted as a random loss, and returns an element in
\chi(\mathcal{G})
.

Definition 1
(Conditional risk measure)

Any operator
\rho:\chi(\mathcal{F})\rightarrow\chi(\mathcal{G})
is called a
\mathcal{G}
-conditional risk measure or just conditional risk measure (if there is no confusion about the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{G}
to which we refer).

Let us recall the definition of comonotonic measurable functions, especially popular in the risk measurement literature.

Definition 2

Let
X
and
Y
be two real-valued measurable functions on
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F}\right)
. We say that
X
and
Y
are comonotonic if for any pair
(\omega,\bar{\omega})\in\Omega\times\Omega
,

\left(X(\omega)-X(\bar{\omega})\right)\left(Y(\omega)-Y(\bar{\omega})\right)\geq 0
.

Definition 3
(Comonotonic additivity)

Let
\rho:\chi(\mathcal{F})\rightarrow\chi(\mathcal{G})
. We say that
\rho
is comonotonic additive if
\rho(X+Y)(\omega)=\rho(X)(\omega)+\rho(Y)(\omega)
, for all
\omega\in\Omega
,
for all
X\in\chi(\mathcal{F})
and
Y\in\chi(\mathcal{F})
which are comonotonic.

Remark 1

If
\rho
is comonotonic additive, Lipschitz continuous (with respect to the supremum norm
||\cdot||
) and normalised (that is,
\rho(1)=1
), then
\rho
satisfies the positive homogeneity property (that is, for any
a\geq 0
, for any
X\in\chi(\mathcal{F})
, for any
\omega\in\Omega
,
\rho(aX)(\omega)=a\rho(X)(\omega)
). Indeed, let
X\in\chi(\mathcal{F})
. We can show by induction that, due to the comonotonic additivity of
\rho
, for any integer
n\geq 1
,

\rho(nX)(\omega)=n\rho(X)(\omega)
.
Let
\beta
be in
\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,\infty)
, then
\beta
can be written as
\beta=\frac{n}{m}
, where
n
and
m
are positive integers. By using the property for positive integers, we have:

n\rho(X)(\omega)=\rho(nX)(\omega)=\rho(m\frac{n}{m}X)(\omega)=m\rho(\frac{n}{m}X)(\omega)
,
which gives
\rho(\frac{n}{m}X)(\omega)=\frac{n}{m}\rho(X)(\omega)
.
For any
a\geq 0
, there exists a sequence of rational numbers
\beta_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,\infty)
such that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\beta_{n}=a
.
We have:
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\beta_{n}X(\omega)=aX(\omega)
for all
\omega\in\Omega
, and the convergence is also in sup-norm as
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}||(\beta_{n}-a)X||=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}|\beta_{n}-a|\,||X||=0
.
As
\rho
is Lipschitz continuous, we have:

\rho(aX)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rho(\beta_{n}X)
in sup-norm,
which implies the pointwise convergence

\rho(aX)(\omega)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rho(\beta_{n}X)(\omega)
.
Hence,

\rho(aX)(\omega)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rho(\beta_{n}X)(\omega)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\beta_{n}\rho(X)(\omega)=a\rho(X)(\omega),\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\omega\in\Omega.

2.2
Capacities, quantile functions with respect to capacities, and stochastic orderings

In this subsection, we recall some definitions and properties of capacities, which can be found in
Denneberg 1994
and/or
Föllmer and Schied 2016
.
Moreover, we recall some results related to the first-order stochastic dominance with respect to a capacity, which is an extension of the classical first-order stochastic dominance (with respect to a probability measure). For more results on stochastic orderings with respect to a capacity, we refer to

Grigorova 2014a
and
Grigorova 2014b
.

Definition 4

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
be a measurable space.
A set function
c:\mathcal{F}\rightarrow[0,1]
is called
a capacity
if it satisfies
c\left(\emptyset\right)=0
(groundedness),
c\left(\Omega\right)=1
(normalisation) and the
monotonicity property: if
A,B\in\mathcal{F}
are such that
A\subset B
, then
c(A)\leq c(B)
.

Definition 5

A capacity
c
is called
continuous from below
if for any sequence of events

(A_{n})\subset\mathcal{F}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
A_{n}\subset A_{n+1}
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}c(A_{n})=c(\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n})
.

Remark 2

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a non-empty set of probability measures.
Then,
c(\cdot):=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}P(\cdot)
is a capacity which is continuous from below. Note, however, that the non-linear functional
\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{P}(\cdot)
is in general different from the Choquet integral
\mathbb{E}_{c}(\cdot)
.

We recall the notion of (non-decreasing) distribution function with respect to a capacity (cf., e.g., Definition 4.85 in
Föllmer and Schied 2016
).

Definition 6

Let
c
be a given capacity.
For a real-valued measurable function
X
,
the distribution function

G_{X}
of
X
with respect to
c
is defined by

G_{X,c}\left(x\right)=G_{X}\left(x\right):=1-c\left(X>x\right)
, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

We extend the above definition by setting
G_{X}(+\infty):=1
and
G_{X}(-\infty):=0
.

The following assumption is imposed in some results in the sequel.

Assumption 1

Let
c
be a capacity.
We assume that there exists a real-valued measurable function
Z
with distribution function with respect to the capacity
c
,
G_{Z}
, which is continuous and satisfies:

\lim_{x\rightarrow-\infty}G_{Z}(x)=0\,\,\,\,and\,\,\,\,\lim_{x\rightarrow+\infty}G_{Z}(x)=1.

The following lemma can be found in
Grigorova 2014a
.
It is well-known in the case where
c
is a probability measure as a way of constructing a random variable with a uniform distribution on
[0,1]
.

Lemma 1

We assume Assumption
1
.
Set
U:=G_{Z}(Z)
.
Then, the distribution function
G_{U}
of
U
is:

G_{U}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}0,&\text{if}\,\,x<0,\\
x,&\text{if}\,\,x\in[0,1],\\
1,&\text{if}\,\,x>1.\end{array}\right.

Remark 3

Let us consider the particular case where
c=P
is a probability measure. In this case, Assumption
1
amounts to
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
being atomless (which can be seen as a standard assumption in the risk measurement literature), and which is equivalent to
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
carrying a uniform random variable on the interval
[0,1]
.

For any
X\in\chi(\mathcal{F})
, the function
G_{X}
is non-decreasing (as
c
is monotone); it is thus possible to define its generalised inverse (cf., e.g., the Appendix of
Föllmer and Schied 2016
).

Definition 7

Let
c
be a capacity.
For a real-valued measurable function
X
,
every function
r_{X}:\left(0,1\right)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\sup\{x\in\mathbb{R}:G_{X}(x)<t\}\leq r_{X}\left(t\right)\leq\sup\{x\in\mathbb{R}:G_{X}(x)\leq t\},\,\,\,\,for\,\,all\,\,t\in\left(0,1\right)

is called
a quantile function
of
X
with respect to
c
, where the convention
\sup\{\emptyset\}=-\infty
is used.

The functions
r_{X}^{-}
and
r_{X}^{+}
defined by

r_{X}^{-}(t):=\sup\{x\in\mathbb{R}:G_{X}(x)<t\}\,\,\text{for all}\,\,t\in(0,1),

r_{X}^{+}(t):=\sup\{x\in\mathbb{R}:G_{X}(x)\leq t\}\,\,\text{for all}\,\,t\in(0,1)

are called
the lower
and
upper quantile functions
of
X
with respect to
c
.

We extend the definition to
t=0
and
t=1
, by setting

r_{X}^{+}(1):=r_{X}^{-}(1):=\lim_{t\uparrow 1}r_{X}^{-}(t)=\sup_{t<1}r_{X}^{-}(t)

and

r_{X}^{-}(0):=r_{X}^{+}(0):=\lim_{t\downarrow 0}r_{X}^{+}(t)=\inf_{t>0}r_{X}^{+}(t)
.

For notational convenience, we omit the dependence on
c
in the notation
G_{X}
and

r_{X}
when there is no possible confusion about the capacity.
The following remark (cf.
Föllmer and Schied 2016
) gives us another useful way of writing the lower and upper quantile functions (with respect to a capacity).

Remark 4

The lower and upper quantile functions of
X
with respect to
c
can be written: for all
t\in(0,1)
,

r_{X}^{-}(t)=\inf\{x\in\mathbb{R}:G_{X}(x)\geq t\},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,r_{X}^{+}(t)=\inf\{x\in\mathbb{R}:G_{X}(x)>t\}.

The following observation on the distribution function (with respect to a capacity) of a bounded measurable function
X
can be found in
Grigorova 2014a
(cf. Remark 2.5).

Remark 5

Let
c
be a capacity. For
X\in\chi(\mathcal{F})
, we have:

\lim_{x\rightarrow-\infty}G_{X}(x)=0
and
\lim_{x\rightarrow+\infty}G_{X}(x)=1
.
We denote by
G_{X}(x-)
and
G_{X}(x+)
the left-hand and right-hand limits of
G_{X}
at
x
(these limits exist as
G_{X}
is non-decreasing).
A function
r_{X}
is a quantile function of
X
(with respect to
c
) if and only if
G_{X}(r_{X}(t)-)\leq t\leq G_{X}(r_{X}(t)+),\,\,\text{for all}\,\,t\in(0,1).

To fix the notation, we recall that the (classical) Choquet
integral of
X\in\chi(\mathcal{F})
with respect to a capacity
c

is defined by:

\mathbb{E}_{c}(X)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}c(X>x)dx+\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left[c(X>x)-1\right]dx.

We also recall the definition of the first-order stochastic dominance with respect to a capacity (cf.
Grigorova 2013b
for more results on this ordering relation), generalizing the classic first-order stochastic dominance (with respect to a probability).

Definition 8

Let
X
and
Y
be in
\chi(\mathcal{F})
and let
c
be a capacity. We say that
X
is smaller than
Y
in the first-order stochastic dominance (or in the first-order stochastic ordering) with respect to the capacity
c
, denoted by
X\preceq_{st,c}Y
, if:

\mathbb{E}_{c}\left(u\left(X\right)\right)\leq\mathbb{E}_{c}\left(u\left(Y\right)\right)

(1)

for all deterministic functions
u:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
which are non-decreasing.

From an economic point of view,
X
is smaller than
Y
in the first-order stochastic dominance with respect to the capacity
c
, if all Choquet expected utility (CEU) maximizers (having a non-decreasing utility function) prefer having
Y
to
X
.

The following lemma gives some useful tools when working with the first-order stochastic dominance with respect to the capacity
c
.

Lemma 2

Let
X
and
Y
be in
\chi(\mathcal{F})
.
Let
c
be a capacity.
If
X\preceq_{st,c}Y
, then all of the following statements hold true:

(i)

G_{X}(x)\geq G_{Y}(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

(ii)

r^{+}_{X}(t)\leq r^{+}_{Y}(t)
for all
t\in[0,1]
.

(iii)

r^{-}_{X}(t)\leq r^{-}_{Y}(t)
for all
t\in[0,1]
.

Proof

For the proof of the first two statements, we refer to the proof of Proposition 3.3.1 in
Grigorova 2013b
.
The proof of the third statement is similar. We provide a full proof of the lemma in the appendix for the convenience of the reader.∎

In the following, we define the ”distribution invariance” property of
\rho
(with respect to
c
).
It is used
in
Grigorova 2014a
(cf. Remark 3.5) for the case where
\rho
is a static risk measure on a capacity space.

Definition 9
(”Distribution invariance”)

Let
c
be a capacity.
Let
\rho:\chi(\mathcal{F})\rightarrow\chi(\mathcal{G})
.
We say that
\rho
is ”distribution invariant” (with respect to
c
) if:

G_{X}(x)=G_{Y}(x)
, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, implies
\rho(X)(\omega)=\rho(Y)(\omega),
for all
\omega\in\Omega
.

In other words,
\rho
is ”distribution invariant” (with respect to
c
) if any two random losses
X
and
Y
with the same distribution function (with respect to
c
) have also the same risk (
\rho(X)(\omega)=\rho(Y)(\omega),
for all
\omega\in\Omega
).

3
The randomly distorted Choquet integral with respect to a
\mathcal{G}
-randomly distorted capacity

In this section, we consider
\mathcal{G}
-randomly distorted Choquet integrals with respect to a
\mathcal{G}
-randomly distorted capacity
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c
, where
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}
is a
\mathcal{G}
-measurable random distortion function (which we call also
\mathcal{G}
-random distortion function).
The following is the precise definition of a
\mathcal{G}
-random distortion function (cf. also
Vega and Elliott 2021
and
de Castro et al. 2023b
).

Definition 10

We say that a mapping
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}:\Omega\times[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
is a
\mathcal{G}
-random distortion function (or random distortion function, if no possible confusion on the
\sigma
-algebra) if:

1)

For any
\omega\in\Omega
, the function
t\in[0,1]\mapsto\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,t)
is non-decreasing and normalised, that is,
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,0)=0
and
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,1)=1
.

2)

For any
t\in[0,1]
,
\omega\mapsto\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,t)
is
\mathcal{G}
-measurable.

When
\mathcal{G}=\{\Omega,\emptyset\}
, then
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}=\phi
is deterministic (does not depend on
\omega
).
In this case, we recover the usual definition of a (deterministic) distortion function, popular in decision theory and in risk measures.

We define the randomly distorted Choquet integral (with respect to a
\mathcal{G}
-random distortion function
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}
and a capacity
c
) as follows:

Definition 11

Let
c
be a capacity on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
.
Let
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}
be a
\mathcal{G}
-random distortion function.
The
\mathcal{G}
-randomly distorted Choquet

integral
of a measurable function
X\in\chi(\mathcal{F})

is defined by: for all
\omega\in\Omega
,

\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)(\omega)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,c(X>x))dx+\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left[\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,c(X>x))-1\right]dx.

Since

\phi^{\mathcal{G}}
is a
\mathcal{G}
-random distortion,

\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)
is
\mathcal{G}
-measurable.
Let us note also that when
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}=\phi
is deterministic and
c
is a probability measure,
we recover the usual Choquet integral with respect to a distorted probability.
When
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}=\phi=id
is the deterministic identity function and
c
is a capacity, we recover the usual Choquet integral with respect to the capacity
c
.

Proposition 1

The randomly distorted Choquet integral
\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

(”Distribution invariance”) If
X,Y\in\chi(\mathcal{F})
are such that
G_{X}(x)=G_{Y}(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, then
\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(Y)
.

(ii)

(Monotonicity) If
X,Y\in\chi(\mathcal{F})
are such that
X\leq Y
, then
\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)\leq\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(Y)
.

(iii)

(Translation invariance) For
X\in\chi(\mathcal{F})
, for
a\in\mathbb{R}
, we have:
\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(a+X)=a+\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)
.

Proof

(i)

It follows from Definition
11
and from the definition of
G_{X}
and
G_{Y}
.

(ii)

Let
X,Y
be in
\chi(\mathcal{F})
such that
X\leq Y
. By the monotonicity of
c
, for any given
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we have:

c(X>x)\leq c(Y>x)
.
Therefore,
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\left(\omega,c(X>x)\right)\leq\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\left(\omega,c(Y>x)\right)
, for each
\omega\in\Omega
(by using the monotonicity of
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}
).
Hence, we obtain the desired result.

(iii)

Let
a\in\mathbb{R}
. By Definition
11
, we have: for all
\omega\in\Omega
,

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X+a)(\omega)\\
&=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\left(\omega,c(X>x-a)\right)dx+\int_{-\infty}^{0}[\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\left(\omega,c(X>x-a)\right)-1]dx\\
&=\int_{-a}^{+\infty}\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\left(\omega,c(X>t)\right)dt+\int_{-\infty}^{-a}[\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\left(\omega,c(X>t)\right)-1]dt\\
&=a+\int_{0}^{+\infty}\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\left(\omega,c(X>t)\right)dt+\int_{-\infty}^{0}[\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\left(\omega,c(X>t)\right)-1]dt\\
&=a+\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)(\omega),\end{split}

where we have used a change of variables.∎

The translation invariance is sometimes referred to as cash invariance in the risk measurement literature.
Our next important goal is to establish the comonotonic additivity of
\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(\cdot)
.

Theorem 3.1
(Comonotonic additivity)

Let
c
be a continuous from below capacity.
We assume Assumption
1
.
Let
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}
be a
\mathcal{G}
-random distortion function.
If
X
and
Y
in
\chi(\mathcal{F})
are comonotonic,
then:

\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X+Y)=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)+\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(Y).

(2)

We present some results which will be used in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, and which are interesting on their own account.

Let
X
be a non-negative step function defined by:

X=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}},

(3)

where the
x_{i}
’s are non-negative real numbers, and where the
A_{i}
’s are disjoint and form a measurable partition of
\Omega
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that the numbers
x_{i}
are ranged in descending order (i.e.
x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq\cdots\geq x_{n}\geq 0
).

Lemma 3
(The
\mathcal{G}
-randomly distorted Choquet integral formula for a non-negative step function)

Let
c
be a capacity on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
.
For a non-negative step function
X=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}
of the form defined in (
3
),
we have: for all
\omega\in\Omega
,

\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}\right)(\omega)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}-x_{i+1})\,\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,c(\cup_{k=1}^{i}A_{k})),\,\,\text{where}\,\,x_{n+1}:=0.

(4)

Proof

We have: for all
\omega\in\Omega
,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)(\omega)&=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,c(X>z))dz=\int_{0}^{x_{1}}\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,c(X>z))dz\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{x_{i+1}}^{x_{i}}\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,c(X>z))dz=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{x_{i+1}}^{x_{i}}\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,c(\cup_{k=1}^{i}A_{k}))dz\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,c(\cup_{k=1}^{i}A_{k}))\int_{x_{i+1}}^{x_{i}}dz=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}-x_{i+1})\,\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,c(\cup_{k=1}^{i}A_{k})),\end{split}

where we have used that
\{X>z\}=\cup_{k=1}^{i}A_{k}
, for each
z\in[x_{i+1},x_{i})
(since
x_{k}>z
for
k\leq i
).∎

For the case where
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}
is deterministic, the above formula (
4
) can be found, for instance, in
Schmeidler 1986
.

In the following lemma, we show that the
\mathcal{G}
-randomly distorted Choquet integral is Lipschitz continuous on
\chi(\mathcal{F})
endowed with the sup-norm
||\cdot||
.

Lemma 4
(Lipschitz continuity of
\mathcal{G}
-randomly distorted Choquet integrals)

Let
c
be a capacity.
The
\mathcal{G}
-randomly distorted Choquet integral
is Lipschitz continuous (with respect to the supremum norm
||\cdot||
);
more precisely,
for all
X,Y
in
\chi(\mathcal{F})
, we have:

||\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)-\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(Y)||\leq||X-Y||.

Proof

The proof of the lemma uses the same arguments as Lemma 4.3 in
Föllmer and Schied 2016
.
Let
X,Y\in\chi(\mathcal{F})
. Then,
X\leq Y+||X-Y||
and
Y\leq X+||X-Y||
.
Therefore, by the monotonicity and the cash invariance of
\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}
we have: for all
\omega\in\Omega
,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)(\omega)\leq\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(Y+||X-Y||)(\omega)=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(Y)(\omega)+||X-Y||\\
\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(Y)(\omega)\leq\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X+||X-Y||)(\omega)=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)(\omega)+||X-Y||.\end{split}

Hence, we obtain:
|\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)(\omega)-\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(Y)(\omega)|\leq||X-Y||
for all
\omega\in\Omega
.
Hence,
||\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)-\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(Y)||\leq||X-Y||
.∎

Remark 6

In fact, this result holds true for any
\rho:\chi(\mathcal{F})\rightarrow\chi(\mathcal{G})
such that
\rho
is monotone and cash invariant. This result is well-known in the (conditional) risk measurement literature (cf., e.g.,
Föllmer and Schied 2016
).

Remark 7

As a consequence of Lemma
4
,
\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}
is a continuous (non-linear) operator in sup-norm.
If
||X_{n}-X||\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
,
then
||\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X_{n})-\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)||\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

The following lemma provides a useful characterisation of the comonotonicity of two non-negative step functions (cf.
Schmeidler 1986
). We provide in the Appendix a proof of this lemma for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 5

and

in

, a partition of

into

.

Proposition 2
(Approximation of non-negative comonotonic pair in

)

Let
c
be a continuous from below capacity.
We assume Assumption
1
.
Let
U:=G_{Z}(Z)
(as in Lemma

).
Let
X,Y
be two non-negative

.
Then, we have the following:

•

and

(respectively

.

•

Moreover,
X+Y
and
r^{-}_{X}(U)+r^{-}_{Y}(U)
have the same distribution function with respect to

.

•

There exist two sequences of non-negative step functions
(X_{n})
and
(Y_{n})
such that

and

, and

as

.

•

Moreover, for all

,

\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X_{n})(\omega)\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{}\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)(\omega)
,

\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(Y_{n})(\omega)\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{}\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(Y)(\omega)

.

In order to prove the above proposition, we make the following observations
(for the case where
c
is a probability measure, they can be found, for instance, in
Embrechts and Hofert 2013
or
Föllmer and Schied 2016
).

Remark 8

Let
c
be a capacity.
If
c
is
continuous from below
, then
G_{X}
is right-continuous and
r_{X}^{-}
is left-continuous.
Indeed, let
(x_{n})
and
x
be such that
x_{n}\downarrow x
.
We set
A_{n}:=\{X>x_{n}\}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
A:=\{X>x\}
.
Then,
A_{n}\subset A_{n+1}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Moreover,
\cup_{n}A_{n}=A
.
Hence, as
c
is continuous from below, we get
c(A)=c(\cup_{n}A_{n})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}c(A_{n})
.
Therefore, we obtain:
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}G_{X}(x_{n})=G_{X}(x)
.

Remark 9

Let
c
be a capacity.

a)

For any
X\in\chi(\mathcal{F})
,

G_{X}(x)\geq t
implies
r^{-}_{X}(t)\leq x
.
Indeed, by Remark
4
, we have:
r_{X}^{-}(t)=\inf\{x\in\mathbb{R}:G_{X}(x)\geq t\}
for
t\in(0,1)
.
Hence, if
x\in\mathbb{R}
is such that
G_{X}(x)\geq t
, then
r^{-}_{X}(t)\leq x
.

b)

Moreover, if
c
is continuous from below, the other implication also holds true; more precisely,
r^{-}_{X}(t)\leq x
implies
G_{X}(x)\geq t
.
Indeed, as
c
is continuous from below,
G_{X}
is right-continuous (cf. Remark
8
).
As a consequence, we can show that
G_{X}(r^{-}_{X}(t))\geq t
. Indeed, let
A:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:G_{X}(x)\geq t\}
. Then, by Remark
5
, there exists a sequence
(x_{n})\subseteq A
such that
x_{n}\downarrow\inf A=r^{-}_{X}(t)
and
G_{X}(x_{n})\geq t
.
Hence, by the the right-continuity of
G_{X}
,
t\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}G_{X}(x_{n})=G_{X}(r^{-}_{X}(t))
, which gives
G_{X}(r^{-}_{X}(t))\geq t
.
Therefore, if

r^{-}_{X}(t)\leq x
, then by the non-decreasingness of
G_{X}
and by the fact that
t\leq G_{X}(r^{-}_{X}(t))
we have
t\leq G_{X}(r^{-}_{X}(t))\leq G_{X}(x)
.

c)

If
c
is continuous from below, then
r_{X}^{-}
is left-continuous.
Indeed, let
(t_{n})
and
t
be such that
t_{n}\uparrow t
.
Then, as
r_{X}^{-}
is non-decreasing, we have:
r_{X}^{-}(t_{n})
is non-decreasing and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}r_{X}^{-}(t_{n})\leq r_{X}^{-}(t)
.

We set
z:=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}r_{X}^{-}(t_{n})
.
It remains to show that
z\geq r_{X}^{-}(t)
.
For each
n
,
z=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}r_{X}^{-}(t_{k})\geq r_{X}^{-}(t_{n})
.
Then, by the non-decreasingness of
G_{X}
and by the fact that
G_{X}(r^{-}_{X}(t_{n}))\geq t_{n}
(which follows from the right-continuity of
G_{X}
as shown in b)), we have:

G_{X}(z)\geq G_{X}(r^{-}_{X}(t_{n}))\geq t_{n}
.
Therefore,

G_{X}(z)\geq t
.
Hence,
z\geq r_{X}^{-}(t)
(by Remark
4
).

Proof
(Proof of Proposition
2
)

Let
U:=G_{Z}(Z)
(as in Lemma
1
).
Let us show
G_{X}(x)=G_{r_{X}^{-}(U)}(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
As the capacity
c
is continuous from below, we use Remark
9
.
Hence, we have:

G_{r_{X}^{-}(U)}(x)=1-c(r_{X}^{-}(U)>x)=1-c(U>G_{X}(x))=G_{U}(G_{X}(x)).

By using Lemma
1
, we obtain
G_{U}(G_{X}(x))=G_{X}(x)
.
Hence,
X
and
r_{X}^{-}(U)
have the same distribution function (with respect to
c
).

Let us show
G_{X+Y}(x)=G_{(r_{X}^{-}+r_{Y}^{-})(U)}(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
We set
f:=r_{X}^{-}+r_{Y}^{-}
. We have that
f
is non-decreasing;
also
f
and
G_{U}
do not have common discontinuities (as
G_{U}
is continuous by Lemma
1
).
Therefore, (cf., e.g., the arguments of the proof of Proposition 3.2 in
Yan 2010
), we have:

G_{(r_{X}^{-}+r_{Y}^{-})(U)}(z)=G_{f(U)}(z)=G_{U}\circ\check{f}(z),

where
\check{f}(z):=\sup\{y:f(y)\leq z\}
for all
z\in\mathbb{R}
. By Lemma
1
, we have:
G_{U}(\check{f}(z))=\check{f}(z)
.
Let us compute

\check{f}(z)=\sup\{y:(r_{X}^{-}+r_{Y}^{-})(y)\leq z\}
.
As
X
and
Y
are comonotonic measurable functions, it follows by Proposition 1.3.1 in
Grigorova 2013b

that
(r_{X}^{-}+r_{Y}^{-})(y)=r_{X+Y}^{-}(y)
for all
y\in(0,1)
.
Hence, we have

\begin{split}\check{f}(z)&=\sup\{y:(r_{X}^{-}+r_{Y}^{-})(y)\leq z\}=\sup\{y:r_{X+Y}^{-}(y)\leq z\}=\sup\{y:y\leq G_{X+Y}(z)\}\\
&=G_{X+Y}(z),\end{split}

where we have used Remark
9
(and the continuity from below of the capacity
c
) in the last but one equality.
Thus,
X+Y
and
(r_{X}^{-}+r_{Y}^{-})(U)
have the same distribution function (with respect to the capacity).

Let us construct a suitable sequence of non-negative step functions
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
that converges to
r_{X}^{-}(U)
in
||\cdot||
.
For each
n
, let us denote
\Pi_{n}=\{b^{n}_{n},\dots,b^{n}_{1},b^{n}_{0}\}
a partition of
[0,1]
with
0=b^{n}_{n}\leq b^{n}_{n-1}\leq\dots\leq b^{n}_{1}\leq b^{n}_{0}=1
, such that
\max_{1\leq i\leq n}(b^{n}_{i-1}-b^{n}_{i})\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
Let
X_{n}
be a step function defined by:
X_{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x^{n}_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{A^{n}_{i}}
, where
x^{n}_{i}:=r_{X}^{-}(b^{n}_{i})

and

A^{n}_{n}=\{0\leq U\leq b^{n}_{n-1}\}\,\,\,\,and\,\,\,\,A^{n}_{i}=\{b^{n}_{i}<U\leq b^{n}_{i-1}\}\,\,\,\,for\,\,\,\,i=1,\dots,n-1.

(5)

For
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta=\delta(\epsilon)>0
and a positive integer
N(\delta)
such that for all
n\geq N(\delta)
, we have:

b^{n}_{i-1}-b^{n}_{i}<\delta
, for
i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}

(since
\max_{1\leq i\leq n}(b^{n}_{i-1}-b^{n}_{i})\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
).
Hence, we have: for all
n\geq N(\delta)
,

b^{n}_{i-1}-\delta<b^{n}_{i}\leq b^{n}_{i-1},\,\,\text{for}\,\,i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}.

Let us fix
n\geq N(\delta)
.
Then, by the monotonicity and the left-continuity properties of
r^{-}_{X}(\cdot)
(cf. Remark
9
), we obtain: for all
i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
,

r^{-}_{X}(b^{n}_{i-1})-r^{-}_{X}(b^{n}_{i})<\epsilon.

Hence,

\epsilon_{n}:=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}(r_{X}^{-}(b^{n}_{i-1})-r_{X}^{-}(b^{n}_{i}))<\epsilon.

Suppose that
\omega\in A^{n}_{i}
,
that is,
U(\omega)\in(b^{n}_{i},b^{n}_{i-1}]
.
Then, as
r_{X}^{-}
is non-decreasing, we have:

r_{X}^{-}(b^{n}_{i})\leq r_{X}^{-}(U(\omega))\leq r_{X}^{-}(b^{n}_{i-1}).

Hence, we obtain, (for
\omega\in A^{n}_{i}
):

|X_{n}(\omega)-r_{X}^{-}(U(\omega))|=|r_{X}^{-}(b^{n}_{i})-r_{X}^{-}(U(\omega))|\leq r_{X}^{-}(b^{n}_{i-1})-r_{X}^{-}(b^{n}_{i})\leq\epsilon_{n}<\epsilon.

Then, for all
\omega\in\Omega
, we get:

|X_{n}(\omega)-r_{X}^{-}(U(\omega))|\leq\epsilon_{n}<\epsilon.

Hence,
||X_{n}-r_{X}^{-}(U)||<\epsilon.

Hence,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}||X_{n}-r_{X}^{-}(U)||=0
.

For the measurable function
r_{Y}^{-}(U)
, by using the same techniques (with
r_{Y}^{-}
instead of
r_{X}^{-}
and the same sets
A^{n}_{i}
as before), we have that there exists a sequence of step functions
Y_{n}
of the form
Y_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y^{n}_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{A^{n}_{i}}
, where
y_{i}^{n}=r^{-}_{Y}(b^{n}_{i})
, such that
||Y_{n}-r_{Y}^{-}(U)||\rightarrow 0
.

Moreover, by Lemma
5
(”only if” direction), we conclude that for each
n
,
X_{n}
and
Y_{n}
are comonotonic step functions. Indeed, we have
X_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x^{n}_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{A^{n}_{i}}
and

Y_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y^{n}_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{A^{n}_{i}}
, where
x^{n}_{1}\geq x^{n}_{2}\geq\cdots\geq x^{n}_{n}
, and where
y^{n}_{1}\geq y^{n}_{2}\geq\cdots\geq y^{n}_{n}
, and where the
{A^{n}_{i}}
’s form a partition of
\Omega
.

Furthermore, since
X
and
r_{X}^{-}(U)
have the same distribution function (with respect to
c
), we have by Proposition
1
:
\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(r_{X}^{-}(U))
.
And similarly

\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(Y)=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(r_{Y}^{-}(U))
. Moreover, as
X
and

are comonotonic, we have:

X+Y
and

have the same distribution function (with respect to

).
Hence, by Proposition
1
,
\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X+Y)=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(r_{X}^{-}(U)+r_{Y}^{-}(U))
.
Combining this with Lemma

and

gives: for all

,

\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X_{n})(\omega)\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{}\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)(\omega),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(Y_{n})(\omega)\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{}\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(Y)(\omega),

and

\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X_{n}+Y_{n})(\omega)\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{}\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X+Y)(\omega).

The proof is completed.∎

.
The proof relies on Lemma

and Proposition

.

(Proof of Theorem

)

Step 1.

2
) for non-negative step functions, by using Lemma
3
. Let
X
and
Y
be two non-negative comonotonic step functions. By Lemma
5
we can represent
X
and

as follows:

X=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}

, where

and

, and the

’s form a partition of

.

,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X+Y)(\omega)&=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}\right)(\omega)\\

where we have used Lemma

Equation (

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X+Y)(\omega)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}((x_{i}-x_{i+1})+(y_{i}-y_{i+1}))\,\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,c(\cup_{j=1}^{i}A_{j}))\\

where we have used again Lemma
3

4
).
We conclude that
\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X+Y)=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)+\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(Y)
.

(Comonotonic additivity for non-negative measurable functions)

) for non-negative comonotonic measurable functions in

. Let

.

,

and

such that

and

\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X_{n}+Y_{n})(\omega)=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X_{n})(\omega)+\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(Y_{n})(\omega)

,

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X+Y)(\omega)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X_{n}+Y_{n})(\omega)\\
&=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X_{n})(\omega)+\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(Y_{n})(\omega)=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)(\omega)+\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(Y)(\omega).\end{split}

Step 3.

(Comonotonic additivity for measurable functions in
\chi(\mathcal{F})
)
We prove the desired equality (
2
) for the general case.
Let
X
and
Y
be comonotonic measurable functions in
\chi(\mathcal{F})
.
We define the measurable functions
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
by
Z_{1}:=X+||X||
,
Z_{2}:=Y+||Y||
, respectively.
Then,
Z_{1},Z_{2}\geq 0
and they are also comonotonic measurable functions.
It follows from Step 2 that for all
\omega\in\Omega
,

\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(Z_{1}+Z_{2})(\omega)=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(Z_{1})(\omega)+\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(Z_{2})(\omega)
.
Therefore, by the translation invariance property of
\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(\cdot)
, we have: for all
\omega\in\Omega
,

\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(Z_{1}+Z_{2})(\omega)=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X+Y)(\omega)+||X||+||Y||,

and
\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(Z_{1})(\omega)=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X+||X||)(\omega)=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)(\omega)+||X||
, and

\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(Z_{2})(\omega)=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(Y+||Y||)(\omega)=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(Y)(\omega)+||Y||
.
Hence,

\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X+Y)(\omega)=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)(\omega)+\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(Y)(\omega)
, for all
\omega\in\Omega
.

The proof is completed.∎

4
Randomly distorted Choquet integrals and first-order stochastic dominance with respect to a capacity

We complete hereafter the list of properties of the randomly distorted Choquet integral.

Proposition 3
(Properties)

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
be a measurable space.
Let
c
be a capacity.
Let
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}
be a
\mathcal{G}
-random distortion function.
Let
X\in\chi(\mathcal{F})
.
Let us consider
\rho
defined by:

\rho(X):=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X).

Then,
\rho
is a
\mathcal{G}
-conditional risk measure valued in
\chi(\mathcal{G})
satisfying the following properties:

(i)

(Consistency in the first-order stochastic dominance with respect to the capacity
c
) If
X,Y\in\chi(\mathcal{F})
are such that
X\preceq_{st,c}Y
. Then,

\rho(X)\leq\rho(Y)
.

(ii)

(Comonotonic additivity)
Under Assumption
1
,
if
c
is continuous from below capacity, and
X
and
Y
are comonotonic, then
\rho\left(X+Y\right)=\rho\left(X\right)+\rho\left(Y\right)
.

(iii)

(Monotonicity) If
X,Y\in\chi(\mathcal{F})
are such that
X\leq Y
. Then,
\rho(X)\leq\rho(Y)
.

(iv)

(Normalisation)
\rho(1)=1
.

(v)

(Translation invariance) For
a\in\mathbb{R}
, we have:
\rho(a+X)=a+\rho(X)
.

(vi)

(Positive homogeneity) For
a\geq 0
, we have:
\rho(aX)=a\rho(X)
.

Proof

Let us prove (i). Let
X,Y\in\chi(\mathcal{F})
be such that
X\preceq_{st,c}Y
. Then, for each
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we have:
c(X>x)\leq c(Y>x)
(by using Definition
6
and Lemma
2
).
As
\phi
is non-decreasing in the second argument, we obtain: for each
\omega\in\Omega
, for each
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\left(\omega,c(X>x)\right)\leq\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\left(\omega,c(Y>x)\right)
.
Hence,

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\left(\omega,c(X>x)\right)dx+\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left[\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\left(\omega,c(X>x)\right)-1\right]dx\\
\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\left(\omega,c(Y>x)\right)dx+\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left[\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\left(\omega,c(Y>x)\right)-1\right]dx.\end{split}

Therefore,
\rho(X)(\omega)=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)(\omega)\leq\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(Y)(\omega)=\rho(Y)(\omega)
.

The proof of (ii) was done in Theorem
3.1
. For the proof of
(iii), we refer to our Proposition
1
.
For (iv), by a direct computation (using that
c(\Omega)=1
), we have:
\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(1_{\Omega})(\omega)=\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\left(\omega,1\right)=1
, for all
\omega\in\Omega
.

For the proof of (v), we refer to Proposition
1
.

Let us prove (vi).
For
a>0
, by Definition
11
, we obtain: for all
\omega\in\Omega
,

\begin{split}&\rho(aX)(\omega)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\left(\omega,c(aX>x)\right)dx+\int_{-\infty}^{0}[\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\left(\omega,c(aX>x)\right)-1]dx\\
&=a\left[\int_{0}^{+\infty}\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\left(\omega,c(X>z)\right)dz+\int_{-\infty}^{0}[\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\left(\omega,c(X>z)\right)-1]dz\right]=a\rho(X)(\omega),\end{split}

where we have used a change of variables. ∎

We are now interested in conditional risk measures which can be represented as
\mathcal{G}
-randomly distorted Choquet integrals with respect to a
\mathcal{G}
-randomly distorted capacity. For the case where
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}
is deterministic, the representation result (
6
) hereafter
can be found in
Grigorova 2014a
(Lemma 3.3).

Theorem 4.1
(Representation in terms of
\mathcal{G}
-randomly distorted Choquet integrals)

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
be a measurable space.
Let
c
be a continuous from below capacity.
We assume Assumption
1
.
Let
\rho:\chi(\mathcal{F})\rightarrow\chi(\mathcal{G})
be a conditional risk measure satisfying the following properties:

(i)

Monotonicity (consistency) in the first-order stochastic dominance with respect to the capacity
c
: If
X\preceq_{st,c}Y
, then
\rho\left(X\right)\leq\rho\left(Y\right)
.

(ii)

Comonotonic additivity: If
X
and
Y
are comonotonic, then
\rho\left(X+Y\right)=\rho\left(X\right)+\rho\left(Y\right)
.

(iii)

Translation invariance: For
a\in\mathbb{R}
, we have:
\rho(X+a)=\rho(X)+a
.

(iv)

Positive homogeneity: For
a\geq 0
, we have:
\rho(aX)=a\rho(X)
.

Then, there exists a
\mathcal{G}
-random distortion function
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}

\rho\left(X\right)=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X),\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,X\in\chi(\mathcal{F}).

(6)

Remark 10

We recall that any
\rho:\chi(\mathcal{F})\rightarrow\chi(\mathcal{G})
satisfying the translation invariance and the positive homogeneity is normalised (
\rho(1)=1
) and grounded (
\rho(0)=0
). Indeed, by the translation invariance invariance, we have:
\rho(2)=\rho(1+1)=\rho(1)+1.
By the positive homogeneity, we have:

\rho(2)=2\rho(1).
Hence,
\rho(1)=1.
Using this, and the translation invariance again, gives:
1=\rho(1)=\rho(0+1)=\rho(0)+1.
Hence,
\rho(0)=0.

Remark 11

By the above theorem, for
X=\mathbbm{1}_{A}
, where
A\in\mathcal{F}
, we have: for all
\omega\in\Omega
,
\rho(\mathbbm{1}_{A})(\omega)=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(\mathbbm{1}_{A})(\omega)=\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,c(A))
.
Moreover, due to Assumption
1
and Lemma
1
, we have: for each

, there exists
A\in\mathcal{F}
such that
c(A)=t
, and in this case
\rho(\mathbbm{1}_{A})(\omega)=\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,t)
, for all
\omega\in\Omega
.
In other words, under Assumption
1
,
\rho
induces a random distortion function
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}
.
Furthermore,
\rho
uniquely induces
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}
.
Indeed, the consistency in the first-order stochastic dominance (with respect to the capacity
c
) implies the ”distribution invariance” with respect to
c
(cf. Definition
9
).
Hence, for each
t\in[0,1]
, if
A,B\in\mathcal{F}
are such that

\rho(\mathbbm{1}_{A})(\omega)=\rho(\mathbbm{1}_{B})(\omega)=\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,t)
, for all
\omega\in\Omega
.

Proof
(Proof of Theorem
4.1
)

Let
t\in[0,1]
.
There exists
A\in\mathcal{F}
such that
c(A)=t
.
Indeed, if we set
A:=\{U>1-t\}
, where
U:=G_{Z}(Z)
, then, according to Lemma
1
, we have:
c(A)=c(U>1-t)=1-G_{U}(1-t)=1-(1-t)=t
.
We define
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}
as follows (cf. Remark
11
): for all
\omega\in\Omega

\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,t)=\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,c(A))=\rho(\mathbbm{1}_{A})(\omega).

(7)

It is easy to check that
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}
is a
\mathcal{G}
-random distortion function.
Indeed, for each

is
\mathcal{G}
-measurable (as

is
\mathcal{G}

Moreover, for

, for all

(as

and as
\rho
is grounded).
Similarly, for
t=1
, for all
\omega\in\Omega
,
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,1)=\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,c(\Omega))=\rho(\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega})(\omega)=\rho(1)(\omega)=1
(as
c(\Omega)=1
and as
\rho
is normalised).
Let
0\leq s\leq t\leq 1
.
There exist
A\in\mathcal{F}
and
B\in\mathcal{F}
such that
B\subseteq A
,
c(B)=s
and
c(A)=t
.
Indeed, by setting

and

,

(as Lemma

), we have that

.
Moreover, applying Lemma

Hence, for all

\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,s)=\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,c(B))=\rho(\mathbbm{1}_{B})(\omega)\leq\rho(\mathbbm{1}_{A})(\omega)=\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,c(A))=\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,t),

. Hence,
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}
is a
\mathcal{G}
-random distortion function.

It follows from Property (i) that

is ”distribution invariant”. Hence,

is well-defined and in fact is unique (cf. Remark
11
).
The sequel of the proof is divided in four steps:

Step 1.

For
X=\mathbbm{1}_{A}
, where

, we have, by using (
7

\rho(\mathbbm{1}_{A})(\omega)=\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,c(A))=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(\mathbbm{1}_{A})(\omega),\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\omega\in\Omega.

Step 2.

Let
X
be a non-negative step function of the form

,

, and

By the comonotonic additivity and positive homogeneity of

\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)(\omega)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{x}_{i}\,\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,c(\bar{A}_{i})),\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\omega\in\Omega.

By Step 1, for each

\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,c(A_{i}))=\rho(\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}})(\omega)
, for all

. Hence,

\rho(X)(\omega)=\rho\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{x}_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{\bar{A}_{i}}\right)(\omega)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{x}_{i}\,\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,c(\bar{A}_{i}))=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)(\omega),\,\,\text{for all}\,\omega\in\Omega.

Step 3.

Let
X
be a non-negative measurable function in
\chi(\mathcal{F})
.
As

2
.
By Proposition

, there exists a sequence of non-negative step functions

in
||\cdot||
. Moreover,

and

have the same distribution.

and

.

By Lemma

By Remark

On the other hand, by Step 2, we have:

.
Using this, and equations (

.

Step 4.

Let

In this section, we first recall the definition of the increasing convex stochastic ordering (with respect to a capacity

Definition 12

Let

and

be in
\chi(\mathcal{F})

\mathbb{E}_{c}\left(u\left(X\right)\right)\leq\mathbb{E}_{c}\left(u\left(Y\right)\right)

Definition 13

Let
X
and
Y
be in
\chi(\mathcal{F})
and let
c

in the stop-loss ordering with respect to the capacity

The increasing convex stochastic dominance (with respect to a capacity) implies the stop-loss stochastic dominance (with respect to a capacity).
The following characterisation of the stop-loss ordering relation with respect to a capacity can be found in
Grigorova 2014b
.

Proposition 4

Let
c
be a capacity.
Let
X
and
Y
be in
\chi(\mathcal{F})
.
The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\mathbb{E}_{c}\left(\left(X-b\right)^{+}\right)\leq\mathbb{E}_{c}\left(\left(Y-b\right)^{+}\right)

for all
b\in\mathbb{R}
.

(ii)

\int_{x}^{+\infty}\left(1-G_{X}\left(z\right)\right)dz\leq\int_{x}^{+\infty}\left(1-G_{Y}\left(z\right)\right)dz

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

(iii)

\int_{\alpha}^{1}r_{X}\left(u\right)du\leq\int_{\alpha}^{1}r_{Y}\left(u\right)du

for all
\alpha\in\left(0,1\right)
.

(iv)

For all
g:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
integrable and non-decreasing functions, we have:

\int_{0}^{1}g(t)r_{X}(t)dt\leq\int_{0}^{1}g(t)r_{Y}(t)dt.

The following proposition is a generalisation of property (iv), when the function
g
is a random function (depending on
\omega
and
t
). It will be used in the sequel.

Proposition 5

Let
c
be a capacity.
Let
X,Y\in\chi(\mathcal{F})
.
The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

For all
g:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}

\int_{0}^{1}g(t)r_{X}(t)dt\leq\int_{0}^{1}g(t)r_{Y}(t)dt.

(ii)

For all random functions
g:\Omega\times[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
, which are integrable and non-decreasing (in the second argument), we have: for all
\omega\in\Omega
,

\int_{0}^{1}g(\omega,t)r_{X}(t)dt\leq\int_{0}^{1}g(\omega,t)r_{Y}(t)dt.

Proof

Let
\omega\in\Omega
be fixed. The implication
(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
is trivially obtained by taking
g(\omega,t):=g(t)
, which is non-negative, non-decreasing and integrable.

Let us now prove the converse implication.
Let us fix
\omega
.
Since
\omega
is fixed, the function
t\mapsto g(\omega,t)
is non-negative, integrable,
and non-decreasing.
Hence, by applying (i) with the function
t\mapsto g(\omega,t)
(for a fixed
\omega\in\Omega
) we get:

\int_{0}^{1}g(\omega,t)r_{X}(t)dt\leq\int_{0}^{1}g(\omega,t)r_{Y}(t)dt.

As this can be done for all
\omega\in\Omega
, the result holds. ∎

Lemma 6

be a capacity.
Let

be a

\mathcal{G}
-random distortion function.
For all

(12)

Proof

The proof of the lemma follows the same arguments as Lemma 3.16 in
Grigorova 2014a

It suffices to prove (

Let

Due to the nondecreasingness of

(13)

For
\omega

on
(0,1)

Let

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{1}{\phi}^{{}^{\prime}}(\omega,1-t)r_{X}^{+}(t)dt=\int_{0}^{1}{\phi}^{{}^{\prime}}(\omega,1-t)\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbbm{1}_{\{G_{X}(s)\leq t\}}dsdt\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}{\phi}^{{}^{\prime}}(\omega,1-t)\mathbbm{1}_{\{G_{X}(s)\leq t\}}dtds=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}{\phi}^{{}^{\prime}}(\omega,z)\mathbbm{1}_{\{z\leq 1-G_{X}(s)\}}dzds\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1-G_{X}(s)}{\phi}^{{}^{\prime}}(\omega,z)dzds\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left({\phi^{\mathcal{G}}}(\omega,1-G_{X}(s))-{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}}(\omega,0^{+})\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\{G_{X}(s)<1\}}ds.\end{split}

Hence, by the definition of the
\mathcal{G}
-randomly distorted Choquet integral and the fact that

\sup_{t<1}r_{X}^{+}(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbbm{1}_{\{G_{X}(s)<1\}}ds,

we obtain:

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{1}{\phi}^{{}^{\prime}}(\omega,1-t)r_{X}^{+}(t)dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}}(\omega,1-G_{X}(s))ds-{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}}(\omega,0^{+})\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbbm{1}_{\{G_{X}(s)<1\}}ds\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)(\omega)-{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}}(\omega,0^{+})\sup_{t<1}r_{X}^{+}(t)=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)(\omega)-{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}}(\omega,0^{+})r_{X}^{+}(1).\end{split}

∎

Proof
(Proof of Proposition
6
)

Let
X,Y\in\chi(\mathcal{F})
be such that
X\preceq_{sl,c}Y
.
Let us show that
\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)\leq\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(Y)
. Let
\omega\in\Omega
be fixed.
Due to Lemma
6
, it is enough to show that

\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,0+)\,\sup_{t<1}r_{X}^{+}(t)+\int_{0}^{1}{\phi}^{{}^{\prime}}(\omega,1-t)r_{X}^{+}(t)dt\leq\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,0+)\,\sup_{t<1}r_{Y}^{+}(t)\int_{0}^{1}{\phi}^{{}^{\prime}}(\omega,1-t)r_{Y}^{+}(t)dt.

By using Proposition
5
, we have:

\int_{0}^{1}{\phi}^{{}^{\prime}}(\omega,1-t)r_{X}^{+}(t)dt\leq\int_{0}^{1}{\phi}^{{}^{\prime}}(\omega,1-t)r_{Y}^{+}(t)dt.

As
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,0+)\geq 0
, it remains to show that
\sup_{t<1}r_{X}^{+}(t)\leq\sup_{t<1}r_{Y}^{+}(t)
.
By contradiction, assume that
\sup_{t<1}r_{X}^{+}(t)>\sup_{t<1}r_{Y}^{+}(t)
. Then, there exists
t_{0}\in[0,1)
such that

r_{X}^{+}(s)\geq r_{X}^{+}(t_{0})>\sup_{t<1}r_{Y}^{+}(t)\,\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,s\geq t_{0}.

This implies that
r_{X}^{+}(s)>r_{Y}^{+}(s)
, for all
s\geq t_{0}
.
Hence, we get:

\int_{t_{0}}^{1}r_{X}^{+}(s)-r_{Y}^{+}(s)\,ds>0
,
which contradicts the fact that
X\preceq_{sl,c}Y
(cf. Theorem
4
(iii)).
Hence,
X\preceq_{sl,c}Y
implies that
\sup_{t<1}r_{X}^{+}(t)\leq\sup_{t<1}r_{Y}^{+}(t)
.∎

Theorem 5.1
(Representation with respect to a concave random distortion)

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
be a measurable space.
Let
c
be a continuous from below.
We assume Assumption
1
.
Let
\rho:\chi(\mathcal{F})\rightarrow\chi(\mathcal{G})
be a conditional risk measure satisfying the following properties:

(i)

Monotonicity (consistency) in the stop-loss stochastic dominance with respect to the capacity
c
: If
X\preceq_{sl,c}Y
, then
\rho\left(X\right)\leq\rho\left(Y\right)
.

(ii)

Comonotonic additivity: If
X
and
Y
are comonotonic, then
\rho\left(X+Y\right)=\rho\left(X\right)+\rho\left(Y\right)
.

(iii)

Translation invariance: For
a\in\mathbb{R}
, we have:
\rho(X+a)=\rho(X)+a
.

(iv)

Positive homogeneity: For
a\geq 0
, we have:
\rho(aX)=a\rho(X)
.

Then, there exists a
concave

\mathcal{G}
-random distortion function
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}
such that

\rho\left(X\right)=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X),\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,X\in\chi(\mathcal{F}).

Proof

Since the consistency in the
\preceq_{sl,c}
stochastic dominance implies the consistency in the
\preceq_{st,c}
stochastic dominance (cf.
Grigorova 2014a
), we apply Theorem
4.1
.
By Theorem
4.1
, there exists a
\mathcal{G}
-random distortion function
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}
such that

\rho\left(X\right)=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,X\in\chi(\mathcal{F}).

It remains to show that the
\mathcal{G}
-random distortion function
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}
is concave.
By Assumption
1
, we have the following: for any
s,t\in[0,1]
, there exist
A,B,C\in\mathcal{F}
such that
c(A)=s
,
c(B)=t
,
A\subset B
, and
c(C)=\frac{s+t}{2}
.
We set

X:=\frac{1}{2}\mathbbm{1}_{A}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbbm{1}_{B}
and
Y:=\mathbbm{1}_{C}
.
By the proof of Theorem 3.7. in
Grigorova 2014a
, we have
X\preceq_{sl,c}Y
.
The consistency with respect to
\preceq_{sl,c}
of
\rho
implies that
\rho(X)\leq\rho(Y)
. Hence, for all
\omega\in\Omega
, we have:

\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathbbm{1}_{A}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbbm{1}_{B}\right)(\omega)\leq\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{C}\right)(\omega).

As
\frac{1}{2}\mathbbm{1}_{A}
and
\frac{1}{2}\mathbbm{1}_{B}
are comonotonic (as
A\subset B
),
by the positive homogeneity and comonotonic additivity of
\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(\cdot)
, we get:

\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathbbm{1}_{A}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbbm{1}_{B}\right)(\omega)=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(\mathbbm{1}_{A})(\omega)+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(\mathbbm{1}_{B})(\omega)\leq\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(\mathbbm{1}_{C})(\omega).

Hence,

\frac{1}{2}\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,c(A))+\frac{1}{2}\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,c(B))\leq\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,c(C)).

The concavity of
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}
follows
as
c(A)=s
,
c(B)=t
,
c(C)=\frac{s+t}{2}
and as
s
and
t
are arbitrary. ∎

6
Examples

In this section, we will present some extensions of well-known distortion risk measures to our framework.
From a mathematical point of view, we will ”randomise” the distortion function.

6.1
A ”randomised” Value at Risk

In a probabilistic framework,
the static Value at Risk (VaR) at level
\alpha\in(0,1)
of a given
potential loss

X\in\chi(\mathcal{F})
is defined by:

VaR_{\alpha}(X)=q^{-}_{X}(\alpha)
.
The deterministic distortion function induced by
VaR_{\alpha}
is of the following form
\psi(x)=\mathbbm{1}_{(1-\alpha,1]}(x)
for all
x\in[0,1]
.
The same sign convention in the definition of the VaR as the one used in this paper is used, for instance, in

Embrechts et al. 2014
,

Denuit et al. 2006
,

Song and Yan 2009

or
Dhaene et al. 2006
.
The probabilistic VaR has been extended to a capacity framework in
Grigorova 2013b
, under the name GVaR (for Generalized VaR).

Example 1

Let us consider the simple case where the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{G}
is
\mathcal{G}=\{\emptyset,\Omega,A,A^{c}\}
. Our framework allows to have two different levels for the VaR, depending on whether
\omega
is in
A
or in
A^{c}
, say
\alpha
and
\beta
, where
\alpha<\beta
.
We define a random distortion function
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}
as follows: for all
t\in[0,1]
,

\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,t):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbbm{1}_{(1-\alpha,1]}(t),&\text{if}\,\,\omega\in A\\
\mathbbm{1}_{(1-\beta,1]}(t),&\text{if}\,\,\omega\in A^{c}.\end{array}\right.

Let
c
be a given capacity (modelling ambiguity).
For the above choice of
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}
, we consider the randomly distorted Choquet integral

\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)
, for
X\in\chi
.
We will compute
\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ c}(X)
in the simple case where
X
is defined as
X:=\mathbbm{1}_{C}
, with
C\in\mathcal{F}
.

a.

The probabilistic framework

In the particular case where
c
is a probability measure, we set:
P(C)=p
, where
p\in[0,1]
.
We obtain:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ P}(X)(\omega)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ P}(\mathbbm{1}_{C})(\omega)=\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,P(C))=\phi^{\mathcal{G}}(\omega,p)

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{1}_{(1-\alpha,1]}(p)\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\omega)+\mathbbm{1}_{(1-\beta,1]}(p)\mathbbm{1}_{A^{c}}(\omega).

Hence, as
\alpha<\beta
, we have:

1.

If
p\leq 1-\beta
, then

\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ P}(X)(\omega)=0
, for all
\omega\in\Omega
.

2.

If
1-\beta<p\leq 1-\alpha
, then

\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ P}(X)(\omega)=\mathbbm{1}_{A^{c}}(\omega)
, for all
\omega\in\Omega
.

3.

If
p>1-\alpha
, then
\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\mathcal{G}}\circ P}(X)(\omega)=1
, for all
\omega\in\Omega
.

An application:

Let us consider two experts who agree on the class of risk measures to be used, in this case
\{VaR_{\alpha}:\alpha\in(0,1)\}
but who disagree on the acceptable level of risk within this class of risk measures, either
\alpha
or
\beta
(with
\alpha<\beta
).
The two experts consider that they face a random loss of the form
X=\mathbbm{1}_{C}
, and agree on the probability
P(C)=p
.
By the definition of the VaR,
if
p\leq 1-\beta
, then both experts attribute a risk of
0
to
X
.
Also, if
p>1-\alpha
, then both experts attribute a risk of
1
to
X
.
But if
1-\beta<p\leq 1-\alpha
, then they disagree: the first expert attributes
0
and the second expert attributes
1
.

Using the randomly distorted Choquet integrals, with
\phi^{\mathcal{G}}
as above, allows us to randomise the risk measure in the situation where the two experts disagree (that is, where
1-\beta<p\leq 1-\alpha
): we use the binary random variable
\mathbbm{1}_{A^{c}}
instead of a fixed number (when
1-\beta<p\leq 1-\alpha
).
In this interpretation, depending on how confident a decision maker is in the first expert or in the second expert, the decision maker can take a different set
A

In some sense,
A
is the set where the decision maker considers that the first expert is ”right” and
A^{c}
is the set where the decision maker considers that the second expert is ”right” (in the situation when the two experts disagree on the level of risk to be used). Thus,
\mathcal{G}=\{\emptyset,\Omega,A,A^{c}\}
can be interpreted as the information which the decision maker has on the experts.

6.2

In the usual probabilistic framework,
the Average Value at Risk (AVaR) (also known as Tail Value at Risk (TVaR)) at level
\alpha\in(0,1)

\psi(x)=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\min\left\{x,1-\alpha\right\}

Embrechts et al. 2014

Denuit et al. 2006

Dhaene et al. 2006

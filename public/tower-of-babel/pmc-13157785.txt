Closed loop full process management and application of body fluid specimens in hospitalised patients
Authors: Dan Zhao, Yingxin Peng, Jie Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo develop and implement a closed-loop full-process management system for body fluid specimens in hospitalised patients, with the aim of improving submission timeliness and specimen quality.MethodsA multidisciplinary inpatient specimen management team was established to analyse the causes of inefficiencies in the collection-to-submission process of body fluid specimens. Based on these findings, targeted interventions were formulated and implemented in February 2025. The preanalytical turnaround times and unqualified specimen rate before and after implementation were compared.ResultsAfter implementing the closed-loop management system, the delayed submission rate of body fluid specimens decreased from 15.62% to 5.8% and the unqualified specimen rate decreased from 0.70% to 0.39%. The improvement in submission timeliness and specimen quality was statistically significant (p<0.05). All indicators showed significant differences at 3 and 6 months after intervention compared with before intervention (p<0.05).ConclusionThe implementation of a closed-loop full-process management system for body fluid specimens effectively improved the timeliness of specimen submission, reduced delays and decreased the occurrence of unqualified specimens, thereby enhancing patient safety.Keywords: Quality improvement, Management, Nurses

Introduction

Timely and accurate laboratory testing forms the foundation of effective clinical diagnosis, treatment planning and patient safety. Among hospitalised patients, the collection, transportation and processing of body fluid specimens—particularly urine and stool—are critical to the diagnostic workflow. Nevertheless, prolonged preanalytical turnaround times (pre-TATs) and poor specimen quality continue to compromise laboratory efficiency and ultimately hinder patient care.1 Preanalytical errors such as improper collection technique, mislabeling, insufficient volume, haemolysis and transportation delays remain significant contributors to specimen rejection and diagnostic inaccuracy.2 3

Body fluid specimens are particularly vulnerable to such errors due to their variable collection methods, contamination risks and the time-sensitive nature of certain assays.4 The rejection rate of urine specimens can be as high as 9.2%–13.8%.5 Incomplete workflows and weak handoff mechanisms between clinical departments, nursing staff and laboratory personnel can result in specimen degradation, increased pre-TATs and unnecessary test repetitions.2

With the advancement of hospital digitalisation and smart medical infrastructure, there is growing potential to design closed-loop management systems that standardise and monitor specimen workflows in real time.3 4 6

A structured closed-loop management system—one that standardises the process from collection to result delivery, implements real-time monitoring and strengthens departmental coordination—has great potential in reducing preanalytical errors, improving traceability and enhancing overall specimen quality. However, most reported interventions have focused primarily on blood specimens or outpatient/emergency care rather than inpatient body fluid workflow.3

This quality improvement (QI) project aims to develop and implement a comprehensive closed-loop management model for hospitalised body fluid specimens to improve the timeliness of specimen submission and reduce the incidence of non-compliant body fluid specimens.

Methods

Study design and setting

This single-centre quasiexperimental study was conducted at the Fourth Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University, a tertiary general hospital with 800 beds and 27 inpatient wards. This study was conducted according to the guidelines of the Standards for Quality Improvement Reporting Excellence7 (online supplemental file 1).

Sample

All 27 wards of the research hospital participated in the study. The data selection period before the intervention was from 1 January 2025 to 31 January 2025. The data selection period after the intervention was from 1 March 2025 to 31 March 2025.

Programme description

Establishment of a specimen management team for inpatients

A multidisciplinary management team was established under the leadership of the nursing department to oversee the closed-loop management of body fluid specimens among inpatients. The team was led by the director of nursing and comprised head nurses from internal medicine and surgery, three staff from the nursing department, six additional nurse managers, two full-time personnel from the hospital’s logistics transportation system, one senior staff member from the laboratory department and two specialists from the information technology department.

Development and implementation of a smart digital-driven closed-loop management system for specimens

The nursing department leveraged the hospital information system to monitor key time points including barcode printing, specimen collection and laboratory receipt. Data on the sampling-to-submission interval and rejection rates of body fluid specimens were extracted and analysed across all inpatient departments. Departments with a 90th percentile pre-TATs exceeding 2 hours were targeted for supervision. Root causes for delays and specimen rejection were explored collaboratively.

Based on the analysis, the management team identified key issues and created a fishbone diagram (figure 1). These interventions focused on four domains: standardised operations, smart system support, process optimisation and multidisciplinary collaboration (table 1).

Intervention measures

PDA, personal digital assistant.

To ensure that the improvements achieved in specimen timeliness and quality are sustained over time, several long-term strategies were integrated into routine practice following the intervention. The nursing department established regular audits of key quality indicators, including pre-TATs and unacceptable specimen rate, to monitor ongoing performance. On-site supervision during specimen distribution and submission was strengthened to reinforce adherence to standardised procedures. Newly employed nurses now receive structured training on specimen collection and submission workflows as part of mandatory onboarding. Additionally, any deviation from the required process—such as delays, improper handling or incomplete labelling—is reported as a nursing adverse event, creating accountability and promoting continuous compliance. These measures were designed to institutionalise the improved processes and reduce the risk of regression once the formal intervention period ended. Labelling errors and container mismatches are classified as adverse events and trigger systematic corrective actions to prevent recurrence.

Measurements

Timeliness of specimen submission for inpatients’ body fluid samples

The timeliness of body fluid specimen submission was compared between January 2025 (pre intervention) and March 2025 (1 month post intervention). The information system was used to extract the sampling time and the time when the laboratory received the specimen, thereby calculating pre-TATs. For the purposes of standardised statistical analysis, only urine and stool specimens were included in the body fluid sample category. Specimens with incomplete sampling or laboratory receipt time records were excluded from the analysis.

Delayed specimen submission was defined as a body fluid sample not being received by the laboratory within 2 hours after collection. Delayed submission rate (%) = (number of delayed submissions/total number of specimens during the period)×100%.

Quality of body fluid specimen results in inpatients

The quality of the body fluid specimens was assessed based on the incidence of unqualified (non-compliant) specimens. The incidence of unqualified body fluid unqualified specimen rate (%)=(number of unqualified specimens/total number of specimens during the period)×100%.

Sustainability assessment

To assess the long-term sustainability of the improvement measures, the timeliness of specimen delivery and the non-compliance rate were monitored 3 months (May 2025) and 6 months (August 2025) after the intervention was implemented.

Data collection

The collection time and receipt time of body fluid specimens are collected by the hospital information department, and the frequency and specific reasons for unqualified body fluid specimens are reported by the laboratory department.

Data analysis

SPSS V.26.0 software was used to establish a database and perform statistical analyses. The measurement data that did not conform to the normal distribution were expressed as median (IQR). The rank sum test was used for comparison between the two groups, and the χ2 test was used for comparison of count data. P value<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Discussion

This study constructed a closed-loop management system for body fluid specimens with spatiotemporal linkage, which significantly optimised the transportation efficiency of body fluid specimens. By adjusting the execution process of body fluid specimens to three stages of ‘issuance → collection → delivery for inspection’ and developing an independent information module for segmented tracking, the risk of confusion between ‘execution time and actual sampling time’ in the previous process was effectively avoided. Through the hierarchical management strategy of the logistics system of scheduled centralised delivery for inspection+priority transportation of emergency specimens and combined with a foam shockproof device, while ensuring the quality of specimens, the delivery time of routine specimens is shortened to within 30 min. This ‘data-driven decision-making+intelligent resource scheduling’ model optimises the spatiotemporal resource allocation logic of specimen delivery for inspection, realises the full-process and refined closed-loop management of body fluid specimens of hospitalised patients and significantly improves pre-TATs of body fluid samples of hospitalised patients.8

Timely submission of urine and stool specimens is crucial for maintaining sample integrity, ensuring sample quality and thus improving patient safety. For urine culture specimens, pre-TATs exceeding 2 hours before testing increase the false negative rate by 12.3% and the contamination rate by 12.3%.9,11 The results of this study indicate that implementing closed-loop management of the entire inpatient specimen process reduced the incidence of substandard inpatient body fluid specimens compared with before implementation. In addition to shortening pre-TATs, this study adopted several measures to reduce the potential risk of pretest quality problems. First, relying on a ‘double-checking’ mechanism and PDA（personal digital assistant） barcode scanning verification, the identification error rate is close to zero, effectively preventing label-container mismatch situations.12 Second, the shock-resistant design of the logistics system reduces the damage rate during transportation. With the help of a digitally driven inpatient specimen information system, medical staff can intuitively understand the stage of a specimen’s development. This helps them proactively identify problems throughout the entire process of specimen collection, delivery and testing, and take positive intervention measures. It also enables a feedback mechanism within the comprehensive test results management system, reducing the rate of substandard body fluid specimens. A recent analysis13 using a logistic regression-clustering joint model to examine pre-TATs for urine culture specimens in hospital wards reported that prolonged turnaround time was strongly associated with operational and workflow factors such as delayed collection, batching behaviour and suboptimal transport processes.

The multidepartmental quality management model plays an irreplaceable synergistic role in improving the quality management efficiency of specimens before testing.14 15 This study found that the multi-departmental quality management model can improve the timeliness of body fluid specimens and improve the quality of specimens, which is similar to previous studies. In this study, the nursing department took the lead and integrated multiple resources such as clinical departments, nursing units, laboratory departments and information departments, which can systematically solve the problems of information transmission errors and poor process connection in the pretest stage. The information department needs to build an electronic specimen tracking system to achieve full process traceability from doctor’s order issuance to specimen receipt. The multidimensional collaborative model with quality culture as the link ultimately forms a closed-loop management of ‘standard setting-execution monitoring-feedback optimisation’, providing structural support for the continuous improvement of pretest quality management.16

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, it was conducted in only one tertiary hospital and the duration of the study intervention was short, which may limit the generalisability of the findings to other healthcare systems with different institutional policies, staffing patterns and technological infrastructure. This study only focused on urine and faeces specimens, and data from other body fluid specimens were not included in the statistics, which limits the further promotion of the research results. The primary focus of this QIs project is optimising the sample delivery process, rather than directly addressing the sample collection process. Future plans include implementing a visual collection tutorial and introducing nurse verification before sample delivery to reduce non-compliance during the centralised collection process.

Conclusion

The implementation of a closed-loop full-process management system for inpatient specimens has improved the timeliness of body fluid specimen submission, reduced the incidence of unqualified specimens and enhanced patient safety. Moving forward, efforts will focus on refining the collection and submission processes for other types of body fluid specimens, such as sputum samples, building on the current achievements to further improve preanalytical specimen quality.
